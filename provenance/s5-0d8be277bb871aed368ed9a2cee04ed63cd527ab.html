The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite good types \(G\), agent types \(T\), subadditive valuations \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), population masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita supplies \(b_g\), and a feasible reference allocation \(\widetilde{x}\), find masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:g\in S}x_{t,S}=b_g\), such that every positive-mass bundle \(S\) of type \(t\) is EF1 toward every positive-mass bundle \(R\) of type \(u\), and \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)\ge e^{-2/e}\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widetilde{x})\), where \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)=\prod_{t,S}v_t(S)^{x_{t,S}}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a set of \(m\geq1\) good types; each bundle \(S\subseteq G\) contains at most one copy of each good type.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\geq1\) agent types \(T\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a normalized, monotone, subadditive valuation \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\), accessed through an exact value oracle. Supplies \(b_g\in[0,1]\) are binary rationals.
An allocation consists of nonnegative bundle masses satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:g\in S}x_{t,S}=b_g\). A feasible reference allocation \(\widetilde{x}\) is supplied by listing its positive binary-rational entries; output allocations use the same representation.
Supportwise EF1 means that whenever \(x_{t,S}>0\), \(x_{u,R}>0\), and \(R\neq\varnothing\), some \(g\in R\) satisfies \(v_t(S)\geq v_t(R\setminus\{g\})\); this includes comparisons within one type.
Define \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)=\prod_{t,S:x_{t,S}>0}v_t(S)^{x_{t,S}}\), with value zero if any positive-mass bundle has zero value. Let \(L\) be the total explicit input bit length plus an upper bound on the bit length of any oracle answer.

Can every such instance be assigned a supportwise EF1 allocation \(x\) satisfying \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)\geq e^{-2/e}\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widetilde{x})\) using time and value queries polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or can this approximation guarantee fail to exist or be computationally intractable?

For finitely many agents and indivisible goods, a complete EF1 allocation retaining at least \(e^{-2/e}\) of any supplied allocation’s NSW is computable in polynomial time using value queries. [Barman–Suzuki](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12461v4#S3)
No result settling this exact supportwise mass-allocation guarantee was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored normalized and monotone valuations, as required by the discrete model.
  • Normalized total population mass to one so that the stated welfare product is a geometric mean.
  • Interpreted bundles as sets, allowing at most one copy of each good type per individual; supplies are binary rationals in [0,1].
  • Retained the anchor’s exact value-oracle model, restricted oracle values to nonnegative rationals, and included an oracle-answer bit bound in L.
  • Specified reference and output allocations through their positive binary-rational entries.
  • Made EF1 vacuous toward empty bundles and applied it to all positive-mass bundle pairs, including pairs belonging to the same type.
  • Omitted zero-mass factors from the welfare product and assigned welfare zero whenever a positive-mass bundle has zero value; no positive-reference-welfare promise was imposed.

Exact-supply partial results

1. Scope of the results

The general question remains unresolved. The following two special cases admit stronger welfare guarantees:

  • Cardinality-dependent valuations: If, for every type $t$, the value $v_t(S)$ depends only on $|S|$, an exact-supply supportwise EFx allocation retaining at least half the reference welfare is computable in polynomial time.
  • Proportional additive valuations: If all valuations are positive multiples of one additive valuation, an exact-supply supportwise EF1 allocation attaining the maximum feasible welfare exists and has a polynomial-size rational representation. The proof does not establish a polynomial-time algorithm.

Both results allow arbitrary rational masses and supplies. All supply constraints below are equalities.

Write
$$ B=\sum_{g\in G}b_g. $$

I use supportwise EFx to mean that, for every supported pair $(t,S),(u,R)$ with $R\neq\varnothing$,
$$ v_t(S)\geq v_t(R\setminus\{g\}) \qquad\text{for every }g\in R. $$
This implies supportwise EF1.

2. A polynomial construction with balanced bundle sizes

The following construction uses only the supplies.

Lemma. For any rational vector $b\in[0,1]^m$, one can compute a rational distribution $(q_S)_{S\subseteq G}$ such that
$$ \sum_Sq_S=1, \qquad \sum_{S:g\in S}q_S=b_g, $$
and every supported bundle has size either $\lfloor B\rfloor$ or $\lceil B\rceil$. The distribution has at most $m+1$ positive entries and is computable in time polynomial in $m,L$.

Moreover, after ordering the goods as $g_1,\ldots,g_m$, every two supported bundles have prefix counts differing by at most one.

Proof. Define cumulative sums
$$ a_0=0, \qquad a_j=\sum_{i=1}^jb_{g_i}. $$
For $u\in(0,1)$, away from the fractional parts of the $a_j$, define
$$ S(u)= \left\{ g_j: u+\ell\in[a_{j-1},a_j) \text{ for some }\ell\in\mathbb Z \right\}. $$

Each interval $[a_{j-1},a_j)$ has length $b_{g_j}\leq1$. Consequently, for such a $u$, it contains at most one point of the form $u+\ell$. Thus this definition never assigns multiple copies of a good.

The prefix count
$$ N_j(u)=|S(u)\cap\{g_1,\ldots,g_j\}| $$
equals the number of points $u+\ell$ in $[0,a_j)$. Write $a_j=d+r$, where $d$ is an integer and $0\leq r<1$. Direct counting gives
$$ N_j(u)= \begin{cases} d+1,&u<r,\\ d,&u>r. \end{cases} $$
When $r=0$, the second case applies throughout $(0,1)$. Therefore,
$$ N_j(u)\in\{\lfloor a_j\rfloor,\lceil a_j\rceil\}. $$
In particular, two supported bundles have prefix counts differing by at most one. Taking $j=m$ proves the asserted bundle-size bound.

Taking $u$ uniformly in $(0,1)$ gives each good its required marginal. Indeed,
$$ \begin{aligned} \Pr(g_j\in S(u)) &=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z} \int_0^1 \mathbf 1_{[a_{j-1},a_j)}(u+\ell)\,du\\ &=a_j-a_{j-1}\\ &=b_{g_j}. \end{aligned} $$
The sum of indicators takes values only zero and one, which justifies its interpretation as a probability.

For an explicit finite representation, sort the distinct numbers
$$ 0,\quad 1,\quad a_j-\lfloor a_j\rfloor \quad (1\leq j\leq m). $$
The bundle $S(u)$ is constant on every open interval between consecutive numbers. Assign that bundle the mass equal to the interval’s length, and aggregate repeated bundles. There are at most $m+1$ intervals.

All endpoints and masses are rational. A common denominator for the supplies has bit length $O(L)$, and the cumulative sums have magnitude at most $m$. Hence their encoding lengths are polynomial in $m,L$. Sorting the endpoints and computing the bundles therefore take polynomial time. $\square$

Given this distribution, define
$$ x_{t,S}=\mu_tq_S. $$
Then
$$ \sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t,S:g\in S}x_{t,S} =\left(\sum_t\mu_t\right)b_g=b_g. $$
The resulting allocation has at most $\tau(m+1)$ positive entries, with polynomial rational encoding length.

3. Cardinality-dependent subadditive valuations

Theorem. Suppose that, for every type $t$, there is a function
$$ f_t:\{0,\ldots,m\}\longrightarrow\mathbb Q_{\geq0} $$
such that
$$ v_t(S)=f_t(|S|). $$
Under the normalization, monotonicity, and subadditivity assumptions in the question, one can compute an exact-supply supportwise EFx allocation $x$ in polynomial time satisfying
$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x) \geq \frac12\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widetilde{x}). $$

If $B\geq1$ and $k=\lfloor B\rfloor$, the construction satisfies the sharper bound
$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x) \geq \frac{k}{B+k-1} \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widetilde{x}). $$

Proof.

Case $B<1$

Every feasible allocation $z$ has positive empty-bundle mass. Indeed,
$$ B=\sum_{t,S}|S|z_{t,S} \geq \sum_{t,S\neq\varnothing}z_{t,S}, $$
so its total empty-bundle mass is at least $1-B>0$. Normalization therefore implies
$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widetilde{x})=0. $$

Use
$$ x_{t,\{g\}}=\mu_tb_g, \qquad x_{t,\varnothing}=\mu_t(1-B). $$
This allocation has the required masses and supplies. Every nonempty target bundle is a singleton, whose deletion leaves the empty bundle, of value zero. Thus the allocation is supportwise EFx, and the required welfare inequality holds.

Case $B\geq1$: construction and fairness

Set $k=\lfloor B\rfloor\geq1$, and use the distribution from Section 2 with
$$ x_{t,S}=\mu_tq_S. $$
Every supported bundle has size at least $k$ and at most $k+1$.

For any supported source bundle $S$, any supported nonempty target bundle $R$, and every $g\in R$, monotonicity of $f_t$ gives
$$ v_t(S)=f_t(|S|) \geq f_t(k) \geq f_t(|R|-1) =v_t(R\setminus\{g\}). $$
Hence $x$ is supportwise EFx, including comparisons within a type.

If the reference welfare is zero, the welfare requirement is already satisfied. Assume henceforth that it is positive.

Positivity of the comparison values

For each type $t$, some reference bundle has positive value. Iterated subadditivity and cardinality dependence give
$$ v_t(S)\leq\sum_{g\in S}v_t(\{g\}) =|S|f_t(1). $$
Thus $f_t(1)>0$. Since $k\geq1$, monotonicity yields
$$ f_t(k)>0. $$
Every output bundle consequently has positive value, and logarithmic welfare is well-defined.

Bounding the reference welfare

For each positive integer $r\leq m$, partition an $r$-element bundle into
$$ d=\left\lceil\frac rk\right\rceil $$
nonempty pieces, each of size at most $k$. Subadditivity and monotonicity imply
$$ f_t(r)\leq d\,f_t(k). $$

All reference bundles are nonempty because the reference welfare is positive. Therefore,
$$ \begin{aligned} \log\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widetilde{x}) &=\sum_{t,S}\widetilde{x}_{t,S}\log f_t(|S|)\\ &\leq \sum_t\mu_t\log f_t(k) + \sum_{t,S}\widetilde{x}_{t,S} \log\left\lceil\frac{|S|}{k}\right\rceil. \end{aligned} $$

The reference masses sum to one. Applying concavity of the logarithm gives
$$ \sum_{t,S}\widetilde{x}_{t,S} \log\left\lceil\frac{|S|}{k}\right\rceil \leq \log\left( \sum_{t,S}\widetilde{x}_{t,S} \left\lceil\frac{|S|}{k}\right\rceil \right). $$
For every positive integer $r$,
$$ \left\lceil\frac rk\right\rceil \leq\frac{r+k-1}{k}. $$
Reference feasibility gives
$$ \sum_{t,S}|S|\widetilde{x}_{t,S}=B. $$
Combining these facts,
$$ \log\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widetilde{x}) \leq \sum_t\mu_t\log f_t(k) + \log\frac{B+k-1}{k}. $$

Every output bundle has value at least $f_t(k)$ for its recipient type. Hence
$$ \log\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x) \geq \sum_t\mu_t\log f_t(k). $$
Subtracting and exponentiating proves
$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x) \geq \frac{k}{B+k-1} \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widetilde{x}). $$
Finally, $B<k+1$ implies
$$ B+k-1<2k, $$
so the displayed factor exceeds $1/2$.

The construction has polynomial running time and output length by Section 2. It requires no value queries when the cardinality-dependence hypothesis is promised. $\square$

Since $e^{-2/e}<1/2$, this settles the requested approximation guarantee under the additional cardinality-dependence hypothesis.

4. Proportional additive valuations: no welfare loss in existence

Theorem. Suppose there are nonnegative weights $w_g$ and positive constants $c_t$ such that
$$ v_t(S)=c_tW(S), \qquad W(S)=\sum_{g\in S}w_g. $$
Then an exact-supply supportwise EF1 allocation attaining the maximum feasible $\operatorname{NSW}_\infty$ exists. It has a polynomial-size rational representation.

A fairness construction for a common additive ranking

First, the construction of Section 2 is EF1 for any collection of additive valuations sharing the chosen ranking of the goods.

Order goods so that an evaluating type’s weights satisfy
$$ w_1\geq w_2\geq\cdots\geq w_m\geq0. $$
Let $S,R$ be supported bundles, with $R\neq\varnothing$, and let $g_r$ be the first good of $R$ in this order.

For a prefix ending before $r$, the bundle $R\setminus\{g_r\}$ has prefix count zero. For a prefix ending at or after $r$, its prefix count is $N_j(R)-1$, which is at most $N_j(S)$ by the prefix-count lemma. Thus
$$ N_j(S)\geq N_j(R\setminus\{g_r\}) \qquad(1\leq j\leq m). $$

Set $w_{m+1}=0$. The identity
$$ W(S)=\sum_{j=1}^m(w_j-w_{j+1})N_j(S) $$
and nonnegativity of all coefficients give
$$ W(S)\geq W(R\setminus\{g_r\}). $$
This proves EF1. In particular, it supplies an exact-supply fair allocation for proportional additive valuations even when no positive-welfare feasible allocation exists.

Reduction of welfare to an aggregate distribution

For any feasible allocation, put
$$ q_S=\sum_tx_{t,S}. $$
Then $q$ is a distribution with marginals $b_g$. Conversely, every such distribution induces a feasible allocation through $x_{t,S}=\mu_tq_S$.

For a positive-welfare allocation,
$$ \log\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x) = \sum_t\mu_t\log c_t + \sum_Sq_S\log W(S). $$
Thus maximizing welfare is equivalent to maximizing the aggregate objective
$$ \sum_Sq_S\log W(S). $$

If no positive-welfare feasible allocation exists, every feasible allocation has welfare zero, and the preceding fairness construction proves the theorem. Otherwise, the polytope
$$ \mathcal Q= \left\{ q\geq0: \sum_Sq_S=1,\ \sum_{S:g\in S}q_S=b_g,\ q_S=0\text{ whenever }W(S)=0 \right\} $$
is nonempty and compact. Its objective is a finite linear function, so it has a maximizing vertex $q$.

An improving exchange rules out every EF1 violation

Suppose supported bundles $S,R$ violate EF1 under $W$. Then
$$ W(S)<W(R\setminus\{g\}) \qquad\text{for every }g\in R. $$
Write
$$ a=W(S)>0, \qquad b=W(R)>0. $$
Additivity gives
$$ b-a>\max_{g\in R}w_g. $$

There is a good $h\in R\setminus S$ with $w_h>0$: otherwise,
$$ W(R)-W(S) =W(R\setminus S)-W(S\setminus R)\leq0, $$
contradicting the preceding strict inequality. Put $c=w_h$. Then
$$ c>0, \qquad b-a>c. $$

Replace an equal mass
$$ \delta=\min\{q_S,q_R\}>0 $$
of the bundles $S,R$ by the bundles
$$ S\cup\{h\}, \qquad R\setminus\{h\}. $$
Total mass and every good marginal are preserved, because $h$ is transferred between the two bundles and all other memberships are unchanged.

The new bundle values are $a+c$ and $b-c$, both positive. Moreover,
$$ (a+c)(b-c)-ab =c(b-a-c)>0. $$
Therefore the logarithmic objective strictly increases by
$$ \delta\log\frac{(a+c)(b-c)}{ab}>0. $$
This contradicts maximality of $q$.

Hence its support is EF1 under $W$. Multiplication by the positive constant $c_t$ preserves every comparison, so
$$ x_{t,S}=\mu_tq_S $$
is supportwise EF1 for all types and attains maximum feasible welfare.

Rational representation

A vertex of $\mathcal Q$ has at most $m+1$ positive coordinates. Indeed, with more positive coordinates, their columns in the total-mass and marginal constraint matrix would be linearly dependent. A sufficiently small perturbation in either direction along that dependence would preserve feasibility, contradicting extremality.

On its positive coordinates, the vertex is determined by an invertible square submatrix of the constraint matrix. This submatrix has entries in $\{0,1\}$ and dimension at most $m+1$. If $D$ is a common denominator for the supplies, Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s determinant bound give coordinate encoding lengths
$$ O\bigl(L+m\log(m+1)\bigr). $$
Multiplication by the input masses preserves polynomial encoding length. Thus the induced allocation has at most $\tau(m+1)$ positive rational entries and polynomial total representation length. $\square$

5. What remains unresolved

The cardinality-dependent result proves exact supply use, polynomial computation, and the requested welfare guarantee under a substantive additional hypothesis.

The proportional additive result proves exact-supply existence with no welfare loss. Its optimization ranges over exponentially many bundles, so the existence proof alone supplies no polynomial-time algorithm.

Neither argument applies to arbitrary monotone subadditive valuations. In particular, neither establishes a general exact-supply completion theorem, a counterexample to the requested guarantee, or a computational lower bound for that guarantee.
