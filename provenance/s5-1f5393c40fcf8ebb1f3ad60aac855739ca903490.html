Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), complete type data \((e_t,v_t)\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), can an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) output a compactly represented mass allocation \(x_{t,b}\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^m\), satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_gx_{t,b}=q_g\), where every allocated unit of category \(g\) goes to a type maximizing \(v_t(g)/e_t\), and \(f(\rho(x))W(x)\ge W^*\)? Here \(W(x)=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}\log(e_t+\sum_g b_gv_t(g))\), \(W^*\) is its maximum over feasible allocations, \(\rho(x)=\max_{t,b:x_{t,b}>0}\sum_g b_gv_t(g)/e_t\), \(f(r)=(r+r^3/3)/\ln(1+r)\) with \(f(0)=1\), and the input satisfies \(\sum_t\mu_t\log e_t\ge 0\).

For finite agents and indivisible goods, decentralized allocations are computable in polynomial time and satisfy \(f(R)W(A)\geq W(A^*)\), where \(R=\max_i v_i(G)/e_i\), provided \(\sum_i\ln e_i\geq0\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07821v2)

Result

Yes. There is a polynomial-time algorithm producing at most \(m+\tau\) positive entries. In fact, every compact feasible decentralized allocation satisfies the stronger bound

\(\displaystyle \frac{\rho(x)}{\ln(1+\rho(x))}\,W(x)\geq W^*,\)

with the factor interpreted as \(1\) when \(\rho(x)=0\).

We prove this bound and then give the compact construction.

1. A universal upper bound on welfare

Define the normalized valuations, baseline welfare, and maximal normalized total value by

\(\displaystyle a_{t,g}=\frac{v_t(g)}{e_t},\qquad C=\sum_t\mu_t\ln e_t\geq0,\qquad D=\sum_gq_g\max_t a_{t,g}.\)

For an integral bundle \(b\), write \(r_t(b)=\sum_gb_ga_{t,g}\). For any feasible allocation \(y\), nonnegativity allows us to interchange sums, giving

\(\displaystyle S(y):=\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}r_t(b)=\sum_g\sum_ta_{t,g}\sum_bb_gy_{t,b}\leq\sum_g\left(\max_ta_{t,g}\right)q_g=D.\)

Since \(0\leq\ln(1+r)\leq r\) for \(r\geq0\),

\(\displaystyle G(y):=\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}\ln(1+r_t(b))\)

is finite and satisfies \(0\leq G(y)\leq D\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \ln\!\left(e_t+\sum_gb_gv_t(g)\right)=\ln e_t+\ln(1+r_t(b)).\)

This identity can be summed even for infinite-support allocations: the absolute sum is bounded by \(\sum_t\mu_t|\ln e_t|+D\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle W(y)=C+G(y)\leq C+D.\)

Taking the supremum over all feasible allocations proves

\(\displaystyle W^*\leq C+D.\)

No recipient restriction was imposed on \(y\).

2. Every compact decentralized allocation satisfies the guarantee

Let \(x\) be compact, feasible, and decentralized. Whenever \(x_{t,b}>0\) and \(b_g>0\), decentralization gives \(a_{t,g}=\max_s a_{s,g}\). Thus every inequality in the calculation of \(S(x)\) becomes an equality:

\(\displaystyle S(x)=D.\)

Put \(\rho=\rho(x)\). Suppose first that \(\rho>0\). Concavity of \(r\mapsto\ln(1+r)\), applied to the chord joining \(0\) and \(\rho\), gives

\(\displaystyle \ln(1+r)\geq\frac{r}{\rho}\ln(1+\rho)\qquad(0\leq r\leq\rho).\)

Every bundle in the support of \(x\) meets this hypothesis. Summing yields

\(\displaystyle G(x)\geq\frac{\ln(1+\rho)}{\rho}S(x)=\frac{\ln(1+\rho)}{\rho}D.\)

Define \(h(\rho)=\rho/\ln(1+\rho)\). The inequality \(\ln(1+\rho)\leq\rho\) implies \(h(\rho)\geq1\). Using \(C\geq0\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle h(\rho)W(x)=h(\rho)C+h(\rho)G(x)\geq C+D\geq W^*.\)

Also, \(W(x)=C+G(x)\geq0\), and

\(\displaystyle f(\rho)=\left(1+\frac{\rho^2}{3}\right)h(\rho)\geq h(\rho).\)

Therefore \(f(\rho)W(x)\geq W^*\).

If \(\rho=0\), then \(S(x)=0\), so \(D=0\). Section 1 now gives \(G(y)=0\) for every feasible \(y\). Hence \(W(x)=W^*=C\), and the required inequality holds with \(f(0)=1\).

This proves the guarantee for every compact feasible decentralized allocation, including allocations whose actual \(\rho(x)\) is small.

3. A compact rational construction

For each category \(g\) with \(q_g>0\), choose a type

\(\displaystyle t(g)\in\arg\max_t\frac{v_t(g)}{e_t}.\)

Choose \(N_g\) to be the smallest power of two satisfying

\(\displaystyle N_g\geq\max\left\{1,\frac{mq_g}{\mu_{t(g)}}\right\},\)

and set \(c_g=q_g/N_g\). Let \(b^{(g)}\) be the integral bundle having coordinate \(N_g\) in category \(g\) and zero in every other category.

Output the entry \(x_{t(g),b^{(g)}}=c_g\). For each type \(t\), define

\(\displaystyle z_t=\mu_t-\sum_{g:t(g)=t}c_g.\)

If \(z_t>0\), also output \(x_{t,0}=z_t\). All other entries are zero.

Nonnegativity and type masses. By the choice of \(N_g\),

\(\displaystyle c_g\leq\frac{\mu_{t(g)}}m.\)

At most \(m\) categories choose a given type, so their combined mass is at most \(\mu_t\). Therefore \(z_t\geq0\), and the total output mass of type \(t\) is exactly \(\mu_t\).

Supplies. Only \(b^{(g)}\) contributes to category \(g\), and its contribution is

\(\displaystyle c_gN_g=q_g.\)

Categories with zero supply occur in no output bundle. Thus every supply constraint holds.

Decentralization. Each nonempty output bundle contains only its designated category \(g\) and is assigned to the chosen maximizer \(t(g)\). Empty bundles impose no recipient condition.

The construction therefore produces a feasible decentralized allocation with at most \(m+\tau\) positive entries.

4. Polynomial running time and encoding length

The maximizing types can be found by exact rational comparisons in polynomial time.

Each ratio \(mq_g/\mu_{t(g)}\) has bit length \(O(L+\log m)\). Its smallest dominating power of two can be found using integer bit lengths and exact comparisons. In particular,

\(\displaystyle N_g\leq2\max\left\{1,\frac{mq_g}{\mu_{t(g)}}\right\},\)

so every bundle coordinate has \(O(L+\log m)\) bits.

Each \(c_g=q_g/N_g\) has polynomial bit length. Each remainder \(z_t\) is obtained by subtracting at most \(m\) such rationals from \(\mu_t\); using a common denominator gives polynomial bit length and polynomial arithmetic cost. If the input rationals are dyadic, all output masses remain dyadic because division is only by powers of two.

There are at most \(m+\tau\) entries, each with \(m\) binary-encoded coordinates. Thus both the running time and total output length are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The algorithm does not need to evaluate any logarithms.

The proof is self-contained; [Theorem 1 of the supplied paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07821v2) provides the finite-agent comparison.
