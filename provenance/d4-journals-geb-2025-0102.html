The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type set \(T\), good-class set \(G\), rational population distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), valuations \(v_t(g)\), and divisible-good sets \(D_t\), find a finite-support allocation \(x_{t,b}\) of type mass to bundles \(b\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\), with \(b_g\in[0,1]\) for \(g\in D_t\) and \(b_g\in\{0,1\}\) for \(g\notin D_t\). Require non-wastefulness and the paper's EF1M comparison for every pair of supported bundles, and find such an allocation in time polynomial in \(|T|\), \(|G|\), and the input bit length, independently of population denominators.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq 1\) good classes \(G\), with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\in(0,1]\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type has binary-rational values \(v_t(g)\geq 0\) and a divisible-good set \(D_t\subseteq\{g:v_t(g)>0\}\). Every class is positively valued by some supported type. A bundle \(b\) has \(b_g\in[0,1]\) for \(g\in D_t\) and \(b_g\in\{0,1\}\) otherwise when assigned to type \(t\).
For any bundle \(c\), its value to type \(t\) is \(u_t(c)=\sum_{g\in D_t}v_t(g)c_g+\sum_{g\notin D_t:c_g=1}v_t(g)\). Thus a proper fraction of an indivisible good has zero value to that type, even in comparisons.
An allocation is a finite list of rational bundles and positive rational masses \(x_{t,b}\), satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\). Non-wastefulness requires \(b_g>0\Rightarrow v_t(g)>0\) for every supported \((t,b)\).
For every ordered pair of supported assignments \((t,b),(s,c)\), including pairs of the same type, define \(I_t(c)=\{g\notin D_t:c_g=1,\ v_t(g)>0\}\). EF1M requires \(u_t(b)\geq u_t(c)\) if \(I_t(c)=\varnothing\); otherwise it requires \(u_t(b)\geq u_t(c)-v_t(g)\) for some \(g\in I_t(c)\).

Is constructing such an allocation, or correctly reporting infeasibility, solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), independently of population denominators except through \(L\), or is this problem NP-hard?

For finite lists of agents with subjective divisibility, complete EF1M and non-wasteful allocations always exist and are polynomial-time computable ([Bei, Liu, and Lu](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00976v2#S4.SS2)). No result settling the exact distribution-over-types problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational valuations, positive value for every divisible good, and positive value to some supported type for every good class, carrying over the discrete model's assumptions.
  • Restricted retained good classes to positive per-capita supplies at most one, consistent with the bundle bounds and total population mass one; zero-supply classes can be omitted.
  • Resolved the EF1M quantifier as existence of one removable good, following the explanatory text and proof, and required that the comparing type regard that positively valued good as indivisible and that the compared bundle contain it whole.
  • Specified subjective utility when evaluating another type's fractional bundle, rather than using the recipient's divisibility.
  • Applied comparisons to all ordered pairs of positive-mass supported assignments, including assignments within one type.
  • Specified rational coordinates and masses for an explicitly encoded finite-support output.
  • Allowed reporting infeasibility because arbitrary per-capita supplies do not inherit the finite-agent existence guarantee.

A new partial result

The general polynomial-time-versus-NP-hardness question remains unresolved. The following restriction admits a stronger result than those established in the notes.

Theorem. Suppose that at most one class is divisible for any supported type:
$$ \left|\bigcup_{t\in T}D_t\right|\leq 1. $$
Then:

  • Given the types and whole-good patterns of a proposed support, completion to a valid allocation is decidable in polynomial time.
  • Every feasible instance has an allocation with at most $m+\tau$ assignments and total rational encoding length polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.
  • Feasibility belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.
  • Allowing real masses and bundle coordinates does not change feasibility.
  • There is an exact algorithm, independent of population denominators except through $L$, with running time bounded by

$$ (m+\tau)(\tau 2^m)^{m+\tau}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

This theorem permits arbitrarily many indivisible classes and arbitrary subjective divisibility of the remaining class. It establishes neither polynomial-time solvability nor NP-hardness of the unrestricted problem.

1. Support compression

We first prove the support bound, since it is needed for the complexity conclusions.

Lemma. Every feasible allocation has a feasible allocation with at most $m+\tau$ assignments. This holds both for rational allocations and for the variant allowing real masses and bundle coordinates.

Proof. Index the supported assignments by $i=1,\ldots,n$. Let their types, bundles, and masses be $t_i,b^i,x_i$. Define
$$ a^i=(e_{t_i},b^i)\in\mathbb{R}^{\tau+m}, $$
where $e_t$ is the coordinate vector corresponding to type $t$. The mass and supply equations become
$$ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i a^i=(\mu,q). $$

If $n>m+\tau$, choose a nonzero linear dependence
$$ \sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i a^i=0. $$
Summing the first $\tau$ coordinates gives $\sum_i\lambda_i=0$. Thus some $\lambda_i$ are positive and some are negative.

Set
$$ \varepsilon=\min_{\lambda_i>0}\frac{x_i}{\lambda_i}, \qquad x'_i=x_i-\varepsilon\lambda_i. $$
Every $x'_i$ is nonnegative, at least one is zero, and all mass and supply equations remain satisfied. Delete the assignments of zero mass.

The bundles have not changed. Every remaining ordered pair was present before deletion, so admissibility, non-wastefulness, and EF1M remain satisfied. Repeating gives the bound.

For a rational allocation, the columns $a^i$ are rational, so the dependence can be chosen rational. The modified masses then remain rational. $\square$

2. Fixing the discrete patterns

Assume now that
$$ D_t\subseteq\{h\}\qquad\text{for every }t\in T $$
for some class $h$. If every $D_t$ is empty, choose any $h\in G$, which is possible because $m\geq1$.

Consider a proposed support of $n$ assignments. Its discrete pattern specifies, for each assignment $i$:

  • its type $t_i$;
  • a coordinate $a_{ig}\in\{0,1\}$ for every $g\neq h$;
  • a flag $z_i\in\{0,1\}$ specifying whether its $h$ coordinate is whole.

Write $y_i=b^i_h$. The pattern imposes:

  • if $z_i=1$, then $y_i=1$;
  • if $z_i=0$ and $h\notin D_{t_i}$, then $y_i=0$;
  • if $z_i=0$ and $h\in D_{t_i}$, then $0\leq y_i<1$.

Let
$$ F=\{i:z_i=0,\ h\in D_{t_i}\}. $$

Reject the pattern if some $a_{ig}=1$ has $v_{t_i}(g)=0$, or if some $z_i=1$ has $v_{t_i}(h)=0$. After these checks, non-wastefulness holds for every bundle consistent with the pattern: variable positive $h$ coordinates occur only for types whose divisible-good set contains $h$, and those types value $h$ positively.

For a comparing type $t$, define
$$ A_{tj}=\sum_{g\neq h}v_t(g)a_{jg}. $$
For a bundle respecting the pattern,
$$ u_t(b^j)=A_{tj}+ \begin{cases} v_t(h)y_j,&h\in D_t,\\ v_t(h)z_j,&h\notin D_t. \end{cases} $$
The second branch uses the task’s convention that a proper fraction of an indivisible class has zero comparison value.

Define the fixed removal allowance
$$ r_{tj}= \max\left( \{v_t(g):g\neq h,\ a_{jg}=1,\ v_t(g)>0\} \cup \{v_t(h):h\notin D_t,\ z_j=1,\ v_t(h)>0\} \cup\{0\} \right). $$

Lemma. For a pattern-consistent bundle, EF1M for the ordered pair $(t_i,b^i),(t_j,b^j)$ is equivalent to
$$ u_{t_i}(b^i)\geq u_{t_i}(b^j)-r_{t_i j}. $$

Proof. If $I_{t_i}(b^j)$ is empty, then $r_{t_i j}=0$, and this is the required ordinary non-envy inequality.

Otherwise, $r_{t_i j}$ is the largest value of an allowed removal. Removing such a good gives the displayed inequality. Conversely, an inequality satisfied after some allowed removal is also satisfied after removing a good of maximum allowed value. $\square$

There are consequently two kinds of constraints.

If $h\notin D_{t_i}$, EF1M is the fixed rational inequality
$$ A_{t_i i}+v_{t_i}(h)z_i \geq A_{t_i j}+v_{t_i}(h)z_j-r_{t_i j}. $$
Reject the pattern if any such inequality fails.

If $h\in D_{t_i}$, then $v_{t_i}(h)>0$, and EF1M becomes the difference inequality
$$ y_i-y_j\geq \frac{A_{t_i j}-A_{t_i i}-r_{t_i j}}{v_{t_i}(h)}. $$

These constraints include every ordered pair, including assignments of the same type.

Let $P\subseteq[0,1]^n$ be the closed polytope defined by these difference inequalities and the fixed-coordinate equations above, temporarily replacing $y_i<1$ by $y_i\leq1$ for $i\in F$. Define
$$ P^\circ=\{y\in P:y_i<1\text{ for every }i\in F\}. $$

The actual admissible, non-wasteful, EF1M completions of the pattern are exactly $P^\circ$. Points in $P\setminus P^\circ$ are auxiliary boundary points: their comparison formulas still use the fixed pattern flags, so they are used only to compute bounds.

3. Coordinatewise least and greatest completions

The difference-inequality structure gives a useful property.

Lemma. If $P$ is nonempty, it has a coordinatewise least point $y^-$ and a coordinatewise greatest point $y^+$. Both are computable in polynomial time and have polynomial rational encoding length.

Proof. First, $P$ is closed under coordinatewise minimum and maximum.

For minimum, suppose $a,b\in P$, and write $c_i=\min(a_i,b_i)$. Consider a constraint
$$ y_i\geq y_j+d. $$
If $c_i=a_i$, then
$$ c_i=a_i\geq a_j+d\geq c_j+d. $$
The case $c_i=b_i$ is identical.

For maximum, write $e_i=\max(a_i,b_i)$. If $e_j=a_j$, then
$$ e_i\geq a_i\geq a_j+d=e_j+d. $$
Again, the other case is identical. Bounds and fixed-coordinate equations are also preserved.

Since $P$ is compact, each coordinate attains its minimum. Choose a point attaining the minimum of coordinate $i$, for every $i$, and take their coordinatewise minimum. Closure under minimum shows that the resulting point belongs to $P$ and simultaneously attains every coordinate minimum. This is $y^-$. Coordinatewise maximum gives $y^+$.

Thus
$$ y^-\leq y\leq y^+\qquad\text{coordinatewise for every }y\in P. $$
The point $y^-$ is the unique minimizer of $\sum_i y_i$ over $P$: any different feasible point has a strictly larger coordinate and no smaller coordinate. Similarly, $y^+$ uniquely maximizes that sum.

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a rational LP of polynomial encoding length can be solved exactly in polynomial time, with a polynomial-size rational optimizer when its optimum is attained. Here the variables are bounded, the number of constraints is polynomial, and all coefficients have polynomial encoding length. Therefore the two optimizers have the asserted complexity and encoding bounds. $\square$

It follows that
$$ P^\circ\neq\varnothing \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y^-_i<1\text{ for every }i\in F. $$
Indeed, $y^-$ itself proves sufficiency. For necessity, if $y^-_i=1$, every point of $P$ has coordinate $i$ equal to $1$.

Henceforth suppose that $P^\circ$ is nonempty.

For a positive mass vector $x$, define
$$ a(x)=\sum_i x_i y^-_i, \qquad b(x)=\sum_i x_i y^+_i. $$

Lemma. The supplies of class $h$ attainable with this fixed positive mass vector are exactly
$$ \left\{\sum_i x_i y_i:y\in P^\circ\right\} = \begin{cases} [a(x),b(x)],&y^+\in P^\circ,\\ [a(x),b(x)),&y^+\notin P^\circ. \end{cases} $$

Proof. Coordinatewise extremality bounds every attainable supply between $a(x)$ and $b(x)$. The lower endpoint is attained at $y^-\in P^\circ$.

If $y^+\in P^\circ$, the upper endpoint is attained. Convex interpolation between $y^-$ and $y^+$ attains every intermediate value.

If $y^+\notin P^\circ$, some $k\in F$ satisfies $y^+_k=1$. Every $y\in P^\circ$ has $y_k<1$, and $x_k>0$. Consequently,
$$ \sum_i x_i y_i<b(x). $$
For every $0\leq\lambda<1$, however,
$$ y(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)y^-+\lambda y^+ $$
belongs to $P^\circ$. It belongs to $P$ by convexity, and each proper coordinate remains strictly below $1$ because the corresponding coordinate of $y^-$ is strictly below $1$.

Moreover,
$$ \sum_i x_i y_i(\lambda) =(1-\lambda)a(x)+\lambda b(x). $$
This attains every value in the half-open interval. In this case $a(x)<b(x)$, because $y^-_k<y^+_k$ and all masses are positive. $\square$

4. Polynomial-time completion of a pattern

The mass constraints for the fixed pattern, excluding class $h$, are
$$ \sum_{i:t_i=t}x_i=\mu_t \qquad(t\in T), $$
$$ \sum_i x_i a_{ig}=q_g \qquad(g\neq h), $$
$$ x_i>0 \qquad(i=1,\ldots,n). $$

The preceding interval characterization eliminates the remaining bilinear supply equation.

Proposition. A discrete pattern has a valid completion if and only if all previous pattern checks pass, $P^\circ$ is nonempty, and the following linear system is feasible:

  • the displayed type-mass and non-$h$ supply equations;
  • $x_i>0$ for every $i$;
  • $a(x)\leq q_h$;
  • $q_h\leq b(x)$ if $y^+\in P^\circ$, and $q_h<b(x)$ otherwise.

Proof. Any completion has $y\in P^\circ$, so the interval lemma proves necessity.

Conversely, take a mass vector satisfying this system. If $a(x)=b(x)$, its inequalities force $q_h=a(x)$, and use $y=y^-$.

Otherwise, define
$$ \lambda=\frac{q_h-a(x)}{b(x)-a(x)}, \qquad y=(1-\lambda)y^-+\lambda y^+. $$
The inequalities give $0\leq\lambda\leq1$. If $y^+\notin P^\circ$, the strict upper inequality gives $\lambda<1$. Thus the interval lemma gives $y\in P^\circ$, and
$$ \sum_i x_i y_i=q_h. $$

Use these $y_i$ as the $h$ coordinates and the fixed $a_{ig}$ as all other coordinates. Membership in $P^\circ$ establishes admissibility, non-wastefulness, and every required EF1M comparison. The mass and supply equations hold by construction. $\square$

Strict feasibility in this system is polynomial-time decidable. Introduce a common margin $\varepsilon$ and impose
$$ 0\leq\varepsilon\leq1, \qquad x_i\geq\varepsilon\quad\text{for every }i. $$
When the upper supply inequality is strict, replace it by
$$ b(x)\geq q_h+\varepsilon. $$
Keep all other equations and weak inequalities, and maximize $\varepsilon$.

The original strict system is feasible exactly when this LP has a positive optimum. A strictly feasible point has a positive common margin because there are finitely many strict inequalities. Conversely, a positive margin satisfies all of them.

This LP is bounded: summing the type-mass equations gives $\sum_i x_i=1$, so its nonnegative masses lie in $[0,1]$. Its coefficients, including $y^-$ and $y^+$, have polynomial encoding length. Exact rational LP therefore supplies polynomial-size rational masses whenever a completion exists.

Finally, $a(x)$, $b(x)$, $\lambda$, and the interpolated coordinates are computed using polynomially many rational arithmetic operations on polynomial-size numbers. Their encoding lengths are polynomial as well. This proves both polynomial-time completion and polynomial-size rational output for a fixed pattern.

5. Complexity consequences

Let
$$ N=m+\tau. $$

A feasible real allocation can be compressed to at most $N$ assignments by Section 1. Its discrete pattern passes the completion criterion. Section 4 then constructs a rational allocation for that same pattern, with polynomial encoding length.

Thus, under the one-divisible-class hypothesis:

  • real feasibility and rational feasibility coincide;
  • every feasible instance has a polynomial-size rational allocation with at most $N$ assignments.

For $\mathrm{NP}$ membership, a certificate can specify only the discrete pattern of at most $N$ assignments. Its length is
$$ O\bigl(N(m+\log\tau)\bigr). $$
The completion algorithm verifies it in polynomial time. Equivalently, its resulting polynomial-size rational allocation can be checked directly.

For an exact deterministic algorithm, enumerate all ordered patterns of lengths $1,\ldots,N$. Each assignment has at most $\tau 2^m$ patterns: one type choice and $m$ binary flags. Therefore the number of candidates is at most
$$ N(\tau 2^m)^N. $$
Complete each candidate using the polynomial-time procedure. If all candidates fail, support compression proves infeasibility. This establishes the claimed running-time bound without expanding any population denominator.

6. Remaining open points

The theorem resolves the rational encoding question for instances satisfying
$$ \left|\bigcup_t D_t\right|\leq1. $$
It also gives a polynomial-time completion algorithm after their discrete patterns are fixed.

For several divisible classes, a supported assignment has several fractional coordinates. Its utility comparisons can involve sums of those coordinates, and the closed completion set need not have coordinatewise least and greatest points. There are also several bilinear supply equations to satisfy simultaneously. The scalar interval argument proved here therefore does not establish a completion algorithm or a polynomial rational encoding bound in that setting.

Even within the one-divisible-class restriction, the pattern enumeration remains exponential. No polynomial-time algorithm or NP-hardness reduction is established here.
