$$ f_t(s')-f_t(s)\ge s'-s \qquad\text{whenever }s'\ge s. $$

$$ \Delta=\max_t\left(\max_a v_{ta}-\min_a v_{ta}\right). $$

Lemma 1: uniform rent bound

Every envy-free allocation satisfies
$$ m-\Delta\le r_a\le m+\Delta \qquad\text{for every }a. $$

Proof. Choose categories $h,\ell$ of maximum and minimum rent. Because $q_h>0$, the column margin implies $x_{th}>0$ for some type $t$. Envy-freeness gives
$$ f_t(r_h)-f_t(r_\ell) \le v_{th}-v_{t\ell} \le\Delta. $$
The preceding monotonicity inequality then yields
$$ r_h-r_\ell\le\Delta. $$

$$ r_\ell\le m\le r_h. $$
The asserted bounds follow. $\square$

Existence

I use the following existence consequence of Velez’s discrete theorem: for finitely many agents and equally many rooms, utilities of the prescribed form with nonnegative budgets and coefficients in a fixed finite set containing zero admit a maxmin envy-free allocation at every real total rent; negative room rents are allowed. These are precisely the relevant hypotheses of [Velez, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.02966).

Aggregating the assignments and dividing their counts by $N$ therefore gives an envy-free allocation in the mass model, with the required margins and per-capita rent $m$.

This proves existence, but not a polynomial algorithm: $N$ can be exponential in $L$.

These finitely many programs define compact rational polytopes. They are compact because rents and utilities are bounded and $0\le x_{ta}\le1$. At least one is nonempty by existence. Consequently, the global optimum is attained.

Each program has polynomially many variables and constraints with polynomial-size rational coefficients. 2. An exact characterization in type-utility space

The new algorithm uses $\tau$ utility coordinates instead of $K$ rent coordinates.

The inverse of $f_t$ is
$$ g_t(s)= \min\left\{ s,\frac{s+\rho_tb_t}{1+\rho_t} \right\}. $$
To verify the formula, if $s\le b_t$, the second expression is at least $s$, and $f_t(s)=s$. If $s\ge b_t$, the second expression is at most $s$, belongs to $[b_t,\infty)$, and applying the above-budget formula for $f_t$ returns $s$. This also covers $\rho_t=0$.

For a proposed utility vector $U\in\mathbb R^\tau$, define
$$ \phi_{ta}(U)=g_t(v_{ta}-U_t), $$
and
$$ R_a(U)=\max_t\phi_{ta}(U). $$
Define the graph
$$ E(U)=\{(t,a):\phi_{ta}(U)=R_a(U)\}. $$

Lemma 2: utility-envelope characterization

A vector $U$ is the vector of maximizing type utilities of some envy-free allocation with total rent $m$ if and only if:

  • $E(U)$ supports a nonnegative transportation flow with row margins $\mu_t$ and column margins $q_a$;

2.
$$ \sum_aq_aR_a(U)=m. $$

When these conditions hold, the associated rents are necessarily
$$ r_a=R_a(U). $$

Proof. First suppose $(r,x)$ is envy-free and
$$ U_t=\max_a u_t(r_a,a). $$
For every pair,
$$ v_{ta}-f_t(r_a)\le U_t. $$
Since $f_t$ is strictly increasing and has inverse $g_t$, this is equivalent to
$$ r_a\ge g_t(v_{ta}-U_t)=\phi_{ta}(U). $$
Hence $r_a\ge R_a(U)$.

Because $q_a>0$, some type $t$ satisfies $x_{ta}>0$. Envy-freeness makes the preceding utility inequality an equality on that edge, so
$$ r_a=\phi_{ta}(U)\le R_a(U). $$
Therefore $r_a=R_a(U)$. Every positive assignment edge belongs to $E(U)$, proving both conditions.

Conversely, suppose the two conditions hold, and let $x$ be a supported transportation flow. Set $r_a=R_a(U)$. For every pair,
$$ r_a\ge\phi_{ta}(U), $$
so
$$ u_t(r_a,a)\le U_t. $$
On every edge in $E(U)$, equality holds. Because $\mu_t>0$, the flow uses at least one such edge in every row. Thus
$$ U_t=\max_a u_t(r_a,a), $$
and every positive assignment edge is maximizing. The allocation is envy-free and has the required rent total. $\square$

The positive supply assumption is essential to the necessity proof: it forces every category rent to meet the envelope.

3. Polynomial-time exact optimization for fixed support size

Theorem 3

For every fixed $\tau$, exact maxmin envy-free optimization is polynomial-time solvable in $K,L$.

The theorem remains valid if the coefficients $\rho_t$ are arbitrary binary rational nonnegative input values.

Proof. Each function $\phi_{ta}$ is the minimum of two affine functions of $U$:
$$ \ell_{ta}^{0}(U)=v_{ta}-U_t, $$
$$ \ell_{ta}^{1}(U)= \frac{v_{ta}+\rho_tb_t-U_t}{1+\rho_t}. $$

Construct an arrangement in $\mathbb R^\tau$ containing the following hyperplanes:

  • For every $(t,a)$, the branch boundary

$$ U_t=v_{ta}-b_t. $$

  • For every category $a$, distinct types $t,s$, and $\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\}$, the comparison boundary

$$ \ell_{ta}^{\alpha}(U)=\ell_{sa}^{\beta}(U). $$

There are
$$ H=O(K\tau^2) $$
hyperplanes, all with polynomial-size rational coefficients.

Step 1: quantities constant on a face

Let $F$ be a nonempty arrangement face, understood through its relative interior. On $F$:

  • each $\phi_{ta}$ has a fixed affine formula;
  • every comparison $\phi_{ta}-\phi_{sa}$ has a fixed sign;
  • consequently, the maximizing set

$$ T_a(F)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_t\phi_{ta}(U) $$
is independent of the choice of $U\in F$.

This follows directly from the branch and comparison hyperplanes included in the arrangement. At a branch boundary, either formula is valid because the two coincide.

Choose $t_a\in T_a(F)$ and let $R_a^F$ be the affine formula for $\phi_{t_a a}$ on $F$. Define
$$ E_F=\{(t,a):t\in T_a(F)\}. $$

By continuity, on the closure $\overline F$,
$$ R_a^F(U)=R_a(U), $$
and
$$ E_F\subseteq E(U). $$
Indeed, take a sequence in $F$ converging to a point of $\overline F$ and pass to the limit in the affine identities and maximizing inequalities.

Step 2: a flow test and one linear program per face

Test whether $E_F$ supports a transportation flow with the prescribed margins. This is a rational max-flow problem. If it does not, discard the face. Otherwise, retain one such flow $x^F$.

Solve the linear program
$$ \max z $$
subject to
$$ U\in\overline F, $$
$$ \sum_aq_aR_a^F(U)=m, $$
$$ z\le U_t\qquad\text{for every }t, $$
and
$$ \underline U\le U_t,z\le\overline U. $$

The closure is described by the face’s equalities and weak sign inequalities, so all constraints are linear and rational.

Every feasible solution gives an original envy-free allocation. The retained flow is supported on $E_F\subseteq E(U)$, and $R_a^F(U)=R_a(U)$; Lemma 2 applies.

Conversely, take any original envy-free allocation and its maximizing utility vector $U$. It lies in the relative interior of some arrangement face $F$. Its assignment proves flow feasibility on $E_F=E(U)$, and its utility vector satisfies that face’s program. Its maxmin value is therefore included among the values considered.

Taking the best feasible face program gives the exact global optimum.

Step 3: polynomial enumeration when $\tau$ is fixed

An arrangement of $H$ hyperplanes in fixed dimension $\tau$ has
$$ O_\tau\bigl((H+1)^\tau\bigr) $$
faces of all dimensions.

Here is a counting justification that also permits degeneracies. A codimension-$j$ face has an affine hull determined by $j$ independent arrangement hyperplanes. There are at most $\binom Hj$ choices. Within such an affine subspace, the number of full-dimensional regions is at most
$$ \sum_{i=0}^{\tau-j}\binom Hi. $$
This region bound follows by inserting hyperplanes one at a time: the number of new regions created by one hyperplane is bounded by the number of regions induced on it, giving induction on dimension and the number of hyperplanes. Summing over $j$ gives the asserted bound. Multiple representations of an affine hull only overcount.

Faces can be enumerated incrementally by their sign patterns. For each newly inserted hyperplane, test all three extensions of each retained pattern. To test strict inequalities, replace each signed strict inequality by the same weak inequality with a common margin $\varepsilon$, impose
$$ 0\le\varepsilon\le1, $$
and maximize $\varepsilon$. Retain the pattern exactly when its equality system is feasible and the maximum margin is positive. A point satisfying finitely many strict inequalities has a positive common margin, so this test is exact.

Each intermediate arrangement also has polynomially many faces for fixed $\tau$. Thus enumeration uses polynomially many rational linear programs. The flow tests and face optimization programs likewise have polynomial input size.

All feasible face programs are compact because of the utility bounds. Rational linear programming therefore computes their exact attained optima in polynomial time. Recover rents as $R_a^F(U)$ and use the retained rational flow $x^F$. $\square$

4. Exponentially many slope-weight values within one budget regime

The discrete progress argument uses matching weights formed from logarithms of local utility slopes. The corresponding mass quantity can be defined without expanding the population.

For an envy-free rent vector $r$, let
$$ M_t(r)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_a u_t(r_a,a), $$
and define the slope for a small rent decrease by
$$ \lambda_{ta}(r)= \begin{cases} 1+\rho_t,&r_a>b_t,\\ 1,&r_a\le b_t. \end{cases} $$
Define
$$ W(r)= \max_x\sum_{t,a}x_{ta}\log\lambda_{ta}(r), $$
where $x$ has the prescribed margins and is supported on
$$ \{(t,a):a\in M_t(r)\}. $$
Multiplying this quantity by a replication denominator gives the corresponding discrete matching weight. The use of logarithmic slope weights is the criterion in [Velez, Sections 3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.02966).
