The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), item categories \(J\) with rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), utilities \(u_{tj}\), and effectively represented nonnegative, nondecreasing, differentiable concave valuations \(v_t\), choose a finite-support mass assignment \(y_{t,b}\geq 0\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{J}\). Require \(\sum_b y_{t,b}=\mu_t\) for each type and \(\sum_{t,b} b_j y_{t,b}\leq q_j\) for each category, and maximize \(\sum_{t,b} y_{t,b}v_t(\sum_j u_{tj}b_j)\). Does there exist an algorithm polynomial in the compressed input size \((\tau, |J|, L, 1/\epsilon)\) returning welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/((1+\epsilon)\max_t\kappa_t)\), where \(\kappa_t\) is the paper’s local multiplicative curvature for \(v_t\) and the category utilities?

Valuations are represented by evaluation programs that, on rational \(x\geq0\) and precision \(p\), return rational approximations to \(v_t(x)\) and \(v_t'(x)\) with relative error at most \(2^{-p}\), returning zero exactly for zero values, in time polynomial in the representation length, the bit length of \(x\), and \(p\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including these representations.

Result

Yes. Under the stipulated evaluation model, a polynomial-time algorithm achieves the requested guarantee. It returns rational masses on at most \(2\tau(m+1)\) type-bundle pairs.

The proof combines a continuous relaxation with a decomposition that preserves each type’s mean utility. Only the final rounding of one coordinate incurs the factor \(\kappa_t\).

1. The curvature parameters are finite

Fix a type and write its valuation as \(v\), with \(d=v'(0)\).

If \(v\) is not identically zero, then \(v(w)>0\) for every \(w>0\). Indeed, if \(v(w)=0\), nonnegativity and monotonicity give \(v(x)=0\) for \(0\leq x\leq w\). For \(x>w\), concavity gives

\(0=v(w)\geq (w/x)v(x)+(1-w/x)v(0)\),

so \(v(x)=0\) as well.

For \(w>0\), \(z\geq0\), and \(0<a<w\), let

\(D=v(z)+a\sigma(z,w)=(1-a/w)v(z)+(a/w)v(z+w)\).

Monotonicity implies both \(D\geq v(z)\) and \(D\geq (a/w)v(w)\). Concavity and the finite derivative at zero imply

\(v(z+a)\leq v(z)+ad\leq D+ad\leq \bigl(1+wd/v(w)\bigr)D\).

Concavity also gives \(v(z+a)\geq D\). Consequently every nonzero valuation has finite curvature for each of the finitely many category utilities. An identically zero valuation has curvature \(1\) under the prescribed convention.

Thus, writing \(K=\max_t\kappa_t\), we have \(1\leq K<\infty\).

2. A continuous relaxation bounds every feasible assignment

Let \(J_+=\{j:q_j>0\}\). If \(J_+\) is empty, assigning the empty bundle to every type is optimal. Assume henceforth that \(J_+\neq\varnothing\).

Introduce variables \(s_{tj}\) for \(t\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) and \(j\in J_+\), with feasible region

\(P=\{s:s_{tj}\geq0,\ \sum_t s_{tj}\leq1\text{ for every }j\in J_+\}\).

The corresponding mean bundle of type \(t\) is

\(x_{tj}=q_js_{tj}/\mu_t\),

with \(x_{tj}=0\) for \(j\notin J_+\). Define

\(U_t(s)=\sum_{j\in J_+}u_{tj}q_js_{tj}/\mu_t\),

and

\(F(s)=\sum_t\mu_t v_t(U_t(s))\).

The function \(F\) is continuous, nonnegative, and concave. The region \(P\) is compact, so \(F^*=\max_{s\in P}F(s)\) exists.

For any feasible finite-support assignment, let

\(\bar x_{tj}=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_b b_jy_{t,b}\).

These mean bundles determine a point of \(P\). Applying concavity to the finite distribution of bundles within each type gives

\(\sum_b y_{t,b}v_t(u_t\cdot b)\leq \mu_t v_t(u_t\cdot\bar x_t)\).

Summing over types proves

\(\mathrm{OPT}\leq F^*\).

This argument applies to arbitrarily large bundles; it requires no truncation.

3. Polynomial-time optimization with the specified numerical oracles

We prove that a rational point \(\widehat s\in P\) satisfying

\(F(\widehat s)\geq F^*/(1+\epsilon)\)

can be computed in polynomial time. The details below account for approximate derivative evaluations and exact feasibility.

Numerical bounds

Let \(n=\tau|J_+|\). The point \(s^0\) with every coordinate equal to \(1/(2\tau)\) satisfies

\(B(s^0,r_0)\subseteq P,\qquad r_0=1/(4\tau)\),

where \(B\) denotes a Euclidean ball. Each coordinate remains positive in this ball, and each category’s coordinate sum is at most \(1/2+\tau r_0=3/4\). Also \(P\subseteq B(0,n)\).

Evaluate each \(v_t(U_t(s^0))\) at relative precision \(2^{-2}\), and let \(S_0\) be the mass-weighted sum of the returned values.

If \(S_0=0\), exact-zero reporting implies \(F(s^0)=0\). A nonnegative concave function that vanishes at an interior point of \(P\) vanishes throughout \(P\): for any \(s\in P\), a sufficiently short extension from \(s\) through \(s^0\) gives a point \(w\in P\) and a representation \(s^0=\lambda s+(1-\lambda)w\), with \(\lambda>0\). Concavity then gives \(0\geq\lambda F(s)\). Therefore \(F^*=\mathrm{OPT}=0\), and the empty assignment suffices.

Otherwise set \(B=S_0/2\). The relative-error guarantee gives

\(0<B\leq F(s^0)\leq F^*\).

Using precision \(2^{-2}\) evaluations at zero, choose rational bounds

\(A_t=2\widetilde v_t(0),\qquad D_t=2\widetilde v'_t(0)\).

They satisfy \(A_t\geq v_t(0)\) and \(D_t\geq v'_t(0)\). Define

\(H=\sum_t\mu_t A_t+\sum_{t,j\in J_+}q_ju_{tj}D_t\).

The supporting line at zero gives \(v_t(x)\leq v_t(0)+v'_t(0)x\). Hence, throughout \(P\),

\(F(s)\leq H\).

A supergradient of \(F\), including at boundary points, has coordinates

\(g_{tj}(s)=q_ju_{tj}v'_t(U_t(s))\).

Since concave nondecreasing valuations have \(0\leq v'_t(x)\leq v'_t(0)\), we also have

\(\|g(s)\|_1\leq H\).

The rational numbers \(B\) and \(H\) have polynomial encoding length: all their oracle calls use fixed precision and rational arguments of polynomial encoding length.

Approximate gradients give valid cuts for a near-optimal region

Set

\(\delta=\epsilon B/[4(1+\epsilon)]\).

Choose an integer \(p\geq2\) such that \(2^{-p}H\leq\delta\). At any rational \(s\in P\), precision-\(p\) evaluations produce rational approximations satisfying

\(|\widetilde F(s)-F(s)|\leq\delta,\qquad \|\widetilde g(s)-g(s)\|_1\leq\delta\).

The required precision is polynomial in the input length and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\).

Consider the closed convex set

\(C=\{s\in P:F(s)\geq F^*-\delta\}\).

It contains a ball of explicitly bounded radius. Let \(s^*\) maximize \(F\), and put \(\alpha=\delta/(2H)\). For every \(w\in B(s^0,r_0)\), concavity and nonnegativity give

\(F((1-\alpha)s^*+\alpha w)\geq(1-\alpha)F^*\geq F^*-\delta/2\).

Thus \(C\) contains a ball of radius

\(r=\alpha r_0=\delta r_0/(2H)\).

Now run the rational central-cut ellipsoid procedure, initially enclosing \(P\) in \(B(0,n)\).

At a center \(s\notin P\), a violated rational defining inequality of \(P\) supplies a central cut retaining \(P\). At a center \(s\in P\), record \(s\) and \(\widetilde F(s)\), and retain the halfspace

\(\widetilde g(s)\cdot(z-s)\geq0\).

Suppose such a feasible center satisfies \(F(s)<F^*-2\delta\). For every \(z\in C\), the supergradient inequality gives

\(g(s)\cdot(z-s)\geq F(z)-F(s)>\delta\).

Because both points belong to \([0,1]^n\), the gradient error contributes at most \(\delta\) to this scalar product. Therefore

\(\widetilde g(s)\cdot(z-s)>0\),

so the cut retains all of \(C\).

If \(\widetilde g(s)=0\), the same error bound instead gives \(F^*\leq F(s)+\delta\); that center can be returned immediately.

Ellipsoid termination and selection

The rational central-cut ellipsoid method can process polynomially many rational cuts while maintaining polynomial encoding lengths, until its enclosing ellipsoid has volume at most any specified positive rational threshold. Its enclosure guarantee applies to every fixed set retained by all the cuts. This is the finite-precision content of [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Theorem 3.2.1 and its proof](https://www.mpi-inf.mpg.de/fileadmin/inf/d1/ellipsoid-lovasz.pdf). In dimension one, ordinary interval bisection gives the same property.

Use the threshold \(\nu=(r/n)^n\). Its encoding length is polynomial. If every recorded feasible center had value below \(F^*-2\delta\), all cuts would retain \(C\). The final ellipsoid would then contain \(C\) while having volume at most \(\nu\). This is impossible: a ball of radius \(r\) contains a cube of side \(2r/n\), so

\(\operatorname{vol}(C)\geq(2r/n)^n>\nu\).

Consequently some recorded center has value at least \(F^*-2\delta\). Choose the recorded point \(\widehat s\) with largest approximate value. The two value-approximation errors give

\(F(\widehat s)\geq F^*-4\delta=F^*-\epsilon B/(1+\epsilon)\geq F^*/(1+\epsilon)\).

Only centers belonging exactly to \(P\) are candidates. All valuation arguments, derivative arguments, oracle precisions, and ellipsoid computations have polynomial encoding length. This establishes the claimed polynomial-time optimization step.

4. Rounding a rational mean bundle

We now prove the rounding lemma needed for each type.

Lemma. For any rational \(x\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^m\), there is a polynomial-time construction of a rational distribution on at most \(2(m+1)\) integral bundles such that

\(\mathbb E[b]=x,\qquad \mathbb E[v_t(u_t\cdot b)]\geq v_t(u_t\cdot x)/\kappa_t\).

Utility-preserving vertex decomposition

Write \(x=k+f\), where \(k=\lfloor x\rfloor\) and \(f\in[0,1]^m\). Consider the rational polytope

\(Q=\{h\in[0,1]^m:u_t\cdot h=u_t\cdot f\}\).

Every vertex of \(Q\) has at most one coordinate strictly between zero and one. Indeed:

  • A fractional coordinate with zero utility can be perturbed in both directions.
  • If two positive-utility coordinates \(i,j\) are fractional, small opposite perturbations proportional to \(u_{tj}\) and \(u_{ti}\) preserve the utility equality.

Either perturbation contradicts extremality. Conversely, a point with at most one fractional coordinate, whose utility coefficient is positive, is a vertex: all bound coordinates are fixed in any convex decomposition, and the utility equality fixes the remaining coordinate.

We can decompose

\(f=\sum_{\ell=1}^{h}\lambda_\ell h^\ell,\qquad h\leq m+1\),

into vertices of \(Q\), with rational nonnegative weights summing to one, in polynomial time. Here are constructive and encoding details.

Starting at a residual point, choose a vertex of its minimal face by polynomial-time rational linear programming. Unless the residual point is already that vertex, extend the ray from the vertex through the residual point until it reaches the boundary of the face. This expresses the residual point as a convex combination of the chosen vertex and a point in a strictly smaller face. Repeating takes at most \(m\) such reductions.

Every vertex has polynomial encoding length: its possible fractional coordinate is obtained from the utility equality after fixing all other coordinates to zero or one. The weights also have polynomial encoding length. At each reduction, choose a newly attained coordinate bound. All subsequent vertices satisfy that bound, whereas the vertex just removed does not. These equations form a nonsingular triangular system for the accumulated weights. Its rational coefficients have polynomial encoding length, so determinant bounds give polynomial encoding length for its solution. The residual points consequently have polynomial encoding length as well.

Rounding the remaining coordinate

For each vertex \(h^\ell\), the vector \(k+h^\ell\) has utility exactly \(u_t\cdot x\).

If it is integral, keep it. Otherwise let \(j\) be its unique fractional coordinate, with fractional part \(\theta\in(0,1)\). Its utility coefficient satisfies \(w=u_{tj}>0\). Round that coordinate down with probability \(1-\theta\) and up with probability \(\theta\). This preserves the vector’s expectation.

Let \(z\) be the utility of the rounded-down bundle. Then

\(u_t\cdot x=z+\theta w\),

and the expected valuation is

\((1-\theta)v_t(z)+\theta v_t(z+w)=v_t(z)+\theta w\sigma_t(z,w)\).

By the definition of \(\kappa_t\), this is at least

\(v_t(z+\theta w)/\kappa_t=v_t(u_t\cdot x)/\kappa_t\).

If the secant expression is zero, monotonicity forces the intermediate valuation to be zero, so the inequality still holds.

Combining these distributions with the weights \(\lambda_\ell\) proves the lemma. All probabilities are rational, and each vertex contributes at most two bundles.

5. The resulting assignment and guarantee

From the rational point \(\widehat s\), form

\(\widehat x_{tj}=q_j\widehat s_{tj}/\mu_t\).

Apply the rounding lemma separately to each type. If its resulting bundle probabilities are \(p_{t,b}\), output

\(y_{t,b}=\mu_t p_{t,b}\).

These masses are rational and satisfy \(\sum_b y_{t,b}=\mu_t\). Preservation of each mean bundle gives exact supply feasibility:

\(\sum_{t,b}b_jy_{t,b}=\sum_t\mu_t\widehat x_{tj}=q_j\sum_t\widehat s_{tj}\leq q_j\).

The welfare satisfies

\(W(y)\geq\sum_t\mu_t v_t(u_t\cdot\widehat x_t)/\kappa_t\geq F(\widehat s)/K\geq F^*/[(1+\epsilon)K]\geq\mathrm{OPT}/[(1+\epsilon)K]\).

The output uses at most \(2\tau(m+1)\) type-bundle pairs. Moreover every output coordinate obeys

\(b_j\leq\lceil q_j/\mu_t\rceil\),

so bundle coordinates have polynomial encoding length despite the absence of an individual bundle-size bound.

The algorithm never needs to compute \(K\). All optimization, derivative evaluations, decomposition steps, and output arithmetic take polynomial time in the stipulated parameters.
