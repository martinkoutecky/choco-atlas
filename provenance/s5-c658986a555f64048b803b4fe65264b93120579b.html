The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a finite machine-type set \(T\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^2\), a rational type distribution \(\mu\), and two job-class supplies \(q_1,q_2>0\) per unit mass, what is the smallest \(\rho\) achievable by a direct truthful-in-expectation mechanism? The mechanism must specify, for every population distribution \(\mu\) and every individual report \(r\in T\), a lottery over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^2\) and a payment, satisfy dominant-strategy truthfulness for each machine while holding the other machines’ distribution fixed, balance each job-class supply in aggregate, and guarantee makespan at most \(\rho\) times the unconstrained optimum.

Fix a nonempty public report domain \(T\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^{2}\) and public supplies \(q_1,q_2\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\), with \(m=2\) job classes. A machine of type \(t\) incurs processing cost \(t\cdot b\) for an integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{2}\).
A society is nonatomic with total mass \(1\). It is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary rational masses \(\mu(t)\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. State any running times in \(m,\tau,|T|\), and the total input bit length \(L\).
For fixed \(T,q\), a direct mechanism specifies, for every rational distribution \(\mu\) on \(T\) and every report \(r\in T\), a bundle lottery \(P_{\mu,r}\) with finite first moments and a finite payment \(p_{\mu,r}\in\mathbb{R}\).
Interpret \(P_{\mu,t}(b)\) as the fraction of type-\(t\) mass receiving bundle \(b\). Exact aggregate feasibility requires \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu(t)\mathbb{E}_{P_{\mu,t}}[b_j]=q_j\) for \(j\in\{1,2\}\).
An individual deviation leaves \(\mu\) unchanged. Dominant-strategy truthfulness in expectation requires \(p_{\mu,t}-t\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P_{\mu,t}}[b]\ge p_{\mu,r}-t\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P_{\mu,r}}[b]\) for every \(\mu\) and \(t,r\in T\); indifference is permitted.
Define makespan by \(C_M(\mu)=\sup\{t\cdot b:\mu(t)P_{\mu,t}(b)>0\}\). Let \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)\) be the infimum of this makespan over all aggregate-feasible bundle lotteries, without incentive constraints.

What is the sharp achievable approximation ratio \(\rho^*(T,q)=\inf_M\sup_{\mu} C_M(\mu)/\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)\), where \(M\) ranges over the feasible truthful-in-expectation mechanisms defined above and \(\mu\) ranges over rational distributions on \(T\)?

For two discrete machines and two tasks with positive processing costs, deterministic dominant-strategy truthful mechanisms have optimal worst-case approximation ratio \(2\), attained by VCG. [Christodoulou–Koutsoupias–Vidali](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0807.3427)
For two discrete machines, universally truthful randomized mechanisms achieve expected-makespan approximation ratio \(1.6737\). [Lu–Yu](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2008.1314)

Partial resolution: stronger bounds and exact computation for finite domains

I prove three further results:

  • Every public domain admits a feasible truthful-in-expectation mechanism with approximation ratio at most $2$.
  • A two-type family has sharp ratio approaching $3/2$. Thus the $4/3$ bound in the notes is not a universal upper bound.
  • For an explicitly listed finite domain, the sharp ratio is exactly computable by a finite real-algebraic decision procedure.

Consequently, if $R_2$ denotes the best approximation guarantee uniformly over all public domains and supplies, then
$$ \frac32\le R_2\le2. $$
The exact value of $R_2$ remains open here.

Throughout, societies have finite support, as specified by the support-list input model. The public report domain itself may be infinite.

1. Fixed-society optimization and extension to absent reports

Fix a society $\mu$ with nonempty support $S$ and $|S|=\tau$. Write $x_t$ for the expected bundle assigned to report $t$.

1.1. Extending a truthful support menu

Suppose lotteries and finite payments have been specified on $S$, satisfying
$$ p_t-t\cdot x_t\ge p_r-t\cdot x_r \qquad(t,r\in S). $$
For every absent report $u\in T\setminus S$, choose
$$ h(u)\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{r\in S} \{p_r-u\cdot x_r\}, $$
and give report $u$ the lottery and payment of $h(u)$.

The maximum exists because the menu is finite and every utility is finite. Present types already maximize utility over that menu. Each absent type receives an entry maximizing its utility over the same menu. Since the extension adds no new entries, every truthfulness inequality on $T$ holds.

Absent types have zero mass, so this extension preserves feasibility and makespan. Thus optimization at a fixed society requires incentive constraints only between its $\tau$ positive-mass types.

1.2. Configuration polytopes and attainment

For $C\ge0$, define
$$ B_t(C)=\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2}:t\cdot b\le C\}, \qquad K_t(C)=\operatorname{conv} B_t(C). $$
Positivity of both coordinates of $t$ makes $B_t(C)$ finite. An expected bundle belongs to $K_t(C)$ exactly when it can be implemented by a lottery of makespan at most $C$ for type $t$.

Consequently, truthful feasibility at threshold $C$ is equivalent to
$$ x_t\in K_t(C)\qquad(t\in S), $$
$$ \sum_{t\in S}\mu(t)x_t=q, $$
$$ p_t-p_r\ge t\cdot(x_t-x_r) \qquad(t,r\in S). $$
These are a finite rational LP when $C$ is rational: introduce nonnegative probabilities $\lambda_{t,b}$ with
$$ \sum_{b\in B_t(C)}\lambda_{t,b}=1, \qquad x_t=\sum_{b\in B_t(C)}\lambda_{t,b}b. $$
Deleting payments and incentive inequalities gives the unconstrained feasibility LP.

Set
$$ U_S=\max_{t\in S}\sum_{j=1}^{2}t_j\lceil q_j\rceil. $$
A report-independent lottery that rounds each coordinate of $q$ to its floor or ceiling has expectation $q$ and makespan at most $U_S$. Identical payments make it truthful.

Every allocation of makespan at most $U_S$ uses only the finite collection of configurations $B_t(U_S)$. Its makespan therefore belongs to the finite set
$$ V_S=\{t\cdot b:t\in S,\ b\in B_t(U_S)\}. $$
Both the unconstrained and truthful optima are consequently attained at a smallest feasible threshold in $V_S$. Denote the latter by $A(\mu,q)$. Also,
$$ 0<\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)\le A(\mu,q), $$
because makespan zero permits only zero bundles.

Since individual deviations leave $\mu$ fixed, menus can be chosen independently at different societies. The extension above therefore proves
$$ \rho^*(T,q)= \sup_{\mu} \frac{A(\mu,q)}{\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)}. $$
Indeed, every mechanism has makespan at least $A(\mu,q)$ at each society. Conversely, choosing an optimal support menu independently at every society, and extending it, defines one admissible mechanism attaining $A(\mu,q)$ everywhere.

For a supplied society, explicit configuration enumeration and rational LP feasibility compute both optima in
$$ 2^{O(L)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \qquad(m=2) $$
bit operations. To justify the bound, every configuration coordinate is at most $U_S/t_j\le2^{O(L)}$, so the total number of configurations is at most $\tau2^{O(L)}$. Enumeration, sorting candidate loads, and binary search over LP feasibility have polynomial cost in that number and the input length. The support calculation has no dependence on absent reports or $|T|$.

2. A universal truthful approximation ratio of $2$

The construction uses a scalar reassignment lemma.

2.1. Scalar reassignment lemma

Let
$$ 0<c_1<\cdots<c_h $$
be costs, with positive group masses $w_i$ summing to $1$. Let $K\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ be finite, and let $\kappa_k$ be prescribed aggregate masses of counts $k\in K$, also summing to $1$.

Suppose there is a feasible assignment $\theta_{i,k}$ satisfying
$$ \sum_k\theta_{i,k}=w_i, \qquad \sum_i\theta_{i,k}=\kappa_k, \qquad \theta_{i,k}=0\ \text{if }c_i k>D. $$
Then there is such an assignment whose conditional expected counts
$$ y_i=\frac1{w_i}\sum_k k\theta_{i,k} $$
satisfy
$$ y_1\ge\cdots\ge y_h. $$
Moreover, these expected allocations admit truthful scalar payments.

Proof. Minimize
$$ \sum_{i,k}c_i k\theta_{i,k} $$
over the feasible assignments. The feasible region is a nonempty compact polytope: all variables are nonnegative and their total is $1$. Hence a minimizer exists.

Suppose it assigns positive mass of count $k$ to group $i$ and count $\ell$ to group $a$, where
$$ i<a,\qquad k<\ell. $$
Exchange a sufficiently small positive mass $\delta$ between these assignments. The new assignments satisfy the load bound because
$$ c_i\ell\le c_a\ell\le D, \qquad c_a k\le c_a\ell\le D. $$
The exchange preserves all row and column sums. Its objective change is
$$ \delta(c_a-c_i)(k-\ell)<0, $$
contradicting minimality.

Thus every positively assigned count in a cheaper group is at least every positively assigned count in a more expensive group. Taking conditional expectations proves the asserted ordering.

Define payments by
$$ \pi_h=0, \qquad \pi_i-\pi_{i+1}=c_i(y_i-y_{i+1}). $$
For $i<a$, telescoping gives
$$ \pi_i-\pi_a = \sum_{r=i}^{a-1}c_r(y_r-y_{r+1}). $$
All successive count differences are nonnegative, and $c_i\le c_r\le c_a$. Therefore
$$ c_i(y_i-y_a) \le \pi_i-\pi_a \le c_a(y_i-y_a). $$
The two bounds are precisely the incentive inequalities in the two directions. This proves scalar truthfulness. $\square$

2.2. Applying the lemma separately to the two classes

Fix a society and an optimal unconstrained allocation of makespan
$$ C=\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q). $$
By Section 1, it has finite bundle support.

For each original bundle $b=(b_1,b_2)$, consider replacing it by
$$ (2b_1,0)\quad\text{with probability }\frac12, \qquad (0,2b_2)\quad\text{with probability }\frac12. $$
Both resulting bundles are integral. Their expected bundle is $b$, and their loads on the original type are at most $2C$.

We now reassign the conditional pure-class bundles separately.

Fix a class $j$. Group support types by their coordinate $t_j$. Let the distinct coordinate values be
$$ c_{j,1}<\cdots<c_{j,h_j}, $$
and let $w_{j,i}$ be the total mass of group $i$.

The original allocation induces aggregate masses $\kappa_{j,k}$ of scaled counts
$$ k=2b_j. $$
An initially feasible assignment to the groups exists: retain each scaled count on its original type. Whenever this assignment has positive mass,
$$ t_j k=2t_jb_j\le2t\cdot b\le2C. $$
The scalar lemma, with $D=2C$, supplies a reassignment preserving every aggregate count mass and having decreasing expected counts $y_{j,i}$ as $c_{j,i}$ increases.

Give every type in group $i$ the same conditional count lottery, obtained by dividing its group assignment by $w_{j,i}$. Let $\pi_{j,i}$ be the scalar truthful payments from the lemma.

For a support type $t$, let $i_j(t)$ be its group in class $j$. Define its actual bundle lottery by choosing each class with probability $1/2$ and then drawing that class’s conditional pure bundle. Its expected bundle and payment are
$$ x_{t,j}=\frac12y_{j,i_j(t)}, \qquad p_t=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{2}\pi_{j,i_j(t)}. $$

Feasibility. Reassignment preserved the aggregate scaled counts. Therefore, for each class,
$$ \sum_t\mu(t)y_{j,i_j(t)}=2q_j, $$
and hence $\sum_t\mu(t)x_{t,j}=q_j$.

Makespan. Every positively assigned pure bundle satisfies $t_j k\le2C$. Its other coordinate is zero, so its full processing cost is at most $2C$.

Truthfulness. For support types $t,r$, scalar truthfulness in each class gives
$$ \pi_{j,i_j(t)}-t_jy_{j,i_j(t)} \ge \pi_{j,i_j(r)}-t_jy_{j,i_j(r)}. $$
Multiplying by $1/2$ and summing over the classes yields
$$ p_t-t\cdot x_t\ge p_r-t\cdot x_r. $$
Extend the resulting menu to all absent reports using Section 1.1.

This construction applies independently at every society. It proves
$$ \boxed{1\le\rho^*(T,q)\le2} $$
for every allowed public domain, including infinite domains.

Computing the optimal benchmark and the two reassignment LPs takes
$$ 2^{O(L)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \qquad(m=2) $$
bit operations for a supplied society.

3. An exact two-type family approaching $3/2$

Let $h>2$ be rational, and define
$$ t=(1,h),\qquad s=(h,h+1), \qquad T_h=\{t,s\}, \qquad q=\left(\frac12,\frac12\right). $$

Theorem.
$$ \boxed{\rho^*(T_h,q)=\frac{h+1}{h}.} $$

3.1. Upper bound for every society

For every report and society, use the common lottery
$$ P((1,0))=P((0,1))=\frac12, \qquad p=0. $$
Its expectation is $q$, so it is aggregate-feasible. All reports give identical outcomes, so it is truthful.

Its makespan is at most $h+1$. Every feasible allocation has makespan at least $h$: positive class-2 supply requires some positive-mass individual to receive a class-2 job, whose processing cost is at least
$$ \min\{t_2,s_2\}=h. $$
Thus this mechanism has ratio at most $(h+1)/h$ at every society.

3.2. Matching incentive obstruction

Consider the rational society
$$ \mu(t)=\mu(s)=\frac12. $$
Giving bundle $(0,1)$ to type $t$ and $(1,0)$ to type $s$ is feasible with makespan $h$. The preceding lower bound proves
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)=h. $$

Suppose a truthful feasible allocation had makespan strictly below $h+1$.

Type $s$ cannot receive any class-2 job. Type $t$ can receive at most one, since
$$ 2h>h+1. $$
Exact class-2 supply $1/2$, together with type-$t$ mass $1/2$, forces every type-$t$ individual to receive exactly one class-2 job almost surely. Such an individual cannot also receive a class-1 job, since that would cost at least $h+1$. Consequently,
$$ x_t=(0,1). $$

Type $s$ can receive at most one class-1 job, again because $2h>h+1$. Exact class-1 supply then forces
$$ x_s=(1,0). $$

Adding the two incentive inequalities gives the necessary condition
$$ (t-s)\cdot(x_t-x_s)\le0. $$
But here
$$ (t-s)\cdot(x_t-x_s) = (1-h,-1)\cdot(-1,1) = h-2>0. $$
This is a contradiction. Every truthful mechanism therefore has makespan at least $h+1$ at this society.

The common lottery attains that bound, proving the theorem.

Taking rational $h$ decreasing to $2$ gives
$$ \sup_{\substack{T\text{ finite, nonempty}\\q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^{2}}} \rho^*(T,q)\ge\frac32. $$
In particular, choosing $2<h<3$ already gives a sharp ratio strictly larger than $4/3$.

4. Exact computation for explicitly listed finite domains

Now suppose $T$ is finite, explicitly listed, and $n=|T|$. Let $L$ be the total encoding length of this list and $q$.

Define
$$ U=\max_{t\in T}\sum_{j=1}^{2}t_j\lceil q_j\rceil, $$
and list the distinct candidate loads as
$$ V=\{t\cdot b:t\in T,\ b\in B_t(U)\} =\{0=v_0<v_1<\cdots<v_K\}. $$
At every society on $T$, both optima belong to $V$.

The following procedure gives an exact finite formula for the sharp ratio.

4.1. The witness tests

For every nonempty $S\subseteq T$ and indices $i,k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$, test whether there is a rational mass vector, positive on $S$, such that:

  • unconstrained feasibility holds at threshold $v_k$;
  • truthful feasibility fails at threshold $v_{i-1}$.

Call this test $E(S,i,k)$. Then
$$ \boxed{ \rho^*(T,q)= \max_{\substack{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq T\\ 1\le i,k\le K\\E(S,i,k)}} \frac{v_i}{v_k}. } $$

To prove the formula, first take any witness for $E(S,i,k)$. Its truthful optimum satisfies
$$ A(\mu,q)>v_{i-1}. $$
Since $A(\mu,q)\in V$, it follows that $A(\mu,q)\ge v_i$. Also $\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)\le v_k$. Thus its actual ratio is at least $v_i/v_k$.

Conversely, take any rational society and write
$$ A(\mu,q)=v_i,\qquad \operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)=v_k. $$
Its support and masses witness $E(S,i,k)$, so its exact ratio appears in the displayed maximum. The maximum is nonempty because societies exist and their optima are positive.

It remains to make the rational witness tests effective.

4.2. Closedness of truthful feasibility

Fix $S$ and $C$. On the closed simplex
$$ \Delta_S=\left\{\mu\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{S}: \sum_t\mu(t)=1\right\}, $$
define an auxiliary truthful feasibility set $F_C^{\mathrm{IC}}$ by requiring expected allocations in $K_t(C)$ for every $t\in S$, together with the aggregate and incentive constraints of Section 1.2.

At boundary points, these auxiliary constraints still apply to zero-mass types. Only the restriction to strictly positive masses will represent societies in the witness tests.

I claim that $F_C^{\mathrm{IC}}$ is closed.

Normalize one payment to zero. Put
$$ R_j=\max_{t\in S}\frac{C}{t_j}, \qquad D=\max_{t\in S}\sum_{j=1}^{2}t_jR_j. $$
Every expected allocation satisfies $0\le x_{t,j}\le R_j$. The incentive inequalities between a type and the normalized report imply
$$ -D\le p_t\le D. $$
Indeed, those two inequalities bound its payment between two dot products of an allocation difference; each dot product has absolute value at most $D$.

Thus all relevant allocations and normalized payments lie in a fixed compact set. The aggregate equations are continuous in the masses, allocations, and payments. The joint feasible set is compact, and its projection onto the mass coordinates is compact, hence closed.

Therefore truthful infeasibility is relatively open in $\Delta_S$.

4.3. Benchmark feasibility is a rational polytope

Let $F_C^0$ denote unconstrained feasibility on $\Delta_S$. It is the projection of the system
$$ \nu_{t,b}\ge0, \qquad \sum_{b\in B_t(C)}\nu_{t,b}=\mu(t), $$
$$ \sum_{t,b}\nu_{t,b}b=q. $$
Here $\nu_{t,b}$ is population mass assigned to a type-bundle pair. These constraints define a bounded rational polytope: the total mass of the $\nu$ variables is $1$. Hence $F_C^0$ is a rational polytope.

Every point of a rational polytope is a limit of rational points in that polytope. For completeness, its vertices are rational because each vertex is the unique solution of an appropriate full-rank subsystem of rational linear equations. Express a point as a convex combination of vertices and approximate the combination coefficients by nonnegative rational coefficients summing to $1$.

It follows that
$$ F_{v_k}^0 \cap \bigl(\Delta_S\setminus F_{v_{i-1}}^{\mathrm{IC}}\bigr) \cap \{\mu(t)>0\text{ for every }t\in S\} $$
contains a rational point whenever it contains a real point. The last two conditions define a relatively open set, so sufficiently close rational points of the benchmark polytope retain them.

This is the required justification for replacing rational witness tests by real witness tests.

4.4. A two-block real-algebraic decision formula

Each $K_t(C)$ is a rational polygon, segment, or point and admits a finite linear description.

The real version of $E(S,i,k)$ is the following sentence:
$$ \exists \mu,y\; \left[ \begin{aligned} &\mu(t)>0\quad(t\in S),\qquad \sum_t\mu(t)=1,\\ &y_t\in K_t(v_k)\quad(t\in S),\qquad \sum_t\mu(t)y_t=q,\\ &\neg\exists x,p\; \left( \begin{aligned} &x_t\in K_t(v_{i-1})\quad(t\in S),\\ &\sum_t\mu(t)x_t=q,\\ &p_t-p_r\ge t\cdot(x_t-x_r)\quad(t,r\in S) \end{aligned} \right) \end{aligned} \right]. $$
After moving the negated existential quantifier to the front, this has two quantifier blocks. The first contains $3\tau$ variables, and the second contains $3\tau$ variables. Every polynomial has degree at most $2$: only the aggregate equations are quadratic.

Real quantifier elimination therefore decides every test exactly. Section 4.3 proves that its answer is also the answer for rational societies.

4.5. Bit-complexity bound

Let
$$ N=\sum_{t\in T}|B_t(U)|. $$
For $m=2$,
$$ N\le n\,2^{O(L)}, \qquad K\le N. $$
Each polygon description has at most a linear number of constraints in its configuration count. Its coefficients have $O(L)$ bits: vertices have $O(L)$-bit integer coordinates, and edge equations use determinants of those coordinates. Degenerate segments and points are described by linear equations and inequalities.

Thus each witness sentence uses
$$ s=O(N+n^2) $$
polynomials of degree at most $2$, with integer coefficient lengths $O(L)$ after clearing denominators.

The two-block specialization of the Basu–Pollack–Roy quantifier-elimination bound decides such a sentence in
$$ s^{O(\tau^2)}\,2^{O(\tau^2)}\operatorname{poly}(L,\tau) $$
bit operations. This follows from its arithmetic-operation bound and its bound on intermediate integer lengths. The applicable theorem allows arbitrary Boolean combinations and both existential and universal blocks. [Basu–Pollack–Roy theorem, stated as Theorem 2.16 in Basu’s exposition](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011.pdf).

There are fewer than $2^n$ supports and at most $N^2$ threshold pairs. Including enumeration and polygon construction gives the conservative total bound
$$ \boxed{ 2^{O(L|T|^2)} \operatorname{poly}(m,|T|,L) \qquad(m=2). } $$
This computes the sharp ratio itself. It does not assert polynomial-time solvability.

5. Infinite domains and remaining open questions

For arbitrary $T$, the support-extension argument also gives
$$ \boxed{ \rho^*(T,q)= \sup_{\substack{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq T\\S\text{ finite}}} \rho^*(S,q). } $$
To verify this, a society supported on $S$ has the same unconstrained optimum in both domains. Its optimal truthful support menu extends to $T$, so its truthful optimum is also unchanged. Taking suprema over all finite-support societies proves the identity.

Thus infinite domains do not enlarge the sharp universal constant beyond the supremum over finite domains. The results proved here establish
$$ \frac32 \le \sup_{T,q}\rho^*(T,q) \le2, $$
and give an exact finite procedure for each explicitly listed finite $T$.

The remaining mathematical gap is the sharp universal constant and a structural evaluation of the domain-dependent ratio beyond the decision procedure. No argument here proves that either endpoint of the universal interval is sharp.
