Theorem 3 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) bidder types \(\theta=(a,E)\), item classes \(K\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_k\), where all profiles share class values \(v_k\) and sizes \(w_k\), determine whether a succinct polynomial-time direct randomized mechanism can be computed. It must assign each bidder an integral feasible bundle \(B\subseteq E\), keep total assigned mass of each class \(k\) at most \(\sigma_k\), be truthful against every compatibility-hiding report \(E'\subseteq E\) at every fixed background report distribution, and achieve expected welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\sigma)/4\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(K\) be \(m\) item classes, with public binary-rational values \(v_k\geq0\), sizes \(w_k>0\), and per-capita supplies \(\sigma_k\geq0\). A bidder type is \((a,E)\), where \(a>0\) is a public binary-rational capacity and \(E\subseteq K\) is private compatibility; only reports \(E'\subseteq E\) are permitted.
A society is an atomless population of total mass \(1\), given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Thus \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A feasible integral bundle is \(B\subseteq E\) with \(\sum_{k\in B}w_k\leq a\); it contains at most one unit of each class and gives utility \(u(B)=\sum_{k\in B}v_k\). The empty bundle is allowed, and there are no payments.
A direct mechanism specifies a lottery \(q_\nu(a,F)\) over feasible bundles for every report \((a,F)\) at every finitely supported rational background report distribution \(\nu\). An individual report leaves \(\nu\) fixed, including when the report has zero mass in \(\nu\).
Interpret lottery probabilities as fractions of each reported type's mass receiving integral bundles. Require \(\sum_{(a,F)\in\operatorname{supp}\nu}\nu(a,F)\Pr_{q_\nu(a,F)}[k\in B]\leq\sigma_k\) for every \(k\) and every \(\nu\).
Truthfulness means \(\mathbb E_{q_\nu(a,E)}u(B)\geq\mathbb E_{q_\nu(a,E')}u(B)\) for every \(\nu,a,E\) and \(E'\subseteq E\).
Let \(W(q,\mu)=\sum_{(a,E)\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}\mu(a,E)\mathbb E_{q_\mu(a,E)}u(B)\), and let \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\sigma)\) be the maximum welfare over all feasible bundle lotteries, without incentive constraints.
Here succinct polynomial computation means a uniform algorithm producing a representation of polynomial bit length that returns an explicit rational lottery of polynomial support for any queried report, with construction and query times polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and the query bit length.

Does there exist a succinct polynomial-time direct randomized mechanism satisfying these feasibility and truthfulness conditions and guaranteeing \(W(q,\mu)\geq\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\sigma)/4\) for every input, or is this approximation guarantee unattainable under these requirements?

For finitely many strategic bidders and individually listed items with common values and sizes across bidders, a polynomial-time truthful-in-expectation mechanism achieves a \(4\)-approximation ([Fadaei–Bichler](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.04273)). No result settling the atomless, support-encoded problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted a as the bidder's public capacity; compatibility alone is private and manipulable.
  • Normalized the atomless society to total mass 1, matching the distribution and per-capita supply terminology.
  • Specified nonnegative rational values and supplies, and strictly positive rational sizes and capacities.
  • Used weak capacity feasibility and additive utility at equality. The source's isolated strict inequality conflicts with its formal feasibility condition and its unit-capacity examples.
  • Interpreted each class as supplying copies of an item, with at most one copy per bidder, preserving the stated bundle-as-subset convention.
  • Interpreted lottery probabilities as actual fractions of population mass receiving integral bundles, so the displayed aggregate supply constraints govern assigned mass.
  • Defined deviations as reports by a zero-mass individual at a fixed background distribution, and included reports absent from its support.
  • Defined OPT using feasible integral-bundle lotteries without truthfulness constraints.
  • Made succinctness precise through polynomial-size rational lottery representations and polynomial-time queries, including off-support reports.

1. Result

Such a mechanism exists. The construction below satisfies the stated feasibility and truthfulness conditions and achieves the required \(4\)-approximation with polynomial construction time, query time, and representation length.

The fractional-knapsack step is related to Algorithm 1 of [Fadaei–Bichler](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.04273). The proof here supplies a support-encoded construction and defines the mechanism explicitly for zero-mass reports.

2. A local fractional choice and its lottery

Fix a public capacity \(a\), a report \(F\), and a set \(S\) of currently available classes. Define

\( \displaystyle V(a,F,S)= \max\left\{ \sum_k v_kx_k: 0\leq x_k\leq1,\quad \sum_k w_kx_k\leq a,\quad x_k=0\text{ unless }k\in F\cap S\text{ and }w_k\leq a \right\}. \)

The exclusion of classes with \(w_k>a\) is necessary for the rounding below and loses no integral feasible bundle.

Fix a global ordering of classes by decreasing \(v_k/w_k\), breaking ties by class index. Compute \(x(a,F,S)\) by greedily filling the capacity in this order, using only the allowed classes. This vector consists of a prefix \(P\) of fully selected classes and at most one fractionally selected class \(j\), whose coordinate is denoted by \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).

We first verify that this greedy vector attains \(V(a,F,S)\). If the capacity is not filled, every allowed coordinate equals one, which is optimal because values are nonnegative. Otherwise choose a cutoff density \(\lambda\geq0\): the density of the fractional class, if present, or the last fully selected class otherwise. For any feasible vector \(z\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_k v_k(z_k-x_k) = \lambda\sum_k w_k(z_k-x_k) + \sum_k(v_k-\lambda w_k)(z_k-x_k) \leq0. \)

The first term is nonpositive because \(x\) fills the capacity. In the second term, fully selected coordinates have nonnegative coefficients and \(z_k-x_k\leq0\); unselected coordinates have nonpositive coefficients and \(z_k-x_k\geq0\); any fractional coordinate has coefficient zero. This proves optimality.

Convert \(x\) into the following lottery:

  • Give \(P\) with probability \(1/2\).
  • Give \(\{j\}\) with probability \(\alpha/2\).
  • Give the empty bundle with probability \((1-\alpha)/2\).

If there is no fractional class, omit the singleton outcome and give the empty bundle with probability \(1/2\). Identical outcomes are combined.

Every outcome is feasible: the total size of \(P\) is at most \(a\), and the fractional class was restricted to satisfy \(w_j\leq a\). Each coordinate has marginal probability exactly \(x_k/2\). Thus this lottery, denoted by \(R(a,F,S)\), has at most three outcomes and satisfies

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E_{R(a,F,S)}u(B)=\frac12V(a,F,S). \)

Finally, for fixed \(a,S\), if \(F'\subseteq F\), the fractional feasible region for \(F'\) is contained in that for \(F\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle V(a,F',S)\leq V(a,F,S). \)

3. Constructing the background schedule

Fix an arbitrary finitely supported rational background distribution

\( \displaystyle \nu=\{((a_i,F_i),p_i):i=1,\ldots,\tau\}. \)

The schedule below is a virtual fractional allocation. The actual mechanism will use the lotteries from Section 2.

Initialize residual supplies by \(s_k=\sigma_k\). A class is available precisely when its residual supply is positive. Process the distinct public capacities appearing in \(\nu\) in increasing order.

Consider one capacity \(a\), and let

\( \displaystyle I_a=\{i:a_i=a\}. \)

Use a progress variable \(\theta\), initially zero, for this capacity group. While \(\theta<1\), let \(S=\{k:s_k>0\}\), compute \(x_i=x(a,F_i,S)\) for every \(i\in I_a\), and define the consumption rates

\( \displaystyle r_k=\sum_{i\in I_a}p_i x_{ik}. \)

Choose the phase length

\( \displaystyle \delta= \min\left( \{1-\theta\}\cup \left\{\frac{s_k}{r_k}:r_k>0\right\} \right). \)

Record the phase \((S,\delta)\), and update

\( \displaystyle s_k\leftarrow s_k-\delta r_k,\qquad \theta\leftarrow\theta+\delta. \)

Delete every class whose residual supply becomes zero before starting another phase. If all rates are zero, the formula simply gives \(\delta=1-\theta\).

Each phase has positive length. Every phase either finishes its capacity group or exhausts at least one previously available class. If there are \(d\) distinct capacities, the total number of phases is therefore at most \(m+d\), hence at most \(m+\tau\). The phase lengths within each capacity group sum to one.

For a support type \(i\), define its average virtual fractional allocation by

\( \displaystyle \bar x_i= \sum_{\ell\text{ in the group }a_i} \delta_\ell x(a_i,F_i,S_\ell). \)

The supply updates give

\( \displaystyle \sum_i p_i\bar x_{ik}\leq\sigma_k \quad\text{for every }k. \)

Moreover, if class \(k\) has zero residual supply at the end, equality holds. This includes classes whose initial supply is zero.

4. Defining all report queries

The representation stores the capacity groups, their phases, and the available-class set immediately before and after each group.

For a queried report \((a,F)\), define \(q_\nu(a,F)\) as follows.

If \(a\) occurs in the support of \(\nu\), use its stored phases. Choose phase \(\ell\) with probability \(\delta_\ell\), then use \(R(a,F,S_\ell)\).

If \(a\) does not occur in the support, let \(S(a)\) be the available-class set immediately after all capacity groups strictly below \(a\) have been processed. Set

\( \displaystyle q_\nu(a,F)=R(a,F,S(a)). \)

This defines the mechanism even when the queried report has zero background mass. For fixed \(\nu,a\), the sets and phase probabilities used by a query are independent of \(F\).

For a support report, its marginal probability of receiving class \(k\) is \(\bar x_{ik}/2\). Consequently, actual consumption satisfies

\( \displaystyle \sum_i p_i\Pr_{q_\nu(a_i,F_i)}[k\in B] = \frac12\sum_i p_i\bar x_{ik} \leq\frac{\sigma_k}{2} \leq\sigma_k. \)

Together with the local bundle feasibility proved earlier, this establishes all required feasibility conditions for every background \(\nu\).

For truthfulness, fix any \(\nu,a,E\) and \(E'\subseteq E\). When \(a\) is a support capacity,

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E_{q_\nu(a,E)}u(B) = \frac12\sum_\ell\delta_\ell V(a,E,S_\ell) \geq \frac12\sum_\ell\delta_\ell V(a,E',S_\ell) = \mathbb E_{q_\nu(a,E')}u(B). \)

When \(a\) is absent from the support, the same argument uses the single set \(S(a)\). Thus truthfulness holds for every queried report, including reports with zero mass.

5. Welfare guarantee

Apply the construction to the true distribution \(\mu\). Write its support as \(((a_i,E_i),p_i)\), and let the virtual welfare be

\( \displaystyle G=\sum_i p_i\sum_k v_k\bar x_{ik}. \)

Because local rounding preserves exactly half of every virtual marginal,

\( \displaystyle W(q,\mu)=G/2. \)

Let \(D\) be the classes with zero residual supply at the end of the schedule, and put \(J=K\setminus D\).

Consider any feasible integral-bundle lotteries, with marginal probabilities \(y_{ik}\). Their welfare splits into contributions from \(D\) and \(J\).

For exhausted classes, supply feasibility and nonnegative values imply

\( \displaystyle \sum_{k\in D}v_k\sum_i p_i y_{ik} \leq \sum_{k\in D}v_k\sigma_k = \sum_{k\in D}v_k\sum_i p_i\bar x_{ik} \leq G. \)

For the remaining classes, fix a type \(i\). Its restricted marginal vector \(y_i|_J\) satisfies

\( \displaystyle 0\leq y_{ik}\leq1,\qquad \sum_{k\in J}w_k y_{ik}\leq a_i, \)

and is zero outside \(E_i\) and on classes with \(w_k>a_i\). These facts follow respectively from at most one unit per class, expected bundle feasibility, and integral bundle feasibility.

Every class in \(J\) remains available throughout the entire schedule: residual supplies only decrease, and its final residual supply is positive. Thus \(y_i|_J\) is feasible for the local fractional problem in every phase of type \(i\)'s capacity group. Local optimality gives

\( \displaystyle \sum_{k\in J}v_k y_{ik} \leq V(a_i,E_i,S_\ell) \quad\text{for every such phase }\ell. \)

Average over those phases, whose lengths sum to one, and then over types:

\( \displaystyle \sum_i p_i\sum_{k\in J}v_k y_{ik} \leq \sum_i p_i\sum_\ell\delta_\ell V(a_i,E_i,S_\ell) =G. \)

The two contributions therefore sum to at most \(2G\). This holds for every feasible lottery allocation. The optimum exists because each support type has finitely many feasible bundles and their feasible probability vectors form a compact polytope. Hence

\( \displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\sigma)\leq2G, \qquad W(q,\mu)=\frac G2\geq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\sigma)}4. \)

6. Polynomial representation and exact computation

The construction uses at most \(m+\tau\) phases. Each phase requires only fractional greedy computations, rational sums, comparisons, and supply updates.

We verify that exact rational arithmetic also has polynomial bit complexity. Every local fractional coordinate is either zero, one, or of the form

\( \displaystyle \frac{a-\sum_{k\in P}w_k}{w_j}. \)

Its bit length is polynomial in the input length. Each rate \(r_k\), being a sum of at most \(\tau\) products of input masses and such coordinates, likewise has polynomial bit length, uniformly over all possible phase sets.

To bound the evolving supplies, represent the current residual supplies and group progress with a common denominator:

\( \displaystyle s_k=S_k/D,\qquad \theta=T/D. \)

For the current rates, choose a common denominator \(R\) and write \(r_k=n_k/R\). Both \(R\) and the \(n_k\) have polynomial bit length.

If a phase ends by exhausting class \(j\), then

\( \displaystyle \delta=\frac{S_jR}{Dn_j}, \quad s_k'=\frac{S_kn_j-S_jn_k}{Dn_j}, \quad \theta'=\frac{Tn_j+S_jR}{Dn_j}. \)

Thus the new common denominator can be \(Dn_j\). If the phase instead ends the group, use \(\delta=(D-T)/D\); the new supplies have common denominator \(DR\).

Each phase therefore increases the common denominator's bit length by only a polynomial amount. There are at most \(m+\tau\) phases. Residual supplies remain between zero and their initial values, and progress remains between zero and one, so numerator lengths are polynomial as well.

The stored representation consists of polynomially many capacity values, available-class masks, and rational phase lengths. It has polynomial bit length.

A query performs local greedy computations for its capacity group's phases, or one computation if its capacity is absent. Each local lottery has at most three outcomes. A capacity group has at most \(m+1\) phases, so the returned explicit rational lottery has at most \(3(m+1)\) outcomes before combining duplicates. Its probabilities, construction time, and query time are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and the query bit length.

No expansion of rational masses into individually listed bidders is needed. The required succinct polynomial-time mechanism therefore exists.
