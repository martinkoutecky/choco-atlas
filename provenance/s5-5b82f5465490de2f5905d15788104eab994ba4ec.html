The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 1 (Identifiability) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an unknown distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings of \(n\) items, with pairwise marginals \(q_{ij}=\sum_{\pi:\,i\succ_\pi j}\mu_\pi=w_i/(w_i+w_j)\) for positive strengths \(w_i\), sample \(L\) independent rankings for each edge of a random graph \(\mathcal G\sim G_{n,p_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) and observe their pairwise preferences. From the graph and observations, return the \(K\) items with largest strengths. What sample threshold and running time suffice for exact recovery with high probability as a function of \(n\), \(p_{\mathrm{obs}}\), \(L\), and the boundary gap \(\Delta_K=(w_{(K)}-w_{(K+1)})/w_{\max}\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m=n\geq2\) labeled items and \(1\leq K<n\). A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) strict complete rankings with positive binary rational masses summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(n!\). Write \(L\) for encoding bit length and \(r\) for comparisons per edge.
The society defines an unknown distribution \(\mu\), promised to satisfy \(q_{ij}=\sum_{\pi:i\succ_\pi j}\mu_\pi=w_i/(w_i+w_j)\), where \(w_i\in[a,b]\) for fixed known constants \(0<a<b\). The support and strengths are hidden from the estimator.
Let \(w_{(1)}\geq\cdots\geq w_{(n)}\) be the sorted strengths and assume \(\Delta_K=(w_{(K)}-w_{(K+1)})/b>0\). Internal ties are allowed; the output is the unordered set of the strongest \(K\) items.
Independently draw \(\mathcal G\sim G_{n,p_{\mathrm{obs}}}\), where \(c_0\log n/n\leq p_{\mathrm{obs}}\leq1\) for a sufficiently large constant \(c_0\). For each edge, draw \(r\geq1\) fresh rankings from \(\mu\), independently across edges and repetitions, and retain only that edge's comparison.
The estimator receives the graph and edge win counts. Assume \(r\) is polynomially bounded in \(n\); recovery probability is over the graph, samples, and estimator randomness, uniformly over promised societies.

Is exact top-\(K\) recovery with probability at least \(1-Cn^{-2}\) achievable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? What sample threshold suffices, with running time stated in \(m=n\), \(\tau\), \(L\), \(r\), \(p_{\mathrm{obs}}\), \(K\), and \(\Delta_K\)?

The verified BTL result gives recovery at \(r\geq c\log n/(np_{\mathrm{obs}}\Delta_K^2)\), using \(O(|\mathcal E|\log^2 n)\) arithmetic operations on edge statistics. [Chen–Suh](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/chena15.pdf)
The independently sampled, retained comparisons specified here have exactly that observation model; consequently this statistical recovery question is already covered by the known result.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Conclusion

Exact top-\(K\) recovery is achievable in polynomial time under the stated sample condition. There are constants \(c,C>0\), depending only on the fixed strength bounds, such that
\(r\geq c\log n/(np_{\mathrm{obs}}\Delta_K^2)\)
suffices for success probability at least \(1-Cn^{-2}\).

The positive-gap assumption alone does not assert recovery for every \(r\). The conclusion is conditional on having sufficiently many comparisons.

1. The retained observations have exactly the BTL law

Fix a promised society. For an edge \(\{i,j\}\), orient the comparison toward \(i\), and let \(X_{ij}^{(s)}\) indicate that \(i\) defeats \(j\) in repetition \(s\). By the promise,
\(\Pr(X_{ij}^{(s)}=1)=q_{ij}=w_i/(w_i+w_j)\).

Each indicator is a function of a fresh ranking. Independence of the ranking draws therefore implies independence of all retained indicators across edges and repetitions, conditional on the graph. Consequently, the win count
\(S_{ij}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}X_{ij}^{(s)}\)
has distribution
\(S_{ij}\mid\mathcal G\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(r,w_i/(w_i+w_j))\),
and these counts are conditionally independent across edges.

In particular, the conditional joint probability of any count vector is
\(\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\mathcal E}\binom{r}{S_{ij}}q_{ij}^{S_{ij}}(1-q_{ij})^{r-S_{ij}}\).

This law depends on the society only through its pairwise probabilities. Dependence among comparisons within a single complete ranking has no effect here, because each draw supplies only one retained comparison.

2. Application of the verified recovery theorem

The Chen–Suh theorem applies to independent BTL comparisons on an Erdős–Rényi graph. With fixed bounded strength ratio, polynomially bounded repetitions, and \(p_{\mathrm{obs}}\geq c_0\log n/n\), it gives exact recovery with probability at least \(1-Cn^{-2}\) when
\(r\geq c\log n/(np_{\mathrm{obs}}\Delta_K^2)\).
Its algorithm takes edge averages as input and uses \(O(|\mathcal E|\log^2 n)\) arithmetic operations. See [Theorem 1 and Remark 1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/chena15.pdf).

Every hypothesis holds here:

  • Section 1 establishes the required conditional independence and BTL probabilities.
  • The comparison graph has the stipulated distribution and observation probability.
  • The strength ratio satisfies \(w_{\max}/w_{\min}\leq b/a\), a fixed constant.
  • The repetition count \(r\) is polynomially bounded.
  • Taking the theorem’s strength bounds to be \(a,b\) gives exactly the specified separation \(\Delta_K\).

The estimator computes \(S_{ij}/r\) and applies the theorem’s algorithm. It needs neither the strengths nor the society support.

Internal ties cause no ambiguity: the strict boundary gap ensures that
\(T=\{i:w_i>w_{(K+1)}\}\)
has exactly \(K\) elements. The required output is this unordered set.

Because the theorem is uniform over admissible strength vectors, and the observation law depends only on those vectors, its guarantee is also uniform over all promised societies satisfying the sample condition.

3. Sample threshold and running time

An integer sufficient repetition count is
\(r_\star=\max\{1,\lceil c\log n/(np_{\mathrm{obs}}\Delta_K^2)\rceil\}\).

The actual number of retained comparisons is \(N=r|\mathcal E|\), with expectation
\(\mathbb E[N]=r\binom n2p_{\mathrm{obs}}\).

For completeness, let \(M=\binom n2p_{\mathrm{obs}}\). The edge count is binomial, and Chernoff bounds give
\(\Pr(|\mathcal E|\notin[M/2,2M])\leq 2e^{-M/12}\).
Since \(M\geq c_0(n-1)\log n/2\), this probability is \(O(n^{-2})\) for sufficiently large \(c_0\). Thus \(N=\Theta(rn^2p_{\mathrm{obs}})\) with that probability.

At \(r=r_\star\), a sufficient total sample budget is therefore
\(N=O(n^2p_{\mathrm{obs}}+n\log n/\Delta_K^2)\)
with probability at least \(1-O(n^{-2})\). The first term accounts for the requirement of at least one comparison per observed edge.

Using an edge-list representation and allowing a final sort of estimated strengths, the estimator uses
\(O(m\log m+|\mathcal E|\log^2 m+K)\)
arithmetic operations, where \(m=n\). Hence its running time is
\(O(m^2p_{\mathrm{obs}}\log^2 m)\)
with probability at least \(1-O(m^{-2})\), and \(O(m^2\log^2 m)\) in the worst case.

There is no dependence on the hidden \(\tau\) or \(L\). Once counts are supplied, there is no factor \(r\) in the arithmetic-operation bound; \(r\) determines the counts’ bit length. The edge-list input occupies \(O(m+|\mathcal E|(\log m+\log(r+1)))\) bits. The quoted running-time bound counts arithmetic operations, rather than individual bit operations. If raw comparison outcomes must first be aggregated, that preprocessing additionally takes \(O(r|\mathcal E|)\) time.

Thus the stated recovery problem is covered by the supplied polynomial-time statistical result. No computation over permutations or reconstruction of the society is required.
