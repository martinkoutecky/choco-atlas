The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates, a finite support \(T_+\) of strict rankings with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and a rational threshold \(B\), decide whether there is a strict ranking \(\ell\) such that \(r_a(\mu)\le 2\) and \(\sum_{t\in T_+}\mu_t\,\mathrm{KT}(\ell,t)\le B\), where \(r_a(\mu)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_c\left(1+\max_{t\in T_+}\operatorname{pos}_t(c)-\min_{t\in T_+}\operatorname{pos}_t(c)\right)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge 1\) candidates. A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T_+\) of \(\tau\) distinct strict rankings of \(C\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Let \(\operatorname{pos}_t(c)\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) be the position of \(c\), with the best candidate at position \(0\), and let \(\mathrm{KT}\) denote Kendall–Tau distance. Define \(r_a(\mu)=\frac1m\sum_{c\in C}(1+\max_{t\in T_+}\operatorname{pos}_t(c)-\min_{t\in T_+}\operatorname{pos}_t(c))\).
Given this society and a positive binary rational threshold \(B\), decide whether there exists a strict ranking \(\ell\) of \(C\) satisfying both \(r_a(\mu)\le 2\) and \(\sum_{t\in T_+}\mu_t\mathrm{KT}(\ell,t)\le B\). Instances with \(r_a(\mu)>2\) are rejected; any qualifying ranking is allowed.

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For explicitly listed unweighted votes, Kemeny Score is NP-complete even under the restriction \(r_a\le 2\). [Betzler et al.](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas09/pdf/01_Full%20Papers/11_25_140_FP_0418.pdf)
No result explicitly treating this precise rational-mass formulation was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified a nonempty candidate set and support so that the average and extrema are defined.
  • Fixed positions to run from zero for the best candidate to m−1 for the worst, following the discrete definition.
  • Restricted the rational threshold to B>0, carrying over the discrete input condition k>0.
  • Defined L as total input bit length, including the rankings, masses, and threshold.
  • Treated the range bound as a conjunct of the decision predicate, as stated in the graded question, so inputs exceeding it are rejected.
  • Allowed any qualifying strict ranking, without a tie-breaking rule.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) exists if and only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Here, “binary rational” means a rational number whose numerator and denominator are encoded in binary.

Polynomial-time reduction

Use the stated hardness result for explicitly listed unweighted votes with average range at most \(2\). The range definition in [Betzler et al., Section 2 and Theorem 3](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas09/pdf/01_Full%20Papers/11_25_140_FP_0418.pdf) agrees with the definition in the question: their candidate range is \(1+\max_v\operatorname{pos}_v(c)-\min_v\operatorname{pos}_v(c)\).

Let a source instance consist of \(n\ge 1\) explicitly listed votes \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) over \(m\) candidates and a positive integer threshold \(k\). Its question is whether some ranking \(\ell\) satisfies \(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{KT}(\ell,v_i)\le k\).

Group identical votes. Let \(T_+\) be the resulting list of \(\tau\) distinct rankings, and let \(n_t\) be the number of occurrences of ranking \(t\). Construct the target instance by setting
\(\mu_t=n_t/n\) and \(B=k/n\).

These masses are positive, and \(\sum_{t\in T_+}\mu_t=1\). The threshold is positive.

The range restriction is preserved exactly. For every candidate \(c\), removing repeated copies of rankings changes neither its minimum nor its maximum position. Hence
\(r_a(\mu)=\frac1m\sum_{c\in C}(1+\max_i\operatorname{pos}_{v_i}(c)-\min_i\operatorname{pos}_{v_i}(c))\).
Thus the source restriction \(r_a\le 2\) holds precisely when the target restriction does.

The score condition is preserved exactly. For every ranking \(\ell\),
\(\sum_{t\in T_+}\mu_t\mathrm{KT}(\ell,t)=\frac1n\sum_{t\in T_+}n_t\mathrm{KT}(\ell,t)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{KT}(\ell,v_i)\).
Since \(n>0\), the target score is at most \(B=k/n\) if and only if the source score is at most \(k\). This proves both directions of the reduction.

The construction has polynomial size and running time. Write \(L_{\mathrm{src}}\) for the source input length. Because the votes are explicitly listed, \(n\le L_{\mathrm{src}}\). Sorting the rankings, counting their multiplicities, and writing the fractions take polynomial time in \(L_{\mathrm{src}}\). Each mass requires \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits, and the threshold requires \(O(\log(k+1)+\log(n+1))\) bits. After numbering candidates, the target length satisfies
\(L=O(\tau m\log(m+1)+\tau\log(n+1)+\log(k+1)+\log(n+1))\).
In particular, the output is polynomially bounded in the source length.

The problem is therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate is a strict ranking \(\ell\), encoded in \(O(m\log(m+1))\) bits. Verify that it contains every candidate exactly once.

Compute all input positions and their extrema. The range condition is equivalent to the integer inequality
\(\sum_{c\in C}(1+\max_t\operatorname{pos}_t(c)-\min_t\operatorname{pos}_t(c))\le 2m\).
It is therefore checked exactly, without rational arithmetic.

For each \(t\), compute \(d_t=\mathrm{KT}(\ell,t)\) by counting unordered candidate pairs whose orders differ. This uses \(O(\tau m^2)\) pair comparisons, and \(0\le d_t\le \binom m2\).

Write \(\mu_t=p_t/q_t\) and \(B=a/b\), with positive integer denominators. Define \(Q=b\prod_{t\in T_+}q_t\). Because the total denominator encoding length is at most \(L\), the bit length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\). The score condition is exactly
\(\sum_{t\in T_+}p_t d_t(Q/q_t)\le a(Q/b)\).
Every division is exact. Since the masses are positive and sum to \(1\), the left side is at most \(Q\binom m2\); all arithmetic therefore uses \(O(L+\log(m+1))\)-bit integers.

Using ordinary integer arithmetic, a sufficient bound for certificate verification is
\(O((\tau m^2+\tau+m)(L+\log(m+1))^2)\)
bit operations. This is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), proving membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Conclusion

Combining the reduction with certificate verification proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for the precise support-based rational-mass formulation. The transfer uses aggregation and normalization of explicitly listed votes; it does not require expanding arbitrary rational masses into individual votes.
