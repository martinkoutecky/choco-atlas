The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\lambda_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational entitlement rates \(b_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_t\lambda_t b_t=1\), and a non-atomic probability valuation \(\nu_t\) for each type specified by a finite rational piecewise-constant density, let \(D\) be a common denominator of the masses. For each \(N=kD\), form \(N\lambda_t\) agents of type \(t\), each requiring value at least \(b_t/N\), and let \(K_N\) be the minimum number of cuts in a division meeting every individual requirement. Determine or approximate to additive error \(\varepsilon\) the asymptotic cut rate \(\kappa(\lambda,\nu,b)=\limsup_{k\to\infty} K_{kD}/(kD)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\lambda_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The cake is \([0,1]\). Each type has a binary-rational entitlement rate \(b_t\geq0\), with \(\sum_t\lambda_t b_t=1\), and a probability valuation \(\nu_t\) given by a nonnegative piecewise-constant density with rational breakpoints and heights, integrating to \(1\).
Let \(m\) be the number of intervals in the common refinement of all density breakpoints, and \(L\) the total binary input length, including a rational accuracy parameter \(0<\varepsilon<1\).
Let \(D\) be the least common denominator of the masses. For each \(N=kD\geq2\), create \(N\lambda_t\) distinct agents of type \(t\), each requiring an allocation \(X\) with \(\nu_t(X)\geq b_t/N\).
A division uses finitely many distinct cuts in \((0,1)\) and assigns every resulting interval to exactly one agent; an agent may receive several intervals, and zero-demand agents may receive nothing. Endpoint assignments do not affect valuations.
Let \(K_N\) be the minimum number of cuts among such divisions, and define \(\kappa(\lambda,\nu,b)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}K_{kD}/(kD)\).

Is computing \(\kappa(\lambda,\nu,b)\) to additive error \(\varepsilon\) possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this approximation problem NP-hard?

For finitely many agents with nonnegative demands summing to \(1\), a constructive procedure guarantees at most \(3n-4\) cuts for \(n\geq2\); some instances require \(2n-2\) cuts. [Crew–Narayanan–Spirkl](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~narayanan/pdf/disprop_div.pdf)
No classification of the replicated-type asymptotic approximation problem above was located in the literature search.

  • Defined divisions as full allocations of cut intervals, permitting disconnected allocations and empty allocations for zero-demand agents; cuts count distinct interior positions.
  • Chose the least common denominator of the masses and omitted the possible initial one-agent instance.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The problem is polynomial-time solvable, and the value can be computed exactly:

\( \displaystyle \kappa(\lambda,\nu,b)=\sum_{t:\,b_t>0}\lambda_t. \)

In fact, the limsup in the definition is a limit. The proof gives matching finite bounds with an additive gap independent of the replication size.

1. Notation and lower bound.

Let \( P=\{t\in T:b_t>0\} \), \( s=|P| \), and \( \rho=\sum_{t\in P}\lambda_t \). Since \( \sum_t\lambda_t b_t=1 \), we have \( s\geq1 \).

Fix an allowed population size \( N=kD\geq2 \). Write \( n_t=N\lambda_t \), which is a positive integer, and let \( n_+=\sum_{t\in P}n_t=N\rho \) be the number of positive-demand agents.

A division with \( K \) cuts produces \( K+1 \) intervals. Each positive-demand agent must receive at least one of these intervals: an allocation consisting only of endpoints has valuation zero, because the valuations have densities. Since each interval belongs to exactly one agent, distinct positive-demand agents require distinct intervals. Consequently,

\( \displaystyle K_N\geq n_+-1=N\rho-1. \)

2. A construction with bounded additional cuts.

Write the common refinement as intervals \( I_1,\ldots,I_m \), with lengths \( \ell_j>0 \). Let \( h_{tj} \) be the constant density of type \( t \) on \( I_j \). Normalization gives

\( \displaystyle \sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{tj}\ell_j=1 \qquad(t\in T). \)

For each \( t\in P \), define \( q_t=\lambda_t b_t \). These numbers are positive and satisfy \( \sum_{t\in P}q_t=1 \).

Within every refinement interval \( I_j \), place consecutive blocks \( B_{tj} \), one for each \( t\in P \) in a fixed order, with lengths

\( \displaystyle |B_{tj}|=q_t\ell_j. \)

Thus these blocks partition \( I_j \), up to endpoints. Put \( B_t=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}B_{tj} \). The sets \( B_t \) partition the cake, up to endpoints, and piecewise constancy implies

\( \displaystyle \nu_t(B_t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{tj}|B_{tj}|=q_t\sum_{j=1}^{m}h_{tj}\ell_j=q_t. \)

This is exactly the aggregate requirement of the agents of type \( t \), because

\( \displaystyle n_t\frac{b_t}{N}=\lambda_t b_t=q_t. \)

It remains to divide \( B_t \) among those agents.

Define the cumulative restricted valuation

\( \displaystyle F_t(x)=\nu_t(B_t\cap[0,x]),\qquad 0\leq x\leq1. \)

This function is continuous and nondecreasing, with \( F_t(0)=0 \) and \( F_t(1)=q_t \). For each \( r\in\{1,\ldots,n_t-1\} \), continuity therefore supplies a point \( z_{t,r} \) satisfying

\( \displaystyle F_t(z_{t,r})=\frac{r b_t}{N}. \)

These points are strictly ordered: their function values are strictly increasing, and \( F_t \) is nondecreasing. Set \( z_{t,0}=0 \) and \( z_{t,n_t}=1 \).

Give the \( r \)-th agent of type \( t \) the allocation

\( \displaystyle X_{t,r}=B_t\cap[z_{t,r-1},z_{t,r}]. \)

Assign endpoints consistently to eliminate overlaps. Endpoints have zero valuation, so each such agent receives exactly

\( \displaystyle \nu_t(X_{t,r})=F_t(z_{t,r})-F_t(z_{t,r-1})=\frac{b_t}{N}. \)

Zero-demand agents receive nothing.

To realize this allocation as a division of the specified form, cut at every boundary between the initial blocks and at every \( z_{t,r} \). Each resulting interval lies in one initial block and between two successive thresholds for its type, so it belongs entirely to one agent.

There are \( ms \) initial blocks, requiring at most \( ms-1 \) cuts. The thresholds add at most

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in P}(n_t-1)=n_+-s \)

cuts. Coincident cuts are counted only once. Therefore,

\( \displaystyle K_N\leq ms-1+n_+-s=N\rho+s(m-1)-1. \)

All allocations are finite unions of intervals. Moreover, the block boundaries are rational, and the functions \( F_t \) are piecewise linear with rational data, so rational threshold points can be chosen.

3. Limit and computational complexity.

Combining the bounds gives, for every allowed \( N \),

\( \displaystyle \rho-\frac1N\leq\frac{K_N}{N}\leq\rho+\frac{s(m-1)-1}{N}. \)

For the fixed input society, \( m \) and \( s \) are independent of \( N \). Both bounds converge to \( \rho \) as \( N=kD \) tends to infinity. Hence

\( \displaystyle \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{K_{kD}}{kD}=\rho, \)

which proves the claimed formula for \( \kappa \).

The algorithm simply identifies the types with \( b_t>0 \) and sums their masses exactly. Zero testing and rational addition take polynomial time in the binary input length; the resulting rational also has polynomial encoding length. Thus the requested additive approximation is computable in time polynomial in \( m,\tau,L \), with error zero.
