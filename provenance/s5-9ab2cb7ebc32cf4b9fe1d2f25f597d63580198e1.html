The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 2.1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given projects \(P\), costs \(c(p)\), a budget \(B\), \(\lambda\), a finite support \(T\) of complete utility vectors \(u_t:P\to\mathbb{N}\) with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\geq 0\) summing to \(1\), and a rational target \(q\), does there exist \(S\subseteq P\) with \(\sum_{p\in S}c(p)\leq B\) such that \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\,\mathrm{sat}^{\lambda}_t(S)\geq q\)? Here \(\mathrm{sat}^{\lambda}_t(S)=0\) when \(|S|<\lambda\), and otherwise it is the \(\lambda\)-th largest utility \(u_t(p)\) among \(p\in S\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a finite project set \(P\) with \(m=|P|\), positive integer costs \(c(p)\), a positive integer budget \(B\), a positive integer \(\lambda\), and a rational threshold \(q\), assume \(c(p)\leq B\) for every project.
A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) distinct complete utility vectors \(u_t:P\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). The parameter \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
All integers are binary encoded; let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including \(B\) and \(\lambda\). Measure running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and any additional parameters used.
For an indivisible bundle \(S\subseteq P\), define \(\mathrm{sat}^{\lambda}_t(S)=0\) if \(|S|<\lambda\); otherwise it is the \(\lambda\)-th largest value in the multiset \(\{u_t(p):p\in S\}\). Equal utilities are allowed and counted with multiplicity, so tie-breaking does not affect satisfaction.
The decision problem asks whether some \(S\subseteq P\) satisfies \(\sum_{p\in S}c(p)\leq B\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\mathrm{sat}^{\lambda}_t(S)\geq q\).

Is this decision problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For an explicitly listed finite society, the corresponding decision problem with total, unweighted satisfaction is NP-hard for every fixed positive integer \(\lambda\).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Resolved the unspecified cost and budget domains as positive integers, following the discrete model's use of costs at least one; retained its assumption that every project costs at most the budget.
  • Resolved the ambiguous natural-number utility domain as nonnegative integers, since zero utility is explicitly permitted in the discrete model.
  • Specified that lambda is a positive integer input; values exceeding the number of projects retain the stated zero-satisfaction convention.
  • Specified binary encoding for all integer data and defined L as total input bit length.
  • Removed zero-mass entries and consolidated identical utility vectors so that the society is represented by its positive-mass support.
  • Made explicit that equal utility values count with multiplicity and require no tie-breaking.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The decision problem is NP-complete, and it is NP-hard for every fixed positive integer \(\lambda\). This holds even when every utility belongs to \(\{0,1\}\), the threshold is \(q=1\), and all masses are positive dyadic rationals.

Membership in NP

A certificate is a subset \(S\subseteq P\), represented by \(m\) bits.

Its total cost can be computed and compared with \(B\) using polynomial-time integer arithmetic. For each support type, if \(|S|<\lambda\), its satisfaction is zero. Otherwise, sorting the selected utilities determines its \(\lambda\)-th largest utility in polynomial time. This procedure does not require iterating up to the binary-encoded value of \(\lambda\).

The weighted sum can also be compared with \(q\) exactly in polynomial time. Indeed, a common denominator obtained by multiplying the input denominators has bit length at most the sum of their bit lengths, hence polynomial in \(L\). The resulting numerators likewise have polynomial bit length.

Thus verification takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), establishing membership in NP.

Reduction from 3-SAT

Fix any positive integer \(\lambda\). We reduce from the standard NP-complete problem 3-SAT.

Let the input formula have variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), where \(n\geq 1\), and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_r\).

Projects, costs, and budget

For each variable \(x_i\), introduce two literal projects \(p_i^+\) and \(p_i^-\), each of cost \(\lambda\). Introduce \(\lambda-1\) auxiliary projects, each of cost \(1\). When \(\lambda=1\), there are no auxiliary projects.

Set the budget to

\(B=\lambda n+\lambda-1.\)

Every project has positive integer cost at most \(B\), since \(n\geq 1\).

Utility vectors

Create the following complete utility vectors, with all unspecified utilities equal to zero:

  • For each variable \(x_i\), a vector assigning utility \(1\) to \(p_i^+\), \(p_i^-\), and every auxiliary project.
  • For each clause \(C_j\), a vector assigning utility \(1\) to every literal project corresponding to a literal occurring in \(C_j\), and to every auxiliary project.

Remove duplicate vectors, retaining one representative of each distinct vector. Let the resulting support size be \(\tau\). It is positive because there is at least one variable vector.

Let \(K\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(K\geq\tau\). After indexing the distinct vectors, assign masses

\(\mu_t=1/K\) for \(1\leq t<\tau\), and \(\mu_\tau=(K-\tau+1)/K\).

These masses are positive dyadic rationals and satisfy

\(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=((\tau-1)+(K-\tau+1))/K=1.\)

Set \(q=1\).

For fixed \(\lambda\), the construction has \(m=2n+\lambda-1\) projects and at most \(n+r\) support vectors. Duplicate removal and the complete output encoding take polynomial time in the formula length. Each mass requires only \(O(\log(\tau+1))\) bits.

Correctness of the reduction

For a utility vector with values in \(\{0,1\}\), satisfaction equals \(1\) exactly when the selected bundle contains at least \(\lambda\) projects of utility \(1\). Otherwise, satisfaction equals zero.

Consequently, every support type has satisfaction at most \(1\). Because the masses are positive and sum to \(1\), the weighted satisfaction is at least \(q=1\) if and only if every support type has satisfaction \(1\). Removing duplicate vectors therefore preserves precisely the conjunction of all original satisfaction requirements.

A satisfying assignment gives a feasible bundle

Suppose the formula has a satisfying assignment. Select all \(\lambda-1\) auxiliary projects and, for each variable, select its positive literal project if the variable is true and its negative literal project otherwise.

The total cost is

\(\lambda n+\lambda-1=B.\)

Each variable vector has utility \(1\) on its selected literal project and all auxiliary projects, giving \(\lambda\) selected projects of utility \(1\).

Each clause has a true literal. Its vector therefore also has utility \(1\) on at least one selected literal project and all auxiliary projects, again giving at least \(\lambda\) selected projects of utility \(1\).

Every support type has satisfaction \(1\), so the bundle meets the threshold.

A feasible bundle meeting the threshold gives a satisfying assignment

Conversely, suppose \(S\) is feasible and meets the threshold. Every original variable and clause vector then has satisfaction \(1\), by the preceding characterization and the preservation of requirements under duplicate removal.

Let \(a\) be the number of selected literal projects and \(d\) the number of selected auxiliary projects. We have \(d\leq\lambda-1\), and feasibility gives

\(\lambda a+d\leq\lambda n+\lambda-1.\)

If \(a\geq n+1\), its literal projects alone would cost at least \(\lambda(n+1)>\lambda n+\lambda-1\). Hence \(a\leq n\).

For each variable \(x_i\), its vector has satisfaction \(1\). Since there are at most \(\lambda-1\) auxiliary projects, at least one of \(p_i^+\) and \(p_i^-\) must belong to \(S\). These pairs are disjoint, so \(a\geq n\).

Thus \(a=n\), and exactly one literal project is selected for each variable. Define an assignment by setting \(x_i\) true precisely when \(p_i^+\) is selected.

For each clause vector, satisfaction \(1\) requires at least one selected literal project corresponding to that clause: auxiliary projects alone cannot supply \(\lambda\) projects of utility \(1\). That selected literal is true under the assignment just defined. Every clause is therefore satisfied.

Conclusion

The construction is a polynomial-time many-one reduction for every fixed positive integer \(\lambda\). Together with membership in NP, it proves NP-completeness. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
