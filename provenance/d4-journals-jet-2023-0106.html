The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a unit-mass population with private signal \(x\in\{0,1\}\), masses \(\mu_x=1/2\), the paper’s signal precisions \(p,r\), and a single unit of prize mass, compute the optimal BIC allocation-rate density \(a_{b,y}\ge 0\), where \(b\) is the reported self-signal and \(y\) the referee report. Require \(\sum_{x,y}\nu_{xy}a_{x,y}=1\), where \(\nu_{xy}=\mu_x\Pr(Y=y\mid X=x)\), and for each \(x\in\{0,1\}\), require \(\sum_y\Pr(Y=y\mid X=x)a_{x,y}\ge\sum_y\Pr(Y=y\mid X=x)a_{1-x,y}\). Maximize \(\sum_{x,y}\nu_{xy}q_{xy}a_{x,y}\), where \(q_{xy}=\Pr(\Theta=1\mid X=x,Y=y)\); determine the optimum and compare it with the optimum under DSIC constraints.

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here the population has unit mass, private signals \(x\in\{0,1\}\), and \(\mu_0=\mu_1=1/2\), so \(\tau=2\).
Input precisions are binary rationals satisfying \(1/2<r\le p<1\). Latent suitability \(\Theta\in\{0,1\}\) has equal prior probabilities; conditional on \(\Theta\), private signal \(X\) and nonstrategic referee report \(Y\) are independent, with \(\Pr(X=\theta\mid\Theta=\theta)=p\) and \(\Pr(Y=\theta\mid\Theta=\theta)=r\) for both \(\theta\).
Write \(c_{xy}=\Pr(Y=y\mid X=x)\), \(\nu_{xy}=\mu_xc_{xy}\), and \(q_{xy}=\Pr(\Theta=1\mid X=x,Y=y)\). All these probabilities are determined by the specified information structure.
An anonymous mechanism without transfers assigns a finite nonnegative prize density \(a_{b,y}\) to reported private signal \(b\) and referee report \(y\); utility is proportional to this density. Densities may exceed one. One unit of prize mass must be allocated: \(\sum_{x,y}\nu_{xy}a_{x,y}=1\).
BIC requires \(\sum_y c_{xy}a_{x,y}\ge\sum_y c_{xy}a_{1-x,y}\) for each \(x\). The DSIC benchmark instead requires \(a_{x,y}\ge a_{1-x,y}\) for every \(x,y\). Indifference permits truthful reporting.
Let \(V_B(p,r)\) and \(V_D(p,r)\) be the respective maxima of \(\sum_{x,y}\nu_{xy}q_{xy}a_{x,y}\). For complexity, include a binary rational threshold \(t\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length and \(m=2\) the signal alphabet size.

Is deciding \(V_B(p,r)\ge t\) in P or NP-hard, with running times expressed in \(m,\tau,L\)?
What are \(V_B(p,r)\), its maximizing densities, and its comparison with \(V_D(p,r)\) throughout \(1/2<r\le p<1\)?

For finite populations of \(n\ge2\) agents, optimal anonymous BIC selection is characterized by shortlisting maximal referee reports and adjusting tie-breaking using self-reports; optimal BIC value strictly exceeds optimal DSIC value in this precision domain. 1. Result

Throughout the specified domain \(1/2<r\le p<1\),
\[ V_B(p,r)=V_D(p,r)=r. \]
The unique maximizing density under either incentive requirement is
\[ a_{0,0}=a_{1,0}=0,\qquad a_{0,1}=a_{1,1}=2. \]
Thus deciding \(V_B(p,r)\ge t\) belongs to \(\mathrm P\): it amounts to comparing \(r\) and \(t\).

Here and below, the density model is interpreted exactly as stated, with finite nonnegative densities and the single aggregate prize-mass constraint.

2. Probabilities determined by the information structure

Define
\[ s=pr+(1-p)(1-r),\qquad d=p(1-r)+(1-p)r. \]
Then \(s+d=1\), \(d>0\), and
\[ s-\frac12=2\left(p-\frac12\right)\left(r-\frac12\right)>0. \]
Consequently, \(s>d>0\).

The symmetric prior and signal accuracy imply \(\Pr(X=0)=\Pr(X=1)=1/2\). Conditional independence given \(\Theta\) therefore gives
\[ (c_{xy})_{x,y\in\{0,1\}} = \begin{pmatrix} s&d\\ d&s \end{pmatrix}, \qquad (\nu_{xy})_{x,y\in\{0,1\}} = \frac12 \begin{pmatrix} s&d\\ d&s \end{pmatrix}. \]
Bayes’ rule gives
\[ (q_{xy})_{x,y\in\{0,1\}} = \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{(1-p)(1-r)}s&\dfrac{(1-p)r}d\\[4pt] \dfrac{p(1-r)}d&\dfrac{pr}s \end{pmatrix}. \]
All denominators are strictly positive.

Write \(A=(1-p)(1-r)\) and \(B=pr\). The objective of a density \(a\) is consequently
\[ J(a)=\frac12\left[Aa_{0,0}+(1-p)r\,a_{0,1} +p(1-r)a_{1,0}+Ba_{1,1}\right]. \]

3. A complete upper bound for BIC

For any BIC density, define
\[ z=a_{0,0}-a_{1,0},\qquad w=a_{1,1}-a_{0,1}. \]
The incentive constraints for private signals \(0\) and \(1\), respectively, become
\[ sz\ge dw,\qquad sw\ge dz. \]
These inequalities force both differences to be nonnegative. Indeed,
\[ s^2z\ge sdw\ge d^2z, \]
so \((s^2-d^2)z\ge0\), and hence \(z\ge0\). Interchanging \(z\) and \(w\) gives \(w\ge0\).

Set
\[ u=a_{1,0}\ge0,\qquad v=a_{0,1}\ge0,\qquad h=z-\frac ds w\ge0. \]
Thus the four densities have the form
\[ (a_{0,0},a_{0,1},a_{1,0},a_{1,1}) =(u+z,v,u,v+w). \]
The prize-mass constraint becomes
\[ \frac12\left[u+v+s(z+w)\right]=1, \]
while the objective becomes
\[ J(a)=\frac12\left[(1-r)u+rv+Az+Bw\right]. \]

Subtracting this objective from \(r\) times the normalized prize mass yields
\[ r-J(a) =\frac12\left[(2r-1)u+(rs-A)z+(rs-B)w\right]. \]
Substitute \(z=h+(d/s)w\). Using \(s+d=1\), this gives
\[ r-J(a) =\frac12\left[(2r-1)u+(rs-A)h+ \left(r-B-\frac{Ad}s\right)w\right]. \]
The last coefficient simplifies explicitly:
\[ r-B-\frac{Ad}s =\frac{1-p}{s}\left[rs-(1-r)d\right] =\frac{1-p}{s}\left[s-(1-r)\right] =\frac{p(1-p)(2r-1)}s. \]
We have therefore proved the exact identity
\[ r-J(a) =\frac12\left[ (2r-1)u+(rs-A)h+ \frac{p(1-p)(2r-1)}s\,w \right]. \]

Every coefficient on the right is strictly positive. For the middle coefficient, \(r>1/2\) and \(s>1/2\) imply \(rs>1/4\), whereas \(p>1/2\) and \(r>1/2\) imply \(A<1/4\). Since \(u,h,w\) are nonnegative, this proves \(J(a)\le r\).

Moreover, equality requires \(u=h=w=0\). Then \(z=0\), and normalization forces \(v=2\). Hence any BIC maximizer must be precisely
\[ (a_{0,0},a_{0,1},a_{1,0},a_{1,1})=(0,2,0,2). \]
This density is feasible: both reports give identical utility for every referee report, and its prize mass is \(2\Pr(Y=1)=1\). Its objective is \(r\). Thus the BIC bound is attained, and the maximizer is unique.

4. DSIC and comparison with the finite-population result

DSIC imposes both \(a_{0,y}\ge a_{1,y}\) and \(a_{1,y}\ge a_{0,y}\). Therefore
\[ a_{0,0}=a_{1,0}=u,\qquad a_{0,1}=a_{1,1}=v. \]
Normalization and the objective reduce to
\[ \frac{u+v}{2}=1,\qquad J(a)=\frac{(1-r)u+rv}{2}. \]
Because \(r>1-r\), the unique optimum is \(u=0\), \(v=2\), with value \(r\).

Consequently, the BIC–DSIC gap is zero everywhere in the stated precision domain, including \(p=r\).

This does not contradict the cited finite-population result. That paper studies selection probabilities assigning one indivisible prize among finitely many contestants, as functions of their report profiles. Its feasibility requirement differs from the aggregate density constraint used here. [Bloch, Dutta and Dziubiński, *Selecting a Winner with External Referees*](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/176793/1/WRAP-selecting-winner-external-referees-Dutta-2023.pdf)

5. Exact decision algorithm and running time

On the promised input domain, the algorithm returns “yes” exactly when \(r\ge t\).

If \(r=N_r/D_r\) and \(t=N_t/D_t\), with positive denominators, this is the exact integer comparison
\[ N_rD_t\ge N_tD_r. \]
Using schoolbook multiplication, it takes \(O(L^2)\) bit operations and \(O(L)\) space. For dyadic rationals in explicit binary representation, comparison can instead be performed in \(O(L)\) bit operations.

In this family \(m=\tau=2\), so the bound is \(O(L^2)\), polynomial in \((m,\tau,L)\). The direct density formulation has \(\tau m=4\) variables and \(\tau(m-1)=2\) BIC inequalities; the explicit solution eliminates the need to solve that linear program. No enumeration of unsupported types is involved.
