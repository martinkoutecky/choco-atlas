Status and input model

Under uniform polynomial-time demand evaluation, the stated mass-flow problem is $\mathsf{PPAD}$-complete under polynomial-time search reductions. This does not resolve the requested alternative between polynomial-time solvability and $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness.

The phrase “a polynomial running-time guarantee” requires care. Two interpretations give different conclusions:

  • Uniform evaluation: There are fixed constants $C,d$, independent of the supplied demand program, such that a query of bit length $b$ is evaluated in at most $C(L+b)^d$ steps.
  • Individual polynomial guarantees: Each supplied program has a polynomial bound in its query length, but that polynomial may have arbitrarily large binary-encoded coefficients.

I treat the first as the intended computational model. Under the second interpretation, I prove $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness separately below.

The main additional quantitative result is a deterministic algorithm using $O(1/\epsilon)$ demand queries, improving the quadratic enumeration bound in the notes. All arguments needed from the notes are supplied here.

1. Fractional allocation reduces to three distance conditions

Write
$$ d_j(p)=\operatorname{dist}_1(p,P_j). $$

Proposition. For a fixed $p\in\Sigma_3$, a feasible allocation matrix exists if and only if
$$ d_j(p)\leq\epsilon \qquad\text{for every }j\in\{1,2,3\}. $$

Proof. If $x$ is feasible, column $j$ has nonnegative entries summing to $1/3$. Consequently, some entry in that column is positive. The support condition for that entry implies $d_j(p)\leq\epsilon$.

Conversely, suppose all three distance inequalities hold. Define
$$ x_{aj}= \begin{cases} 1/3,&a=j,\\ 0,&a\neq j. \end{cases} $$
Every row and column sums to $1/3$, and every positive entry satisfies the required distance condition. $\square$

Thus the fractional allocation introduces no additional difficulty or flexibility: the search task is exactly to find a permitted price near all three demand sets.

We also need the following fact when transferring the discrete hardness theorem.

Distance attainment. Every $P_j$, for $j\in\{1,2,3\}$, is nonempty, and its distance from any point is attained.

To prove nonemptiness, consider the price with coordinate $j$ equal to zero and the other coordinates equal to one. Positive-category coverage requires this price to belong to some $P_k$. Price exclusion rules out every $k\neq j$, so it belongs to $P_j$.

Now fix $p$ and let $r=d_j(p)$. Choose $q_n\in P_j$ with
$$ \|p-q_n\|_1\leq r+1/n. $$
This sequence is bounded. A subsequence therefore converges in finite-dimensional space to a point $q$. Closedness gives $q\in P_j$, and continuity of the norm gives
$$ \|p-q\|_1=r. $$
In particular, $d_j(p)\leq\epsilon$ supplies an actual witness at distance at most $\epsilon$, even though computing such a witness is not part of the output.

2. A rational grid construction

Define the simplex
$$ \Delta_2=\left\{y\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^3:\sum_{j=1}^3y_j=1\right\}. $$
For $y\in\Delta_2$, put
$$ M(y)=\max_j y_j, \qquad h(y)_j=1-\frac{y_j}{M(y)}. $$

Since $M(y)\geq1/3$, this map is well-defined. Its coordinates belong to $[0,1]$, and a coordinate maximizing $y_j$ maps to zero. Therefore
$$ h(y)\in\Sigma_3. $$

Define a label by
$$ c(y)=\min\{j\in\{1,2,3\}:f(h(y),j)=1\}. $$
Coverage of $\Sigma_3$ ensures that this label exists. If $y_j=0$, then $h(y)_j=1$, so price exclusion gives
$$ c(y)\neq j. $$
Thus $c$ obeys the Sperner boundary rule. Choosing the smallest available membership here is a computational operation; it adds no assumption about how indifferent agents behave.

Continuity bound

Let $y,z\in\Delta_2$, and write
$$ M=M(y),\qquad N=M(z),\qquad \delta=\|y-z\|_1. $$
We have $|M-N|\leq\delta$, $M,N\geq1/3$, and $\|z\|_1=1$. Hence
$$ \begin{aligned} \|h(y)-h(z)\|_1 &=\left\|\frac yM-\frac zN\right\|_1\\ &\leq\frac{\|y-z\|_1}{M} +\left|\frac1M-\frac1N\right|\|z\|_1\\ &=\frac{\delta}{M}+\frac{|M-N|}{MN}\\ &\leq12\delta. \end{aligned} $$

No continuity assumption on the demand algorithm is used.

Grid and output decoder

Choose $K$ and $D$ to be the least powers of two satisfying
$$ K\geq\frac{48}{\epsilon}, \qquad D\geq\frac6{\epsilon}. $$
Their sizes consequently satisfy
$$ \frac{48}{\epsilon}\leq K<\frac{96}{\epsilon}, \qquad \frac6{\epsilon}\leq D<\frac{12}{\epsilon}. $$

Triangulate $\Delta_2$ using vertices
$$ V_K=\left\{\frac{(i,j,K-i-j)}K: i,j\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\ i+j\leq K\right\}. $$

For completeness, in the coordinates $(i,j)$, its elementary triangles have vertices
$$ (i,j),\ (i+1,j),\ (i,j+1) \qquad\text{when }i+j\leq K-1, $$
or
$$ (i+1,j),\ (i,j+1),\ (i+1,j+1) \qquad\text{when }i+j\leq K-2. $$
The third simplex coordinate is determined by the first two. There are
$$ \frac{K(K+1)}2+\frac{K(K-1)}2=K^2 $$
elementary triangles. Each has $\ell_1$-diameter $2/K$.

Suppose a trichromatic triangle has vertices $v^1,v^2,v^3$, labeled $1,2,3$, respectively. Put
$$ q^j=h(v^j), \qquad p_0=q^1. $$
By the definition of the labels, $q^j\in P_j$. Moreover,
$$ \|p_0-q^j\|_1 \leq12\|v^1-v^j\|_1 \leq\frac{24}{K} \leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}. $$

Produce a dyadic price vector by rounding downward:
$$ p_j=\frac{\lfloor D(p_0)_j\rfloor}{D}. $$
The coordinate bounds and an existing zero coordinate are preserved, so $p\in\Sigma_3$. Also,
$$ \|p-p_0\|_1\leq\frac3D\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}. $$
Consequently,
$$ d_j(p) \leq\|p-q^j\|_1 \leq\epsilon \qquad\text{for every }j. $$
The diagonal matrix from Section 1 completes the required output.

All witnesses constructed here lie in $\Sigma_3$, and hence are allowed by the task’s unrestricted ambient-space distance definition.

3. Finding the triangle with only linear grid complexity

The grid contains $K^2$ triangles, but inspecting all of them is unnecessary.

For a grid-aligned triangular region $T$, let $B(T)$ denote, modulo two, the number of elementary edges on its boundary whose endpoint labels are $1$ and $2$ in either order.

Initial parity

On the boundary side $y_1=0$, label $1$ is forbidden. On the side $y_2=0$, label $2$ is forbidden. Thus neither side contributes any such edge.

On the remaining side, $y_3=0$, only labels $1$ and $2$ occur. Its endpoints $e^1,e^2$ have labels $1,2$, respectively: at $e^i$, the other two labels are forbidden. Along a finite sequence of two labels, different endpoint labels imply an odd number of changes. Therefore
$$ B(\Delta_2)=1. $$

Recursive parity identity

Suppose $T$ has $s$ elementary grid edges along each side, where $s>1$ is a power of two. Join its three side midpoints. This partitions $T$ into four grid-aligned triangles $T_1,\ldots,T_4$, each having $s/2$ elementary edges per side.

Every elementary edge on the exterior boundary appears once among the four child boundaries. Every elementary edge on an internal boundary appears twice. It follows that
$$ B(T)=B(T_1)+B(T_2)+B(T_3)+B(T_4)\pmod2. $$
Hence, if $B(T)=1$, at least one child also has odd boundary parity.

The algorithm queries the labels on the four child boundaries, computes their parities, and continues with one odd child. The midpoint coordinates remain grid vertices because $s$ is even.

Termination and correctness

After $\log_2K$ subdivisions, the selected region is an elementary triangle with odd boundary parity.

For a triangle with three vertex labels:

  • if both labels $1$ and $2$ do not occur, it has zero boundary edges of the specified kind;
  • if its labels use exactly $1$ and $2$, it has two such edges;
  • if its labels are $1,2,3$, it has exactly one such edge.

Thus odd parity at the final triangle implies that it is trichromatic.

This also proves the required existence statement directly.

Number of queries

For a parent with side length $s$ in grid units, the four child boundaries contain at most
$$ 4\cdot3\left(\frac s2+1\right)=6s+12 $$
vertex occurrences. Duplicate queries may be avoided, but this is unnecessary for the bound.

Only one child is pursued at each level. Thus the total number of label evaluations is at most
$$ \sum_{r=0}^{\log_2K-1} \left(\frac{6K}{2^r}+12\right) =O(K). $$
Each label requires at most three demand queries. Therefore the total demand-query complexity is
$$ O(K)=O(1/\epsilon). $$

4. Running time and output length for the stated instance

Let $E_f(b)$ be a worst-case upper bound for evaluating the supplied demand program on a rational query of bit length at most $b$, including interpretation overhead.

A grid vertex has $O(\log K)$ bits per coordinate. Writing its coordinates as $a_j/K$ and $A=\max_j a_j$ gives
$$ h(y)_j=1-\frac{a_j}{A}. $$
Thus the membership queries also have total bit length $O(\log K)$, since there are three coordinates.

The recursive algorithm and its decoder therefore run in
$$ O\!\left( \epsilon^{-1} \left[ 3E_f\!\left(O(\log(1/\epsilon))\right) +\operatorname{poly}(L) \right] \right). $$
Under the uniform evaluation hypothesis, this becomes
$$ O\!\left(\epsilon^{-1}\operatorname{poly}(L)\right). $$

The output price has $O(\log(1/\epsilon))$ bits per coordinate. The allocation has nine entries drawn from $\{0,1/3\}$ and constant encoding length.

Here $m=\tau=3$, so both dimensions are fixed. In particular, no enumeration of possible types occurs. Since $\epsilon$ is binary encoded, $\epsilon^{-1}$ can be exponential in $L$; this is not a polynomial-time bound in the stated input length.

Exact existence, as a consequence

The premises also guarantee an exact common-demand price, though it need not be rational.

Indeed, apply the construction with tolerances tending to zero, obtaining prices $p_n\in\Sigma_3$ and witnesses $q_n^j\in P_j$ with
$$ \|p_n-q_n^j\|_1\longrightarrow0. $$
Compactness of $\Sigma_3$ supplies a convergent subsequence $p_{n_r}\to p^*\in\Sigma_3$. Then $q_{n_r}^j\to p^*$ for each $j$. Closedness gives
$$ p^*\in P_1\cap P_2\cap P_3. $$

5. Complexity under uniform demand evaluation

Membership by a polynomial-time search reduction

The construction in Section 2 produces a succinct two-dimensional Sperner instance: its input consists of binary-encoded $K$ and an algorithm evaluating $c$ on grid vertices.

Under uniform demand evaluation, this coloring algorithm runs in polynomial time in $L$ and the vertex encoding length. Its description has polynomial length, and $K$ has $O(\log(1/\epsilon))$ bits.

The standard search problem of finding a trichromatic cell in this binary-sized triangular grid is $\mathsf{PPAD}$-complete; this is precisely the grid and boundary convention stated in Theorem 4.6 of the supplied paper. [Lock, Qiu and Teytelboym, Section 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08484v2#S4.SS2).

Every trichromatic cell decodes in polynomial time to the valid $(p,x)$ constructed above. Hence the mass-flow task reduces to a $\mathsf{PPAD}$ search problem.

This is a statement about search reductions. It does not assume that the distance condition for an arbitrary submitted price can be verified efficiently.

Hardness transfer

The supplied paper establishes $\mathsf{PPAD}$-hardness of approximate HousingMarket with three agents having identical preferences. Its closedness, exclusion, coverage, and ambient $\ell_1$ approximation conditions match those used here. [Lock, Qiu and Teytelboym, Sections 2.1 and 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08484v2).

Take an instance from that hard family, with common demand function $f$ and tolerance $\eta>0$. Construct the stated mass-flow instance using the same $f$ and
$$ \epsilon=\min\{\eta,1/8\}. $$
This transformation is polynomial-time and ensures $0<\epsilon<1/4$.

Given any mass-flow solution $(p,x)$, Section 1 implies
$$ d_j(p)\leq\epsilon\leq\eta \qquad\text{for every }j. $$
Distance attainment supplies a point of each $P_j$ within distance $\eta$ of $p$. Assign discrete house $j$ to agent $j$. Because preferences are identical, this is an approximate discrete equilibrium.

The restriction $p\in\Sigma_3$ causes no problem: such a price is admissible in the discrete problem. The decoder merely outputs $p$ and the identity assignment.

Therefore the mass-flow formulation is $\mathsf{PPAD}$-hard already at $m=\tau=3$. Together with the preceding reduction, it is $\mathsf{PPAD}$-complete under uniform evaluation.

6. Explicit dependence on category and support sizes

Although the assigned problem fixes $m=\tau=3$, the role of support size can be made explicit in the analogous common-demand problem with variable $m$ and $\tau$.

Assume listed type masses $\mu_a$ and category supplies $s_j$ satisfy
$$ \mu_a\geq0,\qquad s_j\geq0,\qquad \sum_{a=1}^{\tau}\mu_a=\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_j=1. $$
Once every category is within $\epsilon$ of its demand set, the product allocation
$$ x_{aj}=\mu_a s_j $$
has the required margins. Constructing its $m\tau$ entries takes $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ bit operations.

Here is an explicit higher-dimensional price-search bound, under the corresponding coverage and exclusion assumptions.

Triangulation

Let $d=m-1$, with $m\geq2$. For $y\in\Delta_{m-1}$, use cumulative coordinates
$$ t_i=\sum_{j=1}^{i}y_j, \qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}. $$
Their domain is
$$ 0\leq t_1\leq\cdots\leq t_d\leq1. $$
The inverse map is
$$ y_1=t_1,\qquad y_j=t_j-t_{j-1}\quad(2\leq j\leq d), \qquad y_m=1-t_d. $$

Partition $[0,1]^d$ into cubes of side $1/K$. For each cube with integer base vector $k$ and each permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1,\ldots,d\}$, take the Kuhn simplex with vertices
$$ t^r=\frac1K \left(k+\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}e_{\sigma(\ell)}\right), \qquad r\in\{0,\ldots,d\}. $$
Retain the simplices contained in the ordered-coordinate domain.

These form a triangulation of that domain. To see coverage, take a point in a grid cube and order its fractional coordinates decreasingly. It belongs to the corresponding Kuhn simplex. When two base coordinates are equal, the ordered-domain inequalities order their fractional coordinates consistently; ties can be ordered so that the larger coordinate index is incremented first. Consequently, all vertices of the chosen simplex remain ordered. The usual Kuhn simplices meet along faces, as is also seen from their descriptions by weak orders of fractional coordinates.

There are at most
$$ d!K^d $$
retained simplices. Two points in one simplex differ by at most $1/K$ in every $t$ coordinate. The inverse map satisfies
$$ \|y(t)-y(u)\|_1 \leq2\sum_{i=1}^{d}|t_i-u_i|, $$
so each simplex has $\ell_1$-diameter at most $2d/K$ in $y$ coordinates.

Labels and approximation

Use
$$ h(y)_j=1-\frac{y_j}{\max_k y_k}. $$
Now $\max_k y_k\geq1/m$, and the calculation from Section 2 gives
$$ \|h(y)-h(z)\|_1 \leq m(m+1)\|y-z\|_1. $$
Coverage again supplies a label, and $y_j=0$ forbids label $j$.

Sperner’s lemma states that a triangulated simplex with this face-label restriction contains a simplex having all $m$ labels. Its hypotheses have just been verified.

Choose the least powers of two satisfying
$$ K\geq\frac{4m(m+1)(m-1)}{\epsilon}, \qquad D\geq\frac{2m}{\epsilon}. $$
In a fully labeled simplex, the mapped vertices then have pairwise distances at most $\epsilon/2$. Downward rounding of one mapped vertex to denominator $D$ adds at most $m/D\leq\epsilon/2$ and preserves a zero coordinate.

Thus exhaustive enumeration gives a valid price.

Each mapped membership query has bit length
$$ B=O\!\left(m\log(m/\epsilon)\right). $$
Without assuming any caching, each simplex requires at most $m^2$ membership queries: at most $m$ queries for each of its $m$ vertices. Hence a valid general bound is
$$ O\!\left( (m-1)! \left(\frac{C_0m^3}{\epsilon}\right)^{m-1} \left[m^2E_f(B)+\operatorname{poly}(m,L)\right] \right) +\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L), $$
for an absolute constant $C_0$.

This extension is for common demands. Its dependence on $\tau$ comes from processing the listed support and producing the allocation, rather than enumerating hypothetical types. For $m=3$, the parity algorithm gives the sharper bound in Section 4.

7. Why the uniform classification does not settle the requested alternatives

The established $\mathsf{PPAD}$-hardness implies that a polynomial-time algorithm here would give polynomial-time algorithms for every $\mathsf{PPAD}$ search problem. No such algorithm is proved in this document.

There is also a precise obstruction to $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness under the usual total, solution-preserving search reductions.

Conditional proposition. Suppose there is a polynomial-time reduction from satisfiability search that:

  • produces a promised-valid instance of this problem for every Boolean formula; and
  • on a satisfiable formula, decodes every solution into a satisfying assignment.

Under uniform demand evaluation, such a reduction implies
$$ \mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{coNP}. $$

Proof. Compose the hypothesized reduction with Section 5’s reduction to succinct two-dimensional Sperner.

For any Boolean formula $\varphi$, the resulting Sperner instance is valid and therefore has a trichromatic cell. Such a cell has polynomial encoding length, and its validity can be checked in polynomial time by evaluating its three labels and checking its grid coordinates.

Consider the following certificate for unsatisfiability: a valid trichromatic cell whose decoded assignment fails to satisfy $\varphi$.

If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, every valid cell decodes through the two reductions to a satisfying assignment, so no such certificate exists.

If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, a valid cell exists, and every decoded assignment fails the satisfaction check. Thus such a certificate exists.

Consequently, unsatisfiability belongs to $\mathsf{NP}$. Since satisfiability is $\mathsf{NP}$-complete, this implies $\mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{coNP}$. $\square$

The requirement that the reduction produce promised-valid instances on unsatisfiable formulas is essential to this argument. A reduction defined only on a satisfiable-input promise does not automatically yield this consequence.

This is a conditional obstruction, not an unconditional exclusion of $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness.

8. Hardness with unrestricted binary-encoded time coefficients

This section concerns the second evaluation model from the opening section. It does not apply under uniform polynomial-time evaluation.

Let $\varphi$ be a Boolean formula with $k\geq1$ variables. Let $s$ be the integer encoding the lexicographically first satisfying assignment, or set $s=0$ if the formula is unsatisfiable. Thus
$$ 0\leq s<2^k. $$
Define
$$ b_1=\frac38+\frac{s}{2^{k+4}}, \qquad b_2=\frac34, \qquad b_3=\frac14, \qquad \epsilon=2^{-(k+7)}. $$
Let
$$ P_0=\mathbb R^3, $$
and, for $j\in\{1,2,3\}$, let
$$ P_j= \left\{ q\in\mathbb R^3: q_j\leq b_j,\quad q_j-b_j\leq q_\ell-b_\ell \text{ for every }\ell \right\}. $$

The supplied membership program contains $\varphi$. On a positive-category query, it computes $s$ by exhaustive assignment search and then checks these rational inequalities.

Verification of all promises

Each positive-category set is a finite intersection of closed half-spaces. Hence all four sets are closed.

The bounds
$$ \frac38\leq b_1<\frac7{16}, \qquad b_2=\frac34, \qquad b_3=\frac14 $$
show that every $b_j<1$. Since membership in $P_j$ requires $q_j\leq b_j$, price exclusion holds.

Global coverage follows from $P_0=\mathbb R^3$.

For positive-category coverage, take $p\in\Sigma_3$. Some coordinate $p_\ell$ is zero, and hence
$$ p_\ell-b_\ell<0. $$
Choose $j$ minimizing $p_j-b_j$. Its adjusted price is negative, so $p_j<b_j$, and all the comparison inequalities defining $P_j$ hold. Thus $p\in P_j$.

These promises hold whether or not $\varphi$ is satisfiable. There is even an explicit exact common-demand price:
$$ p^*=(b_1-b_3,\ b_2-b_3,\ 0)\in\Sigma_3. $$
At this price, all adjusted prices equal $-b_3$, so $p^*\in P_1\cap P_2\cap P_3$.

Every solution reveals the selected assignment

Let $(p,x)$ be any solution. Section 1 gives $d_j(p)\leq\epsilon$ for every $j$.

Fix distinct $j,\ell$. For any $q\in P_j$,
$$ q_j-b_j\leq q_\ell-b_\ell. $$
Therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} (p_j-b_j)-(p_\ell-b_\ell) &\leq(p_j-q_j)-(p_\ell-q_\ell)\\ &\leq|p_j-q_j|+|p_\ell-q_\ell|\\ &\leq\|p-q\|_1. \end{aligned} $$
Taking the infimum over $q\in P_j$ and then reversing $j,\ell$ gives
$$ \left|(p_j-b_j)-(p_\ell-b_\ell)\right| \leq\epsilon. $$

If $p_1=0$, then
$$ p_1-b_1\leq-\frac38, \qquad p_3-b_3\geq-\frac14. $$
Their difference is at least $1/8$, contradicting $\epsilon<1/8$.

If $p_2=0$, then
$$ p_2-b_2=-\frac34, \qquad p_3-b_3\geq-\frac14, $$
giving a difference at least $1/2$, also impossible.

Because $p\in\Sigma_3$, at least one coordinate is zero. It follows that $p_3=0$. The pairwise bound now yields
$$ \left|p_1-\left(\frac18+\frac{s}{2^{k+4}}\right)\right| \leq\epsilon. $$
Multiplying by $2^{k+4}$ gives
$$ \left|2^{k+4}\left(p_1-\frac18\right)-s\right| \leq\frac18. $$
Thus nearest-integer rounding recovers $s$ unambiguously.

Checking the recovered assignment against $\varphi$ decides satisfiability; on satisfiable formulas it returns a satisfying assignment. This also handles a satisfying all-zero assignment correctly.

Encoding the guarantee

For a rational query of bit length $b$, the demand program can be implemented to run in
$$ 2^k\operatorname{poly}(|\varphi|+b) $$
time. More explicitly, fixed constants $C_1,d_1$ can be chosen for the implementation so that its time is bounded by
$$ A(b+1)^{d_1}, \qquad A=C_1\,2^k(|\varphi|+1)^{d_1}. $$
The binary encoding of $A$ has polynomial length in $|\varphi|$.

The reduction outputs the formula-containing program and this bound; it never computes $s$ itself. The program, guarantee, and tolerance therefore have polynomial total encoding length and are produced in polynomial time.

For each fixed program, the displayed bound is polynomial in query length. It is not uniformly polynomial in the combined input length. Hence this proves $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness exactly when such unrestricted coefficients are permitted.

9. Proven conclusions and remaining questions

For the intended uniform evaluation model, the precise mass-flow task is $\mathsf{PPAD}$-complete under polynomial-time search reductions. A deterministic algorithm finds a valid output in
$$ O\!\left(\epsilon^{-1}\operatorname{poly}(L)\right) $$
time, with $m=\tau=3$. This bound can be exponential in $L$.

The polynomial-time-versus-$\mathsf{NP}$-hard question remains unresolved under that model. With unrestricted individual polynomial query-time guarantees, the separate construction above proves $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness.
