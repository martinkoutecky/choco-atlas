The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(\Theta\) of complete worker cost types, a rational mass vector \(\mu\in\Delta(\Theta)\), finite category-labelled chore templates, and horizon \(H\), with \(c_\theta(B)=|B|-r_\theta(B)\) for a partition-matroid rank \(r_\theta\), an adversary reveals a per-capita stream of chore copies online. Each copy is assigned immediately and wholly to one agent, with no batch coordination; the fluid state is the mass \(z_{\theta,B}\) of type-\(\theta\) agents holding integral bundle \(B\). Decide whether a nonanticipating deterministic policy can be complete and \(\alpha\)-EF1 for \(\mu\)-almost every ordered pair at every prefix, or minimize \(\alpha\ge1\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An atomless society has total mass \(1\) and is given by its support: \(\tau\) complete worker types \(\theta\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) chore templates. Each type specifies a partition of the templates into categories, with capacity \(1\) per category; distinct copies of a template share its category. For an integral multiset bundle \(B\), let \(r_\theta(B)\) count its occupied categories and \(c_\theta(B)=|B|-r_\theta(B)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\).
The types, templates and positive binary-rational horizon \(H\) are known initially. Cumulative arrival volume \(s\) is measured in copies per capita. An adversary reveals only the current template \(a(s)\), using finitely many constant segments with rational endpoints, and may stop at any \(s\le H\).
Initially \(z_{\theta,\varnothing}=\mu_\theta\). A deterministic nonanticipating policy selects measurable nonnegative transfer rates \(u_{\theta,B}(s)\), moving worker mass from \(B\) to \(B+\mathbf e_{a(s)}\); every affected worker receives one whole copy immediately and irrevocably, without storage or reallocation.
Precisely, bundle-state coordinates are absolutely continuous and satisfy \(\dot z_{\theta,B}=\mathbf1_{\{B_{a(s)}>0\}}u_{\theta,B-\mathbf e_{a(s)}}-u_{\theta,B}\), with \(u_{\theta,B}=0\) wherever \(z_{\theta,B}=0\). Completeness requires \(\sum_{\theta,B}u_{\theta,B}=1\) almost everywhere, \(z\ge0\), \(\sum_Bz_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\), and \(\sum_{\theta,B}|B|z_{\theta,B}=s\).
At every prefix, \(\alpha\)-EF1 means that for every pair of positive-mass states \((\theta,B),(\phi,C)\) with \(B\ne\varnothing\), some copy \(e\in B\) satisfies \(c_\theta(B\setminus\{e\})\le\alpha c_\theta(C)\). This is fairness for almost every ordered worker pair under the product population measure.
Running times are measured in \(m,\tau,H,L\) and the number of revealed segments, where \(L\) is the binary encoding length of the instance and revealed stream.

Does some finite universal factor \(\alpha\ge1\) permit a complete deterministic nonanticipating policy maintaining \(\alpha\)-EF1 for every such society and adversarial stream, or is every finite universal factor impossible?

For finite societies, every finite EF1 factor is impossible for complete deterministic online allocation, already with two workers and capacity-one partition costs ([Wang–Wei](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24321v2#S4.SS2.SSS2)). Offline, binary supermodular costs admit polynomial-time EF1 and Pareto-efficient allocation ([Barman–Narayan–Verma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11530)).
No result settling this exact continuous online model was located in the literature search.

Partial progress: persistent witness cycles and positive-cost deadlocks

The universal-factor question remains unresolved here. The main new result is an exact criterion for a narrower question: from a given fair state, can one template be repeated arbitrarily long while maintaining some fixed finite factor?

A directed graph of zero-cost witnesses answers that question. A cycle prevents arbitrarily long continuation for every finite factor; an acyclic graph permits such continuation with a factor depending on the initial state. I also construct reachable deadlocks with strictly positive comparison costs, even when the two partitions are ordered by refinement.

These results do not prove that an adversary can force every policy into a deadlock.

1. Basic consequences of the model

Write $\mathbf e_a$ for one copy of template $a$. For every type $\theta$, define
$$ d_\theta(B)=\max\{c_\theta(B)-1,0\}. $$

1.1. Reformulating fairness

For every nonempty bundle $B$,
$$ \min_{e\in B}c_\theta(B\setminus\{e\})=d_\theta(B). $$

Indeed, deletion decreases cardinality by one and decreases the number of occupied categories by either zero or one. Thus it decreases cost by at most one. If the cost is positive, some category contains at least two copies, and deleting a copy from that category decreases cost by exactly one. If the cost is zero, deletion leaves cost zero.

For a state $z$, let
$$ S=\{(\phi,C):z_{\phi,C}>0\}, \qquad h_\theta=\min_{(\phi,C)\in S}c_\theta(C). $$
The minimum exists because it is the minimum of a nonempty set of nonnegative integers.

Consequently, $\alpha$-EF1 is equivalent to
$$ d_\theta(B)\le \alpha h_\theta \qquad \text{for every }(\theta,B)\in S. $$
In particular,
$$ h_\theta=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_\theta(B)\le 1 \quad\text{for every supported bundle owned by }\theta. $$
This implication holds for every finite factor.

1.2. Fair states have finite support

Let $k_\theta$ be the number of categories of type $\theta$. At a complete state of volume $s$, some supported bundle $C$ satisfies
$$ |C|\le \lfloor s\rfloor. $$
Otherwise the population mean cardinality would exceed $s$.

For every evaluator $\theta$,
$$ c_\theta(C)\le \max\{0,\lfloor s\rfloor-1\}. $$
Fairness therefore implies, for every supported own-bundle $B$,
$$ |B| \le k_\theta+1+ \left\lfloor \alpha\max\{0,\lfloor s\rfloor-1\} \right\rfloor. $$
There are finitely many multisets under this bound, so every finite-factor fair state has finite support.

1.3. Evaluator minima never decrease

Adding a copy never decreases any evaluator’s cost. This implies that each $h_\theta(s)$ is nondecreasing along every complete trajectory.

Here is a direct justification using the stipulated dynamics. Fix a time $s_0$ and put $h=h_\theta(s_0)$. If $h=0$, the assertion is immediate. Otherwise the set
$$ \mathcal D=\{C:c_\theta(C)<h\} $$
is finite, because
$$ |C|=c_\theta(C)+r_\theta(C)\le h-1+k_\theta. $$
No transfer can enter $\mathcal D$ from outside it: that would decrease $\theta$-cost. Summing the differential equations over all types and all bundles in $\mathcal D$ shows that their total mass is nonincreasing. It starts at zero and remains nonnegative, so it stays zero. Hence $h_\theta(s)\ge h$ for all later times.

In particular, once a type has a positive evaluator minimum, it never again has a zero-cost comparison bundle.

2. An exact criterion for repeating one template

Fix a finite-factor fair state $z^0$ of volume $s_0$, and fix a template $a$. All definitions in this section refer to this initial state.

Let
$$ Z=\{\theta:h_\theta=0\}. $$
For an evaluator $\theta$, define
$$ \varepsilon_\theta(C,a)= \begin{cases} 1,&\text{if the category containing }a\text{ is unoccupied in }C,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
For every integer $j\ge 1$,
$$ c_\theta(C+j\mathbf e_a) = c_\theta(C)+j-\varepsilon_\theta(C,a). $$

If $\phi\in Z$ owns a supported bundle $C$, then $c_\phi(C)\le 1$. Define
$$ q_\phi(C,a)=1-c_\phi(C)+\varepsilon_\phi(C,a). $$
Thus $q_\phi(C,a)\in\{0,1,2\}$, and it is exactly the largest number of additional copies of $a$ that this worker can receive while its own cost remains at most one.

If $c_\theta(C)=0$, define
$$ t_\theta(C,a)=\varepsilon_\theta(C,a). $$
This is exactly the largest number of additional copies of $a$ for which the bundle still has zero $\theta$-cost.

2.1. The witness graph

Define a directed graph $G_a(z^0)$ on the vertex set $Z$. Put an edge
$$ \theta\longrightarrow\phi $$
if some supported bundle $C$ owned by $\phi$ satisfies
$$ c_\theta(C)=0, \qquad q_\phi(C,a)\le t_\theta(C,a). $$

The edge means that $C$ remains a zero-cost witness for $\theta$ even after its owner $\phi$ has received every additional copy allowed while $h_\phi=0$.

There are no self-loops. Indeed, when $c_\theta(C)=0$,
$$ q_\theta(C,a) = 1+\varepsilon_\theta(C,a) > t_\theta(C,a). $$

2.2. The criterion

Theorem. The following are equivalent:

  • The graph $G_a(z^0)$ is acyclic.
  • There exists a finite factor $\beta$ and a deterministic complete continuation, using only template $a$, that maintains $\beta$-EF1 for every additional length.

More precisely:

  • If the graph contains a directed cycle, every continuation maintaining any fixed finite factor has bounded additional length.
  • If the graph is acyclic, a continuation exists with

$$ \beta=M+2, \qquad M=\max_{(\phi,C)\in S}c_\phi(C). $$

The factor in this theorem depends on the state. It is not a universal factor.

2.3. Proof: a cycle creates permanent witnesses

Choose a directed cycle with vertex set $T$. For each $\theta\in T$, choose its cycle edge $\theta\longrightarrow\phi$ and a supported bundle $C_\theta$ witnessing that edge. Write
$$ q_\theta=q_\phi(C_\theta,a). $$
Every bundle in the chain
$$ C_\theta,\ C_\theta+\mathbf e_a,\ \ldots,\ C_\theta+q_\theta\mathbf e_a $$
has zero $\theta$-cost.

Let $W_\theta(s)$ be the total mass in these chain states, owned by $\phi$. Initially,
$$ W_\theta(s_0)>0. $$

Suppose all $W_\theta$ are positive on an interval. Then $h_\theta=0$ for every $\theta\in T$. In particular, the owner $\phi$ of each selected chain has $h_\phi=0$.

The final chain state has own cost one, and adding another $a$ would give own cost two. Such a target state must have zero mass while $h_\phi=0$, under every finite fairness factor. Its outgoing rate is zero by the zero-mass restriction. Its differential equation consequently forces the transfer rate out of the final chain state to be zero almost everywhere.

Summing the equations along the chain now gives
$$ \dot W_\theta\ge 0 $$
almost everywhere: there may be incoming mass at the beginning, but there is no outgoing mass at the end.

These inequalities show that
$$ W_\theta(s)\ge W_\theta(s_0)>0 $$
throughout any fair continuation. To make the persistence argument explicit, take the maximal interval starting at $s_0$ on which all the $W_\theta$ remain positive. On this interval they are nondecreasing. Continuity then prevents any of them from reaching zero at a finite endpoint, so the interval extends through the entire continuation.

Thus every selected witness persists.

Fix one selected chain and put
$$ D=|C_\theta|+q_\theta. $$
At every time, some supported bundle in this chain has cardinality at most $D$. Hence, for every evaluator $\psi$,
$$ h_\psi(s)\le \max\{0,D-1\}. $$
If the maintained factor is $\beta<\infty$, fairness implies
$$ |B| \le k_\psi+1+ \left\lfloor\beta\max\{0,D-1\}\right\rfloor $$
for every supported bundle owned by $\psi$. Taking population means yields the absolute volume bound
$$ s\le \sum_\psi\mu_\psi(k_\psi+1) + \left\lfloor\beta\max\{0,D-1\}\right\rfloor. $$
Therefore the continuation cannot have arbitrarily large length.

2.4. Proof: an acyclic graph allows all zero witnesses to be removed

Set $\beta=M+2$. The initial state is $\beta$-EF1: types with zero evaluator minimum have own cost at most one; types with positive evaluator minimum have own deletion-cost at most $M$, while their minimum is at least one.

For bookkeeping, distinguish workers by their initial type-state. These labels only split coordinates internally. Summing labelled transfer rates produces valid rates in the original model. A positive labelled source has a positive original source coordinate, so the zero-mass restriction is respected.

First perform the following transfers:

  • Each worker initially owned by $\phi\in Z$, with initial bundle $C$, receives exactly $q_\phi(C,a)$ copies of $a$.
  • Each worker initially owned by a type outside $Z$ receives exactly two copies of $a$.

Process these finite chains in a fixed deterministic order.

Every worker owned by a type in $Z$ keeps own cost at most one. Every other owner has evaluator minimum at least one throughout, by Section 1.3. Such workers receive at most two additional copies, so their own cost is at most $M+2$, and their deletion-cost is at most $M+1\le\beta$. Thus every intermediate state is $\beta$-EF1.

At the end of this phase, an evaluator $\theta\in Z$ has a zero-cost comparison bundle exactly when an edge
$$ \theta\longrightarrow\phi $$
remains to an owner $\phi\in Z$. Indeed:

  • Two added copies of $a$ give positive cost under every evaluator.
  • An initially positive evaluator-cost cannot become zero.
  • For an initially zero-cost bundle owned by $\phi\in Z$, zero cost remains exactly when

$$ q_\phi(C,a)\le t_\theta(C,a). $$

Since the graph is acyclic, it has a vertex $\theta$ with no outgoing edge. Its evaluator minimum is therefore positive. Top up every worker of this type to exactly two added copies of $a$. These transfers remain fair by the same $M+2$ bound. They remove all zero-cost witnesses owned by this type.

Delete that vertex and repeat. A finite acyclic graph always has a vertex with no outgoing edge, so eventually every worker has received exactly two additional copies of $a$. The total additional volume used is exactly two, and every evaluator minimum is now positive.

Continue in full population passes, giving one further copy of $a$ to every worker in each pass. During a pass after $n$ completed additional copies per worker, every worker has received either $n$ or $n+1$ copies, where $n\ge 2$. Therefore
$$ h_\theta\ge n-1, \qquad d_\theta(B)\le M+n. $$
The required inequality follows from
$$ (M+2)(n-1)-(M+n) = (M+1)(n-2)\ge 0. $$

All transfers use finitely many labelled chains per pass. Their rates can be set to zero at depletion instants. The construction is deterministic and can be interrupted at any prefix; it does not require knowledge of the stopping time. This proves the theorem.

3. A fair state with admissible recipients but unavoidable eventual deadlock

The graph criterion gives a stronger obstruction than an immediate deadlock: every current recipient may initially be admissible, yet a sufficiently long constant continuation can still be impossible.

Take types $A,B$, each of mass $1/2$, with partitions
$$ A:\ \{\{a,b\},\{c\}\}, \qquad B:\ \{\{a,c\},\{b\}\}. $$
At volume one, consider
$$ z_{A,\{b\}} = z_{A,\{c\}} = z_{B,\{b\}} = z_{B,\{c\}} = \frac14. $$
Every bundle is a singleton, so all own costs and deletion-costs are zero. In particular, every recipient can initially accept $a$ fairly.

3.1. The witness cycle

The bundle $\{c\}$ owned by $B$ witnesses $A\longrightarrow B$:

  • It has zero $A$-cost.
  • Its owner $B$ already occupies the category of $a$, so it can receive only one additional $a$ while own cost stays at most one.
  • Under evaluator $A$, the category of $a$ is initially unoccupied, so $\{a,c\}$ still has zero cost.

Similarly, $\{b\}$ owned by $A$ witnesses $B\longrightarrow A$.

Thus both evaluator minima remain zero throughout every finite-factor fair constant-$a$ continuation. All own costs must consequently remain at most one.

3.2. Exact maximum continuation length

The four initial groups can receive at most the following numbers of additional copies:
$$ \begin{array}{c|cccc} \text{initial type-state} &(A,\{b\})&(A,\{c\})&(B,\{b\})&(B,\{c\})\\ \hline \text{maximum added copies of }a &1&2&2&1 \end{array} $$
For example, adding $a$ to $(A,\{b\})$ immediately incurs cost one; a second added copy would incur cost two. For $(A,\{c\})$, the first copy occupies a new category and the second incurs cost one.

During a constant-$a$ continuation, the initial counts of $b$ and $c$ are preserved. The dynamics therefore retain mass $1/4$ in each of these four groups of descendants. The total additional volume is at most
$$ \frac14(1+2+2+1)=\frac32. $$

This bound is attainable. First give one $a$ to every worker, using volume one. Then give another $a$ to the two groups whose first added copy occupied a new own category, using volume $1/2$. Own costs never exceed one, so all prefixes are EF1.

The resulting state, at volume $5/2$, is an immediate deadlock: every own cost is one, both evaluator minima are zero, and adding $a$ to any recipient raises its own cost to two.

3.3. Fair reachability

The initial singleton state is reached by revealing:

  • Template $b$ for volume $1/2$, transferring at rate $1/2$ from each type’s empty state.
  • Template $c$ for volume $1/2$, using the same rates from the remaining empty states.

Every prefix has only empty bundles and singletons, so every prefix is EF1.

This proves that identical bundle distributions across types, combined with initially admissible choices, can lead to a state from which a long constant continuation is impossible. It does not prove that every policy must reach this state.

4. Refinement chains exclude zero-witness cycles

Say that partition $P_\phi$ refines $P_\theta$ if each category of $P_\phi$ is contained in a category of $P_\theta$. Then
$$ c_\phi(C)\le c_\theta(C) $$
for every bundle $C$.

Corollary. If all type partitions are totally ordered by refinement, then $G_a(z^0)$ is acyclic for every template $a$ and every finite-factor fair state.

Proof. Suppose there is an edge $\theta\longrightarrow\phi$ witnessed by $C$. Assume, for contradiction, that $P_\phi$ refines $P_\theta$.

Since $c_\theta(C)=0$, refinement gives $c_\phi(C)=0$, and hence
$$ q_\phi(C,a)=1+\varepsilon_\phi(C,a). $$

If $\varepsilon_\theta(C,a)=0$, then
$$ q_\phi(C,a)\ge 1>t_\theta(C,a)=0. $$
If $\varepsilon_\theta(C,a)=1$, refinement implies $\varepsilon_\phi(C,a)=1$: occupying the finer category of $a$ would also occupy its coarser category. Therefore
$$ q_\phi(C,a)=2>t_\theta(C,a)=1. $$
Both cases contradict the edge condition.

Thus every edge points from a strictly finer partition to a coarser partition. A directed cycle is impossible. ∎

This corollary concerns zero-cost obstructions. The next construction shows that positive-cost obstructions remain possible.

5. Reachable positive-cost deadlocks for every finite factor

Fix any finite $\alpha\ge 1$, and define integers
$$ p=1+\lfloor\alpha\rfloor, \qquad q=1+\lfloor\alpha p\rfloor. $$
Then
$$ p-1\le\alpha<p, \qquad q-1\le\alpha p<q, \qquad q\ge p+1. $$

Use $q$ templates
$$ a,b_2,\ldots,b_q $$
and two types of mass $1/2$:

  • Type $F$ puts every template in a separate category.
  • Type $G$ puts all templates in one category.

These partitions are ordered by refinement.

Consider the state
$$ z_{F,B}=\frac12, \qquad z_{G,C}=\frac12, $$
where
$$ B=(p+1)\mathbf e_a, \qquad C=2\mathbf e_a+\sum_{j=2}^{q}\mathbf e_{b_j}. $$
Its volume is
$$ s_0=\frac{p+q+2}{2}. $$

5.1. Fairness and deadlock

The costs are
$$ c_F(B)=p,\qquad c_F(C)=1, $$
$$ c_G(B)=p,\qquad c_G(C)=q. $$
Thus
$$ h_F=1,\qquad h_G=p. $$
The state is $\alpha$-EF1 because
$$ d_F(B)=p-1=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\le\alpha h_F, $$
$$ d_G(C)=q-1=\lfloor\alpha p\rfloor\le\alpha h_G. $$

However, adding $a$ gives
$$ d_F(B+\mathbf e_a)=p>\alpha h_F, $$
$$ d_G(C+\mathbf e_a)=q>\alpha h_G. $$

To verify that these inequalities forbid every positive continuation, note that every coordinate is $1$-Lipschitz: its incoming and outgoing rates are nonnegative and their sum is at most the total rate one. Therefore both initial states remain positive for an additional interval of length less than $1/4$.

On that interval, the two initial comparison bundles force both target coordinates to remain zero by fairness. Their outgoing rates are zero, and their differential equations force the outgoing rates from both initial sources to be zero almost everywhere.

Every other coordinate starts at zero. Induction on cardinality shows that it cannot acquire mass or send mass: during a constant-$a$ segment, its only possible predecessor has smaller cardinality and has zero outgoing rate. Since there are countably many coordinates, all outgoing rates are simultaneously zero almost everywhere. This contradicts completeness.

Thus this is an immediate deadlock, although both evaluator minima are strictly positive.

5.2. Fair reachability

The deadlock state has a complete, deterministic, nonanticipating fair reaching trajectory.

First reveal $a$ for volume two, giving two copies to every worker in two population passes. All bundles have cardinality at most two during this phase, so deleting one copy leaves cost zero under every type.

Continue revealing $a$ and give each type-$F$ worker another $p-1$ copies. Throughout this phase:

  • Type-$G$ workers retain $\{a,a\}$, whose $F$-cost is one.
  • Type-$F$ own costs never exceed $p$, so deletion-costs never exceed

$$ p-1\le\alpha. $$

  • Type-$G$ own deletion-cost is zero.

Every prefix remains $\alpha$-EF1.

Finally reveal each $b_j$ for volume $1/2$, giving one copy to every type-$G$ worker. These templates occupy new categories under $F$, so every type-$G$ bundle continues to have $F$-cost one.

Suppose the current $G$-cost before one such population pass is $\ell$. During the pass, the supported $G$-costs are $\ell$ and $\ell+1$, while the type-$F$ bundle has $G$-cost $p$. It is enough to check
$$ \ell\le\alpha\min\{p,\ell\}. $$
If $\ell\le p$, this follows from $\alpha\ge 1$. If $\ell>p$, it follows from
$$ \ell\le q-1=\lfloor\alpha p\rfloor\le\alpha p. $$
Thus every prefix is fair, and the stated deadlock is reached.

All segment endpoints are rational: every population pass has length $1$ or $1/2$, and $p,q$ are integers.

Again, this is fair reachability by particular choices, not an adversary defeating every policy.

6. What remains open

The exact constant-template criterion separates two issues:

  • Zero-cost witness cycles create permanent bounded-size comparison bundles and defeat every finite factor on a sufficiently long repetition.
  • When that graph is acyclic, zero witnesses can be removed within two additional copies per capita, using a state-dependent finite factor.

Nevertheless, positive-cost deadlocks exist for every finite factor, even with nested partitions. Therefore preventing zero-cost cycles alone does not furnish a universal policy.

The missing global argument is still one of the following: a fixed finite-factor policy that avoids all relevant obstructions on every stream, or an adversary that forces an obstruction against every deterministic nonanticipating policy.

3. Cycle obstruction

The continuity argument closes the apparent circularity: none of the initially positive chain masses can reach zero at a finite endpoint of the interval on which all remain positive.

The resulting volume bound is correct. A surviving chain supplies a comparison bundle of cardinality at most $D$ for every evaluator. Hence each supported own-bundle satisfies the claimed cardinality bound, and taking population means gives
