The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given items \(M\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) over finitely many complete XOS buyer types in ordered arrival cohorts, and rational per-capita capacities \(q_j\), consider infinitesimal cohorts whose buyers choose utility-maximizing integral bundles at static anonymous item prices under fixed tie-breaking. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) maximize \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_j\), and \(x_{t,S}\ge0\). Compute prices \(p\ge0\) whose integrated sequential welfare is at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/2\), or determine that none exist.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\ge1\). A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible valuations. Each type specifies a cohort \(c(t)\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\) and a complete binary-rational value table for a normalized, monotone, nonnegative XOS valuation \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\). Capacities \(q_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) are binary rationals; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Cohorts arrive in order. Each lasts one unit of time, with type \(t\) arriving at constant mass rate \(\mu_t\) during its cohort. At fixed anonymous prices \(p\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^m\), each buyer chooses an integral bundle maximizing \(v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j\) among subsets of items with positive remaining capacity, including \(\varnothing\). Ties select the lexicographically smallest characteristic vector in item order.
Allocate these chosen bundles at their arrival rates until a capacity reaches zero or the cohort ends; remove exhausted items and recompute choices. A mass \(a\) receiving \(S\) consumes \(a\) units of each item in \(S\). Let \(W(p)\) be the integrated valuation welfare.
Define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_{x\ge0}\sum_{t,S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) for every type and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_j\) for every item.

Can prices satisfying \(W(p)\ge\mathrm{OPT}/2\), or a correct determination that none exist, be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is this half-welfare pricing task NP-hard?

For discrete buyers with independent valuations and one copy per item, static anonymous prices achieving half the expected optimal welfare exist for XOS valuations ([Braun–Kesselheim](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00707)).
With sampling, XOS-oracle access, and access to an allocation algorithm, discrete prices achieving half that algorithm’s expected welfare minus an additive error are computable efficiently ([Feldman–Gravin–Lucier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.4916)); no result settling the precise continuous task above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted complete buyer types as explicit value tables, promised to be XOS; all valuation entries are binary rationals.
  • Normalized total buyer mass to one and interpreted capacities as supply per unit total buyer mass, allowing nonnegative rational capacities.
  • Specified ordered cohorts by input cohort labels; within each cohort, types arrive simultaneously at constant rates equal to their masses over a unit interval.
  • Specified capacity depletion by continuous consumption at arrival rates, with exhausted items removed immediately and bundle choices recomputed.
  • Fixed otherwise unspecified tie-breaking to lexicographic order on bundle characteristic vectors, including the empty bundle.
  • Required rational output prices and defined the input bit length to include complete valuation tables, masses, capacities, and cohort assignments.

Result

The task is polynomial-time solvable. For every instance satisfying the stated promises, rational anonymous prices achieving \(W(p)\ge \mathrm{OPT}/2\) exist and can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

The construction uses additive supporting weights, as does the item-contribution pricing argument in [Feldman–Gravin–Lucier, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.4916). The proof below directly handles the continuous allocation rule and arbitrary rational capacities.

1. Computing an optimal fractional allocation and supporting weights

Write \(N=\tau 2^m\). Because every type’s complete value table is explicitly supplied, \(N\le L\).

The linear program defining \(\mathrm{OPT}\) has \(N\) variables and \(\tau+m\) principal constraints. It is feasible: assign each type’s entire mass to the empty bundle. Moreover, every feasible coordinate satisfies \(0\le x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\), so its feasible region is bounded. The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore gives an exact rational optimum \(x^*\), with polynomial encoding length, in polynomial time.

For every pair \((t,S)\) with \(x^*_{t,S}>0\), we compute nonnegative weights \(a_{t,S,j}\) satisfying:

  • \(a_{t,S,j}=0\) for \(j\notin S\);
  • \(\sum_{j\in S}a_{t,S,j}=v_t(S)\);
  • \(\sum_{j\in A}a_{t,S,j}\le v_t(A)\) for every \(A\subseteq M\).

These weights exist by the XOS hypothesis. Indeed, choose a nonnegative additive clause attaining \(v_t(S)\), and set its coefficients outside \(S\) to zero. This preserves equality on \(S\), and only decreases that clause’s value on every other bundle.

They can be computed without knowing an XOS representation: solve the rational feasibility LP consisting of the three displayed requirements. It has \(m\) variables and \(2^m+O(m)\) constraints. It is feasible by the preceding argument and bounded because its singleton constraints give \(a_{t,S,j}\le v_t(\{j\})\). Rational linear programming thus supplies weights of polynomial encoding length.

There are at most \(N\) such auxiliary LPs. Their combined encoding length and running time are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

2. The prices

Define the optimal allocation’s consumption and supporting welfare contribution for item \(j\) by

\(d_j=\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x^*_{t,S}\),

\(B_j=\sum_{t,S:x^*_{t,S}>0}x^*_{t,S}a_{t,S,j}\).

Feasibility gives \(d_j\le q_j\). The supporting equalities give

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\in M}B_j=\sum_{t,S}x^*_{t,S}v_t(S)=\mathrm{OPT}.\)

If \(q_j=0\), then \(d_j=0\). Nonnegativity of the allocation variables implies that every positive allocation coordinate uses a bundle excluding \(j\), and consequently \(B_j=0\).

Set

\(\displaystyle p_j=\frac{B_j}{2q_j}\quad\text{when }q_j>0,\)

and set \(p_j=0\) when \(q_j=0\).

These are nonnegative rational prices. Exact rational arithmetic computes them with polynomial encoding length in polynomial time. In particular, for every item,

\(\displaystyle q_jp_j=\frac{B_j}{2}.\)

3. Welfare guarantee for the continuous process

Run the process at these prices. Let \(z_j\) be the total consumption of item \(j\), and define

\(F=\{j\in M:q_j-z_j>0\},\qquad E=M\setminus F.\)

Thus \(F\) contains the items with positive remaining capacity at the end. Every item in \(F\) was available throughout the process, since remaining capacities never increase.

The process has finitely many phases. Within a cohort, choices remain fixed while the available item set remains fixed. Every exhaustion event permanently removes at least one item, so there are at most \(m\) such events, in addition to cohort boundaries. Consequently, the welfare and utility integrals below are well defined.

Write \(p(A)=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\). Let \(U\) be the integrated buyer utility and \(R\) the total payments. Pointwise, valuation equals utility plus payment, so

\(W(p)=U+R.\)

Revenue from exhausted items

For every \(j\in E\), its final remaining capacity is zero, so \(z_j=q_j\). Because all prices are nonnegative,

\(\displaystyle R=\sum_{j\in M}p_jz_j\ge\sum_{j\in E}p_jq_j=\frac12\sum_{j\in E}B_j.\)

This includes initially unavailable items: both \(q_jp_j\) and \(B_j\) are zero for them.

Utility from items remaining available

Fix a type \(t\). At every time during its cohort, every bundle \(S\cap F\) is available. If its chosen bundle at time \(s\) is \(D_t(s)\), utility maximization gives, for every \(S\subseteq M\),

\(v_t(D_t(s))-p(D_t(s))\ge v_t(S\cap F)-p(S\cap F).\)

The coefficients \(x^*_{t,S}/\mu_t\) are nonnegative and sum to one. Taking their weighted average yields

\(\displaystyle v_t(D_t(s))-p(D_t(s))\ge\sum_{S\subseteq M}\frac{x^*_{t,S}}{\mu_t}\bigl(v_t(S\cap F)-p(S\cap F)\bigr).\)

Type \(t\) arrives at rate \(\mu_t\) for one unit of time. Integrating and summing over types therefore gives

\(\displaystyle U\ge\sum_{t,S}x^*_{t,S}\bigl(v_t(S\cap F)-p(S\cap F)\bigr).\)

For every positive allocation coordinate, the supporting inequalities imply

\(\displaystyle v_t(S\cap F)\ge\sum_{j\in S\cap F}a_{t,S,j}.\)

Using this inequality and exchanging finite sums,

\(\displaystyle U\ge\sum_{j\in F}B_j-\sum_{j\in F}p_jd_j.\)

Since \(p_j\ge0\) and \(d_j\le q_j\),

\(\displaystyle U\ge\sum_{j\in F}(B_j-p_jq_j)=\frac12\sum_{j\in F}B_j.\)

Combining the bounds

We conclude that

\(\displaystyle W(p)=U+R\ge\frac12\sum_{j\in F}B_j+\frac12\sum_{j\in E}B_j=\frac12\sum_{j\in M}B_j=\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}2.\)

The argument uses only utility maximization, so it applies in particular to the prescribed lexicographic tie rule.

4. Algorithmic conclusion

The algorithm computes \(x^*\), obtains supporting weights by the auxiliary LPs, computes \(B_j\), and returns the prices \(p_j\) above. All steps take polynomial time under the explicit-table input model.

The welfare proof works for every cohort order. Thus every promised instance admits qualifying prices; the “none exist” outcome is unnecessary.
