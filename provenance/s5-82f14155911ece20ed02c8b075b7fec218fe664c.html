The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of roommate-preference types \(T\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and a complete weak order over roommate types for each \(t\), compute a Pareto-optimal assignment of the population to rooms of size three. Represent each room by a multiset \(q\) of three types and let \(x_q\) be the fraction of rooms with composition \(q\). Require \(\sum_q x_q=1\) and \(\sum_q n_t(q)x_q=3\mu_t\) for each \(t\), where \(n_t(q)\) is the number of type-\(t\) occupants. Pareto dominance compares occupants' worst-roommate outcomes, allowing a type-preserving pairing between the original and reassigned occupants.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). The support size is not the number of possible preference types. Each type has an explicitly listed complete weak order over \(T\), including its own type; all roommate types are acceptable. Here \(m=|T|=\tau\), and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Let \(Q\) contain every multiset of three types, allowing repetitions. An assignment is \(x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{Q}\) satisfying \(\sum_{q\in Q}x_q=1\) and \(\sum_{q\in Q}n_t(q)x_q=3\mu_t\) for every \(t\), where \(n_t(q)\) is the multiplicity of \(t\). Every occupant is assigned to a full room; there is no outside option.
For a type-\(t\) occupant in \(q\), remove one occurrence of \(t\); the outcome is the indifference class of the worse of the two remaining types under \(t\)'s order. Equal worst-roommate classes give equal outcomes, without further tie-breaking.
The mass of type-\(t\) occupants in composition \(q\) is \(n_t(q)x_q/3\). Assignment \(y\) dominates \(x\) if, for every type, there is a coupling of its old and new outcome masses that pairs each old outcome with a weakly preferred new outcome, and the total mass paired with strictly preferred outcomes is positive. An assignment is Pareto optimal if no feasible assignment dominates it.

Can a Pareto-optimal assignment with binary-rational coordinates be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite individual players, computing a Pareto-optimal partition into rooms of size three is NP-hard with complete weak preferences and worst-roommate comparison. [Cseh, Fleiner, and Harján](https://www.mechanism-design.org/arch/v004-1/p_03.pdf).
No published result settling the exact mass-and-coupling problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Same-type roommates are allowed and each preference order includes the occupant's own type; repeated types represent distinct occupants, and evaluation removes only one occurrence of the occupant's type.
  • Assignment coordinates may be arbitrary nonnegative reals, while the computational output must have binary-rational coordinates.
  • Strict Pareto improvement requires a positive mass of strictly improved occupants, excluding changes confined to a null set.
  • The type-preserving pairing is formalized as a coupling of old and new outcome masses with the prescribed marginals.
  • There is no separate candidate universe: the number m of ranked roommate types equals the support size tau.

Conclusion and encoding convention

The search problem is polynomial-time solvable, interpreting “binary-rational” as a rational number whose numerator and denominator are encoded in binary. An exact linear-programming optimum is Pareto optimal under the specified coupling relation.

This convention matters: the argument guarantees binary-encoded rational coordinates, without requiring their denominators to be powers of two.

1. Scores for outcome classes

For each type \(t\), number its indifference classes from worst to best by \(0,\ldots,k_t-1\), where \(k_t\leq m\). Let \(r_t(s)\) be the class number of roommate type \(s\).

For a composition \(q\) containing \(t\), remove one occurrence of \(t\), leaving roommate types \(a,b\). Define

\(u_t(q)=\min\{r_t(a),r_t(b)\}.\)

Thus \(u_t(q)\) represents exactly the stipulated worst-roommate outcome, including its ties.

For any feasible assignment \(x\), define the total mass-weighted score

\(W(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{\substack{q\in Q\\ n_t(q)>0}}\frac{n_t(q)x_q}{3}\,u_t(q).\)

This is a linear function of \(x\).

2. Every domination strictly increases the score

Lemma. If \(y\) dominates \(x\), then \(W(y)>W(x)\).

Proof. Write the mass of type \(t\) receiving class \(i\) under \(x\) as

\(p^x_{t,i}=\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{q\in Q\\ n_t(q)>0,\ u_t(q)=i}}\frac{n_t(q)x_q}{3}.\)

Feasibility gives \(\sum_i p^x_{t,i}=\mu_t\), and likewise for \(y\).

A coupling witnessing domination is a nonnegative matrix \(\gamma^t_{ij}\) with row sums \(p^x_{t,i}\), column sums \(p^y_{t,j}\), and \(\gamma^t_{ij}=0\) whenever \(j<i\). Its marginals imply

\(\displaystyle\sum_j j\,p^y_{t,j}-\sum_i i\,p^x_{t,i} =\sum_{i,j}(j-i)\gamma^t_{ij}.\)

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Summing over types therefore gives

\(W(y)-W(x)=\displaystyle\sum_t\sum_{j>i}(j-i)\gamma^t_{ij}.\)

By the strict-improvement requirement, \(\sum_t\sum_{j>i}\gamma^t_{ij}>0\). Since every integer difference \(j-i\) appearing here is at least \(1\), the displayed difference is positive. This proves the lemma. ∎

Consequently, every maximizer of \(W\) over feasible assignments is Pareto optimal: a dominating assignment would have a strictly larger objective value.

3. The algorithm

Enumerate all compositions in \(Q\), and set

\(c_q=\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{t\in T\\ n_t(q)>0}}n_t(q)u_t(q).\)

Solve the rational linear program

\(\displaystyle\max\sum_{q\in Q}c_qx_q\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle x_q\geq0\quad(q\in Q),\qquad \sum_{q\in Q}n_t(q)x_q=3\mu_t\quad(t\in T).\)

These constraints imply the required normalization: summing over types and using \(\sum_t n_t(q)=3\) yields \(3\sum_qx_q=3\).

The feasible region is nonempty. Indeed, the assignment

\(\bar x_{\{t,t,t\}}=\mu_t\quad(t\in T)\)

with all other coordinates zero satisfies every constraint. It is also bounded, because nonnegativity and normalization give \(0\leq x_q\leq1\). Thus an optimum exists.

The objective equals \(3W(x)\). By the lemma, every optimum returned by this program is Pareto optimal.

4. Polynomial running time and exact output size

There are

\(N=|Q|=\binom{m+2}{3}=O(m^3)\)

variables and \(m\) mass equations. Their coefficients belong to \(\{0,1,2,3\}\), and \(0\leq c_q\leq3(m-1)\). All scores and coefficients can therefore be constructed in polynomial time, and the resulting rational program has encoding length polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\).

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a nonempty bounded rational linear program can be optimized exactly in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. Exact polynomial-time LP solution methods are discussed, for example, by [Gleixner and Steffy](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.12820v1).

For completeness, the output-size bound can also be established directly. Let \(A_{tq}=n_t(q)\). The homogeneous-room columns form \(3I_m\), so \(A\) has row rank \(m\). An optimal basic feasible solution exists and has at most \(m\) positive coordinates; the standard basic-solution argument is given in [Karger’s linear-programming notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n11-lp.html).

Let \(B\) be its nonsingular \(m\)-column basis matrix. Write \(\mu_t=a_t/d_t\), and put \(D=\prod_t d_t\). The binary length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Cramer’s rule gives each basic coordinate as

\(x_j=\displaystyle\frac{\det B^{(j)}}{D\det B},\)

where \(B^{(j)}\) replaces column \(j\) of \(B\) by the integer vector \(3D\mu\).

Every column of \(B\) has Euclidean norm at most \(3\), because its entries are nonnegative and sum to \(3\). Also,

\(\|3D\mu\|_2\leq3D\|\mu\|_1=3D.\)

Hadamard’s inequality therefore gives

\(|\det B|\leq3^m,\qquad |\det B^{(j)}|\leq D3^m.\)

Hence each coordinate has a binary numerator-and-denominator encoding of length \(O(L+m)\). An optimal basic feasible solution can be obtained in polynomial time, so both exact computation and output length meet the requested bound. Since \(m=\tau\), the running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

5. Why the finite-player hardness does not transfer

The cited finite-player result concerns partitions of indivisible players; its worst-roommate hardness statement is [Theorem 5.5 of Cseh, Fleiner, and Harján](https://www.mechanism-design.org/arch/v004-1/p_03.pdf).

Here composition masses may split continuously, and the complete feasible set has the polynomial-size linear description used above. The construction consequently computes a Pareto-optimal mass assignment without requiring an integral partition.
