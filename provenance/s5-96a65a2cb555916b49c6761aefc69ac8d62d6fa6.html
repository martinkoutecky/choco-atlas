The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over approval types \(A\subseteq C\), with support \(\mathcal A\) of size \(\tau\), can one compute in time polynomial in \(|C|,\tau,L\) a size-\(k\) committee \(W\) satisfying core+ at \(q_*=\frac12\left(\frac1k+\frac1{k+1}\right)\)? Equivalently, find payments \(p_{A,c}\ge0\) for \(c\in A\cap W\) and reserves \(r_A\ge0\) such that \(r_A+\sum_{c\in A\cap W}p_{A,c}=1\), \(p_{A,c}\le r_A\), \(\sum_{A:c\in A}\mu_Ap_{A,c}\le q_*\) for each \(c\in W\), and \(\sum_{A:d\in A}\mu_A r_A<q_*\) for each \(d\notin W\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set of size \(m\), and let \(1\le k\le m\). A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal A\) of \(\tau\) distinct approval types \(A\subseteq C\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_A\), where \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A=1\). Empty approval sets are allowed. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Set \(q_*=\frac12(\frac1k+\frac1{k+1})\). A committee \(W\subseteq C\), with \(|W|=k\), satisfies core+ if it admits nonnegative payments \(p_{A,c}\) for \(c\in A\cap W\) and reserves \(r_A\) satisfying, for every supported type, \(r_A+\sum_{c\in A\cap W}p_{A,c}=1\) and \(p_{A,c}\le r_A\).
Additionally, \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A:c\in A}\mu_Ap_{A,c}\le q_*\) for every \(c\in W\), and \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A:d\in A}\mu_A r_A<q_*\) for every \(d\in C\setminus W\). Payments and reserves are amounts per unit mass; committees remain integral.

Can a size-\(k\) core+ committee be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For explicitly listed finite voters, a core+ committee is computable in polynomial time at quota \(\frac12(\frac nk+\frac n{k+1})\). [Becker, Greger, and Peters](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11912v1).

Result

The rational-mass search problem is polynomial-time solvable.

The key is to compress the finite-voter local-search algorithm by approval type. Concavity makes this compression exact, and the number of replacements is independent of the number of replicated voters.

1. The finite-voter result used

For a probability vector \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\), define

\(f_\ell(x)=\displaystyle\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}\frac{\sum_{j\in S}x_j}{\ell+|S|}\),

and define the truncated objective

\(F_T(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{T-1}\left(\frac{1}{\ell+1}-f_\ell(x)\right)\).

For each fixed cardinality of \(S\), its largest coordinate sum is attained by the largest coordinates of \(x\). Thus this definition agrees with the paper’s definition.

Here is the finite-voter guarantee we use. With \(n\) voters, quota \(Q=nq_*\), and \(T=8k^2(k+1)\), let \(V_T^{(n)}(W)\) maximize \(\sum_i F_T(p_i,r_i)\) over nonnegative payments and reserves satisfying unit budgets and winner-payment bounds \(Q\). Starting from any size-\(k\) committee, repeatedly accept any replacement increasing this value by at least \(n/(4k(k+1))\). When none exists, the committee admits a core+ payment certificate. There are at most \(4k(k+1)H_k\) replacements, where \(H_k=\sum_{j=1}^k1/j\). This is [Theorem 7.1, together with the payment characterization in Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11912v1).

We will implement exactly these comparisons without constructing the replicated electorate.

2. Exact compression of the objective

Write each mass as \(\mu_A=a_A/b_A\), with positive integers \(a_A,b_A\). Choose the common denominator

\(N=\displaystyle\prod_{A\in\mathcal A}b_A\),

and let \(n_A=N\mu_A\). These are positive integers, and \(\sum_A n_A=N\).

Consider, only for the proof, an electorate with \(n_A\) voters approving \(A\), and quota \(Q=Nq_*\). This electorate satisfies the finite theorem’s hypotheses: its candidate set and committee size are unchanged, its approval sets are subsets of \(C\), and

\(\displaystyle\frac{N}{k+1}<Q<\frac{N}{k}\).

No algorithm below constructs these voters.

For a committee \(W\), let \(\mathcal P_\mu(W)\) contain the nonnegative payments and reserves satisfying

\(r_A+\displaystyle\sum_{c\in A\cap W}p_{A,c}=1\),

and, for every \(c\in W\),

\(\displaystyle\sum_{A:c\in A}\mu_Ap_{A,c}\le q_*\).

At this stage, we do not impose the additional core+ inequalities. Define

\(V_T^\mu(W)=\displaystyle\max_{(p,r)\in\mathcal P_\mu(W)}\sum_A\mu_AF_T(p_A,r_A)\).

The feasible set is nonempty, since zero payments are feasible, and compact, since all payments and reserves lie in \([0,1]\).

Compression claim. For every size-\(k\) committee,

\(V_T^{(N)}(W)=N V_T^\mu(W)\).

Proof. Each \(f_\ell\) is the maximum of finitely many linear functions, so it is convex and continuous. Consequently, \(F_T\) is concave and continuous.

First, give every replicated voter of type \(A\) the same vector \((p_A,r_A)\). Feasibility follows from \(n_A=N\mu_A\), and the finite objective equals \(N\sum_A\mu_AF_T(p_A,r_A)\). Hence

\(V_T^{(N)}(W)\ge N V_T^\mu(W)\).

Conversely, take any feasible payment system for the replicated electorate. Average its payment vectors separately within each approval type. Denote these averages by \((\bar p_A,\bar r_A)\). Averaging preserves unit budgets, nonnegativity, and aggregate payments to each candidate. Dividing the finite candidate bounds by \(N\) therefore gives \((\bar p,\bar r)\in\mathcal P_\mu(W)\).

Concavity gives, for every type \(A\),

\(\displaystyle F_T(\bar p_A,\bar r_A)\ge\frac{1}{n_A}\sum_{i:\,A_i=A}F_T(p_i,r_i)\).

Multiplying by \(n_A\) and summing proves the reverse inequality. The maxima exist by compactness and continuity. This proves the claim.

3. Computing the compressed objective by an exact LP

We now show that \(V_T^\mu(W)\) is computable in time polynomial in the encoded input and \(T\).

For each type \(A\), let \(x_A=(p_A,r_A)\) have dimension \(d_A=|A\cap W|+1\). Introduce nonnegative variables \(t_{A\ell}\) and \(a_{A\ell j}\), for \(0\le\ell<T\) and \(1\le j\le d_A\), satisfying

\(a_{A\ell j}\ge x_{Aj}-t_{A\ell}\),

\(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d_A}a_{A\ell j}\le\ell t_{A\ell}\).

These constraints express exactly \(t_{A\ell}\ge f_\ell(x_A)\). To verify this, put

\(E_x(t)=\displaystyle\sum_j\max\{x_j-t,0\}\).

The auxiliary variables exist exactly when \(E_x(t)\le\ell t\), because their smallest feasible values are \(\max\{x_j-t,0\}\). Also,

\(E_x(t)=\displaystyle\max_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}\left(\sum_{j\in S}x_j-|S|t\right)\).

For \(t\ge0\), the empty-set inequality is automatic. The remaining inequalities are precisely

\(\displaystyle t\ge\frac{\sum_{j\in S}x_j}{\ell+|S|}\qquad(\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\})\).

Their conjunction is \(t\ge f_\ell(x)\), including when \(\ell=0\).

Together with the constraints defining \(\mathcal P_\mu(W)\), maximize

\(\displaystyle H_T-\sum_A\mu_A\sum_{\ell=0}^{T-1}t_{A\ell}\).

Every \(\mu_A\) is positive. Thus, for any fixed payment vectors, the maximizing choices are \(t_{A\ell}=f_\ell(x_A)\). Since \(\sum_A\mu_A=1\), the resulting objective is exactly \(\sum_A\mu_AF_T(x_A)\). The LP therefore computes \(V_T^\mu(W)\).

There are \(O(\tau(k+1)T)\) variables and constraints. Its coefficients are the input rationals and integers of magnitude at most \(T\). The constant \(H_T\) may be omitted during optimization and cancels in comparisons. Standard polynomial-time algorithms for rational linear programming compute the optimal value exactly, with polynomial bit complexity.

Empty approval sets cause no difficulty: their vectors consist only of the reserve coordinate.

4. The algorithm and its correctness

If \(k=m\), return \(C\), with all payments zero and all reserves one. There are no losing candidates, so this is a valid certificate.

Otherwise, set

\(T=8k^2(k+1),\qquad h=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4k(k+1)}\).

Start from any size-\(k\) committee \(W\). Compute its compressed objective and those of all committees \((W\setminus\{c\})\cup\{d\}\), for \(c\in W\) and \(d\notin W\). Whenever a replacement \(W'\) satisfies

\(V_T^\mu(W')-V_T^\mu(W)\ge h\),

perform it and repeat. Return \(W\) when no such replacement exists.

By the compression identity, this comparison is equivalent to

\(V_T^{(N)}(W')-V_T^{(N)}(W)\ge\displaystyle\frac{N}{4k(k+1)}\).

Thus every replacement and the stopping condition agree exactly with the finite-voter algorithm on the conceptual replicated electorate. Its theorem guarantees termination and a finite-voter core+ certificate.

It remains to verify that this certificate gives the precise certificate required here. Average its payments and reserves within each type. Unit budgets and the inequalities \(p_{A,c}\le r_A\) are preserved by averaging. For every winner,

\(\displaystyle\sum_{A:c\in A}\mu_A\bar p_{A,c}=\frac1N\sum_{i:c\in A_i}p_{i,c}\le q_*\).

For every loser,

\(\displaystyle\sum_{A:d\in A}\mu_A\bar r_A=\frac1N\sum_{i:d\in A_i}r_i<q_*\).

The strict inequality is preserved exactly. Hence the returned committee satisfies the definition in the question.

5. Running time and rational certificates

The number of replacements is at most \(4k(k+1)H_k\), which is polynomial in \(k\), since \(H_k\le k\). Each iteration evaluates at most \(1+k(m-k)\) committees. Each evaluation is a rational LP of polynomial size, because \(T=O(k^3)\).

Therefore the complete search runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\). The numerical value of \(N\) never enters its running time.

If payments are also desired, solve one additional rational LP for the returned committee. Include its unit-budget, winner-capacity, and \(p_{A,c}\le r_A\) constraints. Introduce \(0\le\eta\le1\), impose

\(\displaystyle\sum_{A:d\in A}\mu_A r_A\le q_*-\eta\qquad(d\notin W)\),

and maximize \(\eta\).

The averaged certificate just proved has positive slack for each loser. Since there are finitely many losers, their minimum slack is positive, so this LP has positive optimum. An exact rational optimal solution therefore supplies the required strict certificate with polynomial encoding length. No inverse-polynomial lower bound on that slack is needed.
