The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{\tau}\) summing to \(1\), and complete median-game profiles \(v_{\theta}(\eta)\), does there exist a finite-support measurable partition into coalition mass vectors \(z^1,\ldots,z^R\) and singleton mass \(s\), with \(\sum_r z^r+s=\mu\), such that no positive-mass coalition \(b\) strictly improves every participant (CR), or weakly improves all participants and strictly improves a positive mass (SCR)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is atomless and given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) types \(\Theta\) with positive masses \(\mu_\theta\) encoded as binary rationals, where \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each complete profile lists \(v_\theta(\eta)\in\mathbb Q\) for every \(\eta\in\Theta\); values may have either sign and ties are allowed. Let \(m=\tau\) be the profile dimension and \(L\) the total input bit length.
For a nonzero coalition vector \(z\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{\tau}\), put \(M(z)=\sum_\eta z_\eta\). Its weighted median interval for type \(\theta\) is \(I_\theta(z)=\{t\in\mathbb R:\sum_{\eta:v_\theta(\eta)<t}z_\eta\leq M(z)/2,\ \sum_{\eta:v_\theta(\eta)>t}z_\eta\leq M(z)/2\}\); its payoff is \(U_\theta(z)=(\min I_\theta(z)+\max I_\theta(z))/2\). Higher payoffs are preferred; singleton payoff is \(v_\theta(\theta)\).
An outcome consists of finitely many positive-mass measurable coalitions with vectors \(z^1,\ldots,z^R\), and residual mass \(s\) partitioned into individual singletons, satisfying \(\sum_{r=1}^Rz^r+s=\mu\). Types may split across coalitions; all outcome masses may be real, and \(R\geq0\) has no prescribed bound.
A deviation selects vectors \(0\leq d^r\leq z^r\) and \(0\leq d^0\leq s\), forming one coalition \(b=\sum_{r=0}^Rd^r\) with \(M(b)>0\). Write \(u_\theta^r=U_\theta(z^r)\) for \(r\geq1\) and \(u_\theta^0=v_\theta(\theta)\). It CR-blocks if \(U_\theta(b)>u_\theta^r\) whenever \(d_\theta^r>0\); it SCR-blocks if these inequalities are weak and the total selected mass experiencing a strict inequality is positive. An outcome is stable when it has no corresponding blocking deviation.

For each \(\alpha\in\{\mathrm{CR},\mathrm{SCR}\}\), is deciding whether an \(\alpha\)-stable outcome exists in \(\mathrm P\) or NP-hard, with running times measured in \(m,\tau,L\)?

Discrete median-game core and strict-core existence are NP-hard, even with nonnegative utilities ([Peters–Elkind, 2015](https://arxiv.org/html/1507.03474)). No result settling this exact atomless, finite-support decision problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified an atomless society so that masses can be split measurably.
  • Encoded complete cardinal profiles as binary rationals, allowing signed values and ties; singleton payoff is the diagonal profile value.
  • Extended the cardinal median convention to masses by taking the midpoint of the weighted median interval, retaining the mean-of-two-middle-values convention at an exact half-mass boundary.
  • Interpreted finite support as finitely many positive-mass coalitions plus individual singletons, allowing arbitrary real outcome masses and no prescribed bound on the number of coalitions.
  • Specified deviations by masses selected from each current coalition and from singleton mass, so comparisons use each participant's current payoff.
  • Required a positive-mass deviation and positive mass of strict beneficiaries for SCR; null sets do not affect stability.
  • There is no separate candidate parameter; defined the profile dimension as m equal to the support size.

Partial resolution with a sharper exact algorithm

The $\mathrm{CR}$ existence problem is in $\mathrm P$: every valid instance admits a $\mathrm{CR}$-stable outcome.

The general $\mathrm{SCR}$ classification remains unresolved here. The new results are:

  • An exact algorithm running in

$$ 2^{O(\tau\log(\tau+1))}\operatorname{poly}(L), $$
improving the bound in the notes.

  • A polynomial-time classification when all profiles have the same set $T$ of maximizers: an $\mathrm{SCR}$-stable outcome exists exactly when

$$ \sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta>\frac12. $$

  • A convexity property of weighted-median payoff level sets.

All arguments below apply to signed rational values and allow ties.

1. Blocking is determined by each type’s minimum payoff

For a given outcome, define
$$ p_\theta = \min\{u_\theta^r:z_\theta^r>0,\ r\geq1\}, $$
including $u_\theta^0=v_\theta(\theta)$ when $s_\theta>0$. This is a minimum over a nonempty finite set, because $\mu_\theta>0$.

For a vector $w\geq0$, write
$$ \operatorname{supp}(w)=\{\theta:w_\theta>0\}. $$

Multiplication by a positive scalar preserves every median interval. Hence
$$ U_\theta(\lambda w)=U_\theta(w) \qquad(\lambda>0). $$

Lemma 1. An outcome is $\mathrm{CR}$-blocked if and only if there exists a nonzero $w\geq0$ satisfying
$$ U_\theta(w)>p_\theta \qquad \text{for every }\theta\in\operatorname{supp}(w). $$
It is $\mathrm{SCR}$-blocked if and only if there exists a nonzero $w\geq0$ satisfying
$$ U_\theta(w)\geq p_\theta \qquad \text{for every }\theta\in\operatorname{supp}(w), $$
with strict inequality for at least one participating type.

Proof. For an actual deviation $b$, every selected origin of type $\theta$ has payoff at least $p_\theta$. Consequently, the deviation satisfies the displayed comparisons. Under $\mathrm{SCR}$, positive strictly improving selected mass implies a strict comparison for at least one participating type.

Conversely, suppose $w$ satisfies the relevant comparisons. For every $\theta$ in its support, choose an origin attaining $p_\theta$, and denote the available mass there by $a_\theta>0$. Since the support is finite, there exists $\lambda>0$ such that
$$ \lambda w_\theta\leq a_\theta \qquad \text{for every }\theta\in\operatorname{supp}(w). $$
Select these masses from the chosen origins. Atomlessness permits selections of these prescribed sizes. Their aggregate vector is $\lambda w$, with the same payoffs as $w$. In the $\mathrm{SCR}$ case, a strictly improving type contributes strictly improving mass $\lambda w_\theta>0$. Thus this is a blocking deviation. $\square$

Two consequences will be used:

  • Increasing all the minimum payoffs preserves stability.
  • Every stable outcome is individually rational:

$$ p_\theta\geq v_\theta(\theta). $$
Otherwise, a small pure-type coalition drawn from a minimum-payoff origin strictly improves all its participants.

2. A finite representation of coalition payoffs

For each type $\theta$, list the distinct values in its profile:
$$ a_{\theta,1}<\cdots<a_{\theta,k_\theta}, \qquad k_\theta\leq\tau. $$
Define
$$ H_{\theta,j}(z) = \sum_{\eta:v_\theta(\eta)\leq a_{\theta,j}}z_\eta, \qquad H_{\theta,0}(z)=0. $$

For a nonzero $z$, the lower median endpoint is $a_{\theta,p}$ exactly when
$$ H_{\theta,p-1}(z)<\frac{M(z)}2\leq H_{\theta,p}(z). $$
The upper endpoint is $a_{\theta,q}$ exactly when
$$ H_{\theta,q-1}(z)\leq\frac{M(z)}2<H_{\theta,q}(z). $$

Indeed, the lower endpoint is the first profile value at which cumulative mass reaches half, while the upper endpoint is the first at which cumulative mass exceeds half. These descriptions also cover an interval between two supported values separated by an exactly balanced split.

It follows that every payoff belongs to
$$ D_\theta = \left\{ \frac{a_{\theta,p}+a_{\theta,q}}2: 1\leq p\leq q\leq k_\theta \right\}, $$
where
$$ |D_\theta|\leq D:=\frac{\tau(\tau+1)}2. $$

A signature specifies a nonempty support $S$ and both median endpoints for every type. There are at most
$$ K:=2^\tau D^\tau = 2^{O(\tau\log(\tau+1))} $$
candidate signatures.

Each signature defines a set $\mathcal C_j$ through homogeneous rational linear conditions:

  • $z_\theta>0$ for $\theta\in S$;
  • $z_\theta=0$ for $\theta\notin S$;
  • the endpoint conditions displayed above.

Every nonempty $\mathcal C_j$ is closed under addition and positive scaling, and has a constant payoff vector $u^j$ and support $S_j$.

Nonemptiness is decidable by linear programming. Impose $M(z)=1$, replace every strict inequality $f(z)>0$ by
$$ f(z)\geq\delta, $$
impose $0\leq\delta\leq1$, and maximize $\delta$. The signature is nonempty exactly when the optimum is positive: any feasible normalized vector has a positive minimum slack over its finitely many strict inequalities, and a positive optimum satisfies all of them.

Choose one normalized representative from each nonempty signature. Every possible coalition has the same support and payoffs as one of these representatives. By Lemma 1, this menu detects every blocking deviation.

3. Complete classification for $\mathrm{CR}$

We use the following form of Scarf’s lemma.

For a finite nonnegative matrix $A$ with no zero column, positive capacities $b$, and a strict ordering of the positive-entry columns in each row, there exists an extreme point of
$$ \{x\geq0:Ax\leq b\} $$
that dominates every column. Domination of column $j$ in row $i$ means that $A_{ij}>0$, the row is saturated, and every positively used column incident with row $i$ is ranked at least as highly as $j$. This is [Lemma 1 of Biró–Csáji–Schlotter](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol334-icalp2025/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.31/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.31.pdf).

Apply the lemma with the normalized representatives as columns of $A$, and $b=\mu$. Include the pure-type columns $e_\theta$. In row $\theta$, order incident columns by $U_\theta$, breaking payoff ties arbitrarily.

All hypotheses hold: the matrix is finite and nonnegative, every column has total mass one, capacities are positive, and the row orderings are strict. Its feasible polytope is bounded, since
$$ \sum_jx_j = \sum_\theta(Ax)_\theta \leq \sum_\theta\mu_\theta = 1. $$

Let $x$ be supplied by the lemma. The pure-type column $e_\theta$ can be dominated only in row $\theta$. Thus every row is saturated:
$$ Ax=\mu. $$
For each $x_j>0$, form a coalition with vector $x_j a^j$, where $a^j$ is its normalized representative. This finite outcome covers the society.

Consider any menu column $j$. Choose a row $\theta$ dominating it. Every used coalition containing type $\theta$ is ranked at least as highly as $j$, so
$$ p_\theta\geq U_\theta(a^j). $$
Here $\theta\in\operatorname{supp}(a^j)$. Consequently, this composition cannot strictly improve all its participating types.

Lemma 1 and the finite-menu representation exclude every $\mathrm{CR}$-block.

Conclusion. Every valid instance has a $\mathrm{CR}$-stable outcome. Therefore the existence decision problem is in $\mathrm P$. This proof does not assert polynomial-time construction of an outcome.

4. Uniformity under $\mathrm{SCR}$

Lemma 2. In an $\mathrm{SCR}$-stable outcome, every positive-mass portion of a type receives the same payoff.

Proof. Consider an existing coalition $z^r$. By definition,
$$ U_\theta(z^r)\geq p_\theta \qquad (\theta\in\operatorname{supp}(z^r)). $$
If any inequality were strict, Lemma 1 would make this composition an $\mathrm{SCR}$-block after selecting a sufficiently small copy from minimum-payoff origins.

If a residual singleton portion received strictly more than its type’s minimum, a pure-type composition would block by the same argument. Hence every portion receives $p_\theta$. $\square$

Write the resulting uniform payoff vector as $q$. Such an outcome is stable exactly when there is no composition $w\neq0$ for which
$$ U_\theta(w)\geq q_\theta \qquad (\theta\in\operatorname{supp}(w)), $$
with at least one strict inequality.

Every coordinate $q_\theta$ belongs to $D_\theta$.

5. A new convexity property of median payoffs

The midpoint convention has a useful aggregation property.

Lemma 3. Fix any profile $v$. For nonzero vectors $x,y\geq0$,
$$ \min\{U_v(x),U_v(y)\} \leq U_v(x+y) \leq \max\{U_v(x),U_v(y)\}. $$

Proof. First prove that, for every real $c$, the condition $U_v(z)\geq c$ is preserved under addition.

Partition the profile entries into
$$ A=\{\eta:v(\eta)<c\}, \qquad B=\{\eta:v(\eta)\geq c\}. $$

If $z(A)<M(z)/2$, both median endpoints are at least $c$, and therefore $U_v(z)\geq c$. If $z(A)>M(z)/2$, both endpoints are below $c$, and therefore $U_v(z)<c$.

When $z(A)=M(z)/2$, both parts have positive mass. Define
$$ \ell(z)=\max\{v(\eta):\eta\in A,\ z_\eta>0\}, $$
$$ h(z)=\min\{v(\eta):\eta\in B,\ z_\eta>0\}. $$
The median interval is $[\ell(z),h(z)]$, so
$$ U_v(z)\geq c \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ell(z)+h(z)\geq2c. $$

Now suppose $U_v(x)\geq c$ and $U_v(y)\geq c$. Both have mass on $A$ at most half their total mass. If either inequality is strict, their sum also has less than half its mass on $A$, and hence satisfies the desired payoff bound.

Otherwise, both are exactly balanced. Then
$$ \ell(x+y)=\max\{\ell(x),\ell(y)\}, \qquad h(x+y)=\min\{h(x),h(y)\}. $$
Choose $t\in\{x,y\}$ attaining the latter minimum. We obtain
$$ \ell(x+y)+h(x+y) \geq \ell(t)+h(t) \geq2c. $$
Thus $U_v(x+y)\geq c$.

Negating the profile negates the payoff:
$$ U_{-v}(z)=-U_v(z), $$
because its median interval is the reflection of the original interval. Applying the proved upper-level property to $-v$ shows that $U_v(z)\leq c$ is also preserved under addition.

Use these two properties with $c$ equal to the minimum and maximum of the two original payoffs. $\square$

Consequently, for a fixed support $S$ and prescribed payoffs $q_\theta$, the set
$$ \left\{ z:\operatorname{supp}(z)=S,\ U_\theta(z)=q_\theta\text{ for every }\theta\in S \right\} $$
is convex and closed under positive scaling.

These sets need not be topologically closed. For example, with values $0,1$, the vectors $(1,1+\varepsilon)$ have payoff $1$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, while their limit $(1,1)$ has payoff $1/2$. The next procedure therefore retains the strict inequalities.

6. A covering lemma that avoids enumerating coalition collections

This lemma is the source of the improved running time.

Suppose $\mathcal C_1,\ldots,\mathcal C_N$ are specified by homogeneous rational linear equalities, weak inequalities, and finitely many strict inequalities
$$ f_{j,\ell}(x)>0. $$
Assume their vectors are nonnegative, and each description requires a nonempty support through positive-coordinate constraints.

We want to decide whether
$$ \mu=\sum_{j=1}^N x^j, \qquad x^j\in\mathcal C_j\cup\{0\}. $$
The zero option means that the corresponding cone is unused.

Lemma 4. This covering question is decidable in time polynomial in the total size of these cone descriptions and the bit length of $\mu$.

Proof. Maintain a set $T$ of cones still permitted, initially all of them.

For this set, construct the closed linear feasibility system $P_T$ by requiring
$$ \sum_{j\in T}x^j=\mu, \qquad x^j\geq0, $$
and imposing every cone condition except that each strict inequality is relaxed to
$$ f_{j,\ell}(x^j)\geq0. $$

If $P_T$ is infeasible, reject. This cannot discard a genuine covering, since every genuine covering satisfies the relaxation.

For each $j\in T$, solve a linear program over $P_T$ maximizing $\delta$, subject to
$$ 0\leq\delta\leq1, \qquad f_{j,\ell}(x^j)\geq\delta \quad\text{for all }\ell. $$
Call $j$ activatable if its optimum is positive.

If a genuine covering uses cone $j$, its nonzero vector satisfies every strict inequality. Taking the minimum of these positive slacks and $1$ gives a feasible positive $\delta$. Therefore every nonactivatable cone must be unused in every genuine covering. Delete all such cones and repeat.

It remains to justify acceptance when every remaining cone is activatable. For each $j\in T$, choose a feasible witness $X^{(j)}$ in which every strict expression of component $j$ is positive. Average these $|T|$ witnesses.

The covering equation, all equalities, and all weak inequalities hold in the average. Fix $k\in T$. Every strict expression of its component is nonnegative in every witness and positive in witness $X^{(k)}$. It is therefore positive in the average. Thus the averaged component belongs to $\mathcal C_k$.

The average consequently supplies a genuine covering using all remaining cones.

Each nonterminal round deletes at least one cone, so there are at most $N$ deletion rounds. Each round uses at most $N+1$ linear programs. All their variables are bounded by the covering equation and nonnegativity, and the margin variable is bounded explicitly. Rational linear programming therefore gives the claimed polynomial-time procedure. $\square$

This proof also explains why merely testing the closed relaxation would be insufficient: it may cover $\mu$ using boundary vectors whose median signatures are incorrect.

7. Improved exact algorithm for general $\mathrm{SCR}$

Enumerate and test all median signatures from Section 2. Store the nonempty cones $\mathcal C_j$, their supports $S_j$, and their payoff vectors $u^j$.

Next enumerate
$$ q\in\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}D_\theta. $$
For each candidate, perform the following steps.

First, check blocking. Reject $q$ if some nonempty signature satisfies
$$ u_\theta^j\geq q_\theta \qquad(\theta\in S_j), $$
with a strict inequality for at least one $\theta\in S_j$.

By Lemma 1, this test detects exactly the compositions that would block an outcome with uniform payoffs $q$.

Second, check attainability. Retain precisely the signatures satisfying
$$ u_\theta^j=q_\theta \qquad(\theta\in S_j). $$
Apply Lemma 4 to decide whether
$$ \mu=\sum_jx^j, \qquad x^j\in\mathcal C_j\cup\{0\}, $$
using only these retained signatures.

Accept if this covering exists.

Correctness

An accepted covering gives finitely many coalitions, each paying $q_\theta$ to every participating type $\theta$. Since the first step excluded every blocking signature, Lemma 1 proves that this outcome is $\mathrm{SCR}$-stable.

Conversely, suppose an $\mathrm{SCR}$-stable outcome exists. Lemma 2 supplies a uniform payoff vector $q$, belonging to the enumerated product.

Replace each positive residual singleton mass by one pure-type coalition of the same mass. Its payoff is unchanged. Group coalitions having the same signature and add their vectors. Their sum remains in that signature cone: homogeneous equalities and weak inequalities are preserved, while every required strict expression remains positive.

This gives exactly a covering by retained cones. Its payoff vector passes the blocking test, so the algorithm accepts.

Running time

There are at most $D^\tau$ candidate payoff vectors and at most
$$ K=2^\tau D^\tau $$
signatures. Constructing the menu requires $K$ linear programs of polynomial size in $(\tau,L)$.

For each payoff candidate, the blocking scan takes polynomial time in $(K,\tau,L)$. Lemma 4 also takes polynomial time in $(K,\tau,L)$: its linear programs have at most $\tau K+1$ variables and polynomially many constraints.

Thus the total running time is
$$ D^\tau\operatorname{poly}(K,\tau,L) = 2^{O(\tau\log(\tau+1))}\operatorname{poly}(L). $$

Since $m=\tau$, this gives an exact algorithm parameterized by the actual support size. It does not establish polynomial time when that size varies.

8. A polynomial-time $\mathrm{SCR}$ classification with a common maximizing set

Assume there exists a nonempty set $T\subseteq\Theta$ such that, for every type $\theta$,
$$ \{\eta:v_\theta(\eta)=V_\theta\}=T, \qquad V_\theta:=\max_{\eta\in\Theta}v_\theta(\eta). $$
The profiles may rank all nonmaximizing types differently.

Theorem 5. Under this assumption, an $\mathrm{SCR}$-stable outcome exists if and only if
$$ \mu(T):=\sum_{\theta\in T}\mu_\theta>\frac12. $$

Proof.

First suppose a stable outcome exists, with uniform payoffs $q$.

For every $h\in T$, its singleton payoff is $V_h$. Individual rationality and the fact that no median payoff exceeds a profile maximum imply
$$ q_h=V_h. $$

Now consider $i\notin T$. If $q_i<V_i$, choose any $h\in T$ and the composition
$$ w=e_i+2e_h. $$
Type $h$ has a strict majority, so
$$ U_i(w)=v_i(h)=V_i, \qquad U_h(w)=v_h(h)=V_h=q_h. $$
This weakly improves both participating types and strictly improves $i$. Lemma 1 gives an $\mathrm{SCR}$-block. Hence
$$ q_i=V_i \qquad\text{for every }i. $$

A nonzero coalition gives its participating types their profile maxima exactly when
$$ z(T)>\frac{M(z)}2. $$
To verify this, if the inequality holds, more than half the mass is at the maximum value in every profile, so both median endpoints equal that maximum. If $z(T)<M(z)/2$, the upper endpoint is below the maximum. If equality holds, the upper endpoint is the maximum and the lower endpoint is strictly below it, so their midpoint is below the maximum.

Types outside $T$ cannot remain singletons, since their singleton payoffs are strictly below their maxima. Replace any residual singleton masses of types in $T$ by pure-type coalitions. All coalitions in the resulting finite covering satisfy
$$ z(T)>z(\Theta\setminus T). $$
Summing these strict inequalities gives
$$ \mu(T)>\mu(\Theta\setminus T)=1-\mu(T), $$
and therefore $\mu(T)>1/2$.

Conversely, if $\mu(T)>1/2$, the grand coalition gives every type its profile maximum. No deviation can strictly improve any selected mass, so it is $\mathrm{SCR}$-stable. $\square$

The hypothesis and the mass inequality can both be checked in polynomial time.

For a concrete no-instance, take two equally massive types with both profiles assigning value $0$ to the first type and $1$ to the second. Here $T$ contains only the second type and $\mu(T)=1/2$, so no $\mathrm{SCR}$-stable outcome exists.

9. Polynomial-size witnesses and the remaining classification

The sharper algorithm is compatible with the previously established upper bound
$$ \mathrm{SCR}\text{-existence}\in\Sigma_2^p. $$
For completeness, the witness arguments are given here.

At most $\tau$ coalitions suffice

Start from a stable outcome and replace singleton residuals by pure-type coalitions.

If more than $\tau$ coalition vectors remain, they are linearly dependent. Express their sum using positive coefficients. Move those coefficients along a nonzero linear dependence until one first becomes zero, keeping all coefficients nonnegative.

Such a dependence has coefficients of both signs: a nonzero nonnegative combination of nonzero nonnegative vectors cannot equal zero. Therefore this movement is possible and removes a coalition while preserving the covering vector.

Repeat until at most $\tau$ coalitions remain. Every retained vector is positively rescaled, so its payoff is unchanged. For $\mathrm{SCR}$, uniform payoffs are preserved; more generally, no type’s minimum decreases. Lemma 1 therefore preserves stability.

Rational witnesses have polynomial bit length

Fix the signatures of these at most $\tau$ coalition vectors. Their coordinates satisfy the covering equation and rational linear signature conditions.

Introduce a common margin $\delta$ for all strict inequalities, with $0\leq\delta\leq1$, and maximize it. The original real outcome supplies a positive feasible margin. Nonnegativity and the covering equation bound every coordinate, so this linear program has an optimal rational vertex with positive margin.

There are at most $\tau^2+1$ variables. Its coefficients have polynomial bit length in the original input. Clearing denominators and using Cramer’s rule with determinant bounds gives polynomial bit length for the vertex coordinates. The positive margin preserves every signature, hence all supports, payoffs, and blocking comparisons.

Thus a stable outcome, if one exists, has a polynomial-size rational certificate.

Likewise, any blocking composition can be normalized to total mass one. Fixing its signature and applying the same margin argument produces a rational blocking composition of polynomial bit length. Its blocking inequalities can be verified by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

Consequently, one can guess a polynomial-size outcome and use an $\mathrm{NP}$ oracle to test whether a blocking composition exists. This establishes membership in $\Sigma_2^p$.

Open point. Neither this upper bound nor the improved exact algorithm proves a polynomial-time algorithm or NP-hardness for general $\mathrm{SCR}$-existence. The requested general classification remains open in this write-up.
