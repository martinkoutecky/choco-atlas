The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational worker-type masses \(\mu_t\), job-type supplies \(\nu_s\), utilities \(u_{ts}\), and job priorities over worker types, can a polynomial-time oracle compute a finite lottery over internally stable feasible mass allocations such that every type with positive \(\mathrm{OSS}_t\) receives expected per-capita utility at least \(\mathrm{OSS}_t/(C\log(2+|T|))\), for a universal constant \(C\)? Here \(\mathrm{OSS}_t\) is the greatest utility attainable by positive mass of type \(t\) in any stable feasible allocation.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|T|\geq1\) worker types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=|S|\geq1\) job types with positive binary-rational supplies \(\nu_s\), whose total need not equal one. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Utilities \(u_{ts}\in[0,1]\) are binary rationals; worker preferences may have ties. Each job type has a strict priority order \(\succ_s\) over worker types, treats workers within a type equally, and prefers any worker to a vacancy. Unmatched workers have utility zero, and zero-utility assignments are forbidden.
A feasible mass allocation is a real matrix \(x\geq0\) with \(\sum_s x_{ts}\leq\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_{ts}\leq\nu_s\), and \(x_{ts}=0\) whenever \(u_{ts}=0\). Set \(x_{t\bot}=\mu_t-\sum_s x_{ts}\), \(x_{\bot s}=\nu_s-\sum_t x_{ts}\), and \(u_{t\bot}=0\).
An allocation is weakly stable if there are no \(t\in T\), \(s\in S\), and \(q\in S\cup\{\bot\}\) with \(x_{tq}>0\), \(u_{ts}>u_{tq}\), and either \(x_{\bot s}>0\) or \(x_{rs}>0\) for some \(r\in T\) with \(t\succ_s r\).
It is internally stable if there are no \(t,r\in T\) and \(q,s\in S\) with \(x_{tq}>0\), \(x_{rs}>0\), \(u_{ts}>u_{tq}\), and \(t\succ_s r\); unmatched worker mass and vacant job mass cannot participate in internal blocking.
Define \(\mathrm{OSS}_t=\max(\{0\}\cup\{u_{ts}:x_{ts}>0\text{ in some weakly stable feasible allocation }x\})\) and per-capita utility \(U_t(x)=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_s u_{ts}x_{ts}\). A finite lottery \(D\) explicitly lists rational allocations and rational probabilities summing to one.
Does there exist a universal constant \(C>0\) and an oracle running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) that always computes a finite lottery \(D\) over internally stable feasible allocations satisfying \(\mathbb E_{x\sim D}U_t(x)\geq\mathrm{OSS}_t/(C\log(2+\tau))\) for every \(t\) with \(\mathrm{OSS}_t>0\), or is this approximation guarantee impossible or NP-hard to achieve?
For finite individual workers and jobs, a polynomial-time algorithm computes a lottery over internally stable matchings with OSS-ratio \(O(\log N)\); some instances require ratio \(\Omega(\log N)\) even without internal stability. [Source](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/8b492b025e64b633d3422cc42571bcf8-Paper-Conference.pdf).
No result settling this exact mass-allocation approximation question was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total worker mass to one; retained arbitrary positive total job supply because the discrete offline model permits unequal numbers of workers and jobs.
  • Carried over rational utilities in [0,1], worker-side ties, rejection of zero-utility assignments, and zero unmatched utility.
  • Specified strict priorities between worker types and equal priority within each type; the supplied type data do not specify priorities between individual workers of the same type.
  • Defined mass blocking using positive mass of dissatisfied workers and either vacancies or positive mass of lower-priority occupants; internal blocking excludes unmatched workers and vacancies.
  • Interpreted OSS as the largest job utility received by positive type mass in any stable allocation, with zero when no positive assignment is attainable; interpreted expected per-capita utility as total expected type utility divided by its mass.
  • Allowed real feasible allocations in the benchmark and required explicitly represented rational allocations and probabilities in the oracle's finite output.
  • Required nonempty worker and job supports and used m for the number of job types.

1. Conclusion

The requested guarantee is impossible, regardless of computation time. This holds even for \(\tau=2\) and \(m=3\), with equal worker masses, total job supply one, strictly positive utilities, strict worker preferences, and a common strict priority order at every job.

The immediate one-type obstruction is that an arbitrarily small supply of a utility-one job makes OSS equal to one. The construction below shows that the obstruction persists in a balanced market with full employment and strict preferences.

2. Construction

Fix an integer \(k\geq2\), and set \(\varepsilon=2^{-k}\).

There are two worker types, with masses
\[ \mu_1=\mu_2=\frac12. \]
There are three job types, with supplies
\[ \nu_a=\nu_b=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}, \qquad \nu_c=1-\varepsilon. \]
Thus total worker mass and total job supply both equal one.

Utilities are
\[ \begin{array}{c|ccc} & a & b & c\\ \hline 1 & 1 & \varepsilon/2 & \varepsilon\\ 2 & \varepsilon/2 & 1 & \varepsilon \end{array} \]
Every utility is positive. Since \(0<\varepsilon/2<\varepsilon<1\), both types have strict preferences.

Every job has the strict priority order \(1\succ_s2\). All numerical inputs are binary rationals.

3. A weakly stable allocation establishing both benchmarks

Consider the rational allocation
\[ x^*= \begin{array}{c|ccc} & a & b & c\\ \hline 1 & \varepsilon/2 & 0 & (1-\varepsilon)/2\\ 2 & 0 & \varepsilon/2 & (1-\varepsilon)/2 \end{array}. \]
Each row sums to its worker mass, and each column sums to its job supply. Hence this allocation is feasible, with no unmatched workers and no vacancies.

We check weak stability directly.

For type \(1\), positive mass is assigned only to \(a\) and \(c\). Mass assigned to \(a\) cannot strictly improve. Mass assigned to \(c\) strictly prefers only \(a\), which is full and contains only type \(1\). Because workers within a type have equal priority, this creates no blocking triple.

For type \(2\), the same argument applies with \(b\) in place of \(a\): its only possible strict improvement is from \(c\) to \(b\), which is full and contains only type \(2\).

These exhaust the possible source assignments, so \(x^*\) is weakly stable. In particular, it is internally stable, since every internal blocking configuration would also be a weak blocking configuration.

Both types receive positive mass at utility one in \(x^*\). Since all utilities are at most one, the definition of OSS gives
\[ \mathrm{OSS}_1=\mathrm{OSS}_2=1. \]

4. An upper bound for every feasible lottery

Let \(x\) be any feasible allocation; stability is not assumed. For either type \(t\), let \(h_t\) denote its utility-one job and \(h_{3-t}\) the other such job.

The row and column constraints imply
\[ x_{t h_t}+x_{t h_{3-t}}+x_{tc}\leq\frac12, \qquad x_{t h_t}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. \]
Consequently,
\[ \begin{aligned} U_t(x) &=2\left(x_{t h_t} +\frac{\varepsilon}{2}x_{t h_{3-t}} +\varepsilon x_{tc}\right)\\ &\leq2\left(x_{t h_t} +\varepsilon\left(x_{t h_{3-t}}+x_{tc}\right)\right)\\ &\leq2\left(x_{t h_t} +\varepsilon\left(\frac12-x_{t h_t}\right)\right)\\ &=\varepsilon+2(1-\varepsilon)x_{t h_t}\\ &\leq2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2. \end{aligned} \]
The last inequality uses \(1-\varepsilon>0\).

This pointwise bound survives taking expectations. Thus every finite lottery \(D\) over feasible allocations satisfies, for both types,
\[ \mathbb E_{x\sim D}U_t(x)\leq2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2. \]
It therefore applies to every lottery over internally stable allocations.

Moreover, \(x^*\) attains equality for both types. Hence the exact smallest simultaneous OSS approximation factor in this instance, even with internal stability imposed, is
\[ R_k=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2}. \]

5. Failure of the proposed guarantee

Suppose a universal constant \(C>0\) provided the requested guarantee. Our instances have \(\tau=2\) and both benchmarks equal to one, so the guarantee would require
\[ \mathbb E_{x\sim D}U_t(x)\geq\frac{1}{C\log4}. \]
Choose \(k\) sufficiently large that
\[ 2^{1-k}<\frac{1}{C\log4}. \]
But the proved feasibility bound gives
\[ \mathbb E_{x\sim D}U_t(x) \leq2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2 <2^{1-k}, \]
a contradiction.

Thus no such lottery necessarily exists, even when internal stability is dropped. More strongly, no finite approximation factor depending only on support size can hold under the stated OSS definition.

The issue is that OSS records the best utility received by *any positive portion* of a type, whereas \(U_t\) averages over its entire mass. Here, only an \(\varepsilon\) fraction of each type can receive utility one. The finite-worker result cited in the question does not eliminate this distinction between an individual benchmark and a type-wide per-capita benchmark.
