The new deterministic result follows from an expander-walk amplification of a constant-gap biclique problem. All reductions below apply to the stated continuous objective, including fractional assignments, empty approval sets, and unused supply.

An algorithmic baseline is a $\tau$-approximation running in $O(m\tau L^2)$ bit operations. Exact optimization takes $2^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time.

$$ F(x)\geq\frac{\operatorname{OPT}}{\rho}. $$
Polynomial-time algorithms are understood in the standard binary Turing model, with explicitly represented rational assignments.

2. The continuous objective and algorithmic baseline

For a feasible assignment, define
$$ y_j=\sum_i x_{ij}, \qquad a_i(x)=\sum_{j:u_{ij}=1}x_{ij}, \qquad W(x)=\sum_i a_i(x), $$
and let
$$ S_0(x)=\{i:v_i(x)=0\}. $$

If $i\in S_0(x)$, nonnegativity implies that every house approved by $i$ has zero assigned mass. Thus $a_i(x)=0$, and all mass $\mu_i$ is envy-free. If $i\notin S_0(x)$, its disapproved-house recipients are envious, so precisely $a_i(x)$ of its mass is envy-free. Consequently,
$$ F(x)=\sum_{i\in S_0(x)}\mu_i+W(x). $$

A polynomial-time $\tau$-approximation

Let $f_i(x)$ denote type $i$'s contribution to $F(x)$. Its maximum possible contribution is
$$ b_i= \begin{cases} \mu_i, &\displaystyle\sum_{j:u_{ij}=0}q_j\geq1,\\[4pt] \displaystyle\min\left\{\mu_i,\sum_{j:u_{ij}=1}q_j\right\}, &\displaystyle\sum_{j:u_{ij}=0}q_j<1. \end{cases} $$

In the first case, assigning everyone exclusively to houses disapproved by $i$ is feasible and makes $i$ inactive.

In the second case, every feasible assignment uses some house approved by $i$, because the total disapproved supply is less than the total resident mass. Therefore $f_i(x)=a_i(x)$. Allocate the displayed minimum of approved supply to $i$. This allocation can be completed: after allocating mass $t$, remaining supply is at least $1-t$, exactly the remaining demand, and there are no restrictions on assigning residents to disapproved houses.

Choose a type maximizing $b_i$ and construct an assignment attaining its contribution. Then
$$ F(x)\geq\max_i b_i, \qquad \operatorname{OPT}\leq\sum_i b_i\leq\tau\max_i b_i. $$
This proves the approximation guarantee.

The scan and allocation require $O(m\tau)$ rational operations. A common denominator has $O(L)$ bits, giving the conservative bit bound $O(m\tau L^2)$.

Attainment and exact optimization

For $S\subseteq I$, define
$$ N(S)=\{j:\text{some }i\in S\text{ approves }j\}. $$
Consider the linear program
$$ \lambda(S)= \max\left\{ \sum_{i\in S}\mu_i+W(x): x\text{ is feasible},\quad y_j=0\text{ for }j\in N(S) \right\}. $$
Discard infeasible programs.

Every feasible solution makes all types in $S$ inactive, so its linear objective is at most $F(x)$. Conversely, every assignment is feasible for the program indexed by $S_0(x)$, where the linear objective equals $F(x)$. Hence
$$ \operatorname{OPT}=\max_{S\subseteq I}\lambda(S). $$

The program for $S=\varnothing$ is feasible because total supply is at least one. Every feasible program is closed and bounded, since $0\leq x_{ij}\leq\mu_i$, and therefore attains its maximum. This also proves that the continuous optimum is attained.

Each program has $m\tau$ variables and polynomial encoding length in $m,\tau,L$. Rational linear programming therefore gives an exact algorithm in
$$ 2^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time.

3. A reduction that respects fractional assignments and support size

Let $G=(A,B,\mathcal E)$ be bipartite with $|A|=|B|=n\geq1$. Set
$$ N=2^{\lceil\log_2 n\rceil}, \qquad b=\frac1{16N^3}. $$
Thus $n\leq N<2n$.

For every $r\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, construct a housing instance $\mathcal H_r$.

Initially introduce a resident label for each $a\in A$, and one special label $*$. Their masses are
$$ \mu_a=\frac1{2N}, \qquad \mu_*=1-\frac n{2N}. $$
Introduce houses $h_0$ and $h_z$ for $z\in B$, with supplies
$$ q_0=1-\left(r-\frac12\right)b, \qquad q_z=b. $$
No resident approves $h_0$. The special resident approves every $h_z$, and
$$ u_{a,h_z}=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (a,z)\notin\mathcal E. $$

All masses are positive and sum to one. All supplies are nonnegative, and
$$ \sum_jq_j =1+\left(n-r+\frac12\right)b>1. $$
Every number is dyadic with $O(\log n)$ bits.

If labels have identical approval rows, merge them and add their masses. This preserves $F$: their activation statuses coincide, and their approved-assignment contributions add. Conversely, split a merged assignment proportionally among its original labels. House totals and the objective remain unchanged. Therefore the actual support size satisfies
$$ \tau\leq n+1, \qquad m=n+1, \qquad L=O(n^2+n\log n). $$

Extraction from every feasible assignment

Given any feasible assignment, split merged rows as just described and define
$$ T=\{z\in B:y_z>0\}, \qquad S=\{a\in A:v_a(x)=0\}, \qquad s=|S|. $$

Because $y_0\leq q_0$,
$$ \sum_{z\in B}y_z =1-y_0 \geq\left(r-\frac12\right)b>0. $$
Thus the special resident is active. Also, $y_z\leq b$, so
$$ |T|b\geq\left(r-\frac12\right)b. $$
The integer $|T|$ is consequently at least $r$.

For $a\in S$ and $z\in T$, approval of $h_z$ would imply $v_a(x)\geq y_z>0$. Hence $a$ disapproves $h_z$, which means $(a,z)\in\mathcal E$. Thus
$$ S\times T\subseteq\mathcal E, \qquad |T|\geq r. $$

Approved assignment mass can occur only at houses indexed by $B$, whose total supply is $nb$. The objective identity therefore gives
$$ F(x)=\frac{s}{2N}+W(x), \qquad 0\leq W(x)\leq nb. $$
These statements require no lower bound on positive assignment entries.

Define
$$ C_r(G)= \max\{|S|:S\times T\subseteq\mathcal E \text{ for some }T\subseteq B,\ |T|\geq r\}. $$
The extraction proves
$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\mathcal H_r)\leq\frac{C_r(G)}{2N}+nb. $$

For the reverse inequality, take a witnessing biclique and choose $r$ right vertices. Use $b$ from each of $r-1$ corresponding houses, $b/2$ from the last, and $q_0$ from $h_0$. These amounts total one. Distribute them proportionally among resident masses. Every resident in the chosen left side approves none of the used houses and is inactive. Therefore
$$ \frac{C_r(G)}{2N} \leq\operatorname{OPT}(\mathcal H_r) \leq\frac{C_r(G)}{2N}+nb. $$

Approximation transfer

Suppose a housing algorithm guarantees factor $\tau^\alpha$, where $0<\alpha<1$. Run it on all $n$ instances and extract their bicliques. Balance each by taking equally sized subsets, and return the largest.

Let $k\geq1$ be the maximum balanced biclique side size in $G$, and put
$$ D_0=(n+1)^\alpha. $$
For the instance with $r=k$, the guarantee gives
$$ F(x)\geq\frac{k}{2ND_0}. $$
Consequently,
$$ s\geq\frac{k}{D_0}-2Nnb. $$
The error satisfies
$$ 2Nnb=\frac{n}{8N^2} \leq\frac1{8N} \leq\frac1{4D_0}, $$
where the last inequality follows from $D_0\leq n+1\leq2N$. Hence
$$ s\geq\frac{k-\frac14}{D_0} \geq\frac{k}{2D_0}. $$
Since $|T|\geq k$, balancing retains side size at least $k/(2D_0)$.

Thus a $\tau^\alpha$-approximation yields a
$$ 2(n+1)^\alpha $$
approximation for Maximum Balanced Biclique. Graphs with $k=0$ are recognized by checking for an edge.

The transfer makes $n$ calls on instances with $m,\tau\leq n+1$ and $L=O(n^2+n\log n)$, so it runs in polynomial time.

4. Known results used for deterministic amplification

We use two precisely stated external results.

Deterministic biclique gap from SSEH. For every fixed $\delta>0$, SSEH implies NP-hardness of distinguishing bipartite graphs with $n$ vertices on each side between:

  • Yes: a biclique with at least $(1/2-\delta)n$ vertices on each side;
  • No: no biclique with both sides of size at least $\delta n$.

Cardinality thresholds are interpreted as integer inequalities. This is [Manurangsi, Lemma 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.03581). Its reduction is deterministic.

Explicit expanders. For every fixed integer degree $d\geq3$ and fixed $\varepsilon>0$, for every sufficiently large $n$ with $nd$ even, a deterministic $\operatorname{poly}(n)$-time algorithm constructs a $d$-regular graph on exactly $n$ vertices whose nontrivial adjacency eigenvalues have absolute value at most
$$ 2\sqrt{d-1}+\varepsilon. $$
This is [Alon, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11673).

We next prove the amplification needed here.

5. A spectral lemma for counting walks

Let $H$ be a $d$-regular undirected graph on $n$ vertices. Write $P$ for its normalized adjacency matrix and
$$ J=\frac{\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}}n. $$
Assume
$$ \|P-J\|_2\leq\lambda. $$

For $U\subseteq V(H)$, put $\rho=|U|/n$. Let $\ell$ be a positive even integer. Among the $nd^\ell$ walks with $\ell$ edges, let $p_U$ be the fraction whose every vertex lies in $U$. Then
$$ p_U\leq\rho(\rho+\lambda)^\ell. $$
If $\rho>\lambda$, also
$$ p_U\geq\rho(\rho-\lambda)^\ell. $$

Proof. Let $D_U$ be the diagonal indicator matrix of $U$, let $e=\mathbf1_U$, and set
$$ Q=D_UPD_U. $$
Counting walks through matrix multiplication gives
$$ p_U=\frac1n e^{\mathsf T}Q^\ell e. $$
Moreover,
$$ \|Q\|_2 \leq\|D_UJD_U\|_2+\|D_U(P-J)D_U\|_2 \leq\rho+\lambda. $$
Since $\|e\|_2^2=n\rho$, the upper bound follows.

For the lower bound,
$$ e^{\mathsf T}Qe =e^{\mathsf T}Pe \geq n\rho^2-\lambda n\rho =n\rho(\rho-\lambda). $$
Diagonalize the real symmetric matrix $Q$. If its eigenvalues are $\theta_a$, the squared coordinates of $e$, divided by $\|e\|_2^2$, give nonnegative weights $w_a$ summing to one. Since $\ell$ is even, the function $t\mapsto t^\ell$ is convex on $\mathbb R$. Jensen's inequality gives
$$ \frac{e^{\mathsf T}Q^\ell e}{\|e\|_2^2} =\sum_a w_a\theta_a^\ell \geq \left(\sum_a w_a\theta_a\right)^\ell = \left(\frac{e^{\mathsf T}Qe}{\|e\|_2^2}\right)^\ell \geq(\rho-\lambda)^\ell. $$
The last step uses $\rho-\lambda>0$. Multiplication by $\rho$ proves the claim. The evenness assumption explicitly handles possible negative eigenvalues.

6. Deterministic hardness for every exponent below one-half

Fix
$$ 0<\alpha<\frac12. $$
We prove that achieving factor $\tau^\alpha$ is NP-hard under SSEH.

Choose a fixed $\beta$ satisfying
$$ \alpha<\beta<\frac12. $$
Choose an integer $D\geq8$ sufficiently large that, with
$$ \delta=\frac1D, \qquad d=16D^2, \qquad B=\frac D8, $$
we have
$$ B>d^\beta. $$
Such a choice exists because
$$ \lim_{D\to\infty} \frac{\log(D/8)}{\log(16D^2)} =\frac12. $$
Finally choose a positive integer $K$ with
$$ K(\beta-\alpha)>\alpha. $$
All these parameters are constants depending only on $\alpha$.

Construction of the amplified graph

Take an instance $G=(A,B_G,\mathcal E)$ of the deterministic biclique gap, with $n$ vertices on each side and parameter $\delta$.

For sufficiently large $n$, construct a degree-$d$ expander on $n$ vertices using Alon's theorem with $\varepsilon=1$. Its normalized nontrivial eigenvalues have absolute value at most
$$ \lambda \leq\frac{2\sqrt{d-1}+1}{d} \leq\frac3{\sqrt d} =\frac3{4D} \leq\delta. $$
The theorem's parity condition holds because $d$ is even.

Identify copies of the expander's vertex set with $A$ and $B_G$. Let $\ell$ be the least positive even integer such that
$$ d^\ell\geq n^K. $$
For $n\geq2$,
$$ n^K\leq d^\ell<d^2n^K. $$

Construct a bipartite graph $\widehat G$ whose left and right vertices are all $\ell$-edge expander walks on the respective sides. Each side has
$$ p=nd^\ell $$
vertices. If walks are written
$$ a=(a_0,\ldots,a_\ell), \qquad b=(b_0,\ldots,b_\ell), $$
declare them adjacent precisely when
$$ (a_t,b_u)\in\mathcal E \quad\text{for every }0\leq t,u\leq\ell. $$

The construction is deterministic. It takes
$$ \operatorname{poly}(n)+O\!\left(p^2(\ell+1)^2\right) $$
time, and
$$ n^{K+1}\leq p<d^2n^{K+1}. $$
It is therefore polynomial-time for the fixed parameters.

Completeness

In a yes-instance, choose a biclique $S\times T$ in $G$ with both densities at least
$$ c=\frac12-\delta. $$
Every left walk entirely in $S$ is adjacent to every right walk entirely in $T$.

Since $\lambda\leq\delta$ and $D\geq8$,
$$ c\geq\frac14, \qquad c-\lambda\geq\frac12-2\delta\geq\frac14. $$
The spectral lemma shows that each side contains at least
$$ p\,c(c-\lambda)^\ell \geq\frac{p}{4^{\ell+1}} $$
such walks. Thus the optimum balanced side size in $\widehat G$ is at least
$$ k_{\mathrm Y}=\frac{p}{4^{\ell+1}}. $$

Soundness

Consider any biclique $\mathcal S\times\mathcal T$ in $\widehat G$. Flatten its walks into sets of original vertices:
$$ U=\bigcup_{a\in\mathcal S}\{a_0,\ldots,a_\ell\}, \qquad V=\bigcup_{b\in\mathcal T}\{b_0,\ldots,b_\ell\}. $$
The definition of adjacency gives
$$ U\times V\subseteq\mathcal E. $$

In a no-instance, therefore either $|U|<\delta n$ or $|V|<\delta n$. Suppose the first holds. Every walk in $\mathcal S$ stays in $U$. With $\rho=|U|/n<\delta$, the spectral upper bound gives
$$ |\mathcal S| \leq p\,\rho(\rho+\lambda)^\ell \leq p\,\delta(2\delta)^\ell \leq p(2\delta)^{\ell+1}. $$
The other case gives the same bound on $|\mathcal T|$. Consequently every balanced biclique has side size at most
$$ k_{\mathrm N} =p\left(\frac2D\right)^{\ell+1}. $$

The gap exceeds the transferred approximation factor

The ratio of the two bounds is
$$ \frac{k_{\mathrm Y}}{k_{\mathrm N}} =\left(\frac D8\right)^{\ell+1} =B^{\ell+1} >Bd^{\beta\ell} \geq Bn^{K\beta}. $$
On the other hand,
$$ 2(p+1)^\alpha \leq2^{1+\alpha}p^\alpha <2^{1+\alpha}d^{2\alpha}n^{\alpha(K+1)}. $$
The exponent difference is
$$ K\beta-\alpha(K+1) =K(\beta-\alpha)-\alpha>0. $$
Thus, for all sufficiently large $n$,
$$ \frac{k_{\mathrm Y}}{k_{\mathrm N}}>2(p+1)^\alpha. $$

If a polynomial-time $\tau^\alpha$-approximation for housing existed, Section 3 would return a balanced biclique in $\widehat G$ of side size at least
$$ \frac{k_{\mathrm Y}}{2(p+1)^\alpha}>k_{\mathrm N} $$
on every yes-instance. On no-instances, no such biclique exists. Comparing the returned side size against the explicitly computable rational threshold $k_{\mathrm N}$ decides the SSE-hard promise problem.

All housing calls have
$$ m,\tau\leq p+1, \qquad L=O(p^2+p\log p). $$
There are $p$ calls, and $p$ is polynomial in the original $n$. The finitely many smaller original instances can be decided by exhaustive enumeration.

This proves deterministic conditional NP-hardness for every fixed $\alpha<1/2$. Setting $\alpha=1-\gamma$ settles the requested hardness alternative whenever
$$ \frac12<\gamma<1. $$
As usual, conditional NP-hardness rules out a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm if $\mathrm P\neq\mathrm{NP}$.

7. Full-range hardness with the additional assumption

For the remaining range, the earlier biclique transfer remains valid, but its complexity qualification cannot be dropped.

[Manurangsi, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.03581) states that, assuming SSEH and $\mathrm{NP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{BPP}$, Maximum Balanced Biclique on graphs with $v$ total vertices has no polynomial-time $v^{1-\varepsilon}$-approximation for any fixed $\varepsilon>0$. Appendix B uses randomized amplification.

Fix any $\gamma\in(0,1)$. A housing approximation of factor $\tau^{1-\gamma}$ would, by Section 3, give factor
$$ 2(n+1)^{1-\gamma} $$
for biclique graphs with $v=2n$ vertices. For sufficiently large $n$,
$$ 2(n+1)^{1-\gamma}\leq(2n)^{1-\gamma/2}, $$
because the ratio of the left side to the right side tends to zero. The finitely many smaller graphs can be solved exactly. Applying the cited theorem with $\varepsilon=\gamma/2$ gives a contradiction under the two stated assumptions.

Thus the full requested exponent is established with $\mathrm{NP}\nsubseteq\mathrm{BPP}$ added. Under SSEH alone, the deterministic argument above settles $\gamma>1/2$; it does not settle $0<\gamma\leq1/2$.
