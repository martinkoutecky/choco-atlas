The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite bipartite role graph \(H=(A\cup B,E)\), rational role masses \(\mu_v\) summing to \(1\), rational edge surpluses \(w_e\), and tolerance \(\epsilon>0\), maximize \(\sum_{e\in E} w_e x_e\) subject to \(x_e\geq 0\) and \(\sum_{e\ni v}x_e\leq\mu_v\). Under a promise that this mass matching LP has a unique optimum with gap \(\Delta>0\) to the next-best vertex and admits a stable balanced outcome, compute a maximizing flow \(x\) and per-role earnings \(\gamma_v\) satisfying \(\gamma_u+\gamma_v\geq w_{uv}\), \(x_{uv}>0\Rightarrow\gamma_u+\gamma_v=w_{uv}\), \(\gamma_v=0\) for roles with unused mass, and \(\epsilon\)-balance on every traded edge using each role’s best alternative. Does such an algorithm run in time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(|E|\), input bit length \(L\), \(1/\Delta\), and \(1/\epsilon\), independently of the represented headcount?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a finite simple bipartite role graph \(H=(A\cup B,E)\) and the list of its \(\tau=|A\cup B|\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_v\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Put \(m=|E|\geq1\). Edge surpluses are binary rationals \(0<w_e\leq1\), with \(\max_e w_e=1\); the tolerance \(\epsilon>0\) is a binary rational.
Let \(P=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{E}:\sum_{e\ni v}x_e\leq\mu_v\text{ for every }v\}\). Earnings \(\gamma_v\geq0\) are common per-unit earnings for role \(v\), not aggregate income.
A stable outcome consists of \(x\in P\) and \(\gamma\) satisfying \(\gamma_u+\gamma_v\geq w_{uv}\) on every edge, equality whenever \(x_{uv}>0\), and \(\gamma_v=0\) whenever \(\sum_{e\ni v}x_e<\mu_v\).
For \(uv\in E\), define the best alternative \(a_{u\setminus v}(\gamma)=\max(\{0\}\cup\{w_{uz}-\gamma_z:uz\in E,\ z\ne v\})\). An outcome is \(\epsilon\)-balanced if \(\left|(\gamma_u-a_{u\setminus v}(\gamma))-(\gamma_v-a_{v\setminus u}(\gamma))\right|\leq\epsilon\) on every edge with \(x_{uv}>0\); exact balance means zero discrepancy.
Promise that maximizing \(w\cdot x\) over \(P\) has a unique optimum \(x^*\), and supply a binary rational \(\Delta>0\) such that \(w\cdot x^*-w\cdot y\geq\Delta\) for every other vertex \(y\) of \(P\). Promise also that \(x^*\) admits a stable, exactly balanced earnings vector. The computational task is to return \(x^*\) and rational earnings forming a stable, \(\epsilon\)-balanced outcome.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. Express running times in \(m,\tau,L\), with dependence on \(1/\Delta\) and \(1/\epsilon\) explicit, independently of any represented headcount.

Is this promised computation in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete unit-capacity networks with normalized positive weights and a unique integral matching-LP optimum, natural bargaining dynamics finds a stable, \(\epsilon\)-balanced outcome in \(O(\max\{n^4/g^2,1/\epsilon^2\})\) iterations for fixed damping, where \(g\) is the discrete gap. [Bayati et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1004.2079v2)
Integer-capacity network bargaining also admits a polynomial-time algorithm, but permits contract-specific earnings and uses different outside options. [Farczadi, Georgiou and Könemann](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.4302)
No result settling this precise rational-mass, common-role-earnings formulation was identified.

Result

The promised computation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard in the search sense: a polynomial-time algorithm that succeeds on every promised input would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The hardness holds with all edge weights equal to \(1\), dyadic masses, fixed tolerance \(\epsilon=1/4\), and a supplied gap satisfying \(1/\Delta<3\tau\). Consequently, unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), there is no algorithm with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\Delta,1/\epsilon\).

The proof below explicitly handles the existence promise.

1. The Boolean source problem

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of \(3\)-SAT; see [Barak’s textbook](https://introtcs.org/public/lec_13_Cook_Levin.html).

First transform a \(3\)-CNF formula into a CNF formula whose clauses are either entirely positive or entirely negative. For each original variable \(z_j\), introduce Boolean variables \(p_j,n_j\), replace the literals \(z_j,\neg z_j\) by \(p_j,n_j\), respectively, and add the clauses

\[ (p_j\lor n_j),\qquad(\neg p_j\lor\neg n_j). \]

These two clauses require exactly one of \(p_j,n_j\) to be true. Thus the transformed formula is satisfiable exactly when the original formula is satisfiable, and any satisfying assignment determines one for the original formula.

Let the transformed formula have variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_q\), \(c\) nonempty clauses, and \(r\) literal occurrences. Write a positive clause as \(\bigvee_{i\in C}z_i\), and a negative clause as \(\bigvee_{i\in C}\neg z_i\). Repeated literals can be removed. We may restrict to \(q,c\geq1\), since trivial input formulas can be handled directly.

2. Construction of the society

All edge weights are \(1\). We distinguish support edges, on which the constructed optimum will be positive, from additional edges.

Variable roles

For each Boolean variable \(z_i\), introduce six roles:

  • Two leaves \(a_i^1,a_i^2\in A\), and a center \(C_i\in B\).
  • Two leaves \(b_i^1,b_i^2\in B\), and a center \(D_i\in A\).

The four support edges are

\[ a_i^1C_i,\quad a_i^2C_i,\quad D_ib_i^1,\quad D_ib_i^2. \]

Add the two edges

\[ a_i^1b_i^1,\qquad a_i^2b_i^2. \]

Clause roles

For each positive clause \(C\), introduce a leaf \(\ell_C\in A\) and a center \(h_C\in B\). Add the support edge \(\ell_Ch_C\), and the additional edges

\[ \ell_Cb_i^1\qquad(i\in C). \]

For each negative clause \(C\), introduce a leaf \(\ell_C\in B\) and a center \(h_C\in A\). Add the support edge \(h_C\ell_C\), and the additional edges

\[ a_i^1\ell_C\qquad(i\in C). \]

Every clause center has degree one. The resulting graph is simple and bipartite.

Masses and numerical parameters

Give every leaf an auxiliary integer capacity \(1\), and every center an auxiliary integer capacity \(2\). Their sum is

\[ T=8q+3c. \]

Let \(Q=2^{\lceil\log_2T\rceil}\). Increase the capacity of one clause center by \(Q-T\), and denote the resulting capacities by \(d_v\). Define

\[ \mu_v=\frac{d_v}{Q},\qquad \Delta=\frac1Q,\qquad \epsilon=\frac14. \]

Every mass is positive and dyadic, and their sum is \(1\). Every clause center has mass at least \(2/Q\).

The construction has

\[ \tau=6q+2c,\qquad m=6q+c+r. \]

Moreover,

\[ \frac1\Delta=Q<2T\leq3\tau,\qquad \frac1\epsilon=4. \]

The input length is \(L=O((q+c+r)\log(q+c+r))\), and the construction takes polynomial time.

3. Unique optimum and the promised gap

Let \(S\) be the set of support edges. Define

\[ x_e^*= \begin{cases} 1/Q,&e\in S,\\ 0,&e\notin S. \end{cases} \]

This vector is feasible:

  • Every leaf uses exactly its mass \(1/Q\).
  • Every variable center uses \(2/Q\), exactly its mass.
  • Every clause center uses \(1/Q\), strictly less than its mass.

There are \(K=4q+c\) leaves. Every support edge joins a leaf to a center, whereas every additional edge joins two leaves. Consequently, for every feasible \(x\),

\[ \sum_e x_e = \sum_{\text{leaves }v}\sum_{e\ni v}x_e - \sum_{e\notin S}x_e \leq \frac KQ-\sum_{e\notin S}x_e. \]

All weights are \(1\), so \(w\cdot x^*=K/Q\). If a feasible \(x\) attains this value, the displayed identity forces every additional edge to have zero flow and every leaf to be saturated. Each leaf has exactly one support edge. Therefore every support edge has flow \(1/Q\), and \(x=x^*\).

Thus \(x^*\) is the unique optimum.

To establish the supplied vertex gap, scale flows by writing \(z=Qx\). The resulting capacity constraints have integer right-hand sides. The bipartite vertex-edge incidence matrix is totally unimodular: after negating the rows indexed by \(B\), each column has one \(+1\) and one \(-1\). For any square submatrix, a column with at most one nonzero permits determinant expansion; if every column has two nonzeros, the rows sum to zero. Induction therefore gives determinants in \(\{0,1,-1\}\). Adding the nonnegativity rows preserves this property.

At a vertex, an invertible square matrix of active constraints consequently has determinant \(1\) or \(-1\). Its integer right-hand side implies that the vertex \(z\) is integral. Hence every vertex objective \(w\cdot x\) is an integer multiple of \(1/Q\).

Since \(x^*\) is uniquely optimal, every other vertex \(y\) satisfies

\[ w\cdot x^*-w\cdot y\geq\frac1Q=\Delta. \]

Both optimization promises hold for every constructed formula, independently of its satisfiability.

4. Stable earnings and balance discrepancies

Consider stable earnings with \(x^*\). Equality on the variable support edges gives parameters \(t_i,s_i\) such that

| Role | Earnings |
|---|---|
| \(a_i^1,a_i^2\) | \(t_i\) |
| \(C_i\) | \(1-t_i\) |
| \(D_i\) | \(s_i\) |
| \(b_i^1,b_i^2\) | \(1-s_i\) |

Nonnegativity gives \(t_i,s_i\in[0,1]\). Stability on \(a_i^jb_i^j\) gives

\[ t_i+(1-s_i)\geq1, \]

or \(t_i\geq s_i\).

Each clause center is unsaturated, so stability requires its earnings to be zero. Equality on its support edge then gives clause-leaf earnings equal to \(1\).

Conversely, any parameters satisfying

\[ 0\leq s_i\leq t_i\leq1 \]

give stable earnings with \(x^*\): the additional variable edges satisfy stability by \(t_i\geq s_i\), and every clause incidence edge has an endpoint earning \(1\).

We now calculate every required balance discrepancy.

Variable support edges

For an edge \(a_i^jC_i\), the other support neighbor of \(C_i\) offers \(1-t_i\), exactly \(C_i\)’s earnings. Thus \(C_i\)’s surplus over its alternative is zero.

The alternative of \(a_i^j\) is \(s_i\): its additional variable edge offers \(1-(1-s_i)=s_i\), and any incident clause leaf earns \(1\), offering zero. Therefore \(a_i^j\)’s surplus is \(t_i-s_i\).

Similarly, on \(D_ib_i^j\), the center \(D_i\) has surplus zero, while \(b_i^j\)’s alternative is \(1-t_i\). Its surplus is

\[ (1-s_i)-(1-t_i)=t_i-s_i. \]

Hence all four variable support edges have discrepancy \(t_i-s_i\).

Positive clauses

A positive clause leaf earns \(1\). Excluding its center, its best alternative is

\[ a_{\ell_C\setminus h_C}=\max_{i\in C}s_i. \]

Its center has earnings and alternative both zero. The support-edge discrepancy is therefore

\[ 1-\max_{i\in C}s_i. \]

Negative clauses

A negative clause leaf has best alternative

\[ a_{\ell_C\setminus h_C} = \max_{i\in C}(1-t_i) = 1-\min_{i\in C}t_i. \]

Its support-edge discrepancy is consequently

\[ \min_{i\in C}t_i. \]

These calculations use precisely the task’s role-based alternatives, excluding the partner role.

5. Exact existence and extraction from approximate earnings

Satisfying assignments provide exactly balanced earnings

Suppose the transformed Boolean formula has a satisfying assignment \(\beta_i\in\{0,1\}\). Set

\[ t_i=s_i=\beta_i. \]

The preceding characterization proves stability. Every variable support-edge discrepancy is zero.

Every positive clause contains an index with \(\beta_i=1\), so its discrepancy \(1-\max_{i\in C}s_i\) is zero. Every negative clause contains an index with \(\beta_i=0\), so its discrepancy \(\min_{i\in C}t_i\) is zero.

Therefore the constructed society satisfies the exact-balance existence promise whenever the formula is satisfiable. The earnings supplied by this argument are rational.

Every requested approximate outcome provides a satisfying assignment

Now suppose the construction admits a stable, \(1/4\)-balanced outcome with \(x^*\). Define a Boolean assignment by

\[ \beta_i= \begin{cases} 1,&t_i>1/2,\\ 0,&t_i\leq1/2. \end{cases} \]

For a positive clause, approximate balance implies

\[ \max_{i\in C}s_i\geq3/4. \]

Choose an index attaining that maximum. Stability gives \(t_i\geq s_i\geq3/4\), so \(\beta_i=1\). The positive clause is satisfied.

For a negative clause, approximate balance implies

\[ \min_{i\in C}t_i\leq1/4. \]

An index attaining that minimum has \(\beta_i=0\), satisfying the negative clause.

Thus every valid requested output yields a satisfying assignment. In particular, an unsatisfiable formula produces no stable, \(1/4\)-balanced outcome with \(x^*\).

6. Why the existence promise does not defeat the reduction

The construction gives a promise-preserving reduction from finding an assignment for a satisfiable \(3\)-CNF formula:

  • A satisfiable formula produces an input satisfying every promise.
  • Every permitted output yields a satisfying assignment in polynomial time.

For completeness, the following bounded-simulation argument establishes the stated implication for decision complexity without assuming correct behavior outside the promise.

Suppose an algorithm succeeds on every promised input within a polynomial running-time bound \(p(m,\tau,L)\). Given any \(3\)-CNF formula:

  • Construct the society above.
  • Simulate the algorithm for \(p(m,\tau,L)\) steps.
  • If an output is obtained, threshold its earnings as above, translate the assignment back to the original variables, and check the original formula.
  • Accept exactly when that check succeeds.

On satisfiable formulas, every promise holds, so the algorithm returns a valid outcome within the bound and the check succeeds. On unsatisfiable formulas, no assignment can pass the check, regardless of the algorithm’s behavior; a timeout also results in rejection.

This would decide \(3\)-SAT in polynomial time. Therefore a polynomial-time solver for the promised computation would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The same argument excludes a running-time bound polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\Delta,1/\epsilon\), because our instances have \(1/\Delta<3\tau\) and \(1/\epsilon=4\).

7. A general upper bound

A finite upper bound, independent of any represented headcount, is

\[ O\!\left(\tau^{2m}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\right). \]

First compute \(x^*\) by rational linear programming. Rational linear programming runs in time polynomial in the number of variables, constraints, and coefficient bit length; here those quantities are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

For each ordered endpoint of each positive-flow edge \(uv\), enumerate a term attaining its outside-option maximum: either zero or one of the terms \(w_{uz}-\gamma_z\), with \(z\ne v\). There are at most \(\tau\) choices at each of at most \(2m\) ordered endpoints.

For each selection, impose:

  • All stable-earnings constraints.
  • Linear inequalities making each selected term dominate every other candidate.
  • Exact balance, using the selected affine terms as the alternatives.

This is a rational linear feasibility problem with polynomially many constraints and polynomial coefficient bit length. The exact-balance promise guarantees that some selection is feasible, and a feasible rational solution supplies the required earnings.

This algorithm returns exact balance, so its bound has no additional dependence on \(1/\Delta\) or \(1/\epsilon\). The hardness proof shows why a polynomial dependence on those parameters cannot replace the exponential enumeration unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
