The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), per-capita supplies \(q_g\ge 0\) for \(m\) good categories, and nonnegative valuation vectors \(v_1,v_2\) satisfying \(\sum_g q_g v_r(g)=1\) for each \(r\in\{1,2\}\), consider unit population mass with type masses \(\alpha\) and \(1-\alpha\). Choose masses \(x_{r,z}\ge 0\) over integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^m\), with \(\sum_z x_{1,z}=\alpha\), \(\sum_z x_{2,z}=1-\alpha\), and \(\sum_{r,z}x_{r,z}z_g=q_g\) for each category \(g\). Require EF1 across every pair of supported bundles: for each \((r,z),(s,z')\) with \(x_{r,z},x_{s,z'}>0\) and \(z'\ne 0\), some \(g\) with \(z'_g>0\) satisfies \(v_r\cdot z\ge v_r\cdot(z'-e_g)\); empty bundles impose no envy constraint. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the supremum welfare \(\sum_{r,z}x_{r,z}(v_r\cdot z)\) over feasible allocations. Can one return a feasible allocation with finite rational support and welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/2\) in time polynomial in \(m\) and the binary input length \(L\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass encoded as a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here \(\tau=2\), with distinct additive valuation vectors \(v_1,v_2\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\) and masses \(a_1=\alpha\), \(a_2=1-\alpha\), where \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1)\); total population mass is one.
There are \(m\ge1\) good categories with per-capita supplies \(q_g\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). Assume \(v_1(g)+v_2(g)>0\) for every category and \(\sum_g q_gv_r(g)=1\) for each \(r\in\{1,2\}\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
An allocation consists of nonnegative real masses \(x_{r,z}\) on integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), satisfying \(\sum_zx_{r,z}=a_r\) and \(\sum_{r,z}x_{r,z}z_g=q_g\) for every category. Bundle support may be countably infinite.
Require EF1 across all supported type–bundle pairs, including pairs within one type: whenever \(x_{r,z},x_{s,z'}>0\) and \(z'\ne0\), some \(g\) with \(z'_g>0\) satisfies \(v_r\cdot z\ge v_r\cdot(z'-e_g)\), where \(e_g\) is the coordinate unit vector. An empty target bundle imposes no constraint.
Define \(W(x)=\sum_{r,z}x_{r,z}(v_r\cdot z)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\sup\{W(x):x\text{ is feasible and EF1}\}\).

Can an algorithm always return a feasible EF1 allocation with finite rational support and welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/2\), with bundles and masses encoded in binary, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For finite societies with two distinct normalized nonnegative additive valuations, a polynomial-time \(2\)-approximation for welfare maximization among complete EF1 allocations is known. Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

A polynomial-time \(2\)-approximation

Yes. There is an algorithm running in time polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\) that returns a feasible EF1 allocation with rational masses, at most \(m+2\) supported type–bundle pairs, and welfare at least \(1\). Since \(\mathrm{OPT}\leq 2\), this gives the required approximation.

The construction uses the valuation-ratio ordering appearing in the cited paper’s Approx1 algorithm. [Source, Section 2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol359-isaac2025/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2025.49/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2025.49.pdf). The key additional step is an exact batching procedure that handles binary-encoded quantities without expanding the population or the goods.

1. An ordered allocation suffices for the welfare guarantee

Define \(\rho(g)=v_1(g)/v_2(g)\), with \(\rho(g)=+\infty\) when \(v_2(g)=0\). The assumption \(v_1(g)+v_2(g)>0\) makes this well-defined.

Call an allocation ordered if every category appearing in a type-1 bundle has ratio at least that of every category appearing in a type-2 bundle.

For a complete allocation, let \(c_{r,g}=\sum_z x_{r,z}z_g\). Thus \(c_{1,g}+c_{2,g}=q_g\). Set

\(A=\sum_g c_{1,g}v_1(g)\), \(B=\sum_g c_{1,g}v_2(g)\).

Normalization gives

\(\sum_g c_{2,g}v_1(g)=1-A\), \(\sum_g c_{2,g}v_2(g)=1-B\).

If the allocation is ordered, then whenever \(c_{1,g}c_{2,h}>0\),

\(v_1(g)v_2(h)-v_2(g)v_1(h)\geq 0\).

Consequently,

\(A-B=A(1-B)-B(1-A)=\sum_{g,h}c_{1,g}c_{2,h}\bigl(v_1(g)v_2(h)-v_2(g)v_1(h)\bigr)\geq 0\).

Its welfare therefore satisfies

\(W=A+(1-B)\geq 1\).

For every feasible allocation, including one with countably infinite support, nonnegativity and the supply equations give

\(W\leq \sum_{r,z}x_{r,z}\bigl((v_1+v_2)\cdot z\bigr)=\sum_g q_g(v_1(g)+v_2(g))=2\).

The equality is valid for countable support by interchanging nonnegative sums. Hence \(\mathrm{OPT}\leq 2\).

It remains to construct a complete, ordered EF1 allocation efficiently.

2. Representation and ordering invariants

Ignore categories with zero supply and sort the remaining categories by nonincreasing \(\rho\).

Represent a partial allocation by a finite list of classes \((r_i,w_i,z_i)\), where \(w_i>0\) is the mass receiving bundle \(z_i\). Duplicate classes may be merged at the end. Let \(Q_g\) be the remaining supply of category \(g\).

We maintain:

  • \(\sum_{i:r_i=r}w_i=a_r\) for each type \(r\).
  • \(\sum_i w_i z_{i,g}=q_g-Q_g\), with \(Q_g\geq 0\).
  • The current supported type–bundle pairs satisfy EF1.
  • Every category already assigned to type 1 has ratio at least every remaining category and every category assigned to type 2. Every category assigned to type 2 has ratio at most every remaining category.

Initially, the two classes are \((1,\alpha,0)\) and \((2,1-\alpha,0)\), and \(Q_g=q_g\). All invariants hold.

In a phase, let \(f\) and \(h\) be the first and last remaining categories. They may coincide. Type 1 receives only additional copies of \(f\), and type 2 receives only additional copies of \(h\). Define

\(g_1=f\), \(g_2=h\), \(b_r=v_r(f)\), \(d_r=v_r(g_r)\).

Call type \(r\) active when \(b_r>0\). At least one type is active.

Every active type has \(d_r>0\). This is immediate for type 1. For type 2, if \(v_2(f)>0\), then \(\rho(f)\) is finite; since \(\rho(h)\leq\rho(f)\), we must also have \(v_2(h)>0\).

Two inequalities will be used. For every class of an active type \(r\), and either observing type \(s\), the ordering invariant gives

\(v_s\cdot z_i\leq \dfrac{v_s(g_r)}{d_r}\,(v_r\cdot z_i)\). \((1)\)

For \(s=r\), this is equality. For the other type, it follows by summing the categorywise ratio inequality: old type-1 goods have ratio at least \(\rho(f)\), and old type-2 goods have ratio at most \(\rho(h)\).

Also, for every active \(r\) and either \(s\),

\(\dfrac{v_s(g_r)}{d_r}\,b_r\leq b_s\). \((2)\)

For \(r=1\), this is equality. For \(r=2,s=1\), it is precisely the consequence

\(v_1(f)v_2(h)\geq v_2(f)v_1(h)\)

of \(\rho(f)\geq\rho(h)\). The case \(r=s\) is again equality.

3. A threshold batch preserves EF1

Choose a rational parameter \(t\geq 0\), and put \(t_r=b_rt\). Write \(U_i=v_{r_i}\cdot z_i\).

For a class of an active type \(r_i\), define

\(k_i(t)=\max\left\{0,\left\lceil\dfrac{t_{r_i}-U_i}{d_{r_i}}\right\rceil\right\}\).

For an inactive type, set \(k_i(t)=0\).

First give every member of class \(i\) exactly \(k_i(t)\) additional copies of \(g_{r_i}\). Optionally, some mass of that class may receive one further copy, but only when

\(U_i+k_i(t)d_{r_i}=t_{r_i}\). \((3)\)

A partially selected class is split into two classes.

We prove that any batch of this form preserves EF1, provided its supply requirements are met.

Every resulting bundle of type \(r\) has own utility at least \(t_r\). For an active type this follows from the ceiling definition; for an inactive type, \(t_r=0\) and utilities are nonnegative.

Consider a target bundle that received additional goods. Removing one newly added copy of \(g_r\) leaves own utility at most \(t_r\):

  • If the target received only its baseline \(k_i(t)>0\) copies, then \(U_i+(k_i(t)-1)d_r<t_r\).
  • If it received the optional extra copy, equality \((3)\) gives utility exactly \(t_r\) after removing that copy.

By \((1)\), which remains valid after adding copies of \(g_r\), and then \((2)\), its value to any observing type \(s\), after that removal, is at most

\(\dfrac{v_s(g_r)}{d_r}\,t_r\leq t_s\).

Every resulting source bundle of type \(s\) has utility at least \(t_s\). Thus all EF1 inequalities with an updated target hold.

For an unchanged nonempty target, use its previous EF1 witness. Every resulting source bundle extends its original bundle coordinatewise, so nonnegative valuations preserve that inequality. An empty target imposes no constraint.

This proves EF1 across all resulting supported pairs, including pairs within one type. The ordering invariant also persists, because only the current first category goes to type 1 and only the current last category goes to type 2.

4. Finding an exact batch that exhausts a category

We now choose \(t\) so that the baseline batch fits the remaining supplies, and optional copies can exhaust a category.

For active classes define

\(k_i^+(t)=\max\left\{0,\left\lfloor\dfrac{t_{r_i}-U_i}{d_{r_i}}\right\rfloor+1\right\}\),

and set \(k_i^+(t)=0\) for inactive classes.

For each distinct category \(g\) among \(f,h\), define

\(F_g(t)=\sum_{i:g_{r_i}=g}w_i k_i(t)\), \(H_g(t)=\sum_{i:g_{r_i}=g}w_i k_i^+(t)\).

If \(f=h\), these sums include both types and use the single remaining supply \(Q_f\).

For each class, \(k_i^+(t)-k_i(t)\) is either zero or one. It equals one exactly for active classes satisfying \((3)\). Therefore \(H_g(t)-F_g(t)\) is precisely the mass eligible for an optional copy of \(g\).

An exact rational grid

Let \(B\) be the product of the denominators of all input valuations, in reduced form. For each active type write \(b_r=p_r/s_r\) in reduced form, and set

\(D=B\prod_{r:b_r>0}p_r\).

Every breakpoint of the functions above has the form

\(t=\dfrac{U_i+j d_{r_i}}{b_{r_i}}\), where \(j\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}\).

Since bundles are integral, both \(BU_i\) and \(Bd_{r_i}\) are integers. Thus every breakpoint is an integer multiple of \(1/D\).

Find the smallest nonnegative integer \(j_*\) such that, for some current endpoint category \(g\),

\(H_g(j_*/D)>Q_g\). \((4)\)

This is a monotone integer search.

To verify that the search has a bounded endpoint, choose any active type \(r\). Partial feasibility and normalization imply

\(\sum_{i:r_i=r}w_iU_i\leq 1\), \(d_rQ_{g_r}\leq 1\).

Using \(\max\{0,\lfloor y\rfloor+1\}\geq y\), we obtain

\(H_{g_r}(t)\geq \dfrac{a_rb_rt-\sum_{i:r_i=r}w_iU_i}{d_r}\geq\dfrac{a_rb_rt-1}{d_r}\).

At \(t=3/(a_rb_r)\), this exceeds \(Q_{g_r}\). Hence binary search may use the upper endpoint

\(J=\left\lceil\dfrac{3D}{a_rb_r}\right\rceil\).

Set \(t=j_*/D\). Then

\(F_g(t)\leq Q_g\)

for every current endpoint category. Indeed, if \(j_*=0\), all baseline counts are zero. Otherwise, because all breakpoints lie on the grid,

\(F_g(j_*/D)=H_g((j_*-1)/D)\),

and minimality of \(j_*\) gives the required inequality.

Choose a category \(g_*\) witnessing \((4)\), and put

\(\delta=Q_{g_*}-F_{g_*}(t)\).

Then

\(0\leq\delta<H_{g_*}(t)-F_{g_*}(t)\).

Allocate the baseline copies to all classes. Among classes eligible for an optional copy of \(g_*\), select exactly mass \(\delta\): take whole classes successively, splitting at most one class to obtain the exact remaining amount.

This exhausts \(g_*\), uses no excessive supply, and has the form proved EF1 in Section 3.

Each phase exhausts at least one category. There are therefore at most \(m\) phases. Baseline updates do not increase the number of classes, and optional allocation splits at most one class per phase. Starting with two classes, the final support has at most \(m+2\) entries.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

It remains to show that the algorithm is polynomial in the binary input length, rather than in numerical quantities.

Let \(D_0\) be the product of the denominators of \(\alpha\) and all \(q_g\). Initially, every class mass and every remaining supply is an integer multiple of \(1/D_0\). This property persists:

  • Baseline allocation subtracts integer multiples of class masses from supplies.
  • The split amount is \(\delta=Q_g-F_g(t)\).
  • Splitting replaces a mass \(w_i\) by \(\delta\) and \(w_i-\delta\).

Thus every positive class mass is at least \(1/D_0\). Partial feasibility consequently bounds every supported bundle coordinate by

\(w_i z_{i,g}\leq q_g\), hence \(z_{i,g}\leq D_0q_g\).

The numbers \(D_0\) and \(q_g\) have \(O(L)\) bits, so every bundle coordinate has \(O(L)\) bits. All class masses also have \(O(L)\)-bit representations.

The products \(B\) and \(D\) have \(O(L)\) bits. Dot products \(U_i\), the upper endpoint \(J\), and the rational quantities evaluated during the search have polynomially many bits. In particular, \(\log(J+1)=O(L)\), so each phase uses \(O(L)\) binary-search iterations. Each iteration evaluates sums over at most \(m+2\) classes using exact rational arithmetic.

Ratio sorting uses exact rational comparisons. There are at most \(m\) phases, and bundle updates, class splitting, and supply updates involve polynomially many operations on numbers of polynomial bit length. The total running time is therefore polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\).

At termination all supplies are allocated, the masses are rational, and EF1 and ordering hold. Section 1 then gives \(W\geq 1\geq\mathrm{OPT}/2\).

The calculation
\[ A-B=A(1-B)-B(1-A) \]

The upper bound \(W\leq 2\) is also correct for countably supported allocations. All summands are nonnegative, so interchanging sums is justified, and the supply equations give the stated value \(2\). No attainment of the supremum is needed.

The two initial empty-bundle classes have the required positive masses, meet the partial supply equations, and satisfy EF1 vacuously. Ignoring zero-supply categories is legitimate: their output bundle coordinates can remain zero.

The endpoint allocation rule preserves the ordering invariant, including when the endpoints coincide or their ratios tie.

The assertion that every active type has \(d_r>0\) is correct. In particular, if type 2 is active, then \(v_2(f)>0\), so \(\rho(f)\) is finite. A category \(h\) with \(v_2(h)=0\) would have infinite ratio, contradicting \(\rho(h)\leq\rho(f)\).

These statements remain valid when the baseline count is zero and the optional copy is the only addition.

Inequality (1) continues to apply after adding copies of the endpoint category: those copies satisfy its categorywise comparison with equality. Combining it with (2) correctly bounds the reduced target’s value to every observing type by that observer’s threshold.

For an unchanged nonempty target, its previous EF1 witness remains valid because every resulting source extends its original bundle coordinatewise. This covers sources created by splitting. Empty targets impose no constraint.

\[ H_{g_r}(t)\geq\frac{a_rb_rt-1}{d_r}. \]
At \(t=3/(a_rb_r)\), this lower bound is \(2/d_r\), whereas \(Q_{g_r}\leq 1/d_r\). The strict inequality required by (4) therefore holds.

5. Binary representation and running time

The common-denominator argument is sound in substance. Initial masses and supplies lie on the \(1/D_0\) lattice, and integer bundle updates, residual-supply calculations, and splits preserve that lattice.

Every positive class mass is therefore at least \(1/D_0\). Partial feasibility gives the claimed bound
\[ z_{i,g}\leq D_0q_g. \]
The products defining \(D_0\), \(B\), and \(D\) have bit length bounded by a constant multiple of the input length. Thus supported bundle coordinates and masses have polynomial-size binary representations.

The search endpoint also has polynomial bit length, including when a type mass or an active valuation is very small. Hence binary search uses polynomially many iterations. The class count, phase count, exact arithmetic, sorting, and output size are all polynomially bounded.

One sentence needs a minor correction:

“Splitting replaces a mass \(w_i\) by \(\delta\) and \(w_i-\delta\).”

Here \(\delta\) was defined as the total optional mass selected across classes. If whole classes have already been selected, the split uses the remaining selection amount, not that original total. Literally using the original \(\delta\) can exceed the mass of the class being split.

Section 4 already specifies splitting to obtain “the exact remaining amount,” so this does not invalidate the algorithm or lattice argument. The minimum clarification is to distinguish the total selected mass from the residual amount used in the single split.
