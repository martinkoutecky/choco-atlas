The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite role set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), a symmetric regular interaction kernel \(W\), a seed-mass budget \(\kappa\), and a rational threshold \(b\), decide whether there are masses \(z_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_t z_t=\kappa\) and \(-\operatorname{Cut}_W(z)/(2\delta)\ge b\), where \(\operatorname{Cut}_W(z)=\sum_{t,u\in T}W_{tu}z_t(\mu_u-z_u)\) and \(\sum_u\mu_uW_{tu}=\delta\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support \(T\): the list of its \(\tau=|T|\ge2\) roles with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types.
The interaction kernel is an explicitly encoded symmetric matrix \(W\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{T\times T}\), with \(W_{tt}=0\), connected positive-weight support, and \(\sum_u\mu_uW_{tu}=\delta>0\) for every \(t\). Let \(m\) count unordered positive-weight role pairs and \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Given a binary-rational budget \(\kappa\in[0,1]\) and threshold \(b\in\mathbb{Q}\), feasible seed masses are real numbers \(z_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_tz_t=\kappa\). Define \(\operatorname{Cut}_W(z)=\sum_{t,u\in T}W_{tu}z_t(\mu_u-z_u)\).
The decision problem asks whether a feasible \(z\) satisfies \(-\operatorname{Cut}_W(z)/(2\delta)\ge b\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete fixation maximization with favorable bias is NP-hard even on connected regular undirected graphs; its weak-selection objective differs from the negative seed cut by a term constant at fixed seed cardinality. [Durocher et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08207)
Continuous quadratic formulations of graph partitioning are known, but the verified formulation adds a diagonal term to the cut objective; it does not settle the exact kernel problem above. [Hager, Phan, and Zhang](https://www.math.lsu.edu/~hozhang/papers/graph.pdf) No result settling this exact mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational kernel entries and explicit encoding, following the nonnegative interaction weights and making bit complexity well-defined.
  • Imposed zero diagonal and connected positive-weight support, carrying over the anchor's loopless connected undirected interaction structure; together with positive degree this requires at least two roles.
  • Specified a strictly positive common weighted degree to exclude an undefined denominator.
  • Specified a binary-rational budget in the normalized interval [0,1], retaining the exact-budget equality and allowing the endpoint budgets.
  • Specified real-valued feasible seed masses and an unrestricted rational threshold, since the objective is a negative cut rather than a fixation probability.
  • Defined m as the number of unordered positive-weight role pairs and L as the total binary input length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when all masses are equal dyadic rationals, every off-diagonal kernel entry is a positive integer, and the positive-weight support is complete.

The reduction uses two roles for each auxiliary graph vertex. Equalizing the seed masses within each pair creates an effective diagonal quadratic term. A rounding argument then proves that fractional seeds cannot improve upon selecting whole pairs.

1. Source problem and padding

We reduce from minimum bisection on simple cubic graphs: given a graph \(G=(V,E)\) with \(n=|V|\) and an integer \(q\), decide whether some \(S\subseteq V\), with \(|S|=n/2\), has at most \(q\) crossing edges. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard; the cited [Durocher et al. paper, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08207), explicitly invokes this hardness result.

Because \(G\) is cubic, \(n\) is even. We may restrict to \(0\le q\le |E|\): thresholds outside this interval have an immediate answer. Write \(k=n/2\).

Choose the smallest power of two \(N\) satisfying \(N\ge 2n\). Thus \(2n\le N<4n\). Add a set \(R\) of \(r=N-n\) auxiliary vertices, so \(r\ge n>k\), and put \(I=V\cup R\).

Let \(H=|E|+1\). Define symmetric weights \(c_{ij}\), for distinct \(i,j\in I\), by

\( c_{ij}=1+\mathbf{1}_{\{\{i,j\}\in E\}}+ H\mathbf{1}_{\{i,j\in R\}}, \qquad c_{ii}=0. \)

Here original edges belong to \(V\); consequently the two indicator terms never overlap.

For \(A\subseteq I\), define its weighted cut by

\( C(A)=\sum_{\substack{i\in A\\j\in I\setminus A}}c_{ij}. \)

If \(|A|=k\), put \(p=|A\cap R|\) and \(S=A\cap V\). The complete-graph contribution is \(k(N-k)\), the original-edge contribution is the number \(\operatorname{cut}_G(S)\) of crossing edges in \(G\), and the auxiliary-clique contribution is \(Hp(r-p)\). Hence

\( C(A)=k(N-k)+\operatorname{cut}_G(S)+Hp(r-p). \)

Define \(B=k(N-k)+q\). If \(p\ge1\), then \(p\le k<r\), so \(p(r-p)\ge1\). Since \(\operatorname{cut}_G(S)\ge0\) and \(H>q\), this implies \(C(A)>B\).

Therefore

\( \exists A\subseteq I:\ |A|=k,\ C(A)\le B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists S\subseteq V:\ |S|=n/2,\ \operatorname{cut}_G(S)\le q. \)

The padding makes the eventual masses dyadic without changing the answer.

2. Constructing the society

Define

\( d_i=\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij},\qquad d_*=\max_{i\in I}d_i,\qquad D=4d_*,\qquad M_i=D-2d_i. \)

Since \(c_{ij}\ge1\) for distinct vertices, \(d_*>0\). Moreover, \(M_i\ge2d_*>0\).

Create two roles \((i,0),(i,1)\) for each \(i\in I\). Set

\( T=I\times\{0,1\},\qquad \mu_{(i,a)}=\frac1{2N},\qquad \kappa=\frac{k}{N}. \)

Both the masses and the budget are dyadic rationals because \(N\) is a power of two.

Define the kernel by

\( W_{(i,0),(i,1)}=W_{(i,1),(i,0)}=M_i, \)

and, whenever \(i\ne j\), by

\( W_{(i,a),(j,b)}=c_{ij} \quad\text{for all }a,b\in\{0,1\}. \)

Set every diagonal entry to zero.

This kernel is symmetric, and every off-diagonal entry is positive. Its support is therefore connected. Each role has weighted row sum

\( \sum_{u\in T}\mu_uW_{(i,a),u} =\frac{M_i+2d_i}{2N} =\frac{D}{2N}. \)

Thus the required common value is \(\delta=D/(2N)>0\). All society hypotheses hold.

For every feasible \(z\), regularity gives the identity

\( \operatorname{Cut}_W(z) =\sum_t z_t\sum_u\mu_uW_{tu}-z^{\mathsf T}Wz =\delta\kappa-z^{\mathsf T}Wz. \)

Consequently, minimizing the cut is equivalent to maximizing \(z^{\mathsf T}Wz\).

3. Fractional seeds can be rounded to whole pairs

We prove that every feasible \(z\) can be replaced by a feasible vector selecting exactly \(k\) whole pairs, with no larger cut.

Equalization within pairs

Let \(s_i=z_{(i,0)}+z_{(i,1)}\). The quadratic objective is

\( z^{\mathsf T}Wz =2\sum_i M_i z_{(i,0)}z_{(i,1)} +2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}s_is_j. \)

For fixed \(s_i\), replacing both coordinates of pair \(i\) by \(s_i/2\) is feasible: \(0\le s_i\le1/N\). This replacement preserves the total seed mass and all terms involving other pairs. It does not decrease the internal term, because

\( z_{(i,0)}z_{(i,1)} \le \frac{s_i^2}{4}, \)

which follows from \((z_{(i,0)}-z_{(i,1)})^2\ge0\).

After equalizing all pairs, define \(x_i=Ns_i\). Then

\( 0\le x_i\le1,\qquad \sum_i x_i=k, \)

and

\( z^{\mathsf T}Wz=\frac{Q(x)}{N^2}, \qquad Q(x)=\frac12\sum_iM_ix_i^2+ 2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}x_ix_j. \)

Rounding the pair totals

Suppose \(x\) has a fractional coordinate. Since \(\sum_i x_i=k\) is an integer, it must have at least two fractional coordinates, say \(x_i,x_j\).

Vary these coordinates as \(x_i+t,x_j-t\), preserving their sum. The feasible interval is

\( \max\{-x_i,x_j-1\}\le t\le \min\{1-x_i,x_j\}. \)

Because both coordinates are fractional, zero lies strictly between the endpoints. At either endpoint, at least one coordinate becomes zero or one.

Along this interval, \(Q\) is a quadratic polynomial in \(t\). Its quadratic coefficient is

\( \frac{M_i+M_j}{2}-2c_{ij}. \)

Since \(M_i,M_j\ge2d_*\) and \(c_{ij}\le d_*\), this coefficient is nonnegative. Thus the polynomial is convex. Its value at zero cannot exceed the larger of its endpoint values, by the defining convexity inequality.

Choose an endpoint with value at least \(Q(x)\). This reduces the number of fractional coordinates and does not decrease the objective. Repeating finitely many times yields \(y\in\{0,1\}^I\) satisfying

\( \sum_i y_i=k,\qquad Q(y)\ge Q(x). \)

The corresponding seed vector is

\( z'_{(i,0)}=z'_{(i,1)}=\frac{y_i}{2N}. \)

Combining equalization, rounding, and the identity from Section 2 proves

\( \operatorname{Cut}_W(z')\le\operatorname{Cut}_W(z). \)

This establishes the required equivalence for all feasible real seed vectors.

4. Threshold equivalence

For a whole-pair seed vector, let \(A=\{i:y_i=1\}\). Internal pair edges contribute nothing to the cut. For each auxiliary graph pair crossing between \(A\) and its complement, the four role pairs contribute a total of \(c_{ij}/N^2\). Hence

\( \operatorname{Cut}_W(z')=\frac{C(A)}{N^2}. \)

Because \(2\delta=D/N\), it follows that

\( -\frac{\operatorname{Cut}_W(z')}{2\delta} =-\frac{C(A)}{DN}. \)

Set the input threshold to

\( b=-\frac{B}{DN} =-\frac{k(N-k)+q}{DN}. \)

If the original graph has a bisection of cut at most \(q\), selecting its corresponding whole pairs achieves the threshold.

Conversely, if any feasible real seed vector achieves the threshold, Section 3 replaces it by a whole-pair vector whose score is at least as large. Its selected set \(A\) satisfies \(C(A)\le B\). Section 1 then forces \(A\cap R=\varnothing\) and gives a bisection of \(G\) with cut at most \(q\).

Thus the original and constructed decision instances have exactly the same answer.

5. Polynomial encoding and conclusion

There are \(\tau=2N=O(n)\) supported roles and \(m=\binom{2N}{2}=O(n^2)\) positive unordered role pairs.

Since \(G\) is cubic, \(H=O(n)\), \(d_*=O(n^2)\), and \(D,M_i=O(n^2)\). Every kernel entry, mass, budget, and threshold has \(O(\log n)\) bits. The total output length is \(L=O(n^2\log n)\), and the construction takes polynomial time.

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. In particular, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
