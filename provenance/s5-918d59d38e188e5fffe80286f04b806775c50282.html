The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite valuation types \(T\), masses \(\mu_t\), a compact polygonal estate \(C\), a scaled per-capita capacity field \(B\), and absolutely continuous valuations \(V_t\), define \(\mathcal{Q}_t(r)=\{q\subseteq C:q\text{ is a parallel square and }V_t(q)\ge rV_t^{\mathrm{Squares}}(C)\}\). Decide whether there exist measures \(\lambda_t\) over whole-square placements with \(\lambda_t(\mathcal{Q}_t(r))=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\int \mathbf{1}_q(x)\,d\lambda_t(q)\le B(x)\) almost everywhere; equivalently, maximize \(r\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supplied types, not possible types.
The estate \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) is a compact polygonal domain with rational vertices and nonempty interior. A supplied rational polygonal partition of \(C\) specifies bounded, nonnegative, rational piecewise-constant densities \(v_t\) and capacity \(B\). Let \(m\) count all vertices in this geometric description and \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Let \(V_t(A)=\int_A v_t(x)\,dx\). Allowed placements are whole squares \(q=[a,a+s]\times[b,b+s]\subseteq C\), with real \(a,b\) and \(s>0\), in the fixed input coordinate system. Define \(M_t=\sup_q V_t(q)\) and require \(M_t>0\) for every type.
Given a binary-rational threshold \(r\in[0,1]\), let \(\mathcal Q_t(r)=\{q:V_t(q)\geq rM_t\}\). Feasibility means that each type has a finite nonnegative Borel measure \(\lambda_t\) on the square-placement parameter space, supported on \(\mathcal Q_t(r)\), with total mass \(\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t\int\mathbf1_q(x)\,d\lambda_t(q)\leq B(x)\) for Lebesgue-almost-every \(x\in C\). Capacity is measured per unit total population; unused capacity is allowed.

Is this exact feasibility problem in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For \(n\) finite agents, a constructive guarantee gives each agent a disjoint parallel square worth at least \(M_i/(8n-6)\); this is a guaranteed fairness level, not an exact threshold-decision algorithm ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/1510.03170)).

Exact feasibility is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard

The problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. This holds even when $r=1/2$, the estate is a simple orthogonal polygon with a constant number of vertices, the supplied partition has a constant number of rectangular cells, and all masses are dyadic rationals.

The reduction respects arbitrary fractional splitting. Its key step is that a fractional exact tiling of an interval by finitely many prescribed lengths must contain an ordinary tiling by those lengths.

1. Source problem and integer lengths

We reduce from PARTITION: given positive integers $w_1,\ldots,w_n$ in binary, determine whether there is a subset $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ satisfying

$$ 2\sum_{i\in I}w_i=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i. $$

This problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete. See [Karp, *Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Let $N$ be the smallest power of two satisfying $N\geq\max(n,2)$. Define

$$ a_i= \begin{cases} 2w_i,&1\leq i\leq n,\\ 0,&n<i\leq N, \end{cases} \qquad A=\sum_{i=1}^N a_i. $$

Thus $A$ is a positive even integer, and the original instance is affirmative exactly when some $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ satisfies

$$ \sum_{i\in I}a_i=A/2. $$

Define the following positive integers:

$$ \rho=N+1,\qquad D=NA+1,\qquad U=\sum_{i=1}^N\rho^{i-1}, $$

$$ K=(N+1)(DU+A),\qquad H=K+D\rho^{N-1}+A, $$

$$ W=NK+DU+A/2. $$

For each index, put

$$ d_i=K+D\rho^{i-1}. $$

For $i\leq n$, the two available lengths are $d_i$ and $d_i+a_i$. For $i>n$, only $d_i$ is available.

Every available length $\ell$ satisfies

$$ K<\ell\leq H. $$

Furthermore,

$$ H-K=D\rho^{N-1}+A \leq DU+A =\frac{K}{N+1}. $$

Consequently,

$$ H<2K,\qquad W>NK\geq2K>H. $$

In particular, every available length exceeds $1$ and fits within both dimensions of the rectangle $[0,W]\times[0,H]$.

Lemma 1: the lengths encode PARTITION

A finite sequence of available lengths, allowing repetitions, has sum $W$ if and only if the original PARTITION instance is affirmative.

Moreover, every such sequence contains exactly $N$ lengths and exactly one length belonging to each index.

Proof. Suppose a sequence contains $k$ lengths and has sum $W$.

Since $DU+A/2<K$, we have $W<(N+1)K$. Thus $k\geq N+1$ is impossible, because every length exceeds $K$.

Also,

$$ (N-1)H \leq (N-1)K+\frac{N-1}{N+1}K =\left(N-\frac{2}{N+1}\right)K <NK<W. $$

Hence $k\leq N-1$ is impossible. Therefore $k=N$.

Let $c_i$ count selected lengths belonging to index $i$. Let $z$ be the sum of the $a_i$ offsets contributed by selected lengths of the form $d_i+a_i$. Subtracting $NK$ from the length sum gives

$$ D\sum_{i=1}^N c_i\rho^{i-1}+z =DU+A/2. $$

There are $N$ selected lengths, so

$$ 0\leq z\leq NA<D, \qquad 0\leq A/2<D. $$

Reduction modulo $D$ therefore gives $z=A/2$. It follows that

$$ \sum_{i=1}^N c_i\rho^{i-1} =\sum_{i=1}^N\rho^{i-1}. $$

Each $c_i$ lies between $0$ and $N=\rho-1$. Uniqueness of base-$\rho$ representation implies $c_i=1$ for every $i$. Thus the indices at which the longer length was selected form a subset $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with

$$ \sum_{i\in I}a_i=A/2. $$

Conversely, given such an $I$, select $d_i+a_i$ for $i\in I$, select $d_i$ for the remaining original indices, and select $d_i$ for every padded index. These $N$ lengths sum to $W$.

The complementary selection on the original indices also sums to $W$, because

$$ \sum_{\substack{1\leq i\leq n\\i\notin I}}a_i=A/2. $$

This proves the lemma. $\square$

2. A fractional interval tiling contains an ordinary tiling

The following lemma accounts for fractional splitting.

Lemma 2: endpoint conservation

Let $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_p$ be finitely many positive lengths not exceeding $W$. For each $j$, let $\nu_j$ be a finite nonnegative Borel measure on $[0,W-\ell_j]$. Suppose that, for some $c>0$,

$$ f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^p \int \mathbf 1_{[a,a+\ell_j]}(x)\,d\nu_j(a) =c $$

for Lebesgue-almost-every $x\in[0,W]$.

Then there is a finite sequence of these lengths, allowing repetitions, whose sum is $W$.

Proof. Define the measures of left and right endpoints by

$$ \alpha=\sum_{j=1}^p\nu_j, \qquad \beta=\sum_{j=1}^p(a\mapsto a+\ell_j)_*\nu_j. $$

For every continuously differentiable function $\phi$ on $[0,W]$, Fubini’s theorem gives

$$ \begin{aligned} \int_0^W \phi'(x)f(x)\,dx &=\sum_{j=1}^p \int\left(\phi(a+\ell_j)-\phi(a)\right)\,d\nu_j(a)\\ &=\int\phi\,d(\beta-\alpha). \end{aligned} $$

Because $f=c$ almost everywhere, the left side equals $c(\phi(W)-\phi(0))$. Continuously differentiable functions are uniformly dense in the continuous functions on this compact interval. Since all the measures are finite, the identity extends to every continuous test function, and therefore

$$ \alpha-\beta=c\delta_0-c\delta_W. $$

No interval ends at $0$, so $\beta(\{0\})=0$. Hence

$$ \alpha(\{0\})=c>0. $$

Since there are finitely many measures $\nu_j$, some $j$ satisfies $\nu_j(\{0\})>0$. Select that interval. Its right endpoint is $\ell_j$, and

$$ \beta(\{\ell_j\})\geq\nu_j(\{0\})>0. $$

If this endpoint is less than $W$, the measure identity implies

$$ \alpha(\{\ell_j\})=\beta(\{\ell_j\})>0. $$

Thus some interval with positive atomic mass starts there, and we can select it next.

Continue in this way. Each selected interval starts where the previous one ends. Every endpoint is at most $W$, and every step advances by at least $\min_j\ell_j>0$. The procedure must terminate after finitely many steps. At an interior endpoint, conservation always supplies another interval, so termination can occur only at $W$.

The selected lengths therefore sum to $W$. $\square$

This proof uses the almost-everywhere coverage identity through integration; it imposes no capacity condition at individual endpoints.

3. Constructing the society and estate

For each $i\leq n$, create two types, with associated lengths and masses

$$ \ell_{i,-}=d_i,\qquad \ell_{i,+}=d_i+a_i, \qquad \mu_{i,-}=\mu_{i,+}=\frac{1}{2N}. $$

For each padded index $n<i\leq N$, create one type with

$$ \ell_{i,0}=d_i,\qquad \mu_{i,0}=\frac{1}{N}. $$

There are $\tau=N+n$ types. All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and

$$ \sum_t\mu_t =\frac{n}{N}+\frac{N-n}{N} =1. $$

The mass-weighted lengths satisfy

$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_t\mu_t\ell_t &=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^n(2d_i+a_i) +\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=n+1}^N d_i\\ &=\frac{NK+DU+A/2}{N} =\frac{W}{N}. \end{aligned} $$

The associated lengths are distinct. Within an original index, $a_i>0$ distinguishes its two lengths. Between consecutive indices,

$$ d_{i+1}-d_i=DN\rho^{i-1}>A, $$

so the longer option at one index is smaller than the shorter option at the next.

Define three rectangles:

$$ E=[0,W]\times[0,H], $$

$$ P=[-3,-2]\times[0,1], \qquad J=[-2,0]\times[0,1/4]. $$

Let

$$ C=E\cup J\cup P. $$

This is a compact simple orthogonal polygon with connected interior. Its boundary vertices, in cyclic order, are

$$ (-3,0),\ (W,0),\ (W,H),\ (0,H),\ (0,1/4),\ (-2,1/4),\ (-2,1),\ (-3,1). $$

Let the bottom strip of the main rectangle be

$$ S=[0,W]\times[0,1]. $$

Supply the rectangular partition consisting of $S$, the rectangle above it in $E$, the calibration rectangle $P$, and the connector $J$. Boundary subdivisions, if required by the partition representation, introduce only constantly many vertices.

For each type $t$, define

$$ v_t(x)= \begin{cases} 1,&x\in S,\\ 2\ell_t,&x\in P,\\ 0,&\text{elsewhere}. \end{cases} $$

Define capacity by

$$ B(x)= \begin{cases} 1/N,&x\in E,\\ 0,&x\in C\setminus E. \end{cases} $$

Values on partition boundaries can be assigned arbitrarily, since they affect neither valuations nor almost-everywhere capacity constraints. Set $r=1/2$.

Exact normalization

For every type,

$$ M_t=2\ell_t. $$

Indeed, the unit square $P$ itself has value $2\ell_t$.

For the converse bound, a square contained in $C$ cannot intersect both $P$ and $S$ in positive area. Such a square would have horizontal extent greater than $2$ and would cross the connector. At any horizontal coordinate strictly between $-2$ and $0$, the estate’s vertical section has height $1/4$. Containment would therefore require the square’s side to be at most $1/4$, a contradiction.

Every square contained in $C$ has side at most $H$, because $C$ lies between vertical coordinates $0$ and $H$. Its contribution from $S$ is consequently at most $H$. Its contribution from $P$ is at most $2\ell_t$, since $P$ has area $1$. These contributions cannot both be positive, and

$$ H<2K<2\ell_t. $$

Thus every square has value at most $2\ell_t$, proving the normalization formula. In particular, all promised normalization constants are positive, and the quality requirement is exactly

$$ V_t(q)\geq\ell_t. $$

4. Feasibility forces a fractional interval tiling

Suppose the constructed instance is feasible, with measures $\lambda_t$. Write its load as

$$ F(x)=\sum_t\int\mathbf1_q(x)\,d\lambda_t(q). $$

4.1. All allocated squares lie in the main rectangle

Capacity is zero outside $E$. Tonelli’s theorem and feasibility give

$$ 0=\int_{C\setminus E}F(x)\,dx =\sum_t\int \operatorname{area}(q\setminus E)\,d\lambda_t(q). $$

Hence $\operatorname{area}(q\setminus E)=0$ for $\lambda_t$-almost every square of every type.

A positive-side square not contained in the closed rectangle $E$ has positive area outside $E$: at least one of its coordinate intervals extends strictly beyond an endpoint of the corresponding interval of $E$. Therefore $q\subseteq E$ almost surely.

Discarding these placement-measure null sets, write each square as

$$ q=[a,a+s]\times[b,b+s]\subseteq E. $$

Its valuation is now precisely $\operatorname{area}(q\cap S)$.

4.2. Quality and strip capacity are both tight

Every allocated square satisfies

$$ \operatorname{area}(q\cap S)\geq\ell_t. $$

Consequently,

$$ \begin{aligned} \int_S F(x)\,dx &=\sum_t\int\operatorname{area}(q\cap S)\,d\lambda_t(q)\\ &\geq\sum_t\mu_t\ell_t =\frac{W}{N}. \end{aligned} $$

Capacity gives the reverse inequality:

$$ \int_S F(x)\,dx \leq\int_S B(x)\,dx =\frac{W}{N}. $$

Thus equality holds throughout. Nonnegativity of all slack terms implies

$$ F(x)=\frac1N \quad\text{for almost every }x\in S, $$

and, for every type,

$$ \operatorname{area}(q\cap S)=\ell_t \quad\text{for }\lambda_t\text{-almost every }q. $$

4.3. Saturation forces bottom alignment and prescribed sides

The strip has height $1$, so $\operatorname{area}(q\cap S)\leq s$. Since $\ell_t>1$, every allocated square has $s>1$.

Its intersection with $S$ has positive area, so $0\leq b<1$. Hence $b+s>1$, and

$$ \operatorname{area}(q\cap S)=s(1-b). $$

For $0<y<1$, define the horizontally integrated load

$$ G(y)=\int_0^W F(x,y)\,dx. $$

Tonelli’s theorem gives

$$ G(y)=\sum_t\int s\,\mathbf1_{\{b\leq y\}}\,d\lambda_t. $$

Strip saturation and Fubini’s theorem imply $G(y)=W/N$ for almost every $y\in(0,1)$. Choose a sequence of such coordinates decreasing to $0$. Since $s\leq H$ and the measures have finite total mass, dominated convergence yields

$$ \sum_t\int s\,\mathbf1_{\{b=0\}}\,d\lambda_t =\frac{W}{N}. $$

On the other hand,

$$ \sum_t\int s(1-b)\,d\lambda_t =\int_S F =\frac{W}{N}. $$

Subtracting these identities gives

$$ \sum_t\int s(1-b)\mathbf1_{\{b>0\}}\,d\lambda_t=0. $$

The integrand is strictly positive whenever $0<b<1$. Thus $b=0$ almost surely. The tight quality identity then gives $s=\ell_t$ almost surely.

Every allocated square therefore has the form

$$ [a,a+\ell_t]\times[0,\ell_t]. $$

4.4. Extracting a PARTITION solution

Push each $\lambda_t$ forward to its horizontal start coordinate, obtaining a measure $\nu_t$ on $[0,W-\ell_t]$.

All lengths exceed $1$, so throughout the interior of $S$ the load depends only on the horizontal coordinate. Strip saturation gives

$$ \sum_t\int \mathbf1_{[a,a+\ell_t]}(x)\,d\nu_t(a) =\frac1N $$

for almost every $x\in[0,W]$.

Lemma 2 supplies a finite sequence of available lengths summing to $W$. Lemma 1 then supplies a solution to the original PARTITION instance.

Thus feasibility implies an affirmative PARTITION instance, even when the allocation measures split every type arbitrarily.

5. A PARTITION solution gives a feasible allocation

Conversely, suppose $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ satisfies

$$ \sum_{i\in I}a_i=A/2. $$

Form two sequences, each ordered by index:

  • The first uses $d_i+a_i$ for $i\in I$ and $d_i$ for the other original indices.
  • The second makes the complementary choices on the original indices.

Both sequences also use $d_i$ at every padded index. Each sequence contains $N$ lengths and sums to $W$, by Lemma 1.

For either sequence $\ell^{(1)},\ldots,\ell^{(N)}$, place the squares

$$ q_j= \left[ \sum_{h<j}\ell^{(h)}, \sum_{h\leq j}\ell^{(h)} \right] \times[0,\ell^{(j)}]. $$

These squares lie in $E$: their horizontal intervals partition $[0,W]$, and their sides are at most $H$. Within each sequence their interiors are disjoint.

Put population mass $1/(2N)$ on every square in each sequence, assigned to its corresponding type.

Each original type appears exactly once across the two sequences, receiving its required mass $1/(2N)$. Each padded type appears once in each sequence, receiving total mass $1/N$.

Each square of type $t$ has bottom coordinate zero and side $\ell_t$, so

$$ V_t(q)=\ell_t=rM_t. $$

Away from the finitely many square boundaries, each sequence contributes load at most $1/(2N)$. Their combined load is therefore at most $1/N$ in $E$ and zero outside $E$. This is exactly the capacity constraint.

The constructed instance is feasible.

6. Polynomial size and conclusion

We have proved the equivalence

$$ \text{the PARTITION instance is affirmative} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed exact feasibility instance is feasible}. $$

It remains to check the reduction’s encoding size.

The padded count satisfies $N=O(n+1)$. Since

$$ U=\sum_{i=1}^N(N+1)^{i-1}, $$

all constructed integers have binary length

$$ O\!\left(N\log(N+1)+\log(A+1)\right), $$

which is polynomial in the original input length. They can be computed by polynomially many integer arithmetic operations on numbers of these lengths.

There are $\tau=N+n=O(n+1)$ supplied types. Their densities are nonnegative integers, their masses are positive dyadic rationals summing to $1$, and every $M_t$ is positive. The threshold is the fixed dyadic value $1/2$. The estate and common partition use only constantly many rational vertices, so $m$ is bounded by an absolute constant.

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction. Therefore the exact distribution-of-types, whole-square capacity feasibility problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard. A running time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$ would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.
