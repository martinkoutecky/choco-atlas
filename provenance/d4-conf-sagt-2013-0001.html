The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite community template \(G=(V,E)\), thresholds \(c_v\), rational acceptance probabilities \(p_v\), and a fixed schedule \(\pi\), consider a continuum of copies indexed by \(x\in[0,1]\), with edges only between corresponding roles within each copy. What is the exact expected adopting mass \(M=\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{v\in V}\Pr[X_v=1]\) under the paper's cascade process?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a nonempty finite simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\), let \(m=|V|\). Each role \(v\) has a threshold \(c_v\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and an acceptance probability \(p_v\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\); the fixed schedule \(\pi\) is a permutation of \(V\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here types are labeled roles, each of mass \(1/m\), so \(\tau=m\); support size means the number of listed types, not the number of possible types. Total mass is \(1\).
The population consists of copies indexed by \(x\in[0,1]\) with Lebesgue measure; role \(v\) has mass element \(dx/m\). An edge joins \((u,x)\) to \((v,x)\) exactly when \(\{u,v\}\in E\), and there are no edges between copies.
Within each copy, initial preferences \(B_v\in\{-1,1\}\) are independent, with \(\Pr(B_v=1)=p_v\). All copies have this same law and follow \(\pi\).
When role \(v\) is scheduled, put \(D_v=\sum_{u\in N(v):\,\pi^{-1}(u)<\pi^{-1}(v)}X_u\). Its permanent decision is \(X_v=1\) if \(D_v\ge c_v\), \(X_v=-1\) if \(D_v\le-c_v\), and \(X_v=B_v\) otherwise. Influence uses these neighbor counts, without mass weighting.
The quantity to compute exactly, as a binary rational, is the expected adopting mass \(M=\frac1m\sum_{v\in V}\Pr(X_v=1)\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the graph, schedule, thresholds, probabilities, and support masses.

Is exact computation of \(M\) solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

For finite populations on arbitrary graphs, exact expected-adopter computation for a fixed schedule is reported as \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete; on complete graphs it has a polynomial-time algorithm. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.5925)
No result addressing this precise continuum-copy formulation was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted thresholds to positive integers, following the formal cascade definition rather than the introductory allowance of nonnegative thresholds.
  • Specified a nonempty simple undirected template and a schedule that is a permutation of its roles.
  • Used labeled roles as support types, each with binary-rational mass 1/m and total mass 1, preserving the graded question's uniform normalization.
  • Specified independent initial preferences within each copy and the same finite-dimensional law for every copy; cross-copy dependence is immaterial to the requested expectation.
  • Retained unweighted counts of earlier neighbors within each copy as influence, with inclusive threshold comparisons and permanent decisions.
  • Interpreted exact computation as outputting a binary rational and specified polynomial-time Turing reductions for the function-problem hardness question.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Exact computation of \(M\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. Two oracle calls suffice to recover the number of satisfying assignments of a Boolean formula. The reduction uses only dyadic probabilities and dyadic support masses.

For general rational inputs, “exact binary rational output” is understood as a numerator and denominator written in binary.

1. The continuum quantity equals a normalized finite expectation.

Fix one copy. Its decisions are determined recursively by its initial preference vector and the schedule. Because edges never connect different copies, this recursion is exactly the finite process on \(G\). Independence of the initial preferences within the copy gives the specified finite joint distribution. These finite decision rules also agree with those formalized in [Section 2 of the supplied paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.5925).

Let \(A=\sum_{v\in V}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_v=1\}}\) be the number of adopting roles in one copy. By linearity of expectation,

\[ mM=\sum_{v\in V}\Pr(X_v=1)=\mathbb{E}[A]. \]

Thus an oracle for the continuum quantity supplies the normalized expected number of adopters in the finite process. No independence between different copies is needed for this equality.

We now prove hardness directly.

2. Deterministic OR and AND gates.

Interpret decision \(1\) as Boolean true and decision \(-1\) as Boolean false.

Suppose a role has exactly \(r\ge 1\) neighbors scheduled before it. Set its threshold to \(r\).

If its acceptance probability is \(1\), it computes the OR of those neighbors’ decisions. Indeed, if every input is \(-1\), then \(D=-r\), so the role rejects. Otherwise \(D>-r\): the role either adopts because \(D\ge r\), or uses its initial preference, which is deterministically \(1\).

If its acceptance probability is \(0\), it computes their AND. When every input is \(1\), we have \(D=r\), so the role adopts. Otherwise \(D<r\): the role either rejects because \(D\le-r\), or uses its initial preference, which is deterministically \(-1\).

These statements concern earlier neighbors only; edges to roles scheduled later have no effect.

3. Constructing the two oracle instances.

Start with a \(3\)-CNF formula \(\varphi\) on variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\), where \(n\ge1\), with nonempty clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_s\). Satisfiability remains \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete under these restrictions. Repeated occurrences of a literal within a clause may be deleted without changing its satisfying assignments.

Set

\[ \varepsilon=2^{-(2n+2)}. \]

Construct the following roles and undirected edges.

  • For each variable \(z_i\), introduce two source roles \(u_i^+\) and \(u_i^-\), each with threshold \(1\) and acceptance probability \(\varepsilon\). Sources have no edges to other sources.
  • For each \(i\), introduce an OR role \(P_i\), adjacent to \(u_i^+\) and \(u_i^-\), with threshold \(2\) and acceptance probability \(1\).
  • For each clause \(C_j\), introduce an OR role \(Q_j\). Connect it to \(u_i^+\) for each literal \(z_i\) in that clause, and to \(u_i^-\) for each literal \(\neg z_i\). Its threshold is the number of its distinct source neighbors, and its acceptance probability is \(1\).
  • Introduce an output role \(t\), adjacent to all \(P_i\) and \(Q_j\), with acceptance probability \(0\).

Write \(h=n+s\). There are initially \(m_0=2n+h+1\) roles. Pad with isolated roles to make the total number \(m\) the least power of two at least \(m_0\). Each padding role has threshold \(1\) and acceptance probability \(0\).

The schedule places all sources first, then all OR roles and padding roles, and finally \(t\). The graph is simple: all endpoints are distinct roles, and every required edge is inserted once.

List every role as a separate support type with mass \(1/m\). Thus \(\tau=m\), the total mass is \(1\), and every support mass is dyadic.

The two instances differ only in the output threshold:

  • In the first instance, \(c_t=h\).
  • In the second instance, \(c_t=h+1\).

Let their exact expected adopting masses be \(M_{\mathrm{on}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{off}}\).

In the first instance, \(t\) computes the AND of the \(h\) OR roles. In the second, \(|D_t|\le h<c_t\), so \(t\) always uses its deterministic rejecting preference.

Every other role has the same decision in both instances under the same source preferences: \(t\) is scheduled last, and changing its threshold cannot affect an earlier decision. Consequently,

\[ q:=\Pr_{\mathrm{on}}(X_t=1) =m\bigl(M_{\mathrm{on}}-M_{\mathrm{off}}\bigr). \]

This isolates the output probability using two oracle calls.

4. Recovering the number of satisfying assignments.

For each source, let \(Y_i^\pm=\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{u_i^\pm}=1\}}\). Since sources have no earlier neighbors, these \(2n\) variables are independent Bernoulli variables with parameter \(\varepsilon\).

By the gate construction, the output adopts exactly when the following monotone Boolean formula is true:

\[ F(Y)= \left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}(Y_i^+\lor Y_i^-)\right) \land \left(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{s} \bigvee_{\ell\in C_j}Y_\ell\right), \]

where \(Y_{z_i}=Y_i^+\) and \(Y_{\neg z_i}=Y_i^-\).

Let \(a_k\) be the number of vectors in \(\{0,1\}^{2n}\) that satisfy \(F\) and contain exactly \(k\) ones.

Each pair condition requires at least one selected source per variable, so \(a_k=0\) for \(k<n\). A satisfying vector with exactly \(n\) ones therefore selects exactly one source from each pair. Such vectors correspond bijectively to assignments of \(\varphi\), by setting \(z_i\) true precisely when \(Y_i^+=1\). Under this correspondence, every clause condition has exactly its usual meaning. Hence

\[ a_n=\#\{\text{satisfying assignments of }\varphi\}. \]

Independence of the sources gives

\[ q=\sum_{k=n}^{2n} a_k\varepsilon^k(1-\varepsilon)^{2n-k}. \]

Define

\[ \rho=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon} =\frac{1}{2^{2n+2}-1}. \]

Dividing the preceding identity yields

\[ \frac{q}{\varepsilon^n(1-\varepsilon)^n} =a_n+\sum_{k=n+1}^{2n}a_k\rho^{k-n}. \]

The remainder is nonnegative. Since \(0<\rho<1\), and there are only \(2^{2n}\) source vectors in total,

\[ 0\le \sum_{k=n+1}^{2n}a_k\rho^{k-n} \le \rho\sum_{k=n+1}^{2n}a_k \le \frac{2^{2n}}{2^{2n+2}-1} <1. \]

Therefore the exact decoding formula is

\[ \#\{\text{satisfying assignments of }\varphi\} = \left\lfloor \frac{m(M_{\mathrm{on}}-M_{\mathrm{off}})} {\varepsilon^n(1-\varepsilon)^n} \right\rfloor. \]

In particular, \(\varphi\) is satisfiable if and only if this integer is positive.

5. Polynomial size and exact arithmetic.

For a \(3\)-CNF input, the construction has \(m=O(n+s)\) roles and \(O(n+s)\) edges. Padding increases the number of roles by less than a factor of two. Every threshold is at most \(h+1\), and the explicitly listed support contains exactly \(m\) types.

The binary denominator of \(\varepsilon\) has \(2n+3\) bits. Consequently, the graph, schedule, thresholds, probabilities, and support masses all have total length polynomial in the formula’s input length.

Only the \(2n\) sources are random. Put \(d=2^{2n+2}\). Each source vector has probability with denominator \(d^{2n}\), so both oracle outputs have a common denominator

\[ m d^{2n}. \]

Their exact numerator and denominator lengths are therefore \(O(n^2+\log m)\). The scaling factor \(\varepsilon^n(1-\varepsilon)^n\) also has numerator and denominator of polynomial bit length. Subtraction, scaling, and integer division in the decoding formula can thus be performed in polynomial time.

We have constructed a polynomial-time, two-query Turing reduction from Boolean satisfiability to exact computation of \(M\). This proves the requested \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness, even with \(\tau=m\) and entirely dyadic inputs. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

For the OR role, all inputs equal to \(-1\) give \(D=-r\), forcing rejection. Every other input configuration gives \(D>-r\), so rejection cannot be forced; either adoption is forced or the deterministic adopting preference applies.

For the AND role, all inputs equal to \(1\) give \(D=r\), forcing adoption. Every other configuration gives \(D<r\), so adoption cannot be forced; either rejection is forced or the deterministic rejecting preference applies.

The threshold boundaries are handled correctly. Later neighbors have no influence under the task’s definition of \(D_v\).
