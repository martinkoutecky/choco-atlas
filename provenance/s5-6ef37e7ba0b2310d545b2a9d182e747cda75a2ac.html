The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite project set \(P\), a finite support \(T\) of nonempty approval sets, and positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compute a weighted NASH allocation \(x^N\in\arg\max_{x\in\Delta(P)}\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\log u_t(x)\), where \(u_t(x)=\sum_{p\in A_t}x_p\). For this allocation, determine the sharp worst-case value of \(\rho=\min_{t\in T}u_t(x^N)/W^*\), where \(W^*=\max_{x\in\Delta(P)}\min_{t\in T}u_t(x)\), over all such societies.

Result

The sharp worst-case guarantee is \(\boxed{\inf \rho=0}\). This holds already with fixed \(m=\tau=2\) and a unique Nash maximizer. Every individual admissible society nevertheless has \(\rho>0\), so the infimum is not attained.

Positivity and a mass-dependent bound

Write \(F(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\log u_t(x)\). At the uniform allocation \(\bar x_p=1/m\), nonempty approvals imply \(u_t(\bar x)\ge 1/m\), hence \(F(\bar x)\ge-\log m\).

The extended-real function \(F\) is upper semicontinuous on the compact simplex: each summand is upper semicontinuous, and all summands are nonpositive. Therefore \(F\) attains its maximum. Any allocation giving some type zero utility has \(F(x)=-\infty\), because that type has positive mass. Consequently every Nash maximizer gives every type strictly positive utility.

Also, \(1/m\le W^*\le1\): the lower bound follows from the uniform allocation, and the upper bound follows from \(u_t(x)\le1\). Thus every permitted maximizer satisfies \(0<\rho\le1\).

A useful quantitative bound is \(u_t(x^N)\ge\mu_t\). To prove it, fix a project \(p\), let \(e_p\) allocate the entire budget to \(p\), and consider \(x(s)=(1-s)x^N+se_p\). All utilities at \(s=0\) are positive, so the right derivative exists. Optimality gives

\( 0\ge \left.\frac{d}{ds}F(x(s))\right|_{s=0+} =\sum_{r:p\in A_r}\frac{\mu_r}{u_r(x^N)}-1. \)

For a given type \(t\), choose \(p\in A_t\), which exists by assumption. All terms in the sum are nonnegative, so \(\mu_t/u_t(x^N)\le1\), proving the bound. In particular, with \(\mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t\),

\( \rho\ge \frac{\mu_{\min}}{W^*}\ge\mu_{\min}. \)

This bound is positive for each input, but unrestricted positive binary rational masses have no uniform positive lower bound.

A family approaching zero

For every integer \(k\ge2\), take two projects \(P=\{a,b\}\) and two distinct approval sets

\( A_1=\{a\},\qquad A_2=\{b\},\qquad \mu_1=\varepsilon=2^{-k},\qquad \mu_2=1-\varepsilon. \)

These masses are positive binary rationals and sum to one.

Write an allocation as \(x=(z,1-z)\), with \(0\le z\le1\). Its Nash objective is

\( f(z)=\varepsilon\log z+(1-\varepsilon)\log(1-z). \)

Both endpoints have objective \(-\infty\). On the interior,

\( f'(z)=\frac{\varepsilon}{z}-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1-z} =\frac{\varepsilon-z}{z(1-z)}. \)

The derivative is positive for \(z<\varepsilon\) and negative for \(z>\varepsilon\). Hence the unique Nash maximizer is \(x^N=(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)\), and its minimum utility is \(\varepsilon\).

Meanwhile,

\( W^*=\max_{0\le z\le1}\min\{z,1-z\}=\frac12. \)

Indeed, the minimum cannot exceed half the sum of the two coordinates, and equality holds at \(z=1/2\). Therefore

\( \rho=\frac{\varepsilon}{1/2}=2\varepsilon=2^{1-k}\longrightarrow0. \)

Together with the universal nonnegativity proved above, this establishes the claimed infimum.

Input size and the equally weighted comparison

The witness has \(m=\tau=2\) throughout. Its total input length is \(L=\Theta(k)\). Constructing it and writing its exact Nash allocation, benchmark, and ratio takes \(O(k)=O(L)\) bit operations using ordinary binary rational encoding. Thus the ratio can approach zero while both project count and support size remain fixed.

For \(n=2^k\), the same support represents an equally weighted list containing one agent approving only \(a\) and \(n-1\) agents approving only \(b\). Its ratio is exactly \(2/n\), consistent with the upper bound in the [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09637). The support size remains two; it is not the number of agents.

Consequently each weighted summand is upper semicontinuous and nonpositive. Their finite sum is upper semicontinuous; no undefined combination of positive and negative infinities occurs.

The invoked maximum-attainment theorem applies: an upper semicontinuous extended-real function on a nonempty compact set attains its supremum, and the finite value at \(\bar x\) ensures that this supremum is not \(-\infty\).

If any positive-mass type has zero utility, the objective is \(-\infty\). Such an allocation cannot maximize the objective. This proves strictly positive utilities for every permitted maximizer, without assuming uniqueness.

The uniform allocation establishes \(W^*\ge1/m\), and utilities bounded above by one establish \(W^*\le1\). The benchmark maximum exists because the minimum of finitely many continuous utilities is continuous on the compact simplex.

The conclusion \(0<\rho\le1\) follows: its numerator is positive, and the definition of \(W^*\) bounds the minimum utility of any allocation above by \(W^*\). Thus the ratio is well-defined for every admissible input.
