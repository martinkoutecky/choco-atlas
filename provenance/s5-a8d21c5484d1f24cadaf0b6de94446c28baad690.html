The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\subseteq\mathbb R\), finite candidate set \(M\subseteq\mathbb R\), society \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), and reported distribution \(\widehat\mu\), does there exist a deterministic mechanism \(F(M,\widehat\mu)\in M^2\) that is positive-mass group-strategyproof and guarantees, for every \(\mu\), a maximum-cost ratio strictly below \(3\), where \(\operatorname{MC}_{\mu}(Y)=\max_{t:\mu_t>0}\min_{y\in Y}|t-y|\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T\subset\mathbb Q\) and \(M\subset\mathbb Q\) be nonempty finite public sets of permissible locations and candidate locations, with \(q=|T|\) and \(m=|M|\). A society is a probability distribution \(\mu\) on \(T\), given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary rational masses. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; express any running times in \(m,\tau,q,L\).
A deterministic mechanism \(F_{T,M}\) maps every rational reported distribution on \(T\) to \(M^2\); coincident facilities are allowed, and all choices, including ties, are deterministic. Define \(c_t(Y)=\min_{y\in Y}|t-y|\), \(\operatorname{MC}_{\mu}(Y)=\max_{t:\mu_t>0}c_t(Y)\), and \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu}=\min_{Y\in M^2}\operatorname{MC}_{\mu}(Y)\).
A coalition deviation is a rational matrix \((a_{ts})_{t,s\in T}\) with \(a_{ts}\ge0\), \(b_t=\sum_s a_{ts}\le\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t b_t>0\): mass \(a_{ts}\) of true type \(t\) reports \(s\), producing \(\mu'_s=\mu_s-b_s+\sum_t a_{ts}\). Coalition members may split their mass among reports.
Positive-mass group-strategyproofness means that for every \(\mu\) and every such deviation, some \(t\) with \(b_t>0\) satisfies \(c_t(F_{T,M}(\mu))\le c_t(F_{T,M}(\mu'))\); thus a forbidden deviation must strictly benefit every participating type.
Does there exist a family of deterministic positive-mass group-strategyproof mechanisms and a uniform constant \(1\le\alpha<3\) such that \(\operatorname{MC}_{\mu}(F_{T,M}(\mu))\le\alpha\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu}\) for every \(T,M,\mu\), or is every such family unable to guarantee any uniform factor below \(3\)? The inequality also applies when \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu}=0\).
For finite lists of agents on a line, deterministic strategyproof mechanisms cannot guarantee a maximum-cost factor below \(3\), while an anonymous group-strategyproof mechanism achieves \(3\); randomized strategyproof mechanisms have lower bound \(2\). [Verified discrete bounds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09640v2).
No published result settling precisely the distribution-and-coalition model defined above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted coordinates to binary rationals to give a finite input encoding; masses are rational, nonnegative, and sum to one.
  • Made T a public permissible reporting domain, including types currently having zero mass; the mechanism may depend on T, and q records its size separately from support size.
  • Quantified over all finite domains and societies and required one uniform approximation constant below 3, rather than a guarantee for an isolated reported distribution.
  • Defined positive-mass coalitions through rational mass transfers, allowing partial participation and splitting a type's mass among reports.
  • Used the discrete definition's requirement that every coalition member strictly benefits, rather than the stronger condition of weak improvement for all and strict improvement for some.
  • Allowed coincident facilities, as prescribed by the outcome domain M squared, and required nonempty location and candidate sets.
  • Defined approximation by the multiplicative inequality, requiring zero cost whenever the optimum is zero.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Every deterministic positive-mass group-strategyproof family is unable to guarantee a uniform approximation factor below \(3\). Moreover, factor \(3\) is achievable in the stated model, including when \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu}=0\).

The lower bound already holds with \(m=3\), \(q=5\), and \(\tau=3\), using a deviation by one positive-mass type.

1. Lower bound: no uniform factor below \(3\)

Suppose, for a contradiction, that such a family guarantees a constant \(1\leq\alpha<3\).

Choose a rational number \(\varepsilon\) satisfying

\(0<\varepsilon<\tfrac12,\qquad \alpha(1+\varepsilon)<3.\)

Such a choice exists because \(3/\alpha-1>0\). Fix the public sets

\(T=\{-1,\,1-\varepsilon,\,1+\varepsilon,\,3,\,10\},\qquad M=\{0,2,10\}.\)

Write \(F=F_{T,M}\). Consider three distributions, each assigning mass \(1/3\) to its three listed support locations:

| Distribution | Support |
|---|---|
| \(\mu^0\) | \(\{1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon,10\}\) |
| \(\mu^+\) | \(\{1-\varepsilon,3,10\}\) |
| \(\mu^-\) | \(\{-1,1+\varepsilon,10\}\) |

We identify an outcome with its set of occupied candidates when calculating costs; the ordering of the two facilities has no effect.

1.1. Enumerating the relevant outcomes

Every distribution above has positive mass at \(10\). Consequently, any outcome omitting candidate \(10\) has maximum cost at least \(8\).

An outcome placing both facilities at \(10\) has maximum cost at least \(7\), because each distribution also contains a location at most \(3\).

The remaining possibilities are the occupied sets \(\{0,10\}\) and \(\{2,10\}\). Their exact maximum costs are:

| Distribution | Outcome \(\{0,10\}\) | Outcome \(\{2,10\}\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(\mu^0\) | \(1+\varepsilon\) | \(1+\varepsilon\) |
| \(\mu^+\) | \(3\) | \(1+\varepsilon\) |
| \(\mu^-\) | \(1+\varepsilon\) | \(3\) |

For example, under \(\mu^+\), outcome \(\{2,10\}\) gives costs \(1+\varepsilon\), \(1\), and \(0\), respectively. The other entries follow directly from the distances to the two occupied candidates.

These cases exhaust \(M^2\), up to facility ordering. Since \(1+\varepsilon<3/2\), they establish

\(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu^0}=\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu^+}=\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu^-}=1+\varepsilon.\)

The assumed approximation guarantee therefore requires maximum cost strictly below \(3\) on all three distributions. Thus:

  • \(F(\mu^0)\) occupies either \(\{0,10\}\) or \(\{2,10\}\).
  • \(F(\mu^+)\) must occupy \(\{2,10\}\).
  • \(F(\mu^-)\) must occupy \(\{0,10\}\).

1.2. Either initial choice permits a forbidden deviation

Case 1: \(F(\mu^0)\) occupies \(\{0,10\}\).

Let the entire mass at true type \(1+\varepsilon\) report \(3\). Formally, set

\(a_{1+\varepsilon,\,3}=\tfrac13,\)

with every other matrix entry zero. This is a rational feasible deviation: its only positive row sum equals the available mass \(1/3\). The reported distribution is exactly \(\mu^+\).

The participating type’s cost changes from \(1+\varepsilon\) to \(1-\varepsilon\), since \(F(\mu^+)\) occupies \(\{2,10\}\). This is a strict improvement.

Case 2: \(F(\mu^0)\) occupies \(\{2,10\}\).

Let the entire mass at true type \(1-\varepsilon\) report \(-1\), using

\(a_{1-\varepsilon,\,-1}=\tfrac13,\)

and zero elsewhere. The reported distribution is exactly \(\mu^-\).

Since \(F(\mu^-)\) occupies \(\{0,10\}\), this participating type’s cost also decreases from \(1+\varepsilon\) to \(1-\varepsilon\).

In both cases, every participating type strictly benefits—there is exactly one participating type. This contradicts positive-mass group-strategyproofness.

Therefore no such family guarantees any uniform factor below \(3\).

This adapts the distant-third-location construction in [Tang et al., Theorem 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09640v2). Including both possible deviation reports in the same public set \(T\) makes both branches apply to one fixed mechanism; no symmetry assumption is needed.

2. Matching upper bound in the distribution model

We now construct a family attaining factor \(3\) and prove its incentive property directly for the permitted mass-splitting deviations.

For each real number \(x\), define:

  • \(p(x)\): the largest candidate in \(M\) minimizing \(|x-y|\);
  • \(r(x)\): the smallest candidate in \(M\) minimizing \(|x-y|\).

For a reported distribution \(\mu\), let

\(\ell=\min\operatorname{supp}(\mu),\qquad h=\max\operatorname{supp}(\mu).\)

Define the deterministic mechanism

\(G_{T,M}(\mu)=(p(\ell),r(h)).\)

2.1. Monotonicity of nearest-candidate selectors

Both selectors are nondecreasing.

Indeed, suppose \(x<z\), but the chosen nearest candidates satisfy \(u>v\), where \(u\) is chosen at \(x\) and \(v\) at \(z\). Their nearest-candidate inequalities imply

\(|x-u|\leq|x-v|\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\geq\tfrac{u+v}{2},\)

and

\(|z-v|\leq|z-u|\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z\leq\tfrac{u+v}{2}.\)

This contradicts \(x<z\). At equal arguments, each selector returns the same candidate because its tie rule is fixed.

2.2. Positive-mass group-strategyproofness

Consider any feasible deviation from \(\mu\), producing \(\mu'\).

Each endpoint type already receives its minimum possible cost over all outcomes: the facility \(p(\ell)\) is a nearest candidate to \(\ell\), and \(r(h)\) is a nearest candidate to \(h\).

Therefore, if either endpoint participates, that type cannot strictly improve, as required.

It remains to consider deviations with \(b_{\ell}=b_h=0\). Both endpoint types then retain positive reported mass, because

\(\mu'_{\ell}=\mu_{\ell}+\sum_t a_{t\ell}>0,\qquad \mu'_h=\mu_h+\sum_t a_{th}>0.\)

Hence, writing the new support endpoints as \(\ell'\) and \(h'\), we have

\(\ell'\leq\ell,\qquad h'\geq h.\)

Let

\(u=p(\ell),\quad u'=p(\ell'),\qquad v=r(h),\quad v'=r(h').\)

Monotonicity gives \(u'\leq u\) and \(v'\geq v\).

If \(u'<u\), nearest-candidate optimality at \(\ell\) gives

\(|\ell-u|\leq|\ell-u'|\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell\geq\tfrac{u+u'}2.\)

Thus every true supported type \(t\geq\ell\) satisfies \(|t-u|\leq|t-u'|\). The same inequality holds trivially when \(u'=u\).

Similarly, if \(v'>v\), optimality at \(h\) gives

\(|h-v|\leq|h-v'|\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h\leq\tfrac{v+v'}2.\)

Every true supported type \(t\leq h\) therefore satisfies \(|t-v|\leq|t-v'|\), also trivially when \(v'=v\).

Taking the minimum of the two facility distances yields, for every true supported type,

\(c_t(G_{T,M}(\mu))\leq c_t(G_{T,M}(\mu')).\)

Every participating type is truly supported, because \(b_t>0\) and \(b_t\leq\mu_t\). Thus no permitted deviation strictly benefits all its participants. This proof allows arbitrary rational mass splitting.

2.3. Approximation factor \(3\)

Let an optimal outcome have candidates \(z_1\leq z_2\), and set \(R=\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu}\). Partition the true support into

\(S_1=\{t:|t-z_1|\leq|t-z_2|\},\qquad S_2=\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\setminus S_1.\)

If \(z_1<z_2\), these are respectively a prefix and a suffix of the ordered support, separated at their midpoint. Consequently, whenever \(S_1\) is nonempty it contains \(\ell\), and whenever \(S_2\) is nonempty it contains \(h\). If \(z_1=z_2\), all support belongs to \(S_1\).

For \(t\in S_1\), both \(t\) and \(\ell\) are within distance \(R\) of \(z_1\). Since \(p(\ell)\) is a nearest candidate to \(\ell\),

\(c_t(G_{T,M}(\mu)) \leq|t-p(\ell)| \leq|t-z_1|+|z_1-\ell|+|\ell-p(\ell)| \leq3R.\)

For \(t\in S_2\), the identical argument using \(h\), \(z_2\), and \(r(h)\) gives

\(c_t(G_{T,M}(\mu))\leq3R.\)

Therefore

\(\operatorname{MC}_{\mu}(G_{T,M}(\mu))\leq3\operatorname{OPT}_{\mu}.\)

No division by \(R\) was used, so this also proves the required guarantee when \(R=0\).

The mechanism uses a support scan and two candidate scans, with no expansion of rational masses into agents. A conservative total bit-time bound, including reading the public sets, is \(O((q+m+\tau)L^2)\), using elementary exact rational arithmetic.
