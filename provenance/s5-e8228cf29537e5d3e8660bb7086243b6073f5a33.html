The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supports of complete manager and provider types with rational masses μ and ν, market rates and deadlines, and type-conditional rival bid and provider offer laws whose mass-weighted mixtures define \(F\) and \(G\), compute the focal manager type’s optimal second-price bid function \(b^*(r)\) over its participation horizon with regret at most ε and a certificate of that bound.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) manager and provider types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Manager masses \(\mu_i\) and provider masses \(\nu_j\) each sum to \(1\). There is one resource type, so \(m=1\), and each manager seeks one unit.
Each manager type specifies rational utility \(u_i>0\), discount rate \(\gamma_i\geq0\), deadline \(T_i>0\), participation probability \(\alpha_i\in(0,1]\), and a stationary rival-bid CDF \(F_i\); each provider type specifies a stationary offer CDF \(G_j\). These laws have nonnegative piecewise-polynomial densities, explicitly encoded by rational coefficients and breakpoints on a common rational interval \([0,B]\). Let \(K\) count all encoded pieces and coefficients and \(L\) be total input bit length.
The stationary market laws are \(F=\sum_i\mu_iF_i\) and \(G=\sum_j\nu_jG_j\); they are exogenous. Input also includes rational flat price \(0<\varpi\leq B\) and positive rational rates \(\lambda_A,\lambda_M,\lambda_P\). Fix a focal manager type with parameters \((u,\gamma,T,\alpha)\).
Its auction opportunities form a Poisson process of rate \(\alpha\lambda_A\). At each opportunity, independently draw \(N\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_M)\) rival bids from \(F\) and \(Q\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_P)\) provider offers from \(G\), independently across opportunities.
An admissible policy is a measurable nonincreasing bid function \(b:[0,T]\to[0,\varpi]\), with \(b(0)=\varpi\), where \(r\) is residual time. It wins precisely when \(Q>0\), some offer is strictly below \(b(r)\), and its bid exceeds every rival bid. It then obtains the lowest-offer provider's unit and pays the highest rival bid, or \(b(T)\) when \(N=0\); the offer is an eligibility condition, not a payment floor. Otherwise it continues. Winning ends participation; failing to win by the deadline forces purchase at \(\varpi\). Continuous densities eliminate rival ties.
Let \(S\) be elapsed time to acquisition and \(P\) its payment, taking \((S,P)=(T,\varpi)\) after failure. Define \(J(b)=\mathbb E[e^{-\gamma S}(e^{-\gamma(T-S)}u-P)]\) and \(V^*=\sup_bJ(b)\). Given binary-rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), the computational problem is to return a rational piecewise-linear admissible policy \(\widehat b\) and rational bounds \(\ell\leq J(\widehat b)\leq V^*\leq U\) with \(U-\ell\leq\varepsilon\).

Can this certified additive-regret bidding problem be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L,1/\varepsilon\), or is it NP-hard?

For the usual explicit representation of a piecewise-linear policy by its breakpoints and values, the requested polynomial running time is impossible: some inputs require exponentially many output pieces.

I prove this obstruction below, with the domain error identified in NOTES repaired. I also establish two further results:

1. Exact characterization for the full input class

Let
$$ a=\alpha\lambda_A,\qquad \eta=e^{-\lambda_M}. $$
Write $f$ and $g$ for the densities of the mixtures $F$ and $G$. The input assumptions imply that these densities are nonnegative, integrable, and piecewise polynomial. In particular, $F$ and $G$ are absolutely continuous.

For a bid $x\in[0,\varpi]$, define
$$ A(x)=1-e^{-\lambda_PG(x)},\qquad H(x)=e^{-\lambda_M(1-F(x))}. $$
Here $A(x)$ is the probability that some provider offer is strictly below $x$, and $H(x)$ is the probability that every rival bid is strictly below $x$, including the no-rivals event. Continuity of the distributions makes strict inequalities immaterial except for the no-rivals atom, which is handled separately below.

Independence gives the winning probability
$$ q(x)=A(x)H(x). $$

The largest rival bid has an atom of mass $\eta$ at zero, corresponding to no rivals, and density $H'(y)$ on $(0,B)$. Define
$$ I(x)=\int_0^x yH'(y)\,dy,\qquad w(x)=A(x)I(x). $$
Thus, if $c=b(T)$, the unconditional expected payment at an opportunity with bid $x$ is
$$ w(x)+c\eta A(x). $$
This formula uses the stipulated payment rule: provider offers affect eligibility, but do not impose a payment floor.

1.1 The scalar differential equation

Define
$$ R(x,z)=q(x)z-w(x),\qquad \Phi(z)=\max_{0\leq x\leq\varpi}R(x,z). $$
Consider
$$ v'(r)=-\gamma v(r)-a\Phi(v(r)),\qquad v(0)=\varpi. $$

For each $x$, the function $R(x,\cdot)$ is affine with slope in $[0,1]$. Consequently,
$$ |\Phi(z_1)-\Phi(z_2)|\leq |z_1-z_2|. $$
The differential equation therefore has a unique global solution by the existence and uniqueness theorem for globally Lipschitz vector fields.

Since $A(0)=0$, we have $q(0)=w(0)=0$. Hence, for $z\geq0$,
$$ 0\leq\Phi(z)\leq z. $$
Also $\Phi(0)=0$, so zero is an equilibrium. Uniqueness prevents a positive solution from crossing zero. The differential inequalities then give
$$ \varpi e^{-(\gamma+a)r}\leq v(r)\leq\varpi, \qquad v'(r)\leq0. $$

1.2 A monotone maximizing selector

For $z\in[0,\varpi]$, let $\beta(z)$ be the largest maximizer of $R(\cdot,z)$. This maximizer exists because $R$ is continuous and the bid interval is compact.

The function $\beta$ is nondecreasing. To prove this, suppose $z_1<z_2$ but
$$ x_1=\beta(z_1)>\beta(z_2)=x_2. $$
The function
$$ D(z)=R(x_1,z)-R(x_2,z) $$
is nondecreasing because $q(x_1)\geq q(x_2)$. Optimality gives $D(z_1)\geq0$ and $D(z_2)\leq0$. Thus both are zero, making $x_1$ a maximizer at $z_2$. This contradicts the choice of $x_2$ as the largest maximizer. A monotone function is measurable, so $\beta$ is measurable.

We also need $\beta(\varpi)=\varpi$. Almost everywhere,
$$ q'(x)=A'(x)H(x)+A(x)H'(x), $$
and
$$ w'(x)=A'(x)I(x)+A(x)xH'(x). $$
Since
$$ 0\leq I(x)\leq xH(x), $$
we obtain
$$ 0\leq w'(x)\leq xq'(x). $$
Therefore
$$ \frac{\partial R}{\partial x}(x,\varpi) =\varpi q'(x)-w'(x) \geq(\varpi-x)q'(x)\geq0 $$
almost everywhere. Absolute continuity implies that $R(\cdot,\varpi)$ is nondecreasing, proving $\beta(\varpi)=\varpi$.

Define
$$ b_*(r)= \begin{cases} \beta(v(r)),&0\leq r<T,\\ 0,&r=T. \end{cases} $$
Because $v$ is nonincreasing and $\beta$ is nondecreasing, this policy is nonincreasing. It is measurable and satisfies $b_*(0)=\varpi$.

The endpoint modification is essential. It changes no acquisition probabilities, because an opportunity occurs at elapsed time zero with probability zero, but it sets every no-rivals payment to zero.

1.3 Verification of optimality

For any admissible policy $b$, put $c=b(T)$ and
$$ p_b(t)=\exp\left(-a\int_0^t q(b(T-s))\,ds\right). $$
Independent Poisson marking implies that $p_b(t)$ is the probability of remaining unacquired at elapsed time $t$.

Let
$$ D(b)=\mathbb E[e^{-\gamma S}P]. $$
The terminal purchase and opportunity payments give
$$ D(b)=e^{-\gamma T}p_b(T)\varpi +a\int_0^T e^{-\gamma t}p_b(t) \left[w(b(T-t))+c\eta A(b(T-t))\right]\,dt. $$

The absolutely continuous function $e^{-\gamma t}p_b(t)v(T-t)$ has derivative, almost everywhere,
$$ a e^{-\gamma t}p_b(t) \left[\Phi(v(T-t))-q(b(T-t))v(T-t)\right]. $$
Integrating and substituting the payment expression yields
$$ D(b)-v(T) = a\int_0^T e^{-\gamma t}p_b(t) \left[ \Phi(v(T-t))-R(b(T-t),v(T-t)) +c\eta A(b(T-t)) \right]\,dt. $$
Both terms inside the brackets are nonnegative. Thus $D(b)\geq v(T)$ for every admissible policy.

For $b_*$, the maximizing-selector term vanishes almost everywhere, and $c=0$. Hence $D(b_*)=v(T)$.

Finally, the utility term is independent of acquisition time:
$$ e^{-\gamma S}e^{-\gamma(T-S)}u=e^{-\gamma T}u. $$
We have therefore proved the exact characterization
$$ \boxed{V^*=e^{-\gamma T}u-v(T).} $$

This result settles the mathematical optimization over measurable admissible policies. It does not supply a polynomial bound for the required explicit piecewise-linear output.

and
$$ \alpha=\lambda_A=\lambda_M=\lambda_P=1. $$
There is one manager type and one provider type, each with mass $1$, and both distributions are uniform on $[0,B]$.

These inputs satisfy every stated restriction. We have $m=1$, $\tau=2$, and constant $K$. Their bit length is
$$ L_k=\Theta(k). $$

Normalize bids by writing
$$ x(r)=\frac{b(r)}B,\qquad c=x(2), $$
and put
$$ \eta=e^{-1},\qquad \theta=1-\eta. $$
The normalized opportunity quantities are
$$ A(x)=1-e^{-x},\qquad q(x)=e^{-1}(e^x-1), $$
$$ I(x)=\int_0^x y e^{y-1}\,dy =e^{-1}\bigl((x-1)e^x+1\bigr), \qquad w(x)=A(x)I(x). $$

Define the normalized functions
$$ R(x,z)=q(x)z-w(x),\qquad \Phi(z)=\max_{0\leq x\leq1}R(x,z), $$
and let
$$ z'(r)=-\Phi(z(r)),\qquad z(0)=1. $$
The preceding verification proves
$$ V^*=1-Bz(2). $$

3. The optimal selector has positive curvature

Direct calculation gives
$$ R(x,z) =e^{-1}\left[(e^x-1)(z-x+1)-1+e^{-x}\right], $$
and
$$ \frac{\partial R}{\partial x}(x,z) =e^{x-1}\bigl(z-\psi(x)\bigr), \qquad \psi(x)=x-e^{-x}+e^{-2x}. $$
Moreover,
$$ \psi'(x)=(1-e^{-x})(1+2e^{-x})>0 \qquad(0<x\leq1). $$
Thus, for $z>0$, the unique maximizing bid is
$$ \beta(z)= \begin{cases} 1,&z\geq\psi(1),\\ \psi^{-1}(z),&0<z<\psi(1). \end{cases} $$
For this section, abbreviate the residual-time selector by
$$ \beta(r)=\beta(z(r)). $$

While the bid equals $1$,
$$ q(1)=\theta,\qquad w(1)=\theta\eta, $$
so
$$ z(r)=\eta+\theta e^{-\theta r}. $$
The bid becomes interior when
$$ z(r)=\psi(1)=\eta+\theta^2. $$
This occurs at
$$ r_0=-\frac{\log\theta}{\theta}. $$

We have $r_0<1$. Indeed, the exponential series gives $e>8/3$, hence $\theta>5/8$, and
$$ -\log\theta<\log(8/5)<3/5<5/8<\theta. $$
Here $\log(8/5)<3/5$ follows from $\log t<t-1$ for $t>1$.

At an interior optimum, substitution gives
$$ \Phi(\psi(x))=e^{x-1}(1-e^{-x})^3. $$
This is positive for $x>0$. Consequently, $z$ keeps decreasing after the transition, and the selector stays interior for $r>r_0$.

Differentiating $z(r)=\psi(\beta(r))$ gives
$$ \beta'(r)=-h(\beta(r)), \qquad h(x)=\frac{e^{x-1}(1-e^{-x})^2}{1+2e^{-x}}. $$
For $x>0$, $h(x)>0$, and
$$ \frac{h'(x)}{h(x)} = 1+\frac{2e^{-x}}{1-e^{-x}} +\frac{2e^{-x}}{1+2e^{-x}}>0. $$
Therefore
$$ \beta''(r)=h'(\beta(r))h(\beta(r))>0 \qquad(r>r_0). $$

Set
$$ D=[1,3/2],\qquad a_0=\min_{r\in D}\beta(r)>0. $$
The earlier bound $z(r)\geq e^{-r}$ ensures positivity. Continuity and compactness now give a constant $\kappa>0$, independent of $B$, such that
$$ \beta''(r)\geq\kappa \qquad(r\in D). $$

4. Regret controls squared approximation error

For an arbitrary admissible normalized policy $x$, let
$$ p(t)=\exp\left(-\int_0^t q(x(2-s))\,ds\right). $$
The verification identity from Section 1, after normalization, becomes
$$ V^*-J(b) = B\int_0^2 p(t) \left[ \Phi(z(2-t))-R(x(2-t),z(2-t)) +c\eta A(x(2-t)) \right]\,dt. $$

For $s\in[0,1]$, concavity of $1-e^{-s}$ gives
$$ 1-e^{-s}\geq\theta s. $$
Hence
$$ \psi'(s)\geq\theta s. $$
For $y\in[a_0,1]$ and $s\in[0,1]$, integration yields
$$ |\psi(y)-\psi(s)| \geq\frac{\theta}{2}|y^2-s^2| \geq\frac{\theta a_0}{2}|y-s|. $$
The restriction $y\leq1$ repairs the domain error in NOTES.

Using the derivative formula for $R$, and integrating in the appropriate direction on either side of its maximizer, we obtain
$$ \begin{aligned} R(y,\psi(y))-R(x,\psi(y)) &=\int_{\min(x,y)}^{\max(x,y)} e^{s-1}|\psi(y)-\psi(s)|\,ds\\ &\geq\frac{\eta\theta a_0}{2} \int_{\min(x,y)}^{\max(x,y)}|y-s|\,ds\\ &=\frac{\eta\theta a_0}{4}(x-y)^2. \end{aligned} $$
This holds for every $x\in[0,1]$ and $y\in[a_0,1]$.

Since $q(x)\leq1$, we also have $p(t)\geq e^{-2}$. Restricting the verification integral to residual times in $D$, and discarding the nonnegative no-rivals term, proves
$$ \boxed{ V^*-J(b) \geq B e^{-2}\frac{\eta\theta a_0}{4} \int_D |x(r)-\beta(r)|^2\,dr. } $$

5. Lower bound for piecewise-linear policies

We first prove an approximation lemma.

Lemma. Suppose $f$ is twice continuously differentiable on an interval of length $d$, and $f''\geq\kappa>0$. For every affine function $l$,
$$ \int |f-l|^2\geq\frac{\kappa^2d^5}{720}. $$

Proof. Translate the interval to $[0,d]$, and put
$$ \phi(s)=s^2(d-s)^2. $$
Both $\phi$ and $\phi'$ vanish at the endpoints. Two integrations by parts therefore give
$$ \int_0^d(f-l)\phi''\,ds =\int_0^d f''\phi\,ds \geq\kappa\int_0^d\phi\,ds. $$
Direct integration gives
$$ \int_0^d\phi\,ds=\frac{d^5}{30}, \qquad \int_0^d(\phi'')^2\,ds=\frac{4d^5}{5}. $$
Cauchy–Schwarz implies
$$ \int_0^d|f-l|^2\,ds \geq \frac{(\kappa d^5/30)^2}{4d^5/5} =\frac{\kappa^2d^5}{720}. $$
This proves the lemma.

Now let $b$ have $n$ affine pieces. Intersecting them with $D$ partitions $D$ into $j\leq n$ positive-length intervals, with lengths $d_1,\ldots,d_j$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{i=1}^j d_i=\frac12. $$
The lemma applies on each interval. Convexity of the fifth power gives
$$ \begin{aligned} \int_D|x-\beta|^2\,dr &\geq\frac{\kappa^2}{720}\sum_{i=1}^j d_i^5\\ &\geq\frac{\kappa^2}{720} \frac{(1/2)^5}{j^4}\\ &\geq\frac{\kappa^2(1/2)^5}{720n^4}. \end{aligned} $$
Combining this with Section 4 proves that a constant $c_0>0$, independent of $k$, satisfies
$$ \boxed{V^*-J(b)\geq\frac{c_0B}{n^4}.} $$
The argument allows arbitrary real breakpoints and coefficients, so it applies in particular to rational piecewise-linear policies.

6. A matching upper bound with rational policies

The preceding lower bound is sharp up to constants. I give a construction because it distinguishes the output obstruction from a failure of piecewise-linear approximation.

The interior branch of $\beta$ has bounded first and second derivatives on $[r_0,2]$, taking right derivatives at $r_0$. This follows from
$$ \beta'=-h(\beta),\qquad \beta''=h'(\beta)h(\beta), $$
because $\beta([r_0,2])$ is a compact subset of $(0,1]$, and $h$ is smooth there. Define finite constants
$$ M_1=\sup_{[r_0,2]}|\beta'|, \qquad M_2=\sup_{[r_0,2]}|\beta''|, $$
and
$$ M_R=\sup_{(x,z)\in[0,1]^2} \left|\frac{\partial^2R}{\partial x^2}(x,z)\right|. $$

Fix a sufficiently large integer $n$, and put
$$ \delta=n^{-4}. $$
Choose rational numbers $r_-\leq r_0\leq r_+$ satisfying
$$ 0<r_-,\qquad r_+-r_-\leq n^{-2}, \qquad r_+<2-\delta. $$
For example, consecutive points of a rational grid of mesh $1/(2n^2)$ can bracket $r_0$.

Partition $[r_+,2-\delta]$ into $n$ equal intervals. At every node $s$ of this partition, choose the rational value
$$ v_s=\frac{\lfloor n^2\beta(s)\rfloor}{n^2}. $$
These values are nonincreasing and satisfy
$$ 0\leq\beta(s)-v_s<n^{-2}. $$

Construct $x_n$ as follows:

  • Set $x_n=1$ on $[0,r_-]$.
  • Interpolate linearly from $(r_-,1)$ to $(r_+,v_{r_+})$.
  • Interpolate the values $v_s$ on the $n$ partition intervals.
  • Interpolate linearly from $(2-\delta,v_{2-\delta})$ to $(2,0)$.

This is a rational, continuous, nonincreasing policy with at most $n+3$ affine pieces. Thus $b_n=Bx_n$ is admissible.

6.1 Approximation away from the endpoint taper

On $[r_-,r_+]$, both $\beta$ and $x_n$ lie between $v_{r_+}$ and $1$. Consequently,
$$ |x_n(r)-\beta(r)| \leq1-v_{r_+} \leq(M_1+1)n^{-2}. $$

On an interval of length $h$ in the smooth branch, interpolation of the exact endpoint values has error at most $M_2h^2/8$. To verify this bound, subtract the affine interpolant and compare the resulting function with the two quadratic functions having second derivatives $\pm M_2$ and zero endpoint values; convexity gives the bound $M_2h^2/8$.

Our partition has $h\leq2/n$. Rounding both endpoint values changes the interpolant by less than $n^{-2}$. Hence
$$ |x_n(r)-\beta(r)| \leq\left(\frac{M_2}{2}+1\right)n^{-2} $$
on $[r_+,2-\delta]$.

With
$$ E=\max\left(M_1+1,\frac{M_2}{2}+1\right), $$
we have therefore shown
$$ |x_n(r)-\beta(r)|\leq En^{-2} \qquad(0\leq r\leq2-\delta). $$

6.2 Converting this approximation into regret

When $r\geq r_0$, the maximizing bid satisfies
$$ R_x(\beta(r),z(r))=0. $$
Taylor's theorem gives
$$ \Phi(z(r))-R(x_n(r),z(r)) \leq\frac{M_R}{2}|x_n(r)-\beta(r)|^2. $$

For $r\leq r_-$, the bid agrees exactly with the maximizer. On the remaining boundary portion $[r_-,r_0]$,
$$ R_x(1,z(r))=z(r)-\psi(1). $$
Since $z(r_0)=\psi(1)$ and $|z'|\leq1$,
$$ 0\leq R_x(1,z(r))\leq r_0-r\leq n^{-2}. $$
Taylor's theorem therefore gives, throughout $[0,2-\delta]$,
$$ \Phi(z(r))-R(x_n(r),z(r)) \leq \left(E+\frac{M_RE^2}{2}\right)n^{-4}. $$

On the final taper interval, a uniform bound suffices. Since $0\leq z\leq1$, $q\leq1$, and $w\leq1$,
$$ 0\leq\Phi(z)-R(x,z)\leq2. $$
Its integral over the taper is at most $2\delta$.

Finally, $x_n(2)=0$, so the no-rivals term in the verification identity vanishes. Using $p(t)\leq1$, we obtain
$$ \boxed{ V^*-J(b_n)\leq\frac{C_0B}{n^4}, } $$
where
$$ C_0=2E+M_RE^2+2 $$
is independent of $B$ and $n$.

Thus the uniform family admits rational policies with $O(n)$ pieces and regret $O(B/n^4)$, matching the lower bound.

7. Consequence for the requested running time

Every valid output certificate satisfies
$$ V^*-J(\widehat b) \leq U-\ell \leq\varepsilon, $$
because $\ell\leq J(\widehat b)$ and $V^*\leq U$.

If the returned policy has $n$ affine pieces, Section 5 forces
$$ n\geq\left(\frac{c_0B}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/4}. $$
Fixing $\varepsilon=1/4$ and taking $B=2^k$ gives
$$ n\geq(4c_0)^{1/4}2^{k/4} =2^{\Omega(L_k)}. $$
An explicit list of breakpoints and values requires at least one record per piece. Merely writing the policy therefore takes exponential time, while $m$, $\tau$, $K$, and $1/\varepsilon$ remain constant.

Moreover, Section 6 proves that this dependence is sharp: for sufficiently large $B/\varepsilon$, the minimum possible piece count on this family is
$$ \Theta\left((B/\varepsilon)^{1/4}\right). $$

8. What remains open

The standard explicit-output formulation cannot have the proposed polynomial running time. This conclusion is unconditional and follows from output length.

However, an exponential output requirement does not itself establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. No hardness reduction has been proved here. The polynomial-time-versus-$\mathrm{NP}$-hardness alternatives therefore cannot be treated as an exhaustive dichotomy on the basis of this argument.

If the intended output permits a succinct description of exponentially many affine pieces, the output-size obstruction does not settle that formulation. The general scalar characterization in Section 1 supplies a starting point, but certified complexity bounds for such a representation remain open.
