Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(K\), deviation bound \(L\), tolerance \(\epsilon>0\), and a distribution \(\mu\) over approval-set or ranking types, compute a finite-support lottery \(\Delta\) over committees \(S\subseteq C\) with \(|S|=K\) such that, for every deviation \(S'\subseteq C\) with \(1\le |S'|\le L\), \(\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\Delta}[\sum_{t:S'\succ_t S}\mu_t]\le (1+\epsilon)|S'|/K\).

Given a candidate set \(C\) with \(m=|C|\), integers \(1\le \ell\le K\le m\), and a positive binary rational \(\epsilon\), consider the following lottery computation problem.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type specifies either an approval set \(A_t\subseteq C\), possibly empty, or a strict total ranking of \(C\); mixtures of these two kinds are permitted. An approval type strictly prefers \(S'\) to \(S\) exactly when \(|S'\cap A_t|>|S\cap A_t|\); a ranking type does so exactly when its favorite candidate in \(S'\) outranks its favorite candidate in \(S\). Indifference never counts as strict preference.
Compute an explicitly listed finite lottery \(\Delta\), with nonnegative binary rational probabilities summing to one, over committees \(S\subseteq C\) of size exactly \(K\), satisfying \(\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\Delta}[\sum_{t:S'\succ_t S}\mu_t]\le(1+\epsilon)|S'|/K\) for every \(S'\subseteq C\) with \(1\le |S'|\le\ell\).

Partial results for mixed profiles

The unrestricted search problem remains unresolved here. The following results strengthen the fixed-support tractability result in the notes:

  • Mixed profiles admit a polynomial-time randomized algorithm when the number of approval types is fixed, even when the number of ranking types is unrestricted.
  • Every mixed profile admits an approximately stable dyadic lottery supported on at most $a\ell+m+1$ committees, where $a$ is the number of approval types.

The algorithm has running time
$$ \operatorname{poly}\!\left(m,\tau,L,K,\ell,1/\epsilon,(\ell+1)^{a+1}\right) $$
and success probability at least $5/6$. Its dependence on $a$ prevents it from settling the requested unrestricted bound.

The proof uses the probability-matching idea of [Cheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.04438v1), but proves the required rounding inequalities directly.

1. Definitions and a stronger system of inequalities

Partition the supported types into approval types $\mathcal A$ and ranking types $\mathcal R$, and write $a=|\mathcal A|$. For a size-$K$ committee $S$ and a nonempty deviation $T$, define
$$ g(S,T)=\sum_t\mu_t\mathbf 1[T\succ_t S]. $$
For a lottery $\Delta$, let $g(\Delta,T)=\mathbb E_{S\sim\Delta}[g(S,T)]$.

For each candidate $c$, define its ranking capture mass against $S$ by
$$ r_c(S)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal R}\mu_t\mathbf 1[\{c\}\succ_t S]. $$
Consider the stronger capture expression
$$ \widetilde g(S,T) = \sum_{t\in\mathcal A}\mu_t \mathbf 1[|T\cap A_t|>|S\cap A_t|] + \sum_{c\in T}r_c(S). $$

For every ranking type, if $T$ is strictly preferred to $S$, then some candidate in $T$ is strictly preferred to the favorite candidate in $S$. Consequently,
$$ g(S,T)\le\widetilde g(S,T). $$
The same inequality holds after taking expectations over $S$.

Since the masses sum to one, for $1\le |T|\le\ell$ we also have
$$ 0\le\widetilde g(S,T)\le |T|. $$
Indeed, an approval type contributes at most its mass, and a ranking type contributes at most $|T|$ times its mass.

We will prove feasibility and tractability results for the stronger inequalities
$$ \widetilde g(\Delta,T)\le\frac{|T|}{K}. $$

2. Dependent rounding and an elementary probability bound

Lemma 1: rounding with negative dependence

Given rational numbers $\alpha_c\in[0,1]$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{c\in C}\alpha_c=K, $$
one can sample a size-$K$ committee $S$ such that, writing $X_c=\mathbf 1[c\in S]$,
$$ \mathbb E[X_c]=\alpha_c $$
and, for every $U\subseteq C$,
$$ \mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{c\in U}(1-X_c)\right] \le \prod_{c\in U}(1-\alpha_c). $$

Proof. Start with the fractional vector $x=\alpha$. While two coordinates $x_i,x_j$ are fractional, put
$$ u=\min\{1-x_i,x_j\}, \qquad v=\min\{x_i,1-x_j\}. $$
Replace $(x_i,x_j)$ by
$$ (x_i+u,x_j-u) $$
with probability $v/(u+v)$, and otherwise by
$$ (x_i-v,x_j+v). $$
The update preserves their sum and their individual expectations. At least one of the two coordinates becomes integral.

Fix $U$. If it contains neither updated coordinate, its product is unchanged. If it contains exactly one, the product has unchanged conditional expectation. If it contains both, write the update as $(x_i+t,x_j-t)$. Then
$$ (1-x_i-t)(1-x_j+t) = (1-x_i)(1-x_j)+(x_j-x_i)t-t^2. $$
Since $\mathbb E[t]=0$, its conditional expectation cannot increase. Multiplication by the remaining nonnegative factors preserves this conclusion.

Thus the relevant product is a supermartingale. There are at most $m$ updates. At termination all coordinates are integral: one remaining fractional coordinate would contradict the integral coordinate sum $K$. This proves the claims. $\square$

Lemma 2: a reciprocal-moment bound

Let $X=\sum_{i=1}^hX_i$, where the $X_i$ are zero-one variables satisfying the negative-dependence inequality in Lemma 1. Put $\lambda=\mathbb E[X]>0$. For every positive integer $j$,
$$ \Pr[X<j]\le j\frac{1-e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda}. $$

Proof. For $z\in[0,1]$, expand
$$ z^X=\prod_{i=1}^h\bigl(z+(1-z)(1-X_i)\bigr). $$
All coefficients in this expansion are nonnegative. Negative dependence therefore gives
$$ \mathbb E[z^X] \le \prod_{i=1}^h\bigl(1-(1-z)\mathbb E[X_i]\bigr) \le e^{-\lambda(1-z)}. $$
Integrating and using the finite support of $X$ yields
$$ \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{X+1}\right] = \int_0^1\mathbb E[z^X]\,dz \le \frac{1-e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda}. $$
On the event $X<j$, integrality gives $X+1\le j$. Hence
$$ \mathbf 1[X<j]\le\frac{j}{X+1}. $$
Taking expectations proves the result. $\square$

3. A response to every lottery of deviations

Proposition 3: the stronger system has a probability-matching response

Let $\Gamma$ be any lottery over deviations $T$ satisfying $1\le |T|\le\ell$. There is an efficiently samplable lottery $\mathcal D_\Gamma$ over size-$K$ committees such that
$$ \mathbb E_{S\sim\mathcal D_\Gamma,\ T\sim\Gamma} \left[\widetilde g(S,T)-\frac{|T|}{K}\right]\le0, $$
where $S$ and $T$ are sampled independently.

Proof. Define
$$ p_c=\Pr_{T\sim\Gamma}[c\in T], \qquad \beta=\frac{\mathbb E_{T\sim\Gamma}[|T|]}{K}. $$
Then $0<\beta\le1$ and
$$ \sum_cp_c=\beta K. $$
Therefore
$$ \sum_c\min\{1,p_c/\beta\}\le K. $$
Increase these quantities, if necessary, to a vector $\alpha\in[0,1]^m$ whose sum is exactly $K$. Such an increase is possible because $K\le m$. Sample $S$ using Lemma 1.

We establish a contribution bound of $\beta$ for each type.

Approval types

Fix an approval set $A$. Partition it into
$$ F=\{c\in A:\alpha_c=1\}, \qquad B=A\setminus F. $$
Almost surely $F\subseteq S$, so
$$ |T\cap A|>|S\cap A| \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |T\cap B|>|S\cap B|. $$
Put
$$ X=|S\cap B|, \qquad Y=|T\cap B|, \qquad \lambda=\mathbb E[X]. $$
For every $c\in B$, the inequality $\alpha_c<1$ implies $p_c\le\beta\alpha_c$. Consequently,
$$ \mathbb E[Y]\le\beta\lambda. $$

If $\lambda=0$, then $\mathbb E[Y]=0$, and both variables vanish almost surely. Capture is impossible.

Otherwise, $X$ and $Y$ are independent, and Lemma 2 gives
$$ \begin{aligned} \Pr[X<Y] &=\sum_{j\ge1}\Pr[Y=j]\Pr[X<j]\\ &\le \frac{1-e^{-\lambda}}{\lambda} \sum_{j\ge1}j\Pr[Y=j]\\ &\le\beta. \end{aligned} $$
Thus the approval type's capture probability is at most $\beta$.

Ranking types

Fix a ranking
$$ c_1\succ c_2\succ\cdots\succ c_m. $$
Let $h$ be the first index with $\alpha_{c_h}=1$, or let $h=m+1$ if there is no such index.

For $j\ge h$, singleton capture by $c_j$ is impossible. For $j<h$, Lemma 1 gives
$$ \Pr[\{c_j\}\succ S] = \Pr[X_{c_1}=\cdots=X_{c_j}=0] \le \prod_{i=1}^j(1-\alpha_{c_i}). $$
Since $p_{c_j}\le\beta\alpha_{c_j}$ for these indices,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{j=1}^mp_{c_j}\Pr[\{c_j\}\succ S] &\le \beta\sum_{j=1}^{h-1} \alpha_{c_j}\prod_{i=1}^j(1-\alpha_{c_i})\\ &\le \beta\sum_{j=1}^{h-1} \alpha_{c_j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}(1-\alpha_{c_i})\\ &= \beta\left(1-\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}(1-\alpha_{c_i})\right)\\ &\le\beta. \end{aligned} $$
This bounds precisely the expected sum of singleton captures contributed by this ranking type.

Weighting both kinds of bounds by their masses gives
$$ \mathbb E[\widetilde g(S,T)]\le\beta. $$
The expected deviation cost is also $\beta$, proving the proposition. $\square$

The construction needs only the candidate inclusion marginals of $\Gamma$. It does not need an explicit enumeration of its support.

4. Strong feasibility and shorter lottery descriptions

Theorem 4: existence with support at most $a\ell+m+1$

Every instance admits a lottery satisfying
$$ \widetilde g(\Delta,T)\le\frac{|T|}{K} \qquad (1\le |T|\le\ell), $$
supported on at most $a\ell+m+1$ committees, with rational probabilities of polynomial encoding length.

For every positive $\epsilon$, the same support bound is attainable with dyadic probabilities satisfying the original requested inequalities.

Proof. Consider the finite zero-sum game with defender actions all size-$K$ committees, attacker actions all permitted deviations, and attacker payoff
$$ H(S,T)=\widetilde g(S,T)-\frac{|T|}{K}. $$
Proposition 3 shows that against every mixed attacker strategy, some defender action has expected payoff at most zero. The finite minimax theorem therefore gives a defender lottery satisfying all the stronger inequalities. Both action sets are finite and nonempty, so the theorem applies.

For support reduction, associate to $S$ the following vector with $d_0=a\ell+m$ coordinates:

  • For each approval type $t$ and each $1\le j\le\ell$, the coordinate $\mathbf 1[|S\cap A_t|<j]$.
  • For each candidate $c$, the coordinate $r_c(S)$.

Every value $\widetilde g(\Delta,T)$ is a linear function of the expected vector.

Any convex combination of these vectors can be reduced to at most $d_0+1$ vectors. Indeed, if more coefficients are positive, the augmented vectors $(1,\Phi(S))$ are linearly dependent. Thus there are coefficients $\gamma_S$, of both signs, satisfying
$$ \sum_S\gamma_S=0, \qquad \sum_S\gamma_S\Phi(S)=0. $$
Replacing probabilities $p_S$ by $p_S-\theta\gamma_S$, where
$$ \theta=\min_{\gamma_S>0}\frac{p_S}{\gamma_S}, $$
preserves their sum and expected vector, preserves nonnegativity, and removes at least one positive coefficient. Iteration proves the support bound.

Fix the resulting $R\le d_0+1$ committees. The polytope of probabilities on them satisfying the stronger inequalities is nonempty, compact, and rational. Choose a vertex.

Write $\mu_t=u_t/v_t$ and put $Q=\prod_tv_t$, so $\log Q\le L$. Multiplying each deviation constraint by $KQ$ gives integer coefficients and right-hand sides bounded in absolute value by $KQ\ell$. At the vertex, $R$ independent active constraints determine the probabilities. Cramer's rule bounds their numerators and denominators by
$$ R!(KQ\ell)^R. $$
Thus each probability has bit length
$$ O\!\left(R(L+\log K+\log\ell+\log R)\right). $$

Finally, choose a power of two $N\ge RK/\epsilon$. Round the probabilities to multiples of $1/N$: take their floors after multiplication by $N$, then increment enough distinct entries to make their sum $N$. Each coordinate changes by at most $1/N$, so total variation changes by at most $R/(2N)$.

Because $g(S,T)\in[0,1]$, every original capture expectation changes by at most this total variation distance. The rounded lottery $\widehat\Delta$ therefore satisfies
$$ g(\widehat\Delta,T) \le \frac{|T|}{K}+\frac{\epsilon}{2K} \le (1+\epsilon)\frac{|T|}{K}. $$
Rounding introduces no new committees. $\square$

This is an existence and encoding theorem; it does not construct the support in unrestricted polynomial time.

5. An attacker representation depending only on approval types

We next give an explicit polynomial-size representation when $a$ is fixed.

Order the candidates $c_1,\ldots,c_m$. Build a directed acyclic graph whose states are
$$ (i,s,z_1,\ldots,z_a), $$
where $i$ candidates have been processed, $s\le\ell$ have been selected, and
$$ z_t=|T\cap A_t| $$
among the processed candidates.

The initial state is $(0,0,0,\ldots,0)$. At candidate $c_{i+1}$:

  • Exclusion leaves $s$ and all counts unchanged.
  • Inclusion increases $s$ by one and increases $z_t$ by $\mathbf 1[c_{i+1}\in A_t]$.

At layer $m$, states with $1\le s\le\ell$ connect to a common sink. A source-to-sink path specifies exactly one permitted deviation, and every permitted deviation has such a path.

The graph has
$$ O\!\left((m+1)(\ell+1)^{a+1}\right) $$
vertices and edges. Let $d$ be its number of edges.

Let $P\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be its nonnegative unit source-to-sink flow polytope. Every flow decomposes into a probability distribution over paths: repeatedly extract a positive-flow path and subtract its minimum edge flow. Acyclicity ensures that this process accounts for the entire flow. Thus $P$ represents lotteries of deviations.

Every coordinate lies in $[0,1]$, so
$$ \|x-u\|_2^2\le d \qquad (x,u\in P). $$

For a fixed defender committee $S$, define an edge vector $w(S)$ as follows:

  • An inclusion edge for candidate $c$ receives weight $r_c(S)-1/K$.
  • An exclusion edge receives weight zero.
  • A terminal edge with count vector $z$ receives weight

$$ \sum_{t\in\mathcal A}\mu_t\mathbf 1[z_t>|S\cap A_t|]. $$

Every coordinate has absolute value at most one. A path representing $T$ has total weight $H(S,T)$. Consequently, for the deviation lottery represented by $x$,
$$ w(S)\cdot x=\mathbb E[H(S,T)]. $$

Both the candidate marginals needed by Proposition 3 and this expected payoff are computable directly from $x$ in polynomial time.

6. Polynomial-time computation for fixed approval-type count

Theorem 5

There is a randomized algorithm with running time
$$ \operatorname{poly}\!\left( m,\tau,L,K,\ell,1/\epsilon,(\ell+1)^{a+1} \right) $$
that outputs an explicitly listed dyadic lottery satisfying the requested inequalities with probability at least $5/6$.

Proof. Put
$$ \epsilon_0=\min\{\epsilon,1\}, \qquad \delta=\frac{\epsilon_0}{4K}. $$
Let $q$ be the smallest power of two satisfying $q\ge d/\delta$, and set
$$ N=q^2, \qquad \eta=\frac1q. $$
Start with any path flow $x_1\in P$.

Obtaining a defender response

At iteration $j$, apply Proposition 3 to the deviation lottery represented by $x_j$. Independently sample committees from its rounding construction until finding $S_j$ with
$$ w(S_j)\cdot x_j\le\delta. $$

To bound the success probability of a trial, write
$$ Z=w(S)\cdot x_j. $$
Proposition 3 gives $\mathbb E[Z]\le0$, while $Z\ge-1$, because capture is nonnegative and deviation cost is at most one. If $p=\Pr[Z\le\delta]$, then
$$ 0\ge\mathbb E[Z]\ge-p+\delta(1-p). $$
Hence
$$ p\ge\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}. $$
Thus polynomially many trials suffice.

After accepting $S_j$, set
$$ x_{j+1} = \operatorname{proj}_P\bigl(x_j+\eta w(S_j)\bigr), $$
where the projection minimizes squared Euclidean distance.

The regret calculation

Write $w_j=w(S_j)$. For every $u\in P$, the projection inequality gives
$$ \|x_{j+1}-u\|_2^2 \le \|x_j+\eta w_j-u\|_2^2. $$
For completeness, if $y$ is the projection of $z$, first-order optimality gives $(z-y)\cdot(u-y)\le0$ for every $u\in P$. Expanding squared distances proves the stated inequality.

Rearranging and summing over $j$ gives
$$ \sum_{j=1}^Nw_j\cdot(u-x_j) \le \frac{\|x_1-u\|_2^2}{2\eta} + \frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{j=1}^N\|w_j\|_2^2. $$
The bounds $\|x_1-u\|_2^2\le d$ and $\|w_j\|_2^2\le d$ imply
$$ \frac1N\sum_{j=1}^Nw_j\cdot(u-x_j) \le \frac dq \le\delta. $$
Every accepted response satisfies $w_j\cdot x_j\le\delta$, so
$$ \frac1N\sum_{j=1}^Nw_j\cdot u\le2\delta \qquad (u\in P). $$

Return the uniform lottery on $S_1,\ldots,S_N$. Taking $u$ to be the path flow of any permitted deviation $T$ yields
$$ \widetilde g(\Delta,T)-\frac{|T|}{K}\le2\delta. $$
Therefore
$$ g(\Delta,T) \le \frac{|T|}{K}+\frac{\epsilon_0}{2K} \le (1+\epsilon)\frac{|T|}{K}. $$
This conclusion holds simultaneously for all deviations. Since $N$ is a power of two, every output probability $1/N$ is dyadic. Repeated committees may be merged.

Rational arithmetic and projection complexity

Each projection is a rational convex quadratic program over the explicitly described nonempty compact polytope $P$, with positive definite Hessian $2I$. Polynomial-time rational convex quadratic programming therefore applies. See [Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189).

We must additionally rule out excessive bit growth over the iterations. Let $y$ be the projection of rational $z$. Choose independent active constraint rows $B$ at $y$, with right-hand side $b$. The flow constraints have entries in $\{0,1,-1\}$, and
$$ y=z-B^{\mathsf T}(BB^{\mathsf T})^{-1}(Bz-b). $$
Indeed, $y-z$ is orthogonal to the direction space of the minimal face containing $y$, and the displayed formula is the orthogonal projection onto its affine hull.

The integer determinant $\det(BB^{\mathsf T})$ has bit length $O(d\log(d+1))$ by Hadamard's inequality. Thus projection increases a common denominator's bit length by at most that amount. Adding $\eta w_j$ increases it by at most $O(L+\log q)$. Over $N$ iterations the bit lengths remain polynomial in $N,d,L,\log q$.

In the rounding construction, all current coordinates can use a common denominator. Pair updates add or subtract such coordinates and preserve that denominator. Each rounding trial consequently uses polynomial-bit arithmetic.

A bounded running time and success probability

Cap the number of trials per iteration at
$$ J= \left\lceil\frac{1+\delta}{\delta}\right\rceil \left\lceil\log_2(12N)\right\rceil. $$
For any iteration, conditional on its preceding history, the probability of accepting no trial is at most
$$ \left(1-\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\right)^J \le\frac1{12N}. $$
A union bound over iterations gives failure probability at most $1/12$.

Rational Bernoulli choices can be sampled by drawing uniform integers with binary rejection sampling. Each rejection attempt fails with probability less than $1/2$. There are at most $NJm$ such choices. Capping each sampler after
$$ B=\left\lceil\log_2(12NJm)\right\rceil $$
attempts gives additional failure probability at most $1/12$, by coupling with the uncapped exact samplers.

The bounded algorithm therefore succeeds with probability at least $5/6$. Its iteration count, trial count, arithmetic lengths, and projection costs are polynomial in the claimed parameters. $\square$

For fixed $a$, this proves the requested form of polynomial-time tractability even with arbitrarily many ranking types. It does not give uniform polynomial dependence on $a$.

7. Approval-only computation and the quota clarification

For completeness, the approval-only result in the notes extends to rational masses as follows.

Choose
$$ N_{\mathrm v}=\left\lceil\frac{\tau K}{\epsilon}\right\rceil. $$
Round the masses to $\nu_t=n_t/N_{\mathrm v}$, with nonnegative integers $n_t$ summing to $N_{\mathrm v}$, using floors followed by increments. Then
$$ \frac12\sum_t|\mu_t-\nu_t| \le\frac{\tau}{2N_{\mathrm v}} \le\frac{\epsilon}{2K}. $$
For every committee and deviation, capture mass changes by at most this total variation distance. To justify this, the mass difference has sum zero, and the captured-type indicator lies in $[0,1]$; its inner product with the difference is bounded by the total positive mass of that difference.

Construct the election containing $n_t$ copies of approval type $t$. [Becker, Greger, and Peters, Theorem 7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11912v1) computes a core+ committee in polynomial time using quota
$$ q_{\mathrm B} = \frac12\left(\frac{N_{\mathrm v}}K+ \frac{N_{\mathrm v}}{K+1}\right). $$
Core+ excludes, in particular, integral blocking coalitions at this quota. Since $q_{\mathrm B}<N_{\mathrm v}/K$, it also excludes every blocking coalition at the Hare quota $N_{\mathrm v}/K$.

Thus its returned committee $S$ satisfies
$$ g_\nu(S,T)<\frac{|T|}{K} $$
for every nonempty permitted deviation. Returning $S$ with probability one gives
$$ g_\mu(S,T) < \frac{|T|}{K}+\frac{\epsilon}{2K} \le (1+\epsilon)\frac{|T|}{K}. $$
The replicated election has polynomial size in $\tau,K,1/\epsilon$, and the rounding arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity. This establishes deterministic polynomial-time search for approval-only instances.

8. What remains open

The fixed-approval-type algorithm still uses a graph of size exponential in the variable parameter $a$. The approval-only algorithm handles unrestricted $a$, but its guarantee does not supply the responses needed for mixed profiles.

Accordingly, the unrestricted target requires either:

  • An algorithm eliminating this exponential dependence for arbitrary mixed support; or
  • A search reduction proving hardness of computing any valid lottery.

No such unrestricted algorithm or search reduction is established here.
