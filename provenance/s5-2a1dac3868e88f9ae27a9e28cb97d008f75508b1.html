The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), \(m\) items, rational nonnegative intrinsic values \(b^i_t\) and block interaction weights \(K^i_{tu}\), and nonnegative-coefficient polynomial utilities \(f^i_t\) of degree less than \(d\), can a randomized algorithm running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,d\) return feasible type fractions \(z_{ti}\) with \(\mathbb{E}[W(z)]\geq \mathrm{OPT}/d\), where \(\sum_i z_{ti}\leq 1\) and \(W(z)=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_i z_{ti}f^i_t\!\left(b^i_t+\sum_u\mu_uK^i_{tu}z_{ui}\right)\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) items with unlimited copies. Input data comprise binary-rational intrinsic values \(b_t^i\geq0\), directed block interaction weights \(K_{tu}^i\geq0\) (including within-type interactions), and explicitly listed polynomials \(f_t^i:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) with nonnegative binary-rational coefficients, \(f_t^i(0)=0\), and degree less than the positive integer \(d\); zero polynomials are allowed. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
A feasible allocation consists of real fractions \(z_{ti}\geq0\) with \(\sum_i z_{ti}\leq1\): fraction \(z_{ti}\) of type \(t\) receives item \(i\), and unassigned mass has zero utility. Intrinsic value is independent of the assigned fraction; \(K_{tu}^i\) measures the influence on type \(t\) per unit mass of type \(u\) receiving the same item.
Define \(W(z)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\sum_{i=1}^m z_{ti}f_t^i\!\left(b_t^i+\sum_{u\in T}\mu_uK_{tu}^iz_{ui}\right)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max\{W(z):z\text{ is feasible}\}\).

Does a randomized algorithm running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,d\) return feasible rational fractions \(z\) satisfying \(\mathbb{E}[W(z)]\geq\mathrm{OPT}/d\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee computationally hard?

For finite agents with at most one item each, nonnegative directed influences and nonnegative-coefficient utility polynomials of degree less than \(d\), a polynomial-time randomized \(d\)-approximation is known ([Etesami](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.08915v4)).
A related nonatomic complete-graph model admits a constant-factor revenue approximation via sequential posted pricing under additional distributional and behavioral assumptions ([Bhalgat–Gollapudi–Munagala](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~fiat/cgt12/EC_2012/docs/p179.pdf)); this concerns a different objective and model. No result settling the precise mass welfare problem above was identified in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize positive support masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a probability distribution.
  • Require nonnegative interaction weights, including within-type blocks, and permit asymmetric directed influences, matching the anchor's positive-externality setting.
  • Restore the discrete model's condition that every utility vanishes at zero; specify its domain as the nonnegative reals.
  • Specify polynomial coefficients as explicitly listed binary rationals and define L as total binary input length.
  • Take m and d to be positive integers, allow zero polynomials, require nonnegative allocation fractions, and define OPT over all feasible real fractions.
  • Retain unlimited item copies and zero utility for unassigned mass; distinguish intrinsic value from within-type interaction.
  • Require rational output fractions so the computational output has a finite binary representation.

Result

Yes. In fact, a deterministic algorithm running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,d\) returns an allocation \(z\in\{0,1\}^{\tau\times m}\) satisfying \(W(z)\geq\mathrm{OPT}/d\).

The key structural fact is that splitting a type is unnecessary at an optimum. This permits an exact reduction to the finite-agent problem. Below, I prove that fact and give a polynomial-time relaxation and rounding argument, including the binary-encoding details.

If \(d=1\), every permitted polynomial is zero, so the zero allocation settles the problem. Henceforth assume \(d\geq2\).

1. An optimum assigns each type wholly to one item

Write \(P=\{z\geq0:\sum_i z_{ti}\leq1\text{ for every }t\}\). This set is compact, and \(W\) is continuous, so a maximizer exists.

Fix a type \(t\) and fix all allocation rows other than its row. Denote its variable row by \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\). Because interactions occur only between recipients of the same item, the resulting objective has the form

\( \displaystyle W(x,z_{-t})=\sum_{i=1}^m q_i(x_i), \)

where every \(q_i\) is a univariate polynomial with nonnegative coefficients. Indeed, each affected utility argument is a nonnegative constant plus a nonnegative multiple of \(x_i\). Composing with a nonnegative-coefficient polynomial preserves nonnegative coefficients; the recipient factor is either \(x_i\) or a fixed nonnegative number.

Consequently, \(q_i''(s)\geq0\) for \(s\geq0\). Thus \(W(x,z_{-t})\) is convex in the entire row \(x\).

Every feasible row is the convex combination

\( \displaystyle x=\left(1-\sum_i x_i\right)0+\sum_i x_i e_i. \)

Convexity therefore gives

\( \displaystyle W(x,z_{-t})\leq \left(1-\sum_i x_i\right)W(0,z_{-t}) +\sum_i x_i W(e_i,z_{-t}) \leq\max_{v\in\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_m\}}W(v,z_{-t}). \)

Starting from a global maximizer, replace one row at a time by a maximizing vertex. Each replacement preserves feasibility and cannot decrease welfare. After \(\tau\) replacements, all rows are vertices. Hence

\( \displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}=\max_{z\in P\cap\{0,1\}^{\tau\times m}}W(z). \)

This argument uses convexity separately in each row; it does not require joint convexity of \(W\).

2. Exact finite-agent representation

For each item \(i\) and subset \(S\subseteq T\), define

\( \displaystyle F_i(S)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t f_t^i\!\left(b_t^i+\sum_{u\in S}\mu_uK_{tu}^i\right). \)

An integral allocation corresponds to disjoint sets \(S_1,\ldots,S_m\), with welfare \(\sum_i F_i(S_i)\).

Equivalently, create one finite agent for each supported type and set

\( \displaystyle a_{tu}^i= \begin{cases} \mu_uK_{tu}^i,&u\neq t,\\ b_t^i+\mu_tK_{tt}^i,&u=t, \end{cases} \qquad g_t^i(s)=\mu_t f_t^i(s). \)

For a receiving agent \(t\in S_i\), its finite-agent utility is exactly its contribution to \(F_i(S_i)\). All weights and polynomial coefficients remain nonnegative, and polynomial degrees remain less than \(d\). Thus the hypotheses of [Etesami’s finite-agent approximation theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.08915v4#S5.SS1) hold.

The remaining sections establish an explicit deterministic implementation with polynomial dependence on \(d\) and the binary input length.

3. A concave upper-bound relaxation

For each item, consider the polynomial

\( \displaystyle Q_i(y)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t y_t f_t^i\!\left(b_t^i+\sum_{u\in T}\mu_uK_{tu}^i y_u\right). \)

Its coefficients are nonnegative, its constant term is zero, and its total degree is at most \(d\).

On binary vectors, replace every positive power of a variable by that variable and collect equal monomials. This gives coefficients \(c_{i,S}\geq0\) such that

\( \displaystyle F_i(A)=\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\neq S\subseteq A\\|S|\leq d}}c_{i,S}. \)

This expansion is used only in the proof; the algorithm never constructs it.

Define

\( \displaystyle H_i(x_i)= \sum_{\substack{\varnothing\neq S\subseteq T\\|S|\leq d}} c_{i,S}\min_{t\in S}x_{ti}, \qquad H(x)=\sum_i H_i(x_i). \)

Each minimum is concave, so \(H\) is concave. At binary vectors, \(H\) equals welfare. Therefore the relaxation

\( \displaystyle R=\max_{x\in P}H(x) \)

satisfies \(R\geq\mathrm{OPT}\).

It remains to show that this relaxation is exactly solvable without enumerating its monomials.

Fix a permutation \(\pi\) of \(T\), and put \(A_j=\{\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(j)\}\), with \(A_0=\varnothing\). Define the linear form

\( \displaystyle \ell_{i,\pi}(x_i)= \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}x_{\pi(j),i}\bigl(F_i(A_j)-F_i(A_{j-1})\bigr). \)

For each monomial indexed by \(S\), this linear form uses the coordinate belonging to the last member of \(S\) in the permutation. That coordinate is at least \(\min_{t\in S}x_{ti}\). Hence \(\ell_{i,\pi}(x_i)\geq H_i(x_i)\).

If \(\pi\) sorts the coordinates in nonincreasing order, that last coordinate is a minimum on \(S\), giving equality. Thus

\( \displaystyle H_i(x_i)=\min_\pi\ell_{i,\pi}(x_i). \)

Consequently, the relaxation is the rational linear program

\( \displaystyle \max\sum_i v_i \quad\text{subject to}\quad x\in P,\quad 0\leq v_i\leq F_i(T),\quad v_i\leq\ell_{i,\pi}(x_i)\ \text{for every }i,\pi. \)

A separation oracle first checks the explicit constraints. For each item, it then sorts \(x_i\), evaluates the corresponding chain linear form, and either returns that violated inequality or certifies all permutation inequalities for that item.

The standard strong separation–optimization theorem for well-described rational polyhedra provides exact polynomial-time linear optimization from such an oracle. Here the dimension, constraint encoding lengths, and oracle running time are polynomially bounded; the polytope is nonempty and bounded. Thus it returns an optimal rational \(x\). See [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Chapter 6](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4).

4. Deterministic rounding

Fix an optimal relaxation solution \(x\). For \(U\subseteq T\), let \(x^U\) retain the rows in \(U\) and set all other rows to zero.

Suppose \(U\) is the set of types not yet assigned. For an item \(i\) and threshold \(\theta\in(0,1]\), let

\( \displaystyle A_i(\theta)=\{t\in U:x_{ti}\geq\theta\}. \)

For a possible batch \(A=A_i(\theta)\), define its potential loss by

\( \displaystyle \Delta(A)=H(x^U)-H(x^{U\setminus A}). \)

I claim that, whenever some retained coordinate is positive, a nonempty threshold batch satisfies

\( \displaystyle F_i(A)\geq\frac{\Delta(A)}d. \)

To prove this, choose \(I\) uniformly among the items and \(\theta\) uniformly in \((0,1]\), solely for the analysis. The probability that \(S\subseteq A_i(\theta)\) is \(\min_{t\in S}x_{ti}\). Therefore

\( \displaystyle \mathbb{E}[F_I(A_I(\theta))]=\frac{H(x^U)}m. \)

A monomial indexed by \(S\subseteq U\) disappears from the potential exactly when the chosen batch intersects \(S\). This event has probability

\( \displaystyle \frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\max_{t\in S}x_{tj} \leq\frac1m\sum_{t\in S}\sum_{j=1}^m x_{tj} \leq\frac{|S|}m \leq\frac dm. \)

All monomial contributions are nonnegative. Summing their expected losses yields

\( \displaystyle \mathbb{E}[\Delta(A_I(\theta))] \leq\frac d m H(x^U). \)

Combining the two inequalities gives

\( \displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[F_I(A_I(\theta)) -\frac{\Delta(A_I(\theta))}d\right]\geq0. \)

Empty batches contribute zero. If some retained coordinate is positive, nonempty batches have positive probability. Consequently, at least one nonempty batch of positive probability satisfies the claimed inequality.

For each item, there are at most \(\tau\) distinct nonempty threshold sets, obtained by taking thresholds equal to its distinct positive retained coordinates. Enumerate these sets and choose one satisfying the inequality. Assign its types to that item and remove them from \(U\).

If all retained coordinates are zero, then \(H(x^U)=0\); leave the remaining types unassigned and terminate. Otherwise, each iteration removes at least one type, so there are at most \(\tau\) iterations.

Let the chosen batches be \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\), assigned respectively to items \(i_1,\ldots,i_k\). Their potential losses telescope, giving

\( \displaystyle \sum_{r=1}^k F_{i_r}(A_r) \geq\frac{H(x)}d=\frac Rd. \)

For each item, its batches are disjoint. The nonnegative monomial representation implies

\( \displaystyle F_i\!\left(\bigcup_{r:i_r=i}A_r\right) \geq\sum_{r:i_r=i}F_i(A_r): \)

every monomial counted on the right is counted on the left, and no nonempty monomial is counted in two disjoint batches.

Thus the final allocation satisfies

\( \displaystyle W(z)\geq\sum_{r=1}^kF_{i_r}(A_r) \geq\frac Rd\geq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}d. \)

5. Binary running time and output

Each value \(F_i(S)\) is evaluated directly from the input formula. Rational sums and products have polynomial encoding length, and raising a rational argument to an exponent less than \(d\) produces encoding length polynomial in \(L,\tau,d\). Evaluating all explicitly listed polynomial terms therefore takes polynomial time.

The separation oracle uses sorting and at most \(\tau\) such evaluations per item. Every chain inequality has polynomial encoding length, so exact rational linear optimization has polynomial bit complexity and returns \(x\) with polynomial encoding length.

During rounding, each of at most \(\tau\) iterations examines at most \(m\tau\) batches. Their rewards and potential losses are exactly computable using the same chain evaluations. No exponential monomial expansion is performed.

Finally, the output consists entirely of zeroes and ones. It is therefore a feasible rational allocation of polynomial encoding length. The deterministic guarantee immediately implies the requested expected-welfare guarantee.
