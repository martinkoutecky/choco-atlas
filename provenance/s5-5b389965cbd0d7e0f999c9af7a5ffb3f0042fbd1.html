1. Measure-preserving coalition calculations

Write $\mu$ for Lebesgue measure. I use measurable finite coalition membership in the standard sense that the members of a coalition, listed in increasing arrival order, give measurable member maps.

For an admissible assignment $\pi$ and a bounded measurable function $f$, define
$$ P_\pi f(s)=\frac1{|\pi(s)|}\sum_{r\in\pi(s)}f(r). $$

Averaging lemma.
$$ \int P_\pi f\,d\mu=\int f\,d\mu. $$
The same assertion holds on an arrival prefix.

Proof. Let $S_k$ consist of agents in size-$k$ coalitions, and let $R_{k,i}$ consist of their $i$th members. Put $B_k=R_{k,1}$. The map
$$ \phi_{k,i}:B_k\longrightarrow R_{k,i} $$
taking the first member to the $i$th member is a measurable within-coalition partial bijection. By the prescribed mass-preservation condition, it preserves measure. Consequently,
$$ \int_{S_k}f\,d\mu = \sum_{i=1}^k\int_{B_k}f(\phi_{k,i}(x))\,d\mu(x). $$
Since $P_\pi f$ is constant on each coalition,
$$ \int_{S_k}P_\pi f\,d\mu = k\int_{B_k}\frac1k \sum_{i=1}^k f(\phi_{k,i}(x))\,d\mu(x) = \int_{S_k}f\,d\mu. $$
Summing over $k$ proves the result. Boundedness of $f$ and finiteness of the assigned measure justify the summation. $\square$

For a finite coalition $C$, define its total utility by
$$ \mathrm{SW}(C) = \frac2{|C|} \sum_{\{s,t\}\subseteq C}w(\theta(s),\theta(t)). $$
Symmetry of $w$ gives this expression. Applying the averaging lemma to individual utility yields
$$ W(\pi) = \int\frac{\mathrm{SW}(\pi(s))}{|\pi(s)|}\,d\mu(s). $$
In particular, coalitions with nonpositive total utility have nonpositive contributions to this integral.

If
$$ B=\max_{a,b\in T}|w(a,b)|, $$
every individual utility has absolute value at most $B$, so all these integrals are well defined.

Equal-measure role constructions

We will also use the following standard measure-space fact, with the equal-measure qualification required by the verifier:

If measurable subsets $E,F\subseteq[0,1]$ have the same positive finite Lebesgue measure, there are measurable, measure-preserving bijections between conull subsets of $E$ and $F$.

One way to obtain these isomorphisms is through the cumulative functions
$$ G_E(t)=\mu(E\cap[0,t]) $$
and their measurable generalized inverses. Restricted to $E$, the cumulative function sends Lebesgue measure to Lebesgue measure on $[0,\mu(E)]$. Its generalized inverse is inverse to it almost everywhere; nontrivial constant intervals account only for null exceptions.

Thus finitely or countably many role sets of equal measure can be identified to form coalitions. Put all exceptional null agents into singletons. Any measurable within-coalition partial bijection then decomposes into countably many restrictions of the role identifications and their compositions, together with singleton identities. Each restriction preserves measure, and countable additivity proves the required mass preservation.

We first determine the first two observed phases, then introduce a hidden subdivision of one prefix type. Because that subdivision changes no previously observed valuation, causality makes this a fixed-instance adversarial construction.

2.1. Coloring a zero-valuation prefix

Color the prefix agents with temporary types $a,b$, each of mass $q/2$, so that every nonsingleton coalition of $\pi_q$ contains numbers $u,v$ of these types satisfying
$$ u\le2v. $$

Here is an explicit measurable construction. For even coalition size $k$, color the first $k/2$ members $a$ and the others $b$.

For odd $k$, split the first-member set $B_k$ into two measurable subsets of equal measure. In coalitions represented by the first subset, color the first $(k+1)/2$ members $a$. In the other coalitions, color the first $(k-1)/2$ members $a$. All remaining members are $b$.

The measure-preserving member maps show that precisely half of each size stratum has type $a$. Therefore both types have mass $q/2$. For $k\ge2$,
$$ \left\lceil\frac k2\right\rceil \le 2\left\lfloor\frac k2\right\rfloor, $$
which proves the required coalitionwise inequality. The construction also covers singleton coalitions and is measurable.

All valuations among $a,b$ agents will remain zero. Hence this coloring does not change the policy’s observed prefix history or its assignment $\pi_q$.

2.2. The second phase creates only one kind of positive coalition

Every second-phase arrival has type $z$. Set
$$ w(a,z)=1, \qquad w(b,z)=-2, \qquad w(z,z)=-2. $$

We use the following consequence of the allowed operations.

Old-agent invariant. Relative to any fixed time $t$, the old-agent subset of a later coalition is either empty, a singleton, or an entire coalition present at time $t$.

Indeed, an arrival cannot join two old agents from different coalitions. Dissolution separates all members of the affected coalition, and two separated old agents cannot subsequently reunite: neither arrives again. The prohibition on regrouping at accumulation times or at the terminal time ensures that such reunification cannot occur outside an allowed arrival operation.

Apply this invariant at time $q$. If a coalition contains an intact nonsingleton prefix coalition with $u$ agents of type $a$, $v$ of type $b$, and $h$ agents of type $z$, its unordered-edge valuation sum is
$$ h(u-2v)-h(h-1)\le0. $$
Its total utility is therefore nonpositive.

If its old-agent subset is one $a$ agent, its unordered-edge sum is
$$ h-h(h-1)=h(2-h). $$
For an integer $h\ge0$, this is positive exactly when $h=1$.

A coalition with one old $b$ agent has nonpositive total utility, as does a coalition consisting entirely of $z$ agents. Coalitions without $z$ agents have zero total utility.

Thus, at time $t_*$, the only positive-total-utility coalitions are $a,z$ pairs. Let $D_a$ be their $a$ endpoints, and put
$$ H=\mu(D_a). $$
The partner map preserves measure, so
$$ 0\le H\le p. $$

$$ 0\le h\le H, \qquad h\ge H-\delta p. $$

They are binary rationals and sum to $1$. Moreover,
$$ \frac q2-h\ge\frac12-2p>0. $$
If $h=0$, type $a^+$ is omitted from the positive-mass support.

The path is measurable. The subdivision into $a^+,a^-$ changes no observation before time $t_*$, so determinism and causality reproduce exactly the assignments already used to define $D_a$ and $E$.

2.4. Third-phase positive pairs can only replace earlier positive pairs

Apply the old-agent invariant at time $t_*$.

First consider an intact old coalition that was not a positive $a,z$ pair. It contains no member of $E$, because every member of $E$ belonged to such a pair. Its total utility was nonpositive, and all valuations from its members to new $v$ agents are $-2$. Adding $v$ agents preserves nonpositivity.

Next consider an intact positive pair.

If its $a$ endpoint belongs to $E$, adding $j\ge1$ agents of type $v$ gives unordered-edge sum
$$ 1+j(1-2)-j(j-1)=1-j^2\le0. $$
If its $a$ endpoint does not belong to $E$, that sum is
$$ 1-4j-j(j-1)=1-3j-j^2\le0. $$

If the old-agent subset of a terminal coalition is a singleton, it can have positive total utility only when that old agent belongs to $E$. With $j$ new agents, the unordered-edge sum is then
$$ j-j(j-1)=j(2-j), $$
which is positive exactly when $j=1$.

Coalitions containing only new agents have nonpositive total utility. Therefore the only positive terminal coalitions are:

  • Original $a,z$ pairs from time $t_*$ that survive intact.
  • Pairs consisting of one agent in $E$ and one agent of type $v$.

Charge every such pair to its $a$ endpoint. Every charged endpoint belongs to $D_a$, and no endpoint can be charged twice because terminal coalitions are disjoint.

Each positive pair has valuation $1$. Measure preservation gives equal mass to its two endpoint sets, so its welfare contribution equals the mass of its charged endpoint set. All other coalitions have nonpositive contributions by the averaging lemma. Consequently,
$$ W(A)\le H. $$

2.5. An offline assignment obtains the additional welfare

The mass of $a^-$ agents is sufficient to give each $z$ agent $\ell$ partners:
$$ \ell p=\frac12-2p \le \frac q2-h. $$
Divide a subset of the $a^-$ cohort into $\ell$ role sets of measure $p$, and identify them measure-preservingly with the $z$ cohort. Each resulting coalition has one $z$ agent and $\ell$ agents of type $a^-$.

Its total utility is
$$ \frac{2\ell}{\ell+1}. $$
The welfare of all these stars is therefore
$$ D=\frac{2p\ell}{\ell+1}. $$

Separately, pair all agents in $E$ with a subset of the $v$ cohort of measure $h$. This is possible because $h\le p$, and contributes welfare $h$. All remaining agents are singletons. The equal-measure role construction proves admissibility.

Thus
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\ge D+h>0. $$
Since $h\ge H-\delta p$,
$$ \frac{W(A)}{\mathrm{OPT}} \le \frac{H}{D+h} \le \frac{H}{D+H-\delta p}. $$
Here $D-\delta p>0$, because $\ell\ge2$ and $\delta<1/2$. Hence the last expression is increasing in $H$. Using $H\le p$ gives
$$ \frac{W(A)}{\mathrm{OPT}} \le \frac{p}{D+p-\delta p} = \frac1{\,2\ell/(\ell+1)+1-\delta\,} <c. $$
This proves the theorem. $\square$

3. Exact offline configuration formulation

The following formulation also establishes the basis for the bounded-size approximation theorem.

Let $S$ be the positive-mass support, with $|S|=\tau$. A configuration is a vector
$$ \mathbf n=(n_a)_{a\in S} $$
of nonnegative integers with
$$ k(\mathbf n)=\sum_{a\in S}n_a\ge1. $$
Its total utility is
$$ F(\mathbf n) = \frac{ \mathbf n^{\mathsf T}w\mathbf n - \sum_{a\in S}n_aw(a,a) }{k(\mathbf n)}. $$
The subtracted term removes self-valuations while retaining valuations between distinct agents of the same type.

Configuration proposition.
$$ \mathrm{OPT} = \sup_{\lambda} \sum_{\mathbf n}\lambda_{\mathbf n}F(\mathbf n), $$
subject to
$$ \lambda_{\mathbf n}\ge0, \qquad \sum_{\mathbf n}n_a\lambda_{\mathbf n}=\mu_a \quad(a\in S). $$

Proof. Given an admissible assignment, let $S_{\mathbf n}$ be its configuration stratum and set
$$ \lambda_{\mathbf n} = \frac{\mu(S_{\mathbf n})}{k(\mathbf n)}. $$
Coalition averaging applied to the type-$a$ indicator gives
$$ \mu(S_{\mathbf n}\cap\theta^{-1}(a)) = n_a\lambda_{\mathbf n}. $$
Summing proves the resource constraints. Coalition averaging also shows that the welfare contribution of this stratum is
$$ \lambda_{\mathbf n}F(\mathbf n). $$

Conversely, for feasible $\lambda$, divide each type cohort into the countably many required role sets. Configuration $\mathbf n$ requires $n_a$ sets of type $a$, each of measure $\lambda_{\mathbf n}$. The resource equalities give exactly the available mass. Identify the equal-measure role sets for each configuration and assign null exceptions to singletons. Section 1 proves admissibility, and the resulting welfare is the displayed objective.

Finally,
$$ |F(\mathbf n)|\le Bk(\mathbf n), \qquad \sum_{\mathbf n}k(\mathbf n)\lambda_{\mathbf n}=1, $$
so all objective sums converge absolutely. $\square$

In particular, the offline supremum depends only on masses and valuations, not on the arrival path.

4. A sharp approximation by bounded coalition sizes

Let $\mathrm{OPT}_K$ be the maximum welfare over admissible assignments with coalition sizes at most $K$.

By Section 3, it is exactly the finite linear program using configurations with
$$ 1\le k(\mathbf n)\le K. $$
There are
$$ \binom{\tau+K}{K}-1 $$
such configurations. Its feasible region is nonempty, closed, and bounded, so its maximum is attained.

Bounded-size theorem. For every integer $K\ge2$,
$$ \left(1-\frac1K\right)\mathrm{OPT} \le \mathrm{OPT}_K \le \mathrm{OPT}. $$

Proof. Start with any feasible configuration allocation. Replace every configuration with $F(\mathbf n)<0$ by singleton configurations using the same type resources. This does not decrease welfare. We may therefore assume that all configurations in use have nonnegative total utility.

Keep configurations of size at most $K$ unchanged. For a configuration $\mathbf n$ of size $k>K$, choose uniformly a $K$-element subset of its $k$ labeled roles. Let $\mathbf t$ be the sampled configuration. Its distribution is
$$ P_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf t) = \frac{\prod_{a\in S}\binom{n_a}{t_a}}{\binom{k}{K}}, \qquad \sum_a t_a=K. $$
Replace an amount $\lambda_{\mathbf n}$ of the original configuration by amounts
$$ \lambda_{\mathbf n}\frac{k}{K}P_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf t) $$
of the sampled configurations.

For each type $a$,
$$ \mathbb E[t_a]=\frac Kk n_a. $$
Hence this replacement uses precisely the original resources:
$$ \lambda_{\mathbf n}\frac{k}{K}\mathbb E[t_a] = \lambda_{\mathbf n}n_a. $$

Every unordered pair of original roles is included in the sample with probability
$$ \frac{K(K-1)}{k(k-1)}. $$
Writing $E_{\mathbf n}$ for the original unordered-edge valuation sum, we obtain
$$ \mathbb E[F(\mathbf t)] = \frac2K\frac{K(K-1)}{k(k-1)}E_{\mathbf n} = \frac{K-1}{k-1}F(\mathbf n). $$
Thus the replacement welfare equals
$$ \lambda_{\mathbf n} \frac{k(K-1)}{K(k-1)}F(\mathbf n). $$
Because $k>K$,
$$ \frac{k(K-1)}{K(k-1)} \ge 1-\frac1K. $$
All retained configuration values are nonnegative, so the final allocation has welfare at least $1-1/K$ times the welfare before replacement, and hence at least that factor times the original welfare.

The countable replacements preserve resources, their objectives converge absolutely, and Section 3 realizes the resulting allocation admissibly. Taking the supremum over the original allocations proves the lower bound. The upper bound follows by inclusion of feasible assignments. $\square$

The factor is tight. With one type of mass $1$ and valuation $w(a,a)=1$, size-$k$ coalitions give every agent utility $1-1/k$. Therefore
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=1, \qquad \mathrm{OPT}_K=1-\frac1K. $$
For $K=2$, this recovers the tight offline matching factor of $1/2$.
