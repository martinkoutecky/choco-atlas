Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal P\) of \(\tau\) bilateral types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_p\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type comprises two nonnegative rational utility vectors \(u_i^p\) satisfying \(\sum_{j=1}^m u_{ij}^p=1\), with \(m\geq2\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; measure running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Each cell has its own unit supply of each divisible item. Utilities are linear, payments are forbidden, and unallocated fractions are allowed.
A single rule \(A\) maps pairs of reports in \(\Delta_m=\{v\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m:\sum_jv_j=1\}\) to allocations satisfying \(A_{ij}\geq0\) and \(A_{1j}+A_{2j}\leq1\). Strategyproofness is dominant-strategy truthfulness throughout \(\Delta_m\), including reports outside the support; the society is held fixed during deviations.
Output a description of such a rule and its allocations at every supported type. Define \(W(A)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\mu_p\sum_{i=1}^2\sum_{j=1}^m u_{ij}^pA_{ij}(u_1^p,u_2^p)\) and \(W^*=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\mu_p\sum_{j=1}^m\max\{u_{1j}^p,u_{2j}^p\}\).

Is the approximation guarantee \(W(A)\geq0.67776W^*\) achievable by a strategyproof rule for every input society, or is it impossible?

Conclusion and construction

The guarantee is achievable for every input society. We use the pointwise guarantee supplied in the task; the passage to a society is an exact weighted-sum argument.

Here is an explicit description of the rule. For reports \(b_1,b_2\in\Delta_m\), write \(b_i(x_i)=\sum_j b_{ij}x_{ij}\). For each \(c>0\), choose a feasible allocation \(a^c\) maximizing
\(b_1(a_1^c)b_2(a_2^c)^c\), with deterministic tie-breaking, and define
\[ P^c_{1j}(b_1,b_2)=b_2(a_2^c)^c a_{1j}^c,\qquad P^c_{2j}(b_1,b_2)=b_1(a_1^c)^{1/c}a_{2j}^c. \]
Let \(E_{ij}=1/2\), and let \(Q\) choose whichever of \(P^1\) and \(E\) has greater reported welfare, choosing \(P^1\) on a tie. Set
\[ A=\frac{1029}{4000}P^{421/1000} +\frac{1029}{4000}P^{1000/421} +\frac{971}{2000}Q. \]
This is precisely the mechanism in [Cheung’s Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1604.05243#S7), which establishes strategyproofness and a pointwise competitive ratio of at least \(0.67776\).

For each supported type \(p\), output the allocation with coordinates \(A_{ij}(u_1^p,u_2^p)\), evaluated by these formulas. The same formula defines the rule on all reports outside the support.

Feasibility and global strategyproofness

These properties also have direct proofs.

The feasible allocation set is compact. The product maximized in the definition of \(P^c\) is continuous, so a maximizer exists. Giving each agent half of every item yields product \(2^{-(1+c)}>0\); hence both utilities at a maximizer are positive. Normalization and feasibility imply \(b_i(a_i^c)\leq1\). Consequently each scaling factor belongs to \((0,1]\), so \(P^c\) is feasible. Both alternatives defining \(Q\) are feasible, and the coefficients defining \(A\) are nonnegative and sum to \(1\). Thus \(A\) is feasible and requires no payments.

Fix agent \(1\)’s true utility \(u_1\) and an arbitrary report \(b_2\). Under truthful reporting, her utility from \(P^c\) equals
\[ Z=\max_{a\text{ feasible}}u_1(a_1)b_2(a_2)^c. \]
If a deviation selects some feasible allocation \(a\), her actual utility is \(u_1(a_1)b_2(a_2)^c\leq Z\). Thus agent \(1\) cannot benefit from deviating.

For agent \(2\), with an arbitrary fixed report \(b_1\), truthful reporting maximizes
\[ b_1(a_1)^{1/c}u_2(a_2) =\bigl(b_1(a_1)u_2(a_2)^c\bigr)^{1/c}. \]
Since taking the positive \(c\)-th root preserves order, the same argument proves truthfulness for agent \(2\).

For \(P^1\), both agents’ reported utilities equal
\(Z(b_1,b_2)=\max_a b_1(a_1)b_2(a_2)\).
Its reported welfare is therefore \(2Z(b_1,b_2)\), whereas \(E\) has welfare \(1\). Fixing either agent’s true utility and the other report, let \(Z_0\) be that agent’s truthful utility under \(P^1\). Her truthful utility under \(Q\) is \(\max\{Z_0,1/2\}\). A deviation that selects \(P^1\) gives utility at most \(Z_0\); one that selects \(E\) gives exactly \(1/2\). Hence \(Q\) is strategyproof.

Finally, utilities under \(A\) are the corresponding weighted sums. Multiplying each component’s truthfulness inequality by its nonnegative coefficient and adding proves truthfulness of \(A\).

All these arguments allow arbitrary true utilities and opposing reports in \(\Delta_m\). In particular, they cover deviations outside the support while the society remains fixed.

The society guarantee

We apply Cheung’s verified pointwise theorem, as permitted by the given premises. Its hypotheses match the task: two agents, any \(m\geq2\), nonnegative normalized linear utilities, unit item supplies, and permission to leave fractions unallocated.

Define
\[ S_p=\sum_{i=1}^2\sum_{j=1}^m u_{ij}^pA_{ij}(u_1^p,u_2^p), \qquad O_p=\sum_{j=1}^m\max\{u_{1j}^p,u_{2j}^p\}. \]
The theorem gives \(S_p\geq0.67776\,O_p\) for every supported type. Since \(\mu_p>0\), multiplication by \(\mu_p\) preserves each inequality. Summing yields
\[ W(A)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\mu_pS_p \geq0.67776\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\mu_pO_p =0.67776\,W^*. \]
The rule is independent of the masses and support, so no additional distributional incentive argument is needed.

Exact computation on the support

For completeness, the supported allocations admit exact descriptions computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Consider one product maximization with rational reports. Because utilities are nonnegative, some maximizer allocates every item completely. Write agent \(1\)’s share as \(t_j\in[0,1]\), and put
\(x=\sum_jb_{1j}t_j\) and \(y=\sum_jb_{2j}(1-t_j)\).
At a maximizer, \(x,y>0\), so maximizing the product is equivalent to maximizing \(\log x+c\log y\). Its derivative with respect to \(t_j\) is
\[ \frac{b_{1j}}x-\frac{cb_{2j}}y. \]
A positive derivative forces \(t_j=1\), and a negative derivative forces \(t_j=0\), since otherwise a sufficiently small feasible movement would increase the objective.

Order items by decreasing \(b_{1j}/b_{2j}\), putting positive-over-zero ratios first and items valued by neither agent last. Only items at the threshold ratio \(cx/y\) can be split. Within a tied threshold group, preserving agent \(1\)’s total \(b_2\)-weighted share also preserves both \(x\) and \(y\). Allocating that share successively through the group therefore produces a maximizer with a prefix assigned to agent \(1\), a suffix assigned to agent \(2\), and at most one split item.

Enumerate all prefix allocations and all possible split positions. For a split item with values \(a,b>0\), its interval has utilities
\(x=X+at\) and \(y=Y+b(1-t)\), where \(X,Y\) are the contributions from the other items. The derivative vanishes only at
\[ t=\frac{a(Y+b)-cbX}{(1+c)ab}. \]
Thus the endpoints and this value, when it lies in \([0,1]\), suffice. If either \(a\) or \(b\) is zero, the objective is monotone or constant on the interval, so endpoints suffice.

There are \(O(m)\) candidates after sorting. For \(c=d/e\), comparing positive products \(xy^c\) is equivalent to comparing the rational quantities \(x^ey^d\). Here \(d,e\leq1000\) are fixed constants. All candidate shares are rational, and the final allocation coordinates can be output as finite expressions using rational numbers and fixed rational powers. No numerical rounding is required.

Each supported profile therefore requires \(O(m\log m)\) rational operations and fixed integer powers, on numbers of polynomial bit length. Across the support, the total bit running time and exact output length are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
