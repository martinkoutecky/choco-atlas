The anchor (named result in the paper)

Proposition 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu\) and a finite-description monotone complete rule \(V\) whose class-aggregate SSI or PBI limits exist under integer replications, find nonnegative class weights and a quota minimizing the distance between \(V\)'s limiting power distribution and that of a weighted rule.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\), where \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
A coalition is described by \(x\in\prod_i[0,\mu_i]\). The input rule \(V(x)\) is an AND/OR circuit with \(m\) gates whose leaves test \(\sum_i a_i x_i\geq b\), with binary-rational \(a_i\geq0\) and \(b>0\); promise \(V(0)=0\) and \(V(\mu)=1\).
Let \(D\) be the least common denominator of the masses. For each positive integer \(k\), form \(V_k\) with \(kD\mu_i\) voters of type \(i\), evaluating a coalition with type counts \(s_i\) at \(x_i=s_i/(kD)\).
Promise every \(V_k\) is complete: its desirability relation is total, where \(u\succeq v\) means \(V_k(S\cup\{u\})\geq V_k(S\cup\{v\})\) for every coalition \(S\) excluding both voters.
Choose \(p\in\{\operatorname{SSI},\operatorname{PBI}\}\), meaning the Shapley–Shubik index or normalized Penrose–Banzhaf index. Define \(P_i(V)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{u\text{ of type }i}p_u(V_k)\), and promise these limits exist.
An admissible weighted rule is \(W_{w,q}(x)=\mathbf1\{\sum_iw_ix_i\geq q\}\), with real \(w_i\geq0\), \(\sum_i\mu_iw_i=1\), and \(0<q\leq1\), whose class-aggregate limits exist under the same replication. Weights and quota remain fixed throughout replication.
Given \(a\in\{1,\infty\}\) and binary-rational \(\delta\geq0\), decide whether some admissible weighted rule satisfies \(\|P(V)-P(W_{w,q})\|_a\leq\delta\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard, for each choice of \(p\) and \(a\)?

Discrete enumeration determines the worst-case nearest-weighted power distances for complete games with seven and eight voters; for example, the SSI Manhattan distance is approximately \(0.0666667\) for both sizes. This is a finite extremal result, not a complexity classification. [Kurz](https://epub.uni-bayreuth.de/id/eprint/5539/1/wvg_sufficiently_good_arxiv.pdf)
Asymptotic Banzhaf results study weighted games as the number of small voters tends to infinity; no result settling the exact decision problem above was located. [Dubey and Shapley](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.4.2.99)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to total mass one and specified the coalition-mass domain.
  • Made 'finite-description rule' concrete using rational nonnegative threshold tests combined by AND/OR gates, following the discrete representation by Boolean combinations of weighted games; m counts circuit gates.
  • Specified integer replication using the least common denominator and coalition counts divided by the replicated population size.
  • Required complete, nonconstant simple games at every replication, retaining the discrete domain and allowing null voters.
  • Interpreted limiting power as total normalized power of each type, and retained both distance choices as input selectors.
  • Used fixed real per-voter class weights and a fixed quota across replications; normalized total weight to one and retained positive quota with a winning grand coalition.
  • Restricted candidate weighted rules to those with existing class-aggregate limits, so their objective values are defined.
  • Expressed exact minimization through the decision question of whether a weighted rule attains distance at most a rational threshold, without assuming that a minimum is attained.

Result. All four versions are polynomial-time solvable. In fact, every promised input is a YES instance, including when \(\delta=0\).

The substantive reason is that every probability vector on the given types is exactly attainable as the limiting aggregate power vector of a fixed weighted rule. The proof below treats real weights, zero weights, and lattice effects explicitly.

1. The target vector is a probability vector.

Write
\[ \Delta_{\tau-1}=\left\{z\in\mathbb R^\tau:z_i\geq0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}z_i=1\right\}. \]

Each replicated rule \(V_k\) is monotone: every leaf is monotone because its coefficients are nonnegative, and AND/OR gates preserve monotonicity. The endpoint promises make \(V_k\) a nonconstant simple game.

For SSI, every voter has nonnegative power, and every permutation has exactly one pivotal voter. Thus the individual indices sum to one.

For normalized PBI, all swing counts are nonnegative. Their sum is positive: along any chain that adds voters from the empty coalition to the grand coalition, at least one addition changes the outcome. Normalization therefore also gives individual indices summing to one.

Consequently, the aggregate vector belongs to \(\Delta_{\tau-1}\) for every \(k\). Since \(\tau\) is fixed and the coordinate limits are promised to exist, passing to those limits gives
\[ P(V)\in\Delta_{\tau-1}. \]

2. Construction for an arbitrary target vector.

Fix any \(z\in\Delta_{\tau-1}\). Define
\[ w_i=\frac{z_i}{\mu_i},\qquad q=\frac12. \]

These are fixed real weights, independent of replication. Since every mass is positive,
\[ w_i\geq0,\qquad \sum_i\mu_iw_i=\sum_i z_i=1. \]

Put \(d_i=D\mu_i\), so each \(d_i\) is a positive integer and \(\sum_i d_i=D\). The replicated weighted game has \(kd_i\) voters of type \(i\). Equivalently, give each such voter weight
\[ \alpha_{i,k}=\frac{w_i}{kD} \]
and use quota \(\frac12\). The total individual weight is one, while
\[ \max_i\alpha_{i,k}\longrightarrow0. \]

We prove that this same construction satisfies
\[ P_i(W_{w,1/2})=\mu_iw_i=z_i \]
for both indices.

3. SSI: exact limiting aggregate weights.

We use Neyman’s renewal theorem for sampling without replacement. Its relevant statement is:

For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there are constants \(\eta>0\) and \(K>0\) such that, for a finite probability weight vector \((\alpha_u)\), if
\[ \rho=\max_u\alpha_u<\eta,\qquad K\rho<t<1-K\rho, \]
then the probabilities \(H_u(t)\) that voter \(u\) first makes the accumulated weight *strictly exceed* \(t\) in a uniformly random permutation satisfy
\[ \sum_u|H_u(t)-\alpha_u|<\varepsilon. \]
This is the main theorem of [Neyman, *Renewal Theory for Sampling Without Replacement* (1982)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/38361781_Renewal_Theory_for_Sampling_Without_Replacement).

The strict threshold convention requires attention. At quota \(\frac12\), reversing a permutation identifies the pivotal voter for the weak threshold with the pivotal voter for the strict threshold.

Indeed, suppose voter \(u\), of weight \(\alpha_u\), has predecessor weight \(s\) in the original permutation. It is pivotal for the weak threshold precisely when
\[ s<\frac12\leq s+\alpha_u. \]
Its predecessor weight in the reversed permutation is \(1-s-\alpha_u\), and the displayed condition is equivalent to
\[ 1-s-\alpha_u\leq\frac12<1-s. \]
This is exactly strict-threshold pivotality in the reversed permutation. Reversal is a bijection, so
\[ \operatorname{SSI}_u(W_k)=H_u(1/2). \]

Zero-weight voters never pivot. They can be omitted when applying the theorem: the relative order induced on the positive-weight voters remains uniformly random.

Here \(\rho_k=\max_i w_i/(kD)\) tends to zero, and \(t=\frac12\) is fixed. Thus both hypotheses of Neyman’s theorem hold for every sufficiently large \(k\), giving
\[ \sum_u\left|\operatorname{SSI}_u(W_k)-\alpha_{u,k}\right|\longrightarrow0. \]

For each type \(i\), the triangle inequality now yields
\[ \left|\sum_{u\text{ of type }i}\operatorname{SSI}_u(W_k)-\mu_iw_i\right| \leq \sum_u\left|\operatorname{SSI}_u(W_k)-\alpha_{u,k}\right| \longrightarrow0, \]
because the \(kd_i\) voters of type \(i\) have total weight
\[ kd_i\frac{w_i}{kD}=\mu_iw_i. \]

This proves existence of the SSI aggregate limits and the desired identity.

4. PBI: a local limit argument covering real weights.

Work in unnormalized weight units: each type-\(i\) voter has weight \(w_i\), the total weight is \(kD\), and the quota is \(kD/2\).

Let \(\beta_i(k)\) be the probability that a uniformly random coalition of all voters except a specified type-\(i\) voter is losing but becomes winning when that voter joins. If \(S_{i,k}\) is its random weight, then
\[ \beta_i(k)=\Pr\left\{\frac{kD}{2}-w_i\leq S_{i,k}<\frac{kD}{2}\right\}. \]
Uniformly random coalitions include each available voter independently with probability \(\frac12\). Since every voter has the same number of possible coalitions excluding it,
\[ \sum_{u\text{ of type }i}\operatorname{PBI}_u(W_k) = \frac{kd_i\beta_i(k)}{\sum_jkd_j\beta_j(k)}. \]

If \(w_i=0\), then \(\beta_i(k)=0\) identically. Assume henceforth that \(w_i>0\).

Partition the voters into \(k\) blocks, each containing \(d_j\) voters of type \(j\). Let \(Z\) be the random weight contributed by one full block:
\[ Z=\sum_jw_j\sum_{\ell=1}^{d_j}B_{j,\ell}, \]
where the \(B_{j,\ell}\) are independent Bernoulli variables with parameter \(\frac12\). Then
\[ \mathbb EZ=\frac D2,\qquad \sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(Z)=\frac14\sum_jd_jw_j^2>0. \]
The variance is positive because \(\sum_jd_jw_j=D>0\).

Place the excluded voter in the last block. Let \(R_i\) be that block’s random weight with this voter removed. Then
\[ S_{i,k}=Z_1+\cdots+Z_{k-1}+R_i, \]
with independent full-block variables \(Z_\ell\) and independent \(R_i\). The residual variable has finite support.

Conditional on \(R_i=r\), the swing event is
\[ Z_1+\cdots+Z_{k-1}-\frac{(k-1)D}{2} \in \left[\frac D2-w_i-r,\frac D2-r\right). \]
This is a fixed interval of length \(w_i\).

We use the classical lattice local limit theorem and the fixed-interval nonlattice local limit theorem. The latter requires only nonlattice support and finite positive variance, without a strong nonlattice assumption; see [Shepp, *A Local Limit Theorem* (1964), Theorems 2–3 and the lattice discussion](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Local-limit-theorem.pdf).

There are two cases.

*Nonlattice case.* If \(Z\) is nonlattice, its centered independent sums satisfy, for every fixed \(a<b\),
\[ \Pr\left\{Z_1+\cdots+Z_n-\frac{nD}{2}\in[a,b)\right\} = \frac{b-a}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi n}}+o(n^{-1/2}). \]
Finite support verifies the moment hypothesis. Applying this to each of the finitely many residual values \(r\), and then averaging, gives
\[ \beta_i(k)=\frac{w_i}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi(k-1)}}+o(k^{-1/2}). \]

*Lattice case.* The support of \(Z\) contains zero and every positive \(w_j\): these outcomes arise by selecting no positive-weight voter, or just one voter of the chosen type. Hence, if \(Z\) is lattice with maximal span \(h>0\), every \(w_j\) is an integer multiple of \(h\), and \(Z\) and \(R_i\) take values in \(h\mathbb Z\).

For fixed \(r\), the uncentered swing interval is
\[ \left[\frac{kD}{2}-w_i-r,\frac{kD}{2}-r\right). \]
Its length is \(w_i=\ell_i h\) for a positive integer \(\ell_i\). Every half-open interval of this length contains exactly \(\ell_i\) points of \(h\mathbb Z\), including when an endpoint lies on the lattice.

Each such point differs from the full-block mean \((k-1)D/2\) by a bounded amount. The lattice local limit theorem therefore assigns each point probability
\[ \frac{h}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi(k-1)}}+o(k^{-1/2}). \]
Summing the \(\ell_i\) point probabilities and averaging over the finite residual support gives the same asymptotic formula:
\[ \beta_i(k)=\frac{w_i}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi(k-1)}}+o(k^{-1/2}). \]

Thus, in both cases,
\[ \sqrt{k}\,\beta_i(k)\longrightarrow\frac{w_i}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}. \]
The identity also holds when \(w_i=0\). Since \(\tau\) is finite and \(\sum_jd_jw_j=D\), normalization gives
\[ \lim_{k\to\infty} \frac{kd_i\beta_i(k)}{\sum_jkd_j\beta_j(k)} = \frac{d_iw_i}{\sum_jd_jw_j} = \mu_iw_i = z_i. \]

This proves existence of the normalized PBI aggregate limits and exact attainment of \(z\).

5. Decision and complexity.

For the selected index, take \(z=P(V)\), which belongs to \(\Delta_{\tau-1}\) by Section 1. Sections 2–4 prove that the fixed rule with
\[ w_i=\frac{P_i(V)}{\mu_i},\qquad q=\frac12 \]
is admissible and satisfies
\[ P(W_{w,1/2})=P(V). \]
Therefore,
\[ \|P(V)-P(W_{w,1/2})\|_a=0\leq\delta \]
for either permitted norm.

The decision algorithm simply returns YES on every promised input. It does not need to compute the target vector or output a witness. This is constant time in the promise-problem model, or linear time if the input is first read.

The argument applies to arbitrary support size and does not use completeness beyond the supplied promises. Finite-voter approximation distances do not obstruct this result: the exact match here concerns limiting aggregate powers under replication.
