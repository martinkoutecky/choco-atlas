The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite library of complete advertiser types and reports, a rational finite-support population distribution \(\mu\), and per-capita space \(\bar W\), what is the best worst-case welfare approximation achievable in polynomial time by feasible truthful direct mechanisms? Specify each mechanism by per-advertiser assignment probabilities \(q_j(r;\mu)\) and transfers for every report \(r=(b,S)\), including reports outside the support of \(\mu\); require at most one ad per advertiser in expectation, aggregate space at most \(\bar W\), and truthful reporting for each individual holding the population distribution fixed.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An advertiser has a public class \(h\), with advertiser-specific copies of ad formats \(J_h\) and public binary rational click probabilities \(\alpha_{hj}\in[0,1]\) and sizes \(w_{hj}>0\). Let \(m=\sum_h|J_h|\). Its private type is \((v,A)\), with binary rational \(v\ge0\) and \(A\subseteq J_h\).
The input explicitly lists a finite library \(T\) of complete types \(t=(h,v,A)\), containing all admissible reports: within each class, every listed bid can be paired with every subset of every listed ad set. Write \(\rho=|T|\). Class cannot be misreported; a type \((h,v,A)\) may report any listed \((h,b,S)\) with \(S\subseteq A\), including overbids and the empty set.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\mu\) sums to \(1\), and \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Per-capita space is a positive binary rational \(\bar W\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A direct mechanism specifies rational assignment probabilities \(q_{hj}(r;\mu)\) and expected transfers \(p_h(r;\mu)\in\mathbb Q\) for every report, including reports outside the support. Require \(q_{hj}\ge0\), \(q_{hj}=0\) when \(j\notin S\), and \(\sum_jq_{hj}(r;\mu)\le1\) for every report \(r=(h,b,S)\).
Require \(\sum_{t=(h,v,A)}\mu(t)\sum_jw_{hj}q_{hj}(t;\mu)\le\bar W\). The remaining probability means no ad; the space constraint is aggregate expected space, with no additional constraint on realized lotteries.
For every listed true type \(t=(h,v,A)\) and admissible report \(r=(h,b,S)\), holding \(\mu\) fixed, require \(v\sum_j\alpha_{hj}q_{hj}(t;\mu)-p_h(t;\mu)\ge v\sum_j\alpha_{hj}q_{hj}(r;\mu)-p_h(r;\mu)\). Require an implementation of these expected transfers that gives nonnegative truthful utility in every realized outcome.
Define \(\operatorname{SW}_M=\sum_{t=(h,v,A)}\mu(t)v\sum_j\alpha_{hj}q_{hj}(t;\mu)\). Let \(\operatorname{OPT}\) be the maximum of the same welfare over all feasible assignment probabilities without incentive constraints. An approximation factor \(\gamma\ge1\) means \(\operatorname{SW}_M\ge\operatorname{OPT}/\gamma\) on every input; zero-optimum inputs impose no ratio condition.

What is the best worst-case welfare approximation factor achievable by feasible truthful direct mechanisms with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,\rho,L\)?

In finite societies, algorithms whose expected clicks are nondecreasing separately in bid and reported ad set cannot achieve factors below \(2\) deterministically or \(12/11\) randomly; a polynomial-time truthful monotone mechanism achieves factor \(3\). These lower bounds concern monotone allocation rules, rather than all truthful mechanisms. [Source](https://www.proceedings.com/content/068/068431-2050open.pdf)
No result settling this exact finite-library population problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Public click probabilities and ad sizes are indexed by an immutable advertiser class; format copies belong to individual advertisers and do not create shared-item constraints.
  • The finite library explicitly includes the finite bid domain and all subset reports, including empty reports, and its size is an additional runtime parameter. Incentive constraints cover every listed true type, including zero-mass types.
  • Masses sum to one, values and bids are nonnegative binary rationals, click probabilities lie in the unit interval, and sizes and per-capita space are positive binary rationals.
  • Ad sizes have no upper bound imposed by per-capita space: the discrete exclusion of ads larger than total space uses integral feasibility and does not justify excluding them under the specified aggregate expected-space constraint.
  • Truthfulness is in expected utility with the distribution fixed; individual rationality in every realized outcome is retained, and transfers may have either sign.
  • The welfare benchmark is the unconstrained-by-incentives optimum under the same continuous feasibility constraints; approximation guarantees are uniform over inputs.
  • No extra monotonicity restriction is imposed on truthful mechanisms, because the graded question asks about all truthful direct mechanisms.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result and interpretation

The best worst-case approximation factor is \(1\). There is a polynomial-time truthful direct mechanism attaining \(\operatorname{OPT}\) exactly.

The proof uses the stipulated interpretation: \(\mu\) remains fixed when checking a deviation, and feasibility concerns aggregate expected space. We construct a common space price, then resolve indifferences among surplus-maximizing choices to obtain a welfare-optimal feasible allocation.

2. A welfare upper bound

Write \(P=\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\). Introduce a null outcome \(0\), meaning no ad, with \(w_{h0}=\alpha_{h0}=0\).

For any listed type \(t=(h,v,A)\) and nonnegative space price \(\lambda\), define its maximum surplus by

\(U_t(\lambda)=\max_{j\in A\cup\{0\}}\{v\alpha_{hj}-\lambda w_{hj}\}.\)

Define

\(F(\lambda)=\lambda\bar W+\sum_{t\in P}\mu(t)U_t(\lambda).\)

For any feasible assignment \(q\), give the null outcome the remaining probability. The resulting lottery is supported on \(A\cup\{0\}\), so its expected surplus is at most \(U_t(\lambda)\). Consequently,

\(v\sum_j\alpha_{hj}q_{hj}(t)\le U_t(\lambda)+\lambda\sum_jw_{hj}q_{hj}(t).\)

Multiplying by the masses, summing, and using feasibility and \(\lambda\ge0\) gives

\(\operatorname{SW}(q)\le F(\lambda).\)

Thus, for every \(\lambda\ge0\),

\(\operatorname{OPT}\le F(\lambda).\)

We will construct a truthful allocation attaining this upper bound at a minimizing price.

3. Computing a minimizing price

Each \(U_t\) is the maximum of finitely many affine functions. Hence \(F\) is continuous and piecewise affine. Also \(F(\lambda)\ge\lambda\bar W\), because every \(U_t(\lambda)\ge0\). Since \(\bar W>0\), \(F(\lambda)\) tends to infinity as \(\lambda\) tends to infinity, and therefore has a minimum on the nonnegative half-line.

This minimum can be found by exact rational arithmetic. Form the candidate set

\(\mathcal C=\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac{v(\alpha_{hj}-\alpha_{hk})}{w_{hj}-w_{hk}}:\ t=(h,v,A)\in P,\ j,k\in A\cup\{0\},\ w_{hj}\ne w_{hk},\ \frac{v(\alpha_{hj}-\alpha_{hk})}{w_{hj}-w_{hk}}\ge0\right\}.\)

Every breakpoint of every \(U_t\) belongs to \(\mathcal C\). Between consecutive candidates, \(F\) is affine, so its minimum on such an interval occurs at an endpoint unless it is constant, in which case the endpoints also minimize it. Beyond the largest candidate, all real-ad surplus lines are nonpositive: their intersections with the null line were included among the candidates. Hence \(F(\lambda)=\lambda\bar W\) there and is increasing.

It follows that some global minimizer belongs to \(\mathcal C\). Evaluate \(F\) at every candidate and choose a minimizer \(\lambda^*\).

4. Resolving ties to satisfy capacity

For each \(t=(h,v,A)\in P\), let

\(B_t=\{j\in A\cup\{0\}:v\alpha_{hj}-\lambda^*w_{hj}=U_t(\lambda^*)\}.\)

Choose \(j_t^-\in B_t\) with minimum size and \(j_t^+\in B_t\) with maximum size. Define

\(s_t^-=w_{h j_t^-},\qquad s_t^+=w_{h j_t^+},\)

and

\(S^-=\sum_{t\in P}\mu(t)s_t^-,\qquad S^+=\sum_{t\in P}\mu(t)s_t^+.\)

We now prove the inequalities needed for feasibility.

For a maximum of finitely many affine functions, the right derivative at a point is the largest slope among the maximizing lines, and the left derivative is the smallest such slope. Indeed, sufficiently near the point, nonmaximizing lines remain below the maximum; among the maximizing lines, moving right favors the largest slope and moving left favors the smallest slope.

Here the slopes are \(-w_{hj}\). Therefore,

\(F'_+(\lambda^*)=\bar W-S^-,\)

and, when \(\lambda^*>0\),

\(F'_-(\lambda^*)=\bar W-S^+.\)

At an interior minimum, the left derivative is nonpositive and the right derivative is nonnegative. Thus, if \(\lambda^*>0\),

\(S^-\le\bar W\le S^+.\)

At the boundary minimum \(\lambda^*=0\), the right derivative is nonnegative, giving \(S^-\le\bar W\).

Construct the support allocations as follows.

  • If \(\lambda^*=0\), assign \(j_t^-\) to each support type.
  • If \(\lambda^*>0\) and \(S^-<S^+\), set \(\theta=(\bar W-S^-)/(S^+-S^-)\). For every support type, assign \(j_t^-\) with probability \(1-\theta\) and \(j_t^+\) with probability \(\theta\).
  • If \(\lambda^*>0\) and \(S^-=S^+\), the inequalities imply \(S^-=\bar W\); assign \(j_t^-\).

The null outcome in these lotteries means no ad. Every report receives at most one ad. Aggregate expected space is at most \(\bar W\), and equals \(\bar W\) whenever \(\lambda^*>0\).

For every report outside the support, choose any outcome maximizing its reported surplus at the same price \(\lambda^*\). These reports have zero mass and therefore do not affect aggregate feasibility.

This defines assignment probabilities for every report in \(T\).

5. Transfers, truthfulness, and realized individual rationality

For every report \(r=(h,b,S)\), set

\(p_h(r;\mu)=\lambda^*\sum_jw_{hj}q_{hj}(r;\mu).\)

Every outcome assigned to \(r\) maximizes \(b\alpha_{hj}-\lambda^*w_{hj}\) over \(S\cup\{0\}\).

Fix any listed true type \(t=(h,v,A)\). Its truthful expected utility is exactly

\(v\sum_j\alpha_{hj}q_{hj}(t;\mu)-p_h(t;\mu)=U_t(\lambda^*),\)

because every outcome in its truthful lottery is a surplus maximizer.

Consider any admissible deviation \(r=(h,b,S)\), including an overbid. Since \(S\subseteq A\), every outcome of the reported lottery is available to the true type. Therefore its utility from deviating is

\(v\sum_j\alpha_{hj}q_{hj}(r;\mu)-p_h(r;\mu)=\sum_jq_{hj}(r;\mu)(v\alpha_{hj}-\lambda^*w_{hj})\le U_t(\lambda^*).\)

The last inequality follows by including the null outcome: the expression is a convex combination of true-type surpluses, each at most \(U_t(\lambda^*)\). This proves every required incentive constraint, including those for types outside the support.

To implement transfers with nonnegative utility even when click randomness is included, charge nothing without a click. When ad \(j\) with \(\alpha_{hj}>0\) is clicked, charge

\(c_{hj}=\lambda^*w_{hj}/\alpha_{hj}.\)

Conditional on assigning that ad, the expected charge is \(\lambda^*w_{hj}\), so the expected transfer is exactly \(p_h(r;\mu)\).

For a truthful type, every selected ad satisfies

\(v\alpha_{hj}-\lambda^*w_{hj}=U_t(\lambda^*)\ge0,\)

because the null outcome is available. Thus \(c_{hj}\le v\): utility is nonnegative on a click, and is zero without a click. If a selected ad has \(\alpha_{hj}=0\), the same inequality forces \(\lambda^*w_{hj}=0\); charge zero. Charge zero for the null outcome as well.

If outcomes refer only to the assignment lottery, charging \(\lambda^*w_{hj}\) upon assigning \(j\) likewise gives nonnegative conditional utility \(v\alpha_{hj}-\lambda^*w_{hj}\).

6. Exact optimality and polynomial running time

Let \(Q\) be aggregate expected space under the constructed mechanism. Each support type receives only surplus-maximizing outcomes, so

\(\operatorname{SW}_M=\sum_{t\in P}\mu(t)U_t(\lambda^*)+\lambda^*Q.\)

If \(\lambda^*>0\), then \(Q=\bar W\). If \(\lambda^*=0\), the term involving \(Q\) vanishes. In both cases,

\(\operatorname{SW}_M=F(\lambda^*).\)

Combining feasibility with the upper bound proved earlier yields

\(\operatorname{SW}_M\le\operatorname{OPT}\le F(\lambda^*)=\operatorname{SW}_M.\)

Hence \(\operatorname{SW}_M=\operatorname{OPT}\), including on zero-optimum inputs.

There are at most \(1+\tau(m+1)^2\) candidate prices. Evaluating each candidate requires at most \(\tau(m+1)\) surplus evaluations. Constructing allocations and transfers for the complete library requires at most \(\rho(m+1)\) further evaluations.

All operations are rational additions, multiplications, divisions, and comparisons. Candidate prices have bit length polynomial in \(L\). The mass-weighted sums, mixing probability, assignment probabilities, and transfers also have polynomial bit length: each sum has polynomially many terms, each obtained from a bounded number of operations on input rationals and the selected price. Thus the entire construction runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,\rho,L\), with rational output of polynomial size.

7. Relation to the cited finite-society bounds

The [cited paper](https://www.proceedings.com/content/068/068431-2050open.pdf) defines truthfulness through deviations in a finite advertiser report profile. Here the incentive inequalities expressly hold \(\mu\) fixed. Consequently, the common price \(\lambda^*(\mu)\) stays fixed during every deviation, which is the key fact in the proof. The construction also directly accommodates the specified aggregate expected-space constraint.

Since factor \(1\) is achieved, it is the best factor allowed by the question’s definition.
