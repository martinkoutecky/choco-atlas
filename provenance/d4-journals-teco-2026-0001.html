The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix a series-parallel network with one origin–destination pair, a maximum coalition size \(A\), and a finite set \(T\) of commuter types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Type \(t\) has preferences \((\alpha_t,\beta_t)\); all types share capacity-sharing disutilities \(\Delta\alpha(k),\Delta\beta(k)\). Given rational per-capita edge capacities \(\kappa_e\), compute a welfare-maximizing market equilibrium. For each integral coalition composition \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{T}\) with \(1\le |b|\le A\) and route \(r\), let \(x_{r,b}\ge 0\) be its allocation intensity per unit of population, and let \(V_r(b)=\sum_{t\in T}b_t(\alpha_t-\beta_t d_r-\Delta\alpha(|b|)-\Delta\beta(|b|)d_r)\). Maximize \(\sum_{r,b}V_r(b)x_{r,b}\), subject to \(\sum_{r,b}b_t x_{r,b}\le\mu_t\) for each type and \(\sum_{r,b:e\in r}x_{r,b}\le\kappa_e\) for each edge. Return supporting type utilities \(u_t\ge 0\) and edge prices \(\pi_e\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_t b_tu_t+\sum_{e\in r}\pi_e\ge V_r(b)\) for every \((b,r)\), with equality on allocated patterns and zero price on slack edges; payments can then be recovered per assigned trip.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,A,L\), including an exact rational allocation, supporting utilities, edge prices, and payments.

The key is that repeated types and continuous intensities permit an exact replacement of mixed coalitions by homogeneous coalitions. This yields a polynomial-size flow LP.

1. Exact reduction to homogeneous coalitions

Let \(\mathbf e^t\) denote the unit vector for type \(t\). Call a pattern homogeneous if its coalition is \(k\mathbf e^t\) for some \(1\le k\le A\).

Consider a pattern \((r,b)\) of intensity \(x\), with \(k=|b|\). Replace it by the patterns \((r,k\mathbf e^t)\), assigning intensity

\[ \text{} \]

\( \displaystyle y_t=\frac{b_t}{k}x \)

to each type with \(b_t>0\). These replacements are permitted because repeated types are allowed and intensities are continuous.

For every type \(t\), its consumed mass remains

\( \displaystyle ky_t=b_tx. \)

The total replacement intensity on route \(r\) is

\( \displaystyle \sum_t y_t=\frac{\sum_t b_t}{k}x=x, \)

so every edge’s consumption is preserved. Welfare is also preserved:

\( \displaystyle \sum_t y_tV_r(k\mathbf e^t) =x\sum_t b_t\bigl[\alpha_t-\beta_td_r-\Delta\alpha(k)-\Delta\beta(k)d_r\bigr] =xV_r(b). \)

Applying this replacement to every allocated pattern proves:

Lemma. Restricting the allocation LP to homogeneous patterns preserves its optimal value. In particular, a homogeneous welfare-maximizing allocation exists.

This reduction uses the continuous formulation directly; it does not require expanding the society into individual agents.

2. A polynomial-size flow formulation

Write \(s\) and \(z\) for the origin and destination. A directed two-terminal series-parallel network is acyclic: this holds for a single directed edge and is preserved by directed series and parallel composition. Its underlying graph is connected, so \(|N|\le m+1\).

For every \(t\in T\) and \(1\le k\le A\), define

\( \displaystyle c_{t,k}=k\bigl[\alpha_t-\Delta\alpha(k)\bigr], \qquad q_{t,k}=k\bigl[\beta_t+\Delta\beta(k)\bigr]. \)

Thus

\( \displaystyle V_r(k\mathbf e^t)=c_{t,k}-q_{t,k}d_r. \)

Introduce nonnegative variables:

  • \(F_{t,k}\): total trip intensity for type \(t\) and coalition size \(k\);
  • \(f_{t,k,e}\): the corresponding intensity traversing edge \(e\).

Let \(\delta^+(v)\) and \(\delta^-(v)\) denote outgoing and incoming edges, and let \(\sigma_s=1\), \(\sigma_z=-1\), and \(\sigma_v=0\) elsewhere. Consider the LP

\( \displaystyle \max\ \sum_{t,k}c_{t,k}F_{t,k} -\sum_{t,k,e}q_{t,k}d_ef_{t,k,e}, \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{A}kF_{t,k}\le\mu_t \qquad(t\in T), \)

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t,k}f_{t,k,e}\le\kappa_e \qquad(e\in E), \)

\( \displaystyle \sum_{e\in\delta^+(v)}f_{t,k,e} -\sum_{e\in\delta^-(v)}f_{t,k,e} =\sigma_vF_{t,k} \qquad(t,k,v), \)

and \(F_{t,k},f_{t,k,e}\ge0\).

Equivalence with the homogeneous pattern LP

Given homogeneous pattern intensities \(x_{r,k\mathbf e^t}\), set

\( \displaystyle F_{t,k}=\sum_r x_{r,k\mathbf e^t}, \qquad f_{t,k,e}=\sum_{r:e\in r}x_{r,k\mathbf e^t}. \)

The flow equations hold, and the type and edge constraints are exactly the original resource constraints. Furthermore,

\( \displaystyle \sum_e d_ef_{t,k,e} =\sum_r d_rx_{r,k\mathbf e^t}, \)

so welfare is preserved.

Conversely, every feasible flow for a fixed \((t,k)\) decomposes into directed \(s\)-\(z\) paths. Here is a constructive justification.

If its remaining value is positive, there is a positive-flow edge leaving \(s\). Following positive-flow edges cannot terminate at an internal vertex, because conservation would then be violated. Acyclicity prevents revisiting a vertex, so the walk reaches \(z\). Subtract the minimum flow on this path and reduce the remaining flow value by the same amount.

Each subtraction eliminates at least one positive-flow edge. Thus there are at most \(m\) iterations. Once the remaining flow value is zero, any residual flow would be a nonnegative circulation. An acyclic graph has no nonzero nonnegative circulation: the earliest vertex in a topological order incident to positive residual flow would have positive outgoing flow and no positive incoming flow, contradicting conservation.

Assign each extracted path’s intensity to the pattern \((r,k\mathbf e^t)\). This preserves all resource consumption and the objective.

Together with Section 1, this proves that the compact flow LP has exactly the original optimum. Its decomposition produces at most \(m\tau A\) nonzero patterns.

3. Supporting utilities and edge prices

The compact LP’s dual has nonnegative variables \(u_t\) and \(\pi_e\), together with unrestricted flow potentials \(h_{t,k,v}\). Its objective is

\( \displaystyle \min\ \sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_e\kappa_e\pi_e. \)

Its constraints are

\( \displaystyle \pi_e+q_{t,k}d_e+h_{t,k,v}-h_{t,k,w}\ge0 \qquad(e=(v,w),\,t,k), \)

and

\( \displaystyle ku_t+h_{t,k,z}-h_{t,k,s}\ge c_{t,k} \qquad(t,k). \)

These signs follow directly by writing each flow equality as outgoing flow minus incoming flow minus \(\sigma_vF_{t,k}=0\): the edge variable’s primal objective coefficient is \(-q_{t,k}d_e\), and the \(F_{t,k}\) coefficient is \(c_{t,k}\).

Feasibility for every pattern

Write \(\pi_r=\sum_{e\in r}\pi_e\). Summing the edge inequalities along any route \(r\) gives

\( \displaystyle \pi_r+q_{t,k}d_r\ge h_{t,k,z}-h_{t,k,s}. \)

Combining this with the \(F_{t,k}\) inequality yields

\( \displaystyle ku_t+\pi_r\ge c_{t,k}-q_{t,k}d_r =V_r(k\mathbf e^t). \)

Now take an arbitrary mixed coalition \(b\) with \(|b|=k\). Multiply the homogeneous inequality for type \(t\) by \(b_t/k\) and sum over types. Since \(\sum_t b_t/k=1\), the result is

\( \displaystyle \sum_t b_tu_t+\pi_r \ge\sum_t\frac{b_t}{k}V_r(k\mathbf e^t) =V_r(b). \)

Thus the computed utilities and prices satisfy the supporting inequality for every pattern, including mixed coalitions omitted from the compact formulation.

Equality on allocated patterns and slack conditions

The compact primal is feasible, since the zero allocation is feasible. It is bounded: \(F_{t,k}\le\mu_t/k\) and \(f_{t,k,e}\le\kappa_e\). Hence its feasible set is compact and an optimum exists.

The dual is also feasible: \(u_t=\alpha_t\), \(\pi_e=0\), and all potentials zero satisfy its constraints because the disutilities and \(q_{t,k}\) are nonnegative. Strong LP duality therefore applies. [MIT notes on LP duality](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/18/Notes/n12-duality.html)

Choose optimal primal and dual solutions, and decompose the primal into the allocation \(x\) constructed above. Define its resource slacks and pattern gaps by

\( \displaystyle S_t=\mu_t-\sum_{r,b}b_tx_{r,b}, \qquad C_e=\kappa_e-\sum_{r,b:e\in r}x_{r,b}, \)

\( \displaystyle g_{r,b}=\sum_tb_tu_t+\pi_r-V_r(b). \)

All these quantities are nonnegative. Equality of the primal and dual objectives gives the identity

\( \displaystyle 0=\sum_tu_tS_t+\sum_e\pi_eC_e+\sum_{r,b}x_{r,b}g_{r,b}. \)

Every summand is nonnegative, so each vanishes. Consequently:

  • \(x_{r,b}>0\) implies \(g_{r,b}=0\);
  • \(S_t>0\) implies \(u_t=0\);
  • \(C_e>0\) implies \(\pi_e=0\).

These are exactly the required supporting equality and slack-resource conditions.

Payments

For each allocated pattern, compute the prescribed payments

\( \displaystyle p_{t,r,b} =\alpha_t-\beta_td_r-\Delta\alpha(|b|) -\Delta\beta(|b|)d_r-u_t. \)

Each participating member receives utility \(u_t\ge0\). Moreover, the supporting equality implies

\( \displaystyle \sum_tb_tp_{t,r,b} =V_r(b)-\sum_tb_tu_t =\pi_r. \)

Thus payments cover the route’s edge prices. Negative individual payments are allowed, as required. Unserved mass receives zero utility and pays zero.

4. Running time and exact output

The compact primal has \(\tau A(m+1)\) variables and

\( \displaystyle \tau+m+\tau A|N|=O(m\tau A) \)

constraints, apart from nonnegativity. The explicit dual also has \(O(m\tau A)\) variables and constraints.

Every coefficient has bit length \(O(L+\log A)\): constructing \(c_{t,k}\) and \(q_{t,k}d_e\) requires only a constant number of rational additions and multiplications. Consequently, both LPs have total encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,A,L\).

The standard exact rational LP theorem supplies optimal rational solutions in time polynomial in the LP encoding length, with polynomial-length rational coordinates. This is a bit-complexity guarantee. [MIT notes on polynomial-time LP algorithms](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html)

Including construction, LP solution, decomposition, and payments, a valid overall bound is

\( \displaystyle \bigl(m\tau A\,[L+\log(m\tau A)]\bigr)^{O(1)} \)

bit operations.

Path decomposition takes \(O(\tau A m^2)\) rational operations. Its output intensities also have polynomial bit length: for each flow, take the product of its edge-flow denominators. This product has polynomial bit length, and every residual flow and extracted intensity is an integer multiple of its reciprocal.

The output therefore contains at most \(m\tau A\) patterns, each with at most \(m\) edges, together with polynomial-length rational intensities, utilities, prices, and payments.

For independent verification of the exponentially many supporting inequalities, assign edge weight

\( \displaystyle w_{t,k,e}=\pi_e+q_{t,k}d_e. \)

All supporting inequalities hold exactly when

\( \displaystyle ku_t+\operatorname{dist}_{w_{t,k}}(s,z)\ge c_{t,k} \qquad(t,k). \)

Indeed, shortest paths check all homogeneous inequalities, and Section 3 proves that those imply every mixed inequality. On the acyclic network, these checks take \(O(\tau A m)\) rational operations.

The argument uses only acyclicity of the given topology and shared disutilities. Their convexity assumptions impose no additional computational difficulty.
