The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), goods classes \(Q\) with rational supplies \(\lambda_q\), values \(v_q\), capacity \(k\), and eligibility sets \(\Omega_t\), define coalition worth \(V(z)\) by maximizing \(\sum_{t,q}v_qx_{tq}\) subject to \(\sum_qx_{tq}\le kz_t\), \(\sum_tx_{tq}\le\lambda_q\), and \(x_{tq}=0\) when \(q\notin\Omega_t\). Let \(D_tV(z)=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}(V(z+\epsilon e_t)-V(z))/\epsilon\) and \(H_t=\{t\}\cup\{s:\Omega_s\cap\Omega_t\ne\emptyset\}\). For \(u\in[0,1]\), set \(z^-_s(u)=z^+_s(u)=u\mu_s\) for \(s\in H_t\), set \(z^-_s(u)=\mu_s\) for \(s\notin H_t\), and set \(z^+_s(u)=0\) otherwise. Can one compute the exact endpoints \(L_t=\int_0^1D_tV(z^-(u))\,du\) and \(U_t=\int_0^1D_tV(z^+(u))\,du\), prove they bound \(\sigma_t=\int_0^1D_tV(u\mu)\,du\), and determine the running time in \(\tau=|T|\), \(|Q|\), input bit length, and maximum type-neighborhood size?

Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; masses need not sum to one. Goods classes \(Q\), with \(m=|Q|\), have binary-rational supplies \(\lambda_q\ge0\) and values \(v_q\ge0\). Each type has an explicitly listed eligibility set \(\Omega_t\subseteq Q\); the common capacity \(k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) is binary encoded. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For every \(z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\), define \(V(z)=\max\{\sum_{t\in T,q\in Q}v_qx_{tq}:x_{tq}\ge0,\ \sum_qx_{tq}\le kz_t,\ \sum_tx_{tq}\le\lambda_q,\ x_{tq}=0\text{ if }q\notin\Omega_t\}\). Allocations are divisible, goods values are independent of recipient, and unused supply is allowed.
Define \(D_tV(z)=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}[V(z+\epsilon e_t)-V(z)]/\epsilon\), where \(e_t\) is the coordinate unit vector; this uses the stated extension of \(V\) beyond the society's masses. Set \(H_t=\{t\}\cup\{s\in T:\Omega_s\cap\Omega_t\ne\emptyset\}\) and \(d=\max_t(|H_t|-1)\).
For \(u\in[0,1]\), let \(z^{t,-}_s(u)=z^{t,+}_s(u)=u\mu_s\) for \(s\in H_t\), and let \(z^{t,-}_s(u)=\mu_s\), \(z^{t,+}_s(u)=0\) otherwise. Define the per-unit quantities \(L_t=\int_0^1D_tV(z^{t,-}(u))\,du\), \(U_t=\int_0^1D_tV(z^{t,+}(u))\,du\), and \(\sigma_t=\int_0^1D_tV(u\mu)\,du\).
Do these endpoints always satisfy \(L_t\le\sigma_t\le U_t\)? Is exact computation of all \((L_t,U_t)\) in \(\mathrm P\) or NP-hard, and what is its running time in \(m,\tau,L,d\)?

Result

The endpoints always satisfy \(L_t\le \sigma_t\le U_t\). Moreover, all endpoints are exactly computable in polynomial time.

Let \(E=\sum_{t\in T}|\Omega_t|\), \(n=m+\tau+2\), and \(a=E+m+\tau\). An exact algorithm uses \(O(\tau m(d+1))\) maximum-flow computations on networks with at most \(n\) vertices and \(a\) arcs. One conservative bound on its total bit complexity is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L^2+\tau m(d+1)(m+\tau+2)(m\tau+m+\tau)^2L^2\right).\)

This is an upper bound, without an optimality claim. The proof below supplies the algorithm and establishes polynomial bounds on all rational encodings.

If \(k=0\), \(m=0\), or every value is zero, all quantities in question are zero. Henceforth assume \(k>0\), \(m>0\), and some value is positive.

1. Maximum-flow ranks and the welfare decomposition

For a nonnegative capacity vector \(c\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\) and a goods subset \(A\subseteq Q\), let \(R_A(c)\) be the maximum total quantity allocable using only goods in \(A\), subject to type capacities \(c_t\) and the stated eligibility and supply constraints.

Write \(c(S)=\sum_{t\in S}c_t\), \(\lambda(B)=\sum_{q\in B}\lambda_q\), and define

\(\displaystyle N_Q(S)=\bigcup_{t\in S}\Omega_t,\qquad N_T(B)=\{t\in T:\Omega_t\cap B\ne\varnothing\}.\)

The max-flow min-cut theorem gives both representations

\[\displaystyle R_A(c)=\min_{S\subseteq T}\left[c(T\setminus S)+\lambda(A\cap N_Q(S))\right],\tag{1}\]

and

\[\displaystyle R_A(c)=\min_{B\subseteq A}\left[c(N_T(B))+\lambda(A\setminus B)\right].\tag{2}\]

For (1), use arcs from the source to types with capacities \(c_t\), eligibility arcs from types to goods in \(A\), and arcs from goods to the sink with capacities \(\lambda_q\). Give every eligibility arc a finite capacity greater than \(c(T)+\lambda(Q)\). A minimum cut cannot cross such an arc. If its source-side types are \(S\), its source-side goods must include \(A\cap N_Q(S)\), giving (1). Reversing the network gives (2).

We next establish the weighted welfare decomposition, including the property needed for its application.

The goods rank is submodular

Fix \(c\), and put \(r(A)=R_A(c)\). This function is normalized and monotone: \(r(\varnothing)=0\), and allowing additional goods cannot reduce the maximum allocable quantity.

It is also submodular. Indeed, \(f(B)=c(N_T(B))\) is a nonnegative weighted coverage function, so

\(\displaystyle f(B)+f(C)\ge f(B\cup C)+f(B\cap C).\)

For goods subsets \(X,Y\), choose minimizers \(B_X\subseteq X\) and \(B_Y\subseteq Y\) in (2). Modularity of \(\lambda\) gives

\(\displaystyle \lambda(X\setminus B_X)+\lambda(Y\setminus B_Y) = \lambda((X\cup Y)\setminus(B_X\cup B_Y)) + \lambda((X\cap Y)\setminus(B_X\cap B_Y)). \)

Combining this identity with submodularity of \(f\), then applying (2) to the union and intersection, yields

\[\displaystyle r(X)+r(Y)\ge r(X\cup Y)+r(X\cap Y).\tag{3}\]

Aggregate feasibility and greedy optimization

An aggregate goods vector \(y\) is achievable precisely when

\[\displaystyle y\ge0,\qquad y(A)\le r(A)\quad\text{for every }A\subseteq Q.\tag{4}\]

Necessity follows from the definition of \(r\). For sufficiency, (2) implies \(r(A)\le c(N_T(A))\) and \(r(\{q\})\le\lambda_q\). Thus (4) implies the supply bounds and

\(\displaystyle y(A)\le c(N_T(A))\quad\text{for every }A\subseteq Q.\)

In the network directed from goods to types, with source capacities \(y_q\), every cut has capacity at least \(y(Q)\): the cut corresponding to goods subset \(A\) has capacity \(y(Q\setminus A)+c(N_T(A))\). Consequently, a flow allocating all of \(y\) exists.

Order the goods as \(q_1,\ldots,q_m\) so that \(w_i=v_{q_i}\) is nonincreasing. Put \(w_{m+1}=0\), \(\delta_i=w_i-w_{i+1}\ge0\), and \(A_i=\{q_1,\ldots,q_i\}\), with \(A_0=\varnothing\).

The vector

\(\displaystyle y_{q_i}=r(A_i)-r(A_{i-1})\)

is feasible. To verify (4), fix \(B\subseteq Q\) and set \(B_i=B\cap A_i\). Whenever \(q_i\in B\), submodularity applied to \(A_{i-1}\) and \(B_i\) gives

\(\displaystyle r(A_i)-r(A_{i-1})\le r(B_i)-r(B_{i-1}).\)

Summing over \(q_i\in B\) gives \(y(B)\le r(B)\).

For any feasible aggregate vector, telescoping gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_q v_qy_q=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_i y(A_i)\le\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_i r(A_i).\)

The constructed vector attains equality because \(y(A_i)=r(A_i)\). Therefore, for every \(z\ge0\),

\[\displaystyle V(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_i R_{A_i}(kz).\tag{5}\]

Terms with \(\delta_i=0\) can be omitted.

2. Right derivatives decrease with capacities

Fix \(A\subseteq Q\). Define

\(\displaystyle g_A(S)=\lambda(A\cap N_Q(S)),\qquad \phi_c(S)=g_A(S)-c(S).\)

The function \(g_A\) is a nonnegative weighted coverage function, hence submodular. Subtracting a modular function preserves submodularity.

The minimizers of \(\phi_c\) are closed under union and intersection: if two sets minimize it, the submodularity inequality and optimality force their union and intersection to minimize it as well. Thus there is a unique inclusionwise largest minimizer, denoted \(S_A(c)\).

Derivative formula

By (1), \(R_A\) is the minimum of finitely many affine functions. At \(c\), increasing coordinate \(c_t\) gives slope zero for cuts containing \(t\), and slope one for cuts excluding \(t\). Its right derivative is therefore the minimum of these slopes among active cuts:

\[\displaystyle \partial_t^+R_A(c)=\mathbf1_{\{t\notin S_A(c)\}}.\tag{6}\]

For completeness, only active cuts need be considered because there are finitely many cuts: every inactive cut has a positive gap at \(c\), so none can determine the value for sufficiently small positive increments.

Monotonicity of largest minimizers

Suppose \(c\le c'\) coordinatewise. Set \(P=S_A(c)\) and \(R=S_A(c')\). Submodularity gives

\[\displaystyle \phi_c(P\cap R)+\phi_{c'}(P\cup R)\le\phi_c(P)+\phi_{c'}(R).\tag{7}\]

Explicitly, the left side minus the right side equals

\(\displaystyle g_A(P\cap R)+g_A(P\cup R)-g_A(P)-g_A(R) -(c'-c)(P\setminus R), \)

which is nonpositive.

Optimality gives the reverse inequality term by term. Hence equality holds in (7), and \(P\cup R\) minimizes \(\phi_{c'}\). Since \(R\) is its largest minimizer, \(P\subseteq R\). We have proved

\[\displaystyle c\le c'\ \Longrightarrow\ S_A(c)\subseteq S_A(c').\tag{8}\]

Combining (5) and (6),

\[\displaystyle D_tV(z)=k\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_i\mathbf1_{\{t\notin S_{A_i}(kz)\}}.\tag{9}\]

Thus the stated right derivative exists everywhere, including at zero coordinates and at the society’s masses. By (8) and \(\delta_i\ge0\),

\[\displaystyle z\le z'\ \Longrightarrow\ D_tV(z)\ge D_tV(z').\tag{10}\]

3. The endpoint inequalities

For every \(u\in[0,1]\), the definitions give the coordinatewise ordering

\(\displaystyle z^{t,+}(u)\le u\mu\le z^{t,-}(u).\)

Applying (10),

\(\displaystyle D_tV(z^{t,-}(u))\le D_tV(u\mu)\le D_tV(z^{t,+}(u)).\)

These functions are integrable. For example, (9) expresses them as finite sums of indicators of membership in nested sets along increasing capacity paths; each indicator changes at most once. They are also bounded between zero and \(kw_1\).

Integrating proves

\(\displaystyle \boxed{L_t\le\sigma_t\le U_t\quad\text{for every }t\in T.}\)

No normalization of the masses was used.

4. Polynomially many cut pieces along an endpoint path

Fix a type \(t\), an endpoint sign, and a goods prefix \(A_i\). Write the endpoint capacities as

\(\displaystyle c_s(u)=b_s+ua_s,\)

where \(a_s=k\mu_s>0\) for \(s\in H_t\), and \(a_s=0\) otherwise. For the lower endpoint, \(b_s=k\mu_s\) outside \(H_t\); for the upper endpoint, \(b_s=0\) everywhere. In both cases \(b_s=0\) on \(H_t\).

Put \(h=|H_t|\le d+1\). Formula (1) becomes

\(\displaystyle F(u):=R_{A_i}(c(u))=\min_{S\subseteq T}\ell_S(u),\)

with

\[\displaystyle \ell_S(u)=\alpha_S+\beta_Su,\quad \alpha_S=b(T\setminus S)+\lambda(A_i\cap N_Q(S)),\quad \beta_S=a(T\setminus S).\tag{11}\]

The function \(F\) is concave and piecewise affine.

By (8), its largest minimizing type sets are nested as \(u\) increases. Whenever their slope \(\beta_S\) strictly decreases, at least one additional member of \(H_t\) enters the set. Each member enters at most once, so there are at most \(h\) strict slope decreases.

Two distinct optimal affine lines cannot have the same slope: the line with larger intercept would never be optimal. Consequently, there are at most \(h+1\) distinct affine lines returned by largest-minimizer queries, and at most \(h+1\) affine pieces of positive length.

Obtaining the largest minimum cut

At any rational parameter, one maximum-flow computation also supplies the largest source-side minimum cut. In its residual graph, take all vertices that cannot reach the sink. This set contains the source and has no outgoing residual arc, so it is a minimum-cut source side. Every minimum-cut source side is closed under residual reachability and excludes the sink, hence is contained in this set.

Its type vertices are exactly \(S_{A_i}(c(u))\). Indeed, every minimizing type set in (1) extends to a minimum cut, and the type projection of any minimum cut minimizes (1).

5. An exact breakpoint algorithm

The following elementary recursion finds the pieces of \(F\) using \(O(h+1)\) maximum-flow queries.

At a query parameter, return \(F(u)\) and the affine line associated with its largest minimizing set. Begin with queries at zero and one.

Consider an interval \([p,q]\) whose endpoint lines are \(\ell_p,\ell_q\).

  • Identical endpoint lines. Concavity, together with \(F\le\ell_p\), implies \(F=\ell_p\) throughout \([p,q]\).
  • Distinct endpoint lines. Nesting implies their slopes satisfy \(\beta_p>\beta_q\). Their intersection lies in \([p,q]\), at

\(\displaystyle u_*=\frac{\alpha_q-\alpha_p}{\beta_p-\beta_q}.\)

Query there.

If \(F(u_*)=\ell_p(u_*)=\ell_q(u_*)\), concavity and the affine upper bounds imply \(F=\ell_p\) on \([p,u_*]\) and \(F=\ell_q\) on \([u_*,q]\).

Otherwise, the returned line \(\ell_*\) lies strictly below both lines at \(u_*\). Endpoint optimality then implies

\(\displaystyle \beta_p>\beta_*>\beta_q.\)

Recurse on \([p,u_*]\) and \([u_*,q]\).

Every strict split discovers a new optimal slope. The two resulting slope ranges are disjoint, so that slope cannot be rediscovered in another branch. Since there are at most \(h+1\) optimal slopes, the recursion makes \(O(h+1)\) queries.

Integrating the coordinate derivative

Query the midpoint of every nonempty affine interval; this adds \(O(h+1)\) queries.

The derivative in (6) is constant on each such open interval. To see this, any affine line active at an interior point of an affine piece must have the piece’s slope; otherwise it would fall below the piece on one side. If membership of \(t\) changed within the interval, nesting and \(a_t>0\) would force a strict slope decrease, a contradiction.

Membership of \(t\) is monotone. Hence the derivative of \(R_{A_i}(kz^{t,\pm}(u))\) with respect to \(z_t\) equals \(k\) before one cutoff and zero afterwards, apart from irrelevant breakpoint values. Let \(\theta_{i,t}^{\pm}\in[0,1]\) be the length of the portion where it equals \(k\). This cutoff is zero, one, or a breakpoint found by the recursion.

Equation (9) now gives the exact formulas

\[\displaystyle L_t=k\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_i\theta_{i,t}^{-},\qquad U_t=k\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_i\theta_{i,t}^{+}.\tag{12}\]

There are two paths per type and at most \(m\) relevant prefixes. Thus computing all endpoints requires \(O(\tau m(d+1))\) maximum-flow computations.

Lower-endpoint networks retain the full support: only \(|H_t|\) source capacities vary, but the flow computations can involve all \(\tau\) types.

6. Encoding lengths and running time

We must also bound bit lengths; polynomially many flow calls alone would not suffice.

Take a common denominator \(D\) for the input masses and supplies, for example the product of their encoded denominators. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). After multiplication by \(D\), every coefficient \(\alpha_S,\beta_S\) in (11) is an integer of \(O(L)\) bits. This includes multiplication by the binary-encoded integer \(k\) and summation over subsets.

Every breakpoint queried by the recursion is a ratio of differences of these coefficients. Its numerator and denominator therefore have \(O(L)\) bits. Midpoints also have \(O(L)\)-bit encodings.

At a query parameter \(p/q\), scale the network by \(Dq\). All capacities become nonnegative integers of \(O(L)\) bits. A fixed eligibility-arc capacity

\(\displaystyle M=1+k\sum_s\mu_s+\sum_q\lambda_q\)

is sufficient throughout every endpoint path.

Using Edmonds–Karp, each maximum-flow computation takes \(O(na^2)\) additions and comparisons. Including exact parameter arithmetic and capacity scaling, a conservative per-query bound is \(O(na^2L^2)\) bit operations.

Each cutoff in (12) has \(O(L)\) bits. Summing the at most \(m\) rational terms produces an endpoint with \(O(mL)\) bits and can be done in \(O(m^2L^2)\) bit operations using ordinary binary arithmetic. This cost, and the input preprocessing, fit within

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L^2+\tau m(d+1)na^2L^2\right).\)

Substituting \(E\le m\tau\) gives the bound stated at the beginning. In particular, the exact endpoint computation belongs to \(\mathrm{FP}\), the function version of polynomial time.

The discrete neighbor-profile algorithm in [Lupia et al., Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.04176) enumerates subsets of neighbors. The discrete hardness theorem in [Greco et al., Theorem 3.2](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/083.pdf) concerns averaging coalition marginal contributions. Here each endpoint follows a single increasing capacity path, whose nested minimum cuts give the polynomial bound proved above.
