The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of piecewise-uniform valuation types \(t\), rational masses μ_t summing to \(1\), and a chore supply scaled with population, can a deterministic mechanism partition the aggregate chores into type allocations \(D_t\) such that \(|D_t ∩ V_t| ≤ μ_t |V_t|\) for every type and no cohort can lower its true aggregate cost by changing its reported valuation set?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of the \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\) encoded as binary rationals, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The aggregate chore supply \(C\) is a nonempty finite union of bounded intervals with rational endpoints; total population mass is normalized to \(1\), and supply remains fixed under reports. Each valuation set \(V_t\subseteq C\) is a possibly empty finite union of rational intervals, with marginal cost \(1\) on \(V_t\) and \(0\) elsewhere; aggregate cost of an allocation \(D\) is \(|D\cap V_t|\), where bars denote length.
Support entries have fixed public identities, ordering, and masses. A cohort means one entire support entry; it may replace its reported valuation set by any set in the same domain, while other reports remain fixed. Coinciding reports retain separate cohort identities.
A deterministic mechanism must return a complete partition \((D_t)_t\) of \(C\) into finite unions of rational intervals, disregarding endpoints. At every truthful profile it must satisfy \(|D_t\cap V_t|\leq\mu_t|V_t|\); there is no prescribed total length for \(D_t\).
Cohort strategyproofness means \(|D_t\cap V_t|\leq|D'_t\cap V_t|\) for every unilateral alternative report, where \(D'_t\) is that cohort's allocation after the report. Let \(m\) count the distinct input interval endpoints and \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Does this mass model admit a deterministic proportional, cohort-strategyproof mechanism computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is computing allocations under these requirements \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finitely many labeled agents with arbitrary positive workloads summing to \(1\), split rulership is deterministic, proportional, and strategyproof; no polynomial bit-complexity bound is asserted. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.00456)
For two finite agents with equal workloads, a deterministic truthful, envy-free chore mechanism allocating the entire resource is known. [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7497335/) No result settling the stated mass-model computational question was located.

$$ (c,b,\ldots), \qquad (w_c,w_a+w_b,\ldots), $$

3. A homogeneous-supply lemma
