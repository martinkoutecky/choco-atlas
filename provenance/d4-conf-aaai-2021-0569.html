The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite goods categories \(M\), complete-ranking types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_g\), decide whether there exist masses \(x_{t,B}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq M\) satisfying \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}x_{t,B}=\rho_g\), such that every positive-support pair satisfies EFX and \(R_j(x)=\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}\mathbf{1}[g_j(t)\in B]\) is lexicographically maximal among feasible allocations.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) be a set of \(m\geq1\) goods categories. Each type \(t\) is a strict complete ranking of \(M\), extended lexicographically to bundles; write \(\succeq_t\) for its weak bundle preference and \(g_j(t)\) for its good at rank \(j\).
A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Supplies \(\rho_g\) are binary rationals satisfying \(0<\rho_g\leq1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A feasible allocation consists of real masses \(x_{t,B}\geq0\) for \(t\in T\) and integral bundles \(B\subseteq M\), including the empty bundle, satisfying \(\sum_{B\subseteq M}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}x_{t,B}=\rho_g\). Bundles contain at most one unit of each category; all supplies must be allocated.
It is EFX if, for every ordered pair \((t,B),(u,C)\) with \(x_{t,B}>0\) and \(x_{u,C}>0\), and every \(g\in C\), one has \(B\succeq_t C\setminus\{g\}\). This includes comparisons within a type.
Define \(R_j(x)=\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}\mathbf{1}[g_j(t)\in B]\). An allocation is rank-maximal if \((R_1(x),\ldots,R_m(x))\) is lexicographically maximal over all feasible allocations, without imposing EFX on the benchmark.

Is deciding whether an EFX and rank-maximal allocation exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents and distinct indivisible goods, existence of an EFX and rank-maximal allocation under strict lexicographic preferences is NP-complete. [Hosseini–Sikdar–Vaish–Xia](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i6.16689)
Related continuous work studies envy-freeness and Pareto efficiency for divisible shares, rather than EFX between supported integral bundles. [Schulman–Vazirani](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2015.543) No result settling exactly the problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified strict complete rankings and their lexicographic extension to bundles.
  • Restricted supplies to positive per-capita quantities at most one: every listed category is present, and bundles permit at most one unit per category.
  • Made support-pair EFX directional and universal, including comparisons between different supported bundles of the same type; empty envied bundles impose no condition.
  • Specified that rank-maximality is measured against all supply-feasible allocations, independently of EFX, as in the discrete definition.
  • Allowed real allocation masses and empty bundles; defined L as total input bit length and required a nonempty goods set.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when all types have the same dyadic mass and every good has supply equal to that mass.

The reduction adapts the signature-and-dummy construction of [Hosseini–Sikdar–Vaish–Xia](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i6.16689). The essential additional argument proves that arbitrary splitting of population masses cannot activate both signs of a variable.

1. Rank-maximality depends only on marginals

Write \(r_t(g)\) for the rank of good \(g\) under type \(t\), and define its allocation marginal by

\[ y_{t,g}:=\sum_{B:g\in B}x_{t,B}. \]

The feasible marginal vectors are exactly those satisfying

\[ 0\leq y_{t,g}\leq\mu_t, \qquad \sum_t y_{t,g}=\rho_g. \]

Necessity follows directly from allocation feasibility. For sufficiency, put \(p_{t,g}=y_{t,g}/\mu_t\) and define

\[ x_{t,B} = \mu_t \prod_{g\in B}p_{t,g} \prod_{g\notin B}(1-p_{t,g}). \]

Summing this product distribution over all bundles gives mass \(\mu_t\); summing over bundles containing \(g\) gives marginal \(y_{t,g}\). Thus these marginals are realizable, including when some probabilities equal zero or one.

For each good and rank, define

\[ c_{g,j}:=\sum_{t:r_t(g)=j}\mu_t. \]

The lexicographically optimal allocation of the supply of \(g\) fills these capacities in increasing rank order. Its aggregate mass at rank \(j\) is

\[ a_{g,j} = \min\left\{ c_{g,j}, \max\left\{0,\rho_g-\sum_{k<j}c_{g,k}\right\} \right\}. \]

Indeed, after the earlier ranks have been maximized, rank \(j\) can receive at most its capacity and the remaining supply. This bound is attainable by assigning that much mass among the types at rank \(j\).

Consequently, the optimal rank signature is

\[ R_j^*=\sum_g a_{g,j}. \]

To justify that optimizing each good separately optimizes their sum, let \(z_{g,j}\) be the rank aggregates of any feasible marginal allocation. Each vector \(z_g\) is lexicographically at most \(a_g\). If any differs, take the earliest coordinate at which any differs. At that coordinate every difference \(a_g-z_g\) is nonnegative, and at least one is positive; all earlier differences vanish. Hence \(\sum_g z_g\) is strictly lexicographically below \(\sum_g a_g\).

In particular:

Minimum-rank observation. Suppose every type has mass \(q\) and every good has supply \(q\). An allocation is rank-maximal exactly when each good is assigned exclusively to types ranking it highest among the support.

For each good, at least one highest-ranking type has enough capacity to receive its whole supply. Thus its optimal rank vector places mass \(q\) at that minimum rank and zero at all other ranks.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

The preceding formula computes \(R^*\) in polynomial time using rational arithmetic.

Suppose a feasible EFX allocation with signature \(R^*\) exists. Restrict attention to its supported type-bundle pairs. On these columns impose:

  • the \(\tau\) type-mass equalities;
  • the \(m\) supply equalities;
  • the \(m\) equalities \(R_j=R_j^*\).

This is a system \(Az=b\), \(z\geq0\), with at most \(d:=\tau+2m\) rows and entries of \(A\) in \(\{0,1\}\).

Choose a feasible solution having the smallest number of positive coordinates. Its positive columns are linearly independent. Otherwise, a nonzero dependence among them gives a direction preserving all equalities. Choosing its sign so that some coordinate decreases, and moving until the first coordinate becomes zero, preserves nonnegativity and reduces the support, a contradiction. Therefore the solution has at most \(d\) positive coordinates.

Its masses have polynomial rational encoding length. To see this explicitly, let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses and supplies. Then \(\log Q\leq L\), and every coordinate of \(b\), including \(R_j^*\), is an integer multiple of \(1/Q\). Select a nonsingular square submatrix on the independent columns. Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s determinant bound give encoding length \(O(L+d\log d)\) for each resulting mass.

The new support is a subset of the original support, so EFX is preserved: deleting supported pairs cannot introduce a violating comparison.

A certificate therefore lists at most \(\tau+2m\) type-bundle pairs and polynomially encoded rational masses. Feasibility, equality with \(R^*\), and every required EFX comparison can all be checked in polynomial time. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. Reduction from \(\mathrm{3SAT}\)

Let a \(\mathrm{3SAT}\) instance have variables \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) and nonempty clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_s\). Choose the smallest power of two

\[ r\geq\max\{n,s,2\}, \]

and add unused variables through \(X_r\). This padding preserves satisfiability and is polynomial in size.

For a sign \(\sigma\in\{+,-\}\), let \(X_i^\sigma\) denote \(X_i\) or \(\neg X_i\), respectively.

Create four types per variable:

\[ \ell_i^+,\quad \ell_i^-,\quad d_i^+,\quad d_i^-. \]

The \(\ell_i^\sigma\) are literal types and the \(d_i^\sigma\) are dummy types. Every type has mass

\[ q:=\frac1{4r}. \]

Create the following goods:

  • signature goods \(s_i^+,s_i^-\);
  • top and bottom dummy goods \(h_i,b_i\);
  • clause goods \(c_j\).

Every good has supply \(q\). Thus \(\tau=4r\), \(m=4r+s\), the type masses sum to one, and all masses and supplies are dyadic rationals.

Fix a reference order consisting of all signature goods, then all top dummy goods, then \(c_1,\ldots,c_s\), then all bottom dummy goods.

Literal rankings

Type \(\ell_i^\sigma\) ranks \(s_i^\sigma\) first.

For each \(j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}\), its position \(j+1\) contains:

  • \(c_j\), if \(X_i^\sigma\) occurs in \(C_j\);
  • otherwise, the next unused signature good other than \(s_i^\sigma\), in reference order.

There are enough filler signatures because \(s\leq r\leq2r-1\). Append all remaining goods in reference order.

This defines a strict complete ranking. Every incident clause good \(c_j\) has rank \(j+1\), while every nonincident clause good lies after position \(s+1\).

Dummy rankings

Type \(d_i^\sigma\) begins with

\[ h_i\succ s_i^\sigma\succ b_i, \]

and appends all remaining goods in reference order.

All constructed types are distinct. The construction and its encoding have polynomial size.

4. What rank-maximality forces

The highest-ranking types for each good are exactly as follows.

| Good | Minimum rank | Types attaining it |
|---|---:|---|
| \(s_i^\sigma\) | \(1\) | \(\ell_i^\sigma\) |
| \(h_i\) | \(1\) | \(d_i^+,d_i^-\) |
| \(b_i\) | \(3\) | \(d_i^+,d_i^-\) |
| \(c_j\) | \(j+1\) | \(\ell_i^\sigma\) with \(X_i^\sigma\) occurring in \(C_j\) |

Here are the relevant checks. Only the indicated types rank their signature or top dummy good first. Every literal type places all signatures and top dummy goods before every bottom dummy good, so its bottom-good ranks exceed \(3\). Other dummy types place \(b_i\) after their first three positions.

For clauses, incident literal types have the prescribed rank \(j+1\), while nonincident literal types rank them after position \(s+1\). In every dummy ranking, \(3r+1\) goods precede the clause block, so \(c_j\) has rank \(3r+1+j>j+1\).

By the minimum-rank observation, every rank-maximal allocation therefore has these support restrictions:

\[ B=\{s_i^\sigma\}\cup D \quad\text{for a supported bundle of }\ell_i^\sigma, \]

where

\[ D\subseteq\{c_j:X_i^\sigma\text{ occurs in }C_j\}, \]

and

\[ B\subseteq\{h_i,b_i\} \quad\text{for a supported bundle of }d_i^\sigma. \]

The signature must occur in every supported bundle of its literal type: its supply \(q\) is allocated exclusively to that type, whose total mass is also \(q\).

5. EFX prevents both signs from becoming active

Call \(\ell_i^\sigma\) active if some positively supported bundle of that type contains a clause good.

Lemma. In any EFX and rank-maximal allocation, activity of \(\ell_i^\sigma\) forces the entire mass \(q\) of \(d_i^\sigma\) to receive \(h_i\).

Proof. Choose a positively supported literal bundle \(C\) containing a clause good \(c_j\). It also contains \(s_i^\sigma\).

Suppose \(d_i^\sigma\) has a positively supported bundle \(A\) without \(h_i\). By the support restrictions, \(A\subseteq\{b_i\}\). The bundle \(C\setminus\{c_j\}\) still contains \(s_i^\sigma\).

Under \(d_i^\sigma\), the first-ranked good \(h_i\) occurs in neither bundle, while the second-ranked good \(s_i^\sigma\) occurs only in \(C\setminus\{c_j\}\). Hence

\[ C\setminus\{c_j\}\succ_{d_i^\sigma}A, \]

contradicting EFX. Thus every supported bundle of \(d_i^\sigma\) contains \(h_i\), proving the lemma.

Since \(h_i\) has supply \(q\), both \(\ell_i^+\) and \(\ell_i^-\) cannot be active: the lemma would require its two dummy types to receive total mass \(2q\) of \(h_i\).

This argument allows arbitrary real masses and arbitrary splitting within every type.

6. Soundness

Suppose the constructed instance admits an EFX and rank-maximal allocation.

Each clause good \(c_j\) has positive supply. Its allocation is restricted to the literal types corresponding to literals occurring in \(C_j\). Therefore at least one such literal type is active.

Assign \(X_i\) to be true if \(\ell_i^+\) is active, false if \(\ell_i^-\) is active, and arbitrarily if neither is active. Section 5 ensures that these choices are consistent.

Every clause has an active incident literal, and the chosen assignment makes that literal true. Thus the original formula is satisfiable.

7. Completeness

Conversely, suppose the formula has a satisfying assignment. Extend it arbitrarily to the padded variables.

Give each type a single bundle, with its entire mass \(q\), as follows:

  • give \(s_i^\sigma\) to \(\ell_i^\sigma\);
  • give \(h_i\) to the dummy type whose sign is true;
  • give \(b_i\) to the dummy type whose sign is false;
  • for each clause, choose one true literal occurring in it and give its clause good to the corresponding literal type.

Every good belongs to exactly one such bundle, so its allocated mass is \(q\). The allocation is feasible. Every good goes to a minimum-rank type from the table, so it is rank-maximal.

It remains to verify EFX. A singleton target bundle becomes empty after its good is removed, and every bundle is weakly preferred to the empty bundle. Hence only target bundles containing at least two goods require further checking. Such a target is a literal bundle consisting of its signature and one or more clause goods, belonging to a true literal.

For every observing type:

  • A different literal type receives its own first-ranked signature, which is absent from the target. It therefore strictly prefers its own bundle to the target.
  • The target’s own literal type receives that same bundle and weakly prefers it to every bundle obtained by deletion.
  • A dummy type receiving \(h_i\) receives its first-ranked good, absent from every literal bundle, and strictly prefers its own bundle to the target.
  • A dummy type \(d_i^\sigma\) receiving \(b_i\) has a false associated literal. Consequently, the target cannot belong to \(\ell_i^\sigma\). Its signature and all its clause goods are ranked below \(b_i\) by this dummy type, so the dummy strictly prefers \(\{b_i\}\) to the target.

Whenever the observer weakly prefers its bundle to the whole target, it also weakly prefers it after any deletion, by monotonicity and transitivity of lexicographic preference. This checks every ordered supported pair and every required deletion, including within-type comparisons. The allocation is EFX.

8. Conclusion

The reduction is polynomial and preserves satisfiability in both directions. Its soundness explicitly covers real allocation masses and population splitting. Together with membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), it proves that the stated decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

The argument that separate optimization yields the global optimal signature is valid. At the earliest coordinate where any good’s aggregate differs from its optimum, every differing good has a deficit and no good has a surplus. The minimum-rank observation follows correctly. When each type has mass \(q\) and each good has supply \(q\), any type attaining that good’s minimum rank has enough marginal capacity to absorb its entire supply.
