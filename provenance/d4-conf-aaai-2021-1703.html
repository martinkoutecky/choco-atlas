The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 3.16 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a star \(S_m\), a finite set of additive valuation types \(T\), a distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\), and per-location capacities \(q_v\), find the maximum \(\alpha\) for which there is a mass allocation \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\) over connected integral bundles \(B\subseteq V(S_m)\), satisfying \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B\ni v}x_{t,B}=q_v\), and \(u_t(B)\ge \alpha\,\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S_m\) be a star on \(m\ge2\) vertices, with center \(c\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has nonnegative binary-rational additive vertex values \(u_t(v)\). Capacities \(q_v\in[0,1]\) are binary rationals.
Define the unconstrained mass maximin share by
\(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)=\max\{z\ge0:\exists(y_B)_{B\subseteq V},\ y_B\ge0,\ \sum_B y_B=1,\ \sum_{B\ni v}y_B=q_v\ \forall v,\ y_B>0\Rightarrow u_t(B)\ge z\}\).
Allowed allocation bundles form \(\mathcal C=\{\varnothing\}\cup\{\{v\}:v\in V\}\cup\{B\subseteq V:c\in B\}\); thus empty bundles are permitted and all nonempty bundles are connected and integral.
For \(\alpha\ge0\), an admissible mass allocation satisfies \(x_{t,B}\ge0\), \(\sum_{B\in\mathcal C}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B\in\mathcal C:v\in B}x_{t,B}=q_v\), and \(x_{t,B}>0\Rightarrow u_t(B)\ge\alpha\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\). The optimization problem asks for the largest feasible \(\alpha\), allowing infeasibility or an unbounded value; zero benchmarks impose no positive utility requirement.
Is deciding whether an admissible mass allocation exists for a given binary-rational \(\alpha\ge0\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, including \(\alpha\).
For discrete stars with \(m\ge n\ge2\), a polynomial-time algorithm guarantees each agent \(1/(m-n+1)\) of her unconstrained MMS, and this universal factor is tight ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.05433v3)).
The related AnyPrice share uses distributions over integral bundles with uniform marginal upper bounds; computing it is NP-hard even for additive values and entitlement \(1/2\) ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.04304)). This concerns a benchmark, not the connected mass-allocation decision problem above; no result settling that exact problem was located.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions, already with support size \(\tau=2\) and fixed \(\alpha=2\). Both benchmarks in the reduction are explicitly known and positive.

For general inputs, an exact algorithm runs in \(2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\) time. Thus the problem is polynomial-time solvable for fixed \(m\), but a polynomial-time algorithm for unrestricted \(m\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Reduction from PARTITION

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem PARTITION: given positive binary integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\), decide whether some subset has half their total sum. See [these university notes](https://ktiml.mff.cuni.cz/~fink/teaching/computability_2017_18/tutorial_5.pdf). We may assume the total is even: multiplying every input integer by two preserves the answer and ensures this condition.

Write \(\sum_{i=1}^k a_i=2T\), where \(T>0\). Define

\(r=\lceil\log_2(k+2)\rceil,\qquad p=2^{-r},\qquad D=2T+1,\qquad K=2D-T=3T+2.\)

In particular, \(0<p\le 1/(k+2)\).

Construct a star with center \(c\), one distinguished leaf \(h\), and leaves \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k\). Hence \(m=k+2\). Set

\(q_c=p,\qquad q_h=1-p,\qquad q_{\ell_i}=p/2.\)

There are exactly two types, denoted \(A\) and \(B\), with masses

\(\mu_A=p,\qquad \mu_B=1-p.\)

Their additive vertex values are:

| Vertex | Type \(A\) | Type \(B\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(c\) | \(K\) | \(1/2\) |
| \(h\) | \(D\) | \(1\) |
| \(\ell_i\) | \(a_i\) | \(0\) |

Finally, set \(\alpha=2\). All values, masses, and capacities satisfy the required hypotheses; indeed, all rational numbers used are dyadic.

2. Exact benchmark values

We prove

\(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_A(q)=D,\qquad \operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_B(q)=1/2.\)

A common distribution proving both lower bounds

Let \(I=\{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k\}\). Consider the distribution

\(y_{\{h\}}=1-p,\qquad y_{\{c\}}=p/2,\qquad y_{\{c\}\cup I}=p/2,\)

with all other masses zero.

Its masses sum to one. Its center marginal is \(p\), its distinguished-leaf marginal is \(1-p\), and every original-leaf marginal is \(p/2\). Thus it has exactly the required marginals.

For type \(A\), its three supported bundles have utilities \(D\), \(K\), and \(K+2T\). Since \(K-D=T+1>0\), all have utility at least \(D\). Therefore

\(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_A(q)\ge D.\)

For type \(B\), the same bundles have utilities \(1\), \(1/2\), and \(1/2\). Consequently,

\(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_B(q)\ge 1/2.\)

Upper bound for type \(A\)

Consider any distribution over integral bundles having marginals \(q\), and let \(F\) be a random bundle drawn from it. The union bound gives

\(\Pr(F\cap(\{c\}\cup I)\ne\varnothing)\le q_c+\sum_{i=1}^k q_{\ell_i}=p(1+k/2)\le 1/2.\)

Thus with positive probability, \(F\) contains neither the center nor any original leaf. Such a bundle is either \(\varnothing\) or \(\{h\}\), and its type-\(A\) utility is at most \(D\).

Every distribution with the required marginals therefore has a positive-mass bundle of utility at most \(D\). No benchmark strictly greater than \(D\) is feasible. This proves

\(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_A(q)=D.\)

Upper bound for type \(B\)

In any distribution with the required marginals, the probability of omitting \(h\) is \(1-q_h=p>0\). Every bundle omitting \(h\) has type-\(B\) utility at most \(1/2\), because only the center can contribute positive utility.

Hence no benchmark strictly greater than \(1/2\) is feasible, proving

\(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_B(q)=1/2.\)

3. Every admissible allocation yields a partition

Suppose an admissible allocation exists at \(\alpha=2\). Its required utilities are therefore \(2D\) for type \(A\) and \(1\) for type \(B\).

Type \(A\) consumes all center capacity

Every allowed bundle omitting the center is empty or a singleton leaf. None meets type \(A\)'s requirement:

\(u_A(\varnothing)=0<2D,\qquad u_A(\{h\})=D<2D,\qquad a_i\le 2T<2D.\)

Thus every positive-mass type-\(A\) bundle contains \(c\). Its total mass is \(p\), exactly the center capacity. Therefore no positive-mass type-\(B\) bundle contains \(c\).

Type \(B\) consumes all distinguished-leaf capacity

Every bundle omitting \(h\) has type-\(B\) utility at most \(1/2<1\). Hence every positive-mass type-\(B\) bundle contains \(h\).

The type-\(B\) mass is \(1-p\), exactly \(q_h\). Consequently, no positive-mass type-\(A\) bundle contains \(h\).

Moreover, a type-\(B\) bundle contains \(h\) and omits \(c\). Connectivity then forces it to be precisely \(\{h\}\). In particular, type \(B\) receives no original leaves.

Every type-\(A\) bundle contains a subset of value exactly \(T\)

For an allocation bundle \(F\), write

\(s(F)=\sum_{\{i:\ell_i\in F\}}a_i.\)

Every positive-mass type-\(A\) bundle contains \(c\) and omits \(h\), so its utility requirement implies

\(K+s(F)\ge 2D,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad s(F)\ge 2D-K=T.\)

All original-leaf capacity goes to type \(A\). Therefore

\(\sum_{F\in\mathcal C}x_{A,F}s(F)=\sum_{i=1}^k a_iq_{\ell_i}=(p/2)(2T)=pT.\)

Since \(\sum_{F\in\mathcal C}x_{A,F}=p\), subtraction gives

\(\sum_{F\in\mathcal C}x_{A,F}(s(F)-T)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Thus every positive-mass type-\(A\) bundle satisfies \(s(F)=T\). At least one such bundle exists because \(\mu_A=p>0\). Its original leaves give a subset of the input integers summing to \(T\), and hence a solution to PARTITION.

4. Every partition yields an admissible allocation

Conversely, suppose \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in J}a_i=T\). Its complement also has sum \(T\).

Set

\(x_{A,\{c\}\cup\{\ell_i:i\in J\}}=p/2,\)

\(x_{A,\{c\}\cup\{\ell_i:i\notin J\}}=p/2,\)

\(x_{B,\{h\}}=1-p,\)

and set all other allocation masses to zero.

All three bundles are allowed. The type masses are correct; the center has marginal \(p\), the distinguished leaf has marginal \(1-p\), and each original leaf occurs in exactly one of the two type-\(A\) bundles, giving marginal \(p/2\).

Each type-\(A\) bundle has utility \(K+T=2D\), and the type-\(B\) bundle has utility \(1\). These are exactly the required utilities at \(\alpha=2\).

Thus the allocation instance is feasible if and only if the PARTITION instance is a yes-instance.

5. Encoding and hardness conclusion

If the PARTITION input has bit length \(\ell\), the construction has \(m=k+2\), \(\tau=2\), and output bit length \(O(\ell+k\log k)\). The numbers \(T,D,K\) are obtained by polynomial-time integer arithmetic, and \(p\) uses \(O(\log k)\) bits. The reduction is therefore polynomial-time.

This proves hardness of the allocation decision itself, even on instances whose benchmarks are explicitly computable by the preceding elementary arguments. The reduction establishes hardness at \(\alpha=2\); it does not establish hardness for the narrower restriction \(0\le\alpha\le1\).

6. Exact running-time upper bound

Computing the benchmarks

For each listed type \(t\), enumerate the \(2^m\) subsets and their utilities. For a candidate value \(z\), benchmark feasibility is the rational linear program with variables \(y_F\) for subsets satisfying \(u_t(F)\ge z\), subject to

\(\sum_F y_F=1,\qquad \sum_{F\ni v}y_F=q_v\quad(v\in V),\qquad y_F\ge0.\)

The benchmark is the largest feasible subset-utility value. Indeed, every distribution has finite nonempty support, and its minimum supported utility is one of these values. Conversely, a feasible linear program supplies the required distribution.

Feasibility is monotone in \(z\), so sorting the candidate values and binary-searching them requires \(O(m)\) linear-program feasibility calls per type. Value zero is always feasible: independent vertex inclusion with probabilities \(q_v\) supplies a distribution with the required marginals.

Deciding allocation feasibility

Once the benchmarks are known, retain exactly the pairs \((t,F)\) with \(F\in\mathcal C\) and \(u_t(F)\ge\alpha\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\). Solve the linear program expressing the type-mass and vertex-capacity equalities over these pairs.

There are \(|\mathcal C|=2^{m-1}+m\) allowed bundles, at most \(\tau(2^{m-1}+m)\) allocation variables, and \(\tau+m\) equality constraints. The constructed rational coefficients have bit length polynomial in \(L\). Polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore gives total running time

\(2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L).\)

The same bound handles exact optimization. First test feasibility at \(\alpha=0\); failure means infeasibility for every \(\alpha\). If all benchmarks vanish and this test succeeds, the value is unbounded. Otherwise, the finite optimum is among zero and the ratios \(u_t(F)/\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) for positive-benchmark types and allowed bundles. Between consecutive ratios, the eligible pairs are unchanged, so sorting these ratios and binary-searching feasibility finds the exact optimum within the same running-time bound.
