The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a star \(S_m\), a finite set of additive valuation types \(T\), a distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\), and per-location capacities \(q_v\), find the maximum \(\alpha\) for which there is a mass allocation \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\) over connected integral bundles \(B\subseteq V(S_m)\), satisfying \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B\ni v}x_{t,B}=q_v\), and \(u_t(B)\ge \alpha\,\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S_m\) be a star on \(m\ge2\) vertices, with center \(c\). Each type \(t\) has nonnegative binary-rational additive values \(u_t(v)\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
Let \(q_v\in[0,1]\) be binary-rational capacities. Permitted bundles are the empty set, singleton leaves, and sets containing \(c\); goods within each bundle remain indivisible.
A mass allocation has real variables \(x_{t,B}\ge0\), satisfying \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B\ni v}x_{t,B}=q_v\).
For \(\alpha\ge0\), require \(u_t(B)\ge\alpha\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\). The benchmark \(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) remains undefined in the supplied specification.
Is computing the maximum feasible \(\alpha\) polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), or NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total input bit length?
For discrete stars with \(m\ge n\ge2\), a polynomial-time algorithm guarantees every agent \(1/(m-n+1)\) of her unconstrained MMS; this universal guarantee is tight ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05433v3)).
AnyPrice share is a distinct extension of MMS to arbitrary entitlements and can strictly exceed MMS even for equal entitlements ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04304)); it does not define the missing benchmark here. No result settling this exact mass-allocation question was verified.
