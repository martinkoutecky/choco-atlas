The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a star \(S_m\), a finite set of additive valuation types \(T\), a distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\), and per-location capacities \(q_v\), find the maximum \(\alpha\) for which there is a mass allocation \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\) over connected integral bundles \(B\subseteq V(S_m)\), satisfying \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B\ni v}x_{t,B}=q_v\), and \(u_t(B)\ge \alpha\,\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S_m\) be a star on \(m\) vertices. Types have nonnegative additive valuations, specified by binary rational vertex values.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each vertex has a binary rational capacity \(q_v\in[0,1]\). Let \(\mathcal C\) contain the integral vertex subsets inducing connected subgraphs, together with the empty bundle.
A mass allocation consists of \(x_{t,B}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{B\in\mathcal C}x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B\ni v}x_{t,B}=q_v\).
For a factor \(\alpha\ge0\), require \(x_{t,B}>0\Rightarrow u_t(B)\ge\alpha\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\). The benchmark \(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) remains undefined in the supplied specification.
Is finding the optimal feasible factor \(\alpha\) polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total binary input length?
For finite societies with \(n\ge2\) agents and \(m\ge n\) goods on a star, a polynomial-time algorithm guarantees every agent \(1/(m-n+1)\) of her ordinary MMS; this universal factor is tight. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.05433v3)
For a divisible graphical cake on a forest, graph-constrained MMS allocations exist; that result concerns divisible edges and finitely many agents. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0023.pdf)
