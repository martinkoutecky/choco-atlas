Proposition-4 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given buyer and seller types with rational masses summing to \(1\), rational strictly concave quadratic utilities increasing over feasible ranges, and seller generation caps, compute the unique maximizer of weighted social welfare subject to mass-weighted energy balance and the caps, together with a supporting clearing price.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There is one divisible energy commodity, so \(m=1\). A society is given by its support: disjoint, nonempty lists \(B\) and \(S\) of buyer and seller types, with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\) and \(\nu_j\) satisfying \(\sum_{i\in B}\mu_i+\sum_{j\in S}\nu_j=1\). The support size is \(\tau=|B|+|S|\), not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Each seller has binary-rational generation \(g_j\geq0\), available for consumption or sale. Each buyer has utility \(u_i\), and each seller has utility \(v_j\) for retained energy; these are strictly concave quadratic polynomials with binary-rational coefficients. Put \(G=\sum_{j\in S}\nu_jg_j\); require \(u_i'>0\) on \([0,G/\mu_i]\), \(v_j'>0\) on \([0,g_j]\), and some \(i,j\) with \(g_j>0\) and \(u_i'(0)>v_j'(g_j)\).
A type receives a common per-capita allocation: buyer consumption \(d_i\geq0\) and seller supply \(0\leq a_j\leq g_j\). Energy balance is \(\sum_i\mu_i d_i=\sum_j\nu_j a_j\), and welfare is \(W(d,a)=\sum_i\mu_i u_i(d_i)+\sum_j\nu_j v_j(g_j-a_j)\). There are no transmission losses or surcharges.
Compute the unique welfare-maximizing allocation and a supporting clearing price \(p\geq0\): each \(d_i\) maximizes \(u_i(x)-px\) over \(x\geq0\), and each \(a_j\) maximizes \(v_j(g_j-y)+py\) over \(0\leq y\leq g_j\). Output quantities are exact binary rationals; any supporting price is allowed.

Is this exact allocation-and-price problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents, price-taking equilibrium allocations are unique and maximize aggregate buyer utility plus seller utility from retained energy; this is an equilibrium characterization, not a polynomial-time complexity bound. [Faqiry and Das](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.06564)
That result supplies no complexity classification for the rational-support, quadratic-utility problem defined here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted masses as one distribution across both buyer and seller types, with total mass one, and allocations and generation as per-capita quantities.
  • Restored disjoint nonempty buyer and seller populations, nonnegative consumption and supply, and the positive-trade marginal-utility condition; the witnessing seller must have positive generation.
  • Interpreted seller generation caps as endowments that may be retained or sold, so seller welfare is utility of generation minus supply.
  • Restricted the increasing-utility requirement to the attainable consumption intervals, since a strictly concave quadratic cannot be increasing on the entire nonnegative half-line.
  • Defined a supporting price through individual price-taking payoff maximization, including zero-consumption and supply-boundary cases, rather than imposing interior marginal-utility equalities everywhere.
  • Specified exact binary-rational output and allowed any supporting price; only the allocation is required to be unique.
  • Set the commodity parameter to one and defined the total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Conclusion and encoding convention

The problem is polynomial-time solvable for \(m=1\). An exact algorithm uses \(O(\tau^2+\tau\log\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations, with all intermediate and output bit lengths polynomial in \(\tau,L\).

Here, an exact binary rational means a rational represented by an integer numerator and a positive integer denominator, both encoded in binary. If outputs were restricted to terminating binary expansions, valid instances could have unrepresentable answers; an example appears below.

1. Individual demand and supply

Write the strictly concave quadratic utilities as

\(u_i(x)=c_i+b_i x-\frac{\beta_i}{2}x^2,\qquad v_j(z)=e_j+t_jz-\frac{r_j}{2}z^2,\)

where \(\beta_i>0\) and \(r_j>0\). All these coefficients are rational and can be obtained from the input with polynomial bit length.

Define

\(\ell_j=t_j-r_jg_j=v_j'(g_j).\)

The assumed positivity of derivatives gives \(b_i=u_i'(0)>0\) and \(\ell_j>0\).

For a price \(p\geq0\), buyer \(i\)'s objective has derivative \(b_i-p-\beta_i x\). Its unique maximizer over \(x\geq0\) is therefore

\(d_i(p)=\max\left\{0,\frac{b_i-p}{\beta_i}\right\}.\)

Seller \(j\)'s objective, as a function of supply \(y\), has derivative

\(\frac{d}{dy}\bigl(v_j(g_j-y)+py\bigr)=p-\ell_j-r_jy.\)

Consequently, its unique maximizer over \(0\leq y\leq g_j\) is

\(a_j(p)=\min\left\{g_j,\max\left\{0,\frac{p-\ell_j}{r_j}\right\}\right\}.\)

These formulas also handle \(g_j=0\), when supply is identically zero.

2. Existence and uniqueness of a clearing price

Define aggregate excess demand by

\(F(p)=\sum_{i\in B}\mu_i d_i(p)-\sum_{j\in S}\nu_j a_j(p).\)

Each demand function is continuous and nonincreasing; each supply function is continuous and nondecreasing. Thus \(F\) is continuous and nonincreasing.

Let

\(P=\max_{i\in B}b_i>0.\)

At zero price, all sellers supply zero because \(\ell_j>0\), whereas all buyers demand positive quantities. Hence

\(F(0)=\sum_{i\in B}\frac{\mu_i b_i}{\beta_i}>0.\)

At price \(P\), every buyer demands zero. The profitable-trade assumption supplies types \(i,j\) with \(g_j>0\) and \(b_i>\ell_j\). Since \(P\geq b_i>\ell_j\), the supply formula gives \(a_j(P)>0\). Therefore \(F(P)<0\).

By continuity, some \(p^*\in(0,P)\) satisfies \(F(p^*)=0\).

In fact, this price is unique. Choose a buyer \(i_0\) with \(b_{i_0}=P\). For \(0\leq p<q\leq P\),

\(d_{i_0}(p)-d_{i_0}(q)=\frac{q-p}{\beta_{i_0}}>0.\)

All other buyers contribute nonnegative demand differences, and all sellers contribute nonnegative supply increases. Hence

\(F(p)-F(q)\geq\frac{\mu_{i_0}}{\beta_{i_0}}(q-p)>0.\)

Thus \(F\) is strictly decreasing on \([0,P]\). For \(p>P\), demand remains zero and supply is at least its positive value at \(P\), so there are no additional clearing prices.

3. Exact algorithm

Buyer demand changes its affine formula only at \(b_i\). Seller supply changes its affine formula only at

\(\ell_j\quad\text{and}\quad \ell_j+r_jg_j=t_j.\)

Construct the set

\(T=\{0,P\}\cup\bigl(\{b_i:i\in B\}\cup\{\ell_j,t_j:j\in S\}\bigr)\cap[0,P].\)

Remove duplicates and sort it as

\(0=q_0<q_1<\cdots<q_K=P.\)

There are at most \(|B|+2|S|+2\leq2\tau+2\) points. On every interval \([q_k,q_{k+1}]\), the function \(F\) is affine: no individual response has a breakpoint in the interval's interior, and continuity extends its affine formula to the endpoints.

The algorithm is as follows:

  • Evaluate \(F(q_k)\) exactly at every point.
  • If \(F(q_k)=0\), return \(p^*=q_k\).
  • Otherwise, find adjacent points with \(F(q_k)>0>F(q_{k+1})\), and set

\(p^*=q_k+(q_{k+1}-q_k)\frac{F(q_k)}{F(q_k)-F(q_{k+1})}.\)

  • Return \(p^*\), together with all \(d_i(p^*)\) and \(a_j(p^*)\).

The endpoint signs and strict monotonicity ensure that either step 2 or step 3 applies. In step 3, the denominator is positive and the fraction lies strictly between zero and one. Since \(F\) is affine on that interval, the displayed interpolation formula gives exactly \(F(p^*)=0\).

Every operation is rational, so the price and allocations are exact rationals.

4. Welfare optimality and uniqueness

Let \(d_i^*=d_i(p^*)\) and \(a_j^*=a_j(p^*)\). Their nonnegativity and capacity constraints follow from their formulas, and \(F(p^*)=0\) gives energy balance. Thus the constructed allocation is feasible.

For any other feasible allocation \((d,a)\), individual optimality gives

\(u_i(d_i)-p^*d_i\leq u_i(d_i^*)-p^*d_i^*\)

for every buyer, and

\(v_j(g_j-a_j)+p^*a_j\leq v_j(g_j-a_j^*)+p^*a_j^*\)

for every seller.

Multiply these inequalities by the corresponding positive masses and sum them. The resulting inequality is

\(W(d,a)-p^*\sum_i\mu_i d_i+p^*\sum_j\nu_j a_j \leq W(d^*,a^*)-p^*\sum_i\mu_i d_i^*+p^*\sum_j\nu_j a_j^*.\)

Energy balance cancels the payment terms on both sides. Therefore

\(W(d,a)\leq W(d^*,a^*).\)

If the allocations differ, at least one individual quantity differs from its unique payoff maximizer. That individual's inequality is then strict; its mass is positive, and all remaining inequalities hold in the same direction. Consequently,

\((d,a)\neq(d^*,a^*)\ \Longrightarrow\ W(d,a)<W(d^*,a^*).\)

This proves both welfare optimality and uniqueness. The individual maximization formulas also prove that \(p^*\geq0\) is a supporting clearing price.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

Represent each rational in reduced numerator–denominator form.

Every input integer has bit length at most \(L\). Computing \(\beta_i,r_j,\ell_j\) and the breakpoints uses a bounded number of rational operations per type, so each breakpoint has bit length \(O(L)\).

At a breakpoint \(q_k\), each weighted demand or supply term is obtained using a bounded number of operations on rationals of bit length \(O(L)\). Each such term therefore has bit length \(O(L)\).

A sum of at most \(\tau\) such rationals has bit length \(O(\tau L)\): a common denominator can be chosen as the product of their denominators, whose bit length is the sum of their denominator bit lengths. The corresponding summed numerator also has bit length \(O(\tau L)\). Reducing the fraction cannot increase either length.

Thus every \(F(q_k)\) has bit length \(O(\tau L)\). The interpolation formula uses a bounded number of operations on these values and the interval endpoints, so \(p^*\) also has bit length \(O(\tau L)\). Substituting it into the individual response formulas gives the same bound for each output quantity.

Sorting takes \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) rational comparisons. Evaluating \(F\) at \(O(\tau)\) points takes \(O(\tau^2)\) rational operations. Exact integer arithmetic, comparison, and fraction reduction take polynomial time in their operand bit lengths. Hence the complete algorithm runs in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), and therefore in \(m,\tau,L\) for the stipulated \(m=1\).

The algorithm operates directly on support types and their masses; it never expands the masses into individual agents.

6. If “binary rational” means a terminating binary expansion

That stricter output interpretation would make the problem impossible on some valid inputs.

Take one buyer and one seller, both of mass \(1/2\), with \(g=1\), and utilities

\(u(x)=3x-\frac{x^2}{2},\qquad v(z)=3z-z^2.\)

Here \(G/\mu=1\), \(u'(x)=3-x>0\) on \([0,1]\), and \(v'(z)=3-2z>0\) on \([0,1]\). Also \(u'(0)=3>1=v'(1)\), so every stipulated hypothesis holds.

Balance requires \(d=a\). The welfare along this feasible interval is

\(W(a,a)=1+a-\frac34a^2,\qquad 0\leq a\leq1.\)

Its derivative vanishes uniquely at \(a=2/3\), giving

\(d^*=a^*=\frac23,\qquad p^*=\frac73.\)

Neither quantity has a terminating binary expansion. Both have finite binary numerator–denominator encodings, under which the polynomial-time result above applies.
