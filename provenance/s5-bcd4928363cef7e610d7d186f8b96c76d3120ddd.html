Theorem 5 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given atomless populations on sides \(A\) and \(B\), each represented by finitely many types with rational masses and strict rankings over counterpart types, a set of critical \(A\)-types whose entire mass must be matched, and an allowed type pair \((t,s)\), does there exist a feasible measurable one-to-one allocation that is popular against every other feasible allocation and assigns positive mass to \((t,s)\)? Popularity compares the mass of agents on both sides who prefer each allocation’s counterpart type.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: disjoint lists \(T_A,T_B\) of types with positive binary rational masses \(q_u\); \(\tau=|T_A|+|T_B|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses have combined total \(1\); the two sides need not have equal mass. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An input relation \(E\subseteq T_A\times T_B\), with \(m=|E|\), specifies acceptable pairs. Each type strictly ranks its acceptable counterpart types above being unmatched; partners of the same type are indifferent.
Realize each type as a disjoint Lebesgue interval of length \(q_u\). An allocation is a measurable, measure-preserving bijection between measurable subsets of the two sides, with measurable inverse, using only pairs in \(E\), modulo null sets; agents outside these subsets are unmatched.
Given \(C\subseteq T_A\), an allocation is feasible if it matches almost every agent of every type in \(C\). Promise that a feasible allocation exists; \(C\) may be empty.
For allocations \(M,N\), let \(\phi(N,M)\) be the combined measure of agents who strictly prefer their counterpart type in \(N\) to their counterpart type in \(M\), including unmatched status. Comparisons use the same individual agents; indifference contributes no vote.
A feasible allocation \(M\) is popular if \(\phi(N,M)\leq\phi(M,N)\) for every feasible measurable allocation \(N\).

Given these data and \((t,s)\in E\), is deciding whether a popular feasible allocation assigns positive mass to \((t,s)\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents with strict neighbor preferences and critical agents on one side, a specified edge belongs to some popular feasible matching exactly when it belongs to a minimum-size popular feasible matching or a dominant feasible matching; this yields a polynomial-time decision algorithm. [Source](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol248-isaac2022/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2022.54/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2022.54.pdf)
No result settling the exact atomless decision problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified an arbitrary acceptable-pair relation and strict rankings restricted to its neighbors, following the discrete graph model.
  • Placed unmatched status below every acceptable counterpart type; agents are indifferent between partners of the same type.
  • Normalized combined mass to one by common rescaling, preserving potentially unequal side totals and equal voting weight per unit mass.
  • Realized atomless types as Lebesgue intervals and defined one-to-one allocations as measurable measure-preserving partial bijections modulo null sets.
  • Required comparisons to retain individual identities across allocations, with popularity quantified over all feasible measurable allocations.
  • Carried over the assumption that at least one feasible allocation exists; allowed an empty critical set.
  • Defined m as the number of acceptable type pairs and L as total input bit length.

Partial progress: popularity is determined by the positive support

The general complexity question remains unresolved here. The following results give a further structural reduction:

  • A proposed allocation is popular exactly when a directed graph determined by its positive support has no negative cycle.
  • Consequently, popularity of a feasible positive support is independent of the masses.
  • Every yes-instance has a popular allocation whose matched support consists of paths, with at most one unmatched endpoint per path.
  • For arbitrary rational masses, the decision problem is polynomial-time solvable when the acceptable-pair graph is a disjoint union of stars.

All comparisons below concern the same individual agents, as required in the task.

1. Aggregate allocations and realization

For each acceptable pair $e=(t,s)$, let $x_e$ be its matched mass. For each type $u$, let $r_u$ be its unmatched mass. Feasibility is equivalent to
$$ x_e\geq 0,\qquad r_u\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{e\ni u}x_e+r_u=q_u,\qquad r_u=0\quad(u\in C). $$

Indeed, every measurable allocation satisfies these equations by measure preservation. Conversely, partition each type interval into pieces with the prescribed matched and unmatched masses. Pair the two pieces assigned to each acceptable pair by a measure-preserving bijection modulo null sets.

We will also need this realization procedure for pieces selected from an existing allocation. Equal-measure Lebesgue-measurable subsets of intervals admit a measure-preserving bijection with measurable inverse modulo null sets: their cumulative restricted-measure functions identify them, almost everywhere, with an interval of their common measure. Thus the same construction applies to such selected pieces.

Fix a feasible allocation $M$. Write
$$ K=\{e\in E:x_e>0\}. $$
For a type $u$, its set of positive-mass statuses is
$$ S_u=\{v:x_{uv}>0\}\cup \{\bot:r_u>0\}, $$
where $\bot$ denotes being unmatched. Since $q_u>0$, every $S_u$ is nonempty.

For statuses $i,j$ of type $u$, define
$$ \eta_u(j,i)= \begin{cases} 1,&j\succ_u i,\\ -1,&i\succ_u j,\\ 0,&i=j. \end{cases} $$
Every acceptable partner type is above $\bot$. Thus $\eta_u(j,i)$ is the signed vote of an agent moving from status $i$ to status $j$.

2. An exact negative-cycle characterization

Construct a directed multigraph $D(K,r)$ with vertex set
$$ \{0\}\cup K. $$
The vertex $0$ represents unmatched statuses. For $i\in S_u$, define
$$ k(u,i)= \begin{cases} (u,i)\in K,&i\neq\bot,\\ 0,&i=\bot. \end{cases} $$
Here $(u,i)$ denotes the acceptable pair, irrespective of endpoint order.

The graph has the following arcs.

  • For every $(t,s)\in E$, $i\in S_t$, and $j\in S_s$, add

$$ k(t,i)\longrightarrow k(s,j) $$
with weight
$$ -\eta_t(s,i)-\eta_s(t,j). $$

  • For every $e=(t,s)\in K$ with $t\notin C$, add

$$ e\longrightarrow 0 $$
with weight $1$.

  • For every $e=(t,s)\in K$, add

$$ 0\longrightarrow e $$
with weight $1$.

The last two kinds of arcs allow a challenger to leave a previously matched agent unmatched. There is no such option for a critical agent on side $A$.

Theorem 1. The allocation $M$ is popular if and only if $D(K,r)$ has no negative directed cycle.

Proof: absence of negative cycles implies popularity

Every vertex $e\in K$ is reachable from $0$ through an arc of weight $1$. If the graph has no negative cycle, let $h_e$ be the shortest-path distance from $0$ to $e$, and put $h_0=0$. Every arc $a\longrightarrow b$ of weight $w$ satisfies
$$ h_b-h_a\leq w. $$

Assign a value to each positive old-status category:
$$ \alpha_{u,i}= \begin{cases} h_{k(u,i)},&u\in T_A,\\ -h_{k(u,i)},&u\in T_B. \end{cases} $$
In particular, $\alpha_{u,\bot}=0$ whenever unmatched status has positive mass.

The matching arcs give
$$ \alpha_{t,i}+\alpha_{s,j} \geq \eta_t(s,i)+\eta_s(t,j) $$
for every acceptable $(t,s)$ and positive old statuses $i\in S_t$, $j\in S_s$.

The remaining arcs give
$$ \alpha_{u,i}\geq -1 $$
for every positive matched status of a noncritical type. Together with $\alpha_{u,\bot}=0$, this says
$$ \alpha_{u,i}\geq\eta_u(\bot,i) \qquad(u\notin C). $$

Now consider any feasible challenger $N$. Partition its matched pairs according to the endpoints’ old statuses in $M$. For mass matched between categories $(t,i)$ and $(s,j)$, its signed vote contribution is at most that mass times
$$ \alpha_{t,i}+\alpha_{s,j}. $$
For mass left unmatched by $N$, its signed vote is at most that mass times $\alpha_{u,i}$. Feasibility ensures that this latter case occurs only for noncritical types.

Summing these bounds counts each agent’s old-category value exactly once. Therefore
$$ \phi(N,M)-\phi(M,N) \leq \sum_u\sum_{i\in S_u}p_{u,i}\alpha_{u,i}, $$
where $p_{u,i}$ is the mass of the old-status category.

The expression on the right is zero. Every positive old matching edge $e=(t,s)$ contributes
$$ x_e\bigl(\alpha_{t,s}+\alpha_{s,t}\bigr) =x_e(h_e-h_e)=0, $$
and every old unmatched category contributes $r_u\alpha_{u,\bot}=0$.

Hence every feasible $N$ satisfies
$$ \phi(N,M)-\phi(M,N)\leq 0. $$
Thus $M$ is popular.

Proof: a negative cycle gives a winning measurable challenger

Suppose the graph has a negative directed cycle. A negative closed walk contains a negative simple cycle, so choose such a cycle $Z$.

Choose $\varepsilon>0$ small enough that:

  • $x_e\geq\varepsilon$ for every matched-edge vertex $e$ on $Z$;
  • $r_u\geq\varepsilon$ for every old unmatched category used by a matching arc on $Z$.

All these required masses are positive by construction.

For every vertex $e=(t,s)\in K$ on $Z$, remove a measurable portion of $M$ of mass $\varepsilon$ on that edge. This frees mass $\varepsilon$ at both endpoints, with old statuses $s$ and $t$, respectively.

At vertex $e$:

  • its outgoing arc either uses the freed $A$-side agents in a new matched pair or leaves them unmatched through an arc $e\longrightarrow 0$;
  • its incoming arc either uses the freed $B$-side agents in a new matched pair or leaves them unmatched through an arc $0\longrightarrow e$.

Because $Z$ is simple, each such freed portion is used exactly once.

A matching arc incident with $0$ uses a portion of the corresponding old unmatched category. There is at most one such portion on each side because $0$ occurs at most once on a simple cycle. Match the indicated pieces using the realization construction from Section 1. Keep every other original pair unchanged.

The resulting allocation is feasible. In particular, an $A$-side portion can be newly left unmatched only through an arc $e\longrightarrow 0$, which exists only for a noncritical endpoint.

A matching arc of weight $w$ contributes signed vote $-\varepsilon w$. An unmatched arc has weight $1$ and contributes $-\varepsilon$, because it changes matched agents to unmatched status. Consequently,
$$ \phi(N,M)-\phi(M,N) =-\varepsilon\sum_{a\in Z}w(a)>0. $$
This challenger defeats $M$. The theorem follows. $\square$

3. Consequences for supports and witnesses

3.1 Popularity is independent of the positive masses

Corollary 2. Suppose two feasible allocations, possibly for different positive type masses, have exactly the same positive matched edges and exactly the same types with positive unmatched mass. If their acceptable pairs, rankings, and critical set agree, then either both allocations are popular or neither is.

Proof. Their graphs in Theorem 1 are identical. $\square$

Thus the numerical masses affect whether a support is feasible; once it is feasible, its popularity is a combinatorial property.

There is also a hereditary version.

Corollary 3. If a positive support is popular, every feasible allocation using a subset of its positive matched and unmatched coordinates is popular. The type masses may differ.

Proof. The values $\alpha_{u,i}$ constructed in the proof of Theorem 1 satisfy all required inequalities on the original positive categories, and they cancel on every original positive matching edge. Restrict them to the categories retained by the smaller support.

All comparison inequalities needed for the smaller support are among those already satisfied. Their weighted sum still cancels on every retained edge and unmatched category. The same vote bound therefore proves popularity. $\square$

3.2 Two statuses have dual values differing by exactly one

The witness values satisfy
$$ \alpha_{t,s}+\alpha_{s,t}=0 \qquad((t,s)\in K), $$
and $\alpha_{u,\bot}=0$ for positive unmatched categories.

Proposition 4. In a popular allocation, if $i,j\in S_u$ and $i\succ_u j$, then
$$ \alpha_{u,j}-\alpha_{u,i}=1. $$

Proof. First suppose $i$ is a matched status. Apply the matching inequality to the acceptable pair $(u,i)$, using old category $j$ at $u$ and old category $u$ at its counterpart. Cancellation on the original edge gives
$$ \alpha_{u,j}-\alpha_{u,i} \geq\eta_u(i,j). $$
If $i=\bot$, the same inequality follows from the unmatched inequality and $\alpha_{u,\bot}=0$.

Apply this argument in both directions. Since $i\succ_u j$,
$$ \alpha_{u,j}-\alpha_{u,i}\geq 1, \qquad \alpha_{u,i}-\alpha_{u,j}\geq -1. $$
These imply the asserted equality. $\square$

Corollary 5. Every type in a popular allocation has at most two positive-mass statuses, including unmatched status.

Proof. If $i_1\succ_u i_2\succ_u i_3$ were all positive, Proposition 4 would give
$$ \alpha_{u,i_2}-\alpha_{u,i_1}=1, \qquad \alpha_{u,i_3}-\alpha_{u,i_2}=1, $$
but also
$$ \alpha_{u,i_3}-\alpha_{u,i_1}=1. $$
The first two equalities make the last difference equal to $2$, a contradiction. $\square$

3.3 Small integral witnesses and efficient verification

Write $\kappa=|K|$. All arc weights are integers in $[-2,2]$. A shortest path can be chosen simple, so it has at most $\kappa$ arcs. The direct arcs $0\longrightarrow e$ also give $h_e\leq 1$. Hence the witness above can be chosen integral with
$$ -2\kappa\leq h_e\leq 1. $$

When $C=\varnothing$, the arcs $e\longrightarrow 0$ additionally imply $h_e\geq -1$. Thus every supported category has an integral witness value in
$$ \{-1,0,1\}. $$

To verify a proposed feasible allocation, first reject it if any type has more than two positive statuses. Otherwise,
$$ 2|K|=\sum_u\deg_K(u)\leq 2\tau, $$
so $\kappa\leq\tau$. There are at most $4m$ matching arcs and at most $2\kappa$ unmatched arcs. Negative-cycle detection therefore takes
$$ O\bigl((\tau+1)(m+\tau)\bigr) $$
graph operations using Bellman–Ford.

The graph calculation uses only support and rankings. Rational arithmetic is needed separately to verify aggregate feasibility and determine which coordinates are positive.

4. Popular vertex witnesses consist of paths

Let $\mathcal P$ be the aggregate feasibility polytope from Section 1. It is nonempty by the promise and bounded because every coordinate is bounded by an incident type mass.

Proposition 6. If a popular feasible allocation uses the specified edge positively, then a popular vertex of $\mathcal P$ uses that edge positively.

Proof. Express its aggregate vector as a convex combination of vertices:
$$ w=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_\ell v_\ell, \qquad \lambda_\ell>0,\qquad \sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_\ell=1. $$
Nonnegativity implies that every positive coordinate of $v_\ell$ is positive in $w$. Corollary 3 therefore makes every $v_\ell$ popular.

If the specified edge coordinate is positive in $w$, it is positive in at least one $v_\ell$. $\square$

Theorem 7. At a popular vertex, every nontrivial connected component of the positive matched-edge graph is a path. Such a path has either no positive unmatched mass or positive unmatched mass at exactly one endpoint.

Proof. Corollary 5 gives matched degree at most two. An internal vertex of matched degree two cannot also have positive unmatched mass.

A cycle component cannot occur at a vertex of $\mathcal P$. Since the graph is bipartite, its cycle has even length. Adding and subtracting a sufficiently small common amount on alternating cycle edges preserves every type’s aggregate equation and gives two distinct feasible vectors averaging to the original vector.

Thus every nontrivial component is a path.

Finally, the positive-coordinate columns of the aggregate equality matrix are linearly independent at a vertex. Otherwise, a nonzero dependence supported on positive coordinates would permit sufficiently small feasible perturbations in both directions.

For a path with $k$ type vertices, there are $k-1$ positive matched-edge columns. If it had positive unmatched coordinates at both endpoints, it would have $k+1$ positive columns supported on only $k$ rows. Those columns would be dependent. Therefore there is at most one positive unmatched endpoint. $\square$

Explicit mass conditions on a path

Consider a path
$$ u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_k,\qquad k\geq 2. $$
Orient it so that $u_1$ is fully matched. This is possible because at most one endpoint is unmatched.

Let $x_i$ be the mass on $(u_i,u_{i+1})$. The equations force
$$ x_1=q_{u_1}, \qquad x_i=q_{u_i}-x_{i-1} \quad(2\leq i\leq k-1). $$
Equivalently,
$$ x_i=\sum_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}q_{u_j}. $$
The final residual is
$$ r_{u_k}=q_{u_k}-x_{k-1}. $$

The prescribed positive support is feasible precisely when all $x_i>0$ and:

  • $r_{u_k}=0$ if the path is fully matched;
  • $r_{u_k}>0$ and $u_k\notin C$ if the final endpoint is declared unmatched.

All other unmatched coordinates on the path are zero. An isolated type has unmatched mass $q_u$ and must be noncritical.

This gives an exact reformulation of the general edge question: find a spanning collection of such paths and isolated types containing the specified edge, satisfying these numerical conditions and the negative-cycle test of Theorem 1. The unresolved issue is how to find such a collection efficiently, or prove that doing so is hard.

5. A small finite representative of each popular support

There is a further consequence of mass independence.

Proposition 8. Every popular feasible positive support has a popular finite representative with at most two agents of each type and at most $2\tau$ agents in total.

Proof. Put
$$ n_u=|S_u|\in\{1,2\}, \qquad N=\sum_u n_u. $$
Assign new masses
$$ q'_u=\frac{n_u}{N}, \qquad x'_e=\frac1N\quad(e\in K), $$
and assign unmatched mass $1/N$ exactly to the types whose original unmatched mass was positive.

Each type’s total is $|S_u|/N=q'_u$. Critical types remain fully matched. The positive support is unchanged, so Corollary 2 proves popularity.

Now create $n_u$ finite agents of type $u$. Assign one agent to every positive status and match one pair for each $e\in K$. Agents inherit strict rankings of counterpart types and are indifferent among agents of the same counterpart type.

The potential inequalities from Theorem 1 bound the signed votes of every finite challenger exactly as they bound measurable challengers. Each original matched edge contributes $h_e-h_e=0$, and each unmatched agent contributes zero. Hence this finite representative is popular. Its population is $N\leq 2\tau$. $\square$

This is a support-dependent representative, not a reduction preserving the original masses. In particular, it does not settle the general decision problem: the unknown support must still satisfy the original numerical path conditions.

6. Polynomial time for disjoint unions of stars

Here a star component consists of one center and any number of leaves; isolated vertices are also allowed. The center may lie on either side.

Theorem 9. For arbitrary positive binary rational masses and $C\subseteq T_A$, the popular feasible edge problem is polynomial-time solvable when the acceptable-pair graph is a disjoint union of stars.

Proof for one star

Let its center have degree $d$. Corollary 5 implies that a popular allocation uses at most two positive matched edges at the center. If the center also has positive unmatched mass, it uses at most one positive matched edge.

Enumerate the following support descriptions:

  • Choose at most two center–leaf edges to be positive.
  • Choose whether the center’s unmatched mass is positive, respecting the two-status bound.
  • For each chosen leaf, choose whether its unmatched mass is positive.
  • Every unchosen leaf is entirely unmatched.

There are $O((d+1)^2)$ descriptions: there are quadratically many edge choices and only a constant number of unmatched choices for each.

Reject any description declaring a critical type unmatched. Check whether its exact positive support is feasible by a rational linear program. Introduce $\delta$ with
$$ 0\leq\delta\leq 1. $$
Impose the aggregate feasibility equations, set every declared zero coordinate to zero, and require every declared positive coordinate to be at least $\delta$. Maximize $\delta$.

The description has a feasible realization with exactly its declared support if and only if the optimum is positive:

  • a positive optimum supplies such a realization;
  • any such realization has finitely many positive coordinates, whose minimum supplies a positive feasible $\delta$.

This LP has polynomial size and encoding length. If its optimum is positive, test the support graph for a negative cycle. By Theorem 1 and Corollary 2, this decides whether the entire feasible support is popular.

For the specified-edge question, retain only descriptions containing that edge. The enumeration includes every possible popular support, so this procedure is complete.

Combining components

Allocations and challengers restrict independently to components. Their signed vote margins add across components.

If one component allocation is unpopular, a challenger can defeat the full allocation by changing only that component. If every component allocation is popular, every challenger has a nonpositive vote margin on each component and hence on their union.

Therefore the complete instance is a yes-instance exactly when:

  • the specified-edge component has a popular feasible allocation using that edge;
  • every other component has some popular feasible allocation.

Apply the star procedure independently. An isolated noncritical type is necessarily unmatched and contributes no votes; an isolated critical type would contradict the feasibility promise. The total running time is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. $\square$

7. Remaining general question

The results above separate two requirements exactly:

  • popularity of a positive support is determined by a negative-cycle test using rankings and the critical set;
  • feasibility of a popular vertex support is determined by alternating sums of the prescribed masses along its paths.

What is still missing is a polynomial algorithm selecting such a popular path support, or an $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness reduction controlling both requirements. The graph characterization and the star algorithm do not establish either alternative for arbitrary acceptable-pair graphs.
