The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational profile masses \(\mu_{p,\theta}\) for protected group \(p\) and complete utility profile \(\theta\), rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_j\) of flat classes \(j\), utilities \(u_{\theta j}\), and threshold \(U\), decide whether there are bundle masses \(b_{pj}\) and flows \(x_{p,\theta,j}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_j x_{p,\theta,j}\le\mu_{p,\theta}\), \(\sum_\theta x_{p,\theta,j}\le b_{pj}\), \(\sum_p b_{pj}\le\sigma_j\), with each flow maximizing \(v_p(b_p)=\max_x\sum_{\theta,j}u_{\theta j}x_{p,\theta,j}\), such that \(\sum_p v_p(b_p)\ge U\) and \(v_p(b_p)\ge v_p(b_q)\) for every \(p,q\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(k\ge1\) protected groups and \(m\ge1\) flat classes. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types \((p,\theta)\) with positive mass \(\mu_{p,\theta}\), each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible profiles; \(\theta=(u_{\theta1},\ldots,u_{\theta m})\) is a complete vector of nonnegative rational utilities.
Normalize \(\sum_{p,\theta}\mu_{p,\theta}=1\), require every group to have positive mass, and give positive rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_j\) and a nonnegative rational threshold \(U\). At least one supported profile has positive utility for some flat class. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
For any bundle \(b\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\), define \(v_p(b)=\max\{\sum_{\theta,j}u_{\theta j}x_{\theta j}:x_{\theta j}\ge0,\ \sum_jx_{\theta j}\le\mu_{p,\theta},\ \sum_\theta x_{\theta j}\le b_j\}\), summing over profiles supported in group \(p\).
An allocation consists of nonnegative real bundle masses \(b_{pj}\) with \(\sum_pb_{pj}\le\sigma_j\), together with a maximizing flow for each \(v_p(b_p)\). Supplies may be withheld and bundle mass may remain unmatched. Values are total utilities, without division by group mass; ties between maximizing flows are unrestricted.
The decision problem asks whether such an allocation satisfies \(\sum_pv_p(b_p)\ge U\) and exact group envy-freeness \(v_p(b_p)\ge v_p(b_q)\) for every \(p,q\); evaluating another group's bundle uses the evaluating group's own profile masses.

Is this continuous welfare-threshold decision problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

The discrete welfare-threshold problem with maximum-weight matching group valuations and group envy-freeness up to one item is NP-complete even for three groups. [Benabbou et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0014.pdf)
For divisible items, approximation guarantees for class envy-freeness are known in online matching with binary utilities; these concern a different problem. [Hosseini et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03751) No result settling the exact continuous decision problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total society mass to one; supplies and the welfare threshold are measured per capita in that normalization.
  • Use nonnegative rational utilities and threshold, positive supplies for the listed flat classes, positive mass for every listed group, and at least one positive supported utility.
  • Interpret a support type as a protected-group/profile pair, so the support size counts positive-mass pairs.
  • Allow real nonnegative bundle masses and flows, with withholding and unmatched bundle mass permitted by the discrete allocation definition.
  • Use total group utility rather than average utility, and evaluate an envied bundle with the evaluating group's own masses.
  • Retain exact envy-freeness from the graded continuous question; no removal of a positive amount of flat mass is permitted.
  • Allow any maximizing within-group flow when maximizers tie.

Classification

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even with \(k=3\) and every utility in \(\{0,1,2\}\). Consequently, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The reduction below applies directly to the stated continuous model, including withholding, unmatched bundle mass, and unrestricted maximizing-flow ties.

1. Reduction from PARTITION

An instance of PARTITION consists of positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), encoded in binary. Writing \(S=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\), the question is whether some subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=S/2\). This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [MIT lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-046j-design-and-analysis-of-algorithms-spring-2015/f2829e57caade7e7f21d765b8e7b9058_MIT6_046JS15_lec16.pdf)

Choose the smallest power of two \(D\) strictly greater than \(7S\). Thus \(7S<D\le14S\).

We construct three groups, denoted \(A,B,C\), and \(n+1\) flat classes. Classes \(1,\ldots,n\) encode the integers; class \(0\) is private to group \(C\).

The supported types are as follows. All utilities not explicitly specified are zero.

| Group | Profile mass | Positive utilities |
|---|---:|---|
| \(A\) | \(2S/D\) | Utility \(2\) for every class \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\) |
| \(B\) | \(2S/D\) | Utility \(2\) for every class \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\) |
| \(C\), one profile for each \(i\) | \(2a_i/D\) | Utility \(1\) for class \(i\) |
| \(C\), private profile | \(S/D\) | Utility \(1\) for class \(0\) |
| \(C\), dummy profile | \((D-7S)/D\) | None |

The total mass is

\(\displaystyle \frac{2S+2S+2S+S+(D-7S)}{D}=1.\)

Every supported mass is positive, and every group has positive mass. Moreover, all masses are dyadic rationals, so the construction satisfies even the restrictive interpretation of “binary rational.”

Set the supplies and threshold to

\(\displaystyle \sigma_i=\frac{4a_i}{D}\quad(1\le i\le n),\qquad \sigma_0=\frac{S}{D},\qquad U=\frac{9S}{D}.\)

For any bundle \(b\), the resulting valuations are

\(\displaystyle v_A(b)=v_B(b)=2\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^n b_i,\frac{2S}{D}\right\},\)

and

\(\displaystyle v_C(b)=\min\left\{b_0,\frac{S}{D}\right\}+\sum_{i=1}^n\min\left\{b_i,\frac{2a_i}{D}\right\}.\)

The first formula follows because each of \(A,B\) has one profile, with utility \(2\) for every variable class and total matching capacity \(2S/D\). The second follows because each positive-utility profile of \(C\) values a different single class, so their matching problems are independent.

1.1. The welfare threshold forces exhaustion

Consider any allocation, together with its maximizing flows. Define:

  • \(h\): total variable-class mass matched to the positive-utility profiles of \(A\) and \(B\);
  • \(d\): total variable-class mass matched along positive-utility edges in \(C\);
  • \(z\): class-\(0\) mass matched to \(C\)'s private profile.

Zero-utility matches contribute nothing. Therefore welfare is exactly

\(\displaystyle W=2h+d+z.\)

Supply feasibility gives

\(\displaystyle h+d\le\frac{4S}{D},\qquad z\le\frac{S}{D}.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle W\le2\left(\frac{4S}{D}-d\right)+d+\frac{S}{D}=\frac{9S}{D}-d\le U.\)

If \(W\ge U\), every inequality above must be tight. Hence

\(\displaystyle d=0,\qquad h=\frac{4S}{D},\qquad z=\frac{S}{D}.\)

Each positive-utility profile in \(A,B\) has capacity \(2S/D\). Thus each group matches exactly \(2S/D\) variable-class mass.

These equalities also determine the relevant bundle masses. Indeed, the variable-class supply totals \(4S/D\), and all of it is matched in \(A,B\). For each variable class, matched mass is bounded by the corresponding bundle mass, whose sum is bounded by supply. Since the sums of these inequalities are equalities, every classwise inequality is an equality. Thus

\(\displaystyle b_{Ci}=0,\qquad b_{Ai}+b_{Bi}=\frac{4a_i}{D}\quad(1\le i\le n),\)

and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^n b_{Ai}=\sum_{i=1}^n b_{Bi}=\frac{2S}{D}.\)

Similarly, \(z=S/D\) exhausts the private supply, giving

\(\displaystyle b_{C0}=\frac{S}{D},\qquad b_{A0}=b_{B0}=0.\)

In particular, \(v_C(b_C)=S/D\). Withholding or unmatched bundle mass cannot evade these conclusions.

1.2. Envy-freeness forces whole-class choices

For any positive \(c\) and any \(x\in[0,2c]\),

\(\displaystyle \min\{x,c\}\ge\frac{x}{2},\)

with equality exactly when \(x=0\) or \(x=2c\). To verify this, if \(x\le c\), equality requires \(x=x/2\), hence \(x=0\); if \(x\ge c\), equality requires \(c=x/2\), hence \(x=2c\).

Apply this inequality with \(x=b_{Ai}\) and \(c=2a_i/D\). The supply bound ensures \(0\le b_{Ai}\le4a_i/D=2c\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle v_C(b_A)=\sum_{i=1}^n\min\left\{b_{Ai},\frac{2a_i}{D}\right\}\ge\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n b_{Ai}=\frac{S}{D}.\)

Envy-freeness of \(C\) toward \(A\) requires the reverse inequality, because \(v_C(b_C)=S/D\). Equality must therefore hold in every summand: each summand’s difference from \(b_{Ai}/2\) is nonnegative, and their sum is zero.

It follows that

\(\displaystyle b_{Ai}\in\left\{0,\frac{4a_i}{D}\right\}\quad\text{for every }i.\)

Let \(I=\{i:b_{Ai}=4a_i/D\}\). The bundle total now gives

\(\displaystyle \frac4D\sum_{i\in I}a_i=\frac{2S}{D},\)

so \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=S/2\). Thus any envy-free allocation meeting the threshold yields a PARTITION solution.

1.3. A partition yields a feasible allocation

Conversely, suppose \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=S/2\). Give the entire supply of class \(i\) to \(A\) when \(i\in I\), and to \(B\) otherwise. Give the entire private supply to \(C\).

Each of \(A,B\) receives variable-class mass \(2S/D\), attaining its maximum possible value \(4S/D\). Hence neither envies any group.

Group \(C\) receives value \(S/D\), and evaluates the other bundles as

\(\displaystyle v_C(b_A)=\frac2D\sum_{i\in I}a_i=\frac{S}{D},\qquad v_C(b_B)=\frac2D\sum_{i\notin I}a_i=\frac{S}{D}.\)

Thus all envy constraints hold. Matching all allocated variable mass to the respective receiver profile, and the private mass to \(C\)'s private profile, gives maximizing flows. Welfare is

\(\displaystyle \frac{4S}{D}+\frac{4S}{D}+\frac{S}{D}=U.\)

This proves both directions of the reduction.

1.4. Encoding size

The construction has \(k=3\), \(m=n+1\), and \(\tau=n+4\). Since \(\log D=O(\log S)\), its explicitly listed utility vectors and rational quantities have total length \(O(n^2+n\log S)\), and can be constructed in polynomial time.

Therefore the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard already for three groups. This reduction establishes ordinary hardness; it does not establish strong hardness for three groups.

2. Polynomial-size certificates

We next prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), including for allocations initially allowed to use arbitrary real numbers.

Let \(T_p\) denote the supported profiles in group \(p\), and put \(t_p=|T_p|\). Thus \(\sum_p t_p=\tau\).

The dual of the flow program defining \(v_p(b)\) is

\(\displaystyle v_p(b)=\min_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal D_p}\left(\sum_{\theta\in T_p}\mu_{p,\theta}\alpha_\theta+\sum_{j=1}^m b_j\beta_j\right),\)

where

\(\displaystyle \mathcal D_p=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\ge0:\alpha_\theta+\beta_j\ge u_{\theta j}\ \text{for every }\theta\in T_p,\ j\right\}.\)

The primal is feasible at zero and bounded because every profile has finite mass. The dual is feasible, for example by setting every \(\alpha_\theta\) to the largest utility and every \(\beta_j\) to zero. Linear-programming strong duality therefore applies.

The dual polyhedron lies in a nonnegative orthant and contains no line. The fundamental theorem of linear programming consequently gives an optimal dual vertex for every nonnegative bundle \(b\).

Its dimension is \(d_p=t_p+m\), and it is described by

\(\displaystyle N_p=mt_p+t_p+m\)

inequalities. Every vertex is determined by \(d_p\) linearly independent tight constraints. Their coefficients and right-hand sides have polynomial encoding length. Cramer’s rule, after clearing denominators, therefore shows that every dual vertex has polynomial encoding length in \(m,\tau,L\).

Now choose a dual vertex \((\alpha^{pq},\beta^{pq})\) for each ordered pair \(p\ne q\). With these vertices fixed, introduce bundle variables \(b_{pj}\) and feasible own-flow variables \(x_{p,\theta j}\). Write

\(\displaystyle w_p=\sum_{\theta\in T_p,j}u_{\theta j}x_{p,\theta j}.\)

Impose supply feasibility, own-flow feasibility, and the following linear inequalities:

\(\displaystyle \sum_p w_p\ge U,\)

\(\displaystyle w_p\ge\sum_{\theta\in T_p}\mu_{p,\theta}\alpha^{pq}_\theta+\sum_j b_{qj}\beta^{pq}_j\quad(p\ne q).\)

This formulation is equivalent to the original decision problem after allowing the vertex choices:

  • From an original solution: use its maximizing own flows and, for each \(p\ne q\), an optimal dual vertex evaluating \(b_q\). All displayed inequalities hold.
  • From a feasible linear system: own-flow feasibility gives \(w_p\le v_p(b_p)\), while dual feasibility gives the chosen affine expression at least \(v_p(b_q)\). Hence

\(\displaystyle v_p(b_p)\ge w_p\ge v_p(b_q)\),
and \(\sum_p v_p(b_p)\ge\sum_p w_p\ge U\). Maximizing own flows exist and can replace the certificate flows.

For fixed vertex choices, this is a rational linear system of polynomial size. Its bundle and flow variables are bounded by supplies and profile masses. If feasible, it therefore has a rational vertex of polynomial encoding length, again by Cramer’s rule.

Thus every yes-instance has a polynomial-size certificate consisting of the selected dual vertices and rational bundle/flow variables. All certificate conditions are checked by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. The problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

3. Running times

For a prescribed rational bundle, valuation is a linear program with \(t_pm\) variables and \(t_p+m\) capacity constraints, and is computable in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time. A prescribed rational allocation can be checked using \(k^2\) such evaluations, in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L+B)\) time, where \(B\) is its encoding length.

An explicit exact decision algorithm enumerates dual vertices and solves the linear systems above. Each \(\mathcal D_p\) has at most

\(\displaystyle \binom{N_p}{d_p}\le N_p^{d_p}\)

vertices, enumerable by testing sets of tight constraints. Since \(N_p\le(m+1)(\tau+1)\), for \(k\ge2\) the resulting running time is bounded by

\(\displaystyle \bigl((m+1)(\tau+1)\bigr)^{(k-1)(\tau+km)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L).\)

For \(k=1\), envy constraints are automatic, and one flow linear program decides the problem in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time.

The three-group hardness rules out a general polynomial running time unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
