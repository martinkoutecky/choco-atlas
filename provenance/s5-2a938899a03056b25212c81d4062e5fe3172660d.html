The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supports of circular preference types in categories \(A,B,C\), with rational masses \(\mu^A,\mu^B,\mu^C\) each summing to \(1\), does there exist a nonnegative coupling \(x_{abc}\) with those marginals such that no triple \((a,b,c)\) has positive mass of type \(a\) assigned to less-preferred \(B\)-types than \(b\), positive mass of type \(b\) assigned to less-preferred \(C\)-types than \(c\), and positive mass of type \(c\) assigned to less-preferred \(A\)-types than \(a\)? Find such a stable coupling or report that none exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B,C\) be nonempty finite supports of types. Each type has a complete strict ranking of the next category, cyclically \(A\to B\to C\to A\); members of a type are indifferent between members of the same partner type. All triples are acceptable, and preferences depend only on the next-category partner.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|A|+|B|+|C|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Each category's masses sum to \(1\). Support size counts listed types, not possible rankings. Put \(m=\max\{|A|,|B|,|C|\}\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A coupling is \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{A\times B\times C}\) with \(\sum_{b,c}x_{abc}=\mu^A_a\), \(\sum_{a,c}x_{abc}=\mu^B_b\), and \(\sum_{a,b}x_{abc}=\mu^C_c\).
Writing \(b\succ_a b'\) for strict preference, a triple \((a,b,c)\) blocks precisely when all three quantities \(\sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}\sum_{c'}x_{ab'c'}\), \(\sum_{c':\,c\succ_b c'}\sum_{a'}x_{a'bc'}\), and \(\sum_{a':\,a\succ_c a'}\sum_{b'}x_{a'b'c}\) are positive. A coupling is stable if no triple blocks.

Is deciding whether a stable coupling exists polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard? What is the complexity of finding a stable coupling when one exists?

Discrete circular matching always admits a stable matching with at most four agents per category ([Eriksson–Sjöstrand–Strimling](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411028)); for unrestricted size, existence with complete strict lists is NP-complete ([Lam–Plaxton](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02844)).
For general hypergraphs with strict rankings of incident hyperedges and unit vertex capacities, finding a stable fractional matching is PPAD-complete even at maximum degree three ([Ishizuka–Kamiyama](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2018.11)); this does not settle the specified circular coupling problem. No result settling this exact problem was identified.

  • Specified that every triple is acceptable and all mass must be assigned, carrying over complete discrete matchings.

2. Universal existence: a complete proof

We use the following form of Scarf’s lemma.

Scarf’s lemma. Let $D$ be a nonnegative matrix with no zero column, and let $\mu$ be a vector of positive capacities. Give each row a strict preference order on the columns having positive entries in that row. Then the polytope
$$ P=\{x\ge 0:Dx\le\mu\} $$
has an extreme point $x$ with the following property: every column $e$ has a row $v$ such that

  • $D_{v,e}>0$;
  • $(Dx)_v=\mu_v$;
  • every column $f$ with $D_{v,f}x_f>0$ is at least as preferred as $e$ in row $v$.

This is the domination formulation in [Lemma 1 of Biró–Csáji–Schlotter](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol334-icalp2025/html/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.31/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.31.html).

Applying the lemma

Let
$$ V=A\sqcup B\sqcup C,\qquad E=A\times B\times C, $$
and let $D$ be the vertex–triple incidence matrix. Each column has exactly three entries equal to $1$, so no column is zero. Let $\mu$ concatenate the three given mass vectors.

At each $a\in A$, order incident triples primarily by $a$’s ranking of their $B$-partner, breaking ties arbitrarily. Define the orders at $B$ and $C$ cyclically. These are strict orders that preserve every strict comparison between partner types.

The polytope $P$ contains zero and is bounded: every feasible coordinate satisfies
$$ 0\le x_{abc}\le \min\{\mu^A_a,\mu^B_b,\mu^C_c\}. $$
All capacities are positive. Hence Scarf’s lemma supplies a dominating extreme point $x$.

All capacities are filled

Let
$$ s=\sum_{a,b,c}x_{abc}. $$
Since every triple contains one vertex from each category,
$$ \sum_{a\in A}(Dx)_a = \sum_{b\in B}(Dx)_b = \sum_{c\in C}(Dx)_c =s. $$
Each category has total capacity $1$, so $s\le 1$.

Suppose $s<1$. Each category then has a vertex with a positive capacity deficit. Choose $a,b,c$ with
$$ (Dx)_a<\mu^A_a,\qquad (Dx)_b<\mu^B_b,\qquad (Dx)_c<\mu^C_c. $$
Complete acceptability means that $(a,b,c)$ is a column. Its dominating row must be one of these three vertices, and domination requires saturation there. This contradicts their three deficits.

Therefore $s=1$. Within each category, the nonnegative capacity deficits sum to zero, so every deficit is zero. Thus
$$ Dx=\mu, $$
and $x$ is a coupling.

Domination implies the specified stability

Fix $e=(a,b,c)$. If its dominating vertex is $a$, every positive incident triple $(a,b',c')$ ranks at least as highly as $e$ in the refinement. Because the refinement preserves strict comparisons between $B$-types, no such $b'$ is strictly worse than $b$. Consequently,
$$ \sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}\sum_{c'}x_{ab'c'}=0. $$
Domination at $b$ or $c$ gives the corresponding second or third zero blocking quantity.

Every triple therefore has at least one zero blocking quantity. The coupling is stable.

This proves universal existence. Reading and accepting a promised valid input takes $O(L)$ time; validating the masses and rankings, if required, takes polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$.

3. Stable supports and improved rational-output bounds

Stability is preserved by restricting support

Lemma. If $x$ is a stable coupling and $y$ is a coupling with
$$ \operatorname{supp}(y)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x), $$
then $y$ is stable.

Proof. For every candidate triple, stability of $x$ supplies a zero blocking quantity. That quantity is a sum of nonnegative coordinates, so every coordinate appearing in it is zero in $x$. Support containment makes those coordinates zero in $y$ as well. The same triple therefore cannot block $y$. This holds for every triple. $\square$

Thus, if a support $S$ admits a stable coupling positive only on $S$, every coupling in
$$ T_S=\{y\ge0:Dy=\mu,\ y_e=0\text{ for }e\notin S\} $$
is stable.

The incidence rank is $\tau-2$

A vector $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ is in the left nullspace of $D$ exactly when
$$ \alpha_a+\beta_b+\gamma_c=0 \quad\text{for every }(a,b,c). $$
Fixing $b,c$ and varying $a$ shows that $\alpha$ is constant. The other two coefficient families are constant by the same argument. Their three constants satisfy one equation.

The left nullspace therefore has dimension $2$, giving
$$ \operatorname{rank}(D)=\tau-2. $$

A sparse stable coupling exists

Take the support $S$ of any stable coupling. The polytope $T_S$ is nonempty and bounded, so it has an extreme point $y$. By the support lemma, $y$ is stable.

Its positive support columns are linearly independent. Otherwise there would be a nonzero vector $h$, supported on $\operatorname{supp}(y)$, with $Dh=0$. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, both
$$ y+\varepsilon h,\qquad y-\varepsilon h $$
would be distinct nonnegative points of $T_S$ whose midpoint is $y$, contradicting extremality.

It follows that
$$ |\operatorname{supp}(y)|\le\tau-2. $$
Given a rational stable coupling, such sparsification can also be performed in polynomial time by finding an extreme point of $T_S$ using rational linear programming.

Each positive coordinate needs only $O(L+\tau)$ bits

Let $k=|\operatorname{supp}(y)|$. Select $k$ rows on which its support columns form an invertible square matrix $M$. Then
$$ My_S=\mu_R. $$
Let $Q$ be the product of the input mass denominators. Its binary length is $O(L)$, and $Q\mu_R$ is integral.

Every column of $M$ contains at most three ones, so its Euclidean norm is at most $\sqrt3$. Moreover,
$$ \sum_{v\in R}\mu_v^2 \le \sum_{v\in R}\mu_v \le 3, $$
because each mass is at most $1$ and the total mass across all three categories is $3$.

Let $M_i$ replace column $i$ of $M$ by $Q\mu_R$. Hadamard’s inequality gives
$$ |\det M|\le 3^{k/2}, \qquad |\det M_i|\le Q\,3^{k/2}. $$
Cramer’s rule yields
$$ (y_S)_i=\frac{\det M_i}{Q\det M}. $$
Both numerator and denominator therefore have $O(L+k)$ bits. Since $k\le\tau-2$, each coordinate has encoding length $O(L+\tau)$.

Including triple indices, a sparse stable coupling has total encoding length
$$ O\!\left(\tau\bigl(L+\tau+\log(m+1)\bigr)\right). $$
The improvement over the notes comes from using the three nonzero entries per incidence column.

4. Improved exact search by eliminating one category’s thresholds

Assign rank $1$ to the best partner, and write the rank functions as
$$ r_a(b),\qquad r_b(c),\qquad r_c(a). $$

For a coupling $x$, define its worst positive partner ranks by
$$ t_a(x)=\max\{r_a(b):\textstyle\sum_c x_{abc}>0\}, $$
and cyclically define $t_b(x),t_c(x)$. Positive masses ensure that all these maxima exist.

The first blocking quantity of $(a,b,c)$ is positive exactly when
$$ r_a(b)<t_a(x). $$
Indeed, the quantity is positive precisely when a positive assignment uses some partner ranked worse than $b$. The other two quantities obey the cyclic versions.

Therefore $x$ is stable exactly when no triple satisfies
$$ r_a(b)<t_a(x),\qquad r_b(c)<t_b(x),\qquad r_c(a)<t_c(x). $$

Inferring all $C$-thresholds

Enumerate only
$$ t_a\in\{1,\ldots,q\}\quad(a\in A), \qquad t_b\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\quad(b\in B). $$
For each $c\in C$, define
$$ u_c= \min\left( \{p\}\cup \{r_c(a):\exists b,\ r_a(b)<t_a,\ r_b(c)<t_b\} \right). $$

For every triple, at least one of
$$ r_a(b)\ge t_a,\qquad r_b(c)\ge t_b,\qquad r_c(a)\ge u_c $$
holds. If the first two fail, the definition of $u_c$ gives the third.

Solve coupling feasibility restricted to triples satisfying
$$ r_a(b)\le t_a,\qquad r_b(c)\le t_b,\qquad r_c(a)\le u_c. $$

Soundness. Any feasible coupling is stable. For a candidate triple, choose a vertex satisfying one of the preceding three lower bounds. Every positive assignment at that vertex has rank at most its threshold, while the candidate’s rank is at least that threshold. Its blocking quantity is zero.

Completeness. Let $x$ be a stable coupling and enumerate its actual $A$- and $B$-thresholds. Whenever
$$ r_a(b)<t_a(x),\qquad r_b(c)<t_b(x), $$
stability forces
$$ r_c(a)\ge t_c(x). $$
Also $p\ge t_c(x)$. Hence $u_c\ge t_c(x)$ for every $c$, and all positive coordinates of $x$ satisfy the restricted inequalities. The corresponding LP is feasible.

Universal existence guarantees that this enumeration succeeds.

Running time

The number of candidates when $C$-thresholds are inferred is
$$ q^p r^q. $$
Cyclic rotation allows either other category to be inferred, giving candidate counts
$$ r^q p^r,\qquad p^r q^p. $$
Choose the smallest count. Computing the inferred thresholds takes $O(N+\tau)$ operations per candidate. Each feasibility LP has at most $m^3$ variables and $\tau$ marginal equations, with encoding length polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

Thus exact search takes time
$$ \min\{q^p r^q,\ r^q p^r,\ p^r q^p\} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
In particular, inferring the thresholds of a largest category gives
$$ m^{\tau-m}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

5. Polynomial-time search with one common category ranking

Assume every $a\in A$ has the same complete ranking of $B$. The rankings at $B$ and $C$ remain arbitrary, and masses may be unequal.

Maintain residual masses $\rho^A,\rho^B,\rho^C$, initially equal to the input masses. Start with $x=0$. While residual mass remains:

  • Choose the highest-ranked $b$ in the common order with $\rho^B_b>0$.
  • Choose $b$’s highest-ranked $c$ with $\rho^C_c>0$.
  • Choose $c$’s highest-ranked $a$ with $\rho^A_a>0$.
  • Set

$$ \delta=\min\{\rho^A_a,\rho^B_b,\rho^C_c\}, $$
add $\delta$ to $x_{abc}$, and subtract it from the three residual masses.

Termination and feasibility

The residual totals of the three categories remain equal, because every iteration subtracts $\delta$ from each total. Thus all three choices exist whenever residual mass remains.

Each iteration exhausts at least one previously positive type. The final iteration exhausts at least one type in each category. Hence there are at most $\tau-2$ iterations.

Residual masses stay nonnegative. At termination all residual totals, and therefore all individual residual masses, are zero. The resulting allocation is a coupling.

Stability

Fix a candidate triple $(a,b,c)$. If its first or second blocking quantity is zero, it does not block. Suppose both are positive.

The positive first quantity means that some positive assignment gives $a$ a partner $b'$ ranked below $b$ in the common order. The algorithm processes all mass of $b$ before any mass of $b'$. Consequently, $a$ has positive residual mass throughout the processing of $b$, since some of its mass will be allocated later to $b'$.

The positive second quantity means that at some iteration $i$, the algorithm assigns $b$ to a $c'$ that $b$ ranks below $c$. Step 2 could select $c'$ only if $c$’s residual mass was already zero at the start of iteration $i$.

Every positive allocation involving $c$ therefore occurred at an iteration $k<i$. At each such iteration, $a$ still had positive residual mass. Step 3 selects $c$’s best remaining $A$-type, so it could not assign $c$ to any type ranked below $a$.

Thus the third blocking quantity is zero. This contradiction rules out a blocking triple.

Running time

There are at most $\tau-2$ iterations. Scanning the preference lists to make each choice uses $O(m)$ operations per iteration. Thus the algorithm uses $O(\tau m)$ comparisons and arithmetic operations after preprocessing.

Using the common denominator $Q$ from Section 3, residual masses and increments can be represented by integers between $0$ and $Q$. Their bit lengths are $O(L)$. The total bit running time is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

The same result holds when the common ranking belongs to $B$ or $C$, by cyclic rotation. This proof does not extend to arbitrary rankings: it specifically uses the fact that every improving $B$-partner of $a$ is processed before $a$’s worse partner.

6. Exact search belongs to $\mathrm{PPAD}$

Here is a precise reduction, including the score convention and decoding clarification requested by the verifier.

The computational Scarf theorem takes rational matrices $H,O$ with $d<n$ columns, where $H$ starts with $I_d$, a nonnegative vector $\mu$, bounded feasibility
$$ \{\alpha\ge0:H\alpha=\mu\}, $$
and ordinal inequalities
$$ O_{ii}\le O_{ik}\le O_{ij} \quad (i,j\le d,\ i\ne j,\ k>d). $$
It returns a set $J$ of $d$ columns such that:

  • some nonnegative feasible vector is supported within $J$;
  • for every column $k$, some row $i$ satisfies

$$ O_{ik}\le O_{ij}\quad\text{for all }j\in J. $$

Finding such $J$ belongs to $\mathrm{PPAD}$ by [Theorems 5.1–5.2 of Kintali–Poplawski–Rajaraman–Sundaram–Teng](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.1435). A feasible vector supported within $J$ can then be computed by rational LP feasibility.

Constructing the instance

Use $d=\tau$ and
$$ H=[I_\tau\ D]. $$
Write its feasible coordinates as $(z,x)$, so
$$ z+Dx=\mu,\qquad z,x\ge0. $$
This set is nonempty and bounded: $z_v\le\mu_v$, and every triple coordinate is bounded by any of its three incident capacities. There are $\tau+N>\tau$ columns.

Use the strict incident-triple refinements from Section 2. Assign incident scores from $1$ through the row’s degree, with larger scores meaning more preferred triples. Every degree is at most $m^2$.

Put $K=m^2+1$. In row $v$, assign:

  • score $0$ to its own slack column;
  • score $K+1$ to each other slack column;
  • its incident preference score to each incident triple;
  • score $K$ to each nonincident triple.

The required ordinal inequalities hold. Repeated nonincident scores are allowed by the computational theorem.

The constructed encoding length is
$$ O\!\left(L+\tau(N+\tau)\log(m+\tau+1)\right), $$
which is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

Decoding every returned solution

Obtain $J$, and solve the feasible LP restricted to $J$, producing $(z,x)$.

Fix a triple $e$. Subordination supplies a row $v$ whose score for $e$ is at most the score of every positive coordinate.

That row must be incident to $e$. Otherwise its score for $e$ is $K$. Since $\mu_v>0$, the equation
$$ z_v+\sum_{f\ni v}x_f=\mu_v $$
forces a positive own-slack or incident-triple coordinate. Its score is strictly below $K$, contradicting subordination.

At an incident row, $e$’s score is positive. Therefore $z_v=0$, since a positive own-slack coordinate would have score $0$. Moreover, every positive incident triple ranks at least as highly as $e$.

Thus every triple has an incident, saturated dominating vertex. The capacity-filling and stability arguments in Section 2 apply: all slacks vanish and $x$ is a stable coupling.

Construction and decoding are polynomial-time operations in $m,\tau,L$, proving membership of exact rational search in $\mathrm{PPAD}$. Section 3 ensures polynomial-length outputs and permits sparse output.

7. A small obstruction to direct convexification

The stable-coupling set need not be convex, even with two types per category and all masses equal to $1/2$.

Take the rankings
$$ \begin{array}{c|c} a_1&b_1\succ b_2\\ a_2&b_2\succ b_1\\ b_1&c_1\succ c_2\\ b_2&c_2\succ c_1\\ c_1&a_1\succ a_2\\ c_2&a_2\succ a_1 \end{array} $$
and define two couplings by their positive entries:
$$ x^{(1)}_{a_1b_1c_2} =x^{(1)}_{a_2b_2c_1} =\frac12, $$
$$ x^{(2)}_{a_1b_2c_2} =x^{(2)}_{a_2b_1c_1} =\frac12. $$

Both are stable. In $x^{(1)}$, every $A$-type receives its best partner, so every first blocking quantity is zero. In $x^{(2)}$, every $B$-type receives its best partner, so every second blocking quantity is zero.

Their average
$$ y=\frac{x^{(1)}+x^{(2)}}2 $$
is blocked by $(a_1,b_1,c_1)$. Its three blocking quantities are, respectively,
$$ \frac14,\qquad \frac14,\qquad \frac12. $$
All are positive.

Consequently, the entire stable-coupling set cannot be the projection of one LP feasible region, because such a projection is convex. This is only an obstruction to that particular formulation; it does not prove computational hardness.
