The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite bipartite type graph \(H=(U,V;E)\), worker and firm type masses \(p_i,q_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) summing to \(1\) on each side, strict rankings of acceptable counterpart types, and a rational budget \(\kappa\), can the type masses be split among post-intervention rankings and assigned by a matching flow so that all mass is matched, the resulting type-level matching is stable, and the total mass-weighted adjacent-swap cost is at most \(\kappa\)? Stability requires that no worker-ranking variant and firm-ranking variant both have positive mass assigned to less-preferred counterpart types while preferring each other.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a finite bipartite acceptability graph \(H=(U,V;E)\), each type \(z\in U\cup V\) has an initial strict ranking \(\ell_z\) of all its neighbors. Worker masses \(p_i\) and firm masses \(q_j\) are positive binary rationals, with \(\sum_i p_i=\sum_j q_j=1\); the budget is \(\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\).
A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass and their masses. Write \(\tau=|U|+|V|\) for the support size, not the number of possible rankings, \(m=\max_z\deg_H(z)\), and \(L\) for the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
For each type \(z\), let \(\mathcal P_z\) contain all permutations of its unchanged neighbor set. A variant \(\sigma\in\mathcal P_z\) costs \(d_z(\sigma)\) per unit mass, where \(d_z(\sigma)\) is the minimum number of adjacent swaps transforming \(\ell_z\) into \(\sigma\). Swaps may be made on both sides.
Choose nonnegative real variant masses \(a_{i\sigma},b_{j\pi}\) satisfying \(\sum_\sigma a_{i\sigma}=p_i\) and \(\sum_\pi b_{j\pi}=q_j\), and nonnegative real matching flows \(x_{i\sigma,j\pi}\), permitted only when \((i,j)\in E\).
All mass must be matched: \(\sum_{j,\pi}x_{i\sigma,j\pi}=a_{i\sigma}\) and \(\sum_{i,\sigma}x_{i\sigma,j\pi}=b_{j\pi}\). Rankings compare underlying counterpart types only; variants of the same counterpart type are equally acceptable.
Stability means that, for every \((i,j)\in E\) and every \(\sigma\in\mathcal P_i,\pi\in\mathcal P_j\), the quantities \(\sum_{h:\,j\succ_\sigma h}\sum_\rho x_{i\sigma,h\rho}\) and \(\sum_{g:\,i\succ_\pi g}\sum_\eta x_{g\eta,j\pi}\) are not both positive, even if \(x_{i\sigma,j\pi}>0\).
The decision problem asks whether such masses and a stable full matching flow exist with \(\sum_{i,\sigma}a_{i\sigma}d_i(\sigma)+\sum_{j,\pi}b_{j\pi}d_j(\pi)\le\kappa\).

Is this continuous decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For discrete agents, obtaining a perfect stable matching using at most \(k\) adjacent swaps is NP-complete and W[1]-hard parameterized by \(k\), even on graphs admitting a perfect matching. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15361v2)
For fixed strict preferences and unit-capacity agents, maximizing the number of fully matched agents in an ordinally stable fractional matching is polynomial-time solvable; this does not include endogenous ranking variants or swap costs. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The budget's sign was unspecified; require a nonnegative rational budget, consistent with a swap budget.
  • Post-intervention rankings are arbitrary permutations of the original complete neighbor lists; acceptability is unchanged and intervention is allowed on both sides.
  • Mass splitting and matching flows may use nonnegative real values, with no indivisibility or denominator restriction.
  • Adjacent-swap cost means the minimum number of unit-cost adjacent swaps to reach each variant, charged separately per unit mass on both sides.
  • Preferences distinguish counterpart types, not their ranking variants; all variants of the same counterpart type have the same position.
  • Stability applies to every acceptable variant pair, including pairs carrying positive matching flow, whenever both variants also assign positive mass to strictly worse counterpart types.
  • Define the size parameters as total support size, maximum preference-list length, and total binary input length.

Result

The continuous decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when \(m\le 3\), all type masses are equal dyadic rationals, and the acceptability graph admits a perfect matching.

The essential point is that, for equal masses, splitting types among ranking variants cannot reduce the minimum cost below the scaled discrete optimum. I prove this through an exact formulation using one integer threshold per type.

1. Stability gives an endpoint condition for each edge

Write \(d_z=\deg_H(z)\), and let \(r_z(h)\in\{1,\ldots,d_z\}\) be the position of neighbor \(h\) in the initial ranking of \(z\), with position \(1\) best.

For a worker variant, define

\(A_{i\sigma}(j)=\sum_{h:\,j\succ_\sigma h}\sum_\rho x_{i\sigma,h\rho}\).

For a firm variant, define

\(B_{j\pi}(i)=\sum_{g:\,i\succ_\pi g}\sum_\eta x_{g\eta,j\pi}\).

For each edge \((i,j)\), stability is equivalent to

\(\bigl[A_{i\sigma}(j)=0\text{ for every }\sigma\bigr]\quad\text{or}\quad\bigl[B_{j\pi}(i)=0\text{ for every }\pi\bigr].\)

Indeed, if both alternatives fail, there are variants \(\sigma,\pi\) for which both quantities are positive, contradicting the stipulated stability condition. Conversely, either alternative prevents such a pair of positive quantities.

Choose one endpoint satisfying this zero condition for each edge, and say that endpoint *protects* the edge. Let \(S_z\) be the neighbors whose edges are protected by \(z\).

If a variant of \(z\) sends positive mass to \(h\), it must rank \(h\) above every member of \(S_z\setminus\{h\}\). Otherwise its zero condition for one of those protected edges would fail.

This inference uses the requirement that stability is tested for every pair of variants, including pairs carrying positive flow.

2. The exact cost of protecting a neighbor set

For \(S\subseteq N_H(z)\), define

\(t_z(S)=\min_{s\in S}r_z(s)\),

with \(t_z(\varnothing)=d_z+1\). Write \(u_+=\max\{u,0\}\).

Lemma. The minimum adjacent-swap cost of a ranking that places \(h\) above every member of \(S\setminus\{h\}\) is

\(c_z(h,S)=\bigl(r_z(h)-t_z(S)\bigr)_+.\)

Proof. Put \(r=r_z(h)\) and \(t=t_z(S)\).

If \(r\le t\), the initial ranking already satisfies the condition, so the minimum is zero.

Suppose \(r>t\). Choose \(s\in S\) with initial position \(t\). The required final ranking places \(h\) above \(s\), reversing their initial order.

For each neighbor \(v\) initially strictly between \(s\) and \(h\), at least one of the pairs \(\{s,v\}\) and \(\{v,h\}\) must also have its order reversed. Otherwise the final ranking would still have \(s\) above \(v\) above \(h\), which is impossible. These pairs are distinct as \(v\) varies and distinct from \(\{s,h\}\). Consequently at least

\(1+(r-t-1)=r-t\)

pairwise orders must change. An adjacent swap changes the relative order of exactly its two swapped elements, so at least \(r-t\) swaps are necessary.

For the upper bound, move \(h\) upward from position \(r\) to position \(t\), using exactly \(r-t\) adjacent swaps. Every member of \(S\setminus\{h\}\) is then below \(h\). This attains the lower bound. \(\square\)

3. An exact formulation without enumerating permutations

Choose thresholds

\(t_z\in\{1,\ldots,d_z+1\}\)

subject to the edge conditions

\(r_i(j)\ge t_i\quad\text{or}\quad r_j(i)\ge t_j\qquad\text{for every }(i,j)\in E.\)

For fixed thresholds, assign the edge cost

\(w_{ij}(t)=\bigl(r_i(j)-t_i\bigr)_++\bigl(r_j(i)-t_j\bigr)_+.\)

Consider the transportation LP with variables \(y_{ij}\) indexed by \(E\):

\(\displaystyle \min\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}(t)y_{ij}\),

subject to

\(\displaystyle y_{ij}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{j:\,(i,j)\in E}y_{ij}=p_i,\qquad \sum_{i:\,(i,j)\in E}y_{ij}=q_j.\)

Proposition. The original instance is feasible within budget \(\kappa\) if and only if some thresholds satisfying the edge conditions admit a transportation flow with objective at most \(\kappa\).

Proof, forward direction. Take any feasible stable variant flow. Choose protecting endpoints as in Section 1, and set \(t_z=t_z(S_z)\).

Every protected edge satisfies \(r_z(h)\ge t_z\), so the threshold edge conditions hold. Aggregate the flow by underlying types:

\(\displaystyle y_{ij}=\sum_{\sigma,\pi}x_{i\sigma,j\pi}.\)

This satisfies the transportation equations.

Whenever \(x_{i\sigma,j\pi}>0\), Section 1 and the lemma give

\(d_i(\sigma)\ge\bigl(r_i(j)-t_i\bigr)_+,\qquad d_j(\pi)\ge\bigl(r_j(i)-t_j\bigr)_+.\)

Using the variant marginal equations, the original cost is

\(\displaystyle C=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\sum_{\sigma,\pi}x_{i\sigma,j\pi}\bigl(d_i(\sigma)+d_j(\pi)\bigr).\)

Therefore

\(\displaystyle C\ge\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}(t)y_{ij}.\)

Proof, reverse direction. Take thresholds and a feasible transportation flow \(y\). For each type \(z\) and neighbor \(h\), construct a ranking \(\sigma_{z,h}\) by moving \(h\) upward to position \(t_z\) if \(r_z(h)>t_z\), and otherwise leaving the initial ranking unchanged.

Its exact cost is \(\bigl(r_z(h)-t_z\bigr)_+\), by the lemma applied to the suffix

\(S_z^t=\{s\in N_H(z):r_z(s)\ge t_z\}.\)

Moreover, this ranking places \(h\) above every member of \(S_z^t\setminus\{h\}\).

Send mass \(y_{ij}\) between variants \(\sigma_{i,j}\) and \(\sigma_{j,i}\). Define variant masses by their resulting marginals, aggregating coincident rankings. This gives a full matching with cost exactly \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}(t)y_{ij}\).

To verify stability, fix an edge \((i,j)\). If \(r_i(j)\ge t_i\), every positive assignment of every worker variant of \(i\) places its assigned counterpart weakly above \(j\). Thus \(A_{i\sigma}(j)=0\) for every \(\sigma\). If instead \(r_j(i)\ge t_j\), the analogous firm quantities are all zero. The edge condition guarantees at least one alternative. Hence the constructed flow is stable. \(\square\)

4. Equal masses admit rounding without increasing scaled cost

Suppose \(|U|=|V|=n\) and every type has mass \(1/n\).

Rounding lemma. From any feasible stable continuous solution of cost \(C\), there exists a discrete perfect matching and one modified ranking per type that make it stable with total adjacent-swap distance at most \(nC\).

Proof. The forward direction of the proposition supplies thresholds and a transportation flow \(y\) such that

\(\displaystyle \sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}(t)y_{ij}\le C.\)

Extend \(Y=ny\) by zeros outside \(E\). It is a nonnegative matrix with every row and column sum equal to \(1\).

Such a matrix is a convex combination of incidence matrices of perfect matchings supported on its positive entries. Here is a justification that also checks the support restriction. For any worker subset \(A\), its total outgoing mass is \(|A|\), and each neighboring firm receives at most \(1\); hence \(|N(A)|\ge|A|\). Hall’s theorem therefore gives a supported perfect matching. Subtract the minimum entry on that matching. The remaining row and column sums are all the same. If nonzero, normalize and repeat. Each subtraction eliminates a positive entry, so this process terminates and yields the asserted decomposition.

Consequently, one perfect matching \(M\) in the decomposition satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{(i,j)\in M}w_{ij}(t)\le\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}(t)Y_{ij}\le nC.\)

For each type \(z\), choose the ranking \(\sigma_{z,M(z)}\) constructed in Section 3. Their total swap distance is exactly \(\sum_{(i,j)\in M}w_{ij}(t)\).

These rankings make \(M\) stable: for each acceptable edge, the threshold condition ensures that at least one endpoint weakly prefers its assigned partner to the other endpoint. Thus the edge cannot block. \(\square\)

Conversely, any discrete perfect stable matching with total ranking distance \(D\) gives a continuous solution of cost \(D/n\), by assigning all mass of each type to its chosen ranking.

Thus, for equal masses, the continuous minimum cost is exactly the discrete minimum swap cost divided by \(n\).

5. Polynomial reduction and hardness

Use the discrete problem Swap Distance to Perfect Stable Matching. Theorem 4.4 of [Eiben et al., *Preference Swaps for the Stable Matching Problem*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.15361v2) proves it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Their reduction retains a graph of maximum degree three admitting a perfect matching.

Take such a discrete instance with \(n\) agents on each side and integer swap budget \(k\). To ensure that the masses have finite binary expansions, let \(N\) be the smallest power of two with \(N\ge n\). Add \(N-n\) disjoint worker–firm pairs, each with a single acceptable neighbor.

These pairs are forced in every perfect matching and require no swaps. Therefore padding preserves the discrete answer and maximum degree three.

Construct the continuous instance using this padded graph and the same initial rankings, with

\(p_i=q_j=1/N,\qquad \kappa=k/N.\)

All masses and the budget are dyadic rationals.

If the discrete instance is a YES-instance, its padded stable perfect matching yields a continuous solution of cost at most \(k/N\).

Conversely, if the continuous instance has cost at most \(k/N\), the rounding lemma gives a discrete stable perfect matching with total swap distance at most

\(N(k/N)=k.\)

Deleting the added pairs yields a solution to the original discrete instance.

The construction has support size \(\tau=2N\), with \(N<2n\), and polynomial output bit length. It is therefore a polynomial-time many-one reduction. This proves the claimed hardness, including the restrictions \(m\le3\) and equal dyadic masses.

6. Membership and running time

The threshold formulation also proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\). A certificate consists only of the thresholds, using \(O(\tau\log(m+1))\) bits. A verifier checks the edge conditions and solves the corresponding rational transportation LP with the budget inequality.

That LP has \(|E|\le m\tau/2\) variables and \(\tau\) marginal equations; its edge costs are integers bounded by \(2m\). Exact rational LP feasibility is decidable in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time. Thus verification is polynomial, despite the factorial number of possible rankings.

Enumerating all thresholds gives a deterministic algorithm with running time

\(\displaystyle (m+1)^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L).\)

An isolated positive-mass type is immediately infeasible. Otherwise the formulation applies directly.

Combining membership with the reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. In particular, a general algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). The [fixed-preference fractional-matching result](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf) does not remove the discrete choice of thresholds introduced by endogenous ranking changes.
