The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods \(i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), buyer types \(t\in T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), integer values \(v_{ti}\), demand limits \(d_t\), and nonnegative rational per-capita supplies \(q_i\), compute a component-wise least Walrasian price vector \(p\) and a supporting allocation. Let \(Y_t=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^m:\sum_i y_i\le d_t,\ y_i=0\text{ whenever }q_i=0\}\), and let \(\lambda_{t,y}\ge0\) be the mass of type \(t\) receiving bundle \(y\in Y_t\). Require \(\sum_{y\in Y_t}\lambda_{t,y}=\mu_t\), positive-mass bundles to maximize \(\sum_i(v_{ti}-p_i)y_i\) over \(Y_t\), aggregate use of each good to be at most \(q_i\), total sales to equal \(\min\{\sum_i q_i,\sum_t\mu_t d_t\}\), and each positive-priced good to be fully sold.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) goods, binary-encoded values \(v_{ti}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), demand limits \(d_t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), and per-capita supplies \(q_i\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each individual of type \(t\) chooses an integer bundle from \(Y_t=\{y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_i y_i\le d_t,\ y_i=0\text{ if }q_i=0\}\); positive supplies impose aggregate constraints, not individual bundle bounds.
At prices \(p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\), preferred bundles maximize \(\sum_i(v_{ti}-p_i)y_i\) over \(Y_t\). All maximizers are allowed, including bundles containing units of zero net utility.
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(\lambda_{t,y}\), represented by their nonzero entries, satisfying \(\sum_{y\in Y_t}\lambda_{t,y}=\mu_t\); every positive-mass bundle must be preferred.
Write \(a_i=\sum_t\sum_{y\in Y_t}\lambda_{t,y}y_i\). A Walrasian equilibrium additionally satisfies \(a_i\le q_i\), \(\sum_i a_i=\min\{\sum_i q_i,\sum_t\mu_t d_t\}\), and \(a_i=q_i\) whenever \(p_i>0\).
A component-wise least Walrasian price vector \(p^*\) satisfies \(p_i^*\le p_i\) for every Walrasian price vector \(p\) and every good \(i\).
Is computing an exact component-wise least Walrasian price vector and a supporting allocation solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite buyers with integer supplies and individual bundle bounds given by those supplies, a flow-based ascending auction computes component-wise least competitive prices and a supporting Walrasian allocation; its analyzed running time is pseudo-polynomial, while direct price computation by LP is known ([Eickhoff et al.](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/bitstreams/7e5cbedc-7d73-46f1-a781-5bba41f9e3f4/download)).

Exact polynomial-time computation

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Two rational linear programs suffice. A supporting allocation can be output using at most \(\tau(m+1)\) nonzero entries.

The proof uses the stated mass model directly, including its absence of individual supply bounds and its allowance of zero-net-utility bundles.

1. Aggregate allocations have a compact representation

Let \(A=\{i:q_i>0\}\) and \(T=\{t:d_t>0\}\). Define \(c_t=\mu_t d_t\) for \(t\in T\), and let

\( \displaystyle K=\min\left\{\sum_{i\in A}q_i,\sum_{t\in T}c_t\right\}. \)

If either \(A\) or \(T\) is empty, zero prices and the allocation \(\lambda_{t,0}=\mu_t\) form an equilibrium and give the component-wise least prices. Henceforth assume both are nonempty.

For an allocation, define its aggregate type-good quantities by

\( \displaystyle x_{ti}=\sum_{y\in Y_t}\lambda_{t,y}y_i. \)

These quantities satisfy

\( \displaystyle x_{ti}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{i\in A}x_{ti}\le c_t,\qquad \sum_{t\in T}x_{ti}\le q_i. \)

Conversely, every rational matrix satisfying these inequalities can be realized by a rational allocation. For \(t\in T\), set

\( \displaystyle \lambda_{t,d_t e_i}=\frac{x_{ti}}{d_t}\quad(i\in A),\qquad \lambda_{t,0}=\mu_t-\frac{\sum_{i\in A}x_{ti}}{d_t}, \)

where \(e_i\) is the \(i\)-th coordinate unit vector. All other entries are zero. Types outside \(T\) receive the zero bundle with mass \(\mu_t\).

The masses are nonnegative, sum to \(\mu_t\), and reproduce \(x_{ti}\). Crucially, every bundle \(d_t e_i\) with \(i\in A\) belongs to \(Y_t\), regardless of the magnitude of \(q_i\).

This establishes aggregate feasibility. Preference compatibility will be proved below.

2. Welfare maximization and the consumption requirement

Consider the rational linear program \((P)\):

  • Maximize \( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{i\in A}v_{ti}x_{ti} \).
  • Subject to \(x_{ti}\ge0\), \( \displaystyle \sum_{i\in A}x_{ti}\le c_t \), and \( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}x_{ti}\le q_i \).

Its feasible region is nonempty and compact. Let \(W\) be its optimal value.

Lemma. There is an optimal solution of \((P)\) with total consumption \(K\).

Proof. Among all welfare-optimal solutions, choose one maximizing

\( \displaystyle R=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{i\in A}x_{ti}. \)

Such a solution exists by compactness. Feasibility gives \(R\le K\).

Suppose \(R<K\). Because \(R<\sum_t c_t\), some type \(t\) has positive residual capacity. Because \(R<\sum_i q_i\), some good \(i\) has positive residual supply. Thus

\( \displaystyle \delta=\min\left\{c_t-\sum_{j\in A}x_{tj},\ q_i-\sum_{s\in T}x_{si}\right\}>0. \)

Increasing \(x_{ti}\) by \(\delta\) preserves feasibility and increases welfare by \(\delta v_{ti}\). Since \(v_{ti}\ge0\), welfare cannot decrease. Optimality rules out a strict increase, so welfare stays equal to \(W\). But total consumption increases, contradicting the choice of \(R\). Therefore \(R=K\). ∎

Consequently, adding the equality

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{i\in A}x_{ti}=K \)

to \((P)\) leaves its optimal value equal to \(W\).

3. Dual-optimal prices are exactly Walrasian prices

The dual of \((P)\) is \((D)\):

  • Minimize \( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}c_t u_t+\sum_{i\in A}q_i p_i \).
  • Subject to \(u_t\ge0\), \(p_i\ge0\), and \(u_t+p_i\ge v_{ti}\) for every \(t\in T,i\in A\).

The dual is feasible: take \(p_i=0\) and \(u_t=\max_{i\in A}v_{ti}\). Strong linear-programming duality therefore applies and gives an attained dual optimum equal to \(W\).

For any nonnegative price vector on \(A\), define

\( \displaystyle b_t(p)=\max\left\{0,\max_{i\in A}(v_{ti}-p_i)\right\}, \)

and

\( \displaystyle F(p)=\sum_{t\in T}c_t b_t(p)+\sum_{i\in A}q_i p_i. \)

For fixed \(p\), the smallest feasible \(u_t\) is \(b_t(p)\). Hence

\( \displaystyle \min_{p\ge0}F(p)=W. \)

Also, the maximum utility of an individual of type \(t\) is exactly \(d_t b_t(p)\): every unit contributes at most \(b_t(p)\), and this bound is attained by a full bundle of a maximizing good when \(b_t(p)>0\), or by the zero bundle when \(b_t(p)=0\).

Every Walrasian price minimizes \(F\)

Suppose \(p\) supports a Walrasian allocation, and let \(x\) be its aggregate matrix. Since every positive-mass bundle is preferred,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i\in A}(v_{ti}-p_i)x_{ti}=c_t b_t(p) \)

for every \(t\in T\). Writing \(a_i=\sum_t x_{ti}\), we obtain

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t,i}v_{ti}x_{ti} =\sum_t c_t b_t(p)+\sum_i p_i a_i =F(p). \)

The last equality follows because \(a_i=q_i\) whenever \(p_i>0\); coordinates with \(p_i=0\) contribute zero.

Primal feasibility and weak duality now imply

\( \displaystyle F(p)=\sum_{t,i}v_{ti}x_{ti}\le W\le F(p). \)

Thus \(F(p)=W\).

Every minimizer of \(F\) supports a Walrasian allocation

Conversely, suppose \(F(p)=W\). Choose a rational welfare-optimal aggregate matrix \(x\) with total consumption \(K\), whose existence follows from Section 2 and rational linear programming. Put \(r_t=\sum_i x_{ti}\) and \(a_i=\sum_t x_{ti}\).

The primal-dual gap has the identity

\( \displaystyle 0=F(p)-\sum_{t,i}v_{ti}x_{ti} =\sum_t b_t(p)(c_t-r_t) +\sum_i p_i(q_i-a_i) +\sum_{t,i}\bigl(b_t(p)+p_i-v_{ti}\bigr)x_{ti}. \)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore:

  • If \(x_{ti}>0\), then \(v_{ti}-p_i=b_t(p)\).
  • If \(r_t<c_t\), then \(b_t(p)=0\).
  • If \(p_i>0\), then \(a_i=q_i\).

Apply the allocation construction from Section 1. Each positive-mass bundle \(d_t e_i\) attains utility \(d_t b_t(p)\), so it is preferred. If the zero bundle receives positive mass, then \(r_t<c_t\), hence \(b_t(p)=0\), so that bundle is also preferred.

The allocation satisfies the supply inequalities, consumes exactly \(K\), and exhausts every positively priced good. It is therefore Walrasian.

We have proved that the Walrasian price vectors on \(A\) are exactly

\( \displaystyle S=\{p\ge0:F(p)=W\}. \)

Prices of goods outside \(A\) do not affect preferences or feasibility and can be set to zero.

4. The optimal-price set is closed under coordinate-wise minimum

For price vectors \(p,r\), let \(p\wedge r\) and \(p\vee r\) denote their coordinate-wise minimum and maximum.

For every type \(t\),

\( \displaystyle b_t(p\wedge r)=\max\{b_t(p),b_t(r)\}. \)

Indeed, \(v_{ti}-\min\{p_i,r_i\}=\max\{v_{ti}-p_i,v_{ti}-r_i\}\), and taking the maximum over goods and zero gives the identity.

Moreover,

\( \displaystyle b_t(p\vee r)\le\min\{b_t(p),b_t(r)\}, \)

because increasing coordinates cannot increase \(b_t\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle b_t(p\wedge r)+b_t(p\vee r)\le b_t(p)+b_t(r). \)

The linear price term satisfies

\( \displaystyle \sum_i q_i(p\wedge r)_i+\sum_i q_i(p\vee r)_i =\sum_i q_i p_i+\sum_i q_i r_i. \)

Multiplying the preceding type inequalities by \(c_t\) and summing yields

\( \displaystyle F(p\wedge r)+F(p\vee r)\le F(p)+F(r). \)

If \(p,r\in S\), the right side equals \(2W\), while each term on the left is at least \(W\). Both therefore equal \(W\). In particular,

\( \displaystyle p,r\in S\ \Longrightarrow\ p\wedge r\in S. \)

5. A second LP computes the component-wise least prices

Consider the linear program \((B)\):

  • Minimize \( \displaystyle \sum_{i\in A}p_i \).
  • Subject to \(u_t,p_i\ge0\), \(u_t+p_i\ge v_{ti}\), and

\( \displaystyle \sum_t c_t u_t+\sum_i q_i p_i=W \).

Its feasible region is the dual-optimal face. It is nonempty by strong duality. It is also compact: all \(c_t\) and \(q_i\) here are strictly positive, so the objective equality bounds every nonnegative variable.

Its projection onto the price coordinates is precisely \(S\). Indeed, any feasible pair has \(F(p)\le W\), and weak duality gives \(F(p)\ge W\). Conversely, for any \(p\in S\), choosing \(u_t=b_t(p)\) gives a feasible pair.

Let \(p^*\) be the price vector returned by \((B)\). For any \(r\in S\), Section 4 gives \(p^*\wedge r\in S\). If some \(r_i<p_i^*\), then

\( \displaystyle \sum_i(p^*\wedge r)_i<\sum_i p_i^*, \)

contradicting optimality in \((B)\). Thus \(p_i^*\le r_i\) for every coordinate and every \(r\in S\).

Setting \(p_i^*=0\) outside \(A\) gives the component-wise least Walrasian price vector for all goods. Section 3 shows that the aggregate optimum already computed supports these prices.

6. Algorithm, exactness, and output size

The algorithm is:

  • Compute \(A,T,c_t,K\), handling the empty-set cases as above.
  • Solve \((P)\) with the additional total-consumption equality \(K\). Obtain a rational optimal matrix \(x\) and its exact welfare \(W\).
  • Solve \((B)\). Obtain \(p^*\), and set the prices outside \(A\) to zero.
  • Output the rational allocation from Section 1, omitting zero entries.

The first LP has \(|T||A|\) variables and a polynomial number of constraints. The second has \(|T|+|A|\) variables and \(|T||A|\) utility inequalities, together with nonnegativity and one equality.

All coefficients have polynomial encoding length. In particular, forming \(c_t=\mu_t d_t\) and \(K\) takes polynomial-time rational arithmetic. The standard rational linear-programming theorem provides exact optimal solutions, of polynomial encoding length, in time polynomial in the LP encoding size. Its hypotheses hold here: both LPs have nonempty compact feasible regions. The resulting value \(W\) also has polynomial encoding length, so inserting it into the second LP preserves polynomial size.

Finally, dividing \(x_{ti}\) by the binary-encoded integer \(d_t\) preserves polynomial encoding length. There are at most \(|A|+1\) allocation entries per positive-demand type and one per zero-demand type, hence at most \(\tau(m+1)\) entries overall. Explicitly writing their integer bundle vectors also takes polynomial space and time.

The finite-buyer model in the [cited Eickhoff et al. paper](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/bitstreams/7e5cbedc-7d73-46f1-a781-5bba41f9e3f4/download) imposes individual supply bounds in its demand definition. Here, allowing the full bundles \(d_t e_i\) and splitting a type’s mass among them is what makes the compact aggregate formulation exact.
