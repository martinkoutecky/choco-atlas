The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) alternatives, a distribution \(\mu\) over ranking types satisfying the paper's Mallows assumption with dispersion \(\theta\) and ground-truth ranking, and a finite respondent scale \(n\), sample respondents from \(\mu\) and run LDP-KwikSort:RR with \(K\) queries and per-query privacy budget \(\epsilon/K\). Can the type-mass version of the protocol output estimated pairwise comparisons and a KwikSort ranking while retaining Theorem 2's bound of fewer than \(6\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}}\) pairwise-comparison errors with probability at least \(1-2^{-6\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}}}\), where \(\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}}=2\binom{m}{2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2K}{(\epsilon+2K)^2}\frac{(\theta^*)^2n}{m(m-1)}\right)\) and \(\theta^*=\frac{\theta}{2-\theta}\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) strict ranking types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with masses summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Let \(m\geq2\), \(n\geq1\), \(1\leq K\leq\binom m2\) be integers, and let \(\epsilon>0\) and \(\theta\in[0,1]\) be binary rationals. The distributional promise is that there exists a strict ground-truth ranking \(\pi^*\) such that every pair agrees with \(\pi^*\) with probability \(p_M=1/(2-\theta)\); no independence between different pairs within a ranking is assumed. The curator does not receive \(\pi^*\).
LDP-KwikSort:RR samples \(n\) independent rankings from \(\mu\). Independently for each respondent, it chooses a uniformly random subset of \(K\) distinct unordered pairs, independently of the rankings; each true comparison bit is retained with probability \(p=e^{\epsilon/K}/(1+e^{\epsilon/K})\) and flipped otherwise, using independent response coins.
For each labeled pair \(a<b\), let \(Y_{ab}\) and \(Y_{ba}\) count reports favoring its two orientations. Output the symbolic estimate \(\widehat d_{ab}=(Y_{ab}-Y_{ba})/(2p-1)\), with both counters zero for unqueried pairs. KwikSort recursively chooses a uniform pivot and places each other alternative before or after it according to these estimates, resolving zero estimates by independent fair coins.
Define \(d_{ab}=\mu(a\succ b)-\mu(b\succ a)\) and the comparison-error count \(E=|\{(a,b):a<b,\ d_{ab}\widehat d_{ab}<0\}|\); ties are not errors. Probability includes respondent sampling, query selection, response coins, and sorting randomness.
Set \(\theta^*=\theta/(2-\theta)\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}}=2\binom m2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\epsilon^2K}{(\epsilon+2K)^2}\frac{(\theta^*)^2n}{m(m-1)}\right)\).

Is implementing this support-input protocol with the uniform guarantee \(\Pr(E<6\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}})\geq1-2^{-6\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}}}\) possible in randomized polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,n,K\), or is this computational task NP-hard?

The finite-agent result states the same comparison-error bound under its stated Mallows assumption; it is a probabilistic utility result, rather than a complexity classification. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04486).

A promised input violating the guarantee

Let \(N=\binom m2\). Choose
\(m=25\), \(N=300\), \(K=N\), \(n=54\), \(\epsilon=60000\), and \(\theta=1/2\).

Let \(\pi^*\) be \(1\succ2\succ\cdots\succ25\), and let \(\overline{\pi^*}\) be its reversal. Define the society by
\(\mu(\pi^*)=2/3\) and \(\mu(\overline{\pi^*})=1/3\).
Thus its support size is \(\tau=2\).

Every pair agrees with \(\pi^*\) with probability
\(2/3=1/(2-\theta)\), so the distributional promise holds. Moreover, for every labeled pair \(a<b\),
\(d_{ab}=1/3>0\).
The comparison bits within a sampled ranking are perfectly correlated, which the promise permits.

The claimed failure threshold

Here \(\theta^*=1/3\) and \(\epsilon/K=200\). Write \(\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}}=2N e^{-\alpha}\). Substitution into the specified formula gives

\( \alpha=\dfrac{\epsilon^2K}{(\epsilon+2K)^2}\dfrac{(\theta^*)^2n}{m(m-1)} =\dfrac{n}{18}\left(\dfrac{\epsilon}{\epsilon+2N}\right)^2 =3\left(\dfrac{100}{101}\right)^2 =\dfrac{30000}{10201}. \)

Set \(R=6\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}}=12N e^{-\alpha}\). We will prove
\(109<R<N\).

First, \(\alpha>11/4\). The exponential series gives \(e^2>7\) and \(e^{3/4}>1+3/4=7/4\). Therefore,
\(e^\alpha>e^{11/4}>49/4>12\), and hence \(R<N\).

Second, \(\alpha<3\) and \(e<3\), so
\(e^\alpha<27\). Consequently,

\( R>\dfrac{12N}{27}=\dfrac{400}{3}>109. \)

The elementary inequality \(e<3\) follows from its series: for \(j\geq2\), \(j!\geq2^{j-1}\), with strict inequality for \(j\geq3\).

An event producing errors on every pair

Let \(F\) be the event that:

  • All \(54\) respondents sample \(\overline{\pi^*}\).
  • Every randomized-response coin retains its true comparison bit.

Because \(K=N\), every respondent is queried on every pair. On \(F\), for each \(a<b\),
\(Y_{ab}=0\) and \(Y_{ba}=54\). Since \(p>1/2\), this implies
\(\widehat d_{ab}=-54/(2p-1)<0\).
Together with \(d_{ab}=1/3>0\), it follows that \(E=N=300\).

Sorting randomness cannot change this conclusion: \(E\) is defined entirely by the comparison estimates. Since \(R<N\), we have
\(F\subseteq\{E\geq R\}\).

There are \(nK=16200\) response coins, each retaining the bit with probability
\(p=e^{200}/(1+e^{200})\). Independence gives

\( \Pr(F)=3^{-54}p^{16200}. \)

Furthermore, \(1-p<e^{-200}\). Bernoulli’s inequality therefore gives

\( p^{16200}\geq1-16200(1-p)>1-16200e^{-200}>\dfrac12. \)

The last inequality holds because \(e>2\), so
\(e^{200}>2^{200}>32400\).

Thus,

\( \Pr(E\geq R)\geq\Pr(F)>\dfrac12\,3^{-54}>\dfrac12\,4^{-54}=2^{-109}>2^{-R}. \)

But the requested guarantee is equivalent to
\(\Pr(E\geq R)\leq2^{-R}\). This is a strict contradiction.

What remains valid without independence between pairs

The counterexample does not prevent a useful expectation bound. In fact, every promised input satisfies
\(\mathbb E[E]\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}}/2\).

Here is a proof that accounts for the random number of queries received by each pair.

Assume \(\theta>0\); when \(\theta=0\), all \(d_{ab}\) vanish and \(E=0\). Fix a pair and orient its reports according to \(\pi^*\). Define

\( q=K/N,\qquad r=2p-1,\qquad b=r\theta^*. \)

For respondent \(i\), let \(X_i\) equal zero if the pair is unqueried, positive one if the report agrees with \(\pi^*\), and negative one otherwise. Then

\( \Pr(X_i=0)=1-q,\qquad \Pr(X_i=1)=q(1+b)/2,\qquad \Pr(X_i=-1)=q(1-b)/2. \)

The variables \(X_i\) are independent across respondents by the stipulated independence of their rankings, query selections, and response coins. The pair is an error exactly when \(\sum_iX_i<0\).

For \(u=\frac12\log((1+b)/(1-b))>0\), Markov’s inequality yields

\( \Pr\!\left(\sum_iX_i<0\right) \leq\left(\mathbb E[e^{-uX_1}]\right)^n =\left(1-q+q\sqrt{1-b^2}\right)^n \leq e^{-nqb^2/2}. \)

Here the equality follows by substituting \(u\); the final inequality uses
\(\sqrt{1-b^2}\leq1-b^2/2\) and \(1-z\leq e^{-z}\).
Also \(b<1\), since finite \(\epsilon\) implies \(r<1\).

Writing \(x=\epsilon/K\), the inequality \(e^x\geq1+x\) gives

\( r=\dfrac{e^x-1}{e^x+1}\geq\dfrac{x}{x+2} =\dfrac{\epsilon}{\epsilon+2K}. \)

Consequently, the error probability of each pair is at most

\( \exp\!\left( -\dfrac{\epsilon^2K}{(\epsilon+2K)^2} \dfrac{(\theta^*)^2n}{m(m-1)} \right). \)

Summing these bounds over the \(N\) pairs proves
\(\mathbb E[E]\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}}/2\), without any independence assumption between pair-error indicators. Markov’s inequality then gives the valid, weaker guarantee

\( \Pr(E<6\lambda_{\mathrm{RR}})\geq11/12. \)

It does not give the requested exponential tail.

Why the cited result does not settle this formulation

The supplied paper states its utility result under a Mallows assumption. Its final aggregation step invokes a Chernoff lemma explicitly requiring independent Bernoulli indicators. That hypothesis does not follow from the pairwise promise in this task. [Source: Section 5.2.1, Theorem 2 and Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04486).

The counterexample satisfies every stated promise and uses only two support rankings. Therefore, the task of faithfully implementing this protocol with the specified uniform guarantee is impossible, even with unlimited computation.

4. Probability of failure and contradiction

The stipulated independence across respondents and response coins gives
\[ \Pr(F)=3^{-54}p^{16200}. \]

\[ 1-p=\frac1{1+e^{200}}<e^{-200}. \]

Consequently, every inequality in the decisive chain is valid:
\[ \Pr(E\ge R) \ge\Pr(F) > \frac12\,3^{-54} > \frac12\,4^{-54} = 2^{-109} > 2^{-R}. \]
The final inequality uses \(R>109\).

The requested guarantee is exactly equivalent to
\[ \Pr(E\ge R)\le2^{-R}. \]
This equivalence remains valid for nonintegral \(R\), since the events \(E<R\) and \(E\ge R\) are complements. The contradiction is rigorous.
