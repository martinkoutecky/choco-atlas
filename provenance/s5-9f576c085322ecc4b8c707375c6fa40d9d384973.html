The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite rooted river tree with type masses μ_t that are positive binary rationals summing to one, per-capita supplies e_t, and rational jointly concave piecewise-linear direct utilities g_t, compute the per-capita withdrawals q_t in a continuous ANBS outcome. For each type t, define available water by F_t(q) = ( μ_t e_t + ∑_{s∈P(t)} μ_s(e_s-q_s) )/μ_t, where P(t) is its set of upstream ancestors; impose 0 ≤ μ_t q_t ≤ μ_t e_t + ∑_{s∈P(t)} μ_s(e_s-q_s). Is the resulting ANBS withdrawal vector a maximizer of ∑_t μ_t g_t(F_t(q),q_t), and how efficiently can it be computed?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge2\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Each type occupies one vertex of a river tree directed toward its downstream root; \(P(t)\) contains all strictly upstream vertices.
Supplies \(e_t\ge0\) are binary rationals. Direct utilities \(g_t(F,q)\) are jointly concave piecewise-linear functions, explicitly represented as minima of rational affine functions on \(0\le q\le F\); they may be negative, satisfy \(g_t(F,0)=0\), and are nondecreasing in \(F\). Let \(m\) count all listed affine pieces and \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For per-capita withdrawals \(q_t\), set \(F_t(q)=(\mu_te_t+\sum_{s\in P(t)}\mu_s(e_s-q_s))/\mu_t\). Feasibility requires \(0\le q_t\le F_t(q)\). Write \(W(q)=\sum_t\mu_tg_t(F_t(q),q_t)\) and \(w=\max_{q\text{ feasible}}W(q)\).
Input also includes rational disagreement utilities \(d_t\ge0\) and bargaining weights \(a_t\ge0\), with \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=1\) and the promise \(\sum_t\mu_td_t<w\). Assume some welfare maximizer has positive withdrawal at a nonroot vertex.
Transfers \(z_t\in\mathbb R\) are unrestricted per-capita monetary transfers subject to \(\sum_t\mu_tz_t\le0\); final utilities are \(u_t=g_t(F_t(q),q_t)+z_t\), with \(u_t\ge d_t\).
A continuous asymmetric Nash bargaining solution (ANBS) is any feasible maximizer of \(\prod_{t:a_t>0}(u_t-d_t)^{\mu_ta_t}\). Multiple withdrawal vectors are allowed; computing withdrawals means finding any one of them exactly. For a rational threshold \(r\), the associated decision problem asks whether an ANBS outcome has \(W(q)\ge r\).

Does every continuous ANBS withdrawal vector maximize \(W\), and does every maximizer of \(W\) extend to an ANBS outcome?
Can continuous ANBS withdrawals be computed exactly in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the associated threshold decision problem NP-hard?

For finitely many agents with transferable utility, ANBS water allocations coincide with utilitarian welfare maximizers, including the correspondence between sets of maximizers; this is a structural result, not a complexity bound. [Houba, van der Laan and Zeng](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/13051.pdf).
The published version also states the welfare/transfer decomposition. [Author institutional record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/asymmetric-nash-solutions-in-the-river-sharing-problem-2/). No result for exactly the support-mass formulation above was located.

1. Result

Both correspondences hold. Every continuous ANBS withdrawal vector maximizes \(W\), and every maximizer of \(W\) extends to an ANBS outcome.

Moreover, one such withdrawal vector can be computed exactly in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). The threshold decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm P\); consequently, it cannot be NP-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof below establishes the correspondence directly for the support-mass formulation, including zero bargaining weights and multiple welfare maximizers. It is consistent with the welfare/transfer decomposition in [Houba, van der Laan and Zeng](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/13051.pdf).

2. Water feasibility and existence of a welfare maximizer

Let \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) index the supported vertices, and let \(\rho\) be the downstream root. Introduce aggregate withdrawals and supplies:

\(x_t=\mu_tq_t,\qquad b_t=\mu_te_t.\)

Because every \(\mu_t\) is positive, passing between \(x\) and \(q\) is bijective. Define the subtree supply

\(E_t=\sum_{s\in P(t)\cup\{t\}}b_s.\)

Multiplying the constraint \(q_t\le F_t(q)\) by \(\mu_t\) gives

\(x_t+\sum_{s\in P(t)}x_s\le E_t.\)

Thus the aggregate withdrawal polytope is precisely

\(X=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^\tau:\ x_t\ge0,\ \sum_{s\in P(t)\cup\{t\}}x_s\le E_t\ \text{for every }t\right\}.\)

It is nonempty because \(x=0\) is feasible. At the root, every other vertex is strictly upstream, so

\(\sum_{t\in T}x_t\le E_\rho.\)

Consequently \(X\) is bounded. It is closed, being defined by finitely many linear inequalities, and hence compact.

Each utility is a finite minimum of affine functions and is therefore continuous on its domain. Since \(F_t(q)\) depends affinely on \(q\), \(W\) is continuous on the compact feasible withdrawal set. Therefore \(w\) is finite and attained.

3. Exact transfer optimization for a fixed withdrawal vector

Write

\(D=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_td_t,\qquad I=\{t:a_t>0\},\qquad \theta_t=\mu_ta_t.\)

The normalization of the bargaining weights implies

\(\sum_{t\in I}\theta_t=1,\)

so \(I\) is nonempty and all its \(\theta_t\) are positive.

Fix a feasible withdrawal vector \(q\), and abbreviate its direct utilities by \(G_t=g_t(F_t(q),q_t)\). A proposed final utility vector \(u\) is achievable by transfers if and only if

\(\sum_t\mu_tu_t\le W(q).\)

Indeed, necessity follows from \(u_t=G_t+z_t\) and the transfer budget. For sufficiency, set \(z_t=u_t-G_t\); the displayed inequality is exactly the required transfer budget.

Define the nonnegative gains \(v_t=u_t-d_t\). The transfer and individual-rationality constraints become

\(v_t\ge0,\qquad \sum_t\mu_tv_t\le R,\qquad R=W(q)-D.\)

In particular, if \(R<0\), no individually rational outcome extends \(q\). If \(R=0\), positivity of all masses forces every \(v_t=0\), so the Nash product is zero.

Now suppose \(R>0\). Define the positive constant

\(K=\prod_{t\in I}a_t^{\theta_t}.\)

We claim that the maximum Nash product for this fixed \(q\) is exactly \(KR\), attained uniquely at

\(v_t=a_tR\quad\text{for every }t.\)

First, this gain vector is feasible because

\(\sum_t\mu_ta_tR=R.\)

Its Nash product is

\(\prod_{t\in I}(a_tR)^{\theta_t}=KR,\)

using \(\sum_{t\in I}\theta_t=1\).

For the upper bound, any gain vector having \(v_t=0\) for some \(t\in I\) has product zero, strictly below \(KR\). Otherwise all active gains are positive, and

\(\log\left(\frac{\prod_{t\in I}v_t^{\theta_t}}{KR}\right)=\sum_{t\in I}\theta_t\log\left(\frac{v_t}{a_tR}\right).\)

The elementary inequality \(\log h\le h-1\), valid for \(h>0\) with equality only at \(h=1\), gives

\(\sum_{t\in I}\theta_t\log\left(\frac{v_t}{a_tR}\right)\le\sum_{t\in I}\theta_t\left(\frac{v_t}{a_tR}-1\right)=\frac{\sum_{t\in I}\mu_tv_t}{R}-1\le0.\)

This proves the upper bound. Equality forces \(v_t=a_tR\) for every \(t\in I\). These active gains already exhaust the entire budget \(R\); because the remaining gains are nonnegative and their masses positive, every inactive gain must be zero. This is exactly \(v_t=a_tR\) for all types.

Thus, whenever \(W(q)>D\), the unique optimal final utilities and transfers conditional on \(q\) are

\(u_t=d_t+a_t(W(q)-D),\)

\(z_t=d_t+a_t(W(q)-D)-g_t(F_t(q),q_t).\)

Their aggregate transfer is zero.

4. Equality of the withdrawal solution sets

By the promise,

\(S=w-D>0.\)

Every welfare maximizer \(q^*\) extends to the outcome

\(u_t^*=d_t+a_tS,\qquad z_t^*=d_t+a_tS-g_t(F_t(q^*),q_t^*).\)

These utilities satisfy individual rationality, and

\(\sum_t\mu_tz_t^*=D+S-w=0.\)

The Nash product equals \(KS>0\).

For any feasible bargaining outcome with withdrawal vector \(q\), Section 3 bounds its Nash product by \(K(W(q)-D)\) when \(W(q)>D\), and gives product zero when \(W(q)=D\). If \(W(q)<w\), either bound is strictly below \(KS\). Therefore such a withdrawal vector cannot occur in an ANBS outcome.

Conversely, every welfare maximizer attains the global Nash-product bound \(KS\) using the transfers above. Hence

\(\{\text{ANBS withdrawal vectors}\}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{q\text{ feasible}}W(q).\)

In fact, every ANBS outcome has the same final utilities \(u_t^*=d_t+a_tS\). Its transfers are uniquely determined once its withdrawal vector is fixed.

This proof uses neither uniqueness of welfare maximizers nor strictly positive bargaining weights. The additional promise about positive withdrawal at a nonroot vertex is compatible with the result but unnecessary for it.

5. A polynomial-size rational linear program for welfare

Write the supplied representation of each utility as

\(g_t(F,q)=\min_{1\le j\le m_t}\{\alpha_{tj}F+\beta_{tj}q+\gamma_{tj}\},\qquad \sum_tm_t=m.\)

Introduce aggregate available water

\(f_t=\mu_tF_t(q)=E_t-\sum_{s\in P(t)}x_s.\)

For every water-feasible vector,

\(0\le x_t\le f_t\le E_t\le E_\rho.\)

Multiplying the utility representation by \(\mu_t>0\) yields

\(\mu_tg_t(F_t(q),q_t)=\min_j\{\alpha_{tj}f_t+\beta_{tj}x_t+\mu_t\gamma_{tj}\}.\)

Introduce variables \(h_t\) for aggregate direct utilities. Consider the linear program with objective

\(\max_{x,f,h}\sum_th_t,\)

subject to the following constraints:

  • \(f_t+\sum_{s\in P(t)}x_s=E_t\) for every \(t\);
  • \(0\le x_t\le f_t\) for every \(t\);
  • \(h_t\le\alpha_{tj}f_t+\beta_{tj}x_t+\mu_t\gamma_{tj}\) for every listed piece \(j\) of type \(t\).

For a fixed feasible \((x,f)\), the largest permissible \(h_t\) is exactly \(\mu_tg_t(f_t/\mu_t,x_t/\mu_t)\). Since every \(h_t\) has positive objective coefficient, every optimal LP solution satisfies these equalities. Therefore the LP optimum is exactly \(w\), and every optimal LP withdrawal vector gives a welfare maximizer.

Negative direct utilities cause no difficulty: the variables \(h_t\) are permitted to be negative.

For completeness, the LP can be made bounded without changing its optimum. Compute

\(H=\max_{t,j}\{E_\rho(|\alpha_{tj}|+|\beta_{tj}|)+\mu_t|\gamma_{tj}|\},\)

and add \(-H\le h_t\le H\). Every listed affine value lies in this interval on the feasible water domain, so its minimum does too. Hence every genuine utility assignment remains feasible. The augmented LP is nonempty and bounded.

6. Exact polynomial-time computation

The augmented LP has \(3\tau\) variables and \(O(m+\tau)\) constraints. All coefficients are rational.

Its construction has polynomial bit complexity:

  • The upstream sets can be computed explicitly in polynomial time in \(\tau\).
  • Each \(b_t=\mu_te_t\), subtree supply \(E_t\), coefficient \(\mu_t\gamma_{tj}\), and bound \(H\) is obtained through polynomially many rational arithmetic operations.
  • Products and sums of these explicitly encoded rationals have polynomial bit length. For example, a sum can use the product of its input denominators as a common denominator, whose bit length is at most their total bit length.
  • Consequently, even a dense encoding of the LP has size polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

We apply the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: a linear program with binary-encoded rational data can be solved in polynomial time in its encoding length, returning an exact rational optimum when it is feasible with finite optimum. Exact polynomial-time LP algorithms were established by [Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239). Here the hypotheses hold because the constructed LP is rational, nonempty, and bounded.

There is also no output-length obstruction. A nonempty bounded rational polytope has an optimal vertex. After clearing denominators, its coordinates are ratios of determinants of square integer matrices formed from active constraints. If their dimension is \(n\) and their entries have absolute value at most \(M\), each determinant has absolute value at most \(n!M^n\). Thus these coordinates have polynomial bit length.

From an exact optimal LP solution, return

\(q_t^*=x_t^*/\mu_t.\)

The division is valid because \(\mu_t>0\), and preserves polynomial bit length. By Section 4, this is an ANBS withdrawal vector. If desired, its exact rational transfers follow from the formula in Section 4.

The algorithm never needs to evaluate the Nash product or its possibly irrational value.

Finally, every ANBS outcome has \(W(q)=w\). Therefore the threshold question is equivalent to

\(w\ge r.\)

Compute \(w\) exactly by the LP and compare the two rationals exactly. This takes polynomial time, including the bit length of \(r\) in the decision input.
