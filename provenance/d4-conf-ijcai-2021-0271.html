Theorem 6.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu\), a differentiable coalition-value function \(F\) with \(F(0)=0\), a gradient oracle on the path \(\lambda\mu\), a partition of \([0,1]\) with known coordinate range bounds, and a query budget \(B\), estimate \(\Phi_t=\int_0^1 \partial_t F(\lambda\mu)\,d\lambda\) for every type. Using iid samples of \(\lambda\) within the path intervals, find an allocation of the \(B\) queries that minimizes a valid vector-SEBB confidence radius, and return the estimate, radius, and aggregate allocations \(\mu_t\hat\Phi_t\); characterize the query budget needed to reach a specified radius.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; the number of gradient coordinates is \(m=\tau\).
Let \(F\) be continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of \([0,\mu]\), with \(F(0)=0\); values and derivatives may have either sign. A gradient query at \(\lambda\mu\) returns all coordinates of \(g(\lambda)=\nabla F(\lambda\mu)\). Define \(\Phi_t=\int_0^1g_t(\lambda)\,d\lambda\).
Input includes a rational partition \(0=a_0<\cdots<a_K=1\), weights \(w_i=a_i-a_{i-1}\), rational bounds \(\ell_{it}\le g_t(\lambda)\le u_{it}\) on interval \(i\), widths \(D_{it}=u_{it}-\ell_{it}\ge0\), confidence parameter \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and integer budget \(B\ge4K\).
Each query samples \(\lambda\) uniformly within its chosen interval, independently of other draws. Spend \(2K\) queries on two pilot draws per interval and let \(v_{it}\) be their unbiased coordinate sample variances. Choose integer production counts \(n_i\ge2\) with \(\sum_i n_i=B-2K\), using the pilot data; production draws are independent of the pilot.
For any nonnegative variance table \(s\), put \(p=\delta/\tau\), \(\alpha_t=\frac4{17}\sum_iw_i^2D_{it}^2/n_i\), \(\beta_t=\log(3/p)\max_i(w_i^2D_{it}^2/n_i^2)\), and \(\gamma_t=2\sum_iw_i^2(n_i-1)s_{it}/n_i^2+\log(6K/p)\sum_iw_i^2D_{it}^2/n_i^2+\beta_t\).
Define the vector-SEBB radius by \(R(n,s)^2=\log(6/p)\sum_t[\alpha_t+(\sqrt{\beta_t}+\sqrt{\gamma_t})^2]\). Allocation means global minimization of the planning criterion \(R(n,v)\), holding the pilot variances fixed.
From production samples, form interval means \(\bar g_i\), unbiased variances \(s_{it}\), estimates \(\hat\Phi_t=\sum_iw_i\bar g_{it}\), and aggregate allocations \(\mu_t\hat\Phi_t\); the reported radius must satisfy \(\Pr(\|\hat\Phi-\Phi\|_2\le R(n,s))\ge1-\delta\).
Use an ideal sampling and gradient-oracle model, counting queries separately from arithmetic. For numerical allocation, input rational \(r\ge0\) and \(\eta>0\), with the promise that the minimum planning radius is at most \(r\) or at least \(r+\eta\); \(L\) is the total bit length of the rational inputs and supplied pilot statistics. State running times in \(m,\tau,K,B,L\), and \(\log(1/\eta)\).

Is this promised vector-SEBB allocation problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?
What query budget is necessary and sufficient, in the worst case over admissible \(F\), to obtain a reported radius at most a specified \(\varepsilon>0\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\)?

For finite vector-valued strata, a vector-SEBB concentration bound is known; the accompanying sequential method minimizes a one-step forecast with variances held fixed, without establishing global allocation optimality. [Burgess and Chapman](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0011.pdf)
No result settling this specific continuous allocation problem was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive support masses to sum to one and identified the gradient dimension with the support size.
  • Strengthened differentiability to continuous differentiability on a neighborhood of the coalition box so the path integrals are well-defined; allowed signed values and gradients.
  • Specified positive-length rational intervals, uniform independent draws, full-vector gradient queries, and nonnegative coordinate range widths.
  • Specified Euclidean error for the per-type vector and an explicit confidence parameter; aggregate allocations are mass-weighted estimates.
  • Resolved the undefined empirical allocation objective using pilot variances held fixed, following the discrete method's forecasting convention; requested global minimization rather than assuming the sequential method achieves it.
  • Separated pilot and production samples so data-dependent planning does not itself invalidate the production confidence statement; counted both phases against the budget and required two samples per interval in each phase.
  • Specified the with-replacement vector-SEBB expression, using unbiased production variances and a coordinate-adjusted probability parameter.
  • Used a separated-threshold promise and an ideal sampling and gradient-oracle model to make numerical allocation complexity precise without assuming a finite representation of an arbitrary differentiable function.
  • Interpreted the requested budget characterization as a worst-case probabilistic guarantee on the reported radius.

Results

The promised numerical allocation problem is NP-hard, even when every pilot variance is zero and $B$ is linear in $K$.

For the prescribed reported radius $R(n,s)$, the necessary and sufficient worst-case query budget, up to universal multiplicative constants, is
$$ \Theta\left( K+ \frac{qH^2}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{\sqrt{qc}\,J^{3/2}}{\varepsilon} \right), $$
where
$$ q=\log\frac{6\tau}{\delta}, \qquad c=\log\frac{6K\tau}{\delta}, \qquad d_i=w_i\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}D_{it}^2}, $$
and
$$ H=\sum_{i=1}^K d_i, \qquad J=\sum_{i=1}^K d_i^{2/3}. $$

Explicit necessary and sufficient finite-budget inequalities are proved below. The sufficient inequality makes $R(n,s)\le\varepsilon$ for every production outcome consistent with the stated bounds.

All logarithms are natural. We assume the bounds admit at least one admissible $F$. If every width is zero, the radius is identically zero, and the prescribed protocol requires exactly $4K$ queries.

1. Validity of the continuous, pilot-dependent confidence radius

Let $\mathcal P$ denote all pilot data. Conditional on $\mathcal P$, the chosen counts $n_i$ are fixed, and the production draws remain independent uniform draws within their respective intervals.

For coordinate $t$, the mean in interval $i$ is
$$ \theta_{it} = \frac{1}{w_i} \int_{a_{i-1}}^{a_i}g_t(\lambda)\,d\lambda. $$
Consequently,
$$ \mathbb E[\hat\Phi_t\mid\mathcal P] = \sum_iw_i\theta_{it} = \Phi_t. $$

We now justify applying the finite-stratum bound to these continuous distributions. The finite vector-SEBB theorem, with sampling with replacement, positive weights $w_i$, coordinate widths $D_{it}$, unbiased sample variances, and coordinate failure parameter $p=\delta/\tau$, gives the radius in the question: the replacement factors are both $1/n_i$. See the multidimensional extension in [Burgess and Chapman](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0011.pdf).

Fix the pilot data and hence the counts. For each positive integer $M$, replace the uniform distribution in interval $i$ by the uniform distribution on its $M$ midpoint values of $g$. Couple a continuous production draw $\lambda$ with the midpoint of the cell containing it.

Because $F$ is continuously differentiable, $g$ is continuous and therefore uniformly continuous on $[0,1]$. For the finitely many production draws, this coupling gives almost-sure convergence of:

  • every sampled gradient vector;
  • every interval sample mean and unbiased variance;
  • the stratified estimate and its target;
  • the radius, since its defining operations are continuous.

Every finite approximation obeys the original coordinate bounds, and sampling remains independent across draws. The finite theorem therefore applies to each approximation.

Write $E_M$ for the norm of the approximation’s estimation error and $R_M$ for its radius. Their almost-sure limits are $E$ and $R$. If $E>R$, then eventually $E_M>R_M$, so
$$ \mathbf 1_{\{E>R\}} \le \liminf_{M\to\infty}\mathbf 1_{\{E_M>R_M\}}. $$
Fatou’s lemma gives
$$ \Pr(E>R\mid\mathcal P) \le \liminf_{M\to\infty}\Pr(E_M>R_M\mid\mathcal P) \le\delta. $$
Averaging over the pilot data proves
$$ \Pr\!\left(\|\hat\Phi-\Phi\|_2\le R(n,s)\right)\ge1-\delta. $$

This argument permits arbitrary dependence among coordinates of a sampled vector. It also permits either sign of the gradient values.

The aggregate allocations have the expected target:
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\Phi_t = \int_0^1\nabla F(\lambda\mu)\cdot\mu\,d\lambda = F(\mu). $$
Moreover, on the confidence event,
$$ \left\| (\mu_t\hat\Phi_t)_t-(\mu_t\Phi_t)_t \right\|_2 \le \left(\max_t\mu_t\right)R(n,s). $$

2. NP-hardness of global allocation

2.1 Source problem and construction

We reduce from CLIQUE on regular simple graphs. This problem is NP-complete; the restriction follows, for example, by complementing cubic instances of INDEPENDENT SET. The regular-graph statement is recorded as Proposition 17 of [Fomin, Golovach, Strømme, and Thilikos](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-020-00677-8).

Let $G$ be a $d$-regular graph on $K$ vertices, with edge set $E$, and let the requested clique size satisfy $2\le k\le K$. Discarding the trivial cases preserves NP-hardness.

Create one stratum per vertex, with
$$ a_i=\frac{i}{K}, \qquad w_i=\frac1K. $$
Set
$$ B=4K+k, \qquad \delta=\frac12, \qquad M=10K. $$

Use one coordinate per edge and one additional coordinate per vertex. Pad with zero-width coordinates until the coordinate count $\tau$ is a power of two. Thus
$$ m=\tau, \qquad K+|E|\le\tau<2(K+|E|)\le2K^2. $$
Assign every supported type mass $1/\tau$. These masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one.

All lower bounds are zero. Choose the upper bounds as follows:

  • For an edge coordinate associated with $\{u,v\}$, its width is $K$ in strata $u,v$ and zero elsewhere.
  • For the additional coordinate associated with vertex $i$, its width is $KM$ in stratum $i$ and zero elsewhere.
  • Padding coordinates have width zero everywhere.

Supply pilot variances $v_{it}=0$. These statistics are realizable: the admissible function $F\equiv0$ produces them deterministically. The bounds need not be tight.

For this construction, put
$$ a=\frac4{17}, \qquad q=\log(12\tau), \qquad b=\log(6\tau), \qquad c=\log(12K\tau). $$
We minimize the normalized squared objective
$$ \mathcal J(n)=\frac{R(n,0)^2}{q}. $$

2.2 The additional coordinates force counts to be two or three

A coordinate supported only in stratum $i$, with $w_iD_{it}=M$, contributes
$$ M^2f(n_i) $$
to $\mathcal J$, where
$$ f(x)=\frac ax+\frac h{x^2}, \qquad h=\left(\sqrt b+\sqrt{c+b}\right)^2. $$
Since
$$ f''(x)=\frac{2a}{x^3}+\frac{6h}{x^4}>0, $$
its consecutive decrements decrease as $x$ increases. In particular,
$$ f(2)-2f(3)+f(4) = \frac a{12}+\frac{13h}{144} \ge \frac{13c}{144}. $$

An edge coordinate with endpoint counts $x,y$ contributes
$$ e(x,y) = a\left(\frac1x+\frac1y\right) + \left( \frac{\sqrt b}{\min(x,y)} + \sqrt{ c\left(\frac1{x^2}+\frac1{y^2}\right) + \frac b{\min(x,y)^2} } \right)^2. $$
This expression decreases in either count. For $x,y\ge2$,
$$ 0\le e(x,y)\le e(2,2) \le a+c+b \le3c. $$

The production budget is
$$ \sum_i n_i=B-2K=2K+k. $$
If some count $n_u\ge4$, another count must equal two: otherwise the total would exceed $3K$, whereas $2K+k\le3K$.

Transfer one sample from such a stratum $u$ to a stratum $v$ with count two. The additional-coordinate contribution decreases by at least
$$ M^2\frac{13c}{144}. $$
The total edge-coordinate contribution can increase by at most
$$ |E|\,e(2,2)\le\frac32K^2c. $$
But
$$ M^2\frac{13c}{144} = \frac{325}{36}K^2c > \frac32K^2c. $$
The transfer strictly decreases the objective.

Therefore every global minimizer satisfies
$$ n_i\in\{2,3\}, $$
with exactly $k$ strata receiving three samples.

2.3 The remaining objective encodes a clique

Write
$$ e_{22}=e(2,2), \qquad e_{23}=e(2,3), \qquad e_{33}=e(3,3). $$
Direct substitution gives
$$ e_{22} = a+\frac{\left(\sqrt b+\sqrt{2c+b}\right)^2}{4}, $$
$$ e_{33} = \frac{2a}{3} +\frac{\left(\sqrt b+\sqrt{2c+b}\right)^2}{9}, $$
and
$$ e_{23} = \frac{5a}{6} +\frac{\left(3\sqrt b+\sqrt{13c+9b}\right)^2}{36}. $$

Define the interaction coefficient
$$ \lambda=e_{22}-2e_{23}+e_{33}. $$
Expanding the preceding expressions yields
$$ \lambda = \frac{\sqrt b}{18} \left( 13\sqrt{2c+b} - 6\sqrt{13c+9b} - 5\sqrt b \right). $$
Both quantities in the following comparison are positive, and
$$ 36(13c+9b)-169(2c+b)=130c+155b>0. $$
Hence
$$ 6\sqrt{13c+9b}>13\sqrt{2c+b}, $$
which proves
$$ \lambda<-\frac{5b}{18}<0. $$

Let $S$ be the $k$ vertices whose strata receive three samples, and let $e_G(S)$ be the number of edges induced by $S$. Regularity gives exactly
$$ dk-2e_G(S) $$
edges with one endpoint in $S$, and
$$ |E|-dk+e_G(S) $$
edges with neither endpoint in $S$.

The additional-coordinate contribution is independent of $S$. Therefore
$$ \mathcal J(n)=T_0+\lambda e_G(S), $$
where
$$ T_0 = M^2\bigl((K-k)f(2)+kf(3)\bigr) + |E|e_{22} + dk(e_{23}-e_{22}). $$
Since $\lambda<0$, global minimization maximizes $e_G(S)$.

Let
$$ C_k=\binom{k}{2}, \qquad Z=q(T_0+\lambda C_k), \qquad \kappa=-\lambda. $$
If $G$ has a $k$-clique, then
$$ \min_n R(n,0)^2=Z. $$
If it has no $k$-clique, then every $k$-vertex set has at most $C_k-1$ edges, so
$$ \min_n R(n,0)^2\ge Z+q\kappa. $$

2.4 The gap is large enough for the numerical promise

We verify positivity and polynomial bounds before taking square roots.

Because every $e_{xy}$ above is at most $3c$,
$$ |\lambda|\le12c. $$
Also $f(3)\ge c/9$. Consequently,
$$ \frac Zq \ge \frac{100}{9}K^3c-9K^2c >0. $$

For every feasible count vector,
$$ f(n_i)\le f(2)\le2c. $$
Using the edge bound,
$$ R(n,0)^2 \le 202qcK^3. $$
Our choice of $\tau$ implies
$$ q\le c\le\log(24K^3)\le4K \qquad(K\ge2). $$
Thus every feasible radius is at most
$$ U=100K^3. $$

Moreover,
$$ q\kappa>\frac{5qb}{18}>\frac14. $$
Writing $\rho=\min_nR(n,0)$, a no-instance therefore satisfies
$$ \rho-\sqrt Z = \frac{\rho^2-Z}{\rho+\sqrt Z} \ge \frac{q\kappa}{2U} > \frac1{8U}. $$
Set
$$ G_0=\frac1{8U}. $$

Compute a rational $r$ satisfying
$$ \sqrt Z\le r\le\sqrt Z+\frac{G_0}{4}, $$
and set
$$ \eta=\frac{G_0}{2}. $$
Then:

  • A clique gives $\min_nR(n,0)\le r$.
  • Absence of a clique gives $\min_nR(n,0)\ge r+\eta$.

Certified evaluation of the displayed logarithms and square roots constructs $r$ in polynomial time. Their arguments and the radius are polynomially bounded in $K$, and the required precision is only $O(\log K)$ bits beyond the integer part. In particular,
$$ \frac1\eta=1600K^3. $$

The constructed input has
$$ m=\tau=O(K^2), \qquad B=O(K), \qquad L=\operatorname{poly}(K), \qquad \log(1/\eta)=O(\log K). $$
This proves NP-hardness of the promised numerical problem. Unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, it has no algorithm polynomial in all the parameters requested in the question.

3. Necessary query budget

For any feasible count vector, define
$$ A(n)=\sum_i\frac{d_i^2}{n_i}, \qquad C(n)=\sum_i\frac{d_i^2}{n_i^2}, \qquad N=\sum_i n_i=B-2K. $$

For every nonnegative variance table $s$, the radius satisfies
$$ R(n,s)^2 \ge q\left(\frac4{17}A(n)+cC(n)\right). $$
Indeed, summing the $\alpha_t$ terms gives $(4/17)A(n)$, and summing just the width-sum terms in $\gamma_t$ gives $cC(n)$. All remaining terms are nonnegative.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$ H^2 = \left( \sum_i\frac{d_i}{\sqrt{n_i}}\sqrt{n_i} \right)^2 \le A(n)N. $$
Hölder’s inequality, with exponents $3$ and $3/2$, gives
$$ J = \sum_i \left(\frac{d_i^2}{n_i^2}\right)^{1/3} n_i^{2/3} \le C(n)^{1/3}N^{2/3}. $$
Hence
$$ A(n)\ge\frac{H^2}{N}, \qquad C(n)\ge\frac{J^3}{N^2}. $$

It follows that every reported radius obeys the deterministic inequality
$$ R(n,s)^2 \ge \frac{4qH^2}{17N} + \frac{qcJ^3}{N^2}. $$

Thus even positive probability of reporting a radius at most $\varepsilon$ requires
$$ \frac{4qH^2}{17(B-2K)} + \frac{qcJ^3}{(B-2K)^2} \le\varepsilon^2. $$
In particular, the following conditions are necessary:
$$ B\ge4K, $$
$$ B-2K\ge\frac{4qH^2}{17\varepsilon^2}, $$
and
$$ B-2K\ge \frac{\sqrt{qc}\,J^{3/2}}{\varepsilon}. $$

These lower bounds hold for every admissible $F$, every pilot outcome, and every production outcome.

4. Sufficient query budget for globally optimal pilot allocation

4.1 Deterministic bounds on sample variances

For $n$ observations in an interval of width $D$, their biased empirical variance is at most $D^2/4$. To verify this, let $\bar x$ be their mean. The inequality
$$ (x-\ell)(u-x)\ge0 $$
implies
$$ \frac1n\sum_jx_j^2-\bar x^2 \le (\bar x-\ell)(u-\bar x) \le\frac{D^2}{4}. $$
Therefore their unbiased variance satisfies
$$ s\le\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{D^2}{4}. $$

For the two pilot observations, the unbiased variance is
$$ v=\frac{(x_1-x_2)^2}{2}\le\frac{D^2}{2}. $$

Let
$$ A_t(n)=\sum_i\frac{w_i^2D_{it}^2}{n_i}, \qquad C_t(n)=\sum_i\frac{w_i^2D_{it}^2}{n_i^2}, $$
and put
$$ b=\log\frac{3\tau}{\delta}. $$
Since $b\le c$, the pilot bounds imply
$$ \gamma_t(n,v) \le A_t(n)+cC_t(n)+\beta_t(n), $$
and
$$ \beta_t(n)\le bC_t(n). $$
Using $(\sqrt x+\sqrt y)^2\le2x+2y$, we obtain
$$ R(n,v)^2 \le q\left(\frac{38}{17}A(n)+6cC(n)\right). $$

4.2 Comparing production and planning radii

The production variance bound gives
$$ 2\sum_iw_i^2\frac{(n_i-1)s_{it}}{n_i^2} \le\frac12A_t(n). $$
Because the pilot variances are nonnegative,
$$ \gamma_t(n,s)\le\gamma_t(n,v)+\frac12A_t(n). $$

For fixed $\beta\ge0$, define
$$ h_\beta(x)=(\sqrt\beta+\sqrt x)^2. $$
On $x\ge\beta$, increasing $x$ by $z\ge0$ increases $h_\beta(x)$ by at most $2z$: for positive $x$, its derivative is
$$ 1+\sqrt{\beta/x}\le2, $$
and the case $\beta=0$ is immediate.

Both planning and production $\gamma_t$ contain $\beta_t$. It follows that
$$ R(n,s)^2\le R(n,v)^2+qA(n). $$
Since
$$ R(n,v)^2\ge\frac{4q}{17}A(n), $$
we conclude
$$ R(n,s)^2\le\frac{21}{4}R(n,v)^2. $$

This comparison is deterministic and holds for every feasible count vector.

4.3 A feasible allocation provides an upper bound on the optimum

Assume $H>0$; otherwise all widths are zero. Define benchmark counts
$$ n_i^{(0)} = 2+ \left\lceil \frac{24qHd_i}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{8\sqrt{qc}\,J^{1/2}d_i^{2/3}}{\varepsilon} \right\rceil. $$
They satisfy
$$ \sum_i n_i^{(0)} \le 3K+ \frac{24qH^2}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{8\sqrt{qc}\,J^{3/2}}{\varepsilon}. $$

Suppose the total budget obeys
$$ B\ge \left\lceil 5K+ \frac{24qH^2}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{8\sqrt{qc}\,J^{3/2}}{\varepsilon} \right\rceil. $$
The benchmark counts fit within the production budget $B-2K$. Add any unused production samples to obtain a feasible vector $\tilde n$.

For strata with $d_i>0$, the defining lower bounds on $\tilde n_i$ imply
$$ A(\tilde n)\le\frac{\varepsilon^2}{24q} $$
and
$$ C(\tilde n)\le\frac{\varepsilon^2}{64qc}. $$
Zero-width strata contribute zero to both sums.

Let $n^\star$ be any global minimizer of the pilot planning criterion. Its defining optimality gives
$$ R(n^\star,v)^2 \le R(\tilde n,v)^2. $$
Applying the preceding inequalities,
$$ \begin{aligned} R(n^\star,s)^2 &\le \frac{21}{4}R(n^\star,v)^2\\ &\le \frac{21}{4}q \left( \frac{38}{17}A(\tilde n)+6cC(\tilde n) \right)\\ &\le \frac{21}{4} \left( \frac{38}{17\cdot24}+\frac6{64} \right)\varepsilon^2\\ &= \frac{2135}{2176}\varepsilon^2 < \varepsilon^2. \end{aligned} $$

Therefore the displayed budget is sufficient for every admissible $F$ and every sample outcome. Combining this deterministic radius bound with Section 1 gives
$$ \Pr\!\left( \|\hat\Phi-\Phi\|_2\le R(n^\star,s)\le\varepsilon \right)\ge1-\delta. $$

The lower and upper bounds prove the query order stated at the beginning. Global optimality is used here to compare against the benchmark allocation; finding that optimizer remains NP-hard.

For example, if $w_i=1/K$ and $D_{it}=D$ for every stratum and coordinate, then
$$ H=\sqrt{\tau}\,D, \qquad J^{3/2}=\sqrt{K\tau}\,D, $$
and the query order becomes
$$ \Theta\left( K+ \frac{q\tau D^2}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{D\sqrt{qcK\tau}}{\varepsilon} \right). $$
The widths are essential parameters: unrestricted widths preclude a uniform budget depending only on $\tau,K,\delta,\varepsilon$.

5. Arithmetic running times

The protocol uses exactly $B$ gradient queries. Reading the returned vectors and maintaining pilot and production statistics takes $O(mB)$ ideal arithmetic operations. Computing the estimates and aggregate allocations takes another $O(mK)$ operations.

For the supplied rational planning data, a fixed count vector’s radius can be evaluated to absolute error at most $\eta/8$ in
$$ O(mK)\, \operatorname{poly}\left( L+\log B+\log(mK+1)+\log^+(1/\eta) \right) $$
bit operations, where
$$ \log^+(x)=\max\{0,\log x\}. $$
The coordinate maxima involve rational quantities and can be compared exactly. The logarithms and square roots admit certified rational approximations in polynomial time. Their arguments, intermediate magnitudes, and required absolute precision have bit bounds polynomial in the displayed parameters.

There are exactly
$$ C_B = \binom{B-3K-1}{K-1} $$
feasible production count vectors: subtracting two from every count leaves $B-4K$ samples to distribute among $K$ strata.

Enumerating these vectors and evaluating each radius to error $\eta/8$ solves the promised decision problem. If $\tilde\rho$ is the smallest evaluated radius, compare it with $r+\eta/2$. In the lower alternative,
$$ \tilde\rho\le r+\eta/8; $$
in the upper alternative,
$$ \tilde\rho\ge r+7\eta/8. $$
Thus the comparison is correct.

The resulting allocation decision time is
$$ C_B\,O(mK)\, \operatorname{poly}\left( L+\log B+\log(mK+1)+\log^+(1/\eta) \right). $$
The same enumeration produces an additively accurate minimizer. The NP-hardness reduction rules out a general polynomial running time in $m,\tau,K,B,L,\log^+(1/\eta)$ unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.
