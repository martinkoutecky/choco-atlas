Result

For the stipulated \(m=2\), finding an exact minimizing feasible tuple is polynomial-time solvable. The associated threshold decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\).

The proof below gives an algorithm using \(O(\tau^3)\) exact rational operations on numbers of polynomial bit length. It handles split atoms, coincident facilities, and the specified universal feasibility constraints.

The cited paper assumes absolute continuity, an interval support, and a differentiable density positive in its interior. Thus its distributional result does not directly establish this discrete complexity classification. The following argument is independent of that result. [Source: Basic Assumptions](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.16034v2#S2).

1. Exact transport formula for a fixed mass cut

Define cumulative masses \(C_0=0\) and \(C_i=\sum_{k=1}^{i}w_k\) for \(1\le i\le\tau\). Positivity gives \(0=C_0<C_1<\cdots<C_\tau=1\).

Fix an ordering of the capacities, and write \(a=q_{\pi(1)}\), \(b=q_{\pi(2)}\). If the left facility receives mass \(t\), the capacity constraints are exactly

\(t\in[A,B],\qquad A=1-b,\quad B=a.\)

The hypotheses imply \(0<A\le B<1\). This transport mass \(t\) is independent of the ranking levels \(v_1,v_2\).

For \(0<t<1\), define

\(\ell(t)=\min\{i:C_i\ge t\},\qquad h(t)=\min\{i:C_i>t\}.\)

For locations \(y_1\le y_2\), set

\(H(t;y_1,y_2)=\max\{|x_1-y_1|,\ |x_{\ell(t)}-y_1|,\ |x_{h(t)}-y_2|,\ |x_\tau-y_2|\}.\)

Lemma. For every \(0<t<1\) and \(y_1\le y_2\),

\(W_\infty\bigl(\mu,t\delta_{y_1}+(1-t)\delta_{y_2}\bigr)=H(t;y_1,y_2).\)

Proof. A labeled transport plan consists of nonnegative numbers \(\gamma_{ij}\) satisfying \(\gamma_{i1}+\gamma_{i2}=w_i\) and \(\sum_i\gamma_{i1}=t\). If the locations coincide, any coupling can be split into these two labels without changing its distances.

Consider the prefix plan

\(\gamma^*_{i1}=\min\{w_i,\max\{0,t-C_{i-1}\}\},\qquad \gamma^*_{i2}=w_i-\gamma^*_{i1}.\)

It sends mass \(t\) to the left facility. Its positive left assignments have indices \(1,\ldots,\ell(t)\); its positive right assignments have indices \(h(t),\ldots,\tau\). The maximum distance on each of these ordered sets occurs at an endpoint, so this plan has cost \(H(t;y_1,y_2)\).

Conversely, suppose a labeled plan has cost at most \(\rho\). We show that the prefix plan also has cost at most \(\rho\).

For any index with \(C_{i-1}<t\), which receives positive left assignment in the prefix plan:

  • If \(x_i<y_1-\rho\), then \(y_2\ge y_1\) implies that neither facility can serve \(x_i\), contradicting \(w_i>0\).
  • If \(x_i>y_1+\rho\), no index \(k\ge i\) can supply the left facility. Its total received mass is therefore at most \(C_{i-1}<t\), a contradiction.

Thus every positive prefix assignment to the left has distance at most \(\rho\).

For any index with \(C_i>t\), which receives positive right assignment in the prefix plan:

  • If \(x_i>y_2+\rho\), neither facility can serve it.
  • If \(x_i<y_2-\rho\), no index \(k\le i\) can supply the right facility. All their mass must go left, forcing left mass at least \(C_i>t\).

These are again contradictions. Hence every positive prefix assignment to the right also has distance at most \(\rho\).

The prefix plan is therefore optimal, proving the formula. \(\square\)

In particular, at \(t=C_i\), the left support ends at \(x_i\) and the right support starts at \(x_{i+1}\). Inside \(C_{i-1}<t<C_i\), both facilities serve positive portions of the atom at \(x_i\).

2. Replacing the mass interval by finitely many rational cuts

For each capacity ordering, form the sorted set of distinct breakpoints

\(E_\pi=\{A,B\}\cup\{C_i:1\le i<\tau,\ A\le C_i\le B\}.\)

Let \(T_\pi\) contain every element of \(E_\pi\), together with the midpoint of every two consecutive distinct elements of \(E_\pi\).

Then \(|T_\pi|\le 2\tau+1\). If \(A=B\), the set contains just that single cut.

On each open interval between consecutive breakpoints, both \(\ell(t)\) and \(h(t)\) are constant: their defining comparisons can change only at cumulative masses. Consequently, \(H(t;y_1,y_2)\) is constant there for every fixed pair of locations. Breakpoint values are included separately, preserving the strict inequality in the definition of \(h(t)\).

It follows that

\(\min_{t\in[A,B]}H(t;y_1,y_2)=\min_{t\in T_\pi}H(t;y_1,y_2).\)

Thus the exact optimized transport cost for any fixed ordered locations can be computed with \(O(\tau)\) evaluations after preprocessing the indices \(\ell(t),h(t)\).

3. Enumerating exactly the feasible quantile locations

The quantile cells are

\(Q^{-1}(x_1)=[0,C_1],\qquad Q^{-1}(x_i)=(C_{i-1},C_i]\quad\text{for }i\ge2.\)

Hence every tuple produces an ordered pair \((x_i,x_j)\) with \(i\le j\).

Coincident locations

Every pair \((x_i,x_i)\) is feasible: choose \(v_1=v_2=C_i\), which satisfies \(Q(v_1)=Q(v_2)=x_i\). Equal levels are feasible by the stated definition.

If a feasible tuple has distinct levels but coincident quantile locations, replacing its levels by this equal pair preserves its locations, permutation, and objective value. Thus these diagonal candidates cover all coincident-location possibilities.

Distinct locations

Fix \(i<j\) and a capacity ordering \(\pi\). The pair \((x_i,x_j)\) is obtainable by a feasible tuple if and only if

\(C_i\ge A\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_{j-1}<B.\)

For necessity, distinct locations imply distinct levels. Universal feasibility requires \(v_1\ge A\) and \(v_2\le B\). The quantile cells require \(v_1\le C_i\) and \(v_2>C_{j-1}\), giving the stated inequalities.

For sufficiency, choose the explicit rational witness

\(v_1=C_i,\qquad v_2=\min\{C_j,B\}.\)

Since both \(C_j\) and \(B\) exceed \(C_{j-1}\), we have

\(v_1=C_i\le C_{j-1}<v_2.\)

Furthermore, \(Q(v_1)=x_i\), \(Q(v_2)=x_j\), \(v_1\ge A\), and \(v_2\le B\). Therefore both universal feasibility inequalities hold.

The strict test \(C_{j-1}<B\) is essential: equality would place the largest permitted second level in an earlier quantile cell.

Exact numerator minimum

For every admitted candidate \((\pi,i,j)\), compute

\(N_{\pi,i,j}=\min_{t\in T_\pi}H(t;x_i,x_j).\)

By Sections 1–2, this is exactly its optimized numerator.

There are two permutations and \(O(\tau^2)\) candidate location pairs. Every feasible tuple is represented by an admitted candidate, and every admitted candidate has the explicit feasible witness above. Therefore

\(N_*=\min_{\text{admitted }(\pi,i,j)}N_{\pi,i,j}\)

is attained and is exactly the minimum feasible numerator. Retaining its witness gives an exact minimizing tuple.

4. Computing the unrestricted denominator exactly

For a fixed mass cut \(t\), the left served support has endpoints \(x_1,x_{\ell(t)}\), and the right served support has endpoints \(x_{h(t)},x_\tau\).

For any endpoints \(u\le v\) and center \(y\), the triangle inequality gives

\(v-u\le |u-y|+|v-y|\le2\max\{|u-y|,|v-y|\}.\)

Thus the minimum maximum endpoint distance is \((v-u)/2\), attained at \(y=(u+v)/2\).

Applying this separately to the two served supports gives

\(G(t)=\frac12\max\{x_{\ell(t)}-x_1,\ x_\tau-x_{h(t)}\}.\)

Both minima are attained simultaneously at

\(y_1=\frac{x_1+x_{\ell(t)}}2,\qquad y_2=\frac{x_{h(t)}+x_\tau}2.\)

These centers satisfy the required ordering: \(\ell(t)\le h(t)\), so

\(x_1+x_{\ell(t)}\le x_{h(t)}+x_\tau.\)

Consequently, the lemma proves that the minimum unrestricted cost at cut \(t\) is exactly \(G(t)\).

The function \(G\) depends only on \(\ell(t),h(t)\), so the same finite-cut reduction applies. Allowing both capacity permutations yields

\(D=\min_{\sigma\in S_2}\ \min_{t\in T_\sigma}\frac12\max\{x_{\ell(t)}-x_1,\ x_\tau-x_{h(t)}\}.\)

This computes \(D\) exactly as a rational number, including cases where an atom is split or the optimal centers coincide.

5. Correctness and bit complexity

The algorithm performs the following finite steps:

  • Compute the cumulative masses and, for both permutations, the cut sets and their indices.
  • Enumerate the feasible location pairs using Section 3 and compute \(N_*\), retaining a witnessing tuple.
  • Compute \(D\) using Section 4.
  • Return the retained tuple. For a threshold \(r\), answer yes exactly when \(N_*\le rD\).

The promise \(D>0\) ensures that minimizing \(N\) is equivalent to minimizing \(R=N/D\), and that the threshold comparison is valid.

There are \(O(\tau^2)\) location candidates, each requiring \(O(\tau)\) cost evaluations. Straightforward preprocessing also fits within \(O(\tau^3)\) rational operations.

These are polynomial-time bit operations as well. Cumulative sums of the encoded rational masses have polynomial bit length: even for general rational encodings, a common denominator can be the product of the input denominators, whose bit length is bounded by their total encoding length. The remaining computed numbers are obtained through comparisons, differences, and midpoints of these cumulative masses, capacities, and coordinates. They therefore also have polynomial bit length. Exact rational arithmetic and comparisons consequently take polynomial time in \(L\).

For the decision problem, the final product and comparison are polynomial in \(L\) and the encoding length of \(r\). This proves polynomial-time solvability for the specified \(m=2\), without making a claim for variable \(m\).
