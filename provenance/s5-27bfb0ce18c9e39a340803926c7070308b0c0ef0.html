The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of user types \(R\) with rational masses \(p_r>0\), \(\sum_{r\in R}p_r=1\), and resource classes \(J\) with rational per-capita capacities \(q_j\ge 0\), \(\sum_{j\in J}q_j\ge 1\), each type-resource pair \((r,j)\) has an unknown reward distribution \(F_{rj}\) on \([0,1]\) with mean \(\mu_{rj}\). Define \(\gamma^*=\max\{\gamma: \exists x\ge 0,\ \sum_j x_{rj}=p_r,\ \sum_r x_{rj}\le q_j,\ x_{rj}>0\Rightarrow\mu_{rj}\ge\gamma\}\). At each of \(T\) turns, users choose resource classes through a decentralized protocol using their type and private reward and admission history; if demand exceeds capacity, a uniformly selected mass up to \(q_j\) is admitted, and rejected users receive zero. What is the minimax order of \(\mathcal R_\infty(T)=\sum_{t=1}^T(\gamma^*-\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{a\in[0,1]}\mathbb E[u_a(t)])\), and can a decentralized protocol attain that rate using only local feedback?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(p_r\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\ge1\) resource classes with binary-rational capacities \(q_j\ge0\), with \(\sum_jq_j\ge1\); \(L\) is the total encoding length of the public support and capacities.
Users form an atomless probability population, indexed by \(a\in[0,1]\), with type masses \(p_r\). An admitted type-\(r\) user choosing class \(j\) receives a fresh reward from an unknown stationary distribution \(F_{rj}\) on \([0,1]\), of mean \(\mu_{rj}\); rewards are independent across users and turns.
Use an exact-aggregation continuum model: the population distribution of private histories evolves by the individual transition probabilities. If demand mass for class \(j\) is \(d_j>0\), each demander is admitted with probability \(\min\{1,q_j/d_j\}\), independently of type, history and reward; admitted mass is exactly \(\min\{d_j,q_j\}\). Rejected users receive zero, and every user observes their admission bit and realized utility.
All users cooperate, share a clock and know the public support and capacities. They use the same measurable protocol conditional on type, with independent private randomness; actions may depend only on type, time and private action, reward and admission history. They have no observable individual identifiers, communication, aggregate observations or knowledge of the reward laws or horizon \(T\).

Partial solution: general decentralized sublinear regret

The general minimax order remains unresolved. I prove three advances:

  • An exact characterization of the public inputs for which minimax regret is zero.
  • An anytime decentralized upper bound $O(\sqrt{kT})$ for one type and arbitrary capacities, where $k$ is the number of positive-capacity classes.
  • An anytime decentralized upper bound of order $T^{2/3}$, with explicit parameter dependence, for arbitrary support and capacities.

The general construction uses only the permitted local feedback. Its main ingredient is a sensing procedure whose estimates have the same conditional distribution across types. This permits aggregate capacity control even though users’ realized estimates differ.

1. Definitions and preliminary facts

Let
$$ J=\{j:q_j>0\},\qquad k=|J|,\qquad c_j=\min\{q_j,1\}\quad(j\in J), $$
and put
$$ C=\sum_{j\in J}c_j. $$
Then $1\le k\le m$ and $C\ge1$. Clipping capacities at one preserves all feasible allocations because the population has total mass one.

Define
$$ X=\left\{x\ge0: \sum_{j\in J}x_{rj}=p_r,\quad \sum_{r=1}^{\tau}x_{rj}\le c_j \right\}. $$
This set is nonempty: with
$$ v_j=\frac{c_j}{C}, $$
the allocation
$$ x^0_{rj}=p_rv_j $$
belongs to $X$.

Typewise expected utility

Under any admissible protocol, users of the same type have the same marginal private-history law. Indeed, their initial laws and transition rules agree, while exact aggregation determines demand and admission probabilities from these laws. Induction over turns therefore gives identical marginal laws within each type.

Write their common unconditional expected utility as $U_r(t)$. Since every supported type has positive mass,
$$ \operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{a\in[0,1]}\mathbb E[u_a(t)] =\min_{1\le r\le\tau}U_r(t). $$

This equality concerns unconditional expectations, including private protocol randomness.

Benchmark attainment and stability

The benchmark is attained. Every feasible allocation has a nonempty support, and its minimum supported mean belongs to the finite set of usable edge means. Maximizing over feasible supports therefore gives a maximum.

For two mean matrices $\mu,\nu$, let
$$ \varepsilon=\|\mu-\nu\|_\infty. $$
An allocation witnessing $\gamma^*(\mu)$ uses only edges with $\nu_{rj}\ge\gamma^*(\mu)-\varepsilon$. Consequently,
$$ \gamma^*(\nu)\ge\max\{0,\gamma^*(\mu)-\varepsilon\}. $$
Interchanging the matrices proves
$$ |\gamma^*(\mu)-\gamma^*(\nu)|\le\|\mu-\nu\|_\infty. $$

The benchmark can be smaller than an attainable expected utility. For example, with one type, capacities $(3/5,3/5)$, and means $(1,0)$, every feasible allocation uses both classes, so $\gamma^*=0$. Equal action probabilities give expected utility $1/2$. Thus individual instances can have negative regret.

Nevertheless,
$$ 0\le V(T;p,q)\le T. $$
The all-zero reward instance proves the lower bound. Bounded nonnegative utilities and $\gamma^*\le1$ prove the upper bound.

2. Exact characterization of zero minimax regret

Call an edge $(r,j)$ forced if every $x\in X$ has $x_{rj}>0$. Let $E_{\mathrm f}$ be the forced edges.

Lemma 1: explicit forced-edge criterion

For every usable edge,
$$ (r,j)\in E_{\mathrm f} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_r>C-c_j. $$

Proof. If $p_r>C-c_j$, the other classes cannot hold the entire type-$r$ mass, so every feasible allocation uses $(r,j)$.

Conversely, suppose $p_r\le C-c_j$. Allocate type $r$ entirely among the other classes. The remaining total capacity is $C-p_r\ge1-p_r$. Every other type can use every class, so its remaining mass can be distributed within these residual capacities. This produces a full allocation omitting $(r,j)$. $\square$

Define the maximum mass transportable using only forced edges by
$$ M_{\mathrm f}= \max\left\{ \sum_{(r,j)\in E_{\mathrm f}}y_{rj}: y\ge0,\quad \sum_jy_{rj}\le p_r,\quad \sum_ry_{rj}\le c_j \right\}, $$
and put
$$ \beta=1-M_{\mathrm f}. $$
Also define
$$ W=\sum_{(r,j)\notin E_{\mathrm f},\,j\in J}p_r. $$
Since each type has at most $k$ usable incident edges, $W\le k$.

Theorem 2: zero regret and a quantitative obstruction

For every positive integer $T$,
$$ V(T;p,q)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \beta=0. $$
Moreover,
$$ V(T;p,q)\ge\frac{\beta}{1+W}\ge\frac{\beta}{k+1}. $$

Proof of sufficiency. If $\beta=0$, a maximizing forced-edge flow has total mass one. Because the row upper bounds sum to one, every row is saturated, so it gives some $y\in X$ supported on forced edges.

Every forced edge occurs in an allocation witnessing $\gamma^*$, and therefore its mean is at least $\gamma^*$. Let each type-$r$ user choose class $j$ with probability $y_{rj}/p_r$. Aggregate demand respects capacities, so admission is certain and
$$ U_r(t)=\frac1{p_r}\sum_jy_{rj}\mu_{rj}\ge\gamma^*. $$
Regret is nonpositive on every instance. The all-zero instance gives regret zero, proving $V=0$.

Proof of the lower bound. Fix a protocol. Its first-turn action masses, denoted $b_{rj}$, are independent of the reward laws. Include possible choices of zero-capacity classes when defining these masses.

Let $y_{rj}$ be the first-turn admitted masses, and write
$$ R=1-\sum_{r,j\in J}y_{rj},\qquad B=\sum_{(r,j)\notin E_{\mathrm f},\,j\in J}b_{rj}. $$
The admitted forced-edge mass is at most $1-\beta$, while admitted nonforced mass is at most $B$. Hence
$$ \beta\le R+B. $$

Consider a finite family of deterministic reward instances:

  • Every reward is one.
  • For each nonforced usable edge $e$, its reward is zero and every other reward is one.

Each instance has $\gamma^*=1$. For the second family, this follows because deleting a nonforced edge leaves a feasible allocation.

Let $\Delta$ be the largest first-turn regret over this family. In the all-one instance, the population-average rejected fraction is $R$, so the largest typewise rejected fraction is at least $R$. Thus
$$ R\le\Delta. $$
In the instance with edge $(r,j)$ set to zero, type $r$ loses utility whenever it selects that edge. Therefore
$$ \frac{b_{rj}}{p_r}\le\Delta $$
for every nonforced usable edge. Summing gives $B\le W\Delta$, and consequently
$$ \beta\le(1+W)\Delta. $$

Every later regret summand in this family is nonnegative, because $\gamma^*=1$ and utilities are at most one. Thus some instance has total regret at least $\beta/(1+W)$. Taking the infimum over protocols proves the lower bound and necessity. $\square$

Explicit public criteria

For one type, put
$$ M=\{j\in J:C-c_j<1\}. $$
Then
$$ V(T;p,q)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{j\in M}c_j\ge1. $$
Indeed, $M$ is precisely the set of forced classes, and these classes can carry the population exactly when their total capacity is at least one.

For at least two types, the characterization simplifies further:
$$ V(T;p,q)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists j\in J: q_j\ge1,\qquad \sum_{\ell\in J\setminus\{j\}}c_\ell<p_{\min}, $$
where $p_{\min}=\min_rp_r$.

To prove necessity, suppose a forced-edge allocation exists. Choose a smallest-mass type $r_0$ and a forced incident class $j_0$. Then $p_{r_0}\le1/2$.

There cannot be a forced edge $(s,\ell)$ with $\ell\ne j_0$. If $s=r_0$, the two forced-edge inequalities imply
$$ 2p_{r_0}>2C-c_{j_0}-c_\ell\ge C\ge1, $$
contradicting $2p_{r_0}\le1$. If $s\ne r_0$, they imply
$$ p_{r_0}+p_s>2C-c_{j_0}-c_\ell\ge C\ge1, $$
contradicting $p_{r_0}+p_s\le1$.

Thus every forced edge uses $j_0$. A full forced-edge allocation requires $c_{j_0}=1$. Since $(r_0,j_0)$ is forced,
$$ C-c_{j_0}<p_{\min}. $$
This proves necessity. Conversely, the displayed condition makes $(r,j)$ forced for every type $r$, and allocating everyone to $j$ is feasible.

In particular, $k=1$ always gives zero minimax regret.

3. One type: a capacity-independent square-root upper bound

Theorem 3

For $\tau=1$, an anytime admissible decentralized protocol satisfies
$$ \sup_F\mathcal R_\infty^\pi(T) \le\min\{T,C_1\sqrt{kT}\}, $$
where $C_1$ is universal.

There is no reward-gap dependence and no capacity dependence in this regret bound.

Capacity-safe distributions

Let
$$ K=\left\{z\ge0:\sum_jz_j=1,\quad z_j\le c_j\right\}. $$
If every history-conditioned action distribution belongs to $K$, then
$$ d_j(t)=\mathbb E[z_{t,j}]\le c_j\le q_j. $$
Admission is therefore certain.

We construct a bandit algorithm competing with every $x\in K$. The proof uses the square-root regularizer
$$ R(z)=-2\sum_j\sqrt{z_j} $$
and its Bregman divergence
$$ D_R(x,z)= \sum_j\left(\frac{x_j}{\sqrt{z_j}}+\sqrt{z_j}-2\sqrt{x_j}\right). $$

Algorithm in an epoch

Use public epochs of lengths $1,2,4,\ldots$. Fix an epoch length $h\ge2$, and define
$$ v_j=\frac{c_j}{C},\qquad \ell_j=\frac{v_j}{h},\qquad K_h=\{z\in K:z_j\ge\ell_j\}. $$
For a comparator $x\in K$, set
$$ x^{(h)}=(1-1/h)x+v/h\in K_h. $$
Its expected reward differs from that of $x$ by at most $1/h$.

Set
$$ \eta=\sqrt{\frac2h},\qquad \ell_{\min}=\min_j\ell_j,\qquad \xi=h^{-2}. $$
Initialize $z_1$ by an approximate Bregman projection of the uniform vector $u$ onto $K_h$. Each projection is computed as a feasible point within $\ell_{\min}^{3/2}/h^2$ in $\ell_1$ distance of the exact projection.

At each turn:

  • Choose $I$ with distribution $z$ and observe reward $Y$.
  • Form

$$ \widehat L_j=\frac{\mathbf1_{\{I=j\}}(1-Y)}{z_j}. $$

  • Set

$$ w_j=(z_j^{-1/2}+\eta\widehat L_j)^{-2}. $$

  • Approximately project $w$ onto $K_h$ as above.

All actual action distributions belong to $K_h$, so admission is certain.

Projection error

Let $z^\star$ be the exact Bregman projection of $w$ onto $K_h$. Its first-order optimality condition gives
$$ D_R(x^{(h)},z^\star)\le D_R(x^{(h)},w). $$
For any coordinate of $z\in K_h$,
$$ \left| \frac{\partial}{\partial z_j}D_R(x^{(h)},z) \right| = \frac{|z_j-x_j^{(h)}|}{2z_j^{3/2}} \le\ell_{\min}^{-3/2}. $$
Thus the stipulated approximation accuracy gives
$$ D_R(x^{(h)},z^+)\le D_R(x^{(h)},w)+\xi. $$
The same argument at initialization yields
$$ D_R(x^{(h)},z_1)\le D_R(x^{(h)},u)+\xi\le2\sqrt{k}+\xi, $$
because
$$ D_R(x^{(h)},u)=2\sqrt{k}-2\sum_j\sqrt{x_j^{(h)}}. $$

Regret calculation

The update satisfies
$$ \nabla R(w)=\nabla R(z)-\eta\widehat L. $$
Expanding the divergences gives the exact identity
$$ \eta\langle z-x^{(h)},\widehat L\rangle = D_R(x^{(h)},z)-D_R(x^{(h)},w)+D_R(z,w). $$

Put $a_j=\eta\widehat L_j\sqrt{z_j}\ge0$. Direct calculation gives
$$ D_R(z,w) = \sum_j\sqrt{z_j} \left(1+a_j+\frac1{1+a_j}-2\right) = \sum_j\frac{\eta^2z_j^{3/2}\widehat L_j^2}{1+a_j}. $$
Consequently,
$$ \eta\langle z-x^{(h)},\widehat L\rangle \le D_R(x^{(h)},z)-D_R(x^{(h)},z^+) +\eta^2\sum_jz_j^{3/2}\widehat L_j^2+\xi. $$

Conditional on the private past,
$$ \mathbb E[\widehat L_j]=1-\mu_j $$
and
$$ \mathbb E\left[\sum_jz_j^{3/2}\widehat L_j^2\right] = \sum_j\sqrt{z_j}\,\mathbb E[(1-Y_j)^2] \le\sum_j\sqrt{z_j}\le\sqrt{k}. $$
Here $Y_j$ denotes a fresh reward from class $j$.

Summing over any $s\le h$ turns and discarding the nonnegative final divergence gives
$$ \sum_{t=1}^{s} \left(\langle x^{(h)},\mu\rangle-\mathbb E[Y_t]\right) \le \frac{2\sqrt{k}+(s+1)\xi}{\eta}+\eta s\sqrt{k}. $$
Comparing with $x$ adds at most $s/h\le1$. Hence every epoch prefix has comparison regret at most
$$ 2\sqrt{2kh}+3. $$
The length-one epoch uses $v$.

Choose $x$ witnessing $\gamma^*$; then $\langle x,\mu\rangle\ge\gamma^*$. Summing the doubling-epoch bounds, using the geometric sum of their square roots, proves the theorem.

Finite implementation of the projections

For a positive reference $w$, write $a_j=w_j^{-1/2}$. The exact projection has coordinates
$$ \phi_j(\lambda)= \begin{cases} c_j,&a_j+\lambda\le c_j^{-1/2},\\ (a_j+\lambda)^{-2},& c_j^{-1/2}<a_j+\lambda<\ell_j^{-1/2},\\ \ell_j,&a_j+\lambda\ge\ell_j^{-1/2}, \end{cases} $$
where $\sum_j\phi_j(\lambda)=1$. This follows from the first-order conditions of the separable convex minimization problem.

The functions are continuous and nonincreasing, with Lipschitz constant at most two. A root is bracketed by
$$ -\max_ja_j \quad\text{and}\quad \max_j\ell_j^{-1/2}. $$
Bisection to interval width $\varepsilon/(4k)$ gives a vector at the lower endpoint whose coordinate sum is at least one. Reducing its excess mass, without crossing the lower bounds, produces a feasible vector within $\varepsilon$ in $\ell_1$ distance of the exact projection: the endpoint error is at most $2k$ times the interval width, and the correction adds at most the same amount.

For the updates,
$$ a_j\le\ell_{\min}^{-1/2}+\eta/\ell_{\min}. $$
Taking $\varepsilon=\ell_{\min}^{3/2}/h^2$ therefore requires
$$ O\!\left(k\log\frac{2kh}{v_{\min}}\right) $$
operations per projection, where $v_{\min}=\min_jv_j$.

In an exact-real computation model with unit-cost arithmetic, comparisons and square roots, the per-user running time through turn $T$ is
$$ O\!\left(Tk\log\frac{2kT}{v_{\min}}\right), $$
with $O(k)$ stored real values. Private uniform draws are also required. No unspecified exact optimization oracle is used.

4. General support: decentralized admission sensing

Assume now that some usable class has capacity strictly between zero and one. This is the only case requiring the sensing construction; Section 5 handles the remaining case.

Choose a probe class $b$ maximizing $q_b(1-q_b)$ among such classes. Define
$$ s=q_b(1-q_b),\qquad \eta=\frac{1-q_b}{2}, $$
and, for every usable edge,
$$ w_{rj}=\min\{p_r,c_j\},\qquad H_{rj}=\frac{2}{s\,w_{rj}}. $$
Put
$$ D=\tau k,\qquad G=\left\lceil\log_2(2D)\right\rceil,\qquad S=\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j\in J}H_{rj}^2. $$

Theorem 4: general gap-free upper bound

There is an anytime admissible decentralized protocol satisfying
$$ \sup_F\mathcal R_\infty^\pi(T) \le \min\left\{ T,\; C_2\left[ (SG)^{1/3}T^{2/3} + D\log_2(2kT)\log_2(2T) \right] \right\}, $$
where $C_2$ is universal.

The parameter dependence is explicit:
$$ S=\frac4{s^2}\sum_{r,j\in J}\frac1{\min\{p_r,c_j\}^2} \le \frac{4\tau k}{s^2\min\{p_{\min},c_{\min}\}^2}, $$
where $c_{\min}=\min_{j\in J}c_j$.

In particular, for every fixed public input this bound is $o(T)$.

4.1. Measuring one mean using admission bits

Fix an edge $(r,j)$. Choose publicly a class $\ell(j)\in J\setminus\{j\}$.

First use one sampling turn:

  • Type $r$ chooses $j$.
  • Every other type chooses $\ell(j)$.

The demand for $j$ is exactly $p_r$. A type-$r$ user’s observed utility $B\in[0,1]$ consequently satisfies
$$ \mathbb E[B] = \min\{1,q_j/p_r\}\mu_{rj} = \frac{w_{rj}}{p_r}\mu_{rj}. $$

Now use a sensing block:

  • Every type other than $r$ chooses $b$.
  • Each type-$r$ user chooses $\ell(b)$ with probability $\eta B$, and chooses $b$ otherwise.
  • The users repeat this rule with fresh private action randomness, keeping the sampled value $B$ fixed.

Exact aggregation gives the same probe demand in every turn of this block:
$$ d_b=1-\eta p_r\mathbb E[B] =1-\eta w_{rj}\mu_{rj}. $$
Furthermore,
$$ d_b\ge1-\eta=\frac{1+q_b}{2}>q_b. $$
Thus every probe selection yields an admission bit with probability
$$ \alpha_{rj}=\frac{q_b}{1-\eta w_{rj}\mu_{rj}}. $$
This probability determines the mean through
$$ \mu_{rj} = f_{rj}(\alpha_{rj}), \qquad f_{rj}(z)=\frac{1-q_b/z}{\eta w_{rj}}. $$

The protocol never observes $d_b$ or $\alpha_{rj}$ directly. These are quantities used only in the proof.

4.2. Identical estimate laws across types

For an integer $n_{rj}$, let the sensing block last $4n_{rj}$ turns. Each user records the admission bits on its first $n_{rj}$ selections of the probe class. Completion counts probe selections, not admissions.

A type-$r$ user chooses the probe with probability
$$ 1-\eta B\ge1/2. $$
Conditional on $B$, its number of probe selections is binomial with mean at least $2n_{rj}$. The multiplicative Chernoff bound gives
$$ \Pr(\text{fewer than }n_{rj}\text{ probe selections}) \le e^{-n_{rj}/4}. $$
Every nonsender type makes $4n_{rj}$ probe selections and completes deterministically.

Crucially, the action rule continues unchanged after a user has recorded enough bits. Actions in the block depend on $B$ and fresh action coins, never on the recorded admission bits.

Conditional on the sampling utilities and action coins, the recorded probe admission bits are independent Bernoulli variables of parameter $\alpha_{rj}$. This follows from the fixed probe demand and the admission transition rule. Sampling variables for later blocks are fresh and their actions ignore previous recorded bits. Therefore, conditioning on completion does not bias the recorded bits, jointly across blocks.

After measuring every edge, conditional on completing all blocks, every type has the same joint observation law: the product, over edges $(r,j)$, of $n_{rj}$ Bernoulli observations with parameter $\alpha_{rj}$. Denote this common law by $\mathsf Q$.

A type participates as sender only in the $k$ blocks corresponding to its own row. Thus, writing $\delta_r$ for its probability of incomplete measurement,
$$ \delta_r\le k e^{-n_{\min}/4}, \qquad n_{\min}=\min_{r,j}n_{rj}. $$

4.3. Mean estimates and their error

For each edge, let $\bar A_{rj}$ be the recorded admission-bit average. Clip it to the known interval
$$ \left[q_b,\frac{q_b}{1-\eta w_{rj}}\right], $$
and apply $f_{rj}$ to obtain $\widehat\mu_{rj}\in[0,1]$.

Clipping cannot increase error from the true $\alpha_{rj}$. On this interval,
$$ |f'_{rj}(z)| = \frac{q_b}{\eta w_{rj}z^2} \le\frac1{\eta w_{rj}q_b} =H_{rj}. $$
Therefore,
$$ |\widehat\mu_{rj}-\mu_{rj}| \le H_{rj}|\bar A_{rj}-\alpha_{rj}|. $$

Suppose $n_{rj}\ge H_{rj}^2v$ for some public $v>0$. Under $\mathsf Q$, Hoeffding’s exponential-moment inequality gives
$$ \mathbb E_{\mathsf Q} e^{\lambda H_{rj}(\bar A_{rj}-\alpha_{rj})} \le e^{\lambda^2/(8v)} $$
for either sign of $\lambda$. Summing over the $2D$ signed errors and optimizing the exponential-moment bound yields
$$ \mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}\|\widehat\mu-\mu\|_\infty \le \sqrt{\frac{\log(2D)}{2v}} \le \sqrt{\frac{G}{2v}}. $$

4.4. Compatible allocations without agreement on estimates

Fix a deterministic rule that maps each estimated matrix to an allocation
$$ \widehat x\in X $$
witnessing $\gamma^*(\widehat\mu)$. Such a rule is supplied by the deterministic flow computation in Section 6.

A successful type-$r$ user selects class $j$ with probability $\widehat x_{rj}/p_r$. An unsuccessful user selects according to the public fallback allocation $x^0$.

For a realized successful estimate, put
$$ \varepsilon=\|\widehat\mu-\mu\|_\infty. $$
Benchmark stability and the supported-edge condition give
$$ \frac1{p_r}\sum_j\widehat x_{rj}\mu_{rj} \ge\gamma^*(\widehat\mu)-\varepsilon \ge\gamma^*(\mu)-2\varepsilon. $$
Hence
$$ M_r:= \mathbb E_{\mathsf Q} \left[\frac1{p_r}\sum_j\widehat x_{rj}\mu_{rj}\right] \ge \gamma^*-2\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}\varepsilon. $$

Let $\delta=\max_r\delta_r$. Equality of the successful estimate laws gives actual aggregate demand
$$ d_j = \sum_r(1-\delta_r)\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}[\widehat x_{rj}] +\sum_r\delta_rx^0_{rj}. $$
Since every $\widehat x$ and $x^0$ belong to $X$,
$$ d_j\le c_j+\delta c_j=(1+\delta)c_j. $$
Every selected usable class therefore admits with probability at least $1/(1+\delta)$.

Ignoring the nonnegative utility of unsuccessful users,
$$ U_r\ge\frac{1-\delta_r}{1+\delta}M_r. $$
Because $0\le M_r\le1$,
$$ \gamma^*-U_r \le2\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}\varepsilon+2\delta. $$
Consequently every exploitation turn satisfies
$$ \gamma^*-\min_rU_r \le \sqrt{\frac{2G}{v}}+2\delta. $$

This capacity argument does not require continuity or uniqueness of the allocation optimizer.

4.5. Anytime schedule and regret

Use doubling epochs. For an epoch of length $h$, define
$$ Q_h=\left\lceil\log_2(2kh)\right\rceil. $$
Choose a public dyadic rational $v$ satisfying

5. Nonbinding capacities: sharp one-type order and multitype bounds

Suppose every usable class has $q_j\ge1$. Selections in $J$ are always admitted; selections outside $J$ are deterministically rejected. Admission feedback therefore contains no information about other users.

Write

$$ e_r(t)=b_r-U_r(t)\ge0 $$

$$ \gamma^*-\min_rU_r(t) \le\max_re_r(t)\le\sum_re_r(t). $$
Summing proves the stated $\tau\sqrt{kT}$ upper bound.

A type-count-independent upper bound

Within an epoch of length $h$, let every user sample each usable class $n$ times, then choose a class maximizing its private empirical mean for the remainder.

For each type, bounded independent rewards give
$$ \mathbb E\max_j|\widehat\mu_{rj}-\mu_{rj}| \le\sqrt{\frac{\log(2k)}{2n}}. $$
The selected class has expected mean at least
$$ b_r-2\mathbb E\max_j|\widehat\mu_{rj}-\mu_{rj}|. $$
Thus every type has exploitation deficit at most $\sqrt{2G_k/n}$, without summing over types.

Choose $n$ by power-of-two rounding of
$$ \left(\frac{h\sqrt{G_k}}k\right)^{2/3}. $$
If the sampling phase would exceed the epoch, use a fixed usable class. The resulting epoch-prefix bound is
$$ O\!\left((kG_k)^{1/3}h^{2/3}\right). $$
Doubling epochs proves the second upper bound. An epoch-by-epoch public choice between these two algorithms attains their displayed minimum up to universal constants.

Self-contained lower bound

Fix one supported type $r_0$. Give every other type deterministic reward one on each usable class.

For each $j\in J$, define instance $j$ by giving type $r_0$ Bernoulli means $1/2+\varepsilon$ on class $j$ and $1/2$ elsewhere in $J$. Then $\gamma^*=1/2+\varepsilon$.

Let $P_0$ be one type-$r_0$ user’s private-history law when every usable mean is $1/2$, and let $P_j$ be its law in instance $j$. Let $N_j$ count its selections of $j$ through time $T$.

Admission bits are deterministic functions of the selected class in this regime. The adaptive-history relative-entropy chain rule therefore gives
$$ \operatorname{KL}(P_0\Vert P_j) = \mathbb E_0[N_j]\, \operatorname{kl}(1/2,1/2+\varepsilon). $$
For $0<\varepsilon\le1/4$,
$$ \operatorname{kl}(1/2,1/2+\varepsilon) =-\frac12\log(1-4\varepsilon^2) \le4\varepsilon^2. $$
Pinsker’s inequality and $0\le N_j\le T$ imply
$$ \mathbb E_j[N_j] \le \mathbb E_0[N_j] + T\varepsilon\sqrt{2\mathbb E_0[N_j]}. $$
Average over $j$, use $\sum_j\mathbb E_0[N_j]\le T$, and apply Cauchy–Schwarz:
$$ \frac1k\sum_j\mathbb E_j[N_j] \le \frac Tk+T\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{2T}{k}}. $$
Choose
$$ \varepsilon= \frac1{4\sqrt2}\min\{1,\sqrt{k/T}\}. $$
Because $k\ge2$, the right-hand side is at most $3T/4$.

In instance $j$, society’s regret is at least type $r_0$’s regret, which is at least
$$ \varepsilon\bigl(T-\mathbb E_j[N_j]\bigr). $$
Averaging proves that some instance satisfies
$$ \mathcal R_\infty^\pi(T) \ge \frac1{16\sqrt2}\min\{T,\sqrt{kT}\}. $$
This holds for every protocol. Its constant does not depend on the positive mass $p_{r_0}$.

Thus a gap-free logarithmic minimax guarantee is impossible even in a nonbinding special case.
