The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of forecaster types with rational masses \(\mu_a>0\), horizon \(H\), and losses \(\ell_{a,t}\in[0,1]\) revealed each round, compute online weights using the weighted-LMSR update \(w_{a,t}\propto\mu_a\exp(-\eta\sum_{s<t}\ell_{a,s})\). What running time and regret guarantee against the best supported type can be achieved as functions of support size, input bit length, and requested precision?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(A\) of \(\tau\geq1\) forecaster types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), normalized by \(\sum_{a\in A}\mu_a=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; there is no independent \(m\) parameter.
The horizon \(H\geq1\) is known in advance. Before round \(t\)'s losses are revealed, the prescribed weights are \(w_{a,t}=\mu_a\exp(-\eta\sum_{s<t}\ell_{a,s})/\sum_{b\in A}\mu_b\exp(-\eta\sum_{s<t}\ell_{b,s})\), for a fixed positive binary-rational learning rate \(\eta\).
After choosing weights, the learner observes every loss \(\ell_{a,t}\in[0,1]\), supplied as a binary rational; losses may be adversarial. Given binary-rational precision \(0<\delta<1\), output a rational probability vector \(\widehat w_t\) satisfying \(\|\widehat w_t-w_t\|_1\leq\delta\).
Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the support, masses, horizon, learning rate, precision, and revealed losses. Regret means \(\widehat R_H=\sum_{t=1}^{H}\sum_{a\in A}\widehat w_{a,t}\ell_{a,t}-\min_{a\in A}\sum_{t=1}^{H}\ell_{a,t}\).

Is this online evaluation problem solvable in polynomial bit time in \(\tau,H,L,\log(1/\delta)\), or is it NP-hard?
What worst-case regret guarantee holds for the computed weights, including its dependence on the masses, learning rate, and precision, and its value when the learning rate is tuned before play?

For finite experts, stable convex-cost learning updates have a square-root-horizon regret bound; uniform LMSR achieves \(2\sqrt{H\log\tau}\) regret with its prescribed tuning. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.0034v1).
Exponential weights with an arbitrary prior, including the displayed finite-support update, and a prior-dependent regret analysis are already established; the verified result does not specify the binary-rational implementation above. 1. Result

The online evaluation problem is solvable in polynomial bit time in \(\tau,H,L,\log(1/\delta)\), under the usual binary encoding of rational numerators and denominators.

Write \(C_{a,t}=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\ell_{a,s}\), and let \(\mu_{\min}=\min_{a\in A}\mu_a\). For every positive learning rate \(\eta\), the computed weights satisfy, simultaneously for every expert \(a\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{H}\sum_{b\in A}\widehat w_{b,t}\ell_{b,t}-C_{a,H}\leq \frac{\log(1/\mu_a)}{\eta}+\frac{\eta H}{8}+\frac{H\delta}{2}.\)

Consequently, putting \(K=\log(1/\mu_{\min})\),

\(\displaystyle \widehat R_H\leq \min\left\{H,\frac{K}{\eta}+\frac{\eta H}{8}+\frac{H\delta}{2}\right\}.\)

For \(\tau\geq2\), the real-valued tuning \(\eta_*=\sqrt{8K/H}\) gives

\(\displaystyle \widehat R_H\leq \min\left\{H,\sqrt{\frac{HK}{2}}+\frac{H\delta}{2}\right\}.\)

Because the prescribed learning rate must be binary rational, this tuning generally requires approximation. Below, we prove that a binary-rational learning rate chosen before play achieves the displayed bound with any desired positive additive slack, in polynomial bit time.

For \(\tau=1\), output the sole probability vector exactly; regret is zero.

2. Polynomial bit-time evaluation

The arbitrary-prior exponential-weights update is already established in the cited literature. The issue here is implementing it with a certified rational output. [Van der Hoeven, van Erven, and Kotłowski, equations (1)–(2)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v75/hoeven18a/hoeven18a.pdf).

Shifting the exponents

At round \(t\), compute the cumulative losses exactly, and define

\(\displaystyle c_t=\min_{a\in A}C_{a,t-1},\qquad x_a=\eta(C_{a,t-1}-c_t),\qquad v_a=\mu_a e^{-x_a}.\)

The common exponential factor cancels, so \(w_{a,t}=v_a/S\), where \(S=\sum_a v_a\).

Every \(x_a\) is nonnegative. At least one is zero, and therefore

\(\displaystyle S\geq\mu_{\min}>0.\)

This denominator bound permits absolute approximation of the exponentials, even when some \(x_a\) have enormous numerical value.

A rational approximation to the exponential

Set

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\frac{\delta\mu_{\min}}{2},\qquad B=\left\lceil\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\right\rceil,\qquad n=8B.\)

The integer \(B\) can be found by exact rational comparisons with powers of two.

For rational \(x\geq0\), define

\(\displaystyle q(x)= \begin{cases} 0,&x\geq B,\\ \left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^k}{k!}\right)^{-1},&0\leq x<B. \end{cases}\)

We prove that \(\lvert q(x)-e^{-x}\rvert\leq\varepsilon\).

If \(x\geq B\), then \(e^{-x}\leq2^{-B}\leq\varepsilon\).

Otherwise, put \(P=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}x^k/k!\). Taylor’s theorem gives

\(\displaystyle 0\leq e^x-P\leq e^B\frac{B^n}{n!}.\)

The estimate \(n!\geq(n/e)^n\) follows from

\(\displaystyle \log(n!)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log k\geq\int_1^n\log u\,du=n\log n-n+1.\)

Using \(n=8B\), \(e<4\), and \(e/8<1/2\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle e^B\frac{B^n}{n!}\leq2^{2B}\left(\frac{eB}{n}\right)^n\leq2^{2B-n}=2^{-6B}\leq\varepsilon.\)

Since \(1\leq P\leq e^x\),

\(\displaystyle 0\leq\frac1P-e^{-x}=\frac{e^x-P}{Pe^x}\leq e^x-P\leq\varepsilon.\)

Thus the approximation claim holds in both cases. Also, \(q(x)\geq0\), and \(q(0)=1\) exactly.

Normalization and its error

Compute, using exact rational arithmetic,

\(\displaystyle \widetilde v_a=\mu_a q(x_a),\qquad \widetilde S=\sum_a\widetilde v_a,\qquad \widehat w_{a,t}=\frac{\widetilde v_a}{\widetilde S}.\)

An expert with \(x_a=0\) contributes its positive mass exactly, so \(\widetilde S>0\). Hence the output is a rational probability vector.

Let \(E=\sum_a\lvert\widetilde v_a-v_a\rvert\). Because the masses sum to one,

\(\displaystyle E\leq\sum_a\mu_a\varepsilon=\varepsilon,\qquad \lvert\widetilde S-S\rvert\leq E.\)

The triangle inequality gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_a\left|\frac{\widetilde v_a}{\widetilde S}-\frac{v_a}{S}\right| \leq \sum_a\left|\frac{\widetilde v_a}{\widetilde S}-\frac{\widetilde v_a}{S}\right| +\frac{E}{S} =\frac{|\widetilde S-S|+E}{S} \leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\mu_{\min}} =\delta. \)

Thus every round meets the requested precision.

Bit complexity

Under ordinary binary rational encoding, \(\log_2(1/\mu_{\min})=O(L)\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle B,n=O\bigl(L+\log(1/\delta)\bigr).\)

Exact cumulative sums have polynomial bit length: a common denominator can be taken as the product of the revealed denominators, whose total bit length is at most \(L\); numerator lengths grow by at most an additional \(O(\log H)\). Multiplication by the rational \(\eta\) preserves polynomial bit length.

Crucially, the algorithm compares \(x_a\) with \(B\) before constructing its Taylor approximation. Thus its running time does not depend polynomially on the numerical value of \(\eta H\).

For a retained rational \(x=p/d<B\), the Taylor sum has common denominator \(d^{n-1}(n-1)!\). Its numerator and denominator have polynomial bit length, since \(n\) and the bit lengths of \(p,d\) are polynomially bounded. Taking reciprocals, multiplying by masses, summing over \(\tau\) experts, and normalizing also preserve polynomial bit length.

There are polynomially many rational arithmetic operations over all \(H\) rounds. Standard integer arithmetic therefore gives the claimed polynomial bit-time algorithm. It uses only losses already revealed.

3. Regret of the computed weights

A one-round inequality

Fix a probability vector \(p\) and numbers \(z_a\in[0,1]\). Define

\(\displaystyle g(u)=\log\sum_a p_a e^{-uz_a}.\)

Differentiating the finite sum shows that \(g'(0)=-\sum_a p_a z_a\), while \(g''(u)\) is the variance of the \(z_a\) under the exponentially tilted distribution.

Every distribution supported on \([0,1]\) has variance at most \(1/4\): its variance is at most its expected squared distance from \(1/2\), and that squared distance is at most \(1/4\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle g(\eta)=g(0)+\eta g'(0)+\int_0^\eta(\eta-u)g''(u)\,du \leq-\eta\sum_a p_a z_a+\frac{\eta^2}{8}.\)

This proves the needed bounded-loss inequality for every \(\eta>0\).

The potential argument

Define

\(\displaystyle Z_t=\sum_a\mu_a e^{-\eta C_{a,t-1}},\qquad h_t=\sum_a w_{a,t}\ell_{a,t}.\)

Since \(Z_1=1\) and

\(\displaystyle \frac{Z_{t+1}}{Z_t}=\sum_a w_{a,t}e^{-\eta\ell_{a,t}},\)

the one-round inequality, applied with \(p=w_t\) and \(z_a=\ell_{a,t}\), yields

\(\displaystyle \log Z_{H+1}\leq-\eta\sum_{t=1}^{H}h_t+\frac{H\eta^2}{8}.\)

For every expert \(a\),

\(\displaystyle Z_{H+1}\geq\mu_a e^{-\eta C_{a,H}}.\)

Combining these inequalities and dividing by \(\eta>0\) gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{H}h_t-C_{a,H}\leq\frac{\log(1/\mu_a)}{\eta}+\frac{\eta H}{8}.\)

The argument holds for every realized loss sequence, including losses chosen adaptively after the learner outputs its weights.

Effect of approximation

Let \(d_a=\widehat w_{a,t}-w_{a,t}\). Both vectors are probability vectors, so \(\sum_a d_a=0\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{d_a>0}d_a=\sum_{d_a<0}(-d_a)=\frac12\sum_a|d_a|.\)

Using \(0\leq\ell_{a,t}\leq1\),

\(\displaystyle \left|\sum_a d_a\ell_{a,t}\right|\leq\frac12\|\,\widehat w_t-w_t\,\|_1\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.\)

Summing over rounds proves the expert-dependent bound stated in Section 1.

There is also a stronger bound when several experts tie for best cumulative loss. Define

\(\displaystyle C_*=\min_a C_{a,H},\qquad A_*=\{a:C_{a,H}=C_*\},\qquad M_*=\sum_{a\in A_*}\mu_a.\)

Since \(Z_{H+1}\geq M_*e^{-\eta C_*}\), the same argument proves

\(\displaystyle \widehat R_H\leq\frac{\log(1/M_*)}{\eta}+\frac{\eta H}{8}+\frac{H\delta}{2}.\)

Because \(M_*\geq\mu_{\min}\), this implies the uniform worst-case bound using \(K\). Independently, regret is at most \(H\), which justifies the minimum with \(H\).

4. Tuning before play with a binary-rational learning rate

Assume \(\tau\geq2\). Then \(\mu_{\min}\leq1/2\), so \(K\geq\log2>0\). For the uniform worst-case upper bound, minimize

\(\displaystyle F(\eta)=\frac{K}{\eta}+\frac{H\eta}{8}.\)

Its derivative vanishes at \(\eta_*=\sqrt{8K/H}\), and its second derivative is positive. Thus

\(\displaystyle F(\eta_*)=\sqrt{\frac{HK}{2}}.\)

This minimizes the proved upper bound; it is not a claim about the exact minimax regret.

To handle the rational-input requirement, fix any binary-rational \(0<\rho<1\). We can compute a binary-rational rate satisfying

\(\displaystyle \eta_*\leq\eta\leq(1+\rho)\eta_*\)

in polynomial bit time.

Here are explicit ingredients. Write \(1/\mu_{\min}=2^j y\), where the integer \(j\) is found by rational comparisons and \(1\leq y<2\). For such \(y\),

\(\displaystyle \log y=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{2k+1},\qquad z=\frac{y-1}{y+1}\in[0,1/3].\)

After \(N\) terms, the positive remainder is at most \(3^{-2N}\). The same formula computes \(\log2\). Hence rational intervals enclosing \(K=j\log2+\log y\), with width at most \(\rho/8\), require only polynomially many terms and polynomial bit length.

Choose an upper endpoint \(K^+\) satisfying \(K\leq K^+\leq K+\rho/8\). Since \(K\geq1/2\),

\(\displaystyle s=\sqrt{8K^+/H}\leq(1+\rho/8)\eta_*.\)

Exact comparisons of rational squares and dyadic bisection produce a dyadic \(\eta\in[s,s+\rho/H]\). Since \(\eta_*\geq2/\sqrt H\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{\eta}{\eta_*}\leq1+\frac{\rho}{8}+\frac{\rho}{H\eta_*} \leq1+\frac{5\rho}{8}\leq1+\rho.\)

All these computations occur before play.

Writing \(r=\eta/\eta_*\), direct substitution gives

\(\displaystyle F(\eta)=\sqrt{\frac{HK}{2}}\frac{r+r^{-1}}{2} =\sqrt{\frac{HK}{2}}\left(1+\frac{(r-1)^2}{2r}\right).\)

Thus this rational tuning guarantees

\(\displaystyle \widehat R_H\leq \min\left\{H,\left(1+\frac{\rho^2}{2}\right)\sqrt{\frac{HK}{2}}+\frac{H\delta}{2}\right\}.\)

In particular, any prescribed positive binary-rational additive slack \(\kappa\) can be achieved by choosing \(\rho\) sufficiently small. Because \(K=O(L)\), this requires only polynomial bit time in the original parameters and \(\log(1/\kappa)\).

5. Uniform masses and the cited guarantee

For uniform masses, \(K=\log\tau\). The sharper bounded-loss analysis above gives the ideal tuned upper bound

\(\displaystyle \sqrt{\frac{H\log\tau}{2}}+\frac{H\delta}{2},\)

with arbitrarily small additional slack for binary-rational tuning.

The cited paper uses the coarser bound \(H\eta+\log\tau/\eta\); its prescribed real-valued tuning yields \(2\sqrt{H\log\tau}\). [Chen and Vaughan, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.0034v1). Consequently, that guarantee also transfers to computed weights with an additional \(H\delta/2\), subject to the same rational-tuning qualification.

For arbitrary masses, the corresponding prior dependence is \(K=\log(1/\mu_{\min})\), with the stronger expert-dependent and tied-best-expert bounds proved above. A fixed positive precision contributes a potentially linear term; choosing \(\delta=O(\sqrt{K/H})\) keeps this contribution within square-root order.
