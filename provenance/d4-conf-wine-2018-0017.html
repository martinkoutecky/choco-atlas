The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\), rational \(\eta\in[0,1/3)\), and an observed rational distribution \(\rho\) over rankings \(\mathcal R=S_m\), listed on its \(\tau\)-type support, define \(\mathcal H_\eta(\rho)=\{\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal R):\exists\nu\in\Delta(\mathcal R),\ \rho=(1-\eta)\mu+\eta\nu\}\) and \(U=\{(a,b):\exists\mu\in\mathcal H_\eta(\rho)\text{ with }\mu(\{p:a\succ_p b\})=1\}\). Decide whether \(U\) is acyclic; if not, return a directed-cycle certificate. If it is, return a ranking \(q\) respecting every pair in \(U\) that minimizes \(\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal H_\eta(\rho)}D(q,\mu)/\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\), along with a worst-case compatible \(\mu\), where \(D(q,\mu)=\sum_p\mu_p\tau(q,p)\), \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=\min_sD(s,\mu)\), and a zero denominator gives ratio \(1\) when \(D=0\) and \(+\infty\) otherwise.

Result

The sharp uniform guarantee for the task as stated is

\(\alpha(0)=1\), and \(\alpha(\eta)=+\infty\) for every \(0<\eta<1/3\).

Here “uniform” means independent of \(m,\tau,L\). For every fixed positive \(\eta\), there are societies with acyclic \(U\) that force a unique output ranking and whose robust ratio grows without bound as \(m\) grows. Their support size stays fixed.

Consequently, no algorithm, regardless of running time, can achieve a finite uniform guarantee when \(\eta>0\). At \(\eta=0\), deterministic polynomial-time algorithms achieve \(1+\varepsilon\) for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\); achieving \(1\) in polynomial time is equivalent to \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

An exact rational distribution attaining the requested supremum always exists for a ranking respecting \(U\). Thus the negative result is not an issue of nonattainment.

1. Compatible distributions and forced comparisons

Write the observed support as \(p_1,\ldots,p_\tau\), with masses \(r_1,\ldots,r_\tau\). Introduce the unnormalized retained masses \(x_i=(1-\eta)\mu_{p_i}\).

Compatibility is equivalent to

\[ \mathcal P=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^\tau:0\le x_i\le r_i,\ \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}x_i=1-\eta\right\}. \]

Indeed, compatibility forces \(\mu\) to have no mass outside the observed support and gives these inequalities. Conversely, for \(\eta>0\), define \(\mu_{p_i}=x_i/(1-\eta)\) and \(\nu_{p_i}=(r_i-x_i)/\eta\). These are probability distributions satisfying the required decomposition. For \(\eta=0\), the conditions force \(x=r\) and \(\mu=\rho\).

For distinct candidates \(a,b\), let \(w_{ab}=\rho(\{p:a\succ_p b\})\). A compatible distribution can be unanimous on \(a\succ b\) exactly when the capacity on that event is sufficient to retain mass \(1-\eta\). Therefore,

\[ (a,b)\in U\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w_{ab}\ge 1-\eta. \]

Computing these quantities takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Standard deterministic graph search then either returns a directed cycle or constructs a topological ordering of \(U\).

2. Unboundedness for every positive contamination level

Fix \(0<\eta<1/3\). Choose a power of two \(k\) satisfying \(k\eta\ge 1-\eta\). This \(k\) remains fixed throughout the construction.

For any integer \(m\ge 2k\), use candidates \(1,\ldots,m\), and define

\[ q_0=(1,2,\ldots,m),\qquad s_0=(m,m-1,\ldots,1). \]

2.1 Constructing the support

For each \(r\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\), partition the increasing list \(1,\ldots,m\) into consecutive blocks by cutting after every \(i<m\) satisfying \(i\equiv r\pmod{k}\).

Every block has size at most \(k\). Define \(p_r\) by reversing the order of the blocks while keeping the candidates within each block in increasing order.

Two properties follow directly:

  • For each adjacent pair \(i,i+1\), exactly one of the \(k\) rankings \(p_r\) puts \(i+1\) above \(i\): the partition with a cut between them.
  • Each \(p_r\) is close to \(s_0\). They disagree precisely on pairs belonging to the same block.

Choose a dyadic rational \(h\) such that

\[ \eta<h\le \eta+\frac{1-\eta}{m^2}. \]

Such a number exists by density of dyadic rationals. Set

\[ \rho=h\delta_{q_0}+\frac{1-h}{k}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\delta_{p_r}. \]

Because \(k\) is a power of two, every observed mass is dyadic. For \(m\ge 2k\), the displayed rankings are distinct, so \(\tau=k+1\).

2.2 The forced relation is acyclic and forces \(q_0\)

For every \(a<b\), the mass at \(q_0\) implies \(w_{ab}\ge h>\eta\). Hence \(w_{ba}<1-\eta\), so no backward pair \((b,a)\) belongs to \(U\).

For every adjacent pair,

\[ w_{i,i+1}=1-\frac{1-h}{k} \ge 1-\frac{1-\eta}{k} \ge 1-\eta. \]

Thus \(U\) contains the entire chain

\[ 1\succ 2\succ\cdots\succ m. \]

All its edges point forward in \(q_0\), so \(U\) is acyclic. Since it contains this chain, its only respecting ranking is \(q_0\).

2.3 A compatible society makes its ratio grow linearly

Let \(M=\binom m2\), \(B_r=\tau(s_0,p_r)\), and \(B=k^{-1}\sum_r B_r\).

If a block has size \(b\le k\), it contributes \(\binom b2\le b(k-1)/2\) disagreements with \(s_0\). Summing over the blocks gives

\[ B_r\le \frac{m(k-1)}2,\qquad B\le \frac{m(k-1)}2. \]

Since \(q_0\) and \(s_0\) orient every pair oppositely,

\[ \tau(q_0,p_r)=M-B_r. \]

Now remove mass \(\eta\) entirely from the observed atom \(q_0\). Writing \(e=h-\eta>0\), the resulting compatible distribution is

\[ \mu=\frac{e}{1-\eta}\delta_{q_0} +\frac{1-h}{k(1-\eta)}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\delta_{p_r}. \]

The decomposition uses \(\nu=\delta_{q_0}\). Direct calculation yields

\[ D(q_0,\mu)=\frac{(1-h)(M-B)}{1-\eta}, \]

and

\[ D(s_0,\mu)=\frac{eM+(1-h)B}{1-\eta}. \]

Because \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\le D(s_0,\mu)\),

\[ R(q_0)\ge \frac{(1-h)(M-B)}{eM+(1-h)B}. \]

The bounds on \(h,e,B\) give

\[ R(q_0)\ge \frac{(1-1/m^2)m(m-k)}{1+(k-1)m}. \]

For \(m\ge 2k\), we have \(1-1/m^2\ge 1/2\), \(m-k\ge m/2\), and \(1+(k-1)m\le km\). Consequently,

\[ R(q_0)\ge \frac{m}{4k}. \]

This tends to infinity. Every admissible procedure must output \(q_0\), so the construction proves \(\alpha(\eta)=+\infty\) for every fixed positive \(\eta\).

The proof uses neither a cyclic \(U\) nor a zero denominator.

3. Exact maximizing witnesses always exist

Let \(q\) respect an acyclic \(U\).

Compatible point masses

Suppose some compatible distribution is \(\delta_p\). By the capped-simplex characterization, \(\rho_p\ge 1-\eta\). Every comparison in \(p\) then belongs to \(U\), forcing \(q=p\).

For every compatible \(\mu\),

\[ \mu_p\ge \frac{\rho_p-\eta}{1-\eta} \ge \frac{1-2\eta}{1-\eta}>\frac12. \]

For any \(s\ne p\), the triangle inequality gives

\[ D(s,\mu)-D(p,\mu) \ge (2\mu_p-1)\tau(s,p)>0. \]

Thus \(p\) is the unique Kemeny median of every compatible distribution. All relevant ratios equal \(1\), including under the prescribed zero-denominator convention. Hence \(R(q)=1\), attained, for example, by \(\mu=\rho\).

No compatible point mass

Otherwise, \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)>0\) throughout the compact compatible polytope. Since it is the minimum of finitely many continuous linear functions, it is continuous and has a strictly positive minimum there.

For each ranking \(s\), define \(A(x)=\sum_i x_i\tau(q,p_i)\) and \(B_s(x)=\sum_i x_i\tau(s,p_i)\). Then

\[ R(q)=\max_{s\in S_m}\max_{x\in\mathcal P}\frac{A(x)}{B_s(x)}. \]

Each denominator is positive. A linear-fractional function with positive denominator attains its maximum at a vertex: if \(x=\sum_j\lambda_jv_j\), then

\[ \frac{A(x)}{B_s(x)} = \frac{\sum_j\lambda_jB_s(v_j)\,[A(v_j)/B_s(v_j)]} {\sum_j\lambda_jB_s(v_j)}, \]

which is a weighted average of its vertex values.

Every vertex of \(\mathcal P\) is rational; at most one coordinate lies strictly between its lower and upper bounds. Therefore a rational compatible \(\mu\) attaining \(R(q)\) exists, with encoding length polynomial in the input length.

A deterministic exact procedure can enumerate the vertices and rankings, evaluate all ratios, and return a maximizing witness. It can also minimize \(R(q)\) over rankings respecting \(U\). This establishes an exact procedure with exponential running time; it does not assert polynomial-time adversarial-witness search.

4. The uncontaminated case

When \(\eta=0\), the compatible set is \(\{\rho\}\). Choosing an exact Kemeny median gives \(R(q)=1\).

It remains to check the requirement to respect \(U\).

Unanimity repair lemma

Suppose every supported ranking puts \(a\) above \(b\), but \(q\) puts \(b\) above \(a\). Exchange the positions of \(a,b\).

The contribution of \(\{a,b\}\) decreases by \(1\). For each candidate \(c\) positioned between them, the combined contribution of \(\{a,c\},\{b,c\}\) decreases by

\[ 2\rho(\{p:a\succ_p c\succ_p b\})\ge 0. \]

To verify this, the possible orders in a supported ranking are \(c\succ a\succ b\), \(a\succ c\succ b\), and \(a\succ b\succ c\); their decreases are respectively \(0,2,0\). All other pair contributions remain unchanged.

Hence the exchange strictly decreases \(D(q,\rho)\). Every exact Kemeny median therefore respects \(U\), proving \(\alpha(0)=1\).

Deterministic polynomial-time guarantees

The weighted tournament PTAS of Kenyon-Mathieu and Schudy can be derandomized and gives a \(1+\varepsilon\) approximation for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\). Its hypotheses hold here: take the pair weight \(w_{ab}=\rho(a\succ b)\), so \(w_{ab}\ge0\) and \(w_{ab}+w_{ba}=1\). These weights are computable using polynomial-time rational arithmetic from the support representation. [Kenyon-Mathieu and Schudy, Theorem 1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2006/144/download).

Apply the unanimity repair above whenever necessary, deterministically choosing the first violated pair. Each exchange decreases the normalized cost by at least \(1\), while the initial cost is at most \(\binom m2\). Thus at most \(\binom m2\) exchanges occur. The approximation guarantee is preserved, and the required maximizing witness is simply \(\mu=\rho\).

Achieving exactly \(1\) would compute an exact Kemeny median. That problem is NP-hard even for four equally weighted rankings, which are valid dyadic inputs here. [Dwork et al., Appendix B](https://archives.iw3c2.org/www10/cdrom/papers/577/). Conversely, if \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\), polynomial-time decision and search recover an exact median.

Thus the finite uniform guarantees are classified as follows:

| Contamination | Sharp unrestricted-time guarantee | Deterministic polynomial-time guarantees |
|---|---|---|
| \(\eta=0\) | \(1\) | Every fixed \(1+\varepsilon\); exactly \(1\) iff \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\) |
| \(0<\eta<1/3\) | \(+\infty\) | No finite uniform guarantee |

5. Why the familiar contamination bound does not settle this task

The factor \(1/(1-2\eta)\) does hold if one may output an exact Kemeny median \(q\) of \(\rho\) without imposing the comparisons in \(U\).

Indeed, for any compatible decomposition and any Kemeny median \(s\) of \(\mu\), observed optimality and the triangle inequality imply

\[ (1-\eta)\bigl(D(q,\mu)-D(s,\mu)\bigr) \le \eta\,\tau(q,s) \le \eta\bigl(D(q,\mu)+D(s,\mu)\bigr). \]

Therefore \((1-2\eta)D(q,\mu)\le D(s,\mu)\), with the zero-denominator convention also respected.

The present task additionally demands validity simultaneously for every compatible society. Its high-support comparisons can force a long chain even though their individual unanimity witnesses differ. Section 2 proves that this requirement precludes a finite uniform ratio. The reported distributed bound therefore cannot supply the requested guarantee under this precise observation model and output condition. [Melnyk, Wang and Wattenhofer](https://disco.ethz.ch/courses/hs19/distsys/lnotes/byzantine%20preferential%20voting%20paper.pdf).
