The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a pure positional scoring rule with vector \(s_m\), at most \(\tau\) partial-chain types \(P\) with rational masses \(\mu_P\geq 0\) summing to \(1\), and a target \(c\), decide whether there are nonnegative masses \(x_{P,\pi}\), for each full ranking \(\pi\) extending \(P\), such that \(\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P)}x_{P,\pi}=\mu_P\) for each type and \(\sum_{P,\pi}x_{P,\pi}s_m(\pi(c))\geq\sum_{P,\pi}x_{P,\pi}s_m(\pi(d))\) for every \(d\in C\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix a pure positional scoring rule \(r\), whose nonnegative integer scoring vectors \(s_m=(s_{m,1},\ldots,s_{m,m})\) are computable in time polynomial in \(m\), satisfy \(s_{m,1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{m,m}\) and \(s_{m,1}>s_{m,m}\), and obtain each successive vector by inserting one score while preserving this order.
An instance has a candidate set \(C\) with \(m\geq2\) and a distinguished candidate \(c\in C\).
A partial-chain type \(P\) is a strict total order on a nonempty subset of \(C\); candidates outside that subset are incomparable with every candidate. Write \(\operatorname{Ext}(P)\) for its strict full-ranking extensions and \(\pi(a)\) for candidate \(a\)'s position, counted from the top.
The society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_P\), satisfying \(\sum_P\mu_P=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total binary input length, including the scoring vector. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Mass may be divided arbitrarily among extensions: choose real numbers \(x_{P,\pi}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P)}x_{P,\pi}=\mu_P\) for every type.
The decision problem asks whether such masses exist with \(\sum_P\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P)}x_{P,\pi}s_{m,\pi(c)}\geq\sum_P\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P)}x_{P,\pi}s_{m,\pi(d)}\) for every \(d\in C\); ties count as winning.

For each fixed pure positional scoring rule \(r\), is this continuous possible-winner problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete possible winner on partial chains is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for every pure positional scoring rule other than plurality and veto, and is in \(\mathrm{P}\) for plurality and veto.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Treat the scoring rule as fixed and polynomial-time computable, matching the discrete result's rule-by-rule formulation; include its scoring vector in the input-length accounting.
  • Require at least two candidates and nonconstant, nonnegative integer scoring vectors, following the discrete scoring-rule domain.
  • Require each partial chain to order a nonempty subset strictly, with no comparisons involving omitted candidates.
  • Discard zero-mass entries and combine duplicate types so that tau denotes the actual positive support size.
  • Allow completion masses to be arbitrary nonnegative real numbers; retain the given unit total mass and the discrete convention that ties count as winning.

Classification

For every fixed rule \(r\) in the question, the continuous possible-winner problem belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\). Its running time is \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\).

The proof actually applies to every nonincreasing integer scoring vector supplied in binary; purity is unnecessary.

Write \(s_j=s_{m,j}\), let \(R=C\setminus\{c\}\), and put \(S=s_1-s_m>0\).

1. Optimizing over extensions of one partial chain

Lemma. Given a partial-chain type \(P\) and rational weights \(w_a\) for all \(a\in C\), one can compute
\(M_P(w)=\max_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P)}\sum_{a\in C}w_as_{\pi(a)}\),
and a maximizing extension, using \(O(m^2)\) rational arithmetic operations. The bit complexity is polynomial in \(m\), \(L\), and the encoding length of \(w\).

Proof. Write the prescribed chain as \(a_1\succ\cdots\succ a_k\). List the remaining candidates as \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\), where \(q=m-k\), sorted so that
\(w_{b_1}\geq\cdots\geq w_{b_q}\).
Fix an arbitrary order within ties.

There is an optimal extension in which the outside candidates occur in this order. Indeed, suppose outside candidates \(u,v\) occupy positions \(p<\ell\), but \(w_u<w_v\). Swapping them preserves the prescribed chain and changes the objective by
\((w_v-w_u)(s_p-s_\ell)\geq0\).
The inequality follows from the nonincreasing scoring vector. Repeatedly exchanging inversions sorts the outside subsequence without decreasing the objective. Swaps within weight ties have zero effect and permit the chosen tie order.

It therefore suffices to optimize over interleavings of the two fixed lists.

For \(0\leq i\leq k\) and \(0\leq j\leq q\), let \(D_{i,j}\) be the maximum contribution from filling the first \(i+j\) positions with an interleaving of \(a_1,\ldots,a_i\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_j\). Initialize \(D_{0,0}=0\). For every other state, take the maximum of the applicable expressions:

  • \(D_{i-1,j}+w_{a_i}s_{i+j}\), when \(i>0\);
  • \(D_{i,j-1}+w_{b_j}s_{i+j}\), when \(j>0\).

These alternatives are exhaustive: the last candidate in such an interleaving must be \(a_i\) or \(b_j\). Conversely, appending that candidate to an optimal preceding state produces a permissible interleaving. Induction on \(i+j\) proves the recurrence. Thus \(M_P(w)=D_{k,q}\), and backtracking produces a maximizing extension.

Sorting costs \(O(m\log m)\) comparisons, and the table has \((k+1)(q+1)=O(m^2)\) entries.

For bit complexity, let \(B\) be the total encoding length of the weights. Scale all weights by the product of their positive denominators, whose bit length is \(O(B)\). The dynamic program then uses integers representing sums of at most \(m\) products. Their bit lengths are \(O(B+L+\log m)\). Hence the computation is polynomial in the stated binary input lengths. \(\square\)

2. A linear program for the winning margin

For every extension \(\pi\) and rival \(d\in R\), define
\(v_{\pi,d}=s_{\pi(c)}-s_{\pi(d)}\).
Thus \(-S\leq v_{\pi,d}\leq S\).

Consider the finite linear program that maximizes a real variable \(z\), subject to

\(x_{P,\pi}\geq0\quad\text{for every }P,\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P)}x_{P,\pi}=\mu_P\quad\text{for every supported type }P,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_P\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P)}x_{P,\pi}v_{\pi,d}\geq z\quad\text{for every }d\in R.\)

Let its optimum be \(\gamma\). The instance is a yes-instance exactly when \(\gamma\geq0\).

To justify attainment, the possible mass allocations form a nonempty compact product of simplices: every chain has a full-ranking extension. For each allocation, the largest permissible \(z\) is the minimum of its rival margins, a continuous function of the allocation. Consequently its maximum is attained. Every rival margin lies in \([-S,S]\), because the total allocated mass is one. In particular, \(\gamma\) is finite.

The primal has too many variables to enumerate. Its dual has only \(m-1+\tau\) variables.

3. The dual and its separation oracle

Introduce variables \(\lambda_d\geq0\) for \(d\in R\), and unrestricted variables \(t_P\) for the supported types. The dual minimizes
\(\displaystyle \sum_P\mu_Pt_P\),
subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{d\in R}\lambda_d=1,\)

\(\displaystyle t_P\geq\sum_{d\in R}\lambda_dv_{\pi,d} \quad\text{for every }P,\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P).\)

For completeness, this dual follows by adding each margin constraint to the maximization objective with multiplier \(\lambda_d\), and each mass equality with multiplier \(t_P\) in the form
\(t_P(\mu_P-\sum_\pi x_{P,\pi})\).
The resulting coefficient of \(z\) is \(1-\sum_d\lambda_d\); maximizing over unrestricted \(z\) requires that coefficient to vanish. The coefficient of \(x_{P,\pi}\) is
\(\sum_d\lambda_dv_{\pi,d}-t_P\);
maximizing over nonnegative \(x_{P,\pi}\) requires it to be nonpositive. The remaining expression is precisely the stated dual objective.

The primal is feasible and has a finite optimum, so finite-dimensional linear-programming strong duality applies. The dual optimum equals \(\gamma\).

We may additionally impose \(-S\leq t_P\leq S\). Indeed, for any \(\lambda\) satisfying the simplex constraints,
\(\displaystyle h_P(\lambda)=\max_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P)}\sum_{d\in R}\lambda_dv_{\pi,d}\)
lies in \([-S,S]\). Replacing every feasible \(t_P\) by \(h_P(\lambda)\) preserves feasibility and cannot increase the objective, since every \(\mu_P>0\). Hence these bounds preserve the optimum. Call the resulting bounded dual polytope \(Q\). It is nonempty: any simplex vector \(\lambda\), together with \(t_P=S\) for all \(P\), is feasible.

We now give an exact separation oracle for \(Q\). First check the explicit simplex constraints and the bounds on \(t_P\). Any violation directly supplies a separating inequality.

Otherwise, for each supported type \(P\), compute \(h_P(\lambda)\). Using \(\sum_d\lambda_d=1\), we have
\(\displaystyle \sum_{d\in R}\lambda_dv_{\pi,d} =s_{\pi(c)}-\sum_{d\in R}\lambda_ds_{\pi(d)}.\)
This is the objective in the lemma with weights
\(w_c=1\) and \(w_d=-\lambda_d\) for \(d\in R\).

If \(h_P(\lambda)>t_P\), the maximizing extension \(\pi\) returned by the lemma identifies the violated defining inequality
\(\displaystyle t_P\geq\sum_{d\in R}\lambda_dv_{\pi,d}.\)
If no type yields a violation, all defining inequalities hold, so the queried point belongs to \(Q\).

Thus a separation call uses \(O(\tau m^2)\) rational arithmetic operations, with polynomial bit complexity. It examines only the listed support.

4. Exact polynomial-time decision

We use the standard rational linear-programming separation theorem: a bounded rational polytope in \(n\) variables can be optimized over exactly in polynomial time given an exact polynomial-time separation oracle and polynomial bounds on the encoding lengths of its defining inequalities, objective, and containing box. This statement permits exponentially many inequalities and lower-dimensional polytopes. See the separation-implies-optimization theorem in [these linear-programming notes](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs.cmu.edu/academic/class/15859-s15/www/lectures/lecture17.pdf).

All hypotheses hold here:

  • \(Q\) has \(n=m-1+\tau\) variables and is nonempty and bounded.
  • Its coordinates satisfy \(0\leq\lambda_d\leq1\) and \(-S\leq t_P\leq S\).
  • Its defining coefficients are integers among the score differences, together with \(0\), \(1\), and \(-1\). Each defining row therefore has polynomial encoding length in \(m,\tau,L\).
  • The rational objective coefficients are the input masses, whose total encoding length is at most \(L\).
  • The preceding section supplies an exact separation oracle with polynomial bit complexity.

Consequently the dual optimum \(\gamma\) can be computed exactly in time \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\). Comparing it with zero decides the problem, including the boundary case \(\gamma=0\), where ties count as winning.

Therefore every scoring rule specified in the question gives a continuous possible-winner problem in \(\mathrm{P}\). The supplied discrete hardness classification does not transfer to this divisible-mass model.
