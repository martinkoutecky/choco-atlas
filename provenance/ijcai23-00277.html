The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\Theta\), rational distribution \(\mu\), additive disutilities \(d_\theta(c)\) for chore kinds \(c\in C\), rational base occurrence masses \(q_c\), and a finite admissible family \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{|C|}\) of whole bundles, find \(x_{\theta,B}\ge0\) and duplicate masses \(\sigma_c\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_B x_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B}x_{\theta,B}B_c=q_c+\sigma_c\), minimizing \(\sum_c\sigma_c\) or deciding whether it is at most \(1\), subject to support-wise \(\mathrm{EF1}\) and fractional Pareto optimality under the same augmented supply \(q+\sigma\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|\Theta|\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_\theta\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|C|\ge1\) chore kinds, positive rational base occurrence masses \(q_c\), and nonnegative rational additive disutilities \(d_\theta(c)\). Copies have the same disutilities as their originals.
The input explicitly lists a nonempty finite family \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\) of admissible whole bundles, with binary coordinates; the empty bundle is permitted. Write \(d_\theta(B)=\sum_c B_c d_\theta(c)\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation uses nonnegative real masses \(x_{\theta,B}\) and duplicate masses \(\sigma_c\), satisfying \(\sum_Bx_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B}x_{\theta,B}B_c=q_c+\sigma_c\). Each member of mass \(x_{\theta,B}\) receives the whole bundle \(B\).
Support-wise EF1 requires, for every \(x_{\theta,B}>0\) and \(x_{\phi,D}>0\), either \(d_\theta(B)\le d_\theta(D)\), or some \(c\) with \(B_c>0\) satisfies \(d_\theta(B)-d_\theta(c)\le d_\theta(D)\).
For fractional Pareto optimality, let \(S=\{(\theta,B):x_{\theta,B}>0\}\). Require that no nonnegative real array \(y_{\theta,B,c}\), indexed by \((\theta,B)\in S\), satisfies \(\sum_{(\theta,B)\in S}y_{\theta,B,c}=q_c+\sigma_c\) for every \(c\) and \(\sum_c d_\theta(c)y_{\theta,B,c}\le x_{\theta,B}d_\theta(B)\) for every occupied cell, with at least one strict inequality. Fractional comparisons are unrestricted by \(\mathcal B\) and need not satisfy EF1.

Is deciding whether such an EF1 and fractionally Pareto-optimal allocation exists with \(\sum_c\sigma_c\le1\) in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|\mathcal B|,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with unrestricted bundles, an EF1 and fractionally Pareto-optimal allocation with at most \(n-1\) duplicate chores can be computed in polynomial time ([Akrami et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.04788)).
Without surplus, EF1 and fractional Pareto optimality are now known to coexist for additive chores; EF1 and Pareto-optimal allocations are computable in polynomial time for a fixed number of agents ([Mahara](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09544)).
No result settling exactly the support-wise continuous decision problem above was found in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the rational distribution to total mass one; occurrence and duplicate masses use that same population scale.
  • Took disutilities to be nonnegative binary rationals, allowing zero costs and ties, as in the additive chore domain.
  • Restricted the chore-kind list to kinds with positive base occurrence mass, so surplus duplicates only original kinds.
  • Made the admissible family an explicit nonempty input list with binary integer coordinates; an empty bundle is allowed but need not be listed.
  • Defined support-wise EF1 pairwise across all occupied type–bundle cells, including different bundles assigned to the same type; removal means one whole occurrence from the envious bundle.
  • Defined fractional Pareto comparisons for occupied type–bundle cells, with a strict improvement on positive mass, rather than comparing only type-average disutilities.
  • Allowed arbitrary fractional chore reassignment in Pareto comparisons, retaining the same augmented supply but imposing neither bundle admissibility nor fairness on the comparison.
  • Selected the stated decision alternative with fixed surplus threshold one; did not replace it with a support-size analogue of the discrete surplus bound.

Proven result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The reduction below works even when all type masses are dyadic rationals and the empty bundle belongs to the admissible family.

Consequently, an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,|\mathcal B|,L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

Source problem

We reduce from Exact Cover by Three-Sets: given a universe \(U\) with \(|U|=3K\) and a family \(T_1,\ldots,T_N\) of three-element subsets, decide whether some subfamily covers every element exactly once.

Its \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness follows immediately from three-dimensional matching, which is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete by [Karp’s theorem](https://studylib.net/doc/27985824/karp1972): take the universe to be the disjoint union of the three coordinate sets and replace each allowed triple by its corresponding three-element subset. An exact cover is precisely a perfect three-dimensional matching.

We may assume \(N\ge1\) and that \(K\ge2\) is a power of two. Indeed, trivial source instances can be handled separately. To obtain the restriction on \(K\), enlarge the universe with disjoint triples, each accompanied by its unique covering set, until the required cover size is the smallest power of two at least \(\max\{K,2\}\). Every added set is forced into an exact cover, so this polynomial-size padding preserves the answer.

Construction

Choose \(D\) to be the smallest power of two satisfying \(D\ge N+2\), and put

\(\displaystyle \alpha=\frac1{DK},\qquad \beta=1-N\alpha,\qquad H=D+1.\)

In particular, \(D>1\), \(\alpha>0\), and \(\beta>0\).

There are \(N+1\) types, indexed by \(\Theta=\{0,1,\ldots,N\}\), with masses

\(\displaystyle \mu_0=\beta,\qquad \mu_i=\alpha\quad(1\le i\le N).\)

These masses sum to one. Because \(D\) and \(K\) are powers of two, every mass is dyadic.

The chore kinds are:

  • an anchor \(a_i\) for each \(i\in\Theta\);
  • one common marker chore \(p\);
  • an element chore \(e_u\) for each \(u\in U\).

Their positive base occurrence masses are

\(\displaystyle q_{a_i}=\mu_i,\qquad q_p=1,\qquad q_{e_u}=\alpha.\)

For every type \(j\), define additive disutilities by

\(\displaystyle d_j(p)=1,\qquad d_j(e_u)=0,\qquad d_j(a_i)=H\,\mathbf 1_{\{i\ne j\}}.\)

Thus each anchor has zero disutility for its designated type and positive disutility for every other type.

Write \(\mathbf v_c\) for the unit bundle containing one copy of chore kind \(c\). The admissible family contains the empty bundle, the bundle

\(\displaystyle B_0=\mathbf v_{a_0}+\mathbf v_p,\)

and, for every \(1\le i\le N\), the two bundles

\(\displaystyle B_i^-=\mathbf v_{a_i}+\mathbf v_p,\)

\(\displaystyle B_i^+=\mathbf v_{a_i}+(D+1)\mathbf v_p+\sum_{u\in T_i}\mathbf v_{e_u}.\)

All coordinates not specified here are zero. In particular, every nonempty bundle contains exactly one anchor.

Fractional Pareto optimality forces bundle ownership

Lemma. In any fractionally Pareto-optimal allocation, a nonempty bundle containing \(a_i\) can have positive mass only at type \(i\).

Proof. Suppose \(x_{j,B}>0\), where \(B\) contains \(a_i\) and \(j\ne i\). Because \(\mu_i>0\), some occupied cell \((i,A)\) exists, possibly with \(A\) empty.

Start with the fractional array representing the allocation itself:

\(\displaystyle y^0_{\theta,B,c}=x_{\theta,B}B_c.\)

It satisfies all commodity conservation equations, and its disutility at each occupied cell equals that cell’s original disutility.

Move the entire amount \(x_{j,B}\) of anchor \(a_i\) from cell \((j,B)\) to cell \((i,A)\), changing no other entries. This is possible because \(B_{a_i}=1\). The resulting array is nonnegative and preserves every chore total.

The disutility of cell \((j,B)\) decreases strictly by \(Hx_{j,B}>0\). The receiving cell’s disutility is unchanged, since \(d_i(a_i)=0\). Every other cell is unchanged. This is exactly a fractional Pareto improvement forbidden by the definition. \(\square\)

Now let \(w_i\) be the total mass receiving a nonempty bundle containing \(a_i\). The lemma implies that all this mass belongs to type \(i\), so \(w_i\le\mu_i\). Since each such bundle contains exactly one copy of \(a_i\), anchor conservation gives

\(\displaystyle w_i=q_{a_i}+\sigma_{a_i}=\mu_i+\sigma_{a_i}\ge\mu_i.\)

Therefore \(w_i=\mu_i\) and \(\sigma_{a_i}=0\).

It follows that no type receives the empty bundle with positive mass. Type \(0\) receives \(B_0\) with its entire mass, and each type \(i\ge1\) distributes its entire mass between \(B_i^-\) and \(B_i^+\).

Support-wise EF1 forces discrete choices

For type \(i\ge1\),

\(\displaystyle d_i(B_i^-)=1,\qquad d_i(B_i^+)=D+1.\)

Every chore in \(B_i^+\) has disutility either zero or one to type \(i\). Removing one marker copy leaves disutility \(D\); removing an anchor or element chore leaves disutility \(D+1\).

Since \(D>1=d_i(B_i^-)\), the ordered pair

\(\displaystyle (i,B_i^+),\ (i,B_i^-)\)

violates EF1 whenever both cells are occupied. Thus they cannot both have positive mass.

Combining this with the preceding section, every type \(i\ge1\) makes exactly one of the choices

\(\displaystyle x_{i,B_i^-}=\alpha\quad\text{or}\quad x_{i,B_i^+}=\alpha.\)

All other cells of that type have zero mass. This uses the requirement that support-wise EF1 also compares different occupied bundles of the same type.

Let

\(\displaystyle I=\{i:x_{i,B_i^+}=\alpha\},\qquad k=|I|.\)

The continuous allocation has therefore determined a discrete subfamily of the source instance.

A feasible allocation yields an exact cover

For each \(u\in U\), define its coverage count

\(\displaystyle r_u=|\{i\in I:u\in T_i\}|.\)

Only selected bundles contain element chores. Hence conservation for \(e_u\) gives

\(\displaystyle \alpha r_u=q_{e_u}+\sigma_{e_u}=\alpha+\sigma_{e_u}.\)

Because \(\sigma_{e_u}\ge0\) and \(\alpha>0\), we have \(r_u\ge1\). The selected sets cover \(U\).

Their total number of element incidences is \(3k\). Covering all \(3K\) elements therefore requires \(k\ge K\).

Next, every occupied bundle contains one baseline marker copy, and every selected bundle contains \(D\) additional marker copies. The total marker occurrence mass is consequently

\(\displaystyle \beta+(N-k)\alpha+(D+1)k\alpha=1+D\alpha k.\)

Since \(q_p=1\), this forces

\(\displaystyle \sigma_p=D\alpha k=\frac{k}{K}.\)

All duplicate masses are nonnegative. The prescribed budget therefore implies

\(\displaystyle \frac{k}{K}=\sigma_p\le\sum_{c\in C}\sigma_c\le1,\)

so \(k\le K\). Together with \(k\ge K\), this yields \(k=K\).

Finally,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{u\in U}r_u=3k=3K=|U|.\)

Every \(r_u\ge1\), so every \(r_u=1\). Thus \(\{T_i:i\in I\}\) is an exact cover.

An exact cover yields a feasible allocation

Conversely, suppose \(I\) indexes an exact cover. Allocate the entire mass of type \(i\ge1\) to \(B_i^+\) when \(i\in I\), and to \(B_i^-\) otherwise. Allocate type \(0\) entirely to \(B_0\).

We verify each required condition.

Conservation and duplicate budget. Every anchor occurs with mass \(\mu_i=q_{a_i}\). Every element occurs in exactly one selected set, hence with mass \(\alpha=q_{e_u}\). Since \(|I|=K\), the marker occurrence mass is

\(\displaystyle 1+D\alpha K=2.\)

Thus the conservation equations hold with \(\sigma_p=1\) and all other duplicate masses zero. The total duplicate mass is exactly one.

Support-wise EF1. Each type occupies only one bundle, so comparisons within that type have equal disutilities.

For distinct types \(i\ne j\), the occupied bundle of type \(j\) contains \(a_j\) and at least one marker copy. Its disutility to type \(i\) is therefore at least

\(\displaystyle H+1=D+2.\)

Type \(i\)’s own occupied bundle has disutility at most \(D+1\). Consequently, every comparison between distinct types satisfies the first EF1 alternative, namely absence of envy.

Fractional Pareto optimality. In the constructed allocation, every anchor is held by its zero-disutility type, and all element chores have zero disutility. Only marker chores contribute disutility. The sum of the original disutilities over occupied cells is therefore exactly two.

For any unrestricted fractional comparison array \(y\), every marker unit contributes disutility one, independently of its recipient. Marker conservation and nonnegativity of all other disutilities imply

\(\displaystyle \sum_{(\theta,B)\in S}\sum_{c\in C}d_\theta(c)y_{\theta,B,c}\ge\sum_{(\theta,B)\in S}y_{\theta,B,p}=2.\)

If every occupied cell weakly improved and at least one improved strictly, summing those finitely many inequalities would instead give total disutility strictly below two. This contradiction proves fractional Pareto optimality under the exact comparison model in the question.

Polynomial size and conclusion

The constructed instance has

\(\displaystyle \tau=N+1,\qquad m=N+3K+2,\qquad |\mathcal B|=2N+2.\)

The chosen power of two satisfies \(D<2(N+2)\). Bundle coordinates belong to \(\{0,1,D+1\}\), and disutilities belong to \(\{0,1,D+1\}\). All rational numbers have binary descriptions of polynomial length. The padding and the complete explicit lists of bundles and disutilities are computable in polynomial time.

We have proved both directions: the source instance has an exact cover if and only if the constructed society admits an EF1 and fractionally Pareto-optimal allocation with total duplicate mass at most one. This establishes the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.
