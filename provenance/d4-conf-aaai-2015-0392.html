The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item categories, \(\tau\) complete valuation types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and normalized monotone \(\mathcal{MOPH}\)-\(k\) valuations \(v_t\) accessible by demand oracles, find a feasible allocation maximizing welfare. Variables \(x_{t,S}\geq 0\) give the fraction of the whole population of type \(t\) assigned the integral bundle \(S\subseteq[m]\); require \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq q_j\), and maximize \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M=[m]\), with \(m\geq1\) and \(1\leq k\leq m\). A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) complete valuation types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Supplies are binary rationals \(q_j\geq0\).
Each type has a normalized monotone valuation \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\), promised to be \(\mathcal{MPH}\)-\(k\): \(v_t(S)=\max_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_t}\sum_{\emptyset\neq E\subseteq S,\,|E|\leq k}w_{t,\ell,E}\), with nonnegative weights and an arbitrary index set. This representation need not be supplied.
An exact demand oracle, queried at nonnegative rational item prices \(p_j\), returns any bundle maximizing \(v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j\); exact value queries are also available. Let \(L\) include the numerical input length and a supplied bound on the binary length of every valuation value; count oracle calls and bit operations separately.
An allocation assigns masses \(x_{t,S}\geq0\) to integral bundles \(S\subseteq M\), including the empty bundle, with \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:\,j\in S}x_{t,S}\leq q_j\). Welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is its maximum over feasible allocations; allocations are represented by their nonzero entries.

Can a feasible allocation with expected welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/(k+1)\) be computed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\), with polynomially many exact oracle calls, or is achieving this approximation NP-hard even when the oracles have polynomial-time implementations?

For finitely many agents and single-copy items, a polynomial-time randomized demand-oracle algorithm achieves expected welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/(k+1)\). The associated configuration LP is known to be solvable in polynomial time with demand queries ([Feige et al.](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~mfeldman/papers/FFIILSc15.pdf)); this verified statement concerns unit agent and item capacities.

  • The anchor is an approximation result, so the target retains its (k+1) expected-welfare guarantee instead of the graded question's exact optimization target.
  • Restricted the hierarchy parameter to 1 through m and required at least one item category.
  • Required nonnegative rational supplies; unused supply is allowed.
  • Required rational valuation values with a supplied bit-length bound to make the oracle complexity model precise.
  • Specified exact demand queries at nonnegative rational prices with arbitrary maximizing tie-breaking, and allowed exact value queries as supported by the source's oracle model.
  • Specified allocations by their nonzero entries rather than an exponentially long array.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, even exactly. A deterministic algorithm computes a feasible allocation of welfare \(\mathrm{OPT}\), using polynomially many exact demand and value queries and polynomially many additional bit operations in \(m,\tau,L\). The allocation can have at most \(m+\tau\) nonzero entries.

The proof applies to every normalized monotone valuation satisfying the stated oracle and encoding assumptions; the \(\mathcal{MPH}\)-\(k\) promise is unnecessary.

1. The allocation problem and its dual

Consider the finite, exponentially sized linear program

\(\displaystyle \max_{x\geq0}\ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\subseteq M}v_t(S)x_{t,S}, \)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t \quad(t\in[\tau]), \)

and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq q_j \quad(j\in M). \)

Call this program \(\mathrm{P}\). Its feasible solutions are exactly the allocations in the question, so its optimum is \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

It is feasible: assign mass \(\mu_t\) to the empty bundle for each type. Its feasible region is compact, since every coordinate satisfies \(0\leq x_{t,S}\leq\mu_t\). Consequently, its optimum is finite and attained.

Associate an unrestricted dual variable \(\alpha_t\) with each type equality and a nonnegative variable \(p_j\) with each supply constraint. The dual is

\(\displaystyle \min_{\alpha,p}\ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\alpha_t+\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_jp_j, \)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t+\sum_{j\in S}p_j\geq v_t(S) \quad(t\in[\tau],\ S\subseteq M), \qquad p_j\geq0. \)

Call this program \(\mathrm{D}\). The empty-bundle inequality implies \(\alpha_t\geq0\), because normalization gives \(v_t(\emptyset)=0\).

The dual is feasible: take \(p=0\) and \(\alpha_t=v_t(M)\). Monotonicity verifies every bundle inequality. Finite-dimensional LP strong duality therefore gives an attained dual optimum equal to \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

The crucial dimension is \(d=m+\tau\): the dual has only \(d\) variables, regardless of the number of bundles.

2. Strong separation using the supplied oracles

We construct a strong separation oracle for the feasible region of \(\mathrm{D}\). Its input is a rational point \((\alpha,p)\).

  • If some \(p_j<0\), return the violated defining inequality \(p_j\geq0\).
  • Otherwise, if some \(\alpha_t<0\), return the violated empty-bundle inequality \(\alpha_t\geq v_t(\emptyset)=0\).
  • Otherwise, all prices are nonnegative. For each supported type \(t\), query its demand oracle at \(p\), obtaining a bundle \(S_t\). Query \(v_t(S_t)\). If

\(\displaystyle v_t(S_t)-\sum_{j\in S_t}p_j>\alpha_t, \)

return the violated defining inequality

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t+\sum_{j\in S_t}p_j\geq v_t(S_t). \)

  • If none of these inequalities is violated, certify feasibility.

To justify the final certification, the demand guarantee gives, for every \(S\subseteq M\),

\(\displaystyle v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j \leq v_t(S_t)-\sum_{j\in S_t}p_j \leq\alpha_t. \)

Thus every bundle inequality holds. Arbitrary tie-breaking is harmless because every returned demand bundle attains the same maximum utility.

Each separation call uses at most \(\tau\) demand queries and \(\tau\) value queries. Its remaining arithmetic is polynomial in the encoding length of its input and in \(m,\tau,L\).

3. Exact optimization and allocation recovery

We use the following standard rational-polyhedral oracle theorem:

Given a rational polyhedron in dimension \(d\), a bound on the encoding length of its defining inequalities, and a strong separation oracle returning such inequalities, exact linear optimization is oracle-polynomial. When the optimum is finite and attained, optimal dual multipliers supported on polynomially many returned inequalities can also be recovered.

The multiplier-recovery statement is the essential additional ingredient beyond computing an optimal objective value. It is the GLS oracle-duality result, Lemma 6.5.15, discussed explicitly in [*On Finding Exact Solutions of Linear Programs in the Oracle Model*, Section 1.1](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35149/35149.pdf).

All hypotheses hold here:

  • The feasible region of \(\mathrm{D}\) is a rational polyhedron in dimension \(d=m+\tau\).
  • Each bundle inequality has coefficients in \(\{0,1\}\) and one valuation value as its right-hand side. Its encoding length is polynomial in \(d,L\).
  • The separation oracle always returns a defining inequality with this encoding bound.
  • The objective coefficients are the supplied rational masses and supplies.
  • The optimum is finite and attained, as proved above.

The theorem therefore provides exact optimal multipliers on polynomially many defining inequalities. We now identify these multipliers with an allocation.

For this purpose, regard all coordinates \((\alpha,p)\) as unrestricted variables, and treat \(p_j\geq0\) as explicit inequalities. Let \(x_{t,S}\geq0\) denote the multiplier of the bundle inequality indexed by \((t,S)\), and let \(s_j\geq0\) denote the multiplier of \(p_j\geq0\).

The standard LP dual of this inequality formulation has equations

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t \quad(t\in[\tau]), \)

and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}+s_j=q_j \quad(j\in M). \)

Its objective is

\(\displaystyle \max\ \sum_{t,S}v_t(S)x_{t,S}, \)

because the inequalities \(p_j\geq0\) have zero right-hand side.

Thus the recovered optimal multipliers give exactly a feasible allocation, together with supply slacks. Its welfare equals \(\mathrm{OPT}\). Empty-bundle multipliers are included as ordinary allocation entries.

4. Polynomial output size and bit complexity

Recovery supplies only polynomially many bundle columns and supply-slack columns. Restrict the preceding standard-form LP to these columns. The recovered solution is feasible and has objective \(\mathrm{OPT}\). Every feasible solution of the restricted LP is feasible for the full LP, so its optimum is exactly \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

Solve this explicit rational LP and obtain an optimal basic feasible solution. It has \(d=m+\tau\) equality rows, hence at most \(d\) positive variables, including supply slacks. Its allocation therefore has at most \(m+\tau\) nonzero entries.

Its coordinates also have polynomial encoding length. Indeed, the constraint matrix has coefficients in \(\{0,1\}\). A basic solution is determined by a nonsingular square submatrix of order at most \(d\). Hadamard’s inequality bounds its determinant by \(d^{d/2}\); clearing the denominators of the supplied right-hand sides and applying Cramer’s rule gives polynomial binary length in \(d,L\).

The oracle algorithm makes polynomially many separation calls at rational points of polynomial encoding length. Consequently:

  • Demand and value oracle calls are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
  • Additional bit operations are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
  • If the supplied oracles have polynomial-time implementations, the entire algorithm runs in polynomial time.

These bounds are also polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

5. Relation to the finite-agent approximation

For finitely many agents and single-copy items, the configuration LP allows fractional assignments that must subsequently be rounded to an integral allocation; this is the setting of the cited [Feige et al. paper](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~mfeldman/papers/FFIILSc15.pdf).

Here, the masses \(x_{t,S}\) themselves constitute the requested allocation. The argument above verifies separation and allocation recovery directly for arbitrary rational masses and supplies, without extending a unit-capacity rounding theorem.

The deterministic output \(x^\star\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}[W(x^\star)] =W(x^\star) =\mathrm{OPT} \geq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{k+1}. \)
