The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets on both sides, rational type masses, compatible pairs, strict rankings over counterpart types with unmatched last, and a critical label for each type, model each type as an atomless population. A matching pairs equal-mass subsets one-to-one along compatible pairs; it is critical if it maximizes the mass of matched critical individuals. For two matchings, define the vote margin by comparing each same individual’s assigned counterpart type, counting the mass who prefer the assignment in \(N\) minus the mass who prefer it in \(M\). Does a popular critical matching \(M\), satisfying \(\Delta(N,M)\leq 0\) for every critical \(N\), always exist, and can one with maximum matched mass be computed in time polynomial in the number of types, compatible pairs, and input bit length?

1. Pair masses and unrestricted reassignments

For each compatible pair, define
$$ c_{ab}=\mathbf 1_{a\in C}+\mathbf 1_{b\in C}. $$
The feasible pair-mass polytope is
$$ P=\left\{ x\in\mathbb R_{\geq 0}^{E}: \sum_{e\ni t}x_e\leq s_t \text{ for every }t\in A\cup B \right\}. $$
Every measurable matching produces a vector in $P$. Conversely, every vector in $P$ can be realized by partitioning the type intervals into pieces of the specified edge masses and pairing corresponding pieces by translation. This also works for irrational coordinates.

Define
$$ \kappa=\max_{x\in P}\sum_{e\in E}c_ex_e, \qquad P_C=\left\{ x\in P:\sum_{e\in E}c_ex_e=\kappa \right\}. $$
The polytope $P$ is nonempty and compact: the zero vector is feasible, and every edge coordinate is bounded by both endpoint capacities. Thus $P_C$ is a nonempty compact face of $P$. Its vectors are exactly the pair-mass vectors of critical matchings.

For a type $t$, let $D_t$ be its compatible counterpart types together with the unmatched symbol $\bot$. Define its status masses by
$$ d_{a,b}(x)=d_{b,a}(x)=x_{ab}, \qquad d_{t,\bot}(x)=s_t-\sum_{e\ni t}x_e. $$
These are nonnegative affine functions on $P$, and
$$ \sum_{r\in D_t}d_{t,r}(x)=s_t. $$

We will use the following realization fact.

Reassignment lemma. Fix a matching $M$ with pair masses $x$. Suppose $y\in P$ and, for every type, nonnegative transition masses $z^t_{r,h}$ satisfy
$$ \sum_{h\in D_t}z^t_{r,h}=d_{t,r}(x), \qquad \sum_{r\in D_t}z^t_{r,h}=d_{t,h}(y). $$
Then these transitions can be realized by a measurable matching with pair masses $y$, up to changes on null sets.

Proof. Partition each old status stratum into subsets of masses $z^t_{r,h}$. Atomlessness permits these finite partitions. For every edge $(a,b)$, the resulting subsets assigned to that edge at its two endpoints both have measure $y_{ab}$, so they can be paired.

Here we use the standard measure-space isomorphism theorem in the following form: two Lebesgue measurable subsets of bounded intervals with equal positive measure admit a measure-preserving measurable bijection after deleting null subsets. Its hypotheses hold because, after taking Borel representatives modulo null sets and normalizing the measures, these are atomless standard probability spaces. Leave the exceptional null subsets unmatched; this changes neither pair masses nor voting integrals.

There is no requirement that an old pair remain together, or that the transition arrays at opposite endpoints agree. Only their new edge totals must agree. $\square$

Write
$$ \sigma_t(h,r)= \mathbf 1_{h\succ_t r}-\mathbf 1_{r\succ_t h}. $$
The voting margin of the realized transitions is
$$ \sum_t\sum_{r,h\in D_t}\sigma_t(h,r)z^t_{r,h}. $$
Thus the lemma applies to precisely the unrestricted individual comparisons in the question.

2. A local obstruction and nonexistence

Proposition. A matching is undefeated by all reassignments preserving its pair masses if and only if every type has at most two positive status masses.

Proof. Suppose type $t$ has positive masses at three statuses
$$ r_1\succ_t r_2\succ_t r_3. $$
Choose
$$ 0<\varepsilon\leq \min\{d_{t,r_1}(x),d_{t,r_2}(x),d_{t,r_3}(x)\}. $$
Reassign mass $\varepsilon$ along each transition
$$ r_1\longmapsto r_3, \qquad r_2\longmapsto r_1, \qquad r_3\longmapsto r_2. $$
Every status retains its total mass. Preserve the status assignments at all other types and apply the reassignment lemma.

Two groups improve and one worsens, giving margin $\varepsilon>0$. Since the pair masses are unchanged, criticality is preserved.

Conversely, when a type has two positive statuses, conservation of their masses forces equal amounts to move in the two opposing directions. Their votes cancel. A type with one positive status contributes zero. Summing over types proves the converse. $\square$

A three-type counterexample

Take
$$ A=\{a\},\qquad B=\{b_1,b_2\},\qquad E=A\times B, $$
with
$$ s_a=3,\qquad s_{b_1}=s_{b_2}=1. $$
Type $a$ ranks
$$ b_1\succ_a b_2\succ_a\bot, $$
and each $b_i$ has only the compatible type $a$.

First let $C=A\cup B$. Then $K(M)=2S(M)$, and the maximum size is $2$. Every critical matching therefore has
$$ x_{ab_1}=x_{ab_2}=1, \qquad d_{a,\bot}(x)=1. $$
Type $a$ has three positive statuses, so the proposition defeats every critical matching.

The same instance has no popular matching when $C=\varnothing$. Indeed, every matching is then critical. If $S(M)<2$, there is unmatched positive mass at $a$ and at some $b_i$; pairing portions of these unmatched sets improves both groups and defeats $M$. If $S(M)=2$, the three-status obstruction applies.

This is the smallest possible support size for nonexistence. With at most two types there is at most one compatible edge. If that edge exists, match mass
$$ \min\{s_a,s_b\}. $$
For any competitor $N$, the binary matched-versus-unmatched comparison gives
$$ \Delta(N,M)=2\bigl(S(N)-S(M)\bigr)\leq 0. $$
This matching is critical for every choice of $C$. With no compatible edge, the empty matching is popular critical.

Imbalance is not essential: with $s_a=3$ and three compatible counterpart types of mass $1$ each, taking every type critical forces three positive partner statuses at $a$, again precluding popularity.

These examples do not meet the strict preferences over individual partners assumed by the finite theorem: partners of the same type are tied here. [Kavitha, Theorem 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-025-01347-3)

3. An exact linear characterization of popularity

The following theorem is the main additional result.

Status potentials

For each type, choose a nonempty set $R_t\subseteq D_t$ of size at most two.

If $R_t=\{r\}$, define
$$ f_{t,R_t}(h)=\sigma_t(h,r). $$

If $R_t=\{p,n\}$ with $p\succ_t n$, define
$$ f_{t,R_t}(h)= \max\left\{ 1+\sigma_t(h,p),\, \sigma_t(h,n) \right\}. $$
In particular,
$$ f_{t,R_t}(p)=1,\qquad f_{t,R_t}(n)=0. $$
Its values are explicitly
$$ f_{t,R_t}(h)= \begin{cases} 2,&h\succ_t p,\\ 1,&p\succeq_t h\succ_t n,\\ 0,&n\succeq_t h. \end{cases} $$
Here $\succeq_t$ means equality or strict preference.

For either construction,
$$ \sigma_t(h,r)\leq f_{t,R_t}(h)-f_{t,R_t}(r) \quad \text{for every }r\in R_t,\ h\in D_t. $$
For a singleton this is equality. For two statuses, the inequalities follow directly from the two terms in the defining maximum.

Define the affine objective
$$ W_R(y)= \sum_t\sum_{h\in D_t}f_{t,R_t}(h)d_{t,h}(y). $$
In edge coordinates,
$$ W_R(y)= \sum_t f_{t,R_t}(\bot)s_t + \sum_{(a,b)\in E} \left( f_{a,R_a}(b)-f_{a,R_a}(\bot) + f_{b,R_b}(a)-f_{b,R_b}(\bot) \right)y_{ab}. $$
Hence maximizing $W_R$ over $P_C$ is an LP with $m$ variables.

Characterization theorem

Theorem. Let $x\in P_C$, and define its actual positive status supports by
$$ R_t(x)=\{r\in D_t:d_{t,r}(x)>0\}. $$
A matching with pair masses $x$ is popular critical if and only if:

  • Every $R_t(x)$ has size at most two.
  • With $R=(R_t(x))_t$, the vector $x$ maximizes $W_R$ over $P_C$.

Proof: sufficiency. Consider any critical competitor with pair masses $y$ and transition arrays $z$. Every positive old row belongs to $R_t(x)$. The potential inequality therefore gives
$$ \begin{aligned} \Delta(N,M) &\leq \sum_t\sum_{r,h} \left(f_{t,R_t}(h)-f_{t,R_t}(r)\right)z^t_{r,h}\\ &= W_R(y)-W_R(x). \end{aligned} $$
The equality follows from the row and column sums. If $x$ maximizes $W_R$ over $P_C$, the last expression is nonpositive.

Proof: necessity. The local obstruction already proves the first condition. Suppose instead that some $y\in P_C$ satisfies
$$ W_R(y)>W_R(x). $$
For $\lambda\in(0,1]$, put
$$ y^\lambda=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y. $$
Convexity gives $y^\lambda\in P_C$.

We show that for sufficiently small positive $\lambda$, transitions from $x$ to $y^\lambda$ attain the potential bound exactly.

At a type with one positive old status $r$, assign transition masses
$$ z^t_{r,h}=d_{t,h}(y^\lambda). $$
Every transition attains equality in the potential inequality.

Now fix a type with two positive old statuses $p\succ_t n$, and write
$$ \alpha=d_{t,p}(x)>0,\qquad \beta=d_{t,n}(x)>0. $$
Choose
$$ 0<\theta<\min\{\alpha,\beta\}. $$
The four transitions between $p$ and $n$ attain equality in the potential inequality. At $\lambda=0$, they can have the strictly positive masses
$$ \begin{array}{c|cc} &\text{new }p&\text{new }n\\ \hline \text{old }p&\alpha-\theta&\theta\\ \text{old }n&\theta&\beta-\theta \end{array}. $$

For each $h\notin\{p,n\}$, choose an old status $r(h)\in\{p,n\}$ attaining the maximum defining $f_{t,R_t}(h)$. Then
$$ \sigma_t(h,r(h)) = f_{t,R_t}(h)-f_{t,R_t}(r(h)). $$
Send mass $\lambda d_{t,h}(y)$ from $r(h)$ to $h$. Let $U_p,U_n$ be the total amounts sent outside $\{p,n\}$ from the two old rows. Set
$$ \delta_p=\lambda\bigl(d_{t,p}(y)-\alpha\bigr), \qquad \delta_n=\lambda\bigl(d_{t,n}(y)-\beta\bigr). $$
Conservation of total type mass implies
$$ \delta_p+\delta_n+U_p+U_n=0. $$

Choose the remaining four transition masses as
$$ \begin{aligned} z_{p,p}&=\alpha-\theta-U_p,\\ z_{p,n}&=\theta,\\ z_{n,p}&=\theta+\delta_p+U_p,\\ z_{n,n}&=\beta-\theta-U_n-\delta_p-U_p. \end{aligned} $$
Together with the outside transitions, their old row totals are $\alpha,\beta$. Their new column totals at $p,n$ are
$$ \alpha+\delta_p,\qquad \beta+\delta_n, $$
as required. All four entries are strictly positive at $\lambda=0$ and depend continuously on $\lambda$, so they remain nonnegative for sufficiently small positive $\lambda$.

Every positive transition attains equality in the potential inequality. There are finitely many types, so a common sufficiently small positive $\lambda$ works for all of them.

By the reassignment lemma, these arrays produce a critical competitor with margin
$$ \Delta(N,M) = W_R(y^\lambda)-W_R(x) = \lambda\bigl(W_R(y)-W_R(x)\bigr)>0. $$
This contradicts popularity. Therefore $x$ must maximize $W_R$. $\square$

The argument applies to arbitrary real pair masses and arbitrary measurable realizations.

4. Popular vectors form a finite union of faces

For any collection $R=(R_t)_t$ of nonempty status sets of size at most two, compute
$$ H_R=\max_{y\in P_C}W_R(y). $$
Define
$$ Q_R= \left\{ x\in P_C: d_{t,h}(x)=0\text{ whenever }h\notin R_t,\quad W_R(x)=H_R \right\}. $$

Corollary. The set of popular critical pair-mass vectors is exactly
$$ \bigcup_R Q_R. $$
Every nonempty $Q_R$ is a face of $P_C$, and therefore a face of $P$.

Proof. If $x\in Q_R$, all positive old statuses belong to $R_t$. The potential inequality from the sufficiency proof gives
$$ \Delta(N,M)\leq W_R(y)-W_R(x)\leq 0 $$
for every critical competitor. This remains valid if only one of two chosen statuses has positive mass.

Conversely, a popular vector has actual supports of size at most two and, by the theorem, belongs to the corresponding $Q_R$.

Each equation $d_{t,h}(x)=0$ specifies a face when feasible, since $d_{t,h}$ is nonnegative on $P_C$. The equation $W_R(x)=H_R$ specifies a maximizing face. A nonempty intersection of these faces is a face. Finally, a face of a face of a polytope is a face of that polytope. $\square$

This characterization reduces the problem to a finite choice of status sets followed by linear programming.

5. Rational optimal solutions and membership in $\mathrm{NP}$

Polynomial-time verification

Given rational pair masses $x$, perform the following checks:

  • Check $x\in P$ and compute $\kappa$ by linear programming.
  • Check $x\in P_C$.
  • Compute all status masses. Reject if some type has three or more positive statuses.
  • Construct $W_R$ from the actual positive supports and check

$$ W_R(x)=\max_{y\in P_C}W_R(y). $$

The characterization theorem proves correctness. All calculations and LPs have polynomial encoding length in the instance and candidate encoding lengths. Thus verification takes polynomial bit time.

Rationality of vertices

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the input masses. It has bit length $O(L)$, and every $Ds_t$ is an integer.

Lemma. Every vertex of $P$ lies in
$$ D^{-1}\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}^{E} $$
and has polynomial total encoding length.

Proof. Scale all coordinates by $D$, so the capacity right-hand sides are integers.

The capacity matrix is the bipartite vertex-edge incidence matrix. Negate its rows indexed by $B$. Each edge column then has one positive and one negative entry.

To verify total unimodularity, consider a square submatrix. A column with at most one nonzero entry permits determinant expansion and induction. Otherwise, every column has exactly two opposite entries. The sum of all rows, as a vector, is then zero, so the determinant vanishes. Consequently all square minors have determinant in $\{0,1,-1\}$.

Adding the unit rows for nonnegativity preserves total unimodularity by determinant expansion along those rows. At any vertex, a nonsingular square subsystem of tight constraints therefore has determinant of absolute value $1$. Its integer right-hand side implies integer scaled coordinates.

Finally,
$$ 0\leq Dx_{ab}\leq D\min\{s_a,s_b\}. $$
These bounds and the denominator $D$ have bit length $O(L)$, giving polynomial total encoding length for all coordinates. $\square$

Maximum-size attainment

Suppose a popular critical vector $x$ exists. By Section 4 it belongs to some nonempty face $Q_R$. Maximizing size over that face attains an optimum at a vertex $v$ of $Q_R$, with
$$ S(v)\geq S(x). $$
Every point of $Q_R$ is popular, and every vertex of $Q_R$ is a vertex of $P$. Thus a popular vertex dominates $x$ in size.

There are finitely many vertices of $P$. Selecting the largest popular vertex therefore gives a maximum-size popular critical matching whenever one exists. The lemma gives it polynomial-length rational coordinates.

Corollary. The threshold decision problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

Proof. If a popular critical matching of size at least $q$ exists, the preceding argument supplies a popular vertex of size at least $q$ with a polynomial-length rational encoding. Use its pair masses as the certificate. Check the size threshold and apply the polynomial-time verifier above.

Conversely, an accepted certificate has the prescribed canonical interval realization and satisfies criticality, popularity, and the threshold. $\square$

6. An improved exact algorithm

The finite union of faces gives the following algorithm.

  • Compute $\kappa$ and hence $P_C$.
  • Enumerate every collection of nonempty sets

$$ R_t\subseteq D_t,\qquad |R_t|\leq 2. $$

  • For each collection, compute $H_R$ by maximizing $W_R$ over all of $P_C$.
  • Test feasibility of $Q_R$. If nonempty, maximize $S$ over $Q_R$.
  • Return the largest solution found. If every $Q_R$ is empty, report nonexistence.

Section 4 proves that every returned solution is popular and that every popular vector is covered. Thus the algorithm correctly optimizes size and detects nonexistence. Rational LP output can be realized by the interval partitions and translations required in the question.

To count the collections, let
$$ n_t=|D_t|=\deg(t)+1. $$
The number of choices at type $t$ is
$$ n_t+\binom{n_t}{2} = \frac{n_t(n_t+1)}2 \leq n_t^2. $$
Also,
$$ \sum_t n_t=2m+\tau. $$
For $\tau>0$, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality yields
$$ \prod_t n_t^2 \leq \left(\frac{2m+\tau}{\tau}\right)^{2\tau}. $$
Each collection requires only polynomial-time LP computations. Hence the total running time is
$$ \left(1+\frac{2m}{\tau}\right)^{2\tau} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
Since $m\leq\tau^2/4$, this is also
$$ 2^{O(\tau\log(\tau+1))} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
For empty support, the empty matching is the sole matching and is handled directly.
