The continuous mirror question, as graded

Let a latent event draw a mass vector \(q\in\Delta(\Theta)\) from an unknown prior \(\nu\), and let an atomless unit-mass society have signal types \(\theta\in\Theta\) with masses \(q_\theta\). Is there a mechanism independent of \(\nu\) that uses a measure-preserving peer matching, signal reports, and peer forecasts of \(q\), scored against the aggregate report masses, to make truthful reporting a strict Bayesian equilibrium for almost every agent whenever distinct signals satisfy the corresponding second-order relevance condition? In that condition, for every \(\theta\ne\theta'\), there is a peer signal \(z\) with \(\Pr[z\mid\theta]>0\) such that, if \(\Pr[\theta'\mid z]>0\), then \(\mathcal L(q\mid\theta,z)\ne\mathcal L(q\mid\theta',z)\); otherwise \(\mathcal L(q\mid\theta,z)\ne\mathcal L(q\mid z)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(\Theta\) be a finite signal set of size \(m\geq2\). An atomless society has total mass \(1\); a latent event draws \(q\in\Delta(\Theta)\) from a common prior \(\nu\), known to agents but unknown to the designer, with \(\mathbb E_\nu[q_\theta]>0\) for every \(\theta\). Agents are risk-neutral, care only about payments, and initially observe only their own signal; identities convey no information.
Each realized society is represented by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Thus \(\nu\) is supported on rational mass vectors; \(\tau\) denotes realized support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be this encoding's bit length; any running-time claims must use \(m,\tau,L\) and other explicit parameters. The encoding is not disclosed before reporting.
Peer matching is a randomized, type-blind, measure-preserving bijection \(T\), with \(a,T(a),T^{-1}(a)\) distinct almost everywhere and assignments hidden before initial reports. Conditional on \(q\), an agent's signal \(\theta\) and a matched peer's signal \(z\) have joint law \(\Pr[\theta,z\mid q]=q_\theta q_z\); all conditional probabilities below use this law and \(\nu\).
Agents first simultaneously report \(x_a\in\Theta\), then privately receive \(x_{T^{-1}(a)}\) and report a probability law \(p_a\) on \(\Delta(\Theta)\). Aggregate report masses \(\widehat q\) are revealed only after forecasts; an individual deviation does not change them. Admissible payments use only own and matched agents' messages and strictly proper scores \(R(p,\widehat q)\), with no verified signals or access to \(\nu\). Payments may be signed; conditional expected-payoff comparisons must be well defined.
Truthful play reports \(x_a=\theta_a\) and, upon receiving \(z\), forecasts \(\mathcal L(q\mid\theta_a,z)\). At an impossible incoming report, where \(\Pr[z\mid\theta_a]=0\), the prescribed belief and forecast are \(\mathcal L(q\mid\theta_a)\). Use strict perfect Bayesian equilibrium, with strictness at each information set for almost every agent.
Second-order relevance means that for every ordered pair \(\theta\ne\theta'\), some \(z\) satisfies \(\Pr[z\mid\theta]>0\), and: if \(\Pr[\theta'\mid z]>0\), then \(\mathcal L(q\mid\theta,z)\ne\mathcal L(q\mid\theta',z)\); otherwise \(\mathcal L(q\mid\theta,z)\ne\mathcal L(q\mid z)\).

Is there a single admissible mechanism, independent of \(\nu\), admitting a strict truthful perfect Bayesian equilibrium for almost every agent for every \(\nu\) satisfying second-order relevance, or is such universal elicitation impossible?

For finite populations with \(n\geq3\), Knowledge-Free Peer Prediction admits a strict truthful perfect Bayesian equilibrium under second-order stochastic relevance without designer knowledge of the common prior. [Zhang and Chen](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2014/aamas/p245.pdf)
Bayesian Truth Serum establishes truthful Nash equilibrium for a countably infinite population under common-prior and distinct-posterior assumptions, eliciting expected frequencies rather than peer-conditioned probability laws. [Prelec](https://nel.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/bts.pdf) No result settling the precise atomless protocol above was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The mandatory binary-rational society convention restricts realized mass vectors, and therefore the prior's support, to rational points of the simplex. The support encoding describes a realization and is not public information before reporting.
  • Set m to the number of possible signals, require at least two signals, and exclude signals of zero ex ante probability so that all signal-conditioned beliefs are defined.
  • Specify that the prior is common knowledge among risk-neutral agents, while identities reveal no additional information and agents initially know only their own signal.
  • Interpret measure-preserving peer matching as a randomized directed bijection with distinct incoming and outgoing peers, hidden assignments before initial reporting, and conditionally independent matched signals given the mass vector.
  • Interpret forecasts as full probability laws on the simplex, rather than expected mass vectors; aggregate report masses are unavailable until forecasts have been submitted.
  • Restore the sequential perfect Bayesian equilibrium concept and the prescribed belief that ignores an impossible incoming report, as used by the discrete anchor.
  • Allow signed payments based on local messages and strictly proper scores against aggregate report masses, without imposing an unstated budget constraint; require well-defined conditional expected-payoff comparisons.

Universal strict truthful elicitation is possible

Theorem. For every finite signal set \(\Theta\), there is a mechanism independent of \(\nu\) that admits the prescribed strict truthful perfect Bayesian equilibrium for every prior satisfying second-order relevance.

The payment is the sum of an agent’s own forecast score and the forecast score of the peer who receives that agent’s initial report. This follows the payment structure of [Zhang and Chen’s Knowledge-Free Peer Prediction mechanism, Section 4](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2014/aamas/p245.pdf). Below, we prove its validity for the stated atomless protocol, including bounded payments and the prescribed beliefs at impossible incoming reports.

1. A bounded strictly proper score for probability laws

Write \(X=\Delta(\Theta)\), with its Borel sigma-algebra, and identify its coordinates with \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\). Reports are Borel probability measures on \(X\).

Enumerate the countable family of sets

\( A_t=\{u\in X:u_i\leq t_i\text{ for every }i\}, \qquad t\in(\mathbb Q\cap[0,1])^m, \)

as \((A_k)_{k\geq1}\). This family contains \(X\), is closed under finite intersections, and generates the Borel sigma-algebra of \(X\). Indeed, setting all thresholds except one equal to \(1\) gives every rational coordinate sublevel set; those sets generate the relative topology’s Borel sigma-algebra.

For a reported law \(p\) and outcome \(u\in X\), define

\( R(p,u)= \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k} \left(2p(A_k)\mathbf 1_{A_k}(u)-p(A_k)^2\right). \)

Each summand’s parenthesized expression lies in \([-1,1]\). Consequently, the series converges absolutely and uniformly, and \(|R(p,u)|\leq1\).

For probability laws \(P,p\), let

\( S(P,p)=\int_X R(p,u)\,P(du). \)

Uniform absolute convergence permits integration term by term. Subtraction gives

\( S(P,P)-S(P,p) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\bigl(P(A_k)-p(A_k)\bigr)^2. \)

Denote this quantity by \(D(P,p)\). It is nonnegative. Moreover, if it equals zero, \(P\) and \(p\) agree on every \(A_k\). The uniqueness theorem for probability measures on a generating intersection-closed family then implies \(P=p\). Thus

\( D(P,p)>0\quad\text{whenever }P\neq p, \)

so \(R\) is strictly proper on the entire report space. Its boundedness ensures that every conditional expected-payoff comparison below is finite.

2. The mechanism

Use the matching \(T\) from the question and retain exactly the prescribed two reporting stages.

After forecasts and aggregate reports are available, pay agent \(a\)

\( U_a=R(p_a,\widehat q)+R(p_{T(a)},\widehat q). \)

This payment uses only the agent’s forecast, the matched recipient’s forecast, and strictly proper scores against the aggregate report masses. It requires no knowledge of \(\nu\).

Under truthful initial reports, \(\widehat q=q\). A unilateral deviation leaves this equality intact, because an individual has zero mass.

Fix an agent outside the null set of matching exceptions, and write

\( h=T^{-1}(a),\qquad b=T(a). \)

These three agents are distinct. The initial report of \(a\) is delivered to \(b\); the incoming report of \(a\) comes from \(h\).

3. Posterior notation and the prescribed assessment

For every signal \(\theta\), define

\( \lambda_\theta=\mathbb E_\nu[q_\theta]>0, \qquad M_{\theta z}=\mathbb E_\nu[q_\theta q_z]. \)

The stipulated signal law gives

\( \Pr[z\mid\theta]=\frac{M_{\theta z}}{\lambda_\theta}. \)

For a Borel set \(B\subseteq X\), define

\( P_\theta(B) = \frac{\int_B q_\theta\,\nu(dq)}{\lambda_\theta}. \)

When \(M_{\theta z}>0\), also define

\( P_{\theta z}(B) = \frac{\int_B q_\theta q_z\,\nu(dq)}{M_{\theta z}}. \)

These are respectively \(\mathcal L(q\mid\theta)\) and \(\mathcal L(q\mid\theta,z)\). In particular, \(P_{\theta z}=P_{z\theta}\).

Define the forecast function

\( F(\theta,z)= \begin{cases} P_{\theta z},&M_{\theta z}>0,\\ P_\theta,&M_{\theta z}=0. \end{cases} \)

The proposed strategy is:

  • Report the true initial signal.
  • After observing own signal \(\theta\) and incoming report \(z\), forecast \(F(\theta,z)\), regardless of one’s own earlier report.

At every incoming report with positive conditional probability, beliefs follow Bayes’ rule. At an impossible incoming report, retain the conditional law \(P_\theta\), as required by the question.

These off-path beliefs can regard the predecessor’s message as an uninformative deviation while retaining truthful reporting by everyone else. Such histories are compatible with the protocol, and a predecessor’s individual deviation leaves aggregate masses equal to \(q\). Bayes’ rule imposes no restriction at these zero-probability information sets.

After an agent’s own initial misreport, the same forecasting prescription applies: the agent remembers their true signal, and their misreport does not affect the predecessor’s already submitted report.

4. Strict optimality at every forecasting information set

Fix own signal \(\theta\), own earlier report \(x\), and incoming report \(z\), including an impossible incoming report.

The agent’s belief about the scoring outcome \(\widehat q=q\) is \(F(\theta,z)\). Changing \(p_a\) affects only the first term of

\( U_a=R(p_a,q)+R(p_b,q). \)

Indeed, forecasts are simultaneous, and \(b\)’s forecast depends on \(b\)’s own signal and the initial report \(x\), rather than on \(p_a\).

Therefore the conditional expected payoff difference between the prescribed forecast and any alternative \(p\) is exactly

\( S(F(\theta,z),F(\theta,z)) - S(F(\theta,z),p) = D(F(\theta,z),p). \)

This difference is strictly positive whenever \(p\neq F(\theta,z)\). The prescribed forecast is consequently the unique optimal action at every forecasting information set, including those following an initial misreport or an impossible incoming report.

5. Strict optimality of the initial signal report

Fix true signal \(\theta\), and suppose all other agents follow the proposed strategy.

The agent’s own forecast score is unaffected by their initial report

Changing the initial report affects neither \(q\) nor the predecessor’s initial report. Thus the agent’s information available for forecasting has the same distribution under every initial report.

By strict propriety, the maximum expected own forecast score is

\( V_\theta = \sum_{\substack{w\in\Theta\\M_{\theta w}>0}} \frac{M_{\theta w}}{\lambda_\theta} S(P_{\theta w},P_{\theta w}). \)

This value is attainable after any initial report by following the prescribed forecasting strategy. No alternative continuation can yield a larger expected own forecast score.

Truthful reporting maximizes the recipient’s score

Suppose the initial report is \(x\). If recipient \(b\)’s true signal is \(z\), its forecast is \(F(z,x)\). Under the deviating agent’s actual signal \(\theta\), the conditional law of \(q\) given the recipient’s signal \(z\) is \(P_{\theta z}\), whenever \(M_{\theta z}>0\).

Hence the expected recipient-score component is

\( I_\theta(x) = \sum_{\substack{z\in\Theta\\M_{\theta z}>0}} \frac{M_{\theta z}}{\lambda_\theta} S(P_{\theta z},F(z,x)). \)

For a truthful report, \(F(z,\theta)=P_{\theta z}\) on every term in this sum. Therefore

\( I_\theta(\theta)-I_\theta(x) = \sum_{\substack{z\in\Theta\\M_{\theta z}>0}} \frac{M_{\theta z}}{\lambda_\theta} D(P_{\theta z},F(z,x)). \)

Every term is nonnegative. If \(x\neq\theta\), second-order relevance provides a signal \(z\) with \(M_{\theta z}>0\) satisfying one of two cases:

  • If \(\Pr[x\mid z]>0\), then \(F(z,x)=P_{xz}\), and relevance gives \(P_{\theta z}\neq P_{xz}\).
  • If \(\Pr[x\mid z]=0\), then \(F(z,x)=P_z\), and relevance gives \(P_{\theta z}\neq P_z\).

In either case, the corresponding divergence is strictly positive and has positive weight. Consequently,

\( I_\theta(\theta)>I_\theta(x) \qquad\text{for every }x\neq\theta. \)

Truthful reporting with optimal forecasting yields \(V_\theta+I_\theta(\theta)\). Any deviation beginning with report \(x\neq\theta\), even with an arbitrary subsequent forecasting strategy, yields at most \(V_\theta+I_\theta(x)\), which is strictly smaller.

Thus the true signal is the unique optimal initial report. Randomization cannot improve this conclusion: any positive probability of a false initial report causes a strictly positive expected loss.

6. Conclusion and scope

The assessment satisfies Bayes’ rule wherever applicable, uses the specified beliefs at impossible incoming reports, and gives a unique optimal action at every information set. It is therefore a strict truthful perfect Bayesian equilibrium for almost every agent.

The mechanism and scoring rule are independent of \(\nu\). Payments lie in \([-2,2]\), so no integrability qualification is needed. The proof actually accommodates arbitrary Borel priors on \(\Delta(\Theta)\); rational realized mass vectors are a permitted special case.

This establishes existence in the stated report space of probability laws. No finite-time implementation claim is made: the task specifies an encoding for realized societies, but does not specify finite encodings or evaluation procedures for forecast laws.
