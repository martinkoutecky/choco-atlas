The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supports of worker types \(I\) and job types \(J\), rational masses \(p_i,q_j\) summing to \(1\) on each side, known strict job-type priorities over worker types, and sampling oracles for the unknown worker-type/job-type reward means, determine the sample complexity of returning with probability at least \(1-\alpha\) the job-proposing extremal stable transport plan \(x\). The plan must satisfy \(\sum_j x_{ij}=p_i\), \(\sum_i x_{ij}=q_j\), and have no blocking type pair: for every \((i,j)\), either \(\sum_{j':\,j\succ_i j'}x_{ij'}=0\) or \(\sum_{i':\,i\succ_j i'}x_{i'j}=0\). Establish whether a type-level AE/DA learner can achieve sample complexity governed by the number of relevant type pairs and the preference gap \(\Delta\), rather than by the total population.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Worker and job societies are specified by their positive-mass supports \(I,J\), with binary rational masses \(p_i,q_j>0\) satisfying \(\sum_i p_i=\sum_j q_j=1\). Let \(m=|J|\geq2\), \(\tau=|I|+|J|\), and \(L\) be the bit length of the explicit input; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
All pairs are acceptable. Each job has a known strict total priority order \(\succ_j\); workers rank jobs by unknown real means \(\mu_{ij}\), distinct within each row. Define \(\Delta=\min_i\min_{j\ne k}|\mu_{ij}-\mu_{ik}|>0\), which is not supplied to the learner.
A centralized learner may query any pair \((i,j)\); each query costs one sample and returns an independent reward with mean \(\mu_{ij}\) and centered \(1\)-subgaussian noise, independently of its mass. The confidence parameter is a binary rational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).
A stable transport plan satisfies \(x_{ij}\geq0\), \(\sum_jx_{ij}=p_i\), \(\sum_ix_{ij}=q_j\), and, for every \((i,j)\), either \(\sum_{j':\,j\succ_i j'}x_{ij'}=0\) or \(\sum_{i':\,i\succ_j i'}x_{i'j}=0\).
The job-optimal extremal plan \(x^J\) is the stable plan whose column for every job is lexicographically maximal, in that job's priority order, among stable plans.
Define \(R_i(x)=\{j:\sum_{i':\,i\succ_j i'}x_{i'j}>0\}\), and \(E_i(x)=\varnothing\) if \(R_i(x)=\varnothing\), otherwise \(E_i(x)=R_i(x)\cup\{j:x_{ij}>0\}\). Let \(r(x)=\sum_i|E_i(x)|\). Sample complexity bounds the total queries on the same event on which the exact output is correct; express running times in \(m,\tau,L,\Delta,\alpha\) and the number of samples.

Can a type-level action-elimination/deferred-acceptance learner return \(x^J\), with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), using \(\widetilde O(r(x^J)(1+\Delta^{-2})\log(2/\alpha))\) samples, or is this bound impossible? Here suppressed logarithms may depend only on \(m,\tau,\Delta^{-1},\alpha^{-1}\), not on mass denominators or a replicated population size.

For finite agents and arms, an action-elimination arm-proposing learner is claimed to return the arm-optimal stable matching with \(\widetilde O(|ES|\Delta^{-2}\log(\alpha^{-1}))\) samples, where \(ES\) is the corresponding envy-set. [Hosseini–Roy–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04376v2)
With known preferences, stable allocations admit strongly polynomial computation and unique side-optimal extrema; this does not address sampling unknown preferences. [Baïou–Balinski, corrected version](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302)

Result

Yes—the bound is achievable. It requires retaining samples across comparisons and accelerating cycles of fractional proposals.

Write \(n=|I|=\tau-m\), \(N=nm\), and \(h=\max\{1,\Delta^{-2}\}\). There is a type-level learner, not supplied with \(\Delta\), such that with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), it returns exactly \(x^J\) and its total number \(S\) of queries satisfies

\(S=O\!\left(r(x^J)\,h\log\!\frac{N h}{\alpha}\right).\)

This implies the requested \(\widetilde O\!\left(r(x^J)(1+\Delta^{-2})\log(2/\alpha)\right)\) bound. No mass denominator or replicated population size occurs in the sampling logarithm.

The implementation uses \(O(m^3\tau+S)\) arithmetic operations. Its mass bookkeeping can be performed in \(O(m^3\tau L^2)\) bit operations. Arithmetic on observed real rewards is counted separately in the usual reward-oracle model, since the question specifies no finite encoding of those rewards.

For \(n=1\), the answer is immediate: \(x_{1j}=q_j\), \(r(x^J)=0\), and no samples are needed. The proof below covers general supported societies.

The comparison rule follows the finite-agent AE–DA approach in [Hosseini–Roy–Zhang, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04376v2). The allocation and acceleration arguments below are proved directly for the stated type-level model.

1. A deterministic job-proposing procedure

First suppose comparisons between worker means are available through a correct comparison oracle.

Maintain a nonnegative held allocation \(x\), initially zero, and each job’s free mass

\(f_j=q_j-\sum_i x_{ij}.\)

Every row satisfies \(\sum_jx_{ij}\le p_i\). Each job has a pointer to its highest-priority worker that has not yet rejected it. A rejection closes that pair permanently: the job never subsequently increases its allocation there.

Choose a job \(j\) with \(f_j>0\), and let \(i\) be its current pointer.

  • The worker has spare capacity. Transfer \(\min\{f_j,p_i-\sum_kx_{ik}\}\) to \(x_{ij}\). This needs no worker comparison.
  • The worker is full. Let \(k\) be its least-preferred job with \(x_{ik}>0\).
  • If \(j=k\), or \(k\succ_i j\), worker \(i\) rejects the free proposal from \(j\). Close \((i,j)\), retain its existing allocation, and advance \(j\)’s pointer.
  • If \(j\succ_i k\), transfer an amount \(t\), with \(0<t\le\min\{f_j,x_{ik}\}\), from \(x_{ik}\) to \(x_{ij}\). Thus \(f_j\) decreases by \(t\) and \(f_k\) increases by \(t\). If \(k\)’s pointer is currently \(i\), close \((i,k)\) and advance that pointer. If it has already passed \(i\), the pair remains closed.

The implementation ordinarily takes the maximal permitted transfer. Allowing smaller transfers will be useful when proving that a cycle acceleration is valid.

The following invariants follow immediately from these operations:

  • A job holds mass only at its current pointer and at higher-priority workers.
  • Allocations on closed pairs can only decrease.
  • Once a worker becomes full, it remains full.
  • After a rejection at a full worker, that worker’s least-preferred held job can only improve.

A free job cannot have passed every worker. Indeed, every worker it passed was full at its rejection and remains full. If it had passed all workers while retaining free mass, all rows would be full although total held mass were less than \(1\), contradicting \(\sum_i p_i=\sum_jq_j=1\).

Comparisons are requested only when a full worker receives a positive free proposal.

2. Polynomial acceleration of fractional cycles

Ordinary fractional transfers can repeat many times. This is a genuine computational issue: the greedy procedure in [Baïou–Balinski, Example 1](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302) can require arbitrarily many steps even with two types on each side.

Here is a direct acceleration for the preceding procedure.

Structural events

Call any of the following a structural event:

  • a job pointer advances;
  • a positive allocation entry becomes zero;
  • a worker first becomes full.

There are at most \(nm\) pointer advances and at most \(n\) worker-filling events. There are also at most \(nm\) disappearances of positive entries: an entry can decrease only when its pair is closed, or is being closed, and it can never increase after that.

Thus the total number of structural events is at most

\(K=2nm+n.\)

Let \(F=|\{j:f_j>0\}|\). A structural operation increases \(F\) by at most one. Consequently, the total number of decreases of \(F\) is at most \(m+K\).

Following free mass

After a maximal displacement at a full worker, continue with the displaced job. Reset the recorded path whenever a structural event occurs or \(F\) decreases.

Between resets, every transfer has the following properties:

  • the worker is full;
  • the displaced edge is closed and stays positive;
  • the receiving job was previously without free mass;
  • the whole active free mass passes to that job;
  • pointers and least-preferred held jobs do not change.

The last property holds because the added job is strictly preferred to the displaced job, and the displaced job remains positive.

Therefore, between resets the route is fixed: a job points to its current worker, which points to its least-preferred held job. After at most \(m\) such transfers, either a reset occurs or a job repeats.

Rotating a repeated cycle

Suppose the repeated cycle has distinct jobs \(j_1,\ldots,j_\ell\), with \(j_{\ell+1}=j_1\), and workers \(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell\), where

  • \(i_a\) is the current pointer of \(j_a\);
  • \(j_{a+1}\) is the least-preferred positive holder at \(i_a\);
  • \(j_a\succ_{i_a}j_{a+1}\);
  • \((i_a,j_{a+1})\) is closed.

The workers are distinct. Otherwise two cycle jobs would have the same successor, whereas successors on a simple directed cycle are distinct.

Set

\(\delta=\min_{1\le a\le\ell}x_{i_a,j_{a+1}}.\)

Simultaneously perform

\(x_{i_a,j_a}\leftarrow x_{i_a,j_a}+\delta,\qquad x_{i_a,j_{a+1}}\leftarrow x_{i_a,j_{a+1}}-\delta.\)

Every row total and every column total is unchanged. All entries remain nonnegative, and at least one closed positive entry becomes zero.

This rotation is equivalent to a finite sequence of legal proposal transfers. The repeated job has positive free mass \(f\). Pass an amount \(\varepsilon\le f\) around the entire cycle, returning that amount to the starting job. Each worker exchanges a worse held job for a better proposing job. Repeat with amounts \(\min\{f,\text{remaining rotation amount}\}\) until their sum is \(\delta\). No pointer advances occur because all decreased edges are closed. No decreased edge disappears before its permitted final decrease. Because the cycle workers are distinct, that final round can be completed even if an earlier edge in the round reaches zero.

This finite simulation is only a correctness argument. The implementation performs the rotation directly, using \(O(m)\) operations.

Each rotation produces a structural event. Hence the total number of ordinary processing steps is

\(O\!\left(m(K+m)\right)=O(nm^2).\)

In particular, the accelerated procedure terminates independently of mass denominators. At termination all \(f_j\) vanish. Column totals are therefore \(q_j\); since row totals are bounded by \(p_i\) and their sums agree, every row total is exactly \(p_i\).

3. Every terminating execution returns the job-optimal plan

We now prove both stability and job optimality. Comparisons in this section are correct.

Stability

Suppose the terminal plan assigns positive mass of job \(j\) to a worker below \(i\) in \(j\)’s priority order. Job \(j\) must have passed \(i\).

When its pointer passed \(i\), that worker was full and held only jobs weakly preferred to \(j\):

  • either its free proposal was rejected because all held jobs were at least as good as \(j\);
  • or some held mass of \(j\), which was then least preferred, was displaced by a better proposal.

Subsequent changes preserve fullness and improve the least-preferred held job. Thus the terminal row at \(i\) contains no job strictly worse than \(j\).

Consequently, whenever

\(\sum_{i':\,i\succ_j i'}x_{i'j}>0,\)

we have

\(\sum_{j':\,j\succ_i j'}x_{ij'}=0.\)

This is precisely the required stability condition.

Rejection bounds valid for every stable plan

For each pair maintain a cap \(u_{ij}\):

  • initially, \(u_{ij}=p_i\);
  • once the pair is closed, \(u_{ij}=x_{ij}\), updated whenever that held allocation decreases.

We claim that every stable transport plan \(y\) for the original preferences satisfies \(y_{ij}\le u_{ij}\) throughout the procedure.

Initially this follows from the row mass constraint. It remains to justify each cap reduction.

Consider a displacement at a full worker \(i\). Let \(k\) be its least-preferred holder, let \(j\succ_i k\) be the proposer, and let \(t>0\) be transferred. Define \(z\) to be the held matrix immediately before removing the displaced mass, but after adding \(t\) at \((i,j)\). Write \(x'_{ik}=x_{ik}-t\).

Because \(k\) was least preferred,

\(\sum_{a:\,a\succ_i k}z_{ia}=p_i-x'_{ik}.\)

Suppose a stable plan \(y\), respecting the previous caps, had \(y_{ik}>x'_{ik}\). Then

\(\sum_{a:\,a\succ_i k}y_{ia}\le p_i-y_{ik}<p_i-x'_{ik}=\sum_{a:\,a\succ_i k}z_{ia}.\)

Therefore some job \(a\succ_i k\) satisfies \(y_{ia}<z_{ia}\).

For this job \(a\), every worker higher-priority than \(i\) is already a closed pair. This holds whether \(a\) was previously held at \(i\) or is the incoming proposer. On those higher-priority pairs, the previous caps equal the held allocations. Hence

\(\sum_{b:\,b\succ_a i}y_{ba}\le\sum_{b:\,b\succ_a i}z_{ba}.\)

Combining this with \(y_{ia}<z_{ia}\) gives

\(\sum_{b:\,b\succ_a i}y_{ba}+y_{ia}<\sum_{b:\,b\succ_a i}z_{ba}+z_{ia}\le q_a.\)

The final inequality holds because the proposer added at most its free mass; every other job column was unchanged. Since \(y\)’s column total is \(q_a\), it assigns positive mass of \(a\) below \(i\).

But \(y_{ik}>x'_{ik}\ge0\), and \(a\succ_i k\). Thus \((i,a)\) blocks \(y\), a contradiction.

This proves that reducing the cap on the displaced pair is valid.

For a rejected free proposal from \(j\), the full worker holds only jobs weakly preferred to \(j\). If \(y_{ij}>x_{ij}\), then

\(\sum_{a:\,a\succ_i j}y_{ia}<p_i-x_{ij}=\sum_{a:\,a\succ_i j}x_{ia}.\)

The same argument finds a preferred held job \(a\) that blocks \(y\). Thus closing \((i,j)\) with cap \(x_{ij}\) is also valid.

Cycle rotations preserve this invariant because they are finite sequences of these legal transfers.

Lexicographic optimality

At termination, fix a job \(j\). All pairs above its current pointer are closed, and their allocations equal their caps. It holds no mass below its pointer, and its total held mass is \(q_j\).

Therefore its terminal column is the greedy filling of the final caps in priority order:

\(x_{ij}=\min\!\left\{u_{ij},\left(q_j-\sum_{a:\,a\succ_j i}u_{aj}\right)_+\right\},\)

where \((v)_+=\max\{0,v\}\).

Greedy filling is lexicographically maximal among vectors with total mass \(q_j\) and coordinate bounds \(u_{\cdot j}\). To verify this, consider the first priority position where another vector differs. If the greedy coordinate equals its cap, the other coordinate cannot exceed it. If it is below its cap, the preceding coordinates and this coordinate already exhaust \(q_j\), so again the other coordinate cannot exceed it.

Every stable plan respects these final caps. Thus the terminal column of every job is lexicographically maximal among stable plans. Together with stability, this proves that the terminal plan is exactly \(x^J\).

4. Only final envy-set pairs need comparisons

The comparison implementation exposes jobs at worker \(i\) only when a positive free proposal reaches a full row. At that moment expose the proposing job and all positive holders.

Maintain a sorted list \(C_i\) of all jobs ever exposed there. Insert newly exposed jobs by comparison-based binary insertion; never revise the relative order of previously inserted jobs. The least-preferred positive holder can then be found by scanning the list. All queried pairs belong to some \(C_i\).

We prove

\(C_i\subseteq E_i(x^J).\)

Every rejected pair belongs to the final \(R_i\)

When job \(j\)’s pointer first passes worker \(i\), all its held mass is at \(i\) or above, and it has strictly positive free mass after the rejection. Consequently, its held mass at \(i\) and above is strictly less than \(q_j\).

All these pairs are now closed and can only decrease. At termination its column has total mass \(q_j\), so it must assign positive mass below \(i\). Therefore \(j\in R_i(x^J)\).

Any later reduction of an already closed pair has the same conclusion, because its pointer previously passed that worker.

Charging an exposure

At an exposure at worker \(i\), the ensuing operation rejects either positive free mass of the proposer or positive held mass of an incumbent. Hence \(R_i(x^J)\ne\varnothing\).

Now take any job exposed at this operation.

  • A positive holder either remains positive in the terminal plan or subsequently loses held mass, making its pair rejected.
  • The proposer either is rejected immediately or acquires positive held mass. In the latter case it likewise either remains positive or subsequently suffers rejection.

Thus every exposed job belongs to

\(R_i(x^J)\cup\{j:x^J_{ij}>0\}=E_i(x^J).\)

This proves the claimed inclusion, including the essential condition that \(E_i\) is empty when \(R_i\) is empty. In particular,

\(\sum_i|C_i|\le r(x^J).\)

5. Confidence comparisons without knowing the gap

For every supported pair, retain all its samples across all comparison calls. After \(t\ge1\) samples, let its empirical mean be \(\widehat\mu_{ij}(t)\), and set

\(b(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{t}\log\frac{4Nt^2}{\alpha}}.\)

Use the interval \([\widehat\mu_{ij}(t)-b(t),\widehat\mu_{ij}(t)+b(t)]\); with no samples use the entire real line.

To compare two distinct jobs at one worker, sample whichever has fewer retained samples until their intervals are disjoint. Return the resulting certified order. This rule does not use \(\Delta\).

One event controls correctness and sample complexity

For each pair, regard its observations as a pre-generated independent stream. Subgaussian concentration gives

\(\Pr\!\left(|\widehat\mu_{ij}(t)-\mu_{ij}|>b(t)\right)\le2e^{-tb(t)^2/2}=\frac{\alpha}{2Nt^2}.\)

A union bound over all supported pairs and all positive integers \(t\), using \(\sum_{t\ge1}t^{-2}\le2\), shows that the event

\(\mathcal G=\left\{|\widehat\mu_{ij}(t)-\mu_{ij}|\le b(t)\text{ for every supported pair and every }t\ge1\right\}\)

has probability at least \(1-\alpha\). This remains valid for adaptive sampling.

On \(\mathcal G\), every certified comparison is correct. Accordingly, all exposed lists have their true order, and the preceding allocation and charging proofs apply.

A uniform per-pair sample bound

For analysis only, define

\(A=\log\frac{1024Nh}{\alpha},\qquad B=\left\lceil1024hA\right\rceil.\)

Because \(N\ge2\), we have \(B\le2048hA\), \(\log B\le2A\), and \(\log(4N/\alpha)\le A\). Thus

\(b(B)^2=\frac{2}{B}\left(\log\frac{4N}{\alpha}+2\log B\right)\le\frac{10}{1024h}<\frac1{64h}\le\frac{\Delta^2}{64}.\)

When both jobs in a comparison have \(B\) samples, their intervals are disjoint on \(\mathcal G\): their empirical means differ in the true direction by at least \(\Delta-2b(B)\), and

\(\Delta-4b(B)\ge\Delta/2>0.\)

The less-sampled-first rule therefore never samples a pair beyond \(B\). Indeed, if one count equals \(B\), it samples the other until separation; if both equal \(B\), separation already holds.

By the exposure bound, at most \(r(x^J)\) distinct pairs are sampled. Hence, on the same event \(\mathcal G\) on which the output is exactly correct,

\(S\le r(x^J)B=O\!\left(r(x^J)\,h\log\frac{Nh}{\alpha}\right).\)

Since \(N\le m\tau\) and \(h\le1+\Delta^{-2}\), all suppressed logarithms have precisely the permitted dependencies.

6. Implementation and running time

There is no population replication.

For exact mass arithmetic, one may compute the product \(D\) of the input mass denominators and store \(Dx_{ij}\), \(Df_j\), \(Dp_i\), and \(Dq_j\) as integers. The bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Transfers and cycle rotations use only integer comparisons, additions, and subtractions on \(O(L)\)-bit quantities.

The accelerated procedure has \(O(nm^2)\) processing steps. Each step requires at most \(O(m)\) list and support scanning. Each exposed job is inserted once, using \(O(\log(2m))\) confidence comparisons. Sample updates and confidence checks add \(O(S)\) real arithmetic operations.

Consequently, a conservative bound is:

  • \(O(m^3\tau+S)\) arithmetic operations;
  • \(O(m^3\tau L^2)\) bit operations for mass and combinatorial bookkeeping.

The maintained insertion lists also make the computation well-defined outside \(\mathcal G\): their relative orders are preserved, so they extend to fixed strict artificial preferences. The structural-event analysis therefore bounds computation in terms of the realized number of samples, regardless of comparison correctness.

The claimed success event simultaneously guarantees exact output, the stated sample bound, and polynomial computation. Denominators affect arithmetic precision through \(L\), not the number of proposal events or the sampling logarithms.
