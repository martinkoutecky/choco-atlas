The continuous mirror question, as graded

1. Consequences of exact normalization

Fix a column \(j\). The bounded random variables \(X_{tj}^2\) are independent and identically distributed, and
\[ \sum_{t=1}^T X_{tj}^2=T \quad\text{almost surely}. \]
Taking variances gives
\[ 0=\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{t=1}^T X_{tj}^2\right) =T\operatorname{Var}(X_{1j}^2). \]
Consequently \(X_{1j}^2\) is constant almost surely. Taking expectations in the normalization identifies that constant as \(1\).

Since there are finitely many entries, almost surely
\[ |X_{tj}|=1\quad\text{for every }t,j. \]
In particular,
\[ q\ge m\ge K,\qquad G_{jj}=\Sigma_{jj}=1. \]
The population coherence hypothesis therefore becomes
\[ |\Sigma_{ij}|<\frac1{4K}\qquad(i\ne j). \]

Also, choosing \(u=e_j\) for any \(j\in S\) in the population RE assumption shows that
\[ \kappa_\Sigma^2\le1. \]

2. Feasible allocations and deterministic welfare control

Let \(s_j=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}A_{ij}\). Every column is nonzero and integral, so \(s_j\ge1\). For every \(z\in F\),
\[ 1=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i =\sum_{j=1}^m s_jz_j \ge\sum_{j=1}^m z_j. \]
Thus \(F\subseteq[0,1]^m\). It is nonempty and closed, hence compact, so both welfare maximizers exist, irrespective of coefficient signs.

Write \(\Delta=\hat\beta-\beta^\star\) and let \(R\) denote the welfare regret. Optimality of \(\hat z\) for the estimated objective gives
\[ \begin{aligned} R &=(\beta^\star)^\top(z^\star-\hat z)\\ &=\hat\beta^\top(z^\star-\hat z) +\Delta^\top(\hat z-z^\star)\\ &\le\Delta^\top(\hat z-z^\star) \le\|\Delta\|_1, \end{aligned} \]
because \(|\hat z_j-z_j^\star|\le1\) for every \(j\). Optimality of \(z^\star\) also gives \(R\ge0\).

This argument applies to every choice of the two maximizers.
