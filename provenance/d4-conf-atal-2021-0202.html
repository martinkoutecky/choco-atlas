The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a target \(c\), a scoring rule \(r\in\{\mathrm{Borda},2\text{-approval}\}\), and partial-chain types \(P_1,\ldots,P_\tau\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), let \(\operatorname{Ext}(P_t)\) be the total rankings extending \(P_t\). Does there exist a nonnegative real allocation \(x_{t,\pi}\) satisfying \(\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P_t)}x_{t,\pi}=\mu_t\) for each \(t\), such that \(\sum_{t,\pi}x_{t,\pi}s_r(\pi,c)\ge\sum_{t,\pi}x_{t,\pi}s_r(\pi,d)\) for every \(d\ne c\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set with \(m=|C|\ge 3\), let \(c\in C\), and let \(r\in\{\mathrm{Borda},2\text{-approval}\}\).
A partial-chain type \(P_t\) specifies a strict total order on a nonempty subset \(C_t\subseteq C\); candidates outside \(C_t\) have no specified comparisons. The subsets may differ across types, and complete rankings are permitted.
A society is given by its support: an explicit list of the \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Let \(\operatorname{Ext}(P_t)\) be the strict total rankings of \(C\) extending \(P_t\), and let \(s_r(\pi,a)\) denote candidate \(a\)'s score in ranking \(\pi\) under \(r\).
Call the instance feasible if there exist real masses \(x_{t,\pi}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P_t)}x_{t,\pi}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), such that \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P_t)}x_{t,\pi}\bigl(s_r(\pi,c)-s_r(\pi,d)\bigr)\ge 0\) for every \(d\in C\setminus\{c\}\). Thus mass may split among extensions, and ties for the highest score count as wins.

For each fixed rule \(r\in\{\mathrm{Borda},2\text{-approval}\}\), is deciding feasibility solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete possible winner on partial chains is NP-complete for Borda and for \(2\)-approval. [Source](https://research.ibm.com/publications/classifying-the-complexity-of-the-possible-winner-problem-on-partial-chains)
No result settling this exact divisible-mass problem was identified in the literature search.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

For both Borda and \(2\)-approval, deciding the stated divisible-mass feasibility problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\).

The essential fact is that a linear objective over the extensions of one partial chain can be optimized by sorting its unconstrained candidates and then using dynamic programming. This gives a polynomial-time separation oracle for a linear program with \(m-1+\tau\) variables.

1. Optimizing over one partial chain

Write the positional scoring vector as \(\beta_1\ge\cdots\ge\beta_m\), where position \(1\) is highest. For Borda, \(\beta_j=m-j\). For \(2\)-approval, \(\beta_1=\beta_2=1\) and the remaining entries are zero.

Lemma. Given a partial chain \(P\) and rational candidate weights \(w_a\), one can compute an extension maximizing \(\sum_{a\in C}w_a s_r(\pi,a)\) using \(O(m^2)\) rational arithmetic operations.

Proof. Let the prescribed chain be \(a_1\succ\cdots\succ a_k\), and let \(q=m-k\). Sort the candidates outside the chain into a list \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\) satisfying \(w_{b_1}\ge\cdots\ge w_{b_q}\).

There is an optimal extension whose outside candidates occur in this order. To prove this, suppose outside candidates \(u,v\) occur at positions \(i<j\) with \(w_u<w_v\). Swapping them preserves the prescribed chain, because neither candidate belongs to it. The change in objective is

\((w_v-w_u)(\beta_i-\beta_j)\ge 0.\)

Consequently, sorting the outside candidates while keeping the chain candidates in their existing positions cannot decrease the objective. Equal weights may be ordered arbitrarily. Thus some optimum is an interleaving of the two lists \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\).

Define \(F(i,j)\) as the maximum contribution from the first \(i+j\) positions among interleavings of \(a_1,\ldots,a_i\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_j\). Set \(F(0,0)=0\). Every nonempty interleaving ends with either \(a_i\) or \(b_j\), so

\(F(i,j)=\max\bigl\{F(i-1,j)+w_{a_i}\beta_{i+j},\ F(i,j-1)+w_{b_j}\beta_{i+j}\bigr\},\)

where a term is omitted when its corresponding index is zero.

The recurrence considers every possible last candidate, and hence is exact by induction on \(i+j\). Its final value \(F(k,q)\) is the required optimum; backtracking produces a maximizing extension.

Sorting costs \(O(m\log m)\) comparisons, and the table has \((k+1)(q+1)=O(m^2)\) entries. This also handles complete chains, for which \(q=0\). All arithmetic has bit complexity polynomial in the encoding length of the supplied weights and in \(m\). \(\square\)

2. A primal linear program for feasibility

Let \(D=C\setminus\{c\}\), and define the margin of an extension against opponent \(d\) by

\(g_{t,\pi,d}=s_r(\pi,c)-s_r(\pi,d).\)

Set \(M=m-1\) for Borda and \(M=1\) for \(2\)-approval. In either case, \(|g_{t,\pi,d}|\le M\).

Consider the following finite, potentially exponentially large linear program. Its variables are \(x_{t,\pi}\ge 0\) and an unrestricted real variable \(\lambda\):

  • Maximize \(\lambda\).
  • Require \(\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P_t)}x_{t,\pi}=\mu_t\) for every type \(t\).
  • Require \(\sum_t\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P_t)}x_{t,\pi}g_{t,\pi,d}\ge\lambda\) for every \(d\in D\).

Let its optimum be \(\lambda^\star\).

Each prescribed chain has at least one extension, so the mass constraints admit a solution. Their solution set is a product of finite-dimensional simplices and is therefore compact. The minimum opponent margin is a continuous function on this set; consequently, its maximum \(\lambda^\star\) is attained.

Because the total mass is one and every individual margin belongs to \([-M,M]\), we have \(-M\le\lambda^\star\le M\). By its definition,

\(\text{the instance is feasible}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lambda^\star\ge 0.\)

In particular, equality correctly includes instances where the best achievable outcome is a tied win.

3. A dual with polynomially many variables

Introduce nonnegative variables \(y_d\) for the opponent-margin inequalities and unrestricted variables \(z_t\) for the mass equalities. The dual is:

  • Minimize \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tz_t\).
  • Require \(y_d\ge 0\) for every \(d\in D\) and \(\sum_{d\in D}y_d=1\).
  • Require \(z_t\ge\sum_{d\in D}y_dg_{t,\pi,d}\) for every type \(t\) and every \(\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P_t)\).

For completeness, these constraints follow from the Lagrangian

\(\lambda+\sum_d y_d\bigl(\sum_{t,\pi}x_{t,\pi}g_{t,\pi,d}-\lambda\bigr)+\sum_tz_t\bigl(\mu_t-\sum_\pi x_{t,\pi}\bigr).\)

Collecting terms gives

\(\lambda\bigl(1-\sum_dy_d\bigr)+\sum_t\mu_tz_t+\sum_{t,\pi}x_{t,\pi}\bigl(\sum_dy_dg_{t,\pi,d}-z_t\bigr).\)

Its supremum over unrestricted \(\lambda\) and nonnegative \(x\) is finite exactly when the displayed dual constraints hold. In that case the supremum equals \(\sum_t\mu_tz_t\).

The primal is a finite feasible linear program with finite optimum. Therefore, finite-dimensional linear-programming strong duality applies, and the dual optimum equals \(\lambda^\star\).

We can additionally impose \(-M\le z_t\le M\) without changing that optimum. Indeed, for a fixed feasible \(y\), put

\(h_t(y)=\max_{\pi\in\operatorname{Ext}(P_t)}\sum_{d\in D}y_dg_{t,\pi,d}.\)

Since \(y\) is a probability vector and the margins are bounded, \(h_t(y)\in[-M,M]\). Replacing \(z_t\) by \(h_t(y)\) preserves feasibility and cannot increase the objective, because \(\mu_t>0\).

Denote this bounded dual feasible region by \(Q\). It is nonempty: any probability vector \(y\), together with \(z_t=M\) for every \(t\), is feasible. It has \(n=m-1+\tau\) variables.

4. Polynomial-time separation

Given a rational query point \((y,z)\), first check the nonnegativity, normalization, and box constraints. A violated constraint immediately separates the point from \(Q\).

If those checks pass, then for every type \(t\) compute \(h_t(y)\). Its objective can be rewritten as

\(\sum_{d\in D}y_d\bigl(s_r(\pi,c)-s_r(\pi,d)\bigr)=w_cs_r(\pi,c)+\sum_{d\in D}w_ds_r(\pi,d),\)

where \(w_c=\sum_{d\in D}y_d=1\) and \(w_d=-y_d\).

The lemma computes this maximum and a maximizing extension \(\pi_t^\star\) in \(O(m^2)\) rational arithmetic operations.

If \(z_t<h_t(y)\), return the violated defining inequality

\(z_t\ge\sum_{d\in D}y_dg_{t,\pi_t^\star,d}.\)

Otherwise, if every type passes, all extension inequalities hold and the query point belongs to \(Q\).

This is an exact strong separation oracle. One call costs \(O(m+\tau+\tau m^2)\) rational arithmetic operations, with polynomial bit complexity in the query encoding length and \(m,\tau\). It never enumerates the extensions.

5. Exact optimization and running time

We use the standard rational separation–optimization theorem: a rational polyhedron with a known polynomial bound on the encoding length of each defining inequality admits exact polynomial-time linear optimization when supplied with a polynomial-time strong separation oracle. The theorem also accommodates lower-dimensional polyhedra. See the discussion of the classical bit-complexity result in [Dadush, Végh, and Zambelli, *On finding exact solutions of linear programs in the oracle model*](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35149/35149.pdf).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • The dimension is \(n=m-1+\tau\).
  • The polyhedron is nonempty and bounded, since \(y\) is a probability vector and \(|z_t|\le M\).
  • Every defining inequality has integer coefficients and right-hand side of magnitude at most \(M\). Its encoding length is \(O(n\log(m+1))\), independently of how many extensions exist.
  • The rational objective coefficients are the input masses, whose total encoding length is at most \(L\).
  • Section 4 supplies an exact polynomial-time strong separation oracle.

The theorem therefore computes the dual optimum exactly in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) bit operations. Accept precisely when that optimum is nonnegative. By strong duality and Section 2, this decision is equivalent to the required feasibility condition.

Thus the algorithm handles zero margins exactly, uses the explicitly supplied support size \(\tau\), and applies to arbitrary binary rational masses.

6. Relation to discrete possible winner

The cited discrete NP-completeness results concern choosing one extension for each indivisible vote. Here each type may distribute its mass among extensions, giving exactly the continuous variables of the primal linear program. The polynomial-time algorithm depends on that permission to split mass.

The argument resolves both requested rules. In fact, the sorting and dynamic-programming lemma applies to any nonincreasing rational positional scoring vector of polynomial encoding length.
