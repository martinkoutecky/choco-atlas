Theorem 5.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses μ, additive valuations \(v_t\) over \(q\) good types, per-capita supply \(b\), a bundle cap \(K\), and \(\varepsilon>0\), compute a finite-support lottery over feasible mass allocations \(x_{t,z}\), where each bundle \(z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^q\) has \(\sum_g z_g\le K\), \(\sum_z x_{t,z}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_g=b_g\). Each type’s expected value must be at least its proportional share \(v_t(b)\); in every outcome, every recipient must receive at least \((0.9-\varepsilon)M_t\), and each recipient below \((1-\varepsilon)M_t\) must be EFX-satisfied against every bundle assigned positive mass. Here \(M_t\) is the greatest value threshold attainable by assigning the same per-capita supply \(b\) among a unit-mass population with valuation \(v_t\), using integral bundles from the capped bundle set.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=q\geq1\) good types, nonnegative rational additive valuations \(v_t\), nonnegative rational per-capita supplies \(b_g\), a positive integer cap \(K\), and rational \(0<\varepsilon<9/10\). Assume \(\sum_g b_g\leq K\). All numerical input, including \(K\), is binary encoded; \(L\) is its total bit length.
Write \(\mathcal B_K=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m:\sum_g z_g\leq K\}\). A mass allocation has \(x_{t,z}\geq0\), \(\sum_zx_{t,z}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_g=b_g\) for every good type \(g\).
Define \(M_t=\max\{a:\exists y_z\geq0,\ \sum_z y_z=1,\ \sum_z y_z z=b,\ y_z>0\Rightarrow v_t(z)\geq a\}\), with bundles restricted to \(\mathcal B_K\).
In an outcome \(x\), a recipient of type \(t\) with bundle \(z\) is EFX-satisfied if \(v_t(z)\geq v_t(w-e_g)\) whenever \(x_{s,w}>0\) and \(w_g>0\), including when \(v_t(e_g)=0\); \(e_g\) denotes one copy of good type \(g\).
The computational problem is to construct a finite lottery with rational probabilities \(p_j\) and rational mass allocations \(x^j\), or determine that none exists, satisfying \(\sum_jp_j\mu_t^{-1}\sum_zx^j_{t,z}v_t(z)\geq v_t(b)\) for every type \(t\).
Every positive-mass assignment in every positive-probability outcome must satisfy \(v_t(z)\geq(9/10-\varepsilon)M_t\); whenever \(v_t(z)<(1-\varepsilon)M_t\), that recipient must be EFX-satisfied.

Does this problem admit an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\) that achieves these same approximation and exact fairness guarantees, or is achieving them NP-hard?

For three discrete agents with nonnegative additive valuations, an FPTAS constructs an ex-ante proportional lottery guaranteeing ex-post \((9/10-\varepsilon)\)-MMS and EFX satisfaction for every agent below \((1-\varepsilon)\)-MMS. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14668v2).

A fully polynomial algorithm exists

The stated mass-allocation problem admits an algorithm running in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)$ time. In fact, it constructs a single rational mass-allocation outcome in which every positive-mass assignment satisfies
$$ v_t(z)\geq(1-\varepsilon)M_t. $$
Every type receives exactly its proportional value in conditional expectation. Consequently, the requested lower guarantee holds, and the conditional EFX requirement holds because its antecedent never occurs.

The proof uses the freedom, specific to this mass model, to give each type its own bundle distribution with mean $b$. The substantive algorithmic step is computing such a distribution with minimum value close to its benchmark, without expanding the binary-encoded cap $K$.

1. Reduction to a single valuation

Fix a nonnegative rational valuation vector $v=(v_1,\ldots,v_m)$, and write
$$ v(z)=\sum_g v_gz_g,\qquad V=v(b). $$
Let $M$ denote its benchmark, as defined in the question.

A distribution with mean $b$ always exists: give bundle $Ke_g$ probability $b_g/K$, and give the empty bundle the remaining probability
$$ 1-\frac{\sum_g b_g}{K}. $$
These probabilities are nonnegative by the supply assumption.

Every distribution with mean $b$ has expected value $V$. Since $\mathcal B_K$ is finite, the largest achievable minimum supported value is attained and is a bundle value. Thus
$$ 0\leq M\leq V. $$
For any threshold $a$, define
$$ H(a)=\{z\in\mathcal B_K:v(z)\geq a\}. $$
The definition gives
$$ M\geq a \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b\in\operatorname{conv}H(a). $$

We will construct, in fully polynomial time, a rational distribution $y$ satisfying
$$ \sum_z y_z=1,\qquad \sum_z y_zz=b,\qquad y_z>0\Longrightarrow v(z)\geq(1-\varepsilon)M. $$

Applying this construction separately to every supported type and setting
$$ x_{t,z}=\mu_ty^t_z $$
will produce the required outcome. The remaining sections prove the single-valuation construction.

2. Exact rounding with bounded value loss

We first need a rounding lemma that preserves the mean and the bundle cap exactly.

Lemma. Let $N$ be a nonnegative integer, let $w\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^m$ satisfy $\sum_gw_g\leq N$, and let rational coefficients $s_g$ satisfy $0\leq s_g\leq C$. In polynomial time, one can construct a rational distribution on at most $m+1$ integer vectors $\widehat w\in\mathcal B_N$ such that
$$ \mathbb E[\widehat w]=w $$
and every supported vector satisfies
$$ \sum_gs_g\widehat w_g\geq\sum_gs_gw_g-C. $$
The running time is polynomial in the encoded data, including $\log(N+1)$.

Proof. Add a dummy coordinate
$$ w_{m+1}=N-\sum_{g=1}^m w_g $$
with coefficient $s_{m+1}=0$. Put $d=m+1$, and reorder the coordinates so that
$$ s_1\leq s_2\leq\cdots\leq s_d. $$
Write
$$ f_i=\lfloor w_i\rfloor,\qquad r_i=w_i-f_i,\qquad S_i=\sum_{j=1}^i r_j,\qquad S_0=0. $$
Because $\sum_iw_i=N$ is an integer,
$$ J=\sum_i r_i $$
is an integer.

For $\theta\in[0,1)$, define
$$ \widehat w_i(\theta) = f_i+\lfloor S_i+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor S_{i-1}+\theta\rfloor. $$
The difference of floors is either zero or one. Consequently, all coordinates are nonnegative integers, and
$$ \sum_{i=1}^d\widehat w_i(\theta) = \sum_i f_i+\lfloor J+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor\theta\rfloor =N. $$
Discarding the dummy coordinate therefore gives a vector in $\mathcal B_N$.

For uniform $\theta\in[0,1)$, the identity
$$ \mathbb E[\lfloor u+\theta\rfloor]=u $$
holds for every real $u$. Hence $\mathbb E[\widehat w_i]=w_i$.

To bound the value loss, define the prefix discrepancies
$$ D_i=\sum_{j=1}^i(\widehat w_j-w_j) =\lfloor S_i+\theta\rfloor-S_i. $$
They satisfy $|D_i|<1$ and $D_d=0$. Summation by parts gives
$$ \sum_{i=1}^d s_i(\widehat w_i-w_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(s_i-s_{i+1})D_i \geq -\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(s_{i+1}-s_i) \geq-C. $$
The dummy coordinate contributes zero.

Finally, the rounded vector changes only at the internal breakpoints
$$ \theta=1-\{S_i\}, $$
where $\{S_i\}$ is nonzero. There are at most $d-1$ such breakpoints. Assign each resulting interval its rational length as probability and use its midpoint to compute the corresponding vector. This gives at most $d=m+1$ supported vectors.

All calculations use rational arithmetic, sorting, and integer floors on polynomial-bit numbers. No enumeration up to $N$ is required. $\square$

3. A polynomial-size relaxation of the benchmark threshold

Fix rational numbers
$$ a>0,\qquad 0<\delta<\frac12. $$
We construct a rational linear feasibility system with the following two properties:

  • If $M\geq a$, the system is feasible.
  • From any rational feasible solution, we can construct a rational distribution with mean $b$, supported on bundles of value at least $(1-2\delta)a$.

Its size is polynomial in $m$, the encoded data, and $1/\delta$.

3.1. A bounded core of large goods

Classify good $g$ as large if $v_g>\delta a$, and as small otherwise. Define
$$ h=\left\lceil\frac1\delta\right\rceil+1,\qquad \Delta=\frac{\delta a}{h},\qquad R=\left\lfloor\frac a\Delta\right\rfloor =\left\lfloor\frac h\delta\right\rfloor. $$
For each large good, define the integer
$$ \ell_g=\left\lfloor\frac{\min\{v_g,a\}}{\Delta}\right\rfloor. $$

For any bundle $c$ consisting of at most $h$ large goods, put
$$ r(c)=\min\left\{R,\sum_g\ell_gc_g\right\}. $$
Then
$$ \min\{v(c),a\}-\delta a \leq r(c)\Delta \leq v(c). $$
Indeed, if $k=\sum_gc_g\leq h$, then
$$ \sum_g\ell_gc_g\Delta \geq \sum_g\min\{v_g,a\}c_g-k\Delta \geq \min\{v(c),a\}-\delta a. $$
The second inequality holds because either some selected copy has value at least $a$, or all selected values are unchanged by truncation at $a$. Also,
$$ R\Delta\geq a-\Delta\geq a-\delta a. $$
Taking the minimum with $R\Delta$ preserves the claimed lower bound. The upper bound follows from $\ell_g\Delta\leq v_g$.

Every bundle $z\in H(a)$ has a large-good subbundle $c$ of size at most $h$ with
$$ r(c)\Delta+ \sum_{g\text{ small}}v_g(z_g-c_g) \geq(1-\delta)a. $$
To prove this, consider two cases.

  • If the total value of its large goods is less than $a$, take all of them as $c$. Their number is less than $1/\delta$, because each has value greater than $\delta a$. The remainder contains only small goods, and the preceding rounding bound gives the displayed inequality.
  • If their total value is at least $a$, take copies successively until their accumulated value first reaches $a$. Before the last copy the value is less than $a$, so the selected number is less than $1/\delta+1$, and hence at most $h$. Now $r(c)\Delta\geq(1-\delta)a$, which suffices.

Thus a bounded core captures the large goods needed for the threshold; any remaining large goods can be treated as extras.

3.2. The linear system

Construct a directed acyclic graph with nodes
$$ (k,r),\qquad 0\leq k\leq\min\{K,h\},\quad 0\leq r\leq R. $$
Its source is $(0,0)$. For each large good $g$, whenever the next level exists, include an arc labelled $g$:
$$ (k,r)\longrightarrow \bigl(k+1,\min\{R,r+\ell_g\}\bigr). $$
A path ending at $(k,r)$ represents an integer large-good core of size $k$ and rounded value $r\Delta$.

Let $f_e\geq0$ be the flow on each graph arc, and let $\lambda_q\geq0$ be the flow terminating at node $q$. Impose unit-flow conservation:
$$ \sum_{e:\operatorname{tail}(e)=q}f_e+\lambda_q -\sum_{e:\operatorname{head}(e)=q}f_e = \begin{cases} 1,&q=(0,0),\\ 0,&q\neq(0,0). \end{cases} $$
Summing these equations gives $\sum_q\lambda_q=1$.

Define
$$ s_g= \begin{cases} v_g,&g\text{ small},\\ 0,&g\text{ large}. \end{cases} $$
For every node $q=(k,r)$, introduce nonnegative variables $W_{qg}$ representing the mass-weighted fractional residual bundle. Impose
$$ \sum_gW_{qg}\leq(K-k)\lambda_q $$
and
$$ \sum_gs_gW_{qg}+r\Delta\lambda_q \geq(1-\delta)a\lambda_q. $$
Finally, impose the mean equations
$$ \sum_{e:\operatorname{label}(e)=g}f_e+\sum_qW_{qg}=b_g \qquad\text{for every }g. $$

There are
$$ O\bigl((h+1)(R+1)\bigr)=O(\delta^{-3}) $$
nodes, at most $m$ times as many graph arcs, and $m$ residual variables per node. The binary-encoded $K$ appears only in coefficients and in the truncated level bound $\min\{K,h\}$.

3.3. Every benchmark-feasible threshold is feasible in the system

Suppose $M\geq a$, and take a distribution on $H(a)$ with mean $b$.

For each supported bundle $z$, choose the core $c$ established in Section 3.1. Route its probability along a path encoding that core, terminate at its node $(k,r)$, and contribute its probability times $z-c$ to the residual variables there.

The residual has size at most $K-k$, and the core inequality proves the residual-value constraint. Aggregating over the distribution gives the unit-flow and mean equations. Thus the linear system is feasible.

3.4. Every feasible solution yields an approximately qualifying distribution

Take a rational feasible solution. Decompose its unit flow into source-to-terminal paths. This is constructive: follow positive-flow arcs to a positive termination, subtract the smallest flow on that path including its termination, and repeat. Each iteration eliminates at least one positive arc or termination variable, so the number of paths is polynomial.

Fix a terminal node $q=(k,r)$ with $\lambda_q>0$. Define
$$ w^q_g=\frac{W_{qg}}{\lambda_q}. $$
The residual constraints imply
$$ \sum_gw^q_g\leq K-k,\qquad r\Delta+\sum_gs_gw^q_g\geq(1-\delta)a. $$
Apply the rounding lemma with
$$ N=K-k,\qquad C=\delta a. $$
Its hypotheses hold because $0\leq s_g\leq\delta a$. It produces a rational distribution of integer residual bundles $\widehat w$ satisfying
$$ \mathbb E[\widehat w]=w^q,\qquad \sum_gs_g\widehat w_g \geq\sum_gs_gw^q_g-\delta a. $$

For each flow path terminating at $q$, let $c$ be its core and append a residual drawn from this distribution. Every resulting bundle belongs to $\mathcal B_K$, and
$$ \begin{aligned} v(c+\widehat w) &\geq r\Delta+\sum_gs_g\widehat w_g\\ &\geq r\Delta+\sum_gs_gw^q_g-\delta a\\ &\geq(1-2\delta)a. \end{aligned} $$
Here $v(c)\geq r\Delta$, and $v(\widehat w)\geq\sum_gs_g\widehat w_g$ by nonnegativity.

The expected core counts are the labelled arc flows. The expected residual contribution from node $q$ is
$$ \lambda_qw^q=W_q. $$
Consequently, the mean equations give expected bundle exactly $b$.

Each path uses at most $m+1$ residual bundles. The resulting distribution therefore has polynomial support, rational probabilities, and polynomial-bit data.

We have proved the promised implications:
$$ M\geq a \Longrightarrow \text{the linear system is feasible} \Longrightarrow M\geq(1-2\delta)a, $$
with an explicit distribution witnessing the second implication.

4. Finding a sufficient threshold without computing the benchmark

Set
$$ \delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{4}, \qquad \eta=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. $$
Then $1-2\delta=1-\eta>0$.

If $V=0$, we have $M=0$, and the elementary distribution from Section 1 suffices.

Otherwise, define the smallest positive single-good value
$$ a_{\min}=\min_{g:v_g>0}v_g. $$
This set is nonempty because $V>0$. Since $M$ is a bundle value, nonnegativity and integer bundle coordinates imply
$$ M>0\Longrightarrow M\geq a_{\min}. $$

First test feasibility of the Section 3 system at $a=a_{\min}$.

  • If it is infeasible, the first implication proves $M<a_{\min}$, hence $M=0$. Output the elementary mean-$b$ distribution.
  • If it is feasible, the second implication proves $M>0$. Hence

$$ a_{\min}\leq M\leq V. $$

In the latter case, initialize
$$ l=a_{\min},\qquad u=V, $$
retaining a feasible solution at $l$. Repeatedly test the midpoint $a=(l+u)/2$:

  • If feasible, replace $l$ by $a$ and retain its feasible solution.
  • If infeasible, replace $u$ by $a$.

The maintained properties are that $l$ is feasible and $u\geq M$. The latter is preserved because an infeasible threshold necessarily exceeds $M$. This reasoning requires no monotonicity assumption about the threshold-dependent relaxation.

Stop once
$$ u-l\leq\eta a_{\min}. $$
Then
$$ l\geq M-\eta a_{\min}\geq(1-\eta)M. $$
Extract the distribution from the retained feasible solution at $l$. Every supported bundle has value at least
$$ (1-2\delta)l \geq (1-\eta)^2M = \left(1-\varepsilon+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\right)M \geq(1-\varepsilon)M. $$

The number of midpoint tests is
$$ O\left(1+\log\frac{V}{a_{\min}}+\log\frac1\varepsilon\right). $$
Since $V\leq K\max_gv_g$, the logarithm of $V/a_{\min}$ is polynomially bounded by the numerical input bit length. The thresholds generated by these tests likewise have polynomial bit length.

5. Exact computation and encoding bounds

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility of a finite system of rational linear equalities and inequalities can be decided in time polynomial in its encoded length, and a polynomial-bit rational feasible point can be returned when one exists. This includes lower-dimensional feasible sets. See [Khachiyan’s polynomial-time linear-programming result](https://doi.org/10.1016/0041-5553(80)90061-0).

Its hypotheses apply to the system in Section 3: every coefficient is explicitly computed and rational. Moreover,
$$ h=O(1/\varepsilon),\qquad R=O(1/\varepsilon^2), $$
so the number of variables and constraints is polynomial in $m$ and $1/\varepsilon$. Coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in the original input length and the threshold bit length. In particular, dependence on $K$ is through $\log(K+1)$, rather than through enumeration of copies.

Flow decomposition uses polynomially many rational subtractions. Residual normalization, the rounding lemma, and multiplication of path and residual probabilities also use polynomially many operations on polynomial-bit rationals. For example, the product of the denominators of all coordinates of a feasible solution supplies a common denominator of polynomial bit length for the flow-decomposition calculations.

Thus the entire single-valuation algorithm, including its explicit output, runs in time polynomial in $m$, the numerical input length, and $1/\varepsilon$.

6. Assembly and verification of all requested guarantees

Run the algorithm for each of the $\tau$ listed types. It produces rational distributions $y^t$ with
$$ \sum_z y^t_z=1,\qquad \sum_z y^t_zz=b, $$
and
$$ y^t_z>0\Longrightarrow v_t(z)\geq(1-\varepsilon)M_t. $$

Construct one outcome, with lottery probability $1$, by setting
$$ x_{t,z}=\mu_ty^t_z. $$
These are rational masses with polynomial-size explicit support. Their type sums are
$$ \sum_zx_{t,z}=\mu_t, $$
and their supplies are exactly
$$ \sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_g = \sum_t\mu_tb_g =b_g. $$

For every type, additivity and the mean equation give exact conditional proportionality:
$$ \mu_t^{-1}\sum_zx_{t,z}v_t(z) = \sum_z y^t_zv_t(z) = v_t(b). $$

Every positive assignment satisfies
$$ v_t(z)\geq(1-\varepsilon)M_t \geq(9/10-\varepsilon)M_t. $$
There are no positive assignments with value strictly below $(1-\varepsilon)M_t$. Therefore the conditional EFX requirement is satisfied exactly, including its stipulated treatment of zero-valued goods.

The algorithm iterates only over the $\tau$ supported types and runs in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)$ time. Every instance satisfying the stated input assumptions admits the constructed outcome.
