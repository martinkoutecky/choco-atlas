The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given item types \(j\in[m]\) with rational per-capita capacities \(q_j\), buyer types \(\theta\in\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) satisfying \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\), multi-unit valuations \(v_\theta\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^m\), and a finite-support benchmark allocation \(Y\) with masses \(y_{\theta,b}\), compute a finite package catalogue \(\mathcal P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\), package masses \(z_P\), nonnegative linear package prices \(p_P\), and an allocation \(x_{\theta,r}\) of type mass to integral package bundles \(r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{\mathcal P}\). Require \(\sum_{P\in\mathcal P}z_P P_j\le q_j\), \(\sum_r x_{\theta,r}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,r}x_{\theta,r}r_P\le z_P\), and, whenever \(x_{\theta,r}>0\), require \(r\) to maximize \(v_\theta(\sum_P r_P P)-\sum_P r_Pp_P\). The objective is to obtain \(\mathrm{SW}(x)\ge\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{SW}(Y)\), with complexity polynomial in \(m\), \(|\Theta|\), encoding length, benchmark support, and demand queries, independent of the raw replication factor \(N\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|\Theta|\) buyer types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) item types with capacities \(q\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\). Valuations \(v_\theta:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) are coordinatewise nondecreasing and satisfy \(v_\theta(0)=0\); supplied binary-integer bounds \(d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}^m\) satisfy \(v_\theta(b)=v_\theta(\min\{b,d\})\), with the minimum taken coordinatewise.
The benchmark is a finite list of nonnegative rational masses \(y_{\theta,b}\), satisfying \(\sum_b y_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,b}y_{\theta,b}b_j\le q_j\); let \(s\) be its support size and \(\mathrm{SW}(Y)=\sum_{\theta,b}y_{\theta,b}v_\theta(b)\).
For any finite catalogue \(\mathcal P\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\setminus\{0\}\) and nonnegative rational prices, a demand oracle returns an integral maximizer and its value for \(v_\theta(\sum_P r_PP)-\sum_P r_Pp_P\) over all \(r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathcal P}\); value queries are also available. Let \(L\) include the binary input length and a uniform bound on valuation-answer bit length.
An outcome consists of a finite catalogue, positive rational package masses \(z_P\), nonnegative rational prices \(p_P\), and finitely supported nonnegative rational allocation masses \(x_{\theta,r}\), with \(\sum_Pz_PP_j\le q_j\), \(\sum_rx_{\theta,r}=\mu_\theta\), and \(\sum_{\theta,r}x_{\theta,r}r_P\le z_P\).
Whenever \(x_{\theta,r}>0\), its package bundle must maximize the oracle objective, including comparison with \(r=0\). Any maximizing bundle is acceptable, and type mass may split among tied maximizers; demand is not rationed by package masses. Unpackaged inventory and unsold packages are permitted.
Define \(\mathrm{SW}(x)=\sum_{\theta,r}x_{\theta,r}v_\theta(\sum_Pr_PP)\).

Can an outcome with \(\mathrm{SW}(x)\ge\tfrac12\mathrm{SW}(Y)\) always be computed using polynomial time and polynomially many oracle queries in \(m,\tau,L,s\), independently of any replication factor \(N\), or is obtaining this guarantee NP-hard even with the stipulated oracle access?

For finite buyers and indivisible objects, a polynomial-time algorithm with demand queries over reduced bundle markets converts any supplied allocation into a combinatorial Walrasian equilibrium retaining at least half its welfare. [Feldman–Gravin–Lucier](https://cs.tau.ac.il/~mfeldman/papers/FGLj16.pdf)
For continuum consumers with finite integral consumption domains, equilibrium existence and welfare efficiency are known; these results do not establish the succinct oracle complexity asked here. [Azevedo–Weyl–White](https://eduardomazevedo.github.io/papers/Azevedo-et-al-Walrasian.pdf)

  • Restored nonnegative, coordinatewise monotone, normalized valuations from the discrete model and specified nonnegative capacities.
  • The unbounded integral consumption domain lacked a demand-attainment condition. Added supplied binary-encoded saturation bounds, a natural finite-consumption analogue, while retaining maximization over all integral package bundles.
  • Required the benchmark to be feasible, with nonnegative rational masses and complete accounting of each type's mass, including empty bundles.
  • Specified value queries and demand queries over arbitrary finite package catalogues, returning exact maximizers and their values; included a valuation-answer bit bound in L.
  • Specified finite rational output, excluded empty packages and zero-mass catalogue entries, and allowed allocation among any tied utility maximizers.

A polynomial-time construction

The guarantee is polynomial-time computable with polynomially many stipulated oracle queries, independently of \(N\). Singleton packages suffice.

The construction uses a positive reserve on each singleton price. This keeps every queried demand bundle within the supplied bounds and gives an exact equilibrium whose welfare is at least half the benchmark.

1. Truncate the benchmark

Let

\(\displaystyle \mathcal B=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\{0,\ldots,d_j\},\qquad \ell(b)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_j,\qquad D=\sum_{j=1}^{m}d_j.\)

Replace every benchmark bundle \(b\) by its coordinatewise truncation \(\min\{b,d\}\), combining repeated entries. By the saturation hypothesis, this preserves welfare. It weakly decreases consumption, so feasibility is preserved. Write the resulting benchmark as \(\bar Y\), and let

\(\displaystyle W=\mathrm{SW}(\bar Y)=\mathrm{SW}(Y).\)

Compute \(W\) using at most \(s\) value queries. All arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity: it involves at most \(s\) rational masses and valuation answers of the stipulated bounded encoding length.

If \(W=0\), use the empty catalogue and allocate the empty bundle to every type. This is feasible, satisfies demand, and meets the guarantee. Henceforth assume \(W>0\), and define the positive rational reserve

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\frac{W}{2D}.\)

Its encoding length is polynomial in the stated parameters.

2. The configuration LP

For every type \(\theta\) and bundle \(b\in\mathcal B\), introduce a nonnegative allocation mass \(a_{\theta,b}\). Consider the finite LP

\(\displaystyle \max_a\ \sum_{\theta,b}a_{\theta,b}\bigl(v_\theta(b)-\varepsilon\ell(b)\bigr)\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{b\in\mathcal B}a_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\quad\text{for every }\theta,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta,b}a_{\theta,b}b_j\le q_j\quad\text{for every }j,\qquad a_{\theta,b}\ge0.\)

Although this LP may have exponentially many variables, its allocations are directly admissible mass allocations: no integral rounding is needed.

The feasible region is nonempty because allocating only \(0\) is feasible. It is compact because each variable lies between \(0\) and its type mass. Consequently, the optimum is finite and attained.

Its dual is

\(\displaystyle \min_{u,p}\ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta u_\theta+\sum_jq_jp_j\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle u_\theta+p\cdot b\ge v_\theta(b)-\varepsilon\ell(b)\quad\text{for every }\theta,\ b\in\mathcal B,\qquad p_j\ge0.\)

The variables \(u_\theta\) are unrestricted, although the constraints for \(b=0\) imply \(u_\theta\ge0\). Dual feasibility follows, for example, by taking \(p=0\) and \(u_\theta=v_\theta(d)\): monotonicity gives \(v_\theta(b)\le v_\theta(d)\).

3. Exact separation using bounded demand answers

We implement a strong separation oracle for this dual.

For a rational candidate \((u,p)\), first check \(p_j\ge0\). A negative coordinate supplies an explicit violated inequality. Otherwise, query each type’s demand oracle with catalogue

\(\displaystyle \{e_1,\ldots,e_m\}\)

and strictly positive prices

\(\displaystyle \pi_j=p_j+\varepsilon.\)

Here \(e_j\) denotes the \(j\)-th unit vector.

Every maximizer returned by this query belongs to \(\mathcal B\). Indeed, if an integral bundle \(r\) has some coordinate exceeding \(d_j\), truncating it to \(\hat r=\min\{r,d\}\) preserves its value and strictly decreases its payment. Thus \(r\) cannot maximize utility.

In particular, arbitrary oracle tie-breaking cannot produce an oversized answer: every coordinate has encoding length bounded by that of \(d_j\).

For each type, the returned bundle \(b^\theta\) therefore satisfies

\(\displaystyle b^\theta\in\arg\max_{b\in\mathcal B}\bigl\{v_\theta(b)-(p+\varepsilon\mathbf1)\cdot b\bigr\}.\)

The type’s dual constraints are equivalent to

\(\displaystyle u_\theta\ge v_\theta(b)-(p+\varepsilon\mathbf1)\cdot b\quad\text{for every }b\in\mathcal B.\)

Hence, if

\(\displaystyle u_\theta<v_\theta(b^\theta)-(p+\varepsilon\mathbf1)\cdot b^\theta,\)

we return the corresponding violated defining inequality. If no type violates this comparison, all dual constraints hold.

Each separation call uses at most \(\tau\) demand queries and, if needed, \(\tau\) value queries. Its remaining arithmetic is polynomial in the candidate’s encoding length and the stated input parameters.

4. Exact optimization and recovery of allocation masses

We use the following standard rational oracle-LP result: for an LP with polynomially many variables, bounded rational coefficient encoding lengths, and a strong separation oracle returning violated defining inequalities, exact optimization and recovery of an optimal dual certificate supported on polynomially many defining inequalities are polynomial-time operations. The bounds depend on dimension and encoding lengths, rather than the number of implicit inequalities. This includes recovery of the opposite LP’s solution; see the discussion of rational polyhedra and optimal dual certificates in [Dadush–Végh–Zambelli, referring to Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Lemma 6.5.15](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35145/35145.pdf).

All hypotheses hold here:

  • The implicit dual has \(m+\tau\) variables.
  • Every defining inequality has polynomial encoding length: \(b_j\le d_j\), valuation answers have the stipulated bound, and \(\varepsilon\) has polynomial encoding length.
  • Section 3 gives exact separation returning a defining inequality.
  • Both LPs are feasible, and their common finite optimum is attained.

Applied to the displayed dual, the theorem returns optimal rational prices and utilities \((p^*,u^*)\), together with a finitely supported optimal primal allocation \(a^*\).

For completeness, the certificate’s multipliers have exactly the required interpretation. A multiplier for the constraint indexed by \((\theta,b)\) is \(a^*_{\theta,b}\); multipliers for \(p_j\ge0\) give nonnegative inventory slacks \(t_j\). The certificate equations are

\(\displaystyle \sum_ba^*_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_{\theta,b}a^*_{\theta,b}b_j+t_j=q_j.\)

Thus primal recovery gives feasible allocation masses, rather than only an optimal objective value.

The certificate uses polynomially many configurations. Solving the primal restricted to those configurations and choosing a basic optimal solution gives at most \(m+\tau\) positive allocation entries: after adding the \(m\) inventory slacks, there are \(m+\tau\) equality constraints. All output numbers have polynomial encoding length.

5. Construct the outcome and verify demand

Let

\(\displaystyle J=\{j:q_j>0\},\qquad \mathcal P=\{e_j:j\in J\}.\)

For each package \(e_j\), set

\(\displaystyle z_{e_j}=q_j,\qquad p_{e_j}=p_j^*+\varepsilon.\)

These package masses and prices are strictly positive, and packaging uses exactly the available inventory.

If \(q_j=0\), primal feasibility and nonnegativity imply \(b_j=0\) whenever \(a^*_{\theta,b}>0\). Therefore every positively allocated configuration can be represented using \(\mathcal P\). Assign mass \(a^*_{\theta,b}\) to the package bundle with multiplicities \(r_{e_j}=b_j\). This defines \(x\), preserves all type masses, and satisfies package-supply constraints.

It remains to check unrestricted demand, including the empty bundle.

Complementary slackness implies that whenever \(a^*_{\theta,b}>0\),

\(\displaystyle u_\theta^*=v_\theta(b)-(p^*+\varepsilon\mathbf1)\cdot b.\)

Now take any package bundle available in the output catalogue, and let \(t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\) be its item vector, with zero coordinates outside \(J\). Set \(\hat t=\min\{t,d\}\). Saturation and nonnegative prices give

\(\displaystyle v_\theta(t)-(p^*+\varepsilon\mathbf1)\cdot t\le v_\theta(\hat t)-(p^*+\varepsilon\mathbf1)\cdot\hat t\le u_\theta^*.\)

The second inequality is dual feasibility. Every assigned bundle attains this upper bound by complementary slackness. Consequently, every positively assigned bundle maximizes utility over all integral package bundles, regardless of available package masses.

Since \(t=0\) is included in this comparison, demand also includes comparison with buying nothing. No rationing or favorable oracle tie-breaking is required.

6. Welfare guarantee and complexity

The truncated benchmark is feasible for the primal LP. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta,b}\bar y_{\theta,b}\ell(b)\le D\sum_{\theta,b}\bar y_{\theta,b}=D,\)

because every bundle lies in \(\mathcal B\) and total population mass is \(1\).

Optimality of \(a^*\) therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta,b}a^*_{\theta,b}\bigl(v_\theta(b)-\varepsilon\ell(b)\bigr)\ge W-\varepsilon D=\frac{W}{2}.\)

The reserve deduction is nonnegative, so the constructed outcome satisfies

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{SW}(x)=\sum_{\theta,b}a^*_{\theta,b}v_\theta(b)\ge\frac{W}{2}=\frac12\mathrm{SW}(Y).\)

The algorithm uses polynomial time and polynomially many oracle queries in \(m,\tau,L,s\). Its catalogue has at most \(m\) entries, and its allocation support can have at most \(m+\tau\) entries. All consumption quantities are encoded in binary; no replicated population or enumeration of \(\mathcal B\) is constructed.

The same proof, with \(\varepsilon=\eta W/D\), yields welfare at least \((1-\eta)W\) for any supplied rational \(0<\eta<1\), with additional dependence only on its encoding length.
