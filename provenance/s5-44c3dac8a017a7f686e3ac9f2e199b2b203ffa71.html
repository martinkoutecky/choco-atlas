The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational arrival shares \(\alpha_t\) summing to \(1\), sojourn durations \(\ell_t \ge \epsilon\), degree parameter \(d \ge 2\), and tolerance \(\delta > 0\), consider arrivals at rate \(m\), with type \(t\) arriving at rate \(m\alpha_t\), and independent pairwise compatibility probability \(d/m\). In the continuous type-and-age mass model for GDY with uniform partner choice, compute the long-run fraction of arrival mass that expires unmatched to additive error at most \(\delta\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\alpha_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a deterministic, finite binary-rational sojourn duration \(\ell_t\geq\epsilon>0\). Inputs also include binary rationals \(m>d\geq2\) and \(0<\delta<1\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.
GDY matches an arrival immediately with a uniformly chosen compatible pool member, removing both; otherwise the arrival waits until matched by a later arrival or expires at age \(\ell_t\). Compatibility is symmetric, persistent, independent across pairs, and type independent, with probability \(d/m\).
Define its continuous mass analogue by the sparse-market Poisson closure: at pool mass \(N\), unmatched arrivals constitute fraction \(q(N)=\exp(-dN/m)\), and matching arrivals remove pool mass uniformly across types and ages.
Precisely, let \(u_t(s,a)\geq0\) be the pool mass density at time \(s\) and age \(0\leq a<\ell_t\), initially zero, and put \(N(s)=\sum_{t\in T}\int_0^{\ell_t}u_t(s,a)\,da\). The evolution is \((\partial_s+\partial_a)u_t=-h(N(s))u_t\), with \(u_t(s,0)=m\alpha_tq(N(s))\), where \(h(N)=m(1-q(N))/N\) for \(N>0\) and \(h(0)=d\); mass reaching age \(\ell_t\) expires.
Define the long-run loss as \(\Lambda=\limsup_{H\to\infty}(mH)^{-1}\int_0^H\sum_{t\in T}u_t(s,\ell_t^-)\,ds\).

Can a rational approximation to \(\Lambda\) with additive error at most \(\delta\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), including the binary encoding of all durations, \(d\), \(\epsilon\), and \(\delta\), or is this approximation problem NP-hard?

For the discrete stochastic market, GDY has limiting loss at most \(\exp(-\epsilon d/(2\ln2))\) when every sojourn is at least \(\epsilon\); this is a performance bound, not a polynomial-time loss-computation theorem. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.10287v3).
Fluid invariant-state results exist for a different bipartite demand-and-supply model with general patience and prescribed matching rates. They do not settle the computational complexity of the mass evolution defined here. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.02710).

Result

The approximation problem for the specified mass evolution is polynomial-time computable. In fact, its running time can be polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), without numerical dependence on \(m\).

The essential steps are to prove convergence from the empty initial condition, identify the limiting loss by a scalar equation, and evaluate that equation with certified polynomial-bit computations.

1. Characteristics, existence, and a uniform pool bound

Write

\[ b(N)=m e^{-dN/m}, \qquad \phi(x)=\int_0^1 e^{-xr}\,dr. \]

Then \(h(N)=d\phi(dN/m)\), including at \(N=0\). In particular,

\[ 0<h(N)\leq d,\qquad |b'(N)|\leq d,\qquad |h'(N)|\leq \frac{d^2}{2m} \]

for \(N\geq0\). Also, \(b\) and \(h\) are strictly decreasing, and

\[ h(N)N=m-b(N). \]

The characteristic formula, for \(0\leq a<\min\{s,\ell_t\}\), is

\[ u_t(s,a) = m\alpha_t e^{-dN(s-a)/m} \exp\!\left(-\int_{s-a}^{s}h(N(r))\,dr\right). \]

For \(s<a<\ell_t\), the density is zero. Values on the characteristic \(a=s\) do not affect any integrals.

Consequently, \(N\) satisfies the nonlinear Volterra equation

\[ N(s)= m\sum_t\alpha_t \int_0^{\min\{s,\ell_t\}} e^{-dN(s-a)/m} \exp\!\left(-\int_{s-a}^{s}h(N(r))\,dr\right)\,da. \]

Here is a direct well-posedness justification. Put \(\bar\ell=\sum_t\alpha_t\ell_t\). The right-hand side maps nonnegative continuous candidate paths into paths bounded by \(m\bar\ell\). Let \(K_q=d/m\) and \(K_h=d^2/(2m)\), the respective Lipschitz constants for \(q\) and \(h\). On an extension interval of length \(r_0\), with the past fixed, its Lipschitz constant in the supremum norm is at most

\[ m\bar\ell K_h r_0+mK_qr_0+mK_hr_0^2. \]

Indeed, older cohorts differ only through their survival factors, while new cohorts can differ through both their boundary inflow and survival factors. This bound is less than one for sufficiently small \(r_0>0\), uniformly in the starting time. Successive contraction arguments therefore give a unique global nonnegative solution.

Let \(E(s)=\sum_tu_t(s,\ell_t^-)\) denote the expiration rate. The characteristic formula gives \(0\leq E(s)\leq m\). Integrating the transport equation yields, almost everywhere,

\[ N'(s)=b(N(s))-h(N(s))N(s)-E(s) =m\bigl(2e^{-dN(s)/m}-1\bigr)-E(s). \]

Thus \(N\) is Lipschitz, with \(|N'(s)|\leq2m\).

Define \(B=m\ln2/d\). Whenever \(N>B\), the displayed derivative is nonpositive. Applying the absolutely continuous positive-part identity to \((N-B)_+\), and using \(N(0)=0\), proves

\[ 0\leq N(s)\leq B. \]

It follows that throughout the evolution,

\[ h(N(s))\geq h(B)=\frac{d}{2\ln2}=:c>0. \]

2. The unique stationary state

For \(x\geq0\), define

\[ S(x)=\sum_t\alpha_t e^{-d\ell_t\phi(x)}, \qquad G(x)=e^x-2+S(x). \]

Since

\[ \phi'(x)=-\int_0^1 r e^{-xr}\,dr<0, \]

we have

\[ G'(x) = e^x+ \sum_t\alpha_t d\ell_t e^{-d\ell_t\phi(x)} \int_0^1r e^{-xr}\,dr \geq1. \]

All durations are positive and finite. Therefore

\[ G(0)=-1+\sum_t\alpha_t e^{-d\ell_t}<0, \qquad G(\ln2)=S(\ln2)>0. \]

There is exactly one root \(x_*\in(0,\ln2)\).

Set

\[ N_*=\frac{mx_*}{d}, \qquad q_*=e^{-x_*}, \qquad h_*=d\phi(x_*). \]

The stationary profiles are

\[ u_t^*(a)=m\alpha_tq_*e^{-h_*a}, \qquad 0\leq a<\ell_t. \]

To verify their total mass, the root equation gives \(S(x_*)=2-e^{x_*}\). Hence

\[ h_*\sum_t\int_0^{\ell_t}u_t^*(a)\,da = mq_*\bigl(1-S(x_*)\bigr) = m(1-q_*) = h_*N_*. \]

Since \(h_*>0\), their total mass is exactly \(N_*\). They therefore satisfy the stationary transport equation and its boundary conditions. Conversely, any stationary state must have these exponential profiles and must satisfy \(G(x)=0\), so this stationary state is unique.

Uniqueness alone does not identify the empty-start loss. Convergence is proved next.

3. Convergence from the empty initial condition

Define

\[ w_t(s,a)=u_t(s,a)-u_t^*(a), \qquad V(s)=\sum_t\int_0^{\ell_t}|w_t(s,a)|\,da, \qquad \Delta(s)=N(s)-N_*. \]

The triangle inequality gives \(|\Delta(s)|\leq V(s)\).

A contraction inequality

For a scalar transport equation

\[ (\partial_s+\partial_a)w=-Hw+f, \]

the absolute-value inequality along characteristics is

\[ (\partial_s+\partial_a)|w|\leq-H|w|+|f|. \]

Integrating over the age intervals adds absolute boundary inflow and subtracts absolute terminal outflow. This remains valid for the initial characteristic discontinuity: it travels along a characteristic and contributes no interior source. Thus the resulting norm is locally absolutely continuous.

At a fixed time, put

\[ R=\max\{N(s),N_*\}, \qquad r=\min\{N(s),N_*\}. \]

If \(N(s)\geq N_*\), subtraction of the equations gives

\[ (\partial_s+\partial_a)w_t = -h(R)w_t+\bigl(h(r)-h(R)\bigr)u_t^*. \]

If \(N(s)\leq N_*\), it gives

\[ (\partial_s+\partial_a)w_t = -h(R)w_t-\bigl(h(r)-h(R)\bigr)u_t. \]

The density in the source term has total mass \(r\) in either case. Consequently, writing \(A(s)=\sum_t|w_t(s,\ell_t^-)|\), we obtain

\[ V'(s)\leq b(r)-b(R)-h(R)V(s) +\bigl(h(r)-h(R)\bigr)r-A(s). \]

The identity \(h(z)z=m-b(z)\) implies

\[ b(r)-b(R)+\bigl(h(r)-h(R)\bigr)r =h(R)(R-r). \]

Therefore

\[ V'(s) \leq -h(R)\bigl(V(s)-|\Delta(s)|\bigr)-A(s) \leq -c\bigl(V(s)-|\Delta(s)|\bigr). \]

In particular, \(V\) is nonincreasing. Since \(V(0)=N_*\), it has a finite limit \(V_\infty\), and

\[ \int_0^\infty \bigl(V(s)-|\Delta(s)|\bigr)\,ds \leq \frac{N_*}{c}. \]

The difference cannot retain a positive limiting norm

Put \(g(s)=V(s)-|\Delta(s)|\geq0\). We first show \(g(s)\to0\).

Otherwise, some \(\gamma>0\) and arbitrarily large times \(s_j\) satisfy \(g(s_j)\geq\gamma\). For sufficiently large \(s_j\), we have \(V(s_j)-V_\infty\leq\gamma/4\). Since \(|\Delta|\) is \(2m\)-Lipschitz, throughout

\[ s_j\leq s\leq s_j+\frac{\gamma}{8m} \]

we then have

\[ g(s)\geq g(s_j)-\bigl(V(s_j)-V_\infty\bigr)-\frac{\gamma}{4} \geq\frac{\gamma}{2}. \]

Choosing disjoint such intervals contradicts the integrability of \(g\). Hence \(g(s)\to0\).

Suppose now that \(V_\infty>0\). Then \(|\Delta(s)|\to V_\infty\). By continuity, \(\Delta\) eventually has a fixed sign and satisfies \(|\Delta(s)|\geq\eta\), where \(\eta=V_\infty/2>0\).

There are two cases.

Case 1: \(N(s)\geq N_*+\eta\) eventually. Define \(q_+=e^{-d(N_*+\eta)/m}<q_*\). Choose \(a_0\in(0,\epsilon)\) so small that

\[ q_*e^{-da_0}\geq\frac{q_*+q_+}{2}. \]

For sufficiently large \(s\) and every \(0<a<a_0\), the characteristic formula and \(h_*\leq d\) give

\[ u_t(s,a)\leq m\alpha_tq_+, \qquad u_t^*(a)\geq m\alpha_tq_*e^{-da_0}. \]

Thus \(w_t(s,a)\leq-m\alpha_t(q_*-q_+)/2\). Since \(\Delta(s)>0\),

\[ g(s)=2\sum_t\int_0^{\ell_t}(w_t(s,a))_-\,da \geq ma_0(q_*-q_+)>0, \]

contradicting \(g(s)\to0\).

Case 2: \(N(s)\leq N_*-\eta\) eventually. Here \(\eta\leq N_*\), since \(V_\infty\leq V(0)=N_*\). Define \(q_-=e^{-d(N_*-\eta)/m}>q_*\). Choose \(a_0\in(0,\epsilon)\) so small that

\[ q_-e^{-da_0}\geq\frac{q_-+q_*}{2}. \]

For sufficiently large \(s\) and \(0<a<a_0\), the characteristic formula and \(h(N)\leq d\) give

\[ u_t(s,a)\geq m\alpha_tq_-e^{-da_0}, \qquad u_t^*(a)\leq m\alpha_tq_*. \]

Thus \(w_t(s,a)\geq m\alpha_t(q_--q_*)/2\). Since \(\Delta(s)<0\),

\[ g(s)=2\sum_t\int_0^{\ell_t}(w_t(s,a))_+\,da \geq ma_0(q_--q_*)>0, \]

again a contradiction.

Both cases are impossible. Therefore

\[ V(s)\longrightarrow0, \qquad N(s)\longrightarrow N_*. \]

4. Identification of the long-run loss

For \(s>\max_t\ell_t\), the expiration rate is

\[ \frac{E(s)}m = \sum_t\alpha_t e^{-dN(s-\ell_t)/m} \exp\!\left(-\int_{s-\ell_t}^{s}h(N(r))\,dr\right). \]

Each duration is fixed and finite. The convergence of \(N\), together with continuity of \(h\), therefore implies

\[ \frac{E(s)}m\longrightarrow q_*S(x_*). \]

Since \(E(s)/m\) is bounded, its Cesàro averages have the same limit. Thus the limsup in the question is an actual limit, and

\[ \boxed{\Lambda=q_*S(x_*)=2e^{-x_*}-1.} \]

In particular, \(0<\Lambda<1\). The parameter \(m\) cancels from this characterization.

5. Certified approximation of the scalar root

The root lies in \([0,1]\), since \(G(0)<0\) and \(G(1)>e-2>0\). Moreover, \(G'(x)\geq1\) on this interval, so

\[ |x-x_*|\leq |G(x)| \]

for every \(x\in[0,1]\).

Set \(\rho=\delta/16\). Maintain a rational interval containing \(x_*\), initially \([0,1]\). At its midpoint \(x\), compute a rational \(\widetilde G(x)\) satisfying

\[ |\widetilde G(x)-G(x)|\leq\rho. \]

If \(|\widetilde G(x)|\leq\rho\), stop: then \(|x-x_*|\leq2\rho=\delta/8\). Otherwise its sign is certified, so the appropriate half-interval still contains the root.

Also stop when the interval width is at most \(\delta/4\), choosing its midpoint. This again gives \(|x-x_*|\leq\delta/8\).

There are at most \(O(\log(1/\delta))=O(L)\) iterations. Finally, compute a rational approximation to \(2e^{-x}-1\) with error at most \(\delta/4\), and project it onto \([0,1]\). Because the derivative of \(2e^{-x}-1\) has absolute value at most \(2\) on \([0,1]\), the total error is at most

\[ 2\frac{\delta}{8}+\frac{\delta}{4} =\frac{\delta}{2}\leq\delta. \]

Projection cannot increase the distance to \(\Lambda\in[0,1]\).

6. Polynomial bit complexity, including large durations

It remains to justify the certified evaluations. This can be done directly by rational Taylor arithmetic.

Put

\[ D=\max\{1,\max_t d\ell_t\}, \qquad a=\frac{\rho}{4D}. \]

Both \(D\) and \(a\) have binary descriptions of length \(O(L)\), and \(\log(1/a)=O(L)\).

For rational \(x\in[0,1]\), use

\[ \phi(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^jx^j}{(j+1)!}. \]

The alternating-series remainder after index \(J\) is at most \(1/(J+2)!\). Thus \(O(L)\) terms produce a rational \(\widetilde\phi\) with error at most \(a\). This approximation is positive, since \(\phi(x)\geq e^{-1}>1/3\) and \(a<1/64\).

Set \(y_t=d\ell_t\widetilde\phi\). Then \(y_t\geq0\) and

\[ |y_t-d\ell_t\phi(x)|\leq\rho/4. \]

The function \(e^{-y}\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz on the nonnegative real axis, so this exponent perturbation changes each exponential by at most \(\rho/4\).

Choose an integer \(K=O(L)\) with \(2^{-K}\leq\rho/4\).

  • If \(y_t\geq K\), return zero for its exponential. Its error relative to \(e^{-y_t}\) is at most \(e^{-K}\leq2^{-K}\leq\rho/4\).
  • If \(0\leq y_t<K\), use the Taylor polynomial for \(e^{-y_t}\) through degree \(n-1\), with \(n\geq6K\). Its remainder is at most \(y_t^n/n!\). Using \(n!\geq(n/e)^n\) and \(e<3\), this is at most \(2^{-n}\leq\rho/4\).

Thus each computed term differs from \(e^{-d\ell_t\phi(x)}\) by at most \(\rho/2\). Because the positive weights sum to one, the error in the weighted sum \(S(x)\) is also at most \(\rho/2\).

The Taylor series for \(e^x\), with \(x\in[0,1]\), gives error at most \(\rho/2\) using \(O(L)\) terms. Combining the two evaluations gives the required error bound \(\rho\) for \(G(x)\).

All series have \(O(L)\) terms. Bisection midpoints have \(O(L)\)-bit encodings, and the exact rational powers, factorials, sums, and products have polynomial bit length. The weighted sum uses precisely the \(\tau\) support entries. Hence every root evaluation, every bisection step, and the final rational output take polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

This completes both the convergence proof and the approximation algorithm.
