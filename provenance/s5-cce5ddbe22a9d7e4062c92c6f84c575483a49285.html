The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), type rankings \(\succ_t\), acceptable ordered type pairs \(A\), rational per-capita limits \(\kappa_{tu}\), and rational weights \(\ell_{tu}\) satisfying \(u\succ_t u' \Rightarrow \ell_{tu}\ge \ell_{tu'}\), compute a maximum-weight Pareto-optimal mass circulation. For \(x_{tu}\), the mass of service received by type \(t\) from type \(u\), require \(0\le x_{tu}\le\mu_t\kappa_{tu}\) and \(\sum_u x_{tu}=\sum_s x_{st}\) for every \(t\), and maximize \(\sum_{(t,u)\in A}\ell_{tu}x_{tu}\) subject to Pareto optimality under the lexicographic rankings of the per-capita receipt vectors \((x_{tu}/\mu_t)_{u:\,(t,u)\in A}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type supplies its own service, so the number of service types is \(m=\tau\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Acceptable pairs form a simple loopless directed graph \(A\subseteq T\times T\). Each type \(t\) strictly ranks its acceptable suppliers by \(\succ_t\) and is indifferent about who receives its service. Each pair has positive rational per-capita capacity \(\kappa_{tu}\) and positive rational weight \(\ell_{tu}\), with \(u\succ_t u'\Rightarrow\ell_{tu}\geq\ell_{tu'}\); equal weights are allowed.
A feasible mass circulation has real entries \(x_{tu}\), zero outside \(A\), satisfying \(0\leq x_{tu}\leq\mu_t\kappa_{tu}\) and \(\sum_u x_{tu}=\sum_s x_{st}\) for every \(t\). There is no additional bound on total service per capita.
Type \(t\) compares receipt vectors \((x_{tu}/\mu_t)_u\) lexicographically in decreasing supplier preference, preferring a larger entry at the first difference. A circulation is Pareto optimal if no feasible circulation weakly improves every type and strictly improves at least one.
The objective is to compute a Pareto-optimal circulation maximizing \(\sum_{(t,u)\in A}\ell_{tu}x_{tu}\).

2. Construction and feasibility

\[ c_{tu}=\mu_t\kappa_{tu}. \]

\[ 0\leq z_{tu}\leq c_{tu}, \qquad \sum_u z_{tu}=\sum_s z_{st} \quad\text{for every }t\in T. \]

Equivalence with the source’s cycle representation

The cited paper represents balanced exchanges by directed cycles carrying nonnegative quantities. This representation gives the same feasible set.

Indeed, any weighted collection of directed cycles induces a nonnegative balanced circulation. Conversely, let \(x\) be a nonzero nonnegative circulation. Choose an arc with positive flow and follow positive-flow outgoing arcs. Whenever a vertex is reached along a positive-flow arc, balance guarantees a positive-flow outgoing arc. Since the graph is finite, this walk contains a directed cycle.

Subtract from that cycle the minimum flow on its arcs. This preserves nonnegativity and balance, and makes at least one previously positive arc zero. Repeating terminates after at most \(|A|\) steps, because positive arcs disappear and no new positive arcs appear. Thus every feasible circulation has a cycle decomposition.

For such a decomposition, assigning a cycle the sum of its arc weights yields objective

\[ \sum_C f(C)\sum_{e\in C}\ell_e = \sum_{e\in A}\ell_e x_e. \]

Therefore the cycle representation also preserves the objective exactly.

3. Preference and Pareto equivalence

Fix a type \(t\), and list its acceptable suppliers in decreasing preference order as \(u_1,\ldots,u_{d_t}\).

For any two feasible circulations \(x,y\), positivity of \(\mu_t\) gives, for every index \(i\),

\[ \frac{x_{tu_i}}{\mu_t}=\frac{y_{tu_i}}{\mu_t} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_{tu_i}=y_{tu_i}, \]

and

\[ \frac{x_{tu_i}}{\mu_t}>\frac{y_{tu_i}}{\mu_t} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_{tu_i}>y_{tu_i}. \]

Hence the first differing coordinate, and the direction of its inequality, are identical in the normalized and unnormalized receipt vectors. Every type’s strict preference and indifference comparisons are therefore preserved.

It follows that \(y\) Pareto dominates \(x\) in the original model if and only if it does so in the constructed finite-agent model. In particular, their Pareto-optimal feasible sets coincide under the identity map.

The weights remain positive, rational, and preference-concordant because they are unchanged:

\[ u\succ_t u' \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell_{tu}\geq\ell_{tu'}. \]

Finally, the objective is unchanged. Thus a maximum-weight Pareto-optimal exchange returned by the finite-agent algorithm is a maximum-weight Pareto-optimal mass circulation.

4. Binary encoding and running time

The construction uses \(\tau\) agents and \(|A|\leq\tau(\tau-1)\) arcs.

Write the input rationals as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\) and \(\kappa_{tu}=p_{tu}/q_{tu}\), with positive integer denominators. We can encode the new capacity as

\[ c_{tu}=\frac{a_tp_{tu}}{b_tq_{tu}}. \]

The bit length of an integer product is at most the sum of the factors’ bit lengths. Therefore each constructed capacity has \(O(L)\) bits, and the entire constructed instance has \(O((|A|+1)L)\) bits. Computing these products, and reducing the fractions if required, takes polynomial time.

Applying the supplied finite-agent algorithm consequently takes time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\). Translating its output back uses the identity map on arc quantities; a cycle output can instead be summed over its arcs. Since \(m=\tau\), the overall running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

No population expansion is needed: the number of constructed agents is exactly the support size, regardless of the denominators of the masses. This completes the reduction and settles the stated problem.
