Theorem 5 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many agent types \(\mathcal T\) with masses \(\mu_t\), finitely many good kinds \(\mathcal G\) with per-capita supplies \(0\le\rho_g\le1\), and monotone submodular valuations \(v_t:2^{\mathcal G}\to\mathbb R_+\), choose masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) of type \(t\) assigned the integral bundle \(S\subseteq\mathcal G\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:g\in S}x_{t,S}\le\rho_g\), to maximize \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)=\exp(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S))\), with value zero when positive mass receives zero value; determine whether a \(1/5\)-approximation can be computed and characterize its pricing complexity.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) good kinds \(\mathcal G\), with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_g\in[0,1]\). Each type has a normalized monotone submodular valuation \(v_t:2^{\mathcal G}\to\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\), with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\), accessible through a value oracle.
Let \(L\) be the explicit input bit length together with a bound on each oracle answer's bit length; for pricing, include the price-vector encoding length. Measure running time and oracle queries in \(m,\tau,L\).
An allocation assigns mass \(x_{t,S}\geq0\) to integral bundles \(S\subseteq\mathcal G\), with \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:g\in S}x_{t,S}\leq\rho_g\). Allocations are encoded by their nonzero rational coordinates.
Maximize \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)=\exp(\sum_{t,S:x_{t,S}>0}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S))\), defined as zero if positive mass receives zero value; let \(\operatorname{OPT}\) be its maximum.
For a type \(t\) and nonnegative rational item prices \(p_g\), exact pricing asks for a bundle maximizing \(\log v_t(S)-\sum_{g\in S}p_g\) among bundles with \(v_t(S)>0\), or reports that none exists; ties may be resolved arbitrarily.

Can a randomized algorithm, polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), achieve \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)\geq\operatorname{OPT}/5\) with probability at least \(2/3\), or is this guarantee computationally or value-oracle intractable?
Is exact pricing polynomial-time solvable, NP-hard, or subject to a superpolynomial value-query lower bound?

For discrete unweighted submodular NSW, a randomized polynomial-time value-oracle \(1/5\)-approximation is established ([Chekuri–Kulkarni–Mehta–Vondrák](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21099)).
For the related weighted log-configuration LP with each agent's bundle probabilities summing to one and each item's supply equal to one, polynomial-time optimization within additive error \(\log(e/(e-1)+\epsilon)\) is known for every fixed \(\epsilon>0\) ([Feng–Hu–Li–Zhang](https://rohitvaish.in/data/Papers/%5BFeng%2B%5D%20Constant%20Approximation%20for%20Weighted%20Nash%20Social%20Welfare%20with%20Submodular%20Valuations.pdf)); that statement does not cover arbitrary rational mass and supply vectors.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one, consistent with per-capita supplies and the geometric-mean objective.
  • Restored valuation normalization v_t(emptyset)=0 and the value-oracle access model from the discrete definition.
  • Restricted valuation answers to nonnegative binary rationals with bounded bit length so computational complexity is well-defined.
  • Defined pricing as exact maximization of log valuation minus nonnegative additive item prices, with arbitrary tie-breaking and explicit handling of types having no positive-value bundle.

Result

The welfare guarantee is achievable. In fact, a deterministic algorithm, polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), returns a rational feasible allocation satisfying
\(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)\geq \operatorname{OPT}/4\). Thus the requested randomized \(1/5\)-guarantee holds with probability \(1\).

Exact pricing is intractable in both senses asked about: it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even for explicitly given additive valuations, and general monotone submodular valuations require \(2^{\Omega(m)}\) value queries for exact pricing, including for randomized algorithms with success probability at least \(2/3\).

The approximation proof below works directly with the given rational masses and supplies.

Positive welfare and the configuration LP

First query every singleton value. Construct a flow network with:

  • an arc of capacity \(\mu_t\) from the source to each type \(t\);
  • an arc from type \(t\) to good \(g\) whenever \(v_t(\{g\})>0\);
  • an arc of capacity \(\rho_g\) from each good \(g\) to the sink.

The type-to-good arcs may all have capacity \(1\).

There exists an allocation of positive welfare if and only if this network admits flow of value \(1\).

For the forward implication, normalization and submodularity give
\(v_t(S)\leq\sum_{g\in S}v_t(\{g\})\).
Consequently, every positive-value bundle contains a positive-value singleton. Replace each allocated positive-value bundle by one such singleton. This preserves every type’s allocated mass and can only decrease each good’s usage, so it defines the required flow.

Conversely, a flow of value \(1\) allocates positive-value singletons to all the mass of every type.

If this flow does not exist, then \(\operatorname{OPT}=0\); assigning everyone the empty bundle satisfies the requested approximation. Henceforth assume it exists. Fix the resulting rational singleton allocation as a feasible starting allocation.

Write
\(\mathcal C_t=\{S\subseteq\mathcal G:v_t(S)>0\}\).
The relevant optimization problem is the finite, exponentially sized LP

\(\displaystyle F^*=\max\sum_t\sum_{S\in\mathcal C_t}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S),\)

subject to the allocation constraints in the question. Its feasible region is nonempty and compact, and
\(\operatorname{OPT}=\exp(F^*)\).

Since every positive oracle answer has encoding length at most \(L\), we may use the bounds
\(2^{-L}\leq v_t(S)\leq2^L\).
In particular,
\(\lvert\log v_t(S)\rvert\leq L\).

A polynomial approximate-pricing oracle

We first prove a pricing statement that controls the bundle’s cost without any multiplicative loss.

Cost-preserving approximate covering

Fix a normalized monotone submodular valuation \(v\), nonnegative rational costs \(p_g\), and a positive rational target \(V\leq v(\mathcal G)\). Define

\(\displaystyle C_V=\min\{p(S):v(S)\geq V\},\qquad p(S)=\sum_{g\in S}p_g.\)

There is a deterministic polynomial-time value-oracle algorithm returning \(T\) with

\(p(T)\leq C_V,\qquad v(T)\geq V/2.\)

We use the following known theorem: monotone submodular maximization under one knapsack constraint has a deterministic \(1-1/e\) approximation using polynomially many value queries. The algorithm uses \(O(m^5)\) queries and arithmetic polynomial in the binary encoding lengths. [Sviridenko, Theorem 1](https://thibaut.horel.org/submodularity/papers/sviridenko2004.pdf).

Here are the details needed to obtain the stated covering guarantee.

Include all zero-cost goods, denoting their set by \(Z\). On the remaining goods, use the normalized monotone submodular function
\(w(U)=v(Z\cup U)-v(Z)\).
If a budget permits a bundle of original value at least \(V\), the knapsack algorithm returns a bundle of original value at least

\(\displaystyle v(Z)+(1-1/e)\bigl(V-v(Z)\bigr)\geq (1-1/e)V>V/2.\)

Clear the denominators of the positive costs. Their integer encodings, and the total integer cost, have polynomial bit length. Let \(C_V'\) be the minimum integer cost attaining \(V\).

For each integer budget \(b\), run the knapsack algorithm and call the budget successful when its returned original value is at least \(V/2\). Every budget \(b\geq C_V'\) is successful.

If budget \(0\) is successful, return its bundle. Otherwise, binary search between unsuccessful budget \(0\) and the successful total-cost budget. Maintain an unsuccessful lower endpoint and a successful upper endpoint. The lower endpoint is always strictly below \(C_V'\), because every budget at least \(C_V'\) must succeed. At termination, the endpoints are consecutive integers, so the successful upper endpoint is at most \(C_V'\).

This argument does not require the algorithm’s success predicate to be monotone. It proves both required guarantees, with polynomially many knapsack calls.

From covering to approximate pricing

Consider the geometric target grid

\(\displaystyle V_j=2^{-L}(5/4)^j,\)

up to the first term exceeding \(2^L\). It has \(O(L)\) terms, all of polynomial encoding length. For every target at most \(v(\mathcal G)\), run the covering algorithm.

For every positive-value bundle \(S\), some grid target satisfies

\(V_j\leq v(S)<(5/4)V_j.\)

The corresponding returned bundle \(T\) satisfies

\(p(T)\leq p(S),\qquad v(T)\geq V_j/2\geq (2/5)v(S).\)

Therefore

\(\displaystyle \log v(T)-p(T)\geq\log v(S)-p(S)-\log(5/2).\)

Since \(\log(5/2)<1\), these polynomially many candidate bundles contain a pricing solution within additive error \(1\).

We next make this statement entirely rational. Set
\(\eta=1/64\).
Whenever needed, compute a deterministic rational approximation \(c_{t,S}\) satisfying

\(\lvert c_{t,S}-\log v_t(S)\rvert\leq\eta.\)

Such approximations are computable in polynomial time from rational oracle answers. Choose, among the candidate bundles, one maximizing the rational score
\(c_{t,S}-p(S)\).
The returned bundle \(T_t\) then satisfies

\(\displaystyle \max_{S\in\mathcal C_t}\bigl(c_{t,S}-p(S)\bigr) \leq c_{t,T_t}-p(T_t)+\Delta,\)

where
\(\Delta=1+2\eta=33/32\).

This oracle is deterministic and polynomial in the valuation and price encoding lengths.

Recovering an exactly feasible allocation

We now justify the LP step, including arbitrary rational right-hand sides and sparse rational output.

Replace the logarithmic coefficients by the rational coefficients \(c_{t,S}\), and let \(O_c\) denote the resulting LP optimum. Because total allocated mass is \(1\),

\(\lvert O_c-F^*\rvert\leq\eta.\)

The dual is

\(\displaystyle \min D(p,\alpha)=\sum_g\rho_gp_g+\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t,\)

subject to

\(p_g\geq0,\qquad \alpha_t+p(S)\geq c_{t,S}\quad(S\in\mathcal C_t).\)

The \(\alpha_t\) are unrestricted in sign.

At a rational point satisfying the explicit constraints, call the approximate-pricing oracle for every type.

  • If some returned bundle satisfies \(c_{t,T_t}-p(T_t)>\alpha_t\), its dual inequality is genuinely violated and supplies a valid cut.
  • Otherwise, \((p,\alpha+\Delta\mathbf1)\) is feasible for the full dual.

The second assertion follows directly from the pricing guarantee. Its objective is

\(\displaystyle D(p,\alpha+\Delta\mathbf1) =D(p,\alpha)+\Delta\sum_t\mu_t =D(p,\alpha)+\Delta.\)

This is precisely where normalization of total mass controls the additive error.

Uniform bounds for restricted duals

Let \(d=m+\tau\), and choose \(B=L+2\), so every rational objective coefficient has absolute value at most \(B\).

Consider any collection \(J\) of configuration columns containing the feasible singleton starting allocation. Its restricted primal is feasible and bounded.

Its dual is nonempty: take \(p=0\) and \(\alpha_t=B\). It is also pointed. Indeed, a lineality direction must have all price coordinates zero because \(p\geq0\), and then each type’s available configuration inequality forces its \(\alpha_t\)-coordinate to be zero.

By finite-dimensional LP duality, the restricted dual has an optimal vertex. Its constraint matrix has entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). At a vertex, Cramer’s rule applied to \(d\) independent tight constraints gives the uniform coordinate bound

\(\displaystyle \lvert y_i\rvert\leq R_0=d!\,B.\)

Indeed, the nonzero integer denominator determinant has absolute value at least \(1\), and the numerator determinant has absolute value at most \(d!B\). Thus every such restricted dual has an optimum in the box with price coordinates in \([0,R_0]\) and type coordinates in \([-R_0,R_0]\).

Set \(R=R_0+1\). Its encoding length is polynomial.

The ellipsoid certificate used here

Set \(h=1/32\). For a rational objective threshold \(z\), run the bounded ellipsoid method on:

  • the box \([-R,R]^d\);
  • the inequalities \(p_g\geq0\);
  • the objective inequality \(D(p,\alpha)\leq z\);
  • the starting singleton configuration inequalities.

At each center, check these explicit inequalities first. If they hold, invoke the approximate-pricing oracle and either cut using a genuinely violated configuration inequality or stop.

If the procedure stops, the shifted dual solution proves

\(O_c\leq z+\Delta.\)

If it does not stop, retain the generated configuration columns in \(J\). We show that polynomially many cuts certify

\(O_J>z-h,\)

where \(O_J\) is the restricted primal optimum.

Suppose instead that \(O_J\leq z-h\). Choose an optimal restricted-dual vertex \(y_0=(p,\alpha)\) within the smaller box established above. Define

\(\displaystyle s=\frac{h}{4(m+1)},\qquad r=\frac{s}{2d},\)

and increase every coordinate of \(y_0\) by \(s\).

Every configuration inequality now has slack at least \(s\), and every price is at least \(s\). The objective increases by at most

\(\displaystyle s\left(\sum_g\rho_g+\sum_t\mu_t\right)\leq s(m+1)=h/4.\)

Each configuration-row normal has Euclidean norm at most \(d\), as does the objective-row normal: all supplies and masses belong to \([0,1]\). Consequently, the Euclidean ball of radius \(r\) around the perturbed point still satisfies all configuration inequalities, nonnegativity inequalities, and the objective bound. It also remains in the larger box, whose coordinate margin was \(1\).

Thus the polyhedron defined by all recorded inequalities would contain a ball of radius \(r\).

Start the ellipsoid in a ball of radius \(dR\), which contains the box. The standard bounded-polytope ellipsoid volume theorem says that polynomially many central cuts—\(O(d^2\log(dR/r))\)—reduce the containing volume below that of a radius-\(r\) ball. Its rational implementation has polynomial bit complexity. Here every cut has polynomial-bit rational coefficients, and both \(\log R\) and \(\log(1/r)\) are polynomially bounded.

All cuts contain the recorded polyhedron. The asserted ball would therefore contradict the volume bound. Hence \(O_J>z-h\), as claimed.

This argument supplies the needed primal guarantee without assuming that an approximately accepted dual point is exactly feasible.

Thresholds, approximation, and encoding

Test thresholds on a grid of spacing \(h\) covering \([-B-2,B]\), retaining all generated configuration columns. There are \(O(L)\) thresholds.

Because \(O_c\in[-B,B]\), there is a tested threshold satisfying

\(\displaystyle O_c-\Delta-h\leq z<O_c-\Delta.\)

At this threshold the procedure cannot stop: stopping would imply \(O_c\leq z+\Delta<O_c\). Therefore its recorded columns have restricted optimum greater than \(z-h\).

Solve the rational primal on the union of all retained columns and the starting singleton columns. The returned allocation \(x\) is exactly feasible and satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}c_{t,S}\geq O_c-\Delta-2h.\)

Returning to the true logarithmic objective gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S) \geq F^*-\Delta-2h-2\eta =F^*-9/8.\)

Therefore

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)\geq e^{-9/8}\operatorname{OPT} >\operatorname{OPT}/4.\)

For the final strict inequality, \(9/8<5/4\), \(e<3\), and \(3^5<4^4\) imply \(e^{9/8}<4\).

All oracle calls, integer-cost binary searches, ellipsoid operations, and final LP operations have polynomial bit complexity. A basic optimal solution of the restricted primal has at most \(m+\tau\) nonzero allocation coordinates: after adding supply slacks there are \(m+\tau\) equality rows. These coordinates are rational with polynomial encoding length.

Thus the algorithm meets the allocation encoding requirement and is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Exact pricing is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even for additive valuations

Reduce from positive-integer SUBSET SUM. Let its item weights be \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) and its positive target be \(B\). Define

\(\displaystyle v(S)=\sum_{i\in S}a_i,\qquad p_i=a_i/B.\)

This valuation is normalized, monotone, and additive, hence submodular. Both its explicit representation and the rational price vector have polynomial encoding length.

For a nonempty bundle, write \(u=\sum_{i\in S}a_i\). Its pricing objective is

\(\displaystyle \phi(u)=\log u-u/B.\)

On the positive real axis,

\(\displaystyle \phi'(u)=1/u-1/B.\)

Thus \(\phi\) is strictly increasing below \(B\), strictly decreasing above \(B\), and has its unique maximum at \(B\).

If a subset of weight \(B\) exists, every exact pricing maximizer has weight \(B\). If none exists, no returned bundle can have that weight. Running exact pricing and checking the returned bundle’s integer weight therefore decides SUBSET SUM.

This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. No membership-in-\(\mathrm{NP}\) claim concerning logarithmic comparisons is needed.

Exact pricing has an exponential value-query lower bound

We give a separate, unconditional oracle lower bound.

Let \(m\geq4\) be even, let \(k=m/2\), and define

\(\displaystyle f(s)=2ms-s^2\qquad(0\leq s\leq m).\)

For each hidden \(k\)-element set \(H\subseteq\mathcal G\), define

\(\displaystyle v_H(S)=f(|S|)+\frac12\mathbf1_{\{S=H\}}.\)

All oracle answers have \(O(\log m)\)-bit rational encodings.

The hidden valuations satisfy the hypotheses

Normalization is immediate because \(H\neq\varnothing\).

The unperturbed marginal from cardinality \(s\) to \(s+1\) is

\(\displaystyle f(s+1)-f(s)=2m-2s-1\geq1.\)

The perturbation can reduce an inclusion marginal by at most \(1/2\). Thus \(v_H\) is monotone.

For distinct goods \(i,j\notin A\), the unperturbed local submodularity difference is

\(\displaystyle f(|A|+1)+f(|A|+1)-f(|A|)-f(|A|+2)=2.\)

The four sets involved are distinct, so at most one equals \(H\). The perturbation reduces this difference by at most \(1/2\). Hence every local submodularity inequality holds. These local inequalities imply diminishing returns, and therefore submodularity.

Thus every \(v_H\) is an admissible valuation.

A rational price makes the hidden set uniquely optimal

Set

\(\displaystyle a=\log f(k)-\log f(k-1),\qquad b=\log f(k+1)-\log f(k).\)

We have \(0<b<a\). Moreover, the interval between them has inverse-polynomial length. To see this, for real \(s\in[k-1,k+1]\), write

\(\displaystyle q(s)=\log f(s)=\log s+\log(2m-s).\)

Then

\(\displaystyle q''(s)=-1/s^2-1/(2m-s)^2\leq-1/m^2.\)

Integrating the difference of consecutive unit-interval derivatives gives

\(a-b\geq1/m^2.\)

We can therefore compute a positive dyadic rational \(p\), of \(O(\log m)\) encoding length, strictly between \(b\) and \(a\), by approximating these logarithms to inverse-polynomial accuracy. Give every good price \(p\).

The sequence \(\log f(s)\) has strictly decreasing successive differences. Because \(b<p<a\), the unperturbed objective

\(\log f(|S|)-p|S|\)

is uniquely maximized in cardinality at \(|S|=k\). All \(k\)-element sets tie under the unperturbed valuation. Under \(v_H\), exactly \(H\) receives a strictly larger value, so \(H\) is the unique exact pricing maximizer.

Indistinguishability

Let

\(\displaystyle N=\binom{m}{m/2}.\)

The oracle \(v_H\) differs from the known baseline oracle \(v_0(S)=f(|S|)\) only on the single query \(S=H\).

Fix an algorithm’s random coins and run it against the baseline. Suppose it makes at most \(q\) queries. Until it queries \(H\), its execution against \(v_H\) follows this same transcript.

At most \(q\) hidden sets appear among the baseline queries. For every other hidden set, the final output is the baseline output, which can equal at most one additional hidden set. Since exact pricing must return \(H\), success over uniformly random \(H\) is at most

\(\displaystyle \frac{q+1}{N}.\)

Averaging over the algorithm’s random coins preserves this bound. Consequently, success probability at least \(2/3\) on every \(v_H\) requires

\(\displaystyle q\geq\frac23\binom{m}{m/2}-1.\)

Finally,

\(\displaystyle \binom{m}{m/2}\geq\frac{2^m}{m+1},\)

so the required number of queries is \(2^{\Omega(m)}\).

Conclusion

The target questions are completely settled: a deterministic polynomial-time value-oracle algorithm achieves the requested welfare approximation for arbitrary input masses and supplies, while exact pricing is both computationally hard and exponentially hard in the value-oracle model.
