Proposition III — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite support \(T\) of equal-size, independent path communities, where type \(t\) has one opinion boundary at \(x_t\), a fixed status gradient with right-step probability \(a_t\), and rational mass \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compute the expected fraction of residents who eventually hold opinion \(1\). For each type, \(p(x_t,a_t)=\frac{a_t^{x_t}(1-a_t)^{L-x_t}-a_t^L}{(1-a_t)^L-a_t^L}\); the aggregate is \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p(x_t,a_t)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type represents independent path communities with a common number \(m\geq2\) of residents, supplied in unary. Type \(t\) specifies an integer \(1\leq x_t\leq m-1\) and a binary-rational probability \(0<a_t<1\). Initially the leftmost \(m-x_t\) residents hold opinion \(1\), and the remaining residents hold opinion \(0\).
The fixed status gradient is represented directly by \(a_t\): the boundary coordinate \(Y\), counting residents with opinion \(1\), starts at \(m-x_t\) and moves right with probability \(a_t\) and left with probability \(1-a_t\). States \(0\) and \(m\) are absorbing, representing unanimous opinions \(0\) and \(1\), respectively.
The expected eventual fraction holding opinion \(1\) is \(F=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p(x_t,a_t)\), where \(p(x,a)=\frac{a^x(1-a)^{m-x}-a^m}{(1-a)^m-a^m}\) for \(a\neq\tfrac12\), and \(p(x,\tfrac12)=(m-x)/m\). Exact computation means producing \(F\) as a binary rational; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Can \(F\) be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or is its exact computation NP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

The discrete single-community result supplies a closed expression for absorption probabilities, without a bit-complexity classification ([Tupikina](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.01647v1)). Classical gambler’s-ruin results also give the single-walk hitting probability ([Portland State lecture notes](https://web.pdx.edu/~gjay/teaching/mth271_2020/html/13_GamblersRuin.html)); no complexity classification for this exact mass-aggregate problem was located.

  • Renamed community size to m, reserving L for total input bit length; supplied m in unary to retain the explicit finite-path size convention.
  • Interpreted x_t as the number of rightmost opinion-0 residents, consistent with the phase-space figure caption, so the given formula agrees with a right-step probability a_t.
  • Used absorbing boundary coordinates 0 and m, corresponding to the two unanimous configurations.
  • Required initially nonunanimous communities and rational probabilities strictly between 0 and 1; probabilities are supplied directly rather than through potentially irrational status-to-probability transformations.
  • Included the symmetric case a_t=1/2 using the continuous extension of the stated expression.
  • Specified exact binary-rational computation and polynomial-time Turing reductions for the function problem.

Conclusion and output convention

The exact aggregate \(F\) is computable in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), with polynomial output length, when a rational is represented by a numerator and denominator written in binary.

There is a minor terminology issue: \(F\) need not be dyadic, even when all input probabilities and masses are dyadic. For example, one type with \(\mu=1\), \(m=3\), \(x=1\), and \(a=\tfrac14\) gives \(F=\tfrac4{13}\). Thus a finite binary expansion cannot always represent the answer. We settle the intended exact-rational computation problem using a ratio of binary integers.

The single-community expression appears in [Tupikina, Section 3.2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1708.01647v1); the [Portland State notes](https://web.pdx.edu/~gjay/teaching/mth271_2020/html/13_GamblersRuin.html) also give the classical gambler’s-ruin expression. The bit-complexity proof below starts directly from the formula supplied in the task.

1. Integer representation of each absorption probability

Write \(a_t=u_t/v_t\), where \(u_t,v_t\) are positive integers satisfying \(u_t<v_t\), and put \(b_t=v_t-u_t\). Write the mass as \(\mu_t=c_t/d_t\), with positive integers \(c_t,d_t\). Reduction to lowest terms is unnecessary.

Fix a type and suppress its subscript. If \(2u\neq v\), substitution into the given formula and cancellation of the common factor \(v^{-m}\) give

\[ p(x,a)=\frac{u^x b^{m-x}-u^m}{b^m-u^m}. \]

Define \(H=u^x(b^{m-x}-u^{m-x})\) and \(G=b^m-u^m\). Because \(m-x\geq1\), both \(H\) and \(G\) have the sign of \(b-u\), and neither is zero. Consequently, setting \(N=|H|\) and \(D=|G|\) gives \(p(x,a)=N/D\), with positive integers \(N,D\).

Moreover,

\[ D-N=b^{m-x}|b^x-u^x|>0. \]

To verify this identity, multiply \(G-H=b^{m-x}(b^x-u^x)\) by the common sign of \(G\) and \(H\). The strict inequality follows from \(x\geq1\) and \(b\neq u\). Thus \(0<N<D\).

If \(2u=v\), set \(N=m-x\) and \(D=m\). The unbiased formula again gives \(p(x,a)=N/D\), with \(0<N<D\).

2. Polynomial bounds on individual bit lengths

For a positive integer \(z\), let \(\ell(z)=\lfloor\log_2 z\rfloor+1\) denote its binary length.

In the biased case, \(u,b<v\), so every power or product used above is less than \(v^m\). In particular,

\[ N,D<v^m<2^{m\ell(v)}, \]

and therefore \(\ell(N),\ell(D)\leq m\ell(v)\). In the unbiased case, both lengths are at most \(\ell(m)\).

Each contribution is represented exactly by

\[ \mu_t p(x_t,a_t)=\frac{n_t}{q_t}, \qquad n_t=c_tN_t,\qquad q_t=d_tD_t. \]

Since the positive masses sum to one, \(c_t\leq d_t\). Hence \(0<n_t<q_t\), and

\[ \ell(q_t)\leq\ell(d_t)+m\ell(v_t)+\ell(m). \]

The sums of the input numerator and denominator lengths are \(O(L)\). This also holds if input dyadic rationals are initially supplied as explicit finite binary expansions: converting them to integer fractions increases their total length by only a constant factor.

3. Exact aggregation with controlled intermediate size

Enumerate the support as \(t_1,\ldots,t_\tau\). Initialize \(A_0=0\), \(B_0=1\), and compute

\[ A_k=A_{k-1}q_{t_k}+n_{t_k}B_{k-1}, \qquad B_k=B_{k-1}q_{t_k}. \]

Induction gives

\[ \frac{A_k}{B_k} =\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{t_j}p(x_{t_j},a_{t_j}), \qquad B_k=\prod_{j=1}^{k}q_{t_j}. \]

Indeed, the recurrence is precisely the integer numerator-and-denominator rule for adding the next fraction.

Each partial sum is nonnegative and at most \(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{t_j}\leq1\), because every absorption probability lies strictly between zero and one. Therefore \(0\leq A_k\leq B_k\).

Define

\[ K=1+\sum_{t\in T}\ell(d_t) +m\sum_{t\in T}\ell(v_t) +\tau\ell(m). \]

The denominator-product bound gives \(\ell(B_k)\leq K\), and the same bound applies to nonzero \(A_k\). The multiplication operands and temporary products also have \(O(K)\) bits. Thus aggregation never requires integers of superpolynomial length.

Furthermore,

\[ K=O\bigl(mL+\tau\log_2(m+1)\bigr), \]

which is polynomial in the requested parameters.

4. Bit-operation running time

For each type, computing successive powers of \(u_t\) and \(b_t\) up to exponent \(m\) takes \(O(m)\) integer multiplications. These powers suffice to compute \(N_t,D_t,n_t,q_t\).

The aggregation uses a constant number of additions and multiplications per type. Schoolbook multiplication on \(O(K)\)-bit integers costs \(O(K^2)\) bit operations. Consequently, constructing \(A_\tau,B_\tau\) takes

\[ O\bigl(L+\tau mK^2\bigr) \]

bit operations. The pair represents \(F\) exactly.

If a reduced fraction is required, compute \(g=\gcd(A_\tau,B_\tau)\) and output \(A_\tau/g\), \(B_\tau/g\). Euclid’s algorithm takes \(O(K)\) divisions: every two successive remainder steps at least halve the relevant remainder. Schoolbook division costs \(O(K^2)\), so reduction adds at most \(O(K^3)\) bit operations.

Both the total running time and the output length are therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Since \(m\) is supplied in unary and the support is explicitly listed, this is also polynomial in total input length.

5. Complexity classification

The exact computation problem belongs to \(\mathrm{FP}\).

If it were NP-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, replacing each oracle call by the algorithm proved above would decide every language in \(\mathrm{NP}\) in polynomial time. Such NP-hardness would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). The unconditional conclusion is the polynomial-time algorithm.
