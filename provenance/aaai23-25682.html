The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many complete participant types \(p\) with rational masses \(\mu_p\), \(k\) tables, \(T\) sessions, and mass-normalized representativeness constraints, choose trajectory masses \(x_{p,\sigma} \ge 0\) for \(\sigma \in [k]^T\) with \(\sum_{\sigma} x_{p,\sigma} = \mu_p\) and feasible per-session table masses, so that \(\Phi_f(x) = 1/2 \sum_{p,q} \sum_{\sigma,\sigma'} x_{p,\sigma} x_{q,\sigma'} f(|\{r : \sigma_r = \sigma'_r\}|)\) simultaneously \(\alpha_T\)-approximates \(\max_{x'} \Phi_f(x')\) for every monotone concave saturation function \(f\) with \(f(0) = 0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct complete types \(p\in\{0,1\}^m\), describing possession of \(m\) features, with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_p\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
There are integers \(k\ge1\) tables and \(T\ge2\) sessions, and binary-rational feature quotas \(0\le\ell_j\le u_j\le1/k\), identical across tables and sessions.
A schedule consists of divisible trajectory masses \(x_{p,\sigma}\ge0\), where \(\sigma\in\{1,\ldots,k\}^T\), with \(\sum_\sigma x_{p,\sigma}=\mu_p\). For every session \(r\), table \(a\), and feature \(j\), require \(\sum_{p,\sigma:\sigma_r=a}x_{p,\sigma}=1/k\) and \(\ell_j\le\sum_{p,\sigma:\sigma_r=a}p_jx_{p,\sigma}\le u_j\). Promise feasibility.
A saturation function is \(f:\{0,\ldots,T\}\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), nondecreasing and discretely concave, with \(f(0)=0\). Define \(c(\sigma,\sigma')=|\{r:\sigma_r=\sigma'_r\}|\) and \(\Phi_f(x)=\tfrac12\sum_{p,q}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma'}x_{p,\sigma}x_{q,\sigma'}f(c(\sigma,\sigma'))\), including equal types and equal trajectories; masses describe an atomless population.
Set \(\alpha_T=\tfrac{1-1/e}{2}\bigl(\tfrac1{1+\log_2 T}-\tfrac1T\bigr)\). Algorithmic schedules are represented by a list of nonzero rational trajectory masses.

Is there an algorithm running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,T,L\) that, without receiving \(f\), constructs one feasible schedule \(x\) satisfying \(\Phi_f(x)\ge\alpha_T\max_{x'\text{ feasible}}\Phi_f(x')\) for every saturation function \(f\), or is achieving this simultaneous guarantee NP-hard?

For discrete concave coverage with an explicitly listed collection of selectable sets and repetitions allowed, SIMAPPROX achieves this simultaneous factor in polynomial time; discrete table allocation instead uses ILP subproblems, and partition feasibility is NP-hard. [Barrett et al.](https://procaccia.info/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/deliberation.pdf)
No result settling the trajectory-mass problem defined here was found in the literature search.

  • Defined complete types as possession vectors for all m features, with tau counting only positive-mass support types.
  • Normalized total society mass to one and replaced integer floor/ceiling table sizes by equal table mass 1/k.
  • Interpreted mass-normalized quotas as bounds on feature mass relative to the whole society, identical across tables and sessions, with binary-rational bounds between zero and 1/k.
  • Carried over the feasibility promise and T at least two; allowed any positive integer k.
  • Specified saturation functions only on attainable integer meeting counts, with discrete concavity and nonnegative real values.
  • Instantiated the undefined alpha_T with the anchor's exact approximation factor, retaining its zero value at T equal to two.
  • Used an atomless population with freely divisible types, retaining all same-type and same-trajectory terms in the supplied objective.
  • Specified sparse rational schedule representation and polynomial running time in the numerical session count T, as well as m, tau, k and L; the optimum ranges over real feasible masses.

A polynomial-time simultaneous approximation

The requested algorithm exists. In fact, it constructs a feasible schedule satisfying the stronger guarantee
\( \Phi_f(x)\ge \tfrac12\max_{x'\text{ feasible}}\Phi_f(x') \)
for every admissible saturation function \(f\), without receiving \(f\).

The proof uses divisibility and the identical quotas across tables. It does not require solving the discrete partition problem.

1. Eliminating the feature constraints

For each feature \(j\), define its total population mass by
\( M_j=\sum_p\mu_p p_j \).

Summing the feature constraints over the \(k\) tables in any session shows that feasibility implies
\( k\ell_j\le M_j\le ku_j \).
Thus, by the feasibility promise,
\( \ell_j\le M_j/k\le u_j \).

Let \(y\) be any probability distribution on trajectories with uniform session marginals:
\( \sum_{\sigma:\sigma_r=a}y_\sigma=1/k \)
for every session \(r\) and table \(a\). Define
\( x_{p,\sigma}=\mu_p y_\sigma \).

These masses sum to \(\mu_p\) for each type. Each table has mass \(1/k\), and its feature-\(j\) mass is
\( \sum_{p,\sigma:\sigma_r=a}p_jx_{p,\sigma}=M_j/k \).
Consequently, this schedule is feasible.

Conversely, every feasible schedule induces a trajectory distribution
\( y_\sigma=\sum_p x_{p,\sigma} \)
with uniform session marginals. Its objective depends only on this distribution:
\( \Phi_f(x)=\tfrac12\sum_{\sigma,\sigma'}y_\sigma y_{\sigma'}f(c(\sigma,\sigma')) \).

Equivalently, if \(\Sigma,\Sigma'\) are independent trajectories drawn from \(y\), and \(C=c(\Sigma,\Sigma')\), then
\( \Phi_f(x)=\tfrac12\mathbb E[f(C)] \).

Therefore, the attainable objectives are exactly those obtained from trajectory distributions with uniform session marginals.

2. Constructing pairwise independent session labels

We construct a rational trajectory distribution \(y\) satisfying
\( \Pr(\Sigma_r=a)=1/k \)
and, for distinct sessions \(r,s\),
\( \Pr(\Sigma_r=a,\Sigma_s=b)=1/k^2 \).

The construction maintains a distribution on prefixes and extends it one session at a time.

For the first session, give each label mass \(1/k\). Suppose that a distribution on \(r-1\) sessions has positive masses \(v_i\) on prefixes \(\sigma^{(i)}\), indexed by \(i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\), and already has the required pairwise independence.

Introduce variables \(z_{i,b}\), representing the mass assigned to prefix \(\sigma^{(i)}\) followed by label \(b\). Impose the following rational linear constraints:

  • \( z_{i,b}\ge0 \).
  • \( \sum_{b=1}^k z_{i,b}=v_i \) for every prefix \(i\).
  • \( \sum_{i:\sigma^{(i)}_s=a}z_{i,b}=1/k^2 \) for every \(s<r\) and every pair of labels \(a,b\).

This polytope is nonempty: \(z_{i,b}=v_i/k\) satisfies every constraint. It is bounded because \(0\le z_{i,b}\le v_i\).

Choose a vertex of this polytope and retain its positive variables as the new prefix distribution. The prefix-mass equations preserve all old marginals and pair distributions. The last family of equations gives independence between the new session and every old session. Summing those equations over \(a\) also gives the new session’s uniform marginal.

Support bound

There are \(M+(r-1)k^2\) equality constraints. A vertex has at most this many positive variables.

Indeed, write the equalities as \(Az=b\). If the columns indexed by positive variables were linearly dependent, there would be a nonzero vector \(h\), supported on those variables, with \(Ah=0\). For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), both \(z+\varepsilon h\) and \(z-\varepsilon h\) would remain nonnegative and feasible, contradicting that \(z\) is a vertex.

Writing \(M_r\) for the support size after session \(r\), we obtain
\( M_1=k \)
and
\( M_r\le M_{r-1}+(r-1)k^2 \).
Hence the final distribution has support at most
\( M_T\le k+\binom{T}{2}k^2 \).

Polynomial time and bit length

We use the standard theorem that rational linear programs can be solved exactly in time polynomial in their binary encoding length. See [Khachiyan’s polynomial algorithms for linear programming](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239). A vertex of a nonempty bounded rational polytope can be obtained by successively minimizing its coordinates: each minimization restricts to an optimal face, and after all coordinates have been fixed the resulting singleton is a vertex.

Here every extension has polynomially many variables and constraints. We also verify that rational encodings remain polynomially bounded across extensions.

Suppose that all old masses have common denominator \(D_{r-1}\). Every right-hand side then has denominator dividing \(D_{r-1}k^2\). At a vertex with \(d\) positive variables, their columns are independent, so selecting \(d\) suitable rows gives an invertible matrix \(B\) with entries in \(\{0,1\}\). Cramer’s rule shows that the new masses have a common denominator dividing
\( D_{r-1}k^2|\det B| \).

Hadamard’s inequality gives
\( |\det B|\le d^{d/2} \),
where
\( d\le M_{r-1}+(r-1)k^2\le k+\binom{T}{2}k^2 \).
Thus each extension increases the common denominator’s bit length by at most
\( 2\log_2 k+\tfrac d2\log_2 d \).
There are only \(T-1\) extensions, so all mass encodings have polynomial bit length. Since every mass lies in \([0,1]\), its numerator has the same bound.

The intermediate coordinate minimizations also have polynomial encoding length: each intermediate optimum is attained at a vertex of the original extension polytope, whose coordinates have the determinant bound just established.

Finally, output \(x_{p,\sigma}=\mu_p y_\sigma\). There are at most
\( \tau\bigl(k+\binom{T}{2}k^2\bigr) \)
positive type–trajectory masses, each with polynomial bit length. The entire construction therefore runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,T,L\).

3. Collision moments

Fix the constructed distribution, and draw independent trajectories \(\Sigma,\Sigma'\). Define
\( I_r=\mathbf 1_{\{\Sigma_r=\Sigma'_r\}} \)
and
\( C=\sum_{r=1}^T I_r \).

Uniform marginals imply
\( \mathbb E[I_r]=\sum_{a=1}^k(1/k)^2=1/k \).

For distinct sessions \(r,s\), independence of the two sampled trajectories and pairwise uniformity within each trajectory imply
\( \mathbb E[I_rI_s]=\sum_{a,b}\Pr(\Sigma_r=a,\Sigma_s=b)^2=k^2(1/k^2)^2=1/k^2 \).

Consequently, for the collision count \(D_h=\sum_{r=1}^h I_r\) over the first \(h\) sessions,
\( \mathbb E[D_h]=h/k \)
and
\( \mathbb E[D_h(D_h-1)]=h(h-1)/k^2 \).

The inclusion of equal trajectories in the objective is fully accounted for by these independent draws.

4. Simultaneously approximating every truncation

For an integer \(s\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), define
\( g_s(z)=\min(z,s) \).

We first prove the pointwise inequality
\( \min(z,s)\ge z-\frac{z(z-1)}{2s} \)
for every nonnegative integer \(z\).

If \(z\le s\), the subtracted term is nonnegative, so the inequality follows. If \(z\ge s\), put \(d=z-s\). The inequality is equivalent to
\( z(z-1)-2s(z-s)\ge0 \),
and its left-hand side equals
\( s(s-1)+d(d-1)\ge0 \),
because \(s\) and \(d\) are nonnegative integers with \(s\ge1\).

Now choose
\( h=\min(T,sk) \)
and write
\( \nu=h/k=\min(T/k,s) \).
Since \(D_h\le C\), the moment identities give
\( \mathbb E[\min(C,s)]\ge\mathbb E[\min(D_h,s)]\ge\nu-\frac{h(h-1)}{2sk^2} \).

Furthermore,
\( \frac{h(h-1)}{2sk^2}\le\frac{\nu^2}{2s}\le\frac{\nu}{2} \),
where the last inequality uses \(\nu\le s\). Hence the constructed schedule satisfies
\( \Phi_{g_s}(x)\ge\nu/4 \).

For comparison, consider any feasible schedule \(x'\) and its collision count \(C'\). Its uniform session marginals give
\( \mathbb E[C']=T/k \).
Because \(\min(C',s)\le C'\) and \(\min(C',s)\le s\),
\( \Phi_{g_s}(x')\le\tfrac12\min(T/k,s)=\nu/2 \).

Therefore, for every threshold \(s\) and every feasible \(x'\),
\( \Phi_{g_s}(x)\ge\tfrac12\Phi_{g_s}(x') \).

5. Extending the guarantee to every saturation function

Let \(f\) be any admissible saturation function. Define its increments by
\( \Delta_s=f(s)-f(s-1) \)
for \(s\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), and set \(\Delta_{T+1}=0\).

Monotonicity and discrete concavity give
\( \Delta_1\ge\Delta_2\ge\cdots\ge\Delta_T\ge0 \).
Thus the coefficients
\( w_s=\Delta_s-\Delta_{s+1} \)
are nonnegative.

For every integer \(z\in\{0,\ldots,T\}\),
\( f(z)=\sum_{s=1}^T w_s\min(z,s) \).
To verify this identity, both sides vanish at zero, and the increment of the right-hand side from \(z-1\) to \(z\) is
\( \sum_{s=z}^T w_s=\Delta_z \).

Linearity of the objective in the saturation function now yields, for every feasible \(x'\),
\( \Phi_f(x)=\sum_{s=1}^T w_s\Phi_{g_s}(x)\ge\tfrac12\sum_{s=1}^T w_s\Phi_{g_s}(x')=\tfrac12\Phi_f(x') \).

The feasible set is a nonempty compact polytope, and the objective is continuous, so its maximum exists. Consequently,
\( \Phi_f(x)\ge\tfrac12\max_{x'\text{ feasible}}\Phi_f(x') \).

The construction depends only on \(k,T\) and the type masses; it never receives \(f\).

6. Comparison with the requested factor

For \(T\ge2\),
\( \alpha_T\le(1-1/e)/4<1/2 \).
Thus the polynomial-time construction satisfies the requested simultaneous guarantee, with a stronger factor.
