The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite goods \(G\), a finite support \(T\) of buyer types with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and each type's utility vector \(U_{tg}\), bargaining weight \(\beta_t>0\), and disagreement utility \(c_t\ge 0\), compute an optimal allocation \(x_{tg}\) and supporting equilibrium prices, or report infeasibility. The allocation must satisfy \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tg}\le q_g\), \(z_t\le\sum_g U_{tg}x_{tg}\), and \(z_t>c_t\), and maximize \(\sum_t\mu_t\beta_t\log(z_t-c_t)\). The algorithmic target is a running time polynomial in \(|G|\), \(|T|\), and the binary input length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
Let \(G\) be a nonempty set of divisible goods, \(m=|G|\). Inputs are binary rationals \(q_g>0\), \(U_{tg}\ge0\), \(\beta_t>0\), and \(c_t\ge0\); every type and every good has at least one positive utility entry. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation \(x_{tg}\ge0\) gives the per-capita amount of good \(g\) to type \(t\). Feasibility requires \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tg}\le q_g\) and \(c_t<z_t\le\sum_gU_{tg}x_{tg}\). Maximize \(\sum_t\mu_t\beta_t\log(z_t-c_t)\).
Supporting equilibrium prices satisfy \(p_g>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tg}=q_g\), and \(U_{tg}/p_g\le(z_t-c_t)/\beta_t\), with equality whenever \(x_{tg}>0\); at an optimum, \(z_t=\sum_gU_{tg}x_{tg}\). Any optimal allocation and supporting prices are admissible.

Can an exact optimal allocation and supporting equilibrium prices, or a report of infeasibility, be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finitely listed buyers, nonsymmetric Arrow–Debreu Nash bargaining with linear utilities admits a polynomial-time combinatorial algorithm for exact optimization and infeasibility detection ([Végh](https://arxiv.org/html/1109.3893v3)). No result specifically addressing the support-encoded mass formulation above was verified.

  • Carried over the assumption that every buyer type and every good has a positive utility entry.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), using the finite-buyer exact nonsymmetric Arrow–Debreu Nash bargaining theorem stated in the question.

The reduction creates exactly \(\tau\) buyers. Rational masses enter utility coefficients and objective weights; they do not require replication of buyers.

1. Reduction to a finite-buyer problem

Let \(T\) be the listed support, so \(|T|=\tau\) and \(\mu_t>0\) for every \(t\in T\).

Because every objective coefficient \(\mu_t\beta_t\) is positive, increasing \(z_t\) strictly increases the objective. Consequently, at any optimum,

\[ z_t=\sum_{g\in G}U_{tg}x_{tg}. \]

Define normalized allocation variables, utility coefficients, and objective weights by

\[ a_{tg}=\frac{\mu_t x_{tg}}{q_g}, \qquad V_{tg}=\frac{q_gU_{tg}}{\mu_t}, \qquad w_t=\mu_t\beta_t. \]

All divisions are valid because \(q_g,\mu_t>0\). The transformation is invertible:

\[ x_{tg}=\frac{q_ga_{tg}}{\mu_t}. \]

For each good \(g\), the original supply constraint becomes

\[ \sum_{t\in T}a_{tg}\le 1. \]

Moreover,

\[ \sum_{g\in G}V_{tg}a_{tg} = \sum_{g\in G}U_{tg}x_{tg}. \]

Thus the original problem is equivalent to

\[ \max_{a\ge 0} \sum_{t\in T} w_t\log\left(\sum_{g\in G}V_{tg}a_{tg}-c_t\right), \]

subject to

\[ \sum_{t\in T}a_{tg}\le 1 \quad\text{for every }g, \qquad \sum_{g\in G}V_{tg}a_{tg}>c_t \quad\text{for every }t. \]

This is nonsymmetric Arrow–Debreu Nash bargaining with \(\tau\) explicitly listed buyers and one unit of each good. The transformation preserves feasibility and objective values exactly.

2. Clearing denominators

To apply the integer-data formulation of the finite-buyer theorem, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all \(V_{tg}\) and \(c_t\), and let \(H\) be the product of the denominators of all \(w_t\). Define

\[ A_{tg}=DV_{tg}, \qquad C_t=Dc_t, \qquad W_t=Hw_t. \]

Then \(A_{tg}\) and \(C_t\) are nonnegative integers, and \(W_t\) is a positive integer.

The integer-data program is

\[ \max_{a\ge 0} F(a):= \sum_{t\in T} W_t\log\left(\sum_{g\in G}A_{tg}a_{tg}-C_t\right), \]

with the same unit-supply constraints and strictly positive surpluses.

Its feasible allocations are exactly those of the preceding program, because

\[ \sum_gA_{tg}a_{tg}-C_t = D\left(\sum_gV_{tg}a_{tg}-c_t\right). \]

Its objective satisfies

\[ F(a) = H\sum_t w_t \log\left(\sum_gV_{tg}a_{tg}-c_t\right) + H\left(\sum_t w_t\right)\log D. \]

The second term is independent of \(a\), and \(H>0\). Therefore the two programs have exactly the same optimal allocations.

3. Applying the finite-buyer theorem

The exact nonsymmetric ADNB theorem applies to finitely listed buyers, unit supplies, nonnegative integer utilities and disagreement utilities, and positive integer objective weights. It computes an exact optimum or detects infeasibility in polynomial time. See [Végh, Section 8 and Theorem 8.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1109.3893v3#S8).

Every required hypothesis holds here:

  • There are exactly \(\tau\) buyers and \(m\) goods.
  • Every \(W_t\) is positive.
  • Every \(A_{tg}\) and \(C_t\) is nonnegative.
  • Every buyer and every good has a positive utility entry, since \(A_{tg}>0\) exactly when \(U_{tg}>0\).

Thus the theorem returns either infeasibility or an exact rational optimal allocation \(a^*\). Infeasibility transfers to the original problem by the equivalences proved above.

4. Recovering supporting prices exactly

Suppose the algorithm returns \(a^*\). Define the integer-program surpluses and marginal objective values by

\[ S_t=\sum_gA_{tg}a^*_{tg}-C_t>0, \qquad h_{tg}=\frac{W_tA_{tg}}{S_t}. \]

Set

\[ P_g=\max_{t\in T}h_{tg}. \]

These are exactly computable rational numbers. Because every good has a positive utility entry, \(P_g>0\).

We next verify their supporting properties directly.

First, every good is fully allocated. If \(\sum_ta^*_{tg}<1\), choose a buyer \(t\) with \(A_{tg}>0\). Increasing \(a^*_{tg}\) by a sufficiently small positive amount preserves feasibility and strictly increases \(F\), contradicting optimality. Hence

\[ \sum_ta^*_{tg}=1. \]

Second, if \(a^*_{tg}>0\), then \(h_{tg}=P_g\). Indeed, for any other buyer \(s\), transfer an amount \(\varepsilon>0\) of good \(g\) from \(t\) to \(s\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), allocations remain nonnegative and all surpluses remain positive. The directional derivative of \(F\) at \(\varepsilon=0\) is

\[ h_{sg}-h_{tg}. \]

Optimality requires this derivative to be nonpositive. Therefore \(h_{tg}\ge h_{sg}\) for every \(s\), proving the assertion.

Now return to the original variables:

\[ x^*_{tg}=\frac{q_ga^*_{tg}}{\mu_t}, \qquad z^*_t=\sum_gU_{tg}x^*_{tg}, \qquad p_g=\frac{P_g}{Hq_g}. \]

Since \(S_t=D(z^*_t-c_t)\), substitution gives

\[ h_{tg} = \frac{H\mu_t\beta_t\cdot Dq_gU_{tg}/\mu_t} {D(z^*_t-c_t)} = Hq_g\frac{\beta_tU_{tg}}{z^*_t-c_t}. \]

Consequently,

\[ p_g= \max_t\frac{\beta_tU_{tg}}{z^*_t-c_t}>0. \]

It follows that

\[ \frac{U_{tg}}{p_g} \le \frac{z^*_t-c_t}{\beta_t}. \]

Equality holds whenever \(x^*_{tg}>0\), because this is equivalent to \(a^*_{tg}>0\). Finally,

\[ \sum_t\mu_tx^*_{tg} = q_g\sum_ta^*_{tg} = q_g. \]

Thus \(x^*,z^*,p\) satisfy every requested equilibrium condition.

5. Encoding length and running time

Each rational \(V_{tg}=q_gU_{tg}/\mu_t\) and \(w_t=\mu_t\beta_t\) has binary encoding length bounded by a constant times \(L\).

For a product of positive integer denominators, the binary length is at most the sum of their binary lengths. Therefore \(D\) and \(H\), and all integer coefficients \(A_{tg},C_t,W_t\), have encoding lengths polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Computing these coefficients uses polynomial-time integer arithmetic.

The transformed instance has \(\tau\) buyers, \(m\) goods, and at most \(m\tau\) positive utility entries. Its total encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), so the finite-buyer exact algorithm runs in polynomial time in those parameters.

The returned rational allocation has polynomial output length. Recovering \(x^*,z^*\) and the prices above requires only polynomially many rational arithmetic operations on numbers of polynomial encoding length.

Hence the requested exact allocation and supporting prices, or an infeasibility report, can be computed in polynomial time.
