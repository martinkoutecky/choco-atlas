The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item categories, three multi-demand types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and rational values and caps, does there exist a state-based nonnegative pricing policy, computable from residual type masses and supplies, such that every measurable arrival order and every utility-maximizing tie choice by infinitesimal mass yields terminal welfare equal to the configuration-LP optimum? If so, can such a policy be constructed and evaluated in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau=3\), and input bit length \(L\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=3\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) categories, binary-rational supplies \(q_j\geq0\), binary-rational values \(w_{tj}\geq0\), and integer demand caps \(1\leq k_t\leq m\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A configuration is any subset \(S\subseteq[m]\), containing at most one unit of each category. Its multi-demand value \(v_t(S)\) is the sum of its \(\min\{k_t,|S|\}\) largest item values. Configurations larger than the cap remain permissible; the cap limits value, not purchases. Mass \(d\alpha\) receiving \(S\) consumes \(d\alpha\) of every category in \(S\) and contributes \(v_t(S)d\alpha\) to welfare.
Define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max\{\sum_{t,S}v_t(S)x_{tS}:x_{tS}\geq0,\ \sum_Sx_{tS}=\mu_t,\ \sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{tS}\leq q_j\}\), including the empty configuration. Unsold supply is allowed; impose no equality between aggregate demand and supply.
A policy is a finitely described measurable map \(P(r,Q)\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m\), depending on the input and residual masses and supplies, chosen before the next arriving type is revealed. At state \((r,Q)\), type \(t\) may choose any configuration maximizing \(v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}P_j(r,Q)\) among \(S\subseteq\{j:Q_j>0\}\), with arbitrary ties.
An arrival order is a measurable map \(a:[0,1]\to[3]\) with \(\lambda(a^{-1}(t))=\mu_t\), where \(\lambda\) is Lebesgue measure. An execution is a measurable configuration choice \(S(s)\) and nonnegative absolutely continuous residual state starting at \((\mu,q)\), satisfying \(\dot r_t(s)=-\mathbf1_{\{a(s)=t\}}\), \(\dot Q_j(s)=-\mathbf1_{\{j\in S(s)\}}\), and the demand condition almost everywhere.
A policy must admit an execution through time \(1\) for every arrival order. It is successful if every such execution, under every measurable demanded configuration choice, has welfare \(\int_0^1v_{a(s)}(S(s))\,ds=\mathrm{OPT}\). Its description must define it on real residual states; computational evaluation means exact rational prices at rational states, whose encoding length is denoted \(B\).

Is deciding whether a successful policy exists in \(\mathrm P\), or is it NP-hard?
In the polynomial-time case, can a successful policy on every yes-instance be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and evaluated in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,B\)?

For finite markets with at most three multi-demand buyers, optimal dynamic prices exist and can be computed in polynomial time, guaranteeing optimal welfare for every arrival order and tie choice ([Berger–Eden–Feldman](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06863)).
Efficient optimal dynamic pricing is also known for arbitrarily many finite bi-demand buyers ([Bérczi–Bérczi-Kovács–Szögi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05131)). No result settling the continuum policy problem defined here was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the unspecified rational caps to positive integers, truncated at the number of categories, following the discrete multi-demand definition.
  • Specified nonnegative rational values and supplies, positive normalized masses, and at most one unit of each category per configuration.
  • Retained purchases beyond the valuation cap and arbitrary utility ties, including ties with the empty configuration.
  • Defined the configuration-LP benchmark using masses assigned to all subsets, with supply inequalities and unsold inventory permitted; imposed no balanced-market restriction.
  • Formalized infinitesimal arrivals and inventory consumption as measurable orders and absolutely continuous executions, with exhausted categories unavailable.
  • Required an execution for every arrival order to exclude vacuous welfare guarantees arising from nonexistent trajectories.
  • Specified a measurable policy on real residual states and exact rational-state evaluation, accounting separately for the residual state's encoding length.

1. Result

Every valid instance admits a successful policy. Thus the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm P\). A successful policy can be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and evaluated exactly at rational residual states in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,B\).

The proof actually works for every finite support size \(\tau\), including the stipulated \(\tau=3\).

The main idea is to select prices from the relative interior of an optimal dual face. Every demanded configuration then has the correct welfare derivative for every dual solution in that face. We prove both welfare optimality of all executions and existence of an execution for each measurable arrival order.

2. A compact dual for residual welfare

For any nonnegative real residual state \(z=(r,Q)\), let \(F(z)\) denote the configuration-LP optimum with masses \(r\) and supplies \(Q\). In particular, \(F(\mu,q)=\mathrm{OPT}\).

This LP is feasible, since all residual mass can receive the empty configuration. Its feasible set is compact: every variable satisfies \(0\le x_{tS}\le r_t\). Consequently, its optimum is attained.

By finite-dimensional LP duality,

\(F(r,Q)=\min\left\{\sum_t r_tu_t+\sum_jQ_jp_j:\ p_j\ge0,\quad u_t+\sum_{j\in S}p_j\ge v_t(S)\ \text{for every }t,S\right\}.\)

The constraint for the empty configuration implies \(u_t\ge0\). Define

\(D_t(p)=\max_{S\subseteq[m]}\left(v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j\right).\)

Thus the dual constraints are precisely \(p\ge0\) and \(u_t\ge D_t(p)\).

2.1. Computing the demand utility

Because prices are nonnegative, the maximum defining \(D_t(p)\) can be attained by a configuration of size at most \(k_t\). Indeed, from any configuration \(S\), retain its \(\min\{k_t,|S|\}\) highest-valued items, obtaining \(T\). Then

\(v_t(T)=v_t(S),\qquad \sum_{j\in T}p_j\le\sum_{j\in S}p_j.\)

Therefore,

\(D_t(p)=\max_{\substack{T\subseteq[m]\\ |T|\le k_t}}\sum_{j\in T}(w_{tj}-p_j).\)

For any \(\eta\ge0\) and any such \(T\),

\(\sum_{j\in T}(w_{tj}-p_j)\le k_t\eta+\sum_j(w_{tj}-p_j-\eta)_+,\)

where \(x_+=\max\{x,0\}\).

Equality is attained by taking \(\eta\) to be the \(k_t\)-th largest member of the list \((w_{tj}-p_j)_+\). To verify this, write the list in decreasing order as \(\gamma_{(1)},\ldots,\gamma_{(m)}\) and set \(\eta=\gamma_{(k_t)}\). Then

\(k_t\eta+\sum_j(\gamma_j-\eta)_+=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_t}\gamma_{(\ell)}.\)

Hence

\(D_t(p)=\min_{\eta\ge0}\left(k_t\eta+\sum_j(w_{tj}-p_j-\eta)_+\right).\)

This identity concerns the maximum utility. Oversized configurations remain permissible, and the subsequent proof applies to every demanded configuration, including oversized ones.

2.2. A fixed bounded dual polytope

Set

\(V=\max_t\sum_jw_{tj},\qquad M=V+1.\)

For every \(p\ge0\), we have \(0\le D_t(p)\le V\).

An optimal configuration-dual solution can be chosen with \(0\le p_j,u_t\le M\). To see this, start with an optimal dual solution and replace each \(p_j>M\) by \(M\). Since \(w_{tj}<M\) for every type, the corresponding adjusted item value remains negative. The formula above shows that this replacement leaves every \(D_t(p)\) unchanged. Next replace each \(u_t>M\) by \(M\); feasibility is preserved because \(D_t(p)\le V<M\). Both replacements weakly decrease the objective, whose coefficients are nonnegative. Optimality therefore remains intact.

Introduce auxiliary variables \(\eta_t,\xi_{tj}\), and let \(H\) be the fixed rational polytope consisting of \(h=(p,u,\eta,\xi)\) satisfying:

  • \(0\le p_j,u_t,\eta_t,\xi_{tj}\le M\);
  • \(p_j+\eta_t+\xi_{tj}\ge w_{tj}\);
  • \(u_t\ge k_t\eta_t+\sum_j\xi_{tj}\).

Its projection onto \((p,u)\) is exactly

\(\left\{(p,u)\in[0,M]^{m+\tau}:u_t\ge D_t(p)\ \text{for every }t\right\}.\)

One inclusion follows from the demand-utility formula. For the other, choose the threshold \(\eta_t\) described above and set \(\xi_{tj}=(w_{tj}-p_j-\eta_t)_+\). These auxiliary variables are at most \(V\), and their expression equals \(D_t(p)\).

For \(h\in H\), define

\(\ell_h(r,Q)=\sum_t r_tu_t+\sum_jQ_jp_j.\)

We have proved

\(F(z)=\min_{h\in H}\ell_h(z).\)

The polytope \(H\) has \(n=m+2\tau+\tau m\) variables and polynomially many inequalities, all with encoding length polynomial in the input size.

Since \(H\) is compact, \(F\) is the minimum of finitely many linear functions, one for each vertex of \(H\). Moreover,

\(|F(z)-F(z')|\le M\|z-z'\|_1.\)

Thus \(F\) is Lipschitz on the nonnegative residual-state space.

3. A canonical relative-interior selector

For each residual state, define its optimal dual face

\(G(z)=\{h\in H:\ell_h(z)=F(z)\}.\)

We select a point in the relative interior of this face using a construction that depends only on the face itself.

Write all defining inequalities of \(H\) as \(g_i(h)\ge0\), for \(i=1,\ldots,R\), with each \(g_i\) affine. Fix a coordinate order for lexicographic minimization.

For a nonempty face \(G\) of \(H\), let:

  • \(h_0(G)\) be the lexicographically smallest point of \(G\);
  • \(h_i(G)\) be the lexicographically smallest maximizer of \(g_i\) over \(G\).

Define

\(\bar h(G)=\frac{h_0(G)+\sum_{i=1}^R h_i(G)}{R+1}.\)

All these points exist and are unique because the relevant sets are nonempty compact polytopes.

If \(g_i\) vanishes everywhere on \(G\), it also vanishes at \(\bar h(G)\). Otherwise its maximum on \(G\) is positive, so \(g_i(\bar h(G))>0\). Consequently, every inequality not identically tight on \(G\) is strict at \(\bar h(G)\). A sufficiently small relative neighborhood of this point in the affine hull of \(G\) therefore lies in \(G\). This proves

\(\bar h(G)\in\operatorname{relint}G.\)

Define the policy by taking its price coordinates:

\(P(r,Q)=p\bigl(\bar h(G(r,Q))\bigr).\)

This definition uses only the residual state and the input.

3.1. Measurability

The polytope \(H\) has finitely many faces. For each face, the states at which it is exactly the optimal face are described by finitely many linear equalities and strict inequalities comparing vertex objectives.

These state sets are Borel. The selector is constant on each such set, so the policy is a finitely described Borel measurable map on real residual states.

3.2. A relative-interior fact

We will use the following elementary fact.

Lemma. If an affine function \(\phi\) is nonnegative on a convex set \(G\), and \(\phi(\bar h)=0\) at a point \(\bar h\in\operatorname{relint}G\), then \(\phi\) vanishes everywhere on \(G\).

Proof. If \(\phi(h_1)>0\) at some \(h_1\in G\), relative interior implies that, for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),

\(h_2=\bar h+\varepsilon(\bar h-h_1)\in G.\)

But then \(\phi(h_2)=-\varepsilon\phi(h_1)<0\), a contradiction. ∎

4. Every demanded configuration is tight throughout the optimal face

Lemma. Let \(z=(r,Q)\), suppose \(r_t>0\), and let \(S\) be any configuration demanded by type \(t\) at prices \(P(z)\), among the available categories. Then, for every \(h=(p,u,\eta,\xi)\in G(z)\),

\(u_t+\sum_{j\in S}p_j=v_t(S).\)

Proof. Write \(\bar h=\bar h(G(z))\), and let \(x\) be an optimal residual configuration allocation. Its primal-dual gap with \(\bar h\) is

\(\ell_{\bar h}(z)-F(z)=\sum_{t,T}x_{tT}\left(\bar u_t+\sum_{j\in T}\bar p_j-v_t(T)\right)+\sum_j\bar p_j\left(Q_j-\sum_{t,T:j\in T}x_{tT}\right)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative.

Since \(r_t>0\), some \(T\) has \(x_{tT}>0\). Such a configuration uses only available categories: if \(Q_j=0\), feasibility forces every allocation containing \(j\) to have zero mass. The gap identity implies

\(v_t(T)-\sum_{j\in T}\bar p_j=\bar u_t.\)

Every configuration has utility at most \(\bar u_t\) by dual feasibility. Therefore the maximum utility among available configurations equals \(\bar u_t\), and the demanded configuration \(S\) satisfies

\(\bar u_t+\sum_{j\in S}\bar p_j-v_t(S)=0.\)

The affine function

\(\phi(h)=u_t+\sum_{j\in S}p_j-v_t(S)\)

is nonnegative throughout \(H\), and hence throughout \(G(z)\). Applying the relative-interior lemma proves the claim. ∎

This lemma is the central property of the policy. It holds for every demanded tie choice.

5. Every execution has optimal welfare

Consider any execution through time \(1\), with any admissible measurable arrival order and configuration choices. Write its residual state as \(z(s)=(r(s),Q(s))\).

At almost every interior time, the execution equations hold and the residual state is differentiable. At such a time, the arriving type \(t=a(s)\) has \(r_t(s)>0\). Indeed, a differentiable nonnegative function has derivative zero wherever it equals zero at an interior point, whereas the execution equation gives \(\dot r_t(s)=-1\).

Let \(S=S(s)\). The preceding lemma yields, for every \(h\in G(z(s))\),

\(\frac{d}{ds}\ell_h(z(s))=-u_t-\sum_{j\in S}p_j=-v_t(S).\)

Recall that \(F\) is the minimum of the finitely many vertex objectives. At a point where \(z\) is differentiable, each active vertex objective has the same first-order change, namely \(-v_t(S)\). Inactive vertices have strictly positive objective gaps and cannot affect the first-order minimum. Consequently,

\(\frac{d}{ds}F(z(s))=-v_{a(s)}(S(s))\)

almost everywhere.

Because \(F\) is Lipschitz and \(z\) is absolutely continuous, \(F\circ z\) is absolutely continuous. Integrating gives

\(\int_0^1v_{a(s)}(S(s))\,ds=F(z(0))-F(z(1)).\)

The arrival-order constraints imply \(r(1)=0\). With no residual mass, the residual welfare optimum is zero regardless of unsold supply, so \(F(z(1))=0\). Therefore

\(\int_0^1v_{a(s)}(S(s))\,ds=F(\mu,q)=\mathrm{OPT}.\)

This proves the required welfare guarantee for every execution. It remains to establish execution existence.

6. Every measurable arrival order admits an execution

Fix an arbitrary measurable arrival order \(a\) with the prescribed type masses. We construct an execution in finitely many phases.

Suppose a phase starts at time \(s_0<1\) in a nonnegative state \(z_0=(r_0,Q_0)\) consistent with the preceding execution. Set \(G=G(z_0)\).

For each type with \(r_{0t}>0\), choose one demanded configuration \(S_t\) at prices \(P(z_0)\), using available categories. These configurations exist because the choice set is finite and includes the empty configuration.

For a type with \(r_{0t}=0\), its remaining arrival set has measure zero: consistency gives

\(r_{0t}=\mu_t-\lambda\{s<s_0:a(s)=t\}.\)

For such a type, set \(S_t=\varnothing\); this choice cannot affect the execution on a set of positive measure.

Define the cumulative arrivals during this phase by

\(c_t(s)=\int_{s_0}^{s}\mathbf1_{\{a(u)=t\}}\,du.\)

The tentative state trajectory is

\(r_t(s)=r_{0t}-c_t(s),\qquad Q_j(s)=Q_{0j}-\sum_{t:j\in S_t}c_t(s).\)

These functions are absolutely continuous. Coordinates initially equal to zero remain zero: exhausted types have no remaining arrival mass, and the selected configurations avoid exhausted categories.

By the lemma in Section 4, for every \(h\in G\),

\(\ell_h(z(s))=F(z_0)-\sum_t c_t(s)v_t(S_t).\)

Here types with zero residual mass contribute nothing. Thus all members of \(G\) retain the same objective along the tentative trajectory.

Choose any \(h^\ast\in G\). For every vertex \(h\) of \(H\) outside \(G\), define its gap

\(\Delta_h(s)=\ell_h(z(s))-\ell_{h^\ast}(z(s)).\)

Each gap is continuous and strictly positive at \(s_0\).

End the phase at the first of the following events:

  • a residual coordinate positive at \(s_0\) reaches zero;
  • some \(\Delta_h\) reaches zero;
  • time \(1\) is reached.

Unless \(s_0=1\), the phase has positive duration. There are finitely many initially positive coordinates and finitely many initially positive vertex gaps, all continuous.

Before the endpoint, the state is nonnegative, its positive-coordinate pattern is unchanged, and every outside vertex gap remains positive. Hence its optimal face is exactly \(G\). The policy therefore keeps the same prices throughout the phase, and the configurations \(S_t\) remain demanded.

At the endpoint, all gaps and coordinates are nonnegative. Thus the new optimal face contains \(G\). If an outside vertex gap has reached zero, this containment is proper.

Now start the next phase using the new state.

6.1. The construction terminates after finitely many phases

There are two kinds of nonterminal events:

  • a previously positive residual coordinate becomes zero;
  • the optimal dual face strictly enlarges.

Once a residual coordinate becomes zero, it remains zero. Therefore there are at most \(m+\tau\) events of the first kind.

The optimal faces form a nested chain. Each proper inclusion of faces increases affine dimension, and \(H\) has dimension at most \(n\). Therefore there are at most \(n\) events of the second kind.

Every nonterminal phase ends with at least one of these events. Consequently, the construction reaches time \(1\) after at most \(n+m+\tau+1\) phases.

The resulting configuration choice is measurable: on each phase it is a fixed function of the measurable arriving type. The residual trajectory is nonnegative and absolutely continuous, with the required derivatives. Demand holds throughout phase interiors; the finitely many endpoints are irrelevant to the almost-everywhere condition.

We have therefore constructed an execution through time \(1\) for the arbitrary arrival order \(a\).

Together with Section 5, this proves that the policy is successful.

7. Polynomial construction and exact evaluation

The policy description consists of the rational polytope \(H\), the fixed coordinate order, and the selector algorithm of Section 3. Its size and construction time are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

At a rational residual state of encoding length \(B\), evaluate the policy as follows:

  • Solve the rational LP minimizing \(\ell_h(r,Q)\) over \(H\).
  • Add the equality fixing its objective to the optimal value, thereby describing \(G(r,Q)\).
  • Compute \(h_0(G)\).
  • For each defining inequality, maximize its slack over \(G\) and compute the lexicographically smallest maximizer.
  • Average these points and return the price coordinates.

Lexicographic minimization requires at most \(n\) ordinary LP optimizations, fixing one coordinate after another. Hence the total number of LP calls is polynomial.

We invoke the standard exact rational-LP theorem: an LP with rational, finitely encoded data can be optimized in polynomial bit time, with a rational optimizer of polynomial encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here: the number of variables and constraints is polynomial, the coefficient encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,B\), and all optimization sets are nonempty compact polytopes.

The average therefore gives exact rational prices, with polynomial encoding length, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,B\). No enumeration of faces or vertices is needed by the evaluator; those finite sets were used only in the proof.

The finite-market results cited in the question concern indivisible buyers: [Berger–Eden–Feldman](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06863) and [Bérczi–Bérczi-Kovács–Szögi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05131). The argument above establishes the continuum guarantee directly through the residual configuration LP and absolute continuity, while permitting unsold supply and arbitrary demanded ties.
