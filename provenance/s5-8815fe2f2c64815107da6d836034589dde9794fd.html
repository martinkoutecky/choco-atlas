The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(d\ge 1\) and a finite-support society \(\mu=\sum_t\mu_t\delta_{x_t}\) with \(x_t\in\mathbb{Q}^d\), define weighted RR-CWM by Haar-rotating the support, taking weighted coordinatewise medians, and rotating back; does this replication-compatible strategyproof mechanism satisfy \(C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M),C_{\mathrm{post}}(\mu,M)\le 2r(\mu)\), and is its worst-case factor exactly \(2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(d\ge1\). A society is given by its support: \(\mu=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\delta_{x_t}\), with distinct \(x_t\in\mathbb Q^d\), positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length and \(m=d\).
For a scalar distribution, its weighted median interval consists of the points having mass at most \(1/2\) strictly on either side. Select either endpoint with probability \(1/2\), independently across coordinates conditional on the rotation; a singleton interval gives a deterministic median.
Weighted RR-CWM \(M\) samples a Haar-uniform \(R\in SO(d)\), takes these medians of the coordinates of \(R x_t\), and returns \(Y=R^\top h\). Splitting a mass into colocated pieces leaves its output law unchanged.
Define \(r(\mu)=\min_{z\in\mathbb R^d}\max_t\|x_t-z\|_2\), \(C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M)=\max_t\mathbb E\|x_t-Y\|_2\), and \(C_{\mathrm{post}}(\mu,M)=\mathbb E\max_t\|x_t-Y\|_2\). Expectations include all mechanism randomness; when \(r(\mu)=0\), a ratio is \(1\) for zero cost and infinite otherwise.

For every fixed \(d\ge1\), does weighted RR-CWM satisfy \(C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M),C_{\mathrm{post}}(\mu,M)\le2r(\mu)\) for every society, and is \(\sup_{\mu:r(\mu)>0}C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M)/r(\mu)=\sup_{\mu:r(\mu)>0}C_{\mathrm{post}}(\mu,M)/r(\mu)=2\)?

For finite agent multisets, RR-CWM has tight factor \(2\) for both objectives in every dimension, with independent fair choices between the two central order statistics when the population is even. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14499v2)
No separate result explicitly treating the weighted society formulation was located in the search.

  • Defined the optimum radius and both costs using maxima over positive-mass support, rather than mass-weighted averages.

Result

Yes. For every fixed \(d\ge 1\), both costs are at most \(2r(\mu)\), and both suprema equal \(2\). In fact, a society with two support points attains both ratios exactly.

The upper bound follows by an exact transfer from the finite-agent theorem stated in the question. The essential point is that replication preserves the prescribed endpoint randomization, including ties.

1. Exact representation by a finite multiset

Write each mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), where \(a_t,b_t\) are positive integers. Choose a positive common multiple \(D\) of the denominators and set \(N=2D\). Then

\[ n_t:=N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}n_t=N. \]

Let \(P\) be the multiset containing \(n_t\) copies of \(x_t\). Its uniform empirical probability measure is exactly \(\mu\).

We next verify that weighted RR-CWM and finite-agent RR-CWM have identical outputs when coupled using the same rotation and coordinate coins.

2. Median intervals and central order statistics

Fix any rotation \(R\in SO(d)\) and coordinate \(j\). List the corresponding coordinates of the replicated multiset in nondecreasing order, retaining multiplicities:

\[ a_1\le \cdots\le a_N, \qquad N=2k. \]

For every real \(z\), equality of the empirical measures gives

\[ \sum_{t:(Rx_t)_j<z}\mu_t = \frac{\#\{i:a_i<z\}}{N}, \qquad \sum_{t:(Rx_t)_j>z}\mu_t = \frac{\#\{i:a_i>z\}}{N}. \]

Claim. The weighted median interval is exactly \([a_k,a_{k+1}]\).

Proof. If \(a_k\le z\le a_{k+1}\), the first \(k\) observations are at most \(z\), and the last \(k\) observations are at least \(z\). Consequently, at most \(k\) observations lie strictly on either side, so both corresponding masses are at most \(1/2\).

Conversely, if \(z<a_k\), observations indexed \(k,\ldots,2k\) all exceed \(z\), giving strictly more than half the mass to its right. If \(z>a_{k+1}\), observations indexed \(1,\ldots,k+1\) all lie below \(z\), giving strictly more than half the mass to its left. Thus neither case is a weighted median. This also proves the claim when order statistics coincide. \(\square\)

Therefore, choosing either weighted-median endpoint fairly is exactly choosing between the two central order statistics fairly. When they coincide, both procedures are deterministic. Using the same independent coordinate coins gives identical median vectors for every rotation, and hence identical outputs after applying \(R^\top\).

Thus the two mechanisms have exactly the same output law; no approximation or limiting argument is involved.

3. Transfer of the upper bounds

The finite-agent result supplied in the question states that Haar-uniform RR-CWM on \(SO(d)\), with independent fair central-order-statistic choices for even populations, satisfies both egalitarian upper bounds with factor \(2\). These conventions are explicitly given in Definition 4 and Algorithm 4, and the bound is Theorem 21 of the [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14499v2#S7).

All its hypotheses hold for \(P\): it is a finite multiset in \(\mathbb R^d\), its population is even, and its rotation and median randomization agree exactly with those just coupled.

Replication does not change the enclosing radius:

\[ r(P)=\min_z\max_{p\in P}\|p-z\|_2 =\min_z\max_t\|x_t-z\|_2 =r(\mu). \]

Because every support point occurs in \(P\), replication also leaves both maximum-cost objectives unchanged. Together with equality of output laws, the finite-agent theorem therefore yields

\[ C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M)\le 2r(\mu), \qquad C_{\mathrm{post}}(\mu,M)\le 2r(\mu). \]

The replicated population may be large relative to the binary input length \(L\). This is an existence argument transferring an inequality, not a claim that explicitly constructing \(P\) is computationally efficient.

If \(r(\mu)=0\), all support points coincide: indeed, any two support points have distance at most \(2r(\mu)\). Distinctness then forces \(\tau=1\). Every coordinate median equals the sole rotated support point, so the mechanism returns that point and both costs are zero. The specified ratio is therefore \(1\).

4. Exact attainment of both ratios

Fix any \(d\ge 1\), let \(e_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)\), and take the admissible society

\[ \mu=\frac34\delta_0+\frac14\delta_{e_1}. \]

For every rotation and coordinate, mass at least \(3/4\) is at zero. Its scalar median interval is exactly \(\{0\}\): any positive candidate has more than half the mass strictly to its left, any negative candidate has more than half strictly to its right, and zero satisfies both median inequalities.

Consequently, \(Y=0\) deterministically, and

\[ C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M) =C_{\mathrm{post}}(\mu,M) =\|e_1\|_2=1. \]

For any center \(z\), the triangle inequality gives

\[ 1=\|e_1\|_2 \le \|z\|_2+\|e_1-z\|_2 \le 2\max\{\|z\|_2,\|e_1-z\|_2\}. \]

Hence \(r(\mu)\ge 1/2\); the center \(e_1/2\) attains radius \(1/2\). Both ratios are therefore exactly \(2\). Combined with the upper bounds, this proves the two asserted supremum equalities.
