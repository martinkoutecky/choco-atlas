The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite \(C\), \(k\), \(T\subseteq 2^{C}\), and rational \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{T}\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), compute \(W\subseteq C\) with \(|W|=k\) such that for every \(\ell\in[k]\) and \(x\) satisfying \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_t>\ell/(k+1)\), and \(\left(\bigcap_{x_t>0}t\right)\setminus W\ne\varnothing\), some \(t\) with \(x_t>0\) has \(|t\cap W|\ge\ell\); equivalently, compute a committee returned by mass-Droop-GJCR using \(g_{W,\ell}(c)=\sum_{t:\,c\in t,\ |t\cap W|\le\ell-1}\mu_t\) and trigger \(g_{W,\ell}(c)>\ell/(k+1)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a finite candidate set with \(m=|C|\), and let \(1\le k\le m\) be an integer.
A society is given by its support: a list \(T\subseteq 2^C\) of \(\tau=|T|\) approval types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Empty approval types are allowed; \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A subgroup is a vector \(x\in\mathbb R^T\) with \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\). A committee \(W\subseteq C\), \(|W|=k\), satisfies mass-Droop-EJR+ if, for every integer \(1\le\ell\le k\) and every subgroup \(x\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}x_t>\ell/(k+1)\) and \((\bigcap_{t:x_t>0}t)\setminus W\ne\varnothing\), some type \(t\) with \(x_t>0\) satisfies \(|t\cap W|\ge\ell\).

Is finding a committee satisfying mass-Droop-EJR+ polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For explicitly listed discrete voters, Droop GJCR is polynomial-time computable and always returns a size-\(k\) committee satisfying Droop-EJR+. [Casey and Elkind](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00811v1)
No result settling this exact computational problem with binary rational masses was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Require an integer committee size with \(1\le k\le |C|\), so the requested committee exists within the candidate domain.
  • Remove zero-mass types from the supplied list to implement the required positive-mass support convention; retain empty approval types, which the discrete ballot domain permits.
  • Allow real subgroup masses, making explicit that a subgroup may contain any fraction of a type's mass.
  • Use the primary search problem of finding any committee satisfying the stated axiom; do not impose the additional requirement that the committee be obtainable through a particular greedy rule.

Result

Finding a committee satisfying mass-Droop-EJR+ is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

The algorithm is a weighted implementation of Droop GJCR from [Casey and Elkind, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00811v1#S3.SS2). Below, we prove correctness directly for masses and establish polynomial bit complexity without expanding the society into individual voters.

1. A finite characterization of violations

For any candidate set \(U\subseteq C\), define the representation of type \(t\) by \(r_t(U)=|t\cap U|\). For \(c\in C\setminus U\) and \(1\le\ell\le k\), let

\[ S(c,\ell,U)=\{t\in T:c\in t,\ r_t(U)<\ell\}, \qquad M(c,\ell,U)=\sum_{t\in S(c,\ell,U)}\mu_t. \]

Lemma 1. A size-\(k\) committee \(W\) satisfies mass-Droop-EJR+ if and only if \(M(c,\ell,W)\le\ell/(k+1)\) for every \(c\in C\setminus W\) and \(1\le\ell\le k\).

Proof. Suppose \(M(c,\ell,W)>\ell/(k+1)\). Set \(x_t=\mu_t\) for \(t\in S(c,\ell,W)\), and set \(x_t=0\) otherwise. This is an admissible subgroup exceeding the required mass threshold. All its positive types approve the unelected candidate \(c\), and every such type has representation below \(\ell\). Thus it violates the axiom.

Conversely, suppose a subgroup \(x\) violates the axiom at level \(\ell\). Choose \(c\in(\bigcap_{t:x_t>0}t)\setminus W\). Every type with \(x_t>0\) belongs to \(S(c,\ell,W)\), because every such type has representation below \(\ell\). Consequently,

\[ M(c,\ell,W)\ge\sum_{t:x_t>0}\mu_t \ge\sum_{t\in T}x_t>\frac{\ell}{k+1}. \]

This proves the equivalence. \(\square\)

In particular, fractional subgroups require no continuous optimization: a violation exists precisely when one of these finitely many full-type sums exceeds its threshold.

2. The algorithm

Fix any ordering of the candidates. Initialize \(W=\varnothing\). Process the levels \(\ell=k,k-1,\ldots,1\) in that order.

At level \(\ell\), repeatedly perform the following operation:

  • Find a candidate \(c\in C\setminus W\) with \(M(c,\ell,W)>\ell/(k+1)\).
  • If one exists, add the first such candidate to \(W\).
  • Otherwise, finish this level and proceed to the next.

After all levels finish, add arbitrary unelected candidates until \(|W|=k\).

We must prove that the greedy phase never selects more than \(k\) candidates, so the completion step is well defined.

3. The greedy phase selects at most \(k\) candidates

We use charges only to analyze the algorithm; it does not compute them.

Whenever the algorithm selects a candidate \(c\) at level \(\ell\), write \(S=S(c,\ell,W)\) for the supporting types immediately before selection, and let \(s=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\). Charge each \(t\in S\) an amount \(\mu_t/s\). Types outside \(S\) receive no charge.

Each selected candidate therefore generates total charge exactly \(1\). The charge per unit mass to a participating type is \(1/s\). Since selection requires \(s>\ell/(k+1)\), this charge satisfies

\[ \frac1s<\frac{k+1}{\ell}. \]

Lemma 2. At every prefix of the greedy phase, each type’s cumulative charge per unit mass is strictly below \(k+1\).

Proof. Fix a prefix and a type \(t\). If \(t\) has received no charge, the assertion holds.

Otherwise, consider its last charge within that prefix, incurred at some level \(\ell\). Immediately before that selection, eligibility gives \(r_t(W)\le\ell-1\). Every earlier charge to \(t\) came from a distinct selected candidate that \(t\) approves. Hence \(t\) had received at most \(\ell-1\) earlier charges, and at most \(\ell\) charges including this last one.

Because the algorithm processes levels in decreasing order, every one of these charges occurred at a level \(h\ge\ell\). Each charge per unit mass was therefore strictly below

\[ \frac{k+1}{h}\le\frac{k+1}{\ell}. \]

The cumulative charge per unit mass is strictly below \(\ell\cdot(k+1)/\ell=k+1\). There are no later charges within the chosen prefix, by the choice of the last charge. \(\square\)

Now suppose a prefix has selected \(q\) candidates, and let \(b_t\) denote type \(t\)’s cumulative charge per unit mass. Summing charges by candidates and then by types gives

\[ q=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_t <\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t(k+1)=k+1. \]

The strict inequality follows from Lemma 2, positivity of the masses, and their sum being \(1\). Since \(q\) is an integer, \(q\le k\).

Thus every prefix contains at most \(k\) selections. In particular, the greedy phase selects at most \(k\) candidates, and padding to size \(k\) is possible because \(m\ge k\).

4. Correctness of the final committee

Let \(V\) be the final committee, including padding. Fix \(1\le\ell\le k\), and let \(W_\ell\) be the selected set when processing level \(\ell\) finished.

The stopping condition at that level guarantees

\[ M(c,\ell,W_\ell)\le\frac{\ell}{k+1} \quad\text{for every }c\in C\setminus W_\ell. \]

Consider any \(c\in C\setminus V\). Since \(W_\ell\subseteq V\), the candidate \(c\) was also outside \(W_\ell\). Moreover, adding candidates cannot decrease a type’s representation, so \(r_t(V)\ge r_t(W_\ell)\) for every \(t\). Therefore

\[ S(c,\ell,V)\subseteq S(c,\ell,W_\ell), \qquad M(c,\ell,V)\le M(c,\ell,W_\ell) \le\frac{\ell}{k+1}. \]

This holds for every required \(c\) and \(\ell\). Lemma 1 proves that \(V\) satisfies mass-Droop-EJR+.

Empty approval types cause no difficulty: they belong to no supporting set and receive no charges.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=p_t/q_t\), where \(p_t,q_t\) are positive binary integers. Compute a common denominator and integer weights:

\[ D=\prod_{t\in T}q_t, \qquad a_t=p_t(D/q_t). \]

These are exact integers, with \(\mu_t=a_t/D\) and \(\sum_t a_t=D\). The bit length of \(D\) is at most \(1+\sum_t\log_2 q_t\), hence \(O(L)\). Since \(0<a_t\le D\), the weights also have \(O(L)\) bits.

Every selection test can now be implemented by the integer comparison

\[ (k+1)\sum_{t\in S(c,\ell,W)}a_t>\ell D. \]

All integers in these comparisons have \(O(L+\log k)\) bits. In particular, an arbitrarily small positive excess over the Droop threshold is detected exactly.

Maintain the counts \(r_t(W)\), updating them after each selection. A complete candidate scan uses \(O(m\tau)\) arithmetic operations. Each successful scan selects one candidate, and each unsuccessful scan finishes one level. There are at most \(k\) successful scans and exactly \(k\) level-ending scans. Thus the greedy phase uses \(O(km\tau)\) arithmetic operations on integers of polynomial bit length.

Constructing the integer weights, updating counts, and padding the committee also take polynomial time. Consequently, the entire algorithm runs in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

The charges used in the proof are never computed, and no population of \(D\) individual voters is constructed.
