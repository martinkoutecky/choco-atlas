The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) of size \(\tau\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supply \(0<\lambda\le K\), and explicit rational monotone valuation tables \(v_t:\{0,\ldots,K\}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), find a competitive bundling equilibrium in time polynomial in \(K,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the input bit length. A bundle has an integral size \(j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), mass \(q_j\), and price \(p_j\), with \(\sum_j jq_j=\lambda\). Each type’s mass may split across integral package-choice vectors \(z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^K\) with \(\sum_j jz_j\le K\); every positive-mass choice must maximize \(v_t(\sum_j jz_j)-\sum_jp_jz_j\), and the assignment must clear each package type. The equilibrium welfare should be at least an \(1/O(\log(2+\min\{K,\tau\}))\) fraction of \(\mathrm{OPT}\), the maximum of \(\sum_{t,k}y_{t,k}v_t(k)\) over \(y_{t,k}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_k y_{t,k}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,k}ky_{t,k}=\lambda\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains an integer \(K\ge1\), binary rational per-capita supply \(0<\lambda\le K\), and explicit monotone valuation tables \(v_t:\{0,\ldots,K\}\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) with \(v_t(0)=0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; \(K\) is the quantity parameter replacing \(m\), and running times are measured in \(K,\tau,L\).
A bundling consists of offered sizes \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}\), positive rational package masses \(q_j\), and nonnegative rational anonymous prices \(p_j\), satisfying \(\sum_{j\in J}jq_j=\lambda\). Packages cannot be opened or resold.
An individual's feasible choices are \(Z_J=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{J}:\sum_{j\in J}jz_j\le K\}\), including the empty choice. Only offered sizes may be purchased; their aggregate masses do not bound an individual's choice.
An equilibrium assigns rational masses \(x_{t,z}\ge0\), represented by their nonzero entries, satisfying \(\sum_zx_{t,z}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_j=q_j\) for every \(j\in J\). Whenever \(x_{t,z}>0\), the choice \(z\) maximizes \(v_t(\sum_jjz_j)-\sum_jp_jz_j\) over \(Z_J\); any maximizing choices may share mass.
Its welfare is \(W=\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}v_t(\sum_jjz_j)\). Define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max\{\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k=0}^Ky_{t,k}v_t(k):y_{t,k}\ge0,\ \sum_{k=0}^Ky_{t,k}=\mu_t,\ \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k=0}^Kky_{t,k}=\lambda\}\).

Can an equilibrium with \(W\ge\mathrm{OPT}/[C\log(2+\min\{K,\tau\})]\), for some universal constant \(C>0\), always be computed in time \(\operatorname{poly}(K,\tau,L)\), or is this approximation guarantee unattainable in polynomial time?

For discrete multi-unit markets, a competitive bundling equilibrium with an \(O(\log(2+\min\{m,n\}))\) welfare approximation exists and is computable in polynomial time using demand queries; a matching logarithmic welfare loss is sometimes unavoidable even with subadditive valuations. Result. An equilibrium attaining \(W=\mathrm{OPT}\) can always be computed in time \(\operatorname{poly}(K,\tau,L)\). Offering only singleton packages suffices.

1. A welfare LP with a nonnegative supply price.

Consider the auxiliary linear program
\( \begin{aligned} A=\max_y\quad& \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k=0}^{K}y_{t,k}v_t(k)\\ \text{subject to}\quad& \sum_{k=0}^{K}y_{t,k}=\mu_t &&(t\in T),\\ &\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k=0}^{K}k y_{t,k}\leq\lambda,\\ &y_{t,k}\geq0. \end{aligned} \)

Its feasible set is nonempty, because assigning all mass quantity zero is feasible. It is compact, because each variable belongs to \([0,\mu_t]\). Thus an optimum exists.

We first prove \(A=\mathrm{OPT}\), including the existence of an optimum that uses exactly \(\lambda\) units.

Every feasible allocation defining \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is feasible for the auxiliary LP, so \(\mathrm{OPT}\leq A\). Let \(y\) attain \(A\), and write \(s=\sum_{t,k}k y_{t,k}\). If \(s=\lambda\), the reverse inequality follows immediately.

Otherwise \(s<\lambda\leq K\). Define the allocation \(h\) by \(h_{t,K}=\mu_t\) and \(h_{t,k}=0\) for \(k\neq K\). It uses exactly \(K\) units, since \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Set
\( \alpha=\frac{\lambda-s}{K-s}, \qquad \widehat y=(1-\alpha)y+\alpha h. \)
Here \(0<\alpha\leq1\), and \(\widehat y\) preserves all type masses and uses exactly \(\lambda\) units.

Monotonicity gives
\( \sum_t\mu_t v_t(K) \geq \sum_{t,k}y_{t,k}v_t(k)=A. \)
Consequently, the welfare of \(\widehat y\) is at least \(A\). Because \(\widehat y\) is feasible for the auxiliary LP, its welfare is also at most \(A\). It therefore attains \(A\) and is feasible for the defining LP of \(\mathrm{OPT}\). This proves \(A=\mathrm{OPT}\).

The dual of the auxiliary LP is
\( \begin{aligned} \min_{a,p}\quad& \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t+\lambda p\\ \text{subject to}\quad& a_t+kp\geq v_t(k) &&(t\in T,\ 0\leq k\leq K),\\ &p\geq0, \end{aligned} \)
where each \(a_t\) is unrestricted in sign. The sign restriction \(p\geq0\) follows from the primal supply inequality.

Both programs have rational optimal solutions, and linear programming strong duality applies: the primal is feasible with finite optimum, and the dual is feasible, for example with \(p=0\) and \(a_t=v_t(K)\).

2. Optimal allocations are individually demanded.

Let \(\widehat y\) be a rational optimal primal allocation using exactly \(\lambda\), and let \((a,p)\) be a rational optimal dual solution. Strong duality yields
\( \begin{aligned} 0 &=\sum_t\mu_t a_t+\lambda p -\sum_{t,k}\widehat y_{t,k}v_t(k)\\ &=\sum_{t,k}\widehat y_{t,k} \bigl(a_t+kp-v_t(k)\bigr). \end{aligned} \)

Every summand is nonnegative by primal and dual feasibility. Thus
\( \widehat y_{t,k}>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_t(k)-kp=a_t. \)
Meanwhile, dual feasibility gives \(v_t(\ell)-\ell p\leq a_t\) for every \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\). Hence every quantity receiving positive mass maximizes that type’s utility at unit price \(p\), including comparison with the empty choice.

3. Construction and polynomial running time.

Offer \(J=\{1\}\), with package mass \(q_1=\lambda>0\) and price \(p_1=p\).

For this bundling, an individual’s feasible choices are exactly \(z_1=k\), for \(k\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\). Assign
\( x_{t,(k)}=\widehat y_{t,k}. \)

The type constraints give \(\sum_k x_{t,(k)}=\mu_t\). Market clearance holds because
\( \sum_{t,k}x_{t,(k)}k=\lambda=q_1. \)
Section 2 proves utility maximization for every positive assignment. The resulting welfare is therefore
\( W=\sum_{t,k}\widehat y_{t,k}v_t(k)=\mathrm{OPT}. \)

For computation, solve the auxiliary rational LP and its dual using polynomial-time rational linear programming. If the computed primal optimum has slack supply, apply the explicit interpolation in Section 1.

There are \(\tau(K+1)\) primal variables and polynomially many explicitly represented constraints. Their coefficient bit lengths are polynomially bounded in the input length and \(\log K\). Rational linear programming supplies optimal solutions with polynomial encoding length; the interpolation also uses only polynomially many rational arithmetic operations and preserves polynomial encoding length. The output has at most \(\tau(K+1)\) nonzero allocation entries. Thus the entire construction runs in \(\operatorname{poly}(K,\tau,L)\) time.

4. Why the discrete welfare loss does not transfer.

The [cited theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.0576) concerns finite consumers and indivisible allocations. Here each type’s mass may be divided among utility-maximizing quantities. The proof above shows that this permitted division implements the welfare LP exactly at an anonymous unit price. It also uses the stipulated rule that package supply does not restrict an individual’s feasible choices.

Exact optimality implies the requested logarithmic approximation for a suitable universal constant \(C>0\).
