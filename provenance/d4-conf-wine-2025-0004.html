The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite good classes \(G\), agent types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), ratios \(r_t>1\), high-value sets \(H_t\subseteq G\), per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and an explicit current allocation \(\lambda_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\in\{0,1\}^{G}\), decide whether there exists a reassignment flow \(y_{t,B,B'}\) preserving source masses and every \(q_g\), with \(u_t(B')\ge u_t(B)\) for every used transition and \(\sum y_{t,B,B'}(u_t(B')-u_t(B))>0\), where \(u_t(B)=r_t|B\cap H_t|+|B\setminus H_t|\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) good classes. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a binary-rational ratio \(r_t>1\) and a set \(H_t\subseteq G\). Allowed bundles are all \(B\in\mathcal B=\{0,1\}^{G}\), with \(u_t(B)=r_t\sum_{g\in H_t}B_g+\sum_{g\notin H_t}B_g\).
The current allocation is a sparse explicit list of positive binary-rational entries \(\lambda_{t,B}\), with omitted entries zero and \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\). Supplies \(q_g\in[0,1]\) are binary rationals satisfying \(\sum_{t,B}\lambda_{t,B}B_g=q_g\) for every \(g\).
A reassignment is a nonnegative real flow \(y_{t,B,B'}\) over \(T\times\mathcal B\times\mathcal B\), satisfying \(\sum_{B'}y_{t,B,B'}=\lambda_{t,B}\) and \(\sum_{t,B,B'}y_{t,B,B'}B'_g=q_g\) for every \(g\).
Every positive transition must satisfy \(u_t(B')\geq u_t(B)\), and an improvement requires \(\sum_{t,B,B'}y_{t,B,B'}(u_t(B')-u_t(B))>0\). Mass may split among destinations, including the unchanged bundle; equal utilities are allowed without tie-breaking.

Is deciding whether such an improvement exists in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), the number \(s\) of listed allocation entries, and the total input bit length \(L\).

For finite agents and indivisible goods, deciding Pareto-optimality is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete even with only two distinct ratios; equivalently, deciding whether a Pareto-improvement exists is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Integer ratios admit polynomial-time verification. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18251v2).
No result settling this exact mass-flow decision problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total society mass to one, making supplies per capita.
  • Require ratios to be binary rationals so the decision problem has a finite input encoding.
  • Interpret the explicit current allocation as a sparse list of positive entries, with omitted entries zero; include its support size and total input bit length in running-time parameters.
  • Require the current allocation to allocate every type's entire mass and exactly exhaust every specified supply, matching complete allocations in the discrete definition.
  • Allow every binary bundle as a destination, including empty and unchanged bundles; flow values are nonnegative reals and source mass may split.
  • Require at least one good class and retain unrestricted high-value sets, including empty and full sets, as permitted by the discrete utility definition.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\), for arbitrary binary-rational ratios. It admits an exact linear program with \(O(sm^2)\) variables and constraints, and a running time of

\(\bigl(L+\tau m+sm^2\bigr)^{O(1)}\)

bit operations.

The proof preserves the requirement that each positive transition weakly increases utility.

1. Index the positive source entries

Index the listed allocation entries by \(i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}\). Write their types, source bundles, and masses as \(t_i,B_i,\lambda_i\). Define

\(H_i=H_{t_i},\quad K_i=G\setminus H_i,\quad h_i=|H_i|,\quad \ell_i=|K_i|,\quad r_i=r_{t_i},\quad v_i=u_{t_i}(B_i).\)

For a destination bundle, let \(a\) count its goods in \(H_i\), and let \(b\) count its goods in \(K_i\). Its utility is exactly \(r_i a+b\). Consequently, its admissibility depends only on the count pair. Define

\(D_i=\{(a,b):a\in\{0,\ldots,h_i\},\ b\in\{0,\ldots,\ell_i\},\ r_i a+b\geq v_i\}.\)

There are at most \((h_i+1)(\ell_i+1)=O(m^2)\) such pairs, and exact rational arithmetic determines membership.

2. A fixed-cardinality decomposition lemma

Lemma. Let \(E\) be a finite set and let \(k\in\{0,\ldots,|E|\}\). A vector \(p\in[0,1]^E\) is a convex combination of incidence vectors of \(k\)-element subsets of \(E\) if and only if \(\sum_{g\in E}p_g=k\).

Proof. Necessity follows by summing coordinates.

For sufficiency, induct on the number of fractional coordinates. If there are none, the vector itself is an incidence vector of a \(k\)-element subset. There cannot be exactly one fractional coordinate, because the coordinate sum is an integer.

Otherwise, choose two fractional coordinates \(p_g,p_j\), and define

\(\delta_+=\min\{1-p_g,p_j\}>0,\qquad \delta_-=\min\{p_g,1-p_j\}>0.\)

Writing \(e_g,e_j\) for coordinate unit vectors, set

\(p^+=p+\delta_+(e_g-e_j),\qquad p^-=p-\delta_-(e_g-e_j).\)

Both vectors lie in \([0,1]^E\), have coordinate sum \(k\), and have fewer fractional coordinates. Moreover,

\(p=\dfrac{\delta_-}{\delta_++\delta_-}p^++\dfrac{\delta_+}{\delta_++\delta_-}p^-.\)

Apply the induction hypothesis to both vectors. The empty-set case is included. \(\square\)

Thus coordinate marginals satisfying these bounds and a fixed integer sum are realized by a probability distribution over binary subsets of that exact cardinality.

3. The polynomial-size linear program

Introduce the following nonnegative variables:

  • \(z_{iab}\), for \((a,b)\in D_i\): mass from source \(i\) assigned count pair \((a,b)\).
  • \(p_{ia}\), for \(0\leq a\leq h_i\): total mass assigned high-good count \(a\).
  • \(w_{ib}\), for \(0\leq b\leq\ell_i\): total mass assigned low-good count \(b\).
  • \(x_{iag}\), for \(g\in H_i\): mass receiving good \(g\) among destinations with high-good count \(a\).
  • \(d_{ibg}\), for \(g\in K_i\): mass receiving good \(g\) among destinations with low-good count \(b\).

All constraints below apply over these index ranges.

Source-mass conservation:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{(a,b)\in D_i}z_{iab}=\lambda_i.\)

Count marginals:

\(\displaystyle p_{ia}=\sum_{b:(a,b)\in D_i}z_{iab},\qquad w_{ib}=\sum_{a:(a,b)\in D_i}z_{iab}.\)

High-good marginals:

\(\displaystyle 0\leq x_{iag}\leq p_{ia},\qquad \sum_{g\in H_i}x_{iag}=a\,p_{ia}.\)

Low-good marginals:

\(\displaystyle 0\leq d_{ibg}\leq w_{ib},\qquad \sum_{g\in K_i}d_{ibg}=b\,w_{ib}.\)

Supply conservation, for every \(g\in G\):

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i:g\in H_i}\sum_{a=0}^{h_i}x_{iag}+\sum_{i:g\in K_i}\sum_{b=0}^{\ell_i}d_{ibg}=q_g.\)

Maximize

\(\displaystyle \Phi=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{(a,b)\in D_i}z_{iab}(r_i a+b-v_i).\)

Every objective coefficient is nonnegative. We will prove that the attainable objective values are exactly the attainable total utility gains in the original problem.

4. Every admissible reassignment gives an LP solution

Take a reassignment satisfying the source, supply, and weak-utility conditions. For source \(i\), abbreviate

\(y_i(B')=y_{t_i,B_i,B'}.\)

For each destination, write

\(a_i(B')=\sum_{g\in H_i}B'_g,\qquad b_i(B')=\sum_{g\in K_i}B'_g.\)

Aggregate its mass by count pair:

\(\displaystyle z_{iab}=\sum_{B':\,a_i(B')=a,\ b_i(B')=b}y_i(B').\)

Positive transitions are admissible, so their count pairs belong to \(D_i\). Define \(p,w\) through the count-marginal equations, and set

\(\displaystyle x_{iag}=\sum_{B':\,a_i(B')=a}y_i(B')B'_g,\qquad d_{ibg}=\sum_{B':\,b_i(B')=b}y_i(B')B'_g.\)

Because \(B'_g\in\{0,1\}\), the marginal bounds hold. Summing over the relevant goods gives \(a p_{ia}\) and \(b w_{ib}\), respectively. The original source and supply equalities give their LP counterparts.

Finally, each destination in count pair \((a,b)\) has gain \(r_i a+b-v_i\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \Phi=\sum_i\sum_{B'}y_i(B')\bigl(u_{t_i}(B')-u_{t_i}(B_i)\bigr).\)

5. Every LP solution gives an admissible reassignment

Take any feasible LP solution.

For every \(i,a\) with \(p_{ia}>0\), the vector

\(\displaystyle \left(\frac{x_{iag}}{p_{ia}}\right)_{g\in H_i}\)

lies in \([0,1]^{H_i}\) and has coordinate sum \(a\). By the lemma, there exists a probability distribution \(P_{ia}\) over \(a\)-element subsets of \(H_i\) whose inclusion probabilities are these coordinates.

Likewise, when \(w_{ib}>0\), there exists a probability distribution \(Q_{ib}\) over \(b\)-element subsets of \(K_i\) with inclusion probabilities \(d_{ibg}/w_{ib}\).

For each \(z_{iab}>0\), both \(p_{ia}\) and \(w_{ib}\) are positive. Split mass \(z_{iab}\) among bundles \(A\cup C\) by assigning

\(\displaystyle z_{iab}P_{ia}(A)Q_{ib}(C)\)

to each \(a\)-element subset \(A\subseteq H_i\) and \(b\)-element subset \(C\subseteq K_i\). This defines finitely many transitions from source \(B_i\). Set transitions from omitted sources to zero.

The two distributions have total probability one, so source \(i\) sends exactly \(\sum_{(a,b)\in D_i}z_{iab}=\lambda_i\).

For \(g\in H_i\), its receiving mass is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a:p_{ia}>0}\sum_{b:(a,b)\in D_i}z_{iab}\frac{x_{iag}}{p_{ia}}=\sum_a x_{iag}.\)

Here zero marginal masses also force their \(x\)-variables to zero. Similarly, a good \(g\in K_i\) receives mass \(\sum_b d_{ibg}\). Hence the LP supply equations give exactly the required supplies.

Every positive transition constructed from pair \((a,b)\) has utility \(r_i a+b\geq v_i\), because that pair belongs to \(D_i\). Thus weak improvement holds on each transition, including ties. Its total utility gain is exactly \(\Phi\).

This proves the claimed equivalence.

6. Exact decision and running time

The LP is feasible: assign each source entirely to its original bundle and use its original good marginals. This gives objective zero.

The feasible region is bounded. Source conservation bounds every \(z_{iab}\) by \(\lambda_i\); the marginal equations and bounds then bound all remaining variables by \(\lambda_i\). Consequently, the maximum \(\Phi^*\) exists, and the preceding equivalence proves

\(\text{an improvement exists}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Phi^*>0.\)

Per source, the number of variables is

\(O\bigl((h_i+1)(\ell_i+1)+h_i(h_i+1)+\ell_i(\ell_i+1)\bigr)=O(m^2).\)

There are also \(O(sm^2+m)\) constraints and \(O(sm^2)\) nonzero constraint coefficients. Since \(m,s\geq1\), these are \(O(sm^2)\).

Constructing the LP requires \(O(\tau m+sm^2)\) rational arithmetic operations, plus reading the input. Every rational coefficient has bit length \(O(L+\log(m+1))\): ratios are used through arithmetic and comparison, never through iteration up to their numerical values.

Apply the standard theorem that rational linear programming admits exact optimization in time polynomial in its encoding length; see [MIT’s linear-programming notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html). Its hypotheses hold here: the LP is explicitly represented, rational, feasible, and bounded. The resulting bit complexity is

\(\bigl(L+\tau m+sm^2\bigr)^{O(1)}.\)

The sign test is exact. Indeed, rational LP vertices and their objective values have polynomial bit length by Cramer’s rule, so strict positivity does not require a numerical tolerance.

7. Relation to the finite-agent result

The cited [finite-agent hardness theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18251v2#S3.SS3) concerns allocations to indivisible agents. Here a source mass may split among several bundles, with supplies enforced across the resulting masses. The decomposition above uses precisely that permitted splitting.

Therefore the finite-agent hardness result does not settle this model. The stated mass-flow decision problem is polynomial-time solvable, including with fractional ratios.
