The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) of types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), a population size \(N\) with \(N\mu_t\) integral, \(k\) goods, bundle size bound \(d\), and per-capita supplies \(q_j\), compute a randomized allocation for the \(N\) named agents. A type \(t\) specifies a valuation \(v_t(B)\in[0,1]\) for each bundle \(B\) of size at most \(d\); the type-level allocation \(x_{tB}\) records the fraction of its mass assigned bundle \(B\). Require \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-joint differential privacy when one named agent changes type, and seek high welfare with small total supply violation under supplies \(Nq_j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Require \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and give a binary integer \(N\geq1\) with \(N\mu_t\) integral. Let \(m=k\geq1\), \(1\leq d\leq k\), and \(\mathcal B=\{B\subseteq[k]:|B|\leq d\}\).
Each type explicitly lists rational values \(v_t(B)\in[0,1]\). Public per-capita supplies are rational \(q_j\in[0,1]\); public rational parameters satisfy \(0<\epsilon\leq1\) and \(0<\delta,\beta<1/2\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including valuation tables and \(N\).
A randomized mechanism computes a fractional allocation \(x_{tB}\geq0\) with \(\sum_Bx_{tB}\leq1\); these are conditional fractions within type \(t\), and the remaining fraction is unserved with value zero. Each named agent privately receives her type's allocation vector. The internal type-indexed table is a compact representation; it is not publicly released, and delivery is by a trusted lookup using the recipient's own type.
Define \(W(x)=N\sum_t\mu_t\sum_Bv_t(B)x_{tB}\) and \(V(x)=\sum_{j=1}^k\max\{0,N\sum_t\mu_t\sum_{B\ni j}x_{tB}-Nq_j\}\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) maximize \(W\) over fractional allocations with \(V=0\).
Privacy is \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-joint differential privacy of the delivered vectors: changing one named agent's valuation type must preserve differential privacy of the combined outputs to all other agents. This change transfers mass \(1/N\) between types and may change the support; the support and masses are private.
Require, jointly with probability at least \(1-\beta\), \(W(x)\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\alpha\) and \(V(x)\leq\alpha\), where \(\alpha=C\frac{kd}{\epsilon}\sqrt{\log(Nd/\delta)}\log(Ndk/\beta)\) for a universal constant \(C\).

Is attaining these welfare, supply-violation, and privacy guarantees possible by a randomized algorithm with preprocessing and per-recipient lookup time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,d,1/\epsilon,\log(1/\delta),\log(1/\beta)\), or is attaining them NP-hard?

For an explicit list of agents, a jointly private algorithm achieves the stated additive welfare and total supply-violation bounds, efficiently with demand oracles ([Hsu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1411.0998)).
A later packing algorithm achieves additive welfare error and exact feasibility under minimum-supply conditions, with runtime linear in the explicit population size when other parameters are suppressed ([Huang and Zhu](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.00767)); this is a different guarantee and encoding.

  • Specified the fractional benchmark, total rather than maximum supply violation, confidence parameter, and additive error bound from the allocation corollary.

1. The fractional problem and a repair inequality

Write

$$ n_t=N\mu_t,\qquad s_j=Nq_j. $$

The hypotheses imply that the $n_t$ are positive integers and sum to $N$.

Let $M=|\mathcal B|$. Substituting $z_{tB}=n_tx_{tB}$ expresses the nonprivate optimization problem as the rational linear program

$$ \begin{aligned} \text{maximize}\quad& \sum_{t,B}v_t(B)z_{tB},\\ \text{subject to}\quad& \sum_Bz_{tB}\leq n_t &&\text{for each }t,\\ & \sum_t\sum_{B\ni j}z_{tB}\leq s_j &&\text{for each }j,\\ & z_{tB}\geq0. \end{aligned} $$

There are $\tau M$ variables and $\tau+k$ substantive constraints. Explicit valuation tables make its encoding length polynomial in $L$. The feasible region is nonempty and bounded. Polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore solves the nonprivate problem in the required support encoding.

We shall also use the following inequality.

Repair lemma. Every allocation satisfying the personal constraints obeys

$$ W(x)\leq\mathrm{OPT}+V(x). $$

Proof. Work with the count variables $z_{tB}=n_tx_{tB}$. Process goods sequentially. When a good’s current demand exceeds its supply, remove exactly that excess from allocation variables whose bundles contain the good.

This is possible because the excess is no larger than its current demand. Removing allocation preserves the personal constraints and cannot increase any other demand. Consequently, the amount removed while processing good $j$ is at most that good’s original excess.

The resulting allocation is feasible, and the total allocation mass removed is at most $V(x)$. Each removed unit has value at most $1$. The resulting welfare is therefore at least $W(x)-V(x)$, and is at most $\mathrm{OPT}$. This proves the inequality. $\square$

Define the penalized welfare

$$ Q(x)=W(x)-2V(x). $$

The repair lemma gives a useful sufficient condition:

$$ Q(x)\geq\mathrm{OPT}-a \quad\Longrightarrow\quad W(x)\geq\mathrm{OPT}-a,\qquad V(x)\leq a. $$

Indeed, the welfare assertion follows from $V(x)\geq0$. For violation,

$$ \mathrm{OPT}-a \leq W(x)-2V(x) \leq\mathrm{OPT}-V(x). $$

2. Smooth local responses admit a good price vector

Introduce an additional action $\bot$, meaning unserved. Its value and resource vector are zero. For a bundle $B$, write $a(B)\in\{0,1\}^k$ for its incidence vector. There are

$$ A=M+1\leq 2^{k+1} $$

actions, including $\bot$.

Set the public smoothing parameter

$$ \gamma=\frac{1}{N(k+1)}. $$

For a price vector $p\in[0,2]^k$, define

$$ u_{tB}(p)=v_t(B)-p\cdot a(B),\qquad u_{t\bot}(p)=0, $$

and the probability distribution

$$ r_{ta}(p)= \frac{\exp(u_{ta}(p)/\gamma)} {\sum_b\exp(u_{tb}(p)/\gamma)}. $$

The bundle coordinates of $r_t(p)$ form a valid allocation vector; the $\bot$ coordinate is its unserved fraction.

Let $W(p)$, $D_j(p)$, $V(p)$, and $Q(p)$ denote the resulting welfare, demands, violation, and penalized welfare.

Lemma. There exists $p^*\in[0,2]^k$ such that

$$ Q(p^*)\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\log 2. $$

Proof. Consider the smooth convex function

$$ F_\gamma(p) = p\cdot s+ \sum_tn_t\gamma \log\!\left(\sum_a\exp(u_{ta}(p)/\gamma)\right). $$

For every feasible allocation $y$, completed to a probability distribution by adding the unserved action,

$$ \gamma\log\!\left(\sum_a e^{u_{ta}(p)/\gamma}\right) \geq \max_a u_{ta}(p) \geq \sum_a y_{ta}u_{ta}(p). $$

Because $p\geq0$ and the demands of $y$ are at most $s$, summing yields

$$ F_\gamma(p)\geq W(y). $$

In particular,

$$ F_\gamma(p)\geq\mathrm{OPT} \qquad\text{for every }p\in[0,2]^k. $$

Continuity and compactness supply a minimizer $p^*$. Differentiation gives

$$ \frac{\partial F_\gamma}{\partial p_j}(p)=s_j-D_j(p). $$

The coordinatewise first-order conditions at a minimum over the box imply

$$ \begin{aligned} p_j^*=0&\ \Longrightarrow\ D_j(p^*)\leq s_j,\\ 0<p_j^*<2&\ \Longrightarrow\ D_j(p^*)=s_j,\\ p_j^*=2&\ \Longrightarrow\ D_j(p^*)\geq s_j. \end{aligned} $$

Thus, coordinate by coordinate,

$$ p_j^*(s_j-D_j(p^*)) =-2\max\{D_j(p^*)-s_j,0\}. $$

For a probability vector $r$, let

$$ H(r)=-\sum_a r_a\log r_a. $$

The defining Gibbs probabilities satisfy the exact identity

$$ \gamma\log\!\left(\sum_a e^{u_{ta}(p)/\gamma}\right) = \sum_a r_{ta}(p)u_{ta}(p)+\gamma H(r_t(p)). $$

At $p^*$, it follows that

$$ F_\gamma(p^*) = Q(p^*)+\gamma\sum_tn_tH(r_t(p^*)). $$

Since $H(r_t)\leq\log A$ and $\sum_tn_t=N$,

$$ \gamma\sum_tn_tH(r_t(p^*)) \leq\gamma N\log A \leq\log2. $$

Combining this with $F_\gamma(p^*)\geq\mathrm{OPT}$ proves the lemma. $\square$

This result accommodates positive empty-bundle values: the empty bundle is an ordinary action, distinct from the zero-valued unserved action.

3. A public finite price grid preserves the guarantee

We next bound how much penalized welfare changes with prices.

Let $p,p'\in[0,2]^k$, and put $h=\|p'-p\|_\infty$. Along the line segment between these prices, every action utility has derivative of absolute value at most $dh$. Differentiating the Gibbs probabilities gives

$$ r_{ta}'= \frac{r_{ta}}{\gamma} \left(u_{ta}'-\sum_b r_{tb}u_{tb}'\right). $$

Therefore,

$$ \sum_a|r_{ta}'|\leq\frac{2dh}{\gamma}, $$

and integration gives

$$ \operatorname{TV}(r_t(p),r_t(p')) \leq\frac{dh}{\gamma}. $$

Values lie in $[0,1]$, so

$$ |W(p)-W(p')| \leq\frac{Ndh}{\gamma}. $$

Every resource vector has $\ell_1$ norm at most $d$. Hence

$$ \|D(p)-D(p')\|_1 \leq\frac{2Nd^2h}{\gamma}. $$

The map $D\mapsto\sum_j\max\{D_j-s_j,0\}$ is $1$-Lipschitz in $\ell_1$. Consequently,

$$ |Q(p)-Q(p')| \leq \frac{N(d+4d^2)h}{\gamma} \leq 5N^2(k+1)d^2h. $$

Define the public integer

$$ G=20N^2(k+1)d^2 $$

and the public candidate set

$$ \mathcal P= \left\{0,\frac1G,\frac2G,\ldots,2\right\}^k. $$

Its cardinality is

$$ K=(2G+1)^k. $$

Some grid point is within $1/G$ of $p^*$ in $\ell_\infty$. Thus

$$ \max_{p\in\mathcal P}Q(p) \geq\mathrm{OPT}-\log2-\frac14. $$

The candidate set depends only on public parameters. In particular, it does not depend on the private support.

4. Rational local responses and score sensitivity

$$ \operatorname{TV}(\widetilde r_t(p),r_t(p)) \leq \xi, \qquad \xi=\frac{1}{16N(1+4d)}. $$

$$ \widetilde Q(p)=\widetilde W(p)-2\widetilde V(p) $$

$$ |\widetilde W(p)-W(p)|\leq N\xi, $$

$$ \|\widetilde D(p)-D(p)\|_1\leq2Nd\xi, $$

$$ \max_{p\in\mathcal P}\widetilde Q(p)\geq\mathrm{OPT}-2. $$

Here is a certified polynomial-time implementation of the local approximation.

For each type and price, subtract the maximum action utility before exponentiating. Write the shifted exponents as

$$ h_a=\frac{u_{ta}(p)-\max_bu_{tb}(p)}{\gamma}\leq0. $$

At least one corresponding exponential weight equals $1$.

$$ 2^H\geq\frac{64A}{\xi}. $$

$$ Ae^{-H}\leq A2^{-H}\leq\frac{\xi}{64}. $$

$$ b_0=\frac{\xi}{128A}, $$

clamping approximations to $[0,1]$ and keeping one maximum weight exactly $1$.

These approximations can be computed by rational Taylor sums. If the Taylor polynomial has degree $J$, its remainder for $h_a\in[-H,0]$ is at most

$$ \frac{H^{J+1}}{(J+1)!}. $$

Using $j!\geq(j/e)^j$, it suffices to choose

$$ J+1\geq12H, \qquad J+1\geq\left\lceil\log_2(1/b_0)\right\rceil. $$

Finally choose $b$ with $2^b\geq8A/\xi$, round down all probabilities except one distinguished coordinate to multiples of $2^{-b}$, and assign the remaining probability to that coordinate. This produces nonnegative probabilities summing exactly to $1$, with additional total variation error at most $A2^{-b}\leq\xi/8$.

The combined error is smaller than $\xi$. All arithmetic uses polynomially many bits and polynomial time in the input size and public parameters.

Sensitivity lemma. For each fixed public price $p$, replacing one named agent’s type changes $\widetilde Q(p)$ by at most

$$ S=1+4d. $$

Proof. The welfare contribution of either type is in $[0,1]$, so welfare changes by at most $1$. Each type’s expected resource vector is nonnegative and has $\ell_1$ norm at most $d$. Replacing it changes aggregate demand by at most $2d$ in $\ell_1$, and hence changes total violation by at most $2d$. Thus the penalized score changes by at most $1+4d$. $\square$

This argument remains valid when a type enters or leaves the support.

6. Why this does not settle preprocessing complexity

The construction is a bounded-time finite-bit algorithm, but its preprocessing enumerates

$$ K=\left(40N^2(k+1)d^2+1\right)^k $$

candidates. Its runtime is $K$ times a polynomial in the stated input parameters. This dependence is not polynomial in the binary input length of $N$.

One might try to replace enumeration by private approximate minimization of the convex function $F_\gamma$. The following example shows why a dual objective approximation alone does not justify that replacement.

Take $k=d=1$, one supported type, singleton value $1$, empty-bundle value $0$, and supply $s=N/2$. With $\gamma=1/(2N)$, the singleton fraction is

$$ r(p)=\frac{e^{(1-p)/\gamma}}{2+e^{(1-p)/\gamma}}. $$

The convex dual minimizer is

$$ p^*=1-\gamma\log2, $$

because $r(p^*)=1/2$. At $p=1$, however,

$$ r(1)=\frac13, \qquad W(1)=\frac N3, \qquad \mathrm{OPT}=\frac N2. $$

Thus the welfare loss is $N/6$.

Nevertheless,

$$ F_\gamma'(p)=\frac N2-Nr(p), \qquad |F_\gamma'(p)|\leq\frac N2, $$

so

$$ 0\leq F_\gamma(1)-F_\gamma(p^*) \leq\frac N2\gamma\log2 =\frac{\log2}{4}. $$

A constant dual objective error can therefore coexist with welfare loss proportional to $N$.

The penalized score itself also need not be concave. On $p<p^*$ in this example,

$$ Q(p)=N(1-r(p)), $$

and

$$ r''(p)= \frac{r(p)(1-r(p))(1-2r(p))}{\gamma^2}<0. $$

Consequently $Q''(p)>0$ there. Efficient sampling from the score-based distribution cannot simply be justified by log-concave sampling.

The proven progress is a compact, support-independent private coordination mechanism with the desired accuracy and polynomial lookup. Efficient preprocessing for arbitrary $k$, or an appropriate hardness reduction, remains open in this write-up.
