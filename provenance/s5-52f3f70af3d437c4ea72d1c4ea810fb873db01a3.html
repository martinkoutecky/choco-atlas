The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite variables \(V\), domains, a DAG \(G\), rational masses \(\mu\) over finitely many UCP-network types sharing the qualitative skeleton, and a partial assignment \(z\), find a completion \(v^\star\in\operatorname{Comp}(z)\) maximizing per-capita welfare \(\bar u_\mu(v)=\sum_t\mu_tu_t(v)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(V=\{X_1,\ldots,X_m\}\) have explicitly listed, finite nonempty domains, and let \(G\) be a DAG with parent sets \(P_i\). An outcome assigns every variable a domain value.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; require \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
Type \(t\) supplies explicit tables \(f_{t,i}:\operatorname{Dom}(X_i)\times\operatorname{Dom}(P_i)\to\mathbb Q\), defining \(u_t(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{t,i}(v_i,v_{P_i})\). Entries may have either sign; these cardinal utilities are used as supplied, without rescaling.
Each type is promised to satisfy the UCP condition: for every \(i\), parent assignment \(p\), values \(a,b\), and assignment \(r\) to the remaining variables, \(f_{t,i}(a,p)\ge f_{t,i}(b,p)\) implies \(u_t(a,p,r)\ge u_t(b,p,r)\). Thus local improvements cannot be outweighed by changes in other factors. Ties are permitted.
The shared qualitative skeleton means the same variables, domains, and DAG; conditional value orders may differ between types. Given a valid partial assignment \(z\), let \(\operatorname{Comp}(z)\) contain all outcomes extending it, with no additional feasibility constraints. Find any \(v^\star\in\arg\max_{v\in\operatorname{Comp}(z)}\sum_t\mu_tu_t(v)\).
Let \(d=\max_i|\operatorname{Dom}(X_i)|\) and \(L\) be the total binary input length, including all tables. State running times in \(m,\tau,d,L\).

Is this exact welfare maximization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,d,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a single UCP-network, optimal completion is computable by a forward sweep in linear time in the tabular network size, counting arithmetic operations ([Boutilier, Bacchus, and Brafman](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~cebly/Papers/ucpnets.pdf)).
No result settling exactly the rational-support welfare problem specified here was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted the undefined shared qualitative skeleton as common variables, domains, and DAG, without requiring identical conditional value orders.
  • Normalized positive rational masses to sum to one, matching the distribution and per-capita formulation.
  • Specified explicitly listed nonempty domains and explicit binary-rational factor tables; factor entries may have either sign.
  • Made the intended local domination condition an input promise, rather than requiring the prover to recognize valid UCP-networks.
  • Used cardinal utilities exactly as supplied, without imposing an additional individual utility normalization.
  • Allowed ties and accepted any welfare-maximizing completion.
  • Restricted feasibility to extending a valid partial assignment, as in the anchor; introduced no other constraints.
  • Defined m as the number of variables, d as the largest domain size, and L as the total binary input length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact welfare maximization is NP-hard

The problem is NP-hard even when \( \tau=2 \), \( d=2 \), both masses are \( 1/2 \), the partial assignment is empty, and every node has at most two parents. The reduction below satisfies the UCP promise with strict local preferences.

Consequently, a running time polynomial in \( m,\tau,d,L \) would imply \( \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP} \).

1. Source problem and construction

We reduce from unweighted Maximum Cut. Its decision version asks whether a finite simple undirected graph has a cut containing at least a specified number of edges; this problem is NP-complete. [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer, “Some simplified NP-complete problems”](https://doi.org/10.1145/800119.803884).

Let the source graph be \( H=(N,E) \), with \( n=|N| \) and \( q=|E| \). Set \( M=q+1 \).

Construct the following binary variables:

  • A root variable \( X_a \) for each vertex \( a\in N \).
  • A leaf variable \( Y_e \) for each edge \( e=\{a,b\}\in E \), with parents \( X_a,X_b \).

These are all the arcs. Thus the network is a DAG, its maximum indegree is two, and \( m=n+q \). Every domain is explicitly \( \{0,1\} \). Take \( z=\varnothing \).

There are two types, denoted \( + \) and \( - \), each with mass \( 1/2 \). Define the binary cut indicator by \( c(a,b)=1 \) when \( a\ne b \), and \( c(a,b)=0 \) otherwise.

For every root, supply the tables

\( f_{+,X_a}(x)=Mx,\qquad f_{-,X_a}(x)=-Mx. \)

For every edge leaf, supply the tables

\( f_{+,Y_e}(y,x_a,x_b)=c(x_a,x_b)+y,\qquad f_{-,Y_e}(y,x_a,x_b)=c(x_a,x_b)-y. \)

These formulas specify all entries of the explicit tables. Their negative entries are permitted by the question.

For an outcome \( (x,y) \), write

\( C(x)=\sum_{\{a,b\}\in E}c(x_a,x_b). \)

The resulting utilities are exactly

\( u_+(x,y)=M\sum_{a\in N}x_a+C(x)+\sum_{e\in E}y_e \)

and

\( u_-(x,y)=-M\sum_{a\in N}x_a+C(x)-\sum_{e\in E}y_e. \)

2. Verification of the UCP promise

We verify the required implication for every variable, parent assignment, and assignment to the remaining variables.

Leaf variables

Fix an edge \( e=\{a,b\} \), its parent values, and all other variable values.

For type \( + \), changing \( Y_e \) from \( 0 \) to \( 1 \) increases its local factor by exactly \( 1 \). Because \( Y_e \) has no children, this changes no other factor, so total utility also increases by exactly \( 1 \).

For type \( - \), changing \( Y_e \) from \( 1 \) to \( 0 \) likewise increases both its local factor and total utility by exactly \( 1 \).

Thus both types satisfy the UCP implication at every leaf.

Root variables

Fix a vertex \( a \) and an arbitrary assignment to every other variable. Let \( \Delta_a \) be the change in \( C(x) \) when \( X_a \) changes from \( 0 \) to \( 1 \).

Only edges incident to \( a \) can change their cut indicators. Each indicator changes by either \( 1 \) or \( -1 \), so

\( |\Delta_a|\le \deg_H(a)\le q. \)

For type \( + \), the locally preferred change is from \( 0 \) to \( 1 \). Its total utility change is

\( M+\Delta_a\ge M-\deg_H(a)\ge q+1-q=1. \)

For type \( - \), the locally preferred change is from \( 1 \) to \( 0 \). Its total utility change is

\( M-\Delta_a\ge M-\deg_H(a)\ge1. \)

These bounds hold for every assignment to the remaining variables, including all leaf values.

At every node the two distinct domain values have strictly different local factor values. Hence the only premises requiring verification are the preferred changes just checked and comparisons of a value with itself; the latter give equality of total utilities. This proves the full stated UCP condition for both types.

The types share precisely the required skeleton. Their opposing local orders are allowed by the formulation.

3. Welfare equals cut size

The masses are positive binary rationals and sum to one. By direct cancellation,

\( W(x,y)=\frac12u_+(x,y)+\frac12u_-(x,y)=C(x). \)

Thus welfare is independent of the leaf values and equals the number of graph edges crossing the partition represented by \( x \).

Every vertex assignment \( x\in\{0,1\}^{n} \) extends to an outcome: the leaf values can be chosen arbitrarily. Therefore

\( \max_{(x,y)\in\operatorname{Comp}(\varnothing)}W(x,y)=\max_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n}}C(x). \)

In particular, every welfare-maximizing outcome yields a maximum cut by taking \( S=\{a\in N:x_a=1\} \).

For a source threshold \( K \), a cut of size at least \( K \) exists if and only if the constructed instance has an outcome of welfare at least \( K \). An exact welfare optimizer therefore solves Maximum Cut.

4. Encoding size and running times

Across both types there are \( 4n \) root-table entries and \( 16q \) leaf-table entries. Root entries have magnitude at most \( q+1 \); leaf entries belong to \( \{-1,0,1,2\} \).

With conventional binary indexing, the construction has

\( m=n+q,\qquad \tau=2,\qquad d=2,\qquad L=O\bigl(m\log(m+1)\bigr). \)

Writing the entire instance takes \( O\bigl(m\log(m+1)\bigr) \) bit operations. Extracting the cut from an optimizing outcome takes \( O(m) \) elementary operations. Thus the reduction is polynomial, and hardness already holds with constant support size and constant domain size.

For comparison, exhaustive enumeration gives a general exact upper bound of

\( O\bigl(L+d^m m\tau L^2\bigr) \)

bit operations using schoolbook integer arithmetic. There are at most \( d^m \) completions. For each completion, evaluate each type’s \( m \) selected entries, multiply its utility by its mass, and sum the resulting \( \tau \) rationals. Intermediate numerators and denominators have \( O(L) \) bits: within each type, selected table denominators occur once, and each mass is multiplied once after that type’s sum. Hence evaluation and comparison require \( O(m\tau L^2) \) bit operations per completion.

5. Why the single-type forward sweep does not apply

For a single promised UCP-network, the forward sweep computes an optimal completion, as stated in Proposition 4 of [Boutilier, Bacchus, and Brafman](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~cebly/Papers/ucpnets.pdf).

The weighted average of the tables need not satisfy UCP. In this construction, every averaged root factor is identically zero. Its values therefore tie locally, although changing a root can change welfare through incident edge factors. For example, starting with all roots zero, flipping any nonisolated root increases welfare. This violates the UCP implication for the averaged root’s local tie.

The reduction therefore establishes NP-hardness while preserving the promise separately for every supplied type.
