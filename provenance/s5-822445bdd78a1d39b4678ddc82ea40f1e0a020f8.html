Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), a finite hypothesis set, and a finite-precision encoded stream of type-conditional average public log-belief ratios, determine the sample and arithmetic complexity of estimating a nonnegative column-stochastic type influence matrix \(B\) and expected log-likelihood matrix \(\bar L\) to mass-weighted mean-square error at most \(\varepsilon\), under block-lumpability, boundedness, and type-level persistent-excitation assumptions.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(m=|\Theta|\ge2\), fix a reference hypothesis \(\theta_0\), and let \(L\) be the bit length of the encoded masses and scalar parameters.
For strictly positive public beliefs, define \(X_{i,tj}=\mathbb E[\log(\psi_i(\theta_0)/\psi_i(\theta_j))\mid t]\), where \(\theta_j\ne\theta_0\) and the conditional average is over population mass within type \(t\). Block-lumpability means that these averages satisfy exactly \(X_i=(1-\delta)B^{\mathsf T}X_{i-1}+\delta Z_i\), with known rational \(0<\delta<1\). The unknown matrix \(B\ge0\) is column-stochastic; \(B_{st}\) is the aggregate weight recipient type \(t\) assigns to source type \(s\). Its directed graph is strongly connected and has at least one positive diagonal entry.
The unobserved matrices \(Z_i\in\mathbb R^{\tau\times(m-1)}\) are type-conditional averages of private log-likelihood ratios under a fixed true hypothesis. They are independent and identically distributed over time, independent of the past, and have unknown signed mean \(\bar L=\mathbb E Z_i\). Private log-likelihood ratios between any two hypotheses are bounded in absolute value by a known rational \(b>0\); each wrong hypothesis has positive averaged KL-divergence from the truth in at least one positive-mass type.
Type-level persistent excitation means \(\mathbb E[(Z_i-\bar L)(Z_i-\bar L)^{\mathsf T}]\succeq\kappa I_\tau\), for known rational \(\kappa>0\). Observe one stationary trajectory, with each entry of \(X_0,\ldots,X_N\) rounded to the nearest multiple of \(2^{-q}\), ties rounded upward; no private observations or likelihoods are supplied.
For rational \(\varepsilon>0\), estimation means producing rational \(\widehat B\ge0\), column-stochastic, and signed \(\widehat L\), with \(\mathbb E[\sum_t\mu_t(\sum_s(\widehat B_{st}-B_{st})^2+\sum_j(\widehat L_{tj}-\bar L_{tj})^2)]\le\varepsilon\), uniformly over the promised models; expectation includes trajectory and estimator randomness.
What sample length \(N\), precision \(q\), and arithmetic complexity are required: does this estimation problem admit sample, precision, and bit-operation bounds polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,b,\kappa^{-1},\delta^{-1},(1-\delta)^{-1},\varepsilon^{-1}\), or is attaining the stated accuracy NP-hard?
Known finite-agent results give exponential convergence to steady-state influence-matrix mean-square error \(O(\eta)+O(1/(\delta^3M^2))\), with learning step \(\eta\) and averaging window \(M\), under an additional assumption that estimation error is conditionally independent of recent observations; a companion bound controls expected log-likelihood estimation. These are asymptotic guarantees, not polynomial bit-complexity bounds. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13292).
Nearby results give finite-sample identification bounds for fully observed linear systems with zero-mean sub-Gaussian innovations and excitation conditions, but do not establish the joint unknown-mean, quantized, mass-weighted formulation above. [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v75/simchowitz18a/simchowitz18a.pdf).

Conclusion

The estimation problem admits sample, precision, and bit-operation bounds polynomial in the stated parameters. Below is a constructive proof, including explicit sufficient bounds. These bounds are conservative; no optimality claim is made.

Write \(n=\tau\), \(d=m-1\), and \(a=1-\delta\). All norms below are Frobenius norms unless the subscript \(\mathrm{op}\) is shown.

Define the following positive rational quantities:

\[ H=1+b,\qquad \lambda=\delta^2\kappa,\qquad e=\min\{\varepsilon,1\}, \]

\[ W=1+\frac{2ndH^2}{\delta^2}, \qquad \rho=\frac{\lambda a e}{6nW}, \]

\[ C_0=540n^2(n+1)d^2H^4,\qquad C_1=\frac{144n^2dH^2}{\delta^2}, \qquad T=1+C_1+\frac{WC_0}{a^2\rho^2}. \]

It suffices to take

\[ N=\left\lceil\frac{4T}{\delta e}\right\rceil, \qquad q=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{4T}{e}\right\rceil. \]

The estimator is deterministic and uses rational arithmetic throughout. Its arithmetic-operation count is

\[ O\!\left(Nn^2d+n^3+n^2\log(n+1)+nd\right). \]

Intermediate bit lengths are polynomially bounded, as verified below. Consequently, the total bit-operation count is polynomial in the parameters requested in the question.

1. Boundedness and population identification

Set \(A=aB^{\mathsf T}\), so the recursion is

\[ X_i=AX_{i-1}+\delta Z_i. \]

Because \(B\) is column-stochastic, \(B^{\mathsf T}\) is row-stochastic. Thus, for the entrywise maximum norm,

\[ \|AU\|_{\max}\le a\|U\|_{\max}. \]

The bounded private log-likelihood ratios imply \(\|Z_i\|_{\max}\le b\): conditional averaging preserves this bound.

We first show that stationarity implies \(\|X_i\|_{\max}\le b\) almost surely. Iterating the recursion gives

\[ \|X_k\|_{\max} \le a^k\|X_0\|_{\max}+b(1-a^k). \]

For every \(s>0\), stationarity therefore gives

\[ \Pr(\|X_0\|_{\max}>b+s) = \Pr(\|X_k\|_{\max}>b+s) \le \Pr(a^k\|X_0\|_{\max}>s). \]

The right-hand side tends to zero because \(X_0\) is finite almost surely and \(a<1\). This proves the asserted boundedness. In particular, all moments used below exist.

Let

\[ M=\mathbb E X_i,\qquad C=\mathbb E[(X_i-M)(X_i-M)^{\mathsf T}], \]

\[ D=\mathbb E[(X_i-M)(X_{i-1}-M)^{\mathsf T}], \qquad Q=\mathbb E[(Z_i-\bar L)(Z_i-\bar L)^{\mathsf T}]. \]

Taking expectations yields

\[ M=AM+\delta\bar L. \]

Subtracting this identity from the recursion gives

\[ X_i-M=A(X_{i-1}-M)+\delta(Z_i-\bar L). \]

The innovation is independent of the past and has mean zero. Hence both cross terms in the covariance expansion vanish, and

\[ C=ACA^{\mathsf T}+\delta^2Q \succeq \delta^2\kappa I_n =\lambda I_n. \]

The same independence gives

\[ D=AC. \]

Consequently, the parameters are identified by

\[ A=DC^{-1},\qquad B=a^{-1}A^{\mathsf T},\qquad \bar L=\delta^{-1}(M-AM). \]

These identities handle the unknown signed innovation mean directly.

2. Finite-sample moment bounds

Let \(Y_i\) denote the rounded observation of \(X_i\), and put \(h=2^{-q}\). Rounding, including the specified tie convention, satisfies

\[ \|Y_i-X_i\|_{\max}\le h/2\le h. \]

Define the rational empirical moments

\[ \widetilde M=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N Y_{i-1}, \]

\[ \widetilde R=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N Y_{i-1}Y_{i-1}^{\mathsf T}, \qquad \widetilde S=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N Y_iY_{i-1}^{\mathsf T}, \]

\[ \widetilde C=\widetilde R-\widetilde M\widetilde M^{\mathsf T}, \qquad \widetilde D=\widetilde S-\widetilde M\widetilde M^{\mathsf T}. \]

Notice that

\[ \widetilde C = \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N (Y_{i-1}-\widetilde M)(Y_{i-1}-\widetilde M)^{\mathsf T} \succeq0. \]

We will prove, with \(V=(N\delta)^{-1}+h^2\), that

\[ \mathbb E\|\widetilde M-M\|^2 \le18ndH^2V, \]

\[ \mathbb E\|\widetilde C-C\|^2 \le180n^2d^2H^4V, \qquad \mathbb E\|\widetilde D-D\|^2 \le180n^2d^2H^4V. \]

Dependence along the stationary trajectory

Let \(\mathcal F_i=\sigma(X_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_i)\). For a lag \(r\ge1\), construct a future trajectory starting from zero at time \(i\), driven by \(Z_{i+1},\ldots,Z_{i+r}\). Denote it by \(X'_k\). It is independent of \(\mathcal F_i\), and contraction gives

\[ \|X_k-X'_k\|_{\max}\le b a^{k-i}, \qquad k\ge i. \]

Also \(\|X'_k\|_{\max}\le b\).

Consider a scalar sequence \(f_i\) of any one of the following forms:

  • an entry of \(X_{i-1}\);
  • an entry of \(X_{i-1}X_{i-1}^{\mathsf T}\);
  • an entry of \(X_iX_{i-1}^{\mathsf T}\).

For the first form, replacing the future states by their primed versions changes \(f_{i+r}\) by at most \(ba^{r-1}\). Since \(|f_i|\le b\), independence of the primed future gives

\[ |\operatorname{Cov}(f_i,f_{i+r})| \le2b^2a^{r-1}. \]

For either quadratic form, \(|f_i|\le db^2\). The inequality

\[ |uv-u'v'| \le |u-u'||v|+|u'||v-v'| \]

shows that replacement changes \(f_{i+r}\) by at most \(2db^2a^{r-1}\). Therefore,

\[ |\operatorname{Cov}(f_i,f_{i+r})| \le4d^2b^4a^{r-1}. \]

For a stationary scalar sequence,

\[ \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N f_i\right) = \frac1{N^2} \left( N\operatorname{Var}(f_1) + 2\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}(N-r)\operatorname{Cov}(f_1,f_{1+r}) \right). \]

Using \(\sum_{r\ge1}a^{r-1}=\delta^{-1}\), the preceding bounds imply:

\[ \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N f_i\right) \le\frac{9b^2}{N\delta} \]

for a linear entry, and

\[ \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N f_i\right) \le\frac{9d^2b^4}{N\delta} \]

for a quadratic entry.

Centering and quantization

Let \(M_N,R_N,S_N,C_N,D_N\) denote the corresponding empirical quantities formed from the unrounded \(X_i\). Summing the entrywise variance bounds gives

\[ \mathbb E\|M_N-M\|^2\le\frac{9ndb^2}{N\delta}, \]

\[ \mathbb E\|R_N-\mathbb E(X_iX_i^{\mathsf T})\|^2 \le\frac{9n^2d^2b^4}{N\delta}, \]

and the same bound for the uncentered lag moment \(S_N\).

Moreover,

\[ \|M_NM_N^{\mathsf T}-MM^{\mathsf T}\| \le (\|M_N\|+\|M\|)\|M_N-M\| \le2b\sqrt{nd}\,\|M_N-M\|. \]

Applying \(\|U+V\|^2\le2\|U\|^2+2\|V\|^2\) yields

\[ \mathbb E\|C_N-C\|^2\le\frac{90n^2d^2b^4}{N\delta}, \qquad \mathbb E\|D_N-D\|^2\le\frac{90n^2d^2b^4}{N\delta}. \]

For \(h\le1\), deterministic quantization bounds give

\[ \|\widetilde M-M_N\|\le\sqrt{nd}\,h, \]

\[ \|\widetilde R-R_N\|\le3ndHh, \qquad \|\widetilde S-S_N\|\le3ndHh, \]

and

\[ \|\widetilde M\widetilde M^{\mathsf T}-M_NM_N^{\mathsf T}\| \le3ndHh. \]

Thus

\[ \|\widetilde C-C_N\|\le6ndHh, \qquad \|\widetilde D-D_N\|\le6ndHh. \]

Combining these inequalities proves all three claimed moment bounds.

3. Rational estimator and its error

Define

\[ A_{\mathrm{raw}} = \widetilde D(\widetilde C+\rho I_n)^{-1}. \]

The inverse always exists because \(\widetilde C\succeq0\) and \(\rho>0\).

Let

\[ A_\rho=D(C+\rho I_n)^{-1} =AC(C+\rho I_n)^{-1}. \]

Because \(C\succeq\lambda I_n\),

\[ \|A_\rho-A\| \le\frac{\rho\|A\|}{\lambda} \le\frac{\rho\sqrt n}{\lambda}. \]

Here \(\|A\|\le a\sqrt n\le\sqrt n\), since each row of \(B^{\mathsf T}\) is a probability vector. Also,

\[ \|A_\rho\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sqrt n. \]

The exact identity

\[ A_{\mathrm{raw}}-A_\rho = \left[ (\widetilde D-D)+A_\rho(C-\widetilde C) \right] (\widetilde C+\rho I_n)^{-1} \]

therefore implies

\[ \|A_{\mathrm{raw}}-A\| \le \frac{\|\widetilde D-D\|}{\rho} + \frac{\sqrt n\,\|\widetilde C-C\|}{\rho} + \frac{\rho\sqrt n}{\lambda}. \]

Squaring and applying the moment bounds gives

\[ \mathbb E\|A_{\mathrm{raw}}-A\|^2 \le \frac{C_0}{\rho^2}V +\frac{3n\rho^2}{\lambda^2}. \]

Enforcing stochasticity

Form \(B_{\mathrm{raw}}=a^{-1}A_{\mathrm{raw}}^{\mathsf T}\). Project each column onto

\[ \Delta_n=\{v\in\mathbb R^n:v\ge0,\ \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}v=1\}, \]

and call the resulting matrix \(\widehat B\).

Euclidean projection cannot increase distance to any point in the projected convex set. Indeed, if \(p\) is the projection of \(v\), its minimizing property gives

\[ \langle v-p,z-p\rangle\le0 \]

for every feasible \(z\). Expanding \(\|v-z\|^2\) then gives \(\|p-z\|^2\le\|v-z\|^2\). Applying this with each true column of \(B\) proves

\[ \mathbb E\|\widehat B-B\|^2 \le \frac1{a^2} \left( \frac{C_0}{\rho^2}V+\frac{3n\rho^2}{\lambda^2} \right). \]

This projection is exactly rational. For a rational column \(v\), sort its entries decreasingly, choose the largest \(k\) satisfying

\[ v_{(k)}> \frac{\sum_{\ell=1}^k v_{(\ell)}-1}{k}, \]

and put

\[ c=\frac{\sum_{\ell=1}^k v_{(\ell)}-1}{k}, \qquad p_s=\max\{v_s-c,0\}. \]

The resulting vector has sum one. Its projection property follows by checking the preceding variational inequality: active coordinates have \(v_s-p_s=c\), and inactive coordinates have \(v_s\le c\).

Estimating the signed mean

Set \(\widehat A=a\widehat B^{\mathsf T}\). Define \(\widehat L\) by clipping each entry of

\[ \delta^{-1}(\widetilde M-\widehat A\widetilde M) \]

to \([-b,b]\). Since \(\bar L\) lies in this interval entrywise, clipping cannot increase squared error.

Using \(\bar L=\delta^{-1}(M-AM)\),

\[ \widetilde M-\widehat A\widetilde M-\delta\bar L = (I_n-\widehat A)(\widetilde M-M)+(A-\widehat A)M. \]

Because \(\widehat B\) is column-stochastic,

\[ \|I_n-\widehat A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\sqrt n, \qquad \|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le H\sqrt{nd}. \]

Consequently,

\[ \mathbb E\|\widehat L-\bar L\|^2 \le \frac{8n}{\delta^2}\mathbb E\|\widetilde M-M\|^2 + \frac{2a^2ndH^2}{\delta^2} \mathbb E\|\widehat B-B\|^2. \]

Every mass satisfies \(\mu_t\le1\), so the requested weighted risk is bounded by the unweighted squared error. Combining the inequalities yields

\[ \begin{aligned} &\mathbb E\sum_t\mu_t \left( \sum_s(\widehat B_{st}-B_{st})^2 + \sum_j(\widehat L_{tj}-\bar L_{tj})^2 \right) \\ &\qquad\le \left(\frac{WC_0}{a^2\rho^2}+C_1\right)V + \frac{3Wn\rho^2}{a^2\lambda^2} \\ &\qquad\le TV+\frac{e^2}{12nW}. \end{aligned} \]

The last equality for the bias term follows from the specified choice of \(\rho\).

4. Verification of the sample and precision choices

By the stated choice of \(N\),

\[ \frac1{N\delta}\le\frac{e}{4T}. \]

By the stated choice of \(q\),

\[ h=2^{-q}\le\frac{e}{4T}\le1, \qquad h^2\le\frac{e}{4T}. \]

Hence \(TV\le e/2\). Also \(nW\ge1\) and \(e\le1\), so

\[ \frac{e^2}{12nW}\le\frac e{12}. \]

The requested risk is therefore at most

\[ \frac e2+\frac e{12} =\frac{7e}{12} \le\varepsilon. \]

This holds uniformly over all promised models.

5. Bit complexity and scope of the conclusion

Every rounded entry is a signed dyadic rational of magnitude at most \(H\), requiring \(O(q+\log_2(1+H))\) bits. The empirical moments have common denominators dividing \(N^2 2^{2q}\); their numerators consequently have bit length

\[ O(q+\log N+\log n+\log d+\log(1+H)). \]

The parameters \(\rho,W,T\) require a fixed number of rational arithmetic operations on the scalar inputs and \(n,d\). Their encoded lengths are polynomial in \(L+\log n+\log d\).

For exact inversion, clear the common denominator of \(\widetilde C+\rho I_n\), obtaining an integer matrix. If its entries have at most \(s\) bits, the determinant expansion bounds every minor’s bit length by \(O(n(s+\log n))\). Exact elimination with fraction reduction, or fraction-free elimination, therefore computes the inverse in polynomial bit complexity. The subsequent products, simplex projections, and clipping likewise have polynomial bit complexity. Sorting requires only comparisons of rational numbers of polynomial bit length.

Finally, substituting \(\rho^{-1}=6nW/(\delta^2\kappa a e)\) into \(T\) shows explicitly that \(T\), and hence \(N\), is bounded by a polynomial in

\[ m,\tau,1+b,\kappa^{-1},\delta^{-1},(1-\delta)^{-1}, \max\{1,\varepsilon^{-1}\}. \]

The precision satisfies \(q=O(\log(T/e))\), and the arithmetic and bit-operation bounds are polynomial in the requested parameters and \(L\).

No lower bound on a positive edge weight, no minimum-mass bound, and no estimator-error independence assumption is used.
