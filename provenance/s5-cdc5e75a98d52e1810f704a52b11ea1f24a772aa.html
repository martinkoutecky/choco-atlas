The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given known renter types \(t\in T\) with masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1-\eta\), \(m\) room categories with normalized capacities \(q_r\) summing to \(1\), and one hidden renter of mass \(\eta=1/N\), determine whether there is a rent vector \(p\) with \(p_r\geq 0\) and \(\sum_r q_r p_r=\bar R\) such that, for every category \(r\) the hidden renter might choose, the known mass can be assigned to categories it most prefers at \(p\). Formally, find flows \(x^{[r]}_{t,s}\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_s x^{[r]}_{t,s}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x^{[r]}_{t,s}\leq q_s-\eta\mathbf{1}[s=r]\), and \(x^{[r]}_{t,s}>0\) only if \(s\in D_t(p)\), where \(D_t(p)\) is type \(t\)'s set of most-preferred categories.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) known renter types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
The input includes integers \(m,N\geq2\), a hidden renter of mass \(\eta=1/N\), positive rational capacities \(q_1,\ldots,q_m\) with \(\sum_s q_s=1\), and positive rational rent \(\bar R\); the known masses satisfy \(\sum_t\mu_t=1-\eta\).
Let \(P=\{p\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m:\sum_s q_sp_s=\bar R\}\). Each type is specified by real-valued continuous piecewise-affine utilities \(u_{t,s}:P\to\mathbb R\), given by an explicit rational polyhedral partition and rational affine formulas on its cells. Let \(K\) count all listed cells and \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Define \(D_t(p)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{s\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}u_{t,s}(p)\), retaining every tie. Inputs are promised to satisfy \(D_t(p)=\{s:p_s=0\}\) whenever a free category exists; otherwise \(D_t(p)\) is nonempty. There is no outside option.
A feasible price vector is a single \(p\in P\) such that, for every \(r\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), there are real flows \(x^{[r]}_{t,s}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_s x^{[r]}_{t,s}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x^{[r]}_{t,s}\leq q_s-\eta\mathbf1_{\{s=r\}}\), and \(x^{[r]}_{t,s}=0\) whenever \(s\notin D_t(p)\). The hidden renter takes its entire mass from one category; the flows may depend on that choice.

Is deciding whether a feasible price vector exists in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L,\log N\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finitely many renters and equally many rooms, a secretive envy-free rent division exists under the free-room and closed-preference conditions; a constructive algorithm for approximate divisions is known, without a polynomial runtime claim ([Frick–Houston-Edwards–Meunier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.07325)).
Polynomial-time computation is known for discrete secretive rent division with quasilinear utilities ([Arunachaleswaran–Barman–Rathi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.10859)); existence is also known with integer room capacities and one secretive tenant ([Segal-Halevi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.13249)).
No classification of this exact rational-mass decision problem was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Preferences lacked a finite encoding. Specify continuous piecewise-affine utilities on the full price vector through explicitly listed rational polyhedral partitions and affine formulas; utilities and their coefficients may have either sign.
  • Restore the free-room condition: all free categories are tied as best and strictly preferred to paid categories. Retain all maximizing categories, with no tie-breaking or outside option.
  • Use exact preferences and flows, as the continuous question requests, and impose continuity to retain the closed-preference condition underlying exact existence.
  • Require at least two categories, an integer N at least two encoded in binary, positive rational capacities, and positive rational rent.
  • Treat N as the reciprocal hidden mass, independently of the number of categories; allow arbitrary rational capacities and known masses without integer-multiple restrictions.
  • Require robustness to every category, including when its capacity is smaller than the hidden mass, as the stated flow constraints dictate.
  • Interpret prices as rent per unit mass and bar R as total rent for the society of total mass one; permit divisible known mass and assignments that change with the hidden renter's choice.

Classification

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. In fact, feasibility is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete on the promised input class.

The hardness holds even when \(\bar R=1\), \(N=m\), every capacity is \(1/m\), and all known masses are dyadic rationals. The reduction below uses polynomially many explicitly listed polyhedral cells, including a polynomial-size common partition for each type’s utilities.

1. Two consequences of robustness

Suppose \(q_s=\eta=1/m\) for every category.

First, no positive-mass type can have a unique demanded category at a feasible price vector. If \(D_t(p)=\{s\}\), the hidden choice \(r=s\) leaves zero capacity in that category, so the required flow for type \(t\) cannot exist.

Second, a type of mass greater than \(1-2/m\) must demand every category at a feasible price vector. Indeed, suppose its demand set \(A\) omits a category. Choose \(r\in A\), which is possible because demand is nonempty. The total capacity available in \(A\) after that hidden choice is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{s\in A}\left(\frac1m-\frac1m\mathbf 1_{\{s=r\}}\right)=\frac{|A|-1}{m}\leq\frac{m-2}{m}.\)

All of the type’s mass must flow into \(A\), contradicting its mass being greater than \(1-2/m\).

These observations will force a large type to demand every category and smaller types to satisfy encoded equations.

2. Parameters and masses

Reduce from a Boolean formula \(\Phi\) in \(3\)-CNF, with \(n\geq1\) variables and \(c\) clauses. Put \(d=n+c\).

Choose powers of two \(m\) and \(H\) such that

\(\displaystyle n+3\leq m<2(n+3),\qquad d\leq H<2d.\)

Set

\(\displaystyle N=m,\qquad \eta=q_s=\frac1m,\qquad \bar R=1.\)

Thus the price domain is

\(\displaystyle P=\left\{p\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m:\sum_{s=1}^m p_s=m\right\}.\)

There are \(d\) constraint types, each with mass

\(\displaystyle a=\frac1{2mH}.\)

Their total mass is

\(\displaystyle \delta=da\leq\frac1{2m}.\)

There is also a guard type, indexed by \(0\), with mass

\(\displaystyle \mu_0=1-\frac1m-\delta.\)

All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and their sum is \(1-\eta\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \mu_0\geq1-\frac{3}{2m}>1-\frac2m.\)

Consequently, Section 1 implies that any feasible price vector must make the guard type demand all \(m\) categories.

3. Guard utilities with a polynomial-size partition

We construct continuous piecewise-affine guard utilities whose demand set contains every category exactly on

\(\displaystyle B=\{p\in P:p_s\geq\tfrac12\text{ for every }s\}.\)

For \(p\in P\), define

\(\displaystyle g=\min_s p_s,\qquad z_s=p_s-g,\qquad S_k=\sum_{s=1}^k z_s,\qquad S_0=0,\)

and

\(\displaystyle T=\max\{0,m(1-2g)\}.\)

Define the guard utilities by

\(\displaystyle u_{0,s}(p)=-\min\{z_s,\max\{0,T-S_{s-1}\}\}.\)

These functions are continuous because they are formed from finitely many affine functions using minima and maxima.

Guard demand set

We have \(z_s\geq0\) and

\(\displaystyle S_m=m(1-g),\qquad 0\leq T\leq S_m.\)

For any nonnegative \(z_s\),

\(\displaystyle \min\{z_s,\max\{0,T-S_{s-1}\}\}=\min\{T,S_s\}-\min\{T,S_{s-1}\}.\)

To verify this identity, consider the three cases \(T\leq S_{s-1}\), \(S_{s-1}\leq T\leq S_s\), and \(S_s\leq T\). Both sides are respectively \(0\), \(T-S_{s-1}\), and \(z_s\). Summing therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{s=1}^m u_{0,s}(p)=-T.\)

Every guard utility is nonpositive. At a category attaining \(g\), we have \(z_s=0\), so its utility is zero. Hence the maximum guard utility is zero, and

\(\displaystyle D_0(p)=\{1,\ldots,m\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\in B.\)

Free-category promise

If a free category exists, then \(g=0\), so \(z_s=p_s\) and \(T=m=S_m\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle u_{0,s}(p)=-p_s.\)

Thus the guard demands exactly the free categories.

Explicit cells

The guard has a common rational polyhedral partition with at most \(m^2+1\) listed cells.

One cell is \(B\), on which all utilities are zero. For each \(j,k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), use the cell defined by

\(\displaystyle p_j\leq p_i\ \text{for every }i,\qquad p_j\leq\tfrac12,\)

and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i<k}(p_i-p_j)\leq m(1-2p_j)\leq\sum_{i\leq k}(p_i-p_j),\)

together with \(p\in P\). On this cell, the guard utilities are the following affine formulas:

  • For \(s<k\), \(u_{0,s}=-(p_s-p_j)\).
  • For \(s=k\), \(u_{0,k}=-m(1-2p_j)+\sum_{i<k}(p_i-p_j)\).
  • For \(s>k\), \(u_{0,s}=0\).

The cells cover \(P\): outside \(B\), choose a minimum-price index \(j\), and then a prefix \(k\) at which the nondecreasing sums \(S_k\) reach \(T\). The formulas agree on overlaps because each equals the continuous formula already defined.

All cell inequalities and affine coefficients have polynomial binary length.

4. Equations encoding the Boolean formula

Set \(\varepsilon=1/(2m)\), and encode variable \(i\) by

\(\displaystyle y_i(p)=\frac{p_{i+2}-1}{\varepsilon}=2m(p_{i+2}-1).\)

For each variable, define the nonnegative violation function

\(\displaystyle f_i(p)=\min\{|p_{i+2}-1|,\ |p_{i+2}-(1+\varepsilon)|\}.\)

Then

\(\displaystyle f_i(p)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y_i(p)\in\{0,1\}.\)

This is a continuous rational piecewise-affine function with four affine pieces. As a function of \(z=p_{i+2}\), its breakpoints are \(1\), \(1+\varepsilon/2\), and \(1+\varepsilon\); its successive formulas are

\(\displaystyle 1-z,\qquad z-1,\qquad 1+\varepsilon-z,\qquad z-1-\varepsilon.\)

For a literal \(\ell\), define its affine value by

\(\displaystyle v_\ell(p)=y_i(p)\ \text{if }\ell=X_i,\qquad v_\ell(p)=1-y_i(p)\ \text{if }\ell=\neg X_i.\)

For each clause \(C\), define

\(\displaystyle f_C(p)=\max\left\{0,1-\sum_{\ell\in C}v_\ell(p)\right\}.\)

This function has two affine pieces and is nonnegative everywhere. Once the variable equations hold, the literal values are Boolean, so

\(\displaystyle f_C(p)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C\text{ is satisfied}.\)

The \(d\) constraint types correspond to these \(n\) variable functions and \(c\) clause functions.

5. Constraint utilities and verification of the promise

For any one of the nonnegative violation functions \(f\), define

\(\displaystyle A(p)=2(m+2)\min_s p_s-(m+1).\)

The associated constraint type has utilities

\(\displaystyle u_{f,1}(p)=\max\{-p_1,A(p)\},\)

\(\displaystyle u_{f,2}(p)=\max\{-p_2,A(p)-f(p)\},\)

and \(u_{f,s}(p)=-p_s\) for \(s\geq3\).

These functions are continuous and piecewise affine.

If a free category exists, then \(A(p)=-(m+1)\). Since every \(p_s\leq m\) and \(f(p)\geq0\), both maxima select their \(-p_s\) branches. Thus every constraint type also has utilities exactly \(-p_s\) on the boundary and demands exactly the free categories.

If \(p\in B\), then \(A(p)\geq1\), whereas every \(-p_s\leq-1/2\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle D_f(p)=\{1,2\}\ \text{if }f(p)=0,\qquad D_f(p)=\{1\}\ \text{if }f(p)>0.\)

For the second assertion, category \(1\) has utility \(A(p)\). Both arguments defining category \(2\)’s utility are strictly smaller than \(A(p)\), and every other category has negative utility.

Explicit partition size

For each constraint type, first select a minimum-price index, giving \(m\) polyhedral regions. Refine by the at most four pieces of \(f\), and by the two branch choices for each of the two maxima.

This gives a common partition with at most \(16m\) cells. On each cell all utilities have rational affine formulas. Each cell is specified by polynomially many rational linear inequalities.

Thus the entire construction has polynomially bounded \(\tau\), \(K\), \(L\), and \(\log N\). In particular, the construction does not hide an exponentially large subdivision.

6. Correctness of the reduction

A feasible price vector yields a satisfying assignment

Suppose \(p\) is feasible.

Because \(\mu_0>1-2/m\), Section 1 forces \(D_0(p)=\{1,\ldots,m\}\). The guard construction therefore implies \(p\in B\).

For each constraint type, Section 5 now says its demand set is either \(\{1,2\}\) or \(\{1\}\). The latter is impossible for a positive-mass type by Section 1. Hence every violation function vanishes.

The variable violations yield \(y_i(p)\in\{0,1\}\) for every \(i\). Every clause violation then says that its clause is satisfied. These Boolean values consequently satisfy \(\Phi\).

A satisfying assignment yields prices and all required flows

Conversely, let \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\) satisfy \(\Phi\). Write \(k=\sum_i b_i\).

Set

\(\displaystyle p_1=p_2=1,\qquad p_{i+2}=1+\varepsilon b_i\quad(1\leq i\leq n),\qquad p_m=1-k\varepsilon,\)

and set every remaining coordinate to \(1\). The buffer category \(m\) is distinct from the variable categories because \(m\geq n+3\).

The price sum is \(m\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle p_m=1-\frac{k}{2m}\geq1-\frac{n}{2m}>\frac12,\)

and all other coordinates are at least \(1\). Hence \(p\in B\).

The guard demands every category. Every variable and clause violation is zero, so all constraint types demand \(\{1,2\}\).

Fix a hidden choice \(r\). Assign every constraint type entirely to category

\(\displaystyle b(r)=2\ \text{if }r=1,\qquad b(r)=1\ \text{otherwise}.\)

This category is demanded by every constraint type and differs from \(r\). Its total assigned mass is \(\delta\leq1/(2m)<1/m\).

Assign the guard the remaining capacities:

\(\displaystyle x^{[r]}_{0,s}=\frac1m-\frac1m\mathbf1_{\{s=r\}}-\delta\mathbf1_{\{s=b(r)\}}.\)

Every entry is nonnegative. Their sum is

\(\displaystyle 1-\frac1m-\delta=\mu_0.\)

Every category is demanded by the guard. Together with the specified constraint flows, these assignments satisfy every row requirement and fill each residual capacity exactly.

This construction works for every \(r\). Therefore the price vector is feasible.

We have proved

\(\displaystyle \Phi\text{ is satisfiable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{the constructed society admits a feasible price vector}.\)

Since the construction is polynomial, feasibility is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

7. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

We give a polynomial verification procedure for arbitrary promised inputs.

Nondeterministically choose one listed cell for each utility, and choose a set \(E_t\) of permitted demand edges for every type. On the selected cells, write each utility as its rational affine formula \(a_{t,s}(p)\).

Construct a rational linear feasibility problem with variables \(p\) and all \(x^{[r]}_{t,s}\). Impose:

  • Membership of \(p\) in \(P\) and every selected utility cell.
  • Every flow equality, capacity inequality, and nonnegativity condition from the question.
  • \(x^{[r]}_{t,s}=0\) whenever \(s\notin E_t\).
  • \(a_{t,s}(p)\geq a_{t,k}(p)\) for every \(s\in E_t\) and every category \(k\).

The last constraints ensure that every permitted flow edge is a genuine demand edge, with ties retained. The linear problem has polynomially many variables and constraints and polynomial encoding length. Rational linear feasibility is decidable in polynomial time.

If the linear problem is feasible, its solution is a feasible price vector with the required flows. Conversely, given a feasible price vector and flows, choose cells containing that price vector and set \(E_t=D_t(p)\). All the linear constraints then hold.

Thus feasibility belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\) on the promised input class. Together with the reduction, this proves the stated classification.

8. Relation to the cited existence results

The reduction uses unequal masses: one type has mass greater than \(1-2/m\), while each constraint type has mass strictly smaller than the hidden mass \(1/m\). These small masses cannot be represented as integer numbers of renters of mass \(1/m\).

The individual-tenant existence results of [Frick–Houston-Edwards–Meunier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.07325), and the capacity extension discussed by [Segal-Halevi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.13249), therefore do not establish existence for these weighted instances. The constructed utilities also fall outside the quasilinear model of the polynomial algorithm of [Arunachaleswaran–Barman–Rathi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.10859).

Consequently, a running time polynomial in the requested parameters would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
