The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational additive values \(p_{tj}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{tj}=r\) for each type, and per-capita supplies \(b_j\), what is the greatest threshold \(q\) for which masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) can be assigned to integral bundles \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\), and \(x_{t,S}=0\) whenever \(\sum_{j\in S}p_{tj}<q\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) resource categories. Inputs are binary rational values \(p_{tj}\ge0\), a common normalization \(r>0\) satisfying \(\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_{tj}=r\), and binary rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\ge0\).
Each individual receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), containing at most one unit of each category, with utility \(u_t(S)=\sum_{j\in S}p_{tj}\). Types may be split into arbitrary real masses receiving different bundles; empty bundles are allowed.
A threshold \(q\in[0,r]\) is feasible if real masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) satisfy \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_t\sum_{S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\), and \(x_{t,S}=0\) whenever \(u_t(S)<q\). Unused supply is allowed. Let \(q^*\) be the greatest feasible threshold.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including a queried rational threshold \(q\).

Is deciding whether \(q^*\ge q\) solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete egalitarian allocation with a common valuation sum is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even for two agents with identical valuations. [Carrero–Rodríguez–Rubio](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09354v1)
Exact max-min configuration-LP feasibility is also known to be \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard; its identical-agent special case coincides with the bundle-mixture model above after per-capita normalization. [Jansen–Rohwedder](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/01oasics/oasics-vol061_sosa2018/OASIcs.SOSA.2018.11/OASIcs.SOSA.2018.11.pdf)

  • Require positive common normalization r, following the definition of r-limited utilities rather than the proposition's inclusion of the degenerate zero case.
  • Take supplies to be nonnegative binary rationals, with unused supply permitted as specified by the graded inequalities.
  • Use nonempty support and at least one resource category; retain empty bundles and unrestricted splitting of each type's mass.

Result

The decision problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete, even when there is only one supported type and every per-capita supply equals $1/2$. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in $m,\tau,L$ would imply $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.

The hardness proof below applies directly to arbitrary real bundle mixtures.

1. Threshold feasibility and the optimum

Write $[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}$, and let
$$ V=\{u_t(S):1\le t\le\tau,\ S\subseteq[m]\}. $$
This is a finite set containing $0$. Threshold $0$ is feasible by assigning every individual the empty bundle.

If a threshold $q$ is feasible, take a feasible allocation and define
$$ v=\min\{u_t(S):x_{t,S}>0\}. $$
The minimum exists because there are finitely many variables and their total mass is $1$. Every positively used bundle has utility at least $q$, so $v\ge q$. The same allocation is feasible at threshold $v$, and $v\in V$.

It follows that the greatest feasible threshold exists and is the largest feasible member of $V$. Feasibility is downward closed, so
$$ q^*\ge q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\text{ is feasible}. $$

2. A polynomial reduction from $\mathrm{PARTITION}$

We use the standard $\mathrm{NP}$-complete problem $\mathrm{PARTITION}$: given positive integers $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ in binary, decide whether some subset $S\subseteq[n]$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{j\in S}a_j=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^n a_j. $$

Set $A=\sum_{j=1}^n a_j$. Construct a society with
$$ m=n,\qquad \tau=1,\qquad \mu_1=1, $$
and
$$ p_{1j}=a_j,\qquad r=A,\qquad b_j=\frac12,\qquad q=\frac A2. $$
All input hypotheses hold: the valuations are positive, their sum is $r>0$, and $q\in[0,r]$. The construction has polynomial binary length and can be computed in polynomial time.

A partition gives a feasible mixture

Suppose $S$ has valuation $A/2$. Its complement also has valuation $A/2$.

Assign mass $1/2$ to bundle $S$ and mass $1/2$ to bundle $[n]\setminus S$. Both bundles meet the threshold. Each category appears in exactly one of the two bundles, so its total consumption is $1/2$, exactly its supply. Thus $q$ is feasible.

A feasible mixture gives a partition

Conversely, suppose the constructed threshold is feasible. Write $x_S=x_{1,S}$ and define the consumption of category $j$ by
$$ c_j=\sum_{S:j\in S}x_S. $$
Feasibility implies
$$ \sum_Sx_S=1,\qquad c_j\le\frac12. $$
Every positively used bundle has utility at least $A/2$. Hence
$$ \frac A2 = \frac A2\sum_Sx_S \le \sum_Sx_Su_1(S) = \sum_{j=1}^n a_jc_j \le \frac12\sum_{j=1}^n a_j = \frac A2. $$
Both inequalities must therefore be equalities. In particular,
$$ \sum_Sx_S\left(u_1(S)-\frac A2\right)=0. $$
Each summand is nonnegative: if $x_S>0$, its bundle meets the threshold. Consequently,
$$ x_S>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_1(S)=\frac A2. $$
At least one bundle has positive mass because the masses sum to $1$. That bundle supplies a solution to $\mathrm{PARTITION}$.

Thus
$$ (a_1,\ldots,a_n)\text{ is a yes-instance of }\mathrm{PARTITION} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q^*\ge q $$
for the constructed society. This proves $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.

The key point is that arbitrary mixing cannot evade the reduction: the supply constraints force the average utility to equal the required minimum utility.

3. Polynomial-size certificates despite real masses

We now prove membership in $\mathrm{NP}$.

Fix an input threshold $q$, and retain only the allowed configurations
$$ \mathcal C_q=\{(t,S):u_t(S)\ge q\}. $$
Introduce a nonnegative slack variable $s_j$ for each supply constraint. Feasibility becomes
$$ \sum_{S:(t,S)\in\mathcal C_q}x_{t,S}=\mu_t \qquad(1\le t\le\tau), $$
$$ \sum_{(t,S)\in\mathcal C_q:j\in S}x_{t,S}+s_j=b_j \qquad(1\le j\le m). $$
Write this finite system as
$$ Hz=c,\qquad z\ge0. $$
Here $H$ has $d=\tau+m$ rows, all its entries belong to $\{0,1\}$, and
$$ c=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau,b_1,\ldots,b_m)^{\mathsf T}. $$

A feasible solution uses at most $d$ positive variables

Assume the system is feasible. Among its feasible solutions, choose one with the fewest positive coordinates.

Its positive-coordinate columns of $H$ are linearly independent. Indeed, otherwise there is a nonzero vector $h$, supported on those coordinates, with $Hh=0$. Change its sign if necessary so that some coordinate of $h$ is positive, and set
$$ \varepsilon=\min_{i:h_i>0}\frac{z_i}{h_i}. $$
Since all these $z_i$ are positive, $\varepsilon>0$. The vector $z-\varepsilon h$ remains nonnegative, satisfies the same equations, and has strictly fewer positive coordinates. This contradicts the choice of $z$.

Thus the number $k$ of positive coordinates satisfies $k\le d$.

Those coordinates have polynomial binary length

Select $k$ rows on which these independent columns form a nonsingular square matrix $B$. The positive coordinates are uniquely determined by
$$ Bz_{\mathrm{pos}}=c_R, $$
where $c_R$ is the selected part of $c$.

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of all entries of $c$, as encoded in the input. Then $Dc$ is integral. Since the total input length is $L$,
$$ \log_2D=O(L), \qquad \log_2\!\left(1+\max_i|(Dc)_i|\right)=O(L). $$
By Cramer's rule, every positive coordinate has the form
$$ (z_{\mathrm{pos}})_i=\frac{\det B_i'}{D\det B}, $$
where $B_i'$ replaces column $i$ of $B$ by $Dc_R$.

The determinant expansion gives
$$ |\det B|\le k!, \qquad |\det B_i'|\le k!\max_\ell|(Dc_R)_\ell|. $$
Therefore every positive coordinate is rational with binary length
$$ O\!\left(L+k\log(k+1)\right). $$

A certificate can consequently list at most $\tau+m$ positively used configurations, each specified by its type, its $m$-bit bundle, and its rational mass. The slack variables can be omitted.

A verifier checks nonnegativity, each listed bundle’s utility, all type-mass equalities, and all supply inequalities using exact rational arithmetic. The certificate length and verification time are polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. Hence the problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

4. Relation to the cited hardness results

Discrete allocation hardness alone does not automatically prove hardness after allowing fractional mixtures; Section 2 establishes the necessary equivalence directly.

The construction also agrees with Appendix A, Theorem 18, of [Jansen–Rohwedder](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/01oasics/oasics-vol061_sosa2018/OASIcs.SOSA.2018.11/OASIcs.SOSA.2018.11.pdf), which uses $\mathrm{PARTITION}$ and two identical machines to prove configuration-LP hardness and states that the max-min proof is analogous.

Together, the reduction and the certificate argument completely settle the queried decision problem.
