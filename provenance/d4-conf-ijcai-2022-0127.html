The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t\) of advertiser types \(t=(w,v,b)\), slot-class supplies \(q_k\), group-specific click-through rates \(\alpha_{wk}\), quality factors \(\gamma_w\), and \(\beta\leq 1\), compute group allocations \(x_{wk}\) with \(x_{hk}+x_{\ell k}=q_k\). For each group \(w\), define \(V_w(z)\) as the maximum of \(\sum_{t\in T_w,k}\gamma_w\alpha_{wk}b_t y_{tk}\), subject to \(\sum_{t\in T_w}y_{tk}=z_k\), \(\sum_k y_{tk}\leq\mu_t\), and \(y_{tk}\geq0\). Require \(V_h(x_h)\geq\beta V_h(x_\ell)\) and \(V_\ell(x_\ell)\geq\beta V_\ell(x_h)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) advertiser types \(t=(w,v_t,b_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(w\in\{h,\ell\}\) and \(v_t,b_t\geq0\) are rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; masses need not sum to one. Valuations \(v_t\) do not enter the fairness criterion, which uses reported bids \(b_t\).
There are \(m\geq1\) divisible slot classes with positive rational supplies \(q_k\). Put \(T_w=\{t:w_t=w\}\) and \(Q=\sum_{k=1}^m q_k\), and require \(\sum_{t\in T_w}\mu_t\geq Q\) for both groups.
All input numbers are binary rationals; \(L\) denotes their total encoding length. Quality factors satisfy \(\gamma_w\in[0,1]\), click-through rates satisfy \(1\geq\alpha_{w1}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{wm}\geq0\) for both groups in the same class order, and \(0<\beta\leq1\).
For \(0\leq z_k\leq q_k\), define \(V_w(z)=\max\{\sum_{t\in T_w}\sum_{k=1}^m\gamma_w\alpha_{wk}b_t y_{tk}:\sum_{t\in T_w}y_{tk}=z_k\ (\forall k),\ \sum_{k=1}^m y_{tk}\leq\mu_t\ (\forall t\in T_w),\ y_{tk}\geq0\}\). Each comparison evaluates this maximum independently.
The computational problem is to find rational allocations \(x_{wk}\geq0\) satisfying \(x_{hk}+x_{\ell k}=q_k\) for every class and \(V_h(x_h)\geq\beta V_h(x_\ell)\), \(V_\ell(x_\ell)\geq\beta V_\ell(x_h)\).

Can such an allocation be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For indivisible slots, group envy-cycle elimination computes a group \(\beta\)-EFX allocation: each group values its bundle at least \(\beta\) times its optimal assignment value for the other bundle after removal of any one slot. [Birmpas et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08271)
For a different model with online arrivals and binary matching preferences, fractional class envy-freeness admits a \(1-1/e\) approximation. [Hosseini et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03751) The search located no result addressing exactly the mass-allocation problem above.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Mass normalization was unspecified; masses are unnormalized advertiser quantities, preserving the interpretation of finite group sizes.
  • The discrete assumption that each group contains at least as many advertisers as there are slots becomes the requirement that each group's total mass is at least total slot supply, ensuring every comparison bundle is feasible.
  • The common monotone slot order is retained for both groups; click-through rates are probabilities in [0,1], with ties allowed.
  • Supplies are positive, quality factors lie in [0,1], bids and valuations are nonnegative, and all numerical inputs are binary rationals.
  • The fairness parameter is restricted to positive rationals at most one, consistently with its positive-ratio interpretation.
  • Allocations are nonnegative rational quantities; each counterfactual group value is optimized independently under the same type capacities.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The search problem is polynomial-time solvable. Equal division settles the full stated model, including arbitrary support sizes, bids, masses, and class supplies.

Construction. For every class \(k\), output
\[ x_{hk}=x_{\ell k}=\frac{q_k}{2}. \]
These rational allocations are nonnegative and satisfy \(x_{hk}+x_{\ell k}=q_k\).

Proof that the comparison values are well-defined. Fix a group \(w\) and any vector \(z\) satisfying \(0\le z_k\le q_k\). Write \(M_w=\sum_{t\in T_w}\mu_t\). The assumptions give \(M_w\ge Q>0\). Define
\[ y_{tk}=\frac{\mu_t z_k}{M_w}. \]
Then
\[ \sum_{t\in T_w}y_{tk}=z_k, \qquad \sum_{k=1}^{m}y_{tk} =\frac{\mu_t\sum_{k=1}^{m}z_k}{M_w} \le\mu_t, \]
because \(\sum_k z_k\le Q\le M_w\). Thus the feasible set defining \(V_w(z)\) is nonempty. It is closed and bounded: each variable satisfies \(0\le y_{tk}\le\mu_t\). Consequently the linear objective attains a finite maximum. Its coefficients are nonnegative, so \(V_w(z)\ge0\).

Proof of fairness. Let \(r=(q_1/2,\ldots,q_m/2)\). Since \(x_h=x_\ell=r\), both independent comparisons for group \(w\) evaluate exactly the same optimization problem. Therefore
\[ V_h(x_h)-\beta V_h(x_\ell) =(1-\beta)V_h(r)\ge0, \]
and
\[ V_\ell(x_\ell)-\beta V_\ell(x_h) =(1-\beta)V_\ell(r)\ge0. \]
Both required inequalities hold, including when \(\beta=1\).

Complexity and scope. The algorithm performs \(m\) divisions by two; each resulting rational has encoding length at most a constant larger than that of its input supply. Reading the input and producing the allocation therefore takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Computing the maxima is unnecessary.

This construction applies to every permitted instance. A further requirement such as welfare optimization would create an additional question, but no such requirement occurs in the stated search problem.

This argument also covers zero coordinates of \(z\) and the boundary case \(M_w=Q\).

The feasible set has finitely many variables. Its linear equalities and weak inequalities make it closed, and the capacity constraints imply \(0\leq y_{tk}\leq\mu_t\), making it bounded. Together with nonemptiness, these facts ensure that the continuous linear objective attains a finite maximum.

Every objective coefficient \(\gamma_w\alpha_{wk}b_t\) is nonnegative under the stated assumptions, so \(V_w(z)\geq0\). Zero bids, zero quality factors, and zero click-through rates introduce no exception.
