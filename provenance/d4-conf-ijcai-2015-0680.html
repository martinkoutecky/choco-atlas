The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a constant number \(m\) of goods, a finite set \(T\) of bidder types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), explicitly listed integral bundles \(D_t\) with rational values \(v_t(d)\), and rational per-capita supplies \(b_\ell\), choose masses \(x_{t,d}\ge 0\) to maximize \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{d\in D_t}v_t(d)x_{t,d}\), subject to \(\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}\le\mu_t\) for each \(t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{d\in D_t}d_\ell x_{t,d}\le b_\ell\) for each good \(\ell\).

Each type has an explicitly listed nonempty demand set \(D_t\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\setminus\{0\}\) and positive binary-rational values \(v_t(d)\). Require \(v_t(d)\le v_t(e)\) whenever \(d\le e\) componentwise. The value of any integral bundle \(a\) is \(\max(\{v_t(d):d\in D_t,\ d\le a\}\cup\{0\})\).
Supplies \(b_\ell\) are positive binary rationals measured per capita; individual bundles need not satisfy \(d_\ell\le b_\ell\). Mass may be split among alternatives, with unassigned mass receiving nothing.
For \(x_{t,d}\ge0\), write \(W(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{d\in D_t}v_t(d)x_{t,d}\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}(b)\) be the maximum of \(W(x)\) subject to \(\sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{d\in D_t}d_\ell x_{t,d}\le b_\ell\).
Let \(K=\sum_{t\in T}|D_t|\) and let \(L\) be the total binary encoding length of the instance.

The decisive premise is that mass may be split: the allocation variables are continuous. Thus the linear program in the question describes the allocation problem itself.

1. An explicit rational linear program

Let \(I=\{(t,d):t\in T,\ d\in D_t\}\), so \(|I|=K\). Define a matrix \(A\) with \(K\) columns indexed by \(I\) and \(\tau+m\) rows as follows:

  • In the row for type \(s\), set \(A_{s,(t,d)}=1\) if \(s=t\), and \(0\) otherwise.
  • In the row for good \(\ell\), set \(A_{\tau+\ell,(t,d)}=d_\ell\).

Let \(h\) consist of the masses followed by the supplies, and set \(c_{(t,d)}=v_t(d)\). The optimization problem is precisely

\( \displaystyle \max\{c^\top x:Ax\le h,\ x\ge0\}. \)

All coefficients are rational and explicitly available from the input. The program has \(K\) variables and \(\tau+m+K\) inequalities when nonnegativity is included.

Its encoding length is polynomial in \(\tau,K,m,L\). Indeed, even a dense representation introduces only \(K(\tau+m)\) matrix entries; the demand coordinates are copied from the input, and the additional entries are zeros and ones. In particular, no expansion of rational masses into individual bidders is required.

2. Feasibility, boundedness, and exact optimization

The feasible set is nonempty because \(x=0\) is feasible.

For every feasible point and every \((t,d)\in I\), nonnegativity and the type constraint imply

\( \displaystyle 0\le x_{t,d}\le\sum_{e\in D_t}x_{t,e}\le\mu_t\le1. \)

Consequently, the feasible set is bounded. It is also closed, being defined by finitely many non-strict linear inequalities. Therefore it is compact, and the continuous objective attains a finite maximum.

We apply the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: an explicitly encoded rational linear program can be solved in time polynomial in its binary encoding length; when feasible with finite optimum, an exact rational optimal solution can be returned. The relevant complexity is bit complexity, including coefficient lengths. This classical result is recalled in [Adler–Beling, *Polynomial Algorithms for Linear Programming over the Algebraic Numbers*, Introduction](https://adler.ieor.berkeley.edu/ilans_pubs/lp_algebraic_stoc_1992.pdf).

Every hypothesis has now been checked: the program is explicit, rational, feasible, and bounded. Hence an exact rational optimizer \(x^\star\) is computable in time polynomial in \(\tau,K,m,L\).

There is also no obstruction from the size of the exact output. To see this, let \(S\) be the program’s binary encoding length. A bounded nonempty polyhedron has an optimal vertex. At a vertex, \(K\) linearly independent active constraints determine its coordinates. Clearing denominators in those equations produces an integer system whose coefficients and right-hand side have absolute values at most \(2^{2S}\). Cramer’s rule and the determinant expansion bound every coordinate’s numerator and denominator by \(K!2^{2SK}\). Their bit lengths are therefore \(O(K(S+\log K))\), which is polynomial. Output rationals can be represented by binary numerators and denominators.

3. The required guarantees

Return the exact optimizer \(x^\star\) under the original supplies. By its optimality and feasibility,

\( \displaystyle W(x^\star)=\mathrm{OPT}(b), \)

\( \displaystyle \sum_{d\in D_t}x^\star_{t,d}\le\mu_t\qquad(t\in T), \)

and

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{d\in D_t}d_\ell x^\star_{t,d}\le b_\ell\le(1+\varepsilon)b_\ell\qquad(\ell=1,\ldots,m). \)

These are stronger than the requested guarantees. The allocation algorithm does not use \(\varepsilon\), so its running time is certainly polynomial in \(\tau,K,L,1/\varepsilon\) for every fixed \(m\).

4. Why indivisible bidders differ

In the [Krysta–Telelis–Ventre setting](https://ranger.uta.edu/~weems/NOTES6319/PAPERSTWO/Mechanisms_for_Multi-unit_Comb.pdf), each bidder receives one alternative or nothing. Here, a type’s mass may be distributed among alternatives, so the continuous variables have their stipulated allocation meaning.

Integral demand coordinates do not impose integrality on allocated mass. For example, with \(m=1\), one type of mass \(1\), demand set \(\{(2)\}\), value \(1\), and supply \(1\), the mass allocation \(x_{t,(2)}=1/2\) is feasible and optimal.

Thus exact linear programming settles the intended mass model, including demands larger than per-capita supplies. No additional restriction or interpretation is needed.
