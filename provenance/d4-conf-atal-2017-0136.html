The continuous mirror question, as graded

For \(m\) candidates and \(k\) sites, take a globally ordered stream of at most \(r\) updates \((s,A,q)\), where \(s\) is a site, \(A\subseteq C\) is an approval ballot, and \(q>0\) is a binary rational encoded in at most \(L\) bits. At each prefix, let \(\lambda(A)\) be the total mass of ballot type \(A\), \(M=\sum_A\lambda(A)\), and \(\mu(A)=\lambda(A)/M\). A candidate \(c\) is an \(\epsilon\)-winner if some nonnegative mass measure \(\nu\), with \(\sum_A\nu(A)\le\epsilon M\), makes \(c\) maximize approval score in \(\lambda+\nu\). What is the minimum worst-case communication, in bits, of a deterministic protocol that reports an \(\epsilon\)-winner after every update, as a function of \(m,k,\epsilon,r,L\) and the total-mass bound \(N\)? In particular, for \(\epsilon<1/16\) and sufficiently large \(m\), does the lower bound \(\Omega((mk/\epsilon)\log(r/k))\) hold on rational-mass streams?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge2\) candidates; there are \(k\ge1\) sites and one center, connected by private bidirectional links in a star. Public parameters are \(r\ge1\), \(L\ge1\), rational \(N>0\), and rational \(0<\epsilon<1\).
An adversary supplies at most \(r\) globally ordered insertions \((s,A,q)\), with \(s\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), \(A\subseteq C\), and positive rational \(q\) whose binary numerator-denominator encoding has at most \(L\) bits; cumulative mass never exceeds \(N\). Only site \(s\) observes this insertion.
A society is represented by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) approval types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here these lists are maintained locally from insertions; the center is not given the global support. \(\tau\) denotes actual support size, not \(2^m\).
At a nonempty prefix, write \(\lambda(A)\) for cumulative mass, \(M=\sum_{A\subseteq C}\lambda(A)\), and \(\mu(A)=\lambda(A)/M\). Total mass is not normalized to one. Approval uses these masses, and every score maximizer is a co-winner.
A candidate \(c\) is an \(\epsilon\)-winner if some nonnegative real mass measure \(\nu\) on all approval types satisfies \(\sum_A\nu(A)\le\epsilon M\) and makes \(c\) a co-winner in \(\lambda+\nu\); added types need not belong to the existing support.
Protocols are deterministic, with unrestricted local computation. Communication triggered by an insertion finishes before the next insertion; sites can initiate communication upon an insertion or received message, and the center upon a received message. There are no accessible clocks, public update counters, or free notifications of insertions.
After every insertion, the center's maintained answer must be an \(\epsilon\)-winner, available without further communication. Cost is the worst-case total number of transmitted bits in both directions; local output is free. Express running times, if supplied, in \(m,\tau,L,r,k\) and the encoding lengths of \(\epsilon,N\).
What is the asymptotically minimum worst-case communication \(B(m,k,\epsilon,r,L,N)\)?
For \(\epsilon<1/16\) and sufficiently large \(m\), does \(B=\Omega((mk/\epsilon)\log(r/k))\) hold on rational-mass streams, or can it fail, and in which parameter regimes?
For unit-voter streams, deterministic Approval-winner tracking has a verified lower bound \(\Omega((mk/\epsilon)\log(n/k))\) bits under these approximation conditions, and a deterministic upper bound \(O((mk/\epsilon)\log(k/\epsilon)\log n)\) bits. [Source](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~arnoldf/papers/FT19.pdf).
No result settling the stated rational-mass communication problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified the private star network, local visibility of insertions, instantaneous processing between insertions, and maintained answers requiring no query communication.
  • Excluded clocks, public update counters, and free insertion notifications; adopted event-triggered communication to make the globally ordered stream an external order rather than shared information.
  • Made all stream bounds public, took N to be a positive rational upper bound on cumulative mass, and specified binary numerator-denominator encoding for arbitrary rational update masses.
  • Restricted correctness to nonempty prefixes, retained unnormalized cumulative mass, allowed empty approval ballots, and treated tied score maximizers as co-winners.
  • Allowed hypothetical added mass to be real and to use any approval type, including types absent from the current support.
  • Applied the support representation convention locally without giving the center the global society, which would change the communication problem.
  • Made the lower-bound question explicitly parameter-sensitive because the independent update, mass, and encoding bounds need not support every discrete asymptotic regime.

Rigorous partial solution

The proposed lower bound is not uniform over the stated parameters. The declared insertion limit can substantially exceed the feasible horizon; very small $\epsilon$ causes finite-horizon saturation; and the single-site case has much lower communication.

The general optimum remains open. The results below strengthen the supplied notes in two ways:

  • Masses can be communicated through a public multiplicative discretization, improving the cost of transmitting each insertion.
  • The finite-horizon lower bound holds for every $k\ge2$ and every feasible horizon of at least two insertions. This gives a matching bound in a saturation regime.

I also determine the optimum for at most two insertions when the mass cap permits every pair of admissible masses.

1. Definitions and feasible horizon

Let $\mathcal Q_L$ be the finite set of positive rationals admitted by the stipulated encoding. If $\mathcal Q_L$ is empty, or its minimum exceeds $N$, there are no legal insertions and $B=0$.

Otherwise, define
$$ a=\min\mathcal Q_L,\qquad b=\max\mathcal Q_L,\qquad R=\min\{r,\lfloor N/a\rfloor\},\qquad W=\min\{N,Rb\}. $$

Every insertion has mass at least $a$, so every legal stream has at most $R$ insertions. Conversely, $R$ repetitions of mass $a$ are legal. Thus $R$ is exactly the maximum feasible insertion count. At every nonempty prefix,
$$ a\le M\le W. $$

All logarithms below have base two. Under the usual binary numerator-denominator encoding,
$$ 2^{-L}\le a\le b\le2^L, \qquad |\mathcal Q_L|\le2^{L+1}. $$
Using the exact alphabet $\mathcal Q_L$ avoids dependence on minor encoding conventions.

Fix a public ordering of candidates and an initial candidate $c_0$ stored at the center. No running-time bound is asserted: the protocols use unrestricted local computation, as permitted by the task.

2. Exact characterization of an $\epsilon$-winner

For a mass measure $\lambda$, write
$$ S_\lambda(c)=\sum_{A\ni c}\lambda(A), \qquad S_\lambda^*=\max_{d\in C}S_\lambda(d). $$

Lemma 1. A candidate $c$ is an $\epsilon$-winner if and only if
$$ S_\lambda^*-S_\lambda(c)\le\epsilon M. $$

Proof. Suppose added mass $\nu$, of total mass $T\le\epsilon M$, makes $c$ a co-winner. For every $d$,
$$ S_\lambda(d)-S_\lambda(c) \le S_\nu(c)-S_\nu(d) \le T. $$
Taking the maximum over $d$ proves necessity.

Conversely, add mass $S_\lambda^*-S_\lambda(c)$ to the approval type $\{c\}$. This makes $c$ a co-winner and uses at most $\epsilon M$ mass. The definition permits this type whether or not it belongs to the existing support. $\square$

3. General upper bounds

3.1. Communicating rounded masses

Define the individually feasible mass alphabet
$$ \mathcal Q=\{q\in\mathcal Q_L:q\le N\}. $$
Set
$$ \beta=1+\epsilon/2. $$

Partition $\mathcal Q$ into the occupied intervals
$$ [a\beta^j,a\beta^{j+1}), \qquad j=0,1,\ldots. $$
Let $J$ be the number of occupied intervals. In each occupied interval, choose its smallest member as the public representative.

Upon an insertion $(s,A,q)$, site $s$ sends:

  • the $m$-bit characteristic vector of $A$;
  • the rank of $q$'s occupied interval among the $J$ occupied intervals.

The center replaces $q$ by the corresponding representative $\widehat q$ and maintains an exact maximizer of the resulting rounded election $\widehat\lambda$.

For every insertion,
$$ \widehat q\le q<\beta\widehat q, $$
so
$$ 0\le q-\widehat q < \left(1-\frac1\beta\right)q < \frac{\epsilon q}{2}. $$
Consequently, at every prefix, the total rounding loss satisfies
$$ E=M-\widehat M<\frac{\epsilon M}{2}. $$

If $c$ maximizes the rounded scores, then, for every $d$,
$$ S_\lambda(d)-S_\lambda(c) \le S_{\widehat\lambda}(d)+E-S_{\widehat\lambda}(c) \le E. $$
Lemma 1 proves correctness.

The link identifies the inserting site. The message itself supplies the notification of the insertion, so the protocol uses no free notifications. Its cost is
$$ B=O\!\left(R\bigl(m+\log(2+J)\bigr)\right). $$

Moreover,
$$ J\le|\mathcal Q_L| $$
and
$$ J\le1+\frac{\log(b/a)}{\log(1+\epsilon/2)}. $$
For $0<\epsilon<1$, the usual encoding bounds therefore give
$$ \log(2+J) = O\!\left( \min\left\{ L,\, \log(2+L)+\log(1/\epsilon) \right\} \right). $$

Define
$$ D= \min\left\{ L,\, \log(2+L)+\log(1/\epsilon) \right\}. $$

We have proved the improved general bound
$$ \boxed{B=O\!\left(R(m+D)\right).} $$

In particular, for fixed $\epsilon$, communicating a mass requires only $O(\log(2+L))$ bits in this protocol.

3.2. Simulating virtual unit voters

The deterministic unit-voter protocol supplied in the task has bit communication
$$ O\!\left( \frac{mk}{\eta} \log(2k/\eta)\log(2+n) \right) $$
at approximation parameter $\eta$. Its deterministic bit bound is discussed in Section 5.2 of [Filtser–Talmon, *Distributed monitoring of election winners*](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~arnoldf/papers/FT19.pdf).

Set
$$ h=\frac{\epsilon a}{4}, \qquad \eta=\frac{\epsilon}{2}. $$
Locally replace an insertion of mass $q$ by $\lfloor q/h\rfloor$ virtual unit voters with the same approval set.

Let $\widetilde\lambda$ be the virtual election scaled by $h$. Each insertion loses less than $h$, and
$$ h=\frac{\epsilon a}{4}\le\frac{\epsilon q}{4}. $$
Thus
$$ 0\le E=M-\widetilde M\le\frac{\epsilon M}{4}. $$

If the simulated protocol returns $c$, then
$$ \begin{aligned} S_\lambda^*-S_\lambda(c) &\le S_{\widetilde\lambda}^*-S_{\widetilde\lambda}(c)+E\\ &\le\frac{\epsilon}{2}\widetilde M+E\\ &\le\frac{3\epsilon M}{4}. \end{aligned} $$
Lemma 1 again proves correctness.

Every actual insertion produces virtual voters because $q/h\ge4/\epsilon$. Their total number is at most
$$ \frac{W}{h}=\frac{4W}{\epsilon a}. $$
Therefore
$$ B= O\!\left( \frac{mk}{\epsilon} \log(2k/\epsilon) \log\!\left(2+\frac{W}{\epsilon a}\right) \right). $$

The virtual insertions are local computation. All simulated communication completes before the next actual insertion, and the center receives only messages generated by the simulated protocol.

Since the protocol choice depends only on public parameters, the two bounds combine to give
$$ \boxed{ B= O\!\left( \min\left\{ R(m+D),\, \frac{mk}{\epsilon} \log(2k/\epsilon) \log\!\left(2+\frac{W}{\epsilon a}\right) \right\} \right). } $$

3.3. One site

When $k=1$, the site knows the entire election. It mirrors the center's current candidate, initially $c_0$. Whenever this candidate ceases to be an $\epsilon$-winner, the site sends the name of an exact maximizer.

Suppose a report occurs at mass $M_0$, followed by another report after additional mass $\Delta$. The previously reported candidate's deficit can increase by at most $\Delta$: every competitor gains at most $\Delta$, while its own score cannot decrease. A new report therefore requires
$$ \Delta>\epsilon(M_0+\Delta), $$
and hence
$$ M_0+\Delta>\frac{M_0}{1-\epsilon}. $$

The first report occurs at mass at least $a$, and no prefix exceeds $W$. Thus
$$ \boxed{ B(m,1,\epsilon,r,L,N) = O\!\left( \log m\cdot \min\left\{ R,\, 1+\frac{\log(W/a)} {\log(1/(1-\epsilon))} \right\} \right). } $$

This includes the bound $O(R\log m)$.

4. A communication lemma for weighted phases

The lower bounds use a fixed schedule of blocks, each involving one active site.

4.1. Serialization and transcript rectangles

For clarity, use the following serialization convention in the communication reduction.

The public block order identifies the active site. Only that site receives insertions during the block. A fixed delivery order serializes messages initiated by the active site and all resulting messages. Center-to-site recipients are determined by the simulated center's state and message history; site-to-center messages have a fixed recipient. Thus sender and recipient identities do not need to be transmitted as arbitrary site labels.

Encode a nonempty original message by doubling its payload bits and adding a terminator. Add completion indicators for activated handlers and one marker when the active site finishes its whole block. The number of handler activations is at most the number of original messages plus the number of blocks. This gives total overhead
$$ O(\text{original transmitted bits}+\text{number of blocks}). $$

A block may contain many unit insertions. The active site processes them locally; only one block-completion marker is required. These markers belong to the reduction and are never delivered to the simulated tracking protocol. Similarly, validation messages below do not modify its state.

Under this convention, the next transmitting role is determined by the public schedule and transcript. A fixed deterministic transcript consequently defines a rectangle in the sites' private inputs: replacing each site's input by another input consistent with that transcript preserves the transcript. This follows inductively, because each transmitted bit depends only on that site's input and its preceding received history.

4.2. Hard approval arrays

Use an even number $K=2p$ of sites. There is a code
$$ \mathcal E\subseteq\{0,1\}^p $$
with pairwise Hamming distance greater than $p/4$ and
$$ |\mathcal E|\ge2^{\gamma p}, \qquad \gamma=1-H_2(1/4)>0, $$
where
$$ H_2(u)=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u). $$

To prove this, greedily select strings and remove Hamming balls of radius $\lfloor p/4\rfloor$. Each ball contains at most
$$ \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor p/4\rfloor}\binom pj \le2^{H_2(1/4)p} $$
strings. For the last inequality, put $u=1/4$. For every integer $j\le p/4$,
$$ u^j(1-u)^{p-j} \ge u^{p/4}(1-u)^{3p/4}. $$
Apply this inequality to the corresponding terms in the binomial expansion of $(u+(1-u))^p=1$. The greedy packing bound follows.

For each candidate and each of $t$ phases, independently choose a codeword. In site pair $j$, assign its $j$th bit to the first site and the complementary bit to the second. Every candidate is therefore approved by exactly $p$ sites in each phase.

There are at least
$$ |\mathcal E|^{mt}\ge2^{\gamma mpt} $$
such arrays.

Consider two different arrays. At some phase-candidate coordinate their codewords differ in $d>p/4$ positions. Mix the arrays by selecting each site's entire input from one of the two arrays, choosing an approving row whenever possible at that coordinate. The selected candidate then has
$$ p+d>\frac{5p}{4} $$
approving sites.

Consequently, these arrays form a fooling family for the promise problem:

  • negative inputs have at most $p$ approvals at every phase-candidate coordinate;
  • positive inputs have more than $5p/4$ approvals at some coordinate.

If two members of the family shared a transcript of a correct deterministic protocol, their positive mixed input would share that transcript, contradicting correctness. Counting transcripts gives a lower bound
$$ \Omega(mKt) $$
bits.

4.3. Realizing the arrays as elections

Let $q_s>0$ be the public aggregate block mass in phase $s$, and define
$$ x_1=0, \qquad x_{s+1}=x_s+q_s. $$

In phase $s$:

  • Each of the $K$ sites inserts aggregate mass $q_s$ approving its private set $A_{j,s}$.
  • The reduction inspects the center's maintained candidate.
  • Each site inserts aggregate mass $q_s$ approving $C\setminus A_{j,s}$.

A block can be one weighted insertion or several equal-weight insertions.

At the beginning of phase $s$, every site has total mass $2x_s$, and every candidate has local score $x_s$. The complementary block restores this invariant with $x_s$ replaced by $x_{s+1}$.

At the inspected prefix, if candidate $c$ has $h_c$ current approving sites, then
$$ S(c)=Kx_s+h_cq_s, \qquad M=K(2x_s+q_s). $$

Assume
$$ \frac{q_s}{4}>2\epsilon(2x_s+q_s). $$
If some candidate has more than $5p/4$ approvals, its score exceeds that of every candidate with at most $p$ approvals by more than
$$ \frac{pq_s}{4} > \epsilon K(2x_s+q_s) = \epsilon M. $$
Thus an $\epsilon$-winner must have more than $p$ approvals.

The reduction broadcasts the maintained candidate's name and collects its membership bit from every site. A negative input never yields more than $p$ memberships; a positive input necessarily does so at its relevant phase.

Validation and block markers cost
$$ O\!\left(t(K+\log m)\right) $$
bits, in addition to a constant multiple of the tracking communication. For sufficiently large $m$, this overhead is absorbed by the fooling-family lower bound.

We have proved:

Lemma 2. If $t$ phases are legally realizable and satisfy
$$ \frac{q_s}{4}>2\epsilon(2x_s+q_s) $$
in every phase, then
$$ B=\Omega(mKt) $$
for sufficiently large $m$.

5. A lower bound for every nontrivial feasible horizon

Assume
$$ k\ge2,\qquad R\ge2,\qquad 0<\epsilon<1/16. $$

Theorem 3. For sufficiently large $m$,
$$ \boxed{ B= \Omega\!\left( \frac{mk}{\epsilon} \log\!\left(1+\frac{\epsilon R}{k}\right) \right). } $$

Proof for a fixed even number of sites. First use $K\le k$ even, with $R\ge2K$. Implement every elementary insertion with mass $a$. Set
$$ q_1=a, \qquad q_s=a\left\lceil\frac{32\epsilon x_s}{a}\right\rceil \quad(s\ge2). $$
These are aggregate block masses; the elementary insertions all have the admissible mass $a$.

For phase one, Lemma 2's inequality follows from $\epsilon<1/16$. For subsequent phases,
$$ q_s\ge32\epsilon x_s, $$
so
$$ \begin{aligned} q_s(1/4-2\epsilon)-4\epsilon x_s &\ge 32\epsilon x_s(1/4-2\epsilon)-4\epsilon x_s\\ &= 4\epsilon x_s(1-16\epsilon)>0. \end{aligned} $$
This is the required inequality.

Writing $u_s=x_s/a$ gives
$$ u_{s+1}\le(1+32\epsilon)u_s+1, \qquad u_1=0. $$
Induction yields
$$ u_{t+1} \le \frac{(1+32\epsilon)^t-1}{32\epsilon}. $$
Both approval and complementary blocks together use $2Ku_{t+1}$ insertions. Therefore choose
$$ t= \left\lfloor \frac{\log(1+16\epsilon R/K)} {\log(1+32\epsilon)} \right\rfloor. $$
The construction uses at most $R$ insertions and hence mass at most $Ra\le N$.

Because $R\ge2K$, the expression being floored is at least one, so its floor is at least half its value. Also $\log(1+32\epsilon)=O(\epsilon)$. Lemma 2 gives
$$ B= \Omega\!\left( \frac{mK}{\epsilon} \log\!\left(1+\frac{\epsilon R}{K}\right) \right). $$

Choosing the number of active sites. For $R\ge4$, take
$$ K= 2\left\lfloor \frac{\min\{k,R/2\}}2 \right\rfloor. $$
Then $R\ge2K$ and
$$ K\ge\frac12\min\{k,R/2\}. $$

If $k\le R/2$, then $K\ge k/2$, and the last lower bound is at least a constant multiple of the claimed bound.

If $k>R/2$, then $K\ge R/4$ and
$$ \frac{\epsilon R}{K}\le4\epsilon<1/4. $$
Using $\log(1+z)\ge z/((1+z)\ln2)$, the lower bound is $\Omega(mR)$. This implies the claimed bound because
$$ \frac{k}{\epsilon}\log(1+\epsilon R/k) \le\frac{R}{\ln2}. $$

For $R=2$ or $R=3$, it remains to prove $\Omega(m)$ communication. Two sites insert mass $a$, approving respectively $U$ and $V$. If $U\cap V$ is nonempty, every $\epsilon$-winner lies in the intersection: such candidates have score $2a$, while all others have score at most $a$, and
$$ a>2\epsilon a. $$

Thus the tracker, followed by candidate-membership validation, decides whether $U\cap V$ is empty. The $2^m$ pairs
$$ (U,C\setminus U) $$
form a fooling family for this decision problem: for distinct $U,U'$, at least one crossed pair has nonempty intersection. Hence the decision problem requires at least $m-O(1)$ bits. Validation and serialization add only $O(\log m)$ bits, proving $B=\Omega(m)$.

For $R\in\{2,3\}$, the claimed expression is $O(mR)=O(m)$, completing the proof. $\square$

A matching saturation regime

For $0\le z\le1$, $\log(1+z)$ is bounded above and below by positive constant multiples of $z$. Therefore, if
$$ k\ge2,\qquad R\ge2,\qquad \frac{\epsilon R}{k}\le1, $$
Theorem 3 gives $B=\Omega(mR)$.

Combining this with Section 3.1 proves:
$$ \boxed{ B=\Theta(mR) \quad\text{if}\quad \frac{\epsilon R}{k}\le1 \ \text{and}\ D=O(m), } $$
for $\epsilon<1/16$ and sufficiently large $m$.

This resolves a substantial regime of simultaneous parameter growth.

6. Exact optimum for at most two insertions

Assume
$$ r=2,\qquad k\ge2,\qquad N\ge2b,\qquad 0<\epsilon<1/16. $$
The mass-cap condition ensures that every pair of admissible masses is legal.

Define
$$ \rho=\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}. $$
Let $P$ be the maximum cardinality of a subset of $\mathcal Q_L$ whose elements, in increasing order, have consecutive ratios strictly greater than $\rho$.

Theorem 4. For sufficiently large $m$,
$$ \boxed{B=\Theta\!\left(m+\log(2+P)\right).} $$

Upper bound

Use the multiplicative-grid protocol from Section 3.1. We show that its number of occupied bins satisfies
$$ J\le8P. $$

Greedily select the smallest remaining mass $q$, remove all remaining masses in $[q,\rho q]$, and repeat. The selected masses form a $\rho$-separated set, so their number is at most $P$. Their removal intervals cover $\mathcal Q_L$.

Each such interval intersects at most
$$ \left\lceil \frac{\log\rho}{\log(1+\epsilon/2)} \right\rceil+2 $$
grid bins. For $\epsilon<1/16$,
$$ \ln\rho\le\frac{2\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}, \qquad \ln(1+\epsilon/2)\ge\frac{\epsilon}{2+\epsilon}. $$
Their ratio is less than $5$, so eight bins suffice. Hence $J\le8P$.

There are at most two insertion messages, giving
$$ B=O(m+\log(2+P)). $$

Lower bound from approval sets

The two-insertion intersection construction in Theorem 3 is legal because $N\ge2b\ge2a$. It gives
$$ B=\Omega(m). $$

Lower bound from mass precision

Choose separated masses
$$ q_1<\cdots<q_P. $$
Fix two candidates $c,d$. Site one inserts $(\{c\},x)$, followed by site two inserting $(\{d\},y)$.

For $i<j$, separation gives
$$ q_j-q_i>\epsilon(q_i+q_j). $$
Thus $d$ is the unique $\epsilon$-winner on $(x,y)=(q_i,q_j)$, while $c$ is the unique $\epsilon$-winner on $(q_j,q_i)$.

Consider the $P$ diagonal inputs $(q_i,q_i)$. If two diagonals shared a transcript, the rectangle property would give that same transcript on both crossed inputs. The center would return the same candidate on those inputs, contradicting their different unique $\epsilon$-winners.

Consequently there are at least $P$ transcripts. Serialization adds only constant overhead for the two insertion blocks, yielding
$$ B=\Omega(\log P)-O(1). $$

Combining the two lower bounds proves the theorem. $\square$

Explicit dependence on the encoding budget

Under the usual binary numerator-denominator encoding, for sufficiently large $L$,
$$ \log(2+P) = \Theta\!\left( \min\{L,\log(2+L)+\log(1/\epsilon)\} \right). $$

The upper bound follows from $P\le|\mathcal Q_L|$ and
$$ (P-1)\log\rho<\log(b/a)=O(L), $$
with $\log\rho$ bounded below by a positive constant multiple of $\epsilon$.

For the lower bound, powers of two with exponents between zero and a sufficiently small constant multiple of $L$ are admissible. Their ratios are $2>\rho$, giving
$$ P=\Omega(L). $$

Also choose $d=\lfloor cL\rfloor$ for a sufficiently small fixed $c>0$. The dyadic rationals
$$ \frac{j}{2^d}, \qquad 2^d\le j\le2^{d+1}, $$
are admissible. Set
$$ h=\lfloor4\epsilon2^d\rfloor+1. $$
Taking every $h$th numerator gives a set of size
$$ \Omega\!\left(\min\{2^d,1/\epsilon\}\right). $$
Distinct selected masses differ by more than $4\epsilon$. Since both lie in $[1,2]$, their difference exceeds $\epsilon$ times their sum, which is equivalent to ratio greater than $\rho$.

These constructions give both
$$ \log(2+P)=\Omega(\log(2+L)) $$
and
$$ \log(2+P)=\Omega(\min\{L,\log(1/\epsilon)\}). $$
Together they imply the asserted lower bound.

Thus Theorem 4 becomes
$$ \boxed{ B= \Theta\!\left( m+\min\{L,\log(2+L)+\log(1/\epsilon)\} \right) } $$
in this two-insertion regime.

7. When the proposed lower bound holds or fails

A sufficient regime where it holds

Assume
$$ k\ge2,\qquad N\ge ra,\qquad r/k\ge\epsilon^{-2},\qquad 0<\epsilon<1/16. $$
Then $R=r$. Writing $y=r/k$, we have $\epsilon y\ge\sqrt y$, so
$$ \log(1+\epsilon y)\ge\frac12\log y. $$
Theorem 3 consequently proves
$$ \boxed{ B=\Omega\!\left( \frac{mk}{\epsilon}\log(r/k) \right). } $$

These are sufficient conditions, not a characterization of all regimes.

Failure through the mass cap

If $R=1$, the observing site sends the name of any approved candidate when needed. If its approval set is empty, the initial candidate remains a winner. Thus communication is $O(\log m)$.

Conversely, singleton approval sets at one fixed site force $m$ different answers, since $\epsilon<1$. Transcript counting gives $\Omega(\log m)$. Therefore
$$ \boxed{B=\Theta(\log m)\quad\text{when }R=1.} $$
The declared $r$ can be arbitrarily large.

The failure persists with a growing feasible horizon. Fix $k=2$, $\epsilon=1/32$, and constant $L$ admitting a positive rational. Set
$$ N=a\lfloor\log r\rfloor. $$
Then $R=\lfloor\log r\rfloor$. The virtual-voter upper bound gives $O(m\log R)$, while Theorem 3 gives $\Omega(m\log R)$. Hence
$$ B=\Theta(m\log\log r). $$
The proposed expression has order $m\log r$, exceeding the optimum by an unbounded factor.

More generally, for fixed $k\ge2$, fixed $\epsilon<1/16$, and fixed admissible $L$, as $R$ tends to infinity,
$$ B=\Theta(m\log R) $$
for sufficiently large $m$.

Failure through finite-horizon saturation

Fix $k=2$ and constant admissible $L$, and take
$$ N=ra,\qquad \epsilon=r^{-2}. $$
Then $R=r$ and $D=O(1)$. Section 3.1 gives $B=O(mr)$, and the saturation regime gives the matching lower bound:
$$ B=\Theta(mr). $$
The proposed expression is
$$ \Theta(mr^2\log r), $$
so it fails by an unbounded factor.

This obstruction already occurs when every insertion has the same mass. The supplied unit-voter formula therefore also requires a parameter regime; it cannot be uniform over arbitrarily small $\epsilon$ and finite horizons.

Failure with one site

Fix $\epsilon=1/32$, constant admissible $L$, $N=ra$, and $k=1$. Then $W/a=O(r)$, so Section 3.3 gives
$$ B=O(\log m\log r). $$
For example, with $m=r^2$, this is $O((\log r)^2)$, while the proposed expression has order $r^2\log r$.

Thus the single-site exception is intrinsic to the stated model.

8. Rational masses can sustain linearly many hard phases

The mass cap alone does not force logarithmic dependence on the insertion count.

Fix
$$ k=2,\qquad \epsilon=1/32,\qquad N=2,\qquad L=\Theta(r). $$

This example shows why rational-mass tracking cannot generally be described solely by the logarithmic horizon dependence of equal-weight streams.
