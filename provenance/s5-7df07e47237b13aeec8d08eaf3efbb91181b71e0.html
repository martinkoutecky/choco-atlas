Lemma 18 (Confidence intervals) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_u>0\) summing to one, positive rational weights \(w_u\), nonnegative rational coefficients \(\kappa_{tu}\), a rational quota offset \(\theta\), a target type \(t\), and accuracy \(\epsilon>0\), form populations of size \(N\) with \(N\mu_u\) members of each type. Set \(q_N=N\bar w/2+\lfloor\theta\sqrt N\rfloor\), where \(\bar w=\sum_u\mu_u w_u\); for distinct members \(i,j\) of types \(t,u\), set \(a^{(N)}_{ij}=\lfloor\kappa_{tu}\sqrt N\rfloor/N\), with \(a^{(N)}_{ii}=1\). Choose uniformly a coalition containing a tagged type-\(t\) member, and let \(\beta_{t,N}\) be the probability that it meets the paper’s association-adjusted criticality condition. Compute the limit \(\lim_{N\to\infty}\beta_{t,N}\) to additive error \(\epsilon\).

Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; there is no separate candidate parameter \(m\).
For integers \(N\) with every \(N\mu_u\) integral, create \(N\mu_u\) members of type \(u\), each of weight \(w_u\). Define \(\bar w=\sum_u\mu_uw_u\) and \(q_N=N\bar w/2+\lfloor\theta\sqrt N\rfloor\). For distinct members of types \(v,u\), put \(a^{(N)}_{ij}=\lfloor\kappa_{vu}\sqrt N\rfloor/N\), and put \(a^{(N)}_{ii}=1\). Restrict to sufficiently large admissible \(N\) so that \(N\geq2\), \(q_N>0\), and all association entries are at most \(1\).
Fix a tagged member \(i\) of type \(t\). The adjustment sums over the entire population. Let \(\beta_{t,N}\) be this event's probability under the uniform distribution on coalitions containing \(i\).

Does \(B_t=\lim_{N\to\infty}\beta_{t,N}\), along admissible population sizes, always exist and admit additive approximation within \(\epsilon\), with probability at least \(1-\delta\), in time polynomial in \(\tau,L,\epsilon^{-1},\log(\delta^{-1})\); or is this additive approximation problem NP-hard?

For finite populations, independent uniform coalition sampling gives additive error \(\epsilon\) with confidence at least \(1-\delta\) using \(\lceil\ln(2/\delta)/(2\epsilon^2)\rceil\) samples ([confidence guarantee](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.08029)). Result

The limit always exists, and its additive approximation is deterministically polynomial-time solvable.

Define
\(S=\sum_{u\in T}\mu_u w_u^2\) and \(A_t=\sum_{u\in T}\mu_u\kappa_{tu}w_u\).
Let \(\Phi\) be the standard normal distribution function. Then

\(\displaystyle B_t=\Phi\!\left(\frac{2(\theta+A_t)}{\sqrt S}\right)-\Phi\!\left(\frac{2\theta}{\sqrt S}\right).\)

Here \(S>0\), because every supported type has positive mass and weight. The algorithm below approximates this expression within \(\epsilon\) in time polynomial in \(\tau,L,\epsilon^{-1}\). Its success probability is \(1\), so it meets every requested confidence \(1-\delta\).

1. The criticality interval

Fix the input throughout the population limit. Write \(n_u=N\mu_u\) and

\(\displaystyle K_{u,N}=\lfloor\kappa_{tu}\sqrt N\rfloor.\)

The total adjustment for the tagged member is deterministic. Accounting separately for its diagonal entry gives

\(\displaystyle D_{t,N}:=\sum_{j=1}^N a^{(N)}_{ij}w_{u(j)} =w_t+\sum_{u\in T}\left(n_u-\mathbf 1_{\{u=t\}}\right)\frac{K_{u,N}}Nw_u.\)

In particular, \(D_{t,N}>0\). The given criticality condition is therefore exactly

\(\displaystyle q_N\leq W(C)<q_N+D_{t,N}.\)

Set \(\rho_{u,N}=\kappa_{tu}\sqrt N-K_{u,N}\), so \(0\leq\rho_{u,N}<1\). Substituting \(n_u=N\mu_u\) yields

\(\displaystyle D_{t,N} =A_t\sqrt N+w_t-\sum_{u\in T}\mu_uw_u\rho_{u,N} -\frac{w_tK_{t,N}}N.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \left|\frac{D_{t,N}}{\sqrt N}-A_t\right| \leq\frac{w_t+\bar w}{\sqrt N}+\frac{\kappa_{tt}w_t}{N} \longrightarrow0.\)

Thus the population-wide adjustment has limiting width \(A_t\) on the \(\sqrt N\) scale. The tagged member’s diagonal contribution vanishes on that scale.

There are arbitrarily large admissible population sizes: every multiple of a common denominator of the masses makes all \(N\mu_u\) integral. Moreover, \(\bar w>0\) implies \(q_N>0\) eventually, and

\(\displaystyle \frac{\lfloor\kappa_{vu}\sqrt N\rfloor}{N}\leq\frac{\kappa_{vu}}{\sqrt N}\leq1\)

eventually for every pair of types. Hence the restrictions remove only finitely many admissible sizes.

2. Gaussian convergence

Under the uniform distribution on coalitions containing \(i\), each other member belongs to the coalition independently with probability \(1/2\). Let \(X_j\) be these independent Bernoulli variables. Then

\(\displaystyle W(C)=w_t+\sum_{j\ne i}w_{u(j)}X_j.\)

Its mean and variance are

\(\displaystyle M_N:=\mathbb E[W(C)]=\frac{N\bar w}{2}+\frac{w_t}{2},\qquad V_N:=\operatorname{Var}(W(C))=\frac{NS-w_t^2}{4}.\)

For sufficiently large admissible \(N\), \(V_N>0\), and

\(\displaystyle \frac{\sqrt{V_N}}{\sqrt N}\longrightarrow\frac{\sqrt S}{2}.\)

We apply the Lindeberg central limit theorem in the following form: for a triangular array of independent, centered random variables, division by the square root of the total variance gives convergence to a standard normal variable if, for every \(h>0\), the normalized sum of second moments restricted to summands exceeding \(h\) times that square root tends to zero.

Here the centered summands are

\(\displaystyle \xi_{j,N}=w_{u(j)}(X_j-\tfrac12),\qquad j\ne i.\)

Their total variance is \(V_N\), and each satisfies

\(\displaystyle |\xi_{j,N}|\leq\frac12\max_{u\in T}w_u.\)

The right-hand side is fixed, while \(\sqrt{V_N}\) tends to infinity. For every fixed \(h>0\), all the indicators in the Lindeberg condition are therefore zero for sufficiently large \(N\). All hypotheses hold, giving

\(\displaystyle Y_N:=\frac{W(C)-M_N}{\sqrt{V_N}}\ \Longrightarrow\ Z,\)

where \(Z\) is standard normal.

The standardized criticality endpoints are

\(\displaystyle \alpha_N=\frac{q_N-M_N}{\sqrt{V_N}},\qquad \gamma_N=\frac{q_N+D_{t,N}-M_N}{\sqrt{V_N}}.\)

Because

\(\displaystyle q_N-M_N=\lfloor\theta\sqrt N\rfloor-\frac{w_t}{2},\)

the preceding limits imply

\(\displaystyle \alpha_N\longrightarrow x:=\frac{2\theta}{\sqrt S}, \qquad \gamma_N\longrightarrow y:=\frac{2(\theta+A_t)}{\sqrt S}.\)

Since \(A_t\geq0\), we have \(x\leq y\).

To justify the moving endpoints, suppose \(z_N\to z\). For every \(h>0\), eventually

\(\displaystyle \Pr(Y_N\leq z-h)\leq\Pr(Y_N<z_N)\leq\Pr(Y_N\leq z+h).\)

Convergence in distribution and continuity of \(\Phi\), followed by \(h\downarrow0\), show that \(\Pr(Y_N<z_N)\to\Phi(z)\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \beta_{t,N} =\Pr(Y_N<\gamma_N)-\Pr(Y_N<\alpha_N) \longrightarrow\Phi(y)-\Phi(x).\)

This proves the asserted formula, including the case \(A_t=0\), when \(B_t=0\). No assumption about the lattice of coalition weights is needed.

3. A polynomial-time approximation algorithm

A numerical formula alone does not establish the requested bit complexity: the binary inputs may encode very large weights or very small masses. We give an explicit rational-arithmetic algorithm that handles these possibilities.

If \(\epsilon\geq1\), output \(0\). Otherwise, set

\(\displaystyle \eta=\frac{\epsilon}{64},\qquad R=\left\lceil\eta^{-1}\right\rceil.\)

Define

\(\displaystyle f(z)=e^{-z^2/2},\qquad Z_0=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(z)\,dz.\)

The formula already proved can equivalently be written as

\(\displaystyle B_t=\frac{J}{Z_0},\qquad J=\int_x^y f(z)\,dz.\)

3.1 Truncation

Let \(\operatorname{clip}_R(z)=\max(-R,\min(z,R))\), and put

\(\displaystyle a=\operatorname{clip}_R(x),\quad b=\operatorname{clip}_R(y),\quad J_R=\int_a^b f(z)\,dz,\quad D_R=\int_{-R}^R f(z)\,dz.\)

For \(R>0\),

\(\displaystyle \int_R^\infty f(z)\,dz \leq\frac1R\int_R^\infty zf(z)\,dz =\frac{e^{-R^2/2}}R\leq\frac1R.\)

By symmetry, the total omitted tail \(T_R=Z_0-D_R\) satisfies \(T_R\leq2\eta\).

Also, \(f(z)\geq1/2\) on \([-1,1]\), using \(e^{-r}\geq1-r\). Since \(R\geq1\), both \(D_R\) and \(Z_0\) are at least \(1\). As \(0\leq J-J_R\leq T_R\) and \(J_R\leq D_R\),

\(\displaystyle \left|\frac{J}{Z_0}-\frac{J_R}{D_R}\right| \leq\frac{J-J_R}{Z_0}+\frac{J_RT_R}{Z_0D_R} \leq2T_R\leq4\eta.\)

3.2 Computing the clipped endpoints

The rationals \(S\), \(A_t\), \(2\theta\), and \(2(\theta+A_t)\) are computable exactly with bit lengths polynomial in the input length.

For rational \(c\), comparisons of \(c/\sqrt S\) with any rational \(r\) require only rational arithmetic. First compare signs; when signs agree, compare \(c^2\) with \(r^2S\), reversing the squared comparison when both numbers are negative.

Thus one can determine whether \(c/\sqrt S\) lies outside \([-R,R]\), and otherwise bisect that interval using exact comparisons. After \(O(\log(R/\eta))\) steps this produces a rational approximation to its clipped value within \(\eta\). No explicit square-root evaluation is required.

Apply this to \(x\) and \(y\), and sort the two approximations to obtain \(a'\leq b'\). Sorting preserves the endpoint error bounds: if \(p\) approximates \(a\) and \(q\) approximates \(b\), then \(a-\eta\leq\min(p,q)\leq a+\eta\), and similarly for the maximum and \(b\).

Since \(0<f\leq1\), it follows that

\(\displaystyle \left|\int_{a'}^{b'}f(z)\,dz-J_R\right|\leq2\eta.\)

3.3 Rational quadrature

We approximate each of

\(\displaystyle \int_{a'}^{b'}f(z)\,dz\quad\text{and}\quad D_R\)

to absolute error at most \(\eta\).

The derivative satisfies

\(\displaystyle |f'(z)|=|z|e^{-z^2/2}\leq1,\)

so \(f\) is Lipschitz with constant \(1\). Partition either integration interval into equal subintervals of length at most

\(\displaystyle h=\frac{\eta}{2R}.\)

Its total length is at most \(2R\). Midpoint quadrature therefore has error at most \((2R)h/2=\eta/2\).

At each rational midpoint \(z\), approximate \(f(z)\) within

\(\displaystyle \rho=\frac{\eta}{4R}.\)

The resulting additional quadrature error is at most \(2R\rho=\eta/2\).

These point evaluations can also use exact rational arithmetic. Set \(q=z^2/2\) and \(H=\lceil R^2/2\rceil\), so \(0\leq q\leq H\). Evaluate

\(\displaystyle P_{m-1}(q)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-q)^k}{k!},\)

where the integer \(m\) satisfies \(m\geq6H\) and \(2^{-m}\leq\rho\). Taylor’s remainder theorem gives

\(\displaystyle |e^{-q}-P_{m-1}(q)|\leq\frac{q^m}{m!}.\)

The elementary factorial bound \(m!\geq(m/e)^m\), together with \(e<3\), implies

\(\displaystyle \frac{q^m}{m!}\leq\left(\frac{3H}{m}\right)^m\leq2^{-m}\leq\rho.\)

Thus this procedure achieves the stated integration errors. Denote the two rational results by \(\widetilde J\) and \(\widetilde D\). Then

\(\displaystyle |\widetilde J-J_R|\leq3\eta,\qquad |\widetilde D-D_R|\leq\eta.\)

In particular, \(\widetilde D\geq1-\eta\geq1/2\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \left|\frac{\widetilde J}{\widetilde D}-\frac{J_R}{D_R}\right| \leq\frac{3\eta}{\widetilde D} +\frac{J_R\eta}{D_R\widetilde D} \leq8\eta.\)

Combining this with truncation proves

\(\displaystyle \left|\frac{\widetilde J}{\widetilde D}-B_t\right| \leq12\eta<\epsilon.\)

Output this rational ratio clipped to \([0,1]\). Clipping cannot increase its distance from \(B_t\in[0,1]\).

3.4 Bit complexity and confidence

The construction uses:

  • \(O(\eta^{-3})\) quadrature points;
  • Taylor degree \(O(\eta^{-2}+\log(\eta^{-1}))\);
  • \(O(\log(\eta^{-1}))\) exact comparisons per clipped endpoint.

Every rational operand has polynomial bit length. In particular, Taylor powers and factorials have polynomial bit length, and summing polynomially many rational terms preserves a polynomial bound on bit length. Standard integer arithmetic therefore gives total running time polynomial in \(\tau,L,\epsilon^{-1}\).

The algorithm is deterministic and does not depend on \(\delta\). Its error guarantee holds with probability \(1\), establishing the requested polynomial-time approximation for the exact population limit.
