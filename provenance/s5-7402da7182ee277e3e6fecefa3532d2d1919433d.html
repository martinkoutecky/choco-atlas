The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many types \(t\in[\tau]\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), entitlement coefficients \(a_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t a_t=1\), and bounded rational finite-support value distributions \(F_t\), consider \(N\) tenants with \(N\mu_t\) tenants of type \(t\), each with share \(a_t/N\). Under the Competitive Subsidy Mechanism with \(\bar b=8/3\), determine the worst-case limiting ratio of a type-\(t\) representative’s expected utility to their ideal utility when they use the top-\(a_t/N\)-quantile request policy and other tenants may use arbitrary strategies; in particular, does the ratio remain at least \(5/8\) as \(N,T\to\infty\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Assume \(\mu_t>0\), \(a_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=1\), with rational entitlements. Each \(F_t\) lists rational values in \([0,1]\) and rational probabilities. Let \(m\) be the total number of listed value atoms and \(L\) the input bit length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L,N,T\) and any additional parameters.
Fix this input and a focal type \(t\) with \(\Pr_{F_t}(V>0)>0\). For every integer \(N\ge\max\{2,\max_s a_s\}\) with all \(N\mu_s\) integral, there are \(N\mu_s\) tenants of type \(s\), each with share \(\alpha_s=a_s/N\). Values are private, drawn independently across tenants and rounds from their type distributions.
The Competitive Subsidy Mechanism runs for \(T\) rounds, with one indivisible item per round and initial credit balance \(\alpha_sT\) for each type-\(s\) tenant. Requests are simultaneous and binary; tenants with nonpositive balance cannot request. With \(k>0\) eligible requesters, select a winner uniformly and deduct \((8/3)k/(k+1)\) credits from that winner alone; this last payment may overdraw the balance. With no requesters, leave the item unallocated. Credits cannot be transferred.
The focal tenant requests while their balance is positive exactly on the top \(\alpha_t\) fraction of values, using fresh independent randomization at the cutoff atom to make the request probability exactly \(\alpha_t\). Define their per-round ideal utility by \(v^*_{t,N}=\max\{\mathbb E_{F_t}[V\rho(V)]:\rho:\operatorname{supp}(F_t)\to[0,1],\ \mathbb E_{F_t}[\rho(V)]\le a_t/N\}\).
Other tenants may collude and use arbitrary randomized strategies depending on public history and their coalition's private values, but cannot observe the focal tenant's current value, request, or fresh randomness before submitting requests. Let \(\Sigma_{-i}^{N,T}\) denote these strategy profiles and \(W_i[r]\) indicate that the focal tenant wins round \(r\).
Define \(R_t=\liminf_{\substack{N,T\to\infty\\N\mu_s\in\mathbb Z\ \forall s}}\inf_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{-i}^{N,T}}\frac{\mathbb E_\sigma[\sum_{r=1}^{T}V_i[r]W_i[r]]}{T v^*_{t,N}}\), taking a joint lower limit over all admissible population sizes and horizons, with the society fixed.
What is the worst-case limiting robustness ratio \(R_t\)? Does \(R_t\ge5/8\) hold for every admissible society and focal type?
For finite populations and fixed positive shares, the top-share request policy guarantees an expected ideal-utility fraction of at least \(5/8-O(\sqrt{\log T/T})\). Numerical evidence concerning alternative payment rules is also reported, without a proved matching optimality bound. [Verified finite-population results](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09934v2).
No result settling this joint population-and-horizon limit was identified in the literature search.

Result

For every admissible society and focal type in the question, \(\boxed{R_t=0}\). Consequently, the proposed universal bound \(R_t\ge 5/8\) is false.

The joint limit permits horizons much smaller than the population, but that possibility is unnecessary: the path \(T=N\) already forces the ratio to zero. Along this path, every type-\(s\) tenant has the fixed initial balance \(a_s\).

1. The focal policy realizes the ideal benchmark

Fix an admissible population size \(N\), and write \(\alpha=a_t/N\). Let \(\rho_N(v)\) be the focal tenant’s request probability at value \(v\), before enforcing the balance restriction. The specified cutoff policy satisfies \(\mathbb E[\rho_N(V)]=\alpha\).

We verify that \(\mathbb E[V\rho_N(V)]=v^*_{t,N}\). Choose its cutoff \(c\ge 0\): the policy requests surely above \(c\), never below \(c\), and randomizes at \(c\). For any feasible policy \(\rho\), pointwise we have

\((v-c)(\rho(v)-\rho_N(v))\le 0.\)

Taking expectations and using \(\mathbb E[\rho(V)]\le\alpha=\mathbb E[\rho_N(V)]\) gives

\(\mathbb E[V(\rho(V)-\rho_N(V))]\le c\,\mathbb E[\rho(V)-\rho_N(V)]\le 0.\)

Thus \(\rho_N\) is optimal. Moreover, \(v^*_{t,N}>0\): requesting with positive probability at any positive-value atom yields a positive feasible utility.

2. An admissible adversarial strategy

Every other tenant requests in every round in which their balance is positive, regardless of their value.

This deterministic strategy is admissible. It depends only on eligibility and observes none of the focal tenant’s current value, request, or fresh randomness.

Let \(K_r\) denote the number of eligible opponents at the start of round \(r\). Initially, all \(N-1\) opponents are eligible, because their initial balances are positive.

Only a winner’s balance decreases. Therefore, an opponent who has become ineligible must have won an earlier round. There have been only \(r-1\) earlier rounds, each with at most one winner. Hence, pathwise,

\(K_r\ge (N-1)-(r-1)=N-r.\)

In particular, this bound holds for every \(1\le r\le N\), independently of the entitlements and payment amounts.

3. A finite-horizon upper bound

Let \(\mathcal H_{r-1}\) contain the complete history before round \(r\), and let \(A_r\) indicate that the focal tenant’s balance is positive. Both \(A_r\) and \(K_r\) are determined by that history.

Conditional on \(\mathcal H_{r-1}\), the focal tenant’s current value and cutoff randomness are fresh and independent. If eligible and requesting, the focal tenant competes against exactly \(K_r\) opponents and wins with probability \(1/(K_r+1)\). Consequently,

\(\mathbb E[V_i[r]W_i[r]\mid\mathcal H_{r-1}]=A_r\,\dfrac{\mathbb E[V\rho_N(V)]}{K_r+1}=A_r\,\dfrac{v^*_{t,N}}{K_r+1}.\)

For \(r\le N\), the preceding eligibility bound implies

\(\mathbb E[V_i[r]W_i[r]\mid\mathcal H_{r-1}]\le\dfrac{v^*_{t,N}}{N-r+1}.\)

Define \(H_q=\sum_{k=1}^{q}1/k\), with \(H_0=0\). Summing and dividing by the positive benchmark yields, for every \(1\le T\le N\),

\(\displaystyle \inf_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{-i}^{N,T}} \frac{\mathbb E_\sigma[\sum_{r=1}^{T}V_i[r]W_i[r]]}{T v^*_{t,N}} \le \frac{1}{T}\sum_{r=1}^{T}\frac{1}{N-r+1} = \frac{H_N-H_{N-T}}{T}. \)

In particular, when \(T=N\),

\(\displaystyle \inf_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{-i}^{N,N}} \frac{\mathbb E_\sigma[\sum_{r=1}^{N}V_i[r]W_i[r]]}{N v^*_{t,N}} \le \frac{H_N}{N} \le \frac{1+\log N}{N}. \)

The last inequality follows from \(\sum_{k=2}^{N}1/k\le\int_1^N dx/x\).

4. Taking the admissible joint limit

Let \(D\) be the least common multiple of the denominators of the rational masses \(\mu_s\), written in lowest terms. Every population size \(N_j=Dj\) satisfies \(N_j\mu_s\in\mathbb Z\) for every supported type. For sufficiently large \(j\), it also satisfies the required population lower bound.

Set \(T_j=N_j\). Then both parameters tend to infinity, and Section 3 gives an upper bound tending to zero:

\(\displaystyle \frac{H_{N_j}}{N_j}\longrightarrow 0. \)

The joint lower limit is at most the lower limit along this admissible sequence. Every utility ratio is nonnegative, so it is also at least zero. Therefore,

\(\boxed{R_t=0}.\)

This proves the claim for every fixed society satisfying the hypotheses, rather than merely exhibiting one counterexample.

5. Relation to the fixed-population guarantee

The stated fixed-population guarantee concerns fixed positive shares as the horizon grows. Here, along \(T=N\), the focal share \(a_t/N\) tends to zero. Thus that guarantee cannot be transferred to this joint limit without a uniform estimate in the population and shares. See [Theorem 3.1 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09934v2#S3).

Overdrawing also creates a relevant population-dependent accounting term. Since each tenant can overdraw only on their final payment, and every payment is less than \(8/3\), the opponents’ total expenditure is bounded by

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\ne i}\sum_{r=1}^{T}P_j[r] \le (1-\alpha)T+\frac83(N-1). \)

After division by \(T\), the additional term vanishes for fixed \(N\), but does not vanish along \(T=N\). The proof above uses eligibility directly and needs no expenditure approximation.

6. Computational dependence

Under the promised input hypotheses, the answer is always zero; no optimization over the \(m\) atoms or \(\tau\) supported types is needed. Reading the input takes \(O(L)\) bit operations. The auxiliary integer \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits and can be computed in time polynomial in \(L\) using greatest-common-divisor calculations. No population enumeration or simulation is required.
