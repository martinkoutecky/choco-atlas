The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), contact intensities \(q_{tu}\), activation probabilities \(p_{tu}\), and a seed budget \(\kappa\), choose seed masses \(x_t\) with \(0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_t=\kappa\) to maximize \(C(x)=\sum_u v_u^\infty\). Define \(z^0=a^0=v^0=x\), and for each round set \(z_u^{r+1}=(\mu_u-a_u^r)(1-\exp[-\sum_t q_{tu}p_{tu}z_t^r])\), \(a_u^{r+1}=a_u^r+z_u^{r+1}\), and \(v_u^{r+1}=v_u^r+(\mu_u-v_u^r)(1-\exp[-\sum_t q_{tu}z_t^r])\). For rational inputs and \(\epsilon>0\), can one find a feasible seed-mass vector with coverage at least \((1-1/e-\epsilon)\max_y C(y)\) in time polynomial in the number of types, input bit length, and \(1/\epsilon\)?

Result

The requested approximation can be found in polynomial time. More precisely, in the standard binary encoding of rational numbers, there is a deterministic algorithm that returns a feasible rational vector \(x\) satisfying

\(C(x)\geq (1-1/e-\epsilon)\sup_{y\text{ feasible}}C(y)\)

in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\).

The proof requires addressing two issues: the limiting dynamics can be discontinuous when a type has zero seed mass, and direct iteration need not converge in polynomial time. We avoid the first issue by reserving a small positive seed mass for every type, and the second by evaluating the positive-seed fixed point through convex optimization.

1. Fixed-point representation and scaling

Write \(n=\tau\), and define matrices with destination types indexing rows:

\(B_{ut}=q_{tu}p_{tu},\qquad Q_{ut}=q_{tu}.\)

All vector inequalities below are componentwise. For \(0\leq x\leq\mu\), define

\(F_x(a)_u=\mu_u-(\mu_u-x_u)\exp(-(Ba)_u)\)

and

\(H_x(a)_u=\mu_u-(\mu_u-x_u)\exp(-(Qa)_u).\)

Telescoping the susceptible-mass recurrence gives, with \(a^{-1}=0\),

\(a^r=F_x(a^{r-1}),\qquad v^r=H_x(a^{r-1})\qquad(r\geq0).\)

Indeed, for \(r\geq1\),

\(\mu_u-a_u^r=(\mu_u-x_u)\exp\bigl(-\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}(Bz^k)_u\bigr)=(\mu_u-x_u)\exp(-(Ba^{r-1})_u),\)

and the corresponding calculation for \(v^r\) uses \(Q\).

The map \(F_x\) is increasing in both \(x\) and \(a\). Starting from \(a^{-1}=0\), its iterates increase and remain bounded by \(\mu\). Consequently, their limit \(a(x)\) exists, and

\(a(x)=F_x(a(x)),\qquad C(x)=\sum_u H_x(a(x))_u.\)

This also proves that \(a(x)\), the limiting covered vector, and \(C(x)\) are increasing in \(x\). Moreover,

\(\sum_u x_u\leq C(x)\leq1.\)

We will use these statements on the entire box \(0\leq x\leq\mu\), including vectors whose total seed mass is less than the budget.

Scaling lemma. For \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\),

\(C(\lambda x)\geq\lambda C(x).\)

To prove it, fix a coordinate and put \(s=(Ba)_u\). The scalar function

\(h(t)=\mu_u-(\mu_u-tx_u)e^{-ts}\)

satisfies \(h(0)=0\) and

\(h''(t)=-e^{-ts}\bigl(2x_us+(\mu_u-tx_u)s^2\bigr)\leq0\qquad(0\leq t\leq1).\)

Thus

\(F_{\lambda x}(\lambda a)\geq\lambda F_x(a).\)

Induction over the iterates starting from zero gives

\(a^r(\lambda x)\geq\lambda a^r(x),\)

and hence \(a(\lambda x)\geq\lambda a(x)\). The same scalar calculation with \(Q\), followed by monotonicity of \(H_{\lambda x}\), proves the coverage inequality.

This proof does not assume continuity at vectors with zero coordinates.

2. Positive seeds give uniqueness and diminishing derivatives

Throughout this section, suppose every coordinate of \(x\) is positive.

2.1 Uniqueness of the fixed point

Each coordinate of \(F_x(a)\) is concave in \(a\), and \(F_x(0)=x>0\). Therefore, for every \(\rho>1\) and \(a\geq0\),

\(F_x(\rho a)\leq\rho F_x(a)-(\rho-1)x<\rho F_x(a).\)

Suppose \(a,c\) were distinct fixed points. Both are positive. After interchanging them if necessary, let

\(\rho=\max_u a_u/c_u>1.\)

Then \(a\leq\rho c\), so

\(a=F_x(a)\leq F_x(\rho c)<\rho F_x(c)=\rho c.\)

This contradicts equality in a coordinate attaining the maximum ratio. Thus \(a(x)\) is the unique nonnegative fixed point.

2.2 Derivatives and their signs

For this subsection, all quantities are evaluated at \(x\). Set

\(E=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-Ba}),\qquad S=\operatorname{diag}(\mu-a),\qquad J=SB.\)

The fixed-point equation implies

\((Ja)_u=(\mu_u-x_u)e^{-(Ba)_u}(Ba)_u\leq(\mu_u-x_u)(1-e^{-(Ba)_u})=a_u-x_u,\)

where the inequality uses \(se^{-s}\leq1-e^{-s}\) for \(s\geq0\).

Since \(a>0\) and \(x>0\), this establishes a strict contraction for \(J\) in the weighted maximum norm with weights \(a_u\). In particular, its spectral radius is less than one, and

\(R=(I-J)^{-1}=\sum_{k\geq0}J^k\)

exists and is nonnegative.

The implicit-function theorem now gives

\(Da(x)=RE.\)

Here \(Da(x)\) denotes the Jacobian of the limiting active vector. The formula also applies at an upper boundary \(x_u=\mu_u\): the implicit equation extends smoothly to a neighborhood, and \(I-J\) remains invertible there.

Let \(w(x)=H_x(a(x))\) be the limiting covered vector, and set

\(T=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-Qa}),\qquad W=\operatorname{diag}(\mu-w).\)

Differentiating gives

\(Dw(x)=T+WQRE,\qquad \nabla C(x)=(Dw(x))^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1.\)

All entries are nonnegative.

Furthermore, if \(0<x\leq x'\leq\mu\), monotonicity of \(a\) and \(w\) shows that \(S,E,T,W\) decrease componentwise. Therefore \(J\) decreases, and its nonnegative Neumann series shows that \(R\) decreases as well. The derivative formulas consequently give

\(\nabla C(x')\leq\nabla C(x).\)

Thus, on every box bounded away from zero seeds, \(C\) is differentiable, monotone, and has diminishing coordinate derivatives.

A useful consequence is the following bound for any positive vector \(b\leq\mu\):

\(b^{\mathsf T}\nabla C(b)\leq C(b).\)

Indeed, for \(0<s<1\), diminishing derivatives give

\(C(b)-C(sb)=\int_s^1 b^{\mathsf T}\nabla C(tb)\,dt\geq(1-s)b^{\mathsf T}\nabla C(b).\)

Use \(C(sb)\geq0\) and let \(s\) decrease to zero. Again, continuity at zero is unnecessary.

3. A polynomial-time approximate gradient oracle

We next prove the bit-complexity statement needed by the optimization algorithm:

Oracle lemma. Given a rational vector \(0<x\leq\mu\) and a rational accuracy \(\rho>0\), one can compute a rational vector \(\widetilde g\) satisfying

\(\|\widetilde g-\nabla C(x)\|_\infty\leq\rho\)

in time polynomial in the input bit length, \(n\), the encoding length of \(x\), and \(\log(1/\rho)\).

3.1 Computing the active fixed point by convex optimization

Put \(d=\min_u x_u>0\). Consider

\(K_x=\{a\in[0,\mu]:a_u+(\mu_u-x_u)e^{-(Ba)_u}\leq\mu_u\text{ for every }u\}.\)

Each defining nonlinear function is convex, so \(K_x\) is convex.

Every \(a\in K_x\) satisfies \(a\leq F_x(a)\). Iterating \(F_x\) from this point produces an increasing bounded sequence whose limit is a fixed point. Uniqueness therefore implies \(a\leq a(x)\). Conversely, \(a(x)\in K_x\). Hence \(a(x)\) is the unique maximizer of the linear objective

\(\max_{a\in K_x}\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}a.\)

The body has explicit inner and outer bounds. Let \(c=(d/2)\mathbf1\). The Euclidean ball of radius \(d/4\) about \(c\) lies in \(K_x\): every point in this ball has coordinates between \(d/4\) and \(3d/4\), whereas

\(F_x(a)\geq x\geq d\mathbf1.\)

Also \(K_x\subseteq[0,1]^n\), giving an outer radius \(n\).

We use the standard ellipsoid-method consequence that a bounded convex body with a specified interior ball and a polynomial-time weak separation oracle admits approximate linear optimization in time polynomial in the dimension, encoding lengths, logarithms of the radius bounds, and logarithm of the requested accuracy. This is the weak-separation/weak-optimization theorem of [Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf).

Here are the remaining oracle checks explicitly. Write

\(\phi_u(a)=a_u+(\mu_u-x_u)e^{-(Ba)_u}-\mu_u.\)

Outside the rational box \([0,\mu]\), an exact box separator is available. Inside the box, both \(\phi_u\) and its gradient can be approximated to any prescribed accuracy using rational arithmetic.

To see that these approximations suffice for weak separation, suppose all constraint violations at a queried point \(y\) are at most \(2\theta\). Since \(\phi_u(c)\leq-d/2\), convexity implies that

\(y'=(1-t)y+tc,\qquad t=\frac{4\theta}{d+4\theta},\)

belongs to \(K_x\). Its distance from \(y\) is at most \(4\theta\sqrt n/d\).

Otherwise, a constraint with a certified positive violation supplies a separating hyperplane through its convex supporting-plane inequality. For example, if \(\phi_u(y)>3\theta/4\), an approximation to its gradient with coordinate error at most \(\theta/(8n)\) remains a valid separator, because \(y,z\in[0,\mu]\) imply \(\|z-y\|_1\leq n\). Choosing \(\theta\) proportional to the desired weak-separation tolerance times \(d/n\) therefore implements the required oracle.

Exponential evaluations also have polynomial bit complexity. For an absolute error \(2^{-b}\) and rational \(s\geq0\), return zero if \(s\geq b\). Otherwise \(s<b\), and a Taylor expansion with \(O(b)\) terms gives the requested accuracy using arithmetic of polynomial bit length. All exponential arguments here are nonnegative; the large values of contact intensities therefore cause no difficulty.

Finally, approximate linear optimization determines the whole vector \(a(x)\), rather than merely its sum. Suppose its output \(\widehat a\) is within Euclidean distance \(\delta\) of \(K_x\), and

\(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\widehat a\geq\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}a(x)-\delta.\)

Choose \(a'\in K_x\) within distance \(\delta\). Since \(a'\leq a(x)\),

\(\|a(x)-a'\|_\infty\leq\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}(a(x)-a')\leq(1+\sqrt n)\delta.\)

Consequently,

\(\|\widehat a-a(x)\|_\infty\leq(2+\sqrt n)\delta.\)

Clipping the output to \([0,\mu]\) cannot increase this error. Thus the fixed point can be approximated to coordinate error \(h\) in time polynomial in the input lengths and \(\log(1/h)\).

3.2 Precision needed for the gradient

The following bounds verify that no numerical precision requirement grows beyond polynomial bit length.

Let

\(H=\max\{1,\max_{u,t}B_{ut},\max_{u,t}Q_{ut}\}.\)

Use the induced maximum-row-sum matrix norm. Since \(a_u\leq1\) and \(x_u\geq d\), the earlier inequality gives

\(Ja\leq(1-d)a.\)

Therefore

\(Ra\leq a/d,\qquad \|R\|_\infty\leq d^{-2}.\)

Suppose the computed active vector has coordinate error at most \(h\), and the exponential factors used in the derivative formulas are computed to absolute error at most \(h\), clipped to \([0,1]\). Denote the resulting quantities by tildes. Since the exponential function \(e^{-s}\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz for \(s\geq0\),

\(\|\widetilde J-J\|_\infty\leq2n^2H^2h.\)

If

\(h\leq d^2/(4n^2H^2),\)

the usual inverse perturbation identity gives

\(\|\widetilde R\|_\infty\leq2d^{-2},\qquad \|\widetilde R-R\|_\infty\leq4n^2H^2h/d^4.\)

Writing \(A=RE\), these bounds imply

\(\|\widetilde A-A\|_\infty\leq8n^2H^2h/d^4.\)

Substituting into \(Dw=T+WQA\), using that the diagonal entries of \(T,W,\widetilde T,\widetilde W\) lie in \([0,1]\), gives

\(\|\widetilde{Dw}-Dw\|_\infty\leq12n^3H^3h/d^4.\)

Column summation therefore gives

\(\|\widetilde g-\nabla C(x)\|_\infty\leq12n^4H^3h/d^4.\)

It suffices to choose

\(h\leq\min\{d^2/(4n^2H^2),\ \rho d^4/(16n^4H^3)\}.\)

The logarithm of the reciprocal of this precision is polynomial in the relevant input lengths. The approximate derivative formula involves only rational matrix inversion and rational arithmetic after the exponential approximations have been obtained. These operations also have polynomial bit complexity.

This proves the oracle lemma.

4. The approximation algorithm

Let

\(\mathrm{OPT}=\sup\{C(y):0\leq y\leq\mu,\ \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}y=\kappa\},\qquad c_*=1-1/e.\)

In particular, \(\mathrm{OPT}\geq\kappa\).

Put \(\eta=\min\{\epsilon,1/2\}\), and choose a dyadic number \(\alpha\) such that

\(\eta/16\leq\alpha\leq\eta/8.\)

Define the positive reserve

\(b_u=\alpha\min\{\mu_u,\kappa/n\},\qquad s_b=\sum_u b_u.\)

Then

\(s_b\leq\alpha\kappa,\qquad 0<b_u\leq\alpha\mu_u.\)

Consider the fixed residual polytope

\(P=\{u\geq0:u\leq\mu-b,\ \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}u\leq\kappa-s_b\},\)

and the function \(f(u)=C(b+u)\), defined on the entire box \(0\leq u\leq\mu-b\).

Choose a power of two \(N\) satisfying

\(N\geq8n/(\alpha\eta),\)

and set \(\Delta=1/N\) and \(\rho=\eta/16\).

Starting from \(u^0=0\), perform the following for \(j=0,\ldots,N-1\):

  • Compute \(\widetilde g^j\) with \(\|\widetilde g^j-\nabla f(u^j)\|_\infty\leq\rho\).
  • Choose \(v^j\in P\) maximizing \((\widetilde g^j)^{\mathsf T}v\).
  • Set \(u^{j+1}=u^j+\Delta v^j\).

The linear optimization in step 2 is a fractional-knapsack problem: sort the coefficients and fill coordinates subject to their caps and the single mass budget.

Each \(u^j\) is feasible. Indeed, it is \(j/N\) times an average of points in the convex, downward-closed polytope \(P\).

After the last iteration, increase coordinates of \(b+u^N\), within their remaining caps, until the total mass equals \(\kappa\). Such completion is possible because \(\sum_u\mu_u=1\geq\kappa\). Denote the completed vector by \(x\). Monotonicity gives

\(C(x)\geq f(u^N).\)

5. Approximation proof

5.1 The positive reserve loses little

For every original feasible vector \(y\), the vector \(u=(1-\alpha)y\) belongs to \(P\). Its coordinate caps follow from \(b\leq\alpha\mu\), and its budget follows from \(s_b\leq\alpha\kappa\).

Using monotonicity and the scaling lemma,

\(f((1-\alpha)y)=C(b+(1-\alpha)y)\geq C((1-\alpha)y)\geq(1-\alpha)C(y).\)

Let \(M=\max_{u\in P}f(u)\). This maximum exists: all seeds in this domain are positive, so the fixed-point and derivative results imply continuity, and \(P\) is compact. Taking a supremum over \(y\) gives

\(M\geq(1-\alpha)\mathrm{OPT}.\)

Thus we do not need the original supremum to be attained.

5.2 The greedy direction closes the value gap

Let \(u^*\in P\) attain \(M\). Diminishing derivatives and monotonicity imply, for every \(u\in P\),

\(M-f(u)\leq f(u\vee u^*)-f(u)\leq\nabla f(u)^{\mathsf T}(u^*-u)_+\leq\nabla f(u)^{\mathsf T}u^*.\)

The coordinatewise maximum need not belong to \(P\); it belongs to the box on which \(f\) is defined, which is sufficient.

Let \(g^j=\nabla f(u^j)\). Approximate-gradient maximization then gives

\((g^j)^{\mathsf T}v^j\geq M-f(u^j)-2\rho\kappa.\)

Here each gradient replacement costs at most \(\rho\kappa\), because every vector in \(P\) has total mass at most \(\kappa\).

5.3 A telescoping discretization bound

Define coordinate direction bounds

\(r_u=\min\{\mu_u-b_u,\kappa-s_b\}.\)

Every \(v\in P\) satisfies \(v\leq r\). Along each update, derivatives decrease componentwise, so

\(f(u^{j+1})-f(u^j)=\int_0^\Delta\nabla f(u^j+tv^j)^{\mathsf T}v^j\,dt\geq\Delta(g^{j+1})^{\mathsf T}v^j.\)

Consequently,

\(f(u^{j+1})-f(u^j)\geq\Delta(g^j)^{\mathsf T}v^j-\Delta r^{\mathsf T}(g^j-g^{j+1}).\)

The last error term is nonnegative. Combining this with the preceding gap bound and unrolling the recurrence gives

\(f(u^N)\geq[1-(1-\Delta)^N]M-2\rho\kappa-\Delta r^{\mathsf T}g^0.\)

To justify the final term, the geometrically weighted sum of discretization errors is at most their unweighted sum, which telescopes to

\(\Delta r^{\mathsf T}(g^0-g^N)\leq\Delta r^{\mathsf T}g^0.\)

Since \((1-1/N)^N\leq e^{-1}\),

\(f(u^N)\geq c_*M-2\rho\kappa-\Delta r^{\mathsf T}\nabla C(b).\)

This bound uses no Lipschitz constant for the gradient.

5.4 Bounding all losses

For each coordinate,

\(r_u\leq\min\{\mu_u,\kappa\}\leq n\min\{\mu_u,\kappa/n\}=(n/\alpha)b_u.\)

Since the gradient is nonnegative, the bound from Section 2 gives

\(r^{\mathsf T}\nabla C(b)\leq(n/\alpha)b^{\mathsf T}\nabla C(b)\leq(n/\alpha)C(b).\)

The reserve \(b\) can be completed to a feasible vector, so \(C(b)\leq\mathrm{OPT}\). Hence

\(\Delta r^{\mathsf T}\nabla C(b)\leq\frac{n}{\alpha N}\mathrm{OPT}\leq(\eta/8)\mathrm{OPT}.\)

Also,

\(2\rho\kappa=(\eta/8)\kappa\leq(\eta/8)\mathrm{OPT}.\)

Combining these inequalities with \(M\geq(1-\alpha)\mathrm{OPT}\) yields

\(C(x)\geq f(u^N)\geq[c_*(1-\alpha)-\eta/4]\mathrm{OPT}\geq[c_*-3\eta/8]\mathrm{OPT}.\)

Because \(\eta\leq\epsilon\), this proves the requested guarantee.

6. Running time and rational output

The smallest valid power of two \(N\) is \(O(n/\eta^2)\), so the number of gradient-oracle calls is polynomial in \(n,1/\epsilon\).

Every queried seed vector satisfies \(x_u\geq b_u\). Thus, uniformly over all oracle calls,

\(\log(1/d)\leq\log(1/\alpha)+\max\{\log(1/\min_u\mu_u),\log(n/\kappa)\}.\)

This is polynomially bounded by the input bit length and \(\log(1/\eta)\). Likewise, \(\log H\) is bounded by the input encoding length. Section 3 therefore gives polynomial time for every gradient query, even when masses are very small or intensities are very large.

The reserve, residual caps, and residual budget are rational numbers of polynomial encoding length. Fractional-knapsack directions use these numbers and their sums and differences. A common denominator of polynomial bit length can be used for all directions; averaging introduces only the additional factor \(N\). Budget completion also uses rational sums and differences. Therefore the returned vector has polynomial encoding length. If the inputs are dyadic, choosing \(\alpha\) dyadic and \(N\) a power of two makes the returned vector dyadic as well.

The resulting algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\) and produces the required feasible binary rational vector. The proof allows arbitrary nonnegative contact intensities, zero activation probabilities, and within-type contacts.
