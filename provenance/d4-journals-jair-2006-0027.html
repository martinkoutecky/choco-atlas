The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many complete MDP types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), each type’s local capacity limits, and per-capita stocks \(\bar b_o\), choose bundle masses \(y_{t,w}\ge 0\) over each type’s locally feasible integral bundles \(W_t\). Require \(\sum_{w\in W_t} y_{t,w}=\mu_t\) for every type and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{w\in W_t} y_{t,w}w_o\le \bar b_o\) for every resource \(o\). Is there such an assignment with \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{w\in W_t} y_{t,w}V_t(w)\ge Y\), where \(V_t(w)\) is the maximum expected discounted reward for type \(t\) under bundle \(w\)?

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge 1\) non-consumable resource types and \(K\ge 1\) capacity dimensions. Each type supplies an explicitly tabulated finite discounted MDP \((S_t,A_t,p_t,r_t,\alpha_t)\), including its initial probability distribution, and action requirements \(\rho_t(a,o)\in\{0,1\}\). The common discount factor satisfies \(0<\gamma<1\); rewards are signed rationals. Every type has a resource-free, zero-reward action that leaves the state unchanged.
Resource capacity costs \(\kappa(o,c)\ge 0\) are common to all types; local bounds \(b_t(c)\ge 0\) and per-capita stocks \(\bar b_o\ge 0\) are rational. All numerical input is encoded in binary.
Define \(W_t=\{w\in\{0,1\}^m:\sum_o\kappa(o,c)w_o\le b_t(c)\text{ for every }c\}\). These sets are implicit, not enumerated input.
Under bundle \(w\), precisely the actions satisfying \(\rho_t(a,o)\le w_o\) for every resource \(o\) are enabled. Resources are allocated once before execution, are never consumed or reallocated, and types' MDPs operate independently thereafter.
Let \(V_t(w)\) be the maximum expected total discounted reward from \(\alpha_t\) in the MDP restricted to those enabled actions; stationary deterministic policies suffice, and ties do not affect this value.
Given a rational threshold \(Y\), the decision problem asks whether there exist real masses \(y_{t,w}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_{w\in W_t}y_{t,w}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), \(\sum_t\sum_{w\in W_t}y_{t,w}w_o\le\bar b_o\) for every \(o\), and \(\sum_t\sum_{w\in W_t}y_{t,w}V_t(w)\ge Y\).
Is this mass allocation decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
State running times in \(m,\tau,K,n,a,L\), where \(n=\max_t|S_t|\), \(a=\max_t|A_t|\), and \(L\) is the total binary input length.
The discrete single-agent capacity-constrained policy decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already with binary action requirements and one capacity dimension; discrete multiagent welfare decision is also \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Classification

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds already with \(\tau=1\), \(K=1\), \(\gamma=\tfrac12\), unit capacity costs, rewards in \(\{0,1\}\), and absorbing states.

Allowing real masses does not remove the hardness: when stocks impose no additional restriction, the welfare of a mixture cannot exceed the best locally feasible bundle value.

The [Dolgov–Durfee paper](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ddolgov/papers/dolgov_resourceMDP_jair06.pdf) establishes hardness for discrete capacity-constrained policies. The reduction below proves hardness directly for the stated mass allocation problem.

1. Values of fixed bundles are computable in polynomial time

Fix a type \(t\) and a locally feasible bundle \(w\). Let \(A_t(w)\) denote its enabled actions. The resource-free action ensures that this set is nonempty.

Define the Bellman operator by

\( (T_wv)(s)=\max_{a\in A_t(w)}\left(r_t(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}p_t(s'\mid s,a)v(s')\right). \)

Because transition probabilities sum to one, this operator is a contraction with factor \(\gamma<1\) in the maximum norm. It therefore has a unique fixed point \(v^*\), the optimal state-value vector.

It can be obtained from the rational linear program

\( \displaystyle \min_v\sum_{s\in S_t}v(s), \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle v(s)\ge r_t(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}p_t(s'\mid s,a)v(s') \qquad(s\in S_t,\ a\in A_t(w)). \)

To verify this formulation, any feasible vector satisfies \(v\ge T_wv\). Monotonicity gives \(v\ge T_w^kv\) for every \(k\), and contraction implies \(T_w^kv\to v^*\). Thus every feasible vector dominates \(v^*\) coordinatewise. Since \(v^*\) itself is feasible, it is the unique minimizer.

This linear program has at most \(n\) variables and \(na\) inequalities, with explicitly represented rational coefficients. Polynomial-time algorithms for rational linear programming therefore compute

\( \displaystyle V_t(w)=\sum_s\alpha_t(s)v^*(s) \)

exactly in \(\operatorname{poly}(n,a,L)\) bit operations.

The resulting rational value has polynomial binary length. Indeed, choose an optimal stationary deterministic policy \(\pi\). Its value satisfies

\( \displaystyle (I-\gamma P_\pi)v_\pi=r_\pi. \)

The matrix is invertible because \(\|\gamma P_\pi\|_\infty=\gamma<1\). Clearing denominators and applying determinant bounds shows that the solution, and hence \(\alpha_t^\mathsf{T}v_\pi\), has binary length polynomial in \(n\) and \(L\). This remains true when \(\gamma\) is close to one.

2. Real masses admit polynomial-size certificates

Consider the finite allocation polytope \(Q\) defined by the type-mass equalities, stock inequalities, and nonnegativity, without the welfare threshold.

Although its columns are implicit in the input, their number is finite: at most \(\tau2^m\). The polytope is nonempty because every type can receive the empty bundle. It is bounded because

\( \displaystyle 0\le y_{t,w}\le\mu_t. \)

Consequently, the linear welfare objective attains its maximum at a vertex of \(Q\).

A vertex uses at most \(\tau+m\) bundles

Suppose a vertex has \(q>\tau+m\) positive coordinates. Restrict the coefficient matrix of the type-mass equalities and all stock inequalities to these coordinates. It has \(\tau+m\) rows and \(q\) columns, so there is a nonzero vector \(d\) in its nullspace.

For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both \(y+\varepsilon d\) and \(y-\varepsilon d\), with zero perturbation outside the positive coordinates, remain nonnegative. They preserve every type mass and every stock usage. They are therefore two distinct feasible points whose midpoint is \(y\), contradicting extremality.

Thus every vertex has at most \(\tau+m\) positive coordinates. In particular, every yes-instance has an allocation meeting the threshold with this support bound.

The positive masses have polynomial binary length

Let a vertex have \(q\le\tau+m\) positive coordinates. The type equalities and tight stock inequalities, restricted to these coordinates, must have column rank \(q\).

Otherwise, a nonzero null direction would preserve these constraints. Small perturbations in both directions would preserve nonnegativity and the remaining stock inequalities, which have positive slack, again contradicting extremality.

Choose \(q\) independent rows. The positive masses are the unique solution of

\( \displaystyle By=d, \)

where \(B\) is an invertible \(q\times q\) matrix with entries in \(\{0,1\}\), and the entries of \(d\) are input masses or stocks.

Clearing the denominators in \(d\), followed by Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s determinant bound, gives a binary-length bound of

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(q\bigl(L+\log(q+1)\bigr)\right) \)

for each mass. Hence these masses have polynomial-size rational representations.

Verification

A certificate can list at most \(\tau+m\) tuples, each containing:

  • a type and a binary bundle;
  • its positive rational mass;
  • an enabled stationary deterministic policy for that bundle.

The verifier checks local capacities, type masses, stocks, and policy enablement. It evaluates each listed policy by solving its rational linear system, then checks that the weighted policy values meet \(Y\).

This is sound because each listed policy’s value is at most \(V_t(w)\). It is complete because a maximizing vertex can be equipped with optimal stationary deterministic policies.

The certificate size and verification time are both \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,n,a,L)\). Therefore the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. A direct reduction from \(3\)-SAT

Let a \(3\)-SAT instance have variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_h\), with \(d,h\ge1\). We construct one type.

Resources and capacities

For each variable, create two resources \(o_{i,+}\) and \(o_{i,-}\), representing its positive and negative literals. Thus

\( \displaystyle m=2d,\qquad \tau=1,\qquad \mu_1=1. \)

Use one capacity dimension with

\( \displaystyle \kappa(o,1)=1,\qquad b_1(1)=d. \)

Therefore a bundle is locally feasible exactly when it contains at most \(d\) resources.

Set every per-capita stock to one:

\( \displaystyle \bar b_o=1. \)

These stock constraints impose no additional restriction: for any mass-one allocation, the mass receiving a given resource is at most one.

The MDP

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\ge d+h\). Create:

  • a variable state \(s_i\) for each variable;
  • a clause state \(z_j\) for each clause;
  • \(N-(d+h)\) padding states.

Give every state initial probability \(1/N\), and set \(\gamma=\tfrac12\). All states are absorbing under every action.

For every literal \(\ell\), create an action \(a_\ell\) requiring exactly the resource representing \(\ell\). Add a resource-free action \(a_0\) with zero reward everywhere.

Define rewards as follows:

  • At \(s_i\), actions \(a_{x_i}\) and \(a_{\neg x_i}\) give reward one; every other action gives zero.
  • At \(z_j\), an action gives reward one precisely when its literal occurs in \(C_j\).
  • Every action gives zero reward at padding states.

This satisfies all premises. In particular, the action requirements are binary, and \(a_0\) is a resource-free, zero-reward action leaving the state unchanged.

Exact bundle values

Call a variable state covered when the bundle contains at least one of its two literal resources. Call a clause state covered when the bundle contains a resource representing a literal in that clause. Let \(c(w)\) be the number of covered variable and clause states.

At a covered state, an enabled action can earn reward one forever, giving discounted value

\( \displaystyle \sum_{k=0}^\infty(1/2)^k=2. \)

At an uncovered state, every enabled action earns zero. Since all states are absorbing, it follows exactly that

\( \displaystyle V_1(w)=\frac{2c(w)}{N}. \)

Set the decision threshold to

\( \displaystyle Y=\frac{2(d+h)}{N}. \)

Every bundle has value at most \(Y\), with equality precisely when it covers every variable state and every clause state.

Satisfiability implies feasibility

Suppose the formula has a satisfying assignment. Select exactly one resource for each variable: its positive resource when the variable is true, and its negative resource otherwise.

The resulting bundle contains \(d\) resources and is locally feasible. It covers every variable state. Since the assignment satisfies every clause, it also covers every clause state. Thus its value is \(Y\).

Assigning the entire mass to this bundle gives a yes-instance of mass allocation.

Feasibility implies satisfiability

Conversely, suppose a feasible mass allocation reaches the threshold. Since its total mass is one and every bundle value is at most \(Y\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_w y_{1,w}\bigl(Y-V_1(w)\bigr)\le0. \)

Every summand is nonnegative, so every positive-mass bundle has value \(Y\). At least one such bundle exists.

Choose one. Covering every variable state requires at least one resource for each of the \(d\) variables. Local feasibility permits at most \(d\) resources. Hence the bundle contains exactly one literal resource for each variable.

These choices define a truth assignment. Every clause state is covered, so every clause contains a selected literal, which is true under this assignment. The formula is therefore satisfiable.

Reduction size

The construction has

\( \displaystyle m=2d,\quad \tau=K=1,\quad n=N<2(d+h),\quad a=2d+1. \)

Transitions, rewards, initial probabilities, and requirements can all be explicitly tabulated in polynomial time. All probabilities and thresholds are dyadic rationals, and all numerical magnitudes are polynomially bounded.

Thus this is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from \(3\)-SAT. Combined with membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), it proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

4. Exact running times and fixed-resource tractability

An exact deterministic algorithm can enumerate the bundles, compute their values, and solve the resulting allocation linear program.

There are at most \(\tau2^m\) type-bundle pairs. Testing local feasibility and identifying enabled actions takes

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(\tau2^m m(K+a)\right)\operatorname{poly}(L) \)

bit operations. Computing all feasible bundle values takes

\( \displaystyle \tau2^m\operatorname{poly}(n,a,L). \)

The final allocation linear program has at most \(\tau2^m\) variables, \(\tau\) type equalities, \(m\) stock inequalities, and rational coefficients of polynomial binary length. Its solution time is

\( \displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(\tau2^m,\tau+m,n,L). \)

Together, these give an exact deterministic bound of

\( \displaystyle 2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,n,a,L). \)

Consequently, the problem is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed \(m\). For unrestricted \(m\), it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, so a polynomial-time algorithm in all the stated input parameters would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
