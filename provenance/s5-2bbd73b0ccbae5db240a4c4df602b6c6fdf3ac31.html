The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t\) over complete worker types \(t\), two task types \(x,y\), their weights \(w^x,w^y\), per-worker task supplies \(s_x,s_y\), and each type's valuations and productivities, compute a market-clearing, per-capita balanced DPM equilibrium and its aggregate productivity, including any mass split at the relative-valuation cutoff.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) complete worker types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=2\) task types \(x,y\), positive rational per-worker supplies \(s_x,s_y\), and positive rational weights \(w^x,w^y\) with \(w^x+w^y=1\). Each worker type specifies positive rational valuations \(v_t^x,v_t^y\) and nonnegative rational productivities \(Y_t^x,Y_t^y\); utility is additive.
Write \(B=w^xs_x+w^ys_y\). A market-clearing, per-capita balanced Dynamic Pseudomarket (DPM) equilibrium consists of positive prices \(p_x,p_y\), normalized by \(p_x+p_y=1\), positive endogenous budgets \(b_t\), and nonnegative per-worker task bundles \(a_t=(a_t^x,a_t^y)\).
Each \(a_t\) maximizes \(v_t^xz_x+v_t^yz_y\) over \(z\in\mathbb R_+^2\) subject only to \(p_xz_x+p_yz_y\leq b_t\); balance requires \(w^xa_t^x+w^ya_t^y=B\), and clearing requires \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t^j=s_j\) for \(j\in\{x,y\}\).
Compute any such equilibrium and its aggregate productivity per worker \(\mathcal P=\sum_t\mu_t(Y_t^xa_t^x+Y_t^ya_t^y)\). Tied relative valuations are permitted: at the cutoff \(v_t^x/v_t^y=p_x/p_y\), include the fractional allocation, equivalently the mass split \(\alpha_t=w^xa_t^x/B\) between the balanced pure-task bundles. No productivity-maximizing tie rule is imposed.

Result

The stated problem is exactly solvable in polynomial time. For \(m=2\), sorting the supported types by relative valuation and computing a weighted cutoff yields rational prices, budgets, bundles, cutoff splits, and aggregate productivity.

The construction permits arbitrary rational productivities and tied valuations. The threshold characterization in the cited paper is consistent with this approach, but the proof below is independent of its finite-worker statistical assumptions and integral quota. [Source, Appendix B.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23222v1#A2)

1. Demand and balance characterize the possible allocations

Fix positive prices \(p_x,p_y\) and a positive budget \(b_t\). Define
\[ M_t=\max\left\{\frac{v_t^x}{p_x},\frac{v_t^y}{p_y}\right\}. \]

For any affordable bundle \(z\),
\[ v_t^xz_x+v_t^yz_y \leq M_t(p_xz_x+p_yz_y) \leq M_tb_t. \]

This bound is attained by spending the entire budget on a task attaining the maximum ratio. Consequently, a bundle is optimal if and only if it spends the entire budget and assigns positive quantities only to tasks attaining that maximum. Indeed, equality in the two inequalities requires exactly these conditions.

Write \(r_t=v_t^x/v_t^y\) and \(c=p_x/p_y\). Thus:

  • If \(r_t>c\), optimality requires \(a_t^y=0\).
  • If \(r_t<c\), optimality requires \(a_t^x=0\).
  • If \(r_t=c\), every nonnegative bundle costing exactly \(b_t\) is optimal.

Now impose balance. Since \(B=w^xs_x+w^ys_y>0\), every nonnegative balanced bundle has a unique representation
\[ a_t^x=\frac{B}{w^x}\alpha_t,\qquad a_t^y=\frac{B}{w^y}(1-\alpha_t), \qquad 0\leq\alpha_t\leq1. \]

Define the required mass share
\[ q=\frac{w^xs_x}{B}. \]

The positivity of both weighted supplies implies \(0<q<1\). Clearing task \(x\) is equivalent to
\[ \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t=q. \]

This equality also implies clearing task \(y\), because
\[ \sum_t\mu_ta_t^y =\frac{B}{w^y}\left(1-\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t\right) =\frac{B}{w^y}(1-q) =s_y. \]

Therefore, the allocation problem reduces to finding a cutoff \(c>0\) and splits satisfying:

  • \(\alpha_t=1\) when \(r_t>c\);
  • \(\alpha_t=0\) when \(r_t<c\);
  • \(0\leq\alpha_t\leq1\) when \(r_t=c\);
  • \(\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t=q\).

2. Exact cutoff construction

Sort the distinct valuation ratios in decreasing order:
\[ R_1>R_2>\cdots>R_k. \]

For each ratio, let
\[ E_i=\sum_{t:r_t=R_i}\mu_t,\qquad C_i=\sum_{j=1}^{i}E_j,\qquad C_0=0. \]

Every \(E_i\) is positive and \(C_k=1\). Hence there is a smallest index \(i_*\) satisfying \(C_{i_*}\geq q\). Minimality gives
\[ C_{i_*-1}<q\leq C_{i_*}. \]

Set
\[ R=R_{i_*},\qquad H=C_{i_*-1},\qquad E=E_{i_*},\qquad \lambda=\frac{q-H}{E}. \]

Here \(E>0\) and \(0<\lambda\leq1\). Choose the splits as follows:

  • Set \(\alpha_t=1\) for \(r_t>R\).
  • Set \(\alpha_t=\lambda\) for \(r_t=R\).
  • Set \(\alpha_t=0\) for \(r_t<R\).

Their weighted sum is exactly
\[ \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t=H+\lambda E=q. \]

Thus all clearing conditions hold. Giving every cutoff type the same split is merely a deterministic choice among permissible tied allocations; it does not use productivity.

The boundary case \(q=C_{i_*}\) is included: then \(\lambda=1\), so the entire cutoff group receives its balanced pure-\(x\) bundle.

3. Prices and budgets complete the equilibrium

Choose normalized prices
\[ p_x=\frac{R}{1+R},\qquad p_y=\frac{1}{1+R}. \]

Because valuations are positive, \(R>0\). Therefore both prices are positive, their sum is one, and \(p_x/p_y=R\).

For each type, output
\[ a_t^x=\frac{B}{w^x}\alpha_t,\qquad a_t^y=\frac{B}{w^y}(1-\alpha_t),\qquad b_t=p_xa_t^x+p_ya_t^y. \]

Each bundle is nonzero because its weighted workload is \(B>0\). Positive prices therefore imply \(b_t>0\).

To verify optimality, consider each possible comparison:

  • If \(r_t>R\), task \(x\) has strictly greater utility per unit expenditure. The constructed bundle contains only \(x\) and costs exactly \(b_t\).
  • If \(r_t<R\), the corresponding statement holds for task \(y\).
  • If \(r_t=R\), both tasks have equal utility per unit expenditure. The constructed bundle costs exactly \(b_t\), so it attains the upper bound proved in Section 1.

Every bundle consequently maximizes utility over the specified budget constraint. Balance and clearing were already verified. This proves that the construction always returns an equilibrium.

4. Exact aggregate productivity

For the returned equilibrium, compute
\[ \mathcal P =\frac{B}{w^x}\sum_t\mu_tY_t^x\alpha_t +\frac{B}{w^y}\sum_t\mu_tY_t^y(1-\alpha_t). \]

All quantities in this expression are rational, so the result can be computed exactly.

Productivity need not be identical across equilibria. For example, take two types of mass \(1/2\), supplies \(s_x=s_y=1/2\), weights \(w^x=w^y=1/2\), and valuations \(v_t^x=v_t^y=1\). Let their productivity vectors be \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). Then \(B=1/2\) and \(q=1/2\).

At prices \(p_x=p_y=1/2\), any splits with \(\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1\) give an equilibrium, with bundles \((\alpha_t,1-\alpha_t)\) and budgets \(1/2\). Their productivity is
\[ \mathcal P =\frac12\alpha_1+\frac12(1-\alpha_2) =\alpha_1. \]

It can therefore range from zero to one. Our common-split construction returns \(\alpha_1=\alpha_2=1/2\) and \(\mathcal P=1/2\). This satisfies the requested computation of any equilibrium together with its productivity.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

Represent each rational by a binary numerator and positive binary denominator.

Sorting requires \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) exact comparisons. Ratios can be compared by cross multiplication: \(r_t>r_u\) is equivalent to
\[ v_t^xv_u^y>v_u^xv_t^y. \]
Each comparison involves integers of \(O(L)\) bits.

The remaining construction uses \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations. The bit lengths remain polynomial:

  • Each mass sum has an \(O(L)\)-bit representation. A common denominator is the product of the relevant input denominators; its bit length is bounded by the sum of their bit lengths. Summing the resulting numerators adds only \(O(\log\tau)\) bits.
  • The quantities \(B,q,R,H,E,\lambda\), prices, and each type’s output coordinates require only a constant number of arithmetic operations on \(O(L)\)-bit rationals. They therefore have \(O(L)\)-bit representations.
  • Each productivity summand has \(O(L)\) bits. Summing \(\tau\) such terms using a product common denominator requires at most \(O(\tau L+\log\tau)\) bits.

Integer addition, multiplication, division, and fraction reduction all admit algorithms polynomial in operand bit length. Thus the entire computation, including writing the output, takes polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), and hence in \(m,\tau,L\) for the stipulated \(m=2\).

No expansion of rational masses into individual workers is required.
