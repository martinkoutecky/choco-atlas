The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu \in \mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{\tau}\) summing to \(1\), and a symmetric rational valuation matrix \(W=(w_{ts})\) satisfying \(V_\mu:=\sum_{t,s}\mu_t\mu_s w_{ts}\ge 0\), output in randomized time polynomial in \(\tau\) and encoding length \(L\) a finite mass partition \(\Pi=(x^\ell)_{\ell=1}^{r}\) with \(x^\ell\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{\tau}\) and \(\sum_{\ell}x^\ell_t\le\mu_t\), such that \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu\le O(\log(\tau+1))\,\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{SW}_\mu(\Pi)]\), where \(\operatorname{SW}_\mu(\Pi)=\sum_{\ell}\sum_{t,s}w_{ts}x^\ell_t x^\ell_s\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Input also includes a symmetric matrix \(W\in\mathbb Q^{\tau\times\tau}\) with unrestricted signs and \(V_\mu:=\sum_{t,s}\mu_t\mu_sw_{ts}\ge0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; there is no separate candidate parameter \(m\).
A feasible mass partition is any finite list \(\Pi=(x^\ell)_{\ell=1}^r\) of nonzero vectors \(x^\ell\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\tau}\) satisfying \(\sum_\ell x_t^\ell\le\mu_t\). Types may be split among coalitions; residual mass consists of isolated individuals and contributes zero welfare. The empty list is allowed, and coalition sizes and their number are unrestricted.
Define \(\operatorname{SW}_\mu(\Pi):=\sum_\ell\sum_{t,s}w_{ts}x_t^\ell x_s^\ell\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu:=\sup_{\Pi}\operatorname{SW}_\mu(\Pi)\) over these feasible real partitions. Diagonal entries describe interactions between distinct individuals of the same type and are unrestricted; individual self-interactions have zero mass. Algorithmic outputs must list binary-rational coalition masses.

Does there exist a randomized algorithm with expected running time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\) that, on every such input, outputs a feasible partition satisfying \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu\le C\log(\tau+1)\,\mathbb E[\operatorname{SW}_\mu(\Pi)]\) for some universal constant \(C\ge1\)?

For finite symmetric additively separable hedonic games with nonnegative total valuation, a randomized polynomial-time algorithm achieves an expected \(O(\log n)\)-approximation, where \(n\) is the number of agents. [Bullinger, Chatziafratis, and Shahkar](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06017v1)
No result settling this precise divisible-mass approximation question was located in the literature search.

  • Removed zero-mass types so that tau counts precisely the positive-mass support.
  • Defined the previously undefined OPT_mu as the supremum over all finite feasible real mass partitions, without presupposing attainment.
  • Interpreted unallocated mass as isolated individuals with zero welfare, retaining the stated componentwise inequality and the discrete availability of singleton coalitions.
  • Allowed the empty list and excluded zero vectors from listed coalitions; imposed no bound on coalition size or number.
  • Required binary-rational algorithmic outputs to make polynomial running time meaningful in the bit model, while retaining real-valued benchmark partitions.
  • Interpreted unrestricted diagonal entries as valuations between distinct individuals sharing a type, rather than individual self-valuations.
  • Used expected polynomial running time, matching the discrete randomized approximation convention, and made the hidden approximation constant universal.
  • Specified that this coalition-formation problem has no separate candidate parameter m.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

A logarithmic approximation in the support size

Yes. There is a randomized algorithm with running time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\) that outputs at most two coalitions and satisfies
\(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu\le 1024\ln(\tau+1)\,\mathbb E[\operatorname{SW}_\mu(\Pi)]\).

The proof below handles unrestricted diagonal entries and produces binary-rational masses.

1. Reduction to quadratic maximization on a box

Write \(n:=\tau\), \(P:=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n)\), and \(A:=PWP\). Thus \(A\) is symmetric and rational, and
\(\mathbf 1^\mathsf T A\mathbf 1=V_\mu\ge0\).

Define
\(M_A:=\max_{z\in[-1,1]^n}z^\mathsf TAz\).
This maximum exists by compactness and continuity, and \(M_A\ge0\), since \(z=0\) is feasible.

Lemma 1. \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu\le M_A\).

Proof. Fix any feasible finite partition \(\Pi=(x^\ell)_{\ell=1}^r\), and put \(y^\ell:=P^{-1}x^\ell\). Positivity of the masses makes \(P^{-1}\) well-defined. Feasibility gives
\(y^\ell\ge0\) and \(\sum_\ell y_i^\ell\le1\) for every \(i\).

Let \(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_r\) be independent uniform signs, and set \(z:=\sum_\ell\varepsilon_\ell y^\ell\). For every outcome,
\(|z_i|\le\sum_\ell y_i^\ell\le1\).
Independence and zero means give

\(\mathbb E[z^\mathsf TAz] =\sum_{\ell,k}\mathbb E[\varepsilon_\ell\varepsilon_k](y^\ell)^\mathsf TAy^k =\sum_\ell(x^\ell)^\mathsf TWx^\ell =\operatorname{SW}_\mu(\Pi).\)

Since \(z^\mathsf TAz\le M_A\) for every outcome, every feasible partition has welfare at most \(M_A\). Taking the supremum proves the lemma. \(\square\)

Conversely, every \(z\in[-1,1]^n\) defines a feasible partition using all the mass:

\(x^+:=\tfrac12P(\mathbf1+z),\qquad x^-:=\tfrac12P(\mathbf1-z).\)

Both vectors are nonnegative and sum to \(\mu\); a zero vector is omitted. Direct expansion gives

\(\operatorname{SW}_\mu(x^+,x^-) =\tfrac12\mathbf1^\mathsf TA\mathbf1+\tfrac12z^\mathsf TAz =\tfrac12V_\mu+\tfrac12z^\mathsf TAz.\)

Consequently, it suffices to approximate \(M_A\) logarithmically. Notice that these identities include all diagonal terms exactly.

2. The semidefinite relaxation

Consider

\(S:=\max\{\operatorname{tr}(AX):X\succeq0,\ X_{ii}\le1\text{ for all }i\}.\)

Here \(X\succeq0\) means that \(X\) is positive semidefinite. Every feasible box vector gives a feasible matrix \(X=zz^\mathsf T\), so
\(S\ge M_A\ge\operatorname{OPT}_\mu\).

The feasible region is compact: positive semidefiniteness implies
\(|X_{ij}|\le\sqrt{X_{ii}X_{jj}}\le1\).
It is strictly feasible, since \(X=I/2\) satisfies \(X\succ0\) and every diagonal inequality strictly.

Its dual is

\(\min\{\sum_i\lambda_i:\lambda_i\ge0,\ D-A\succeq0,\ D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)\}.\)

The dual is feasible by taking \(D=cI\) with sufficiently large \(c\). Slater duality therefore gives an attained dual optimum \(D\) satisfying
\(\sum_i\lambda_i=S\).
This is the standard strict-feasibility duality theorem; see [Lovász, Section 3.1](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~thomas/teaching/m514_web/Lovasz_semidef.pdf).

For the rounding argument, suppose that \(S>0\) and we have a binary-rational matrix \(B\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) satisfying

\(\sum_j B_{ij}^2\le1\quad\text{for every }i,\qquad p:=\operatorname{tr}(ABB^\mathsf T)\ge S-\epsilon,\qquad 0<\epsilon\le S/8.\)

Section 4 constructs such a matrix in polynomial time.

3. Rounding with fair bits

Set \(k:=\lceil\log_2(24n)\rceil\), and let \(R\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(R^2\ge2k\). Then
\(R^2<8k\).

Draw independent uniform signs \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n\), and put \(g:=B\xi\). Define the event
\(\mathcal E:=\{\max_i|g_i|\le R\}\).

The algorithm selects \(z\) as follows:

  • If \(\mathcal E\) holds and \(g^\mathsf TAg\ge0\), set \(z:=g/R\).
  • Otherwise, set \(z:=0\).

Thus \(z\in[-1,1]^n\) in every outcome. All calculations and comparisons are exact rational operations.

3.1. Probability of rejecting a sample

For each row of \(B\),

\(\mathbb E[e^{tg_i}] =\prod_j\cosh(tB_{ij}) \le\exp\!\left(\tfrac12t^2\sum_jB_{ij}^2\right) \le e^{t^2/2}.\)

The inequality uses \(\cosh u\le e^{u^2/2}\). Applying the exponential Markov inequality with \(t=R\), and also to \(-g_i\), gives
\(\Pr(|g_i|>R)\le2e^{-R^2/2}\).

Hence, by the union bound and \(k\ge\ln(24n)\),

\(\Pr(\mathcal E^c)\le2ne^{-R^2/2}\le2ne^{-k}\le1/12.\)

3.2. Controlling the indefinite objective

Let \(D\) be an optimal dual matrix from Section 2, and define

\(H:=B^\mathsf TDB,\qquad K:=B^\mathsf T(D-A)B.\)

Both \(H\) and \(K\) are positive semidefinite. For every outcome,

\(g^\mathsf TAg=\xi^\mathsf TH\xi-\xi^\mathsf TK\xi.\)

Moreover, with \(X:=BB^\mathsf T\),

\(\operatorname{tr}K =\sum_i\lambda_iX_{ii}-p \le\sum_i\lambda_i-p =S-p \le\epsilon.\)

Here we used \(\lambda_i\ge0\) and \(X_{ii}\le1\). Also,
\(\operatorname{tr}H=p+\operatorname{tr}K\ge S-\epsilon\).

Put \(h:=\xi^\mathsf TH\xi\). Since \(H\succeq0\), we have \(h\ge0\), and
\(\mathbb E[h]=\operatorname{tr}H\).
Expanding the square and using independence of the signs yields

\(\mathbb E[h^2] =(\operatorname{tr}H)^2+4\sum_{i<j}H_{ij}^2 \le(\operatorname{tr}H)^2+2\operatorname{tr}(H^2) \le3(\operatorname{tr}H)^2.\)

The last inequality follows because the eigenvalues of \(H\) are nonnegative, so
\(\operatorname{tr}(H^2)\le(\operatorname{tr}H)^2\).

Cauchy–Schwarz now gives

\(\mathbb E[h\,\mathbf1_{\mathcal E^c}] \le\sqrt{\mathbb E[h^2]\Pr(\mathcal E^c)} \le\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}H.\)

Therefore
\(\mathbb E[h\,\mathbf1_{\mathcal E}]\ge\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}H\).

For a real number \(a\), write \(a_+:=\max\{a,0\}\). Because \(\xi^\mathsf TK\xi\ge0\),

\(\mathbb E[(g^\mathsf TAg)_+\mathbf1_{\mathcal E}] \ge\mathbb E[h\mathbf1_{\mathcal E}] -\mathbb E[\xi^\mathsf TK\xi] \ge\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}H-\operatorname{tr}K.\)

The bounds above imply

\(\mathbb E[(g^\mathsf TAg)_+\mathbf1_{\mathcal E}] \ge\tfrac12(S-\epsilon)-\epsilon \ge\tfrac14S.\)

By the definition of \(z\), we have proved

\(\mathbb E[z^\mathsf TAz]\ge S/(4R^2).\)

This argument is valid for arbitrary signs on the diagonal of \(A\).

4. Polynomial precision and binary-rational construction

Two issues remain: choosing a sufficiently small tolerance without knowing \(S\), and constructing the rational factor \(B\).

4.1. A computable lower bound on a positive optimum

First test exactly whether \(A\preceq0\), using rational symmetric elimination. This takes polynomial time: a negative pivot, or a zero diagonal with a nonzero row, certifies failure of positive semidefiniteness when testing \(-A\); positive pivots permit Schur-complement elimination.

If \(A\preceq0\), then \(M_A=0\). Lemma 1 and feasibility of the empty partition imply \(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu=0\), so the algorithm outputs the empty partition.

Suppose henceforth that \(A\not\preceq0\). Choose a positive integer \(d\) clearing all denominators of \(A\), and write \(J:=dA\). Define

\(M:=n\max_{i,j}|J_{ij}|,\qquad \beta:=1/(dM^{n-1}).\)

Since \(J\ne0\), we have \(M\ge1\). Every eigenvalue of \(J\) has absolute value at most \(M\), by the maximum absolute row-sum bound.

Let \(r\) be the rank of \(J\). The product of its nonzero eigenvalues, up to sign, is the coefficient of the lowest-degree nonzero term of its characteristic polynomial. It is therefore a nonzero integer and has absolute value at least one. Consequently, every nonzero eigenvalue \(\theta\) satisfies

\(|\theta|\ge M^{-(r-1)}\ge M^{-(n-1)}.\)

Since \(A\not\preceq0\), it has a positive eigenvalue at least \(\beta\). A corresponding Euclidean unit eigenvector belongs to \([-1,1]^n\); hence
\(S\ge M_A\ge\beta\).

The integers \(d\) and \(J\) have polynomial bit length: for example, \(d\) can be the product of the entry denominators. Thus \(\beta\) also has polynomial bit length. We choose
\(\epsilon:=\beta/8\).
This ensures \(0<\epsilon\le S/8\), with \(\log(1/\epsilon)\) polynomial in the input length.

4.2. Obtaining a rational feasible SDP solution

We use the standard ellipsoid optimization theorem: a rational linear objective over a compact convex set with known inner and outer balls and a polynomial-time separation oracle can be optimized to additive tolerance in time polynomial in the input length and the logarithm of the inverse tolerance. The SDP version is described in [Lovász, Section 3.2](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~thomas/teaching/m514_web/Lovasz_semidef.pdf).

For our feasible region, the necessary geometric bounds are explicit. In the space of symmetric matrices with Frobenius norm, the ball of radius \(1/4\) around \(I/2\) is feasible: every matrix in this ball has smallest eigenvalue at least \(1/4\) and diagonal entries at most \(3/4\). Conversely, every feasible matrix satisfies
\(\|X\|_F\le\operatorname{tr}X\le n\).

There is an exact rational separation oracle. A violated diagonal inequality supplies a coordinate cut. Otherwise, rational symmetric elimination either verifies positive semidefiniteness or returns a rational vector \(v\) with \(v^\mathsf TXv<0\); the valid inequality \(v^\mathsf TYv\ge0\) then separates \(X\) from the feasible region. The elimination and its certificates have polynomial bit length.

We can therefore compute a rational matrix \(Y\) satisfying

\(Y\succeq0,\qquad Y_{ii}\le1,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(AY)\ge S-\epsilon/2,\)

in polynomial time.

4.3. Constructing a dyadic factor

Let \(Q:=\max\{1,\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|\}\), so \(Q\ge\|A\|_F\). Choose a positive dyadic number \(\eta\) satisfying

\(\eta\le1/2,\qquad \eta\le\epsilon/(4Qn(n+1)).\)

It requires only polynomially many bits.

Rational symmetric elimination gives a factorization
\(Y=U\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)U^\mathsf T\),
where \(U\) and the nonnegative \(a_i\) are rational. Zero pivots are handled by omitting zero rows and columns and padding the resulting factor with zero columns. The rational entries have polynomial bit length, as they are ratios of minors of \(Y\).

Define the real factor
\(F:=U\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{a_1},\ldots,\sqrt{a_n})\).
Then \(FF^\mathsf T=Y\), and every row of \(F\) has Euclidean norm at most one.

Approximate each entry of \(F\) by a dyadic number with error at most \(\eta/n\), producing a dyadic matrix \(C\) with
\(\|C-F\|_F\le\eta\).
This is polynomial-time scalar arithmetic: square roots of nonnegative rationals can be enclosed to the required precision by bisection, followed by multiplication and dyadic rounding.

Set \(B:=(1-\eta)C\). Each row satisfies

\(\|B_{i,*}\|_2\le(1-\eta)(1+\eta)\le1.\)

Furthermore,

\(\|B-F\|_F \le(1-\eta)\|C-F\|_F+\eta\|F\|_F \le\eta(n+1).\)

Since both \(B\) and \(F\) have Frobenius norm at most \(\sqrt n\),

\(\|BB^\mathsf T-Y\|_F \le(\|B\|_F+\|F\|_F)\|B-F\|_F \le2n(n+1)\eta.\)

It follows that

\(|\operatorname{tr}(ABB^\mathsf T)-\operatorname{tr}(AY)| \le\|A\|_F\|BB^\mathsf T-Y\|_F \le\epsilon/2.\)

Thus \(B\) satisfies all the requirements of Section 2, including
\(\operatorname{tr}(ABB^\mathsf T)\ge S-\epsilon\).
Its entries are dyadic and have polynomial bit length.

5. The algorithm and its guarantee

The complete algorithm is:

  • Form \(A=PWP\). If \(A\preceq0\), output the empty partition.
  • Compute \(\beta\), set \(\epsilon=\beta/8\), and construct \(B\) as in Section 4.
  • Perform the rounding in Section 3 to obtain \(z\).
  • Output the nonzero vectors among \(x^+=P(\mathbf1+z)/2\) and \(x^-=P(\mathbf1-z)/2\).

Every output is feasible. Because \(V_\mu\ge0\), its expected welfare satisfies

\(\mathbb E[\operatorname{SW}_\mu(\Pi)] =\tfrac12V_\mu+\tfrac12\mathbb E[z^\mathsf TAz] \ge S/(8R^2) \ge\operatorname{OPT}_\mu/(8R^2).\)

To bound the constant, observe that

\(k\le\log_2(48n)\le6+\log_2(n+1)\le7\log_2(n+1).\)

Consequently,

\(R^2<8k\le(56/\ln2)\ln(n+1)<128\ln(n+1).\)

Combining these inequalities proves

\(\operatorname{OPT}_\mu \le1024\ln(n+1)\,\mathbb E[\operatorname{SW}_\mu(\Pi)].\)

All deterministic computations have polynomial bit complexity. Rounding uses exactly \(n\) fair bits and polynomial-time rational arithmetic, so even the worst-case running time is polynomial. Because \(B\) is dyadic and \(R\) is a power of two, the output coordinates are dyadic fractions of the input masses and are binary-rational; in particular, dyadic input masses give dyadic output masses.

The continuous quadratic step is consistent with broader logarithmic SDP bounds for indefinite homogeneous quadratic optimization, such as [He, Luo, Nie, and Zhang, Theorem 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701070). The proof above establishes the required box case directly, including the finite-precision construction. No expansion into a potentially enormous finite population is needed.
