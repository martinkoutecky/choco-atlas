Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Target. Is it polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard, to decide whether some size-\(k\) committee obtainable by this process contains at least \(s\) candidates approved by a distinguished type \(t^\star\), i.e. satisfies \(|W\cap t^\star|\ge s\)?

Complexity of the Rational-Mass Sequential-PAV Problem

Theorem

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, allowing rational masses over explicitly listed approval types preserves the known hardness for finite unweighted approval profiles exactly; no approximation or limiting argument is needed.

1. Simplifying the marginal contribution

Let \(S\subseteq C\) and \(c\in C\setminus S\). For every type \(t\in T\),

\[ H_{|t\cap(S\cup\{c\})|}-H_{|t\cap S|} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{|t\cap S|+1},&c\in t,\\[4pt] 0,&c\notin t. \end{cases} \]

Therefore

\[ \Delta_\mu(c\mid S) = \sum_{\substack{t\in T\\c\in t}} \frac{\mu_t}{|t\cap S|+1}. \]

This form will be used both for verification and for the reduction.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate is an ordered sequence of distinct candidates

\[ (c_1,\ldots,c_k). \]

For \(j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), let

\[ S_{j-1}=\{c_1,\ldots,c_{j-1}\}. \]

The verifier checks that, for every \(j\) and every \(d\in C\setminus S_{j-1}\),

\[ \Delta_\mu(c_j\mid S_{j-1}) \geq \Delta_\mu(d\mid S_{j-1}). \]

This is precisely the condition that \(c_j\) is an allowed tie choice at step \(j\). The verifier then checks

\[ |S_k\cap t^\star|\geq s. \]

It remains to justify that the marginal comparisons can be performed in polynomial time.

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=p_t/q_t\). Every term in a marginal has the form

\[ \frac{p_t}{q_t(|t\cap S|+1)}. \]

The additional factor \(|t\cap S|+1\) is at most \(|C|+1\). A common denominator obtained by multiplying the denominators of at most \(|T|\) terms has bit length at most the sum of their bit lengths, namely polynomial in the input size. Hence every marginal can be computed and compared exactly using polynomially many operations on polynomial-bit integers.

There are at most \(k|C|\) relevant marginal comparisons. Thus the certificate is verifiable in polynomial time, proving that the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. Reduction from finite unweighted sequential PAV

Use the supplied known result:

Given a finite unweighted approval profile, a committee size \(k\), a distinguished voter, and a threshold \(s\), deciding whether some sequential-PAV winning committee contains at least \(s\) candidates approved by that voter is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Consider such an instance. Let the voters be indexed by \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), let \(A_i\subseteq C\) be voter \(i\)'s approval set, and let \(i^\star\) be the distinguished voter.

Assume first that \(A_{i^\star}\neq\varnothing\). Let

\[ V^+=\{i:A_i\neq\varnothing\}, \qquad N=|V^+|. \]

Because \(A_{i^\star}\neq\varnothing\), we have \(N\geq1\).

For every distinct nonempty approval set \(t\) occurring in the profile, define

\[ n_t=|\{i:A_i=t\}|. \]

Construct the rational-mass instance by setting

\[ T=\{A_i:i\in V^+\}, \qquad \mu_t=\frac{n_t}{N}, \qquad t^\star=A_{i^\star}. \]

Repeated approval sets occur only once in \(T\). Their multiplicities are encoded in the masses. Every mass is positive, and

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t\in T}n_t = 1. \]

4. Exact preservation of every sequential choice

Let \(\Delta_V(c\mid S)\) denote the ordinary unweighted sequential-PAV marginal in the original profile. For every state \(S\subseteq C\) and every \(c\in C\setminus S\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta_\mu(c\mid S) &= \sum_{t\in T} \frac{n_t}{N} \left( H_{|t\cap(S\cup\{c\})|} - H_{|t\cap S|} \right)\\ &= \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i\in V^+} \left( H_{|A_i\cap(S\cup\{c\})|} - H_{|A_i\cap S|} \right). \end{aligned} \]

A voter with \(A_i=\varnothing\) contributes zero to every marginal. Consequently,

\[ \Delta_\mu(c\mid S)=\frac{1}{N}\Delta_V(c\mid S). \]

The factor \(1/N\) is positive and independent of \(c\). Hence, for all remaining candidates \(c,d\),

\[ \Delta_\mu(c\mid S)\geq\Delta_\mu(d\mid S) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Delta_V(c\mid S)\geq\Delta_V(d\mid S). \]

Thus the maximizing candidates, including all ties, are identical at every state \(S\).

Induction on the number of selection steps now shows that an ordered candidate sequence is permitted in the original unweighted instance if and only if it is permitted in the rational-mass instance. Therefore the two instances have exactly the same obtainable size-\(k\) committees.

Moreover,

\[ |W\cap t^\star| = |W\cap A_{i^\star}|, \]

so the distinguished-voter condition is also preserved exactly. It is immaterial if other voters share \(A_{i^\star}\), because the target condition depends only on the distinguished approval set, not on the identity or mass of the corresponding type.

5. Empty distinguished ballots

If \(A_{i^\star}=\varnothing\), then every committee \(W\) satisfies

\[ |W\cap A_{i^\star}|=0. \]

Such an instance is therefore decidable immediately from whether a size-\(k\) sequential-PAV outcome exists and whether \(0\geq s\). For standard well-formed instances with \(0\leq k\leq|C|\), an outcome always exists.

These trivial cases can be mapped to fixed valid yes- and no-instances of the rational-mass problem. For example:

  • A fixed yes-instance has \(C=\{a\}\), \(k=1\), \(T=\{\{a\}\}\), \(\mu_{\{a\}}=1\), \(t^\star=\{a\}\), and \(s=1\).
  • A fixed no-instance has \(C=\{a,b\}\), \(k=1\), masses \(\mu_{\{a\}}=1/3\) and \(\mu_{\{b\}}=2/3\), distinguished type \(t^\star=\{a\}\), and \(s=1\). Candidate \(b\) is the unique first-step maximizer.

Hence the requirement that all explicitly represented types be nonempty does not obstruct the reduction.

6. Polynomial size and conclusion

The distinct approval types can be obtained by grouping equal approval ballots. There are at most \(n\) such types, and every mass \(n_t/N\) has polynomial encoding length. The construction is therefore a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard by this reduction and belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\) by Section 2. Therefore it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, it is not polynomial-time solvable unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Step-by-step audit

1. Marginal-contribution formula

For \(c\notin S\), if \(c\in t\), adding \(c\) increases \(|t\cap S|\) by exactly one, so the harmonic-score increment is

\[ \frac{1}{|t\cap S|+1}. \]

If \(c\notin t\), the increment is zero. The resulting formula for \(\Delta_\mu(c\mid S)\) is correct.

2. Membership in NP

An ordered sequence of distinct candidates correctly records all tie choices. Comparing its selected candidate with every remaining candidate at each step is exactly the condition for the sequence to be obtainable.

The exact-arithmetic argument is sound:

  • Each denominator is \(q_t(|t\cap S|+1)\).
  • Products of polynomially many input denominators have polynomial bit length.
  • Sums and comparisons of the resulting rational numbers therefore take polynomial time.

There is a minor encoding edge case. The claimed bound of at most \(k|C|\) comparisons is polynomial only after observing that a yes-instance must have \(k\leq |C|\), or after specifying that the verifier first rejects \(k>|C|\).   • Every constructed type is nonempty.
  • The distinguished ballot is represented whenever \(A_{i^\star}\neq\varnothing\).
  • The masses are positive rationals summing to one.
  • There are at most \(n\) types.

The construction meets the target input format.
