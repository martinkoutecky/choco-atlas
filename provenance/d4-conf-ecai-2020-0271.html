The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a target \(p\), and a finite support \(\mathcal A\) of approval types with binary rational masses \(\mu_A\) summing to \(1\), compute the maximum retained mass \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}x_A\), subject to \(0\le x_A\le\mu_A\) and, for every \(a\ne p\), \(\sum_{A:p\in A,\ a\notin A}x_A\ge\sum_{A:a\in A,\ p\notin A}x_A\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a finite nonempty candidate set, \(m=|C|\), and \(p\in C\). An approval type \(A\subseteq C\) strictly prefers approved candidates to disapproved candidates and is indifferent within each group; \(A=\varnothing\) and \(A=C\) are allowed.
A society is given by its support: the list \(\mathcal A\) of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_A\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval types.
Retention may split each type's mass: choose real numbers \(0\le x_A\le\mu_A\), without renormalizing the retained masses. Define the continuous Young score by
\(S(p)=\max\left\{\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}x_A:\ \sum_{A\in\mathcal A:p\in A,\ a\notin A}x_A\ge\sum_{A\in\mathcal A:a\in A,\ p\notin A}x_A\text{ for every }a\in C\setminus\{p\}\right\}\), subject also to the retention bounds.
Pairwise ties are permitted, and retaining zero mass is allowed. The threshold problem additionally takes a binary rational \(q\in[0,1]\) and asks whether \(S(p)\ge q\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Can \(S(p)\) be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is its threshold problem NP-hard?

For finite electorates with dichotomous preferences, deciding whether a candidate's Young score is at least a given integer is NP-complete, whereas deciding whether a candidate has maximum Young score is in \(\mathrm P\).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified real retention variables and no renormalization, making explicit the divisible-mass interpretation.
  • Allowed empty and universal approval sets, as permitted by the discrete definition.
  • Explicitly allowed zero retained mass and pairwise ties, following the weak Condorcet condition.
  • Introduced a binary rational threshold q in [0,1] to give NP-hardness a decision formulation; L includes the entire input, including q when present.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Theorem. The continuous Young score \(S(p)\) can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Its threshold problem belongs to \(\mathrm P\), and therefore is not NP-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

Proof: linear programming formulation. Enumerate the supported types as \(\mathcal A=\{A_1,\ldots,A_\tau\}\), and write \(\mu_j=\mu_{A_j}\). For each opponent \(a\in C\setminus\{p\}\), define
\( d_{aj}=\mathbf 1_{\{p\in A_j,\ a\notin A_j\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{a\in A_j,\ p\notin A_j\}}. \)
Thus \(d_{aj}\in\{-1,0,1\}\).

Consider the rational linear program
\( \max \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}x_j \)
subject to
\( \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}d_{aj}x_j\ge 0 \)
for every \(a\ne p\), and
\( 0\le x_j\le\mu_j \)
for every \(j\).

For each opponent, its constraint states precisely that the retained mass strictly preferring \(p\) to that opponent is at least the mass strictly preferring that opponent to \(p\). Types indifferent between the two contribute zero. Consequently, this program’s feasible points are exactly the permitted retentions, and its optimum is \(S(p)\).

The feasible region contains the zero vector. It is closed and contained in the bounded box \(\prod_{j=1}^{\tau}[0,\mu_j]\), so it is compact. Hence the maximum is attained, and \(0\le S(p)\le\sum_j\mu_j=1\). This argument also covers \(m=1\), when there are no opponent constraints and \(S(p)=1\).

Proof: polynomial encoding and exact computation. We use the standard rational linear programming theorem: a linear program with binary rational data can be solved in polynomial bit complexity; for a nonempty bounded feasible region, an exact rational optimum and an optimal rational solution can be returned. This is the rational-data polynomial-time solvability theorem underlying the ellipsoid method. See [Adler and Beling, introduction](https://adler.ieor.berkeley.edu/ilans_pubs/lp_algebraic_stoc_1992.pdf).

Here there are \(\tau\) variables and \(m-1+2\tau\) inequalities. Every constraint coefficient belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\); the only nonconstant right-hand sides are the input masses. Membership tables for the explicitly listed types, and hence all coefficients, can be constructed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Even a dense encoding of the full program has length \(O(m\tau+\tau^2+L)\). The theorem therefore yields exact computation in the required polynomial time.

For completeness, exact output also has polynomial length. Write each input mass as \(\mu_j=u_j/v_j\), where \(u_j,v_j\) are integers and \(v_j>0\), and let \(D=\prod_jv_j\). Then \(\log_2 D\le L\). After setting \(y_j=Dx_j\), every right-hand side is an integer, and every constraint coefficient remains in \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

A linear objective over a nonempty bounded polyhedron has an optimal vertex. At such a vertex, \(\tau\) linearly independent active constraints give a system \(By=b\), with \(B\) an invertible integer matrix and \(b\) an integer vector. Cramer’s rule implies that
\( \sum_jy_j=K/\det B \)
for some integer \(K\). The determinant expansion gives
\( 1\le|\det B|\le\tau!, \)
because it has \(\tau!\) terms, each of absolute value at most one. Thus the reduced denominator of
\( S(p)=K/(D\det B) \)
is at most \(D\tau!\). Since \(S(p)\in[0,1]\), its reduced numerator is no larger than its denominator. Both therefore have \(O(L+\tau\log(\tau+1))\) bits.

Threshold decision and the finite-electorate distinction. Given the rational threshold \(q\), add the single inequality
\( \sum_jx_j\ge q. \)
The resulting rational system is feasible exactly when \(S(p)\ge q\): attainment of the maximum justifies the forward implication, and any feasible retention justifies the reverse implication. Rational linear programming feasibility decides this in polynomial time.

The stated finite-electorate hardness does not contradict this result. If type \(A_j\) contains \(n_j\) voters, retaining whole voters requires an integer variable \(z_j\in\{0,\ldots,n_j\}\). With normalized masses \(\mu_j=n_j/n\), the continuous problem instead permits every real \(x_j\in[0,n_j/n]\), including fractions of individual voters. The finite problem’s integrality restriction is absent here; the continuous problem is exactly the linear program proved above.
