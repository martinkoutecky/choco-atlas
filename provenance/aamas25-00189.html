The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(R=B\cup S\) of complete order types \(t=(\sigma_t,p_t,q_t)\), rational society masses \(\mu\in\Delta_R\), and compatibility \(p_b\ge p_s\), compute a nonnegative flow \(z_{bs}\) maximizing \(\sum_{b,s}z_{bs}\), subject to \(z_{bs}=0\) for incompatible pairs, \(\sum_s z_{bs}\le q_b\mu_b\), and \(\sum_b z_{bs}\le q_s\mu_s\).

Result

Computing an exact maximum-volume allocation is polynomial-time solvable for the stated case \(m=1\). In fact, a greedy algorithm operates directly on the supported types and produces at most \(\tau\) positive allocation entries.

The proof below handles divisible quantities and binary-encoded rational masses without expanding quantities into unit orders.

1. Capacities and existence of an optimum

Write \(c_t=q_t\mu_t\). Thus a type \(t\) has total available capacity \(c_t\), and the problem is to maximize

\[ \sum_{b\in B,s\in S}z_{bs} \]

subject to the stated compatibility and capacity constraints.

If either side is empty, the zero allocation is optimal. Otherwise, the feasible set is nonempty, closed, and bounded: every coordinate satisfies \(0\leq z_{bs}\leq\min(c_b,c_s)\). It is therefore compact, so the continuous objective attains a maximum.

The same existence argument applies to every residual instance obtained by reducing capacities and deleting exhausted types.

2. Exchange lemma

Lemma. Consider a residual instance with strictly positive capacities. Let \(b\) be a buyer of minimum price and \(s\) a seller of minimum price. If \(p_b\geq p_s\), there is an optimal allocation satisfying

\[ z_{bs}=\min(c_b,c_s). \]

Proof. Among all optimal allocations, choose one maximizing \(z_{bs}\). Such a choice exists by compactness. Suppose, for a contradiction, that \(z_{bs}<\min(c_b,c_s)\). Define its unused capacities by

\[ r_b=c_b-\sum_{v\in S}z_{bv}, \qquad r_s=c_s-\sum_{u\in B}z_{us}. \]

There are four cases.

  • Both \(r_b>0\) and \(r_s>0\). Increasing \(z_{bs}\) by \(\delta=\min(r_b,r_s)>0\) is feasible and increases total volume, contradicting optimality.
  • \(r_b>0\) and \(r_s=0\). Because \(z_{bs}<c_s\), some buyer \(d\ne b\) satisfies \(z_{ds}>0\). Transfer \(\delta=\min(r_b,z_{ds})>0\) from \(z_{ds}\) to \(z_{bs}\). Seller \(s\)'s total is unchanged, buyer \(b\) remains within capacity, and buyer \(d\)'s total decreases. Volume is unchanged, but \(z_{bs}\) increases, contradicting its maximality among optimal allocations.
  • \(r_b=0\) and \(r_s>0\). Because \(z_{bs}<c_b\), some seller \(e\ne s\) satisfies \(z_{be}>0\). Transfer \(\delta=\min(r_s,z_{be})>0\) from \(z_{be}\) to \(z_{bs}\). This is feasible, preserves volume, and increases \(z_{bs}\), again a contradiction.
  • Both \(r_b=0\) and \(r_s=0\). There are \(e\ne s\) and \(d\ne b\) with \(z_{be}>0\) and \(z_{ds}>0\). Since \(b\) has minimum buyer price and \(z_{be}>0\) is compatible,

\[ p_d\geq p_b\geq p_e. \]

Thus \(d\) and \(e\) are compatible. For \(\delta=\min(z_{be},z_{ds})>0\), make the changes

\[ z_{be}\leftarrow z_{be}-\delta,\qquad z_{ds}\leftarrow z_{ds}-\delta,\qquad z_{bs}\leftarrow z_{bs}+\delta,\qquad z_{de}\leftarrow z_{de}+\delta. \]

All row and column totals are unchanged, all entries remain nonnegative, and both increased entries are compatible. Volume is unchanged while \(z_{bs}\) increases, a contradiction.

Every case contradicts the chosen allocation. Hence \(z_{bs}=\min(c_b,c_s)\). \(\square\)

3. Algorithm and correctness

Sort buyers and sellers separately by increasing price, breaking ties arbitrarily. Initialize all allocation entries to zero. Repeatedly take the first remaining buyer \(b\) and seller \(s\):

  • If \(p_b<p_s\), discard \(b\).
  • Otherwise, allocate \(\delta=\min(c_b,c_s)\) to the pair \((b,s)\), subtract \(\delta\) from both residual capacities, and remove every exhausted type.

Stop when either list is empty.

Feasibility. The algorithm allocates only between compatible types and never exceeds a residual capacity. Thus its output satisfies every original constraint.

Optimality. If \(p_b<p_s\), every remaining seller has price at least \(p_s\), so \(b\) has no compatible seller. Discarding it does not change the optimal volume.

Otherwise, the exchange lemma provides an optimum containing the algorithm's trade of size \(\delta\). Subtracting that trade from this optimum gives a feasible allocation for the residual instance. Conversely, adding the trade to any feasible residual allocation gives a feasible allocation for the current instance. Therefore,

\[ \operatorname{OPT}(\text{current}) = \delta+\operatorname{OPT}(\text{residual}). \]

At least one type is exhausted by each trade. Induction on the number of remaining types now proves that the algorithm's total volume is optimal.

Equal prices are handled by the compatible branch because its condition is \(p_b\geq p_s\).

4. Exact arithmetic and polynomial bit complexity

Represent each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/d_t\), with positive integers \(a_t,d_t\) in binary. Define

\[ D=\prod_{t\in R}d_t, \qquad C_t=Dc_t=q_ta_t\frac{D}{d_t}. \]

Every \(C_t\) is an integer. Scaling every capacity and allocation by \(D\) preserves feasibility and multiplies the objective by \(D\), so we may run the algorithm using these integer capacities and divide its output by \(D\).

Although \(D\) can be numerically large, its bit length is polynomially bounded:

\[ \log_2 D=\sum_{t\in R}\log_2 d_t=O(L). \]

Each \(C_t\) also has \(O(L)\) bits. During the algorithm, residual capacities and traded quantities never exceed their initial capacities, so all capacity arithmetic uses \(O(L)\)-bit integers.

Rational prices are compared exactly by cross multiplication. Their comparison operands have \(O(L)\) bits. Sorting takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) comparisons, and the subsequent algorithm has at most \(\tau\) iterations because each iteration removes a type. Integer multiplication, division, comparison, and subtraction on these bit lengths take polynomial time.

Each nonzero output is an integer traded quantity divided by \(D\), hence has polynomial encoding length. There are at most \(\tau\) such entries; even writing the entire matrix, including zeros, requires only polynomial space and time.

Consequently, the algorithm computes an exact maximum-volume allocation in time polynomial in \(\tau,L\), and therefore in the requested parameters \(m,\tau,L\) with \(m=1\).

The cited [Polychroniadou–Chan–Agrawal paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13415v1) represents order quantities using unit nodes. The proof above establishes the support-encoded result directly; its running time does not depend on the number of units represented by the capacities.
