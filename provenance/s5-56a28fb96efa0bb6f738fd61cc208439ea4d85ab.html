The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem (costly voting PJR under greedyMonroe) — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(C\), \(k\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over approval types \(A\subseteq C\) satisfying \(|C\setminus A|\ge k\), compute a mass-report plan \(\rho_{A,B}\) and quota-removal schedule such that every sufficiently large denominator-clearing lift with \(k\mid n\) is a costly-voting equilibrium of GreedyMonroe and every supported committee satisfies mass-PJR.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=|C|\) and \(1\le k<m\). A society is given by its support: the list \(\mathcal T\) of the \(\tau\) approval types \(A\subseteq C\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_A\) a binary rational; \(\sum_A\mu_A=1\) and \(|C\setminus A|\ge k\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Empty approval types are allowed.
A mass-report plan is a finite list of positive binary-rational entries \(\rho_{A,B}\), with omitted entries zero, \(B\subseteq A\), and \(\sum_B\rho_{A,B}=\mu_A\). Abstention is \(B=\emptyset\), but unilateral deviations may use any \(B\subseteq C\).
A sincere quota-removal schedule consists of distinct candidates \(c_1,\ldots,c_j\), \(0\le j\le k\), and rational removals \(x_{t,A}\ge0\): at step \(t\), remove total mass \(1/k\) from remaining types approving \(c_t\), without exceeding any remaining type mass. Stop only when \(j=k\) or every unelected candidate has remaining sincere support below \(1/k\). Write \(S=\{c_1,\ldots,c_j\}\).
For an integer \(n\) clearing all input, report-plan, and schedule denominators with \(k\mid n\), the lift has \(n\rho_{A,B}\) voters of true type \(A\) reporting \(B\). GreedyMonroe repeatedly chooses uniformly among unelected candidates with at least \(n/k\) remaining approvals, elects the chosen candidate, and removes a uniformly chosen subset of exactly \(n/k\) supporting ballots. When no candidate qualifies or \(k\) are elected, it fills remaining seats with a uniformly chosen subset of unelected candidates.
For true type \(A\), let \(\pi_A(r)\) be a committee lottery's probability of \(|W\cap A|=r\). Compare lotteries lexicographically through \(r=0,\ldots,k\), preferring the smaller probability at the first difference; identical vectors are indifferent. A costly-voting equilibrium admits no strictly beneficial unilateral ballot change, and every nonempty reporter strictly prefers participation to abstention. Voting cost is only this indifference refinement.
A committee \(W\) satisfies mass-PJR if, for every \(1\le\ell\le k\) and nonempty \(\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal T\), \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal U}\mu_A\ge\ell/k\) and \(|\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal U}A|\ge\ell\) imply \(|W\cap\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal U}A|\ge\ell\).
The required plan and schedule must ensure that every sufficiently large denominator-clearing lift is a costly-voting equilibrium, its committee lottery consists of \(S\) plus a uniformly chosen \((k-j)\)-subset of \(C\setminus S\), and every positive-probability committee satisfies mass-PJR.

Can such a mass-report plan and quota-removal schedule be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite societies with \(k\mid n\) and at least \(k\) disapproved candidates per voter, a costly-voting equilibrium satisfying PJR exists, with reports contained in true approvals and an outcome associated with a sincere first-stage execution ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09346)). This is an existence result, not a stated complexity classification.
No result settling this exact rational-mass construction problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one, retained the domain 1 ≤ k < |C|, and allowed empty approval types as the discrete model does.
  • Defined report plans as sparse rational mass allocations, allowing different individuals of one true type to submit different ballots.
  • Required reported ballots to be subsets of true approvals, following the anchor's proof rather than its reversed inclusion sign; deviations remain unrestricted approval ballots.
  • Interpreted the quota-removal schedule as a rational witness to one sincere first-stage execution, and retained the anchor's coincidence requirement through that execution's uniform completion lottery.
  • Defined denominator-clearing lifts using all input and output denominators, and quantified over every sufficiently large such lift with k dividing n.
  • Retained random candidate selection, uniform random supporting-ballot removal, uniform completion, and the upward-lexicographic lottery preference.
  • Specified costly voting as an abstention preference only when outcome lotteries are indifferent, rather than introducing a numerical participation cost.
  • Defined mass-PJR over nonempty collections of positive-mass types; this also captures violations by groups containing only part of a type's mass.

Result

The search problem is polynomial-time solvable. A greedy rational-mass schedule, followed by singleton reports for the mass removed at each step, satisfies all requirements.

The singleton-report construction is suggested by the finite existence proof in [Lee, Theorems 4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.09346). Below is a direct proof of its rational-mass implementation, polynomial encoding size, and validity for every sufficiently large denominator-clearing lift.

1. Constructing the schedule and report plan

Fix arbitrary orders of candidates and approval types. Initially, let the remaining mass of type \(A\) be \(R_A=\mu_A\).

Repeatedly perform the following operation, until \(k\) candidates have been selected:

  • Find an unelected candidate \(c\) with remaining sincere support \(\sum_{A:c\in A}R_A\ge 1/k\).
  • If no such candidate exists, stop.
  • Select \(c\). Traverse the types approving \(c\), removing available mass until exactly \(1/k\) has been removed.

More explicitly, begin the removal with an unmet quota \(h=1/k\). At each approving type \(A\), remove \(\min\{R_A,h\}\), decrease both \(R_A\) and \(h\) by that amount, and stop this traversal when \(h=0\). The support inequality guarantees that the quota can be met.

Write the selected candidates as \(c_1,\ldots,c_j\), their set as \(S\), and the removal from type \(A\) at step \(t\) as \(x_{t,A}\). This is a sincere quota-removal schedule: candidates are distinct, every removal uses approving types, each step removes exactly \(1/k\), and the stopping condition is precisely the required one.

Define the report plan by

\( \rho_{A,\{c_t\}}=x_{t,A} \)

for every positive removal, and by

\( \rho_{A,\emptyset}=\mu_A-\sum_{t=1}^{j}x_{t,A} \)

when this remainder is positive. Omit all zero entries.

Every report is contained in its true approval type, and the entries for each type sum to its input mass. In particular,

\( \sum_A\rho_{A,\{c_t\}}=1/k \)

for every selected candidate.

2. Every completion of the schedule satisfies mass-PJR

We prove the stronger statement that \(S\) itself already supplies the representation required by every cohesive collection of types.

Fix an integer \(1\le\ell\le k\) and a nonempty collection \(\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal T\) satisfying

\( \sum_{A\in\mathcal U}\mu_A\ge\ell/k \)

and

\( \left|\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal U}A\right|\ge\ell. \)

Let \(D=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal U}A\), and suppose, for a contradiction, that \(r=|S\cap D|<\ell\).

A step selecting a candidate outside \(D\) removes no mass from types in \(\mathcal U\): none of these types approves that candidate. Each step selecting a candidate in \(D\) removes at most \(1/k\) from these types, because that is the entire mass removed at the step. Consequently, their total remaining mass satisfies

\( \sum_{A\in\mathcal U}R_A\ge\sum_{A\in\mathcal U}\mu_A-r/k\ge(\ell-r)/k\ge1/k. \)

There are now two cases.

  • If \(j=k\), the schedule has removed total mass \(k(1/k)=1\). Since the initial total mass is one and remaining masses are nonnegative, all remaining masses are zero. This contradicts the displayed inequality.
  • If \(j<k\), some candidate \(d\in\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal U}A\) is outside \(S\). Indeed, this intersection contains at least \(\ell\) candidates, whereas \(S\) contains fewer than \(\ell\) candidates from its superset \(D\). Every remaining type in \(\mathcal U\) approves \(d\), so its remaining sincere support is at least \(1/k\). This contradicts the stopping condition.

Thus \( |S\cap D|\ge\ell \).

Every committee containing \(S\) therefore satisfies mass-PJR.

3. The lifted outcome lottery

Fix any denominator-clearing lift with \(k\mid n\) and \(n\ge2k\). Set \(q=n/k\), so \(q\ge2\).

Under the constructed plan, each candidate \(c\in S\) has a group \(G_c\) of exactly \(q\) voters reporting the singleton \(\{c\}\). These groups are disjoint. Every other voter abstains.

Initially, the qualifying candidates are exactly \(S\). Selecting \(c\in S\) removes all of \(G_c\), since it has exactly \(q\) supporting ballots. This does not affect any other group. Hence all candidates in \(S\) are selected in the quota stage, in some order, and no candidate outside \(S\) is selected there.

The resulting lottery is therefore exactly

\( W=S\cup F, \)

where \(F\) is a uniformly chosen \((k-j)\)-subset of \(C\setminus S\). Section 2 proves mass-PJR for every committee in this lottery.

4. Arbitrary unilateral deviations

For any set \(T\subseteq C\) with \(|T|\le k\), denote by \(L_T\) the lottery that contains \(T\) and fills the remaining seats uniformly from \(C\setminus T\).

We show that the lifted profile is a costly-voting equilibrium against all ballots \(B\subseteq C\).

4.1 Deviations by abstaining voters

Suppose an abstaining voter changes their ballot to an arbitrary \(B\).

Every candidate outside \(S\) has at most one supporting ballot, so cannot reach quota \(q\ge2\).

For each \(d\in S\), its original group \(G_d\) still consists of \(q\) unchanged singleton ballots. Those ballots cannot be removed by selecting another candidate. Thus every unelected candidate in \(S\) remains qualified until selected.

The quota-stage set remains exactly \(S\), and the committee lottery remains \(L_S\). Such a deviation gives no strict lottery improvement; participation also cannot improve the outcome under the stated cost refinement.

4.2 Deviations by singleton-reporting voters

Suppose voter \(v\), of true type \(A\), originally reports \(\{c\}\), where \(c\in S\cap A\). Let \(T=S\setminus\{c\}\).

After an arbitrary ballot change by \(v\):

  • Every candidate outside \(S\) has at most one supporting ballot, and cannot qualify.
  • Every candidate \(d\in T\) retains its \(q\) unchanged singleton supporters, whose ballots cannot be removed by another candidate.
  • Candidate \(c\) has \(q-1\) unchanged singleton supporters. It qualifies precisely while \(v\) remains present and reports approval of \(c\).

Therefore the quota-stage set is always either \(S\) or \(T\). All candidates in \(T\) must be selected: they remain qualified, and there are at most \(j\le k\) possible quota-stage winners.

Conditional on either quota-stage set, the uniform filling step gives the corresponding lottery. Thus every unilateral deviation induces a lottery of the form

\( (1-\lambda)L_S+\lambda L_T \)

for some \(0\le\lambda\le1\).

It remains to compare these lotteries according to the voter’s specified preferences.

Let \(a=|S\cap A|\) and \(f=k-j\). Since \(c\in A\), we have \(a\ge1\). Let \(d\) be the number of disapproved candidates outside \(S\). The hypothesis on disapproved candidates gives

\( d=|C\setminus A|-|S\setminus A|\ge k-(j-a)=f+a\ge f+1. \)

Every committee under \(L_S\) contains at least \(a\) approved candidates.

Every committee under \(L_T\) contains at least \(a-1\) approved candidates. Moreover, it contains exactly \(a-1\) with positive probability: the filling step can choose \(f+1\) disapproved candidates, because at least that many are available outside \(T\). Specifically,

\( \pi_A^{L_T}(a-1)=\binom{d}{f+1}/\binom{m-j+1}{f+1}>0. \)

For every \(r<a-1\), both lotteries assign probability zero to \(r\) approved candidates. At \(r=a-1\), \(L_S\) assigns probability zero, whereas any mixture with \(\lambda>0\) assigns positive probability.

Hence the voter strictly prefers \(L_S\) to every such mixture with \(\lambda>0\), by the prescribed lexicographic comparison. When \(\lambda=0\), the lottery is unchanged. No unilateral ballot change is strictly beneficial.

4.3 Strict preference for participation

If \(v\) abstains, candidate \(c\) has only \(q-1\) supporters and never qualifies. Every candidate in \(T\) has exactly its original quota of singleton supporters. Thus abstention induces exactly \(L_T\).

The preceding comparison proves that \(v\) strictly prefers \(L_S\) to \(L_T\). Every nonempty reporter therefore strictly prefers participation to abstention.

This establishes the costly-voting equilibrium requirement for every denominator-clearing lift with \(n\ge2k\).

5. Polynomial bit complexity

Write each input mass as \(\mu_A=p_A/d_A\), with numerator and denominator encoded in binary. Define

\( Q=k\prod_{A\in\mathcal T}d_A. \)

The bit length of \(Q\) is \(O(L+\log k)\). Every initial mass is an integer multiple of \(1/Q\), and the quota \(1/k\) is also an integer multiple of \(1/Q\).

Implement the schedule using integer units of \(1/Q\):

  • Initial type masses are the integers \(Q\mu_A\).
  • Each quota is the integer \(Q/k\).
  • Supports are sums of remaining integer masses.
  • Removals use integer minima and subtraction.

All masses and supports lie between zero and \(Q\), so every manipulated integer has \(O(L+\log k)\) bits. No denominator growth occurs.

There are at most \(k\) removal steps. Scanning candidate supports and allocating removals uses \(O(km\tau)\) integer operations. The schedule has at most \(k\tau\) removal entries, and the report plan has at most \((k+1)\tau\) positive entries. Their numerators and denominators all have polynomial bit length.

Thus the complete schedule and report plan can be computed and written in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\). Sections 2–4 verify every requested property.
