The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a symmetric compatibility graph on \(T\), and a strict ranking of compatible partner types for each type, consider an atomless population with mass \(\mu_t\) of type \(t\). Each agent can be paired with one compatible agent or left unmatched, with being unmatched ranked last. Decide whether there exists a measurable matching \(M\) such that for every feasible measurable matching \(N\), the mass of agents who prefer their partner’s type under \(N\) to their status under \(M\) is no greater than the mass who prefer their status under \(M\) to their partner’s type under \(N\).

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. The input also specifies a simple undirected compatibility graph \(G=(T,E)\) and, for each type \(t\), a strict order \(\succ_t\) on its neighbors, with unmatched status \(\bot\) strictly last. Let \(m=|E|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Represent the population by disjoint intervals \(X_t\) of Lebesgue measure \(\mu_t\), with union \(X\), measure \(\lambda\), and type map \(\theta\). A feasible matching is a measurable, measure-preserving involution \(M:X\to X\): fixed points are unmatched, and every nonfixed pair satisfies \(\{\theta(x),\theta(M(x))\}\in E\), almost everywhere. Write \(s_M(x)=\bot\) at fixed points and \(s_M(x)=\theta(M(x))\) otherwise. Agents are indifferent between partners of the same type.
Call \(M\) popular if every feasible matching \(N\) on the same population satisfies \(\lambda\{x:s_N(x)\succ_{\theta(x)}s_M(x)\}\leq\lambda\{x:s_M(x)\succ_{\theta(x)}s_N(x)\}\).

Is deciding whether this atomless society admits a popular matching solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents with strict neighbor rankings, popular-matching existence in arbitrary simple graphs is NP-complete ([Gupta–Misra–Saurabh–Zehavi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09370)).

Result

Deciding whether the society admits a popular matching is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete. The hardness holds even when every mass is dyadic and has $O(\log \tau)$ bits.

The reduction below uses the exact voting definition in the question, including unrestricted comparisons between agents’ old and new statuses.

1. A finite problem used in the reduction

We use the following established hardness result.

Constrained bipartite popularity problem. Given a finite bipartite graph $H$ with strict neighbor rankings and two specified, nonadjacent vertices $z,z'$, deciding whether there is a popular matching that leaves $z,z'$ unmatched and matches every other vertex is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard.

This is precisely the “desired popular matching” problem established by Kavitha’s reduction: the constructed bipartite instance admits such a matching if and only if the input positive $1$-in-$3$ formula is satisfiable. See Sections 4.1–4.2, Lemma 11 and Theorem 5 of [*The Popular Roommates problem*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00141). The two distinguished vertices arise by splitting one vertex and are nonadjacent.

Write

$$ R=V(H)\setminus\{z,z'\}, \qquad n=|R|. $$

All rankings in this finite source problem are strict, and unmatched status is last.

2. An exact certificate for atomless popularity

We first prove the certificate used throughout the reduction.

For each type $t$, let

$$ A_t=N_G(t)\cup\{\bot\}, $$

and define its vote by

$$ v_t(b,a)= \begin{cases} 1,&b\succ_t a,\\ 0,&b=a,\\ -1,&a\succ_t b. \end{cases} $$

For a feasible matching $M$, define its status masses

$$ p_{t,a}=\lambda\{x\in X_t:s_M(x)=a\}. $$

Involutivity and measure preservation imply

$$ p_{t,u}=p_{u,t} $$

on every edge. Thus allocations are described by

$$ P_\mu= \left\{ x\in\mathbb R_{\geq 0}^{E}: \sum_{u\in N_G(t)}x_{\{t,u\}}\leq \mu_t \text{ for every }t \right\}, $$

with

$$ p_{t,u}=x_{\{t,u\}}, \qquad p_{t,\bot}=\mu_t-\sum_u x_{\{t,u\}}. $$

Every such allocation is realizable: partition each type interval into its prescribed status pieces, pair corresponding endpoint pieces on each edge by inverse measure-preserving bijections, and fix unmatched pieces.

The measure-theoretic fact required here also applies to subdivisions of an existing matching’s status sets. If $A,B$ are measurable subsets of intervals with equal positive measure $a$, their restricted Lebesgue spaces are isomorphic modulo null sets. To see this, the cumulative function

$$ F_A(x)=\lambda(A\cap(-\infty,x]) $$

pushes restricted Lebesgue measure on $A$ to Lebesgue measure on $[0,a]$. It is injective after removing a null set: noninjectivity occurs on flat intervals, whose interiors intersect $A$ in measure zero, and their countably many endpoints. Its generalized inverse is measurable. Applying the same construction to $B$ gives the desired isomorphism on conull subsets. Exceptional null sets can be fixed.

2.1 The challenger program

For a challenger $N$, put

$$ c_{t,a,b} = \lambda\{x\in X_t:s_M(x)=a,\ s_N(x)=b\}. $$

These numbers satisfy

$$ c_{t,a,b}\geq 0, \qquad \sum_b c_{t,a,b}=p_{t,a}, $$

and, for each edge $\{t,u\}$,

$$ \sum_a c_{t,a,u} = \sum_a c_{u,a,t}. $$

Conversely, every array satisfying these conditions is realizable by a challenger on the same population. Subdivide each old-status set according to $c_{t,a,b}$, pair the unions assigned corresponding new matched statuses, and fix the unions assigned $\bot$.

Consequently, the maximum voting advantage of any challenger is the optimum $W(p)$ of

$$ \max_c\ \sum_{t,a,b}v_t(b,a)c_{t,a,b} $$

under the displayed constraints. The diagonal array is feasible and has objective zero, so

$$ W(p)\geq 0, \qquad M\text{ is popular}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ W(p)=0. $$

2.2 Certificate theorem

Theorem. A feasible allocation $p$ is popular if and only if there are numbers $h_{t,u}$ on directed compatibility edges satisfying

$$ h_{t,u}=-h_{u,t}, \qquad h_{t,\bot}=0, $$

and

$$ h_{t,b}-h_{t,a}\geq v_t(b,a) \quad \text{whenever }p_{t,a}>0,\quad b\in A_t. $$

Proof. Orient each compatibility edge. Assign unrestricted dual variables $\alpha_{t,a}$ to the row equations of the challenger program. Represent the balance-equation dual variables by antisymmetric $h_{t,u}$. The dual is

$$ \min_{\alpha,h}\ \sum_{t,a}p_{t,a}\alpha_{t,a}, $$

subject to

$$ \alpha_{t,a}+h_{t,b}\geq v_t(b,a). $$

The primal is nonempty and compact, since its variables are nonnegative and their sum is $1$. Linear-programming duality therefore supplies an attained dual optimum with the same value.

Taking $b=a$ gives

$$ \alpha_{t,a}+h_{t,a}\geq 0. $$

Edge balance and antisymmetry give

$$ \sum_{t,a}p_{t,a}h_{t,a}=0. $$

If $p$ is popular, an optimal dual solution has objective zero. Hence

$$ \sum_{t,a}p_{t,a}(\alpha_{t,a}+h_{t,a})=0. $$

All summands are nonnegative, so $\alpha_{t,a}=-h_{t,a}$ on every positive-status row. Substituting proves the certificate inequalities.

Conversely, given such $h$, set $\alpha_{t,a}=-h_{t,a}$ on positive-status rows. On zero-status rows set

$$ \alpha_{t,a} = \max_b\bigl(v_t(b,a)-h_{t,b}\bigr). $$

This is dual feasible and has objective zero. Weak duality and $W(p)\geq 0$ imply $W(p)=0$. $\square$

2.3 Consequences

We will repeatedly use four consequences.

First, each type has at most two positive statuses. If $a\succ_t b$ are both positive, the inequalities in both directions force

$$ h_{t,a}-h_{t,b}=1. $$

Three positive statuses $a\succ_t b\succ_t c$ would therefore require both

$$ h_{t,a}-h_{t,c}=2 \quad\text{and}\quad h_{t,a}-h_{t,c}=1. $$

Second, every positive matched edge has bounded certificate values:

$$ p_{t,u}>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -1\leq h_{t,u}\leq 1. $$

Indeed, comparison to $\bot$ gives $h_{t,u}\leq 1$, and the same argument at $u$, together with antisymmetry, gives the lower bound.

Third, adjacent types cannot both have positive unmatched mass. Their unmatched rows would require

$$ h_{t,u}\geq 1, \qquad h_{u,t}\geq 1, $$

contradicting antisymmetry.

Fourth, suppose $v$ has positive unmatched mass and is the top choice of a neighboring type $u$. Then $u$ is fully matched solely to $v$, and $v$ has no other positive matched status.

To prove this, the unmatched row at $v$ gives $h_{v,u}\geq 1$. If $p_{u,v}=0$, choose a positive status at $u$. If it is $\bot$, then $h_{u,v}\geq 1$. Otherwise it is a neighbor $a\neq v$, and

$$ h_{u,v}\geq h_{u,a}+1\geq 0. $$

Either possibility contradicts antisymmetry. Thus $p_{u,v}>0$, and boundedness forces

$$ h_{v,u}=1,\qquad h_{u,v}=-1. $$

A second positive matched status at $u$, necessarily worse than $v$, would have certificate value $-2$, which is impossible. A positive unmatched status at $u$ would instead require $h_{u,v}\geq 1$. Thus $u$ is fully matched solely to $v$. The two-status bound at $v$, counting its unmatched status, excludes every other positive matched status.

The certificate also shows that any feasible allocation whose positive statuses are contained in those of a popular allocation is itself popular.

3. Finite popularity on a bipartite graph transfers to uniform atomless types

We need the following special case, where the transfer is valid.

Lemma. Let $H$ be bipartite with strict rankings. Give every vertex a type of equal mass. If a finite matching $P$ is popular, the allocation assigning every type solely its status in $P$ is atomlessly popular.

Proof. Scale the common capacity to $1$. The fractional matching polytope is

$$ P_1(H)= \left\{ q\geq 0: \sum_{e\ni t}q_e\leq 1 \text{ for every }t \right\}. $$

Its vertices are integral. Here is a direct proof. At a nonintegral point, consider edges with values strictly between $0$ and $1$.

If these edges contain a cycle, that cycle is even because $H$ is bipartite. Alternately increasing and decreasing its edge values gives two distinct feasible perturbations whose midpoint is the original point.

Otherwise a nonempty component is a tree. Its leaves have slack capacity: each has exactly one incident fractional edge and cannot have an incident edge of value $1$. Alternately perturbing the path between two leaves again gives feasible perturbations in both directions. Thus a nonintegral point cannot be a vertex.

The polytope is compact, so every fractional matching is a convex combination of integral matchings.

Against the proposed incumbent, every agent of a type has the same old status. Therefore its voting margin against any challenger is a linear function of the challenger’s edge allocation and unmatched masses. This function is nonpositive on integral matchings by finite popularity, and hence on all of $P_1(H)$ by convexity. The incumbent is atomlessly popular. $\square$

In particular, Section 2 supplies an antisymmetric certificate for this allocation.

4. A gadget that forces complete matching of designated types

This is the main part of the reduction.

Let $R$ be $n$ external types of capacity $1$. Introduce four types $0,1,2,3$, induce a complete graph on them, and connect $0$ to every type in $R$. Give them capacities

$$ b_0=b_1=K,\qquad b_2=b_3=1, \qquad K>n+2. $$

Only $0$ has external neighbors. The rankings are

$$ \begin{aligned} 0 &: 1\succ 2\succ 3\succ \text{all external neighbors},\\ 1 &: 2\succ 3\succ 0,\\ 2 &: 3\succ 1\succ 0,\\ 3 &: 1\succ 2\succ 0. \end{aligned} $$

Their order among external neighbors at $0$ is arbitrary.

Gadget lemma. In every popular allocation,

$$ x_{\{0,1\}}=K,\qquad x_{\{2,3\}}=1, $$

and every other edge incident to the gadget has zero mass. Every external type in $R$ is fully matched. Moreover, every popularity certificate satisfies

$$ h_{0,1}=h_{2,3}=1. $$

Proof. Fix a popular allocation and a certificate.

4.1 Type $1$ is fully matched

Suppose $1$ has positive unmatched mass. Type $3$ ranks $1$ first, so Section 2.3 implies that $1$ has no matched status other than $3$. In particular, $x_{\{0,1\}}=0$.

Without the edge to $1$, type $0$ can match at most total mass $n+2<K$. Hence $0$ also has positive unmatched mass. This contradicts the fact that adjacent types $0,1$ cannot both have positive unmatched mass.

Thus $1$ is fully matched. Because its other neighbors have total capacity $2<K$,

$$ x_{\{0,1\}}>0. $$

4.2 Type $0$ is fully matched

Suppose $0$ has positive unmatched mass. Since its edge to $1$ is positive, the two-status bound says it matches only to $1$. Thus

$$ h_{0,1}=1. $$

Type $1$ is fully matched and receives less than $K$ from $0$, so it has exactly one additional positive status $q\in\{2,3\}$. As $q\succ_1 0$,

$$ h_{1,q}=0. $$

If $q=2$, then $h_{2,1}=0$. The unmatched row at $0$ implies

$$ h_{0,2}\geq 1,\qquad h_{2,0}\leq -1. $$

Type $2$ cannot have positive status $3$: that would force $h_{2,3}=1$, and comparison to $0$ would give $h_{2,0}\geq 0$. It cannot be unmatched, since a positive unmatched row would require $h_{2,1}\geq 1$. Therefore $2$ is fully matched solely to $1$.

Type $3$ then has no positive matched edge. But its unmatched row gives $h_{3,1}\geq 1$, while the positive status $0$ at type $1$ gives $h_{1,3}\geq 0$. This contradicts antisymmetry.

If $q=3$, then $h_{3,1}=0$, so $3$ cannot be unmatched. If $3$ also uses $2$, then

$$ h_{3,2}=-1,\qquad h_{2,3}=1. $$

Comparison to $0$ at type $2$ gives $h_{2,0}\geq 0$, contradicting $h_{2,0}\leq -1$. Hence $3$ is fully matched solely to $1$, leaving $2$ unmatched. Now

$$ h_{1,2}\geq h_{1,3}+1=1, \qquad h_{2,1}\geq 1, $$

again a contradiction.

Thus $0$ is fully matched.

4.3 The edge $\{0,1\}$ carries all mass $K$

Put

$$ \delta=K-x_{\{0,1\}}. $$

Suppose $\delta>0$. Since $0$ and $1$ are fully matched and each has at most two positive statuses, each has exactly one additional positive neighbor. Write these as

$$ r\in\{2,3\}\cup R \quad\text{at }0, \qquad q\in\{2,3\} \quad\text{at }1. $$

Set $H=h_{0,1}$. Their two positive statuses force

$$ h_{0,r}=H-1, \qquad h_{1,q}=1-H. $$

Boundedness on positive edges gives

$$ 0\leq H\leq 1. $$

We consider all possibilities for $q,r$.

Case $q=3$, $r=2$. Type $2$ uses $0$, so $h_{2,0}=1-H$. Since $1\succ_2 0$,

$$ h_{2,1}\geq 2-H. $$

Since $2\succ_1 3$,

$$ h_{1,2}\geq 2-H. $$

Their sum is positive, contradicting antisymmetry.

Case $q=3$, $r\neq 2$. Type $2$ can match only to $3$. It cannot have positive unmatched mass, since that would give

$$ h_{2,1}\geq 1, \qquad h_{1,2}\geq 2-H\geq 1. $$

Thus $2$ is fully matched to $3$. If $r=3$, type $3$ has three positive matched statuses $0,1,2$, which is impossible. Otherwise its positive statuses are $1,2$. Their order gives

$$ h_{3,1}=H-1, \qquad h_{3,2}=H-2. $$

Boundedness forces $H=1$. Hence $h_{2,3}=1$, and comparison to $1$ at type $2$ gives $h_{2,1}\geq 0$. But $h_{1,2}\geq 1$, again impossible.

Case $q=2$, $r=2$. At type $2$, both $1$ and $0$ are positive, and $1\succ_2 0$. Therefore

$$ h_{2,1}-h_{2,0}=1. $$

Substitution gives

$$ (H-1)-(1-H)=1, $$

so $H=3/2$, contrary to $H\leq 1$.

Case $q=2$, $r=3$. Type $3$ uses $0$, so $h_{3,0}=1-H$. Because $1\succ_3 0$,

$$ h_{3,1}\geq 2-H. $$

At type $1$, comparison from its positive status $0$ to $3$ gives

$$ h_{1,3}\geq 1-H. $$

Thus their sum is at least $3-2H\geq 1$, a contradiction.

Case $q=2$, $r\in R$. Type $3$ can match only to $2$. Positive unmatched mass at $3$ would contradict

$$ h_{3,1}\geq 1, \qquad h_{1,3}\geq 1-H\geq 0. $$

Hence $3$ is fully matched to $2$. Type $2$ uses both $1$ and $3$, with $3\succ_2 1$, so

$$ h_{2,3}=H,\qquad h_{3,2}=-H. $$

The edge $\{1,3\}$ requires

$$ h_{1,3}\geq 1-H, \qquad h_{3,1}\geq 1-H. $$

Consequently $H=1$. Since $0$ ranks $2$ above its external positive status $r$,

$$ h_{0,2}\geq h_{0,r}+1=1. $$

Since $2$ ranks $3$ above $0$,

$$ h_{2,0}\geq h_{2,3}-1=0. $$

This contradicts antisymmetry.

All cases are impossible. Thus $\delta=0$ and $x_{\{0,1\}}=K$.

Types $2,3$ can now match only to each other. Their equal capacities imply equal unmatched masses; these must be zero because they are adjacent. Hence $x_{\{2,3\}}=1$.

4.4 Certificate values and external saturation

Write $J=h_{2,3}$. The unused edge $\{1,3\}$ joins two types that prefer each other to their allocated statuses. Thus

$$ h_{1,3}\geq 1-H, \qquad h_{3,1}\geq 1-J. $$

Antisymmetry implies $H+J\geq 2$. Since both values are at most $1$,

$$ H=J=1. $$

For any external type $v$, comparison from type $0$’s positive status $1$ to $v$ gives

$$ h_{0,v}\geq 0. $$

If $v$ had positive unmatched mass, its unmatched row would give $h_{v,0}\geq 1$, contradicting antisymmetry. Hence every external type is fully matched. $\square$

5. The reduction

Start with an instance $H,z,z'$ of the constrained bipartite problem. Set

$$ K=n+3. $$

Use the following unnormalized capacities:

  • each type in $R$ has capacity $1$;
  • $z,z'$ each have capacity $K$;
  • introduce private leaves $a_z,a_{z'}$, each of capacity $1$, adjacent only to its corresponding distinguished type;
  • introduce the four-type gadget of Section 4, with capacities $K,K,1,1$, and connect its type $0$ to every type in $R$.

Retain every edge of $H$. At each $v\in R$, append $0$ as its last neighbor. At $z$ and $z'$, prepend the corresponding private leaf as the first neighbor. Preserve all original relative rankings.

The total capacity is

$$ C=4K+n+4. $$

Choose the smallest power of two $Q\geq C$ and divide all capacities by $Q$. If $Q>C$, add one isolated type of mass $(Q-C)/Q$. These are positive dyadic masses summing to $1$.

Below, allocation amounts are expressed in units of $1/Q$. This rescaling changes neither positive-status sets nor certificate inequalities.

5.1 Distinguished types cannot use source edges

Consider any popular allocation in the constructed society. The source neighbors of $z$ have total capacity at most $n$, and its private leaf has capacity $1$. Since $z,z'$ are nonadjacent,

$$ n+1<K $$

shows that $z$ must have positive unmatched mass.

Its private leaf cannot also have unmatched mass, because they are adjacent. Thus the leaf is fully matched to $z$, and their edge has mass $1$.

Type $z$ already has two positive statuses: its private leaf and $\bot$. The two-status bound therefore forbids every positive source edge at $z$. The same argument applies to $z'$.

5.2 A source solution gives an atomless popular matching

Suppose $H$ has a finite popular matching $P$ matching exactly $R$.

By Section 3, its uniform atomless allocation has a certificate $h$ on $H$. In particular,

$$ -1\leq h_{v,P(v)}\leq 1 \quad(v\in R), $$

and, because $z,z'$ are unmatched,

$$ h_{z,v}\geq 1, \qquad h_{z',v}\geq 1 $$

on their source edges.

Construct the target allocation by assigning:

$$ x_e=1\quad(e\in P), $$

$$ x_{\{z,a_z\}}=x_{\{z',a_{z'}\}}=1, $$

and

$$ x_{\{0,1\}}=K,\qquad x_{\{2,3\}}=1. $$

All other edges have mass zero. Distinguished types have unmatched mass $K-1$, and any isolated padding type is unmatched.

Extend the source certificate as follows:

$$ h_{z,a_z}=h_{z',a_{z'}}=1, $$

$$ h_{0,1}=h_{2,3}=1, $$

and set $h=0$ on every other gadget edge and every edge $\{0,v\}$ with $v\in R$. Reverse directed values are their negatives.

We verify all new inequalities.

At $z$, the positive statuses are its private leaf and $\bot$. The unmatched-row inequalities on source neighbors already hold. Since the private leaf ranks above every source neighbor,

$$ h_{z,v}-h_{z,a_z}\geq 0\geq -1. $$

Comparison from the private leaf to $\bot$ gives equality $-1$. At the leaf, its sole positive matched value is $-1$, and comparison to $\bot$ satisfies the required inequality. The same checks apply at $z'$.

Inside the four-type gadget, the positive directed values at types $0,1,2,3$ are respectively

$$ 1,\quad -1,\quad 1,\quad -1. $$

All unused internal values are zero. The displayed rankings therefore give differences at least $1$ for every preferred alternative and at least $-1$ for every worse alternative.

At $0$, an external neighbor is worse than its allocated status $1$, and its certificate difference is $-1$. At $v\in R$, the new neighbor $0$ ranks below $P(v)$, and

$$ h_{v,0}-h_{v,P(v)} = -h_{v,P(v)} \geq -1. $$

Thus the extended certificate satisfies every inequality. Section 2 proves that the allocation is popular for the exact atomless voting definition.

5.3 An atomless popular matching gives a source solution

Conversely, suppose the constructed society has a popular allocation and certificate $h$.

Section 5.1 forbids source-edge allocations at $z,z'$. The gadget lemma forces its two internal pairs and makes every type in $R$ fully matched. No type in $R$ matches to the gadget.

Therefore the positive source edges on $R$ form a fractional perfect matching with unit capacities in the bipartite graph $H[R]$.

Let $A,B$ be its bipartition. Summing the fractional allocations on both sides gives $|A|=|B|$. For any $S\subseteq A$,

$$ |S| = \sum_{a\in S}\sum_{b\in B}x_{\{a,b\}} \leq |N(S)|, $$

where neighbors are taken in the graph of positive edges. Hall’s theorem supplies a perfect matching $P$ on $R$ using only positive edges. Leave $z,z'$ unmatched.

The target certificate, restricted to source edges, satisfies

$$ h_{t,b}-h_{t,P(t)}\geq v_t(b,P(t)) $$

for every source vertex $t$ and source status $b$. For $t\in R$, this holds because the selected status was positive in the atomless allocation. For $z,z'$, it holds because their unmatched statuses were positive.

Now take any finite source matching $N$. Sum these inequalities with $b=N(t)$:

$$ \sum_t v_t(N(t),P(t)) \leq \sum_t h_{t,N(t)} - \sum_t h_{t,P(t)} =0. $$

Each final sum is zero: matched edges cancel by antisymmetry, and unmatched statuses have value zero. Thus $P$ is a finite popular matching of $H$, matching exactly $R$.

This proves both directions of the reduction.

6. Complexity and membership in $\mathrm{NP}$

If the source has $m_H$ edges, the constructed instance has

$$ \tau\in\{n+8,n+9\}, \qquad m=m_H+n+8. $$

Since $K=n+3$ and $Q$ is the next power of two above $5n+16$, each mass has $O(\log(n+2))$ bits. The construction and its output bit length are polynomial in the source size. This establishes $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.

For membership in $\mathrm{NP}$, guess for every type a nonempty positive-status set

$$ S_t\subseteq A_t,\qquad |S_t|\leq 2. $$

This takes $O(\tau\log(\tau+1))$ bits. Verify it using two rational linear programs.

First, test allocation feasibility with exactly those positive statuses. Introduce $x\in P_\mu$ and $0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1$, impose

$$ p_{t,a}=0\quad(a\notin S_t), \qquad p_{t,a}\geq\varepsilon\quad(a\in S_t), $$

and maximize $\varepsilon$. The requested support is feasible exactly when the program is feasible and its optimum is positive.

Second, test feasibility of the certificate inequalities with $a\in S_t$. There are $m$ independent certificate variables and at most

$$ \sum_t |S_t|(d_t+1)\leq 4m+2\tau $$

inequalities.

Both programs have encoding length polynomial in $m,\tau,L$ and can be solved, including exact comparison of the first optimum with zero, in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time. The certificate theorem and two-status bound prove that this verifier accepts exactly the yes-instances.

Hence the decision problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete. A polynomial-time algorithm in $m,\tau,L$ would therefore imply $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.
