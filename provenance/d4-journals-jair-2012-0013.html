The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an agenda \(\Phi\), explicitly represented complete consistent judgment types \(T\subseteq\mathcal J(\Phi)\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), and \(L\subseteq\Phi\), does some \(K\in\mathcal J(\Phi)\) with \(L\subseteq K\) minimize \(D_\mu(K)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t H(K,t)\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An agenda \(\Phi\) is a finite nonempty set of propositional formulas, closed under syntactic complementation and containing no doubly negated formulas; tautologies and contradictions are allowed. Let \(\mathcal J(\Phi)\) be its complete, logically consistent judgment sets. Write \(\Phi^+\) for the formulas in \(\Phi\) without an outer negation, and let \(H(J,J')=\sum_{\varphi\in\Phi^+}|\mathbf 1_{\varphi\in J}-\mathbf 1_{\varphi\in J'}|\).
Given explicitly listed types \(T\subseteq\mathcal J(\Phi)\), binary-encoded rational masses \(\mu_t\geq 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), and \(L\subseteq\Phi\), ask whether some \(K\in\mathcal J(\Phi)\) containing \(L\) minimizes \(D_\mu(K)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tH(K,t)\) over all \(\mathcal J(\Phi)\).

Is this rational-mass winner determination problem in \(P\), or is it \(NP\)-hard?

For a finite list of agents with equal weight, the corresponding winner determination problem is \(\Theta_2^p\)-complete. No separate complexity result for the exact rational-mass formulation was verified.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

Proven result

The rational-mass winner determination problem is NP-hard, even with exactly two types, both of mass \(1/2\), and an agenda containing only three complementary pairs.

We prove this by a polynomial-time many-one reduction from \(3\)-SAT. The proof does not require the stated complexity result for equal-weight agents.

Construction

Let \(F\) be a \(3\)-CNF formula. Choose fresh propositional variables \(p,q\) not occurring in \(F\), and define \(g=p\lor(q\land F)\).

Construct the agenda
\[ \Phi=\{p,\neg p,q,\neg q,g,\neg g\}. \]
Here and below, the displayed expression is equivalently \( \Phi=\{p,\neg p,q,\neg q,g,\neg g\} \).

This agenda is finite, nonempty, and closed under syntactic complementation. Its three formulas without outer negation are \(p,q,g\), and none of its formulas is doubly negated.

Take the two types \(t_+=\{p,q,g\}\) and \(t_-=\{\neg p,\neg q,\neg g\}\), with masses \(\mu_{t_+}=\mu_{t_-}=1/2\). Both are complete and logically consistent:

  • Setting \(p=q=\mathrm{true}\) satisfies every formula in \(t_+\), regardless of the values of the variables in \(F\).
  • Setting \(p=q=\mathrm{false}\) makes \(g\) false and thus satisfies every formula in \(t_-\), again regardless of \(F\).

Finally, set \(L=\{\neg p,q,g\}\).

The construction has size linear in the size of \(F\), and the rational masses have constant-size binary encodings.

Every consistent judgment set minimizes the objective

Let \(K\in\mathcal J(\Phi)\). For each \(\varphi\in\Phi^+=\{p,q,g\}\), write \(b_\varphi=\mathbf 1_{\varphi\in K}\). The two types take opposite positions on every such formula. Consequently, that formula contributes

\( \frac12|b_\varphi-1|+\frac12|b_\varphi-0|=\frac12 \)

to \(D_\mu(K)\), because \(b_\varphi\in\{0,1\}\).

Summing over the three formulas gives \(D_\mu(K)=3/2\). Thus every member of \(\mathcal J(\Phi)\) is a global minimizer.

Correctness of the reduction

Suppose first that \(F\) is satisfiable. Extend a satisfying valuation by setting \(p=\mathrm{false}\) and \(q=\mathrm{true}\). Then \(g=p\lor(q\land F)\) is true. The judgment set induced by this valuation is complete, logically consistent, and contains \(L\). By the preceding calculation, it minimizes \(D_\mu\).

Conversely, suppose some minimizing \(K\in\mathcal J(\Phi)\) contains \(L\). Logical consistency supplies a valuation satisfying every formula in \(K\), hence satisfying \(\neg p,q,g\). Under \(p=\mathrm{false}\) and \(q=\mathrm{true}\), the formula \(g=p\lor(q\land F)\) has exactly the truth value of \(F\). Therefore that valuation satisfies \(F\).

We have proved that \(F\) is satisfiable if and only if the constructed winner determination instance has answer yes. This establishes NP-hardness. In particular, a polynomial-time algorithm for the rational-mass problem would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
