The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite catalogue of undirected FEN module types \(H\), each with \(|V(H)|\le q\), fixed coalition-size bound \(c\), and rational agent masses \(\lambda_H\) summing to \(1\), what is the minimum normalized relation-edit mass needed to give every module copy a perfect coalition partition? Equivalently, distinguish \(\mathrm{OPT}=0\) from \(\mathrm{OPT}>\epsilon\), where \(\mathrm{OPT}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{H,a}x_{H,a}\frac{|R_a|}{|V(H)|}\), \(a\) ranges over valid local repair-and-partition plans, and \(\sum_a x_{H,a}=\lambda_H\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) module types with positive binary-rational agent masses \(\lambda_H\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
Each type is an explicitly given nonempty simple undirected graph \(H=(V_H,F_H\cup E_H)\), with disjoint friend and enemy edge sets; absent pairs are neutral. Assume \(1\le |V_H|\le q\) and maximum degree at most a fixed positive integer \(d\). Fix a positive integer coalition bound \(c\), and put \(m=\sum_H|V_H|\).
Module copies are independent: edits and coalitions remain within a copy. A valid local plan \(a\) consists of a repaired graph \(H_a=(V_H,F_a\cup E_a)\) of maximum degree at most \(d\), and a partition into blocks of size at most \(c\), each containing every friend and no enemy of each of its members in \(H_a\). This is the perfection convention used here.
Its edit count is \(|R_a|=|F_H\triangle F_a|+|E_H\triangle E_a|\): inserting or deleting an undirected labelled edge costs one, and changing its label costs two. Plans are implicit, not supplied as an input list.
Assign nonnegative real agent masses \(x_{H,a}\) to plans, with \(\sum_a x_{H,a}=\lambda_H\), and define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\min_x\frac1d\sum_{H,a}x_{H,a}\frac{|R_a|}{|V_H|}\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including a nonnegative rational threshold \(B\).

Is deciding whether \(\mathrm{OPT}\le B\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, for fixed \(c,d\) and variable \(q\)? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,q\), with dependence on \(c,d\) explicit.

For finite undirected bounded-degree FEN games, existence of a perfect partition with constant coalition bound has a randomized one-sided property tester using constantly many queries for fixed distance tolerance, degree bound, and coalition bound. Complete classification

For every fixed pair of positive integers $c,d$, the decision problem has the following classification:

| Fixed bounds | Complexity |
|---|---|
| $c\le 2$ or $d\le 2$ | $\mathrm{P}$ |
| $c\ge 3$ and $d\ge 3$ | $\mathrm{NP}$-complete |

Hardness holds with $\tau=1$, mass $1$, no enemy edges, and maximum degree at most $3$. Thus the previously unresolved range $c\ge4,d\ge3$ is also settled.

The proof uses a transformation preserving partitions into connected sets of exactly $c$ vertices. It reduces the degree to $3$ and then makes the girth greater than $c$. On these graphs, a suitable repair threshold forces every coalition to be connected and have exactly $c$ vertices.

1. Eliminating repairs and mixing

For a listed type $H$, write $n_H=|V_H|$. Given a partition $\mathcal P$ into blocks of size at most $c$, define
$$ \delta_H(\mathcal P) = |\{uv\in F_H:u,v\text{ are in different blocks}\}| + |\{uv\in E_H:u,v\text{ are in the same block}\}|. $$

Lemma 1. The minimum edit count of a valid plan using $\mathcal P$ is $\delta_H(\mathcal P)$.

Proof. Every original friend edge crossing blocks must lose its friend label. Every original enemy edge inside a block must lose its enemy label. Each such edge requires at least one edit under the specified symmetric-difference cost, and these requirements concern distinct edges.

Conversely, delete exactly these edges. Every remaining friend edge is internal, and every remaining enemy edge crosses blocks, so the partition is perfect. Deletion preserves the maximum-degree bound. This construction uses exactly $\delta_H(\mathcal P)$ edits. $\square$

Define
$$ r_H=\min_{\mathcal P:\,|S|\le c\text{ for every }S\in\mathcal P} \delta_H(\mathcal P). $$
The minimum exists because there are finitely many partitions.

Lemma 2. The distributional optimum is
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=\frac1d\sum_H\lambda_H\frac{r_H}{n_H}. $$

Proof. Every plan for $H$ costs at least $r_H$. Consequently,
$$ \sum_a x_{H,a}\frac{|R_a|}{n_H} \ge \frac{r_H}{n_H}\sum_a x_{H,a} = \lambda_H\frac{r_H}{n_H}. $$
Assigning all the mass $\lambda_H$ to a minimizing plan attains this bound for each type simultaneously. $\square$

For a vertex set $S$, let
$$ w_H(S)=|F_H[S]|-|E_H[S]|. $$
Counting internal and crossing edges gives
$$ \delta_H(\mathcal P) = |F_H|-\sum_{S\in\mathcal P}w_H(S). $$

Lemma 3. An optimal partition can be chosen with every block connected in the original friend graph.

Proof. Split each block into the connected components of its induced friend graph. No friend edge becomes crossing, because no friend edge joins distinct components. Internal enemy edges can become crossing, which only decreases the cost. Block sizes do not increase. $\square$

In particular, different friend components can be optimized independently.

2. Membership in $\mathrm{NP}$ and rational arithmetic

A certificate supplies one partition for each listed type, using
$$ O(m\log(q+1)) $$
bits. The verifier checks block sizes, calculates $\delta_H(\mathcal P_H)$, and compares
$$ \sum_H\lambda_H\frac{\delta_H(\mathcal P_H)}{n_H} \le dB. $$

An accepted certificate gives feasible plans by Lemma 1. Every yes-instance has an accepted certificate by Lemma 2. Thus the problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$ for every fixed $c,d$.

For numerical running times, put
$$ K= L+\tau\left\lceil\log_2(q+1)\right\rceil +\left\lceil\log_2(d+1)\right\rceil+10. $$
A common denominator for the sum can be obtained by multiplying the input mass denominators and the integers $n_H$. Its bit length is $O(K)$. Moreover,
$$ 0\le\delta_H(\mathcal P_H) \le |F_H|+|E_H| \le \frac{dn_H}{2}, $$
so the nonnegative partial sums are at most $d/2$. Their numerators also have $O(K)$ bits. Including the final comparison with $dB$, schoolbook arithmetic takes
$$ O(\tau K^2) $$
bit operations.

3. Preserving connected partitions while reducing degree

A connected $c$-partition of an unlabelled graph is a vertex partition into sets of exactly $c$ vertices, each inducing a connected graph.

3.1. A splitting lemma

Let $G$ be a finite simple graph, let $v\in V(G)$, and partition its neighbourhood into disjoint sets $A,B$, where $B$ is nonempty and $A$ may be empty.

Construct $G'$ as follows:

  • Add new vertices $z_1,\ldots,z_{2c}$ and the path

$$ v,z_1,\ldots,z_{2c}. $$

  • For every $b\in B$, replace the edge $vb$ by $z_{2c}b$.
  • Leave all other edges unchanged.

Lemma 4. The graph $G$ has a connected $c$-partition if and only if $G'$ does.

Proof, forward direction. Suppose $G$ has such a partition. Let $S$ be its block containing $v$.

Every component of $G[S\setminus\{v\}]$ has a neighbour of $v$: otherwise $G[S]$ would be disconnected. Assign a component to side $A$ if it contains a neighbour in $A$, and assign it to side $B$ otherwise. Every component assigned to side $B$ contains a neighbour in $B$.

Let $S_A$ consist of $v$ and the components assigned to side $A$, and let $S_B$ consist of the components assigned to side $B$. Write
$$ a=|S_A|,\qquad b=|S_B|. $$
Then $a+b=c$, $a\ge1$, and $0\le b\le c-1$.

Replace $S$ by the following three blocks:
$$ S_A\cup\{z_1,\ldots,z_b\}, $$
$$ \{z_{b+1},\ldots,z_{b+c}\}, $$
$$ S_B\cup\{z_{b+c+1},\ldots,z_{2c}\}. $$
Each has exactly $c$ vertices.

The first block is connected through $v$: each of its original components retains an edge to $v$ through $A$. The middle block is a path. The last block is connected through $z_{2c}$, because every component in $S_B$ has a neighbour in $B$. If $b=0$, the last block is simply a path of $c$ vertices.

All other original blocks remain connected, because the only deleted edges were incident to $v$, which belongs to $S$. This gives a connected $c$-partition of $G'$.

Proof, reverse direction. Suppose $G'$ has a connected $c$-partition. Put
$$ W=\{z_1,\ldots,z_{2c}\}. $$
Only $v$ and $z_{2c}$ provide connections between the new path and original vertices.

Let $P_0$ be the block containing $v$, and let $P_1$ be the block containing $z_{2c}$.

First suppose $P_0=P_1$. This block cannot contain the whole new path, since that path has $2c$ new vertices. Its intersection with $W$ therefore consists of a prefix of length $p$ and a suffix of length $t$, where $t\ge1$. Any omitted interval between them cannot belong to this block, because it would have no connection within the block to either endpoint.

Every other block meeting $W$ lies entirely in $W$ and is a path interval of exactly $c$ vertices. Hence
$$ 2c-p-t\equiv0\pmod c. $$
But the common endpoint block contains the original vertex $v$, so
$$ 1\le p+t\le c-1. $$
These statements contradict divisibility by $c$. Thus $P_0\ne P_1$.

Now $P_0\cap W$ is a prefix of length $p$, possibly zero, and $P_1\cap W$ is a suffix of length $t\ge1$. Every other block meeting $W$ is entirely a path interval of size $c$. Therefore
$$ p+t\equiv0\pmod c. $$
Because $P_0$ contains $v$,
$$ 0\le p\le c-1,\qquad 1\le t\le c. $$
It follows that
$$ p+t=c. $$

Define
$$ Q=(P_0\cup P_1)\setminus W. $$
Since the endpoint blocks are distinct and each has size $c$,
$$ |Q|=2c-(p+t)=c. $$

The original vertices in $P_0$ are connected to $v$ using original vertices: the new prefix is a pendant path within this block and cannot connect two original vertices.

Every original vertex in $P_1$ has a path within $P_1$ to $z_{2c}$. Before first reaching a new vertex, such a path uses only original vertices, and its final edge is $bz_{2c}$ for some $b\in B$. Restoring $vb$ therefore connects that vertex to $v$ in $G[Q]$. Thus $G[Q]$ is connected.

Replace $P_0,P_1$ by $Q$, discard all blocks lying entirely in $W$, and retain all other blocks. Those retained blocks contain neither $v$ nor new vertices, so their internal edges were unchanged. This gives a connected $c$-partition of $G$. $\square$

3.2. Degree reduction and subdivision

We use the following published theorem: for every fixed integer $c\ge3$, deciding whether a simple planar bipartite graph has a connected $c$-partition is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete. This is Theorem 2.1(a) of [Dyer and Frieze, *On the complexity of partitioning graphs into connected subgraphs*](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~af1p/Texfiles/COMPLEXPART.pdf).

We may restrict source instances to positive vertex counts divisible by $c$. Empty instances and instances with other vertex counts have immediate answers and can be replaced by fixed positive-order yes- or no-instances whose orders are divisible by $c$.

Let a source graph have $N_0$ vertices and $M_0$ edges.

Whenever a vertex has degree $r\ge4$, apply Lemma 4 with $|B|=2$. Its degree decreases to $r-1$, the new endpoint has degree $3$, and every other new vertex has degree $2$. The quantity
$$ \sum_v\max\{0,\deg(v)-3\} $$
decreases by exactly one. Thus at most
$$ s\le2M_0 $$
operations produce a graph $G_1$ of maximum degree at most $3$. Its sizes are
$$ N_1=N_0+2cs,\qquad M_1=M_0+2cs. $$
Every operation preserves connected $c$-partition existence.

Next, replace each edge of $G_1$ by a path with $2c$ new internal vertices. This is precisely the operation of Lemma 4 with $B$ consisting of the other endpoint of the chosen edge. Consequently, it also preserves connected $c$-partition existence and maximum degree.

Call the resulting graph $G$. Every cycle in $G$ traverses a complete replacement path, so its length exceeds $c$. Thus $G$ has girth greater than $c$, taking the girth of a forest to be infinite.

Its number of vertices is
$$ n=N_1+2cM_1 \le N_0+(8c^2+6c)M_0. $$
The construction takes $O(c^2(N_0+M_0))$ graph operations. Vertex-count divisibility by $c$ is preserved throughout.

We have therefore proved:

Proposition 5. For every fixed $c\ge3$, connected $c$-partition is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard on simple graphs of maximum degree at most $3$ and girth greater than $c$.

4. Hardness of the repair problem for every $c\ge3,d\ge3$

Fix $c\ge3$ and $d\ge3$. Apply Proposition 5 to obtain a graph $G=(V,A)$ with
$$ n=|V|>0,\qquad c\mid n,\qquad e=|A|. $$

Since $G$ has girth greater than $c$, every induced subgraph on at most $c$ vertices is a forest. For any nonempty set $S$ with $|S|\le c$,
$$ |A[S]| \le |S|-1 \le \frac{c-1}{c}|S|. $$
Equality throughout holds exactly when $|S|=c$ and $G[S]$ is connected.

It follows that every partition $\mathcal P$ into blocks of size at most $c$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{S\in\mathcal P}|A[S]| \le \frac{c-1}{c}n, $$
with equality exactly for connected $c$-partitions.

If
$$ e<\frac{c-1}{c}n, $$
then $G$ cannot have a connected $c$-partition. Output a fixed negative repair instance: one friend path on $c+1$ vertices, mass $1$, and threshold $0$. Zero edits would force this whole connected friend graph into one coalition, which is impossible.

Otherwise, construct one module with
$$ H=(V,F_H=A,E_H=\varnothing),\qquad \lambda_H=1,\qquad q=n, $$
and set
$$ B=\frac{e-(c-1)n/c}{dn}. $$
This is a nonnegative rational of polynomial encoding length. The module is nonempty, simple, and has degree at most $3$, hence at most $d$.

By Lemmas 1 and 2,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\le B \iff r_H\le e-\frac{c-1}{c}n \iff \max_{\mathcal P}\sum_{S\in\mathcal P}|A[S]| \ge\frac{c-1}{c}n. $$
The upper bound and its equality characterization show that this holds exactly when $G$ has a connected $c$-partition.

This is a polynomial reduction for each fixed $c,d$. Together with membership in $\mathrm{NP}$, it proves $\mathrm{NP}$-completeness for every fixed $c\ge3,d\ge3$.

5. Polynomial cases and running times

Below, $\widetilde O$ suppresses logarithmic factors in input and graph sizes. Dependence on $c,d$ is displayed. Exact rational aggregation contributes $O(\tau K^2)$ bit operations.

5.1. Coalition bound $c=1$

Every friend edge must be deleted; enemy edges can remain. Hence
$$ r_H=|F_H|. $$
The total running time is
$$ \widetilde O(L+dm+\tau K^2). $$

5.2. Coalition bound $c=2$

A singleton has score zero. A pair has score $1$ for a friend edge, $-1$ for an enemy edge, and $0$ for a neutral pair.

Replacing a nonfriend pair by singletons never decreases the score. The remaining pairs form a matching in the friend graph. Therefore
$$ r_H=|F_H|-\nu(V_H,F_H), $$
where $\nu$ denotes maximum matching size.

A standard deterministic general-graph maximum matching algorithm takes $O(n_H^3)$ time per type; see the algorithmic background in [Gabow, *Data Structures for Weighted Matching and Extensions to $b$-Matching and $f$-Factors*](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/j-chen3/courses/669/2024/reading/Gabow2018.pdf). The friend graphs satisfy its finite undirected graph hypotheses.

The total running time is
$$ \widetilde O\left( L+dm+\sum_H n_H^3+\tau K^2 \right). $$
Using $n_H\le q$ gives both bounds
$$ \widetilde O(L+dm+mq^2+\tau K^2) $$
and
$$ \widetilde O(L+dm+\tau q^3+\tau K^2). $$

5.3. Degree bound $d\le2$

Every friend component is an isolated vertex, a path, or a cycle. Lemma 3 permits independent optimization of these components.

Consider a friend-path component with $s$ vertices. An internal path vertex already has degree $2$ in the friend graph, so it has no incident enemy edge. Consequently, the only possible enemy edge internal to this component joins its endpoints. Let $\varepsilon$ indicate whether this edge exists; for an isolated vertex, set $\varepsilon=0$.

The component’s optimum is
$$ \max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{s}{c}\right\rceil-1,\varepsilon\right\}. $$

Indeed, connected blocks are path intervals. At least $\lceil s/c\rceil$ intervals are required, cutting at least $\lceil s/c\rceil-1$ friend edges.

If $s>c$, consecutive intervals attain this bound and separate the endpoints, avoiding the enemy penalty. If $s\le c$ and $\varepsilon=0$, retaining the whole component costs zero. If $s\le c$ and $\varepsilon=1$, zero edits are impossible because the friend path forces the enemy endpoints together; deleting that enemy edge attains cost one.

A friend-cycle component has no incident enemy edges. Its optimum is
$$ \begin{cases} 0,&s\le c,\\ \left\lceil s/c\right\rceil,&s>c. \end{cases} $$
For $s>c$, every connected block is a proper cyclic interval. A partition into $k$ such intervals cuts exactly $k$ cycle edges, and $k\ge\lceil s/c\rceil$. Consecutive intervals attain equality.

Summing these formulas computes all local optima in linear graph time, independently of the magnitude of $c$. The total running time is
$$ \widetilde O(L+m+\tau K^2). $$

6. An exact algorithm for arbitrary bounds

The following algorithm processes only the $\tau$ listed types.

Define
$$ A_{c,d}=\sum_{s=1}^{c}d^{2(s-1)}, \qquad f(c,d)=c^2(d+1)A_{c,d}. $$

Every friend-connected set of $s$ vertices containing a specified root is the support of a closed walk of length $2(s-1)$: traverse a spanning tree, using every tree edge twice. There are at most $d^{2(s-1)}$ walks of that length from the root.

Thus enumeration of these walks yields every eligible connected block, with at most $A_{c,d}$ candidates per root. Duplicate supports may be retained.

For one type, define $D(U)$ as the maximum total score of a partition of $U$ into friend-connected blocks of size at most $c$. Set $D(\varnothing)=0$. For nonempty $U$, choose its least vertex $v$ and use
$$ D(U)= \max_{\substack{v\in S\subseteq U\\ |S|\le c\\ (V_H,F_H)[S]\text{ connected}}} \left(w_H(S)+D(U\setminus S)\right). $$

Every admissible partition has one block containing $v$, whose removal gives the corresponding subproblem. Conversely, each displayed choice extends a subproblem partition. Singletons ensure that the maximum is never over an empty collection.

Lemma 3 therefore gives
$$ r_H=|F_H|-D(V_H). $$

There are $2^{n_H}$ states. Charging conservatively for candidate generation, scores, numerical values, and subset masks gives total time
$$ \widetilde O\left( L+f(c,d)\sum_H n_H2^{n_H}+\tau K^2 \right). $$
In terms of the requested parameters, this is at most
$$ \widetilde O\left(L+f(c,d)m2^q+\tau K^2\right), $$
and also at most
$$ \widetilde O\left(L+f(c,d)\tau q2^q+\tau K^2\right). $$

7. Zero threshold and property testing

For every $c,d$, the special case $B=0$ is polynomial-time decidable.

Because all masses are positive, Lemma 2 implies that $\mathrm{OPT}=0$ exactly when every listed type has $r_H=0$. A module has zero repair cost exactly when every friend component has size at most $c$ and contains no enemy edge.

Necessity follows because friend paths force their vertices into the same coalition. Sufficiency follows by taking the friend components themselves as coalitions. This characterization is checkable in
$$ \widetilde O(L+dm) $$
time.

The [published one-sided property tester](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-021-09505-x) concerns existence of a perfect partition versus being a fixed positive distance from that property. Its guarantee does not decide arbitrary exact repair thresholds. It is therefore compatible with the classification proved here.

6. Reduction to the repair threshold
