The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given schools \(M\cup\{\phi\}\), finite complete types \(t=(a,\succ)\) with masses \(\mu_t\), quota families \(Z(s)\), and per-capita bounds \(\underline q_{R,s},\bar q_{R,s}\), compute the GPS mass assignment \(x_{t,s}\) satisfying \(\sum_s x_{t,s}=\mu_t\), all quota constraints, and \(\sum_{t,s\ne\phi}x_{t,s}=\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\), while guaranteeing within-class envy-freeness and ordinal efficiency; realize \(x\) by assigning one indivisible school to each agent in the corresponding mass partition.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq1\) regular schools \(M\) and an unconstrained outside option \(\phi\). A complete type is \(t=(a(t),\succ_t)\), where \(a(t)\) is a public class and \(\succ_t\) is a strict ranking placing every regular school above \(\phi\).
A society is given by its support: the list \(S\) of \(\tau\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
For each regular school, an explicitly listed family \(Z(s)\) contains subsets of the occurring public classes, with binary-rational bounds \(0\leq\underline q_{R,s}\leq\bar q_{R,s}\). Let \(Q=\sum_s|Z(s)|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An assignment has \(x_{t,s}\geq0\), \(\sum_{s\in M\cup\{\phi\}}x_{t,s}=\mu_t\), and \(\underline q_{R,s}\leq\sum_{t:a(t)\in R}x_{t,s}\leq\bar q_{R,s}\). Assume these constraints are feasible; define \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\) as the maximum of \(\sum_{t,s\in M}x_{t,s}\) over feasible assignments.
An incomplete consumption vector \(y\) is extendable if some feasible optimum \(z\) satisfies \(z_{t,s}\geq y_{t,s}\) for every regular school. School \(s\) is available to class \(a\) if such a \(z\) satisfies \(\sum_{t:a(t)=a}z_{t,s}>\sum_{t:a(t)=a}y_{t,s}\); the outside option is always available.
GPS starts at zero: during time \(h\in[0,1]\), each type consumes its favorite available school at aggregate rate \(\mu_t\), keeping consumption extendable. When availability disappears, the school closes simultaneously to its entire class; consumption ends at time \(1\).
For the resulting \(x\), put \(p_{t,s}=x_{t,s}/\mu_t\). Within-class envy-freeness means \(p_t\) stochastically dominates \(p_u\) under \(\succ_t\) whenever \(a(t)=a(u)\); ordinal efficiency means no feasible assignment makes every normalized row weakly better in stochastic dominance and at least one strictly better.
An indivisible realization partitions each atomless interval \(I_t=[0,\mu_t)\) into school-labelled subintervals of lengths \(x_{t,s}\), assigning each point one school. Fairness and ordinal efficiency refer to \(p\) before realization; the realized school masses must satisfy the quotas exactly.

Is exact computation of this GPS mass assignment and an indivisible realization, with the stated envy-freeness and ordinal-efficiency guarantees, polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,Q,L\), or NP-hard?

For explicitly listed discrete agents, GPS runs in polynomial time and produces a feasible optimum that is within-type envy-free and ordinally efficient; its lottery implementation may violate quotas by the number of public types ([Ashlagi–Saberi–Shameli](https://web.stanford.edu/~iashlagi/papers/final-paper.pdf)).
Continuum PS is defined for ordinary school capacities ([Che–Kojima](https://www.columbia.edu/~yc2271/files/publications/psrp-Final.pdf)); VER handles arbitrary linear constraints efficiently in a finite-agent model ([Aziz–Brandl](https://brandlf.com/docs/ver.pdf)). No explicit result for the full support-encoded GPS computation and exact realization above was located.

Exact support-encoded GPS is polynomial-time solvable

Theorem. Under the stated hypotheses, the exact GPS mass assignment and an indivisible realization can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,Q,L\). The assignment is a feasible optimum, is within-class envy-free, and is ordinally efficient. Its realization satisfies every quota without violation.

The finite-agent GPS construction already computes consumption events by linear programming. The proof below establishes the required support-size and bit-complexity bounds directly, without expanding rational masses into discrete agents. See [Ashlagi–Saberi–Shameli, Section 4.2](https://web.stanford.edu/~iashlagi/papers/final-paper.pdf) for the finite-agent construction.

1. Aggregation by public class

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the set of occurring public classes, let \(A=|\mathcal A|\leq\tau\), and put

\[ c_a=\sum_{t:a(t)=a}\mu_t. \]

Consider the following polytope of regular-school class masses:

\[ \mathcal P= \left\{ W\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{\mathcal A\times M}: \sum_{s\in M}W_{a,s}\leq c_a\ \text{for every }a,\quad \underline q_{R,s}\leq\sum_{a\in R}W_{a,s}\leq\bar q_{R,s} \ \text{for every listed }(R,s) \right\}. \]

The unused mass in each class goes to \(\phi\). Consequently,

\[ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu)= \max_{W\in\mathcal P}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A,\ s\in M}W_{a,s}. \]

To justify this equality, aggregating any feasible complete-type assignment gives a point of \(\mathcal P\). Conversely, any \(W\in\mathcal P\) can be distributed among complete types in class \(a\) proportionally to their masses, with the remainder assigned to \(\phi\). This preserves all quota expressions and the objective.

The polytope is nonempty by the feasibility assumption and compact because \(0\leq W_{a,s}\leq c_a\leq1\). Write its nonempty optimal face as

\[ \mathcal P^*= \left\{W\in\mathcal P: \sum_{a,s}W_{a,s}=\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\right\}. \]

Lemma 1: Extendability depends only on class totals

Let \(y\) be nonnegative regular-school consumption satisfying \(\sum_{s\in M}y_{t,s}\leq\mu_t\). Define

\[ Y_{a,s}=\sum_{t:a(t)=a}y_{t,s}, \qquad \mathcal K(Y)=\{W\in\mathcal P^*:W_{a,s}\geq Y_{a,s}\ \text{for all }a,s\}. \]

Then \(y\) is extendable if and only if \(\mathcal K(Y)\) is nonempty. Moreover, the possible class totals of optimal extensions of \(y\) are exactly \(\mathcal K(Y)\).

Proof. Aggregating an optimal extension proves one direction.

For the converse, take \(W\in\mathcal K(Y)\). Set

\[ r_t=\mu_t-\sum_{s\in M}y_{t,s}, \qquad r_a=\sum_{t:a(t)=a}r_t =c_a-\sum_{s\in M}Y_{a,s}. \]

The nonnegative residual school masses satisfy

\[ \sum_{s\in M}(W_{a,s}-Y_{a,s})\leq r_a. \]

If \(r_a>0\), define, for \(a(t)=a\),

\[ z_{t,s} =y_{t,s}+\frac{r_t}{r_a}(W_{a,s}-Y_{a,s}) \quad(s\in M), \qquad z_{t,\phi}=\mu_t-\sum_{s\in M}z_{t,s}. \]

The displayed residual bound makes \(z_{t,\phi}\) nonnegative. Summing over complete types in class \(a\) gives class masses \(W_{a,s}\).

If \(r_a=0\), the same residual bound forces \(W_{a,s}=Y_{a,s}\) for every regular school; use \(z_{t,s}=y_{t,s}\) and \(z_{t,\phi}=0\).

Thus \(z\) extends \(y\), has the prescribed row masses, satisfies the quotas, and attains \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\). \(\square\)

It follows that availability is determined exactly by the LP value

\[ g_{a,s}(Y)= \max_{W\in\mathcal K(Y)}(W_{a,s}-Y_{a,s}). \]

School \(s\) is available to class \(a\) precisely when \(g_{a,s}(Y)>0\). Since every feasible objective value is nonnegative, unavailability means that every \(W\in\mathcal K(Y)\) has \(W_{a,s}=Y_{a,s}\).

Lemma 2: Closures are permanent

Suppose \(Y'\geq Y\) and both are extendable. If \((a,s)\) is unavailable at \(Y\), then

\[ Y'_{a,s}=Y_{a,s}, \]

and \((a,s)\) remains unavailable at \(Y'\).

Proof. We have \(\mathcal K(Y')\subseteq\mathcal K(Y)\). Every \(W\in\mathcal K(Y')\) therefore satisfies

\[ Y'_{a,s}\leq W_{a,s}=Y_{a,s}\leq Y'_{a,s}. \]

All quantities are equal, proving both conclusions. \(\square\)

In particular, after a class–school pair closes, no complete type in that class can consume more of that school: individual consumption is nondecreasing, and its class total cannot increase.

2. An exact algorithm with polynomially many phases

Maintain time \(h\), complete-type consumption \(y\), and its regular-school class totals \(Y\). Initially all are zero.

At the start of a phase, solve the availability LPs. Each complete type \(t\) chooses its favorite available option \(b_t\), including the always-available \(\phi\). Define the regular-school aggregate direction

\[ d_{a,s}=\sum_{\substack{t:a(t)=a\\b_t=s}}\mu_t. \]

Compute the phase duration by the LP

\[ \text{maximize }\delta \quad\text{subject to}\quad W\in\mathcal P^*,\qquad W_{a,s}\geq Y_{a,s}+\delta d_{a,s}\ \text{for all }a,s,\qquad 0\leq\delta\leq1-h. \]

For its optimum \(\delta^*\), update each chosen complete-type coordinate by

\[ y_{t,b_t}\leftarrow y_{t,b_t}+\mu_t\delta^*, \]

and update \(Y\) and \(h\) accordingly. Repeat until \(h=1\).

Every phase has positive duration

Assume \(h<1\). If \(d=0\), the LP permits \(\delta=1-h\).

Otherwise, every pair with \(d_{a,s}>0\) is available. For each such pair, choose a witness in \(\mathcal K(Y)\) having strictly positive slack there. Average these finitely many witnesses. Convexity and nonnegative slack ensure that the average \(\bar W\) has strictly positive slack at every pair with positive direction.

Therefore any sufficiently small positive \(\delta\) satisfying

\[ \delta\leq1-h, \qquad \delta\leq \min_{d_{a,s}>0}\frac{\bar W_{a,s}-Y_{a,s}}{d_{a,s}} \]

is feasible. Hence \(\delta^*>0\).

The algorithm follows the stated GPS process

Let \(W^*\) witness the endpoint of a phase. At any earlier offset \(0\leq\theta<\delta^*\), it remains an optimal extension of \(Y+\theta d\). For every actively consumed regular pair,

\[ W^*_{a,s}-(Y_{a,s}+\theta d_{a,s}) \geq(\delta^*-\theta)d_{a,s}>0. \]

Thus every chosen regular school remains available throughout the phase interior. Previously unavailable schools cannot reopen by Lemma 2, and closures of other schools cannot change a type’s current favorite. A class choosing \(\phi\) already has every regular school closed.

The choices therefore remain exactly the GPS choices until the computed endpoint. Lemma 1 also establishes extendability throughout the phase, since each regular row consumes at most its total mass.

There are at most \(Am+1\) phases

Suppose a phase ends before time \(1\). At least one actively consumed pair must then be unavailable.

Indeed, if every positive-direction pair were still available at the endpoint, the same averaging argument would allow an additional positive advance in direction \(d\). This contradicts maximality of \(\delta^*\).

Every nonfinal phase therefore closes a previously available class–school pair permanently. There are only \(Am\) such pairs. Consequently the algorithm reaches time \(1\) after at most

\[ Am+1\leq\tau m+1 \]

phases. Simultaneous closures require no tie-breaking: all availability LPs are evaluated at the same endpoint.

3. The final assignment is a feasible optimum

Let \(x=y(1)\), and let \(Y\) denote its regular-school class totals. By construction,

\[ \sum_{s\in M\cup\{\phi\}}x_{t,s}=\mu_t, \qquad \mathcal K(Y)\neq\varnothing. \]

Take any \(W\in\mathcal K(Y)\). We show \(W=Y\).

For a class with no outside consumption, \(\sum_{s\in M}Y_{a,s}=c_a\). Since \(W\geq Y\) and \(\sum_sW_{a,s}\leq c_a\), equality holds coordinatewise.

For a class with positive outside consumption, consider the start of its first outside-consumption phase. At that time every regular school is unavailable to the class, because every complete type ranks every regular school above \(\phi\). Every optimal extension therefore has each regular class coordinate equal to the consumed coordinate at that time. Lemma 2 freezes those coordinates thereafter. Since the final extension set is contained in the extension set at that earlier time, again \(W_{a,s}=Y_{a,s}\) for every regular school.

Thus \(Y=W\in\mathcal P^*\). The complete-type assignment \(x\) has these class totals and the correct row masses, so it is feasible and attains \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\).

This argument also resolves the outside-option issue: although extendability constrains only regular-school coordinates, outside consumption cannot leave an unfulfilled optimal regular-school assignment.

4. Exact bit complexity

Counting phases alone does not establish polynomial time; the rational numbers used in successive LPs must also remain polynomial in size.

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly described rational LP can be solved exactly in polynomial time in its encoding length; for a nonempty bounded feasible polytope, an optimal vertex can be returned. Polynomial-time LP algorithms and recovery of exact vertices are discussed in [MIT’s linear-programming notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

Here every initial, availability, and duration LP has a nonempty compact feasible region. They have at most \(Am+1\) variables and \(O(Am+A+Q)\) constraints.

A fixed denominator for coefficients

Let \(D_{\mathrm{in}}\) be the product of all input denominators. Its logarithm is at most \(L\).

The initial optimization over \(\mathcal P\) has integer constraint coefficients in \(\{0,1,-1\}\), with right-hand-side denominators dividing \(D_{\mathrm{in}}\). Cramer’s rule at an optimal vertex shows that \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\) has denominator dividing \(D_{\mathrm{in}}|\Delta_0|\), where \(\Delta_0\) is a nonzero determinant of order \(Am\). Hadamard’s inequality bounds its bit length polynomially in \(Am\).

Choose a fixed integer \(D\) divisible by every input denominator and by the denominator of \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\). We may therefore choose it with

\[ \log D=O\bigl(L+Am\log(Am+1)\bigr). \]

Every direction coordinate \(d_{a,s}\) is a sum of input masses. Its denominator divides \(D\), and \(0\leq d_{a,s}\leq1\).

State denominators grow additively in bit length

Put \(N=Am+1\). Suppose all stored consumption coordinates and \(h\) have denominators dividing an integer \(E_j\), with \(D\mid E_j\).

Multiply each constraint of the phase-duration LP by \(D\). Its coefficient matrix is then integral, with entries of absolute value at most \(D\). This bound is independent of the current consumption numbers: those occur only on the right-hand side.

At an optimal vertex, choose \(N\) linearly independent active constraints. For their coefficient determinant \(\Delta_j\neq0\), Hadamard’s inequality gives

\[ |\Delta_j|\leq N^{N/2}D^N. \]

The scaled right-hand-side denominators divide \(E_j\), so Cramer’s rule shows that the optimal duration has denominator dividing \(E_j|\Delta_j|\).

Updating consumption multiplies this duration by an input mass. Thus a common denominator for the next state is

\[ E_{j+1}=E_j|\Delta_j|D. \]

Starting with \(E_0=D\), we obtain

\[ \log E_{j+1} \leq\log E_j+(N+1)\log D+\frac N2\log N. \]

There are at most \(N\) phases. Hence every stored denominator has polynomial bit length. Stored times and consumption coordinates lie in \([0,1]\), so their numerators have polynomial bit length as well.

Availability LPs use these same polynomial-size right-hand sides and fixed integer coefficient matrices. Their optimum values can therefore be compared with zero exactly in polynomial time.

There are at most \(Am\) availability LPs and one duration LP per phase, plus the initial optimization. The total number of LP calls is \(O((Am+1)^2)\). Each call has polynomial encoding length, proving polynomial running time in \(m,\tau,Q,L\).

No expansion into a number of agents proportional to a mass denominator is involved.

5. Within-class envy-freeness

For a complete type \(t\), let \(b_t(h)\) be its consumed option during a phase. Since its aggregate consumption rate is \(\mu_t\),

\[ p_{t,s}=\int_0^1\mathbf1_{\{b_t(h)=s\}}\,dh. \]

The integrands are piecewise constant; choices at finitely many event times do not affect the integral.

Fix \(t,u\) with \(a(t)=a(u)\), and let \(B\) be any initial segment of \(t\)’s ranking. Both types face the same available menu at every time.

If \(b_u(h)\in B\), that option is available to \(t\). Because \(t\) chooses its favorite available option, \(b_t(h)\in B\) as well. Thus

\[ \mathbf1_{\{b_t(h)\in B\}} \geq \mathbf1_{\{b_u(h)\in B\}} \]

throughout every phase. Integrating gives

\[ \sum_{s\in B}p_{t,s}\geq\sum_{s\in B}p_{u,s}. \]

These are exactly all stochastic-dominance inequalities under \(\succ_t\). Hence GPS is within-class envy-free, including when \(\mu_t\neq\mu_u\).

6. Ordinal efficiency

Suppose, for contradiction, that a feasible assignment \(v\) makes every normalized row weakly better than \(x\) in stochastic dominance and at least one strictly better.

All regular schools form an initial segment of every ranking. Therefore, for each \(t\),

\[ \sum_{s\in M}v_{t,s}\geq\sum_{s\in M}x_{t,s}. \]

Summing shows that the objective of \(v\) is at least \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\). Feasibility gives the reverse inequality, so \(v\) is itself optimal.

Since \(v\neq x\) and their total regular-school masses agree, some regular coordinate satisfies \(x_{t,s}>v_{t,s}\).

Let \(y(h)\) be the GPS trajectory and define

\[ h_0=\max\left\{ h\in[0,1]: y_{t,s}(h)\leq v_{t,s}\ \text{for every }t,\ s\in M \right\}. \]

Continuity and coordinatewise monotonicity make this set a closed initial interval. The preceding strict inequality implies \(h_0<1\).

Because the trajectory has finitely many linear phases, there is a complete type \(t\) consuming a regular school \(s\) immediately to the right of \(h_0\) such that

\[ y_{t,s}(h_0)=v_{t,s}, \qquad x_{t,s}>v_{t,s}. \]

The assignment \(v\) is an optimal extension of \(y(h_0)\).

Consider any school \(r\succ_t s\). It must be unavailable to class \(a(t)\) at \(h_0\), since otherwise \(t\) would choose \(r\) instead of \(s\). Unavailability means every optimal extension has zero aggregate slack at that pair. Since \(v\geq y(h_0)\) on all regular coordinates, this forces

\[ v_{t,r}=y_{t,r}(h_0). \]

Permanent closure also prevents further consumption of \(r\), so

\[ x_{t,r}=y_{t,r}(h_0)=v_{t,r}. \]

Consequently, for the initial segment ending at \(s\),

\[ \sum_{r\succeq_t s}x_{t,r} > \sum_{r\succeq_t s}v_{t,r}. \]

Dividing by the positive mass \(\mu_t\) contradicts stochastic dominance of \(v_t/\mu_t\) over \(x_t/\mu_t\). Thus \(x\) is ordinally efficient.

7. Exact indivisible realization

Fix an ordering \(s_1,\ldots,s_m,\phi\). For each complete type \(t\), compute

\[ b_{t,0}=0, \qquad b_{t,k}=\sum_{j=1}^k x_{t,s_j}, \]

where \(s_{m+1}=\phi\). Then \(b_{t,m+1}=\mu_t\).

Label the half-open subinterval

\[ [b_{t,k-1},b_{t,k}) \]

by school \(s_k\), omitting empty intervals. These intervals partition \(I_t=[0,\mu_t)\), assigning every point exactly one school. The school-\(s\) subinterval in \(I_t\) has length exactly \(x_{t,s}\).

Therefore each constrained realized mass is exactly

\[ \sum_{t:a(t)\in R}x_{t,s}, \]

which satisfies its specified bounds. In particular, equality quotas hold exactly. Fairness and ordinal efficiency remain properties of the unchanged normalized mass rows, as required.

There are at most \(\tau(m+1)\) labelled intervals. Their rational endpoints have polynomial bit length by Section 4, so this is a polynomial-size realization computable in polynomial time.
