1. The source problem

A binary simple stochastic game has two absorbing vertices, called the zero sink and the one sink. Every other vertex has two successors and is controlled either by the maximizing player, by the minimizing player, or by a fair random choice. The payoff is the probability of eventually reaching the one sink.

The game is stopping if, from every vertex and under every pair of strategies, an absorbing vertex is reached almost surely.

Solving general simple stochastic games is polynomial-time reducible to solving this stopping binary class; this standard reduction is stated in [Etessami, Papadimitriou, Rubinstein, and Yannakakis, Section 6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.03210v2). Consequently, the reduction proved below establishes $\mathrm{SSG}$-hardness. This comparison does not establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.

We first prove the fixed-point characterization needed for the reduction.

Lemma 1: stopping-game values are uniquely determined by Bellman equations

Let the game have $n\ge 1$ nonabsorbing vertices. Write $t_0,t_1$ for the sink coordinates. Define the Bellman map $B$ by
$$ B_0(t)=0,\qquad B_1(t)=1, $$
and, at a nonabsorbing vertex $i$ with successors $j,k$, by
$$ B_i(t)= \begin{cases} \max\{t_j,t_k\},&\text{at a maximizing vertex},\\ \min\{t_j,t_k\},&\text{at a minimizing vertex},\\ (t_j+t_k)/2,&\text{at a random vertex}. \end{cases} $$

Then $B$ has exactly one fixed point in $[0,1]^{n+2}$. That fixed point is the game-value vector.

Proof. Define $S_k(i)$ as the largest probability of avoiding absorption for the next $k$ transitions, allowing both players to cooperate in avoiding absorption. At a sink, set $S_k=0$. At nonabsorbing vertices, $S_0=1$.

Backward induction gives
$$ S_{k+1}(i)= \begin{cases} \max\{S_k(j),S_k(k)\},&\text{at either controlled kind of vertex},\\ (S_k(j)+S_k(k))/2,&\text{at a random vertex}. \end{cases} $$
Here the repeated symbol $k$ in a successor position denotes the second successor; the recursion index is the subscript.

These probabilities decrease with the horizon. Let
$$ A_\ell=\{i:S_\ell(i)=1\}. $$
The sets $A_\ell$ are decreasing. A controlled vertex belongs to $A_{\ell+1}$ exactly when at least one successor belongs to $A_\ell$; a random vertex belongs exactly when both successors do.

If a nonempty $A_\ell$ satisfied $A_{\ell+1}=A_\ell$, each controlled vertex in this set would have a successor in the set, and both successors of every random vertex in the set would remain in it. Choosing the indicated controlled transitions would prevent absorption forever. This contradicts stopping.

Starting with $n$ nonabsorbing vertices, the sets must therefore become empty within $n$ steps. Hence $S_n(i)<1$ everywhere. Since $S_n(i)$ is dyadic with denominator dividing $2^n$,
$$ S_n(i)\le q:=1-2^{-n}<1. $$
Conditioning after successive blocks of $n$ transitions consequently bounds survival for $kn$ transitions by $q^k$, uniformly over all strategies.

For two vectors $u,v$ with the prescribed sink coordinates, induction gives
$$ |B^\ell(u)_i-B^\ell(v)_i| \le S_\ell(i)\|u-v\|_\infty. $$
Indeed, differences of minima or maxima are bounded by the largest successor difference, while a random vertex takes their average. In particular,
$$ \|B^n(u)-B^n(v)\|_\infty\le q\|u-v\|_\infty. $$

Existence follows by iterating $B$ from the vector whose nonabsorbing coordinates are zero and whose sink coordinates are $0,1$. This sequence increases coordinatewise, is bounded, and converges. Continuity makes its limit a fixed point.

If $u,v$ are fixed points, the preceding inequality gives
$$ \|u-v\|_\infty\le q\|u-v\|_\infty, $$
so $u=v$.

Finally, backward induction identifies the iterates with finite-horizon game values. For every strategy pair, truncating after $kn$ transitions changes the reachability probability by at most $q^k$. This uniform bound implies that both the lower and upper infinite-horizon values converge to the same finite-horizon limit. Thus the unique fixed point is the game-value vector. $\square$

The same proof gives a useful scaled version. If the sink coordinates are prescribed as $0,\lambda$, where $0\le\lambda\le1$, the fixed point is uniquely
$$ t=\lambda v, $$
where $v$ is the original game-value vector. The nonabsorbing Bellman formulas commute with multiplication by the nonnegative scalar $\lambda$.

The coordinates of $v$ are rational with polynomial bit length. To see this, select an attaining successor at every minimizing and maximizing vertex. The resulting equations have the form
$$ v_{\mathrm{int}}=Pv_{\mathrm{int}}+b. $$
Stopping implies $P^\ell\to0$, so $I-P$ is invertible. Its coefficients have denominators dividing $2$. Cramer's rule, after multiplying by $2$, gives coordinate bit length $O(n\log n)$ by the determinant bound.

2. A class of choices satisfying path independence

The following elementary construction will implement the reduction.

Lemma 2: retained-input threshold choices

Partition the coordinates of $[0,1]^d$ into sets $I,O,R$. For each $o\in O$, let
$$ Q_o:[0,1]^I\longrightarrow[0,1] $$
be coordinatewise antitone. Define
$$ C(u)_j= \begin{cases} u_j,&j\in I,\\ \min\{u_j,Q_j(u_I)\},&j\in O,\\ 0,&j\in R. \end{cases} $$
Then $0\le C(u)\le u$ and
$$ C(u\vee v)=C(C(u)\vee v). $$

Proof. Contractivity is immediate. The $I$-coordinates of both sides of the path-independence identity equal $u_I\vee v_I$, and the $R$-coordinates vanish.

Fix $o\in O$. Put
$$ q=Q_o(u_I),\qquad c=Q_o(u_I\vee v_I). $$
Antitonicity gives $c\le q$. The two output coordinates to compare are
$$ \min\{\max\{u_o,v_o\},c\} $$
and
$$ \min\{\max\{\min\{u_o,q\},v_o\},c\}. $$
They are equal because
$$ \min\{\max\{a,b\},c\} =\max\{\min\{a,c\},\min\{b,c\}\}, $$
and $c\le q$ implies
$$ \min\{\min\{u_o,q\},c\}=\min\{u_o,c\}. $$
This proves the identity in every coordinate. $\square$

Thus a type may retain designated input coordinates in full and cap its output coordinates by antitone functions of those inputs. Coordinates outside these designated sets can be rejected completely.

3. A monotone equation system containing the game

Take a stopping binary simple stochastic game with value vector $v$. Introduce variables
$$ r,s,\lambda,t_0,t_1,t_2,\ldots,t_{n+1}\in[0,1]. $$
Use the equations
$$ r=r,\qquad s=s,\qquad \lambda=\min\{r,s\}, $$
$$ t_0=0,\qquad t_1=\lambda, $$
and the original minimum, maximum, or averaging Bellman equation at each nonabsorbing game vertex.

Every right-hand side is monotone and is one of:

  • a constant;
  • a copy of another coordinate;
  • the minimum or maximum of two coordinates;
  • the average of two coordinates.

By Lemma 1, the fixed points of this system are exactly
$$ r,s\in[0,1],\qquad \lambda=\min\{r,s\},\qquad t=\lambda v. $$

In particular, two fixed points are explicitly known without computing $v$:
$$ (r,s,\lambda,t)=(1,0,0,0) $$
and
$$ (r,s,\lambda,t)=(0,1,0,0). $$

Preparing the dependency graph

For the matching construction, we arrange that the equation system has:

  • no self-loops or repeated directed edges;
  • at least one and at most two outgoing edges at every vertex;
  • at most three input coordinates in every equation.

This can be done in polynomial time as follows.

First add an ignored occurrence of each original variable to its own right-hand side. This does not change any function, and ensures that every original variable is used somewhere. Original equations now have at most three input occurrences.

If an original variable has $d$ outgoing occurrences, create a binary tree of new copy variables with $d$ distinct leaves. The root copies the original variable, and every other tree vertex copies its parent. Route each outgoing occurrence through its own leaf.

At a fixed point, every copy variable must equal its original source. Conversely, every fixed point of the original system has exactly one extension to these copy variables. Consequently, this modification preserves the fixed points and their coordinatewise order.

Every original vertex now has one outgoing edge to its copy-tree root. Internal tree vertices have at most two outgoing edges, and leaves have one outgoing edge to their assigned target. All vertices have positive outgoing degree. Since the tree vertices are new and the occurrence leaves are distinct, there are no self-loops or repeated directed edges.

Let $V$ be the resulting vertex set. Write its equations as
$$ \theta_v=g_v\bigl((\theta_u)_{u\to v}\bigr),\qquad v\in V. $$
Each $g_v$ is monotone, maps its input cube into $[0,1]$, and is one of the elementary formulas above, possibly with an ignored input. The number of vertices is polynomial in the game size.

4. Constructing the society

Choose a power of two $N$ with
$$ |V|\le N<2|V|. $$
Create $N$ firms and $N$ workers. For each $v\in V$, designate a firm $f_v$ and worker $w_v$; the remaining types are padding types.

Give every type mass $1/N$. Thus all masses are positive dyadic rationals, both sides have total mass one, and every link has upper bound
$$ h=\frac1{N^2}. $$

Work first in normalized matching coordinates
$$ \xi_{fw}=N^2z_{fw}\in[0,1]. $$
For a bundle $u\in[0,1/N]^N$, its normalized coordinates are $Nu$. A normalized choice $\widehat C$ defines the actual choice by
$$ C(u)=\frac1N\widehat C(Nu). $$
Positive scaling preserves maxima and the path-independence identity.

There are two kinds of designated links:

  • For each $v\in V$, a main link

$$ d_v=(f_v,w_v). $$

  • For each dependency $u\to v$, a dependency link

$$ e_{uv}=(f_v,w_u). $$

The absence of self-loops and repeated edges ensures that these links are distinct.

Firm choices

Firm $f_v$ retains all its incoming dependency coordinates in full. It caps its main coordinate by
$$ Q_v(\xi) = g_v\bigl((1-\xi_{e_{uv}})_{u\to v}\bigr): $$
$$ [\widehat C_{f_v}(\xi)]_{d_v} = \min\{\xi_{d_v},Q_v(\xi)\}. $$
It rejects every other coordinate.

Since $g_v$ is monotone, $Q_v$ is antitone in the retained inputs. Lemma 2 proves the firm-choice promises.

Worker choices

Worker $w_u$ retains its main coordinate in full. For every outgoing dependency $u\to v$, it chooses
$$ [\widehat C_{w_u}(\xi)]_{e_{uv}} = \min\{\xi_{e_{uv}},1-\xi_{d_u}\}. $$
It rejects every other coordinate.

There are at most two such outputs. Their common threshold is antitone in the retained main coordinate, so Lemma 2 also proves the worker-choice promises.

Every padding type rejects all coordinates.

Explicit polyhedral representations

Each firm threshold is affine or is the minimum or maximum of two affine formulas. In the latter case, divide the domain into the two closed polyhedra identifying the selected branch. On each branch, divide once more according to whether the main offer is below or above its threshold. This gives at most four closed rational polyhedral regions.

Each worker has at most two capped outputs. Independently divide according to whether each offer is below or above the common threshold. This also gives at most four regions.

The affine formulas agree on every overlap because tied branches have equal values. Rescaling gives closed rational polyhedral covers on the required bundle domains. All coefficients have polynomial bit length.

Thus the construction supplies the precise explicit representations required by the task, with
$$ m=N,\qquad \tau=2N, $$
and total encoding length polynomial in the source-game size.

5. Stable matchings correspond exactly to fixed points

For a fixed point $\theta$ of the prepared equation system, define $Z(\theta)$ by
$$ Z(\theta)_{d_v}=h\theta_v, $$
$$ Z(\theta)_{e_{uv}}=h(1-\theta_u), $$
and set every other entry to zero.

Lemma 3: exact stability correspondence

The stable matchings of the constructed society are exactly the matrices $Z(\theta)$ for fixed points $\theta$ of the equation system.

Proof. Every designated link is retained in full at one endpoint and capped at the other endpoint. The capped endpoint's threshold depends only on its retained coordinates, so increasing the capped coordinate itself leaves its threshold unchanged.

Suppose its normalized current amount is $a$ and its threshold is $Q$. Individual rationality requires $a\le Q$.

If $a<Q$, choose
$$ 0<\epsilon\le Q-a. $$
Since $Q\le1$, this increment respects the link bound. The retaining endpoint accepts the entire increment, and so does the capped endpoint. In original matching units, the increment is
$$ \delta=h\epsilon>0, $$
and it is a blocking increment exactly as defined in the task.

If $a=Q$, every available positive increment is rejected at the capped endpoint. Hence, at every designated link, individual rationality and nonblocking together are equivalent to equality with its threshold.

Every undesignated link is rejected at both endpoints. Individual rationality forces its amount to be zero, and it cannot block.

Therefore stability is equivalent to
$$ \xi_{e_{uv}}=1-\xi_{d_u} $$
and
$$ \xi_{d_v} = g_v\bigl((1-\xi_{e_{uv}})_{u\to v}\bigr) = g_v\bigl((\xi_{d_u})_{u\to v}\bigr). $$
Setting $\theta_v=\xi_{d_v}$ gives precisely the prepared fixed-point equations. Conversely, those equations make every designated link equal its threshold and every undesignated link zero. This proves the correspondence. $\square$

This argument uses the stated absence of additional row or column capacity constraints: retaining several designated inputs simultaneously is allowed.

6. The Blair orders correspond to coordinatewise orders

Lemma 4: order correspondence

For fixed points $\theta,\eta$,
$$ Z(\theta)\succeq_W Z(\eta) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \theta\ge\eta, $$
and
$$ Z(\theta)\succeq_F Z(\eta) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \theta\le\eta. $$
All comparisons on the right are coordinatewise.

Proof for workers. Worker $w_u$ retains its main coordinate in full. Thus choosing $Z(\theta)$ from the coordinatewise maximum of the two bundles requires
$$ \max\{\theta_u,\eta_u\}=\theta_u. $$
Necessity follows for every $u$.

Conversely, suppose $\theta\ge\eta$. The main offer to worker $w_u$ is $\theta_u$. Every outgoing dependency offer is
$$ \max\{1-\theta_u,1-\eta_u\}=1-\eta_u. $$
Its threshold is $1-\theta_u$, so the chosen amount is $1-\theta_u$. The worker therefore chooses exactly its bundle in $Z(\theta)$.

Proof for firms. Suppose $\theta\le\eta$. At firm $f_v$, every incoming dependency offer is
$$ \max\{1-\theta_u,1-\eta_u\}=1-\theta_u. $$
The main threshold consequently equals
$$ g_v\bigl((\theta_u)_{u\to v}\bigr)=\theta_v. $$
The main offer is $\eta_v$, and the firm chooses $\theta_v$. It therefore chooses its bundle in $Z(\theta)$.

Conversely, a firm retains each incoming dependency coordinate in full. Choosing the bundle of $Z(\theta)$ thus requires, on every edge $u\to v$,
$$ \max\{1-\theta_u,1-\eta_u\}=1-\theta_u, $$
which implies $\theta_u\le\eta_u$. Every prepared vertex has an outgoing edge, so these inequalities cover every coordinate. $\square$

Because the prepared fixed points are uniquely parameterized by $r,s$, their coordinatewise order is exactly the product order on these parameters. Indeed, if $r\ge r'$ and $s\ge s'$, then
$$ \min\{r,s\}\ge\min\{r',s'\}, $$
and all game coordinates increase because they are the nonnegative vector $v$ multiplied by this minimum. Copy coordinates preserve these inequalities. The converse follows by inspecting $r,s$ themselves.

Hence the stable set under the worker order is order-isomorphic to $[0,1]^2$, and the firm order is its reverse.

7. The two promised inputs and their four bounds

Let $\theta^x,\theta^y$ be the prepared extensions of
$$ (r,s,\lambda,t)=(1,0,0,0) $$
and
$$ (r,s,\lambda,t)=(0,1,0,0). $$
Supply
$$ x=Z(\theta^x),\qquad y=Z(\theta^y). $$

Every coordinate of these fixed points is zero or one: copy coordinates simply copy their original source. Thus every supplied matching entry is either $0$ or $h$. Both matrices are computable in polynomial time without knowing any game value, and Lemma 3 proves that both are stable.

A common stable worker upper bound must have
$$ r\ge1,\qquad s\ge1. $$
Therefore $r=s=1$. The equation system then forces
$$ \lambda=1,\qquad t=v, $$
and all copies are uniquely determined. There is exactly one common stable worker upper bound. It is consequently the least stable worker upper bound.

Likewise, a common stable worker lower bound must have $r=s=0$. It uniquely forces $\lambda=0,t=0$.

Writing $\theta^{11}$ and $\theta^{00}$ for these two fixed points, Lemma 4 gives all four requested bounds:
$$ x\vee_W y=Z(\theta^{11}), \qquad x\wedge_W y=Z(\theta^{00}), $$
$$ x\wedge_F y=Z(\theta^{11}), \qquad x\vee_F y=Z(\theta^{00}). $$

In particular, every requested bound exists on these constructed inputs.

For every original game vertex $i$, its value is recovered from the corresponding main link of the worker join:
$$ v_i = N^2[x\vee_W y]_{d_i}. $$
The identical extraction works from the firm meet.

All construction steps, input encodings, and extraction operations have polynomial complexity. The recovered values have polynomial bit length, as proved in Section 1. This completes the polynomial-time reduction.
