The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings and a seat count \(S\), compute the size-\(S\) committees selected by sequential RCV and STV, using a fixed tie-breaking rule and STV quota \(1/(S+1)\), and determine whether the committees are disjoint.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\) candidates and let \(2\le S\le m\) be an integer seat count. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) complete strict rankings with positive masses, each a binary rational, summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L,S\). Fix a total priority order on candidates for all ties.
Sequential RCV runs weighted IRV repeatedly for \(S\) seats, each time using the original masses and rankings with only previously elected candidates deleted; eliminations from earlier IRV runs are reset.
For STV, initially assign each ranking's mass to its first candidate and fix \(q=1/(S+1)\). Continuing candidates are neither elected nor eliminated. Each tally is the total current weight assigned to that candidate.
If any continuing candidate has tally at least \(q\), elect one with maximum tally, resolving ties by priority. If its tally is \(a\), multiply every weight assigned to it by \((a-q)/a\) and transfer the resulting weights to their highest-ranked continuing candidates. Otherwise eliminate a minimum-tally candidate, resolving ties by priority, and transfer all its current weights in the same manner.
Stop after \(S\) elections; if the number of continuing candidates equals the remaining vacancies, elect them all. All arithmetic and comparisons are exact, without rounding. Output both committees and whether their intersection is empty.

Is computing both committees and testing their disjointness solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,S\), or is this problem NP-hard?

For finite electorates, disjoint committees exist for every \(S\ge2\) with \(m=2S\), also extending to \(m\ge2S\); this is an existence result, not a complexity classification. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.17341)
An exact-rational weighted-transfer STV implementation is documented with quota \(1/(S+1)\), but elects only strictly above quota; it supplies no complexity classification for the problem defined here. [Source](https://yingtongli.me/blog/2021/07/24/opentally-wigm.html)

Partial results: exponential growth of reduced STV fractions

I do not settle the unrestricted polynomial-time versus $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness classification.

The main new result is an explicit family, with $m=2S$ and $\tau=S+1$, in which a current STV weight has a reduced denominator containing at least $2^{S-2}$ bits before the final election. Thus the large integers in the earlier simulation are not merely an artifact of failing to reduce fractions.

This is not a computational hardness proof: the STV committee in that family is easy to identify directly. I also prove a refined exact simulation bound that isolates which transfers can cause quadratic denominator growth.

1. Representation and conservation of weight

Index the input rankings by $i\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}$, and write their masses as
$$ p_i=\frac{a_i}{b_i}>0. $$
Define
$$ d=S+1,\qquad E_0=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i,\qquad h_i=a_i\frac{E_0}{b_i}. $$
Then
$$ p_i=\frac{h_i}{E_0}, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}h_i=E_0. $$
The integers $E_0$ and $h_i$ have $O(L)$ bits and can be computed in polynomial time. Indeed, the sum of the bit lengths of the input denominators is at most $L$.

Each ranking has one current weight and one current assignment. The specified transfer rule never splits a ranking’s weight between candidates.

An elimination preserves total current weight. A quota election with tally $a$ removes exactly
$$ a-a\frac{a-q}{a}=q=\frac1d. $$
Consequently, after $k$ individual quota elections, before termination, total current weight is
$$ 1-\frac{k}{d}=\frac{d-k}{d}. $$

Transfers needed for subsequent counting always have destinations. Before an elimination, the number of continuing candidates exceeds the remaining vacancies, so another continuing candidate remains. After an election that leaves a vacancy, at least one continuing candidate also remains. Complete rankings therefore prevent exhaustion at these steps.

The transfer after the final election can be omitted, since it cannot affect the requested committees.

2. Exact algorithms and their bit complexity

Put
$$ b=L+\left\lceil\log_2(S+1)\right\rceil+2. $$

2.1 Sequential RCV is polynomial-time computable

In every IRV run, represent the original masses by the integers $h_i$ with common denominator $E_0$. Every tally is a sum of some $h_i$, so every tally numerator lies between $0$ and $E_0$.

Tally comparisons are integer comparisons. A strict majority is tested by comparing twice a tally numerator with $E_0$. The denominator and original weights never change.

For each run, maintain a pointer into each ranking, advancing past previously elected candidates and candidates eliminated in that run. Every pointer advances at most $m$ times. Each run has at most $m$ counting rounds.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, a conservative bit-time bound is
$$ O\!\left(Sm(\tau+m)b^2\right). $$
Resetting the elimination set between runs, while retaining previously elected candidates as deleted, implements the specified sequential RCV rule.

2.2 A division-free exact STV simulation

Maintain nonnegative integers $N_i$ and a positive integer $E$ such that
$$ w_i=\frac{N_i}{dE}. $$
Initially take
$$ E=E_0,\qquad N_i=dh_i. $$

For each continuing candidate $c$, define
$$ A_c=\sum_{\text{rankings assigned to }c}N_i. $$
Its tally is $A_c/(dE)$, and the quota test is exactly
$$ \frac{A_c}{dE}\ge\frac1d \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A_c\ge E. $$
Thus the weak quota inequality in the task is preserved.

Eliminations change only assignments. If candidate $c$ is elected, put $A=A_c$ and update, using the old values on the right,
$$ E'=EA, $$
$$ N_i'= \begin{cases} N_i(A-E),&\text{if ranking }i\text{ was assigned to }c,\\ N_iA,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
For an assigned ranking,
$$ \frac{N_i'}{dE'} = \frac{N_i}{dE}\frac{A-E}{A} = w_i\frac{a-q}{a}. $$
For every other ranking, its weight is unchanged. Since $A\ge E>0$, the denominator stays positive and the new numerators are nonnegative. Equality $A=E$ correctly produces zero transferred weights.

Let $D=dE$. Since total current weight is at most $1$,
$$ N_i\le D,\qquad A_c\le D. $$
Therefore an election update satisfies
$$ D'=DA\le D^2. $$
Eliminations do not enlarge the integers. At most $S-1$ election updates are needed, so all maintained integers have
$$ O(2^S b) $$
bits.

There are at most $m$ candidate-removal rounds. Recomputing tallies and selecting a candidate uses $O(\tau+m)$ arithmetic operations per round, and the ranking pointers advance at most $\tau m$ times overall. Hence both committees and their intersection can be computed in
$$ O\!\left(m(\tau+m)4^S b^2\right) $$
bit operations.

This proves polynomial-time solvability for fixed $S$. It does not establish polynomial time when $S$ varies.

3. New result: reduced fractions can require exponentially many bits

Proposition

For every $S\ge2$, there is an instance with $m=2S$, $\tau=S+1$, and input length
$$ L=O\!\left(S^2\log(S+1)\right) $$
such that, immediately before the final STV election, one current weight has a reduced denominator with at least $2^{S-2}$ bits.

The family can be restricted to $S+1$ being a power of two, so all input masses are dyadic rationals.

Construction

Let the candidates be
$$ c_1,\ldots,c_S,\ z,\ u_1,\ldots,u_{S-1}. $$
Every ranking has mass
$$ q=\frac1{S+1}. $$
There are $S+1$ rankings:

  • One distinguished ranking is

$$ c_1\succ c_2\succ\cdots\succ c_S\succ z \succ u_1\succ\cdots\succ u_{S-1}. $$

  • For each $j\in\{1,\ldots,S\}$, one ranking starts

$$ c_j\succ z, $$
then lists all other $c$-candidates, and finally all $u$-candidates, in any fixed orders.

These rankings are complete, strict, and distinct. Their masses sum to $1$.

Define integers
$$ y_0=1,\qquad y_{k+1}=y_k(y_k+1), $$
and weights
$$ \delta_k=\frac{q}{y_k}. $$

Proof of the election sequence

I prove by induction that after $k$ elections, for $0\le k\le S-1$:

  • The elected candidates are $c_1,\ldots,c_k$.
  • The distinguished ranking has weight $\delta_k$.
  • Candidate $c_{k+1}$ has tally $q+\delta_k$.
  • Every $c_j$ with $j>k+1$ has tally $q$.
  • Candidate $z$ has tally $q-\delta_k$.
  • Every $u_j$ has tally zero.

For $k=0$, the distinguished ranking and the first auxiliary ranking both support $c_1$. Thus $c_1$ has tally $2q$, every other $c_j$ has tally $q$, and $z$ and the $u$-candidates have zero tally. Since $\delta_0=q$, all assertions hold.

Suppose they hold after $k$ elections, with another transfer required. Candidate $c_{k+1}$ is the unique maximum-tally continuing candidate, with tally $q+\delta_k>q$. Its surplus factor is
$$ \frac{\delta_k}{q+\delta_k} = \frac1{y_k+1}. $$
The distinguished ranking therefore receives new weight
$$ \frac{\delta_k}{y_k+1} = \frac{q}{y_k(y_k+1)} = \delta_{k+1}, $$
and transfers to $c_{k+2}$.

The auxiliary ranking originally supporting $c_{k+1}$ still has weight $q$ before this election. Its transferred weight is
$$ \frac{q}{y_k+1} = \delta_k-\delta_{k+1}. $$
It transfers to $z$, whose new tally becomes
$$ q-\delta_k+\delta_k-\delta_{k+1} = q-\delta_{k+1}. $$
All other auxiliary rankings remain at their original candidates with weight $q$. This proves the induction step.

The terminal bulk-election rule never intervenes: after $k$ elections, the number of continuing candidates is $2S-k$, while the remaining vacancies number $S-k$. No elimination occurs, because a quota-eligible candidate is always present.

Thus STV elects
$$ \{c_1,\ldots,c_S\}. $$

Proof of the bit-length lower bound

We have $y_1=2$. For every $k\ge1$,
$$ y_{k+1}=y_k(y_k+1)\ge y_k^2. $$
Induction gives
$$ y_k\ge 2^{2^{k-1}}. $$

Immediately before the final election, the distinguished ranking has weight
$$ \delta_{S-1} = \frac1{(S+1)y_{S-1}}. $$
This fraction is already reduced, since its numerator is $1$. Its denominator therefore has at least
$$ \log_2 y_{S-1}\ge 2^{S-2} $$
bits.

Each input ranking lists $2S$ candidates, and there are $S+1$ rankings. Their encoding, together with the masses, has length $O(S^2\log(S+1))$. Hence the reduced denominator length is superpolynomial in the input length along this family.

What this proves—and what it leaves open

This rules out a general polynomial bound on the lengths of explicitly stored reduced current weights. Reducing every fraction after each operation does not repair that simulation approach.

It does not rule out a different polynomial-time committee algorithm. In this very family, the committee follows directly from the induction without expanding any $y_k$.

4. New refinement: which transfers require denominator squaring?

The generic update from Section 2 is unnecessary in two broader circumstances.

For a quota election, let $I$ be the indices of its positive-weight contributing rankings. Let $J$ be the indices of all currently positive-weight rankings. Define the current multiplier of ranking $i$ by
$$ g_i=\frac{w_i}{p_i}. $$

Call an election update exceptional if both:

  • $I$ is a proper subset of $J$;
  • the multipliers $g_i$, for $i\in I$, are not all equal.

Let $r$ be the number of exceptional updates performed before termination, omitting the final election’s transfer.

4.1 All positive weights contribute

Suppose $I=J$, and $k$ quota elections have already occurred. The elected tally is the total current weight,
$$ a=\frac{d-k}{d}. $$
Its surplus factor is
$$ \frac{d-k-1}{d-k}. $$
Thus the exact update can be represented by
$$ E'=E(d-k),\qquad N_i'=N_i(d-k-1) $$
for every ranking. Zero weights remain zero.

This enlarges the denominator by a factor at most $d$.

4.2 Contributing rankings share a multiplier

Suppose all $g_i$ for $i\in I$ equal a common value $\lambda$. Put
$$ H=\sum_{i\in I}h_i. $$
Then
$$ a=\sum_{i\in I}w_i = \lambda\frac{H}{E_0}, $$
so, for each $i\in I$,
$$ \frac{w_i}{a}=\frac{h_i}{H}. $$
Its new weight is therefore
$$ w_i' = w_i-q\frac{w_i}{a} = w_i-\frac{h_i}{dH}. $$
An exact integer update is
$$ E'=EH, $$
$$ N_i'= \begin{cases} N_iH-Eh_i,&i\in I,\\ N_iH,&i\notin I. \end{cases} $$
Indeed, dividing the first expression by $dEH$ gives precisely the displayed new weight. Nonnegativity follows from the quota condition and the original surplus formula.

Here $H\le E_0$, so denominator growth is again multiplication by an input-sized integer.

This case is detectable exactly. Since
$$ g_i=\frac{E_0N_i}{dEh_i}, $$
we have
$$ g_i=g_j \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N_i h_j=N_j h_i. $$

4.3 Refined bit-time bound

Use the two special updates whenever applicable and the generic update otherwise.

Let
$$ M=\max\{d,E_0\}. $$
For $D=dE$, a nonexceptional update satisfies
$$ D'\le DM, $$
while an exceptional update satisfies
$$ D'\le D^2. $$

Writing $t=\log_2D$, each nonexceptional update adds at most $\log_2M$, and each exceptional update doubles $t$. There are at most $S-1$ updates. Every additive increment is doubled at most $r$ times, so
$$ t\le 2^r\left(\log_2(dE_0)+(S-1)\log_2M\right). $$
Consequently, all maintained integers have
$$ O\!\left(2^r(S+1)b\right) $$
bits.

The extra multiplier comparisons use $O(\tau)$ integer multiplications per election. Combining this simulation with sequential RCV yields the bit-time bound
$$ O\!\left( m(\tau+m)4^r(S+1)^2b^2 \right). $$

Thus, for every fixed bound on $r$, both committees are computable in polynomial time on instances whose executions satisfy that bound. The algorithm detects the applicable cases itself; it does not require the election sequence in advance.

Initially every multiplier is $1$, so the first quota election is nonexceptional. In particular,
$$ r\le S-2. $$
The refined bound still does not become polynomial for unrestricted $r$.

5. Small support: polynomial computation, and a sharp intersection boundary

5.1 Support size at most two

When $\tau\le2$, every quota election either receives all positive weights or receives just one positive ranking. The former is covered by Section 4.1; the latter necessarily has a common multiplier. Thus $r=0$, and the refined algorithm is polynomial-time.

Moreover, the committees always intersect.

If all rankings have the same first choice $a$, that candidate wins the first IRV run and is elected by STV, unless the initial bulk-election rule already elects everyone.

Otherwise there are two distinct first choices, $a$ and $b$, with masses $u$ and $1-u$.

If $u\ne1/2$, the heavier first choice has a strict majority and wins the first IRV run. It is also STV’s unique initial maximum, with tally greater than $1/2\ge q$, and is elected first.

If $u=1/2$, IRV eliminates every zero-tally candidate before either $a$ or $b$. Its first winner is therefore one of them.

STV elects one of $a,b$ first, unless a bulk election already includes both. After that election, the other retains tally at least $1/2$, whereas every outside candidate has tally at most
$$ \frac12-q<\frac12. $$
The other first choice is consequently elected next, or included in a terminal bulk election. Since $S\ge2$, both first choices belong to the STV committee.

In every case, the first sequential RCV winner belongs to the STV committee.

5.2 Three rankings already permit disjoint committees

The intersection property cannot be extended to $\tau=3$, even for $S=2$ and $m=4$.

Consider the following support:

| Ranking | Mass |
|---|---:|
| $a\succ b\succ c\succ d$ | $9/32$ |
| $b\succ c\succ a\succ d$ | $10/32$ |
| $d\succ a\succ b\succ c$ | $13/32$ |

In the first IRV run, $c$ has zero tally and is eliminated. Candidate $a$, with tally $9/32$, is then the unique minimum. Its mass transfers to $b$, giving $b$ a strict majority of $19/32$.

For the second run, delete $b$ and reset eliminations. The initial positive tallies are $9/32$ for $a$, $10/32$ for $c$, and $13/32$ for $d$. Eliminating $a$ transfers its mass to $c$, which wins with $19/32$. Thus sequential RCV elects
$$ \{b,c\}. $$

For STV, $q=1/3$. Initially $d$ is the unique maximum, with tally $13/32>q$. Its surplus is
$$ \frac{13}{32}-\frac13=\frac7{96}. $$
That surplus transfers to $a$, whose tally becomes
$$ \frac9{32}+\frac7{96}=\frac{17}{48}. $$
This exceeds both $q=16/48$ and $b$’s tally $10/32=15/48$. Hence STV elects
$$ \{d,a\}. $$
The committees are disjoint, independently of the priority order.

Therefore three is the minimum support size permitting disjoint committees for $S=2$. This is an existence statement, not a hardness reduction.

6. General arithmetic-circuit upper bound

Define $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ to be the following decision problem: given a division-free integer straight-line program using constants $0,1$ and operations $+,-,\times$, decide whether its output is positive.

Store the integers in the generic STV simulation as arithmetic-circuit outputs, rather than expanding their binary representations.

The initial integers have polynomial bit length and can be constructed from $0,1$ using repeated doubling and addition. Every subsequent update uses only permitted operations. With shared subcircuits, at most $m$ rounds and $O(\tau+m)$ arithmetic operations per round produce polynomial-size circuits.

All comparisons can be answered by $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ queries. For example,
$$ A_c>A_{c'} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A_c-A_{c'}>0, $$
and
$$ A_c\ge E \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E-A_c\text{ is not positive}. $$
Equality and the fixed priority order can therefore be handled exactly.

Assignments, ranking pointers, elected sets, and eliminated sets require only polynomial storage. It follows that
$$ \text{committee computation}\in \mathsf{FP}^{\mathrm{PosSLP}}, $$
$$ \text{disjointness testing}\in \mathsf{P}^{\mathrm{PosSLP}}. $$

Allender, Bürgisser, Kjeldgaard-Pedersen, and Miltersen prove that $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ lies in the counting hierarchy, hence in $\mathsf{PSPACE}$. Their theorem applies because our queries are polynomial-size, division-free integer programs with the specified constants and operations. [On the Complexity of Numerical Analysis](https://people.cs.rutgers.edu/~allender/papers/slp.pdf).

Simulating the polynomially many adaptive oracle calls with reusable polynomial space gives polynomial-space algorithms for the requested outputs.

7. Unresolved classification

The unrestricted classification remains unproved here.

The new denominator construction eliminates one possible route to polynomial time: explicit reduced fractions do not always have polynomial bit length. It does not eliminate algorithms that compare succinct representations or exploit other properties of the count.

Conversely, neither exponentially large denominators nor membership in $\mathsf{P}^{\mathrm{PosSLP}}$ supplies an $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness reduction. Resolving the task requires a general polynomial bit-time algorithm or a valid hardness reduction for the specified output or disjointness problem.
