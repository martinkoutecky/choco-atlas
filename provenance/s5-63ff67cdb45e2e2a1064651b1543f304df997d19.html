Theorem 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each fixed \(d\ge3\), given rational planar sites \(P\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^2\) with rational masses \(\mu_p\ge0\) summing to \(1\), decide whether there are masses \(x_A\ge0\) on all size-\(d\) multisets \(A\) of sites such that \(\sum_A k_p(A)x_A=\mu_p\) for every \(p\in P\), and, for every proposed group profile \(B\), some \(p\) occurring in \(B\) has \(\sum_{A:c_p(A)>c_p(B)}k_p(A)x_A=0\), where \(k_p(A)\) counts occurrences of \(p\) and \(c_p(A)\) is the sum of distances from \(p\) to the other members of \(A\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix an integer \(d\ge3\). A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(P\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^2\) of \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_p\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length and the ambient dimension is \(m=2\).
Let \(\mathcal{G}_d(P)\) contain every size-\(d\) multiset of sites, including repeated sites. Write \(k_p(A)\) for the multiplicity of \(p\) in \(A\), and, for \(k_p(A)>0\), define \(c_p(A)=\sum_{q\in P}k_q(A)\lVert p-q\rVert_2\). Lower cost is preferred; equal costs remain tied.
An allocation consists of real group masses \(x_A\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal{G}_d(P)}k_p(A)x_A=\mu_p\) for every \(p\in P\).
It is stable if, for every \(B\in\mathcal{G}_d(P)\), there exists \(p\) with \(k_p(B)>0\) such that \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal{G}_d(P):\,k_p(A)>0,\,c_p(A)>c_p(B)}k_p(A)x_A=0\). Thus blocking requires positive mass of strictly improving participants of every type occurring in the proposed group.

For each fixed \(d\ge3\), is deciding whether such a stable allocation exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m=2\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), with dependence on \(d\) explicit.

For finite societies, deciding whether the agents can be partitioned into size-\(d\) groups with no group whose members all strictly improve their summed Euclidean distances is NP-complete for every fixed \(d\ge3\). Conclusion

The decision problem as defined is in \(\mathrm{P}\): every valid input has a unique stable allocation. Allowing repeated sites makes the homogeneous group consisting of \(d\) copies of one type available both as an allocation and as a blocking group.

This is not a special case of the input: it settles the entire stated problem.

Construction and stability

For each \(p\in P\), let \(A_p\) denote the multiset containing \(d\) copies of \(p\). Define
\[ x_{A_p}=\frac{\mu_p}{d}, \qquad x_A=0\quad\text{for every other }A. \]

For each type \(p\), the allocated mass is \(k_p(A_p)x_{A_p}=d(\mu_p/d)=\mu_p\), so the allocation is feasible.

Moreover, \(c_p(A_p)=0\). Every Euclidean distance is nonnegative, so for any proposed group \(B\) and any \(p\) occurring in \(B\), we have \(c_p(B)\ge0\). The only positive-mass group containing \(p\) is \(A_p\), whose cost is therefore not strictly greater than \(c_p(B)\). Consequently,
\[ \sum_{\substack{A:\,k_p(A)>0\\c_p(A)>c_p(B)}}k_p(A)x_A=0. \]
Thus the stability condition holds for every \(B\).

Uniqueness

Let \(x\) be any stable allocation. Fix \(p\in P\) and apply stability to \(B=A_p\). Since \(p\) is its only occurring type and \(c_p(A_p)=0\), stability requires
\[ \sum_{\substack{A:\,k_p(A)>0\\c_p(A)>0}}k_p(A)x_A=0. \]

Every summand is nonnegative. Hence every group \(A\) containing \(p\) with positive cost for \(p\) must have \(x_A=0\).

Now suppose \(A\) contains two distinct types \(p\) and \(q\). Because the sites are distinct, \(\lVert p-q\rVert_2>0\), and therefore
\[ c_p(A)\ge k_q(A)\lVert p-q\rVert_2>0. \]
The preceding equation forces \(x_A=0\). Thus every mixed group has zero mass.

Only the homogeneous groups remain, and feasibility forces \(dx_{A_p}=\mu_p\) for every \(p\). This proves uniqueness.

Running time and the finite-agent result

On the promised valid inputs, the decision algorithm simply returns YES. It takes constant time without reading the input, or \(\mathrm{O}(L)\) time if input reading is included.

A sparse representation of the unique allocation uses \(\tau\) entries. Represent each group by its type index and multiplicity \(d\). If \(\mu_p=a_p/b_p\), its group mass can be output exactly as \(a_p/(db_p)\); reduction to lowest terms is unnecessary. With ordinary binary multiplication, construction takes
\[ \mathrm{O}\!\left(L(1+\log d)+\tau\log(\tau+1)\right) \]
bit operations. For fixed \(d\ge3\), this is \(\mathrm{O}(L+\tau\log(\tau+1))\). No distance computations are needed in ambient dimension \(m=2\); the proof also works in every Euclidean dimension.

The cited [finite-agent hardness theorem](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol244-esa2022/LIPIcs.ESA.2022.36/LIPIcs.ESA.2022.36.pdf) concerns partitions into groups of distinct agents. It does not transfer to this model: here, every positive-mass type can supply a homogeneous group of arbitrarily small positive group mass. Such groups both provide the stable allocation and rule out every allocation using mixed groups.

2. Stability

The equality \(c_p(A_p)=0\) follows directly from the definition and \(\lVert p-p\rVert_2=0\).

For any proposed group \(B\) and any occurring type \(p\), every term defining \(c_p(B)\) is nonnegative. Hence \(c_p(B)\ge0\). The only positive-mass allocated group containing \(p\) therefore cannot satisfy \(c_p(A)>c_p(B)\).

Every summand in the stability expression is consequently zero. The prover establishes this for every occurring type, which is stronger than the required existence of one such type.

This reasoning respects strict improvement: equality of costs does not block. Every proposed group has an occurring type because its size is \(d\ge3\).
