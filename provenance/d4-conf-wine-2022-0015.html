Theorem 5 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite rational type set \(T\subseteq[0,L]\) and rational mass vector \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), determine a rule \(F\) with \(F(\mu)\in[0,L]\) that satisfies continuum PF for every measurable group \(A\), namely \(\lvert F(\mu)-x\rvert\le L(1-\mu(A))+r(A)\) for all \(x\in A\), where \(r(A)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in A}x-\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x\in A}x\). Minimize the worst-case welfare ratio \(\rho(F)=\sup_\mu\frac{\max_{y\in[0,L]}\Phi_\mu(y)}{\Phi_\mu(F(\mu))}\), where \(\Phi_\mu(y)=\int(L-\lvert y-x\rvert)\,d\mu(x)\), and compute \(F(\mu)\) exactly.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(\ell>0\) be a binary rational interval length; there is one facility, so \(m=1\). A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\subseteq[0,\ell]\cap\mathbb{Q}\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_x\), where \(\sum_{x\in T}\mu_x=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A deterministic rule \(F\) assigns a location \(F(\mu)\in[0,\ell]\) to every such society. It satisfies continuum proportional fairness (PF) if, for every nonempty measurable group \(A\subseteq T\) and every \(x\in A\), \(|F(\mu)-x|\le\ell(1-\mu(A))+r(A)\), where \(\mu(A)=\sum_{x\in A}\mu_x\) and \(r(A)=\max A-\min A\). This includes \(A=T\).
Define \(\Phi_\mu(y)=\sum_{x\in T}\mu_x(\ell-|y-x|)\) and \(\rho(F)=\sup_\mu\frac{\max_{y\in[0,\ell]}\Phi_\mu(y)}{\Phi_\mu(F(\mu))}\), where the supremum ranges over all finite rational supports and positive rational mass vectors on the fixed interval. The admissible rules are all deterministic continuum-PF rules.

What is the optimal worst-case welfare approximation ratio \(\inf_F\rho(F)\), and can an attaining rule compute \(F(\mu)\) exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For \(n\ge2\) equally weighted agents, the rule returning the median of their locations and the phantoms \(j\ell/n\), \(1\le j<n\), is optimal among all PF rules, with ratio \(\max_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,0\le k\le n/2}\frac{n(n-k)}{k^2+(n-k)^2}\); these ratios converge to \((\sqrt{2}+1)/2\). [Discrete approximation result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.01566v3).
No result addressing exactly this mass-input PF approximation problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Renamed the interval length to ell, required it to be a positive binary rational, and reserved L for total input bit length; m=1 denotes the number of facilities.
  • Interpreted measurable groups as nonempty subsets of the positive-mass support, excluding null types and the empty group; their essential extrema are their ordinary extrema.
  • Made the worst-case domain all finite rational supports and positive rational probability vectors on the fixed interval, rather than mass vectors on a single prescribed support.
  • Allowed singleton supports, since a unanimous discrete population can have only one distinct type.
  • Used all deterministic PF rules as the comparison class, without imposing strategyproofness, matching the anchor's approximation comparison.

Result

The optimal worst-case ratio is \(\displaystyle \inf_F\rho(F)=\frac{\sqrt2+1}{2}\).

An attaining deterministic PF rule can compute its location exactly in time polynomial in \(\tau,L\), hence in \(m,\tau,L\) for the prescribed \(m=1\). In fact, the rule below maximizes welfare among all PF-feasible locations on every input.

1. Normalize the interval

Set \(a=x/\ell\) and \(z=y/\ell\). Then the interval becomes \([0,1]\), PF becomes

\(\displaystyle |z-a|\le 1-\mu(A)+r(A),\)

and welfare becomes \(\Phi_\mu(\ell z)=\ell W_\mu(z)\), where

\(\displaystyle W_\mu(z)=\sum_a\mu_a(1-|z-a|).\)

Thus normalization preserves welfare ratios and rationality. We work on \([0,1]\) and rescale the output by \(\ell\).

Write the distinct normalized support locations as \(a_1<\cdots<a_\tau\), their masses as \(w_1,\ldots,w_\tau\), and their cumulative masses as

\(\displaystyle S_0=0,\qquad S_i=\sum_{j=1}^i w_j.\)

2. Exact characterization of PF locations

Lemma. The PF-feasible locations form the interval \([\alpha,\beta]\), where

\(\displaystyle \alpha=\max\left\{0,\max_{1\le i\le\tau}(a_i-S_{i-1})\right\},\qquad \beta=\min\left\{1,\min_{1\le i\le\tau}(a_i+1-S_i)\right\}.\)

Proof. For a nonempty group \(A\), let \(a=\min A\), \(b=\max A\), and \(w=\mu(A)\). Its PF constraints are

\(\displaystyle |z-x|\le 1-w+b-a\quad\text{for every }x\in A.\)

The strongest lower bound comes from \(x=b\), and the strongest upper bound from \(x=a\). Consequently, these constraints are equivalent to

\(\displaystyle a+w-1\le z\le b+1-w.\)

Among groups whose minimum is \(a_i\), the largest mass is \(1-S_{i-1}\), attained by the entire suffix \(\{a_i,\ldots,a_\tau\}\). Hence the strongest lower bound with that minimum is \(a_i-S_{i-1}\).

Among groups whose maximum is \(a_i\), the largest mass is \(S_i\), attained by the entire prefix \(\{a_1,\ldots,a_i\}\). Hence the strongest upper bound with that maximum is \(a_i+1-S_i\).

Intersecting these bounds with \([0,1]\) gives exactly the stated interval. \(\square\)

This interval is always nonempty. Indeed, the mean \(\bar z=\sum_iw_i a_i\) satisfies PF: for any group \(A\) and \(x\in A\), the triangle inequality gives

\(\displaystyle |\bar z-x| \le\sum_iw_i|a_i-x| \le\mu(A)r(A)+(1-\mu(A)) \le r(A)+1-\mu(A). \)

Therefore \(\alpha\le\bar z\le\beta\).

Also, singleton PF constraints imply that every PF-feasible \(z\) has positive welfare:

\(\displaystyle W_\mu(z)\ge\sum_iw_i^2>0.\)

Thus all ratios used below are well-defined.

3. An exact polynomial-time rule

Define the candidate set

\(\displaystyle C_\mu=\{\alpha,\beta\}\cup\{a_i:\alpha\le a_i\le\beta\}.\)

Let \(z^\star\) be the smallest candidate maximizing \(W_\mu\), and return \(F^\star(\mu)=\ell z^\star\).

This rule maximizes welfare over the entire PF interval. To see this, \(W_\mu\) is continuous and affine between consecutive support locations. Partition \([\alpha,\beta]\) at its support locations. On each resulting interval, an affine function attains its maximum at an endpoint. All such endpoints belong to \(C_\mu\).

The algorithm sorts the support, computes cumulative masses and the two interval endpoints, and evaluates welfare at at most \(\tau+2\) candidates. It uses \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) rational comparisons and \(O(\tau^2)\) rational arithmetic operations.

All calculations involve rational sums, differences, products, and comparisons of polynomial bit length: a common denominator can be formed by multiplying input denominators, whose combined bit length is bounded by the input length. Consequently, the algorithm runs in polynomial bit time and returns an exact rational location.

4. Transfer of the discrete upper bound

We use the supplied finite-agent result: for \(N\ge2\), the uniform phantom median satisfies PF and has welfare ratio

\(\displaystyle R_N=\max_{\substack{k\in\mathbb Z\\0\le k\le N/2}} \frac{N(N-k)}{k^2+(N-k)^2}. \)

These are Proposition 3 and Theorem 5 of the [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.01566v3).

Fix any rational-mass society. Choose a common denominator \(N\ge2\), so that \(n_i=Nw_i\) is a positive integer for every \(i\). Form an auxiliary finite-agent profile containing \(n_i\) agents at \(a_i\), and let \(u_N\) be its uniform phantom outcome.

For every support group \(A\), take the agent group consisting of all copies of its types. This group has relative size \(\mu(A)\) and range \(r(A)\). Its discrete PF inequalities are exactly the mass-input PF inequalities for \(A\). Hence \(u_N\in[\alpha,\beta]\).

The auxiliary profile’s total welfare at any \(z\) is \(NW_\mu(z)\). Its welfare ratio therefore equals the normalized mass-input ratio. Since \(z^\star\) maximizes welfare over the PF interval,

\(\displaystyle \frac{\max_zW_\mu(z)}{W_\mu(z^\star)} \le \frac{\max_zW_\mu(z)}{W_\mu(u_N)} \le R_N. \)

This replication is only a proof device; the algorithm in Section 3 never constructs the \(N\) agents or phantoms.

To bound \(R_N\), define

\(\displaystyle f(q)=\frac{1-q}{q^2+(1-q)^2}\qquad(0\le q\le\tfrac12).\)

Then \(R_N=\max_k f(k/N)\), and differentiation gives

\(\displaystyle f'(q)=\frac{1-4q+2q^2}{(q^2+(1-q)^2)^2}. \)

The numerator changes from positive to negative at \(q_\star=1-1/\sqrt2\). Thus

\(\displaystyle R_N\le f(q_\star)=\frac{\sqrt2+1}{2}. \)

Since the society was arbitrary, \(\rho(F^\star)\le(\sqrt2+1)/2\).

5. Matching lower bound for every PF rule

For any rational \(q\in(0,\tfrac12]\), consider the two-type society with mass \(1-q\) at \(0\) and mass \(q\) at \(1\).

Singleton PF for the type at \(0\) requires \(z\le q\). Singleton PF for the type at \(1\) requires \(z\ge q\). Therefore every PF rule must return \(z=q\).

For this society,

\(\displaystyle W_\mu(z)=(1-q)(1-z)+qz=1-q+(2q-1)z. \)

Because \(q\le\tfrac12\), its unrestricted maximum is \(1-q\), attained at \(z=0\). At the forced PF location,

\(\displaystyle W_\mu(q)=(1-q)^2+q^2.\)

Thus every PF rule has ratio at least \(f(q)\) for every such rational \(q\). Rational numbers in this interval approach \(q_\star=1-1/\sqrt2\), and continuity yields

\(\displaystyle \rho(F)\ge f(q_\star)=\frac{\sqrt2+1}{2}.\)

Together with Section 4, this proves the claimed optimum and its attainment by the exact polynomial-time rule \(F^\star\). The lower bound uses a supremum over rational societies; an irrational input mass is not required.
