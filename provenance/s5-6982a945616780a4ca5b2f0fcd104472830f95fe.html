Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) be a set of \(m\ge1\) divisible click types. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) complete campaign types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Each type specifies binary-rational per-campaign budget \(b_t>0\), ROI target \(r_t>0\), and values \(v_{tj}\ge0\); per-capita supplies \(S_j>0\) are binary rationals. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An equilibrium consists of uniform prices \(\alpha_j\ge0\), per-campaign allocations \(x_{tj}\ge0\), and outside-option quantities \(z_t\ge0\). The outside option has unlimited supply and costs and yields utility \(r_t\) per unit.
For every \(t\), the bundle \((x_t,z_t)\) maximizes \(W_t=\sum_j(v_{tj}/r_t)x_{tj}+r_tz_t\) over all nonnegative bundles subject to \(\sum_j\alpha_jx_{tj}+r_tz_t\le b_t\); individual demand has no supply caps.
Require \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}\le S_j\), with equality whenever \(\alpha_j>0\). Any utility-maximizing division at indifference is allowed. Define the pacing multiplier \(q_t=b_t/W_t\in(0,1]\).

Can such an equilibrium and its pacing multipliers be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computation NP-hard?

For a finite list of campaigns, pacing multipliers are computable directly from an augmented Eisenberg–Gale optimum, without running auctions. Exact polynomial-time computation for rational finite first-price pacing markets is also established by [Conitzer et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.07166).
On the continuous side, [Gao and Kroer](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16684) establish equilibrium and approximation results for finitely many buyers and a continuum of goods; that result does not address the supported distribution of campaigns specified here.

  • Restored the discrete model's domains: strictly positive budgets, ROI targets, and supplies, and nonnegative values; click and supported campaign sets are nonempty.
  • Specified nonnegative divisible allocations and an unlimited personalized outside option, with no individual supply caps in the demand problem.
  • Allowed any utility-maximizing allocation at indifference, consistent with the demand correspondence and divisible auction ties.
  • Interpreted computation as exact computation and defined L as the total binary input bit length.

Result

An equilibrium and its pacing multipliers are exactly computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The key is to represent each supported campaign type by one finite bidder, scaling its budget by its mass. No expansion of rational masses into individual campaigns is required.

1. Construct a finite pacing market

Write \(a_{tj}=v_{tj}/r_t\). Construct a finite market with \(\tau\) bidders and \(m\) divisible goods, each having unit supply. Give bidder \(t\) budget and valuations

\(B_t=\mu_t b_t,\qquad V_{tj}=S_j a_{tj}=S_jv_{tj}/r_t.\)

All budgets are positive rationals, and all valuations are nonnegative rationals.

We use the following established result: a finite first-price pacing market with positive rational budgets, nonnegative rational valuations, and unit supplies admits an equilibrium computable exactly in polynomial time. The augmented Eisenberg–Gale characterization and its exact rational computation guarantee are given in Theorem 3 and the following computational discussion of [Conitzer et al., Section 5, pp. 21–23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.07166).

Let its computed pacing multipliers, allocations, and prices be \(\beta_t,y_{tj},p_j\). Thus:

  • \(0\leq\beta_t\leq1\), and \(p_j=\max_t\beta_tV_{tj}\).
  • \(y_{tj}\geq0\), and \(y_{tj}>0\) implies \(p_j=\beta_tV_{tj}\).
  • \(C_t:=\sum_jp_jy_{tj}\leq B_t\).
  • \(\sum_ty_{tj}\leq1\), with equality whenever \(p_j>0\).
  • \(C_t<B_t\) implies \(\beta_t=1\).

These are the finite first-price pacing equilibrium conditions.

Every \(\beta_t\) is strictly positive. Indeed, if \(\beta_t=0\), condition 2 makes every allocated good cost zero, so \(C_t=0<B_t\). Condition 5 would then require \(\beta_t=1\), a contradiction.

Define the residual budget

\(\delta_t=B_t-C_t\geq0.\)

Condition 5 gives

\(\delta_t>0\Longrightarrow\beta_t=1,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\beta_t\delta_t=\delta_t.\)

2. Recover per-campaign allocations and prices

Set

\(\alpha_j=p_j/S_j,\qquad x_{tj}=S_jy_{tj}/\mu_t,\qquad z_t=\delta_t/(\mu_t r_t).\)

These quantities are well-defined because \(S_j,\mu_t,r_t\) are strictly positive.

For each type, the recovered bundle exhausts its per-campaign budget:

\(\sum_j\alpha_jx_{tj}+r_tz_t =\frac{\sum_jp_jy_{tj}+\delta_t}{\mu_t} =\frac{B_t}{\mu_t} =b_t.\)

Supply feasibility follows directly:

\(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}=S_j\sum_ty_{tj}\leq S_j.\)

If \(\alpha_j>0\), then \(p_j>0\), so the finite market clears good \(j\). Consequently \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}=S_j\), as required.

It remains to prove individual utility maximization under the task’s demand definition.

3. Verify demand without individual supply caps

The finite price formula implies, for every \(t,j\),

\(\alpha_j=\frac{p_j}{S_j}\geq\frac{\beta_tV_{tj}}{S_j}=\beta_ta_{tj}.\)

Moreover, \(x_{tj}>0\) implies \(y_{tj}>0\), and hence equality:

\(x_{tj}>0\Longrightarrow\alpha_j=\beta_ta_{tj}.\)

The utility of the recovered bundle is

\(W_t=\sum_ja_{tj}x_{tj}+r_tz_t =\frac{\sum_jV_{tj}y_{tj}+\delta_t}{\mu_t}.\)

On every positive allocation, \(\beta_tV_{tj}=p_j\). Together with \(\beta_t\delta_t=\delta_t\), this yields

\(\beta_tW_t =\frac{\sum_j\beta_tV_{tj}y_{tj}+\beta_t\delta_t}{\mu_t} =\frac{C_t+\delta_t}{\mu_t} =b_t.\)

Now take any nonnegative alternative bundle \((\widetilde x_t,\widetilde z_t)\) satisfying the individual budget constraint. We impose no supply bounds on this alternative. Using \(\beta_ta_{tj}\leq\alpha_j\) and \(\beta_t\leq1\),

\(\beta_t\left(\sum_ja_{tj}\widetilde x_{tj}+r_t\widetilde z_t\right) \leq\sum_j\alpha_j\widetilde x_{tj}+r_t\widetilde z_t \leq b_t.\)

Since \(\beta_t>0\), every feasible alternative has utility at most \(b_t/\beta_t\). The recovered bundle attains exactly that value. It is therefore utility-maximizing under precisely the task’s uncapped individual demand definition.

Finally,

\(q_t=\frac{b_t}{W_t}=\beta_t\in(0,1].\)

Thus the recovered quantities form the requested equilibrium, and the finite market’s pacing multipliers are exactly its per-campaign pacing multipliers.

4. Exact computation and bit complexity

The constructed market has exactly \(\tau\) bidders and \(m\) goods. Each \(B_t\) and \(V_{tj}\) is obtained from input rationals through a constant number of multiplications and divisions. Its rational encoding therefore has bit length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Apply the cited exact polynomial-time finite-market algorithm to this constructed instance. The recovery formulas then use only rational arithmetic, with polynomial bit complexity. In particular, dividing by a small mass \(\mu_t\) does not require enumerating campaigns or expanding its denominator.

For completeness, the finite computation can use the augmented Eisenberg–Gale program

\(\max_{y,\delta\geq0,\ U>0}\left\{\sum_tB_t\log U_t-\sum_t\delta_t\right\},\)

subject to

\(U_t\leq\sum_jV_{tj}y_{tj}+\delta_t\quad\text{for every }t,\qquad \sum_ty_{tj}\leq1\quad\text{for every }j.\)

The cited finite-market theorem supplies the exact computation guarantee for this program. The reduction above verifies all its market hypotheses and proves that its output solves the supported-distribution problem.
