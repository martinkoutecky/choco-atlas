Theorem 1 (NP-hardness of IP-stable clustering) — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a rational metric \(d\) on \(T\), and \(k\), can \(T\) be partitioned into \(k\) positive-mass clusters \(T_1,\ldots,T_k\), with each type assigned wholly to one cluster, so that for every \(t\in T_i\) and \(j\ne i\), \(\frac{\sum_{s\in T_i}\mu_s d(t,s)}{M_i}\leq\frac{\sum_{s\in T_j}\mu_s d(t,s)}{M_j}\), where \(M_i=\sum_{s\in T_i}\mu_s\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains a rational metric \(d\) on \(T\), encoded by its distance matrix, and an integer \(1\leq k\leq\tau\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(\tau,k,L\). There is no additional size parameter \(m\).
Each type represents a continuum of individuals of that type; each individual has zero mass. A clustering partitions \(T\) into exactly \(k\) nonempty sets \(T_1,\ldots,T_k\), assigning each type wholly to one cluster. Write \(M_i=\sum_{s\in T_i}\mu_s\).
A clustering is stable if, for every \(t\in T_i\) and every \(j\ne i\), \(\frac{\sum_{s\in T_i}\mu_s d(t,s)}{M_i}\leq\frac{\sum_{s\in T_j}\mu_s d(t,s)}{M_j}\). Same-type mass remains in the denominator; ties satisfy stability.

Is deciding whether such a stable clustering exists solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

For finite individuals, excluding the individual itself from its own-cluster average and declaring singleton clusters stable, existence is NP-hard even for \(k=2\) and metric distances ([Ahmadi et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/ahmadi22a/ahmadi22a.pdf)).
A related distributional framework studies conditional average distances with a separation factor \(\gamma>1\) and minimum cluster mass \(\alpha>0\) ([Daniely, Linial, and Saks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.4891)); those additional conditions differ from the problem here. No result settling this exact mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The range of k was unspecified; set it to integers from 1 through the support size, as required for a partition into nonempty clusters.
  • Clarified that a positive type mass represents a continuum of zero-mass individuals: excluding one individual leaves the own-cluster denominator unchanged, preserving the graded inequality.
  • Specified distance-matrix encoding and total input bit length L. This metric-support problem has no independent parameter m.

Partial results for the exact mass model

The polynomial-time versus $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness classification remains unresolved in this write-up. I prove a new sufficient condition for stable two-cluster partitions that applies to general metrics. For equal masses, a slightly stronger condition gives a polynomial-time construction.

The condition depends on how much the types’ average distances to the whole society vary. In particular, if those averages are all equal, a stable two-cluster partition always exists for arbitrary positive masses.

1. Definitions and exact verification

For a nonempty subset $C\subseteq T$, define
$$ M(C)=\sum_{s\in C}\mu_s, \qquad A(t,C)=\frac{\sum_{s\in C}\mu_s d(t,s)}{M(C)}. $$
These definitions include the mass $\mu_t$ in the denominator when $t\in C$.

The cases $k=1$ and $k=\tau$ always admit stable clusterings: stability is respectively vacuous, or every own-cluster average is zero.

For general $k$, a certificate assigns each type a cluster label. Verify that every label occurs, and compute
$$ M_i=\sum_{s\in T_i}\mu_s, \qquad S_{tj}=\sum_{s\in T_j}\mu_s d(t,s). $$
Since every $M_i$ is positive, stability is equivalent to
$$ S_{ti}M_j\leq S_{tj}M_i \qquad (t\in T_i,\ j\ne i). $$

Computing the sums and checking the inequalities requires $O(\tau^2+\tau k)$ arithmetic operations. Common denominators for all input masses and distances have $O(L)$ bits: their bit lengths are bounded by the sum of the input denominators’ bit lengths. Clearing these denominators leaves all verification quantities with $O(L)$ bits. Schoolbook arithmetic therefore gives the verification bound
$$ O\bigl((\tau^2+\tau k)L^2\bigr). $$
The certificate has $O(\tau\log k)$ bits. Thus the decision problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

Exhaustive enumeration also gives an exact algorithm with running time
$$ O\bigl(k^\tau(\tau^2+\tau k)L^2\bigr). $$

2. Global average distances and a centered potential

Throughout the remaining sections, assume $\tau\geq2$ and consider $k=2$.

Define each type’s global average distance, and their population average, by
$$ b_t=\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s d(t,s), \qquad \beta=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_t. $$
Define the following quantities, all determined by the input:
$$ h_t=2b_t-\beta, \qquad h_{\min}=\min_{t\in T}h_t, \qquad \mu_{\min}=\min_{t\in T}\mu_t, \qquad R=\max_{t\in T}b_t-\min_{t\in T}b_t. $$

Lemma 1: the metric implies a positive diagonal correction

For every type $t$,
$$ h_t>0. $$

Indeed, because the masses sum to $1$,
$$ h_t= \sum_{s,u\in T}\mu_s\mu_u \bigl(d(t,s)+d(t,u)-d(s,u)\bigr). $$
Every summand is nonnegative by the triangle inequality. Choose any $s\ne t$. The summand with $u=s$ is
$$ 2\mu_s^2d(t,s)>0, $$
because the masses are positive and $d$ is a metric. Consequently $h_t>0$.

In particular, $h_{\min}>0$ and $\beta>0$.

Definition of the potential

For any subset $A\subseteq T$, including the empty and full subsets, put
$$ x=M(A), \qquad u_A(s)=\mu_s\bigl(\mathbf 1_A(s)-x\bigr). $$
Define
$$ F(A)=\sum_{s,u\in T}u_A(s)u_A(u)d(s,u). $$

For calculations, also define
$$ W_A=\sum_{s,u\in A}\mu_s\mu_u d(s,u), \qquad B_A=\sum_{s\in A}\mu_s b_s. $$
Expanding the definition gives
$$ F(A)=W_A-2xB_A+\beta x^2. $$

If $B=T\setminus A$ and $y=1-x$, then $u_B=-u_A$, so
$$ F(B)=F(A). $$
Moreover,
$$ F(\varnothing)=F(T)=0, \qquad F(\{t\})=-\mu_t^2h_t<0. $$
Therefore every global minimizer of $F$ is a nonempty proper subset.

Lemma 2: a uniform lower bound

For every $A\subseteq T$,
$$ F(A)\geq-\frac{\beta}{4}. $$

To prove this, let
$$ W_{AB}=\sum_{s\in A,\ u\in B}\mu_s\mu_u d(s,u). $$
The centered-vector definition gives
$$ F(A)=y^2W_A+x^2W_B-2xyW_{AB}. $$
All three distance sums are nonnegative, and
$$ \beta=W_A+W_B+2W_{AB}. $$
Thus
$$ F(A)\geq-2xyW_{AB}\geq-xy\beta\geq-\frac{\beta}{4}. $$

3. Exact relations between potential changes and stability

Fix a nonempty proper subset $A$, with complement $B$, and write $x=M(A)$ and $y=M(B)$.

Define
$$ S_t(A)=\sum_{s\in A}\mu_s d(t,s), \qquad g_t(A)=S_t(A)-xb_t, \qquad c(A)=B_A-x\beta. $$

Since $S_t(B)=b_t-S_t(A)$,
$$ A(t,A)-A(t,B) = \frac{S_t(A)}x-\frac{b_t-S_t(A)}y = \frac{g_t(A)}{xy}. $$
Consequently:

  • A type $t\in A$ is stable precisely when $g_t(A)\leq0$.
  • A type $t\in B$ is stable precisely when $g_t(A)\geq0$.

These conditions compare the existing clusters, exactly as required in the task.

Lemma 3: exact change formulas

Let $w=\mu_t$. If $t\notin A$, then
$$ F(A\cup\{t\})-F(A) = 2w\bigl(g_t(A)-c(A)\bigr)-w^2h_t. $$
If $t\in A$, then
$$ F(A\setminus\{t\})-F(A) = -2w\bigl(g_t(A)-c(A)\bigr)-w^2h_t. $$

Here is a direct derivation. Regard $\mu$ as the vector of masses and let $e_t$ be the coordinate vector for type $t$. Adding $t$ changes the centered vector by
$$ u_{A\cup\{t\}}=u_A+w(e_t-\mu). $$
Writing $D$ for the distance matrix, the cross term is
$$ u_A^{\mathsf T}D(e_t-\mu)=g_t(A)-c(A). $$
The quadratic term is
$$ (e_t-\mu)^{\mathsf T}D(e_t-\mu) = d(t,t)-2b_t+\beta=-h_t. $$
Expansion proves the addition formula. Removing $t$ changes the vector by $-w(e_t-\mu)$ and proves the removal formula.

Lemma 4: inequalities at a global minimum

If $A$ globally minimizes $F$, then
$$ g_t(A)\leq c(A)-\frac{\mu_t h_t}{2} \qquad(t\in A), $$
and
$$ g_t(A)\geq c(A)+\frac{\mu_t h_t}{2} \qquad(t\in B). $$

Every addition or removal has nonnegative potential change at a global minimum. Substituting the formulas in Lemma 3 and dividing by $2\mu_t>0$ gives the stated inequalities.

In particular, at least one entire cluster of such a partition is strictly stable. If $c(A)\leq0$, every type in $A$ has $g_t(A)<0$. If $c(A)\geq0$, every type in $B$ has $g_t(A)>0$.

The remaining obstruction to full stability is the offset $c(A)$.

4. A sufficient condition for existence

The offset has a useful bound. Define
$$ \overline b_A=\frac{B_A}{x}, \qquad \overline b_B=\frac{B_B}{y}. $$
Since $\beta=B_A+B_B$,
$$ c(A)=xy\bigl(\overline b_A-\overline b_B\bigr). $$
Both cluster averages lie between the minimum and maximum of the numbers $b_t$. Hence
$$ |c(A)|\leq xyR\leq\frac R4. $$

Theorem 1: existence when global averages vary little

If
$$ R\leq2\mu_{\min}h_{\min}, $$
then a stable two-cluster partition exists.

Proof. Choose a global minimizer $A$ of $F$. Section 2 shows that $A$ and its complement are nonempty.

For $t\in A$, Lemma 4 gives
$$ g_t(A) \leq c(A)-\frac{\mu_t h_t}{2} \leq\frac R4-\frac{\mu_{\min}h_{\min}}2 \leq0. $$
For $t\in B$, it gives
$$ g_t(A) \geq c(A)+\frac{\mu_t h_t}{2} \geq-\frac R4+\frac{\mu_{\min}h_{\min}}2 \geq0. $$
These are precisely the stability conditions from Section 3. Equality is permitted because ties satisfy stability. $\square$

Corollary: constant global averages

If
$$ b_t=\beta\qquad\text{for every }t\in T, $$
then a stable two-cluster partition exists for every positive mass vector satisfying this condition.

Here $R=0$, so Theorem 1 applies. Lemma 4 additionally shows that a global minimizer gives strict stability against the other cluster.

With equal masses, this hypothesis says that every row of the distance matrix has the same sum. For example, shortest-path metrics on cycles satisfy it: cyclic rotation preserves distances and maps any row to any other row. Thus the condition includes metrics with cyclic geometry.

5. Polynomial construction for equal masses

A stronger version of the sufficient condition permits a direct improvement algorithm.

Theorem 2: construction under a stronger condition

Suppose
$$ R\leq\mu_{\min}h_{\min}. $$
Start with any singleton $A$. Whenever a type is unstable, move its whole type mass to the other cluster. This process terminates at a stable two-cluster partition after fewer than $\mu_{\min}^{-2}$ moves.

If all masses equal $1/\tau$, checking the condition and constructing the partition takes
$$ O(\tau^3L^2) $$
bit operations.

Proof of potential decrease. Initially,
$$ F(A)<0. $$
Suppose $t\in A$ is unstable. Then $g_t(A)>0$. By Lemma 3 and the offset bound,
$$ \begin{aligned} F(A\setminus\{t\})-F(A) &=-2w g_t(A)+2w c(A)-w^2h_t\\ &\leq-2w g_t(A)+\frac{wR}{2}-w^2h_t. \end{aligned} $$
The hypothesis implies
$$ R\leq\mu_{\min}h_{\min}\leq wh_t. $$
Therefore
$$ F(A\setminus\{t\})-F(A) < -\frac{w^2h_t}{2} \leq-\frac{\mu_{\min}^2h_{\min}}2. $$

If $t\in B$ is unstable, then $g_t(A)<0$. The addition formula similarly gives
$$ \begin{aligned} F(A\cup\{t\})-F(A) &=2w g_t(A)-2w c(A)-w^2h_t\\ &\leq2w g_t(A)+\frac{wR}{2}-w^2h_t\\ &<-\frac{\mu_{\min}^2h_{\min}}2. \end{aligned} $$

Thus every move strictly decreases $F$ by the stated amount. Since $F$ remains negative, neither cluster can become empty: an empty or full $A$ would have potential zero.

Proof of the move bound. Lemma 2 bounds the potential below by $-\beta/4$. Hence the number $N$ of moves satisfies
$$ N<\frac{\beta}{2\mu_{\min}^2h_{\min}}. $$
Let $b_{\min}=\min_t b_t$. Since $\beta$ is a weighted average of the $b_t$,
$$ \beta-h_{\min} = 2(\beta-b_{\min}) \leq2R \leq2\mu_{\min}h_{\min}. $$
As $\tau\geq2$, we have $\mu_{\min}\leq1/2$. Consequently
$$ \beta\leq(1+2\mu_{\min})h_{\min}\leq2h_{\min}, $$
which proves
$$ N<\mu_{\min}^{-2}. $$
At termination no type is unstable, so the resulting partition is stable.

Proof of running time. Compute all $b_t$, $\beta$, and the hypothesis in $O(\tau^2)$ arithmetic operations. Maintain $x$ and the vector $S_t(A)$. Moving type $t$ changes them by
$$ x\longmapsto x\pm\mu_t, \qquad S_s(A)\longmapsto S_s(A)\pm\mu_t d(s,t). $$
Updating the vector and scanning for another unstable type each require $O(\tau)$ arithmetic operations.

For equal masses, the move bound is $N<\tau^2$, giving $O(\tau^3)$ arithmetic operations overall. All maintained quantities are sums and products of input rationals with common denominators of $O(L)$ bits. Each arithmetic operation therefore costs $O(L^2)$ bit operations, proving the claimed bound. $\square$

For arbitrary binary rational masses, this move bound need not be polynomial in $L$. Strict potential decrease nevertheless prevents revisiting any subset, yielding the worst-case running time
$$ O\bigl((\tau^2+\tau 2^\tau)L^2\bigr) $$
under the theorem’s hypothesis.

The weaker hypothesis of Theorem 1 also permits an exponential construction by minimizing $F$ over all subsets. Consecutive subsets in a Gray-code enumeration differ by one type; Lemma 3 and the same maintained sums give the same worst-case bound.

Concrete nonconstant example

Take six equally weighted types on a cycle with unit edge lengths. Retain all shortest-path distances except that the distance between opposite types $0$ and $3$ is reduced from $3$ to $299/100$.

This remains a metric. Triangles in which the changed distance is on the left become easier to satisfy. In any triangle containing that pair, either unchanged distance is at most $2$, whereas the changed distance plus the other unchanged distance is at least $399/100$.

Its global averages are
$$ b_0=b_3=\frac{899}{600}, \qquad b_1=b_2=b_4=b_5=\frac32. $$
Therefore
$$ \beta=\frac{2699}{1800}, \qquad h_{\min}=\frac{539}{360}, \qquad R=\frac1{600}. $$
Since
$$ R=\frac1{600} < \frac16\cdot\frac{539}{360} = \mu_{\min}h_{\min}, $$
Theorem 2 applies despite the global averages being unequal.

6. Why this potential does not settle arbitrary metrics

The offset in Lemma 4 cannot simply be discarded. The following exact example shows that a global minimum of $F$ can be unstable.

Take four types indexed by $0,1,2,3$, with masses
$$ (\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)=\frac1{28}(14,3,3,8) $$
and distance matrix
$$ D= \begin{pmatrix} 0&9&5&17\\ 9&0&4&12\\ 5&4&0&12\\ 17&12&12&0 \end{pmatrix}. $$
This is a metric. For the four triples of distinct types, the largest-distance triangle inequalities are respectively
$$ 9=5+4,\qquad 17\leq9+12,\qquad 17=5+12,\qquad 12\leq4+12. $$
The other inequalities follow because the distances are positive.

Direct calculation gives
$$ (b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3) = \left(\frac{89}{14},\frac{117}{14}, \frac{89}{14},\frac{155}{14}\right), \qquad \beta=\frac{388}{49}. $$
Because $F(A)=F(T\setminus A)$, it suffices to examine the seven nonempty subsets not containing type $3$:

| $A$ | $38416\,F(A)$ |
|---|---:|
| $\{0\}$ | $-46060$ |
| $\{1\}$ | $-3879$ |
| $\{0,1\}$ | $-40867$ |
| $\{2\}$ | $-2115$ |
| $\{0,2\}$ | $-47335$ |
| $\{1,2\}$ | $-8460$ |
| $\{0,1,2\}$ | $-44608$ |

These values follow by substitution into
$$ F(A)=W_A-2M(A)B_A+\beta M(A)^2. $$
Thus the globally minimizing partition is
$$ A=\{0,2\}, \qquad B=\{1,3\}. $$
Type $1$ is unstable:
$$ A(1,B)=\frac{96}{11} > \frac{138}{17}=A(1,A), $$
because $96\cdot17=1632>1518=138\cdot11$.

This example still admits a stable partition, namely
$$ \{0,1,2\},\qquad\{3\}. $$
The first cluster’s own averages are
$$ \frac{21}{10},\qquad \frac{69}{10},\qquad \frac{41}{10}, $$
respectively, and their distances to the singleton are $17,12,12$. Type $3$ has own average zero.

The example therefore establishes a precise limitation of the optimization approach: globally minimizing $F$ alone does not guarantee stability for arbitrary input metrics and masses.

7. What remains open

The results above establish $\mathrm{NP}$ membership and a new tractable family of two-cluster instances. They provide neither a polynomial-time algorithm for all prescribed inputs nor a valid hardness reduction for the exact mass model.

Both the unrestricted classification and the classification for arbitrary metrics with $k=2$ remain open in this write-up.
