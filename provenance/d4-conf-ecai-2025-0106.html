The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over types \(t=(I_t,p_t)\), where \(I_t\) is a closed interval, \(p_t\in[0,1]^2\) sums to \(1\), and each type is a reporting cohort, design a randomized mechanism choosing one facility \(f_j\) and a point \(y\). Require that for every supported type \(t\) and alternate report \(t'\), its expected utility under true type \(t\) does not increase when the cohort of mass \(\mu_t\) reports \(t'\), and guarantee expected welfare at least \(\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\), where \(W_\mu(f_j,y)=\sum_t\mu_t p_{t,j}\mathbf{1}[y\in I_t]\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=\max_{j,y}W_\mu(f_j,y)\). Seek a single mechanism for all finite rational supports with an implementation polynomial in support size \(\tau\) and input bit length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m=2\) facilities. A type is \(t=(I_t,p_t)\), where \(I_t=[a_t,b_t]\) is nonempty with finite rational endpoints \(a_t\le b_t\), and \(p_t\in\mathbb Q^2\cap[0,1]^2\) satisfies \(p_{t,1}+p_{t,2}=1\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A mechanism without monetary transfers chooses a lottery over \((f_j,y)\in\{f_1,f_2\}\times\mathbb R\). True utility is \(u_t(f_j,y)=p_{t,j}\mathbf1[y\in I_t]\); welfare is \(W_\mu(f_j,y)=\sum_t\mu_tu_t(f_j,y)\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=\max_{j,y}W_\mu(f_j,y)\).
Both interval and preference are private; mass is public and fixed. Each supported type is one reporting cohort, which may replace its entire report by any rational type \(t'\) in the same domain, producing \(\mu'=\mu-\mu_t\delta_t+\mu_t\delta_{t'}\); coincident reports are merged.
Require truthfulness in expectation: \(\mathbb E_{z\sim M(\mu)}u_t(z)\ge\mathbb E_{z\sim M(\mu')}u_t(z)\) for every society, supported type, and alternative report. No cohort may split its mass among reports.

Does a single randomized mechanism satisfying this condition guarantee \(\mathbb E_{z\sim M(\mu)}W_\mu(z)\ge\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\) on every such society, with implementation time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this guarantee impossible under these requirements?

For finite lists of individual agents with both components private, a randomized strategyproof mechanism achieves approximation factor \(2\), with a tight worst-case factor for that mechanism. [Verified source](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/FAIA250951).
No result settling exactly the rational-mass cohort problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonempty bounded closed intervals, allowing singleton intervals; endpoints and preference entries are rational to make the finite binary input model explicit.
  • Normalized total mass to 1, as prescribed by the distribution formulation.
  • Made masses public and immutable; a deviation changes both private components of the entire cohort's report, without splitting its mass.
  • Defined the deviated distribution by moving the cohort's mass to its alternative report and merging coincident reports.
  • Restricted alternative reports to the same rational type domain as the inputs.
  • Used truthfulness in expectation, with utility evaluated at the original true type; multiplying utility by the cohort's fixed positive mass gives the same condition.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Yes. A single mechanism achieves the required guarantee in polynomial time. The construction ignores reported preferences, maximizes total interval mass, and chooses the facility uniformly.

1. Construction and well-definedness

For a society \(\mu\), define its coverage function by
\[ C_\mu(y)=\sum_t\mu_t\mathbf1[y\in I_t]. \]
Let \(y_\mu\) be the leftmost point maximizing \(C_\mu\). The mechanism outputs \((f_1,y_\mu)\) or \((f_2,y_\mu)\), each with probability \(1/2\).

We first justify that this leftmost maximizer exists and can be found among the reported left endpoints.

For any point \(y\) with positive coverage, let \(S=\{t:y\in I_t\}\) and \(a=\max_{t\in S}a_t\). For every \(t\in S\), we have \(a_t\le a\le y\le b_t\), so \(a\in I_t\). Consequently,
\[ C_\mu(a)\ge C_\mu(y). \]
Since every interval is nonempty and every mass is positive, the maximum coverage is positive. Thus it equals the maximum over the finite set of reported left endpoints.

Choose the smallest left endpoint attaining that maximum. For every maximizing point \(y\), the preceding construction gives a maximizing left endpoint \(a\le y\). Therefore our chosen endpoint is at most every maximizing point, as required.

2. Truthfulness under whole-cohort deviations

Fix a society \(\mu\), a supported true type \(t=(I,p)\), its mass \(w=\mu_t\), and any alternative report \(t'=(J,p')\). Write \(C=C_\mu\), \(C'=C_{\mu'}\), \(y=y_\mu\), and \(y'=y_{\mu'}\).

Because coverage ignores preferences and adds masses, even when coincident reports merge,
\[ C'(x)=C(x)-w\mathbf1[x\in I]+w\mathbf1[x\in J]. \]
In particular, \(C'(x)\le C(x)\) for \(x\in I\), while \(C'(x)\ge C(x)\) for \(x\notin I\).

The true cohort’s expected utility is
\[ \mathbb E_{z\sim M(\mu)}u_t(z) =\frac{p_1+p_2}{2}\mathbf1[y\in I] =\frac12\mathbf1[y\in I]. \]
The same formula applies after the deviation, with \(y'\) in place of \(y\). Hence a profitable deviation would require \(y\notin I\) and \(y'\in I\).

Suppose these memberships held. Using the two coverage inequalities and maximality of \(y\) and \(y'\), respectively, gives
\[ C'(y')\le C(y')\le C(y)\le C'(y)\le C'(y'). \]
Every inequality must therefore be an equality.

In particular, \(y'\) is also a maximizer of \(C\), so the leftmost-maximizer rule gives \(y\le y'\). Likewise, \(y\) is a maximizer of \(C'\), so that rule gives \(y'\le y\). Thus \(y=y'\), contradicting their different memberships in \(I\).

No profitable deviation exists. This proves truthfulness in expectation for every allowed whole-cohort report, including simultaneous changes to interval and preference.

3. Welfare guarantee

At the chosen location, expected welfare is
\[ \mathbb E_{z\sim M(\mu)}W_\mu(z) =\frac12\sum_t\mu_t(p_{t,1}+p_{t,2})\mathbf1[y_\mu\in I_t] =\frac12 C_\mu(y_\mu). \]
For every facility \(f_j\) and every location \(x\), the bound \(p_{t,j}\le1\) implies
\[ W_\mu(f_j,x)\le C_\mu(x)\le C_\mu(y_\mu). \]
Taking the maximum over facilities and locations yields \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\le C_\mu(y_\mu)\). Therefore
\[ \mathbb E_{z\sim M(\mu)}W_\mu(z)\ge\frac12\mathrm{OPT}(\mu). \]

The guarantee is attained exactly for a society consisting of one type with preference \(p=(1,0)\): optimal welfare is \(1\), whereas this mechanism’s expected welfare is \(1/2\).

4. Polynomial implementation

Evaluate coverage at each of the at most \(\tau\) reported left endpoints, and choose the smallest endpoint having maximum coverage. This requires at most \(\tau^2\) interval-membership checks and mass additions.

All computations can be exact. If the masses have denominators \(d_t\), take the common denominator \(D=\prod_t d_t\). Its bit length is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence is \(O(L)\). Coverage comparisons then reduce to comparisons of integer sums of the scaled masses \(D\mu_t\); these sums are at most \(D\). Rational endpoint comparisons also have polynomial bit complexity.

Thus the mechanism runs in time polynomial in \(\tau,L\), and therefore in \(m,\tau,L\) for the prescribed \(m=2\). Sampling the facility requires one fair random bit.
