Theorem III.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite providers \(N\), channel sets \(C_i\), a finite state space \(\Omega\) with rational probabilities \(p_\omega\), and a finite support \(T\) of rational subscriber masses \(\mu_t\), where each type \(t\) has provider \(i(t)\), rates \(R_{\ell t\omega}\), concave utility \(u_t\), and minimum expected rate \(m_t\), find an efficient schedule and a core imputation. For each coalition \(S\), choose schedules \(a_{\ell t\omega}\in[0,1]\) for types with \(i(t)\in S\) and channels \(\ell\in C_S\), subject to \(\sum_\ell a_{\ell t\omega}\le 1\), \(\sum_{t:i(t)\in S}\mu_t a_{\ell t\omega}\le q_\ell\), and \(\sum_\omega p_\omega\sum_\ell a_{\ell t\omega}R_{\ell t\omega}\ge m_t\). Set \(r_t(\omega)=\sum_\ell a_{\ell t\omega}R_{\ell t\omega}\) and \(z_\ell(\omega)=\sum_t\mu_ta_{\ell t\omega}\). Define \(v(S)\) as the maximum expected \(\sum_{t:i(t)\in S}\mu_tu_t(r_t(\omega))\) minus the providers’ convex costs of channel use \(z_\ell(\omega)\). Return a grand-coalition optimizer and \(x\) with \(x(N)=v(N)\) and \(x(S)\ge v(S)\) for every coalition \(S\).

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; masses need not sum to one. Each utility \(u_t:\mathbb R_{\ge0}\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) is nondecreasing, concave, and zero at zero; each provider cost \(V_i:\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{C_i}\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) is coordinatewise nondecreasing, convex, and zero at the origin. Utilities are supplied as finite minima of rational affine functions, and costs as finite maxima of rational affine functions. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including these lists.
For a nonempty coalition \(S\), put \(T_S=\{t:i(t)\in S\}\). Result

The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|N|,|\Omega|,L\). An exact optimal schedule and an exact rational core imputation can be obtained from a polynomial-size rational linear program and its dual.

The proof uses the dual-allocation principle appearing in Section III of the [discrete spectrum-pooling paper](https://www.seas.upenn.edu/~swati/Infocom09-techreport.pdf), but constructs the programs directly from the binary-encoded masses. No expansion into individual subscribers is required.

1. A linear program for the mass-input model

Write the supplied affine representations as

\( u_t(r)=\min_{k\in\mathcal K_t}(A_{tk}r+B_{tk}) \)

and

\( V_i(z)=\max_{h\in\mathcal H_i} \left(\sum_{\ell\in C_i}D_{ih\ell}z_\ell+E_{ih}\right). \)

All these coefficients are rational. The finite index sets \(\mathcal K_t\) and \(\mathcal H_i\) are explicitly supplied.

Because each listed mass satisfies \(\mu_t>0\), introduce the mass-weighted scheduling variables

\( b_{\ell t\omega}=\mu_ta_{\ell t\omega}. \)

For a coalition \(S\), define the linear expressions

\( w_{t\omega}=\sum_{\ell\in C_S}R_{\ell t\omega}b_{\ell t\omega}, \qquad z_{\ell\omega}=\sum_{t\in T_S}b_{\ell t\omega}. \)

Thus \(w_{t\omega}=\mu_tr_t(\omega)\), and

\( \mu_tu_t(r_t(\omega)) = \min_{k\in\mathcal K_t} \left(A_{tk}w_{t\omega}+\mu_tB_{tk}\right). \)

Introduce unrestricted real variables \(y_{t\omega}\) for total utility and \(c_{i\omega}\) for cost. Consider the following linear program, denoted \(\mathsf P(S)\).

Its objective is

\( \displaystyle \max\ \sum_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega \left(\sum_{t\in T_S}y_{t\omega}-\sum_{i\in S}c_{i\omega}\right). \)

Its constraints are:

  • For every \(t\in T_S\) and \(\omega\in\Omega\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_{\ell\in C_S}b_{\ell t\omega}\le\mu_t. \)

  • For every \(\ell\in C_S\) and \(\omega\in\Omega\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_S}b_{\ell t\omega}\le q_\ell. \)

  • For every \(t\in T_S\),

\( \displaystyle -\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\sum_{\ell\in C_S} p_\omega R_{\ell t\omega}b_{\ell t\omega} \le-\mu_tm_t. \)

  • For every \(t\in T_S\), \(k\in\mathcal K_t\), and \(\omega\in\Omega\),

\( y_{t\omega}-A_{tk}w_{t\omega}\le\mu_tB_{tk}. \)

  • For every \(i\in S\), \(h\in\mathcal H_i\), and \(\omega\in\Omega\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_{\ell\in C_i}D_{ih\ell}z_{\ell\omega}-c_{i\omega} \le-E_{ih}. \)

Finally, all \(b_{\ell t\omega}\) are nonnegative.

Equivalence to the scheduling problem

The first three constraint families, together with nonnegativity, are exactly the original scheduling constraints after multiplying each type’s time and minimum-rate constraints by \(\mu_t\). In particular,

\( 0\le b_{\ell t\omega}\le\mu_t, \)

so \(a_{\ell t\omega}=b_{\ell t\omega}/\mu_t\) also satisfies the required individual upper bound.

For fixed feasible \(b\), the largest permissible value of \(y_{t\omega}\) is precisely \(\mu_tu_t(r_t(\omega))\), and the smallest permissible value of \(c_{i\omega}\) is precisely \(V_i(z(\omega))\). Since \(p_\omega>0\), maximizing over these auxiliary variables gives exactly the original payoff. Consequently,

\( \operatorname{opt}\mathsf P(S)=v(S). \)

Feasibility and attainment

Every coalition is feasible: take feasible singleton schedules for its members and set all assignments between different providers to zero. Disjointness of the channel sets ensures that their capacity constraints remain satisfied.

The feasible scheduling set is closed and bounded, since every \(b_{\ell t\omega}\) lies in \([0,\mu_t]\). The payoff is continuous, because finite minima and maxima of affine functions are continuous. Therefore \(v(S)\) is finite and attained. Setting the auxiliary variables to their actual utility and cost values produces an attained optimum of \(\mathsf P(S)\).

2. The dual and its provider decomposition

Associate nonnegative dual variables with the five constraint families:

\( \alpha_{t\omega},\quad \beta_{\ell\omega},\quad \gamma_t,\quad \lambda_{tk\omega},\quad \delta_{ih\omega}, \)

respectively.

Let \(j(\ell)\) be the unique provider owning channel \(\ell\). The dual, denoted \(\mathsf D(S)\), minimizes

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \mathcal D_S ={}& \sum_{t\in T_S}\mu_t \left( \sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\alpha_{t\omega} -m_t\gamma_t +\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_t} B_{tk}\lambda_{tk\omega} \right)\\ &+\sum_{\ell\in C_S}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega} q_\ell\beta_{\ell\omega} -\sum_{i\in S}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_i} E_{ih}\delta_{ih\omega}. \end{aligned} \)

Its constraints are

\( \displaystyle \sum_{k\in\mathcal K_t}\lambda_{tk\omega}=p_\omega \qquad(t\in T_S,\ \omega\in\Omega), \)

\( \displaystyle \sum_{h\in\mathcal H_i}\delta_{ih\omega}=p_\omega \qquad(i\in S,\ \omega\in\Omega), \)

and, for every \(\ell\in C_S\), \(t\in T_S\), and \(\omega\in\Omega\),

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \theta_{\ell t\omega}:={}& \alpha_{t\omega}+\beta_{\ell\omega} -p_\omega R_{\ell t\omega}\gamma_t\\ &-R_{\ell t\omega} \sum_{k\in\mathcal K_t}A_{tk}\lambda_{tk\omega} +\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_{j(\ell)}} D_{j(\ell)h\ell}\delta_{j(\ell)h\omega} \ \ge 0. \end{aligned} \)

All dual variables are nonnegative.

To verify these signs and equalities, form the maximizing Lagrangian by adding each nonnegative multiplier times its constraint’s right-hand side minus its left-hand side. The coefficients of the unrestricted variables \(y_{t\omega}\) and \(c_{i\omega}\) are, respectively,

\( p_\omega-\sum_k\lambda_{tk\omega}, \qquad -p_\omega+\sum_h\delta_{ih\omega}. \)

Their unrestricted maximization requires the displayed equalities. The coefficient of \(b_{\ell t\omega}\) is \(-\theta_{\ell t\omega}\); maximization over \(b_{\ell t\omega}\ge0\) requires \(\theta_{\ell t\omega}\ge0\). The constant term is exactly \(\mathcal D_S\).

Thus this is the ordinary LP dual. Since \(\mathsf P(S)\) is feasible with finite optimal value, linear programming strong duality gives an attained dual optimum and

\( \operatorname{opt}\mathsf D(S)=v(S). \)

This invocation requires no strict-feasibility assumption.

3. An optimal grand-coalition dual yields a core imputation

Let

\( (\alpha^*,\beta^*,\gamma^*,\lambda^*,\delta^*) \)

be any optimal solution of \(\mathsf D(N)\). Assign provider \(i\) the portion of its objective associated with that provider:

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} x_i={}& \sum_{t:\,i(t)=i}\mu_t \left( \sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\alpha^*_{t\omega} -m_t\gamma^*_t +\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_t} B_{tk}\lambda^*_{tk\omega} \right)\\ &+\sum_{\ell\in C_i}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega} q_\ell\beta^*_{\ell\omega} -\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_i} E_{ih}\delta^*_{ih\omega}. \end{aligned} \)

We prove both required core properties.

Efficiency

Every term in the grand-coalition dual objective belongs to exactly one provider. Hence, by strong duality,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i\in N}x_i = \mathcal D_N(\alpha^*,\beta^*,\gamma^*,\lambda^*,\delta^*) = v(N). \)

Coalition inequalities

Fix a nonempty coalition \(S\). Restrict the grand-coalition dual solution by retaining:

  • \(\alpha^*,\gamma^*,\lambda^*\) for types in \(T_S\);
  • \(\beta^*\) for channels in \(C_S\);
  • \(\delta^*\) for providers in \(S\).

This restriction is feasible for \(\mathsf D(S)\). Indeed, the normalization equalities involve only the affine pieces of one retained type or provider, so their sums remain unchanged.

For a retained scheduling pair \(\ell\in C_S\), \(t\in T_S\), the inequality \(\theta_{\ell t\omega}\ge0\) involves only variables belonging to type \(t\), channel \(\ell\), and provider \(j(\ell)\). All are retained, since \(j(\ell)\in S\). Thus that inequality is identical to its grand-coalition counterpart.

The restricted dual objective is exactly \(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\). Weak duality therefore gives

\( \displaystyle v(S)=\operatorname{opt}\mathsf P(S) \le \mathcal D_S(\text{restricted dual solution}) = \sum_{i\in S}x_i. \)

For \(S=\varnothing\), both sides are zero. Consequently \(x\) belongs to the core. In particular, the singleton inequalities ensure individual rationality.

4. Polynomial-time exact computation

Put \(s=|\Omega|\), and let

\( K=\sum_t|\mathcal K_t|, \qquad H=\sum_i|\mathcal H_i|. \)

The grand-coalition primal has

\( m\tau s+\tau s+|N|s \)

variables and

\( \tau s+ms+\tau+Ks+Hs \)

constraints, apart from nonnegativity. The dual has

\( \tau s+ms+\tau+Ks+Hs \)

variables, with \(m\tau s\) scheduling inequalities and \((\tau+|N|)s\) normalization equalities.

Since the affine lists are explicit, \(K\) and \(H\) are bounded by the input length up to encoding constants. Expanding the displayed linear expressions therefore produces primal and dual descriptions of polynomial length.

Their coefficients use products such as \(\mu_tB_{tk}\), \(\mu_tm_t\), and \(p_\omega R_{\ell t\omega}\). Multiplying binary-encoded rationals increases their bit lengths only polynomially. Thus both LPs have encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,|N|,s,L\).

We use the standard exact rational linear programming theorem: a rational LP can be solved in time polynomial in its binary encoding length, returning an exact rational optimum when it is feasible with finite optimal value. The feasibility and bounded-optimum hypotheses were proved above for the primal; strong duality supplies them for the dual. The returned solutions have polynomial encoding length.

The algorithm is therefore:

  • Solve \(\mathsf P(N)\) exactly and return \(a^*_{\ell t\omega}=b^*_{\ell t\omega}/\mu_t\).
  • Solve \(\mathsf D(N)\) exactly and compute \(x_i\) from the provider decomposition above.

Rational division and the sums defining \(x_i\) also have polynomial bit complexity. The algorithm neither enumerates coalitions nor replicates subscribers according to their masses.

Thus it returns both requested objects exactly in polynomial time under the stated assumptions.
