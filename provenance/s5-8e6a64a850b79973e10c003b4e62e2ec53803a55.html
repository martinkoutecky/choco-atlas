1. Bundle configurations and their realization

For a type $t$, let $H_t$ be its set of neighboring types and put $d_t=|H_t|$. Define its finite configuration set by
$$ \mathcal B_t= \left\{ b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{H_t}: |b|:=\sum_{s\in H_t}b_s\leq q_t^+ \right\}. $$

The coordinate $b_s$ counts actual partners of type $s$. The number of unmatched occurrences is $q_t^+-|b|$. Define
$$ \delta_t(b)=\max\{0,q_t^- -|b|\}. $$

An allocation determines configuration masses $p_{t,b}$ satisfying
$$ p_{t,b}\geq0,\qquad \sum_{b\in\mathcal B_t}p_{t,b}=w_t, $$
and, for every acceptable pair $(i,j)$,
$$ \sum_{b\in\mathcal B_i}b_jp_{i,b} = \sum_{c\in\mathcal B_j}c_ip_{j,c}. $$

Its deficiency and cardinality are
$$ D(p)=\sum_t\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_t}\delta_t(b)p_{t,b}, \qquad C(p)=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_i}|b|p_{i,b}. $$

The converse requires checking the prohibition on repeated individual partnerships.

Realization lemma. Every nonnegative configuration-mass vector satisfying the displayed mass and balance equations is realized by an admissible allocation.

Proof. First use a base interval $U_t$ of length $w_t$, partitioned into pieces of lengths $p_{t,b}$. Give each base individual in a piece labeled $b$ exactly $b_s$ slots labeled for neighbor type $s$.

For each acceptable pair $(i,j)$, the total measures of slots labeled toward the opposite endpoint agree. Choose a measurable measure-preserving bijection between these slot sets. These base bijections may connect two slots of one individual to two slots of another individual.

To remove this problem, replace each base individual $u$ by individuals $(u,r)$ with $r\in[0,1)$, using product measure. The resulting type space still has mass $w_t$. It is measure-isomorphic, modulo null sets, to $X_t$: after normalization, interleaving binary digits gives an isomorphism between a unit interval and a unit square. Preservation of measure follows first on dyadic rectangles and then on the generated sigma-algebra.

Let
$$ \alpha_k=\frac{k}{Q+1},\qquad 1\leq k\leq Q. $$
Whenever a base slot bijection sends $(u,k)$ on the $I$ side to $(v,\ell)$ on the $J$ side, match
$$ ((u,r),k) \quad\text{to}\quad ((v,r+\alpha_k\pmod1),\ell). $$

This is a measurable measure-preserving slot bijection. Its inverse uses the inverse base map and subtracts $\alpha_k$.

Two distinct slots $k,k'$ of the same source individual cannot reach the same partner individual. If their target base individuals differ, their partners differ. If their target base individuals coincide, their target auxiliary coordinates differ because $\alpha_k\neq\alpha_{k'}$. Thus no individual partnership is repeated.

Every lifted individual has its prescribed configuration. Transporting the construction to the original intervals completes the proof. $\square$

The construction has a finite symbolic description. Repeated slot blocks can be packed using their configuration counts, rather than listed slot by slot. In particular, binary quotas do not require an output listing $Q$ slots.

2. Criticality has a compact polynomial-time formulation

Let $x_{ij}$ be the partnership mass on an acceptable pair. Write
$$ n_t=\sum_{s\in H_t}x_{ts}, $$
where $x_{ts}$ denotes the corresponding edge variable regardless of which side contains $t$.

Consider the rational linear program
$$ \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad &\sum_t z_t,\\ \text{subject to}\quad &x_{ij}\geq0,\\ &n_t\leq q_t^+w_t,\\ &z_t\geq0,\\ &z_t\geq q_t^-w_t-n_t. \end{aligned} $$

Proposition. Its optimum equals the minimum allocation deficiency, denoted by $D_*$.

Proof. Every allocation gives feasible edge masses. For each type,
$$ \int_{X_t}\max\{0,q_t^- -d_M(x)\}\,dx \geq \max\{0,q_t^-w_t-n_t\}. $$
Indeed, the integral is nonnegative and is at least the integral of $q_t^- -d_M(x)$. Thus the linear-program optimum is a lower bound.

Conversely, fix feasible edge masses. Set
$$ a_s=\frac{x_{ts}}{w_t}, \qquad \mu_t=\sum_s a_s=\frac{n_t}{w_t}. $$

For integer endpoints $A\leq B$, the polytope
$$ \left\{a\geq0:A\leq\sum_s a_s\leq B\right\} $$
has integral vertices. To verify this, two positive coordinates permit a small opposite perturbation, so a vertex has at most one positive coordinate. A positive total at a vertex must be an endpoint; the zero vector is possible when $A=0$. All such vertices are integral. The zero-dimensional case is immediate.

If $\mu_t\leq q_t^-$, decompose $a$ into configurations with degrees between $0$ and $q_t^-$. Their deficiency is affine in degree, giving total deficiency
$$ q_t^-w_t-n_t. $$
If $\mu_t\geq q_t^-$, decompose $a$ into configurations with degrees between $q_t^-$ and $q_t^+$. Their deficiency is zero.

Applying the realization lemma to these configuration distributions produces an allocation with deficiency
$$ \sum_t\max\{0,q_t^-w_t-n_t\}. $$
This attains the lower bound. $\square$

The program has $m+\tau$ variables and polynomially many constraints. Products such as $q_t^+w_t$ have polynomial bit length under the stated encoding. Exact rational linear programming therefore computes $D_*$ in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ bit operations; here I use the standard polynomial bit-complexity theorem for rational linear programming. [Khachiyan, *A polynomial algorithm in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Consequently, the critical configuration distributions form the nonempty bounded rational polytope
$$ P= \left\{ p\geq0: \begin{array}{l} \text{the type-mass and edge-balance equations hold},\\ D(p)=D_* \end{array} \right\}. $$

Every point of $P$ is realized by a critical allocation.

3. Exact treatment of common actual partners

For configurations $b,c\in\mathcal B_t$, define $V_t(b,c)$ as the largest vote favoring the rival configuration $c$ under the following rule:

  • pad both configurations to $q_t^+$;
  • fix all common unmatched occurrences;
  • assume no actual partner is common;
  • maximize over the remaining slot correspondences.

This is a finite integer optimization. Votes depend only on partner types.

Comparison lemma. For any candidate allocation, its largest possible aggregate margin against critical rivals depends only on its configuration masses. It equals the maximum obtained from the coefficients $V_t(b,c)$ and arbitrary couplings between candidate and rival configurations.

Proof. First, $V_t(b,c)$ is an upper bound on any actual comparison. Extend a permitted correspondence by putting back every fixed common actual partner as a zero-vote pair. This gives a correspondence in the optimization defining $V_t(b,c)$.

For the reverse inequality, prescribe a rival configuration distribution and, within each type, a coupling with the candidate configurations. Realize the rival by the realization lemma. By splitting configuration classes into pieces of the prescribed coupling masses, choose measure-preserving individual pairings implementing that coupling. Equivalently, relabel the rival onto the candidate population and use identity pairings.

It remains to remove common actual partnerships while preserving these configuration pairs pointwise. On the $J$ side, partition each type into its rival configuration classes. Each positive-measure class is an atomless Lebesgue space and hence admits measure-preserving rotations after identification with an interval. Independently choose a uniform rotation in each such class, and relabel only the rival’s $J$ endpoints.

The rival configuration at every $J$ individual is unchanged, because relabeling occurs within one configuration class. The rival configurations on $I$ also remain unchanged, because partner types remain unchanged.

For a fixed $I$ individual, every old rival partner is carried to an atomlessly distributed individual within its class. Its probability of coinciding with one of the individual’s finitely many candidate partners is zero. Fubini’s theorem, applied to the finite partnership counts, implies that some choice of rotations leaves no common partnership almost everywhere. Measure preservation gives the same conclusion at the other endpoints.

Thus the prescribed configuration coupling can be compared using no common actual partners. For each of the finitely many configuration pairs, choose a correspondence attaining $V_t(b,c)$; this choice is measurable. The upper bound is attained. $\square$

This argument uses the task’s rule that common partner types alone do not fix slots.

4. The worst-margin function is concave

For $p\in P$, define $F(p)$ as the maximum of
$$ \sum_t\sum_{b,c\in\mathcal B_t}V_t(b,c)z_{t,b,c} $$
subject to
$$ z_{t,b,c}\geq0,\qquad \sum_c z_{t,b,c}=p_{t,b}, $$
and the requirement that
$$ r_{t,c}:=\sum_b z_{t,b,c} $$
belongs to $P$.

The feasible set is compact, so the maximum exists. By the comparison lemma, $F(p)$ is exactly the largest permitted vote margin against critical rivals.

Also,
$$ F(p)\geq0. $$
To see this, take rival distribution $r=p$, couple identical configurations, and correspond equal partner types. This comparison has vote zero, so its maximum has nonnegative vote.

Consequently,
$$ p\text{ is popular critical}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(p)=0. $$

Concavity lemma. For $p,p'\in P$ and $0\leq\lambda\leq1$,
$$ F(\lambda p+(1-\lambda)p') \geq \lambda F(p)+(1-\lambda)F(p'). $$

Proof. Take optimal couplings for $p$ and $p'$. Their convex combination has the required candidate marginal. Its rival marginal belongs to $P$ because $P$ is convex. Its objective is the corresponding convex combination of the two optimum values. $\square$

Vertex consequence. If a popular distribution has a vertex decomposition
$$ p=\sum_k\lambda_kv_k,\qquad \lambda_k>0,\qquad \sum_k\lambda_k=1, $$
then every $v_k$ is popular.

Indeed,
$$ 0=F(p)\geq\sum_k\lambda_kF(v_k)\geq0. $$
Every summand must therefore vanish.

This proves a conditional existence statement without requiring a continuity argument.

Theorem. If a popular critical allocation exists, a maximum-cardinality popular critical allocation exists and can be chosen at a vertex of $P$.

Proof. Every popular point decomposes into popular vertices. Since cardinality is linear,
$$ C(p)=\sum_k\lambda_kC(v_k) \leq \max_k C(v_k). $$
There are finitely many vertices. A popular vertex of greatest cardinality therefore dominates the cardinality of every popular distribution. The realization lemma produces the claimed allocation. $\square$

5. Popularity has a compact verification program

The definition of $F$ uses potentially many configurations. Nevertheless, a candidate supported on $B$ configurations admits a polynomial-size verification program.

Fix one supported candidate configuration $b$ of type $t$. Write
$$ q=q_t^+,\qquad \ell=q_t^-,\qquad d=|b|. $$
For real partner types $a,j\in H_t$, let
$$ \sigma_t(j,a)= \begin{cases} 1,&j\succ_t a,\\ 0,&j=a,\\ -1,&a\succ_t j. \end{cases} $$

We split rival degrees $n$ according to the two comparisons
$$ n\leq d\ \text{or}\ n\geq d, \qquad n\leq\ell\ \text{or}\ n\geq\ell. $$
Each of the at most four resulting cases is an integer interval $[A,U]$; empty intervals are omitted. Endpoint overlap causes no problem because the formulas agree there.

For each case introduce a mass variable $\lambda\geq0$.

5.1 Cases with $n\geq d$

Introduce variables $h_{a,j}\geq0$ and $h_{0,j}\geq0$. The first records comparisons between candidate and rival real partners; the second records rival real partners compared with candidate unmatched occurrences. Impose
$$ \sum_j h_{a,j}=b_a\lambda \qquad(a\in H_t). $$
Set
$$ N=\sum_{a,j}h_{a,j}+\sum_jh_{0,j}, \qquad A\lambda\leq N\leq U\lambda. $$

The contribution to the objective is
$$ \sum_{a,j}\sigma_t(j,a)h_{a,j}+\sum_jh_{0,j}. $$
The rival’s partnership mass toward neighbor $j$ is
$$ \sum_a h_{a,j}+h_{0,j}. $$

These formulas fix precisely the common unmatched occurrences: when $n\geq d$, all rival unmatched occurrences are common, leaving $n-d$ candidate unmatched occurrences to compare with real partners.

5.2 Cases with $n\leq d$

Introduce $h_{a,j}\geq0$ and $h_{a,\bot}\geq0$, with
$$ \sum_jh_{a,j}+h_{a,\bot}=b_a\lambda. $$
Set
$$ N=\sum_{a,j}h_{a,j}, \qquad A\lambda\leq N\leq U\lambda. $$

The objective contribution is
$$ \sum_{a,j}\sigma_t(j,a)h_{a,j}-\sum_a h_{a,\bot}. $$
The rival’s partnership mass toward $j$ is $\sum_a h_{a,j}$.

Here all candidate unmatched occurrences are common. The remaining $d-n$ rival unmatched occurrences are compared with candidate real partners.

5.3 Deficiency and global constraints

In either degree regime, the rival deficiency contribution is
$$ \ell\lambda-N $$
in a case with $n\leq\ell$, and zero in a case with $n\geq\ell$.

For every supported candidate configuration, impose
$$ \sum_{\text{its cases}}\lambda=p_{t,b}. $$
Finally impose all rival edge-balance equations and require its total deficiency to equal $D_*$. Maximize the sum of the displayed vote objectives.

Proposition. This compact program has optimum $F(p)$.

Proof. Every permitted comparison supplies feasible variables: record the measures of each comparison category and split individuals by the four degree cases. Thus its optimum is at least every actual margin.

For the converse, consider a block with $\lambda>0$ and divide its variables by $\lambda$. We must show that the normalized fractional correspondence data are convex combinations of integral data for individual bundles.

In the high-degree case, the real rows are independent simplices with integer row totals $b_a$. The extra row has nonnegative coordinates whose total lies in the integer interval $[A-d,U-d]$. Each of these polytopes has integral vertices, so their product does too.

In the low-degree case, the normalized variables form a flow polytope. Each candidate-type row has integer supply $b_a$, can send flow to real-type columns or to a bottom column, and the total sent to real columns lies in $[A,U]$. All supplies and bounds are integral.

For completeness, the relevant flow integrality fact follows directly from vertex structure. At a vertex, arcs strictly between their bounds cannot contain an undirected cycle: a signed perturbation around such a cycle preserves conservation and allows motion in both directions. Those arcs therefore form a forest. After fixing all other arcs at integral bounds, conservation at a leaf forces its remaining arc to be integral; repeated leaf removal proves integrality throughout the forest. Hence every vertex is integral.

Thus every normalized block decomposes into integral slot-correspondence matrices. Each matrix specifies a rival configuration and a legal correspondence, with the common unmatched occurrences already fixed. The degree case also makes deficiency affine, so this decomposition preserves the block’s deficiency exactly.

Combining the decompositions gives a coupling of candidate and rival configurations. The global equations make the rival feasible and critical. The realization and comparison lemmas realize this coupling and its correspondences. Its vote equals the compact program’s objective. $\square$

Let

The program has $O(B(\Delta+1)^2)$ variables and polynomially many constraints. Its coefficients use the configuration counts, quotas, masses, and $D_*$, all in binary.

6. Polynomial-size certificates and the $\mathrm{NP}$ upper bound

The standard-form description of $P$ has at most
$$ r=m+\tau+1 $$
equality rows: $\tau$ type-mass rows, $m$ edge-balance rows, and one deficiency row.

Sparse-vertex lemma. Every vertex of $P$ has at most $r$ positive configuration masses.

Proof. If its positive-support columns were linearly dependent, a nonzero vector supported on those columns would preserve every equality. A sufficiently small perturbation in either direction would preserve nonnegativity, contradicting extremality. Hence the positive-support columns are independent, and their number is at most $r$. $\square$

Every such vertex has polynomial encoding length. Its supported configurations use binary counts bounded by $Q$. To bound its masses, select a nonsingular square subsystem on its support. Its matrix has integer entries of absolute value at most $Q$, and its right-hand side consists of masses and $D_*$, all with polynomial bit length. Cramer’s rule and the Hadamard bound
$$ |\det H|\leq B^{B/2}Q^B $$
give polynomial bit length for every supported mass.

Theorem. The general threshold decision problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

Proof. Suppose a popular critical allocation has cardinality at least $K$. Decompose its distribution into vertices. By Section 4, every vertex used is popular, and at least one has cardinality at least $K$.

That vertex has a polynomial-size certificate by the sparse-vertex lemma and determinant bound. A verifier computes $D_*$, checks its balance equations, deficiency, and cardinality, then runs the compact popularity program. All these operations take polynomial bit time. $\square$

This upper bound applies to arbitrary binary quotas.

7. An explicit exhaustive algorithm

The total number of configurations is
$$ S=\sum_t|\mathcal B_t| =\sum_t\binom{q_t^++d_t}{d_t}. $$
Since every coordinate is between $0$ and $Q$,
$$ S\leq\tau(Q+1)^\Delta. $$

Enumerate supports of size at most $r=m+\tau+1$. For each support:

  • Test whether its equality columns are independent.
  • Solve the equality system and check consistency and nonnegativity.
  • Verify popularity using the compact program.
  • Retain the popular candidate with greatest cardinality.

Every vertex occurs in this enumeration. Section 4 proves that the retained candidate, if any, has maximum cardinality among all popular critical allocations. If none is retained, no popular critical allocation exists.

The running time is bounded by
$$ (S+1)^{m+\tau+1}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L), $$
and hence by
$$ \bigl(1+\tau(Q+1)^\Delta\bigr)^{m+\tau+1} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

For $Q=1$,
$$ S=\sum_t(d_t+1)=2m+\tau, $$
giving
$$ (2m+\tau+1)^{m+\tau+1}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

These bounds are exhaustive-search bounds, not polynomial-time bounds for unrestricted inputs.

8. Universal existence still fails

Here is a self-contained counterexample.

Take
$$ I=\{a\},\qquad J=\{b_1,b_2,b_3\}, $$
with all three pairs acceptable. Set
$$ w_a=3,\qquad w_{b_1}=w_{b_2}=w_{b_3}=1, \qquad q_t^-=q_t^+=1. $$
Type $a$ ranks
$$ b_1\succ_a b_2\succ_a b_3. $$
Every leaf has only neighbor $a$.

For every allocation,
$$ D(M)=6-2C(M). $$
Partitioning $X_a$ into three pieces of measure one and matching each piece to its corresponding leaf attains $C=3$ and $D=0$. Thus every critical allocation matches everyone almost everywhere.

Fix any critical allocation $M$. Let $A_j$ be the center individuals assigned to $b_j$; each has measure one. Using measure-preserving bijections between these sets, construct a rival $N$ so that:

  • individuals in $A_1$ receive the partnerships formerly held in $A_3$;
  • individuals in $A_2$ receive those formerly held in $A_1$;
  • individuals in $A_3$ receive those formerly held in $A_2$.

Such bijections exist modulo null sets for equal-measure Lebesgue subsets, for example through their cumulative-measure maps and generalized inverses. The resulting allocation remains measure-preserving and fully matched, so it is critical.

Under identity individual pairings, the center’s margin favoring $N$ is
$$ -1+1+1=1. $$
Every leaf still has a partner of type $a$, so all leaf votes are zero. Thus $N$ defeats $M$.

No critical allocation in this society is popular. Its parameters are
$$ m=3,\qquad \tau=4,\qquad Q=1. $$

The universal existence assertion is false. Conditional on existence, maximum cardinality is attained, and the threshold problem has polynomial-size certificates with polynomial-time verification.

The unresolved step is finding such a certificate in polynomial time or proving that this search is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard. The exhaustive algorithm above does not settle that distinction.
