The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over types \(t=(p_t,w_t)\), where \(p_t\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), \(w_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and each individual has mass \(1/N\), along with a rational prediction \(\hat{o}\) and \(c\in[0,1)\) such that \(cN\) is integral, construct a deterministic mechanism \(F(\mu,\hat{o})\), computable in time polynomial in support size \(\tau\) and input bit length \(L\). A unilateral report changes one individual’s location and moves mass \(1/N\) between types, with that individual’s public weight fixed. Writing \(C_\mu(f)=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\lVert f-p_t\rVert_2\), \(o_\mu\in\arg\min_f C_\mu(f)\), and \(W_{\min},W_{\max}\) for the least and greatest supported weights, require strategyproofness and \(C_\mu(F(\mu,o_\mu))/C_\mu(o_\mu)\leq\frac{\sqrt{(1+c)^2W_{\min}^2+(1-c)^2W_{\max}^2}}{(1+c)W_{\min}}\), with \(C_\mu(F(\mu,\hat{o}))/C_\mu(o_\mu)\leq\frac{\sqrt{(1-c)^2W_{\min}^2+(1+c)^2W_{\max}^2}}{(1-c)W_{\min}}\) for every prediction \(\hat{o}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Types are \(t=(p_t,w_t)\), with \(p_t\in\mathbb Q^2\), public \(w_t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
Input also includes binary-encoded \(N\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(c\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1)\), with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(cN\in\mathbb Z\), and a prediction \(\hat o\in\mathbb Q^2\). Each individual has mass \(1/N\); \(N\) and \(c\) are fixed independently of reports. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length and \(m=2\) the spatial dimension.
A deterministic mechanism \(F_{N,c}(\mu,\hat o)\) chooses one facility in \(\mathbb R^2\), without payments. For strategic and approximation guarantees it must extend to real locations and predictions; computation is required on rational inputs.
An individual of type \((p,w)\) may report any \(q\in\mathbb R^2\), producing \(\mu'=\mu-N^{-1}\delta_{(p,w)}+N^{-1}\delta_{(q,w)}\). Strategyproofness requires \(w\lVert F_{N,c}(\mu,\hat o)-p\rVert_2\leq w\lVert F_{N,c}(\mu',\hat o)-p\rVert_2\) for every such move, with the prediction fixed.
Write \(C_\mu(f)=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\lVert f-p_t\rVert_2\), \(C_\mu^*=\min_{f\in\mathbb R^2}C_\mu(f)\), and \(W_{\min}=\min_t w_t\), \(W_{\max}=\max_t w_t\).
Define \(\alpha_c=\frac{\sqrt{(1+c)^2W_{\min}^2+(1-c)^2W_{\max}^2}}{(1+c)W_{\min}}\) and \(\beta_c=\frac{\sqrt{(1-c)^2W_{\min}^2+(1+c)^2W_{\max}^2}}{(1-c)W_{\min}}\).
The simultaneous guarantees are \(C_\mu(F_{N,c}(\mu,o))\leq\alpha_c C_\mu^*\) for every \(o\in\arg\min C_\mu\), and \(C_\mu(F_{N,c}(\mu,\hat o))\leq\beta_c C_\mu^*\) for every prediction; these inequalities also apply when \(C_\mu^*=0\).
Does there exist a deterministic strategyproof mechanism achieving both guarantees uniformly over these societies, computable on rational inputs in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log N)\), or are these simultaneous guarantees unattainable within that time bound?
For explicitly listed finite agents, deterministic strategyproof mechanisms achieve the stated \(\alpha_c\) and \(\beta_c\) bounds. [Shi–Xue](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06509v3)
For equal weights, the verified finite-agent bounds are \(\sqrt{2+2c^2}/(1+c)\) and \(\sqrt{2+2c^2}/(1-c)\). [Agrawal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.01120) No result addressing exactly the support-encoded problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made N an explicit positive integer input, encoded in binary, and required N times every supported mass to be a positive integer so that the specified individual reporting move is well-defined.
  • Required public weights and c to be rational so that the computational input has a finite binary encoding; included all input data in L.
  • Made N, c, and the prediction fixed during a unilateral deviation, retained the individual's public weight, and allowed reports to create new supported types.
  • Required a mathematical extension to real locations and predictions, while imposing the bit-complexity bound only on rational inputs, so exact consistency remains meaningful when an optimal location is irrational.
  • Required consistency for every optimal location, resolving unspecified selection among multiple optima.
  • Replaced cost ratios by equivalent multiplicative inequalities on positive-optimum instances and extended them to zero optimum, requiring zero output cost in that case.

2. Definition on the support

$$ n_t=N\mu_t. $$
The input hypotheses imply
$$ n_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad \sum_t n_t=N. $$

$$ h=cN, \qquad K=N+h, \qquad r=\left\lceil\frac K2\right\rceil. $$
Thus $r$ is the lower-median rank among $K$ entries, including when $K$ is even.

$$ (p_{t,j},n_t)\quad\text{for every supported type }t, $$
together with $(\hat o_j,h)$ if $h>0$.

$$ F_{N,c}(\mu,\hat o)=(f_1,f_2). $$
Ties between entries have no effect because their coordinate values coincide.

To verify the definition, conceptually expand each type into $n_t$ individuals. The sorted coordinate multiset of those individuals, augmented by $h$ prediction copies, has exactly the multiplicities used above. Consequently, our output is exactly the finite-agent CMP output.

This definition also applies to real locations and predictions: the coordinate values can be sorted mathematically without requiring rational representations. It therefore supplies the required extension to real reports.

3. Direct proof of strategyproofness

Fix $N,c$, the prediction, and every report except one individual’s report. We first analyze a single coordinate.

The other individuals and the prediction copies contribute $K-1$ fixed coordinate values. Write their sorted values as
$$ s_1\leq\cdots\leq s_{K-1}. $$
Use the conventions
$$ s_0=-\infty, \qquad s_K=+\infty, $$
and set
$$ a=s_{r-1}, \qquad b=s_r. $$

If the remaining individual reports coordinate $z$, the $r$-th order statistic of the augmented list is
$$ g(z)=\min\{b,\max\{a,z\}\}. $$
Indeed:

  • If $z<a$, inserting $z$ shifts the previous $(r-1)$-st value to rank $r$, giving $a$.
  • If $a\leq z\leq b$, the value at rank $r$ is $z$.
  • If $z>b$, the previous $r$-th value remains at rank $r$, giving $b$.

The same statements cover boundary ranks through the infinite-endpoint conventions.

Let $x$ be the individual’s true coordinate. The value $g(x)$ is the closest point to $x$ in the interval $[a,b]$. Every possible outcome $g(z)$ lies in that interval. Hence
$$ |g(x)-x|\leq |g(z)-x| $$
for every real report $z$.

Apply this argument separately to both coordinates. If $f$ is the truthful outcome and $f'$ is the outcome after any report $q\in\mathbb R^2$, then
$$ |f_j-p_j|\leq |f'_j-p_j| \qquad \text{for }j=1,2. $$
Squaring, adding, and taking square roots yields
$$ \|f-p\|_2\leq\|f'-p\|_2. $$
Multiplication by the individual’s positive public weight $w$ proves the required strategyproofness inequality.

A support change representing one individual’s deviation changes exactly one entry of the conceptual expanded profile. Since $N,c$ and the prediction stay fixed, the argument applies even when the deviation creates or removes a supported type.

4. Transfer of both approximation guarantees

Consider the conceptual expanded profile
$$ P=((p_i,w_i))_{i=1}^N, $$
containing $n_t$ copies of each supported type $t$. Define its average weighted cost by
$$ D_P(f)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N w_i\|f-p_i\|_2. $$
For every facility location $f$,
$$ D_P(f) = \sum_t\frac{n_t}{N}w_t\|f-p_t\|_2 = C_\mu(f). $$
Therefore
$$ \min_f D_P(f)=C_\mu^*, \qquad \arg\min_f D_P(f)=\arg\min_f C_\mu(f). $$
The expanded profile also has exactly the same minimum and maximum public weights as the support.

All hypotheses of the stipulated finite-agent guarantee are thus preserved:

  • The objective, its minimizers, and its weight extrema agree with those of the society.

Zero optimal cost

We verify this case directly, without using approximation ratios.

The cost function is continuous and coercive. In fact, with
$$ A=\sum_t\mu_t w_t>0, \qquad B=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\|p_t\|_2, $$
the triangle inequality gives
$$ C_\mu(f)\geq A\|f\|_2-B. $$
It therefore attains its minimum.

If $C_\mu^*=0$, every term at a minimizer must vanish because every coefficient $\mu_t w_t$ is positive. Thus all supported locations equal one point $s$.

Since $h<N$,
$$ h<r\leq N. $$
In each coordinate, there are $N$ copies of $s_j$ and only $h$ prediction copies. At most $h<r$ values lie below $s_j$, while at least $N\geq r$ values lie at or below $s_j$. The selected coordinate is therefore $s_j$, regardless of the prediction.

Hence
$$ F_{N,c}(\mu,\hat o)=s, \qquad C_\mu(F_{N,c}(\mu,\hat o))=0. $$
Both required inequalities hold when $C_\mu^*=0$.
