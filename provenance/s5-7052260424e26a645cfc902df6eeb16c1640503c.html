The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite machine set \(K\), a finite support \(T\) of job types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, compatible-machine sets \(E_t\), public values \(v_{tk}\), capacity uses \(c_{tk}\), and per-capita machine capacities \(b_k\), does there exist a polynomial-time universally truthful direct mechanism with a constant approximation to \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_x\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k\in E_t}v_{tk}x_{tk}\), subject to \(0\le x_{tk}\), \(\sum_k x_{tk}\le\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t c_{tk}x_{tk}\le b_k\)? Each individual job must receive at most one machine; its report may withhold compatible pairs, and truthful reporting must maximize its assigned-value utility in every deterministic component of the mechanism’s lottery. The mechanism must implement measurable individual assignments realizing the aggregate allocation and obey capacities for every report profile.

A society is a labeled atomless probability space of total mass \(1\), represented by the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=|K|\geq1\) machines. Type \(t\) specifies a private compatibility set \(E_t\subseteq K\) and public binary-rational values \(v_{tk}>0\) and capacity-use densities \(c_{tk}>0\); machine capacities \(b_k>0\) are public binary rationals.
Each individual privately reports any subset \(B\subseteq E_t\), including the empty set. There are no payments. An individual assigned machine \(k\) receives utility \(v_{tk}\); an unassigned individual receives zero. Equal values mean indifference; any auxiliary tie-breaking must be fixed independently of reports.
A deterministic direct mechanism uses public data and submitted reports to assign each individual at most one reported machine, measurably. If \(x_{tk}\) is the mass of type \(t\) assigned to \(k\), feasibility requires \(x_{tk}\geq0\), \(\sum_{k\in E_t}x_{tk}\leq\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t:k\in E_t}c_{tk}x_{tk}\leq b_k\). Capacities constrain these integrals, not the capacity use of a single zero-mass individual.
Universal truthfulness means a report-independent lottery over deterministic mechanisms in each of which every individual weakly maximizes assigned-value utility by reporting its full compatibility set, holding every other report fixed. This requirement is pointwise, including unilateral changes on singleton sets; profiles are not identified modulo null sets. Every component must remain feasible for every measurable report profile.
At truthful reports, define \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max\{\sum_t\sum_{k\in E_t}v_{tk}x_{tk}:x\text{ satisfies the preceding constraints}\}\). Welfare is the integral of assigned values. Computational inputs use finite support with binary-rational masses; reported refinements have their own support size. Polynomial time means \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), where \(L\) is total input bit length, for computing aggregates and a finite description of a measurable individual assignment rule realizing them.

Does this problem admit a polynomial-time universally truthful direct mechanism whose expected welfare is at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/C\) on every truthful input, for some absolute constant \(C\geq1\), or is every such constant-factor guarantee impossible?

1. An impossibility result for rules using only aggregate masses and the individual’s report

$$ A\cap S^\omega_\alpha. $$

Thus the assignment rule may depend on the label, the aggregate $\alpha$, public data, and the individual’s own report. Its offer set cannot otherwise depend on the labeled set $A$.

This is an additional restriction. The full task permits assignment rules with more extensive dependence on the submitted profile.

$$ \lambda(S^\omega_\alpha)\leq b $$
for every deterministic component $\omega$.

Proof. Fix a component and abbreviate its offer set by $S$. Suppose $\lambda(S)>b$.

Because both $\alpha$ and $\lambda(S)$ exceed $b$, choose
$$ b<\gamma<\min\{\alpha,\lambda(S)\}. $$
Lebesgue measure is atomless, so there is a measurable $D\subseteq S$ with $\lambda(D)=\gamma$. Extend $D$ to a measurable set $A$ of mass $\alpha$. Such an extension exists because the complement of $D$ has mass $1-\gamma\geq\alpha-\gamma$.

contradicting feasibility. $\square$

1.3 No constant approximation

$$ \alpha=\frac1N, \qquad b=\frac1{2N}. $$
All these numbers are dyadic rationals.

$$ \lambda(S^\omega_\alpha)\leq b. $$

$$ \sum_{j=1}^{N} \mathbb E_\omega\!\left[\lambda(Q_j\cap S^\omega_\alpha)\right] = \mathbb E_\omega\!\left[\lambda(S^\omega_\alpha)\right] \leq b. $$

2. Null-invariant reconstruction on separable measure algebras

Let $(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)$ be a probability space. Its measure algebra consists of measurable sets identified when their symmetric difference is null, with metric
$$ d(A,B)=\mu(A\triangle B). $$
Assume this metric space is separable.

All constructions below are fixed independently of reports.

2.1 A fixed sequence of finite partitions

Choose a countable dense family of measurable sets
$$ D_1,D_2,\ldots. $$
Let $\mathcal P_n$ be the finite measurable partition generated by $D_1,\ldots,D_n$. These partitions refine one another.

The family of all unions of cells of all $\mathcal P_n$ is dense in the measure algebra: it contains each $D_j$.

For a measurable set $A$, define
$$ U_n(A)= \bigcup_{\substack{C\in\mathcal P_n\\ \mu(A\cap C)>\mu(C)/2}}C. $$
The strict inequality fixes the tie convention independently of reports.

Within a cell $C$, excluding that cell incurs disagreement mass $\mu(A\cap C)$, while including it incurs disagreement mass $\mu(C)-\mu(A\cap C)$. Hence
$$ e_n(A):=\mu(A\triangle U_n(A)) = \sum_{C\in\mathcal P_n} \min\{\mu(A\cap C),\,\mu(C)-\mu(A\cap C)\}. $$
In particular, $U_n(A)$ minimizes disagreement with $A$ among unions of cells of $\mathcal P_n$.

Density and refinement imply
$$ e_n(A)\longrightarrow0. $$
Indeed, given $\varepsilon>0$, choose a union $D$ of cells of some $\mathcal P_N$ with $\mu(A\triangle D)<\varepsilon$. For every $n\geq N$, the set $D$ remains a union of cells, so minimality gives $e_n(A)<\varepsilon$.

2.2 A canonical representative

For each positive integer $\ell$, let $n_\ell(A)$ be the least $n$ satisfying
$$ e_n(A)\leq2^{-\ell}. $$
The preceding convergence proves that this index exists.

Define
$$ R(A)= \bigcup_{q=1}^{\infty} \bigcap_{\ell\geq q}U_{n_\ell(A)}(A). $$
Thus $R(A)$ contains exactly the points eventually contained in all the selected approximants.

Lemma. The map $R$ has both properties:
$$ \mu(R(A)\triangle A)=0, $$
and
$$ \mu(A\triangle A')=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad R(A)=R(A')\ \text{pointwise}. $$

Proof. For the first property, let
$$ E_\ell=A\triangle U_{n_\ell(A)}(A). $$
Then $\mu(E_\ell)\leq2^{-\ell}$. The set of points belonging to infinitely many $E_\ell$ has measure at most
$$ \inf_q\sum_{\ell\geq q}2^{-\ell}=0. $$
Outside this null set, membership in the selected approximants eventually agrees with membership in $A$, which proves the first property.

For the second property, null-equivalent sets satisfy
$$ \mu(A\cap C)=\mu(A'\cap C) $$
for every cell of every fixed partition. Their approximants, errors, selected indices, and final sets are therefore exactly identical. $\square$

This construction is measurable and explicit in terms of a fixed countable family of measure queries. It does not use a differentiation theorem.

2.3 Reconstructing a finite report partition

Let $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ be the positive-mass groups determined by public coefficients and submitted reports. Order their identifiers by a fixed order on public coefficient vectors and report subsets.

Set
$$ H_j=R(A_j)\setminus\bigcup_{i<j}R(A_i). $$
The sets $H_j$ are measurable and pointwise disjoint. Moreover,
$$ \mu(H_j\triangle A_j)=0. $$
To see this, each $R(A_j)$ differs from $A_j$ only on a null set, and intersections of different $R(A_j)$ are consequently null. Removing finitely many such intersections preserves the claimed equality.

The uncovered set is null because the positive-mass report groups cover the society almost everywhere.

Most importantly, changing reports on a measurable null set leaves every $H_j$ unchanged at every label.

3. Optimal pointwise truthful offers from the reconstruction

Divisibility lemma. If $A$ is measurable and $0\leq a\leq\mu(A)$, there is a measurable subset of $A$ of mass $a$.

$$ s_n=\sup\{\mu(B):B\subseteq T_n,\ B\text{ measurable},\ \mu(B)\leq r_n\}. $$
The small-subset property gives $s_n>0$. Choose $B_n$ with
$$ s_n/2<\mu(B_n)\leq r_n. $$

$$ T_\infty=A\setminus\bigcup_n B_n $$

$$ \mu(T_\infty)=\mu(A)-a+r. $$

$$ 0<\mu(D)\leq r. $$
This same $D$ is eligible in the definition of every $s_n$, because $D\subseteq T_n$ and $r_n\geq r$. Thus every selected mass exceeds $\mu(D)/2$, contradicting the finite total selected mass.

3.2 The reported optimization problem

For each positive-mass reported group $j$, let:

  • $B_j$ be its submitted compatibility set;

$$ \begin{aligned} \max\quad &\sum_j\sum_{k\in B_j}v_{jk}x_{jk}\\ \text{subject to}\quad &x_{jk}\geq0,\\ &\sum_{k\in B_j}x_{jk}\leq\alpha_j,\\ &\sum_{j:k\in B_j}c_{jk}x_{jk}\leq b_k. \end{aligned} $$
Its feasible region is nonempty and compact: zero is feasible and every coordinate is bounded by $\alpha_j$. An optimum therefore exists.

3.3 Exact realization by ranks

For each reconstructed group $H_j$, define
$$ F_j(u)=\mu\bigl(H_j\cap\{i:h(i)\leq u\}\bigr). $$
For $u<w$,
$$ 0\leq F_j(w)-F_j(u) \leq\mu\{i:u<h(i)\leq w\}=w-u. $$
Thus $F_j$ is continuous, with
$$ F_j(0)=0, \qquad F_j(1)=\alpha_j. $$

The following rank identity supplies exact assigned masses:
$$ \mu\{i\in H_j:a\leq F_j(h(i))<b\}=b-a \qquad (0\leq a\leq b\leq\alpha_j). $$

To verify it, fix $z\in(0,\alpha_j]$ and let
$$ t_z=\inf\{u:F_j(u)\geq z\}. $$
Continuity gives $F_j(t_z)=z$. By monotonicity,
$$ \{u:F_j(u)<z\}=\{u:u<t_z\}. $$
The distribution of $h$ restricted to $H_j$ has no point masses, because the unrestricted distribution is uniform. Therefore
$$ \mu\{i\in H_j:F_j(h(i))<z\} = \mu\{i\in H_j:h(i)<t_z\} = F_j(t_z)=z. $$
For $z=0$, the corresponding mass is zero. Taking differences proves the rank identity.

$$ i\in H_j, \qquad s_{j,\ell-1}\leq F_j(h(i))<s_{j,\ell}. $$

The rank identity shows that the offered mass for each group-machine pair is exactly $x_{jk}$. Almost every member of $H_j$ belongs to $A_j$ and accepts the offer, so the acceptance check preserves these masses.

All sets and functions in the offer rule are measurable. The realized masses equal the feasible optimization solution, proving feasibility and reported-optimal welfare.

Now consider measurable profiles differing only at one individual. Their disagreement singleton is measurable, since the profiles are finite-valued and measurable. Atomlessness makes its mass zero.

Consequently, the change preserves:

  • all reported group masses;
  • the selected optimization solution;

  • the entire offer function, pointwise.

Thus full reporting weakly maximizes utility at every individual, including exceptional labels.

Proven conclusion. On every atomless space with separable measure algebra, this construction is an optimal deterministic pointwise truthful mechanism. It is universally truthful through a lottery concentrated on that mechanism.

4. A terminating polynomial implementation with finite interval descriptions

This section adds a representation hypothesis:

Labels are $[0,1]$ with Lebesgue measure, and each public-data/report group is supplied as a finite union of intervals with rational endpoints, up to null discrepancies. The current pointwise report remains available to the final acceptance check.

The endpoint descriptions count toward input length. This is stronger than the mass-list representation in the task.

4.1 A null-invariant rule with a simple branch

For an arbitrary measurable reported group $A_j$, define
$$ O_j= \bigcup_{\substack{I\text{ in a fixed countable}\\ \text{rational relative-open interval basis}\\ \lambda(I\setminus A_j)=0}}I. $$
Thus $O_j$ consists of labels having a neighborhood occupied by group $j$ almost everywhere.

Three facts follow directly.

First, $O_j$ is relatively open and measurable.

Second,
$$ \lambda(O_j\setminus A_j)=0, $$
because it is a countable union of sets with that property.

Third, distinct $O_j$ are pointwise disjoint. If two defining neighborhoods overlapped at a label, their intersection would be a nonempty relative-open interval of positive measure. Almost all of that intersection would have to belong to two disjoint report groups, a contradiction.

Every $O_j$ depends only on the null-equivalence class of $A_j$.

Define a mechanism as follows:

  • If $\lambda(\bigcup_jO_j)=1$, use the sets $O_j$ as the reconstructed groups in Section 3, with $h(u)=u$.
  • Otherwise, use the reconstruction of Section 2 from the fixed dyadic interval partitions.

The branch condition is null-invariant. In the first branch, each $\lambda(O_j)\leq\alpha_j$, and the sums of both families of masses equal $1$. Hence
$$ \lambda(O_j)=\alpha_j $$
for every $j$, so $O_j$ and $A_j$ differ only on a null set.

Therefore both branches are optimal, feasible, and pointwise truthful by the proof in Section 3. This defines the mechanism on all measurable profiles, including unilateral singleton changes.

4.2 Why finite interval inputs need no limiting computation

When the groups have the promised finite interval representations, sort all endpoints and inspect the group on each positive-length interval between consecutive endpoints. Merge adjacent intervals carrying the same group identifier.

This computes the $O_j$ exactly:

  • redundant boundaries within one group disappear;
  • boundaries between different groups remain excluded;
  • null discrepancies in actual reports have no effect.

Only finitely many boundary labels are uncovered, so the first branch applies.

On each interval component of $O_j$,
$$ F_j(u)=\lambda(O_j\cap[0,u]) $$
is an explicitly computable affine function with slope $1$. On gaps it is constant. Its values at endpoints are rational sums of interval lengths.

If there are $R$ interval pieces and $n$ positive-mass groups, the optimization problem has at most $nm$ variables and $n+m$ aggregate constraints. It is a nonempty bounded rational linear program. The standard polynomial-time rational linear-programming theorem therefore computes an exact optimal rational solution with polynomial encoding length. Fixed lexicographic selection requires at most $nm$ further rational linear programs.

The assignment rule consists of:

  • the finitely many interval components;
  • the affine formulas for $F_j$;
  • the rational allocation thresholds;
  • the actual-report acceptance check.

Walking through a group’s interval components in label order introduces at most one new cut per allocation threshold. Consequently, a description with $O(R+nm)$ pieces suffices.

All sorting, rational arithmetic, optimization, and description generation take polynomial time in the augmented input length. No approximation search or density-limit evaluation remains in this branch.

Proven conclusion. With this additional interval representation, an optimal deterministic pointwise truthful mechanism is polynomial-time implementable.

This does not establish the same bound when the input supplies only masses and coefficient/report identifiers.

5. A sharp obstruction to fixed countable reconstruction on arbitrary spaces

The separability assumption in Section 2 has an exact relationship to its reconstruction method.

A measure algebra is separable if and only if there exists a sequence of finite measurable partitions whose cell unions are dense in measure.

The forward implication was proved in Section 2. For the reverse implication, the union of the finite families of cell unions is countable and dense, which is precisely separability.

There are atomless spaces lacking this property.

5.1 An uncountable Bernoulli space

Let $I$ be uncountable and consider the completed product probability space
$$ \Omega=\{0,1\}^{I}, $$
with independent fair coordinate bits.

Every set in the uncompleted product sigma-algebra depends on only countably many coordinates. Indeed, sets with this property form a sigma-algebra containing every finite-coordinate cylinder: complements preserve the coordinate set, and countable unions use the countable union of the coordinate sets.

Every completed measurable set therefore agrees almost everywhere with a set depending on countably many coordinates.

The space is atomless. Given a positive-mass measurable set $C$, choose an almost-everywhere equal representative depending on a countable coordinate set $J$. A coordinate outside $J$ divides $C$ into two measurable pieces, each of mass $\mu(C)/2$.

5.2 Every fixed countable partition sequence misses a report group

Fix any sequence of finite measurable partitions $\mathcal P_n$. There are countably many cells altogether, so their representatives collectively depend on a countable coordinate set $J$.

Choose $q\in I\setminus J$ and define
$$ A=\{\omega:\omega_q=1\}. $$
Then $\mu(A)=1/2$, and independence gives, for every cell $C$,
$$ \mu(A\cap C)=\frac12\mu(C). $$

For any union $U$ of cells of any $\mathcal P_n$,
$$ \mu(A\triangle U) = \mu(A)+\mu(U)-2\mu(A\cap U) = \frac12. $$
Thus those partitions cannot approximate $A$ at all.

The failure already occurs for the two-group partition $A,\Omega\setminus A$, with rational masses $1/2$ and $1/2$.

This rules out extending the fixed-countable-partition reconstruction to every arbitrary atomless space. It is not an impossibility theorem for other mechanisms or other assignment-description languages.
