The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many consumer types \(t\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), finite goods sets \(V_t\), compactly represented monotone supermodular utilities \(F_t:2^{V_t}\to\mathbb{R}_+\), prices \(p_t\), and budgets \(b_t\), compute a utility-maximizing mass allocation \(x_{t,S}\) over integral bundles \(S\subseteq V_t\) satisfying \(p_t(S)\le b_t\), with \(\sum_{S:p_t(S)\le b_t}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(x_{t,S}\ge0\). In the one-type, unit-price case with \(b_t=k\), determine the complexity of maximizing \(\sum_{S:|S|\le k}x_{t,S}F_t(S)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a nonempty goods set \(V_t\), strictly positive rational item prices \(p_{t,v}\), a nonnegative rational budget \(b_t\), and a weakly monotone supermodular utility \(F_t:2^{V_t}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\). Utilities are supplied as Boolean circuits mapping bundle incidence vectors to binary numerators and positive denominators; monotonicity and supermodularity are promises, and no normalization of \(F_t(\varnothing)\) is imposed.
Write \(m=|\bigcup_t V_t|\), \(p_t(S)=\sum_{v\in S}p_{t,v}\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the circuits. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and the budget parameter \(k\) below.
An allocation assigns nonnegative real masses \(x_{t,S}\) to integral bundles affordable individually, with \(\sum_{S:p_t(S)\le b_t}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\). Its welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_t\sum_{S:p_t(S)\le b_t}x_{t,S}F_t(S)\); these are the only constraints. Represent a computed allocation by its nonzero bundle masses as binary rationals.
Consider the restriction \(\tau=1\), \(\mu_1=1\), unit item prices, and integer budget \(1\le k\le m\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the maximum welfare over feasible allocations.

For each fixed constant \(c\ge1\), is there a polynomial-time algorithm producing a feasible mass allocation with \(W(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}/2^{m^c}\), or is such an approximation impossible assuming \(\mathrm{NP}\not\subseteq\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\mathrm{BPTIME}(2^{n^\varepsilon})\)?

For a single integral bundle, monotone supermodular maximization under a cardinality constraint has no polynomial-time \(2^{m^c}\)-approximation for any fixed \(c\ge1\), under that assumption ([source](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~dgolovin/papers/revealpref.pdf)).
No result settling this precise mass-allocation formulation was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized positive rational type masses to sum to one because the society is a distribution over types.
  • Made compact utility representation precise using Boolean evaluation circuits with binary-rational outputs and included their descriptions in the input bit length.
  • Used strictly positive additive item prices and nonnegative budgets, following the consumer-choice domains.
  • Interpreted monotonicity weakly and left empty-bundle utility unnormalized, consistent with the anchor's constructed utilities.
  • Specified nonempty goods sets and the integer cardinality-budget domain 1 <= k <= m.

Conclusion

For every fixed constant \(c\ge 1\), the requested polynomial-time approximation is impossible under the stated assumption. Explicitly represented mass allocations and single integral bundles are equivalent for this approximation problem: a feasible allocation always contains a listed bundle whose utility is at least the allocation’s welfare.

Here \(k\) is allowed to vary with the input. Fixed \(k\), and the endpoint \(k=m\), admit exact polynomial-time algorithms.

1. The allocation optimum equals the bundle optimum

Under the restriction in the question, define the finite feasible family \(\mathcal B_k=\{S\subseteq V:|S|\le k\}\) and let \(M=\max_{S\in\mathcal B_k}F(S)\).

A feasible allocation satisfies \(x_S\ge 0\) and \(\sum_{S\in\mathcal B_k}x_S=1\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle W(x)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal B_k}x_SF(S)\le\sum_{S\in\mathcal B_k}x_SM=M.\)

Conversely, choose a maximizing bundle \(S^\star\in\mathcal B_k\). Assigning mass \(1\) to \(S^\star\) is feasible and has welfare \(M\). Thus \(\mathrm{OPT}=M\).

This argument uses only the allocation constraints. It does not require monotonicity, supermodularity, or normalization at the empty bundle.

2. Extracting a bundle without losing approximation quality

Lemma. From an explicitly represented feasible allocation, one can compute an affordable integral bundle \(S^\dagger\) with \(F(S^\dagger)\ge W(x)\), in time polynomial in the input and allocation lengths.

Proof. Write the allocation’s nonzero entries as \((S_i,a_i)\), for \(1\le i\le r\). Feasibility gives \(|S_i|\le k\), \(a_i>0\), and \(\sum_{i=1}^r a_i=1\). In particular, \(r\ge 1\).

Evaluate the utility circuit on every listed bundle and choose \(S^\dagger\) with maximum utility. Then

\(\displaystyle W(x)=\sum_{i=1}^r a_iF(S_i)\le\sum_{i=1}^r a_iF(S^\dagger)=F(S^\dagger).\)

All comparisons are exact. If two circuit outputs represent \(u/v\) and \(u'/v'\), their positive denominators permit comparison by the integer products \(uv'\) and \(u'v\). Each circuit output has bit length \(O(L)\), so schoolbook arithmetic takes \(O(L^2)\) time per comparison.

If the allocation encoding has length \(B\), extraction takes \(O(B+r(m+L^2))\) bit operations. This proves the lemma.

Now suppose an allocation algorithm runs in polynomial time \(T(m,1,L,k)\). Its explicit output length satisfies \(B\le T(m,1,L,k)\), up to the fixed machine-model overhead. Running that algorithm and applying the lemma therefore takes polynomial time in \(m,L,k\).

If its guarantee were \(W(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}/2^{m^c}\), the extracted bundle would satisfy

\(\displaystyle F(S^\dagger)\ge W(x)\ge\frac{\max_{|S|\le k}F(S)}{2^{m^c}}.\)

Thus it would be an integral-bundle approximation with exactly the forbidden factor. The extraction also preserves any per-run approximation success event of a randomized algorithm.

3. Compatibility with the circuit input model

We use the integral-bundle hardness given in the question, stated as Theorem 17 of [Echenique, Golovin, and Wierman](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~dgolovin/papers/revealpref.pdf). Its hard construction uses \(F(S)=2^{e_G(S)^d}\), where \(e_G(S)\) counts edges induced by \(S\), and \(d\) is a fixed positive integer chosen for the desired approximation exponent.

These utilities meet the promises. The edge-count function is nonnegative and monotone. Its marginal for adding a vertex counts neighbors already present, so its marginals increase under inclusion. For \(g(z)=2^{z^d}\), convexity and monotonicity on \(z\ge 0\) imply that \(g(z+a)-g(z)\) is nondecreasing in both \(z\) and \(a\ge 0\). Hence the composition also has increasing marginals and is supermodular.

The encoding is polynomial: compute \(q=e_G(S)^d\), then output the integer having its sole nonzero bit at position \(q\), with denominator \(1\). Since \(q\le\binom m2^d\), both circuit size and construction time are \(m^{O(d)}\). Thus \(L=m^{O(d)}\), and a polynomial-time circuit-input algorithm would run in polynomial time on this hard family. Also \(F(\varnothing)=1\), which is expressly permitted here.

Therefore Section 2 contradicts the supplied hardness theorem under \(\mathrm{NP}\not\subseteq\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\mathrm{BPTIME}(2^{n^\varepsilon})\).

4. Running times and tractable budget cases

The contradiction already occurs at support size \(\tau=1\); no dependence on larger \(\tau\) can avoid it. More generally, because types have no coupling constraints, their optimum is \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\max_{p_t(S)\le b_t}F_t(S)\), achieved by one bundle per type.

For the restricted instance, monotonicity ensures that some optimum has exactly \(k\) items: every smaller feasible bundle extends to a \(k\)-item bundle without decreasing utility. Enumerating all such bundles therefore computes an exact allocation in time \(O\!\left(\binom mk(m+L^2)\right)\).

This is polynomial when \(k\), or \(m-k\), is a fixed constant. For \(k=m\), assigning mass \(1\) to \(V\) is optimal and requires only \(O(m)\) output time. These cases do not settle the problem with unrestricted input parameter \(k\), for which the impossibility above applies.
