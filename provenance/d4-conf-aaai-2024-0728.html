The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), explicit approval types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), and fixed tie-breaking, compute the committee produced by mass-based GJCR followed by AV completion, and compute or threshold-test its normalized utilitarian welfare against the best integral \(k\)-committee.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge 1\) candidates and let \(1\le k\le m\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) distinct approval types \(A_t\subseteq C\) with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets. Fix a total order on candidates for all tie-breaking; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Mass-based GJCR starts with \(W=\varnothing\). For every \(c\in C\setminus W\) and integer \(1\le\ell\le k\), define \(s_{c,\ell}(W)=\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t,\ |A_t\cap W|<\ell}\mu_t\). Among pairs satisfying \(s_{c,\ell}(W)\ge\ell/k\), select a candidate whose pair maximizes \(s_{c,\ell}(W)\), using the fixed order for candidate ties, and add it to \(W\). Stop when no pair qualifies or \(|W|=k\).
AV completion retains \(W\) and fills its remaining seats with candidates of highest mass approval score, using the same order; denote the resulting integral committee by \(\widehat W\).
Define \(U(S)=\sum_t\mu_t|A_t\cap S|\), \(U^*=\max_{S\subseteq C,\ |S|=k}U(S)\), and \(R=U(\widehat W)/U^*\), with \(R=1\) when \(U^*=0\). The computational question is to produce \(\widehat W\) and \(R\), or test \(R\ge q\) for an input binary rational \(q\in[0,1]\); state running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

Does mass-based GJCR followed by AV completion guarantee \(R\ge \frac{2}{\sqrt{k}}-\frac{1}{k}\) on every such input, or is there a counterexample?

For finite voter lists, GJCR followed by AV completion guarantees a utilitarian ratio of at least \(\frac{2}{\sqrt{k}}-\frac{1}{k}\). [Brill and Peters](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.08187)
The literature search located no result explicitly addressing this mass-based welfare guarantee.

Yes. On every input specified in the question, mass-based GJCR followed by AV completion guarantees \(R\ge 2/\sqrt{k}-1/k\). Moreover, the committee, its exact ratio, and the threshold test are computable in polynomial time from the support representation.

The proof uses exact replication of rational masses. No approximation or continuity argument is needed.

2. Exact correspondence with a finite electorate

Write each mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), where \(a_t,b_t\) are positive integers. Set \(D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t\) and \(n_t=a_t(D/b_t)\). Then \(n_t=D\mu_t\) is a positive integer, and normalization gives \(\sum_t n_t=D\).

Construct, conceptually, a finite electorate containing \(n_t\) voters with approval set \(A_t\), for every type \(t\). Its total number of voters is \(n=D\).

Fix a committee \(W\), an unselected candidate \(c\), and \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\). Let \(Q_{c,\ell}(W)\) be all replicated voters who approve \(c\) and have fewer than \(\ell\) approved members of \(W\). By construction,

\( |Q_{c,\ell}(W)|=\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t,\ |A_t\cap W|<\ell}n_t=D\,s_{c,\ell}(W). \)

In finite-voter GJCR, a witness group for this candidate and level is a subset \(N'\subseteq Q_{c,\ell}(W)\) satisfying \(|N'|\ge \ell n/k\). Such a group exists exactly when the entire set \(Q_{c,\ell}(W)\) satisfies that inequality. Consequently,

\( |Q_{c,\ell}(W)|\ge \ell n/k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_{c,\ell}(W)\ge \ell/k. \)

For a fixed pair, the largest possible witness group is \(Q_{c,\ell}(W)\). Thus maximizing witness-group cardinality in finite-voter GJCR is exactly equivalent to maximizing \(s_{c,\ell}(W)\): every score is multiplied by the same positive factor \(D\).

Starting from the empty committee, induction now proves that the two procedures select the same candidate at every step. Indeed, whenever their current committees agree, their qualifying pairs agree, their maximizing candidates agree, and the fixed candidate order resolves ties identically. Their stopping conditions therefore agree as well.

For AV completion, define the mass approval score \(p_c=\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t}\mu_t\). Candidate \(c\)'s finite approval score is \(Dp_c\). Completion therefore also selects exactly the same candidates, with identical tie-breaking.

3. Transfer of the welfare guarantee

The finite-voter result we use is Theorem 9 of [Brill and Peters](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.08187): for every finite approval profile and valid committee size, GJCR followed by AV completion has utilitarian ratio at least \(2/\sqrt{k}-1/k\). Their formulation allows tied outputs, so the specified candidate order is covered.

All hypotheses hold for the replicated electorate: it has a positive finite number \(D\) of voters, approval ballots over the same candidate set, and \(1\le k\le m\). The preceding correspondence identifies its output with \(\widehat W\).

For every candidate set \(S\), its finite utilitarian welfare is

\( \operatorname{uw}_{D}(S)=\sum_t n_t|A_t\cap S|=D\,U(S). \)

Taking maxima over all sets of size \(k\) gives \(\operatorname{uw}_{D}^{*}=D\,U^*\). If \(U^*>0\), the finite theorem therefore yields

\( R=\frac{U(\widehat W)}{U^*}=\frac{\operatorname{uw}_{D}(\widehat W)}{\operatorname{uw}_{D}^{*}}\ge \frac{2}{\sqrt{k}}-\frac{1}{k}. \)

If \(U^*=0\), the prescribed ratio is \(R=1\). This also satisfies the bound, since \(1-(2/\sqrt{k}-1/k)=(1-1/\sqrt{k})^2\ge 0\).

This completes the proof for every input in the question.

4. Exact computation and running time

The electorate need not be expanded. Although \(D\) can be exponentially large numerically, its binary length is \(O(L)\), because \(\log_2 D=\sum_t\log_2 b_t\). Each integer weight \(n_t\) likewise has \(O(L)\) bits.

Maintain \(r_t=|A_t\cap W|\). At each greedy step, for each unselected candidate form the bins

\( h_{c,j}=\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t,\ r_t=j}n_t,\qquad 0\le j<k. \)

Their prefix sums give \(S_{c,\ell}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}h_{c,j}=D\,s_{c,\ell}(W)\). Test qualification using the exact integer inequality \(kS_{c,\ell}\ge \ell D\), and maximize \(S_{c,\ell}\) among qualifying pairs. After selecting \(c\), increment \(r_t\) for precisely the types approving \(c\).

Building the bins and checking their prefixes takes \(O(m(\tau+k))\) arithmetic operations per step. There are at most \(k\) steps. Precompute \(P_c=\sum_{t:\,c\in A_t}n_t\), and sort candidates by \(P_c\), breaking ties by the given order. This supplies both AV completion and an optimal size-\(k\) committee \(S^*\), because

\( U(S)=\sum_{c\in S}p_c. \)

Set \(T=\sum_{c\in\widehat W}P_c\) and \(H=\sum_{c\in S^*}P_c\). Output \(R=T/H\) when \(H>0\), and \(R=1\) otherwise. For \(q=u/v\), with \(v>0\), the positive-denominator case is decided exactly by \(vT\ge uH\).

Including common-denominator preprocessing, the arithmetic-operation bound is

\( O\!\left(\tau+km(\tau+k)+m\log(m+1)\right). \)

All integers used have \(O(L+\log(k+1))\) bits. With schoolbook integer arithmetic, a conservative bit-operation bound for producing \(\widehat W\) and the exact rational ratio, or testing the threshold, is

\( O\!\left(L+\left[\tau+km(\tau+k)+m\log(m+1)\right]\left[L+\log(k+1)\right]^2\right). \)

Here the algorithm scans only the \(\tau\) listed approval types.
