The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) goods, per-capita supplies \(s_j\), finitely many route types \(t\in T\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and a per-person bundle \(x_t\) for each type satisfying route proportions and \(\sum_t \mu_t x_{tj}\le s_j\), decide whether common continuous strictly increasing curves \(f_j\), with \(f_j(0)=0\), make each type’s bundle affordable at unit budget and utility-maximizing for \(u_t(z)=\min_{j\in R_t}z_j\). If so, compute the curve values at the allocated-quantity breakpoints.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge 1\) divisible goods have nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge 1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a nonempty route \(R_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and a prescribed bundle satisfying \(x_{tj}=a_t\) for \(j\in R_t\) and \(x_{tj}=0\) otherwise, where \(a_t\ge 0\) is a binary rational. At least one \(a_t\) is positive, and \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\le s_j\) for every good.
A common price curve for good \(j\) is a continuous strictly increasing function \(f_j:\mathbb R_{\ge 0}\to\mathbb R_{\ge 0}\) with \(f_j(0)=0\). A person's bundle \(z\) costs \(C_f(z)=\sum_j f_j(z_j)\), and every person has budget \(1\).
The curves support the prescribed allocation when, for every type \(t\), \(C_f(x_t)\le 1\) and \(x_t\) maximizes \(u_t(z)=\min_{j\in R_t}z_j\) over all \(z\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^m\) with \(C_f(z)\le 1\). Individual alternatives are not capped by supplies; aggregate feasibility requires only the inequalities above, without supply exhaustion.
On a yes-instance, the computational problem also asks for exact rational values \(f_j(q)\) at every distinct positive allocated quantity \(q\in\{x_{tj}:t\in T\}\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is deciding whether such supporting curves exist in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? If it is in \(\mathrm P\), can the requested breakpoint values also be computed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For finitely many individually listed agents with Leontief utilities and equal budgets, Goel, Hulett, and Plaut establish polynomial-time recognition and construction of supporting strictly increasing price curves. Their result applies in particular to nonempty unweighted routes.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified supplies and prescribed quantities as nonnegative binary rationals, and defined L as the total binary input length.
  • Made route proportions explicit: equal quantities on the nonempty route and zero quantities outside it.
  • Carried over the condition that at least one prescribed bundle is nonempty.
  • Specified additive per-person costs and unrestricted nonnegative individual alternatives, following the discrete demand definition.
  • Retained aggregate supply inequalities without imposing supply exhaustion.
  • Specified exact rational output at distinct positive allocated quantities; the value at zero is fixed by normalization.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Recognition is in \(\mathrm{P}\), and exact rational breakpoint values can also be computed in polynomial time. Both tasks require \((L+m\tau)^{O(1)}\) bit operations.

1. Individual optimality is equivalent to spending the whole budget

For a type \(t\), define the cost of obtaining utility \(b\) using only its route by
\[ B_t(b)=\sum_{j\in R_t}f_j(b). \]
Because \(R_t\) is nonempty, \(B_t\) is continuous and strictly increasing.

We claim that the prescribed bundle is affordable and utility-maximizing exactly when \(B_t(a_t)=1\).

Indeed, affordability gives \(B_t(a_t)\le 1\). If this inequality is strict, continuity gives some \(\delta>0\) with \(B_t(a_t+\delta)<1\). Purchasing quantity \(a_t+\delta\) of every route good then strictly improves utility within budget. Conversely, if \(B_t(a_t)=1\), every bundle \(z\) with \(u_t(z)>a_t\) satisfies \(z_j>a_t\) for every \(j\in R_t\). Thus strict increase and nonnegativity imply
\[ C_f(z)\ge\sum_{j\in R_t}f_j(z_j)> \sum_{j\in R_t}f_j(a_t)=1. \]
Consequently, such a bundle is unaffordable.

In particular, any type with \(a_t=0\) makes the instance a no-instance, since \(B_t(0)=0\). Henceforth assume every \(a_t>0\).

2. An equivalent finite system

List the distinct allocated quantities in increasing order as \(0=q_0<q_1<\cdots<q_K\), where \(K\le\tau\). Let \(\ell(t)\) denote the index satisfying \(a_t=q_{\ell(t)}\).

Introduce one increment variable \(d_{jk}\) for each good \(j\) and index \(k\). Supporting curves necessarily give
\[ d_{jk}=f_j(q_k)-f_j(q_{k-1})>0, \]
and the preceding optimality condition becomes
\[ \sum_{j\in R_t}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell(t)}d_{jk}=1 \qquad(t\in T). \]

These conditions are also sufficient. Given strictly positive increments satisfying the equations, set \(y_{j0}=0\) and \(y_{jk}=\sum_{h=1}^{k}d_{jh}\). Define, for \(q_{k-1}\le b\le q_k\),
\[ f_j(b)=y_{j,k-1}+ \frac{d_{jk}}{q_k-q_{k-1}}(b-q_{k-1}), \]
and, for \(b\ge q_K\), define \(f_j(b)=y_{jK}+b-q_K\).

The formulas agree at their endpoints. Every segment has positive slope, including the final ray, so each curve is continuous, strictly increasing, nonnegative, and zero at zero. The equations ensure that every prescribed bundle costs exactly \(1\); Section 1 proves individual optimality.

Thus supporting curves exist exactly when the finite equations admit strictly positive increments.

3. Handling strict positivity by a bounded linear program

First, we may impose \(d_{jk}\le1\) without changing strict feasibility. If an increment appears in a budget equation, it is a positive summand in a sum equal to \(1\), so it is already at most \(1\). Any increment appearing in no budget equation may be replaced by \(1\).

Now maximize a variable \(\varepsilon\), subject to:

  • \(\sum_{j\in R_t}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell(t)}d_{jk}=1\) for every \(t\in T\);
  • \(0\le\varepsilon\le1\);
  • \(\varepsilon\le d_{jk}\le1\) for every \(j,k\).

If strictly positive increments exist, normalize them as above and take \(\varepsilon=\min_{j,k}d_{jk}>0\). This gives a feasible solution with positive objective value. Conversely, any feasible solution with \(\varepsilon>0\) gives strictly positive increments.

The feasible region is closed and contained in \([0,1]^{mK+1}\), so, when nonempty, an optimum exists. Therefore the decision rule is:

  • Return no if the linear program is infeasible.
  • Otherwise, return yes exactly when its exact optimum \(\varepsilon^\star\) is positive.

This requires exact rational optimization, rather than a numerical tolerance. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming provides infeasibility detection and exact rational optimal solutions of polynomial encoding length; see [Gleixner and Steffy, Section 1.3](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/7507.pdf).

4. Complexity and exact construction

The linear program has \(n=mK+1\) variables, \(\tau\) equality constraints, and \(2mK+2\) inequality constraints. Every coefficient lies in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and every right-hand side lies in \(\{0,1\}\). Its dense binary encoding has length
\[ O\bigl((mK+\tau+1)(mK+1)\bigr)=O\bigl((m\tau+1)^2\bigr). \]
Sorting the input rational quantities and finding their indices takes polynomial time in \(L\). Exact rational linear programming therefore gives recognition in \((L+m\tau)^{O(1)}\) bit operations.

On a yes-instance, obtain an exact rational optimal solution. Output
\[ f_j(q_k)=\sum_{h=1}^{k}d_{jh} \qquad(1\le j\le m,\;1\le k\le K). \]
These are all the requested values, including quantities never allocated on a particular good. Polynomially many additions of polynomial-length rationals take polynomial time and produce polynomial-length outputs. The interpolation in Section 2 also constructs supporting curves with an exact rational description.

For an explicit existence bound on output length, an optimal vertex can be chosen because the feasible region is a nonempty compact polytope. At a vertex, \(n\) linearly independent active constraints determine the coordinates. Their coefficient matrix and right-hand side have entries of absolute value at most \(1\). Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s inequality bound the absolute values of the relevant determinants by \(n^{n/2}\). All coordinates therefore have a common denominator of absolute value at most \(n^{n/2}\); cumulative breakpoint values require only \(O(n\log n+\log K)\) bits each.

Finally, the masses \(\mu_t\) and supplies \(s_j\) enter only the aggregate feasibility condition, which is already assumed. The construction supports the given allocation and hence preserves those inequalities. Since every person of a type faces the same demand problem, one budget equation per listed type suffices. No expansion by mass denominators, or enumeration of types outside the support, is needed.
