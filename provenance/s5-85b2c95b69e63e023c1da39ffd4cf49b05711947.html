The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(U,V\), rational masses \(\mu_u,\nu_v\) summing to \(1\) on each side, rankings over acceptable counterpart types, and deviator types \(D\subseteq U\), does there exist a perfect mass matching \(x\geq 0\) with row sums \(\mu\), column sums \(\nu\), and \(x_{uv}=0\) on unacceptable pairs, such that for every acceptable pair \((u,v)\) with \(u\in D\), \(\left(\sum_{v':\,v\succ_u v'}x_{uv'}=0\right)\lor\left(\sum_{u':\,u\succ_v u'}x_{u'v}=0\right)\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(U,V\) be disjoint, nonempty finite sets of types. Each side is a society given by its support: an explicit list of types with strictly positive binary rational masses \(\mu_u,\nu_v\), with \(\sum_{u\in U}\mu_u=\sum_{v\in V}\nu_v=1\). Let \(\tau=|U|+|V|\) be the total support size, not the number of possible types, \(m\) the number of acceptable type pairs, and \(L\) the total input bit length.
Acceptability is symmetric: an acceptable pair appears in both endpoints' lists. Each type has a strict ranking of its acceptable counterpart types; lists may be incomplete or empty. Let \(D\subseteq U\) be the deviator types; all other types are conformists.
A perfect mass matching is \(x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{U\times V}\) satisfying \(\sum_{v\in V}x_{uv}=\mu_u\), \(\sum_{u\in U}x_{uv}=\nu_v\), and \(x_{uv}=0\) on unacceptable pairs. Mass may be split among partners.
Call \(x\) deviator-stable if every acceptable pair \((u,v)\) with \(u\in D\) satisfies \(\left(\sum_{v':\,v\succ_u v'}x_{uv'}=0\right)\lor\left(\sum_{u':\,u\succ_v u'}x_{u'v}=0\right)\), where sums range over acceptable counterparts. Conformists may participate in blocking pairs with deviators; only pairs between conformists are exempt.

Is deciding whether a deviator-stable perfect mass matching exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

For individual agents with strict incomplete preferences, deciding whether a perfect matching has no blocking pair involving a designated deviator is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, even with lists of length at most \(3\) and deviators confined to one side. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18573v2#Thmtheorem3.1)
No complexity classification for exactly the mass problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified strict rankings and symmetric acceptability, following the discrete definitions; incomplete and empty lists remain allowed.
  • Restricted the support to strictly positive masses and defined tau as the combined support size of the two sides.
  • Defined m as the number of acceptable type pairs and L as the total input bit length, since the graded question did not define size notation.
  • Specified real nonnegative matching entries and permitted mass splitting, as the natural continuous interpretation.

Classification

The problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, even when every preference list has length at most \(3\) and every type has the same dyadic rational mass.

The key is that, for equal masses, a deviator-stable fractional solution can always be rounded to a deviator-stable discrete perfect matching.

1. Stability is preserved by restricting the support

Write \(E\subseteq U\times V\) for the acceptable pairs, and define \(\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{(u,v)\in E:x_{uv}>0\}\).

Lemma 1. Suppose \(x\) is deviator-stable and \(y\) is a nonnegative allocation with \(\operatorname{supp}(y)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x)\). Then \(y\) satisfies every deviator-stability disjunction. If \(y\) also has the prescribed marginals, it is a deviator-stable perfect mass matching.

Proof. Fix an acceptable pair \((u,v)\) with \(u\in D\). Stability of \(x\) says that at least one of its two worse-partner sums is zero. Every summand is nonnegative, so every entry appearing in that zero sum is individually zero in \(x\). Support containment makes each corresponding entry zero in \(y\). Thus the same side of the disjunction holds for \(y\). This applies to every required pair. \(\square\)

2. Equal masses permit discrete rounding

Lemma 2. Suppose \(|U|=|V|=n\) and every type has mass \(1/n\). A deviator-stable perfect mass matching exists if and only if the corresponding individual-agent instance has a perfect matching with no blocking pair involving a deviator.

Proof.

First suppose \(M\) is such a discrete perfect matching. Define \(x_{uv}=1/n\) when \((u,v)\in M\), and \(x_{uv}=0\) otherwise. This has the required marginals. For an acceptable pair \((u,v)\), its two worse-partner sums are both positive precisely when both endpoints strictly prefer each other to their partners in \(M\). Consequently, absence of blocking pairs involving deviators is exactly the required mass stability condition.

Conversely, let \(x\) be a deviator-stable perfect mass matching. Consider the bipartite graph \(H=(U,V,\operatorname{supp}(x))\). For \(S\subseteq U\), let \(N_H(S)\) be its neighborhood. Since every positive entry from a row in \(S\) ends in \(N_H(S)\), the marginal equations give

\[ \frac{|S|}{n} =\sum_{u\in S}\sum_{v\in N_H(S)}x_{uv} \leq \sum_{v\in N_H(S)}\sum_{u\in U}x_{uv} =\frac{|N_H(S)|}{n}. \]

Thus \(|S|\leq |N_H(S)|\) for every \(S\subseteq U\). Hall’s marriage theorem therefore supplies a perfect matching \(M\) in \(H\).

We verify its stability directly. If an acceptable pair \((u,v)\), with \(u\in D\), blocked \(M\), then \(v\succ_u M(u)\) and \(u\succ_v M(v)\). Because \(M\) lies in \(H\), both \(x_{u,M(u)}>0\) and \(x_{M(v),v}>0\). These entries belong to the two respective worse-partner sums for \((u,v)\), making both sums positive. This contradicts deviator-stability of \(x\). \(\square\)

3. Polynomial reduction and hardness

We use the stated discrete result: deciding whether a strict incomplete bipartite preference instance admits a perfect matching with no blocking pair involving a designated deviator is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, even with lists of length at most \(3\) and deviators confined to one side. [Glitzner, Kwanashie, and Manlove, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18573v2#Thmtheorem3.1).

We may restrict the source to nonempty instances with equally large sides: unequal side sizes immediately preclude a perfect matching and can be replaced by a fixed balanced no-instance. Relabel the sides so that all deviators belong to the first side. Let each side contain \(n\) agents.

Let \(N\) be the least power of two satisfying \(N\geq n\). Add \(N-n\) isolated private pairs. Each new agent accepts only its paired counterpart, and all new agents are conformists. This padding preserves the answer:

  • Any original deviator-stable perfect matching extends by matching every private pair.
  • Any padded perfect matching must match every private pair; its restriction to the original agents is perfect and has exactly the original blocking pairs.

Now make each padded agent a distinct type, retaining its acceptable partners, strict preferences, and deviator status. Give every type mass \(1/N\).

Both societies have total mass \(1\), all masses are strictly positive dyadic rationals, and acceptability remains symmetric. Lemma 2 proves that the resulting mass instance is a yes-instance exactly when the padded discrete instance—and hence the original instance—is a yes-instance.

The construction is polynomial: \(N<2n\), except that \(N=n=1\) is also possible; there are \(2N\) types, at most \(N-n\) new acceptable pairs, and each mass requires only logarithmically many bits. Original lists retain length at most \(3\), and new lists have length \(1\).

Therefore the mass problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard under all the stated restrictions.

4. Membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\) for arbitrary rational masses

We must justify polynomial certificates even though solutions are allowed to have real entries.

Express the input masses as \(\mu_u=a_u/b_u\) and \(\nu_v=c_v/d_v\), with positive integer denominators, and set \(Q=\prod_{u\in U}b_u\prod_{v\in V}d_v\). Then every \(Q\mu_u\) and \(Q\nu_v\) is an integer. Moreover, \(Q\) has at most \(O(L)\) bits, since its logarithm is the sum of the input denominators’ logarithms.

Suppose a deviator-stable perfect mass matching \(x\) exists, and let \(H=\operatorname{supp}(x)\). Construct a directed flow network with:

  • an arc from the source to each \(u\), of capacity \(Q\mu_u\);
  • an arc from \(u\) to \(v\) for each \((u,v)\in H\), of capacity \(Q\);
  • an arc from each \(v\) to the sink, of capacity \(Q\nu_v\).

The allocation \(Qx\), together with its marginals on the source and sink arcs, defines a feasible flow of value \(Q\). The total source capacity is \(Q\), so the maximum flow value is exactly \(Q\).

All capacities are integers. By the integral max-flow theorem, there is an integral flow of value \(Q\). Write its middle-arc values as \(z_{uv}\), extending them by zero outside \(H\). Because both the total source capacity and total sink capacity equal the flow value, every source and sink arc is saturated. Hence

\[ \sum_v z_{uv}=Q\mu_u, \qquad \sum_u z_{uv}=Q\nu_v. \]

The allocation \(y=z/Q\) therefore has the required marginals. Its support is contained in \(\operatorname{supp}(x)\), so Lemma 1 proves that it is deviator-stable.

Each integer \(z_{uv}\) satisfies \(0\leq z_{uv}\leq Q\). Thus the \(m\) integers on acceptable pairs form a certificate of polynomial length. A verifier computes \(Q\), checks nonnegativity and the marginal equations, and checks that at least one worse-partner sum is zero for every acceptable pair incident to a deviator. These are exact integer computations on polynomially many bits, so verification is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

This proves membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\), completing the classification.
