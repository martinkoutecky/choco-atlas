The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 0.9 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_s,\mu_\ell\) summing to one, sample counts \(n_s<n_\ell\), parameters \(\mu_e,\sigma^2\), and a population scale \(H\), compute a stable measurable partition of the two type masses into federation pools and local learners. For a type \(t\) in a pool whose other-client mass fractions are \(q_s,q_\ell\), use \(A(q)=n_s q_s+n_\ell q_\ell\), \(B(q)=n_s^2q_s+n_\ell^2q_\ell\), and expected error \(E_{t,H}(q)=\frac{\mu_e}{n_t+HA(q)}+\sigma^2\frac{HB(q)+H^2A(q)^2}{(n_t+HA(q))^2}\); stability rules out any positive-mass move that strictly improves the movers’ error and leaves destination incumbents weakly better off. Determine the high-multiplicity behavior as \(H\to\infty\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless probability space given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(m=\tau=2\), with types \(s,\ell\), masses \(\mu_s,\mu_\ell>0\), and \(\mu_s+\mu_\ell=1\).
Inputs are positive integers \(n_s<n_\ell\) and positive binary rationals \(\mu_e,\sigma^2,H\), with \(n_\ell>\mu_e/\sigma^2\). Let \(L\) be their total binary encoding length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and any additional parameters.
An arrangement consists of finitely many disjoint measurable federation pools of positive mass and a residual set of local learners. It is represented by pool mass vectors \(x^j=(x_s^j,x_\ell^j)\) and local masses \(z_t\), satisfying \(z_t+\sum_jx_t^j=\mu_t\); exact coordinates may be real algebraic numbers.
For \(q=(q_s,q_\ell)\), set \(A(q)=n_sq_s+n_\ell q_\ell\), \(B(q)=n_s^2q_s+n_\ell^2q_\ell\), and \(E_{t,H}(q)=\frac{\mu_e}{n_t+HA(q)}+\sigma^2\frac{HB(q)+H^2A(q)^2}{(n_t+HA(q))^2}\). A type \(t\) member of pool \(j\) has error \(E_{t,H}(x^j)\): removing a single atomless member changes no mass. A local learner has error \(\mu_e/n_t\).
A deviation takes any positive-mass measurable set of movers, possibly from several sources, into one existing pool, into a newly formed pool, or into local learning. Recompute errors using the resulting pool masses. It is admissible when almost every mover strictly reduces error and almost every destination incumbent weakly reduces error; new pools and local learning have no destination incumbents. Source incumbents have no veto. An arrangement is stable if no admissible deviation exists.
Is finding a stable arrangement, or deciding that none exists, solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?
With all inputs except \(H\) fixed, what are the possible limiting local masses and type-average errors along stable arrangements as \(H\to\infty\)?
For finite agents with two sample counts and \(n_\ell>\mu_e/\sigma^2\), an individually stable arrangement exists and can be computed efficiently; this stability concerns single-agent deviations, and the constructed arrangement need not be core stable ([Donahue–Kleinberg](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.00753v3)).
No result settling the specified atomless problem with positive-mass deviations was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required both type masses to be positive, positive integer sample counts, positive rational error parameters and population scale, and the anchor's condition n_ell > mu_e/sigma^2.
  • Interpreted q as mass relative to the whole society, not composition normalized within a pool; an atomless member has zero mass, so q equals its pool's mass vector.
  • Used finitely many measurable pools and residual individual local learning to give the computational output a finite representation; allowed exact real algebraic pool masses rather than imposing rational allocations.
  • Interpreted the graded question's positive-mass moves as arbitrary measurable groups, including mixed types and multiple sources; included new-pool formation and departure to local learning, with no source veto.
  • Interpreted strict improvement and destination consent almost everywhere, with indifference permitting consent but not initiating a deviation.
  • Included deciding nonexistence because the discrete existence theorem does not establish existence under positive-mass deviations.
  • Made high-multiplicity behavior precise as possible limiting local masses and type-average errors, holding the other inputs fixed.

Result

For the specified model with \(m=\tau=2\), a stable arrangement always exists and can be computed in polynomial time. The construction uses at most one federation pool, with coordinates of algebraic degree at most \(2\).

The limiting local masses and type-average errors can also be characterized completely.

1. Normalization

Write
\(a=n_s\), \(b=n_\ell\), and \(r=\mu_e/\sigma^2\).
The hypotheses give \(0<a<b\) and \(r<b\).

For a pool of mass vector \(q\), introduce
\(x=Ha q_s\), \(y=Hb q_\ell\), \(W=x+y=HA(q)\), and \(V=ax+by=HB(q)\).
The society’s available quantities are
\(S=Ha\mu_s\) and \(T=Hb\mu_\ell\).

Divide all errors by the positive constant \(\sigma^2\). This preserves every strict or weak comparison. For sample count \(n\), normalized pool error is

\(\displaystyle f_n(x,y)=\frac{r(n+W)+V+W^2}{(n+W)^2},\)

and normalized local error is \(r/n\).

An identity useful below is

\(\displaystyle f_n(x,y)-\frac rn =\frac{n(V-rW)+(n-r)W^2}{n(n+W)^2}.\)

2. The regime \(a\ge r\)

Here
\(V-rW=(a-r)x+(b-r)y\ge0\).

If \(a>r\), every member of every positive-mass pool has error strictly greater than its local error: in the preceding identity, \(n-r>0\) and \(W>0\). Any positive-mass group of such members can therefore deviate to local learning. Consequently, the all-local arrangement is the unique stable arrangement. It is stable because no mover can improve by entering any pool.

If \(a=r\), every pooled large-type learner still strictly prefers local learning, because \(b-r>0\). Thus a stable arrangement contains no pooled large-type mass. In a pure small-type pool, however, \(V=aW\), so \(f_s=1=r/a\).

It follows that, when \(a=r\), the stable arrangements are exactly those with all large-type learners local and an arbitrary division of small-type mass between local learning and finitely many pure small-type pools. Indeed, small-type learners cannot strictly improve from their common error \(1\), and large-type learners cannot improve from local learning by joining any pool.

In both cases, the algorithm may simply output the all-local arrangement.

3. Construction when \(a<r<b\)

For the rest of the construction, define the positive quantities

\(u=r-a\), \(v=b-r\), and \(d=b-a=u+v\).

The normalized errors simplify to

\(\displaystyle f_s(x,y)=1+\frac{u}{a+x+y}+\frac{dy}{(a+x+y)^2},\)

\(\displaystyle f_\ell(x,y)=1-\frac{v}{b+x+y}-\frac{dx}{(b+x+y)^2}.\)

The large type’s local error is \(r/b=1-v/b\). Direct multiplication by positive denominators gives

\[\displaystyle f_\ell(x,y)\le\frac rb \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v(x+y)(b+x+y)\le bd\,x. \tag{1}\]

We will put all small-type mass into one pool, choosing its large-type mass to minimize small-type error subject to large-type participation.

3.1 A global comparison lemma

Consider every pool using available resources, so \(0\le x\le S\) and \(0\le y\le T\), subject to the additional condition \(f_\ell(x,y)\le r/b\) whenever \(y>0\).

Lemma. The minimum small-type error over these pools is attained by a pool with \(x=S\).

Proof. Let \(W=x+y\).

If \(W\le S\), then

\(\displaystyle f_s(x,y)\ge1+\frac{u}{a+W} \ge1+\frac{u}{a+S}=f_s(S,0).\)

If \(W>S\), replace the pool by \((S,W-S)\). This uses available resources because
\(0<W-S\le y\le T\). Its total \(W\) is unchanged. The error changes are exactly

\(\displaystyle f_s(S,W-S)=f_s(x,y)-\frac{d(S-x)}{(a+W)^2},\)

\(\displaystyle f_\ell(S,W-S)=f_\ell(x,y)-\frac{d(S-x)}{(b+W)^2}.\)

Both errors weakly decrease. In particular, large-type participation remains satisfied. Thus every admissible competitor is weakly worse for the small type than some feasible pool containing all small-type mass. This proves the lemma.

3.2 Choosing the large-type mass

For fixed \(x=S\), large-type error strictly increases with \(y\):

\(\displaystyle \frac{\partial f_\ell(S,y)}{\partial y} =\frac{v}{(b+S+y)^2}+\frac{2dS}{(b+S+y)^3}>0.\)

Put \(M=bu/v\).

If \(S\ge M\), no positive \(y\) satisfies (1), since
\(vS(b+S)\ge bdS\) and increasing \(y\) strictly increases the left side. Set \(Y=0\).

If \(S<M\), define

\(\displaystyle R=\frac{-b+\sqrt{b^2+4bdS/v}}{2}, \qquad Y=\min\{T,R-S\}.\)

Here \(R>S\): the increasing function \(w\mapsto vw(b+w)\) takes a value below \(bdS\) at \(w=S\) and equals \(bdS\) at \(w=R\). By (1), the feasible values are exactly \(0\le y\le Y\).

Small-type error over this interval has derivative

\(\displaystyle \frac{\partial f_s(S,y)}{\partial y} =\frac{v(a+S)-(2u+v)y}{(a+S+y)^3}.\)

The numerator decreases strictly with \(y\). Hence the function increases and then decreases, and its minimum is at an endpoint.

Moreover,

\(\displaystyle f_s(S,y)-f_s(S,0) =\frac{y\bigl(v(a+S)-uy\bigr)} {(a+S)(a+S+y)^2}.\)

Define \(k=v(a+S)/u>0\), and choose

\(\displaystyle y_*= \begin{cases} Y,&Y\ge k,\\ 0,&Y<k. \end{cases}\)

Thus ties between the endpoints are resolved in favor of \(Y\).

The output is one pool and residual local masses:

\(\displaystyle q=\left(\mu_s,\frac{y_*}{Hb}\right), \qquad (z_s,z_\ell)=\left(0,\mu_\ell-\frac{y_*}{Hb}\right).\)

These coordinates are feasible because \(0\le y_*\le T\). They are measurably realizable: an atomless finite measure restricted to a measurable set of mass \(c\) admits a measurable subset of every prescribed mass in \([0,c]\). Each type set satisfies those hypotheses.

Let \(e_s=f_s(S,y_*)\). The comparison lemma and endpoint calculation establish:

Every pool using available resources and satisfying large-type participation whenever large-type mass is present has small-type error at least \(e_s\).

Also,

\(\displaystyle e_s\le f_s(S,0)=1+\frac{u}{a+S}<1+\frac ua=\frac ra.\)

4. Proof of stability

We check every permitted destination.

4.1 Local learning

Every small-type learner has error strictly below its local error. Every pooled large-type learner has error at most its local error by construction. Learners already local retain their error.

Thus no positive-mass deviation to local learning makes almost every mover strictly better off.

4.2 A new pool

Suppose a proposed new pool contains positive small-type mass. Every small-type mover currently has error \(e_s\), so admissibility would require its new error to be strictly below \(e_s\).

If the pool also contains large-type movers, their current errors are all at most \(r/b\). Their strict improvement therefore requires the new large-type error to be strictly below \(r/b\). The new pool consequently belongs to the comparison class in Section 3.1, contradicting the minimum property of \(e_s\).

If the new pool contains only small-type mass, it also belongs to that comparison class, yielding the same contradiction.

Finally, a new pool containing only large-type mass \(y>0\) has

\(\displaystyle f_\ell(0,y)=1-\frac{v}{b+y}>1-\frac vb=\frac rb.\)

Its movers cannot improve from current errors at most \(r/b\). Hence no new-pool deviation is admissible.

4.3 The existing pool

All learners outside the constructed pool are local large-type learners. Any nontrivial incoming deviation therefore adds a positive quantity \(\delta\) of large-type mass.

If \(y_*>0\), the large-type residents’ error strictly increases, because \(f_\ell(S,y)\) strictly increases with \(y\). Their incumbent consent fails.

If \(y_*=0\), the construction chose the pure small-type pool because \(Y<k\). For incoming large-type movers to improve from local learning, their final mass \(\delta\) must satisfy large-type participation, and hence \(0<\delta\le Y<k\). The endpoint-difference identity then gives

\(\displaystyle f_s(S,\delta)>f_s(S,0).\)

The small-type incumbents therefore reject the deviation. If no such feasible positive \(\delta\) exists, the movers themselves cannot improve.

These arguments cover all destinations. The constructed arrangement is stable.

5. Exact polynomial-time computation

For \(m=\tau=2\), the construction takes \(O(L^2)\) bit operations using elementary integer arithmetic, with \(L\) understood as the full binary input length. If the stated \(L\) excludes the support masses, the bound is \(O((L+L_\mu)^2)\), where \(L_\mu\) is their combined encoding length.

To verify this bound, all intermediate rational quantities have \(O(L)\) bits, and only a constant number of arithmetic operations and comparisons are needed. Square roots need not be approximated.

In the branch \(S<M\), the decision \(Y\ge k\) is equivalent to the two rational comparisons

\(\displaystyle T\ge k, \qquad v(S+k)(b+S+k)\le bdS.\)

Similarly, \(Y=T\) is equivalent to

\(\displaystyle v(S+T)(b+S+T)\le bdS.\)

If a mixed pool is chosen and this last comparison holds, its coordinates are rational. Otherwise its large-type mass \(q_\ell\) is the unique root in \((0,\mu_\ell)\) of

\(\displaystyle P(X)=v(S+HbX)(b+S+HbX)-bdS.\)

After clearing denominators, this is an integer polynomial with \(O(L)\)-bit coefficients. Its constant coefficient is negative and its leading coefficient positive, so its two roots have opposite signs. The active branch gives \(P(\mu_\ell)>0\); consequently \((0,\mu_\ell)\) isolates its positive root.

The local mass \(\mu_\ell-q_\ell\) is likewise represented by \(P(\mu_\ell-X)\) with isolating interval \((0,\mu_\ell)\). The other root lies above \(\mu_\ell\).

Thus standard exact algebraic representations—square-free integer polynomials with rational isolating intervals—require only \(O(L)\) output bits. The algorithm outputs at most one pool and uses algebraic degree at most \(2\).

Therefore both the search problem and the existence decision problem are polynomial-time solvable. On every input satisfying the promises, the existence answer is affirmative.

6. Limits along all stable arrangements

Define the type-average error by

\(\displaystyle \overline E_t= \frac{z_t\,\mu_e/n_t+\sum_j x_t^jE_{t,H}(x^j)}{\mu_t}. \)

The following classification concerns arbitrary stable arrangements, not only the construction above.

6.1 Eventual uniqueness when \(a<r<b\)

First, every stable arrangement has \(z_s=0\). Otherwise a positive-mass set of local small-type learners could form a pure small-type pool, obtaining error strictly below \(r/a\).

Second, every pool containing positive large-type mass must satisfy \(f_\ell\le r/b\), because otherwise its large-type members could deviate to local learning. Equation (1), together with \(x\le W\), yields

\(\displaystyle vW(b+W)\le bd\,x\le bdW.\)

Since \(W>0\), this implies

\(\displaystyle W\le\frac{bu}{v}=M.\)

In particular, a pure large-type pool cannot occur in a stable arrangement.

Suppose now that \(S\ge M\). Consider the pure pool containing all small-type mass; its normalized error would be

\(\displaystyle e_0=1+\frac{u}{a+S}.\)

Any existing mixed pool has \(W\le M\le S\) and \(y>0\), so its small-type error satisfies

\(\displaystyle f_s(x,y)=1+\frac{u}{a+W}+\frac{dy}{(a+W)^2}>e_0.\)

Any pure small-type pool containing less than all small-type mass also has error strictly above \(e_0\).

Consequently, unless all small-type learners already occupy one pure pool, all small-type learners can jointly move into a new pure pool and strictly improve. Source incumbents have no veto, exactly as stipulated in the question.

Hence, for every

\(\displaystyle H\ge H_*= \frac{b(r-a)}{a\mu_s(b-r)},\)

the unique stable arrangement, up to null sets, is

\(\displaystyle q=(\mu_s,0), \qquad (z_s,z_\ell)=(0,\mu_\ell).\)

Its type-average errors are exactly

\(\displaystyle \overline E_s= \sigma^2+\frac{\mu_e/a-\sigma^2}{1+H\mu_s}, \qquad \overline E_\ell=\frac{\mu_e}{b}. \)

Thus every sequence of stable arrangements has these same limits as \(H\to\infty\).

6.2 Complete limiting classification

Combining this result with Section 2 gives:

| Condition | Possible limiting local masses \((z_s,z_\ell)\) | Limiting type-average errors \((\overline E_s,\overline E_\ell)\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(n_s<\mu_e/\sigma^2\) | \((0,\mu_\ell)\) | \((\sigma^2,\mu_e/n_\ell)\) |
| \(n_s=\mu_e/\sigma^2\) | \((\lambda,\mu_\ell)\), any \(\lambda\in[0,\mu_s]\) | \((\sigma^2,\mu_e/n_\ell)\) |
| \(n_s>\mu_e/\sigma^2\) | \((\mu_s,\mu_\ell)\) | \((\mu_e/n_s,\mu_e/n_\ell)\) |

Every listed limit is attained. In the equality case, fix the desired local small-type mass \(\lambda\) and place the remaining small-type mass in a pure small-type pool; this arrangement is stable for every \(H\). Such sequences need not have convergent local masses, but all their subsequential limits lie in the displayed interval. Their type-average errors are constant.
