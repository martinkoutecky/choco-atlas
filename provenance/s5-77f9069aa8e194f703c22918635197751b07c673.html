The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and a rational piecewise-affine worth function \(W\) on \(Q=\{q:0\le q_t\le\mu_t\}\) with \(W(0)=0\), consider an atomless population partitioned into type classes of masses \(\mu_t\). A measurable coalition \(A\) has worth \(W(q(A))\), where \(q_t(A)\) is its mass of type \(t\). Assume the core is nonempty. Is the core stable: does every imputation \(p\ge0\) almost everywhere with total payoff \(W(\mu)\), but outside the core, admit a core allocation \(x\) and a positive-mass measurable coalition \(A\) such that \(x>p\) almost everywhere on \(A\) and \(\int_A x\le W(q(A))\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(m=\tau\) is the number of mass coordinates.
Represent the population by \(\Omega=[0,1]\) with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\), partitioned into intervals \(\Omega_t\) of masses \(\mu_t\). Coalitions are measurable sets \(A\), with \(q_t(A)=\lambda(A\cap\Omega_t)\).
The input worth function \(W:Q\to\mathbb R\), where \(Q=\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\), satisfies \(W(0)=0\). It is specified by an explicit rational polyhedral subdivision of \(Q\) into \(K\) cells and rational affine formulas agreeing on shared faces; negative worths are allowed. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Imputations are all \(p\in L^1(\Omega,\lambda)\) with \(p\ge0\) almost everywhere and \(\int_\Omega p\,d\lambda=W(\mu)\); they need not be constant within types. Define \(C=\{x\text{ an imputation}:\int_A x\,d\lambda\ge W(q(A))\text{ for every measurable }A\}\), and promise \(C\ne\varnothing\).
Call the core stable if every \(p\notin C\) that is an imputation admits \(x\in C\) and a measurable coalition \(A\) with \(\lambda(A)>0\), \(x>p\) almost everywhere on \(A\), and \(\int_A x\,d\lambda\le W(q(A))\).

Is deciding whether this core is stable solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite balanced TU games, nested balancedness characterizes core stability and yields a finite decision procedure, without a polynomial-time bound. [Grabisch–Sudhölter](https://findresearcher.sdu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/197277277/core_stability_09_08_21_final_manuscript.pdf)
For atomless games whose worth is the minimum of finitely many absolutely continuous atomless probability measures, the core is stable; this covers a special class rather than the general input above. [Einy–Holzman–Monderer–Shitovitz](https://holzman.technion.ac.il/files/2012/09/cores.pdf)

  • Specified the atomless population as the Lebesgue unit interval partitioned into type intervals.
  • Specified the worth-function encoding as an explicit rational polyhedral subdivision with compatible affine formulas, taking piecewise-affine to mean continuous across shared faces.
  • Defined allocations as integrable densities modulo almost-everywhere equality, allowing arbitrary variation within each type.
  • Defined the core within the graded question's nonnegative imputation domain, with coalitional inequalities imposed for every measurable coalition.
  • Allowed negative coalition worths, as in the finite TU-game definition; retained zero total worth as an admissible case.
  • Identified m with the number of mass coordinates, tau, and introduced K and total input bit length L.

Partial result: a polynomial-time criterion for a class of nonsingleton cores

The general complexity question remains unresolved here. I prove a new restricted result: one can recognize in polynomial time whether the core consists exactly of all allocations satisfying certain typewise pointwise bounds and efficiency; for every core in this class, stability is decidable in polynomial time.

This class includes nonsingleton cores with allocations varying within types. I also give stable and unstable three-cell examples with the same nonsingleton core.

Throughout, allocations are identified up to almost-everywhere equality.

1. Pointwise bounds on every core allocation

Write
$$ w=W(\mu). $$
The nonempty-core promise implies $w\ge 0$.

Since $W$ is continuous and piecewise affine on a finite subdivision, its restrictions to the relevant coordinate segments are affine sufficiently close to their endpoints. Thus, for every type $t$, there are rational numbers $d_t,u_t$ and positive numbers $\eta_t,\rho_t$ such that
$$ W(se_t)=d_t s \qquad (0\le s\le\eta_t) $$
and
$$ W(\mu-se_t)=w-u_t s \qquad (0\le s\le\rho_t). $$
Here $e_t$ is the $t$th coordinate unit vector. Define
$$ \ell_t=\max\{0,d_t\}. $$

Proposition 1. Every $x\in C$ satisfies
$$ \ell_t\le x(\omega)\le u_t \quad\text{for almost every }\omega\in\Omega_t. $$

Proof. Nonnegativity gives the lower bound when $d_t\le 0$. If $d_t>0$ and $\{x<d_t\}\cap\Omega_t$ had positive measure, atomlessness would provide a measurable subset $B$ of positive measure $s\le\eta_t$. Then
$$ \int_B x\,d\lambda<d_t s=W(se_t), $$
contradicting core membership.

For the upper bound, suppose $\{x>u_t\}\cap\Omega_t$ had positive measure. Choose a measurable subset $B$ of positive measure $s\le\rho_t$. Efficiency and the coalition constraint for its complement would give
$$ w-\int_B x\,d\lambda =\int_{\Omega\setminus B}x\,d\lambda \ge W(\mu-se_t) =w-u_t s. $$
But $\int_Bx\,d\lambda>u_t s$, a contradiction. $\square$

Consequently, the promise implies
$$ 0\le\ell_t\le u_t, \qquad \underline w:=\ell\cdot\mu\le w\le u\cdot\mu=:\overline w. $$

In particular, every core allocation belongs to $L^\infty$, although outside imputations may be unbounded.

Another observation valid for every input is that a domination coalition must be tight. Indeed, if $x\in C$ dominates $p$ through $A$, then
$$ \int_Ax\,d\lambda\ge W(q(A)) $$
by core membership, while domination requires the reverse inequality. Therefore
$$ \int_Ax\,d\lambda=W(q(A)). $$

2. Recognizing cores determined by the endpoint bounds

Define
$$ \mathcal B= \left\{ x\in L^1: \ell_t\le x\le u_t\text{ a.e. on }\Omega_t,\quad \int_\Omega x\,d\lambda=w \right\}. $$
Proposition 1 gives $C\subseteq\mathcal B$.

Set
$$ H(q)=\max\left\{\ell\cdot q,\, w-u\cdot(\mu-q)\right\}. $$

Lemma 2. For every measurable coalition $A$ with profile $q$,
$$ \min_{x\in\mathcal B}\int_Ax\,d\lambda=H(q). $$

Proof. Every $x\in\mathcal B$ satisfies
$$ \int_Ax\,d\lambda\ge\ell\cdot q $$
and, by efficiency,
$$ \int_Ax\,d\lambda =w-\int_{\Omega\setminus A}x\,d\lambda \ge w-u\cdot(\mu-q). $$
Thus $H(q)$ is a lower bound.

To prove attainment, put
$$ a=\ell\cdot q,\qquad b=u\cdot q, $$
$$ a'=\ell\cdot(\mu-q),\qquad b'=u\cdot(\mu-q), $$
and let $z=\max\{a,w-b'\}$.

The inequalities $\underline w\le w\le\overline w$ imply
$$ a\le z\le b,\qquad a'\le w-z\le b'. $$
For example, $z\le b$ follows from $a\le b$ and $w-b'\le b$. Also, both $a$ and $w-b'$ are at most $w-a'$, so $z\le w-a'$.

On $A$, interpolate between the densities $\ell_t$ and $u_t$ using one scalar parameter, chosen to obtain total payment $z$. On the complement, use another scalar parameter to obtain payment $w-z$. These parameters exist because the respective attainable payment intervals are $[a,b]$ and $[a',b']$. If an interval has zero length, its required payment is its sole endpoint.

The resulting measurable allocation belongs to $\mathcal B$ and pays $H(q)$ to $A$. $\square$

Proposition 3.
$$ C=\mathcal B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W(q)\le H(q)\text{ for every }q\in Q. $$

Proof. If $W\le H$, Lemma 2 shows that every allocation in $\mathcal B$ satisfies every coalition constraint. Hence $\mathcal B\subseteq C$, and Proposition 1 gives equality.

Conversely, assume $C=\mathcal B$. Every $q\in Q$ is realized by a measurable coalition, by selecting the prescribed measure independently in each type. Lemma 2 supplies an allocation in $\mathcal B=C$ paying exactly $H(q)$ to that coalition. Its core constraint implies $H(q)\ge W(q)$. $\square$

This equality of cores is polynomial-time recognizable. On a cell $D_j$, write
$$ W(q)=\alpha_j\cdot q+\beta_j. $$
Solve the linear program
$$ \min_{q,z}\ z-\alpha_j\cdot q-\beta_j $$
subject to
$$ q\in D_j,\qquad z\ge\ell\cdot q,\qquad z\ge w-u\cdot(\mu-q). $$
Its optimum is exactly
$$ \min_{q\in D_j}\bigl(H(q)-W(q)\bigr). $$
Therefore $C=\mathcal B$ precisely when all these optima are nonnegative.

The domains are nonempty compact polyhedra, so these optima are finite and attained. The programs have rational descriptions of polynomial length. Cells supplied by explicit vertex lists can be handled by expressing $q$ as a convex combination of their listed vertices.

3. An exact stability criterion for the nonsingleton case

Assume henceforth that the recognition test succeeded, so $C=\mathcal B$, and that
$$ \underline w<w<\overline w. $$
Define
$$ D_t=u_t-\ell_t,\qquad J=\{t:D_t>0\},\qquad M=\overline w-w. $$
Thus $M>0$, and
$$ D\cdot\mu=\overline w-\underline w>M. $$

Types outside $J$ have a fixed core density: every core allocation equals $\ell_t=u_t$ there.

Define the closed polytope
$$ R= \left\{ q\in Q: q_t=0\text{ for }t\notin J,\quad D\cdot q\ge M \right\}. $$

Theorem 4. Under these hypotheses,
$$ C\text{ is stable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W(q)=w-u\cdot(\mu-q) \text{ for every }q\in R. $$

3.1. Sufficiency

Assume the equality on $R$, and let $p$ be an imputation outside $C$.

First suppose $p<\ell_t$ on a positive-measure subset of some type. Necessarily $\ell_t>0$, because $p\ge0$. By definition of $\ell_t$,
$$ d_t=\ell_t. $$
Choose a measurable coalition $A$ inside that deficit set, of positive measure $s$, small enough that
$$ s\le\eta_t,\qquad D_t s<M. $$
When $D_t=0$, the second condition holds automatically.

There exists an allocation in $\mathcal B$ equal to $\ell_t$ on $A$. Indeed, imposing that value leaves minimum possible total payment $\underline w$ and maximum possible total payment
$$ \overline w-D_t s>w. $$
Interpolation between these lower and upper allocations yields total payment $w$. Let $x$ be such an allocation. Then $x\in C$, $x>p$ almost everywhere on $A$, and
$$ \int_Ax\,d\lambda =\ell_t s =d_t s =W(se_t). $$
Thus $x$ dominates $p$.

It remains to consider the case $p\ge\ell$ almost everywhere. Since $p\notin\mathcal B$, the set $\{p>u\}$ has positive measure, where $\ell$ and $u$ denote the corresponding typewise density functions.

Put
$$ B=\{\omega:p(\omega)\ge u(\omega)\}, \qquad A=\Omega\setminus B, $$
and define
$$ E=\int_B(p-u)\,d\lambda,\qquad S=\int_A(u-p)\,d\lambda. $$
Then $E>0$, and efficiency gives
$$ S-E=\int_\Omega u\,d\lambda-\int_\Omega p\,d\lambda=M>0. $$
Hence $S>E>0$. Set
$$ \theta=\frac ES\in(0,1) $$
and define
$$ x= \begin{cases} u,&\text{on }B,\\ p+\theta(u-p),&\text{on }A. \end{cases} $$
We have $\ell\le x\le u$, and
$$ \int_\Omega x\,d\lambda=w-E+\theta S=w. $$
Thus $x\in\mathcal B=C$, and $x>p$ almost everywhere on $A$.

Let $q=q(A)$. On types outside $J$, $p\ge\ell_t=u_t$, so $q_t=0$. Moreover,
$$ \int_Ax\,d\lambda =w-\int_Bu\,d\lambda =w-u\cdot(\mu-q). $$
Because $x>p\ge\ell$ on the positive-measure set $A$,
$$ w-u\cdot(\mu-q)>\ell\cdot q. $$
Equivalently, $D\cdot q>M$, so $q\in R$. The assumed equality on $R$ now gives
$$ \int_Ax\,d\lambda=W(q). $$
This proves domination and establishes sufficiency.

3.2. Necessity

Assume $C$ is stable. First fix $q\in Q$ satisfying
$$ q_t=0\text{ for }t\notin J,\qquad D\cdot q>M, $$
and suppose that $q$ is not the vector consisting of all the masses of types in $J$. Thus
$$ v:=\sum_{t\in J}(\mu_t-q_t)>0. $$

Choose measurable $B_t\subseteq\Omega_t$ of measure $q_t$ for every $t\in J$. For
$$ 0<\epsilon<D\cdot q-M, $$
define
$$ \theta_\epsilon=\frac{M+\epsilon}{D\cdot q}\in(0,1), \qquad a_t=u_t-\theta_\epsilon D_t. $$
Construct the imputation
$$ p_\epsilon= \begin{cases} a_t,&\text{on }B_t,\quad t\in J,\\ u_t+\epsilon/v,&\text{on }\Omega_t\setminus B_t,\quad t\in J,\\ u_t,&\text{on }\Omega_t,\quad t\notin J. \end{cases} $$
It is nonnegative and satisfies $p_\epsilon\ge\ell$. Its total payment is
$$ \int_\Omega p_\epsilon\,d\lambda =\overline w-\theta_\epsilon D\cdot q+\epsilon =\overline w-M=w. $$
It is outside the core because it exceeds $u_t$ on a set of measure $v>0$.

By stability, some $x_\epsilon\in C$ dominates $p_\epsilon$ through a positive-measure coalition $A_\epsilon$. Every core allocation is bounded above by $u$, so strict improvement is possible only inside the sets $B_t$. Consequently, its profile $r_\epsilon=q(A_\epsilon)$ satisfies
$$ 0\le r_{\epsilon,t}\le q_t \quad(t\in J), \qquad r_{\epsilon,t}=0 \quad(t\notin J). $$

By Lemma 2, Proposition 3, and domination,
$$ H(r_\epsilon) \le\int_{A_\epsilon}x_\epsilon\,d\lambda \le W(r_\epsilon) \le H(r_\epsilon). $$
All these quantities are therefore equal. Strict improvement additionally gives
$$ H(r_\epsilon) >\int_{A_\epsilon}p_\epsilon\,d\lambda =a\cdot r_\epsilon \ge\ell\cdot r_\epsilon. $$
Thus the upper affine branch of $H$ is active:
$$ W(r_\epsilon) =-M+u\cdot r_\epsilon. $$
The strict inequality above implies
$$ -M+u\cdot r_\epsilon >u\cdot r_\epsilon-\theta_\epsilon D\cdot r_\epsilon, $$
and hence
$$ \theta_\epsilon D\cdot r_\epsilon>M. $$
Since $\theta_\epsilon D\cdot q=M+\epsilon$,
$$ 0\le D\cdot(q-r_\epsilon) <\frac{\epsilon}{\theta_\epsilon}. $$
As $\epsilon$ tends to zero,
$$ \theta_\epsilon\longrightarrow\frac{M}{D\cdot q}>0. $$
Every $D_t$ for $t\in J$ is positive, so the last bound implies coordinatewise convergence $r_\epsilon\to q$. Continuity of $W$ therefore yields
$$ W(q)=-M+u\cdot q=w-u\cdot(\mu-q). $$

Finally, this equality extends to every point of $R$. To check the required density argument, let $\mu^J$ equal $\mu$ on $J$ and zero elsewhere. Since
$$ D\cdot\mu^J>M, $$
one can choose $q^\star=(1-\delta)\mu^J$, with $0<\delta<1$, such that $D\cdot q^\star>M$. For any $q\in R$, the points
$$ q_n=\left(1-\frac1n\right)q+\frac1n q^\star $$
satisfy the strict inequality and leave positive mass outside the chosen blocks. The equality just proved applies to $q_n$, and continuity gives it at $q$. This establishes necessity. $\square$

4. Degenerate cases and the polynomial-time procedure

If $w=\underline w$, then $\mathcal B$ consists of the single allocation $\ell$. If $w=\overline w$, it consists of the single allocation $u$. Indeed, in either case the integral of the nonnegative difference from the appropriate bound is zero.

For completeness, the singleton criterion used here is proved next.

Lemma 5. If $C=\{c\}$, where $c$ is typewise constant, then
$$ C\text{ is stable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_t=c_t\text{ whenever }c_t>0. $$

Proof. Core membership gives $d_t\le c_t$.

If equality holds on every positive-density type, take any imputation $p\ne c$. Equal integrals imply that $\{c>p\}$ has positive measure. Some type contains a positive-measure part of this set, and its density $c_t$ is positive. Choose a small positive-measure coalition $A$ there, of measure $s\le\eta_t$. Then
$$ \int_Ac\,d\lambda=c_t s=d_t s=W(se_t), $$
so $c$ dominates $p$.

Conversely, suppose $c_t>0$ and $d_t<c_t$. Choose
$$ 0<r<\min\{\eta_t,\mu_t,1\} $$
and a measurable $B\subseteq\Omega_t$ of measure $r$. For $0<\epsilon<c_t$, define
$$ p= \begin{cases} c-\epsilon,&\text{on }B,\\ c+\epsilon r/(1-r),&\text{outside }B. \end{cases} $$
This is a nonnegative imputation different from $c$. Its sole possible dominator is $c$, which improves on it only inside $B$. Every positive-measure coalition $A$ there, of measure $s$, satisfies
$$ \int_Ac\,d\lambda=c_t s>d_t s=W(se_t). $$
Therefore no domination exists. $\square$

The complete procedure for the recognized class is now:

  • Compute $w$, the endpoint slopes $d_t,u_t$, and $\ell_t=\max\{0,d_t\}$.
  • Use Proposition 3 to test whether $C=\mathcal B$.
  • If equality holds and $w$ is an endpoint of $[\underline w,\overline w]$, use Lemma 5.
  • Otherwise, apply Theorem 4.

The slopes are polynomial-time computable by intersecting each cell with the coordinate segments used in Section 1. These intersections are rational intervals; a cell covering a positive initial interval supplies the appropriate slope.

For Step 4, on every nonempty intersection $D_j\cap R$, maximize the affine expression
$$ w-u\cdot(\mu-q)-\alpha_j\cdot q-\beta_j. $$
On $R$, the upper branch equals $H$, and the recognition step has already established $W\le H$. Thus this expression is nonnegative throughout the intersection. The required equality holds precisely when every maximum is zero.

All these programs have polynomial-size rational descriptions. Their feasible domains are compact, and all relevant optima are finite and attained. Standard polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore supplies exact decisions, including comparison of optima with zero; see [Khachiyan’s polynomial algorithm](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Consequently, both recognition and the subsequent stability decision run in time polynomial in $m,\tau,K,L$.

Scope limitation: if Step 2 fails, this procedure gives no stability decision for that instance.

5. Stable and unstable games with the same nonsingleton core

Take one type of mass $1$.

The two-cell function
$$ W_{\mathrm{st}}(s)=\max\{0,2s-1\} $$
has $w=1$, $\ell=0$, and $u=2$. Proposition 3 gives
$$ C= \left\{ x\in L^1: 0\le x\le2\text{ a.e.},\quad \int_\Omega x\,d\lambda=1 \right\}. $$
Here $M=1$ and $R=[1/2,1]$. The required equality holds throughout $R$, so Theorem 4 proves stability.

Now define the three-cell function
$$ W_{\mathrm{un}}(s)= \begin{cases} 0,&0\le s\le5/8,\\ 4s-5/2,&5/8\le s\le3/4,\\ 2s-1,&3/4\le s\le1. \end{cases} $$
The formulas agree at the breakpoints. Its endpoint slopes are again $\ell=0$ and $u=2$, and
$$ W_{\mathrm{un}}(s)\le\max\{0,2s-1\}. $$
Indeed, on the middle interval,
$$ 4s-\frac52\le2s-1 $$
because $s\le3/4$. Proposition 3 therefore gives exactly the same core.

This core is nonsingleton: it contains both the constant allocation $1$ and the allocation equal to $0$ on one half of the population and $2$ on the other half.

For a direct proof of instability, choose a measurable $B$ of measure $5/8$ and let
$$ p= \begin{cases} 0,&\text{on }B,\\ 8/3,&\text{outside }B. \end{cases} $$
Its total payment is $1$, and it is outside the core because it exceeds $2$ outside $B$.

Every core allocation is bounded above by $2$, so any coalition on which it strictly improves on $p$ must be contained in $B$ up to null sets. Such a coalition has measure at most $5/8$ and worth zero. Strict improvement over $p=0$ on a positive-measure coalition requires strictly positive payment, which violates affordability. Thus the core is unstable.

6. What remains open

The results above provide an exact polynomial-time decision procedure for a recognizable class of arbitrary-dimensional, nonsingleton cores. They retain all integrable imputations and all measurable domination coalitions.

They do not classify stability when the core has additional constraints beyond the endpoint pointwise bounds and efficiency. No general polynomial-time algorithm or $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness reduction is established here.
