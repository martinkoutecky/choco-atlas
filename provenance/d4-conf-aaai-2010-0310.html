Exact answer

I interpret “binary rational” as a rational number represented by its binary numerator and denominator.

An exact answer is available through an explicit finite coefficient formula. The key structural fact is that two known regions are indistinguishable precisely when their Borda score vectors agree. Consequently, optimal summaries can be constructed by enumerating distinct attainable score vectors.

The resulting optimal compiler is uniformly computable, although its running time can be large. Polynomial-time compilers with explicit length bounds are given below.

1. Indistinguishability equals equality of score vectors

For a ranking \(\pi\), let \(r_j(\pi)\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) be the Borda score assigned to \(c_j\). Define

\(s_j(\mu)=\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mu(\pi)r_j(\pi)\), and \(s(\mu)=(s_1(\mu),\ldots,s_m(\mu))\).

Every region has mass \(1/2\), so

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=1}^{m}s_j(\mu)=\frac{m(m-1)}4.\)

Let \(R\mu\) be obtained by reversing every ranking in \(\mu\), preserving its mass. Reversal preserves distinctness of ranking types, support size, and all numerator and denominator bounds. Thus \(R\mu\) is an allowed complement. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle s_j(R\mu)=\frac{m-1}{2}-s_j(\mu).\)

Lemma. For \(\mu,\mu'\in\mathcal D_{m,\tau,L}\),

\(\displaystyle \bigl[w(\mu+\nu)=w(\mu'+\nu)\text{ for every }\nu\in\mathcal D_{m,\tau,L}\bigr]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s(\mu)=s(\mu').\)

Proof. Equality of score vectors immediately implies equality of winners against every complement.

Conversely, assume equality of winners against every allowed complement, and put \(\delta_j=s_j(\mu')-s_j(\mu)\).

Against \(R\mu\), every candidate in \(\mu+R\mu\) has score \((m-1)/2\); hence its winner is \(c_1\). The scores in \(\mu'+R\mu\) are \((m-1)/2+\delta_j\). Its winner must also be \(c_1\), implying

\(\delta_1\ge\delta_j\quad\text{for every }j.\)

Against \(R\mu'\), the winner of \(\mu'+R\mu'\) is \(c_1\), while the scores in \(\mu+R\mu'\) are \((m-1)/2-\delta_j\). Therefore

\(\delta_1\le\delta_j\quad\text{for every }j.\)

Thus all \(\delta_j\) are equal. Their sum is zero because the two regions have the same total Borda score. Hence every \(\delta_j=0\). \(\square\)

Let

\(\displaystyle N_{m,\tau,L}=\left|\{s(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal D_{m,\tau,L}\}\right|.\)

The lemma shows that every valid compiler must use at least this many distinct strings.

2. The exact bit count

There are exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\ell=0}^{b}2^\ell=2^{b+1}-1\)

binary strings of length at most \(b\). Consequently, distinguishing \(N_{m,\tau,L}\) score vectors requires

\(\displaystyle b\ge\left\lfloor\log_2N_{m,\tau,L}\right\rfloor.\)

This bound is achievable. Sort the distinct attainable score vectors lexicographically, with indices \(i\in\{0,\ldots,N_{m,\tau,L}-1\}\). Encode index \(i\) by writing \(i+1\) in binary and deleting its initial \(1\). In particular, index zero receives the empty string. The maximum length is \(\lfloor\log_2N_{m,\tau,L}\rfloor\).

The decoder prepends \(1\), recovers the index, reconstructs its score vector, adds the complement’s score vector, and applies the fixed tie order.

Thus

\(\boxed{\displaystyle b(m,\tau,L)=\left\lfloor\log_2N_{m,\tau,L}\right\rfloor.}\)

The next section gives an explicit coefficient formula for \(N_{m,\tau,L}\), involving only the parameters and finite polynomial operations.

3. An explicit finite formula for all parameters

Put

\(\displaystyle Q=2^L-1,\qquad D=\operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,Q).\)

The possible individual masses are exactly

\(\displaystyle W_L=\left\{\frac pq:2\le q\le Q,\ 1\le p\le\lfloor q/2\rfloor,\ \gcd(p,q)=1\right\}.\)

Indeed, every mass is at most \(1/2\), so the denominator bound already ensures the numerator bound. Define the integer set

\(\displaystyle E_L=\{Da:a\in W_L\}.\)

Every listed mass is at least \(1/Q\). Therefore a region with \(t\) types satisfies \(t/Q\le1/2\), giving the effective support bound

\(\displaystyle T=\min\left\{\tau,m!,\left\lfloor Q/2\right\rfloor\right\}.\)

This maximum is attainable: choose \(T\) distinct rankings, each with mass \(1/(2T)\). Its reduced denominator satisfies \(2T\le Q\).

For indeterminates \(z,x,y_1,\ldots,y_{m-1}\), form

\(\displaystyle G(z,x,\mathbf y)=\prod_{\pi\in S_m}\left(1+z\sum_{a\in E_L}x^a\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}y_j^{a r_j(\pi)}\right).\)

Here \(S_m\) denotes the set of strict rankings. Let

\(\displaystyle H_{m,\tau,L}(\mathbf y)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}[z^t x^{D/2}]\,G(z,x,\mathbf y).\)

The notation in brackets means coefficient extraction in the displayed variables. Since \(D\) is even, \(D/2\) is an integer. Set \(M=D(m-1)/2\). Then the requested exact answer is

\(\displaystyle \boxed{\ b(m,\tau,L)=\left\lfloor\log_2\left(\sum_{\mathbf e\in\{0,\ldots,M\}^{m-1}}\mathbf 1\!\left\{[\mathbf y^{\mathbf e}]H_{m,\tau,L}>0\right\}\right)\right\rfloor.\ }\)

Proof of the counting formula. In the factor indexed by \(\pi\), choosing \(1\) omits that ranking; choosing another term assigns it one permitted positive mass. Thus a product term chooses a support of distinct rankings and one mass per type.

The exponent of \(z\) counts the support size. The exponent of \(x\) is \(D\) times total mass. Extracting \(z^t x^{D/2}\), for \(1\le t\le T\), therefore selects exactly the allowed regions.

For a selected region, the exponent of \(y_j\) is \(Ds_j(\mu)\). All coefficients are nonnegative integers, so a coefficient is positive precisely when its score vector is attained.

Finally, the first \(m-1\) scores determine the last score through their fixed sum \(m(m-1)/4\). Counting the positive coefficients therefore counts distinct complete score vectors, without counting different representations of the same vector separately. This proves the formula. \(\square\)

4. Uniformly computable optimal maps and running times

The coefficient formula can be evaluated by enumeration without constructing a polynomial with enormous exponents.

Write \(n=m!\), \(R=|W_L|\), and

\(\displaystyle A=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\binom nt R^t.\)

There are at most \(A\) candidate supports with assigned masses before checking total mass. Enumerate them, discard those whose masses do not sum to \(1/2\), compute their score vectors, sort, and remove duplicates. This produces the dictionary used by the optimal compiler and decoder.

For an explicit conservative bit-complexity bound, define

\(\displaystyle C=T\bigl(m\lceil\log_2m\rceil+2L\bigr)+\lceil\log_2(T+1)\rceil+3,\)

and

\(\displaystyle F=Q^2L^3+A C^4.\)

Both maps can reconstruct the dictionary in \(O(F)\) bit operations, using elementary integer arithmetic. To justify this bound:

  • Enumerating reduced permitted fractions costs \(O(Q^2L^3)\).
  • Products of at most \(T\) denominators, and the numerators needed for mass and score calculations, have \(O(C)\) bits.
  • Each candidate can be checked and scored in \(O(mT C^2)\) bit operations.
  • Sorting at most \(A\) vectors takes \(O(A\log(A+1)mC^2)\) bit operations.

Here \(m\), \(T\), and \(\log(A+1)\) are all \(O(C)\), so these costs are covered by \(O(F)\).

If the actual known and complementary support sizes are \(t\) and \(u\), respectively, conservative total bounds are:

\(\displaystyle \text{compiler: }O\!\left(F+mt\bigl(tL+\log_2(m(t+1))\bigr)^2\right),\)

\(\displaystyle \text{decoder: }O\!\left(F+m(u+1)\bigl((T+u)L+\log_2(m(T+u+1))\bigr)^2\right).\)

The additional terms cover computing the input scores and exact winner comparisons. These are uniform algorithms: their finite loops and arithmetic operations are determined by \(m,\tau,L\).

A simpler description of the enumeration cost is

\(\displaystyle 2^{\,O(T(m\log m+L))}\operatorname{poly}(m,T,L).\)

Thus the exact optimum is achievable by computable maps; this construction does not establish polynomial-time optimal compilation.

5. Polynomial-time upper-bound compilers

Let

\(\displaystyle h=\lceil\log_2(T+1)\rceil,\qquad r=\lceil\log_2(m!)\rceil,\qquad \ell=\lceil\log_2m\rceil.\)

For a known region with actual support size \(t\), two straightforward encodings are available.

Encode the support. Store \(t\) in \(h\) bits. For each type, store its permutation index in \(r\) bits and its reduced numerator and denominator in \(L\) bits each. The length is

\(\displaystyle h+t(r+2L).\)

Encode exact scores. Let \(d\) be the product of the \(t\) reduced denominators. Then \(d<2^{tL}\), and \(a_j=ds_j(\mu)\) is an integer satisfying \(0\le a_j\le d(m-1)/2\).

Store \(t\), then \(d\) in \(tL\) bits, and the first \(m-1\) integers \(a_j\), each in \(tL+\ell\) bits. The last numerator is recovered as

\(\displaystyle a_m=\frac{dm(m-1)}4-\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}a_j.\)

The length is

\(\displaystyle h+mtL+(m-1)\ell.\)

Using one mode bit and selecting the shorter encoding gives the actual length bound

\(\displaystyle 1+h+\min\{t(r+2L),\,mtL+(m-1)\ell\}.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \boxed{\ b(m,\tau,L)\le1+h+\min\{T(r+2L),\,mTL+(m-1)\ell\}.\ }\)

Both encodings and their decoders run in polynomial time. With ordinary permutation indexing and schoolbook integer arithmetic, conservative bounds are

\(\displaystyle \text{compiler: }O\!\left(tm^4(\log_2m)^2+mt\bigl(tL+\log_2(m(t+1))\bigr)^2\right),\)

\(\displaystyle \text{decoder: }O\!\left(tm^4(\log_2m)^2+m(t+u)\bigl((t+u)L+\log_2(m(t+u+1))\bigr)^2\right).\)

In the score mode, the permutation-processing term is unnecessary.

6. Tight special cases and general lower bounds

One type, or the smallest mass bound

If \(\tau=1\), the sole mass must be \(1/2\). Every ranking yields a distinct score vector. Therefore

\(\boxed{\displaystyle b(m,1,L)=\lfloor\log_2(m!)\rfloor.}\)

If \(L=2\), then \(Q=3\) and \(T=1\), regardless of \(\tau\). Hence

\(\boxed{\displaystyle b(m,\tau,2)=\lfloor\log_2(m!)\rfloor.}\)

Two types

For all parameters, the pure-ranking regions give

\(\displaystyle b(m,\tau,L)\ge\lfloor\log_2(m!)\rfloor=\Omega(m\log m).\)

Suppose \(\tau\ge2\) and \(L\ge3\). Fix two distinct rankings, and let \(q=2^{L-1}\). For every odd \(p\) with \(1\le p<q/2\), assign the two masses

\(\displaystyle \frac pq,\qquad \frac12-\frac pq=\frac{q/2-p}{q}.\)

Both reduced denominators are at most \(q\le Q\), and both numerators satisfy the bound. There are \(2^{L-3}\) such choices. Their score vectors are distinct because the difference between the two ranking score vectors is nonzero. Including the two endpoint regions gives

\(\displaystyle N_{m,\tau,L}\ge2+2^{L-3}.\)

Combining this with the pure-ranking bound yields, for \(\tau\ge2\),

\(\displaystyle b(m,\tau,L)=\Omega(m\log m+L).\)

For \(\tau=2\), support serialization gives the matching upper bound. Thus, uniformly in \(m,L\),

\(\boxed{\displaystyle b(m,2,L)=\Theta(m\log m+L).}\)

An exact scalar formula for two candidates

For \(m=2\) and \(\tau\ge2\), a region is determined by a mass \(a\in[0,1/2]\) on the ranking placing \(c_1\) first.

For an interior mass \(a=p/q\) in lowest terms, its complementary mass is \((q-2p)/(2q)\). If \(q\) is odd, this fraction has reduced denominator \(2q\), so it is allowed exactly when \(q\le\lfloor Q/2\rfloor\). If \(q\) is even, its reduced denominator is either \(q\) or \(q/2\), so it is always allowed when \(q\le Q\).

For each \(q>2\), precisely \(\varphi(q)/2\) reduced fractions have \(0<p/q<1/2\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle N_{2,\tau,L}=2+\frac12\left(\sum_{\substack{4\le q\le Q\\q\text{ even}}}\varphi(q)+\sum_{\substack{3\le q\le\lfloor Q/2\rfloor\\q\text{ odd}}}\varphi(q)\right),\)

and

\(\boxed{\displaystyle b(2,\tau,L)=\lfloor\log_2N_{2,\tau,L}\rfloor.}\)

For example, \(L=3\) gives \(N_{2,\tau,3}=5\) and \(b(2,\tau,3)=2\). The preceding lower bound and the \(O(Q^2)\) count of possible fractions also show \(b(2,\tau,L)=\Theta(L)\).

Transferring the discrete lower bound

The supplied discrete theorem gives an additional lower bound. Take \(T\) known votes and \(T\) complementary votes, and scale each vote to mass \(1/(2T)\). After merging repeated types, every mass has reduced numerator at most \(T\) and denominator at most \(2T\le Q\); each support has at most \(T\le\tau\) types. Scaling preserves the Borda winner, including the fixed tie order. Therefore the discrete lower bound applies:

\(\displaystyle b(m,\tau,L)=\Omega\bigl(m\log(mT)\bigr).\)

The required discrete result, with the same fixed-order tie convention, is [Xia and Conitzer, Theorem 2](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/7627/7488).

These bounds do not assert one elementary asymptotic formula uniformly over all three parameters. The coefficient formula above nevertheless determines the minimum exactly for every parameter triple, with uniformly computable maps attaining it.
