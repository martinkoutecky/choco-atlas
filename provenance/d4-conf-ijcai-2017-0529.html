Each role \(u\in U\) has normalized mass \(1/q\) per copy, and \(H\) specifies the ties within each copy. A policy chooses role seeds in copies based on the paper's partial edge feedback, with \(\int_0^1 |S_x|\,dx\le b\) on every realization, and maximizes \(F(\pi)=\frac1q\mathbb{E}[\int_0^1\sum_{u\in U}\mathbf{1}\{u\text{ is eventually active in copy }x\}\,dx]\). Result

The answer is yes, with an exact guarantee stronger than the requested one. Choose the rational cap \(\displaystyle h=\min\{1,\alpha b\}\). Then mass \(\alpha\)-greedy has a finite rational mass-flow description and satisfies

\(\displaystyle F(\pi)\geq \alpha(1-e^{-1/\alpha})\operatorname{OPT}.\)

Writing \(\displaystyle e=|E|\), an achievable exact running time is

\(\displaystyle 2^{O(e)}\operatorname{poly}(q,L,1/h).\)

Since \(\displaystyle e\leq q^2\) and \(\displaystyle m=\tau=q\), this is also \(\displaystyle 2^{O(m^2)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/h,1/\varepsilon)\). The algorithm does not need to use \(\displaystyle \varepsilon\).

The finite guarantee cited in the question is [Yuan and Tang, Theorem 2](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0546.pdf). The proof below establishes the mass-flow statement directly.

1. Conditional histories and reachability

For an edge realization \(\displaystyle \omega\), let \(\displaystyle R_\omega(S)\) denote the roles reachable from seed set \(\displaystyle S\) by live paths, including the seeds themselves. Eventual activation under IC equals this reachability set.

Fix a positive-mass module history \(\displaystyle \eta\), with current seeds \(\displaystyle S\). Conditional on this history, unrevealed edge states remain independent with their original probabilities.

To justify this posterior assertion, expose feedback slot by slot. The outgoing edges exposed next have tails whose reachability is already certified by previously exposed live paths. Consequently, the choice of the next edges to reveal depends only on previously revealed states. A specified history therefore imposes only the states of its revealed edges. Seed choices and mass cuts impose no additional restrictions on unrevealed edges, because such cuts cannot depend on those states.

Define the support graph at \(\displaystyle \eta\) by retaining:

  • every revealed live edge;
  • every unrevealed edge with positive live probability.

Let \(\displaystyle A_\eta\) be the roles reachable from \(\displaystyle S\) in this support graph, and put \(\displaystyle O_\eta=U\setminus A_\eta\).

A role has positive conditional activation probability exactly when it belongs to \(\displaystyle A_\eta\). Indeed, a support path has positive conditional probability of being entirely live, whereas a live path must be a support path. Thus every role in \(\displaystyle O_\eta\) has conditional activation probability zero.

Two structural properties will be used:

  • No support edge goes from \(\displaystyle A_\eta\) to \(\displaystyle O_\eta\), since such an edge would make its endpoint reachable.
  • No revealed edge has its tail in \(\displaystyle O_\eta\), since every revealed tail is certified reachable from a seed.

In particular, the edge states inside the induced graph on \(\displaystyle O_\eta\) are still independent with their original probabilities.

2. A diminishing-marginal lemma for initially unreachable roles

For an unseeded role \(\displaystyle z\), write its current conditional marginal as

\(\displaystyle \Delta_\eta(z)= \mathbb E_\eta\!\left[|R_\omega(S\cup\{z\})|-|R_\omega(S)|\right].\)

Lemma. Fix an initial history \(\displaystyle \eta\). At any later positive-probability history obtained by additional legal seeding and feedback, the conditional marginal gain in the fixed target set \(\displaystyle O_\eta\), from adding any role \(\displaystyle z\), is at most \(\displaystyle \Delta_\eta(z)\). For a role already seeded initially, this marginal is zero.

Proof. Let \(\displaystyle S'\supseteq S\) be the later seed set. Let \(\displaystyle K\) be the roles reachable from \(\displaystyle S'\) by paths consisting entirely of revealed live edges. Every role in \(\displaystyle K\) is certainly eventually active. Every revealed edge tail belongs to \(\displaystyle K\).

First suppose \(\displaystyle z\in A_\eta\). No completion consistent with \(\displaystyle \eta\) has a live path from \(\displaystyle A_\eta\) into \(\displaystyle O_\eta\). Adding \(\displaystyle z\) therefore has zero marginal gain in \(\displaystyle O_\eta\).

Now suppose \(\displaystyle z\in O_\eta\). In a completion \(\displaystyle \omega\), consider a role in

\(\displaystyle \bigl(R_\omega(S'\cup\{z\})\setminus R_\omega(S')\bigr)\cap O_\eta.\)

Choose a live path to this role starting at \(\displaystyle z\). This path cannot enter \(\displaystyle A_\eta\), because it could not subsequently return to \(\displaystyle O_\eta\). It also cannot meet \(\displaystyle R_\omega(S')\): otherwise its endpoint would already be reachable from \(\displaystyle S'\). Consequently, the entire path lies in \(\displaystyle O_\eta\setminus K\).

All edges with tails in \(\displaystyle O_\eta\setminus K\) are unrevealed at the later history. Their conditional states remain independent with the original probabilities. The later expected marginal in \(\displaystyle O_\eta\) is therefore at most the expected number of roles reachable from \(\displaystyle z\) in the induced graph on \(\displaystyle O_\eta\setminus K\), with value zero if \(\displaystyle z\in K\).

Deleting vertices cannot increase reachability in any edge realization. Hence this upper bound is at most the original expected reachability from \(\displaystyle z\) in the induced graph on \(\displaystyle O_\eta\). Initially, no role of \(\displaystyle O_\eta\) is reachable from \(\displaystyle S\), so this last quantity equals the initial marginal restricted to \(\displaystyle O_\eta\). It is at most \(\displaystyle \Delta_\eta(z)\). This proves the lemma. \(\displaystyle \square\)

3. Comparing a current allocation with an arbitrary policy

Use unnormalized active-role mass. At a current greedy state, define

\(\displaystyle C=\int_0^1\sum_{u\in U}p_{x,u}\,dx,\qquad A=\int_0^1\sum_{u\in U}\mathbf 1\{p_{x,u}>0\}\,dx.\)

Let \(\displaystyle D\) be the largest conditional marginal over all positive-mass histories and their unseeded roles.

For any feasible comparison policy \(\displaystyle \rho\), let \(\displaystyle V_\rho=qF(\rho)\). We claim that

\[\displaystyle V_\rho\leq A+bD. \tag{1}\]

To prove this, couple the current greedy prefix and \(\displaystyle \rho\) using the same edge states. After the prefix, simulate \(\displaystyle \rho\), ignoring observations supplied only by the prefix.

This simulation is legal as an auxiliary comparison procedure. There is no deadline, so it may wait until every feedback observation requested by the simulated policy is available. A role already seeded by the prefix need not be seeded again. The final union of seed sets activates every role activated by \(\displaystyle \rho\). The auxiliary procedure uses at most \(\displaystyle b\) additional seed mass; its combined cost need not obey the original budget, since it is used only for comparison.

Retain each copy’s initial history as a tag, and use its fixed target set \(\displaystyle O_\eta\). Initially, activation in this set has probability zero. By the lemma, each subsequent unit of seed mass has conditional expected marginal at most \(\displaystyle D\) in this target set. Summing these gains bounds its final expected active-role mass by \(\displaystyle bD\).

Outside the tagged target sets, active-role mass is at most \(\displaystyle A\), their total complementary role mass. Since the union activates everything activated by \(\displaystyle \rho\), inequality \(\displaystyle (1)\) follows.

The same argument covers policies with infinitely many moves: apply it to finite prefixes, then use monotone convergence. Eventual activation is the increasing union of the reachability sets generated by those prefixes. Nonnegative marginal gains permit the corresponding sum and integral to pass to the limit.

Taking the supremum over feasible comparison policies, and writing \(\displaystyle V^*=q\operatorname{OPT}\), gives

\[\displaystyle V^*\leq A+bD. \tag{2}\]

Whenever the greedy gate permits seeding, \(\displaystyle C\geq\alpha A\). Thus

\[\displaystyle D\geq\frac{V^*-C/\alpha}{b}. \tag{3}\]

Before the first move, \(\displaystyle C=A=0\), so \(\displaystyle (3)\) also holds there by \(\displaystyle (2)\).

4. Exact gains, waiting, and the approximation recurrence

Suppose a permitted greedy move seeds mass \(\displaystyle a\). It chooses a history-role pair attaining \(\displaystyle D\), and its mass selection does not depend on unrevealed edges. Therefore its increase in \(\displaystyle C\) is exactly

\[\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{new}}-C=aD. \tag{4}\]

Waiting does not change \(\displaystyle C\). The seeds remain fixed, and the exact feedback split preserves their expected eventual active-role count by the law of total expectation.

Moreover, the gate becomes satisfied within at most \(\displaystyle q\) consecutive waiting slots. Once all current seeds have age at least \(\displaystyle q\), feedback reveals outgoing edges of every reachable role: every reachable role admits a simple live path of length at most \(\displaystyle q-1\). The eventual active set is then determined in every resulting history. All activation probabilities are zero or one, so the gate ratio is one.

Let \(\displaystyle a_1,\ldots,a_M\) be the greedy batch masses, and let \(\displaystyle C_i\) be the value after the first \(\displaystyle i\) batches, including any intervening waits. Define

\(\displaystyle \delta_i=V^*-C_i/\alpha.\)

Combining \(\displaystyle (3)\) and \(\displaystyle (4)\) yields

\[\displaystyle \delta_i \leq \left(1-\frac{a_i}{\alpha b}\right)\delta_{i-1}. \tag{5}\]

Our cap satisfies \(\displaystyle a_i\leq h\leq\alpha b\), so all factors in \(\displaystyle (5)\) are nonnegative. Consequently, once termination and full budget expenditure are established,

\[\displaystyle \delta_M \leq V^*\prod_{i=1}^{M}\left(1-\frac{a_i}{\alpha b}\right) \leq V^*\exp\!\left(-\frac{\sum_i a_i}{\alpha b}\right). \tag{6}\]

The next section establishes both required facts.

5. Finite termination despite arbitrarily small histories

The issue is that a move can cost less than \(\displaystyle h\) by exhausting a small history. A lower bound on batch size is therefore unavailable.

Maintain auxiliary positive-mass fragments. Fragments with identical observed histories may be grouped when computing available history mass, but their genealogies remain separate.

For a seeding allocation, take whole fragments in a fixed order, followed by at most one partial fragment. Thus each move cuts at most one fragment into two positive pieces. A nonterminal move costing less than \(\displaystyle h\) exhausts its selected history and cuts no fragment.

Set

\(\displaystyle K_0=\lfloor b/h\rfloor+1,\qquad P=2^e(K_0+1). \)

There are at most \(\displaystyle K_0\) moves consisting of a full-cap move or a terminal move. Hence there are at most \(\displaystyle K_0\) proper fragment cuts.

For a current fragment, let \(\displaystyle r\) be the number of edge states revealed along its genealogy. Give it weight \(\displaystyle 2^{e-r}\).

  • Revealing one new edge creates at most two positive children, each with weight \(\displaystyle 2^{e-r-1}\). Total weight does not increase.
  • Cutting a fragment for seeding creates two pieces with the same revealed states. Total weight increases by at most \(\displaystyle 2^e\).
  • Seeding a whole fragment does not change this weight.

Initially there is one fragment of weight \(\displaystyle 2^e\). Therefore total fragment weight is always at most \(\displaystyle P\). Every fragment has weight at least one, so there are at most \(\displaystyle P\) current fragments.

Now fix any finite execution prefix. Every nonterminal move costing less than \(\displaystyle h\) seeds all fragments of its selected history. Charge that move to one descendant fragment present at the end of the prefix. All moves charged to a given descendant lie on its genealogy, and each adds a previously unseeded role. At most \(\displaystyle q\) moves can therefore be charged to it.

It follows that every finite execution prefix has at most

\[\displaystyle M\leq K_0+qP \tag{7}\]

seeding moves. An infinite execution would violate this uniform bound. Since there are at most \(\displaystyle q\) waiting slots between successive seeding moves, the entire execution is finite.

If it stops before exhausting the budget, every role must have been seeded almost everywhere, which costs \(\displaystyle q\). Because \(\displaystyle b\leq q\), this can occur only when the budget has also been exhausted. Hence

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{M}a_i=b.\)

Substituting into \(\displaystyle (6)\) gives

\(\displaystyle V^*-C_M/\alpha\leq e^{-1/\alpha}V^*, \)

and therefore

\(\displaystyle F(\pi)=C_M/q \geq\alpha(1-e^{-1/\alpha})\operatorname{OPT}. \)

6. Finite description and exact running time

The algorithm stores positive-mass fragments, groups them by their exact module histories, and records every mass allocation rationally. A history records seed identities, seed times, and revealed edge states. These records suffice to determine the feedback available after a wait.

For each history:

  • Enumerate the at most \(\displaystyle 2^e\) completions of its unrevealed edges.
  • Compute activation probabilities and all conditional marginals by live-graph reachability.
  • Compute feedback outcomes after a waiting slot by the same enumeration.
  • Split masses according to their exact conditional probabilities.

All quantities are rational. Choose a fixed lexicographic ordering of encoded history-role pairs for ties. Fragment cuts use only observed histories and the fixed fragment order, so they never depend on unrevealed edge states. Under the stipulated exact-flow interpretation, these rational cuts specify legal measurable submass selections.

For completeness, arithmetic bit lengths are also bounded. Let \(\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{den}}\) be the product of the denominators of the edge probabilities. Its binary length is \(\displaystyle O(L)\). Because the posterior on unrevealed edges is a product distribution, every conditional feedback probability has denominator dividing \(\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{den}}\).

Let \(\displaystyle Q\) be a common denominator of \(\displaystyle b\) and \(\displaystyle h\). After \(\displaystyle T\) global waits, every fragment mass has denominator dividing

\(\displaystyle QD_{\mathrm{den}}^T.\)

This follows inductively: seeding cuts use a stored history mass, the remaining budget, or \(\displaystyle h\); a feedback split multiplies by a probability whose denominator divides \(\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{den}}\). Thus mass bit lengths are

\(\displaystyle O(L+\ell_h+TL),\)

where \(\displaystyle \ell_h\) is the binary length of \(\displaystyle h\). For our chosen cap, \(\displaystyle \ell_h=O(L)\).

By \(\displaystyle (7)\),

\(\displaystyle P=O\!\left(2^e(1+b/h)\right),\qquad M=O\!\left(q\,2^e(1+b/h)\right),\qquad T\leq qM. \)

There are at most \(\displaystyle P\) current fragments. Each history calculation enumerates at most \(\displaystyle 2^e\) worlds and uses polynomial-time graph computations and exact arithmetic. Combining these bounds gives

\(\displaystyle 2^{O(e)}\operatorname{poly}(q,L,1/h) \)

bit operations, using \(\displaystyle b\leq q\). The finite execution log itself provides the requested description by module histories and rational mass allocations.
