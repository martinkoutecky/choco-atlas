The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of roles \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\geq0\) summing to \(1\), symmetric rational valuations \(w_{ts}=w_{st}\), and a rational threshold \(r\), decide whether an atomless society with role masses \(\mu_t\) can be measurably partitioned into coalitions so that its essential minimum utility is at least \(r\). Each type-\(t\) agent in coalition \(K\) has utility \(\sum_{s\in T} w_{ts}\operatorname{mass}(K\cap\Omega_s)\); require the graph of nonzero role interactions to have treewidth at most \(2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) roles with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input also includes signed binary rational valuations \(w_{ts}=w_{st}\) and a signed binary rational threshold \(r\). Diagonal valuations describe interactions between distinct agents of the same role and are allowed.
Let \(G=(T,E)\), where \(E=\{\{t,s\}:t\ne s,\ w_{ts}\ne0\}\); require \(\operatorname{tw}(G)\le2\). Write \(m=|E|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Realize the society as the Lebesgue probability space \(\Omega=[0,1]\), divided into measurable role sets \(\Omega_t\) of masses \(\mu_t\). A coalition structure is a partition \(\mathcal P\) into measurable coalitions, with no bound on their number; singleton and other null coalitions are permitted, and roles may be split.
For \(a\in\Omega_t\), let \(K(a)\) be its coalition and define \(u_{\mathcal P}(a)=\sum_{s\in T}w_{ts}\lambda(K(a)\cap\Omega_s)\). Require this utility function to be measurable. Feasibility means \(\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{a\in\Omega}u_{\mathcal P}(a)\ge r\).

Is deciding whether a feasible coalition structure exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite societies, maximum egalitarian symmetric additively separable hedonic games are weakly \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even on planar graphs of treewidth \(2\) and pathwidth at most \(4\); on trees they admit a linear-time algorithm in the arithmetic-operation model. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.11554v2).
No result settling this exact atomless decision problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Discard zero-mass roles so that the input lists exactly the positive-mass support.
  • Use the Lebesgue probability space on the unit interval as the concrete atomless society.
  • Allow arbitrarily many measurable coalitions, including singleton and null coalitions, and require the induced utility function to be measurable.
  • Retain diagonal valuations as interactions between distinct agents of the same role; an individual's own contribution has zero mass. Compute treewidth on the simple graph of nonzero interactions between distinct roles.
  • Define m as the number of nonzero off-diagonal role-interaction edges and L as the total binary input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with equal role masses and an off-diagonal graph of pathwidth at most \(2\). The permitted diagonal valuations allow us to force each role into a single coalition, up to a null set.

Reduction from PARTITION. An instance of PARTITION consists of positive binary integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\). Writing \(W=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i\), the question is whether some \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=W/2\). This decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Construct roles \(T=\{p,q,t_1,\ldots,t_k\}\), with \(\tau=k+2\), and give every role mass \(\mu_t=1/\tau\). Set \(R=4W+1\) and threshold \(r=R\). Define the symmetric valuations as follows:

  • \(w_{p t_i}=w_{q t_i}=\tau a_i\).
  • \(w_{pq}=-\tau(W+1)\).
  • \(w_{t_i t_i}=\tau(R-a_i)\).
  • \(w_{pp}=w_{qq}=\tau(R-W/2)\).
  • All remaining off-diagonal valuations are zero.

All masses are positive and sum to \(1\). Every diagonal valuation is positive and at most \(\tau R\). All coefficients are binary rationals.

The off-diagonal graph has \(m=2k+1=2\tau-3\) edges. The bags \(B_i=\{p,q,t_i\}\), arranged consecutively along a path, form a path decomposition: every edge occurs in a bag, each item role occurs once, and both central roles occur throughout. Every bag has size \(3\), so the pathwidth, and hence the treewidth, is at most \(2\).

Why feasible roles cannot split. Consider any feasible measurable coalition structure \(\mathcal P\) for this constructed instance. For a measurable coalition \(K\), write \(x_s(K)=\lambda(K\cap\Omega_s)\). Necessarily \(0\le x_s(K)\le1/\tau\).

Define \(P_t=(1/\tau)\sum_{s\ne t}\max\{w_{ts},0\}\). The construction gives \(P_p=P_q=W\) and \(P_{t_i}=2a_i\le2W\). Consequently, an agent \(a\in\Omega_t\) whose coalition is \(K\) has utility bounded above by
\(u_{\mathcal P}(a)\le w_{tt}x_t(K)+P_t\):
each positive off-diagonal contribution is at most \(w_{ts}/\tau\), and discarding negative contributions only increases the bound.

If \(x_t(K)\le1/(2\tau)\), positivity of the diagonal valuation and the bound \(w_{tt}\le\tau R\) imply
\(u_{\mathcal P}(a)\le R/2+2W=R-1/2<R\).
Thus every agent meeting the threshold belongs to a coalition containing strictly more than half of that agent’s role mass.

Because the utility function is measurable and its essential infimum is at least \(R\), the measurable set
\(F=\{a\in\Omega:u_{\mathcal P}(a)\ge R\}\)
has measure \(1\). Hence \(\lambda(F\cap\Omega_t)=1/\tau\) for every role \(t\).

All agents in \(F\cap\Omega_t\) must belong to the same coalition. Otherwise, two distinct, disjoint coalitions would each contain more than \(1/(2\tau)\) mass of role \(t\), exceeding its total mass \(1/\tau\). Denote this unique coalition by \(K_t\). Since it contains \(F\cap\Omega_t\), it contains the full mass of role \(t\).

Furthermore, if \(K_s\ne K_t\), disjointness implies
\(\lambda(K_t\cap\Omega_s)=0\), because \(K_s\) already contains the full mass of role \(s\). Therefore
\(\lambda(K_t\cap\Omega_s)=1/\tau\) when \(K_s=K_t\), and equals zero otherwise.

This argument imposes no countability assumption on \(\mathcal P\); arbitrary null coalitions are harmless.

A feasible structure yields a partition of the integers. First, \(K_p\ne K_q\). If they were equal, then any agent in \(F\cap\Omega_p\) would receive utility at most
\(R-W/2+W-(W+1)=R-W/2-1<R\).
Here the positive contributions from all item roles total at most \(W\), while the full mass of role \(q\) contributes \(-(W+1)\). This contradicts feasibility.

Next, every \(K_{t_i}\) equals either \(K_p\) or \(K_q\). If it equalled neither, its off-diagonal contributions would vanish: the only neighbours of \(t_i\) are \(p\) and \(q\). Its agents in \(F\) would therefore receive
\(w_{t_i t_i}/\tau=R-a_i<R\), again a contradiction.

Let \(I=\{i:K_{t_i}=K_p\}\). All remaining item roles belong to \(K_q\). The utilities of the central roles on \(F\) are consequently
\(R-W/2+\sum_{i\in I}a_i\)
and
\(R-W/2+\sum_{i\notin I}a_i\).
Feasibility requires both sums to be at least \(W/2\). Their sum is \(W\), so both equal \(W/2\). Thus the original PARTITION instance is a yes-instance.

A partition of the integers yields a feasible structure. Conversely, suppose \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=W/2\). Use precisely the two measurable coalitions
\(K_p=\Omega_p\cup\bigcup_{i\in I}\Omega_{t_i}\)
and
\(K_q=\Omega_q\cup\bigcup_{i\notin I}\Omega_{t_i}\).

Each central role receives \(R-W/2+W/2=R\). Each item role \(t_i\) receives its diagonal contribution \(R-a_i\) and the contribution \(a_i\) from its central role, also totalling \(R\). Thus the utility function is identically \(R\), is measurable, and satisfies the required essential-infimum inequality.

The construction is therefore a many-one reduction: the atomless instance is feasible exactly when the PARTITION instance is a yes-instance.

Encoding and running time. Every constructed coefficient has \(O(\log\tau+\log(W+1))\) bits. Thus the output length \(L\) is polynomial in the source input length, including an explicitly listed valuation matrix if required.

Constructing the nonzero coefficients uses \(O(m+\tau)\) arithmetic operations. Using elementary binary arithmetic, a conservative construction bound is \(O((m+\tau)L^2+L)\) bit operations, with the final \(L\) term accounting for writing the encoding. Since \(m=2\tau-3\), hardness holds even with linearly many off-diagonal edges. A decision algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Step-by-step verification

  • Source problem and reduction parameters.

The stated version of PARTITION—positive binary integers with a subset summing to half the total—is NP-complete. Its binary encoding is essential and matches the reduction’s use of potentially large valuations.

With \(W=\sum_i a_i\), \(\tau=k+2\), and \(R=4W+1\), all constructed masses are positive binary rationals summing to \(1\). All valuations are symmetric binary rationals, and the threshold is allowed by the task.

The asserted diagonal bounds hold: positivity follows from \(a_i\le W<R\) and \(W/2<R\), while both diagonal formulas are at most \(\tau R\).

  • Graph restriction.

All \(2k\) item-to-central edges are present because \(a_i>0\), and the central edge is present because \(W+1>0\). Thus \(m=2k+1=2\tau-3\).

The proposed bags \(\{p,q,t_i\}\) cover every vertex and edge. The bags containing any fixed vertex form a contiguous interval: each item appears once, while both central vertices appear throughout. This is a valid path decomposition of width \(2\), establishing the required treewidth bound. Diagonal valuations do not contribute edges under the task’s definition.

  • Uniform utility bound.

For every coalition \(K\), its mass from each role is at most \(1/\tau\). Discarding negative contributions and bounding each positive contribution by the corresponding full-role contribution therefore gives
\(u_{\mathcal P}(a)\le w_{tt}x_t(K)+P_t\).

The calculated bounds \(P_p=P_q=W\) and \(P_{t_i}=2a_i\le2W\) are correct. Consequently, if \(x_t(K)\le1/(2\tau)\), then
\(u_{\mathcal P}(a)\le R/2+2W=R-1/2<R\).
The inequality is strict, as required to exclude even a coalition containing exactly half of a role’s mass.

  • Measure-theoretic argument preventing splitting.

Measurability of the utility function makes \(F=\{a:u_{\mathcal P}(a)\ge R\}\) measurable. The essential-infimum condition implies that \(F\) has full measure, so its intersection with each positive-mass role has that role’s full mass.

Every agent in \(F\cap\Omega_t\) belongs to a coalition containing more than half of role \(t\). Two distinct such coalitions would be disjoint and exceed the total available role mass. Therefore all those agents belong to one coalition \(K_t\), which contains the full mass of role \(t\).

The subsequent dichotomy is valid: \(K_t\) contains full mass from role \(s\) if \(K_t=K_s\), and zero mass from it otherwise.

This argument uses only disjointness of two coalitions at a time. It does not assume countability of the partition or take an unjustified union of null sets. Permitted null coalitions cause no gap.

  • Separation of the central roles.

If \(K_p=K_q\), the diagonal contribution to role \(p\) is \(R-W/2\), the contribution from role \(q\) is \(-(W+1)\), and all positive item contributions total at most \(W\). Hence its utility is at most
\(R-W/2+W-(W+1)=R-W/2-1<R\).

Since \(F\cap\Omega_p\) has positive measure, this contradicts its defining threshold condition. Thus \(K_p\ne K_q\).

  • Placement of every item role.

If \(K_{t_i}\) equals neither central coalition, it contains zero mass from both central roles. All other off-diagonal valuations for \(t_i\) vanish. Its utility is then \(R-a_i<R\), contradicting feasibility because \(a_i>0\).

Each item role therefore belongs to exactly one central coalition.

  • Extraction of a PARTITION solution.

For the stated index set \(I\), the two central utilities are exactly
\(R-W/2+\sum_{i\in I}a_i\) and
\(R-W/2+\sum_{i\notin I}a_i\).

Feasibility forces both item sums to be at least \(W/2\). They sum to \(W\), so both must equal \(W/2\). This establishes the forward implication, including rejection when \(W\) is odd.

  • Construction from a PARTITION solution.

The two proposed coalitions are measurable, disjoint, and cover the society because the role sets form a measurable division of \(\Omega\). Each central role receives utility \(R\). Each item role receives \(R-a_i\) from its own role and \(a_i\) from its central role, also giving \(R\).

Thus the utility function is constant, measurable, and feasible. The reverse implication is established.

  • Encoding and running time.

The coefficient bit bound \(O(\log\tau+\log(W+1))\) is valid, including the possible denominator \(2\) in central diagonal valuations. Both sparse and explicitly listed matrix encodings have polynomial output length in the source input length.

The arithmetic-operation count covers computing \(W\) and the nonzero coefficients. The stated conservative bit bound \(O((m+\tau)L^2+L)\) is sufficient; writing any explicitly listed zero entries is covered by the output-length term.
