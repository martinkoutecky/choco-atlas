The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), integral capacities \(q_t\), and rational subjective-homophily scores satisfying approximate symmetry, together with a feasible initial matching represented by rational masses over integral partner-type bundles and consistent pairings of matching slots, can one compute a finite sequence of rational mass updates to a pairwise-stable state? Each update pairs equal positive masses of mutually blocking types, using disjoint cohorts for a same-type pair; an agent at capacity drops a least-preferred current partner. Stability means no pair of types has positive mass on both sides whose agents mutually block. The target complexity question is whether such a path can be produced in time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(Q=\max_t q_t\), and input bit length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Each type has capacity \(q_t\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}\); put \(Q=\max_t q_t\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Type \(t\) has scores \(\sigma_t(u)\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) for every \(u\in T\), including its own type. All partners are acceptable; lower scores are preferred and equal scores give indifference. Approximate symmetry means, for all \(t,u,v,w\in T\), \(\sigma_t(u)\leq\sigma_v(w)\Rightarrow\max\{\sigma_t(u),\sigma_u(t)\}\leq\min\{\sigma_v(w),\sigma_w(v)\}\).
Represent type \(t\)'s agents by an atomless interval of length \(\mu_t\). A state is an explicit finite rational interval-and-slot table: intervals carry constant integral multisets of partner types of size at most \(q_t\), and occupied slots are paired by piecewise translations with rational endpoints and reciprocal inverse maps. These maps specify actual partners; self-matches and repeated matches between the same two agents are forbidden, but different partners may share a type. The input includes a feasible initial state in this representation.
An unmatched pair of distinct agents of types \(t,u\) blocks when each endpoint either has spare capacity or strictly prefers the other endpoint to some current partner.
An update selects two cohorts of equal positive rational mass, with constant bundles, and pairs them by a rational piecewise translation consisting entirely of blocking pairs; cohorts must be disjoint when \(t=u\). Each endpoint at capacity deletes one least-preferred partner, with arbitrary choices among ties. Deleted partners lose the corresponding slots, including both slots if one agent is dropped by both endpoints; all other matches persist. Rational refinements of the table preserve existing pairings.
A state is pairwise-stable if no such positive-mass blocking cohorts exist. A path is a finite explicit sequence of these updates and their resulting rational tables.

Can a path from every feasible initial state to a pairwise-stable state be produced in time polynomial in \(\tau,Q,L\), or is this path-production problem NP-hard?

For finite agents, a finite blocking-pair path from every initial matching exists under these score restrictions ([Duggan](https://doi.org/10.3390/g11010011)).
For continuous edge allocations on bipartite graphs, polynomial-length better-response paths and expected-polynomial convergence in correlated markets are known ([Cseh–Skutella](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.2169)); these results concern edge allocations rather than distributions of integral partner bundles. 1. Symmetry and persistence

Write $s_{tu}=\sigma_t(u)$.

Exact symmetry

In the stated approximate-symmetry condition, substitute $v=t$ and $w=u$. The premise holds, and the conclusion is
$$ \max\{s_{tu},s_{ut}\}\leq \min\{s_{tu},s_{ut}\}. $$
Hence $s_{tu}=s_{ut}$.

Conversely, exact symmetry makes the conclusion of the stated implication identical to its premise. Thus the score restriction is equivalent to exact symmetry.

Every match therefore has one score shared by its endpoints.

Willingness at a threshold

For an agent $x$ of type $t$ and a rational threshold $r$, let $c_r(x)$ be the number of its current partners whose scores are at most $r$.

An agent is willing to accept a proposed partner of score $r$ exactly when
$$ c_r(x)<q_t. $$

Indeed, if $c_r(x)=q_t$, the agent is full and has no strictly worse partner. If $c_r(x)<q_t$, it either has spare capacity or is full with a partner of score greater than $r$.

In an update adding matches of score $s$, every deleted match has score strictly greater than $s$. A full selected endpoint must have a partner worse than $s$, so its least-preferred partner is worse than $s$. Symmetry gives the same score at the deleted partner’s endpoint.

Consequently, an update of score $s$ cannot decrease $c_r(x)$ at any agent when $r\leq s$.

Two consequences will be used below:

  • In a path whose update scores are nondecreasing, every newly added match persists.
  • If all agents of a type have $q_t$ partners of score at most $r$, updates of score at least $r$ preserve those partners and cannot make that type willing at threshold $r$.

These statements include collateral deletions: symmetry ensures that the deleted edge is worse than the update score at both endpoints.

2. Pairing rational cohorts while avoiding existing partners

Use half-open intervals for the interval representation.

Cohort-pairing lemma

Let $A$ and $B$ be finite unions of rational intervals belonging to specified types, with equal positive rational mass $m$. If their types coincide, assume $A\cap B=\varnothing$.

There is a rational piecewise translation
$$ f:A\longrightarrow B $$
that is bijective and pairs no agent with an existing partner. It can be constructed in time polynomial in the current explicit table and the descriptions of $A$ and $B$.

The cohorts need not have constant bundles for this auxiliary lemma.

Proof. List each cohort’s interval pieces in coordinate order. Concatenation gives piecewise translations
$$ h_A:[0,m)\longrightarrow A, \qquad h_B:[0,m)\longrightarrow B. $$

Consider every current partner map from an agent in $A$ to an agent in $B$. Restrict its domain accordingly and conjugate it by the two concatenation maps. Every resulting piece has the form
$$ z\longmapsto z+d. $$

There are finitely many such pieces; denote their number by $F$. Their descriptions can be obtained by intersecting existing translation domains with cohort pieces and translated target pieces. Thus $F$ and the work needed to list them are polynomial in the input descriptions.

For $a\in(0,m)$, define the rotation
$$ R_a(z)=(z+a)\bmod m. $$
On a piece $z\mapsto z+d$, equality with $R_a$ can occur only if
$$ a\equiv d\pmod m. $$
Each existing-map piece therefore forbids at most one rotation parameter in $(0,m)$.

Among the $F+1$ rational candidates
$$ a_j=\frac{jm}{F+2}, \qquad 1\leq j\leq F+1, $$
at least one is not forbidden. Choose such an $a$ and put
$$ f=h_B\circ R_a\circ h_A^{-1}. $$

This map is a bijection, and the excluded residues ensure that none of its pairs is already matched. Disjointness excludes self-matches when the types coincide.

If the chosen descriptions of $A$ and $B$ contain respectively $a_0$ and $b_0$ interval pieces, then $f$ has at most $a_0+b_0+2$ translation pieces. Its breakpoints come from the source concatenation, the rotation breakpoint, and preimages of target concatenation breakpoints. Its inverse is obtained explicitly by reversing these translations. All calculations are rational and have polynomial bit complexity. $\square$

Stability criterion

For types $t,u$, let $A_t(u)$ be the set of type-$t$ agents willing to accept a type-$u$ partner, and let $a_t(u)$ be its mass. Then a state is pairwise-stable exactly when
$$ a_t(u)=0\quad\text{or}\quad a_u(t)=0 $$
for every $t,u$. For $t=u$, this says $a_t(t)=0$.

Proof. A blocking update supplies positive-mass subsets of both willingness sets. Therefore the displayed zero-mass condition implies stability.

Conversely, suppose both masses are positive. Because bundles are constant on finitely many rational intervals, choose equal-length positive rational intervals within the two willingness sets on which the bundles are constant. When $t=u$, choose them disjointly.

The cohort-pairing lemma supplies a rational piecewise translation consisting entirely of unmatched pairs. Every endpoint is willing, so this is a legal blocking update. Thus failure of the displayed condition implies instability. $\square$

This also repairs the logical-direction issue identified in the verifier’s report. Computing the willingness masses and testing this criterion takes polynomial time in the explicit state size and the preference input.

3. Polynomial paths when own-type partners are weakly best

Theorem

Suppose
$$ s_{tt}\leq s_{tu} \qquad \text{for every }t,u\in T. $$
From every feasible initial state, an explicit path to a pairwise-stable state can be produced in time polynomial in $\tau,Q,L$.

The hypothesis permits ties.

3.1 Preparing fixed intervals

Refine the initial table into a fixed collection of positive-length rational intervals such that, on each interval:

  • the initial bundle is constant;
  • every initial occupied slot has one translation map with a fixed reciprocal target-slot identifier.

Such a refinement has polynomial size. It is obtained by collecting the initial bundle and translation-domain breakpoints, together with any preimages needed to distinguish the original target-slot records. This involves only intersections of finitely many explicitly listed rational translation pieces.

Let $N$ be the total number of resulting fixed intervals. Let $P_0$ be the number of directed translation pieces after preparation. Both are polynomially bounded in $L$.

These fixed intervals are the units processed by the algorithm. Additional subdivisions may be used in the output tables, but each algorithmic update will cover one whole fixed interval.

3.2 Processing types in increasing own-type score

Put
$$ r_t=s_{tt}, $$
and process the types in nondecreasing order of $r_t$.

The invariant after processing a collection of types is:

  • Every agent of a processed type $t$ has exactly $q_t$ partners, all of score $r_t$.
  • All algorithm-created matches are internal to a fixed interval of their endpoint type.
  • On each fixed interval of an unprocessed type, the surviving original slots are uniform.

Here “uniform” means that each original slot is either present throughout the interval or absent throughout it, and each present original map remains its original translation.

Initially, the invariant holds.

To see why the third assertion holds after a whole type $t$ is processed, observe that every original edge incident to type $t$ with score greater than $r_t$ must have disappeared: its type-$t$ endpoint is now full with score-$r_t$ partners. Every surviving original edge incident to $t$ has score $r_t$.

During processing of $t$, no original score-$r_t$ edge is deleted. Later updates also cannot delete it, because their scores are at least $r_t$. Thus, at the end of processing $t$, removal of original matches between $t$ and any unprocessed type is determined uniformly by the type-pair score. This preserves uniformity on each fixed original interval.

It remains to describe how to process the intervals of $t$.

3.3 Filling one fixed interval

Fix a prepared interval
$$ I=[b,b+2m) $$
of type $t$.

At the beginning of its processing, let $c_I$ be the number of partners of score $r_t$ at each agent of $I$. It is constant on $I$ by the invariant.

Every current partner has score at least $r_t$, because own-type partners are weakly best. Perform
$$ h_I=q_t-c_I $$
updates, each adding one type-$t$ partner to every agent of $I$.

Throughout these updates, the bundle remains constant on all of $I$:

  • every newly added partner has score $r_t$;
  • any deleted partner has score greater than $r_t$ and therefore has another type;
  • no other interval is being selected during the processing of $I$;
  • matches between two type-$t$ agents have score $r_t$ and are never deleted.

If the agents of $I$ are full before an update, they have fewer than $q_t$ score-$r_t$ partners, so at least one partner has score greater than $r_t$. Choose one least-preferred surviving original slot, uniformly on $I\!. $ Its map is one fixed translation. Deleting that slot throughout $I$ is exactly the union of the deletions required at the selected endpoints.

If the agents have spare capacity, no deletion is required.

Thus each update increases $c_{r_t}$ by one throughout $I$. After $h_I$ updates, every agent of $I$ has $q_t$ score-$r_t$ partners.

3.4 The internal partner maps

For every update on $I$, use the two constant-bundle cohorts
$$ A=[b,b+m), \qquad B=[b+m,b+2m). $$

For $a\in(0,m)$, pair them by
$$ f_a(b+z)=b+m+\bigl((z+a)\bmod m\bigr), \qquad 0\leq z<m. $$
The reciprocal map is its inverse. Together they give an internal involution on $I$ with at most four directed translation pieces.

We must choose $a$ so that no pair is already matched.

On $I$, every current same-type partner map is either:

  • an original map, with at most $Q$ translation pieces in total on $I$; or
  • one of the internal maps already added while processing $I$.

After $k$ additions, the latter contribute at most $2k$ forward translation pieces from $A$ to $B$. Therefore there are at most
$$ Q+2k\leq 3Q $$
forbidden rotation residues.

Use the fixed candidate set
$$ a_j=\frac{jm}{3Q+2}, \qquad 1\leq j\leq 3Q+1. $$
At least one candidate is allowed. This is precisely the avoidance argument of the cohort-pairing lemma, specialized to two intervals.

The cohorts are disjoint, have equal positive rational mass, and have constant bundles. Every endpoint is willing at score $r_t$, and the chosen map pairs only unmatched agents. Each step is therefore one legal update.

3.5 Completing the invariant and proving stability

Within type $t$, processing one fixed interval cannot remove a partner from another type-$t$ interval: every deletion has score greater than $r_t$, whereas every type-$t$ to type-$t$ match has score $r_t$.

After all intervals of $t$ are processed, every agent of $t$ is full with score-$r_t$ partners. Subsequent updates have score at least $r_t$, so those matches persist. This proves the first two invariant assertions; the uniform-removal argument above proves the third.

At termination, every agent of type $t$ has $q_t$ partners of score $r_t$. Every possible partner has score at least $r_t$. Thus no agent is willing to accept any new partner, and the final state is pairwise-stable.

3.6 Explicit output size and bit complexity

Each fixed interval uses at most $Q$ updates, so the total number is
$$ K\leq NQ. $$

Each update adds at most four directed translation pieces. If it causes a deletion, the deleted original slot has one translation on the whole selected fixed interval. Its reciprocal deletion occurs on one translated interval. Hence a deletion introduces at most two new bundle boundaries at its target; the selected interval’s boundaries were already fixed.

Over the entire path:

  • there are at most $N+2K$ bundle intervals, before harmless additional subdivisions;
  • surviving original maps have polynomially many pieces, because each deletion contributes only a bounded number of new restriction endpoints;
  • new maps contribute at most $4K$ directed pieces.

For completeness, given $n$ bundle intervals and $p$ directed translation pieces, an explicit table can be written by restricting each map piece at source bundle boundaries and at preimages of target bundle boundaries. This uses at most $p(2n+1)$ pieces. The occupied-slot maps remain reciprocal, and every resulting piece lies within constant-bundle intervals. Piecewise slot maps are permitted by the representation in the question. Thus every intermediate table has polynomial size.

Let $D_0$ be a common denominator for all initial coordinates and translation offsets, obtained by taking their denominator product. Then
$$ \log D_0=O(L). $$
Every prepared interval endpoint has denominator dividing $D_0$. The newly constructed internal maps use only halves of these intervals and the fixed denominator $3Q+2$. Their endpoints therefore have denominators dividing
$$ 2D_0(3Q+2). $$
Original-map restrictions introduce only translations by original offsets. Consequently, all output coordinates have bit length
$$ O(L+\log Q). $$

The algorithm, all updates, and all intermediate tables can therefore be produced in polynomial time. This proves the theorem.

4. Polynomial paths when initial matches are mutually top-ranked

This case allows unrestricted symmetric scores, including societies where own-type partners are not best.

Theorem

Define
$$ m_t=\min_{u\in T}s_{tu}. $$
Suppose every initial match between types $t,u$ satisfies
$$ s_{tu}=m_t=m_u. $$
Then an explicit path to stability can be produced in time polynomial in $\tau,Q,L$.

The condition is vacuous for the empty initial state.

4.1 Processing type pairs

List all unordered type pairs, including own-type pairs, in nondecreasing score order. Let
$$ M=\frac{\tau(\tau+1)}2. $$

We construct a path using additions only.

When the current pair is $t,u$ with score $r=s_{tu}$, every current partner at a type-$t$ or type-$u$ agent has score at most $r$:

  • an initial partner has score $m_t\leq r$ or $m_u\leq r$;
  • an algorithm-created partner was added at an earlier or equal score.

Thus, for this pair, willingness is exactly spare capacity.

Let $W_t$ be the set of type-$t$ agents with spare capacity.

For $t\neq u$, while both $W_t$ and $W_u$ have positive mass, perform the following auxiliary round:

  • Take all of the smaller-mass set.
  • Take an equal-mass prefix, in coordinate order, of the larger set.
  • Pair the cohorts using the cohort-pairing lemma.

For $t=u$, take all of $W_t$, split it into two equal-mass cohorts in coordinate order, and pair those cohorts using the same lemma.

The word “round” is only bookkeeping. To emit legal updates, describe the cohorts using their current constant-bundle interval pieces and restrict the new map at those pieces and their target preimages. Each resulting translation pairs two equal-length intervals with constant bundles. Emit these restrictions as separate updates.

All selected endpoints have spare capacity. Moreover, the source and target pieces in a round are disjoint, so no agent participates twice in that round. Earlier updates in the round therefore do not change the bundles or spare capacity of endpoints scheduled for later updates. Every emitted update is legal and causes no deletion.

4.2 Bounding the number of rounds

For type $t$, let
$$ \Delta_t=\max_x\bigl(q_t-\deg(x)\bigr), $$
where the maximum is over its agents.

This is an integer between zero and $q_t$. Since there are no deletions, it never increases.

In a round covering all spare agents of type $t$, every positive deficit decreases by one. Therefore $\Delta_t$ decreases by one.

For a distinct-type pair, charge each round to a side whose entire spare set it covers. There are at most $q_t$ charges to $t$ and $q_u$ charges to $u$. Thus a pair uses at most
$$ q_t+q_u\leq 2Q $$
rounds. An own-type pair uses at most $q_t\leq Q$ rounds.

The total number $B$ of rounds satisfies
$$ B\leq 2QM=O(\tau^2Q). $$

4.3 Stability

When processing $t,u$ ends, at least one of their spare-capacity sets has zero mass. All current partners at that type have score at most $r=s_{tu}$. Consequently its willingness mass for the other type is zero.

Later updates have score at least $r$. By the persistence lemma, they cannot decrease any agent’s count $c_r$. Hence the eliminated blocking opportunity cannot reappear.

For an own-type pair, all spare agents of that type are exhausted, and the same argument applies.

Every unordered pair has been processed at termination. The stability criterion therefore proves that the final state is pairwise-stable.

4.4 Bounding bundle intervals and partner-map pieces

Let $n_0$ be the number of initial bundle intervals and $p_0$ the number of initial directed translation pieces.

At the end of each round, adding the same partner type throughout each selected cohort changes bundles uniformly:

  • the smaller spare set is a union of whole current bundle intervals;
  • the prefix of the larger spare set introduces at most one new interior boundary;
  • in an own-type round, every spare agent receives the same partner type, so the temporary halving boundary need not remain a bundle boundary.

The partner maps can retain their piecewise descriptions when identical neighboring bundle descriptions are coalesced. Thus after $j$ rounds we need at most
$$ n_j\leq n_0+j $$
bundle intervals.

The cohorts in a round have $O(n_j+1)$ interval pieces. The cohort-pairing lemma therefore creates $O(n_j+1)$ new translation pieces and requires $O(n_j+1)$ actual updates.

No existing match is changed. Hence throughout the construction the number of stored directed translation pieces is at most
$$ p_0+O\bigl(B(n_0+B+1)\bigr). $$

Within a round, the actual updates introduce at most $O(n_j+1)$ temporary bundle boundaries, because each update changes bundles only on its two selected intervals. Thus the same polynomial bound applies to intermediate states, not merely to states at the ends of rounds.

The total number of actual updates is
$$ O\bigl(B(n_0+B+1)\bigr). $$

As in Section 3, restricting stored maps at source and target bundle boundaries converts these descriptions into explicit interval-and-slot tables with polynomially many records. Therefore writing every intermediate table also takes polynomial space and time.

4.5 Rational bit complexity

At a round with $n$ bundle intervals and $p$ stored translation pieces, forming the existing partner maps in concatenation coordinates requires at most
$$ F\leq p(2n+5) $$
pieces: each original translation piece is split only at source cohort boundaries and translated target cohort boundaries.

All these quantities are polynomially bounded by the preceding analysis.

Suppose all current coordinates and offsets have denominators dividing $D$. Cohort masses and concatenation coordinates use sums and differences of such rationals. An own-type split may additionally introduce a factor of two. The rotation parameter has denominator dividing $F+2$ times the cohort-mass denominator.

We can therefore maintain a common denominator satisfying
$$ D_{j+1}=2(F_j+2)D_j. $$
With an initial denominator product $D_0$ having $\log D_0=O(L)$, this gives
$$ \log D_B \leq O(L)+\sum_{j=0}^{B-1}\log\bigl(2(F_j+2)\bigr). $$

Since $B$ and every $F_j$ are polynomially bounded in $\tau,Q,L$, all coordinates have polynomial bit length. Cohort selection, collision avoidance, emitting individual updates, and writing their resulting tables therefore take polynomial time. This proves the theorem.

5. Consequences and the remaining question

The first theorem covers arbitrary initial states and arbitrary capacities whenever every type weakly prefers its own type. In particular, it resolves the single-type case for every capacity.

The second theorem covers arbitrary symmetric preference matrices from an empty initial state. Thus a stable state, together with a path from the empty state, can always be constructed in polynomial time. It also covers arbitrary feasible initial states when all scores are equal.

Neither additional hypothesis follows from symmetry. For example,
$$ s_{11}=s_{22}=2, \qquad s_{12}=s_{21}=1 $$
is symmetric but violates the own-type-best hypothesis. An initial own-type match in this society also violates the initially-top-ranked hypothesis.

For general prescribed initial states, updates may delete original matches on proper subintervals and thereby change bundles at their translated partners. The two constructions above control this effect respectively by deleting from whole fixed original intervals and by avoiding deletions altogether. I have not established a polynomial bound for the corresponding interval refinements in the unrestricted case, nor an NP-hardness reduction.
