Given task classes \(J\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), values \(u_j\), budget \(\beta\), and a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over worker types \((A,c)\), compute an anonymous randomized allocation and payment rule from the type masses. For replications \(N\) with integral type counts \(N\mu\) and task supplies \(Nq_j\), require each outcome to respect eligibility, task capacity, and total budget \(N\beta\); achieve welfare at least \((e-1)/(2e-1)\) times the cost-aware optimum; and retain Theorem 3’s large-market truthfulness guarantees as \(\theta_N=u_{\max}/\mathrm{OPT}_N\to0\), where a unilateral report changes that worker’s type mass by \(1/N\).

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational, summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
For every positive integer \(N\) making \(N\mu\) and all \(Nq_j\) integral, there are \(N\) workers and \(Nq_j\) indivisible tasks of class \(j\), with budget \(N\beta\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}_N\) be the maximum total task value of an eligible matching whose sum of true worker costs is at most \(N\beta\); consider replications with \(\mathrm{OPT}_N>0\).
An anonymous randomized rule depends on workers only through reported type masses, and its joint outcome distribution is invariant under relabeling workers. Every realized outcome must respect eligibility, worker and task capacities, and total payments at most \(N\beta\); payments are nonnegative, unmatched workers receive zero, and truthful assigned workers receive at least their costs.
Write \(U_{p,N}(x,d_{-p})=\mathbb E[P_p-c_p I_p]\) for a worker's utility when reporting \(x\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), where \(I_p\) indicates assignment and other workers report \(d_{-p}\). A unilateral report moves mass \(1/N\) from \((A_p,c_p)\) to \((A_p,x)\), possibly creating a new support type.

Status and new partial results

The unrestricted target remains unresolved. I prove a support-polynomial mechanism for arbitrary task values that is exactly truthful, strictly budget feasible, individually rational, and satisfies
$$ \mathbb E[W_N]\ge \frac15\,\mathrm{OPT}_N $$
at every admissible replication.

I also prove that an exactly truthful component of this mechanism satisfies
$$ W_N\ge \frac{\mathrm{OPT}_N-2u_{\max}}{3}, \qquad u_{\max}=\max_j u_j. $$
Consequently, its welfare ratio has limit inferior at least $1/3$ as $\theta_N\to0$.

These guarantees fall short of the requested fraction
$$ \alpha=\frac{e-1}{2e-1}. $$
The principal new computational result is that the greedy allocation and its exact critical payments can be computed from the support in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,\log N$.

1. Definitions and the matching-value function

Fix an admissible replication. For reported type $i$, write
$$ n_i=N\mu_i,\qquad Q_j=Nq_j,\qquad B=N\beta. $$
Thus all $n_i$ and $Q_j$ are integers, and $\sum_i n_i=N$.

For the proofs, let $P$ be the conceptual set of individual workers. It need not be constructed by the algorithm. For $S\subseteq P$, define
$$ v(S)=\max\left\{ \text{total task value of an eligible matching using workers in }S \right\}. $$
Costs do not enter this definition. Workers in $S$ may remain unmatched.

At truthful reports, the benchmark has the equivalent description
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_N= \max\left\{ v(S):S\subseteq P,\ \sum_{p\in S}c_p\le B \right\}. $$
Indeed, every cost-feasible matching supplies such a set of matched workers. Conversely, a matching attaining $v(S)$ uses a subset of $S$, so its cost is at most $\sum_{p\in S}c_p$.

We require two structural properties of $v$.

1.1. Monotonicity and submodularity

The function $v$ is normalized and monotone:
$$ v(\varnothing)=0,\qquad S\subseteq T\Longrightarrow v(S)\le v(T). $$

To establish submodularity, list the distinct task values as
$$ a_1>a_2>\cdots>a_s>0, \qquad a_{s+1}=0. $$
Let $H_\ell$ contain the individual task copies whose values are at least $a_\ell$, and let $r_\ell(S)$ be the maximum cardinality of a matching between $S$ and $H_\ell$.

For fixed $H_\ell$, the worker subsets matchable into $H_\ell$ form a matroid. Heredity follows by restricting a matching. For augmentation, take matchings saturating independent worker sets $X,Y$ with $|X|<|Y|$. Their symmetric difference contains an alternating path with one more edge from the matching of $Y$. Its endpoints are a worker in $Y\setminus X$ and a task unmatched by the matching of $X$. Flipping this path augments the matching of $X$.

Thus $r_\ell$ is a matroid rank function and is submodular. For completeness, the rank inequality follows by taking a basis of $X\cap Y$, extending it to a basis of $X$, and then to a basis of $X\cup Y$. The elements added in the last extension lie in $Y\setminus X$; together with the original basis of $X\cap Y$, they form an independent subset of $Y$. Therefore
$$ r_\ell(X\cup Y)-r_\ell(X) \le r_\ell(Y)-r_\ell(X\cap Y). $$

We next show that
$$ v(S)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}(a_\ell-a_{\ell+1})r_\ell(S). $$
Every matching has at most $r_\ell(S)$ matched tasks in $H_\ell$, giving the upper bound in this identity.

For attainment, the task subsets matchable into $S$ also form a matroid, by the same augmentation argument. Greedily scan task copies in nonincreasing value order, retaining a task whenever the retained tasks remain matchable into $S$. At the end of each prefix $H_\ell$, the retained tasks form a maximal independent subset of that prefix and consequently have cardinality $r_\ell(S)$. A matching of the final retained task set therefore attains the displayed sum.

The coefficients $a_\ell-a_{\ell+1}$ are positive. Hence $v$, being a nonnegative linear combination of submodular rank functions, is submodular. In particular, for $S\subseteq T$ and $p\notin T$,
$$ v(S\cup\{p\})-v(S) \ge v(T\cup\{p\})-v(T). $$

1.2. A one-worker marginal is one task value

Lemma. For $p\notin S$,
$$ v(S\cup\{p\})-v(S)\in\{0,u_1,\ldots,u_m\}. $$
Moreover, if an optimal matching on $S$ saturates $S$ and this marginal is positive, an optimal matching on $S\cup\{p\}$ saturates $S\cup\{p\}$.

Proof. Take optimal matchings $M$ on $S$ and $M'$ on $S\cup\{p\}$. Their symmetric difference consists of alternating paths and cycles.

Consider a component not containing $p$. Flipping it in either matching remains feasible for that matching’s worker set. If its contribution in $M'$ had greater value, flipping it would improve $M$. If its contribution in $M$ had greater value, flipping it would improve $M'$. Thus the contributions have equal value.

Replace all such components of $M'$ by their contributions from $M$. The resulting matching remains optimal and differs from $M$ only on the component containing $p$.

If $p$ is unmatched, the marginal is zero. Otherwise that component is a path beginning at $p$ with an edge of $M'$. Its other endpoint is either:

  • a worker, in which case the matched task set is unchanged and the value difference is zero; or
  • a task newly matched by $M'$, in which case every internal task is matched in both matchings and the value difference is precisely that endpoint task’s value.

In the positive case the path ends at a task. Flipping it preserves the assignments of all previously matched workers and additionally matches $p$. This proves both assertions. $\square$

This discrete marginal-value property is what permits compression of the greedy mechanism below.

2. An exactly truthful proportional-share mechanism

Set
$$ D=\frac B2. $$
At any reported profile, discard from consideration workers whose reports exceed $B$.

Fix a public ordering of eligibility sets by lexicographic comparison of their incidence vectors in the input class order. This comparison takes time polynomial in $m$.

Conceptually, draw independent continuous uniform priorities for individual workers, independently of all reports. These priorities induce exchangeable, almost surely strict orders.

Starting with $S=\varnothing$, repeat the following procedure.

For each remaining worker $p$, write its report as $d_p$ and its current marginal as
$$ \Delta_p(S)=v(S\cup\{p\})-v(S). $$
Choose a worker maximizing
$$ \frac{\Delta_p(S)}{d_p}. $$
Resolve ties first by the public eligibility order and then by the report-independent priorities.

If the largest marginal is zero, stop. Otherwise, for the chosen worker $p$, test
$$ d_p\le D\frac{\Delta_p(S)}{v(S\cup\{p\})}. $$
If the test fails, stop. If it passes, add $p$ to $S$ and continue.

Every accepted marginal is positive. The lemma in Section 1.2 therefore proves inductively that the final selected set has a matching that saturates every selected worker and attains value $v(S)$.

Pay each selected worker its critical value
$$ t_p= \sup\{x\in\mathbb Q_{>0}:p\text{ is selected when reporting }x\}, $$
with other reports and priorities fixed. Pay unmatched workers zero.

The next sections prove that these payments are well-defined and strictly budget feasible.

2.1. Monotonicity

Fix all priorities and all other reports. Suppose $p$ is selected at report $d_p$, and lower its report to $x<d_p$.

Until $p$ is chosen in the new run, the new run agrees with the original run, except that $p$ can move earlier: its density increases, while every other density at an unchanged selected set stays unchanged.

Let $T$ be the selected set when the new run chooses $p$, and let $S_p$ be the set preceding $p$ in the original run. Then
$$ T\subseteq S_p. $$
Submodularity and monotonicity give
$$ \Delta_p(T)\ge\Delta_p(S_p), \qquad v(T\cup\{p\})\le v(S_p\cup\{p\}). $$
The original acceptance test consequently implies
$$ x<d_p \le D\frac{\Delta_p(S_p)}{v(S_p\cup\{p\})} \le D\frac{\Delta_p(T)}{v(T\cup\{p\})}. $$
The new run accepts $p$.

Thus selection is nonincreasing in the reported cost. Every selected worker has a positive threshold. Every winning report is at most $D$, because $\Delta_p(T)\le v(T\cup\{p\})$ in its acceptance test. Hence the threshold is finite.

2.2. Exact truthfulness and individual rationality

For fixed other reports and priorities, a selected worker receives the same payment $t_p$ at every winning report.

If its true cost is below $t_p$, truthful reporting wins and gives utility $t_p-c_p>0$. If its true cost is above $t_p$, truthful reporting loses; winning by another report would give negative utility. If its true cost equals $t_p$, either assignment status gives utility zero.

Therefore truthful reporting maximizes utility for each fixed priority realization. Threshold ties cause no difficulty.

Also, a selected worker’s report is at most its threshold. Thus truthful assigned workers receive at least their costs.

3. Strict budget feasibility

Fix any reported profile and priority realization. Let the selected workers, in order, have reports $d_1,\ldots,d_k$ and acceptance marginals $\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_k$. Write
$$ F=v(S)=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\Delta_r. $$
If $k=0$, there are no payments. Assume $k>0$.

3.1. The selected reports sum to at most the half-budget

The selected densities are nonincreasing. Indeed, after an acceptance, submodularity makes every remaining marginal weakly decrease, while reports stay fixed.

The last acceptance test gives
$$ \frac{d_k}{\Delta_k}\le\frac D F. $$
For every earlier selected worker,
$$ \frac{d_r}{\Delta_r}\le\frac{d_k}{\Delta_k}. $$
Consequently,
$$ \sum_{r=1}^{k}d_r \le\frac D F\sum_{r=1}^{k}\Delta_r =D. $$

3.2. A bound on each critical payment

Fix an originally selected worker $p$, and let $\Delta_p^0$ be its original acceptance marginal.

Suppose $p$ remains selected after raising its report to $x\ge d_p$. Let $T$ be the set preceding it in that new run. The new run agrees with the original run through the original prefix preceding $p$: raising $p$ cannot cause it to precede an originally earlier choice. Therefore that original prefix is contained in $T$, and
$$ \Delta_p(T)\le\Delta_p^0. $$

At the moment the new run chooses $p$, every worker $q\in S\setminus(T\cup\{p\})$ is still available. Density maximality gives
$$ \Delta_q(T)\le \frac{\Delta_p(T)}x\,d_q. $$
Using monotonicity and submodularity,
$$ \begin{aligned} F &\le v(T\cup S)\\ &\le v(T\cup\{p\}) +\sum_{q\in S\setminus(T\cup\{p\})}\Delta_q(T)\\ &\le v(T\cup\{p\}) +\frac{\Delta_p(T)}x \sum_{q\in S\setminus(T\cup\{p\})}d_q. \end{aligned} $$
The new acceptance test and Section 3.1 imply
$$ v(T\cup\{p\})\le D\frac{\Delta_p(T)}x, \qquad \sum_{q\in S\setminus(T\cup\{p\})}d_q\le D. $$
Hence
$$ F\le 2D\frac{\Delta_p(T)}x \le 2D\frac{\Delta_p^0}x. $$
Every such winning report satisfies
$$ x\le\frac{2D\Delta_p^0}{F}. $$
The same argument applies at $x=d_p$, so winning reports below $d_p$ satisfy this bound as well. Taking the supremum yields
$$ t_p\le\frac{2D\Delta_p^0}{F} =\frac{B\Delta_p^0}{F}. $$

Summing over selected workers proves
$$ \sum_{p\in S}t_p \le\frac B F\sum_{p\in S}\Delta_p^0 =B. $$
Thus every realized outcome is strictly budget feasible.

4. Welfare for arbitrary task values

In this section reports are truthful. Define the largest feasible singleton value
$$ M=\max\left(\{v(\{p\}):c_p\le B\}\cup\{0\}\right). $$
In particular,
$$ M\le u_{\max}. $$

Let $F$ be the welfare of the greedy mechanism.

If the procedure exhausts its candidates or stops because every remaining marginal is zero, submodularity implies $v(T)\le F$ for every cost-feasible worker set $T$. Thus
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_N\le F. $$

Otherwise, let $p$ be the first worker whose acceptance test fails, and write
$$ \delta=\Delta_p(S)>0. $$
Its density is maximal among remaining candidates, and failure means
$$ c_p>D\frac{\delta}{F+\delta}. $$
For an optimal cost-feasible set $T$, all workers in $T$ have costs at most $B$, so none was discarded initially. Submodularity gives
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_N=v(T) \le F+\sum_{q\in T\setminus S}\Delta_q(S). $$
By density maximality and cost feasibility,
$$ \sum_{q\in T\setminus S}\Delta_q(S) \le\frac{\delta}{c_p}\sum_{q\in T\setminus S}c_q \le B\frac{\delta}{c_p}. $$
Since $D=B/2$, the failed acceptance test implies
$$ B\frac{\delta}{c_p}<2(F+\delta). $$
Therefore
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_N<3F+2\delta. $$
Submodularity gives $\delta\le v(\{p\})$, and $c_p\le B$ because $p$ was a candidate. Hence $\delta\le M$.

Combining both stopping cases,
$$ \boxed{\mathrm{OPT}_N\le 3F+2M.} $$
In particular,
$$ \boxed{F\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}_N-2u_{\max}}3.} $$
For the replications considered in the question,
$$ \frac{F}{\mathrm{OPT}_N} \ge \frac13-\frac23\theta_N. $$
Thus this exactly truthful mechanism has asymptotic welfare fraction at least $1/3$.

4.1. An exact finite guarantee of one fifth

Consider a second mechanism that selects one worker as follows.

Among workers reporting at most $B$, choose one maximizing the public singleton value $v(\{p\})$. Resolve ties by the public eligibility order and report-independent priorities. Assign that worker a highest-value eligible task and pay $B$. If no positive-value candidate exists, assign nobody.

This mechanism is exactly truthful. For fixed other reports and priorities, a worker either wins at every report in $(0,B]$ or wins at none of them. Reports above $B$ lose. A winner’s critical payment is consequently $B$.

It is also individually rational and strictly budget feasible. At truthful reports its welfare is $M$.

Choose the greedy mechanism with probability $3/5$ and the singleton mechanism with probability $2/5$, independently of reports. The resulting rule is exactly truthful and satisfies all feasibility requirements. Its welfare obeys
$$ \mathbb E[W_N] =\frac35F+\frac25M =\frac{3F+2M}{5} \ge\frac15\,\mathrm{OPT}_N. $$

5. Computing the greedy allocation from the support

We now establish the claimed runtime without enumerating workers.

5.1. A compressed value oracle

For any vector of selected type counts, compute $v$ using a capacitated flow network:

  • a source-to-type arc has capacity equal to that type’s selected count;
  • an eligible type-to-class arc has capacity $N$;
  • a class-to-sink arc has capacity $Q_j$;
  • a sink-to-source return arc has capacity $N$.

Put value coefficient $u_j$ on each class-to-sink arc and zero elsewhere. Maximizing circulation value computes $v$.

The network has polynomially many vertices and arcs. All capacities are integers with bit length polynomial in $L+\log N$.

I use the standard polynomial-time minimum-cost-flow theorem: a finite network with integer capacities and rational arc costs admits an integral optimal flow, computable in time polynomial in the network size and input bit length. Its hypotheses hold here, after negating the value coefficients.

The same network computes a final matching saturating selected workers by fixing each source-to-type flow to the selected count. Such a matching attaining $F$ exists by Section 1.2.

5.2. Batching identical workers

At a current selected-count vector, all remaining workers of reported type $i$ have the same marginal $\delta_i$.

By submodularity, each type’s marginal weakly decreases as the selected set grows. By Section 1.2, its possible values belong to
$$ \{0,u_1,\ldots,u_m\}. $$

Suppose type $i$ currently wins the density comparison and has positive marginal $\delta$. As long as its marginal remains $\delta$, it continues winning: its own density stays fixed, every competing density weakly decreases, and the public tie order stays fixed.

Let the current value be $F_0$. If there are $r$ further identical workers available, their successive marginals are nonincreasing. Consequently,
$$ v(S+r\text{ workers of type }i)=F_0+r\delta $$
holds precisely while all of those marginals equal $\delta$.

Using the value oracle and binary search, find the largest such $r$ in $O(\log N)$ oracle calls.

Within this constant-marginal batch, the acceptance test for its $h$th worker is
$$ d_i\le D\frac{\delta}{F_0+h\delta}, $$
equivalently,
$$ h\le \frac D{d_i}-\frac{F_0}{\delta}. $$
Thus the affordable batch length is obtained by one exact rational floor operation.

If affordability ends before the marginal plateau ends, the next worker of the same type is still the density maximizer and fails the test, so the procedure stops. Otherwise a completed batch either exhausts that type or strictly decreases its marginal.

Each type’s marginal can strictly decrease at most $m$ times. Therefore there are at most $\tau(m+1)$ completed batches, followed by at most one stopping batch. The complete allocation computation uses polynomially many value-oracle calls and arithmetic operations.

The identical reasoning applies after removing one worker from a type and inserting a singleton type for a hypothetical report.

6. Computing exact critical payments

Monotone allocation alone does not justify a support-polynomial implementation: exact payments must also be computable. Here their rational structure supplies the necessary bound.

6.1. Possible threshold boundaries

Fix a selected worker $p$ and all other reports. Let $x$ denote its hypothetical report.

In the conceptual finite-worker algorithm, selection can change only at one of the following kinds of boundary:

  • the consideration boundary $x=B$;
  • a density comparison boundary

$$ x=d_q\frac{u_a}{u_b}; $$

  • an acceptance boundary for $p$,

$$ x=D\frac{u_a}{v(T\cup\{p\})}, \qquad v(T\cup\{p\})>0. $$

Here $q$ is another worker, $a,b$ are task classes, and $T$ is some selected prefix.

To justify this list, away from these boundaries every comparison involving $p$ is strict. For a fixed selected prefix, every positive marginal is a task value. Comparisons and acceptance tests involving only other workers do not depend on $x$. Hence the finite sequence of decisions determining whether $p$ is selected is locally constant away from the listed boundaries.

Because selection is monotone and $p$ has a finite positive threshold, that threshold must be one of these boundary values. The number of possible prefixes need not be polynomial; only the bit length of their boundary values is used below.

6.2. A polynomial bound on threshold denominators

Let $L$ be the current reported support’s input length and set
$$ H=2^L. $$
Every input numerator and denominator is at most $H$. A common denominator $D_u$ for the task values can be chosen with
$$ D_u\le H, $$
because the product of their denominators has bit length at most their contribution to $L$.

Every integral matching value has the form
$$ v(T\cup\{p\})=\frac Z{D_u}, \qquad 0\le Z\le NH^2. $$
The upper bound follows from at most $N$ matched tasks and $u_{\max}\le H$.

A density boundary has denominator at most $H^3$ before reduction. An acceptance boundary has denominator at most
$$ 2H\cdot H\cdot NH^2=2NH^4 $$
before reduction. The boundary $B$ also satisfies this bound.

Thus every positive critical threshold, in lowest terms, has denominator at most the explicitly computable integer
$$ Q=2N\,2^{4L}. $$
Its bit length is polynomial in $L+\log N$.

6.3. Exact recovery by allocation queries

For a selected representative $p$, begin with the interval
$$ [a,b]=[d_p,B+1]. $$
The lower endpoint wins and the upper endpoint loses.

Use bisection and the compressed allocation algorithm to retain an interval containing $t_p$. After polynomially many queries, its width is less than
$$ \frac1{4Q^2}. $$
All query reports still have polynomial bit length.

Exact recovery uses the standard continued-fraction criterion:

If a reduced rational $h/k$ satisfies
$$ > \left|z-\frac hk\right|<\frac1{2k^2}, > $$
then $h/k$ is a convergent of the continued fraction of $z$.

Take $z$ to be the midpoint of the retained interval. Since the denominator of $t_p$ is at most $Q$,
$$ |z-t_p|<\frac1{8Q^2} \le\frac1{2\,\operatorname{den}(t_p)^2}. $$
Therefore $t_p$ occurs among the continued-fraction convergents of $z$. These are computable by the Euclidean algorithm in polynomial time.

There is at most one rational of denominator at most $Q$ in the retained interval: two distinct such rationals differ by at least $1/Q^2$. Selecting the convergent in the interval with denominator at most $Q$ therefore recovers $t_p$ exactly.

6.4. One payment calculation per reported type

The numerical threshold is independent of the random priorities.

Outside the finite boundary set above, $p$ has no positive-density tie with another worker. Other priority ties can occur only between workers of the same reported type: workers with the same eligibility have identical marginals, and distinct positive reports then give distinct densities. Resolving those ties changes identities but not aggregate selected counts.

Thus, away from the boundary set, selection of $p$ depends only on the aggregate profile and its public eligibility. Monotonicity then implies that all priority realizations give the same threshold supremum, even if assignment at the threshold itself differs.

Removing any representative of a reported type also leaves the same aggregate profile. Hence all selected workers of that type have the same threshold.

For the bisection queries, one may use any consistent fixed tie position for the distinguished representative, implemented as a singleton group. There are at most $\tau$ selected types, so all exact payments require polynomially many compressed allocation computations.

7. Anonymous compressed outcomes and the incentive requirements

For the greedy component, output:

  • the selected count of each reported type;
  • integral type-to-class matching counts attaining $F$ and saturating those selected counts;
  • the exact critical payment for each selected type;
  • instructions to partition each type’s workers uniformly among its class-assignment slots and unmatched slots.

This representation has polynomial size.

The conceptual independent priorities select a uniformly random subset of the prescribed size from each reported type. Their induced orders on disjoint types are independent. Thus the uniform identity expansion has the same selected-set distribution as the priority mechanism. Choosing class slots uniformly then gives the stated matching distribution without changing utilities.

For the singleton component, singleton values are public. After the public value and eligibility tie rules determine the winning eligibility set, the priority winner is uniform among its workers reporting at most $B$. Its type probabilities are therefore rational ratios of type counts. Its payment is $B$.

The mixed rule can consequently be represented by a table with at most $\tau+1$ compressed components and binary rational probabilities. No sampling approximation or population-size expansion is required to compute that table.

Both components’ joint outcome distributions are invariant under relabeling workers. Every outcome respects eligibility and capacities, pays unmatched workers zero, and has total payments at most $B$.

Finally, exact truthfulness holds against arbitrary other reports, including unilateral reports creating a singleton support type. For every worker, every report $x$, and every replication,
$$ U_{p,N}(x,d_{-p}) \le U_{p,N}(c_p,d_{-p}). $$
It follows that the first required expression is exactly zero:
$$ \sup_x\left[ U_{p,N}(x,d_{-p})-U_{p,N}(c_p,d_{-p}) \right]=0. $$
There are no strictly profitable deviations, so the second required expression is also exactly zero:
$$ \sup\left\{ |x-c_p|: U_{p,N}(x,d_{-p})>U_{p,N}(c_p,d_{-p}) \right\}=0. $$
These statements hold without taking a large-market limit.

8. Remaining gap
