The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), categories \(k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), finite goods \(O^k\), per-agent integral endowments \(e_{\theta,o}\), and acceptable sets \(G_\theta^k\), let \(v_\theta(B)=\sum_k \mathbf{1}[B\cap G_\theta^k\neq\emptyset]\) for integral bundles \(B\) containing at most one good per category, and let \(q_o=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta e_{\theta,o}\). Compute a mass allocation \(z_{\theta,B}\ge0\) with \(\sum_B z_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B:o\in B}z_{\theta,B}\le q_o\), such that no positive-mass selection \(0\le s_{\theta,B}\le z_{\theta,B}\) admits variables \(y_{\theta,B,B'}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{B'}y_{\theta,B,B'}=s_{\theta,B}\), \(v_\theta(B')>v_\theta(B)\) whenever \(y_{\theta,B,B'}>0\), and \(\sum_{\theta,B,B':o\in B'}y_{\theta,B,B'}\le\sum_{\theta,B}s_{\theta,B}e_{\theta,o}\) for every good \(o\).

Let \(O=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{K}O^k\), where \(K\geq1\) and categories are finite and nonempty; write \(m=|O|\). Each type has explicitly listed acceptable sets \(G_\theta^k\subseteq O^k\) and binary-encoded endowments \(e_{\theta,o}\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\), with \(\sum_o e_{\theta,o}\geq1\). The allowable bundles are \(\mathcal B=\{B\subseteq O:|B\cap O^k|\leq1\text{ for every }k\}\), including the empty bundle. Utility is \(v_\theta(B)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf1[B\cap G_\theta^k\neq\emptyset]\); equal utilities mean indifference. Endowments need not be allowable consumption bundles.
A mass allocation satisfies \(z_{\theta,B}\geq0\), \(\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}z_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta\), and \(\sum_{\theta,B:o\in B}z_{\theta,B}\leq q_o\). Unused goods may be discarded.
It is in the weak core if there are no real selections \(0\leq s_{\theta,B}\leq z_{\theta,B}\) with \(\sum_{\theta,B}s_{\theta,B}>0\) and real transfers \(y_{\theta,B,B'}\geq0\), indexed by \(B,B'\in\mathcal B\), satisfying \(\sum_{B'}y_{\theta,B,B'}=s_{\theta,B}\), \(y_{\theta,B,B'}>0\Rightarrow v_\theta(B')>v_\theta(B)\), and \(\sum_{\theta,B,B':o\in B'}y_{\theta,B,B'}\leq\sum_{\theta,B}s_{\theta,B}e_{\theta,o}\) for every \(o\). Thus every participating member strictly improves using the coalition's original endowments.
Represent an exact output by listing its nonzero \(z_{\theta,B}\) as binary rationals and their bundles; all omitted entries are zero. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Is computing a weak-core mass allocation, or determining that none exists, solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

1. Definitions and the normalized blocking criterion

Write
$$ A_\theta=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}G_\theta^k, \qquad n_\theta=\bigl|\{k:G_\theta^k\neq\emptyset\}\bigr|. $$

An acceptable bundle for type $\theta$ is an allowable bundle contained in $A_\theta$. Its utility equals its cardinality, and the maximum attainable utility is $n_\theta$.

For a feasible allocation $z$, define
$$ a_\theta=\min\{v_\theta(B):z_{\theta,B}>0\}. $$
This minimum exists because the bundle family is finite and $\mu_\theta>0$. In particular,
$$ 0\leq a_\theta\leq n_\theta. $$

For an integer vector $a$, define
$$ \mathcal C_\theta(a) = \{C\in\mathcal B:C\subseteq A_\theta,\ |C|=a_\theta+1\}. $$

Lemma 1. A feasible allocation with minimum supported utilities $a$ is blocked if and only if there exist numbers $h_{\theta,C}\geq0$, indexed by $C\in\mathcal C_\theta(a)$, such that
$$ \sum_{\theta,C}h_{\theta,C}=1 $$
and
$$ \sum_{\theta,C:o\in C}h_{\theta,C} \leq \sum_{\theta,C}h_{\theta,C}e_{\theta,o} \qquad(o\in O). $$

Proof. Suppose a block exists. Every participating type-$\theta$ member starts with utility at least $a_\theta$. Because utilities are integers, every target bundle has utility at least $a_\theta+1$. Delete its unacceptable goods and retain exactly $a_\theta+1$ acceptable goods. This preserves allowability and decreases consumption.

Aggregate the resulting transfers by type and trimmed target bundle. Dividing by the positive total participating mass gives the required $h$.

Conversely, suppose such an $h$ exists. For each type, choose a bundle $B_\theta$ attaining its minimum supported utility, with
$$ z_{\theta,B_\theta}>0. $$
Set
$$ t_\theta=\sum_C h_{\theta,C}. $$
Choose $c>0$ small enough that
$$ ct_\theta\leq z_{\theta,B_\theta} $$
for every type with $t_\theta>0$. This is possible because there are finitely many such types.

Select mass $ct_\theta$ from $B_\theta$ and transfer mass $ch_{\theta,C}$ to $C$. Every selected member improves from utility $a_\theta$ to $a_\theta+1$, and the resource inequalities follow by multiplying those for $h$ by $c$. Thus this is a block. $\square$

Consequently, stability depends only on the minimum supported utilities, not on the magnitudes of the positive allocation entries.

2. Polynomial linear programs for thresholds and blocking

The following decomposition establishes the required equivalence between bundle distributions and good marginals.

Lemma 2. Fix a type $\theta$ and a nonnegative integer $r$. Suppose $t>0$ and nonnegative numbers $h_o$, for $o\in A_\theta$, satisfy
$$ \sum_{o\in A_\theta}h_o=rt, \qquad \sum_{o\in G_\theta^k}h_o\leq t \qquad(k=1,\ldots,K). $$
Then $h$ is the good-marginal vector of a distribution of total mass $t$ over acceptable bundles of cardinality $r$.

For rational $t,h$, such a distribution can be constructed in polynomial time, with at most $m+K+1$ positive entries and polynomially bounded rational encoding length.

Proof. Maintain residual mass $t$ and residual marginals $h$. Write
$$ H_k=\sum_{o\in G_\theta^k}h_o. $$
Call a category tight if $H_k=t$.

There are at most $r$ tight categories: each contributes $t$ to the total $rt$. There are at least $r$ categories with positive residual marginals: each contributes at most $t$.

Choose exactly $r$ positive categories, including every tight category, and choose one good with positive residual mass from each chosen category. Let $v$ be this bundle’s incidence vector, and let $v_k$ indicate whether category $k$ was chosen.

Define
$$ \delta = \min\left( \{t\} \cup \{h_o:v_o=1\} \cup \{t-H_k:v_k=0\} \right). $$
Every term is positive. Selected goods have positive mass, and every unselected category is slack.

Output mass $\delta$ on this bundle and replace
$$ h_o\leftarrow h_o-\delta v_o, \qquad t\leftarrow t-\delta. $$
Nonnegativity remains valid. For a selected category,
$$ H_k-\delta\leq t-\delta. $$
For an unselected category, the definition of $\delta$ gives
$$ H_k\leq t-\delta. $$
The cardinality equation remains valid because
$$ \sum_o(h_o-\delta v_o)=rt-r\delta=r(t-\delta). $$

Previously zero coordinates remain zero, and previously tight categories remain tight. If $\delta<t$, at least one previously positive coordinate becomes zero or one previously slack category becomes tight. Hence there are at most $m+K$ iterations with positive residual mass afterward, followed by at most one final iteration.

If the residual mass becomes zero, the cardinality equation and nonnegativity force all residual marginals to be zero. Thus the output has exactly the prescribed total mass and marginals.

For rational inputs, all operations are comparisons, minima, and subtraction. A common denominator for the initial entries remains a common denominator throughout. Residual entries stay bounded by their initial total mass, so their bit lengths remain polynomial. The choices and the number of iterations are polynomial. The case $r=0$ outputs the empty bundle with mass $t$. $\square$

For $a\in\prod_\theta\{0,\ldots,n_\theta\}$, let $F(a)$ be the following feasibility LP, with variables $w_{\theta,o}\geq0$ for acceptable goods:
$$ \sum_{o\in A_\theta}w_{\theta,o}=\mu_\theta a_\theta \qquad(\theta\in\Theta), $$
$$ \sum_{o\in G_\theta^k}w_{\theta,o}\leq\mu_\theta \qquad(\theta\in\Theta,\ k=1,\ldots,K), $$
$$ \sum_{\theta:o\in A_\theta}w_{\theta,o}\leq q_o \qquad(o\in O). $$

Proposition 3. The LP $F(a)$ is feasible if and only if there is a feasible allocation giving every type-$\theta$ member utility exactly $a_\theta$. Such an allocation can be constructed from a rational feasible solution in polynomial time.

Proof. Given the allocation, delete unacceptable goods and take its good marginals. The resulting variables satisfy $F(a)$.

Conversely, apply Lemma 2 to each type with total mass $\mu_\theta$ and cardinality $a_\theta$. Its bundle distribution has the specified marginals. The supply inequalities then establish feasibility. Rational linear programming produces a feasible solution of polynomial bit length, and Lemma 2 supplies the required exact output. $\square$

The feasible threshold vectors are downward closed. Indeed, if $b_\theta\leq a_\theta$ for every type and $F(a)$ is feasible, multiply type $\theta$’s marginals by $b_\theta/a_\theta$ when $a_\theta>0$. When $a_\theta=0$, its marginals are already zero. This gives a feasible solution of $F(b)$.

For blocking, put $r_\theta=a_\theta+1$. Let $H(a)$ have nonnegative variables $t_\theta$ and $f_{\theta,o}$, with constraints
$$ \sum_\theta t_\theta=1, $$
$$ \sum_{o\in A_\theta}f_{\theta,o}=r_\theta t_\theta \qquad(\theta\in\Theta), $$
$$ \sum_{o\in G_\theta^k}f_{\theta,o}\leq t_\theta \qquad(\theta\in\Theta,\ k=1,\ldots,K), $$
$$ \sum_{\theta:o\in A_\theta}f_{\theta,o} \leq \sum_\theta t_\theta e_{\theta,o} \qquad(o\in O). $$

Proposition 4. An allocation with minimum supported utilities $a$ is in the weak core if and only if $H(a)$ is infeasible.

Proof. A normalized block from Lemma 1 gives a feasible solution by taking
$$ t_\theta=\sum_C h_{\theta,C}, \qquad f_{\theta,o}=\sum_{C:o\in C}h_{\theta,C}. $$

Conversely, apply Lemma 2 to every type with $t_\theta>0$. This decomposes its marginals into acceptable bundles of cardinality $a_\theta+1$, giving the numbers required by Lemma 1. If $t_\theta=0$, the cardinality equation and nonnegativity force all its marginals to be zero. $\square$

Both LPs have polynomially many variables and constraints, with polynomial coefficient bit lengths. Thus weak-core membership is decidable in polynomial time in the input and listed output lengths, including against real-valued blocks.

3. An exact price characterization

For $p\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{O}$, write
$$ p(C)=\sum_{o\in C}p_o, \qquad p(e_\theta)=\sum_o p_o e_{\theta,o}. $$

Theorem 5. A feasible allocation with minimum supported utilities $a$ is in the weak core if and only if there exists a nonnegative price vector $p$, normalized by
$$ \sum_o p_o=1, $$
such that
$$ p(C)>p(e_\theta) \qquad \bigl(\theta\in\Theta,\ C\in\mathcal C_\theta(a)\bigr). $$
Whenever such prices exist, rational prices with polynomially bounded bit length exist.

Proof. For each improvement bundle, define its net-resource vector
$$ d_{\theta,C}=\mathbf1_C-e_\theta. $$
Let $\mathcal D$ be the finite family of these vectors.

If the asserted prices exist, every vector in $\mathcal D$ has positive scalar product with $p$. Every convex combination therefore also has positive scalar product. But a normalized block would give a convex combination that is componentwise nonpositive. Its scalar product with $p\geq0$ would be nonpositive, a contradiction.

For the converse, assume there is no block. If $\mathcal D$ is empty, every normalized nonnegative price vector works. Otherwise, let
$$ T=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal D), \qquad Q=\mathbb R_{\leq0}^{O}. $$
Lemma 1 says exactly that $T\cap Q=\emptyset$.

Because $T$ is compact, choose $d^*\in T$ minimizing
$$ \sum_o\max\{d_o,0\}^2. $$
Set
$$ u_o^*=\min\{d_o^*,0\}, \qquad p_o=d_o^*-u_o^*=\max\{d_o^*,0\}. $$
The pair $(d^*,u^*)$ minimizes the squared distance between $T$ and $Q$. Since the sets are disjoint, $p\neq0$.

For any $d\in T$ and $0\leq\varepsilon\leq1$, the point
$$ d^*+\varepsilon(d-d^*) $$
belongs to $T$. Minimality therefore implies
$$ \left\|d^*+\varepsilon(d-d^*)-u^*\right\|^2 \geq \|d^*-u^*\|^2. $$
Taking the right derivative at $\varepsilon=0$ gives
$$ p\cdot(d-d^*)\geq0. $$
Furthermore,
$$ p\cdot d^*=\|p\|^2>0. $$
Consequently,
$$ p\cdot d\geq\|p\|^2>0 \qquad(d\in T). $$
Normalize $p$ by its positive coordinate sum. This proves the price inequalities.

For the encoding claim, consider the finite LP
$$ p_o\geq0,\qquad \sum_o p_o=1,\qquad 0\leq\delta\leq1, $$
$$ p\cdot d_{\theta,C}\geq\delta \qquad(\theta,C). $$
The preceding argument supplies a feasible point with $\delta>0$. The feasible region is compact, so maximizing $\delta$ has a vertex optimum with positive value.

There are $m+1$ variables. All coefficients are integers whose bit lengths are bounded by the input length, even though the number of rows can be exponential. A vertex is determined by $m+1$ independent tight rows. Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s determinant bound therefore give polynomial bit lengths for its coordinates. $\square$

Define the largest affordable acceptable cardinality at prices $p$ by
$$ b_\theta(p) = \max\{|C|:C\subseteq A_\theta,\ C\in\mathcal B,\ p(C)\leq p(e_\theta)\}. $$
The empty bundle guarantees that this maximum exists.

Corollary 6. A weak-core allocation exists if and only if there is a normalized nonnegative price vector $p$ such that $F(b(p))$ is feasible.

More precisely, every feasible allocation giving type $\theta$ utility exactly $b_\theta(p)$ is in the weak core.

Proof. By definition of $b_\theta(p)$, every acceptable bundle of cardinality $b_\theta(p)+1$ costs strictly more than $p(e_\theta)$. Theorem 5 proves the second assertion.

Conversely, take a weak-core allocation with minima $a$. Trimming each bundle to exactly $a_\theta$ acceptable goods proves that $F(a)$ is feasible. Choose prices from Theorem 5.

If $b_\theta(p)>a_\theta$, an affordable acceptable bundle has an acceptable subbundle of size $a_\theta+1$. Nonnegative prices make this subbundle affordable, contradicting Theorem 5. Thus
$$ b(p)\leq a $$
componentwise. Downward closure of $F$ gives feasibility of $F(b(p))$. $\square$

For any specified rational $p$, the vector $b(p)$ is computable in polynomial time. For each nonempty acceptable category, take its cheapest acceptable good, sort these category prices, and find the longest prefix whose sum is at most $p(e_\theta)$. A cheapest acceptable bundle of cardinality $r$ uses precisely the $r$ cheapest categories.

4. Existence and short exact outputs

Here is the finite theorem used, with its hypotheses stated explicitly:

A finite economy with distinct objects, pairwise disjoint nonempty endowments, categorical consumption, and additively separable dichotomous preferences has a nonempty weak core.

This is [Echenique–Goel–Lee, Theorem 1](https://goelsumit.com/files/exchange_stable.pdf). Their categorical model permits unrestricted endowments and discarding, and their weak core excludes coalitions in which every member strictly improves.

Theorem 7. Every valid instance in the question has a weak-core mass allocation.

Proof. Choose an integer $D>0$ clearing all mass denominators. For each positive integer $n$, put
$$ N_n=nD. $$
Construct a finite economy with $N_n\mu_\theta$ agents of type $\theta$. Each owns $e_{\theta,o}$ distinct labeled copies of good $o$. Copies retain their original categories and acceptability.

Each agent has a nonempty endowment. All endowed copies are distinct and partition the object set. Each original good has
$$ N_nq_o>0 $$
copies, an integer. Thus every category is nonempty, and the finite theorem applies.

Choose a finite weak-core allocation. Retain at most one acceptable object per category for each agent and discard the rest. Utilities are preserved, so the modified allocation remains in the weak core: a block of the modified allocation would also block the original one.

Project copies to original good labels and divide agent counts by $N_n$. This gives a feasible mass allocation $z^{(n)}$.

The mass-allocation feasible set is compact: it is a closed bounded subset of the finite-dimensional space with coordinates $(\theta,B)$. Hence a subsequence converges to a feasible allocation $z^*$.

Suppose $z^*$ is blocked. Let $a$ be its minimum supported utilities and choose, for each participating type, a bundle $B_\theta$ with
$$ z^*_{\theta,B_\theta}>0, \qquad v_\theta(B_\theta)=a_\theta. $$
By Lemma 1, its normalized blocking system is feasible. This is a finite rational polytope: its coefficients are integers and its nonnegative variables sum to one. It therefore has a rational feasible point.

Clearing denominators yields nonnegative integers $H_{\theta,C}$, not all zero, satisfying
$$ \sum_{\theta,C:o\in C}H_{\theta,C} \leq \sum_{\theta,C}H_{\theta,C}e_{\theta,o} \qquad(o\in O). $$
Put
$$ H_\theta=\sum_C H_{\theta,C}. $$

Along the convergent subsequence, $z^{(n)}_{\theta,B_\theta}$ is eventually bounded below by a positive constant. Since $N_n$ tends to infinity, eventually
$$ N_nz^{(n)}_{\theta,B_\theta}\geq H_\theta $$
for every participating type.

Select $H_\theta$ actual agents currently receiving projected bundle $B_\theta$. Give $H_{\theta,C}$ of them target bundle $C$. The integer resource inequalities ensure that enough distinct copies of each good occur in their original endowments to realize all targets.

Every selected agent improves from utility $a_\theta$ to $a_\theta+1$. This contradicts the finite allocation’s weak-core membership. Therefore $z^*$ is in the mass weak core. $\square$

Thus the “none exists” outcome never occurs on valid inputs. The remaining task is to compute an allocation.

Proposition 8. Every valid instance has a rational weak-core allocation with at most $\tau+m$ positive entries and polynomially bounded encoding length.

Proof. Take the allocation from Theorem 7 and its minima $a$. Trim every allocated bundle of type $\theta$ to exactly $a_\theta$ acceptable goods. This preserves feasibility and, by Lemma 1, stability.

Restrict the allocation LP to these acceptable cardinality-$a_\theta$ bundles. Add one supply slack for each good. The resulting nonempty bounded polytope has $\tau+m$ equality rows and a coefficient matrix with entries in $\{0,1\}$. A vertex therefore has at most $\tau+m$ positive allocation variables.

A common denominator for the masses also clears all supplies. Its bit length is polynomial in $L$, as are the cleared right-hand-side entries. Basic submatrices have order at most $\tau+m$. Hadamard’s inequality bounds their determinants, and Cramer’s rule gives polynomial numerator and denominator bit lengths.

Every positive bundle still gives its type utility exactly $a_\theta$, so the resulting allocation remains in the weak core. $\square$

5. Polynomial computation for fixed number of goods

Theorem 9. For every fixed $m$, a weak-core mass allocation can be computed in time polynomial in $\tau,K,L$.

Proof. Enumerate, for each type, all its acceptable allowable bundles. There are at most $2^m$ per type. For each pair $(\theta,C)$, define
$$ \ell_{\theta,C}(p)=p(C)-p(e_\theta). $$
Let $N$ be the number of these forms, so
$$ N\leq\tau2^m. $$

Their signs determine which acceptable bundles are affordable, and hence determine $b(p)$. It suffices to enumerate all realizable sign patterns on the price simplex
$$ \Delta=\left\{p\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{O}:\sum_o p_o=1\right\}. $$

Enumerating sign patterns exactly

Process the forms successively. For each retained sign pattern, try extending it with a negative, zero, or positive sign for the next form.

A proposed pattern $\sigma$ is realizable on $\Delta$ precisely when the following rational LP has optimum $\varepsilon>0$:
$$ p\in\Delta,\qquad 0\leq\varepsilon\leq1, $$
$$ \ell_j(p)\geq\varepsilon \qquad\text{if }\sigma_j=1, $$
$$ \ell_j(p)\leq-\varepsilon \qquad\text{if }\sigma_j=-1, $$
$$ \ell_j(p)=0 \qquad\text{if }\sigma_j=0. $$

Indeed, a point realizing finitely many strict inequalities has a positive minimum strict margin. Conversely, positive $\varepsilon$ realizes the proposed signs. This also handles identically zero forms and equality cases.

Bounding the number of patterns

Let
$$ d=m-1. $$
The price simplex lies in the affine space
$$ \sum_o p_o=1, $$
of dimension $d$. Consider the arrangement of the $N$ affine hyperplanes in that entire space, omitting forms whose zero sets are empty or the entire space.

The number of full-dimensional regions formed by $N$ hyperplanes in dimension $r$ is at most
$$ R_r(N)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom Nj. $$
To verify this bound, add hyperplanes successively. A new hyperplane splits at most as many existing regions as the induced arrangement has regions on that hyperplane. This gives the recurrence
$$ R_r(N)\leq R_r(N-1)+R_{r-1}(N-1), $$
with the usual initial bounds $R_r(0)=1$ and $R_0(N)=1$, proving the displayed estimate by induction.

A nonempty sign cell of dimension $r$ has an affine hull determined by $d-r$ independent zero equations from the arrangement. There are at most
$$ \binom N{d-r} $$
choices of such equations. Inside each resulting affine space, the possible cells are full-dimensional regions of the restricted arrangement, numbering at most $R_r(N)$.

Therefore the total number of realizable sign patterns is at most
$$ \sum_{r=0}^{d}\binom N{d-r} \sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom Nj \leq (d+1)^2(N+1)^d. $$
Intersecting with $\Delta$ cannot increase the number of sign patterns.

Thus the incremental enumeration performs polynomially many LP calls when $m$ is fixed. Each call has polynomial encoding length and returns a rational representative price vector of polynomial bit length.

Constructing the allocation

For each final sign pattern, take its representative $p$, compute $b(p)$, and test $F(b(p))$. Whenever this LP is feasible, Proposition 3 constructs an exact allocation, and Corollary 6 proves its weak-core membership.

Theorem 7 and Corollary 6 guarantee that some sign pattern passes the test. A bound for this algorithm is
$$ \bigl(\tau2^m+1\bigr)^{O(m)} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,L). $$
For fixed $m$, this is polynomial. $\square$

This adds a polynomial case complementary to fixed support size. For completeness, fixed $\tau$ is also polynomial: enumerate the at most
$$ (K+1)^\tau $$
threshold vectors, test $F(a)$ and $H(a)$, and construct an allocation when $F(a)$ is feasible and $H(a)$ is infeasible. Existence and trimming guarantee success.

6. Polynomial computation with one-object endowments

Theorem 10. Suppose
$$ \sum_o e_{\theta,o}=1 \qquad(\theta\in\Theta). $$
Then a weak-core allocation can be computed in polynomial time, with unrestricted $m,\tau,K$.

Proof. Construct a directed graph on types. Include an edge
$$ \theta\longrightarrow\phi $$
if some good acceptable to $\theta$ is endowed to $\phi$. Self-loops are allowed.

Initially set the unserved mass to
$$ r_\theta=\mu_\theta. $$
While the subgraph induced by types with $r_\theta>0$ has a directed cycle, choose a simple cycle
$$ \theta_1\longrightarrow\theta_2 \longrightarrow\cdots \longrightarrow\theta_\ell \longrightarrow\theta_1. $$
For each edge, choose a witnessing good $o_i$, acceptable to $\theta_i$ and endowed to $\theta_{i+1}$, with cyclic indexing. Put
$$ \varepsilon=\min_i r_{\theta_i}. $$
Allocate mass $\varepsilon$ of type $\theta_i$ the singleton $\{o_i\}$, and subtract $\varepsilon$ from every cycle type’s unserved mass. At termination, give all remaining mass the empty bundle.

Feasibility. Within a cycle, each demand is charged to one unit of the next type’s endowment. Each cycle type supplies exactly one demand. Thus cycle consumption is bounded coordinatewise by
$$ \varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}e_{\theta_i,\cdot}. $$
Across iterations, total mass charged to type $\theta$ is at most $\mu_\theta$. Total consumption is therefore bounded by $q$.

Complexity. Every iteration exhausts at least one type’s unserved mass, so there are at most $\tau$ iterations. Cycle detection is polynomial. Rational minima and subtraction preserve a common denominator for the input masses, giving polynomial bit lengths and output size.

Stability. Every served member receives utility one, and every unserved member receives utility zero.

Suppose a block exists. Let $S_0$ and $S_1$ be its participating masses currently receiving utilities zero and one, respectively. Every target for the first group contains at least one good; every target for the second group contains at least two goods. Therefore total target consumption, summed over all goods, is at least
$$ S_0+2S_1. $$
Each participating member owns exactly one object, so the coalition’s total endowed quantity is
$$ S_0+S_1. $$
Summing the resource inequalities gives
$$ S_0+2S_1\leq S_0+S_1. $$
Hence $S_1=0$: only unserved members can participate.

Let $T$ be the types with positive participating mass. Each type in $T$ must receive a positive quantity of an acceptable good. The resource inequality for that good implies that some type in $T$ endows it. Thus every vertex of the graph induced by $T$ has an outgoing edge within $T$.

A finite directed graph with this property contains a directed cycle: follow outgoing edges until a vertex repeats. But $T$ is contained in the terminal unserved set, whose induced graph has no directed cycle. This contradiction proves stability. $\square$

The same cycle algorithm works for $K=1$ with arbitrary endowments. Its feasibility proof uses only that each witnessing endowment coordinate is at least one. For stability, served members then have maximum attainable utility and cannot participate; the remaining directed-cycle argument applies unchanged.

7. Totality and the meaning of NP-hardness

Propositions 4 and 8, together with Theorem 7, give a polynomially bounded total search formulation.

To make totality formal on every input string, define the relation as follows:

  • For an invalid economy encoding, the unique accepted output is a distinguished invalid-input marker.
  • For a valid encoding, accepted outputs are weak-core allocations satisfying the polynomial support and bit-length bound of Proposition 8.

Validity is checkable in polynomial time. The explicitly listed sets and indices can be checked directly; rational masses, their sum, nonempty endowments, and positive supplies can be checked by exact arithmetic of polynomial bit length.

On valid inputs, output verification is polynomial by Proposition 4. Proposition 8 supplies a fixed polynomial witness-length bound, and Theorem 7 establishes totality. Thus this relation belongs to $\mathrm{TFNP}$, the class of polynomially bounded, polynomially verifiable total search relations.

There is a corresponding conditional restriction on one common meaning of NP-hardness. Suppose a polynomial-time reduction maps each SAT instance $\varphi$ to a valid economy $I_\varphi$, and a polynomial-time decoder correctly determines satisfiability from every valid weak-core output for $I_\varphi$.

Then an unsatisfiable formula has a polynomial certificate: a short weak-core output whose decoder returns “unsatisfiable.” Core membership and the decoder’s result are polynomially checkable. A satisfiable formula cannot have such a certificate, by decoder correctness. Consequently,
$$ \mathrm{UNSAT}\in\mathrm{NP}, $$
which implies
$$ \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$

This is a conditional statement under the specified reduction convention. It does not settle the unrestricted search problem.

8. What remains unresolved

The general search is equivalently the task of finding prices $p$ for which
$$ F(b(p)) $$
is feasible. For specified prices, this condition is decidable in polynomial time. The unresolved step is finding suitable prices when both the number of goods and the support size vary.

The arrangement enumeration can genuinely encounter exponentially many affordable-threshold patterns. For example, take $n$ categories
$$ O^i=\{x_i,y_i\}, $$
and two types per category, each of mass $1/(2n)$. One owns $x_i$ and accepts only $y_i$; the other owns $y_i$ and accepts only $x_i$. Each independent comparison between $p_{x_i}$ and $p_{y_i}$ gives two different threshold pairs. Choosing these comparisons independently gives $2^n$ distinct threshold vectors.

This only limits the enumeration method. These particular instances are polynomial-time solvable by Theorem 10.

The proved results therefore provide an exact price certificate, polynomial computation for fixed $m$, and polynomial computation for arbitrary categories with one-object endowments. They establish neither an unrestricted polynomial-time algorithm nor an NP-hardness reduction.
