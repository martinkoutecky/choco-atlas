The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given seller and buyer type sets \(I,J\), rational type masses \(\bar\mu_i,\bar\nu_j\), and nonnegative rational surpluses \(a_{ij}\), define \(W(x,y)=\max\{\sum_{ij}a_{ij}z_{ij}: z_{ij}\geq0,\ \sum_j z_{ij}\leq x_i,\ \sum_i z_{ij}\leq y_j\}\) for every coalition mass vector \((x,y)\in[0,\bar\mu]\times[0,\bar\nu]\). Do there exist per-capita payoff maps for all nonzero coalition mass vectors that satisfy exact efficiency, \(\sum_i x_i p_i(x,y)+\sum_j y_j r_j(x,y)=W(x,y)\), and payoff monotonicity whenever the coalition masses increase?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: disjoint finite seller and buyer type sets \(I,J\), with \(I\cup J\neq\varnothing\), and positive masses \(\bar\mu_i,\bar\nu_j\) encoded as binary rationals. Masses need not sum to one. Let \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) be the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m=|I||J|\) and \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The input also gives nonnegative rational surpluses \(a_{ij}\). Coalition masses range over all real vectors \(q=(x,y)\in D:=([0,\bar\mu]\times[0,\bar\nu])\setminus\{0\}\), including coalitions confined to one market side.
Define \(W(x,y)=\max\{\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}a_{ij}z_{ij}:z_{ij}\geq0,\ \sum_jz_{ij}\leq x_i,\ \sum_iz_{ij}\leq y_j\}\).
A scheme assigns real per-capita payoffs \(p_i(q)\) when \(x_i>0\) and \(r_j(q)\) when \(y_j>0\), uniformly within each type, with no continuity or measurability requirement.
Efficiency requires \(\sum_{i:x_i>0}x_ip_i(q)+\sum_{j:y_j>0}y_jr_j(q)=W(q)\) for every \(q\in D\).
Monotonicity requires, whenever \(q=(x,y)\leq q'=(x',y')\) coordinatewise, \(p_i(q)\leq p_i(q')\) for every \(x_i>0\), and \(r_j(q)\leq r_j(q')\) for every \(y_j>0\); this includes increases in the recipient's own type mass.
Is deciding whether such an efficient, monotonic scheme exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite assignment games, existence of a population monotonic allocation scheme is recognizable in polynomial time through a structural characterization of the surplus matrix. [Solymosi](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00355-023-01477-z)
No result settling this exact continuous decision problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Masses are strictly positive on the listed support and are not normalized, preserving the interpretation as quantities of agents.
  • The seller and buyer sets are disjoint and their union is nonempty; either side may be empty, as allowed by the discrete assignment-game definition.
  • Coalition coordinates range over real intervals, including coalitions containing only sellers or only buyers.
  • Payoffs are real and defined only for types present with positive coalition mass; absent types contribute no term to efficiency.
  • Payoff monotonicity means weak coordinatewise increase for every type already present, including when that type's own mass increases.
  • No continuity or measurability condition is imposed on payoff maps, since the discrete scheme definition imposes no analogous restriction.
  • The size parameter m counts surplus-matrix entries, and L is the total binary input length.

Complete characterization

An efficient, monotonic scheme exists if and only if \(a_{ij}=0\) for every \((i,j)\in I\times J\). Consequently, the decision problem is solvable in polynomial time.

If one market side is empty, the condition is vacuous and \(W\) is identically zero. The substantive case is a society containing a positive-surplus pair. We prove that even one such pair prevents existence, using the explicitly required monotonicity under increases in the recipient’s own type mass.

1. Every payoff must be nonnegative

Suppose an efficient, monotonic scheme exists. Fix a coalition \(q=(x,y)\in D\) and a seller type \(i\) with \(x_i>0\).

Let \(q^{i}\) be the coalition containing only mass \(x_i\) of seller type \(i\). Since this coalition has no buyers, \(W(q^{i})=0\). Efficiency gives \(x_i p_i(q^{i})=0\), hence \(p_i(q^{i})=0\).

Because \(q^{i}\leq q\), monotonicity implies \(p_i(q)\geq0\). The same argument with a coalition containing only buyer type \(j\) proves \(r_j(q)\geq0\) whenever \(y_j>0\).

Thus every payoff appearing in the scheme is nonnegative.

2. A positive-surplus pair gives a contradiction

Suppose \(a_{ij}=a>0\) for some seller \(i\) and buyer \(j\). Write \(M=\bar\mu_i>0\) and \(N=\bar\nu_j>0\), and choose

\(t=\dfrac{MN}{2(M+N)}\).

Then \(0<t<M\) and \(0<t<N\).

For positive \(s\leq M\) and \(u\leq N\), let \(q(s,u)\) denote the coalition containing only seller mass \(s\) of type \(i\) and buyer mass \(u\) of type \(j\). All other coordinates are zero. The capacity constraints force every matching variable except \(z_{ij}\) to vanish, so

\(W(q(s,u))=a\min\{s,u\}\).

Consider the three coalitions \(q(t,t)\), \(q(M,t)\), and \(q(t,N)\). Efficiency at the balanced coalition gives

\(p_i(q(t,t))+r_j(q(t,t))=a.\)

At \(q(M,t)\), efficiency and nonnegativity give

\(M p_i(q(M,t))+t r_j(q(M,t))=at,\)

and therefore \(p_i(q(M,t))\leq at/M\). Since \(q(t,t)\leq q(M,t)\), monotonicity yields

\(p_i(q(t,t))\leq at/M.\)

Similarly, efficiency and nonnegativity at \(q(t,N)\) give \(r_j(q(t,N))\leq at/N\). Since \(q(t,t)\leq q(t,N)\), monotonicity yields

\(r_j(q(t,t))\leq at/N.\)

Combining these inequalities with efficiency at \(q(t,t)\), we obtain

\(a\leq at\left(\dfrac1M+\dfrac1N\right)=\dfrac a2,\)

a contradiction because \(a>0\).

Hence existence of a scheme forces every surplus entry to be zero. This argument uses only three coalitions and requires no continuity or measurability.

3. Sufficiency and computational complexity

Conversely, suppose every \(a_{ij}=0\). Then \(W(q)=0\) for every \(q\in D\). Assign payoff zero to every present type at every coalition. This scheme is efficient and monotonic.

The decision algorithm therefore scans the surplus matrix and accepts exactly when every entry is zero. Exact zero testing of binary rationals, together with reading the input, takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\); a direct scan takes \(O(L+m+\tau)\) bit operations under a standard explicit encoding.

The obstruction arises from monotonicity under increases in a recipient’s own type mass. The cited finite assignment-game characterization concerns fixed individual players and does not remove this additional requirement in the continuous problem. [Solymosi, *Assignment games with population monotonic allocation schemes*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00355-023-01477-z).
