The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\geq 0\) summing to \(1\), a maximum team size \(K\), and strict rankings \(\succ_t\) over integral team compositions \(q\in\{0,\ldots,K\}^{T}\) with \(1\leq\sum_t q_t\leq K\) and \(q_t>0\), does there exist a core-stable allocation, and can one be returned? An allocation is a vector of team densities \(\lambda_q\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_q q_t\lambda_q=\mu_t\) for each \(t\). For each possible team \(q\), let \(b_t(q,\lambda)=\sum_{r:r_t>0,\ q\succ_t r} r_t\lambda_r\). It is stable exactly when no \(q\) has \(b_t(q,\lambda)>0\) for every type \(t\) with \(q_t>0\), so each formed team remains finite and integral while the population mass is continuous.

3. Universal existence

The theorem used

We use the following version of Scarf’s lemma. Let $A,C$ have $n$ rows and $n+p$ columns, with $n,p\geq1$. Assume:

  • $b\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{n}$, and $\{x\geq0:Ax=b\}$ is nonempty and bounded.

$$ C_{i,i}<C_{i,k}<C_{i,j}. $$

Then some $x\geq0$ satisfies $Ax=b$ and, for every column $k$, there is a row $i$ such that
$$ C_{i,k}\leq C_{i,j} \qquad\text{for every }j\in\operatorname{supp}(x). $$

This is the domination conclusion of [Biró–Fleiner, Theorem 5](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/economics/seminars/seminars/conferences/ctn/bf12dp.pdf). The nonnegativity assumption on $b$, omitted in the previous write-up, is included here.

$$ \sum_t y_t+ \sum_q\left(\sum_tq_t\right)\lambda_q=1. $$

Every composition has total size at least $1$. Thus every nonnegative feasible vector satisfies
$$ \sum_t y_t+\sum_q\lambda_q\leq1, $$
which proves boundedness. Also, $b=\mu$ is positive.

$$ C_{t,d_t}=0, \qquad C_{t,d_s}=M+1\quad(s\neq t), $$
and
$$ C_{t,q}= \begin{cases} u_t(q),&q_t>0,\\ M,&q_t=0. \end{cases} $$

Since $1\leq u_t(q)\leq m$, all actual-column entries lie strictly between the artificial diagonal and off-diagonal entries. Scarf’s lemma therefore applies.

Take a resulting feasible dominating vector $x=(y,\lambda)$.

A row dominating an actual composition must participate

$$ C_{t,q}=M. $$

Therefore row $t$ cannot dominate column $q$. Every row dominating an actual composition is a participating type.

$$ u_t(q)\leq u_t(r) \qquad \text{for every }r\in S(\lambda)\cap Q_t. $$

The support criterion proves that $\lambda$ is core-stable.

4. Exact rational outputs, verification, and an enumeration algorithm

Sparse stable allocations exist

Start with any stable allocation, and consider its positive composition columns in $G$.

If those columns are linearly dependent, choose a nonzero vector $h$, supported on the current positive support, with
$$ Gh=0. $$

Summing the rows gives
$$ \sum_q\left(\sum_tq_t\right)h_q=0. $$

All coefficients $\sum_tq_t$ are positive, so $h$ must have both positive and negative coordinates. Set
$$ \varepsilon= \min_{q:h_q>0}\frac{\lambda_q}{h_q}>0, \qquad \lambda'=\lambda-\varepsilon h. $$

Then $G\lambda'=G\lambda=\mu$. Coordinates with $h_q>0$ remain nonnegative; coordinates with $h_q<0$ increase. At least one positive coordinate becomes zero, and no previously zero coordinate becomes positive. Thus $\lambda'$ is feasible and remains stable by support containment.

Repeating this operation terminates with linearly independent positive columns. Their number $k$ satisfies
$$ 1\leq k\leq\tau. $$

Rational encoding bound

Select $k$ rows making the matrix $B$ of these positive columns square and nonsingular. Its entries are integers between $0$ and $K$.

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the input masses. Then
$$ \log_2D\leq L. $$

The selected right-hand side can be written as $c/D$, where $c$ is integral and $0<c_i\leq D$. Cramer’s rule gives
$$ \lambda_j=\frac{\det B^{(j)}}{D\det B}, $$
where $B^{(j)}$ replaces column $j$ of $B$ by $c$.

Expanding the determinants yields
$$ |\det B|\leq k!K^k, \qquad |\det B^{(j)}|\leq k!DK^{k-1}. $$

Therefore each positive density has a binary rational encoding of length
$$ O\!\left( L+\tau\log(\tau+1)+\tau\log(K+1) \right). $$

Thus an exact stable allocation always exists with at most $\tau$ positive coordinates and polynomial total encoding length.

Polynomial verification

For a proposed rational allocation, check nonnegativity and all mass equations by exact arithmetic.

Feasibility and $\mu_t>0$ ensure that every type has a supported incident composition. Compute
$$ h_t=\min_{r\in S(\lambda)\cap Q_t}u_t(r). $$

The support criterion becomes
$$ \forall q\in Q\;\exists t\text{ with }q_t>0: \quad h_t\geq u_t(q). $$

Feasibility and stability can consequently be checked using $O(m\tau)$ arithmetic operations. The bit complexity is polynomial in $m,\tau,K,L$ and the proposed output length.

A guaranteed construction algorithm

Enumerate every $S\subseteq Q$ with $|S|\leq\tau$. For each set:

  • Check the support criterion with $S$ in place of $S(\lambda)$.
  • If it passes, solve the rational linear feasibility problem

$$ \sum_{q\in S}q_t\lambda_q=\mu_t, \qquad \lambda_q\geq0. $$

A feasible solution has support contained in $S$, so it is stable. The sparse existence result guarantees that some enumerated set succeeds.

The running time is
$$ \left(\sum_{j=0}^{\tau}\binom mj\right) \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,L) \leq (m+1)^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,K,L). $$

This is polynomial time for every fixed $\tau$.

Two elementary cases also have direct solutions. If $K=1$, the unique allocation assigns density $\mu_t$ to each singleton. If $\tau=1$, stability forces all mass into the type’s most preferred composition; if that composition has size $a$, its density is $1/a$.

$$ \sum_{e\ni v}w_e\leq1 \qquad(v\in V), $$
and, for each edge $e$, a vertex $v\in e$ satisfying
$$ \sum_{\substack{f\ni v\\f\succeq_v e}}w_f=1. $$

The required source hardness follows from [Kintali et al., Sections 3.4–3.6 and Theorem 5.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.1435): their hard preference games can have incoming degree at most $2$, and their hypergraph reduction uses edges
$$ \{i^*\} \quad\text{and}\quad \{i,i^*\}\cup J, \qquad J\subseteq\operatorname{in}(i). $$

Thus every produced edge has at most $4$ vertices. There are at most $5n$ edges for $n$ game players, since each incoming set has at most four subsets. This proves $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hardness of fractional stable matching on simple hypergraphs of edge size at most $4$, with strict orders.

The incoming-degree bound, rather than merely the total-degree bound of $3$, supplies the improvement over the notes.

Decoding any stable allocation

Extras have zero density. A newly added singleton is below every original incident edge, so it also has zero density at this witness. All of the witness’s mass must therefore lie on original edges ranked at least as highly as $e$:
$$ \sum_{\substack{f\ni v\\f\succeq_v e}}\lambda_{q^f} =\mu_v=\frac1N. $$

Multiplying by $N$ gives the source stability condition. Hence every returned stable allocation decodes into a fractional stable matching.

This is nonnegative. If $\{v\}\in E$, stability of that source singleton forces total incident weight to equal $1$, so no residual mass is needed. Set every extra density to zero.

The mass equations now hold. A source stability witness has weight exactly $1$ on edges ranked at least as highly as the tested edge. Capacity feasibility consequently forces zero weight on worse edges and zero residual singleton mass there. Thus every original composition is nonblocking.

New singletons have no supported composition below them. Extras are below every supported composition at each participating type. They are therefore nonblocking as well.
