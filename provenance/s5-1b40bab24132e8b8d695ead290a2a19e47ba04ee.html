The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite goods \(G\), a finite set \(T\) of complete normalized monotone submodular valuation types, rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_g\), \(c>0\), and one private valuation input per positive-mass type, decide whether integral bundle templates \(B_t\subseteq G\) exist with \(\sum_{t:g\in B_t}\mu_t\le\sigma_g\) for every \(g\) and \(v_t(B_t)\ge c\,v_t(B_u)\) for all positive-mass \(t,u\); output the templates or certify infeasibility, with worst-case randomized communication measured between type representatives.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge3\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible valuations. There are \(m\ge1\) goods \(G\), public binary-rational supplies \(0<\sigma_g\le1\), and a fixed rational \(c\in(0,1]\).
Each type representative privately holds a complete monotone submodular valuation \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\) and \(v_t(G)>0\). Each value has at most \(b\) bits, where \(b\) is polynomial in \(m\). Type identifiers and masses are public; valuation tables are private.
An allocation assigns one integral template \(B_t\subseteq G\) to every member of type \(t\), exhausting supplies: \(\sum_{t:g\in B_t}\mu_t=\sigma_g\) for every \(g\). It is \(c\)-envy-free when \(v_t(B_t)\ge c\,v_t(B_u)\) for all \(t,u\in T\).
Representatives communicate on a public blackboard using public randomness and unrestricted local computation. They must output such templates or declare infeasibility, correctly with probability at least \(2/3\) on every input. Communication cost is the worst-case number of transmitted bits, including the output. Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the public input plus \(b\); express resource bounds in \(m,\tau,L\), with dependence on \(c\) explicit.

Does this problem admit randomized communication polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) for every fixed \(c\in(0,1]\), or does it require communication exponential in \(m\), even for fixed \(\tau\ge3\) and polynomial \(L\)?

For discrete allocations that partition all goods, randomized communication is exponential for every fixed number of players greater than two and every fixed positive approximation factor, even with submodular valuations; with two players, randomized communication for envy-freeness is \(O(m)\). [Plaut–Roughgarden](https://bplaut.github.io/CC.pdf)

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Conclusion

The problem requires randomized communication exponential in \(m\), even for every fixed \(\tau\ge3\) and every fixed rational \(c\in(0,1]\), with polynomial \(L\). This holds even when all masses and supplies are dyadic rationals.

More precisely, for \(m=2k+\tau-2\), there are instances requiring
\(\Omega\!\left(\binom{2k}{k}\right)\) communicated bits, while each valuation entry needs only \(O(\log m+\ell(c))\) bits. Here \(\ell(c)\) denotes the binary encoding length of \(c\).

1. The discrete lower bound and its hard instances

We use the quantitative form of [Plaut–Roughgarden, Theorem 7.1 and its proof](https://bplaut.github.io/CC.pdf): for \(\tau>2\), deciding existence of a \(c\)-envy-free partition of \(2k+\tau-2\) goods requires randomized communication \(\Omega\!\left(\binom{2k}{k}\right)\).

Their hard instances have \(2k\) ordinary goods \(H\) and anchor goods \(a_3,\ldots,a_\tau\). Only valuations \(w_1,w_2\) are private. The valuations are normalized, monotone, and submodular, and satisfy:

  • For \(j\ge3\), \(w_j(S)=1\) if \(a_j\in S\), and \(w_j(S)=0\) otherwise.
  • For \(i\in\{1,2\}\), \(w_i(S)=k\) whenever \(a_3\in S\).
  • If \(S\subseteq H\) and \(|S|<k\), then \(w_i(S)=c|S|\).
  • Every value belongs to \(\{0,1,k,c(k-\tfrac12)\}\cup\{cr:0\le r\le k\}\).

The lower bound holds with public randomness and error at most \(1/3\), including when all anchor valuations are public.

These properties are the only features of the construction needed below.

2. Every fair discrete partition has a restricted form

Let \((A_1,\ldots,A_\tau)\) be a \(c\)-envy-free partition of one of these hard instances.

First, \(a_j\in A_j\) for every \(j\ge3\). Otherwise, \(w_j(A_j)=0\), whereas the player receiving \(a_j\) has a bundle valued at \(1\) by player \(j\). This contradicts \(c>0\).

Consequently, \(A_1,A_2\subseteq H\). Moreover, because \(a_3\in A_3\), envy-freeness gives, for \(i\in\{1,2\}\),
\(w_i(A_i)\ge c\,w_i(A_3)=ck\).

If \(|A_i|<k\), the construction instead gives \(w_i(A_i)=c|A_i|<ck\), a contradiction. Thus both private players receive at least \(k\) ordinary goods. Since there are exactly \(2k\) such goods and the allocation is a partition, necessarily
\(|A_1|=|A_2|=k\), \(A_1\cup A_2=H\), and \(A_j=\{a_j\}\) for every \(j\ge3\).

Therefore, restricting partitions to this form preserves existence of a \(c\)-envy-free partition.

3. Dyadic supplies enforce exactly that form

Fix \(\tau\ge3\). Define
\(r=\lceil\log_2(2(\tau-1))\rceil\) and \(d=2^{-r}\).

Choose the public masses
\(\mu_1=\mu_2=d\),
\(\mu_3=1-2d(\tau-2)\), and
\(\mu_j=2d\) for \(4\le j\le\tau\).

These masses sum to \(1\). They are positive dyadic rationals: indeed, \(2d(\tau-1)\le1\), so \(\mu_3\ge2d\). Thus every anchor type has mass at least \(2d\).

Set supplies to
\(\sigma_h=d\) for \(h\in H\), and
\(\sigma_{a_j}=\mu_j\) for \(j\ge3\).

Give each representative valuation \(v_j=w_j\).

Ordinary goods. For an ordinary good \(h\), exhaustion requires the masses of its recipients to sum to \(d\). An anchor type cannot receive it, because its individual mass is at least \(2d\). Types \(1\) and \(2\) each have mass \(d\), so exactly one receives \(h\).

Anchor goods. Positive supply implies that each anchor \(a_j\) has some recipient. If type \(j\) does not receive it, its own value is \(0\), while it values a recipient’s template at \(1\). This violates \(c\)-envy-freeness. Hence \(a_j\in B_j\).

Once type \(j\) receives \(a_j\), it alone consumes supply \(\sigma_{a_j}=\mu_j\). Positivity of all other masses prevents any additional recipient.

It follows that every feasible fair template allocation consists of a partition of \(H\) between types \(1,2\), together with \(B_j=\{a_j\}\) for every \(j\ge3\).

Conversely, every allocation of this form exhausts the prescribed supplies. Its envy inequalities are exactly the discrete envy inequalities. Section 2 therefore proves the equivalence:

A fair template allocation exists if and only if a fair discrete partition exists.

For exactly three types, the construction simply uses masses \(1/4,1/4,1/2\), ordinary-good supplies \(1/4\), and anchor supply \(1/2\).

4. Encoding and communication checks

Write \(c=p/q\) in lowest terms. The listed values have numerators and denominators of bit length \(O(\log k+\ell(c))\): for example,
\(c(k-\tfrac12)=p(2k-1)/(2q)\).
Thus the construction satisfies the polynomial precision requirement.

If “types” must have pairwise distinct valuations, replace \(v_2\) by \(2w_2\). Positive scaling preserves every envy inequality, normalization, monotonicity, and submodularity. For \(k\ge2\), total values of types \(1,2\) are then \(k,2k\), distinct from each other and from the anchor types’ total value \(1\). Different anchor types are distinguished by their respective anchors.

Masses and supplies need \(O(\log\tau)\) bits each. With indexed goods and types, the public input and precision bound satisfy
\(L=O((m+\tau)\log(m+\tau)+\ell(c))\).

Suppose a template protocol communicates at most \(C\) bits. Two parties holding the discrete instance’s private valuations can simulate it on its public blackboard. All anchor representatives have public valuations, so their messages are computable by both parties. The reduction requires no additional communication, and successful template output determines the discrete instance’s feasibility.

The discrete lower bound therefore yields
\(C\ge K\binom{2k}{k}\)
for an absolute positive constant \(K\). The communication lower bound has no loss depending on \(c\); its encoding dependence is through \(\ell(c)\).

Since \(\binom{2k}{k}=\Theta(4^k/\sqrt{k})\) and \(m=2k+\tau-2\), this is
\(\Omega(2^{m-\tau+2}/\sqrt{m-\tau+2})\).
For every fixed \(\tau\), it is exponential in \(m\). The displayed family already rules out polynomial communication; one additional universally valueless good handles the other parity of \(m\).

5. A general upper bound

There is a deterministic protocol communicating
\(O(\tau 2^m b+\tau m+1)\) bits.

Each representative transmits its complete valuation table. With unrestricted local computation, the representatives then enumerate all template tuples, check the public supply equalities and all envy inequalities exactly, and output a valid tuple or declare infeasibility. Encoding the output requires at most \(\tau m+1\) bits.

Since \(b\le L\), this gives an upper bound \(O(\tau 2^m L+\tau m+1)\), without an additional communication factor depending on \(c\).

Thus the proposed polynomial alternative is ruled out by a complete reduction, including for fixed three-type support.
