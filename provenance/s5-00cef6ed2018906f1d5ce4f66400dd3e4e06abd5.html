The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite shift-class set \(K\), finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), integer bundle sizes \(q_t\), endowments \(e_t\), trichotomous marginal sets \(A_t,B_t\), and finite priority blocks, find a rational mass plan \(x_{t,b}\) over integral bundles \(b\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}b_kx_{t,b}=\sum_t\mu_te_{t,k}\), and component-wise individual-rationality constraints, then run the type-block gradual-revelation procedure and compute a final plan guaranteed to be unambiguously individually rational and efficient for every consistent integral realization and responsive completion.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(K\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) shift classes. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Each type has a binary-encoded endowment \(e_t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\), bundle size \(q_t=\sum_k e_{t,k}\ge1\), and disjoint sets \(A_t,B_t\subseteq K\) with \(\operatorname{supp}(e_t)\subseteq A_t\cup B_t\). Copies within a class are interchangeable; marginal preferences have tied tiers \(A_t\), then \(B_t\), then \(K\setminus(A_t\cup B_t)\); tiers may be empty. An ordered partition \((P_1,\ldots,P_r)\) of the supported types specifies priority blocks. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A bundle is \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\) with \(\sum_k b_k=q_t\); write \(a_t(b)=\sum_{k\in A_t}b_k\). A rational mass plan satisfies \(x_{t,b}\ge0\), \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,b}b_kx_{t,b}=\sum_t\mu_te_{t,k}\). Component-wise individual rationality requires every positive-mass bundle to satisfy \(a_t(b)\ge a_t(e_t)\) and \(b_k=0\) outside \(A_t\cup B_t\).
A plan \(y\) weakly improves \(x\) if, for each type, there is a nonnegative coupling \(z_{t,b,c}\) with marginals \(x_{t,b}\) and \(y_{t,c}\), supported on pairs with \(a_t(c)\ge a_t(b)\). Define \(W_t(x)=\sum_ba_t(b)x_{t,b}\).
For a revealed-type set \(J\), let \(F_J^-(x)\) and \(F_J^+(x)\) contain the balanced plans weakly improving \(x\), with \(a_t(b)\ge a_t(e_t)\) throughout. Revealed types satisfy the full component-wise constraints. Unrevealed types instead satisfy \(b_k\le e_{t,k}\) for \(k\notin A_t\) in \(F_J^-(x)\), and have no additional restrictions in \(F_J^+(x)\).
A priority selection from a feasible set successively maximizes \(\sum_{t\in P_j}W_t\) for \(j=1,\ldots,r\), retaining all previous maxima; remaining ties may be resolved by selecting any rational plan.
Initialize \(x_{t,e_t}=\mu_t\) and \(J=\varnothing\). Select \(y\) by priority from \(F_J^-(x)\); set \(J'=\{t:\nexists v\in F_J^+(y),\ W_t(v)>W_t(y)\}\). Replace \((x,J)\) by \((y,J')\) and repeat until \(J=T\), then make one final priority selection from \(F_T^-(x)\).
For a rational final plan, an integral realization at any positive integer scale \(N\) clearing its masses and entries has \(N\mu_t\) agents endowed with \(e_t\), exactly \(Nx_{t,b}\) of whom receive bundle \(b\), and distinct object copies with the prescribed class totals. The required guarantee is individual rationality and Pareto efficiency of every such realization under every profile of responsive bundle preferences consistent with the tiers; alternative allocations may redistribute all copies while preserving each agent's bundle size.

Is computing a final rational mass plan through this type-block gradual-revelation procedure, with the stated universal welfare guarantee, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,r\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents and distinct indivisible objects, gradual revelation terminates and its final priority selections are unambiguously individually rational and efficient. [Manjunath and Westkamp](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06499v2) establish this discrete result; the source does not treat distributional societies or type-block priorities. No result settling the precise continuous problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized positive rational masses to sum to one; encoded all integer bundle sizes and endowment counts in binary.
  • Required nonempty endowments and excluded endowed objects from the bottom tier; allowed empty tiers.
  • Interpreted shift classes as interchangeable copies, permitting multiple copies of a class in an integral bundle.
  • Imposed component-wise individual rationality separately on every positive-mass bundle, rather than only on type averages.
  • Preserved the initial restriction to attractive objects and one's own endowment through coordinate caps on non-attractive class counts; allowing all copies of an endowed class would omit the ownership restriction.
  • Defined weak welfare improvement by a within-type coupling that never decreases an individual's attractive-object count.
  • Interpreted finite priority blocks as an ordered partition of supported types, maximizing total attractive-object mass successively across blocks, with arbitrary rational tie selection.
  • Defined revelation by the impossibility of increasing a type's attractive-object mass under optimistic unrevealed preferences; recomputed the revealed set each round and included the final priority selection.
  • Defined integral realizations by clearing both society and allocation denominators, with robustness over all resulting assignments and all agent-specific responsive completions.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, including the stated universal individual-rationality and Pareto-efficiency guarantee.

The proof has two main ingredients:

  • A cycle-splicing argument establishes termination without expanding the society into individual agents.
  • Plans whose top-tier counts are concentrated on two adjacent integers admit an exact polynomial-size linear-programming formulation.

All computations below use exact rational arithmetic.

1. Preliminary observations

Write \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), and define

\(a_t^0=a_t(e_t)\), \(S_k=\sum_t\mu_te_{t,k}\).

For a plan \(x\), let

\(f_{t,k}(x)=\sum_b b_kx_{t,b}\).

Thus \(W_t(x)=\sum_{k\in A_t}f_{t,k}(x)\).

Weak improvement is reflexive and transitive. To verify transitivity, suppose couplings \(z_{t,b,c}\) and \(z'_{t,c,d}\) witness that \(y\) weakly improves \(x\) and that \(v\) weakly improves \(y\). The coupling

\(h_{t,b,d}=\sum_{c:y_{t,c}>0}z_{t,b,c}z'_{t,c,d}/y_{t,c}\)

has marginals \(x\) and \(v\). Every positive term satisfies \(a_t(d)\ge a_t(c)\ge a_t(b)\).

Moreover, weak improvement implies \(W_t(y)\ge W_t(x)\), because

\(W_t(y)-W_t(x)=\sum_{b,c}(a_t(c)-a_t(b))z_{t,b,c}\ge0\).

Consequently, if every type's \(W_t\) weakly increases and at least one strictly increases, the vector of priority-block objectives strictly increases lexicographically: consider the first block containing a type with a strict increase.

2. Improving cycles and their splicing

We first establish the combinatorial argument needed for termination and welfare.

2.1 Extracting cycles from an expanded improvement

Fix a rational plan \(y\) satisfying the full component-wise constraints for every type. Let \(U\subseteq T\).

Consider balanced plans \(v\) weakly improving \(y\), where types outside \(U\) retain their full component-wise constraints, while types in \(U\) may receive any class, subject to their top-tier lower bound. This is precisely the restriction pattern of \(F_J^+(y)\) when \(U=T\setminus J\).

Suppose such a \(v\) satisfies \(W_t(v)>W_t(y)\). Put

\(f_{s,k}=f_{s,k}(y)\), \(g_{s,k}=f_{s,k}(v)\),

and define outgoing and incoming amounts

\(d_{s,k}=(f_{s,k}-g_{s,k})_+\), \(h_{s,k}=(g_{s,k}-f_{s,k})_+\).

For each type \(s\), their totals are equal because bundle sizes are unchanged. Also,

\(\sum_{k\in A_s}h_{s,k}-\sum_{k\in A_s}d_{s,k}=W_s(v)-W_s(y)\ge0\).

Therefore we can rationally match all outgoing top-tier amounts to incoming top-tier amounts. Match the remaining outgoing amounts to the remaining incoming amounts.

Each matched amount defines a directed edge \(k\to l\), labelled by \(s\). Its source satisfies \(f_{s,k}>0\), and its possibilities are:

  • If \(k\in A_s\), then \(l\in A_s\).
  • If \(k\notin A_s\) and \(s\notin U\), then \(l\in A_s\cup B_s\).
  • If \(k\notin A_s\) and \(s\in U\), then \(l\) is unrestricted.

Call an edge strict if \(k\notin A_s\) and \(l\in A_s\). The total weight of strict edges labelled by \(s\) equals \(W_s(v)-W_s(y)\).

Balance implies that these weighted edges form a circulation on the class set \(K\): at every class, total incoming weight equals total outgoing weight. A finite nonnegative circulation decomposes into directed cycles, by repeatedly extracting a positive cycle and subtracting its minimum edge weight.

Since \(W_t(v)>W_t(y)\), some extracted cycle contains a strict edge labelled by \(t\).

2.2 The splicing lemma

Lemma. Suppose \(U\ne\varnothing\), and every type \(t\in U\) can strictly increase \(W_t\) through an expanded improvement as above. Then there is a rational balanced modification of \(y\) such that:

  • every type's top-tier count weakly increases pointwise;
  • at least one type's \(W_t\) strictly increases;
  • types outside \(U\) receive only classes in their actual top two tiers;
  • every replacement made for a type in \(U\) receives a top-tier object.

Proof. For each \(t\in U\), choose a cycle obtained in Section 2.1 containing a strict edge labelled by \(t\). Rotate the cycle to begin with that edge,

\(k_t\to l_t\),

where \(k_t\notin A_t\), \(l_t\in A_t\), and \(f_{t,k_t}>0\).

Follow the cycle until just before the next edge whose label lies in \(U\) and whose source is outside that label's top tier. If no such edge occurs before returning to the initial edge, stop at its source \(k_t\).

This produces a path \(Q_t\) with the following properties:

  • Its first edge is the strict edge labelled by \(t\).
  • Every subsequent edge labelled by a type in \(U\) goes from its top tier to its top tier.
  • Every edge labelled outside \(U\) is a weakly improving swap within that type's actual top two tiers.
  • Its terminal class, say \(h_t\), is positively held outside the top tier by some type \(V(t)\in U\).

The map \(V:U\to U\) has a directed cycle. Write its distinct types as \(t_1,\ldots,t_d\), with \(V(t_i)=t_{i+1}\), interpreting indices cyclically.

Concatenate their paths as follows. In \(Q_{t_i}\), replace the source of its first edge \(k_{t_i}\to l_{t_i}\) by \(h_{t_{i-1}}\). This replacement is valid: \(h_{t_{i-1}}\) is positively held outside the top tier by \(t_i\), whereas \(l_{t_i}\in A_{t_i}\).

The resulting paths form a closed directed walk. All its edges weakly improve their labelled type's top-tier count, and its modified first edges strictly improve it. Every edge labelled in \(U\) receives a top-tier class.

To implement the walk as a rational mass modification, choose, for each edge labelled by \(s\) with source \(k\), a positive-mass bundle of \(s\) containing \(k\). Replace one copy of \(k\) by the edge's target on a rational mass \(\delta>0\). Choose \(\delta\) small enough that the pieces assigned to different edge occurrences are disjoint, including occurrences choosing the same source bundle.

The walk is closed, so aggregate class changes cancel. Each replacement preserves bundle size. The identity coupling on unchanged mass, together with these replacement couplings, witnesses pointwise nondecrease of top-tier counts. At least one strict edge gives a strict increase in some \(W_s\). All asserted restrictions hold. \(\square\)

In particular, if types in \(U\) currently satisfy the restrictive caps \(b_k\le e_{t,k}\) outside \(A_t\), the constructed modification preserves those caps: their non-top coordinates only decrease or remain unchanged.

3. Termination of gradual revelation

We prove termination for every rational priority selection, not merely for the choices used by the algorithm below.

Maintain the following invariant at the beginning of a round:

  • \(x\) satisfies the full component-wise constraints for every type and the restrictive caps for unrevealed types.
  • For every \(t\in J\), no plan in \(F_J^+(x)\) strictly increases \(W_t\).

The initial state satisfies this invariant. In particular, \(x\in F_J^-(x)\), so selection is feasible.

Let \(y\) be a priority selection from \(F_J^-(x)\). It satisfies the full component-wise constraints even for unrevealed types: outside \(A_t\cup B_t\), the cap is \(b_k\le e_{t,k}=0\).

There cannot be a plan in \(F_J^-(y)\) weakly increasing every \(W_t\) and strictly increasing one. Such a plan would belong to \(F_J^-(x)\) by transitivity and would have a lexicographically larger priority vector than \(y\).

Suppose \(J\ne T\) but no unrevealed type belongs to \(J'\). Then every type in \(U=T\setminus J\) has an expanded improvement strictly increasing its \(W_t\). The splicing lemma produces an improvement in \(F_J^-(y)\): revealed types retain their full constraints, and unrevealed types receive only top-tier objects in its swaps, preserving all restrictive caps. This contradicts the preceding paragraph.

Hence \(J'\) contains at least one previously unrevealed type.

It remains to show \(J\subseteq J'\). For \(t\in J\), the invariant and \(y\in F_J^+(x)\) imply \(W_t(y)=W_t(x)\). Every plan in \(F_J^+(y)\) also belongs to \(F_J^+(x)\), by transitivity. Thus none can increase \(W_t\) above \(W_t(y)\), and \(t\in J'\).

Finally, \(F_{J'}^+(y)\subseteq F_J^+(y)\), because additional revelations impose additional restrictions. The definition of \(J'\) therefore establishes the second invariant for the next round. The first invariant also holds: newly revealed types already satisfy their actual component-wise constraints, and still-unrevealed types retain their caps.

Thus \(J\) strictly grows whenever \(J\ne T\). There are at most \(\tau\) revelation rounds, followed by the prescribed final selection.

4. Balanced plans and exact LP compression

4.1 Balanced top-tier counts

Call a plan balanced within types if, for each type \(t\), its positive-mass bundles have top-tier counts in

\(\{\lfloor u_t\rfloor,\lceil u_t\rceil\}\), where \(u_t=W_t(x)/\mu_t\).

The normalized distribution of these counts is uniquely determined by \(u_t\). Write it as \(\nu_{u_t}\): it places probabilities \(1-\theta\) and \(\theta\) on \(\lfloor u_t\rfloor\) and \(\lfloor u_t\rfloor+1\), where \(\theta=u_t-\lfloor u_t\rfloor\).

For \(u\le v\), the distribution \(\nu_v\) weakly improves \(\nu_u\). Indeed, using the same uniform random variable \(H\), define

\(R_u(H)=\lfloor u\rfloor+\mathbf 1\{H<u-\lfloor u\rfloor\}\).

Then \(R_u(H)\le R_v(H)\) pointwise. When \(u,v\) are rational, the resulting finite coupling has rational entries.

A coupling of top-tier counts lifts to a bundle coupling by using the conditional bundle distributions at each count. Consequently, between balanced plans, coordinatewise nondecrease of the \(W_t\)'s is sufficient for weak improvement. It is necessary for all plans by Section 1.

The initial endowment plan is balanced.

4.2 Integral local polytopes

Consider one type, with integer bundle size \(q\), top set \(A\), integer coordinate capacities \(U_k\), and integers \(\ell\le h\). Define

\(C=\{d\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{m}:\sum_kd_k=q,\ d_k\le U_k,\ \ell\le\sum_{k\in A}d_k\le h\}\).

All vertices of \(C\) are integral.

To verify this, represent \(d\) by a network flow. A root sends \(q\) units through two group nodes, one for \(A\) and one for its complement. The root-to-\(A\) arc has lower and upper capacities \(\ell,h\). Each group sends flow to its class nodes, with capacities \(U_k\); class nodes send their flow to a sink demanding \(q\).

This representation is bijective: all arc flows are determined by \(d\). Every capacity and node supply is integral. The integral network-flow theorem therefore makes every vertex integral, and the same holds for \(C\).

Unrestricted coordinates may use capacity \(q\), and forbidden coordinates capacity zero.

4.3 Exact projection of the feasible sets

Suppose the current plan \(x\) is balanced, and put \(w_t=W_t(x)\).

Use variables \(f_{t,k}\), interpreted as aggregate class holdings. The common LP constraints are

\(f_{t,k}\ge0\), \(\sum_kf_{t,k}=\mu_tq_t\), \(\sum_tf_{t,k}=S_k\),

and

\(\sum_{k\in A_t}f_{t,k}\ge w_t\).

For either sign, revealed types additionally satisfy

\(f_{t,k}=0\quad\text{for }k\notin A_t\cup B_t\).

For the minus sign, unrevealed types additionally satisfy

\(f_{t,k}\le\mu_te_{t,k}\quad\text{for }k\notin A_t\).

For the plus sign, unrevealed types have no coordinate restrictions.

These LPs are exactly the projections of \(F_J^-(x)\) and \(F_J^+(x)\) onto aggregate holdings.

Necessity. Every feasible plan satisfies the resource, size, and coordinate constraints. Weak improvement gives the displayed \(W_t\) lower bounds.

Sufficiency. Take any feasible LP vector \(f\). For each type, define

\(d_{t,k}=f_{t,k}/\mu_t\), \(u_t=\sum_{k\in A_t}d_{t,k}\).

Apply Section 4.2 with top-count bounds

\(\ell_t=\lfloor u_t\rfloor\), \(h_t=\lceil u_t\rceil\),

and coordinate capacities encoding the relevant restrictions.

The vector \(d_t\) lies in this local polytope. It therefore has a rational convex decomposition into integral bundles. Every such bundle has top-tier count \(\ell_t\) or \(h_t\).

Furthermore,

\(u_t\ge w_t/\mu_t\ge a_t^0\),

so \(\ell_t\ge a_t^0\), since \(a_t^0\) is integral. Every decomposed bundle satisfies the required top-tier lower bound and coordinate restrictions.

Multiplying the decomposition weights by \(\mu_t\) constructs a balanced rational plan having aggregate holdings \(f\). It is balanced within types and weakly improves \(x\), by Section 4.1. The class-total equations ensure global balance.

This proves sufficiency.

5. Polynomial-time algorithm and output size

At every round, use the minus LP of Section 4.3.

For priority block \(P_j\), maximize

\(\sum_{t\in P_j}\sum_{k\in A_t}f_{t,k}\),

retaining equality to every earlier block's optimum. Reconstruct a balanced rational plan from the final LP vector. The exact projection result proves that this is a valid priority selection in the original procedure.

For each type \(t\), use the plus LP with baseline equal to this selected plan, and maximize

\(\sum_{k\in A_t}f_{t,k}\).

The type belongs to \(J'\) exactly when this maximum equals its current \(W_t\). All comparisons are exact.

After at most \(\tau\) rounds, perform the final priority selection using the same minus LP with \(J=T\).

Each LP has \(m\tau\) variables and polynomially many constraints. The procedure uses at most

\(\tau(r+\tau)+r\)

objective optimizations, apart from polynomial overhead for obtaining vertices and reconstructing plans.

5.1 Polynomial-size reconstruction

For completeness, the integral decompositions above can be computed in polynomial time with at most \(m+1\) bundles per type.

Work with a residual aggregate vector \(h\) and residual mass \(\rho\), initially \(h=f_t\), \(\rho=\mu_t\). Let \(C\) be the local polytope from Section 4.3.

Find an integral point \(b\) in the minimal face of \(C\) containing \(h/\rho\). This requires imposing the currently tight bounds in the network representation and finding an integral feasible flow. All these imposed bounds are integral.

Write the inequalities of \(C\) as \(a_i d\le c_i\). Remove the largest mass \(\epsilon\) of bundle \(b\) that preserves residual feasibility. Explicitly,

\(\epsilon=\min\left(\{\rho\}\cup\{(\rho c_i-a_i h)/(c_i-a_i b):c_i-a_i b>0\}\right)\).

Replace \(h,\rho\) by \(h-\epsilon b,\rho-\epsilon\), and record mass \(\epsilon\) on \(b\).

All previously tight inequalities remain tight. If positive residual mass remains, a new inequality becomes tight and strictly reduces the minimal face's dimension. If \(\epsilon=\rho\), boundedness of \(C\) forces the residual vector to be zero.

There are consequently at most \(m+1\) iterations. The returned plan has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive entries.

5.2 Bit complexity

Set \(n=m\tau\) and \(Q=\max_tq_t\). Both \(Q\) and all endowment coordinates have polynomial bit length.

The aggregate LP matrices, including priority equalities, have coefficients in \(\{0,1,-1\}\). Initially, all right-hand sides have a common denominator of bit length at most \(L\), obtained by multiplying the input mass denominators.

If a current LP's right-hand sides have common denominator \(D\), a basic feasible solution has common denominator dividing \(D|\Delta|\), where \(\Delta\) is a nonsingular basis determinant. Hadamard's inequality gives

\(|\Delta|\le n^{n/2}\).

Thus each successive vertex computation increases the required denominator bit length by at most a polynomial amount. There are only polynomially many such computations. Coordinates and objective values are bounded by \(Q\), so their numerators also have polynomial bit length. Standard exact rational LP algorithms, with polynomial vertex extraction if needed, therefore suffice.

In reconstruction, each denominator \(c_i-a_i b\) in the peeling step is an integer of magnitude at most \(2Q\). At most \(m+1\) steps are performed per type, so reconstruction also uses polynomial bit length and time.

There is no expansion proportional to the endowment magnitudes or to an integral-realization scale.

6. Universal individual rationality and efficiency

Let \(x^\star\) be the final selected plan.

By transitivity and final priority maximization, there is no plan in \(F_T^-(x^\star)\) weakly increasing every \(W_t\) and strictly increasing at least one. Otherwise it would also be feasible for the final selection's preceding baseline and would have a larger priority vector.

We prove the guarantee for any clearing scale \(N\), any integral realization, and any responsive preference profile consistent with the tiers.

6.1 Responsive comparisons from a bundle with no bottom-tier objects

The following elementary observation will be used.

Suppose an agent's current bundle \(b\) contains only top- and middle-tier objects. For any same-size bundle \(c\):

  • If \(a(c)<a(b)\), then \(b\) is strictly preferred to \(c\).
  • If \(a(c)=a(b)\) and \(c\) contains a bottom-tier object, then \(b\) is strictly preferred to \(c\).
  • If \(a(c)=a(b)\) and \(c\) contains no bottom-tier object, the bundles are indifferent.

To verify these statements, cancel common objects. If \(a(b)\ge a(c)\), match every remaining top-tier object of \(c\) to a top-tier object of \(b\), then match its remaining objects to the remaining top- or middle-tier objects of \(b\).

Replacing objects of \(c\) by their matched objects of \(b\) gives only weakly improving single-object replacements. A strict replacement occurs if the top count is smaller or if \(c\) contains a bottom-tier object. When top counts agree and neither bundle contains bottom-tier objects, every replacement can be within a tied tier. Responsiveness and transitivity give the assertions.

In particular, any weak improvement from \(b\) must weakly increase its top-tier count. Any strict improvement must strictly increase that count, and receiving a bottom-tier object in a weak improvement also requires a strict top-count increase.

6.2 Individual rationality

Every positive-mass bundle of \(x^\star\) contains only top- and middle-tier objects and has at least \(a_t^0\) top-tier objects.

The endowed bundle likewise contains only the top two tiers. The replacement argument in Section 6.1 therefore shows that the assigned bundle is weakly preferred to the endowment under every consistent responsive preference.

This applies separately to every agent in every realization.

6.3 Pareto efficiency

Suppose, for contradiction, that an alternative integral allocation Pareto improves a realization of \(x^\star\).

Temporarily regard each individual agent \(i\) as a separate label of mass \(1/N\). Let \(b_i\) and \(c_i\) be that agent's current and alternative bundles, with the tiers of its original type.

Section 6.1 gives

\(a_i(c_i)\ge a_i(b_i)\quad\text{for every }i\),

with strict inequality for at least one agent.

Let \(U\) be the agents whose alternative bundles contain bottom-tier objects. Every agent in \(U\) has a strict top-count increase.

If \(U=\varnothing\), the circulation construction of Section 2.1 applied to the two allocations contains a strict cycle consisting entirely of weakly improving swaps within the agents' actual top two tiers. Implementing it on sufficiently small rational pieces of their masses gives a balanced rational modification with no top-count decreases and at least one strict increase.

If \(U\ne\varnothing\), the alternative allocation is an expanded improvement in which precisely the labels in \(U\) may receive bottom-tier objects. Every label in \(U\) strictly gains top-tier objects. The splicing lemma therefore gives a balanced rational modification in which labels in \(U\) receive only top-tier objects in their swaps, and all other labels make weakly improving swaps within their actual top two tiers. Again, top counts never decrease, and at least one increases.

In either case, aggregate these individual labels back into their original types. The resulting rational plan:

  • weakly improves \(x^\star\), witnessed by the unchanged pieces and the single-object replacement couplings;
  • satisfies all full component-wise constraints;
  • preserves class totals and type masses;
  • weakly increases every \(W_t\), and strictly increases at least one.

It belongs to \(F_T^-(x^\star)\), contradicting final priority maximization.

The small rational pieces used here are only witnesses against the mass-plan optimum; they need not be realizable at the original scale \(N\). Their existence already contradicts that optimum.

Thus every integral realization is Pareto efficient under every consistent responsive preference profile.

7. Conclusion

The procedure terminates after at most \(\tau\) revelation rounds. Exact polynomial-size LPs implement all priority selections and revelation tests, and polynomial-time integral decompositions produce an explicit final rational plan with polynomial encoding length.

Every clearing-scale realization of that plan has the required universal individual-rationality and Pareto-efficiency properties.
