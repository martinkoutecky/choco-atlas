The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given firm and worker type sets \(I,J\), rational type masses, integer pair surpluses and outside options, and a feasible initial matching represented by \(K\) state-cohort classes with integral payoffs, can an algorithm polynomial in \(|I|+|J|+K+L\) output a finite sequence of pair improvements ending in a stable competitive equilibrium? Here \(L\) is the input bit length; each move transfers equal rational mass between cohorts not currently paired with each other, gives the newly matched agents integral payoffs that weakly improve both and strictly improve one, and returns abandoned partners to their outside options.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be disjoint, nonempty finite sets of firm and worker types. A society is given by its support: the \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Masses need not sum to one, and the two sides may have unequal total mass. Each type is an atomless population. Pair surpluses \(v_{ij}\) and outside options \(a_i,b_j\) are arbitrary integers.
A state is a finite list of matched-cohort records \((i,j,h,p,q)\), with rational mass \(h>0\), integral per-agent payoffs \(p,q\), and \(p+q=v_{ij}\), together with unmatched-cohort records paying the relevant outside option. Records account for every type's full mass; each agent has at most one partner. The initial state has \(K\) records and need not be individually rational. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
A pair improvement selects equal positive rational masses from a firm cohort and a worker cohort whose selected agents are not currently partners, matches them with integral payoffs weakly improving both and strictly improving at least one, and returns their abandoned partners to their outside options. A unilateral improvement may instead dissolve positive rational mass of matches when the initiating cohort strictly prefers its outside option; both former partners become unmatched. Cohorts may split or merge, and all other agents retain their matches and payoffs.
A state is a stable competitive equilibrium if every cohort receives at least its outside option and \(p+q\ge v_{ij}\) for every positive-mass firm cohort of type \(i\) with payoff \(p\) and worker cohort of type \(j\) with payoff \(q\).

Can an explicit finite sequence of pair improvements from any feasible initial state to a stable competitive equilibrium be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), where \(m=|I|+|J|\), or is this construction problem NP-hard?

For finite agents, [Chen, Fujishige and Yang (2016)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/99299/1/CFY_Jet_Publication_Version_2016.pdf) establish constructive finite reachability from any initial state; they do not establish a polynomial-time bound.
No result settling this exact rational-mass path-construction problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Mass normalization was unspecified: allow arbitrary positive rational masses, without requiring equal totals on the two sides.
  • Interpret type masses as atomless populations, permitting rational splitting of cohorts while retaining one-to-one partnerships at the agent level.
  • Define the K state-cohort classes as matched records specifying both types, equal paired mass and both per-agent payoffs, plus unmatched records; require conservation of every type's mass.
  • Feasibility does not imply individual rationality: retain arbitrary feasible initial integral payoffs and restore the discrete definition's omitted unilateral dissolution move.
  • Allow negative surpluses, outside options and payoffs, as the discrete domains are unrestricted integers.
  • Define stability across every pair of positive-mass cohorts, rather than using average payoffs for a type.
  • Interpret sequence construction as producing an explicit finite sequence, with rational move masses encoded in binary.

Polynomial-time construction

Under the intended interpretation, allowing the unilateral dissolutions expressly permitted in the question, the construction is polynomial-time solvable.

If unilateral moves were excluded, reachability could fail: take one type on each side, masses $1,1$, surplus $0$, outside options $1,0$, and an initial full matching paying $0,0$. No pair improvement is possible, although individual rationality fails. The proof below uses unilateral moves only to repair individual irrationality at the beginning.

The construction has two stages. First, a circulation decomposition allows us to reach a maximum-surplus matching using polynomially many cohort improvements. Second, short operations create and propagate cohorts receiving fixed equilibrium payoffs. All operations are described and verified below.

1. Normalization and a fixed equilibrium reference

Let the type masses be $\alpha_i>0$ and $\beta_j>0$. Normalize payoffs and surpluses by
$$ s_{ij}=v_{ij}-a_i-b_j,\qquad P=p-a_i,\qquad Q=q-b_j. $$
Outside options are now $0$. Matched payoffs satisfy $P+Q=s_{ij}$, and all improvement comparisons are preserved.

Dissolve every initial matched record having a negative normalized payoff. An endpoint receiving a negative payoff can initiate this unilateral improvement. At most $K$ dissolutions suffice, and the resulting state is individually rational. Every subsequent operation below preserves individual rationality.

Consider the transportation linear program
$$ \max \sum_{i,j}s_{ij}x_{ij} $$
subject to
$$ x_{ij}\geq 0,\qquad \sum_jx_{ij}\leq\alpha_i,\qquad \sum_ix_{ij}\leq\beta_j. $$
Its dual is
$$ \min \sum_i\alpha_i u_i+\sum_j\beta_j w_j $$
subject to
$$ u_i,w_j\geq 0,\qquad u_i+w_j\geq s_{ij}. $$

The primal is feasible and bounded. The dual is feasible by taking
$$ u_i=\max\{0,\max_{k,\ell}s_{k\ell}\},\qquad w_j=0. $$
The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore gives optimal rational solutions $x,u,w$, with polynomial encoding lengths.

Complementary slackness gives
$$ x_{ij}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u_i+w_j=s_{ij}, $$
and positive unmatched reference mass forces the corresponding dual payoff to be $0$.

We can make the reference payoffs integral. Replace
$$ u_i\longleftarrow\lfloor u_i\rfloor,\qquad w_j\longleftarrow\lceil w_j\rceil. $$
To verify feasibility, write the unrounded values as $u_i^{\mathrm{old}},w_j^{\mathrm{old}}$. If the rounded sum were smaller than the integer $s_{ij}$, then
$$ u_i^{\mathrm{old}}+w_j^{\mathrm{old}} < \lfloor u_i^{\mathrm{old}}\rfloor+ \lceil w_j^{\mathrm{old}}\rceil+1 \leq s_{ij}, $$
a contradiction. On a tight edge, integrality of $s_{ij}$ gives
$$ \lfloor u_i^{\mathrm{old}}\rfloor+ \lceil w_j^{\mathrm{old}}\rceil=s_{ij}. $$
Zero payoffs remain zero. Consequently, the rounded reference remains optimal and complementary to $x$.

Henceforth, $u_i,w_j$ denote these integral reference payoffs.

Call a cohort reference-paid if it receives its type’s reference payoff. A matched record is reference-paid when its payoffs are $(u_i,w_j)$.

We will repeatedly use the following property.

Lemma 1. In every individually rational state of maximum surplus:

  • every positive matched record uses a reference-tight edge;
  • every unmatched cohort has reference payoff $0$.

Proof. Let $V^*$ be the optimal transportation value and $W(S)$ the surplus of the state. Accounting for all population mass gives
$$ V^*-W(S) = \sum_{\text{matched records }(i,j,h)} h(u_i+w_j-s_{ij}) + \sum_{\text{unmatched firm records }(i,h)}hu_i + \sum_{\text{unmatched worker records }(j,h)}hw_j. $$
All summands are nonnegative. If $W(S)=V^*$, each positive-mass summand must vanish. $\square$

In particular, in such a state a matched record has one reference-paid endpoint if and only if both endpoints are reference-paid.

2. Reaching maximum surplus with polynomially many improvements

The reference-edge step below is related to the finite-agent construction of [Biró, Bomhoff, Golovach, Kern and Paulusma](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1456303). We prove everything needed here, including the rational-cohort compression.

2.1. Decomposing the current and reference matchings

Introduce one auxiliary vertex $z$. Construct a directed multigraph with the supported types and $z$ as vertices.

Its arcs, with rational amounts, are:

  • for each current matched record, an arc $i\to j$ carrying its mass and retaining its payoff labels;
  • for each $x_{ij}>0$, a reference arc $j\to i$ carrying $x_{ij}$;
  • for each type $i$, an arc $i\to z$ carrying its current unmatched mass and an arc $z\to i$ carrying its reference unmatched mass;
  • for each type $j$, an arc $z\to j$ carrying its current unmatched mass and an arc $j\to z$ carrying its reference unmatched mass.

Omit zero-amount arcs.

At each firm vertex, the total outgoing amount is $\alpha_i$, and the total incoming amount is $\alpha_i$. At each worker vertex, both totals are $\beta_j$. Conservation also holds at $z$, either by summing these equalities or directly from the unmatched-mass identities. Thus the amounts form a circulation.

There are at most
$$ E=K+|I||J|+2\tau $$
arcs: initial matched records remain at most $K$, and unmatched records can be merged by type.

Decompose the circulation into simple directed cycles. Repeatedly find a directed cycle, subtract the minimum amount on its arcs, and record that cycle with the subtracted amount. Each iteration exhausts an arc, so there are at most $E$ cycles. Each cycle contains at most $\tau$ type vertices.

A cycle of amount $d$ represents one population cohort of mass $d$ at each of its type vertices. Its current arcs specify a matching of these cohorts; its reference arcs specify another matching. Arcs incident with $z$ specify unmatched cohorts, and $z$ itself represents no population.

This interpretation is valid because, at a firm vertex, the outgoing arc is current and the incoming arc is reference; at a worker vertex, the incoming arc is current and the outgoing arc is reference. Thus each represented cohort has at most one partner in each matching.

The decomposition is only bookkeeping. Split each original matched record consistently at its two endpoints according to its cycle portions. Atomlessness permits these splits. No agent’s match or payoff changes.

2.2. Processing one cycle

Temporarily regard the cohorts of one cycle as finite vertices, all having mass $d$. Their current matching has nonnegative integral payoffs. Their reference matching has payoffs inherited from $u,w$.

While a reference-matching edge $ij$ is blocking, match its two cohorts. If their current payoffs are $P,Q$, use
$$ P'=P,\qquad Q'=s_{ij}-P. $$
Because $P+Q<s_{ij}$, the firm retains its payoff and the worker strictly improves. Abandoned partners become unmatched.

Each reference edge can be satisfied at most once. After it is satisfied, its endpoints are matched to one another. Every other reference edge is disjoint from it, so later operations cannot disturb it. A currently matched reference edge cannot itself block because its payoffs sum to its surplus.

Therefore this cycle needs at most $\tau$ improvements.

At termination, let $M$ be its current matching, $M^*$ its reference matching, and $P_t$ its current payoff at vertex $t$. Write $r_t$ for its reference payoff. Since no reference edge blocks,
$$ \sum_{ij\in M^*}s_{ij} \leq \sum_{ij\in M^*}(P_i+P_j) \leq \sum_tP_t = \sum_{ij\in M}s_{ij}. $$
Reference feasibility, and the fact that reference-unmatched vertices have reference payoff $0$, give
$$ \sum_{ij\in M}s_{ij} \leq \sum_t r_t = \sum_{ij\in M^*}s_{ij}. $$
All inequalities are equalities. The cycle’s current matching therefore has the same surplus as its reference matching.

Process all cycles independently. Multiplying each cycle’s surplus equality by its amount $d$ and summing proves that the resulting society has surplus $V^*$.

This stage uses at most $E\tau$ pair improvements and polynomially many records. By Lemma 1, every resulting matched edge is reference-tight, and every unmatched agent’s reference payoff is $0$.

3. Propagating reference payoffs

We now remain at maximum-surplus states between the short operations described below.

For each type $t$, let $B_t$ be the total mass currently receiving its reference payoff. Call a type seeded when $B_t>0$.

Suppose a non-reference matched record of types $i,j$ has mass $h$ and payoffs
$$ P+Q=s_{ij}=u_i+w_j. $$
Assume first that firm type $i$ is seeded.

Merge reference-paid matched records having the same type pair, and merge unmatched records by type. A reference-paid firm cohort of type $i$ is then either unmatched or belongs to one of at most $|J|$ reference-paid matched records. Choose one such cohort, of mass $b>0$, and set
$$ d=\min\{h,b\}. $$

3.1. A matched helper cohort

Suppose the helper belongs to a reference-paid record of types $i,k$.

If $P>u_i$, then $Q<w_j$. Perform:

  • Match mass $d$ of helper firms with mass $d$ of the original workers at $(u_i,w_j)$. The workers strictly improve. The original firms and helper workers become unmatched.
  • Match those abandoned firms and workers at $(u_i,w_k)$, provided $s_{ik}>0$.

The second move is legal: both endpoints currently receive $0$, and their proposed payoffs are nonnegative and sum to $s_{ik}$. If $s_{ik}=0$, then $u_i=w_k=0$; leave both abandoned cohorts unmatched instead.

If $P<u_i$, perform:

  • Match mass $d$ of the original firms with mass $d$ of helper workers at $(u_i,w_k)$. The firms strictly improve. The helper firms and original workers become unmatched.
  • Match those abandoned cohorts at $(u_i,w_j)$.

Here $u_i>0$, so the second move strictly improves at least one endpoint.

In either case, the helper agents finish reference-paid, and mass $d$ of the original record becomes reference-paid. No new non-reference record is created.

3.2. An unmatched helper cohort

An unmatched helper has $u_i=0$. Since $P\geq0$ and the original record is non-reference, necessarily $P>u_i$ and $Q<w_j$.

Match helper firms with mass $d$ of the original workers at $(u_i,w_j)$. The workers strictly improve. The abandoned original firms receive $0=u_i$.

Again, the original endpoints become reference-paid, and the helper retains its reference payoff.

The same construction applies when the seeded endpoint is a worker, by exchanging the two sides. The move rules, payoff identities, and stability inequalities are invariant under this exchange.

3.3. Progress and termination

Call one of these one- or two-move operations a propagation batch. At its end:

  • the selected non-reference record loses mass $d$;
  • no new non-reference record exists;
  • every $B_t$ is nondecreasing;
  • for the helper’s type, $B_t$ increases by $d$;
  • all remaining matches are reference-tight, and all unmatched agents have reference payoff $0$.

Thus the state again has maximum surplus.

Choose a largest reference-paid cohort of the helper’s type. There are at most $\tau$ such records incident with that type, including its unmatched record, so
$$ b\geq \frac{B_t}{\tau}. $$
Every batch either exhausts the selected non-reference record or, when $h>b$, increases
$$ B_t\longmapsto B_t+b \geq \left(1+\frac1\tau\right)B_t. $$

For any invocation, the starting rational masses lie on a common rational grid. Propagation preserves that grid. Since $B_t$ is bounded by the total mass of type $t$, the multiplicative increases can occur only finitely many times; record exhaustions also occur only finitely many times.

Consequently, we can finish propagation until no non-reference record is incident with a seeded type. At that point,
$$ B_t>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{every agent of type }t \text{ receives its reference payoff}. $$
Unmatched cohorts cause no exception, by Lemma 1.

4. A four-move operation that creates a new seed

Suppose propagation has finished but the state remains unstable. Individual rationality already holds, so there are positive-mass firm and worker cohorts with
$$ P+Q<s_{ij}\leq u_i+w_j. $$
At least one endpoint is underpaid relative to the reference. Assume first that
$$ Q<w_j. $$

The worker cohort is matched: unmatched workers already receive their reference payoff. Let its record, called $B$, have types $l,j$ and mass $h_B$. Its edge is tight:
$$ s_{lj}=u_l+w_j. $$
Moreover, $w_j>0$, hence $s_{lj}>0$.

Let $A$ be the selected firm cohort, of mass $h_A$; it may be matched or unmatched. If matched, its record differs from $B$, since endpoints of one matched record cannot block.

Choose
$$ d=\min\{h_A,h_B/2\}>0. $$
Reserve two disjoint portions $B_1,B_2$, each of mass $d$, within $B$.

Set
$$ Y=\min\{s_{ij}-P,\ w_j-1\},\qquad X=s_{ij}-Y. $$
All quantities are integral. Blocking and $Q<w_j$ imply
$$ s_{ij}-P\geq Q+1,\qquad w_j-1\geq Q. $$
Therefore
$$ X\geq P,\qquad Y\geq Q,\qquad Y<w_j,\qquad X+Y=s_{ij}. $$

Perform the following moves.

Move 1. Match mass $d$ of firms from $A$ with the workers of $B_1$, paying $(X,Y)$.

This is an improvement because both endpoints weakly improve and their previous payoff sum was strictly smaller than $s_{ij}$. The firms of $B_1$ become unmatched. If $A$ was matched, its selected workers also become unmatched.

Move 2. Match the abandoned firms of $B_1$ with the workers of $B_2$, paying $(u_l,w_j)$.

The firms start at $0$, and the workers strictly improve from $Q<w_j$. The firms of $B_2$ become unmatched.

Move 3. Match the abandoned firms of $B_2$ with the workers selected in Move 1, paying $(u_l,w_j)$.

The firms start at $0$, and the workers strictly improve from $Y<w_j$. The firms selected from $A$ become unmatched.

Move 4, when needed. If $A$ originally belonged to a matched record of types $i,k$ with positive surplus, match its abandoned firms and workers at $(u_i,w_k)$.

That original edge is reference-tight. Both cohorts currently receive $0$, so this is an improvement. If its surplus was $0$, both reference payoffs are $0$, and we leave those cohorts unmatched. If $A$ was originally unmatched, its reference payoff is $u_i=0$, so its firms already finish reference-paid.

Each move selects agents who are not currently partners: in Moves 2–4, one or both selected cohorts are unmatched. The two portions $B_1,B_2$ are disjoint.

At the end:

  • both portions of $B$, total mass $2d$, are reference-paid on their original type pair;
  • the selected portion of $A$ is reference-paid;
  • every other agent retains its original match and payoff;
  • no new non-reference record remains;
  • all matched edges are reference-tight, and every unmatched cohort has reference payoff $0$.

Thus maximum surplus is restored. Existing reference-paid mass has not decreased.

If instead the firm endpoint is underpaid, exchange the two sides and apply the same operation.

Crucially, the underpaid endpoint’s type was unseeded. Otherwise, completed propagation would force all its agents to receive their reference payoff. The operation therefore creates a seed in a previously unseeded type.

5. The complete algorithm and correctness

After normalization and the initial unilateral dissolutions:

  • Compute the integral equilibrium reference from Section 1.
  • Use the circulation decomposition and reference-edge operations of Section 2 to reach maximum surplus.
  • Perform propagation until no non-reference record is incident with a seeded type.
  • If the state is stable, stop.
  • Otherwise, apply the seed-creating operation of Section 4 and return to Step 3.

Propagation terminates by Section 3.

Each seed-creating operation increases the number of seeded types. Existing seeded types remain seeded after every complete operation. Therefore there are at most $\tau$ seed-creating operations.

If all types become seeded, completed propagation makes every cohort reference-paid. Reference feasibility then gives
$$ u_i+w_j\geq s_{ij} $$
for every firm-worker cohort pair, so the state is stable. The algorithm must consequently terminate, possibly earlier at another stable payoff vector.

All states are feasible. Every actual matching change is a permitted pair improvement, except for the initial individually irrational dissolutions.

6. Polynomial bounds on moves, records, and bit lengths

It remains to bound the rational arithmetic and the explicit output.

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of all input masses and all entries of the computed reference flow $x$. Its binary length is polynomial: the input denominators have total encoding length at most $L$, and $x$ has polynomial encoding length.

The circulation decomposition and its improvement operations use only additions, subtractions, and minima, so their masses remain multiples of $1/D$.

Each seed-creating operation may introduce one additional factor of $2$ into the common denominator. There are at most $\tau$ such operations. Propagation introduces no further denominator factors.

Hence every mass used anywhere is a multiple of
$$ \delta=\frac1{2^\tau D}. $$
Every positive seed mass is at least $\delta$.

For a supported type $t$ with mass $M_t$, define
$$ R=\max_t\left\lceil\log_2(M_t/\delta)\right\rceil. $$
This is polynomially bounded in the input parameters: the bit lengths of $D$ and the type masses are polynomial.

Let $H$ be the number of non-reference matched records immediately after the maximum-surplus stage. We have
$$ H=O(E\tau). $$
Neither propagation nor a complete seed-creating operation creates a new non-reference record. Thus at most $H$ propagation batches exhaust a record.

In every other propagation batch, the helper type’s reference-paid mass increases by a factor at least $1+1/\tau$. Since
$$ \log_2(1+1/\tau)\geq\frac1{2\tau}, $$
there are $O(\tau(R+1))$ such batches for each type, and
$$ O\bigl(\tau^2(R+1)\bigr) $$
over all types. Each batch uses at most two pair improvements.

The complete path therefore contains
$$ O\bigl(K+E\tau+\tau^2(R+1)\bigr) $$
improvements, a polynomial bound.

Record counts are also polynomial. After merging identical reference-paid records, there are at most $|I||J|$ reference-paid matched records and $\tau$ unmatched records. Non-reference matched records never exceed $H$ after the maximum-surplus stage. Each short operation adds only constantly many temporary records.

All mass encodings are polynomially bounded by the grid $\delta$ and the total population mass. All payoffs are integral and have polynomial bit lengths. Finding a blocker, choosing a largest helper cohort, maintaining records, and performing each arithmetic operation therefore take polynomial time.

This also permits explicitly listing every intermediate state: both the number of states and the size of each state are polynomial.

Restoring the outside options completes the required construction.

5. Creating a new seed
