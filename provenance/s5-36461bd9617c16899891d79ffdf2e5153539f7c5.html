The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a society μ with support size τ over complete rankings of m ideas, accessed only through queries that sample a resident from μ and ask for a pairwise comparison, what is the minimum worst-case number of queries for a randomized procedure to output a normalized Borda score vector \hat{n} and its induced ranking such that, with probability at least 1-δ, every coordinate differs from n_μ by at most 2ε/m and the top-ranked idea is an ε-Borda winner?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge 2\) ideas. A society \(\mu\) is represented by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings with positive binary rational masses summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total encoding length, including rational parameters \(\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)\).
The support list is hidden. The procedure knows \(m,\tau,L,\varepsilon,\delta\); each query chooses two distinct ideas, independently samples a fresh resident from \(\mu\), and reveals only that resident's truthful comparison. Queries may be adaptive; residents cannot be retained or identified.
Write \(b_\mu(x)\) for the expected standard Borda score and \(n_\mu(x)=b_\mu(x)/\sum_{y\in C}b_\mu(y)\). An \(\varepsilon\)-Borda winner satisfies \(b_\mu(x)\ge(1-\varepsilon)\max_y b_\mu(y)\).
The output is a nonnegative rational vector \(\hat n\) summing to \(1\), together with its decreasing-score ranking, with ties broken arbitrarily. With probability at least \(1-\delta\), simultaneously \(\max_x|\hat n(x)-n_\mu(x)|\le 2\varepsilon/m\) and the first-ranked idea is an \(\varepsilon\)-Borda winner.

What is the minimum worst-case number \(Q(m,\tau,L,\varepsilon,\delta)\) of queries required by a randomized procedure with these guarantees, uniformly over such societies? State computational running times in these same parameters.

For finite electorates, [Lee–Goel–Aitamurto–Landemore](https://web.stanford.edu/~ashishg/papers/scugc-2014-cr.pdf) give an \(O(m\varepsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta))\) comparison algorithm for approximate normalized Borda rankings and approximate winners.
For unrestricted stochastic pairwise comparisons, [Maxing and Ranking with Few Assumptions](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/7281-maxing-and-ranking-with-few-assumptions.pdf) gives PAC Borda ranking and winner algorithms, using additive score guarantees. Neither result identifies the optimum for the joint guarantee with prescribed rational support size and encoding length.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one and required at least two ideas, so normalized Borda scores are defined.
  • Interpreted complete rankings as strict rankings and comparisons as truthful.
  • Reconciled support representation with oracle-only access by hiding the support list while supplying its size and encoding length.
  • Specified independent fresh sampling per query, comparison-only feedback, no resident identification or retention, and adaptive choice of distinct ideas.
  • Interpreted a normalized output vector as nonnegative rational coordinates summing to one, with arbitrary ranking tie-breaking.
  • Required both approximation properties on one event of probability at least one minus delta, and interpreted worst-case query count as a bound over societies and execution outcomes.
  • Included rational accuracy and confidence parameters in the total encoding length.

Partial solution: two rankings already attain the general lower bound

The optimum remains unresolved for arbitrary prescribed exact encoding lengths. However, the small-support gap in the notes can be substantially closed: two supported rankings suffice to force the full query bound, at a common encoding length.

The results proved here are:

  • For every feasible parameter tuple,

$$ Q(m,\tau,L,\varepsilon,\delta) =O\!\left(m\varepsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta)\right). $$

  • If $m\ge64$, $\varepsilon\le1/32$, and $\delta\le1/8$, then for every $2\le\tau\le m!$ there is a common exact length $L_0$ such that

$$ Q(m,\tau,L_0,\varepsilon,\delta) =\Theta\!\left(m\varepsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta)\right). $$
The hard societies have dyadic masses, and their lengths satisfy
$$ L_0=O\!\left(\tau m\log m+\log(1/\varepsilon)+\ell_{\mathrm{pub}}\right), $$
where $\ell_{\mathrm{pub}}$ is the encoding length of the public parameters.

  • For $\tau=1$, $m\ge16$, $\varepsilon\le1/16$, and $\delta\le1/4$, the optimum is

$$ \Theta\!\left(m\log\min\{m,\varepsilon^{-1}\}\right). $$

  • Exact encoding length can restrict the masses enough to improve the universal bound. Under the explicit encoding below, the shortest two-ranking inputs force equal masses and admit an

$$ O\!\left(m^2\log(m/\delta)\right) $$
query algorithm, independently of $\varepsilon$.

The main new ingredient is an adaptive sign-recovery lower bound that does not require posterior independence.

1. Encoding convention and Borda identities

Exact-length statements require an encoding convention. Relabel the ideas by integers from $0$ through $m-1$. Encode each ranking using $m\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ bits.

Encode a positive integer $z$ by $\lfloor\log_2z\rfloor$ zero bits followed by its binary representation. Its code length is
$$ 2\lfloor\log_2z\rfloor+1. $$
Encode a positive rational $a/b$ in lowest terms by concatenating the codes for $a$ and $b$. Let $\ell_{\mathrm{pub}}$ include the fixed header and the encodings of $\varepsilon,\delta$. Thus
$$ L=\ell_{\mathrm{pub}}+\tau m\lceil\log_2m\rceil +\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\ell_{\mathrm{rat}}(w_j). $$
No padding is used. In particular, a fixed multiset of masses gives a fixed total length.

Let
$$ p_{xy}=\Pr_\mu(x\text{ is ranked above }y). $$
Strict completeness implies $p_{xy}+p_{yx}=1$. Standard Borda scores therefore satisfy
$$ b_\mu(x)=\sum_{y\ne x}p_{xy}, \qquad S:=\sum_x b_\mu(x)=\binom m2. $$
Consequently,
$$ n_\mu(x)=\frac{b_\mu(x)}S, \qquad n_\mu(x)\le\frac2m, \qquad \max_x n_\mu(x)\ge\frac1m. $$

The requested vector guarantee is equivalent to
$$ |S\hat n(x)-b_\mu(x)|\le\varepsilon(m-1) \quad\text{for every }x. $$
This conversion will be used in the lower bounds.

2. Universal upper bound and running time

Choose an unordered pair uniformly and query it. If $W$ is the reported winner, then
$$ \Pr(W=x) =\frac{\sum_{y\ne x}p_{xy}}{\binom m2} =n_\mu(x). $$
Independent pair choices and fresh independent residents therefore produce independent samples from $n_\mu$.

This is the sampling principle in Algorithm 1 of [Lee–Goel–Aitamurto–Landemore](https://web.stanford.edu/~ashishg/papers/scugc-2014-cr.pdf). The following proof establishes the joint guarantee directly in the present model.

Set
$$ a=\left\lceil\log_2(4m/\delta)\right\rceil, \qquad N=\left\lceil32m\varepsilon^{-2}a\right\rceil. $$
Perform $N$ independent uniform-pair queries. Let $K_x$ count the reported wins of $x$, and output
$$ \hat n(x)=K_x/N, $$
ranked by decreasing $K_x$. This vector is nonnegative, rational, and sums to one.

For fixed $x$, put $p=n_\mu(x)$ and $t=\varepsilon/(2m)$. The indicators of $W=x$ are independent Bernoulli variables. Bernstein’s inequality gives
$$ \Pr\!\left(|\hat n(x)-p|>t\right) \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{Nt^2}{2p+2t/3}\right). $$
Since $p\le2/m$ and $\varepsilon<1$,
$$ 2p+\frac{2t}{3}\le\frac{13}{3m}. $$
Hence
$$ \Pr\!\left(|\hat n(x)-n_\mu(x)|>t\right) \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{3N\varepsilon^2}{52m}\right) \le\frac{\delta}{2m}. $$
A union bound shows that, with probability at least $1-\delta/2$,
$$ \max_x|\hat n(x)-n_\mu(x)|\le\frac{\varepsilon}{2m}. $$

Let $x^*$ maximize the true score and let $\hat x$ be first in the output. On this event,
$$ n_\mu(\hat x) \ge\hat n(\hat x)-t \ge\hat n(x^*)-t \ge n_\mu(x^*)-\frac{\varepsilon}{m}. $$
Because $n_\mu(x^*)\ge1/m$, this implies
$$ b_\mu(\hat x)\ge(1-\varepsilon)b_\mu(x^*). $$
The requested vector error also holds on the same event.

To obtain a strict computational bound, generate each uniform pair using two uniform-integer rejection samplers. Each attempt succeeds with probability at least $1/2$. Cap each sampler at
$$ A=\left\lceil\log_2(4N/\delta)\right\rceil $$
attempts. There are at most $2N$ samplers, so the probability of any abort is at most $\delta/2$. On abort, output an arbitrary rational probability vector and its ranking. Coupling this implementation with the uncapped sampler proves total failure probability at most $\delta$.

The query count is always at most $N$. Excluding polynomial-time preprocessing in $L$, the expected word-operation running time is
$$ O(N+m\log m), $$
and the capped worst-case running time is
$$ O\!\left(N\log(N/\delta)+m\log m\right). $$
A conservative worst-case bit-operation bound is
$$ O\!\left((N+m\log m+1)(L+\log m)^3\right). $$
Indeed, all counters, sampling limits, and rational-arithmetic operands have $O(L+\log m)$ bits. No support reconstruction is needed.

3. An adaptive lower bound for local noisy queries

Lemma. Let $k\ge16$, $0<h\le1/4$, and $0<\eta\le1/8$. An unknown vector
$$ \theta\in\{-1,1\}^k $$
is observed through adaptive queries. Each query chooses a Boolean function $f$ depending on at most two coordinates of $\theta$, and receives $Y\in\{-1,1\}$ with
$$ \Pr(Y=f(\theta))=\frac12+h. $$
Responses use fresh independent noise. Parameter-independent auxiliary randomness and observations are allowed.

If a procedure uses at most $q$ queries and recovers the entire vector with probability at least $1-\eta$ for every $\theta$, then
$$ q\ge\frac{k}{4096h^2}\log(k/\eta). $$

Proof. Give $\theta$ the uniform prior and include all parameter-independent random seeds in the transcript $T$. A stopped procedure can be padded to $q$ steps using queries independent of $\theta$.

Write $\theta^{(i)}$ for $\theta$ with coordinate $i$ flipped, and define the transcript log-likelihood ratio
$$ \Lambda_i =\log\frac{\Pr(T\mid\theta)}{\Pr(T\mid\theta^{(i)})}. $$
Conditional on the same realized history and seed, the query rule is identical under both hypotheses. Thus adaptive choices introduce no additional likelihood factors.

Put
$$ a=\log\frac{1+2h}{1-2h}\le8h. $$
At a query where flipping coordinate $i$ changes $f(\theta)$, the increment of $\Lambda_i$ is
$$ a\,f(\theta)Y. $$
Its conditional mean, in a filtration containing the true $\theta$, is $2ha$, and its conditional variance is at most $a^2$. At every other query the increment is zero.

Let $N_i$ count queries where flipping coordinate $i$ changes the queried function. Since each function depends on at most two coordinates,
$$ \sum_iN_i\le2q. $$
Write
$$ \Lambda_i=2haN_i+M_i. $$
The centered increments defining $M_i$ are martingale differences. Orthogonality of these increments gives
$$ \mathbb E M_i^2\le a^2\mathbb E N_i. $$
Therefore,
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E\sum_i|\Lambda_i| &\le16h^2\sum_i\mathbb E N_i +8h\sum_i\sqrt{\mathbb E N_i}\\ &\le32h^2q+8h\sqrt{2kq} =:B. \end{aligned} $$

Let $\pi_T$ denote the posterior distribution. Under the uniform prior,
$$ \frac{\pi_T(\theta^{(i)})}{\pi_T(\theta)} =\exp(-\Lambda_i). $$
Keeping only the true vector and its $k$ distinct one-coordinate flips in the posterior denominator gives
$$ \pi_T(\theta) \le \frac{1}{1+\sum_i\exp(-\Lambda_i)}. $$

With probability at least $1/2$, Markov’s inequality gives
$$ \sum_i|\Lambda_i|\le2B. $$
On this event, convexity of the exponential yields
$$ \sum_i\exp(-\Lambda_i) \ge\sum_i\exp(-|\Lambda_i|) \ge k\exp(-2B/k) =:c. $$
Consequently,
$$ \mathbb E[1-\pi_T(\theta)] \ge\frac12\frac{c}{1+c}. $$

The uniform success assumption implies that the Bayes success probability is at least $1-\eta$:
$$ \mathbb E\max_\vartheta\pi_T(\vartheta)\ge1-\eta. $$
Conditional on $T$, the true vector has distribution $\pi_T$. Hence
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E\pi_T(\theta) &=\mathbb E\sum_\vartheta\pi_T(\vartheta)^2\\ &\ge\mathbb E\left[\max_\vartheta\pi_T(\vartheta)\right]^2\\ &\ge(1-\eta)^2. \end{aligned} $$
It follows that
$$ \frac12\frac{c}{1+c}\le2\eta. $$
Since $\eta\le1/8$,
$$ c\le\frac{4\eta}{1-4\eta}\le8\eta. $$
Thus
$$ \frac{2B}{k}\ge\log\frac{k}{8\eta}=:T_0. $$

Set $z=h^2q/k$. The preceding inequality becomes
$$ 64z+16\sqrt{2z}\ge T_0. $$
Here $T_0\ge1$. If $z\le T_0/2048$, then
$$ 64z+16\sqrt{2z} \le\frac{T_0}{32}+\frac{\sqrt{T_0}}2 \le\frac{17T_0}{32}<T_0, $$
a contradiction. Finally, $k/\eta\ge128$ implies
$$ T_0\ge\frac12\log(k/\eta). $$
The stated lower bound follows. $\square$

The posterior need not factor into independent coordinates. The proof uses posterior odds against all one-coordinate alternatives.

4. Two supported rankings force the full lower bound

Assume
$$ m\ge64,\qquad \varepsilon\le1/32,\qquad \delta\le1/8. $$
Put
$$ k=\lfloor m/4\rfloor,\qquad d=m-k. $$
There are $k$ main ideas, indexed from $1$ through $k$, and $d$ anchors.

Choose $h=2^{-r}$ to be the smallest reciprocal power of two at least $4\varepsilon$. Then
$$ 4\varepsilon\le h<8\varepsilon, \qquad h\le1/8. $$

For each $\theta\in\{-1,1\}^k$, define the ranking $R_\theta$ by placing:

  • main ideas with $\theta_i=1$, in increasing index order;
  • all anchors, in a fixed order;
  • main ideas with $\theta_i=-1$, in increasing index order.

Let $R_\theta^{\mathrm{rev}}$ be its complete reversal. Define the two-ranking society
$$ \nu_\theta =\left(\frac12+h\right)R_\theta +\left(\frac12-h\right)R_\theta^{\mathrm{rev}}. $$

Score separation

Let $s_i$ be main idea $i$’s Borda score in $R_\theta$. Reversal changes that score to $m-1-s_i$, so
$$ b_{\nu_\theta}(i) =\frac{m-1}{2}+h\bigl(2s_i-(m-1)\bigr). $$

If $\theta_i=1$, then $s_i\ge d$. If $\theta_i=-1$, then $s_i\le k-1$. Therefore,
$$ \theta_i\left(b_{\nu_\theta}(i)-\frac{m-1}{2}\right) \ge h(m-2k+1) \ge\frac{hm}{2} \ge2\varepsilon m. $$
This exceeds the allowed Borda estimation error $\varepsilon(m-1)$.

Thus any output satisfying the vector guarantee recovers every sign by testing whether
$$ S\hat n(i)>\frac{m-1}{2}. $$

Each comparison is a local noisy query

For a queried pair, let $f_\theta$ be its orientation in $R_\theta$. Its orientation in the reversal is opposite. The response therefore equals $f_\theta$ with probability $1/2+h$.

Moreover:

  • A main–anchor orientation depends on one sign.
  • A main–main orientation depends only on the two signs of those ideas.
  • An anchor–anchor orientation is independent of all signs.

Hence the procedure, followed by the sign decoder, is a protocol covered by the lemma. Uniform success probability at least $1-\delta$ forces
$$ q\ge\frac{k}{4096h^2}\log(k/\delta) =\Omega\!\left(m\varepsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta)\right). $$

All societies have exactly two rankings and the same multiset of dyadic masses. They therefore have a common exact length
$$ L_0=O\!\left(m\log m+\log(1/\varepsilon)+\ell_{\mathrm{pub}}\right). $$
Together with Section 2, this proves the matching query bound for $\tau=2$ at these lengths.

The lower bound uses only the vector guarantee.

5. Extension to every support size

The preceding lower bound extends to every $2\le\tau\le m!$ without rounding.

Let
$$ \mathcal U =\{R_\theta,R_\theta^{\mathrm{rev}}:\theta\in\{-1,1\}^k\}. $$
The rankings $R_\theta$ are distinct because their main ideas’ positions relative to the anchors identify $\theta$. Reversing the fixed anchor order distinguishes every reversed ranking from every unreversed ranking. Thus
$$ u:=|\mathcal U|=2^{k+1}. $$

For $\tau=2$, retain $\nu_\theta$. For $\tau\ge3$, construct a fixed background society $F$ and put
$$ \mu_\theta=\frac34\nu_\theta+\frac14F. $$

Prescribing the support

If $\tau<u$, choose $\tau-2$ distinct background rankings outside $\mathcal U$. There are enough: indeed,
$$ m!\ge2^{m-1}\ge2^{k+2}=2u. $$
Give these rankings positive dyadic masses. An economical allocation on $s=\tau-2$ rankings uses a power of two $T\ge s$, with $T<2s$ when $s>1$: assign mass $1/T$ to the first $s-1$ rankings and the remaining mass to the last. The combined society has exactly $\tau$ supported rankings.

If $\tau\ge u$, choose a fixed set $\mathcal B$ of $\tau$ rankings containing $\mathcal U$.

For $\tau=u$, let $F$ be uniform on $\mathcal U$. For $\tau>u$, choose a distinguished ranking in $\mathcal B\setminus\mathcal U$. Let $T$ be the smallest power of two at least $\tau$. Give every other ranking mass $1/T$, and give the distinguished ranking mass
$$ 1-\frac{\tau-1}{T}>0. $$
Then every member of $\mathcal U$ has the same background mass. The support of $\mu_\theta$ is exactly $\mathcal B$, of size $\tau$.

In every case, all final masses are positive and dyadic.

Separation survives the background

Write $f_i=b_F(i)$ and define the fixed center
$$ \gamma_i=\frac34\frac{m-1}{2}+\frac14f_i. $$
The preceding score bound gives
$$ \theta_i\bigl(b_{\mu_\theta}(i)-\gamma_i\bigr) \ge\frac34h(m-2k+1) \ge\frac{3hm}{8} \ge\frac32\varepsilon m >\varepsilon(m-1). $$
Thus the vector guarantee recovers every sign by comparing $S\hat n(i)$ with $\gamma_i$.

Adaptive information reduction

Simulate each comparison by first selecting its primary or background branch independently.

On the primary branch, supply the local noisy comparison from Section 4. On the background branch, sample from the fixed $F$ and supply its comparison.

For the lower-bound analysis, include the branch choices and background randomness in the independent seed. Conditional on this seed and the realized history, a background response is independent of $\theta$. A primary response is one local noisy query. There are at most $q$ such queries.

The lemma therefore again gives
$$ q=\Omega\!\left(m\varepsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta)\right). $$

Common exact length

In the small-support construction, the background masses are fixed and the two primary masses are unchanged across $\theta$.

In the large-support construction, every possible primary ranking has the same background mass. Thus the two increased masses have fixed values, while all remaining masses have a fixed multiset. The total encoding length is consequently identical across $\theta$.

Only the two primary masses require precision depending on $h$. The other masses have $O(\log(\tau+1))$ bits each. Hence
$$ L_0 =O\!\left( \tau m\log m+\tau\log(\tau+1) +\log(1/\varepsilon)+\ell_{\mathrm{pub}} \right). $$
Since $\tau\le m!$, we have $\log(\tau+1)=O(m\log m)$, proving the length bound stated at the beginning.

This establishes matching query bounds, at constructed common lengths, for every stochastic support size.

6. The deterministic case

Suppose $\tau=1$. Every query is a noiseless comparison in one unknown total order.

Upper bound

Set
$$ B=\lfloor\varepsilon(m-1)\rfloor+1. $$
Recursively select the exact median and partition, stopping when a subproblem has at most $B$ ideas. Treat each selected pivot as a singleton bucket. This produces ordered buckets occupying consecutive positions, each of size at most $B$.

Exact median selection takes linear comparisons. To recall why, group the input into groups of five and recursively select the median of their medians. Apart from incomplete and pivot groups, each median supplies three elements on its corresponding side. Thus at least $3s/10-10$ elements can be discarded, giving
$$ T(s)\le T(\lceil s/5\rceil) +T(\lceil7s/10\rceil+10)+cs. $$
Handle $s<300$ directly. For $s\ge300$, both recursive arguments decrease and their sum is at most $0.95s$. Strong induction, with a sufficiently large linear constant covering the base cases, proves $T(s)=O(s)$.

At each bucket-construction depth, subproblem sizes sum to at most $m$. The number of depths is
$$ O\!\left(1+\log(m/B)\right). $$
The comparison count is therefore
$$ O\!\left(m(1+\log(m/B))\right). $$

For a bucket occupying positions $a$ through $b$, assign each member the estimated Borda score
$$ m-\frac{a+b}{2}. $$
This is the average of that bucket’s true positional scores. Therefore the estimates sum to $S$, are nonnegative, and have individual error at most
$$ \frac{b-a}{2}\le\frac{\varepsilon(m-1)}2. $$
After normalization, the error is at most $\varepsilon/m$.

Every idea in the first bucket has score at least
$$ m-B\ge(1-\varepsilon)(m-1). $$
Thus arbitrary tie-breaking in the first bucket gives an $\varepsilon$-Borda winner. The procedure succeeds deterministically.

Lower bound

Put $r=\varepsilon(m-1)$. For one fixed output vector, the vector guarantee restricts each integer Borda score to an interval of length $2r$. Such an interval contains at most $\lfloor2r\rfloor+1$ integers. Hence one output can be valid for at most
$$ V=(\lfloor2r\rfloor+1)^m $$
orders; independent counting only overcounts permissible permutations.

Fixing the procedure’s random seed gives a binary comparison tree with at most $2^q$ leaves. Its output can succeed on at most $2^qV$ orders. Averaging over a uniform unknown order and then over seeds yields
$$ 2^qV\ge(1-\delta)m!. $$

Let $A=\min\{m,\varepsilon^{-1}\}$. We have
$$ \lfloor2r\rfloor+1\le\frac{3m}{A}, \qquad m!\ge(m/e)^m. $$
The factorial inequality follows from integrating $\log t$ below the sum of $\log j$. Therefore
$$ q\ge m\log_2\!\left(\frac{A}{3e}\right) +\log_2(1-\delta). $$
For $m\ge16$, $\varepsilon\le1/16$, and $\delta\le1/4$, this is $\Omega(m\log A)$, matching the upper bound.

Under the fixed encoding, all one-ranking societies have the same length. The algorithm uses $O(m\log A)$ word operations, apart from polynomial preprocessing in $L$. A conservative bit-operation bound is
$$ O\!\left((m\log A+1)(L+\log m)^3\right). $$

7. A regime where known length improves the bound

The common-length lower bounds do not establish hardness at every prescribed length. The following example shows why that distinction matters.

For $\tau=2$, define
$$ L_{\min} =\ell_{\mathrm{pub}}+2m\lceil\log_2m\rceil+8. $$
Every supported mass lies strictly between zero and one. In the chosen rational encoding, such a mass requires at least four bits. A four-bit mass must be either $1/2$ or $1/3$: its numerator must be $1$, and its denominator must be $2$ or $3$.

Two four-bit positive masses summing to one must therefore both equal $1/2$. Conversely, two distinct rankings with these masses attain $L_{\min}$. Thus the public tuple with $L=L_{\min}$ forces equal masses.

Every pairwise probability then belongs to
$$ \{0,1/2,1\}. $$
Let $M=\binom m2$ and query every pair
$$ r_0=\left\lceil\log_2(2M/\delta)\right\rceil $$
times. If both orientations occur, record probability $1/2$; otherwise record the consistently observed orientation as deterministic.

An agreeing pair is always classified correctly. A disagreeing pair is misclassified only when all $r_0$ responses coincide, an event of probability
$$ 2^{1-r_0}\le\delta/M. $$
A union bound shows that all pairwise probabilities are recovered exactly with probability at least $1-\delta$.

Sum the recovered probabilities to compute the Borda scores and normalize by $S$. Even on a classification failure, the estimates are nonnegative and sum to $S$, because the two orientations of each pair receive complementary probabilities. On successful classification, the output vector is exact and its first idea is an exact Borda winner.

This uses
$$ Mr_0=O\!\left(m^2\log(m/\delta)\right) $$
queries and $O(Mr_0+m\log m)$ word operations, apart from polynomial preprocessing in $L$. A conservative bit-operation bound is
$$ O\!\left((Mr_0+m\log m+1)(L+\log m)^3\right). $$

For $m=2$ at this length, the two rankings necessarily have equal masses and $n_\mu=(1/2,1/2)$, so zero queries suffice.

8. Remaining open points

The general sampling bound is sharp even with two rankings, at economical common exact lengths. The adaptive lower-bound argument and background construction remove the previous requirement of quadratic support size.

What remains unresolved is the optimum for each prescribed exact length, and the parameter regimes outside those proved above. In particular, the equal-mass example shows that encoding restrictions cannot simply be discarded when characterizing the full function $Q$.
