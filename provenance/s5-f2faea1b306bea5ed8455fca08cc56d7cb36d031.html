A replacement of one student changes mass \(h\) from one type to another; the threshold law should be \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-differentially private under such replacements, giving each student expected utility loss at most \(\varepsilon+\delta\) from truthful reporting. For school $u$, define the scalar priority
$$ p_u(t,z)=s_{t,u}-1+z. $$
Outside coordinate endpoints, scalar priority agrees with the specified lexicographic priority. The endpoints form a null set. Moreover, for every scalar $\ell$,
$$ \nu\{(t,z):p_u(t,z)=\ell\}=0, $$
because each type contributes at most one coordinate to that set.

We therefore work with scalar cutoffs $\theta_u\in[0,n]$, where eligibility means $p_u\ge\theta_u$ almost everywhere. The cutoff $\theta_u=n$ gives an empty eligible set almost everywhere.

Every rational scalar cutoff has a permitted rational-pair representation. If $\theta_u<n$, use
$$ c_u=1+\lfloor\theta_u\rfloor, \qquad r_u=\theta_u-\lfloor\theta_u\rfloor. $$
For $\theta_u=n$, use $(c_u,r_u)=(n+1,0)$.

For a type $t$, set
$$ e_{t,u}(\theta_u) = \min\{1,\max\{0,\theta_u-s_{t,u}+1\}\}. $$
Eligibility at $u$ is equivalent, almost everywhere, to $z\ge e_{t,u}$.

If $u\succ_t\bot$, define
$$ b_{t,u}(\theta) = \min\bigl(\{1\}\cup \{e_{t,v}(\theta_v):v\succ_t u\}\bigr). $$
The schools in this minimum are precisely those ranked above $u$. A student chooses $u$ exactly when she is eligible there and ineligible at every preferred school. Thus
$$ x_{t,u}(\theta) = \mu_t\bigl(b_{t,u}(\theta)-e_{t,u}(\theta_u)\bigr)_+. $$
For $u\prec_t\bot$, the contribution is zero.

Traversing each ranking down to $\bot$, while maintaining the smallest preceding eligibility threshold, evaluates all demands
$$ X_u(\theta)=\sum_t x_{t,u}(\theta) $$
with $O(\tau m)$ rational arithmetic operations. Their bit complexity is polynomial in the input and cutoff encoding lengths.

Three properties follow.

  • Each $X_u$ is continuous.
  • With competitors’ cutoffs fixed, $X_u$ is nonincreasing in $\theta_u$.
  • With $\theta_u$ fixed, raising competitors’ cutoffs cannot decrease $X_u$: it only removes alternatives preferred to $u$.

Finally, every cutoff-induced assignment satisfies occupied-seat stability. A student preferring $u$ to her assignment must be ineligible at $u$, while every admitted student is eligible there. The former therefore has lower priority than the latter, almost everywhere.

2. Canonical cutoffs for exactly stable assignments

$$ \kappa_u=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{y\in A_u}p_u(y). $$

$$ \nu\{y\in A_u:p_u(y)<\ell\}>0. $$

Therefore $B(s)=s$.

8. Privacy still implies the required truthfulness bound

The following implication remains valid independently of the computational construction.

Fix a truthful society $D$ and its neighbor $D'$ obtained by changing one ranking while keeping scores fixed. Let $P,Q$ be their cutoff laws.

For a cutoff output $b$, let $f(b)$ be the reporting student’s expected true utility under truthful choice, integrating over her uniform coordinate. Let $g(b)$ be her expected true utility under the false ranking.

Eligibility is independent of ranking reports. Truthful choice maximizes true utility among eligible alternatives, so
$$ 0\le g(b)\le f(b)\le1. $$

If the cutoff law is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-private, the inequality
$$ Q(S)\le e^\varepsilon P(S)+\delta $$
integrated over the level sets of $f$ gives
$$ \mathbb E_Q f\le e^\varepsilon\mathbb E_P f+\delta. $$
Therefore the utility gain is bounded by
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E_Q g-\mathbb E_P f &\le \mathbb E_Q f-\mathbb E_P f\\ &\le (1-e^{-\varepsilon})\mathbb E_Q f+e^{-\varepsilon}\delta\\ &\le 1-e^{-\varepsilon}+e^{-\varepsilon}\delta\\ &\le\varepsilon+\delta. \end{aligned} $$

Thus truthfulness would follow from a successful private cutoff construction.
