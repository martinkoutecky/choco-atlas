The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(p_t\) over additive valuation types, rational supplies \(q_g\) of indivisible item kinds, and \(\alpha \in [0, \varphi-1]\), compute a complete mass allocation \(y_{tB}\) over bundles \(B\) such that the type-mass and item-capacity constraints hold, every occupied bundle pair satisfies support-wise \(\alpha\)-EFX and EF1, and \(\mathrm{NW}_\infty(y)\) is at least \(1/(\alpha+1)\) times the maximum continuous Nash welfare over feasible complete mass allocations.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) additive valuation types with positive mass \(p_t\), each mass a binary rational, with \(\sum_t p_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) item kinds, with binary rational supplies \(q_g\ge0\) and values \(v_{tg}\ge0\). The input also contains a binary rational \(\alpha\in[0,\varphi-1]\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A bundle is a finite multiset \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), with \(v_t(B)=\sum_g v_{tg}B_g\). Individuals receive whole items; fractional quantities describe masses of individuals receiving bundles.
A complete mass allocation consists of finitely many nonzero entries \(y_{tB}\ge0\), satisfying \(\sum_B y_{tB}=p_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}B_g y_{tB}=q_g\) for every kind \(g\).
Support-wise fairness requires, for every ordered pair \((t,B),(s,C)\) with \(y_{tB}y_{sC}>0\), including pairs within one type, \(v_t(B)\ge\alpha v_t(C-e_g)\) for every \(g\) with \(C_g>0\), and EF1: this inequality without \(\alpha\) holds for at least one such \(g\). Both requirements are satisfied when \(C\) is empty; \(e_g\) removes one copy, and zero-valued items are included.
Define \(NW_\infty(y)=\exp(\sum_{t,B:y_{tB}>0}y_{tB}\log v_t(B))\), with value zero if any occupied bundle has zero utility. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be its supremum over all complete mass allocations, without fairness restrictions.

Can a complete rational mass allocation satisfying support-wise \(\alpha\)-EFX and EF1 and \(NW_\infty(y)\ge\mathrm{OPT}/(\alpha+1)\) always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is attaining this guarantee NP-hard?

For finite additive societies, complete \(\alpha\)-EFX and EF1 allocations achieving \(1/(\alpha+1)\) of optimal Nash welfare exist throughout this range; a polynomial-time construction is available given a maximum-Nash-welfare allocation ([Feldman–Mauras–Ponitka](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.09633)).
No result settling exactly this support-wise mass-allocation formulation was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total society mass to one and interpret supplies as quantities relative to that normalization.
  • Interpret item kinds as permitting repeated copies: bundles are finite integer multisets, with no additional per-person copy cap.
  • Use nonnegative rational additive item values and nonnegative rational supplies, allowing zero values and supplies.
  • Encode alpha as a binary rational in the stated interval.
  • Use allocations with finite support; allow real masses in the welfare benchmark and require rational masses for the computed allocation.
  • Define NW_infinity as the geometric mean of individual bundle utilities, weighted by allocated mass, with zero welfare whenever positive mass receives zero utility.
  • Use the supremum for the unrestricted welfare benchmark because the multiset bundle domain is unbounded, retaining the exact requested approximation factor.
  • Apply fairness to every ordered occupied pair, including pairs within a type; remove one copy and include zero-valued items in alpha-EFX.
  • Treat an empty comparison bundle as satisfying EF1, avoiding the literal existential-definition failure for empty bundles.

Result and scope

Attaining the stated guarantee is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard when \(\alpha\) is part of the input. This holds even with one valuation type, strictly positive integer item values, supplies all equal to \(\frac12\), and strictly positive dyadic \(\alpha\).

The reduction uses values of \(\alpha\) approaching zero. It therefore settles the formulation in the question, but does not establish hardness for a prescribed positive constant \(\alpha\).

The proof does not merely exploit the endpoint \(\alpha=0\). Moreover, every instance constructed below admits a complete rational allocation satisfying the requested fairness and welfare conditions.

1. Encoding PARTITION with unrestricted integer bundles

We reduce from PARTITION: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), decide whether some subset sums to half their total. Its \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness is standard; see, for example, these [MIT lecture notes](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/6.045-2020/lec17-color.pdf).

We may assume \(n\ge2\) and that the total is even. The first restriction removes only immediately decidable instances; doubling every integer enforces the second without changing the answer. Write

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^n a_i=2A,\qquad a_{\max}=\max_i a_i.\)

Define the following positive integers:

\(\displaystyle b=n+1,\qquad M=na_{\max}+1,\qquad D_i=Mb^{i-1},\qquad S=\sum_{i=1}^nD_i,\)

\(\displaystyle H=S+a_{\max}+A,\qquad K=nH+1,\qquad T=nK+S+A.\)

There is one type, of mass \(1\), and two item kinds \((i,0),(i,1)\) for each index \(i\). Their values and supplies are

\(\displaystyle w_{i,0}=K+D_i,\qquad w_{i,1}=K+D_i+a_i,\qquad q_{i,0}=q_{i,1}=\frac12.\)

Thus \(m=2n\) and \(\tau=1\). Write \(v(B)\) for this type’s utility and \(|B|\) for a bundle’s total number of items, counting multiplicities.

For later use, put

\(\displaystyle d=\max_iD_i+a_{\max},\qquad W=K+d.\)

Every item value belongs to \([K,W]\). Also, \(d\le S+a_{\max}<H\), so

\(\displaystyle W<K+\frac Kn,\qquad S+A<K.\)

Encoding lemma. An integer bundle \(B\) satisfies \(v(B)=T\) if and only if it contains exactly one item from every pair \((i,0),(i,1)\), and the indices for which it contains \((i,1)\) form a PARTITION solution.

Proof. First suppose \(v(B)=T\).

If \(|B|\ge n+1\), then

\(\displaystyle v(B)\ge(n+1)K>T.\)

If \(|B|\le n-1\), then

\(\displaystyle v(B)\le(n-1)W<(n-1)\left(K+\frac Kn\right)<nK\le T.\)

Consequently, \(|B|=n\). Let

\(\displaystyle c_i=B_{i,0}+B_{i,1},\qquad h=\sum_i a_iB_{i,1}.\)

Subtracting \(nK\) from \(v(B)=T\) gives

\(\displaystyle M\sum_i c_ib^{i-1}+h=M\sum_i b^{i-1}+A.\)

Because \(B\) contains \(n\) items,

\(\displaystyle 0\le h\le na_{\max}<M,\qquad 0<A<M.\)

Reduction modulo \(M\) therefore gives \(h=A\). Substitution yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_i c_ib^{i-1}=\sum_i b^{i-1}.\)

Each \(c_i\) lies between \(0\) and \(n=b-1\). Uniqueness of base-\(b\) representation implies \(c_i=1\) for every \(i\). Thus \(B_{i,1}\in\{0,1\}\), and \(h=A\) identifies a subset of sum \(A\).

Conversely, choosing \((i,1)\) for the indices of a subset of sum \(A\), and \((i,0)\) otherwise, gives utility \(nK+S+A=T\). This proves the lemma, including its applicability to bundles with unrestricted multiplicities. \(\square\)

2. Mean utility and a quantitative welfare gap

For every complete allocation, the mass-weighted mean utility is fixed:

\(\displaystyle \sum_B y_Bv(B)=\sum_{i,j}q_{i,j}w_{i,j}=nK+S+A=T.\)

Since the total mass is \(1\), concavity of the logarithm gives

\(\displaystyle NW_\infty(y)\le T.\)

This also holds when an occupied bundle has zero utility, because then the welfare is zero.

We need a strict bound when no occupied bundle has utility \(T\).

Gap lemma. If \(T\ge2\), all bundle utilities are nonnegative integers, their mass-weighted mean is \(T\), and no occupied bundle has utility \(T\), then

\(\displaystyle NW_\infty(y)\le\sqrt{T^2-1}.\)

Proof. An occupied zero-utility bundle makes the claim immediate. Otherwise all occupied utilities are positive integers, hence lie outside the open interval \((T-1,T+1)\).

Let \(\ell\) be the affine function through the two points

\(\displaystyle (T-1,\log(T-1)),\qquad (T+1,\log(T+1)).\)

Concavity of \(\log\) implies that its graph lies below the extension of this secant outside \([T-1,T+1]\). Explicitly, for \(x<T-1\), express \(T-1\) as a convex combination of \(x\) and \(T+1\), apply concavity, and rearrange to obtain \(\log x\le\ell(x)\). For \(x>T+1\), use the convex-combination expression of \(T+1\) in terms of \(T-1\) and \(x\). Equality holds at the endpoints.

Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \sum_B y_B\log v(B)\le\sum_B y_B\ell(v(B))=\ell(T)=\frac12\log(T^2-1),\)

where the middle equality uses the mean-utility identity. Exponentiating proves the claim. \(\square\)

3. Choosing a strictly positive input parameter

Let \(k\) be the binary bit length of \(T\), and set

\(\displaystyle \alpha=2^{-(2k+2)}.\)

This is a strictly positive dyadic rational with polynomial encoding length, and

\(\displaystyle 0<\alpha\le\frac1{4T^2}\le\frac1{16}<\varphi-1.\)

The chosen parameter satisfies

\(\displaystyle \frac{T}{1+\alpha}>\sqrt{T^2-1}.\)

Indeed, put \(x=T^{-2}\). Since \(T\ge2\), we have \(0<x<1\), and

\(\displaystyle \frac1{1+\alpha}\ge1-\alpha\ge1-\frac x4>1-\frac x2\ge\sqrt{1-x}.\)

The last inequality follows by squaring its nonnegative sides: \((1-x/2)^2\ge1-x\).

4. Yes-instances have optimal welfare \(T\)

Suppose the PARTITION instance has a solution. Let \(B\) be its encoded bundle from the encoding lemma, and let \(C\) choose the opposite item from every pair.

Both bundles have utility \(T\), because the complementary subset also sums to \(A\). Assign mass \(\frac12\) to each bundle. This allocation is complete and rational, and its welfare equals \(T\). The mean-utility upper bound therefore shows that

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}=T.\)

It also satisfies both requested fairness conditions. Every occupied bundle has utility \(T\), while removing any item from either bundle leaves utility at most \(T\). This verifies every ordered comparison, including comparisons within the same type.

Consequently, on a yes-instance, any output satisfying the requested welfare guarantee must have

\(\displaystyle NW_\infty(y)\ge\frac{T}{1+\alpha}>\sqrt{T^2-1}.\)

By the gap lemma, such an output must contain an occupied bundle of utility exactly \(T\). The encoding lemma then extracts a PARTITION solution.

5. Every constructed instance admits a rational fair optimum

We now verify that the reduction does not rely on infeasible no-instances. In fact, every constructed instance has a rational allocation attaining \(\mathrm{OPT}\), and this allocation satisfies EF1 and the chosen \(\alpha\)-EFX.

Attainment and rationality

For an integer \(R\ge n+1\), restrict attention to nonempty bundles with \(|B|\le R\). This gives a finite linear program:

\(\displaystyle \max\sum_B y_B\log v(B),\)

subject to the prescribed masses and supplies.

Its feasible set is nonempty: assign mass \(\frac12\) to the bundle containing all \((i,0)\), and mass \(\frac12\) to the bundle containing all \((i,1)\). Both bundles contain \(n\) items. Its feasible set is compact, because \(y_B\ge0\) and \(\sum_B y_B=1\). Thus a maximum exists.

We claim that every maximizing allocation in this restricted problem uses only bundles of utility at most \(T+W\).

Suppose instead that an occupied bundle \(C\) has utility \(w>T+W\). Since mean utility is \(T\), some occupied bundle \(B\) has utility \(u\le T\). Choose an item \(g\) in \(C\), of value \(z\in[K,W]\). Replace equal positive masses of \(B,C\) by \(B+e_g,C-e_g\).

This preserves every mass and supply constraint. It remains within the restricted bundle set: \(u\le T<(n+1)K\) implies \(|B|\le n\), so \(|B+e_g|\le n+1\le R\). Also, \(w>T+W>W\ge z\), so \(C-e_g\) is nonempty.

The change strictly increases the objective, since

\(\displaystyle (u+z)(w-z)-uw=z(w-u-z)>0,\)

using \(w-u>W\ge z\). This contradicts maximality.

Next,

\(\displaystyle T+W=(n+1)K+S+A+d<(n+2)K.\)

For the strict inequality, note that \(S+A+d\le2H\le nH<K\). Every maximizing occupied bundle therefore contains at most \(n+1\) items.

It follows that the optimal values of all these restricted problems, for \(R\ge n+1\), coincide. Every finite-support allocation with positive welfare belongs to one such restricted problem; allocations with zero welfare cannot improve the positive feasible welfare exhibited above. Hence the common restricted optimum equals the unrestricted supremum \(\mathrm{OPT}\), and attains it.

Choose an optimal vertex in the problem restricted to \(|B|\le n+1\). Such a vertex exists even though the objective coefficients are real: a linear objective on a polytope attains its maximum at a vertex. The constraints are rational, so this vertex has rational masses. It has at most \(m+1=2n+1\) positive entries, because there are \(m+1\) equality constraints.

These entries also have polynomial encoding length. Bundle coordinates are at most \(n+1\); the masses solve a nonsingular subsystem with integer coefficients bounded by \(n+1\) and right-hand sides in \(\{1,\frac12\}\). Cramer’s rule and determinant bounds give polynomial bit length.

EF1

Let \(y^*\) be this global optimum. All occupied utilities are positive.

If an ordered pair \(B,C\) violated EF1, then, writing \(u=v(B)\) and \(w=v(C)\), we would have

\(\displaystyle u<w-w_g\qquad\text{for every item kind $g\) present in \(C\)}.\)

Choose any such item, of value \(z=w_g>0\), and transfer it from an equal positive mass of \(C\) to an equal positive mass of \(B\). The resulting utilities remain positive, and

\(\displaystyle (u+z)(w-z)-uw=z(w-u-z)>0.\)

This strictly improves welfare while preserving completeness, contradicting global optimality. Thus \(y^*\) is support-wise EF1.

The chosen \(\alpha\)-EFX

Let \(u_{\min},u_{\max}\) be the minimum and maximum occupied utilities. EF1 and the bound \(w_g\le W\) imply, for every occupied bundle \(C\),

\(\displaystyle u_{\min}\ge v(C)-W.\)

Averaging gives \(u_{\min}\ge T-W\). Applying EF1 to every occupied bundle against a bundle of maximum utility and averaging likewise gives \(u_{\max}\le T+W\).

Moreover,

\(\displaystyle W<K+\frac Kn\le\frac32K\le\frac34T.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle T-W>\frac T4,\qquad T+W<\frac{7T}{4}.\)

Since \(\alpha\le\frac1{16}<\frac17\), every occupied ordered pair \(B,C\), and every item \(g\) in \(C\), satisfies

\(\displaystyle v(B)\ge T-W>\alpha(T+W)\ge\alpha v(C-e_g).\)

Thus \(y^*\) is support-wise \(\alpha\)-EFX. It attains \(\mathrm{OPT}\), so it also satisfies the requested welfare guarantee.

This establishes feasible, polynomial-size rational outputs on both yes- and no-instances.

6. Polynomial reduction and conclusion

All constructed integers have polynomial binary encoding length. In particular,

\(\displaystyle \log_2D_i=O(\log n+\log a_{\max}+n\log n),\)

and \(S,K,T\) have the same polynomial-order bit bound. The denominator encoding of \(\alpha\) also has polynomial length. Hence the reduction is computable in polynomial time.

Suppose an algorithm computed the requested allocation in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), using the explicit finite list of rational masses and integer bundles specified in the question. Run it on the constructed instance and inspect whether any positive-mass bundle has utility \(T\).

  • On a yes-instance, the welfare guarantee and the gap lemma force such a bundle to appear.
  • On a no-instance, the encoding lemma proves that no integer bundle of utility \(T\) exists.

This inspection uses only exact integer arithmetic on a polynomial-length output. It would decide PARTITION in polynomial time.

Therefore attaining the requested guarantee is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and no such polynomial-time algorithm exists unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

This is compatible with the [Feldman–Mauras–Ponitka finite-society theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.09633), which gives the stated existence guarantee and a construction supplied with a maximum-Nash-welfare allocation. The obstruction proved here is computational and already occurs for one valuation type and strictly positive input-dependent \(\alpha\).
