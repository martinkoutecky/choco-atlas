The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many claim types \(t\in T\), rational claims \(c_t>0\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and rational per-capita estate \(e\in(0,\sum_t\mu_tc_t)\), choose a common denominator \(D\) for the masses. For each positive integer \(k\), form a bankruptcy problem with \(kD\mu_t\) creditors of type \(t\), each claiming \(c_t\), and estate \(kDe\). Compute the exact limit \(a_t=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{kD\mu_t}\sum_{i\in N_t^{(k)}}x_i^{(k)}\), where \(x^{(k)}\) is the finite game's nucleolus.

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) claim types, each with a positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\) and a positive binary-rational claim \(c_t\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The binary-rational per-capita estate satisfies \(0<e<\sum_t\mu_tc_t\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; there is no separate \(m\) parameter.
Let \(D\) be the least common denominator of the masses. For each positive integer \(k\), let \(N_t^{(k)}\) contain \(kD\mu_t\) creditors with individual claim \(c_t\), and put \(N^{(k)}=\bigcup_tN_t^{(k)}\). Define the bankruptcy game by \(v_k(S)=\max\{kDe-\sum_t c_t|N_t^{(k)}\setminus S|,0\}\) for every \(S\subseteq N^{(k)}\), and let \(x^{(k)}\) be its nucleolus over imputations.
The required awards are the exact replica limits \(a_t=\lim_{k\to\infty}(kD\mu_t)^{-1}\sum_{i\in N_t^{(k)}}x_i^{(k)}\).

Do these limits exist for every permitted input, and is their exact computation solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite bankruptcy games, the nucleolus equals the unique CG-consistent allocation: for every pair of creditors, their combined award is divided by first granting each the amount the other does not claim and then halving the contested remainder. This is the [Aumann–Maschler characterization](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/raumann/pdf/45.pdf).

1. Result and exact formula

The limits exist for every permitted input, and their exact computation takes time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\). In fact, the per-creditor awards are independent of the replica number \(k\).

Define
\(C=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t c_t\), \(h_t=c_t/2\), and \(H=\max_{t\in T}h_t\).

Let
\(b=\min\{e,C-e\}\).
There is a unique \(\lambda\in(0,H]\) satisfying
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\min\{h_t,\lambda\}=b. \]
The required awards are
\[ a_t= \begin{cases} \min\{c_t/2,\lambda\},& e\le C/2,\\ c_t-\min\{c_t/2,\lambda\},& e>C/2. \end{cases} \]

We prove the formula directly from the supplied finite-game characterization, then establish its bit complexity.

2. Pairwise CG identities

For two creditors with claims \(d_i,d_j>0\), let their combined award be \(s\in[0,d_i+d_j]\). Write \(u^+=\max\{u,0\}\).

The amount conceded to creditor \(i\) is \((s-d_j)^+\). Consequently, the CG award to \(i\) is
\[ g_i(s;d_i,d_j) =\frac{s+(s-d_j)^+-(s-d_i)^+}{2}. \]
The two CG awards sum to \(s\).

We use the given Aumann–Maschler characterization: for a finite bankruptcy problem with positive claims and estate strictly between zero and their sum, the unique CG-consistent allocation is its nucleolus. This combines Theorems A and D of [Aumann–Maschler’s paper](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1985-aumann.pdf).

Lemma 1. For every \(\lambda\ge0\), the awards
\(y_i=\min\{d_i/2,\lambda\}\) and
\(y_j=\min\{d_j/2,\lambda\}\)
are the CG division of their sum.

Proof. Relabel the creditors so that \(d_i\le d_j\), and put \(s=y_i+y_j\). There are three cases.

  • If \(\lambda\le d_i/2\), then \(y_i=y_j=\lambda\) and \(s=2\lambda\le d_i\le d_j\). Both positive-part terms vanish, so \(g_i(s)=\lambda=y_i\).
  • If \(d_i/2\le\lambda\le d_j/2\), then

\(y_i=d_i/2\), \(y_j=\lambda\), and
\(d_i\le s=d_i/2+\lambda\le(d_i+d_j)/2\le d_j\).
Therefore
\(g_i(s)=[s-(s-d_i)]/2=d_i/2=y_i\).

  • If \(\lambda\ge d_j/2\), then

\(y_i=d_i/2\), \(y_j=d_j/2\), and
\(d_i\le s=(d_i+d_j)/2\le d_j\).
Again, \(g_i(s)=d_i/2=y_i\).

In each case, the other CG award is \(s-g_i(s)=y_j\). This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

Lemma 2. The CG rule satisfies
\(g_i(d_i+d_j-s;d_i,d_j)=d_i-g_i(s;d_i,d_j)\).
Hence, if \((y_i,y_j)\) is CG-consistent, so is
\((d_i-y_i,d_j-y_j)\).

Proof. Using \((d-s)^+=(s-d)^++d-s\), we obtain
\[ \begin{aligned} g_i(d_i+d_j-s;d_i,d_j) &=\frac{d_i+d_j-s+(d_i-s)^+-(d_j-s)^+}{2}\\ &=\frac{2d_i-s+(s-d_i)^+-(s-d_j)^+}{2}\\ &=d_i-g_i(s;d_i,d_j). \end{aligned} \]
Apply this identity to the combined award \(s=y_i+y_j\). The complementary awards have combined sum \(d_i+d_j-s\), giving the assertion. \(\square\)

3. Identification of every replica nucleolus

First, define
\(F(u)=\sum_t\mu_t\min\{h_t,u\}\) for \(u\in[0,H]\).

This function is continuous, with \(F(0)=0\) and \(F(H)=C/2\). It is strictly increasing: if \(0\le u<v\le H\), choose a type with \(h_t=H\). Its contribution increases by \(\mu_t(v-u)>0\), while every other contribution is nondecreasing.

Because \(0<e<C\), we have \(0<b\le C/2\). Thus continuity and strict increase give a unique \(\lambda\in(0,H]\) satisfying \(F(\lambda)=b\).

Let \(a_t\) be the formula in Section 1. It satisfies
\(0\le a_t\le c_t\) and
\(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=e\).
Indeed, when \(e\le C/2\), the weighted sum is \(F(\lambda)=e\); when \(e>C/2\), it is \(C-F(\lambda)=C-(C-e)=e\).

Fix any positive integer \(k\). Its total estate and total claims are
\(E_k=kDe\) and \(C_k=kDC\), respectively, so \(0<E_k<C_k\). Assign
\(z_i=a_t\) to every creditor \(i\in N_t^{(k)}\).

This allocation is efficient:
\[ \sum_{i\in N^{(k)}}z_i =\sum_t kD\mu_ta_t =kDe=E_k. \]

It is also an imputation. Each \(z_i\) is nonnegative and does not exceed the corresponding claim \(c_i\). Therefore
\[ z_i =E_k-\sum_{j\ne i}z_j \ge E_k-\sum_{j\ne i}c_j. \]
Together with nonnegativity, this gives
\(z_i\ge\max\{E_k-\sum_{j\ne i}c_j,0\}=v_k(\{i\})\), the required individual rationality.

Finally, the allocation is CG-consistent for every pair:

  • When \(e\le C/2\), its awards have the form in Lemma 1.
  • When \(e>C/2\), its losses \(c_i-z_i\) have that form. Lemma 1 establishes consistency of those losses, and Lemma 2 establishes consistency of the awards.

The finite-game characterization therefore identifies \(z\) as \(x^{(k)}\). If the replica contains only one creditor, efficiency alone determines its unique imputation, so the same conclusion holds.

Consequently, for every type \(t\) and every positive integer \(k\),
\[ \frac{1}{kD\mu_t}\sum_{i\in N_t^{(k)}}x_i^{(k)}=a_t. \]
The sequence defining each replica limit is constant. In particular, all limits exist and equal the stated formula.

At \(e=C/2\), the defining equation gives \(\lambda=H\), so every type receives \(c_t/2\).

4. Exact polynomial-time computation

The algorithm operates directly on the support; it never constructs a replica.

Sort the types so that
\(h_1\le\cdots\le h_\tau\), retaining their corresponding masses, and set \(h_0=0\). For \(0\le j<\tau\), define
\(P_j=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mu_i h_i\) and
\(M_j=\sum_{i=j+1}^{\tau}\mu_i\).

Since all masses are positive, \(M_j>0\). On the interval
\(h_j\le u\le h_{j+1}\), the defining function is
\(F(u)=P_j+M_ju\).

Find the smallest \(j\in\{0,\ldots,\tau-1\}\) satisfying
\(b\le F(h_{j+1})\).
Such an index exists because \(F(h_\tau)=C/2\ge b\). Minimality, together with \(F(h_0)=0<b\), ensures
\(F(h_j)\le b\le F(h_{j+1})\).
Hence the exact solution is
\[ \lambda=\frac{b-P_j}{M_j}. \]
This also handles repeated claims and equality at a breakpoint. Substitute this rational value into the award formula and restore the original type order.

Sorting takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) comparisons. Prefix and suffix sums, the scan, and the output require \(O(\tau)\) further arithmetic operations.

To verify polynomial bit complexity, let \(Q\) be twice the product of the denominators of all input masses, claims, and the estate. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Every \(\mu_t\), \(h_t\), and \(e\) is an integer multiple of \(1/Q\), with numerator length \(O(L)\). Thus:

  • Every \(P_j\), \(C\), and \(b\) has a representation with denominator \(Q^2\).
  • Every \(M_j\) has a representation with denominator \(Q\).
  • The corresponding numerators have length \(O(L+\log(\tau+1))\).

Writing \(b-P_j=A/Q^2\) and \(M_j=B/Q\), with \(B>0\), gives
\(\lambda=A/(QB)\). Its numerator and denominator therefore also have polynomial bit length. The same holds for every output award.

All comparisons, arithmetic operations, and optional fraction reductions consequently take polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\). This proves exact polynomial-time solvability.
