The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_\rho\) of bidder valuation/allocation-role types \(\rho\), a finite bundle-configuration catalogue \(B\), repeated resource capacities \(q\), valuations \(v_\rho(b)\), and an efficient aggregate allocation, define \(w(z)\) as the maximum value obtainable by serving coalition submass \(z\) under \(q\). Compute an exact leximin-maximal utility profile \(u\rho\), with multiplicities \(\mu_\rho\), satisfying \(u_\rho\ge 0\) and \(\sum_\rho(\mu_\rho-z_\rho)u_\rho \leq w(\mu)-w(z)\) for every \(0\leq z\leq\mu\), and output role-specific payments \(p_\rho=v_\rho(b_\rho)-u_\rho\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) valuation/allocation-role types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\rho\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; total mass \(T=\sum_\rho\mu_\rho\) need not equal one.
There are \(m\geq1\) resource kinds, nonnegative binary-rational capacities \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^m\), and an explicit catalogue \(B\subseteq\{0,1\}^m\) containing the empty bundle \(0\). Each bundle consumes its indicated resources per unit mass. Valuations \(v_\rho(b)\in\mathbb{Q}\) are explicitly given, may have either sign, and satisfy \(v_\rho(0)=0\).
For every real coalition submass \(0\leq z\leq\mu\), define \(w(z)=\max\{\sum_{\rho,b\in B}v_\rho(b)x_{\rho b}:x\geq0,\ \sum_{b\in B}x_{\rho b}\leq z_\rho,\ \sum_{\rho,b\in B}b_jx_{\rho b}\leq q_j\ (1\leq j\leq m)\}\). Mass may split among bundles; coalition members may change their allocation roles.
The input includes a fixed efficient allocation: each role \(\rho\) receives bundle \(b_\rho\in B\), with \(\sum_\rho\mu_\rho b_{\rho j}\leq q_j\) and \(\sum_\rho\mu_\rho v_\rho(b_\rho)=w(\mu)\). Any efficient allocation may be supplied when there are ties.
A feasible utility profile assigns a constant \(u_\rho\geq0\) per unit mass of each role, with \(u_\rho=0\) when \(b_\rho=0\), and satisfies \(\sum_\rho(\mu_\rho-z_\rho)u_\rho\leq w(\mu)-w(z)\) for every real \(0\leq z\leq\mu\). Payments per unit mass are \(p_\rho=v_\rho(b_\rho)-u_\rho\); the seller receives \(\sum_\rho\mu_\rho p_\rho\).
Weighted leximin compares the nondecreasing utility step functions on \([0,T)\), where value \(u_\rho\) has interval length \(\mu_\rho\), preferring the larger value on the first interval where they differ. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is computing an exact weighted-leximin-maximal feasible profile \(u\) and its payments \(p\) polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,|B|,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete BLO computation requires at most \(\frac{|W|(|W|+1)}{2}+1\) winner-determination oracle queries and \(O(|W|^5)\) additional time, where \(W\) is the winner set; this is an oracle-conditional algorithmic guarantee ([Cheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06443v1)).
No result settling this precise continuous mass problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The mass normalization was unspecified; positive rational masses have arbitrary total mass, with capacities measured in the same mass units.
  • Bundle configurations and capacity consumption were unspecified; configurations are incidence vectors of bundles of resource kinds, and consumption scales linearly with allocated mass.
  • Coalition winner determination was unspecified; coalition mass can split among catalogue bundles, receives at most one bundle per unit mass, and may change its original allocation role.
  • The efficient aggregate allocation did not specify homogeneous payment roles; each support type includes its assigned bundle, splitting a valuation type into separate roles when necessary.
  • Valuation signs were unspecified; signed rational valuations are allowed, following the real-valued discrete domain, with empty-bundle valuation zero.
  • The omitted empty-allocation payment condition is restored by requiring zero utility and hence zero payment for empty-bundle roles.
  • Leximin with rational multiplicities was undefined; it compares sorted utility step functions with interval lengths equal to masses.
  • Allocation tie-breaking was unspecified; the supplied efficient allocation is fixed, and any optimum may be supplied.
  • The computational access model was unspecified; the continuous input is explicit and running time counts all computation, without a free winner-determination oracle.

Result

Computing the exact weighted-leximin-maximal feasible profile and its payments is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,|B|,L\). The proof gives a polynomial-size extended LP formulation of feasibility and an exact algorithm using \(O(\tau^2)\) LP solves.

All claims below are proved for the stated model, including signed valuations, zero capacities, arbitrary positive masses, and allocation ties. If \(\tau=0\), the output is the empty profile; assume henceforth that \(\tau>0\).

1. A finite characterization of feasibility

Write \(W=w(\mu)=\sum_\rho\mu_\rho v_\rho(b_\rho)\), whose exact rational value is available from the supplied efficient allocation.

Let \(X\) be the grand-coalition allocation polytope:

\(X=\{x\geq0:\sum_{b\in B}x_{\rho b}\leq\mu_\rho\ \text{for every }\rho,\ \sum_{\rho,b\in B}b_jx_{\rho b}\leq q_j\ \text{for every }j\}\).

This polytope is nonempty and compact: it contains zero, and every coordinate satisfies \(x_{\rho b}\leq\mu_\rho\).

For any \(u\geq0\), define

\(H(u)=\max_{x\in X}\sum_{\rho,b\in B}(v_\rho(b)-u_\rho)x_{\rho b}\).

Lemma 1. For every \(u\geq0\),

\(\max_{0\leq z\leq\mu}\bigl(w(z)-z\cdot u\bigr)=H(u)\).

Proof. If \(x\) is feasible for coalition \(z\), then its allocated role mass \(y_\rho=\sum_bx_{\rho b}\) satisfies \(y\leq z\). Consequently,

\(\sum_{\rho,b}v_\rho(b)x_{\rho b}-z\cdot u\leq\sum_{\rho,b}(v_\rho(b)-u_\rho)x_{\rho b}\leq H(u)\),

using \(u\geq0\). Maximizing over allocations feasible for \(z\) proves one inequality.

Conversely, for any \(x\in X\), choose \(z_\rho=\sum_bx_{\rho b}\). Then \(0\leq z\leq\mu\), and \(x\) is feasible for this coalition. Thus

\(w(z)-z\cdot u\geq\sum_{\rho,b}(v_\rho(b)-u_\rho)x_{\rho b}\).

Taking an optimizer of \(H(u)\) proves the reverse inequality and attainment. \(\square\)

The coalition inequalities therefore hold exactly when

\(H(u)\leq W-\mu\cdot u\).

The supplied allocation defines \(x^0_{\rho b_\rho}=\mu_\rho\), with all other entries zero. This includes allocations of the empty bundle. Since \(x^0\in X\),

\(H(u)\geq\sum_\rho\mu_\rho(v_\rho(b_\rho)-u_\rho)=W-\mu\cdot u\).

Hence the coalition inequalities are equivalent to \(H(u)=W-\mu\cdot u\).

The dual LP defining \(H(u)\) has nonnegative variables \(a_\rho\) and \(\lambda_j\), constraints

\(a_\rho+\sum_jb_j\lambda_j\geq v_\rho(b)-u_\rho\quad(\rho,\ b\in B)\),

and objective \(\min(\mu\cdot a+q\cdot\lambda)\). Strong LP duality applies because the primal is feasible and has a finite attained optimum.

Set \(s=a+u\). We obtain the following characterization.

Lemma 2. For \(u\geq0\), all coalition inequalities hold if and only if there exist \(s\) and \(\lambda\) satisfying

  • \(s_\rho\geq u_\rho\) for every \(\rho\), and \(\lambda_j\geq0\) for every \(j\);
  • \(s_\rho+\sum_jb_j\lambda_j\geq v_\rho(b)\) for every \(\rho,b\);
  • \(\mu\cdot s+q\cdot\lambda=W\).

Proof. If the coalition inequalities hold, an optimal dual solution for \(H(u)\) gives these conditions after substituting \(s=a+u\).

Conversely, these conditions make \(a=s-u\geq0\) dual-feasible for \(H(u)\), with objective \(W-\mu\cdot u\). Weak duality gives \(H(u)\leq W-\mu\cdot u\), which implies all coalition inequalities by Lemma 1. \(\square\)

The required zero utilities for empty allocation roles follow automatically. Indeed, any \(s,\lambda\) in Lemma 2 satisfy

\(0=\mu\cdot s+q\cdot\lambda-W=\sum_\rho\mu_\rho\bigl(s_\rho+b_\rho\cdot\lambda-v_\rho(b_\rho)\bigr)+\lambda\cdot\bigl(q-\sum_\rho\mu_\rho b_\rho\bigr)\).

Every summand is nonnegative. Since every \(\mu_\rho>0\), each role slack is zero. When \(b_\rho=0\), this gives \(s_\rho=0\), and therefore \(u_\rho=0\).

Thus Lemma 2, together with \(u\geq0\), is an exact extended formulation of the feasible utility set \(P\). It has \(2\tau+m\) variables and \(\tau|B|+O(\tau+m)\) constraints.

2. Compactness and convexity

The set \(P\) is convex, either directly from its coalition inequalities or from its extended formulation.

It is nonempty: \(u=0\) satisfies every coalition inequality because coalition allocation sets are contained in \(X\), so \(w(z)\leq W\).

Taking \(z=0\), for which \(w(0)=0\), gives

\(\mu\cdot u\leq W\).

Since utilities are nonnegative and masses positive, \(0\leq u_\rho\leq W/\mu_\rho\). Finally, \(P\) is closed because its original definition is an intersection of closed halfspaces and coordinate constraints. Therefore \(P\) is compact.

3. Exact progressive freezing

The following procedure uses LPs over the extended formulation.

Initially set \(P^{(0)}=P\) and \(R_0=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\). At stage \(k\), with \(R_{k-1}\neq\varnothing\):

  • Compute \(t_k=\max_{u\in P^{(k-1)}}\min_{\rho\in R_{k-1}}u_\rho\). This is an LP: introduce \(t\geq0\), impose \(u_\rho\geq t\) for all remaining roles, and maximize \(t\).
  • Define \(P^{(k)}=\{u\in P^{(k-1)}:u_\rho\geq t_k\text{ for all }\rho\in R_{k-1}\}\).
  • For each remaining role, compute \(M_\rho=\max_{u\in P^{(k)}}u_\rho\). Freeze the set \(I_k=\{\rho\in R_{k-1}:M_\rho=t_k\}\), and set \(R_k=R_{k-1}\setminus I_k\).

All maxima exist by compactness.

Lemma 3. Each stage freezes at least one role. If roles remain afterward, then \(t_{k+1}>t_k\).

Proof. If \(I_k\) were empty, every remaining role would have a witness in \(P^{(k)}\) giving it utility strictly greater than \(t_k\). Averaging these finitely many witnesses preserves feasibility by convexity. All remaining coordinates are at least \(t_k\) in every witness, and each is strictly greater in its own witness. Their average therefore makes every remaining coordinate strictly greater than \(t_k\), contradicting the definition of \(t_k\).

For every role in \(I_k\), its utility equals \(t_k\) throughout \(P^{(k)}\). If \(R_k\neq\varnothing\), the same averaging argument, restricted to witnesses for roles in \(R_k\), produces a point of \(P^{(k)}\) making all these roles strictly greater than \(t_k\). Therefore the next maximum minimum is strictly greater than \(t_k\). \(\square\)

The procedure terminates after \(h\leq\tau\) stages. Its final set consists of the single profile \(u^*\) defined by \(u^*_\rho=t_k\) for \(\rho\in I_k\).

4. Correctness for the mass-weighted step-function order

Let \(Q_u\) denote the nondecreasing utility step function with role interval lengths \(\mu_\rho\).

Take any feasible \(v\neq u^*\). Because the final set is a singleton, there is a first stage \(k\) with \(v\in P^{(k-1)}\) but \(v\notin P^{(k)}\). Thus

\(d=\min_{\rho\in R_{k-1}}v_\rho<t_k\).

All previously frozen roles have exactly the same utilities in \(v\) and \(u^*\). For \(k>1\), the remaining coordinates of \(v\) are at least \(t_{k-1}\), while all previously frozen utilities are at most \(t_{k-1}\). For \(k=1\), there are no previously frozen roles.

Let \(M\) be the total mass of the previously frozen roles. The two sorted step functions agree on \([0,M)\): all those roles precede the remaining roles, allowing ties.

At least one remaining role has utility \(d\) in \(v\). Its total mass, together with all remaining roles at this value, is some \(\delta>0\). Hence \(Q_v=d\) on \([M,M+\delta)\). Every remaining role in \(u^*\) has utility at least \(t_k>d\), so \(Q_{u^*}\geq t_k\) on this interval.

The first interval on which the functions differ therefore favors \(u^*\). This proves that \(u^*\) is the unique weighted-leximin-maximal feasible profile. The proof uses the actual positive masses and does not expand them into individual agents.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

There are at most \(\tau\) stages and at most \(\tau(\tau+1)/2\) coordinate-maximization LPs, hence at most \(\tau(\tau+3)/2\) LP solves altogether.

It remains to bound the encoding lengths of the successively computed levels. A polynomial number of LP calls alone would not establish polynomial bit time.

Consider the final partition \(I_1,\ldots,I_h\). Construct a rational polyhedron \(Q\) from the extended formulation in Lemma 2 by adjoining variables \(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_h\) and constraints

\(0\leq\eta_1\leq\cdots\leq\eta_h,\qquad u_\rho=\eta_k\quad(\rho\in I_k)\).

This formulation contains only original input coefficients, the rational number \(W\), and constants \(0,1,-1\). It has at most \(3\tau+m\) variables and polynomial encoding length.

The vector \((t_1,\ldots,t_h)\) is lexicographically maximal among the level vectors feasible in \(Q\). To verify this, suppose a feasible level vector were lexicographically larger, first differing at coordinate \(k\). Previously frozen utilities would equal their computed values, while every role still active at stage \(k\) would have utility at least \(\eta_k>t_k\). That point would belong to \(P^{(k-1)}\) and contradict the definition of \(t_k\).

Consequently, the points in \(Q\) with this optimal level vector form a nonempty face, obtained by successively maximizing the level coordinates. All variables of \(Q\) are nonnegative, so \(Q\) contains no line. A nonempty face of such a polyhedron contains a vertex, which is also a vertex of \(Q\).

At that vertex, all levels are coordinates of the solution to a nonsingular system of tight constraints from the original-size formulation. After clearing row denominators, suppose its integer coefficients and right-hand sides have absolute value at most \(2^H\), with \(H\) polynomial in the input length. For a system of dimension \(N\leq3\tau+m\), Cramer’s rule bounds numerator and denominator magnitudes by \(N!2^{NH}\). Thus every \(t_k\) has polynomial binary encoding length.

Each algorithmic LP therefore has polynomially many variables and constraints and polynomial-length rational data, including its previously computed levels. Exact rational LP optimization takes polynomial bit time; the standard exact-solvability theorem is reviewed in [Gleixner and Steffy, Section 1.3](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/7507.pdf). Coordinate maxima can consequently be compared with \(t_k\) exactly.

This establishes polynomial running time in \(m,\tau,|B|,L\).

6. Payments and conclusion

Once \(u^*\) is computed, calculate each payment exactly as \(p_\rho=v_\rho(b_\rho)-u^*_\rho\). These subtractions take polynomial bit time and produce polynomial-length rational outputs.

The algorithm settles the stated continuous-mass problem using explicit rational LPs. Its guarantee is unconditional for the given explicit catalogue and splittable allocations.
