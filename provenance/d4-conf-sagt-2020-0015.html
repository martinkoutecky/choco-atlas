The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of complete weighted-OXS valuation types, rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), item species \(O\), and rational per-capita supplies \(b_o\), choose \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) for integral bundles \(S\subseteq O\) satisfying \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:o\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_o\). First maximize \(p=\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\); among allocations attaining \(p\), compute one maximizing \(\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A weighted-OXS valuation is \(v_t(S)\), the maximum weight of a matching between the items in \(S\) and a finite member set \(M_t\), with nonnegative rational edge weights.

Given types \(T\) of masses \(\mu_t\), species \(O\) with supplies \(b_o\), choose \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) for \(S\subseteq O\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:o\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_o\). Lexicographically maximize \(p=\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\), then \(H=\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S)\).

Target. Is computing such a lexicographic optimum polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard?

Nearest known results. For two discrete agents with general assignment valuations, computing a leximin allocation or the positive-utility-refined MNW allocation is NP-hard.
No result for the mass-allocation problem above is assumed here.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Computing an exact lexicographic optimum is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with one type, an additive valuation, unit mass, and supply \(\tfrac12\) for every species.

Reduction. We reduce from PARTITION: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), decide whether some subset sums to half their total. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; restricting to \(n\ge2\) removes only trivial instances.

Write \(A=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\). Construct one type of mass \(\mu=1\), species set \(O=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), supplies \(b_i=\tfrac12\), and valuation

\(v(S)=\sum_{i\in S}w_i,\qquad w_i=\frac{2a_i}{A}.\)

This is a weighted-OXS valuation: introduce members \(m_1,\ldots,m_n\), give edge \((i,m_i)\) weight \(w_i\), and give all other edges weight zero. The diagonal edges for items in \(S\) form a matching of weight \(v(S)\), and no matching can obtain more.

The construction has polynomial encoding length. All weights are positive rational numbers, and \(\sum_i w_i=2\). In particular, \(v(S)>0\) exactly when \(S\ne\varnothing\).

First objective. Suppress the type index and write \(x_S\) for the allocation variables. Since their total mass is one,

\(p=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}x_S=1-x_{\varnothing}\le1.\)

Assigning mass \(\tfrac12\) to each of \(\{1\}\) and \(O\setminus\{1\}\) is feasible: both bundles are nonempty, and every item is consumed with mass exactly \(\tfrac12\). Thus \(p^\star=1\).

Consequently, the second-stage optimization is over feasible allocations satisfying \(x_{\varnothing}=0\).

Upper bound and equality condition. Fix any such allocation. Its average valuation satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\ne\varnothing}x_Sv(S) =\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\sum_{S:i\in S}x_S \le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n w_i =1. \)

For every positive real \(z\),

\(\log z\le z-1,\)

with equality exactly when \(z=1\). Indeed, the derivative of \(z-1-\log z\) is \(1-1/z\), so this function has its unique minimum, zero, at one.

Because the nonempty bundles have total mass one, it follows that

\(\displaystyle H=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}x_S\log v(S) \le\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}x_S\bigl(v(S)-1\bigr) \le0. \)

Moreover, if \(H=0\), both inequalities must be equalities. The first inequality has slack

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\ne\varnothing}x_S\bigl(v(S)-1-\log v(S)\bigr). \)

Every summand is nonnegative. Thus zero slack forces \(v(S)=1\) for every bundle with \(x_S>0\).

Equivalence with PARTITION. Suppose the PARTITION instance is a yes-instance, witnessed by \(R\subseteq O\) with \(\sum_{i\in R}a_i=A/2\). Then

\(v(R)=v(O\setminus R)=1.\)

Assigning mass \(\tfrac12\) to each of these complementary bundles is feasible and attains \((p,H)=(1,0)\). The established upper bounds show that this is lexicographically optimal.

Conversely, suppose a feasible allocation attains \((p,H)=(1,0)\). There is at least one bundle \(S\) with positive mass. The equality condition gives

\(\displaystyle 1=v(S)=\frac{2}{A}\sum_{i\in S}a_i, \)

so \(S\) witnesses a yes-instance of PARTITION.

An optimum exists: the feasible region has finitely many coordinates and is compact; its nonempty face defined by \(p=1\) is compact; and \(H\) is linear in those coordinates with finite coefficients. Therefore,

\(\text{PARTITION is yes}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(p^\star,H^\star)=(1,0).\)

Computational conclusion. Deciding whether the optimal second-stage value is zero is therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The threshold is the rational number zero, so the reduction does not require encoding a transcendental threshold.

Computing an optimal allocation in the usual explicit support representation also decides PARTITION without evaluating logarithms: select any positive-mass bundle in the returned optimum and check whether its integer weights sum to \(A/2\). In a yes-instance every supported bundle passes this check; in a no-instance no bundle can pass.

Hence exact computation of the requested lexicographic optimum is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard already in the stated additive special case. This reduction does not assert strong hardness.
