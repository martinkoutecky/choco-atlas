The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many valuation types θ with rational masses μ_θ summing to \(1\), finitely many good types, dates, rational per-date supplies, and a fixed rational α_0>0, decide whether goods can be assigned so that each person receives an integral bundle history and every prefix is α_0-TEFX. Equivalently, choose nonnegative masses \(x_{θ,h}\) over type-history pairs, meeting each type mass and good supply, such that for every two positive-mass pairs \((θ,h)\), \((θ',h')\), every prefix \(t\), and every good \(g\) in \(h'_{≤ t}\), \(v_θ(h_{≤ t}) ≥ α_0[v_θ(h'_{≤ t})-v_θ(g)]\). The optimization version asks for the largest feasible α.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) good types and \(T\geq1\) dates, with binary-rational supplies \(s_{g,t}\geq0\) and nonnegative binary-rational additive singleton values \(v_\theta(g)\). All supplies and valuations are known in advance.
An integral history \(h=(h_{g,t})\) has \(h_{g,t}\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\), counting copies received on their arrival date; goods cannot be delayed, discarded, or reassigned. Write \(h_{\leq t}\) for its cumulative multiset through date \(t\).
An allocation assigns nonnegative real masses \(x_{\theta,h}\) to type-history pairs, satisfying \(\sum_hx_{\theta,h}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,h}x_{\theta,h}h_{g,t}=s_{g,t}\) for every \(g,t\).
For \(\alpha\in(0,1]\), it is \(\alpha\)-TEFX if, for every two positive-mass pairs \((\theta,h),(\theta',h')\), every date \(t\), and every individual copy \(g\in h'_{\leq t}\), \(v_\theta(h_{\leq t})\geq\alpha\bigl(v_\theta(h'_{\leq t})-v_\theta(g)\bigr)\). Zero-valued copies are included; an empty envied prefix imposes no condition.
State any running times in \(m,\tau,T,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length.

Does some universal constant \(c\in(0,1]\) guarantee a \(c\)-TEFX allocation for every such mass instance, or does every such \(c\) have an instance admitting no \(c\)-TEFX allocation?

Discrete societies admit no universal positive constant TEFX guarantee, even with two agents having identical valuations. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12835v3#S4.SS3.SSS1)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the fairness factor to (0,1], as required by the discrete approximation definition.
  • Specified nonnegative rational additive valuations and nonnegative rational supplies; supplies measure copies per unit society mass.
  • Required exact allocation of every date's supply without scheduling, disposal, or reassignment, matching the anchor's setting.
  • Interpreted histories as integer multisets with unrestricted multiplicities, since no per-person quota is specified.
  • Allowed real allocation masses over the countable set of finite integer histories, with the displayed sums defining feasibility.
  • Included zero-valued goods in the removal quantifier and made the condition vacuous for empty envied prefixes.

Result

Every constant \(c\in(0,1]\) has an instance admitting no \(c\)-TEFX allocation. This holds with \(m=2\), \(\tau=1\), \(T=2\), and strictly positive singleton values.

We prove the stronger statement that the following family has optimal TEFX factor exactly \(1/M\).

Construction

Fix an integer \(M\geq2\). There is one valuation type, of mass \(1\), and two good types \(a,b\), with
\(v(a)=1\) and \(v(b)=M\).

The supplies are
\(s_{a,1}=1\), \(s_{b,1}=0\), \(s_{a,2}=0\), and \(s_{b,2}=1/2\).

All data are binary-rational. Write \(x_h\) for the mass assigned to history \(h\). Thus \(\sum_h x_h=1\).

Because every supply constraint sums nonnegative terms, the zero supplies force \(h_{b,1}=h_{a,2}=0\) for every positive-mass history. Define
\(n(h)=h_{a,1}\) and \(r(h)=h_{b,2}\).
The remaining constraints are
\(\sum_h x_h n(h)=1\) and \(\sum_h x_h r(h)=1/2\).

Necessity: every attainable factor satisfies \(\alpha\leq1/M\)

Assume an allocation is \(\alpha\)-TEFX for some \(\alpha>0\).

First, every positive-mass history receives exactly one copy of \(a\) at date \(1\).

Suppose a positive-mass history \(h^0\) has \(n(h^0)=0\). If another positive-mass history \(h\) had \(n(h)\geq2\), comparing \(h^0\) with \(h\) at date \(1\), and removing one copy of \(a\), would require
\(0\geq\alpha(n(h)-1)>0\), a contradiction.

Consequently, all positive-mass histories would have \(n(h)\in\{0,1\}\). Let
\(q=\sum_{h:n(h)=0}x_h\).
Our supposition gives \(q>0\), so
\(\sum_h x_h n(h)=1-q<1\), contradicting the supply constraint.

Thus \(n(h)\geq1\) on every positive-mass history. Since their total mass and average count are both \(1\),
\(\sum_h x_h(n(h)-1)=0\).
Every summand is nonnegative, so \(n(h)=1\) whenever \(x_h>0\).

Second, positive mass both receives and does not receive \(b\).

Let \(p=\sum_{h:r(h)\geq1}x_h\). Integrality gives
\(p\leq\sum_h x_h r(h)=1/2\).
The positive supply also implies \(p>0\). Hence there exist positive-mass histories \(h^-\) and \(h^+\) with
\(r(h^-)=0\) and \(r(h^+)\geq1\).

At date \(2\), history \(h^-\) has value \(1\). History \(h^+\) contains its previously received copy of \(a\) and at least one copy of \(b\). Applying TEFX after removing that copy of \(a\) requires
\(1\geq\alpha M r(h^+)\geq\alpha M\).

Therefore \(\alpha\leq1/M\). This argument permits arbitrary splitting into positive real masses.

Sufficiency: the factor \(1/M\) is attained

Assign mass \(1/2\) to each of two histories:

  • \(h^-\): one copy of \(a\) at date \(1\), and nothing at date \(2\).
  • \(h^+\): one copy of \(a\) at date \(1\), and one copy of \(b\) at date \(2\).

These masses satisfy every supply constraint.

At date \(1\), removing the sole copy from either envied prefix leaves value \(0\). At date \(2\), removing any copy from either envied prefix leaves value at most \(M\): the possible residual values are \(0\), \(1\), and \(M\). Every own prefix has value at least \(1\). Thus, whenever \(\alpha\leq1/M\),
\(v(h_{\leq2})\geq1\geq\alpha M\),
which verifies every required comparison.

The optimal factor for this instance is therefore exactly \(1/M\).

Conclusion

Given any \(c\in(0,1]\), choose an integer \(M>1/c\). The construction then admits no \(c\)-TEFX allocation, since necessity would require \(c\leq1/M<c\).

Thus allowing divisible population masses does not yield a universal positive TEFX guarantee.
