The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rooted unweighted tree \(G=(V,E)\) with hub \(h\), \(n\) couriers, and a rational distribution \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}^{T}_{\ge 0}\) over \(T=V\setminus\{h\}\), where \(x_{it}\ge 0\) and \(\sum_i x_{it}=\mu_t\), compute a feasible mass allocation that is MMS and Pareto optimal under \(c_i(x)=\sum_{e\in E}\mathbf{1}[\sum_{t\in D_e}x_{it}>0]\), with \(M_\infty=\min_y\max_i c_i(y)\), requiring \(c_i(x)\le M_\infty\) for all \(i\) and no feasible \(y\) with \(c_i(y)\le c_i(x)\) for every \(i\), strictly for one.

Polynomial-Time Solvability

1. Main theorem

Let \(m=|V\setminus\{h\}|=|E|\). The least integer \(B\) for which the required distribution exists is computable in time polynomial in \(|V|\) and in the binary encoding length of the rational demands.

Thus the distribution-over-bundles problem is polynomial-time solvable. The supplied NP-hardness result for finite MMS allocations does not transfer to this convexified formulation.

2. Threshold feasibility as convex-hull membership

Fix an integer \(B\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\). Define

\[ X_B=\left\{\chi^S\in\{0,1\}^{V\setminus\{h\}}:c(S)\leq B\right\} \]

and let \(P_B=\operatorname{conv}(X_B)\).

Lemma 1

There exists a distribution supported on bundles of cost at most \(B\) with marginals \(d\) if and only if \(d\in P_B\).

Proof

If such a distribution exists, then

\[ d=\sum_{S:c(S)\leq B}\lambda_S\chi^S, \qquad \lambda_S\geq0, \qquad \sum_S\lambda_S=1. \]

Hence \(d\) is a convex combination of points in \(X_B\).

Conversely, any representation of \(d\) as a convex combination of points in \(X_B\) gives coefficients \(\lambda_S\) satisfying the normalization, nonnegativity, support, and marginal conditions. ∎

It therefore suffices to decide exact membership in \(P_B\).

3. A separation formulation

Because \(P_B\) is a compact polytope, the strict separating-hyperplane theorem gives the following characterization.

Lemma 2

The point \(d\) does not belong to \(P_B\) if and only if there exists \(w\in\mathbb{R}^{V\setminus\{h\}}\) such that

\[ (d-x)\mathbin{\cdot}w\geq1 \quad\text{for every }x\in X_B. \]

Proof

Suppose first that such a \(w\) exists. If \(d=\sum_x\lambda_xx\) were in \(P_B\), then

\[ 0 = \left(d-\sum_x\lambda_xx\right)\mathbin{\cdot}w = \sum_x\lambda_x(d-x)\mathbin{\cdot}w \geq \sum_x\lambda_x = 1, \]

a contradiction.

Conversely, suppose \(d\notin P_B\). Strict separation gives some \(w_0\) satisfying

\[ \delta = w_0\mathbin{\cdot}d-\max_{x\in X_B}w_0\mathbin{\cdot}x > 0. \]

Setting \(w=w_0/\delta\) gives \((d-x)\mathbin{\cdot}w\geq1\) for every \(x\in X_B\). ∎

Let \(Q_B\) denote the system in Lemma 2. Given a candidate \(w\), its most violated constraint is obtained by solving

\[ \max_{x\in X_B}w\mathbin{\cdot}x. \]

Indeed, if \(x^*\) is a maximizer, then

\[ \min_{x\in X_B}(d-x)\mathbin{\cdot}w = d\mathbin{\cdot}w-w\mathbin{\cdot}x^*. \]

Thus linear optimization over \(X_B\) supplies a separation oracle for \(Q_B\).

4. Linear optimization over feasible bundles

Fix arbitrary rational weights \(w_v\), which may be positive or negative. Put

\[ p_v=\max\{w_v,0\} \]

for \(v\neq h\), and put \(p_h=0\).

A rooted subtree means a connected subgraph \(H\) of \(G\) containing \(h\).

Lemma 3

For every \(B\),

\[ \max_{S:c(S)\leq B}\sum_{v\in S}w_v = \max_{\substack{H\text{ rooted subtree}\\ |E(H)|\leq B}} \sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{h\}}p_v. \]

Proof

Take any feasible bundle \(S\), and let \(H=G|S\). Then \(H\) is a rooted subtree with at most \(B\) edges. Moreover,

\[ \sum_{v\in S}w_v \leq \sum_{v\in S}p_v \leq \sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{h\}}p_v. \]

This proves that the left-hand optimum is at most the right-hand optimum.

Conversely, take any rooted subtree \(H\) with at most \(B\) edges and define

\[ S_H=\{v\in V(H)\setminus\{h\}:w_v>0\}. \]

The minimal rooted subtree connecting \(S_H\) is contained in \(H\), so \(c(S_H)\leq|E(H)|\leq B\). Furthermore,

\[ \sum_{v\in S_H}w_v = \sum_{v\in V(H)\setminus\{h\}}p_v. \]

Thus every value on the right is attained by a feasible bundle on the left. ∎

Dynamic program

For a vertex \(v\), let \(G_v\) be the descendant subtree rooted at \(v\). Define \(D_v(k)\) to be the maximum total prize of a connected subtree of \(G_v\) that contains \(v\) and uses exactly \(k\) edges. Infeasible states have value \(-\infty\).

Suppose that \(v\) has children \(u_1,\ldots,u_t\). Initialize

\[ A_0(0)=p_v, \qquad A_0(k)=-\infty\text{ for }k>0. \]

After processing children \(u_1,\ldots,u_i\), use the recurrence

\[ A_i(k) = \max\left\{ A_{i-1}(k), \max_{0\leq q\leq k-1} \left( A_{i-1}(k-1-q)+D_{u_i}(q) \right) \right\}. \]

The first alternative omits the subtree rooted at \(u_i\). The second includes the edge \(vu_i\), using one edge for \(vu_i\) and \(q\) edges below \(u_i\). After all children have been processed, set \(D_v(k)=A_t(k)\).

Every connected subtree containing \(v\) decomposes uniquely according to these child choices, so induction on the descendant subtree proves the recurrence. The desired optimum is

\[ \max_{0\leq k\leq B}D_h(k). \]

Storing the maximizing choices permits reconstruction of an optimal subtree and hence, by Lemma 3, an optimal bundle \(S_H\).

There are \(|E|=m\) child-merging operations. Each takes \(O(B^2)\) exact rational operations. Thus the oracle runs in

\[ O(|V|B^2) \]

arithmetic operations, and therefore in polynomial time because \(B\leq m\). The bit lengths remain polynomial because every finite DP value is a sum of at most \(|V|\) input prizes.

5. Exact feasibility and bit complexity

The system \(Q_B\) has exponentially many inequalities, but the preceding dynamic program is an exact polynomial-time separation oracle.

For completeness, the ellipsoid method can be applied with polynomially bounded coordinates. Write \(d_i=a_i/q_i\) and let \(Q=\prod_iq_i\). Its binary encoding length is at most the total encoding length of the demands. Multiplying every constraint of \(Q_B\) by \(Q\) gives integer coefficients of absolute value at most \(Q\) and right-hand side \(Q\).

If \(Q_B\) is feasible, minimize \(z\) subject to its constraints and \(-z\leq w_i\leq z\). This linear program has an optimal basic feasible solution. By Cramer's rule, one may choose such a solution with

\[ |w_i|\leq (m+1)!\,Q^{m+1}. \]

Consequently, a bounding box for the ellipsoid method has polynomial binary encoding length. Standard exact rational ellipsoid feasibility, using the dynamic program as its separation oracle, therefore decides whether \(Q_B\) is feasible in polynomial time.

By Lemma 2:

  • if \(Q_B\) is feasible, then no required distribution exists at threshold \(B\);
  • if \(Q_B\) is infeasible, then \(d\in P_B\), and the required distribution exists.

6. Computing the minimum threshold

The families are nested:

\[ X_B\subseteq X_{B+1}, \qquad P_B\subseteq P_{B+1}. \]

Hence threshold feasibility is monotone in \(B\).

Moreover, \(B=m\) is always feasible: every bundle uses at most all \(m\) tree edges, so \(X_m=\{0,1\}^{V\setminus\{h\}}\) and \(P_m=[0,1]^{V\setminus\{h\}}\), which contains \(d\).

Binary search over \(B\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\), using the exact threshold-feasibility algorithm above, returns the least feasible \(B\) in polynomial time.

7. Relation to finite MMS hardness

A finite MMS allocation must choose a single integral partition among the agents, coupling all bundles simultaneously. Here the variables \(\lambda_S\) form an arbitrary convex combination of integral bundles, and only their vertex marginals are prescribed. This removes the discrete partition-selection obstruction.

Although the convex-hull description has exponentially many bundle configurations, its pricing problem on an unweighted tree is precisely the polynomial dynamic program above. Therefore the known finite-agent MMS hardness does not imply hardness of the present formulation.

Step-by-step audit

1. Convex-hull formulation

Lemma 1 is correct.

A distribution supported on bundles satisfying \(c(S)\leq B\) is exactly a convex combination of their incidence vectors. Conversely, every such convex combination provides nonnegative coefficients summing to one and having the prescribed marginals.

There are finitely many bundles, so no measure-theoretic or infinite-support issue arises.

2. Normalized separating system

Lemma 2 is correct.

If \(d\notin P_B\), strict separation applies because \(P_B\) is a nonempty compact convex polytope. It yields a positive separation gap

\[ \delta=w_0\mathbin{\cdot}d-\max_{x\in X_B}w_0\mathbin{\cdot}x>0. \]

Scaling by \(1/\delta\) produces the asserted inequalities. The reverse implication follows correctly by averaging the inequalities over a hypothetical convex representation of \(d\).

The signs and normalization are consistent.

3. Separation oracle for \(Q_B\)

For fixed \(w\), the least left-hand side among the inequalities defining \(Q_B\) is

\[ d\mathbin{\cdot}w-\max_{x\in X_B}w\mathbin{\cdot}x. \]

Thus maximizing \(w\mathbin{\cdot}x\) identifies a violated constraint whenever one exists. This correctly gives an exact strong separation oracle once the maximization problem is solved exactly.

4. Reduction to a prize-collecting rooted subtree

Lemma 3 is correct, including for negative and zero weights.

For a feasible bundle \(S\), its connecting subtree \(G|S\) may contain additional vertices. Replacing each weight by its positive part can only increase the objective, giving the first inequality.

Conversely, for a rooted subtree \(H\), selecting precisely its positive-weight vertices produces a bundle whose connecting subtree is contained in \(H\). Zero-weight vertices can be omitted without affecting the equality because their positive parts are zero. Hence every value on the subtree side is realized by a feasible bundle.

5. Dynamic program

The dynamic-programming state and recurrence correctly enumerate connected descendant subtrees containing the current root.

When child \(u_i\) is included, one edge is spent on \(vu_i\), and the selected portion below \(u_i\) must be a connected subtree containing \(u_i\). The index relation

\[ (k-1-q)+1+q=k \]

is correct. Omitting the child is represented by the first alternative.

The initialization also handles leaves and \(B=0\). At the hub, maximizing over \(0\leq k\leq B\) correctly permits subtrees using fewer than \(B\) edges.

There are \(O(|V|)\) child merges, each taking \(O(B^2)\) comparisons and additions. Since \(B\leq |E|\), the arithmetic running time is polynomial. Every DP entry is a sum of at most \(|V|\) oracle-input weights, so its encoding length remains polynomial in the encoding length of those weights and in \(|V|\).

6. Coordinate bound

Clearing denominators with \(Q=\prod_iq_i\) does yield integral inequality coefficients of magnitude at most \(Q\), with right-hand side \(Q\). The binary length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the denominator encoding lengths.

The displayed Cramer bound is compatible with an \((m+1)\)-variable active subsystem whose entries and right-hand sides have magnitude at most \(Q\).
