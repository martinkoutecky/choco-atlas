The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t\) over finitely many advertiser types \(t=(v_{t1},\ldots,v_{tJ},\lambda_t,\tau_t)\), and per-capita supplies \(q_j\) of slot classes, compute market-clearing prices \(p_j\) and per-advertiser type allocations \(x_{tj}\). Each type must maximize \(\sum_j v_{tj}x_{tj}\) subject to \(\sum_j p_jx_{tj}\le\lambda_t\) and \(\sum_j v_{tj}x_{tj}\ge\tau_t\sum_j p_jx_{tj}\), with allocations clearing supply, \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}=q_j\).

Proven result

The coordinated equilibrium problem as stated is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). If an equilibrium exists, the algorithm returns one with rational coordinates of polynomial encoding length.

The argument uses an auxiliary finite first-price pacing market to determine prices and multipliers, followed by a linear program that enforces the per-advertiser caps. No expansion of the masses into individual advertisers is needed.

1. Normalizing the equilibrium multipliers

Write
\(U_t=\sum_j v_{tj}x_{tj}\),
\(S_t=\sum_j p_jx_{tj}\), and
\(c_t=1/r_t\).

Every equilibrium price is positive: each class has a positive valuation, and all multipliers are positive. The bidding conditions give
\(v_{tj}\le p_j/w_t\), with equality whenever \(x_{tj}>0\). Consequently,

\(S_t=w_tU_t.\)

If type \(t\) receives a positive allocation, then \(U_t>0\). Its RoS constraint binds exactly when

\(U_t-r_tS_t=U_t(1-r_tw_t)=0,\)

which, because \(U_t>0\), is equivalent to \(w_t=c_t\). Thus a positively allocated type with a slack budget must have \(w_t=c_t\).

Now consider a type receiving nothing. Optimality of its zero allocation implies

\(v_{tj}\le r_tp_j\quad\text{for every }j.\)

Indeed, if \(v_{tj}>r_tp_j\), its coordinated bound for that class is \(1\). Taking only that class in any amount
\(0<\varepsilon\le\min\{1,\lambda_t/p_j\}\)
would be feasible and yield positive value, contradicting optimality.

For such an unallocated type, replace its multiplier by \(c_t\). Its new bids satisfy
\(c_tv_{tj}\le p_j\).
Prices remain unchanged: market clearance guarantees that every class has a positively allocated winner, whose multiplier was unchanged. Allocations, coordinated bounds, and individual optimization problems also remain unchanged.

Therefore, whenever an equilibrium exists, there is one satisfying

\[S_t<\lambda_t\ \Longrightarrow\ w_t=c_t.\tag{1}\]

Call this a normalized equilibrium.

2. Individual optimality follows from the normalized conditions

Conversely, suppose prices, multipliers, and allocations satisfy:

  • \(0<w_t\le c_t\) and \(p_j=\max_t w_tv_{tj}\);
  • \(0\le x_{tj}\le1\), market clearance, and allocation only to maximizers;
  • \(S_t\le\lambda_t\) and condition \((1)\).

These conditions imply all the remaining equilibrium requirements.

First, \(S_t=w_tU_t\) and \(w_t\le1/r_t\) imply RoS feasibility. The allocation also satisfies its coordinated bounds by their definition.

For individual optimality, there are two cases.

Binding budget. If \(S_t=\lambda_t\), every feasible alternative \(z\) satisfies

\(\sum_jv_{tj}z_j\le \frac{1}{w_t}\sum_jp_jz_j\le\frac{\lambda_t}{w_t}=U_t.\)

Thus \(x_{t\cdot}\) maximizes value.

Slack budget. If \(S_t<\lambda_t\), condition \((1)\) gives \(w_t=1/r_t\), so \(v_{tj}\le r_tp_j\) for every class. An alternative satisfying RoS must satisfy

\(\sum_j(v_{tj}-r_tp_j)z_j\ge0.\)

Every summand is nonpositive. Hence \(z_j=0\) whenever \(v_{tj}<r_tp_j\).

On every tie class \(v_{tj}=r_tp_j\), the coordinated bound equals \(x_{tj}\): this follows from the fractional-allocation rule, the full-allocation rule, and the special zero-allocation rule, respectively. Therefore any feasible alternative satisfies \(z_j\le x_{tj}\) on all classes it can receive, and cannot improve value.

In this second case \(U_t=r_tS_t\), so RoS binds. In the first case the budget binds.

We have proved that normalized equilibria are exactly the allocations satisfying the bidding, clearance, cap, budget, and no-unnecessary-pacing conditions above.

3. The auxiliary finite pacing market

Construct a market with one bidder for each support type and one divisible good of unit supply for each class. Set

\(B_t=\mu_t\lambda_t,\qquad A_{tj}=\frac{q_jv_{tj}}{r_t}.\)

We use the following known finite first-price pacing equilibrium result: with nonnegative rational valuations and positive rational budgets, an exact rational FPPE can be computed in polynomial time, and its multiplier vector is unique. An FPPE allocates goods to highest scaled bids, respects budgets, sells every positive-price good completely, and assigns multiplier \(1\) whenever a bidder’s budget is slack. These results are established by Corollary 1 and Section 5 of [Conitzer et al., *Pacing Equilibrium in First Price Auction Markets*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.07166).

The hypotheses hold here: \(B_t>0\), all \(A_{tj}\) are nonnegative rationals, and each good has a positive valuation. The constructed input has polynomial encoding length.

Denote auxiliary multipliers, prices, and allocations by \(\alpha_t,P_j,y_{tj}\). Its multipliers are positive: if \(\alpha_t=0\), any good it receives has zero price, so its spending is zero; its positive budget and the no-unnecessary-pacing condition would then force \(\alpha_t=1\), a contradiction. Consequently every auxiliary price is positive.

Given a normalized coordinated equilibrium, define

\(\alpha_t=r_tw_t,\qquad P_j=q_jp_j,\qquad y_{tj}=\frac{\mu_tx_{tj}}{q_j}.\)

Then

\(\alpha_tA_{tj}=q_jw_tv_{tj},\)

so auxiliary prices are highest bids, and allocations go only to highest bidders. Furthermore,

\(\sum_t y_{tj}=\frac{\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}}{q_j}=1,\)

and

\(\sum_jP_jy_{tj}=\mu_tS_t\le\mu_t\lambda_t=B_t.\)

If \(\alpha_t<1\), then \(w_t<c_t\), so normalization requires \(S_t=\lambda_t\). Thus the auxiliary budget binds. The transformed outcome is an auxiliary FPPE.

The original per-advertiser cap becomes

\[y_{tj}\le\frac{\mu_t}{q_j}.\tag{2}\]

Conversely, any auxiliary FPPE satisfying \((2)\) transforms back through

\(w_t=\frac{\alpha_t}{r_t},\qquad p_j=\frac{P_j}{q_j},\qquad x_{tj}=\frac{q_jy_{tj}}{\mu_t}\)

to the normalized conditions proved sufficient in Section 2.

Therefore, the original problem has an equilibrium if and only if the auxiliary market has an FPPE allocation satisfying \((2)\).

4. Testing existence by a linear program

Compute an exact auxiliary FPPE, and retain its multipliers \(\alpha^*\) and prices \(P^*\). Uniqueness of the auxiliary multiplier vector implies that every auxiliary FPPE has these same multipliers and prices.

Solve the following feasibility problem in variables \(y_{tj}\):

  • \(0\le y_{tj}\le\mu_t/q_j\) for every \(t,j\);
  • \(\sum_t y_{tj}=1\) for every \(j\);
  • \(y_{tj}=0\) whenever \(\alpha_t^*A_{tj}<P_j^*\);
  • \(\sum_jP_j^*y_{tj}\le B_t\) for every \(t\);
  • \(\sum_jP_j^*y_{tj}=B_t\) whenever \(\alpha_t^*<1\).

This is a rational linear program: the strict comparisons select constraints using already computed rational constants. Also, nonnegativity and \(\sum_t y_{tj}=1\) imply \(y_{tj}\le1\), as required for auxiliary unit-supply allocations.

If the program is feasible, return

\(x_{tj}=\frac{q_jy_{tj}}{\mu_t},\qquad w_t=\frac{\alpha_t^*}{r_t},\qquad p_j=\frac{P_j^*}{q_j}.\)

Sections 2 and 3 prove that this is an exact coordinated equilibrium.

If the program is infeasible, no coordinated equilibrium exists. Otherwise, normalizing any hypothetical equilibrium and applying the transformation in Section 3 would produce an auxiliary FPPE with multipliers \(\alpha^*\), prices \(P^*\), and an allocation feasible for this program—a contradiction.

Uniqueness is essential here: failure of one auxiliary allocation to respect the caps does not establish nonexistence; infeasibility of the program over all allocations at the uniquely determined multipliers does.

5. Complexity and exact output

The construction uses \(\tau\) bidders and \(m\) goods. Its rational coefficients are obtained by a constant number of arithmetic operations per entry, so their total encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Exact auxiliary FPPE computation takes polynomial time. Its rational output has polynomial encoding length. The subsequent linear program has \(m\tau\) variables, polynomially many constraints, and rational coefficients of polynomial encoding length. Rational linear programming therefore decides feasibility and, when feasible, returns an exact rational solution in polynomial time.

The inverse transformation preserves polynomial encoding length. If polynomial-and-isolating-interval output is required, a rational coordinate \(a/b\) is represented by the linear polynomial \(bX-a\) and a rational isolating interval. Thus the requested algebraic representation introduces no additional difficulty.

6. Existence can genuinely fail

For example, take one class and two types with

\(\mu_1=\mu_2=\tfrac12,\quad q_1=\tfrac34,\quad v_{11}=2,\quad v_{21}=1,\quad \lambda_1=\lambda_2=10,\quad r_1=r_2=1.\)

The auxiliary budgets are both \(5\), and valuations are \(3/2\) and \(3/4\). Since the entire good costs at most \(3/2\), both auxiliary budgets are slack, forcing both multipliers to equal \(1\). Type \(1\) is the unique highest bidder, so clearance requires \(y_{11}=1\). But its per-advertiser cap requires

\(y_{11}\le\frac{\mu_1}{q_1}=\frac23.\)

The feasibility program correctly reports that no coordinated equilibrium exists.

The algorithm therefore settles both exact equilibrium construction and nonexistence detection for the stated support-input problem.
