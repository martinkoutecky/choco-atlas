The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite-support buyer and seller types with rational masses, links, buyer parameters, seller capacity functions, a current aggregate transaction vector \(w\), and residual per-agent demands \(d_a\), scale buyer demands by \(\mu_a\) and seller capacities by \(\nu_j\). For a buyer type \(a\), compute a coordinatewise maximal clinch vector of transaction masses \(\xi_{aj}\) such that fixing that type’s clinch leaves unchanged every other type’s set of feasible aggregate future quantities.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) and \(J\) be nonempty buyer and seller type sets, with \(m=|J|\), and links \(E\subseteq A\times J\); write \(E_a,E_j\) for incident links and \(z(F)=\sum_{e\in F}z_e\). Goods are divisible and homogeneous.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|A|+|J|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Buyer masses \(\mu_a\) and seller masses \(\nu_j\) each sum to \(1\) on their respective side.
Each seller has a normalized, monotone submodular capacity function \(f_j:2^{E_j}\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), supplied by a Boolean circuit returning binary rational values. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including these circuits.
Define aggregate feasibility by \(P=\{z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}:z(F)\le\nu_j f_j(F)\text{ for every }j\in J,\ F\subseteq E_j\}\). The input includes a feasible rational current transaction vector \(w\in P\), residual per-agent demands \(d_b\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\cup\{\infty\}\), and a designated buyer type \(a\in A\).
Set \(R=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}:w+x\in P,\ x(E_b)\le\mu_b d_b\text{ for every }b\in A\}\), with infinite demands imposing no bound.
For \(\xi\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E_a}\), define \(Q_a(\xi)=\{u\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{A\setminus\{a\}}:\exists x\in R,\ x|_{E_a}=\xi,\ x(E_b)=u_b\text{ for every }b\ne a\}\). A clinch satisfies \(Q_a(\xi)=Q_a(0)\), preserving the joint feasible set of all other types. It is coordinatewise maximal if no distinct clinch \(\eta\ge\xi\) exists.

Is computing a coordinatewise maximal rational clinch solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many individual buyers and sellers with divisible goods, a maximal clinch is polynomial-time computable given capacity value oracles; the clinching region is a polymatroid. [Hirai–Sato](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.04881)
No separate result explicitly treating the mass-type formulation above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized buyer and seller masses separately to one, interpreting the two populations as distributions over types.
  • Defined m as the number of seller types and tau as the combined buyer and seller support size.
  • Placed each seller capacity function on its incident type links and multiplied all its capacity bounds by its seller mass.
  • Specified succinct capacity representations as Boolean circuits returning exact binary rational values, so polynomial time and NP-hardness refer to an encoded input problem.
  • Required the supplied nonnegative rational current transaction vector to satisfy the scaled seller constraints.
  • Allowed nonnegative rational residual demands and the infinity symbol, retaining the unbounded-demand states admitted by the discrete definition.
  • Interpreted preservation as equality of the joint feasible aggregate-quantity set of all other types, rather than equality of separate one-type projections.
  • Treated residual demands as supplied inputs; valuations, bids, budgets, and price clocks do not enter the single-step clinching definition once those demands are fixed.
  • Required an exact rational output and allowed any coordinatewise maximal clinch, without an additional selection or tie-breaking rule.

Result

A coordinatewise maximal rational clinch is computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

The mass-type formulation reduces exactly to a finite polymatroidal market with one buyer vertex and one seller vertex per supported type. Below, the reduction is combined with an exact rational implementation and a proof of maximality.

1. Absorb the masses into capacities and demands

For each seller \(j\), define
\[ \]
\(g_j(F):=\nu_j f_j(F)\qquad(F\subseteq E_j).\)

For each buyer \(b\), define \(D_b:=\mu_b d_b\) when \(d_b\) is finite, and \(D_b:=\infty\) otherwise.

Because \(\nu_j>0\), the function \(g_j\) is normalized, monotone, and submodular: multiplication by a positive scalar preserves each defining equality or inequality. Its values are rational and computable in polynomial time by evaluating the supplied Boolean circuit and multiplying by \(\nu_j\).

The residual feasible set is now exactly
\[ \]
\(R=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}:w(F)+x(F)\le g_j(F)\text{ for all }j,F\subseteq E_j,\quad x(E_b)\le D_b\text{ for all }b\}.\)

Thus we can treat each supported buyer type as one finite-market buyer with demand bound \(D_b\), and each supported seller type as one seller with capacity function \(g_j\). This construction preserves \(R\), every set \(Q_a(\xi)\), and consequently the clinches and their coordinatewise maximality.

The construction uses precisely \(\tau\) vertices. Writing \(N:=|E|\), we have \(N\le |A|m\le\tau m\). No expansion according to mass denominators is involved.

2. Replace infinite demands by finite bounds

Define the rational number
\[ \]
\(U:=\sum_{j\in J}g_j(E_j).\)

Every \(x\in R\) satisfies
\[ \]
\(x(E)=\sum_{j\in J}x(E_j)\le\sum_{j\in J}\bigl(g_j(E_j)-w(E_j)\bigr)\le U.\)

Here the first inequality uses seller feasibility, and the second uses \(w\ge0\).

Consequently, replacing every infinite \(D_b\) by \(U\), and every finite \(D_b\) by \(\min\{D_b,U\}\), leaves \(R\) unchanged. All resulting demand bounds are finite rationals of polynomial encoding length.

If \(U=0\), then \(R=\{0\}\), so the zero vector is the unique clinch and the problem is solved. Similarly, if \(E_a=\varnothing\), its unique vector is a maximal clinch. Assume henceforth that \(U>0\) and \(E_a\ne\varnothing\).

3. The precise clinching theorem

We use the following mathematical form of Hirai–Sato’s Theorem 5.2(ii). In a finite bipartite market with normalized monotone submodular seller capacities, feasible current transactions, and nonnegative buyer demand bounds, let \(R\) be the residual feasible set. Put \(B:=E\setminus E_a\) and
\[ \]
\(r(T):=\max_{x\in R}x(T)\qquad(T\subseteq E).\)

Then the function
\[ \]
\(h(F):=r(B\cup F)-r(B)\qquad(F\subseteq E_a)\)

is normalized, monotone, and submodular, and the vectors preserving the joint feasible set of other buyers are exactly
\[ \]
\(\mathcal C_a=\{\xi\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E_a}:\xi(F)\le h(F)\text{ for every }F\subseteq E_a\}.\)

This is the rank characterization in [Hirai–Sato, Section 5.1, Theorem 5.2(ii)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.04881). Their projected feasible set, defined in equation (20), is exactly the \(Q_a(\xi)\) in this question.

Every hypothesis holds after Sections 1–2: the graph is finite, the functions \(g_j\) have the required properties, \(w\) is feasible by assumption, and all demand bounds are nonnegative and finite. We therefore obtain the stated characterization of \(\mathcal C_a\).

It remains to show that the required values of \(r\) can be computed exactly in polynomial binary time.

4. Exact polynomial-time computation of \(r(T)\)

Separation over \(R\)

Given a rational query vector \(y\), first check its nonnegativity and all buyer demand inequalities. These checks are explicit and polynomial-time.

For each seller \(j\), minimize the set function
\[ \]
\(\phi_j(F):=g_j(F)-w(F)-y(F)\qquad(F\subseteq E_j).\)

This function is submodular because \(g_j\) is submodular and \(F\mapsto w(F)+y(F)\) is modular. Its value oracle is computable exactly in polynomial time.

Submodular function minimization supplies an exact minimizing subset \(F_j\). If \(\phi_j(F_j)<0\), the inequality
\[ \]
\(x(F_j)\le g_j(F_j)-w(F_j)\)

is valid for \(R\) and violated by \(y\), so it separates \(y\) from \(R\). If every minimum is nonnegative, all seller inequalities hold; together with the explicit checks, this proves \(y\in R\).

For this step one can use the strongly polynomial submodular minimization algorithm employing only additions, subtractions, comparisons, and value-oracle calls described by [Iwata–Orlin](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/0164efa5-50d2-44e3-bcca-f8b88c4bdbd9).

Its exact rational implementation has polynomial bit complexity here. There are polynomially many oracle values, each of polynomial bit length. A common denominator for those values has polynomial bit length, and polynomially many additions and subtractions increase integer coefficient lengths by at most a polynomial amount. No common denominator over all exponentially many subsets is needed.

Thus this procedure is a polynomial-time strong separation oracle for \(R\).

Exact optimization and encoding bounds

The polytope \(R\) is nonempty because \(0\in R\), and it is bounded because \(R\subseteq[0,U]^E\).

It also has a rational inequality description of polynomial individual inequality length. Its coefficient vectors have entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and its right-hand sides are demand bounds or numbers \(g_j(F)-w(F)\). Circuit output lengths, mass lengths, and the lengths of sums defining \(w(F)\) are polynomially bounded by the input parameters.

The separation–optimization theorem for well-described rational polyhedra therefore computes
\[ \]
\(r(T)=\max\{\mathbf 1_T^{\mathsf T}x:x\in R\}\)

exactly in polynomial time. This theorem does not require full dimensionality; see [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Chapter 6, especially Theorem 6.4.9](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4_7).

For completeness, polynomial output length also follows directly from vertex bounds. Let \(\beta\) bound the numerator and denominator bit lengths of the right-hand sides. At a vertex, select \(N\) linearly independent tight inequalities and clear each row’s denominator. Their integer entries have magnitude at most \(2^\beta\). Hadamard’s bound and Cramer’s rule give coordinate numerator and denominator lengths \(O(N(\beta+\log N))\). A linear objective attains its maximum at a vertex, so every value \(r(T)\) has polynomial encoding length.

5. Construct and verify a maximal clinch

Choose any ordering \(e_1,\ldots,e_k\) of \(E_a\), where \(k=|E_a|\le m\). Set \(S_0:=\varnothing\) and \(S_i:=\{e_1,\ldots,e_i\}\).

Compute the \(k+1\) exact rational values
\[ \]
\(q_i:=r(B\cup S_i)\qquad(0\le i\le k),\)

and output
\[ \]
\(\xi_{e_i}:=q_i-q_{i-1}=h(S_i)-h(S_{i-1}).\)

Monotonicity of \(h\) gives \(\xi\ge0\).

To prove feasibility, fix \(F\subseteq E_a\). For each \(i\) with \(e_i\in F\), the inclusion \(F\cap S_{i-1}\subseteq S_{i-1}\) and submodularity give
\[ \]
\(h(S_i)-h(S_{i-1})\le h((F\cap S_{i-1})\cup\{e_i\})-h(F\cap S_{i-1}).\)

Summing over the indices with \(e_i\in F\), the right-hand side telescopes to \(h(F)-h(\varnothing)=h(F)\). Hence \(\xi(F)\le h(F)\) for every \(F\). By Section 3, \(\xi\) is a clinch, so \(Q_a(\xi)=Q_a(0)\).

Moreover,
\[ \]
\(\xi(E_a)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(h(S_i)-h(S_{i-1}))=h(E_a).\)

If a distinct clinch \(\eta\ge\xi\) existed, at least one coordinate would be strictly larger, yielding \(\eta(E_a)>\xi(E_a)=h(E_a)\). This contradicts the defining inequality for \(\mathcal C_a\) at \(F=E_a\). Therefore \(\xi\) is coordinatewise maximal.

The construction performs at most \(m+1\) exact polynomial-time optimizations in at most \(\tau m\) variables. All output coordinates have polynomial encoding length. This proves the claimed polynomial-time solvability.
