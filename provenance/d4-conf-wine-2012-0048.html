The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given simple activities \(A\), complete preference types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), an allowed-pair relation \(E\subseteq T\times A\), and rational thresholds \(\ell_{t,a}\ge0\) for allowed pairs, decide whether there exist assignment masses \(x_{t,a}\ge0\) and activity masses \(z_a=\sum_t x_{t,a}\) such that \(\sum_a x_{t,a}=\mu_t\) for every type and \(x_{t,a}>0\Rightarrow ((t,a)\in E\land z_a\ge\ell_{t,a})\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) complete preference types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\) non-void activities \(A\), an explicitly listed relation \(E\subseteq T\times A\), and binary rational thresholds \(\ell_{t,a}\ge0\) for allowed pairs. At a positive group mass \(s\in(0,1]\), type \(t\) approves activity \(a\) exactly when \((t,a)\in E\) and \(s\ge\ell_{t,a}\). Each type is indifferent among approved alternatives, strictly prefers them to the void activity, and strictly prefers the void activity to all disapproved alternatives.
Activities are simple: each supports at most one group, and no two activities have identical approval profiles across all types and positive group masses. A type's mass may be split among activities.
A perfect individually rational assignment consists of real masses \(x_{t,a}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{a\in A}x_{t,a}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), with \(z_a=\sum_{t\in T}x_{t,a}\), and \(x_{t,a}>0\Rightarrow((t,a)\in E\land z_a\ge\ell_{t,a})\). Thus all mass must participate in non-void activities. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is deciding whether such a perfect individually rational assignment exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite indivisible agents, existence of a perfect individually rational assignment is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even with simple activities and increasing approval preferences ([published result](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~lang/papers/DEKLSW18.pdf)). No result settling this exact distribution-over-types version was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Removed zero-mass types from the input support, as required by the support convention.
  • Interpreted complete preference types as trichotomous approval preferences, with approved alternatives above the void activity and disapproved alternatives below it.
  • Made simplicity explicit as one group per activity and pairwise distinct approval profiles, following the discrete definition.
  • Specified real assignment masses and permitted splitting each type's mass among activities.
  • Measured group size by total participating mass in (0,1]; retained the graded question's nonnegative thresholds, including zero and thresholds exceeding one.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even when every allowed pair has the same positive threshold.

The reduction below handles both divisible masses and the requirement that the support list contain distinct preference types.

1. Reduction from exact cover

We use Exact Cover by 3-Sets: given a universe \(U\) with \(|U|=3q\), where \(q\ge 1\), and a family \(\mathcal S\) of three-element subsets, decide whether some subfamily partitions \(U\). This is the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete source problem also used in [Theorem 1 of the cited paper](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~lang/papers/DEKLSW18.pdf).

Delete duplicate sets; this does not change the existence of an exact cover. If the family is empty, output a fixed infeasible society consisting of one type of mass \(1\), one activity, and no allowed pairs. Henceforth assume the sets are distinct and the family is nonempty.

Construction of the support

For \(u,v\in U\), define \(u\sim v\) when they belong to exactly the same sets of \(\mathcal S\). Let \(\mathcal C\) be the resulting equivalence classes.

Create one type \(t_C\) for each \(C\in\mathcal C\), with mass

\(\displaystyle \mu_{t_C}=\frac{|C|}{3q}.\)

These masses are positive and sum to \(1\).

Create one activity \(a_S\) for each \(S\in\mathcal S\). Define

\(\displaystyle (t_C,a_S)\in E \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C\subseteq S,\)

and give every allowed pair threshold

\(\displaystyle \ell_{t_C,a_S}=\frac1q.\)

Specify the complete preferences by placing approved alternatives above the void activity and disapproved alternatives below it, with indifference within each of those two classes.

By the definition of \(\sim\), every equivalence class \(C\) is either entirely contained in \(S\) or disjoint from \(S\). Consequently, every activity has exactly the following total mass of types that allow it:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{C:\,C\subseteq S}\mu_{t_C}=\frac{|S|}{3q}=\frac1q.\)

Verification of the input requirements

Distinct equivalence classes have different incidence patterns across \(\mathcal S\). At positive group mass \(1/q\), their approval patterns therefore differ. Thus the constructed types are distinct, and the support size is \(\tau=|\mathcal C|\le 3q\).

Distinct sets also yield distinct activity profiles. Indeed, for \(S\ne R\), choose an element \(u\) belonging to exactly one of them. Its equivalence class is contained in that set and disjoint from the other. At group mass \(1/q\), its type approves exactly one of \(a_S,a_R\). Hence no two activities have identical approval profiles. Each activity supports only the single group represented by its column of assignment masses.

The equivalence classes can be computed by comparing incidence vectors. There are at most \(3q\) types, and each mass and threshold has binary encoding length \(O(\log q)\). The construction is therefore polynomial in the explicit exact-cover input length.

2. Correctness of the reduction

An exact cover gives a perfect assignment

Suppose \(\mathcal S'\subseteq\mathcal S\) partitions \(U\).

Each equivalence class \(C\) lies entirely in exactly one member \(S\in\mathcal S'\): membership is constant throughout \(C\), and the selected sets partition the universe.

Assign all mass of \(t_C\) to that activity \(a_S\). Every type is fully assigned. Each selected activity receives mass

\(\displaystyle z_{a_S}=\sum_{C:\,C\subseteq S}\mu_{t_C}=\frac1q.\)

Every positive assignment uses an allowed pair and meets its threshold. This is a perfect individually rational assignment.

A perfect assignment gives an exact cover

Conversely, suppose a perfect individually rational assignment exists.

If \(a_S\) is used, then \(z_{a_S}>0\), so some type assigns positive mass to it. Individual rationality implies

\(\displaystyle z_{a_S}\ge\frac1q.\)

Only types with \(C\subseteq S\) can contribute to \(a_S\). Also, nonnegativity and the row-sum constraint imply \(x_{t_C,a_S}\le\mu_{t_C}\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle z_{a_S}=\sum_{C:\,C\subseteq S}x_{t_C,a_S}\le\sum_{C:\,C\subseteq S}\mu_{t_C}=\frac1q.\)

Thus every used activity has mass exactly \(1/q\).

Moreover, equality forces every allowed type to contribute its entire mass to that activity. Explicitly,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{C:\,C\subseteq S}\bigl(\mu_{t_C}-x_{t_C,a_S}\bigr)=0.\)

Each summand is nonnegative, so each equals zero. Hence

\(\displaystyle x_{t_C,a_S}=\mu_{t_C}\qquad\text{for every }C\subseteq S.\)

This is the step that rules out a fractional escape from the reduction.

Two used activities cannot correspond to intersecting sets: an element in their intersection would belong to a type whose entire positive mass must go to both activities, contradicting its row-sum constraint.

Finally, the sets corresponding to used activities cover \(U\). For every equivalence class \(C\), its positive mass is fully assigned, so it contributes positively to some used activity \(a_S\), necessarily with \(C\subseteq S\).

The used sets therefore partition \(U\). Since each has three elements, they form an exact cover of size \(q\). This proves the reduction and establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

3. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Real assignment variables do not prevent membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

A certificate specifies a subset \(D\subseteq E\) of pairs permitted to carry mass. Verify feasibility of the rational linear system consisting of:

  • \(x_{t,a}\ge 0\) for \((t,a)\in D\);
  • \(\displaystyle \sum_{a:\,(t,a)\in D}x_{t,a}=\mu_t\) for every type \(t\);
  • \(\displaystyle \sum_{r:\,(r,a)\in D}x_{r,a}\ge\ell_{t,a}\) for every \((t,a)\in D\).

All pairs outside \(D\) receive zero mass.

If a perfect assignment exists, choosing its positive support as \(D\) makes this system feasible. Conversely, every feasible solution satisfies individual rationality: each positive pair belongs to \(D\), and its activity mass meets the corresponding threshold.

The certificate has polynomial length. The system has at most \(|E|\) variables and \(\tau+|E|\) substantive constraints, with rational coefficients of polynomial encoding length. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear-programming feasibility therefore supplies a polynomial-time verifier.

Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Thresholds and preferences. The common threshold \(1/q\) belongs to \((0,1]\), including when \(q=1\). The specified ranking of approved alternatives, void, and disapproved alternatives defines complete preferences consistent with the task. Indifference among disapproved alternatives is permitted.

Eligible mass per activity. The classes contained in \(S\) partition its three elements. Consequently,
\[ \sum_{C:\,C\subseteq S}\mu_{t_C}=\frac1q. \]

Distinct support types. Different classes have different incidence patterns. At the admissible positive mass \(1/q\), an activity witnessing this difference is approved by one type and disapproved by the other. Therefore their complete preference types differ.

Distinct activity profiles. Different sets have an element in their symmetric difference. Its class distinguishes the corresponding activities at mass \(1/q\). Thus activity profiles remain distinct after elements are merged into types.

One group per activity. The construction uses one activity for each set, and the assignment model represents its sole group by one column. It introduces no activity copies or additional groups.

Polynomial encoding. Incidence vectors and their equivalence classes can be computed in polynomial time from the explicit source input. There are at most \(3q\) types and \(|\mathcal S|\) activities. The reduction therefore has polynomial running time and output length.

3. Forward implication

If selected sets partition \(U\), every class is contained in exactly one selected set. Assigning its entire mass there is therefore well-defined and satisfies every row sum.

Each selected activity receives all classes contained in its set, giving mass exactly \(1/q\). Every positive assignment is allowed and meets its threshold. Unselected activities receive zero mass and impose no individual-rationality obligations.

The forward implication is fully justified.

4. Reverse implication and divisible masses

For a used activity, its positive column sum entails a positive contribution from at least one type. Individual rationality then gives \(z_{a_S}\ge1/q\).

Disallowed pairs cannot carry positive mass. Nonnegativity and the row-sum equations give \(x_{t_C,a_S}\le\mu_{t_C}\). Summing these bounds over eligible classes yields \(z_{a_S}\le1/q\). Therefore every used activity has mass exactly \(1/q\).

The subsequent saturation argument is valid: the finitely many nonnegative deficits \(\mu_{t_C}-x_{t_C,a_S}\) sum to zero, so every deficit is zero. Thus every eligible type sends its entire mass to each used activity for which it is eligible. This explicitly excludes fractional splitting as an escape from the reduction.

If two used sets intersect, the class containing an intersection element is eligible for both. Saturation would assign its full positive mass to both columns, contradicting its row sum. Hence used sets are pairwise disjoint.

Every class has positive mass and must contribute to some activity. That activity is used and its set contains the class. Thus used sets cover \(U\). Together with disjointness, this gives an exact cover, necessarily containing \(q\) sets.

Elements belonging to no source set cause no hidden exception: their class has no allowed activity and prevents a perfect assignment, consistently with the absence of an exact cover.
