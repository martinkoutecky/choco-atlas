The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given chore classes \(G\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and a value oracle for each type's cost function \(c_t\) on integral count bundles \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{G}\), where \(c_t(0)=0\) and each class has marginal cost in \(\{0,1\}\), compute a rational finite-support assignment \(x_{t,a,b}\) in time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(|G|\), and input bit length \(L\). Require \(b\in\{0,1\}\), \(\sum_{a,b}x_{t,a,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,a,b}a_gx_{t,a,b}=q_g\), and \(\sum_{t,a,b}b x_{t,a,b}\le 1\). For every two positive-mass outcomes \((t,a,b)\) and \((s,a',b')\), require \(c_t(a)-b\le c_t(a')-b'\); also require \(c_t(a)\le c_t(a')\), or some \(g\) with \(a_g>0\) and \(c_t(a-e_g)\le c_t(a')\), for EF1 before subsidies.

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Let \(G\) be a finite set of chore classes, \(m=|G|\), with nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\). Bundles are integral count vectors \(a\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{G}\); copies within each class are indistinguishable.
Each type has a value oracle for \(c_t:\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{G}\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\), with \(c_t(0)=0\) and \(c_t(a+e_g)-c_t(a)\in\{0,1\}\) for every \(a,g\), where \(e_g\) is the coordinate unit vector. No additivity or other structural assumption is imposed.
An assignment is a finite list of nonzero rational masses \(x_{t,a,b}\geq0\), with subsidies \(b\in\{0,1\}\), satisfying \(\sum_{a,b}x_{t,a,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,a,b}a_gx_{t,a,b}=q_g\), and \(\sum_{t,a,b}b x_{t,a,b}\leq1\).

Also require EF1 before subsidies: \(c_t(a)\leq c_t(a')\), or there exists \(g\) with \(a_g>0\) and \(c_t(a-e_g)\leq c_t(a')\).
Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the explicit rational input; oracle queries and output counts use binary encoding, and running time includes their bit operations and oracle calls.

Can such an assignment always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) in the value-oracle model, or is this search problem NP-hard, even with polynomial-time evaluable cost functions?

For finitely many agents and indivisible chores with arbitrary binary marginal costs, an EF1 allocation admitting envy-free subsidies in \(\{0,1\}\), totaling at most \(n-1\), always exists and is computable in polynomial oracle time. [Sarkar–Sadhukhan](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14465v1)

Partial result: polynomial time for a single supported type

The general complexity question remains unresolved here. I prove a new special case beyond those in the notes:

When $\tau=1$, a valid assignment can be computed in polynomial time in $m$ and $L$, with arbitrary rational supplies and arbitrary binary-marginal costs. The algorithm produces at most $m+1$ positive-mass outcomes. It also extends to any number of types whose cost functions are identical.

The proof does not expand the common denominator into individual agents. Its key properties are that zero-cost additions can be batched by binary search, and every population split exhausts a residual chore class.

I also give self-contained proofs of general existence and polynomial-size certificates, and state precisely the remaining complexity gap.

1. Normalization and the empty-class case

The marginal hypothesis implies coordinatewise monotonicity:
$$ a\leq a' \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_t(a)\leq c_t(a'). $$
Indeed, one can reach $a'$ from $a$ through coordinate additions, each of which changes cost by zero or one. Applying this argument from the zero bundle also gives
$$ 0\leq c_t(a)\leq \sum_{g\in G}a_g. $$

If $G=\varnothing$, give each type the zero bundle with subsidy zero and mass $\mu_t$. All comparisons are equalities, so this resolves that case. Henceforth assume $m\geq1$.

Write
$$ u_g=\lfloor q_g\rfloor,\qquad r_g=q_g-u_g,\qquad R=\sum_{g\in G}r_g. $$
Thus $0\leq r_g<1$ and $R<m$.

Giving every person the common bundle $u$ accounts for the integral part of every supply. Define the residual cost
$$ f_t(z)=c_t(u+z)-c_t(u), \qquad z\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{G}. $$
It satisfies
$$ f_t(0)=0,\qquad f_t(z+e_g)-f_t(z)\in\{0,1\}. $$
For comparisons evaluated by type $t$, the constant $c_t(u)$ cancels. Consequently, an envy-free residual assignment with supply $r$ becomes an envy-free assignment for supply $q$ after adding $u$ to every bundle. An EF1 witness obtained by removing a residual chore remains a legal EF1 witness after this addition.

Choose a positive integer $D$ divisible by all denominators in the explicit rational input. The product of those denominators suffices, so
$$ \log D=O(L). $$
In particular, every $Dr_g$ and $D\mu_t$ is an integer.

2. A batching lemma for one cost function

For this section there is one residual cost function $f$.

A population group consists of a positive rational mass $d$ receiving one residual bundle $z$. Suppose that
$$ f(z)=h. $$
An addition of class $g$ is zero-cost for this group if $f(z+e_g)=h$; otherwise its marginal cost is one.

Lemma 1: a costly marginal stays costly at the same cost level

Suppose
$$ f(z)=h,\qquad f(z+e_g)=h+1. $$
If $w\geq z$ coordinatewise and $f(w)=h$, then
$$ f(w+e_g)=h+1. $$

Proof. Monotonicity gives
$$ f(w+e_g)\geq f(z+e_g)=h+1. $$
The binary-marginal condition gives
$$ f(w+e_g)\leq f(w)+1=h+1. $$
Both bounds coincide. $\square$

Thus, while a group receives only zero-cost additions, a class that has become costly cannot become zero-cost again. The statement also holds for descendants created by splitting the group, since their bundles are coordinatewise above the original bundle.

Lemma 2: zero-cost additions can be batched

Suppose a group of mass $d$ has bundle $z$ with $f(z)=h$, a positive residual supply $\rho_g$ remains, and
$$ f(z+e_g)=h. $$
One can, using polynomially many bit operations and
$$ O\!\left(1+\log\!\left(1+\frac{\rho_g}{d}\right)\right) $$
oracle calls, perform zero-cost additions until either:

  • class $g$ is exhausted; or
  • its next addition to this group has marginal cost one.

The operation creates at most one additional group. If it creates one, it exhausts class $g$.

Proof. Set
$$ N=\left\lfloor\frac{\rho_g}{d}\right\rfloor. $$
Since $f$ is monotone, binary search finds the largest integer $\ell\in\{0,\ldots,N\}$ such that
$$ f(z+\ell e_g)=h. $$
Give all mass $d$ the bundle
$$ z'=z+\ell e_g $$
and reduce the residual supply to
$$ \rho'_g=\rho_g-\ell d. $$

If $\ell<N$, maximality and binary marginals imply
$$ f(z'+e_g)=h+1. $$
This gives the second conclusion.

Otherwise $\ell=N$, so
$$ 0\leq\rho'_g<d. $$
If $\rho'_g=0$, the class is exhausted. If $\rho'_g>0$, query $f(z'+e_g)$. A value $h+1$ gives the second conclusion. A value $h$ permits the split
$$ \begin{array}{c|c} \text{mass}&\text{bundle}\\ \hline d-\rho'_g&z'\\ \rho'_g&z'+e_g. \end{array} $$
Both masses are positive. The extra chore given to mass $\rho'_g$ uses exactly the remaining supply $\rho'_g$, so class $g$ is exhausted. Both resulting bundles still have cost $h$. $\square$

3. Polynomial-time algorithm when $\tau=1$

Theorem 3

For $\tau=1$, a valid assignment can be computed in time polynomial in $m$ and $L$. The output has at most $m+1$ positive-mass outcomes.

3.1 Maintained state

Work with the residual cost $f=f_1$. Maintain groups
$$ (d_j,z_j),\qquad j=1,\ldots,K, $$
remaining supplies $\rho_g$, and an integer $h$, satisfying
$$ d_j>0,\qquad \sum_jd_j=1, $$
$$ \sum_jd_jz_{j,g}+\rho_g=r_g \qquad\text{for every }g, $$
and
$$ f(z_j)=h\qquad\text{for every }j. $$
Additionally, every mass $d_j$ and every remaining supply $\rho_g$ is an integer multiple of $1/D$.

Initially there is one group of mass one with bundle zero:
$$ K=1,\qquad d_1=1,\qquad z_1=0,\qquad \rho=r,\qquad h=0. $$

3.2 Saturating zero-cost additions

Whenever $\rho_g>0$ and $f(z_j+e_g)=h$, apply Lemma 2 to group $j$ and class $g$.

The supply equations and the common-cost invariant remain valid. The denominator-grid invariant also remains valid: whole-group additions subtract integer multiples of $d_j$, and a split uses the masses $\rho'_g$ and $d_j-\rho'_g$.

Repeat until either all residual supply is exhausted or
$$ f(z_j+e_g)=h+1 \qquad \text{for every }j\text{ and every }g\text{ with }\rho_g>0. $$

This repetition terminates with a polynomial number of batching operations; the bound is proved in Section 3.5.

If all supply is exhausted, return these groups with subsidy zero.

3.3 A full synchronized increase

Suppose zero-cost saturation has finished, and put
$$ P=\sum_g\rho_g. $$
First consider $P\geq1$.

Give exactly one residual chore to every person. This can be done with rational groups as follows. Process the current groups in a fixed order and the nonempty residual classes in a fixed order. For an unprocessed portion of a group of mass $d$ and a class with remaining supply $\rho_g$, allocate one class-$g$ chore to mass
$$ \delta=\min\{d,\rho_g\}. $$
Continue until all population mass one has received a chore. The assumption $P\geq1$ ensures that this is possible.

A group splits only if the chosen class runs out partway through it. Thus every split exhausts a residual class.

Because zero-cost saturation has finished, every recipient’s cost changes from $h$ to $h+1$. Replace $h$ by $h+1$. The state invariants are restored, and the algorithm returns to zero-cost saturation.

3.4 The final partial increase

Now consider $0<P<1$ after zero-cost saturation.

Use the same procedure to give one residual chore to population mass $P$, exhausting all remaining supplies. Give no additional chore to the remaining mass $1-P$. Every person receives at most one chore during this final step.

Give subsidy one to the recipients and subsidy zero to everyone else. Their residual costs and subsidies are therefore
$$ \begin{array}{c|c|c} &\text{residual cost}&\text{subsidy}\\ \hline \text{recipient}&h+1&1\\ \text{nonrecipient}&h&0. \end{array} $$
Every outcome has subsidized residual cost exactly $h$. Hence every ordered envy-free comparison, including comparisons between outcomes of the same type, is an equality.

EF1 also holds:

  • A nonrecipient has cost $h$, weakly below every final bundle’s cost.
  • For a recipient, removing the chore added in this final step restores her preceding bundle, whose residual cost is $h$. This is weakly below every final bundle’s cost.

The total subsidy is $P<1$.

Finally, add $u$ to every residual bundle. Section 1 shows that all required comparisons and EF1 witnesses transfer to the original costs.

3.5 Termination and support size

Every split in the algorithm exhausts a residual class:

  • this follows from Lemma 2 during zero-cost saturation;
  • during a synchronized or final increase, a split occurs only when the chosen class runs out partway through a group.

Residual supplies never increase. Each class can therefore be exhausted at most once. Starting with one group, the number of groups always satisfies
$$ K\leq m+1. $$

To bound zero-cost saturation, give each group a persistent identifier; when it splits, one descendant retains the identifier and the other receives a new one.

At a fixed level $h$, every batching operation either exhausts a class or makes the selected group-class marginal costly. By Lemma 1, such a costly marginal remains costly under subsequent zero-cost additions and is inherited by descendants.

There are at most $m+1$ identifiers and $m$ classes. Hence at any fixed level there are at most $m(m+1)$ operations ending by making a marginal costly, together with at most $m$ operations exhausting classes.

Every full synchronized increase consumes exactly one unit of total per-capita residual supply. Zero-cost additions consume nonnegative additional supply. Consequently, the number of full synchronized increases is at most
$$ \lfloor R\rfloor<m. $$
There are therefore at most $\lfloor R\rfloor+1\leq m$ zero-cost saturation phases.

This proves termination and a polynomial bound on the number of batching operations.

3.6 Bit complexity and oracle-query sizes

The denominator-grid invariant gives
$$ d_j\geq\frac1D. $$
Supply conservation and nonnegativity imply, for every positive group,
$$ d_jz_{j,g}\leq r_g, $$
and thus
$$ z_{j,g}\leq Dr_g. $$
These counts have $O(L)$ bits.

In a batching operation,
$$ N=\left\lfloor\frac{\rho_g}{d_j}\right\rfloor\leq D. $$
Binary search therefore uses $O(1+\log D)=O(1+L)$ oracle calls.

The queried counts have polynomial bit length as well. Indeed,
$$ d_j(z_{j,g}+N) \leq d_jz_{j,g}+\rho_g \leq r_g. $$
Queries used by binary search have residual coordinate at most $Dr_g$, and the additional one-step query has coordinate at most $Dr_g+1$.

All maintained masses and remaining supplies lie in $[0,1]$ and are multiples of $1/D$, so their exact arithmetic uses $O(L)$ bits. Oracle evaluations use
$$ f(z)=c_1(u+z)-c_1(u). $$
The earlier bound $c_1(a)\leq\sum_ga_g$ ensures polynomial bit length for their returned values.

There are polynomially many batching operations, synchronized increases, and group scans. Therefore the algorithm runs in polynomial time in $m$ and $L$, including bit operations and oracle calls. This completes the proof. $\square$

4. Extension to identical cost functions

Suppose there are $\tau$ supported types, but
$$ c_t=c \qquad\text{for every }t. $$
Run Theorem 3 using $c$, obtaining categories $(d_j,a_j,b_j)$. Define
$$ x_{t,a_j,b_j}=\mu_t d_j, $$
merging identical outcomes if necessary.

The type-mass equations hold because
$$ \sum_j\mu_td_j=\mu_t. $$
The supply equations hold because
$$ \sum_{t,j}\mu_td_ja_{j,g} =\sum_jd_ja_{j,g} =q_g. $$
Every type evaluates every comparison with the same cost function, so envy-freeness and EF1 follow directly from the single-type construction. Total subsidy is unchanged.

There are at most $\tau(m+1)$ outcomes, and multiplying the rational masses increases their bit lengths only polynomially. Thus this extension is also polynomial-time computable.

5. General existence and polynomial-size certificates

The preceding algorithm does not handle arbitrary distinct costs. Nevertheless, every admissible instance has a polynomial-size valid assignment.

5.1 Existence

Use the finite-agent theorem supplied in the question: arbitrary normalized binary-marginal chore costs admit a complete EF1 allocation with envy-free subsidies in $\{0,1\}$, totaling at most the number of agents minus one. Such an allocation is polynomial-time computable in the finite instance’s value-oracle model. [Sarkar–Sadhukhan](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14465v1)

Construct $D\mu_t$ agents of type $t$, and $Dr_g$ distinguishable residual chores of class $g$. There are exactly $D$ agents and
$$ \sum_gDr_g=DR\leq mD $$
residual chores.

For a residual set $S$, let $\alpha(S)$ be its class-count vector. Give a type-$t$ agent cost
$$ \widetilde c_t(S)=c_t(u+\alpha(S))-c_t(u). $$
This cost is normalized, nonnegative, and has binary marginals. Thus the finite theorem applies.

If the returned bundles and subsidies are $(S_i,b_i)$, give outcome
$$ (t_i,u+\alpha(S_i),b_i) $$
mass $1/D$, merging identical outcomes.

The type masses are correct. For every class,
$$ \frac1D\sum_i\bigl(u_g+\alpha(S_i)_g\bigr) =u_g+r_g=q_g. $$
The total subsidy is at most $(D-1)/D$.

For a comparison evaluated by type $t$, the constant $c_t(u)$ cancels from both finite-agent costs, so envy-freeness transfers exactly. Removing a residual chore of class $g$ corresponds to subtracting $e_g$ from its recipient’s original bundle, so EF1 transfers too.

This proves existence, but its runtime is polynomial in the numerical value of $D$, which can be exponential in $L$.

5.2 Compression to at most $m+\tau$ outcomes

Every bundle constructed above satisfies
$$ a_g\leq u_g+Dr_g\leq Dq_g. $$
Hence its counts have polynomial bit length.

Set $s=m+\tau$. For each positive outcome $i=(t_i,a_i,b_i)$, form the integer column
$$ v_i=(e_{t_i},a_i)\in\mathbb Z^s. $$
The mass and supply equations are
$$ \sum_i x_iv_i=(\mu,q). $$

If the support columns are dependent, choose a nonzero rational dependence
$$ \sum_i\lambda_iv_i=0. $$
Summing its type coordinates gives $\sum_i\lambda_i=0$. Thus $\lambda$ has both positive and negative entries. Set
$$ \varepsilon=\min_{\lambda_i>0}\frac{x_i}{\lambda_i}, \qquad x'_i=x_i-\varepsilon\lambda_i. $$
All new masses are nonnegative, the equations remain satisfied, and at least one positive outcome disappears.

Deleting outcomes preserves envy-freeness and EF1: every surviving comparison was already required. The subsidy constraint also remains satisfied, because subsidies are binary and the total mass is one:
$$ \sum_i b_ix'_i\leq\sum_i x'_i=1. $$

Repeating leaves independent columns, hence at most $s=m+\tau$ outcomes.

Their masses have polynomial bit length. To see this, let $\ell\leq s$ be the remaining support size and choose $\ell$ rows forming a nonsingular square matrix $B$. Its right-hand side is $h/D$ for an integer vector $h$.

Define
$$ C=\max\bigl(\{1,D\}\cup\{Dq_g:g\in G\}\bigr). $$
All entries of $B$ and $h$ have absolute value at most $C$, and $\log C$ is polynomially bounded by $L$.

Cramer’s rule expresses each mass as
$$ x_j=\frac{\det B_j(h)}{D\det B}. $$
The determinant expansion gives
$$ |\det B|,\ |\det B_j(h)|\leq \ell!C^\ell. $$
Therefore every mass has polynomial bit length.

5.3 Polynomial verification

For a polynomial-size proposed assignment, exact rational arithmetic verifies the type masses, supplies, and subsidy bound.

For each ordered outcome pair, oracle evaluations verify the envy-free inequality. EF1 is checked by first testing the no-envy branch and otherwise testing each coordinate with a positive count for a valid removal. There are at most $m$ such coordinates per comparison.

The query vectors have polynomial bit length, and
$$ c_t(a)\leq\sum_ga_g $$
bounds the oracle-answer bit lengths. Thus verification takes polynomial time and polynomially many oracle calls.

Existence of these certificates does not give a polynomial-time method for finding their support.

6. A precise limitation on SAT-search hardness reductions

For explicitly represented, uniformly polynomial-time evaluable costs, the certificate result yields the following conditional barrier.

Suppose a polynomial-time reduction maps every Boolean formula $\varphi$ to an admissible instance, and a polynomial-time decoder maps every valid assignment for that instance to a satisfying truth assignment whenever $\varphi$ is satisfiable.

Then $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.

Proof. A certificate for unsatisfiability consists of a polynomial-size valid assignment for the constructed instance. Verify the assignment, run the decoder, and accept exactly when its output is not a satisfying truth assignment of $\varphi$.

If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, a valid certificate exists by Section 5, and the decoder cannot output a satisfying assignment.

If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, the reduction’s universal decoding guarantee ensures that no valid certificate is accepted.

Thus $\mathrm{UNSAT}\in\mathrm{NP}$. Since $\mathrm{UNSAT}$ is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete, this implies $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. $\square$

This barrier applies to the stated reduction notion; it does not settle every possible hardness formulation.

7. What remains open

The new theorem resolves arbitrary rational supplies for one supported type, including instances with many nonintegral coordinates and
$$ 1<R<|\{g:r_g>0\}|-1. $$

Its proof depends on all groups sharing one cost function and one cost level. For distinct types, a bundle can change its assigned evaluator during an envy-preserving reassignment. Lemma 1 then does not supply the same persistent group-class blocking bound. No polynomial bound for such a generalization is proved here.

Accordingly, neither a polynomial-time algorithm nor a hardness classification is established for the unrestricted problem with distinct cost functions.
