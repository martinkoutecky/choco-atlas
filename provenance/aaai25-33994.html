The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(\mathcal{T}\), rational type masses \(\mu\), seed fractions \(s_a\), type-to-type contact rates \(\lambda_{ab}\), activation probabilities \(p_{ab}\), horizon \(H\), blocking costs \(q_{ab}\), budget \(B\), and \(\varepsilon>0\), choose retained-contact fractions \(\rho\in[0,1]^{\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}}\) satisfying \(\sum_{a,b}q_{ab}(1-\rho_{ab})\le B\) to obtain an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal value of \(\sum_b z_{b,H}\), where \(x_{b,0}=s_b\mu_b\), \(z_{b,h}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{h}x_{b,\ell}\), and \(x_{b,h+1}=(\mu_b-z_{b,h})\left(1-\exp\left(-\sum_a\lambda_{ab}p_{ab}\rho_{ab}x_{a,h}/\mu_b\right)\right)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_b\), with \(\sum_b\mu_b=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m=\tau^2\) count ordered type pairs.
Inputs are binary rationals \(s_b,p_{ab}\in[0,1]\), \(\lambda_{ab}\ge0\), \(q_{ab}>0\), \(B>0\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1\), together with a positive integer horizon \(H\) encoded in unary. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Choose a single, time-independent matrix \(\rho\in[0,1]^{\mathcal T\times\mathcal T}\) satisfying \(\sum_{a,b}q_{ab}(1-\rho_{ab})\le B\); \(q_{ab}\) is the cost of completely blocking that ordered pair.
Define newly activated masses \(x_{b,0}=s_b\mu_b\), cumulative activated masses \(z_{b,h}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{h}x_{b,\ell}\), and, for \(0\le h<H\),
\(x_{b,h+1}=(\mu_b-z_{b,h})\left(1-\exp\left(-\sum_a\lambda_{ab}p_{ab}\rho_{ab}x_{a,h}/\mu_b\right)\right)\).
Only newly activated mass transmits at the next step; activated mass never becomes susceptible again. Minimize \(F(\rho)=\sum_bz_{b,H}\), including seeds.
The computational problem is to find a feasible rational \(\rho\) with \(F(\rho)\le\min_{\rho'\text{ feasible}}F(\rho')+\varepsilon\), using ordinary bit computation.

Is this additive-\(\varepsilon\) optimization problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite directed graphs, minimizing expected terminal independent-cascade influence by deleting a prescribed number of edges is NP-hard ([Lee et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01031v1)).
Their continuous edge-retention method optimizes a learned surrogate with budget and integrality penalties; it does not classify the mass recurrence above. No complexity classification for this precise recurrence was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive support masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a distribution over types.
  • Specified binary-rational numeric inputs, nonnegative contact rates, seed fractions and activation probabilities in the unit interval, positive blocking costs, and a positive budget.
  • Interpreted each blocking cost as the total cost of completely blocking its ordered type pair, without additional mass weighting.
  • Made epsilon-optimality additive in total activated mass and required a feasible rational retention matrix in the ordinary bit model; epsilon is included in the input bit length.
  • Encoded the supplied positive horizon in unary and retained it as an independent parameter, since the mass recurrence need not terminate after the support-size number of steps.
  • Defined m as the number of ordered type pairs and L as total input bit length; retained-contact fractions remain fixed throughout the horizon.

Classification

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\), already when \(H=1\). Consequently, an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof uses the stated binary encoding of \(\varepsilon\). It gives a polynomial reduction from SUBSET SUM and handles fractional retention matrices directly.

1. Source problem and construction

SUBSET SUM asks whether positive binary-encoded integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) have a subset summing to an integer \(T\). This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [MIT lecture notes](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/6.1400-2025/lec19-before-class.pdf)

Write \(W=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\). We may assume \(0<T<W\), since the excluded cases are immediately decidable.

Let \(D\) be the smallest power of two strictly greater than \(2W\). Thus \(2W<D\le 4W\). Construct a society with support
\(\mathcal T=\{\sigma,d,1,\ldots,n\}\), where \(\sigma\) is the seeded source and \(d\) is an isolated auxiliary type.

Set the masses to

\(\displaystyle \mu_\sigma=\frac12,\qquad \mu_i=\frac{w_i}{D}\quad(1\le i\le n),\qquad \mu_d=\frac12-\frac WD.\)

Every mass is positive, and their sum is one. Set \(s_\sigma=1\) and every other seed fraction to zero.

The only pairs with nonzero transmission parameters are

\(\displaystyle p_{\sigma i}=1,\qquad \lambda_{\sigma i}=\frac{2w_i}{D}\quad(1\le i\le n).\)

Set all other \(p_{ab}\) and \(\lambda_{ab}\) to zero. Define the blocking costs by

\(\displaystyle q_{\sigma i}=w_i,\)

and set every other \(q_{ab}=1\). Finally, take

\(\displaystyle B=T,\qquad H=1,\qquad \varepsilon=\frac{1}{64D}.\)

All input numbers are dyadic rationals, including the integers.

2. Exact reduction of the recurrence

Write \(r_i=\rho_{\sigma i}\). Initially, only the source has newly activated mass, namely \(x_{\sigma,0}=1/2\). For target type \(i\), the exposure in the recurrence is exactly

\(\displaystyle \lambda_{\sigma i}p_{\sigma i}\rho_{\sigma i}\frac{x_{\sigma,0}}{\mu_i} =\frac{2w_i}{D}\,r_i\,\frac{1/2}{w_i/D} =r_i.\)

Consequently, the objective is

\(\displaystyle F(\rho)=\Phi(r):=\frac12+\frac1D\sum_{i=1}^n w_i(1-e^{-r_i}).\)

The source contributes its seeded mass; the auxiliary type contributes zero.

Every feasible matrix satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^n w_i(1-r_i)\le T,\)

because these terms are part of the budget sum and all remaining terms are nonnegative. Conversely, any vector satisfying this inequality extends to a feasible matrix by setting every other retention coordinate to one. Those coordinates do not affect the objective.

Thus the optimization problem is exactly minimization of \(\Phi\) over

\(\displaystyle P=\left\{r\in[0,1]^n:\sum_i w_i r_i\ge W-T\right\}.\)

3. A quantitative concavity bound

Define \(f(t)=1-e^{-t}\) and \(c=f(1)=1-e^{-1}\). For every \(t\in[0,1]\),

\[\displaystyle f(t)-ct\ge \frac18t(1-t)\ge \frac1{16}\min\{t,1-t\}. \tag{1}\]

To prove the first inequality, define

\(\displaystyle g(t)=f(t)-ct-\frac18t(1-t).\)

We have \(g(0)=g(1)=0\), and

\(\displaystyle g''(t)=-e^{-t}+\frac14\le -e^{-1}+\frac14<0,\)

using the elementary bound \(e<4\). Hence \(g\) is concave and lies above the chord joining its endpoints, so \(g(t)\ge0\).

For the second inequality, put \(u=\min\{t,1-t\}\). Then \(u\le1/2\) and \(t(1-t)=u(1-u)\ge u/2\). This proves (1). We also have \(c>1/2\), since \(e>2\).

4. A lower bound and a rounding guarantee

Define the reference value

\(\displaystyle C=\frac12+\frac{c(W-T)}D.\)

For any \(r\in P\), let its budget slack in retention coordinates be

\(\displaystyle S=\sum_i w_i r_i-(W-T)\ge0.\)

Round each coordinate to a nearest endpoint:

\(\displaystyle \widehat r_i=0\ \text{if }r_i<\frac12,\qquad \widehat r_i=1\ \text{if }r_i\ge\frac12.\)

Define the weighted rounding error

\(\displaystyle E=\sum_i w_i|r_i-\widehat r_i| =\sum_i w_i\min\{r_i,1-r_i\}.\)

By (1),

\[\displaystyle \Phi(r)-C =\frac1D\left(cS+\sum_i w_i\bigl(f(r_i)-cr_i\bigr)\right) \ge\frac1D\left(cS+\frac E{16}\right) \ge\frac{S+E}{16D}. \tag{2}\]

In particular, \(\Phi(r)\ge C\) for every feasible vector. Moreover, the triangle inequality gives

\[\displaystyle \left|\sum_i w_i\widehat r_i-(W-T)\right| \le E+S \le16D\bigl(\Phi(r)-C\bigr). \tag{3}\]

This bound applies to every feasible fractional vector; no integrality assumption about an optimizer is needed.

YES instances

Suppose a subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}w_i=T\). Choose \(r_i=0\) for \(i\in I\) and \(r_i=1\) otherwise. This vector is feasible and has

\(\displaystyle \Phi(r)=\frac12+\frac{c(W-T)}D=C.\)

Together with (2), this proves that the optimum is exactly \(C\).

If an additive-\(\varepsilon\) optimizer returns a feasible matrix, its active coordinates therefore satisfy

\(\displaystyle \Phi(r)-C\le\varepsilon=\frac1{64D}.\)

Equation (3) yields

\(\displaystyle \left|\sum_i w_i\widehat r_i-(W-T)\right|\le\frac14.\)

Both quantities inside the absolute value are integers. They must therefore be equal. Consequently, the rounded blocked subset

\(\displaystyle \widehat I=\{i:\widehat r_i=0\}\)

satisfies \(\sum_{i\in\widehat I}w_i=T\).

NO instances

If no subset sums to \(T\), no rounded blocked subset can sum to \(T\). Equivalently, for every feasible \(r\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_i w_i\widehat r_i\ne W-T.\)

The difference is an integer, so its absolute value is at least one. By (3),

\(\displaystyle \Phi(r)\ge C+\frac1{16D}.\)

Thus fractional choices cannot close the separation gap.

5. Bit complexity and conclusion

The construction has \(\tau=n+2\), \(m=(n+2)^2\), and unary horizon \(H=1\). Since \(D\le4W\), its binary length is polynomial in the SUBSET SUM input length. The same holds for every constructed parameter, including \(\varepsilon=1/(64D)\), and for the complete pair tables.

Now suppose the requested optimizer ran in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,L\). To decide SUBSET SUM:

  • Construct the society and call the optimizer once.
  • Round its rational coordinates \(\rho_{\sigma i}\) by exact comparison with \(1/2\).
  • Accept precisely when the resulting blocked subset sums to \(T\).

For YES instances, the rounding guarantee proves acceptance. For NO instances, no subset can pass the check. All postprocessing uses polynomial-time rational and integer arithmetic; no exponential evaluation is required.

This would decide an \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem in polynomial time, establishing the claimed hardness. The reduction permits very small binary-encoded accuracy; it does not establish hardness for fixed \(\varepsilon\).
