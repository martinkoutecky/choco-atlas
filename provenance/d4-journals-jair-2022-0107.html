The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite \(T\) and goods \(M\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), rational caps \(c_t\), and finitely encoded nonincreasing marginal sequences \(u_{t,j,\ell}\), find a finite-support distribution \(x_{t,B}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{|M|}\) satisfying \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}b_jx_{t,B}=q_j\), where \(\bar u_t(B)=\min\!\left(c_t,\sum_j\sum_{\ell\le b_j}u_{t,j,\ell}\right)\), such that \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}\log\bar u_t(B)\right)\) is within factor \((1+\gamma/4)e^{e^{-1/(1+\gamma)}}\) of optimum and the allocation satisfies continuous EF1 and uncapped approximate Pareto optimality.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts represented types, not possible types. There are \(m=|M|\ge1\) goods with positive rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\). Each type has a positive rational cap \(c_t\), or the explicit uncapped flag \(c_t=+\infty\), and explicitly listed nonnegative rational marginal values \(u_{t,j,1}\ge\cdots\ge u_{t,j,K_j}\), extended by zero thereafter. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For an integral bundle \(B=(b_j)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\), define \(u_t(B)=\sum_j\sum_{\ell=1}^{b_j}u_{t,j,\ell}\) and \(\bar u_t(B)=\min\{c_t,u_t(B)\}\). A feasible allocation is a finite-support mass distribution satisfying \(x_{t,B}\ge0\), \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,B}b_jx_{t,B}=q_j\) for every good.
Define \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)=\exp(\sum_{t,B:x_{t,B}>0}x_{t,B}\log\bar u_t(B))\), with value zero if any positive-mass bundle has zero capped utility; let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be its supremum over feasible allocations, without fairness constraints.
Fix a constant \(\gamma\in(0,1]\), and write \(r=1+\gamma/4\) and \(A_\gamma=r\exp(\exp(-1/(1+\gamma)))\). Continuous approximate EF1 means that for every \(x_{t,B}>0\) and \(x_{s,C}>0\) with \(C\ne0\), some \(j\) with \(C_j>0\) satisfies \(\bar u_t(B)\ge\bar u_t(C-e_j)/(2+\gamma)\), where \(e_j\) is a unit vector; comparison with an empty bundle is automatic.
When all types are uncapped, require continuous \(r\)-approximate Pareto optimality: there is no finite-support nonnegative reassignment \(z_{t,B,C}\) with \(\sum_Cz_{t,B,C}=x_{t,B}\) and \(\sum_{t,B,C}C_jz_{t,B,C}=q_j\), such that \(u_t(C)\ge r u_t(B)\) whenever \(z_{t,B,C}>0\), with strict inequality on positive mass.
Can a feasible rational finite-support allocation satisfying \(A_\gamma\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}\), continuous approximate EF1, and the stated uncapped Pareto guarantee be computed in polynomial time, or is achieving these joint guarantees NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and the listed sequence lengths, for each fixed \(\gamma\).
For finite societies, a polynomial-time algorithm achieves this same NSW factor and approximate EF1 guarantee, with approximate Pareto optimality when all valuations are uncapped. [Verified discrete result](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/12911/26798/30539).
No result settling these joint guarantees for this precise mass-allocation problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Resolved the unspecified finite encoding by explicitly listing nonnegative rational marginal sequences and extending them by zero beyond their listed lengths.
  • Required nonempty type and good supports, positive supplies, and positive finite caps; represented uncapped valuations by an explicit infinite-cap flag.
  • Carried over the approximation parameter as a fixed constant gamma in (0,1].
  • Resolved continuous EF1 as a comparison between every pair of positive-mass realized bundles, removing one copy from the envied bundle, with the anchor's factor 1/(2+gamma); empty envied bundles satisfy the condition automatically.
  • Restricted the Pareto guarantee to instances in which all valuations are uncapped, as in the discrete result.
  • Resolved continuous approximate Pareto optimality through mass-preserving reassignments of realized individuals, requiring factor-r improvement everywhere and strict improvement on positive mass; this avoids the vacuous self-comparison when every utility is zero.
  • Defined the zero-utility convention for logarithmic welfare and compared with the supremum over all feasible allocations, without imposing fairness on the benchmark.
  • Required rational output masses and binary-encoded integral bundles, while allowing real masses in the welfare benchmark and Pareto comparisons.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Polynomial-time construction

The joint guarantees are computable in polynomial time for every fixed $\gamma\in(0,1]$.

The construction below obtains a stronger welfare guarantee: its approximation factor is $\exp(\varepsilon)$, where $\varepsilon$ can be chosen inverse-polynomially small. It optimizes a rational surrogate for logarithmic utility over a polynomial-size flow formulation. Exact optimization of this surrogate supplies the local inequalities needed for fairness and the global inequality needed for the uncapped Pareto guarantee.

1. Parameters and surrogate objectives

Write
$$ f_t(B)=\bar u_t(B),\qquad K=\sum_{j=1}^m K_j,\qquad S=\tau K. $$
Here $S$ is the total number of listed marginal values.

For the fixed constant $\gamma$, choose a fixed positive rational $\kappa\le\gamma$, and set
$$ \varepsilon=\frac{\kappa}{64(K+1)}. $$
The algorithm uses $\kappa$, so its arithmetic remains rational even if $\gamma$ itself is not rational.

For any finite-support allocation $x$, define
$$ P(x)=\sum_{t,B:f_t(B)>0}x_{t,B}, \qquad F(x)=\sum_{t,B:f_t(B)>0}x_{t,B}\log f_t(B). $$
The first objective records positive-utility mass. When $P(x)=1$,
$$ F(x)=\log\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x). $$

We will construct rational scores $s_t(B)$ satisfying the following properties:

  • If $f_t(B)=0$, then $s_t(B)=0$.
  • If $f_t(B)>0$, then

$$ \log f_t(B)-\varepsilon\le s_t(B)\le\log f_t(B). $$

  • On positive-utility bundles, $s_t(B)$ is nondecreasing under coordinatewise increases of $B$.
  • The resulting configuration optimization has a polynomial-size rational flow formulation.

Define
$$ G(x)=\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}s_t(B). $$
We will compute an allocation maximizing $(P,G)$ lexicographically: first maximize $P$, then maximize $G$ while preserving the optimal value of $P$.

For every allocation,
$$ F(x)-\varepsilon P(x)\le G(x)\le F(x). $$

2. Polynomial-size rounded-utility formulation

2.1 Truncating bundles

Let
$$ \mathcal B=\prod_{j=1}^m\{0,\ldots,K_j\}, \qquad \widehat B_j=\min\{B_j,K_j\}. $$
Since all later marginal values are zero,
$$ f_t(\widehat B)=f_t(B). $$

Initially we optimize over distributions on $\mathcal B$, with supply inequalities:
$$ \sum_{B\in\mathcal B}x_{t,B}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t,B\in\mathcal B}B_jx_{t,B}\le q_j. $$
Section 3 restores supply equalities.

If every listed marginal value is zero, every utility is zero. Any rational feasible allocation then satisfies all requested guarantees, including the uncapped Pareto guarantee because strict utility improvement is impossible. Such an allocation is obtained by the supply-completion construction in Section 3, starting from empty bundles. Hence assume some marginal value is positive.

Let
$$ \lambda=\min\{u_{t,j,\ell}:u_{t,j,\ell}>0\}, \qquad W=\max_t\sum_j\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_j}u_{t,j,\ell}. $$
Both are positive rationals of polynomial encoding length. Every positive raw bundle utility is at least $\lambda$, and every truncated bundle has raw utility at most $W$.

Set
$$ \delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{2m}, \qquad \beta=1+\delta. $$
Use the positive utility grid
$$ \lambda,\lambda\beta,\ldots,\lambda\beta^R, $$
where $R$ is the largest nonnegative integer satisfying $\lambda\beta^R\le W$.

For $z\in[\lambda,W]$, let $\rho(z)$ be the largest grid value at most $z$, and put $\rho(0)=0$. Then
$$ \frac z\beta\le\rho(z)\le z. $$

For a truncated bundle $B$, define its rounded accumulated raw utility recursively:
$$ a_{t,0}(B)=0, $$
$$ a_{t,j}(B)= \rho\left( a_{t,j-1}(B)+ \sum_{\ell=1}^{B_j}u_{t,j,\ell} \right). $$
Whenever the argument is positive, it is at least $\lambda$: either the previous state is positive, or the added prefix contains a positive marginal value.

Let
$$ U_{t,j}(B)=\sum_{i=1}^j\sum_{\ell=1}^{B_i}u_{t,i,\ell}. $$
Induction on $j$ gives
$$ \beta^{-j}U_{t,j}(B)\le a_{t,j}(B)\le U_{t,j}(B). $$
Indeed, assuming the lower bound at stage $j-1$ and writing the next prefix value as $v\ge0$,
$$ a_{t,j}(B) \ge \frac{a_{t,j-1}(B)+v}{\beta} \ge \beta^{-j}\bigl(U_{t,j-1}(B)+v\bigr). $$
The upper bound follows from rounding downward.

Consequently,
$$ a_{t,m}(B)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u_t(B)=0. $$
Define the rounded capped utility
$$ \widetilde f_t(B)= \begin{cases} \min\{c_t,a_{t,m}(B)\},&c_t<+\infty,\\ a_{t,m}(B),&c_t=+\infty. \end{cases} $$
Taking a positive cap preserves the multiplicative bound:
$$ \beta^{-m}f_t(B)\le\widetilde f_t(B)\le f_t(B). $$
For positive utility,
$$ 0\le\log f_t(B)-\log\widetilde f_t(B) \le m\log\beta \le m\delta =\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. $$

2.2 Rational logarithmic scores

For every type and positive terminal grid state $a$, compute a rational lower approximation $\ell_{t,a}$ satisfying
$$ \log\min\{c_t,a\}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2} \le\ell_{t,a} \le\log\min\{c_t,a\}, $$
with the cap omitted when uncapped.

These approximations are computable in polynomial time. To make this explicit, for a positive rational $z$, choose an integer $h$ such that
$$ z=2^h y,\qquad 1\le y<2. $$
For $v=(y-1)/(y+1)$, we have $0\le v<1/3$, and
$$ \log y=2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{v^{2i+1}}{2i+1}. $$
The same series with $v=1/3$ computes $\log 2$. After $n$ terms, the remainder is at most
$$ 3\cdot 3^{-(2n+1)}. $$
Thus rational upper and lower bounds for
$$ \log z=h\log 2+\log y $$
of prescribed inverse-polynomial accuracy require only polynomially many bit operations. For negative $h$, the upper bound for $\log 2$ is used when forming a lower bound for $h\log 2$. Rounding the resulting lower bound downward to a sufficiently fine dyadic rational preserves the required error bound.

Order the positive grid states increasingly and replace each $\ell_{t,a}$ by the maximum of the lower approximations up to that state. This makes the scores nondecreasing without invalidating either bound: logarithmic capped utility is itself nondecreasing.

Set
$$ s_t(B)= \begin{cases} 0,&a_{t,m}(B)=0,\\ \ell_{t,a_{t,m}(B)},&a_{t,m}(B)>0. \end{cases} $$
Combining the two approximation errors gives
$$ \log f_t(B)-\varepsilon\le s_t(B)\le\log f_t(B) $$
whenever $f_t(B)>0$.

The rounded accumulation is coordinatewise nondecreasing, because prefix values and $\rho$ are nondecreasing. Therefore $s_t$ is nondecreasing between positive-utility bundles.

For an unrestricted bundle, define
$$ s_t(B)=s_t(\widehat B). $$
All the preceding properties continue to hold.

2.3 The flow linear programs

For each type $t$, construct a directed layered graph.

At layer zero, the sole reachable state is $0$. At layer $j$, states are rounded accumulated utilities after considering goods $1,\ldots,j$.

From a reachable state $a$ at layer $j-1$, include an edge for every choice $k\in\{0,\ldots,K_j\}$, leading to
$$ \rho\left(a+\sum_{\ell=1}^k u_{t,j,\ell}\right). $$
This edge consumes $k$ copies of good $j$. Every reachable transition has argument at most $W$, because rounded accumulated utility never exceeds actual prefix utility.

Join each final state to a sink. Give this terminal edge:

  • primary coefficient $1$ for a positive state and $0$ for state zero;
  • secondary coefficient $\ell_{t,a}$ for a positive state and $0$ for state zero.

Send flow $\mu_t$ through the graph of type $t$. Impose flow conservation and the $m$ aggregate supply inequalities.

Every truncated bundle determines exactly one path. Conversely, every source-to-sink path determines a truncated bundle, because its edges specify each coordinate. Thus:

  • a distribution on truncated bundles gives a feasible flow with the same supplies and objectives;
  • a feasible flow decomposes into paths, giving a distribution with the same supplies and objectives.

The two optimization problems are therefore equivalent.

Solve two rational linear programs: first maximize the primary objective, obtaining $p^*$; then impose primary objective $p^*$ and maximize the secondary objective.

The feasible flow polytope is nonempty, since empty-bundle paths are available. It is bounded, since every edge flow is at most the corresponding type mass. All coefficients are rational. Hence exact rational optimization and polynomial-size rational output follow from the polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming. [Polynomial-time linear programming](https://lucatrevisan.github.io/cs261/lecture05.pdf)

Decompose the resulting flows into paths. Repeatedly subtract the minimum positive edge flow along a positive-flow path. Each iteration eliminates an edge, so the total number of supported paths is at most the number of graph edges.

3. Completing supplies without losing surrogate optimality

Let $y$ be the truncated allocation just computed, and let
$$ a_j=\sum_{t,B}B_jy_{t,B}\le q_j. $$
Choose a supported pair $(t_0,B^0)$ of rational mass $\alpha>0$. Its required mean increment is
$$ v_j=\frac{q_j-a_j}{\alpha}. $$
Write
$$ v_j=d_j+\theta_j,\qquad d_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad 0\le\theta_j<1. $$

Replace that supported pair by the distribution of bundles
$$ B^0+d+\bigl(\mathbf 1[h<\theta_j]\bigr)_{j=1}^m, $$
where $h$ is uniform on $[0,1]$. The distribution changes only at the $m$ rational thresholds $\theta_j$, so it has at most $m+1$ bundles with rational masses. Its expected increment is exactly $v$.

The resulting allocation $x$ satisfies every supply equality.

Adding goods cannot decrease the pair $(P,G)$ lexicographically. Positive-utility mass cannot decrease; if it stays unchanged, the scores of positive-utility bundles cannot decrease.

On the other hand, truncating any unrestricted feasible allocation produces a feasible truncated allocation for the supply inequalities, preserving $P$ and $G$. Therefore no unrestricted feasible allocation has a lexicographic objective above the flow optimum. It follows that the completed allocation $x$ is itself a lexicographic maximizer of $(P,G)$ over all feasible finite-support allocations.

This fact will be used throughout the remaining proof.

4. Welfare guarantee

If $P(x)<1$, lexicographic optimality implies that no feasible allocation has positive utility throughout its support. Every feasible allocation consequently has NSW zero, and
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=0. $$

Suppose instead that $P(x)=1$. Let $y$ be any feasible allocation with positive NSW. Then $P(y)=1$, and secondary optimality gives
$$ G(x)\ge G(y). $$
The uniform score bounds imply
$$ F(x)\ge G(x)\ge G(y)\ge F(y)-\varepsilon. $$
Exponentiating and taking the supremum over feasible $y$ yields
$$ \exp(\varepsilon)\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Moreover,
$$ \log r =\log(1+\gamma/4) \ge\frac{\gamma}{4+\gamma} \ge\frac{\gamma}{5} >\varepsilon. $$
The first inequality follows by integrating $1/(1+z)$ over $[0,\gamma/4]$ and bounding the integrand below by its endpoint value.

Thus
$$ \exp(\varepsilon)<r<A_\gamma, $$
so the requested welfare guarantee holds.

5. Continuous approximate EF1

We prove that every supported comparison satisfies the factor $2+\gamma$.

5.1 Two valuation inequalities

For a type $t$ and bundle $B$, define
$$ a_{t,j}(B)=f_t(B+e_j)-f_t(B). $$
These gains are nonnegative and nonincreasing under coordinatewise increases of $B$. Explicitly, the gain is the next raw marginal value, limited by the remaining room below the cap. Both quantities can only decrease as coordinates increase.

Consequently, for every integral $D\ge0$,
$$ f_t(D)\le f_t(B+D) \le f_t(B)+\sum_jD_j a_{t,j}(B). $$

Next, fix a type $s$ and bundle $C$ with $V=f_s(C)>0$. For $C_j>0$, put
$$ d_j=V-f_s(C-e_j). $$
Then
$$ \sum_{j:C_j>0}C_jd_j\le V. $$
To prove this, write
$$ W_C=u_s(C),\qquad w_j=u_{s,j,C_j}. $$
Diminishing marginals give
$$ \sum_jC_jw_j\le W_C. $$
If uncapped, or $W_C\le c_s$, then $d_j=w_j$, proving the inequality.

Otherwise let $h=W_C-c_s>0$. Then
$$ d_j=\max\{w_j-h,0\}. $$
For $J=\{j:d_j>0\}$, the claim is immediate if $J$ is empty. If $J$ is nonempty,
$$ \sum_jC_jd_j =\sum_{j\in J}C_jw_j-h\sum_{j\in J}C_j \le W_C-h =c_s =V, $$
because $\sum_{j\in J}C_j\ge1$.

5.2 Exchange inequalities from surrogate optimality

Fix supported pairs $(t,B)$ and $(s,C)$, with $C\ne0$, and write
$$ U=f_t(B),\qquad V=f_s(C). $$

One copy of good $j$ can be transferred from $C$ to $B$ on equal sufficiently small positive masses. This preserves type masses and supplies. If the supported pairs coincide, use two disjoint portions of that mass.

Whenever the transfer preserves positive-utility mass, secondary optimality says that the sum of the two new scores is at most the sum of the two old scores.

If all four involved utilities are positive, the score errors therefore give
$$ \log\frac{f_t(B+e_j)}{U} + \log\frac{f_s(C-e_j)}{V} \le2\varepsilon. $$

Set
$$ \eta=\exp(2\varepsilon)-1. $$
Since $\varepsilon\le1/64$,
$$ \eta\le4\varepsilon. $$
Indeed, for $0\le z\le1/2$, the power series gives
$$ \exp(z)\le\frac1{1-z}, \qquad \exp(z)-1\le2z. $$

We now consider all possibilities for $U$ and $V$.

5.3 Zero-utility recipient

Suppose $U=0$. If some present good $j$ satisfies $f_t(C-e_j)=0$, the desired fairness inequality holds.

Otherwise every deletion leaves positive utility for $t$. Hence $C$ contains at least two copies, counted with multiplicity, of goods whose first marginal value to $t$ is positive. If there were at most one such copy, deleting it would leave zero utility.

Since $f_t(B)=0$ and the cap is positive, $B$ contains no copy of any such good. Transferring any one of them to $B$ creates positive utility.

If $V=0$, removing a copy leaves the donor at zero utility, so this transfer increases $P$, a contradiction.

If $V>0$, a deletion destroys donor positivity only if it removes the donor’s unique copy of a good with positive first marginal value and there are no other valuable goods in $C$. Among the at least two copies valuable to $t$, choose one other than that unique copy, if it exists. Its transfer preserves donor positivity and again increases $P$, a contradiction.

Thus some deletion gives $f_t(C-e_j)=0$.

5.4 Positive recipient and zero-utility donor

Suppose $U>0$ and $V=0$. Removing any copy leaves donor utility zero. The score comparison, with only the recipient having positive utility, gives
$$ \log\frac{U+a_{t,j}(B)}{U}\le\varepsilon. $$
Thus, for every present good,
$$ \frac{a_{t,j}(B)}{U}\le\exp(\varepsilon)-1\le\eta. $$

Choose any present good $g$, and put
$$ D_j=\min\{C_j-\mathbf1[j=g],K_j\}. $$
Truncation preserves utility, so
$$ f_t(C-e_g)=f_t(D) \le U+\sum_jD_ja_{t,j}(B) \le U(1+K\eta). $$
Since $K\eta\le4K\varepsilon\le\kappa/16$, this is smaller than $(2+\gamma)U$.

5.5 Both utilities positive

Suppose $U>0$ and $V>0$. Define
$$ h_j=\frac{d_j}{V} $$
for present goods. The removal-loss inequality gives
$$ \sum_jC_jh_j\le1. $$

Whenever $h_j<1$, donor positivity survives deletion. The exchange inequality implies
$$ \left(1+\frac{a_{t,j}(B)}U\right)(1-h_j) \le1+\eta, $$
and therefore
$$ \frac{a_{t,j}(B)}U \le\frac{h_j+\eta}{1-h_j}. $$

Choose a present good $g$ maximizing $h_j$, and put
$$ n_j=\min\{C_j-\mathbf1[j=g],K_j\}. $$

For every $j$ with $n_j>0$, we have $h_j\le1/2$. To see this, a value exceeding $1/2$ can occur on at most one copy, because $\sum_jC_jh_j\le1$. That copy must maximize $h_j$ and is precisely the copy removed.

We also have
$$ \sum_{j:n_j>0}\frac{n_jh_j}{1-h_j}\le1. $$
If $h_g<1$, maximality of $h_g$ gives
$$ \sum_j\frac{n_jh_j}{1-h_j} \le \frac{\sum_j(C_j-\mathbf1[j=g])h_j}{1-h_g} \le \frac{1-h_g}{1-h_g} =1. $$
If $h_g=1$, the loss bound forces $C_g=1$ and all other $h_j=0$, so the sum is zero.

Applying the diminishing-gain inequality to the truncated bundle after deletion,
$$ \frac{f_t(C-e_g)}U \le 1+\sum_{j:n_j>0}n_j\frac{a_{t,j}(B)}U $$
$$ \le 1+\sum_j\frac{n_jh_j}{1-h_j} +\eta\sum_j\frac{n_j}{1-h_j} \le 2+2K\eta \le 2+8K\varepsilon \le 2+\frac{\kappa}{8} \le 2+\gamma. $$

All supported comparisons have now been covered. Therefore $x$ satisfies continuous approximate EF1.

The use of $K$ rather than the possibly enormous size of $C$ is essential: truncation removes every utility-irrelevant surplus copy from the gain calculation.

6. Uncapped approximate Pareto optimality

Assume every type is uncapped. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that a reassignment $z$ as in the question satisfies
$$ u_t(C)\ge r\,u_t(B) $$
on every positive reassigned mass, with strict inequality on positive mass.

Aggregate it into the feasible finite-support allocation
$$ y_{t,C}=\sum_Bz_{t,B,C}. $$

Positive baseline utility remains positive. If any positive mass moves from zero to positive utility, then
$$ P(y)>P(x), $$
contradicting primary optimality. Thus zero-utility mass remains at zero, and
$$ P(y)=P(x)=p. $$

If $p=0$, all baseline and final utilities are zero, contradicting the required strict improvement.

If $p>0$, the multiplicative improvement on positive baseline mass gives
$$ F(y)\ge F(x)+p\log r. $$
The uniform surrogate bounds then imply
$$ G(y) \ge F(y)-p\varepsilon \ge F(x)+p(\log r-\varepsilon) >F(x) \ge G(x). $$
This contradicts secondary optimality because $\varepsilon<\log r$.

Hence the stated continuous $r$-approximate Pareto guarantee holds.

7. Running time and output encoding

Every positive input marginal lies between $2^{-L}$ and $2^L$, so
$$ \log(W/\lambda)\le2L+\log(K+1). $$
Since $\log(1+\delta)\ge\delta/2$ for our choice of $\delta$,
$$ R+1 = O\left( 1+\frac{m(L+\log(K+1))}{\varepsilon} \right) = O_\gamma\left( m(K+1)(L+\log(K+1)+1) \right). $$

Each type graph has at most $O(m(R+1))$ nodes and
$$ O\bigl((R+1)(K+m+1)\bigr) $$
edges. Thus the two linear programs have
$$ D= O\bigl(\tau(R+1)(K+m+1)\bigr) $$
variables and $O(\tau m(R+1)+m)$ constraints.

Grid numbers have bit length
$$ O\left(L+R\log(m/\varepsilon+2)\right). $$
Transitions, rational logarithmic approximations, and all LP coefficients are computable with polynomial bit length and polynomial work in these parameters. The two exact rational LP optimizations therefore take
$$ \operatorname{poly}_\gamma(m,\tau,K,L) $$
bit operations, equivalently
$$ \operatorname{poly}_\gamma(m,\tau,S,L). $$
This bound depends on the explicitly listed sequence lengths, not on the numerical supplies or reciprocals of type masses.

Path decomposition uses at most $D$ paths. Supply completion increases support by at most $m$, giving at most $D+m$ supported type–bundle pairs.

The LP output has polynomial encoding length. Path decomposition preserves polynomial bit length: a common denominator of all rational edge flows has polynomial bit length, and every extracted path mass is an integer multiple of its reciprocal. In supply completion, division by a positive rational path mass, taking integer parts, and computing threshold differences also preserve polynomial bit length. Thus even very large surplus bundle coordinates have polynomial binary encodings.

Consequently, the algorithm outputs a feasible rational finite-support allocation in polynomial time, with all three requested guarantees. No equal-weight expansion into a finite society is required.
