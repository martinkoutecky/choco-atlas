Theorem 4.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) alternatives and a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over report types \((S,\pi)\), where \(S\subseteq[n]\) and \(\pi\) ranks the alternatives in \(S\), with each pair appearing in reports of total mass at least \(p\), find a complete ranking \(\sigma\) maximizing \(\sum_{(S,\pi)}\mu_{S,\pi}\left|\{\{i,j\}\subseteq S:\sigma\text{ and }\pi\text{ order }i,j\text{ identically}\}\right|\). For fixed \(\beta>0\), this is the weighted selective-Mallows likelihood objective up to terms independent of \(\sigma\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=n\ge2\) be the number of alternatives. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) report types \((S,\pi)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_{S,\pi}\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(S\subseteq[m]\) and \(\pi\) is a strict total order on \(S\).
The input includes a binary rational \(p\in(0,1]\), with \(\sum_{(S,\pi):\{i,j\}\subseteq S}\mu_{S,\pi}\ge p\) for every distinct \(i,j\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Find a strict complete ranking \(\sigma\) maximizing \(A(\sigma)=\sum_{(S,\pi)}\mu_{S,\pi}|\{\{i,j\}\subseteq S:\sigma\text{ and }\pi\text{ agree on the pair}\}|\). Any maximizer is admissible. The distribution is arbitrary subject to the stated conditions.
For fixed \(\beta>0\), this also maximizes weighted selective-Mallows log-likelihood: each report contributes \(-\beta d_{KT}(\sigma|_S,\pi)-\log Z(|S|,\beta)\), where the normalizer depends only on subset size and \(\beta\).

Is exact maximization of \(A\) polynomial-time solvable, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), including any dependence on \(p\).

For independent selective-Mallows reports with every pair occurring in at least a \(p\) fraction of reports, exact maximum-likelihood aggregation has a high-probability algorithm polynomial for fixed positive \(p,\beta\) ([Fotakis–Kalavasis–Stavropoulos](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/fotakis21a/fotakis21a.pdf)).

Result

Exact maximization is NP-hard, even when \(p=1\), every report is complete, and \(\tau\le 4\). Thus a polynomial-time algorithm for arbitrary valid inputs would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

An exact algorithm runs in
\(O\!\left(L^3+(\tau m^2+m2^m)(L+\log m)\right)\)
bit operations under the usual binary encoding of rational numerators and denominators. Its running time has no additional dependence on \(p\).

1. Pairwise formulation and likelihood equivalence

Write \(i\prec_\pi j\) when report \(\pi\) places \(i\) before \(j\). For distinct alternatives, define
\(w_{ij}=\sum_{(S,\pi):\,i,j\in S,\ i\prec_\pi j}\mu_{S,\pi}\).

Each reported pair agrees with a complete ranking precisely when that ranking chooses its reported orientation. Consequently,
\(A(\sigma)=\sum_{i\prec_\sigma j}w_{ij}\).
The coverage hypothesis is exactly
\(w_{ij}+w_{ji}\ge p\)
for every distinct \(i,j\).

Define the ranking-independent quantity
\(C=\sum_{(S,\pi)}\mu_{S,\pi}\binom{|S|}{2}\).
For each report, agreement and disagreement partition its unordered pairs. Therefore
\(\sum_{(S,\pi)}\mu_{S,\pi}d_{KT}(\sigma|_S,\pi)=C-A(\sigma)\).

The weighted log-likelihood in the question is thus
\(\ell(\sigma)=\beta A(\sigma)-\beta C-\sum_{(S,\pi)}\mu_{S,\pi}\log Z(|S|,\beta)\).
The last two terms are independent of \(\sigma\). Since \(\beta>0\), its maximizers are exactly those of \(A\). This equivalence requires neither a sampling assumption nor numerical evaluation of the normalizers.

2. Polynomial reduction to rational supports

We use the following established theorem: computing a Kemeny-optimal complete ranking from four complete rankings is NP-hard, and the corresponding distance-threshold decision problem is NP-complete. This is Theorem 11 in Appendix B of [Dwork–Kumar–Naor–Sivakumar](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~naor/PAPERS/rank_www10.html).

Let \(\rho_1,\rho_2,\rho_3,\rho_4\) be an instance on \([m]\), with objective
\(D(\sigma)=\sum_{a=1}^4d_{KT}(\sigma,\rho_a)\).

Construct the support as follows. For each distinct ranking \(\rho\) among these four rankings, include the type \(([m],\rho)\). If it occurs \(c_\rho\) times, assign mass
\(\mu_{[m],\rho}=c_\rho/4\).
Set \(p=1\).

This construction satisfies every promise:

  • There are at most four distinct support types, so \(\tau\le4\).
  • Every mass is positive and dyadic, with constant encoding length.
  • The masses sum to \(1\), because \(\sum_\rho c_\rho=4\).
  • Every report contains every pair, so each pair has coverage exactly \(1\).

For every complete ranking,
\(A(\sigma)=\binom m2-\frac14D(\sigma)\).
Hence every maximizer of \(A\) minimizes \(D\). The allowance that any maximizer is admissible therefore does not weaken the reduction.

Merging four explicitly given rankings and assigning their masses takes polynomial time and produces an instance of polynomial bit length. An exact optimizer for the stated problem would consequently solve four-ranking Kemeny aggregation in polynomial time.

The reduction also preserves decision thresholds: for an integer threshold \(K\),
\(D(\sigma)\le K\)
if and only if
\(A(\sigma)\ge\binom m2-K/4\).
The transformed threshold has polynomial encoding length.

For completeness, the rational-threshold decision version belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\): a certificate is a complete ranking, whose score can be evaluated and compared with the threshold using polynomial-time exact rational arithmetic. The stated promises can also be checked in polynomial time. Thus that decision version is NP-complete.

Hardness at \(p=1\) also gives hardness for every fixed rational coverage bound \(p_0\in(0,1]\): the same constructed society satisfies coverage at least \(p_0\).

3. An exact algorithm and its bit complexity

Integer preprocessing

Index the support types by \(t\in[\tau]\), and write their masses as
\(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), with positive binary-encoded integers. Set
\(Q=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t\)
and
\(v_t=a_t(Q/b_t)\).

Then \(v_t=Q\mu_t\) is a positive integer and
\(\sum_t v_t=Q\).
Define integer pair weights
\(W_{ij}=Qw_{ij}\), and set \(W_{ii}=0\).

Compute these weights by adding \(v_t\) to \(W_{ij}\) whenever report \(t\) places \(i\) before \(j\). This requires at most
\(\sum_t\binom{|S_t|}{2}=O(\tau m^2)\)
integer additions.

The bit length of \(Q\) is at most one plus the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Each pair weight is at most \(Q\), and every ranking score is at most \(Q\binom m2\). Accordingly, all score arithmetic uses
\(B=O(L+\log m)\)
bits. Constructing \(Q\) and all \(v_t\) costs at most \(O(L^3)\) bit operations using schoolbook multiplication and division.

Subset dynamic programming

For \(T\subseteq[m]\), let \(F(T)\) be the maximum integer agreement score among orders of \(T\), counting only pairs within \(T\). Set \(F(\varnothing)=0\).

For nonempty \(T\), use
\(F(T)=\max_{x\in T}\left(F(T\setminus\{x\})+\sum_{y\in T\setminus\{x\}}W_{yx}\right)\).

To prove this recurrence, consider any order of \(T\) ending in \(x\). Its pairs partition into pairs wholly within \(T\setminus\{x\}\) and pairs involving \(x\). The latter contribute exactly \(\sum_{y\in T\setminus\{x\}}W_{yx}\), because every such \(y\) precedes \(x\). Conversely, appending \(x\) to an optimal order of \(T\setminus\{x\}\) achieves the corresponding expression. Maximizing over the possible last elements proves the recurrence.

Precompute
\(G_x(U)=\sum_{y\in U}W_{yx}\)
for every \(x\) and \(U\subseteq[m]\setminus\{x\}\). Starting from \(G_x(\varnothing)=0\), for any chosen \(y\in U\),
\(G_x(U)=G_x(U\setminus\{y\})+W_{yx}\).
This takes \(O(m2^m)\) additions. The recurrence for \(F\) then takes \(O(m2^m)\) additions and comparisons. Recording a maximizing last element at each state permits reconstruction of an optimal ranking.

Combining preprocessing and dynamic programming gives:

  • Time: \(O\!\left(L^3+(\tau m^2+m2^m)(L+\log m)\right)\) bit operations.
  • Space: \(O\!\left((m^2+m2^m)(L+\log m)+L\right)\) bits.

These bounds process the support directly. They contain no factor involving \(1/p\).

4. Why the probabilistic result does not imply worst-case tractability

[Fotakis–Kalavasis–Stavropoulos, Theorem 4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/fotakis21a/fotakis21a.pdf) assumes independent selective-Mallows reports around a common central ranking and a pair-frequency condition. Its guarantee holds with high probability over those reports.

Here, the support and masses are arbitrary. Pair coverage alone does not impose the sampling assumptions needed for that theorem; indeed, the hard instances above have maximal coverage.

Also, support size \(\tau\) is not sample size. Expanding rational masses into repeated reports can require exponentially many reports: a type of mass \(2^{-k}\) requires any exact empirical representation to have a total number of reports divisible by \(2^k\), although that mass has encoding length \(O(k)\).

Therefore fixed positive \(p\) and \(\beta\) do not make arbitrary rational-support inputs polynomial-time solvable unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). The hardness reduction and exact algorithm above settle the stated worst-case question.
