The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of insurance types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\), per-member expected losses \(\ell_t\), type certainty-equivalent loadings \(\vartheta_t\), and a grand-coalition outcome with per-capita reinsurer certainty-equivalent profit \(\pi\), is \(\vartheta_t<\pi/\bar\ell\) for every supported type, where \(\bar\ell=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\ell_t\), a sufficient certificate that no proper positive-mass coalition \(q\leq\mu\) can form a price-fair contract giving the reinsurer and every participating type at least their grand-coalition utility?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(T\), the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(m\) count jointly specified loss scenarios and \(L\) be the total bit length of rational data; state computational bounds in \(m,\tau,L\).
On these scenarios, type losses \(X_t\geq0\) have means \(\ell_t>0\). Define \(S_q=\sum_tq_tX_t\), \(M_q=\mathbb E[S_q]\), and \(\bar\ell=M_\mu\); require \(S_\mu\) nonconstant. Utilities \(U_t,U_R:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) are twice continuously differentiable, strictly increasing and strictly concave; initial wealths are real and the reinsurance deadweight loading is \(c\geq0\).
A proper coalition has real masses \(0\leq q_t\leq\mu_t\) with \(0<\sum_tq_t<1\), and must include the reinsurer. Its contract has \(P\geq0\), per-member risk densities \(a_t\geq0\), and \(a_R\in[0,1]\), satisfying \(a_R+\sum_tq_ta_t=1\). Side payments \(b_t\in\mathbb R\) satisfy \(\sum_tq_tb_t=P\) and price fairness \(\mathbb E[a_tS_q+b_t]=(1+\rho)\ell_t\), with \(\rho=P/M_q-a_R\), for every \(q_t>0\).
Terminal wealths are \(W_t=w_t-a_tS_q-b_t\) and \(W_R=w_R+P-(1+c)a_RS_q\). The same contract definition applies to the grand society \(q=\mu\).
The supplied grand outcome is attainable by such a contract and strictly improves every participant's disagreement utility: \(\hat u_R>U_R(w_R)\) and \(\hat u_t>U_t(w_t-(1+\theta)\ell_t)\), where \(\theta\geq0\) and \(U_t(w_t-(1+\theta)\ell_t)\geq\mathbb E[U_t(w_t-X_t)]\). Its supplied rational CE summaries satisfy \(\hat u_t=U_t(w_t-(1+\vartheta_t)\ell_t)\) and \(\hat u_R=U_R(w_R+\pi)\), with \(\pi>0\). Because total society mass is one, \(\pi\) is also the total CE profit; it is held fixed when comparing coalitions.
A coalition is viable when some contract satisfies \(\mathbb E[U_R(W_R)]\geq\hat u_R\) and \(\mathbb E[U_t(W_t)]\geq\hat u_t\) for every \(q_t>0\); no strict improvement is required.

Does \(\vartheta_t<\pi/\bar\ell\) for every supported type give a sound polynomial-time certificate that no proper positive-mass coalition is viable, or can a viable coalition satisfy these inequalities?

For finite peer lists, the verified sufficient condition is \(\vartheta_i<\Pi/(\mathbb E[S]-\min_{j\ne i}\mathbb E[X_j])\) for every peer; it rules out even weakly improving proper coalitions. This is a sufficient test, not an exact stability algorithm. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09859v1#Thm4.3)

Conclusion

The proposed inequalities are a sound polynomial-time sufficient test: no viable proper coalition can satisfy them.

However, under the stated promise that the grand outcome is attainable, the inequalities cannot hold. Thus the test is vacuous on the promised instances. Both conclusions follow directly from price fairness and Jensen’s inequality; no assumption of optimality of the grand contract is needed.

1. Necessary conditions for coalition viability

Fix a positive-mass coalition \(q\) and suppose a contract makes it viable. Its mean loss is positive:
\[ M_q=\sum_t q_t\ell_t>0. \]
Here and below, inequalities concerning a member apply only when \(q_t>0\).

Price fairness gives
\[ \mathbb E[W_t]=w_t-(1+\rho)\ell_t. \]
Concavity, viability, and the supplied CE representation therefore imply
\[ U_t\!\left(w_t-(1+\rho)\ell_t\right) \geq \mathbb E[U_t(W_t)] \geq U_t\!\left(w_t-(1+\vartheta_t)\ell_t\right). \]
Since \(U_t\) is strictly increasing and \(\ell_t>0\), this yields
\[ \rho\leq\vartheta_t. \]

For the reinsurer, the same argument gives
\[ U_R\!\left(w_R+P-(1+c)a_RM_q\right) \geq \mathbb E[U_R(W_R)] \geq U_R(w_R+\pi). \]
Strict monotonicity implies
\[ P-(1+c)a_RM_q\geq\pi. \]
Using \(P=(\rho+a_R)M_q\), we obtain
\[ (\rho-ca_R)M_q\geq\pi, \qquad \rho\geq\frac{\pi}{M_q}+ca_R\geq\frac{\pi}{M_q}. \]

Combining these bounds proves the necessary condition
\[ \boxed{\quad \vartheta_t\geq\rho\geq\frac{\pi}{M_q} \quad\text{for every participating type.}\quad} \]
Only weak utility improvement was used, as required.

2. Every proper coalition is excluded

Because \(q_t\leq\mu_t\) for every type and \(\sum_tq_t<\sum_t\mu_t=1\), at least one type has \(q_t<\mu_t\). Every \(\ell_t\) is positive, so
\[ \bar\ell-M_q=\sum_t(\mu_t-q_t)\ell_t>0. \]
Consequently,
\[ \frac{\pi}{M_q}>\frac{\pi}{\bar\ell}. \]

A positive-mass coalition contains at least one supported type. Viability would require every participating type to satisfy
\[ \vartheta_t\geq\frac{\pi}{M_q}>\frac{\pi}{\bar\ell}, \]
contradicting the proposed inequalities. This covers arbitrary real coalition masses, including coalitions containing only fractions of types.

3. Why the certificate is vacuous under grand attainability

Apply Section 1 to an attaining grand contract, with \(q=\mu\). It follows that
\[ \vartheta_t\geq\frac{\pi}{\bar\ell} \qquad\text{for every supported type.} \]
Thus even one strict inequality in the proposed direction contradicts grand attainability.

The assumption that \(S_\mu\) is nonconstant strengthens this conclusion: at least one supported type satisfies
\[ \vartheta_t>\frac{\pi}{\bar\ell}. \]

To prove this, consider the grand contract’s allocation.

  • If \(a_R>0\), the reinsurer’s wealth is nonconstant. Strict concavity makes its Jensen inequality strict, giving \(\rho>\pi/\bar\ell\). Hence every \(\vartheta_t>\pi/\bar\ell\).
  • If \(a_R=0\), market clearing gives \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=1\), so some \(a_t>0\). That type’s wealth is nonconstant, and strict Jensen gives \(\vartheta_t>\rho\geq\pi/\bar\ell\).

The finite-peer sufficient condition quoted in the task has a smaller denominator, and therefore a larger threshold, than \(\Pi/\mathbb E[S]\). It does not have this immediate incompatibility. [Source: Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09859v1#Thm4.3). The mass-coalition conclusion above is proved independently.

4. Computational bound

Assume the scenario probabilities \(p_s\) are explicitly supplied rational data. Compute
\[ \bar\ell=\sum_{s=1}^{m}\sum_{t\in T}p_s\mu_tX_t(s), \]
then test the equivalent rational inequalities
\[ \vartheta_t\bar\ell<\pi \qquad(t\in T). \]

This takes \(O(m\tau+\tau)\) exact rational arithmetic operations. Reduced intermediate fractions have \(O(L+\log(m\tau+1))\)-bit numerators and denominators. Elementary integer arithmetic therefore gives the conservative polynomial bound
\[ O\!\left((m\tau+\tau)\bigl(L+\log(m\tau+1)\bigr)^3\right) \]
bit operations.

This bounds evaluation of the sufficient test, taking the problem’s attainability and utility assumptions as promises. It is not an exact coalition-stability algorithm.
