The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given chore categories \(G\), worker types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), costs \(c_{tg}\), and integer per-worker limits \(h_g\), does there exist a rational mass table \(x_{t,b}\geq 0\) over integral legal bundles \(b\in\prod_{g\in G}\{0,\ldots,h_g\}\) such that \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\), and every pair of positive-mass cohorts satisfies the exact chore-PMMS inequalities under their respective type costs?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a set of \(m\) chore categories. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) worker types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also specifies nonnegative rational supplies \(q_g\), nonnegative rational costs \(c_{tg}\), and nonnegative integer per-worker limits \(h_g\), all binary encoded. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length. Copies within each category are identical, and \(c_t(b)=\sum_{g\in G}c_{tg}b_g\).
Legal worker bundles are \(B=\prod_{g\in G}\{0,\ldots,h_g\}\), including the empty bundle. For an integral multiset vector \(s\), define \(\beta_t(s)=\min_{a\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{G},\,a\leq s}\max\{c_t(a),c_t(s-a)\}\). These bipartitions allow empty parts and are not constrained by the limits \(h_g\).
A feasible allocation is a rational table \(x_{t,b}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{b\in B}x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T,b\in B}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\) for every \(g\).
It is exact chore-PMMS if \(c_t(b)\leq\beta_t(b+d)\) for every ordered pair of positive-mass cohorts \((t,b),(u,d)\). This includes comparisons within a type and within a cohort, since each positive-mass cohort contains distinct workers; \(b+d\) represents two workers' combined multiset.

Is deciding whether such an exact chore-PMMS allocation exists in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents and indivisible chores with nonnegative additive costs, exact PMMS existence is NP-hard, even with strictly positive integer costs ([Bei et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10493v1)). No result settling the mass-table problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Costs and supplies were given without sign domains; both are nonnegative rationals, consistent with additive chores and supplies.
  • Limits were given without a sign or encoding convention; they are nonnegative binary-encoded integers, and all other numeric input is also binary encoded.
  • The pairwise benchmark was undefined; it minimizes the maximum additive cost over all integral bipartitions of the combined multiset, allowing empty parts.
  • Whether worker limits restrict benchmark bipartitions was unspecified; they restrict allocated bundles only, since the discrete PMMS benchmark considers all bipartitions.
  • Comparisons involving the same type or cohort were unspecified; all ordered positive-mass cohort pairs are included, representing distinct workers in the nonatomic society.
  • The meanings of m and L were unspecified; m counts chore categories and L is the total input bit length.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even with one worker type, strictly positive integer costs, and all but one per-worker limit equal to \(\!1\).

The proof uses the stipulated rule that the bipartitions defining \(\beta_t\) need not respect the per-worker limits.

1. Reduction from PARTITION

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem PARTITION: given positive binary-encoded integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), decide whether some subset has sum \(\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\). Its hardness is established in [Karp’s original paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Write \(H=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\). Construct a society with one type \(t\), of mass \(\mu_t=1\), and categories \(G=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}\). Set

\(c_{t0}=1,\qquad h_0=H,\qquad q_0=H,\)

and, for every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), set

\(c_{ti}=2a_i,\qquad h_i=1,\qquad q_i=\frac12.\)

Suppress the type subscript below. A legal bundle has the form \(b=(b_0,z)\), where \(0\leq b_0\leq H\) is integral and \(z\in\{0,1\}^n\).

This construction has polynomial encoding length. Indeed, \(H\) has at most \(\max_i\operatorname{bits}(a_i)+\lceil\log_2 n\rceil+1\) bits, and neither bundles nor individual copies are enumerated.

2. Every supported bundle contains exactly \(H\) unit-cost chores

Let \(x\) be any feasible table. Since its total mass is \(1\), feasibility for category \(0\) gives

\(\sum_b x_b(H-b_0)=H\sum_b x_b-\sum_b x_bb_0=H-H=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative because \(b_0\leq H\). Consequently, \(x_b>0\) implies \(b_0=H\).

Thus every supported bundle is \(b=(H,z)\). Define

\(A(z)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i z_i.\)

Then \(0\leq A(z)\leq H\) and \(c(b)=H+2A(z)\).

3. Exact calculation of the pairwise benchmark

Lemma. For any \(z,w\in\{0,1\}^n\), with \(b=(H,z)\) and \(d=(H,w)\),

\(\beta(b+d)=H+A(z)+A(w).\)

Proof. Put \(A=A(z)\) and \(D=A(w)\). The combined multiset has total cost

\(c(b+d)=2H+2A+2D.\)

In every bipartition, the maximum of the two part costs is at least half their sum. Hence

\(\beta(b+d)\geq H+A+D.\)

For the reverse inequality, take the first part to contain the original-category vector \(z\) and exactly

\(k=H+D-A\)

copies of category \(0\). Because \(0\leq A,D\leq H\), we have \(0\leq k\leq2H\). Also \(z\leq z+w\) coordinatewise. This therefore defines an admissible integral submultiset of \(b+d\).

Its cost is

\(2A+k=H+A+D.\)

The complementary part has the same cost, since the total cost is twice this quantity. Therefore \(\beta(b+d)\leq H+A+D\), proving equality.

The count \(k\) can exceed \(h_0=H\); this is allowed in the benchmark by the problem’s definition. \(\square\)

4. Soundness: a PMMS table yields a partition

Suppose the constructed instance admits an exact chore-PMMS table.

For any two supported bundles \(b=(H,z)\) and \(d=(H,w)\), PMMS and the lemma imply

\(H+2A(z)\leq H+A(z)+A(w),\)

so \(A(z)\leq A(w)\). Applying PMMS to the reverse ordered pair gives \(A(w)\leq A(z)\). Thus all supported bundles have the same value \(A_*\) of \(A(z)\).

The supply equations determine that value:

\(A_*=\sum_b x_bA(z)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\sum_b x_bz_i=\sum_{i=1}^n a_iq_i=\frac H2.\)

Here the first equality uses \(\sum_bx_b=1\).

The support is nonempty. Choosing any supported \(z\), the subset \(\{i:z_i=1\}\) consequently has weight \(H/2\). The PARTITION instance is a yes-instance.

5. Completeness: a partition yields a PMMS table

Conversely, suppose some \(z\in\{0,1\}^n\) satisfies \(A(z)=H/2\). Its complement satisfies \(A(\mathbf1-z)=H/2\).

Assign mass \(1/2\) to each of the legal bundles

\(b=(H,z),\qquad d=(H,\mathbf1-z),\)

and zero mass to every other bundle. This rational table has total mass \(1\), supplies \(H\) copies of category \(0\), and supplies \(1/2\) copy of every other category.

Both supported bundles have cost \(2H\). For every ordered pair of supported bundles, including a bundle compared with itself, the lemma gives benchmark \(2H\). Hence every required PMMS inequality holds with equality.

6. Conclusion

The construction admits an exact chore-PMMS allocation if and only if the original PARTITION instance admits a partition. It is a polynomial-time many-one reduction satisfying every stated input restriction.

Therefore the requested existence problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
