The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite goods \(G\), finite types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), type budgets \(e_t\), utilities \(U_{tj}\), and per-capita supplies \(q_j\), compute prices \(p_j\) and aggregate spending \(y_{tj}\) satisfying \(\sum_j y_{tj}=\mu_t e_t\), \(\sum_t y_{tj}=q_jp_j\), nonnegativity, and the bang-per-buck conditions, in time polynomial in \(|T|+|G|\), the positive-support size, and input bit length, independently of a population denominator \(N\) with \(\mu_t=a_t/N\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be divisible goods and \(T\) be types with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), budgets \(e_t>0\), per-capita supplies \(q_j>0\), and nonnegative linear utilities \(U_{tj}\). Each type and good has at least one positive-utility pair. An exact equilibrium consists of prices \(p_j>0\) and aggregate spending \(y_{tj}\geq 0\) such that \(\sum_j y_{tj}=\mu_t e_t\), \(\sum_t y_{tj}=q_jp_j\), and \(y_{tj}>0\) only if \(U_{tj}/p_j=\max_k U_{tk}/p_k\).

Is computing such an equilibrium in polynomial time in \(|T|+|G|\), the positive-utility support size, and input bit length, independently of \(N\) when \(\mu_t=a_t/N\), or is it NP-hard?

For discrete linear Fisher markets with integral buyer budgets and utilities, an exact equilibrium has a strongly polynomial algorithm with running time \(O((n^2\log n)(m+n\log n))\), where \(n\) counts buyers and goods and \(m\) counts positive-utility pairs ([Orlin, 2010](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol10171/DagSemProc.10171.2/DagSemProc.10171.2.pdf)). No separate result for this exact mass-encoded formulation was verified.

Conclusion and input convention

The exact equilibrium is computable in polynomial time in the encoded input size, without expanding the population into individual buyers. One buyer per type suffices.

As required for a bit-complexity statement, assume that budgets, supplies, and utilities, as well as masses, are rational numbers given in binary. Let \(L\) be their total encoding length, \(n=|T|+|G|\), and \(m=|\{(t,j):U_{tj}>0\}|\).

1. Reduction to a unit-supply Fisher market

For each type \(t\), introduce a single buyer with budget \(b_t=\mu_t e_t>0\). For each good \(j\), introduce one unit of a normalized good, with utility coefficient \(v_{tj}=q_jU_{tj}\).

The normalized unit represents the entire original supply \(q_j\). Accordingly, its price is \(P_j=q_jp_j\).

This transformation preserves equilibrium exactly. Indeed, the original spending conditions become

\(\displaystyle \sum_j y_{tj}=b_t,\qquad \sum_t y_{tj}=P_j.\)

Furthermore,

\(\displaystyle \frac{v_{tj}}{P_j}=\frac{q_jU_{tj}}{q_jp_j}=\frac{U_{tj}}{p_j}.\)

Thus the maximizing goods for every buyer are unchanged. Conversely, from any positive-price equilibrium \((P,y)\) of the normalized market, setting \(p_j=P_j/q_j\) recovers every required condition of the original problem.

The normalized market has exactly \(|T|\) buyers and \(|G|\) goods. Since \(q_j>0\), its positive-utility support is exactly the original support.

2. Reduction from rational to integral data

Write the positive rational budgets \(b_t\) and nonzero rational utilities \(v_{tj}\) in reduced form. Choose positive integers

\(\displaystyle D=\prod_{t\in T}\operatorname{den}(b_t),\qquad C=\prod_{(t,j):v_{tj}>0}\operatorname{den}(v_{tj}).\)

Define integral budgets and utilities by

\(\displaystyle B_t=Db_t,\qquad V_{tj}=Cv_{tj}.\)

Every \(B_t\) is a positive integer, and every \(V_{tj}\) is a nonnegative integer. The support remains unchanged.

Suppose \((\widehat P,\widehat y)\) is an exact equilibrium for this integral, unit-supply market. Set

\(\displaystyle p_j=\frac{\widehat P_j}{Dq_j},\qquad y_{tj}=\frac{\widehat y_{tj}}D.\)

The budget equations follow directly:

\(\displaystyle \sum_jy_{tj}=\frac{B_t}{D}=\mu_te_t.\)

The market-clearing equations also follow:

\(\displaystyle \sum_ty_{tj}=\frac{\widehat P_j}{D}=q_jp_j.\)

Finally,

\(\displaystyle \frac{U_{tj}}{p_j}=\frac{Dq_jU_{tj}}{\widehat P_j}=\frac DC\,\frac{V_{tj}}{\widehat P_j}.\)

The factor \(D/C\) is positive and independent of the good. Consequently, whenever \(y_{tj}>0\), the integral market’s equilibrium condition implies exactly the required maximum-bang-per-buck condition.

3. Applying the exact Fisher-market algorithm

The applicable result is Orlin’s exact algorithm for unit-supply linear Fisher markets with positive integral buyer budgets and nonnegative integral utilities, assuming every buyer and every good has a positive-utility incident pair. Its arithmetic-operation bound is \(O((n^2\log n)(m+n\log n))\). These are precisely the hypotheses established above. The paper’s formal model uses spending variables, so its output provides the needed aggregate spending as well as prices. [Orlin, 2010, Sections 1–1.1](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol10171/DagSemProc.10171.2/DagSemProc.10171.2.pdf)

Strict positivity of the computed prices is also available: the algorithm initializes each good at a strictly positive price under the incident-pair assumption and keeps prices nondecreasing. Hence \(\widehat P_j>0\), and therefore \(p_j>0\). [Orlin, 2010, Sections 1.4 and 3.1](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol10171/DagSemProc.10171.2/DagSemProc.10171.2.pdf)

Thus the reduction supplies an exact equilibrium satisfying all the conditions in the question.

4. Bit complexity and population encoding

The denominator clearing above does not cause an exponential increase in encoding length.

Each product \(b_t=\mu_te_t\) and \(v_{tj}=q_jU_{tj}\) has bit length \(O(L)\): multiplying two encoded rationals adds the bit lengths of their numerators and denominators, and reduction cannot increase them. Therefore the sum of the encoding lengths of all derived budgets and supported utilities is \(O((n+m)L)\).

The bit length of a product of positive integers is at most the sum of their bit lengths. Consequently, both \(D\) and \(C\), and all integral data \(B_t,V_{tj}\), have polynomial bit length. Their construction uses polynomial-time integer arithmetic.

Applying the strongly polynomial Fisher-market algorithm to this polynomial-size integral instance gives polynomial bit complexity; its arithmetic-operation bound should not be confused with a bit-operation bound. The final rational divisions likewise take polynomial time.

If \(\mu_t=a_t/N\), the algorithm simply forms \(b_t=a_te_t/N\). It never creates \(a_t\) buyers or loops over \(N\) individuals. Dependence on the binary encoding length of \(N\) is permitted and unavoidable when that encoding is part of the input; there is no dependence polynomial in its numerical value.

Hence the mass-encoded formulation is polynomial-time solvable by an explicit reduction to the stated exact Fisher-market algorithm.
