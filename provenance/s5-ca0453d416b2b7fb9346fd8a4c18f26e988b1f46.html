The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(A\), a finite support \(T\) of strict rankings with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), a target \(a^\star\), and a rational budget \(\beta\), does there exist deleted mass \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}x_t\leq\beta\) such that \(a^\star\) is in the upward uncovered set of the remaining society? For each ranking \(t\), let \(\sigma_t(a,b)=1\) if \(t\) ranks \(a\) above \(b\), and \(-1\) otherwise, and let \(D_x(a,b)=\sum_{t\in T}(\mu_t-x_t)\sigma_t(a,b)\). The required condition is that for every \(b\ne a^\star\), either \(D_x(b,a^\star)\leq0\), or there is a \(c\in A\) with \(D_x(c,b)>0\) and \(D_x(c,a^\star)\leq0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) be a finite nonempty candidate set, \(m=|A|\), and \(a^\star\in A\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau=|T|\) distinct strict rankings of \(A\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. The deletion budget is a binary rational \(\beta\geq0\).
Deletion chooses real masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_t x_t\leq\beta\); deleting all mass is permitted. For distinct candidates, let \(\sigma_t(a,b)=1\) if \(t\) ranks \(a\) above \(b\), and \(\sigma_t(a,b)=-1\) otherwise; set \(\sigma_t(a,a)=0\). Define \(D_x(a,b)=\sum_t(\mu_t-x_t)\sigma_t(a,b)\).
A candidate beats another exactly when its margin is positive; ties create no dominance edge. A candidate \(b\) upward covers \(a\) when \(D_x(b,a)>0\) and every \(c\in A\) satisfying \(D_x(c,b)>0\) also satisfies \(D_x(c,a)>0\). The upward uncovered set consists of candidates covered by none.
The decision problem asks whether an admissible deletion makes \(a^\star\) upward uncovered, equivalently whether \(\forall b\in A\setminus\{a^\star\}: D_x(b,a^\star)\leq0\ \lor\ \exists c\in A\,[D_x(c,b)>0\land D_x(c,a^\star)\leq0]\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the total input bit length \(L\).

For explicitly listed indivisible voters with strict rankings, constructive control by deleting at most a given number of voters to obtain upward uncovered membership is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard ([Berghammer–Schnoor](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221715003185)). No result settling the exact fractional deletion problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The budget's sign was unspecified; require a nonnegative binary rational budget, with no upper bound.
  • The candidate domain was unspecified; require a finite nonempty set, permitting a single candidate as in the discrete definition.
  • The diagonal comparison was undefined despite allowing every candidate as a covering witness; set diagonal signs and margins to zero.
  • Deletion masses are real and may split any supported type; remaining mass need not sum to one.
  • Permit deletion of all mass, consistent with the explicit empty-electorate convention; ties create no dominance edges and winning means membership, not unique membership.

Result

The fractional deletion problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete. Hardness holds even when all masses and the budget are dyadic rationals and the support size satisfies $\tau=O(m^2)$.

An exact deterministic algorithm runs in
$$ m^{m-1}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time. The proof below gives both this upper bound and a polynomial-time reduction from $3$-SAT. It does not rely on hardness for indivisible voters.

1. Membership in $\mathrm{NP}$ and an exact algorithm

For each candidate $b\ne a^\star$, guess one of the following reasons why $b$ does not cover $a^\star$:

  • $D_x(b,a^\star)\leq 0$;
  • a witness $c\in A\setminus\{a^\star,b\}$ satisfying $D_x(c,b)>0$ and $D_x(c,a^\star)\leq 0$.

Restricting witnesses this way loses no successful deletion: when $D_x(b,a^\star)>0$, neither $b$ nor $a^\star$ can be a witness.

For a fixed collection of guesses, introduce a common slack $\varepsilon$ and solve the rational linear program that maximizes $\varepsilon$, subject to
$$ 0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t,\qquad \sum_t x_t\leq\beta,\qquad 0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1, $$
together with the guessed non-strict inequalities and, for every guessed witness, the constraint
$$ D_x(c,b)\geq\varepsilon. $$

The guesses admit a deletion satisfying all required strict inequalities exactly when this program is feasible and its optimum is positive. Indeed, finitely many positive witness margins have a positive minimum. Conversely, a positive common slack makes every witness margin positive. If no witnesses were guessed, $\varepsilon$ is independent of the deletion constraints and can equal $1$.

This program has $\tau+1$ variables, $O(\tau+m)$ constraints, and rational encoding length polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. Polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore verifies the guesses in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time. Their encoding uses $O(m\log m)$ bits, proving membership in $\mathrm{NP}$.

Enumerating at most $m^{m-1}$ collections of guesses gives the stated deterministic running time. For $m=1$, the answer is immediately yes.

2. Reduction: candidates and desired comparisons

Start with a $3$-CNF formula having $n\geq 1$ variables and $q\geq 1$ clauses. Add unused variables so that the resulting number $N$ of variables is a power of two. This preserves satisfiability and gives $N<2n$.

Besides the target $a^\star$, introduce:

  • two literal candidates $\ell_i^+,\ell_i^-$ for every variable $i$;
  • a variable candidate $v_i$ for every variable $i$;
  • a clause candidate $b_j$ for every clause $j$;
  • two additional candidates $h$ and $e$.

Write
$$ \Lambda=\{\ell_i^+,\ell_i^-:1\leq i\leq N\}, \qquad \mathcal B=\{v_i:1\leq i\leq N\}\cup\{b_j:1\leq j\leq q\}, $$
and
$$ U=\Lambda\cup\mathcal B\cup\{h\}. $$
Thus
$$ k=|U|=3N+q+1,\qquad m=k+2. $$

Specify a tournament on $U$ with these directions:

  • $h\to\ell$ for every $\ell\in\Lambda$.
  • $b\to h$ for every $b\in\mathcal B$.
  • $\ell_i^s\to v_r$ exactly when $i=r$; otherwise $v_r\to\ell_i^s$.
  • $\ell_i^+\to b_j$ exactly when variable $i$ occurs positively in clause $j$, and $\ell_i^-\to b_j$ exactly when it occurs negatively. All other literal–clause edges point toward the literal.
  • Orient the remaining pairs arbitrarily.

Let $\gamma(u,v)\in\{-1,1\}$ denote the specified direction: $\gamma(u,v)=1$ means $u\to v$.

We will realize these directions with margins large enough to survive every admissible deletion.

3. A comparison profile with controlled margins

Set
$$ K=\binom{k}{2}, $$
and let $H$ be the smallest power of two satisfying $H\geq K$. Consequently, $H<2K$.

Construct a multiset $P$ of $2H$ strict rankings of $U$, each assigned mass $1/(2H)$, as follows.

For each specified edge $u\to v$, list the other candidates as $r_1,\ldots,r_{k-2}$ and add the two rankings
$$ u\succ v\succ r_1\succ\cdots\succ r_{k-2}, $$
$$ r_{k-2}\succ\cdots\succ r_1\succ u\succ v. $$
These rankings both put $u$ above $v$, while their contributions to every other pairwise margin cancel. Thus this pair contributes $1/H$ to the margin of $u$ over $v$.

There are $2K$ rankings so far. Add $H-K$ pairs consisting of an arbitrary ranking and its reversal. Each such pair contributes zero to every margin.

It follows directly that the normalized profile $P$ has margins
$$ D_P(u,v)=\frac{\gamma(u,v)}{H} \qquad (u,v\in U,\ u\ne v). $$

4. The society and robust comparisons

Define
$$ \beta=\frac{1}{16H},\qquad w=\frac{\beta}{N},\qquad \delta=2\beta, $$
and
$$ B=\frac{1-\beta}{2},\qquad Q=\frac{1-7\beta}{2}. $$
Both $B$ and $Q$ are positive.

Construct the society from the following rankings. Within any displayed candidate block, use a fixed strict order unless another order is specified.

| Family | Ranking | Mass per ranking |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | $p\succ a^\star\succ e$, for every occurrence $p\in P$ | $B/(2H)$ |
| Main control | $e\succ a^\star\succ p$, for every occurrence $p\in P$ | $Q/(2H)$ |
| Literal choice | $e\succ\ell_i^s\succ a^\star\succ(U\setminus\{\ell_i^s\})$, for every $i,s$ | $w$ |
| Ballast | $e\succ\mathcal B\succ a^\star\succ\Lambda\succ h$ | $\delta$ |

The total mass is
$$ B+Q+2Nw+\delta = (1-4\beta)+2\beta+2\beta = 1. $$

The baseline family has total mass $B$. Call all other families control rankings; their total mass is
$$ C=Q+2Nw+\delta=\frac{1+\beta}{2}. $$

Merge identical rankings by adding their masses. This produces a support of distinct rankings, as required. The families remain distinguishable: baseline rankings have $e$ last, while control rankings have $e$ first; among control rankings, the candidates above $a^\star$ distinguish main, literal-choice, and ballast rankings. In particular, each literal-choice ranking is a distinct support type.

Proven property: every specified comparison on $U$ survives deletion

The baseline and main-control families together contribute
$$ \frac{B+Q}{H}\gamma(u,v) = \frac{1-4\beta}{H}\gamma(u,v) $$
to each margin on $U$.

The literal-choice and ballast families have total mass $4\beta$, so their contribution to any margin has absolute value at most $4\beta$. Deleting at most $\beta$ changes any margin by at most $\beta$. Hence every admissible deletion satisfies
$$ \gamma(u,v)D_x(u,v) \geq \frac{1-4\beta}{H}-5\beta. $$
Since $\beta=1/(16H)$ and $H\geq 1$,
$$ \frac{1-4\beta}{H}-5\beta = \frac{1}{H} \left(\frac{11}{16}-\frac{1}{4H}\right) \geq \frac{7}{16H} > 0. $$
Thus every specified edge on $U$ remains strictly positive, and every reverse margin remains strictly negative.

5. The guard forces exact spending

Let $X_B$ and $X_C$ be the total deletions from baseline and control rankings, respectively.

Every baseline ranking places $a^\star$ above $e$, whereas every control ranking places $e$ above $a^\star$. Therefore
$$ D_x(e,a^\star) = C-X_C-(B-X_B) = \beta-X_C+X_B. $$

Furthermore, every baseline ranking places every $u\in U$ above $e$, whereas every control ranking places $e$ above every $u\in U$. Consequently,
$$ D_x(u,e)=-D_x(e,a^\star) \qquad (u\in U). $$
The same identity holds for $u=a^\star$.

If $D_x(e,a^\star)>0$, no candidate beats $e$. The covering condition is then vacuously satisfied, so $e$ covers $a^\star$.

On the other hand, the budget implies
$$ X_B+X_C\leq\beta, $$
and hence
$$ D_x(e,a^\star)=\beta-X_C+X_B\geq 2X_B\geq 0. $$

It follows that any deletion making $a^\star$ uncovered must satisfy
$$ D_x(e,a^\star)=0,\qquad X_B=0,\qquad X_C=\beta. $$
Thus it deletes no baseline mass and spends the entire budget on control rankings. Under these conditions, $e$ ties every other candidate.

Target margins under the forced spending condition

The retained control mass equals
$$ C-\beta=B. $$
For $u\in U$, let $S_u$ denote the retained control mass placing $u$ above $a^\star$. All baseline rankings place $u$ above $a^\star$, so
$$ D_x(u,a^\star) = B+S_u-(B-S_u) = 2S_u. $$

Let $x_i^s$ denote deletion from the literal-choice type for $\ell_i^s$, and let $x_\delta$ denote deletion from the ballast type. Inspection of the rankings gives
$$ D_x(h,a^\star)=0, $$
$$ D_x(\ell_i^s,a^\star)=2(w-x_i^s), $$
and, for every $b\in\mathcal B$,
$$ D_x(b,a^\star)=2(\delta-x_\delta)\geq 2(\delta-\beta)=2\beta>0. $$

In particular,
$$ D_x(\ell_i^s,a^\star)\leq 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_i^s=w. $$
A literal is an eligible witness precisely when its entire choice type has been deleted.

6. Equivalence with satisfiability

A successful deletion yields a satisfying assignment

Suppose an admissible deletion makes $a^\star$ upward uncovered. Section 5 establishes the forced spending condition.

Fix a variable candidate $v_i$. It has a positive margin over $a^\star$, so it needs a witness $c$ with
$$ D_x(c,v_i)>0,\qquad D_x(c,a^\star)\leq 0. $$

The possible witnesses are exactly the fully deleted literal candidates $\ell_i^+$ and $\ell_i^-$:

  • Every candidate in $\mathcal B$ has a positive margin over $a^\star$.
  • The guard $e$ ties $v_i$.
  • The candidate $h$ loses to $v_i$, by the robust specified comparison.
  • The target $a^\star$ loses to $v_i$.
  • A literal is eligible only if its choice type is fully deleted, and it beats $v_i$ exactly when it belongs to variable $i$.

Therefore, for every $i$, at least one of $x_i^+,x_i^-$ equals $w$. These deletions already cost at least
$$ Nw=\beta. $$
Because the total deletion is exactly $\beta$, exactly one literal-choice type per variable is fully deleted, and no other mass is deleted.

Assign variable $i$ true when $\ell_i^+$ is deleted and false when $\ell_i^-$ is deleted.

Each clause candidate $b_j$ also has a positive margin over $a^\star$. Applying the same witness analysis, its witness must be a fully deleted literal candidate with a specified edge toward $b_j$. Such edges correspond exactly to literals occurring in clause $j$. The corresponding literal is true under the assignment just defined. Thus every clause is satisfied.

A satisfying assignment yields a successful deletion

Conversely, suppose the formula has a satisfying assignment, extended arbitrarily to the unused variables.

For each variable, delete the entire choice type of its true literal: delete $\ell_i^+$’s type when the variable is true, and $\ell_i^-$’s type when it is false. Delete nothing else. The total deletion is
$$ Nw=\beta. $$

The forced spending condition holds. We verify every possible covering candidate:

  • The candidates $e$ and $h$, and every selected literal candidate, tie $a^\star$.
  • Every unselected literal beats $a^\star$, but has witness $h$: the margin of $h$ over that literal is positive, while $D_x(h,a^\star)=0$.
  • Every variable candidate $v_i$ has its selected literal as a witness.
  • Every clause candidate $b_j$ has a selected literal from its clause as a witness, because the assignment satisfies that clause.

Thus no candidate covers $a^\star$.

We have proved
$$ \text{the formula is satisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed deletion instance is a yes-instance}. $$

7. Encoding size and conclusion

The construction uses
$$ m=3N+q+3=O(n+q) $$
candidates and, before merging duplicate rankings, at most
$$ 4H+2N+1=O(m^2) $$
ranking types. Therefore $\tau=O(m^2)$.

Both $N$ and $H$ are powers of two. Consequently, $\beta,w,\delta,B,Q$, and all constructed ranking masses are dyadic rationals. Their bit lengths are $O(\log m)$; merging duplicates preserves this bound. With explicit rankings and binary candidate identifiers, the total input length is
$$ L=O(m^3\log m). $$
The entire reduction can therefore be performed in polynomial time.

This proves $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness for the exact fractional model, including its strict-edge and tie conventions. Together with Section 1, the problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete.
