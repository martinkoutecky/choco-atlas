The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) of patient–donor pair types with rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), directed compatible and half-compatible arcs \(C,H\subseteq T\times T\), no inherently incompatible arcs, and rational bounds \(q,\rho\in[0,1]\), does there exist nonnegative mass flow \(x_{ab}\) on \(C\cup H\) such that, for each \(t\in T\), \(\sum_b x_{tb}=\sum_a x_{at}\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_{(a,b)\in H}x_{ab}\le q\), and \(\sum_{(a,b)\in C}x_{ab}\ge\rho\)? Here \(x_{ab}\) is the mass of type-\(a\) donors assigned to type-\(b\) recipients; the balance constraints make participating pair mass both donate and receive, and a feasible circulation decomposes into exchange cycles.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) patient–donor pair types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Explicit directed arc sets \(C,H\) partition \(T\times T\): every donor–recipient combination is compatible or half-compatible, with no inherently incompatible combinations. An arc \((a,b)\) assigns a type-\(a\) donor to a type-\(b\) recipient; half-compatible transplantation consumes one unit of suppressant per unit mass, while compatible transplantation consumes none.
There are no unpaired recipients or altruistic donors. Mass is divisible, cycles have unrestricted length, and loops represent within-type transplantation. Unused pair mass may abstain; participating pair mass must both donate and receive.
Given binary rational bounds \(q,\rho\in[0,1]\), feasibility means existence of real flows \(x_{ab}\ge0\) satisfying
\(\sum_{b\in T}x_{tb}=\sum_{a\in T}x_{at}\le\mu_t\quad(t\in T),\qquad \sum_{(a,b)\in H}x_{ab}\le q,\qquad \sum_{(a,b)\in C}x_{ab}\ge\rho.\)
Let \(m=|C|+|H|\), and let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is this mass-flow feasibility problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete patient–donor pairs with unrestricted exchange cycles and no inherently incompatible combinations, deciding whether at least a specified number receive compatible kidneys using at most a specified number of suppressants is polynomial-time solvable ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.02253)).
No prior result settling exactly the continuous problem above was identified in the literature search.

Result

The mass-flow feasibility problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). It can be decided exactly in \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations by rational linear programming.

This conclusion applies to the stated continuous model, including binary rational masses, abstention, unrestricted cycles, and loops.

1. A polynomial-size linear program

Index the types by \(\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\). Introduce one real variable \(x_{ab}\) for each arc \((a,b)\in C\cup H\). There are exactly \(m\) variables; because \(C,H\) partition \(T\times T\), we also have \(m=\tau^2\).

Consider the following linear system:

  • Nonnegativity: \(x_{ab}\ge 0\) for every arc.
  • Balance: \(\sum_b x_{tb}-\sum_a x_{at}=0\) for every type \(t\).
  • Capacity: \(\sum_b x_{tb}\le\mu_t\) for every type \(t\).
  • Suppressant budget: \(\sum_{(a,b)\in H}x_{ab}\le q\).
  • Compatible-transplant target: \(-\sum_{(a,b)\in C}x_{ab}\le-\rho\).

These are exactly the constraints in the question: balance makes the incoming and outgoing mass equal, and the capacity inequality bounds their common value.

Replacing each balance equality by two inequalities produces a system \(Ax\le b\) with \(m\) variables and \(m+3\tau+2\) inequalities. Every entry of \(A\) belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). A loop variable cancels in its balance equation but remains in its capacity and transplantation constraints, as required.

Thus the original instance is feasible if and only if this rational linear system is feasible. No relaxation or approximation has been introduced.

2. Exact decision and running time

We use the polynomial-time rational linear-programming feasibility theorem:

Given a finite system of non-strict linear inequalities with binary rational coefficients, one can decide whether it has a real solution in time polynomial in its total binary encoding length.

This is the rational-input form of [Khachiyan’s polynomial-time linear-programming theorem](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319). It decides feasibility exactly, including when the feasible set has empty interior or feasibility requires equality at a bound.

All hypotheses hold here: the system is finite, its coefficients and right-hand sides are rational, and every constraint is non-strict.

Let \(B\) be the binary length of a dense encoding of the constructed system. The coefficient matrix has \(m(m+3\tau+2)\) entries of constant encoding length. The nonconstant right-hand sides are the given masses and bounds, whose total encoding length is at most \(L\). Consequently,

\(B=O(m^2+m\tau+L)\).

Constructing this dense system takes \(O(m^2+m\tau+L)\) bit operations using fixed-width encodings for the constant matrix entries. Applying exact rational LP feasibility therefore takes

\((m^2+m\tau+L)^{O(1)}=(m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\)

bit operations. Because the arcs are listed explicitly, \(m,\tau=O(L)\), so this is also \(L^{O(1)}\).

In particular, the algorithm never expands a binary rational mass into individual patient–donor pairs.

3. Balanced flows represent exchange cycles

Although feasibility is already defined by the displayed constraints, their interpretation as divisible exchanges can also be verified.

Let \(x\) be any nonnegative balanced flow. If a positive-flow arc exists, follow positive-flow arcs from it. Whenever a vertex is reached along such an arc, its incoming flow is positive; balance implies that its outgoing flow is positive, so continuation is possible. Since there are finitely many vertices, this walk contains a directed cycle, possibly a loop.

For a simple directed cycle \(K\), let \(\delta=\min_{e\in K}x_e>0\). Subtract \(\delta\) from every arc of \(K\). The residual flow remains nonnegative and balanced: each vertex on the cycle loses equal incoming and outgoing mass. At least one positive arc disappears.

Repeating this operation terminates after at most \(m\) subtractions and gives a decomposition

\(x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_i\mathbf{1}_{K_i}\), with \(k\le m\).

At each type, the total participating cycle mass equals its outgoing flow and hence is at most \(\mu_t\). Divisibility permits allocating this mass among the cycles; the remaining mass abstains. Both suppressant consumption and compatible-transplant mass are preserved because they are linear sums of arc flows.

Thus unrestricted cycles create no additional constraint beyond the polynomial-size flow system.
