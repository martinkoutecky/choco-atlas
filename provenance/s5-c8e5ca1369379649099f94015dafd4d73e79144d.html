The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(k\ge2\), \(q\) rational elementary cake intervals, and a finite type distribution \(\mu\) over types \(t\), where each type has a family label \(f(t)\), mass \(\mu_t\), and a normalized rational piecewise-constant valuation \(V_t\), let \(p_j=\sum_{t:f(t)=j}\mu_t\). Find a permutation \(\pi\) of the families and cut points \(0=x_0\le x_1\le\cdots\le x_k=1\), defining \(X_{\pi(r)}=[x_{r-1},x_r]\), such that for every family \(j\), \(\sum_{t:f(t)=j,\;V_t(X_j)\ge 1/k}\mu_t\ge p_j/k\).

The cake is \([0,1]\), partitioned into \(m=q\ge1\) elementary intervals by rational endpoints \(0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_m=1\). There are \(k\ge2\) families with equal entitlements \(1/k\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Each type has one fixed family label \(f(t)\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\); every family has positive mass \(p_j=\sum_{t:f(t)=j}\mu_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a nonnegative rational density, constant on each elementary interval, defining an additive valuation \(V_t\) normalized by \(V_t([0,1])=1\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).
The search problem is to find a permutation \(\pi\) of the families and rational cuts \(0=x_0\le\cdots\le x_k=1\), giving \(X_{\pi(r)}=[x_{r-1},x_r]\), such that \(\sum_{t:f(t)=j,\;V_t(X_j)\ge1/k}\mu_t\ge p_j/k\) for every \(j\). Assign shared endpoints to one adjacent piece; they have zero value. Both inequalities include equality.

Is this exact allocation search problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

For the finite-agent analogue, a connected allocation satisfying the same fraction \(1/k\) in every family always exists and can be found by an efficient protocol ([Segal-Halevi and Nitzan](https://arxiv.org/html/1510.03903v6)).

Result

The exact allocation search problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

The moving-knife idea in [Segal-Halevi and Nitzan, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1510.03903v6#S5.SS1) extends to weighted support. Below is a complete proof, including exact tie handling and a polynomial bound on the cuts’ encoding lengths. The algorithm works directly with the \(\tau\) supported types.

1. Weighted marks and their quantiles

Write \(T_j=\{t:f(t)=j\}\). Throughout the algorithm, \(p_j=\sum_{t\in T_j}\mu_t\) denotes the original family mass; it is never recomputed after types are removed.

Suppose the remaining cake is \([\ell,1]\), and family \(j\) has an active set \(A_j\subseteq T_j\). Assume every active type values the remaining cake at least \(1/k\). For each \(t\in A_j\), define its leftmost mark

\(z_t=\min\{x\in[\ell,1]:V_t([\ell,x])\ge 1/k\}\).

The valuation of a prefix is continuous and nondecreasing, so this minimum exists and satisfies

\(V_t([\ell,z_t])=1/k\).

Assume also that the active mass is at least \(p_j/k\). Define the family’s weighted quantile

\(Q_j=\min\left\{z\in\{z_t:t\in A_j\}:\sum_{t\in A_j:\,z_t\le z}\mu_t\ge p_j/k\right\}\).

By definition,

\(\sum_{t\in A_j:\,z_t\le Q_j}\mu_t\ge p_j/k\).

Crucially, minimality also gives the strict inequality

\(\sum_{t\in A_j:\,z_t<Q_j}\mu_t<p_j/k\).

Indeed, if the latter sum reached \(p_j/k\), an earlier distinct mark would already meet the quantile threshold. This argument allows arbitrary positive rational weights and arbitrary ties.

2. Algorithm and invariant

Maintain the unallocated families, a left endpoint \(\ell\), and their active sets \(A_j\). Initially, all families are unallocated, \(\ell=0\), and \(A_j=T_j\).

When \(n\) families remain, maintain these two properties for every remaining family \(j\):

  • \(\sum_{t\in A_j}\mu_t\ge np_j/k\).
  • \(V_t([\ell,1])\ge n/k\) for every \(t\in A_j\).

Both hold initially because \(n=k\), the active mass is \(p_j\), and every valuation is normalized to \(1\).

If \(n=1\), give the remaining family the entire remaining interval.

If \(n\ge2\), perform the following round:

  • Compute all active types’ marks \(z_t\) and all remaining families’ quantiles \(Q_j\).
  • Choose a family \(w\) minimizing \(Q_j\), breaking ties arbitrarily, and set \(x=Q_w\).
  • Give \([\ell,x]\) to family \(w\) and remove that family.
  • For every other family \(j\), retain precisely the active types with \(z_t\ge x\).
  • Replace \(\ell\) by \(x\).

Every allocated family meets its target

The invariant ensures that every active type can mark \(1/k\), and that every family has sufficient active mass to define its quantile.

For the winning family \(w\), all active types with \(z_t\le x=Q_w\) value \([\ell,x]\) at least \(1/k\). Their mass is at least \(p_w/k\). Thus its allocation satisfies the required condition.

The invariant survives each round

Fix a nonwinning family \(j\). Since \(x\le Q_j\), its discarded mass satisfies

\(\sum_{t\in A_j:\,z_t<x}\mu_t\le\sum_{t\in A_j:\,z_t<Q_j}\mu_t<p_j/k\).

Consequently, its new active mass is greater than

\(np_j/k-p_j/k=(n-1)p_j/k\).

Now consider any retained type \(t\), so \(z_t\ge x\). Monotonicity and the mark equation imply

\(V_t([\ell,x])\le V_t([\ell,z_t])=1/k\).

Using additivity and the previous invariant,

\(V_t([x,1])=V_t([\ell,1])-V_t([\ell,x])\ge n/k-1/k=(n-1)/k\).

Endpoints have zero value, so the displayed additivity identity is valid under any permitted endpoint assignment. Both invariant properties therefore hold with \(n-1\) families remaining.

The final family meets its target

When one family \(j\) remains, its active mass is at least \(p_j/k\), and every active type values \([\ell,1]\) at least \(1/k\). Assigning it that interval satisfies its requirement.

Thus every family meets the target. The assignment order determines the permutation \(\pi\), and the successive endpoints determine the cuts.

Tie handling is essential: types whose marks equal the chosen cut are retained in nonwinning families. Discarding them could invalidate the strict bound on lost mass.

3. Exact rational implementation

Let \(d_{t,i}\) be type \(t\)’s density on \([a_{i-1},a_i]\). Define

\(F_t(y)=V_t([0,y])\), and \(C_{t,i}=F_t(a_i)\).

Precompute all cumulative values using

\(C_{t,0}=0\),
\(C_{t,i}=C_{t,i-1}+d_{t,i}(a_i-a_{i-1})\).

For a current endpoint \(\ell\), the mark’s target cumulative value is

\(H_t=F_t(\ell)+1/k\).

Find the smallest index \(i\) such that \(C_{t,i}\ge H_t\). Since \(H_t>0=C_{t,0}\), minimality gives

\(C_{t,i-1}<H_t\le C_{t,i}\).

Hence \(d_{t,i}>0\), and the leftmost mark is exactly

\(z_t=a_{i-1}+\frac{H_t-C_{t,i-1}}{d_{t,i}}\).

This formula also handles equality with an elementary endpoint and zero-density intervals. Every mark is rational whenever \(\ell\) is rational. Induction therefore proves that every output cut is rational.

The cumulative values are nondecreasing, so the relevant elementary interval can be found by binary search. Weighted quantiles are computed by sorting marks and accumulating their exact rational masses.

4. Bit lengths and running time

Polynomial encoding lengths

All precomputed cumulative values have encoding length \(O(L)\). To see this, a product of the input denominators provides a common denominator of \(O(L)\) bits for the input rationals; its square suffices for the density-times-length products. Summing these products increases numerator lengths by at most \(O(L)\) further bits.

On any elementary interval, write

\(F_t(y)=d_{t,h}y+b_{t,h}\),

where the precomputed offset \(b_{t,h}\) also has \(O(L)\)-bit encoding.

If \(\ell\) lies in interval \(h\), and the mark lies in interval \(i\), its formula becomes

\(z_t=\frac{d_{t,h}}{d_{t,i}}\ell+\frac{b_{t,h}+1/k-b_{t,i}}{d_{t,i}}\).

Thus each candidate mark is a rational affine function of the previous cut, with coefficients of \(O(L)\)-bit encoding. Here \(\log k=O(L)\), since every family is represented in the input.

If a rational argument has encoding length \(s\), applying such an affine function produces a rational of encoding length at most \(s+O(L)\): for coefficients \(a/b\), \(c/d\), and argument \(u/v\), the result is

\(\frac{aud+cbv}{bvd}\).

The next cut is one of the candidate marks. There are at most \(k-1\) rounds, so all cuts and candidate marks have encoding length \(O(kL)\). Exact mass sums and comparisons likewise use numbers of at most this length.

Running time

Preprocessing requires \(O(m\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations.

In each round:

  • evaluating and finding all marks takes \(O(\tau\log(m+1))\) operations;
  • sorting within families and finding weighted quantiles takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) operations;
  • selecting the winner and updating active sets takes \(O(\tau+k)\) operations.

There are at most \(k-1\) rounds, and positive family masses imply \(k\le\tau\). The total is therefore

\(O\!\left(m\tau+k\tau\bigl(\log(m+1)+\log(\tau+1)\bigr)\right)\)

exact rational operations on \(O(kL)\)-bit numbers.

Using the conservative bound \(O(B^3)\) for elementary arithmetic and fraction reduction on \(B\)-bit numbers gives the explicit polynomial bit-time bound

\(O\!\left(\left[m\tau+k\tau\bigl(\log(m+1)+\log(\tau+1)\bigr)\right](kL)^3\right)\).

The algorithm therefore computes an exact valid allocation in polynomial time in the requested parameters.
