The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a connected graph \(G=(V,E)\), an integer capacity scale \(N\), rational type masses \(N\mu_t\), and utilities \(u_t:E\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), decide whether measures \(\lambda_t\) over connected pieces of the scaled graph exist with total mass \(N\mu_t\), fully allocate every edge point, and satisfy \(u_t(P)\ge u_t(Q)\) for \(\lambda_t\)-almost every \(P\) and \(\lambda_s\)-almost every \(Q\), for all types \(t,s\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions. Let \(T\) be a finite type set, \(N\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\), and \(\mu_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Replace every edge of a connected graph \(G=(V,E)\) by a copy of \([0,N]\), with copies incident at a vertex regarded as adjacent there; connected pieces may share vertices. For a connected piece \(P\), let \(u_t(P)=\sum_{e\in E}u_t(e)\,\ell(P\cap e)\).

A feasible allocation is a finite Borel measure \(\lambda_t\) on connected pieces for each \(t\), with \(\lambda_t(\mathcal C)=N\mu_t\), such that \(\sum_t\int_{\mathcal C}\mathbf 1[x\in P]\,d\lambda_t(P)=1\) for every edge-coordinate point \(x\). It is envy-free if, for all \(t,s\in T\), \(u_t(P)\geq u_t(Q)\) for \((\lambda_t imes\lambda_s)\)-almost every \((P,Q)\).

Target. Is this continuous envy-free graph-cutting decision problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results. The discrete envy-free graph-cutting and vertex-disjoint graph-cutting problems are NP-hard already with two agents on graphs of treewidth \(2\) and maximum degree \(3\). No complexity result for the exact mass/distribution formulation above is assumed here.

Complexity of Continuous Envy-Free Graph Cutting

Result

Under the standard finite-input interpretation in which the edge utilities \(u_t(e)\) are rational numbers encoded in binary, the problem is NP-hard.

More precisely, weak NP-hardness already holds under all of the following restrictions:

  • \(N=2\);
  • there are exactly two types \(T=\{1,2\}\);
  • \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\);
  • the two types have identical positive integral edge utilities;
  • \(G\) is a star, and hence a tree of treewidth \(1\).

The reduction is from \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\). It does not establish strong NP-hardness or membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof uses the stated convention that connected pieces may share abstract graph vertices. Coverage is imposed on the separate edge-coordinate copies; pieces sharing the center of a star may therefore remain disjoint on all edge-coordinate copies.

A concentration lemma for envy-free measures

Let the two types have the same utility function \(u\), and suppose \(\lambda_1(\mathcal C)=\lambda_2(\mathcal C)=1\).

Lemma 1

For each \(t\in\{1,2\}\), there is a number \(c_t\) such that \(u(P)=c_t\) for \(\lambda_t\)-almost every \(P\). Moreover, \(c_1=c_2\).

Proof

Fix \(t\). Let \(P,Q\) be independently distributed according to \(\lambda_t\). Envy-freeness with \(s=t\) gives

\[ u(P)\geq u(Q) \]

for \((\lambda_t\times\lambda_t)\)-almost every \((P,Q)\). Since the product measure is invariant under exchanging \(P\) and \(Q\), it also follows that

\[ u(Q)\geq u(P) \]

almost surely. Hence \(u(P)=u(Q)\) almost surely.

Two independent identically distributed real random variables can be equal almost surely only if their common distribution is concentrated at one point. Indeed, if the distribution were not concentrated at one point, there would be a rational \(q\) such that \(0<\Pr[u(P)\leq q]<1\). Independence would then give positive probability to \(u(P)\leq q<u(Q)\), a contradiction. Thus \(u(P)=c_t\) for \(\lambda_t\)-almost every \(P\).

Applying envy-freeness to \(t=1,s=2\) gives \(c_1\geq c_2\), while applying it to \(t=2,s=1\) gives \(c_2\geq c_1\). Therefore \(c_1=c_2\). Denote the common value by \(c\). \(\square\)

Structure of feasible allocations on a star

Let \(G\) be a star with arms \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\). Each arm is a copy of \([0,2]\), with coordinate \(0\) incident with the center. Give both types the identical edge utilities

\[ u_1(e_i)=u_2(e_i)=a_i, \]

where every \(a_i\) is a positive integer. Put

\[ W=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i. \]

Assume

\[ 2a_i<W \]

for every \(i\).

Lemma 2

The star instance has a feasible envy-free allocation if and only if there is a subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) satisfying

\[ \sum_{i\in S}a_i=\frac{W}{2}. \]

Proof: necessity

Suppose that \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\) is feasible and envy-free. By Lemma 1, every piece occurring outside a null set has the same utility \(c\).

Using Tonelli's theorem and the pointwise coverage constraint,

\[ \begin{aligned} 2c &=\sum_{t=1}^2\int_{\mathcal C}u(P)\,d\lambda_t(P)\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i\int_0^2 \sum_{t=1}^2\int_{\mathcal C} \mathbf 1[x_i(r)\in P]\,d\lambda_t(P)\,dr\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i\int_0^2 1\,dr\\ &=2W. \end{aligned} \]

Thus \(c=W\).

Define the probability measure

\[ \nu=\frac{\lambda_1+\lambda_2}{2}. \]

Every interior coordinate \(x_i(r)\), where \(0<r<2\), then satisfies

\[ \nu\bigl(\{P:x_i(r)\in P\}\bigr)=\frac12. \]

A connected piece of utility \(W\) cannot lie in a single arm, because the total utility available on arm \(e_i\) is \(2a_i<W\). Consequently, every such piece uses the center and intersects at least two arms.

A connected subset of a tree contains the unique path between any two of its points. Therefore, for a piece using the center, its intersection with each arm is an initial interval from the center, up to irrelevant choices about boundary points.

For \(0<r<s<2\), let

\[ A_{i,r}=\{P:x_i(r)\in P\}. \]

Outside a \(\nu\)-null set, connectedness gives \(A_{i,s}\subseteq A_{i,r}\). Both events have \(\nu\)-measure \(1/2\), so

\[ \nu(A_{i,r}\setminus A_{i,s})=0. \]

Apply this to all rational pairs \(0<r<s<2\). There are only countably many such pairs. Hence, for \(\nu\)-almost every piece \(P\), no arm is occupied by a proper positive-length prefix: for every \(i\),

\[ \ell(P\cap e_i)\in\{0,2\}. \]

For such a piece, define

\[ S(P)=\{i:\ell(P\cap e_i)=2\}. \]

Since \(u(P)=W\),

\[ W=u(P)=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i\ell(P\cap e_i) =2\sum_{i\in S(P)}a_i. \]

Thus \(S(P)\) is a solution of the required partition equation.

Proof: sufficiency

Conversely, suppose \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) satisfies

\[ \sum_{i\in S}a_i=\frac W2. \]

Let \(P_S\) be the union of the full arms indexed by \(S\), and let \(P_{\overline S}\) be the union of the remaining full arms. Both pieces are connected because arms in the same piece meet at the center, and sharing that vertex is permitted.

Define

\[ \lambda_1=\delta_{P_S}, \qquad \lambda_2=\delta_{P_{\overline S}}. \]

Each measure has mass \(1=N\mu_t\). Every edge-coordinate point belongs to exactly one of these two pieces. Moreover,

\[ u(P_S)=2\sum_{i\in S}a_i=W \]

and, similarly, \(u(P_{\overline S})=W\). All envy inequalities therefore hold with equality. This proves feasibility and envy-freeness. \(\square\)

Reduction from \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\)

An instance of \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) consists of positive binary-encoded integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\), with \(W=\sum_i a_i\), and asks whether some subset sums to \(W/2\).

The reduction first handles two elementary cases:

  • If some \(a_i>W/2\), the \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) instance is a no-instance. Output the fixed three-arm star with all arm utilities equal to \(1\). It is infeasible by Lemma 2, since \(W=3\) and no subset sums to \(3/2\).
  • If some \(a_i=W/2\), the \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) instance is a yes-instance. Output the fixed two-arm star with both arm utilities equal to \(1\). Assign one full arm to each type; this is feasible and envy-free.

In every remaining case, \(a_i<W/2\) for all \(i\), equivalently \(2a_i<W\). Construct the star of Lemma 2 with arm utilities \(a_i\), \(N=2\), and two types of mass \(1/2\). Lemma 2 shows that the resulting continuous instance is feasible and envy-free exactly when the original \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) instance is positive.

The construction has \(m+1\) vertices and \(m\) edges, and it copies the binary integers \(a_i\) without numerical blow-up. It is therefore a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

Conclusion and limitations

The exact continuous mass/distribution formulation does not eliminate discrete hardness. Envy-freeness forces every supported piece to have the same utility, while exact uniform coverage on a star forces almost every arm to be taken either completely or not at all. This recovers the partition constraint despite allowing arbitrary Borel probability distributions over pieces.

Thus the target problem is NP-hard, unless no finite encoding of the edge utilities is intended. The proof gives only weak NP-hardness. It does not show that finite-support certificates always exist, so it does not establish membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Concentration lemma

With \(N=2\) and \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\), both \(\lambda_t\) have total mass \(1\), so treating them as probability measures is valid.

For \(s=t\), envy-freeness gives \(u(P)\geq u(Q)\) almost surely under \(\lambda_t\times\lambda_t\). Symmetry of the product measure gives the reverse inequality almost surely. Hence \(u(P)=u(Q)\) almost surely.

The argument that two independent identically distributed real random variables equal almost surely only when their distribution is a point mass is correct. Applying the two cross-type envy inequalities then correctly yields \(c_1=c_2\).

2. Determination of the common utility

Tonelli’s theorem applies because the utility integrands are nonnegative. Substituting the pointwise coverage constraint into the integrated total utility gives

\(2c=2W\),

and therefore \(c=W\). The constants and measure masses are handled correctly.

3. Geometric structure on the star

The strict hypothesis \(2a_i<W\) implies that a utility-\(W\) piece cannot be contained in one arm, whose total available utility is \(2a_i\). Thus almost every relevant piece intersects multiple arms and must pass through the center.

For center-containing connected subsets of a tree, the intersection with an arm is an initial interval, up to endpoint choices. Consequently, for \(0<r<s<2\), membership at coordinate \(s\) implies membership at coordinate \(r\).

Coverage gives \(\nu(A_{i,r})=\nu(A_{i,s})=1/2\). The almost-sure nesting therefore implies \(\nu(A_{i,r}\setminus A_{i,s})=0\). Taking the countable union over rational pairs correctly excludes, almost surely, every proper positive-length prefix: any length strictly between \(0\) and \(2\) would admit rational \(r<s\) witnessing such a difference.

It follows correctly that every arm has length contribution either \(0\) or \(2\) almost surely. Combining this with \(u(P)=W\) yields

\(W=2\sum_{i\in S(P)}a_i\),

so a partition subset exists.

Given a partition subset \(S\), the two unions of full arms are connected and may share the center under the problem’s stated vertex-sharing convention. Their Dirac measures have the required masses, cover every edge-coordinate point exactly once, and both pieces have utility \(W\). All self-type and cross-type envy inequalities therefore hold with equality.
