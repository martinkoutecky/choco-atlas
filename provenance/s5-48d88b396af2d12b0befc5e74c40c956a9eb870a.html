The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite template set \(X\), a finite support of agent types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(\kappa_x\), and a fixed fluid service schedule, compute a polynomial-time local policy \(\Pi\) for a target type \(t^\star\) that improves its mean final utility by a factor of at least \(1+\epsilon\) over the all-greedy fluid execution, whenever such a policy exists. Each individual receives an integral feasible bundle; the policy induces the mass flow, and all other types remain greedy.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(X\) be an ordered set of \(m\geq2\) item templates. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Fix a target \(t^\star\) with \(0<\mu_{t^\star}<1\), positive binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\kappa_x\), and a constant \(\epsilon\in(0,0.2)\).
Each type has a normalized, nonnegative, rational-valued monotone submodular utility \(f_t\) and a \(p_t\)-system \(\mathcal I_t\), specified by polynomial-size value and membership circuits; all types other than \(t^\star\) have additive utilities. Each individual starts empty and holds an integral feasible subset of \(X\), with at most one item of each template.
The supplied schedule fixes a permutation of the types, repeated for \(H=\lceil\sum_x\kappa_x\rceil\) rounds. Each type's block lasts one unit: if mass \(a_B\) holds bundle \(B\) at its start, that cohort receives service at rate \(a_B\), with each individual served exactly once in the block.
At service, an individual may add one available template outside its bundle, preserving feasibility, or pass. Allocating template \(x\) to mass \(d\) consumes supply \(d\); exhausted templates are unavailable. Newly served individuals receive no further service in that block.
Greedy service selects an available feasible template maximizing \(f_t(B\cup\{x\})-f_t(B)\), breaking ties by the order on \(X\), and passes only if none exists, including selecting zero-marginal items when possible.
A target policy may use the individual's bundle, block time, and full public fluid state, and split each served cohort among legal actions. Admissible executions have finitely many pieces with rational endpoints and constant rational action rates; a local policy specifies these splits, while all other types remain greedy.
Let \(q_{t,B}\) be terminal bundle masses and \(U(\Pi)=\mu_{t^\star}^{-1}\sum_B q_{t^\star,B}f_{t^\star}(B)\); let \(U_G\) be the all-greedy baseline. The improvement problem asks for a policy with \(U(\Pi)\geq(1+\epsilon)U_G\) whenever an admissible policy attaining that threshold exists, with arbitrary behavior otherwise.
Is this continuous improvement problem polynomial-time solvable, including policy construction and execution, or NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,H\), and \(\max_t p_t\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length.
For finite-agent Round-Robin, consistently obtaining a \((1+\epsilon)\)-improvement whenever possible is NP-hard, even without feasibility constraints and with all other agents additive and greedy ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15155v3)). No result settling the fluid problem defined above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized society mass to one, interpreted supplies per unit society mass, and required positive target and non-target masses to retain competition.
  • Specified normalized, nonnegative, monotone submodular utilities and p-system feasibility; retained additive utilities for non-target types and fixed epsilon in the anchor's range.
  • Encoded polynomial-time value and membership access by circuits so that binary input length and ordinary complexity claims are well defined.
  • Interpreted templates as set elements: an individual can receive at most one copy of each template.
  • Completed the unspecified fluid schedule using a fixed type ordering, uniform service within each starting bundle cohort, and the per-capita analogue of the Round-Robin round bound.
  • Allowed passing, retained mandatory greedy selection of feasible zero-marginal items, and fixed template-order tie-breaking.
  • Defined local policies to permit cohort splitting and full public-state information; restricted admissible executions to finite rational piecewise-constant flows to specify exact computation.
  • Defined mean utility conditional on target type, used the weak multiplicative threshold, and retained the anchor's whenever-possible search guarantee.

Proven result

The stated improvement problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, already when

\(\tau=2,\qquad \max_t p_t=1,\qquad H=2m,\)

the target has a coverage utility and a cardinality constraint, and the other type has an additive utility and no feasibility constraint.

Consequently, an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,H,\max_t p_t)\), including policy construction and execution, would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

I use the standard bit-model interpretation of execution: its rational cohort transitions, or terminal bundle masses, are computed explicitly, and their size counts toward running time. Merely producing short code for an exponentially long execution does not meet the requested execution bound.

1. The source problem

An instance of maximum coverage consists of an explicitly listed universe \(V\), subsets \(C_1,\ldots,C_m\subseteq V\), and a budget \(1\leq k\leq m\). Define

\(f(B)=\left|\bigcup_{i\in B}C_i\right|,\qquad M=\max_{|B|\leq k}f(B).\)

Feige’s Theorem 5.3 establishes that, for every fixed \(\delta>0\), obtaining a polynomial-time approximation ratio \(1-1/e+\delta\) for maximum coverage is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The assumption here is \(\mathrm{P}\neq\mathrm{NP}\), rather than the stronger assumption appearing in some set-cover hardness results. [Feige, *A Threshold of ln n for Approximating Set Cover*, Theorem 5.3](https://courses.cs.duke.edu/cps296.2/spring07/papers/p634-feige.pdf).

We may restrict attention to \(m\geq2\), since instances with fewer sets are directly solvable.

2. Constructing the fluid instance

Create one template for each coverage set, ordered by its index. Use two types, in the repeated order \(t^\star,t^o\), with

\(\mu_{t^\star}=\mu_{t^o}=\tfrac12,\qquad \kappa_x=2\quad\text{for every }x.\)

Thus the prescribed number of rounds is \(H=2m\).

The target has utility \(f_{t^\star}=f\) and feasibility family

\(\mathcal I_{t^\star}=\{B\subseteq X:|B|\leq k\}.\)

The other type has

\(f_{t^o}(B)=|B|,\qquad \mathcal I_{t^o}=2^X.\)

These data satisfy every input promise:

  • Coverage is normalized and nonnegative. It is monotone because adding sets cannot shrink their union.
  • Its marginal for template \(x\) is

\(\displaystyle f(B\cup\{x\})-f(B)=\left|C_x\setminus\bigcup_{i\in B}C_i\right|.\)
This decreases when \(B\) grows, proving submodularity.

  • Both feasibility families are \(1\)-systems: within any restricted ground set, all maximal feasible subsets have the same cardinality.
  • The other utility is additive.
  • The masses and supplies are positive binary rationals.
  • The coverage value circuit computes membership in the union and counts covered elements. Its size is polynomial in the explicitly listed coverage instance. Cardinality and unrestricted membership also have polynomial-size circuits.

The other type actively receives items greedily; it is not an empty or zero-utility type.

Supply never runs out. For any template, total consumption throughout any legal execution is at most the society’s total mass, namely \(1\), because each individual holds at most one copy. Its initial supply is \(2\), so remaining supply is always at least \(1\).

Consequently, the target’s choices are affected only by its own bundle and cardinality constraint.

3. The fluid optimum equals the discrete optimum

Consider any admissible target policy. Every terminal target bundle has cardinality at most \(k\), hence value at most \(M\). Target mass is conserved, so

\(\displaystyle U(\Pi)=2\sum_B q_{t^\star,B}f(B)\leq 2M\sum_B q_{t^\star,B}=M.\)

Conversely, fix any \(S\subseteq X\) with \(|S|\leq k\). In the first \(|S|\) target blocks, give every target individual the same successive elements of \(S\); thereafter, pass.

Every prefix is feasible, supply is available, and \(H\geq|S|\). Each block uses one constant action rate \(1/2\), with integer block endpoints. Newly served individuals receive no additional service within that block. Thus this is an admissible execution, and its terminal conditional utility is \(f(S)\).

Choosing an optimal \(S\) proves

\(\displaystyle \max_{\Pi\text{ admissible}}U(\Pi)=M.\)

In particular, splitting cohorts cannot raise the optimum above the best integral feasible bundle.

4. Identifying and bounding the greedy baseline

Since supply remains available, all target individuals remain in one cohort under the all-greedy execution. During each of their first \(k\) blocks, they receive the next ordinary greedy coverage choice, with the specified index tie-breaking. Greedy also takes zero-marginal items when necessary, exactly as required by the question.

Let \(S_j\) be the first \(j\) greedy choices and write \(G_j=f(S_j)\), \(G=G_k\). Then \(U_G=G\).

For completeness, let \(O\) be an optimal coverage solution. For \(j<k\), monotonicity and submodularity imply

\(\displaystyle M\leq f(S_j\cup O)\leq G_j+\sum_{x\in O\setminus S_j}\bigl(f(S_j\cup\{x\})-G_j\bigr).\)

There are at most \(k\) summands. Every such addition is feasible at this stage, and the greedy marginal is at least each summand. Therefore

\(\displaystyle G_{j+1}-G_j\geq\frac{M-G_j}{k}.\)

Starting with \(G_0=0\), induction gives

\(\displaystyle M-G_j\leq(1-1/k)^jM.\)

Hence, setting \(c=1-1/e\),

\(\displaystyle G\geq\bigl(1-(1-1/k)^k\bigr)M\geq cM.\)

The final inequality follows from \(1-u\leq e^{-u}\); it also holds when \(k=1\).

5. An improvement algorithm would beat the coverage hardness threshold

Suppose an algorithm constructs and executes the required policy in polynomial time.

Run it on the constructed fluid instance. From its executed terminal distribution, select a positive-mass target bundle \(B\) of maximum coverage value. Such a bundle satisfies

\(f(B)\geq U(\Pi),\)

because conditional utility is an average over terminal bundles.

Return whichever has larger value: \(B\) or the ordinary greedy bundle \(S_k\). Denote the returned value by \(W\).

The permitted arbitrary behavior on instances without an improvement causes no difficulty: retain the greedy bundle regardless, and discard any malformed or infeasible bundle records.

There are two exhaustive cases.

Case 1: \(M\geq(1+\epsilon)G\).

Section 3 supplies an admissible policy reaching the threshold. The assumed algorithm must therefore achieve it, giving

\(\displaystyle W\geq f(B)\geq U(\Pi)\geq(1+\epsilon)G\geq(1+\epsilon)cM.\)

Case 2: \(M<(1+\epsilon)G\).

The retained greedy solution gives

\(\displaystyle W\geq G>\frac{M}{1+\epsilon}.\)

Thus, on every coverage instance, the resulting algorithm guarantees approximation ratio

\(\displaystyle \alpha=\min\left\{(1+\epsilon)c,\frac1{1+\epsilon}\right\}.\)

Both terms are strictly larger than \(c\). The first is larger because \(\epsilon>0\). For the second,

\(\displaystyle \frac1{1+\epsilon}>\frac56>\frac23>1-\frac1e,\)

where the last inequality follows from \(e<3\).

Therefore \(\delta=\alpha-c>0\) is a fixed constant. We have constructed a polynomial-time maximum-coverage approximation with ratio \(1-1/e+\delta\), contradicting Feige’s hardness theorem unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The algorithm never needs to determine which case holds or compute a comparison involving \(\epsilon\): it simply returns the better feasible bundle.

6. Running times and policy execution

The reduction has polynomial binary input length \(L\), with

\(\tau=2,\qquad H=2m,\qquad \max_t p_t=1.\)

Computing the baseline requires \(O(km)\) circuit evaluations. Its complete execution has \(O(\tau H)\) block action records and can be constructed and executed in \(O(H\tau m\,\operatorname{poly}(L))\) bit time.

An execution containing \(K\) explicitly recorded target actions reaches at most \(K+1\) distinct target bundles: initially only the empty bundle exists, and each recorded addition introduces at most one new bundle. Terminal masses can be reconstructed by rational arithmetic, and the best feasible recorded bundle can be found in time polynomial in \(L,m\), and the execution’s total encoding length. Thus extraction preserves any hypothesized polynomial construction-and-execution bound.

An optimal coherent policy, once its bundle is known, also has \(O(\tau H)\) action records and polynomial execution time. On this subclass, enumerating all bundles gives an exact construction-and-execution algorithm in

\(\displaystyle 2^m\operatorname{poly}(L,m)+H\tau\operatorname{poly}(L,m)\)

time. The hardness concerns finding the required improvement, rather than executing a known coherent policy.

This settles the problem with the allowed feasibility constraints. The proof does not classify the fluid subclass having no feasibility constraints at all.
