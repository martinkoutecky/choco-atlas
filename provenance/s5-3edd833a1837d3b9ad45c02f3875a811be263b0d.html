The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(d\), a finite support \(T\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^d\), and rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), determine the worst-case egalitarian approximation ratio of the weighted SD-lift rule and whether it is strategyproof under unilateral deviations \(\mu_{\epsilon,z}=(1-\epsilon)\nu+\epsilon\delta_z\), for every \(\epsilon>0\), other-agent distribution \(\nu\), true location \(p\), and report \(z\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix an integer \(d\geq1\). A society \(\mu\) is given by its support \(T\subseteq\mathbb Q^d\): the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; write any running times in \(m:=d\), \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and other relevant parameters.
Identify each location \(t\) with \((t,0)\in\mathbb R^{d+1}\). The deterministic weighted SD-lift rule outputs \(F(\mu)=(c_\mu,\sigma_\mu)\), where \(c_\mu=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t t\) and \(\sigma_\mu=\sqrt{\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\|t-c_\mu\|_2^2}\).
Define \(C(\mu)=\max_{t\in T}\|(t,0)-F(\mu)\|_2\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}(T)=\min_{a\in\mathbb R^d}\max_{t\in T}\|t-a\|_2\). The worst-case ratio in dimension \(d\) is \(\alpha_d=\sup_{\mu:\operatorname{OPT}(T)>0}C(\mu)/\operatorname{OPT}(T)\), over all such finite societies; zero-radius societies must have zero cost.
For a finitely supported rational probability distribution \(\nu\), rational \(0<\epsilon\leq1\), and \(p,z\in\mathbb Q^d\), let \(\mu_{\epsilon,z}=(1-\epsilon)\nu+\epsilon\delta_z\). A deviator retains its mass \(\epsilon\) and has true location \(p\); strategyproofness means \(\|(p,0)-F(\mu_{\epsilon,p})\|_2\leq\|(p,0)-F(\mu_{\epsilon,z})\|_2\) for every such choice.

Result

For every \(d\geq1\), the exact worst-case ratio is \(\alpha_d=2\). Weighted SD-lift is strategyproof under every unilateral deviation specified in the question. The ratio is a supremum: every individual society with positive optimal radius has ratio strictly below \(2\).

1. The squared-distance identity

Let \(\mu\) be any society, with centroid \(c_\mu\) and variance \(\sigma_\mu^2\). For every \(p\in\mathbb R^d\),

\( \displaystyle \|(p,0)-F(\mu)\|_2^2 =\|p-c_\mu\|_2^2+\sigma_\mu^2 =\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\|p-s\|_2^2. \)

To verify the second equality, expand

\( \displaystyle \|p-s\|_2^2 =\|p-c_\mu\|_2^2+\|c_\mu-s\|_2^2 +2\langle p-c_\mu,c_\mu-s\rangle. \)

Taking the weighted sum eliminates the cross term because
\(\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s(c_\mu-s)=0\). The remaining terms are precisely the squared horizontal distance and the variance.

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle C(\mu)^2 =\max_{t\in T}\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\|t-s\|_2^2. \)

2. Upper bound, including zero-radius societies

Write \(\beta=\min_{t\in T}\mu_t>0\). For any \(a\in\mathbb R^d\), put
\(R(a)=\max_{s\in T}\|s-a\|_2\). The triangle inequality gives
\(\|t-s\|_2\leq2R(a)\) for every \(s,t\in T\).

For each \(t\in T\), the summand corresponding to \(s=t\) is zero. Therefore,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\|t-s\|_2^2 \leq4(1-\mu_t)R(a)^2 \leq4(1-\beta)R(a)^2. \)

Taking the maximum over \(t\), followed by the infimum over \(a\), yields

\( \displaystyle C(\mu)^2\leq4(1-\beta)\operatorname{OPT}(T)^2. \)

Thus, whenever \(\operatorname{OPT}(T)>0\),

\( \displaystyle \frac{C(\mu)}{\operatorname{OPT}(T)} \leq2\sqrt{1-\beta}<2. \)

This proves \(\alpha_d\leq2\).

If \(\operatorname{OPT}(T)=0\), then \(T\) contains only one location. Indeed, any two locations \(s,t\in T\) satisfy
\(\|s-t\|_2\leq2R(a)\) for every \(a\), so their distance is zero when the infimum of \(R(a)\) is zero. For a singleton support, the centroid is that location and the variance is zero. Hence zero-radius societies have zero cost, as required.

3. Matching rational examples in every dimension

Fix \(d\geq1\), let \(u=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb Q^d\), and for each integer \(N\geq2\) define

\( \displaystyle \mu^{(N)}=\frac1N\delta_{-u}+\left(1-\frac1N\right)\delta_u. \)

These are admissible societies with rational locations, positive rational masses, and support size \(\tau=2\).

Their optimal radius equals \(1\): the center \(0\) achieves radius \(1\), while the distance \(2\) between the two support points forces every enclosing radius to be at least \(1\).

The squared-distance identity gives

\( \displaystyle \|(-u,0)-F(\mu^{(N)})\|_2^2=4\left(1-\frac1N\right), \qquad \|(u,0)-F(\mu^{(N)})\|_2^2=\frac4N. \)

Since \(N\geq2\), the first quantity is at least the second. Thus

\( \displaystyle \frac{C(\mu^{(N)})}{\operatorname{OPT}(\{-u,u\})} =2\sqrt{1-\frac1N}\longrightarrow2. \)

Therefore \(\alpha_d\geq2\), completing the proof that \(\alpha_d=2\). In particular, allowing arbitrarily small positive masses already produces the supremum with two support types.

4. Strategyproofness for every retained mass

Fix any admissible \(\nu,\epsilon,p,z\), and define the deviator’s cost under report \(z\) by
\(D(z)=\|(p,0)-F(\mu_{\epsilon,z})\|_2\).

Applying the squared-distance identity to the mixture gives

\( \displaystyle D(z)^2 =(1-\epsilon)\sum_{s\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)} \nu_s\|p-s\|_2^2+\epsilon\|p-z\|_2^2. \)

This identity remains valid when \(z\) coincides with a support point of \(\nu\), since combining masses at a common location preserves the sum.

For the truthful report,

\( \displaystyle D(p)^2 =(1-\epsilon)\sum_{s\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)} \nu_s\|p-s\|_2^2. \)

Subtracting yields the exact deviation penalty

\( \displaystyle D(z)^2-D(p)^2=\epsilon\|p-z\|_2^2\geq0. \)

Because both costs are nonnegative, \(D(p)\leq D(z)\). Moreover, \(\epsilon>0\) makes the inequality strict whenever \(z\neq p\). This also covers \(\epsilon=1\), when truthful reporting has cost zero.

5. Relation to the discrete-agent result

For \(n\) equally weighted agents, every positive aggregated support mass is at least \(1/n\). Section 2 therefore gives the bound \(2\sqrt{(n-1)/n}\), and the construction in Section 3 with \(N=n\) attains it. This recovers the discrete result stated in the [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05132v1). The weighted conclusion follows from the direct proofs above without assuming a weighted extension of that result.
