The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of item kinds \(G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_m\}\), finite complete agent types \(\Theta\) with rational additive values \(v_{\theta,g}\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\), and rational per-capita copy supplies \(b_g\ge0\), promised that every item kind is subjective (\(v_{\theta,g}\ge0\) for some \(\theta\)), find a finite-support mass assignment \(\lambda_{\theta,a}\ge0\) over integral bundle-count vectors \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\), together with nonnegative cell payments \(\pi_{\theta,a}\), satisfying \(\sum_a\lambda_{\theta,a}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,a}\lambda_{\theta,a}a_g=b_g\) for every \(g\). For every two occupied cells \((\theta,a)\) and \((\theta',a')\), require \(\mathrm{EF1}\) for type \(\theta\): either \(u_\theta(a)\ge u_\theta(a')\), or for some \(g\) with \(a_g>0\) or \(a'_g>0\), \(u_\theta(a^{\setminus g})\ge u_\theta(a'^{\setminus g})\); also require \(u_\theta(a)+\pi_{\theta,a}\ge u_\theta(a')+\pi_{\theta',a'}\), where \(u_\theta(a)=\sum_g a_gv_{\theta,g}\) and \(a^{\setminus g}\) removes one copy when present. Return such \((\lambda,\pi)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses satisfy \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta=1\).
There are \(m\) item kinds, binary-rational additive values \(v_{\theta,g}\in\mathbb Q\), and binary-rational per-capita supplies \(b_g\ge0\). Every kind is subjective: \(v_{\theta,g}\ge0\) for some supported type. Write \(u_\theta(a)=\sum_g a_gv_{\theta,g}\) for \(a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\).
Find finitely supported rational masses \(\lambda_{\theta,a}\ge0\) and rational subsidies \(\pi_{\theta,a}\ge0\), satisfying \(\sum_a\lambda_{\theta,a}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,a}\lambda_{\theta,a}a_g=b_g\). Subsidies have no budget bound and yield one utility unit per monetary unit to every type.
For every ordered pair of occupied cells \((\theta,a),(\theta',a')\), require \(u_\theta(a)+\pi_{\theta,a}\ge u_\theta(a')+\pi_{\theta',a'}\). Also require EF1: either \(u_\theta(a)\ge u_\theta(a')\), or \(a_g>0\) and \(u_\theta(a-e_g)\ge u_\theta(a')\) for some kind, or \(a'_g>0\) and \(u_\theta(a)\ge u_\theta(a'-e_g)\) for some kind; \(e_g\) is the unit vector. These comparisons include cells of the same type; empty bundles are allowed.

Is this construction problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, or is it NP-hard?

For explicitly listed finite agents and items partitioned into nonempty bundles each valued nonnegatively by some agent, an algorithm constructs an EF1 and envy-freeable allocation in polynomial time; this includes all-subjective instances.   • Discarded zero-mass types and required the subjective-kind promise to be witnessed by a positive-mass type, matching the discrete requirement that an actual agent values the item nonnegatively.

A polynomial-time construction

The construction problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, a solution can be found with at most \(m+\tau\) occupied cells, all with polynomial encoding length.

The proof compresses iterative matching into at most \(2m\) phases. It uses the matching idea underlying the [cited discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04891v1#S4.SS2.SSS1), but establishes the required compression and support-wide fairness directly.

1. A fractional matching round

Introduce a dummy kind \(0\), representing receipt of no item, and set \(v_{\theta,0}=0\). Let \(r_g\) denote the remaining supply of real kind \(g\), and let \(G=\{g:r_g>0\}\).

Consider the following rational linear program. Its variables are \(x_{\theta,g}\geq 0\), for supported types \(\theta\) and kinds \(g\in G\cup\{0\}\):

\( \displaystyle \sum_{g\in G\cup\{0\}}x_{\theta,g}=\mu_\theta \quad\text{for every }\theta, \qquad \sum_\theta x_{\theta,g}\leq r_g \quad\text{for every }g\in G. \)

First maximize

\( \displaystyle W(x)=\sum_{\theta}\sum_{g\in G}v_{\theta,g}x_{\theta,g}. \)

Among maximizers of \(W\), maximize

\( \displaystyle S(x)=\sum_{\theta}\sum_{g\in G}g\,x_{\theta,g}. \)

Finally choose a vertex of this optimal face. All these operations take polynomial time: a vertex can, for example, be selected by successively optimizing the coordinates over the optimal face.

Write \(c_g=\sum_\theta x_{\theta,g}\). A single round assigns outcome \(g\) to mass \(x_{\theta,g}\) of type \(\theta\), consuming supply \(c_g\).

Three properties of this round will be used.

Property 1: recipients receive only nonnegative-valued items. If \(x_{\theta,g}>0\) and \(v_{\theta,g}<0\), moving a positive amount from \(x_{\theta,g}\) to \(x_{\theta,0}\) preserves feasibility and strictly increases \(W\). Thus

\( \displaystyle x_{\theta,g}>0 \Longrightarrow v_{\theta,g}\geq 0. \)

Property 2: each assigned outcome dominates every kind left after the round. Suppose \(x_{\theta,j}>0\) and \(r_h-c_h>0\). Moving a sufficiently small positive amount from \(x_{\theta,j}\) to \(x_{\theta,h}\) preserves feasibility. Optimality of \(W\) therefore implies

\( \displaystyle v_{\theta,j}\geq v_{\theta,h}. \)

Here \(j=0\) is allowed.

Property 3: a round that exhausts no kind is deterministic within each type. Suppose \(c_g<r_g\) for every \(g\in G\). For each type \(\theta\), let \(f_\theta\) be the unique kind in \(G\cup\{0\}\) maximizing the pair \((v_{\theta,g},g)\) lexicographically.

If \(x_{\theta,j}>0\) for \(j\neq f_\theta\), we can move a sufficiently small amount from \(j\) to \(f_\theta\): every real-kind constraint is slack, and the dummy has no supply constraint. This either increases \(W\), or preserves \(W\) and increases \(S\), a contradiction. Hence

\( \displaystyle x_{\theta,f_\theta}=\mu_\theta,\qquad x_{\theta,j}=0\quad(j\neq f_\theta). \)

Finally, whenever some supply remains, the round consumes a positive amount of real items. Otherwise every type receives only the dummy. Choose a remaining kind \(g\) and a supported type \(\theta\) with \(v_{\theta,g}\geq0\), which exists by subjectivity. Moving a small amount from the dummy to \(g\) improves \(W\) if \(v_{\theta,g}>0\), and improves \(S\) otherwise.

2. Batching gives at most \(2m\) phases

Initially set \(r_g=b_g\). While some supply remains, compute the round above.

  • If \(c_g=r_g\) for some remaining kind, execute this round once. It exhausts at least one kind.
  • Otherwise, Property 3 applies. Execute the same deterministic round

\( \displaystyle k=\left\lfloor\min_{g:c_g>0}\frac{r_g}{c_g}\right\rfloor \)
times, recording this as one batch.

In both cases update \(r_g\leftarrow r_g-kc_g\), with \(k=1\) in the first case.

We verify both the validity of batching and its phase bound.

For a batch of length \(k\), the same \(x\) remains feasible before every one of its rounds, since \(kc_g\leq r_g\). The feasible region only shrinks as supplies decrease. Consequently, because \(x\) remains feasible, it remains optimal for \(W\) and then for \(S\). Properties 1 and 2 therefore hold in every conceptual round of the batch.

If the batch exhausts a kind, it makes immediate progress. Otherwise, all remaining kinds stay available. For a kind \(h\) attaining the minimum defining \(k\),

\( \displaystyle 0<r_h-kc_h<c_h. \)

If the next optimal round also exhausted no kind, Property 3 would give exactly the same deterministic assignment: the available kinds, valuations, and tie-break have not changed. But that assignment consumes \(c_h\), exceeding the new remaining supply of \(h\). This is impossible.

Thus every phase either exhausts a kind or is immediately followed by a phase that does. Since there can be at most \(m\) phases that exhaust kinds, there are at most \(2m\) phases altogether. The algorithm terminates with every supply exhausted.

3. Combining the rounds into bundles

Index the phases by \(p\). Let \(k_p\) be the number of rounds in phase \(p\), and let \(x^p\) be its matching solution.

For each type \(\theta\), take an interval \(I_\theta=[0,\mu_\theta)\). In each phase \(p\), partition this interval into subintervals of lengths \(x^p_{\theta,g}\), labeled by outcomes \(g\). Take the common refinement of all these partitions.

For a refined interval \(J\subseteq I_\theta\), denote its label in phase \(p\) by \(g_p(J)\). Assign to its mass \(|J|\) the integer bundle

\( \displaystyle a(J)=\sum_{p:g_p(J)\neq0}k_p e_{g_p(J)}. \)

This assignment has the required type masses because the refined intervals partition \(I_\theta\). Its supply of kind \(g\) is

\( \displaystyle \sum_\theta\sum_{J\subseteq I_\theta}|J|\,a(J)_g =\sum_p k_p\sum_\theta x^p_{\theta,g} =b_g. \)

Each phase introduces at most \(m\) interior endpoints per type. With at most \(2m\) phases, this gives at most \(\tau(1+2m^2)\) intervals.

4. Rational nonnegative subsidies

For each phase, use an optimal dual solution for its primary matching LP. The dual is

\( \displaystyle \min_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\alpha_\theta+\sum_{g\in G}r_g\beta_g\right) \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle \alpha_\theta+\beta_g\geq v_{\theta,g},\qquad \alpha_\theta\geq0,\qquad \beta_g\geq0. \)

Set \(\beta_0=0\). The constraint \(\alpha_\theta\geq0\) comes from the dummy variable.

Our selected \(x\) remains primary-optimal, so complementary slackness gives

\( \displaystyle x_{\theta,g}>0\Longrightarrow v_{\theta,g}=\alpha_\theta+\beta_g, \)

including \(g=0\).

Let \(B=\max_{g\in G\cup\{0\}}\beta_g\). Attach subsidy \(B-\beta_g\geq0\) to outcome \(g\). A supported outcome \(g\) for type \(\theta\) then satisfies

\( \displaystyle v_{\theta,g}+B-\beta_g=\alpha_\theta+B. \)

For any other supported outcome \(h\), dual feasibility gives

\( \displaystyle v_{\theta,h}+B-\beta_h\leq\alpha_\theta+B. \)

Thus one round, including its subsidies, is envy-free across all its supported outcomes.

For a refined interval \(J\subseteq I_\theta\), assign total subsidy

\( \displaystyle \pi(J)=\sum_p k_p\bigl(B_p-\beta^p_{g_p(J)}\bigr). \)

These subsidies are nonnegative. Summing the round-wise inequalities proves, for every ordered pair of refined intervals \(J\subseteq I_\theta\) and \(J'\subseteq I_{\theta'}\),

\( \displaystyle u_\theta(a(J))+\pi(J)\geq u_\theta(a(J'))+\pi(J'). \)

This includes comparisons within the same type.

Moreover,

\( \displaystyle u_\theta(a(J))+\pi(J) =\sum_p k_p(\alpha^p_\theta+B_p), \)

which is independent of \(J\). Consequently, intervals of the same type receiving the same bundle have the same subsidy. They can therefore be merged into a single cell \((\theta,a)\).

5. EF1 across every occupied pair

Expand the phases conceptually into \(T=\sum_p k_p\) rounds. For an occupied cell \(i\), let \(g_i^t\) be its outcome in round \(t\), with the dummy contributing zero utility.

Fix occupied cells \(i=(\theta,a)\) and \(j=(\theta',a')\). Every outcome of \(i\) has nonnegative value to \(\theta\), by Property 1.

For each \(t<T\), if \(g_j^{t+1}\) is real, that kind has positive remaining supply after round \(t\): a positive mass receives it in the next round. Property 2 therefore gives

\( \displaystyle v_{\theta,g_i^t}\geq v_{\theta,g_j^{t+1}}. \)

If \(g_j^{t+1}=0\), the same inequality follows from \(v_{\theta,g_i^t}\geq0\).

Hence

\( \displaystyle u_\theta(a) =\sum_{t=1}^{T}v_{\theta,g_i^t} \geq\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}v_{\theta,g_i^t} \geq\sum_{t=2}^{T}v_{\theta,g_j^t} =u_\theta(a')-v_{\theta,g_j^1}. \)

If \(g_j^1=0\), or if \(v_{\theta,g_j^1}\leq0\), this implies \(u_\theta(a)\geq u_\theta(a')\).

Otherwise \(g_j^1\) is a real kind with positive value to \(\theta\), and \(a'_{g_j^1}>0\). The displayed inequality is exactly

\( \displaystyle u_\theta(a)\geq u_\theta(a'-e_{g_j^1}). \)

Thus every occupied ordered pair satisfies the requested EF1 condition. This also covers pairs of the same type.

If all supplies are zero, assigning the empty bundle and subsidy zero to every type handles the instance directly.

6. Polynomial encoding length and running time

Let \(D\) be the product of the input denominators appearing in the masses and supplies. Its binary encoding length is at most their total input encoding length.

Every matching-polytope vertex has coordinates in \(D^{-1}\mathbb Z\), provided the remaining supplies do. To see this, scale all variables by \(D\). The right-hand sides become integral. After reversing the signs of the kind-constraint rows, every real-item variable has one positive and one negative matrix entry; dummy and slack variables have one nonzero entry. This is a directed-incidence matrix with unit columns and is totally unimodular. Therefore every vertex of the scaled polytope is integral.

The chosen optimal face is a face of the original matching polytope, so its vertices are vertices of that polytope. Initially the supplies are multiples of \(1/D\), and integer batching preserves this invariant.

Define

\( \displaystyle M=D\sum_g b_g. \)

This is an integer with polynomial binary encoding length. Every conceptual round consumes at least \(1/D\) mass of real items. Therefore

\( \displaystyle T\leq M. \)

Every bundle coordinate is consequently at most \(M\), and every batch length is at most \(M\). Although these numbers can be exponentially large, their binary representations have polynomial length.

Throughout the algorithm, remaining supplies lie between zero and the input supplies and have denominator dividing \(D\). Thus every matching LP has polynomial input length. Optimal rational dual solutions can also be chosen with polynomial encoding length. Summing their subsidies over at most \(2m\) phases, with binary-encoded integer multipliers, preserves polynomial encoding length.

The interval refinement has polynomially many endpoints, all multiples of \(1/D\), and can be computed in polynomial time. Hence the entire construction takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

7. Reducing the final support to at most \(m+\tau\)

Let \(C\) be the polynomial-size set of occupied cells constructed above, with their subsidies fixed. Solve the balance LP on this set:

\( \displaystyle \sum_{a:(\theta,a)\in C}\lambda_{\theta,a}=\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_{(\theta,a)\in C}\lambda_{\theta,a}a_g=b_g,\qquad \lambda_{\theta,a}\geq0. \)

It is feasible by construction. Choose a vertex solution. There are \(m+\tau\) equality constraints, so at most \(m+\tau\) variables can be positive: otherwise their constraint columns would be linearly dependent, allowing a nonzero feasible perturbation in both directions and contradicting extremality.

Retain only these positive cells. Every fairness inequality remains valid because it was already valid for every ordered pair in \(C\). The balance LP has polynomial size and polynomial coefficient encoding length, so its vertex masses also have polynomial encoding length.

This completes the polynomial-time construction.

1. Fractional matching round

The primal LP is feasible: assigning every type its entire mass of dummy outcomes satisfies its constraints. It is bounded because each variable lies between zero and its type’s mass. Thus both successive optimization objectives attain their optima.

The three properties are valid:

  • Dominance over remaining kinds: if a kind has positive residual supply, a sufficiently small transfer to it is feasible. A strict increase in its value would contradict primary optimality. When the source and destination coincide, the claimed inequality is simply equality.
  • Determinism without exhaustion: every real supply constraint is then slack. A transfer to the unique lexicographically best outcome either improves the primary objective or preserves it and improves the secondary objective. The distinct kind indices, including dummy index zero, make this best outcome unique.

The positive-consumption argument also holds. Subjectivity supplies a supported type with a nonnegative value for any remaining kind; its positive type mass permits the proposed transfer. A zero-valued real kind improves the secondary objective because its index is positive.
