The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given three candidates, a target \(c^\star\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over transitive partial preference reports \(p\), is there an allocation \(x_{p,\rho}\geq 0\) of each report's mass among its linear extensions \(\rho\), with \(\sum_{\rho \text{ extends } p} x_{p,\rho}=\mu_p\), and an agenda under which \(c^\star\) wins by majority? Resolve pairwise ties using a fixed candidate priority order.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set with \(m=3\), let \(c^\star\in C\), and fix a strict candidate priority order for breaking pairwise ties.
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct transitively closed strict partial preference orders \(p\) on \(C\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_p\), where \(\sum_p\mu_p=1\). Incomparability means an unrevealed comparison, not indifference. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For each report \(p\), let \(E(p)\) be its strict linear extensions. A completion allocates real masses \(x_{p,\rho}\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_{\rho\in E(p)}x_{p,\rho}=\mu_p\); the same allocation is used in every match.
An agenda is a rooted full binary tree whose leaves contain each candidate exactly once. Each internal node advances the pairwise majority winner of its two children, and the root is the overall winner. Candidate \(a\) defeats \(b\) when \(\sum_p\sum_{\rho\in E(p):\,a\succ_\rho b}x_{p,\rho}>1/2\); at equality, the higher-priority candidate advances.

Is deciding whether some completion and some agenda make \(c^\star\) win solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(m=3\), or is it NP-hard?

For discrete profiles of agents with indivisible positive integer weights and odd total weight, the corresponding decision problem is NP-complete even with three candidates ([discrete result](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~twalsh/prvwkr08.pdf)). No result settling exactly the divisible-mass problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one, with strictly positive masses on its listed support.
  • Specified strict partial orders: missing comparisons represent uncertainty rather than indifference.
  • Specified agendas as full binary trees containing every candidate exactly once, with no additional shape restriction.
  • Allowed real-valued allocations and required one common completion across all matches.
  • Made the fixed tie-breaking priority a strict total order and defined L as the total input bit length.

Result

The divisible-mass problem is polynomial-time solvable for \(m=3\). Two rational linear programs suffice, with strict inequalities handled exactly.

1. Representing completions

Write \(C=\{c^\star,a,b\}\). For each report \(p\), enumerate its linear extensions \(E(p)\) by checking the six strict linear orders on \(C\). A strict partial order is acyclic, so it has a linear extension; thus \(E(p)\) is nonempty.

Introduce one real variable \(x_{p,\rho}\) for every \(\rho\in E(p)\). The completion constraints are

  • \(x_{p,\rho}\geq 0\) for every \(p,\rho\);
  • \(\sum_{\rho\in E(p)}x_{p,\rho}=\mu_p\) for every \(p\).

These constraints describe exactly the permitted completions.

For distinct candidates \(u,v\), define the linear expression

\(M_{u,v}(x)=\sum_p\sum_{\rho\in E(p):\,u\succ_\rho v}x_{p,\rho}\).

Every linear order ranks exactly one of \(u,v\) above the other, and the total mass is one. Consequently,

\(M_{u,v}(x)+M_{v,u}(x)=1\).

Define \(\epsilon(u,v)=0\) if \(u\) has higher tie-breaking priority than \(v\), and \(\epsilon(u,v)=1\) otherwise. The pairwise winning condition is therefore

  • \(M_{u,v}(x)\geq \tfrac12\) when \(\epsilon(u,v)=0\);
  • \(M_{u,v}(x)>\tfrac12\) when \(\epsilon(u,v)=1\).

In particular, each pair has exactly one winner.

2. Reducing the agenda choice to two possibilities

Lemma. For a fixed completion, \(c^\star\) wins under some agenda if and only if, for some ordering \((u,v)\) of its two rivals, \(c^\star\) defeats \(u\) and \(u\) defeats \(v\).

Proof. A rooted full binary tree with three leaves consists of a first match between two candidates, followed by a final against the remaining candidate.

If \(c^\star\) defeats \(u\) and \(u\) defeats \(v\), choose the agenda in which \(u\) first plays \(v\), and their winner plays \(c^\star\). Then \(c^\star\) wins.

Conversely, suppose an agenda makes \(c^\star\) win.

  • If \(c^\star\) enters directly into the final, let \(u\) be the winner of the rivals’ first match and \(v\) its loser. Then \(u\) defeats \(v\), and \(c^\star\) defeats \(u\).
  • If \(c^\star\) participates in the first match, it must defeat both rivals to win the agenda. Let \(u\) be the winner of the pairwise match between the rivals, and \(v\) its loser. This match has a unique winner, including when its majority is tied. Again, \(c^\star\) defeats \(u\), and \(u\) defeats \(v\).

This proves both directions. In particular, whenever a completion admits a winning agenda, that same completion admits a winning agenda with \(c^\star\) entering directly into the final. \(\square\)

Thus only the two rival orderings \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\) need to be considered.

3. Exact linear programs, including strict majorities

For each rival ordering \((u,v)\), introduce an additional variable \(\delta\) and solve the following rational linear program:

Maximize \(\delta\), subject to the completion constraints and

  • \(0\leq\delta\leq\tfrac12\);
  • \(M_{c^\star,u}(x)\geq\tfrac12+\epsilon(c^\star,u)\delta\);
  • \(M_{u,v}(x)\geq\tfrac12+\epsilon(u,v)\delta\).

The same allocation variables occur in both majority constraints. Thus this formulation preserves the requirement that one completion be used in every match.

Lemma. A completion satisfying “\(c^\star\) defeats \(u\) and \(u\) defeats \(v\)” exists if and only if this linear program is feasible and has strictly positive optimal value.

Proof.

Suppose such a completion \(x\) exists. For each of the two required victories whose winner has lower priority, its margin \(M_{r,s}(x)-\tfrac12\) is strictly positive.

If at least one required victory needs a strict majority, choose \(\delta\) to be the minimum of these positive margins and \(\tfrac12\). This minimum is positive. If neither victory needs a strict majority, choose \(\delta=\tfrac12\). In either case, \((x,\delta)\) satisfies every constraint, so the optimal value is positive.

Conversely, suppose the program is feasible with positive optimal value. Its feasible region is closed and bounded: every allocation variable lies between zero and its report mass, and \(\delta\) lies between zero and \(\tfrac12\). Hence the optimum is attained at some \((x,\delta)\) with \(\delta>0\).

For either required victory:

  • if its winner has higher priority, the constraint guarantees support at least \(\tfrac12\), which suffices;
  • if its winner has lower priority, the constraint guarantees support at least \(\tfrac12+\delta>\tfrac12\), which suffices.

The allocation \(x\) therefore realizes both victories. \(\square\)

4. Algorithm and polynomial running time

The decision algorithm is:

  • Enumerate \(E(p)\) for every report.
  • Construct and solve the linear programs for \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\).
  • Answer yes exactly when at least one program is feasible and has positive optimal value.

The two lemmas prove the algorithm correct in both directions.

Each program has at most \(6\tau+1\) variables and \(O(\tau)\) constraints. Its coefficients belong to \(\{0,1,-1\}\), and its right-hand sides are the input masses and fixed rational constants. Consequently, its binary encoding length is polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

We apply the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: feasibility and, for a nonempty bounded feasible region, the exact rational optimal value can be computed in time polynomial in the binary encoding length. The programs here satisfy these hypotheses. Comparing their exact optimal values with zero therefore takes polynomial time; no numerical tolerance or assumed lower bound on a strict majority margin is needed.

There are only two programs. Thus the complete decision procedure runs in polynomial time in \(\tau,L\), and hence in \(m,\tau,L\) on the stipulated domain \(m=3\).

5. Relation to the discrete hardness result

The [cited discrete result](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~twalsh/prvwkr08.pdf) concerns weighted agents whose preferences must each be completed indivisibly. Here, report mass may be split among linear extensions. That permitted splitting makes the completion set a rational polytope and enables the linear-programming algorithm above.

The proof establishes the result for exactly the divisible-mass model in the question.
