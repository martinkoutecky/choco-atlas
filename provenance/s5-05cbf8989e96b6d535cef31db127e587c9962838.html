The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over bank-location types \((r,z)\in\{1,2\}\times\mathbb{R}\), design a randomized bridge rule that is GSP against deviations by positive-mass coalitions and has worst-case expected social-cost ratio at most \(2\). Is \(2\) the best possible ratio under this incentive requirement?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m=2\) parallel banks, with identical facilities fixed at coordinate \(1\) on bank \(1\) and coordinate \(0\) on bank \(2\). A perpendicular bridge at \(s\in\mathbb R\) has zero crossing cost; there are no transfers.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types \((r,z)\in\{1,2\}\times\mathbb Q\), each with its mass as a binary rational. Masses sum to \(1\), and each bank has positive total mass. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Individual costs are \(c_1(z,s)=\min\{|z-1|,|z-s|+|s|\}\) and \(c_2(z,s)=\min\{|z|,|z-s|+|s-1|\}\). An anonymous randomized rule \(F\) maps each reported society to a probability distribution on \(\mathbb R\); individuals compare expected costs at their true locations.
A coalition may select any rational submass \(0<\nu\leq\mu\) and split it among finitely many rational location reports, preserving each member's bank and mass. Formally, a finite rational transport \(\gamma\) from true types to reported types has first marginal \(\nu\), preserves banks, and produces \(\mu'=\mu-\nu+\gamma_2\), where \(\gamma_2\) is its second marginal.
The rule is GSP if no such deviation strictly reduces \(\mathbb E_{s\sim F(\mu)}c_r(z,s)\) for \(\nu\)-almost every participating true type \((r,z)\).
Define \(\operatorname{SC}(\mu,s)=\int c_r(z,s)\,d\mu(r,z)\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=\inf_{s\in\mathbb R}\operatorname{SC}(\mu,s)\). A ratio \(\rho\) means \(\mathbb E_{s\sim F(\mu)}\operatorname{SC}(\mu,s)\leq\rho\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\) for every society, including those with zero optimum.

Does an anonymous randomized GSP bridge rule achieve worst-case expected social-cost ratio at most \(2\), and is \(2\) optimal among all such rules, or can a strictly smaller uniform ratio be achieved?

For finite agents, an anonymous randomized GSP rule achieves ratio \(2\); every randomized SP rule has ratio at least \(1.1\). [Verified discrete bounds](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04309v1).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted coordinates and reports to binary rationals so the input has a finite bit length; bridge outcomes remain unrestricted real coordinates.
  • Normalized total mass to 1 and required positive mass on both banks, retaining the two populated regions of the discrete model.
  • Specified divisible coalitions using finite rational transports, allowing partial participation and different reports by portions of the same type while preserving banks and masses.
  • Interpreted GSP as forbidding strict expected-cost improvement for almost every coalition member, matching the discrete all-members-strictly-gain definition.
  • Carried over anonymity, zero crossing cost, identical facilities at coordinates 1 and 0, and absence of transfers.
  • Defined approximation through an inequality that also requires zero expected social cost whenever the optimum is zero.

Proven results and remaining question

An anonymous randomized GSP rule achieves ratio $2$ in the stated mass-coalition model.

The unrestricted optimality question remains unresolved, but the bounds in the supplied notes can be strengthened:

  • Every GSP rule allowing arbitrary real-valued lotteries has worst-case ratio at least $4/3$.
  • Every GSP rule whose lotteries are supported on $[0,1]$ has worst-case ratio at least $2$.

Thus, writing $\rho^*$ for the infimum of achievable unrestricted uniform ratios,

$$ \frac43\leq \rho^*\leq 2. $$

Any improvement below $2$ must use bridge coordinates outside the facility interval. All claims below are proved directly for the permitted rational transports.

1. Geometry and the social optimum

Define the sensitive types

$$ A=\{(1,z):z<1/2\}, \qquad B=\{(2,z):z>1/2\}. $$

Every type outside $A\cup B$ has bridge-independent cost. Indeed, if $z\geq1/2$, then

$$ |z-s|+|s|\geq |z|\geq |z-1|, $$

so $c_1(z,s)=|z-1|$. If $z\leq1/2$, then

$$ |z-s|+|s-1|\geq |z-1|\geq |z|, $$

so $c_2(z,s)=|z|$.

For sensitive types, the endpoint costs are

$$ \begin{aligned} c_1(z,0)&=|z|, & c_1(z,1)&=1-z>|z| &&\text{if }(1,z)\in A,\\ c_2(z,0)&=z, & c_2(z,1)&=|z-1|<z &&\text{if }(2,z)\in B. \end{aligned} $$

For every $s\leq1/2$, endpoint $0$ weakly dominates $s$ for every type. For a type in $A$,

$$ c_1(z,s)\geq \min\{1-z,|z|\}=|z|=c_1(z,0), $$

using the triangle inequality for the bridge route. For a type in $B$, we have $z>s$ and $s<1$, and therefore

$$ |z-s|+|s-1|=z+1-2s\geq z. $$

Consequently $c_2(z,s)=z=c_2(z,0)$. The other types have constant costs.

The transformation exchanging banks and replacing $(z,s)$ by $(1-z,1-s)$ preserves the cost formulas. It therefore proves that endpoint $1$ weakly dominates every $s\geq1/2$.

It follows that, for every society,

$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu) = \min\{\operatorname{SC}(\mu,0),\operatorname{SC}(\mu,1)\}. $$

In particular, the infimum is attained.

2. An anonymous mass-coalition GSP rule with ratio $2$

Let $a=\mu(A)$ and $b=\mu(B)$. Output endpoint $0$ with probability

$$ p(a,b)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{a^2}{a^2+b^2},&a+b>0,\\[4pt] \dfrac12,&a=b=0, \end{cases} $$

and endpoint $1$ otherwise. This is the mass version of the endpoint lottery in the [finite-agent construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04309v1#S4.SS1). The following proofs establish its validity independently.

Group-strategyproofness

Write $p$ and $p'$ for the probabilities before and after a deviation.

Bridge-independent types cannot strictly benefit. Every true type in $A$ strictly benefits exactly when $p'>p$, while every true type in $B$ strictly benefits exactly when $p'<p$. Thus a successful coalition must consist entirely of types in $A$, or entirely of types in $B$.

Suppose first that its participating submass $\nu$ is supported on $A$. Bank preservation gives $b'=b$, and mass preservation gives

$$ a' = a-\nu(A)+\gamma_2(A) \leq a, $$

because the transport's second marginal has total mass $\nu(A)$. This inequality allows arbitrary splitting among reports.

If $b>0$, the function $p(a,b)$ is nondecreasing in $a$, so $p'\leq p$. If $b=0$, participation implies $a>0$, hence $p=1$, and an increase is again impossible.

For a coalition supported on $B$, the symmetric argument gives $a'=a$ and $b'\leq b$. If $a>0$, this implies $p'\geq p$; if $a=0$, initially $p=0$, which also cannot decrease.

No permitted coalition can strictly benefit every participating true type. The rule is GSP and is anonymous because it depends only on the reported masses $a$ and $b$.

Approximation guarantee

Put $C_i=\operatorname{SC}(\mu,i)$, and define

$$ U=\int_A\bigl((1-z)-|z|\bigr)\,d\mu, \qquad V=\int_B\bigl(z-|z-1|\bigr)\,d\mu. $$

Both integrands belong to $(0,1]$. Therefore

$$ U\leq a,\qquad V\leq b,\qquad C_1-C_0=U-V. $$

Moreover,

$$ C_0\geq \frac b2, \qquad C_1\geq \frac a2, $$

because the corresponding sensitive types have endpoint costs $z>1/2$ and $1-z>1/2$.

If $a+b=0$, all costs are constant and the rule is optimal. Otherwise, if $C_0\leq C_1$, then

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E\operatorname{SC} &=C_0+\frac{b^2}{a^2+b^2}(U-V)\\ &\leq C_0+\frac{ab^2}{a^2+b^2}\\ &\leq C_0+\frac b2\\ &\leq 2C_0. \end{aligned} $$

The third line follows from $2ab\leq a^2+b^2$. If $C_1\leq C_0$, symmetrically,

$$ \mathbb E\operatorname{SC} \leq C_1+\frac{ba^2}{a^2+b^2} \leq C_1+\frac a2 \leq 2C_1. $$

Together with Section 1, this proves the ratio-$2$ guarantee. No division by the optimum was used, so zero-optimum societies are covered.

Running time

The rule requires one scan of the $\tau$ support entries and exact rational arithmetic. Intermediate rational representations and the output probability have $O(L)$ bits. Using schoolbook arithmetic, a conservative bound is

$$ O((m+\tau)L^2) $$

bit operations and $O(L+m)$ working bits, excluding the input, with $m=2$. No expansion into equal-mass agents is needed. This running-time statement concerns the specified two-bank model.

3. An unrestricted lower bound of $4/3$

Consider the three societies

$$ \begin{aligned} \mu_{\mathrm L} &=\tfrac12\delta_{(1,0)} +\tfrac12\delta_{(2,3/4)},\\ \mu_{\mathrm R} &=\tfrac12\delta_{(1,1/4)} +\tfrac12\delta_{(2,1)},\\ \mu_{\mathrm W} &=\tfrac12\delta_{(1,1/4)} +\tfrac12\delta_{(2,3/4)}. \end{aligned} $$

All masses and locations are rational, and both banks have positive mass. Section 1 gives

$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu_{\mathrm L}) = \operatorname{OPT}(\mu_{\mathrm R}) = \frac38. $$

For every real $s$, abbreviate

$$ u(s)=c_1(0,s),\quad g(s)=c_1(1/4,s),\quad h(s)=c_2(3/4,s),\quad v(s)=c_2(1,s). $$

We prove three pointwise inequalities:

$$ u+h\geq g+\frac12, \qquad g+v\geq h+\frac12, \qquad g+h\geq1. $$

For the first inequality, suppose $s\leq1/2$. Then $h(s)=3/4$. Also,

$$ g(s)\leq u(s)+\frac14. $$

To justify this last bound, both route costs defining $c_1(z,s)$ change by at most $1/4$ when $z$ changes from $0$ to $1/4$, by the triangle inequality. Taking their minimum preserves this bound. Hence $u+h-g\geq1/2$.

If $s\geq1/2$, then $u(s)=1$ and $g(s)=3/4$. The triangle inequality gives $h(s)\geq1/4$, so again $u+h-g\geq1/2$.

Reflection proves the second inequality. For the third, when $s\leq1/2$ we have $h(s)=3/4$ and $g(s)\geq1/4$; when $s\geq1/2$, we have $g(s)=3/4$ and $h(s)\geq1/4$.

Now let $F$ be any GSP rule with finite uniform ratio $\rho$, allowing arbitrary distributions on $\mathbb R$. Write $\mathbb E_{\mathrm L}$, $\mathbb E_{\mathrm R}$ and $\mathbb E_{\mathrm W}$ for expectations under its three lotteries.

At $\mu_{\mathrm W}$, the entire bank-$1$ mass can report location $0$, producing $\mu_{\mathrm L}$. All participants have the same true type, so GSP implies

$$ \mathbb E_{\mathrm W}g\leq\mathbb E_{\mathrm L}g. $$

Likewise, the entire bank-$2$ mass can report location $1$, producing $\mu_{\mathrm R}$, and GSP implies

$$ \mathbb E_{\mathrm W}h\leq\mathbb E_{\mathrm R}h. $$

Taking expectations of the pointwise inequalities therefore gives

$$ \begin{aligned} 2\mathbb E_{\mathrm L}\operatorname{SC}(\mu_{\mathrm L},s) &=\mathbb E_{\mathrm L}(u+h) \geq \mathbb E_{\mathrm W}g+\frac12,\\ 2\mathbb E_{\mathrm R}\operatorname{SC}(\mu_{\mathrm R},s) &=\mathbb E_{\mathrm R}(g+v) \geq \mathbb E_{\mathrm W}h+\frac12. \end{aligned} $$

Adding and using $\mathbb E_{\mathrm W}(g+h)\geq1$ yields

$$ \mathbb E_{\mathrm L}\operatorname{SC}(\mu_{\mathrm L},s) + \mathbb E_{\mathrm R}\operatorname{SC}(\mu_{\mathrm R},s) \geq1. $$

The ratio guarantee bounds the same sum by $3\rho/4$. Consequently,

$$ \rho\geq\frac43. $$

All costs used here are bounded by their finite local-facility costs, so these expectations exist for arbitrary lotteries. The proof uses only homogeneous-bank deviations and does not require anonymity.

4. Ratio $2$ is optimal for lotteries supported on $[0,1]$

We prove a quantitative statement that also identifies where a possible unrestricted improvement must place probability.

For rational $d\in(0,1)$, put

$$ x_d=\frac{1-d}{2}, \qquad y_d=\frac{1+d}{2}, $$

and define

$$ \begin{aligned} \mu_d^{\mathrm L} &=\tfrac12\delta_{(1,0)} +\tfrac12\delta_{(2,y_d)},\\ \mu_d^{\mathrm R} &=\tfrac12\delta_{(1,x_d)} +\tfrac12\delta_{(2,1)},\\ \mu_d^{\mathrm W} &=\tfrac12\delta_{(1,x_d)} +\tfrac12\delta_{(2,y_d)}. \end{aligned} $$

By Section 1,

$$ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu_d^{\mathrm L}) = \operatorname{OPT}(\mu_d^{\mathrm R}) = \frac{1+d}{4}. $$

Benefit formulas

For $e\in(0,1]$, define the savings relative to the local facility by

$$ u_e(s)=\frac{1+e}{2}-c_1\!\left(\frac{1-e}{2},s\right), \qquad v_e(s)=\frac{1+e}{2}-c_2\!\left(\frac{1+e}{2},s\right). $$

Write $t_+=\max\{t,0\}$. Direct calculation gives

$$ u_e(s)= \begin{cases} (e+2s)_+,&s<0,\\ \min\{e,(1-2s)_+\},&s\geq0. \end{cases} $$

Indeed, for $s<0$ the bridge route costs $(1-e)/2-2s$. For $s\geq0$, the saving is $e$ up to the true location, then $1-2s$ up to $1/2$, and then zero. Reflection gives

$$ v_e(s)= \begin{cases} \min\{e,(2s-1)_+\},&s\leq1,\\ (e-2(s-1))_+,&s>1. \end{cases} $$

These formulas imply, for every real $s$,

$$ 0\leq u_d(s),v_d(s)\leq d, \qquad u_d(s)+v_d(s)\leq d. $$

The last inequality holds because the two benefits cannot both be positive.

They also imply

$$ \begin{aligned} u_d(s)&\geq d\,u_1(s)-d\,\mathbf 1_{\{s<0\}},\\ v_d(s)&\geq d\,v_1(s)-d\,\mathbf 1_{\{s>1\}}. \end{aligned} $$

For example, when $s\geq0$, let $t=(1-2s)_+\in[0,1]$. Then $u_d(s)=\min\{d,t\}\geq dt=d\,u_1(s)$. When $s<0$, the claimed lower bound follows from $u_d(s)\geq0$ and $u_1(s)\leq1$. The argument for $v_d$ is reflected.

Incentive and ratio inequalities

For any society $\mu$, write

$$ U_\mu(e)=\mathbb E_{F(\mu)}u_e(s), \qquad V_\mu(e)=\mathbb E_{F(\mu)}v_e(s). $$

Define the exterior probabilities

$$ q_{\mathrm L}(d)=F(\mu_d^{\mathrm L})((-\infty,0)), \qquad q_{\mathrm R}(d)=F(\mu_d^{\mathrm R})((1,\infty)). $$

At $\mu_d^{\mathrm W}$, the homogeneous bank-$1$ mass can report $0$, producing $\mu_d^{\mathrm L}$. Because its local-facility cost is fixed, GSP gives

$$ U_{\mu_d^{\mathrm W}}(d)\geq U_{\mu_d^{\mathrm L}}(d). $$

The corresponding bank-$2$ deviation gives

$$ V_{\mu_d^{\mathrm W}}(d)\geq V_{\mu_d^{\mathrm R}}(d). $$

Using the pointwise benefit inequalities,

$$ \begin{aligned} d &\geq U_{\mu_d^{\mathrm W}}(d)+V_{\mu_d^{\mathrm W}}(d)\\ &\geq U_{\mu_d^{\mathrm L}}(d)+V_{\mu_d^{\mathrm R}}(d)\\ &\geq d\left( U_{\mu_d^{\mathrm L}}(1)+V_{\mu_d^{\mathrm R}}(1) -q_{\mathrm L}(d)-q_{\mathrm R}(d) \right). \end{aligned} $$

Since $d>0$,

$$ U_{\mu_d^{\mathrm L}}(1)+V_{\mu_d^{\mathrm R}}(1) \leq1+q_{\mathrm L}(d)+q_{\mathrm R}(d). $$

At $\mu_d^{\mathrm L}$, the sum of the two unweighted local-facility costs is $(3+d)/2$. Thus a ratio-$\rho$ guarantee gives

$$ U_{\mu_d^{\mathrm L}}(1)+V_{\mu_d^{\mathrm L}}(d) \geq \frac{3+d-\rho(1+d)}{2}. $$

Since $V_{\mu_d^{\mathrm L}}(d)\leq d$,

$$ U_{\mu_d^{\mathrm L}}(1) \geq \frac{3-d-\rho(1+d)}{2}. $$

The reflected argument gives the same lower bound for $V_{\mu_d^{\mathrm R}}(1)$. Combining all inequalities proves

$$ q_{\mathrm L}(d)+q_{\mathrm R}(d) \geq 2-d-\rho(1+d). $$

Consequences

If all lotteries are supported on $[0,1]$, both exterior probabilities vanish. Therefore, for every rational $d\in(0,1)$,

$$ \rho\geq\frac{2-d}{1+d}. $$

Letting rational $d$ decrease to zero proves $\rho\geq2$. Together with Section 2, this settles optimality within the entire class of lotteries supported on $[0,1]$.

More generally, any unrestricted rule with $\rho<2$ must satisfy

$$ q_{\mathrm L}(d)+q_{\mathrm R}(d) \geq (2-\rho)-(1+\rho)d. $$

In particular, along rational $d\downarrow0$,

$$ \liminf_{d\downarrow0} \bigl(q_{\mathrm L}(d)+q_{\mathrm R}(d)\bigr) \geq2-\rho. $$

Thus an improvement below $2$ requires quantitatively positive exterior probability on these explicit families.

5. What remains open

The existence of a ratio-$2$ rule is proved. The unrestricted optimum is bounded by $4/3$ and $2$, and ratio $2$ is optimal when outcomes stay within $[0,1]$.

The remaining question is whether exterior coordinates can support an anonymous randomized GSP rule with uniform ratio strictly below $2$, despite arbitrary rational submass deviations and report splitting. Endpoint dominance concerns social cost at a fixed society; it does not establish that projecting a rule’s lotteries to endpoints preserves GSP. None of the arguments above settles the unrestricted optimality question.
