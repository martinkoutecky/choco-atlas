Proposition C.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates, \(\tau\) ranking types with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and a fixed \(K\)-list \(L\), compute the worst-case metric distortion of its induced lottery \(p_L\). Each type’s mass is represented by a voter point shared by all members of that type; the unknown pseudometric on those points and the candidates must be consistent with the type rankings. Return the supremum of weighted social cost divided by the minimum candidate cost, including \(+\infty\) when a zero-optimum obstruction or an unbounded positive-optimum LP exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge1\) candidates. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\ge1\) distinct strict ranking types, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
The input also contains an explicitly listed ordered tuple \(\mathcal L=(c_1,\ldots,c_K)\in C^K\), with \(K\ge1\) and repetitions allowed, inducing \(p_{\mathcal L}(c)=|\{j:c_j=c\}|/K\). Let \(L\) denote the total input bit length.
Represent each type by one voter point \(v_t\), shared by its entire mass. Allow all finite-valued pseudometrics \(d\) on these points and the candidates satisfying \(a\succ_t b\Rightarrow d(v_t,a)\le d(v_t,b)\); distance ties are compatible with either reported order.
Define \(\operatorname{SC}_d(c)=\sum_t\mu_t d(v_t,c)\), \(\operatorname{SC}_d(p)=\sum_c p(c)\operatorname{SC}_d(c)\), and \(\operatorname{OPT}_d=\min_c\operatorname{SC}_d(c)\).
Compute \(\rho(\mathcal L)=\sup_d\operatorname{SC}_d(p_{\mathcal L})/\operatorname{OPT}_d\), interpreting \(0/0\) as \(0\) and a positive numerator over zero as \(+\infty\). The output is the exact finite value or \(+\infty\), including when positive-optimum ratios are unbounded.

Can \(\rho(\mathcal L)\) be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is this computation NP-hard?

For a society explicitly listing \(n\) voters, fixed-list distortion is computable using \(m\) polynomial-size optimization LPs and \(m\) feasibility problems, with \(O((m+n)^2)\) variables and \(O((m+n)^3)\) triangle constraints per program; zero-optimum obstructions and unbounded positive-optimum ratios are included.

Result

The value \(\rho(\mathcal L)\) is computable exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\). The weighted support admits a direct rational LP formulation; there is no need to expand a type into individual voters.

1. Encoding all admissible pseudometrics

Let \(V=C\sqcup\{v_1,\ldots,v_\tau\}\), with \(q=m+\tau\) labeled points. Introduce a variable \(x_{uv}\) for each unordered pair of distinct points. Use the conventions \(x_{vu}=x_{uv}\) and \(x_{uu}=0\).

Define the polyhedral cone \(P\) by the following constraints:

  • \(x_{uv}\ge 0\) for all distinct \(u,v\in V\);
  • \(x_{uw}\le x_{uv}+x_{vw}\) for all \(u,v,w\in V\);
  • \(x_{v_ta}\le x_{v_tb}\) whenever type \(t\) reports \(a\succ_t b\).

Every feasible vector defines exactly an admissible finite-valued pseudometric on the labeled points, and every admissible pseudometric gives a feasible vector. In particular, zero distances between distinct labels are allowed, and the weak ranking inequalities implement the stipulated compatibility of distance ties.

Let \(k_c=|\{j:c_j=c\}|\). Define the linear forms

\(S_c(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t x_{v_tc}\)

and

\(N(x)=\sum_{c\in C}\frac{k_c}{K}S_c(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{c\in C}\frac{\mu_t k_c}{K}x_{v_tc}\).

These are precisely the candidate social costs and the lottery social cost.

2. Detecting a positive numerator with zero optimum

For each candidate \(a\in C\), solve the rational feasibility problem

\(x\in P,\qquad S_a(x)=0,\qquad N(x)\ge 1.\)

Claim. At least one of these problems is feasible if and only if an admissible pseudometric has zero optimum and positive lottery cost.

Proof. A feasible vector has \(S_a(x)=0\). Since all candidate social costs are nonnegative, its optimum is zero, while its lottery cost is positive.

Conversely, suppose an admissible pseudometric has zero optimum and positive lottery cost. Choose a candidate \(a\) attaining the zero optimum. Scaling every distance by the positive factor \(1/N(x)\) preserves all pseudometric and ranking constraints, leaves \(S_a\) zero, and makes \(N\) equal to one. This gives a feasible vector for the problem indexed by \(a\). \(\square\)

Thus, if any feasibility problem succeeds, the algorithm returns \(+\infty\).

3. Capturing all positive-optimum ratios

For every candidate \(a\), consider the optimization LP

\(\text{maximize }N(x)\)

subject to

\(x\in P,\qquad S_a(x)=1,\qquad S_c(x)\ge 1\text{ for every }c\in C.\)

Write \(h_a\) for its supremum, allowing \(h_a=+\infty\) when the objective is unbounded.

Every such LP is feasible: set every distance between distinct labeled points to one. This satisfies the triangle and ranking constraints, and every candidate has social cost \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Its objective is also one.

Claim. The supremum of the distortion ratios over all admissible pseudometrics with positive optimum is \(\max_{a\in C}h_a\).

Proof. First, take an admissible pseudometric represented by \(x\), with positive optimum \(s=\min_c S_c(x)>0\). Choose a candidate \(a\) attaining that minimum and set \(y=x/s\). Homogeneity of all constraints defining \(P\) gives \(y\in P\), and

\(S_a(y)=1,\qquad S_c(y)\ge 1\text{ for every }c.\)

Moreover,

\(N(y)=N(x)/s=\operatorname{SC}_d(p_{\mathcal L})/\operatorname{OPT}_d.\)

Thus every positive-optimum ratio occurs as an objective value in one of these LPs.

Conversely, every feasible vector for the LP indexed by \(a\) defines an admissible pseudometric whose optimum is exactly one: \(S_a=1\), and all other candidate costs are at least one. Its distortion ratio is therefore exactly its objective value \(N(x)\).

The set of positive-optimum ratios is consequently the union of the feasible objective-value sets of these \(m\) LPs. Taking suprema over this finite union proves the claim. \(\square\)

In particular, an unbounded optimization LP proves that positive-optimum ratios are unbounded. Each feasible vector still represents a finite-valued pseudometric; unboundedness means that their ratios can be arbitrarily large.

If all zero-optimum feasibility problems failed, every zero-optimum pseudometric has numerator zero and hence contributes ratio zero under the specified convention. Since the positive-optimum construction above gives ratio one, those zero-optimum cases do not affect the supremum. Therefore, after the feasibility tests fail,

\(\rho(\mathcal L)=\max_{a\in C}h_a.\)

The algorithm returns \(+\infty\) if any optimization LP is unbounded, and otherwise returns the maximum of their exact optimal values.

4. Exact polynomial-time computation

Each program has \(\binom{m+\tau}{2}=O((m+\tau)^2)\) variables. There are \(O((m+\tau)^3)\) triangle inequalities and at most \(\tau\binom m2\) ranking inequalities. Each optimization LP adds \(m\) cost constraints; each feasibility problem adds two.

The coefficients are rational. In particular, the objective coefficient associated with \(x_{v_tc}\) is \(\mu_t k_c/K\), whose binary encoding length is \(O(L+\log K)\). Counting the \(k_c\) from the explicitly listed tuple takes polynomial time. Consequently, constructing all \(m\) feasibility problems and all \(m\) optimization LPs takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\), and their total binary encoding length is polynomial in these parameters.

Apply the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: given a rational LP in binary encoding, feasibility and objective unboundedness can be decided in polynomial time, and a feasible LP with finite optimum has an exact rational optimal value that can be computed in polynomial time. All the programs above satisfy its hypotheses. In the bounded case, their rational optimal values also have polynomial encoding length, as follows from the determinant bounds for basic optimal solutions.

Taking the maximum of \(m\) exact rational values completes the computation. This handles both positive-cost zero-optimum obstructions and unbounded positive-optimum ratios.
