The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete types \(t=(a_t,d_t,v_t)\), with public predictor coefficient \(a_t\), verified data value \(d_t\), and private unit cost \(v_t\), consider direct mechanisms that observe cost reports and assign each user an inclusion probability and expected scaled transfer. Require dominant-strategy truthfulness and individual rationality for each user, and integrated transfers within budget \(B\). For \(\theta_t\in[0,1]\), define residual weight \(R=\sum_t\mu_t|a_t|(1-\theta_t)\); selected users incur privacy cost \(v_t|a_t|/R\). What is the best worst-case approximation ratio for maximizing \(\sum_t\mu_t|a_t|\theta_t\), relative to the cost-aware benchmark \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_{\theta\in[0,1]^\tau}\{\sum_t\mu_t|a_t|\theta_t:\sum_t\mu_t|a_t|(v_t+B)\theta_t\le B\sum_t\mu_t|a_t|\}\), and can a truthful mechanism achieve a ratio below \(2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless population of total mass \(1\), represented by the support list of \(\tau\) complete types \(t=(a_t,d_t,v_t)\), with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. All input numbers are binary rationals, \(a_t\ne0\), \(v_t>0\), \(B>0\), and verified data lie in a fixed bounded interval \([D_{\min},D_{\max}]\) with \(D_{\min}<D_{\max}\). Coefficients are public; only costs can be reported falsely.
A direct mechanism observes the reported distribution \(\nu\), not the true cost distribution, and supplies menus \(\theta_\nu(a,d,r)\in[0,1]\) and \(p_\nu(a,d,r)\ge0\) for every positive rational cost report \(r\), including reports outside its support. These are inclusion probabilities and expected transfers per unit population mass, in the same units as \(B\).
For each reported distribution, write \(\theta_t=\theta_\nu(a_t,d_t,r_t)\), \(p_t=p_\nu(a_t,d_t,r_t)\), and require \(R_\nu=\sum_t\nu_t|a_t|(1-\theta_t)>0\) and \(\sum_t\nu_t p_t\le B\). A selected user of true cost \(v\) incurs cost \(v|a|/R_\nu\); an excluded user incurs zero cost.
A unilateral report changes neither \(\nu\) nor \(R_\nu\). For every \(\nu,a,d,v,r\), dominant-strategy truthfulness requires \(p_\nu(a,d,v)-v|a|\theta_\nu(a,d,v)/R_\nu\ge p_\nu(a,d,r)-v|a|\theta_\nu(a,d,r)/R_\nu\); individual rationality requires the truthful utility to be nonnegative. Both conditions concern expected utility, with residual weight computed from inclusion probabilities.
On truthful input \(\mu\), the objective is \(S_M(\mu)=\sum_t\mu_t|a_t|\theta_t\), and the cost-aware benchmark is \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=\max\{\sum_t\mu_t|a_t|z_t:z\in[0,1]^\tau,\ \sum_t\mu_t|a_t|(v_t+B)z_t\le B\sum_t\mu_t|a_t|\}\). Define \(\rho(M)=\sup_\mu\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)/S_M(\mu)\), with ratio \(+\infty\) when \(S_M(\mu)=0\). State running times in \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and any approximation parameter; the statistic is scalar, so \(m=1\).

What is the infimum worst-case approximation ratio \(\inf_M\rho(M)\) over these truthful, individually rational, budget-feasible mechanisms; in particular, can any such mechanism guarantee \(\rho(M)<2\)?

For finite agents with binary inclusion, no truthful, individually rational, budget-feasible canonical Laplace mechanism guarantees \(2-\varepsilon\) for any \(\varepsilon>0\); a \(5\)-approximation exists, improved to \(2\) for equal absolute weights. [Source](https://ece.northeastern.edu/fac-ece/ioannidis/static/pdf/2014/priv-auction-singlecol_1.pdf).
The finite-agent fractional benchmark without truthfulness has a polynomial-time optimum; this does not settle the atomless truthful problem specified here. No result settling that exact problem was located. [Source](https://ece.northeastern.edu/fac-ece/ioannidis/static/pdf/2014/priv-auction-singlecol_1.pdf).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted distributional masses as an atomless population, rather than strategic agents controlling entire support masses; unilateral deviations leave the aggregate distribution and residual weight fixed.
  • Normalized total population mass to 1 and interpreted transfers as expected payments per unit population mass, integrated directly against the distribution.
  • Specified that the mechanism observes reported costs rather than being given the true private-cost distribution; required menus for reports outside the current support.
  • Used positive rational costs and reports, nonzero rational coefficients of either sign, a positive rational budget, and a nondegenerate bounded verified-data interval; excluded zero residual weight to avoid undefined privacy costs.
  • Specified truthfulness and individual rationality in expected utility, with the residual computed from inclusion probabilities rather than realized random selections.
  • The discrete assumption that each individual can be purchased alone imposes no additional restriction on atomless users, whose individual mass is zero; no corresponding restriction was imposed on an entire type mass.
  • Defined approximation as benchmark divided by achieved selected weight, with infinite ratio for zero achieved weight, and optimized over mechanisms without imposing a polynomial-time restriction.
  • There is no varying feature dimension in the stated scalar problem; set m to 1 and retained support size and total binary input length as computational parameters.

Result

Under the fixed-distribution notion of unilateral deviation in the question,

\[ \inf_M \rho(M)=\frac{e}{e-1}\approx 1.582<2. \]

The infimum is attained by a mechanism computable in \(\mathop{\mathrm{poly}}(\tau,L)\) time. The proof below gives both a truthful mechanism achieving this bound and finite rational instances establishing its optimality.

The fixed-distribution assumption is essential: a unilateral report changes the selected menu entry but leaves the menu and its residual weight unchanged. The finite-agent lower bound in the [cited paper](https://ece.northeastern.edu/fac-ece/ioannidis/static/pdf/2014/priv-auction-singlecol_1.pdf) therefore does not establish a lower bound of \(2\) for this model.

1. Normalize the benchmark

For any reported input, define \(w_t=\nu_t|a_t|\), \(W=\sum_t w_t\), and normalized reported costs \(c_t=r_t/B\). Thus \(W>0\).

Let \(c_1<\cdots<c_k\) be the distinct normalized costs, with \(k\le\tau\), and let

\[ F_i=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{t:c_t\le c_i}w_t. \]

In particular, \(F_k=1\). Define the weighted cost quantile function \(c(u)=c_i\) when \(F_{i-1}<u\le F_i\), with \(F_0=0\).

On truthful input, write \(x_*=\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)/W\). The benchmark constraint, divided by \(BW\), is

\[ \sum_t\frac{w_t}{W}(1+c_t)z_t\le1. \]

For any prescribed selected weight fraction \(x\), its minimum possible cost is \(\int_0^x(1+c(u))\,du\). Indeed, if some higher-cost weight is selected while lower-cost weight remains unselected, transferring selected weight from the former to the latter weakly decreases cost. Repeating this exchange selects costs in increasing order, with possible fractional selection at the last cost.

Consequently, \(x_*\) is the unique solution of

\[ \int_0^{x_*}(1+c(u))\,du=1. \]

Existence and uniqueness follow because the integrand is positive, the integral starts at zero, and its value at \(1\) exceeds \(1\). Thus \(0<x_*<1\).

2. A truthful threshold-mixture mechanism

For each reported distribution, solve the finite linear program

\[ y=\max_{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k\ge0} \left\{ \sum_i\lambda_iF_i: \sum_i\lambda_i\le1,\quad \sum_i\lambda_i(1+c_i)F_i\le1 \right\}. \]

Choose an optimal solution and set \(R=W(1-y)\). For every public pair \((a,d)\) and every positive rational report \(r\), including off-support reports, define

\[ \theta_\nu(a,d,r)=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf{1}_{\{r/B\le c_i\}}, \qquad p_\nu(a,d,r)=\frac{B|a|}{R} \sum_i\lambda_i c_i\mathbf{1}_{\{r/B\le c_i\}}. \]

These formulas define a mechanism on every reported input of the stipulated form.

Positive residual and valid probabilities. Since \(\sum_i\lambda_i\le1\), every inclusion probability belongs to \([0,1]\). Moreover,

\[ 1\ge\sum_i\lambda_i(1+c_i)F_i\ge(1+c_1)y. \]

Therefore \(y<1\), so \(R>0\). Summing inclusion probabilities against the input weights gives selected weight \(Wy\), hence the residual computed from the mechanism is exactly \(W-Wy=R\).

Budget feasibility. Its total expected transfer is

\[ \sum_t\nu_t p_t =\frac{BW}{R}\sum_i\lambda_i c_iF_i \le\frac{BW}{R}(1-y)=B. \]

Truthfulness and individual rationality. Fix \(\nu,a,d\), and a true cost \(v\). Reporting \(r\) gives utility

\[ \frac{B|a|}{R} \sum_i\lambda_i(c_i-v/B)\mathbf{1}_{\{r/B\le c_i\}}. \]

At \(r=v\), each positive summand is included and each negative summand is excluded. Any other report can only remove nonnegative summands or add nonpositive summands. Thus truthful reporting maximizes utility. The truthful utility is nonnegative.

This argument uses precisely the stipulated fact that a unilateral report leaves \(\nu\) and \(R\) fixed.

3. Approximation guarantee

We prove \(y\ge(1-1/e)x_*\).

The dual of the threshold-mixture linear program is

\[ y=\min_{\alpha,\gamma\ge0} \left\{ \alpha+\gamma: \alpha(1+c_i)F_i+\gamma\ge F_i \text{ for every }i \right\}. \]

The primal is feasible and bounded, and the dual is feasible, for example at \((\alpha,\gamma)=(1,0)\). Finite-dimensional linear-programming duality therefore supplies an optimal dual solution with objective \(y\).

Because \(y<1\), its \(\alpha\) is positive: if \(\alpha=0\), the constraint at \(F_k=1\) would imply \(\gamma\ge1\). Set

\[ K=\frac1\alpha,\qquad H=\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}. \]

Then \(Ky=1+H\). Since \(y\ge\alpha\) and \(y<1\), we have \(K>1\). The dual constraints imply

\[ c_i\ge K-1-\frac{H}{F_i}. \]

For \(F_{i-1}<u\le F_i\), using \(H\ge0\) and \(F_i\ge u\) gives the pointwise bound

\[ c(u)\ge\max\left\{0,K-1-\frac{H}{u}\right\}. \]

If \(H=0\), integrating yields \(1\ge Kx_*\), so \(x_*\le1/K=y\).

Now suppose \(H>0\), and define \(z=H/(K-1)\). Since \(Ky=1+H<K\), we have \(0<z<1\), and

\[ y=\frac{1+(K-1)z}{K} =z+\frac{1-z}{K}>z. \]

If \(x_*\le z\), then \(x_*<y\). Otherwise, integrate the quantile lower bound over \((z,x_*)\):

\[ 1=\int_0^{x_*}(1+c(u))\,du \ge Kx_*-H-H\log(x_*/z). \]

Substituting \(Ky=1+H\) and writing \(\eta=(K-1)/K<1\) gives

\[ y\ge x_*-\eta z\log(x_*/z). \]

For \(t>1\), the function \(\log(t)/t\) has derivative \((1-\log t)/t^2\), so its maximum is \(1/e\), attained at \(t=e\). Taking \(t=x_*/z\), we obtain

\[ y\ge x_*\left(1-\frac{\eta}{e}\right) \ge x_*\left(1-\frac1e\right). \]

Thus, on every truthful input,

\[ \frac{\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)}{S_M(\mu)} =\frac{x_*}{y}\le\frac{e}{e-1}. \]

4. An upper bound on what any truthful menu can purchase

For the lower bound, consider inputs with one public pair, \(a=1\), total mass \(1\), and \(B=1\). Fix any allowed mechanism.

Let the supported costs be \(v_1<\cdots<v_m\), with masses \(\mu_i\), and define \(F_i=\sum_{\ell\le i}\mu_\ell\). Write its supported allocation and payment entries as \(\theta_i,p_i\), its selected mass as \(S\), and its residual as \(R=1-S>0\). Define \(q_i=Rp_i\).

Adding the truthfulness inequalities between costs \(v_i<v_j\) gives

\[ (v_j-v_i)(\theta_i-\theta_j)\ge0. \]

Hence \(\theta_1\ge\cdots\ge\theta_m\). Define \(\theta_{m+1}=0\) and \(\delta_i=\theta_i-\theta_{i+1}\). Then \(\delta_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_i\delta_i=\theta_1\le1\).

Truthfulness for cost \(v_i\) against report \(v_{i+1}\) gives \(q_i\ge q_{i+1}+v_i\delta_i\). Individual rationality at \(v_m\) gives \(q_m\ge v_m\theta_m\). Induction therefore yields

\[ q_i\ge\sum_{j=i}^m v_j\delta_j. \]

Summing against masses, and using \(S=\sum_j\delta_jF_j\), gives

\[ \sum_i\mu_iq_i\ge\sum_jv_j\delta_jF_j. \]

Budget feasibility implies \(\sum_i\mu_iq_i\le R=1-S\), so necessarily

\[ 1\ge\sum_j\delta_j(1+v_j)F_j. \]

In particular, if an input satisfies

\[ (1+v_j)F_j\ge nF_j-h \quad\text{for every }j, \]

then

\[ 1\ge nS-h\theta_1\ge nS-h, \qquad\text{and therefore}\qquad S\le\frac{1+h}{n}. \]

This bound applies to every allowed mechanism, independently of its off-support behavior.

5. Finite rational instances approaching the bound

Fix any \(q<e/(e-1)\). We construct an input whose approximation ratio exceeds \(q\).

Let \(h_0=1/(e-2)\). Choose \(s_*\) with \(h_0<s_*<eh_0\), sufficiently close to \(eh_0\), that

\[ \frac{s_*}{1+h_0}>q. \]

Define

\[ I(h,s)=\int_h^s(1-h/u)\,du =s-h-h\log(s/h). \]

Since \(I(h_0,eh_0)=h_0(e-2)=1\), and \(I(h_0,s)\) is strictly increasing for \(s>h_0\), we have \(I(h_0,s_*)<1\).

By continuity and density of rational numbers, choose positive rational \(h,s_J\) such that

\[ s_J>h,\qquad \frac{s_J}{1+h}>q,\qquad I(h,s_J)<1. \]

Choose rational \(h<s_0<s_1<\cdots<s_J\), with \(s_0\) sufficiently close to \(h\) and the partition sufficiently fine, that

\[ C=s_0-h+\sum_{j=1}^J(s_j-s_{j-1})(1-h/s_j)<1. \]

This is possible because the sum is a right Riemann sum, and \(C\) converges to \(I(h,s_J)\) as \(s_0\) decreases to \(h\) and the mesh tends to zero.

Choose an integer \(n\) satisfying

\[ n>s_J,\qquad n(1-h/s_0)>1. \]

Use cumulative masses \(F_j=s_j/n\) and corresponding costs

\[ v_j=n-1-\frac{nh}{s_j}, \qquad 0\le j\le J. \]

Add a final type with cumulative mass \(1\) and cost \(v_{\mathrm{tail}}=n-1-h\). All individual masses are positive: they are \(s_0/n\), successive differences \((s_j-s_{j-1})/n\), and \(1-s_J/n\). The costs are positive and strictly increasing.

Every supported cost satisfies

\[ (1+v_j)F_j=nF_j-h, \]

including the tail. Section 4 therefore implies that every allowed mechanism purchases at most \((1+h)/n\).

On the other hand, the benchmark can select all types through cumulative mass \(s_J/n\). Its normalized benchmark cost is exactly \(C<1\). Consequently,

\[ \mathrm{OPT}\ge\frac{s_J}{n}, \qquad \frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{S_M} \ge\frac{s_J}{1+h}>q. \]

If \(S_M=0\), the ratio is infinite, and the conclusion still holds.

The construction uses finite rational inputs. It also works if “binary rational” is interpreted as requiring dyadic values: choose \(h,s_j\) dyadic and \(n\) a power of two, then round each cost upward on a sufficiently fine dyadic grid. The strict cost ordering and benchmark slack \(C<1\) survive sufficiently small rounding, while the inequalities \((1+v_j)F_j\ge nF_j-h\) are preserved.

All types can use the same rational verified datum in the given interval. For any prescribed \(B>0\), multiplying the constructed costs by \(B\) gives the identical normalized argument.

Since \(q<e/(e-1)\) was arbitrary, every allowed mechanism has \(\rho(M)\ge e/(e-1)\).

6. Running time and conclusion

The upper-bound mechanism needs only the distinct costs and their cumulative weighted masses.

Its linear program can be solved by examining the convex hull of the points \((F_i,(1+c_i)F_i)\), together with the origin. Maximizing the first coordinate subject to the second coordinate being at most \(1\) has an optimum at a feasible original point or at an intersection of a segment between two points with the line of second coordinate \(1\).

Enumerating all such pairs takes \(O(\tau^2)\) rational arithmetic operations after sorting. Every candidate uses rational numbers with bit length polynomial in \(L\). Thus preprocessing takes \(O(\tau^2\mathop{\mathrm{poly}}(L))\) bit time. An optimal menu uses at most two nonzero thresholds. No approximation parameter is needed; the mechanism is computed exactly, and \(m=1\).

Combining the bounds proves

\[ \inf_M\rho(M)=\frac{e}{e-1}<2. \]
