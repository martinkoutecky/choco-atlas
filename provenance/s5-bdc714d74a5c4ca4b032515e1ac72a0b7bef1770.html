The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational weight types \(t\) with weights \(w_t>0\), masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a quota density \(q\), a deletion budget \(\kappa\), and a tagged player of fixed weight \(b\), is there a deletion vector \(x\) with \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_t\le\kappa\) such that \(I_q(\mu-x)>I_q(\mu)\), where \(I_q(\nu)=\inf_{\alpha\in[0,1]^\tau,\,\sum_t\nu_t w_t\alpha_t=q}\sum_t\nu_t[\alpha_t\log(2\alpha_t)+(1-\alpha_t)\log(2(1-\alpha_t))]\), and the infimum is \(+\infty\) when the quota is unreachable? For a finite-precision decision version, require \(I_q(\mu-x)\ge I_q(\mu)+\varepsilon\) for a supplied rational gap \(\varepsilon>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and positive binary rational scalar weights \(w_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; the weight dimension is \(m=1\).
The remaining inputs are binary rationals: a quota \(q>0\), a deletion budget \(0<\kappa\le1\), a gap \(\varepsilon>0\), and a tagged player’s weight \(b>0\). The tagged player is outside the mass distribution and cannot be deleted; \(b\) does not enter the functional below.
For \(\nu\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\tau}\), define \(I_q(\nu)=\inf\{\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\nu_t[\alpha_t\log(2\alpha_t)+(1-\alpha_t)\log(2(1-\alpha_t))]:\alpha\in[0,1]^\tau,\ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\nu_tw_t\alpha_t=q\}\), using natural logarithms and \(0\log0=0\); an empty feasible set has value \(+\infty\).
Deletion chooses real quantities \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) with \(\sum_tx_t\le\kappa\). The quota stays fixed and surviving masses are not renormalized.
Decide whether \(I_q(\mu)<+\infty\) and some such deletion satisfies \(I_q(\mu-x)\ge I_q(\mu)+\varepsilon\). Unreachable post-deletion quotas satisfy this inequality; an unreachable initial quota gives a no-instance. The threshold comparison is exact.
Is this rational-gap deletion decision problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length, including the gap and budget.
For finite weighted voting games, deleting at most a positive budget of untagged players, keeping the quota fixed, to strictly decrease the tagged player’s probabilistic Penrose–Banzhaf index is \(\mathrm{NP}^{\mathrm{PP}}\)-complete ([discrete complexity result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13868)). 1. Reachability and duality

Define
$$ f(a)=a\log(2a)+(1-a)\log(2(1-a)), \qquad h(s)=\log\frac{1+e^s}{2}, $$
and, for a nonnegative mass vector $\nu$, put
$$ N(\nu)=\sum_t\nu_t, \qquad W(\nu)=\sum_t\nu_tw_t. $$

Because all weights are positive, the quota is reachable precisely when
$$ q\le W(\nu). $$
Necessity follows from $\alpha_t\le1$. For sufficiency, take every $\alpha_t=q/W(\nu)$; the denominator is positive because $q>0$.

Lemma 1. For every nonnegative $\nu$,
$$ I_q(\nu)=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb R} \left\{\lambda q-\sum_t\nu_th(\lambda w_t)\right\}, $$
including the value $+\infty$.

Proof. On $(0,1)$,
$$ f'(a)=\log\frac{a}{1-a}, \qquad f''(a)=\frac1a+\frac1{1-a}>0. $$
Consequently,
$$ f(a)\ge sa-h(s), $$
with equality at
$$ a=\frac{e^s}{1+e^s}. $$
The inequality extends to the endpoints by continuity. Multiplying by $\nu_t$, substituting $s=\lambda w_t$, and summing over a feasible $\alpha$ proves the lower bound on $I_q(\nu)$.

Suppose first that $0<q<W(\nu)$. Define
$$ g_\nu(\lambda)= \sum_t\nu_tw_t\frac{e^{\lambda w_t}}{1+e^{\lambda w_t}}. $$
This function has limits $0$ and $W(\nu)$ at the two infinities, and
$$ g_\nu'(\lambda)= \sum_t\nu_tw_t^2 \frac{e^{\lambda w_t}}{(1+e^{\lambda w_t})^2}>0. $$
Strict positivity holds because at least one coordinate has positive mass. Thus there is a unique $\lambda_\nu$ with $g_\nu(\lambda_\nu)=q$. The corresponding logistic choices of $\alpha_t$ are feasible and attain equality in the preceding inequality.

If $q=W(\nu)$, feasibility forces $\alpha_t=1$ on every positive-mass coordinate, so
$$ I_q(\nu)=N(\nu)\log2. $$
Moreover,
$$ \lambda q-\sum_t\nu_th(\lambda w_t) = N(\nu)\log2-\sum_t\nu_t\log(1+e^{-\lambda w_t}) $$
converges to that value as $\lambda\to+\infty$.

If $q>W(\nu)$, the same expression becomes
$$ \lambda(q-W(\nu))+N(\nu)\log2 -\sum_t\nu_t\log(1+e^{-\lambda w_t}), $$
which tends to $+\infty$. This also covers $\nu=0$. $\square$

For every reachable quota,
$$ 0\le I_q(\nu)\le N(\nu)\log2, $$
because $0\le f(a)\le\log2$.

2. The deletion optimization has an exact greedy solution

Order the types so that
$$ w_1\ge w_2\ge\cdots\ge w_\tau. $$
Define
$$ x_i^*= \min\left\{\mu_i,\max\left\{0,\kappa-\sum_{j<i}\mu_j\right\}\right\}, \qquad \nu^*=\mu-x^*. $$
This deletes the largest weights first and spends exactly $\kappa$. It is feasible because $\sum_i\mu_i=1$ and $\kappa\le1$.

Theorem 2.
$$ \max_{\substack{0\le x\le\mu\\ \sum_t x_t\le\kappa}} I_q(\mu-x) = \max\{I_q(\mu),I_q(\nu^*)\}. $$

Proof. Write
$$ F_\nu(\lambda)=\lambda q-\sum_t\nu_th(\lambda w_t). $$
For an admissible deletion,
$$ F_{\mu-x}(\lambda)=F_\mu(\lambda)+\sum_t x_th(\lambda w_t). $$

If $\lambda\le0$, then $h(\lambda w_t)\le0$, giving
$$ F_{\mu-x}(\lambda)\le F_\mu(\lambda)\le I_q(\mu). $$

If $\lambda\ge0$, the coefficients
$$ c_i=h(\lambda w_i) $$
satisfy $c_1\ge\cdots\ge c_\tau\ge0$. Let
$$ S_j(x)=\sum_{i\le j}x_i. $$
The capacity and budget constraints imply
$$ S_j(x)\le \min\left\{\kappa,\sum_{i\le j}\mu_i\right\} =S_j(x^*). $$
Summation by parts gives
$$ \sum_{i=1}^\tau c_ix_i = c_\tau S_\tau(x) +\sum_{j=1}^{\tau-1}(c_j-c_{j+1})S_j(x). $$
Every coefficient on the right is nonnegative. Hence
$$ \sum_i c_ix_i\le\sum_i c_ix_i^*, $$
and therefore
$$ F_{\mu-x}(\lambda)\le F_{\nu^*}(\lambda)\le I_q(\nu^*). $$

Taking the supremum over $\lambda$ proves the upper bound. Both $x=0$ and $x=x^*$ are admissible, so the bound is attained. $\square$

Since $\varepsilon>0$, after checking initial reachability the decision problem is therefore equivalent to
$$ I_q(\nu^*)-I_q(\mu)\ge\varepsilon. $$
The option of spending no budget is essential to the maximum formula.

Let $W=W(\mu)$ and $W^*=W(\nu^*)$. The following cases use only rational arithmetic:

  • If $q>W$, the answer is no, by the initial-reachability requirement.
  • If $q\le W$ and $q>W^*$, the answer is yes.
  • If $q\le W^*/2$, the answer is no: the optimizer for $\nu^*$ has $\lambda_{\nu^*}\le0$, so the negative-parameter inequality above gives $I_q(\nu^*)\le I_q(\mu)$.
  • If both rates are finite and $\varepsilon\ge1$, the answer is no, because their difference is at most $\log2<1$.

Sorting and constructing $\nu^*$ take
$$ O\!\left(\tau\log(\tau+1)\operatorname{poly}(L)\right) $$
bit operations. Only one surviving coordinate can acquire a new nonzero rational value. Its construction uses a prefix sum of the input masses, so the complete description of $\nu^*$ has length $O(L)$.

3. A further structural property: the difference increases with the quota

Hold $\mu,w,\kappa$ fixed, suppose $W^*>0$, and allow $q$ to vary in $(0,W^*)$. Define
$$ \Delta(q)=I_q(\nu^*)-I_q(\mu). $$

Proposition 3. The function $\Delta(q)$ is strictly increasing.

Proof. Because $\kappa>0$, some mass is deleted. Positivity of the weights and of the logistic function gives, for every finite $\lambda$,
$$ g_{\nu^*}(\lambda)<g_\mu(\lambda). $$
Both functions are strictly increasing. Their inverse values at the same quota consequently satisfy
$$ \lambda_{\nu^*}(q)>\lambda_\mu(q). $$

Their derivatives are positive, so the inverse function theorem makes these inverse values differentiable. Differentiating the attained dual formula, the term containing $\lambda_\nu'(q)$ vanishes because $g_\nu(\lambda_\nu(q))=q$. Thus
$$ \frac{d}{dq}I_q(\nu)=\lambda_\nu(q). $$
It follows that
$$ \Delta'(q)=\lambda_{\nu^*}(q)-\lambda_\mu(q)>0. $$
$\square$

This establishes monotonicity, but does not itself decide the exact rational-gap comparison.

4. Certified approximation in polynomial time

Proposition 4. Each finite rate for $\nu=\mu$ or $\nu=\nu^*$ can be approximated with absolute error at most $2^{-p}$ in
$$ O\!\left(\tau(L+p)^C\right) $$
bit operations, for an absolute constant $C$.

Here $p$ is the requested number of accuracy bits.

4.1. Bracketing and locating the optimizer

The endpoint $q=W(\nu)$ requires only an approximation to $N(\nu)\log2$. Suppose instead that $0<q<W(\nu)$.

All relevant rationals have binary length $O(L)$. Choose an integer $B=O(L)$ such that, with
$$ d=\min\{q,W(\nu)-q\}, $$
and $w_{\min}$ the smallest surviving weight,
$$ W(\nu)\le2^B, \qquad d\ge2^{-B}, \qquad w_{\min}\ge2^{-B}. $$
These bounds can be obtained from the rational descriptions.

For $T\ge0$,
$$ g_\nu(-T)\le W(\nu)e^{-Tw_{\min}}, \qquad W(\nu)-g_\nu(T)\le W(\nu)e^{-Tw_{\min}}. $$
Take
$$ T=2^B(2B+2). $$
Then both right-hand sides are less than $2^{-B}\le d$, so $\lambda_\nu\in(-T,T)$. The integer $T$ has $O(L)$ bits.

Put $\eta=2^{-p}$ and
$$ \delta=\frac{\eta}{32T}. $$
Bisect the bracket, evaluating
$$ F_\nu'(a)=q-g_\nu(a) $$
with certified error at most $\delta$.

If the computed derivative has magnitude at most $2\delta$, its true magnitude is at most $3\delta$. Concavity then gives
$$ 0\le F_\nu(\lambda_\nu)-F_\nu(a) \le |F_\nu'(a)|\,|\lambda_\nu-a| \le6T\delta<\frac{\eta}{4}. $$
Otherwise, the computed sign is correct and identifies the half containing the optimizer.

One may also stop when the bracket width is at most $\eta/(4W(\nu))$, because
$$ |F_\nu'(\lambda)|\le W(\nu). $$
There are $O(L+p)$ bisections.

4.2. Explicit elementary-function error bounds

Write
$$ \ell(s)=\frac{e^s}{1+e^s}, \qquad c(s)=\log(1+e^{-|s|}). $$
The stable identity
$$ h(s)=\max\{s,0\}+c(s)-\log2 $$
avoids evaluating a large positive exponential.

Here is a certified procedure for any tolerance $0<\rho<1$. Set
$$ K=\left\lceil\log_2\frac4\rho\right\rceil. $$
If $|s|\ge K$, then
$$ e^{-|s|}\le2^{-K}\le\frac{\rho}{4}. $$
Thus replacing $c(s)$ by zero and $\ell(s)$ by its appropriate limit, zero or one, incurs error at most $\rho/4$.

If $|s|<K$, evaluate $E=e^{-|s|}$ by its Taylor polynomial. For $m$ terms, the remainder is at most
$$ e^K\frac{K^m}{m!}. $$
Let
$$ r=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{16}{\rho}\right\rceil, \qquad m\ge16(K+r+1). $$
Using $m!\ge(m/e)^m$ and $e<4$, the remainder is bounded by
$$ e^K\left(\frac{eK}{m}\right)^m \le4^{K-m} \le2^{-r} \le\frac{\rho}{16}. $$
Clamp the rational approximation to $[0,1]$, which cannot increase its error.

The maps
$$ E\longmapsto\frac1{1+E}, \qquad E\longmapsto\frac{E}{1+E} $$
have derivative magnitude at most one on $[0,1]$, so this also certifies the logistic approximation.

For the logarithm, put $y=E/(2+E)\in[0,1/3]$ and use
$$ \log(1+E) = 2\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{y^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_J. $$
The tail satisfies
$$ |R_J| \le \frac{2y^{2J+1}}{(2J+1)(1-y^2)} \le\frac94\,3^{-(2J+1)}. $$
Taking $J=r+2$ makes this less than $\rho/16$. The logarithm's derivative on $[1,2]$ is at most one, so the preceding error in $E$ contributes at most another $\rho/16$. The same series at $y=1/3$ computes $\log2$.

All these calculations use polynomially many exact rational operations on numbers of polynomial binary length in the argument length and $\log(1/\rho)$.

For the derivative evaluation, approximate each logistic value with error at most
$$ \frac{\delta}{2W(\nu)}. $$
The accumulated error is at most $\delta/2$, since the coefficients sum to $W(\nu)$.

For the objective evaluation, approximate each $c(\lambda w_t)$ and $\log2$ with error at most $\eta/16$. Since $N(\nu)\le1$, their accumulated contribution is at most $\eta/8$. The remaining terms are rational and can be computed exactly.

Combining objective evaluation error with the optimizer-location error proves Proposition 4.

5. Algebraic degree and height bounds

The following bounds concern mathematical representations; the approximation algorithm need not construct them.

Proposition 5. For either rational mass vector $\nu=\mu$ or $\nu=\nu^*$ with reachable quota, the number
$$ E_\nu=e^{I_q(\nu)} $$
is positive algebraic and satisfies
$$ \deg E_\nu\le2^{O(L)}, \qquad h_{\mathrm W}(E_\nu)\le2^{O(L)}, $$
where $h_{\mathrm W}$ denotes absolute logarithmic Weil height.

5.1. Height facts used below

For a nonzero algebraic number $a$ of degree $d$, with primitive integer minimal polynomial
$$ P(X)=a_d\prod_{j=1}^d(X-a_j), $$
the height is
$$ h_{\mathrm W}(a) = \frac1d \left(\log|a_d|+\sum_{j=1}^d\log\max\{1,|a_j|\}\right). $$

We use
$$ h_{\mathrm W}(ab)\le h_{\mathrm W}(a)+h_{\mathrm W}(b), $$
$$ h_{\mathrm W}(a+b)\le h_{\mathrm W}(a)+h_{\mathrm W}(b)+\log2, $$
$$ h_{\mathrm W}(a^{-1})=h_{\mathrm W}(a), \qquad h_{\mathrm W}(a^n)=|n|h_{\mathrm W}(a). $$
These follow from the equivalent local-height formula
$$ h_{\mathrm W}(a) = \frac1{[K:\mathbb Q]} \sum_v n_v\log\max\{1,|a|_v\}, $$
in any number field containing the numbers in question. The product inequality follows pointwise; addition uses the triangle inequality at archimedean places and the ultrametric inequality elsewhere. The inverse identity follows from the product formula, and the integer-power identity follows pointwise.

In particular, if $\beta^v=a^u$ with $v>0$, then
$$ v h_{\mathrm W}(\beta)=|u|h_{\mathrm W}(a). $$
This proves the rational-power height identity used here.

5.2. An algebraic optimizer

Suppose $0<q<W(\nu)$. Choose an integer $d_0$ clearing the weight denominators and put
$$ a_t=d_0w_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad z=e^{\lambda_\nu/d_0}>0. $$
Stationarity becomes
$$ \sum_t\nu_tw_t\frac{z^{a_t}}{1+z^{a_t}}=q. $$
Hence $z$ is a root of the rational polynomial
$$ P_\nu(Z)= \sum_t\nu_tw_tZ^{a_t} \prod_{j\ne t}(1+Z^{a_j}) -q\prod_j(1+Z^{a_j}). $$
Its constant coefficient is $-q$, so it is nonzero.

Clearing coefficient denominators gives a nonzero integer polynomial with
$$ \deg P_\nu\le2^{O(L)}, \qquad \log\|P_\nu\|_1=O(L), $$
where $\|\cdot\|_1$ is the sum of absolute coefficient values. Indeed, $d_0$ and all $a_t$ have $O(L)$ bits, the degree is at most $\sum_ta_t$, and each product has coefficient sum at most $2^\tau$. Clearing rational denominators costs a factor $2^{O(L)}$.

Thus
$$ \deg z\le2^{O(L)}, \qquad h_{\mathrm W}(z)=O(L). $$
For the height bound, the primitive minimal polynomial of $z$ divides the cleared integer polynomial. Mahler measure is multiplicative, is at least one for every nonzero integer polynomial, and satisfies
$$ M(P)\le\|P\|_1. $$
The last inequality follows by integrating $\log|P|$ on the unit circle and using the coefficient-sum bound there. The minimal-polynomial formula for height now gives the claim.

5.3. The exponential rate

The attained dual formula gives
$$ E_\nu = z^{d_0q} \prod_t\left(\frac2{1+z^{a_t}}\right)^{\nu_t}. $$
All bases are positive algebraic, and all exponents are rational. Rational powers of algebraic numbers are algebraic.

Choose an integer $R\le2^{O(L)}$ clearing the denominators of $d_0q$ and the masses $\nu_t$. Then
$$ E_\nu^R = z^{Rd_0q} \prod_t\left(\frac2{1+z^{a_t}}\right)^{R\nu_t} \in\mathbb Q(z). $$
All integer exponents in this expression are at most $2^{O(L)}$. Therefore
$$ \deg E_\nu\le R\deg z\le2^{O(L)}. $$

Also,
$$ h_{\mathrm W}\left(\frac2{1+z^{a_t}}\right) \le a_t h_{\mathrm W}(z)+2\log2. $$
Applying the height inequalities to the displayed expression for $E_\nu^R$ gives
$$ h_{\mathrm W}(E_\nu^R)\le2^{O(L)}. $$
The integer-power identity then bounds $h_{\mathrm W}(E_\nu)$ as required.

At the endpoint $q=W(\nu)$,
$$ E_\nu=2^{N(\nu)}. $$
Its degree is bounded by the denominator of $N(\nu)$, and its height is $N(\nu)\log2$. These satisfy the same bounds. $\square$

Consequently, when both rates are finite,
$$ A=e^\Delta, \qquad \Delta=I_q(\nu^*)-I_q(\mu), $$
is positive algebraic with
$$ \deg A\le2^{O(L)}, \qquad h_{\mathrm W}(A)\le2^{O(L)}. $$
The degree bound follows by placing the two exponential rates in their compositum; the height bound follows from the product and inverse inequalities.

6. New separation bound via the fixed number $e$

The essential improvement is to compare with $e$, rather than directly with $e^\varepsilon$.

We use the following established theorem.

Cijsouw's transcendence estimate, specialized to $e$. There is an effectively computable absolute constant $K_0>0$ such that, for every nonconstant integer polynomial $P$ of degree at most $D$ and maximum absolute coefficient at most $H$,
$$ |P(e)|> \exp\left(-K_0D^2(D+\log H)\right). $$
This is Theorem 1 with algebraic argument $1$ in [Cijsouw, *Transcendence measures of exponentials and logarithms of algebraic numbers*](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1974__28_2_163_0.pdf).

Theorem 6. Whenever both rates are finite,
$$ |\Delta-\varepsilon|\ge\exp\left(-2^{O(L)}\right). $$
In particular, $\Delta\ne\varepsilon$.

Proof. Write the positive rational gap in lowest terms as $\varepsilon=u/v$. Define
$$ \beta=A^{v/u}=\exp(\Delta/\varepsilon). $$
Because $\beta^u=A^v$,
$$ \deg\beta\le u\deg A\le2^{O(L)}, $$
and the rational-power height identity gives
$$ h_{\mathrm W}(\beta) =\frac vu h_{\mathrm W}(A) \le2^{O(L)}. $$
Here $u,v\le2^L$.

Let $P$ be the primitive integer minimal polynomial of $\beta$, with degree $D$ and maximum coefficient $H$. Its coefficients are elementary symmetric functions of its conjugates, so
$$ H\le2^D\exp(Dh_{\mathrm W}(\beta)). $$
Indeed, each coefficient is bounded by a binomial coefficient times
$$ |a_D|\prod_j\max\{1,|\beta_j|\}. $$
Thus
$$ D\le2^{O(L)}, \qquad \log H\le2^{O(L)}. $$
All hypotheses of Cijsouw's estimate hold: $P$ is nonconstant, nonzero, and has integer coefficients.

If $|\Delta-\varepsilon|\ge\varepsilon$, the desired bound follows immediately from $\varepsilon\ge2^{-L}$.

Otherwise,
$$ 0<\frac{\Delta}{\varepsilon}<2, \qquad 1<\beta<e^2. $$
On the interval between $\beta$ and $e$,
$$ |P'(y)|\le HD^2e^{2D}. $$
Since $P(\beta)=0$, the mean value theorem and Cijsouw's estimate imply
$$ |e-\beta| > \exp\left( -K_0D^2(D+\log H) -\log H-2\log D-2D \right). $$
A second application of the mean value theorem, now to $s\mapsto e^{s/\varepsilon}$, gives
$$ |e-\beta| \le\frac{e^2}{\varepsilon}|\varepsilon-\Delta|. $$
Combining these inequalities,
$$ |\varepsilon-\Delta| > \varepsilon e^{-2} \exp\left( -K_0D^2(D+\log H) -\log H-2\log D-2D \right). $$
The established bounds on $D,\log H$, and $\varepsilon$ make the negative logarithm of this lower bound at most $2^{O(L)}$. This proves the theorem. $\square$

The fixed argument $1$ is crucial: the constant in the transcendence estimate is absolute, independent of the input gap.
