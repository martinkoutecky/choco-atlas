The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an online stream of rational-mass trader cohorts with types \(t=(\sigma,v,a,d)\), where each cohort is revealed only upon arrival, sellers remain active throughout the buyer horizon, and total buyer mass is at most total seller mass, does there exist a deterministic direct mechanism running in time polynomial in the support size and input bit length that is feasible, individually rational, truthful for each representative trader, and guarantees welfare at least \(\operatorname{OPT}/2\) for every admissible stream? The mechanism may split a cohort by assigning a measurable subset of its members to trade; each individual receives either a whole unit or none, and receives a specified transfer.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There is one commodity, so \(m=1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each cohort has type \(t=(\sigma,v,a,d)\), with public role \(\sigma\in\{B,S\}\), fixed public mass \(q_t>0\), and private binary-rational values \(v>0\) and \(0\le a\le d\). Masses need not sum to one. Each seller initially owns one unit per member; each buyer demands at most one unit.
A public rational window \([A,H]\) contains every buyer arrival. All sellers arrive before \(A\) and remain active through \(H\); total buyer mass is at most total seller mass. Cohorts are revealed only upon arrival, in adversarial order, without advance knowledge of buyer types, support size, or total buyer mass.
Represent cohort \(t\) by an interval of length \(q_t\) with Lebesgue measure. A deterministic direct mechanism may select measurable subsets, but each member trades either one whole unit or none. Its output must finitely describe these subsets and each member's transfer; ties use fixed public orders independent of reports.
Trades are irrevocable, pair equal seller and buyer mass during both reported active intervals, and require the reported seller value to be at most the reported buyer value. Transfers are nonnegative buyer payments or seller receipts made during reported activity; budget balance is not required.
A representative reports one common type for its cohort and maximizes its members' integrated utility. Permitted deviations keep role and mass fixed, report any positive rational value, and shorten activity to \([a',d']\subseteq[a,d]\); seller reports not covering \([A,H]\) are ineligible. Member utilities are \(v\pi-x\) for buyers and \(x-v\pi\) for sellers, where \(\pi\in\{0,1\}\). Require dominant-strategy truthfulness for every representative and individual rationality for every member under truthful reporting.
Writing \(z_t\) for traded mass, welfare is \(W=\sum_{t:\sigma=B}v_tz_t+\sum_{t:\sigma=S}v_t(q_t-z_t)\). Let \(\operatorname{OPT}\) be maximum welfare over offline feasible trades subject to the same activity and value constraints.

Does there exist a deterministic online direct mechanism satisfying these conditions, running in total time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), that guarantees \(W\ge\operatorname{OPT}/2\) for every admissible stream, or is this guarantee unattainable by such a mechanism?

For finite unit traders with patient sellers and demand at most supply, a deterministic truthful, individually rational greedy mechanism guarantees welfare at least \(\operatorname{OPT}/2\), allowing a deficit. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.0198)
No result settling this exact rational-mass representative-cohort version was located in the literature search.

Result

Such a mechanism exists. Cheapest-first allocation, combined with payments based on each seller cohort’s total traded mass, satisfies all the requirements and runs in polynomial time.

The allocation follows the greedy rule in [Zhao, Zhang, and Perrussel, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.0198). The representative-cohort payment construction and all arguments needed for the rational-mass setting are proved below.

Write \(S\) and \(B\) for the seller and buyer cohorts. Denote seller \(i\)’s reported value by \(c_i\), buyer \(j\)’s reported value by \(b_j\), and their public masses by \(q_i,q_j\). Cohort identities and the stipulated public tie orders remain fixed when reports change.

1. Allocation and transfers

Close seller registration at \(A\). A seller is eligible if it is revealed strictly before \(A\) and its reported interval covers \([A,H]\). Other sellers receive no allocation or transfer. Every truthful seller is eligible. The strict registration deadline also specifies what happens when a seller delays its report to \(A\).

Sort eligible seller cohorts by increasing reported value, using the fixed public order to break ties. Within each seller interval, use increasing coordinate order.

Process each buyer once, immediately upon its reported arrival in \([A,H]\). Give it the cheapest remaining seller mass, up to its mass \(q_j\), stopping when either:

  • its entire mass has been served;
  • no supply remains; or
  • the cheapest remaining seller value exceeds \(b_j\).

Buyers arriving after \(H\) receive no allocation or transfer. Their reported departures otherwise play no role.

For every matched piece of mass, pair equally long seller and buyer subintervals by translation. Buyer members matched to seller \(i\) pay \(c_i\) immediately. Unmatched buyer members pay zero.

Seller receipts are computed and paid at \(H\). Fix all reports except seller \(i\)’s value, and let \(Z_i(u)\) be its total traded mass when its reported value is replaced by \(u>0\), with its eligibility unchanged. Define its total receipt by

\(P_i(c_i)=c_iZ_i(c_i)+\int_{c_i}^{\infty}Z_i(u)\,du.\)

The integral is finite: \(Z_i(u)=0\) whenever \(u\) exceeds every reported buyer value.

If \(Z_i(c_i)>0\), each traded member of seller \(i\) receives \(P_i(c_i)/Z_i(c_i)\); unmatched members receive zero. If \(Z_i(c_i)=0\), every member receives zero. The next section proves that this agrees with the formula for the total receipt.

All trades occur during both reported active intervals. Buyer payments occur at arrival, and eligible sellers remain active at \(H\). Thus transfer timing is feasible.

2. Seller traded mass is monotone

We prove that \(Z_i(u)\) is nonincreasing in \(u\). The proof does not require demand to be at most supply.

For a fixed seller report profile, let

\(K(b)=\sum_{k\in S:\,c_k\le b}q_k,\)

where the sum includes only eligible sellers.

Greedy always consumes a prefix of the sorted seller supply. If \(r\) mass has already been consumed when a buyer of value \(b\) and mass \(q\) arrives, the remaining affordable supply has mass \(\max\{K(b)-r,0\}\). Consequently, the consumed mass after processing that buyer is

\(F_q(r,K(b))=r+\min\{q,\max\{K(b)-r,0\}\}=\max\{r,\min\{r+q,K(b)\}\}.\)

The last expression shows explicitly that \(F_q\) is nondecreasing in each argument.

Now lower seller \(i\)’s report from \(u_2\) to \(u_1<u_2\). For every buyer value \(b\), the corresponding cumulative affordable supply satisfies

\(K_{u_1}(b)\ge K_{u_2}(b).\)

Starting from consumed mass zero and applying the monotonicity of \(F_q\) successively to the fixed buyer sequence proves that total consumed mass satisfies

\(R(u_1)\ge R(u_2).\)

Let \(D_i(u)\) be the seller mass preceding cohort \(i\) in the sorted supply when its report is \(u\). Lowering its report cannot increase its preceding mass, so

\(D_i(u_1)\le D_i(u_2).\)

Because consumed supply is a prefix,

\(Z_i(u)=\min\{q_i,\max\{R(u)-D_i(u),0\}\}.\)

Both inequalities therefore imply \(Z_i(u_1)\ge Z_i(u_2)\). Fixed public tie orders ensure that the argument also holds at equality breakpoints.

In particular, if \(Z_i(c_i)=0\), then \(Z_i(u)=0\) for every \(u>c_i\), and hence \(P_i(c_i)=0\).

3. Seller truthfulness and individual rationality

Fix an eligible seller’s true value \(v>0\), and hold all other reports fixed. Reporting value \(c\) gives its representative integrated utility

\(U(v;c)=P_i(c)-vZ_i(c)=(c-v)Z_i(c)+\int_c^\infty Z_i(u)\,du.\)

Truthful reporting gives

\(U(v;v)=\int_v^\infty Z_i(u)\,du\ge0.\)

If \(c>v\), then

\(U(v;v)-U(v;c)=\int_v^c\bigl(Z_i(u)-Z_i(c)\bigr)\,du\ge0,\)

by monotonicity. If \(c<v\), then

\(U(v;v)-U(v;c)=\int_c^v\bigl(Z_i(c)-Z_i(u)\bigr)\,du\ge0.\)

Thus truthful value reporting maximizes the representative’s integrated utility.

Under truthful reporting, every traded member receives at least its value, because

\(P_i(v)=vZ_i(v)+\int_v^\infty Z_i(u)\,du\ge vZ_i(v).\)

Unmatched members receive zero and incur no loss. This proves individual rationality for every seller member.

For activity reports, every permitted shortening that remains eligible leaves the allocation and payment functions unchanged: all eligible sellers are collected before any buyer is processed, and seller timing otherwise plays no role. A shortening that makes the seller ineligible gives utility zero. Since truthful utility is nonnegative, no combined value-and-activity deviation improves utility.

4. Buyer truthfulness and individual rationality

Fix the other reports and a buyer’s arrival position. Let \(Q\) be the remaining supply mass, and let \(C(x)\), for \(0\le x<Q\), be its nondecreasing seller-value curve.

For a buyer of true value \(v\) and public mass \(q\), any value report selects a prefix of this remaining supply. Its integrated utility is the integral of \(v-C(x)\) over that selected prefix.

The maximum attainable utility is

\(\int_0^{\min\{q,Q\}}\max\{v-C(x),0\}\,dx.\)

Indeed, \(C\) is nondecreasing, so all positive-surplus positions precede all negative-surplus positions. Reporting \(v\) selects precisely the affordable prefix, subject to the mass bound, and therefore achieves this maximum. Positions with \(C(x)=v\) contribute zero.

Every traded buyer member pays a seller value at most its truthful value. Unmatched members pay zero. Hence buyer individual rationality holds member by member.

It remains to check delayed arrival. Compare truthful arrival with a permitted later arrival, holding other reports fixed. Before the original arrival position, the two histories coincide. In the delayed-report history, the intervening buyers only remove supply; no eligible seller is added.

Therefore the later remaining supply is a subset of the earlier remaining supply. Its available mass is no larger, and its sorted value curve is pointwise no lower wherever defined. To verify the latter statement, for every threshold \(h\), the later supply contains no more mass of value at most \(h\) than the earlier supply; consequently its increasing quantiles cannot decrease.

It follows that the maximum utility integral at the later arrival is no greater than the corresponding integral at the original arrival. Any value report at that later arrival yields utility at most that later maximum.

Shortening departure without delaying arrival changes nothing. Delaying arrival beyond \(H\) gives utility zero. Thus truthful reporting dominates every permitted combined value-and-activity deviation.

5. Welfare guarantee

Consider a truthful admissible stream. Define the total initial seller value and total buyer value by

\(V_S=\sum_{i\in S}v_iq_i,\qquad V_B=\sum_{j\in B}v_jq_j.\)

Let \(W_G\) be the mechanism’s welfare.

First, every trade replaces a seller-held unit by a buyer-held unit of at least the same value. Consequently,

\(W_G=V_S+\sum_{\text{matched pieces }(i,j)}(v_j-v_i)\,\text{mass}(i,j)\ge V_S.\)

We next prove \(W_G\ge V_B\).

Let \(r\) be total traded mass, and write

\(U=\sum_{j\in B}q_j-r,\qquad T=\sum_{i\in S}q_i-r\)

for unserved buyer mass and retained seller mass. The demand assumption gives \(T\ge U\).

If \(U=0\), the inequality \(W_G\ge V_B\) is immediate. Suppose \(U>0\). Then \(T>0\).

Every unserved buyer has value strictly below every finally retained seller. To prove this, fix a buyer with positive unserved mass and a finally retained seller piece. That seller piece was available throughout the buyer’s processing. If its value had been at most the buyer’s value, greedy could not have stopped with buyer mass unserved while leaving that piece available. Therefore the seller’s value is strictly greater.

Let \(b_{\max}\) be the largest value among cohorts with positive unserved mass, and \(c_{\min}\) the smallest value among cohorts with positive retained mass. These exist because the support is finite, and the preceding argument gives \(c_{\min}>b_{\max}\). Hence

\(\sum_{i\in S}v_i(q_i-z_i)\ge c_{\min}T\ge b_{\max}U\ge\sum_{j\in B}v_j(q_j-z_j).\)

Adding the traded buyer value proves

\(W_G\ge\sum_{j\in B}v_jz_j+\sum_{j\in B}v_j(q_j-z_j)=V_B.\)

Finally, every offline feasible allocation satisfies

\(\operatorname{OPT}\le V_S+V_B,\)

because its retained seller value is at most \(V_S\), and its traded buyer value is at most \(V_B\). Combining the bounds gives

\(W_G\ge\max\{V_S,V_B\}\ge\frac{V_S+V_B}{2}\ge\frac{\operatorname{OPT}}2.\)

6. Exact computation and finite output

The mechanism works directly with rational masses; it does not expand cohorts into unit traders.

For seller \(i\), consider the breakpoint set

\(\mathcal D_i=\{c_k:k\ne i,\ k\text{ eligible}\}\cup\{b_j:j\in B\}.\)

On each open interval between consecutive breakpoints:

  • seller \(i\)’s position in the sorted supply is unchanged;
  • whether its value is affordable to each buyer is unchanged;
  • all remaining comparisons in the greedy algorithm are unchanged.

Thus \(Z_i(u)\) is constant on each such interval. There are at most \(\tau\) breakpoints. Its value on an interval can be computed by replaying greedy at a rational midpoint. Isolated breakpoint values do not affect the integral; the actual allocation \(Z_i(c_i)\) is already recorded.

Accordingly, \(P_i(c_i)\) is computed exactly by summing interval lengths times their constant allocation values. Values above the largest buyer value contribute zero. If there are no buyers, all receipts are zero.

For each seller, at most \(O(\tau)\) simulations suffice. After sorting sellers, each simulation processes the sorted supply and buyer sequence with \(O(\tau)\) rational operations: each successful matching step exhausts either the current seller piece or the current buyer’s remaining demand. Thus all receipts can be computed using \(O(\tau^3\log(\tau+1))\) arithmetic operations.

The bit lengths also remain polynomial in \(L\). All allocated masses are sums or differences of input masses, so their denominators divide the product of the input mass denominators. Integral terms multiply these quantities by differences of input values. Those denominator products have bit length \(O(L)\), and their numerators have polynomial bit length as well. Midpoint construction and division by positive traded mass preserve polynomial bit length. Exact comparisons and arithmetic therefore take polynomial time.

Finally, describe each trade by its seller and buyer subinterval endpoints and the translation pairing them. Each buyer piece carries its rational per-member payment. Each seller’s traded prefix carries its rational per-member receipt; all complementary intervals carry zero transfers. Greedy creates only polynomially many pieces, so this output is finite and has polynomial length.

The mechanism stores arrivals incrementally and computes seller receipts when the public window closes. It needs no advance knowledge of buyer types, support size, or total buyer mass.

1. Allocation and feasibility

Closing seller registration before processing buyers ensures that every eligible seller is available throughout the public window. Under truthful reporting, every seller satisfies the registration and interval conditions.

Cheapest-first allocation preserves the requirement that a traded seller’s reported value is at most the matched buyer’s reported value. Matching equally long intervals by translation pairs equal masses and assigns each selected member one whole unit. Partial cohort allocation is therefore consistent with the prohibition on fractional member trades.

Buyer payments occur at reported arrival, and eligible seller receipts occur at the covered time \(H\). Both transfer times are feasible, including for buyers arriving at \(H\). Nonnegative payments and receipts follow from positivity of seller reports and nonnegativity of traded mass.

Minor issue: The mechanism specifies increasing coordinate order within seller intervals but does not specify the corresponding selection rule within buyer intervals. Aggregate buyer allocation alone does not identify the measurable subset of buyer members trading.

2. Seller allocation monotonicity

The prefix invariant is valid: each buyer consumes the cheapest remaining affordable supply, so total consumed supply remains a prefix of the fixed sorted seller list.

Consequently, the remaining affordable mass is exactly \(\max\{K(b)-r,0\}\), even when previously consumed supply includes sellers more expensive than the current buyer’s value.

The identity
\(F_q(r,K)=\max\{r,\min\{r+q,K\}\}\)
is correct. This expression is nondecreasing in both arguments.

Lowering one seller’s report weakly increases every affordability total \(K(b)\). Applying the update successively to the unchanged buyer sequence therefore establishes \(R(u_1)\ge R(u_2)\).

Lowering the report also weakly decreases preceding seller mass \(D_i(u)\). The formula
\(Z_i(u)=\min\{q_i,\max\{R(u)-D_i(u),0\}\}\)
then establishes the required monotonicity. Fixed public tie orders preserve these comparisons at equality.

The inference that \(Z_i(c_i)=0\) implies zero integral above \(c_i\) is valid.

The bound \(P_i(v)\ge vZ_i(v)\), together with uniform receipt among traded members, establishes individual rationality for every seller member. The zero-allocation case is separately handled.

Eligible activity shortenings leave the seller list, buyer processing, and payment function unchanged. Ineligible shortenings yield zero utility, which cannot improve upon truthful utility. This also covers combined value and activity deviations.

4. Buyer truthfulness and individual rationality

The delayed-arrival comparison is also valid. Before the original arrival position, the histories coincide. In the delayed history, intervening buyers can only remove supply from that earlier remaining set. Threshold counts therefore weakly decrease, and increasing supply quantiles weakly increase. Together with reduced available mass, this makes the later maximum utility integral no larger.

The argument covers combined delayed-arrival and value deviations. Shortening departure alone has no effect, and arrival beyond \(H\) yields zero utility.

The empty-buyer and zero-unserved-mass cases are covered. No integrality assumption is used.
