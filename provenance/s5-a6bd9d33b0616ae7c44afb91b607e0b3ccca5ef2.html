The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite sets of surplus schools \(S\), deficit schools \(D\), and teacher types \(T\), each type \(t\) has mass \(\mu_t\), origin \(o(t)\), and acceptable destinations \(A_t\subseteq D\). Given per-capita release capacities \(\alpha_s\) and initial deficits \(\beta_d\), choose transfer masses \(x_{t,d}\geq 0\) for \(d\in A_t\), subject to \(\sum_{d\in A_t}x_{t,d}\leq\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t:o(t)=s}\sum_{d\in A_t}x_{t,d}\leq\alpha_s\), and \(\sum_t x_{t,d}\leq\beta_d\). For residual deficits \(h_d=\beta_d-\sum_t x_{t,d}\), compute a feasible transfer whose residual vector Lorenz-dominates every other feasible residual vector.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) and \(D\) be disjoint nonempty sets of surplus and deficit schools, with \(m=|S|+|D|\). A teacher type is a pair \(t=(o(t),A_t)\), where \(o(t)\in S\) and \(\varnothing\ne A_t\subseteq D\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Release capacities \(\alpha_s\) and initial deficits \(\beta_d\) are strictly positive binary rationals, measured in the same mass units as the society.
A feasible transfer consists of real masses \(x_{t,d}\ge0\) for \(d\in A_t\), satisfying \(\sum_{d\in A_t}x_{t,d}\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t:o(t)=s}\sum_{d\in A_t}x_{t,d}\le\alpha_s\), and \(\sum_{t:d\in A_t}x_{t,d}\le\beta_d\). Untransferred mass stays at its origin.
Its residual deficits are \(h_d=\beta_d-\sum_{t:d\in A_t}x_{t,d}\). Write \(h_{[1]}\ge\cdots\ge h_{[|D|]}\); \(h\) Lorenz-dominates \(h'\) when \(\sum_{i=1}^k h_{[i]}\le\sum_{i=1}^k h'_{[i]}\) for every \(1\le k\le|D|\), without requiring equal totals.

Can a feasible transfer whose residual vector Lorenz-dominates every feasible residual vector be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computation \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For individual teachers and integer capacities, a Lorenz-dominant integral transfer exists and is computable in polynomial time; the fractional model also has a constructive Lorenz-dominance result. [Mishra et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18708v2)
For real-capacity multi-terminal networks, lexicographically optimal flows are computable using polynomially many arithmetic operations. [Megiddo](https://theory.stanford.edu/~megiddo/pdf/lexflow.pdf)

  • Require nonempty acceptable sets and strictly positive rational capacities and deficits, preserving the basic discrete model's nondegeneracy conditions.
  • Use disjoint nonempty surplus and deficit school sets, and identify types by origin and acceptable set.
  • Specify Lorenz dominance using descending residual deficits and weak prefix-sum inequalities, including comparison of unequal totals.

Proven conclusion. A Lorenz-dominant feasible transfer can be computed exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The proof combines the fractional existence theorem with one polynomial-size rational linear program.

1. Existence for the support representation. We use the following established result: for finitely many individual unit teachers, with nonempty acceptable sets and positive integer release capacities and deficits, the fractional feasible-transfer model admits a residual vector that Lorenz-dominates every fractional feasible residual vector. This is the fractional MDR result in [Mishra et al., Section 3.4 and Appendix A, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18708v2#A1).

We verify its applicability to the present model. Choose a positive integer \(Q\) divisible by the denominators of all input masses and capacities. For every supported type \(t\), let \(N_t=Q\mu_t\), a positive integer. Replace this type, for purposes of the existence argument, by \(N_t\) individual unit teachers with the same origin and acceptable set. Set the release capacities and initial deficits to \(Q\alpha_s\) and \(Q\beta_d\), respectively. All required capacities are now positive integers.

The fractional feasible sets of the two models correspond exactly after aggregation and scaling:

  • Given an original feasible transfer \(x\), assign each of the \(N_t\) copies of type \(t\) the fractional transfer \(y_{t,j,d}=Qx_{t,d}/N_t\). Each copy transfers at most one unit, because \(\sum_{d\in A_t}y_{t,j,d}\le Q\mu_t/N_t=1\). The school constraints follow by multiplying the original constraints by \(Q\).
  • Conversely, given a feasible fractional transfer of the copies, define \(x_{t,d}=Q^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N_t}y_{t,j,d}\). Summing the copy constraints gives exactly the original feasibility constraints.

The expanded residual vector is \(Qh\). Since multiplication by a positive scalar preserves every Lorenz inequality, the cited existence theorem implies that the original model has a feasible residual vector \(h^\circ\) satisfying

\(\sum_{i=1}^k h^\circ_{[i]}\le \sum_{i=1}^k h_{[i]}\)

for every feasible residual vector \(h\) and every \(k\in\{1,\ldots,|D|\}\).

This expansion is only an existence argument; the algorithm below operates directly on the listed types.

2. A compact formula for each Lorenz sum. Write \(n=|D|\), \(B=\max_{d\in D}\beta_d\), and \(T_k(h)=\sum_{i=1}^k h_{[i]}\). Every feasible residual satisfies \(0\le h_d\le B\).

For every such vector and every \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\),

\(T_k(h)=\min\left\{k\lambda+\sum_{d\in D}z_d:\ 0\le\lambda\le B,\ 0\le z_d\le B,\ z_d\ge h_d-\lambda\ \text{for all }d\in D\right\}.\)

Here is a direct proof. Let \(J\) be a set of indices of \(k\) largest coordinates of \(h\). Any feasible auxiliary variables satisfy

\(k\lambda+\sum_{d\in D}z_d\ge k\lambda+\sum_{d\in J}z_d\ge k\lambda+\sum_{d\in J}(h_d-\lambda)=T_k(h).\)

For equality, choose \(\lambda=h_{[k]}\) and \(z_d=\max\{h_d-\lambda,0\}\). These variables respect their bounds. Every coordinate outside \(J\) contributes zero, and the coordinates inside \(J\) contribute \(\sum_{d\in J}(h_d-\lambda)\), including zero contributions from ties. Thus the objective equals \(T_k(h)\).

3. The linear program and its correctness. Retain the transfer variables \(x_{t,d}\) only for listed acceptable pairs. Introduce residual variables \(h_d\), and, for each \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), auxiliary variables \(\lambda_k\) and \(z_{k,d}\).

Solve the following linear program:

\(\displaystyle \min\ \sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(k\lambda_k+\sum_{d\in D}z_{k,d}\right).\)

Impose all the original transfer feasibility constraints, together with

\(\displaystyle h_d=\beta_d-\sum_{t:\,d\in A_t}x_{t,d},\qquad 0\le h_d\le\beta_d\quad(d\in D),\)

and

\(\displaystyle 0\le\lambda_k\le B,\qquad 0\le z_{k,d}\le B,\qquad z_{k,d}\ge h_d-\lambda_k\quad(k\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\ d\in D).\)

For each fixed feasible transfer, Section 2 shows that the minimum over its auxiliary variables equals \(F(h)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}T_k(h)\). Consequently, the transfer component of any optimal LP solution minimizes \(F\) over all feasible transfers.

Let \(\widehat h\) be the residual vector returned by an optimal solution. The existence result gives

\(T_k(h^\circ)\le T_k(\widehat h)\quad\text{for every }k.\)

Optimality also gives \(F(\widehat h)\le F(h^\circ)\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle 0\le\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigl(T_k(\widehat h)-T_k(h^\circ)\bigr)=F(\widehat h)-F(h^\circ)\le0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative, so every summand is zero. For any feasible residual vector \(h\), it follows that

\(T_k(\widehat h)=T_k(h^\circ)\le T_k(h)\quad\text{for every }k.\)

Thus the LP returns a feasible transfer whose residual vector Lorenz-dominates every feasible residual vector. The argument includes \(k=n\), so it also covers comparisons with unequal total residual deficits.

4. Exact polynomial bit complexity. Let \(q=\sum_t|A_t|\), so \(q\le\tau n\). The LP has \(q+n+n+n^2\) variables and a polynomial number of constraints. Its coefficients consist of input rationals, \(B\), and integers of magnitude at most \(n\). Its complete binary encoding length is therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The feasible polytope is nonempty: take \(x=0\), \(h=\beta\), \(\lambda_k=B\), and \(z_{k,d}=0\). It is bounded: each transfer variable is at most its type mass, and all remaining variables have explicit finite bounds.

The standard exact rational linear-programming theorem therefore applies: a rational LP of this encoding size admits an exact optimal rational solution computable in polynomial bit time. There is also no output-length obstruction. An optimal vertex has polynomial-size rational coordinates, as follows by clearing denominators, applying Cramer’s rule to independent active constraints, and bounding the resulting determinants by Hadamard’s inequality.

Reading the transfer coordinates of this solution produces the required exact output. The potentially large individual-teacher expansion never enters the computation.
