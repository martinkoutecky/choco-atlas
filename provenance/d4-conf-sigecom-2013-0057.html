The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given divisible resources, a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), weights \(b_t\), per-capita capacities \(q_j\), and concave degree-one homogeneous valuation functions \(v_t\), compute a feasible type-level allocation \(x_t\) maximizing \(\sum_t \mu_t b_t\log v_t(x_t)\), or an \(\epsilon\)-approximation to its optimum, and classify the complexity in \(m\), \(|T|\), input length, and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions. Let \(T\) be a finite set of types with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), weights \(b_t>0\), and \(m\) divisible resources with per-capita capacities \(q_j\). A type-level allocation is \(x_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^m\), feasible when \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_{tj}\le q_j\) for every \(j\). Each \(v_t\) is concave and degree-one homogeneous. Maximize \(F(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_t\log v_t(x_t)\), over allocations with positive valuations. An \(\epsilon\)-approximation satisfies \(F(\tilde x)\ge F(x^*)+\log(1-\epsilon)\).

Target. Is this finite-type mass proportional-fair allocation problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m\), \(|T|\), the input length, and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results. For finitely many individual agents with concave degree-one homogeneous valuations, Lemma 3.12 gives a polynomial-time \(\epsilon\)-approximation in the corresponding product objective. That result is stated for an explicit finite agent list, rather than masses aggregated by type.

Result and computational scope

The mass formulation is polynomial-time solvable in the same computational model as the cited finite-agent result. There is an approximation-preserving reduction using exactly one agent per type; expanding rational masses into individual agents is unnecessary.

A necessary qualification is that “input length” requires an encoding. The conclusion below uses binary-encoded rational weights and capacities, and the valuation representation or oracle assumptions under which Lemma \(3.12\) applies. Concavity and homogeneity alone, without computational access to the valuations, do not define a computational problem.

Assume \(0<\epsilon<1\) and that a positive-valuation optimum exists, as presupposed by the question’s \(x^*\).

1. Replace per-capita allocations by total allocations

For each \(t\in T\), define

\(y_t=\mu_t x_t,\qquad w_t=\mu_t b_t.\)

Because every \(\mu_t>0\), this is an invertible transformation, with inverse \(x_t=y_t/\mu_t\). Moreover,

\(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_{tj}\le q_j \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{t\in T}y_{tj}\le q_j.\)

Thus feasible allocations correspond bijectively to allocations among \(|T|\) individual agents with resource capacities \(q_j\).

Degree-one homogeneity gives

\(v_t(y_t)=\mu_t v_t(x_t).\)

In particular, positivity of every valuation is preserved. Define

\(G(y)=\sum_{t\in T}w_t\log v_t(y_t), \qquad C=\sum_{t\in T}w_t\log\mu_t.\)

Then

\(G(y)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t b_t\bigl(\log\mu_t+\log v_t(x_t)\bigr)=F(x)+C.\)

The constant \(C\) is independent of the allocation. Consequently, optimal allocations correspond, and for any two corresponding allocation pairs,

\(G(\widetilde y)-G(y^*)=F(\widetilde x)-F(x^*).\)

This identity already proves exact preservation of additive log-objective error.

2. Put the instance into the finite-agent theorem’s form

The cited Lemma \(3.12\) concerns a weighted product objective and gives a feasible approximation in time polynomial in the problem size and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\). [Cole, Gkatzelis, and Goel, *Mechanism Design for Fair Division*, Lemma \(3.12\)](https://www.cs.drexel.edu/~vg399/PF_journal.pdf).

We account explicitly for unit resource capacities and weights bounded below by one.

First, discard coordinates with \(q_j=0\): feasibility forces their allocations to be zero. For the remaining coordinates set

\(z_{tj}=y_{tj}/q_j.\)

Let \(D\) map \(z_t\) to the original resource coordinates by multiplying coordinate \(j\) by \(q_j\) and inserting zeros in discarded coordinates. Define the individual agent’s valuation by

\(u_t(z_t)=v_t(Dz_t).\)

The constraints become \(\sum_t z_{tj}\le1\). For any nonnegative bundles \(z,z'\) and any \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), concavity of \(v_t\) gives

\(u_t(\lambda z+(1-\lambda)z') =v_t(\lambda Dz+(1-\lambda)Dz') \ge\lambda u_t(z)+(1-\lambda)u_t(z').\)

For every \(a\ge0\), homogeneity gives

\(u_t(az)=v_t(aDz)=a\,u_t(z).\)

Thus the required valuation properties are preserved.

Next, set

\(c=\max\{1,1/\min_{t\in T}w_t\}, \qquad \beta_t=cw_t.\)

Every \(\beta_t\ge1\). The resulting finite-agent log objective is

\(H(z)=\sum_{t\in T}\beta_t\log u_t(z_t)=c\,G(y).\)

Since \(c>0\), its maximizers correspond to those of the original problem.

3. Transfer the approximation guarantee

Apply Lemma \(3.12\) to these \(|T|\) agents, with weights \(\beta_t\), valuations \(u_t\), and approximation parameter \(\epsilon\). Its output satisfies

\(\prod_{t\in T}u_t(\widetilde z_t)^{\beta_t} \ge(1-\epsilon)\prod_{t\in T}u_t(z_t^*)^{\beta_t}.\)

The optimal product is positive, so the output valuations are positive and logarithms are well-defined. Taking logarithms yields

\(H(\widetilde z)\ge H(z^*)+\log(1-\epsilon).\)

Recover the original allocation by

\(\widetilde x_t=D\widetilde z_t/\mu_t.\)

It is feasible by the transformations above. Using the objective identities gives

\(F(\widetilde x)-F(x^*) =\frac{H(\widetilde z)-H(z^*)}{c} \ge\frac{\log(1-\epsilon)}{c} \ge\log(1-\epsilon).\)

The last inequality follows because \(c\ge1\) and \(\log(1-\epsilon)<0\). This is exactly the requested guarantee.

4. Encoding length and running time

The transformed instance has \(|T|\) agents and at most \(m\) resources. Products, reciprocals, and comparisons of the input rationals produce \(w_t\), \(c\), and \(\beta_t\) with polynomial encoding length. Although their numerical magnitudes may be large, their binary lengths remain polynomial.

Each transformed valuation is the original valuation composed with an explicitly given rational diagonal map. Under the cited theorem’s computational model, allowing this elementary change of coordinates preserves polynomial access costs. Recovering \(\widetilde x_t\) also requires only rational scaling.

Therefore, the reduction and the finite-agent algorithm run in time polynomial in \(m\), \(|T|\), the input length, and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\). The denominators of the masses never determine the number of agents.
