The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite-support distributions μ_x and μ_y over welfare levels in a common interval \(X\), with rational levels and masses, equal mean welfare, and continuous Lorenz dominance \(\int_0^r Q_{μ_x}(s)\,ds \leq \int_0^r Q_{μ_y}(s)\,ds\) for every \(r\in[0,1]\), can μ_y be reached from μ_x in at most \(k\) moves? Each move chooses submeasures α,β of the current distribution μ with \(α+β\leqμ\), equal positive mass, and recipient welfare levels below donor welfare levels; for some \(t>0\) that keeps the shifted levels in \(X\), it replaces μ by \(μ-α-β+(z\mapsto z+t)_\#α+(z\mapsto z-t)_\#β.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a rational interval \(X=[a,b]\) with \(a<b\), let \(\mu_x,\mu_y\) be finite-support probability distributions over scalar welfare levels in \(X\); negative levels are allowed. A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Let \(\tau\) be the larger input support size, not the number of possible types; welfare has dimension \(m=1\). All input levels are binary rationals, and \(L\) is the total input bit length, including the positive integer \(k\) encoded in binary.
Promise equal mean welfare and \(\int_0^r Q_{\mu_x}(s)\,ds\leq\int_0^r Q_{\mu_y}(s)\,ds\) for every \(r\in[0,1]\), where \(Q_\mu\) is the quantile function.
A move chooses nonnegative submeasures \(\alpha,\beta\) with \(\alpha+\beta\leq\mu\) and \(\alpha(X)=\beta(X)>0\), with every level in \(\operatorname{supp}\alpha\) strictly below every level in \(\operatorname{supp}\beta\), and a real \(t>0\). It replaces \(\mu\) by \(\mu-\alpha-\beta+(z\mapsto z+t)_\#\alpha+(z\mapsto z-t)_\#\beta\), where \(\#\) denotes pushforward; all resulting levels must remain in \(X\).
The move must preserve welfare order, including relative to unchanged mass: define \(\Gamma=\{(z,z+t):z\in\operatorname{supp}\alpha\}\cup\{(z,z-t):z\in\operatorname{supp}\beta\}\cup\{(z,z):z\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu-\alpha-\beta)\}\); require \(u'\leq v'\) whenever \((u,u'),(v,v')\in\Gamma\) and \(u<v\). Ties may split and resulting ties are allowed. Selected masses and intermediate levels may be real; each entire move counts once.
Is deciding whether \(\mu_y\) is reachable from \(\mu_x\) in at most \(k\) such moves solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\), with \(m=1\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite lists of \(n\) agents, deciding whether an equal-total Lorenz improvement admits at most \(k\) order-preserving pairwise redistributive transfers is NP-hard; such improvements admit a chain of at most \(n\) transfers ([Willot, Belahcene and Destercke](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.3233/FAIA240587)). Partial result: an exact decomposition of the minimum move count

The polynomial-time-versus-$\mathsf{NP}$-hardness classification remains unresolved. I prove a new structural result: the minimum number of moves is additive over the rank intervals on which the target-minus-source Lorenz gap is positive.

This decomposition is computable in polynomial time. It gives polynomially computable lower bounds, an exact algorithm for a nontrivial subclass, and a polynomial-time oracle reduction to instances whose Lorenz gap is strictly positive at every interior rank.

1. Definitions and quantile representation of a move

Let $\lambda$ be Lebesgue measure on $(0,1)$. Write
$$ F(r)=\int_0^r\bigl(Q_{\mu_y}(s)-Q_{\mu_x}(s)\bigr)\,ds. $$
The promise states that $F(r)\geq 0$ for all $r\in[0,1]$, with
$$ F(0)=F(1)=0. $$

For distributions $\mu,\nu$, let $T(\mu,\nu)$ denote the minimum number of legal moves from $\mu$ to $\nu$, with value $+\infty$ if no finite chain exists. Zero moves are allowed.

We first justify using ranks throughout a chain.

Lemma 1. Suppose a finite-support coupling $\pi$ of $\mu,\nu$ satisfies
$$ u<v\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u'\leq v' $$
for every two support pairs $(u,u'),(v,v')$. Then $\pi$ is the law of
$$ \bigl(Q_\mu(r),Q_\nu(r)\bigr),\qquad r\sim\lambda. $$

Proof. Fix thresholds $c,d$, and let
$$ A=\{u\leq c\},\qquad B=\{u'\leq d\}. $$
If both $A\setminus B$ and $B\setminus A$ had positive probability, finite support would provide pairs satisfying
$$ u_1\leq c<u_2,\qquad u'_1>d\geq u'_2, $$
contradicting the order condition. Consequently,
$$ \pi(A\cap B) = \min\{\mu((-\infty,c]),\nu((-\infty,d])\}. $$
These joint cumulative probabilities determine $\pi$ and agree with those of the quantile coupling. $\square$

A legal move supplies precisely such a coupling, including its unchanged mass. Therefore, if $q,q'$ are the quantiles immediately before and after a move with shift $t$, there are measurable rank sets $A,B$ such that, almost everywhere,
$$ q'(r)-q(r)=t\,\mathbf 1_A(r)-t\,\mathbf 1_B(r). $$
Here $A$ represents recipient mass and $B$ donor mass. They satisfy
$$ \lambda(A)=\lambda(B)>0. $$
Strict separation of the selected welfare levels implies that every rank in $A$ precedes every rank in $B$, apart from null sets.

Define the move’s cumulative gain by
$$ G(r)=\int_0^r(q'(s)-q(s))\,ds =t\bigl(\lambda(A\cap(0,r))-\lambda(B\cap(0,r))\bigr). $$

Lemma 2. There are numbers $\ell<u$ such that
$$ G(r)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ell<r<u. $$
In particular, $G(r)\geq0$ everywhere.

Proof. The rank sets are finite unions of intervals, because the distributions and the coupling have finite support. Take $\ell$ to be the leftmost endpoint of $A$ and $u$ the rightmost endpoint of $B$, ignoring null sets.

Before donor ranks begin, any rank strictly after $\ell$ has accumulated positive recipient mass and no donor mass. Once donor ranks begin, all recipient mass has been accumulated; at every rank strictly before $u$, some donor mass remains unaccumulated. Equal total selected masses then imply $G(r)>0$ throughout $(\ell,u)$.

Before $\ell$ neither selected set has contributed. After $u$ both have contributed their equal total masses. Thus $G$ is zero outside $(\ell,u)$. $\square$

This argument includes comparisons with unchanged mass and permits splitting at ties.

2. Tight Lorenz ranks are barriers

Consider any successful chain with quantiles
$$ q_0=Q_{\mu_x},\ q_1,\ldots,q_h=Q_{\mu_y}. $$
Let $G_j$ be the cumulative gain of move $j$. Telescoping gives
$$ F(r)=\sum_{j=1}^hG_j(r). $$
Every summand is nonnegative by Lemma 2. Hence
$$ F(r)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad G_j(r)=0 \quad\text{for every }j. $$

Let
$$ J_1,\ldots,J_g $$
be the connected components of
$$ \{r\in(0,1):F(r)>0\}. $$
Write $J_i=(c_i,d_i)$ and $w_i=d_i-c_i$.

Proposition 3. In every successful chain, all selected rank mass of each move belongs to one component $J_i$, up to null sets.

Proof. By Lemma 2, the positivity set of that move’s cumulative gain is a nonempty interval $(\ell,u)$. Since its gain is bounded above by $F$, this interval lies in $\{F>0\}$ and therefore in one connected component.

The selected recipient and donor ranks lie in $[\ell,u]$. Endpoints have zero rank mass, so all selected mass belongs to that component. $\square$

Thus a move cannot batch selected mass from different positive-gap components. This remains true even when its numerical shift could be used in both components.

3. Exact additivity theorem

For $J_i=(c_i,d_i)$, define normalized local distributions
$$ \mu_x^{(i)} = \frac1{w_i}(Q_{\mu_x})_\#(\lambda|_{J_i}), \qquad \mu_y^{(i)} = \frac1{w_i}(Q_{\mu_y})_\#(\lambda|_{J_i}). $$
Their quantiles satisfy, almost everywhere,
$$ Q_{\mu_x^{(i)}}(s)=Q_{\mu_x}(c_i+w_i s), \qquad Q_{\mu_y^{(i)}}(s)=Q_{\mu_y}(c_i+w_i s). $$

Because $F(c_i)=F(d_i)=0$, the local means agree. Their Lorenz gap is
$$ \int_0^s \bigl(Q_{\mu_y^{(i)}}(v)-Q_{\mu_x^{(i)}}(v)\bigr)\,dv = \frac{F(c_i+w_i s)}{w_i}. $$
It is strictly positive for every $s\in(0,1)$. Thus every local instance satisfies the promise, with no interior tight rank.

Theorem 4. With the convention that a sum containing $+\infty$ equals $+\infty$,
$$ T(\mu_x,\mu_y) = \sum_{i=1}^gT(\mu_x^{(i)},\mu_y^{(i)}). $$
If $g=0$, the sum is zero.

Proof: lower bound. Take any successful global chain of length $h$. Proposition 3 assigns each move to exactly one component. Let $h_i$ be the number assigned to $J_i$.

Restrict the quantile chain to $J_i$ and normalize its rank mass by $w_i$. Moves assigned to other components change nothing in this restriction. Each assigned move becomes a legal local move: both selected masses are divided by the same factor, strict support separation persists, and the global order condition implies the local one. Therefore,
$$ h_i\geq T(\mu_x^{(i)},\mu_y^{(i)}). $$
Summing yields the required lower bound. It also shows that if any local minimum is infinite, no successful global chain exists.

Proof: upper bound. Suppose all local minima are finite, and choose a shortest local chain for each component.

We need to verify that these chains can be embedded into the global society. First, every legal move keeps all resulting levels between the current minimum and maximum. Indeed, for any recipient level $u$ and donor level $v$, legality gives
$$ u+t\leq v-t. $$
Thus both moved outputs lie between $u$ and $v$. By induction, a local chain stays within its initial support interval.

Initial support intervals of successive rank components are ordered and can overlap only at boundary levels. Any intervening ranks outside the components retain their initial quantile values: on such intervals $F=0$, so its derivative
$$ Q_{\mu_y}-Q_{\mu_x} $$
is zero almost everywhere.

Consequently, replacing one component by any stage of its local chain preserves the order of the global quantile, including across component boundaries. Scale that local move’s selected masses by $w_i$. They are valid global submeasures. Strict recipient–donor separation remains valid, and the nondecreasing global output quantile verifies the order condition relative to all unchanged mass. Shared boundary levels cause no problem because the rule permits splitting at ties.

Concatenating the embedded local chains therefore gives a global chain of length
$$ \sum_iT(\mu_x^{(i)},\mu_y^{(i)}). $$

Finally, if $g=0$, then $F$ is identically zero. Differentiating its piecewise-affine expression shows that the two quantiles agree almost everywhere, so $\mu_x=\mu_y$. $\square$

4. Polynomial-time construction and consequences

Merge the cumulative-mass breakpoints of the two input distributions. There are at most
$$ n_x+n_y-1\leq2\tau-1 $$
positive-length intervals in the resulting partition, and $F$ is affine on each.

A nonnegative affine function cannot have an isolated zero in the interior of one of these intervals. Therefore every endpoint of a positive-gap component is a merged breakpoint. Exact rational arithmetic constructs all components and their normalized local distributions in time polynomial in $\tau$ and $L$. Restricting rank masses and dividing by $w_i$ produces rational numbers of polynomial bit length.

There are two useful consequences.

Reduction to strictly positive Lorenz gaps

Suppose there is a polynomial-time decision algorithm for promised instances satisfying
$$ F(r)>0\qquad(0<r<1). $$
Then the general promised problem is also polynomial-time solvable.

For each component, first ask whether its target is reachable within $k$ moves. A negative answer makes the global answer negative. Otherwise, binary search determines its exact minimum in $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ using
$$ O(\log(k+1)) $$
decision calls. Theorem 4 then decides the global instance by summing the local minima.

Since the number and sizes of the local instances are polynomially bounded, and $\log(k+1)$ is bounded by the input length, this is a polynomial-time oracle reduction. Conversely, an algorithm for the general problem applies to the strictly positive-gap subclass.

Thus the unresolved polynomial-time question can be concentrated on that subclass.

A polynomially solvable subclass and a lower bound

For completeness, the following one-move criterion is sufficient to recognize an exact subclass.

Let $\mu\ne\nu$ have equal means, and put
$$ D(r)=Q_\nu(r)-Q_\mu(r). $$
One move is possible if and only if some $t>0$ satisfies:

  • $D(r)\in\{-t,0,t\}$ almost everywhere;
  • both nonzero signs occur on positive-measure rank sets;
  • recipient and donor initial levels satisfy

$$ \max\{Q_\mu(r):D(r)=t\} < \min\{Q_\mu(r):D(r)=-t\}. $$

Necessity follows from Lemma 1 and the move’s strict support separation.

For sufficiency, select exactly the rank masses with displacements $t$ and $-t$. They define disjoint submeasures of $\mu$. Equal means give
$$ 0=\int_0^1D(r)\,dr =t\bigl(\lambda\{D=t\}-\lambda\{D=-t\}\bigr), $$
so the selected masses are equal and positive. Condition 3 gives strict separation. Their outputs are the target quantile, whose monotonicity verifies the full order condition. All outputs lie in $X$.

The criterion is polynomial-time checkable on the merged quantile partition: any nonzero displacement fixes the sole possible value of $t$.

Apply it to every component. If all $g$ components pass, then
$$ T(\mu_x,\mu_y)=g. $$
More generally, if $b$ components fail, then
$$ T(\mu_x,\mu_y)\geq g+b. $$
Indeed, each positive-gap component requires at least one move, and every failed component requires at least two, or is unreachable. Both $g$ and $b$ are polynomial-time computable.
