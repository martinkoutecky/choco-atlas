The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two candidates \(a,b\), rational initial plurality scores, and a finite ordered list of rational-mass cohorts with per-capita weights and roles \(\mathrm{M}\) or \(\mathrm{N}\), where a manipulator cohort chooses \(x_{r,a},x_{r,b}\ge 0\) with \(x_{r,a}+x_{r,b}=\lambda_r\) after observing prior aggregate ballots and a nonmanipulator cohort chooses adversarially, does there exist a history-dependent coalition policy forcing \(S_a=S_b\) for every adversarial policy?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses. Here each type is a cohort identified by its position \(r\) in the voting order, its per-capita weight \(w_r\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\), and its role \(t_r\in\{\mathrm{M},\mathrm{N}\}\); its mass is \(\lambda_r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\). Thus \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Masses need not sum to \(1\).
There are \(m=2\) candidates \(a,b\), initial plurality scores \(s_a,s_b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\), and \(\tau\geq 1\) remaining cohorts, with \(t_1=\mathrm{M}\). The entire order, masses, weights, roles, and initial scores are known.
At stage \(r\), the coalition controls an \(\mathrm{M}\) cohort and an adversary controls an \(\mathrm{N}\) cohort. Either controller chooses real masses \(x_{r,a},x_{r,b}\geq 0\) satisfying \(x_{r,a}+x_{r,b}=\lambda_r\), after observing all earlier aggregate ballots. Each cohort acts once; abstention is unavailable.
Policies are deterministic functions of the observed history. Final scores are \(S_c=s_c+\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}w_rx_{r,c}\) for \(c\in\{a,b\}\). Ties are not broken; the coalition's objective is to prevent either candidate from being a unique winner, equivalently to force \(S_a=S_b\).

Is deciding whether a history-dependent coalition policy forces \(S_a=S_b\) against every adversarial policy in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the total binary input bit length \(L\).

For indivisible voters with nonnegative integer weights, online destructive plurality manipulation in the unique-winner model is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even with two candidates; the hardness already holds when all remaining voters are manipulators and the objective is to prevent every unique winner ([Hemaspaandra, Hemaspaandra, and Rothe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.6997)).
No result settling this exact divisible-cohort problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted initial scores and per-capita weights to nonnegative binary rationals, retaining the discrete model's allowance of zero weights.
  • Required a nonempty remaining sequence whose first cohort is a manipulator, matching the current-manipulator snapshot convention.
  • Used unnormalized positive masses, since the discrete model imposes no normalization on society size.
  • Included voting position in the type identifier so that distinct stages remain distinct support entries even when their weights and roles agree.
  • Specified that adversarial cohorts have the same real-valued splitting domain as manipulator cohorts, with all mass cast and no abstention.
  • Specified deterministic policies observing completed cohort ballots, with no observation or adaptation within a cohort.
  • Interpreted equality as preventing every unique winner, without tie-breaking, matching the destructive objective used in the anchor's hardness construction.

Result

The divisible-cohort problem is in $\mathrm{P}$. For the promised $m=2$, it can be decided using $O(\tau)$ exact rational arithmetic operations, or $O(\tau L^2)$ bit operations with elementary integer arithmetic.

Define the weighted mass and role sign of cohort $r$ by
$$ q_r=w_r\lambda_r,\qquad \varepsilon_r= \begin{cases} 1,&t_r=\mathrm{M},\\ -1,&t_r=\mathrm{N}. \end{cases} $$
Define the signed suffix totals
$$ B_r=\sum_{j=r}^{\tau}\varepsilon_jq_j, \qquad B_{\tau+1}=0. $$

The exact decision criterion is
$$ \boxed{ B_r\geq 0\text{ for every }r\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}, \qquad |s_a-s_b|\leq B_1. } $$

The proof below accounts for arbitrary real allocations and history-dependent policies.

Reduction to a scalar game

Let $d$ denote the current score difference between $a$ and $b$. At stage $r$, an allocation changes this difference by
$$ u=w_r(x_{r,a}-x_{r,b}). $$
Since $x_{r,a}+x_{r,b}=\lambda_r$, the possible increments are exactly
$$ u\in[-q_r,q_r]. $$

Indeed, when $w_r>0$, any such increment is realized by
$$ x_{r,a}=\frac{\lambda_r+u/w_r}{2}, \qquad x_{r,b}=\frac{\lambda_r-u/w_r}{2}. $$
These masses are nonnegative and sum to $\lambda_r$. When $w_r=0$, the only increment is $0$, and every legal allocation realizes it.

Thus the game starts at $d=s_a-s_b$. At each stage, the controlling player selects $u\in[-q_r,q_r]$, and the new difference is $d+u$. The coalition must force the terminal difference to be $0$.

All future legal moves and the terminal objective depend only on the stage and current difference. Earlier history therefore supplies no additional state information relevant to the remaining game.

Backward induction

Let $W_r\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ be the set of differences from which the coalition can force a terminal tie when stages $r,\ldots,\tau$ remain. Then
$$ W_{\tau+1}=\{0\}. $$

We prove by backward induction that every $W_r$ is either empty or a symmetric closed interval. The induction also establishes that every state outside $W_r$ admits an adversarial policy defeating every coalition policy.

If $W_{r+1}$ is empty, then $W_r$ is empty: every legal current action leads to a state from which the adversary can defeat the coalition by the induction hypothesis.

Otherwise, suppose
$$ W_{r+1}=[-R,R],\qquad R\geq 0. $$

A manipulator stage

The coalition can win precisely when some legal increment reaches $W_{r+1}$:
$$ \begin{aligned} d\in W_r &\iff \exists u\in[-q_r,q_r]\colon |d+u|\leq R\\ &\iff |d|\leq R+q_r. \end{aligned} $$

For sufficiency, choose
$$ u=-\operatorname{sgn}(d)\min\{|d|,q_r\}, $$
where $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$. Then
$$ |d+u|=\max\{|d|-q_r,0\}\leq R. $$

For necessity, if $|d|>R+q_r$, every legal increment satisfies
$$ |d+u|\geq |d|-|u|\geq |d|-q_r>R. $$
The adversary can therefore defeat the coalition from the resulting state by induction. Consequently,
$$ W_r=[-(R+q_r),R+q_r]. $$

An adversary stage

The coalition can win precisely when every legal increment reaches $W_{r+1}$. Since
$$ \max_{u\in[-q_r,q_r]}|d+u|=|d|+q_r, $$
this requires
$$ |d|+q_r\leq R. $$
Hence
$$ W_r= \begin{cases} [-(R-q_r),R-q_r],&R\geq q_r,\\ \varnothing,&R<q_r. \end{cases} $$

To verify necessity explicitly, whenever $|d|+q_r>R$, the adversary chooses $u=q_r$ if $d\geq 0$, and $u=-q_r$ otherwise. This produces
$$ |d+u|=|d|+q_r>R, $$
after which the induction hypothesis supplies a defeating continuation.

For sufficiency, every adversarial move enters $W_{r+1}$, and the coalition uses the explicit manipulator rule above at subsequent manipulator stages. Thus the induction constructs one deterministic policy valid for all adversarial histories; it does not exchange existential and universal policy quantifiers.

Criterion and explicit winning policy

Starting with radius $0$ at stage $\tau+1$, the backward recurrence adds $q_r$ at a manipulator stage and subtracts $q_r$ at an adversary stage. A subtraction that would make the radius negative makes the winning set empty, and emptiness persists at all earlier stages.

If every signed suffix total is nonnegative, induction therefore gives
$$ W_r=[-B_r,B_r] $$
for every $r$.

Conversely, suppose some signed suffix total is negative. Choose the largest index $r$ with $B_r<0$. Then $B_{r+1}\geq 0$, and all later suffix totals are nonnegative. Because $q_r\geq 0$, the relation
$$ B_r=B_{r+1}+\varepsilon_rq_r<0 $$
forces $t_r=\mathrm{N}$ and $q_r>B_{r+1}$. The adversary-stage recurrence makes $W_r$ empty, hence also makes $W_1$ empty.

This proves the stated criterion.

On every accepted instance, an explicit winning coalition policy is to choose, at each manipulator stage,
$$ u=-\operatorname{sgn}(d)\min\{|d|,q_r\}, $$
and realize this increment by the allocation formula above. For a zero-weight cohort, choose $x_{r,a}=x_{r,b}=\lambda_r/2$.

The backward induction proves that this policy keeps the current difference in the appropriate winning interval against every adversarial policy. At the terminal stage that interval is $\{0\}$, so $S_a=S_b$.

Algorithm and binary running time

The decision algorithm is a single backward scan:

  • Initialize $B=0$.
  • For $r=\tau,\ldots,1$, compute $q_r=w_r\lambda_r$ and update $B$ to $B+q_r$ for an $\mathrm{M}$ cohort or $B-q_r$ for an $\mathrm{N}$ cohort. Reject immediately if $B<0$.
  • Accept exactly when $|s_a-s_b|\leq B$.

This takes $O(\tau)$ rational multiplications, additions, and comparisons.

For the bit bound, represent input rationals by binary numerators and positive denominators. Products $q_r$ have numerator and denominator lengths bounded by the combined lengths of their input factors. An unreduced denominator for a running suffix sum is a product of denominators of the terms already processed, so its bit length is $O(L)$. The numerator also has $O(L)$ bits: each $q_r$ is at most $2^{O(L)}$, and a suffix sum has absolute value at most $\tau 2^{O(L)}$, with $\tau\leq O(L)$. The initial difference and comparison intermediates likewise have $O(L)$ bits.

Elementary arithmetic therefore costs $O(L^2)$ bit operations per stage. The resulting bounds are
$$ O(\tau L^2)\text{ bit time}, \qquad O(L)\text{ additional working bits}. $$
Including the candidate parameter, since $m=2$ these are equivalently $O(m+\tau)$ arithmetic operations and $O((m+\tau)L^2)$ bit time. No expansion of masses into individual voters is needed.

Why indivisible-voter hardness does not transfer

The cited indivisible-voter result uses a reduction from $\mathrm{Partition}$: assigning each entire weighted voter to one candidate creates a discrete subset-sum constraint. See [Hemaspaandra, Hemaspaandra, and Rothe, proof of Theorem 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.6997).

Here each cohort instead supplies every increment in $[-q_r,q_r]$. That interval property is exactly what makes the backward induction remain one-dimensional and polynomial.

In particular, when every remaining cohort is a manipulator, the criterion reduces to
$$ |s_a-s_b|\leq\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}w_r\lambda_r. $$
Thus the all-manipulator case that supports the indivisible hardness is also polynomial-time solvable in this divisible model.

$$ S_a-S_b=0. $$

$$ u=w_r(x_{r,a}-x_{r,b}),\qquad q_r=w_r\lambda_r. $$

The case $w_r=0$ is handled separately and correctly. It produces only the increment $0$, without any division by zero.

The state reduction is justified because future legal moves depend only on the stage, while the terminal objective depends only on the accumulated difference. Although policies may observe more history, the subsequent induction establishes winning and losing continuations against arbitrary such policies.

2. Terminal condition and induction quantifiers

The terminal condition
$$ W_{\tau+1}=\{0\} $$
is exact. At every other terminal difference, the coalition has already failed; no adversarial action is necessary.

The induction asserts both that states inside $W_r$ have a winning coalition policy and that states outside $W_r$ have an adversarial policy defeating every coalition policy. This stronger formulation supports the necessity arguments.

Continuation policies can depend on the observed successor state. This is allowed by the history-dependent policy model. The induction therefore does not improperly replace a policy quantifier with a move quantifier.

If $W_{r+1}$ is empty, every current move reaches a losing state. Consequently, $W_r$ is empty for either role. The continuation argument is valid even when the current mover is the coalition, because the adversary observes the resulting history before its subsequent actions.

3. Manipulator-stage recurrence

Given $W_{r+1}=[-R,R]$, the asserted recurrence
$$ W_r=[-(R+q_r),R+q_r] $$
is correct.

For sufficiency, the stated move is legal and satisfies
$$ |d+u|=\max\{|d|-q_r,0\}. $$
If $|d|\leq R+q_r$, this is at most $R$.

For necessity, the reverse triangle inequality gives, for every legal move,
$$ |d+u|\geq |d|-q_r>R $$
whenever $|d|>R+q_r$. The induction hypothesis then supplies an adversarial continuation.

The argument includes the boundary cases $d=0$, $R=0$, and $q_r=0$.

The identity
$$ \max_{u\in[-q_r,q_r]}|d+u|=|d|+q_r $$
is correct. The endpoints specified by the prover attain this maximum, including when $d=0$.

Therefore every adversarial move enters $[-R,R]$ exactly when
$$ |d|+q_r\leq R. $$
This yields the stated interval when $R\geq q_r$, and the empty set when $R<q_r$. In particular, equality $R=q_r$ correctly leaves the singleton $\{0\}$.

When the inequality fails, the explicitly selected endpoint reaches a losing successor state. When it holds, every successor state admits the same subsequently prescribed coalition rule. Both directions are established with the required policy quantifiers.

For the converse, the largest index with $B_r<0$ exists whenever any suffix total is negative. All later suffix totals are nonnegative, so the later winning interval has radius $B_{r+1}$. Since $q_r\geq0$, negativity forces the current role to be $\mathrm{N}$ and $q_r>B_{r+1}$. This makes $W_r$ empty, and emptiness propagates to $W_1$.

The claimed criterion follows:
$$ B_r\geq0\text{ for every remaining stage}, \qquad |s_a-s_b|\leq B_1. $$
