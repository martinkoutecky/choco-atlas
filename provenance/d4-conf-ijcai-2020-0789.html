Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite assignment and resource sets, rational rewards and resource uses, per-capita budgets, a finite horizon \(T\), and an explicit finite rational distribution for each stage’s arrival-mass vector \(\Xi_t\), choose offline assignment masses \(x_e\) before arrivals and online assignment masses \(z_{e,t}\) after observing the current arrivals and past history. Each person receives an integral assignment, while aggregate masses may be fractional; all assignments must obey shared resource budgets at every stage. What is the best competitive ratio achievable in polynomial time against a clairvoyant fluid allocator that sees the full arrival path?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(E^1\) be repeatable offline assignments and \(E^2=\bigsqcup_{j\in J}E^2_j\) online assignments available to type \(j\); put \(m=|E^1|+|E^2|\). Rewards \(w_e>0\), resource uses \(a_{e,k}\in[0,1]\), and per-capita budgets \(b_k>0\) are binary rationals. Every offline assignment consumes some resource; there are no offline supply constraints beyond budgets.
Partition the \(K\) resources into nonempty sets \(\mathcal K_1,\mathcal K_2\), with \(a_{e,k}\in\{0,1\}\) on \(\mathcal K_1\). Each assignment uses at most \(\ell_i\) resources in \(\mathcal K_i\); assume \(\ell=\ell_1+\ell_2\ge1\) and \(B=\min_{k\in\mathcal K_2}b_k>2\). Budgets and aggregate allocations may be fractional for either resource class.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Use one mass unit as the population reference for all budgets and allocations.
For each \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}\), an explicit finite distribution with positive rational probabilities specifies \(\Xi_t\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{J}\), with \(\sum_j\Xi_{j,t}=1\). These vectors are sampled independently across stages; \(\tau\) counts types having positive mass in some listed outcome. Let \(S\) be the total number of listed outcomes and \(L\) the total input bit length.
Before any arrivals, choose \(x_e\ge0\) for \(e\in E^1\). After observing \(\Xi_t\) and the past, irrevocably choose \(z_{e,t}\ge0\), satisfying \(\sum_{e\in E^2_j}z_{e,t}\le\Xi_{j,t}\). Unassigned mass is rejected immediately. These masses partition atomless populations into people receiving one whole assignment or rejection.
For every realized history and every \(t\), require \(\sum_{e\in E^1}a_{e,k}x_e+\sum_{s=1}^{t}\sum_{e\in E^2}a_{e,k}z_{e,s}\le b_k\) for every resource \(k\). Reward is \(\sum_{e\in E^1}w_ex_e+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{e\in E^2}w_ez_{e,t}\).
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}(\Xi_1,\ldots,\Xi_T)\) maximize this reward under the same constraints, allowing even \(x\) to depend on the entire arrival path. A policy's competitive ratio is \(\inf_{\mathcal I}\mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}(\mathcal I)]/\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}(\mathcal I)]\), over instances with positive denominator and the specified parameter values; expectation includes policy randomness. Polynomial time means total running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,T,S,L\).

What is the best worst-case competitive ratio achievable by polynomial-time policies for this fluid allocation problem, as a function of \(\ell_1,\ell_2,B,T\)?

For discrete assignments and independent single-person arrivals, a polynomial-time policy guarantees \((1-\varepsilon)/(4\ell)\), where \(\varepsilon=2\max\{1/(B-2),\ell_2\exp(-B/12+1/6)\}\), against an LP upper bound on expected clairvoyant reward. [Xu et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2020/0581.pdf)
For fractional vertex-weighted stochastic matching with independent, nonidentically distributed arrivals, a \(0.718\) guarantee and a \(0.75\) upper bound are known; this is a restricted allocation model. [Tang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06851)

Partial results: improved guarantees and stronger obstructions

1. Status and notation

The best competitive ratio for the full model remains unresolved. I prove an improved lower bound, two stronger upper-bound constructions, and the exact special cases stated below.

Let
$$ R_{\mathrm{poly}}(\ell_1,\ell_2,B,T) $$
denote the supremum of competitive ratios of policies whose total running time is polynomial in the parameters specified in the question. The sparsity parameters are interpreted as nonnegative integers, and $T$ is a positive integer.

Define
$$ Q_B(\beta) = \exp\left( -\frac{B}{\beta} \bigl(\log(1/\beta)-1+\beta\bigr) \right), \qquad 0<\beta<1, $$
and
$$ \Psi(\ell_1,\ell_2,B) = \sup_{0<\beta<1} \left[ \frac{\beta-\ell_2Q_B(\beta)} {1+\ell_1\beta} \right]_+. $$
Here $[u]_+=\max\{u,0\}$.

The general lower bound proved here is
$$ R_{\mathrm{poly}}(\ell_1,\ell_2,B,T) \ge \max\left\{ \frac1{\ell_1+\ell_2+1}, H(\ell_1,B,T), \Psi(\ell_1,\ell_2,B) \right\}, $$
where
$$ H(\ell_1,B,T) = \begin{cases} 1/(T+1),&\ell_1\ge1,\\ B/(B+T),&\ell_1=0. \end{cases} $$

The new lower bound $\Psi$ yields, for every fixed $\ell_2$,
$$ \lim_{B\to\infty} R_{\mathrm{poly}}(1,\ell_2,B,T)=\frac12 $$
uniformly over positive integer horizons $T$.

The upper-bound constructions establish two additional facts:

  • The full fluid model does not admit a universal competitive ratio of $1/2$. Explicit instances below give an upper bound of $4/9$ for sufficiently large first-class sparsity and horizon.
  • Even when $\ell_1=0$ and $\ell_2=1$, for integer $B\ge3$ and $T\ge2B$,

$$ R_{\mathrm{poly}}(0,1,B,T) \le 1-\frac1{8\sqrt B}. $$
Thus a guarantee with loss of order $1/B$, uniformly over $T$, is impossible in this restricted case.

All upper bounds hold without computational restrictions.

2. A polynomial-size upper-bounding LP

Merge identical listed outcomes within each stage. Let $O_t$ be the resulting outcome set, with probabilities $p_{t,o}$ and arrival vectors $\xi^{t,o}$. An observed vector then identifies its outcome.

For an allocation vector $v$, write
$$ A_k(v)=\sum_e a_{e,k}v_e. $$

Consider the LP
$$ \max\quad \sum_{e\in E^1}w_ex_e + \sum_{t,o}p_{t,o} \sum_{e\in E^2}w_ey_e^{t,o}, $$
subject to nonnegativity and
$$ \sum_{e\in E_j^2}y_e^{t,o}\le\xi_j^{t,o} \qquad\text{for every }j,t,o, $$
$$ A_k(y^{t,o})\le b_k \qquad\text{for every }k,t,o, $$
$$ A_k(x)+ \sum_{t,o}p_{t,o}A_k(y^{t,o}) \le b_k \qquad\text{for every }k. $$
Let its optimum be $V$, and fix an optimal solution $(x^*,y^*)$.

Claim.
$$ V\ge\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]. $$

Proof. The arrival-path space is finite. On every path choose a clairvoyant optimum. Set $x$ equal to its expected offline allocation and set
$$ y_e^{t,o} = \mathbb E[z_{e,t}^{\mathrm{OPT}}\mid O_t=o]. $$
Conditional expectation preserves type-supply constraints. Every individual stage consumes at most $b_k$ on every path, because consumption is nonnegative and total consumption is feasible. This establishes the outcome-specific resource constraints. Taking expectations of final consumption establishes the aggregate constraints.

The objective of this projected solution is exactly $\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]$. Consequently, $V$ is an upper bound. $\square$

The LP has at most $|E^1|+S|E^2|$ variables and polynomially many constraints. Every offline variable is bounded because its assignment has a positive resource coefficient: if $a_{e,k}>0$, then $x_e\le b_k/a_{e,k}$. Every online variable is at most one. All coefficients have polynomial encoding length. Thus the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming applies.

3. Attenuation after clipping

3.1 A feasibility and availability argument

Fix rational constants $0<c\le\beta<1$. Choose the offline allocation $cx^*$.

Before stage $t$, let $r_{k,t}$ be the remaining budget, and put
$$ F_{e,t} = \min\left\{ 1,\ \min_{\substack{k\in\mathcal K_1\\a_{e,k}>0}} \frac{r_{k,t}}{\beta b_k} \right\}. $$
An empty inner minimum imposes no restriction.

Let $G_{e,t}$ equal one when
$$ r_{k,t}\ge\beta $$
for every second-class resource used by $e$, and zero otherwise. Define
$$ H_{e,t}=F_{e,t}G_{e,t}, \qquad q_{e,t}=\mathbb E[H_{e,t}]. $$
The expectation is over past arrivals under the policy already specified at earlier stages.

Suppose constants $d_{e,t}\ge c/\beta$ have been fixed. On observing outcome $o$, allocate
$$ z_{e,t} = \frac{c}{d_{e,t}}\,y_e^{t,o,*}H_{e,t}. $$

This policy is feasible on every history, irrespective of the accuracy of the constants $d_{e,t}$.

Indeed, $c/d_{e,t}\le\beta$, so type supply is respected. For a first-class resource $k$, every assignment using it satisfies
$$ z_{e,t}\le \frac{r_{k,t}}{b_k}y_e^{t,o,*}. $$
Therefore
$$ \sum_e a_{e,k}z_{e,t} \le \frac{r_{k,t}}{b_k}A_k(y^{t,o,*}) \le r_{k,t}. $$

For a second-class resource $k$, if $r_{k,t}<\beta$, all allocations using it are zero. Otherwise the entire stage consumes at most $\beta$, since
$$ \sum_e y_e^{t,o,*}\le1, \qquad a_{e,k}\le1, \qquad z_{e,t}\le\beta y_e^{t,o,*}. $$
Thus its consumption is also at most $r_{k,t}$.

We next lower-bound $q_{e,t}$. Assume inductively that preceding stages satisfy
$$ \mathbb E[z_{e,s}\mid O_s=o]\le c\,y_e^{s,o,*}. $$
Write $U_{k,t}=b_k-r_{k,t}$. The LP gives
$$ \mathbb E[U_{k,t}] \le c\left( A_k(x^*)+ \sum_{s<t,o}p_{s,o}A_k(y^{s,o,*}) \right) \le cb_k. $$

For $u\in[0,1]$ and $\beta\le1$,
$$ 1-\min\{1,(1-u)/\beta\}\le u. $$
When $u\le1-\beta$, the left side is zero. Otherwise the desired inequality is equivalent to
$$ u-(1-\beta)\le\beta u, $$
which follows from $u\le1$.

Applying this inequality to each first-class resource used by $e$ yields
$$ \mathbb E[1-F_{e,t}]\le\ell_1c. $$

For a second-class resource $k$, put
$$ D=A_k(x^*), \qquad V_s=A_k(y^{s,O_s,*}). $$
The variables $V_s$ are independent, lie in $[0,1]$, and satisfy
$$ \sum_{s<t}\mathbb E[V_s]\le b_k-D. $$
The interval bound follows from total arriving mass one and coefficients at most one.

Because $c\le\beta$ and all online allocations are pointwise at most $\beta y^*$,
$$ U_{k,t}\le \beta\left(D+\sum_{s<t}V_s\right). $$
Consequently,
$$ r_{k,t}<\beta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D+\sum_{s<t}V_s>b_k/\beta-1. $$

For $\theta>0$, convexity gives
$$ e^{\theta v}\le1+v(e^\theta-1) \qquad(0\le v\le1). $$
Independence and exponential Markov inequality imply
$$ \begin{aligned} \Pr(r_{k,t}<\beta) &\le \exp\left( \theta+\theta D-\theta b_k/\beta +(e^\theta-1)(b_k-D) \right)\\ &\le \exp\left( \theta+b_k(e^\theta-1-\theta/\beta) \right). \end{aligned} $$
The last step uses $\theta-(e^\theta-1)\le0$ and $D\ge0$.

Taking $\theta=\log(1/\beta)$ gives
$$ \Pr(r_{k,t}<\beta) \le \frac1\beta \exp\left( -\frac{b_k}{\beta} \bigl(\log(1/\beta)-1+\beta\bigr) \right) \le \frac{Q_B(\beta)}{\beta}. $$
The final inequality uses $b_k\ge B$ and
$$ \log(1/\beta)-1+\beta>0. $$

Using
$$ FG\ge1-(1-F)-(1-G) $$
and a union bound over the second-class resources used by $e$, we conclude
$$ q_{e,t} \ge 1-\ell_1c-\frac{\ell_2Q_B(\beta)}{\beta}. $$
In particular, if
$$ 0<c\le \frac{\beta-\ell_2Q_B(\beta)} {1+\ell_1\beta}, $$
then
$$ q_{e,t}\ge c/\beta. $$

Since $H_{e,t}$ depends only on the past, stage independence gives
$$ \mathbb E[z_{e,t}\mid O_t=o] = c\,y_e^{t,o,*}\frac{q_{e,t}}{d_{e,t}}. $$
Thus the ideal choice $d_{e,t}=q_{e,t}$ would give expected allocation exactly $cy_e^{t,o,*}$, closing the induction.

3.2 Polynomial-time approximation of the attenuation constants

Exact computation of $q_{e,t}$ by enumerating histories is unnecessary.

Fix rational $\delta\in(0,1)$ and let
$$ \eta=\delta c/6. $$
Construct the constants before actual arrivals, proceeding through stages in order. Once the earlier constants have been fixed, simulate independent arrival prefixes under that policy and estimate every $q_{e,t}$ by a sample mean $\widehat q_{e,t}$.

Round upward on a dyadic mesh of size at most $\eta$ and set
$$ d_{e,t} = \max\left\{ c/\beta,\ \text{upward rounding of }\widehat q_{e,t}+\eta \right\}. $$

If preceding estimates were accurate and
$$ |\widehat q_{e,t}-q_{e,t}|\le\eta, $$
then the availability argument gives
$$ q_{e,t}\le d_{e,t}\le q_{e,t}+3\eta. $$
Since $q_{e,t}\ge c/\beta\ge c$,
$$ 1-\delta/2 \le \frac{q_{e,t}}{d_{e,t}} \le1. $$
Therefore
$$ c(1-\delta/2)y_e^{t,o,*} \le \mathbb E[z_{e,t}\mid O_t=o] \le cy_e^{t,o,*}. $$

Here are the computational details.

A simulated prefix can be sampled with a fixed number of random bits. Inverse-CDF sampling on a sufficiently fine dyadic grid gives total variation error at most $\eta/2$ for the prefix distribution. Since $H_{e,t}\in[0,1]$, this changes its expectation by at most $\eta/2$. Polynomially many bits suffice: a grid with stagewise errors summing to at most $\eta/2$ can be chosen using $S$ and $\eta$.

For $N_{\mathrm{sim}}$ independent simulated prefixes, Hoeffding’s inequality gives
$$ \Pr\left( |\widehat q_{e,t}-\mathbb E[\widehat q_{e,t}]|>\eta/2 \right) \le 2e^{-N_{\mathrm{sim}}\eta^2/2}. $$
Taking
$$ N_{\mathrm{sim}} = O\left( \eta^{-2}\log\frac{mT+1}{\delta} \right) $$
makes the probability of any inaccurate estimate at any stage at most $\delta/2$. Fresh samples justify this bound conditional on previously fixed preprocessing constants.

All stored constants have polynomial encoding length because of dyadic rounding. With those constants fixed, each resource update is piecewise affine with rational coefficients: a clipping minimum selects either a constant or one normalized remaining budget, and a blocking indicator selects zero or one. There are polynomially many possible coefficients, indexed by stages, listed outcomes, assignments, and resources. A common denominator for these coefficients has polynomial bit length; after at most $T$ updates, denominators divide its $T$th power times the initial denominator. Thus simulations and actual allocations use polynomial-bit rational arithmetic.

Conditional on any accurate preprocessing result, expected reward is at least
$$ c(1-\delta/2)V. $$
Actual arrivals are independent of preprocessing randomness. On inaccurate preprocessing results the policy remains feasible and has nonnegative reward. Hence
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \ge (1-\delta/2)^2cV \ge (1-\delta)c\,\mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]. $$

The running time is polynomial in the input parameters, $1/\delta$, and $1/c$. The reciprocal dependence on $c$ is handled by the sparsity guarantee proved next: candidate guarantees below $1/(K+1)$ can be discarded, while every retained candidate has $1/c\le K+1$.

3.3 The sparsity guarantee and the resulting supremum

For completeness, the same simulation argument proves the baseline guarantee.

Put
$$ h=\min\{\ell_1+\ell_2,K\}, \qquad c_0=\frac1{h+1}. $$
Choose offline allocation $c_0x^*$ and use
$$ g_{e,t} = \min_{k:a_{e,k}>0}\frac{r_{k,t}}{b_k}, $$
with $g_{e,t}=1$ for resource-free assignments. Allocate
$$ z_{e,t} = \frac{c_0}{d_{e,t}}y_e^{t,o,*}g_{e,t}, \qquad d_{e,t}\ge c_0. $$
The outcome-specific resource constraints imply feasibility.

Under the inductive expected-allocation upper bound,
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[g_{e,t}] &\ge 1-\sum_{k:a_{e,k}>0} \frac{\mathbb E[U_{k,t}]}{b_k}\\ &\ge1-hc_0=c_0. \end{aligned} $$
The simulation construction above, now with minimum constant $c_0$, gives competitive ratio $(1-\delta)c_0$ in polynomial time. In particular,
$$ R_{\mathrm{poly}}\ge \frac1{\ell_1+\ell_2+1}. $$

For the mixed-resource bound, rational $\beta$ and rational $c$ strictly below the positive expression in Section 3.1 suffice. Their attainable ratios approach $\Psi$.

This can also be implemented uniformly. For any fixed rational $\beta$, compute a downward rational approximation to the expression with error at most a prescribed constant times $c_0$. The required precision is polynomial in the input length and $\log(1/\delta)$; standard polynomial-time approximation of $\log$ and $\exp$ supplies it, with sufficiently small exponential terms replaced by certified upper bounds. If the candidate is below $c_0$, use the baseline policy. Otherwise $1/c\le K+1$, so the simulation bound is polynomial in the specified input parameters. Continuity in $\beta$ and density of rational choices then establish the stated supremal bound $\Psi$.

4. Consequences of the improved lower bound

Fix $\ell_1,\ell_2$ and $\beta<1$. Because
$$ \log(1/\beta)-1+\beta>0, $$
we have $Q_B(\beta)\to0$ as $B\to\infty$. Consequently,
$$ \liminf_{B\to\infty} R_{\mathrm{poly}}(\ell_1,\ell_2,B,T) \ge \frac{\beta}{1+\ell_1\beta}. $$
The bound is independent of $T$. Letting $\beta$ increase to one gives the uniform lower bound
$$ \liminf_{B\to\infty} R_{\mathrm{poly}}(\ell_1,\ell_2,B,T) \ge \frac1{\ell_1+1}. $$

Section 5 proves $R_{\mathrm{poly}}\le1/2$ whenever $\ell_1\ge1$. Thus, for $\ell_1=1$ and fixed $\ell_2$,
$$ R_{\mathrm{poly}}(1,\ell_2,B,T)\longrightarrow\frac12 $$
uniformly over $T$.

For $\ell_1=0$ and fixed $\ell_2$, the same argument gives convergence to one, also uniformly over $T$, since no policy can outperform the clairvoyant optimum on a realized path.

5. Horizon bounds, universal upper bounds, and exact cases

5.1 Horizon-dependent policies

Let $D$ be the optimal reward using offline assignments alone. For each stage and outcome, let $H_t(o)$ be the optimal reward using that stage alone with the full budgets.

Every clairvoyant solution satisfies
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\le D+\sum_tH_t(O_t), $$
because each component is separately feasible with the full budgets.

Choose $1/(T+1)$ times an optimal offline allocation. At each stage choose $1/(T+1)$ times an optimal standalone allocation for its observed outcome. Each of these $T+1$ components consumes at most $b_k/(T+1)$, so the policy is feasible and guarantees $1/(T+1)$. All standalone problems are polynomial-size rational LPs.

If $\ell_1=0$, define $U_t(o)$ by assigning each arriving type to its highest-reward available assignment while ignoring budgets. Types with no available assignment are rejected and contribute zero. Such a stage allocation consumes at most one unit of every resource.

Use the scaling factor
$$ c_B=\frac{B}{B+T}. $$
Choose $c_B$ times an optimal offline allocation and, at each stage, $c_B$ times the allocation defining $U_t(o)$. Each second-class resource has total consumption at most
$$ c_Bb_k+c_BT \le c_Bb_k(1+T/B)=b_k. $$
First-class consumption is zero. Since
$$ \mathrm{OPT}\le D+\sum_tU_t(O_t), $$
this policy guarantees $B/(B+T)$.

5.2 Universal upper bounds

Use one active resource with capacity $C\ge1$. There is one offline assignment with unit cost and unit reward.

At one online stage, with probability $p$, a unit mass arrives with a unit-cost assignment of reward $1/p$. Otherwise only an unassignable type arrives. Other stages contain only the unassignable type.

For every offline choice $x\in[0,C]$, expected policy reward is at most
$$ x+\min\{1,C-x\}\le C. $$
Conditioning on $x$ proves the same upper bound for randomized policies.

Clairvoyance earns $C$ in the low outcome and $C-1+1/p$ in the high outcome. Therefore
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}]=C+1-p. $$
Taking dyadic rational probabilities $p\to0$ gives the upper bound $C/(C+1)$.

If $\ell_1\ge1$, put the active resource in $\mathcal K_1$ with $C=1$ and add an unused second-class resource of budget $B$. If $\ell_1=0$, put it in $\mathcal K_2$ with $C=B$ and add an unused first-class resource. Hence
$$ R_{\mathrm{poly}}(\ell_1,\ell_2,B,T) \le \begin{cases} 1/2,&\ell_1\ge1,\\ B/(B+1),&\ell_1=0. \end{cases} $$

5.3 Exact cases

For $T=1$, the lower and upper bounds coincide:
$$ R_{\mathrm{poly}}(\ell_1,\ell_2,B,1) = \begin{cases} 1/2,&\ell_1\ge1,\\ B/(B+1),&\ell_1=0. \end{cases} $$
The explicit scaling policies attain these ratios.

For $(\ell_1,\ell_2)=(1,0)$, the sparsity attenuation policy can be implemented exactly. Take $c=1/2$. An assignment using resource $k$ has
$$ g_{e,t}=r_{k,t}/b_k. $$
Inductively, its exact expectation is
$$ q_{e,t} = 1-\frac{c}{b_k} \left( A_k(x^*)+ \sum_{s<t,o}p_{s,o}A_k(y^{s,o,*}) \right). $$
The LP implies $q_{e,t}\ge1-c=c$. Setting $d_{e,t}=q_{e,t}$ gives
$$ \mathbb E[z_{e,t}\mid O_t=o]=cy_e^{t,o,*}, $$
which closes the induction. Resource-free assignments use $q_{e,t}=1$. These constants and updates have polynomial rational encoding length.

Thus a deterministic polynomial-time policy attains $1/2$, and
$$ R_{\mathrm{poly}}(1,0,B,T)=\frac12 $$
for every positive integer $T$.

6. A fractional packing obstruction below one half

The following construction uses a row-selection idea familiar from prophet-inequality lower bounds. The packing representation and the analysis of fractional allocations are proved here; an integral row-selection argument alone would not suffice. [Kleinberg and Weinberg, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.4764#S5)

Fix integers $d\ge2$, $N\ge1$, and a rational probability $p\in(0,1)$. Let the active resource capacity be $C\ge1$.

There are $N$ rows, each containing $d$ assignments. Assignment $(r,c)$ has reward one and its own online type. There are no offline assignments.

For every tuple
$$ u=(u_1,\ldots,u_N)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}^N, $$
create a resource of budget $C$. Assignment $(r,c)$ uses this resource with coefficient one exactly when $u_r=c$.

Thus each assignment uses precisely
$$ d^{N-1} $$
resources.

Process the $Nd$ assignments in row order. At the stage for $(r,c)$, its type arrives with unit mass with probability $p$; otherwise an unassignable type arrives. All stages are independent. Let $H_{r,c}$ be the corresponding Bernoulli indicator and $Z_{r,c}$ the allocated mass. Then
$$ 0\le Z_{r,c}\le H_{r,c}. $$

6.1 Exact characterization of fractional feasibility

Define
$$ M_r=\max_{1\le c\le d}Z_{r,c}. $$
The resource constraints are equivalent to
$$ \sum_{r=1}^N M_r\le C. $$

Indeed, every resource constraint has the form
$$ \sum_r Z_{r,u_r}\le C. $$
If $\sum_rM_r\le C$, all these constraints hold. Conversely, choose $u_r$ attaining the maximum in each row. The corresponding resource constraint gives $\sum_rM_r\le C$.

This equivalence holds for arbitrary fractional allocations.

6.2 Upper bound on every online policy

Let
$$ M_{r,c}=\max_{1\le i\le c}Z_{r,i}, \qquad M_{r,0}=0, $$
and
$$ \Delta_{r,c}=M_{r,c}-M_{r,c-1}\ge0. $$
Pointwise,
$$ Z_{r,c}\le H_{r,c}M_{r,c-1}+\Delta_{r,c}. $$
When $H_{r,c}=0$, both $Z_{r,c}$ and $\Delta_{r,c}$ are zero. When $H_{r,c}=1$, the right side equals $M_{r,c}$.

The variable $M_{r,c-1}$ depends only on the past and policy randomness, so it is independent of $H_{r,c}$. Hence
$$ \mathbb E[Z_{r,c}] \le p\,\mathbb E[M_{r,c-1}] + \mathbb E[\Delta_{r,c}]. $$
Summing within a row, using $M_{r,c-1}\le M_r$ and $M_{r,0}=0$, gives
$$ \sum_{c=1}^d\mathbb E[Z_{r,c}] \le \bigl(1+(d-1)p\bigr)\mathbb E[M_r]. $$
Finally, fractional feasibility implies
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \le C\bigl(1+(d-1)p\bigr). $$

6.3 A parameter-dependent upper-bound family

Choose integers $q\ge2$, $r\ge1$, and put
$$ p=1/q, \qquad h=\lceil C\rceil, \qquad N=hrq^d. $$
Assume $h2^{-r}<1$.

Partition the rows into $h$ groups of $rq^d$ rows. A row has all $d$ assignments available with probability $q^{-d}$. The probability that one group contains no such row is
$$ (1-q^{-d})^{rq^d}<2^{-r}. $$
To justify the inequality, for an integer $a\ge2$,
$$ (1-1/a)^{-a} = (1+1/(a-1))^a > 1+\frac{a}{a-1}>2. $$
Take $a=q^d$ and raise the resulting bound to the $r$th power.

A union bound shows that, with probability at least $1-h2^{-r}$, there are at least $h$ completely available rows. On this event clairvoyance assigns unit mass in $\lfloor C\rfloor$ such rows and mass $C-\lfloor C\rfloor$ in one additional row if needed. This is feasible and earns $Cd$.

Therefore
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}] \ge Cd(1-h2^{-r}), $$
and every policy has ratio at most
$$ \boxed{ \frac{1+(d-1)/q} {d(1-h2^{-r})}. } $$

There are two valid embeddings into the task’s model:

  • Put all active resources in $\mathcal K_1$ with $C=1$. This applies when

$$ \ell_1\ge d^{rq^d-1}, \qquad T\ge drq^d. $$
Add an unused second-class resource of budget $B$.

  • Put all active resources in $\mathcal K_2$ with $C=B$. This applies when

$$ \ell_2\ge d^{\lceil B\rceil rq^d-1}, \qquad T\ge d\lceil B\rceil rq^d. $$
Add an unused first-class resource.

Extra stages contain only unassignable mass. The resource list is explicit, although extremely large. Thus this is a valid family of finite inputs, rather than a succinctly represented resource system.

For example, take $C=1$, $d=3$, $q=8$, and $r=4$. Then
$$ N=2048, \qquad Nd=6144, $$
and the upper bound is
$$ \frac{1+2/8}{3(1-1/16)}=\frac49. $$
Consequently,
$$ R_{\mathrm{poly}}(\ell_1,\ell_2,B,T)\le\frac49 $$
whenever $\ell_1\ge3^{2047}$ and $T\ge6144$.

More generally, for fixed $C,d$, letting $q,r$ grow makes the displayed upper bound approach $1/d$. Taking $d$ arbitrarily large proves that no positive absolute competitive ratio is valid independently of sparsity and horizon. The construction does not give sharp dependence on sparsity: its required sparsity is very large.

7. A square-root budget obstruction with one active resource

Now suppose $\ell_1=0$, $\ell_2\ge1$, and $B=n$ is an integer at least three. Assume $T\ge2n$.

Use one active second-class resource of budget $n$, and an unused first-class resource. There is one offline assignment with unit cost and unit reward.

At each of the first $2n$ stages, independently, a unit mass of an online type arrives with probability $1/2$. Its only assignment has unit cost and reward two. Otherwise an unassignable type arrives. Remaining stages are unassignable.

Let
$$ N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(2n,1/2) $$
be the number of assignable stages.

7.1 Optimal online reward

For an offline choice $x\in[0,n]$, set $s=n-x$. The online reward is at most $2\min\{N,s\}$. Thus expected total reward is at most
$$ f(s)=n-s+2\mathbb E[\min\{N,s\}], \qquad 0\le s\le n. $$

For $s\in(j-1,j)$, where $1\le j\le n$,
$$ f'(s)=-1+2\Pr(N\ge j). $$
Symmetry of the binomial distribution gives
$$ \Pr(N\ge j)\ge\Pr(N\ge n)\ge\frac12. $$
Therefore $f$ is nondecreasing on $[0,n]$ and is maximized at $s=n$. This bounds randomized offline choices as well. Hence
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}] \le 2\mathbb E[\min\{N,n\}]. $$
The bound is attained by choosing no offline allocation and accepting online mass until capacity is exhausted.

7.2 Clairvoyant reward and the exact ratio bound

Clairvoyance accepts $\min\{N,n\}$ online units and fills the remaining capacity offline. Its reward is
$$ n+\min\{N,n\}. $$

Put
$$ a_n=\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n}. $$
I claim
$$ \mathbb E[|N-n|]=na_n. $$
By symmetry,
$$ \mathbb E[|N-n|] = \frac2{4^n} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)\binom{2n}{j}. $$
Using the convention $\binom{2n-1}{-1}=0$, the identity
$$ (n-j)\binom{2n}{j} = n\left( \binom{2n-1}{j} - \binom{2n-1}{j-1} \right) $$
makes the sum telescope. Since
$$ \binom{2n-1}{n-1} = \frac12\binom{2n}{n}, $$
the claim follows.

Again by symmetry,
$$ \mathbb E[\min\{N,n\}] = n-\frac12na_n. $$
Thus every policy’s ratio on this instance is at most
$$ \boxed{ \frac{4-2a_n}{4-a_n} = 1-\frac{a_n}{4-a_n}. } $$

Finally,
$$ a_n\ge\frac1{2\sqrt n}. $$
For $n=1$ equality holds. The recurrence
$$ \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{2n+1}{2n+2} \ge \sqrt{\frac n{n+1}} $$
follows by squaring, because $(2n+1)^2\ge4n(n+1)$. Induction proves the bound.

Consequently,
$$ \frac{a_n}{4-a_n}\ge\frac{a_n}{4} \ge\frac1{8\sqrt n}, $$
and
$$ \boxed{ R_{\mathrm{poly}}(0,\ell_2,n,T) \le 1-\frac1{8\sqrt n} \qquad (\ell_2\ge1,\ T\ge2n). } $$

For $n\ge64$, this is strictly stronger than $n/(n+1)$, because $n+1>8\sqrt n$. This obstruction uses only one active resource and two supported online types.

8. What remains open

The proofs establish improved general lower bounds, stronger upper bounds in several parameter regimes, and exact finite-parameter answers for $T=1$ and $(\ell_1,\ell_2)=(1,0)$.

They do not determine the optimal general function. In particular, the multi-resource upper construction does not match the sparsity dependence of the lower bounds, and the one-resource budget construction leaves the optimal finite-$B$, horizon-dependent ratio undetermined.
