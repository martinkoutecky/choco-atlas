statement extracted from the paper’s text layer; consult the paper for the exact wording

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given τ roles with rational masses μ_a>0 summing to 1, a symmetric nonnegative rational influence-density matrix J, type-specific thresholds θ_a^-, θ_a^+ and periodic schedules ℓ_a,ρ_a, and rational initial opinion shares x_a(0)∈[0,1], determine whether the population ever reaches a state after which no positive mass changes opinion at any future update. At time k, role a receives positive influence P_a(x(k))=∑_b μ_b J_{ab}x_b(k). When it listens, its mass holding opinion 1 changes to opinion 0 in amount x_a(k) if P_a<θ_a^+, and its mass holding opinion 0 changes to opinion 1 in amount 1-x_a(k) if P_a≥θ_a^-; otherwise the corresponding flow is zero. The equilibrium condition is zero transition flow, not merely an unchanged share vector.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) roles with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_a\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. There are \(m=2\) opinions. Input also includes a symmetric matrix \(J\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^{\tau\times\tau}\), nonnegative rational thresholds \(\theta_a^-,\theta_a^+\) with no ordering constraint, nonnegative integers \(\ell_a,\rho_a\), and rational shares \(x_a(0)\in[0,1]\). Diagonal entries of \(J\) represent influence within a role and are permitted.
At integer time \(k\geq0\), role \(a\) listens exactly when \(k=\ell_a+i(\rho_a+1)\) for some integer \(i\geq0\). Define \(P_a(x(k))=\sum_{b=1}^{\tau}\mu_bJ_{ab}x_b(k)\).
When listening, define outgoing share \(d_a(k)=x_a(k)\mathbf1\{P_a(x(k))<\theta_a^+\}\) and incoming share \(u_a(k)=(1-x_a(k))\mathbf1\{P_a(x(k))\geq\theta_a^-\}\); otherwise both are zero. All roles update simultaneously by \(x_a(k+1)=x_a(k)-d_a(k)+u_a(k)\), using the shares at time \(k\). Actual transition masses are \(\mu_ad_a(k)\) and \(\mu_au_a(k)\).
Eventual equilibrium means that some integer \(K\geq0\) satisfies \(d_a(k)=u_a(k)=0\) for every role \(a\) and every \(k\geq K\); opposing flows do not cancel for this definition. All numerical inputs are binary encoded; let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including schedules. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is deciding eventual equilibrium for this mass dynamics in \(\mathsf P\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents on undirected graphs with nonnegative integer edge weights, deciding eventual equilibrium under periodic asynchronous threshold dynamics is reported to be strongly \(\mathsf{PSPACE}\)-complete. [Source](https://doi.org/10.3390/appliedmath5010016)
No result settling exactly the mass problem defined above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made schedules nonnegative binary integers, with listening times starting at the offset and repeating every refractory period plus one; time and the repetition index start at zero.
  • Specified nonnegative rational thresholds, following the nonnegative threshold domains in the introductory definition; imposed no ordering or upper bound between thresholds.
  • Specified simultaneous updates one step after listening, with both directional flows evaluated from the pre-update shares.
  • Interpreted the stated flow amounts as within-role shares; actual population masses include the role mass factor.
  • Permitted diagonal influence as interactions between distinct individuals within the same positive-mass role.
  • Set m to two opinions and L to the total binary input length, including schedule integers.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Deciding eventual equilibrium is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even with equal dyadic masses, Boolean initial shares, integer influence entries, and zero diagonal. The problem also belongs to \(\mathsf{PSPACE}\).

The hardness proof below is direct and does not use the reported finite-agent hardness theorem.

1. Boolean shares and normalization.

If \(x_a(k)\in\{0,1\}\), the update keeps \(x_a(k+1)\) Boolean. At a listening time:

  • If \(x_a(k)=0\), it becomes \(1\) exactly when \(P_a(x(k))\geq\theta_a^-\).
  • If \(x_a(k)=1\), it becomes \(0\) exactly when \(P_a(x(k))<\theta_a^+\).

For Boolean shares, incoming and outgoing flows cannot both be positive. Consequently, a role changes its share exactly when it has a nonzero transition.

We will first construct a symmetric nonnegative integer matrix \(w\), with pressure \(\sum_b w_{ab}x_b\), and then normalize it into the required mass model.

2. Construction from a satisfiability instance.

Let \(\varphi\) be a formula in conjunctive normal form with variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\) and nonempty clauses \(D_1,\ldots,D_c\), where \(n,c\geq1\). Restricting to clauses of size three gives the usual \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard satisfiability problem.

Create the following roles:

  • Two literal roles \(v_i^1,v_i^0\) for each variable.
  • A clause role \(C_j\) for each clause.
  • A latch role \(S\) and a broadcast role \(R\).

There are \(r=2n+c+2\) such roles. Set \(A=2c+1\) and \(H=2nA+1\).

Define the symmetric matrix \(w\) through these undirected edges:

| Endpoints | Weight | Condition |
|---|---:|---|
| \(R,v_i^b\) | \(A\) | Every \(i\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\) |
| \(v_i^1,C_j\) | \(2\) | \(z_i\) occurs in \(D_j\) |
| \(v_i^0,C_j\) | \(2\) | \(\neg z_i\) occurs in \(D_j\) |
| \(C_j,S\) | \(1\) | Every \(j\) |
| \(S,R\) | \(H\) | Always |

Repeated occurrences of a literal give just one edge. All other entries, including diagonal entries, are zero.

Every role has \(\ell_a=0\). Thresholds, listening periods, and initial shares are:

| Role | \(\theta_a^-\) | \(\theta_a^+\) | Period \(\rho_a+1\) | Initial share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(v_i^1\) | \(0\) | \(A\) | \(2^{i-1}\) | \(0\) |
| \(v_i^0\) | \(0\) | \(A\) | \(2^{i-1}\) | \(1\) |
| \(C_j\) | \(2\) | \(2\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \(S\) | \(c\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \(R\) | \(H\) | \(H\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |

To obtain valid masses, let \(\tau\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(\tau\geq r\). Add \(\tau-r\) isolated roles, each initially \(0\), with thresholds \(\theta^-=1,\theta^+=0\) and period \(1\). These roles always have zero flow.

For every role, set \(\mu_a=1/\tau\), and define \(J_{ab}=\tau w_{ab}\). Then

\(\displaystyle P_a(x)=\sum_b\mu_bJ_{ab}x_b=\sum_bw_{ab}x_b.\)

Thus the normalization preserves every update exactly. The masses are positive dyadic rationals summing to \(1\), and \(J\) is symmetric and nonnegative.

The construction has polynomial size. Indeed, \(\tau<2r\), all weights and thresholds are polynomially bounded in \(n+c\), and the schedule number \(2^{i-1}-1\) requires only \(O(i)\) bits. The total output length is \(O((n+c)^2\log(n+c)+n^2)\).

3. Exact behavior of the constructed roles.

All shares remain Boolean, by the observation in Section 1.

First, a clause role implements its disjunction exactly. Its pressure is

\(\displaystyle P_{C_j}(x)=2\sum_{v_i^b\text{ representing a literal of }D_j}x_{v_i^b}+x_S.\)

Because \(x_S\in\{0,1\}\), this pressure reaches \(2\) exactly when at least one incident literal role has share \(1\). Both thresholds are \(2\), so, for every \(k\),

\(\displaystyle x_{C_j}(k+1)=\bigvee_{v_i^b\text{ representing a literal of }D_j}x_{v_i^b}(k).\)

Second, the broadcast role copies the latch exactly. Its pressure is

\(\displaystyle P_R(x)=Hx_S+A\sum_{i=1}^n(x_{v_i^1}+x_{v_i^0}).\)

The second term is at most \(2nA<H\). Hence \(P_R(x)\geq H\) exactly when \(x_S=1\), giving

\(\displaystyle x_R(k+1)=x_S(k).\)

Third, once the latch reaches \(1\), it stays there: its outgoing condition is \(P_S<0\), which is impossible. Its pressure is

\(\displaystyle P_S(x)=\sum_{j=1}^c x_{C_j}+Hx_R.\)

Whenever \(x_S(k)=0\), we also have \(x_R(k)=0\). For \(k=0\), this follows from initialization. For \(k\geq1\), it follows from \(x_R(k)=x_S(k-1)\) and the fact that \(S\) never decreases. Therefore, while \(S\) is \(0\), it activates exactly when every clause role is \(1\).

Finally, consider a literal role. Its clause-neighbor contribution is at most \(2c<A\).

  • If \(x_R=0\), a listening literal role toggles: \(0\) becomes \(1\) because its incoming threshold is \(0\), and \(1\) becomes \(0\) because its pressure is below \(A\).
  • If \(x_R=1\), a listening literal role becomes \(1\) and subsequently stays \(1\), because its pressure is at least \(A\).

These statements hold despite the symmetry of all edges.

4. The literal roles enumerate every assignment.

As long as \(R\) remains \(0\), the two roles for each variable toggle simultaneously and remain complementary.

For an integer time \(t\geq1\), role \(v_i^1\) has listened

\(\displaystyle 1+\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2^{i-1}}\right\rfloor\)

times before time \(t\). Since its initial share is \(0\),

\(\displaystyle x_{v_i^1}(t)=1-\left(\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2^{i-1}}\right\rfloor\bmod2\right),\qquad x_{v_i^0}(t)=1-x_{v_i^1}(t).\)

Thus, over \(t=1,\ldots,2^n\), the vector of positive literal shares visits every Boolean assignment: it is the coordinatewise complement of the binary expansion of \(t-1\).

This enumeration uses exponentially long periods, but their binary encodings have polynomial length.

5. Correctness of the reduction.

Suppose first that \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable. We prove that \(S\) and \(R\) remain \(0\).

Otherwise, let \(T\) be the first time with \(x_S(T)=1\). Since \(S\) was previously \(0\), the broadcast role was also \(0\) throughout the preceding times. Hence all literal pairs remained complementary.

The transition to \(x_S(T)=1\) requires every clause role at time \(T-1\) to be \(1\). The case \(T=1\) is impossible because all clause roles start at \(0\). For \(T\geq2\), the clause identities from Section 3 show that all clauses are satisfied by the single assignment represented by the literal shares at time \(T-2\). This contradicts unsatisfiability.

Consequently, \(S=R=0\) forever. In particular, \(v_1^1\), whose period is \(1\), toggles at every step. Each toggle has actual transition mass \(1/\tau>0\). Eventual equilibrium therefore does not occur.

Now suppose that \(\varphi\) is satisfiable. If \(S\) has already activated, proceed to the following paragraph. Otherwise, the enumeration from Section 4 reaches a satisfying assignment at some time \(t\). All clause roles are then \(1\) at time \(t+1\), so \(S\) is \(1\) by time \(t+2\).

Once \(S\) is \(1\), it remains \(1\), and \(R\) becomes permanently \(1\) one step later. Every literal role has a finite period, so each eventually listens with \(R=1\) and becomes permanently \(1\). Since every clause is nonempty, all clause roles subsequently become permanently \(1\).

At this final configuration, every transition is zero:

  • Literal roles have pressure at least \(A\).
  • Clause roles have pressure at least \(2\).
  • \(R\) has pressure at least \(H\).
  • \(S\) has outgoing threshold \(0\).
  • Isolated padding roles remain \(0\) with pressure \(0<1\).

Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \varphi\text{ is satisfiable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{the constructed mass dynamics reaches eventual equilibrium}.\)

This proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness.

6. Polynomial-space upper bound and running time.

This bound applies to arbitrary allowed rational initial shares, including fractional ones.

For a listening role, the four possibilities for its two threshold indicators give the updates

\(\displaystyle x_a\longmapsto x_a,\quad0,\quad1,\quad1-x_a.\)

Consequently, throughout the evolution,

\(\displaystyle x_a(k)\in\{x_a(0),1-x_a(0),0,1\}.\)

There are therefore at most \(4^\tau\) share vectors, and every share can be represented using two bits identifying one of these four input-derived values.

Schedules can be represented by countdowns. Initialize the countdown of role \(a\) to \(\ell_a\). At countdown \(0\), the role listens and its countdown resets to \(\rho_a\); otherwise, decrement it. Writing \(M_a=\max\{\ell_a,\rho_a\}\), there are at most

\(\displaystyle B=4^\tau\prod_{a=1}^{\tau}(M_a+1)\)

augmented states.

We must test zero flows, rather than merely unchanged shares. Define

\(\displaystyle \mathcal E(x)\Longleftrightarrow \bigwedge_{a=1}^{\tau} \left[(x_a=0\ \lor\ P_a(x)\geq\theta_a^+) \ \land\ (x_a=1\ \lor\ P_a(x)<\theta_a^-)\right].\)

If \(\mathcal E(x)\) holds, every listening event has zero incoming and outgoing flow, so the shares remain fixed forever.

Conversely, eventual equilibrium makes the shares constant from some time onward. Every role listens infinitely often. Applying the zero-flow requirement at a future listening time for each role proves \(\mathcal E(x)\) for that constant vector. Thus eventual equilibrium is equivalent to reaching a vector satisfying \(\mathcal E\).

Simulate the first \(B\) augmented states and test \(\mathcal E\) at each. If none satisfies it, some augmented state must repeat before any new state can occur; determinism then prevents reaching \(\mathcal E\) later.

Each pressure can be computed exactly with integers of \(O(L)\) bits: shares never acquire new denominators, and a common denominator for a row has bit length bounded by the total input length. Using elementary integer arithmetic, a simulation step and equilibrium test take \(O(\tau^2L^2)\) time.

Moreover, \(\log_2B\leq2\tau+L\). For the specified \(m=2\), this gives time

\(\displaystyle O(\tau^2L^2B)\subseteq 2^{O(L)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\)

and space polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Hence the problem belongs to \(\mathsf{PSPACE}\).

The target question is therefore settled: it is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard. A polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}\).

Step-by-step verification

1. Boolean dynamics

The Boolean update rules are correct.

For a listening role with share \(0\), outgoing flow is zero and incoming flow is either \(0\) or \(1\). For a role with share \(1\), incoming flow is zero and outgoing flow is either \(0\) or \(1\). Thus Boolean shares remain Boolean, and a nonzero flow occurs exactly when the share changes.

This observation is used only for the hardness construction. The upper bound separately handles fractional shares.

2. Construction and normalization

The construction produces a symmetric, nonnegative integer matrix with zero diagonal. Identifying repeated literal occurrences with a single edge preserves clause truth, since repetitions do not affect disjunction.

The stated constants satisfy the required strict inequalities:

  • \(2c<A=2c+1\).
  • \(2nA<H=2nA+1\).

Padding to a power of two gives \(r\leq\tau<2r\). Every role, including each padding role, has positive mass \(1/\tau\), so \(\tau\) indeed counts supported types. These masses are dyadic and sum to \(1\).

Setting \(J_{ab}=\tau w_{ab}\) gives exactly
\(P_a(x)=\sum_b w_{ab}x_b\).
Consequently, the thresholds in the construction need no further rescaling.

The padding roles remain at \(0\): their pressure is always \(0\), their incoming condition fails, and their outgoing share is zero.

The construction has polynomial output length. In particular, the period \(2^{i-1}\) is represented through \(\rho_a=2^{i-1}-1\), whose binary encoding has \(O(i)\) bits. The dense matrix representation, thresholds, masses, and schedules fit the claimed polynomial bound.

The source problem is satisfiability of three-literal CNF formulas. Requiring at least one variable and one nonempty clause does not invalidate the standard \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard source restriction.

3. Behavior of the gadgets

Clause roles. Their pressure is twice the number of active incident literal roles, plus \(x_S\). Since \(x_S\) is Boolean, its contribution alone cannot reach threshold \(2\). An active incident literal always reaches that threshold. Equal incoming and outgoing thresholds therefore make each clause role copy its clause’s disjunction with exactly one step of delay.

Broadcast role. Its literal contribution is at most \(2nA<H\). Therefore its pressure reaches \(H\) exactly when \(x_S=1\), regardless of the literal configuration. The identity \(x_R(k+1)=x_S(k)\) is valid.

Latch role. Its outgoing condition is impossible because pressure is nonnegative and its outgoing threshold is \(0\). Hence its share never decreases. Initialization and the broadcast identity imply that \(x_S(k)=0\) forces \(x_R(k)=0\). While the latch is zero, its pressure is consequently the number of active clause roles, and it activates exactly when all \(c\) clauses are active.

Literal roles. Their clause contribution is at most \(2c<A\). When \(R=0\), a listening role therefore toggles. When \(R=1\), the broadcast contribution is already \(A\), so a listening role becomes \(1\) and stays there.

These checks account for influence in both directions along every symmetric edge. No gadget relies on directed influence.

4. Assignment enumeration

The timing calculation matches the specified simultaneous update convention.

A role with period \(2^{i-1}\), listening first at time \(0\), has listened
\(1+\left\lfloor (t-1)/2^{i-1}\right\rfloor\)
times before the state at time \(t\), for \(t\geq1\).

While \(R\) remains zero, each literal pair toggles together and remains complementary. The parity calculation in the output is correct: the positive literal share is the complement of bit \(i-1\) of \(t-1\).

Thus the states at times \(1,\ldots,2^n\) visit every Boolean assignment, provided broadcasting has not already stopped the enumeration. Earlier latch activation already gives the successful outcome needed by the reduction.

5. Correctness of both reduction directions

Unsatisfiable formulas. The first-activation argument correctly handles the delays.

If \(T\) were the first time with \(x_S(T)=1\), then \(R\) was zero at all earlier times, and the literal pairs remained complementary. Activation at \(T=1\) is impossible because every clause starts at zero. For \(T\geq2\), all clauses being active at time \(T-1\) means that the single literal assignment at time \(T-2\) satisfies every clause. This contradicts unsatisfiability.

Therefore \(S\) and \(R\) remain zero. The period-one literal role toggles forever, with positive actual transition mass \(1/\tau\), ruling out eventual equilibrium under the task’s zero-flow definition.

Satisfiable formulas. If activation has not already occurred, enumeration supplies a satisfying assignment at some time \(t\). The clauses copy it at time \(t+1\), and the latch activates by time \(t+2\). These delays correctly use the old state at each simultaneous update.

After activation, the latch remains one and broadcasting becomes permanently one. Every literal role has a finite period, so each eventually listens and becomes permanently one. Nonempty clauses then become permanently one.

The final zero-flow check is complete. All nonpadding roles have share one; their incoming flows vanish automatically, and the stated pressures or outgoing threshold prevent outgoing flows. Padding roles have neither flow.

The equivalence between satisfiability and eventual equilibrium is therefore established.

6. Polynomial-space upper bound

The four possible updates for arbitrary rational shares are correctly listed as \(x_a\), \(0\), \(1\), and \(1-x_a\). The set
\(\{x_a(0),1-x_a(0),0,1\}\)
is closed under these operations, giving at most \(4^\tau\) share vectors. Coincident values only reduce this count.

The countdown implementation matches the schedules, including \(\ell_a=0\) and \(\rho_a=0\). Each countdown remains between zero and \(M_a=\max\{\ell_a,\rho_a\}\), so the augmented-state bound
\(B=4^\tau\prod_a(M_a+1)\)
is valid.

The predicate \(\mathcal E\) correctly expresses zero outgoing and incoming flow for every role if it listens. It detects opposing positive flows even when they cancel in the share update.

Both directions of the reachability characterization are justified:

  • Reaching \(\mathcal E\) makes the shares remain fixed and all future flows zero.
  • Eventual equilibrium makes the shares constant; every role listens infinitely often, so that constant vector must satisfy \(\mathcal E\).

Examining the first \(B\) augmented states suffices. If none satisfies \(\mathcal E\), the next state cannot be new, and deterministic repetition prevents later success.

Exact pressures require only \(O(L)\)-bit integers: the possible shares have input-derived denominators, and the denominators and numerators involved in a row have total bit length \(O(L)\).
