The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(A\) and \(B\), rational masses \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\) summing to one on each side, an acceptable-pair graph \(E\), and acyclic pairwise preferences for every type, does there exist a flow \(x_{ab}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_b x_{ab}\leq\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\leq\nu_b\) that is super-stable? For each \((a,b)\in E\), let \(Q_a(b;x)\) be the unmatched mass of \(a\) plus its mass assigned to types other than \(b\) that \(a\) does not strictly prefer to \(b\), and define \(Q_b(a;x)\) symmetrically; require \(Q_a(b;x)Q_b(a;x)=0\) for every acceptable pair.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be disjoint finite type supports, with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_a,\nu_b\) satisfying \(\sum_{a\in A}\mu_a=\sum_{b\in B}\nu_b=1\), and an acceptable-pair graph \(E\subseteq A\times B\).
A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass and their binary rational masses. Set \(\tau=|A|+|B|\), the support size rather than the number of possible types, \(m=|E|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
For each type \(v\), an explicitly listed directed acyclic relation \(\succ_v\) on its acceptable neighbors specifies strict preference: \(c\succ_v d\) means that \(v\) strictly prefers \(c\) to \(d\). Missing comparisons mean incomparability; transitivity is not required and no transitive closure is taken. Every acceptable partner is strictly preferred to being unmatched.
A flow has real entries \(x_{ab}\geq0\) for \((a,b)\in E\), with \(\sum_{b\in N(a)}x_{ab}\leq\mu_a\) and \(\sum_{a\in N(b)}x_{ab}\leq\nu_b\); assignments outside \(E\) are forbidden.
Define \(Q_a(b;x)=\mu_a-\sum_{c\in N(a)}x_{ac}+\sum_{c\in N(a)\setminus\{b\}:\,\neg(c\succ_a b)}x_{ac}\) and \(Q_b(a;x)=\nu_b-\sum_{d\in N(b)}x_{db}+\sum_{d\in N(b)\setminus\{a\}:\,\neg(d\succ_b a)}x_{db}\).
Call the flow super-stable if \(Q_a(b;x)Q_b(a;x)=0\) for every \((a,b)\in E\), including pairs carrying positive flow; mass assigned to the tested partner is excluded from both quantities.

Is deciding existence of a super-stable flow in this model solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete agents with acyclic pairwise preferences on both sides, super-stable matching existence is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even when every agent has at most four acceptable partners ([Cseh–Juhos](https://hpi.de/friedrich/docs/publications/2021/TEAC.pdf)).
No result settling this precise mass-flow problem was located in the literature search.

  • Forbade assignments outside the acceptable-pair graph and made every acceptable partner strictly preferable to being unmatched.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when all masses are equal dyadic rationals and every type has at most four acceptable partners.

The essential point is that choosing a zero endpoint for each stability constraint leaves a flow problem with saturation requirements and forbidden edges. With equal masses, this problem has an integral solution whenever it has a real solution.

1. What a zero stability quantity requires

Write \(p_v\) for the mass of type \(v\), and \(x_{vu}\) for the flow on an incident acceptable pair. Define the unmatched mass by
\[ \]
\(r_v(x)=p_v-\sum_{c\in N(v)}x_{vc}\).

For an acceptable neighbor \(u\), the quantity in the question is

\(Q_v(u;x)=r_v(x)+\sum_{\substack{c\in N(v)\setminus\{u\}\\ \neg(c\succ_v u)}}x_{vc}\).

Every summand is nonnegative for a feasible flow. Consequently,

\(Q_v(u;x)=0\)

holds if and only if both of the following conditions hold:

  • \(v\) is saturated: \(\sum_{c\in N(v)}x_{vc}=p_v\).
  • \(x_{vc}=0\) for every \(c\ne u\) for which \(c\succ_v u\) is not explicitly listed.

This equivalence uses the given relation directly; it takes no transitive closure. In particular, the tested edge \(vu\) is not forbidden by this condition.

Because both stability quantities are nonnegative, their product is zero exactly when at least one is zero.

Thus, choose one endpoint \(\sigma(e)\) of every acceptable edge \(e=vu\). Impose \(Q_v(u;x)=0\) when \(\sigma(e)=v\). Let \(R\) be the set of endpoints selected at least once, and let \(F\) contain every edge forbidden by any of the selected conditions.

Existence of a super-stable flow is therefore equivalent to existence of an endpoint selection and a feasible flow such that

\(\sum_{u\in N(v)}x_{vu}=p_v\quad\text{for every }v\in R,\qquad x_e=0\quad\text{for every }e\in F.\)

Indeed, a super-stable flow supplies such a selection by choosing a zero endpoint on every edge. Conversely, these saturation and zero constraints make the selected stability quantity zero on every edge, including edges carrying positive flow.

2. Feasibility for a fixed endpoint selection

For fixed \(R,F\), construct a directed network with vertices \(s,t\) and all types. Its arcs and lower and upper bounds are:

| Arc | Lower bound | Upper bound |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(s\to a\), for \(a\in A\) | \(\mu_a\) if \(a\in R\), otherwise \(0\) | \(\mu_a\) |
| \(a\to b\), for \(ab\in E\setminus F\) | \(0\) | \(1\) |
| \(b\to t\), for \(b\in B\) | \(\nu_b\) if \(b\in R\), otherwise \(0\) | \(\nu_b\) |
| \(t\to s\) | \(0\) | \(1\) |

A circulation means an assignment satisfying these bounds and conservation at every vertex.

Every desired assignment \(x\) gives a circulation: send its incident total on each \(s\to a\) and \(b\to t\), and its total assigned mass on \(t\to s\). All upper bounds of \(1\) are valid because total assigned mass is at most \(1\).

Conversely, conservation at each type turns any circulation into a feasible assignment with precisely the required saturation and forbidden-edge conditions.

We will also need the following integrality fact.

Lemma. A finite directed network with integer lower and upper bounds has an integer feasible circulation whenever it has a real feasible circulation.

Proof. Let the bounds on arc \(e\) be \(\ell_e,u_e\). Subtract the lower bounds and put

\(d(v)=\sum_{e\text{ entering }v}\ell_e-\sum_{e\text{ leaving }v}\ell_e.\)

The residual arc capacities are \(u_e-\ell_e\). Add a source \(S\), an arc \(S\to v\) of capacity \(d(v)\) when \(d(v)>0\), and an arc \(v\to T\) of capacity \(-d(v)\) when \(d(v)<0\).

Since \(\sum_v d(v)=0\), feasible circulations correspond exactly to flows saturating all arcs leaving \(S\). To check this correspondence, saturation and conservation imply that residual flow leaving \(v\) minus residual flow entering \(v\) equals \(d(v)\), which is exactly the conservation equation after subtracting lower bounds.

All capacities are integers. Starting with zero flow, an augmenting-path algorithm preserves integrality, because every augmentation uses an integer bottleneck. It terminates at a maximum flow; optimality follows from the usual reachable-vertex cut when no augmenting path remains. If a real feasible circulation exists, the maximum value equals the total capacity leaving \(S\), so the resulting integer maximum flow saturates those arcs. Adding back the integer lower bounds proves the claim. \(\square\)

3. Equal masses permit rounding to a matching

Proposition. Suppose both sides have \(N\) types and every type has mass \(1/N\). A super-stable flow exists if and only if a discrete super-stable matching exists on the same graph and preferences.

Proof. First suppose a super-stable flow exists. Select a zero endpoint on every edge, as in Section 1. The corresponding circulation network is feasible.

Multiply all its bounds by \(N\). Every bound is now an integer: type arcs have upper bound \(1\), required type arcs have lower bound \(1\), and the remaining upper bounds are \(N\). By the lemma, there is an integer feasible circulation in this scaled network.

Let \(y_{ab}\) be its flow on \(a\to b\). At each type the incident total is at most \(1\). Hence every \(y_{ab}\) belongs to \(\{0,1\}\), and

\(M=\{ab\in E:y_{ab}=1\}\)

is a matching. The assignment \(y/N\) satisfies the same saturation and forbidden-edge constraints as the original flow. Section 1 therefore shows that \(y/N\) is super-stable.

To verify its discrete interpretation explicitly, for any tested neighbor \(u\),

\(Q_v(u;y/N)=0\)

exactly when either \(M(v)=u\), or \(v\) is matched to a different neighbor \(c\) satisfying \(c\succ_v u\). If \(v\) is unmatched, the quantity is \(1/N\); if its different matched partner is not explicitly preferred to \(u\), the quantity is also \(1/N\).

Consequently, every edge outside \(M\) has an endpoint whose matched partner is strictly preferred to the other endpoint. This is precisely discrete super-stability.

Conversely, given a discrete super-stable matching \(M\), assign mass \(1/N\) to each matching edge and zero elsewhere. On a matching edge both stability quantities are zero, because mass assigned to the tested partner is excluded. On any other edge, discrete super-stability gives an endpoint matched to an explicitly preferred partner, so that endpoint’s quantity is zero. Thus the assignment is a super-stable flow. \(\square\)

4. Polynomial reduction proving hardness

We use the stated discrete hardness result: Cseh–Juhos, Theorem 5.5, proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of super-stable matching existence with acyclic pairwise preferences on both sides and maximum acceptable degree four. Their definition of acyclic preferences permits missing comparisons along directed paths, so it matches the present convention without transitive closure. Their Definition 5.1 uses the same blocking condition for edges outside the matching. [Cseh–Juhos](https://hpi.de/friedrich/docs/publications/2021/TEAC.pdf)

Take an instance of that problem, with sides \(U,W\). Choose the smallest power of two \(N\) satisfying

\(N\ge \max\{1,|U|,|W|\}.\)

Add isolated vertices until both sides have \(N\) vertices. These vertices affect neither matching existence nor stability, because they have no acceptable edges.

Make each vertex a distinct type, copy all acceptable edges and explicitly listed comparisons, and give every type mass \(1/N\). These are positive dyadic rationals, and the masses on each side sum to \(1\). Acyclicity and maximum acceptable degree four are preserved.

By the proposition, the constructed society admits a super-stable flow exactly when the original discrete instance admits a super-stable matching.

If the original support size and input length are \(\tau_0,L_0\), the output satisfies \(\tau=O(\tau_0)\), preserves \(m\), and has bit length \(O(L_0+\tau_0\log(\tau_0+1))\). The reduction therefore runs in polynomial time. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under the asserted restrictions.

5. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) and running time

A certificate consists of the \(m\) endpoint choices. Sections 1 and 2 reduce verification to rational circulation feasibility on a network with \(O(\tau)\) vertices and \(O(m+\tau)\) arcs.

To perform this verification exactly, multiply all bounds by the product of the denominators of the input masses. This product has \(O(L)\) bits. The scaled capacities and the auxiliary capacities in the lemma also have \(O(L)\) bits. Thus scaling does not require expanding the society into individual agents.

After the lower-bound transformation, Edmonds–Karp uses \(O(\tau(m+\tau)^2)\) arithmetic operations. Building the forbidden-edge set requires at most \(O(m^2)\) direct comparison lookups. Including input processing and exact integer arithmetic, a conservative total verification bound is

\(O((m+\tau+L)^5)\)

bit operations. Hence the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Enumerating all endpoint choices also gives an exact deterministic algorithm with running time

\(O(2^m(m+\tau+L)^5).\)

Together with the reduction, this establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. In particular, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
