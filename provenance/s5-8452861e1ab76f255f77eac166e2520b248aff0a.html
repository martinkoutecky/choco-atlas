The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational role masses , finite-support value priors , and a per-capita service capacity , consider a mass-feasible allocation policy with a tagged-agent oracle: on input a role, a report, and the background report-mass profile, it returns the tagged person’s service probability and a feasible allocation of the remaining mass. Can one construct BIC allocation and payment rules that preserve capacity and achieve expected welfare at least less than the policy’s expected welfare, using oracle calls polynomial in support size , input bit length , and , independent of the total population?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq1\) publicly observable roles with positive rational masses \(\alpha_r\), summing to \(1\). Role \(r\) has a common-knowledge prior \(F_r\) with finite rational support \(V_r\subseteq[0,1]\) and positive rational probabilities \(f_r(v)\). The truthful nonatomic society has masses \(h^*_{r,v}=\alpha_rf_r(v)\); an individual's report does not change the background masses.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here a type is a role–value pair, and \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Reports belong to \([0,1]\), roles cannot be misreported, and background report profiles \(h\) have finite rational support with \(\sum_bh_{r,b}=\alpha_r\). Service has zero cost and rational per-capita capacity \(\kappa\in(0,1]\). A feasible allocation assigns service probabilities \(x_{r,b}\in[0,1]\) satisfying \(\sum_{r,b}h_{r,b}x_{r,b}\leq\kappa\) in every realization.
An arbitrary randomized policy \(A\) is accessible only through a tagged-agent oracle: on input \((r,b,h)\), it returns the tagged person's service probability and a feasible allocation of the background mass. These responses must come from one common policy, treating people with the same role and report identically; on occupied cells, the tagged probability agrees with the background allocation. Rational oracle replies have bit length polynomial in the query length, and each call has unit cost.
Mechanisms use the same allocation model and quasi-linear, risk-neutral utilities. Require exact BIC against all reports in \([0,1]\) for values in \(V_r\), with truthful background \(h^*\); payments may be signed and are normalized to zero for report \(0\). Expected per-capita welfare is \(W(M)=\mathbb E[\sum_{r,v}h^*_{r,v}v x^M_{r,v}]\), including all mechanism randomization.

For every rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), can every such policy \(A\) be converted through its oracle into a capacity-feasible BIC allocation and payment mechanism \(M\) with \(W(M)\geq W(A)-\varepsilon\), using expected time and oracle calls polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\), independently of any finite population size, or is this additive guarantee impossible with those resources?

For finite independent single-parameter agents with values in \([0,1]\), a black-box BIC conversion preserves expected welfare up to additive \(\varepsilon\) in time polynomial in \(n,1/\varepsilon\), even with general allocation costs ([Hartline–Lucier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.4756v2)).
No result settling this exact nonatomic tagged-agent oracle problem was identified in the literature search.

Conclusion

Yes. Under the stated allocation model, a mechanism can achieve \(W(M)\geq W(A)\) with zero oracle calls and polynomial computation.

The key premise is that feasibility is the explicitly known capacity inequality. The problem does not require the converted mechanism to use only allocations returned by \(A\). Consequently, we can construct a welfare-maximizing feasible mechanism directly.

Construction on every background

Fix any admissible background \(h\). List its distinct occupied reports in decreasing order as \(\beta_1>\cdots>\beta_s\), and define their aggregate masses by

\(H_i=\sum_r h_{r,\beta_i}\).

These masses are positive and sum to \(1\). Since \(0<\kappa\leq1\), there exists a smallest index \(j\) such that \(\sum_{i=1}^j H_i\geq\kappa\). Define

\(c_h=\beta_j,\qquad S_h=\sum_{i<j}H_i,\qquad q_h=(\kappa-S_h)/H_j\).

Minimality of \(j\) implies \(S_h<\kappa\leq S_h+H_j\), so \(0<q_h\leq1\).

For every role \(r\) and every report \(b\in[0,1]\), including unoccupied reports, prescribe:

  • \(x^M_{r,b}(h)=1\) if \(b>c_h\);
  • \(x^M_{r,b}(h)=q_h\) if \(b=c_h\);
  • \(x^M_{r,b}(h)=0\) if \(b<c_h\).

Define the expected payment by

\(p^M_{r,b}(h)=c_h x^M_{r,b}(h)\).

This payment can be implemented by charging \(c_h\) when service is received and zero otherwise.

The same rule defines both tagged probabilities and background allocations, so they agree on occupied cells. It treats equal role–report pairs identically.

The background allocation is feasible because

\(\sum_{r,b}h_{r,b}x^M_{r,b}(h)=S_h+q_hH_j=\kappa\).

The allocation vector is deterministic and satisfies the required inequality. Also, payments are normalized correctly: if \(c_h>0\), report \(0\) receives no service and pays zero; if \(c_h=0\), every payment is zero. Thus \(p^M_{r,0}(h)=0\).

Exact incentive compatibility

Fix a background \(h\), a role \(r\), and a true value \(v\in[0,1]\). The nonatomic premise ensures that the tagged person’s report does not change \(h\), hence does not change \(c_h\) or \(q_h\).

Expected utility from reporting \(b\) is

\(u_v(b;h)=v x^M_{r,b}(h)-p^M_{r,b}(h)=(v-c_h)x^M_{r,b}(h)\).

There are three cases.

  • If \(v>c_h\), truthful reporting yields service probability \(1\). Because \(v-c_h>0\) and every service probability is at most \(1\), no other report yields greater utility.
  • If \(v<c_h\), truthful reporting yields service probability \(0\) and utility zero. Because \(v-c_h<0\) and service probabilities are nonnegative, every other report yields utility at most zero.
  • If \(v=c_h\), every report yields utility zero.

Therefore truthful reporting is optimal for every value and every report in \([0,1]\), at every fixed admissible background. In particular, the required exact BIC inequalities hold for every \(v\in V_r\) with background \(h^*\). This argument includes irrational deviations and deviations outside the prior support.

Welfare optimality

Apply the construction to the truthful background \(h^*\), and abbreviate its cutoff and allocation by \(c=c_{h^*}\) and \(x^M_{r,v}=x^M_{r,v}(h^*)\).

Let \(y\) be any feasible allocation at this background. Since \(c\geq0\),

\(\sum_{r,v}h^*_{r,v}v y_{r,v} =c\sum_{r,v}h^*_{r,v}y_{r,v} +\sum_{r,v}h^*_{r,v}(v-c)y_{r,v} \leq c\kappa+\sum_{r,v:\,v>c}h^*_{r,v}(v-c)\).

Here the capacity inequality bounds the first term. For the second term, cells with \(v<c\) contribute nonpositively, cells with \(v=c\) contribute zero, and cells with \(v>c\) satisfy \(y_{r,v}\leq1\).

Our mechanism attains this upper bound. It uses exactly capacity \(\kappa\), serves every cell with \(v>c\) fully, and serves no cell with \(v<c\). Hence

\(W(M)=c\kappa+\sum_{r,v:\,v>c}h^*_{r,v}(v-c)\).

Every allocation realization of \(A\) is feasible, so the preceding upper bound applies to each realization. Taking expectations proves

\(W(A)\leq W(M)\).

Thus \(W(M)\geq W(A)-\varepsilon\) for every requested \(\varepsilon\).

Computational resources

The truthful masses \(h^*_{r,v}=\alpha_r f_r(v)\) are rational and computable in polynomial time. Computing the cutoff requires sorting at most \(\tau\) values, aggregating masses, and computing rational prefix sums and one quotient. These operations have polynomial bit complexity in \(m,\tau,L\).

The output mechanism is the explicit rule above. On any supplied background, evaluating it likewise takes polynomial time in that background’s encoding length; at \(h^*\), this is polynomial in the original input size. Reports outside the support require only comparison with the rational cutoff.

Construction uses no oracle calls and no approximation. Its deterministic polynomial running time therefore satisfies the requested expected-time bound, independently of any finite population size.
