The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite male and female type sets \(A,B\), rational masses \(\lambda_a,\nu_b\), male rankings \(R_a\) over \(B\), and each female type's true and reported rankings \(P_b,Q_b\) over \(A\), compute the reported-profile deferred-acceptance flow \(x^0\). For each \(b\), split off tagged mass \(\epsilon\) with true ranking \(P_b\) and an arbitrary report \(Q'_b\); run a specified type-level deferred-acceptance rule on the split population, including an explicit rule for male-side indifference between the tagged and ordinary parts. Decide whether some \(b,Q'_b\) gives a flow that is \(P\)-stable for every sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\) and satisfies \(\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_a x^\epsilon_{a,b,*}\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a)<\frac{1}{\nu_b}\sum_a x^0_{ab}\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a)\), where \(P\)-stability means no type pair has positive mass on both sides preferring each other to their assigned partners.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be nonempty finite sets of male and female types, with positive binary-rational masses satisfying \(\sum_a\lambda_a=\sum_b\nu_b=1\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) positive-mass types, not the universe of possible preference types. Put \(m=\max\{|A|,|B|\}\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Each male type has a complete strict true ranking \(R_a\) over \(B\), which it reports truthfully; each female type has complete strict true and reported rankings \(P_b,Q_b\) over \(A\). Every partner is acceptable. A feasible flow is nonnegative and has row sums \(\lambda_a\) and column sums \(\nu_b\).
Use the following deterministic mass deferred-acceptance rule. Initially all male mass is free. Repeatedly select the first male type with free mass in the supplied support order; it offers all its free mass to its highest-ranked receiver that has never rejected any of its mass. That receiver pools the offer with its holdings, retains its capacity of highest-ranked male mass (or the whole pool if smaller), and rejects the remainder. Rejected mass becomes free, and that male type permanently skips that receiver for subsequent offers. Stop when no mass is free; holdings give the flow. Let \(x^0\) be the outcome with reports \(Q_b\).
For a chosen \(b\), replace its mass by ordinary mass \(\nu_b-\epsilon\), reporting \(Q_b\), and tagged mass \(\epsilon\), reporting a complete strict permutation \(Q'_b\) of \(A\), where \(0<\epsilon<\nu_b\). Both parts retain true ranking \(P_b\). For deferred acceptance, every male replaces \(b\) in its list by the ordinary part followed immediately by the tagged part; in true preferences those two parts are indifferent. All other reports stay fixed. Denote the resulting flow by \(x^\epsilon\), and its tagged column by \(x^\epsilon_{a,b,*}\).
Write \(\pi(c)\) for a receiver's original female type. A flow is \(P\)-stable if there are no male types \(a,a'\) and receivers \(c,d\) with \(x_{ad}>0\), \(x_{a'c}>0\), \(\pi(c)\succ_{R_a}\pi(d)\), and \(a\succ_{P_{\pi(c)}}a'\). Let \(\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a)\) be the position of \(a\), with position \(1\) best.
Decide whether there exist \(b\), a single report \(Q'_b\) independent of \(\epsilon\), and \(\delta\in(0,\nu_b)\) such that \(x^\epsilon\) is \(P\)-stable for every real \(\epsilon\in(0,\delta)\), and the finite limit satisfies \(\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_a x^\epsilon_{a,b,*}\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a)<\frac{1}{\nu_b}\sum_a x^0_{ab}\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a)\). There is no promise that \(x^0\) is \(P\)-stable.
Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For discrete societies with \(n\) men and \(n\) women, complete strict lists, and truthful male reports, existence of a unilateral female permutation yielding a truly stable matching and a strictly better partner is decidable in \(O(n^4)\) time ([Gupta–Iwama–Miyazaki, 2016](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol053-swat2016/LIPIcs.SWAT.2016.23/LIPIcs.SWAT.2016.23.pdf)). No result settling the exact tagged-mass decision problem specified here was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified positive masses and equal total mass, normalized to one on each side, to preserve the balanced, fully matched discrete domain.
  • Required complete strict rankings and permutation-only deviations, with all partners acceptable and truthful male reports.
  • Specified a deterministic sequential mass deferred-acceptance rule, using support order to schedule male proposals and permanent skipping after any rejection.
  • Resolved male indifference during deferred acceptance by placing ordinary mass immediately before tagged mass; retained true indifference between those parts when testing stability.
  • Made stability apply separately to ordinary and tagged receivers using strict true preferences and positive assigned masses.
  • Quantified over all sufficiently small real positive epsilon, required one report independent of epsilon, and required the displayed limit to exist finitely.
  • Defined rank positions, flow marginals, support size, m, and total input bit length L.
  • Retained the graded question's unrestricted baseline rather than adding a baseline true-stability promise, following the anchor algorithm's actual existential test.

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). A conservative bound is \(O(m^4\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

The proof has two main ingredients:

  • There is an explicitly computable interval near zero, uniform over all tagged reports, on which each report produces an affine flow with constant support. The tagged column contains exactly one male type.
  • All attainable tagged partner types, together with their resulting flows, can be found using at most \(|A|\) deferred-acceptance computations per chosen female type.

Neither argument assumes that \(x^0\) is \(P\)-stable.

1. The stable-allocation result used

Write \(p=|A|\) and \(q=|B|\). For a fixed choice of \(b\), denote its ordinary and tagged receivers by \(b_{\mathrm{o}}\) and \(b_*\). Let \(R_a^b\) be the strict receiver ranking obtained by replacing \(b\) with \(b_{\mathrm{o}},b_*\), in that order.

Here, report stability means the absence of a blocking quartet using the strict rankings \(R_a^b\) and the receivers’ reported rankings. This differs from the required \(P\)-stability, which uses original female types.

We use the following established stable-allocation theorem. For a bipartite instance with strict rankings and divisible real vertex quotas, mass deferred acceptance terminates and returns the unique report-stable allocation whose allocation vector at each proposing vertex, ordered by that vertex’s ranking, is lexicographically maximal among all report-stable allocations. This allocation can be computed in \(O(EV)\) arithmetic operations, where \(E\) and \(V\) are the numbers of edges and vertices. See [Dean–Munshi, Lemmas 1 and 5 and Section 3](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • The graph is finite and complete, and all reported rankings are strict.
  • The quotas are positive and the two sides have equal total quota.
  • There are no effective edge bounds. If the stable-allocation formulation uses the redundant bound \(x_{ac}\leq\min\{\lambda_a,\nu_c\}\), any blocking quartet already implies strict inequality in this bound.
  • Permanent skipping after rejection agrees with mass deferred acceptance: once \(c\) rejects some mass of \(a\), it is full with types ranked at least as high as \(a\), and its retained pool only improves subsequently. An additional offer from \(a\) cannot increase its holdings of \(a\).

Offers to receivers that cannot accept additional mass merely produce immediate rejection and do not change the flow. Thus the theorem computes the outcome of the supplied deterministic rule, including for real quotas. Completeness and equal total quota ensure that every row and column is filled.

For brevity, call this unique allocation the male-optimal flow, with optimality understood in the stated rowwise lexicographic sense.

2. Every male-optimal flow has forest support

The support graph of a flow has an edge \(ac\) exactly when \(x_{ac}>0\).

Lemma 1. The support graph of every male-optimal flow is a forest.

Proof. Suppose its support contains a simple undirected cycle. Add and subtract a sufficiently small positive amount alternately around that cycle. Either choice of sign preserves all row sums, column sums, and positive support.

Report stability depends only on positive support, so both perturbed flows are report-stable.

Choose a male vertex on the cycle. Its two cycle edges lead to distinct receivers, which it ranks strictly. Choose the perturbation sign that increases its allocation to the preferred receiver. All earlier coordinates of its allocation vector remain unchanged, so its row becomes lexicographically larger. This contradicts male optimality. \(\square\)

3. A uniform interval for all tagged reports

Choose an integer \(D\) such that every original mass is an integer multiple of \(1/D\). For example, take the product of their encoded denominators. Then \(\log D=O(L)\), and every positive original mass is at least \(1/D\).

Define

\( \displaystyle I=\left(0,\frac{1}{2D}\right),\qquad \eta=\frac{1}{4D}. \)

In particular, \(\eta<\nu_b\) for every \(b\).

Fix \(b\) and a tagged report \(H\). Only the capacities of \(b_{\mathrm{o}}\) and \(b_*\) depend on \(\epsilon\): they are \(\nu_b-\epsilon\) and \(\epsilon\).

Forest flows have simple affine coordinates

Suppose a forest \(F\) is the positive support of a feasible flow at some \(\epsilon_0\in I\).

For each connected component of \(F\), equality of its total male and receiver masses has the form

\( \displaystyle \frac{k}{D}+\sigma\epsilon=0,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z,\quad \sigma\in\{-1,0,1\}. \)

Because this equality holds at \(0<\epsilon_0<1/(2D)\), necessarily \(k=\sigma=0\). Thus the component balance holds identically in \(\epsilon\).

The row and column equations on a forest uniquely determine its edge values. Removing an edge and summing those equations on either resulting side gives

\( \displaystyle z_{F,ac}^{\epsilon}=\alpha_{ac}+\beta_{ac}\epsilon,\qquad \alpha_{ac}\in D^{-1}\mathbb Z,\quad \beta_{ac}\in\{-1,0,1\}. \)

The coefficient bound follows because the only perturbed margins are the two receiver capacities, with opposite coefficients.

An edge positive at \(\epsilon_0\) remains positive throughout \(I\): a nonzero \(\alpha_{ac}\) has magnitude at least \(1/D\), while \(|\beta_{ac}\epsilon|<1/(2D)\); if \(\alpha_{ac}=0\), positivity requires \(\beta_{ac}=1\). Consequently, \(z_F^\epsilon\) is feasible with exactly support \(F\) throughout \(I\).

If it is report-stable at one point, it is report-stable throughout \(I\), since support and rankings are unchanged.

The optimal forest cannot change within the interval

For any two such forest flows, every coordinate difference has the form

\( \displaystyle \frac{k}{D}+\gamma\epsilon,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z,\quad \gamma\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}. \)

Its sign is constant throughout \(I\): when \(k\neq0\), the constant term dominates because \(2\epsilon<1/D\); when \(k=0\), its sign is determined by \(\gamma\). Hence every rowwise lexicographic comparison between two forest flows is constant throughout \(I\).

Let \(F_0\) be the support of the outcome at \(\eta\), and let \(F_1\) be the support of the outcome at any other \(\epsilon\in I\). Both are forests by Lemma 1, and both define feasible report-stable flows throughout \(I\).

At \(\eta\), male optimality gives, for every \(a\),

\( \displaystyle z_{F_0,a}^{\eta}\succeq_{\mathrm{lex}}z_{F_1,a}^{\eta}. \)

The comparison remains valid at \(\epsilon\). Male optimality at \(\epsilon\) gives the reverse comparison there, so the flows are equal.

We have proved:

Lemma 2. For every \(b\) and every fixed tagged report \(H\), the outcome is affine in \(\epsilon\) and has constant positive support throughout \(I\). The same interval \(I\) works for all reports.

The tagged column is pure

For an edge incident to \(b_*\), feasibility gives \(0\leq z_{F,a b_*}^{\epsilon}\leq\epsilon\). Its affine formula throughout \(I\) therefore has \(\alpha_{a b_*}=0\). A positive tagged edge must then equal \(\epsilon\), because its coefficient belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

The tagged column sums to \(\epsilon\), so exactly one tagged edge is positive.

Corollary 3. For every fixed tagged report \(H\), there is a single type \(t(H)\), independent of \(\epsilon\in I\), such that

\( \displaystyle x^\epsilon_{t(H),b,*}=\epsilon,\qquad x^\epsilon_{a,b,*}=0\quad(a\neq t(H)). \)

Thus every fixed report has the finite limit

\( \displaystyle \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_a x^\epsilon_{a,b,*}\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a)=\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(t(H)). \)

Also, its outcome is \(P\)-stable for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\) if and only if its outcome at \(\eta\) is \(P\)-stable. This follows directly from constant support.

4. Searching partner types at the single value \(\eta\)

Fix \(b\), and henceforth run deferred acceptance at \(\epsilon=\eta\). Write \(y^H\) for the outcome under tagged report \(H\).

All masses and capacities are integer multiples of \(\eta\). Every intermediate free amount and holding is therefore an integer multiple of \(\eta\). Since the tagged capacity is exactly \(\eta\), its first positive offer fills it with one male type. Subsequently its holder remains a single type and can change only to a strictly higher type in \(H\).

Call a male type active under \(H\) if it makes a positive offer to \(b_*\) during the supplied deterministic run.

Activity can be read from the final flow

Let \(\operatorname{Act}(H)\) be the active set. Then

\( \displaystyle \operatorname{Act}(H)=\{a:\exists c,\ y^H_{ac}>0,\ \text{and either }c=b_*\text{ or }b_*\succ_{R_a^b}c\}. \)

Indeed, receiving positive mass at the tag or below it requires having offered to the tag. Conversely, when \(a\) first offers to the tag, it has already been rejected by every earlier receiver. Their holdings of \(a\) cannot subsequently increase. A positive amount of its mass must therefore finish at the tag or below it.

Thus the active set is computable from the final flow alone.

Promotion lemmas

Let \(\operatorname{top}(a,H)\) move \(a\) to the front of \(H\), preserving the relative order of all other types.

Lemma 4. If \(a\in\operatorname{Act}(H)\), then \(\operatorname{top}(a,H)\) yields tagged partner type \(a\).

Proof. The two deterministic runs agree until the first offer from \(a\) to the tag: before that event, moving \(a\) does not change any comparison between offered types. That offer has size at least \(\eta\). Under the promoted report, the tag retains \(\eta\) of \(a\), and no other type can subsequently displace it. \(\square\)

Lemma 5. If the final tagged partner under \(H\) is \(a\), promoting \(a\) leaves the entire run unchanged.

Proof. No type ranked above \(a\) in \(H\) can ever offer to the tag. Such an offer would make its holder at least that highly ranked, and the holder could never subsequently become \(a\).

Before the first offer from \(a\), promotion preserves all relevant comparisons. At that offer, both reports retain \(a\). Every later offer from another type is rejected under both reports. Induction over the deterministic events gives identical runs. \(\square\)

One outcome per attainable partner type

Lemma 6. Two tagged reports yielding the same partner type \(a\) yield the same complete flow at \(\eta\), and hence the same active set.

Proof. By Lemma 5, promote \(a\) in both reports without changing their outcomes. Call the promoted reports \(H_1,H_2\), with outcomes \(y^1,y^2\).

Each outcome is report-stable under the other report. All receiver rankings except the tagged ranking coincide; and the tag holds only \(a\), which is first under both reports, so it cannot participate in a reported blocking quartet.

Male optimality under \(H_1\) gives \(y^1_a\succeq_{\mathrm{lex}}y^2_a\) for every male row. Male optimality under \(H_2\) gives the reverse inequalities. Thus \(y^1=y^2\). The activity formula then gives identical active sets. \(\square\)

5. The algorithm and its correctness

Compute \(x^0\), and define

\( \displaystyle r_b(a)=\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a),\qquad \mu_b^0=\frac{1}{\nu_b}\sum_a x^0_{ab}r_b(a). \)

For each female type \(b\), perform the following search.

  • Set \(H_0=Q_b\), compute \(y^{H_0}\), and find \(\operatorname{Act}(H_0)\).
  • For every type \(a\) in that active set, register the report \(H_a=\operatorname{top}(a,H_0)\) and put \(a\) in a queue.
  • When processing a registered type \(a\), compute \(y^{H_a}\). Return yes if it is \(P\)-stable and \(r_b(a)<\mu_b^0\).
  • For every unregistered \(u\in\operatorname{Act}(H_a)\), register \(H_u=\operatorname{top}(u,H_a)\) and enqueue \(u\).
  • Process each type only once. If all searches finish without success, return no.

Lemma 4 ensures that every registered \(H_a\) has tagged partner \(a\). There are at most \(p\) registered types.

Completeness of the search

Take any tagged report \(H\). List the successive distinct holders of the tag in its deterministic run as

\( \displaystyle t_1,t_2,\ldots,t_k. \)

They are distinct and strictly improve in \(H\); \(t_k\) is the final tagged partner.

The first offer to the tag is independent of its report, since its report has not previously been consulted. Thus \(t_1\in\operatorname{Act}(H_0)\), and the algorithm registers \(t_1\).

Suppose \(t_j\) is registered and \(j<k\). Consider \(\operatorname{top}(t_j,H)\). Its run agrees with the run under \(H\) up to and including the offer that introduces \(t_{j+1}\):

  • Before the first offer from \(t_j\), comparisons among other types are unchanged.
  • That offer makes \(t_j\) the holder under both reports.
  • Until \(t_{j+1}\) arrives, every different offered type is rejected under both reports.

Therefore \(t_{j+1}\) is active under \(\operatorname{top}(t_j,H)\). This promoted report has final partner \(t_j\), as does the algorithm’s registered report \(H_{t_j}\). Lemma 6 makes their active sets equal. Consequently, processing \(t_j\) registers \(t_{j+1}\), unless it was already registered.

Induction shows that the algorithm registers \(t_k\). Moreover, Lemmas 5 and 6 imply

\( \displaystyle y^{H_{t_k}}=y^H. \)

Thus the search finds every attainable tagged partner type and the complete outcome associated with it.

Soundness for all real small perturbations

Suppose the algorithm returns yes using \(H_a\). Lemma 2 gives constant support throughout \(I\), so its outcome is \(P\)-stable throughout \(I\). Corollary 3 gives limiting tagged rank \(r_b(a)<\mu_b^0\).

The report \(H_a\) is a fixed permutation independent of the variable \(\epsilon\). Choosing \(\delta=\eta\) supplies the required witness.

Completeness for the original question

Suppose the original question has a witness \(b,H,\delta\). By Lemma 2, the outcome under \(H\) has constant support throughout \(I\). Its stability on a sufficiently small subinterval therefore implies stability at \(\eta\).

By Corollary 3, its limiting tagged rank is the rank of its partner at \(\eta\). The search finds that partner and, by Lemma 6, the same complete outcome. It therefore returns yes.

This proves correctness in both directions.

6. Running time

A split instance has

\( \displaystyle V=\tau+1,\qquad E=p(q+1)=O(m^2). \)

For each \(b\), the search uses one initial computation and at most \(p\) registered-report computations. Across all female types, including the computation of \(x^0\), this is \(O(m^2)\) stable-allocation computations.

Each requires \(O(EV)=O(m^2\tau)\) arithmetic operations, giving \(O(m^4\tau)\) operations overall.

Activity and true stability can each be checked in \(O(E)\) time. For the latter, define

\( \displaystyle w_a=\max_{c:y_{ac}>0}\operatorname{rank}_{R_a}(\pi(c)),\qquad v_c=\max_{a':y_{a'c}>0}r_{\pi(c)}(a'). \)

A true blocking quartet exists exactly when some pair \(a,c\) satisfies both

\( \displaystyle \operatorname{rank}_{R_a}(\pi(c))<w_a,\qquad r_{\pi(c)}(a)<v_c. \)

The maxima provide the required witnesses \(d,a'\), and any blocking quartet implies these inequalities. These checks use the original female-type rankings, including the prescribed indifference between the two parts of \(b\).

For exact computation at \(\eta\), scale all masses by \(4D\). The capacities are then integers with \(O(L)\) bits. The stable-allocation algorithm uses additions, subtractions, minima, and comparisons on quantities of this bit length. The improvement comparisons also involve only \(O(L)\)-bit integers. Including denominator preprocessing and using conservative schoolbook arithmetic gives the claimed bound \(O(m^4\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

The algorithm operates directly on the supplied support.
