The continuous mirror question, as graded

Comparisons may use any rational points in \(S\); membership and comparison calls have unit oracle cost, while other computation uses bit complexity.

Rigorous partial solution

The full noisy problem remains unresolved. The polynomial-time-versus-NP-hard dichotomy is not exhaustive: some permitted instances are information-theoretically impossible.

The results proved below are:

  • Polynomial-time aggregate elicitation for every noiseless instance, and under an explicit sufficient positive-noise condition.
  • Mass-dependent upper and lower query bounds.
  • A new lower bound with $m+m(m+1)/2-1$ independent parameters per type.
  • A matching query bound

$$ Q=\Theta\!\left(\tau m^2\log(1/\kappa)\right) $$
for a specified family of noiseless instances with growing dimension and equal masses.

  • Promised noisy instances for which $Q=\infty$.

Here $Q$ denotes the minimum worst-case number of feasible comparisons needed for the required uniform aggregate guarantee. The lower bounds allow unlimited computation.

1. Coefficient error controls aggregate error

For a symmetric coefficient pair, define
$$ \|(a,B)\|_*=\sqrt{\|a\|_2^2+\|B\|_F^2}. $$
For $r\in[0,1]^m$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$ \left|a^\top r+\frac12r^\top Br\right| \le \|(a,B)\|_* \sqrt{\|r\|_2^2+\frac14\|rr^\top\|_F^2}. $$
Since $\|rr^\top\|_F=\|r\|_2^2\le m$ and $m\ge2$,
$$ \sqrt{m+\frac{m^2}{4}}\le m. $$
Thus coefficient error at most $e/m$ implies uniform utility error at most $e$ on $\mathcal R$. In particular, every promised utility satisfies
$$ \sup_{r\in\mathcal R}|\phi_t(r)|\le m. $$

Consequently, if a set $A$ of types is elicited to respective uniform errors $e_t$, and the remaining estimates are zero, the aggregate error is at most
$$ \sum_{t\in A}\mu_t e_t+ m\sum_{t\notin A}\mu_t. $$

2. Polynomial recovery from symmetric comparisons

Write $d=\rho-\varrho$.

Proposition 1. For $0<e<1$, suppose
$$ \epsilon_\Omega \le \frac{e\,d\,\varrho\,\gamma^8}{3520m^2}. $$
One type can be elicited to uniform utility error at most $e$ using polynomial bit computation and at most
$$ q(e) = (m+2)\left[ 1+(m-1) \left\lceil \log_2\frac{880m^2}{e\,d\,\gamma^6} \right\rceil \right] $$
comparisons. All comparison points lie in $S$.

2.1 Recovering gradient ratios

Fix one type and suppress its index. At each anchor $z\in Z$, put
$$ s(z)=g_1(z), \qquad v(z)=\frac{g(z)}{g_1(z)}. $$
The promises imply
$$ |s(z)|\ge\gamma^2, \qquad |v_j(z)|\le\gamma^{-1}. $$

Also, $\|g(z)\|_2\le1$. Indeed, each anchor is interior to $S$. For any unit vector $u$ and sufficiently small real $t$, Lipschitz continuity gives
$$ |\phi(z+tu)-\phi(z)|\le|t|. $$
Differentiation at $t=0$ yields $|g(z)^\top u|\le1$, proving the claim.

Set
$$ C=\gamma^{-1}, \qquad h=\frac{\varrho}{4(1+C)}, \qquad \eta=\frac{\epsilon_\Omega}{2h\gamma^2}. $$
For every vector $w$, quadraticity gives the exact identity
$$ \phi(z+hw)-\phi(z-hw)=2h\,g(z)^\top w. $$
In particular, there is no local quadratic approximation error.

For $w=e_j-ce_1$ with $|c|\le C$,
$$ \|hw\|_2\le h(1+C)=\varrho/4. $$
Each anchor has margin at least $\varrho$ inside $S$, so these comparison points are feasible.

A comparison with $w=e_1$ determines the sign of $s(z)$ whenever $\eta<1$. After that sign is known, a comparison with $w=e_j-ce_1$ determines the side of $c$ containing $v_j(z)$ whenever
$$ |v_j(z)-c|>\eta. $$

Starting with $[\ell,u]=[-C,C]$, query its midpoint $c$. If the interpreted response indicates the upper side, replace $\ell$ by $\max\{\ell,c-\eta\}$; otherwise replace $u$ by $\min\{u,c+\eta\}$. Either update retains the true ratio, including for arbitrary responses inside the noise band.

The interval width satisfies
$$ W_{n+1}\le W_n/2+\eta. $$
Thus its midpoint after $n$ queries has error at most
$$ C2^{-n}+\eta. $$
For $0<\delta<C$, taking
$$ n=\left\lceil\log_2(C/\delta)\right\rceil $$
therefore yields
$$ |\widehat v_j(z)-v_j(z)|\le\beta, \qquad \beta=\delta+\eta. $$
We set $\widehat v_1(z)=1$. The restriction on $\delta$ fixes the negative-query-count issue identified in the previous verifier report.

2.2 Exact reconstruction from the ratios

Use subscripts $0,-,j$ for values at $o,z_-,z_j$, respectively. Define $c_j=s_j/s_0$.

The affine-gradient identity
$$ s_1v_1+s_-v_-=2s_0v_0 $$
has first coordinate $s_1+s_-=2s_0$. Its second coordinate consequently gives
$$ c_1 = \frac{2(v_{0,2}-v_{-,2})}{v_{1,2}-v_{-,2}}. $$
The denominator has absolute value at least $\gamma$ by the ratio-difference promise.

For $j\ge2$, symmetry of $B$ gives
$$ \frac{s_j-s_0}{d} = B_{1j} = B_{j1} = \frac{s_1v_{1,j}-s_0v_{0,j}}{d}. $$
Therefore
$$ c_j=1+c_1v_{1,j}-v_{0,j}. $$

It follows that
$$ T:=\frac{B}{s_0}, \qquad T_{\cdot j}=\frac{c_jv_j-v_0}{d}, \qquad A:=\frac{a}{s_0}=v_0-To. $$
Finally, writing $\sigma_0=\operatorname{sign}(s_0)$, normalization gives
$$ (a,B)=\sigma_0\frac{(A,T)}{\|(A,T)\|_*}. $$
Thus these gradient directions, together with the recovered sign, identify the coefficients exactly.

Apply these formulas to the estimated ratios, symmetrize the resulting matrix $\widehat T$, and set
$$ \widehat A=\widehat v_0-\widehat T o. $$
Suppose $\beta\le\gamma/4$. The estimated denominator defining $\widehat c_1$ has magnitude at least $\gamma/2$.

Because $|s_j|\le1$ and $|s_0|\ge\gamma^2$,
$$ |c_j|\le\gamma^{-2}. $$
The quotient formula gives

Also, $|\widehat v_{j,\ell}|\le2/\gamma$. Hence, for $j\ge2$,

This last bound also holds for $j=1$. Every entry of the unsymmetrized matrix therefore has error at most
$$ \frac1d \left( \frac{26\beta}{\gamma^4}\frac2\gamma +\frac{\beta}{\gamma^2}+\beta \right) \le \frac{54\beta}{d\gamma^5}. $$
Symmetrization cannot increase Frobenius error relative to the symmetric true matrix, so
$$ \|\widehat T-T\|_F \le \frac{54m\beta}{d\gamma^5}. $$

Here $d\gamma^5\le1$: the inclusion $S\subseteq[0,1]^m$ implies $\rho\le1/m$.

Consequently,
$$ E:=\|(\widehat A,\widehat T)-(A,T)\|_* \le \frac{110m\beta}{d\gamma^5}. $$
The true pair has norm $1/|s_0|\ge1$. For nonzero vectors $x,y$,
$$ \left\|\frac{x}{\|x\|}-\frac{y}{\|y\|}\right\| \le \frac{2\|x-y\|}{\|x\|}. $$
Thus, if $E<1$, normalization produces coefficient error at most
$$ \frac{220m\beta}{d\gamma^5}. $$

Choose
$$ \delta=\frac{e\,d\,\gamma^5}{880m^2}. $$
Since $h\ge\varrho\gamma/8$, the proposition’s noise condition implies

Therefore $\beta\le2\delta$, $\beta\le\gamma/4$, and
$$ E\le e/(4m)<1. $$
The normalized coefficient error is at most $e/(2m)$. Approximating the normalized coefficients by rationals with additional pair-norm error at most $e/(2m)$ gives total coefficient error at most $e/m$, proving the utility guarantee.

All arithmetic before normalization is rational. Square-root normalization can be approximated by rational bisection. The query-coordinate lengths and arithmetic precision are polynomial in the explicit input length and the logarithms of the parameter reciprocals. There are $m+2$ anchors, one sign query per anchor, and $m-1$ searches per anchor, giving $q(e)$.

3. A mass-allocation function

For $0<b<1$, define
$$ H_\mu(b) = \min_{\substack{0<x_t\le1\\ \sum_t\mu_t x_t\le b}} \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\log_2(1/x_t). $$
Set $H_\mu(b)=0$ for $b\ge1$.

There is a unique $\lambda\in(0,\max_t\mu_t)$ satisfying
$$ \sum_t\min\{\mu_t,\lambda\}=b. $$
The minimizing allocation is
$$ x_t^*=\min\{1,\lambda/\mu_t\}, $$
and hence
$$ H_\mu(b) = \sum_t\log_2\max\{1,\mu_t/\lambda\}. $$

To verify optimality directly, concavity of the logarithm gives
$$ \log x_t-\log x_t^* \le \frac{x_t-x_t^*}{x_t^*}. $$
For $\mu_t>\lambda$, the right side equals
$\mu_t(x_t-x_t^*)/\lambda$. For $\mu_t\le\lambda$, we have $x_t^*=1$ and $x_t-1\le0$, so
$$ x_t-1\le\frac{\mu_t}{\lambda}(x_t-1). $$
Summing proves
$$ \sum_t(\log x_t-\log x_t^*) \le \frac1\lambda \sum_t\mu_t(x_t-x_t^*) \le0. $$

Sorting the masses identifies the interval containing $\lambda$. Within that interval, its defining equation is linear. Thus $\lambda$ and $x_t^*$ are computable using rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

3.1 A mass-dependent noiseless upper bound

Take
$$ b=\frac{\kappa}{2m}, \qquad A=\{t:x_t^*<1/2\}. $$
Elicit each selected type to tolerance $e_t=x_t^*$, and set the other estimates to zero.

For omitted types, $m\le2m x_t^*$. For selected types, $e_t=x_t^*\le2m x_t^*$. Therefore the aggregate error is at most
$$ 2m\sum_t\mu_t x_t^*=\kappa. $$

Put
$$ K=\frac{880m^2}{d\gamma^6}. $$
The query bound in Proposition 1 implies
$$ q(e) \le (m+2)(m-1) \left(2+\log_2K+\log_2(1/e)\right). $$
Every selected type contributes more than one to $H_\mu(b)$, so $|A|\le H_\mu(b)$. Consequently, for $\epsilon_\Omega=0$,
$$ \boxed{ Q\le (m+2)(m-1)(3+\log_2K)\, H_\mu\!\left(\frac{\kappa}{2m}\right). } $$

The selected tolerances do not create excessive precision requirements. Indeed,
$$ b=\sum_t\min\{\mu_t,\lambda\}\le\tau\lambda, $$
so each selected $e_t=\lambda/\mu_t$ is at least $b/\tau$.

4. A full-dimensional promised family

The next construction supplies a dimension-dependent aggregate lower bound.

Assume
$$ m\ge2,\qquad 0<\rho\le\frac1{4m},\qquad \varrho=\rho/2,\qquad 0<\gamma\le\frac{\rho}{100m}, \qquad \mathcal R=S. $$
Let
$$ D=m+\frac{m(m+1)}2, \qquad p=D-1, \qquad b_0=\frac1{\sqrt{5m}}. $$
Choose base coefficients
$$ a^{(0)}=(2/5,4/5,0,\ldots,0), \qquad B^{(0)}=-b_0 I. $$
They satisfy
$$ \|a^{(0)}\|_2^2+\|B^{(0)}\|_F^2 = \frac45+\frac15=1. $$

Use orthonormal coordinates for symmetric matrices: diagonal coordinates are $B_{ii}$, and off-diagonal coordinates are $\sqrt2B_{ij}$. Let $y\in\mathbb R^p$ collect these matrix coordinates and $a_2,\ldots,a_m$. At the base point $y^{(0)}$,
$$ \|y^{(0)}\|_2^2=21/25. $$

Define
$$ h=\frac{\rho}{10000m^2p}, \qquad y\in y^{(0)}+[-h,h]^p, \qquad a_1(y)=\sqrt{1-\|y\|_2^2}. $$
The remaining coefficients are specified by $y$. This gives a $p$-dimensional cube of normalized coefficient pairs.

4.1 Normalization and coefficient perturbations

Put
$$ t=\sqrt p\,h = \frac{\rho}{10000m^2\sqrt p}. $$
Then $t\le1/10000$ and
$$ \left|\|y\|_2^2-\|y^{(0)}\|_2^2\right| \le2t+t^2\le3t. $$
In particular, $a_1(y)>1/3$. Moreover,
$$ |a_1(y)-2/5| = \frac{\left|\|y\|_2^2-\|y^{(0)}\|_2^2\right|} {a_1(y)+2/5} \le5t. $$
Thus
$$ \|(a(y),B(y))-(a^{(0)},B^{(0)})\|_* \le6t, \qquad \|B(y)-B^{(0)}\|_F\le t. $$

Since
$$ \frac{t}{b_0} \le \frac{\sqrt5}{40000m^{5/2}} < \frac1{20}, $$
every such matrix is negative definite:
$$ B(y)\preceq-(b_0-t)I\prec0. $$

4.2 Lipschitz continuity and anchor regularity

The base gradient at $o$ satisfies
$$ \|g^{(0)}(o)\|_2^2 = \frac45-\frac{12b_0}{5m}+\frac{b_0^2}{m} \le\frac45. $$
For $r\in S$,
$$ \|g^{(0)}(r)\|_2 \le \sqrt{4/5}+b_0\rho < \frac9{10}+\frac1{24}. $$
Also, $\|r\|_2\le m^{-1/2}+\rho<1$, so the perturbed gradients satisfy
$$ \|g(y;r)-g^{(0)}(r)\|_2 \le \|a(y)-a^{(0)}\|_2+ \|B(y)-B^{(0)}\|_F\|r\|_2 \le7t. $$
It follows that $\|g(y;r)\|_2<1$ throughout $S$. Integrating along segments proves the required Lipschitz condition.

For every $r\in S$, the base first gradient component obeys
$$ g^{(0)}_1(r) \ge \frac25-\frac{b_0}{m}-b_0\rho > \frac25-\frac16-\frac1{24} = \frac{23}{120}. $$
Consequently, the perturbed first component exceeds $1/6$. Thus, at every anchor,
$$ \|g(y;z)\|_2>1/6, \qquad |f_1(y;z)|>1/6. $$
These exceed $\gamma$, since $\gamma\le1/(400m^2)$.

It remains to verify the ratio-difference promise. At the center,
$$ g_2(y;o) > \frac45-\frac16-7t > \frac35. $$
Furthermore,
$$ |B_{11}|\ge19b_0/20, \qquad |B_{21}|\le t\le b_0/20, \qquad |g_1(y;o)|<1. $$
Therefore
$$ |g_2(y;o)B_{11}-g_1(y;o)B_{21}| \ge \frac35\frac{19b_0}{20}-\frac{b_0}{20} \ge b_0/2. $$
Writing $d=\rho/2$, direct subtraction yields
$$ \left| \frac{g_2(z_-)}{g_1(z_-)} - \frac{g_2(z_1)}{g_1(z_1)} \right| = \frac{ 2d\,|g_2(o)B_{11}-g_1(o)B_{21}| }{ g_1(o)^2-d^2B_{11}^2 }. $$
The denominator is the positive product $g_1(z_-)g_1(z_1)$ and is at most one. Hence the ratio difference is at least
$$ d b_0 = \frac{\rho}{2\sqrt{5m}} \ge \frac{\rho}{100m} \ge\gamma. $$
Because $f_2/f_1=g_2/g_1$, this is precisely the promised inequality.

Every point in the coefficient cube therefore defines a valid utility.

5. Aggregate query lower bounds

5.1 Utility accuracy controls coefficient coordinates on a ball

Let
$$ P(r)=a^\top r+\frac12r^\top Br $$
be any quadratic with symmetric $B$, and suppose
$$ \sup_{r\in S}|P(r)|\le E. $$
The second-difference identity gives
$$ \rho^2B_{ii} = P(o+\rho e_i)+P(o-\rho e_i)-2P(o), $$
so
$$ |B_{ii}|\le4E/\rho^2. $$

For $i\ne j$, use the four points
$$ o+\frac{\rho}{2}(\sigma e_i+\upsilon e_j), \qquad \sigma,\upsilon\in\{-1,1\}. $$
They lie in $S$. Their alternating sum is $\rho^2B_{ij}$, implying
$$ |B_{ij}|\le4E/\rho^2. $$

The symmetric first difference gives
$$ |g_j(o)|\le E/\rho. $$
Since $a_j=g_j(o)-m^{-1}\sum_iB_{ji}$ and $\rho\le1$,
$$ |a_j|\le5E/\rho^2. $$
Thus every free coordinate used in Section 4 has magnitude at most
$$ 6E/\rho^2. $$

5.2 Product structure at each transcript

Independently choose each type’s coefficients from the cube in Section 4. Use exact comparison responses, with bit zero at ties. These responses are allowed for every permitted $\epsilon_\Omega$.

Consider a fixed transcript of a successful algorithm. Its compatible parameters form a product
$$ A_1\times\cdots\times A_\tau. $$
Indeed, after a transcript is fixed, each query is fixed, and each comparison constraint concerns only the selected type. Membership answers depend only on the fixed ball $S$.

Let $w_{t,i}\in[0,2h]$ be the width of the $i$th free coordinate on $A_t$.

Any two societies compatible with the transcript receive the same estimate. Their aggregate utilities therefore differ uniformly on $S$ by at most $2\kappa$. Section 5.1 implies that each free aggregate coefficient coordinate differs by at most
$$ 12\kappa/\rho^2. $$

Free coefficient coordinates aggregate linearly. Independence across types means that the range of aggregate coordinate $i$ has width exactly
$$ \sum_t\mu_t w_{t,i}. $$
Thus, for every $i$,
$$ \sum_t\mu_t w_{t,i} \le 12\kappa/\rho^2. $$
This conclusion uses only aggregate accuracy.

5.3 Volume bound

Set
$$ x_{t,i}=\frac{w_{t,i}}{2h}, \qquad \beta=\frac{6\kappa}{h\rho^2}. $$
For each coordinate,
$$ 0\le x_{t,i}\le1, \qquad \sum_t\mu_t x_{t,i}\le\beta. $$
The defining optimization for $H_\mu$ implies
$$ \prod_t x_{t,i}\le2^{-H_\mu(\beta)}. $$
If any width is zero, this inequality holds automatically.

Each $A_t$ lies in its coordinate bounding box. Consequently, the volume of the compatible product set is at most
$$ \prod_t\prod_{i=1}^p w_{t,i} \le (2h)^{p\tau}\,2^{-pH_\mu(\beta)}. $$
These sets are measurable: for a fixed transcript, they are finite intersections of comparison inequalities between continuous utilities.

The full parameter cube has volume $(2h)^{p\tau}$. A comparison tree of depth $Q$ has at most $2^Q$ leaves. Its compatible sets cover that cube, so
$$ 2^Q\ge2^{pH_\mu(\beta)}. $$
Substituting $h$ proves the following.

Proposition 2. For the parameter range in Section 4, already with $\mathcal R=S$,
$$ \boxed{ Q\ge p\,H_\mu\!\left( \frac{60000m^2p\,\kappa}{\rho^3} \right), \qquad p=m+\frac{m(m+1)}2-1. } $$
This bound holds for every permitted noise level.

It also follows that every successful transcript must leave unqueried mass at most
$$ \frac{60000m^2p\,\kappa}{\rho^3}, $$
because every unqueried type retains full width $2h$ in every free coordinate.

5.4 A matching growing-dimension noiseless case

For equal masses $\mu_t=1/\tau$ and $0<\beta<1$,
$$ H_\mu(\beta)=\tau\log_2(1/\beta). $$
Hence Proposition 2 gives
$$ Q\ge p\tau \log_2\frac{\rho^3}{60000m^2p\,\kappa}. $$

Now fix, as functions of $m$,
$$ \rho=\frac1{4m}, \qquad \varrho=\frac1{8m}, \qquad \gamma=\frac1{400m^2}, \qquad \epsilon_\Omega=0. $$
Define
$$ C_m=\frac{\rho^3}{60000m^2p}. $$
If $0<\kappa\le C_m^2$, then
$$ \log_2(C_m/\kappa) \ge \frac12\log_2(1/\kappa). $$
Since $p=\Theta(m^2)$, the lower bound is
$$ Q=\Omega\!\left(\tau m^2\log(1/\kappa)\right). $$

For these same parameters, Proposition 1 gives
$$ Q = O\!\left( \tau m^2\log\frac{7040\cdot400^6m^{15}}{\kappa} \right). $$
The condition $\kappa\le C_m^2$ implies that $\log m$ is bounded by a constant multiple of $\log(1/\kappa)$. Therefore
$$ \boxed{ Q=\Theta\!\left(\tau m^2\log(1/\kappa)\right) } $$
in this regime, with constants independent of $m,\tau,\kappa$.

If binary-rational masses are required to be dyadic, take $\tau$ to be a power of two. This result extends the previously verified fixed-dimensional case to growing dimension.

6. Positive-noise instances with infinite query complexity

For completeness, the information-theoretic obstruction is proved here independently.

Fix rational $0<\rho\le1/4$ and take

$$ \phi_+(r)-\phi_-(r)=\frac{2h}{c_h}q(r). $$

Then $\phi_+-\phi_-$ has oscillation at most $\epsilon_\Omega$.

For a comparison $(r,s)$, let
$$ \Delta_\pm=\phi_\pm(r)-\phi_\pm(s). $$
We have $|\Delta_+-\Delta_-|\le\epsilon_\Omega$. In particular, one gap cannot exceed $\epsilon_\Omega$ while the other is below $-\epsilon_\Omega$.

A response rule valid for both utilities is therefore: return one if either $\Delta_+>\epsilon_\Omega$ or $\Delta_->\epsilon_\Omega$; return zero otherwise. This rule works for every feasible pair.

6.3 Aggregate indistinguishability

At the center,
$$ |\phi_+(o)-\phi_-(o)|=\frac{h}{4c_h}. $$
Assign $\phi_+$ to every type in one society and $\phi_-$ to every type in another. Their aggregate utilities are $\phi_+$ and $\phi_-$, independently of the masses.

The membership oracle and the common comparison rule are identical in the two societies. Any algorithm therefore receives the same transcript and produces the same aggregate estimate. Accuracy for both societies would require
$$ \frac{h}{4c_h}\le2\kappa. $$
Since $c_h<9/8$, this is impossible whenever
$$ \boxed{ \kappa< \min\left\{ \frac1{900}, \frac{\epsilon_\Omega}{63\rho^2} \right\}. } $$
Thus $Q=\infty$ for these parameter tuples, for every positive mass vector summing to one.

For example,
$$ \rho=\frac14,\quad \varrho=\frac18,\quad \gamma=\frac1{40},\quad \epsilon_\Omega=\frac1{800},\quad \kappa=\frac1{10000} $$
satisfies every input condition and makes elicitation impossible.

7. What these results settle and leave open

The noiseless problem is polynomial-time solvable for every promised instance. Under the explicit positive-noise condition in Proposition 1, the same is true. The noisy construction proves that an unconditional polynomial guarantee over all allowed inputs is impossible, without invoking computational hardness.

The new lower bound captures both the quadratic coefficient dimension and the mass vector despite requiring only aggregate accuracy. It yields a matching bound for equal masses in the stated growing-dimension noiseless regime.

The [published single-oracle results](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v180/hiranandani22a/hiranandani22a.pdf) concern individual coefficient recovery. The aggregate lower bounds here instead follow from independent type access and the resulting product structure of transcript-consistent parameter sets.

These arguments do not determine the exact noisy attainable tolerance. They also do not classify attainable noisy regimes outside Proposition 1, or give sharp query complexity for every mass vector and every geometric parameter tuple. No NP-hardness conclusion has been established.
