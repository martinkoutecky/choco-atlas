Theorem 4.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many bidder classes \(a\), rational masses \(\mu_a\) summing to \(1\), finite-support value distributions \(F_a\), and per-capita service capacity \(\kappa\in(0,1)\), determine the sharp worst-case ratio of the optimal truthful, individually rational continuum mechanism to the best greedy posted-price policy, for revenue and welfare. Give a price-lottery algorithm attaining the guarantee.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

The society is nonatomic, with publicly observable bidder classes \(a\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\). Its input is the list of the \(\tau\) positive-mass types \((a,v)\), each with binary-rational mass \(\mu_aF_a(\{v\})\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Values are nonnegative binary rationals, \(\mu_a>0\), and \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each bidder demands one unit of service, has quasilinear utility, and privately knows her value. Serving mass at most the binary-rational capacity \(\kappa\in(0,1)\) is feasible. Type masses are exact, with no aggregate demand uncertainty.
Mechanisms may distinguish public classes and must be universally dominant-strategy truthful and ex post individually rational, with nonnegative payments and zero payments from unserved bidders. Value reports range over \([0,\infty)\). Revenue is total payments; welfare is total served value.
For \(J\in\{\mathrm{rev},\mathrm{wel}\}\), define \(g_{\mathrm{rev}}(v,p)=p\) and \(g_{\mathrm{wel}}(v,p)=v\). Let \(H_{a,J}(q)\) be the maximum of \(\mathbb E[g_J(v,p)\mathbf1_{\{v\ge p\}}]\) over lotteries of nonnegative posted prices, independent of \(v\), having acceptance probability \(q\); a no-offer option contributes zero.
A greedy policy chooses \(q_a\in[0,1]\) with \(\sum_a\mu_aq_a\le\kappa\), omits classes with \(q_a=0\), and gives each remaining class a lottery attaining \(H_{a,J}(q_a)\). It visits classes in decreasing order of \(H_{a,J}(q_a)/q_a\), offering service until capacity is exhausted. Lotteries and priorities split every value mass proportionally; any final rationing is independent of value and offered price. Bidders accept at equality, and class-order ties use a fixed public order.
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}_J(I)\) be the optimal mechanism's objective and \(\mathrm{GREEDY}_J(I)\) the best such greedy policy's objective, with \(0/0=1\).

For each \(\kappa\in(0,1)\), what are the sharp approximation factors \(\beta_J(\kappa)=\sup_{I:\,\mathrm{capacity}(I)=\kappa}\mathrm{OPT}_J(I)/\mathrm{GREEDY}_J(I)\), separately for \(J\in\{\mathrm{rev},\mathrm{wel}\}\)?
Is there a price-lottery algorithm attaining these guarantees in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For independent finite bidders, greedy posted pricing achieves factors \(e/(e-1)\) for matroids, \((1-k^k/(e^k k!))^{-1}\) for \(k\)-unit auctions, and \(p+1\) for \(p\)-independent systems, for both objectives. [Yan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1843)
For a different continuum model with dynamic arrivals and anonymous mechanisms, optimal revenue is implementable using posted prices and capacity lotteries; that result does not state the class-specific greedy ratios above. [Correa, Cristi, and Vargas Koch](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11738v1)

Result and model interpretation

For every admissible binary-rational capacity \(\kappa\in(0,1)\),

\(\boxed{\beta_{\mathrm{rev}}(\kappa)=\beta_{\mathrm{wel}}(\kappa)=1.}\)

An optimal price-lottery greedy policy can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The equality also holds for real capacities if those are allowed; the computational assertion concerns finitely encoded rational capacities.

I use the stated nonatomic type-mass interpretation: the bidder’s publicly observable characteristic is her class, and an individual report does not change aggregate report masses. Auxiliary randomization carries no additional information about her private value. Thus, after fixing the aggregate environment, a class has an own-report allocation and payment rule.

The crucial feature is exact proportional splitting. If the chosen acceptance masses fit within capacity, every acceptance can be served, regardless of class order.

1. Finite descriptions of the price-lottery frontiers

For class \(a\), write its distinct support values in increasing order:

\(0\le v_{a,1}<\cdots<v_{a,n_a}.\)

Let \(w_{a,i}>0\) be the input mass of type \((a,v_{a,i})\), and define

\(\mu_a=\sum_{i=1}^{n_a}w_{a,i},\qquad f_{a,i}=w_{a,i}/\mu_a.\)

Thus \(\sum_i f_{a,i}=1\) and \(\sum_a n_a=\tau\).

For the posted price \(v_{a,i}\), define its conditional acceptance probability and objective contribution by

\(Q_{a,i}=\sum_{j=i}^{n_a}f_{a,j},\)

\(C_{a,\mathrm{rev},i}=v_{a,i}Q_{a,i},\qquad C_{a,\mathrm{wel},i}=\sum_{j=i}^{n_a}f_{a,j}v_{a,j}.\)

Also include the no-offer point \((0,0)\).

These finitely many points suffice. Indeed, any price with positive acceptance can be raised to the least support value weakly above it without changing which support types accept. This preserves welfare and weakly increases revenue. A price exceeding the largest support value is equivalent to no offer.

Consequently, \(H_{a,J}(q)\) is the upper boundary of the convex hull of

\(\{(0,0)\}\cup\{(Q_{a,i},C_{a,J,i}):1\le i\le n_a\}.\)

In particular, \(H_{a,J}\) is continuous, concave, and piecewise affine on \([0,1]\). Every point on its upper boundary is attained by a lottery over at most two hull vertices, where a vertex can represent no offer.

2. A truthful class rule is dominated by a price lottery

Fix a class and suppress its subscript. Let \(x_i\in[0,1]\) be its allocation probability and \(t_i\) its expected payment at support value \(v_i\), including all mechanism randomization.

The nonatomic assumption fixes the aggregate environment under an individual deviation. Dominant-strategy truthfulness therefore implies, for every pair of support reports \(i,j\),

\(v_i x_i-t_i\ge v_i x_j-t_j.\)

Universal truthfulness implies these inequalities after averaging randomization. Ex post individual rationality implies

\(t_i\le v_i x_i.\)

Monotonicity

Applying the truthfulness inequalities in both directions gives

\((v_i-v_j)(x_i-x_j)\ge0.\)

Since the support values increase strictly, we obtain

\(0\le x_1\le\cdots\le x_n\le1.\)

Set \(x_0=0\) and

\(\gamma_i=x_i-x_{i-1}\ge0.\)

Consider the price lottery assigning probability \(\gamma_i\) to price \(v_i\), and probability \(1-x_n\) to no offer.

A type \(v_i\) accepts exactly the prices indexed by \(j\le i\). Its service probability is therefore

\(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\gamma_j=x_i.\)

Thus this lottery preserves every support type’s allocation probability, its class’s acceptance mass, and its welfare.

Revenue domination

Individual rationality gives \(t_1\le v_1x_1\). For \(i\ge2\), truthfulness of type \(v_i\) against reporting \(v_{i-1}\) gives

\(t_i\le t_{i-1}+v_i(x_i-x_{i-1}).\)

Induction yields

\(t_i\le\sum_{j=1}^{i}v_j(x_j-x_{j-1})=\sum_{j=1}^{i}v_j\gamma_j.\)

The expression on the right is precisely the expected payment of type \(v_i\) under the constructed price lottery. Hence the lottery weakly increases revenue, type by type.

This argument includes support atoms at zero and does not require regularity or a continuous value distribution.

3. An upper bound on all admissible mechanisms

Define

\(B_J=\max\left\{\sum_{a=1}^{m}\mu_aH_{a,J}(q_a):q_a\in[0,1],\ \sum_{a=1}^{m}\mu_aq_a\le\kappa\right\}.\)

The maximum exists because its feasible set is compact and its objective is continuous.

Take any admissible mechanism. Let its class-specific allocation probabilities be \(x_{a,i}\), and put

\(q_a=\sum_{i=1}^{n_a}f_{a,i}x_{a,i}.\)

Ex post feasibility, followed by averaging, implies

\(\sum_a\mu_aq_a=\sum_{a,i}w_{a,i}x_{a,i}\le\kappa.\)

Section 2 constructs, separately for every class, a price lottery with acceptance probability \(q_a\) that preserves welfare and weakly increases revenue. By the definition of \(H_{a,J}\), that class’s original objective contribution is therefore at most \(\mu_aH_{a,J}(q_a)\).

Summing over classes proves

\(\mathrm{OPT}_J(I)\le B_J.\)

4. A greedy policy attains the upper bound

Choose a maximizing vector \((q_a)\) defining \(B_J\), and give each included class a lottery attaining \(H_{a,J}(q_a)\).

Under the stipulated proportional splitting, class \(a\) has exactly acceptance mass \(\mu_aq_a\). Total acceptance mass is at most \(\kappa\). Therefore every acceptance is served, even when classes are visited in the required decreasing order of \(H_{a,J}(q_a)/q_a\).

The policy’s objective is exactly

\(\sum_a\mu_aH_{a,J}(q_a)=B_J.\)

The policy also satisfies the mechanism requirements. Fix all random choices and the reports of other bidders. An individual bidder cannot change aggregate demand or the capacity cutoff. She either faces a fixed posted price with an available service opportunity, or cannot receive service. Accepting precisely when her value weakly exceeds that price is dominant. Payments are nonnegative, unserved bidders pay zero, and truthful service gives nonnegative utility. Capacity rationing ensures feasibility on other report profiles as well.

Thus the constructed policy is universally dominant-strategy truthful and ex post individually rational. Since admissible greedy policies are admissible mechanisms,

\(B_J\le\mathrm{GREEDY}_J(I)\le\mathrm{OPT}_J(I)\le B_J.\)

Hence, instance by instance,

\(\boxed{\mathrm{GREEDY}_J(I)=\mathrm{OPT}_J(I)=B_J.}\)

Every ratio equals \(1\), including zero-objective instances under the stated \(0/0=1\) convention. This proves both sharp factors.

5. Polynomial-time computation

Here is an explicit algorithm for either objective.

  • Sort each class’s support, compute its tail quantities, and construct the upper concave hull from Section 1.
  • For each consecutive pair of hull vertices \((u,h)\), \((u',h')\), create a segment with capacity length and marginal objective

\(d=\mu_a(u'-u),\qquad s=(h'-h)/(u'-u).\)

  • Sort all segments of positive slope by decreasing \(s\). For equal slopes, preserve segment order within each class.
  • Fill their capacity lengths in that order until capacity is exhausted or no positive-slope segments remain. At most one segment is partially filled.
  • Recover each \(q_a\), represent its hull point by the corresponding one- or two-option lottery, and sort included classes by \(H_{a,J}(q_a)/q_a\), using the stipulated public tie order.

Why the segment allocation is optimal

Concavity makes each class’s segment slopes nonincreasing. Its objective at any \(q_a\) is the sum of marginal slopes times the capacity lengths used, with its segments filled in prefix order.

If prefix restrictions are temporarily dropped, the problem becomes fractional knapsack. Filling positive slopes in decreasing order is optimal: whenever a lower-slope segment receives capacity while a higher-slope segment is unfinished, transferring capacity to the latter weakly increases the objective.

The prescribed sorting satisfies every class’s prefix restriction because its slopes are nonincreasing, with within-class precedence imposed for ties. Thus the fractional-knapsack optimum is feasible for the original problem and attains \(B_J\). Nonpositive slopes need not be used because capacity is an upper bound.

Encoding and running time

There are at most \(\tau\) hull segments overall. Sorting supports, constructing hulls, and sorting segments and class priorities require polynomially many rational arithmetic operations.

All quantities arise from input rationals through sums, products, differences, and ratios of polynomial encoding length. Hull and slope comparisons can be performed exactly by cross multiplication. The partial-segment length is computed by subtracting previously filled lengths from \(\kappa\); its lottery probabilities are rational and have polynomial encoding length.

The algorithm therefore runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and outputs exact attaining lotteries.

6. Relation to the cited results

The equality here follows from exact acceptance masses and proportional splitting: there is no capacity loss once their sum is feasible. [Yan’s finite-bidder analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1843) instead uses a correlation-gap comparison between dependent winner sets and independent posted-price demand. [Correa, Cristi, and Vargas Koch](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11738v1) study a dynamic anonymous continuum setting. Neither theorem is needed for the static class-specific equality proved above.
