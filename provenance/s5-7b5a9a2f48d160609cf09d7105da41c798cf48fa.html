The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods \(G\) with \(|G|=m\), a finite set \(T\) of agent types with rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), a matroid \(\mathcal M_t\) on \(G\) and rational entitlement \(w_t>0\) for each type, and per-capita capacities \(q_g\ge 0\), choose masses \(x_{t,I}\ge 0\) assigned to independent bundles \(I\in\mathcal I(\mathcal M_t)\). Require \(\sum_{I\in\mathcal I(\mathcal M_t)}x_{t,I}=\mu_t\) for each \(t\) and \(\sum_{t,I:g\in I}x_{t,I}\le q_g\) for each \(g\). Let \(D=\{r/w_t:t\in T,\ 0\le r\le m\}\), ordered increasingly, and let \(H_x(d)=\sum_{t,I:\,|I|/w_t=d}x_{t,I}\). Find a feasible allocation whose vector \((H_x(d))_{d\in D}\) is lexicographically smallest.

There are goods \(G\), with \(|G|=m\); each type has a matroid \(\mathcal M_t\) on \(G\), supplied by a rank oracle, and a binary-rational entitlement \(w_t>0\), with no normalization of entitlements. Each good has a binary-rational per-capita capacity \(q_g\geq0\).
An allocation assigns mass \(x_{t,I}\geq0\) to each independent bundle \(I\in\mathcal I(\mathcal M_t)\), including the empty bundle; its utility is \(|I|\). Require \(\sum_I x_{t,I}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,I:g\in I}x_{t,I}\leq q_g\); unused capacity is permitted.
Let \(D=\{r/w_t:t\in T,\ r\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\}\), with duplicates removed and levels ordered increasingly, and define \(H_x(d)=\sum_{t,I:|I|/w_t=d}x_{t,I}\). The objective is to lexicographically minimize \((H_x(d))_{d\in D}\); any allocation attaining the minimum is acceptable. Represent an allocation by listing its positive bundle masses as binary rationals, with omitted entries zero.
Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the numerical input and \(T_v\) the time per rank-oracle query; state running times in \(m,\tau,L,T_v\).

Is computing an allocation minimizing this histogram lexicographically solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,T_v\), or is it NP-hard even for matroids with polynomial-time rank evaluation?

For finitely many agents with matroid rank valuations and positive entitlements, weighted leximin allocation is computable in polynomial time using valuation oracles ([Viswanathan–Zick](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.07311v3)).

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L,T_v\). An exact optimal allocation can be returned with at most \(m+\tau\) positive bundle masses, each having polynomial bit length.

Here, “binary rational” means a rational represented by its numerator and denominator in binary.

The proof uses a linear program with implicitly represented columns. Matroid greedy provides exact separation for its dual. A denominator bound lets us encode the lexicographic objective by a single linear objective of polynomial bit length.

1. The allocation polytope and its vertices

Put \(n=\tau+m\). Introduce a nonnegative slack \(s_g\) for each capacity constraint. The allocation constraints become

\(\displaystyle \sum_{I\in\mathcal I(\mathcal M_t)}x_{t,I}=\mu_t\quad(t\in T),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,I:g\in I}x_{t,I}+s_g=q_g\quad(g\in G),\)

with all variables nonnegative.

Write this system as \(Ay=b\), where \(y\) contains the bundle variables and slacks. Although \(A\) has potentially exponentially many columns, it has only \(n\) rows, and every entry is either \(0\) or \(1\).

The matrix has full row rank: the columns for the empty bundles give the identity columns on the type rows, and the slack columns give the identity columns on the goods rows.

The resulting polytope \(P\) is nonempty, since everyone can receive the empty bundle. It is bounded because

\(\displaystyle 0\leq x_{t,I}\leq\mu_t,\qquad 0\leq s_g\leq q_g.\)

Consequently, \(P\) has vertices, and each vertex is a basic feasible solution with at most \(n\) positive variables.

A common denominator bound

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of all masses and capacities, and define

\(\displaystyle \Delta=2^L n!.\)

The definition of \(L\) implies \(Q\leq 2^L\), and \(Qb\) is integral.

At a vertex, let \(B\) be its nonsingular \(n\)-column basis matrix. Cramer’s rule shows that every coordinate has a denominator dividing \(Q|\det B|\). The determinant expansion, together with the fact that \(B\) has entries in \(\{0,1\}\), gives

\(\displaystyle 1\leq|\det B|\leq n!.\)

Thus every vertex coordinate has a common denominator at most \(\Delta\). In particular, every histogram coordinate at that vertex has denominator at most \(\Delta\).

Every histogram coordinate belongs to \([0,1]\), since the total allocated mass is \(1\). Therefore its numerator and denominator have bit length

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L+n\log(n+1)\right).\)

This bound depends on the number of constraints, not on the number of independent bundles.

2. An exact scalar encoding of lexicographic minimization

List the distinct levels as

\(\displaystyle D=\{d_1<\cdots<d_K\},\qquad K\leq\tau(m+1),\)

and abbreviate \(H_j(y)=H_x(d_j)\).

Successively minimizing \(H_1,H_2,\ldots,H_K\) over the preceding minimizing face produces a nonempty face of \(P\). This follows because a linear functional attains its minimum on every nonempty compact face, and its minimizers form another face. The final face contains a vertex \(v^\star\) of \(P\). Its histogram, denoted \(h^\star\), is the lexicographic minimum.

Choose the integer base and coefficients

\(\displaystyle b_0=\Delta^2+2,\qquad C_j=b_0^{K-j}.\)

Consider the single linear objective

\(\displaystyle F(y)=\sum_{j=1}^K C_jH_j(y).\)

We prove that every minimizer of \(F\) has histogram \(h^\star\).

Take any vertex \(v\) whose histogram differs from \(h^\star\), and let \(j\) be its first differing coordinate. By lexicographic minimality,

\(\displaystyle H_j(v)>h^\star_j.\)

Both quantities have denominators at most \(\Delta\). Their positive difference is therefore at least \(1/\Delta^2\): after putting two unequal rationals over a common denominator, the numerator of their difference is a positive integer and the denominator is at most \(\Delta^2\).

Earlier histogram coordinates agree, and each later difference has absolute value at most \(1\). Hence

\(\displaystyle F(v)-F(v^\star)\geq \frac{b_0^{K-j}}{\Delta^2}-\sum_{i=j+1}^K b_0^{K-i}.\)

The geometric sum gives

\(\displaystyle F(v)-F(v^\star)\geq \frac{b_0^{K-j}}{\Delta^2}-\frac{b_0^{K-j}-1}{b_0-1}>0,\)

because \(b_0-1>\Delta^2\).

Thus the vertices minimizing \(F\) are exactly those having histogram \(h^\star\). Every point of a bounded polytope is a convex combination of vertices; a minimizing point can involve only minimizing vertices. Since the histogram is linear, every minimizer of \(F\) also has histogram \(h^\star\).

The coefficients have polynomial bit length:

\(\displaystyle \log_2 C_j=O\!\left(K\bigl[L+n\log(n+1)\bigr]\right).\)

This establishes an exact scalar encoding over the entire polytope, including allocations with real masses.

3. The scalar LP and its dual

For each type and cardinality, define

\(\displaystyle c_{t,r}=C_j\quad\text{when }r/w_t=d_j.\)

The scalar allocation LP is

\(\displaystyle \min \sum_{t,I}c_{t,|I|}x_{t,I}\)

subject to the original type-mass and capacity constraints.

Its dual has free variables \(a_t\) for the type equalities and nonnegative variables \(z_g\) for the capacity inequalities:

\(\displaystyle \max \sum_t\mu_ta_t-\sum_gq_gz_g,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle a_t-\sum_{g\in I}z_g\leq c_{t,|I|}\quad\bigl(t\in T,\ I\in\mathcal I(\mathcal M_t)\bigr),\)

and \(z_g\geq0\).

The dual has \(n\) variables. Its potentially exponential family of constraints admits the following exact separation oracle.

4. Exact separation by matroid greedy

First check \(z_g\geq0\). If any coordinate violates this condition, return that violated inequality.

Otherwise, sort the goods so that

\(\displaystyle z_{g_1}\leq\cdots\leq z_{g_m}.\)

For each type, run matroid greedy in this order: accept a good exactly when adding it preserves independence. A rank query tests this condition, since a set \(S\) is independent exactly when its rank equals \(|S|\).

Let \(R_t\) be the number of accepted goods. For each \(r\in\{0,\ldots,R_t\}\), let \(I_{t,r}\) contain the first \(r\) accepted goods, including \(I_{t,0}=\varnothing\).

Greedy gives a minimum-weight set of every feasible cardinality

Fix a type and a feasible cardinality \(r\). Write \(E_i=\{g_1,\ldots,g_i\}\), and let \(\rho\) be this type’s rank function. Then

\(\displaystyle |I_{t,r}\cap E_i|=\min\{r,\rho(E_i)\}.\)

To justify this, before the algorithm has accepted \(r\) elements, its accepted elements in any prefix form a maximal independent subset of that prefix. An element rejected earlier remains dependent when the accepted set grows. By the matroid augmentation axiom, a maximal independent subset has size equal to the prefix rank. Once \(r\) elements have been accepted, the claimed count is \(r\).

For any independent \(J\) of size \(r\),

\(\displaystyle |J\cap E_i|\leq\min\{r,\rho(E_i)\}=|I_{t,r}\cap E_i|.\)

For any size-\(r\) set \(S\), summation by parts gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{g\in S}z_g=rz_{g_m}-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(z_{g_{i+1}}-z_{g_i})|S\cap E_i|.\)

All weight differences are nonnegative. Substituting the prefix-count inequality proves

\(\displaystyle \sum_{g\in I_{t,r}}z_g\leq\sum_{g\in J}z_g.\)

Thus \(I_{t,r}\) is a minimum-weight independent set of cardinality \(r\). Cardinalities exceeding \(R_t=\rho(G)\) have no independent bundles. When \(m=0\), only the empty bundle needs checking.

Checking all dual inequalities

For each feasible pair \((t,r)\), test

\(\displaystyle a_t-\sum_{g\in I_{t,r}}z_g\leq c_{t,r}.\)

If this fails, return the actual dual inequality indexed by \((t,I_{t,r})\).

If every test succeeds, every dual inequality holds. Indeed, an independent bundle \(I\) of cardinality \(r\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle a_t-\sum_{g\in I}z_g\leq a_t-\sum_{g\in I_{t,r}}z_g\leq c_{t,r}.\)

One separation call uses at most \(\tau m\) rank queries, one sorting of the goods, and polynomially many exact rational operations.

5. Exact optimization and recovery of the allocation

We use the following standard consequence of rational ellipsoid optimization and LP duality:

For a finite rational LP with \(n\) explicit constraints and implicitly indexed columns of bounded encoding length, an exact strong separation oracle for the dual permits polynomial-time computation of the optimum and recovery of an optimal primal solution supported on polynomially many columns. The oracle must return an original violated inequality together with its column index. The running time is polynomial in \(n\), the numerical encoding bounds, and the oracle running time.

The recovery procedure identifies polynomially many original dual inequalities sufficient to preserve the optimum. Solving the primal restricted to their corresponding columns recovers an optimal primal solution. This is the sparse dual-solution recovery consequence of the rational-polyhedra theory in [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, *Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4).

All hypotheses hold here:

  • The original primal is feasible and bounded.
  • The dual has \(n=\tau+m\) variables.
  • Every column consists of a type indicator, a goods-incidence vector, and an objective coefficient of polynomial bit length.
  • Section 4 supplies exact strong separation and returns an explicit independent bundle indexing every violated constraint.
  • The right-hand sides and dual objective coefficients are the supplied rationals.

Apply this theorem, then choose an optimal basic feasible solution of the recovered, polynomial-size primal LP, with capacity slacks included. Its basis columns are also columns of the full matrix \(A\), so extending it by zeros gives a basic feasible solution of the full LP.

It therefore has at most \(n\) positive variables, and hence at most \(n=m+\tau\) positive bundle masses. The denominator bound from Section 1 applies to these masses. Omitting the slacks and zero entries gives exactly the requested allocation representation.

By Section 2, its histogram is lexicographically minimal.

6. Running time and output size

The levels \(r/w_t\) can be generated, compared, sorted, and deduplicated using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. Entitlements need no normalization.

For an explicit numerical bound, set

\(\displaystyle S=1+L+n+K\left\lceil\log_2 b_0\right\rceil.\)

Then

\(\displaystyle S=O\!\left(1+L+n+\tau(m+1)\bigl[L+n\log(n+1)\bigr]\right).\)

The exact LP algorithm uses polynomially many separation calls and bit operations in \(n,S\). Each separation call uses at most \(\tau m\) rank queries. Consequently, the total running time is

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\,(1+T_v),\)

and the total number of rank queries is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The output contains at most \(m+\tau\) bundles. Each bundle takes \(m\) bits as an incidence vector, and each positive mass has bit length

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(L+(m+\tau)\log(m+\tau+1)\right).\)

Thus both computation and exact output have polynomial size.
