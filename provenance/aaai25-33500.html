The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses of complete agent types, k identical days with rational per-capita supplies of finitely many indivisible good categories, and type rankings/values respecting the identical-days condition, output a finite-support rational census x over integral bundle histories whose row masses equal the type masses and whose aggregate supplies equal the per-capita supplies, such that every supported pair of histories satisfies SD-EF1 on every day and every supported type-history satisfies the per-capita clone version of SD-PROP1 overall.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(p_i\), normalized by \(\sum_i p_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) good categories, positive binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_c\), and \(k\geq1\) identical days, with \(k\) encoded in unary. Type \(i\) has nonnegative rational additive values \(v_{ic}\), identical across days; copies of a category are interchangeable. Let \(H_i(c)=\{d:v_{id}\geq v_{ic}\}\); ties remain weak in all fairness comparisons.
An integral history is \(h\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{k\times m}\). Output an explicitly listed, finite-support rational census \(x_{i,h}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_h x_{i,h}=p_i\) and \(\sum_{i,h}x_{i,h}h_{tc}=q_c\) for every day \(t\) and category \(c\).
Daily SD-EF1 means that for every ordered pair \((i,h),(j,h')\) with positive census mass and every day \(t\) on which \(h'_t\neq0\), there is a category \(a\) with \(h'_{ta}>0\) such that \(\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}h_{td}\geq\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}h'_{td}-\mathbf1_{\{a\in H_i(c)\}}\) for every category \(c\).
Overall per-capita clone SD-PROP1 means that for every supported \((i,h)\), there is one category \(a\) such that \(\sum_{t=1}^k\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}h_{td}+\mathbf1_{\{a\in H_i(c)\}}\geq\left\lceil k\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}q_d\right\rceil\) for every category \(c\). The added copy is outside the recipient's bundle in a sufficiently large finite clone realization, even if the recipient already owns copies of its category.

Is constructing such a census solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary bit length of the rational input, or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents and explicitly listed goods on identical days, an allocation satisfying SD-EF1 daily and SD-PROP1 overall always exists and is computable in polynomial time. [Cookson, Ebadian, and Shah](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23416).
No result settling this exact rational-mass census problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution of agent types to total mass one, so supplies are measured per capita.
  • Discarded zero-supply categories; retained at least one category with positive supply.
  • Specified complete types by nonnegative rational additive category values, identical across days, with weak rankings induced by those values.
  • Used weak upper contour sets for fairness; equal-valued categories and repeated copies are not separated by artificial tie-breaking.
  • Made the clone SD-PROP1 condition explicit: its proportional threshold is the ceiling of the overall per-capita top-set supply, and one common added copy must meet every threshold.
  • Interpreted an outside good through sufficiently large finite clone realizations: positive-supply categories remain eligible even when the recipient owns some copies.
  • Encoded the number of days in unary because the requested output explicitly lists integral histories across all days.
  • Required explicit finite-support output with binary-rational census masses and binary-encoded integral bundle counts.

A polynomial-time construction

The census can be constructed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\). The construction uses cyclic round-robin orders whose starting points are evenly spaced across the days. A potentially large clone population is represented and processed through intervals of identical clones.

We prove the stronger guarantee that every supported \((i,h)\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{k}\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}h_{td}\ \geq\ \left\lfloor k\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}q_d\right\rfloor\qquad\text{for every category }c.\)

Adding one copy of a most-preferred category therefore establishes the required overall SD-PROP1 condition.

The proof below is direct; it does not infer a compressed-input runtime from the cited [finite-agent theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23416).

1. Remove a common integral bundle

For each category \(c\), define

\(\displaystyle B_c=\lfloor q_c\rfloor,\qquad r_c=q_c-B_c.\)

Thus \(0\leq r_c<1\). We first allocate the residual supplies \(r_c\), and then give every recipient \(B_c\) copies of category \(c\) on every day.

Write

\(\displaystyle R=\sum_{c=1}^{m}r_c<m.\)

Adding the same bundle to all recipients preserves daily SD comparisons: for any threshold set \(H_i(c)\), its contribution cancels when comparing two recipients.

For overall proportionality, if the residual history \(y\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{k}\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}y_{td}\geq\left\lfloor k\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}r_d\right\rfloor,\)

then the full history \(h_{td}=B_d+y_{td}\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{k}\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}h_{td} \geq k\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}B_d+ \left\lfloor k\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}r_d\right\rfloor = \left\lfloor k\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}q_d\right\rfloor. \)

The equality holds because \(k\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}B_d\) is an integer.

If \(R=0\), giving everyone the common bundle already establishes these guarantees. Henceforth suppose \(R>0\).

2. Define a clone population using binary integers

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses and supplies, and set

\(\displaystyle N=kD,\qquad n=N/k=D.\)

Then \(Np_i\), \(Nq_c\), and \(Nr_c\) are integers, and \(N\) is divisible by \(k\). Although \(N\) may be numerically large, its binary length is \(O(L+\log k)\).

Use clone labels \(a\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\). Put

\(\displaystyle A_0=0,\qquad A_i=N\sum_{j=1}^{i}p_j.\)

The clones of type \(i\) have labels in the integer interval \([A_{i-1},A_i)\). These intervals partition the clone population and contain exactly \(Np_i\) labels.

On every day, the residual stock of category \(c\) is

\(\displaystyle S_c=Nr_c,\)

and the total number of residual goods is

\(\displaystyle M=\sum_c S_c=NR.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \left\lceil M/N\right\rceil=\lceil R\rceil\leq m.\)

The allocation below therefore has at most \(m\) round-robin rounds per day.

3. Cyclic greedy picking

Fix a deterministic category order for breaking valuation ties. All clones of a type use this same tie-breaking rule.

On day \(t\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), define the offset

\(\displaystyle o_t=(t-1)n.\)

At pick index \(z\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}\), the picking clone is

\(\displaystyle a_t(z)=(z-o_t)\bmod N.\)

That clone takes a copy of a highest-valued category with positive remaining stock.

Thus each complete round visits every clone once, in a fixed cyclic order for that day. The position of clone \(a\) within a round is

\(\displaystyle \rho_t(a)=(a+o_t)\bmod N.\)

Its pick indices are exactly

\(\displaystyle bN+\rho_t(a),\qquad b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\quad bN+\rho_t(a)<M.\)

Denote its residual history by \(y_{a,tc}\). We next prove fairness for this finite allocation. Section 6 shows how to compute it without enumerating the clones or goods.

4. Daily SD-EF1

Fix a day \(t\), an evaluating clone \(a\) of type \(i\), and another clone \(a'\).

A basic property of greedy picking is the following: if clone \(a\) picks before a particular good is taken by another clone, that good is still available at \(a\)'s pick. Hence the good selected by \(a\) has value at least as large, according to type \(i\).

If \(a=a'\), the comparison is immediate. Otherwise their round positions differ.

Case 1: \(\rho_t(a)<\rho_t(a')\)

For every pick made by \(a'\) in round \(b\), clone \(a\)'s pick in the same round exists and occurs earlier:

\(\displaystyle bN+\rho_t(a)<bN+\rho_t(a')<M.\)

Match each good taken by \(a'\) to the good taken by \(a\) in that round. The matched goods of \(a\) are distinct, and each is weakly preferred by type \(i\) to its matched good.

It follows that \(a\)'s residual bundle SD-dominates the entire residual bundle of \(a'\).

Case 2: \(\rho_t(a)>\rho_t(a')\)

If \(a'\)'s residual bundle is nonempty, remove its first picked good. Every remaining good of \(a'\) was picked in a round \(b\geq1\).

Match that good to \(a\)'s pick in round \(b-1\). This pick exists and occurs earlier, since

\(\displaystyle (b-1)N+\rho_t(a)<bN+\rho_t(a')<M. \)

Again, the matched goods of \(a\) are distinct and weakly preferred by type \(i\).

Thus removing one good from \(a'\)'s residual bundle makes it SD-dominated by \(a\)'s residual bundle.

These matchings apply simultaneously to every weak threshold \(H_i(c)\): whenever a matched good of \(a'\) belongs to \(H_i(c)\), its matching good of \(a\) also belongs to that set.

After adding the common bundle, the same comparisons remain valid. If the target residual bundle is empty, the evaluating full bundle already SD-dominates the target full bundle; when the latter is nonempty, removing any of its goods preserves that dominance.

Therefore the full allocation is SD-EF1 on every day, with ties treated weakly as required.

5. Overall threshold guarantees

Fix a clone \(a\) of type \(i\) and a threshold set \(H=H_i(c)\). Its residual stock on each day is

\(\displaystyle S_H=\sum_{d\in H}S_d=N\sum_{d\in H}r_d.\)

At every pick index \(z<S_H\), at least one residual good from \(H\) remains: only \(z\) goods in total have been removed, whereas initially \(H\) contained \(S_H\) goods.

Consequently, whenever clone \(a\) picks at such an index, it selects a category in \(H\). Indeed, its selected category is weakly preferred to an available category in \(H\), so it also belongs to \(H\).

We now count these early picks across all days.

Write

\(\displaystyle a=jn+\alpha,\qquad 0\leq\alpha<n.\)

Because the offsets are \(0,n,\ldots,(k-1)n\), the round positions of \(a\) across the days are precisely

\(\displaystyle \{\rho_t(a):1\leq t\leq k\}=\{\alpha,\alpha+n,\ldots,\alpha+(k-1)n\}.\)

Hence its pick indices across all days, before truncation at \(M\), are exactly

\(\displaystyle \alpha+\ell n,\qquad \ell\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\)

each occurring once. Since \(S_H\leq M\), every such index below \(S_H\) is an actual pick.

Write \(S_H=un+v\), where \(0\leq v<n\). For every \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,u-1\}\),

\(\displaystyle \alpha+\ell n\leq(n-1)+(u-1)n=un-1<S_H.\)

Thus \(a\) has at least \(u\) early picks, all selecting goods in \(H\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{k}\sum_{d\in H}y_{a,td} \geq u = \left\lfloor S_H/n\right\rfloor = \left\lfloor k\sum_{d\in H}r_d\right\rfloor. \)

Adding the common bundle yields the claimed stronger bound:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{k}\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}h_{a,td} \geq \left\lfloor k\sum_{d\in H_i(c)}q_d\right\rfloor. \)

Choose a most-preferred category \(c_i^\star\) for type \(i\). It belongs to every \(H_i(c)\). Since \(\lfloor w\rfloor+1\geq\lceil w\rceil\), adding one copy of \(c_i^\star\) establishes all the overall SD-PROP1 inequalities simultaneously.

The outside-copy requirement

The resulting census has denominator dividing \(N\). Each clone receives at most \(m\) residual goods per day, so its total number of category-\(c\) copies is at most \(k(B_c+m)\).

Replicate the resulting clone allocation by the integer factor

\(\displaystyle F=m+1+\sum_c B_c.\)

Because \(Nq_c\) is a positive integer,

\(\displaystyle kFNq_c\geq kF>k(B_c+m).\)

The enlarged realization therefore contains more category-\(c\) copies than any single recipient owns, for every category \(c\). In particular, a copy of \(c_i^\star\) exists outside each recipient's bundle. Replication preserves the census.

6. Compute the allocation in polynomially many blocks

It remains to compress the greedy simulation.

Within one round on day \(t\), clone labels are traversed cyclically. Because each type occupies one consecutive label interval, this traversal splits into at most \(\tau+1\) runs. Within each run:

  • all picking clones have the same type;
  • labels increase consecutively without wrapping.

The final partial round has the same bound on its number of runs.

Suppose a run has \(u\) unprocessed labels, and its type's highest-valued available category \(c\) has remaining stock \(W_c\). The next

\(\displaystyle \ell=\min\{u,W_c\}\)

clones all select category \(c\). This follows because no other category's stock changes during those picks, and \(c\) remains available until its stock is exhausted.

Process those picks together. Record their day, category, and consecutive label interval, and subtract \(\ell\) from the stock.

Each operation ends either a type run or a category's stock. There are at most \(m(\tau+1)\) runs per day and at most \(m\) category exhaustions. Thus the total number \(T\) of recorded blocks satisfies

\(\displaystyle T\leq k\bigl(m(\tau+1)+m\bigr).\)

The required runs and their lengths are computed directly from the type boundaries and the cyclic offsets. No iteration over the \(N\) labels is needed.

7. Extract the explicitly listed census

Collect and sort:

  • the type boundaries \(A_0,\ldots,A_\tau\);
  • both endpoints of every recorded label interval.

Partition \([0,N)\) at these endpoints. On every resulting nonempty interval \(J=[u,v)\), all clones have the same type and the same history: membership in every recorded block is constant throughout \(J\).

For that type \(i\) and full history \(h\), assign mass

\(\displaystyle x_{i,h}=(v-u)/N.\)

Merge entries with equal pairs \((i,h)\) by adding their masses. The number of entries is at most \(\tau+2T\).

The type-mass equations hold because the intervals of type \(i\) together contain exactly \(Np_i\) labels:

\(\displaystyle \sum_h x_{i,h}=p_i.\)

Every day's greedy allocation consumes all \(S_c\) residual category-\(c\) goods. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i,h}x_{i,h}h_{tc} = B_c+\frac{S_c}{N} = q_c. \)

Every supported history occurs among actual clones, so the fairness guarantees proved above hold for every required supported pair.

8. Bit complexity and conclusion

The construction uses exact arithmetic with polynomial-size integers:

  • \(N\) has binary length \(O(L+\log k)\).
  • Label endpoints and residual stocks are bounded by \(N\), and \(M<mN\).
  • Each history entry is at most \(B_c+m\).
  • Every output mass has denominator dividing \(N\).
  • There are polynomially many runs, allocation blocks, and census entries.

Category choices require only exact comparisons of the input rational values. Sorting interval endpoints, constructing histories, and merging equal entries also take polynomial time. The algorithm processes \(k\) days, consistent with unary encoding of \(k\).

This proves a deterministic polynomial-time construction for the exact rational-mass census problem, including weak ties and the stated outside-copy interpretation.
