The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite ALC agenda \(\Phi\) of \(m\) axioms, a finite support of \(\tau\) complete rankings \(\pi\) with binary-rational masses \(\mu_\pi\geq 0\) summing to \(1\), and a rational threshold vector \(\theta\in\mathbb{Q}^m\), decide whether there is a consistent subset \(S\subseteq\Phi\) such that \(q_r(S)=\sum_\pi\mu_\pi\mathbf{1}\{\text{the axiom ranked }r\text{ by }\pi\text{ belongs to }S\}\) is lexicographically greater than \(\theta\); the optimization version finds a lexicographically maximal consistent subset.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An agenda \(\Phi\) consists of \(m\) explicitly encoded \(\mathcal{ALC}\) axioms: general concept inclusions, concept assertions, and role assertions; arbitrary TBoxes and ABoxes are allowed. A subset is consistent if it has an \(\mathcal{ALC}\) model.
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings \(\pi\) of \(\Phi\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_\pi\), with \(\sum_\pi\mu_\pi=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
Write \(\pi(r)\) for the axiom ranked \(r\), with rank \(1\) most preferred. For \(S\subseteq\Phi\), define \(q_r(S)=\sum_\pi\mu_\pi\mathbf{1}\{\pi(r)\in S\}\) and \(q(S)=(q_1(S),\ldots,q_m(S))\).
The input also contains \(\theta\in\mathbb{Q}^m\), encoded in binary, with no restriction on its coordinates. Decide whether some consistent \(S\subseteq\Phi\) satisfies \(q(S)>_{\mathrm{lex}}\theta\), meaning that its first differing coordinate is larger. The associated optimization problem finds a consistent subset maximizing \(q(S)\); any maximizer is acceptable.
Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including axiom encodings, rankings, masses, and threshold coordinates. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete optimal repair with explicitly listed voters and rank-count vectors is \(\mathrm{EXPTIME}\)-complete for \(\mathcal{ALC}\), and \(\mathrm{PSPACE}\)-complete with acyclic TBoxes ([source](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2018/0268.pdf)).
No result settling this exact rational-mass formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified that rank 1 is most preferred and that larger coordinates at the first difference are better, translating the reversed lexicographic notation into the graded question's convention.
  • Specified the unrestricted ALC ontology domain: general concept inclusions, concept assertions, and role assertions, with arbitrary TBoxes and ABoxes.
  • Removed zero-mass entries from the supplied list so that tau counts precisely the positive-mass support.
  • Defined L as total input bit length, including the logical axiom encodings.
  • Allowed any optimizing subset when several subsets have the same maximal vector.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{EXPTIME}\)-complete, and therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. In fact, hardness holds with \(\tau=1\), mass \(1\), and even \(m=1\). Consequently, there is no algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), since \(\mathrm{P}\subsetneq\mathrm{EXPTIME}\).

Both decision and optimization admit a deterministic running-time bound of

\(O\!\left(2^m\left[2^{p(L)}+(m+1)(\tau+1)L^2\right]\right)\),

for a fixed polynomial \(p\).

Hardness, even for one axiom and one ranking

We use the following standard theorem: given an explicitly encoded \(\mathcal{ALC}\) concept \(C\) and a general TBox \(T\), deciding whether some model \(\mathcal I\) of \(T\) satisfies \(C^{\mathcal I}\ne\varnothing\) is \(\mathrm{EXPTIME}\)-complete. Both arbitrary concepts and general concept inclusions are allowed in the present task, so this theorem applies. See [Meier and Schneider, *Generalized Satisfiability for the Description Logic ALC*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.0853).

Let an instance of this problem be

\(T=\{C_i\sqsubseteq D_i:1\le i\le k\}\), together with \(C\).

Define

\(E_T=\bigsqcap_{i=1}^{k}(\neg C_i\sqcup D_i)\),

where the empty conjunction denotes \(\top\). Choose a fresh role name \(s\), absent from \(T\) and \(C\), and form the single GCI

\(\alpha=\bigl(\top\sqsubseteq E_T\sqcap\exists s.C\bigr)\).

Claim. The ontology \(\{\alpha\}\) is consistent if and only if \(C\) is satisfiable with respect to \(T\).

*Forward direction.* Suppose \(\mathcal I\models\alpha\). Every domain element belongs to \(E_T^{\mathcal I}\). For every \(i\), this implies

\(C_i^{\mathcal I}\subseteq D_i^{\mathcal I}\),

so \(\mathcal I\models T\). The domain is nonempty. Choose a domain element \(x\). Since \(x\in(\exists s.C)^{\mathcal I}\), some \(s\)-successor of \(x\) belongs to \(C^{\mathcal I}\). Thus \(C^{\mathcal I}\ne\varnothing\).

*Reverse direction.* Suppose \(\mathcal I\models T\) and choose \(d\in C^{\mathcal I}\). Extend \(\mathcal I\) by interpreting the fresh role as

\(s^{\mathcal I}=\Delta^{\mathcal I}\times\{d\}\).

Because \(\mathcal I\models T\), every domain element belongs to \(E_T^{\mathcal I}\). Every domain element also has an \(s\)-successor in \(C^{\mathcal I}\), namely \(d\). Hence the extension satisfies \(\alpha\). Freshness of \(s\) ensures that this extension preserves the interpretations of \(T\) and \(C\). This proves the claim.

Now construct the target instance:

  • Agenda \(\Phi=\{\alpha\}\).
  • The unique ranking \(\pi(1)=\alpha\), with mass \(\mu_\pi=1\).
  • Threshold \(\theta=(0)\).

This is a valid society with \(m=\tau=1\). Its only subsets are \(\varnothing\) and \(\{\alpha\}\), with scores

\(q(\varnothing)=(0)\), and \(q(\{\alpha\})=(1)\).

Therefore,

\(\exists\,S\subseteq\Phi\text{ consistent with }q(S)>_{\mathrm{lex}}\theta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \{\alpha\}\text{ is consistent} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C\text{ is satisfiable with respect to }T\).

The encoding of \(\alpha\) contains each source concept once, together with conjunctions, negations, and one existential restriction. Its size is polynomial in the source input length, and the ranking, mass, and threshold have constant size. This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathrm{EXPTIME}\)-hardness.

Exact rational arithmetic takes polynomial time

Write each mass as \(\mu_\pi=a_\pi/b_\pi\), with positive integers \(a_\pi,b_\pi\). The argument covers arbitrary binary-encoded rational masses, including dyadic ones.

Set

\(D=\prod_{\pi}b_\pi\), and \(u_\pi=a_\pi(D/b_\pi)\).

The total bit length of the denominators is at most \(L\), so \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Since the masses sum to \(1\),

\(\sum_\pi u_\pi=D\).

For any subset \(S\), define the integer rank scores

\(t_r(S)=\sum_\pi u_\pi\mathbf 1\{\pi(r)\in S\}\).

Then

\(q_r(S)=t_r(S)/D\), with \(0\le t_r(S)\le D\).

Thus every score numerator has \(O(L)\) bits.

Write a threshold coordinate as \(\theta_r=c_r/d_r\), where \(c_r\) may be signed and \(d_r>0\). Exact comparison of \(q_r(S)\) with \(\theta_r\) reduces to comparing the signed integers

\(d_r t_r(S)\quad\text{and}\quad c_rD\).

Their bit lengths are \(O(L)\). Schoolbook multiplication takes \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. Computing the scores requires \(m\tau\) contributions, and lexicographic comparison examines at most \(m\) coordinates.

Including common-denominator preprocessing, a sufficient bound for these arithmetic operations is

\(O\!\left((m+1)(\tau+1)L^2\right)\).

This bound handles arbitrary threshold coordinates and requires no expansion of masses into explicitly listed voters.

Upper bound and optimization

Consistency of an explicitly encoded \(\mathcal{ALC}\) knowledge base with arbitrary TBoxes and ABoxes is decidable in deterministic exponential time. In particular, there is a fixed polynomial \(p\) giving a bound \(2^{p(L)}\) for every subset of the input agenda. See [Baader, Horrocks, and Sattler, *Description Logics*, Section 3.4.1](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/ian.horrocks/Publications/download/2007/BaHS07a.pdf).

Enumerate all \(2^m\) subsets \(S\subseteq\Phi\). For each subset, test consistency and compute its exact score. Accept if a consistent subset exceeds the threshold.

Enumeration examines every possible witness, and both tests are exact. Hence the algorithm decides the problem correctly in time

\(O\!\left(2^m\left[2^{p(L)}+(m+1)(\tau+1)L^2\right]\right)\).

Because the agenda and support are explicitly listed, \(m,\tau\le L\). This bound is \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(L)}\), proving membership in \(\mathrm{EXPTIME}\).

For optimization, retain a consistent subset with the greatest score encountered. The empty ontology is consistent, and the search space is finite, so a maximizer exists. Two score vectors have the same denominator \(D\), making their lexicographic comparison an exact comparison of their integer numerator vectors. The same running-time bound applies.

Optimization is also \(\mathrm{EXPTIME}\)-hard under polynomial-time oracle reductions: in the one-axiom construction above, the unique maximizer is \(\{\alpha\}\) when that singleton is consistent, and \(\varnothing\) otherwise. Thus any optimizer decides the source satisfiability problem.

The exact rational-mass formulation is therefore settled without needing to transfer the discrete-voter hardness theorem.
