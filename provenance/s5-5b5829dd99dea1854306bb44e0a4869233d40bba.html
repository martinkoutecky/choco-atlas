The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) recurring project classes with rational sizes \(s_j>0\), values \(v_j\ge 0\), and per-capita supplies \(q_j\ge 0\), plus \(\tau\) agent types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\) and budgets \(B_t>0\), can one compute in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) an allocation with polynomial-size support, where \(L\) is the input bit length? A bundle is an integral vector \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^m\) with \(s(b)=\sum_j s_jb_j\le B_t\), and \(x_{t,b}\ge 0\) is the mass of type \(t\) receiving bundle \(b\); require \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_j\le q_j\) for every class \(j\). For every pair of positive-mass bundles \(b,c\) assigned to types \(t,u\), respectively, and every nonempty integral subbundle \(z\le c\) with \(s(z)\le B_t\), require some \(j\) with \(z_j>0\) such that \(v(b)\ge \tfrac12(v(z)-v_j)\). Apply the same condition to charity comparisons, where \(z\) may use only classes with positive residual supply \(r_j=q_j-\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_j>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge1\) project classes have binary rational sizes \(s_j>0\), common values \(v_j\ge0\), and per-capita supplies \(q_j\ge0\).
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and binary rational budgets \(B_t>0\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
For \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), write \(s(b)=\sum_j s_jb_j\) and \(v(b)=\sum_jv_jb_j\). Bundles assigned to type \(t\) must satisfy \(s(b)\le B_t\); empty bundles are allowed.
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_j\le q_j\). Its support consists of the pairs \((t,b)\) with \(x_{t,b}>0\).
Call it \(\tfrac12\)-EF1 if, for every two supported pairs \((t,b),(u,c)\) and every nonzero integral vector \(z\le c\) with \(s(z)\le B_t\), some class \(j\) with \(z_j>0\) satisfies \(v(b)\ge\tfrac12(v(z)-v_j)\); inequalities between vectors are coordinatewise. Comparisons include bundles received within the same type.
Set \(r_j=q_j-\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_j\). For each supported \((t,b)\), impose the same inequality for every nonzero integral \(z\) with \(s(z)\le B_t\) and \(z_j=0\) whenever \(r_j=0\). Charity comparisons have no multiplicity bound on classes with \(r_j>0\); charity itself makes no comparisons.

Is computing a \(\tfrac12\)-EF1 allocation with support and encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or determining that none exists, solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For explicitly listed finite agents and indivisible goods with common additive values, a budget-feasible \(\tfrac12\)-EF1 allocation, including comparisons against charity, is computable in polynomial time. [Gan–Li–Wu](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14446)
Polynomial-time computation of exact EF2 allocations is also known for that discrete setting. [Barman–Khan–Shyam–Sreenivas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08168)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Defined the omitted additive bundle value as v(b)=sum_j v_j b_j, with values common to all types.
  • Made the output encoding explicit: supported bundle vectors use binary integer coordinates and assigned masses are binary rationals.
  • Interpreted charity comparisons literally as specified: positive residual supply permits arbitrary integral multiplicities within the comparing type's budget, without a bound by the residual quantity.
  • Allowed a report of nonexistence because the continuous question supplies no feasibility promise and the discrete existence guarantee does not itself establish existence under the stated charity convention.
  • Explicitly allowed empty assigned bundles while restricting EF1 comparisons to nonempty subbundles, avoiding the vacuous existential-item defect in the literal discrete definition.

Result

The problem is solvable in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$. Every instance admits an allocation, so the nonexistence alternative never occurs.

I give a construction with at most $m+2\tau-1$ supported pairs and polynomial encoding length. The construction uses the minimum-value and maximum-density principle appearing in [Gan–Li–Wu](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.14446), but operates directly on masses. All feasibility, fairness, and complexity arguments for the mass model are proved below.

1. Preliminaries

For a nonempty bundle $z$, define
$$ W(z)=v(z)-\max_{j:z_j>0}v_j, $$
and put $W(0)=0$. The required comparison against $z$ is equivalent to
$$ 2v(b)\ge W(z). $$
Indeed, removing a copy from a class of maximum value minimizes the remaining value.

We will also use the following monotonicity property:
$$ z\le c \quad\Longrightarrow\quad W(z)\le W(c). $$
To prove it, choose a maximum-value copy $g$ in $c$. If $z$ contains that copy, removing it from $z$ leaves value at most $v(c)-v_g$. If it does not, then already $v(z)\le v(c)-v_g$. Either way, $W(z)\le W(c)$.

Classes of value zero can be left entirely unallocated and omitted from the algorithm. This preserves the fairness question: deleting zero-value copies from any comparison bundle preserves $W(z)$ whenever a positive-value copy remains, and a bundle consisting only of zero-value copies has $W(z)=0$. Thus, throughout the construction, every class considered for allocation has strictly positive value.

Write the distinct budgets as
$$ \beta_1<\cdots<\beta_d, \qquad d\le\tau, $$
and define
$$ H_\ell=\sum_{t:B_t>\beta_\ell}\mu_t. $$

2. Mass configurations and their feasibility

A cohort consists of a positive rational mass $w_a$ and an integral bundle $b^a$. Cohorts initially have no assigned types. Their masses sum to one.

For a configuration of cohorts, define its capacity slacks by
$$ \Delta_\ell = H_\ell-\sum_{a:s(b^a)>\beta_\ell}w_a. $$

Lemma 1. A configuration can be assigned to the given types, splitting cohort masses if necessary, if and only if
$$ \Delta_\ell\ge0 \qquad\text{for every }\ell. $$

Proof. Necessity follows because every bundle of size greater than $\beta_\ell$ must be assigned to a type whose budget is greater than $\beta_\ell$.

For sufficiency, sort cohorts by bundle size and types by budget. Represent each list as a partition of $[0,1]$, with segment lengths equal to cohort masses and type masses, respectively. Assign each positive intersection of two segments to the corresponding type and bundle.

Fix a type of budget $B$. The slack inequality at $B$ implies
$$ \sum_{a:s(b^a)\le B}w_a \ge \sum_{t:B_t\le B}\mu_t. $$
Consequently, every cohort segment of size greater than $B$ starts at or after the end of all type segments with budgets at most $B$. Such a cohort cannot overlap this type segment with positive length. Every assigned bundle therefore fits its assigned budget. $\square$

For each class, let
$$ r_j=q_j-\sum_a w_ab^a_j. $$
A configuration is feasible when all $r_j$ and all $\Delta_\ell$ are nonnegative.

Since $H_d=0$, feasibility implies that every bundle has size at most $\beta_d$, and
$$ \Delta_d=0. $$

For a cohort $a$, define
$$ C_a=\min\{\beta_\ell:\beta_\ell\ge s(b^a),\ \Delta_\ell=0\}. $$
This is always defined because $\beta_d$ belongs to the set.

Lemma 2. One copy of class $j$ can be added to a positive mass of cohort $a$, preserving feasibility, precisely when
$$ r_j>0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad s(b^a)+s_j\le C_a. $$

Proof. Upgrading mass $\delta>0$ from $b^a$ to $b^a+e_j$ reduces $r_j$ by $\delta$. It also reduces by $\delta$ exactly those slacks with
$$ s(b^a)\le\beta_\ell<s(b^a)+s_j. $$
Thus a positive upgrade is possible exactly when $r_j>0$ and every crossed slack is positive.

If the new size exceeds $C_a$, it crosses the tight constraint at $C_a$, making such an upgrade impossible. If the new size is at most $C_a$, every crossed constraint is strictly below the first tight constraint at or above the current size, and hence has positive slack. $\square$

These observations yield two useful monotonicity facts.

  • Every residual $r_j$ and every slack $\Delta_\ell$ is nonincreasing during upgrades.
  • Along any history of a bundle, including a descendant created by splitting a cohort, $C_a$ is nonincreasing.

For the second fact, suppose a budget $\beta$ is tight while the bundle has size at most $\beta$. No positive mass of that bundle can subsequently cross $\beta$: doing so would violate the tight constraint. The budget remains tight, so it remains an upper bound on the first tight budget above every descendant bundle.

3. The symbolic greedy construction

Fix a total order of classes by decreasing density
$$ \rho_j=\frac{v_j}{s_j}, $$
breaking density ties by class index. Give cohorts persistent distinct indices, also used to break ties.

Initialize one active cohort of mass one with the empty bundle. All cohorts remain part of the configuration after they are frozen, but frozen cohorts receive no further goods.

Repeat the following operation while an active cohort remains.

  • Choose an active cohort $a$ minimizing $v(b^a)$, breaking ties by cohort index.
  • Among classes satisfying

$$ r_j>0,\qquad s(b^a)+s_j\le C_a, $$
choose the first class in the density order.

  • If such a class $j$ exists, let

$$ \delta= \min\left( \{w_a,r_j\} \cup \{\Delta_\ell:s(b^a)\le\beta_\ell<s(b^a)+s_j\} \right). $$
The selected class is feasible for positive mass, so $\delta>0$.

Upgrade mass $\delta$ to $b^a+e_j$. If $\delta=w_a$, update the cohort in place. Otherwise retain mass $w_a-\delta$ at $b^a$ and create a new active cohort of mass $\delta$ at $b^a+e_j$.

  • If no class is available, put $T=C_a$ and freeze every active cohort whose bundle has size at most $T$.

Every operation preserves feasibility by Lemma 2.

3.1 The number of cohorts is polynomial

A split occurs only when $\delta<w_a$. In that case, the minimum defining $\delta$ exhausts a positive residual or makes a previously positive capacity slack zero.

Residuals and slacks never increase. There can therefore be at most $m$ residual-exhaustion events and at most $d-1$ newly tight capacity constraints; the largest-budget constraint is tight initially. Hence the total number of cohort identities ever created satisfies
$$ K\le 1+m+(d-1)=m+d. $$

Each persistent cohort identity undergoes only finitely many upgrades: its integral bundle coordinates increase and satisfy
$$ b^a_j\le\left\lfloor\frac{\beta_d}{s_j}\right\rfloor. $$
There are finitely many splits and freezes as well. Thus the symbolic process terminates. Section 6 will implement it without executing potentially exponentially many upgrades.

3.2 Densities along a bundle history never increase

Suppose class $j$ is selected for a cohort. Any more preferred class $h$ either has zero residual, or satisfies
$$ s(b^a)+s_h>C_a. $$
Zero residual remains zero. In the other case, bundle size can only increase and its first tight upper budget can only decrease, so $h$ remains infeasible along every descendant history.

Thus the selected classes along a bundle history move only forward in the fixed density order. In particular, all earlier copies have density at least that of all later copies.

3.3 Budgets associated with a freezing event

Suppose the construction freezes cohorts at threshold $T$. This threshold is tight, so
$$ \sum_{a:s(b^a)\le T}w_a = \sum_{t:B_t\le T}\mu_t. $$
Afterward every active bundle has size greater than $T$. The next freezing threshold, if any, is therefore strictly greater than $T$.

Consequently, in the sorted assignment of Lemma 1, every cohort frozen at this event is assigned only to budgets at most $T$. Every cohort frozen later had size greater than $T$ at the present event.

4. Exact EF1 against the residual classes at a freezing event

This section proves fairness against unlimited multiplicities of the classes with positive residual.

Consider a freezing event. Let $a$ be the minimum-value cohort that triggered it, let
$$ b=b^a,\qquad T=C_a, $$
and evaluate residuals at this event.

For a residual class $h$, define the subbundle
$$ P_h=\text{the copies in }b\text{ having density at least }\rho_h. $$

Lemma 3. If $r_h>0$ at the freezing event, then
$$ s(P_h)+s_h>T. $$

Proof. If all copies of $b$ have density at least $\rho_h$, then $P_h=b$. Since no class is available for the triggering cohort, Lemma 2 gives
$$ s(b)+s_h>T. $$

Otherwise, examine the first upgrade along the history of $b$ that added a copy of density strictly smaller than $\rho_h$. Let $p$ be the bundle immediately before that upgrade. All copies of $p$ have density at least $\rho_h$, so $p\le P_h$.

The residual of class $h$ was positive then, because it is still positive at the freezing event. Nevertheless, the algorithm selected a lower-density class. Class $h$ must therefore have been infeasible, giving
$$ s(p)+s_h>C_{\mathrm{then}}. $$
The first tight upper budget is nonincreasing along this history, so
$$ C_{\mathrm{then}}\ge T. $$
It follows that
$$ s(P_h)+s_h\ge s(p)+s_h>T. $$
$\square$

Lemma 4. For every nonzero integral bundle $z$ with
$$ s(z)\le T, \qquad z_j=0\text{ whenever }r_j=0, $$
some copy $e$ in $z$ satisfies
$$ v(z-e)\le v(b). $$

Proof. Choose a densest copy $e$ of $z$, belonging to class $h$. Lemma 3 gives
$$ s(z-e)\le T-s_h<s(P_h). $$
Every copy of $z-e$ has density at most $\rho_h$, whereas every copy of $P_h$ has density at least $\rho_h$. Therefore
$$ v(z-e) \le \rho_hs(z-e) \le \rho_hs(P_h) \le v(P_h) \le v(b). $$
This also covers the case $z-e=0$. $\square$

Every other cohort frozen at this event has value at least $v(b)$ and is assigned a budget at most $T$. Lemma 4 therefore gives exact EF1 against the residual classes at this event for every cohort frozen here.

The final positive-residual classes are a subset of the positive-residual classes at each earlier event. Hence every final receiver satisfies exact EF1 against the final charity.

5. Fairness between allocated bundles

5.1 An invariant for active cohorts

Throughout the construction,
$$ v(b^a)\ge W(b^c) \qquad \text{for every two active cohorts }a,c. $$

Initially all bundles are empty. Suppose an upgrade is made to a minimum-value active bundle $b$. Removing the newly added copy from $b+e_j$ leaves $b$, so
$$ W(b+e_j)\le v(b). $$
Every other active bundle has value at least $v(b)$. Thus every active receiver satisfies the invariant against the upgraded bundle.

The upgraded bundle also satisfies the invariant against all old bundles: its value is at least the old minimum value, which already satisfied the invariant against them. If a split retains part of the old bundle, its previous comparisons remain valid. Freezing cohorts only restricts the invariant to a subset. This proves the invariant by induction.

5.2 Comparisons against bundles frozen no later

Suppose donor bundle $c$ is frozen before receiver bundle $b$, or at the same event. At the donor’s freezing event, the receiver’s then-current ancestral bundle $p$ was active, and the invariant gives
$$ v(p)\ge W(c). $$
The receiver’s value subsequently only increases. For every $z\le c$,
$$ v(b)\ge v(p)\ge W(c)\ge W(z). $$
These comparisons satisfy exact EF1, regardless of the receiver’s budget.

5.3 Comparisons against bundles frozen later

Fix a freezing event with triggering bundle $b_0$ and threshold $T$. Consider any receiver bundle $b$ frozen at this event. Then
$$ v(b)\ge v(b_0), $$
and its assigned budget is at most $T$.

Let $c$ be a bundle frozen later, and let $P$ be its ancestral bundle at the present event. Since $P$ remained active,
$$ s(P)>T. $$
The active-cohort invariant gives
$$ v(b_0)\ge W(P)=v(P)-v_g, $$
where $g$ is a maximum-value copy in $P$.

Consider a nonzero comparison subbundle $z\le c$ feasible for the receiver. Then
$$ s(z)\le T<s(P). $$
Copies of a class may temporarily be regarded as individually labeled, so intersections of multisets are well defined.

All copies in $P$ have density at least that of all copies in $c-P$. This implies
$$ v(z)\le v(P). $$
Here is the explicit calculation. Put $z_1=z\cap P$ and $z_2=z-P$, and let $\rho$ be the minimum density of a copy in $P$. Then
$$ s(P-z_1)>s(z_2), $$
and
$$ v(P-z_1)\ge \rho s(P-z_1) \ge \rho s(z_2)\ge v(z_2). $$
Adding $v(z_1)$ proves the asserted value inequality.

If $g\in z$, removing $g$ gives
$$ v(z-g)\le v(P)-v_g\le v(b_0)\le v(b). $$

Now suppose $g\notin z$. Then
$$ v(z_1)\le v(P)-v_g\le v(b_0). $$
Every copy in $z_2$ was added after the present event, so its class had positive residual at this event. Also $s(z_2)\le T$.

If $z_2\ne0$, Lemma 4 supplies a copy $e\in z_2$ such that
$$ v(z_2-e)\le v(b_0). $$
Consequently,
$$ v(z-e) = v(z_1)+v(z_2-e) \le 2v(b_0) \le 2v(b). $$
If $z_2=0$, then already $v(z)\le v(b_0)$, and removing any copy suffices.

Thus every comparison against a later-frozen bundle satisfies $\tfrac12$-EF1. Together with the preceding cases and the charity argument, this proves fairness of the final allocation.

6. Polynomial-time batching

The symbolic process can require exponentially many one-copy upgrades. We now batch them using exact arithmetic.

A phase begins with the current active cohorts, residuals, and slacks. For every active cohort $i$, compute its currently selected class $j_i$, or record that none is available.

Choose a positive integer $Q$ divisible by the denominators of all positive input values. Define integer priorities
$$ P_i=Qv(b^i), \qquad A_i=Qv_{j_i} $$
when $j_i$ exists. Since zero-value classes were omitted, $A_i\ge1$.

Let $\beta(i)$ be the smallest distinct budget at least $s(b^i)$, and put
$$ n_i= \left\lfloor \frac{\beta(i)-s(b^i)}{s_{j_i}} \right\rfloor. $$

The next $n_i$ full-cohort upgrades by class $j_i$ keep this cohort within its current budget interval. Call them safe upgrades.

During safe upgrades:

  • no capacity slack changes;
  • the selected class remains feasible;
  • more preferred classes remain infeasible;
  • the selected class remains unchanged until a supply is exhausted.

The priorities immediately before these upgrades form the finite arithmetic stream
$$ (P_i+kA_i,i), \qquad 0\le k<n_i, $$
ordered lexicographically. The cohort’s first event after its safe upgrades has key
$$ (P_i+n_iA_i,i). $$
For a cohort with no available class, this event has key $(P_i,i)$.

Let $E$ be the least of these latter event keys. Before $E$, the symbolic process merges the safe arithmetic streams in lexicographic order, unless a class supply is exhausted earlier.

6.1 Locating the first supply exhaustion

Restrict each safe stream to keys strictly below $E$. For each selected class $j$ and integer $p$, define
$$ F_j(p)= \sum_{i:j_i=j} w_i\, \#\{k:0\le k<n_i,\ (P_i+kA_i,i)<E,\ P_i+kA_i\le p\}. $$

Every count is computable by integer division and clipping. Thus $F_j(p)$ is computable in polynomial time.

Write $P_E$ for the integer-priority component of $E$.

If
$$ F_j(P_E)<r_j, $$
class $j$ is not exhausted before $E$.

Otherwise, binary search finds the smallest integer $p$ with
$$ F_j(p)\ge r_j. $$
At priority $p$, each stream contributes at most one event because $A_i>0$. Scan those events by cohort index to locate the first event whose cumulative consumption reaches or exceeds $r_j$. This is the exact event at which class $j$ is exhausted, possibly by a partial-cohort upgrade.

Take the earliest such supply event over all classes, or $E$ if no supply event precedes it. Call the selected event $K$.

For every stream, count its events strictly before $K$. If this count is $c_i$, batch them by replacing
$$ b^i\longleftarrow b^i+c_ie_{j_i}, $$
and subtracting $w_ic_i$ from the corresponding residual. All these upgrades are safe and occur before any supply exhaustion, so this exactly reproduces the symbolic process up to $K$.

6.2 Handling the phase-ending event

If $K$ is a supply event, perform its one-copy upgrade on mass
$$ \delta=\min\{w_i,r_{j_i}\}. $$
It exhausts that class, and may split the cohort.

Otherwise $K=E$. Recompute the selected class for its minimum-priority cohort.

  • If no class is available, perform the freezing operation.
  • If its selected class has changed, begin a new phase.
  • If its selected class is unchanged, perform one symbolic upgrade. It crosses at least one distinct-budget threshold for the upgraded mass, because all safe upgrades for this cohort have been completed.

This supplies an exact implementation of the symbolic process.

6.3 Number of phases

Every phase ends with at least one of the following events:

  • a class supply is exhausted;
  • a cohort splits;
  • a whole cohort crosses a distinct-budget threshold;
  • a cohort’s selected class moves forward in the fixed class order;
  • at least one cohort is frozen.

There are at most $m$ supply exhaustions and at most $m+d-1$ splits.

There are at most $K\le m+d$ cohort identities. Each identity’s bundle size increases, so it crosses at most $d$ budget thresholds. Its class choices move only forward, so it changes class at most $m$ times. Each identity is frozen once.

It follows that the number of phases is
$$ O\bigl((m+d)^2\bigr). $$

7. Bit complexity and output size

Let $D$ be divisible by the denominators of all input masses and supplies. It can be chosen as their denominator product, with
$$ \log D=O(L). $$

Throughout the algorithm, all cohort masses, residuals, and capacity slacks are integer multiples of $1/D$. This holds initially and is preserved by:

  • integer multiples of cohort masses in batched consumption;
  • sums and differences defining slacks;
  • the minimum defining a partial upgrade.

Cohort masses and slacks lie in $[0,1]$, and residuals lie between zero and their input supplies. Hence these rationals have polynomial bit length.

Every bundle coordinate satisfies
$$ b_j\le U_j:= \left\lfloor\frac{\beta_d}{s_j}\right\rfloor, $$
where $\log(U_j+1)=O(L)$. Bundle sizes and values therefore have polynomial encoding length.

The integer $Q$ also has $O(L)$ bits. All arithmetic-stream priorities and safe-upgrade counts have polynomial bit length. Consequently, the binary searches in Section 6 use polynomially many iterations, and every arithmetic operation in every phase uses polynomial-length integers or rationals.

Finally, assign the completed cohorts to types by the sorted-interval construction of Lemma 1. Two interval partitions with $K$ and $\tau$ segments have at most $K+\tau-1$ positive intersections. Thus the resulting allocation has at most
$$ K+\tau-1 \le m+d+\tau-1 \le m+2\tau-1 $$
supported pairs. Every intersection mass is an integer multiple of $1/D$, so the output has polynomial encoding length.

The algorithm therefore constructs a feasible $\tfrac12$-EF1 allocation in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$, including all within-type comparisons and charity comparisons with unlimited multiplicities of positive-residual classes.
