Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a unit-mass population with finite support \(\Theta\) over complete worker types, rational masses \(\mu_\theta>0\), type beliefs \(P_{\theta,s,j}\), and verified labels \(p_{s,j}\), does the full-feedback update \(w_{s+1}(a)=w_s(a)\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{m_s}\sum_{j=1}^{m_s}(\hat P_{a,s,j}-p_{s,j})^2\right)\) truthfully maximize each tagged worker’s expected cumulative weight and give sublinear regret? The platform aggregates by \(A_{s,j}=\frac{\int w_s(a)\hat P_{a,s,j}\,d\mu(a)}{\int w_s(a)\,d\mu(a)}\), and regret compares its cumulative squared error with the best positive-mass type’s cumulative loss \(\sum_s\frac{1}{m_s}\sum_j(P_{\theta,s,j}-p_{s,j})^2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless unit-mass population with support \(\Theta\): the list of its \(\tau\ge2\) positive-mass types, with binary rational masses \(\mu_\theta>0\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(T\ge2\) rounds and \(m_s\ge1\) prompts in round \(s\); put \(m=\max_s m_s\). A complete type specifies rational beliefs \(P_{\theta,s,j}\in[0,1]\). Under that type’s subjective probability model, labels are independent Bernoulli variables with these parameters; beliefs and labels are unaffected by reports. Different types need not share a subjective probability model.
Workers report measurable values \(\hat P_{a,s,j}\in[0,1]\) before labels \(p_{s,j}\in\{0,1\}\) are revealed. Initially \(w_1(a)=1\); the platform uses \(A_{s,j}=\frac{\int w_s(a)\hat P_{a,s,j}\,d\mu(a)}{\int w_s(a)\,d\mu(a)}\), then updates \(w_{s+1}(a)=w_s(a)\bigl(1-\frac{\alpha}{m_s}\sum_{j=1}^{m_s}(\hat P_{a,s,j}-p_{s,j})^2\bigr)\).
For sufficiently large \(T\), use a rational step size satisfying \(\frac13\sqrt{\frac{2\ln\tau}{T}}\le\alpha\le\frac23\sqrt{\frac{2\ln\tau}{T}}<\frac12\). A tagged worker of type \(\theta\) maximizes \(\mathbb E_\theta[\sum_{s=1}^T w_s(a)]\); truthfulness means truthful reporting is a weak best response against every unilateral history-dependent deviation, with other workers’ strategies fixed.
Under truthful reporting define \(R(T)=\sum_{s=1}^T\frac1{m_s}\sum_{j=1}^{m_s}(A_{s,j}-p_{s,j})^2-\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{s=1}^T\frac1{m_s}\sum_{j=1}^{m_s}(P_{\theta,s,j}-p_{s,j})^2\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; express computational bounds in \(m,\tau,L,T\).
Does this mechanism make truthful reporting a weak best response for every tagged worker and guarantee \(R(T)=O(\sqrt T)\) for every label sequence and each fixed society, or can either guarantee fail? What dependence on the masses and support size is necessary in the regret bound?
For finitely many equally initialized workers, Hao and Duan establish \(R(T)\le3\sqrt{T\ln N/2}\) with their horizon-dependent step size and separately assert cumulative-weight truthfulness. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24052)
Roughgarden and Schrijvers characterize next-round weight incentive compatibility through proper scoring rules; that result concerns a different utility horizon. No result addressing exactly the atomless cumulative-weight formulation was located. [Source](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/3b3dbaf68507998acd6a5a5254ab2d76-Paper.pdf)

1. Conclusion and interpretation

Truthful reporting is a weak best response under the stated subjective independence assumption. The mechanism also has a regret upper bound of order \(\sqrt T\) for every fixed support and fixed positive mass vector. Its constant must depend on the masses.

Write \(\mu_{\min}=\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta\). The worst-case regret, over rational belief tables and label sequences with a given mass vector, has order

\(\displaystyle \Theta\!\left(\min\!\left\{T,\ \ln(1/\mu_{\min})\sqrt{\frac{T}{\ln\tau}}\right\}\right).\)

Consequently, equal masses give the scale \(\sqrt{T\ln\tau}\), whereas a mass-independent bound can fail even with two types.

Here, the conventional regret statement \(R(T)=O(\sqrt T)\) means a one-sided upper bound. An absolute-value interpretation can fail; Section 5 gives an explicit example.

2. Cumulative-weight truthfulness

Fix a tagged worker of type \(\theta\). All expectations in this section use that type’s subjective probability model. For brevity, write \(P_{s,j}=P_{\theta,s,j}\), and define

\(\displaystyle b_s=\frac1{m_s}\sum_{j=1}^{m_s}P_{s,j}(1-P_{s,j}).\)

For any admissible history-dependent strategy, let \(q_{s,j}\) denote its reports and put

\(\displaystyle \delta_s=\frac1{m_s}\sum_{j=1}^{m_s}(q_{s,j}-P_{s,j})^2.\)

Condition on the history and reports immediately before round \(s\)’s labels are revealed. The reports depend on past observations, whereas the unrevealed labels are independent of past labels under the given model. Thus, for each prompt,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_\theta[(q_{s,j}-p_{s,j})^2\mid\text{history and reports}] =P_{s,j}(1-P_{s,j})+(q_{s,j}-P_{s,j})^2.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_\theta[w_{s+1}\mid\text{history and reports}] =w_s(1-\alpha b_s-\alpha\delta_s).\)

This also covers randomized strategies by conditioning on their independent internal randomization.

Define deterministic coefficients backwards by

\(\displaystyle c_{T+1}=0,\qquad c_s=1+(1-\alpha b_s)c_{s+1}\quad(s=T,\ldots,1).\)

In particular, \(c_T=1\), and \(c_s>0\) for every \(s\le T\). The preceding conditional expectation gives

\(\displaystyle c_sw_s-\mathbb E_\theta[c_{s+1}w_{s+1}\mid\text{history and reports}] =w_s+\alpha c_{s+1}w_s\delta_s.\)

Taking expectations and summing over all rounds telescopes. Since \(w_1=1\),

\(\displaystyle c_1 =\mathbb E_\theta\!\left[\sum_{s=1}^T w_s\right] +\alpha\sum_{s=1}^{T-1}c_{s+1}\mathbb E_\theta[w_s\delta_s]. \)

Under truthful reporting, every \(\delta_s\) vanishes, so the expected utility equals \(c_1\). Therefore, for every admissible deviation \(\pi\),

\(\displaystyle U_\theta(\mathrm{truth})-U_\theta(\pi) =\alpha\sum_{s=1}^{T-1}c_{s+1} \mathbb E_\theta[w_s^\pi\delta_s^\pi] \ge0. \)

Every factor in the weight update lies in \([1-\alpha,1]\), so all weights are positive. A deviation from truth before the terminal round therefore causes a strict loss whenever it occurs with positive subjective probability. Reports in round \(T\) do not affect the specified utility, which explains why truthfulness is only weak at that round.

The proof applies separately to each type and requires no common subjective probability model. Other workers’ strategies do not enter the tagged worker’s weight update or utility. Atomlessness additionally ensures that a tagged worker’s unilateral report change does not change population aggregates.

This directly establishes the cumulative-weight claim. The incentive definition in [Roughgarden and Schrijvers](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/3b3dbaf68507998acd6a5a5254ab2d76-Paper.pdf) instead maximizes expected next-round weight.

3. A pathwise regret bound with the correct mass dependence

Fix any label sequence and assume truthful reporting. Workers of the same type have identical weights. Define

\(\displaystyle \ell_{\theta,s} =\frac1{m_s}\sum_{j=1}^{m_s}(P_{\theta,s,j}-p_{s,j})^2,\qquad W_s=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta w_{\theta,s}, \)

and the normalized type weights

\(\displaystyle q_{\theta,s}=\frac{\mu_\theta w_{\theta,s}}{W_s}.\)

These are well-defined because every weight is positive. Let

\(\displaystyle \bar\ell_s=\sum_\theta q_{\theta,s}\ell_{\theta,s}, \qquad \ell_{A,s}=\frac1{m_s}\sum_j(A_{s,j}-p_{s,j})^2. \)

Convexity of squared loss implies \(\ell_{A,s}\le\bar\ell_s\). The total-weight update is exactly

\(\displaystyle W_{s+1}=W_s(1-\alpha\bar\ell_s).\)

Because \(W_1=1\) and \(\ln(1-x)\le-x\),

\(\displaystyle \ln W_{T+1} =\sum_{s=1}^T\ln(1-\alpha\bar\ell_s) \le-\alpha\sum_{s=1}^T\bar\ell_s. \)

For any comparator type \(\theta\),

\(\displaystyle W_{T+1}\ge \mu_\theta\prod_{s=1}^T(1-\alpha\ell_{\theta,s}). \)

For \(0\le x\le\alpha<1/2\), integration gives

\(\displaystyle -\ln(1-x)-x =\int_0^x\frac{t}{1-t}\,dt \le\frac{x^2}{2(1-\alpha)}. \)

Applying this inequality to each comparator loss yields

\(\displaystyle \ln W_{T+1} \ge \ln\mu_\theta -\alpha\sum_s\ell_{\theta,s} -\frac{\alpha^2}{2(1-\alpha)} \sum_s\ell_{\theta,s}^2. \)

Combining the two bounds on \(\ln W_{T+1}\), and using \(\ell_{A,s}\le\bar\ell_s\), proves

\(\displaystyle \sum_s\ell_{A,s}-\sum_s\ell_{\theta,s} \le \frac{\ln(1/\mu_\theta)}{\alpha} +\frac{\alpha}{2(1-\alpha)} \sum_s\ell_{\theta,s}^2. \)

Choose a hindsight-best type \(\theta^\star\). Since its losses lie in \([0,1]\),

\(\displaystyle R(T)\le \frac{\ln(1/\mu_{\theta^\star})}{\alpha}+\alpha T. \)

The prescribed step-size interval therefore gives the explicit bound

\(\displaystyle R(T)\le \frac{3}{\sqrt2}\ln(1/\mu_{\theta^\star}) \sqrt{\frac{T}{\ln\tau}} +\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\sqrt{T\ln\tau}. \)

For fixed support and masses, its right-hand side is a constant times \(\sqrt T\), independently of the belief table and label sequence.

For a uniform bound over comparator identities, put \(d=\ln(1/\mu_{\min})\). Since \(\mu_{\min}\le1/\tau\), we have \(d\ge\ln\tau\). Also,

\(\displaystyle \alpha^2T\le\frac89\ln\tau\le\frac89d.\)

Thus a convenient uniform bound is

\(\displaystyle R(T)\le \min\!\left\{T,\frac{17d}{9\alpha}\right\}. \)

The bound \(R(T)\le T\) follows because platform loss is at most \(T\), while comparator loss is nonnegative.

When masses are equal, \(d=\ln\tau\), giving \(R(T)=O(\sqrt{T\ln\tau})\). This agrees with the scale of the finite, equally initialized result in [Hao and Duan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24052).

4. Matching lower bound and failure of mass-independent guarantees

Fix any positive rational mass vector. Choose a type of minimum mass and denote its mass by \(\varepsilon=\mu_{\min}\). We construct rational, distinct complete types using one prompt per round.

  • The minimum-mass type predicts \(0\) in every round.
  • Every other type predicts \(1\) in rounds \(1,\ldots,T-1\).
  • In round \(T\), give those other types distinct predictions \(i/\tau\), for \(i=1,\ldots,\tau-1\).
  • Every realized label is \(0\).

The terminal predictions ensure that the support really contains \(\tau\) distinct types. The minimum-mass type has total loss zero.

For \(s\le T-1\), its individual weight is \(1\), and every other type’s individual weight is \((1-\alpha)^{s-1}\). Hence

\(\displaystyle A_s=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)(1-\alpha)^{s-1}} {\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)(1-\alpha)^{s-1}}. \)

Write

\(\displaystyle \beta=-\ln(1-\alpha),\qquad H=\ln\!\left(\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\right). \)

Because \(\varepsilon\le1/\tau\le1/2\),

\(\displaystyle H\ge\ln(1/\varepsilon)=d.\)

Also,

\(\displaystyle \beta=\int_0^\alpha\frac{dt}{1-t} \le\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\le2\alpha. \)

Whenever \(s-1\le H/\beta\), the total weight of the other types is at least \(\varepsilon/2\). Therefore \(A_s\ge1/3\), and the platform’s loss is at least \(1/9\).

There are at least

\(\displaystyle \min\!\left\{T-1,\left\lfloor H/\beta\right\rfloor+1\right\} \ge \min\!\left\{T-1,\frac{d}{2\alpha}\right\} \)

such rounds. The last round contributes nonnegative loss. Since \(T-1\ge T/2\),

\(\displaystyle R(T)\ge \frac19\min\!\left\{T-1,\frac{d}{2\alpha}\right\} \ge \frac1{18}\min\!\left\{T,\frac{d}{\alpha}\right\}. \)

Together with Section 3, this proves the claimed worst-case order. Replicating each round’s prediction and label across its prompts gives the same construction for any prescribed prompt counts.

Two consequences deserve emphasis:

  • Equal masses require support dependence. With \(d=\ln\tau\), the prescribed step-size conditions give worst-case regret \(\Theta(\sqrt{T\ln\tau})\).
  • Support size alone is insufficient. With two types, masses \(2^{-T}\) and \(1-2^{-T}\), constant predictions \(0\) and \(1\), and all labels zero, the wrong type retains at least half the normalized weight throughout the horizon. Indeed, \((1-\alpha)^{s-1}\ge2^{-(T-1)}\), which makes its total weight exceed \(2^{-T}\). Thus \(R(T)\ge T/4\).

The second example varies the mass vector with the horizon. It therefore does not contradict the guarantee for each fixed society.

5. If the requested bound concerns absolute regret

Take two equally massive types predicting \(0\) and \(1\), one prompt per round, an even horizon, and alternating labels \(0,1\).

At the beginning of each odd round the two weights are equal, so the platform loss is \(1/4\). At the following even round, its prediction is \((1-\alpha)/(2-\alpha)\), so its loss is \(1/(2-\alpha)^2\). Each type’s total loss is \(T/2\). Hence

\(\displaystyle R(T)=\frac T2\left(\frac14+\frac1{(2-\alpha)^2}-1\right) \le-\frac{11T}{72}, \)

where the inequality uses \(\alpha<1/2\). Thus \(|R(T)|=O(\sqrt T)\) is false even for a fixed society. The one-sided upper bound proved above remains valid.

6. Computational bounds

Given the rational input and a realized label sequence, the population integral reduces exactly to a sum over the \(\tau\) types. All aggregates, updates, and realized regret require

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(\tau\sum_s m_s\right)=O(m\tau T)\)

rational arithmetic operations. Computing \(b_s\) and the coefficients \(c_s\) for all types also takes \(O(m\tau T)\) operations. In particular, computing the truthful optimal utility requires no enumeration of label histories.

Here is an explicit, conservative bit-complexity bound. Let \(D\) be the product of all input rational denominators, including that of \(\alpha\). Then \(\log_2D\le L\), and every input rational has denominator dividing \(D\).

Write \(\alpha=a/D\) and \(P_{\theta,s,j}=u_{\theta,s,j}/D\). Every update factor can be written with denominator \(m_sD^3\). Consequently, all individual weights in round \(s\) share denominator

\(\displaystyle K_s=\prod_{r<s}m_rD^3.\)

Writing \(w_{\theta,s}=n_{\theta,s}/K_s\) and \(\mu_\theta=b_\theta/D\), aggregation gives

\(\displaystyle A_{s,j} =\frac{\sum_\theta b_\theta n_{\theta,s}u_{\theta,s,j}} {D\sum_\theta b_\theta n_{\theta,s}}. \)

Since \(n_{\theta,s}\le K_s\) and \(\sum_\theta b_\theta=D\), these integers have \(O(T(L+\log(m+1)))\) bits. Accumulating losses over all rounds using products of their denominators gives a conservative bound

\(\displaystyle B=O(T^2(L+\log(m+1)))\)

on intermediate integer lengths. Schoolbook integer arithmetic therefore provides an exact implementation within

\(\displaystyle O(m\tau T B^2) = O\!\left(m\tau T^5(L+\log(m+1))^2\right) \)

bit operations. This bound is not intended to be optimal.

Finally, binary encoding implies \(\ln(1/\mu_{\min})\le L\ln2\). Thus an input-length version of the regret upper bound is

\(\displaystyle R(T)=O\!\left( \min\!\left\{T,\ L\sqrt{\frac{T}{\ln\tau}}\right\} \right). \)
