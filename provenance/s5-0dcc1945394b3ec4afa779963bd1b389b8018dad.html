The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a horizon \(T\), consider a fixed population with seller-type masses \(\tfrac16\) at cost \(0\) and \(\tfrac13\) at cost \(a\), and buyer-type masses \(\tfrac13\) at value \(1\) and \(\tfrac16\) at value \(b\), where \(\tfrac14 \le a < b \le \tfrac34\) are hidden. Each round a deterministic policy posts \(p_t \le q_t\), observes accepted mass on each side, and earns GFT from a maximum-mass matching chosen to minimize GFT among maximum matchings. Is the worst-case cumulative regret against the full-information maximum-GFT matching necessarily \(\Omega(T)\) for every such policy?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is represented by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the bit length of these masses. There is one homogeneous good, \(m=1\); state any running-time bounds in \(m,\tau,L,T\).
The fixed society has seller masses \(1/6\) at cost \(0\) and \(1/3\) at cost \(a\), and buyer masses \(1/3\) at value \(1\) and \(1/6\) at value \(b\). Total mass is \(1\), and \(\tau=4\). The real parameters satisfy \(1/4\le a<b\le3/4\).
The policy knows the masses, the support template, these bounds, and the integer horizon \(T\ge1\), but not \(a,b\). Each round restores the same trading capacities; mass is divisible.
A deterministic policy posts real prices \(0\le p_t\le q_t\le1\), using only its previous prices and observed aggregate accepted masses. Sellers accept exactly when their cost is at most \(p_t\); buyers accept exactly when their value is at least \(q_t\). Indifference means acceptance; neither types nor realized GFT are revealed.
A feasible matching is a nonnegative matrix \(x_{ij}\) of traded masses whose row and column sums respect seller and buyer capacities. Its GFT is \(\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}(v_j-c_i)\). Each round uses only accepting types and maximizes \(\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}\), then minimizes GFT among these maximum-mass matchings; denote the resulting GFT by \(G_t\).
Let \(G^\star\) be maximum GFT over all feasible matchings of the entire society, allowing unused capacity. Define \(R_T=\inf_A\sup_{1/4\le a<b\le3/4}\sum_{t=1}^T(G^\star-G_t)\), where \(A\) ranges over deterministic policies and each instance remains fixed throughout the horizon.

Is \(R_T=\Omega(T)\), or can deterministic policies achieve worst-case cumulative regret \(o(T)\)?

For finite markets, every deterministic two-price policy has worst-case GFT regret \(\Omega(T)\), already with three sellers and three buyers under GFT-minimizing maximum matchings ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11691v1)).
No result settling this exact divisible-mass formulation was located in the literature search.

  • Kept hidden costs and values real, as in the discrete model; binary-rational encoding applies to masses and imposes no precision bound on the hidden gap.
  • Normalized the combined seller and buyer mass to one, as specified by the four given masses.
  • Defined divisible matching through capacity-constrained traded-mass matrices and restored capacities each round.
  • Restricted prices to the normalized type interval and made acceptance weak at both thresholds.
  • Retained the graded question's aggregate acceptance-only feedback, excluding realized GFT and matching feedback.
  • Made the worst-case quantifiers explicit: the policy may know the horizon, and the fixed hidden instance may depend on the policy and horizon.
  • Used m for the number of homogeneous goods, which is one here, and L for the binary encoding length of the mass list.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Proven result. Worst-case regret is linear. More precisely, for every integer \(T\ge1\),
\[ \frac{T}{12}\le R_T\le \frac{T}{12}+\frac{\lceil\log_2T\rceil}{6}+\frac18. \]
Thus \(R_T=T/12+O(\log(T+1))\), and deterministic policies cannot achieve worst-case \(o(T)\) regret.

The cited paper’s construction uses seller costs \(0,a,a\) and buyer values \(1,1,b\), which aggregate into the support in this question when each trader receives mass \(1/6\). The proof below checks divisible matchings directly. [Source, Section 4.1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11691v1#S4.SS1.SSS3)

1. The benchmark and one-round regret.

Every buyer value strictly exceeds every seller cost, because \(b>a\). The feasible matching set is compact, so a GFT maximizer exists. If its traded mass were less than \(1/2\), some seller and some buyer would both have unused capacity. Adding a positive amount of trade between them would strictly increase GFT. Consequently, every maximizer saturates all capacities, giving
\[ G^\star=\frac13+\frac b6-\frac a3 =\frac{2+b-2a}{6}. \]

The cost-\(0\) sellers and value-\(1\) buyers always accept. The remaining acceptance decisions are \(p\ge a\) and \(q\le b\). Under the specified matching rule:

| Acceptance conditions | Maximum traded mass | Minimum GFT at that mass | Regret |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(p<a,\ q>b\) | \(1/6\) | \(1/6\) | \((1+b-2a)/6\) |
| \(p<a,\ q\le b\) | \(1/6\) | \(b/6\) | \((1-a)/3\) |
| \(p\ge a,\ q>b\) | \(1/3\) | \((1-a)/3\) | \(b/6\) |
| \(p\ge a,\ q\le b\) | \(1/2\) | \(G^\star\) | \(0\) |

Here are the matching calculations:

  • When only cost-\(0\) sellers accept and both buyer types accept, GFT minimization assigns their entire mass \(1/6\) to value-\(b\) buyers, whose capacity is exactly \(1/6\).
  • When both seller types accept and only value-\(1\) buyers accept, GFT minimization uses only cost-\(a\) sellers, whose capacity equals the traded mass \(1/3\).
  • When all types accept, trading mass \(1/2\) saturates every capacity, so GFT is independent of the pairing.

These arguments permit arbitrary fractional trades.

Since \(b>a\), the table implies that whenever all four types do not accept,
\[ G^\star-G_t\ge\frac{\min\{b,1-a\}}6. \]
Moreover, admitting all four types requires
\[ a\le p_t\le q_t\le b. \]

2. A fixed-instance lower bound.

Fix an arbitrary deterministic policy \(A\) and horizon \(T\). Seller acceptance mass has two possible values, \(1/6\) and \(1/2\); buyer acceptance mass has two possible values, \(1/3\) and \(1/2\). Thus each round has at most four feedback outcomes.

Determinism implies that a feedback history uniquely determines the next prices: previous prices are themselves determined recursively by that history. Therefore the set \(P_A\) of all prices that \(A\) can post during the first \(T\) rounds, across all admissible instances, is finite. Specifically,
\[ |P_A|\le 2\sum_{t=1}^{T}4^{t-1}. \]

Choose any \(0<\delta<1/4\). Because \(P_A\) is finite, there exist
\[ \frac12-\delta<a<b<\frac12+\delta \]
such that \(P_A\cap[a,b]=\varnothing\). These parameters satisfy the stipulated bounds.

Run \(A\) on this single fixed instance. Every posted price belongs to \(P_A\), so no round can satisfy \(a\le p_t\le q_t\le b\). Hence the one-round bound gives
\[ G^\star-G_t \ge\frac{\min\{b,1-a\}}6 >\frac{1/2-\delta}{6}. \]
Summing over the horizon,
\[ \sup_{1/4\le a<b\le3/4} \sum_{t=1}^{T}(G^\star-G_t) \ge \frac{T}{12}-\frac{\delta T}{6}. \]

This holds for every positive \(\delta<1/4\). Taking \(\delta\) arbitrarily small yields a supremum of at least \(T/12\). Since \(A\) was arbitrary,
\[ R_T\ge T/12. \]

The constructed parameters can depend on \(A,T,\delta\), as permitted by the supremum, but remain unchanged throughout all \(T\) rounds.

3. An upper bound with the same leading constant.

Set \(k=\lceil\log_2T\rceil\), which satisfies \(0\le k\le T\). Begin with a bracket \([\ell,u]=[1/4,3/4]\) containing \([a,b]\).

For at most \(k\) rounds, post the common midpoint price \(p=q=r=(\ell+u)/2\).

  • If all four types accept, then \(r\in[a,b]\); repeat this price thereafter, obtaining zero further regret.
  • If the seller mass is \(1/6\), then \(r<a\); replace \(\ell\) by \(r\).
  • Otherwise, if the buyer mass is \(1/3\), then \(r>b\); replace \(u\) by \(r\).

The two rejection cases cannot occur together at a common price, because \(a<b\). Each unsuccessful round preserves \([a,b]\subseteq[\ell,u]\) and halves the bracket width.

Suppose all \(k\) exploratory rounds fail. Write
\[ w=u-\ell=2^{-k-1}\le\frac1{2T}, \qquad c=(\ell+u)/2. \]
For every remaining round, use one of the following fixed price pairs.

If \(c\le1/2\), post
\[ p=q=(1+u)/2. \]
This price exceeds \(u\), so both seller types accept and the value-\(b\) buyers reject. Regret is
\[ \frac b6\le\frac u6 =\frac c6+\frac w{12} \le\frac1{12}+\frac w{12}. \]

If \(c>1/2\), post
\[ p=\ell/2,\qquad q=(1+u)/2. \]
Then \(p<a\) and \(q>b\), so regret is
\[ \frac{1+b-2a}{6} \le\frac{1+u-2\ell}{6} =\frac{1-c}{6}+\frac w4 \le\frac1{12}+\frac w4. \]

Both price pairs satisfy \(0\le p\le q\le1\), since \(1/4\le\ell\le u\le3/4\).

The table bounds every exploratory round’s regret by \(1/4\). Thus, whether exploration succeeds early or all \(k\) rounds fail, cumulative regret is at most
\[ \frac k4+(T-k)\left(\frac1{12}+\frac w4\right) \le\frac T{12}+\frac k6+\frac18. \]
This establishes the claimed upper bound using only aggregate acceptance feedback.

4. Running time and the role of the gap.

For the stipulated \(m=1,\tau=4\), the policy uses \(O(\log(T+1))\) exploratory rounds and dyadic prices of bit length \(O(\log(T+1))\). Including reading the mass representation and emitting all prices, its running time is \(O(m\tau+L+T\log(T+1))\) bit operations, with \(O(m\tau+L+\log(T+1))\) working space. These bounds concern this fixed support.

For each fixed instance, continued midpoint search eventually finds a price in \([a,b]\): after \(j\) failures its bracket width is \(2^{-j-1}\), which cannot fall below \(b-a\) while still containing \([a,b]\). The linear minimax regret arises because the task provides no positive lower bound on \(b-a\), allowing arbitrarily narrow gaps in the worst-case supremum.
