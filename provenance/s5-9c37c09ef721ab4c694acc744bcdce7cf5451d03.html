The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), per-type menus \(H_t\) of item roles with values \(v_{t,h}\), and an explicitly specified population-scaled matroid rank function \(r\), compute a welfare-maximizing measurable assignment of integral bundles to agents. The aggregate selected masses \(x_h\) must satisfy \(x(S)\le r(S)\) for every \(S\subseteq H\), \(x(H)=r(H)\), and \(0\le x_h\le\mu_t\) for \(h\in H_t\). For each positive-mass type-and-bundle cohort \((t,S)\), also compute its marginal externality price density \(\pi_{t,S}=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}[F_{-\epsilon(t,S)}-F+\epsilon\sum_{h\in S}v_{t,h}]/\epsilon\), where \(F_{-\epsilon(t,S)}\) is optimal welfare after removing \(\epsilon\) mass of that cohort and its associated item copies.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types \(t\in T\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Thus \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types; type \(t\) occupies an atomless interval of length \(\mu_t\).
The menus \(H_t\) partition the item-role set \(H\), with \(m=|H|\); every agent of type \(t\) owns one available copy of each role in \(H_t\) and may receive any bundle \(S\subseteq H_t\), including the empty bundle. Values \(v_{t,h}\) are nonnegative binary rationals and additive.
The population-scaled rank is \(r(A)=a\rho(A)\), where \(a>0\) is binary rational and \(\rho\) is a matroid rank function on \(H\), supplied by its complete table. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including this table.
An assignment is described by finitely supported bundle masses \(y_{t,S}\ge0\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq H_t}y_{t,S}=\mu_t\); each individual receives an integral bundle. Its aggregate is \(x_h=\sum_{S\ni h}y_{t,S}\) for \(h\in H_t\), and \(x(A)=\sum_{h\in A}x_h\).
Feasibility requires \(x(A)\le r(A)\) for every \(A\subseteq H\), \(x(H)=r(H)\), and \(0\le x_h\le\mu_t\) for \(h\in H_t\). Welfare is \(\sum_{t,S}y_{t,S}\sum_{h\in S}v_{t,h}\), and its optimum is \(F\); any maximizing assignment is acceptable when there are ties.
Removing \(\epsilon\) mass of a cohort \((t,S)\) removes those agents and all their available copies: replace \(\mu_t\) by \(\mu_t-\epsilon\), keep \(r\), values, other masses, and the required total \(r(H)\) fixed, and reoptimize all remaining assignments. Denote the optimum by \(F_{-\epsilon(t,S)}\); do not renormalize masses.
Promise initial feasibility and, for each type, feasibility after this removal for every sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\). This is the local absence-of-monopsony condition needed for the externality comparison.
For each cohort with \(y_{t,S}>0\), its price density is \(\pi_{t,S}=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\bigl(F_{-\epsilon(t,S)}-F+\epsilon\sum_{h\in S}v_{t,h}\bigr)/\epsilon\).

Is computing a welfare-maximizing assignment together with all its positive-mass cohort price densities solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this continuous problem NP-hard?

For finite buyers with additive nonnegative valuations and a monopsony-free matroid, welfare-maximizing base allocation and Vickrey payments are computable in strongly polynomial time ([Peis–Rieken](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/1020376/files/1020376.pdf)).
No result settling exactly the atomless assignment-and-cohort-density problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the initial society to total mass one and modeled each supported type as an atomless interval; removal does not renormalize the remaining population.
  • Made item roles type-specific and the menus a partition, following the discrete reduction to buyer-specific item copies; allowed all subsets, including the empty bundle.
  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational additive values and allowed arbitrary welfare-maximizing tie choices.
  • Interpreted the undefined population-scaled matroid rank literally as a positive rational multiple of a matroid rank function, supplied by its complete table; included the entire table in L.
  • Represented measurable assignments by finitely supported type-and-bundle masses.
  • Defined cohort removal to delete all available copies owned by the removed agents, including unassigned roles, by decreasing every capacity in their type menu; retained the original total rank requirement.
  • Carried over the absence-of-monopsony requirement as feasibility after every sufficiently small removal from any type, and promised initial feasibility.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). One can compute an exact welfare-maximizing assignment with at most \(m+\tau\) positive-mass cohorts, together with every such cohort’s exact price density.

The proof uses one linear program for welfare and one auxiliary linear program per supported type. The complete rank-table representation is essential to the stated complexity bound.

1. Exact reduction to aggregate role masses

For each role \(h\), let \(t(h)\) be its unique menu owner, and write \(u_h=\mu_{t(h)}\) and \(v_h=v_{t(h),h}\). Define the aggregate feasible set

\(P=\{x\in\mathbb R^H: x(A)\le a\rho(A)\text{ for every }A\subseteq H,\ x(H)=a\rho(H),\ 0\le x_h\le u_h\text{ for every }h\in H\}\).

Every feasible assignment has its aggregate in \(P\). Conversely, every \(x\in P\) can be realized by an assignment of integral bundles, as follows.

For each type \(t\), identify its population with \([0,\mu_t)\). Give the agent at position \(z\) the bundle

\(S_t(z)=\{h\in H_t:z<x_h\}\).

The set of agents receiving role \(h\) has measure exactly \(x_h\). Moreover, \(S_t(z)\) changes only at the distinct breakpoints among \(0,\mu_t\), and the coordinates \(x_h\) for \(h\in H_t\). Thus it is constant on at most \(|H_t|+1\) intervals of positive length. Assigning each interval’s length to its bundle gives the required finitely supported bundle masses.

This construction also covers empty menus and works for any positive type masses, without requiring their sum to equal one. Consequently, it remains valid after a removal.

By additivity, every assignment with aggregate \(x\) has welfare

\(\sum_{t,S}y_{t,S}\sum_{h\in S}v_{t,h}=\sum_{h\in H}v_hx_h=v\cdot x\).

Therefore the welfare optimum is exactly

\(F=\max\{v\cdot x:x\in P\}\).

Initial feasibility makes \(P\) nonempty. The coordinate bounds make it compact, so an optimum exists. Fix any optimal aggregate \(x^*\). The construction above realizes it using at most \(\sum_t(|H_t|+1)=m+\tau\) positive-mass cohorts.

2. Removal depends only on the type

For each type \(t\), define \(b_h^t=1\) when \(h\in H_t\), and \(b_h^t=0\) otherwise. After removing mass \(\epsilon\) of type \(t\), the aggregate feasible set is

\(P_t(\epsilon)=\{x\in\mathbb R^H:x(A)\le a\rho(A)\text{ for every }A\subseteq H,\ x(H)=a\rho(H),\ 0\le x_h\le u_h-\epsilon b_h^t\text{ for every }h\in H\}\).

Let \(F_t(\epsilon)=\max\{v\cdot x:x\in P_t(\epsilon)\}\) whenever this set is nonempty.

Under the stipulated removal rule, all remaining assignments are reoptimized, and the removed agents take all their available copies. Hence, for every positive-mass cohort \((t,S)\),

\(F_{-\epsilon(t,S)}=F_t(\epsilon)\)

whenever \(\epsilon\) is sufficiently small. In particular, the dependence on \(S\) enters the price formula only through the removed cohort’s own bundle value.

We next compute the right derivative of \(F_t\) exactly.

3. A linear program for the removal derivative

At the chosen optimum \(x^*\), define

\(\mathcal A^*=\{A\subseteq H:x^*(A)=a\rho(A)\}\),

\(Z^*=\{h\in H:x_h^*=0\}\), and

\(U^*=\{h\in H:x_h^*=u_h\}\).

For each type \(t\), consider the auxiliary linear program

\(D_t=\max\{v\cdot d:d\in Q_t\}\),

where

\(Q_t=\{d\in\mathbb R^H:d(H)=0,\ d(A)\le0\text{ for every }A\in\mathcal A^*,\ d_h\ge0\text{ for every }h\in Z^*,\ d_h\le-b_h^t\text{ for every }h\in U^*\}\).

The coordinates of \(d\) are otherwise unrestricted.

We prove that this program is feasible, has a finite attained optimum, and satisfies \(F_t(\epsilon)=F+\epsilon D_t\) for every sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\).

3.1. Feasibility of the auxiliary program

By the local feasibility promise, choose \(\epsilon_0>0\), with \(\epsilon_0<\mu_t\), for which \(P_t(\epsilon_0)\) is nonempty. Choose \(z\in P_t(\epsilon_0)\), and set

\(d=(z-x^*)/\epsilon_0\).

Both aggregates have the required total, so \(d(H)=0\). For each \(A\in\mathcal A^*\),

\(d(A)=(z(A)-a\rho(A))/\epsilon_0\le0\).

For \(h\in Z^*\), nonnegativity of \(z_h\) gives \(d_h\ge0\). For \(h\in U^*\),

\(z_h\le u_h-\epsilon_0b_h^t=x_h^*-\epsilon_0b_h^t\),

so \(d_h\le-b_h^t\). Thus \(d\in Q_t\).

3.2. Every auxiliary feasible direction gives a small feasible removal

Fix any \(d\in Q_t\). We claim that \(x^*+\epsilon d\in P_t(\epsilon)\) for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\).

The total equality holds because \(d(H)=0\). Every tight rank inequality holds because \(d(A)\le0\). Every initially tight lower or upper coordinate bound holds by the corresponding defining constraint of \(Q_t\).

For the remaining inequalities, their initial slack is strictly positive:

  • If a rank inequality is not tight, it requires \(\epsilon d(A)\le a\rho(A)-x^*(A)\).
  • If a lower bound is not tight, it requires \(x_h^*+\epsilon d_h\ge0\).
  • If an upper bound is not tight, it requires \(\epsilon(d_h+b_h^t)\le u_h-x_h^*\).

Whenever the coefficient of \(\epsilon\) moves an inequality toward violation, its positive slack supplies a positive upper bound on \(\epsilon\). There are finitely many inequalities, so the minimum of these bounds and \(\mu_t/2\) is positive. This proves the claim.

Because \(P_t(\epsilon)\subseteq P\), optimality of \(x^*\) now implies

\(v\cdot(x^*+\epsilon d)\le F=v\cdot x^*\).

Dividing by \(\epsilon>0\) gives \(v\cdot d\le0\). Thus the auxiliary objective is bounded above.

A nonempty polyhedron with a linear objective bounded above has an attained optimum. Hence \(D_t\) is finite and attained; fix an optimal direction \(d^{t,*}\).

3.3. Exact local formula for welfare

The preceding feasibility argument applied to \(d^{t,*}\) gives, for all sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\),

\(F_t(\epsilon)\ge v\cdot(x^*+\epsilon d^{t,*})=F+\epsilon D_t\).

For the reverse inequality, let \(z\) be any feasible aggregate in \(P_t(\epsilon)\). Exactly the calculation in Section 3.1 shows that

\(d=(z-x^*)/\epsilon\in Q_t\).

Consequently,

\(v\cdot z=F+\epsilon v\cdot d\le F+\epsilon D_t\).

Maximizing over \(z\in P_t(\epsilon)\) proves the reverse inequality. Therefore

\(F_t(\epsilon)=F+\epsilon D_t\)

for every sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\). In particular,

\(\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}(F_t(\epsilon)-F)/\epsilon=D_t\).

This proof applies to any optimal aggregate \(x^*\); it requires neither uniqueness nor a tie-breaking assumption.

4. Exact cohort price densities

For each positive-mass cohort \((t,S)\), write \(V_t(S)=\sum_{h\in S}v_{t,h}\). Restricting \(\epsilon\) further so that \(\epsilon<y_{t,S}\), the definition and the local formula give

\(\pi_{t,S}=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}(F_t(\epsilon)-F+\epsilon V_t(S))/\epsilon=D_t+V_t(S)\).

Thus the algorithm returns the density

\(\boxed{\pi_{t,S}=D_t+\sum_{h\in S}v_{t,h}}\)

for every positive-mass cohort in its constructed assignment. The argument also proves that all these defining limits exist and are finite.

5. Polynomial running time and output size

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly represented rational linear program can be solved in time polynomial in its encoding length, returning an exact rational optimum and optimizer when the finite optimum exists. This is the polynomial-time solvability theorem originating with [Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • Polynomial-size welfare program. There are \(m\) variables, \(2^m\) rank inequalities, \(2m\) coordinate bounds, and one equality. Because the complete rank table is included in the input, \(2^m=O(L)\). Writing each subset’s incidence row and multiplying its rank by \(a\) increases the encoding length by only a polynomial factor.
  • Polynomial-size derivative programs. Exact rational comparisons identify \(\mathcal A^*,Z^*,U^*\). Each auxiliary program has \(m\) variables and at most \(2^m+2m\) inequalities plus one equality. Its constraint coefficients and right-hand sides belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and its objective is the original rational valuation vector. Section 3 verifies feasibility and boundedness.
  • Polynomial-size exact output. Rational linear programming supplies \(x^*\) and each \(D_t\) with polynomial encoding length. Sorting the coordinates within each menu constructs at most \(m+\tau\) cohorts. Their masses are differences of rational breakpoints; their prices are rational sums. Both have polynomial encoding length.

The complete algorithm solves \(1+\tau\) linear programs and then constructs the cohorts and their densities. Its total running time and output length are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

This establishes the requested bit-complexity result. It does not assert a strongly polynomial algorithm or a polynomial bound for a compact rank-oracle representation.
