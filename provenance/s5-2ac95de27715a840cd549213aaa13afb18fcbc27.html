For a ranking \(r_1 \succ_t \cdots \succ_t r_m\), type \(t\) weakly improves when every prefix sum \(\sum_{j=1}^k x_{t r_j}\) does not decrease; efficiency excludes a feasible reassignment that weakly improves every type and strictly improves some positive mass. Give an exact finite description of the efficient set, for example as rational polyhedral cells; each person still receives one indivisible room, while \(x_{tr}\) records type-level mass.

It yields polynomial-time maximality testing and extension to a maximal efficient face when the number of support components is fixed. 1. Definitions and exact computational primitives

Write
$$ R^+=\{r\in R:q_r>0\}, \qquad N=\tau |R^+|. $$
Coordinates involving $R\setminus R^+$ vanish throughout $F$. We retain these categories in preference graphs, but omit them from bipartite support graphs.

For $E\subseteq T\times R$, define
$$ F_E=\{x\in F:x_{tr}=0\text{ whenever }(t,r)\notin E\}. $$
For a nonempty face $H$ of $F$, its full support is
$$ E_H=\{(t,r):x_{tr}>0\text{ for some }x\in H\}. $$

We use the following precise standard theorem about rational linear programming: a linear program with rational coefficients and a bounded feasible region can be tested for feasibility in time polynomial in its binary encoding length; if feasible, its exact rational optimal value and a rational optimizer can be computed in polynomial time. These outputs have polynomial binary encoding length. Consequently, strict positivity of an optimum can be decided exactly.

All allocation programs below are bounded because $0\leq x_{tr}\leq 1$. A positivity program using an additional variable $\delta$ can include $0\leq\delta\leq 1$. Their coefficients have encoding length polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

The original polytope is nonempty: $x_{tr}=\mu_tq_r$ is feasible.

2. Proven characterization of efficient supports

For $E\subseteq T\times R$, let $D(E)$ be the directed graph on $R$ having an arc
$$ r\longrightarrow s $$
whenever $(t,r)\in E$ for some type $t$ and $s\succ_t r$.

For an allocation $x$, write $E(x)=\{(t,r):x_{tr}>0\}$.

Proposition 1. An allocation $x\in F$ is efficient if and only if $D(E(x))$ is acyclic.

This is the familiar trading-cycle characterization; the proof below covers the masses and supplies in the task directly. See also [Bogomolnaia–Moulin, Section 5](https://kylewoodward.com/blog-data/pdfs/references/bogomolnaia%2Bmoulin-journal-of-economic-theory-2001A.pdf).

Proof: a cycle gives an improvement. Suppose the support graph contains a simple directed cycle
$$ r_1\longrightarrow r_2\longrightarrow\cdots \longrightarrow r_\ell\longrightarrow r_1. $$
For each arc choose a witnessing type $t_i$, so that
$$ x_{t_i r_i}>0, \qquad r_{i+1}\succ_{t_i}r_i, $$
with cyclic indices. Choose
$$ 0<\varepsilon<\min_i x_{t_i r_i}. $$
Transfer mass $\varepsilon$ within type $t_i$ from $r_i$ to $r_{i+1}$.

Each transfer preserves its row sum. Each category gains and loses $\varepsilon$, so column sums are preserved. The donor categories are distinct, and therefore no donor coordinate is reduced twice. Nonnegativity follows.

Every transfer weakly increases all cumulative quantities of its type and strictly increases at least one. Their sum consequently gives a feasible allocation dominating $x$.

Proof: an improvement gives a cycle. Suppose $y\in F$ dominates $x$. Fix a type with ranking $r_1\succ_t\cdots\succ_t r_m$, and put
$$ d_j=y_{t r_j}-x_{t r_j}. $$
Dominance and the row equations give
$$ \sum_{i=1}^j d_i\geq 0 \quad\text{for every }j, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^m d_i=0. $$

These differences can be realized by transfers from worse categories to better ones. To verify this explicitly, process ranks from best to worst. A positive $d_i$ creates an unfilled demand of size $d_i$. At a negative $d_j$, send $-d_j$ from $r_j$ to previously unfilled demands. Immediately before this step, their total is
$$ \sum_{i<j}d_i\geq -d_j, $$
by the nonnegative-prefix condition. Thus the step is possible. Total difference zero ensures that all demands are eventually filled.

Every resulting transfer goes from $r_j$ to a strictly preferred $r_i$ with $i<j$. Its donor has
$$ x_{t r_j}>y_{t r_j}\geq 0, $$
so its directed arc belongs to $D(E(x))$.

Perform this construction for every type. The total incoming transfer weight at each category equals its outgoing weight, since $x$ and $y$ have the same column sums. At least one transfer is positive because some cumulative inequality is strict.

A finite directed graph carrying a nonzero nonnegative circulation contains a directed cycle. Indeed, start with a positive-weight arc. At its endpoint, positive incoming weight and flow balance imply a positive outgoing arc. Continuing eventually repeats a vertex, and the resulting closed walk contains a simple directed cycle. Hence $D(E(x))$ is cyclic. This proves the converse. $\square$

3. Proven characterization of efficient faces

Lemma 2. Every nonempty face $H$ satisfies
$$ H=F_{E_H}. $$
Moreover, some point of $H$ is positive on every coordinate in $E_H$.

Proof. For each coordinate in $E_H$, choose a point of $H$ positive there. Their average $z$ belongs to $H$ and is positive on all of $E_H$.

Certainly $H\subseteq F_{E_H}$. Conversely, take $y\in F_{E_H}$. For sufficiently small $0<\lambda<1$,
$$ w=\frac{z-\lambda y}{1-\lambda} $$
is nonnegative. It satisfies the same row and column equations, so $w\in F$. Since
$$ z=\lambda y+(1-\lambda)w\in H, $$
the defining property of a face implies $y\in H$. $\square$

Every nonempty $F_E$ is a face, because it minimizes the nonnegative linear function
$$ \sum_{(t,r)\notin E}x_{tr}. $$

Corollary 3. A nonempty face $H$ consists entirely of efficient allocations if and only if $D(E_H)$ is acyclic.

Proof. If this graph is acyclic, every allocation in $H$ has an acyclic support subgraph, so Proposition 1 applies. Conversely, Lemma 2 supplies a point with support exactly $E_H$. A cycle in $D(E_H)$ makes that point inefficient. $\square$

Thus nonemptiness, canonical encoding, and efficiency of a supplied face are polynomial-time decidable. Impose its proposed zeros, test feasibility, and maximize each remaining coordinate. A coordinate belongs to the full support exactly when its maximum is positive. Then test the resulting support graph for acyclicity.

For canonical supports,
$$ F_E\subseteq F_{E'} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E\subseteq E'. $$
The forward implication follows by taking a point positive on all of $E$; the reverse follows directly from the zero constraints.

4. New result: component cycles characterize nonmaximality

Let $H$ be a nonempty efficient face, and write $E=E_H$.

Define its undirected bipartite support graph $G(E)$ on $T\sqcup R^+$, with edge $\{t,r\}$ for every $(t,r)\in E$. Let its connected components be
$$ V_1,\ldots,V_c. $$
Every component contains a type and a positive-supply category. Consequently,
$$ 1\leq c\leq \min(\tau,|R^+|). $$

Write $a(t)$ and $b(r)$ for the component indices of type $t$ and category $r$. Construct a directed multigraph $\Gamma_E$ on the component indices: each missing coordinate
$$ (t,r)\in (T\times R^+)\setminus E $$
gives a labeled arc
$$ a(t)\longrightarrow b(r). $$
Loops and parallel arcs are allowed.

A component cycle means either one loop, or a directed cycle on distinct component vertices with one labeled arc chosen for each step. Let $B$ be its set of coordinate labels.

4.1. Every component cycle gives a feasible support extension

Lemma 4. For every component cycle $B$, the face $F_{E\cup B}$ is nonempty and has full support exactly $E\cup B$.

Proof. Choose $z\in H$ positive on all of $E$, using Lemma 2.

Index the cycle components cyclically. Write its selected coordinates as
$$ (t_i,r_{i+1}), \qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell, $$
where $t_i$ and $r_i$ lie in the same support component. The case $\ell=1$ is a loop.

Within component $i$, choose a simple path in $G(E)$ from $t_i$ to $r_i$. Its length is odd because its endpoints lie on opposite sides of the bipartition.

Define a change vector $h$ as follows:

  • put $+1$ on every selected coordinate $(t_i,r_{i+1})$;
  • along the internal path from $t_i$ to $r_i$, put alternating values $-1,+1,-1,\ldots,-1$;
  • put zero elsewhere.

Different internal paths lie in different components, so they do not overlap. Each internal path contributes row sum $-1$ at $t_i$ and column sum $-1$ at $r_i$, and contributes zero at its other vertices. The selected cross coordinates contribute $+1$ to exactly those rows and columns. Therefore
$$ \sum_r h_{tr}=0, \qquad \sum_t h_{tr}=0. $$

Every negative entry of $h$ lies in $E$, where $z$ is positive. Taking
$$ 0<\varepsilon<\min_{e\in E}z_e $$
makes $z+\varepsilon h$ feasible, positive on every coordinate of $E\cup B$, and zero outside that set. Thus its support is exactly $E\cup B$, proving both assertions. $\square$

4.2. Every efficient proper superface supplies such a cycle

Theorem 5. The face $H$ is not inclusion-maximal among nonempty efficient faces if and only if some component cycle $B$ satisfies
$$ D(E\cup B)\text{ is acyclic}. $$

Proof: sufficiency. Lemma 4 makes $F_{E\cup B}$ a nonempty face with full support $E\cup B$. Acyclicity makes it entirely efficient by Corollary 3. Since $B$ contains missing coordinates, this face strictly contains $H$.

Proof: necessity. Suppose an efficient face $K$ strictly contains $H$. Its canonical support satisfies
$$ E\subsetneq E_K, $$
and $D(E_K)$ is acyclic.

If some coordinate of $E_K\setminus E$ has both endpoints in the same component of $G(E)$, it gives a loop $B$. Then $D(E\cup B)$ is a subgraph of $D(E_K)$ and is acyclic.

Otherwise, all coordinates in $E_K\setminus E$ join different components. Choose $y\in K$ positive on all of $E_K$.

For component $V_i$, put
$$ T_i=V_i\cap T, \qquad R_i=V_i\cap R^+. $$
An allocation supported on $E$ gives the balance identity
$$ \sum_{t\in T_i}\mu_t=\sum_{r\in R_i}q_r. $$
For $y$, subtracting the internal allocation total from each side yields
$$ \sum_{\substack{t\in T_i\\r\notin R_i}}y_{tr} = \sum_{\substack{t\notin T_i\\r\in R_i}}y_{tr}. $$
Thus the cross-component coordinates of $y$ form a nonzero positive circulation on $\Gamma_E$. As proved in Proposition 1, its positive arcs contain a simple directed cycle.

Let $B$ be the labels of that cycle. Then $B\subseteq E_K\setminus E$, so $D(E\cup B)$ is again an acyclic subgraph of $D(E_K)$. $\square$

In particular, every nonmaximal efficient face has an efficient proper superface obtained by adding at most
$$ c\leq \min(\tau,|R^+|) $$
coordinates.

5. Computational consequences of the new criterion

5.1. Polynomial maximality testing for connected support

Suppose $G(E)$ is connected. Every missing active coordinate is a loop in $\Gamma_E$. Theorem 5 therefore becomes
$$ H\text{ is maximal} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D(E\cup\{(t,r)\})\text{ is cyclic for every missing active coordinate}. $$

This gives a polynomial-time maximality test.

There is also an explicit reachability version. Adding $(t,r)$ adds only arcs
$$ r\longrightarrow s \quad\text{with }s\succ_t r. $$
Since $D(E)$ is acyclic and all new arcs leave the same vertex $r$, any new simple cycle uses exactly one new arc. Hence
$$ D(E\cup\{(t,r)\})\text{ is cyclic} $$
if and only if some $s\succ_t r$ has a directed path to $r$ in $D(E)$.

Thus one reachability computation, followed by tests of the missing coordinates, suffices.

5.2. Polynomial maximality testing for a fixed number of components

For general $c$, enumerate ordered tuples of at most $c$ missing coordinates and retain those forming component cycles. Their number is at most
$$ \sum_{\ell=1}^c N^\ell\leq cN^c. $$
For each, test $D(E\cup B)$ for acyclicity.

By Theorem 5, this decides maximality in time
$$ cN^c\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
This is polynomial when $c$ is fixed.

Furthermore, whenever a successful cycle is found, replace $E$ by $E\cup B$ and repeat. Lemma 4 ensures that the new support is already canonical. The number of support components never increases, and every extension adds a coordinate. There are at most $N$ extensions.

Starting with $c_0$ components, this computes a maximal efficient superface in time bounded by
$$ c_0N^{c_0+1}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

5.3. Finding one maximal efficient face for fixed support size

An initial efficient allocation can be found in polynomial time. For the ranking $r_1\succ_t\cdots\succ_t r_m$, set
$$ u_{t r_j}=m-j, $$
and maximize
$$ U(x)=\sum_{t,r}u_{tr}x_{tr} $$
over $F$.

Indeed, summation by parts gives
$$ U(y)-U(x) = \sum_t\sum_{k=1}^{m-1} \bigl(C_{tk}(y)-C_{tk}(x)\bigr). $$
A strict dominance improvement would strictly increase this objective. Thus any optimizer $x$ is efficient.

The face $F_{E(x)}$ is canonical and entirely efficient. Its support graph has at most $\tau$ components. Applying the extension procedure proves:

Corollary 6. For fixed $\tau$, one maximal efficient face can be computed in time polynomial in $m,L$. For fixed $\tau$, maximality of any supplied efficient face can also be tested in polynomial time.

Neither conclusion asserts polynomial-time enumeration of all maximal faces.

5.4. A sufficient margin condition for connected support

Assume that no nonempty proper subsets $T'\subsetneq T$ and $R'\subsetneq R^+$ satisfy
$$ \sum_{t\in T'}\mu_t=\sum_{r\in R'}q_r. $$
Then every nonempty face has connected bipartite support. Otherwise, any support component would supply precisely such an equality.

Under this hypothesis, maximality testing and extension of every supplied efficient face are polynomial-time by Section 5.1.

This is a hypothesis on the margins; no polynomial-time test for the hypothesis itself is asserted.

5.5. Why single-coordinate tests require connectedness

Take two types and two categories, with
$$ \mu_1=\mu_2=q_a=q_b=\frac12, $$
and rankings
$$ a\succ_1 b, \qquad b\succ_2 a. $$
Let $H$ be the singleton allocation assigning each type its favorite category. Its support is
$$ E=\{(1,a),(2,b)\}, $$
and $D(E)$ has no arcs.

Adding either missing coordinate alone produces an acyclic support graph, but the transportation equations force that coordinate to remain zero. Adding both permits feasible variation, but creates the cycle
$$ a\longrightarrow b\longrightarrow a. $$
Every allocation different from $H$ has both off-diagonal coordinates positive, so it is inefficient. Thus $H$ is maximal.

The component-cycle test handles this example correctly: its only component cycle uses both missing coordinates and fails the acyclicity test.

6. What is established about full enumeration

6.1. A complete factorial-time algorithm

For a permutation $\pi$ of $R$, let $p_\pi(r)$ denote its position and define
$$ A_\pi= \{(t,r):p_\pi(s)>p_\pi(r)\text{ for every }s\succ_t r\}. $$
Every arc in $D(A_\pi)$ increases its position, so every nonempty $F_{A_\pi}$ is entirely efficient.

Conversely, let $H$ be a maximal efficient face. A topological ordering $\pi$ of $D(E_H)$ satisfies
$$ E_H\subseteq A_\pi. $$
Hence $H\subseteq F_{A_\pi}$, and maximality forces equality.

Therefore all requested faces can be enumerated by constructing and canonicalizing these faces for all $m!$ permutations, removing duplicates, and discarding faces strictly contained in another candidate. The containment test is support inclusion by Section 3. This gives the bound
$$ (m!)^2\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
It does not give a polynomial bound in the requested output length $S$.

6.2. The exact coverage problem remains a route to hardness

Consider the decision problem asking whether a supplied list of canonically encoded nonempty efficient faces covers every efficient allocation.

Its complement belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$. A certificate is a coordinate set $E$ such that:

  • $D(E)$ is acyclic;
  • an allocation exists with support exactly $E$;
  • $E$ intersects the zero set of every supplied face.

Condition 2 is checked exactly by maximizing $\delta$ subject to
$$ x\in F,\qquad x_e=0\text{ outside }E,\qquad x_e\geq\delta\text{ on }E,\qquad 0\leq\delta\leq 1. $$
The optimum is positive exactly when the required support exists. The other conditions are polynomial-time tests. Proposition 1 then proves that the certified allocation is efficient and uncovered.

An output-polynomial enumerator would put this coverage problem in $\mathrm P$. To justify the implication, suppose the supplied faces cover the efficient set. For any maximal efficient face $K$, take a point positive on its full support. It belongs to some supplied face $H_i$, implying
$$ E_K\subseteq E_{H_i}, \qquad K\subseteq H_i. $$
Maximality forces $K=H_i$. Thus on every coverage yes-instance, the enumerator’s total output length is bounded by the supplied list length.

Run a purported enumerator for its polynomial total-time bound evaluated at that list length. If the time expires, coverage is false. If it finishes, compare its complete output with the list. Coverage holds exactly when every enumerated maximal face occurs in the list.

Consequently, a $\mathrm{coNP}$-hardness proof for this precise coverage problem would rule out output-polynomial enumeration unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. Such a reduction has not been established here.

What remains unresolved is controlling the work required to find all maximal efficient faces by their total canonical output length, or proving hardness for that enumeration task.
