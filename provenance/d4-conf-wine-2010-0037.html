The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), and type-pair eligibility, profits, and split fractions, can one compute a maximum-weight feasible mass matching and a stable cohort allocation satisfying the paper’s \(\varepsilon\)-correct-division condition, or certify that no such outcome exists, in time polynomial in \(\tau\), input bit length \(L\), and \(1/\varepsilon\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is atomless and given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types \(T\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input also specifies undirected eligible type pairs \(E\), with \(m=|E|\), binary-rational profits \(w_{tu}\in(0,1]\), split fractions \(r_{tu}\in(0,1)\) satisfying \(r_{ut}=1-r_{tu}\), and binary-rational accuracy \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\). Same-type pairs are permitted when eligible, with \(r_{tt}=1/2\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A feasible mass matching partitions each type into finitely many positive-mass cohorts. Each cohort \(c\) has type \(t(c)\) and mass \(\lambda_c\), with \(\sum_{c:t(c)=t}\lambda_c=\mu_t\), and is either unmatched or paired with a distinct cohort \(p(c)\) of equal mass, with \(p(p(c))=c\) and eligible endpoint types. Every individual participates in at most one trade; matching weight is \(\frac12\sum_{c\text{ matched}}\lambda_c w_{t(c),t(p(c))}\).
A cohort allocation assigns a constant per-person payoff \(g_c\ge0\) to each cohort, with \(g_c=0\) when unmatched and \(g_c+g_{p(c)}=w_{t(c),t(p(c))}\) when matched. Arbitrary finite cohort refinements are allowed.
Stability requires \(g_c+g_d\ge w_{t(c),t(d)}\) for every eligible pair of positive-mass cohorts, including \(c=d\) when same-type trade is eligible.
Define \(A_t=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{(w_{t,t(d)}-g_d)_+:d\text{ is a positive-mass cohort},\ \{t,t(d)\}\in E\}\bigr)\), where \((z)_+=\max(z,0)\). Excluding an individual's current partner removes only a null set, so every eligible positive-mass cohort remains an outside option.
For each matched cohort \(c\), put \(t=t(c)\), \(u=t(p(c))\), and \(S_{tu}=w_{tu}-A_t-A_u\). Additive \(\varepsilon\)-correct division requires \(|g_c-A_t-r_{tu}S_{tu}|\le\varepsilon\); stability and trade feasibility remain exact.

Can one compute a maximum-weight feasible mass matching together with a stable cohort allocation satisfying additive \(\varepsilon\)-correct division, or certify that no such outcome exists, in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\), or is this approximation task NP-hard even when \(1/\varepsilon\) is polynomially bounded in the input length?

For finite-agent networks with profits in \((0,1]\), an FPTAS computes an exactly stable, additively \(\varepsilon\)-correct outcome on a maximum-weight matching whenever one exists, and otherwise reports nonexistence. [Kanoria](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.0212)
No result settling the finite-support atomless cohort problem defined above was located in the literature search.

  • A cohort allocation was undefined; use arbitrary finite partitions into cohorts with constant per-person payoffs, pairing equal-mass cohorts and permitting further refinement.
  • Same-type eligibility was unspecified; permit trades between distinct individuals of the same type when eligible, with the symmetric split fraction one half.
  • The continuous outside-option convention was unspecified; exclude only the individual current partner, retaining every eligible positive-mass cohort, including that partner's cohort.
  • The approximation convention was unspecified; retain exact stability and trade feasibility and relax only correct division by an additive per-person epsilon.
  • There is no candidate-count parameter in this network problem; define m as the number of eligible unordered type pairs and L as the total input bit length.

Polynomial-time solvability with exact correct division

Theorem. Every instance satisfying the stated hypotheses admits a maximum-weight feasible mass matching with a stable cohort allocation satisfying exact correct division. Such an outcome can be computed in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), using at most \(2m+\tau\) positive-mass cohorts.

The decisive feature is the specified outside-option rule: the current partner’s positive-mass cohort remains an outside option. Under this rule, stability forces typewise uniform payoffs and zero surplus on every matched pair.

1. Stability forces typewise uniform payoffs

Consider any feasible mass matching with a stable cohort allocation. Since each type has positive mass and finitely many cohorts, define
\(y_t=\min_{c:t(c)=t}g_c\).

For any eligible pair \(\{t,u\}\), apply stability to cohorts attaining these minima. This gives
\(y_t+y_u\ge w_{tu}\).
For a same-type pair, the permitted comparison of a minimum-payoff cohort with itself gives \(2y_t\ge w_{tt}\).

Now let \(c\) and \(p(c)\) be matched cohorts of types \(t\) and \(u\). Trade feasibility and the preceding inequality imply
\[ \text{} \]
\(w_{tu}=g_c+g_{p(c)}\ge y_t+y_u\ge w_{tu}\).

Consequently, equality holds throughout. Because both \(g_c-y_t\) and \(g_{p(c)}-y_u\) are nonnegative and their sum is zero, we obtain
\(g_c=y_t\) and \(g_{p(c)}=y_u\).

Thus every matched cohort of type \(t\) receives \(y_t\). If that type also has an unmatched cohort, its zero payoff forces \(y_t=0\), so its unmatched cohorts receive the same payoff. If the type has no matched cohorts, all its payoffs are zero.

Therefore every cohort of type \(t\) receives \(y_t\).

2. Every stable allocation satisfies exact correct division

By typewise uniformity, the outside option is
\(A_t=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{(w_{tu}-y_u)_+:\{t,u\}\in E\}\bigr)\).

Stability gives \(w_{tu}-y_u\le y_t\), and \(y_t\ge0\). Hence \(A_t\le y_t\).

If type \(t\) participates in a matched pair with type \(u\), the preceding proof gives \(w_{tu}=y_t+y_u\). Its partner’s positive-mass cohort is included among the outside options, so
\(A_t\ge(w_{tu}-y_u)_+=y_t\).
Thus \(A_t=y_t\) for every participating type.

For a type with no matched cohorts, \(y_t=0\), and \(0\le A_t\le y_t\) again gives \(A_t=y_t\).

For every matched pair, therefore,
\(S_{tu}=w_{tu}-A_t-A_u=w_{tu}-y_t-y_u=0\).

It follows that every matched cohort satisfies
\(\left|g_c-A_t-r_{tu}S_{tu}\right|=\left|y_t-y_t-r_{tu}\cdot0\right|=0\).

This proves exact correct division for every prescribed split fraction. It remains to construct a stable allocation on a maximum-weight matching.

3. A linear program exactly represents mass matching

Introduce one variable \(x_e\ge0\) for each eligible undirected pair \(e\). It represents the total mass of trades on that pair: a trade of mass \(x_e\) consumes that mass at each endpoint.

Define the incidence coefficient \(B_{te}\) to be \(1\) when \(e\) has two different endpoints and \(t\) is one of them, \(2\) when \(e=\{t,t\}\), and \(0\) otherwise.

Consider the primal linear program
\[ \text{} \]
\(\displaystyle \max_{x\ge0}\sum_{e\in E}w_e x_e \quad\text{subject to}\quad \sum_{e\in E}B_{te}x_e\le\mu_t \quad(t\in T). \)

Every feasible cohort matching gives a feasible primal vector. Aggregate matched cohort pairs by their eligible type pair, counting each paired cohort pair once. The resulting variables consume exactly the corresponding matched type masses. A same-type trade consumes twice its trade mass. Its objective contribution is \(x_e w_e\), agreeing with the question’s weight convention.

Every feasible primal vector gives a feasible cohort matching. For each \(x_e>0\):

  • If \(e=\{t,u\}\) with \(t\ne u\), create one cohort of each endpoint type, both of mass \(x_e\), and pair them.
  • If \(e=\{t,t\}\), create two distinct cohorts of type \(t\), both of mass \(x_e\), and pair them.

For each type, put its remaining mass
\(s_t=\mu_t-\sum_eB_{te}x_e\)
in one unmatched cohort if \(s_t>0\).

These finitely many prescribed cohorts are admissible in the atomless model. Their masses exhaust each type’s mass, and their matching weight is exactly the primal objective. Consequently, the primal optimum equals the maximum feasible mass-matching weight.

4. An optimal dual supplies a stable allocation

The dual linear program is
\[ \text{} \]
\(\displaystyle \min_{y\ge0}\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t y_t \quad\text{subject to}\quad y_t+y_u\ge w_{tu} \quad(\{t,u\}\in E). \)

For same-type pairs, the constraint means \(2y_t\ge w_{tt}\).

The primal is feasible because \(x=0\) is feasible. Its feasible region is bounded: each variable consumes positive mass at an endpoint and is bounded by that endpoint’s mass. Hence its optimum is finite and attained. The dual is feasible because \(y_t=1/2\) for all types satisfies every constraint, using \(w_{tu}\le1\). Linear-programming strong duality therefore provides optimal solutions \(x^*,y^*\) of equal objective value.

Write \(s_t=\mu_t-\sum_e B_{te}x_e^*\). Their zero duality gap has the explicit decomposition
\[ \text{} \]
\(\displaystyle 0= \sum_t\mu_t y_t^*-\sum_e w_e x_e^* = \sum_t y_t^*s_t+ \sum_{e=\{t,u\}}x_e^*(y_t^*+y_u^*-w_e). \)

Every summand is nonnegative by primal and dual feasibility. Consequently:

  • \(s_t>0\) implies \(y_t^*=0\).
  • \(x_{\{t,u\}}^*>0\) implies \(y_t^*+y_u^*=w_{tu}\), including \(2y_t^*=w_{tt}\) for same-type trades.

Construct cohorts from \(x^*\) as in Section 3 and assign payoff \(y_t^*\) to every cohort of type \(t\). These implications verify zero payoff on unmatched cohorts and exact trade feasibility on matched cohorts. Nonnegativity follows from dual feasibility.

For any eligible cohorts of types \(t,u\), dual feasibility gives
\(g_c+g_d=y_t^*+y_u^*\ge w_{tu}\).
This includes the required same-cohort comparisons. Thus the allocation is exactly stable.

By Section 2, it also satisfies exact correct division.

5. Complexity and the finite-agent distinction

Both linear programs have rational data, dimensions bounded by \(m+\tau\), and encoding length polynomial in \(L\). The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming computes exact rational optimal primal and dual solutions of polynomial encoding length. The cohort construction then uses at most two cohorts per eligible pair and one unmatched cohort per type, giving at most \(2m+\tau\) cohorts.

Thus the running time and output length are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), independently of \(1/\varepsilon\). Every valid instance has the required outcome; the nonexistence branch is never needed.

In the finite-agent model, outside options exclude the current partner, as specified in [Kanoria’s definition of edge surplus](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.0212). Here the partner’s positive-mass cohort remains available. That distinction makes the zero-surplus argument applicable to the full stated atomless problem.
