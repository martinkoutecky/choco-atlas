The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite patient-type and provider-type sets \(T\) and \(R\), rational patient masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, rational provider supplies \(\kappa_r\), match qualities \(\theta_{t,r}\in[0,1]\), and a common acceptance probability \(p\in(0,1]\), assign each patient type fractions \(x_{t,S}\) to upfront menus \(S\subseteq R\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq R}x_{t,S}=1\). Patients of each type arrive uniformly interleaved over \([0,1]\), accept with probability \(p\), and, if accepting, choose their highest-quality available provider type in their menu or abstain; a match consumes one unit of provider supply. Is there a polynomial-time algorithm in the type counts and input bit length that returns a menu policy with expected normalized match quality at least \(p\) times the optimum over all such policies?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau=|T|\) patient types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=|R|\geq1\) provider types with positive binary-rational supplies \(\kappa_r\), measured in patient-mass units and not required to sum to \(1\). Qualities \(\theta_{t,r}\in[0,1]\) and the common acceptance probability \(p\in(0,1]\) are binary rationals; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Each patient type has a supplied total order resolving equal qualities.
An upfront policy assigns fractions \(x_{t,S}\geq0\) to menus \(S\subseteq R\), including the empty menu, with \(\sum_{S\subseteq R}x_{t,S}=1\). These fractions remain fixed throughout arrival; policies may use real fractions, while an algorithm returns a list of nonzero binary-rational fractions and their menus.
Uniform interleaving means that every patient-menu class \((t,S)\) arrives at constant mass rate \(\mu_t x_{t,S}\) over time \(u\in[0,1]\). Exactly a fraction \(p\) of each arriving mass accepts; accepting mass chooses its highest-quality menu type with remaining supply, even if its quality is zero, and abstains only when none is available.
Matching mass \(d\) consumes supply \(d\). Between exhaustion events, each class routes accepted mass at rate \(p\mu_t x_{t,S}\) to its chosen provider type; exhausted types are removed immediately, including all types exhausted simultaneously. Supplies never replenish. This defines the deterministic fluid interpretation of expected matching.
Let \(y_{t,r}(x)\) be the total mass matched from type \(t\) to type \(r\), and define patient-normalized quality \(Q(x)=\sum_{t\in T,r\in R}\theta_{t,r}y_{t,r}(x)\), with unmatched mass contributing zero. Set \(\mathrm{OPT}=\sup_x Q(x)\) over all upfront policies.

Does an algorithm running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) always return an upfront menu policy \(x\) satisfying \(Q(x)\geq p\,\mathrm{OPT}\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?

For finite patients and unit-capacity providers under uniformly random response order and common acceptance probability \(p\), a polynomial-time policy achieves at least \(p\) times optimal expected patient-normalized quality. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10353v2#S3.SS3.SSS2)
No result settling this approximation guarantee for the specified fluid model was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The continuum probability model was unspecified; uniform interleaving and acceptance are interpreted as constant class arrival rates and exact thinning by p, with deterministic supply exhaustion.
  • Provider supplies are positive masses in the same units as patient mass; zero-supply types are omitted, and total provider supply is not normalized.
  • Qualities and p are binary rationals, and L includes all numerical data and tie-breaking orders.
  • Equal qualities are resolved by a supplied total order for each patient type.
  • Acceptance entails choosing an available offered provider even at zero quality; empty or exhausted menus cause abstention, excluding unavailable providers from the maximization.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result and encoding convention.

If “binary-rational” means a rational whose numerator and denominator are encoded in binary, the problem is polynomial-time exactly solvable. Singleton menus suffice.

If it instead means a dyadic rational, the output restriction matters: the requested guarantee is achievable in polynomial time for \(p<1\), but can be impossible at \(p=1\). Both conclusions are proved below.

1. A transportation upper bound.

Consider the linear program with variables \(z_{t,r}\):

\[ V=\max \sum_{t\in T,r\in R}\theta_{t,r}z_{t,r}, \]

subject to

\[ z_{t,r}\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{r\in R}z_{t,r}\leq p\mu_t\quad(t\in T),\qquad \sum_{t\in T}z_{t,r}\leq\kappa_r\quad(r\in R). \]

For any upfront policy \(x\), its matching matrix \(y(x)\) satisfies these constraints. Indeed:

  • Type \(t\) has total accepting mass \(p\mu_t\), so its total matched mass is at most \(p\mu_t\).
  • Matching to provider \(r\) consumes its supply, so total matching to \(r\) is at most \(\kappa_r\).
  • Matching masses are nonnegative.

Consequently, \(Q(x)\leq V\) for every policy, including policies with real fractions. Taking the supremum gives \(\mathrm{OPT}\leq V\).

2. Every feasible transportation flow is implementable.

Let \(z\) be any feasible solution of the linear program. Define a policy using only singleton menus and the empty menu:

\[ x_{t,\{r\}}=\frac{z_{t,r}}{p\mu_t}, \qquad x_{t,\varnothing}=1-\sum_{r\in R}\frac{z_{t,r}}{p\mu_t}, \]

and set all other fractions to zero. These fractions are well-defined because \(p\mu_t>0\), and they are nonnegative and sum to one by the row constraints.

For provider \(r\), put \(q_r=\sum_t z_{t,r}\). While \(r\) remains available, singleton-menu classes send it total accepted mass at constant rate

\[ \sum_t p\mu_t x_{t,\{r\}}=q_r. \]

A singleton class cannot redirect to another provider. Thus, if \(q_r>0\), provider \(r\)'s exhaustion time is \(\kappa_r/q_r\), which is at least \(1\) by feasibility. If \(q_r=0\), it receives no mass.

Every provider therefore remains available throughout \(u\in[0,1)\). Exhaustion exactly at \(u=1\) changes no matched mass. Each class \((t,\{r\})\) consequently matches mass

\[ p\mu_t x_{t,\{r\}}=z_{t,r}. \]

Hence \(y(x)=z\), and \(Q(x)=\sum_{t,r}\theta_{t,r}z_{t,r}\).

Applying this construction to an optimal linear-programming solution proves

\[ \boxed{\mathrm{OPT}=V.} \]

In particular, the supremum is attained.

3. Polynomial-time algorithm for binary-encoded rational fractions.

The linear program has \(\tau m\) variables and \(\tau+m\) supply and mass constraints. Its feasible region is nonempty, since \(z=0\) is feasible, and compact, since every variable satisfies \(0\leq z_{t,r}\leq p\mu_t\).

All coefficients are rational with bit lengths polynomial in the input length \(L\). The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore computes an exact rational optimum \(z^*\), with polynomial output bit length.

Construct the fractions in Section 2 by exact rational arithmetic. Division by the positive rational \(p\mu_t\), and summation of at most \(m\) fractions per type, preserve polynomial bit length. The output contains at most \(\tau(m+1)\) nonzero entries.

Thus the algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and returns a policy satisfying

\[ Q(x)=\mathrm{OPT}\geq p\,\mathrm{OPT}, \]

where the last inequality uses \(\mathrm{OPT}\geq0\) and \(p\leq1\).

The [cited finite-patient result](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10353v2#S3.SS3.SSS2) concerns random individual responses. The exact implementation above follows directly from the specified constant-rate fluid arrivals.

4. If output fractions must be dyadic: a polynomial guarantee for \(p<1\).

A dyadic rational has the form \(a/2^b\), with integers \(a,b\) and \(b\geq0\). The fractions constructed above need not have this form.

Nevertheless, for \(p<1\), downward rounding gives the requested guarantee. If all qualities are zero, return empty menus. Otherwise define

\[ H=\max_{t,r}\theta_{t,r}\min\{p\mu_t,\kappa_r\}>0. \]

The transportation flow consisting of the single maximizing edge with mass \(\min\{p\mu_t,\kappa_r\}\) is feasible. Section 2 therefore proves \(H\leq\mathrm{OPT}\).

Let \(x^*\) be the optimal singleton policy constructed above. Choose a nonnegative integer \(b\) such that

\[ pm\,2^{-b}\leq(1-p)H, \]

and define

\[ \widehat x_{t,\{r\}} =2^{-b}\left\lfloor2^b x^*_{t,\{r\}}\right\rfloor, \qquad \widehat x_{t,\varnothing} =1-\sum_r\widehat x_{t,\{r\}}. \]

These are valid dyadic fractions. Rounding downward reduces every provider's assigned rate, so no provider exhausts before time \(1\). Thus the quality loss is bounded by

\[ Q(x^*)-Q(\widehat x) =\sum_{t,r}\theta_{t,r}p\mu_t \bigl(x^*_{t,\{r\}}-\widehat x_{t,\{r\}}\bigr) \leq pm\,2^{-b}. \]

It follows that

\[ Q(\widehat x) \geq\mathrm{OPT}-(1-p)H \geq p\,\mathrm{OPT}. \]

The positive rational \((1-p)H\) has polynomial bit length, so \(b\) can be chosen polynomially bounded in \(L+\log m\). Computing and outputting these rounded fractions consequently takes polynomial time.

5. Dyadic-only output can be impossible at \(p=1\).

Consider two patient types \(a,b\) and two providers \(A,B\), with

\[ p=1,\qquad \mu_a=\frac34,\quad\mu_b=\frac14,\qquad \kappa_A=\kappa_B=\frac12, \]

and qualities

\[ \theta_{a,A}=\frac34,\quad \theta_{a,B}=\frac12,\quad \theta_{b,A}=1,\quad \theta_{b,B}=0. \]

Every input number is dyadic.

Write \(s=y_{b,A}\), \(v=y_{a,A}\), and \(w=y_{a,B}\). Feasibility gives

\[ s\leq\frac14,\qquad v\leq\frac12-s,\qquad w\leq\frac34-v. \]

Therefore

\[ Q=\frac34v+\frac12w+s \leq\frac38+\frac14v+s \leq\frac12+\frac34s \leq\frac{11}{16}. \]

The singleton policy with fractions \(x_{a,\{A\}}=1/3\), \(x_{a,\{B\}}=2/3\), and \(x_{b,\{A\}}=1\) attains \(11/16\). Hence \(\mathrm{OPT}=11/16\).

Equality in the displayed inequalities requires

\[ y_{b,A}=\frac14,\qquad y_{a,A}=\frac14,\qquad y_{a,B}=\frac12. \]

In particular, \(A\) cannot exhaust at any time \(u<1\): otherwise type \(b\)'s matching to \(A\) would be at most \(\mu_bu<1/4\).

Because \(A\) remains available and type \(a\) strictly prefers \(A\) to \(B\), every type-\(a\) class whose menu contains \(A\) routes to \(A\) throughout the horizon. Optimality therefore requires

\[ \frac34\bigl(x_{a,\{A\}}+x_{a,\{A,B\}}\bigr)=\frac14, \]

or

\[ x_{a,\{A\}}+x_{a,\{A,B\}}=\frac13. \]

A sum of dyadic rationals is dyadic, whereas \(1/3\) is not. Thus no dyadic-fraction policy attains \(\mathrm{OPT}\) in this instance. Since \(p=1\), no such output can satisfy the requested guarantee.

Accordingly, with binary numerator/denominator encoding the answer is exact polynomial-time solvability. With dyadic-only output, the endpoint \(p=1\) creates a representational impossibility; for every \(p<1\), the polynomial-time guarantee still holds.
