The anchor (named result in the paper)

Lemma 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite resource set \(Q\) and a support \(\mathcal T\) of \(\tau\) types, where type \(t\) has mass \(\mu_t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), peak \(p_t\), and eligible sources \(N_t\subseteq Q\), and each source \(r\) has rational supply \(\omega_r\) per unit of normalized population, compute the unique weighted analogue of Lemma 2’s partition \((\mathcal T_+,\mathcal T_0,\mathcal T_-;Q_+,Q_0,Q_-)\) in time polynomial in the input size and rational bit length \(L\). Define \(P(S)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t p_t\), \(\Omega(R)=\sum_{r\in R}\omega_r\), \(f(S)=\bigcup_{t\in S}N_t\), and \(g(R)=\{t:N_t\cap R\ne\varnothing\}\). The partition must satisfy \(E(\mathcal T_-,Q_0\cup Q_-)=E(\mathcal T_0,Q_-)=\varnothing\), \(P(S)<\Omega(f(S)\cap Q_-)\) for each nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal T_+\), and \(\Omega(R)<P(g(R)\cap\mathcal T_-)\) for each nonempty \(R\subseteq Q_+\); the balanced component must admit flows \(y_{tr}\ge0\) on eligible edges in \(\mathcal T_0\times Q_0\) with \(\sum_r y_{tr}=\mu_t p_t\) and \(\sum_t y_{tr}=\omega_r\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(\mathcal T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a finite peak \(p_t\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) per individual and eligible sources \(N_t\subseteq Q\); the nonempty resource set has size \(m=|Q|\) and supplies \(\omega_r\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) per unit population. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
The explicitly given bipartite eligibility graph \(E=\{(t,r):r\in N_t\}\) is connected. Transfers are divisible, nonnegative, and have no edge capacities; all supply must be allocated. Write \(E(S,R)=E\cap(S\times R)\), \(P(S)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_tp_t\), \(\Omega(R)=\sum_{r\in R}\omega_r\), \(f(S)=\bigcup_{t\in S}N_t\), and \(g(R)=\{t:N_t\cap R\ne\varnothing\}\).
Compute the canonical partitions \((\mathcal T_+,\mathcal T_0,\mathcal T_-;Q_+,Q_0,Q_-)\), allowing empty parts, satisfying:
\(E(\mathcal T_-,Q_0\cup Q_-)=E(\mathcal T_0,Q_-)=\varnothing\), and \(p_t>0\) for every \(t\in\mathcal T_-\).
\(P(S)<\Omega(f(S)\cap Q_-)\) for every nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal T_+\), and \(\Omega(R)<P(g(R)\cap\mathcal T_-)\) for every nonempty \(R\subseteq Q_+\).
The balanced component admits transfers \(y_{tr}\ge0\), zero off eligible edges in \(\mathcal T_0\times Q_0\), with \(\sum_{r\in Q_0}y_{tr}=\mu_tp_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_0}y_{tr}=\omega_r\). Is computing this canonical partition solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Polynomial-time solvability

The canonical partition is computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). One maximum-flow computation, followed by two residual-graph searches, suffices.

The finite-agent decomposition appears in Lemma 2 of [Bochet, İlkılıç, and Moulin (2013)](https://yoksis.bilkent.edu.tr/pdf/files/10.1016-j.jet.2012.09.016.pdf). Below is a direct proof for the support representation, including uniqueness, zero-peak types, and binary bit complexity.

1. Aggregate demands and construct the network

For each type, define its aggregate peak demand by \(d_t=\mu_t p_t\). Since \(\mu_t>0\), we have \(d_t>0\) exactly when \(p_t>0\). Moreover, \(P(S)=\sum_{t\in S}d_t\).

Construct a directed network with source \(s\), sink \(z\), resource vertices \(Q\), and type vertices \(\mathcal T\). Its arcs and capacities are:

| Arc | Capacity |
|---|---|
| \(s\to r\), for \(r\in Q\) | \(\omega_r\) |
| \(r\to t\), for \((t,r)\in E\) | \(M\) |
| \(t\to z\), for \(t\in\mathcal T\) | \(d_t\) |

Choose \(M=1+\Omega(Q)+P(\mathcal T)\).

Compute any maximum flow. Write \(x_{rt}\) for its flow on \(r\to t\), and put \(u_r=\sum_t x_{rt}\) and \(v_t=\sum_r x_{rt}\). Thus \(u_r\leq\omega_r\) and \(v_t\leq d_t\).

The flow value is at most \(\Omega(Q)<M\). Consequently, every eligibility arc \(r\to t\) has strictly positive forward residual capacity. Its reverse arc \(t\to r\) has positive residual capacity exactly when \(x_{rt}>0\).

In the residual graph, let:

  • \(A\) be the vertices reachable from \(s\);
  • \(B\) be the vertices from which \(z\) is reachable.

Maximality of the flow implies \(A\cap B=\varnothing\). Define

\( \mathcal T_+=A\cap\mathcal T,\qquad Q_-=A\cap Q, \)

\( \mathcal T_-=B\cap\mathcal T,\qquad Q_+=B\cap Q, \)

and let \(\mathcal T_0\) and \(Q_0\) contain the remaining type and resource vertices.

We prove that these are exactly the required canonical partitions.

2. Eligibility restrictions, zero peaks, and balance

Eligibility restrictions

If \(r\in Q_-\), every eligible neighbor \(t\) is reachable from \(s\), because \(r\to t\) has positive residual capacity. Hence \(g(Q_-)\subseteq\mathcal T_+\).

If \(t\in\mathcal T_-\), every eligible resource \(r\) can reach \(z\) through \(r\to t\). Hence \(f(\mathcal T_-)\subseteq Q_+\).

These inclusions give all required edge restrictions:

\(E(\mathcal T_-,Q_0\cup Q_-)=E(\mathcal T_0,Q_-)=\varnothing.\)

Two further consequences of reverse residual arcs will be useful:

  • If \(x_{rt}>0\) and \(t\in\mathcal T_+\), then \(r\in Q_-\).
  • If \(x_{rt}>0\) and \(r\in Q_+\), then \(t\in\mathcal T_-\).

Together with the eligibility restrictions, these imply that every positive \(x_{rt}\) belongs to one of the three blocks \(\mathcal T_+\times Q_-\), \(\mathcal T_0\times Q_0\), or \(\mathcal T_-\times Q_+\).

Saturation and the positive-peak convention

Every \(t\in\mathcal T_+\) satisfies \(v_t=d_t\). Otherwise \(t\to z\) would have positive residual capacity, yielding an augmenting path from \(s\) to \(z\).

Every \(r\in Q_+\) satisfies \(u_r=\omega_r\). Otherwise \(s\to r\), followed by a residual path from \(r\) to \(z\), would be an augmenting path.

Also, every \(t\in\mathcal T_0\) satisfies \(v_t=d_t\): an unsaturated \(t\to z\) would place \(t\) in \(B\). Similarly, every \(r\in Q_0\) satisfies \(u_r=\omega_r\): an unsaturated \(s\to r\) would place \(r\) in \(A\).

Finally, suppose \(d_t=0\). Then \(v_t=0\), so all \(x_{rt}=0\). The type vertex \(t\) has no outgoing residual arc: neither \(t\to z\) nor any reverse arc \(t\to r\) has positive residual capacity. Therefore \(t\notin B\). Thus \(p_t>0\) for every \(t\in\mathcal T_-\), as required.

Balanced transfers

Restrict the flow to the middle block and define \(y_{tr}=x_{rt}\) there, with zero transfers elsewhere.

The block property and saturation established above give

\(\sum_{r\in Q_0}y_{tr}=d_t=\mu_t p_t\) for every \(t\in\mathcal T_0\),

and

\(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_0}y_{tr}=\omega_r\) for every \(r\in Q_0\).

Thus the balanced component has the required transfers.

3. Proof of both strict inequalities

Strict underdemand

Take a nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal T_+\), and set \(H=f(S)\cap Q_-\).

All demand of \(S\) is met by resources in \(H\). Flow conservation therefore gives

\(\Omega(H)-P(S)=\sum_{r\in H}(\omega_r-u_r)+\sum_{r\in H,\ t\notin S}x_{rt}\geq 0.\)

Suppose equality held. Every term on the right would vanish. Thus all resources in \(H\) would be saturated, and no positive flow from \(H\) would go to a type outside \(S\).

Consider the residual graph restricted to \(A\), and let \(U=H\cup S\). There is no residual arc entering \(U\) from \(A\setminus U\):

  • An arc \(s\to r\), with \(r\in H\), has zero residual capacity because \(r\) is saturated.
  • A reverse arc \(t\to r\), with \(r\in H\) and \(t\notin S\), has zero residual capacity because \(x_{rt}=0\).
  • A forward eligibility arc from \(r\in Q_-\setminus H\) to \(t\in S\) cannot exist, by the definition of \(H\).

These exhaust the possible incoming residual arcs. Since \(s\notin U\), no vertex of \(U\) could be reachable from \(s\). This contradicts the nonempty inclusion \(S\subseteq A\).

Consequently, \(P(S)<\Omega(f(S)\cap Q_-)\).

Strict overdemand

Take a nonempty \(R\subseteq Q_+\), and set \(H=g(R)\cap\mathcal T_-\).

All resources in \(R\) are saturated and send their flow only to \(H\). Hence

\(P(H)-\Omega(R)=\sum_{t\in H}(d_t-v_t)+\sum_{t\in H,\ r\notin R}x_{rt}\geq 0.\)

Suppose equality held. Then all types in \(H\) would be saturated, and no positive flow into \(H\) would come from outside \(R\).

In the residual graph restricted to \(B\), let \(U=R\cup H\). There is no residual arc leaving \(U\) for \(B\setminus U\):

  • An arc \(t\to z\), with \(t\in H\), has zero residual capacity because \(t\) is saturated.
  • A reverse arc \(t\to r\), with \(t\in H\) and \(r\notin R\), has zero residual capacity because \(x_{rt}=0\).
  • A forward eligibility arc from \(r\in R\) to \(t\in\mathcal T_-\setminus H\) cannot exist, by the definition of \(H\).

Since \(z\notin U\), no resource in \(R\) could reach \(z\). This contradicts \(R\subseteq B\).

Therefore, \(\Omega(R)<P(g(R)\cap\mathcal T_-)\).

This proves existence of a partition satisfying every condition in the question.

4. Uniqueness of the partition

We first establish that residual reachability does not depend on the chosen maximum flow, and then show that every partition satisfying the stated conditions has the same reachability sets.

Residual reachability is invariant

For any source–sink cut with source side \(X\), flow conservation gives

\(\operatorname{cap}(X)-F=\sum_{a\text{ leaving }X}(c_a-f_a)+\sum_{a\text{ entering }X}f_a,\)

where \(F\) is the maximum-flow value.

All terms are nonnegative. By the max-flow/min-cut theorem, \(X\) is the source side of a minimum cut exactly when there is no positive-capacity residual arc leaving \(X\).

It follows that:

  • \(A\) is itself a minimum-cut source side and is contained in every minimum-cut source side.
  • The complement of \(B\) is itself a minimum-cut source side and contains every minimum-cut source side.

For the second statement, a vertex outside \(B\) cannot have a residual arc to a vertex in \(B\); and a minimum-cut source side cannot contain a vertex with a residual path to \(z\).

Thus \(A\) is the intersection of all minimum-cut source sides, while the complement of \(B\) is their union. Both are determined by the network, independently of the maximum flow.

A capacitated Hall lemma

For a bipartite graph with resource capacities \(a_r\) and type capacities \(b_t\), nonnegative eligible transfers can:

  • saturate every type capacity exactly when \(\sum_{t\in S}b_t\leq\sum_{r\in f(S)}a_r\) for all type subsets \(S\);
  • saturate every resource capacity exactly when \(\sum_{r\in R}a_r\leq\sum_{t\in g(R)}b_t\) for all resource subsets \(R\).

Here “saturate” means equality at the specified vertices and no capacity violation at the other vertices.

Necessity follows by summing transfers. For sufficiency, use the network from Section 1 with middle-arc capacity exceeding both total capacities. A cut crossing a middle arc already exceeds either target flow value. Any other cut, containing resource subset \(R_X\) and type subset \(T_X\) on its source side, satisfies \(g(R_X)\subseteq T_X\) and has capacity

\(\sum_{r\notin R_X}a_r+\sum_{t\in T_X}b_t.\)

The first Hall condition, applied to the types outside \(T_X\), makes this at least \(\sum_t b_t\). The second, applied to \(R_X\), makes it at least \(\sum_r a_r\). The max-flow/min-cut theorem proves both assertions.

Every admissible partition produces the same sets

Consider any other partition satisfying the question’s conditions. Denote it by hats.

The Hall lemma supplies a flow in \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_+\times\widehat Q_-\) saturating all type demands, because strict underdemand implies the corresponding non-strict Hall inequalities.

It also supplies a flow in \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_-\times\widehat Q_+\) saturating all resource supplies, because strict overdemand implies the other Hall inequalities. Use the stipulated balanced transfers in \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_0\times\widehat Q_0\).

Combining these three flows, with zero flow between blocks, gives a feasible flow in the auxiliary network.

Any residual path starting at \(s\) remains within \(\{s\}\cup\widehat Q_-\cup\widehat{\mathcal T}_+\): only resources in \(\widehat Q_-\) can have unsaturated source arcs; their eligible neighbors lie in \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_+\); these types have saturated sink arcs; and positive reverse arcs remain in the same block. Hence there is no augmenting path, so this flow is maximum.

Likewise, vertices that can reach \(z\) lie within \(\{z\}\cup\widehat{\mathcal T}_-\cup\widehat Q_+\).

We now prove that these containments are equalities.

Let \(S\) be the types in \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_+\) not reachable from \(s\), and let \(W\) be the resources in \(\widehat Q_-\) not reachable from \(s\).

Every resource in \(W\) is saturated. It sends no positive flow to a reachable type, because the resulting reverse arc would make that resource reachable. Therefore \(\Omega(W)\leq P(S)\). Moreover, \(f(S)\cap\widehat Q_-\subseteq W\), since every forward eligibility arc is residual.

If \(S\ne\varnothing\), strict underdemand gives the contradiction

\(P(S)<\Omega(f(S)\cap\widehat Q_-)\leq\Omega(W)\leq P(S).\)

Thus \(S=\varnothing\), and then \(\Omega(W)\leq 0\) forces \(W=\varnothing\), because supplies are positive.

For the other component, let \(U\) be the types in \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_-\) that cannot reach \(z\), and let \(R\) be the resources in \(\widehat Q_+\) that cannot reach \(z\).

Every type in \(U\) is saturated. It receives no positive flow from a resource that can reach \(z\), since the reverse arc would let that type reach \(z\). Consequently, \(P(U)\leq\Omega(R)\). Also, \(g(R)\cap\widehat{\mathcal T}_-\subseteq U\).

If \(R\ne\varnothing\), strict overdemand yields

\(\Omega(R)<P(g(R)\cap\widehat{\mathcal T}_-)\leq P(U)\leq\Omega(R),\)

a contradiction. Thus \(R=\varnothing\), and \(P(U)\leq 0\) forces \(U=\varnothing\), because every type in \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_-\) has positive peak and positive mass.

The residual sets of this maximum flow therefore identify precisely the hatted components. Their invariance proves that the hatted partition equals the partition computed in Section 1. Uniqueness follows.

5. Binary bit complexity

The network has \(n=m+\tau+2\) vertices and \(a=m+\tau+|E|\) arcs. Because the eligibility graph is explicitly given, these quantities are polynomially bounded by the stated input parameters.

Write the input rationals with integer numerators and positive integer denominators. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all masses, peaks, and supplies.

The binary length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Also, \(D\omega_r\) and \(D\mu_t p_t\) are integers: the denominator product defining \(D\) includes both denominators in each \(\mu_t p_t\).

Multiplying every network capacity by \(D\) therefore gives an integer-capacity network with middle capacity

\(DM=D+\sum_r D\omega_r+\sum_t D\mu_t p_t.\)

All capacities have \(O(L+\log(m+\tau))\) bits. Computing them takes polynomial time.

The Edmonds–Karp algorithm computes a maximum flow in a directed network with nonnegative integer capacities using \(O(na^2)\) arithmetic operations, independently of the numerical magnitudes of the capacities. Its hypotheses hold here. Flow values and residual capacities have polynomially many bits, so its arithmetic operations take polynomial bit time. The two residual-graph searches take \(O(n+a)\) time.

Finally, positive scaling preserves maximum flows after rescaling and preserves which residual capacities are positive. Thus the integer network produces exactly the desired rational-input partition.

No population expansion is needed: each supported type contributes one vertex. This establishes polynomial-time computation in \(m,\tau,L\).
