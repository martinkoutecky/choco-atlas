The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many worker types θ with binary-rational population masses μ_θ, a binary-label distribution, per-capita task loads, a finite horizon and payment-scale action set, and each type’s effort costs and response dynamics, find an observation-based payment policy maximizing expected discounted requester utility per capita while making truthful reporting and high effort an equilibrium for every positive-mass type.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when

\[ m=\tau=1,\qquad |\mathcal A|=2, \]

and the masses, accuracies, costs, discount factor, payment scales, utility table, and threshold are fixed constants. Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in the total input length would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof uses the supplied model’s permission to specify payment-dependent hidden-state transitions and stochastic public observations of collected reports. Individual deviations leave those population transitions and observations unchanged.

An exact algorithm runs in polynomial time in the fully expanded history tree. Below, I give an explicit exponential upper bound in the compact input parameters.

1. Reduction from satisfiability

Let \(\varphi\) be a Boolean formula in conjunctive normal form, with variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) and \(k\geq1\) clauses. We may restrict to instances with \(n\geq1\).

Duplicate clauses until their number \(K\) is a power of two, with \(k\leq K<2k\). Duplication preserves satisfiability and makes a uniform random clause selection use dyadic probabilities.

Construct an instance with the following parameters:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Tasks and types | \(m=\tau=1\) |
| Type mass and task load | \(\mu_1=1\), \(d_{1,t}=1\) |
| Horizon | \(T=n+1\) |
| Accuracies | \(P_{1,H}=1\), \(P_{1,L}=1/2\) |
| Effort costs | \(c_{1,H}=1/2\), \(c_{1,L}=0\) |
| Available scales | \(\mathcal A=\{2,4\}\) |
| Other constants | \(b=0\), \(\gamma=1/2\), \(\eta=1\) |
| Utility table | \(F(0)=2\), \(F(1)=3\) |
| Threshold | \(V=0\) |

Every worker receives the single task in every round. High effort reveals its true label perfectly; low effort gives an independent fair binary signal.

Hidden state and transitions

Initially, select a clause index \(C\) uniformly from \(\{1,\ldots,K\}\). This index remains hidden throughout.

The hidden state also contains a flag \(s\), initially zero. For each assignment round \(t\leq n\), interpret the selected scale as

\[ x_t= \begin{cases} 0,&a_t=2,\\ 1,&a_t=4. \end{cases} \]

At the end of that round, set \(s\) to one if it was already one or the selected value of \(x_t\) satisfies a literal of clause \(C\). Otherwise leave it zero.

Consequently, after round \(n\),

\[ s=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the assignment encoded by }a_1,\ldots,a_n \text{ satisfies clause }C. \]

In every round, generate a fresh fair ground-truth label \(Y_t\in\{-1,+1\}\), independently of the clause and previous labels.

These are legitimate payment-dependent transitions. In the first \(n\) rounds, the truthful-high-effort population’s payment per worker will be \(a_t/2\), as verified below. Thus the hidden transition can distinguish the two assignment choices through the common population payment, which is either \(1\) or \(2\). A unilateral deviation has zero effect on this population payment or the flag.

Public observations and timing

Workers choose effort, receive their private signals, and submit reports before the current public observation.

The public observation is a binary statistic \(Z_t\) of collected population reports. Under the truthful-high-effort population, all reports equal \(Y_t\).

For the first \(n\) rounds, the statistic reveals that report without noise:

\[ Z_t=Y_t. \]

In the terminal round:

  • If \(s=1\), the observation equals the population report with probability \(3/4\) and its negation with probability \(1/4\).
  • If \(s=0\), the observation is a fair independent label.

This is a finite stochastic observation channel applied to collected reports. The requester receives this supplied public observation.

For each unilateral deviation, give the representative worker the appropriate perfect or independent fair signal and leave the public observation and hidden population transition laws unchanged. No additional private information about \(C\) or \(s\) is supplied.

Encoding size

The hidden state needs only the round index, \(C\), \(s\), and the current label. Hence it has \(O(nK)\) states. The public observation and private signal alphabets both have size two.

All stochastic probabilities belong to

\[ \{0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1,1/K\}, \]

and are dyadic. The transition and deviation tables can be produced in polynomial time and have polynomial length in the formula size, even using dense transition tables. The horizon is unary and has length \(n+1\).

2. Bayesian scores and terminal incentives

Fix any committed policy, including a randomized one, and evaluate the truthful-high-effort population.

Assignment rounds

For \(t\leq n\), the observation reveals \(Y_t\). Therefore a truthful high-effort report receives score \(1\), and its payment is \(a_t/2\).

Conditional on any preceding worker history, the new label remains fair. A low-effort report, including any signal-dependent choice between the signal and its negation, is independent of that label. Its expected score is therefore \(1/2\), giving expected payment zero.

Truthful high effort consequently gives expected net utility

\[ \frac{a_t}{2}-\frac12>0, \]

whereas low effort gives zero. After high effort, truthful reporting maximizes payment because the worker knows the label exactly.

The terminal posterior

Let \(h\) be a positive-probability public history immediately before the terminal observation, including the chosen terminal scale \(a\). The first \(n\) scales determine an assignment.

The clause index remains uniform conditional on \(h\). Indeed, all preceding public observations are independent fair ground-truth labels; the policy uses only those observations and previous scales, and its randomization is independent of the hidden clause.

Define

\[ p_h= \frac{\#\{\text{clauses satisfied by the assignment encoded in }h\}}{K}. \]

Then

\[ \Pr(Z_T=Y_T\mid h) = p_h\frac34+(1-p_h)\frac12 = \frac12+\frac{p_h}{4}. \]

Write \(r_h=1/2+p_h/4\). Since \(Y_T\) is fair and the observation channel is symmetric, Bayes’ rule gives

\[ q_{T,1}= \begin{cases} r_h,&Z_T=+1,\\ 1-r_h,&Z_T=-1. \end{cases} \]

For a truthful high-effort report, the score minus \(1/2\) equals \(p_h/4\) when \(Z_T=Y_T\), and equals \(-p_h/4\) otherwise. Its conditional expected payment is therefore

\[ a\frac{p_h}{4}(2r_h-1) = \frac{a p_h^2}{8}. \]

Its conditional expected net utility is

\[ u_H(h,a)=\frac{a p_h^2}{8}-\frac12. \]

A terminal low-effort signal is independent of the current truth and public observation. The latter is fair conditional on \(h\), so any report chosen using the worker’s available information has expected score \(1/2\). Thus low effort gives expected net utility zero.

Since \(a\leq4\),

\[ u_H(h,a)\leq\frac{p_h^2-1}{2}\leq0. \]

Equality requires both \(p_h=1\) and \(a=4\).

3. Correctness of the reduction

A satisfying assignment gives a feasible policy

Suppose \(\varphi\) has a satisfying assignment. Use the corresponding fixed scales during the first \(n\) rounds and select scale \(4\) in the terminal round.

Every clause is then satisfied, so \(s=1\) surely.

In each assignment round, truthful high effort maximizes conditional expected utility, as established above. In the terminal round, truthful high effort has expected payment \(1/2\) and cost \(1/2\), giving net utility zero. Low effort also gives zero. Truthful reporting after high effort is optimal: conditional on the known label, the observation agrees with it with probability \(3/4\).

Under this fixed policy, individual choices do not affect future assignments, labels, observations, or transitions. Therefore no history-dependent deviation can improve future opportunities. Applying the preceding conditional utility bounds round by round and taking discounted expectations proves that no randomized or history-dependent deviation improves total utility.

Thus truthful high effort is a best response over the entire horizon. The equality in the terminal round is permitted by the problem’s weak best-response requirement.

An unsatisfiable formula gives no feasible policy

Suppose \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable. Consider any randomized, history-dependent committed policy.

Every assignment leaves at least one clause unsatisfied. Consequently, at every positive-probability terminal history,

\[ p_h\leq1-\frac1K. \]

Consider the unilateral deviation that follows truthful high effort in the first \(n\) rounds, then uses low effort and reports its signal in the terminal round.

Earlier utilities and all population transitions are unchanged. Its terminal expected utility is zero, while truthful high effort has conditional utility at most

\[ \frac12\left(1-\frac1K\right)^2-\frac12<0. \]

The deviation therefore improves total expected discounted utility by at least

\[ \frac{\gamma^n}{2} \left[ 1-\left(1-\frac1K\right)^2 \right]>0. \]

This lower bound holds for every terminal history and every terminal scale. In particular, neither policy randomization nor dependence on earlier observations avoids the deviation.

Hence no feasible policy exists.

The requester threshold

In every round, a report’s score lies in \([0,1]\). With one task, total mass one, \(b=0\), and \(a_t\leq4\), population payment satisfies \(B_t\leq2\).

Since \(F(A_t)\geq2\),

\[ F(A_t)-B_t\geq0. \]

Every policy therefore has nonnegative requester utility when evaluated at the stipulated truthful-high-effort profile. In particular, every feasible policy meets \(V=0\).

We have proved the exact equivalence

\[ \varphi\text{ is satisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed instance admits a feasible policy with utility at least }V. \]

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

4. An exact upper bound and running times

The hardness concerns the compact finite-state input. There is nevertheless a linear-programming algorithm polynomial in the size of the expanded history tree.

Let \(H\) denote the total size of the horizon-expanded trees for the equilibrium model and the \(\tau\) representative-worker deviation models.

Posterior scores are fixed at each public history

For a specified action-observation history, all compatible hidden paths have the same factor arising from the requester’s policy choices. That factor cancels when computing the conditional hidden-state distribution.

Thus the posterior scores and inferred labels at each potentially reachable public history can be computed from the supplied kernels, independently of the policy probabilities being optimized. Histories with zero likelihood under the population model can be discarded; individual deviations cannot make them reachable.

Consequently, both requester utility and worker utility have fixed rational payoff coefficients on the expanded trees.

Linear representation of randomized strategies

Both the requester and a representative worker have perfect recall: their strategies may retain their available histories and previous choices. The sequence-form representation therefore applies. It represents strategies by nonnegative realization variables with linear flow constraints, using size polynomial in the tree. Expected payoffs are bilinear in the two players’ realization variables. These are the standard perfect-recall sequence-form properties; see [von Stengel, Section 4.2](https://ranger.uta.edu/~weems/NOTES6319/PAPERSONE/vonStengel.pdf).

Write the requester’s realization constraints as

\[ Ex=e,\qquad x\geq0. \]

For type \(\theta\), write the worker’s constraints as

\[ D_\theta y=f_\theta,\qquad y\geq0. \]

Let \(M_\theta\) be its discounted payoff matrix, so expected utility is \(x^\top M_\theta y\). Let \(y_\theta^H\) be the fixed realization plan for truthful high effort.

For fixed \(x\), the worker’s best-response problem is

\[ \max\{x^\top M_\theta y:D_\theta y=f_\theta,\ y\geq0\}. \]

Its feasible set is nonempty and bounded, since it represents strategies in a finite tree. Linear-programming duality gives the equivalent dual problem

\[ \min\{f_\theta^\top z_\theta: D_\theta^\top z_\theta\geq M_\theta^\top x\}, \]

where \(z_\theta\) is unrestricted in sign.

Therefore truthful high effort is a best response exactly when there exists \(z_\theta\) satisfying

\[ D_\theta^\top z_\theta\geq M_\theta^\top x, \qquad f_\theta^\top z_\theta\leq x^\top M_\theta y_\theta^H. \]

These are linear constraints jointly in \(x\) and \(z_\theta\). They cover all randomized, history-dependent deviations represented by the worker tree.

Requester utility is a linear function \(r^\top x\), computed under the truthful-high-effort population. Adding

\[ r^\top x\geq V \]

and the incentive constraints for every positive-mass type gives one rational linear feasibility problem. Its feasibility is exactly the required decision problem.

Parameter dependence

Let:

  • \(S\) be the largest full hidden-state alphabet in the equilibrium model or any supplied joint representative-worker model;
  • \(O\) be the public observation alphabet size;
  • \(I\) be the largest complete private observation alphabet per round, including assignments and signals;
  • \(C\) bound the number of complete chance transcripts in one round, conditional on worker choices and scale.

The exact algorithm takes

\[ \operatorname{poly}(H,L,m,\tau,T) \]

bit operations. Rational posterior and payoff coefficients have bit length polynomial in these quantities.

Allowing effort and report choices separately for each of the at most \(m\) assigned tasks gives at most \(4^m\) choice pairs per round. Consequently,

\[ H\leq \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,S,T) \left(1+|\mathcal A|\,4^m C\right)^{O(T)}. \]

When a complete round transcript consists of the current and next hidden states, the ground-truth vector, the public observation, and the private outcome, one may use

\[ C\leq S^2\,2^m OI. \]

This yields the explicit, conservative upper bound

\[ \operatorname{poly}(L,m,\tau,T,|\mathcal A|,S,O,I) \left(1+|\mathcal A|S^2OI\,8^m\right)^{O(T)}. \]

If the input supplies a smaller explicit action alphabet, its size can replace \(4^m\). Additional supplied chance symbols can be included directly in \(C\).

In particular, the algorithm is polynomial in the expanded tree and gives a \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(L)}\) worst-case upper bound for the explicit input with unary horizon. It does not give a polynomial-time algorithm in the compact input length.

5. Scope of the conclusion

The reduction shows that support size is not the source of hardness: \(\tau=1\) already suffices. Hardness comes from designing a committed payment policy through hidden payment-dependent dynamics while enforcing incentives over the horizon.

The conditional long-term incentive guarantee in [Hu et al., Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.00206) does not provide an optimization algorithm for this decision problem. The classification proved here is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness; no claim of \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness is needed.
