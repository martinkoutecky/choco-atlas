The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given at most τ advertiser types \(t=(k_t,c_t)\), with rational masses μ_t summing to \(1\), and a finite rational distribution \(D\) over per-capita total supply \(S\), impressions arrive as infinitesimal supply and the stopping level is hidden. An online policy irrevocably advances advertiser mass through progress states \(0,\ldots,k_t\), earning \(c_t\) per unit mass that completes its campaign. The clairvoyant benchmark at supply \(s\) is \(\max\{\sum_t c_t x_t:\sum_t k_t x_t\le s,\ 0\le x_t\le μ_t\}\). For inputs with binary encoding length \(L\), characterize the minimum worst-case ratio \(\sup_{μ,D}\inf_A \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}_{μ}(S)]/\mathbb{E}[W_A(S)]\), and determine whether the cumulative-hazard lower bound from Proposition 6.1 has a tight analogue with a policy computable in time polynomial in τ, the support size of \(D\), campaign demands, and \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) advertiser types \(t=(k_t,c_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), \(k_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and \(c_t\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), with some \(c_t>0\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Write \(m=\sum_t k_t\) and \(M=\sum_t\mu_t k_t\). The known supply distribution \(D\) has \(q\) positive-probability rational atoms \(0<s_1<\cdots<s_q\le M\), with binary-rational probabilities \(p_j\); \(L\) is the total input bit length. No monotone-hazard assumption is imposed.
Supply arrives continuously and must be allocated immediately or discarded; its stopping level \(S\sim D\) is hidden. All types are known, and policies may randomize without incentive constraints.
An infinitesimal mass \(\delta\) advanced from progress \(j-1\) to \(j\) consumes \(\delta\) supply. Formally, cumulative transition masses \(F_{t,j}(s)\) are nondecreasing and absolutely continuous, start at zero, satisfy \(\mu_t\ge F_{t,1}(s)\ge\cdots\ge F_{t,k_t}(s)\ge0\), and obey \(\sum_{t,j}F'_{t,j}(s)\le1\) almost everywhere. Policies may split mass and pause or resume campaigns; transitions are irreversible.
At stopping supply \(s\), welfare is \(W_A(s)=\sum_t c_tF_{t,k_t}(s)\); incomplete mass earns zero. The benchmark is \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}(s)=\max\{\sum_t c_tx_t:\sum_t k_tx_t\le s,\ 0\le x_t\le\mu_t\}\).
Define \(H(D)=\sum_{j=1}^{q}p_j/\sum_{\ell=j}^{q}p_\ell\) and \(R(D)=\sup_{(t,\mu)}\inf_A\mathbb E_D[\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}(S)]/\mathbb E_{D,A}[W_A(S)]\), over societies satisfying the supply bound; a zero denominator gives infinite ratio.

What is the sharp ratio \(R(D)\), and hence \(\sup_D R(D)\)? Does \(H(D)\) remain a tight worst-case approximation bound, with an attaining policy computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,q,L\), or does the sharp bound differ?

For indivisible bidders with campaign-completion valuations, a cumulative-hazard competitive lower bound and matching upper bound are established; polynomial-time implementation is also given with an additional factor of \(2\). Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Campaign demands are positive integers and completion values are nonnegative rationals, following the discrete valuation domain; at least one value is positive to exclude an undefined zero-welfare ratio.
  • The per-capita supply bound is the total campaign demand weighted by mass. Supply atoms are strictly positive; supply beyond total demand can be truncated.
  • Infinitesimal advancement is defined by cumulative transition flows: advancing mass through one progress stage consumes an equal amount of supply, with splitting, pausing, and resuming permitted and no reversal.
  • Policies know the entire society and supply distribution, may randomize, and receive no information about stopping beyond observed supply; no payments or incentive constraints are required.
  • For a finite rational supply distribution, cumulative hazard is the sum of atom probabilities divided by their inclusive survival probabilities.
  • The omitted supremum over campaign types is included. The distribution-specific worst-case ratio is defined first, with the original unrestricted ratio obtained by taking its supremum over distributions.
  • The running-time parameter m is the sum of demands over listed support types; M separately denotes mass-weighted total demand.

Result

For the stated mass-and-progress model, \(R(D)=1\) for every admissible \(D\). Consequently, \(\sup_D R(D)=1\).

A deterministic policy attains the benchmark at every stopping level and is computable in polynomial time. This holds for arbitrary campaign lengths \(k_t\), not just unit-length campaigns.

1. Every feasible policy is bounded by the benchmark

Fix a society and any feasible policy realization. Absolute continuity, the zero initial conditions, and the supply constraint imply

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\sum_{j=1}^{k_t}F_{t,j}(s)=\int_0^s\sum_t\sum_{j=1}^{k_t}F'_{t,j}(u)\,du\le s.\)

Since \(F_{t,j}(s)\ge F_{t,k_t}(s)\) for every \(j\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_t k_tF_{t,k_t}(s)\le s.\)

Also, \(0\le F_{t,k_t}(s)\le\mu_t\). Thus the completed masses \(x_t=F_{t,k_t}(s)\) constitute a feasible solution to the benchmark optimization problem. Therefore

\(\displaystyle W_A(s)\le\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}(s).\)

This holds for every admissible realization of a randomized policy, so it also holds after taking expectations.

2. A nested family of benchmark optima

Relabel the types so that their welfare densities satisfy

\(\displaystyle d_1\ge d_2\ge\cdots\ge d_\tau\ge0,\qquad d_i=\frac{c_i}{k_i}.\)

Define their supply capacities and cumulative capacities by

\(\displaystyle a_i=k_i\mu_i,\qquad B_0=0,\qquad B_r=\sum_{i=1}^r a_i.\)

In particular, \(B_\tau=M\). For \(0\le s\le M\), define

\(\displaystyle y_i(s)=\min\{a_i,\max\{0,s-B_{i-1}\}\},\qquad x_i(s)=\frac{y_i(s)}{k_i}.\)

These allocations fill the types in decreasing density order. They satisfy \(0\le x_i(s)\le\mu_i\) and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}k_ix_i(s)=s.\)

We verify optimality explicitly. Given any benchmark-feasible allocation \(z_i\), put \(u_i=k_iz_i\). Then \(0\le u_i\le a_i\) and \(\sum_i u_i\le s\). Consequently, for each \(r\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^r u_i\le\min\{s,B_r\}.\)

The constructed allocation satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^r y_i(s)=\min\{s,B_r\},\)

because the first \(r\) types occupy consecutive supply intervals whose union is \([0,B_r]\).

Set \(d_{\tau+1}=0\). The finite telescoping identity gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}c_iz_i=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}d_iu_i=\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}(d_r-d_{r+1})\sum_{i=1}^r u_i.\)

All coefficients \(d_r-d_{r+1}\) are nonnegative. Applying the prefix bounds therefore yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}c_iz_i\le\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}(d_r-d_{r+1})\min\{s,B_r\}=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}c_ix_i(s).\)

Hence \(x(s)\) attains \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}(s)\) for every \(s\in[0,M]\). Crucially, each coordinate \(x_i(s)\) is nondecreasing in \(s\).

3. One feasible online policy realizes all these optima

Define the policy by

\(\displaystyle F_{i,j}(s)=x_i(s)\qquad\text{for every }i\text{ and }1\le j\le k_i.\)

These functions are nondecreasing, absolutely continuous, and zero at \(s=0\). The progress constraints hold because

\(\displaystyle \mu_i\ge F_{i,1}(s)=\cdots=F_{i,k_i}(s)\ge0.\)

For \(s\in(B_{i-1},B_i)\), the only changing type is \(i\), and each of its transition masses increases at rate \(1/k_i\). Thus, away from the finitely many breakpoints,

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\sum_{j=1}^{k_t}F'_{t,j}(s)=k_i\frac1{k_i}=1.\)

After \(M\), keep every function constant. The supply constraint therefore holds almost everywhere.

This is an online policy: its action depends only on the current supply level and the known society. Its welfare satisfies

\(\displaystyle W_A(s)=\sum_i c_iF_{i,k_i}(s)=\sum_i c_ix_i(s)=\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}(s)\)

at every possible stopping level.

The equality of the transition masses is permitted by the stated formal constraints. They impose neither a positive transit delay nor a positive minimum campaign mass.

4. Sharp ratio and implementation

Because some type has positive value, its mass is positive, and every supply atom is positive, \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}(s_j)>0\). The attaining policy therefore has positive expected welfare.

Section 1 shows that every policy has ratio at least \(1\), while Section 3 attains ratio \(1\). Thus, for every eligible society,

\(\displaystyle \inf_A\frac{\mathbb E_D[\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}(S)]}{\mathbb E_{D,A}[W_A(S)]}=1.\)

Taking the supremum over societies proves \(R(D)=1\), and then \(\sup_D R(D)=1\).

To compute the policy, sort the rational densities \(c_i/k_i\), compute the rational breakpoints \(B_i\), and output the rates \(1/k_i\) on their respective intervals. Exact comparisons and arithmetic have polynomial bit complexity in \(\tau,L\). An explicit listing of every transition function takes an additional \(O(m)\) entries. Hence implementation is polynomial in \(m,\tau,q,L\); the policy does not require \(D\).

Finally, \(H(D)=1\) when \(q=1\), while \(H(D)>1\) when \(q>1\), because its last summand is \(1\) and every earlier summand is positive. Thus \(H(D)\) is a valid but nonsharp bound whenever \(q>1\).

The cumulative-hazard result in the [cited paper, Section 6 and Appendix A.5](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/adauctions2009_babioff_blumrosen_roth.pdf) concerns indivisible campaign-completion bidders. Here, divisible masses admit the nested fractional optima constructed above.
