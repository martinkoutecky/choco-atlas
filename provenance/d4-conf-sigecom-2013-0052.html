The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\lambda_t\) over finitely many advertiser portfolio types \(t\), value vectors \(v_t\in[0,1]^m\), a horizon \(H\), and unknown but fixed positive CTR vectors \(\theta_t\), construct a finite-support policy and history-based payment rule with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,H\), and the input bit length. The policy may use an individual’s report, the distribution of reports, and observed clicks; each individual deviation has zero mass and must still receive report-dependent treatment. Require stochastic truthfulness for every \(\theta\), total assignment mass at most one per stage, and per-capita welfare strictly above uniform allocation whenever \(M_2^2\ge\sigma M_1^2\) for some \(\sigma>1\) and \(\max_{t,j}v_{t,j}\theta_{t,j}>\epsilon>0\), where \(M_q=\left(\sum_t\lambda_t\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m(v_{t,j}\theta_{t,j})^q\right)^{1/q}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Consider an atomless society of total mass \(1\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\lambda_t\) a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a public portfolio of \(m\ge1\) distinct ads, private rational values \(v_t\in[0,1]^m\), and fixed CTRs \(\theta_t\in(0,1]^m\), unknown to both advertisers and mechanism. Portfolios belonging to different advertisers are disjoint. The horizon \(H\ge2\) and rational parameters \(\sigma>1\), \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\) are given.
Advertisers report their value vectors once. At each stage a policy chooses a distribution with finite support over feasible assignment plans: each individual receives at most one impression, and total assigned mass is at most \(1\); skips are allowed.
For each type, ad, and stage there is an independent Bernoulli click indicator with parameter \(\theta_{t,j}\), shared by individuals of that true type. An individual observes it only when assigned that ad; the mechanism observes it only through actual assignments. Reports do not change an individual's underlying CTR or click indicators. Unassigned clicks and counterfactual executions are unavailable.
The policy and final payment rule may depend on public portfolios, individual reports and observed histories, and the distribution of reports and observed histories. They must specify treatment for every report in \([0,1]^m\), including reports outside the population support.
Stochastic truthfulness means that, for every CTR vector and every fixed distribution of others' reports, truthful reporting maximizes each individual's expected total click value minus payment, over mechanism randomness and clicks. An individual deviation has zero mass, leaves aggregate distributions unchanged, and changes that individual's treatment according to the reported vector. Payments are signed monetary amounts with finite expectations and depend only on available observations.
At truthful reports, let \(W\) be expected total click value per capita and define \(M_q=\left(\sum_t\lambda_t\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m(v_{t,j}\theta_{t,j})^q\right)^{1/q}\) for \(q\in\{1,2\}\). Require \(W>HM_1\), the welfare of uniform allocation, whenever \(M_2^2\ge\sigma M_1^2\) and \(\max_{t,j}v_{t,j}\theta_{t,j}>\epsilon\).
Is constructing a stochastically truthful mechanism with these guarantees possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,L\), or is this construction problem NP-hard? Here \(L\) is the binary input length; construction must give a finite description whose stage and payment evaluations also take polynomial time on rational reports.
For finitely many advertisers, at least two advertisers, bounded nonnegative values, positive CTRs, and a nonempty exploitation phase, a stochastically truthful mechanism strictly improves on uniform allocation under the corresponding skewness and positive-value conditions. [Discrete result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.4138v2).
The source checks identified no result settling this precise atomless construction problem with finite-support policies.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one and interpreted uniform allocation as giving every individual an independently uniform choice among that individual's portfolio ads at each stage.
  • Required H at least two, preserving the discrete guarantee's need for both exploration and exploitation; each portfolio is nonempty.
  • Treated portfolios as public and fixed, with distinct advertiser-owned ads; reports change values only, not portfolio ownership or CTRs.
  • Made sigma and epsilon supplied rational parameters, so the mechanism must work uniformly over all unknown CTR vectors satisfying their promise.
  • Specified a finite observation model using independent type-ad-stage Bernoulli indicators shared within each true type. The graded question does not specify dependence across individuals; this choice avoids implicitly granting exact CTR observations from continuum click fractions.
  • Interpreted finite support stagewise over feasible assignment plans, allowing a succinct contingent policy rather than requiring enumeration of every possible history.
  • Required report-dependent treatment on the full value domain, including zero-mass deviations to reports outside the supplied support, and dominant-strategy truthfulness holding aggregate reports fixed.
  • Allowed signed, integrable payments, as the discrete randomized payment construction permits realized transfers to advertisers.
  • Defined polynomial running time for both mechanism construction and evaluation, with rational reported values included in the relevant binary input length.

Conclusion. The stated guarantees are impossible, even without truthfulness or computational restrictions. The obstruction persists for arbitrary portfolio size and strictly positive values.

An admissible counterexample. Fix any \(m\ge 2\), \(H\ge 2\), \(\sigma>1\), and \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\). Choose a positive dyadic rational \(p\) satisfying \(p\le 1/(4\sigma)\).

Consider two types, belonging to different advertisers with disjoint portfolios:

  • Type \(A\) has mass \(\lambda_A=p\), with \(v_{A,j}=1\) and \(\theta_{A,j}=1\) for every \(j\).
  • Type \(B\) has mass \(\lambda_B=1-p\), with \(v_{B,j}=p\) and \(\theta_{B,j}=1\) for every \(j\).

Both masses are positive binary rationals. All values and CTRs satisfy the prescribed bounds. Each portfolio contains \(m\) distinct ads.

This counterexample has polynomial encoding length: writing \(\sigma=a/b\) with positive integers \(a,b\), one can take \(p=2^{-k}\), where \(k\) is the binary length of \(a\), plus two.

Verification of the trigger conditions. Since every ad within a given type has the same value per impression,

\(M_1=p+(1-p)p=p(2-p)\),

and

\(M_2^2=p+(1-p)p^2\ge p\).

Consequently,

\(\sigma M_1^2=\sigma p^2(2-p)^2\le 4\sigma p^2\le p\le M_2^2\).

Also, \(\max_{t,j}v_{t,j}\theta_{t,j}=1>\epsilon\). Thus the required strict welfare improvement applies to this society.

A universal welfare upper bound. Here all click indicators equal one almost surely. Under the stated per-individual impression cap, each type-\(A\) individual can obtain at most value \(1\) in a stage, and each type-\(B\) individual can obtain at most value \(p\).

More explicitly, let \(a_{A,h}\) and \(a_{B,h}\) denote the masses receiving impressions at stage \(h\) in any realized execution. Feasibility gives \(a_{A,h}\le p\) and \(a_{B,h}\le 1-p\). Therefore realized stage welfare is at most

\(a_{A,h}+p\,a_{B,h}\le p+p(1-p)=M_1\).

Summing over stages and taking expectations yields \(W\le HM_1\). Uniform allocation attains this bound because it gives every individual an impression, and every ad in that individual’s portfolio has the same value.

The required inequality \(W>HM_1\) is therefore impossible. Payments, randomization, observed histories, and knowledge of the CTRs cannot change this welfare bound.

Why global skewness does not suffice. This is not merely the special case \(m=1\): the construction works for every \(m\ge 2\), with two advertisers and strictly positive values. Its skewness arises between types, while uniform allocation already maximizes welfare within each type.

To see the distinction algebraically, write \(x_{t,j}=v_{t,j}\theta_{t,j}\) and \(\mu_t=m^{-1}\sum_j x_{t,j}\). Expanding the squares gives

\(M_2^2-M_1^2=\sum_t\lambda_t m^{-1}\sum_j(x_{t,j}-\mu_t)^2+\sum_t\lambda_t(\mu_t-M_1)^2\).

In this example, the first term is zero; the second supplies all the skewness. The per-individual cap prevents that between-type variation from producing additional welfare.

The cited discrete rule instead selects one ad among all advertisers’ ads in each round, so its resource constraint differs from the one here. [Discrete source, Section 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.4138v2).

Accordingly, the construction problem as stated is infeasible; polynomial-time construction versus NP-hardness is not the applicable dichotomy.
