The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite good categories \(G\), complete additive-valuation types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and rational values \(v_t(g)\), assign mass \(x_{t,b}\ge 0\) of each type \(t\) to integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{N}^{G}\), using finitely many bundles. Require \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\), and \(v_t(b)\ge v_t(b')\) whenever \(x_{t,b}>0\) and \(x_{s,b'}>0\). Decide whether such an envy-free allocation exists and, if it does, compute one maximizing \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}v_t(b)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a finite set of good categories, with \(m=|G|\). A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a complete additive valuation specified by binary rationals \(v_t(g)\in[0,1]\). Supplies \(q_g\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) are per capita, and \(L\) is the total input bit length. Inputs are arbitrary, without a random-generation promise.
Bundles are \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{G}\), including the empty bundle; multiple copies of a category are allowed. An allocation consists of nonnegative masses \(x_{t,b}\) with finitely many nonzero entries, satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\) for every category.
Envy-freeness requires \(v_t(b)\ge v_t(b')\) whenever \(x_{t,b}>0\) and \(x_{s,b'}>0\), including \(s=t\). Preferences are weak, ties are allowed, and every unit of supply must be allocated. Utilitarian welfare is \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}v_t(b)\).

Is the problem of deciding feasibility and, when feasible, computing an envy-free allocation of maximum utilitarian welfare solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete agents with independent uniform \([0,1]\) item values, assigning each item to its highest valuer is polynomial-time and welfare-maximizing, and is envy-free with probability tending to \(1\) when \(M\ge Cn\ln M\) for a sufficiently large constant \(C\), where \(M\) counts individual items and \(n\) counts agents. [Discrete random-instance guarantee](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/8884/8743).
For arbitrary discrete additive valuations, complete envy-free feasibility is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [Discrete complexity result](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~lang/papers/bouveret-lang08.pdf). No exact complexity classification for the stated support-encoded mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the unspecified rational values to [0,1], matching the discrete valuation domain; zero values remain allowed.
  • Specified nonnegative supplies, permitting zero supply because no positive-supply restriction is justified.
  • Interpreted integral bundles as nonnegative integer vectors, allowing empty bundles and repeated copies of a category, as required by the graded category model.
  • Kept weak envy-freeness, including comparisons between occupied bundles assigned to the same type; valuation ties require no tie-breaking rule.
  • Treated the supplied finite-support instance as arbitrary deterministic input: the anchor's non-atomic random-generation assumptions describe its discrete guarantee, not a promise on this continuous input.
  • Defined L as total binary input length and distinguished the number of categories from the discrete number of individual items.

Result

Feasibility is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even when \(\tau=1\), the sole type has mass \(1\), every supply is \(1/2\), and every valuation is a strictly positive dyadic rational.

Consequently, deciding feasibility and then computing a welfare-maximizing envy-free allocation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The reduction below explicitly allows repeated categories and fractional allocation masses.

1. What within-type envy implies

For any envy-free allocation, all bundles assigned positive mass to a fixed type \(t\) have the same utility. Indeed, if \(x_{t,b}>0\) and \(x_{t,b'}>0\), the two directed envy comparisons give
\(v_t(b)\ge v_t(b')\) and \(v_t(b')\ge v_t(b)\).

Write this common utility as \(u_t\).

For one type of mass \(1\), exact supply allocation therefore gives

\(u=\sum_b x_bv(b)=\sum_g v(g)\sum_bx_bb_g=\sum_gv(g)q_g=v(q).\)

Thus every occupied bundle must individually have utility exactly \(v(q)\). Fractional mixing cannot relax this equality.

2. A polynomial reduction from PARTITION

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem PARTITION: given positive integers \(s_1,\ldots,s_n\), encoded in binary, decide whether some subset has sum equal to half their total. See, for example, [Mertens, *Phase Transition in the Number Partitioning Problem*](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9807077).

Let

\(S=\sum_{i=1}^n s_i,\qquad B=2nS+n+2,\qquad H=B^{n+1}.\)

These choices ensure

\(S<B,\qquad 2nS<B,\qquad n+1<B.\)

Create two categories \(g_i^0,g_i^1\) for each index \(i\), and define integer coefficients

\(a_i^0=H+B^i,\qquad a_i^1=H+B^i+2s_i.\)

Let \(D\) be the least power of two strictly greater than every coefficient \(a_i^\epsilon\). The allocation instance has one type of mass \(1\), with valuations and supplies

\(v(g_i^\epsilon)=a_i^\epsilon/D,\qquad q_{g_i^\epsilon}=1/2.\)

All valuations are strictly between \(0\) and \(1\) and are dyadic rationals.

Define

\(A=nH+\sum_{i=1}^n B^i+S.\)

The utility fixed by the supply vector is

\(v(q)=\frac{1}{2D}\sum_{i=1}^n(a_i^0+a_i^1)=A/D.\)

Hence every occupied bundle in an envy-free allocation must satisfy

\(\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(a_i^0b_i^0+a_i^1b_i^1\bigr)=A.\) \(\qquad (1)\)

Polynomial encoding size

The binary length of \(B\) is \(O(\log n+\log S)\). Each coefficient, and the denominator \(D\), has binary length \(O(n\log B)\). There are \(2n\) categories.

Since the binary length of \(S\) is polynomial in the PARTITION input length, the entire construction has polynomial encoding length and can be computed in polynomial time.

3. Repeated copies cannot produce spurious solutions

We prove that every nonnegative integer bundle satisfying equation \((1)\) selects exactly one category from each pair and encodes a partition.

Let

\(r=\sum_{i=1}^n(b_i^0+b_i^1),\qquad c_i=b_i^0+b_i^1,\qquad w=2\sum_{i=1}^n s_i b_i^1.\)

Equation \((1)\) becomes

\(rH+\sum_{i=1}^n c_iB^i+w=nH+\sum_{i=1}^nB^i+S.\) \(\qquad (2)\)

First, put \(R=\sum_{i=1}^nB^i+S\). Its base-\(B\) digits are \(S\) in position zero and \(1\) in positions \(1,\ldots,n\). Since \(S<B\),

\(0\le R<B^{n+1}=H.\)

Every coefficient \(a_i^\epsilon\) is at least \(H\). Therefore equation \((1)\) implies

\(rH\le A=nH+R<(n+1)H,\)

so \(r\le n\).

Consequently,

\(0\le w\le 2nS<B\)

and

\(0\le\sum_{i=1}^n c_iB^i+w\le nB^n+w<nB^n+B\le(n+1)B^n<H.\)

Both lower-order terms in equation \((2)\) therefore lie between zero and \(H-1\). Uniqueness of quotient and remainder upon division by \(H\) gives

\(r=n,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^n c_iB^i+w=\sum_{i=1}^nB^i+S.\)

Now \(0\le c_i\le n<B\) and \(0\le w<B\), so the left-hand side is already a valid base-\(B\) expansion. Uniqueness of base-\(B\) representation yields

\(c_i=1\text{ for every }i,\qquad w=S.\)

Thus each pair contributes exactly one copy in total. In particular,

\(b_i^1\in\{0,1\},\qquad b_i^0=1-b_i^1,\qquad 2\sum_{i=1}^n s_i b_i^1=S.\)

The selected indices form an equal-sum partition. This proves the required exclusion of repeated-copy solutions.

4. Correctness of the reduction

Feasibility implies a partition

Suppose the constructed instance has an envy-free allocation. Its masses sum to \(1\), so at least one bundle has positive mass.

By Section 1, this bundle satisfies equation \((1)\). Section 3 then extracts an equal-sum partition of the original integers.

A partition implies feasibility

Conversely, suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies

\(2\sum_{i\in I}s_i=S.\)

Define a bundle \(b\) by choosing \(g_i^1\) when \(i\in I\) and \(g_i^0\) otherwise. Define its complementary bundle \(\bar b\) by choosing the other category in every pair.

Their integer coefficient sums are

\(\sum_i(a_i^0b_i^0+a_i^1b_i^1)=nH+\sum_iB^i+2\sum_{i\in I}s_i=A\)

and

\(\sum_i(a_i^0\bar b_i^0+a_i^1\bar b_i^1)=nH+\sum_iB^i+2\sum_{i\notin I}s_i=A.\)

Assign mass \(1/2\) to each bundle.

Every category occurs in exactly one of these bundles, so its allocated supply is exactly \(1/2\). Both bundles have utility \(A/D\), so the allocation is envy-free.

We have proved the equivalence

\(\text{PARTITION instance is positive}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{constructed allocation instance is feasible}.\)

This establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness with \(\tau=1\).

5. General feasibility belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\)

We now address the possibility that feasible allocations initially use very large bundles or irrational masses.

Polynomial bounds on bundle encoding lengths

Consider any envy-free allocation and its common type utilities \(u_t\). Additivity and exact supplies give

\(v_t(q)=\sum_{s,b}x_{s,b}v_t(b)\ge\sum_bx_{t,b}v_t(b)=\mu_tu_t,\)

where the inequality uses nonnegative valuations and masses. Since \(\mu_t>0\),

\(u_t\le v_t(q)/\mu_t.\)

Every occupied bundle \(b\), regardless of its recipient, also satisfies \(v_t(b)\le u_t\) by envy-freeness.

For any category \(g\) valued positively by some type, choose such a type \(t(g)\). Nonnegativity of the other valuation terms implies

\(b_gv_{t(g)}(g)\le v_{t(g)}(b)\le u_{t(g)}\le v_{t(g)}(q)/\mu_{t(g)}.\)

Thus every occupied bundle satisfies

\(b_g\le K_g:=\left\lfloor\frac{v_{t(g)}(q)}{\mu_{t(g)}v_{t(g)}(g)}\right\rfloor.\)

The integer \(K_g\) has binary length polynomial in the input length: it is obtained through sums, products, and quotients of the input rationals.

For a category valued zero by every type, its count affects neither envy nor welfare. We may replace its counts by a mixture of \(\lfloor q_g\rfloor\) and \(\lceil q_g\rceil\) with mean \(q_g\), independently across such categories and across occupied bundles. This produces a finite allocation preserving all type masses, supplies, utilities, and envy comparisons.

Hence these categories can be bounded by \(K_g=\lceil q_g\rceil\), also of polynomial binary length.

Small rational support

After those replacements, let \(J\) be the finite support of the allocation. Introduce one column for each occupied type-bundle pair \((t,b)\), consisting of:

  • the indicator vector of type \(t\);
  • the bundle vector \(b\).

The mass and supply equations form a linear system with \(d=\tau+m\) rows and nonnegative variables.

If its positive-support columns are linearly dependent, choose a nonzero dependence vector \(z\). Summing the type rows shows \(\sum_jz_j=0\), so \(z\) has both positive and negative entries. Moving the mass vector along \(z\) until one entry first becomes zero preserves the equations and nonnegativity and decreases the support.

Repeating gives a feasible solution with linearly independent columns, hence at most \(d\) occupied pairs. Deleting occupied pairs preserves envy-freeness.

Choose as many independent rows as remaining columns. The resulting square system has an invertible integer matrix and a rational right-hand side. Cramer’s rule shows that its unique masses are rational. After clearing the right-hand-side denominators, the determinant expansion bounds their numerator and denominator lengths polynomially in \(d\), the bundle encoding lengths, and the input length.

Therefore every feasible instance has a certificate with at most \(m+\tau\) occupied pairs, polynomial-length bundle coordinates, and polynomial-length rational masses. All mass equations, supply equations, and directed envy comparisons can be checked in polynomial time.

General feasibility is consequently in \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness proof.

6. Welfare maximization and attainment

In the one-type reduction, every complete allocation has welfare

\(\sum_bx_bv(b)=v(q)=A/D.\)

Thus every feasible envy-free allocation in the reduction is already welfare-maximizing. The feasibility requirement alone establishes hardness of the requested combined task.

For completeness, a welfare maximum does exist whenever a general instance is feasible. Section 5 gives a finite set of bounded bundles to which every allocation can be converted without changing welfare. For each subset of type-bundle pairs satisfying all directed envy comparisons, the mass and supply constraints define a closed, bounded polytope. There are finitely many such subsets, and their union contains exactly the envy-free allocations using the bounded bundles. A linear welfare function attains its maximum on this nonempty compact union.

Moreover, the support reduction in Section 5 preserves welfare: all retained bundles of a type have the same utility \(u_t\), and that type’s total mass remains \(\mu_t\). A maximum therefore admits a polynomial-size rational representation as well.

The conclusion concerns the arbitrary deterministic inputs in the question. It does not depend on the discrete random-instance guarantee.
