The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a target \(p\), a rational distribution \(\mu\) over supported complete rankings of \(C\), and a rational deletion budget \(\rho\), can one choose deleted masses \(x_t\) with \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_t\le\rho\) such that, for every \(c\ne p\), \(\sum_t(\mu_t-x_t)\bigl((m-\operatorname{pos}_t(p))-(m-\operatorname{pos}_t(c))\bigr)>0\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq2\) candidates and let \(p\in C\). Types are strict complete rankings of \(C\); \(\operatorname{pos}_t(c)\) is the position of \(c\) in type \(t\), starting at \(1\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The deletion budget is a binary rational \(\rho\geq0\). Assume \(p\) is initially not the unique Borda winner.
Fractional Borda vote deletion asks whether real masses \(x_t\) exist with \(0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_t\leq\rho\), such that \(\sum_t(\mu_t-x_t)(\operatorname{pos}_t(c)-\operatorname{pos}_t(p))>0\) for every \(c\in C\setminus\{p\}\). Scores use the remaining masses without renormalization; ties do not constitute success.

Is fractional Borda vote deletion solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite unweighted electorates, constructive Borda control by deleting votes is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and \(\mathrm{W}[2]\)-hard parameterized by the number \(\ell\) of deletions. [Discrete hardness](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05062v1), [classical dichotomy](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.05264).
No result settling exactly the fractional problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the distribution normalization explicit: positive supported masses sum to one.
  • Specified a nonnegative rational deletion budget, retaining zero as allowed in the discrete definition.
  • Carried over the assumption that the target is initially not the unique winner; this also excludes the single-candidate case.
  • Specified real divisible deletion masses and scores computed from remaining masses without renormalization.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Fractional Borda vote deletion is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Strict victory can be decided by maximizing a common margin in a rational linear program and comparing its exact optimum with zero.

  • Linear-program formulation.

For each supported type \(t\) and opponent \(c\ne p\), define
\(a_{tc}=\operatorname{pos}_t(c)-\operatorname{pos}_t(p)\).
These are integers satisfying \(|a_{tc}|\le m-1\). Define the initial margin
\(s_c=\sum_t\mu_ta_{tc}\).

Introduce the deletion variables \(x_t\) and a common-margin variable \(z\). Consider the linear program
\( \begin{array}{ll} \text{maximize} & z\\ \text{subject to} & 0\le x_t\le\mu_t \quad\text{for every supported type }t,\\ & \sum_t x_t\le\rho,\\ & s_c-\sum_t a_{tc}x_t\ge z \quad\text{for every }c\in C\setminus\{p\},\\ & -(m-1)\le z\le m-1. \end{array} \)

For any permitted deletion vector, its margin against each opponent satisfies
\( \left|\sum_t(\mu_t-x_t)a_{tc}\right| \le (m-1)\sum_t(\mu_t-x_t) \le m-1. \)
Thus the bounds on \(z\) accommodate every possible minimum margin.

The program is feasible: take \(x_t=0\) for every \(t\) and \(z=-(m-1)\). Its feasible region is closed and bounded, so its continuous objective attains an optimum, denoted \(z^\star\).

  • Equivalence with strict victory.

Suppose a successful deletion vector \(x\) exists. There are finitely many opponents, and every remaining margin is strictly positive. Consequently,
\( \delta=\min_{c\ne p}\sum_t(\mu_t-x_t)a_{tc}>0. \)
The margin bound above gives \(\delta\le m-1\), so \((x,\delta)\) is feasible for the linear program. Hence \(z^\star\ge\delta>0\).

Conversely, suppose \(z^\star>0\). At an optimal solution \((x^\star,z^\star)\), every opponent satisfies
\( \sum_t(\mu_t-x_t^\star)a_{tc} \ge z^\star>0. \)
The other constraints ensure that these deletions respect all type masses and the budget. Thus \(x^\star\) is successful.

Therefore, the instance is a yes-instance if and only if \(z^\star>0\). In particular, an optimum of zero is correctly rejected.

  • Polynomial bit complexity.

The program has \(\tau+1\) variables and \(2\tau+m+2\) inequalities. Its matrix coefficients are integers of absolute value at most \(m-1\), and its right-hand sides are rational.

To verify their encoding size, write each input mass as \(\mu_t=u_t/v_t\), with \(v_t>0\), and let \(Q=\prod_t v_t\). The binary length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the denominator lengths, up to an additive constant, and hence is \(O(L)\). Each initial margin can be written as
\( s_c=\frac{\sum_t u_t(Q/v_t)a_{tc}}{Q}. \)
Since \(|s_c|\le m-1\), this representation has numerator and denominator lengths \(O(L+\log m)\). These quantities can be constructed using polynomially many operations on integers of polynomial bit length. The entire linear program therefore has encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The rational linear-programming theorem provides polynomial-time feasibility and exact optimization in the binary encoding model; for a feasible bounded program, an exact rational optimal solution can be obtained. This is the standard polynomial-time result originating with [Khachiyan](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319). All its hypotheses hold here. Comparing the resulting rational \(z^\star\) with zero is an exact polynomial-time operation, so no numerical tolerance is needed.

The initial non-unique-winner assumption is unnecessary for this algorithm. Discrete deletion hardness does not contradict the result: discrete electorates impose indivisibility constraints on deleted votes, whereas the present variables range over real intervals.
