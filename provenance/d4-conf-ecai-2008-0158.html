The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(x_r\), weights \(w_r\), payoff densities \(a_r\), a quota \(q\), and a block assignment for finitely many cells \(r\), with the candidate outcome satisfying each block’s feasibility and payoff condition, decide whether every measurable coalition of weight at least \(q\) receives payoff at least \(1\). Equivalently, decide whether the minimum of \(\sum_r a_r z_r\), subject to \(\sum_r w_r z_r \ge q\) and \(0 \le z_r \le x_r\), is at least \(1\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(x_r\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; total mass is arbitrary and finite.
Type \(r\) represents an atomless cell of mass \(x_r\), with constant rational weight density \(w_r\ge0\), payoff density \(a_r\ge0\), and block label \(b_r\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\). Every block has positive mass. The rational quota satisfies \(0<q\le\sum_r w_rx_r\).
A measurable coalition may select any real mass \(z_r\in[0,x_r]\) from each cell, across any blocks. Its weight and assigned payoff are \(W(z)=\sum_r w_rz_r\) and \(A(z)=\sum_r a_rz_r\); its value is \(1\) when \(W(z)\ge q\), and \(0\) otherwise.
The supplied outcome is promised to be an imputation for its block partition: for every \(j\), \(\sum_{r:b_r=j}a_rx_r=\mathbf{1}\{\sum_{r:b_r=j}w_rx_r\ge q\}\).
Continuous CS-core membership asks whether every measurable winning coalition receives assigned payoff at least \(1\), equivalently whether \(\min\{\sum_r a_rz_r:\sum_r w_rz_r\ge q,\ 0\le z_r\le x_r\}\ge1\).

Is continuous CS-core membership in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the total binary input length \(L\).

For finite indivisible agents, CS-core membership is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete and admits a pseudopolynomial algorithm in the number of agents, total integer weight, and payoff bit length. [Verified source](https://comsoc-community.org/assets/proceedings/comsoc-2008/Chalkiadakis.pdf).
No result settling exactly this continuous membership problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted each support entry as an atomless cell whose type includes weight density, payoff density, and block label; coalition selections may split cells and cross blocks.
  • Allowed arbitrary finite total mass rather than imposing probability normalization, consistent with the unnormalized discrete society and graded mass formulation.
  • Required nonnegative rational weight and payoff densities; zero weights are allowed, as they occur in the discrete construction.
  • Required a positive quota no greater than total societal weight, preserving a winning grand coalition while excluding the degenerate zero-quota case.
  • Specified an exhaustive partition into positive-mass blocks and exact payoff efficiency: each winning block distributes one unit and each losing block distributes zero.
  • Defined m as the number of supplied blocks and L as the total binary input length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Continuous CS-core membership is in \(\mathrm{P}\). An exact algorithm uses \(\mathrm{O}(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational arithmetic operations and comparisons. Including an optional check of the imputation promise, a conservative bit-operation bound is

\(\mathrm{O}\bigl((m+\tau\log(\tau+1))L^2\bigr)\).

The algorithm is a fractional-knapsack computation. We prove its correctness and the bit bound below.

1. Reduction to weight units

If \(w_r=0\), selecting mass from cell \(r\) contributes no weight and contributes nonnegative payoff. Setting \(z_r=0\) therefore preserves feasibility and cannot increase payoff. Such cells can be omitted from the minimization.

Let \(n\le\tau\) be the number of remaining cells. For each remaining cell, define its weight capacity and payoff per unit of weight by

\(h_r=w_rx_r>0,\qquad \rho_r=a_r/w_r\ge0.\)

Writing \(u_r=w_rz_r\), the minimum winning-coalition payoff becomes

\(C^*=\min\left\{\sum_{r=1}^{n}\rho_ru_r:\ \sum_{r=1}^{n}u_r\ge q,\quad 0\le u_r\le h_r\right\}.\)

This change of variables is bijective on each retained cell because \(w_r>0\). The hypothesis \(q\le\sum_r w_rx_r\) guarantees feasibility and \(n\ge1\).

Relabel the retained cells so that

\(\rho_1\le\rho_2\le\cdots\le\rho_n,\)

breaking ties arbitrarily. Put \(H_0=0\) and \(H_i=\sum_{r=1}^{i}h_r\). Let \(k\) be the smallest index with \(H_k\ge q\). Then

\(0<q-H_{k-1}\le h_k.\)

2. Exact optimal value

Define a candidate coalition in weight units by

\(u_r^*=h_r\) for \(r<k\), \(u_k^*=q-H_{k-1}\), and \(u_r^*=0\) for \(r>k\).

It respects every capacity and has total weight exactly \(q\). Its payoff is

\(C_{\mathrm{greedy}}=\sum_{r<k}\rho_rh_r+\rho_k(q-H_{k-1}).\)

To prove optimality, take any feasible \(u\). We have

\(\sum_{r=1}^{n}\rho_ru_r=\rho_k\sum_{r=1}^{n}u_r+\sum_{r<k}(\rho_r-\rho_k)u_r+\sum_{r>k}(\rho_r-\rho_k)u_r.\)

Each term admits the following bound:

  • Since \(\rho_k\ge0\) and \(\sum_r u_r\ge q\), the first term is at least \(\rho_kq\).
  • For \(r<k\), the coefficient \(\rho_r-\rho_k\) is nonpositive. Thus \(u_r\le h_r\) implies \((\rho_r-\rho_k)u_r\ge(\rho_r-\rho_k)h_r\).
  • For \(r>k\), both the coefficient and \(u_r\) are nonnegative, so the corresponding term is nonnegative.

Consequently,

\(\sum_{r=1}^{n}\rho_ru_r\ge\rho_kq+\sum_{r<k}(\rho_r-\rho_k)h_r=\sum_{r<k}\rho_rh_r+\rho_k(q-H_{k-1}).\)

The candidate attains this bound. Therefore the exact minimum is

\(C^*=\sum_{r<k}a_rx_r+\frac{a_k}{w_k}\left(q-\sum_{r<k}w_rx_r\right).\)

Zero payoff densities and tied ratios cause no difficulty: the proof uses non-strict inequalities throughout.

Returning to cell masses gives \(z_r^*=x_r\) for \(r<k\), \(z_k^*=(q-H_{k-1})/w_k\), and zero for all other cells. These masses are permitted by the atomless selection hypothesis.

Hence membership holds if and only if \(C^*\ge1\). If \(C^*<1\), the constructed coalition is an explicit witness of nonmembership.

3. Algorithm and running time

The algorithm is:

  • Discard cells with zero weight.
  • Sort the remaining cells by \(\rho_r=a_r/w_r\).
  • Accumulate capacities until reaching \(q\).
  • Evaluate the displayed formula for \(C^*\) exactly and compare it with \(1\).

Sorting costs \(\mathrm{O}(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) comparisons; the remaining computation uses \(\mathrm{O}(\tau)\) rational operations. Block labels do not enter this minimization because coalitions may select mass across arbitrary blocks.

For the bit bound, represent every input rational by a binary numerator and positive denominator. Their combined encoding length is at most \(L\).

Each capacity \(w_rx_r\), full-cell payoff \(a_rx_r\), and ratio \(a_r/w_r\) has bit length bounded by a constant times the encoding lengths of its operands. A prefix sum can be represented over the product of its summands’ denominators. Since each input denominator appears only a bounded number of times in these products, their bit lengths are \(\mathrm{O}(L)\). The numerators also have \(\mathrm{O}(L)\) bits: each summand has magnitude at most \(2^{\mathrm{O}(L)}\), and \(\log\tau=\mathrm{O}(L)\).

The final formula and ratio comparisons likewise involve only \(\mathrm{O}(L)\)-bit integers. Using elementary integer arithmetic, each rational operation or comparison therefore costs at most \(\mathrm{O}(L^2)\) bit operations. The promised-input membership algorithm runs in

\(\mathrm{O}\bigl(L+\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2\bigr)\).

If desired, the imputation promise can also be checked by accumulating, for every block \(j\), its mass, weight, and payoff, then checking the stated equality. This adds \(\mathrm{O}(m+\tau)\) rational operations, giving the bound claimed above. Since every block contains a support cell, \(m\le\tau\le L\), so this is polynomial in \(L\). No bound on the numerical magnitudes of the masses, weights, or quota is required.

4. Why indivisibility changes the problem

The cited finite-agent result establishes \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-completeness for membership with indivisible agents. [Chalkiadakis, Elkind, and Jennings](https://comsoc-community.org/assets/proceedings/comsoc-2008/Chalkiadakis.pdf).

Here, the partially selected cell is allowed. That permits the exact quota-filling construction proved optimal above; with indivisible agents, that construction need not be feasible.
