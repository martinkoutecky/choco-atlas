Result

The exact maximizing vector is computable in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\). It is unique and has rational coordinates of polynomial encoding length.

The cited paper gives the clipped affine rule for a single municipality in Section 3.7. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08693v1). The argument below proves the exact bit-complexity result for the stated mass allocation problem.

1. Incorporating admissibility

For every supported type \(t\), define

\[ a_t=\omega_b\theta_t-\omega_{T,t}, \qquad u_t= \begin{cases} \bar b_t,&\theta_t\ge T_t,\\ 0,&\theta_t<T_t. \end{cases} \]

Thus equality at the admissibility threshold is included. The problem becomes maximizing

\[ U(b)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\left(a_tb_t-\frac c2b_t^2\right) \]

subject to \(0\le b_t\le u_t\) and \(G(b)\le R\), where

\[ G(b)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_tb_t. \]

The feasible set is nonempty because it contains the zero vector. It is compact because every cap is finite. Consequently, continuity of \(U\) guarantees existence of a maximizer.

Moreover, \(U\) is strictly concave: for distinct vectors \(x,y\) and \(0<\alpha<1\),

\[ U(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y) -\alpha U(x)-(1-\alpha)U(y) = \frac c2\alpha(1-\alpha) \sum_{t\in S}\mu_t(x_t-y_t)^2>0. \]

The strict inequality follows from \(c>0\), every \(\mu_t>0\), and \(x\ne y\). Since the feasible set is convex, its maximizer is unique.

2. A sufficient optimality certificate

For a nonnegative rational or real number \(\lambda\), define

\[ b_t(\lambda) = \min\left\{u_t,\, \max\left\{0,\frac{a_t-\lambda}{c}\right\}\right\}, \qquad F(\lambda)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_tb_t(\lambda). \]

For each type, \(b_t(\lambda)\) uniquely maximizes

\[ \mu_t\left((a_t-\lambda)z-\frac c2z^2\right) \]

over \(0\le z\le u_t\). Indeed, its derivative is \(\mu_t(a_t-\lambda-cz)\), its second derivative is strictly negative, and clipping its unrestricted maximizer gives the displayed formula.

Therefore \(b(\lambda)\) maximizes \(U(b)-\lambda G(b)\) over the box constraints.

Certificate. If

\[ F(\lambda)\le R, \qquad \lambda(R-F(\lambda))=0, \]

then \(b(\lambda)\) is the unique solution of the original problem.

To prove this, let \(x\) be any feasible vector. Box optimality gives

\[ U(x)-\lambda G(x) \le U(b(\lambda))-\lambda F(\lambda). \]

Using \(G(x)\le R\), \(\lambda\ge0\), and the certificate conditions,

\[ U(x) \le U(b(\lambda))+\lambda(G(x)-F(\lambda)) \le U(b(\lambda))+\lambda(R-F(\lambda)) =U(b(\lambda)). \]

This proves optimality; uniqueness follows from Section 1. No constraint qualification or necessity theorem for multipliers is needed.

3. Computing a certificate exactly

If \(R=0\), positivity of all masses and nonnegativity of all coordinates force \(b_t=0\) for every type. Return that vector.

Suppose henceforth that \(R>0\).

If \(F(0)\le R\), return \(b(0)\). The certificate holds with \(\lambda=0\).

It remains to handle \(F(0)>R>0\). Define

\[ M=\max\left(\{0\}\cup\{a_t:t\in S\}\right). \]

Here \(M>0\), since otherwise \(F(0)=0\). Also \(F(M)=0\).

Each \(b_t(\lambda)\) is continuous and nonincreasing. If \(u_t>0\), its only possible changes of affine formula occur at

\[ a_t-cu_t \quad\text{and}\quad a_t. \]

Indeed, it equals its cap when \(\lambda\le a_t-cu_t\), equals \((a_t-\lambda)/c\) between the two breakpoints, and equals zero when \(\lambda\ge a_t\). Types with \(u_t=0\) contribute zero everywhere.

Form a list consisting of \(0\), \(M\), and all these breakpoints that lie strictly between \(0\) and \(M\). Remove duplicates and sort:

\[ 0=q_0<q_1<\cdots<q_k=M. \]

There are at most \(2\tau+2\) listed values. The function \(F\) is continuous, nonincreasing, and affine on every closed interval \([q_j,q_{j+1}]\): each summand is affine on the open interval, and continuity extends that formula to its endpoints.

Evaluate \(F(q_j)\) exactly at every listed value.

If \(F(q_j)=R\), return \(b(q_j)\). Otherwise, because \(F(q_0)>R>F(q_k)\), some consecutive pair \(x=q_j\), \(y=q_{j+1}\) satisfies

\[ F(x)>R>F(y). \]

Define

\[ \lambda = x+ \frac{F(x)-R}{F(x)-F(y)}(y-x). \]

The denominator is positive, and the fraction lies strictly between zero and one. Thus \(x<\lambda<y\). Affineness gives

\[ F(\lambda) = F(x)+\frac{\lambda-x}{y-x}(F(y)-F(x)) =R. \]

Consequently, this \(\lambda\) satisfies the certificate, and the algorithm returns the exact maximizing vector \(b(\lambda)\).

This procedure handles coincident breakpoints and flat segments explicitly: duplicate breakpoints are removed, and interpolation is performed only between endpoints with strictly different demand values.

4. Exact bit complexity

Represent each rational as a binary numerator and a positive binary denominator. All comparisons and arithmetic below use this exact representation.

Every input rational has encoding length at most \(L\). The quantities \(a_t\), \(u_t\), \(a_t-cu_t\), and \(M\) involve only a fixed number of arithmetic operations on input rationals. Their encoding lengths are therefore \(O(L)\).

For any listed breakpoint \(q_j\), each coordinate \(b_t(q_j)\), and then each product \(\mu_tb_t(q_j)\), also has encoding length \(O(L)\).

To bound the size of their sum, consider \(n\) rationals \(p_i/d_i\), with each numerator and denominator having at most \(B\) bits. Their sum can be written with denominator

\[ D=\prod_{i=1}^{n}d_i \]

and numerator

\[ P=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_i\prod_{\ell\ne i}d_\ell. \]

These integers have \(O(nB+\log(n+1))\) bits: the denominator is a product of \(n\) \(B\)-bit integers, and the numerator is a sum of \(n\) products of that same bounded size. Reducing the fraction cannot increase these lengths.

Applying this observation to \(F(q_j)\), every endpoint demand has encoding length

\[ O(\tau L+\log(\tau+1)). \]

The interpolation formula uses a fixed number of operations on endpoint demands, breakpoints, and \(R\). Hence the computed \(\lambda\) has polynomial encoding length. Each final coordinate \(b_t(\lambda)\) does as well.

Sorting requires \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) exact rational comparisons. Evaluating demand at all breakpoints requires \(O(\tau^2)\) rational arithmetic operations. Interpolation and construction of the output require \(O(\tau)\) additional operations. Exact integer arithmetic, division, comparison, and fraction reduction all take polynomial time in the operand lengths. Thus the total bit complexity is polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

The algorithm operates solely on the supplied support list.

5. Exact output may require a non-dyadic rational

Even dyadic inputs need not produce dyadic optimal coordinates. For example, take two types with

\[ (\mu_1,\mu_2)=\left(\frac14,\frac34\right), \quad (\theta_1,\theta_2)=(1,3), \quad T_1=T_2=\omega_{T,1}=\omega_{T,2}=0, \]

and \(\omega_b=c=1\), \(\bar b_1=\bar b_2=3\), \(R=\frac12\).

At \(\lambda=\frac73\), the clipped formula gives

\[ b_1=0, \qquad b_2=\frac23, \qquad F(\lambda)=\frac34\cdot\frac23=\frac12. \]

The certificate proves that this is the unique optimum. Exact output therefore uses rational numerator-denominator encodings; a finite binary fractional expansion is not always available.

The polynomial-time alternative is established. An NP-hardness result under polynomial-time reductions would consequently imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
