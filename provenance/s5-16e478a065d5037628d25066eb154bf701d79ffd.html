The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over interval types \(I_t=[\alpha_t,\beta_t)\) whose union is \([0,1)\), consider sequences of populations with type counts \(n_t(N)\) satisfying \(n_t(N)/N\to\mu_t\). Scale the cake and each type’s interval to \([0,N)\) and \(N I_t\), respectively. Among sequences of direct mechanisms that, for every finite instance in the paper’s domain, produce envy-free, truthful allocations partitioning the cake with each agent’s bundle contained in their reported interval, determine the infimum of \(\limsup_{N\to\infty} K_N/N\), where \(K_N\) is the number of cuts; compute or approximate this rate from \(\mu\) and the interval endpoints.

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct interval types \(I_t=[\alpha_t,\beta_t)\) with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), \(0\leq\alpha_t<\beta_t\leq1\), the endpoints are rational, and \(\bigcup_t I_t=[0,1)\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types; let \(m\) count distinct endpoints and \(L\) be the total input bit length, including a rational threshold \(q\geq0\).
A finite instance consists of labelled agents with nonempty interval reports covering the entire cake. An agent with true interval \(J_i\) values a bundle \(A\) by \(V_i(A)=\operatorname{len}(A\cap J_i)\), without normalization.
An eligible deterministic direct mechanism returns a partition into bundles that are finite unions of half-open intervals, with each bundle contained in its owner's report; it is envy-free and dominant-strategy truthful on every such finite instance. Truthfulness compares utilities under the true interval and covers every unilateral interval report that preserves coverage; mechanisms may distinguish agent labels.
For each population size \(N\), let \(P_N\) be a labelled population consisting only of the listed types, with counts \(n_t(N)\), \(\sum_t n_t(N)=N\), \(n_t(N)/N\to\mu_t\), and coverage of the cake. Its cake is \([0,N)\) and its type intervals are \(NI_t\).
Let \(M_N\) be eligible on all covering profiles of \(N\) nonempty intervals in \([0,N)\), including reports outside the listed support. Let \(K(M_N,P_N)\) count the distinct interior boundaries between adjacent pieces assigned to different agents; cake endpoints are not cuts.
Define \(R(\mu,I)=\inf_{(P_N),(M_N)}\limsup_{N\to\infty}K(M_N,P_N)/N\), with the infimum over all population and eligible mechanism sequences just specified; no running-time restriction is imposed on these mechanisms.

Is deciding whether \(R(\mu,I)\leq q\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite populations, [Asano–Umeda](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2020.15) give an envy-free, truthful mechanism satisfying containment and full allocation, using at most \(2n-2\) cuts in \(O(n^3)\) time; this is an upper bound, not an optimality result.
[Asano](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2021.68) subsequently gives simpler finite mechanisms with the same cut bound and extends the approach to piecewise uniform valuations.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). Under the intended asymptotic interpretation, where covering populations are required for all sufficiently large \(N\), every valid society satisfies
\[ R(\mu,I)=1. \]
Thus the decision rule is simply to test whether \(q\geq1\).

There is a minor finite-size issue if “for each population size \(N\)” includes \(N=1\) literally. I give the corresponding decision rule below as well.

1. A universal lower bound

Consider any covering profile of \(n\) agents on a cake \(C\), and any envy-free partition \((A_1,\ldots,A_n)\). For an agent with nonempty true interval \(J_i\), envy-freeness gives
\[ \operatorname{len}(A_i\cap J_i) \geq \operatorname{len}(A_j\cap J_i) \qquad\text{for every }j. \]
Summing these inequalities and using full allocation yields
\[ n\operatorname{len}(A_i\cap J_i) \geq \sum_{j=1}^{n}\operatorname{len}(A_j\cap J_i) = \operatorname{len}(J_i)>0. \]
Consequently every agent owns a bundle of positive length.

Merge adjacent pieces having the same owner. Because the bundles are finite unions of intervals, this produces finitely many maximal runs of constant ownership. Every agent appears in at least one run, so there are at least \(n\) runs. Their interior ownership boundaries therefore number at least \(n-1\).

Applying this to every eligible mechanism gives
\[ K(M_N,P_N)\geq N-1. \]
Hence every admissible sequence has limiting upper cut rate at least \(1\), and therefore \(R(\mu,I)\geq1\) whenever admissible sequences exist.

2. The finite mechanism used for the upper bound

Use Asano’s Algorithm 2, with deterministic tie-breaking by agent labels. Theorem 2 establishes envy-freeness, truthfulness, containment, and full allocation for covering interval profiles. Its valuation and deviation definitions coincide with those in this question. Thus it supplies an eligible mechanism on the entire required domain. [Asano, Theorem 2 and Section 5](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol212-isaac2021/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2021.68/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2021.68.pdf)

Here are the algorithm’s structural features needed for our cut analysis:

  • In the current cake, it selects all maximal intervals minimizing length divided by the number of current reports contained in the interval.
  • Each selected interval is allocated to those contained-report agents. Their reports are sorted lexicographically by right endpoint, then left endpoint.
  • Each agent receives the required common length by taking the leftmost available cake in its report.
  • The selected intervals are removed. The remaining cake is compressed in its original order, and the procedure repeats.

All grouping below analyzes this existing mechanism’s execution; it does not change its allocations.

For clarity, a core means one selected interval together with its contained-report agents. A block means a maximal consecutive group of identical current reports in a core’s sorted order.

3. Repeated reports give only bounded overhead

Lemma. On a covering profile with \(n\) agents and \(d\) distinct original reports, this mechanism uses at most \(n+3d\) ownership cuts.

Proof. We first bound the numbers of cores and blocks.

Agents with identical original reports have identical residual reports after every removal and compression. Membership in a core depends only on containment of the residual report. Consequently all agents with any one original report are assigned in the same core.

Within that core, their identical residual reports belong to one block. Different original reports can become identical after compression, in which case they belong to the same block. Thus each original report contributes to exactly one block, and distinct blocks involve disjoint sets of original reports.

Let \(B\) be the total number of blocks and \(C_0\) the total number of cores. Every core contains at least one block, so
\[ C_0\leq B\leq d. \]

We now count geometric boundaries, retaining every boundary introduced during execution, even if it does not survive as an ownership cut.

Core boundaries. Compression concatenates the remaining cake components in their original order. On each component it acts by a translation. The inverse image of a selected compressed interval can therefore have new endpoints only in its first and last intersected components. All other endpoints are existing component boundaries.

Marking a core consequently introduces at most two new physical boundary points. Over all cores, this contributes at most \(2C_0\).

Boundaries within a block. Consider a block containing \(k\) agents. Let \(U\) be the cake still available in this core immediately before processing the block, and let \(J\) be their common current report.

The agents consecutively take equal-length prefixes of the remaining available cake in \(J\). Their combined allocation is therefore a single prefix, in spatial order, of \(U\cap J\). Its outer boundaries introduce at most two new points. Boundaries arising from gaps in the available cake were already present.

Separating this combined allocation among its \(k\) agents introduces at most \(k-1\) further new points. Indeed, each cumulative-length division falls either inside an available component, introducing one point, or at a gap, whose endpoints were already marked.

Thus this block introduces at most \(k+1\) new boundary points. This reasoning also applies after undoing compression: any additional endpoints created by restoring previously removed gaps are already marked core boundaries.

Every final ownership boundary is among the marked points. Since the block sizes sum to \(n\),
\[ K \leq 2C_0+\sum_{\text{blocks}}(k+1) = 2C_0+n+B \leq n+3d. \]
This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

The lemma is a refinement for repeated reports; it does not assert finite-population optimality.

4. Passing to the asymptotic society

For each \(N\), obtain a mechanism on \([0,N)\) by rescaling reports to \([0,1)\), applying the finite mechanism above, and rescaling the resulting allocation back.

Rescaling multiplies every utility comparison by the positive factor \(N\). It therefore preserves envy-freeness and dominant-strategy truthfulness. It also preserves containment, full allocation, finite interval unions, and the number of ownership cuts. In particular, these mechanisms remain eligible for reports outside the listed support.

A population supported on the listed types has at most \(\tau\) distinct reports. The lemma therefore gives
\[ K(M_N,P_N)\leq N+3\tau. \]
Combined with the lower bound,
\[ 1-\frac1N \leq \frac{K(M_N,P_N)}N \leq 1+\frac{3\tau}{N}. \]
Here \(\tau\) is fixed for the society under consideration. Both bounds converge to \(1\).

Covering populations with the required frequencies exist for every \(N\geq\tau\). To construct them, start with
\[ n_t(N)=1+\left\lfloor(N-\tau)\mu_t\right\rfloor \]
for every type, and assign the remaining agents to one fixed type. There are fewer than \(\tau\) remaining agents. The counts sum to \(N\), every type is present, and \(n_t(N)/N\to\mu_t\). Coverage follows from the support’s coverage.

Thus the upper bound is achieved by eligible mechanism and population sequences. Together with Section 1, it proves \(R(\mu,I)=1\) under the asymptotic interpretation.

5. Literal requirement at every positive population size

If the definition requires a covering supported population even at \(N=1\), such a population exists exactly when the support lists \([0,1)\).

When that type is listed, use only that type for the finitely many sizes \(N<\tau\), and use the construction above thereafter. The proof still gives \(R(\mu,I)=1\).

When that type is absent, no population sequence satisfying the literal definition exists. With the usual extended-real convention \(\inf\varnothing=+\infty\), the literal value is therefore
\[ R(\mu,I)= \begin{cases} 1,&\text{if the support lists }[0,1),\\ +\infty,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

6. Decision complexity

Under the intended asymptotic interpretation, answer yes exactly when \(q\geq1\).

Under the literal interpretation with the empty-infimum convention, answer yes exactly when the support lists \([0,1)\) and \(q\geq1\).

These tests use rational comparisons and a scan of the input, so their running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The complexity conclusion follows from the proved cut refinement, rather than from the general finite bound alone.

3. Repeated-report lemma

The grouping argument is valid:

  • Identical original reports undergo identical removal and compression operations while their agents remain unassigned.
  • Their core membership is therefore identical.
  • Selected maximal cores are disjoint in the cited mechanism, so one nonempty residual report cannot assign its agents to multiple cores.
  • Lexicographic sorting places identical residual reports consecutively.
  • Original reports that become identical can merge into a block; this does not split any original report among blocks.

Consequently,
\[ C_0\leq B\leq d \]
is justified.

The geometric accounting also survives scrutiny.

For a core, inverse compression acts by translations on surviving components. Only the two extremities of the selected compressed interval can introduce previously unmarked physical endpoints. Endpoints at intervening removed gaps already belong to earlier core boundaries. Thus the allowance of two new points per core is valid.

For a block, consecutively allocating equal-length leftmost prefixes makes the combined allocation a spatial prefix of the initially available cake within the common report. Its only potentially new exterior endpoints are its start and finish. All internal gaps arise from previously allocated cake or previously removed cores, whose endpoints have already been marked.

Each of the \(k-1\) divisions between consecutive agents introduces at most one additional point. A division occurring at a gap introduces no new point because that gap’s endpoints are already accounted for. Undoing compression introduces only previously marked gap endpoints.

The invariant that every final ownership boundary is marked is therefore maintained. The computation
