The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite classes \(P\), complete agent types \(T\) with class map \(p:T\to P\), item categories \(J\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), rational supply masses \(\sigma_j\), and utilities \(u_t(j)\in[0,1]\), find rational class allocations \(b_{pj}\ge0\) with \(\sum_{p\in P}b_{pj}\le\sigma_j\) and within-class transports attaining each assignment valuation \(V_p(b_p)\), such that \(2V_p(b_p)\ge V_p(b_q)\) for every \(p\ne q\), while no positive item-category mass can be transferred between classes to increase the receiving class's valuation without decreasing the giving class's valuation; otherwise report infeasibility.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P\) be \(k\ge1\) classes and \(J\) be \(m\ge1\) item categories. A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type specifies its class \(p(t)\) and rational utilities \(u_t(j)\in[0,1]\); every class has positive mass. Supplies \(\sigma_j>0\) are binary rationals, with unrestricted total supply. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For \(T_p=\{t:p(t)=p\}\) and a nonnegative bundle vector \(b\), define the assignment valuation \(V_p(b)=\max\{\sum_{t\in T_p,j\in J}u_t(j)x_{tj}:x_{tj}\ge0,\ \sum_jx_{tj}\le\mu_t,\ \sum_{t\in T_p}x_{tj}\le b_j\}\). Thus each unit of agent mass receives at most one unit of item mass.
An allocation consists of rational \(b_{pj}\ge0\) with \(\sum_pb_{pj}\le\sigma_j\), together with rational transports attaining \(V_p(b_p)\) and satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T_p}x_{tj}=b_{pj}\). It is \(\tfrac12\)-class envy-free if \(2V_p(b_p)\ge V_p(b_q)\) for all distinct \(p,q\).
Write \(r_j=\sigma_j-\sum_pb_{pj}\) and let \(e_j\) be the coordinate vector for category \(j\). Non-wastefulness means there are neither \(p,j,0<\delta\le r_j\) with \(V_p(b_p+\delta e_j)>V_p(b_p)\), nor distinct \(p,q\), a category \(j\), and \(0<\delta\le b_{qj}\) with \(V_p(b_p+\delta e_j)>V_p(b_p)\) and \(V_q(b_q-\delta e_j)=V_q(b_q)\). These conditions quantify over real transfer amounts.

Is finding a rational non-wasteful \(\tfrac12\)-class envy-free allocation and attaining transports, or reporting infeasibility, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is this task NP-hard?

For indivisible items and finitely many agents, round-robin computes a non-wasteful \(\tfrac12\)-class envy-free-up-to-one-item matching in polynomial time: after removing at most one item from the envied bundle, twice the receiving class's utility bounds its assignment valuation. [Yokoyama–Igarashi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14371).
For binary utilities, an online fractional matching algorithm achieves \((1-1/e)\)-class envy-freeness and its stated non-wastefulness condition. This concerns finite unit-capacity agents and a different efficiency definition; it does not settle the arbitrary-utility mass problem above. [Hosseini–Huang–Igarashi–Shah](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/25704/25476).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total agent mass to one; left total supply unrestricted because the discrete model imposes no equality between agent and item counts.
  • Made utilities binary-rational inputs and defined L as the total input bit length.
  • Required positive mass in every class and positive supply for listed item categories.
  • Defined assignment valuations by fractional transports with type capacities equal to their masses, preserving the at-most-one-item constraint per unit of agent mass.
  • Required attaining transports to consume each allocated bundle exactly, since a discrete class bundle consists of items actually matched to its members.
  • Restored the omitted non-wastefulness condition for useful unallocated supply.
  • Interpreted transfers as positive amounts of one category at a time, with arbitrary real amounts, following the discrete single-item move.
  • Used the giving class's valuation to test donor loss, following the definition's prose; the displayed formula retains a receiver-index typo even in the full version.
  • Retained the graded question's fixed one-half fairness inequality without deleting any positive mass from the envied bundle.

Polynomial-time solvability

The task is solvable in polynomial time in $m,\tau,k,L$. Every valid instance is feasible.

The algorithm approximates Nash welfare to a prescribed accuracy, uses that approximation to construct a rational quadratic objective, and solves the resulting convex quadratic program exactly. The key lemma is that, at a weighted-welfare optimum, a class with a small item-price bill has utility close to a fixed rational lattice. This permits an exact factor-$2$ envy bound.

1. Transport formulation and non-wastefulness

Let $P_+$ consist of the classes having at least one strictly positive utility, and put $h=|P_+|$. Give every other class an empty bundle and zero transport. If $h=0$, this already solves the problem.

Assume $h\ge1$. We use transport variables only for types belonging to positive classes, and define the rational polytope
$$ X=\left\{x\ge0: \sum_jx_{tj}\le\mu_t\ \text{for every retained type }t,\quad \sum_tx_{tj}\le\sigma_j\ \text{for every }j \right\}. $$
It is nonempty and compact, since every coordinate satisfies $0\le x_{tj}\le\mu_t\le1$.

For $p\in P_+$, write
$$ U_p(x)=\sum_{t\in T_p,j}u_t(j)x_{tj}, \qquad b_{pj}(x)=\sum_{t\in T_p}x_{tj}. $$
In particular, $0\le U_p(x)\le1$.

Lemma 1. If $x\in X$ maximizes
$$ \sum_{p\in P_+}w_pU_p(x) $$
for strictly positive weights $w_p$, its induced bundles and transports attain their assignment valuations and satisfy both non-wastefulness conditions.

Proof. If a class’s transport failed to attain $V_p(b_p(x))$, replace it by an optimal transport under those bundle bounds. This preserves feasibility in $X$ and strictly increases the weighted objective, a contradiction. The original transport has exactly the column sums defining $b_p(x)$.

If unused supply allowed a strict improvement for a class, replace its transport by an optimal transport under the enlarged bundle bounds. The replacement is feasible in $X$ and strictly improves the weighted objective.

If a transfer from $q$ to $p$ strictly improved $p$ while preserving $q$’s valuation, replace both transports by optimal transports under their modified bundle bounds. Their combined category bounds are unchanged, so the replacement is feasible in $X$. Its weighted objective strictly increases.

These arguments apply to every permitted real transfer amount. A zero-valuation class cannot strictly improve and has nothing to donate. $\square$

2. A dual criterion and the rational lattice lemma

Fix positive weights $w_p$ and a maximizer $x$ of their weighted welfare. Its transport linear program has the dual
$$ \min_{\alpha,\beta} \left\{ \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+\sum_j\sigma_j\beta_j: \alpha_t,\beta_j\ge0,\quad \alpha_t+\beta_j\ge w_{p(t)}u_t(j) \right\}. $$
The primal is feasible and bounded, and the dual is feasible, for example with $\beta_j=0$ and sufficiently large $\alpha_t$. Linear-programming duality therefore gives an optimal dual solution.

Define
$$ A_p=\sum_{t\in T_p}\mu_t\alpha_t, \qquad B_p=\sum_j\beta_jb_{pj}(x), \qquad C_p=w_pU_p(x). $$
Complementary slackness gives
$$ C_p=A_p+B_p. $$
Indeed, every used edge has $\alpha_t+\beta_j=w_pu_t(j)$, and every type with $\alpha_t>0$ has its capacity saturated.

For any transport evaluating class $p$’s valuation of class $q$’s bundle, the dual inequalities give
$$ w_pV_p(b_q(x)) \le A_p+B_q =C_p-B_p+B_q. $$
Consequently, the following conditions suffice for the desired envy bound:
$$ C_p\le1 \quad\text{and}\quad C_p+B_p\ge1 \qquad(p\in P_+). $$
Under these conditions, $B_q\le C_q\le1$, and hence
$$ w_pV_p(b_q(x)) \le C_p-B_p+1 \le2C_p =2w_pU_p(x). $$

We next establish the lattice property used to enforce those conditions.

Choose a positive integer $D$ such that
$$ D\mu_tu_t(j)\in\mathbb Z \qquad\text{for every }t,j. $$
For example, take the product of the denominators of all input masses and utilities. This integer has $O(L)$ bits.

Lemma 2. For every class $p$ at a weighted-welfare optimum as above, there is
$$ R_p\in D^{-1}\mathbb Z_{\ge0} $$
such that
$$ 0\le U_p(x)-R_p\le\frac{B_p}{w_p}. $$

Proof. Let
$$ S_p=\{t\in T_p:\alpha_t>0\}. $$
Every type in $S_p$ receives its full mass $\mu_t$. For each such type, choose a used category $j_t$ minimizing $\beta_j$ among its used categories. Complementary slackness gives
$$ w_pu_t(j_t)=\alpha_t+\beta_{j_t}. $$
Set
$$ R_p=\sum_{t\in S_p}\mu_tu_t(j_t). $$
By the definition of $D$, this belongs to the stated lattice. Moreover,
$$ w_pR_p=A_p+\sum_{t\in S_p}\mu_t\beta_{j_t}. $$
Since each selected type is fully served and $j_t$ has minimum price among its used categories,
$$ \sum_{t\in S_p}\mu_t\beta_{j_t} \le \sum_{t\in S_p,j}\beta_jx_{tj} \le B_p. $$
Using $w_pU_p(x)=A_p+B_p$, we obtain
$$ w_p\bigl(U_p(x)-R_p\bigr) = B_p-\sum_{t\in S_p}\mu_t\beta_{j_t} \in[0,B_p]. $$
Dividing by $w_p>0$ proves the lemma. $\square$

3. A sufficiently accurate Nash-welfare approximation

We first construct an explicit positive lower bound on all Nash-optimal utilities.

Let
$$ \theta=\min\left\{1,\min_t\mu_t,\min_j\sigma_j\right\}, \qquad v=\min\{u_t(j):u_t(j)>0\}, $$
and put
$$ \delta=\frac{\theta}{2h}, \qquad \nu=\delta v, \qquad a=\nu^h. $$
All these numbers are positive rationals with polynomial encoding length.

For each positive class, select one positive-utility type-category pair and assign $\delta$ along that pair. This is feasible: each selected type receives at most half its capacity, and each category receives at most $h\delta=\theta/2$. Every positive class obtains utility at least $\nu$.

The continuous product $\prod_{p\in P_+}U_p(x)$ therefore has a positive maximum on $X$. Let $U^*$ be its maximizing utility vector. Each coordinate is positive, and
$$ \prod_pU_p^*\ge\nu^h=a. $$
Since every other coordinate is at most one,
$$ U_p^*\ge a \qquad(p\in P_+). $$

Let
$$ \mathcal C=\{(U_p(x))_{p\in P_+}:x\in X\}, \qquad f(U)=\sum_p\log U_p. $$
Strict concavity makes $U^*$ unique. First-order optimality gives
$$ \nabla f(U^*)\cdot(U-U^*)\le0 \qquad(U\in\mathcal C). $$
This also holds when $U$ has zero coordinates: an initial segment from $U^*$ toward $U$ remains strictly positive, so its directional derivative is defined.

On positive utility vectors whose coordinates are at most one,
$$ \nabla^2f(U) =-\operatorname{diag}(U_p^{-2}) \preceq-I. $$
It follows that every positive $U\in\mathcal C$ satisfies
$$ f(U^*)-f(U)\ge\frac12\|U-U^*\|_2^2. $$

Set
$$ \rho=\frac{a^3}{1024hD}, \qquad \varepsilon=\frac{\rho^2}{32}. $$
We compute a rational $\widetilde x$ in
$$ K=\{x\in X:U_p(x)\ge a/2\ \text{for all }p\in P_+\} $$
with
$$ f(U(\widetilde x))\ge f(U^*)-\varepsilon. $$
This requires only polynomial time. We use the standard weak convex-optimization result for logarithmic Nash-welfare programs: over a bounded rational polytope with an encoded positive utility lower bound, additive objective accuracy $\varepsilon$ can be obtained in time polynomial in the input length and $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. This follows from ellipsoid optimization; see [Hosseini–Vazirani, Sections 1.2 and 2.1](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/opus/volltexte/2022/15682/pdf/LIPIcs-ITCS-2022-86.pdf).

Here the hypotheses hold:

  • $K$ is a bounded rational polytope described by polynomially many explicit inequalities.
  • It is nonempty and contains the Nash optimum.
  • All utilities on $K$ lie in $[a/2,1]$. Logarithms can be evaluated to any prescribed precision in polynomial time, and the transport-coordinate gradient is the rational quantity $u_t(j)/U_{p(t)}(x)$.
  • The required bounds on the feasible region have polynomial encoding length.

For the last point, an interior ball can also be exhibited explicitly. Let $N$ be the number of retained transport variables. Add
$$ \lambda=\frac{\theta}{8(N+1)} $$
to every coordinate of the small allocation constructed above. Each row and column then uses at most $5\theta/8$, every coordinate is positive, and every class still obtains utility at least $\nu\ge a$. A Euclidean ball around this point of radius
$$ r=\min\left\{\frac{\lambda}{2}, \frac{\theta}{8(N+1)}, \frac{a}{4(N+1)}\right\} $$
lies in $K$: coordinate changes preserve nonnegativity, row and column changes are at most $\theta/8$, and utility changes are at most $a/4$. Also, $K$ lies in a ball of radius $\sqrt N$ around the origin.

Write $\widetilde U_p=U_p(\widetilde x)$. The strong-concavity bound yields
$$ \|\widetilde U-U^*\|_2 \le\sqrt{2\varepsilon} =\frac{\rho}{4}. $$
In particular,
$$ |\widetilde U_p-U_p^*|\le\frac{\rho}{4}. $$

4. The rational quadratic program

For each class, compute
$$ n_p=\left\lfloor D\widetilde U_p+\frac12\right\rfloor. $$
Choose the rational center
$$ c_p= \begin{cases} n_p/D,&\text{if }|\widetilde U_p-n_p/D|\le\rho,\\ \widetilde U_p,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
Thus
$$ |c_p-U_p^*| \le \rho+\frac{\rho}{4} =\frac{5\rho}{4} <2\rho. $$
Since $\rho\le a/1024$ and $a\le U_p^*\le1$, we have
$$ \frac a2\le c_p\le2. $$

Now maximize, over the original polytope $X$, the rational quadratic objective
$$ Q(U(x)) = \sum_{p\in P_+} \left( \frac{2U_p(x)}{c_p} -\frac{U_p(x)^2}{2c_p^2} \right). $$
Its negative Hessian in the transport variables is positive semidefinite, being a sum of positive semidefinite rank-one matrices. Thus this is a convex quadratic minimization problem after negating the objective.

A convex quadratic program with rational coefficients and rational linear constraints can be solved exactly, with rational output, in polynomial time. This is the theorem of [Kozlov–Tarasov–Khachiyan, *The polynomial solvability of convex quadratic programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189). Its hypotheses apply here: the coefficients are rational, the quadratic matrix is positive semidefinite after negation, and the feasible set is nonempty and compact. Clearing denominators produces the integer-coefficient form of that theorem with only polynomial growth in encoding length.

Let $x$ be any exact rational maximizer, and write $U=U(x)$.

5. The quadratic optimum stays close to the Nash optimum

We prove the quantitative estimates needed for exact envy-freeness.

For each coordinate,
$$ \frac{\partial Q}{\partial U_p}(U^*) -\frac{\partial f}{\partial U_p}(U^*) = \frac{2c_p-U_p^*}{c_p^2}-\frac1{U_p^*} = -\frac{(U_p^*-c_p)^2}{c_p^2U_p^*}. $$
Let this gradient difference be $g$. The bounds from the preceding sections give
$$ |g_p|\le\frac{16\rho^2}{a^3}, \qquad \|g\|_2\le\frac{16\sqrt h\,\rho^2}{a^3}. $$

Put $\Delta=U-U^*$. Because $U$ maximizes $Q$ on $\mathcal C$,
$$ \nabla Q(U)\cdot\Delta\ge0. $$
Also,
$$ \nabla Q(U) = \nabla f(U^*)+g -\operatorname{diag}(c_p^{-2})\Delta. $$
Combining this identity with first-order optimality at $U^*$ and $c_p\le2$ gives
$$ 0 \le\nabla Q(U)\cdot\Delta \le g\cdot\Delta-\frac14\|\Delta\|_2^2. $$
Cauchy–Schwarz therefore implies
$$ \|U-U^*\|_2 \le4\|g\|_2 \le\frac{64h\rho^2}{a^3} =\frac{\rho}{16D} \le\frac{\rho}{16}. $$
The same conclusion is immediate if $\Delta=0$.

Consequently,
$$ |U_p-c_p| \le\frac{\rho}{16}+\frac{5\rho}{4} <2\rho, $$
and
$$ |U_p-\widetilde U_p| \le\frac{\rho}{16}+\frac{\rho}{4} <\frac{\rho}{2}. $$

Define the rational welfare weights
$$ w_p=\frac{\partial Q}{\partial U_p}(U) =\frac{2c_p-U_p}{c_p^2}. $$
They are strictly positive. Indeed,
$$ w_pc_p =1-\frac{U_p-c_p}{c_p} \ge1-\frac{4\rho}{a} \ge\frac12. $$
In particular,
$$ w_p\ge\frac1{2c_p}\ge\frac14. $$

First-order optimality of $x$ for $Q(U(x))$ gives
$$ \sum_pw_p\bigl(U_p(z)-U_p(x)\bigr)\le0 \qquad(z\in X). $$
Thus $x$ maximizes the positive weighted linear welfare objective with these weights. Lemma 1 already proves attainment and non-wastefulness.

6. Exact factor-$2$ envy-freeness

Apply Section 2 to $x$ and its positive weights $w_p$, obtaining optimal dual variables and quantities $A_p,B_p,C_p$.

The special quadratic derivative gives the exact identity
$$ C_p=w_pU_p =\frac{2U_p}{c_p}-\frac{U_p^2}{c_p^2} = 1-\frac{(U_p-c_p)^2}{c_p^2}. $$
In particular, $C_p\le1$. Set
$$ E=\frac{16\rho^2}{a^2}. $$
The proximity estimates imply
$$ 1-E\le C_p\le1. $$
Our choice of $\rho$ also gives
$$ E\le\frac14, \qquad 4E\le\frac{\rho}{16}, \qquad \rho<\frac1{2D}. $$
For example,
$$ 4E =\rho\frac{64\rho}{a^2} =\rho\frac{a}{16hD} \le\frac{\rho}{16}. $$

We show that $C_p+B_p\ge1$ for every class.

Case 1: $B_p\ge E$. Then
$$ C_p+B_p\ge1-E+E=1. $$

Case 2: $0\le B_p<E$. By Lemma 2, there is a lattice value $R_p$ satisfying
$$ 0\le U_p-R_p\le\frac{B_p}{w_p}\le4B_p<4E. $$
Therefore,
$$ |\widetilde U_p-R_p| \le|\widetilde U_p-U_p|+|U_p-R_p| <\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{\rho}{16} <\rho. $$
Since $\rho<1/(2D)$, this $R_p$ is the unique nearest multiple of $1/D$ to $\widetilde U_p$. The center-selection rule in Section 4 consequently chose
$$ c_p=R_p. $$
Using the sharper conclusion of Lemma 2 and $w_pc_p\ge1/2$,
$$ 1-C_p =\frac{(U_p-c_p)^2}{c_p^2} \le\frac{B_p^2}{(w_pc_p)^2} \le4B_p^2 \le B_p. $$
The last inequality follows from $B_p<E\le1/4$. Hence $C_p+B_p\ge1$ in this case as well.

We have now established both conditions from Section 2:
$$ C_p\le1, \qquad C_p+B_p\ge1. $$
Thus, for distinct positive classes,
$$ w_pV_p(b_q(x)) \le C_p-B_p+B_q \le C_p-B_p+1 \le2C_p. $$
Dividing by $w_p>0$ and using Lemma 1 gives
$$ V_p(b_q(x)) \le2U_p(x) =2V_p(b_p(x)). $$
Comparisons involving a zero-valuation class hold because such classes receive empty bundles.

7. Algorithm and encoding bounds

The algorithm is therefore:

  • Identify positive classes; assign empty bundles to all other classes.
  • Compute $D,\theta,v,\nu,a,\rho,\varepsilon$ as above.
  • Obtain the rational Nash-welfare approximation $\widetilde x$.
  • Construct the rational centers $c_p$.
  • Solve the rational quadratic program exactly.
  • Output its transport $x$ and the bundles $b_{pj}=\sum_{t\in T_p}x_{tj}$.

Every step has polynomial bit complexity:

  • $D$ has $O(L)$ bits.
  • $\nu$ has polynomial encoding length, so $a=\nu^h$ has encoding length polynomial in $h,L$.
  • Consequently, $\rho,\varepsilon$ and $\log(1/\varepsilon)$ have polynomial size.
  • The approximation has polynomial-size rational output.
  • Finding the nearest lattice value uses exact rational arithmetic.
  • The quadratic program has at most $\tau m$ variables and polynomial-size rational coefficients, so its exact rational solution is computable in polynomial time.

Lemma 1 proves the required attainment and non-wastefulness, including the conditions quantified over real transfer amounts. Section 6 proves exact $\tfrac12$-class envy-freeness. Thus the algorithm returns a valid rational allocation on every valid input.
