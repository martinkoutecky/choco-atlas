The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete voter types \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), acyclic type-level delegations, direct votes \(\widehat b_\theta\), canonical votes \(g_\theta\), and a consistency family \(\Gamma\subseteq\{0,1\}^m\) with polynomial-time partial-completion testing, decide whether there are variables \(q_{\theta,x}\ge 0\) for \(x\in\Gamma\) satisfying \(\sum_{x\supseteq\widehat b_\theta}q_{\theta,x}=\mu_\theta\) for every \(\theta\), with total changed mass \(\sum_{\theta,x}q_{\theta,x}|\{j:\theta\text{ delegates on }j,\ x_j\ne g_{\theta j}\}|\) at most a rational threshold \(K\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions. An instance has complete voter types Θ, rational masses μ_θ, and \(m\) binary issues. For each type \(θ\) and issue \(j\), it either casts the direct vote \(ˆb_{θ j}\in\{0,1\}\) or delegates to another type; delegation graphs are acyclic separately on each issue. The canonical vote \(g_{θ j}\) is the direct vote at the end of \(θ\)'s delegation chain on issue \(j\).

A consistency family is \(Γ\subseteq\{0,1\}^m\), represented so that testing whether a partial assignment has a completion in \(Γ\) is polynomial-time. A mass assignment chooses \(q_{θ,x}\geq0\) for each \(x\inΓ\), with \(∑_{x\supseteqˆb_θ}q_{θ,x}=μ_θ\). Its changed mass is \(∑_{θ,x}q_{θ,x}|\{j:θ\text{ delegates on }j,\ x_j\ne g_{θ j}\}|\).

Target. Is deciding whether such a mass assignment of changed mass at most a rational threshold \(K\) exists solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results. For finite individual voters, the corresponding MINIMALVOTECHANGE problem is NP-complete. Its NP-hardness holds with consistency constraints whose feasible ballots encode independent sets.

Prover output (gpt-5.6-sol / xhigh, Bolzano prover prompt)

Complexity classification

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP\text{-}hard}\). In fact, under the stated representation assumption for \(\Gamma\), it is \(\mathrm{NP\text{-}complete}\), even when all masses are equal and positive and every delegation chain has length at most one.

1. Deterministic-support lemma

For a type \(\theta\), define

\[ A_\theta=\{x\in\Gamma:x\supseteq\hat b_\theta\} \]

and

\[ c_\theta(x)=\left|\{j:\theta\text{ delegates on }j,\ x_j\neq g_{\theta j}\}\right|. \]

Assume \(\mu_\theta>0\). In any feasible mass assignment, mass assigned to ballots outside \(A_\theta\) can be deleted: such mass does not contribute to the equality

\[ \sum_{x\in A_\theta}q_{\theta,x}=\mu_\theta, \]

and its contribution to changed mass is nonnegative.

Consequently, \(q_{\theta,x}/\mu_\theta\), for \(x\in A_\theta\), is a probability distribution. Therefore

\[ \sum_{x\in A_\theta}q_{\theta,x}c_\theta(x) \geq \mu_\theta\min_{x\in A_\theta}c_\theta(x). \]

Equality is attained by putting all mass \(\mu_\theta\) on a minimizing ballot. Thus, provided every positive-mass type has a feasible completion, the optimum is exactly

\[ \sum_{\theta}\mu_\theta \min_{x\in A_\theta}c_\theta(x). \]

Hence fractional splitting does not improve the objective. Nevertheless, finding the minimum-cost completion can be hard even when testing existence of an arbitrary completion is easy.

2. Reduction from Independent Set

Take an instance consisting of a simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\), with \(V=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and an integer \(k\) satisfying \(1\leq k\leq n\). The question is whether \(G\) has an independent set of cardinality at least \(k\).

Create one issue for every vertex. Define

\[ \Gamma_G= \left\{ x\in\{0,1\}^n: x_u+x_v\leq 1\text{ for every }\{u,v\}\in E \right\}. \]

Thus, ballots in \(\Gamma_G\) are precisely characteristic vectors of independent sets.

Polynomial-time completion testing

Let \(p\) be any partial assignment, and let

\[ U_p=\{v:p(v)=1\}. \]

If \(U_p\) contains both endpoints of an edge, no extension in \(\Gamma_G\) exists. Conversely, if \(U_p\) is independent, assigning \(0\) to every unassigned coordinate produces an extension in \(\Gamma_G\). Therefore partial-assignment completion is testable in time polynomial in \(|V|+|E|\).

3. Voter types and delegations

Introduce the following \(n+2\) types:

  • one central type \(a\);
  • one zero type \(z\);
  • one helper type \(h_v\) for every \(v\in V\).

Give every type the same mass

\[ \mu=\frac{1}{n+2}. \]

Thus all masses are positive and their sum is \(1\).

The voting and delegation behavior is as follows.

  • Type \(z\) votes directly \(0\) on every issue.
  • Type \(h_v\) votes directly \(1\) on issue \(v\), and delegates every issue \(u\neq v\) to \(z\).
  • Type \(a\) delegates issue \(v\) to \(h_v\).

For a fixed issue \(v\), the only relevant delegation edges are \(a\to h_v\) and \(h_u\to z\) for \(u\neq v\). Hence every issue-specific delegation graph is acyclic, and every delegation chain has length at most one.

The canonical votes are:

  • \(g_{a,v}=1\) for every \(v\);
  • for \(h_v\), every delegated issue has canonical vote \(0\);
  • \(z\) delegates no issues.

Set the threshold to

\[ K=\mu(n-k)=\frac{n-k}{n+2}. \]

The construction has polynomial size.

4. Correctness of the reduction

Forward implication

Suppose \(G\) has an independent set \(S\) with \(|S|\geq k\). Assign:

  • all mass of \(a\) to the characteristic vector \(\chi^S\);
  • all mass of \(h_v\) to the singleton vector \(e_v\);
  • all mass of \(z\) to the all-zero vector.

Each assigned ballot belongs to \(\Gamma_G\). It also extends the corresponding direct votes.

For \(h_v\), the ballot \(e_v\) agrees with its canonical vote \(0\) on every delegated issue. Thus every helper has zero changed mass. Type \(z\) delegates nowhere and also has zero changed mass.

For \(a\), every issue is delegated and its canonical ballot is the all-one vector. Its changed mass is therefore

\[ \mu\left|\{v:\chi^S_v\neq 1\}\right| =\mu(n-|S|) \leq\mu(n-k) =K. \]

Hence the constructed mass-assignment instance is a yes-instance.

Reverse implication

Suppose there is a mass assignment of changed mass at most \(K\). Since the central type \(a\) has no direct votes, its mass constraint is

\[ \sum_{x\in\Gamma_G}q_{a,x}=\mu. \]

Every \(x\in\Gamma_G\) represents an independent set, and the cost of assigning central mass to \(x\) is \(n-|x|\).

Assume for contradiction that \(G\) has no independent set of size at least \(k\). Then every \(x\in\Gamma_G\) satisfies \(|x|\leq k-1\), so

\[ n-|x|\geq n-k+1. \]

Consequently, the changed mass contributed by \(a\) alone is at least

\[ \sum_{x\in\Gamma_G}q_{a,x}(n-|x|) \geq \mu(n-k+1) > \mu(n-k) = K. \]

All other contributions are nonnegative, contradicting the assumed threshold. Therefore \(G\) contains an independent set of cardinality at least \(k\).

This proves \(\mathrm{NP\text{-}hardness}\).

5. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

By the deterministic-support lemma, every yes-instance has a solution placing all positive mass of each type on a single ballot \(x_\theta\in\Gamma\) extending \(\hat b_\theta\).

A certificate therefore consists of one \(m\)-bit ballot for every positive-mass type. Verification proceeds by:

  • testing \(x_\theta\in\Gamma\) using the given completion tester on the complete assignment \(x_\theta\);
  • checking that \(x_\theta\supseteq\hat b_\theta\);
  • computing the canonical votes from the acyclic delegation graphs;
  • computing \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta c_\theta(x_\theta)\) using exact rational arithmetic and comparing it with \(K\).

All these operations take polynomial time. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Conclusion

The mass-assignment problem is \(\mathrm{NP\text{-}complete}\). Divisibility of masses does not remove the hardness because the objective and constraints are type-separable and linear: fractional mixtures cannot outperform the cheapest feasible ballot for a type. The hardness already arises from finding a feasible ballot nearest to the canonical ballot.

Summary

The prover correctly establishes that the decision problem is NP-complete under the stated representation assumption for \(\Gamma\). The reduction is polynomial, respects the delegation restrictions, and remains valid despite fractional mass assignments.

Step-by-step audit

1. Deterministic-support lemma

For each type \(\theta\), only ballots in \(A_\theta\) contribute to the required equality. Any mass placed outside \(A_\theta\) can therefore be set to zero without violating feasibility, and doing so cannot increase the nonnegative objective.

After this deletion, the normalized coefficients \(q_{\theta,x}/\mu_\theta\) form a probability distribution when \(\mu_\theta>0\). The weighted-average inequality is valid, and concentrating all mass on a minimum-cost feasible ballot attains equality.

The resulting decomposition of the optimum by type is correct. Zero-mass types cause no difficulty because assigning them zero mass is feasible.

2. Construction of \(\Gamma_G\)

The family \(\Gamma_G\) is exactly the set of characteristic vectors of independent sets. The completion test is correct:

  • If the coordinates already assigned \(1\) contain an edge, no completion is possible.
  • If they form an independent set, setting every unassigned coordinate to \(0\) gives a valid completion.

This establishes the required polynomial-time partial-completion property.

3. Types and delegation graphs

The construction uses \(n+2\) types with equal positive rational mass.

For each issue \(v\):

  • Type \(a\) delegates to \(h_v\), which votes directly \(1\) on that issue.
  • Every \(h_u\) with \(u\neq v\) delegates that issue directly to \(z\).
  • Type \(z\) votes directly.

Thus all issue-specific delegation graphs are acyclic, and every delegation chain has one edge at most. The stated canonical votes follow correctly.

4. Forward implication

Given an independent set \(S\) of size at least \(k\):

  • \(\chi^S\), every singleton vector \(e_v\), and the zero vector belong to \(\Gamma_G\).
  • These ballots satisfy the respective direct-vote constraints.
  • Helpers and \(z\) contribute zero changed mass.
  • Type \(a\) contributes exactly \(\mu(n-|S|)\), which is at most \(K=\mu(n-k)\).

The forward implication is correct.

5. Reverse implication

Because \(a\) has no direct votes, all ballots in \(\Gamma_G\) are eligible for its required mass. If no independent set has size at least \(k\), every eligible ballot has at most \(k-1\) ones and hence costs at least \(n-k+1\) for type \(a\).

Therefore \(a\)'s contribution alone is at least \(\mu(n-k+1)>K\), contradicting the assumed feasible threshold. Fractional splitting cannot evade this bound because it applies pointwise to every ballot in the support.

The reverse implication is correct.

6. Polynomial size and source problem

The construction has polynomially many types, issues, delegation edges, and graph constraints. Independent Set remains NP-hard under the harmless restriction \(1\leq k\leq n\).

7. Membership in NP

The deterministic-support lemma guarantees that every yes-instance has a certificate containing one ballot per positive-mass type. Membership in \(\Gamma\) can be checked by applying the assumed partial-completion test to the complete assignment. Direct-vote consistency, canonical votes, objective cost, and the rational threshold comparison are all polynomial-time computations.

Thus membership in NP is established.

Final assessment

No false statements, missing hypotheses, unjustified nontrivial steps, or relevant edge-case failures were found. The claimed NP-completeness result follows rigorously.
