The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given agent types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), admissible partitions \(k\in[m]\), bundle classes with rational supply masses \(\nu_b^k\), rational utilities \(u_{\theta b}^k\), and threshold \(\kappa\), decide whether there are partition probabilities \(p_k\) and assignment masses \(x_{\theta b}^k\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{\theta b}^k=p_k\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta b}^k=p_k\nu_b^k\), and \(\sum_k p_k=1\), such that the induced lottery is ex-ante envy-free, Pareto-optimal, and has social welfare \(W(x)=\sum_{k,\theta,b}u_{\theta b}^k x_{\theta b}^k\)\ at least \(\kappa\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions. An instance has a finite type set \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\ge0\) with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\), templates \(k\) with roles \(b\), rational supplies \(\sigma_{k,b}\), and utilities \(u^\theta_{k,b}\). A feasible mass lottery consists of \(p_k\ge0\) and \(x_{k\theta b}\ge0\) such that \(\sum_b x_{k\theta b}=p_k\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_\theta x_{k\theta b}=p_k\sigma_{k,b}\), and \(\sum_k p_k=1\). For \(\mu_\theta>0\), let \(U_\theta(x)=\mu_\theta^{-1}\sum_{k,b}u^\theta_{k,b}x_{k\theta b}\) and \(U_\theta(x;\phi)=\mu_\phi^{-1}\sum_{k,b}u^\theta_{k,b}x_{k\phi b}\). Require \(U_\theta(x)\ge U_\theta(x;\phi)\) for all positive-mass \(\theta,\phi\), and Pareto-optimality among feasible mass lotteries with respect to \((U_\theta)_{\mu_\theta>0}\). Let \(W_\mu(x)=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta U_\theta(x)\).

Target. Is deciding whether such an \(x\) with \(W_\mu(x)\ge K\) exists NP-hard, or does it admit a polynomial-time algorithm?

Known results. For finite agents with partition-based utilities, deciding whether an ex-ante envy-free and Pareto-optimal allocation lottery of social welfare at least \(K\) exists is NP-complete.

Complexity classification

Theorem

Under the standard explicit rational encoding of templates, roles, masses, supplies, utilities, and \(K\), the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even when every type has equal mass and every role has equal supply.

Thus it does not admit a polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Source problem

Consider the supplied finite-agent problem with agents \(N=[n]\), admissible partitions \(k\in[m]\), and \(n\) bundles \(b\in[n]\) in every partition. Let \(u^i_{k,b}\) be agent \(i\)'s utility for bundle \(b\) in partition \(k\).

A finite allocation lottery has succinct marginal variables \(p_k\) and \(q_{kib}\), where \(q_{kib}\) is the probability that partition \(k\) is selected and agent \(i\) receives bundle \(b\). They satisfy

\(\sum_b q_{kib}=p_k\), \(\sum_i q_{kib}=p_k\), and \(\sum_k p_k=1\).

The expected own and comparison utilities are

\(V_i(q)=\sum_{k,b}u^i_{k,b}q_{kib}\)

and

\(V_i(q;j)=\sum_{k,b}u^i_{k,b}q_{kjb}\).

Its social welfare is \(\operatorname{SW}(q)=\sum_iV_i(q)\). This is exactly the succinct lottery representation used in the finite partition-based model, whose welfare-threshold decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete by Theorem 5.1 of the [AAMAS 2024 paper](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p244.pdf).

2. Polynomial-time reduction

Given a finite instance and threshold \(K_{\mathrm{fin}}\), construct the following mass instance:

  • The type set is \(\Theta=N\).
  • Every type has mass \(\mu_i=1/n\).
  • The templates and roles are unchanged.
  • Every role has supply \(\sigma_{k,b}=1/n\).
  • Set \(u^i_{k,b}\) equal to the finite agent's utility.
  • Set \(K_{\mathrm{mass}}=K_{\mathrm{fin}}/n\).

For a finite lottery \((p,q)\), define

\(x_{kib}=q_{kib}/n\).

Then

\(\sum_bx_{kib}=(1/n)\sum_bq_{kib}=p_k/n=p_k\mu_i\),

and

\(\sum_ix_{kib}=(1/n)\sum_iq_{kib}=p_k/n=p_k\sigma_{k,b}\).

Hence \((p,x)\) is a feasible mass lottery.

Conversely, from a feasible mass lottery define

\(q_{kib}=n x_{kib}\).

The mass constraints imply

\(\sum_bq_{kib}=p_k\)

and

\(\sum_iq_{kib}=p_k\).

Thus \((p,q)\) satisfies the finite lottery constraints.

For completeness, these marginal constraints really do describe distributions over deterministic assignments. If \(p_k>0\), the matrix \(A^k\) defined by \(A^k_{ib}=q_{kib}/p_k\) is doubly stochastic. By the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem, it is a convex combination of permutation matrices. Multiplying the corresponding coefficients by \(p_k\) gives a distribution over deterministic assignments in partition \(k\). If \(p_k=0\), nonnegativity and the marginal constraints force \(q_{kib}=0\).

Therefore the maps \(q\mapsto x=q/n\) and \(x\mapsto q=nx\) give an exact correspondence between the two feasible-lottery polytopes.

3. Preservation of envy-freeness

Because \(\mu_i=1/n\),

\(U_i(x)=n\sum_{k,b}u^i_{k,b}x_{kib}=\sum_{k,b}u^i_{k,b}q_{kib}=V_i(q)\).

Similarly, for every \(i,j\),

\(U_i(x;j)=n\sum_{k,b}u^i_{k,b}x_{kjb}=\sum_{k,b}u^i_{k,b}q_{kjb}=V_i(q;j)\).

Consequently,

\(U_i(x)\ge U_i(x;j)\)

holds exactly when

\(V_i(q)\ge V_i(q;j)\).

Thus the mass lottery is envy-free if and only if the corresponding finite lottery is ex-ante envy-free.

4. Preservation of Pareto-optimality

The correspondence preserves every coordinate of the own-utility vector:

\((U_i(x))_{i\in[n]}=(V_i(q))_{i\in[n]}\).

Moreover, it is surjective in both directions on feasible lotteries. Therefore a feasible mass lottery \(y\) Pareto-dominates \(x\) if and only if its corresponding finite lottery Pareto-dominates the finite lottery corresponding to \(x\).

Hence \(x\) is Pareto-optimal among feasible mass lotteries if and only if \(q\) is ex-ante Pareto-optimal among finite allocation lotteries.

This is the critical point: splitting a type of mass \(1/n\) does not introduce utility vectors unavailable to the finite lottery. It merely records that finite agent's assignment probabilities.

5. Preservation of the welfare threshold

The mass welfare is

\(W_\mu(x)=\sum_i\mu_iU_i(x)=\frac1n\sum_iV_i(q)=\frac1n\operatorname{SW}(q)\).

It follows that

\(W_\mu(x)\ge K_{\mathrm{fin}}/n\)

if and only if

\(\operatorname{SW}(q)\ge K_{\mathrm{fin}}\).

The construction is polynomial-time: only the rational numbers \(1/n\) and \(K_{\mathrm{fin}}/n\) are introduced.

Thus the finite instance is a yes-instance if and only if the constructed mass instance is a yes-instance. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

6. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Given a rational candidate \((p,x)\), feasibility, envy-freeness, and the welfare threshold are checkable by exact rational arithmetic.

Pareto-optimality can also be checked in polynomial time. Let \(\Theta^+=\{\theta:\mu_\theta>0\}\), and solve the linear program

\(\max_y\sum_{\theta\in\Theta^+}U_\theta(y)\)

subject to \(y\) being a feasible mass lottery and

\(U_\theta(y)\ge U_\theta(x)\) for every \(\theta\in\Theta^+\).

The candidate \(x\) itself is feasible for this linear program. Hence \(x\) is Pareto-optimal exactly when its optimum equals \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta^+}U_\theta(x)\): a larger optimum is equivalent to weak improvement in every coordinate and strict improvement in at least one coordinate.

It remains to justify that every yes-instance has a polynomial-size rational witness. Let \(P\) be the rational feasible-lottery polytope and let \(C=U(P)\) be its utility polytope. Suppose a possibly real witness \(x^\ast\) exists, and let \(G\) be the minimal face of \(C\) containing \(U(x^\ast)\). Then \(U(x^\ast)\) lies in the relative interior of \(G\).

Every point of \(G\) is Pareto-optimal. Indeed, suppose \(g\in G\) were dominated by \(v\in C\). Relative interiority gives some \(\varepsilon>0\) such that

\(h=U(x^\ast)+\varepsilon(U(x^\ast)-g)\in G\).

With \(\alpha=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)\), convexity gives

\((1-\alpha)h+\alpha v=U(x^\ast)+\alpha(v-g)\in C\),

which would Pareto-dominate \(U(x^\ast)\), a contradiction.

Because \(G\), \(P\), and all envy and threshold constraints are rational polyhedral, their nonempty intersection has a rational basic feasible point of polynomial encoding length. Its utility lies in \(G\), and is therefore Pareto-optimal. Thus a polynomial-size certificate always exists.

The problem is consequently in \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness proof.

Overall assessment

Accordingly, the proof is best classified as proved with minor gaps. The target question is resolved: the mass problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, so it cannot have a polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Step-by-step audit

1. Source problem

The cited theorem states that, for partition-based utilities and \(K>0\), deciding the existence of an ex-ante envy-free, Pareto-optimal lottery with social welfare at least \(K\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. This agrees with the known result supplied in the task and with [Theorem 5.1 of the cited paper](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p244.pdf).

The finite marginal constraints written by the prover also match the paper’s succinct representation:

  • every agent receives one bundle conditional on template \(k\);
  • every bundle is assigned to one agent;
  • the template probabilities sum to one.

The source theorem assumes \(K>0\). This is sufficient for the reduction and therefore does not restrict the target problem’s hardness.

2. Feasible-lottery correspondence

The scaling \(x_{kib}=q_{kib}/n\) is correct.

For each \(i,k\),

\[ \sum_b x_{kib} = \frac1n\sum_b q_{kib} = \frac{p_k}{n} = p_k\mu_i. \]

For each \(k,b\),

\[ \sum_i x_{kib} = \frac1n\sum_i q_{kib} = \frac{p_k}{n} = p_k\sigma_{k,b}. \]

The inverse scaling \(q_{kib}=nx_{kib}\) recovers the finite marginal constraints. Nonnegativity and \(\sum_kp_k=1\) are preserved.

The Birkhoff–von Neumann application is valid: when \(p_k>0\), \(q_{kib}/p_k\) is a nonnegative square matrix whose row and column sums are one. When \(p_k=0\), the row-sum constraints and nonnegativity indeed force every \(q_{kib}=0\).

Thus the correspondence between feasible finite lotteries and feasible mass lotteries is exact.

3. Envy-freeness

With \(\mu_i=1/n\), both own utility and comparison utility are preserved exactly:

\[ U_i(x)=V_i(q),\qquad U_i(x;j)=V_i(q;j). \]

Consequently, every envy inequality in one formulation is identical to the corresponding inequality in the other formulation. This step is correct.

4. Pareto-optimality

The correspondence is bijective on feasible marginal lotteries and preserves the complete own-utility vector coordinatewise. Therefore Pareto domination exists on one side exactly when it exists on the other side.

The conclusion that Pareto-optimality is preserved is correct. No additional assumption about envy-freeness is improperly introduced into the Pareto comparison.

5. Welfare threshold

The calculation

\[ W_\mu(x) = \frac1n\sum_iV_i(q) = \frac1n\operatorname{SW}(q) \]

is correct. Hence choosing \(K_{\mathrm{mass}}=K_{\mathrm{fin}}/n\) preserves yes-instances and no-instances.

The introduced rational numbers have polynomial encoding length. The reduction therefore proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness, including under equal masses and equal role supplies.

6. Verification of a supplied certificate

For a rational candidate \((p,x)\), feasibility, envy-freeness and the welfare threshold are systems of rational linear equalities or inequalities and are polynomial-time checkable.

The proposed Pareto-optimality linear program is also correct. Under the constraints \(U_\theta(y)\ge U_\theta(x)\), its objective exceeds \(\sum_\theta U_\theta(x)\) exactly when at least one coordinate is strictly improved. Exact rational linear programming permits polynomial-time comparison of the optimum with the stated rational value.

7. Existence of a polynomial-size certificate

The geometric part of the argument is correct:

  • \(U(x^\ast)\) lies in the relative interior of its minimal face \(G\).
  • If \(g\in G\) were dominated by \(v\in C\), the displayed construction would produce a point of \(C\) dominating \(U(x^\ast)\).
  • The convex-combination calculation is correct because, for \(\alpha=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)\),

\[ (1-\alpha)h+\alpha v = U(x^\ast)+\alpha(v-g). \]

Thus every point of \(G\) is Pareto-optimal.

There are nevertheless two related expositional gaps:

  • \(G\subseteq C\) lies in utility space, while \(P\) and the envy and threshold constraints concern lottery-variable space. Their literal “intersection” is not defined. The proof must explicitly refer to the set of lotteries \(y\in P\) satisfying \(U(y)\in G\), together with the envy and threshold constraints.
  • Rational polyhedrality alone does not imply a polynomial encoding-length basic feasible point relative to the original input. The prover must state or cite the applicable facet-complexity or rational-polyhedron bit-size theorem and verify that a face of the rational linear image \(C=U(P)\), and its inverse image under \(U\), have coefficient complexity polynomially bounded in the original instance size.

These are minor because they concern a standard polyhedral bit-complexity justification rather than a new mathematical idea, but they are necessary to make the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-membership argument fully verifiable.

Conclusion

The \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness reduction is rigorous and complete. The stronger \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness claim is mathematically credible and sound in substance, subject only to the stated polynomial-bit-complexity clarification.
