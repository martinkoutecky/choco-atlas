The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of original voter types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), each type’s strict ranking of acceptable partner types, and a rational per-capita budget \(B\), is there a redistribution \(z_{t,r}\) of each type’s mass among rankings reachable by adjacent swaps, together with a mass matching, such that the matching is stable and the total swap cost \(\sum_{t,r} z_{t,r} d_K(\succ_t,r)\) is at most \(B\)? Stability means that for every mutually acceptable pair of post-intervention types, the mass on at least one side who are unmatched or prefer that type to their assigned partner is zero.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) original types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible rankings. Each type has a strict ranking \(\succ_t\) of \(A(t)\subseteq T\setminus\{t\}\), with \(u\in A(t)\iff t\in A(u)\). There is no bipartition requirement. Let \(m=\max_t|A(t)|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the binary-rational budget \(B\geq0\).
An intervention chooses nonnegative real masses \(z_{t,r}\) for every permutation \(r\) of \(A(t)\), with \(\sum_r z_{t,r}=\mu_t\). Acceptability remains fixed; every type may be modified. Its cost is \(\sum_{t,r}z_{t,r}d_K(\succ_t,r)\), where \(d_K\) is the minimum number of unit-cost adjacent swaps.
A post-intervention type is \(a=(t,r)\), with original label \(o(a)=t\). It ranks partners by their original labels using \(r\), and is indifferent between different post-intervention types with the same original label; every acceptable partner is preferred to being unmatched.
A mass matching consists of nonnegative real numbers \(x_{ab}=x_{ba}\), with \(x_{ab}=0\) unless \(o(b)\in A(o(a))\), and \(\sum_bx_{ab}\leq z_a\). Thus \(x_{ab}\) is the mass assigned on each side of the pair, and \(h_a=z_a-\sum_bx_{ab}\) is unmatched mass. Perfectness is not required.
For an acceptable pair \(a=(t,r),b=(u,s)\), define \(D_a(b)=h_a+\sum_{c:\,u\succ_r o(c)}x_{ac}\). Stability requires \(D_a(b)=0\) or \(D_b(a)=0\) for every such pair.

Is deciding whether an intervention of cost at most \(B\) admits a stable mass matching solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

For finite agents with strict preferences and symmetric acceptability, obtaining any stable roommates matching within a unit-cost adjacent-swap budget is NP-complete ([Chen–Schlotter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01215)).
Ordinally stable fractional roommates matchings always exist with unit agent capacities ([Chen–Roy–Sorge](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.12259)); this is a nearby result, without arbitrary input masses or swap interventions. No result settling the precise decision problem above was located.

Result

Every valid instance admits a stable mass matching without any intervention. Consequently, the minimum intervention cost is \(0\), and the decision problem is solvable in \(O(L)\) time, uniformly in \(m\) and \(\tau\).

The substantive point is that unit-capacity fractional existence extends to arbitrary rational masses. The following cloning and aggregation argument proves this extension.

The unit-capacity existence theorem

We use the following known result: every finite graph with symmetric acceptability and strict preferences admits a symmetric fractional matching \(y\), with \(\sum_j y_{ij}\leq 1\), such that every acceptable pair \(i,j\) satisfies

\[ F_i(j)=0\quad\text{or}\quad F_j(i)=0, \qquad F_i(j):=1-\sum_k y_{ik}+\sum_{k:\,j\succ_i k}y_{ik}. \]

Indeed, \(F_i(j)=0\) means that \(i\)'s entire capacity is assigned to partners weakly preferred to \(j\); this is precisely ordinal stability. Existence follows from Proposition 3.5 and Lemma 3.6 of [Chen–Roy–Sorge](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.12259). Isolated vertices can be removed before applying the theorem and then restored with their entire capacity unmatched.

Extending existence to rational masses

Let \(Q\) be a positive integer such that every \(n_t:=Q\mu_t\) is an integer. Such an integer exists because the masses are rational. For each original type \(t\), introduce a set \(C_t\) of \(n_t\) clones, each with unit capacity.

A clone of \(t\) accepts every clone of every \(u\in A(t)\). This acceptability relation is symmetric. Its strict preference order lists the groups of clones according to \(\succ_t\), breaking ties within each group arbitrarily. Thus every clone of a better original label precedes every clone of a worse original label.

Apply the unit-capacity theorem to obtain an ordinally stable fractional matching \(y\) on the clones. Write the unmatched capacity of clone \(i\) as

\[ \eta_i:=1-\sum_j y_{ij}. \]

Aggregate this matching by original labels:

\[ X_{tu}:=\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{i\in C_t}\sum_{j\in C_u}y_{ij}. \]

These values are nonnegative, symmetric, and zero on unacceptable pairs. Moreover,

\[ \sum_u X_{tu} =\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{i\in C_t}\sum_j y_{ij} \leq \frac{n_t}{Q} =\mu_t. \]

The unmatched mass of \(t\) is therefore

\[ H_t:=\mu_t-\sum_u X_{tu} =\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{i\in C_t}\eta_i. \]

We now prove stability of the aggregated matching.

For an acceptable original pair \(t,u\), define its aggregate dissatisfaction at \(t\) by

\[ D_t(u):=H_t+\sum_{v:\,u\succ_t v}X_{tv}. \]

For a clone \(i\in C_t\), define

\[ G_i(u):=\eta_i+ \sum_{v:\,u\succ_t v}\sum_{k\in C_v}y_{ik}. \]

The aggregation identities give

\[ D_t(u)=\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{i\in C_t}G_i(u). \]

All summands are nonnegative. Hence, if \(D_t(u)>0\), some clone \(i\in C_t\) has \(G_i(u)>0\).

For every clone \(j\in C_u\), the preference construction places \(j\) above all clones whose labels are worse than \(u\) in \(\succ_t\). Consequently,

\[ F_i(j)\geq G_i(u). \]

Suppose, for contradiction, that an acceptable pair \(t,u\) satisfies both \(D_t(u)>0\) and \(D_u(t)>0\). Choose clones \(i\in C_t\) and \(j\in C_u\) with \(G_i(u)>0\) and \(G_j(t)>0\). They form an acceptable clone pair, and the preceding inequality yields

\[ F_i(j)>0 \quad\text{and}\quad F_j(i)>0. \]

This contradicts ordinal stability of \(y\). Thus every acceptable original pair satisfies

\[ D_t(u)=0\quad\text{or}\quad D_u(t)=0. \]

This proves existence of a stable mass matching for the original rankings and arbitrary rational input masses.

A zero-cost feasible intervention

For each \(t\), let \(a_t=(t,\succ_t)\) denote its unchanged post-intervention type. Set

\[ z_{a_t}=\mu_t, \qquad z_{t,r}=0\ \text{for }r\neq\succ_t, \]

and set \(x_{a_ta_u}=X_{tu}\), with every other matching entry zero.

The aggregation proof establishes stability for pairs of unchanged types. Every other pair contains a zero-mass post-intervention type; that endpoint has zero unmatched mass and zero incident matching mass, so its dissatisfaction is zero. Therefore stability holds for every acceptable post-intervention pair required by the question.

The intervention cost is

\[ \sum_t\mu_t\,d_K(\succ_t,\succ_t)=0. \]

Since \(B\geq 0\), this intervention meets the budget. Nonnegativity of costs also proves that the optimum is exactly \(0\).

Running time and the finite-agent comparison

The decision algorithm returns YES on every valid input. It takes \(O(1)\) time on the promised domain, or \(O(L)\) time when reading the input is included. This bound holds for every \(m\) and \(\tau\).

The clone expansion is an existence proof; its potentially exponential size is not incurred by the decision algorithm. No permutations need to be enumerated.

The finite-agent adjacent-swap hardness result concerns integral matchings. Here, allowing fractional mass assignments already guarantees existence under the unchanged preferences, including for unequal masses.
