The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given supported buyer types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), resource classes \(A\) with rational per-capita supplies \(q_a\), and the paper's integer valuation and price-bound data, find a constrained Walrasian equilibrium. Output integer prices \(p\), rationing-state masses \(\lambda_{t,S}\) for \(S\subseteq A\), and assignment masses \(x_{t,S,a}\) for \(a\in A\cup\{o\}\). Require \(\sum_S\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_a x_{t,S,a}=\lambda_{t,S}\), and \(x_{t,S,a}>0\) only if \(a\in D_t(p,S)=\arg\max_{b\in S\cup\{o\}}(u_t(b)-p_b)\). For each real resource \(a\), require \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S,a}\le q_a\), with equality whenever \(p_a>\underline p_a\). Whenever \(\lambda_{t,S}>0\) and \(a\notin S\), require \(p_a=\overline p_a\), positive total assignment mass to \(a\), and \(a\in D_t(p,S\cup\{a\})\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|T|\) buyer types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=|A|\) real resource classes with positive binary rational per-capita supplies \(q_a\), and an always available dummy \(o\) with unlimited supply.
Each buyer receives at most one resource. Input valuations \(u_t(a)\) and bounds \(0\le\underline p_a\le\overline p_a\) are nonnegative integers encoded in binary; \(u_t(o)=\underline p_o=\overline p_o=0\). Buyers can afford every admissible price. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A rationing state \(S\subseteq A\) permits exactly \(S\cup\{o\}\); define \(D_t(p,S)=\arg\max_{b\in S\cup\{o\}}(u_t(b)-p_b)\), retaining all ties.
A constrained Walrasian equilibrium consists of integer prices \(\underline p_a\le p_a\le\overline p_a\), \(p_o=0\), and nonnegative rational masses \(\lambda_{t,S},x_{t,S,a}\), represented by their nonzero entries, with omitted entries zero.
Require \(\sum_{S\subseteq A}\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{a\in A\cup\{o\}}x_{t,S,a}=\lambda_{t,S}\), and \(x_{t,S,a}>0\Rightarrow a\in D_t(p,S)\). Types may split across rationing states and assignments.
Writing \(y_a=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S,a}\), require \(y_a\le q_a\) for every real class, with equality whenever \(p_a>\underline p_a\).
For every \(\lambda_{t,S}>0\) and \(a\in A\setminus S\), require \(p_a=\overline p_a\), \(y_a>0\), and \(a\in D_t(p,S\cup\{a\})\).

Is finding such a constrained Walrasian equilibrium solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite buyers and individually supplied items, a terminating rationing auction yields a constrained Walrasian equilibrium; its stated running time is polynomial in the buyer count, item count, and \(\sum_a(\overline p_a-\underline p_a)\), hence pseudopolynomial for binary bounds. [Huang, Lou and Wen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.6573).
No result settling the exact supported-mass problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Supplies had no stated sign restriction; real resource classes are required to have positive supplies, corresponding to resources actually present.
  • The integer data are nonnegative and binary encoded, with ordered price bounds, zero dummy valuation and price, and no binding affordability constraint, as in the discrete model.
  • Demand retains all utility ties; the dummy is always permitted and has unlimited supply.
  • Output masses are nonnegative binary rationals represented sparsely, with omitted entries zero, so the representation does not require listing every possible rationing state.

Result

Finding an equilibrium as defined in the question is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Every admissible instance has such an equilibrium, with at most \(\tau(m+1)\) nonzero assignment entries and at most \(\tau(m+1)\) nonzero rationing-state masses.

The construction solves an assignment linear program incorporating the price floors, obtains integer supporting prices, and clips those prices at the ceilings. Buyer-specific rationing then supports the unchanged allocation.

1. An assignment linear program incorporating the floors

For every \(t\in T\) and \(a\in A\), define the integer adjusted valuation

\(\displaystyle v_{ta}=u_t(a)-\underline p_a.\)

These adjusted valuations may be negative. Consider the primal linear program

\(\displaystyle \text{maximize }\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{a\in A}v_{ta}z_{ta}\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle z_{ta}\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{a\in A}z_{ta}\le\mu_t\quad(t\in T),\qquad \sum_{t\in T}z_{ta}\le q_a\quad(a\in A).\)

Its dual is

\(\displaystyle \text{minimize }\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t U_t+\sum_{a\in A}q_a r_a\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle U_t\ge0,\qquad r_a\ge0,\qquad U_t+r_a\ge v_{ta}\quad(t\in T,\ a\in A).\)

The primal is feasible, since the zero allocation is feasible. Its feasible region is bounded because \(0\le z_{ta}\le\mu_t\). The dual is feasible: take \(r_a=0\) and \(U_t=\max(\{0\}\cup\{v_{ta}:a\in A\})\).

Consequently, strong linear-programming duality gives optimal rational solutions \(z\) and \((U,r)\) with equal objective values. Define

\(\displaystyle z_{to}=\mu_t-\sum_{a\in A}z_{ta},\qquad y_a=\sum_{t\in T}z_{ta}.\)

Thus \(z_{to}\ge0\) and \(\sum_{b\in A\cup\{o\}}z_{tb}=\mu_t\).

For completeness, the dual objective minus the primal objective equals

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}U_tz_{to} +\sum_{a\in A}r_a(q_a-y_a) +\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{a\in A}z_{ta}(U_t+r_a-v_{ta}). \)

Every summand is nonnegative. Optimality makes their sum zero, so

  • \(z_{ta}>0\) implies \(U_t+r_a=v_{ta}\);
  • \(z_{to}>0\) implies \(U_t=0\);
  • \(r_a>0\) implies \(y_a=q_a\).

2. Integer supporting prices by explicit rounding

Define

\(\displaystyle \widehat U_t=\lceil U_t\rceil,\qquad \widehat r_a=\lfloor r_a\rfloor.\)

Both are nonnegative integers.

Feasibility is preserved. For every pair \(t,a\),

\(\displaystyle \widehat U_t+\widehat r_a>U_t+r_a-1\ge v_{ta}-1. \)

Since \(\widehat U_t+\widehat r_a\) and \(v_{ta}\) are integers, this implies

\(\displaystyle \widehat U_t+\widehat r_a\ge v_{ta}.\)

Every used assignment edge remains tight. If \(z_{ta}>0\), then \(U_t+r_a=v_{ta}\), and hence

\(\displaystyle \widehat U_t+\widehat r_a =\lceil U_t\rceil+\lfloor v_{ta}-U_t\rfloor =v_{ta}. \)

Also, \(z_{to}>0\) implies \(\widehat U_t=0\). Finally, \(\widehat r_a>0\) implies \(r_a>0\), so \(y_a=q_a\). Thus the rounded dual satisfies the same complementary-slackness conditions with \(z\); in particular, it is also optimal.

Define integer auxiliary prices

\(\displaystyle \widetilde p_a=\underline p_a+\widehat r_a\quad(a\in A),\qquad \widetilde p_o=0.\)

For every real resource,

\(\displaystyle u_t(a)-\widetilde p_a =v_{ta}-\widehat r_a \le\widehat U_t, \)

with equality whenever \(z_{ta}>0\). For the dummy, its net utility is zero, which is at most \(\widehat U_t\), with equality whenever \(z_{to}>0\).

Therefore, whenever \(z_{tb}>0\), resource \(b\) maximizes type \(t\)'s net utility at the auxiliary prices over all resources, including the dummy.

3. Clip prices and construct rationing states

Set the final prices to

\(\displaystyle p_a=\min\{\widetilde p_a,\overline p_a\}\quad(a\in A),\qquad p_o=0. \)

These prices are integers and satisfy all bounds: \(\widetilde p_a\ge\underline p_a\) and \(\overline p_a\ge\underline p_a\).

Write

\(\displaystyle w_t(b)=u_t(b)-p_b\quad(b\in A\cup\{o\}).\)

For every pair \(t,b\) with \(z_{tb}>0\), clipping can only increase \(b\)'s net utility, so

\(\displaystyle w_t(b)\ge u_t(b)-\widetilde p_b=\widehat U_t\ge0.\)

For such a pair, define the rationing state

\(\displaystyle S_{tb}=\{a\in A:w_t(a)\le w_t(b)\}.\)

This state permits every real resource whose net utility is no greater than that of the assigned resource.

If \(b\) is real, then \(b\in S_{tb}\). Every permitted real resource has net utility at most \(w_t(b)\), and the dummy has net utility zero. Hence

\(\displaystyle b\in D_t(p,S_{tb}).\)

We next verify all requirements for excluded resources. Suppose \(a\notin S_{tb}\). Then

\(\displaystyle w_t(a)>w_t(b)\ge\widehat U_t.\)

If \(\widetilde p_a\le\overline p_a\), clipping would leave its price unchanged, giving

\(\displaystyle w_t(a)=u_t(a)-\widetilde p_a\le\widehat U_t, \)

a contradiction. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \widetilde p_a>\overline p_a,\qquad p_a=\overline p_a.\)

Moreover,

\(\displaystyle \widehat r_a=\widetilde p_a-\underline p_a >\overline p_a-\underline p_a\ge0. \)

Complementary slackness therefore gives \(y_a=q_a>0\).

Finally, every member of \(S_{tb}\cup\{o\}\) has net utility at most \(w_t(b)\), whereas \(w_t(a)>w_t(b)\). Thus adding \(a\) makes it the unique maximizer:

\(\displaystyle D_t(p,S_{tb}\cup\{a\})=\{a\}.\)

This proves every required condition for excluding \(a\).

4. Assemble the required sparse representation

For every positive \(z_{tb}\), place that mass in rationing state \(S_{tb}\), assigned to resource \(b\). Explicitly, define

\(\displaystyle x_{t,S,b}= \begin{cases} z_{tb},&z_{tb}>0\text{ and }S=S_{tb},\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad \lambda_{t,S}=\sum_{b\in A\cup\{o\}}x_{t,S,b}. \)

States shared by multiple assignments are automatically combined.

For every type,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq A}\lambda_{t,S} =\sum_{b\in A\cup\{o\}}z_{tb} =\mu_t. \)

The state-level mass equalities hold by the definition of \(\lambda\). Every positive assignment belongs to the corresponding demand set by Section 3.

The total sales of each real resource remain exactly \(y_a\), so \(y_a\le q_a\). If \(p_a>\underline p_a\), then also \(\widetilde p_a>\underline p_a\), giving \(\widehat r_a>0\) and therefore \(y_a=q_a\).

Finally, if \(\lambda_{t,S}>0\), some positive \(z_{tb}\) generated that state. Section 3 then proves, for every \(a\notin S\),

\(\displaystyle p_a=\overline p_a,\qquad y_a>0,\qquad a\in D_t(p,S\cup\{a\}). \)

All equilibrium conditions are satisfied, including the retention of ties in every demand set.

5. Polynomial running time and output length

The only general algorithmic theorem used is polynomial-time solvability of rational linear programming: a rational LP with a finite attained optimum admits an exact optimal rational solution computable in time polynomial in its encoding length. Such solutions have polynomial encoding length. This is the standard bit-complexity consequence of [polynomial-time linear programming](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Here the primal has \(\tau m\) variables and \(\tau+m\) resource and type constraints. The dual has \(\tau+m\) variables and \(\tau m\) pair constraints. Their encoding lengths are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and feasibility and bounded optimal value were checked in Section 1.

Rounding, clipping, and computing the rationing states require polynomial-time operations on numbers of polynomial bit length. Constructing all states uses at most \(O(\tau m(m+1))\) comparisons.

There are at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive entries \(z_{tb}\). Each generates one assignment entry and at most one rationing state. A state is encoded by an \(m\)-bit characteristic vector. The resulting rational masses, including sums defining \(\lambda\), have polynomial bit length.

Thus the algorithm computes an exact constrained Walrasian equilibrium in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), without expanding the population or iterating through numeric price spans.
