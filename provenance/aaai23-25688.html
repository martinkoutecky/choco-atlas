The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite catalogue of indivisible item types G with rational per-capita supplies q, a finite set of valuation and priority types Θ with rational masses μ, all prioritized types sharing valuation vP, and an explicit or queryable family of integral bundles B, find a complete mass allocation y satisfying agent-type mass conservation and item-supply constraints such that every pair of bundles in its support satisfies EF1 and every prioritized supported type weakly prefers its bundle to every non-prioritized supported bundle.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a catalogue of \(m\) indivisible item types with per-capita supplies \(q\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{m}\). A society is given by its support: the list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) valuation-and-priority types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a priority flag; write \(P\subseteq\Theta\) for the prioritized types. The admissible bundles are an explicitly listed finite family \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\), containing \(0\), with binary-encoded coordinates; multiple copies of an item type are permitted.
Put \(D=\{0\}\cup\mathcal B\cup\{b-e_g:b\in\mathcal B,\ b_g>0\}\), where \(e_g\) is the unit vector for item type \(g\). Each valuation \(v_\theta:D\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) is explicitly tabulated, normalized by \(v_\theta(0)=0\), and coordinatewise monotone. All types in \(P\) share one valuation \(v_P\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
A complete mass allocation consists of rational numbers \(y_{\theta,b}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}y_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\) for every \(\theta\), and \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}b_g y_{\theta,b}=q_g\) for every \(g\).
For every ordered pair \((\theta,b),(\phi,c)\) with \(y_{\theta,b}>0\) and \(y_{\phi,c}>0\), require EF1: either \(c=0\), or some \(g\) with \(c_g>0\) satisfies \(v_\theta(b)\ge v_\theta(c-e_g)\). Additionally, whenever \(\theta\in P\) and \(\phi\notin P\), require \(v_P(b)\ge v_P(c)\). These conditions apply also to different bundles assigned to the same type.

Can such a complete mass allocation be found, or infeasibility decided, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|\mathcal B|,L\), or is its existence decision problem NP-hard?

For finite agents with normalized monotone valuations and unrestricted subsets of the available items as bundles, a complete EF1 allocation with no envy from prioritized to non-prioritized agents always exists and is computable in polynomial time when prioritized agents share a valuation. [Discrete result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.16143)
No result settling this exact mass-allocation formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Selected the explicitly listed bundle-family option; an unspecified query interface does not define a binary-input complexity problem.
  • Normalized total society mass to one, so the given supplies are per capita; allowed nonnegative rational supplies.
  • Interpreted integral bundles as nonnegative integer count vectors, permitting multiple copies of an item type, and included the empty bundle among admissible bundles.
  • Carried over nonnegative, normalized, monotone valuations and chose explicit binary-rational tables on all bundles needed for fairness comparisons, including single-item deletions.
  • Interpreted completeness as exact supply conservation, with no disposal of items.
  • Required fairness for every ordered pair of positive-mass type-bundle assignments, including assignments within the same type, rather than comparing expected utilities.
  • Used the standard empty-target-bundle convention for EF1; the literal existential-removal definition would contradict the stated validity of empty partial allocations.
  • Used weak inequalities for priority comparisons, allowed either priority class to be empty, and imposed no tie-breaking rule.
  • Made infeasibility an allowed outcome because an input bundle catalogue need not admit a complete allocation; chose rational allocation masses.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The existence decision problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when there is exactly one prioritized type, every bundle coordinate belongs to \(\{0,2\}\), every valuation value belongs to \(\{0,1,2\}\), and all masses and supplies are dyadic rationals.

Consequently, an algorithm that always finds a complete allocation or decides infeasibility in the requested polynomial time would imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).

1. Source problem and construction

We reduce from Exact Cover by 3-Sets. Its input consists of a universe \(U\), with \(|U|=3k\), and sets \(S_1,\ldots,S_n\subseteq U\), each of cardinality three. The question is whether some subcollection contains each element of \(U\) exactly once.

We use the standard \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness of this problem. For hardness, it suffices to consider instances obtained from perfect three-dimensional matching: take the disjoint union of its three ground sets as \(U\), and replace each allowed triple by its three-element set. Exact covers then correspond precisely to perfect matchings. Perfect three-dimensional matching is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete. [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9)

Assume \(n\ge1\). Introduce mutually distinct item types

\[ G=\{a,p_1,\ldots,p_n\}\,\dot\cup\,U. \]

Here \(a\) is an anchor item type and \(p_i\) is private to set \(S_i\). Define bundles

\[ A=2e_a,\qquad L_i=2e_{p_i},\qquad H_i=2e_{p_i}+2\sum_{u\in S_i}e_u, \]

and let

\[ \mathcal B=\{0,A\}\cup\{L_i,H_i:1\le i\le n\}. \]

Choose the smallest integer \(\ell\) such that \(2^\ell>n\), and put \(\delta=2^{-\ell}\). There are types \(\theta_0,\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n\), with

\[ P=\{\theta_0\},\qquad \mu_{\theta_0}=1-n\delta,\qquad \mu_{\theta_i}=\delta\quad(1\le i\le n). \]

These masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. Set supplies to

\[ q_a=2(1-n\delta),\qquad q_{p_i}=2\delta,\qquad q_u=2\delta\quad(u\in U). \]

Valuations

Write \(\mathbf1[E]\) for the indicator of a predicate \(E\). Define the following functions on all nonnegative integer vectors, and explicitly tabulate their restrictions to the required domain \(D\):

\[ v_{\theta_0}(d)=\mathbf1[d_a>0], \]

and, for \(1\le i\le n\),

\[ v_{\theta_i}(d)= \max\left\{ \mathbf1[d_a>0],\ \mathbf1[d_{p_i}>0] +\mathbf1\left[d_{p_i}>0\ \wedge\ \sum_{u\in U}d_u>0\right] \right\}. \]

Every predicate appearing here is preserved when coordinates increase. Each indicator is therefore coordinatewise monotone, as are their sum and maximum. Thus the tabulated valuations satisfy the required monotonicity. They are normalized at zero and take values only in \(\{0,1,2\}\).

The following consequences will be used:

  • Every type values \(A\) and \(A-e_a\) at \(1\).
  • Among admissible bundles, \(\theta_0\) values only \(A\) positively.
  • Among admissible bundles, \(\theta_i\) values precisely \(A,L_i,H_i\) positively, with values \(1,1,2\), respectively.
  • Every single-item deletion from \(H_i\) has value \(2\) to \(\theta_i\).

For the last assertion, deleting one item leaves the \(p_i\)-coordinate positive, since it initially equals two. It also leaves a positive universe coordinate, since \(H_i\) contains two copies of each of three universe item types. Both indicators in the second argument of the maximum consequently remain one.

Encoding size

There are \(m=n+1+3k\) item types, \(\tau=n+1\) society types, and \(|\mathcal B|=2n+2\) admissible bundles.

Each \(L_i\) has one deletion vector, each \(H_i\) has four, and \(A\) has one. Hence \(|D|\le7n+3\). All vectors and valuation tables can be generated explicitly in polynomial time. Moreover, \(\ell\) is logarithmic in \(n+1\), so all masses and supplies have polynomial encoding length.

The constructed instance satisfies every input hypothesis, including the common-valuation requirement for prioritized types.

2. A feasible allocation determines an exact cover

Suppose a complete fair mass allocation \(y\) exists.

The prioritized type must receive the anchor bundle

Only \(A\) contains item type \(a\). Its supply equation gives

\[ \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}y_{\theta,A}=1-n\delta=\mu_{\theta_0}>0. \]

Thus some positive-mass type–bundle pair has target bundle \(A\).

Consider any bundle \(b\) assigned with positive mass to \(\theta_0\). EF1 against this target requires

\[ v_{\theta_0}(b)\ge v_{\theta_0}(A-e_a)=1, \]

because \(a\) is the only item type present in \(A\). Only \(A\) has positive value to \(\theta_0\), so its entire mass receives \(A\):

\[ y_{\theta_0,A}=\mu_{\theta_0}. \]

The anchor supply equation and nonnegativity now imply

\[ y_{\theta_i,A}=0\qquad(1\le i\le n). \]

Each remaining type receives only its own two bundles

For any positive-mass pair \((\theta_i,b)\), EF1 against the prioritized pair \((\theta_0,A)\) requires

\[ v_{\theta_i}(b)\ge v_{\theta_i}(A-e_a)=1. \]

The only admissible bundles meeting this inequality are \(A,L_i,H_i\). Since \(\theta_i\) cannot receive \(A\), it follows that

\[ y_{\theta_i,L_i}+y_{\theta_i,H_i}=\delta. \]

EF1 prevents splitting between those bundles

If both terms were positive, EF1 would apply from \((\theta_i,L_i)\) to \((\theta_i,H_i)\). But

\[ v_{\theta_i}(L_i)=1 <2=v_{\theta_i}(H_i-e_g) \]

for every item type \(g\) present in \(H_i\). No deletion could satisfy EF1.

Therefore, for each \(i\), exactly one of the following holds:

\[ (y_{\theta_i,L_i},y_{\theta_i,H_i}) =(\delta,0) \quad\text{or}\quad (0,\delta). \]

This is the step that forces a discrete choice despite allowing rational mass allocations.

The universe supplies enforce an exact cover

Let

\[ I=\{i:y_{\theta_i,H_i}=\delta\}. \]

For any \(u\in U\), the allocation uses precisely

\[ 2\delta\,|\{i\in I:u\in S_i\}| \]

units per capita of item type \(u\). Its required supply is \(2\delta\). Since \(\delta>0\), exact conservation implies

\[ |\{i\in I:u\in S_i\}|=1. \]

Thus \(\{S_i:i\in I\}\) is an exact cover.

3. An exact cover gives a feasible allocation

Conversely, suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) indexes an exact cover. Assign all prioritized mass to \(A\), and assign each other type entirely according to membership in \(I\):

\[ y_{\theta_0,A}=1-n\delta,\qquad y_{\theta_i,H_i}=\delta\ \text{if }i\in I,\qquad y_{\theta_i,L_i}=\delta\ \text{if }i\notin I, \]

with all other entries zero.

The type-mass equations hold by construction. The anchor and private-item supplies also hold. Each universe element occurs in exactly one selected set, so its supply is exactly \(2\delta\).

It remains to verify all ordered fairness comparisons.

  • A type compared with its own assigned bundle. Each type receives just one bundle. Monotonicity ensures that its value for this bundle is at least its value after deleting any item.
  • A non-prioritized type compared with \(A\). Its assigned bundle has value at least \(1\), while \(A-e_a\) has value \(1\).
  • Distinct non-prioritized types. For \(i\ne j\), every deletion from \(L_j\) or \(H_j\) has zero \(a\)- and \(p_i\)-coordinates. Its value to \(\theta_i\) is therefore zero.
  • The prioritized type compared with another type. Every other assigned bundle, and every deletion from it, has zero \(a\)-coordinate, hence value zero to \(\theta_0\).

These cases cover every ordered positive-mass pair and establish EF1. The additional priority condition holds because

\[ v_P(A)=1\ge0=v_P(L_i)=v_P(H_i). \]

The allocation is therefore complete and fair. Together with the preceding direction, this proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness.

4. Membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\)

A polynomial-size rational witness always exists whenever the instance is feasible.

To prove this, take any feasible allocation and let \(S\) be its positive support. Consider the polytope defined by the mass and supply equations, nonnegativity, and setting all variables outside \(S\) to zero.

This polytope is nonempty. It is bounded because every variable satisfies

\[ 0\le y_{\theta,b}\le\mu_\theta. \]

It therefore has a vertex. The positive support of that vertex is a subset of \(S\), so every fairness comparison it requires was already satisfied by the original allocation. Both EF1 and the priority condition are preserved.

The equality matrix has integer coefficients and at most \(m+\tau\) rows. At a vertex, the columns corresponding to positive variables are linearly independent: otherwise a nonzero dependence would permit sufficiently small positive and negative perturbations preserving all equations and nonnegativity, contradicting extremality.

Consequently, the positive coordinates are determined by a nonsingular square subsystem of order \(r\le m+\tau\). Clear the right-hand-side denominators using their product, whose binary length is at most the total input length \(L\). Cramer’s rule then expresses each coordinate as a ratio of integer determinants. Their binary lengths are bounded by \(O(r(L+\log r))\), using the determinant expansion bound. Thus the allocation has polynomial total encoding length.

Given such a certificate, exact rational arithmetic verifies the mass and supply equations. For every ordered positive-mass pair, checking at most \(m\) deletions verifies EF1, and direct table comparisons verify priority. All these checks take polynomial time.

Hence the decision problem belongs to \(\mathsf{NP}\), completing the proof of \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness.

5. Relation to the discrete existence theorem

The referenced discrete theorem guarantees a complete allocation when prioritized agents share a valuation and bundles are unrestricted subsets of the available items. [Bu et al., Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.16143)

Our construction restricts admissible bundles to the explicitly listed \(A,L_i,H_i\) and zero. The discrete theorem does not guarantee an allocation respecting this catalogue. The hardness result therefore does not contradict that theorem.
