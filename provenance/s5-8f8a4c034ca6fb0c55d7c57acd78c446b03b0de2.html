The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a distribution μ over τ receiving types, a known divergence matrix \(D\), and each type \(r\)'s limiting rate vector \(y_r\) and winning hypothesis θ_r^★, recover source-influence masses \(q_{sr}=μ_r x_{sr}>0\) satisfying \(B_r q_r=μ_r y_r\) and \(∑_s q_{sr}=μ_r\), where \(B_r=(\mathbf{1}_H e_{θ_r^★}^{\top}-I_H)D\); report the unique vector when identifiable, and otherwise report non-identifiability.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq2\) hypotheses and \(1\leq S\leq m\) sending components. The known matrix \(D\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^{m\times S}\) records finite KL divergences, with \(D_{\theta s}\) the divergence from component \(s\)'s true distribution to its likelihood for hypothesis \(\theta\); statistical models are homogeneous within each sending component.
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) receiving types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_r\), where \(\sum_r\mu_r=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type supplies an exact rational limiting log-belief rate vector \(y_r\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}\) and a winning hypothesis \(\theta_r^\star\), with \(y_r(\theta_r^\star)=0\) and \(y_r(\theta)<0\) for every other hypothesis; ties are excluded.
Define \(B_r=(\mathbf{1}_m e_{\theta_r^\star}^{\top}-I_m)D\), where \(e_\theta\) is a standard basis vector and \(\mathbf{1}_m\) is the all-ones vector. Inputs are promised consistent: for every type there exists \(x_r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{S}\) with \(B_rx_r=y_r\) and \(\sum_s x_{sr}=1\).
Source-influence masses are \(q_{sr}=\mu_r x_{sr}\). Recover the entire array \(q\) satisfying \(q_{sr}>0\), \(B_rq_r=\mu_ry_r\), and \(\sum_s q_{sr}=\mu_r\) if it is unique; otherwise report non-identifiability. No source totals or constraints between types are imposed. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is this promised recovery and identifiability problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,S,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite network, known divergences jointly absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure yield unique winners and identifiable source influences almost surely when \(m\geq S\). [Matta–Bordignon–Santos–Sayed](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.13905)
No separate complexity result for the support-encoded mass problem was verified.

  • Normalize the receiving distribution to total mass one and encode its positive support masses as binary rationals.
  • Encode the supplied divergence matrix and exact limiting rate vectors as binary rationals so that bit complexity is defined.
  • Promise existence of strictly positive normalized influences, matching the discrete inference problem's assumption that observations arise from a true influence vector.
  • Exclude tied winners by requiring zero rate at the supplied winner and strictly negative rates elsewhere.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The promised problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time, including exact recovery of every influence mass when the array is identifiable.

Write \(D_i\) for row \(i\) of \(D\). Define the common matrix \(C\in\mathbb{Q}^{m\times S}\) by
\[ C_{1s}=1,\qquad C_{is}=D_{is}-D_{1s}\quad(2\leq i\leq m), \]
and, for each type \(r\), define \(b_r\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}\) by
\[ b_r(1)=\mu_r,\qquad b_r(i)=\mu_r\bigl(y_r(1)-y_r(i)\bigr)\quad(2\leq i\leq m). \]

The exact identifiability criterion is \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=S\). In particular, it depends only on \(D\), and is independent of the types’ winners and rates.

To prove the criterion, first fix a type \(r\) and put \(w=\theta_r^\star\). Its original equality constraints are
\[ D_wq_r-D_iq_r=\mu_ry_r(i)\quad(1\leq i\leq m), \qquad \mathbf{1}_S^\top q_r=\mu_r. \]
Subtracting the equation indexed by \(i\) from the equation indexed by \(1\) gives
\[ (D_i-D_1)q_r=\mu_r\bigl(y_r(1)-y_r(i)\bigr), \]
so these constraints imply \(Cq_r=b_r\).

Conversely, suppose \(Cq_r=b_r\). The normalization equation follows from its first row. For every \(i\), including \(i=1\), its remaining rows give
\[ D_iq_r=D_1q_r+\mu_r\bigl(y_r(1)-y_r(i)\bigr). \]
Since \(y_r(w)=0\), this implies \(D_wq_r=D_1q_r+\mu_ry_r(1)\). Subtraction therefore yields
\[ D_wq_r-D_iq_r=\mu_ry_r(i). \]
Thus the original equality constraints are equivalent to \(Cq_r=b_r\). The feasible set for type \(r\) is consequently exactly
\[ F_r=\{q_r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{S}:Cq_r=b_r\}. \]
The consistency promise makes every \(F_r\) nonempty. Because there are no constraints between types, the feasible arrays form the Cartesian product \(F_1\times\cdots\times F_\tau\).

If \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=S\), then \(\ker C=\{0\}\). Any two solutions of \(Cq_r=b_r\) have difference in this kernel, so they coincide. Nonemptiness gives exactly one feasible vector for every type, hence exactly one feasible array.

If \(\operatorname{rank}(C)<S\), choose a nonzero \(v\in\ker C\). Fix a type \(r\) and a promised strictly positive feasible vector \(q_r^0\). Define
\[ \varepsilon=\min_{s:v_s\neq0}\frac{q_{sr}^0}{2|v_s|}>0. \]
For every \(t\) with \(|t|\leq\varepsilon\), each coordinate of \(q_r^0+tv\) is positive: when \(v_s\neq0\), it is at least \(q_{sr}^0/2\), and otherwise it is unchanged. Furthermore,
\[ C(q_r^0+tv)=b_r+tCv=b_r. \]
Taking any nonzero such \(t\) gives a different feasible vector. Keeping all other types fixed gives a different feasible array. This proves non-identifiability, and indeed infinitely many feasible arrays.

The algorithm is now explicit:

  • Construct \(C\) and all \(b_r\), and compute \(\operatorname{rank}(C)\) exactly.
  • If the rank is less than \(S\), report non-identifiability.
  • Otherwise select \(S\) linearly independent rows of \(C\), with index set \(I\). Let \(M=C_{I,:}\). For every type, solve \(Mq_r=(b_r)_I\) exactly and output the resulting vectors.

The last step returns feasible, strictly positive vectors without requiring a separate positivity search. Indeed, the promised feasible vector solves this subsystem, and invertibility of \(M\) makes its solution unique.

Exact rational arithmetic also gives a polynomial bit-complexity bound. Constructing \(C\) and the right-hand sides uses polynomially many rational additions and multiplications on input numbers of total binary length \(L\), so the resulting data have polynomial total binary length. Choose a positive integer \(T\) equal to the product of all denominators in \([C\ b_1\ \cdots\ b_\tau]\). Then
\[ H=TC,\qquad h_r=Tb_r \]
are integer data whose entries have bit length at most some \(B\) polynomial in \(m,S,\tau,L\).

Exact fraction-free Gaussian elimination computes the rank and selects independent rows in polynomial time. Its intermediate quantities are minors, with exact divisions, and their bit lengths are polynomially bounded. Specifically, if integer entries have absolute value at most \(2^B\), any minor of order \(k\) has absolute value at most
\[ k!\,2^{kB}, \]
by the determinant expansion. Its bit length is therefore at most \(kB+O(k\log k)\).

For recovery, put \(N=H_{I,:}\). Cramer’s rule gives
\[ q_{sr}=\frac{\det N^{(s,r)}}{\det N}, \]
where \(N^{(s,r)}\) replaces column \(s\) of \(N\) by \((h_r)_I\). Each determinant is computable by fraction-free elimination in polynomial time and has bit length \(O(SB+S\log S)\). There are \(S\tau\) output coordinates. Thus both computation time and the exact output length are polynomial in the stated parameters.

The dimension condition alone does not guarantee identification. For example, take \(m=S=2\), \(\tau=1\), \(\mu_1=1\), rows \(D_1=(0,0)\) and \(D_2=(1,1)\), winner \(1\), and rates \(y_1=(0,-1)\). Every positive vector with coordinate sum \(1\) is feasible. Here both rows of \(C\) equal \((1,1)\), so its rank is \(1\), exactly as the criterion predicts.

The cited paper’s Theorem 3 establishes almost-sure full column rank and unique winners under its joint absolute-continuity assumption. That generic identifiability statement is consistent with the deterministic criterion proved here; the polynomial-time algorithm also handles exceptional rank-deficient rational inputs.
