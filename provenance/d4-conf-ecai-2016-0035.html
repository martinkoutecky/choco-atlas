Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge2\) candidates, let \(p\in C\), and fix an integer \(\ell\ge3\). Types are strict linear rankings of \(C\); write \(\mathcal R(C)\) for all such rankings and use Kendall–Tau distance \(d_{\mathrm{KT}}\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(S\) of \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The input also specifies a binary rational budget \(0<\beta\le1\) and a model, UNI or NON; initially \(p\) is not a Condorcet winner in UNI, or not a weak Condorcet winner in NON.
A transfer consists of real masses \(x_{t,r}\ge0\), for \(t\in S\) and \(r\in\mathcal R(C)\setminus\{t\}\) with \(d_{\mathrm{KT}}(t,r)\le\ell\), satisfying \(\sum_r x_{t,r}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,r}x_{t,r}\le\beta\). Destinations need not belong to \(S\); all other transfers are zero.
Extend \(\mu\) by zero outside \(S\). The resulting mass at ranking \(r\) is \(\nu_r=\mu_r-\sum_q x_{r,q}+\sum_{t\in S}x_{t,r}\), with outgoing mass zero for sources outside \(S\). Comparisons use total ranking mass; UNI requires \(p\) to beat every opponent strictly, while NON permits pairwise ties.
Is deciding whether such a transfer makes \(p\) win polynomial-time solvable or NP-hard, for each fixed \(\ell\ge3\) and each model? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), with dependence on \(\ell\) explicit.
For discrete voters, the same constructive distance-restricted bribery problem is NP-hard for every fixed \(\ell\ge3\) in both models, and polynomial-time solvable for \(\ell\in\{1,2\}\). [Discrete results](https://doi.org/10.3233/978-1-61499-672-9-363)
Continuous Condorcet Shift Bribery is polynomial-time solvable; it allows only upward shifts of the target candidate and uses shift costs. No result for exactly the transfer problem above was located. [Continuous result](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25444v1)

  • Restricted the rational mass budget to the interval (0,1], carrying over the discrete positive budget bounded by the electorate size.
  • Carried over the promise that the target initially fails to win under the selected model; this also excludes the single-candidate case.
  • Specified strict complete rankings and allowed destination rankings outside the initial support, as arbitrary recast votes are allowed discretely.
  • Specified real divisible transfer masses, simultaneous transfers from original source masses, and the resulting society explicitly.
  • Defined L as the total input bit length and treated each distance bound as a fixed integer.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Both UNI and NON are polynomial-time solvable for every fixed \(\ell\ge3\). In fact, the algorithm has a polynomial bound independent of \(\ell\): it uses one canonical destination per supported type and solves a rational linear program with \(\tau+1\) variables and \(O(m+\tau)\) constraints.

A bit-operation bound is
\(O(\tau mL^2)+\bigl(\tau m+(m+\tau)L\bigr)^{O(1)}\),
where the exponent is an absolute constant independent of \(\ell\).

1. A bound on reversing two candidates

Write \(a\succ_t b\) when ranking \(t\) places \(a\) above \(b\). Kendall–Tau distance counts unordered candidate pairs whose relative order differs.

Lemma. Suppose \(q\) occupies position \(i\) and \(p\) occupies position \(j>i\) in \(t\). Every ranking \(r\) satisfying \(p\succ_r q\) has \(d_{\mathrm{KT}}(t,r)\ge j-i\).

Proof. The pair \(\{q,p\}\) reverses. For each of the \(j-i-1\) candidates \(b\) between them in \(t\), at least one of \(\{q,b\}\) and \(\{b,p\}\) must reverse: otherwise \(q\succ_r b\succ_r p\), contradicting \(p\succ_r q\).

These pairs are distinct for different intermediate candidates and distinct from \(\{q,p\}\). Thus there are at least \(1+(j-i-1)=j-i\) disagreements. \(\square\)

2. One destination per source suffices

For each \(t\in S\), let \(k_t\) be the position of \(p\), and define \(h_t=\min\{\ell,k_t-1\}\). Let \(s_t\) be the ranking obtained by moving \(p\) upward exactly \(h_t\) positions, preserving the relative order of all other candidates.

Let \(G_t\) contain the candidates crossed by this shift. Explicitly,
\(G_t=\{q\in C\setminus\{p\}:k_t-h_t\le\operatorname{pos}_t(q)<k_t\}\).

The shift reverses precisely the pairs \(\{p,q\}\) with \(q\in G_t\), so \(d_{\mathrm{KT}}(t,s_t)=h_t\le\ell\). If \(h_t>0\), it is a permitted destination distinct from \(t\); if \(h_t=0\), then \(s_t=t\).

For a ranking \(r\) and opponent \(q\), define \(b_{r,q}=1\) if \(p\succ_r q\), and \(b_{r,q}=-1\) otherwise.

Dominance lemma. For every permitted destination \(r\) of source \(t\), and every opponent \(q\),
\(b_{s_t,q}\ge b_{r,q}\).

Proof. If \(p\succ_t q\), the upward shift preserves that comparison, so \(b_{s_t,q}=1\).

Now suppose \(q\succ_t p\). If \(q\in G_t\), again \(b_{s_t,q}=1\). Otherwise, \(q\) is more than \(\ell\) positions above \(p\): any predecessor within \(\ell\) positions belongs to \(G_t\). By the preceding lemma, no ranking within Kendall–Tau distance \(\ell\) can reverse this comparison. Consequently \(b_{r,q}=b_{s_t,q}=-1\). \(\square\)

We next verify that this pointwise dominance applies to the specified transfer model, including when destinations belong to the original support.

For any society \(\rho\), define its pairwise margin by
\(M_q(\rho)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R(C)}\rho_r b_{r,q}\).
The transfer definition and linearity give
\(M_q(\nu)=M_q(\mu)+\sum_{t\in S}\sum_r x_{t,r}(b_{r,q}-b_{t,q})\).

Given any feasible transfer, replace all outgoing mass from each source with \(h_t>0\) by a transfer to \(s_t\), of amount
\(y_t=\sum_r x_{t,r}\).
For sources with \(h_t=0\), discard their outgoing transfers and set \(y_t=0\).

The replacement respects every source capacity and does not increase the total transferred mass. For \(h_t>0\), the dominance lemma shows that each replaced transfer weakly increases every margin. For \(h_t=0\), \(p\) is already first, so \(b_{t,q}=1\) for every opponent; every original outgoing transfer has nonpositive margin change, and discarding it weakly increases every margin.

Thus every feasible transfer can be replaced by canonical upward shifts without decreasing any pairwise margin. This preserves both strict and weak winning conditions.

3. A linear program for both models

Compute the initial margins
\(M_q=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t b_{t,q}\)
for all \(q\ne p\). Since total mass is one, each margin belongs to \([-1,1]\).

Set \(u_t=\mu_t\) when \(h_t>0\), and \(u_t=0\) otherwise. Use variables \(y_t\), for \(t\in S\), and a common margin variable \(\delta\). Maximize \(\delta\), subject to:

  • \(0\le y_t\le u_t\) for every \(t\in S\);
  • \(\sum_{t\in S}y_t\le\beta\);
  • \(-1\le\delta\le1\);
  • \(M_q+2\sum_{t:q\in G_t}y_t\ge\delta\) for every \(q\in C\setminus\{p\}\).

The last constraint follows because moving mass \(y_t\) to \(s_t\) changes the margin against each crossed candidate from a contribution of \(-y_t\) to \(+y_t\), an increase of \(2y_t\). It leaves all other comparisons with \(p\) unchanged.

This LP is feasible: take every \(y_t=0\) and \(\delta=-1\). Its feasible region is closed and bounded, so its optimum \(\delta^\star\) is attained.

Correctness for NON

If the original problem admits a weak winning transfer, its canonical replacement has every margin nonnegative. Its amounts \(y_t\), together with \(\delta=0\), satisfy the LP. Hence \(\delta^\star\ge0\).

Conversely, if \(\delta^\star\ge0\), an optimal LP solution specifies permitted transfers \(t\to s_t\) whose margins are all nonnegative. Thus \(p\) is a weak Condorcet winner.

Therefore NON answers yes exactly when \(\delta^\star\ge0\).

Correctness for UNI

If the original problem admits a strict winning transfer, its canonical replacement also has every margin strictly positive. There are only \(m-1\) opponents, so the minimum of these margins is strictly positive. Taking this minimum as \(\delta\) gives an LP solution with positive objective.

Conversely, if \(\delta^\star>0\), the canonical transfers specified by an optimal LP solution make every margin at least \(\delta^\star>0\).

Therefore UNI answers yes exactly when \(\delta^\star>0\). This handles strictness without assuming any fixed positive winning margin.

4. Encoding length and running time

The LP has \(\tau+1\) variables and \(O(m+\tau)\) constraints. Its constraint-matrix entries belong to \(\{0,\pm1,\pm2\}\).

Let \(D\) be the product of the input denominators of all masses and the budget. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Every initial margin and every right-hand side has denominator dividing \(D\), and numerator of binary length \(O(L)\). Thus a dense encoding of the LP has length
\(B=O\bigl(\tau m+(m+\tau)L\bigr)\).

Exact optimization of a rational LP takes polynomial time in its binary encoding length. This standard theorem applies here because the LP is rational, feasible, and bounded. See [MIT’s notes on polynomial-time LP algorithms](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html); polynomial-size rational optimal solutions follow from the vertex characterization and Cramer’s rule, as described in [the accompanying LP notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n11-lp.html). Consequently the sign tests on \(\delta^\star\) are exact.

For completeness, a positive optimum cannot require superpolynomial precision. Put \(n=\tau+1\). At an optimal vertex, choose \(n\) independent tight constraints, with integer coefficient matrix \(A\). Its entries have absolute value at most two, so Hadamard’s inequality gives \(|\det A|\le(2\sqrt n)^n\). Cramer’s rule expresses \(\delta^\star\) with denominator dividing \(D|\det A|\). Hence, if positive,
\(\delta^\star\ge1/\bigl(D(2\sqrt n)^n\bigr)\).
Its positivity is therefore distinguishable using polynomially many bits.

Reading rankings, computing the sets \(G_t\), and computing all margins take at most \(O(\tau mL^2)\) bit operations using elementary integer arithmetic. Adding rational LP optimization yields the stated bound
\(O(\tau mL^2)+\bigl(\tau m+(m+\tau)L\bigr)^{O(1)}\).

The only use of \(\ell\) is in \(h_t=\min\{\ell,k_t-1\}\); neither the LP size nor the polynomial exponent depends on it. The same argument even works when \(\ell\) is supplied in binary, with its encoding included in \(L\).

5. Relation to discrete hardness

The dominance reduction also applies to discrete voters. However, discrete voters cannot be split into arbitrary real amounts, so the corresponding canonical-shift variables retain integrality restrictions. Here the permitted real masses make the linear program exact. Thus the polynomial-time result is consistent with the stated discrete hardness.
