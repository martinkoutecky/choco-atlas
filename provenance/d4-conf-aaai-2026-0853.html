The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) goods and two finite-support rational distributions \(\mu,\nu\) over \(\Delta_m=\{u\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^m:\sum_{j=1}^m u_j=1\}\), with their supports and masses listed explicitly, and a rational threshold \(d\), decide whether there exist a good permutation \(\pi\in S_m\) and a coupling \(\lambda\) of \(\mu,\nu\) such that \(\sum_{u,v}\lambda_{uv}\lVert u-\pi(v)\rVert_1\le d\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge 2\) and \(\Delta_m=\{u\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m:\sum_{j=1}^m u_j=1\}\). Input consists of two probability societies \(\mu,\nu\) on \(\Delta_m\) and a threshold \(d\in\mathbb Q\).
Each society is given by its support: distinct types with positive binary rational masses summing to \(1\). Write \(\tau_\mu,\tau_\nu\) for their support sizes and \(\tau=\tau_\mu+\tau_\nu\); these count listed types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including utility coordinates, masses, and \(d\).
A coupling is an array \(\lambda_{uv}\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) with \(\sum_v\lambda_{uv}=\mu(u)\) and \(\sum_u\lambda_{uv}=\nu(v)\). Mass may split among types.
A single permutation \(\pi\in S_m\) acts on every type of \(\nu\), with \((\pi(v))_j=v_{\pi(j)}\). Decide whether some \(\pi\) and coupling satisfy \(\sum_{u,v}\lambda_{uv}\lVert u-\pi(v)\rVert_1\le d\).

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For two finite lists of equally many normalized utility vectors, minimizing total \(\ell_1\) distance over simultaneous agent and good permutations is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard; fixing either permutation makes the discrete problem polynomial-time solvable. Resolution

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and in fact \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Allowing mass to split does not remove the discrete hardness: for empirical societies obtained from equally long lists, fractional transport has exactly the same optimum as matching the list entries, after normalization.

1. The discrete hardness result

We use the discrete decision problem supplied in the question. Given two lists
\((a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) and \((b_1,\ldots,b_n)\) of rational vectors in \(\Delta_m\), and a rational threshold \(D\), decide whether

\(\displaystyle \min_{\sigma\in S_n,\ \pi\in S_m}\sum_{i=1}^n\lVert a_i-\pi(b_{\sigma(i)})\rVert_1\le D.\)

This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard by [Theorem 5 of *Putting Fair Division on the Map*](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20161v2#A2.SS2). Its reduction uses preference ranks divided by their common sum, so the hardness applies to rational normalized vectors and rational thresholds of polynomial encoding length. The correct rank sum is \(F=\sum_{j=1}^m j=m(m+1)/2\).

The lists may contain repeated vectors. The next lemma explicitly handles this.

2. Empirical transport equals normalized matching

Lemma. Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_n,b_1,\ldots,b_n\in\Delta_m\). Define societies by giving every list occurrence mass \(1/n\), merging identical vectors. Thus, writing

\(r_u=\lvert\{i:a_i=u\}\rvert,\qquad s_v=\lvert\{k:b_k=v\}\rvert,\)

their positive masses are \(\mu(u)=r_u/n\) and \(\nu(v)=s_v/n\). For every fixed \(\pi\in S_m\),

\(\displaystyle \min_{\lambda\text{ coupling }\mu,\nu}\sum_{u,v}\lambda_{uv}\lVert u-\pi(v)\rVert_1 =\frac1n\min_{\sigma\in S_n}\sum_{i=1}^n\lVert a_i-\pi(b_{\sigma(i)})\rVert_1.\)

Proof. Fix \(\pi\), and put \(c_{ik}=\lVert a_i-\pi(b_k)\rVert_1\).

First consider matrices \(X=(x_{ik})\) satisfying

\(x_{ik}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kx_{ik}=1/n,\qquad \sum_ix_{ik}=1/n.\)

Such a matrix induces a society coupling by

\(\displaystyle \lambda_{uv}=\sum_{\substack{i:a_i=u\\k:b_k=v}}x_{ik}.\)

Its row marginal at \(u\) is \(r_u/n\), and its column marginal at \(v\) is \(s_v/n\). Since the cost is constant within each block of identical types,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i,k}x_{ik}c_{ik}=\sum_{u,v}\lambda_{uv}\lVert u-\pi(v)\rVert_1.\)

Conversely, any society coupling \(\lambda\) lifts to such a matrix by setting

\(\displaystyle x_{ik}=\frac{\lambda_{a_i b_k}}{r_{a_i}s_{b_k}}.\)

Indeed, for \(a_i=u\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_kx_{ik}=\sum_v s_v\frac{\lambda_{uv}}{r_us_v} =\frac{\mu(u)}{r_u}=\frac1n.\)

The column calculation is identical. Each block contains \(r_us_v\) entries, so this lift also preserves the cost. Therefore the society transport optimum equals the minimum of \(\sum_{i,k}x_{ik}c_{ik}\) over these matrices.

Now \(P=nX\) is doubly stochastic. The Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem states that every nonnegative square matrix with all row and column sums equal to \(1\) is a convex combination of permutation matrices. Its hypotheses hold here. Consequently, for some nonnegative coefficients \(\alpha_\sigma\) summing to \(1\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i,k}x_{ik}c_{ik} =\frac1n\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\alpha_\sigma\sum_i c_{i,\sigma(i)} \ge\frac1n\min_{\sigma\in S_n}\sum_i c_{i,\sigma(i)}.\)

For the reverse inequality, choose a minimizing permutation \(\sigma\) and set \(x_{i,\sigma(i)}=1/n\), with all other entries zero. This matrix is feasible and attains the right-hand side. The lemma follows. \(\square\)

In particular, the lemma permits arbitrary real split couplings; it imposes no integrality restriction on them.

3. Polynomial reduction

Given a discrete instance \((a_1,\ldots,a_n),(b_1,\ldots,b_n),D\), construct the empirical societies from the lemma and set \(d=D/n\).

The construction meets every input requirement:

  • All utility vectors remain rational, nonnegative, and normalized.
  • Identical vectors are merged, leaving distinct listed support types.
  • Every listed mass is a positive rational of the form \(r/n\), and each society’s masses sum to \(1\).
  • The combined support size satisfies \(\tau\le2n\).
  • Each multiplicity mass has binary encoding length \(O(\log n)\). Dividing the threshold by \(n\) also increases its encoding length by at most \(O(\log n)\).

Equality comparisons between rational vectors and the required rational arithmetic take polynomial time in the discrete input length. Thus the construction is a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

Applying the lemma separately to every good permutation gives

\(\displaystyle \min_{\pi\in S_m}\min_{\lambda\text{ coupling }\mu,\nu} \sum_{u,v}\lambda_{uv}\lVert u-\pi(v)\rVert_1 = \frac1n\min_{\pi\in S_m,\ \sigma\in S_n} \sum_i\lVert a_i-\pi(b_{\sigma(i)})\rVert_1. \)

Hence the constructed instance has cost at most \(d=D/n\) exactly when the discrete instance has cost at most \(D\). This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Notice that the reduction constructs its support directly from the explicitly given lists. It never expands binary masses into a potentially enormous population.

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate consists of the good permutation \(\pi\), encoded using \(O(m\log m)\) bits.

Given this permutation, compute the rational costs

\(\displaystyle c_{uv}=\sum_{j=1}^m|u_j-v_{\pi(j)}|.\)

Then test feasibility of the rational linear program

\(\displaystyle \lambda_{uv}\ge0,\qquad \sum_v\lambda_{uv}=\mu(u),\qquad \sum_u\lambda_{uv}=\nu(v),\qquad \sum_{u,v}c_{uv}\lambda_{uv}\le d. \)

It has \(\tau_\mu\tau_\nu\) variables and polynomially many constraints. Exact rational additions and subtractions produce coefficients of polynomial binary encoding length in \(m,\tau,L\). Polynomial-time feasibility algorithms for rational linear programming therefore provide a polynomial-time verifier.

If the instance is a yes-instance, some permutation makes this program feasible. Conversely, feasibility for the certified permutation supplies exactly the coupling required by the question. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Combining membership and hardness proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
