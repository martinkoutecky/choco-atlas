Interpreting “binary-rational” as a rational represented by a binary numerator and denominator, the support-mass problem is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete under polynomial-time search reductions.

This does not settle the requested unconditional choice between polynomial-time solvability and $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. Those are not exhaustive alternatives for a total search problem. Below I prove the classification, give explicit polynomial-time solutions for the cases $\tau=1$ and $m=1$, and state precisely what remains open.

1. The finite-buyer result

Write $\mathcal F$ for the corresponding finite-buyer problem and $\mathcal S$ for the support-mass problem.

The external theorem used is Chen–Teng’s Theorem 1: finding an $m^{-21}$-approximate equilibrium in a finite Fisher market is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete when utilities are additively separable, have at most two segments per component, are $81$-bounded and $43$-sparse, and satisfy the Maxfield condition. Their Definition 3 requires exact individual utility maximization and the relative clearing inequality for every good. Their formulation requests prices admitting a suitable allocation. [Chen–Teng, Sections 2.2–2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4130)

These hypotheses match the restrictions in the question. In particular, every nonzero component here has final slope at least $1$, and each type has at least one such component.

A polynomial-time search reduction will mean an instance transformation and a solution decoder, both computable in polynomial time, such that every valid solution of the transformed instance decodes to a valid solution of the original instance.

2. From support masses to finite buyers

Given an instance of $\mathcal S$, construct exactly one finite buyer for each support type $t$. Keep the supplies $c_j$, and define
$$ W_t=\mu_t w_t, \qquad U_t(x)=\mu_t u_t(x/\mu_t) =\sum_{j=1}^{m}R_{t,j}(x_j), $$
where
$$ R_{t,j}(z)=\mu_t r_{t,j}(z/\mu_t). $$
Division of vectors is coordinatewise.

Preservation of the restrictions

A zero component remains zero. A linear component of slope $\theta$ remains linear with the same slope:
$$ \mu_t\theta(z/\mu_t)=\theta z. $$

For a two-segment component with breakpoint $b$,
$$ R_{t,j}(z)= \begin{cases} \theta_0z,&0\le z\le\mu_tb,\\ \theta_0\mu_tb+\theta_1(z-\mu_tb),&z\ge\mu_tb. \end{cases} $$
Thus only the breakpoint changes, from $b$ to $\mu_tb$.

Consequently, the transformation preserves both slopes, their strict decrease, the interval $[1,81]$, the number of nonzero components, and positive final slopes. Budgets and breakpoints remain positive rationals. The constructed finite market therefore satisfies every hypothesis of the cited theorem.

Exact correspondence of demand

Fix any nonzero nonnegative price vector $p$. Since $\mu_t>0$, the map
$$ a\longmapsto x=\mu_ta $$
is a bijection between the respective budget sets:
$$ p\cdot x\le W_t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\cdot a\le w_t. $$
Furthermore,
$$ U_t(\mu_ta)=\mu_tu_t(a). $$
Multiplication by the positive number $\mu_t$ preserves every utility comparison. Therefore
$$ x_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\ge0,\ p\cdot x\le W_t}U_t(x) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_t/\mu_t\in \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{a\ge0,\ p\cdot a\le w_t}u_t(a). $$
This equivalence preserves ties and does not require every price coordinate to be positive.

Exact correspondence of clearing

For each good,
$$ \sum_t x_{t,j}=\sum_t\mu_ta_{t,j}. $$
Hence the finite-market clearing condition is exactly
$$ \left|\sum_t\mu_ta_{t,j}-c_j\right| \le m^{-21}c_j. $$
The number of goods remains $m$, so the tolerance also remains unchanged.

Thus the instance transformation preserves the desired solutions exactly. Section 4 supplies the decoder when the finite solver returns only prices.

3. From finite buyers to support masses

Consider a finite instance with $N$ buyers, budgets $w_i$, utilities $u_i$, and supplies $C_j$. Initially give each buyer mass $1/N$, keep its budget and utility, and set
$$ c_j=C_j/N. $$
For every allocation,
$$ \left|\frac1N\sum_i a_{i,j}-\frac{C_j}{N}\right| \le m^{-21}\frac{C_j}{N} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left|\sum_i a_{i,j}-C_j\right| \le m^{-21}C_j. $$
Individual demand problems are unchanged.

Distinct support types and representative bundles

If support types must be distinct, merge all buyers having identical budgets and utility functions. A group of $k$ identical buyers becomes one type of mass $k/N$.

To justify using one representative bundle, fix prices and let $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ be optimal bundles for such a group. Their common utility $u$ is concave: every component has nonincreasing slopes, and sums preserve concavity. Their common budget set is convex. Thus
$$ \bar a=\frac1k\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}a_\ell $$
is feasible. If their common optimal utility value is $V$, concavity gives
$$ u(\bar a)\ge\frac1k\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}u(a_\ell)=V. $$
Feasibility gives $u(\bar a)\le V$, so $\bar a$ is optimal. Its contribution satisfies
$$ \frac{k}{N}\bar a =\frac1N\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}a_\ell. $$

Conversely, assign a merged type’s representative bundle to every buyer in its group. Each copied bundle is optimal, and aggregate demand is preserved.

The resulting support size is $\tau\le N$. All masses are positive rationals summing to $1$. This proves
$$ \mathcal F\le_{\mathrm p}\mathcal S. $$
In particular, $\mathcal S$ is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hard.

4. Recovering rational optimal bundles from prices

We must address the difference between the cited price-only problem and the output requested here.

Work in the transformed finite market of Section 2. Suppose rational prices $p$ admit an allocation satisfying exact individual maximization and approximate clearing.

For each buyer and good introduce variables $x_{t,j}\ge0$ and $z_{t,j}\ge0$. Impose the following component constraints:

  • For a zero component, $z_{t,j}=0$.
  • For a linear component, $z_{t,j}\le\theta x_{t,j}$.
  • For a two-segment component with breakpoint $B$,

$$ z_{t,j}\le\theta_0x_{t,j}, \qquad z_{t,j}\le\theta_1x_{t,j}+(\theta_0-\theta_1)B. $$

In the two-segment case,
$$ R_{t,j}(x)= \min\left\{\theta_0x,\, \theta_1x+(\theta_0-\theta_1)B\right\}. $$
Indeed, the two lines intersect at $B$, and their difference is
$$ (\theta_0-\theta_1)(x-B). $$
The first line is therefore smaller before $B$, and the second is smaller after $B$.

For each buyer, solve the rational linear program maximizing $\sum_jz_{t,j}$ subject to these constraints and
$$ \sum_jp_jx_{t,j}\le W_t. $$
Its optimum equals the buyer’s maximum utility: every feasible pair satisfies $\sum_jz_{t,j}\le U_t(x_t)$, while every budget-feasible bundle permits setting $z_{t,j}=R_{t,j}(x_{t,j})$. Denote this optimum by $V_t$. It is finite and attained because the assumed prices admit maximizing bundles.

Now solve a joint rational linear feasibility problem containing all these constraints, together with
$$ \sum_jz_{t,j}=V_t \qquad\text{for every }t, $$
and
$$ (1-m^{-21})c_j \le\sum_t x_{t,j} \le(1+m^{-21})c_j \qquad\text{for every }j. $$
The allocation admitted by $p$, with its actual component utilities as the $z$ variables, proves feasibility.

For any returned feasible point,
$$ U_t(x_t)\ge\sum_jz_{t,j}=V_t. $$
Budget feasibility and the definition of $V_t$ give the reverse inequality. Thus every returned $x_t$ is exactly utility maximizing.

Polynomial-time rational linear programming returns a rational feasible point of polynomial encoding length. Set
$$ a_t=x_t/\mu_t. $$
Section 2 proves that these are the required representative bundles. This completes
$$ \mathcal S\le_{\mathrm p}\mathcal F. $$
Together with Section 3, it proves $\mathrm{PPAD}$-completeness.

5. Encoding lengths, running times, and totality

Let $L$ be the support instance’s total binary input length.

Each mass is used in one budget multiplication and at most $43$ breakpoint multiplications. Rational multiplication increases encoding length by at most the sum of the operand lengths, up to a constant. Consequently, the transformed finite instance has encoding length
$$ O(L+m\tau), $$
including a dense table of zero components if needed.

A conservative schoolbook-arithmetic bound for constructing it is
$$ O\bigl((m\tau+1)(L+1)^2\bigr) $$
bit operations. There is no numerical dependence on $1/\mu_t$.

If the supplied price vector has encoding length $P$, bundle recovery uses $\tau$ optimization linear programs and one feasibility linear program. Each has at most $O(m\tau)$ variables and constraints, and recovery takes
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,P) $$
time. With the polynomially encoded prices supplied by the finite problem, the entire reduction and decoding overhead is
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$
This is a bound on the reduction overhead, not a polynomial-time algorithm for finding equilibrium prices.

In the reverse direction, construction and merging take
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,N,L_{\mathcal F}) $$
time, with resulting input length at most
$$ O\bigl(L_{\mathcal F}+(m+N)\log(N+1)\bigr). $$
Neither reduction expands a population according to mass denominators.

Existence and polynomially sized witnesses

The cited source also states that the Maxfield condition guarantees equilibrium existence and that rational inputs admit equilibrium prices of polynomial encoding length. Its exact equilibrium definition allows unused supply only at zero prices. [Chen–Teng, Section 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4130)

Here, any zero-price good must have zero utility component for every buyer. Otherwise, a buyer could obtain arbitrarily large utility for free, because every nonzero component has positive final slope; no maximizing bundle would exist.

Therefore any unused supply at a zero-price good can be assigned to an arbitrary buyer without changing utility or expenditure. This turns an exact equilibrium allocation into one clearing every good exactly. Applying the linear-programming construction above, even with zero clearing error, produces polynomially encoded rational bundles.

Thus the problem is total and has polynomially sized solutions. Proposed rational outputs can be checked in polynomial time: check budgets and clearing directly, and compare each bundle’s utility with the optimum of its individual rational linear program.

6. Explicit polynomial-time special cases

One support type

If $\tau=1$, then $\mu_1=1$. Write its utility as $u$ and budget as $w$.

For each good choose
$$ d_j= \begin{cases} 0,&r_j\text{ is zero},\\ \theta,&r_j\text{ is linear of slope }\theta,\\ \theta_0,&r_j\text{ has breakpoint }b\text{ and }c_j<b,\\ \theta_1,&r_j\text{ has breakpoint }b\text{ and }c_j\ge b. \end{cases} $$
The chosen slope defines an affine upper bound touching the utility at $c_j$:
$$ r_j(z)\le r_j(c_j)+d_j(z-c_j) \qquad\text{for all }z\ge0. $$
For the two-segment case this follows from the minimum-of-two-lines formula in Section 4: the selected line is active at $c_j$.

Set
$$ D=\sum_jd_jc_j, \qquad p_j=\frac{w}{D}d_j, \qquad a_j=c_j. $$
At least one $d_j$ is positive by the Maxfield condition, and every supply is positive, so $D>0$. Therefore $p$ is well-defined and nonzero, and $p\cdot c=w$.

For every budget-feasible $a$,
$$ u(a)\le u(c)+\sum_jd_j(a_j-c_j)\le u(c), $$
because $p\cdot a\le w$ implies $\sum_jd_ja_j\le D$. Hence $c$ is optimal and clears exactly.

These rational formulas are computable in $\operatorname{poly}(m,L)$ time.

One good

If $m=1$, every type’s utility is strictly increasing in the sole good: the Maxfield condition requires its only component to have positive final slope.

Define
$$ W=\sum_t\mu_tw_t, \qquad p=\frac{W}{c_1}, \qquad a_{t,1}=\frac{w_t}{p}. $$
The price is positive. Strict increase makes $w_t/p$ the unique maximizing quantity for type $t$. Moreover,
$$ \sum_t\mu_ta_{t,1}=\frac{W}{p}=c_1. $$
This gives exact clearing in $\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)$ time, despite the prescribed tolerance being $1$ when $m=1$.

7. What remains unresolved

The reductions establish the equivalence
$$ \mathcal S\text{ has a }\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \text{-time algorithm} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{every }\mathrm{PPAD}\text{ search problem has a polynomial-time algorithm}. $$
Neither implication supplies such an algorithm.

The proof also does not establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. To make the distinction precise, suppose there were polynomial-time maps reducing a formula $\varphi$ to a valid market and decoding every valid market solution into the correct satisfiability answer. An unsatisfiable formula would then have a polynomial certificate: a polynomially sized valid market solution whose decoded answer is “unsatisfiable.” Verification is polynomial by Section 5. Such a certificate exists by totality, while correctness excludes it for satisfiable formulas. Consequently $\mathrm{UNSAT}$ would belong to $\mathrm{NP}$, implying $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.

This consequence concerns the stated decision-to-search reduction model. It does not prove that every possible meaning of $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness is excluded.

The rigorous general result is therefore $\mathrm{PPAD}$-completeness; the literal requested dichotomy remains unsettled.
