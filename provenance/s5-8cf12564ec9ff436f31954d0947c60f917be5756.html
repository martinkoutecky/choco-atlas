Let \(O_k(v)\) be maximum welfare over feasible type-to-category mass flows, and let \(R_k(v)\) be the limiting expected welfare per person under finite Random Priority markets with \(q/k\) agents of each type and \(q/k\) copies of each category, as \(q\to\infty\). Replica limits, a strict upper bound, and a finite characterization

The existence and inequality questions have affirmative answers. In fact, the replica limit exists and is positive for every utility profile in the stated open cube, and
\(\displaystyle \frac{31}{30}<C<2<1+e.\)

I also prove that \(\mathbb E_v[O_k(v)/R_k(v)]\) tends to \(1\), and give an explicit finite integral characterization of \(C\). I do not evaluate that characterization or identify the maximizing \(k\).

1. The replica limit exists

Fix \(k\) and a utility profile \(v\). Refine each type’s utility ranking by the specified category-index tie rule.

Define Probabilistic Serial as follows. Give each category one unit of supply. Each of the \(k\) types consumes its most preferred available category at speed one, switching when that category is exhausted. Consumption ends at time one. Let \(p_{ij}(v)\) be the amount consumed by type \(i\) from category \(j\).

The process is well defined: between exhaustion events, consumption rates are constant, and each event removes at least one category. Its allocation satisfies
\(\displaystyle \sum_jp_{ij}(v)=1,\qquad \sum_ip_{ij}(v)=1.\)

The replica convergence theorem of Che and Kojima states that, for fixed finitely many categories and fixed strict preferences, replicating agents and category supplies makes Random Priority’s marginal assignment probabilities converge to the continuum Probabilistic Serial probabilities. Their Theorems 1 and 2 also state that Probabilistic Serial is invariant under exact replication. [Che–Kojima discussion paper](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/d1677.pdf)

Its hypotheses hold here: the categories are fixed while \(r\) grows, each category has \(r\) copies, and the population consists of \(r\) replicas of each base type. The refined rankings are strict. If several base types have the same ranking, their multiplicities are combined when applying the theorem. All categories can be ranked above the theorem’s null object.

Let \(p^{(r)}_{ij}(v)\) denote the probability that a particular clone of type \(i\) receives category \(j\). Exchangeability of its \(r\) clones gives
\(\displaystyle W_{k,r}(v)=\frac1k\sum_{i,j}v^i_jp^{(r)}_{ij}(v).\)

Consequently, convergence of the finitely many marginal probabilities proves
\(\displaystyle R_k(v)=\frac1k\sum_{i,j}v^i_jp_{ij}(v).\)

Since every entry is positive and \(\sum_{i,j}p_{ij}=k\),
\(\displaystyle R_k(v)\geq\min_{i,j}v^i_j>0.\)

Thus existence and positivity hold for every profile in the stated open cube, including profiles with utility ties.

2. A deterministic welfare lower bound

Write
\(\displaystyle A_k(v)=\frac1{k^2}\sum_{i,j}v^i_j.\)

I claim that
\(\displaystyle R_k(v)\geq A_k(v).\)

Fix a type \(i\), and list its categories in refined preference order as \(\sigma_i(1),\ldots,\sigma_i(k)\). For any \(\ell\), let
\(\displaystyle S=\{\sigma_i(1),\ldots,\sigma_i(\ell)\},\)
and let \(T\) be the time when the last category in \(S\) is exhausted.

Until time \(T\), type \(i\) always has a category in \(S\) available and therefore consumes within \(S\). After time \(T\), no category in \(S\) is available. Hence
\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\in S}p_{ij}=T.\)

Every other type consumes from \(S\) for at most \(T\) units of time. Summing over all types and using the category supply constraints gives
\(\displaystyle \ell=\sum_h\sum_{j\in S}p_{hj}\leq kT.\)

Therefore,
\(\displaystyle \sum_{a=1}^{\ell}p_{i,\sigma_i(a)}\geq\frac{\ell}{k}.\)

Set \(u_a=v^i_{\sigma_i(a)}\), so \(u_1\geq\cdots\geq u_k\). Summation by parts yields
\(\displaystyle \sum_jv^i_jp_{ij} =u_k+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(u_\ell-u_{\ell+1}) \sum_{a=1}^{\ell}p_{i,\sigma_i(a)} \geq u_k+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}(u_\ell-u_{\ell+1})\frac{\ell}{k} =\frac1k\sum_jv^i_j. \)

Averaging over types proves the claim.

For later use, define
\(\displaystyle Q_k(v)=\frac{O_k(v)}{R_k(v)},\qquad a_k=\mathbb E_v[Q_k(v)].\)

The matrix \(kx\) of any feasible allocation is doubly stochastic. The Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem therefore gives
\(\displaystyle O_k(v)=\frac1k\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_k}\sum_i v^i_{\pi(i)}.\)

In particular, Probabilistic Serial is feasible, and
\(\displaystyle 1\leq Q_k(v)\leq k. \)

For the upper inequality, the matching sum is at most \(\sum_{i,j}v^i_j\), whereas the preceding lower bound gives \(R_k(v)\geq k^{-2}\sum_{i,j}v^i_j\).

3. An average-case upper bound below two for every fixed size

Flatten the \(k^2\) sampled entries into \(N=k^2\) independent uniform variables \(U_1,\ldots,U_N\), and write
\(\displaystyle M=\max_aU_a,\qquad S=\sum_aU_a.\)

Since an allocation has total mass one, \(O_k(v)\leq M\). The welfare lower bound gives \(R_k(v)\geq S/N\). Thus
\(\displaystyle Q_k(v)\leq\frac{NM}{S}.\)

Conditional on the maximizing coordinate and its value \(M\), the remaining coordinates divided by \(M\) are independent \(U(0,1)\) variables. Therefore
\(\displaystyle a_k\leq N\,\mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{1+\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}U_a}\right]. \)

To bound this expectation, let \(f(s)=(1+s)^{-1}\). Convexity gives, for \(s\geq0\) and \(u\in[0,1]\),
\(\displaystyle f(s+u)\leq(1-u)f(s)+uf(s+1).\)

After averaging over a uniform \(u\), the right side is the expectation obtained by replacing \(u\) with an independent Bernoulli variable of parameter \(1/2\). Repeating this replacement for all coordinates gives
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{1+\sum_{a=1}^{N-1}U_a}\right] \leq \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{1+B}\right], \qquad B\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N-1,1/2). \)

The binomial expectation is explicit:
\(\displaystyle N\mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{1+B}\right] =\frac{N}{2^{N-1}}\sum_{b=0}^{N-1} \frac{\binom{N-1}{b}}{b+1} =\frac1{2^{N-1}}\sum_{b=0}^{N-1}\binom N{b+1} =2(1-2^{-N}). \)

We have proved
\(\displaystyle a_k\leq2(1-2^{-k^2})<2.\)

This already implies \(C\leq2<1+e\). Establishing strictness for the supremum requires the next argument.

4. The expected ratio tends to one

Fix \(0<\varepsilon<1\). Call an entry *high* if \(v^i_j\geq1-\varepsilon\).

Let \(G_{k,\varepsilon}\) be the event that every pair of subsets \(B\) of types and \(A\) of categories satisfying
\(\displaystyle |B|\geq\varepsilon k,\qquad |A|\geq\varepsilon k\)
contains a high entry in \(B\times A\).

For fixed such subsets, independence gives
\(\displaystyle \Pr(\text{no high entry in }B\times A) =(1-\varepsilon)^{|B||A|} \leq\exp(-\varepsilon^3k^2). \)

There are at most \(4^k\) pairs of subsets. Hence
\(\displaystyle \Pr(G_{k,\varepsilon}^{\,c}) \leq\exp(2k\log2-\varepsilon^3k^2). \)

Now assume \(G_{k,\varepsilon}\) holds, and consider Probabilistic Serial at time \(t\leq1-\varepsilon\). In the normalized economy, the remaining supply is \(1-t\), and each available category has remaining supply at most \(1/k\). The available set \(A(t)\) therefore satisfies
\(\displaystyle |A(t)|\geq k(1-t)\geq\varepsilon k.\)

Let \(B(t)\) be the types with no high entry in \(A(t)\). The defining property of \(G_{k,\varepsilon}\) implies \(|B(t)|<\varepsilon k\). Every other type consumes a category worth at least \(1-\varepsilon\). Thus the instantaneous normalized welfare rate is at least \((1-\varepsilon)^2\).

Integrating until time \(1-\varepsilon\) proves
\(\displaystyle R_k(v)\geq(1-\varepsilon)^3\quad\text{on }G_{k,\varepsilon}.\)

This argument applies to the dynamically selected available sets because the event was defined uniformly over all subsets.

For \(k>4\), choose
\(\displaystyle \varepsilon_k=(4/k)^{1/3}.\)
Then
\(\displaystyle \Pr(G_{k,\varepsilon_k}^{\,c}) \leq\exp(-(4-2\log2)k)\leq e^{-2k}. \)

On the good event, \(O_k(v)\leq1\) implies \(Q_k(v)\leq(1-\varepsilon_k)^{-3}\). On its complement, use the deterministic bound \(Q_k(v)\leq k\). Consequently,
\(\displaystyle 1\leq a_k \leq\left(1-(4/k)^{1/3}\right)^{-3}+ke^{-2k}. \)

Both terms on the right have the required limits, proving
\(\displaystyle \lim_{k\to\infty}a_k=1.\)

5. A strict lower bound and an explicit finite cutoff

For \(k=1\), \(a_1=1\).

For \(k=2\), write the utilities as
\(\displaystyle v=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}, \qquad D_1=a-b,\quad D_2=c-d. \)

When \(D_1,D_2\) have opposite signs, the types prefer different categories. Probabilistic Serial gives each its preferred category, which is optimal, so \(Q_2=1\).

When their signs agree, both types consume each category for half the time. Therefore
\(\displaystyle R_2=\frac{a+b+c+d}{4},\qquad Q_2=1+\frac{|D_1-D_2|}{a+b+c+d}. \)

The sign of a difference of two independent uniforms is independent of its absolute value and is equally likely to be positive or negative. Put \(G_i=|D_i|\). Then
\(\displaystyle \Pr(G_i>t)=(1-t)^2\quad(0\leq t\leq1).\)

It follows that
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E G_i=\int_0^1(1-t)^2\,dt=\frac13, \qquad \mathbb E\min(G_1,G_2)=\int_0^1(1-t)^4\,dt=\frac15, \)
and hence
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E|G_1-G_2|=2\left(\frac13-\frac15\right)=\frac4{15}.\)

The signs agree with probability \(1/2\). Since \(a+b+c+d<4\) almost surely,
\(\displaystyle a_2-1> \frac14\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac4{15} =\frac1{30}. \)
Strictness holds because the numerator is positive almost surely conditional on agreement of the signs. Thus
\(\displaystyle a_2>\frac{31}{30}.\)

There is also an explicit cutoff. If \(k\geq4{,}000{,}000\), then \(\varepsilon_k\leq1/100\), so
\(\displaystyle (1-\varepsilon_k)^{-3} \leq(1-3\varepsilon_k)^{-1} \leq\frac{100}{97}. \)

Using \(e^k\geq k\) gives \(ke^{-2k}\leq1/k\). Since
\(\displaystyle \frac{31}{30}-\frac{100}{97}=\frac7{2910},\)
the preceding upper bound implies
\(\displaystyle a_k<\frac{31}{30}\qquad(k\geq4{,}000{,}000).\)

Consequently,
\(\displaystyle C=\max_{2\leq k<4{,}000{,}000}a_k. \)

The supremum is therefore attained. Combining this finite maximum with the fixed-size strict upper bounds proves
\(\displaystyle \frac{31}{30}<C<2.\)

6. Explicit finite integral characterization of the sharp constant

The following formula eliminates both the replica limit and the infinite supremum.

For an ordinal profile
\(\displaystyle \sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_k)\in(\mathfrak S_k)^k,\)
define its ordering cell
\(\displaystyle D_\sigma= \{v\in(0,1)^{k^2}: v^i_{\sigma_i(1)}>\cdots>v^i_{\sigma_i(k)} \text{ for every }i\}. \)

Let \(p^\sigma\) be the Probabilistic Serial matrix for that ordinal profile. It is a fixed rational matrix, independent of the utility magnitudes within \(D_\sigma\).

Define
\(\displaystyle I_k= \sum_{\sigma\in(\mathfrak S_k)^k} \int_{D_\sigma} \frac{\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_k}\sum_i v^i_{\pi(i)}} {\sum_{i,j}p^\sigma_{ij}v^i_j}\,dv. \)

The uniform sampling density is one. The ordering cells partition the cube except for utility-tie hyperplanes, which have measure zero. The formulas for optimal and Probabilistic Serial welfare show that \(I_k=a_k\). Thus the exact characterization is
\(\displaystyle \boxed{C=\max_{2\leq k<4{,}000{,}000}I_k.} \)

Every coefficient \(p^\sigma_{ij}\) is obtained by a finite rational exhaustion calculation. If desired, each ordering cell can be subdivided according to the maximizing permutation; this expresses \(I_k\) as a finite sum of integrals of ratios of linear forms over rational polytopes. These integrals are finite because their integrands are bounded by \(k\).

This is an explicit finite characterization, but it has not been evaluated here.

7. Support size and computational parameters

Probabilistic Serial can be computed directly from the listed support. For \(m\) categories and \(\tau\) supported types, sort each supported type’s ranking and simulate at most \(m\) exhaustion phases. A straightforward implementation uses
\(\displaystyle O(\tau m\log m+\tau m^2)\)
comparisons and arithmetic operations. It does not enumerate possible types or expand rational masses into agents.

For rationally encoded instances of total bit length \(L\), these calculations have polynomial bit complexity in \(m,\tau,L\). To see the relevant bit bound, take a common input denominator \(D\), whose bit length is at most \(L\). Each consumption rate is an integer divided by \(D\). Across at most \(m\) phases, denominators acquire at most \(m\) additional integer factors, each with at most \(L\) bits. Intermediate rational quantities therefore have polynomial, indeed \(O(mL)\), bit length.

The optimal welfare is a transportation linear program with \(\tau m\) variables and \(m+\tau\) equality constraints, and is computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In the equal-mass sampled model, \(m=\tau=k\), and the optimal assignment uses \(O(k^3)\) arithmetic operations via a weighted matching algorithm.

The uniform samples themselves are real-valued; these bit-complexity statements concern rational computational instances.
