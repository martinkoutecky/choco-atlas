The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a unit-mass society with rational type masses \(\mu_t\), explicit coverage valuations \(w_t\), item classes \(J\), a known distribution \(p\), horizon \(K\), and packet mass \(\eta\), each of the \(K\) arrivals supplies \(\eta\) slots of a class drawn independently from \(p\). A causal policy may allocate each packet across advertiser mass, moving mass between integral-bundle states \(S\subseteq J\), with each unit of advertiser mass receiving whole slots. Does there exist, for each fixed \(\delta>0\), a policy polynomial in the encoded instance size, \(|J|\), the number of types, and \(K\), that achieves a \((1-1/e+\delta)\)-competitive ratio against the expected offline optimum on every such instance?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(J\) be a nonempty set of item classes and \(m=|J|\). A unit-mass society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has an explicitly listed finite universe \(U_t\) and sets \(A_{t,j}\subseteq U_t\), defining the unweighted coverage valuation \(w_t(S)=|\bigcup_{j\in S}A_{t,j}|\). Repeated slots of the same class add no value.
The input also gives a binary-rational distribution \(p\) on \(J\), an integer horizon \(K\geq1\), and a binary-rational packet mass \(\eta>0\). All input data are known initially; \(L\) is their total encoding length. Independently at each arrival, a class \(j\) is drawn from \(p\), supplying \(\eta\) units of slot mass.
Write \(q_{t,S}\) for advertiser mass of type \(t\) holding the integral bundle \(S\subseteq J\); initially \(q_{t,\varnothing}=\mu_t\). On a class-\(j\) arrival, choose binary-rational transfers \(0\leq x_{t,S}\leq q_{t,S}\) for \(j\notin S\), with \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\leq\eta\), moving that mass from \(S\) to \(S\cup\{j\}\). Transfers use the state before the arrival; unused supply may be discarded. Thus bundles remain integral while advertiser mass may be split arbitrarily.
Allocations are irrevocable and causal, and policies may randomize. Terminal welfare is \(W=\sum_{t,S}q_{t,S}w_t(S)\). For each realized class sequence, \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is the maximum welfare under the same packet and allocation constraints with the whole sequence known in advance.

For each fixed \(0<\delta<1/e\), does a causal policy running in total time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,K)\) exist that guarantees \(\mathbb{E}[W]\geq(1-1/e+\delta)\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{OPT}]\) on every instance, or is this competitive guarantee impossible in polynomial time?

For discrete agents, greedy is \((1-1/e)\)-competitive, and a polynomial-time \((1-1/e+\delta)\)-competitive algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\), even with a known uniform arrival distribution. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.1025v2).
No result settling this precise packet-mass model was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted explicit coverage valuations as explicitly listed unweighted set systems, matching the discrete definition.
  • Specified a nonempty class set, positive integer horizon, positive binary-rational packet mass, and a binary-rational probability distribution; no upper bound on packet mass is imposed.
  • Made the mass dynamics explicit: initially empty bundles, simultaneous transfers from the pre-arrival state, irrevocable allocations, and arbitrary splitting using binary-rational transfer amounts.
  • Allowed unused packet supply to be discarded and omitted repeated-class allocations because they add no coverage value.
  • Defined welfare as mass-weighted coverage and the offline benchmark using exactly the same realized packets and allocation constraints, with expectations over arrivals and policy randomness.
  • Restricted the improvement parameter to the meaningful competitive-ratio range 0<delta<1/e.

Conclusion

For every fixed \(0<\delta<1/e\), a policy with the requested running time and guarantee would imply \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\).

Thus, unless \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\), the guarantee is impossible in polynomial time in the packet-mass model as well. Arbitrary splitting of advertiser mass does not remove the hardness. The reduction below uses only dyadic input probabilities and masses, and its horizon is polynomial in the original hard instance.

1. The hardness theorem used

We use the following strengthened Max \(k\)-cover gap theorem, stated in Section 2 of [Kapralov–Post–Vondrák](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.1025v2).

For fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), it is NP-hard to distinguish the following cases for explicitly listed sets \(B_{a,b}\subseteq U\), arranged in \(k\) groups of the same constant size \(n\):

  • YES: One set from each group covers \(U\).
  • NO: Every selection of \(\ell\leq 2k\) sets covers at most

\( \bigl(1-(1-1/k)^\ell+\varepsilon\bigr)|U| \).

Here the constant \(n\) may depend on \(\varepsilon\). The hard family can be restricted to \(k\) exceeding any fixed constant: bounded \(k\) can be handled by enumerating the \(n^k\) selections.

Fix
\( c=1-1/e \),
and choose positive dyadic constants satisfying
\( \varepsilon\leq\delta/8 \) and \( \gamma\leq\delta/16 \).
Restrict to sufficiently large \(k\geq2\) that

\( 1-(1-1/k)^k\leq c+\delta/8. \)

This is possible because \( (1-1/k)^k\longrightarrow e^{-1} \).

2. Constructing the society

Pad each group by duplicating sets until its size is a power of two \(N\), with \(n\leq N<2n\). Both gap properties survive: duplicates cannot increase the union beyond that of their distinct underlying sets.

Index positions within a group by \( \mathbb Z_N \). Define

\( J=\{1,\ldots,k\}\times\mathbb Z_N,\qquad m=kN. \)

Initially introduce \(N\) types, indexed by \(t\in\mathbb Z_N\), each of mass \(1/N\), with universe \(U\) and coverage sets

\( A_{t,(a,b)}=B_{a,b+t}, \)

where the second index is taken modulo \(N\). Their valuations are therefore explicitly listed, unweighted coverage valuations.

If some types are identical, merge them and add their masses. This gives an actual support of size \(\tau\leq N\), with dyadic positive masses. The labeled types remain useful bookkeeping for the proof; merging them preserves the feasible aggregate transfers and welfare.

In the YES case, choose indices \(\pi(a)\) such that

\( \bigcup_{a=1}^k B_{a,\pi(a)}=U. \)

For each labeled type define

\( S_t=\{(a,\pi(a)-t):1\leq a\leq k\}. \)

These bundles partition \(J\), each has size \(k\), and

\( w_t(S_t)=|U|. \)

In the NO case, any bundle \(S\) corresponds to \(|S|\) underlying sets, possibly with repetitions. Consequently, whenever \(|S|\leq2k\),

\( w_t(S)\leq\bigl(1-(1-1/k)^{|S|}+\varepsilon\bigr)|U|. \)

3. Packets and the arrival distribution

Choose a power of two \(r\) satisfying

\( r\geq\max\left\{\dfrac{m(1+\gamma)}{\gamma^3},\dfrac1\gamma\right\}. \)

Set

\( K=rm=rkN,\qquad \eta=\dfrac1{rN}. \)

The total arriving slot mass is exactly

\( K\eta=k. \)

For fixed \(\delta\), both \(r\) and \(K\) are polynomial in the original instance size.

We also choose a dyadic distribution close to uniform. Let \(D\) be a power of two satisfying \(D\geq m/\gamma\), and write \(D=mh+R\), where \(0\leq R<m\). Give \(R\) classes probability \((h+1)/D\), and the remaining classes probability \(h/D\). Then the probabilities sum to one and satisfy

\( \dfrac{1-\gamma}{m}\leq p_j\leq\dfrac{1+\gamma}{m}. \)

Every constructed numerical input is dyadic and has polynomial encoding length.

4. Proven NO-case bound: splitting mass cannot help

Let \(q_{t,S}\) be any terminal state attainable after these \(K\) arrivals, including one produced with full advance knowledge or unlimited computation. Define its total bundle-size mass by

\( C=\sum_{t,S}q_{t,S}|S|. \)

Every transfer of mass \(x_{t,S}\) increases \(C\) by exactly \(x_{t,S}\). Initially \(C=0\), and every arrival transfers at most \(\eta\). Hence

\( C\leq K\eta=k. \)

Moreover, transfers conserve advertiser mass, so

\( \sum_{t,S}q_{t,S}=1. \)

We must handle bundles larger than \(2k\), where the stated coverage gap need not apply. Put

\( b=1-1/k,\qquad f(x)=1-b^x,\qquad d=f'(k)=-b^k\log b>0. \)

Since

\( f''(x)=-b^x(\log b)^2<0, \)

concavity gives

\( f(x)\leq f(k)+d(x-k). \)

Thus, for \(|S|\leq2k\), the NO-case coverage bound implies

\( \dfrac{w_t(S)}{|U|}\leq f(k)+\varepsilon+d(|S|-k). \)

The same affine bound holds for \(|S|\geq2k\). Indeed, at \(x=2k\) its right-hand side is

\( f(k)+\varepsilon+dk =1+\varepsilon+b^k\bigl(-1-k\log b\bigr)\geq1, \)

because \( -\log(1-1/k)\geq1/k \). The affine function is increasing, while every coverage valuation is at most \(|U|\).

Consequently, the affine bound holds for every bundle. Averaging it against advertiser mass yields

\( \begin{aligned} \dfrac{W}{|U|} &\leq\sum_{t,S}q_{t,S}\bigl(f(k)+\varepsilon+d(|S|-k)\bigr)\\ &=f(k)+\varepsilon+d(C-k)\\ &\leq f(k)+\varepsilon. \end{aligned} \)

Define the exactly computable rational threshold

\( \beta=1-(1-1/k)^k+\varepsilon. \)

Our parameter choices give

\( W\leq\beta|U|,\qquad \beta\leq c+\delta/4<1. \)

This bound holds for every realized sequence and every feasible allocation. It explicitly allows arbitrary splitting of advertiser mass.

5. Proven YES-case bound: the offline benchmark is nearly full coverage

Let \(N_j\) count arrivals of class \(j\), and put

\( r_0=\lfloor(1-2\gamma)r\rfloor. \)

For each class, \(N_j\) is binomial and satisfies

\( (1-\gamma)r\leq\mathbb E[N_j]\leq(1+\gamma)r,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(N_j)\leq(1+\gamma)r. \)

Because \(r_0\leq(1-2\gamma)r\), Chebyshev’s inequality gives

\( \Pr(N_j<r_0)\leq\dfrac{1+\gamma}{\gamma^2r}. \)

The union bound and the choice of \(r\) therefore imply

\( \Pr(N_j\geq r_0\text{ for every }j)\geq1-\gamma. \)

On this event, the following allocation is feasible. Within each labeled type, distinguish \(r_0\) portions of mass \(\eta\). Whenever a class \(j\in S_t\) arrives for the \(h\)-th time, with \(h\leq r_0\), allocate that packet to portion \(h\) of type \(t\). Discard later occurrences.

Each class belongs to exactly one \(S_t\). Each designated portion receives that class only once, so every move is from a pre-arrival bundle lacking the arriving class. The move has mass exactly \(\eta\), respects the packet constraint, and is feasible regardless of arrival order. Aggregating portions with the same bundle gives precisely legal transfers in the stated model.

Every designated portion ultimately holds \(S_t\) and obtains value \(|U|\). Thus, on the event above,

\( \mathrm{OPT}\geq Nr_0\eta\,|U|=\dfrac{r_0}{r}|U|. \)

Since \(r\geq1/\gamma\),

\( \dfrac{r_0}{r}\geq1-2\gamma-\dfrac1r\geq1-3\gamma. \)

It follows that

\( \mathbb E[\mathrm{OPT}] \geq(1-\gamma)(1-3\gamma)|U| \geq(1-4\gamma)|U|. \)

The covering indices are used only to prove this benchmark lower bound; the proposed policy is not supplied with them.

6. Extracting a one-sided randomized decision algorithm

Suppose the requested polynomial-time causal policy exists. Run it on the constructed instance, sampling arrivals from the explicitly known dyadic distribution.

In the YES case, its guarantee gives

\( \begin{aligned} \dfrac{\mathbb E[W]}{|U|} &\geq(c+\delta)(1-4\gamma)\\ &\geq c+\delta-4\gamma\\ &\geq c+3\delta/4, \end{aligned} \)

where the second inequality uses \(c+\delta<1\).

Accept precisely when

\( W>\beta|U|. \)

In a NO instance, Section 4 proves that acceptance is impossible.

In a YES instance, write \(Z=W/|U|\). Since \(0\leq Z\leq1\),

\( \mathbb E[Z]\leq\beta+(1-\beta)\Pr(Z>\beta). \)

Therefore,

\( \Pr(Z>\beta) \geq\dfrac{\mathbb E[Z]-\beta}{1-\beta} \geq\dfrac{\delta}{2}. \)

For fixed \(\delta\), a constant number of independent repetitions raises the acceptance probability above \(1/2\), while retaining zero acceptance probability on NO instances.

The acceptance test is polynomial-time. Under the usual explicit-transfer meaning of the requested running time, a polynomial-time policy writes only polynomially many nonzero rational transfers. Each transfer creates at most one new occupied bundle, so its state has polynomial support. Exact state updates, explicitly listed coverage evaluations, and comparison with the rational threshold \(\beta|U|\) consequently take polynomial time. Sampling the arrivals is also polynomial-time, and the constructed \(K\) is polynomial.

We have obtained a polynomial-time randomized algorithm with one-sided error for the NP-hard gap problem. Composing with its gap reduction puts an NP-complete language in \(\mathrm{RP}\), and hence implies \(\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}\).

7. Scope of the conclusion

This proves the same conditional complexity obstruction as in the discrete-agent setting, directly for the packet-mass model. The essential additional argument is the affine bound in Section 4: it controls all bundle sizes and remains valid after arbitrary mass splitting.

No unconditional separation of \(\mathrm{NP}\) and \(\mathrm{RP}\), or converse assertion under their equality, is claimed.
