The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item types, a finite catalogue \(T\) of complete submodular or XOS valuation types, masses \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), per-capita supplies \(b\), valuation-oracle access, and bounded supporting-price parameters, design a universally truthful randomized mechanism using \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) type-level queries that outputs an aggregate allocation \(x=(x_{t,S})\) satisfying \(\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{SW}(x)]\ge \operatorname{OPT}_{\infty}/O((\log\log m)^3)\); require posted prices and payments and, whenever \(N\mu_t\) and \(Nb_j\) are integral, universally truthful integral \(N\)-agent refinements whose normalized allocations converge to \(x\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\ge2\) item types; each person may receive at most one copy of each type. A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible valuations. Supplies \(b\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\) are per capita.
Each type has a complete, normalized, monotone, nonnegative submodular or XOS valuation \(v_t\), accessed through value and demand oracles, including demand restricted to available item types. Given public rational bounds \(0<\psi_{\min}\le\psi_{\max}\) with \(\psi_{\max}/\psi_{\min}\le m^c\) for a fixed constant \(c\), every valuation admits an XOS representation whose additive coefficients belong to \(\{0\}\cup[\psi_{\min},\psi_{\max}]\). No clause oracle is supplied. Let \(L\) include the binary input length and maximum oracle-answer bit length.
An allocation consists of masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le b_j\). Define \(\operatorname{SW}(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}_{\infty}=\max_x\operatorname{SW}(x)\) over these allocations; output allocations in sparse rational form.
Valuations are private, and the input masses describe the reported profile, rather than an independently certified distribution. Mechanisms must be defined on all admissible reported profiles. Persons have quasilinear utility and may decline purchase; allocated bundles are bought at nonnegative posted item prices, with payment equal to their sum. Fix report-independent tie-breaking.
For every positive integer \(N\) with integral \(N\mu_t\) and \(Nb_j\), a refinement is a universally truthful posted-price mechanism for \(N\) persons and \(Nb_j\) copies of item type \(j\), permitting arbitrary individual reports in the admissible valuation class. At truthful profiles with \(N\mu_t\) persons of each type, its bundle counts divided by \(N\) must converge in probability in \(\ell_1\) to the random output \(x\), under a common coupling, as eligible \(N\to\infty\). Refinements must run in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,N,L)\) oracle time.
Does there exist a universally truthful randomized posted-price mechanism satisfying these refinement requirements, using \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time and type-level value and demand queries, and achieving \(\mathbb E[\operatorname{SW}(x)]\ge\operatorname{OPT}_{\infty}/O((\max\{1,\log\log m\})^3)\), or is this guarantee impossible?
For finite single-copy auctions under the stated supporting-coefficient bounds, [Assadi–Singla](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.02716) obtain universal truthfulness and an expected \(O((\log\log m)^3)\)-approximation using \(O(n)\) demand queries and polynomial time.
For Bayesian XOS auctions, [Feldman–Gravin–Lucier](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.4916) establish posted prices attaining half the expected optimal welfare. Neither result addresses the prescribed mass profile together with the finite-refinement requirement above.

An optimal limiting mechanism exists

Under the stated refinement requirement, the answer is yes. In fact, one can achieve the stronger guarantee
\( \operatorname{SW}(x)=\operatorname{OPT}_{\infty} \).

The construction uses a lottery over posted item-price vectors. Its finite refinements learn the reported profile from a sample whose members receive nothing, and leave a vanishing fraction of the remaining persons unserved.

Two features of the question matter: prices may differ between persons, and the refinements need only converge asymptotically. The construction below does not provide a uniform convergence rate or a welfare guarantee for every finite refinement.

1. Fixing demand choices

Fix a total order on bundles, independent of all reports. At any price vector and availability set, choose the first utility-maximizing bundle in this order. Use the same order throughout.

Write \( D_t(p) \) for this selected demand at nonnegative item prices \( p \), with all item types available.

The tie-breaking has the following useful consistency property:

If \( D_t(p) \) remains available after some items are removed, it is still the selected demand.

Indeed, its utility was maximal over the larger collection of bundles, and every bundle preceding it in the fixed order had strictly smaller utility.

This tie-breaking can be implemented with the given oracles. Order bundles by their binary incidence codes. For a rational query price vector, rationality and the answer-length bound give an inverse-exponential lower bound on any nonzero difference between bundle utilities. Adding a sufficiently small positive price perturbation proportional to the binary item weights therefore selects the smallest-code maximizer without changing the primary utility maximization. The perturbation has polynomial bit length. The posted prices themselves remain the original prices.

2. A joint-configuration LP

A joint configuration is a tuple \( A=(S_t)_{t\in T} \), specifying one bundle for every reported type. Define its aggregate consumption and welfare by

\( d_j(A)=\sum_t\mu_t\mathbf 1_{\{j\in S_t\}} \),

\( w(A)=\sum_t\mu_t v_t(S_t) \).

Consider the finite, exponentially large LP

\( \displaystyle \max_y\sum_A y_Aw(A) \)

subject to

\[ \displaystyle \sum_Ay_A=1,\qquad \sum_Ay_Ad_j(A)\le b_j\quad(j\in M),\qquad y_A\ge0. \tag{P} \]

Its optimum equals \( \operatorname{OPT}_{\infty} \).

To prove this, a feasible \( y \) induces

\( \displaystyle x_{t,S}=\mu_t\sum_{A:S_t=S}y_A \).

These masses satisfy the original allocation constraints and preserve welfare.

Conversely, given a feasible \( x \), independently choose \( S_t \) with probability \( x_{t,S_t}/\mu_t \) for every type. The resulting distribution over joint configurations has precisely the same expected consumption and welfare. Thus both LPs have the same optimum.

The dual of \( \mathrm{(P)} \) is

\( \displaystyle \min_{\alpha,p}\alpha+\sum_jb_jp_j \)

subject to

\[ \displaystyle \alpha+\sum_jp_jd_j(A)\ge w(A)\quad\text{for every }A,\qquad p\ge0, \tag{D} \]

where \( \alpha \) is unrestricted.

For any nonnegative \( p \),

\[ \displaystyle \max_A\bigl(w(A)-p\cdot d(A)\bigr) =\sum_t\mu_t\max_S\bigl(v_t(S)-p(S)\bigr). \tag{1} \]

The equality follows because each type’s bundle can be chosen independently.

Consequently, demand queries at the same price vector \( p \) return a maximizing joint configuration

\( A(p)=(D_t(p))_{t\in T} \).

Value queries then determine \( w(A(p)) \). This gives a strong separation oracle for \( \mathrm{(D)} \): first reject negative coordinates of \( p \); otherwise, query \( A(p) \) and return its constraint if violated.

3. Recovering an optimal lottery over posted prices

We use the standard exact rational LP theorem with primal–dual recovery: a rational LP given by a polyhedral separation oracle can be optimized, with a polynomial-size exact dual certificate, in polynomial oracle time under polynomial coefficient-bit bounds. A formulation covering exact recovery is given by [Dadush–Végh–Zambelli](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/veghl/papers/Dadush-Vegh-Zambelli-SODA-2022.pdf).

Here its hypotheses hold:

  • The optimization dimension is \( m+1 \).
  • Every joint-configuration constraint has coefficient bit length polynomial in \( m,\tau,L \), because its coefficients are sums of the given rational masses and oracle values.
  • Separation returns an actual constraint of the finite system.
  • The dual is feasible, and its optimum is finite and attained: this follows from finite LP duality and feasibility and boundedness of \( \mathrm{(P)} \).
  • Its recession cone is explicitly

\( \{(\Delta\alpha,\Delta p):\Delta\alpha\ge0,\ \Delta p\ge0\} \).
Necessity uses the empty joint configuration; sufficiency follows from \( d_j(A)\ge0 \). Thus the recession-cone oracle required by that formulation is also available.

Every joint-configuration row supplied by the separation oracle comes from demand at a nonnegative price vector. The empty configuration can likewise be annotated with a posted price vector: take every item price greater than \( m\psi_{\max} \), since \( v_t(S)\le m\psi_{\max} \).

Exact primal–dual recovery therefore produces an optimal solution of \( \mathrm{(P)} \) supported on polynomially many configurations, each annotated with a nonnegative rational price vector inducing it. Solving the resulting explicit restricted primal LP and choosing an optimal basic solution reduces the number of positive configuration variables to at most \( m+1 \).

We obtain price vectors \( p^1,\ldots,p^K \) and rational probabilities \( \lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_K \), where \( K\le m+1 \), such that

\[ \displaystyle \sum_k\lambda_k=1,\qquad \sum_k\lambda_k\sum_t\mu_t\mathbf 1_{\{j\in D_t(p^k)\}}\le b_j, \tag{2} \]

and

\[ \displaystyle \sum_k\lambda_k\sum_t\mu_tv_t(D_t(p^k)) =\operatorname{OPT}_{\infty}. \tag{3} \]

The entire computation uses \( \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \) time and type-level value and demand queries. No supporting-clause oracle is used.

Fix a deterministic implementation of this computation. It can be made invariant under the ordering or duplication of identical reported valuations: the LP solver receives only the aggregate answers \( (d(A(p)),w(A(p))) \), along with \( b \). Any numerical bounds needed by the exact solver can be increased deterministically until a primal–dual optimality certificate is verified. Hence the resulting menu depends on these aggregate oracle functions, rather than on a particular support encoding.

4. The mass mechanism

Offer each person a price vector drawn from the lottery \( \lambda \), independently of that person’s report, and let them purchase their selected demanded bundle.

The type-level output is the deterministic rational allocation

\[ \displaystyle x_{t,S} =\mu_t\sum_{k:D_t(p^k)=S}\lambda_k. \tag{4} \]

Equation (2) proves feasibility, and equation (3) proves optimal welfare. There are at most \( \tau(m+1) \) nonzero entries before identical bundles are combined.

At the nonatomic level, changing one person’s report does not change the reported mass profile and therefore does not change their price lottery. Conditional on the offered prices, truthful demand maximizes their quasilinear utility. The empty bundle permits declining purchase.

The following refinements establish exact individual truthfulness in finite societies, without treating the reported masses as certified information.

5. Universally truthful finite refinements

For each eligible \( N \), construct a mechanism using only public supplies, public coefficient bounds, and individual reports.

5.1 An excluded sample

Choose a sample of \( k_N=\lfloor N^{1/2}\rfloor \) persons independently of reports. Sampled persons receive nothing and pay nothing, regardless of what they report.

If \( 2^m>N \), allocate nothing. Otherwise, query every bundle value of every sampled person and group identical valuation tables.

This exhaustive identification costs at most \( N2^m\le N^2 \) value queries. For every fixed mass-profile instance, the condition \( 2^m\le N \) eventually holds.

Let \( \widehat\mu_u \) denote the sample frequency of each distinct sampled valuation.

5.2 Recovering the rational masses

Set

\( Q_N=\lfloor N^{1/32}\rfloor,\qquad \varepsilon_N=\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{1/8}\rfloor} \),

with arbitrary harmless conventions for small \( N \).

For each sample frequency, seek a positive rational number with denominator at most \( Q_N \) within distance \( \varepsilon_N \). Require uniqueness, and require that the recovered masses sum to one. If any check fails, allocate nothing.

This bounded-denominator rational reconstruction is computable in polynomial time by continued fractions. Uniqueness eventually holds because distinct rationals with denominators at most \( Q_N \) differ by at least \( Q_N^{-2} \), while \( 2\varepsilon_N<Q_N^{-2} \) for sufficiently large \( N \).

Run the deterministic price-lottery algorithm from Section 3 on the recovered profile, using the actual public per-capita supplies \( b \).

5.3 Selling

Choose a dyadic \( \eta_N \) satisfying

\( N^{-1/8}\le\eta_N\le2N^{-1/8} \)

for sufficiently large \( N \).

Independently mark each nonsampled person for service with probability \( 1-\eta_N \). Visit marked persons in a report-independent order. Each gets an independently drawn posted-price vector from the computed lottery and buys their selected demand among the item types still in stock.

To ensure bounded random-bit complexity, replace the rational lottery by a dyadic lottery \( \lambda^{(N)} \) with the same support and

\( \|\lambda^{(N)}-\lambda\|_1\le N^{-3} \).

This requires polynomially many bits. Uniform sampling can similarly use \( O(\log N) \)-bit random priorities, with any collision outcome producing no allocation. Taking sufficiently many priority bits makes the collision probability \( O(N^{-2}) \).

5.4 Universal truthfulness

Fix all random choices.

A sampled person always receives nothing, so every report gives utility zero.

For a nonsampled person, their report does not affect the learned menu, their service decision, or their offered prices. Availability before their turn depends only on previous persons’ purchases. At their single purchasing opportunity, truthful demand maximizes utility over their available menu, including declining purchase.

Thus every mechanism obtained by fixing the random seed is truthful. The refinement is universally truthful on all admissible reported profiles, including profiles unrelated to the one being refined.

Its oracle time is \( \operatorname{poly}(m,N,L) \): exhaustive sample identification is used only when \( 2^m\le N \), the recovered support has at most \( N \) types, and the remaining steps are polynomial-time LP computation and at most one restricted demand computation per purchaser.

6. Convergence to the prescribed output

Fix a truthful rational mass-profile instance. If several listed types have identical valuation functions, combine their masses for this argument; the aggregate LP oracle is unchanged.

6.1 Exact profile recovery occurs with probability tending to one

Every positive-mass valuation appears in the sample with probability tending to one. Conditional on successful random-priority sampling, the sample is uniform without replacement.

For each true valuation type \( u \), its sample frequency has variance at most \( 1/(4k_N) \). Hence

\( \displaystyle \Pr\!\left(\left|\widehat\mu_u-\mu_u\right|> \frac{\varepsilon_N}{2}\right) \le\frac{1}{k_N\varepsilon_N^2} =O(N^{-1/4}). \)

There are finitely many types, so all frequency estimates simultaneously satisfy these bounds with probability tending to one.

Every true mass has a fixed finite denominator, eventually at most \( Q_N \). On the simultaneous estimation event, uniqueness of rational reconstruction therefore recovers exactly the true masses. It also recovers exactly the true valuation support.

Thus, with probability tending to one, the refinement computes exactly the limiting price lottery before its dyadic approximation.

6.2 Supply restrictions alter no purchases with probability tending to one

Work on the exact-recovery event.

For analysis, draw service indicators and price vectors for all \( N \) persons, including sampled persons, and consider their demands with full availability. Let \( Z_j \) count the persons marked for service whose ideal bundle contains item \( j \).

Put

\( \displaystyle d_j=\sum_k\lambda_k\sum_t\mu_t \mathbf 1_{\{j\in D_t(p^k)\}}. \)

By (2), \( d_j\le b_j \). Independence of the hypothetical draws gives

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E[Z_j]\le(1-\eta_N)N(b_j+N^{-3}), \qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z_j)\le N. \)

For every fixed \( b_j>0 \), sufficiently large \( N \) therefore satisfy

\( \displaystyle Nb_j-\mathbb E[Z_j]\ge\frac12\eta_NNb_j. \)

Chebyshev’s inequality yields

\[ \displaystyle \Pr(Z_j>Nb_j) \le\frac{4}{\eta_N^2Nb_j^2} =O(N^{-3/4}b_j^{-2})\longrightarrow0. \tag{5} \]

If \( b_j=0 \), equation (2) implies that every positive-probability lottery configuration excludes \( j \) for every positive-mass type. The dyadic approximation introduces no new configurations, so \( Z_j=0 \).

A union bound shows that all ideal demands fit within supply with probability tending to one. Actual purchases omit sampled persons, so their ideal demands also fit.

On this event, induction through the selling order shows that every served person receives their ideal bundle. An exhausted item cannot belong to a subsequent ideal bundle, since that would contradict feasibility of the total ideal counts. The tie-breaking consistency from Section 1 then applies.

6.3 Bundle counts converge

For each fixed type \( t \) and bundle \( S \), independence of the hypothetical service and price draws gives

\( \displaystyle \frac{\#\{\text{type-}t\text{ persons ideally served }S\}}{N} \xrightarrow{\Pr} \mu_t\sum_{k:D_t(p^k)=S}\lambda_k. \)

This follows directly from variance at most \( N \), together with \( \eta_N\to0 \) and \( \|\lambda^{(N)}-\lambda\|_1\to0 \).

Removing the sample changes normalized counts by at most \( k_N/N\to0 \). Profile-recovery failures and supply failures have probability tending to zero. Unserved persons receive the empty bundle, so its count converges as well.

For the fixed instance there are finitely many type–bundle coordinates. Coordinatewise convergence consequently implies

\( \|x^{(N)}-x\|_1\xrightarrow{\Pr}0. \)

Place the random choices for all eligible refinements on one product probability space, together with the deterministic \( x \). This supplies the required common coupling.

7. Conclusion and scope

The mechanism outputs an optimal sparse rational mass allocation in \( \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) \) oracle time and has universally truthful posted-price refinements satisfying the required convergence. Therefore it achieves the requested approximation, with the stronger guarantee

\( \mathbb E[\operatorname{SW}(x)]=\operatorname{OPT}_{\infty}. \)

The refinement may need extremely large \( N \) before it identifies rare types and reconstructs their rational masses. This is compatible with the stated asymptotic requirement. The construction also uses different independently selected price vectors for different persons; it does not impose a single common posted-price vector.
