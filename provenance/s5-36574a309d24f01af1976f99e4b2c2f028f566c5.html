The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a bipartite type graph \(G=(I,J,E)\), positive rational type masses \(a_i,b_j\) with \(\sum_i a_i+\sum_j b_j=1\), and rational surpluses \(w_{ij}>0\), let \(x_{ij}\geq 0\) be a matching mass flow with \(\sum_j x_{ij}\leq a_i\) and \(\sum_i x_{ij}\leq b_j\), and let \(W^*\) be its maximum total surplus. Call a type essential if its full mass is matched in every maximum-surplus flow. A core payoff vector consists of nonnegative per-agent payoffs \(p_i,q_j\) satisfying \(p_i+q_j\geq w_{ij}\) for every edge and \(\sum_i a_i p_i+\sum_j b_j q_j=W^*\). For a common denominator \(D\) of the masses, list \(Da_i\) copies of \(p_i\) and \(Db_j\) copies of \(q_j\) for essential types, sort the list in nondecreasing order, and compute a core payoff vector whose list is lexicographically maximal.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G=(I,J,E)\) be a bipartite graph with \(E\neq\varnothing\), \(m=|E|\), and positive rational edge surpluses \(w_{ij}\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) types with positive masses \(a_i,b_j\), each encoded as a binary rational, with \(\sum_i a_i+\sum_j b_j=1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A matching mass flow has \(x_{ij}\geq0\) for \((i,j)\in E\), with \(\sum_{j:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}\leq a_i\) and \(\sum_{i:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}\leq b_j\). Define \(W^*=\max_x\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}\). A type is essential if its capacity constraint is tight in every maximizing flow.
A core payoff vector consists of nonnegative rational per-agent payoffs \(p_i,q_j\) satisfying \(p_i+q_j\geq w_{ij}\) for every edge and \(\sum_i a_ip_i+\sum_j b_jq_j=W^*\).
Let \(D\) be the least common multiple of the reduced mass denominators. For essential types only, form a list containing \(Da_i\) copies of \(p_i\) and \(Db_j\) copies of \(q_j\), and sort it in nondecreasing order. A leximin core payoff vector lexicographically maximizes this list; equal entries require no tie-breaking. The output is the vector indexed by types.

Is computing a leximin core payoff vector polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite assignment games, a leximin core imputation is computable in strongly polynomial time using \(O(mn^3)\) arithmetic operations, where \(n\) is the number of agents ([Vazirani](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11437v6)).
For bipartite capacity matching games, polynomial-time algorithms compute leximin Owen-set imputations, but their objective sorts total payoffs to capacity-owning vertices, rather than the repeated per-agent payoffs defined here ([Gangam et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06037v3#A4.SS1)). No result for exactly this mass-based objective was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required at least one edge, reflecting the positive-worth assumption underlying the discrete result.
  • Defined m as the number of edges, tau as the total number of supported types, and L as the total binary input length.
  • Selected the least common multiple of reduced mass denominators to make the replicated list canonical.
  • Specified nonnegative rational payoffs, consistent with the discrete imputation domain.
  • Specified that the output is indexed by types and that running time is measured against the compact input, rather than the replicated population size.

1. Result. Computing the specified leximin core payoff vector is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, the algorithm uses \(O(\tau^2)\) rational linear programs and never constructs the repeated list.

The key fact is that, over a compact convex feasible set, positive repetition counts do not change the leximin-maximizing coordinate vector. We prove this fact explicitly and verify polynomial bit complexity.

2. The core and essential types. Write \(V=I\cup J\), and let \(c_v\) denote the mass of type \(v\). Write \(z_v=p_v\) on \(I\) and \(z_v=q_v\) on \(J\).

First compute \(W^*\) using the matching-flow LP in the question. Its feasible set is nonempty and compact: the zero flow is feasible, and every edge flow is bounded by its endpoint capacities. Because an edge exists and its surplus and endpoint masses are positive, \(W^*>0\).

The dual LP minimizes \(\sum_{v\in V}c_vz_v\), subject to nonnegativity and the edge inequalities. Consequently, strong LP duality identifies the real core with

\[ C=\left\{z\in\mathbb R^\tau: z_v\geq0,\quad z_i+z_j\geq w_{ij}\ \ ((i,j)\in E),\quad \sum_{v\in V}c_vz_v=W^* \right\}. \]

Here and below, allowing real coordinates temporarily enlarges the domain; the algorithm will produce a rational optimum.

The dual is feasible, for example by assigning the maximum edge surplus to every left type and zero to every right type. Strong duality therefore gives \(C\neq\varnothing\). Moreover, every \(z\in C\) satisfies \(0\leq z_v\leq W^*/c_v\). Thus \(C\) is a compact rational polytope.

For a feasible flow \(x\), let \(\ell_v(x)\) be its total flow incident to \(v\). The following identity holds for every primal-feasible \(x\) and dual-feasible \(z\):

\[ \sum_{v\in V}c_vz_v-\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij} = \sum_{v\in V}z_v\bigl(c_v-\ell_v(x)\bigr) + \sum_{(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}\bigl(z_i+z_j-w_{ij}\bigr). \]

Every summand on the right is nonnegative. If \(x\) is optimal and \(z\in C\), the left side is zero, so every summand is zero.

In particular, every nonessential type has zero payoff throughout \(C\). Indeed, nonessentiality provides an optimal flow \(x\) with \(\ell_v(x)<c_v\); the identity then forces \(z_v=0\) for every \(z\in C\).

Essentiality is itself computable by LP. For each \(v\), maximize \(c_v-\ell_v(x)\) subject to the flow constraints and \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}=W^*\). The optimum is zero exactly when \(v\) is essential. These are \(\tau\) bounded, feasible rational LPs.

Let \(S\subseteq V\) be the resulting essential set. It is nonempty: otherwise every core coordinate would be zero, contradicting \(W^*>0\).

3. A lemma about repetitions. Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be nonempty, compact, and convex. Choose a nonempty coordinate set \(S\), and assign each \(v\in S\) a positive integer multiplicity \(h_v\). For \(z\in K\), let \(A_h(z)\) be the nondecreasing list containing \(h_v\) copies of \(z_v\).

We give an algorithm whose output maximizes \(A_h(z)\) for every choice of the positive multiplicities.

Start with \(K_0=K\) and \(R_1=S\). At stage \(s\), maximize the minimum remaining coordinate:

\[ \lambda_s=\max_{z\in K_{s-1}}\min_{v\in R_s}z_v. \]

Equivalently, maximize \(t\) subject to \(z\in K_{s-1}\) and \(t\leq z_v\) for every \(v\in R_s\). Define

\[ K_s=\{z\in K_{s-1}:z_v\geq\lambda_s\text{ for every }v\in R_s\}, \]

and

\[ F_s=\{v\in R_s:z_v=\lambda_s\text{ for every }z\in K_s\}. \]

Remove these fixed coordinates by setting \(R_{s+1}=R_s\setminus F_s\).

The maximum defining \(\lambda_s\) exists by compactness, so \(K_s\) is nonempty, compact, and convex.

We next prove that \(F_s\neq\varnothing\). Suppose it were empty. For each \(v\in R_s\), choose \(z^{(v)}\in K_s\) with \(z^{(v)}_v>\lambda_s\). All remaining coordinates of every such point are at least \(\lambda_s\). Their average belongs to \(K_s\) by convexity, and every remaining coordinate of the average is strictly greater than \(\lambda_s\). This contradicts the definition of \(\lambda_s\).

Thus the algorithm terminates after at most \(|S|\) stages, and every coordinate in \(S\) is constant on the final set.

To prove optimality, we use a simple multiset observation: adding the same multiset to two equal-length lists preserves their sorted lexicographic comparison. Indeed, consider the smallest value whose multiplicities differ between the two lists. The list with more copies of that value is lexicographically smaller. Adding a common multiset leaves all multiplicity differences unchanged.

Let \(z^*\) belong to the final set, and consider \(y\in K\). If \(y\) belongs to the final set, its coordinates on \(S\) equal those of \(z^*\). Otherwise, let \(s\) be the first stage for which \(y\notin K_s\). Then \(y\in K_{s-1}\), and

\[ \min_{v\in R_s}y_v<\lambda_s \quad\text{while}\quad \min_{v\in R_s}z^*_v\geq\lambda_s. \]

All coordinates fixed at earlier stages agree between \(y\) and \(z^*\). Cancel their copies from both lists. Since every remaining multiplicity is positive, the remaining list for \(z^*\) has a strictly larger minimum entry than the remaining list for \(y\). The multiset observation now implies that \(A_h(z^*)\) is lexicographically strictly larger than \(A_h(y)\).

This proves the lemma. It also proves that the maximizing coordinate vector on \(S\) is unique and independent of the positive multiplicities.

4. Applying the lemma to the society. Apply the construction with \(K=C\), the essential set \(S\), and \(h_v=Dc_v\). These multiplicities are positive integers by the definition of \(D\). Hence the resulting vector maximizes exactly the repeated list specified in the question.

At each stage, compute \(\lambda_s\) by one LP. To find \(F_s\), solve, for each \(v\in R_s\),

\[ M_{s,v}=\max\{z_v:z\in K_s\}. \]

Because \(z_v\geq\lambda_s\) throughout \(K_s\), we have \(v\in F_s\) exactly when \(M_{s,v}=\lambda_s\). Exact rational LP optimization makes this comparison exact.

At termination, output \(z_v=\lambda_s\) for \(v\in F_s\), and output zero for every nonessential type. The final feasible set is nonempty; all its essential coordinates have these assigned values, and its nonessential coordinates are zero. Therefore the output belongs to \(C\).

The lemma proves that this is the unique type-indexed leximin core vector. It also shows that the same vector would result from giving each essential type multiplicity one.

The LP count is at most

\[ 1+\tau+\sum_s(1+|R_s|)=O(\tau^2). \]

All LPs have polynomially many variables and constraints. No computation or expansion of \(D\) is required.

5. Polynomial bit complexity. A polynomial number of LP calls alone is insufficient unless the successive thresholds have polynomial encoding length. We now establish that bound.

We use the standard rational-LP theorem: feasibility and optimization are solvable in time polynomial in the binary encoding length; a feasible LP with finite optimum admits an optimal rational solution and optimum value of polynomial encoding length. This theorem applies to the initial matching LP, so \(W^*\), and hence the description of \(C\), have polynomial encoding length.

Let \(z^*\) be the vector proved optimal above, and let \(k=|S|\). Choose a permutation \(\sigma\) of \(S\) that orders its coordinates nondecreasingly. Consider the order-restricted polytope

\[ C_\sigma=C\cap \{z:z_{\sigma(1)}\leq z_{\sigma(2)}\leq\cdots\leq z_{\sigma(k)}\}. \]

Its description has polynomial encoding length: it adds only inequalities with coefficients from \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

By the lemma with multiplicities one, \(z^*\) lexicographically maximizes the tuple \((z_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,z_{\sigma(k)})\) over \(C_\sigma\). We claim that \(z^*\) is a vertex of \(C_\sigma\).

Otherwise, write \(z^*=(u+v)/2\) for distinct \(u,v\in C_\sigma\). Nonessential coordinates vanish throughout \(C_\sigma\), so \(u\) and \(v\) differ on \(S\). At their first differing coordinate in the order \(\sigma\), one of them exceeds \(z^*\); at every preceding coordinate both equal \(z^*\). That point has a lexicographically larger tuple, a contradiction.

Vertices of a rational polytope have polynomial encoding length. For completeness, a vertex in \(\mathbb R^\tau\) is determined by \(\tau\) linearly independent active constraint rows. Otherwise, a nonzero direction annihilating every active row would allow sufficiently small feasible movements in both directions, contradicting extremality. Clear denominators in these rows and apply Cramer’s rule. If the polytope description has encoding length \(B\), the resulting integer entries have magnitude at most \(2^{O(B)}\); Hadamard’s determinant bound gives numerator and denominator bit lengths \(O(\tau(B+\log\tau))\).

Thus every coordinate of \(z^*\) has polynomial encoding length. Every stage fixes at least one coordinate to \(\lambda_s\), and that coordinate retains this value in \(z^*\). Consequently, every threshold \(\lambda_s\) also has polynomial encoding length.

All subsequent LPs therefore have polynomial encoding length: they use the constraints of \(C\), coordinate bounds with these thresholds, and, for the max–min step, one additional variable with unit coefficients. The rational-LP theorem applies to every call, establishing polynomial running time in \(m,\tau,L\).

6. Conclusion. The compact mass representation causes no exponential expansion. Convexity lets the algorithm fix universally minimal type payoffs, and the multiset cancellation argument proves that this handles the repeated per-agent objective exactly. Related successive-LP constructions for capacity-weighted vertex payoffs appear in [Gangam et al., Appendix D.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06037v3#A4.SS1); the argument above directly proves the objective posed here.
