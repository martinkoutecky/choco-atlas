Target and applicable theorem

The stated communication lower bound transfers directly to the distributional objective, already for \(\tau=2\). This conclusion uses the same utility-access and communication model as the finite-stakeholder result.

The cited [Proposition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28620v1#S2.SS1) states that, for even \(d\), any protocol that finds an exact maximizer of \(u_1(x)+u_2(x)\) over \(S^{d-1}_+\), with success probability at least \(2/3\) on every admissible pair of coordinate-wise monotone binary utilities, requires \(2^{\Omega(d)}\) communication bits in the worst case.

Proof of the distributional lower bound

For a supported society, define its welfare at \(x\in S^{d-1}_+\) by \(W(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tu_t(x)\).

Take any two-utility instance covered by the cited theorem. Construct a society with two supported types, their original utilities, and masses \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\). These masses are positive binary rationals and sum to \(1\). The feasible set and the monotone extensions of both utilities remain unchanged. Thus every hypothesis concerning utilities, masses, and feasible outcomes is satisfied.

For every feasible \(x\), we have \(W(x)=\frac12(u_1(x)+u_2(x))\). Consequently, for any feasible \(x,y\),

\(W(x)\geq W(y)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ u_1(x)+u_2(x)\geq u_1(y)+u_2(y)\).

This equivalence follows by multiplying both sides of the welfare comparison by the positive number \(2\). Hence

\(\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\in S^{d-1}_+}W(x)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\in S^{d-1}_+}(u_1(x)+u_2(x))\).

These maximizer sets are nonempty: the domain is nonempty, and the sum takes values in the finite set \(\{0,1,2\}\), so its largest attained value is attained at some feasible point. Continuity is unnecessary.

Now suppose a distributional protocol succeeds with probability at least \(2/3\) on every admissible society. Run it on the constructed society. The masses and type labels are fixed public information, so introducing them requires no communication. Keep each utility with the same participant as in the original communication model.

For every realization of the protocol’s randomness, its output maximizes distributional welfare exactly when it maximizes the original utility sum. Thus this simulation preserves both communication cost and success probability. The cited theorem therefore forces the distributional protocol to use \(2^{\Omega(d)}\) communication bits in the worst case, even with \(\tau=2\) and equal masses.

Scope of the conclusion

The parameter \(L\) does not change this reduction. The cited [Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28620v1#A7) constructs its hard utilities from subsets of a finite family of coordinate sets, so the hard instances admit finite descriptions.

However, exponential communication in \(d\) does not by itself establish superpolynomial running time in \(L\): the descriptions can already have length exponential in \(d\). The settled conclusion is the communication lower bound in the inherited model. No additional distributional lower-bound argument is needed.
