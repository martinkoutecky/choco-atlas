The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many good types \(I\), per-capita supplies \(b_i\), and \(\tau\) carrier types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), capacities, and values \(v_t(q)\) for each feasible package \(q\in Q_t\), find a welfare-maximizing mass allocation \(\lambda_{tq}\) and nonnegative type-addressed prices \(p_{tq}\), or report that none exist. Require \(\sum_{q\in Q_t}\lambda_{tq}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,q}q_i\lambda_{tq}\le b_i\), every package with \(\lambda_{tq}>0\) to maximize \(v_t(q)-p_{tq}\) for its type, and the allocation to maximize the seller’s revenue at those prices.

Result

The problem is solvable in polynomial time, and an equilibrium exists for every valid input.

The key observation is that the permitted prices \(p_{tq}=v_t(q)\) make every package a buyer best response. Computing the allocation still requires optimizing welfare, but that optimization is an explicitly given rational linear program.

Construction and proof

Define the welfare of a feasible allocation by
\(W(\lambda)=\sum_t\sum_{q\in Q_t}v_t(q)\lambda_{tq}\).

Solve the following linear program exactly:

\( \displaystyle \max_{\lambda} \sum_t\sum_{q\in Q_t}v_t(q)\lambda_{tq} \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle \lambda_{tq}\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{q\in Q_t}\lambda_{tq}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t, \)

\( \displaystyle \sum_t\sum_{q\in Q_t}q_i\lambda_{tq}\le b_i\quad\text{for every }i. \)

Let \(\lambda^*\) be a rational optimal solution, and set
\(p_{tq}=v_t(q)\) for every listed type and package.

We verify existence of \(\lambda^*\) and all required equilibrium conditions.

  • The allocation LP has a rational optimum. Since capacities are nonnegative, \(0\in Q_t\) for every type. Thus assigning \(\lambda_{t0}=\mu_t\) and zero mass to all other packages is feasible: it consumes no goods. Moreover, nonnegativity and the type mass equations imply \(0\le\lambda_{tq}\le\mu_t\). Hence the feasible region is nonempty, closed, and bounded. A linear objective attains its maximum on this polytope. Because its defining data are rational, an optimal vertex is rational.
  • Prices satisfy the restrictions. Valuations are nonnegative, so \(p_{tq}\ge0\). The normalization \(v_t(0)=0\) gives \(p_{t0}=0\).
  • Every assigned package maximizes buyer utility. For every \(t\) and \(q\in Q_t\),

\(v_t(q)-p_{tq}=0\).
Consequently, \(\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{s\in Q_t}(v_t(s)-p_{ts})=Q_t\). Every package receiving positive mass is therefore a best response. The stipulated treatment of ties permits the chosen mass splitting.

  • The seller maximizes revenue. For every \(\eta\in F\),

\(\sum_{t,q}p_{tq}\eta_{tq}=\sum_{t,q}v_t(q)\eta_{tq}=W(\eta)\).
Since \(\lambda^*\) maximizes \(W\) over \(F\), it also maximizes seller revenue over \(F\).

Thus \((\lambda^*,p)\) satisfies every requirement.

Running time and rational output size

The LP has \(N\) variables, \(\tau\) equality constraints, and \(m\) supply inequalities, together with nonnegativity constraints. Its dense binary encoding has length \(O(L+N(m+\tau))\): all nonconstant coefficients already occur in the input, and the remaining entries are zeros or ones.

Exact rational linear programming is solvable in polynomial bit time; see the [MIT notes on LP complexity and the ellipsoid method](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-854j-advanced-algorithms-fall-2008/908fe54d659623efadda2d033b895caf_notes_lp.pdf). Applied here, this gives total running time

\( \displaystyle (m+\tau+N+L)^{O(1)}. \)

Constructing the price table requires only copying the valuations, taking \(O(L)\) additional bit operations.

For completeness, the allocation also has polynomial output length. After clearing denominators in each constraint, every integer coefficient and right-hand side has \(O(L)\) bits. At a vertex, \(N\) linearly independent active constraints determine the allocation. Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound give \(O(N(L+\log N))\) bits per coordinate, hence \(O(N^2(L+\log N))\) bits for the whole allocation.

Why finite-buyer integrality is unnecessary here

Packages remain indivisible: each listed \(q\) is an integer vector. The decision variables, however, are continuous masses assigned to those packages. The construction splits mass only as expressly permitted by the task.

It neither enumerates individual carriers nor requires an integral allocation optimum. Type-dependent prices and unrestricted utility ties allow full value extraction, which directly aligns the seller’s objective with welfare.
