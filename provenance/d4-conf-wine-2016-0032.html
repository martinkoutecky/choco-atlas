The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite metric facility set \(F_1,\ldots,F_m\), rational per-capita capacities \(q_j\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), preferred points \(a_t\), a fixed priority order, and augmentation factor \(g\), compute the block-Serial-Dictatorship mass assignment \(x\), compute \(\operatorname{OPT}_q(\mu)=\min\{\sum_{t,j}d(a_t,F_j)z_{tj}:\sum_jz_{tj}=\mu_t,\ \sum_tz_{tj}\le q_j,\ z_{tj}\ge0\}\), and characterize the worst-case ratio \(R_g(\mu)=\sum_{t,j}d(a_t,F_j)x_{tj}/\operatorname{OPT}_q(\mu)\) as a function of \(g\) and the number of types \(\tau\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input specifies \(m\ge1\) facilities \(F_j\), preferred points \(a_t\), and a binary-rational distance matrix defining a metric on their distinct locations; coincident locations are allowed. Original per-capita capacities satisfy \(q_j\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) and \(\sum_jq_j\ge1\). The augmentation factor is an integer \(g\ge1\).
A fixed total priority order processes each type as one contiguous block. Block Serial Dictatorship starts with capacities \(gq_j\); it repeatedly sends as much of the current type's remaining mass as possible to its nearest facility with positive residual capacity, until that type is fully assigned. Distance ties are broken by facility index. Write \(x_{tj}\) for the resulting assigned mass.
Define \(\operatorname{OPT}_q(\mu)=\min\{\sum_{t,j}d(a_t,F_j)z_{tj}:\sum_jz_{tj}=\mu_t,\ \sum_tz_{tj}\le q_j,\ z_{tj}\ge0\}\). The associated computational tasks are to compute \(x\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}_q(\mu)\); measure running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length, including \(g\).
For inputs with \(\operatorname{OPT}_q(\mu)>0\), let \(R_g(I)=\sum_{t,j}d(a_t,F_j)x_{tj}/\operatorname{OPT}_q(\mu)\), and let \(W_g(\tau)=\sup_I R_g(I)\), over all such inputs with exactly \(\tau\) support types, arbitrary facility counts, and all priority orders.
What is the tight worst-case approximation ratio \(W_g(\tau)\) of block Serial Dictatorship, as a function of integer \(g\ge1\) and support size \(\tau\)?
Discrete SD with \(n\) agents has approximation upper bounds \(2^n-1\), \(\log_2(n+1)\), and \(g/(g-2)\) for \(g=1\), \(g=2\), and integer \(g\ge3\), respectively. Matching lower bounds occur for \(g=1\), for \(g=2\) at \(n=2^k-1\), and asymptotically as \(n\) grows for \(g\ge3\). [Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-022-01902-8).

Result

The tight ratio is

\[ \boxed{W_g(\tau)=\sum_{i=0}^{\tau-1}\left(\frac{2}{g}\right)^i.} \]

Equivalently,

\[ W_g(\tau)= \begin{cases} 2^\tau-1,&g=1,\\ \tau,&g=2,\\ \displaystyle\frac{g}{g-2}\left(1-\left(\frac{2}{g}\right)^\tau\right),&g\ge3. \end{cases} \]

Both computational tasks are solvable exactly in polynomial bit time in \(m,\tau,L\).

The [cited paper, Theorem 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-022-01902-8) bounds discrete SD using the number of individual agents. The following proof establishes the bound directly using the number of contiguous blocks.

1. Capacity-repair lemma

All distances below come from the given metric; coincident facility locations cause no difficulty.

Lemma. Let \(q,v,w\) be nonnegative facility-load vectors satisfying \(v_j,w_j\le q_j\) and \(\sum_jv_j=\sum_jw_j\). Suppose an assignment \(z\) has facility loads \(r_j=\sum_tz_{tj}\le q_j-v_j\).

Let \(h_{jk}\ge0\) transport \(w\) to \(v\), meaning

\[ \sum_kh_{jk}=w_j,\qquad \sum_jh_{jk}=v_k. \]

Then there is an assignment \(z'\), preserving every type’s assigned mass, with facility loads at most \(q_j-w_j\), and

\[ \operatorname{cost}(z') \le \operatorname{cost}(z)+\sum_{j,k}h_{jk}d(F_j,F_k). \]

Proof. Put \(b_j=w_j-v_j\), and write \(b_j^+=\max\{b_j,0\}\) and \(b_j^-=\max\{-b_j,0\}\).

The directed flow \(h\) has net outflow \(b_j\) at facility \(j\). Add a source sending \(b_j^+\) into each facility and a sink receiving \(b_j^-\). The resulting flow conserves mass at every facility. Decomposing it into source-to-sink paths and directed cycles, discard the cycles and replace each path between facilities \(j\) and \(k\) by a direct transfer. The triangle inequality shows that this produces a transport \(H\) satisfying

\[ \sum_kH_{jk}=b_j^+,\qquad \sum_jH_{jk}=b_k^-,\qquad \sum_{j,k}H_{jk}d(F_j,F_k) \le \sum_{j,k}h_{jk}d(F_j,F_k). \]

Here flow decomposition is finite: repeatedly extract a path or cycle and subtract its minimum positive edge flow, thereby eliminating at least one positive edge.

The amount that must leave facility \(j\) is

\[ \delta_j=\max\{r_j-(q_j-w_j),0\}. \]

Since \(r_j\le q_j-v_j\), we have \(\delta_j\le b_j^+\). Also \(\delta_j\le r_j\), because \(q_j-w_j\ge0\).

At every facility with \(b_j>0\), move \(\delta_j\) of its assigned mass, proportionally across types, using the destination proportions \(H_{jk}/b_j^+\). If \(\delta_j=0\), move nothing.

A source facility’s final load is \(r_j-\delta_j\le q_j-w_j\). A destination facility \(k\) receives at most \(b_k^-=v_k-w_k\), so its final load is at most

\[ r_k+b_k^-\le(q_k-v_k)+(v_k-w_k)=q_k-w_k. \]

Facilities with \(b_j=0\) need no change. Every type’s total mass is preserved.

Moving type \(t\) from facility \(j\) to facility \(k\) increases its per-unit cost by at most \(d(F_j,F_k)\), by the triangle inequality. Each transfer in \(H\) is used with a fraction at most one. Consequently, the total cost increase is at most the cost of \(H\), and hence at most the cost of \(h\). This proves the lemma.

2. Upper bound

Define

\[ C_k=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{2}{g}\right)^i. \]

Then \(C_1=1\), \(C_k=1+(2/g)C_{k-1}\), and \(C_k\ge C_{k-1}\).

We prove by induction that block SD’s cost is at most \(C_k\) times the original-capacity optimum whenever there are \(k\) positive-mass blocks. For the induction, allow arbitrary total mass and nonnegative capacities, subject to total capacity being at least total mass. Zero-capacity facilities can be omitted.

Base case

For one block, all mass has the same preferred point. Filling facilities in nondecreasing distance order minimizes that block’s cost subject to the available capacities: any allocation using a farther facility while a nearer one has unused capacity can transfer mass to the nearer facility without increasing cost.

Thus greedy assignment under capacities \(gq\) costs no more than any feasible assignment under capacities \(q\), proving the bound \(C_1=1\).

Inductive step

Process the first block, and denote its SD allocation by \(u_j\) and its cost by

\[ S_1=\sum_ju_jd(a_1,F_j). \]

Fix an optimal assignment \(z\) under the original capacities. Write

\[ O_1=\sum_jz_{1j}d(a_1,F_j),\qquad O_{\mathrm{rem}}=\sum_{t=2}^k\sum_jz_{tj}d(a_t,F_j). \]

The first block’s greedy allocation minimizes its cost under \(gq\), while \(z_{1\cdot}\) is feasible under \(q\). Therefore \(S_1\le O_1\).

Define reduced comparison capacities

\[ q'_j=q_j-\frac{u_j}{g}. \]

They are nonnegative, and their augmented capacities \(gq'_j=gq_j-u_j\) are precisely the residual capacities seen by the remaining blocks. Moreover,

\[ \sum_jq'_j \ge \sum_{t=1}^k\mu_t-\frac{\mu_1}{g} \ge \sum_{t=2}^k\mu_t. \]

We now repair the remaining rows of \(z\) to fit \(q'\). Apply the lemma with

\[ w_j=\frac{u_j}{g},\qquad v_j=\frac{z_{1j}}{g},\qquad h_{j\ell}=\frac{u_jz_{1\ell}}{g\mu_1}. \]

Both reserve vectors have total mass \(\mu_1/g\), and the stated \(h\) has their required marginals. The remaining assignment has loads at most \(q_j-z_{1j}\le q_j-v_j\). Finally,

\[ \sum_{j,\ell}h_{j\ell}d(F_j,F_\ell) \le \sum_{j,\ell}h_{j\ell} \bigl(d(F_j,a_1)+d(a_1,F_\ell)\bigr) = \frac{S_1+O_1}{g}. \]

The lemma therefore gives

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_{q'}(\mu_{\mathrm{rem}}) \le O_{\mathrm{rem}}+\frac{S_1+O_1}{g}. \]

Applying the induction hypothesis to the remaining \(k-1\) blocks yields

\[ \begin{aligned} S_{\mathrm{total}} &\le S_1+ C_{k-1}\left(O_{\mathrm{rem}}+\frac{S_1+O_1}{g}\right)\\ &\le \left(1+\frac{2C_{k-1}}{g}\right)O_1 +C_{k-1}O_{\mathrm{rem}}\\ &\le C_k(O_1+O_{\mathrm{rem}}). \end{aligned} \]

The second inequality uses \(S_1\le O_1\); the third uses the recurrence for \(C_k\) and \(C_k\ge C_{k-1}\).

This proves \(W_g(\tau)\le C_\tau\), including instances whose optimum is zero before taking ratios.

3. Matching rational construction

Use the usual metric on the real line. Put

\[ N=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}g^{\tau-t},\qquad \mu_t=\frac{g^{\tau-t}}{N},\qquad a_t=2^{t-1}, \]

and process types in increasing order.

Use \(\tau+1\) facilities: facility \(F_j\) is at \(2^j\) for \(1\le j\le\tau\), and \(F_{\tau+1}\) is at \(0\). Set

\[ q_j=\mu_{j+1}\quad(1\le j<\tau),\qquad q_\tau=\mu_\tau,\qquad q_{\tau+1}=\mu_1. \]

All capacities are positive, and their sum is \(1+\mu_\tau\ge1\).

For \(j<\tau\), we have \(gq_j=\mu_j\). Inductively, when type \(t\) is processed, facilities with indices below \(t\) are exhausted. Facility \(F_t\) and the facility at \(0\) are both at distance \(2^{t-1}\) from \(a_t\); every higher-index facility other than the one at \(0\) is farther away. Index tie-breaking selects \(F_t\). Its residual capacity accommodates the whole block, including for \(t=\tau\).

Hence SD costs \(\sum_t\mu_t2^{t-1}\).

An original-capacity assignment sends type \(1\) to the facility at \(0\), and each type \(t\ge2\) to \(F_{t-1}\), at zero cost. Its cost is \(\mu_1\). Conversely, every facility is at distance at least one from \(a_1=1\), so \(\operatorname{OPT}_q(\mu)\ge\mu_1\). Thus the optimum is exactly \(\mu_1>0\), and

\[ R_g(I) = \frac{\sum_t\mu_t2^{t-1}}{\mu_1} = \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\left(\frac{2}{g}\right)^{t-1} = C_\tau. \]

All data are rational with finite binary encodings. The upper bound is therefore attained.

If “binary rational” is intended to mean only dyadic rationals, the same supremum follows. Choose positive dyadic \(\alpha\) tending to \(1/N\) from below, put \(\delta=1-N\alpha\), and replace the masses by \(\mu_1=\alpha g^{\tau-1}+\delta\) and \(\mu_t=\alpha g^{\tau-t}\) for \(t\ge2\). Use the same capacity formulas. The additional mass \(\delta\) of the first block goes to the facility at \(0\), while all subsequent blocks follow the same chain. The resulting ratios tend to \(C_\tau\).

4. Exact computational complexity

For block SD, sort each type’s facilities by distance and then index. Scan its list, assigning the smaller of its remaining mass and the facility’s residual capacity. Total augmented capacity is at least total mass, so every block is assigned fully.

This uses \(O(\tau m\log(m+1))\) comparisons and scanning operations. There are only \(O(m+\tau)\) positive allocation steps: each step exhausts a facility or completes a block. Using a common denominator for the input masses and capacities, all residual quantities have \(O(L)\) bits, including multiplication by binary-encoded \(g\). With schoolbook integer arithmetic, a sufficient bit-time bound is

\[ O\!\left(\tau m\log(m+1)L^2\right). \]

For \(\operatorname{OPT}_q(\mu)\), use the stated rational linear program with \(m\tau\) variables. It is feasible: writing \(Q=\sum_jq_j\ge1\), the assignment \(z_{tj}=\mu_tq_j/Q\) satisfies all constraints. Its feasible region is compact because \(0\le z_{tj}\le\mu_t\). Polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore returns the exact optimum in \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) bit time.

Neither computation requires expanding rational masses into individual agents.
