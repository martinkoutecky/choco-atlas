The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational position-class supplies \(q_j\) summing to \(1\), utilities \(u_{tj}\), and a per-capita threshold \(W\), consider exact HZ equilibria with a unit bundle \(x_t\) for each type, prices \(p_j\), market clearing \(\sum_t \mu_t x_{tj}=q_j\), and each type’s bundle maximizing utility subject to size \(1\) and cost at most \(1\). Under the promise that maximum per-capita welfare \(\sum_t\mu_t\sum_j u_{tj}x_{tj}\) is at least \(W\) or at most \((175/176+\epsilon)W\), distinguish the two cases for fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/176\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) utility types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\) divisible position classes with positive binary-rational supplies \(q_j\) satisfying \(\sum_jq_j=1\), binary-rational utilities \(u_{tj}\in[0,1]\), and a binary-rational threshold \(W>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An exact HZ equilibrium consists of real bundles \(x_t\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{m}\) and prices \(p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{m}\), normalized by \(\min_jp_j=0\), with \(\sum_jx_{tj}=1\) and \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}=q_j\).
Each \(x_t\) maximizes \(\sum_ju_{tj}y_j\) over \(y\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{m}\) satisfying \(\sum_jy_j=1\) and \(\sum_jp_jy_j\le1\). Any utility-maximizing bundle is allowed; there is no minimum-cost tie-breaking requirement.
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the maximum of \(\sum_t\mu_t\sum_ju_{tj}x_{tj}\) over exact HZ equilibria. Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/176\), and promise either \(\mathrm{OPT}\ge W\) or \(\mathrm{OPT}\le(175/176+\epsilon)W\).

For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/176\), can these two promised cases be distinguished in polynomial time, or is this welfare approximation gap problem NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

For finite equally numerous agents and unit-supply goods, distinguishing the corresponding total-welfare cases is NP-hard for every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/176\) ([Chen, Chen, Peng, and Yannakakis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.05746)).
No result settling exactly the support-encoded mass version above was located in the literature search.

Result

For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/176\), the promised welfare gap problem is NP-hard. Consequently, a running time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The reduction below produces dyadic masses, supplies, utilities, and thresholds. Thus the conclusion holds whether “binary rational” means an arbitrary rational encoded in binary or specifically a dyadic rational.

1. The finite-agent hardness theorem applies to the stated tie rule

Write \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{fin}}\) for maximum total welfare in a market with \(n\) agents and \(n\) unit-supply goods.

Theorem 1.2 of [Chen, Chen, Peng, and Yannakakis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.05746) establishes NP-hardness of distinguishing

\( \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{fin}}\ge w \)

from

\( \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{fin}}\le (175/176+\delta)w \)

for every fixed positive \(\delta\). Their utilities lie in \([0,1]\). Definition 2.1 and its footnote 5 explicitly allow every utility-maximizing bundle, without minimum-cost tie-breaking. These are therefore the equilibrium conditions needed here.

Fix the target constant \(\epsilon\), and choose a fixed rational constant \(0<\delta<\epsilon/2\). We reduce from this finite-agent gap problem with parameter \(\delta\).

2. Construction of the support-encoded society

Let the source utilities be \(u_{ij}\), and let its positive threshold be \(w\). Partition the agents into sets \(I_t\) having identical utility vectors. Put \(n_t=|I_t|\), and write \(u_{tj}\) for their common utilities.

Choose a power of two \(N\) satisfying \(2n\le N<4n\).

We first make all utility coefficients and the threshold dyadic. Let \(D\) be the product of the positive denominators appearing in the source utilities and in \(w\). Choose an integer \(k\) such that

\( a=D/2^k,\qquad 1/4<a\le 1/2. \)

Then every \(a u_{tj}\), and also \(aw\), is dyadic. Choose a positive dyadic number \(s\) such that

\( s\le \min\{1/4,\delta aw/N\}. \)

The target has the original \(n\) position classes and one additional class \(d\). Its supplies are

\( q_j=1/N\quad (j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}),\qquad q_d=(N-n)/N. \)

For each original utility type, create a support entry with mass and utilities

\( \mu_t=n_t/N,\qquad v_{tj}=a u_{tj}+s,\qquad v_{td}=0. \)

Create one additional type, denoted \(*\), with

\( \mu_*=(N-n)/N,\qquad v_{*j}=0,\qquad v_{*d}=s. \)

Finally, set

\( W=aw/N+s. \)

All masses and supplies are positive and sum to one. All utilities belong to \([0,1]\): the original types’ utilities on original classes lie between \(s\) and \(1/2+s\le3/4\).

The original support entries remain distinct because their utility vectors undergo the same injective affine transformation. The additional type is distinct from every original type. Hence

\( m=n+1,\qquad \tau\le n+1. \)

3. Every target equilibrium separates the additional class

Consider any target equilibrium with prices \(P\). Write \(y_t\) for an original type’s bundle and \(z\) for the additional type’s bundle.

Let \(C\) be the amount of class \(d\) allocated to original types:

\( C=\sum_t\mu_t y_{td}. \)

Because \(q_d=\mu_*\), market clearing gives

\( C=\mu_*(1-z_d)=\mu_*\sum_{j=1}^n z_j. \)

Suppose \(C>0\). Then \(z_d<1\), and some original type has \(y_{td}>0\).

If \(P_d\le1\), the additional type can afford a whole unit of class \(d\), obtaining utility \(s\). Its assigned bundle instead has utility \(s z_d<s\), contradicting optimality. Thus \(P_d>1\).

For the original type with \(y_{td}>0\), suppose some original class \(j\) satisfies \(P_j\le P_d\). Replacing a positive amount of class \(d\) by class \(j\) preserves bundle size, does not increase cost, and strictly increases utility because \(v_{tj}\ge s>0=v_{td}\). This again contradicts optimality.

Consequently \(P_j>P_d>1\) for every original class. Every size-one bundle then costs more than one, contradicting budget feasibility.

Therefore \(C=0\). Positivity of all masses implies

\( y_{td}=0\ \text{for every original type }t,\qquad z_d=1. \)

This proves separation for every target equilibrium.

4. Equilibrium correspondence and welfare identity

From a target equilibrium to a finite-agent equilibrium

Give every source agent in \(I_t\) the bundle \((y_{tj})_{j=1}^n\). These bundles have size one and satisfy the original budget constraints at prices \((P_j)_{j=1}^n\).

For each original class \(j\),

\( \sum_{i=1}^n x_{ij}=\sum_t n_t y_{tj}=Nq_j=1. \)

On size-one bundles supported on original classes, transformed utility equals

\( \sum_{j=1}^n v_{tj}y_j=a\sum_{j=1}^n u_{tj}y_j+s. \)

Because \(a>0\), maximizing transformed utility is equivalent to maximizing source utility. Target optimality therefore implies source optimality.

The original prices can be normalized without changing these allocations. Indeed, their total is at most \(n\), by market clearing and the \(n\) individual budget inequalities. Let \(c=\min_jP_j\). If \(c<1\), replace them by

\( p_j=(P_j-c)/(1-c). \)

For every size-one bundle \(y\),

\( \sum_jp_jy_j\le1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_jP_jy_j\le1. \)

If \(c\ge1\), the total-price bound forces every original price to equal one; replacing all of them by zero also preserves the feasible set. Thus we obtain a normalized source equilibrium.

From a finite-agent equilibrium to a target equilibrium

Start with a normalized source equilibrium \((x,p)\). For each type, average its agents’ bundles:

\( y_t=\frac1{n_t}\sum_{i\in I_t}x_i. \)

The common feasible set is convex. Every agent in \(I_t\) attains the same optimal utility, so linearity implies that \(y_t\) is also feasible and optimal.

Assign \(y_t\) to target type \(t\), assign a whole unit of class \(d\) to type \(*\), and set \(P_j=p_j\) and \(P_d=1\). Market clearing follows directly from source clearing and the constructed masses.

Some original class has price zero. For each original type, that class has utility at least \(s>0\), whereas class \(d\) has utility zero and price one. Any bundle containing class \(d\) can therefore be strictly improved by replacing that portion with the zero-price original class. Hence original types’ optimal bundles exclude \(d\), and their optimality follows from the affine utility transformation. The additional type receives its maximum possible utility \(s\).

This is a normalized target equilibrium.

Welfare

If the corresponding source equilibrium has total welfare \(S\), target welfare is

\( \frac{aS}{N}+\frac{ns}{N}+\frac{(N-n)s}{N}=\frac{aS}{N}+s. \)

Both directions of the correspondence preserve this identity. Consequently,

\( \mathrm{OPT}=\frac{a}{N}\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{fin}}+s. \)

5. Preservation of the promised gap

Set \(A=aw/N>0\), \(r_0=175/176+\delta\), and \(r=175/176+\epsilon<1\). The target threshold is \(W=A+s\).

If \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{fin}}\ge w\), then

\( \mathrm{OPT}\ge A+s=W. \)

If \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{fin}}\le r_0w\), then

\( \mathrm{OPT}\le r_0A+s. \)

Moreover,

\( rW-(r_0A+s)=(\epsilon-\delta)A-(1-r)s. \)

Our choice \(s\le\delta A\), together with \(0<1-r<1\), gives

\( rW-(r_0A+s)\ge(\epsilon-2\delta)A>0. \)

Thus the source low-welfare case maps into \(\mathrm{OPT}\le rW\). Both promised cases are preserved.

6. Encoding size and running time

Let \(L_0\) be the source input bit length. The integer \(D\) has \(O(L_0)\) bits. For fixed \(\epsilon\), the numbers \(a\), \(s\), and all output coefficients have \(O_\epsilon(L_0+\log n)\) bits.

The construction has

\( m=n+1,\qquad \tau\le n+1,\qquad L=O_\epsilon(L_0^2). \)

Using elementary integer arithmetic and sorting to identify equal utility vectors, a conservative construction-time bound is \(O_\epsilon(L_0^4)\) bit operations. There is no population expansion.

A target algorithm taking time \(T(m,\tau,L)\) would therefore solve the source gap problem in time

\( O_\epsilon(L_0^4)+T(n+1,\tau,O_\epsilon(L_0^2)). \)

In particular, \(T(m,\tau,L)=(m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) would give a polynomial-time algorithm for the NP-hard source problem. This settles the requested gap problem for variable \(m\) and \(\tau\).
