The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses over finitely many complete entitlement-and-valuation types, clone-symmetric matroid-rank valuations over finitely many good classes, and rational per-capita supplies, choose fractions \(q_{t,B}\) of each type receiving whole bundles, respecting supplies and clean support, to maximize mass-weighted utilitarian welfare subject to the paper's \(\mathrm{TWEF}(x,1-x)\) inequality for every pair of positive-support bundle states.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type includes a positive binary-rational entitlement \(w_t\) and its valuation.
There are \(m\) good classes with nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(s_c\). A unary integer \(R\ge1\) bounds valuation ranks. Each valuation \(v_t\) is specified by an evaluation circuit on class-count vectors, promised to represent a matroid-rank valuation of rank at most \(R\) on \(2R\) distinct clones per class, invariant under permutations within each class. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including these circuits.
Whole bundles are vectors \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\) with \(\sum_c b_c\le R\). A bundle is clean for type \(t\) when \(v_t(b)=\sum_c b_c\). Adding bundles means taking distinct clones, so their union is represented by \(b+d\), even when their class counts coincide.
Choose nonnegative rational fractions \(q_{t,b}\), with \(\sum_bq_{t,b}=1\), supported only on clean bundles, and satisfying \(\sum_{t,b}\mu_tq_{t,b}b_c\le s_c\) for every class \(c\); unused supply is allowed. Welfare is \(\sum_{t,b}\mu_tq_{t,b}v_t(b)\), without entitlement factors.
Given a binary-rational \(x\in[0,1]\), require for every ordered pair of states \((t,b),(u,d)\) with \(q_{t,b}q_{u,d}>0\), including states of the same type, either \(v_t(b)=v_t(b+d)\), or some class \(c\) with \(d_c>0\) satisfies \(\frac{v_t(b)+(1-x)(v_t(b+e_c)-v_t(b))}{w_t}\ge\frac{v_t(d)-x(v_t(d)-v_t(d-e_c))}{w_u}\), where \(e_c\) is the class unit vector.

Given a nonnegative binary-rational threshold \(H\), is deciding whether such fractions achieve welfare at least \(H\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,R,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents and indivisible goods with matroid-rank valuations, a clean \(\mathrm{TWEF}(x,1-x)\) allocation maximizing unweighted utilitarian welfare can be computed in polynomial time with valuation-oracle access ([Montanari et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00355-025-01599-6)). No result settling the exact mass-allocation problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one; supplies are consequently per capita.
  • Specified positive rational entitlements, nonnegative rational supplies, and a rational input parameter x in the closed unit interval.
  • Resolved the unspecified valuation encoding using evaluation circuits promised to represent clone-symmetric matroid-rank valuations; circuit sizes count toward L.
  • Made the finite whole-bundle domain explicit through a unary rank bound R and enough distinct clones to evaluate two bundles together. Clean bundles automatically contain at most R goods.
  • Interpreted unions of two bundle states as disjoint copies, represented by addition of class counts, rather than identifying goods of the same class.
  • Restored the transferability alternative in TWEF and applied the condition to all ordered positive-support states, including states of the same type.
  • Allowed unused supply, as clean allocations need not be complete, and used mass-weighted welfare without entitlement multipliers.
  • Expressed exact welfare optimization through a rational-threshold decision problem.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,R,L\). In fact, the maximum welfare attainable under the stated fairness condition equals the maximum welfare attainable using only cleanness and supply constraints.

The proof has two parts: a potential argument establishes this equality, and matroid greedy optimization provides a polynomial-time separation oracle for the welfare LP.

1. The welfare LP without fairness.

Write \(|b|=\sum_c b_c\), and let
\[ \mathcal B_t=\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m: |b|\le R,\ v_t(b)=|b|\}. \]

Replace conditional fractions by population masses:
\[ y_{t,b}=\mu_tq_{t,b}. \]
Because every \(\mu_t>0\), this change of variables preserves which states have positive mass.

Without fairness, welfare maximization is the finite LP
\[ \begin{aligned} W^*=\max\quad &\sum_t\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_t}|b|y_{t,b}\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_t}y_{t,b}=\mu_t &&\text{for every }t,\\ &\sum_t\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_t}b_cy_{t,b}\le s_c &&\text{for every }c,\\ &y_{t,b}\ge0. \end{aligned} \tag{P} \]

This LP is feasible: give everyone the empty bundle. Its feasible region is compact, since \(0\le y_{t,b}\le\mu_t\). Moreover, \(0\le W^*\le R\). All coefficients are rational, so an optimal rational solution exists.

The number of bundles may be exponential. We will handle that in Section 3.

2. A welfare optimum satisfying every pairwise fairness condition.

For each type define
\[ F_t(k)=\frac{k^2+(1-2x)k}{w_t}, \qquad 0\le k\le R, \]
and define the linear potential
\[ \Phi(y)=\sum_t\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_t}y_{t,b}F_t(|b|). \]

This uses the quadratic transfer potential appearing in [Montanari et al., Section 5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00355-025-01599-6). The following argument establishes its consequence directly for population masses.

Among all solutions of (P) attaining \(W^*\), choose \(y^*\) minimizing \(\Phi\). Such a minimizer exists by compactness and can be chosen rational, because this secondary optimization is a rational LP.

We prove that \(y^*\) satisfies the requested fairness condition.

Suppose, to the contrary, that supported states \((t,b)\) and \((u,d)\) violate the condition. Put \(k=|b|\) and \(\ell=|d|\). Cleanness gives
\[ v_t(b)=k,\qquad v_u(d)=\ell. \]
Violation means that
\[ v_t(b+d)>v_t(b) \tag{1} \]
and, for every class \(c\) with \(d_c>0\),
\[ \frac{k+(1-x)(v_t(b+e_c)-v_t(b))}{w_t} < \frac{v_t(d)-x(v_t(d)-v_t(d-e_c))}{w_u}. \tag{2} \]

An augmenting class exists. Represent \(b\) and \(d\) by disjoint clone sets \(B,D\). This is possible because \(b_c+d_c\le2R\) for every class.

If every clone in \(D\) had zero marginal rank at \(B\), diminishing returns would imply that all successive additions from \(D\) also had zero marginal rank. This would contradict (1). Consequently, some class \(c\) with \(d_c>0\) satisfies
\[ v_t(b+e_c)-v_t(b)=1. \tag{3} \]
Here we used that matroid-rank marginals belong to \(\{0,1\}\).

For this class, let
\[ \delta=v_t(d)-v_t(d-e_c)\in\{0,1\}. \]
Since rank never exceeds cardinality and \(1-x\ge0\),
\[ \begin{aligned} v_t(d)-x\delta &=v_t(d-e_c)+(1-x)\delta\\ &\le(\ell-1)+(1-x)\\ &=\ell-x. \end{aligned} \tag{4} \]
Combining (2), (3), and (4) yields
\[ \frac{k+1-x}{w_t}<\frac{\ell-x}{w_u}. \tag{5} \]

Transfer a positive population mass. The bundle \(b+e_c\) is clean for type \(t\), by (3). Its size is at most \(R\), because its rank is \(k+1\) and the valuation has rank at most \(R\). The bundle \(d-e_c\) is clean for type \(u\), because a subset of a matroid-independent set is independent.

Choose a positive rational
\[ \varepsilon<\tfrac12\min\{y^*_{t,b},y^*_{u,d}\}. \]
Remove mass \(\varepsilon\) from each of \((t,b),(u,d)\), and add mass \(\varepsilon\) to each of
\[ (t,b+e_c),\qquad (u,d-e_c). \]
Additions and removals are aggregated if configuration coordinates coincide. The choice of \(\varepsilon\) guarantees nonnegativity even when a source coordinate occurs twice.

This operation preserves every type’s total mass. It also preserves every class’s consumption, since
\[ (b+e_c)+(d-e_c)=b+d. \]
It preserves welfare, since
\[ (k+1)+(\ell-1)=k+\ell. \]
Thus the modified allocation is another feasible solution attaining \(W^*\).

Its potential change is
\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta\Phi &=\varepsilon\bigl(F_t(k+1)-F_t(k) +F_u(\ell-1)-F_u(\ell)\bigr)\\ &=2\varepsilon \left(\frac{k+1-x}{w_t}-\frac{\ell-x}{w_u}\right)\\ &<0, \end{aligned} \]
where the last inequality is (5). This contradicts the choice of \(y^*\).

The argument applies when \(t=u\) as well: both removals and both additions then occur within the same type. An identical state paired with itself cannot violate fairness, because (5) would become
\[ \frac{k+1-x}{w_t}<\frac{k-x}{w_t}, \]
which is impossible. No division by \(x\) or \(1-x\) occurred, so both endpoints \(x=0,1\) are covered.

Therefore \(y^*\) satisfies every required ordered-pair condition. Setting
\[ q_{t,b}=y^*_{t,b}/\mu_t \]
gives rational fractions satisfying the original requirements and attaining \(W^*\).

We have proved
\[ \boxed{\text{Maximum fair welfare}=W^*.} \tag{6} \]

3. Computing \(W^*\) exactly in polynomial time.

The dual of (P) is
\[ \begin{aligned} \min\quad &\sum_c s_c\alpha_c+\sum_t\mu_t\beta_t\\ \text{subject to}\quad &\beta_t+\sum_c\alpha_cb_c\ge |b| &&\text{for every }t,\ b\in\mathcal B_t,\\ &\alpha_c\ge0, \end{aligned} \tag{D} \]
where the \(\beta_t\) are initially unrestricted. The empty-bundle constraints imply \(\beta_t\ge0\). Strong LP duality applies because (P) is feasible and bounded.

For fixed nonnegative \(\alpha\), define
\[ f_t(\alpha)=\max_{b\in\mathcal B_t}\sum_c(1-\alpha_c)b_c. \]
The bundle constraints for type \(t\) are precisely \(\beta_t\ge f_t(\alpha)\).

A bounded dual suffices. We may restrict (D) to
\[ 0\le\alpha_c\le1,\qquad 0\le\beta_t\le R. \tag{7} \]

To verify this, replace any \(\alpha_c>1\) by \(1\). Before replacement, every unit of that class has negative coefficient in the maximization defining \(f_t\); afterward it has coefficient zero. Removing all units of that class preserves cleanness. Hence the maximum \(f_t\) is unchanged by the replacement. Because \(s_c\ge0\), the dual objective cannot increase.

After all such replacements, set \(\beta_t=f_t(\alpha)\). This maintains feasibility and cannot increase the objective, because \(\mu_t>0\). Finally,
\[ 0\le f_t(\alpha)\le R \]
when \(\alpha\in[0,1]^m\). This proves (7).

We therefore optimize over a nonempty bounded rational polytope in dimension \(m+\tau\). Its defining inequalities have integer coefficients of absolute value at most \(R\), apart from the rational objective coefficients.

Separation is matroid greedy optimization. First check the bounds (7). For a point satisfying those bounds, assign every clone of class \(c\) weight \(1-\alpha_c\).

For each type \(t\), the promised matroid has \(2Rm\) clones. A clone subset \(I\) is independent exactly when
\[ v_t(b(I))=|I|, \]
where \(b(I)\) is its class-count vector. Thus the evaluation circuit supplies a polynomial-time independence oracle.

Run the matroid greedy algorithm: process clones in nonincreasing weight order and add a clone whenever independence is preserved. Its resulting independent set has maximum total weight. For completeness, after every prefix of this order, the accepted set is a maximal independent subset of that prefix, hence has its rank. Every other independent set has no larger prefix cardinality. Expressing total weight as a nonnegative linear combination of prefix cardinalities proves greedy optimality.

Every independent clone set has size at most \(R\), and its count vector belongs to \(\mathcal B_t\). Conversely, every bundle in \(\mathcal B_t\) is represented by an independent clone set. Within-class invariance makes its value independent of the particular clone representatives. Consequently, greedy computes exactly \(f_t(\alpha)\).

If the maximizing bundle \(b\) satisfies
\[ f_t(\alpha)>\beta_t, \]
return the violated inequality
\[ \beta_t+\sum_c\alpha_cb_c\ge |b|. \]
If no type has such a violation, the candidate is feasible.

This is an exact separation oracle using at most \(2Rm\) independence tests per type, together with polynomial-time sorting and rational arithmetic. Circuit evaluation takes polynomial time in \(L\) and the count-vector encoding length.

We now apply the standard rational-polytope separation-to-optimization theorem: a bounded rational polytope with a known polynomial bound on inequality encoding lengths can be optimized exactly in polynomial time using a polynomial-time strong separation oracle. This is the exact rational version of the ellipsoid framework of [Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • The dimension is \(m+\tau\).
  • Bounds (7) give an explicit bounding box.
  • Each defining inequality has polynomial encoding length.
  • The objective coefficients \(s_c,\mu_t\) are input rationals.
  • The separation oracle is exact and polynomial-time.

Thus the optimum of (D), and therefore \(W^*\), is computable exactly in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,R,L\). Unary encoding of \(R\) ensures that the \(2Rm\)-clone representation has polynomial size.

4. Decision procedure and conclusion.

Compute \(W^*\) using the bounded dual and its separation oracle, and accept exactly when
\[ W^*\ge H. \]

If the algorithm accepts, Section 2 supplies rational fair fractions attaining \(W^*\). If it rejects, even an allocation without fairness constraints cannot attain \(H\).

Therefore the target decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm P\). In particular, its optimal welfare is independent of the positive entitlements and of the choice of \(x\in[0,1]\), although the fair allocation attaining it can depend on both.
