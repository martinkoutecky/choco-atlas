The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite chore classes \(O=\{o_1,\ldots,o_m\}\), complete bi-valued cost types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}^{T}\), common \(p>q>0\), and rational per-capita supplies \(s_o\in[0,1]\), construct in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,|T|,L)\) a truthful menu of lotteries \(P_r\) over integral bundles and a feasible joint coupling such that \(z_r(o)=\Pr_{B\sim P_r}[o\in B]\), \(\sum_t\mu_tz_t(o)=s_o\), truthfulness and ex-ante envy-freeness hold, the allocation is ex-ante Pareto optimal, and every realized integral assignment is EF1 almost everywhere.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(O=\{o_1,\ldots,o_m\}\), with \(m\geq1\), be chore classes. Each agent may receive at most one chore from each class, so an integral bundle is \(B\subseteq O\). Types are additive cost vectors in \(D=\{p,q\}^{O}\), for common binary rationals \(p>q>0\); constant cost vectors are allowed.
A society is given by its support: the list \(T\subseteq D\) of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Supplies \(s_o\in[0,1]\) are binary rational masses; \(L\) is the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Represent the society by an atomless probability space \((A,\nu)\) with type regions \(A_t\) of measure \(\mu_t\). A menu assigns every report \(r\in D\) a lottery \(P_r\) over bundles, with marginals \(z_r(o)=\Pr_{B\sim P_r}[o\in B]\). Reports do not change the society, since individual agents have zero mass.
A feasible joint coupling is a random measurable assignment \(a\mapsto B_a\subseteq O\), giving almost every agent in \(A_t\) marginal law \(P_t\), such that every realization satisfies \(\nu\{a:o\in B_a\}=s_o\) for each \(o\). In particular, \(\sum_t\mu_tz_t(o)=s_o\).
Truthfulness requires \(c_t(z_t)\leq c_t(z_r)\) for every \(t\in T,r\in D\); ex-ante envy-freeness requires \(c_t(z_t)\leq c_t(z_u)\) for every \(t,u\in T\). Indifferences are permitted and these inequalities are weak.
Ex-ante Pareto optimality is relative to all measurable fractional assignments \(y_a\in[0,1]^O\) with \(\int_A y_a(o)\,d\nu(a)=s_o\): none may weakly lower almost every agent's expected cost and strictly lower it on a set of positive measure.
Every realized assignment must be EF1 for \(\nu\times\nu\)-almost every ordered pair of agents; removal is from the envious agent's bundle. Empty bundles impose no removal requirement.
Menus and couplings must have finite descriptions: allow succinct menus with polynomial-time evaluation on an \(m\)-coordinate report, and rational lotteries over sparse tables recording the mass of each type assigned each bundle, interpreted as anonymous couplings within type regions.

Can such menus and feasible joint couplings be constructed for every input in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), or is this construction problem NP-hard?

For finite societies with common positive bi-valued costs, a polynomial-time randomized mechanism achieves ex-ante truthfulness, Pareto optimality, and envy-freeness, together with ex-post EF1. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01629v2).
No result settling the atomless construction problem specified above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized masses to sum to one and interpreted supplies as chore masses per unit population, with at most one chore per class in each agent's bundle.
  • Made the common costs positive binary rationals included in the input bit length; retained constant cost vectors because bi-valued membership does not require both values to occur.
  • Distinguished the positive-mass support from the full reporting domain: every vector in the common bi-valued domain is an admissible report, including absent types.
  • Interpreted truthfulness as individual deviations in an atomless society, leaving the aggregate distribution fixed; used weak inequalities for indifferences.
  • Required exact supply feasibility in every realization, rather than only in expectation, and specified EF1 for almost every ordered pair of agents.
  • Defined Pareto optimality against unrestricted feasible measurable fractional reallocations, with strict improvement on positive mass.
  • Specified finite representations using succinct evaluable menus and rational lotteries over sparse type-and-bundle mass tables, with anonymous assignment within type regions.

Polynomial constructions in additional regimes

1. Proven results and remaining scope

The general construction problem remains unresolved here. I prove polynomial-time constructions for two additional regimes with arbitrary total supply:

  • Every class supply is at most the smallest supported type mass.
  • Every class deficit is at most the smallest supported type mass.

I also prove a construction when the total deficit is at most one. These results allow heterogeneous types and constant cost vectors.

More precisely, define
$$ I=\{o:0<s_o<1\}, \qquad F=\{o:s_o=1\}, \qquad \mu_{\min}=\min_{t\in T}\mu_t. $$

If $I$ is empty, assigning $F$ to everyone solves the problem. Otherwise, a construction exists in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ time whenever at least one of the following holds:
$$ \sum_{o\in I}s_o\leq 1, $$
$$ \sum_{o\in I}(1-s_o)\leq 1, $$
$$ \max_{o\in I}s_o\leq\mu_{\min}, $$
$$ \max_{o\in I}(1-s_o)\leq\mu_{\min}. $$

The proof produces one rational table with at most $\tau(m+1)$ positive cells. Its anonymous implementation satisfies exact supplies and EF1 in every realization.

The main new ingredient is a rounding lemma. In the direct construction, the preferred-chore count and total bundle size must be rounded together. This correlation is essential to the cross-type EF1 proof.

2. A fractional construction with a checkable cap condition

Temporarily consider a set $J$ of classes with supplies $v_o\in[0,1]$, and put
$$ R=\sum_{o\in J}v_o. $$

Each supported index $t$ has costs $c_t(o)\in\{p,q\}$ on $J$. The proof permits identical restricted cost vectors at different indices.

We consider two interpretations:

  • Direct chores: the preferred classes are

$$ E_t=\{o\in J:c_t(o)=q\}. $$

  • Omitted chores viewed as goods: the preferred classes are

$$ E_t=\{o\in J:c_t(o)=p\}. $$

The second interpretation will ultimately use supplies $v_o=1-s_o$.

If $R=0$, set $x_t=0$ for every $t$. Henceforth suppose $R>0$.

2.1. Leximin preferred flow

Consider the compact rational polytope of flows satisfying
$$ f_{t,o}\geq 0, \qquad f_{t,o}=0\quad\text{if }o\notin E_t, $$
$$ \sum_t f_{t,o}\leq v_o, \qquad h_t=\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_o f_{t,o}\leq R. $$

The vector $h$ records preferred amounts per agent. Choose a flow whose increasingly sorted vector of preferred amounts is lexicographically maximal. This is the leximin objective.

It can be computed with $O(\tau^2)$ rational linear programs:

  • Maximize a common lower bound $\lambda$ on all currently unfixed coordinates.
  • Fix $\lambda$ at its optimum. Retain all previously fixed coordinates and all current constraints $h_t\geq\lambda$.
  • In this same feasible region, maximize each remaining coordinate separately. Fix every coordinate whose maximum equals $\lambda$.
  • Repeat.

At least one coordinate is fixed in each round. Otherwise, for every remaining coordinate there would be a feasible witness making it strictly greater than $\lambda$. Averaging these witnesses would make all remaining coordinates strictly greater than $\lambda$, contradicting optimality.

After the forced coordinates are fixed, the same averaging argument shows that all other coordinates can simultaneously exceed the current level. Thus the procedure successively determines the leximin levels.

Two structural properties follow.

Unused preferred supply. If $h_t<R$ and $o\in E_t$, then
$$ \sum_u f_{u,o}=v_o. $$

Indeed, otherwise a small increase in $f_{t,o}$ would increase $h_t$ without decreasing any coordinate. This strictly improves the increasingly sorted vector.

Transfer property. If $h_t<h_u$ and $o\in E_t$, then
$$ f_{u,o}=0. $$

If this flow were positive, transfer a sufficiently small mass from $u$ to $t$ on class $o$. Both preferred-edge restrictions remain satisfied. Choose the transfer small enough that the recipient’s new preferred amount remains below the donor’s new preferred amount. The smallest altered level increases, so the sorted vector improves lexicographically. This contradicts leximin optimality.

2.2. Completion to a common expected size

Define residual supplies and their total by
$$ r_o=v_o-\sum_t f_{t,o}, \qquad \Delta=\sum_o r_o =\sum_t\mu_t(R-h_t). $$

If $\Delta>0$, set
$$ x_t(o)=\frac{f_{t,o}}{\mu_t} +(R-h_t)\frac{r_o}{\Delta}. $$

If $\Delta=0$, set $x_t(o)=f_{t,o}/\mu_t$. In this case every $h_t=R$, because the positive masses weight nonnegative deficits whose sum is zero.

In either case,
$$ \sum_o x_t(o)=R, \qquad \sum_t\mu_t x_t(o)=v_o. $$

The unused-supply property implies that completion gives an unsaturated type only nonpreferred classes. Consequently,
$$ x_t(E_t)=h_t. $$

For every unsaturated type $t$, meaning $h_t<R$, the following stronger support properties hold:
$$ h_u>h_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_u(o)=0\ \text{for every }o\in E_t, $$
and
$$ x_u(o)>0,\ o\in E_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad o\in E_u. $$

To verify these assertions, unused preferred supply gives $r_o=0$ on $E_t$. Thus any positive $x_u(o)$ there comes from $f_{u,o}$. The transfer property gives the first assertion, and the preferred-edge restriction gives the second.

The construction has not yet imposed the class caps $x_t(o)\leq1$. These caps hold automatically in either of the following situations:
$$ R\leq1, \qquad\text{or}\qquad v_o\leq\mu_{\min}\ \text{for every }o. $$

For the first assertion, each nonnegative coordinate is bounded by its row sum $R$. For the second, supply conservation gives
$$ \mu_t x_t(o)\leq v_o\leq\mu_{\min}\leq\mu_t. $$

All subsequent conclusions assume that the resulting $x$ satisfies the class caps.

2.3. Envy inequalities

For all $t,u$,
$$ x_u(E_t)\leq h_t. $$

If $h_t=R$, this follows from $x_u(E_t)\leq\sum_o x_u(o)=R$.

If $h_t<R$, residual completion contributes nothing on $E_t$. When $h_u>h_t$, the transfer property makes $x_u(E_t)=0$. When $h_u\leq h_t$,
$$ x_u(E_t) =\frac{1}{\mu_u}\sum_{o\in E_t}f_{u,o} \leq\frac{1}{\mu_u}\sum_o f_{u,o} =h_u \leq h_t. $$

For direct chores, therefore,
$$ c_t(x_t)=pR-(p-q)h_t \leq pR-(p-q)x_u(E_t) =c_t(x_u). $$

For omitted chores viewed as goods,
$$ c_t(x_t)=qR+(p-q)h_t \geq qR+(p-q)x_u(E_t) =c_t(x_u). $$

Thus the direct allocation is envy-free for chores, and the omission allocation is envy-free when omissions are valued as goods.

2.4. Pareto optimality against measurable fractional assignments

Let
$$ Z=\{t:h_t=R\}. $$

For direct chores, define positive type weights
$$ w_t= \begin{cases} p/q,&t\in Z,\\ 1,&t\notin Z, \end{cases} $$
and class coefficients
$$ \rho_o= \begin{cases} q,&o\in E_t\text{ for some }t\notin Z,\\ p,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$

For every type and class,
$$ w_t c_t(o)\geq\rho_o. $$

Equality holds whenever $x_t(o)>0$:

  • If some unsaturated type prefers $o$, its supply is exhausted by preferred flow. The transfer property excludes saturated recipients. Every recipient is therefore an unsaturated type with cost $q$.
  • Otherwise, preferred-flow recipients are saturated types with weighted cost $(p/q)q=p$. Completion recipients are unsaturated types with cost $p$.

Hence
$$ \sum_t\mu_t w_t c_t(x_t)=\sum_o\rho_o v_o. $$

For every measurable fractional assignment $y_a\in[0,1]^J$ with the same supplies,
$$ \int_A w_{t(a)}c_{t(a)}(y_a)\,d\nu(a) \geq \sum_o\rho_o\int_A y_a(o)\,d\nu(a) = \sum_o\rho_o v_o. $$

A weak cost improvement almost everywhere, strict on a positive-measure set, would strictly decrease this weighted integral. The weights are positive, and all costs are bounded and measurable. Such an improvement is therefore impossible.

For omissions viewed as goods, instead define
$$ w_t= \begin{cases} q/p,&t\in Z,\\ 1,&t\notin Z, \end{cases} $$
and
$$ \rho_o= \begin{cases} p,&o\in E_t\text{ for some }t\notin Z,\\ q,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$

Now
$$ w_t c_t(o)\leq\rho_o, $$
with equality on every positive allocation coordinate. The same recipient classification proves equality: preferred recipients are either unsaturated high-cost types or saturated high-cost types, and completion recipients are unsaturated low-cost types.

Consequently, the omission allocation maximizes
$$ \int_A w_{t(a)}c_{t(a)}(y_a)\,d\nu(a) $$
over all measurable fractional assignments with supplies $v$. It is Pareto optimal for the interpretation in which a larger cost-weighted omission value is better.

2.5. Rational bit complexity

The leximin computation has polynomial-size LPs with $O(m\tau)$ variables and polynomially many constraints.

The leximin levels have polynomial encoding length. To see this without relying on repeated denominator estimates, choose a permutation that orders the final $h$-coordinates. Add the corresponding ordering inequalities to the original rational flow polytope. Within this ordered polytope, the final $h$ is obtained by successive linear optimization of the ordered coordinates. The resulting nonempty optimal face contains a vertex of the ordered polytope with exactly these $h$-coordinates.

Cramer’s rule and determinant bounds for a rational matrix of polynomial order give polynomial encoding length for that vertex and hence for every leximin level. The final flow can then be chosen as a rational vertex with those levels fixed. The completion formula uses polynomially many rational operations, so $x$ also has polynomial encoding length.

There is also a useful additional cap test. Once $h$ is fixed, $\Delta$ is fixed and the completion formula is linear in $f$. An additional polynomial-size LP can choose a flow realizing this $h$ while imposing
$$ x_t(o)\leq1 $$
on every coordinate. If this LP succeeds, the constructions below apply even outside the four sufficient regimes stated in Section 1. Failure of this LP does not imply nonexistence of a solution to the original problem.

3. Systematic rounding and its correlated counts

Assume now that $x_t(o)\in[0,1]$.

For each type, order its preferred classes first, followed by its other classes. Write that order as $o_1,\ldots,o_d$, where $d=|J|$, and define
$$ C_0=0, \qquad C_i=\sum_{j=1}^i x_t(o_j). $$

For $U\in[0,1)$, include $o_i$ if the half-open interval
$$ [C_{i-1},C_i) $$
contains a point of $U+\mathbb Z$.

Since its length is at most one, each interval contains at most one such point. Thus this construction gives a bundle rather than a multiset.

For uniform $U$, the inclusion probability of $o_i$ equals $x_t(o_i)$. Indeed,
$$ \int_0^1 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z} \mathbf 1_{[C_{i-1},C_i)}(U+k)\,dU =C_i-C_{i-1}. $$

Let
$$ k=\lfloor R\rfloor,\qquad \theta=R-k, $$
and
$$ a_t=\lfloor h_t\rfloor,\qquad \beta_t=h_t-a_t. $$

The total bundle size $n_t(U)$ and preferred count $\ell_t(U)$ satisfy
$$ n_t(U)=k+\mathbf 1_{\{U<\theta\}}, $$
$$ \ell_t(U)=a_t+\mathbf 1_{\{U<\beta_t\}}. $$

These formulas follow by counting shifted lattice points in $[0,R)$ and its preferred initial segment $[0,h_t)$.

The bundle changes only at fractional parts of cumulative sums $C_i$. Hence the construction gives a rational lottery over at most $d+1$ bundles. Each positive lottery probability is the length of an interval between rational breakpoints.

The following correlation will be used for direct chores.

Correlated-count lemma. Suppose $h_t\geq h_u$. If a supported rounded bundle of $t$ has size $k+1$ and a supported rounded bundle of $u$ has size $k$, then
$$ \ell_t\geq\ell_u. $$

If $\lfloor h_t\rfloor>\lfloor h_u\rfloor$, this follows from
$$ \ell_t\geq\lfloor h_t\rfloor \geq\lfloor h_u\rfloor+1 \geq\ell_u. $$

Otherwise their floors agree, and $\beta_t\geq\beta_u$. The only possible violation would have $\ell_t=a_t$ and $\ell_u=a_t+1$. For the larger bundle of $t$, these conditions require
$$ \beta_t\leq U_t<\theta, $$
so $\beta_t<\theta$. For the smaller bundle of $u$, they require
$$ \theta\leq U_u<\beta_u, $$
so $\theta<\beta_u$. This contradicts $\beta_t\geq\beta_u$.

This lemma compares any two supported bundles. It does not require the two agents to use the same rounding parameter.

4. EF1 for both constructions

4.1. Direct chores

Let $B$ be any supported rounded bundle of type $t$, and let $C$ be any supported rounded bundle of type $u$.

Their cardinalities differ by at most one.

If $B$ is nonempty and contains only $q$-cost chores for $t$, removing any one of them gives
$$ c_t(B)-q =q(|B|-1) \leq q|C| \leq c_t(C). $$

Suppose instead that $B$ contains a $p$-cost chore. Then $t$ is unsaturated, because a saturated type receives only preferred chores.

If $h_u>h_t$, the support property in Section 2.2 gives $C\cap E_t=\emptyset$. Therefore
$$ c_t(B)-p \leq p(|B|-1) \leq p|C| =c_t(C). $$

It remains to consider $h_u\leq h_t$. Put
$$ \ell_B=|B\cap E_t|, \qquad \ell_C=|C\cap E_u|. $$

The support property gives $C\cap E_t\subseteq C\cap E_u$, so
$$ c_t(C)\geq p|C|-(p-q)\ell_C. $$

Also,
$$ \ell_C-\ell_B \leq\lceil h_u\rceil-\lfloor h_t\rfloor \leq1. $$

If $|B|\leq|C|$, then
$$ c_t(B)-p-c_t(C) \leq p(|B|-|C|-1)+(p-q)(\ell_C-\ell_B) \leq-p+(p-q) =-q<0. $$

If $|B|=|C|+1$, the correlated-count lemma gives $\ell_B\geq\ell_C$. Hence
$$ c_t(B)-p-c_t(C) \leq(p-q)(\ell_C-\ell_B) \leq0. $$

Thus every ordered pair of supported bundles satisfies chore EF1.

4.2. Complementary construction

Now use supplies $v_o=1-s_o$ and preferred sets
$$ E_t=\{o:c_t(o)=p\}. $$

Let $G_t$ be a rounded omission bundle, and assign the actual chore bundle
$$ B_t=J\setminus G_t. $$

For any supported omission bundles $G_t,G_u$, write
$$ n_t=|G_t|, \qquad n_u=|G_u|, \qquad \ell_t=|G_t\cap E_t|. $$

I first show
$$ |G_u\cap E_t|-\ell_t\leq1. $$

If $t$ is saturated, every omitted class in $G_t$ is preferred, so $\ell_t=n_t$. Consequently,
$$ |G_u\cap E_t|-\ell_t \leq n_u-n_t \leq1. $$

If $t$ is unsaturated and $h_u>h_t$, the support property gives $G_u\cap E_t=\emptyset$.

If $t$ is unsaturated and $h_u\leq h_t$, then
$$ G_u\cap E_t\subseteq G_u\cap E_u, $$
and therefore
$$ |G_u\cap E_t|-\ell_t \leq\lceil h_u\rceil-\lfloor h_t\rfloor \leq1. $$

Actual chore costs satisfy
$$ c_t(B_t)-c_t(B_u) =c_t(G_u)-c_t(G_t). $$

Using the cardinality and preferred-count bounds,
$$ c_t(G_u)-c_t(G_t) = q(n_u-n_t) +(p-q)\bigl(|G_u\cap E_t|-\ell_t\bigr) \leq q+(p-q)=p. $$

If $B_t$ contains a $p$-cost chore, removing that chore eliminates its envy.

If $B_t$ is nonempty and contains only $q$-cost chores, then $G_t$ contains every class in $E_t$. Thus
$$ |G_u\cap E_t|-\ell_t\leq0, $$
and the sharper bound is
$$ c_t(B_t)-c_t(B_u)\leq q(n_u-n_t)\leq q. $$

Removing any chore from $B_t$ suffices. Empty bundles impose no requirement.

Therefore the complementary construction also makes every ordered pair of supported actual bundles EF1.

Its ex-ante properties follow exactly from the omission interpretation. Set
$$ z_t=\mathbf 1-x_t. $$

Then
$$ \sum_t\mu_t z_t(o)=1-v_o=s_o. $$

The omission envy inequalities give
$$ c_t(z_t)=c_t(J)-c_t(x_t) \leq c_t(J)-c_t(x_u) =c_t(z_u). $$

For Pareto optimality, any feasible measurable chore assignment $y_a$ corresponds to the feasible omission assignment $\mathbf1-y_a$. A weak chore-cost decrease, strict on a positive-measure set, becomes a weak omission-value increase with the same strictness. Section 2.4 rules it out.

5. Anonymous coupling, truthful menu, and running time

5.1. One rational table

Let $P_t$ denote the rounded actual-bundle lottery from either construction. Define
$$ b_{t,B}=\mu_t P_t(B). $$

These masses are rational and satisfy
$$ \sum_B b_{t,B}=\mu_t, $$
$$ \sum_{t,B:o\in B}b_{t,B}=s_o. $$

There are at most $\tau(|J|+1)$ positive cells. Section 4 proves EF1 for every ordered pair of positive cells, including pairs within a type.

For completeness, this table has the required anonymous coupling on any atomless society.

The atomless divisibility theorem states that, in an atomless finite measure space, every measurable set has a measurable subset of any prescribed measure between zero and its own measure. Each normalized type region
$$ (A_t,\nu(\,\cdot\,)/\mu_t) $$
is an atomless probability space. Recursively halving its sets therefore gives a measurable coordinate $U_t:A_t\to[0,1)$ with uniform distribution: the successive binary partition choices supply its binary digits, and every dyadic interval has its prescribed measure.

Partition $[0,1)$ into intervals of lengths $P_t(B)$, labeled by bundles. For a uniform random $\Theta\in[0,1)$, give agent $a\in A_t$ the label containing
$$ (U_t(a)+\Theta)\bmod1. $$

For each fixed $\Theta$, translation modulo one preserves every cell’s measure. Hence the table and all supplies are exact in every realization. For each fixed agent, the translated coordinate is uniform, giving marginal law $P_t$.

Every realized bundle belongs to a positive table cell apart from null sets, so the pairwise result of Section 4 gives EF1 for $\nu\times\nu$-almost every ordered pair in every realization.

5.2. Succinct truthful menu

For a supported report $r=t$, offer $P_t$. For an unsupported report, choose a supported lottery minimizing its reported expected cost:
$$ u(r)\in\arg\min_{u\in T}c_r(z_u), $$
and offer $P_{u(r)}$.

Every report thus receives a supported lottery. Supported envy-freeness gives, for every truthful supported type $t$ and every report $r$,
$$ c_t(z_t)\leq c_t(z_r). $$

This is precisely the requested truthfulness condition, since reports do not alter the society. Finding the chosen supported index requires $O(m\tau)$ rational arithmetic operations.

5.3. Forced and absent classes

Classes with supply zero can be omitted. Classes in $F$ can be assigned to everyone.

Indeed, any feasible fractional comparison assignment satisfies
$$ y_a(o)=0\quad\text{almost everywhere if }s_o=0, $$
and
$$ y_a(o)=1\quad\text{almost everywhere if }s_o=1. $$

These conclusions follow from $0\leq y_a(o)\leq1$ and its prescribed integral. Thus fixed-class costs cancel in all ex-ante comparisons, and Pareto optimality on $I$ extends to the original instance.

Appending $F$ also preserves EF1. If a residual bundle is nonempty, use its EF1 removal and cancel the common $F$-cost from both sides. If it is empty, its full bundle is $F$, whose cost is at most that of $F$ plus any comparison residual bundle; removing any chore from $F$, when necessary, preserves the inequality.

Applying the direct construction on $I$ proves the first and third sufficient conditions in Section 1. Applying the complementary construction on $I$ proves the second and fourth.

5.4. Complexity

Each construction uses $O(\tau^2)$ LPs with $O(m\tau)$ variables and polynomially many constraints. All rational data have polynomial encoding length in $m,\tau,L$.

Rounding all types requires $O(\tau m^2)$ rational arithmetic operations by sorting cumulative-sum breakpoints and evaluating the bundles between them. The table has at most $\tau(m+1)$ positive cells, with polynomial-bit probabilities and masses.

Hence construction time, output length, and menu-evaluation time are all polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

6. A heterogeneous example with total supply above one

Take four classes, two types of mass $1/2$, and supplies
$$ s_1=s_2=s_3=s_4=\frac25. $$

Let the low-cost sets be
$$ E_t=\{o_1\}, \qquad E_u=\{o_2,o_3\}. $$

Thus total supply is $8/5>1$, while every class supply is at most $\mu_{\min}=1/2$.

The direct construction gives
$$ h_t=\frac45, \qquad h_u=\frac85, $$
and marginals
$$ x_t=\left(\frac45,0,0,\frac45\right), \qquad x_u=\left(0,\frac45,\frac45,0\right). $$

One resulting table is:

| Type | Bundle | Population mass |
|---|---|---:|
| $t$ | $\{o_1,o_4\}$ | $3/10$ |
| $t$ | $\{o_1\}$ | $1/10$ |
| $t$ | $\{o_4\}$ | $1/10$ |
| $u$ | $\{o_2,o_3\}$ | $3/10$ |
| $u$ | $\{o_2\}$ | $1/10$ |
| $u$ | $\{o_3\}$ | $1/10$ |

Each type has mass $1/2$, and each class has supply $2/5$. The preceding proofs establish truthfulness, ex-ante envy-freeness, Pareto optimality against the specified measurable benchmark, and realized EF1 for every $p>q>0$.

7. What remains open

These results settle additional heterogeneous cases beyond total supply one. They do not establish universal feasibility or a general polynomial-time construction.

The unresolved inputs are those for which neither orientation yields a capped fractional allocation through the preferred-flow construction, and which fall outside the stated sufficient regimes. Failure of either cap LP only rejects that particular fractional construction.

The cited finite-society theorem provides the four guarantees for indivisible-item allocations, but it does not by itself resolve these capped atomless inputs. [Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01629v2#S5)

A universal construction or a valid hardness reduction is still needed.
