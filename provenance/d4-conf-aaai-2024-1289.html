The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 3.4 (Upper Bound for Multiplicative Envy) — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete valuation types \(\Theta\) with masses \(\mu\), an online item stream with values \(v_\theta(s)\in\{0\}\cup[\varepsilon,1]\), and per-capita supply scaled with population size, consider the fluid limit of the paper's greedy rule. Track a measure over individual bundle states, including each type's value for each agent's bundle, and route arriving item flow to states maximizing the paper's priority \(v_\theta(s)/u\). When every type has unbounded monopolistic utility, what is the supremal asymptotic multiplicative envy \(\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x,y} w_{\theta(x)}(y)/w_{\theta(x)}(x)\) over positive-mass states and admissible streams? Is it \(1+2\log(1/\varepsilon)\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix \(\varepsilon\in(0,1]\). A society is specified by its nonempty support \(\Theta\): the list of its \(\tau\) complete valuation types and their positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. All individuals have equal positive weight.
There are \(m\) item kinds, with binary-rational additive values \(v_\theta(j)\in\{0\}\cup[\varepsilon,1]\); each kind is valued positively by some supported type. An adversarial stream \(j(t)\) is piecewise constant with finitely many phases on each bounded interval and supplies one item per unit population per unit time. Require \(\int_0^T v_\theta(j(t))\,\mathrm dt\to\infty\) for every \(\theta\).
The population state is a probability measure \(\nu_t\) on \(\Theta\times\mathbb R_+^\tau\). In state \((\theta,z)\), coordinate \(z_\eta\) is type \(\eta\)'s value for that individual's bundle, and their own utility is \(z_\theta\). Initially \(\nu_0=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\delta_{(\theta,0)}\).
A greedy trajectory has a nonnegative routing measure \(r_t\ll\nu_t\) of total mass one, concentrated on states attaining the \(\nu_t\)-essential maximum of \(v_\theta(j(t))/z_\theta\). Ties may be resolved arbitrarily using only current and past information; interpret a positive number divided by zero as \(+\infty\), and \(0/0=0\).
Receiving an item remains an individual bundle jump: with \(\mathbf v(j)=(v_\eta(j))_{\eta\in\Theta}\), require \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\int f\,\mathrm d\nu_t=\int[f(\theta,z+\mathbf v(j(t)))-f(\theta,z)]\,\mathrm dr_t\) for bounded test functions, with \(\nu_t\) remaining nonnegative. Thus the measure evolves continuously while individual items remain indivisible.
Define \(E(t)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{((\theta,z),(\phi,z'))\sim\nu_t\otimes\nu_t}z'_\theta/z_\theta\), using the same zero-denominator conventions, and \(E^*(\varepsilon)=\sup\limsup_{t\to\infty}E(t)\), where the supremum ranges over all finite supported societies, finite item menus, admissible streams, and greedy trajectories. Any algorithmic running times should be stated in \(m,\tau,L\), the number of stream phases supplied, and other relevant parameters, where \(L\) is the binary input length.

What is the sharp value of \(E^*(\varepsilon)\)? Is \(E^*(\varepsilon)=1+2\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or does the continuous society have a different worst-case asymptotic multiplicative envy factor?

For finite agents, integral greedy has asymptotic multiplicative envy at most \(1+2\log(1/\varepsilon)\), with matching lower-bound instances. [Yang, Liao, and Kroer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09277)
A later full version gives the upper-bound error as \(O(n/V_i)\), where \(V_i\) is the envious agent's monopolistic utility, and retains the matching lower bound; no result settling the bundle-state measure dynamics above was located. [Yang, Liao, Gao, and Kroer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.02403)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one and fixed equal positive individual weights, matching the unweighted envy expression and priority in the continuous question.
  • Specified complete types by a finite rational item menu and streams by locally finite phases; the supremum allows arbitrarily large finite menus and supports.
  • Interpreted population-scaled supply as one whole item per unit population per unit time, and defined the fluid evolution through routing measures and individual bundle jumps.
  • Required all arriving supply to be allocated and excluded item kinds valued by nobody, as in the discrete setup.
  • Specified zero initial bundles, zero-denominator conventions, and arbitrary nonanticipating routing among maximizing states.
  • Defined positive-mass comparisons through the product population measure and placed the limsup inside the supremum over fixed infinite streams, so unbounded monopolistic utility is an actual asymptotic condition.

Sharp asymptotic envy factor

The bundle-state measure dynamics have the same sharp asymptotic factor:

\( \displaystyle E^*(\varepsilon)=1+2\log(1/\varepsilon). \)

The proof below applies directly to the measure dynamics. In particular, it does not require a limit theorem connecting finite populations to continuous societies.

Write \(C_\varepsilon=1+2\log(1/\varepsilon)\), with the natural logarithm. The finite-agent result and its lower-bound construction appear in [Yang, Liao, Gao, and Kroer, Section 7 and Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.02403). Here we establish a population-independent upper bound and construct the required lower bound with a fixed finite menu and a single infinite stream.

1. Elementary consequences of the evolution equation

We use the forward equation in its usual time-integrated sense. All indicator functions below are bounded measurable test functions.

For each supported type \(\theta\), define its monopolistic utility and its essential minimum individual utility by

\( \displaystyle V_\theta(t)=\int_0^t v_\theta(j(s))\,\mathrm ds,\qquad a_\theta(t)=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{(\theta,z)\sim\nu_t}z_\theta. \)

The essential infimum is taken within the type-\(\theta\) population.

Type masses remain constant. Applying the evolution equation to the indicator of a type gives

\( \displaystyle \nu_t(\{\theta\}\times\mathbb R_+^\tau)=\mu_\theta. \)

Consequently, these essential infima always concern populations of positive mass.

Each valuation coordinate has the correct population mean. For every \(\theta\),

\[ \displaystyle \int z_\theta\,\mathrm d\nu_t=V_\theta(t). \tag{1} \]

To justify the unbounded coordinate function, first apply the equation to \(f_K(\phi,z)=\min\{z_\theta,K\}\). Under a jump, its increment lies between zero and \(v_\theta(j(s))\), hence between zero and one. As \(K\) increases, that increment converges to \(v_\theta(j(s))\). Monotone convergence on the left and dominated convergence against \(\mathrm ds\,\mathrm dr_s\) on the right prove (1). In particular, all these means are finite at finite times.

Essential minimum utilities are nondecreasing. For any real \(c\), the indicator of

\( \displaystyle \{(\theta,z):z_\theta<c\} \)

cannot increase under a jump: individuals retain their type and all valuation coordinates increase weakly. Its population mass is therefore nonincreasing. This proves that \(a_\theta(t)\) is nondecreasing. It is finite, since (1) and the positive type mass give

\( \displaystyle a_\theta(t)\le V_\theta(t)/\mu_\theta. \)

Every routed recipient values the current item positively. Some supported type values each item positively, and its mass remains positive. Thus the essential maximum greedy score is strictly positive, possibly infinite. A recipient with zero value has score zero under the stipulated conventions, so cannot attain that maximum. Accordingly, a routed recipient’s own value \(y\) satisfies

\[ \displaystyle \varepsilon\le y\le1. \tag{2} \]

2. A potential that accounts for indivisible bundle jumps

Fix a terminal time \(T\), an evaluating type \(\theta\), and a positive number \(A\) satisfying \(A\ge a_\theta(T)\).

Consider any time \(s\le T\) when the current item has value \(x=v_\theta(j(s))>0\) for \(\theta\). Because \(a_\theta(s)\le A\), the essential maximum greedy score is at least \(x/A\). Indeed, for every positive \(\delta\), a positive mass of type-\(\theta\) individuals has utility below \(A+\delta\); their scores exceed \(x/(A+\delta)\). Letting \(\delta\) decrease to zero gives the assertion.

Suppose the routed recipient has type \(\phi\), own pre-jump utility \(b=z_\phi\), and own item value \(y=v_\phi(j(s))\). Greediness implies, when \(b>0\),

\[ \displaystyle \frac{y}{b}\ge\frac{x}{A},\qquad b\le\frac{Ay}{x}. \tag{3} \]

The second inequality also holds when \(b=0\). Moreover,

\[ \displaystyle \frac{x}{y}\le\frac1\varepsilon,\qquad b\le\frac A\varepsilon. \tag{4} \]

Define a nonnegative function on the nonnegative real line by

\( \displaystyle h_A(u)= \begin{cases} 1/\varepsilon,&0\le u\le1,\\ \min\{1/\varepsilon,A/(u-1)\},&1<u\le1+A/\varepsilon,\\ 0,&u>1+A/\varepsilon, \end{cases} \qquad F_A(b)=\int_0^b h_A(u)\,\mathrm du. \)

We claim that every routed jump satisfies

\[ \displaystyle F_A(b+y)-F_A(b)\ge x. \tag{5} \]

If \(x=0\), this follows from the monotonicity of \(F_A\). If \(x>0\), take \(u\) in the interval from \(b\) to \(b+y\). By (2) and (4),

\( \displaystyle u\le b+1\le A/\varepsilon+1. \)

For \(u\le1\), we have \(h_A(u)=1/\varepsilon\ge x/y\). For \(u>1\), we have \(u-1\le b\). If \(b>0\), (3) gives

\( \displaystyle \frac{A}{u-1}\ge\frac Ab\ge\frac xy. \)

Together with \(1/\varepsilon\ge x/y\), this again implies \(h_A(u)\ge x/y\). When \(b=0\), the entire interval lies below one, so the first case suffices. Integrating proves (5).

The total potential is

\[ \displaystyle \sup_{b\ge0}F_A(b) =\frac1\varepsilon+ \int_0^{A/\varepsilon}\min\{1/\varepsilon,A/u\}\,\mathrm du =\frac1\varepsilon+C_\varepsilon A. \tag{6} \]

For the last equality, split the integral at \(A\varepsilon\). The first part equals \(A\), and the second equals

\( \displaystyle A\log\!\left(\frac{A/\varepsilon}{A\varepsilon}\right) =2A\log(1/\varepsilon). \)

3. The upper bound holds for every greedy trajectory

Fix a recipient type \(\phi\). The inequality

\[ \displaystyle z_\theta\le F_A(z_\phi) \tag{7} \]

holds initially. It is preserved by every routed jump: the left side increases by \(x\), while the right side increases by at least \(x\), by (5).

Here is a direct measure-level justification. Let

\( \displaystyle B=\{(\phi,z):z_\theta>F_A(z_\phi)\}. \)

No routed jump can enter \(B\) from its complement. Therefore the increment of the indicator of \(B\) is everywhere nonpositive on routed states. The forward equation gives

\( \displaystyle \nu_t(B)\le\nu_0(B)=0\qquad(0\le t\le T). \)

Thus (7) holds almost everywhere, without needing individual lineages or a particle representation.

Applying (6), and then taking \(A\) down to \(a_\theta(T)\), gives, for every recipient type \(\phi\),

\( \displaystyle z_\theta\le C_\varepsilon a_\theta(T)+\frac1\varepsilon \quad\text{for $\nu_T\)-almost every \((\phi,z)\)}. \tag{8}
\)

If \(a_\theta(T)=0\), use a countable sequence of positive \(A\) decreasing to zero; the same conclusion follows.

Integrating (8) over the whole probability measure and using (1) yields

\[ \displaystyle V_\theta(T)\le C_\varepsilon a_\theta(T)+\frac1\varepsilon, \qquad a_\theta(T)\ge \frac{V_\theta(T)-1/\varepsilon}{C_\varepsilon}. \tag{9} \]

Admissibility requires \(V_\theta(T)\) to diverge for every supported type. Since the support is finite, (9) implies

\[ \displaystyle \min_{\theta\in\Theta}a_\theta(T)\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{10} \]

Once these minima are positive, (8) bounds every envy ratio:

\[ \displaystyle E(T)\le C_\varepsilon+ \frac{1}{\varepsilon\min_\theta a_\theta(T)}. \tag{11} \]

Consequently, every admissible greedy trajectory satisfies

\[ \displaystyle \limsup_{T\to\infty}E(T)\le C_\varepsilon. \tag{12} \]

This also supplies an explicit population-independent error bound. If \(V_{\min}(T)=\min_\theta V_\theta(T)>1/\varepsilon\), then

\[ \displaystyle E(T)\le C_\varepsilon+ \frac{C_\varepsilon}{\varepsilon V_{\min}(T)-1}. \tag{13} \]

4. Realizing integral greedy allocations inside the society

For the lower bound, use two types, called \(A\) and \(B\), each of mass one half.

Suppose initially each type is concentrated at one bundle state. Consider an item for which type \(A\)’s state attains the maximum greedy score. Let its pre-jump state be \(z\), and let the item vector be \(\mathbf v\).

During an interval of length one half, move type-\(A\) mass according to

\( \displaystyle \nu_{t+s}\big|_A =(1/2-s)\delta_{(A,z)} +s\delta_{(A,z+\mathbf v)}, \qquad 0\le s\le1/2, \)

leaving the type-\(B\) population fixed. For \(s<1/2\), choose

\( \displaystyle r_{t+s}=\delta_{(A,z)}. \)

This routing measure has mass one and is absolutely continuous with respect to the current population measure. Its receiving state remains a maximum-score state: its score is unchanged, the other type’s score is unchanged, and the already-served portion of type \(A\) has a smaller score because its own utility has increased.

The measure formula satisfies the forward equation for every bounded test function. At the end, all type-\(A\) individuals have received one indivisible item and are again synchronized.

The same construction works for type \(B\). Hence any integral greedy sequence for two equal-weight agents can be realized exactly at synchronized times, with each allocation taking one half unit of society time. Each individual bundle changes only by the stipulated full item jump.

5. A fixed finite menu approaching the logarithmic bound

Assume first that \(\varepsilon<1\). Choose dyadic rationals

\( \displaystyle \varepsilon<q=p_0<p_1<\cdots<p_k=1. \)

Use the following fixed menu, writing the two entries as the values of types \(A\) and \(B\):

  • A private item with values \((0,1)\).
  • Items with values \((p_\ell,1)\), for every index from zero through \(k\).
  • Items with values \((1,p_\ell)\), for every index from zero through \(k-1\).

There are \(2k+2\) kinds. Every positive value is binary-rational and lies in the required interval. The types are distinct, and every kind has a positive supported valuation.

Define

\[ \displaystyle S=\sum_{\ell=1}^k\left(1-\frac{p_{\ell-1}}{p_\ell}\right), \qquad D=1+2S. \tag{14} \]

We construct one infinite stream whose envy has limit superior at least \(D\).

A single block

At a synchronized block boundary, let:

  • \(H\) be type \(A\)’s own utility;
  • \(R_{\mathrm{old}}\) be type \(B\)’s own utility;
  • \(W_{\mathrm{old}}\) be type \(B\)’s value for a type-\(A\) bundle.

Type \(B\) will receive only private items, so \(R_{\mathrm{old}}\) is an integer.

Choose a sufficiently large integer \(R>R_{\mathrm{old}}\).

First give type \(B\) exactly \(R-R_{\mathrm{old}}\) private items. These allocations are greedy because type \(A\)’s value is zero. Type \(B\)’s own utility becomes \(R\).

Next, for indices \(\ell=0,\ldots,k\), supply the item \((p_\ell,1)\) until type \(A\)’s own utility first reaches or exceeds \(p_\ell R\). Every allocation before stopping goes greedily to type \(A\): if its current own utility is \(U<p_\ell R\), then

\( \displaystyle \frac{p_\ell}{U}>\frac1R. \)

The stopping utility belongs to the interval from \(p_\ell R\), inclusive, to \(p_\ell R+p_\ell\), exclusive.

Finally, for indices \(\ell=k-1,\ldots,0\), supply the item \((1,p_\ell)\) until type \(A\)’s own utility first reaches or exceeds \(R/p_\ell\). Again, every allocation goes greedily to type \(A\), since before stopping

\( \displaystyle U<R/p_\ell \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac1U>\frac{p_\ell}{R}. \)

The stopping utility belongs to the interval from \(R/p_\ell\), inclusive, to \(R/p_\ell+1\), exclusive.

All phases start below their stopping thresholds if \(R\) is sufficiently large: the fixed menu has strictly separated successive thresholds, while every stopping overshoot is less than one. We also choose \(R\) so that \(H<qR\).

Value accumulated in the block

Let \(\Delta W\) be the increase in type \(B\)’s value for a type-\(A\) bundle.

The first shared phase contains

\( \displaystyle \left\lceil\frac{qR-H}{q}\right\rceil =R-\frac Hq+O(1) \)

items, each worth one to type \(B\).

For each subsequent item \((p_\ell,1)\), the number allocated is

\( \displaystyle R\left(1-\frac{p_{\ell-1}}{p_\ell}\right)+O(1). \)

Indeed, the phase begins at own utility \(p_{\ell-1}R\) plus an overshoot less than one, and each item adds \(p_\ell\).

For the descending phase with item \((1,p_\ell)\), the number allocated is

\( \displaystyle R\left(\frac1{p_\ell}-\frac1{p_{\ell+1}}\right)+O(1). \)

Multiplying by its type-\(B\) value \(p_\ell\), its contribution is

\( \displaystyle R\left(1-\frac{p_\ell}{p_{\ell+1}}\right)+O(1). \)

The menu is fixed, so the sum of all rounding errors is bounded independently of \(H\) and \(R\). Thus

\[ \displaystyle \Delta W=DR-\frac Hq+O_{\mathrm{menu}}(1). \tag{15} \]

At the block endpoint, type \(A\)’s own utility satisfies

\[ \displaystyle R/q\le H_{\mathrm{new}}<R/q+1. \tag{16} \]

One infinite stream

Repeat these blocks. In block \(h\), choose an integer \(R_h\) large enough for all threshold conditions above and satisfying

\( \displaystyle R_h\ge h\bigl(1+H_{h-1}+W_{h-1}+R_{h-1}\bigr). \)

It follows that both \(H_{h-1}/R_h\) and \(W_{h-1}/R_h\) tend to zero. Equation (15) therefore gives

\[ \displaystyle \frac{W_h}{R_h} =D+\frac{W_{h-1}-H_{h-1}/q}{R_h} +O_{\mathrm{menu}}(1/R_h) \longrightarrow D. \tag{17} \]

Realize every allocation using Section 4. The resulting stream uses the same finite menu throughout. Every block has finitely many phases, and the private phase durations grow without bound; hence there are only finitely many phases on each bounded time interval.

Let \(T_h\) be the synchronized endpoint of block \(h\). At that time, type \(B\) has own utility \(R_h\) and values every type-\(A\) bundle at \(W_h\). Consequently,

\[ \displaystyle E(T_h)\ge W_h/R_h\longrightarrow D. \tag{18} \]

The stream is admissible. By (1), synchronization, and the fact that type \(B\)’s private bundles have zero value to type \(A\),

\( \displaystyle V_A(T_h)=H_h/2\ge R_h/(2q), \qquad V_B(T_h)=(R_h+W_h)/2\ge R_h/2. \)

Both diverge; their monotonicity gives the required divergence along the entire stream.

6. Taking the supremum

The sum in (14) is the right-endpoint Riemann sum

\( \displaystyle S=\sum_{\ell=1}^k\frac{p_\ell-p_{\ell-1}}{p_\ell} \)

for the integral of \(1/u\) over the interval from \(q\) to one. Dyadic partitions with mesh tending to zero therefore give

\( \displaystyle S\longrightarrow\int_q^1\frac{\mathrm du}{u}=\log(1/q). \)

For every positive \(\eta\), first choose a dyadic \(q>\varepsilon\) sufficiently close to \(\varepsilon\), and then choose a sufficiently fine dyadic partition. These choices ensure

\( \displaystyle D>1+2\log(1/\varepsilon)-\eta. \)

Sections 4–5 then give a single admissible infinite stream in a finite supported society with a fixed finite menu satisfying

\( \displaystyle \limsup_{t\to\infty}E(t)> 1+2\log(1/\varepsilon)-\eta. \)

Taking the supremum and then letting \(\eta\) decrease to zero proves the lower bound.

For \(\varepsilon=1\), use one type of mass one and one item valued at one. During the interval from integer time \(k\) to \(k+1\), move mass continuously from utility \(k\) to utility \(k+1\), routing to the remaining utility-\(k\) population. This is greedy and satisfies the jump equation. At interior times with \(k\) positive, its envy is \((k+1)/k\), so its asymptotic factor is one.

Together with (12), this establishes the sharp value for every allowed \(\varepsilon\).
