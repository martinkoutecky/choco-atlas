The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), per-member costs \(c_t(x_t)=\langle\theta_t,\phi_t(x_t)\rangle\), compact convex action sets, and a rational per-capita feasibility polytope such as \(\sum_t\mu_t x_t=q\), let one organized aggregator per type be a strategic VCG player with total cost \(\mu_t c_t(x_t)\). Each aggregator answers \(K\) allocation-pair queries with Bradley–Terry probabilities based on its total net utility. Determine whether a D-optimal query and learned-cost VCG mechanism can give high-probability bounds, as functions of \(K,d,\tau,\mu\), on each cohort's gain from arbitrary reports, truthful utility under a zero allocation, and the per-capita welfare gap.

Conclusion

The mechanism has the requested simultaneous bounds for every admissible society, when comparisons may be drawn from each full action set \(X_t\), as intended. The guarantees concern total cohort utility and per-capita social cost; the masses must enter the likelihood exactly as stated.

Here are explicit bounds, followed by a self-contained proof. Computational guarantees are relative to the supplied exact oracles: arbitrary continuous features do not imply polynomial bit complexity.

1. Explicit guarantees

Assume \(0<\delta<1\) and \(d\geq 1\). Define

\[ s=\frac{d(d+1)}2,\qquad n=K-s>0,\qquad A=d\log 5+\log\frac{2\tau}{\delta}. \]

For each listed type, define

\[ \kappa_t=\frac1{2+2\cosh(2\mu_tBR)},\qquad \varepsilon_t= \min\left\{ 4\mu_tBR,\; \frac1{\kappa_t}\sqrt{\frac{2dA}{K-s}} \right\}. \]

With probability at least \(1-\delta\), simultaneously:

\[ G_t\leq\varepsilon_t \quad\text{and}\quad H_t\leq\min\{2\mu_tBR,\varepsilon_t\} \qquad\text{for every }t, \]

and, when all cohorts are truthful,

\[ W\leq\min\left\{BR,\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\varepsilon_t\right\}. \]

The bounds for \(G_t\) and \(H_t\) hold uniformly over every fixed choice of the other cohorts’ transcripts. Thus no union bound over strategic reports is needed.

For example, if \(K\geq 2s\), the statistical term satisfies

\[ \frac1{\kappa_t}\sqrt{\frac{2dA}{K-s}} \leq \bigl(2+2\cosh(2\mu_tBR)\bigr) \sqrt{\frac{4d\left(d\log 5+\log(2\tau/\delta)\right)}{K}}. \]

This gives the requested \(\widetilde{\mathcal O}(K^{-1/2})\) rates, with explicit dependence on \(d,\tau,\mu,B,R,\delta\). No factor involving \(1/\min_t\mu_t\) is required for these total cohort quantities.

If \(d=0\), all true and estimated costs vanish, so all three quantities are zero.

2. D-optimal design and rounding

Write the scaled comparison vector as

\[ z_t(a,b)=\mu_t\bigl(\phi_t(b)-\phi_t(a)\bigr), \qquad a,b\in X_t, \]

and let \(\mathcal Z_t\) be the set of these vectors. Continuity and compactness imply that \(\mathcal Z_t\) is compact. Its linear span is \(\mathbb R^d\), because \(\mu_t>0\) and the feature differences span \(\mathbb R^d\).

I use the budget-respecting rounding convention in [Appendix A.2 of the supplied paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17285v3): round frequencies for \(n=K-s\) upward, then pad to exactly \(K\). The proof below includes the effect of padding.

Optimal-design properties

Consider the compact convex set of matrices

\[ \mathcal Q_t=\operatorname{conv}\{zz^\top:z\in\mathcal Z_t\}. \]

The space of symmetric matrices has dimension \(s\). By the finite-dimensional Carathéodory theorem, every matrix in \(\mathcal Q_t\) is a convex combination of at most \(s+1\) generating matrices. This also establishes compactness of \(\mathcal Q_t\).

The spanning assumption supplies a positive definite matrix in \(\mathcal Q_t\): take a positive mixture of outer products of \(d\) linearly independent comparison vectors. Consequently, a determinant maximizer \(M_t\in\mathcal Q_t\) has positive determinant and is positive definite.

For any \(z\in\mathcal Z_t\), optimality along the segment from \(M_t\) to \(zz^\top\) gives

\[ 0\geq \left.\frac{d}{du} \log\det\bigl((1-u)M_t+uzz^\top\bigr) \right|_{u=0} = z^\top M_t^{-1}z-d. \]

Thus

\[ z^\top M_t^{-1}z\leq d \qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal Z_t. \]

Moreover, \(M_t\) belongs to the exposed face

\[ \left\{Q\in\mathcal Q_t: \operatorname{tr}(M_t^{-1}Q)=d\right\}. \]

Every generating matrix appearing with positive weight in a representation of \(M_t\) must belong to this face: all have trace at most \(d\), and their weighted average has trace exactly \(d\). The face lies in a nontrivial affine hyperplane of the \(s\)-dimensional symmetric-matrix space, so its affine dimension is at most \(s-1\). Applying Carathéodory within that hyperplane yields a design representation

\[ M_t=\sum_{j=1}^{r_t}w_{t,j}z_{t,j}z_{t,j}^{\top}, \qquad r_t\leq s,\quad w_{t,j}>0,\quad \sum_jw_{t,j}=1. \]

These arguments prove both the leverage bound and the required support-size bound.

Rounded empirical design

Query support pair \(j\) at least

\[ N_{t,j}=\left\lceil n w_{t,j}\right\rceil \]

times. Since

\[ \sum_jN_{t,j}\leq n+r_t\leq K, \]

padding to exactly \(K\) is possible. Padding may repeat any fixed support pair.

Let \(z_{t,k}\) denote the resulting \(K\) query vectors and set

\[ V_t=\sum_{k=1}^{K}z_{t,k}z_{t,k}^{\top}. \]

Upward rounding and padding imply

\[ V_t\succeq nM_t\succ0. \]

Inversion reverses the positive-definite matrix order, hence

\[ \sup_{z\in\mathcal Z_t}z^\top V_t^{-1}z \leq\frac dn. \]

Multiplying every comparison vector by \(\mu_t\) multiplies every design matrix by \(\mu_t^2\). Its log determinant changes by the constant \(2d\log\mu_t\). Therefore designing with scaled or unscaled feature differences gives the same optimal designs.

3. Uniform estimation error from truthful feedback

Fix a type \(t\), and suppress its index temporarily. Let \(Y_k=1\) mean that the cohort reports preferring the first queried action. Under truthful feedback,

\[ Y_k\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\bigl(\sigma(\theta^\top z_k)\bigr) \]

independently.

The negative log likelihood is

\[ \ell(v)= \sum_{k=1}^{K} \left[ \log(1+\exp(v^\top z_k))-Y_kv^\top z_k \right], \qquad \|v\|\leq B. \]

It is continuous on the compact parameter ball, so an MLE exists.

A score bound

At the true parameter, the score is

\[ S=\nabla\ell(\theta) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \bigl(\sigma(\theta^\top z_k)-Y_k\bigr)z_k. \]

For any fixed unit vector \(u\), write

\[ u^\top V^{-1/2}S=\sum_k a_k\xi_k, \qquad a_k=u^\top V^{-1/2}z_k. \]

The random variables \(\xi_k\) are independent, centered, and supported in intervals of length \(1\). Also,

\[ \sum_ka_k^2 = u^\top V^{-1/2}VV^{-1/2}u =1. \]

Hoeffding’s inequality therefore gives

\[ \Pr\left(\left|u^\top V^{-1/2}S\right|\geq r\right) \leq2e^{-2r^2}. \]

A Euclidean \(1/2\)-net \(\mathcal N\) of the unit sphere can be chosen with \(|\mathcal N|\leq5^d\). Indeed, a maximal \(1/2\)-separated set covers the sphere; its disjoint balls of radius \(1/4\) lie inside the ball of radius \(5/4\), giving the cardinality bound by comparing volumes. For every vector \(v\),

\[ \|v\|\leq2\max_{u\in\mathcal N}|u^\top v|. \]

Apply the tail bound to this net with

\[ r=\sqrt{\frac{d\log5+\log(2\tau/\delta)}2}. \]

A union bound shows that, with probability at least \(1-\delta/\tau\),

\[ \|S\|_{V^{-1}} = \|V^{-1/2}S\| \leq\sqrt{2A}. \]

Curvature and the constrained MLE

For every admissible parameter \(v\) and comparison vector \(z\),

\[ |v^\top z| \leq B\mu_t\|\phi_t(b)-\phi_t(a)\| \leq2\mu_tBR. \]

The logistic derivative is

\[ \sigma'(u)=\frac1{2+2\cosh u}. \]

Consequently, throughout the parameter ball,

\[ \nabla^2\ell(v) = \sum_k\sigma'(v^\top z_k)z_kz_k^\top \succeq\kappa_tV. \]

Let \(e=\hat\theta_t-\theta_t\). The segment connecting the two parameters lies in the ball. Integrating the Hessian along that segment yields

\[ \bigl(\nabla\ell(\hat\theta_t)-\nabla\ell(\theta_t)\bigr)^\top e \geq\kappa_t\|e\|_V^2. \]

Because \(\hat\theta_t\) minimizes the differentiable convex likelihood over the convex ball, its first-order optimality condition gives

\[ \nabla\ell(\hat\theta_t)^\top(\theta_t-\hat\theta_t)\geq0. \]

Combining these inequalities and applying Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[ \kappa_t\|e\|_V^2 \leq-\nabla\ell(\theta_t)^\top e \leq\|S\|_{V^{-1}}\|e\|_V. \]

If \(e=0\), the desired conclusion is immediate. Otherwise division gives

\[ \|\hat\theta_t-\theta_t\|_{V_t} \leq\frac{\sqrt{2A}}{\kappa_t}. \]

Error over all actions

Define the true total cohort cost and its estimation error by

\[ C_t(a)=\mu_tc_t(a), \qquad E_t(a)=\hat C_t(a)-C_t(a). \]

Using the leverage bound, for every \(a,b\in X_t\),

\[ |E_t(b)-E_t(a)| = |(\hat\theta_t-\theta_t)^\top z_t(a,b)| \leq \frac1{\kappa_t}\sqrt{\frac{2dA}{K-s}}. \]

There is also the deterministic bound

\[ |E_t(b)-E_t(a)| \leq \mu_t\|\hat\theta_t-\theta_t\| \|\phi_t(b)-\phi_t(a)\| \leq4\mu_tBR. \]

A union bound over the \(\tau\) listed types now establishes an event of probability at least \(1-\delta\) on which

\[ |E_t(b)-E_t(a)|\leq\varepsilon_t \qquad \text{for every }t\text{ and every }a,b\in X_t. \]

Since \(\phi_t(0)=0\), we have \(E_t(0)=0\). Thus, on this same event,

\[ |E_t(a)|\leq\varepsilon_t. \]

Independence between different types is unnecessary for this union bound.

4. Deterministic mechanism bounds on that event

The remaining proof is deterministic. Fix a type \(t\) and any transcripts for the other types. Define

\[ Q_{-t}(x)=\sum_{s\ne t}\hat C_s(x_s), \qquad A_t=\min_{x\in F_t^0}Q_{-t}(x). \]

Compactness, continuity, and the assumed nonemptiness of \(F_t^0\) ensure that this minimum exists.

Under any transcript of type \(t\), its utility at the resulting allocation \(x\) is

\[ U_t(x)=A_t-Q_{-t}(x)-C_t(x_t). \]

The quantity \(A_t\) is independent of type \(t\)’s reports.

Deviation gains

Let \(x\) result from type \(t\)’s truthful transcript and let \(y\) result from any replacement transcript. Truthful allocation optimality gives

\[ Q_{-t}(x)+\hat C_t(x_t) \leq Q_{-t}(y)+\hat C_t(y_t). \]

Subtracting utilities and inserting the truthful estimation error yields

\[ \begin{aligned} U_t(y)-U_t(x) &=Q_{-t}(x)+C_t(x_t)-Q_{-t}(y)-C_t(y_t)\\ &\leq E_t(y_t)-E_t(x_t)\\ &\leq\varepsilon_t. \end{aligned} \]

This holds for every replacement transcript, including any transcript generated by a history-dependent strategy. Taking the supremum proves

\[ G_t\leq\varepsilon_t. \]

Truthful utility shortfalls

Choose an exclusion minimizer \(y^0\in F_t^0\). Because \(\hat C_t(0)=0\), truthful allocation optimality gives

\[ Q_{-t}(x)+\hat C_t(x_t) \leq Q_{-t}(y^0)+\hat C_t(0) =A_t. \]

Therefore

\[ U_t(x) = A_t-Q_{-t}(x)-\hat C_t(x_t)+E_t(x_t) \geq E_t(x_t) \geq-\varepsilon_t. \]

Also, directly from the parameter and feature bounds,

\[ |E_t(x_t)|\leq2\mu_tBR. \]

Hence

\[ H_t\leq\min\{2\mu_tBR,\varepsilon_t\}. \]

This is where the stated exclusion convention matters: the comparison allocation belongs to \(F\) and assigns action \(0\) to type \(t\).

Social-cost gap

When all cohorts are truthful, let \(x^*\) minimize \(J\) over \(F\). Existence follows from continuity and compactness. Estimated allocation optimality gives

\[ \sum_t\hat C_t(\hat x_t) \leq \sum_t\hat C_t(x_t^*). \]

Consequently,

\[ \begin{aligned} W &=J(\hat x)-J(x^*)\\ &\leq\sum_t\bigl(E_t(x_t^*)-E_t(\hat x_t)\bigr)\\ &\leq\sum_t\varepsilon_t. \end{aligned} \]

Finally, nonnegativity and Cauchy–Schwarz imply

\[ 0\leq c_t(a)\leq BR, \qquad 0\leq J(x)\leq\sum_t\mu_tBR=BR. \]

Thus \(W\leq BR\), proving the stated capped welfare bound.

These deterministic arguments hold for every choice of other transcripts on the estimation event. They therefore establish all the required simultaneous quantifiers.

5. Runtime and computational qualifications

There are exactly \(\tau K\) binary observations. One implementation uses:

  • \(\tau\) exact design-oracle calls, each producing at most \(s\) support pairs and their frequencies;
  • \(\tau\) global-minimization calls for MLEs in dimension \(d\);
  • one global-minimization call for allocation in dimension \(m\tau\);
  • \(\tau\) global-minimization calls for exclusion problems in dimension \(m\tau\).

Thus there are \(2\tau+1\) global-minimization calls.

Repeated queries can be compressed into success counts. With padding chosen from the existing support, each MLE objective has at most \(s\) terms:

\[ \ell_t(v)= \sum_{j=1}^{r_t} \left[ N_{t,j}\log(1+\exp(v^\top z_{t,j})) -a_{t,j}v^\top z_{t,j} \right], \]

where \(a_{t,j}\) is the number of reported successes and the final multiplicities satisfy \(\sum_jN_{t,j}=K\).

For an explicit oracle-relative accounting, let:

  • \(T_{\mathrm D,t}\) be the design-oracle cost;
  • \(T_{\mathrm M,t}\) be the MLE global-minimization cost;
  • \(T_{\mathrm F}\) and \(T_{\mathrm{0},t}\) be the allocation and exclusion global-minimization costs;
  • \(T_{\phi,t}\) bound one feature evaluation outside those oracle calls.

In an exact arithmetic model supporting the required frequency rounding, a conservative implementation has cost

\[ \begin{aligned} T_{\mathrm{total}} \leq{}& \sum_tT_{\mathrm D,t} +\sum_tT_{\mathrm M,t} +T_{\mathrm F} +\sum_tT_{\mathrm{0},t}\\ &+\mathcal O\!\left( \tau L+\tau K(m+d)+\tau^2d +(K+\tau)\sum_tT_{\phi,t} \right). \end{aligned} \]

Here the likelihood problems have dimension \(d\), at most \(s\) distinct query terms, and counts bounded by \(K\). Allocation and exclusion problems have dimension \(m\tau\) and rational constraint descriptions derived from the input of bit length \(L\). Feature evaluations and numerical work internal to an oracle are included in that oracle’s cost.

This is an oracle-relative operation bound, not an unconditional polynomial bit-time bound. Arbitrary continuous features have no specified effective representation or evaluation complexity; exact designs, frequencies, and minimizers need not be rational. The premises therefore support the oracle-call accounting above, but not a polynomial bound solely in \(m,\tau,L,K,d\).

6. Scope of the answer

For every fixed admissible society, all requested quantities obey the stated rates. The bounds use the number \(\tau\) of listed types, and welfare sums errors in total cohort costs. The masses are already incorporated in those errors and must not be applied a second time.

The result does not assert a support-size-independent rate when \(\tau\) grows with \(K\), or polynomial runtime for arbitrary continuous features. Neither additional assertion is needed for the question’s requested parameter-dependent guarantees.
