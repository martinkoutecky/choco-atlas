The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) interval courses, \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), binary demand sets \(D_t\), and rational per-capita seat supplies \(q_j\), compute the maximum total assigned course mass. Each type’s mass may be distributed among whole schedules \(S\subseteq D_t\) whose courses do not overlap, subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:\,j\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_j\) for every course \(j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge1\) courses, each with one interval \([a_j,b_j)\), where \(0\le a_j<b_j\) are binary-encoded integers; courses meeting only at an endpoint do not conflict.
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Type \(t\) has an explicitly listed demand set \(D_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with utility one for each demanded course and zero otherwise. Courses have unit credits, there are no credit caps, and seat supplies \(q_j\) are nonnegative binary rationals.
Let \(\mathcal S_t\) contain all subsets of \(D_t\) consisting of pairwise nonconflicting courses, including the empty schedule. An allocation uses real masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_t}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_t:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_j\).
Define the maximum total assigned course mass as \(V^*=\max_x\sum_t\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_t}|S|x_{t,S}\). Schedules are implicit, not enumerated in the input; \(L\) is the total input bit length.

Is computing \(V^*\) exactly solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard (for the decision version \(V^*\ge K\), with binary-rational threshold \(K\) included in \(L\))?

The discrete counterpart, assigning whole schedules to individual students, is NP-hard with binary utilities, unit course credits, and no credit caps. [Verified source](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/opus/volltexte/2023/17929/pdf/LIPIcs-FORC-2023-8.pdf)
No result settling this exact continuous problem was located in the literature search.

Result

Computing \(V^*\) exactly is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Consequently, the decision problem \(V^*\ge K\) belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\).

We prove this by constructing a polynomial-size rational linear program whose feasible solutions represent exactly the allowed continuous allocations.

1. A graph representing schedules

Sort the distinct course endpoints as \(c_1<\cdots<c_r\), where \(2\le r\le 2m\). For each type \(t\), construct a directed multigraph \(G_t\) with vertices \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\). Include:

  • An idle arc \(v_i\to v_{i+1}\) for every \(1\le i<r\).
  • A course arc \(e_{t,j}:v_i\to v_k\) for every \(j\in D_t\), where \(a_j=c_i\) and \(b_j=c_k\).

Arcs remain distinct even when they have identical endpoints. Because \(a_j<b_j\), every arc goes from a smaller vertex index to a larger one. Thus \(G_t\) is acyclic. Its arc set \(E_t\) satisfies
\[ \]
\( |E_t|=(r-1)+|D_t|\le 3m-1. \)

Claim. The sets of course labels appearing on paths from \(v_1\) to \(v_r\) are exactly the schedules in \(\mathcal S_t\).

Proof. On any such path, successive course arcs satisfy \(b_j\le a_k\): after completing the first arc, the path can only move forward in time before beginning the next. Hence their half-open intervals do not conflict. All labels belong to \(D_t\), so the resulting set belongs to \(\mathcal S_t\).

Conversely, take \(S\in\mathcal S_t\) and order its courses by starting time. For successive courses \(j,k\), nonconflict implies \(b_j\le a_k\). Starting at \(v_1\), follow idle arcs to the first course’s starting vertex, traverse its course arc, and continue in this manner, finishing with idle arcs to \(v_r\). This constructs a path whose course labels are exactly \(S\). The empty schedule corresponds to the path consisting entirely of idle arcs. This also handles courses meeting only at an endpoint. \(\square\)

Only actual endpoints are used as vertices; the construction does not expand binary-encoded time into individual time steps.

2. The compact linear program

For every type \(t\) and arc \(e\in E_t\), introduce a real variable \(f_{t,e}\ge0\).

Let \(\delta_t^+(v)\) and \(\delta_t^-(v)\) denote the outgoing and incoming arcs at \(v\). Define the prescribed net flow by
\( \beta_t(v_1)=\mu_t \),
\( \beta_t(v_r)=-\mu_t \),
and \( \beta_t(v_i)=0 \) for \(1<i<r\).

The linear program maximizes

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j\in D_t}f_{t,e_{t,j}} \)

subject to flow conservation

\( \displaystyle \sum_{e\in\delta_t^+(v)}f_{t,e}-\sum_{e\in\delta_t^-(v)}f_{t,e}=\beta_t(v) \qquad\text{for every type }t\text{ and vertex }v, \)

seat-supply constraints

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t:\,j\in D_t}f_{t,e_{t,j}}\le q_j \qquad\text{for every course }j, \)

and nonnegativity of all arc flows.

Thus each type sends mass \(\mu_t\) through its schedule graph. Shared seat supplies couple the different types through their course arcs.

3. Exact equivalence with allocations

From an allocation to flows

Let \(x\) be any feasible allocation. For each \(S\in\mathcal S_t\), choose the path \(P_t(S)\) constructed in Section 1 and set

\( \displaystyle f_{t,e}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_t:\,e\in P_t(S)}x_{t,S}. \)

Each path has net flow \(1\) at its source, net flow \(-1\) at its sink, and net flow \(0\) elsewhere. Since \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\), these arc flows satisfy all required conservation equations.

Moreover,

\( \displaystyle f_{t,e_{t,j}}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_t:\,j\in S}x_{t,S}. \)

Therefore the seat-supply inequalities hold. Summing this identity over types and courses proves objective preservation:

\( \displaystyle \sum_t\sum_{j\in D_t}f_{t,e_{t,j}}=\sum_t\sum_{S\in\mathcal S_t}|S|x_{t,S}. \)

From flows to an allocation

We explicitly prove the needed decomposition, including for real-valued flows.

Fix a type \(t\) and a feasible flow. While its residual source outflow is positive, start at \(v_1\) and follow arcs with positive residual flow. Whenever the walk reaches an intermediate vertex along a positive-flow arc, conservation guarantees a positive-flow outgoing arc. Acyclicity prevents revisiting vertices, so the walk reaches \(v_r\).

Let \(P\) be this path and let

\( \displaystyle \lambda=\min_{e\in P}f_{t,e}>0. \)

Subtract \(\lambda\) from every arc of \(P\), and record \(P\) with weight \(\lambda\). The residual flow remains nonnegative and satisfies conservation with its source and sink values reduced by \(\lambda\). At least one positive arc becomes zero. Consequently, at most \(|E_t|\) such steps occur.

When residual source outflow is zero, no positive residual arc can remain. Indeed, otherwise choose the smallest-index vertex having a positive outgoing arc. It has no positive incoming arc, contradicting conservation: its required net flow is now zero. The sink has no outgoing arcs and cannot be that vertex.

We have therefore obtained paths \(P_{t,1},\ldots,P_{t,h_t}\) and positive weights \(\lambda_{t,\ell}\) satisfying

\( \displaystyle h_t\le |E_t|,\qquad \sum_{\ell=1}^{h_t}\lambda_{t,\ell}=\mu_t,\qquad f_{t,e}=\sum_{\ell:\,e\in P_{t,\ell}}\lambda_{t,\ell}. \)

Let \(S_{t,\ell}\) be the set of course labels on \(P_{t,\ell}\). Section 1 gives \(S_{t,\ell}\in\mathcal S_t\). Define

\( \displaystyle x_{t,S}=\sum_{\ell:\,S_{t,\ell}=S}\lambda_{t,\ell}, \)

with zero mass for schedules absent from the decomposition. Then \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and each course’s assigned mass equals its aggregate course-arc flow. Thus all supply constraints hold, and the objective is again preserved.

Both directions together prove that the compact LP has optimal value exactly \(V^*\).

4. Polynomial time and exact arithmetic

The LP has at most \(\tau(3m-1)\) variables, at most \(2m\tau\) conservation equations, and \(m\) supply inequalities, besides nonnegativity constraints. Every matrix coefficient and objective coefficient belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). All right-hand sides are supplied binary rationals or their negatives. Hence even an explicit dense encoding has length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Sorting the binary-encoded endpoints also takes polynomial time.

We invoke the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: given an explicitly encoded LP with rational data, a deterministic algorithm can detect infeasibility or unboundedness, or return an exact rational optimal solution, in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. The polynomial bit-complexity result is discussed in [Adler and Beling’s paper](https://adler.ieor.berkeley.edu/ilans_pubs/lp_algebraic_stoc_1992.pdf).

Its hypotheses hold here. The LP has explicit rational data and is feasible: send every type’s entire mass along its all-idle path. It is also bounded. The path decomposition shows \(0\le f_{t,e}\le\mu_t\) for every arc, so its feasible set is closed and bounded. An optimum therefore exists, and rational LP optimization returns its exact value.

Finally, comparing the returned rational value with the binary-rational threshold \(K\) takes polynomial time by integer cross-multiplication.

The discrete hardness stated in the question is compatible with this result: our representation permits a type’s mass to split among several schedule paths, exactly as the continuous model allows.
