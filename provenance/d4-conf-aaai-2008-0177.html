The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an explicit impression set \(M\), a finite supported rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete bid-tree types \(t\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), find prices \(p_j\geq 0\) and an assignment of each type’s mass \(\mu_t\) over its feasible integral bundles \(B\). Every bundle receiving positive mass must maximize that type’s bid-tree value minus \(\sum_{j\in M}p_jB_j\); total assigned impressions must not exceed \(q_j\), and any impression with slack supply must have price zero.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) be an explicitly listed nonempty impression set, with \(m=|M|\), and let supplies be \(q_j\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) complete bid-tree types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a rooted tree of nonempty subsets of \(M\): the root is \(M\), children partition their parent, and leaves are singletons. Each node \(T\) has an incremental bid \(b_{tT}\in\mathbb Q\) and capacity \(c_{tT}\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\cup\{+\infty\}\); singleton capacities are finite. Capacities apply to each individual bundle, independently of the society's supplies.
Writing \(B(T)=\sum_{j\in T}B_j\), the feasible bundles are \(F_t=\{B\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{M}:B(T)\leq c_{tT}\text{ for every node }T\}\), with value \(v_t(B)=\sum_T b_{tT}B(T)\).
An equilibrium consists of prices \(p\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^{M}\) and finitely supported rational assignment masses \(a_{tB}\geq0\), satisfying \(\sum_{B\in F_t}a_{tB}=\mu_t\). Positive assignment mass requires \(B\in\arg\max_{C\in F_t}\{v_t(C)-p\cdot C\}\); all utility ties are permitted.
For each impression, \(d_j=\sum_t\sum_{B\in F_t}a_{tB}B_j\leq q_j\), and \(d_j<q_j\) requires \(p_j=0\). Let \(K\) be the total number of tree nodes and \(L\) the total binary input length.

Is computing such an equilibrium polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many individual bidders, optimal allocation-LP solutions paired with optimal supply-constraint dual prices form a competitive equilibrium; integral supplies admit an integral optimal allocation, and the LP is polynomial-time solvable ([Lahaie, Parkes, and Pennock, 2008](https://econcs.seas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum6346/files/econcs/files/lahaie_aaai08.pdf)).
No result settling this exact support-encoded problem with mass split over integral bundles was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and allowed nonnegative rational per-capita supplies, including zero.
  • Represented complete bid trees explicitly by their node subsets, completing missing branches with zero incremental bids so children partition their parent and leaves are singletons.
  • Used signed rational incremental bids, as permitted by the bid-tree examples and valuation formula, and nonnegative integral capacities, possibly infinite at internal nodes.
  • Made singleton capacities finite, following the bounded singleton completion used for discrete bidders; kept these individual bounds independent of per-capita supplies, which do not bound a single individual's bundle.
  • Specified rational prices and finitely supported rational assignment masses, with every utility-maximizing tied bundle eligible.

Result

An equilibrium can be computed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L\). The algorithm outputs rational prices and at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive assignment masses, all with polynomial bit length.

The proof uses a compact linear program and an explicit decomposition of each type’s fractional allocation into feasible integral bundles.

1. Linear valuations and fractional bundle polytopes

Let \(\mathcal N_t\) denote the nodes of type \(t\)’s tree, and let \(\mathcal N_t^{\mathrm{fin}}\) contain its finite-capacity nodes. Define

\(w_{tj}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal N_t:\,j\in T}b_{tT}\).

Changing the order of summation gives, for every bundle \(B\),

\(v_t(B)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal N_t}b_{tT}\sum_{j\in T}B_j=\sum_{j\in M}w_{tj}B_j.\)

Thus the stated valuations are linear in bundle quantities, even when incremental bids are negative.

For a real vector \(x\), write \(x(T)=\sum_{j\in T}x_j\), and define

\(P_t=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{M}:x(T)\leq c_{tT}\text{ for every }T\in\mathcal N_t^{\mathrm{fin}}\}.\)

The zero vector belongs to \(P_t\). Finite singleton capacities make \(P_t\) bounded. Moreover,

\(F_t=P_t\cap\mathbb Z^{M}.\)

We next prove constructively that \(P_t=\operatorname{conv}(F_t)\), with an efficient rational decomposition.

2. A constructive decomposition lemma

Lemma. Every rational \(x\in P_t\) can be expressed as a rational convex combination of at most \(m+1\) bundles in \(F_t\). This combination can be computed in polynomial time in the encoding lengths of \(x\) and the tree.

Proof. Order the singleton leaves by a depth-first traversal of the tree, and write the resulting impression order as \(j_1,\ldots,j_m\). The descendant leaves of every node are consecutive in this order. Because children partition their parent, these descendant leaves are exactly the impressions belonging to that node.

Set

\(S_0=0,\qquad S_i=\sum_{h=1}^{i}x_{j_h}\quad(1\leq i\leq m).\)

For \(\theta\in[0,1)\), define an integral bundle by

\(B_{j_i}(\theta)=\lfloor S_i+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor S_{i-1}+\theta\rfloor.\)

Since the prefix sums are nondecreasing, all these coordinates are nonnegative.

Consider any finite-capacity node \(T\), whose impressions occupy positions \(a,\ldots,b\). Telescoping gives

\(B(\theta)(T)=\lfloor S_b+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor S_{a-1}+\theta\rfloor.\)

Feasibility of \(x\) implies \(S_b-S_{a-1}\leq c_{tT}\). Since \(c_{tT}\) is an integer,

\(\lfloor S_b+\theta\rfloor\leq\lfloor S_{a-1}+\theta+c_{tT}\rfloor=\lfloor S_{a-1}+\theta\rfloor+c_{tT}.\)

Consequently, \(B(\theta)(T)\leq c_{tT}\). This holds for every finite-capacity node, so \(B(\theta)\in F_t\).

For any nonnegative real \(s=n+r\), where \(n\) is integral and \(0\leq r<1\), the function \(\lfloor s+\theta\rfloor\) equals \(n\) for an interval of length \(1-r\) and \(n+1\) for an interval of length \(r\), apart from irrelevant endpoints. Therefore,

\(\int_0^1\lfloor s+\theta\rfloor\,d\theta=s.\)

Applying this identity to successive prefix sums yields

\(\int_0^1 B_{j_i}(\theta)\,d\theta=S_i-S_{i-1}=x_{j_i}.\)

It remains to turn this average into a small explicit combination. Each function \(\lfloor S_i+\theta\rfloor\) has at most one breakpoint inside \((0,1)\), located at

\(\beta_i=\lceil S_i\rceil-S_i\)

when this number is nonzero. Sort the distinct interior breakpoints together with the endpoints:

\(0=\gamma_0<\gamma_1<\cdots<\gamma_r=1.\)

There are at most \(m\) interior breakpoints, hence \(r\leq m+1\). For each interval choose

\(\theta_\ell=(\gamma_{\ell-1}+\gamma_\ell)/2,\qquad \alpha_\ell=\gamma_\ell-\gamma_{\ell-1},\qquad B^\ell=B(\theta_\ell).\)

The bundle is constant on each open interval, so the integral identity proves

\(x=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\alpha_\ell B^\ell,\qquad \sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\alpha_\ell=1,\qquad \alpha_\ell>0.\)

For rational \(x\), all prefix sums, breakpoints, weights, and representatives are rational. Their bit lengths remain polynomial: forming a sum of \(m\) encoded rationals increases bit length by at most a polynomial amount, and taking floors, sorting rationals, and forming differences and midpoints are polynomial-time operations. Computing the bundles uses at most \(m+1\) representatives and \(m\) coordinates per representative. This proves the lemma.

The same construction works for real \(x\), with real weights. Since \(P_t\) is convex and contains \(F_t\), it also proves

\(P_t=\operatorname{conv}(F_t).\)

3. A compact welfare linear program

Introduce aggregate quantity variables \(y_{tj}\), representing the total mass-weighted quantity assigned to type \(t\). Write \(y_t(T)=\sum_{j\in T}y_{tj}\).

Consider the linear program

\(\max\ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j\in M}w_{tj}y_{tj}\)

subject to

\(y_{tj}\geq0\quad\text{for all }t,j,\)

\(y_t(T)\leq\mu_t c_{tT}\quad\text{for all }t,\ T\in\mathcal N_t^{\mathrm{fin}},\)

\(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}y_{tj}\leq q_j\quad\text{for all }j\in M.\)

This program has \(\tau m\) variables and at most \(K+m\) constraints apart from nonnegativity.

To verify that it represents precisely the allowed assignment model, first take any feasible assignment and set

\(y_{tj}=\sum_{B\in F_t}a_{tB}B_j.\)

Every bundle obeys its individual capacity constraints, and the type’s assignment masses sum to \(\mu_t\). Consequently,

\(y_t(T)\leq\sum_B a_{tB}c_{tT}=\mu_t c_{tT}.\)

The supply inequalities also hold, and linearity of valuations makes the objective equal to assignment welfare.

Conversely, for any feasible \(y\), positivity of \(\mu_t\) permits the definition

\(x_t=y_t/\mu_t\in P_t.\)

Apply the lemma to obtain

\(x_t=\sum_{\ell}\alpha_{t\ell}B^{t\ell}.\)

Assign mass \(\mu_t\alpha_{t\ell}\) to bundle \(B^{t\ell}\), combining masses if a bundle occurs more than once. These masses sum to \(\mu_t\) and reproduce \(y_t\) exactly.

Thus the compact program is an exact formulation of the welfare problem with mass split over integral bundles.

Its feasible region is nonempty because \(y=0\) is feasible. It is bounded because

\(0\leq y_{tj}\leq\mu_t c_{t\{j\}}.\)

Hence its optimum is finite and attained.

4. Dual prices make every assigned bundle demanded

Associate nonnegative dual variables \(p_j\) with supply constraints, and nonnegative variables \(\lambda_{tT}\) with finite-capacity constraints. The dual program is

\(\min\ \sum_{j\in M}q_jp_j+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{T\in\mathcal N_t^{\mathrm{fin}}}\mu_t c_{tT}\lambda_{tT}\)

subject to

\(p_j+\sum_{T\in\mathcal N_t^{\mathrm{fin}}:\,j\in T}\lambda_{tT}\geq w_{tj}\quad\text{for every }t,j,\)

\(p_j\geq0,\qquad\lambda_{tT}\geq0.\)

It is feasible: set all \(\lambda_{tT}=0\) and choose

\(p_j=\max\{0,w_{1j},\ldots,w_{\tau j}\}.\)

Choose optimal primal and dual solutions \(y\) and \((p,\lambda)\). Strong duality applies because the primal is feasible with a finite optimum.

Define the nonnegative dual slacks

\(r_{tj}=p_j+\sum_{T\in\mathcal N_t^{\mathrm{fin}}:\,j\in T}\lambda_{tT}-w_{tj}.\)

Expanding the difference between dual and primal objectives gives

\(0=\sum_jp_j\bigl(q_j-\sum_t y_{tj}\bigr)+\sum_t\sum_{T\in\mathcal N_t^{\mathrm{fin}}}\lambda_{tT}\bigl(\mu_t c_{tT}-y_t(T)\bigr)+\sum_t\sum_jr_{tj}y_{tj}.\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore every summand vanishes.

In particular,

\(p_j\bigl(q_j-\sum_t y_{tj}\bigr)=0.\)

This is the required condition that unused supply has zero price.

For each type define

\(U_t=\sum_{T\in\mathcal N_t^{\mathrm{fin}}}c_{tT}\lambda_{tT}.\)

For any feasible integral bundle \(C\in F_t\), the definition of \(r_{tj}\) gives

\(v_t(C)-p\cdot C=\sum_{T\in\mathcal N_t^{\mathrm{fin}}}\lambda_{tT}C(T)-\sum_jr_{tj}C_j\leq U_t.\)

The inequality follows from \(C(T)\leq c_{tT}\), \(C_j\geq0\), and nonnegativity of \(\lambda\) and \(r\).

For \(x_t=y_t/\mu_t\), vanishing of the type’s capacity and coordinate slack terms, divided by the positive number \(\mu_t\), gives

\(\sum_T\lambda_{tT}\bigl(c_{tT}-x_t(T)\bigr)+\sum_jr_{tj}x_{tj}=0.\)

Thus

\(w_t\cdot x_t-p\cdot x_t=U_t.\)

Now decompose \(x_t=\sum_\ell\alpha_{t\ell}B^{t\ell}\) as in the lemma. Linearity implies

\(\sum_\ell\alpha_{t\ell}\bigl(v_t(B^{t\ell})-p\cdot B^{t\ell}\bigr)=U_t.\)

Every expression being averaged is at most \(U_t\), and every weight is positive. If any expression were strictly smaller, the average would be strictly smaller. Hence every supported bundle satisfies

\(v_t(B^{t\ell})-p\cdot B^{t\ell}=U_t.\)

Since all feasible bundles have utility at most \(U_t\), every bundle receiving positive assignment mass is utility-maximizing. Ties cause no difficulty.

Finally, the decomposition reproduces \(y\), so actual demand is \(d_j=\sum_t y_{tj}\). Primal feasibility and the supply slack equations give exactly the equilibrium’s supply and price conditions.

5. Polynomial running time and output length

The only general algorithmic theorem needed is the standard theorem for rational linear programming: an explicitly encoded rational linear program with a feasible finite optimum admits exact rational optimal primal and dual solutions computable in time polynomial in its encoding length; such solutions can be chosen with polynomial bit length. Polynomial-time LP algorithms and the determinant bounds underlying the output-size guarantee are described in [MIT’s LP algorithm notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/18/Scribe/s14-lpAlgorithms/s14-lpAlgorithms.html) and [LP bit-complexity notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n11-lp.html).

Its hypotheses were checked above. The compact primal and dual have polynomially many variables and constraints. Their coefficients are rational: the new coefficients are sums of input bids and products \(\mu_t c_{tT}\), all of polynomial bit length. Their complete encodings therefore have size polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

After solving them, division by each positive \(\mu_t\) preserves polynomial bit length. The decomposition lemma then produces at most \(m+1\) bundles per type in polynomial time. Multiplying its rational weights by \(\mu_t\) also preserves polynomial bit length.

The resulting explicit equilibrium consequently has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive assignment masses and is computable in polynomial time. No expansion into a number of individuals determined by the mass denominators is required.
