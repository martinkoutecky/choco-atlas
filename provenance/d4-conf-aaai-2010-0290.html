The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a required skill set \(S\), support types \(t=1,\ldots,\tau\) with skill bundles \(A_t\subseteq S\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and a rational threshold \(\rho\), let \(\mathcal Q\) be the inclusion-minimal type sets whose bundles cover \(S\). Does the LP maximizing \(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal Q}x_Q\), subject to \(x_Q\ge 0\) and \(\sum_{Q\ni t}x_Q\le\mu_t\) for every type \(t\), have optimum at least \(\rho\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) be a nonempty required skill set, with \(m=|S|\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Type \(t\) has a skill bundle \(A_t\subseteq S\) and mass \(\mu_t>0\), with \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=1\). The threshold is \(\rho\in\mathbb Q\).
Let \(\mathcal Q\) consist of all inclusion-minimal subsets \(Q\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) satisfying \(\bigcup_{t\in Q}A_t=S\). This family is specified implicitly by the bundles, not supplied as an enumerated list.
Define \(V=\max\{\sum_{Q\in\mathcal Q}x_Q:x_Q\in\mathbb R_{\ge0},\ \sum_{Q\ni t}x_Q\le\mu_t\text{ for every }t\}\). Unused mass is permitted; if \(\mathcal Q=\varnothing\), set \(V=0\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the bundles, masses, and threshold.

Is deciding whether \(V\ge\rho\) solvable in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents, partitioning them into coalitions to maximize the number whose combined skills cover a nonempty required skill set is NP-complete in its threshold decision form, even when the threshold is \(2\).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required S to be nonempty, following the discrete definition's nonempty task requirements.
  • Defined m as the number of required skills and L as the total binary input length.
  • Made explicit that the family of minimal covers is implicit and is not enumerated in the input.
  • Made explicit that allocation variables are nonnegative real numbers and that an instance with no cover has optimum zero.

Result

Deciding whether \(V\ge\rho\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Consequently, it has no algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof uses fractional graph coloring, so it establishes hardness of the fractional problem directly.

1. Minimality does not change the packing optimum

Let \(\mathcal C\) denote all subsets of types whose bundles cover \(S\), including covers that are not inclusion-minimal.

The packing optimum over \(\mathcal C\) equals \(V\). Indeed, every \(C\in\mathcal C\) contains an inclusion-minimal cover \(Q(C)\subseteq C\). Given a feasible packing over \(\mathcal C\), transfer the entire weight on each \(C\) to \(Q(C)\), adding weights when necessary. This preserves the objective and cannot increase the load on any type. Conversely, every packing over \(\mathcal Q\) is a packing over \(\mathcal C\).

Thus we may work with all covers.

2. An exact relation to fractional graph coloring

Let \(G=(U,E)\) be a finite simple graph with at least one edge. Write \(\mathcal I(G)\) for its independent sets and define its fractional chromatic number by

\[ c=\chi_f(G) =\min\left\{\sum_{I\in\mathcal I(G)}w_I: w_I\ge0,\quad \sum_{I\ni v}w_I\ge1\text{ for every }v\in U \right\}. \]

This minimum is attained: singleton independent sets give a feasible solution of value \(|U|\), and restricting the objective to at most \(|U|\) gives a nonempty compact feasible region. Moreover, \(c\ge2\): for the endpoints of any edge, their coverage requirements sum to at least \(2\), while each independent set contains at most one endpoint.

Let \(T(G)\) be the maximum fractional packing of vertex covers with unit capacity at each vertex:

\[ T(G)= \max\left\{\sum_{C}z_C: z_C\ge0,\quad \sum_{C\ni v}z_C\le1\text{ for every }v\in U \right\}, \]

where \(C\) ranges over all vertex covers of \(G\).

Claim. \(T(G)=c/(c-1)\).

Lower bound. Take an optimal fractional coloring \(w\). The complement \(U\setminus I\) of every independent set is a vertex cover. Assign it weight

\[ z_{U\setminus I}=\frac{w_I}{c-1}. \]

For each vertex \(v\), its packing load is

\[ \sum_{C\ni v}z_C =\frac{c-\sum_{I\ni v}w_I}{c-1} \le1. \]

This packing has total weight \(c/(c-1)\).

Upper bound. Consider any feasible vertex-cover packing of total weight \(\lambda\). If \(\lambda\le1\), then \(\lambda\le c/(c-1)\).

If \(\lambda>1\), assign each independent set \(U\setminus C\) weight

\[ w_{U\setminus C}=\frac{z_C}{\lambda-1}. \]

Its coverage of a vertex \(v\) is

\[ \sum_{I\ni v}w_I =\frac{\lambda-\sum_{C\ni v}z_C}{\lambda-1} \ge1. \]

Hence this is a fractional coloring of total weight \(\lambda/(\lambda-1)\), so

\[ c\le\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}. \]

Since \(c>1\) and \(\lambda>1\), rearranging gives \(\lambda\le c/(c-1)\). This proves the claim.

3. Polynomial-time hardness reduction

We use the known theorem that, for every fixed rational \(r>2\), deciding whether \(\chi_f(G)\le r\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. In particular, this holds for \(r=3\). The theorem is explicitly stated in the proof of Observation 3 of [Hochstättler, Schröder, and Steiner, *On the Complexity of Digraph Colourings and Vertex Arboricity*](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/6020/pdf).

We may restrict the source problem to graphs with at least one edge: edgeless graphs are yes-instances and can be mapped to a fixed graph consisting of one edge.

Given such a graph \(G=(U,E)\), let \(n=|U|\), and let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\ge n\). Construct the society as follows:

  • The required skill set is \(S=E\).
  • Each vertex \(v\in U\) gives a type with bundle \(A_v=\{e\in E:v\in e\}\) and mass \(1/N\).
  • If \(N>n\), add one type with empty bundle and mass \((N-n)/N\).
  • Set \(\rho=3/(2N)\).

The masses are positive and sum to \(1\). They and the threshold are dyadic rationals with \(O(\log n)\)-bit representations.

A subset of vertex types covers \(S\) precisely when the corresponding vertices form a vertex cover. The additional empty-bundle type belongs to no inclusion-minimal cover, since deleting it preserves coverage.

By Section 1 and scaling all vertex capacities from \(1\) to \(1/N\),

\[ V=\frac{T(G)}{N} =\frac{1}{N}\frac{\chi_f(G)}{\chi_f(G)-1}. \]

Writing \(c=\chi_f(G)\ge2\), we obtain

\[ V\ge\rho \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{c}{c-1}\ge\frac32 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2c\ge3(c-1) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c\le3. \]

Thus the construction preserves yes- and no-instances.

The construction lists at most \(n+1\) types and \(|E|\) skills. Its bundles, masses, and threshold have polynomial total encoding length. It never enumerates minimal covers.

If types must have distinct bundles. Merge types with identical bundles and add their masses. This preserves \(V\): a minimal cover uses at most one type from each identical-bundle group, so merging preserves minimal covers and combines their weights. Conversely, split each coalition weight independently among the original representatives, in proportion to their masses. A representative receives its mass proportion of the merged group’s load, so its capacity is respected. All merged masses remain positive dyadic rationals, and support size cannot increase.

Therefore the stated decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

4. Polynomial-size certificates

We also prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), despite the implicit coalition family.

Let \(M\) be the type–coalition incidence matrix, with one column for each \(Q\in\mathcal Q\). Introducing slack variables gives

\[ Mx+s=\mu,\qquad x,s\ge0. \]

This is a finite linear program. It is feasible by taking \(x=0\). It is bounded: every coalition is nonempty because \(S\ne\varnothing\), and summing the capacity constraints gives

\[ \sum_{Q\in\mathcal Q}|Q|x_Q\le1, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \sum_{Q\in\mathcal Q}x_Q\le1. \]

The slack variables are bounded as well. Since the slack columns form an identity matrix, the equality matrix has rank \(\tau\). Standard linear programming therefore supplies an optimal basic feasible solution with at most \(\tau\) positive coalition variables.

Its weights have polynomial bit length. To see this explicitly, write \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\) and put \(D=\prod_t b_t\). The bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). A basis matrix \(B\) is a nonsingular \(\tau\times\tau\) matrix with entries in \(\{0,1\}\). For its basic variables, Cramer’s rule gives

\[ x_j=\frac{\det(B_j)}{D\det(B)}, \]

where \(B_j\) replaces one column of \(B\) by \(D\mu\). Because \(0<\mu_t\le1\), Hadamard’s inequality gives

\[ |\det(B)|\le\tau^{\tau/2}, \qquad |\det(B_j)|\le D\tau^{\tau/2}. \]

Each weight consequently requires \(O(L+\tau\log(\tau+1))\) bits.

A certificate lists at most \(\tau\) coalitions and these rational weights. A verifier checks that:

  • each listed coalition covers \(S\);
  • deleting any one of its types destroys coverage, establishing inclusion-minimality;
  • all weights are nonnegative and all type capacities hold;
  • their sum is at least \(\rho\).

These checks use the explicitly supplied bundles and polynomial-time rational arithmetic. Every yes-instance has such a certificate.

Hence the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.
