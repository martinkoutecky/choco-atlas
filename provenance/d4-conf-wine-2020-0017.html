Proposition 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite supplier type set \(T\), rational type fractions \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), supplier-to-customer ratio \(\rho>0\), rational scores \(v_t>0\) and outside-option scores \(q_t\geq0\), a fixed cap \(K\), and accuracy \(\epsilon>0\), customer mass is \(1\) and supplier mass of type \(t\) is \(\rho\mu_t\). A menu pattern is \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}^{|T|}\) with \(\sum_t a_t\leq K\); choose shares \(x_a\geq0\) summing to \(1\). Define \(p_t(a)=a_tv_t/(1+\sum_s a_sv_s)\), \(y_t=\sum_a x_ap_t(a)\), and \(G_q(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}[h_q(X)]\) for \(X\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\), where \(h_q(0)=0\) and \(h_q(r)=r/(r+q)\) for \(r\geq1\). Find an \(x\) whose value is within additive \(\epsilon\) of the maximum of \(\rho\sum_t\mu_tG_{q_t}(y_t/(\rho\mu_t))\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) supplier types with positive binary rational fractions \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Customer mass is \(1\), and supplier mass of type \(t\) is \(\rho\mu_t\).
Input scores satisfy \(v_t>0\), \(q_t\geq0\), with \(\rho>0\) and \(0<\epsilon<1\), all binary rationals. Fix a positive integer cap \(K\) independently of the input. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including accuracy; there is no finite customer-count parameter \(m\).
Menus are patterns \(a\in A_K:=\{a\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{\tau}:\sum_ta_t\leq K\}\); entries count distinct suppliers of each type, and the empty menu is allowed. No aggregate exposure constraint is imposed.
A feasible allocation has real shares \(x_a\geq0\), \(\sum_{a\in A_K}x_a=1\). Customer outside-option score is \(1\); define \(p_t(a)=a_tv_t/(1+\sum_sa_sv_s)\) and \(y_t=\sum_ax_ap_t(a)\).
Define \(h_q(0)=0\), \(h_q(r)=r/(r+q)\) for integers \(r\geq1\), and \(G_q(\lambda)=\mathbb E[h_q(X)]\) for \(X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\).
Set \(F(x)=\rho\sum_t\mu_tG_{q_t}(y_t/(\rho\mu_t))\). The computational problem is to find rational feasible shares with \(F(x)\geq\max_zF(z)-\epsilon\).

For each fixed \(K\), is this additive-accuracy optimization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

The finite-agent sequential assortment problem with unrestricted menus is strongly NP-hard, even with zero supplier outside-option scores, and admits a polynomial-time constant-factor approximation ([source](https://web.stanford.edu/~iashlagi/papers/assortment.pdf)).
No result settling this precise fixed-cap, Poisson, mass-allocation problem was located in the literature search.

Result

For every fixed positive integer \(K\), the stated additive-accuracy optimization problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

The algorithm returns exactly feasible rational shares. Its dependence on accuracy is polynomial in \(\log(1/\epsilon)\). The proof requires both concavity and a numerical evaluation procedure whose running time remains polynomial when Poisson means are exponentially large.

1. Concavity of the Poisson response

Fix \(q\geq0\). Define the forward differences

\(d_q(r)=h_q(r+1)-h_q(r)\), for \(r\geq0\).

From the definition of \(h_q\),

\(d_q(0)=1/(1+q)\),

and, for \(r\geq1\),

\(d_q(r)=q/((r+q)(r+1+q))\).

These differences are nonnegative and nonincreasing. Indeed,

\(d_q(1)/d_q(0)=q/(q+2)\leq1\),

and the denominator in the formula for \(d_q(r)\) increases with \(r\). This also covers \(q=0\): then \(d_0(0)=1\) and \(d_0(r)=0\) for \(r\geq1\).

Because \(0\leq h_q(r)\leq1\), the Poisson series and its differentiated series converge uniformly on bounded intervals. Differentiating gives

\(G_q'(\lambda)=\mathbb E[d_q(X)]\),

\(G_q''(\lambda)=\mathbb E[d_q(X+1)-d_q(X)]\),

where \(X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\). At zero, these identities hold by the corresponding right derivatives.

Consequently,

\(0\leq G_q'(\lambda)\leq1\), and \(G_q''(\lambda)\leq0\).

Thus \(G_q\) is increasing and concave on \([0,\infty)\). Since \(G_q(0)=0\),

\(0\leq G_q(\lambda)\leq\lambda\),

and

\(\lvert G_q(\lambda)-G_q(\lambda')\rvert\leq\lvert\lambda-\lambda'\rvert\).

Write \(c_t=\rho\mu_t>0\). The function \(y\mapsto c_tG_{q_t}(y/c_t)\) is therefore concave and has Lipschitz constant at most one. It follows that \(F\) is concave in the allocation shares.

Moreover, for every feasible allocation,

\(0\leq F(x)\leq\sum_t y_t\leq1\),

because every menu satisfies

\(\sum_t p_t(a)=\dfrac{\sum_ta_tv_t}{1+\sum_ta_tv_t}\leq1\).

These bounds are independent of the sizes of \(\rho\), \(q_t\), and \(v_t\).

2. Polynomially many variables for fixed \(K\)

Stars and bars gives

\(N:=\lvert A_K\rvert=\binom{\tau+K}{K}\).

For fixed \(K\), this is polynomial in \(\tau\). All menus can be enumerated, and every \(p_t(a)\) can be computed exactly as a rational of polynomial encoding length.

Enumerate the nonempty menus as \(a^1,\ldots,a^d\), where \(d=N-1\geq1\). Eliminate the empty-menu share by writing

\(x_{a^i}=z_i\), and \(x_{\varnothing}=1-\sum_{i=1}^d z_i\).

The feasible domain becomes the full-dimensional simplex

\(D=\{z\in\mathbb R^d:z_i\geq0,\ \sum_i z_i\leq1\}\).

Let \(f(z)=F(x(z))\). Since the empty menu contributes zero, the demand vector is

\(y_t(z)=\sum_{i=1}^d z_i p_t(a^i)\).

For \(z,z'\in D\),

\(\lvert f(z)-f(z')\rvert\leq\sum_t\lvert y_t(z)-y_t(z')\rvert \leq\sum_i\lvert z_i-z_i'\rvert\sum_t p_t(a^i) \leq\lVert z-z'\rVert_1\).

Thus \(f\) is concave, takes values in \([0,1]\), and has a uniform Lipschitz bound.

It remains to establish polynomial-time numerical evaluation and optimization in the binary model.

3. Polynomial-time evaluation of \(G_q\)

Lemma. Given binary rational \(q,\lambda\geq0\) and \(0<\delta\leq1/2\), a rational approximation to \(G_q(\lambda)\) with absolute error at most \(\delta\) can be computed in time polynomial in their encoding lengths. The number of arithmetic operations is polynomial in \(\log(1/\delta)\), rather than in \(\lambda\) or \(q\).

Integral representation

The case \(\lambda=0\) is immediate. For \(\lambda>0\), expanding the Poisson expectation gives

\(G_q(\lambda) =\lambda e^{-\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\lambda^k}{k!(k+1+q)} =\lambda\int_0^1 s^q e^{-\lambda(1-s)}\,ds\).

The interchange of sum and integral is justified by nonnegativity. This formula includes \(q=0\).

Set

\(\alpha=q+1+\lambda,\quad a=(q+1)/\alpha,\quad b=\lambda/\alpha\).

Then \(\alpha\geq1\), \(a+b=1\), and \(0\leq a,b\leq1\). Substituting \(s=e^{-u}\), followed by \(z=\alpha u\), yields

\(G_q(\lambda)=b\int_0^\infty g(z)\,dz\),

where

\(g(z)=\exp\bigl(-az-\lambda(1-e^{-z/\alpha})\bigr)\).

A uniform tail bound

Let

\(E(z)=az+\lambda(1-e^{-z/\alpha})\).

We claim that \(E(z)\geq\sqrt z\) for every \(z\geq4\).

There are three cases.

  • If \(z/\alpha\leq1\), use \(1-e^{-u}\geq u/2\) for \(0\leq u\leq1\). Then

\(E(z)\geq z/2\geq\sqrt z\).

  • If \(z/\alpha>1\) and \(q+1\geq\lambda\), then \(a\geq1/2\), so

\(E(z)\geq z/2\geq\sqrt z\).

  • If \(z/\alpha>1\) and \(\lambda>q+1\), then \(\alpha<2\lambda\), \(q+1\geq1\), and \(1-e^{-1}>1/2\). Hence

\(E(z)\geq z/(2\lambda)+\lambda/2\geq\sqrt z\),
where the last inequality is the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality.

Therefore, for \(B\geq4\),

\(b\int_B^\infty g(z)\,dz \leq\int_B^\infty e^{-\sqrt z}\,dz =2(\sqrt B+1)e^{-\sqrt B}\).

Choose

\(n=\lceil\log_2(1/\delta)\rceil,\qquad B=16(n+1)^2\).

Here \(n\geq1\). Since \(e>2\) and \(8n+10\leq2^{3n+3}\),

\(2(\sqrt B+1)e^{-\sqrt B}\leq2^{-n-1}\leq\delta/2\).

Thus the integral can be truncated at an integer \(B\) polynomial in \(\log(1/\delta)\), uniformly in \(q\) and \(\lambda\).

Polynomial-degree quadrature

Divide \([0,B]\) into unit intervals, with centers \(z_j=j+1/2\).

The function \(g\) is entire. On the complex disk \(\lvert\zeta-z_j\rvert\leq1\), one has \(\operatorname{Re}\zeta\geq-1\), and therefore

\(\log\lvert g(\zeta)\rvert \leq a+\lambda(e^{1/\alpha}-1) \leq a+(e-1)b<3\).

The second inequality follows from \(e^u-1\leq(e-1)u\) for \(0\leq u\leq1\).

Write the Taylor expansion

\(g(z_j+w)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty c_{j,k}w^k\).

Cauchy’s coefficient bound gives \(\lvert c_{j,k}\rvert\leq e^3<21\). On \(\lvert w\rvert\leq1/2\), truncating after degree \(D\) has error at most \(21\,2^{-D}\). Choose

\(D=\lceil\log_2(84B/\delta)\rceil\).

The total integration error from Taylor truncation is then at most \(\delta/4\).

We next show that the coefficients can be approximated efficiently, with certified error.

Put \(H_j=e^{-z_j/\alpha}\). The exponent expansion is

\(g(z_j+w)=c_{j,0}\exp\bigl(\sum_{k=1}^\infty \beta_{j,k}w^k\bigr)\),

where

\(c_{j,0}=e^{-az_j-\lambda(1-H_j)}\),

\(\beta_{j,1}=-a-bH_j\),

and, for \(k\geq2\),

\(\beta_{j,k}=\lambda H_j(-1/\alpha)^k/k!\).

These satisfy

\(0<c_{j,0}\leq1,\quad \lvert\beta_{j,1}\rvert\leq1,\quad \lvert\beta_{j,k}\rvert\leq1/k!\) for \(k\geq2\).

Let

\(\nu=\delta/(400B(D+1))\).

Compute dyadic approximations to \(c_{j,0}\) and each \(\beta_{j,k}\), \(1\leq k\leq D\), with errors at most \(\nu\). From these approximations, compute Taylor coefficients exactly by the rational recurrence

\(\widehat c_{j,k} =\dfrac1k\sum_{\ell=1}^k \ell\,\widehat\beta_{j,\ell}\widehat c_{j,k-\ell}\).

To bound their errors, define

\(P(w)=\sum_{k=1}^D\beta_{j,k}w^k,\qquad \widehat P(w)=\sum_{k=1}^D\widehat\beta_{j,k}w^k\).

On \(\lvert w\rvert\leq1\),

\(\lvert P(w)\rvert<2,\quad \lvert\widehat P(w)\rvert\leq3,\quad \lvert P(w)-\widehat P(w)\rvert\leq D\nu\).

It follows, using the integral formula for the difference of two exponentials, that

\(\lvert c_{j,0}e^{P(w)} -\widehat c_{j,0}e^{\widehat P(w)}\rvert \leq50(D+1)\nu\).

Cauchy’s bound therefore gives this same bound for the difference of each corresponding coefficient. On \(\lvert w\rvert\leq1/2\), summing the coefficient errors gives at most \(100(D+1)\nu\). Across all intervals, the resulting integration error is at most \(\delta/4\).

Each approximate polynomial integrates exactly, since

\(\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}w^k\,dw=0\) for odd \(k\),

and

\(\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}w^k\,dw=1/(2^k(k+1))\) for even \(k\).

Together with the tail bound, the total error is at most \(\delta\).

Bit complexity of the coefficient computations

All exponentials needed above have arguments in \([0,B]\). In particular,

\(0\leq az_j+\lambda(1-H_j)\leq z_j\leq B\).

To control multiplication by a possibly large \(\lambda\), compute \(H_j\) to error at most

\(\nu/(8(1+\lambda))\).

The required precision has encoding length polynomial in the input length and \(\log(1/\delta)\). Form the rational approximation to the exponent of \(c_{j,0}\), clip it to \([0,z_j]\), and evaluate its exponential to sufficiently small error. Clipping cannot increase its error, and \(e^{-u}\) is one-Lipschitz on \([0,\infty)\).

For completeness, \(e^{-r}\), with rational \(0\leq r\leq B\), can be evaluated by its Taylor series using exact rational arithmetic. The degree-\(M\) remainder is at most

\(B^{M+1}/(M+1)!\).

Using \(m!\geq(m/e)^m\), choosing \(M+1\geq6B\) and \(M+1\geq\log_2(1/\theta)\) makes this error at most \(\theta\). Thus the required degree and all intermediate encoding lengths are polynomial.

Finally, choose a common dyadic denominator \(2^P\) for the coefficient inputs. The recurrence produces coefficients whose denominators divide

\(2^{(k+1)P}k!\).

Their numerators also have polynomial encoding length, by the preceding coefficient bounds. Hence the exact rational recurrence and integration have polynomial bit complexity. This proves the lemma.

4. A polynomial-time value oracle for \(f\)

Given rational \(z\in D\), compute all \(y_t(z)\), \(c_t\), and

\(\lambda_t=y_t(z)/c_t\)

exactly. Their encoding lengths are polynomial in the original input length, the query length, and the number of menus.

For a requested absolute error \(\zeta>0\), apply the lemma to every \(G_{q_t}(\lambda_t)\) with accuracy

\(\sigma=\min\{1/2,\zeta/\rho\}\).

Since \(\sum_t c_t=\rho\), the rational weighted sum \(\widetilde f(z)\) satisfies

\(\lvert\widetilde f(z)-f(z)\rvert \leq\sum_t c_t\sigma =\rho\sigma\leq\zeta\).

Its computation is polynomial in the input and query encoding lengths, including the requested precision.

5. Applying weak convex optimization

We use the following standard theorem: for a compact convex body \(C\), supplied with rational bounds

\(B(u_0,r)\subseteq C\subseteq B(0,R)\),

a polynomial-time weak membership oracle yields polynomial-time weak linear optimization. Specifically, for rational \(\delta>0\), it returns a rational point \(\widehat u\) within distance \(\delta\) of \(C\), whose linear objective is within \(\delta\) of the maximum over

\(C_{-\delta}=\{u:B(u,\delta)\subseteq C\}\),

or reports that this inner set is empty. The running time is polynomial in dimension and binary encoding lengths. This is the Yudin–Nemirovskii reduction, as stated in [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Corollary 4.3.12](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1988.pdf).

Apply it to the truncated hypograph

\(C=\{(z,s):z\in D,\ -1\leq s\leq f(z)\}\).

Concavity and continuity of \(f\) imply that \(C\) is compact and convex.

Its geometric bounds are explicit. Let

\(u_0=((1/(2d),\ldots,1/(2d)),-1/2),\qquad r=1/(4d)\).

Every point in \(B(u_0,r)\) has nonnegative \(z_i\), and

\(\sum_i z_i\leq1/2+\sqrt d\,r\leq3/4\).

Its \(s\)-coordinate lies in \([-3/4,-1/4]\), which is contained in \([-1,f(z)]\), because \(f(z)\geq0\). Thus \(B(u_0,r)\subseteq C\).

Also, \(\lVert z\rVert_2\leq1\) and \(\lvert s\rvert\leq1\) on \(C\), so \(C\subseteq B(0,2)\).

A weak membership oracle follows directly from the value oracle. At a rational query \((z,s)\) and tolerance \(\eta>0\):

  • If \(z\notin D\) or \(s<-1\), report that the point is outside \(C_{-\eta}\).
  • Otherwise compute \(\widetilde f(z)\) to error \(\eta/4\). Accept if \(s\leq\widetilde f(z)+\eta/4\); reject otherwise.

An accepted point is within vertical distance \(\eta/2\) of \(C\). A rejected point satisfies \(s>f(z)\), so it is outside \(C\), and hence outside \(C_{-\eta}\). This verifies the oracle requirements.

All hypotheses of the weak optimization theorem are therefore satisfied.

6. Exact feasibility and the additive guarantee

Let

\(f^*=\max_{z\in D}f(z)\), and \(\delta=\epsilon/(16(d+1))\).

Then \(\delta<r\). Apply weak optimization to maximize the \(s\)-coordinate, obtaining rational \((\widehat z,\widehat s)\).

First compare the inner optimum with \(f^*\). Let \(u^*=(z^*,f^*)\in C\) be an optimizer, and set

\(\theta=\delta/r,\qquad u_\theta=(1-\theta)u^*+\theta u_0\).

Convexity implies

\(B(u_\theta,\theta r)\subseteq C\):

each point in that ball is a convex combination of \(u^*\) and a point in \(B(u_0,r)\). Thus \(u_\theta\in C_{-\delta}\). Its \(s\)-coordinate is at least

\(f^*-\theta(f^*+1/2)\geq f^*-3\delta/(2r)\).

The weak optimization guarantee gives

\(\widehat s\geq f^*-3\delta/(2r)-\delta\).

Now repair \(\widehat z\) rationally:

\(w_i=\max\{\widehat z_i,0\},\qquad S=\sum_i w_i,\qquad \overline z_i=w_i/\max\{1,S\}\).

Then \(\overline z\in D\) exactly.

Since \((\widehat z,\widehat s)\) is within distance \(\delta\) of \(C\), there exists \((z',s')\in C\) with

\(\lVert\widehat z-z'\rVert_2\leq\delta,\qquad s'\geq\widehat s-\delta\).

Clipping negative coordinates cannot increase their distance from the nonnegative vector \(z'\), so

\(\lVert w-z'\rVert_1\leq\sqrt d\,\delta\).

If \(S>1\), normalization changes \(w\) by \(\ell_1\)-distance \(S-1\), and

\(S-1\leq\lVert w-z'\rVert_1\),

because \(\sum_i z_i'\leq1\). Consequently,

\(\lVert\overline z-z'\rVert_1\leq2\sqrt d\,\delta\leq2d\delta\).

The Lipschitz bound from Section 2 now yields

\(f(\overline z)\geq f(z')-2d\delta \geq\widehat s-(2d+1)\delta\).

Combining the inequalities and substituting \(r=1/(4d)\),

\(f(\overline z)\geq f^*-(8d+2)\delta>f^*-\epsilon\).

Return

\(x_{a^i}=\overline z_i,\qquad x_{\varnothing}=1-\sum_i\overline z_i\).

These shares are rational, nonnegative, and sum to one exactly. Their encoding lengths remain polynomial.

7. Complexity conclusion

For fixed \(K\), the number of menus is polynomial in \(\tau\). The geometric parameters, requested optimization tolerance, oracle queries, numerical evaluation precision, and output shares all have polynomial encoding length. The weak optimization theorem and the evaluation lemma therefore give a deterministic algorithm running in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

The cited [finite-agent hardness result](https://web.stanford.edu/~iashlagi/papers/assortment.pdf) concerns a different optimization problem. Here the allowed real allocation shares form a convex domain, the Poisson response is concave, and fixed \(K\) permits polynomial enumeration of every menu. No finite customer-count parameter is needed.
