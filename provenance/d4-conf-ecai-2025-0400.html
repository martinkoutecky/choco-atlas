The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given worker types \(T_W\) and firm types \(T_F\), rational masses \(\mu_i\) and \(\nu_j\) on a common population scale, a strict preference order for each type over opposite-side types and \(\bot\), and a fixed order over \(T_W \cup T_F\), compute the aggregate matching produced by cohort serial dictatorship. At each type's turn, its remaining mass takes the highest-ranked available opposite-side mass in preference order, pairing up to the smaller residual mass; any remainder assigned to \(\bot\) leaves the process. Output the flows \(x_{ij}\) and unmatched masses, with running time polynomial in \(|T_W|+|T_F|\) and the input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(T_W\) and \(T_F\) be disjoint, nonempty sets of worker and firm types, with \(n=|T_W|\), \(m=|T_F|\), and \(\tau=n+m\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Worker masses \(\mu_i\) and firm masses \(\nu_j\) use a common population scale; neither normalization nor equality of the two total masses is required.
Each type has a strict total order over all opposite-side types and \(\bot\), the unmatched option. The input also specifies a total order over \(T_W\cup T_F\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Cohort serial dictatorship starts with all mass available and processes each type once in the specified order. At its turn, a type repeatedly selects its highest-ranked available opposite-side type above \(\bot\), matching the smaller of their residual masses and removing that mass from both sides.
The selected partner's preferences impose no acceptance condition. If no available partner ranks above \(\bot\), all remaining mass of the acting type becomes permanently unmatched and unavailable; a type with no residual mass is skipped.
The output consists of exact rational flows \(x_{ij}\) and unmatched masses \(u_i,v_j\), satisfying \(\sum_j x_{ij}+u_i=\mu_i\) and \(\sum_i x_{ij}+v_j=\nu_j\).

Is exact computation of this aggregate cohort serial dictatorship outcome possible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite one-to-one two-sided matching with a fixed agent order, tensor serial dictatorship exactly computes serial dictatorship in \(O(\max\{n,m\}^{4})\) arithmetic operations.
That discrete result does not establish the complexity of exact computation for rational-mass cohorts.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Masses are positive binary rationals on a common scale, with no normalization or equality of side totals, reflecting the discrete model's potentially unequal population sizes.
  • Both sides have nonempty support, following the discrete model's positive worker and firm counts.
  • A selected partner has no acceptance veto, as in the discrete serial dictatorship definition.
  • Mass assigned to the unmatched option becomes permanently unavailable to later types, carrying over the discrete exclusion of agents already assigned to that option.
  • The output is exact rational data; L denotes the bit length of the entire input.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Exact computation is polynomial-time solvable. The cohort procedure can be simulated directly using integers of polynomial bit length. No normalization, equality of total masses, or acceptance condition on the selected partner is needed.

1. Represent all masses on a common integer scale

Write the positive input masses as \(p_t/d_t\), where \(t\) ranges over the \(\tau\) supported types and \(p_t,d_t\) are positive integers encoded in binary. Define
\(D=\prod_t d_t\)
and
\(a_t=p_t(D/d_t)\).
Thus the mass of type \(t\) is exactly \(a_t/D\).

The bit length of \(D\) is at most one plus the sum of the input denominators’ bit lengths, hence is \(O(L)\). Each \(a_t\) also has bit length \(O(L)\), because
\(a_t\leq p_tD\)
and both factors have bit length \(O(L)\).

We will maintain integer residuals \(r_t\), initially \(r_t=a_t\). A positive residual means that the type remains available. A zero residual means that it is unavailable, whether its mass was matched or declared permanently unmatched.

Initialize integer flow entries \(X_{ij}=0\) and integer unmatched quantities \(U_i=V_j=0\).

2. Integer simulation

Process the supported types in the specified order. For an acting type \(t\):

  • If \(r_t=0\), skip it.
  • Otherwise, scan its preference list from the highest-ranked alternative toward \(\bot\).
  • Skip every opposite-side type \(s\) with \(r_s=0\).
  • Upon encountering a type \(s\) with \(r_s>0\), set \(q=\min\{r_t,r_s\}\). Add \(q\) to the corresponding worker–firm flow entry, and replace the residuals by \(r_t-q\) and \(r_s-q\).
  • If \(r_t=0\), finish the turn. Otherwise continue scanning.
  • If the scan reaches \(\bot\) while \(r_t>0\), record \(r_t\) as the acting type’s unmatched quantity and set \(r_t=0\).

Finally, output
\(x_{ij}=X_{ij}/D\),
\(u_i=U_i/D\), and
\(v_j=V_j/D\).

These fractions are exact; reducing them to lowest terms is unnecessary.

3. Correctness

We first justify why a single forward scan implements the repeated selection rule.

Residuals never increase. Consequently, a type skipped because its residual is zero can never become available later. Moreover, if the acting type still has positive residual after matching with \(s\), then \(s\) must have become exhausted: from
\(q=\min\{r_t,r_s\}\)
and \(r_t-q>0\), it follows that \(q=r_s\), so \(r_s-q=0\).

Therefore, whenever the acting type continues scanning, every previously examined opposite-side type is unavailable. The next positive-residual type encountered before \(\bot\) is exactly its highest-ranked available acceptable partner. If the scan reaches \(\bot\), no acceptable partner is available.

Now compare the integer simulation with the stated cohort procedure. Initially, the cohort residual mass of every type is \(r_t/D\). Suppose this correspondence holds immediately before a matching step. The cohort procedure transfers
\(\min\{r_t/D,r_s/D\}=\min\{r_t,r_s\}/D=q/D\).
The integer updates therefore preserve the correspondence for both residual masses and accumulated flows. Declaring residual mass permanently unmatched likewise transfers exactly \(r_t/D\) into the corresponding unmatched quantity and makes the type unavailable.

By induction over the ordered turns and their matching steps, the simulation produces precisely the specified outcome. The selected partner’s preferences are never consulted, as required.

For completeness, mass conservation follows from the invariants
\(a_i=r_i+\sum_jX_{ij}+U_i\)
for workers and
\(a_j=r_j+\sum_iX_{ij}+V_j\)
for firms. Every matching step and every unmatched declaration preserves these equalities. Each type finishes its own turn with zero residual, and residuals subsequently remain zero. Hence all residuals are zero at termination. Dividing the invariants by \(D\) gives the required output equalities.

4. Number of steps

Each worker scans at most \(m+1\) preference entries, including \(\bot\), and each firm scans at most \(n+1\). Thus the total number of examined entries is at most
\(n(m+1)+m(n+1)=2nm+\tau\).

There is also a stronger bound on positive matching steps. Every such step exhausts at least one currently available type, and no exhausted type becomes available again. If there are \(K>0\) matching steps, then immediately before the last one there are at most \(\tau-(K-1)\) available types. A matching step requires at least two available types, so
\(2\leq\tau-(K-1)\),
which implies
\(K\leq\tau-1\).
Unmatched declarations can only decrease availability further.

5. Bit complexity

All maintained residuals and unmatched quantities are bounded by their respective initial scaled masses. Every flow entry is also bounded by its worker’s initial scaled mass. Thus every maintained integer has \(O(L)\) bits.

Using ordinary binary arithmetic:

  • Constructing \(D\) and all \(a_t\) takes \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations by schoolbook multiplication and long division.
  • Scanning preferences and performing comparisons, additions, and subtractions takes \(O((nm+\tau)L)\) bit operations.
  • Writing all \(nm+\tau\) output fractions takes \(O((nm+\tau)L)\) bits and corresponding polynomial time.

Including input reading, a valid overall bound is
\(O\bigl(L+\tau L^2+(nm+\tau)L\bigr)\).
Since \(n\leq\tau\), this is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The finite one-to-one tensor result is therefore unnecessary: the rational cohort problem admits its own direct polynomial-time algorithm, with an explicit bound on exact arithmetic sizes.
