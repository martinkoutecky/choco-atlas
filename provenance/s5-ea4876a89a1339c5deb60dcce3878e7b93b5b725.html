The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 9 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed \(\sigma\), given rational type masses \(\mu_t\) over machine processing vectors \(p_t\) and rational supplies \(\rho_q\) of \(\sigma\) task classes per machine, is there an algorithm polynomial in \(\tau\), the input bit length \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\) that outputs a finite, efficiently implementable mechanism? Each machine must receive an integral bundle; the mechanism must be feasible for every aggregate report distribution, \(\epsilon\)-BIC, and ex-post individually rational, and have expected makespan at most \(2\mathrm{OPT}_\infty+\epsilon\), where \(\mathrm{OPT}_\infty\) is the best value among mechanisms satisfying those conditions.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless population of total machine mass one, given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m:=\sigma\geq1\) task classes, with fixed \(\sigma\); type \(t\) has processing vector \(p_t\in([0,1]\cap\mathbb Q)^\sigma\). Supplies \(\rho_q\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) are task counts per unit machine mass. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length and \(\epsilon\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\).
Each machine reports a support type and receives an integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^\sigma\) and a monetary transfer \(a\in\mathbb R\) from the designer; its utility is \(a-p_t\cdot b\). Transfers have no budget constraint.
An anonymous direct mechanism, at aggregate report distribution \(r\in\Delta_\tau\) and public random outcome \(z\), specifies for each report \(s\) a distribution \(K_s(r,z)\) over bundle-transfer pairs, implemented by splitting the reporting mass into the prescribed proportions. Define these distributions even when \(r_s=0\).
Feasibility requires \(\sum_s r_s\mathbb E_{K_s(r,z)}[b_q]=\rho_q\) for every \(r,q,z\): every task is allocated in every realization. Ex-post IR requires \(a-p_s\cdot b\geq0\) for every truthful recipient in every realization.
A unilateral deviation leaves the aggregate distribution unchanged. Write \(U_t(s)=\mathbb E_z\mathbb E_{K_s(\mu,z)}[a-p_t\cdot b]\); \(\epsilon\)-BIC means \(U_t(t)\geq U_t(s)-\epsilon\) for all support types \(t,s\), with finite expected utilities.
The truthful makespan in realization \(z\) is \(M(z)=\max_t\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{(b,a)\sim K_t(\mu,z)}p_t\cdot b\), and performance is \(\mathbb E_z[M(z)]\). Let \(\mathrm{OPT}_\infty\) be the infimum of this performance over all measurable mechanisms satisfying feasibility, \(\epsilon\)-BIC, and ex-post IR, without computational restrictions.
A finite, efficiently implementable mechanism is a finite program that, on any rational \(r\), samples its public random outcome and outputs finite rational tables for the distributions \(K_s(r,z)\); its expected execution time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\) and the bit length of \(r\). Its rules must also be defined and feasible for real report distributions.
For fixed \(\sigma\), can such a mechanism with expected makespan at most \(2\mathrm{OPT}_\infty+\epsilon\) be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\), or is achieving this guarantee NP-hard? Randomized construction may succeed with probability at least \(2/3\), while feasibility and ex-post IR must always hold.
For finitely many independently distributed machines, a polynomial-time construction gives an efficiently implementable, feasible, ex-post IR, \(\epsilon\)-BIC mechanism with expected makespan at most \(2\mathrm{OPT}+\epsilon\), with high probability; its benchmark is the optimal exactly BIC, IR mechanism. [Daskalakis–Weinberg](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.5940).
No result settling this exact atomless, integral-bundle mechanism problem was identified in the literature search.

Polynomial-time construction

1. Result

The requested guarantee is achievable in polynomial time. In fact, there is a deterministic construction of an efficiently implementable, feasible, ex-post IR, \(\epsilon\)-BIC mechanism satisfying

\(\mathbb E[M]\leq 2\mathrm{OPT}_\infty.\)

The construction works even when \(m\) is part of the input. It preserves integral bundles: a fractional mean bundle is represented by a finite distribution over integral bundles, implemented through the prescribed splitting of reporting mass.

2. Incentives depend only on expected bundles and payments

For any feasible mechanism, define its conditional mean bundles at the truthful aggregate by

\(x_{tq}(z):=\mathbb E_{K_t(\mu,z)}[b_q].\)

Feasibility and positivity of every support mass imply, for every outcome,

\(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tq}(z)=\rho_q,\qquad 0\leq x_{tq}(z)\leq \rho_q/\mu_t.\)

Consequently, all unconditional mean bundles

\(\bar x_t:=\mathbb E_z[x_t(z)]\)

are finite.

Let \(u_t:=U_t(t)\) be a truthful type’s expected utility. Ex-post IR gives \(u_t\geq0\). Since truthful utility and expected processing cost are finite, expected payments are finite and satisfy

\(A_t:=\mathbb E_z\mathbb E_{K_t(\mu,z)}[a]=u_t+p_t\cdot\bar x_t.\)

It follows that

\(U_t(s)=A_s-p_t\cdot\bar x_s=u_s+(p_s-p_t)\cdot\bar x_s.\)

Thus the incentive constraints are precisely

\(u_t\geq u_s+(p_s-p_t)\cdot\bar x_s-\epsilon\qquad\text{for all }t,s.\)

Conversely, suppose a mechanism’s bundle distributions have unconditional means \(\bar x_s\), and nonnegative numbers \(u_s\) satisfy these inequalities. Give every recipient reporting \(s\), with bundle \(b\), the transfer

\(a=p_s\cdot b+u_s.\)

Every truthful recipient then receives utility exactly \(u_s\), in every realization. The mechanism is ex-post IR, and its expected deviation utilities satisfy the displayed formula. Hence it is \(\epsilon\)-BIC.

This observation allows us to preserve incentives by preserving mean bundles exactly.

3. A finite rounding lemma

Lemma. Given rational vectors \(x,c\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^{m}\), one can construct, in polynomial time, a rational distribution supported on at most \(m+1\) integral bundles such that

\(\mathbb E[b]=x,\)

\(b_q=0\quad\text{whenever }x_q=0,\)

and every supported bundle satisfies

\(c\cdot b\leq c\cdot x+\max_{q:x_q>0}c_q.\)

The maximum over an empty set is defined as zero.

Proof. If \(x=0\), use the zero bundle. Otherwise, relabel the coordinates with positive \(x_q\) as \(1,\ldots,d\), ordered so that

\(c_1\geq c_2\geq\cdots\geq c_d\geq0.\)

Inactive coordinates remain zero. Write

\(n_i:=\lfloor x_i\rfloor,\qquad f_i:=x_i-n_i,\qquad F_i:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}f_\ell,\qquad F_0:=0.\)

For an auxiliary uniform variable \(V\) on \([0,1)\), define

\(v_i(V):=\lfloor F_i+V\rfloor-\lfloor F_{i-1}+V\rfloor,\qquad b_i(V):=n_i+v_i(V).\)

Because \(F_i-F_{i-1}=f_i\in[0,1)\), each \(v_i(V)\) belongs to \(\{0,1\}\). Thus every bundle is integral and nonnegative.

For any real number \(a\), writing \(a=k+\theta\), with integer \(k\) and \(\theta\in[0,1)\), shows that \(\lfloor a+V\rfloor\) equals \(k+1\) with probability \(\theta\), and \(k\) otherwise. Therefore,

\(\mathbb E[\lfloor a+V\rfloor]=a.\)

Applying this identity to consecutive prefix sums gives

\(\mathbb E[v_i(V)]=F_i-F_{i-1}=f_i,\)

so \(\mathbb E[b_i(V)]=x_i\).

For the load bound, put

\(D_i(V):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}(v_\ell(V)-f_\ell)=\lfloor F_i+V\rfloor-F_i.\)

Since \(V<1\), we have \(D_i(V)\leq1\). Set \(c_{d+1}:=0\). Summation by parts yields

\(\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_i(v_i(V)-f_i)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(c_i-c_{i+1})D_i(V)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}(c_i-c_{i+1})=c_1.\)

Adding the integral parts proves the claimed load bound.

Finally, the bundle changes only at values of \(V\) where some \(F_i+V\) is an integer. There are at most \(d\) distinct breakpoints inside \((0,1)\). Partitioning \([0,1)\) at these rational breakpoints gives at most \(d+1\) intervals. For each interval, use its rational length as a probability and evaluate the bundle at its midpoint. This produces the required finite rational table.

Sorting, computing prefix sums and breakpoints, and writing the bundles all require polynomial time and polynomial encoding length. ∎

The variable \(V\) is only a device for constructing the table. In the mechanism, the table is implemented by splitting reporting mass.

4. A polynomial-size linear program

Let

\(R:=\sum_q\rho_q,\qquad \mu_{\min}:=\min_t\mu_t,\qquad B:=2+R/\mu_{\min}.\)

In particular, \(B>1\), and every feasible conditional mean allocation satisfies

\(p_t\cdot x_t\leq\sum_q x_{tq}\leq R/\mu_t\leq R/\mu_{\min}<B.\)

List the distinct positive numbers among the processing coordinates as

\(0<d_1<\cdots<d_k\leq1,\)

and put \(d_0:=0\) and \(d_{k+1}:=B\). If all processing coordinates are zero, take \(k=0\). Always \(k\leq\tau m\).

For each interval index \(j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\), introduce nonnegative variables:

  • \(\lambda_j\), representing an outcome probability;
  • \(H_j\), representing probability times a load bound;
  • \(Y_{jtq}\), representing probability times a type’s mean bundle.

Also introduce nonnegative truthful utility variables \(u_t\). Define

\(\bar x_{tq}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k}Y_{jtq}.\)

Minimize

\(\sum_{j=0}^{k}H_j\)

subject to the following linear constraints:

  • \(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\lambda_j=1.\)
  • \(d_j\lambda_j\leq H_j\leq d_{j+1}\lambda_j\) for every \(j\).
  • \(\sum_t\mu_tY_{jtq}=\lambda_j\rho_q\) for every \(j,q\).
  • \(\sum_qp_{tq}Y_{jtq}\leq H_j\) for every \(j,t\).
  • \(Y_{jtq}=0\) whenever \(p_{tq}>d_j\).
  • \(u_t\geq u_s+\sum_q(p_{sq}-p_{tq})\bar x_{sq}-\epsilon\) for every \(t,s\).

The eligibility constraint in item 5 is essential: rounding must not introduce a task whose individual processing time exceeds the chosen load bound.

Feasibility and polynomial solvability

The linear program is feasible. Set

\(\lambda_k=1,\qquad H_k=B,\qquad Y_{ktq}=\rho_q,\)

and set all other outcome variables to zero. All task classes are eligible in interval \(k\), and

\(p_t\cdot\rho\leq R<B.\)

Let

\(C:=\max_t p_t\cdot\rho,\qquad u_t:=C-p_t\cdot\rho.\)

These utilities are nonnegative and satisfy

\(u_s+(p_s-p_t)\cdot\rho=C-p_t\cdot\rho=u_t.\)

Thus even the exact incentive inequalities hold.

The objective is nonnegative, so the feasible linear program has a finite optimum. Denote its optimal value by \(V_{\mathrm{LP}}\).

We use the standard bit-model theorem for rational linear programming: a rational linear program can be solved in polynomial time, with an exact rational optimal solution of polynomial encoding length whenever its optimum is finite and its feasible set is nonempty. Polynomial-time LP algorithms are established, for example, by [Karmarkar](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579150).

Here all hypotheses hold. There are \(O((k+1)\tau m+\tau)\) variables and \(O((k+1)\tau m+\tau^2)\) constraints. Since \(k\leq\tau m\), these counts are polynomial. Every coefficient, including \(B\), has polynomial encoding length in the input.

5. The linear program lower-bounds the unrestricted benchmark

We now prove

\(V_{\mathrm{LP}}\leq\mathrm{OPT}_\infty.\)

Take any admissible mechanism with finite expected makespan \(J\). Its conditional means \(x_t(z)\) satisfy the bounds from Section 2. Define

\(T(z):=\min\{M(z),B\}.\)

We claim that, for every outcome,

\(p_t\cdot x_t(z)\leq T(z),\)

and

\(x_{tq}(z)>0\ \Longrightarrow\ p_{tq}\leq T(z).\)

For the first claim, if \(M(z)\leq B\), integrating the almost-sure load bound gives \(p_t\cdot x_t(z)\leq M(z)\). If \(M(z)>B\), the uniform mean-allocation bound gives \(p_t\cdot x_t(z)<B\).

For the second claim, positive \(x_{tq}(z)\) implies positive probability of an integral bundle with \(b_q\geq1\). Nonnegative processing coordinates then imply \(M(z)\geq p_{tq}\). If \(M(z)\leq B\), this proves the claim directly. If \(M(z)>B\), it follows from \(p_{tq}\leq1<B=T(z)\).

Assign each outcome to the largest index \(j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\) with \(d_j\leq T(z)\). Write this index as \(j(z)\). Then

\(d_{j(z)}\leq T(z)\leq d_{j(z)+1}.\)

Moreover,

\(p_{tq}>d_{j(z)}\ \Longrightarrow\ x_{tq}(z)=0.\)

Indeed, if \(j(z)<k\), the next possible larger processing coordinate is at least \(d_{j(z)+1}>T(z)\). If \(j(z)=k\), no processing coordinate exceeds \(d_k\); when \(k=0\), every processing coordinate is zero.

Define

\(\lambda_j:=\mathbb P(j(z)=j),\)

\(H_j:=\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{\{j(z)=j\}}T(z)],\)

\(Y_{jtq}:=\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{\{j(z)=j\}}x_{tq}(z)].\)

These expectations are finite because both \(T(z)\) and the conditional means are uniformly bounded. The preceding pointwise facts imply the probability, interval, feasibility, capacity, and eligibility constraints of the linear program. Use the original mechanism’s truthful expected utilities as \(u_t\). Section 2 supplies the incentive constraints and nonnegativity.

Hence this is a feasible LP solution with objective

\(\sum_jH_j=\mathbb E[T(z)]\leq\mathbb E[M(z)]=J.\)

The benchmark class has a finite-performance member: give every report the same finite bundle distribution of mean \(\rho\), furnished by the rounding lemma, and pay \(p_s\cdot b+C-p_s\cdot\rho\). This is feasible at every aggregate, ex-post IR, and exactly BIC because its expected payment is \(C\), independently of the report.

Taking the infimum over all finite-performance admissible mechanisms therefore proves

\(V_{\mathrm{LP}}\leq\mathrm{OPT}_\infty.\)

No attainment of \(\mathrm{OPT}_\infty\) is assumed.

6. Constructing the truthful tables

Take an exact rational optimal LP solution. Discard indices with \(\lambda_j=0\). Such indices also have \(H_j=0\); feasibility and positivity of the masses force every \(Y_{jtq}=0\).

For each remaining index, put

\(T_j:=H_j/\lambda_j,\qquad x_{tq}^{\,j}:=Y_{jtq}/\lambda_j.\)

The LP constraints imply

\(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tq}^{\,j}=\rho_q,\)

\(p_t\cdot x_t^{\,j}\leq T_j,\)

and

\(x_{tq}^{\,j}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ p_{tq}\leq d_j\leq T_j.\)

For each outcome \(j\) and type \(t\), apply the rounding lemma with \(x=x_t^{\,j}\) and \(c=p_t\). Let \(D_t^j\) be the resulting bundle distribution. Its mean is exactly \(x_t^{\,j}\), and every supported bundle satisfies

\(p_t\cdot b\leq p_t\cdot x_t^{\,j}+\max_{q:x_{tq}^{\,j}>0}p_{tq}\leq2T_j.\)

This also covers \(T_j=0\): every active processing coordinate is then zero.

Sample public outcome \(j\) with probability \(\lambda_j\). At aggregate \(\mu\), the table for report \(s\) uses the bundle probabilities of \(D_s^j\) and transfer

\(a=p_s\cdot b+u_s.\)

For every public outcome, exact preservation of means gives

\(\sum_s\mu_s\mathbb E_{D_s^j}[b_q]=\rho_q.\)

Thus feasibility holds separately in every realization. Atomless splitting implements these finite distributions exactly.

Every truthful recipient’s utility is \(u_s\geq0\), so ex-post IR holds pointwise. Also,

\(\mathbb E_j\mathbb E_{D_s^j}[b_q]=\sum_jY_{jsq}=\bar x_{sq}.\)

Section 2 and the LP’s incentive inequalities therefore prove \(\epsilon\)-BIC. All expected utilities are finite because there are finitely many finite tables.

Finally, in outcome \(j\),

\(M(j)\leq2T_j.\)

Consequently,

\(\mathbb E[M]\leq2\sum_j\lambda_jT_j=2\sum_jH_j=2V_{\mathrm{LP}}\leq2\mathrm{OPT}_\infty.\)

7. All report distributions and efficient implementation

It remains to define the mechanism away from \(\mu\), including reports with zero mass.

Construct a common finite rational bundle distribution \(D^0\) of mean \(\rho\), using the rounding lemma with \(c=0\). For every real aggregate \(r\neq\mu\), every report \(s\), and every public outcome, use \(D^0\) and transfer

\(a=p_s\cdot b.\)

Then

\(\sum_s r_s\mathbb E_{D^0}[b_q]=\sum_s r_s\rho_q=\rho_q.\)

Thus feasibility holds for every real aggregate and every outcome. Every truthful recipient has zero utility, so ex-post IR holds. Tables are defined even when \(r_s=0\).

The full rule uses the constructed truthful tables when \(r=\mu\), and these common-mean tables otherwise. This is measurable because the exceptional aggregate is a singleton. The stipulated invariance of the aggregate under unilateral deviations ensures that the incentive calculation uses exactly the truthful tables.

All tables have polynomial total encoding length: there are at most \(\tau m+1\) public outcomes and at most \(m+1\) rows per type per outcome. Dividing positive LP probabilities and writing bundle coordinates in binary preserve polynomial bit length. Large supplies are never expanded into individual tasks.

On rational \(r\), the program compares \(r\) with \(\mu\) exactly and outputs the corresponding precomputed tables. Public outcomes can be sampled exactly in expected polynomial time. Specifically, take a common denominator \(D\) for the probabilities and sample a uniform integer below \(D\) by rejection from uniformly generated binary strings. Each trial succeeds with probability greater than one half, and \(\log D\) is polynomially bounded.

The construction is deterministic and polynomial in the input encoding length, \(m\), and \(\tau\); hence it meets the requested polynomial bound involving \(1/\epsilon\). Its execution has the required expected polynomial running time. Feasibility, ex-post IR, \(\epsilon\)-BIC, and the performance bound hold without a construction failure probability.
