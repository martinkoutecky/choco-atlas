The continuous mirror question, as graded

For finite customer and provider type sets \(I,J\), each round supplies rational arrival masses \(a_i^r,b_j^r\) and unknown type-pair utilities \(u_{ij},v_{ji}\in[-1,1]\). Choose a match flow \(x_{ij}^r\ge 0\) subject to the arrival-mass capacities, with zero-sum transfers per matched unit; for each matched type pair, observe independent 1-subgaussian utility samples at a specified finite rate \(\lambda x_{ij}^r\). Define continuous Subset Instability as the maximum over coalitions selecting submass from current matched cohorts and unmatched pools of the coalition’s gain from its best alternative fractional matching. Design an online policy and characterize its expected cumulative-instability regret over \(T\) rounds as a function of the number of types, arrival mass, and feedback rate; in particular, determine whether a bound of order \(\widetilde O(\tau\sqrt{nT})\) is achievable for \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) and maximum per-round mass \(n\).

1. Result: the minimax rate up to logarithmic factors

The uniform bound in the question is not achievable. Whole-round feedback creates an additional cost for previously unobserved pairs.

For fixed nonempty type sets \(I,J\), let \(V_{I,J}(n,T,\lambda)\) be the minimax expected regret over admissible utilities, oblivious arrival sequences with maximum total mass \(n\), and admissible noise laws. Define
\[ F_m=\min\left\{nT,\;mn+\sqrt{\frac{mnT}{\lambda}}\right\}, \qquad m=|I||J|. \]
Then there are universal constants \(c,C>0\) such that
\[ cF_m \;\le\; V_{I,J}(n,T,\lambda) \;\le\; C\min\left\{nT,\;mn+\sqrt{\frac{\beta mnT}{\lambda}}\right\}, \]
where
\[ \beta=\log\!\left(128(m+1)^2(T+1)^2(1+\lambda nT)\right). \]

The upper bound is attained by a polynomial-time online policy. The lower bounds already hold with independent standard Gaussian noises.

Consequently, taking the worst case over type-set splits with \(|I|+|J|=\tau\), the minimax rate is
\[ \boxed{\widetilde{\Theta}\!\left( \min\left\{nT,\;n\tau^2+\tau\sqrt{\frac{nT}{\lambda}}\right\} \right).} \]
For fixed, potentially unbalanced type sets, the sharper expression uses \(m\), rather than replacing it by \(\tau^2\).

The proof below establishes this logarithmic-factor characterization; it does not assert an exact logarithm-free upper bound.

2. Two deterministic facts about instability

Write \(s_{ij}=u_{ij}+v_{ji}\). Let \(W(a,b)\) denote maximum welfare using all current arrivals.

Selecting every cohort and pool is an admissible coalition. Transfers cancel between the two cohorts of each matched pair, so
\[ \mathcal I(x,p)\ge W(a,b)-\sum_{ij}s_{ij}x_{ij}. \tag{1} \]
Thus instability always dominates welfare regret, regardless of transfers.

For the upper bound, suppose \(\bar u_{ij},\bar v_{ji}\in[-1,1]\) satisfy
\[ \bar u_{ij}\ge u_{ij}, \qquad \bar v_{ji}\ge v_{ji}. \]
Put \(\bar s_{ij}=\bar u_{ij}+\bar v_{ji}\). Compute an optimal primal-dual pair for the transportation problem
\[ \max_{x\ge0}\sum_{ij}\bar s_{ij}x_{ij}, \qquad \sum_jx_{ij}\le a_i,\quad \sum_ix_{ij}\le b_j, \]
whose dual is
\[ \min_{\alpha,\gamma\ge0} \sum_i a_i\alpha_i+\sum_j b_j\gamma_j, \qquad \alpha_i+\gamma_j\ge\bar s_{ij}. \]
Choose the transfer, on every positive-mass matched pair, as
\[ p_{ij}=\alpha_i-\bar u_{ij}. \]

Complementary slackness implies:

  • If \(x_{ij}>0\), then \(\alpha_i+\gamma_j=\bar s_{ij}\). The two optimistic cohort utilities are therefore \(\alpha_i\) and \(\gamma_j\).
  • If a customer type has positive unmatched mass, then \(\alpha_i=0\).
  • If a provider type has positive unmatched mass, then \(\gamma_j=0\).

For any independently selected cohort coalition \(q\), dual feasibility gives
\[ W(q)\le\sum_i\alpha_i A_i(q)+\sum_j\gamma_j B_j(q). \]
The right side equals its current optimistic utility: every selected matched cohort contributes its corresponding dual value, and every selected unmatched pool contributes zero. Subtracting its true current utility yields
\[ W(q)-\sum_cq_cw_c \le \sum_{ij}\left[ q_{ij}^{\mathrm C}(\bar u_{ij}-u_{ij}) + q_{ij}^{\mathrm P}(\bar v_{ji}-v_{ji}) \right]. \]
Here \(q_{ij}^{\mathrm C}\) and \(q_{ij}^{\mathrm P}\) are separate coalition choices, each bounded by \(x_{ij}\). Hence
\[ \boxed{ \mathcal I(x,p)\le \sum_{ij}x_{ij} \left[(\bar u_{ij}-u_{ij})+(\bar v_{ji}-v_{ji})\right]. } \tag{2} \]
This argument explicitly allows the coalition to select current partners independently.

We will also need a bound when optimism fails. An optimal dual solution can be chosen with
\[ 0\le\alpha_i,\gamma_j\le2. \]
Indeed, capping any larger coordinate at \(2\) preserves feasibility because \(\bar s_{ij}\le2\), and cannot increase the objective.

For the associated transfers, every true cohort utility is at least \(-2\). Every alternative matching has welfare at most \(n\), because its matched mass is at most \(n/2\) and each pair surplus is at most \(2\). Therefore, on every sample path,
\[ 0\le\mathcal I(x,p)\le3n. \tag{3} \]

3. The online policy

Assign each pair an index \(d\) when its two types first coappear. This indexing depends only on the fixed arrival sequence, and at most \(m\) pairs receive indices. No knowledge of future arrivals or of the maximum mass \(n\) is needed.

For a pair with \(k\ge1\) previously observed samples in each direction, define
\[ b_d(k)= \sqrt{\frac{2\log\!\left(128(T+1)(d+1)^2(k+1)^2\right)}{k}}. \]
Its optimistic directional utilities are
\[ \bar u_{ij}=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}(\widehat u_{ij}+b_d(k)), \qquad \bar v_{ji}=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}(\widehat v_{ji}+b_d(k)). \]
For an unobserved pair, set both optimistic utilities to \(1\).

Each round, the policy computes the optimal primal-dual transportation solution above, implements its matching and transfers, and updates the empirical means after receiving that round’s feedback.

4. Confidence bounds under adaptive Poisson sampling

Let
\[ H_e^r=\sum_{t<r}x_e^t, \qquad K_e^r=\sum_{t<r}N_e^t \]
be a pair’s cumulative exposure and sample count before round \(r\). Put \(q=T+1\).

First, represent each directional feedback stream by an independently generated sequence of noises. For every fixed sample count \(k\), independence and the subgaussian assumption give
\[ \Pr\!\left(|\widehat u_e(k)-u_e|>b_d(k)\right) \le \frac{1}{64q(d+1)^2(k+1)^2}. \]
The same bound holds for the other direction. Summing over both directions, all pair indices, and all positive sample counts shows that, with failure probability at most \(1/(8q)\), simultaneously
\[ 0\le\bar u_e^r-u_e\le\min\{2,2b_d(K_e^r)\}, \]
and likewise for \(v_e\). Simultaneous validity over all sample counts justifies using these bounds at adaptive sample counts.

Second, the process
\[ Z_e^r= \exp\!\left[-K_e^r+(1-e^{-1})\lambda H_e^r\right] \]
is a mean-one martingale at round boundaries. This follows directly from the conditional Poisson moment-generating function.

Since \(1/2-e^{-1}>1/8\), Markov’s inequality implies
\[ \Pr\!\left( K_e^r<\frac{\lambda H_e^r}{2}, \quad \lambda H_e^r\ge8\log(8mq^2) \right) \le\frac{1}{8mq^2}. \]
A union bound over pairs and boundaries gives failure probability at most \(1/(8q)\).

Third, write \(N_*=\sum_{r,e}N_e^r\). Conditional Poisson generating functions and the deterministic exposure bound
\[ \sum_{r,e}x_e^r\le\frac{nT}{2} \]
give
\[ \mathbb E e^{N_*} \le \exp\!\left((e-1)\frac{\lambda nT}{2}\right). \]
Consequently, except with probability \(1/(8q)\),
\[ N_*\le K_*:=(e-1)\frac{\lambda nT}{2}+\log(8q). \]

Intersect these three events, obtaining an event \(E\) with
\[ \Pr(E^c)\le\frac{3}{8q}. \]
For \(z=\lambda nT\), we have
\[ K_*+1\le8q(1+z). \]
Since \(d\le m\), this implies, for every positive observed count \(k\),
\[ \log\!\left(128q(d+1)^2(k+1)^2\right)\le3\beta. \]

If \(\lambda H_e^r\ge8\log(8mq^2)\), the sample-count bound therefore gives
\[ \bar u_e^r-u_e \le 2\min\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{12\beta}{\lambda H_e^r}}\right\}. \]
For smaller exposure, the same inequality follows from the bound \(2\), because \(8\log(8mq^2)\le12\beta\). The other direction satisfies the identical inequality.

Define
\[ \kappa=\frac{12\beta}{\lambda}, \qquad f(h)=\min\{1,\sqrt{\kappa/h}\}, \quad f(0)=1. \]
Equation (2) now gives, on \(E\),
\[ R_T\le4\sum_{r,e}x_e^r f(H_e^r). \tag{4} \]

5. The batch-exposure calculation

The following deterministic calculation accounts for the additional term.

For any sequence of increments \(x_t\in[0,b]\), let \(H_t=\sum_{s<t}x_s\) and \(H=\sum_tx_t\). Since \(f\) is nonincreasing, for \(z\in[H_t,H_t+x_t]\),
\[ f(H_t)\le f((z-b)_+). \]
Integrating over these consecutive intervals gives
\[ \sum_tx_tf(H_t) \le \int_0^Hf((z-b)_+)\,dz \le b+\int_0^Hf(z)\,dz \le b+2\sqrt{\kappa H}. \tag{5} \]
The final inequality follows by integrating separately below and above \(\kappa\); it also holds when \(H\le\kappa\).

Each pair’s per-round matched mass is at most \(b=n/2\). Apply (5) pair by pair and use Cauchy–Schwarz:
\[ \sum_{r,e}x_e^rf(H_e^r) \le \frac{mn}{2} + 2\sqrt{\kappa}\sum_e\sqrt{H_e^{T+1}} \le \frac{mn}{2} + 2\sqrt{\frac{\kappa mnT}{2}}. \]
Together with (4), this bounds regret on \(E\) by
\[ C\left(mn+\sqrt{\frac{\beta mnT}{\lambda}}\right). \]
Equations (3) and the failure-probability bound contribute at most \(3n\) to expected regret, which is absorbed by \(mn\). Equation (3) also gives the unconditional bound \(3nT\). This proves the asserted upper bound.

6. Lower bounds

All lower bounds use independent standard Gaussian noises, which satisfy the required noise assumptions.

First-exposure lower bound. Suppose initially that \(T\ge m\). Enumerate every pair, and give each pair a round containing only its two endpoint types, each with mass
\[ A=\frac n2. \]
Any remaining rounds can repeat a pair.

Independently assign each pair a sign \(\theta_e\in\{-1,1\}\), with equal probabilities, and set
\[ u_e=v_e=\frac{\theta_e}{2}. \]
Before a pair’s first round, the policy has received no samples from that pair, and its sign is independent of the history.

If the policy matches mass \(x\), equation (1) bounds instability below by \(A-x\) when the sign is positive, and by \(x\) when it is negative. The expected first-round cost for that pair is therefore at least
\[ \frac12(A-x)+\frac12x=\frac n4. \]
Summing over all first appearances yields \(nm/4\).

For \(2\le T<m\), use \(T-1\) distinct isolated-pair rounds and a final round containing every type with sufficiently small positive binary rational mass. The final total mass can be at most \(n\), and the earlier rounds have total mass exactly \(n\). The same argument gives at least
\[ \frac{n(T-1)}4\ge\frac{nT}{8}. \]

For \(T=1\), concentrate mass \(A\ge n/4\) on each endpoint of one pair, give every other type a sufficiently small positive binary rational mass, and make the total exactly \(n\). Set only the distinguished pair’s surplus to a uniformly random sign; all other utilities are zero. Equation (1) again gives expected regret at least \(A/2\ge n/8\).

Thus, for every \(T\),
\[ V_{I,J}(n,T,\lambda)\ge c\,n\min\{m,T\}. \tag{6} \]
These constructions ensure that every counted type actually appears.

Sampling lower bound. Now suppose \(T\ge m\). Partition the rounds into one contiguous block per pair, with block lengths \(k_e\) differing by at most one. Every round of a pair’s block contains only its endpoints, each with mass \(A=n/2\). Thus
\[ k_{\max}=\max_e k_e\le\frac{2T}{m}. \]
Independently choose signs \(\theta_e\), and set
\[ u_e=v_e=\frac{\theta_e\Delta}{2}, \qquad \Delta=\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda n k_{\max}}}\right\}. \]

Fix a pair and condition on all other signs. Let \(P_+\) and \(P_-\) be the history laws through the end of its block under its two signs.

For any common history and chosen action, the conditional Poisson count laws coincide. Each revealed directional Gaussian observation has means differing by \(\Delta\), so the two directional observations together contribute relative entropy \(\Delta^2\) per sample count. The chain rule therefore gives
\[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_+\Vert P_-) = \lambda\Delta^2\, \mathbb E_+\sum_{\text{block}}x_e^r \le \lambda\Delta^2 A k_e \le\frac18. \]
Pinsker’s inequality implies
\[ \operatorname{TV}(P_+,P_-)\le\frac14. \]

Let \(X_e=\sum_{\text{block}}x_e^r\), which lies in \([0,Ak_e]\). For a random variable in this interval, its expectation differs under the two laws by at most \(Ak_e\operatorname{TV}(P_+,P_-)\). By equation (1), the average block regret under the two signs is therefore at least
\[ \frac{\Delta}{2} \left(Ak_e-\mathbb E_+X_e+\mathbb E_-X_e\right) \ge \frac{3\Delta Ak_e}{8}. \]
Summing blocks and averaging the other signs gives
\[ \mathbb E R_T\ge\frac{3\Delta nT}{16} \ge c\min\left\{nT,\sqrt{\frac{mnT}{\lambda}}\right\}. \tag{7} \]
Averaging over signs proves that some fixed utility instance attains this lower bound for every policy.

For \(T<m\), equation (6) already gives order \(nT\). For \(T\ge m\), combine (6) and (7), using
\[ \max\left\{mn,\min\left(nT,\sqrt{\frac{mnT}{\lambda}}\right)\right\} \ge \frac12\min\left\{nT,mn+\sqrt{\frac{mnT}{\lambda}}\right\}. \]
This proves the matching lower bound.

7. Polynomial computation and the proposed bound

Each round’s optimization is a minimum-cost circulation problem on a network with \(\tau+2\) vertices and at most \(m+\tau+1\) arcs: source-to-customer and provider-to-sink arcs carry the arrival capacities, customer-to-provider arcs have costs \(-\bar s_{ij}\), and a sink-to-source arc closes the circulation. Thus standard strongly polynomial minimum-cost-flow algorithms apply in the arithmetic/comparison model. [Orlin’s minimum-cost-flow algorithm](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.41.2.338)

Only observed types and coappearing pairs need records. Sample updates require linear arithmetic work in the number of processed samples. Confidence radii can be rounded upward to dyadic values within a constant factor, preserving the bounds. The resulting policy is polynomial in the parameters specified in the question, without expanding rational masses into individual arrivals or depending on the smallest positive mass.

To obtain the rate expressed in \(\tau\), use \(m\le\tau^2/4\) for the upper bound and a balanced split, with \(m=\lfloor\tau^2/4\rfloor\), for the lower bound.

Finally, consider the substantive family
\[ |I|=|J|=d,\qquad \tau=2d,\qquad m=d^2,\qquad n=T=d^2,\qquad \lambda=1. \]
Expose each pair exactly once, in its own isolated round. Equation (6) gives
\[ \mathbb E R_T\ge\frac{nm}{4}=\frac{d^4}{4}, \]
whereas
\[ \tau\sqrt{nT}=2d^3. \]
The polynomial gap cannot be hidden by logarithmic factors. Therefore a uniform \(\widetilde O(\tau\sqrt{nT})\) bound fails in this model.

The cited paper states the quoted typed-market bound in Theorem 5.2. Its statement does not establish that bound for the fractional-cohort model and whole-round Poisson feedback specified here. [Jagadeesan et al., Theorem 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.08843)
