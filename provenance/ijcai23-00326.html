The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a star graph \(G\), finitely many rational valuation types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu\), normalized additive valuations \(v_t\), and \(\epsilon>0\), let \(\operatorname{OPT}_N\) be the minimum worst multiplicative envy over connected allocations of \(N\mu_t\) clones of each type. Compute or approximate \(\limsup_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{T}}\operatorname{OPT}_N\), or produce a type-compressed uniform allocation scheme attaining \(3+\epsilon\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a star with \(m\geq2\) edges, each identified with \([0,1]\). Each type \(t\) has a nonnegative additive valuation \(v_t\), specified by piecewise constant densities with binary rational heights and breakpoints, with \(v_t(G)=1\); points have zero value.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including rational \(\epsilon>0\).
For each integer \(N\geq2\) satisfying \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) for every type, there are \(N\mu_t\) clones of type \(t\). An allocation covers all of \(G\), assigning each clone a connected finite union of closed edge intervals; different shares may intersect only in finitely many points, and vertices may be shared.
For an allocation \(A\), let \(E(A)\) be the least \(\alpha\geq1\) such that \(v_{t(i)}(A_j)\leq\alpha v_{t(i)}(A_i)\) for every pair of clones \(i,j\), or \(+\infty\) if none exists. Define \(\operatorname{OPT}_N=\inf_A E(A)\) and \(F=\limsup_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu\in\mathbb Z^T}\operatorname{OPT}_N\).
A type-compressed uniform scheme is a finite program, independent of \(N\), that takes an admissible \(N\), a type \(t\), and an index \(1\leq k\leq N\mu_t\), and returns that clone's share using rational endpoints. Its outputs must jointly form an allocation \(A^{(N)}\); its description size is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\), and each call takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon,\log N\).
Can a type-compressed uniform scheme with \(\limsup_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu\in\mathbb Z^T}E(A^{(N)})\leq3+\epsilon\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\), or is obtaining this guarantee NP-hard?
For an explicit finite list of \(n\) agents, a connected \((3+\epsilon)\)-envy-free allocation on a star is computable in Robertson–Webb time polynomial in \(n,m,1/\epsilon\). [Yuen and Suksompong](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.11659v2)
No result settling the stated uniform, type-compressed problem was located in the literature search.

  • Mass normalization was unspecified; require positive support masses summing to one, so N is the total number of clones.
  • Restore the finite model's conditions: at least two edges and two clones, complete allocation, connected finite unions of closed intervals, and intersections only in finitely many points, including shared vertices.
  • Define multiplicative envy by inequalities rather than ratios, resolving zero-denominator cases without ambiguity.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The scheme can be computed in polynomial time. In fact, the stated assumptions permit the stronger guarantee
$$ \lim_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu\in\mathbb Z^T}E(A^{(N)})=1. $$
Consequently, \(F=1\).

The construction uses a linear Fisher market on the finitely many intervals where every type’s density is constant. An integral flow then converts market expenditures into exact clone counts, while changing each interval’s total clone count by at most \(1\).

1. Common density intervals

Orient each edge so that coordinate \(0\) is the center of the star. Subdivide every edge at all density breakpoints of all types. Call the resulting positive-length closed intervals cells.

Their interiors are pairwise disjoint, their union is \(G\), and every type’s density is constant on each cell. The number of cells and the binary encoding length of their endpoints are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

A cell is null if every type values it at \(0\). Index the remaining cells by \(c\in\{1,\ldots,q\}\). Write
$$ I_c=[a_c,b_c], \qquad u_{tc}=v_t(I_c). $$
Here the edge containing \(I_c\) is part of its description. Every \(u_{tc}\) is a nonnegative rational, and
$$ \sum_{c=1}^q u_{tc}=1 $$
for each type \(t\). In particular, \(q\) is positive, every type values some nonnull cell positively, and every nonnull cell is valued positively by some type.

Since densities are constant within a cell, an interval occupying a fraction \(\lambda\) of its length has value \(\lambda u_{tc}\) to type \(t\).

2. An aggregate Fisher market

Create a linear Fisher market with one unit of each nonnull cell as a good. Buyer \(t\) has budget \(\mu_t\) and utility coefficient \(u_{tc}\) for good \(c\).

We use the following established algorithmic result: a linear Fisher market with positive rational budgets and nonnegative rational utility coefficients admits an exactly computable rational equilibrium in polynomial time, provided every buyer values some good positively and every good has a potential buyer. Orlin’s exact equilibrium algorithm supplies this result. Rational inputs can be converted to integer inputs by clearing denominators, with only polynomial growth in encoding length. [Orlin, *Improved Algorithms for Computing Fisher’s Market Clearing Prices*, Theorem 4.1](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol10171/DagSemProc.10171.2/DagSemProc.10171.2.pdf)

Both hypotheses hold by Section 1. Compute equilibrium prices \(p_c>0\) and expenditures \(w_{tc}\geq0\). They satisfy
$$ \sum_c w_{tc}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_t w_{tc}=p_c. $$
Define
$$ \beta_t=\max_c\frac{u_{tc}}{p_c}. $$
The equilibrium optimality conditions give
$$ u_{tc}\leq\beta_t p_c \quad\text{for all }t,c, \qquad w_{tc}>0\implies u_{tc}=\beta_t p_c. \tag{1} $$
Each \(\beta_t\) is positive. Summing expenditures also gives
$$ \sum_c p_c=\sum_t\mu_t=1. \tag{2} $$

Thus positive expenditures identify cells on which a type receives its maximum utility per unit of expenditure. This support will be preserved when assigning clones.

The prices and expenditures have polynomial binary encoding length. Denominator clearing causes no difficulty: multiplying all budgets by a common denominator scales prices and expenditures by the same factor, while multiplying all utilities by a common denominator preserves utility-maximizing purchases.

3. Rounding expenditures into clone counts

Fix an admissible \(N\), so that every \(N\mu_t\) is an integer.

We claim that one can compute nonnegative integers \(n_{tc}\) satisfying
$$ \sum_c n_{tc}=N\mu_t, \tag{3} $$
$$ n_{tc}=0\quad\text{whenever }w_{tc}=0, \tag{4} $$
and, with \(n_c=\sum_t n_{tc}\),
$$ \lfloor Np_c\rfloor\leq n_c\leq\lceil Np_c\rceil. \tag{5} $$

To prove this, construct a circulation network with a source node \(s\), type nodes, cell nodes, and a sink node \(z\). Its arcs and bounds are:

| Arc | Lower bound | Upper bound |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(s\to t\) | \(N\mu_t\) | \(N\mu_t\) |
| \(t\to c\), when \(w_{tc}>0\) | \(0\) | \(N\) |
| \(c\to z\) | \(\lfloor Np_c\rfloor\) | \(\lceil Np_c\rceil\) |
| \(z\to s\) | \(N\) | \(N\) |

A feasible fractional circulation exists: put \(Nw_{tc}\) on each type-to-cell arc, \(N\mu_t\) on each source-to-type arc, \(Np_c\) on each cell-to-sink arc, and \(N\) on the return arc. The equilibrium expenditure identities verify conservation. Moreover, \(Nw_{tc}\leq N\), so all bounds are respected.

All bounds are integers. Subtracting lower bounds and applying the usual reduction to maximum flow produces a network with integer capacities and integer vertex demands. Fractional feasibility implies that the required maximum-flow value can be attained; an integral maximum flow attains it as well. Restoring lower bounds therefore yields an integral feasible circulation.

Take its type-to-cell flows as \(n_{tc}\). Conservation proves (3) and (5), while the absence of unsupported arcs proves (4).

This computation takes time polynomial in \(\tau,q,L,\log N\). For example, a deterministic Edmonds–Karp implementation has a polynomial number of augmentations independent of the numerical sizes of capacities. Fixing an order for nodes and arcs makes the output deterministic.

4. Connected shares covering the whole star

Let
$$ p_{\min}=\min_c p_c>0, \qquad N_0=\max\left\{2,\left\lceil\frac{2}{p_{\min}}\right\rceil\right\}. \tag{6} $$
For \(N\geq N_0\), inequality (5) implies that every \(n_c\) is positive.

Divide each nonnull cell \(I_c\) into \(n_c\) equal-length closed intervals. Assign exactly \(n_{tc}\) of them to type \(t\), using a fixed order of types within each cell. By (3), every clone receives exactly one such base interval. The base intervals cover every nonnull cell and overlap only at endpoints.

It remains to allocate null cells.

Let \(Z\) be the union of all null cells. Each connected component of \(Z\) is a connected finite union of closed edge intervals and has value \(0\) to every type. Every such component meets some nonnull cell at an endpoint. Indeed, the subdivision gives a finite connected graph of cells; following a path from the component towards any nonnull cell gives such an incidence.

For each component, choose one incident nonnull cell and one common endpoint, with choices fixed during preprocessing. Append the component to the base interval in that cell containing the chosen endpoint.

Each appended component intersects its recipient’s base interval. Hence every resulting share is connected. Components of \(Z\) are assigned once, and their intersections with nonnull cells consist only of endpoints. It follows that the resulting shares cover \(G\) and different shares intersect only in finitely many points.

Appending these components changes no type’s value for any share.

For admissible \(N<N_0\), use a simple valid allocation: give all of \(G\) to one fixed clone and give the singleton center to every other clone. These finitely many population sizes do not affect the required limit.

5. Envy tends to one

Consider an admissible \(N\geq N_0\). Suppose clone \(i\), of type \(t\), receives a base interval in cell \(c\). Then \(n_{tc}>0\), so (4) implies \(w_{tc}>0\). Constant density and (1) therefore give
$$ v_t(A_i)=\frac{u_{tc}}{n_c} =\beta_t\frac{p_c}{n_c}. \tag{7} $$

If another clone \(j\) receives its base interval in cell \(d\), then
$$ v_t(A_j)=\frac{u_{td}}{n_d} \leq\beta_t\frac{p_d}{n_d}. \tag{8} $$
The null extensions contribute no value in either equation.

By (5), write
$$ n_c=Np_c+e_c, \qquad |e_c|\leq1. $$
Set
$$ \delta_N=\frac{1}{Np_{\min}}. $$
For \(N\geq N_0\), we have \(\delta_N\leq\frac12\), and
$$ \frac{p_c}{n_c} =\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{1+e_c/(Np_c)}. $$
Since \(\left|e_c/(Np_c)\right|\leq\delta_N\), this yields
$$ \frac{1}{N(1+\delta_N)} \leq\frac{p_c}{n_c} \leq\frac{1}{N(1-\delta_N)} \quad\text{for every }c. \tag{9} $$

Combining (7)–(9), for every pair of clones \(i,j\),
$$ v_{t(i)}(A_j) \leq \frac{1+\delta_N}{1-\delta_N} v_{t(i)}(A_i). $$
Every clone’s own value is positive by (7). Consequently,
$$ 1\leq E(A^{(N)}) \leq \frac{1+\delta_N}{1-\delta_N}. \tag{10} $$

The right-hand side is at most \(3\) for \(N\geq N_0\) and tends to \(1\). Rational masses have a common denominator, so admissible population sizes are unbounded. Thus (10) proves
$$ \lim_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu\in\mathbb Z^T}E(A^{(N)})=1. $$

Finally, \(1\leq\operatorname{OPT}_N\leq E(A^{(N)})\). Hence \(F=1\).

6. Uniformity and bit complexity

Preprocessing stores the cell descriptions, equilibrium data, expenditure support, null-component attachment choices, and \(N_0\). All have polynomial encoding length. Although the numerical value of \(N_0\) may be large, its binary length is polynomial because \(p_{\min}\) has polynomial rational encoding length.

The program uses the following decoder for a query \((N,t,k)\):

  • If \(N<N_0\), return the corresponding share in the fallback allocation.
  • Otherwise, compute the deterministic integral circulation from Section 3.
  • Find the unique cell \(c\) satisfying

$$ \sum_{d<c}n_{td}<k\leq\sum_{d\leq c}n_{td}. $$

  • Set

$$ r=k-\sum_{d<c}n_{td}, \qquad h=r+\sum_{s<t}n_{sc}. $$
The clone receives interval number \(h\) in cell \(c\), namely
$$ \left[ a_c+\frac{h-1}{n_c}(b_c-a_c), \; a_c+\frac{h}{n_c}(b_c-a_c) \right]. $$

  • Append any null components assigned to this boundary interval.

The row sums in (3) ensure that the selected cell exists. Fixed type ordering ensures that all calls assign every base interval exactly once. Determinism ensures that independently decoded shares belong to the same allocation.

The endpoints are rational and have bit length polynomial in the input length and \(\log N\), since \(n_c\leq N\). The flow network has polynomial size, and the decoder handles counts through binary arithmetic without enumerating clones. Null extensions contain at most the original number of cells, so output size is polynomial as well.

Therefore preprocessing and program description size are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and each call takes time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\). These bounds satisfy the requested dependence on \(1/\epsilon\) without using \(\epsilon\).

The stronger limit relies on keeping the finite valuation description fixed while \(N\) grows. The construction proves tractability of the stated uniform problem directly.

Verdict

The construction resolves the uniform, type-compressed problem in polynomial time. Its stronger asymptotic guarantee, \(\lim E(A^{(N)})=1\), follows from the argument. I found no substantive mathematical or algorithmic gap.

There is one minor wording issue: densities need only be constant almost everywhere on each closed cell. Literal constancy at shared breakpoints is unnecessary and need not hold.

Step-by-step verification

1. Common density intervals

The common subdivision has polynomially many cells: its breakpoints come from the explicitly supplied density descriptions. Sorting these rational breakpoints and computing cell values requires polynomial time and polynomial bit length.

Removing null cells preserves each type’s total value, so \(\sum_c u_{tc}=1\). This establishes all three properties subsequently required: at least one nonnull cell exists, every type values some retained cell positively, and every retained cell has a potential buyer.

The proportional-value formula within a cell is valid because points have zero value.

Minor issue. The sentence “every type’s density is constant on each cell” is too literal for closed cells sharing a breakpoint. Different adjacent densities cannot both determine the same endpoint value.

Minimum clarification: Replace “constant on each cell” with “constant almost everywhere on each cell.” No construction or calculation changes.

2. Fisher-market theorem and its application

The cited theorem applies. Orlin’s model assumes positive integral budgets, nonnegative integral utilities, a positively valued good for every buyer, and a potential buyer for every good. Its equilibrium conditions include complete expenditure of budgets, market clearing, and expenditure only on maximum bang-per-buck edges. Theorem 4.1 computes an exact optimal price-and-expenditure pair in strongly polynomially many arithmetic operations. These are the conditions invoked by the prover. [Orlin, model description and Theorem 4.1](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol10171/DagSemProc.10171.2/DagSemProc.10171.2.pdf)

The rational-to-integral conversion is legitimate. Common denominators have polynomial binary length; scaling all budgets scales prices and expenditures, while uniformly scaling utilities preserves the maximizing expenditure support.

The claimed rational output and polynomial encoding length are consistent with the exact equilibrium result. Strictly positive prices are warranted because every retained good is positively valued by a buyer with positive budget.

Thus the expenditure identities, maximum bang-per-buck conditions, positivity of \(\beta_t\), and normalization \(\sum_c p_c=1\) are valid.

3. Integral circulation

Every network bound is integral for admissible \(N\). The proposed fractional circulation satisfies conservation and all bounds:

  • The type-node balances follow from \(\sum_c w_{tc}=\mu_t\).
  • The cell-node balances follow from \(\sum_t w_{tc}=p_c\).
  • The source and sink balances follow from the normalized total budget.
  • The upper bound on a type-to-cell arc holds because \(w_{tc}\leq\mu_t\leq1\).

The lower-bound reduction and integral maximum-flow property justify an integral feasible circulation. Consequently, the exact type counts, preservation of expenditure support, and rounded cell totals all follow.

The network has polynomial size. Edmonds–Karp has a polynomial augmentation bound independent of capacity magnitudes; binary capacities and intermediate flows have polynomial length in the input and \(\log N\). The decoder therefore does not require time proportional to \(N\).

4. Connected allocation and null components

For \(N\geq N_0\), every cell satisfies \(Np_c\geq2\), so its rounded count is positive. Equal subdivision is therefore well-defined.

The row sums assign exactly one base interval to every clone. Fixed ordering assigns every base interval exactly once.

The null-component attachment argument is valid. A null component is a finite connected union of cells. Since \(G\) is connected and contains a nonnull cell, every null component has an endpoint incidence with a nonnull cell. Attaching it to an incident boundary interval preserves connectedness.

Distinct shares can intersect only at subdivision endpoints or vertices. Null attachments introduce no positive-length overlap with nonnull cells, and each null component is assigned once. Coverage and finite interval descriptions follow.

This also handles null components spanning several edges through the center. If there are no null cells, no attachments are needed.

The fallback allocation is permitted by the task: singleton centers are connected degenerate closed intervals, vertices may be shared, and the whole-star share intersects the singleton shares only at one point. Infinite envy at these finitely many population sizes does not affect the asymptotic limit.

5. Envy calculation and limit

A clone assigned to cell \(c\) has \(n_{tc}>0\), hence \(w_{tc}>0\). Its own value in equation (7) is therefore positive and uses the correct equilibrium equality.

Equation (8) applies to every other clone, including clones of the same type. Null extensions have zero value under every type.

The rounding estimate \(|e_c|\leq1\) is valid. Since \(\delta_N\leq1/2\), all denominators in equation (9) are positive. Combining the upper bound for another share with the lower bound for the clone’s own share gives exactly
\[ E(A^{(N)})\leq\frac{1+\delta_N}{1-\delta_N}. \]

The bound is at most \(3\) at \(\delta_N=1/2\), and tends to \(1\) for the fixed input as \(N\) grows. Rational masses ensure an unbounded admissible subsequence.

The inequality \(1\leq\operatorname{OPT}_N\leq E(A^{(N)})\) consequently establishes \(F=1\).

6. Uniformity and complexity

The stored data have polynomial encoding length, including the binary representation of \(N_0\). No polynomial bound on its numerical value is required by the task.

The cumulative-count decoder selects an existing cell and a valid interval number. Deterministic circulation computation ensures that separate calls describe the same allocation.

Endpoint arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity because \(1\leq n_c\leq N\). Null extensions add only polynomially many intervals. Thus both output size and per-call time satisfy the required bounds.

The construction needs no dependence on \(\epsilon\), which satisfies the requested polynomial dependence on \(1/\epsilon\).
