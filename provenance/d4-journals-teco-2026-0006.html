Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(I\) and \(J\), rational masses \(\mu_v\ge 0\) for \(v\in I\cup J\) summing to \(1\), a compatibility graph \(E\subseteq I\times J\) partitioned into \(E_1\) and \(E_2\), and complete weak preferences over compatible types and being unmatched (with every compatible type preferred to being unmatched), decide whether a stable mass assignment exists and construct one if it does. A feasible assignment has variables \(x_e\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{e\in E(v)}x_e\le\mu_v\); let \(r_v=\mu_v-\sum_{f\in E(v)}x_f\), \(W_v(e)=r_v+\sum_{f\in E(v):\,e\succeq_v f}x_f\), and \(S_v(e)=\sum_{f\in E(v):\,e\succ_v f}x_f\). For \(e=\{u,v\}\in E_1\), it is blocking exactly when \(W_u(e)>0\), \(W_v(e)>0\), and \(W_u(e)>x_e\) or \(W_v(e)>x_e\); for \(e\in E_2\), it is blocking exactly when both weakly willing masses are positive and \(S_u(e)>0\) or \(S_v(e)>0\). Ask for complexity in the number of positive-mass types, \(|E|\), and the binary input length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be disjoint finite sets of types. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) positive-mass types, each with mass \(\mu_v\) encoded as a binary rational, with \(\sum_v\mu_v=1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
The input includes a simple bipartite compatibility graph \(E\subseteq I\times J\), a partition \(E=E_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}E_2\), and complete weak preferences \(\succeq_v\) on incident edges and being unmatched; every incident edge is strictly preferred to being unmatched. Ties remain unbroken. Let \(m=|E|\) and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Write \(E(v)\) for the incident edges. A feasible mass assignment is \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}\) with \(\sum_{e\in E(v)}x_e\le\mu_v\); define \(r_v=\mu_v-\sum_{e\in E(v)}x_e\).
For \(e\in E(v)\), define \(W_v(e)=r_v+\sum_{f\in E(v):\,e\succeq_v f}x_f\) and \(S_v(e)=r_v+\sum_{f\in E(v):\,e\succ_v f}x_f\).
An edge \(e=\{u,v\}\in E_1\) blocks exactly when \(W_u(e)>0\), \(W_v(e)>0\), and \(W_u(e)>x_e\lor W_v(e)>x_e\).
An edge \(e=\{u,v\}\in E_2\) blocks exactly when \(W_u(e)>0\), \(W_v(e)>0\), and \(S_u(e)>0\lor S_v(e)>0\). An assignment is stable if no edge blocks.

Is deciding whether a stable mass assignment exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), with construction of an assignment when one exists, or is the decision problem NP-hard?

For discrete one-to-one matchings, existence and construction under this edge-dependent combination of super-stability and strong stability are polynomial-time solvable ([Kamiyama](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16271)). No result settling the exact mass problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Added residual unmatched mass to S_v(e): every compatible partner is strictly preferred to being unmatched, so unmatched mass must count as strictly willing.
  • Grouped each blocking condition as the conjunction of both positive weakly willing masses and the parenthesized disjunction of the remaining inequalities.
  • Removed zero-mass types from the input support and restricted the compatibility graph and preferences accordingly.
  • Defined m as the number of compatibility edges and L as the total binary input length; retained total mass one without imposing equal mass on the two sides.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, including construction of a stable mass assignment.

The algorithm uses proposals, edge deletions, and maximum flows with binary rational capacities. Its deletion framework follows [Kamiyama’s discrete algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16271), but the weighted arguments needed for this mass problem are proved below. No expansion into individual agents is required.

Write \(\mu(X)=\sum_{v\in X}\mu_v\) for a set of types \(X\).

1. Structural consequences of stability

Lemma 1. Every stable assignment has the following properties:

  • Every type with a positive incident assignment is fully matched.
  • All positive incident assignments at a type belong to one preference tie.
  • If \(e=(i,j)\in E_1\) has positive assignment, then it is the only positive edge at either endpoint, and \(x_e=\mu_i=\mu_j\).

Proof. Suppose \(x_e>0\). At both endpoints, \(W_v(e)\ge x_e>0\).

If \(e\in E_2\), stability therefore requires \(S_i(e)=S_j(e)=0\). If \(e\in E_1\), stability requires \(W_i(e)=W_j(e)=x_e\), which also implies \(S_i(e)=S_j(e)=0\), because

\(0\le S_v(e)\le W_v(e)-x_e\).

Since \(r_v\) is a nonnegative summand of \(S_v(e)\), both endpoints are fully matched.

If two positive edges \(e,f\) at a type \(v\) satisfied \(e\succ_v f\), then \(S_v(e)\ge x_f>0\), contradicting the preceding conclusion. Completeness of the weak preference order consequently implies that all positive incident edges are tied.

Finally, let \(e\in E_1\) be positive. Every positive edge at either endpoint is tied with \(e\), so fullness gives \(W_v(e)=\mu_v\). Since \(W_v(e)=x_e\), we obtain \(x_e=\mu_v\) at both endpoints, leaving no mass for another incident edge. \(\square\)

In particular, an edge in \(E_1\) with unequal endpoint masses can never be positive.

2. Proposals and the algorithm

The algorithm maintains a set \(F\subseteq E\) of undeleted edges. Initially \(F=E\).

For each \(i\in I\), let \(A(i)\) consist of its most preferred incident edges in \(F\), or be empty if \(F(i)=\varnothing\). Set \(A=\bigcup_{i\in I}A(i)\).

For each \(j\in J\), let \(B(j)\) consist of its most preferred edges in \(A(j)\). Define its accepted proposals \(H(j)\) as follows:

  • If \(B(j)\cap E_1=\varnothing\), accept all of \(B(j)\).
  • If \(B(j)\cap E_1=\{e\}\), where \(e=(i,j)\) and \(\mu_i=\mu_j\), accept only \(e\).
  • Otherwise, accept nothing.

Empty proposal sets yield empty accepted sets. Put \(H=\bigcup_{j\in J}H(j)\) and \(Q=A\setminus H\).

When \(Q=\varnothing\), we have \(H=A\). Define

\(I_F=\{i\in I:F(i)\ne\varnothing\}\), \(\qquad J_H=\{j\in J:H(j)\ne\varnothing\}\),

and \(M=\mu(I_F)\), \(B=\mu(J_H)\).

Construct a flow network with source \(s\), sink \(t\), and arcs

  • \(s\to i\), of capacity \(\mu_i\), for \(i\in I_F\);
  • \(i\to j\), of capacity \(2\), for each \((i,j)\in H\);
  • \(j\to t\), of capacity \(\mu_j\), for \(j\in J_H\).

The capacity \(2\) exceeds every possible total flow because the total society mass is \(1\).

For \(X\subseteq I_F\), let \(\Gamma_H(X)\) denote its neighbors in \(H\), and define

\(\rho_H(X)=\mu(\Gamma_H(X))-\mu(X)\).

The algorithm repeats these steps:

  • Reject proposals. Compute \(A,H,Q\). If \(Q\ne\varnothing\), delete \(Q\) from \(F\) and restart.
  • Resolve a flow deficit. Compute a maximum flow. If its value is less than \(M\), find an inclusion-wise minimal minimizer \(N\) of \(\rho_H\), delete every edge in \(H\) incident to \(N\), and restart.
  • Check total mass. If the flow value is \(M\) but \(B>M\), output No. Otherwise \(B=M\).
  • Construct a candidate. Set \(x_e\) to the flow on \(e\in H\), and set all other coordinates to zero. If \(x\) is stable, output \(x\).
  • Repair a blocking deletion. Choose a blocking edge \(e=(i,j)\). If \(j\notin J_H\), output No. Otherwise delete \(H(j)\) from \(F\) and restart.

The following sections prove every deletion and both nonexistence conclusions sound.

3. Invariants and soundness of proposal rejection

We maintain the invariant:

Every stable assignment for the original instance is supported on \(F\).

Here “supported on \(F\)” means that its coordinates outside \(F\) are zero. The invariant is true initially; we prove that each deletion preserves it.

We also use the preference-prefix property:

\[ \text{For every deleted }e\in E(i)\setminus F \text{ and every }f\in F(i),\quad e\succeq_i f. \]

Equivalently, using the prescribed inline notation, every deleted \(e\in E(i)\setminus F\) satisfies \(e\succeq_i f\) for every \(f\in F(i)\).

Indeed, every deletion in the algorithm consists of current proposals in \(A\), which are most preferred among the remaining edges. This proves the property inductively.

Let \(\sigma\) be any stable assignment supported on the current \(F\). For every proposal \(g=(i,j)\in A\), every positive edge of \(\sigma\) at \(i\) is weakly worse than \(g\). Consequently,

\(W_i^\sigma(g)=\mu_i>0.\)

Lemma 2. No edge in \(Q=A\setminus H\) can be positive in \(\sigma\).

Proof. Suppose \(f=(i,j)\in Q\) has \(\sigma_f>0\). There are three cases.

If \(f\notin B(j)\), choose \(g=(k,j)\in B(j)\). Then \(g\succ_j f\). By Lemma 1, \(\sigma\) fully matches \(j\) at the tie containing \(f\). Thus

\(\sigma_g=0,\qquad S_j^\sigma(g)=W_j^\sigma(g)=\mu_j>0.\)

Also \(W_k^\sigma(g)=\mu_k>0\), since \(g\in A\). Hence \(g\) blocks under either edge class.

Next, suppose \(f\in B(j)\) and there is a distinct \(g=(k,j)\in B(j)\cap E_1\). Since \(g\sim_j f\), Lemma 1 gives \(W_j^\sigma(g)=\mu_j>0\). Moreover, \(\sigma_g=0\): a positive \(E_1\)-edge at \(j\) would exclude the positive edge \(f\). Together with \(W_k^\sigma(g)=\mu_k>0\), this makes \(g\) block under its \(E_1\) condition.

The remaining possibility for a rejected edge in \(B(j)\) is that \(f\) itself is its unique \(E_1\)-edge and its endpoint masses differ. Lemma 1 directly forbids \(\sigma_f>0\).

Every case is a contradiction. \(\square\)

Thus deleting \(Q\) preserves the invariant.

When \(Q=\varnothing\), the graph \(H=A\) has two useful properties:

  • All its incident edges at each type are tied.
  • If \(H(j)\) contains an \(E_1\)-edge, then \(H(j)\) consists of that single edge, whose endpoint masses are equal.

4. Soundness of deletion after a flow deficit

First, the required minimizer can be obtained by a maximum-flow computation.

Lemma 3. The minimum cut value of the network is \(M+\min_{X\subseteq I_F}\rho_H(X)\). An inclusion-wise minimal minimizer is computable in polynomial time.

Proof. The cut with source side \(\{s\}\) has capacity \(M\le1\). Therefore a minimum cut cannot cross an arc of capacity \(2\).

If its source-side left types are \(X\), its source side must contain \(\Gamma_H(X)\). It contains no additional right types: removing any such type would strictly reduce the cut capacity, since all masses are positive. Its capacity is consequently

\(\mu(I_F\setminus X)+\mu(\Gamma_H(X))=M+\rho_H(X).\)

Conversely, every \(X\subseteq I_F\) gives precisely such a cut.

After computing a maximum flow, take the vertices reachable from \(s\) in the residual network. They form a minimum-cut source side contained in every minimum-cut source side: no residual arc can leave any minimum-cut source side. Its left types therefore give an inclusion-wise minimal minimizer of \(\rho_H\). \(\square\)

If the maximum flow is less than \(M\), this minimizer \(N\) satisfies \(\rho_H(N)<0\), so \(N\ne\varnothing\).

Lemma 4. No stable assignment supported on \(F\) can be positive on an edge of \(H\) incident to \(N\).

Proof. Suppose \(\sigma\) is such an assignment and some such edge is positive. Define

\(T=\{i\in N:\sigma_f>0\text{ for some }f\in H(i)\}.\)

Then \(T\ne\varnothing\). For every \(i\in T\), Lemma 1 implies that its entire mass is assigned within its current top tie. Because \(H=A\), all these assignments use \(H(i)\).

Let \(K\subseteq\Gamma_H(N)\) consist of the right types receiving positive mass from \(T\). Feasibility gives

\(\mu(K)\ge\mu(T).\)

There must be an edge of \(H\) from \(N\setminus T\) to \(K\). Otherwise,

\(\Gamma_H(N\setminus T)\subseteq\Gamma_H(N)\setminus K,\)

and hence

\(\rho_H(N\setminus T)\le\mu(\Gamma_H(N))-\mu(K)-\mu(N)+\mu(T)\le\rho_H(N).\)

Since \(N\) minimizes \(\rho_H\), its proper subset \(N\setminus T\) would also minimize it, contradicting inclusion-wise minimality.

Choose such an edge \(e=(i,j)\), with \(i\in N\setminus T\) and \(j\in K\).

At \(i\), the assignment \(\sigma\) either leaves the type unmatched or assigns its entire mass strictly below its current top tie. Therefore

\(\sigma_e=0,\qquad S_i^\sigma(e)=W_i^\sigma(e)=\mu_i>0.\)

At \(j\), some positive edge arrives from \(T\) through \(H\). Lemma 1 and the tie property of \(H(j)\) imply

\(W_j^\sigma(e)=\mu_j>0.\)

Thus \(e\) blocks: its \(E_2\) condition holds because \(S_i^\sigma(e)>0\), and its \(E_1\) condition holds because \(W_i^\sigma(e)>\sigma_e=0\). This contradiction proves the lemma. \(\square\)

The deletion is nonempty: \(N\ne\varnothing\), and every type in \(I_F\) has an incident edge in \(H=A\).

5. The mass-balance test and the candidate assignment

Lemma 5. Every stable assignment supported on \(F\) fully matches every type in \(J_H\). Consequently, it must satisfy \(B\le M\).

Proof. Let \(j\in J_H\), and choose \(e=(i,j)\in H\). Because \(e\in A\),

\(W_i^\sigma(e)=\mu_i>0.\)

If \(j\) were unmatched, then

\(\sigma_e=0,\qquad W_j^\sigma(e)=S_j^\sigma(e)=\mu_j>0,\)

so \(e\) would block under either class. Thus \(j\) has a positive incident assignment and, by Lemma 1, is fully matched.

The total assigned mass is at least \(B\), because all types in \(J_H\) are fully matched. It is at most \(M\), because \(\sigma\) is supported on \(F\), and types outside \(I_F\) receive no assignment. Hence \(B\le M\). \(\square\)

This proves the soundness of outputting No when \(B>M\).

If a flow of value \(M\) exists, its sink capacities imply \(B\ge M\). Therefore the remaining case is exactly \(B=M\). Such a flow saturates every source arc and every sink arc.

Lemma 6. The candidate \(x\) constructed from a flow of value \(M=B\) has no blocking edge in \(F\).

Proof. It fully matches every type in \(I_F\cup J_H\), with assignments on its incident \(H\)-tie.

For \(e=(i,j)\in H\cap E_2\), both endpoints are fully matched within the tie containing \(e\). Thus

\(S_i^x(e)=S_j^x(e)=0,\)

and \(e\) does not block.

For \(e=(i,j)\in H\cap E_1\), the acceptance rule gives \(H(j)=\{e\}\) and \(\mu_i=\mu_j\). Saturation at \(j\) therefore gives \(x_e=\mu_j=\mu_i\), which uses all of \(i\)'s capacity too. Consequently,

\(W_i^x(e)=W_j^x(e)=x_e,\)

so \(e\) does not block.

Finally, if \(e=(i,j)\in F\setminus H\), then \(e\notin A(i)\), because \(H=A\). Type \(i\) is fully matched on edges strictly better than \(e\), giving \(W_i^x(e)=0\). Again \(e\) does not block. \(\square\)

Thus any blocking edge of the candidate is necessarily deleted.

6. Soundness of the repair step

Let \(e=(i,j)\in E\setminus F\) block the candidate \(x\).

Lemma 7. If \(j\in J_H\), deleting \(H(j)\) preserves every stable assignment.

Proof. Suppose a stable assignment \(\sigma\) supported on \(F\) is positive on some edge of \(H(j)\).

By Lemma 1, \(\sigma\) fully matches \(j\) within the same tie used by \(x\). Therefore, for the edge \(e\),

\(W_j^\sigma(e)=W_j^x(e),\qquad S_j^\sigma(e)=S_j^x(e).\)

The preference-prefix property gives \(e\succeq_i f\) for every \(f\in F(i)\). Hence

\(W_i^\sigma(e)=W_i^x(e)=\mu_i.\)

We also have \(S_i^\sigma(e)\ge S_i^x(e)\). To check this explicitly:

  • If \(F(i)=\varnothing\), both assignments leave \(i\) unmatched.
  • Otherwise, \(x\) fully matches \(i\) in its best remaining tie. If \(e\) is tied with that tie, \(S_i^x(e)=0\).
  • If \(e\) is strictly better than that tie, then every possible assignment supported on \(F\) is strictly worse than \(e\), so both strict-willingness quantities equal \(\mu_i\).

Finally, \(x_e=\sigma_e=0\). Thus the blocking conditions for \(e\) transfer from \(x\) to \(\sigma\): the \(W\)-conditions are preserved, and the \(S\)-conditions can only increase. This contradicts stability.

Therefore no stable assignment supported on \(F\) is positive on \(H(j)\). \(\square\)

This deletion is nonempty because \(j\in J_H\).

Lemma 8. If \(j\notin J_H\), no stable assignment exists.

Proof. Suppose \(\sigma\) were stable. The invariant puts its support in \(F\).

By Lemma 5, it assigns total mass \(B=M\) to \(J_H\). Its overall assigned mass is at most \(M\). It therefore assigns zero mass to every right type outside \(J_H\), including \(j\).

The preference-prefix property gives \(W_i^\sigma(e)=\mu_i>0\), while unmatchedness of \(j\) gives

\(\sigma_e=0,\qquad W_j^\sigma(e)=S_j^\sigma(e)=\mu_j>0.\)

These quantities make \(e\) block under either edge class, a contradiction. \(\square\)

7. Termination and binary complexity

Every restarting iteration deletes at least one undeleted edge. There are therefore at most \(m\) restarting iterations and one terminal iteration.

The preceding lemmas prove the invariant inductively. Every No conclusion excludes all stable assignments supported on the current \(F\), and hence all original stable assignments. Every returned assignment is feasible and has been checked against every original edge. This proves correctness.

For the bit-complexity bound, write the input masses as \(\mu_v=p_v/q_v\), and let

\(D=\prod_v q_v.\)

The binary length of \(D\) is at most the sum of the denominator lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Scale the flow capacities by \(D\). They become integers of binary length \(O(L)\), including the internal-edge capacity \(2D\).

Use a polynomial maximum-flow algorithm such as Edmonds–Karp, whose number of augmentations is independent of the numerical capacity values. Each network has at most \(\tau+2\) vertices and \(m+\tau\) arcs. Residual reachability, proposal calculations, mass comparisons, and blocking checks also take polynomial time with exact arithmetic.

Starting from zero, integral augmentations produce integral scaled flows. The output coordinates can therefore be written as \(x_e=k_e/D\), with \(0\le k_e\le D\), so each coordinate has binary length \(O(L)\).

Thus both decision and construction run in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

Adversarial review (gpt-6.1-sol / medium, Bolzano verifier prompt)

The claimed polynomial-time result is established. The deletion rules preserve every stable assignment, both No conclusions are sound, and the algorithm terminates with polynomial bit complexity. I found no major or minor mathematical issues.

1. Structural consequences

Lemma 1 correctly derives the restrictions on positive assignments.

For a positive edge \(e\), both endpoint willingness quantities satisfy \(W_v(e)\ge x_e>0\). Consequently:

  • For \(e\in E_2\), nonblocking requires both strict-willingness quantities to vanish.
  • For \(e\in E_1\), nonblocking requires \(W_v(e)=x_e\) at both endpoints. The stated inequality \(0\le S_v(e)\le W_v(e)-x_e\) then gives the same conclusion.

Since unmatched mass appears in \(S_v(e)\), every endpoint of a positive edge is fully matched. Two positive edges in distinct preference ties would contradict the vanishing strict-willingness quantity for the better edge. Finally, a positive \(E_1\)-edge exhausts both endpoint masses, which must therefore be equal.

All three structural claims follow.

2. Proposal rejection and the invariants

The preference-prefix property is preserved: every deletion consists of current proposals, and these are maximal among the remaining edges at their left endpoints. Earlier deleted edges remain weakly preferred to all surviving edges.

For any stable assignment supported on \(F\), a proposal \(g=(i,j)\) satisfies \(W_i^\sigma(g)=\mu_i\). This includes the possibility that \(i\) receives no assignment.

Lemma 2 exhausts the rejection cases:

  • If the rejected positive edge is below the best proposal tie at \(j\), a better proposal has positive strict willingness at \(j\) and positive weak willingness at its left endpoint.
  • If a distinct best-tie \(E_1\)-proposal exists, that proposal must have zero assignment, while both endpoint weak-willingness quantities are positive.
  • If the rejected edge is itself the unique best-tie \(E_1\)-proposal with unequal endpoint masses, Lemma 1 excludes positive assignment directly.

Thus every rejected edge must have zero assignment in every surviving stable assignment. The support invariant is preserved.

When rejection stops, \(H=A\), all incident \(H\)-edges at each endpoint are tied, and the stated singleton property for accepted \(E_1\)-edges follows from the acceptance rule.

3. Minimum cuts and the deficit deletion

Lemma 3 correctly identifies the cut objective.

A minimum cut has capacity at most \(M\le1\), so it cannot cross an internal arc of capacity \(2\). If its left source-side vertices are \(X\), its right source-side vertices must include \(\Gamma_H(X)\). Any additional right vertex can be removed with a strict capacity reduction because its mass is positive. The resulting capacity is exactly \(M+\rho_H(X)\).

Residual reachability after a maximum flow yields the smallest minimum-cut source side under inclusion. Its left vertices therefore give an inclusion-wise minimal minimizer of \(\rho_H\). The required maximum-flow and residual-cut facts apply to this finite network with nonnegative capacities.

Lemma 4 is also sound:

  • Every vertex in \(T\) assigns its entire mass through \(H\), so feasibility gives \(\mu(K)\ge\mu(T)\).
  • If no edge connected \(N\setminus T\) to \(K\), the displayed calculation would make the proper subset \(N\setminus T\) another minimizer, contradicting inclusion-wise minimality.
  • On the resulting connecting edge, the left endpoint has strictly positive willingness, and the right endpoint has positive weak willingness because it receives positive assignment in the common \(H\)-tie.

That edge blocks under either applicable class. Hence every edge deleted after a flow deficit is absent from every stable assignment supported on \(F\).

The deletion is nonempty, as asserted.

4. Mass balance and the candidate

Lemma 5 correctly forces every vertex in \(J_H\) to be fully matched in any stable assignment supported on \(F\). An entirely unmatched such vertex would form a blocking edge with one of its proposals; Lemma 1 then turns positive assignment into full matching.

Here “total assigned mass” consistently means the sum of edge assignments. Counting at right endpoints gives the lower bound \(B\); counting at left endpoints gives the upper bound \(M\). Thus \(B>M\) soundly certifies nonexistence.

A flow of value \(M\) implies \(B\ge M\). After the mass-balance test, therefore, \(B=M\), and all source and sink arcs are saturated.

Lemma 6 checks every surviving edge:

  • An \(H\cap E_2\)-edge has zero strict willingness at both endpoints.
  • An \(H\cap E_1\)-edge receives the entire equal mass of both endpoints.
  • An edge in \(F\setminus H\) is strictly below its left endpoint’s assigned tie, giving zero weak willingness there.

Consequently, any blocking edge found during the final check is necessarily deleted.

5. Repairing a blocking deletion

Lemma 7 correctly transfers blocking from the candidate to a hypothetical stable assignment positive on \(H(j)\).

Positive assignment on \(H(j)\) forces the right endpoint to be fully matched in the same preference tie as the candidate. Its weak and strict willingness quantities for the deleted edge therefore agree in both assignments.

At the left endpoint, the preference-prefix property gives weak willingness \(\mu_i\) in both assignments. The stated case analysis establishes that strict willingness cannot decrease. Both assignments give the deleted edge zero mass. These facts preserve its blocking condition for either edge class.

Thus deleting \(H(j)\) preserves the support invariant and removes at least one edge.

Lemma 8 correctly handles \(j\notin J_H\). Any stable assignment supported on \(F\) already assigns edge mass \(B=M\) to \(J_H\), exhausting the available left mass. The right endpoint \(j\) must therefore remain unmatched. Together with the preference-prefix property at \(i\), this makes the selected deleted edge block.

6. Termination, construction, and encoding

Every restart deletes at least one surviving edge. There are at most \(m\) restarts, followed by one terminal iteration. The invariant and the two nonexistence lemmas establish decision correctness; feasibility and the explicit check against every original edge establish construction correctness.

The denominator scaling is valid. The product \(D=\prod_v q_v\) has binary length \(O(L)\), and every scaled capacity is an integer with \(O(L)\) bits. Edmonds–Karp applies and has polynomially many augmentations independent of capacity magnitudes. Exact residual calculations, comparisons, and stability checks remain polynomial in the stated input parameters.

Integral scaled flows yield coordinates \(x_e=k_e/D\) with \(O(L)\)-bit representations. The output size is therefore polynomial as well.

The proof covers empty edge sets, isolated supported types, empty proposal sets, and empty flow networks. No additional clarification or repair is needed.
