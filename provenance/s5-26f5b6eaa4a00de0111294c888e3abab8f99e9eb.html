The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational constituency-role masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_m>0\) summing to \(1\), with independent equiprobable citizen votes within each constituency, choose a monotone simple game \(V\) on the \(m\) delegates minimizing \(\lim_{N\to\infty}\Delta[V[M_{n_1},\ldots,M_{n_m}]]/\sqrt N\) over population sequences with \(n_i/N\to\mu_i\); determine all minimizers.

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here types are constituency roles, so \(\tau=m\geq1\), with \(\mu_i>0\) and \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\).
A council rule is a monotone simple game \(V\subseteq2^{[m]}\), with \(\varnothing\notin V\) and \([m]\in V\); neither properness nor self-duality is required.
For positive integer constituency sizes \(n_i\), let \(N=\sum_i n_i\). All citizens vote independently, each voting yes with probability \(1/2\); each constituency's delegate follows strict simple majority, voting no on a tie. The council accepts precisely when its yes-delegate coalition belongs to \(V\).
If \(Y\) citizens vote yes and \(A\) citizens agree with the council outcome, define \(\Delta=\mathbb E[\max\{Y,N-Y\}-A]\). For fixed \(m,\mu,V\), the objective is \(F_\mu(V)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\Delta/\sqrt N\) along sequences with \(n_i/N\to\mu_i\); include existence and sequence independence of this limit in the question.
For the optimal-value threshold problem, additionally input a nonnegative binary rational \(b\), and ask whether some council rule satisfies \(F_\mu(V)\leq b\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; use the ordinary Turing bit model.

Is the optimal-value threshold problem in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
What are all council rules minimizing \(F_\mu(V)\)?

For large finite constituencies, the verified asymptotic result prescribes delegate weights \(\sqrt{n_i}\) and quota half their total, or sufficiently slightly above it, to minimize mean majority deficit up to vanishing error; minimizers need not be unique. Result

The optimal-value threshold problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even when every mass and the threshold are terminating binary fractions. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in $m,\tau,L$ would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.

The limit exists and is independent of the constituency-size sequence. All minimizing rules use square-root weights: they accept every coalition above half the total weight, reject every coalition below half, and choose arbitrarily at equality.

The key additional ingredient beyond the notes is a construction that makes one expected absolute signed sum equal a computable baseline plus a positive multiple of the Partition solution probability.

1. Existence and formula for the limit

Fix $m$, positive masses $\mu_i$ summing to one, and an admissible council rule $V$. Define
$$ d_V(S)=2\mathbf 1_{\{S\in V\}}-1. $$

Represent each citizen’s vote by an independent uniform sign. Let $M_i$ be their sum in constituency $i$, and put
$$ M=\sum_iM_i=2Y-N. $$
Because constituency ties produce a no delegate, the yes-delegate coalition and council outcome are
$$ S_N=\{i:M_i>0\}, \qquad D_N=d_V(S_N). $$
The agreement count satisfies
$$ A=\frac{N+D_NM}{2}, $$
whereas the larger voting camp has size $(N+|M|)/2$. Thus
$$ \frac{\Delta}{\sqrt N} = \frac12\mathbb E\left[ \left|\frac M{\sqrt N}\right| - D_N\frac M{\sqrt N} \right]. $$

Consider any sequence with $N\to\infty$ and $n_i/N\to\mu_i$. Independence gives
$$ \mathbb E\exp\left(\mathrm i\sum_i t_i\frac{M_i}{\sqrt N}\right) = \prod_i\cos(t_i/\sqrt N)^{n_i}. $$
For fixed $t_1,\ldots,t_m$, the expansion
$$ \log\cos(t_i/\sqrt N) = -\frac{t_i^2}{2N}+O(N^{-2}) $$
shows that this characteristic function converges to
$$ \exp\left(-\frac12\sum_i\mu_it_i^2\right). $$
Indeed, the accumulated remainder is $O(\sum_i n_i/N^2)=O(N^{-1})$. Therefore
$$ (M_i/\sqrt N)_{i=1}^m \ \Longrightarrow\ (Z_i)_{i=1}^m, $$
where the $Z_i$ are independent centered Gaussians with variances $\mu_i$.

Define
$$ g_V(z)= \frac12\left( \left|\sum_i z_i\right| - d_V(\{i:z_i>0\})\sum_i z_i \right). $$
Its discontinuities are contained in the coordinate hyperplanes. Because every $\mu_i>0$, those hyperplanes have probability zero under the limiting Gaussian law. The continuous mapping theorem applies.

Moreover,
$$ 0\leq g_V((M_i/\sqrt N)_i)\leq |M|/\sqrt N, \qquad \mathbb E[M^2/N]=1. $$
The resulting uniform second-moment bound implies uniform integrability. Consequently, expectations converge:
$$ F_\mu(V)=\mathbb E[g_V(Z)]. $$

Write
$$ c_i=\sqrt{\mu_i}, \qquad h(S)=\sum_{i\in S}c_i-\sum_{i\notin S}c_i. $$
The Gaussian sign coalition is uniform over all $2^m$ coalitions, and
$$ \mathbb E\left[\sum_iZ_i\ \middle|\ \{i:Z_i>0\}=S\right] = \sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,h(S). $$
Also, $\sum_iZ_i$ is a centered Gaussian with variance $\sum_i\mu_i=1$, so its expected absolute value is $\sqrt{2/\pi}$. Substituting gives
$$ \boxed{ F_\mu(V)= \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}} \left( 1-2^{-m}\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}d_V(S)h(S) \right). } $$
This proves existence and sequence independence.

2. All minimizing council rules

For every coalition,
$$ d_V(S)h(S)\leq |h(S)|. $$
Equality requires $S$ to win when $h(S)>0$ and lose when $h(S)<0$. Either outcome gives equality when $h(S)=0$.

These pointwise choices satisfy the game constraints. If $S\subsetneq T$, positivity of the weights gives
$$ h(T)-h(S)=2\sum_{i\in T\setminus S}c_i>0. $$
Thus, if a coalition wins under the proposed choices, each strict superset has positive signed weight and also wins. Furthermore, $\varnothing$ has negative signed weight and $[m]$ has positive signed weight. Arbitrary choices on zero coalitions therefore produce monotone simple games.

Conversely, a wrong outcome at any nonzero coalition makes its summand strictly smaller than $|h(S)|$. No other summand can compensate, because each is bounded above by its own absolute value.

Hence the complete characterization is:

  • Accept every $S$ with $\sum_{i\in S}\sqrt{\mu_i}>\frac12\sum_i\sqrt{\mu_i}$.
  • Reject every $S$ with $\sum_{i\in S}\sqrt{\mu_i}<\frac12\sum_i\sqrt{\mu_i}$.
  • Choose either outcome independently for each coalition at equality.

For independent uniform signs $\varepsilon_i$, the optimal value is
$$ \boxed{ F_\mu^*= \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}} \left( 1-\mathbb E\left|\sum_i\sqrt{\mu_i}\,\varepsilon_i\right| \right). } $$
If there are $t$ zero coalitions, there are exactly $2^t$ minimizing rules. For $m=1$, the unique admissible rule has value zero.

Minimizers can be nonweighted

For four equal masses, all pairs have zero signed weight. Accept all coalitions of size at least three, reject all of size at most one, and accept exactly the pairs $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4\}$. This is a minimizer.

Suppose it admitted a weighted representation with acceptance at weight at least $q$. Its winning pairs would imply
$$ u_1+u_2+u_3+u_4\geq 2q. $$
Its losing pairs $\{1,3\}$ and $\{2,4\}$ would imply
$$ u_1+u_2+u_3+u_4<2q. $$
The contradiction excludes such a representation.

3. A signed-sum construction detecting Partition

We reduce from the standard $\mathrm{NP}$-complete Partition problem: given positive binary integers $w_1,\ldots,w_n$, decide whether some signs satisfy $\sum_iw_i\varepsilon_i=0$. See [Karp’s paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Set
$$ W=\sum_iw_i, \qquad R=\sum_iw_i^2. $$
Let $H=2^k$ be the least power of two strictly greater than $4W+4$. All signs below are independent and uniform. Define
$$ X=4\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\varepsilon_i, $$
$$ U=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}2^j\eta_j, $$
and
$$ T=(H+2)\delta_1+(H-1)\delta_2+(H-1)\delta_3. $$
Then
$$ X\in4\mathbb Z, \qquad |X|\leq4W<H-4. $$

The variable $U$ is uniform on
$$ \{-H+1,-H+3,\ldots,H-1\}. $$
To verify this, write $\eta_j=2\xi_j-1$. The binary expansion makes $\sum_j2^j\xi_j$ uniform on $\{0,\ldots,H-1\}$, and
$$ U=2\sum_j2^j\xi_j-(H-1). $$

We will prove
$$ \boxed{ \mathbb E|X+U+T| = \frac{3H}{2}+\frac{3R}{H}+\frac{3}{2H} + \frac{\Pr(X=0)}{2H}. } $$

3.1 Averaging over the uniform sum

Define $f(z)=\mathbb E|U+z|$. For every nonnegative even integer $z$,
$$ f(z)=z+\frac{(H-z)_+^2}{2H}, \qquad r_+=\max\{r,0\}. $$

For $z\geq H$, all possible values of $U+z$ are positive, so $f(z)=z$. For $0\leq z\leq H$, put $d=H-z$. The negative values of $U+z$ have absolute values
$$ 1,3,\ldots,d-1, $$
whose sum is $(d/2)^2$. Since $\mathbb E(U+z)=z$, replacing negative values by their absolute values adds twice this sum divided by $H$. This proves the formula.

The magnitude of $T$ has distribution
$$ \Pr(|T|=3H)=\frac14, \qquad \Pr(|T|=H+2)=\frac12, \qquad \Pr(|T|=H-4)=\frac14. $$
These probabilities follow by enumerating its eight sign choices. Conditional on any magnitude, its sign is uniform.

For fixed $x\in4\mathbb Z$ with $|x|<H-4$, averaging over $T=\pm t$ gives
$$ \frac{f(t+x)+f(t-x)}2. $$
Every argument here is positive and even. For $t=3H$, both arguments exceed $H$, so this average is $3H$. For $t=H+d$, where $d=2$ or $d=-4$, it is
$$ H+d+ \frac{(x-d)_+^2+(-x-d)_+^2}{4H}. $$
Combining the three magnitudes yields
$$ \mathbb E_{U,T}|x+U+T| = \frac{3H}{2}+\frac{B(x)}{16H}, $$
where
$$ B(x)= 2(x-2)_+^2+2(-x-2)_+^2 +(x+4)_+^2+(-x+4)_+^2. $$

This function is even. At zero, $B(0)=32$. For $x\geq4$,
$$ B(x)=2(x-2)^2+(x+4)^2=3x^2+24. $$
Evenness gives the same formula for $x\leq-4$. Since $x$ is a multiple of four, these cases exhaust all possibilities:
$$ B(x)=3x^2+24+8\mathbf 1_{\{x=0\}}. $$
Therefore
$$ \mathbb E_{U,T}|x+U+T| = \frac{3H}{2} + \frac{3x^2+24+8\mathbf 1_{\{x=0\}}}{16H}. $$

Finally, independence and zero means give $\mathbb E X^2=16R$. Averaging over $X$ proves the claimed identity.

4. Constructing one threshold instance

Let $a$ be the positive integer vector
$$ a= (4w_1,\ldots,4w_n,\, 1,2,\ldots,2^{k-1},\, H+2,H-1,H-1). $$
It has
$$ m=n+k+3 $$
coordinates. Define
$$ Q=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i^2, \qquad K_0=\frac{3H}{2}+\frac{3R}{H}+\frac{3}{2H}, \qquad p=\Pr\left(\sum_iw_i\varepsilon_i=0\right). $$
The identity above states
$$ \mathbb E\left|\sum_i a_i\varepsilon_i\right| = K_0+\frac{p}{2H}. $$

First consider the auxiliary rational masses
$$ \mu_i=\frac{a_i^2}{Q}. $$
Their optimal value is
$$ F_\mu^*=f_0-\frac{p}{2H\sqrt{2\pi Q}}, $$
where the baseline
$$ f_0= \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}} \left(1-\frac{K_0}{\sqrt Q}\right) $$
is computable from the Partition input without knowing $p$.

4.1 A separation bound

The sum of the entries of $a$ is
$$ \sum_i a_i=4W+4H-1<5H. $$
Consequently, $\sqrt Q\leq\sum_i a_i<5H$. Using $\sqrt{2\pi}<3$, whenever a partition exists we have
$$ p\geq2^{-n}, $$
and hence
$$ f_0-F_\mu^* = \frac{p}{2H\sqrt{2\pi Q}} > \frac{2^{-n}}{30H^2}. $$
Define the dyadic rational
$$ g=\frac{2^{-n}}{32H^2}. $$
Thus
$$ p=0\ \Longrightarrow\ F_\mu^*=f_0, $$
whereas
$$ p>0\ \Longrightarrow\ F_\mu^*\leq f_0-g. $$

We also need a nonnegative threshold. Direct calculation gives
$$ Q=16R+\frac{10H^2+17}{3}. $$
Because $R\leq W^2<H^2/16$ and $H\geq8$,
$$ \frac{K_0}{H} < \frac32+\frac3{16}+\frac3{128} < \frac74, $$
while
$$ \frac{\sqrt Q}{H}>\frac95. $$
It follows that $K_0/\sqrt Q<35/36$. Therefore
$$ f_0>\frac1{108}, $$
using $1/\sqrt{2\pi}>1/3$.

4.2 A dyadic threshold

Compute a dyadic approximation $\widehat f_0$ satisfying
$$ |\widehat f_0-f_0|\leq g/8, $$
and set
$$ b=\widehat f_0-g/2. $$
Then
$$ f_0-\frac{5g}{8}\leq b\leq f_0-\frac{3g}{8}. $$
Since $n\geq1$, $H\geq8$, and $g\leq1/4096$, the lower bound on $f_0$ proves $b>0$.

This approximation requires only polynomial-time computation. Square roots can be enclosed by rational bisection. For $\pi$, use
$$ \pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239). $$
The identity follows from the tangent addition formula; in particular, the tangent of $4\arctan(1/5)-\arctan(1/239)$ is one, and the angle lies in $(0,\pi/2)$.

For either argument, the truncated series
$$ \arctan(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\frac{(-1)^jx^{2j+1}}{2j+1} +\text{remainder} $$
has remainder of absolute value at most $x^{2s+1}/(2s+1)$. Thus a polynomial number of terms gives the required certified precision. Rational interval arithmetic applied to the formula for $f_0$ then produces $\widehat f_0$. The precision needed is $O(\log H+\log(1/g))$ bits.

4.3 Dyadic masses

To ensure that the actual output uses terminating binary fractions, choose a power of two $D$ with
$$ D>2Q, \qquad D>(16m/g)^2. $$
Output
$$ \widetilde\mu_i=\frac{\lfloor D\mu_i\rfloor}{D} \quad (i<m), \qquad \widetilde\mu_m=1-\sum_{i<m}\widetilde\mu_i. $$
Every original mass is at least $1/Q$. The condition $D>2Q$ therefore makes every rounded mass positive. The last mass is at least its original value, and the masses sum exactly to one.

Their total error satisfies
$$ \sum_i|\widetilde\mu_i-\mu_i|<\frac{2m}{D}. $$
The optimal-value formula, the triangle inequality, and
$$ |\sqrt x-\sqrt y|\leq\sqrt{|x-y|} $$
give
$$ \begin{aligned} |F_{\widetilde\mu}^*-F_\mu^*| &\leq \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}} \sum_i|\sqrt{\widetilde\mu_i}-\sqrt{\mu_i}|\\ &\leq \frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}} \sqrt{m\sum_i|\widetilde\mu_i-\mu_i|}\\ &< \frac{m}{\sqrt D} < \frac g{16}. \end{aligned} $$
The second inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz; the penultimate inequality uses $\pi>1$.

4.4 Equivalence and bit complexity

The reduction outputs the single instance $(\widetilde\mu,b)$.

If no partition exists, then
$$ F_{\widetilde\mu}^* \geq f_0-\frac g{16} > f_0-\frac{3g}{8} \geq b. $$
If a partition exists, then
$$ F_{\widetilde\mu}^* \leq f_0-g+\frac g{16} = f_0-\frac{15g}{16} < f_0-\frac{5g}{8} \leq b. $$
Because Section 2 proves that an optimal admissible rule exists, these inequalities establish
$$ \boxed{ \text{Partition instance is yes} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists V:\ F_{\widetilde\mu}(V)\leq b. } $$

All construction costs are polynomial in the Partition input length:

  • $k=\log_2H=O(\log W)$, so $m=n+k+3$ is polynomial.
  • Every entry of $a$, and $Q$, has polynomial bit length.
  • $\log(1/g)=n+5+2k$.
  • A suitable $D$ has bit length

$O(\log Q+\log m+\log(1/g))$.

  • All output masses and $b$ have polynomial bit length, and their arithmetic and certified approximation require polynomial time.

Each coordinate represents a distinct constituency role, including coordinates with equal masses. Thus the support size is exactly $\tau=m$.

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction with dyadic masses and a dyadic nonnegative threshold. It closes the hardness gap identified in the notes.
