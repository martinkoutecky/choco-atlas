Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive masses, each encoded as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(\tau=2\), with \(\mu_1+\mu_2=1\).
Coalitions are all real mass vectors in \(B=[0,\mu_1]\times[0,\mu_2]\). The winning region \(W\subseteq B\) is given by \(m\) weak linear inequalities with binary rational coefficients; \(L\) is the total input bit length, including masses and costs.
Promise that \((0,0)\notin W\), \((\mu_1,\mu_2)\in W\), and \(W=\{x\in B:a_1x_1+a_2x_2\ge b\}\) for some \(a_1>a_2>0\) in \(\mathbb Z\) and \(b\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\).
Neither type is null: for each coordinate, increasing only that coordinate can turn a losing coalition into a winning one.
Define \(y\preceq x\) by \(y_1\le x_1\) and \(y_1+y_2\le x_1+x_2\). A winning point is shift-minimal if no distinct winning point precedes it; assume at least two shift-minimal winning points.
Given \(c_1,c_2,c_3\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), not all zero, find \(w_1,w_2\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) with \(w_1>w_2\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) minimizing \(c_1w_1+c_2w_2+c_3\theta\), subject to \(x\in W\iff w_1x_1+w_2x_2\ge\theta\) for every \(x\in B\); equality is winning.

Result

The exact optimization problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time. In fact, there is a unique optimum: recover the normal vector of the winning boundary, reduce it to a primitive positive integer pair, and scale the threshold accordingly.

The proof below uses the continuous geometry directly.

1. The boundary meets the interior of the box

Write \(\mu=(\mu_1,\mu_2)\). By the promise, there exist \(a_1>a_2>0\) and \(b>0\) such that

\(W=\{x\in B:a_1x_1+a_2x_2\ge b\}\).

Set \(A=a_1\mu_1+a_2\mu_2\). Since \(\mu\) is winning, \(b\le A\).

If \(b=A\), positivity of both coefficients implies that the only winning point is \(\mu\): every distinct \(x\in B\) has at least one coordinate strictly smaller than the corresponding coordinate of \(\mu\), hence \(a_1x_1+a_2x_2<A\). Thus there would be exactly one shift-minimal winning point, contradicting the promise.

Consequently,

\(0<b<A\).

Both masses are positive because both types belong to the support. Therefore the point

\(p=(b/A)\mu\)

lies in the interior of \(B\) and satisfies \(a_1p_1+a_2p_2=b\). The winning boundary thus contains a nontrivial line segment inside \(B\).

2. Recover a boundary inequality from the input

Orient every input inequality as

\(r_{i1}x_1+r_{i2}x_2\ge q_i,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\),

where all coefficients are rational. Define

\(s_i=r_{i1}\mu_1+r_{i2}\mu_2\).

Since \(\mu\in W\), every row satisfies \(s_i\ge q_i\).

On the diagonal \(x=t\mu\), with \(t\in[0,1]\), the row becomes \(ts_i\ge q_i\). If \(s_i\le0\), then \(ts_i\ge s_i\ge q_i\), so this row imposes no restriction on this diagonal interval. If \(s_i>0\), it requires \(t\ge q_i/s_i\).

It follows that

\(t\mu\in W\iff t\ge t_*, \qquad t_*=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{q_i/s_i:s_i>0\}\bigr)\).

The promised halfspace description also gives \(t\mu\in W\iff t\ge b/A\). Hence

\(t_*=b/A\in(0,1)\).

Choose a row attaining this positive maximum. Denote its normal by \(r=(r_1,r_2)\), its right-hand side by \(q\), and its diagonal coefficient by \(s=r\cdot\mu>0\). At \(p=t_*\mu\), this row is tight:

\(r\cdot p=q\).

We now prove that this row defines the actual winning halfspace, rather than merely some redundant supporting inequality.

Let \(d=(a_2,-a_1)\). Then \(a\cdot d=0\). Since \(p\) is interior to \(B\), for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both \(p+\varepsilon d\) and \(p-\varepsilon d\) belong to \(B\). Both lie on the promised winning boundary, so both satisfy the selected row. Its tightness at \(p\) gives

\(\varepsilon r\cdot d\ge0 \quad\text{and}\quad -\varepsilon r\cdot d\ge0\).

Thus \(r\cdot d=0\), which in two dimensions implies \(r=\lambda a\). Moreover,

\(\lambda=s/A>0, \qquad q=r\cdot p=\lambda b\).

Therefore

\(r_1>r_2>0, \qquad W=\{x\in B:r_1x_1+r_2x_2\ge q\}\).

This recovers the exact supporting inequality using rational arithmetic on the input.

3. Construct the primitive integer representation

Let \(D\) be the least common multiple of the positive denominators of \(r_1\) and \(r_2\), and define

\(v_1=Dr_1,\qquad v_2=Dr_2,\qquad g=\gcd(v_1,v_2)\).

Set

\(u_1=v_1/g,\qquad u_2=v_2/g,\qquad \theta_*=Dq/g\).

Then \(u_1>u_2>0\) are integers, \(\gcd(u_1,u_2)=1\), and \(\theta_*>0\) is rational. Since \(r=(g/D)u\), we have

\(W=\{x\in B:u_1x_1+u_2x_2\ge\theta_*\}\).

Thus \((u_1,u_2,\theta_*)\) is feasible.

4. Characterize every feasible representation

Suppose \((w_1,w_2,\theta)\) is any feasible representation, with \(w_1>w_2>0\) integral.

For any \(z\) on the winning boundary inside the interior of \(B\), feasibility gives \(w\cdot z\ge\theta\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), the point \(z-\varepsilon u\) remains in \(B\), and

\(u\cdot(z-\varepsilon u) =\theta_*-\varepsilon(u_1^2+u_2^2)<\theta_*\).

It is therefore losing, so \(w\cdot(z-\varepsilon u)<\theta\). Taking the limit as \(\varepsilon\) decreases to zero yields \(w\cdot z\le\theta\). Consequently,

\(w\cdot z=\theta\)

at every interior boundary point.

Take two such points whose difference is a nonzero multiple of \((u_2,-u_1)\); they exist by Section 1. Subtracting their equalities gives

\(w_1u_2-w_2u_1=0\).

Thus \(w=ku\) for some \(k>0\), and evaluating at a boundary point gives \(\theta=k\theta_*\).

Finally, \(k\) must be an integer. Since \(\gcd(u_1,u_2)=1\), Bézout’s identity supplies integers \(h_1,h_2\) with \(h_1u_1+h_2u_2=1\). Therefore

\(k=h_1w_1+h_2w_2\in\mathbb Z\).

Conversely, every positive integer scaling preserves the winning inequality. Hence the feasible representations are exactly

\(\{(ku_1,ku_2,k\theta_*):k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\}\).

5. Optimality and bit complexity

Define

\(C=c_1u_1+c_2u_2+c_3\theta_*\).

All three coordinates are positive, while the costs are nonnegative and not all zero. Therefore \(C>0\). The objective at the representation indexed by \(k\) is \(kC\), uniquely minimized at \(k=1\).

The unique optimum is consequently

\((w_1,w_2,\theta)=(u_1,u_2,\theta_*)\),

with optimal value \(C\).

For the running time:

  • Computing the \(s_i\) and selecting the maximum \(q_i/s_i\) requires a number of exact rational operations linear in \(m\).
  • Each such operation involves only a fixed number of input rationals, so intermediate numbers have bit length \(O(L)\). Rational comparisons use cross multiplication.
  • Computing \(D\), \(g\), the output weights, and the threshold requires integer arithmetic and gcd computations on numbers of bit length \(O(L)\), all executable in polynomial time.

Thus the algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\), and therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) for the stipulated \(\tau=2\). The output also has polynomial bit length, even when the numerical weights are large.

The continuous boundary is the decisive feature: it forces all exact representations to be proportional. The discrete minimum-representation results of [Freixas–Kurz](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.0868) are not needed for this proof.
