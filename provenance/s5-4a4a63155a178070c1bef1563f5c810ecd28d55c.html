The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(k\) behaviors, their rational costs and utilities, an intent weight, a finite type set \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), a rational block contact matrix \(\omega_{tq}\), and type-specific resources and adoption thresholds, choose seed masses \(z_{t,i}\geq 0\) subject to \(\sum_i z_{t,i}\leq\mu_t\) and fixed quotas \(\sum_t z_{t,i}=\beta_i\), with each seeded unit assigned one behavior and topped up when needed. Under the deterministic block-mass version of the paper’s sticky, threshold-based, utility-maximizing adoption process, maximize the final mass holding at least one behavior; determine whether an additive-\(\epsilon\) optimum can be computed in time polynomial in \(k\), \(|T|\), \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(m=k\) is the number of behaviors, and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Input consists of rational costs and utilities \(c_i,u_i\in[0,1]\), intent weight \(w\in[0,1]\), resources \(r_t\in[0,1]\), fixed thresholds \(\theta_{t,i}\in[0,1]\), and contact coefficients \(\omega_{tq}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_q\omega_{tq}\mu_q\leq1\). If \(a_{q,i}\) is the mass of type \(q\) holding behavior \(i\), the signal and payoff are \(\ell_{t,i}=\sum_q\omega_{tq}a_{q,i}\) and \(p_{t,i}=wu_i+(1-w)\ell_{t,i}\).
Given rational quotas \(\beta_i\geq0\) with \(\sum_i\beta_i\leq1\), choose disjoint seed masses \(z_{t,i}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_i z_{t,i}\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_tz_{t,i}=\beta_i\). Initially, cohort \((t,i)\) has mass \(z_{t,i}\), holds only behavior \(i\), and has resource \(\max\{r_t,c_i\}\); the remaining cohort of type \(t\) holds nothing and has resource \(r_t\). Top-ups incur no additional constraint or objective cost.
At each synchronous round, every cohort retains its held set \(H\) and adds a subset \(B\subseteq\{i\notin H:\ell_{t,i}\geq\theta_{t,i}\}\) maximizing \(\sum_{i\in B}p_{t,i}\), subject to \(\sum_{i\in H\cup B}c_i\leq R\), where \(R\) is that cohort's resource. Break payoff ties by larger cardinality, then lexicographically by increasing behavior indices. All units in a cohort make the same choice; seeds may subsequently adopt additional behaviors. Stop when no held set changes.
Let \(F(z)\) be the final mass holding at least one behavior, and let \(\mathrm{OPT}=\sup_zF(z)\) over feasible seed allocations.

For rational \(0<\epsilon<1\), can a feasible allocation with \(F(z)\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m=k,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\), or is this additive approximation problem NP-hard?

The published discrete results assert submodularity of expected final participation under independent uniform adoption thresholds, and NP-hardness of participation maximization even with sticky adoption. [Sarkar and Sundaram](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0162014).
No result settling the precisely defined deterministic block-mass problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one so that additive error has a fixed scale; identified the generic size parameter m with the number k of behaviors.
  • Interpreted the contact matrix as per-unit influence coefficients, with signal equal to the coefficient-weighted adopter masses and total incoming influence at most one.
  • Used supplied type-specific thresholds as fixed deterministic inputs rather than drawing new uniform thresholds.
  • Specified homogeneous cohorts according to type and initial seed assignment, synchronous updates, and sticky adoption with costs charged against remaining resources.
  • Resolved unspecified utility ties by larger cardinality and then lexicographic order, including zero-payoff ties.
  • Made top-ups permanent resource increases to the assigned behavior's cost, with no separate top-up budget, and allowed seeds to adopt further behaviors.
  • Required nonnegative feasible quotas and retained the original closed unit-interval domains for costs, utilities, resources, thresholds, and intent weight.
  • Defined the optimum as a supremum because attainment was unspecified for the discontinuous deterministic objective; additive approximation means finding a feasible allocation within epsilon of that supremum.

Result

The additive approximation problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with one behavior, fixed error \(\epsilon=1/4\), and at most three adoption rounds. Consequently, the requested polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The reduction below allows arbitrary fractional seed allocations in every type. All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and all resources, costs, utilities, and the intent weight equal one.

1. Source problem and parameters

We reduce from SET COVER: given a universe \(U\) with \(|U|=d\), sets \(S_1,\ldots,S_n\subseteq U\), and a budget \(b\), decide whether at most \(b\) sets cover \(U\). This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. See [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

We may restrict to \(d,n\geq1\) and \(1\leq b\leq n\): instances outside these conditions are decidable directly.

Let \(D,N,B\) be the smallest powers of two satisfying \(D\geq d\), \(N\geq n\), and \(B\geq b\). Define

\[ h=\frac{1}{4D},\qquad g=\frac{h}{8N},\qquad s=\frac{g}{4B},\qquad \beta=bs. \]

The following bounds will be used:

\[ dh\leq\frac14,\qquad ng\leq\frac h8,\qquad ns\leq\frac{ng}{4},\qquad \beta\leq\frac g4\leq\frac h{32}. \]

In particular,

\[ \beta<g<h,\qquad n(g+s)\leq\frac{5h}{32}\leq\frac5{128}. \]

2. Construction and validity

There is one behavior, with

\[ m=k=1,\qquad c_1=u_1=w=1. \]

Every type has resource \(r_t=1\). Thus an unseeded cohort adopts the behavior exactly when its signal reaches its threshold: adoption is feasible and has payoff one, whereas choosing nothing has payoff zero. No tie-breaking issue arises.

Create the following types:

| Types | Mass | Threshold | Nonzero incoming contact coefficients |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Selector \(A_j\), for each \(j\) | \(s\) | \(1\) | None |
| Gate \(G_j\), for each \(j\) | \(g\) | \(1\) | \(\omega_{G_j,A_j}=1/s\) |
| Element \(E_e\), for each \(e\in U\) | \(h\) | \(1/N\) | \(\omega_{E_e,G_j}=1/(Ng)\) when \(e\in S_j\) |
| Reward type \(Q\) | \(\mu_Q=1-dh-n(g+s)\) | \(d/D\) | \(\omega_{Q,E_e}=1/(Dh)\) for every \(e\in U\) |

All unspecified coefficients are zero. The sole seed quota is \(\beta_1=\beta\).

The support size is \(\tau=2n+d+1\). All masses sum to one, and

\[ \mu_Q\geq1-\frac14-\frac5{128}=\frac{91}{128}>0. \]

Every threshold lies in the required interval. The contact normalization holds separately for every type:

  • A selector has incoming weighted mass zero.
  • A gate has incoming weighted mass \((1/s)s=1\).
  • An element has incoming weighted mass \(|\{j:e\in S_j\}|/N\leq n/N\leq1\).
  • The reward type has incoming weighted mass \(dh/(Dh)=d/D\leq1\).

The coefficients larger than one are therefore permitted by the stated normalization.

All constructed numbers are dyadic rationals. Explicitly,

\[ h=\frac1{4D},\qquad g=\frac1{32DN},\qquad s=\frac1{128DNB}. \]

Their bit lengths, and the total constructed input length, are polynomial in the SET COVER input length. A feasible allocation always exists: the entire quota can be placed in \(Q\), since \(\beta<\mu_Q\).

3. Dynamics for every feasible fractional allocation

Fix any feasible allocation. Write \(z_t=z_{t,1}\), so that

\[ 0\leq z_t\leq\mu_t,\qquad \sum_t z_t=\beta. \]

Define the indices of fully seeded selectors and their covered elements by

\[ J=\{j:z_{A_j}=s\},\qquad C(J)=\bigcup_{j\in J}S_j. \]

Since \(|J|s\leq\beta=bs\), we have \(|J|\leq b\).

Selectors and gates

Selectors receive zero signal and have threshold one. Their unseeded cohorts never adopt, so their holding masses remain \(z_{A_j}\).

Gate \(G_j\) consequently receives the constant signal \(z_{A_j}/s\). Because \(z_{A_j}\leq s\), its threshold is met exactly when \(j\in J\). After the first round, its holding mass is therefore

\[ a_{G_j,1}= \begin{cases} g,&j\in J,\\ z_{G_j},&j\notin J. \end{cases} \]

These masses never change again.

Elements

The threshold condition for element \(E_e\) is exactly

\[ \sum_{j:e\in S_j}a_{G_j,1}\geq g. \]

Initially, the sum on the left is at most the entire seed quota \(\beta<g\). Thus no unseeded element cohort adopts in the first round.

After the gates update:

  • If \(e\in C(J)\), one incoming gate has holding mass \(g\), so the element adopts in the second round.
  • If \(e\notin C(J)\), every incoming gate is inactive. Their total holding mass is at most \(\sum_j z_{G_j}\leq\beta<g\), so the element never adopts.

Hence the final element masses are

\[ a_{E_e,1}= \begin{cases} h,&e\in C(J),\\ z_{E_e},&e\notin C(J). \end{cases} \]

This proves that distributing fractional seeds among inactive gates cannot activate an uncovered element.

Reward type

The reward threshold is equivalent to

\[ \frac{1}{Dh}\sum_{e\in U}a_{E_e,1}\geq\frac dD \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{e\in U}a_{E_e,1}\geq dh. \]

Each element holds at most mass \(h\). Therefore this condition holds exactly when every element holds its full mass.

If \(C(J)=U\), every element is full after round two, and \(Q\) adopts in round three. If \(C(J)\neq U\), an uncovered element holds only \(z_{E_e}\leq\beta<h\), so \(Q\)'s unseeded cohort never adopts.

The reward type has no outgoing contacts. Consequently, all held sets are final after at most three adoption rounds.

Exact participation formula

The initial participating mass is \(\beta\). Each subsequently activated cohort contributes its previously unseeded mass. Thus, for every feasible allocation,

\[ F(z)= \beta+ \sum_{j\in J}(g-z_{G_j})+ \sum_{e\in C(J)}(h-z_{E_e})+ \mathbf{1}_{\{C(J)=U\}}(\mu_Q-z_Q). \]

In particular, this formula accounts for seeds placed directly in gates, elements, or the reward type.

4. Constant participation gap

YES instances

Suppose a cover uses \(q\leq b\) sets. Fully seed their selectors, using mass \(qs\), and place the remaining mass \(\beta-qs\) in \(Q\). This is feasible because the remainder is nonnegative and smaller than \(\mu_Q\).

Every element and the entire reward type eventually participate. Therefore

\[ \mathrm{OPT}\geq dh+\mu_Q =1-n(g+s) \geq\frac{123}{128}. \]

This is an explicit feasible witness; no attainment assumption about the supremum is needed.

NO instances

For every feasible allocation, \(|J|\leq b\). Hence \(C(J)\neq U\), and the reward type gains no additional participating mass.

The exact formula gives

\[ F(z)\leq\beta+ng+(d-1)h \leq\beta+ng+dh \leq\frac{5h}{32}+\frac14 \leq\frac{37}{128}. \]

Because this bound holds for every feasible allocation, it also bounds the supremum:

\[ \mathrm{OPT}\leq\frac{37}{128}. \]

5. Hardness of additive approximation

Suppose an algorithm always returns a feasible allocation satisfying \(F(z)\geq\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon\), in time polynomial in the parameters specified in the question.

Run it on the constructed instance with the fixed value \(\epsilon=1/4\).

On a YES instance, its output satisfies

\[ F(z)\geq\frac{123}{128}-\frac14 =\frac{91}{128}>\frac12. \]

On a NO instance, every feasible output satisfies

\[ F(z)\leq\frac{37}{128}<\frac12. \]

The output's final participation can be computed in polynomial time: the constructed instance has one behavior and at most three adoption rounds, or one can use the exact formula above. With the usual binary encoding of rational allocations, all required arithmetic and comparisons are polynomial-time operations.

Comparing the returned participation with \(1/2\) therefore decides SET COVER in polynomial time. This proves the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

The reduction uses precisely the deterministic cohort dynamics and normalization in the question, including unrestricted fractional seeding. It settles the stated problem already for fixed approximation error.
