The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), \(k\), a listed support \(T\) with positive rational masses \(w_t\) summing to \(1\), binary costs \(d(t,c)\), a threshold \(B\), and a scheme \(R\in\{\mathrm{CC},\mathrm{Monroe}\}\), decide whether there are a committee \(S\subseteq C\), \(|S|=k\), and assigned masses \(x_{tc}\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_{c\in S}x_{tc}=w_t\) for each \(t\), and \(\sum_{t\in T,c\in S}d(t,c)x_{tc}\le B\). For Monroe also require \(\sum_{t\in T}x_{tc}=1/k\) for every \(c\in S\); CC has no such quota.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(w_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}w_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains candidates \(C\), \(m=|C|\ge1\), an integer \(1\le k\le m\), arbitrary misrepresentation costs \(d:T\times C\to\{0,1\}\), a nonnegative binary rational threshold \(B\), and a scheme \(R\in\{\mathrm{CC},\mathrm{Monroe}\}\). Each type is specified by its cost vector; no ranking restrictions apply.
Decide whether there exist a committee \(S\subseteq C\), \(|S|=k\), and divisible assigned masses \(x_{tc}\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) for \(t\in T,c\in S\), satisfying \(\sum_{c\in S}x_{tc}=w_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{c\in S}d(t,c)x_{tc}\le B\).
For Monroe additionally require \(\sum_{t\in T}x_{tc}=1/k\) for every \(c\in S\); Chamberlin–Courant (CC) imposes no quota. Committee selection is discrete, and feasibility is existential, so no tie-breaking is needed. Let \(L\) denote the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

For each scheme separately, is this continuous winner-determination problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, when \(k\) is part of the input?

Discrete winner determination is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for both schemes even with binary costs, and is polynomial-time solvable for constant \(k\) ([Procaccia, Rosenschein and Zohar](https://www.avivz.net/pubs/12/scw_fpr_final.pdf)).
Fractional voter assignments have been studied ([Potthoff and Brams](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0951692898010002001)); the search identified no complexity classification explicitly covering the normalized rational-support problem stated here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified the admissible committee-size domain as 1 ≤ k ≤ m, with a nonempty candidate set and k part of the input.
  • Specified B as a nonnegative binary rational, allowing zero as required by the zero-misrepresentation decision case.
  • Specified types by unrestricted binary cost vectors, following the discrete model rather than imposing ranking-derived costs.
  • Made assigned masses nonnegative real quantities and retained exact Monroe quotas of 1/k, corresponding to the divisible-electorate case.
  • Defined L as total input bit length and made tie-breaking irrelevant through existential feasibility.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Classification

Both continuous winner-determination problems are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when \(k\) is part of the input. Hardness already holds at threshold \(B=0\). Thus, either problem is polynomial-time solvable only if \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof below handles normalized rational masses, divisible assignments, and support types specified by distinct cost vectors. Rational numbers are understood to have numerators and denominators encoded in binary.

1. Polynomial-time verification of a fixed committee

Fix a committee \(S\subseteq C\) with \(|S|=k\).

Chamberlin–Courant

Define \(a_t=\min_{c\in S}d(t,c)\). Every feasible assignment satisfies

\[ \sum_{c\in S}d(t,c)x_{tc}\ge a_t\sum_{c\in S}x_{tc}=a_tw_t. \]

Equality is attainable by assigning all of type \(t\)'s mass to a candidate attaining the minimum. Consequently, the optimal committee cost is

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{CC}}(S) =\sum_{t\in T}w_t\min_{c\in S}d(t,c) =\sum_{\substack{t\in T:\\ d(t,c)=1\text{ for all }c\in S}}w_t. \]

Computing this rational sum and comparing it with \(B\) takes polynomial time in \(\tau,k,L\).

Monroe

Construct a directed network with source \(s\), one vertex for each type, one vertex for each candidate in \(S\), and sink \(z\). Its arcs and capacities are:

  • \(s\to t\), of capacity \(w_t\);
  • \(t\to c\), of capacity \(1\), whenever \(d(t,c)=0\);
  • \(c\to z\), of capacity \(1/k\).

Let \(F(S)\) be the maximum flow value. We prove the exact formula

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{Monroe}}(S)=1-F(S). \]

First, take any feasible Monroe assignment and let \(Z\) be its total mass on zero-cost pairs. Its zero-cost entries form a feasible flow: their type totals are at most \(w_t\), and their candidate totals are at most \(1/k\). Hence \(Z\le F(S)\). Since total assigned mass is \(1\) and costs are binary, the assignment cost is \(1-Z\ge1-F(S)\).

Conversely, take a maximum flow and write \(f_{tc}\) for its type-to-candidate entries, setting \(f_{tc}=0\) on missing arcs. Define residual masses

\[ r_t=w_t-\sum_{c\in S}f_{tc}, \qquad s_c=\frac1k-\sum_{t\in T}f_{tc}. \]

These are nonnegative, and both total \(H=1-F(S)\).

If \(H=0\), the flow itself is a feasible assignment of cost zero. If \(H>0\), extend it by setting

\[ x_{tc}=f_{tc}+\frac{r_ts_c}{H}. \]

The added entries have row totals \(r_t\) and column totals \(s_c\), so this is a feasible Monroe assignment.

Moreover, if \(r_t>0\), \(s_c>0\), and \(d(t,c)=0\), the maximum flow could be increased along \(s\to t\to c\to z\). Indeed, its first and last arcs have positive residual capacity, while \(f_{tc}<w_t\le1\) gives positive residual capacity on the middle arc. This contradicts maximality. Thus every positive added entry has cost \(1\), and the extension has cost exactly \(H=1-F(S)\). The formula follows.

The network has \(\tau+k+2\) vertices and at most \(\tau k+\tau+k\) arcs. Its rational capacities can be scaled to integers of polynomial bit length, so maximum flow is computable in polynomial time.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

For either scheme, a certificate need contain only the \(k\) selected candidate indices. The verifier computes the fixed committee's optimal cost as above and compares it with \(B\). Therefore both decision problems belong to \(\mathrm{NP}\); no certificate consisting of arbitrary real assignment entries is needed.

2. A reduction proving hardness for both schemes

We reduce from the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete Exact Cover by 3-Sets problem: given a universe \(U\) of size \(3q\), with \(q\ge1\), and a family \(\mathcal A\) of three-element subsets of \(U\), decide whether \(q\) members partition \(U\). This source problem and its complexity are stated in [Felsner's dissertation, Section 2.6](https://page.math.tu-berlin.de/~felsner/Paper/diss.pdf).

Create one candidate \(c_A\) for every \(A\in\mathcal A\), and set \(k=q\) and \(B=0\).

If \(|\mathcal A|<q\), the source instance is necessarily negative. In that case output the fixed negative election with one type of mass \(1\), one candidate, \(k=1\), cost \(1\), and threshold \(0\). This is negative under either scheme. Hence assume \(q\le|\mathcal A|\), ensuring the required condition \(1\le k\le m\).

Constructing an actual support

For each element \(u\in U\), define its binary cost vector by

\[ d_u(c_A)= \begin{cases} 0,&u\in A,\\ 1,&u\notin A. \end{cases} \]

Elements may have identical vectors. To obtain a support with distinct types, partition \(U\) into equivalence classes \(G\) of identical vectors. For each class create one type \(t_G\), with that common cost vector and mass

\[ w_{t_G}=\frac{|G|}{3q}. \]

Every mass is positive, and

\[ \sum_Gw_{t_G}=\frac{|U|}{3q}=1. \]

The support size is \(\tau\le3q\), and each mass has numerator and denominator of bit length \(O(\log q)\). Constructing and grouping the vectors takes polynomial time in the source input size. Thus this is a polynomial reduction to the stated support representation.

An exact cover gives a feasible committee

Suppose \(\mathcal A'\subseteq\mathcal A\) is an exact cover with \(|\mathcal A'|=q\). Select \(S=\{c_A:A\in\mathcal A'\}\), and define

\[ x_{t_G,c_A}=\frac{|G\cap A|}{3q} \qquad(A\in\mathcal A'). \]

Because the selected sets partition \(U\), the row total for type \(t_G\) is \(|G|/(3q)=w_{t_G}\).

Every positive entry has zero cost: if \(G\cap A\) is nonempty, its elements' common vector has cost zero at \(c_A\). Therefore the total cost is \(0\), establishing CC feasibility.

For Monroe, each selected candidate also receives exactly

\[ \sum_Gx_{t_G,c_A}=\frac{|A|}{3q}=\frac{3}{3q}=\frac1q=\frac1k. \]

Thus the same assignment establishes Monroe feasibility.

A feasible committee gives an exact cover

Conversely, suppose a committee \(S\) of size \(q\) admits a feasible assignment of cost at most \(B=0\), under either scheme.

All terms in the cost are nonnegative. Consequently, any entry \(x_{t_G,c_A}>0\) must have \(d(t_G,c_A)=0\). Each type has positive mass, so its row constraint guarantees at least one such positive entry. The common cost-vector definition then implies that every element of \(G\) belongs to at least one selected set.

Hence the \(q\) selected triples cover \(U\). Let \(r(u)\) count the selected triples containing \(u\). Coverage gives \(r(u)\ge1\), while

\[ \sum_{u\in U}r(u) =\sum_{c_A\in S}|A| =3q =|U|. \]

These equalities force \(r(u)=1\) for every \(u\): any larger value would make the sum exceed \(|U|\). The selected triples therefore form an exact cover.

This implication uses only the row constraints and zero total cost, so it applies separately to both CC and Monroe. Together with the forward implication, it proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for each scheme.

3. Running times and binary encoding

For clarity, the fixed-committee algorithms yield explicit, non-optimized bit bounds.

Write each mass as \(w_t=a_t/b_t\), with positive integer denominator \(b_t\), and define

\[ D=k\prod_{t\in T}b_t. \]

Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Multiplying every Monroe network capacity by \(D\) gives integers: \(Dw_t\), \(D\), and \(D/k\), each of bit length \(O(L)\). Computing these integers takes \(O(L^3)\) bit operations using elementary arithmetic.

The Edmonds–Karp maximum-flow algorithm uses \(O(VE^2)\) elementary graph and arithmetic operations; its capacities here are finite nonnegative integers, as required. See [Edmonds and Karp's original paper](https://ftp.eecs.umich.edu/~pettie/matching/Edmonds-Karp-network-flow.pdf). Additions, comparisons, and residual-capacity updates cost \(O(L)\) bit operations here.

Consequently, exhaustive committee enumeration gives the following valid bounds:

  • CC: \(O\!\left(\binom{m}{k}\bigl[L^3+\tau kL\bigr]\right)\).
  • Monroe: \(O\!\left(\binom{m}{k}\bigl[L^3+(\tau+k+2)(\tau k+\tau+k)^2L\bigr]\right)\).

These bounds depend on the listed support size \(\tau\), without expanding rational masses into individual voters. For constant \(k\), \(\binom{m}{k}\le m^k\), so both bounds are polynomial.

Combining the reduction with polynomial verification proves the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for both schemes.
