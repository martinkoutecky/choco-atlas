The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite candidate set \(C\) and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete weak rankings, let \(R=\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\) and \(U_\mu=\bigcup_{r\in R}\operatorname{top}_r(C)\). Determine whether there is a linear order \(>\) of \(C\) satisfying the paper’s top-monotonicity conditions for every pair \(r,q\in R\) and every \(S\in\{C\}\cup\binom{U_\mu}{3}\), and construct such an order if one exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a nonempty finite candidate set, with \(m=|C|\). A society is given by its support: a list \(R\) of \(\tau\) distinct complete weak rankings, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu(r)\), where \(\sum_{r\in R}\mu(r)=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Ties are permitted.
For nonempty \(S\subseteq C\), define \(t_r(S)=\{x\in S:x\succeq_r a\text{ for every }a\in S\}\), \(U_\mu=\bigcup_{r\in R}t_r(C)\), and \(\mathcal S=\{C\}\cup\binom{U_\mu}{3}\).
A strict linear order \(>\) is admissible if, for every \(S\in\mathcal S\), every \(r,q\in R\), including \(r=q\), and every \(x\in t_r(S)\), \(y\in t_q(S)\), \(z\in S\), the condition \(x>y>z\) or \(z>y>x\) implies \(y\succeq_r z\) when \(z\in t_r(S)\cup t_q(S)\), and \(y\succ_r z\) otherwise.
The additional requirement that every \(t_r(C)\) be a finite union of closed axis intervals is automatic for finite \(C\).

Is deciding whether an admissible order exists polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard? In the polynomial-time case, can an admissible order also be constructed in polynomial time?

For finite lists of \(n\) agents with complete weak rankings, recognition and construction of a top-monotonic order are polynomial-time solvable, with an \(O(m^4n^2)\) running-time bound (Magiera and Faliszewski, 2019).
This discrete result allows ties and does not require every candidate to be ranked globally on top by some agent.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Require a nonempty candidate set so that top choices are defined; permit a single candidate.
  • Normalize the rational distribution to total mass one and list only distinct rankings with strictly positive mass.
  • Define L as the total bit length of the encoded input, including rankings and masses.
  • Explicitly include equal ranking pairs r=q and retain both the weak-comparison and strict-comparison branches of top-monotonicity.
  • Retain the finite-union-of-closed-intervals condition, noting that it is automatic on a finite candidate set.

Result. Recognition is polynomial-time solvable, and an admissible order can also be constructed in polynomial time. The reduction uses one agent per support ranking; the masses do not affect admissibility.

Reduction to a finite preference profile. Enumerate the support as \(R=\{r_1,\ldots,r_\tau\}\). Construct a profile \(P\) on candidate set \(C\), with agent set \(N=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), by defining

\(\displaystyle x\succeq_i y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\succeq_{r_i}y.\)

These are complete weak rankings, including precisely the original ties. For every nonempty \(S\subseteq C\), the definition of a top choice gives

\(\displaystyle t_i^P(S)=\{x\in S:x\succeq_i a\text{ for every }a\in S\}=t_{r_i}(S).\)

Consequently, the globally top-ranked candidates in \(P\) are exactly

\(\displaystyle T(P)=\bigcup_{i\in N}t_i^P(C)=\bigcup_{r\in R}t_r(C)=U_\mu.\)

Thus the family of subsets used in discrete top monotonicity is exactly \(\mathcal S\): the whole candidate set together with all three-element subsets of \(U_\mu\). This is the family in Definition 3 of Magiera and Faliszewski’s [conference paper](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0046.pdf).

Proof of equivalence. Fix any strict linear order \(>\) on \(C\). We show that it is admissible for the given society if and only if it is a top-monotonic order for \(P\).

The correspondence \(i\mapsto r_i\) is a bijection. Therefore, quantification over all ordered pairs of agents \(i,j\in N\) is exactly quantification over all ordered pairs of support rankings \(r,q\in R\), including equal pairs. For every such pair and every \(S\in\mathcal S\):

  • The possible choices of \(x\), \(y\), and \(z\) coincide, because \(t_i^P(S)=t_{r_i}(S)\).
  • The antecedent \(x>y>z\) or \(z>y>x\) is identical.
  • Membership of \(z\) in the union of the two top sets is identical.
  • The conclusions \(y\succeq_i z\) and \(y\succ_i z\) are respectively identical to \(y\succeq_{r_i}z\) and \(y\succ_{r_i}z\).

Hence every required implication is the same in the two problems. The remaining interval condition holds for every axis: each finite top set is the finite union of its singleton closed intervals \([x,x]\). This proves both directions of the equivalence.

In particular, the actual values of the positive masses impose no further conditions. Replacing them by any other positive masses summing to one preserves every admissible order.

Algorithm and complexity. Apply the discrete recognition-and-construction theorem supplied in the question to \(P\). Its hypotheses hold: the candidate set and agent list are finite, and every preference is a complete weak ranking. No hypothesis requires \(U_\mu=C\). The unrestricted recognition-and-construction conclusion also appears as Theorem 3 in the [conference paper](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0046.pdf).

If the algorithm rejects, the proved equivalence implies that no admissible order exists. If it returns an axis, that same axis is admissible for the original society.

The constructed profile has \(n=\tau\) agents. Therefore the supplied running-time bound becomes \(O(m^4\tau^2)\), with polynomial-time input processing in \(L\). Standard binary encodings of candidate indices and ranking positions give polynomial bit complexity in \(m,\tau,L\).

No replication according to the rational masses is necessary. Thus large binary-encoded denominators do not cause an expansion of the agent list.
