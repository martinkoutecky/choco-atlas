The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite metric space, a multiset of facility slots, an integer \(k\geq 3\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over client-location types, does there exist a feasible committee \(A\) such that \(\mathrm{SS}_{\mu}(A)\leq \rho\min_B\mathrm{SS}_{\mu}(B)\) and \(\mathrm{MS}_{\mu}(A)\leq \rho\min_B\mathrm{MS}_{\mu}(B)\)? Here \(\mathrm{SS}_{\mu}(A)=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_{a\in A}d(t,a)\), and \(\mathrm{MS}_{\mu}(A)=\max_{t\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}\sum_{a\in A}d(t,a)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input: a finite metric space \((M,d)\), given by its nonnegative binary-rational distance matrix, and an explicit list \(F\) of \(m\) facility slots, each located at a point of \(M\); several slots may share a location. Let \(3\leq k\leq m\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) client-location types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
A feasible committee selects exactly \(k\) distinct slots from \(F\). Write \(\ell(a)\) for slot \(a\)'s location; sums count every selected slot, including colocated slots.
Define \(\mathrm{SS}_{\mu}(A)=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_{a\in A}d(t,\ell(a))\) and \(\mathrm{MS}_{\mu}(A)=\max_{t\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}\sum_{a\in A}d(t,\ell(a))\).
For \(\rho\geq1\), simultaneous feasibility means \(\mathrm{SS}_{\mu}(A)\leq\rho\min_B\mathrm{SS}_{\mu}(B)\) and \(\mathrm{MS}_{\mu}(A)\leq\rho\min_B\mathrm{MS}_{\mu}(B)\), with both minima over feasible committees. These inequalities also define feasibility when an optimum is zero.
State any running times in \(m,\tau,|M|,k\) and the total input bit length \(L\).

What is the smallest universal simultaneous approximation factor \(\rho\geq1\) for which every such instance admits a feasible committee satisfying both inequalities?

For finite client lists, the universal factor is at least \((4+\sqrt7)/3\) and at most \(1+\sqrt{5/3}\); the lower bound concerns existence, without a computational restriction. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17063v1).
No separate result settling the sharp factor for rational client distributions was located.

Sharp universal factor

The smallest universal simultaneous approximation factor is
$$ \boxed{\rho_*=\frac{5+\sqrt{17}}4}. $$

The proof gives a universal upper bound and a matching limiting family with $k=3$, $m=5$, and $\tau=2$. Thus it closes the interval stated in the question. For comparison, the previously cited bounds appear in [Han–Anshelevich, Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17063v1#S4.SS1).

1. Notation and zero optima

For any set $D$ of slots, including sets of size other than $k$, define
$$ c_t(D)=\sum_{a\in D}d(t,\ell(a)), \qquad S(D)=\sum_t\mu_t c_t(D). $$
For a feasible committee, write
$$ Z(D)=\max_{t\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}c_t(D). $$
Let
$$ s=\min_{|D|=k}S(D), \qquad z=\min_{|D|=k}Z(D). $$

If $s=0$, an attaining committee has $d(t,\ell(a))=0$ for every support location $t$ and every selected slot $a$: all masses are positive, and all summands are nonnegative. That committee also has $Z(D)=0$. Conversely, a committee with $Z(D)=0$ has $S(D)=0$. Thus the two optima vanish together, and a common zero-cost committee satisfies both inequalities with factor $1$.

Henceforth assume $s,z>0$.

For each slot define its weighted cost
$$ w_a=\sum_t\mu_t d(t,\ell(a)). $$
Then $S(D)=\sum_{a\in D}w_a$. Choose a Sum-Sum optimal committee $O$ and a Max-Sum optimal committee $B$. Put
$$ x=\frac{S(B)}s. $$
Because a weighted average never exceeds the maximum,
$$ 1\leq x, \qquad S(B)\leq Z(B)=z, \qquad \frac{s}{z}\leq\frac1x. $$

All intersections and differences below concern distinct slots, so colocated slots cause no ambiguity.

2. A family of mixed committees

Set
$$ U=O\cap B,\qquad P=O\setminus U,\qquad Q=B\setminus U, \qquad p=|P|=|Q|, \qquad u=S(U). $$

Suppose $p>0$. For each integer $j$ with $1\leq j\leq p$, let:

  • $X_j$ consist of the $j$ cheapest slots in $P$, measured by $w_a$;
  • $Y_j$ consist of the $p-j$ cheapest slots in $Q$;
  • $R_j=Q\setminus Y_j$.

Define
$$ A_j=U\cup X_j\cup Y_j, \qquad \alpha=\frac jp. $$
These unions are disjoint and have exactly $k$ slots.

The sum of the cheapest $j$ among $p$ nonnegative numbers is at most $j/p$ times their total. Therefore
$$ S(X_j)\leq\alpha(s-u), \qquad S(Y_j)\leq(1-\alpha)(S(B)-u). $$
Consequently,
$$ S(A_j) \leq\alpha s+(1-\alpha)S(B), $$
and hence
$$ \frac{S(A_j)}s \leq x-\alpha(x-1). $$

To control Max-Sum, first observe that the triangle inequality, averaged over the society, gives
$$ d(\ell(a),\ell(b)) \leq \sum_t\mu_t\bigl(d(\ell(a),t)+d(t,\ell(b))\bigr) =w_a+w_b. $$

Since $|X_j|=|R_j|=j$, choose any bijection $\pi:X_j\to R_j$. For every support location $t$,
$$ d(t,\ell(a)) \leq d(t,\ell(\pi(a)))+w_a+w_{\pi(a)}. $$
Summing over the replacement slots yields
$$ c_t(A_j)\leq c_t(B)+S(X_j)+S(R_j). $$
Using $S(R_j)\leq S(Q)=S(B)-u$, we obtain
$$ Z(A_j) \leq z+\alpha(s-u)+S(B)-u \leq z+\alpha s+S(B). $$
Thus
$$ \frac{Z(A_j)}z\leq2+\frac{\alpha}{x}. $$

We have proved the two bounds
$$ \boxed{ \frac{S(A_j)}s\leq x-\frac jp(x-1), \qquad \frac{Z(A_j)}z\leq2+\frac{j}{px}. } $$

For $j=p$, the committee is $O$. In particular,
$$ \frac{Z(O)}z\leq2+\frac1x. $$
The committee $B$ has simultaneous factor $x$.

3. Universal upper bound

Write
$$ C=\frac{5+\sqrt{17}}4. $$
It satisfies
$$ 2C^2-5C+1=0, \qquad C>\frac94, \qquad (C-2)(2C-1)=1. $$

We distinguish the number $p$ of differing slots.

Case $p=0$

Here $O=B$, so this committee optimizes both objectives.

Case $p=1$

Let $P=\{a\}$ and $c=|U|=k-1\geq2$. For every support location $t$, averaging the triangle inequality over the slots in $U$ gives
$$ d(t,\ell(a)) \leq \frac{c_t(U)}c+ \frac1c\sum_{q\in U}d(\ell(q),\ell(a)). $$
The weighted triangle inequality proved above implies
$$ \sum_{q\in U}d(\ell(q),\ell(a)) \leq u+cw_a. $$
Therefore
$$ c_t(O) \leq \left(1+\frac1c\right)c_t(U)+\frac uc+w_a \leq \frac32 z+s. $$
The last inequality uses $c_t(U)\leq c_t(B)\leq z$, $c\geq2$, and $u/c+w_a\leq u+w_a=s$.

It follows that
$$ \frac{Z(O)}z\leq\frac32+\frac1x. $$
If $x\leq2$, choose $B$. If $x>2$, choose $O$, whose Max-Sum ratio is less than $2$. Thus this case admits factor $2<C$.

Case $p=2$

The mixed committee $A_1$ satisfies
$$ \frac{S(A_1)}s\leq\frac{1+x}{2}, \qquad \frac{Z(A_1)}z\leq2+\frac1{2x}. $$

Put
$$ T=2C-1=\frac1{C-2}. $$
Since $C>1$, we have $T>C$.

If $x\leq C$, choose $B$.

If $C<x<T$, choose $A_1$. Its Sum-Sum ratio satisfies
$$ \frac{1+x}{2}<\frac{1+T}{2}=C. $$
Also $x>C>T/2$, so its Max-Sum ratio satisfies
$$ 2+\frac1{2x}<2+\frac1T=C. $$

If $x\geq T$, choose $O$. Its Max-Sum ratio satisfies
$$ 2+\frac1x\leq2+\frac1T=C. $$

Thus this case admits factor $C$, including the boundary values.

Case $p\geq3$

In fact, this case admits factor
$$ H=\frac94<C. $$

If $x\leq H$, choose $B$. If $x\geq4$, choose $O$, since
$$ 2+\frac1x\leq\frac94. $$

It remains to treat $H<x<4$. Define
$$ a=\frac{x-H}{x-1}, \qquad b=\frac x4. $$
A mixture with $\alpha=j/p$ satisfies both required bounds whenever
$$ a\leq\alpha\leq b. $$
Indeed, these inequalities respectively imply
$$ x-\alpha(x-1)\leq H, \qquad 2+\frac{\alpha}{x}\leq H. $$

We now verify that this interval contains an admissible multiple of $1/p$. With $y=x-1>0$,
$$ b-a =\frac14\left(y+\frac5y-3\right) \geq\frac{2\sqrt5-3}{4} >\frac13. $$
The first inequality follows from
$$ y+\frac5y-2\sqrt5 =\left(\sqrt y-\frac{\sqrt5}{\sqrt y}\right)^2\geq0. $$
For the strict inequality, $6\sqrt5>13$ follows by squaring, since $180>169$.

Because $p\geq3$, the interval has length greater than $1/p$. Take
$$ j=\lceil pa\rceil. $$
Then
$$ a\leq\frac jp<a+\frac1p<b. $$
Moreover, $a>0$ and $b<1$, so $1\leq j\leq p-1$. The corresponding feasible committee $A_j$ has both ratios at most $H$.

These cases prove that every instance admits simultaneous factor $C$.

4. Matching lower bound on a line

The lower bound uses $k=3$, $m=5$, and two positive-mass client locations.

Let
$$ h_*=3(C-2)=\frac34(\sqrt{17}-3). $$
The identity $(C-2)(2C-1)=1$ gives
$$ \frac3{h_*}=2C-1, \qquad \frac{h_*+3}{2h_*}=C. $$

For parameters $h>0$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$, consider the four line locations
$$ -h,\qquad 0,\qquad 3,\qquad 4, $$
with the ordinary distance metric. Place client mass $1-\varepsilon$ at $0$ and mass $\varepsilon$ at $4$. Provide:

  • one slot $q$ at $0$;
  • two slots of type $a$ at $-h$;
  • two slots of type $b$ at $3$.

Assume
$$ h+6\varepsilon<3. $$

The individual client distances and weighted slot costs are
$$ \begin{array}{c|ccc} &q&a&b\\ \hline \text{distance from }0&0&h&3\\ \text{distance from }4&4&4+h&1\\ w&4\varepsilon&h+4\varepsilon&3-2\varepsilon \end{array} $$
The assumed inequality makes the three cheapest slots precisely $q,a,a$. Hence
$$ s=2h+12\varepsilon. $$

Every feasible committee has one of the following five types. Direct summation gives
$$ \begin{array}{c|cc} \text{committee type}&S&Z\\ \hline qaa&2h+12\varepsilon&12+2h\\ qab&h+3+6\varepsilon&9+h\\ qbb&6&6\\ aab&2h+3+6\varepsilon&9+2h\\ abb&h+6&6+h \end{array} $$
The table exhausts all possibilities because there is only one $q$ slot and only two slots of each other type. Since $h>0$, it also proves
$$ z=6. $$

For clarity, in the last row both clients have cost $6+h$; in the other rows the displayed maximum follows directly from the individual distances above.

Now let $h\to h_*$ and $\varepsilon\to0$ through positive values. The limiting approximation ratios are
$$ \begin{array}{c|ccc} \text{committee type} &S/s&Z/z&\text{simultaneous factor}\\ \hline qaa&1&C&C\\ qab&C&(C+1)/2&C\\ qbb&2C-1&1&2C-1\\ aab&C+1/2&C-1/2&C+1/2\\ abb&2C-1/2&C/2&2C-1/2 \end{array} $$
All entries follow by substituting $3/h_*=2C-1$ and $h_*/3=C-2$. Since $C>1$, the minimum of the five limiting simultaneous factors is exactly $C$.

Admissible binary-rational instances

The irrational parameter is used only to describe the limit. For integers $n\geq3$, choose
$$ h_n=\frac{\lfloor2^n h_*\rfloor}{2^n}, \qquad \varepsilon_n=2^{-n}. $$
Here $0<h_*<1$, so
$$ h_n+6\varepsilon_n<1+\frac68<3. $$
Every distance and both masses are dyadic rationals. Thus these are admissible inputs with
$$ |M|=4,\qquad \tau=2,\qquad m=5,\qquad k=3. $$

At every stage, both client masses are positive; the client at $4$ remains in the Max-Sum support.

The Sum-Sum optimum tends to $2h_*>0$, while the Max-Sum optimum remains $6$. Consequently, every ratio in the five-row table converges to its displayed limit. For finitely many quantities, taking their minimum preserves convergence: explicitly,
$$ \left|\min_i v_{i,n}-\min_i v_i\right| \leq\max_i|v_{i,n}-v_i|. $$
Therefore the best simultaneous factor of these instances tends to $C$.

For every $\rho<C$, a sufficiently large $n$ consequently gives an admissible instance on which every committee has simultaneous factor greater than $\rho$. This proves the matching universal lower bound.

5. Exact construction and running time

The upper proof is constructive once a Max-Sum optimal committee $B$ is available.

Compute the $m$ weights $w_a$ in $O(m\tau)$ rational arithmetic operations and choose the $k$ cheapest slots to obtain $O$. If its cost is zero, return it immediately, before forming any ratios.

Otherwise, enumerate all $\binom mk$ committees to obtain $B$. Computing each committee’s client costs takes $O(k\tau)$ rational arithmetic operations. Form the mixed committees from Section 2 and choose the best among them, $O$, and $B$, using exact rational ratio comparisons. The proof guarantees that the chosen committee has factor at most $C$.

A conservative bit-time bound, including reading the distance matrix, is
$$ O\!\left( \left[ |M|^2+m\tau+m\log m+\binom mk\,k\tau \right]L^3 \right). $$
To justify the bit bound, common denominators for the input masses and distances have $O(L)$ bits. Objective values and the cross-products used to compare ratios therefore also have $O(L)$ bits. Elementary exact arithmetic fits within the displayed conservative bound.

This algorithm is exponential in general; the sharp factor proved above concerns existence without a computational restriction.
