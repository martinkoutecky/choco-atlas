1. Global identities and bounds

Write
$$ d=M+\binom M2, \qquad T(x)=\bigl((x_i)_i,(x_ix_j)_{i<j}\bigr). $$
For a nonempty contract $S$, let
$$ A_S=\{x:x_i=1\text{ for every }i\in S\}. $$
At every finite parameter, both $A_S$ and its complement have positive probability: they contain the all-one and all-zero outcomes, respectively.

For a type $r$, define
$$ D_r(\phi)= \mathrm{KL}\!\left(\mathrm{Bern}(p_r)\,\middle\Vert\, \mathrm{Bern}(q_{S_r}(\phi))\right). $$
Realizability gives
$$ J(\phi):=\sum_r\mu_rD_r(\phi) =F_\mu(\phi)-F_\mu(\phi^\star). $$
Thus $J\geq0$, and the loss in the question is exactly
$$ \mathcal L_t=b\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\mathbb E[J(\phi_s)]. $$
This identity charges only the drawn contract, because $r_s$ is independent of the history determining $\phi_s$.

Gradient and Hessian identities

Fix a contract $S$ and target $p$. Put
$$ m=\mathbb E_\phi T,\qquad m^+=\mathbb E_\phi[T\mid A_S],\qquad m^-=\mathbb E_\phi[T\mid A_S^c], \qquad q=q_S(\phi). $$
Differentiating the finite partition sums gives
$$ \nabla\log q=m^+-m, \qquad \nabla\log(1-q)=m^--m. $$
Consequently, its cross-entropy gradient is
$$ g_{S,p}(\phi) =m-pm^+-(1-p)m^- =(q-p)(m^+-m^-). $$
Every conditional-mean coordinate belongs to $[0,1]$. Therefore
$$ \|g_{S,p}(\phi)\|^2\leq d(q-p)^2\leq d. $$
In particular, every finite-time iterate is finite and
$$ \|\phi_s\|\leq s\eta\sqrt d. $$

Let $C,C^+,C^-$ be the unconditional and corresponding conditional covariance matrices of $T$. Differentiation again gives
$$ \nabla^2\mathrm{CE}(p,q_S(\phi)) =C-pC^+-(1-p)C^-. $$
Each covariance matrix is positive semidefinite and has trace at most $d/4$, since its coordinates are binary. Hence both $C$ and $pC^++(1-p)C^-$ lie between $0$ and $(d/4)I$, and
$$ \left\|\nabla^2\mathrm{CE}(p,q_S(\phi))\right\|_{\mathrm{op}} \leq K, \qquad K=\frac d4. $$
The same global smoothness bound holds for $F_\mu$.

A loss-dependent second-moment bound

For fixed $q\in(0,1)$, consider
$$ f(u)=\mathrm{KL}(\mathrm{Bern}(u)\Vert\mathrm{Bern}(q)). $$
It satisfies $f(q)=f'(q)=0$ and
$$ f''(u)=\frac1{u(1-u)}\geq4. $$
Integrating this second-derivative inequality between $q$ and $p$ yields
$$ \mathrm{KL}(\mathrm{Bern}(p)\Vert\mathrm{Bern}(q)) \geq2(p-q)^2. $$
Combining it with the gradient identity proves the global bound
$$ \boxed{\quad \mathbb E_r\|g_r(\phi)\|^2\leq\frac d2J(\phi). \quad} $$
Thus the conditional gradient variance is globally bounded:
$$ \mathbb E\!\left[ \|g_{r_s}(\phi_s)-\nabla F_\mu(\phi_s)\|^2 \,\middle|\,\phi_s\right]\leq d. $$
More strongly, that variance is at most $dJ(\phi_s)/2$. It vanishes at the realized target; there is no intrinsic additive noise floor there.

Since every individual gradient vanishes at $\phi^\star$, Taylor’s formula and the global Hessian bound also give
$$ D_r(\phi)\leq\frac K2\|\phi-\phi^\star\|^2, \qquad \boxed{\quad J(\phi)\leq\frac d8\|\phi-\phi^\star\|^2.\quad} $$

2. A local theorem for the actual unprojected process

This section establishes a stopped-process guarantee without assuming that all iterates remain in the neighborhood.

Positive curvature at the target

The covariance matrix
$$ C(\phi)=\operatorname{Cov}_\phi(T) $$
is positive definite at every finite parameter. Indeed, if $u^\top C(\phi)u=0$, full support implies that $u^\top T(x)$ is constant over all outcomes. Evaluation at the zero outcome makes the constant zero. Evaluation at each unit vector makes every singleton coefficient zero; evaluation at each outcome with exactly two ones then makes every pair coefficient zero. Thus $u=0$.

At the target,
$$ \nabla^2F_\mu(\phi^\star) = \sum_r \frac{\mu_r}{p_r(1-p_r)} \nabla q_{S_r}(\phi^\star) \nabla q_{S_r}(\phi^\star)^\top. $$
For singleton and pair contracts, their price gradients are the columns of $C(\phi^\star)$. Those columns span $\mathbb R^d$, and every corresponding contract has positive total sampling mass. Therefore
$$ \nabla^2F_\mu(\phi^\star)\succ0. $$

By continuity, there are $R>0$ and $\kappa>0$ such that
$$ \nabla^2F_\mu(\phi)\succeq\kappa I \qquad \text{whenever }\|\phi-\phi^\star\|\leq R. $$
The global smoothness bound implies $\kappa\leq d/4$.

Conditional contraction and loss decrease

Suppose $\phi$ belongs to this ball, and write $e=\phi-\phi^\star$. Strong convexity along the segment from $\phi$ to $\phi^\star$ gives
$$ \langle e,\nabla F_\mu(\phi)\rangle \geq J(\phi)+\frac\kappa2\|e\|^2. $$
Expanding the actual stochastic update and applying the loss-dependent second-moment bound yields
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E_r\|\phi-\eta g_r(\phi)-\phi^\star\|^2 &=\|e\|^2-2\eta\langle e,\nabla F_\mu(\phi)\rangle +\eta^2\mathbb E_r\|g_r(\phi)\|^2\\ &\leq (1-\eta\kappa)\|e\|^2 -\eta\left(2-\frac{\eta d}{2}\right)J(\phi). \end{aligned} $$
Assume henceforth
$$ 0<\eta\leq\frac2d, \qquad a=2-\frac{\eta d}{2}\geq1, \qquad \rho=1-\eta\kappa\in[1/2,1). $$

Exit probability and loss before exit

Assume the prescribed initialization lies strictly inside the ball:
$$ r_0:=\|\phi_0-\phi^\star\|=\|\phi^\star\|<R. $$
Define the stopping time
$$ \xi=\inf\{s\geq0:\|\phi_s-\phi^\star\|\geq R\}, $$
with $\xi=\infty$ if the set is empty. Stopping is only an analytical device; the updates are not changed.

Let
$$ Y_s=\|\phi_{s\wedge\xi}-\phi^\star\|^2. $$
With respect to the history before drawing $r_s$, the preceding inequality gives
$$ \mathbb E[Y_{s+1}\mid\mathcal F_s] \leq Y_s-\mathbf1_{\{s<\xi\}} \left( \eta\kappa\|\phi_s-\phi^\star\|^2 +a\eta J(\phi_s) \right). $$
All these variables are integrable by the finite-time parameter bound in Section 1.

Taking expectations and summing proves
$$ a\eta\sum_{s=0}^{t-1} \mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{s<\xi\}}J(\phi_s)] \leq r_0^2. $$
Independence of the next draw gives
$$ \boxed{\quad b\,\mathbb E\!\left[ \sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\mathbf1_{\{s<\xi\}}D_{r_s}(\phi_s) \right] \leq\frac{br_0^2}{a\eta} \leq\frac{br_0^2}{\eta}. \quad} $$
This includes the trade whose update first exits the ball, because its starting state is inside.

Also, $\mathbb E Y_t\leq r_0^2$. On $\{\xi\leq t\}$, $Y_t\geq R^2$. Consequently,
$$ \boxed{\quad \Pr(\xi<\infty)\leq\frac{r_0^2}{R^2}. \quad} $$

Geometric convergence on trajectories that stay inside

Discarding the nonnegative contribution of trajectories that exit at the next update gives
$$ \mathbb E\!\left[ \|\phi_t-\phi^\star\|^2\mathbf1_{\{\xi>t\}} \right] \leq\rho^t r_0^2. $$
Let $A=\{\xi=\infty\}$ and $\alpha=\Pr(A)$. Then
$$ \alpha\geq1-\frac{r_0^2}{R^2}>0, $$
and
$$ \mathbb E[\|\phi_t-\phi^\star\|^2\mid A] \leq\frac{\rho^t r_0^2}{\alpha}. $$
Moreover,
$$ \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \mathbb E\!\left[ \|\phi_t-\phi^\star\|^2\mathbf1_{\{\xi>t\}} \right]<\infty. $$
Thus the corresponding random series is finite almost surely, which implies $\phi_t\to\phi^\star$ almost surely on $A$.

For every nonempty $S$,
$$ \nabla q_S(\phi)=\operatorname{Cov}_\phi(\mathbf1_{A_S},T). $$
Each covariance coordinate has absolute value at most $1/4$ by Cauchy–Schwarz and the binary variance bound. Hence
$$ |q_S(\phi)-q_S(\phi^\star)|^2 \leq\frac d{16}\|\phi-\phi^\star\|^2. $$
The bound is uniform in $S$, so it also proves
$$ \mathbb E\!\left[ \max_{\varnothing\neq S\subseteq[M]} |q_S(\phi_t)-q_S(\phi^\star)|^2 \mathbf1_{\{\xi>t\}} \right] \leq\frac d{16}\rho^t r_0^2. $$

If containment holds almost surely, these estimates become unconditional and yield
$$ \mathcal L_t\leq\frac{b\|\phi^\star\|^2}{\eta}. $$
This conditional loss coefficient is independent of $M$ and requires neither a dimension-independent curvature constant nor a dimension-independent sensitivity bound. However, the theorem does not control the loss after an exit, and the promises do not ensure $\|\phi^\star\|<R$.

4. No finite spurious stationary points with only singleton and pair contracts

This result holds for every $M$, not just $M=2$.

Suppose every supported contract is a singleton or pair. Combine types sharing a contract into a total mass $\lambda_j>0$; their targets agree by realizability. Index these contracts by the coordinates of $T$, and write
$$ m_j=q_{S_j}(\phi), \qquad m_j^\star=q_{S_j}(\phi^\star). $$
The gradient is
$$ \nabla F_\mu(\phi)=C(\phi)a, \qquad a_j=\lambda_j \frac{m_j-m_j^\star}{m_j(1-m_j)}. $$
Positive definiteness of $C(\phi)$ implies that stationarity forces $a=0$, hence $m=m^\star$.

For completeness, the mean map is injective. If $v=\phi-\psi\neq0$, differentiation along their connecting segment gives
$$ v^\top(m(\phi)-m(\psi)) = \int_0^1v^\top C(\psi+uv)v\,du>0. $$
Thus $m=m^\star$ forces $\phi=\phi^\star$.

So, with only singleton and pair contracts, $\phi^\star$ is the unique finite stationary point. This does not establish boundedness or exclude convergence toward infinity.

I prove that there are no constants $C<\infty$ and $\eta_0>0$, both independent of $M$, such that every promised complete-parlay instance satisfies
$$ \mathcal L_t \leq Cb\left(\frac{\|\phi^\star\|^2}{\eta}+\eta t\right) $$
for every $t\geq1$ and every $0<\eta\leq\eta_0$.

Construction and promises

For $M\geq8$, set
$$ h=\lceil\log_2M\rceil, \qquad a=(1+1/M)^{16h}, \qquad w=\log a. $$
Choose
$$ \theta_i^\star=0, \qquad W_{ij}^\star=w. $$
Use complete-parlay sampling, so $\tau=2^M-1$, and give every contract its exact target.

An outcome with $j$ ones has weight $a^{\binom j2}$. Since $a$ is rational, for $k=|S|$ its target is the rational number
$$ p_S= \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^M \binom{M-k}{j-k}a^{\binom j2}} {\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^M \binom Mj a^{\binom j2}}. $$
Every outcome has positive weight, so every target lies in $(0,1)$. Singleton and pair means identify the finite parameter by the injectivity argument in Section 4.

Using $\log(1+1/M)\leq1/M$,
$$ \|\phi^\star\|^2 =\binom M2w^2 \leq128h^2. $$

Every target exceeds one half

An outcome with $k$ zeros has weight relative to the all-one outcome equal to
$$ \exp\!\left(-\frac{wk(2M-k-1)}2\right) \leq \exp\!\left(-\frac{wk(M-1)}2\right). $$
Since $\log(1+1/M)\geq1/(2M)$,
$$ \frac{w(M-1)}2 \geq2h \geq2\log M. $$
The relative weight is therefore at most $M^{-2k}$. Summing these relative weights over all outcomes gives

Every nonempty contract pays on $\mathbf1$, so $p_S>1/2$ for every $S$.

6. Input size and computational running times

Constructing the loss counterexample

Put
$$ A=(M+1)^{16h}, \qquad B=M^{16h}, \qquad D=\binom M2. $$
Multiplying every outcome weight by $B^D$ gives integer weights
$$ u_j=A^{\binom j2}B^{D-\binom j2}. $$
The target numerator and denominator are the corresponding integer sums from Section 5. Their bit lengths are
$$ K=O(M^2(\log M)^2). $$
Listing the contracts, masses, and targets explicitly therefore gives
$$ L=O(\tau K) =O\!\left(\tau M^2(\log M)^2\right), \qquad \tau=2^M-1. $$

For an explicit conservative construction bound, repeated multiplication computes all $u_j$ in $O(M^3K^2)$ bit operations using schoolbook arithmetic. Computing the $M$ distinct target fractions costs $O(M^2K^2)$ further bit operations. Reduction by the elementary Euclidean algorithm costs at most $O(MK^3)$, and writing the support costs $O(\tau K)$. Hence a valid overall bound is
$$ O(M^3K^2+MK^3+\tau K) $$
bit operations.

Computing gradients and refreshing quotes

After parsing the $L$-bit input, use subset masks to enumerate all outcome weights
$$ v(A)= \exp\!\left( \sum_{i\in A}\theta_i+ \sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq A}W_{ij} \right), \qquad A\subseteq[M]. $$
Direct computation costs $O(M^2 2^M)$ arithmetic operations and $2^M$ exponential evaluations.

Initialize $H(A)=v(A)$. For each $i\in[M]$, perform
$$ H(A)\leftarrow H(A)+H(A\cup\{i\}) \qquad\text{for every }A\text{ not containing }i. $$
This superset-sum transform costs $O(M2^M)$ additions and produces
$$ H(S)=\sum_{A\supseteq S}v(A). $$
Therefore
$$ Z=H(\varnothing), \qquad q_S=\frac{H(S)}Z. $$
It refreshes all coherent quotes with shared work; extracting the $\tau$ supported quotes costs $O(\tau)$ further operations.

If coordinate $j$ of $T$ corresponds to singleton or pair $B_j$, then
$$ \frac{\partial q_S}{\partial\phi_j} =q_{S\cup B_j}-q_Sq_{B_j}. $$
Thus the sampled gradient can be recovered from the quote table in $O(d)$ additional operations.

A round, including the update and subsequent quote refresh, consequently costs
$$ O(M^2 2^M+\tau) $$
real-arithmetic operations, with $O(2^M+\tau+d)$ storage. Over $t$ rounds the operation count is
$$ O\!\left(t(M^2 2^M+\tau)\right), $$
in addition to input parsing and sampling preprocessing.

These are real-arithmetic and exponential-evaluation counts. They do not assert exact finite-bit computation of arbitrary transcendental iterates.

7. Remaining gap

The stopped-process theorem controls actual trades until an exit and proves convergence on the event of permanent containment. It does not control trajectories after an exit. The explicit initialization example shows that global contraction cannot simply be substituted for that missing argument, even when no finite spurious stationary point exists.

For loss, uniform scaling on a dimension-independent positive step-size interval is disproved. Scaling under a precisely specified dimension-dependent or instance-dependent small-step restriction remains open without an additional global argument.
