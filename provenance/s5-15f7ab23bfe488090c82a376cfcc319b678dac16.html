The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of outcomes \(K\), role-specific type sets \(T_1,T_2\) with one side having two types, and rational masses \(\mu(t_1,t_2)\) on supported case types, determine whether nonnegative masses \(x^k(t_1,t_2)\) exist such that \(\sum_{k\in K}x^k(t_1,t_2)=\mu(t_1,t_2)\) for each supported pair and \(\sum_{t_{-i}}x^k(t_i,t_{-i})=\mu_i(t_i)Q_i^k(t_i)\) for each role, type, and outcome.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(K\) be a nonempty finite outcome set, with \(m=|K|\), and let finite role-specific type sets satisfy \(\min\{|T_1|,|T_2|\}=2\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) joint types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Require full support \(T_1\times T_2\), normalization \(\sum_t\mu(t)=1\), and independence \(\mu(t_1,t_2)=\mu_1(t_1)\mu_2(t_2)\), where \(\mu_i(t_i)=\sum_{t_{-i}}\mu(t_i,t_{-i})\).
The input also specifies binary rational interim probabilities \(Q_i^k(t_i)\geq0\), with \(\sum_{k\in K}Q_i^k(t_i)=1\) for every role and type. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Feasibility means existence of real masses \(x^k(t_1,t_2)\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{k\in K}x^k(t_1,t_2)=\mu(t_1,t_2)\) and \(\sum_{t_{-i}}x^k(t_i,t_{-i})=\mu_i(t_i)Q_i^k(t_i)\) for every role, type, and outcome.

Is this feasibility problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Result

The feasibility problem is solvable in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

Under the stated explicit-support encoding, the given constraints already form a rational linear program with $m\tau$ variables and polynomial encoding length. Thus polynomial-time linear programming settles the question. The binary-type assumption additionally permits a direct reduction to a single maximum-flow problem, proved below.

1. Notation and necessary consistency

Relabel the roles, if necessary, so that
$$ T_1=\{a,b\},\qquad T_2=\{1,\ldots,n\}. $$
Full support gives $\tau=2n$. Define
$$ u_j=\mu(a,j),\qquad v_j=\mu(b,j),\qquad h_j=u_j+v_j=\mu_2(j), $$
and let
$$ p=\mu_1(a)=\sum_{j=1}^n u_j. $$
For each outcome $k\in K$, define the required outcome masses
$$ A_k=pQ_1^k(a),\qquad B_k=(1-p)Q_1^k(b),\qquad C_{jk}=h_jQ_2^k(j). $$
The input normalization assumptions imply
$$ \sum_{k\in K}A_k=p,\qquad \sum_{k\in K}B_k=1-p,\qquad \sum_{k\in K}C_{jk}=h_j. $$

A necessary condition for feasibility is
$$ A_k+B_k=\sum_{j=1}^n C_{jk} \qquad\text{for every }k\in K. $$
Indeed, either side must equal the total mass assigned to outcome $k$, obtained by summing the prescribed marginals for the corresponding role. The algorithm first checks these equalities and rejects if any fails.

For the rest of the proof, assume they hold.

2. Eliminating the masses for one type

Set
$$ z_{jk}=x^k(a,j). $$
The role-$2$ marginal constraints force
$$ x^k(b,j)=C_{jk}-z_{jk}. $$
Consequently, nonnegativity of both masses is equivalent to
$$ 0\leq z_{jk}\leq C_{jk}. $$
The remaining requirements for type $a$ are precisely
$$ \sum_{k\in K}z_{jk}=u_j \qquad\text{for every }j, $$
and
$$ \sum_{j=1}^n z_{jk}=A_k \qquad\text{for every }k. $$

These conditions are also sufficient. To verify this, suppose such a matrix $z$ exists and define
$$ x^k(a,j)=z_{jk},\qquad x^k(b,j)=C_{jk}-z_{jk}. $$
Both masses are nonnegative. Their sum is $C_{jk}$, so every role-$2$ marginal constraint holds. The cell sums for type $a$ and its role-$1$ marginals hold by the equations imposed on $z$. For type $b$, the cell sums satisfy
$$ \sum_{k\in K}x^k(b,j) =\sum_{k\in K}C_{jk}-\sum_{k\in K}z_{jk} =h_j-u_j =v_j =\mu(b,j). $$
Finally, the consistency equalities give
$$ \sum_{j=1}^n x^k(b,j) =\sum_{j=1}^n C_{jk}-A_k =B_k. $$
Thus all original constraints hold.

We have proved that, after checking consistency, the original problem is equivalent to the displayed capacitated matrix problem.

3. A single maximum-flow test

Construct a directed network with a source $s$, a sink $t$, one vertex $J_j$ for each $j\in T_2$, and one vertex $O_k$ for each $k\in K$. Its arcs and capacities are:

| Arc | Capacity |
|---|---:|
| $s\longrightarrow J_j$ | $u_j$ |
| $J_j\longrightarrow O_k$ | $C_{jk}$ |
| $O_k\longrightarrow t$ | $A_k$ |

All capacities are nonnegative. The total capacity leaving the source and the total capacity entering the sink are both $p$:
$$ \sum_j u_j=p=\sum_k A_k. $$

Claim. The matrix problem in Section 2 is feasible if and only if this network has a flow of value $p$.

Proof. Given a feasible matrix $z$, assign flows
$$ f(s,J_j)=u_j,\qquad f(J_j,O_k)=z_{jk},\qquad f(O_k,t)=A_k. $$
The matrix equations give flow conservation at every internal vertex, and its bounds give the capacity constraints. The flow value is $p$.

Conversely, let $f$ be a feasible flow of value $p$. Every source arc is saturated: the sum of their capacities is $p$, and any unsaturated source arc would make the total outgoing flow strictly smaller than $p$. The same argument saturates every sink arc. Therefore, setting
$$ z_{jk}=f(J_j,O_k) $$
and using conservation gives
$$ \sum_k z_{jk}=u_j,\qquad \sum_j z_{jk}=A_k. $$
The middle-arc capacity constraints give $0\leq z_{jk}\leq C_{jk}$. This proves the claim.

Since no flow can exceed $p$, the algorithm accepts exactly when the maximum-flow value equals $p$.

4. Exact computation and bit complexity

The network has
$$ V=n+m+2,\qquad E=nm+n+m. $$
Because $n=\tau/2$, both are polynomial in $m,\tau$.

It remains to ensure that binary rational capacities can be handled in polynomial bit time.

Write each input rational with a positive integer denominator. Let $D_\mu$ be the product of all denominators of the input masses, and let $D_Q$ be the product of all denominators of the input interim probabilities. Set
$$ D=D_\mu D_Q. $$
The binary length of $D$ is $O(L)$, because the sum of the binary lengths of these input denominators is at most $L$.

Every marginal mass has denominator dividing $D_\mu$. It follows that all network capacities
$$ u_j,\qquad C_{jk}=h_jQ_2^k(j),\qquad A_k=pQ_1^k(a) $$
have denominators dividing $D$. Multiplying all capacities by $D$ therefore produces integer capacities. Every unscaled capacity lies in $[0,1]$, so each scaled capacity is at most $D$ and has $O(L)$ bits. The target value $Dp$ is also an integer with $O(L)$ bits.

Apply the shortest augmenting-path maximum-flow algorithm to this integer network. Its number of augmentations is polynomial in the number of vertices, independently of capacity magnitudes; this is the classical [Edmonds–Karp theorem](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~pettie/matching/Edmonds-Karp-network-flow.pdf). Each path search takes polynomial time in $V,E$. Starting from zero flow, all flows and residual capacities remain integers bounded by $D$, so the required arithmetic has polynomial bit cost.

The algorithm can therefore compute the maximum-flow value exactly and compare it with $Dp$ in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

If input validity is part of the decision problem, rather than promised, it can also be checked in polynomial time. Check support membership and uniqueness, full-support cardinality, positivity, normalization, interim normalization, and the independence equalities
$$ \mu(t_1,t_2)=\mu_1(t_1)\mu_2(t_2). $$
These require polynomially many exact rational operations on numbers of polynomial bit length.

5. Scope of the conclusion

The flow reduction uses the binary-type assumption; it does not require independence. Hence it applies, in particular, to every input satisfying all the assumptions in the question.

More generally, the explicit support list makes the original rational linear program polynomial in size even without the binary-type assumption. Lang’s finite cut characterization concerns the same implementability problem, as stated in the question; no distinct extension is needed. Nor must its potentially numerous cuts be enumerated: the mass formulation here admits the direct polynomial algorithm proved above.
