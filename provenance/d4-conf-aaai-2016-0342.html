The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), preferred candidate \(p\), finite voter types \(T\), where each \(t\in T\) consists of a complete ranking \(\rho_t\) and price function \(\pi_t\), and rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), choose masses \(x_{t,j}\ge 0\) for \(j=0,\ldots,\operatorname{pos}_{\rho_t}(p)-1\) satisfying \(\sum_j x_{t,j}=\mu_t\). Each mass \(x_{t,j}\) receives exactly \(j\) upward shifts of \(p\); decide whether the total cost \(\sum_{t,j}\pi_t(j)x_{t,j}\) is at most \(b\) and makes \(p\) a Borda winner, or compute the minimum cost.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq1\) candidates and let \(p\in C\). A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types \(t=(\rho_t,\pi_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each \(\rho_t\) is a strict complete ranking, with positions numbered from \(1\). Write \(r_t=\operatorname{pos}_{\rho_t}(p)\). The explicitly tabulated prices \(\pi_t:\{0,\ldots,r_t-1\}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}\) are nondecreasing and satisfy \(\pi_t(0)=0\). The budget \(b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\) is binary encoded; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An action chooses real masses \(x_{t,j}\geq0\) with \(\sum_{j=0}^{r_t-1}x_{t,j}=\mu_t\). Mass \(x_{t,j}\) moves \(p\) upward exactly \(j\) positions, preserving all other candidates' relative order, at cost \(\pi_t(j)x_{t,j}\).
Borda scores are aggregated by mass. Success means that \(p\) is a co-winner, allowing ties, and \(\sum_{t,j}\pi_t(j)x_{t,j}\leq b\).

Is this continuous Borda shift-bribery decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? If it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, is it nevertheless fixed-parameter tractable with parameter \(\tau\), in time \(f(\tau)\operatorname{poly}(m,L)\)?

Discrete Borda shift bribery is [NP-complete](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~slinko/Research/swap.pdf); it is also \(\mathrm{W[1]}\)-hard parameterized by the number of voters, even with unit prices.
The exact continuous problem above is already covered by a [polynomial-time result](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25444v1) for scoring-rule shift bribery with rational populations and nondecreasing rational prices, including minimum-cost optimization. Thus the target is already settled.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Complete rankings are strict linear orders; positions start at 1.
  • Price functions are explicitly tabulated nonnegative integers, nondecreasing, and zero at shift 0, following the discrete definition.
  • The budget is a nonnegative binary rational, appropriate to normalized masses.
  • Zero-mass types are omitted from the support; the specified normalization to total mass 1 is retained.
  • Shifts preserve the relative order of every candidate other than p; tied Borda winners count as success.
  • The decision formulation is selected from the graded question's decision-or-optimization alternatives; the voter-count parameter becomes support size.

Result

The problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Consequently, it is also fixed-parameter tractable with parameter \(\tau\). This agrees with the cited paper’s polynomial-size LP result for continuous shift bribery. [Continuous Computational Social Choice, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25444v1#S3)

Here is a direct proof for the exact model in the question.

1. Scores after a shift

Use the Borda convention that position \(k\) receives \(m-k\) points. For each type \(t\) and opponent \(c\ne p\), write \(q_t(c)=\operatorname{pos}_{\rho_t}(c)\).

After shifting \(p\) upward by \(j\) positions, its position is \(r_t-j\). An opponent \(c\) moves down exactly one position if and only if \(p\) passes it, equivalently,

\(r_t-j\le q_t(c)<r_t.\)

Every other opponent retains its position. Define the indicator

\(I_{t,j,c}=\begin{cases}1,&r_t-j\le q_t(c)<r_t,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\)

Thus, on one unit of mass receiving this shift, the Borda score difference between \(p\) and \(c\) is

\(a_{t,j,c}=(m-r_t+j)-(m-q_t(c)-I_{t,j,c})=q_t(c)-r_t+j+I_{t,j,c}.\)

These are explicitly computable integers. As differences of two Borda scores, they satisfy \(\lvert a_{t,j,c}\rvert\le m-1\).

2. An exact linear programming formulation

Introduce one real variable \(x_{t,j}\) for each listed type \(t\) and each \(j\in\{0,\ldots,r_t-1\}\). Consider the following rational linear feasibility problem:

\(x_{t,j}\ge0\qquad\text{for every }t,j,\)

\(\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r_t-1}x_{t,j}=\mu_t\qquad\text{for every }t,\)

\(\displaystyle\sum_t\sum_{j=0}^{r_t-1}a_{t,j,c}x_{t,j}\ge0\qquad\text{for every }c\in C\setminus\{p\},\)

\(\displaystyle\sum_t\sum_{j=0}^{r_t-1}\pi_t(j)x_{t,j}\le b.\)

Equivalence. Any permitted action satisfies the nonnegativity and mass constraints. By additivity of mass-weighted Borda scores, its final score difference between \(p\) and \(c\) is exactly the left-hand side of the corresponding winning inequality. Hence all winning inequalities hold precisely when \(p\) is a co-winner. The final inequality is exactly the action’s budget requirement.

Conversely, every feasible LP vector specifies a permitted action by assigning mass \(x_{t,j}\) to shift \(j\). The same score calculation and cost expression prove that this action succeeds within budget.

Therefore, the instance is a yes-instance if and only if this LP is feasible. No integrality assumption or rounding is involved.

3. Polynomial encoding length and exact feasibility

The number of variables is

\(\displaystyle N=\sum_t r_t\le\tau m.\)

Besides nonnegativity, there are \(\tau\) mass equalities, \(m-1\) winning inequalities, and one budget inequality. Equalities may be replaced by pairs of inequalities.

All coefficients are rational:

  • Masses, prices, and budget are supplied in the input, so their individual encoding lengths are at most \(L\).
  • Winning coefficients have encoding length \(O(\log(m+1))\).
  • The remaining coefficients are zero or one.

Positions and indicators can be computed directly from the listed rankings, and all winning coefficients can be constructed in \(O(\tau m^2)\) elementary operations. Consequently, the full LP has encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming decides feasibility over the reals in time polynomial in the explicitly encoded system’s length. Its hypotheses hold here: finitely many variables and constraints, explicit rational coefficients, and non-strict linear inequalities. In particular, exact ties and exact budget equality are covered.

For \(m=1\), there are no winning inequalities, and choosing \(x_{t,0}=\mu_t\) succeeds at zero cost, consistently with the formulation.

4. Optimization and the parameter question

Minimum-cost optimization is also polynomial-time solvable: omit the budget constraint and minimize

\(\displaystyle\sum_t\sum_{j=0}^{r_t-1}\pi_t(j)x_{t,j}.\)

The feasible region is nonempty because shifting all mass of every type to place \(p\) first makes \(p\) a co-winner. It is closed and bounded because \(0\le x_{t,j}\le\mu_t\). Therefore, the linear objective attains its minimum, and rational LP optimization computes it exactly in polynomial time.

Finally, a bound polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) is bounded by \(f(\tau)\operatorname{poly}(m,L)\) for a suitable polynomial function \(f\), proving fixed-parameter tractability. NP-hardness would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The argument does not require price monotonicity. The decisive features are divisible masses and an explicitly enumerable set of at most \(m\) shifts per support type.
