The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix a per-capita processing capacity \(b>0\). Given a finite-support rational population over job types \(\theta=(r,d,\ell,v)\), with \(\ell\in[1,\kappa]\), whose cohort masses are revealed only when they arrive, what is the optimal worst-case competitive ratio of deterministic, individually truthful and individually rational online mechanisms against a clairvoyant fluid schedule at capacity \(b\)? In particular, do the lower bounds \(5\) at \(\kappa=1\) and \(\kappa/\ln\kappa+1-o(1)\) for large \(\kappa\) persist?

Types are rational \(\theta=(r,d,\ell,v)\), with \(0\le r<d\), \(v>0\), and \(1\le\ell\le\kappa\); \(b>0\) and \(\kappa\ge1\) are known rational parameters. Running-time bounds use \(m=4\) type coordinates, \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and \(b,\kappa\).

A fluid schedule measurably selects jobs to process at unit speed, with active mass at most \(b\) at every time. Cohorts may be split into arbitrary measurable subcohorts. Each job earns its full value only upon completion by its deadline; unfinished work earns nothing.
Consider separately preemption-resume, requiring \(\ell\) total processing time within \([r,d]\), and preemption-restart, requiring \(\ell\) consecutive processing time within that window. The clairvoyant benchmark has the same capacity and processing restrictions.
A deterministic direct mechanism schedules jobs and charges each individual a nonnegative payment \(p\). Reports satisfy \(\hat r\ge r\), \(\hat d\le d\), \(\hat\ell\ge\ell\), \(\hat v>0\), and \(\hat\ell\in[1,\kappa]\); a report is submitted at \(\hat r\). Utility is \(vq-p\), where \(q\) indicates true completion by the true deadline. Individual dominant-strategy truthfulness and individual rationality apply to each identity, including a single individual's deviation of zero population mass; mechanisms must specify such counterfactual outcomes. Welfare is \(W_M=\int vq\,d\mu\). Define \(C_s(b,\kappa)=\inf_M\sup_{I:W_{\mathrm{OPT},s}(I)>0}W_{\mathrm{OPT},s}(I)/W_M(I)\), over deterministic truthful, individually rational online mechanisms and finite-support rational inputs, for \(s\in\{\mathrm{restart},\mathrm{resume}\}\); a positive numerator divided by zero is \(+\infty\).
What is the optimal worst-case competitive ratio \(C_s(b,\kappa)\) in each model? In particular, do \(C_s(b,1)\ge5\) and \(C_s(b,\kappa)\ge\kappa/\ln\kappa+1-o(1)\) as \(\kappa\to\infty\), with \(b\) fixed, hold, or can either bound be improved upon by a mechanism?
For finite jobs on one unit-speed machine, deterministic truthful mechanisms have competitive ratio at least \(5\) for equal lengths, attained in both models, and at least \(\kappa/\ln\kappa+1-o(1)\) for large \(\kappa\). [Chen et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0707.pdf)

2. Admission rule and its extension to infinite report profiles

Write the population as a nonatomic measure space $(X,\mu)$ with $\mu(X)=1$. As required for a measurable scheduling model, report profiles are measurable. Fix deterministic measurable tie-breaking that permits exact splitting of equal-value sets. For example, order reported structural types first, then use public identities within each type.

Put
$$ \beta=\min\{b,1\}. $$

For a fixed report profile, let
$$ h_t=\mu\{i:\hat r_i=t\}, \qquad \mathcal E=\{t:h_t>0\}, \qquad q_t=\beta h_t. $$
The set $\mathcal E$ is at most countable: for each positive integer $n$, at most $n$ distinct arrival times can have mass at least $1/n$. Moreover,
$$ \sum_{t\in\mathcal E}q_t =\beta\sum_{t\in\mathcal E}h_t \le\beta. $$

The admission opportunities occur at the times in $\mathcal E$. At such a time $t$, the immediately feasible set is
$$ E_t=\{i:\hat r_i\le t,\ t+\hat\ell_i\le\hat d_i\}. $$

The intended rule is:

  • Among immediately feasible jobs not previously admitted, admit the highest reported values, up to mass $q_t$.
  • Every admitted job receives its entire reported length consecutively, starting immediately.
  • Unused quota is discarded.

For finite $\mathcal E$, this is an ordinary chronological greedy algorithm. For infinite $\mathcal E$, chronological recursion alone is insufficient: $\mathcal E$ need not have a first event, and events can accumulate. The following lemma supplies a well-defined extension.

2.1. A contraction lemma

Let $E$ be a measurable eligible set with a fixed priority order. Let $A$ be the set already admitted. Denote by $H_q(E\setminus A)$ the highest-priority portion of $E\setminus A$ of mass
$$ \min\{q,\mu(E\setminus A)\}. $$
Define
$$ G_{E,q}(A)=A\cup H_q(E\setminus A). $$

For measurable sets, use the distance
$$ d_\mu(A,B)=\mu(A\mathbin{\triangle}B). $$

Lemma. For fixed $E$, priority order, and quota $q$,
$$ d_\mu\bigl(G_{E,q}(A),G_{E,q}(B)\bigr) \le d_\mu(A,B). $$
For fixed $E,A$ and two quotas,
$$ d_\mu\bigl(G_{E,q}(A),G_{E,q'}(A)\bigr) \le |q-q'|. $$

Proof. Partition the jobs available in at least one of the two states into
$$ U=E\setminus(A\cup B),\qquad X=E\cap(A\setminus B),\qquad Y=E\cap(B\setminus A). $$
Starting from $A$, the available jobs are $U\cup Y$; starting from $B$, they are $U\cup X$.

Let $U_A,Y_A$ be the portions selected from $U,Y$ in the first update, and let $U_B,X_B$ be the portions selected from $U,X$ in the second. The subsets $U_A,U_B$ are initial portions of the same priority order on $U$, so they are nested, modulo null sets. Consequently,
$$ \begin{aligned} d_\mu\bigl(G_{E,q}(A),G_{E,q}(B)\bigr) ={}&d_\mu(A,B)-\mu(X_B)-\mu(Y_A)\\ &+\left|\mu(U_A)-\mu(U_B)\right|. \end{aligned} $$

If both updates select mass $q$, then
$$ \mu(U_A)+\mu(Y_A)=q=\mu(U_B)+\mu(X_B), $$
and hence
$$ \left|\mu(U_A)-\mu(U_B)\right| =\left|\mu(X_B)-\mu(Y_A)\right| \le\mu(X_B)+\mu(Y_A). $$

If, instead, the first update selects all its available jobs and selects less than $q$, then $U_A=U$ and $Y_A=Y$. If the second update also selects all its available jobs, then $U_B=U$, and the difference term is zero. Otherwise,
$$ \mu(U_B)+\mu(X_B)=q, $$
so
$$ \mu(U_A)-\mu(U_B) =\mu(U)-q+\mu(X_B) \le\mu(X_B), $$
because $\mu(U)\le q$. The case in which only the second update selects less than $q$ is symmetric. This proves the first inequality.

For the second inequality, the selected sets for quotas $q,q'$ are nested. Their mass difference is
$$ \left| \min\{q,\mu(E\setminus A)\} -\min\{q',\mu(E\setminus A)\} \right| \le |q-q'|. $$
This proves the lemma. $\square$

2.2. Constructing the infinite-event admission history

For a finite subset $F\subseteq\mathcal E$, run the greedy algorithm using admission opportunities only at times in $F$. All reports are still received at their actual reported arrivals; only opportunities outside $F$ are suppressed.

Let $A_F(u)$ be the cumulative admitted set after the events in $F$ at times at most $u$. Comparing two finite truncations chronologically and applying the lemma gives
$$ d_\mu\bigl(A_F(u),A_G(u)\bigr) \le \sum_{t\in F\mathbin{\triangle}G}q_t $$
for every time $u$.

Choose increasing finite sets $F_n$ exhausting $\mathcal E$. Since $\sum_tq_t<\infty$, the sets $A_{F_n}(u)$ form a Cauchy sequence in $d_\mu$, uniformly in $u$. Measurable sets modulo null sets are complete for this metric: this follows by viewing their indicators in $L^1$, and taking an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence of an $L^1$-convergent sequence.

Thus there is a limit $A(u)$, independent of the exhaustion. The same argument, using events strictly before $t$, gives a pre-event set $A^-(t)$.

The contraction lemma also implies continuity of the event update. Therefore, for every $t\in\mathcal E$,
$$ A(t)=G_{E_t,q_t}\bigl(A^-(t)\bigr) $$
modulo null sets. Define the newly admitted set
$$ D_t=A(t)\setminus A^-(t). $$
Then $\mu(D_t)\le q_t$, and the sets $D_t$ are pairwise disjoint modulo null sets.

We must also check that the limit has no additional admissions arising only from accumulation. For a fixed finite $S\subseteq\mathcal E$, a finite truncation containing $S$ satisfies
$$ \mu(A_F(u)) \le \sum_{\substack{t\in S\\t\le u}}\mu(D_{t,F}) + \sum_{t\in\mathcal E\setminus S}q_t, $$
where $D_{t,F}$ is its admission set at $t$. Passing to the limit gives
$$ \mu(A(u)) \le \sum_{\substack{t\in S\\t\le u}}\mu(D_t) + \sum_{t\in\mathcal E\setminus S}q_t. $$
Exhausting $\mathcal E$ by finite $S$ shows
$$ A(u)=\bigcup_{\substack{t\in\mathcal E\\t\le u}}D_t $$
modulo null sets. The corresponding equality with $t<u$ holds for $A^-(u)$.

This construction is online. The pre-event state at $t$ is determined entirely by reports and opportunities strictly before $t$. Its limit can be constructed by exhausting that past history alone. The update at $t$ uses only reports submitted by $t$, the newly observed mass $h_t$, and the pre-event state.

No assumption of finite aggregate report support has been made.

3. Counterfactual allocations and payments for every identity

The measure-level construction must now be supplemented with pointwise outcomes, including outcomes for singleton deviations.

3.1. Offer cutoffs

At an event $t$, consider the positive-mass eligible pending pool
$$ E_t\setminus A^-(t). $$

If its mass is at most $q_t$, set the value cutoff $a_t=0$ and offer admission to every eligible pending identity.

Otherwise, let $a_t$ be a quantile satisfying
$$ \mu\{i\in E_t\setminus A^-(t):\hat v_i>a_t\} \le q_t \le \mu\{i\in E_t\setminus A^-(t):\hat v_i\ge a_t\}. $$
Admit all strictly higher bids and split the cutoff-value set according to the fixed tie-breaking rule.

Such a finite cutoff exists. Indeed, the mass of bids above $z$ tends to zero as $z$ tends to infinity, since every reported bid is finite and the population has finite mass. The quantile inequalities follow from continuity of measure for monotone sequences of sets.

These cutoffs and cutoff tie decisions specify an offer to any eligible identity, including a singleton:

  • bids strictly greater than $a_t$ qualify;
  • bids strictly less than $a_t$ do not qualify;
  • bids equal to $a_t$ qualify according to the cutoff tie rule.

Changing one identity’s report leaves all mass-level states, quotas, and cutoff rules unchanged. Its report changes only its own eligibility, bid, and possible position within a cutoff tie.

3.2. Allocation when qualifying times have no minimum

For an identity with report $(\hat r,\hat d,\hat\ell,w)$, let $Q$ be its set of qualifying offer times, including eligibility:
$$ Q=\{t\in\mathcal E:\hat r\le t\le\hat d-\hat\ell,\ \text{the identity qualifies at }t\}. $$

If $Q$ is empty, give it no processing.

If $Q$ is nonempty, define
$$ \sigma=\inf Q. $$
Process it consecutively for $\hat\ell$ time units from $\sigma$, with the usual immaterial choice of interval endpoints. This finishes by its reported deadline because
$$ \hat r\le\sigma\le\hat d-\hat\ell. $$

This allocation is causal even if $\sigma\notin Q$. At a current time $u$, the mechanism knows
$$ Q_u=Q\cap(-\infty,u]. $$
If $Q_u$ is nonempty, then
$$ \inf Q_u=\inf Q=\sigma: $$
all future offer times are greater than $u$, whereas $\inf Q_u\le u$. If $Q$ has no minimum, $Q_u$ is nonempty for every $u>\sigma$. Thus the mechanism can process throughout the interval from $\sigma$ to $\sigma+\hat\ell$, apart from an irrelevant initial endpoint, using only observed offers.

This pointwise allocation agrees almost everywhere with the measure-level admission schedule. To see this, take the union of the null exceptional sets in the selection rule over the countably many events. Outside this union:

  • an identity in $D_t$ qualifies at $t$;
  • it cannot qualify at an earlier event, since it would then have been selected while pending;
  • an identity never admitted cannot qualify at any event.

Hence almost every admitted identity has a first qualifying event equal to its actual admission event. Identities whose qualifying sets have no minimum lie in a null set. Their specified processing consumes zero population mass.

The qualifying sets and their infima are measurable because $\mathcal E$ is countable. Thus this is a measurable schedule.

Finally, at every time, the active mass is at most the total admitted mass:
$$ \mu(\text{active jobs}) \le\sum_{t\in\mathcal E}\mu(D_t) \le\sum_{t\in\mathcal E}q_t \le\beta\le b. $$
The exceptional singleton conventions do not alter this inequality.

3.3. Payments

For fixed structural coordinates, define the feasible offer-event set
$$ T(\hat r,\hat d,\hat\ell) =\{t\in\mathcal E:\hat r\le t\le\hat d-\hat\ell\}. $$
Define its critical payment by
$$ P(\hat r,\hat d,\hat\ell) = \inf_{t\in T(\hat r,\hat d,\hat\ell)}a_t, $$
with $P=+\infty$ when $T$ is empty.

Charge an admitted identity $P(\hat r,\hat d,\hat\ell)$ and charge an unadmitted identity zero.

An admitted identity has a nonempty feasible event set, so its payment is finite. The payment can be determined by its reported deadline: every event in $T$ occurs by $\hat d-\hat\ell<\hat d$.

For every fixed identity and fixed reports of all other identities:

  • if $w>P$, some feasible event has $a_t<w$, so the identity is admitted;
  • if $w<P$, every feasible cutoff exceeds $w$, so it is rejected;
  • admission at $w=P$ may depend on ties or on attainment of the infimum.

No attainment assumption is needed. In particular, for the verifier’s sequence $a_{t_n}=1+1/n$, the critical payment is the well-defined number $1$.

4. Truthfulness, individual rationality, and welfare

4.1. Dominant-strategy truthfulness

Fix an identity of true type $(r,d,\ell,v)$ and fix every other identity’s reports. Since this identity has zero population mass, the events and their aggregate cutoffs are independent of its own report.

Every permitted structural report satisfies
$$ [\hat r,\hat d-\hat\ell]\subseteq[r,d-\ell]. $$
Indeed, $\hat r\ge r$ and
$$ \hat d-\hat\ell\le d-\ell. $$
Consequently,
$$ T(\hat r,\hat d,\hat\ell)\subseteq T(r,d,\ell), $$
and therefore
$$ P(\hat r,\hat d,\hat\ell)\ge P(r,d,\ell). $$
Write $P_{\mathrm{true}}=P(r,d,\ell)$.

Under any permitted report, admission gives true completion. The mechanism supplies the entire reported length consecutively within the reported window, and
$$ \hat\ell\ge\ell,\qquad \hat r\ge r,\qquad \hat d\le d. $$
Rejection gives no processing. Thus there is no possibility of obtaining true completion from an unfinished inflated-length report while paying zero.

If truthful reporting wins, its payment satisfies $P_{\mathrm{true}}\le v$. Any winning deviation pays at least $P_{\mathrm{true}}$ and earns the same value $v$. Any losing deviation earns zero utility, whereas truthful utility is nonnegative.

If truthful reporting loses, the critical-value property implies
$$ v\le P_{\mathrm{true}}. $$
Any winning deviation then has utility
$$ v-P(\hat r,\hat d,\hat\ell)\le0. $$
A losing deviation has utility zero.

Truthful reporting is therefore dominant for every identity, including singleton counterfactuals. Payments are nonnegative, and truthful utility is nonnegative, establishing individual rationality.

4.2. Welfare guarantee on finite-support truthful inputs

Call a job individually feasible if
$$ d-r\ge\ell. $$
An individually infeasible job cannot contribute to either benchmark.

At a truthful arrival time $t$, let $V_t$ be the total value of individually feasible newly arriving jobs. Their mass is at most $h_t$.

By nonatomic splitting, select a $\beta$ fraction of each newly arriving feasible type. This comparison selection has mass at most
$$ \beta h_t=q_t $$
and value exactly $\beta V_t$. Every job in it is immediately feasible.

The greedy rule selects the highest-value eligible pending mass subject to this same quota, from a pool containing the comparison selection. Hence its newly admitted value at $t$ is at least $\beta V_t$.

Admissions at different events are disjoint, and every admitted job completes. Therefore,
$$ W_M\ge\beta\sum_t V_t. $$
The benchmark can complete only individually feasible jobs, so
$$ W_{\mathrm{OPT},s}\le\sum_tV_t. $$
Combining these inequalities yields
$$ W_M\ge\beta W_{\mathrm{OPT},s}, $$
and thus
$$ C_s(b,\kappa)\le\frac1\beta =\frac1{\min\{b,1\}}. $$

This proof applies to both models because the mechanism always supplies consecutive processing.

On finite-support report profiles, there are at most $\tau$ admission events. The construction reduces to sorting residual cohort types and updating rational admitted masses. Payments are minima over finitely many recorded cutoffs. With cohort splitting represented by admitted mass per type, this uses polynomially many bit operations in $\tau$ and $L$; it does not enumerate possible types.

4.3. Exact result when $b\ge1$

The preceding bound gives $C_s(b,\kappa)\le1$. Every feasible mechanism’s schedule is also feasible for its corresponding clairvoyant benchmark, so its welfare cannot exceed benchmark welfare. Hence $C_s(b,\kappa)\ge1$, proving
$$ C_s(b,\kappa)=1 \qquad(b\ge1). $$

There is also a simpler mechanism in this range: process every individually feasible report continuously from its reported arrival, and charge zero. Active mass never exceeds the total population mass $1$. Truthful feasible jobs receive their full value, so no permitted deviation can improve their utility.

5. A lower bound for $0<b<1$

The following construction applies to every deterministic online scheduler, without any truthfulness assumption.

Choose positive masses $a,c$ satisfying
$$ a\le b,\qquad c\le b,\qquad c\ge b-a,\qquad c+2a\le1. $$

Consider two inputs with the following cohorts:

| Cohort | Mass | Release | Deadline | Length | Value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $A$ | $a$ | $0$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $B$ | $c$ | $1/2$ | $3/2$ | $1$ | $2$ |
| $C$, input $0$ | $a$ | $1$ | $3/2$ | $1$ | $2$ |
| $C$, input $1$ | $a$ | $1$ | $2$ | $1$ | $2$ |

Use the same identities and reports before time $1$ in both inputs. Any remaining mass $1-c-2a$ arrives later with an infeasible window, for example with release $3$, deadline $7/2$, length $1$, and value $1$.

All feasible windows above are tight. In the resume model, completing a length-$1$ job in a window of length $1$ requires processing it at unit speed almost everywhere in that window: the integral of a processing rate bounded by $1$ can equal $1$ only this way. The same necessity holds in the restart model.

Let $y$ be the mass of $A$ completed by the scheduler. Its processing before time $1$ determines $y$, and the two inputs have identical histories before then. Their common value of $y$ therefore satisfies
$$ 0\le y\le a\le b. $$

5.1. Input $0$

Cohort $C$ is infeasible. If the scheduler completes mass $x_0$ of $B$, the completed portions of $A$ and $B$ both require processing throughout $(1/2,1)$.

Integrating their required processing over that overlap and applying capacity gives
$$ y+x_0\le b. $$
Thus
$$ W_M^{(0)}=y+2x_0\le2b-y. $$

The benchmark can complete all $c$ mass of $B$, so
$$ W_{\mathrm{OPT},s}^{(0)}\ge2c. $$

5.2. Input $1$

If the scheduler completes masses $x_1,z_1$ of $B,C$, respectively, their required processing overlaps throughout $(1,3/2)$. Hence
$$ x_1+z_1\le b $$
and
$$ W_M^{(1)} =y+2x_1+2z_1 \le y+2b. $$

The benchmark can complete all of $A$ and $C$, together with mass $b-a$ of $B$. This selection exists because
$$ 0\le b-a\le c. $$
On each overlap interval, its active mass is
$$ a+(b-a)=b. $$
Outside the overlaps its active mass is at most $b$. Its value is
$$ a+2a+2(b-a)=2b+a. $$
Therefore,
$$ W_{\mathrm{OPT},s}^{(1)}\ge2b+a. $$

5.3. Combining the two inputs

The denominators below are positive because $0\le y\le b$. At least one input has competitive ratio at least
$$ \max\left\{ \frac{2c}{2b-y}, \frac{2b+a}{2b+y} \right\}. $$
If the scheduler has zero welfare on either input, the corresponding ratio is infinite and the bound already holds.

The maximum of two fractions with positive denominators is at least their denominator-weighted average. Consequently,
$$ \max\left\{ \frac{2c}{2b-y}, \frac{2b+a}{2b+y} \right\} \ge \frac{2c+2b+a}{4b}. $$

Set
$$ a_*=\min\left\{b,\frac{1-b}{2}\right\}. $$
Choose dyadic positive $a,c$ approaching $a_*,b$ from below, with $b-c<a$. These choices satisfy all the required inequalities: in particular,
$$ c+2a<b+2a_*\le1, \qquad c>b-a. $$
The residual mass is also dyadic and nonnegative. Thus the inputs are valid even under the more restrictive interpretation of binary rational masses as dyadic masses.

For every deterministic scheduler, the supremum over these rational inputs is therefore at least the limiting bound
$$ \frac{4b+a_*}{4b} = 1+\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{1-b}{8b}\right\}. $$
Taking the infimum over truthful mechanisms proves
$$ C_s(b,\kappa) \ge 1+\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{1-b}{8b}\right\}. $$

All lengths are $1$, and each input has at most four support types. The bound therefore applies to both models for every $\kappa\ge1$.

6. Consequences and open questions

The proved upper bound gives
$$ C_s(b,1)<5 \qquad\text{for every }b>1/5. $$
For example, when $b=1/2$, the admission mechanism is truthful and has ratio at most $2$ for every $\kappa$.

For every fixed $b>0$, the same bound is independent of $\kappa$, whereas
$$ \frac{\kappa}{\ln\kappa}+1-o(1)\longrightarrow+\infty. $$
Thus the proposed asymptotic lower bound cannot hold in the specified nonatomic model.

What remains open is the exact optimum for $0<b<1$. The results here also do not decide whether the equal-length lower bound $5$ survives for $0<b\le1/5$, or whether the two processing models have different optimal ratios.
