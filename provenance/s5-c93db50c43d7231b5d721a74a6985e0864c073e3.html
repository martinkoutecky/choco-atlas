For every fixed pair of integers \(m\ge 3\) and \(p\ge 2\), the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm P\). The proof uses an explicit rational linear program of polynomial size in the given support representation.

1. Enumerating all group types

Index the support types by \(1,\ldots,\tau\). A multiset of exactly \(m\) support types is uniquely represented by its multiplicity vector

\(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_\tau)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{\tau},\qquad \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}a_t=m.\)

Let \(\mathcal A_m\) be the set of these vectors. By the stars-and-bars formula,

\(N:=|\mathcal A_m|=\binom{\tau+m-1}{m}.\)

Also, \(N\le \tau^m\): every multiset has a unique nondecreasing enumeration, and these enumerations form a subset of all ordered \(m\)-tuples of support types. Thus \(N\) is polynomial in \(\tau\) for fixed \(m\).

For \(a\in\mathcal A_m\), its cost is

\(c_a=\sum_{1\le u<v\le\tau}a_u a_v\sum_{k=1}^{d}|x_{u,k}-x_{v,k}|^p.\)

Indeed, there are \(a_u a_v\) occurrence pairs joining distinct types \(u\) and \(v\). Pairs whose occurrences have the same type contribute zero. This formula therefore agrees exactly with the cost in the question, including multiplicities.

2. An equivalent rational linear program

Introduce a nonnegative variable \(\lambda_a\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A_m\). The requested decision is precisely feasibility of

\(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_m}a_t\lambda_a=\mu_t\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau),\)

\(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_m}c_a\lambda_a\ge q,\)

\(\lambda_a\ge 0\qquad(a\in\mathcal A_m).\)

The correspondence is exact in both directions: a group multiset determines its multiplicity vector, and the corresponding variable records its divisible group mass. The first constraints are the prescribed mass balances, and the second is the prescribed value threshold.

The mass-balance constraints are always feasible. For each type \(t\), assign mass \(\mu_t/m\) to the group containing \(m\) copies of \(t\), and assign zero to every other group.

Moreover, summing the mass-balance constraints gives

\(m\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_m}\lambda_a=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=1.\)

Hence every feasible allocation satisfies \(0\le\lambda_a\le 1/m\). The allocation polytope is consequently nonempty and compact, so its maximum value is attained. Feasibility above is therefore also equivalent to this maximum being at least \(q\).

3. Exact coefficient computation and bit lengths

Because \(p\) is an integer, every coordinate contribution is rational. Explicitly, for rational coordinates \(u/v\) and \(r/s\), with positive denominators,

\(\left|\frac uv-\frac rs\right|^p=\frac{|us-rv|^p}{(vs)^p}.\)

This formula applies to coordinates of either sign and to both odd and even \(p\).

Each input numerator and denominator has at most \(L\) bits. Thus the numerator and denominator of one powered coordinate difference have \(O(p(L+1))\) bits.

Using an enumeration of a group’s occurrences, its cost is a sum of at most

\(K=\binom m2 d\)

such rational contributions. To bound the encoding length, use the product of their denominators as a common denominator. Its bit length is \(O(Kp(L+1))\). The resulting numerator has the same bound, with an additional \(O(\log(K+1))\) bits for summation. Consequently, every \(c_a\) has numerator and denominator bit lengths bounded by

\(O\!\left(pm^2d(L+1)\right).\)

Reduction to lowest terms can only decrease these lengths.

Define

\(B:=pm^2d(L+1),\qquad E:=(\tau+1)(N+1)B+L.\)

The threshold inequality can be written as \(\sum_a c_a\lambda_a-s=q\), with one nonnegative slack variable \(s\). This gives a rational linear feasibility problem in standard nonnegative form, with \(\tau+1\) equations and \(N+1\) variables. Its total binary encoding length is \(O(E)\): the multiplicities have \(O(\log(m+1))\) bits, the costs have \(O(B)\) bits, and the masses and threshold are already included in \(L\).

4. Running time and conclusion

The polynomial-time rational linear-programming theorem states that feasibility of a finite linear system with rational coefficients can be decided exactly in time polynomial in its total binary encoding length. It applies to arbitrary rational coefficients and real variables, so all its hypotheses hold here. See [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

For completeness, a conservative bound for constructing all costs is

\(O\!\left(Nm^2d\log(p+1)B^2\right)\)

bit operations. Compute each cost directly from its occurrence pairs, use repeated squaring for the integer power, and use elementary integer arithmetic on quantities of \(O(B)\) bits. Enumerating the groups and writing the constraint matrix also takes time polynomial in \(E\).

Thus a total running-time bound is

\(O\!\left(Nm^2d\log(p+1)B^2\right)+E^{O(1)},\)

where \(N=\binom{\tau+m-1}{m}\) and \(B=pm^2d(L+1)\). For fixed \(m,p\), this is polynomial in \(\tau,d,L\), and therefore polynomial in \(L\), since the support and coordinate lists are explicitly part of the input.

The distinction from indivisible partitioning is substantive: here the group variables may take nonnegative real values, making the displayed linear program the exact formulation. No expansion of binary-encoded masses into individual agents is required.
