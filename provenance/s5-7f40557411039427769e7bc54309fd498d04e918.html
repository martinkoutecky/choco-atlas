The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and positive rational target shares \(\rho_t\) summing to \(1\), choose positive rational per-member weights \(a_t\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t=1\). For compatible replica sizes \(N\), form a weighted voting game with \(N\mu_t\) identical voters of each type, integer-scaled weights, and quota nearest half the total weight; let \(\Pi_t^N(a)\) be type \(t\)'s aggregate normalized Banzhaf power. Find weights minimizing \(\sum_{t\in T}(\lim_{N\to\infty}\Pi_t^N(a)-\rho_t)^2\), or decide whether the target is attainable.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Input target shares \(\rho_t\) are positive binary rationals with \(\sum_t\rho_t=1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Choose fixed positive rational per-member weights \(a_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=1\). Scale their ratios to the unique positive integer vector \((b_t)\) with \(\gcd_t b_t=1\).
For every positive integer \(N\) with all \(N\mu_t\) integral, create \(N\mu_t\) distinct voters of type \(t\), each of weight \(b_t\). Set \(W_N=\sum_tN\mu_tb_t\) and quota \(q_N=\lceil W_N/2\rceil\); a coalition wins when its weight is at least \(q_N\).
Let \(\Pi_t^N(a)\) be the sum of the normalized Banzhaf indices of all type-\(t\) voters. Call \(a\) admissible when every \(p_t(a)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\Pi_t^N(a)\) exists along all compatible replica sizes.
Exact limiting-target attainability asks whether there is an admissible \(a\) with \(p_t(a)=\rho_t\) for every \(t\), equivalently attaining zero squared error \(\sum_t(p_t(a)-\rho_t)^2\).

Is exact limiting-target attainability in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? There is no independent \(m\) parameter, and \(N\) is not an input.

For finite integer-weight games, the Brams–Affuso identity counts coalitions excluding voter \(i\) by coefficients of \(\prod_{j\ne i}(1+x^{w_j})\); this identity gives no inverse-design complexity classification ([Aziz–Paterson–Leech](https://www.dcs.warwick.ac.uk/report/pdfs/cs-rr-434.pdf)).
For half-quota chains using finitely many positive integer weights, whose infinitely recurring weights have greatest common divisor one, Banzhaf power ratios converge to weight ratios ([Lindner–Machover](https://www.lse.ac.uk/cpnss/assets/documents/voting-power-and-procedures/publications/2004/lindner.pdf)); this is a forward limit result rather than a complexity classification of the inverse decision problem stated here.

  • Resolved nearest-half quota ties upward, giving the ceiling of half the total integer weight; retained the weak winning inequality.

Result

Exact limiting-target attainability is in \(\mathrm{P}\). In fact, every valid instance is attainable, with the unique normalized choice

\(a_t=\rho_t/\mu_t\).

The substantive point is that every allowed rational weight vector is admissible, and its limiting type powers satisfy \(p_t(a)=\mu_ta_t\). We prove this before addressing complexity.

1. All compatible replica sizes form one chain

Fix any positive rational vector \(a\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=1\), and let \(b\) be its primitive positive integer weight vector.

Write each mass in lowest terms as \(\mu_t=u_t/v_t\), and set \(D=\operatorname{lcm}_t v_t\). Because \(\gcd(u_t,v_t)=1\), the condition \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) is equivalent to \(v_t\mid N\). Consequently, the compatible sizes are exactly \(N=kD\), where \(k\) ranges over the positive integers.

Put \(d_t=D\mu_t\). Each \(d_t\) is a positive integer. Label the voters in replica \(k\) by

\(\mathcal V_k=\{(t,j):t\in T,\ 1\leq j\leq kd_t\}\),

giving voter \((t,j)\) weight \(b_t\). These voter sets are nested, and their games are precisely the games in the question, up to relabeling.

Every coalition has integer weight. Hence its weight is at least \(\lceil W_{kD}/2\rceil\) exactly when it is at least \(W_{kD}/2\). Thus this is a chain with relative quota exactly one half, including when the total weight is odd.

2. The forward limit theorem applies to every allowed choice

We use Lindner–Machover’s Theorem 3.6: for a nested chain with relative quota one half, finitely many positive integer weight values, and greatest common divisor one among the weight values occurring infinitely often, the ratio of the normalized Banzhaf powers of any two fixed voters converges to their weight ratio. Their accompanying formula (33) also gives eventual positivity of each fixed voter’s power. [Lindner–Machover, Theorem 3.6 and formula (33)](https://www.lse.ac.uk/cpnss/assets/documents/voting-power-and-procedures/publications/2004/lindner.pdf).

All hypotheses hold here:

  • The preceding construction gives a nested chain with relative quota one half.
  • Its weight values belong to the finite set \(\{b_t:t\in T\}\).
  • Since \(d_t>0\), every weight value occurs infinitely often.
  • Their greatest common divisor is \(\gcd_t b_t=1\), by primitive normalization.

The theorem therefore applies along the sequence containing all compatible replica sizes.

3. From individual power ratios to limiting type powers

Let \(\beta_t^{(k)}\) denote the normalized Banzhaf index of one type-\(t\) voter in replica \(k\). All voters of that type have equal indices: permuting equal-weight voters preserves the game and gives a bijection between their swing coalitions.

Fix a reference type \(r\). Applying the theorem to the fixed representatives \((t,1)\) and \((r,1)\) gives

\(\displaystyle \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\beta_t^{(k)}}{\beta_r^{(k)}}=\frac{b_t}{b_r}\).

These ratios are defined for all sufficiently large \(k\), by eventual positivity. Set \(R_t^{(k)}=\beta_t^{(k)}/\beta_r^{(k)}\) for those \(k\).

Normalized indices sum to one, so

\(\displaystyle 1=\sum_s kD\mu_s\beta_s^{(k)}=kD\beta_r^{(k)}\sum_s\mu_sR_s^{(k)}\).

The aggregate type-\(t\) index consequently satisfies the exact identity

\(\displaystyle \Pi_t^{kD}(a)=kD\mu_t\beta_t^{(k)} =\frac{\mu_tR_t^{(k)}}{\sum_s\mu_sR_s^{(k)}}\).

There are finitely many types, and the limiting denominator is \(\sum_s\mu_sb_s/b_r>0\). Taking limits therefore yields

\(\displaystyle p_t(a)=\frac{\mu_tb_t}{\sum_s\mu_sb_s}\).

Since \(a\) and \(b\) have the same ratios, there is a positive rational \(c\) with \(a_t=cb_t\). The normalization gives \(c\sum_s\mu_sb_s=1\). Thus

\(\displaystyle p_t(a)=\mu_ta_t\).

This proves admissibility for every allowed weight vector and establishes the claimed limiting formula.

4. Exact attainment and uniqueness

Choose \(a_t=\rho_t/\mu_t\). These weights are positive rationals, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_ta_t=\sum_t\rho_t=1\).

The preceding result now gives \(p_t(a)=\mu_ta_t=\rho_t\) for every type. Therefore the squared error is exactly zero.

Conversely, any allowed vector attaining the target must satisfy \(\mu_ta_t=\rho_t\), forcing \(a_t=\rho_t/\mu_t\). Hence the normalized rational witness is unique.

5. Polynomial-time complexity

On the promised domain of valid inputs, the decision algorithm always returns YES. If input validation is required, positivity and the two rational sum conditions can be checked using polynomial-time integer arithmetic.

The witness is also computable in polynomial time. If \(\mu_t=u_t/v_t\) and \(\rho_t=x_t/y_t\), then

\(\displaystyle a_t=\frac{x_tv_t}{y_tu_t}\).

Multiplication and reduction by a greatest common divisor have polynomial bit complexity, and the total encoding length of these rational weights is \(O(L)\).

If the primitive integer vector is desired, write the reduced weights as \(a_t=h_t/\ell_t\), set \(Q=\operatorname{lcm}_t\ell_t\), form \(c_t=Qh_t/\ell_t\), and divide all \(c_t\) by their common greatest common divisor. The bit length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\); the resulting vector has total bit length \(O(\tau L)\). This construction is polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

No replica game needs to be constructed or evaluated. The limit theorem supplies the forward formula, and that formula makes the inverse problem explicitly solvable.
