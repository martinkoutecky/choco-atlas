Theorem 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given known criteria and breakpoints, an oracle for a society μ=½δθ₁+½δθ₂ over two unknown UTA preference types, and permitted two-criterion indifference queries, does there exist a finite adaptive strategy that recovers both complete slope tables up to permutation? Each query returns the exact distribution of answers across the society, including mass answering “None.”

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq2\) criteria with known scales \(X_i=[b_{i,0},b_{i,s_i}]\), where \(s_i\geq1\) and the breakpoints are strictly increasing. A type \(\theta\) specifies continuous piecewise linear marginals \(u_i^\theta\), with strictly positive real slopes \(\gamma_{i,\ell}^\theta\); normalize \(u_i^\theta(b_{i,0})=0\) and \(\gamma_{1,1}^\theta=1\).
A society is represented by its positive-mass support, with binary rational masses; \(\tau\) denotes support size, not the number of possible types. Here the support types are hidden behind an oracle, and \(\mu=\tfrac12\delta_{\theta_1}+\tfrac12\delta_{\theta_2}\); coincident types are allowed and their masses merge.
A query chooses distinct criteria \(i,j\), distinct \(q_i,p_i\in X_i\), and \(q_j\in X_j\). Type \(\theta\) answers the unique \(a\in X_j\) satisfying \(u_i^\theta(q_i)+u_j^\theta(q_j)=u_i^\theta(p_i)+u_j^\theta(a)\), or “None” if no such value exists. The oracle returns the exact answer distribution, including “None”; queries and answers may be real numbers.
Does there exist an adaptive strategy that, for every admissible society, terminates after finitely many queries and recovers both normalized complete slope tables up to permutation?
Known result: anonymous elicitation of two such models by finitely many indifference queries is already claimed in [Identifying two piecewise linear additive value functions from anonymous preference information](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20638v1). The equal-mass answer distribution encodes exactly the same anonymous pair of answers, including multiplicities and “None”; consequently, the stated target is already covered and cannot be presented as open.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Require at least two criteria, nondegenerate closed interval scales, finitely many known segments, and strictly positive real slopes.
  • Fix each model's positive scale by setting its first slope on criterion 1 to one, and fix marginal offsets at the lower endpoints; otherwise absolute slope recovery is undefined.
  • Permit exact real queries and answers, and interpret finite termination instance by instance without imposing a uniform query bound or bit-complexity requirement.
  • Allow coincident normalized types, as the discrete setting allows identical preferences; merge coincident atoms in the support representation.
  • Preserve hidden support types and oracle access: literally supplying the support's slope tables would already reveal the requested output.

Conclusion and known theorem

Yes. An adaptive strategy exists that terminates after finitely many queries for every admissible society and recovers the two normalized slope tables up to permutation.

We use the following existing result: for two additive models with strictly increasing piecewise linear marginals and known breakpoints, finitely many adaptive indifference queries identify both models from their anonymous answers. Identification is up to the irrelevant scale normalization of each model. This is Theorem 1 of [Auriau et al., *Identifying two piecewise linear additive value functions from anonymous preference information*](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20638v1#S1). Their query model also explicitly permits “None” when compensation is impossible; see [Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20638v1#S3).

The proof below transfers this theorem to the distribution oracle. We also handle coincident types directly.

Exact equivalence of the oracle responses

Let \(Q\) be any admissible query, and write \(A_\theta(Q)\) for its answer, including the possible symbol “None.” The distribution returned by the oracle is

\(\displaystyle D_Q=\tfrac12\delta_{A_{\theta_1}(Q)}+\tfrac12\delta_{A_{\theta_2}(Q)}.\)

For every possible response \(z\), its multiplicity in the anonymous pair is exactly \(2D_Q(\{z\})\). Consequently:

  • Two distinct responses each have mass \(\tfrac12\).
  • A response shared by both types has mass \(1\) and represents two occurrences.
  • These rules apply equally to “None.”

Thus the distribution determines the anonymous multiset of two answers exactly. Conversely, that multiset determines the distribution. This remains true when coincident support types have merged.

The query equation in the question is precisely the indifference equation used by the cited theorem. Strict positivity of the slopes makes each marginal strictly increasing, so any feasible answer is unique.

A finite preliminary stage handling coincident types

We first show how to obtain an anonymous pair of slope ratios for any two specified segments on distinct criteria.

Choose criteria \(r\ne t\), a segment \([c,d]\) of criterion \(r\), and a segment \([e,f]\) of criterion \(t\). For successive integers \(n\geq1\), put \(\varepsilon_n=(d-c)2^{-n}\) and query

\(\displaystyle q_r=c+\varepsilon_n,\qquad p_r=c,\qquad q_t=e.\)

Stop when both responses lie in \((e,f)\). If the slopes on these segments are \(\alpha^\theta\) and \(\beta^\theta\), respectively, then for sufficiently large \(n\) both answers are

\(\displaystyle a^\theta=e+\frac{\alpha^\theta}{\beta^\theta}\varepsilon_n\in(e,f).\)

Indeed, both ratios are finite and positive, whereas \(\varepsilon_n\) tends to zero. Hence this loop terminates for every admissible society. At termination, linearity on the chosen segments gives the exact anonymous ratio multiset

\(\displaystyle \left\{\!\left\{\frac{a^{\theta_1}-e}{\varepsilon_n},\frac{a^{\theta_2}-e}{\varepsilon_n}\right\}\!\right\} =\left\{\!\left\{\frac{\alpha^{\theta_1}}{\beta^{\theta_1}},\frac{\alpha^{\theta_2}}{\beta^{\theta_2}}\right\}\!\right\}.\)

Apply this procedure to the following finite collection:

  • Criterion \(1\), segment \(1\), against every segment \(\ell\) of every criterion \(j\geq2\).
  • Every segment \(\ell\geq2\) of criterion \(1\), against segment \(1\) of criterion \(2\).

The first collection yields the pairs of reciprocals \(1/\gamma_{j,\ell}^{\theta}\), since \(\gamma_{1,1}^{\theta}=1\). The second yields the pairs \(\gamma_{1,\ell}^{\theta}/\gamma_{2,1}^{\theta}\).

If every ratio multiset has two equal entries, the first collection proves equality of both types’ slopes on all criteria \(j\geq2\). In particular, their positive slopes \(\gamma_{2,1}^{\theta}\) agree. The second collection then proves equality of their remaining criterion \(1\) slopes. Their first slopes already equal \(1\). Therefore the complete normalized tables coincide.

Moreover, these measurements recover that common table: invert the ratios in the first collection, and multiply each ratio in the second collection by the recovered \(\gamma_{2,1}\). Output the common table twice.

If any measured pair differs, the types are distinct. We then proceed to the known theorem.

Simulation, normalization, and termination

For distinct types, run the theorem’s adaptive elicitation strategy. Whenever it requests a query, submit that same query to the distribution oracle and decode the anonymous answer multiset as above.

All its hypotheses hold: there are at least two criteria; each scale has finitely many known, strictly increasing breakpoints; the marginals are continuous and linear on the specified segments; every slope is strictly positive; and answers are exact. Real query values are permitted. Thus the simulated strategy receives exactly the information required by the theorem and terminates after finitely many queries.

If a recovered table \(\widehat\gamma^\kappa\) uses another positive scale normalization, convert it by

\(\displaystyle \gamma_{i,\ell}^\kappa=\frac{\widehat\gamma_{i,\ell}^\kappa}{\widehat\gamma_{1,1}^\kappa}.\)

The denominator is positive. This fixes the required first slope to \(1\) and removes the common scale ambiguity. The conditions \(u_i^\kappa(b_{i,0})=0\) then determine each marginal uniquely from its slopes.

The preliminary stage terminates because it consists of finitely many terminating loops. It either recovers a coincident pair directly or invokes the finite strategy for distinct types. Therefore the combined strategy meets the stated target for every admissible society.
