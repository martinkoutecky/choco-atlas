The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite catalog \(G\), types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and nonnegative additive values \(v_t(g)\) normalized by \(\sum_{g\in G}q_gv_t(g)=1\), define \(M_t\) as the maximum \(z\) for which a finite-support distribution \(p_t\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{G}\) has \(\sum_b p_t(b)=1\), \(\sum_b p_t(b)b_g\le q_g\) for every \(g\), and \(v_t\cdot b\ge z\) for every bundle in its support. Find finite-support masses \(y_{t,b}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_b y_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}b_g\le q_g\) for every \(g\), and \(y_{t,b}>0\) only when \(v_t\cdot b\ge \frac{2}{3}M_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a nonempty catalog of \(m\) goods. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Supplies \(q_g\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) and additive values \(v_t(g)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) are binary encoded and satisfy \(\sum_g q_gv_t(g)=1\) for each type. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Bundles are vectors \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\), with binary encoded counts; supply constraints apply to aggregate consumption, without an additional bound on individual bundles.
Define \(M_t\) as the maximum \(z\ge0\) admitting a finite-support probability distribution \(p_t\) over bundles with \(\sum_b p_t(b)b_g\le q_g\) for every good and \(\sum_g v_t(g)b_g\ge z\) whenever \(p_t(b)>0\). These benchmarks are defined by the input, not supplied separately.
An allocation consists of finitely many nonzero binary rational masses \(y_{t,b}\ge0\), satisfying \(\sum_b y_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}b_g\le q_g\), and \(y_{t,b}>0\Rightarrow\sum_gv_t(g)b_g\ge\frac23M_t\).

Can such a \(\frac23\)-guarantee allocation always be found in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is finding one NP-hard?

For finitely many agents and explicitly listed indivisible goods with nonnegative additive valuations, a polynomial-time algorithm guarantees each agent \(\frac23\) of her standard maximin share. [Garg, McGlaughlin and Taki](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/OASIcs.SOSA.2019.20)
A related distribution-over-bundles benchmark, AnyPrice share, has polynomial-time \(\frac23\) guarantees for finite agents with equal entitlements; that model uses subsets of explicitly listed goods rather than unrestricted integral catalog bundles. [Babaioff, Ezra and Feige](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.04304)

  • The catalog is nonempty, consistent with the positive normalized total value.

Result

The specified allocation problem is polynomial-time solvable. The algorithm does not compute the benchmarks exactly.

Here, “binary rational” means a rational represented by a numerator and denominator encoded in binary.

The key observation is that types can be treated independently. We construct, for each type, a probability distribution that uses at most the entire supply vector and guarantees at least \(\frac23 M_t\). Multiplying its probabilities by \(\mu_t\) then gives the required allocation.

1. Basic properties of the benchmark

Fix one type and write \(v(g)\) for its values and \(M\) for its benchmark.

Every feasible distribution at threshold \(z\) satisfies
\[ z\le \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_g v(g)b_g\right] =\sum_g v(g)\mathbb E[b_g] \le \sum_g v(g)q_g=1. \]
Thus \(0\le M\le1\).

A good with \(q_g=0\) cannot occur in any positive-probability bundle of a feasible distribution: its nonnegative expected consumption must be zero. Goods with value zero can be removed from bundles without decreasing their values. Hence we may restrict attention to
\[ I=\{g:q_g>0,\ v(g)>0\}. \]
Normalization implies that \(I\) is nonempty.

Attainment

The unrestricted bundle counts do not prevent the benchmark from being attained.

For each \(g\in I\), put \(B_g=\lceil1/v(g)\rceil\). For any feasible threshold \(z\le1\), replace every bundle coordinate by \(\min\{b_g,B_g\}\). This preserves the threshold:

  • If no coordinate changes, its value is unchanged.
  • If a coordinate changes, the resulting bundle contains \(B_g\) copies of that good and therefore has value at least \(B_gv(g)\ge1\ge z\).

Consumption only decreases. Consequently, every feasible threshold has a witness using the finite set
\[ \mathcal U=\prod_{g\in I}\{0,\ldots,B_g\}. \]

The minimum bundle value in any such witness is itself a feasible threshold. It belongs to a finite set of bundle values and is at most \(1\), by the expectation bound above. Therefore the maximum feasible threshold exists.

At that maximum, the probabilities satisfy a finite linear system with rational coefficients. A feasible rational linear system has a rational feasible point, so \(M\) has a finite-support rational witness.

Detecting a zero benchmark

Let \(Q=\sum_{g\in I}q_g\). Then
\[ M>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q\ge1. \]

Indeed, every positive-value bundle contains at least one unit of a good in \(I\). Thus a distribution guaranteeing a positive value must satisfy
\[ 1\le\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{g\in I}b_g\right]\le Q. \]

Conversely, if \(Q\ge1\), choose rational numbers \(r_g\in[0,q_g]\) with \(\sum_{g\in I}r_g=1\). Such numbers can be obtained by filling this total greedily. Giving singleton bundle \(e_g\) probability \(r_g\) guarantees
\[ a:=\min_{g\in I}v(g)>0. \]
Therefore \(M\ge a\).

Because every positive input rational has a denominator with at most \(L\) bits, \(a\ge2^{-L}\). Hence
\[ M>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M\ge2^{-L}. \]

2. A polynomial-size linear program for a trial threshold

Fix a rational trial threshold \(z\in(0,1]\). We construct a rational linear program with these two properties:

  • If a distribution guaranteeing \(z\) exists, the linear program is feasible.
  • Every feasible solution yields a distribution guaranteeing \(\frac34z\), with expected consumption at most \(q\).

Bounding counts for this trial

For \(g\in I\), set
\[ N_g=\left\lceil\frac{z}{v(g)}\right\rceil. \]

Every bundle of value at least \(z\) can be clipped coordinatewise to these bounds while retaining value at least \(z\). If clipping changes any coordinate \(g\), the clipped bundle contains \(N_g\) copies of that good, whose value alone is at least \(z\).

Thus every distribution feasible at \(z\) can be replaced by one supported on bundles satisfying \(0\le b_g\le N_g\).

Moreover, \(z\le1\) and \(v(g)\ge2^{-L}\) imply \(N_g\le2^L\).

Representing counts by binary blocks

Represent the integers from \(0\) through \(N_g\) as subset sums of a list of positive blocks. Take blocks of sizes \(1,2,4,\ldots\), truncating the final block so that their total is exactly \(N_g\).

To verify that every count is representable, suppose existing blocks represent every integer from \(0\) through \(S\). The next block has size \(k\le S+1\). The possible sums after adding it include the intervals
\[ \{0,\ldots,S\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{k,\ldots,k+S\}, \]
which together cover every integer from \(0\) through \(S+k\). Induction proves the claim.

There are at most \(L+1\) blocks per good. Enumerate all blocks by \(i=1,\ldots,n\), where block \(i\) consists of \(k_i\) copies of good \(g_i\), and has value
\[ a_i=k_iv(g_i). \]
Thus \(n\le m(L+1)\).

A subset of these blocks defines an integral catalog bundle. Conversely, every bundle within the bounds \(N_g\) has such a representation.

Rounding values

Set
\[ K=4n,\qquad H=3n,\qquad w_i=\min\!\left\{H,\left\lfloor\frac{Ka_i}{z}\right\rfloor\right\}. \]

Call a block subset \(S\) accepted when \(\sum_{i\in S}w_i\ge H\).

Every accepted subset has actual value at least \(\frac34z\), because
\[ \sum_{i\in S}a_i \ge\frac zK\sum_{i\in S}w_i \ge\frac{Hz}{K} =\frac34z. \]

Conversely, every block subset of actual value at least \(z\) is accepted. If a selected block has rounded weight \(H\), this is immediate. Otherwise no selected weight was capped, and
\[ \sum_{i\in S}w_i =\sum_{i\in S}\left\lfloor\frac{Ka_i}{z}\right\rfloor \ge\frac Kz\sum_{i\in S}a_i-|S| \ge K-n=H. \]

These are the two rounding implications the algorithm needs.

Encoding distributions as flows

Construct a directed acyclic graph with vertices \((i,s)\), where \(i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\) and \(s\in\{0,\ldots,H\}\). For every block \(i\) and state \(s\), include:

  • A skip arc from \((i-1,s)\) to \((i,s)\).
  • A take arc from \((i-1,s)\) to \((i,\min\{H,s+w_i\})\).

Keep these as distinct arcs even when their endpoints coincide.

A path from \((0,0)\) to \((n,H)\) selects an accepted subset of blocks. Associate consumption \(k_i\) of good \(g_i\) with each take arc for block \(i\), and zero consumption with skip arcs.

The linear program asks for a nonnegative unit flow from \((0,0)\) to \((n,H)\), subject to
\[ \sum_{i:g_i=g}k_i\sum_{s=0}^{H}f^{\mathrm{take}}_{i,s}\le q_g \qquad\text{for every }g. \]

This graph has \(O(n^2)\) vertices and arcs.

If a distribution guaranteeing \(z\) exists, clip its bundles, represent their counts by blocks, and choose one representing path for each bundle. The rounding argument makes every path accepted. The weighted sum of their path flows is feasible and preserves the clipped expected consumption.

Conversely, any feasible unit flow decomposes into source-to-sink paths. Their weights sum to one, so they define a probability distribution. The resource constraints give expected consumption at most \(q\), and every path’s bundle has value at least \(\frac34z\).

This proves both claimed properties of the trial linear program.

3. Finding the trial threshold without computing \(M\)

If \(Q<1\), then \(M=0\), and the empty bundle suffices.

Otherwise consider, in decreasing order,
\[ z_j=\left(\frac89\right)^j, \qquad j=0,1,\ldots,6L. \]
Solve the trial linear program at each threshold and stop at the first feasible one.

This scan always succeeds. Indeed,
\[ 2\cdot8^6=524288<531441=9^6, \]
so
\[ z_{6L}<2^{-L}\le M. \]
A benchmark witness is feasible at every threshold at most \(M\), and therefore the trial linear program is feasible at \(z_{6L}\).

Let \(j\) be the first successful index.

If \(j=0\), the resulting distribution guarantees \(\frac34\), which is at least \(\frac23M\), since \(M\le1\).

If \(j>0\), infeasibility at \(z_{j-1}\) implies \(z_{j-1}>M\): otherwise a benchmark witness would make that trial feasible. Hence
\[ z_j=\frac89z_{j-1}>\frac89M. \]
The distribution returned at \(z_j\) therefore guarantees
\[ \frac34z_j>\frac34\cdot\frac89M=\frac23M. \]

Thus the procedure produces the required distribution for one type.

4. Combining the types

Apply the procedure independently to every listed type. Let \(p_t(b)\) denote its resulting probabilities, including the empty-bundle distribution when \(M_t=0\). Define
\[ y_{t,b}=\mu_t p_t(b). \]

The masses satisfy \(\sum_b y_{t,b}=\mu_t\). For each good,
\[ \sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}b_g =\sum_t\mu_t\sum_b p_t(b)b_g \le\sum_t\mu_t q_g=q_g. \]
Every positive mass is assigned to a bundle worth at least \(\frac23M_t\) to its type. Thus these masses form a valid allocation.

5. Running time and output encoding

We use the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming: feasibility of a finite rational linear system can be decided, and a rational feasible point returned when one exists, in time polynomial in its encoded length.

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • There are at most \(6L+1\) trials per type.
  • Each trial has \(n=O(mL)\) blocks.
  • Its linear program has \(O(m^2L^2)\) variables and \(O(m^2L^2+m)\) constraints.
  • Block counts have \(O(L)\) bits. Trial thresholds have \(O(L)\) bits, since \(j\le6L\). Rounded weights are at most \(3n\).
  • Consequently all coefficients have \(O(L+\log(mL))\) bits, and the entire linear program has polynomial encoded length.

In particular, the algorithm makes \(O(\tau L)\) rational linear-programming calls of these sizes. Its total running time is
\[ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \]

For output extraction, repeatedly select a positive-flow source-to-sink path and subtract its minimum arc flow. Each iteration eliminates at least one positive arc. Acyclicity ensures that this decomposes the entire flow, using at most \(O(m^2L^2)\) paths per type.

The rational flow coordinates have polynomial bit length. The decomposition performs only polynomially many rational subtractions, so its probabilities also have polynomial bit length. Every extracted bundle has counts at most \(N_g\le2^L\). Multiplication by the input masses and merging duplicate bundles preserve polynomial encoding length.

The resulting allocation therefore has at most \(O(\tau m^2L^2)\) listed bundles and polynomial total output length.

The expectation argument correctly establishes \(M\le 1\): every bundle in a distribution feasible at threshold \(z\) has value at least \(z\), while its expected value is at most the normalized supply value \(1\). The empty-bundle distribution establishes feasibility at threshold \(0\).

The restriction to \(I=\{g:q_g>0,\ v(g)>0\}\) is valid:

  • Removing zero-value goods cannot reduce bundle value.

There is no boundary-case error involving zero weights, equality at the threshold, or capped weights.

5. Flow linear program

The graph correctly represents accepted subsets. Each path makes exactly one skip-or-take decision per block, while the state records accumulated rounded weight capped at \(H\). Reaching \((n,H)\) is therefore equivalent to acceptance.

Keeping skip and take arcs distinct when their endpoints coincide is necessary and correctly specified: their consumption contributions can differ even when their rounded-state transitions coincide.

Both directions of the feasibility correspondence hold:

  • A distribution feasible at threshold \(z\) can be clipped, represented by block subsets, and converted into accepted path flows without increasing consumption.
  • A feasible nonnegative unit flow decomposes into accepted paths. The associated resource inequalities give the required expected consumption bounds, and the rounding implication gives value at least \(\frac34z\) on every positive-probability bundle.

Acyclicity excludes circulation components. Standard unit-flow conservation ensures that the decomposition accounts for the entire flow and that the path probabilities sum to one.

The direction of this implication is correct. Feasibility above \(M\), which the rounded relaxation may permit, does not undermine the argument.

Every positive allocation mass inherits its type’s bundle-value guarantee. The zero-benchmark case remains valid after multiplication by the positive type mass.
