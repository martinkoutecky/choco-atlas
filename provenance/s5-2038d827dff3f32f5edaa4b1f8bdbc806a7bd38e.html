The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), additive valuation vectors \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}^m\), relative entitlements \(\omega_t>0\), and per-capita supplies \(\rho\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^m\), compute a finite-support mass assignment \(z_{t,b}\ge0\) over integral bundle vectors \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\) satisfying \(\sum_b z_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}z_{t,b}b_e=\rho_e\) for every item kind \(e\), such that whenever \(z_{t,b}z_{u,d}>0\), either \(v_t(b)/\omega_t\ge v_t(d)/\omega_u\), or there is an \(e\) with \(d_e>0\), \(v_t(e)>0\), and \(v_t(b)/\omega_t\ge (v_t(d)-v_t(e))/\omega_u\), or there is an \(e\) with \(b_e>0\), \(v_t(e)<0\), and \((v_t(b)-v_t(e))/\omega_t\ge v_t(d)/\omega_u\); the target complexity is polynomial in \(m\), \(|T|\), and the encoding length, independently of the population multiplier \(K\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(T\) be this support, with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\).
There are \(m\) item kinds, supplies \(\rho\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\), signed additive valuations \(v_t\in\mathbb Q^m\), and relative entitlements \(\omega_t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\). All input rationals are binary encoded, with total bit length \(L\); multiplying every entitlement by the same positive constant changes nothing.
A bundle is \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), allowing multiple copies of each kind, with \(v_t(b)=\sum_{e=1}^m b_e v_t(e)\). A mass assignment is a finitely supported family \(z_{t,b}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), represented explicitly by its positive entries and binary encoded bundles, satisfying \(\sum_b z_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}z_{t,b}b_e=\rho_e\) for every kind \(e\).
The assignment is weighted envy-free up to one item (WEF1) if every ordered pair \((t,b),(u,d)\) of positive entries, including pairs with \(t=u\), satisfies at least one of:
\(\frac{v_t(b)}{\omega_t}\ge\frac{v_t(d)}{\omega_u}\);
\(\exists e:\ d_e>0,\ v_t(e)>0,\ \frac{v_t(b)}{\omega_t}\ge\frac{v_t(d)-v_t(e)}{\omega_u}\);
\(\exists e:\ b_e>0,\ v_t(e)<0,\ \frac{v_t(b)-v_t(e)}{\omega_t}\ge\frac{v_t(d)}{\omega_u}\).
Each witness removes one copy. Let \(K\) denote any positive integer for which every \(K\mu_t\) and \(K\rho_e\) is integral: the corresponding finite society has \(K\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\) and \(K\rho_e\) copies of kind \(e\).
Is computing a WEF1 mass assignment solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), independently of \(K\), or is this search problem NP-hard?
For finite societies with signed additive valuations and arbitrary positive entitlements, WEF1 allocations always exist and can be computed in \(O(nq^2\log q)\) arithmetic operations, where \(n\) counts agents and \(q\) counts individual items. [Lin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02724v1)
No result settling this exact mass-assignment problem was found in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total society mass to one, so supplies are per capita.
  • Require relative entitlements to be positive binary rationals; retain common-scaling invariance rather than impose a separate normalization.
  • Specify rational assignment masses and an explicit finite-support representation with binary encoded bundle coordinates.
  • Define K as a replication multiplier clearing the denominators of masses and supplies.
  • Interpret an item witness as removing exactly one copy of a kind, and require comparisons between different bundles assigned to the same type.

Partial progress: compressing weighted picking sequences

The general complexity question remains unresolved here. The following additional cases are polynomial-time solvable, independently of $K$, with arbitrary positive entitlements:

  • No objective chores: every positive-supply kind is nonnegative for at least one supported type.
  • Nonpositive valuations: $v_t(e)\leq 0$ for every type and kind.

The first case permits valuations that differ in sign across types. Both cases permit multiple kinds and unequal entitlements. The new ingredient is an exact compression of weighted picking sequences: arithmetic searches replace the enumeration of agents and item copies.

I prove these claims below. I also re-establish the compact-certificate result needed to discuss hardness precisely.

1. Common setup and neutral kinds

Fix an arbitrary order of the supported types. Represent the population by consecutive intervals in $[0,1)$, with the interval of type $t$ having length $\mu_t$. A cell is a subinterval whose agents have the same type, integer bundle, and picking counter. Its length is its mass.

Let $K_0$ be the product of the denominators of all masses and supplies. Then
$$ K_0\mu_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}, \qquad K_0\rho_e\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}, \qquad \log K_0\leq L. $$
The intervals can therefore be interpreted as $K_0$ finite agents, in increasing interval order. This interpretation is used for correctness; the algorithms do not enumerate these agents.

Scale all entitlements by one common positive rational so that they become positive integers $a_t$. This preserves every WEF1 inequality. Put
$$ A=\operatorname{lcm}_{t\in T}a_t. $$
Both the scaling and the computation of $A$ have polynomial bit complexity. In particular, $\log A$ is polynomial in $\tau,L$.

For an agent of type $t$, a weighted forward picking sequence assigns her potential picks the times
$$ 0,\frac1{a_t},\frac2{a_t},\ldots. $$
All these times belong to the grid with spacing $1/A$. Ties are broken by increasing interval position.

Kinds of zero supply can be discarded throughout.

Neutral-kind extension lemma

Suppose a kind $e$ satisfies
$$ v_u(e)\leq 0\quad\text{for all }u, \qquad v_t(e)=0\quad\text{for some }t. $$
Any existing WEF1 assignment can be extended to allocate supply $\rho_e$ of this kind entirely to type $t$, without destroying WEF1.

Indeed, define
$$ p=\left\lfloor\frac{\rho_e}{\mu_t}\right\rfloor, \qquad \delta=\rho_e-p\mu_t. $$
Give every agent of type $t$ exactly $p$ copies, and give the first $\delta$ mass of that type one additional copy. This uses exactly
$$ p\mu_t+\delta=\rho_e $$
units of supply.

Every agent’s value of her own bundle is unchanged: only type $t$ receives the added kind, and it values that kind at zero. Every agent’s value of any other bundle weakly decreases. Thus any previous no-envy comparison or removal witness remains valid.

This operation introduces at most one new cell boundary. Moreover, $\delta$ is a multiple of $1/K_0$, so it has the stated finite interpretation.

Consequently, kinds with $\max_t v_t(e)=0$ can be handled after the main allocation.

2. Fairness of forward weighted picking

Consider a finite society and kinds for which some agent has strictly positive value. Start every agent’s counter at zero. Repeatedly:

  • among agents who value some remaining item strictly positively, choose one minimizing her counter divided by her entitlement;
  • give her a remaining item of maximum value to her;
  • increase her counter by one.

Break agent ties by the fixed order and item ties by a fixed kind order.

Every pick has strictly positive value to its recipient. All items are eventually allocated: while any item remains, an agent valuing it positively is eligible.

Proposition

The resulting allocation is WEF1.

Proof

Fix distinct agents $i,j$, with entitlements $a_i,a_j$. Write their bundles as $G_i,G_j$.

For $x>0$, let
$$ N_i(x)=\bigl|\{e\in G_i:v_i(e)\geq x\}\bigr|, \qquad N_j(x)=\bigl|\{e\in G_j:v_i(e)\geq x\}\bigr|. $$
These counts include individual copies.

Suppose $N_j(x)>0$. Consider the last pick by $j$ of an item whose value to $i$ is at least $x$. Just before this pick, let $k_i,k_j$ be the agents’ counters.

The item about to be picked is still available and has positive value to $i$, so $i$ is eligible. Since $j$ is selected,
$$ \frac{k_j}{a_j}\leq\frac{k_i}{a_i}. $$
Every earlier pick by $i$ occurred while that item remained available. Greedy choice therefore made each such pick worth at least $x$ to $i$. Also, $j$ has already made the other $N_j(x)-1$ relevant picks. Hence
$$ N_i(x)\geq k_i, \qquad k_j\geq N_j(x)-1. $$
Combining these inequalities gives
$$ \frac{N_i(x)}{a_i} \geq \frac{N_j(x)-1}{a_j}. $$
If $N_j(x)=0$, nonnegativity gives the corresponding inequality. Thus, for every $x>0$,
$$ \frac{N_i(x)}{a_i} \geq \frac{\max\{N_j(x)-1,0\}}{a_j}. $$

If $G_j$ contains no item positively valued by $i$, then
$$ v_i(G_i)\geq 0\geq v_i(G_j), $$
which proves no envy.

Otherwise choose an item $g\in G_j$ maximizing $v_i(g)$. It has strictly positive value. Since every item in $G_i$ is positive to $i$,
$$ v_i(G_i)=\int_0^\infty N_i(x)\,dx. $$
Removing $g$ removes one item from every positive threshold count where that count is nonzero. Therefore
$$ \sum_{e\in G_j\setminus\{g\}}\max\{v_i(e),0\} = \int_0^\infty \max\{N_j(x)-1,0\}\,dx. $$
Integrating the threshold inequalities yields
$$ \frac{v_i(G_i)}{a_i} \geq \frac{\sum_{e\in G_j\setminus\{g\}}\max\{v_i(e),0\}}{a_j} \geq \frac{v_i(G_j)-v_i(g)}{a_j}. $$
This is a valid positive-item removal witness.

The integrals are merely identities for finite sums of nonnegative numbers; equivalently, one can sum over the finitely many valuation thresholds. Comparisons of an agent with herself hold with equality. This proves WEF1. $\square$

3. Polynomial compression when there are no objective chores

Assume every positive-supply kind satisfies
$$ \max_t v_t(e)\geq 0. $$
First set aside kinds whose maximum value is zero. The neutral-kind lemma will allocate them later.

For every remaining kind, some type has strictly positive value. We now implement the forward procedure from Section 2 using cells.

Let
$$ S=\sum_e\rho_e, \qquad Q=K_0S, $$
where this sum ranges over the remaining kinds. If $S=0$, only the neutral-kind extension is needed.

A cell $C$ records its type $t(C)$, mass $h_C$, bundle $b_C$, and counter $k_C$. Initially there are $\tau$ cells with empty bundles and zero counters.

3.1. Jumping to the next exhaustion time

At the start of a stage, let $x_e>0$ denote each remaining supply. For each type, choose its favorite available kind using the fixed tie-breaking order. Call the type active if this favorite has positive value. Write its favorite as $e_t$.

While no kind exhausts, favorites and active types stay unchanged.

For an active cell $C$ of type $t$, its remaining potential pick times are
$$ \frac{k_C}{a_t}, \frac{k_C+1}{a_t}, \ldots. $$
At grid time $\lambda=j/A$, the numbers of these picks strictly before and at or before $\lambda$ are respectively
$$ P_C^{<}(j) = \max\left\{0,\left\lceil\frac{a_tj}{A}\right\rceil-k_C\right\}, $$
$$ P_C^{\leq}(j) = \max\left\{0,\left\lfloor\frac{a_tj}{A}\right\rfloor+1-k_C\right\}. $$
Define the corresponding demands for kind $e$ by
$$ D_e^{<}(j) = \sum_{\substack{C\text{ active}\\e_{t(C)}=e}}h_CP_C^{<}(j), \qquad D_e^{\leq}(j) = \sum_{\substack{C\text{ active}\\e_{t(C)}=e}}h_CP_C^{\leq}(j). $$

Find the smallest nonnegative integer $j$ for which
$$ D_e^{\leq}(j)\geq x_e $$
for at least one remaining kind $e$. This is a monotone predicate, so binary search applies.

A polynomial-bit upper endpoint is
$$ U=A(2Q+1). $$
To check this bound, maintain inductively the finite interpretation of the cells. Then $k_C\leq Q$ and $h_C\geq 1/K_0$. At time $U/A=2Q+1$, any active cell has at least $Q+2$ scheduled picks. Its favorite therefore has hypothetical demand at least
$$ \frac{Q+2}{K_0}>S\geq x_e. $$
Thus the predicate is true at $U$.

By minimality of $j$,
$$ D_e^{<}(j)<x_e \quad\text{for every remaining kind }e. $$
For $j=0$, this follows because the strict demand is zero. For $j>0$, every event lies on the $1/A$ grid, so strict demand at $j/A$ equals inclusive demand at $(j-1)/A$.

Allocate all picks strictly before $j/A$ in one batch:

  • add $P_C^{<}(j)$ copies of $e_{t(C)}$ to each active cell’s bundle;
  • increase its counter by that integer;
  • subtract the corresponding demands from supplies.

The strict inequalities ensure that no kind exhausts during this batch. Thus every planned favorite remains available, and the batch exactly reproduces the finite picking sequence.

3.2. Processing the boundary time

After batching, consider the active cells whose next pick time is exactly $j/A$. Process their agents in increasing interval order, giving each at most one pick.

Within a cell, give its current favorite to the largest initial subinterval permitted by the remaining supply. If the kind exhausts inside the cell, split the cell at that point and recompute the favorite for the unprocessed remainder. If the type no longer has a positive remaining kind, its unprocessed agents become inactive.

At least one kind exhausts at this boundary time. To prove this, choose a kind $e$ attaining the inclusive-demand threshold. After the batch, its remaining supply is at most the mass of boundary-time agents initially choosing it. If $e$ never exhausted, it would remain the favorite of all those agents: removing other kinds cannot displace an available favorite. They would consume at least its remaining supply, a contradiction.

Each stage therefore exhausts at least one kind. There are at most $m$ stages.

A new cell boundary is introduced only when a kind exhausts inside a cell. Each kind exhausts once, so there are at most $\tau+m$ cells before the neutral-kind extension. Including that extension still gives at most $\tau+m$ cells, because its kinds were set aside from the exhaustion stages.

3.3. Correctness and bit complexity

Every supply, cell length, and new boundary remains a multiple of $1/K_0$:

  • a batch allocates an integer number of copies over a cell;
  • a boundary-time step allocates one copy over either a whole cell or an interval whose length equals a remaining supply.

Inductively, the procedure exactly implements the finite forward allocation for $K_0$ agents. Section 2 proves WEF1, including comparisons between different agents of the same type. Aggregating cells into type–bundle entries preserves those comparisons.

Each bundle coordinate and counter is at most $Q$. Cell lengths have denominator dividing $K_0$. Supplies have numerator at most $Q$ when expressed with denominator $K_0$. All therefore have polynomial encoding length.

There are at most $m$ stages. Each binary search has polynomially many iterations because $\log U$ is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. Each demand evaluation and boundary traversal uses polynomially many operations on polynomial-length numbers.

Finally apply the neutral-kind lemma and merge identical type–bundle entries if desired.

Proved conclusion. When there are no objective chores, a WEF1 mass assignment is computable in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$, with arbitrary positive entitlements and signed valuations.

4. Fairness of reversed weighted picking for objective chores

Now consider a finite society in which every item has strictly negative value to every agent.

First generate a forward sequence of length equal to the number of items. At every step choose an agent minimizing her counter divided by her entitlement, increase her counter by one, and record her identity. All agents participate.

Then traverse the recorded sequence backwards. At each turn, give the selected agent a remaining item of maximum value to her, equivalently minimum cost.

Proposition

The resulting allocation is WEF1, with any necessary witness obtained by removing one strictly negative item from the comparing agent’s own bundle.

Proof

Fix distinct agents $i,j$. Define the positive cost
$$ c_i(e)=-v_i(e)>0. $$
Let their final bundles be $C_i,C_j$. For $x>0$, put
$$ B_i(x)=\bigl|\{e\in C_i:c_i(e)\geq x\}\bigr|, \qquad B_j(x)=\bigl|\{e\in C_j:c_i(e)\geq x\}\bigr|. $$

Suppose $B_i(x)>0$. Consider the first pick, in the reversed execution, at which $i$ receives an item of cost at least $x$.

At that turn, every remaining item has cost at least $x$ to $i$, because $i$ selects a minimum-cost remaining item. All items allocated later consequently have cost at least $x$ to $i$.

Let $k_i$ be $i$’s counter immediately before the corresponding event in the original forward sequence, and let $p_j$ be $j$’s counter then. Forward selection gives
$$ \frac{k_i}{a_i}\leq\frac{p_j}{a_j}. $$
In the reversed execution, $i$ has exactly $k_i$ picks after the considered turn, and $j$ has exactly $p_j$ picks after it.

By the choice of the first threshold-reaching pick,
$$ B_i(x)=k_i+1. $$
All of $j$’s later items have cost at least $x$, so
$$ B_j(x)\geq p_j. $$
It follows that
$$ \frac{B_i(x)-1}{a_i}\leq\frac{B_j(x)}{a_j}. $$
When $B_i(x)=0$, the inequality with a zero left side holds. Hence
$$ \frac{\max\{B_i(x)-1,0\}}{a_i} \leq \frac{B_j(x)}{a_j} \quad\text{for every }x>0. $$

If $C_i$ is empty, agent $i$ already has no envy, since every other bundle has nonpositive value to her.

Otherwise let $g\in C_i$ maximize $c_i(g)$. Integrating the threshold inequalities gives
$$ \frac{c_i(C_i)-c_i(g)}{a_i} \leq \frac{c_i(C_j)}{a_j}. $$
Equivalently,
$$ \frac{v_i(C_i)-v_i(g)}{a_i} \geq \frac{v_i(C_j)}{a_j}. $$
Since $v_i(g)<0$, this is the required own-bundle removal witness. $\square$

5. Polynomial compression for nonpositive valuations

Assume now that
$$ v_t(e)\leq 0 \quad\text{for all }t,e. $$
Set aside kinds whose maximum value is zero. Every remaining kind is an objective chore with strictly negative values. Allocate these by compressing the reversed procedure from Section 4, then use the neutral-kind extension.

Let
$$ S=\sum_e\rho_e, \qquad Q=K_0S $$
for the strictly negative kinds. If $S=0$, the neutral-kind extension suffices.

5.1. Computing the forward sequence’s terminal counters

For $j\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}$ define
$$ F^+(j)= \sum_t\mu_t\left(\left\lfloor\frac{a_tj}{A}\right\rfloor+1\right). $$
Find the smallest $j$ satisfying $F^+(j)\geq S$. Binary search on $[0,U]$, with $U=A(2Q+1)$, suffices.

Set
$$ \ell_t=\left\lceil\frac{a_tj}{A}\right\rceil, \qquad E=\left\{t:\frac{a_tj}{A}\in\mathbb Z\right\}. $$
The number of events per agent strictly before $j/A$ is $\ell_t$.

We have
$$ B:=\sum_t\mu_t\ell_t\leq S. $$
For $j=0$, $B=0$. For $j>0$, writing $A/a_t$ as a positive integer gives
$$ \left\lceil\frac{a_tj}{A}\right\rceil = \left\lfloor\frac{a_t(j-1)}{A}\right\rfloor+1. $$
Thus $B=F^+(j-1)<S$.

Also,
$$ F^+(j)=B+\sum_{t\in E}\mu_t. $$
Consequently,
$$ \delta:=S-B \quad\text{satisfies}\quad 0\leq\delta\leq\sum_{t\in E}\mu_t. $$

Give counter $\ell_t+1$ to the first $\delta$ mass among the eligible type intervals, in population order, and counter $\ell_t$ to everyone else. These are exactly the terminal counters after the first $Q$ events of the finite forward sequence: all events strictly before $j/A$ occur, followed by an initial segment of its tied events.

Only one type interval can be genuinely split by this initial segment. Thus at most $\tau+1$ cells are needed.

5.2. Jumping backwards to exhaustion events

For each cell $C$, let $k_C$ now mean its number of unprocessed reverse picks. Its remaining event times are
$$ \frac{k_C-1}{a_t},\frac{k_C-2}{a_t},\ldots,0 $$
when $k_C>0$.

At a stage, each type has a favorite remaining chore $e_t$. At grid time $j/A$, the numbers of remaining events strictly above and at or above that time are
$$ R_C^{>}(j) = \max\left\{0,k_C-\left\lfloor\frac{a_tj}{A}\right\rfloor-1\right\}, $$
$$ R_C^{\geq}(j) = \max\left\{0,k_C-\left\lceil\frac{a_tj}{A}\right\rceil\right\}. $$
Define
$$ H_e^{>}(j)= \sum_{C:e_{t(C)}=e}h_CR_C^{>}(j), \qquad H_e^{\geq}(j)= \sum_{C:e_{t(C)}=e}h_CR_C^{\geq}(j). $$

Find the largest nonnegative integer $j$ for which
$$ H_e^{\geq}(j)\geq x_e $$
for some remaining kind.

This predicate is nonincreasing in $j$. It is false at $U$, because every counter is at most $Q$.

It is true at zero. Indeed, the invariant
$$ \sum_Ch_Ck_C=\sum_ex_e $$
holds initially and is preserved by every allocation. At zero, every remaining event is counted, so
$$ \sum_eH_e^{\geq}(0)=\sum_ex_e. $$
If every $H_e^{\geq}(0)$ were strictly smaller than its remaining supply, this equality would be impossible.

Thus binary search finds the desired largest $j$. Since all events lie on the grid,
$$ H_e^{>}(j)=H_e^{\geq}(j+1)<x_e $$
for every remaining kind.

Batch all events strictly above $j/A$: add the indicated integer copies to each cell’s bundle, decrease its counter accordingly, and subtract the demand from supplies. No kind exhausts during this batch, so favorites remain correct.

Then process the events exactly at $j/A$ in decreasing interval order, as required by reversing the forward tie order. Within a cell, split only when a kind exhausts, and recompute the favorite for the unprocessed remainder.

The same demand argument as in Section 3 proves that at least one kind exhausts during this boundary step. Hence there are at most $m$ stages.

5.3. Complexity and correctness

Every operation preserves the $1/K_0$ grid. Therefore the procedure exactly reproduces the finite reversed allocation proved WEF1 in Section 4.

There are initially at most $\tau+1$ cells. Each exhausted kind introduces at most one additional boundary. The subsequent neutral-kind extensions introduce at most one boundary per set-aside kind. Thus the output has at most
$$ \tau+m+1 $$
positive entries before merging.

Counters and bundle coordinates are at most $Q$. Cell masses, supplies, search endpoints, and demand evaluations have polynomial encoding length. There are polynomially many searches and cell operations, just as in the forward case.

Proved conclusion. When all valuations are nonpositive, a WEF1 mass assignment is computable in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$, with arbitrary positive entitlements.

6. Compact certificates for the unrestricted problem

For completeness, the unrestricted problem always has a polynomial-size valid answer. This does not follow from the compressed algorithms above; it uses the finite existence theorem supplied in the question.

6.1. Existence and support reduction

Construct the conceptual finite society with $K_0\mu_t$ agents of each type and $K_0\rho_e$ copies of each kind. Give agents of type $t$ valuation $v_t$ and entitlement proportional to $\omega_t$.

This satisfies the hypotheses of the finite WEF1 existence theorem for signed additive valuations and positive entitlements. Its removal witnesses are exactly the witnesses required here. [Lin et al., Definition 2.2 and Theorem 3.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02724v1)

Aggregate the resulting allocation by type and bundle, dividing agent counts by $K_0$. This gives a rational WEF1 mass assignment. Distinct supported entries have distinct representative agents, including entries of the same type; a comparison of an entry with itself holds with equality.

For each supported pair define
$$ a_{t,b}=(\mathbf e_t,b)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m}. $$
The constraints are
$$ \sum_{t,b}z_{t,b}a_{t,b}=(\mu,\rho). $$

If the supported columns are dependent, choose a nonzero rational dependence $h$. The type-coordinate equations imply
$$ \sum_{t,b}h_{t,b}=0, $$
so $h$ has both signs. Set
$$ \varepsilon= \min_{h_{t,b}>0}\frac{z_{t,b}}{h_{t,b}}, $$
and replace $z$ by $z-\varepsilon h$. This preserves nonnegativity and all equalities, and deletes at least one supported entry.

Deleting entries preserves WEF1 because its inequalities depend only on supported type–bundle pairs. Repetition leaves at most
$$ m+\tau $$
linearly independent supported columns.

6.2. Encoding bound

Every original bundle coordinate is at most $K_0\rho_e$, whose binary length is polynomial in $L$.

Suppose the reduced support has size $s\leq m+\tau$. Select $s$ rows on which its columns form an invertible integer matrix $B$. The selected equations have the form
$$ Bz=r/K_0 $$
with integer $r$.

Put
$$ H=\max\{1,K_0,K_0\rho_1,\ldots,K_0\rho_m\}. $$
Every entry of $B$ and $r$ is bounded in absolute value by $H$. Cramer’s rule gives
$$ z_j=\frac{\det B_j(r)}{K_0\det B}, $$
and determinant expansion gives
$$ |\det B|,\ |\det B_j(r)|\leq s!H^s. $$
Since $\log H=O(L)$, every mass has polynomial encoding length.

Thus every unrestricted instance has a polynomially encoded WEF1 assignment with at most $m+\tau$ positive entries.

6.3. Consequence for hardness formulations

A candidate assignment is checkable in polynomial time: verify the mass and supply equalities and check all WEF1 alternatives for every ordered pair. For $s$ entries, checking witnesses requires $O(s^2m)$ rational comparisons.

Together with the preceding existence bound, this gives a polynomially balanced total search formulation in $\mathrm{TFNP}$.

In particular, suppose a polynomial-time reduction maps a Boolean formula $\varphi$ to an instance and a polynomial-time decoder determines satisfiability from every valid returned assignment. Then an unsatisfiable formula has a polynomial certificate: a bounded valid assignment whose decoder reports unsatisfiability. Such a certificate cannot exist for a satisfiable formula. Therefore
$$ \mathrm{UNSAT}\in\mathrm{NP}, \qquad \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$
This is a conditional barrier for that reduction notion, not a classification of the search problem.

7. The remaining mixed case

The compressed procedures settle two broad cases with unequal entitlements. They do not establish how to combine positively valued kinds with objective chores.

Separate WEF1 allocations of those two parts cannot simply be added. Here is a concrete obstruction.

Take two types with masses $1/2$, entitlements $1$ and $2$, and identical valuations
$$ v_1(g)=v_2(g)=1, \qquad v_1(c)=v_2(c)=-1. $$
Let each kind have supply $1/2$. Allocate the good to all agents of type $2$ and the chore to all agents of type $1$.

The goods allocation alone is WEF1: type $1$ can remove the opposing good. The chores allocation alone is WEF1: type $1$ can remove its own chore.

But their union is not WEF1. For the comparison from type $1$ to type $2$:

  • no envy would require $-1\geq 1/2$;
  • removing the opposing good would require $-1\geq 0$;
  • removing the own chore would require $0\geq 1/2$.

All three fail.

This example disproves only the independent-composition approach. It supplies no hardness result. A general polynomial algorithm must coordinate the two parts, and no compressed construction accomplishing that coordination is proved here.
