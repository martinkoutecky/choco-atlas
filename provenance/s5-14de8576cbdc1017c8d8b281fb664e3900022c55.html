The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite sets \(P,Q\) of agent types with positive rational masses, integer capacities at most \(C\), compatibility relations, and weak rankings of counterpart types, compute a Pareto-stable assignment in an atomless two-sided market in time polynomial in the support size \(\tau\), \(C\), and input bit length \(L\). Each individual receives an integral bundle of counterpart types. A solution may be represented by rational masses \(y_{t,B}\) of type-\(t\) agents receiving bundle \(B\), with pairwise link balances \(\sum_B y_{t,B}k_u(B)=\sum_{B'}y_{u,B'}k_t(B')\). Stability forbids positive-mass groups on opposite sides that are both free to match each other; Pareto efficiency forbids a feasible reassignment that weakly improves almost every agent and strictly improves a positive mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P,Q\) be disjoint finite sets of types, with compatibility relation \(E\subseteq P\times Q\) and \(m=|E|\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|P|+|Q|\) types with positive mass, each mass \(a_t\) a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Masses need not sum to one or be equal across sides.
Each type represents an atomless population, has integer capacity \(1\le c_t\le C\), and ranks its compatible counterpart types by positive integers \(\pi_t(u)\), with larger values preferred and equal values tied. All compatible partners are acceptable.
A bundle \(B\) is a multiset of compatible counterpart types with \(|B|\le c_t\); repetitions represent distinct individuals of the same type, with at most one link per individual pair. Write \(k_u(B)\) for multiplicity.
An assignment consists of nonnegative masses \(y_{t,B}\) satisfying \(\sum_B y_{t,B}=a_t\) and \(\sum_B y_{t,B}k_u(B)=\sum_D y_{u,D}k_t(D)\) for every \((t,u)\in E\); these constraints define aggregate feasibility. The computational output lists only positive entries, as binary rationals.
For bundles sorted from best to worst, \(B\succeq_t D\) means \(|B|\ge|D|\) and \(\pi_t(B_i)\ge\pi_t(D_i)\) for every \(1\le i\le|D|\). Strict improvement means additionally greater cardinality or a strict inequality in some compared position.
A type-\(t\) individual with bundle \(B\) is free for compatible type \(u\) if \(|B|<c_t\) or some member of \(B\) is strictly worse than \(u\). Stability means no compatible pair of types has positive masses of individuals free for each other.
Pareto efficiency means no feasible reassignment of the same individuals weakly improves almost everyone and strictly improves a positive mass; comparisons allow arbitrary real masses and measurable splitting of bundle classes. Pareto stability means stability and Pareto efficiency.

Does every such market admit a Pareto-stable assignment with rational bundle masses, and is finding one solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,C,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard? Here \(L\) is the total input bit length.

In finite many-to-many markets with ties and at most one link per individual pair, Pareto-stable assignments always exist and can be found in polynomial time ([Kamiyama](https://catalog.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/24491/MI2012-8.pdf)).
No result settling this exact atomless integral-bundle problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Masses are arbitrary positive binary rationals, without normalization or equal side totals; the discrete model imposes no corresponding population restriction.
  • Capacities are strictly positive integers, as in the discrete definition; compatibility is a single bipartite relation, and all compatible partners are acceptable.
  • Bundle preferences use the sorted coordinatewise partial order, including cardinality and its strict-improvement condition, rather than an unspecified extension of counterpart rankings.
  • Repeated counterpart types in a bundle denote distinct individuals, retaining the discrete restriction of at most one link per individual pair.
  • Aggregate feasibility is defined by the stated bundle-mass conservation and link-balance constraints; omitted output entries have zero mass.
  • Pareto comparisons retain individual identities through measurable reassignment and permit real-valued masses, even though the requested output uses rational masses.
  • Defined m as the number of compatible type pairs and L as total input bit length.

Result

Every market in the question admits a Pareto-stable assignment with rational bundle masses. One can be found in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,C,L\), with at most \(2m+\tau\) positive bundle entries.

The proof uses a polynomial algorithm for stable allocations to obtain initial link masses, then a rational linear program to obtain Pareto efficiency. The essential observation is that, under the question’s definition, stability depends only on aggregate link masses.

1. Aggregate link masses and bundle realization

Write \(N(t)\) for the compatible counterparts of type \(t\), and put \(b_t=a_tc_t\).

An assignment determines one link-mass variable for each compatibility edge:

\( x_{tu}=\sum_B y_{t,B}k_u(B) =\sum_D y_{u,D}k_t(D). \)

We use \(x_{tu}=x_{ut}\) when referring to an edge from either endpoint. Its aggregate feasibility implies

\( x_{tu}\ge 0, \qquad \sum_{u\in N(t)}x_{tu}\le b_t \quad\text{for every }t. \)

Let \(\mathcal F\) be the polytope defined by these inequalities.

Conversely, every vector \(x\in\mathcal F\) can be realized by integral bundles. Let \(B_t(u)\) denote the bundle containing exactly \(c_t\) copies of \(u\). Define

\( y_{t,B_t(u)}=\frac{x_{tu}}{c_t}, \qquad y_{t,\varnothing} =a_t-\frac{1}{c_t}\sum_{u\in N(t)}x_{tu}, \)

and set all other entries to zero. These masses are nonnegative and sum to \(a_t\). Moreover,

\( \sum_B y_{t,B}k_u(B) =c_t\frac{x_{tu}}{c_t} =x_{tu}, \)

so every edge-balance constraint holds.

This realization has at most \(|N(t)|+1\) positive entries for type \(t\), hence at most \(2m+\tau\) overall. Rational \(x\) gives rational bundle masses.

2. Stability is an aggregate condition

For each nonisolated type \(t\), list its distinct preference values as

\( \rho_{t,1}>\rho_{t,2}>\cdots>\rho_{t,r_t}. \)

For a bundle \(B\), define its cumulative rank counts by

\( H_{t,j}(B) =\sum_{\substack{u\in N(t)\\\pi_t(u)\ge\rho_{t,j}}}k_u(B). \)

For an aggregate vector \(x\), define the corresponding cumulative link masses by

\( S_{t,j}(x) =\sum_{\substack{u\in N(t)\\\pi_t(u)\ge\rho_{t,j}}}x_{tu}. \)

Every assignment realizing \(x\) satisfies

\( S_{t,j}(x)=\sum_B y_{t,B}H_{t,j}(B). \)

Let \(j_t(u)\) be the index satisfying \(\pi_t(u)=\rho_{t,j_t(u)}\).

Lemma 1. The mass of type-\(t\) individuals free for \(u\) is zero if and only if

\( S_{t,j_t(u)}(x)=b_t. \)

Proof. A bundle \(B\) is not free for \(u\) precisely when it has cardinality \(c_t\) and every member is ranked at least as highly as \(u\). Equivalently,

\( H_{t,j_t(u)}(B)=c_t. \)

Consequently,

\( b_t-S_{t,j_t(u)}(x) =\sum_B y_{t,B}\bigl(c_t-H_{t,j_t(u)}(B)\bigr). \)

Each summand is nonnegative. The integer factor is zero for a bundle not free for \(u\), and at least one for a bundle free for \(u\). Thus the displayed difference is zero exactly when the free individuals have zero mass. \(\square\)

It follows that an assignment realizing \(x\) is stable exactly when, for every \((t,u)\in E\),

\[ S_{t,j_t(u)}(x)=b_t \quad\text{or}\quad S_{u,j_u(t)}(x)=b_u. \tag{1} \]

In particular, all assignments realizing the same aggregate vector have the same stability status.

Lemma 2. Suppose \(x^0\in\mathcal F\) satisfies (1). If \(x\in\mathcal F\) satisfies

\[ S_{t,j}(x)\ge S_{t,j}(x^0) \quad\text{for every }t,j, \tag{2} \]

then \(x\) also satisfies (1).

Proof. Fix an edge. At least one endpoint, say \(t\), satisfies \(S_{t,j_t(u)}(x^0)=b_t\). Feasibility and (2) imply

\( b_t =S_{t,j_t(u)}(x^0) \le S_{t,j_t(u)}(x) \le\sum_{v\in N(t)}x_{tv} \le b_t. \)

Therefore \(S_{t,j_t(u)}(x)=b_t\), preserving the required equality for this edge. \(\square\)

3. Computing a rational stable starting vector

We use the following known stable-allocation theorem.

Stable-allocation theorem. Given a finite bipartite graph, strict preferences over incident edges, positive vertex quotas, and nonnegative edge upper bounds, a stable allocation can be computed by a strongly polynomial algorithm. Here an edge below its upper bound is blocking when both endpoints have unused quota or positive allocation to strictly worse edges.

This is established by the inductive algorithm in Section 2 of the [corrected Baïou–Balinski paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302), particularly Theorem 2 and Corollary 1.

Apply this theorem as follows:

  • Give vertex \(t\) quota \(b_t\).
  • Break each preference tie arbitrarily, preserving all original strict comparisons.
  • Give every compatible edge upper bound \(M=1+\sum_t b_t\).

These are valid stable-allocation inputs. Every feasible edge allocation satisfies

\( x_{tu}\le\min\{b_t,b_u\}<M, \)

so every compatible edge is strictly below its upper bound.

Let \(x^0\) be the allocation returned by the theorem. Stability under the refined preferences means that, on each edge, some endpoint has its entire quota allocated to partners ranked at least as highly as the counterpart in the refinement. All those partners are also ranked at least as highly in the original preferences. Thus \(x^0\) satisfies (1).

The computation has polynomial bit complexity on our rational data. Indeed, multiply the quotas and bounds by a common denominator \(D\), chosen as the product of the input mass denominators. Its bit length is at most their total input bit length. The inductive algorithm uses sums, differences, comparisons, and minimum-slack updates; hence integer input produces integer allocations. Its strongly polynomial operation count, together with the polynomial bit length of the scaled bounds, gives a polynomial-time rational computation after division by \(D\).

If \(m=0\), simply assign everyone the empty bundle; this is already stable and Pareto efficient.

4. Bundle improvements increase cumulative rank counts

Lemma 3. For two bundles \(B,D\) of type \(t\),

\[ B\succeq_t D \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_{t,j}(B)\ge H_{t,j}(D) \quad\text{for every }j. \tag{3} \]

Under these equivalent conditions, improvement is strict if and only if at least one cumulative-count inequality is strict.

Proof. Suppose \(B\succeq_t D\). Fix a threshold \(\rho_{t,j}\), and let \(h=H_{t,j}(D)\). The first \(h\) members of sorted \(D\) meet this threshold. Their corresponding members in sorted \(B\) are at least as highly ranked, so \(H_{t,j}(B)\ge h\).

Conversely, suppose all cumulative-count inequalities hold. The last threshold gives \(|B|\ge|D|\). For each position \(i\le|D|\), take the threshold equal to \(\pi_t(D_i)\). At least \(i\) members of \(D\) meet it, and therefore at least \(i\) members of \(B\) meet it. Hence \(\pi_t(B_i)\ge\pi_t(D_i)\).

Finally, equality of all cumulative counts determines both cardinality and the number of members at each preference value. It therefore precludes strict improvement. A strict cumulative-count inequality precludes equality of those data and, under weak dominance, entails strict improvement. \(\square\)

This lemma applies pointwise to individuals and therefore also to measurable reassignments with arbitrary real masses.

5. A linear program that ensures Pareto efficiency

Starting from \(x^0\), solve the rational linear program

\( \text{maximize } F(x)=\sum_t\sum_{j=1}^{r_t}S_{t,j}(x) \)

subject to

\[ x\in\mathcal F, \qquad S_{t,j}(x)\ge S_{t,j}(x^0) \quad\text{for every }t,j. \tag{4} \]

For isolated types, the inner sum is empty.

The feasible set contains \(x^0\). It is closed and bounded, because every edge variable lies between zero and its endpoints’ quotas. Thus an optimum exists. Since the program has rational coefficients, standard polynomial-time rational linear programming supplies a rational optimum \(x^*\).

By Lemma 2, \(x^*\) satisfies the stability condition (1). Realize it using the bundles constructed in Section 1.

Lemma 4. This bundle assignment is Pareto efficient against all feasible measurable reassignments, including those with arbitrary real masses.

Proof. Suppose a feasible reassignment weakly improves almost every individual and strictly improves a positive mass. Let \(x'\in\mathcal F\) be its aggregate link vector.

For each type, integrate the pointwise inequalities from Lemma 3 over its population. They give

\( S_{t,j}(x')\ge S_{t,j}(x^*) \ge S_{t,j}(x^0) \)

for every cumulative rank coordinate. Hence \(x'\) satisfies (4).

To establish strict improvement of the objective, define the integer bundle score

\( W_t(B)=\sum_{j=1}^{r_t}H_{t,j}(B). \)

Lemma 3 implies that weak improvement never decreases this score and strict improvement increases it by at least one. Let \(A_t\) be the measurable set of strictly improved type-\(t\) individuals, and let \(\mu_t\) denote their population measure. Integrating the score differences yields

\( F(x')-F(x^*) \ge\sum_t\mu_t(A_t)>0. \)

This contradicts optimality of \(x^*\). The argument places no rationality restriction on \(x'\). \(\square\)

Thus the constructed assignment is both stable and Pareto efficient.

6. Individual-pair links and polynomial output size

The homogeneous bundles also admit an explicit realization respecting the requirement of at most one link per individual pair.

Fix an edge \((t,u)\) with \(x^*_{tu}>0\), and put

\( h=\frac{x^*_{tu}}{c_tc_u}. \)

Partition its type-\(t\) bundle class, of mass \(x^*_{tu}/c_t\), into \(c_u\) atomless pieces of mass \(h\). Partition its type-\(u\) bundle class into \(c_t\) pieces of mass \(h\). Parameterize these pieces by the same interval of mass \(h\).

At each parameter value, join all \(c_u\) type-\(t\) individuals to all \(c_t\) type-\(u\) individuals. Every type-\(t\) individual then has exactly \(c_t\) distinct partners, and every type-\(u\) individual exactly \(c_u\) distinct partners. Each individual pair receives one link. Perform this construction separately for each positive edge; the corresponding population classes are disjoint.

Finally, the computational bounds are polynomial:

  • There are \(m\) aggregate variables.
  • The number of cumulative rank coordinates is \(\sum_t r_t\le 2m\).
  • The linear program has polynomial encoding length, and its rational optimum has polynomial bit length.
  • Bundle realization requires only rational arithmetic and produces at most \(2m+\tau\) positive entries.
  • Even if every bundle repetition is written explicitly, each bundle has at most \(C\) members.

Therefore the requested rational Pareto-stable assignment can be found and listed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,C,L\).
