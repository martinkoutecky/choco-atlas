Determine the best polynomial-time approximation ratio as a function of \(|P|\); the hard instances have \(q\) projects, \(q^2-r\) equal-mass types, and \(\rho_p=q/(q^2-r)\), with optimum \(q\alpha(H)/(q^2-r)\) for the source graph \(H\).

Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; express running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and any approximation parameters.

2. Feasibility, exact arithmetic, and a graph characterization. For $O\subseteq P$, define
$$ w(O)=\sum_{p\in O}\rho_p. $$
Call $O$ feasible if there is an assignment with $z_p=1$ exactly for $p\in O$. Summing the project equations shows that every such assignment has value $w(O)$.

Feasibility is downward closed: if $O$ is feasible and $O'\subseteq O$, delete all assignments to projects outside $O'$. The retained quotas remain satisfied, and no type capacity increases.

To test feasibility, construct a network with source $s$, one vertex per listed type, one vertex per project in $O$, and sink $r$. Its arcs have capacities:

  • $s$ to type $t$: $\mu_t$;

There are at most $\tau+|O|+2$ vertices and $3\tau+|O|$ arcs. A feasible assignment gives a flow of value $w(O)$. Conversely, a flow of value $w(O)$ saturates all project-to-sink arcs, since their capacities sum to $w(O)$, and therefore gives the required assignment.

Let $Q$ be the product of all input denominators. Its binary length is $O(L)$. Scaling by $Q$ produces integer capacities of $O(L)$ bits, so exact feasibility and a rational witness can be computed in polynomial time. A conservative Edmonds–Karp bit-complexity bound for a test on $b$ projects is
$$ O\!\left((\tau+b+2)^3L^2\right), $$
after denominator preprocessing costing $O(L^3)$.

For $U\subseteq P$, let
$$ A(U)=\sum_{t:S_t\cap U\ne\varnothing}\mu_t. $$
The same network gives the following exact Hall-type condition:
$$ O\text{ is feasible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w(U)\le A(U)\text{ for every }U\subseteq O. $$

Here is a cut justification. Replace the type-to-project capacities by $m+1$; this does not change feasible flows because a type has total incoming capacity at most $1$. The cut immediately after the source has capacity at most $1$, so a minimum cut crosses no arc of capacity $m+1$. If $U$ is the set of project vertices on the sink side, every type accepting a project in $U$ must also be on the sink side. The minimum capacity among cuts with this project placement is
$$ A(U)+w(O\setminus U). $$
Thus every cut has capacity at least $w(O)$ exactly when $A(U)\ge w(U)$ for all $U\subseteq O$. The max-flow/min-cut theorem proves the equivalence.

Now define an undirected graph on $P$. For each two-project type accepting $\{u,v\}$, put an edge $uv$ of weight $a_{uv}=\mu_t$. Aggregation ensures that there is at most one such edge per pair. Let $s_v$ be the singleton mass accepting only $v$, or zero if no such type exists. Define
$$ M_v=s_v+\sum_{u:uv\text{ is an edge}}a_{uv}, \qquad c_v=M_v-\rho_v. $$
Thus $M_v$ is the total mass accepting $v$, and $c_v$ is its unused incident-mass allowance when it opens.

If $E(U)$ denotes the edges with both endpoints in $U$, then
$$ A(U)=\sum_{v\in U}M_v-\sum_{e\in E(U)}a_e. $$
Indeed, the first sum counts each internal edge twice, while $A(U)$ counts it once; singleton and boundary-edge masses are counted once in both expressions. Consequently,
$$ O\text{ is feasible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{e\in E(U)}a_e\le\sum_{v\in U}c_v \text{ for every }U\subseteq O. $$

In particular, a project with $c_v<0$ cannot open. Projects with zero quota may be closed without changing the objective. Henceforth, let
$$ R=\{v\in P:0<\rho_v\le M_v\}. $$
Restricting the choice of open projects to $R$ preserves the optimum. The quantities $M_v$ continue to include masses of types also accepting projects outside $R$: those masses remain available.

An equivalent formulation will be useful. For a selected set $O\subseteq R$, assign each internal edge $e=uv$ two nonnegative losses satisfying
$$ y_{e,u}+y_{e,v}=a_e. $$
Then $O$ is feasible exactly when these losses can satisfy
$$ \sum_{\substack{e\in E(O)\\v\in e}}y_{e,v}\le c_v \quad\text{for every }v\in O. $$

To prove existence from the subset inequalities, make a network with source-to-edge capacity $a_e$, edge-to-endpoint capacity $m+2$, and endpoint-to-sink capacity $c_v$. Write $T=\sum_{e\in E(O)}a_e$. A minimum cut crosses no arc of capacity $m+2$, because the source cut has capacity $T\le1$. For a fixed set $U$ of endpoint vertices on the source side, the minimum cut capacity is
$$ T-\sum_{e\in E(U)}a_e+\sum_{v\in U}c_v. $$
It is at least $T$ precisely under the displayed subset inequalities. A flow saturating all source-to-edge arcs therefore gives the losses.

Conversely, given such losses, provisionally assign $a_e-y_{e,v}$ from each internal edge type to endpoint $v$. The two endpoint assignments sum to $a_e$. Assign each singleton or boundary-edge type wholly to its selected endpoint. Project $v$ then receives
$$ M_v-\sum_{e\ni v}y_{e,v}\ge M_v-c_v=\rho_v. $$
Reduce assignments at each selected project to its exact quota. Reductions preserve every type capacity, proving feasibility.

3. An achievable factor, with support-sensitive running time. For every integer $1\le b\le m$, there is a deterministic
$$ \left\lceil\frac{m}{b}\right\rceil $$
approximation with running time
$$ O\!\left( L^3+ \left\lceil\frac{m}{b}\right\rceil 2^b(\tau+b+2)^3L^2 \right). $$

Partition $P$ into $r=\lceil m/b\rceil$ blocks $B_1,\ldots,B_r$, each of size at most $b$. Enumerate all subsets of each block and use the feasibility test to find a feasible subset $A_i\subseteq B_i$ of maximum value. Return the most valuable $A_i$, with its witnessing assignment.

For an optimal feasible set $O^\star$, downward closure makes $O^\star\cap B_i$ feasible. Therefore
$$ w(A_i)\ge w(O^\star\cap B_i). $$
Summing gives
$$ \sum_{i=1}^r w(A_i)\ge \sum_{i=1}^r w(O^\star\cap B_i)=\mathrm{OPT}. $$
The largest block solution has value at least $\mathrm{OPT}/r$. There are at most $r2^b$ feasibility tests, giving the stated running time.

Taking
$$ b=\lfloor\log_2(m+1)\rfloor $$
gives the polynomial-time factor
$$ \left\lceil \frac{m}{\lfloor\log_2(m+1)\rfloor} \right\rceil = O\!\left(\frac{m}{\log(m+1)}\right). $$
More generally, $b$ may be any fixed constant multiple of $\log(m+1)$, capped at $m$, while retaining polynomial running time.

There is also a support-sensitive bound. Let $d$ be the degeneracy of the two-project graph induced by $R$: every nonempty induced subgraph has a vertex of degree at most $d$, and $d$ is the least such integer. Repeated minimum-degree deletion followed by reverse greedy coloring produces at most $d+1$ colors.

Every color class is feasible: it contains no internal edge, so all graph feasibility inequalities hold because $c_v\ge0$. Returning the heaviest color class gives a $(d+1)$ approximation. If the induced graph has $e$ edges, then
$$ \frac{d(d+1)}2\le e\le\tau. $$
For the first inequality, a subgraph witnessing degeneracy $d$ has at least $d+1$ vertices, all of degree at least $d$. Thus
$$ d+1\le 1+\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{8\tau+1}-1}{2}\right\rfloor. $$
Taking the better of this solution and the block-enumeration solution yields factor
$$ \min\!\left\{ \left\lceil \frac{m}{\lfloor\log_2(m+1)\rfloor} \right\rceil,\, d+1 \right\}. $$
Coloring and constructing its assignment take polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$.

4. Hardness with equal quotas and strictly positive slack. Let $G=(V,E)$ be a simple graph with $n\ge1$ vertices. Write $d(v)$ for the degree of $v$, and choose
$$ D=2^{\lceil\log_2(4n^2)\rceil}. $$
Create one project $p_v$ per vertex, so $m=n$, and give every project quota
$$ \rho_{p_v}=\frac nD. $$
Create these types:

  • For each edge $uv\in E$, a type accepting $\{p_u,p_v\}$ with mass $1/D$.
  • For each vertex $v$, a singleton type accepting $\{p_v\}$ with mass

$$ \frac{n-d(v)+1/4}{D}. $$

  • An empty-set type with mass

$$ 1-\frac{n^2-|E|+n/4}{D}. $$

All masses are positive. Singleton masses are positive because $d(v)\le n-1$. Moreover,
$$ \frac{n^2-|E|+n/4}{D} \le \frac{n^2+n/4}{4n^2} \le\frac5{16}, $$
so the empty-set mass is at least $11/16$.

The masses sum to $1$, because the nonempty types have total mass
$$ \frac{|E|+\sum_v(n-d(v)+1/4)}D = \frac{n^2-|E|+n/4}{D}. $$
All numbers are dyadic, all acceptable sets are distinct, and
$$ \tau=n+|E|+1, \qquad L_{\mathrm{out}} = O\!\left((n+|E|+1)\log(n+1)\right). $$
The reduction is therefore polynomial.

For every project,
$$ M_{p_v}=\frac{n+1/4}{D}, \qquad c_{p_v}=\frac1{4D}. $$
In particular, every quota is strictly below its total acceptable mass, with common relative slack
$$ \frac{M_{p_v}-\rho_{p_v}}{M_{p_v}} = \frac1{4n+1}. $$

If adjacent projects $p_u,p_v$ both open, the graph feasibility inequality for their two-element set requires
$$ \frac1D\le c_{p_u}+c_{p_v}=\frac1{2D}, $$
which is false. Thus every feasible project set corresponds to an independent set.

Conversely, for an independent set, every selected subset has zero internal edge mass and nonnegative total allowance. The graph characterization therefore proves feasibility. Hence
$$ O\text{ is feasible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \{v:p_v\in O\}\text{ is independent in }G. $$
Every feasible solution has value $(n/D)|O|$, so
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=\frac nD\,\alpha(G). $$
Any approximation factor depending on $m$ transfers unchanged to maximum independent set on $n=m$ vertices.

For every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$, approximating maximum clique within $n^{1-\varepsilon}$ is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard. This is Zuckerman’s theorem; graph complementation gives the same statement for maximum independent set without changing the vertex count. [Zuckerman, *Linear Degree Extractors and the Inapproximability of Max Clique and Chromatic Number*](https://www.theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v003a006/).

An $m^{1-\varepsilon}$ approximation for our constructed instance would return an independent set $I$ with
$$ \frac nD|I| \ge \frac{(n/D)\alpha(G)}{n^{1-\varepsilon}}, $$
and hence
$$ |I|\ge\frac{\alpha(G)}{n^{1-\varepsilon}}. $$
Thus achieving that factor is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard. In particular, no deterministic polynomial-time algorithm achieves it unless $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.

This argument handles divisible assignments directly. Its obstruction is a capacity inequality, rather than applicant indivisibility.

5. A constant factor under uniform positive slack. Suppose a binary-rational parameter $0<\delta<1/2$ satisfies
$$ c_v\ge\delta M_v \quad\text{for every }v\in R. $$
There is a deterministic $2/\delta$ approximation running in
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L+\ell_\delta), $$
where $\ell_\delta$ is the encoding length of $\delta$. For fixed $\delta$, this is a constant factor.

If instead $\delta\ge1/2$, all of $R$ is feasible: splitting each internal edge’s loss equally gives load at most $M_v/2\le c_v$ at every vertex. Thus that case is exactly solvable.

To prove the approximation for $\delta<1/2$, first consider a random set $A\subseteq R$, independently selecting each vertex with probability $\delta$. For $v\in A$, define
$$ \ell_v(A)=\frac12\sum_{\substack{u\in A\\uv\text{ is an edge}}}a_{uv}. $$
Keep precisely the vertices whose half-edge loss fits:
$$ B(A)=\{v\in A:\ell_v(A)\le c_v\}. $$
Since deleting vertices only reduces these loads, equal loss splitting on the edges internal to $B(A)$ respects all allowances. Thus $B(A)$ is feasible.

All $c_v$ are positive under the hypothesis. Define
$$ \Phi(A)= \sum_{v\in A}\rho_v \left(1-\frac{\ell_v(A)}{c_v}\right). $$
For a retained vertex, its summand lies between zero and $\rho_v$. For a discarded vertex, its summand is negative. Therefore
$$ w(B(A))\ge\Phi(A). $$

Writing $W=\sum_{v\in R}\rho_v$, independence of the selections gives
$$ \mathbb E[\Phi(A)] = \delta W - \frac{\delta^2}{2} \sum_{v\in R}\frac{\rho_v}{c_v} \sum_{\substack{u\in R\\uv\text{ is an edge}}}a_{uv}. $$
The inner edge sum is at most $M_v$, and $M_v/c_v\le1/\delta$. Consequently,
$$ \mathbb E[\Phi(A)] \ge \delta W-\frac{\delta^2}{2}\frac W\delta = \frac{\delta W}{2} \ge \frac{\delta\,\mathrm{OPT}}2. $$

This guarantee can be derandomized exactly. If $z_v$ indicates selection, then
$$ \Phi= \sum_{v\in R}\rho_vz_v - \frac12\sum_{uv}a_{uv}z_uz_v \left(\frac{\rho_u}{c_u}+\frac{\rho_v}{c_v}\right). $$
This is an explicitly given quadratic polynomial. Fix indicators one at a time. The current conditional expectation is a convex combination of the conditional expectations obtained by setting the next indicator to zero or one, so at least one choice does not decrease it. Choose that value.

After all indicators are fixed,
$$ \Phi(A)\ge\mathbb E[\Phi(A)] \ge\frac{\delta\,\mathrm{OPT}}2. $$
Returning $B(A)$ proves the factor. Conditional expectations require only polynomially many exact rational operations. Their denominators have polynomial total binary length, so the claimed running time follows.

The hardness construction above has $\delta=1/(4m+1)$, which tends to zero. Thus it does not contradict this fixed-slack result.

6. A fully polynomial approximation scheme on forests. Suppose the two-project graph induced by $R$ is a forest. For every binary-rational $0<\eta<1$, there is a deterministic algorithm returning a feasible solution of value at least
$$ (1-\eta)\mathrm{OPT} $$
in time
$$ O\!\left( L^3+ \frac{m^5}{\eta^2} \bigl(L+\ell_\eta+\log(m+1)\bigr)^2 + (m+\tau+2)^3L^2 \right). $$
Here $\ell_\eta$ is the encoding length of $\eta$.

Assume $R\ne\varnothing$, and let
$$ W_{\max}=\max_{v\in R}\rho_v. $$
Each vertex in $R$ is individually feasible, so $\mathrm{OPT}\ge W_{\max}$. Set
$$ \kappa=\frac{\eta W_{\max}}m, \qquad h_v=\left\lfloor\frac{\rho_v}{\kappa}\right\rfloor, \qquad K=\sum_{v\in R}h_v. $$
Then
$$ K\le\frac{m^2}{\eta}. $$
I first show how to maximize the integer score $\sum_{v\in O}h_v$ exactly.

Root each forest component. For a vertex $v$, let $T_v$ be its descendant subtree, including $v$. Maintain two tables indexed by scores $k\in\{0,\ldots,K\}$:

  • $C_v(k)$ is true if score $k$ is achievable in $T_v$ with $v$ closed and all selected vertices satisfying their internal-edge loss allowances.
  • $F_v(k)$ is the minimum loss allocated to $v$ from edges inside $T_v$, over feasible score-$k$ solutions with $v$ selected. An unattainable state has value $+\infty$. Finite values must satisfy $F_v(k)\le c_v$.

The edge to the parent is excluded from these states.

Before processing children, initialize
$$ C_v(0)=\mathrm{true}, \qquad F_v(h_v)=0, $$
with all other states unattainable.

Process children one at a time using score convolutions. If $v$ is closed, its edge to a child is not internal to the selected set. Therefore either a closed-child state or an open-child state may be used, with no additional load.

Suppose $v$ is open and $u$ is a child joined by an edge of weight $a_{uv}$. A closed-child state contributes no load to $v$. An open-child state of score $j$ contributes minimum additional load
$$ g_u(j)= \max\{0,\ a_{uv}+F_u(j)-c_u\}. $$

To justify this formula, the child subtree’s minimum load at $u$ is $F_u(j)$, leaving allowance $c_u-F_u(j)$ for the connecting edge. Its weight $a_{uv}$ must be divided between losses at $u$ and $v$. The minimum necessary loss at $v$ is therefore exactly $g_u(j)$. It is attainable by allocating as much of the edge loss to $u$ as its remaining allowance permits.

Any other score-$j$ child solution has load at least $F_u(j)$ and hence cannot require less connecting-edge loss at $v$. Thus keeping only the minimum child load loses no useful solution.

For each old parent score and child score, add their scores and loads, minimize among alternatives, and discard open-parent states whose load exceeds $c_v$. Different child subtrees have no edges between them, so their connecting-edge losses add independently. Induction over children and then vertices proves that both tables have their stated meanings.

At a component root, every attainable closed or open state is feasible for that entire component. Convolving the feasible-score sets of the components gives the maximum attainable total score, and stored choices reconstruct a feasible selected set $O_{\mathrm{alg}}$.

There are $O(m)$ convolutions, each using $O(K^2)$ operations. All loss-table values are obtained by addition, subtraction, comparison, and taking maxima from input masses and allowances. After scaling by $Q$, these are integer operations on $O(L)$-bit numbers. Thus the dynamic program takes $O(mK^2L^2)$ bit operations, within the displayed bound. A final flow computation supplies an assignment with the exact quotas.

Finally, let $O^\star$ be optimal. Exact maximization of the rounded score gives
$$ w(O_{\mathrm{alg}}) \ge \kappa\sum_{v\in O_{\mathrm{alg}}}h_v \ge \kappa\sum_{v\in O^\star}h_v. $$
For every $v$,
$$ \kappa h_v\ge\rho_v-\kappa. $$
Therefore
$$ w(O_{\mathrm{alg}}) \ge \mathrm{OPT}-m\kappa = \mathrm{OPT}-\eta W_{\max} \ge (1-\eta)\mathrm{OPT}. $$
This proves the scheme. Setting $\eta=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)$ yields a $(1+\varepsilon)$ approximation with running time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,\ell_\varepsilon$, and $1/\varepsilon$.

7. Why pairwise conflicts do not settle the general case. The graph characterization involves subsets of unbounded size. This is essential even with equal quotas and dyadic masses.

Fix $k\ge3$ and a power of two $D\ge k^2$. Take $k$ projects and, for every pair, one type of mass $1/D$ accepting that pair. Add an empty-set type carrying the remaining mass. Give each project quota
$$ \rho_v=\frac{k}{2D}. $$
Then
$$ M_v=\frac{k-1}{D}, \qquad c_v=\frac{k-2}{2D}. $$
For a subset of $j>0$ projects, the graph feasibility inequality becomes
$$ \frac{j(j-1)}{2D} \le \frac{j(k-2)}{2D}, $$
which holds exactly when $j\le k-1$.

Hence every proper subset is feasible, but the full $k$-project set is infeasible. In particular, every pair is feasible. The pairwise incompatibility graph is empty and cannot describe the feasible family. This prevents a direct transfer of independent-set approximation algorithms through pairwise conflicts alone.

These arguments do not classify approximation factors inside the remaining subpolynomial gap. In particular, they neither achieve nor rule out $O(m/(\log(m+1))^2)$ for unrestricted instances.
