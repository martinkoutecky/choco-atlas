Theorem 4.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) items of supply one, a population of mass \(N\), and \(\tau\) complete buyer types with rational masses \(N\mu_t\) and known subadditive valuations \(v_t\), does there exist an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\) that computes static anonymous item prices \(p\) so that sequential utility-maximizing demand along every fixed measurable arrival stream achieves welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cont}}/O(\log\log m)-\epsilon\)? Here \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cont}}=\max_x\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=N\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le 1\); \(x_{t,S}\) is the mass of type \(t\) assigned the integral bundle \(S\), with empty bundles absorbing unallocated mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\geq3\) item categories, each with supply one. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) complete buyer types with positive binary rational masses \(a_t=N\mu_t\), where \(N=\sum_ta_t>0\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Each type has a known monotone subadditive valuation \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\), with \(v_t(\emptyset)=0\). Access is through value and demand oracles, including demand restricted to available items at nonnegative rational prices. Let \(L\) bound the total numerical input length and the bit length of every returned value.
An arrival stream is a measurable map \(b:[0,N]\to\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) with \(\operatorname{Leb}(b^{-1}(t))=a_t\), fixed in advance and unknown when prices are computed. Bundles remain subsets of \(M\); assigning a bundle to mass \(d s\) consumes \(d s\) of each included item and contributes \(v_{b(s)}(S)\,d s\) welfare.
Static anonymous prices are a single vector \(p\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^m\). A sequential demand outcome is a measurable bundle map \(S(s)\) satisfying, almost everywhere, \(S(s)\in\arg\max_{A\subseteq\{j:r_j(s)>0\}}\{v_{b(s)}(A)-\sum_{j\in A}p_j\}\), where \(r_j(s)=1-\int_0^s\mathbf{1}_{\{j\in S(u)\}}\,du\geq0\). All measurable choices among utility ties are allowed. Its welfare is \(W=\int_0^N v_{b(s)}(S(s))\,ds\).
Define \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cont}}=\max_{x\geq0}\sum_{t,S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=a_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq1\); empty bundles absorb unallocated mass.

Does there exist a universal constant \(C>0\) and an algorithm using time and oracle calls polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\) that, for every rational \(\epsilon>0\), computes prices guaranteeing \(W\geq\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cont}}/(C\log\log m)-\epsilon\) for every fixed arrival stream and every sequential demand outcome?

For finite buyers with independently drawn valuations, static anonymous item prices achieve an \(O(\log\log m)\) expected-welfare approximation with additive error, computable in polynomial time with demand-oracle access ([Dütting–Kesselheim–Lucier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09784)).
A constant-factor discrete prophet inequality is also known through a purely existential policy, with a dynamic bundle-pricing implementation ([Correa–Cristi](https://www.dii.uchile.cl/~jcorrea/papers/Conferences/2023CC.pdf)). No result settling the specified continuous problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified positive rational type masses, total mass equal to their sum, and probability weights summing to one; total population mass need not be integral.
  • Carried over nonnegative, monotone valuations with zero empty-bundle value; specified rational values and numerical bit bounds.
  • Interpreted known valuations using value and demand oracles, retaining the anchor's demand-oracle computation model rather than assuming explicit exponential valuation tables.
  • Defined continuous arrivals using cumulative population mass, bundle consumption at unit rate per included item, and removal of exhausted items.
  • Required the guarantee for every measurable utility-maximizing tie choice, matching the discrete permission to buy any maximizing bundle.
  • Restricted to at least three items so the logarithmic approximation expression is positive, and made its hidden universal constant explicit.
  • Interpreted the unqualified computation request as a deterministic algorithm producing rational prices; no sampling normalization was imposed because the continuous input lists known types rather than unknown valuation realizations.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Yes. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm that computes rational static anonymous item prices satisfying the stronger guarantee

\( \displaystyle W\geq \frac{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cont}}}{4K},\qquad K=\left\lceil\log_2\log_2(4m)\right\rceil, \)

for every arrival stream and every sequential demand outcome. Thus the requested additive error is unnecessary.

The proof uses the marginal-scale and dual-price idea from [Dütting–Kesselheim–Lucier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09784), applied separately to the bundles in a sparse optimal fractional allocation. The continuous guarantee is proved directly below.

1. A sparse optimal fractional allocation

Write \(B=\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cont}}\). If \(B=0\), zero prices suffice. Henceforth suppose \(B>0\).

The configuration LP is feasible, because each type’s entire mass can receive the empty bundle. Its feasible region is compact: every variable \(x_{t,S}\) lies between zero and \(a_t\). Consequently, its maximum is attained.

An optimal fractional allocation can be computed with polynomially many demand-oracle calls. To establish this algorithmic point, first replace each type equality by the inequality

\( \displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}\leq a_t. \)

This does not change the optimum: any missing mass can subsequently be assigned the empty bundle. The resulting dual is

\( \displaystyle \min_{z,y\geq0}\left\{\sum_ta_tz_t+\sum_jy_j:\ z_t+\sum_{j\in S}y_j\geq v_t(S)\quad\text{for every }t,S\right\}. \)

At nonnegative rational \(y\), a demand query for type \(t\) finds a bundle maximizing \(v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}y_j\). Its value therefore either verifies all constraints for that type or identifies a violated constraint. Negative coordinates are separated directly.

We use the standard exact rational oracle-LP theorem: in dimension \(d\), with bounded rational encoding lengths and a separation oracle returning violated defining inequalities, an LP with finite optimum can be solved in polynomial time, together with a sparse optimal dual certificate. Applied to the displayed dual, this certificate supplies an optimal configuration allocation with polynomially many nonzero entries. Exact solutions and sparse certificates in this oracle setting are discussed in [Dadush–Végh–Zambelli](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35149/35149.pdf).

Here the dimension is \(m+\tau\); inequality normals have entries in \(\{0,1,-1\}\); right-hand sides have bit length at most \(L\); and objective coefficients have input length bounded by \(L\). The recession cone of the displayed dual is the nonnegative orthant, so any required recession-cone separation is immediate. Thus the oracle-LP hypotheses hold.

After adding empty bundles to absorb missing mass, enumerate the supported allocation entries by \(h=1,\ldots,H\), where \(H\) is polynomial in \(m+\tau\). Let

\( \displaystyle (t_h,U_h,w_h),\qquad w_h=x^*_{t_h,U_h}>0, \)

denote these entries. They satisfy

\( \displaystyle \sum_{h:t_h=t}w_h=a_t,\qquad \sum_{h:j\in U_h}w_h\leq1,\qquad \sum_hw_hv_{t_h}(U_h)=B. \)

All these data have polynomial bit length.

2. Local configuration programs

For each \(h\) and rational \(q\in(0,1]\), define

\( \displaystyle f_h(q)=\max_{\lambda\geq0}\sum_{A\subseteq U_h}\lambda_Av_{t_h}(A), \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle \sum_{A\subseteq U_h}\lambda_A=1,\qquad \sum_{\substack{A\subseteq U_h\\j\in A}}\lambda_A\leq q\quad(j\in U_h). \)

Thus \(\lambda\) is a probability distribution on subbundles of \(U_h\), with every item’s inclusion probability at most \(q\). Define

\( \displaystyle F(q)=\sum_hw_hf_h(q). \)

Two bounds will control the endpoints.

First, choosing \(U_h\) with probability \(1/2\) and the empty bundle otherwise gives

\( \displaystyle F(1/2)\geq B/2. \)

Second, for any feasible \(\lambda\), monotonicity and normalization give

\( \displaystyle \sum_A\lambda_Av_{t_h}(A)\leq v_{t_h}(U_h)\Pr(A\neq\emptyset)\leq v_{t_h}(U_h)\sum_{j\in U_h}\Pr(j\in A)\leq mq\,v_{t_h}(U_h). \)

Therefore

\( \displaystyle F(q)\leq mqB. \)

This bound is independent of the smallest supported type mass.

The dual defining \(f_h(q)\) is

\( \displaystyle \min_{z_h,y_h\geq0}\left\{z_h+q\sum_{j\in U_h}y_{h,j}:\ z_h+\sum_{j\in A}y_{h,j}\geq v_{t_h}(A)\quad(A\subseteq U_h)\right\}. \)

Although the probability equality initially gives an unrestricted dual variable \(z_h\), the empty-bundle constraint forces \(z_h\geq0\).

These programs are exactly solvable in polynomial time. Separation uses a demand query restricted to \(U_h\), at nonnegative prices \(y_h\). Their dimension is at most \(m+1\), and all coefficients have polynomial bit length.

3. Prices and an inequality for every removed set

Fix a rational \(q\in(0,1)\). For each \(h\), take an optimal dual solution \((z_h,y_h)\) for \(f_h(q^2)\), extending \(y_{h,j}=0\) outside \(U_h\). Strong duality gives

\( \displaystyle f_h(q^2)=z_h+q^2\sum_jy_{h,j}. \)

Define anonymous item prices by

\( \displaystyle p_j=q\sum_hw_hy_{h,j}. \)

For each \(h\), let \(\lambda^h\) be an optimal distribution defining \(f_h(q)\). These distributions are needed for the proof; the algorithm need not output them.

For a set \(A\), write \(p(A)=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\). We claim that every \(T\subseteq M\) satisfies

\( \displaystyle p(T)+\sum_hw_h\,\mathbb E_{A\sim\lambda^h}\!\left[v_{t_h}(A\setminus T)-p(A)\right]\geq F(q)-F(q^2). \)

To prove the claim, subadditivity gives

\( \displaystyle v_{t_h}(A\setminus T)\geq v_{t_h}(A)-v_{t_h}(A\cap T). \)

Dual feasibility and the marginal constraints imply

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E_{\lambda^h}v_{t_h}(A\cap T)\leq z_h+\sum_{j\in T}y_{h,j}\Pr_{\lambda^h}(j\in A)\leq z_h+q\sum_{j\in T}y_{h,j}. \)

Also, because \(A\subseteq U_h\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_hw_h\Pr_{\lambda^h}(j\in A)\leq q\sum_{h:j\in U_h}w_h\leq q. \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sum_hw_h\mathbb E_{\lambda^h}p(A)\leq q\sum_jp_j. \)

Combining these inequalities, the left-hand side of the claim is at least

\( \displaystyle F(q)-\sum_hw_hz_h-q\sum_{j\in T}\sum_hw_hy_{h,j}-q\sum_jp_j+p(T). \)

By the definition of prices,

\( \displaystyle q\sum_{j\in T}\sum_hw_hy_{h,j}=p(T),\qquad q\sum_jp_j=q^2\sum_hw_h\sum_jy_{h,j}. \)

The terms involving \(T\) cancel. What remains is

\( \displaystyle F(q)-\sum_hw_h\left(z_h+q^2\sum_jy_{h,j}\right)=F(q)-F(q^2), \)

proving the claim.

4. Transfer to every continuous outcome

Fix any admissible arrival stream and any sequential demand outcome at these prices. Define its final exhausted set by

\( \displaystyle T=\{j\in M:r_j(N)=0\}. \)

Each residual function \(r_j\) is nonincreasing. Therefore every item outside \(T\) is available throughout the stream: \(r_j(s)\geq r_j(N)>0\).

Define the utility obtainable from the final remaining items by

\( \displaystyle u_t=\max_{D\subseteq M\setminus T}\{v_t(D)-p(D)\}. \)

Almost everywhere, sequential demand optimality gives

\( \displaystyle v_{b(s)}(S(s))-p(S(s))\geq u_{b(s)}. \)

Integrating and using the prescribed type masses yields

\( \displaystyle \mathcal U:=\int_0^N\left[v_{b(s)}(S(s))-p(S(s))\right]\,ds\geq\sum_ta_tu_t. \)

For every \(A\subseteq U_h\), the bundle \(A\setminus T\) is available throughout the stream. Nonnegative prices imply

\( \displaystyle u_{t_h}\geq v_{t_h}(A\setminus T)-p(A\setminus T)\geq v_{t_h}(A\setminus T)-p(A). \)

Averaging and using \(\sum_{h:t_h=t}w_h=a_t\), we obtain

\( \displaystyle \mathcal U\geq\sum_hw_h\,\mathbb E_{\lambda^h}\!\left[v_{t_h}(A\setminus T)-p(A)\right]. \)

Revenue is

\( \displaystyle \mathcal R:=\int_0^Np(S(s))\,ds=\sum_jp_j(1-r_j(N))\geq p(T), \)

because every exhausted item has sold exactly its unit supply.

Welfare decomposes as \(W=\mathcal U+\mathcal R\). Applying the inequality proved in Section 3 gives

\( \displaystyle W\geq F(q)-F(q^2). \)

This argument uses only utility maximization and the final residual supplies. It consequently holds for every measurable choice among ties and every admissible arrival stream.

5. Selecting the scale

Set

\( \displaystyle K=\left\lceil\log_2\log_2(4m)\right\rceil,\qquad q_i=2^{-2^i}\quad(0\leq i\leq K). \)

Then \(q_0=1/2\), \(q_{i+1}=q_i^2\), and \(q_K\leq1/(4m)\). The endpoint bounds from Section 2 imply

\( \displaystyle F(q_0)-F(q_K)\geq B/2-B/4=B/4. \)

Telescoping gives

\( \displaystyle \sum_{i=0}^{K-1}\left[F(q_i)-F(q_i^2)\right]=F(q_0)-F(q_K)\geq B/4. \)

Compute these differences exactly and choose an index \(i^*\) attaining their maximum. Construct the prices of Section 3 with \(q=q_{i^*}\). Section 4 then proves

\( \displaystyle W\geq F(q_{i^*})-F(q_{i^*}^2)\geq\frac{B}{4K}. \)

To express this in the requested form, take natural logarithms. For \(m\geq3\),

\( \displaystyle K\leq3+2\ln\ln m. \)

Indeed, \(\log_2(4m)\leq4\ln m\), and taking another base-two logarithm and rounding upward gives the stated bound. Since \(\ln\ln m\geq\ln\ln3>0\),

\( \displaystyle 4K\leq C\ln\ln m,\qquad C=8+\frac{12}{\ln\ln3}. \)

Thus this universal constant satisfies

\( \displaystyle W\geq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{cont}}}{C\log\log m}. \)

6. Running time and conclusion

The algorithm computes one sparse optimal configuration allocation, solves polynomially many local LPs at \(K+1\) scales, selects the largest difference, and combines the corresponding optimal dual vectors.

Every \(q_i\) has bit length \(O(\log m)\), because \(2^K<2\log_2(4m)\). Exact rational LP solutions and the resulting weighted sums also have polynomial bit length. All demand queries use nonnegative rational prices, and local queries use the expressly supplied restriction to available items.

The total time and oracle-call count are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). No dependence on the numerical size of \(N\) or the inverse of a supported type mass is required. The construction ignores \(\epsilon\), and its stronger exact guarantee implies the requested guarantee for every positive rational \(\epsilon\).
