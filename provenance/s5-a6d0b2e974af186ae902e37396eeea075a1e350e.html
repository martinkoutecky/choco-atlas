Result

The payout problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). An optimal dual solution of a polynomial-size linear program for the grand coalition gives rational payouts satisfying every real coalition constraint.

The proof below treats negative utility values, negative payouts, absent types, and infeasible coalitions explicitly.

1. An exact linear program for coalition value

Write each utility representation as

\(U_t(x)=\min_{\ell\in I_t}\left(\beta_{t\ell}+\sum_{k\in M_t}\alpha_{t\ell k}x_k\right),\)

where \(I_t\) is a nonempty finite index set and all coefficients are rational. For a type without subscribers, use the single affine function with constant value zero. Let \(H=\sum_t|I_t|\).

Fix any real coalition vector \(q\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\). Introduce nonnegative variables \(a_{jk},y_k\) and unrestricted real variables \(z_t\). Define \(P(q)\) to maximize

\(\sum_t z_t\)

subject to the following inequalities:

  • \(\sum_{k\in M}a_{jk}\le q_{o(j)}\) for every \(j\in B\).
  • \(\sum_{j\in B}a_{jk}\le q_{o(k)}\) for every \(k\in M\).
  • \(y_k-\sum_{j\in B}R_{jk}a_{jk}\le0\) for every \(k\in M\).
  • \(-y_k\le-s_kq_{o(k)}\) for every \(k\in M\).
  • \(z_t-\sum_{k\in M_t}\alpha_{t\ell k}y_k\le\beta_{t\ell}q_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\ell\in I_t\).

Claim. This LP has exactly the coalition value \(v(q)\), with the specified convention for infeasibility.

For \(q_t>0\), the largest permitted value of \(z_t\), at fixed \(y_t\), is

\(\min_{\ell\in I_t}\left(\beta_{t\ell}q_t+\sum_{k\in M_t}\alpha_{t\ell k}y_k\right)=q_tU_t(y_t/q_t).\)

For \(q_t=0\), the service-unit capacity constraints force all rows belonging to type \(t\) to vanish, and the subscriber-time constraints force all columns belonging to type \(t\) to vanish. The rate upper bounds and nonnegativity then give \(y_k=0\) for every \(k\in M_t\). Consequently, all utility inequalities for this type become \(z_t\le0\), whose maximum is zero. Thus absent types have exactly their prescribed behavior.

The remaining constraints are precisely the original service constraints. Every feasible service allocation extends to a feasible LP point by choosing each \(z_t\) at its largest permitted value. Conversely, every LP point yields a feasible service allocation. This proves the claim, including infeasibility. At \(q=0\), the optimum is zero.

Whenever the service constraints are feasible, their feasible set in \((a,y)\) is compact. Indeed,

\(0\le a_{jk}\le q_{o(j)},\qquad 0\le y_k\le\sum_{j\in B}R_{jk}q_{o(j)}.\)

The set is also closed. After maximizing over \(z\), the objective is a finite sum of minima of finitely many affine functions of \(y\), hence is continuous and finite. It therefore attains a finite maximum on this compact set.

In particular, the promised feasibility of \(q=\mu\) guarantees that \(P(\mu)\) has a finite attained optimum.

2. The dual and the payout formula

Assign nonnegative multipliers to the five families of inequalities, respectively:

\(\lambda_j,\quad \eta_k,\quad \rho_k,\quad \sigma_k,\quad \gamma_{t\ell}.\)

The dual feasibility conditions are

\(\lambda_j+\eta_k-R_{jk}\rho_k\ge0\qquad(j\in B,\ k\in M),\)

\(\rho_k-\sigma_k-\sum_{\ell\in I_{o(k)}}\gamma_{o(k)\ell}\alpha_{o(k)\ell k}\ge0\qquad(k\in M),\)

\(\sum_{\ell\in I_t}\gamma_{t\ell}=1\qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau).\)

The first two inequalities correspond to the nonnegative primal variables \(a_{jk}\) and \(y_k\). The equality corresponds to the unrestricted primal variable \(z_t\), whose objective coefficient is one.

For any dual-feasible multipliers, define

\(p_t=\sum_{j\in B_t}\lambda_j+\sum_{k\in M_t}\eta_k-\sum_{k\in M_t}s_k\sigma_k+\sum_{\ell\in I_t}\beta_{t\ell}\gamma_{t\ell}.\)

The dual objective for coalition \(q\) is then

\(\sum_t q_t p_t.\)

This follows by collecting the right-hand-side terms of the primal constraints according to their owner type.

Crucially, the dual feasible set does not depend on \(q\). Only its objective does. The payout formula imposes no sign restriction on \(p_t\).

3. Every dual-feasible solution bounds every real coalition

We verify the coalition inequality directly.

For fixed dual-feasible multipliers, put

\(d_{jk}=\lambda_j+\eta_k-R_{jk}\rho_k\ge0,\)

\(e_k=\rho_k-\sigma_k-\sum_{\ell\in I_{o(k)}}\gamma_{o(k)\ell}\alpha_{o(k)\ell k}\ge0.\)

Let \((a,y,z)\) be any feasible point of \(P(q)\). Multiply each primal inequality by its associated nonnegative multiplier and add them all. Because \(\sum_{\ell\in I_t}\gamma_{t\ell}=1\), the resulting inequality is exactly

\(\sum_t z_t+\sum_{j\in B,\ k\in M}d_{jk}a_{jk}+\sum_{k\in M}e_ky_k\le\sum_t q_tp_t.\)

All terms following \(\sum_tz_t\) on the left are nonnegative. Hence

\(\sum_tz_t\le\sum_tq_tp_t.\)

Maximizing over primal feasible points proves

\(v(q)\le\sum_tq_tp_t\)

for every feasible coalition.

For an infeasible coalition, the inequality holds because \(v(q)=-\infty\). For \(q=0\), both sides are zero.

This argument applies directly to real coalition vectors; it uses no discretization, approximation, or enumeration of coalitions.

4. Grand-coalition optimality gives efficiency

Solve the dual LP with objective

\(\min\sum_t\mu_tp_t.\)

The primal \(P(\mu)\) is feasible and has a finite attained optimum, as proved above. The strong duality theorem for linear programming therefore guarantees an attained dual optimum with the same value. These are precisely the hypotheses needed for the theorem. [Strong duality, Theorem 2.2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/gc0v/webpub/OptFinFirstEdition2006.pdf).

Choose an optimal dual solution and compute \(p_t\) by the formula in Section 2. Strong duality gives

\(\sum_t\mu_tp_t=v(\mu).\)

Section 3 gives, for the same payouts,

\(\sum_tq_tp_t\ge v(q)\qquad\text{for every }q\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t].\)

Thus these payouts satisfy both required conditions.

5. Exact rational output and polynomial complexity

The algorithm consists of constructing the dual LP for \(\mu\), solving it exactly, and evaluating the payout formula.

The primal has

\(|B||M|+|M|+\tau\)

variables and

\(|B|+3|M|+H\)

inequalities, excluding variable-domain constraints. The dual has

\(|B|+3|M|+H\)

nonnegative variables, with \(|B||M|+|M|\) additional inequalities and \(\tau\) equalities.

Because utilities are explicitly represented, \(H\) is bounded by the input length plus the at most \(\tau\) singleton representations introduced for types without subscribers. Also, \(|B||M|\le m^2\). Therefore both LP dimensions are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

All grand-coalition coefficients are rational. Its dual objective uses only input coefficients and products such as \(\mu_ts_k\) and \(\mu_t\beta_{t\ell}\). A product of two encoded rationals has bit length at most the sum of their bit lengths up to a constant. Copying coefficients into a polynomial-size matrix consequently gives a polynomial total encoding length.

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly encoded rational LP can be solved in polynomial time in its binary encoding length, with an exact rational optimizer when a finite optimum exists. This is a bit-complexity statement; it does not require the feasible polyhedron to be bounded. Polynomial algorithms and polynomial bounds on the encoding size of basic solutions are covered in [MIT’s linear programming notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

Applied to the dual above, this theorem produces optimal rational multipliers of polynomial encoding length. Each payout is a sum of polynomially many products of these multipliers and input rationals. Exact rational arithmetic computes all payouts in polynomial time, and their total output length is polynomial.

No expansion of masses into individual providers is needed, and no normalization of \(\mu\) is used.
