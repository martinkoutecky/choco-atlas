The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite host graph, \(k\) facilities, rational masses \(d_t\) of customer types with reachability ranges \(R_t\), and \(\varepsilon > 0\), output a facility placement \(s\) such that every facility's uniformly captured mass is within factor \(1+\varepsilon\) of its best unilateral deviation.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H=(V,E)\) be a finite directed host graph with \(m=|V|\geq1\), and let \(k\geq1\) facilities each choose any vertex; co-location is allowed.
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) customer types, each with an explicitly listed nonempty reachability range \(R_t\subseteq V\) and positive binary rational mass \(d_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; masses need not sum to \(1\).
For a placement \(s\in V^k\), set \(c_t(s)=|\{j:s_j\in R_t\}|\). Type \(t\) divides its mass equally among these facilities when \(c_t(s)>0\), and contributes nothing when \(c_t(s)=0\).
Facility \(j\) maximizes its captured mass \(u_j(s)=\sum_{t:s_j\in R_t}d_t/c_t(s)\). After a deviation, all shares are recomputed.
Given positive binary rational \(\varepsilon\), the required placement satisfies \(u_j(v,s_{-j})\leq(1+\varepsilon)u_j(s)\) for every facility \(j\) and every vertex \(v\in V\).
Does this mass problem admit an FPTAS with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L,1/\varepsilon\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, or is computing such a placement NP-hard for some fixed rational \(\varepsilon>0\)?
For finite weighted clients whose ranges are closed outgoing neighborhoods, an FPTAS for the same approximation guarantee is known; computing an exact equilibrium is PLS-complete. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.14016#S5).
No separately stated result for the arbitrary-range mass formulation was verified; the known model already permits rational client weights.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Because the graded question supplies ranges but no customer locations, ranges are explicitly listed nonempty subsets of host vertices; closed outgoing neighborhoods remain a special case.
  • Masses are positive binary rationals with no normalization, matching purchasing-power weights; zero-mass types are omitted from the support.
  • Every facility may choose every host vertex, and multiple facilities may occupy the same vertex.
  • Uniform sharing counts individual facilities, including co-located facilities; uncovered types contribute no captured mass.
  • The approximation condition uses deviation payoff at most the stated factor times current payoff. The full version retains a reversed-factor typographical error, inconsistent with its algorithm and intended guarantee.
  • The accuracy parameter is a positive binary rational, and running time includes the facility count and inverse accuracy.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The arbitrary-range mass problem admits an FPTAS. The algorithm below takes at most
\(\left\lceil k^2 h_k(1+1/\varepsilon)\right\rceil\)
best-response moves, where \(h_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^k1/\ell\), and uses exact rational arithmetic of polynomial bit length.

The cited paper establishes the neighborhood version in Theorem 8. Here is a self-contained proof for the explicitly listed arbitrary ranges. [Source, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.14016#S5).

1. An exact potential

Set \(h_0=0\) and \(h_r=\sum_{\ell=1}^r1/\ell\) for \(r\geq1\). Define
\(\Phi(s)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}d_t h_{c_t(s)}\).

We claim that whenever facility \(j\) changes its location from \(a=s_j\) to \(b\), producing \(s'=(b,s_{-j})\),
\(\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)=u_j(s')-u_j(s)\).

To prove this, consider each type separately.

  • If both \(a,b\in R_t\), its facility count is unchanged, and its contribution to the deviator’s utility is unchanged.
  • If neither \(a\) nor \(b\) belongs to \(R_t\), both changes are zero.
  • If \(a\in R_t\) and \(b\notin R_t\), write \(c=c_t(s)\geq1\). The potential contribution decreases by \(d_t(h_c-h_{c-1})=d_t/c\), exactly the utility contribution lost.
  • If \(a\notin R_t\) and \(b\in R_t\), write \(c=c_t(s)\geq0\). The potential contribution increases by \(d_t(h_{c+1}-h_c)=d_t/(c+1)\), exactly the utility contribution gained.

Summing proves the identity, including the cases where a type becomes covered or uncovered.

2. Uniform bounds on potential and best responses

For each vertex, define its accessible mass by
\(W(v)=\sum_{t:v\in R_t}d_t\), and put \(M=\max_{v\in V}W(v)\).

Because at least one type exists, every range is nonempty, and every mass is positive, \(M>0\).

Let \(C(s)=\sum_{t:c_t(s)>0}d_t\) be the covered mass. Since every covered type has \(c_t(s)\geq1\),
\(\displaystyle C(s)\leq\sum_t d_t c_t(s)=\sum_{j=1}^kW(s_j)\leq kM.\)

Also, \(c_t(s)\leq k\), so
\(\displaystyle 0\leq\Phi(s)\leq h_k C(s)\leq kMh_k.\)

Next, fix any facility \(j\) and any placement of its opponents. Let
\(B_j(s)=\max_{v\in V}u_j(v,s_{-j})\)
be its best-response payoff.

Choose \(v^\star\) with \(W(v^\star)=M\). If facility \(j\) moves there, every type whose range contains \(v^\star\) shares its mass among at most \(k\) facilities. Thus
\(\displaystyle B_j(s)\geq u_j(v^\star,s_{-j})\geq\sum_{t:v^\star\in R_t}\frac{d_t}{k}=\frac{M}{k}.\)

This holds for every opponent placement. Co-location being allowed ensures that \(v^\star\) is always available.

3. Algorithm and termination bound

Start from any placement. Repeatedly:

  • Compute every facility’s current payoff and a best response by scanning all vertices.
  • If some facility \(j\) satisfies \(B_j(s)>(1+\varepsilon)u_j(s)\), move it to a best response.
  • Otherwise return \(s\).

At termination, every facility and vertex satisfy
\(u_j(v,s_{-j})\leq B_j(s)\leq(1+\varepsilon)u_j(s)\),
which is exactly the required guarantee.

For any executed move, abbreviate \(B=B_j(s)\) and \(u=u_j(s)\). The strict improvement test implies
\(\displaystyle B-u>B-\frac{B}{1+\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}B\geq\frac{\varepsilon M}{(1+\varepsilon)k}.\)

By the exact-potential identity, this is also a lower bound on the potential increase.

Consequently, if \(N>0\) moves occur, then
\(\displaystyle N\frac{\varepsilon M}{(1+\varepsilon)k}<\Phi(s^{(N)})-\Phi(s^{(0)})\leq kMh_k.\)

Cancelling \(M>0\) gives
\(\displaystyle N<k^2h_k\frac{1+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}=k^2h_k(1+1/\varepsilon).\)

An infinite execution is impossible, because a sufficiently long finite prefix would violate this bound. Since \(h_k\leq1+\ln k\), the move count is polynomial in \(k\) and \(1/\varepsilon\).

Using best responses is essential to this bound: the uniform lower bound applies to their payoffs, and need not hold for an arbitrary improving destination.

4. Exact arithmetic and polynomial running time

Precompute the membership indicators \(A_{t,v}\), equal to \(1\) when \(v\in R_t\) and \(0\) otherwise. For a candidate move of facility \(j\) to \(v\), the recomputed count is
\(\displaystyle c'_t=c_t(s)-A_{t,s_j}+A_{t,v}.\)

Its payoff is therefore
\(\displaystyle u_j(v,s_{-j})=\sum_{t:A_{t,v}=1}\frac{d_t}{c'_t}.\)

Every denominator appearing here lies between \(1\) and \(k\). A complete scan of all facilities and destinations uses \(O(km\tau)\) arithmetic operations.

Write \(d_t=p_t/q_t\), with positive integers \(p_t,q_t\), and use the common denominator
\(\displaystyle Q=k!\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}q_t.\)

For every possible positive count \(c\leq k\), \(q_tc\) divides \(Q\). Thus every payoff is represented exactly as an integer divided by \(Q\).

The bit length of \(Q\) is \(O(L+k\log(k+1))\). Every payoff is at most \(D=\sum_t d_t\), and \(D\leq\tau 2^L\); hence its numerator under denominator \(Q\) has bit length
\(O(L+k\log(k+1)+\log\tau)\).

If \(\varepsilon=a/b\), the improvement test between integer payoff numerators \(X\) and \(Y\) becomes \(bX>(a+b)Y\). It is therefore an exact integer comparison of polynomial bit complexity.

Combining the polynomial cost per scan with the move bound proves a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L,1/\varepsilon\). No normalization, expansion into unit-weight customers, or neighborhood restriction is required.
