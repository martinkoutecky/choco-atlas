The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two unit-mass populations with finite supported type sets \(U,V\), rational type masses \(p_u,q_v\), strict rankings over counterpart types, and unknown pairwise reward means \(a_{uv},b_{vu}\), each query of a supported pair \((u,v)\) returns a reward sample for each side. Determine the sample complexity needed to output, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), a type-level matching flow \(x\) maximizing \(\sum_{u,v}x_{uv}(a_{uv}+b_{vu})\), subject to \(\sum_v x_{uv}=p_u\), \(\sum_u x_{uv}=q_v\), \(x_{uv}\ge0\), and, for every \((u,v)\), the exact stability condition \(\bigl(\sum_{v':\,v'\succeq_u v}x_{uv'}=p_u\bigr)\lor\bigl(\sum_{u':\,u'\succeq_v u}x_{u'v}=q_v\bigr)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Two unit-mass populations have nonempty supports \(U,V\), positive binary-rational masses \(p_u,q_v\), and \(\sum_u p_u=\sum_v q_v=1\). Each society is given by its support list; \(\tau=|U|+|V|\) is the total support size, not the number of possible types. Put \(m=\max\{|U|,|V|\}\), and let \(L\) be the bit length of the supplied data.
Every type has a known strict total ranking of all counterpart types. Unknown means \(a_{uv},b_{vu}\in[0,C]\), for a fixed positive constant \(C\), induce exactly these rankings; \(\succeq\) includes equality.
An adaptive query of any \((u,v)\in U\times V\) returns both rewards and costs one query. Rewards are independent across queries and sides, with stationary distributions and centered \(1\)-subgaussian noise.
A stable flow satisfies \(x_{uv}\ge0\), \(\sum_vx_{uv}=p_u\), \(\sum_ux_{uv}=q_v\), and, for every \((u,v)\), \(\bigl(\sum_{v':v'\succeq_u v}x_{uv'}=p_u\bigr)\lor\bigl(\sum_{u':u'\succeq_v u}x_{u'v}=q_v\bigr)\).
Its welfare is \(R(x)=\sum_{u,v}x_{uv}(a_{uv}+b_{vu})\). Promise a unique welfare-maximizing stable flow \(x^*\). A supplied binary-rational \(\rho>0\) guarantees that every perturbation of each mean by less than \(\rho\) preserves the rankings and the same unique optimizer.
Given binary-rational \(0<\alpha<1/2\), output \(x^*\) exactly with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). Measure sample complexity by the worst-case expected number of pair queries over instances satisfying the promise.

Is this learning problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\rho^{-1},\log(1/\alpha)\), or is it NP-hard? What is its sample complexity as a function of these parameters?

For finite balanced markets, an exact-identification algorithm uses \(2N^2\beta^{-2}\log(4N^2/\alpha)\) pair samples, where \(\beta=\min\{\delta/(4N),\Delta_a/2,\Delta_b/2\}\), \(\delta>0\) is the best-versus-second-best stable welfare gap, and \(\Delta_a,\Delta_b\) are minimum within-person utility gaps. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00301v1#A3)
With known costs, minimum-cost bipartite stable allocation with real quotas and strict preferences is polynomial-time solvable; this is a full-information result, not a sampling result. [Source](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf)

1. Reduction to minimum-cost stable allocation

Set $n_U=|U|$ and $n_V=|V|$. Construct the complete bipartite graph on $U,V$, with vertex quotas $p_u,q_v$ and edge capacities
$$ c_{uv}=\min\{p_u,q_v\}. $$
Every nonnegative flow with the prescribed marginals already satisfies $x_{uv}\leq c_{uv}$.

For supplied rational welfare coefficients $w_{uv}$, assign edge cost $-w_{uv}$. The relevant full-information theorem is:

Minimum-cost stable-allocation theorem. A bipartite stable-allocation instance with strict preferences, vertex quotas, edge capacities, and linear edge costs admits polynomial-time minimum-cost optimization, without requiring integral quotas or capacities. Its construction uses a rotation representation followed by minimum-cost closure. The bipartite optimization construction is in Section 4 of [Dean–Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

Here are the stability hypotheses checked explicitly. By the row marginal equality,
$$ \sum_{v':\,v'\succeq_u v}x_{uv'}=p_u $$
holds exactly when $u$ assigns zero mass to every counterpart strictly worse than $v$. The analogous column statement holds for $v$.

Thus failure of both equalities means that both endpoints have positive allocations they prefer less than the proposed pair. Stable allocation additionally requires $x_{uv}<c_{uv}$ for a blocking pair. This qualification makes no difference here: if $x_{uv}=c_{uv}$, then $x_{uv}=p_u$ or $x_{uv}=q_v$, so at least one of the two stability equalities holds.

Consequently, the two notions of stability coincide. Rational welfare maximization over the flows in the question is therefore a full-information polynomial-time problem. No expansion of rational masses into individual agents is needed.

The source’s arithmetic running-time statement also yields polynomial bit complexity here. Let $D$ be the product of the supplied mass denominators, so its bit length is $O(L)$. Allocation updates in the rotation construction use differences and minima of quotas and residual capacities; hence allocation quantities and rotation multiplicities remain multiples of $D^{-1}$ with polynomial bit length. With rational objective coefficients, rotation costs are polynomial-length rational sums and products. The resulting closure problem has polynomial size and can be solved as an exact rational minimum-cut problem.

2. Stable flows decompose into stable forest flows

For a flow $x$, define its support graph by
$$ \operatorname{supp}(x)=\{(u,v):x_{uv}>0\}. $$

Support lemma. If $x$ is stable and $y$ is feasible with
$$ \operatorname{supp}(y)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x), $$
then $y$ is stable.

Proof. For each pair, one of the stability equalities for $x$ holds. That equality says that a specified collection of worse-ranked coordinates is zero. Those coordinates remain zero in $y$, and its marginal equality therefore gives the same stability equality. This applies to every pair. $\square$

Decomposition lemma. Every stable flow is a convex combination of stable flows whose support graphs are forests.

Proof. Suppose a stable flow $x$ has a support cycle. On this bipartite cycle, choose a vector $d$ with alternating entries $1,-1$ and zero entries elsewhere. Every row and column sum of $d$ is zero.

Define
$$ s_+=\min_{d_{uv}=-1}x_{uv}, \qquad s_-=\min_{d_{uv}=1}x_{uv}. $$
Both numbers are positive. The flows
$$ x^+=x+s_+d, \qquad x^-=x-s_-d $$
are feasible, have supports contained in that of $x$, and each loses at least one support edge. They are stable by the support lemma. Moreover,
$$ x= \frac{s_-}{s_++s_-}x^+ + \frac{s_+}{s_++s_-}x^-. $$
Apply this operation recursively to any cyclic support. Every branch strictly decreases its number of support edges, so the recursion terminates in forest flows. $\square$

This proves the following consequences.

Unique optimizers have forest support. If a uniquely optimal stable flow decomposes as above, every positively weighted component must have the same optimal welfare. Uniqueness forces every component to equal the optimizer. Hence its support is a forest, with at most $\tau-1$ positive coordinates.

Forest flows have polynomial-length rational coordinates. On a forest, remove a leaf. Its unique incident edge is determined by its remaining quota; subtract that amount from the neighboring quota and continue. Starting with quotas that are multiples of $D^{-1}$, every resulting coordinate is a multiple of $D^{-1}$. Since each coordinate lies in $[0,1]$, it has $O(L)$ bits.

Only finitely many forest flows are possible. Each forest support determines at most one feasible flow by leaf elimination, and there are finitely many supports. Thus stable welfare maximization can be viewed as maximization over finitely many rational forest flows, although enumerating them would generally be inefficient.

These conclusions justify exact output without assuming that unknown means are rational.

3. A quantitative consequence of robustness

Let $x^*$ be the promised optimizer, let $y\neq x^*$ be any stable flow, and write
$$ d_{uv}=x^*_{uv}-y_{uv}, \qquad g(y)=R(x^*)-R(y). $$

The following inequality remains valid even if the promise is interpreted as allowing only perturbations that remain in $[0,C]$:
$$ \begin{aligned} g(y)\geq {}& \sum_{d_{uv}>0}d_{uv} \left(\min\{\rho,a_{uv}\}+\min\{\rho,b_{vu}\}\right)\\ &+ \sum_{d_{uv}<0}(-d_{uv}) \left(\min\{\rho,C-a_{uv}\}+\min\{\rho,C-b_{vu}\}\right). \end{aligned} $$

Proof. Fix $0<t<\rho$. On coordinates with $d_{uv}>0$, decrease each mean by $t$, clipping at zero. On coordinates with $d_{uv}<0$, increase each mean by $t$, clipping at $C$.

Every coordinate changes by at most $t<\rho$. The promise preserves the rankings and keeps $x^*$ uniquely optimal. Consequently, $y$ remains stable and its perturbed welfare is strictly smaller than that of $x^*$. Rearranging this strict inequality gives the displayed expression with $t$ in place of $\rho$. Letting $t$ increase to $\rho$ proves the claim. $\square$

In particular, if all means lie in $[\rho,C-\rho]$, then
$$ R(x^*)-R(y)\geq 2\rho\|x^*-y\|_1. $$
The same inequality holds without the interior assumption if the promise permits unrestricted real perturbations.

Conversely, this normalized separation inequality ensures that every perturbation of all means by less than $\rho$ preserves the unique optimizer, provided it preserves the rankings. Indeed, because there are finitely many coordinates, their maximum perturbation magnitude is some $\varepsilon<\rho$. The change in the welfare difference is at most
$$ 2\varepsilon\|x^*-y\|_1, $$
leaving a strictly positive difference.

This is a separation statement relative to flow distance. It does not imply a positive gap between the optimum and all other stable flows.

4. Exact identification by sampling

First consider exact real-valued observations. Query every supported pair
$$ n=\left\lceil 8\rho^{-2}\log\frac{4E}{\alpha}\right\rceil $$
times. Denote the empirical means by $\bar a_{uv},\bar b_{vu}$.

The centered $1$-subgaussian assumption gives
$$ \Pr\!\left(|\bar a_{uv}-a_{uv}|\geq\rho/2\right) \leq 2\exp(-n\rho^2/8), $$
and the same inequality for the other side. A union bound over the $2E$ means shows that, with probability at least $1-\alpha$, every empirical mean has error strictly less than $\rho/2$.

Project estimates to $[0,C]$ and approximate them by rationals in that interval with additional error at most $\rho/4$. Projection cannot increase distance from the true mean. Hence, on the concentration event, every final rational estimate differs from its true mean by less than $3\rho/4$.

Run exact minimum-cost stable allocation with edge costs
$$ -\widehat w_{uv}, \qquad \widehat w_{uv}=\widehat a_{uv}+\widehat b_{vu}. $$
The robustness promise says these perturbed means have the same rankings and the same unique optimizer. The returned flow is therefore exactly $x^*$.

The query count is deterministic:
$$ Q\leq E\left\lceil 8\rho^{-2}\log\frac{4E}{\alpha}\right\rceil. $$
It consequently also bounds worst-case expected query complexity.

If either support is a singleton, the marginal equalities determine the only feasible flow, and zero queries suffice.

5. Finite-precision implementation and bit running time

The statistical model does not specify how a machine accesses real rewards. For the polynomial bit-time statement, use this convention: given a clipping bound $B$ and accuracy $h$, the oracle permits a rational approximation to
$$ \operatorname{clip}_{[-B,B]}(X) $$
with absolute error at most $h$, in time polynomial in the requested output precision. Treat the fixed constant $C$ as available to this precision. The sample-complexity statement itself does not require this computational convention.

Here is an implementation with the same asymptotic query bound. Put
$$ r=\min\{\rho,1\}. $$
Choose a dyadic $h\leq r/32$, an integer $t\geq1$ satisfying
$$ 2e^{-t^2/2}\leq r/16, $$
and an integer $B\geq C+t$.

For a reward $X$ with mean $\mu\in[0,C]$, let
$$ Y=\operatorname{clip}_{[-B,B]}(X). $$
The subgaussian tail bound gives
$$ |\mathbb EY-\mu| \leq 2\int_t^\infty e^{-s^2/2}\,ds \leq \frac{2}{t}e^{-t^2/2} \leq r/16. $$

The centered clipped reward is $\sqrt{2}$-subgaussian. To verify this, take independent copies $X',Y'$. Jensen’s inequality, symmetry, and the fact that clipping is nonexpansive give, for every real $\lambda$,
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E e^{\lambda(Y-\mathbb EY)} &\leq \mathbb E e^{\lambda(Y-Y')}\\ &=\mathbb E\cosh(\lambda(Y-Y'))\\ &\leq\mathbb E\cosh(\lambda(X-X'))\\ &\leq e^{\lambda^2}. \end{aligned} $$
The last inequality follows by multiplying the two original centered subgaussian moment-generating-function bounds.

Use
$$ n=\left\lceil64r^{-2}\log\frac{4E}{\alpha}\right\rceil $$
samples per pair. With probability at least $1-\alpha$, every empirical average of the unrounded $Y$ values differs from its expectation by less than $r/4$. Per-sample approximation contributes at most $h$ to an average. Projection and final rational approximation contribute at most another $h$. Thus every final estimated mean has error less than
$$ r/4+r/16+2h\leq3r/8<\rho. $$
The optimization argument from the previous section applies.

All retained observations and coefficients have polynomial bit length. The sampling work and rational optimization therefore take polynomial bit time under the stated oracle convention.

Finally, robustness forces every adjacent within-person ranking gap to be at least $2\rho$: if a gap were smaller, moving both means to their midpoint would be an allowed perturbation producing a tie. A ranking of $m$ counterparts consequently requires
$$ 2\rho(m-1)\leq C. $$
For nonsingleton supports, this bounds $\rho$ by a constant depending only on $C$. The finite-precision query count, including its ceiling, is therefore
$$ O_C\!\left(E\rho^{-2}\log\frac{E}{\alpha}\right) = O_C\!\left(m\tau\rho^{-2}\log\frac{m\tau}{\alpha}\right). $$

6. A robust lower-bound family

Let $k\geq4$ be a power of two. Construct $k$ groups, each with two types on each side. Give every type mass
$$ \eta=\frac{1}{2k}. $$
Thus
$$ m=2k,\qquad \tau=4k. $$
Assume
$$ 0<\rho\leq\frac{C}{6k+20}, \qquad H=6k\rho. $$

Each type ranks its two group partners above all outsiders. Its outsider means, in increasing order, are
$$ 0,3\rho,\ldots,3(2k-3)\rho. $$
Inside group $i$, use the following two matrices, with rows indexed by the receiving side:
$$ A_i=H\mathbf1+\rho \begin{pmatrix} d_i&0\\ 0&12 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B_i=H\mathbf1+\rho \begin{pmatrix} 0&12\\ 12&0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad d_i\in\{4,20\}. $$
Here $\mathbf1$ is the matrix whose entries are all one.

All means lie in $[0,C]$. Every ranking gap is at least $3\rho$, and the rankings are independent of the hidden choices $d_i$. Perturbations of individual means by less than $\rho$ therefore preserve all rankings.

Stable flows cannot cross groups. If a group sends positive mass outside, its equal total masses on the two sides imply that it also receives positive mass from outside. Choose a sending row type $u$ and a receiving column type $v$ in that group. Both assign positive mass to outsiders, whom they rank below their internal pair $(u,v)$. Both stability equalities fail, a contradiction.

Within group $i$, every feasible flow is
$$ x_i(z_i)= \begin{pmatrix} z_i&\eta-z_i\\ \eta-z_i&z_i \end{pmatrix}, \qquad 0\leq z_i\leq\eta. $$
Every such flow is stable. Each diagonal edge is the less-preferred internal edge at its column endpoint, and each off-diagonal edge is the less-preferred internal edge at its row endpoint. Outsider coordinates are zero, so the corresponding stability equalities hold. An outsider pair also satisfies its row equality because all positive row allocations are internal.

The welfare slope in $z_i$ is
$$ \rho(d_i+12-12-12)=\rho(d_i-12)\in\{-8\rho,8\rho\}. $$
Thus the unique optimizer has $z_i=0$ for $d_i=4$ and $z_i=\eta$ for $d_i=20$.

Each of the eight internal means enters this slope with coefficient of absolute value one. Perturbing each by less than $\rho$ changes the slope by strictly less than $8\rho$, preserving its sign. Hence every constructed instance satisfies the complete robustness promise.

Give every reward independent Gaussian noise of variance one. Only the first-side reward on $(u_{i,1},v_{i,1})$ depends on bit $i$. Its two possible means differ by $16\rho$, giving KL divergence
$$ 128\rho^2 $$
per informative pair query.

7. Simultaneous identification lower bound

Consider an algorithm correct with probability at least $1-\alpha$ on every constructed instance. Infinite worst-case expected query count already satisfies the lower bound, so assume finite expected count.

Put independent uniform priors on the hidden bits, encoded as $S_i\in\{-1,1\}$. Include the algorithm’s independent randomization in the transcript $T$. Conditional on $T$, the posterior factors over the bits: query choices and stopping decisions introduce no additional bit-dependent likelihood factors, while each observation likelihood involves only its queried bit.

Let $\ell_i$ be the posterior log odds for bit $i$, and let $N_i$ count its informative queries. An informative observation has the form
$$ X=H+12\rho+8\rho S_i+Z, \qquad Z\sim N(0,1), $$
and contributes $16\rho(X-H-12\rho)$ to $\ell_i$. Therefore
$$ \mathbb E[S_i\ell_i]=128\rho^2\mathbb EN_i. $$

For completeness, the optional-stopping justification uses the standardized martingale
$$ M_i=\sum_{\text{informative queries for }i}S_iZ. $$
At the stopping time its second moment is $\mathbb EN_i$. Truncating the stopping time and then using convergence in squared mean gives $\mathbb EM_i=0$. The martingale term in $S_i\ell_i$ is $16\rho M_i$, retaining the required scale factor.

Define the smaller posterior probability
$$ e_i=\frac{1}{1+e^{|\ell_i|}}. $$
The largest posterior probability of a complete bit vector is
$$ P=\prod_{i=1}^k(1-e_i). $$
Conditional success of any estimator is at most $P$, so
$$ \mathbb EP\geq1-\alpha>1/2. $$
Since $P\leq1$, this implies
$$ \Pr(P\geq1/4)\geq1/3. $$
On this event,
$$ \sum_i e_i\leq-\log P\leq\log4. $$
Convexity of $-\log$ then yields
$$ \sum_i\log(1/e_i)\geq k\log\frac{k}{\log4}. $$

For $x\geq0$,
$$ x(1-\tanh(x/2))=\frac{2x}{e^x+1}\leq1, \qquad \log(1+e^x)\leq x+\log2. $$
Also,
$$ \mathbb E[S_i\ell_i\mid T] = |\ell_i|\tanh(|\ell_i|/2). $$
Consequently,
$$ \mathbb E\log(1/e_i) \leq128\rho^2\mathbb EN_i+1+\log2. $$
Writing $N$ for total pair queries, so $N\geq\sum_iN_i$, gives
$$ \mathbb EN\geq \frac{k}{128\rho^2} \left( \frac13\log\frac{k}{\log4}-1-\log2 \right). $$

To obtain a positive bound also when the bracket is small, use entropy. If the complete bit-vector estimate has error probability at most $\alpha$, the error-indicator entropy argument gives
$$ I(S;T)\geq(1-\alpha)k\log2-\log2 \geq\frac{k\log2}{4}, $$
where the last inequality uses $k\geq4$ and $\alpha<1/2$.

Posterior factorization and the binary log-odds formula give
$$ \begin{aligned} I(S;T) &= \sum_i\mathbb E \left[ \frac{S_i\ell_i}{2}-\log\cosh(\ell_i/2) \right]\\ &\leq64\rho^2\sum_i\mathbb EN_i \leq64\rho^2\mathbb EN. \end{aligned} $$
Thus
$$ \mathbb EN\geq\frac{k\log2}{256\rho^2}. $$
Combining the last two query bounds proves, uniformly for $k\geq4$ and $0<\alpha<1/2$,
$$ \mathbb EN=\Omega(k\rho^{-2}\log k). $$

For the confidence dependence, compare any fixed bit vector with the vector obtained by flipping bit $i$. The event that the algorithm returns the original optimizer has probability at least $1-\alpha$ on the original instance and at most $\alpha$ on the flipped instance. KL data processing gives
$$ 128\rho^2\mathbb E_SN_i \geq (1-2\alpha)\log\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}. $$
For $0<\alpha\leq1/4$, summing over $i$ proves
$$ \mathbb E_SN=\Omega(k\rho^{-2}\log(1/\alpha)). $$
For $1/4<\alpha<1/2$, $\log(1/\alpha)$ is bounded and is absorbed by the already proved $\log k$ bound. Hence, throughout the requested confidence range,
$$ \mathbb EN = \Omega\!\left(k\rho^{-2}\log\frac{k}{\alpha}\right). $$
Bayesian expected cost is at most worst-case expected cost. Since $\tau=4k$, the claimed lower bound follows.

8. What is settled and what remains open

The polynomial-time alternative holds under the explicit finite-precision oracle convention. Exact identification has the proved upper bound
$$ O_C\!\left(m(\tau-m)\rho^{-2} \log\frac{m(\tau-m)}{\alpha}\right). $$
Singleton supports require zero queries.

For balanced supports $\tau=4k$ with $k\geq4$ a power of two, and
$$ \rho\leq\frac{C}{6k+20}, $$
the proved lower bound is
$$ \Omega\!\left(\tau\rho^{-2}\log\frac{\tau}{\alpha}\right) $$
for every $0<\alpha<1/2$. This does not establish a matching bound for arbitrary support shapes.

The lower-bound family also explains why the supplied finite-market welfare gap cannot simply be imported. Moving an optimal group coordinate a distance $\varepsilon$ into its stable interval produces a nonoptimal stable flow with welfare loss $8\rho\varepsilon$. This tends to zero despite the robustness promise.

The remaining mathematical question is whether the factor $m$ in the general upper bound is necessary, or whether an algorithm can exploit stability to avoid estimating every supported pair to accuracy proportional to $\rho$.
