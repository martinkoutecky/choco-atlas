Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Input finite sets of household types \(A\) and unit types \(J\), rational population masses \(\mu_a\) summing to \(1\), per-capita unit supplies \(q_j\), initial compatible sets \(N_a\), relaxable sets \(D_a\), discomforts \(\rho_{aj}\), and a per-capita budget \(\beta\). Choose advice masses \(z_{a,S}\geq 0\) for bundles \(S\subseteq D_a\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq D_a}z_{a,S}=\mu_a\), satisfying either \(\sum_{a,S}z_{a,S}|S|\leq\beta\) or \(\sum_{a,S}z_{a,S}\sum_{j\in S}\rho_{aj}\leq\beta\). For every partial-compliance split \(y_{a,S,R}\geq0\), where \(R\subseteq S\) and \(\sum_{R\subseteq S}y_{a,S,R}=z_{a,S}\), form cohorts compatible with \(N_a\cup R\). Require that, in every resulting maximum mass flow, initially protected type masses and every cohort with \(R\neq\varnothing\) are fully matched. Find a feasible advice policy maximizing the maximum-flow value under full compliance.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|A|\) household types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), where \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Each household demands one unit. There are \(m=|J|\geq1\) unit types with binary-rational supplies \(q_j\geq0\), not necessarily summing to one.
For each \(a\), input initial compatibilities \(N_a\subseteq J\), relaxable incompatibilities \(D_a\subseteq J\setminus N_a\), and positive binary-rational discomforts \(\rho_{aj}\) for \(j\in D_a\); all other incompatibilities are forbidden. Input a binary-rational budget \(\beta\geq0\) and select one cost rule: \(c_a(S)=|S|\) or \(c_a(S)=\sum_{j\in S}\rho_{aj}\).
An advice policy consists of real masses \(z_{a,S}\geq0\), indexed by \(S\subseteq D_a\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq D_a}z_{a,S}=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_{a,S}z_{a,S}c_a(S)\leq\beta\). Bundles are implicit, not enumerated in the input; \(S=\varnothing\) means no advice, and costs are charged for advice regardless of compliance.
For any real partial-compliance split \(y_{a,S,R}\geq0\), indexed by \(R\subseteq S\), require \(\sum_{R\subseteq S}y_{a,S,R}=z_{a,S}\). Cohort \((a,S,R)\) has mass \(y_{a,S,R}\) and compatibilities \(N_a\cup R\).
Allocation is a bipartite maximum flow: source-to-cohort capacity equals cohort mass, allowed cohort-to-unit arcs have unlimited capacity, and unit-to-sink capacity is \(q_j\). A cohort is fully matched when its source arc is saturated; every maximum flow is possible, with no prescribed tie-breaking.
Let \(P\subseteq A\) contain exactly the types whose entire mass is saturated in every baseline maximum flow, using cohorts of masses \(\mu_a\) and compatibilities \(N_a\). A policy is admissible if, for every compliance split, every maximum flow fully matches each cohort with \(a\in P\) or \(R\neq\varnothing\).
Let \(V(z)\) be the maximum-flow value under full compliance, where \(y_{a,S,S}=z_{a,S}\). The optimization problem maximizes \(V(z)\) over admissible policies; its threshold decision version additionally inputs a binary rational \(t\in[0,1]\) and asks whether some admissible policy has \(V(z)\geq t\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

For each of the two cost rules, is this continuous threshold problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The discrete counterpart with strong no harm and strong benefit under every subset of advised relaxations has a polynomial-time exact optimization algorithm for both cost rules. [Verified discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18325v1).
No result settling this exact continuous formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Defined initially protected types by saturation of their entire mass in every baseline maximum flow, and carried that protection to all their compliance cohorts.
  • Specified unit demand per household, nonnegative rational supplies without supply normalization, and a nonnegative rational budget.
  • Required relaxable sets to exclude initial compatibilities; unlisted incompatibilities cannot be relaxed.
  • Required strictly positive rational discomforts, consistent with the discrete algorithm's explicit strict-positivity assumption.
  • Allowed arbitrary nonnegative real advice and compliance masses, including empty advice and empty compliance bundles.
  • Made the cost rule an input choice and charged for all advised relaxations independently of compliance.
  • Specified cohort flow capacities and universal quantification over maximum flows, with no tie-breaking restriction.
  • Introduced a rational threshold decision version of the stated optimization problem and specified that exponentially many possible bundles are implicit.

Result

For both cost rules, the continuous threshold problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

In fact, the optimum is attained by a rational policy using only singleton advice bundles. The proof below treats real masses directly; it does not rely on expanding the society into discrete households or applying the [discrete algorithm cited in the question](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18325v1).

1. Notation and refinement of advice

Let \(\nu\) be the baseline maximum-flow value. Define
\[ \mathcal E_N=\{(a,j):j\in N_a\}, \qquad \mathcal E_D=\{(a,j):j\in D_a\}. \]

For singleton advice, its cost coefficient is
\[ \kappa_{aj}= \begin{cases} 1,&c_a(S)=|S|,\\ \rho_{aj},&c_a(S)=\sum_{j\in S}\rho_{aj}. \end{cases} \]

We first establish a useful preservation property.

Refinement property. Suppose a policy is admissible. Split each advised cohort into pieces, and replace the advice bundle \(S\) of each piece by some \(T\subseteq S\). The resulting policy is admissible.

To justify this, retain the original parent label on every piece. Under any compliance split of the refined policy, a piece advised \(T\) accepts some \(R\subseteq T\subseteq S\). Thus these same accepted sets define a permitted compliance split of the original policy.

Splitting or merging cohorts with identical compatibilities preserves the maximum-flow value: an allocation to a merged cohort can be distributed among its pieces up to their respective capacities. Moreover, a merged cohort is fully saturated exactly when all its pieces are fully saturated. Consequently, any maximum flow violating a guarantee for a refined cohort would induce a maximum flow violating the corresponding original guarantee. This proves the property.

Both costs weakly decrease under this refinement, because replacing \(S\) by a subset weakly decreases its cardinality and its sum of positive discomforts.

Singleton reduction. Every admissible policy has an admissible singleton policy with the same full-compliance value and no greater cost.

Indeed, choose a full-compliance maximum flow of the original policy. Split each cohort according to the unit receiving its allocation. A piece allocated to \(j\in S\) receives advice \(\{j\}\); a piece allocated through an initial compatibility receives empty advice. Any unmatched piece also receives empty advice.

This is a refinement, hence is admissible and costs no more. The chosen allocation remains feasible, so the new full-compliance value is at least the original value. Conversely, every allocation under the refined full-compliance policy lifts to an allocation under the original full-compliance policy, since all refined compatibilities are subsets of the original ones. Therefore the values are equal.

Henceforth, write \(x_{aj}\) for the mass advised \(\{j\}\), with
\[ x_{aj}\geq0, \qquad \sum_{j\in D_a}x_{aj}\leq\mu_a. \]
The empty-advice mass is \(\mu_a-\sum_{j\in D_a}x_{aj}\).

2. A cut formula for singleton policies

For a vector \(r\) satisfying \(0\leq r_{aj}\leq x_{aj}\), let \(r_{aj}\) be the mass accepting singleton advice \(\{j\}\). All remaining mass of type \(a\) has initial compatibilities \(N_a\).

Let \(\Phi(r)\) be the maximum-flow value of this compliance instance. In particular, \(\Phi(0)=\nu\), and full compliance with policy \(x\) has value \(\Phi(x)\).

For \(U\subseteq J\), define
\[ B_U=\sum_{j\in U}q_j+ \sum_{a:N_a\not\subseteq U}\mu_a, \]
and
\[ C_U=\{(a,j)\in\mathcal E_D: N_a\subseteq U,\ j\notin U\}. \]

The minimum-cut formula gives
\[ \boxed{\displaystyle \Phi(r)=\min_{U\subseteq J} \left(B_U+\sum_{(a,j)\in C_U}r_{aj}\right).} \tag{1} \]

Here is the calculation. Fix the source-side unit set \(U\). A cohort can lie on the source side without cutting a compatibility arc exactly when all its compatibilities lie in \(U\). Thus the cut capacity is
\[ \sum_{j\in U}q_j+ \sum_a\left(\mu_a-\sum_{j\in D_a}r_{aj}\right) \mathbf1_{\{N_a\not\subseteq U\}} + \sum_{(a,j)\in\mathcal E_D}r_{aj} \mathbf1_{\{N_a\cup\{j\}\not\subseteq U\}}. \]
Collecting the coefficient of \(r_{aj}\) yields \(1\) precisely for \((a,j)\in C_U\), and \(0\) otherwise, proving (1).

In particular, \(\Phi\) is continuous and coordinatewise nondecreasing. Every affine function in (1) has coefficients in \(\{0,1\}\).

Reducing a singleton policy coordinatewise preserves admissibility, by the refinement property.

3. Two residual-flow facts

The following facts hold for real capacities. Unlimited compatibility capacities can be replaced by any finite capacity exceeding total household mass.

Fact A: enlargement permits augmentation without decreasing cohort allocations

Suppose compatibility arcs are added, while all cohort masses and unit supplies remain fixed. Starting from any feasible flow, there exists a maximum flow in the enlarged network whose allocation to every cohort is at least its starting allocation.

To prove this, take a maximum flow in the residual network and discard its circulation components. Its remaining components are source-to-sink paths. Such paths never revisit the source, so they cannot traverse a reverse source-to-cohort arc. Applying them therefore never decreases any cohort's source-arc flow. The resulting flow is maximum by the residual maximum-flow theorem.

Fact B: deleting arcs from forced cohorts preserves attainable source allocations if the value is unchanged

Let \(G\) be an allocation network, and let \(H\) be a collection of positive-mass cohorts saturated in every maximum flow of \(G\). Form \(G'\) by deleting compatibility arcs only from cohorts in \(H\), keeping all capacities fixed. Suppose \(G'\) and \(G\) have the same maximum-flow value.

Then every vector of cohort allocations attainable by a maximum flow of \(G\) is also attainable by a maximum flow of \(G'\).

Proof. Choose a maximum flow \(g'\) of \(G'\). It is also maximum in \(G\). Let \(g\) be any maximum flow of \(G\).

The difference between these flows is a circulation in the residual network of \(g'\) in \(G\): both flows have the same value, so their difference has zero divergence at every vertex, including the source and sink. Decompose this circulation into directed simple cycles.

A residual cycle through the source cannot visit the sink, because its source-to-sink prefix would be an augmenting path, contradicting maximality.

It also cannot visit any cohort \(h\in H\). Indeed, the source arc of \(h\) is saturated in \(g'\), so its reverse arc has positive residual capacity. A residual path from the source to \(h\), followed by that reverse arc, would give a residual cycle that decreases the allocation to \(h\) while preserving total flow value. This contradicts the definition of \(H\).

Therefore every cycle through the source avoids \(H\), and hence avoids all deleted compatibility arcs. Apply only these cycles to \(g'\). This is feasible in \(G'\): their total use of each residual arc is bounded by that of the entire difference circulation.

Cycles not through the source do not affect source-arc flows. Thus the resulting maximum flow in \(G'\) has exactly the same cohort allocations as \(g\). \(\square\)

4. Normalization of admissible singleton policies

Call \(w\) minimal for its value if there is no distinct vector \(u\) with \(0\leq u\leq w\) and \(\Phi(u)=\Phi(w)\).

We prove the central claim:

Normalization lemma. If \(w\) is an admissible singleton policy minimal for its value, then
\[ \boxed{\displaystyle \Phi(w)=\nu+\sum_{(a,j)\in\mathcal E_D}w_{aj}.} \tag{2} \]

4.1. Removing one coordinate decreases value by exactly its mass

Write \(v=\Phi(w)\), and let \(e\in\mathcal E_D\) have \(w_e>0\). Denote its coordinate unit vector by \(\mathbf e_e\).

At least one cut attaining the minimum in (1) at \(w\) has coefficient \(1\) on coordinate \(e\). Otherwise all minimizing cuts would be unchanged by decreasing this coordinate, and, since there are finitely many cuts, a sufficiently small decrease would preserve \(v\). That would contradict minimality.

All cut expressions decrease by at most \(\delta\), and this minimizing cut decreases by exactly \(\delta\). Hence
\[ \Phi(w-\delta\mathbf e_e)=v-\delta \qquad (0\leq\delta\leq w_e). \tag{3} \]

Let \(w^{-e}=w-w_e\mathbf e_e\). Thus
\[ \Phi(w^{-e})=v-w_e. \tag{4} \]

4.2. Removing a coordinate preserves minimality

We claim that \(w^{-e}\) is minimal for its own value.

Suppose otherwise. By coordinatewise monotonicity, there is some \(f\neq e\) and some \(\delta>0\), chosen also to satisfy \(\delta<w_e\), such that
\[ \Phi(w^{-e}-\delta\mathbf e_f)=\Phi(w^{-e})=v-w_e. \tag{5} \]
For example, take a strictly smaller vector preserving the value, choose one decreased coordinate, and decrease only that coordinate by a sufficiently small amount.

By (3), applied to \(f\),
\[ \Phi(w-\delta\mathbf e_f)=v-\delta. \tag{6} \]

We now derive a contradiction using admissibility.

Represent the mass \(w_e\) as a distinct cohort throughout. In policy \(w\), this cohort accepts its singleton advice and is fully saturated in every maximum flow. Choose a maximum flow and delete all allocation supplied by this cohort. Reduce its compatibilities to its initial set.

The resulting flow is feasible for \(w^{-e}\), has value \(v-w_e\), and leaves this entire cohort unmatched. By (4), it is maximum.

Next, split the advised cohort \(f\) into two identical pieces, one of mass \(\delta\). In the instance \(w^{-e}\), both pieces are saturated in every maximum flow, because \(w^{-e}\) is admissible and they accept advice.

Make the mass-\(\delta\) piece ignore its advice. This deletes compatibility arcs only from a forced cohort. Equation (5) says that the maximum-flow value is unchanged. Fact B therefore gives a maximum flow for \(w^{-e}-\delta\mathbf e_f\) in which the entire mass-\(w_e\) cohort remains unmatched.

Finally, let that cohort accept advice \(e\). This produces the instance \(w-\delta\mathbf e_f\). By Fact A, there is a maximum flow obtained without decreasing any other cohort's allocation. Its total increase over the preceding flow is, by (5) and (6),
\[ (v-\delta)-(v-w_e)=w_e-\delta. \]
Since the mass-\(w_e\) cohort started unmatched, its allocation in this maximum flow is at most \(w_e-\delta<w_e\).

But \(w-\delta\mathbf e_f\) is a subpolicy of \(w\), hence is admissible. The cohort accepting advice \(e\) must therefore be fully saturated in every maximum flow—a contradiction.

Thus \(w^{-e}\) is minimal.

4.3. Induction

Induct on the number of positive coordinates of \(w\).

If there are none, \(\Phi(w)=\Phi(0)=\nu\). Otherwise remove a positive coordinate \(e\). The resulting policy is admissible and minimal by the preceding argument. Induction and (4) give
\[ \Phi(w) =\Phi(w^{-e})+w_e =\nu+\sum_f w_f. \]
This proves (2). \(\square\)

For every admissible singleton policy \(x\), such a minimal subpolicy exists with the same value. Indeed, minimize \(\sum_e w_e\) over
\[ 0\leq w\leq x,\qquad \Phi(w)=\Phi(x). \]
This feasible set is nonempty and compact, because \(\Phi\) is continuous. A minimizer is minimal for its value, remains admissible, and costs no more than \(x\).

Thus every admissible policy has an admissible singleton subpolicy satisfying (2), with the same value and no greater cost.

5. The polynomial-size linear program

Use nonnegative variables \(f_{aj}\) for \((a,j)\in\mathcal E_N\), and \(x_{aj}\) for \((a,j)\in\mathcal E_D\).

Maximize
\[ \nu+\sum_{(a,j)\in\mathcal E_D}x_{aj} \tag{7} \]
subject to
\[ \sum_{(a,j)\in\mathcal E_N}f_{aj}=\nu, \tag{8} \]
\[ \sum_{j\in N_a}f_{aj} +\sum_{j\in D_a}x_{aj}\leq\mu_a \qquad(a\in A), \tag{9} \]
\[ \sum_{a:j\in N_a}f_{aj} +\sum_{a:j\in D_a}x_{aj}\leq q_j \qquad(j\in J), \tag{10} \]
and
\[ \sum_{(a,j)\in\mathcal E_D}\kappa_{aj}x_{aj}\leq\beta. \tag{11} \]

For threshold decision, additionally require
\[ \nu+\sum_{(a,j)\in\mathcal E_D}x_{aj}\geq t. \tag{12} \]

The only difference between the two cost rules is the choice of \(\kappa_{aj}\).

6. Exact equivalence with the original problem

6.1. Every feasible LP solution gives an admissible policy

Define
\[ z_{a,\{j\}}=x_{aj}, \qquad z_{a,\varnothing}=\mu_a-\sum_{j\in D_a}x_{aj}, \]
with all other bundle masses zero. Constraints (9) and (11) ensure valid masses and budget.

By (8), \(f\) is a baseline maximum allocation. Therefore it fully matches every type in \(P\). Constraint (9) consequently implies
\[ x_{aj}=0 \qquad(a\in P,\ j\in D_a). \tag{13} \]

Consider any real compliance vector \(0\leq r\leq x\). Allocate \(f\) to the empty-advice cohorts and allocate each accepting cohort of mass \(r_{aj}\) to \(j\).

This is feasible: (9) fits \(f\) inside the empty-advice masses, and (10) remains valid when \(x\) is replaced by \(r\). Its value is
\[ \nu+\sum_{(a,j)\in\mathcal E_D}r_{aj}. \tag{14} \]

Conversely, in any allocation for this compliance instance, delete all allocation using relaxed compatibility arcs. What remains is a feasible baseline allocation after merging cohorts by household type, so it has value at most \(\nu\). The deleted allocation has value at most \(\sum r_{aj}\). Thus (14) is the maximum-flow value.

Equality forces every accepting cohort to send its entire mass through its relaxed arc: otherwise the total relaxed allocation would be strictly less than \(\sum r_{aj}\), making (14) impossible. The remaining baseline allocation has value exactly \(\nu\), so it fully matches every type in \(P\).

By (13), types in \(P\) receive no nonempty advice. Therefore every protected cohort and every accepting cohort is fully matched in every maximum flow. The policy is admissible.

Under full compliance, its value is exactly (7).

6.2. Every admissible policy gives an LP solution of the same value

Start with any admissible policy of value \(v\). Apply the singleton reduction, then choose a minimal subpolicy \(w\) preserving \(v\). Its cost is no greater than the original cost. By the normalization lemma,
\[ v=\nu+\sum_e w_e. \tag{15} \]

Choose a full-compliance maximum flow for \(w\). Let \(h\) be its total allocation through relaxed arcs. Since each advised cohort has mass \(w_e\),
\[ h\leq\sum_e w_e. \]
Its remaining allocation uses only initial compatibilities and is baseline-feasible after merging cohorts, so
\[ v-h\leq\nu. \]
Together with (15), these inequalities force
\[ h=\sum_e w_e, \qquad v-h=\nu. \tag{16} \]

Because each singleton cohort's relaxed allocation is at most its mass, equality in (16) means that every such cohort sends its entire mass through its relaxed arc.

Set \(x=w\), and let \(f_{aj}\) be the flow through the initial compatibility \((a,j)\). Equations (16) give (8). Household and unit capacities give (9) and (10), while the nonincreasing cost gives (11).

The LP objective is \(v\). In particular, any admissible policy meeting the threshold yields an LP solution satisfying (12).

This proves exact equivalence.

7. Bit complexity and attainment

The baseline network has \(m+\tau+2\) vertices and at most \(\tau m+m+\tau\) arcs. Its rational maximum-flow value \(\nu\) is computable in polynomial time and has polynomial bit length. For example, multiplying by the product of the input denominators gives integral capacities with polynomial bit length; no population expansion is needed.

The LP has
\[ |\mathcal E_N|+|\mathcal E_D|\leq\tau m \]
variables, since initial and relaxable compatibilities are disjoint. It has polynomially many constraints, and every coefficient has bit length polynomial in \(L\). Exact rational linear programming therefore solves its feasibility and optimization versions in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The LP is feasible—take \(x=0\) and any baseline maximum allocation—and its feasible region is compact by the household bounds. Consequently, an optimum exists and can be chosen rational with polynomial encoding length. Its singleton policy is an optimal policy for the original continuous formulation.
