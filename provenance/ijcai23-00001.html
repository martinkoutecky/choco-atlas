The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of repeated network types with rational masses \(\mu\in\Delta^\tau\), an equitable type-level row-stochastic influence matrix \(W_T\), type-specific dynamics and prospect parameters, target \(x^\star\), finite channel menus \(Q_l\), and exact prospects \(u_{t,l}^{q}\), choose \(A=\{(l,q_l):q_l\in Q_l\}\) with one action per channel to minimize \(g_\mu(A)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t(x_{\mathrm{ss},t}^{A}-x^\star)^2\), or compute the local-search solution with Theorem 3's guarantee.

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq2\) channels with explicitly listed nonempty finite menus \(Q_l\). An action is broadcast to every type; a feasible strategy \(A\) selects exactly one pair \((l,q)\) per channel, without randomization or splitting masses between actions.
The input includes a nonnegative row-stochastic matrix \(W_T\) whose directed graph is strongly connected. Its entry \(W_{ts}\) is the total influence that every member of type \(t\) assigns to type \(s\). Let \(\Lambda_j=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{tj})\), where \(\lambda_{tj}\in[0,1]\), \(\sum_{j=1}^3\lambda_{tj}=1\), and \(\Lambda_1\neq I\). Also given are trust coefficients \(d_t\geq0\), predispositions \(b_t\in\mathbb R\), a common target \(x^\star\in\mathbb R\), and signed exact prospects \(u_{t,l}^{q}\). All numerical inputs are binary rationals; prospects are supplied directly rather than computed from nonlinear perception functions.
Put \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d_t)\), \(H=(I-\Lambda_1W_T)^{-1}\), \(\psi=H\Lambda_2b\), and \(a_{t,l,q}=(H\Lambda_3D u_l^q)_t\). The steady-state opinion is \(x_{\mathrm{ss},t}^{A}=\psi_t+\sum_{(l,q)\in A}a_{t,l,q}\), and the loss is \(g_\mu(A)=\sum_t\mu_t(x_{\mathrm{ss},t}^{A}-x^\star)^2\).
To specify the shifted approximation objective, let \(S=\bigcup_l\{(l,q):q\in Q_l\}\), \(c_{t,l}=\sum_{q\in Q_l:a_{t,l,q}<0}a_{t,l,q}\), \(\alpha_{t,l,q}=a_{t,l,q}-c_{t,l}\), and \(\beta_t=x^\star-\psi_t-\sum_l c_{t,l}\). Result

The problem admits a deterministic algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,r,L\). In fact, the algorithm returns a feasible strategy \(A\) satisfying

\(f_Z(A)\geq \frac14\max_{A'\text{ feasible}}f_Z(A').\)

Thus it achieves the requested \(\frac16\)-approximation. The proof uses the quadratic structure of the supplied objective.

1. Exact preprocessing and the quadratic objective

First, \(H\) is well defined. Put \(P=\Lambda_1W_T\). Since \(P\) is nonnegative and its row sums are at most \(1\), every eigenvalue has modulus at most \(1\).

Suppose \(Pv=\zeta v\), where \(v\neq0\) and \(|\zeta|=1\). Let \(M=\max_t|v_t|\), and let \(U=\{t:|v_t|=M\}\). For every \(t\in U\),

\(M=\left|\lambda_{t1}\sum_sW_{ts}v_s\right|\leq\lambda_{t1}\sum_sW_{ts}|v_s|\leq\lambda_{t1}M\leq M.\)

Equality forces \(\lambda_{t1}=1\) and forces every \(s\) with \(W_{ts}>0\) to belong to \(U\). Strong connectivity therefore implies \(U=T\), and hence \(\Lambda_1=I\), a contradiction. Consequently \(\rho(P)<1\), so \(H=(I-P)^{-1}\) exists.

All preprocessing can be performed with exact rational arithmetic. In particular, matrix inversion has polynomial bit complexity: after clearing denominators, determinant and cofactor bounds give polynomial bit lengths, and fraction-free Gaussian elimination computes the inverse in polynomial time.

For an action \(e=(l,q)\), write \(\alpha_{t,e}=\alpha_{t,l,q}\). These coefficients are nonnegative. Indeed, if \(a_{t,l,q}\geq0\), then \(c_{t,l}\leq0\); if \(a_{t,l,q}<0\), then the sum defining \(c_{t,l}\) includes that term and possibly additional negative terms, so \(c_{t,l}\leq a_{t,l,q}\).

Define the extension

\(F_0(B)=Z-\bar g_\mu(B),\qquad B\subseteq S.\)

The promise gives \(F_0(B)>0\) for every subset. For every feasible strategy \(A\),

\(\sum_{e\in A}\alpha_{t,e}-\beta_t=\psi_t+\sum_{e\in A}a_{t,e}-x^\star,\)

so \(F_0(A)=f_Z(A)\).

Let \(M_0\) contain the unique action from every singleton menu, and let \(V=S\setminus M_0\). Fix these mandatory actions and define

\(F(B)=F_0(M_0\cup B),\qquad B\subseteq V.\)

If \(V\) is empty, the unique feasible strategy is optimal. Otherwise, the remaining channel blocks partition \(V\), and every block contains at least two actions.

Set

\(\widetilde\beta_t=\beta_t-\sum_{e\in M_0}\alpha_{t,e}.\)

Expanding the squares, and using indicator variables \(x_e\in\{0,1\}\), gives

\(F(x)=C+\sum_{e\in V}h_ex_e-\sum_{\{e,f\}\subseteq V}w_{ef}x_ex_f,\)

where

\(C=Z-\sum_t\mu_t\widetilde\beta_t^2,\)

\(h_e=\sum_t\mu_t\left(2\widetilde\beta_t\alpha_{t,e}-\alpha_{t,e}^2\right),\)

and

\(w_{ef}=2\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_{t,e}\alpha_{t,f}\geq0.\)

Thus \(F\) is a quadratic submodular function, strictly positive on every subset of \(V\). Its coefficients are computable with polynomially many rational operations and have polynomial bit length.

2. A nonnegative directed-cut representation

We now prove the representation needed for the approximation.

Choose an arbitrary ordering of \(V\). Construct a directed network with source \(s_0\), sink \(t_0\), and one vertex for each \(e\in V\). For every \(e<f\), add an arc \(e\to f\) of capacity \(w_{ef}\). Define

\(k_e=h_e-\sum_{f>e}w_{ef}.\)

If \(k_e\geq0\), add an arc \(e\to t_0\) of capacity \(k_e\). If \(k_e<0\), add an arc \(s_0\to e\) of capacity \(-k_e\). All capacities are nonnegative.

For \(B\subseteq V\), let \(\operatorname{cap}(B)\) be the capacity of the cut whose source side is \(\{s_0\}\cup B\). An internal arc contributes \(w_{ef}x_e(1-x_f)\). The terminal arcs contribute \(k_ex_e\) when \(k_e\geq0\), and \(-k_e(1-x_e)\) when \(k_e<0\). Therefore

\(F(B)=K+\operatorname{cap}(B),\qquad K=C+\sum_{e:k_e<0}k_e.\)

Compute a maximum flow of value \(\nu\), and form its residual network. For every source–sink cut, its residual capacity equals its original capacity minus \(\nu\). To check this identity, subtract the flow on outgoing cut arcs and add the flow on incoming cut arcs; their difference is exactly \(\nu\) by flow conservation.

The max-flow/min-cut theorem consequently gives

\(\gamma:=K+\nu=\min_{B\subseteq V}F(B)>0.\)

Let \(p_e\) be the residual capacity from \(e\) to \(t_0\), let \(q_e\) be the residual capacity from \(s_0\) to \(e\), and let \(d_{ef}\) be the residual capacity from \(e\) to \(f\), for distinct \(e,f\in V\). Missing arcs have capacity zero.

Residual arcs into the source or out of the sink never cross these cuts. There is no direct source-to-sink arc in this construction. Thus, for every indicator vector,

\(F(x)=\gamma+\sum_ep_ex_e+\sum_eq_e(1-x_e)+\sum_{e\neq f}d_{ef}x_e(1-x_f).\)

Every coefficient in this representation is nonnegative. This is the crucial consequence of positivity on all subsets, together with quadratic submodularity.

3. Choosing two candidates per channel

Define the affine surrogate

\(\mathcal L(x)=\gamma+\sum_ep_ex_e+\sum_eq_e(1-x_e)+\sum_{e\neq f}d_{ef}x_e.\)

For every indicator vector, deleting the factors \(1-x_f\) can only increase the value, so

\(\mathcal L(x)\geq F(x)>0.\)

Consider the fractional domain

\(\mathcal P=\left\{x:\sum_{e\in V_l}x_e=1\text{ for every remaining channel }l,\quad 0\leq x_e\leq\frac12\right\}.\)

It is nonempty because every remaining block has at least two elements. Write

\(\mathcal L(x)=\gamma+\sum_eq_e+\sum_e\sigma_ex_e,\qquad \sigma_e=p_e-q_e+\sum_{f\neq e}d_{ef}.\)

A maximizer \(\widehat x\) over \(\mathcal P\) is obtained by taking the two actions with largest \(\sigma_e\) in each block and assigning each probability \(\frac12\).

For completeness, if a block’s scores are ordered as \(\sigma_1\geq\sigma_2\geq\cdots\), every feasible vector in that block satisfies

\(\sum_i\sigma_ix_i\leq\sigma_1x_1+\sigma_2(1-x_1)\leq\frac12(\sigma_1+\sigma_2).\)

The proposed choice attains this bound.

Let \(B^\star\) maximize \(F\) over feasible selections of the remaining channels, and put \(\mathrm{OPT}=F(B^\star)\). Choose a feasible selection \(D\) disjoint from \(B^\star\), by choosing a different action in each block. Then

\(y=\frac12\mathbf1_{B^\star}+\frac12\mathbf1_D\)

belongs to \(\mathcal P\). Since \(\mathcal L\) is affine,

\(\mathcal L(\widehat x)\geq\mathcal L(y)=\frac12\mathcal L(\mathbf1_{B^\star})+\frac12\mathcal L(\mathbf1_D)\geq\frac12\mathrm{OPT}.\)

The last inequality uses \(\mathcal L(\mathbf1_{B^\star})\geq F(B^\star)\) and \(\mathcal L(\mathbf1_D)>0\). Neither \(B^\star\) nor \(D\) is needed by the algorithm.

4. Rounding and the approximation bound

For the analysis, independently choose one of the two selected candidates in every remaining channel, each with probability \(\frac12\). Let \(R\) denote the resulting feasible selection. Its marginals are \(\widehat x_e\).

For actions in different channel blocks, independence gives

\(\mathbb E[\mathbf1_R(e)(1-\mathbf1_R(f))]=\widehat x_e(1-\widehat x_f)\geq\frac12\widehat x_e,\)

because \(\widehat x_f\leq\frac12\).

For distinct actions in the same block, they cannot both be selected, so

\(\mathbb E[\mathbf1_R(e)(1-\mathbf1_R(f))]=\widehat x_e\geq\frac12\widehat x_e.\)

The constant and unary terms have exactly their corresponding values in \(\mathcal L(\widehat x)\). Since all these terms are nonnegative, the directed-cut representation yields

\(\mathbb E[F(R)]\geq\frac12\mathcal L(\widehat x)\geq\frac14\mathrm{OPT}.\)

We remove the randomness by conditional expectations. Process the remaining channels in any order. For each channel, compute the conditional expected objective for each of its two candidates, keeping the unprocessed choices independent and equiprobable. Fix whichever candidate gives the larger expectation. The expectation before fixing the channel is the average of these two conditional expectations, so it never decreases.

After all channels are fixed, the resulting deterministic feasible selection \(\widehat B\) satisfies

\(F(\widehat B)\geq\mathbb E[F(R)]\geq\frac14\mathrm{OPT}.\)

Returning \(A=M_0\cup\widehat B\) therefore gives

\(f_Z(A)\geq\frac14\max_{A'\text{ feasible}}f_Z(A')\geq\frac16\max_{A'\text{ feasible}}f_Z(A').\)

The auxiliary probability distribution is only a proof device. The returned strategy selects exactly one action per channel and broadcasts it to every type, without randomization or splitting masses.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

The algorithm consists of exact preprocessing, one maximum-flow computation, selecting two candidates per remaining channel, and conditional-expectation rounding.

The network has at most \(r+2\) vertices and \(O(r^2)\) arcs. Its rational capacities have polynomial bit length. Clearing their denominators produces integer capacities with polynomial bit length; a polynomial-time maximum-flow algorithm therefore computes the required flow exactly.

Each conditional expectation is computed from the quadratic representation using unary and pairwise probabilities. Different unprocessed channels remain independent, while distinct actions in one channel have joint selection probability zero. Thus each expectation requires \(O(r^2)\) rational operations, and there are at most \(2m\) candidate evaluations.

All intermediate numbers have polynomial bit length. There is no expansion of a type into individual agents, and no iteration count depending on the numerical size of a positive objective gap. The total running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,r,L\).

6. Relation to the cited results

The general theorem supplies a \((\frac16-\varepsilon)\)-approximation for nonnegative submodular maximization over matroid bases when two disjoint bases exist. [Lee–Mirrokni–Nagarajan–Sviridenko, Theorem 22](https://thibaut.horel.org/submodularity/papers/lee2009.pdf). That guarantee alone does not give the exact factor requested here.

The proof above instead uses the explicitly available quadratic coefficients and proves the stronger exact factor \(\frac14\). It consequently does not require repairing the reversed inequality displayed in Theorem 3 of the finite-agent paper. [Finite-agent paper](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0001.pdf).
