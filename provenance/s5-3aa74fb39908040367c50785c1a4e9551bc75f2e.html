Theorem 11 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite left and right type sets \(I,J\), rational masses \(a_i,b_j\) summing to one on each side, pair costs \(c_{ij},d_{ji}\), outside-option costs \(u_i,v_j\), and finitely many scenarios \(s\) with probabilities \(q_s\) and surviving fractions \(\alpha_i^s,\beta_j^s\), compute an exact minimum-cost mass matching \(x\) and its value. The constraints are \(x_{ij}\geq 0\), \(\sum_j x_{ij}\leq a_i\), and \(\sum_i x_{ij}\leq b_j\). Minimize \(\sum_s q_s[\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}(\alpha_i^s\beta_j^s(c_{ij}^2+d_{ji}^2)+\alpha_i^s(1-\beta_j^s)u_i^2+(1-\alpha_i^s)\beta_j^s v_j^2)+\sum_i(a_i-\sum_jx_{ij})\alpha_i^s u_i^2+\sum_j(b_j-\sum_ix_{ij})\beta_j^s v_j^2]\). Determine an exact algorithm polynomial in \(|I|+|J|\), the number of scenarios, and the rational input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be nonempty finite sets of left and right types. Each society is given by its support: its list of types with positive binary-rational masses \(a_i,b_j\), with \(\sum_i a_i=\sum_j b_j=1\). Put \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) and \(m=\max\{|I|,|J|\}\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Pair costs \(c_{ij},d_{ji}\) and unmatched costs \(u_i,v_j\) are nonnegative binary rationals. For each left type, its pair costs and unmatched cost are pairwise distinct, and likewise for each right type. All cross-side pairs are allowed; stability is not required.
There are \(S\geq1\) explicitly listed scenarios, with binary-rational probabilities \(q_s\geq0\), \(\sum_s q_s=1\), and surviving fractions \(\alpha_i^s,\beta_j^s\in[0,1]\). Within each scenario, paired mass splits according to the products of the endpoint survival fractions. A survivor whose partner leaves becomes unmatched; no rematching occurs.
A mass matching is a real matrix \(x\) satisfying \(x_{ij}\geq0\), \(\sum_jx_{ij}\leq a_i\), and \(\sum_ix_{ij}\leq b_j\). Its expected sum of squared individual costs is
\(F(x)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}q_s\left[\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}\left(\alpha_i^s\beta_j^s(c_{ij}^2+d_{ji}^2)+\alpha_i^s(1-\beta_j^s)u_i^2+(1-\alpha_i^s)\beta_j^s v_j^2\right)+\sum_i\left(a_i-\sum_jx_{ij}\right)\alpha_i^s u_i^2+\sum_j\left(b_j-\sum_ix_{ij}\right)\beta_j^s v_j^2\right]\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. Any minimizing matching is acceptable when the optimum is attained by several matchings.
Is computing an exact minimizer of \(F\) and its value polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,S,L\), or is this problem NP-hard?
For discrete societies with \(n\) agents on each side and at most one departing agent, relaxed perturbation-robust matching admits an \(\mathcal{O}(n^4)\)-time algorithm, including the pure expected squared-cost objective used here. No prior result for this precise continuous formulation has been verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The graded question leaves cost signs and preference ties unspecified; costs are nonnegative binary rationals and each type strictly orders all opposite-side types and its unmatched option, as in the discrete model.
  • Scenario probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one; surviving fractions lie in the closed unit interval, and all scenario data are binary rationals.
  • The objective specializes the robustness parameter to its pure social-cost case, with no regret term.
  • The survival products specify the split of paired mass within each scenario; surviving endpoints of broken pairs remain unmatched without rematching.
  • Both supports are nonempty and contain only positive masses. Define the combined support size as \(\tau\), the maximum side support size as \(m\), and total binary input length as \(L\).
  • No tie-breaking among optimal matchings is required; any exact minimizer may be returned.

1. Result

An exact minimizer and its value can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,S,L\). The objective is affine in the matching masses and reduces exactly to a minimum-cost transportation problem.

The proof below uses a standard polynomial-time minimum-cost flow theorem; it does not require a prior result for this continuous matching formulation.

2. Simplifying the objective

Define the expected joint-survival coefficient

\[ p_{ij}:=\sum_{s=1}^{S}q_s\alpha_i^s\beta_j^s, \]

the constant

\[ K:=\sum_{s=1}^{S}q_s\left(\sum_i a_i\alpha_i^s u_i^2+\sum_j b_j\beta_j^s v_j^2\right), \]

and the pair coefficient

\[ w_{ij}:=p_{ij}\left(c_{ij}^2+d_{ji}^2-u_i^2-v_j^2\right). \]

Here and below, all these numbers are computed exactly as rationals.

To verify the simplification, fix a scenario and a pair, and abbreviate its survival fractions by \(\alpha,\beta\). Expanding the initially unmatched terms, the coefficient of \(x_{ij}\) in that scenario becomes

\[ \begin{aligned} &\alpha\beta(c_{ij}^2+d_{ji}^2) +\alpha(1-\beta)u_i^2+(1-\alpha)\beta v_j^2 -\alpha u_i^2-\beta v_j^2\\ &=\alpha\beta(c_{ij}^2+d_{ji}^2-u_i^2-v_j^2). \end{aligned} \]

Summing over scenarios therefore gives the identity

\[ F(x)=K+\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}x_{ij}. \]

Thus the squares in the objective are squares of fixed input costs. The optimization variables occur only linearly.

The feasible set is nonempty because it contains the zero matrix. It is closed and bounded: every entry satisfies \(0\leq x_{ij}\leq\min(a_i,b_j)\). Consequently, the continuous objective attains its minimum.

3. Exact transportation reduction

Introduce one dummy left type \(i_0\) and one dummy right type \(j_0\). Give the augmented left types supplies

\[ A_i=a_i\quad(i\in I),\qquad A_{i_0}=1, \]

and the augmented right types demands

\[ B_j=b_j\quad(j\in J),\qquad B_{j_0}=1. \]

Both total supply and total demand equal \(2\). Allow every augmented left-right arc. Its cost is \(w_{ij}\) when both endpoints are original types and zero otherwise.

A feasible transportation matrix \(z\) must be nonnegative and have row sums \(A\) and column sums \(B\).

For any feasible matching \(x\), write \(t:=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}\) and set

\[ \begin{aligned} z_{ij}&=x_{ij},\\ z_{i j_0}&=a_i-\sum_jx_{ij},\\ z_{i_0 j}&=b_j-\sum_ix_{ij},\\ z_{i_0j_0}&=t. \end{aligned} \]

Every entry is nonnegative. The original rows and columns have the required sums by construction. The dummy row has sum

\[ \sum_j\left(b_j-\sum_i x_{ij}\right)+t=1, \]

and the dummy column has sum \(1\) by the same calculation.

Conversely, restricting any feasible \(z\) to its original-original entries gives a feasible matching: the omitted dummy entries are nonnegative, so the original row and column sums satisfy the matching inequalities.

Finally, the transportation cost is exactly

\[ \sum_{i,j}w_{ij}z_{ij}=\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}x_{ij}=F(x)-K. \]

The two optimization problems therefore have corresponding minimizers.

4. Bit lengths and exact rational output

Write each input rational as an integer numerator divided by a positive integer denominator. Let \(D\) be the product of all these denominators. Under the given binary encoding,

\[ \log_2 D\leq L. \]

Every input denominator divides \(D\). Hence \(Da_i\) and \(Db_j\) are integers.

Each summand defining \(w_{ij}\) is a product of a scenario probability, two survival fractions, and a squared cost. Its denominator therefore divides \(D^5\). The same holds for each summand defining \(K\). It follows that

\[ W_{ij}:=D^5w_{ij} \]

is an integer, and \(D^5K\) is an integer.

Scale transportation masses by setting \(y:=Dz\). The resulting instance has integer supplies \(DA\), integer demands \(DB\), and integer costs \(W\), with zero costs on dummy arcs. Its objective satisfies

\[ F(x)=K+\frac{1}{D^6}\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}y_{ij}. \]

All scaled supplies are at most \(D\). We may therefore put capacity \(D\) on every arc without excluding any feasible transportation matrix.

These integers have polynomial bit length. Indeed, \(D\) has at most \(L+1\) bits; input cost magnitudes have at most \(O(L)\) bits; squaring costs and multiplying by \(D^5\) still gives \(O(L)\)-bit integers. Summation adds at most a polynomial number of bits. Computing all coefficients takes \(O(S|I||J|+S\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations on numbers of polynomial bit length.

For completeness, the scaled transportation problem has an integral optimal solution. Choose an optimum with the fewest positive entries. If its positive-support bipartite graph contained a cycle, alternately increasing and decreasing its cycle entries by a sufficiently small amount would preserve all balances in either direction. Optimality forces the cost change along this direction to be zero; otherwise one direction would improve the objective. Increasing the perturbation until one decreased entry becomes zero would then produce another optimum with fewer positive entries, a contradiction.

Thus this support is a forest. At a leaf, its sole incident flow equals the leaf’s remaining supply or demand, which is integral. Removing that edge and updating the other endpoint’s balance preserves integrality. Induction proves that every positive entry is integral.

Consequently, an exact optimum can be represented with

\[ x_{ij}=\frac{y_{ij}}{D},\qquad 0\leq y_{ij}\leq D, \]

and its value has denominator dividing \(D^6\). Both requested outputs have polynomial encoding length.

5. Polynomial-time algorithm and conclusion

Use the standard minimum-cost flow theorem: a feasible finite directed network with binary-encoded integer supplies, demands, capacities, and costs admits computation of an exact optimal integral flow in polynomial bit time. For example, Orlin’s strongly polynomial algorithm supplies such an exact flow algorithm. [Orlin, *A Faster Strongly Polynomial Minimum Cost Flow Algorithm* (1993)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.41.2.338).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • The graph has \(\tau+2\) vertices and \((|I|+1)(|J|+1)\leq(m+1)^2\) arcs.
  • Supplies and demands balance.
  • Feasibility follows from the transportation extension of \(x=0\).
  • Every capacity, balance, and cost is an integer of polynomial bit length.
  • Finite capacities make the objective bounded.

Signed costs cause no difficulty. If nonnegative costs are desired, add \(H:=\max(0,-\min W)\) to every augmented arc cost. Every feasible transportation matrix sends total mass \(2D\), so this adds the same constant \(2DH\) to every objective and preserves all minimizers.

The algorithm is therefore:

  • Compute \(K\) and all \(w_{ij}\) exactly.
  • Construct and scale the augmented transportation instance.
  • Compute an exact optimal integral flow \(y^*\).
  • Return \(x^*_{ij}=y^*_{ij}/D\) on original pairs and evaluate its value using the formula above.

Every step takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,S,L\). Pairwise distinctness of the individual costs is unnecessary for this argument, and multiple optimal matchings require no additional treatment.
