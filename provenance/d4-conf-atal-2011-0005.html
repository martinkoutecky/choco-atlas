The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a finite-support rational mass measure \(\rho\) on \(\mathbb R\), with total mass \(M\ge2\) and every positive type mass at least \(1\), apply the left-right-middle lottery to the support endpoints and their midpoint with probabilities \(1/4,1/4,1/2\). Against deviations in which one resident replaces their truthful unit report by any finite set of unit identity reports, is this mechanism false-name-proof, and is its exact worst-case ratio of expected social cost to the optimum weighted social cost \(M/2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\rho=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}w_j\delta_{x_j}\), with distinct locations \(x_j\in\mathbb R\), positive binary-rational masses \(w_j\ge1\), and total mass \(M=\sum_jw_j\ge2\), without normalization. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
For computational claims, locations are also encoded as binary rationals; state running times in \(\tau\), total input bit length \(L\), and any additional parameters. There is no candidate-count parameter \(m\).
For every nonzero finite positive report measure \(\mu\), let \(F(\mu)\) select its support minimum, support maximum, and their midpoint with probabilities \(1/4,1/4,1/2\), respectively; coincident outcomes have their probabilities combined.
A resident at \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\rho\) may replace one truthful unit report by a nonempty finite multiset of unit reports at arbitrary real locations \(z_1,\ldots,z_k\), producing \(\rho'=\rho-\delta_x+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\delta_{z_\ell}\). Reports need not include \(x\); the remaining report measure stays fixed, and \(\rho'\) need not satisfy the truthful mass restrictions.
False-name-proofness means \(\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\rho)}|x-Y|\le\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\rho')}|x-Y|\) for every such deviation. Define \(C_\rho(y)=\sum_jw_j|x_j-y|\), \(\operatorname{OPT}(\rho)=\min_{y\in\mathbb R}C_\rho(y)\), and \(R(M)=\sup_{\rho:\,\rho(\mathbb R)=M,\,\operatorname{OPT}(\rho)>0}\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\rho)}C_\rho(Y)/\operatorname{OPT}(\rho)\), over the specified truthful domain.
Is \(F\) false-name-proof on this mass domain, and is \(R(M)=M/2\) for every rational \(M\ge2\)?
For finite societies of \(n\ge2\) unit agents, left-right-middle is false-name-proof and has exact worst-case social-cost ratio \(n/2\); every randomized false-name-proof mechanism has social-cost ratio \(\Omega(n)\). [Source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2011/papers/B5_G62.pdf)
No result settling this precise rational-mass model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted a finite set of identities as a nonempty multiset of locations: distinct identities may report the same location, and all reports may misstate the resident's location. Empty deviations are excluded, consistent with treating participation separately.
  • Defined a resident's deviation by removing exactly one unit of mass and keeping all remaining reports fixed; extended the lottery to every nonzero finite positive report measure because deviations can leave residual type masses below one.
  • Defined the worst-case ratio separately for each fixed total mass M and excluded zero-optimum societies from the quotient; singleton-support societies remain in the incentive domain.
  • Retained real locations for the mathematical question and specified binary-rational locations for computational claims so that the input bit length is defined.

Result and encoding convention

Both statements hold: \(F\) is false-name-proof, and \(R(M)=M/2\) for every rational \(M\ge 2\). The supremum is attained.

Here “binary-rational” means a rational represented by binary-encoded numerator and denominator, consistently with the question’s quantifier over every rational total mass. If it instead means a dyadic rational, the result holds for every admissible dyadic total mass; other total masses have no admissible societies.

The unit-agent analogue is Theorem 6 of [Todo, Iwasaki, and Yokoo](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2011/papers/B5_G62.pdf). We prove the stated mass version directly.

1. An endpoint-cost lemma

For real numbers \(u\le v\), define the expected distance of a resident at \(x\) under the endpoint-and-midpoint lottery by

\(H_x(u,v)=\frac14|x-u|+\frac14|x-v|+\frac12\left|x-\frac{u+v}{2}\right|=\frac{|x-u|+|x-v|+|2x-u-v|}{4}.\)

This formula remains valid when \(u=v\), since coincident outcomes have their probabilities combined.

The reverse triangle inequality gives

\(|2x-u-v|=|(x-u)+(x-v)|\ge \bigl||x-u|-|x-v|\bigr|.\)

Consequently,

\(H_x(u,v)\ge \frac{|x-u|+|x-v|+\bigl||x-u|-|x-v|\bigr|}{4}=\frac12\max\{|x-u|,|x-v|\}.\)

If \(x\in[u,v]\), the two quantities \(x-u\) and \(x-v\) have opposite signs, so this inequality is an equality:

\(H_x(u,v)=\frac12\max\{x-u,v-x\}.\)

Furthermore, for every \(t\in[u,v]\),

\(|x-t|\le \max\{|x-u|,|x-v|\}.\)

Indeed, when \(u<v\), write \(t=(1-\lambda)u+\lambda v\), where \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), and apply the triangle inequality. When \(u=v\), the assertion is immediate. Thus we have proved

\(H_x(u,v)\ge \frac12|x-t|\qquad\text{for every }t\in[u,v].\)

2. False-name-proofness

Fix an admissible society \(\rho\) and a resident location \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\rho\). Since the mass at \(x\) is at least one, \(\rho-\delta_x\) is a nonnegative measure. Every allowed deviation produces a nonzero measure with finite support.

If the truthful support has only one location, the resident’s truthful expected distance is zero. No deviation can improve upon zero.

Now suppose the truthful support has at least two locations. Let

\(a=\min\operatorname{supp}\rho,\qquad b=\max\operatorname{supp}\rho.\)

Choose \(t\in\{a,b\}\) satisfying

\(|x-t|=\max\{x-a,b-x\}.\)

Because \(a<b\), this maximum is positive, so \(t\ne x\). Removing one unit at \(x\) therefore leaves the entire positive mass at \(t\) unchanged.

Consider any allowed deviation

\(\rho'=\rho-\delta_x+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\delta_{z_\ell},\qquad k\ge 1,\)

and denote its support endpoints by \(A\le B\). The unchanged mass at \(t\) implies \(t\in[A,B]\). Applying the lemma to the deviating lottery, and its equality formula to the truthful lottery, gives

\(\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\rho')}|x-Y|=H_x(A,B)\ge \frac12|x-t|=H_x(a,b)=\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\rho)}|x-Y|.\)

This applies to every finite collection of real reports, including deviations that omit \(x\). Hence \(F\) is false-name-proof on the stated domain.

3. Exact worst-case social-cost ratio

Universal upper bound

Fix an admissible society with \(\operatorname{OPT}(\rho)>0\). Its support has at least two locations. Write

\(a=\min\operatorname{supp}\rho,\qquad b=\max\operatorname{supp}\rho,\qquad D=b-a>0,\qquad c=\frac{a+b}{2}.\)

An optimum exists: projection onto \([a,b]\) weakly decreases every distance to a support location, and \(C_\rho\) is continuous on this compact interval.

For every support location \(x_j\in[a,b]\),

\(|x_j-a|+|x_j-b|=D,\qquad |x_j-c|\le D/2.\)

Multiplying by the masses and summing yields

\(C_\rho(a)+C_\rho(b)=MD,\qquad C_\rho(c)\le MD/2.\)

Therefore,

\(\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\rho)}C_\rho(Y)=\frac14\bigl(C_\rho(a)+C_\rho(b)\bigr)+\frac12C_\rho(c)\le MD/2.\)

Let \(w_a,w_b\) denote the masses at the two endpoints. The truthful-domain restriction gives \(w_a,w_b\ge 1\). For every real \(y\),

\(C_\rho(y)\ge w_a|a-y|+w_b|b-y|\ge |a-y|+|b-y|\ge D,\)

where the final inequality is the triangle inequality. Taking the minimum gives \(\operatorname{OPT}(\rho)\ge D\). Thus

\(\frac{\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\rho)}C_\rho(Y)}{\operatorname{OPT}(\rho)}\le \frac{MD/2}{D}=M/2.\)

Attainment for every rational total mass

For any rational \(M\ge 2\), consider

\(\rho_M=(M-1)\delta_0+\delta_1.\)

Both masses are rational and at least one, and the total mass is \(M\). The endpoint lower bound just proved gives \(\operatorname{OPT}(\rho_M)\ge 1\), while \(C_{\rho_M}(0)=1\). Hence \(\operatorname{OPT}(\rho_M)=1\).

The three outcome costs are

\(C_{\rho_M}(0)=1,\qquad C_{\rho_M}(1)=M-1,\qquad C_{\rho_M}(1/2)=M/2.\)

Consequently,

\(\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\rho_M)}C_{\rho_M}(Y)=\frac14+\frac{M-1}{4}+\frac12\frac M2=M/2.\)

This admissible two-location society attains the upper bound. Therefore \(R(M)=M/2\) for every rational \(M\ge 2\).

4. Computational bounds

For a truthful input of support size \(\tau\) and total bit length \(L\), computing \(F(\rho)\) requires scanning the locations for their minimum and maximum, then computing their midpoint. This uses \(O(\tau)\) rational comparisons and \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations with schoolbook arithmetic.

Given a binary-encoded rational \(M\) of bit length \(B\), both \(R(M)=M/2\) and the attaining society \((M-1)\delta_0+\delta_1\) can be constructed in \(O(B^2)\) bit operations, with output length \(O(B)\). No population expansion is required.
