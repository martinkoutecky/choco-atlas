Definitions. Let \(T(C)\) be the strict ranking types over \(C\). The input explicitly gives binary-encoded rational vectors \((\mu_t)_{t\in T(C)}\) and \(( u_t)_{t\in T(C)}\), and \(\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\). Choose \(x_{t,t'}\ge0\) with \(\sum_{t'}x_{t,t'}\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_{t,t'}\le u_{t'}\), and \(\sum_{t,t'}x_{t,t'}\le\lambda\), yielding \(\mu'_u=\mu_u-\sum_{t'}x_{u,t'}+\sum_t x_{t,u}\).

For \(a,b\in C\), let \(D(a,b)=\sum_{t:a\succ_t b}\mu'_t-\sum_{t:b\succ_t a}\mu'_t\). Define \(P(a,b)\) as the maximum, over directed \(a\)-to-\(b\) paths, of the minimum \(D\)-value on the path. A Schulze winner \(a\) satisfies \(P(a,b)\ge P(b,a)\) for every \(b e a\); it is unique if no other candidate does.

Constructive Control by Replacing Voter Mass for Schulze Elections

Theorem

Assume that, as usual for constructive control, the input contains a distinguished candidate \(p\in C\). Under the stated explicit encoding of all \(m!=|T(C)|\) ranking types, constructive control by replacing voter mass for Schulze elections is solvable in polynomial time in both:

  • the nonunique-winner model, and
  • the unique-winner model.

Here \(m=|C|\). The polynomial-time bound is measured in the full input length, which is at least \(m!\).

1. Eliminating the transportation variables

For every type \(t\), define its removed and inserted masses by

\[ d_t=\sum_{t'}x_{t,t'} \qquad\text{and}\qquad e_t=\sum_{t'}x_{t',t}. \]

Every feasible replacement therefore satisfies

\[ 0\le d_t\le\mu_t,\qquad 0\le e_t\le\nu_t,\qquad \sum_t d_t=\sum_t e_t,\qquad \sum_t d_t\le\lambda. \]

Conversely, suppose rational vectors \(d,e\) satisfy these constraints. Since their total masses agree, there exists a nonnegative transportation matrix \(x\) having row sums \(d_t\) and column sums \(e_t\). This follows, for example, by repeatedly sending the minimum of a positive remaining row demand and a positive remaining column demand. The resulting matrix automatically satisfies all the original row, column, and budget constraints.

Thus the feasible final electorate vectors are exactly

\[ \widehat\mu_t=\mu_t-d_t+e_t \]

where \(d,e\) satisfy the displayed linear constraints.

For distinct \(u,v\in C\), let

\[ \sigma_t(u,v)= \begin{cases} 1,&u\succ_t v,\\ -1,&v\succ_t u. \end{cases} \]

The resulting margin is the affine-linear expression

\[ w_{uv}=D(u,v) =\sum_{t\in T(C)} \sigma_t(u,v)(\mu_t-d_t+e_t). \]

Consequently, every constraint comparing margins is a rational linear constraint in \(d,e\).

2. A polynomially enumerable certificate for all widest paths from one root

Consider an arbitrary complete directed graph on \(C\) with real arc weights \(w_{uv}\). For a fixed root \(r\), write

\[ S_r(v)=\max_{\pi:r\leadsto v}\min_{a\in\pi}w_a. \]

It suffices to consider simple paths: deleting a directed cycle from a walk cannot decrease its minimum arc weight. Hence the maximum is attained.

Widest-path certificate lemma

The complete vector \((S_r(v))_{v\ne r}\) can be certified by the following combinatorial data:

  • an ordered partition

\[ (B_1,\ldots,B_k) \]

of \(C\setminus\{r\}\), and

  • a directed spanning arborescence rooted at \(r\), with every tree arc directed away from \(r\).

Associate a real level \(L_i\) with block \(B_i\), subject to

\[ L_1>L_2>\cdots>L_k. \]

For \(v\in B_i\), put \(\ell_v=L_i\). Let \(\operatorname{par}(v)\) be the parent of \(v\) in the arborescence. Impose the following constraints.

First, for every \(v\ne r\),

\[ w_{\operatorname{par}(v),v}\ge \ell_v, \]

and, whenever \(\operatorname{par}(v)\ne r\),

\[ \ell_{\operatorname{par}(v)}\ge \ell_v. \]

Second, for every block \(B_i\), define

\[ R_i=\{r\}\cup B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_{i-1}. \]

For every arc leaving \(R_i\), impose

\[ w_{uv}\le L_i \qquad \text{for all }u\in R_i,\ v\notin R_i. \]

Then these constraints hold if and only if

\[ \ell_v=S_r(v) \qquad \text{for every }v\ne r. \]

Proof: soundness

Fix \(v\in B_i\). On the tree path from \(r\) to \(v\), the level labels are nonincreasing. Every tree arc entering a vertex \(z\) has weight at least \(\ell_z\), and every such \(\ell_z\) is at least \(\ell_v=L_i\). Hence the tree path has strength at least \(L_i\), proving

\[ S_r(v)\ge L_i. \]

On the other hand, \(r\in R_i\) while \(v\notin R_i\). Every directed path from \(r\) to \(v\) must contain an arc leaving \(R_i\). Every such arc has weight at most \(L_i\). Therefore every \(r\)-to-\(v\) path has strength at most \(L_i\), so

\[ S_r(v)\le L_i. \]

Thus \(S_r(v)=L_i=\ell_v\).

Proof: completeness

Now set \(\ell_v=S_r(v)\). Partition \(C\setminus\{r\}\) into equal-strength blocks, ordered by strictly decreasing strength. Let their distinct strengths be

\[ L_1>L_2>\cdots>L_k. \]

For a fixed \(i\), the vertices reachable from \(r\) using only arcs of weight at least \(L_i\) are exactly

\[ \{r\}\cup B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_i. \]

Indeed, every vertex in this set has widest-path strength at least \(L_i\), while any reachable vertex has widest-path strength at least \(L_i\).

Starting with \(r\), process the blocks in order. When processing \(B_i\), perform a directed search using arcs of weight at least \(L_i\), treating all previously processed vertices as already reached. Choose a discovery arc for each vertex of \(B_i\). Together with the previously chosen arcs, these form an arborescence rooted at \(r\). Every selected arc entering \(B_i\) has weight at least \(L_i\), and its parent lies either in an earlier block or in \(B_i\). Hence all tree constraints hold.

It remains to verify the cut constraints. Suppose that \(u\in R_i\), \(v\notin R_i\), and \(w_{uv}>L_i\). If \(u=r\), the single arc \(r\to v\) would give \(S_r(v)>L_i\). Otherwise, \(S_r(u)>L_i\), so concatenating a path to \(u\) of strength greater than \(L_i\) with the arc \(u\to v\) again yields \(S_r(v)>L_i\). Both conclusions contradict \(v\notin R_i\). Therefore every arc leaving \(R_i\) has weight at most \(L_i\).

This proves the lemma.

3. Applying the certificate to the Schulze comparisons

Fix the distinguished candidate \(p\).

Apply the certificate lemma once to the original margin graph rooted at \(p\). Denote the certified levels by

\[ \alpha_b=P(p,b). \]

Apply it a second time to the transposed graph, whose arc \(u\to v\) has weight \(w_{vu}\). A \(p\)-to-\(b\) path in the transposed graph is exactly the reversal of a \(b\)-to-\(p\) path in the original graph. Therefore the second certificate gives levels

\[ \beta_b=P(b,p). \]

For any fixed pair of certificates, all their conditions are rational linear equalities, weak inequalities, and strict inequalities in

\[ (d_t)_{t\in T(C)},\quad (e_t)_{t\in T(C)},\quad (\alpha_b)_{b\ne p},\quad (\beta_b)_{b\ne p}. \]

In the nonunique-winner model, add

\[ \alpha_b\ge\beta_b \qquad \text{for every }b\ne p. \]

By the definition of a Schulze winner, these constraints are necessary and sufficient for \(p\) to be a winner.

4. Characterization of a unique Schulze winner

Define the strict Schulze relation by

\[ a\mathrel{\triangleright}b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P(a,b)>P(b,a). \]

Lemma

The relation \(\triangleright\) is transitive.

Proof

Suppose \(a\triangleright b\) and \(b\triangleright c\). Put

\[ A=P(a,b),\qquad B=P(b,c),\qquad z=\min\{A,B\}. \]

Concatenating strongest paths shows

\[ P(a,c)\ge z. \]

Suppose for contradiction that \(P(c,a)\ge z\).

If \(A\le B\), concatenate a \(b\)-to-\(c\) path of strength \(B\) with a \(c\)-to-\(a\) path of strength at least \(A\). This gives

\[ P(b,a)\ge A, \]

contradicting \(P(a,b)>P(b,a)\).

If \(B\le A\), concatenate a \(c\)-to-\(a\) path of strength at least \(B\) with an \(a\)-to-\(b\) path of strength \(A\). This gives

\[ P(c,b)\ge B, \]

contradicting \(P(b,c)>P(c,b)\).

Thus \(P(c,a)<z\le P(a,c)\), proving \(a\triangleright c\).

Because \(\triangleright\) is an irreflexive transitive relation, its maximal elements are precisely the Schulze winners. In any finite strict partial order, a unique maximal element lies above every other element: starting from any nonmaximal element and repeatedly moving to a larger element must terminate at a maximal element.

It follows that \(p\) is the unique Schulze winner if and only if

\[ P(p,b)>P(b,p) \qquad \text{for every }b\ne p. \]

Thus, in the unique-winner model, it suffices to add the strict linear constraints

\[ \alpha_b>\beta_b \qquad \text{for every }b\ne p. \]

5. Deciding strict linear feasibility

For a fixed pair of certificates, collect every strict inequality, including the strict inequalities between consecutive block levels, in the form

\[ g_j(z)>0. \]

Introduce a variable \(\delta\) and replace them with

\[ g_j(z)\ge\delta,\qquad 0\le\delta\le1. \]

Maximize \(\delta\) subject to all weak constraints.

Let

\[ M=\sum_t\mu_t. \]

Replacement preserves total mass, so every margin and every genuine widest-path value belongs to \([-M,M]\). These bounds may be imposed explicitly on all level variables. The resulting linear program is bounded.

The original strict system is feasible if and only if the optimum satisfies \(\delta>0\). One direction is immediate. Conversely, at any point satisfying finitely many strict inequalities, the minimum of their positive slacks is positive and can be chosen as \(\delta\). Exact rational linear programming and exact comparison of its optimum with zero are polynomial-time operations.

If a certificate contains no strict inequalities, ordinary linear feasibility is sufficient.

6. Number of certificates

Let \(m=|C|\).

The number of ordered partitions of \(m-1\) labeled vertices is at most

\[ m^{m-1}, \]

because an ordered partition is encoded by assigning each vertex its block index.

The number of rooted directed arborescences is at most

\[ m^{m-2}. \]

Therefore the number of widest-path certificates for one root is less than

\[ m^{2m}. \]

We need one certificate for the original graph and one for the transposed graph, so fewer than

\[ m^{4m} \]

certificate pairs are considered.

The input explicitly contains one entry for every ranking type, so if

\[ N=|T(C)|=m!, \]

then the input length is at least \(N\). For \(m\ge4\),

\[ m!\ge\left(\frac m2\right)^{m/2}, \]

and consequently

\[ (m!)^{16} \ge \left(\frac m2\right)^{8m} \ge m^{4m}. \]

The finitely many cases \(m<4\) are constant-sized. Hence the number of certificate pairs is polynomial in the stated explicit input length.

Each associated linear program has \(O(N+m^2)\) variables and polynomial encoding length. The margin equations contain \(O(Nm^2)\) coefficients, and the certificate contributes only polynomially many additional constraints.

7. Conclusion

Enumerating all pairs of widest-path certificates and solving the corresponding rational linear programs decides whether the designated candidate can be made a Schulze winner. The same procedure, with strict winner comparisons, decides whether the candidate can be made the unique Schulze winner.

Therefore constructive control by replacing voter mass for Schulze elections is polynomial-time solvable in both winner models under the explicit full-type-vector encoding.

The known finite-list NP-completeness results do not transfer: the present formulation both permits fractional replacement and explicitly incurs the \(m!\)-sized type vector, making the \(m^{O(m)}\) certificate enumeration polynomial in the input length.
