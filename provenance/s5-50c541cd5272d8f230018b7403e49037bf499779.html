The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a choreography DAG, rational masses \(\mu_t\) of provider types with per-capita service capacities and costs, unit demand, and budget \(p\), let \(C(y)\) be the minimum cost of routing one unit of \(s\)-to-\(t\) flow using capacity supplied by coalition mass \(y\), and set \(v_p(y)=\max\{0,\min(p,C(\mu-y))-C(y)\}\). Under measurable positive-mass objections and counter-objections, compute an anonymous stable payoff density \(x_t\) for the type masses, or determine that none exists.

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) provider types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), normalized by \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Realize each type as an atomless interval \(\Omega_a\) of measure \(\mu_a\), and write \(\Omega=\bigsqcup_a\Omega_a\), with measure \(\lambda\).
The input includes a choreography DAG \(G=(V\cup\{s,t\},E)\), with \(m=|V|\), in which every \(s\)-to-\(t\) path contains at least two service vertices. Type \(a\) has independent per-capita capacities \(k_{aw}\geq0\) and per-unit operating costs \(c_{aw}\geq0\) at each \(w\in V\); these and the budget \(p>0\) are binary rationals. Arcs are free and uncapacitated; \(s,t\) are free.
For \(0\leq y\leq\mu\), let \(C(y)\) be the minimum cost of a divisible unit \(s\)-to-\(t\) flow: its throughput at \(w\) is supplied by quantities \(q_{aw}\) satisfying \(0\leq q_{aw}\leq k_{aw}y_a\), and its cost is \(\sum_{a,w}c_{aw}q_{aw}\). Set \(C(y)=+\infty\) when infeasible.
A measurable coalition \(P\subseteq\Omega\) has masses \(y_a(P)=\lambda(P\cap\Omega_a)\) and worth \(v(P)=\max\{0,\min\{p,C(\mu-y(P))\}-C(y(P))\}\), with worth zero when its own flow is infeasible. This worth uses supremal attainable profit, so equal competing costs yield zero profit.
An anonymous imputation is a density \(x(u)=x_a\geq0\) on \(\Omega_a\), satisfying \(\sum_a\mu_ax_a=v(\Omega)\).

Result

An anonymous stable imputation can be computed, or its nonexistence determined, in polynomial time in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

The proof uses the objection definition exactly as given, including the possibility \(I=P\) and \(J=\Omega\setminus P\). Under this definition, stability is equivalent to membership in the core. Convexity of the divisible-flow cost then yields a polynomial-size linear program.

1. Basic properties and the zero-worth case

Write \(W(y)\) for the worth of any coalition with mass vector \(y\), and write \(x\cdot y=\sum_a x_a y_a\). Every vector satisfying \(0\leq y\leq\mu\) is realized by a measurable coalition: choose a subinterval of length \(y_a\) in each \(\Omega_a\).

Increasing \(y\) increases every capacity bound. Consequently,

\(C(y')\leq C(y)\quad\text{whenever }y\leq y'\leq\mu.\)

Also, \(C(0)=+\infty\): zero provider capacity cannot supply a unit flow through service vertices.

Let \(T=v(\Omega)\). If the grand-coalition flow is infeasible, every coalition's flow is infeasible, so all worths vanish. If \(C(\mu)\geq p\), monotonicity likewise gives zero worth to every coalition. In either case, the zero density is stable: an objection would require a strictly positive integral while having zero available worth.

Henceforth assume

\(C_0:=C(\mu)<p,\qquad T=p-C_0>0.\)

For every coalition \(P\),

\(0\leq v(P)\leq T.\)

Indeed, if \(v(P)>0\), its own flow is feasible and

\(v(P)\leq p-C(y(P))\leq p-C_0=T.\)

We also have the complementary-worth property

\(v(P)>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v(\Omega\setminus P)=0.\)

To verify it, positive worth implies
\(C(y(P))<\min\{p,C(\mu-y(P))\}\).
If the complement is infeasible, its worth is zero by definition. Otherwise its cost strictly exceeds \(C(y(P))\), so its own profit expression is negative.

2. Stability is equivalent to the core inequalities

For an anonymous imputation \(x\), define
\(X(P)=\int_P x\,d\lambda=x\cdot y(P)\).
The core inequalities are

\(X(P)\geq v(P)\qquad\text{for every measurable }P.\)

Proposition. An anonymous imputation is stable if and only if it satisfies these inequalities.

Proof. Suppose first that all core inequalities hold. An objection would satisfy \(h-x\geq0\) almost everywhere on \(P\), with \(h-x>0\) almost everywhere on a positive-measure set \(I\). Because this difference is integrable, its integral is strictly positive. Thus

\(\int_P h\,d\lambda>X(P)\geq v(P),\)

contradicting the objection's budget constraint. There are therefore no objections.

Conversely, suppose \(v(P)>X(P)\). Then \(v(P)>0\), and efficiency together with \(v(P)\leq T\) gives

\(X(\Omega\setminus P)=T-X(P)>T-v(P)\geq0.\)

Both \(P\) and its complement have positive measure. Set

\(\varepsilon=\dfrac{v(P)-X(P)}{2\lambda(P)},\qquad h=x+\varepsilon\ \text{on }P,\qquad I=P,\qquad J=\Omega\setminus P.\)

This is an objection: it strictly improves everyone in \(P\), and its total payment is below \(v(P)\).

Any counter-objection coalition must contain the whole complement and avoid the whole of \(P\). Therefore \(Q=\Omega\setminus P\), modulo null sets. Its worth is zero by the complementary-worth property. But its required payment satisfies

\(\int_Q z\,d\lambda\geq X(\Omega\setminus P)>0.\)

No counter-objection exists. Hence \(x\) is unstable. This proves the proposition.

This argument covers arbitrary integrable deviation densities; it does not impose anonymity on them.

3. Convexity reduces the core to a cost condition

The extended-valued function \(C\) is convex. To see this, take feasible flows and provider quantities at two mass vectors. Their convex combinations satisfy flow conservation and the interpolated capacity bounds, and their costs interpolate linearly. Taking optimal solutions gives, whenever both endpoint costs are finite,

\(C((1-r)y+r y')\leq(1-r)C(y)+rC(y')\qquad(0\leq r\leq1).\)

Optimal solutions exist when feasible: on a DAG, a nonnegative unit flow decomposes into paths, so every arc flow is at most one. The feasible flow set is consequently bounded and closed.

We next establish a useful infeasibility bound. Every unit flow decomposes into paths whose weights sum to one. Since every such path contains at least two service vertices, its total service throughput is at least two. Hence feasibility at a mass vector \(z\) requires

\(\sum_{a,w}k_{aw}z_a\geq2.\)

In particular, for any fixed vector \(d\geq0\), the mass vector \(rd\) is infeasible for sufficiently small positive \(r\).

Proposition. For \(T>0\), a nonnegative efficient anonymous imputation belongs to the core if and only if

\(C(y)+x\cdot y\geq p\qquad\text{for every feasible }y\text{ with }0\leq y\leq\mu.\) \((1)\)

Proof of necessity. Fix a feasible \(y\), and put \(d=\mu-y\). If \(d=0\), condition \((1)\) is efficiency.

Otherwise choose sufficiently small \(r>0\), and define \(y_r=\mu-rd=(1-r)\mu+ry\). The complementary mass vector \(rd\) is infeasible by the throughput bound. Moreover, convexity gives

\(C(y_r)\leq(1-r)C_0+rC(y)<p\)

for sufficiently small \(r\), since \(C_0<p\) and \(C(y)\) is finite. Thus

\(W(y_r)=p-C(y_r).\)

The core inequality at \(y_r\), together with \(x\cdot\mu=p-C_0\), yields

\(p-C_0-rx\cdot d=x\cdot y_r\geq p-C(y_r).\)

Consequently,

\(rx\cdot d\leq C(y_r)-C_0\leq r(C(y)-C_0).\)

Dividing by \(r>0\) gives \(x\cdot d\leq C(y)-C_0\), which rearranges to \((1)\).

Proof of sufficiency. At every feasible \(y\),

\(W(y)\leq\max\{0,p-C(y)\}.\)

Condition \((1)\) gives \(x\cdot y\geq p-C(y)\), while nonnegativity gives \(x\cdot y\geq0\). Together they imply \(x\cdot y\geq W(y)\). Infeasible coalitions have worth zero and also satisfy the core inequality.

Thus stability is characterized completely by nonnegativity, efficiency, and \((1)\).

4. An explicit polynomial-size linear program

Split every service vertex \(w\) into \(w^-\) and \(w^+\). For each type \(a\), add a provider arc from \(w^-\) to \(w^+\), with flow \(q_{aw}\), cost \(c_{aw}\), and capacity \(k_{aw}y_a\). Redirect each original arc through the corresponding split endpoints; these arcs remain free and uncapacitated. Call their set \(E_0\).

This network has \(2m+2\) vertices and \(|E|+m\tau\) arcs. Its unit-flow formulation is exactly the definition of \(C(y)\).

For fixed \(x\geq0\), consider the linear program

\(\Phi(x)=\min\left\{\sum_{a,w}c_{aw}q_{aw}+\sum_a x_a y_a:\ \text{unit flow},\ 0\leq q_{aw}\leq k_{aw}y_a,\ 0\leq y_a\leq\mu_a\right\}.\)

Equivalently,

\(\Phi(x)=\min_{0\leq y\leq\mu,\ C(y)<+\infty}\bigl(C(y)+x\cdot y\bigr).\)

For an efficient \(x\), the grand-coalition optimal flow supplies a feasible solution of objective value \(C_0+x\cdot\mu=p\). Therefore condition \((1)\) is precisely \(\Phi(x)\geq p\).

To express this condition linearly while also choosing \(x\), introduce unrestricted vertex potentials \(\pi_v\) and nonnegative multipliers \(\alpha_{aw}\), \(\beta_a\). The dual of the program defining \(\Phi(x)\) is

\(\max\left\{\pi_s-\pi_t-\sum_a\mu_a\beta_a\right\},\)

subject to

  • \(\pi_u-\pi_v\leq0\) for each free arc \((u,v)\in E_0\);
  • \(\pi_{w^-}-\pi_{w^+}\leq c_{aw}+\alpha_{aw}\) for each provider arc;
  • \(\sum_w k_{aw}\alpha_{aw}-\beta_a\leq x_a\) for each type \(a\);
  • \(\alpha_{aw}\geq0\) and \(\beta_a\geq0\).

For completeness, use flow balance “outflow minus inflow,” equal to one at \(s\), minus one at \(t\), and zero elsewhere. Add the following terms to the primal objective:

\(\sum_{a,w}\alpha_{aw}(q_{aw}-k_{aw}y_a)+\sum_a\beta_a(y_a-\mu_a)+\sum_v\pi_v(b_v-\operatorname{out}_v+\operatorname{in}_v).\)

The constant term is \(\pi_s-\pi_t-\sum_a\mu_a\beta_a\). The coefficients of free-arc flows, provider-arc flows, and masses are, respectively,

\(\pi_v-\pi_u,\qquad c_{aw}+\alpha_{aw}-\pi_{w^-}+\pi_{w^+},\qquad x_a-\sum_w k_{aw}\alpha_{aw}+\beta_a.\)

Requiring these coefficients to be nonnegative gives exactly the stated dual.

The primal is feasible, has a finite optimum, and is bounded below by zero. Linear-programming strong duality therefore applies, and its dual attains the same optimum.

It follows that the desired imputation exists exactly when the following single feasibility LP has a solution:

  • \(x_a,\alpha_{aw},\beta_a\geq0\), with unrestricted \(\pi_v\);
  • \(\sum_a\mu_a x_a=p-C_0\);
  • all the dual constraints above;
  • \(\pi_s-\pi_t-\sum_a\mu_a\beta_a\geq p\).

If this LP is feasible, weak duality gives \(\Phi(x)\geq p\), so the returned \(x\) satisfies \((1)\) and is stable.

Conversely, if a stable anonymous imputation exists, the preceding propositions give \(\Phi(x)=p\). Strong duality supplies dual variables satisfying the feasibility LP. Thus LP infeasibility proves nonexistence.

5. Algorithm and bit complexity

The algorithm is:

  • Compute \(C(\mu)\) using the rational unit-flow LP.
  • If it is infeasible or \(C(\mu)\geq p\), return \(x_a=0\) for every type.
  • Otherwise solve the feasibility LP above. Return its \(x\)-coordinates if feasible, and report nonexistence if infeasible.

Both LPs have polynomially many variables and constraints. Their coefficients have polynomial bit length; the computed rational optimum \(C_0\) also has polynomial bit length by the standard determinant bounds for rational LP vertices. Exact rational linear programming decides feasibility and produces a rational feasible point in polynomial time.

Since \(|E|\leq(m+2)^2\), the running time and output bit length are polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

6. Existence and uniqueness are not automatic

Two examples illustrate the scope of the result. In both, the graph is \(s\to w_1\to w_2\to t\), and all operating costs are zero.

Nonexistence. Take one type with \(\mu_1=1\) and \(k_{1w_1}=k_{1w_2}=2\). Efficiency forces \(x_1=p\). A coalition of mass \(3/4\) is feasible, while its complement is infeasible, so its worth is \(p\). Its payment is only \(3p/4\). It violates the core inequality, so no stable imputation exists.

Nonuniqueness. Take two types of mass \(1/2\) each, with \(k_{aw}=1\) for every type and service. Feasibility requires \(y_1+y_2\geq1\); within the available mass box, this forces \(y=\mu\). Every proper coalition, modulo null sets, therefore has worth zero. Every nonnegative efficient allocation satisfying \(x_1+x_2=2p\) is stable.

The [cited finite-provider paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.11596) defines objections and counter-objections against individual players and uses shortest-path enactment. The proof here instead uses the stated freedom to choose entire sets \(I\) and \(J\), together with convex divisible-flow costs. It does not rely on the paper’s claimed characterization.
