Theorem B.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) course types \(G\), valuation types \(t\in[\tau]\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita capacities \(q_g\in[0,1]\), additive valuations \(v_t(B)=\sum_{g\in B}a_{t,g}\), and a common ranking of \(G\), find rational masses \(x_{t,B}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq G\) satisfying \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}x_{t,B}=q_g\), and \(v_t(B)\ge v_t(D\setminus\{g\})\) whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\), \(x_{u,D}>0\), and \(g\in D\).

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with one valuation type, strictly positive integer values, total mass one, and capacities in \(\{1/2,1\}\). Under the standard binary encoding of rational input values, it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

1. Reduction from PARTITION

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete PARTITION problem: given positive integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\), decide whether some \(S\subseteq[n]\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}w_i=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\). Its hardness follows from the standard reduction from SUBSET SUM; see these [Cornell lecture notes](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs6820/2022fa/Handouts/NPC4.pdf).

Construct a mass-distribution instance with one valuation type of mass \(\mu_1=1\). Let the courses be \(G=\{z,g_1,\ldots,g_n\}\), with values and capacities

  • \(a_{1,z}=1\) and \(q_z=1\);
  • \(a_{1,g_i}=2w_i\) and \(q_{g_i}=1/2\), for every \(i\in[n]\).

All values are strictly positive integers. With only one valuation type, the identical-ranking condition holds automatically. The construction has polynomial encoding length.

Write \(x_B=x_{1,B}\), and define \(W(B)=\sum_{i:g_i\in B}w_i\).

Every occupied bundle contains the special course

In any feasible allocation, \(\sum_B x_B=1\) and \(\sum_{B:z\in B}x_B=q_z=1\). Subtracting gives \(\sum_{B:z\notin B}x_B=0\).

Since all coordinates are nonnegative, \(x_B>0\) implies \(z\in B\). Thus every occupied bundle has value \(v_1(B)=1+2W(B)\).

EFX forces equal weight in all occupied bundles

Let \(B,D\) be any two occupied bundles. Since \(z\in D\), the EFX inequality for removing \(z\) from \(D\) gives

\(1+2W(B)=v_1(B)\ge v_1(D\setminus\{z\})=2W(D)\).

Consequently, \(2(W(D)-W(B))\le1\). The difference \(W(D)-W(B)\) is an integer, so \(W(D)\le W(B)\). Interchanging \(B\) and \(D\) proves \(W(B)=W(D)\).

Therefore there is an integer \(K\) such that \(W(B)=K\) for every occupied bundle.

An EFX allocation yields a partition

Using total mass one and the course-capacity equations,

\(K=\sum_Bx_BW(B)=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\sum_{B:g_i\in B}x_B=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\).

The first equality holds because every positive-mass bundle has weight \(K\); the second simply exchanges finite sums.

There is at least one occupied bundle because total mass is one. For any such bundle \(B\), the set \(S=\{i:g_i\in B\}\) therefore solves the PARTITION instance.

A partition yields an EFX allocation

Conversely, suppose \(S\subseteq[n]\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}w_i=\sum_{i\notin S}w_i=K\). Define

\(B_S=\{z\}\cup\{g_i:i\in S\}\), and \(B_{\overline S}=\{z\}\cup\{g_i:i\notin S\}\).

Assign mass \(1/2\) to each of these two bundles and zero to every other bundle.

The type mass is one. The special course occurs in both bundles, giving capacity one, and every other course occurs in exactly one bundle, giving capacity one half.

Both occupied bundles have value \(1+2K\). For any ordered pair \(B,D\) of occupied bundles and any \(g\in D\), positivity of the values gives

\(v_1(D\setminus\{g\})=v_1(D)-a_{1,g}\le v_1(D)=v_1(B)\).

Thus all EFX inequalities hold.

The construction is therefore a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

2. Polynomial certificates

Assume all numerical inputs, including the additive values, are binary-encoded rationals. We prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), despite the exponentially many possible bundles.

Let \(m=|G|\) and \(d=\tau+m\). For each pair \((t,B)\), define its column \(c_{t,B}\in\{0,1\}^d\): its type coordinates indicate type \(t\), and its course coordinates indicate membership in \(B\). The allocation equations are exactly

\(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}c_{t,B}=b\), where \(b=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau,(q_g)_{g\in G})\).

Sparse support

Suppose an EFX allocation exists, with support \(S\). Among rational feasible allocations supported within \(S\), choose one, \(y\), with the fewest positive coordinates.

Its occupied columns are linearly independent. Otherwise, since these columns are rational, there is a nonzero rational vector \(h\) on its support with \(\sum_j h_jc_j=0\). Changing its sign if necessary, assume some \(h_j>0\). Set

\(\lambda=\min_{j:h_j>0}y_j/h_j\), and \(y'_j=y_j-\lambda h_j\).

Then \(y'\) is rational and nonnegative, satisfies the same allocation equations, and has strictly smaller support. This contradicts minimality.

Hence \(y\) has at most \(d\) occupied coordinates. It remains EFX: every required comparison concerns two coordinates from the original EFX support, and the inequality depends only on their types and bundles, not their positive masses.

Polynomial bit length

Let \(r\le d\) be the support size. If \(r=0\), the zero allocation is already a short certificate. Otherwise, column independence lets us select \(r\) rows giving an invertible matrix \(A\in\{0,1\}^{r\times r}\). The positive masses satisfy \(Ay=b'\) for the corresponding subvector \(b'\).

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of the entries of \(b\). Its bit length is at most the sum of their input bit lengths, and \(Qb'\) is integral. By Cramer’s rule,

\(y_j=\det(A_j)/(Q\det(A))\),

where \(A_j\) replaces column \(j\) of \(A\) by \(Qb'\).

The determinant expansion gives \(|\det(A)|\le r!\) and \(|\det(A_j)|\le r!\max\{1,\max_i|Qb'_i|\}\). These bounds have polynomial bit length. Thus all certificate masses have polynomial bit length.

A verifier checks nonnegativity, all allocation equations, and every EFX inequality between the at most \(d\) occupied coordinates. There are at most \(d^2m\) such inequalities, and exact rational arithmetic takes polynomial time. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. Conclusion

The stated feasibility problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete under rational binary encoding. Hardness already holds for a single valuation type with strictly positive integer values.

The reduction uses the prescribed capacities essentially: a universally assigned low-value course forces equal weights across the support, and the remaining marginal capacities fix that weight to exactly half the total. The discrete-agent existence result does not establish feasibility under these additional constraints.
