1. A valid mass-allocation PAE bound

Fix confidence levels \(\alpha_t\in(0,1)\), chosen before the random experiment, with \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\alpha_t\le\delta\). Define

\(\displaystyle E_\delta(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}\eta_{t,\alpha_t}(S).\)

In particular, one may take \(\alpha_t=\delta/\tau\).

Proven bound. The following is an envelope-derived PAE loss bound:

\(\displaystyle B_\delta=\min\left\{1,\frac{E_\delta(x^\star)+E_\delta(\widehat x)}{W(x^\star)}\right\}.\)

A sharper valid bound is

\(\displaystyle B_\delta^{\mathrm{diff}}=\min\left\{1,\frac{\sum_{t,S}|x^\star_{t,S}-\widehat x_{t,S}|\eta_{t,\alpha_t}(S)}{W(x^\star)}\right\}.\)

These statements require neither independent prediction errors nor nonnegative predictions.

Proof

Let \(F\) denote the feasible allocation set. It is nonempty: allocating mass \(\mu_t\) to the empty bundle for every type satisfies all constraints. It is closed and bounded, since every coordinate satisfies \(0\le x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\). Thus both welfare objectives attain maxima on \(F\).

For each type, define the event

\(\displaystyle G_t=\left\{\forall S\subseteq M:\ |v_t(S)-\widetilde v_t(S)|\le\eta_{t,\alpha_t}(S)\right\}.\)

The hypotheses give \(\Pr(G_t^c)\le\alpha_t\). Consequently, the union bound gives

\(\displaystyle \Pr\left(\bigcap_{t=1}^{\tau}G_t\right)\ge1-\sum_t\alpha_t\ge1-\delta.\)

Write \(G=\bigcap_tG_t\). On \(G\), for every feasible allocation \(x\),

\(\displaystyle |W(x)-\widetilde W(x)|\le\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}|v_t(S)-\widetilde v_t(S)|\le E_\delta(x).\)

This holds simultaneously for all feasible allocations, so it applies to the random allocations \(x^\star\) and \(\widehat x\).

Predicted optimality gives \(\widetilde W(x^\star)-\widetilde W(\widehat x)\le0\). Therefore, on \(G\),

\(\displaystyle W(x^\star)-W(\widehat x)\)

\(\displaystyle =\bigl(W(x^\star)-\widetilde W(x^\star)\bigr)+\bigl(\widetilde W(x^\star)-\widetilde W(\widehat x)\bigr)+\bigl(\widetilde W(\widehat x)-W(\widehat x)\bigr)\)

\(\displaystyle \le E_\delta(x^\star)+E_\delta(\widehat x).\)

For the sharper version, put \(d_{t,S}=x^\star_{t,S}-\widehat x_{t,S}\). On the same event,

\(\displaystyle W(x^\star)-W(\widehat x)=\widetilde W(x^\star)-\widetilde W(\widehat x)+\sum_{t,S}d_{t,S}\bigl(v_t(S)-\widetilde v_t(S)\bigr)\)

\(\displaystyle \le\sum_{t,S}|d_{t,S}|\eta_{t,\alpha_t}(S).\)

True optimality and nonnegative true valuations give

\(\displaystyle 0\le1-\frac{W(\widehat x)}{W(x^\star)}\le1\)

almost surely. Dividing the preceding inequalities by the almost surely positive \(W(x^\star)\), and clipping at \(1\), proves both bounds. Their strict-exceedance events are contained in \(G^c\), whose probability is at most \(\delta\). ∎

The deterministic welfare argument parallels [Proposition 1 of the later discrete analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.08042v4#S4.SS1.SSS1). The confidence allocation here deliberately uses a union bound: the multiplicative confidence calculation appearing in [Theorem 1 of the 2017 paper](https://people.bu.edu/blubin/papers/md/PAE-CA-ML2017AAAI.pdf) cannot be inferred from the present assumptions. Independent bundle sampling does not imply independent error events when valuations may correlate.

2. Consequences and an observable certificate

Define

\(\displaystyle h_{t,\delta}=\max_{S\subseteq M}\eta_{t,\alpha_t}(S),\qquad H_\delta=\sum_t\mu_t h_{t,\delta}.\)

For every feasible allocation \(x\), the type-mass equalities imply

\(\displaystyle E_\delta(x)\le\sum_t h_{t,\delta}\sum_Sx_{t,S}=H_\delta.\)

Thus another valid bound is

\(\displaystyle B_\delta^{\mathrm{uniform}}=\min\left\{1,\frac{2H_\delta}{W(x^\star)}\right\}.\)

In particular, if every envelope radius is at most \(\eta\), then \(H_\delta\le\eta\), because \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The resulting numerator is \(2\eta\); it does not acquire an additional factor of \(\tau\).

The sharper numerator also cancels overlapping allocated mass. Indeed,

\(\displaystyle \sum_S|x^\star_{t,S}-\widehat x_{t,S}|\le\sum_S(x^\star_{t,S}+\widehat x_{t,S})=2\mu_t.\)

Neither monotonicity nor empty-bundle normalization entered these arguments.

A certificate requiring no additional true-value queries

The allocation-dependent bounds above need not be observable: finding \(x^\star\) is a separate true-welfare optimization problem. An alternative uses predictions and envelopes only.

Let

\(\displaystyle p=\widetilde W(\widehat x),\qquad e=E_\delta(\widehat x),\qquad \ell=p-e.\)

Define

\(\displaystyle B_\delta^{\mathrm{obs}}= \begin{cases} \min\{1,(H_\delta+e)/\ell\},&\ell>0,\\ 1,&\ell\le0. \end{cases}\)

On \(G\), the previous proof gives

\(\displaystyle W(x^\star)-W(\widehat x)\le H_\delta+e,\)

while

\(\displaystyle W(x^\star)\ge W(\widehat x)\ge p-e=\ell.\)

If \(\ell>0\), division proves the asserted bound. If \(\ell\le0\), the bound \(1\) always holds. Hence \(B_\delta^{\mathrm{obs}}\) is also a valid PAE bound.

Computing this certificate requires evaluating the supplied envelopes. Their probabilistic coverage assumption alone does not specify evaluation costs or a rational representation.

Why positivity alone does not give a small relative loss

Take one type, one good, and supply \(1\). For positive rationals \(\eta\) and \(a\) with \(0<a<\eta\), let

\(\displaystyle v(\varnothing)=0,\quad v(\{1\})=2\eta-a,\quad \widetilde v(\varnothing)=\eta,\quad \widetilde v(\{1\})=\eta-a.\)

The constant envelope \(\eta\) holds deterministically. Nevertheless, the predicted optimum allocates all mass to the empty bundle, whereas the true optimum allocates all mass to the good. Relative loss is \(1\).

This example is compatible with a supplied learner that ignores its training data. It also shows that the factor \(2\) in the uniform absolute loss bound is sharp: the absolute loss is \(2\eta-a\), approaching \(2\eta\) as \(a\) decreases to zero. Thus arbitrarily small absolute envelopes need not yield small relative loss without a suitable welfare scale.

\(\displaystyle \max_{x\ge0}\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\widetilde v_t(S)\)

\(\displaystyle T\le C_{\mathrm{samp}}+C_{\mathrm{val}}+C_A+\sum_{t,S}C_{\mathrm{pred}}(t,S)+\operatorname{poly}(\tau2^m,m+\tau,L).\)

With uniform per-call upper bounds \(C_s,C_v,C_p\), this becomes

\(\displaystyle T\le\tau q(C_s+C_v)+C_A+\tau2^mC_p+2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L).\)

Here \(L\) includes generated rational data; it must not be interpreted as the length of an unprovided true-valuation table.

The welfare guarantee holds for any fixed measurable tie-breaking rule, but the problem’s assumptions do not bound the computational cost of an arbitrary specified rule; that cost must be supplied separately.

Sparse optimal allocations

There exists an optimal vertex with at most \(\tau+m\) positive coordinates.

To prove this, associate to coordinate \((t,S)\) its column of type indicators and good indicators in \(\mathbb R^{\tau+m}\). If a feasible point has more than \(\tau+m\) positive coordinates, those columns are linearly dependent. There is consequently a nonzero perturbation \(d\), supported on its positive coordinates, that preserves every type total and every good usage.

For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both \(x+\varepsilon d\) and \(x-\varepsilon d\) remain nonnegative. They preserve all constraints, and their midpoint is \(x\). Thus \(x\) is not a vertex.

Vertex coordinates have polynomial rational bit length: after adding supply slacks, a basis has size \(\tau+m\) and integer entries of bounded magnitude. Cramer’s rule bounds its determinant bit length by \(O((\tau+m)\log(\tau+m))\), and the rational right-hand side has encoding length at most \(L\).

Sparse output therefore does not require enumerating the entire output allocation. It does not, however, make finding an optimum polynomial in the succinct input size.

5. Succinct SVR does not guarantee polynomial-time optimization

Proven limitation. Exact predicted-welfare optimization is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even with one type, unrestricted supplies, a fixed quadratic kernel, polynomially many support vectors, and zero prediction error.

Reduction

Use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete integer-weighted MAX CUT decision problem, established in [Karp’s original paper](https://cgi.di.uoa.gr/~sgk/teaching/grad/handouts/karp.pdf).

Let its graph have vertices \(1,\ldots,m\), edges \(E\), and integer weights \(w_{ij}\). For a bundle indicator \(s\in\{0,1\}^m\), define

\(\displaystyle f(s)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}(s_i+s_j-2s_is_j).\)

For binary \(s_i,s_j\), the parenthesis is \(1\) exactly when the edge crosses the cut. Thus \(f(s)\) is its cut weight.

Let

\(\displaystyle A_0=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}|w_{ij}|,\qquad B=1+A_0,\qquad d_i=\sum_{j:\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}.\)

Use the fixed kernel

\(\displaystyle \kappa(s,z)=\langle s,z\rangle^2.\)

It is rational-valued and positive semidefinite: the feature map \(\phi(s)=s\otimes s\) satisfies \(\langle\phi(s),\phi(z)\rangle=\langle s,z\rangle^2\).

Writing \(e_i\) for the singleton indicator gives

\(\displaystyle \kappa(s,e_i)=s_i,\qquad \kappa(s,e_i+e_j)=(s_i+s_j)^2.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle f(s)=\sum_i2d_i\kappa(s,e_i)-\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}\kappa(s,e_i+e_j).\)

This is the required signed-coefficient representation, with at most \(m+|E|\) nonzero terms. Add the empty-bundle term with coefficient

\(\displaystyle c_{\varnothing}=-3\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}.\)

It contributes zero to predictions, since \(\kappa(s,0)=0\), and makes the total coefficient sum zero.

Now take one type with mass \(1\), supplies \(\beta_j=1\), and

\(\displaystyle v(s)=\widetilde v(s)=B+f(s).\)

These valuations are positive: \(f(s)\ge-A_0\), so \(v(s)\ge1\). Every probability distribution on bundles is feasible. Hence

\(\displaystyle \max_{x\in F}\widetilde W(x)=B+\max_{s\in\{0,1\}^m}f(s).\)

Thus computing optimal predicted welfare computes maximum cut weight. Fractional allocation provides no improvement: an average of cut weights cannot exceed their maximum. Every positive-mass bundle in an optimal allocation must itself attain that maximum.

All coefficients and support vectors have polynomial encoding length. The prediction error is zero, so zero envelopes satisfy the coverage hypothesis for every confidence level.

Compatibility with actual SVR training

The construction can also arise from ordinary SVR training, rather than merely having an SVR-shaped expansion.

Query the empty bundle, every singleton, and every edge bundle. Let \(X\) have exactly this support and let \(q\) equal its size; without-replacement sampling then queries all these bundles.

Index them by \(Z_a\), let \(c_a\) be the coefficients above, and put

\(\displaystyle \bar w=\sum_ac_a\phi(Z_a),\qquad y_a=B+\langle\bar w,\phi(Z_a)\rangle.\)

Train SVR with zero insensitive width and penalty \(C>\max_a|c_a|\), minimizing

\(\displaystyle J(w,b)=\frac12\|w\|^2+C\sum_a|y_a-\langle w,\phi(Z_a)\rangle-b|.\)

The candidate \((\bar w,B)\) has zero training error. For any candidate \((w,b)\), let its residuals be \(r_a=y_a-\langle w,\phi(Z_a)\rangle-b\). Since \(\sum_ac_a=0\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_ac_ar_a=\|\bar w\|^2-\langle\bar w,w\rangle.\)

Also \(C\sum_a|r_a|\ge\sum_ac_ar_a\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle J(w,b)\ge\frac12\|\bar w\|^2+\frac12\|w-\bar w\|^2.\)

The candidate \((\bar w,B)\) attains the lower bound. Every minimizer must have \(w=\bar w\); attaining its objective value then requires zero residuals and hence \(b=B\). Thus the trained predictions are exactly \(B+f\).

This proves hardness even for genuine SVR outputs with \(K\le q\) and polynomial-size training data. The enumeration bounds above are valid general upper bounds; a polynomial bound in the succinct parameters would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
