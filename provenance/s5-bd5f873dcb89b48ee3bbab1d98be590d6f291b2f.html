The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational \(d\in[0,1]\) and a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over types \(t=(x,p)\), where \(x\in[0,1]\), \(p\in\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}\), and the masses sum to \(1\), construct a randomized direct mechanism over feasible placements \(Y_d=\{(y_1,y_2)\in[0,1]^2:|y_1-y_2|\ge d\}\). Agents report locations while preferences remain public; for a finite realization with population size \(n\), a report changes mass in increments of \(1/n\). Is there a group-strategyproof mechanism whose worst-case ratio of optimal to achieved per-capita expected social utility is at most \(2\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types \(t=(x,p)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), where \(x\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\) and \(p\in\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; there are \(m=2\) labeled facilities. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the binary-rational distance constraint \(d\in[0,1]\).
A randomized direct mechanism maps reported societies to finitely supported lotteries with binary-rational probabilities and placements in \(Y_d=\{(y_1,y_2)\in[0,1]^2:|y_1-y_2|\ge d\}\). Locations are private and preferences public; there are no payments. For a lottery \(Q\), define \(U_t(Q)=\mathbb E_{y\sim Q}[p_1|x-y_1|+p_2|x-y_2|]\).
A coalition consists of finitely many portions \((t_k,a_k)\), with positive rational masses satisfying \(\sum_{k:t_k=t}a_k\le\mu_t\). Each portion may report any rational location \(r_k\in[0,1]\), retaining its public preference. The resulting report distribution \(\mu'\) removes these portions from their true types and adds them at their reported types. Group-strategyproofness means that for every such deviation some portion \(k\) satisfies \(U_{t_k}(M_d(\mu'))\le U_{t_k}(M_d(\mu))\); thus a deviation is forbidden when every coalition portion strictly benefits in expectation.
Define per-capita social utility \(W(\mu,Q)=\sum_t\mu_tU_t(Q)\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}_d(\mu)=\max_{y\in Y_d}\sum_t\mu_t(p_1|x-y_1|+p_2|x-y_2|)\). The approximation requirement is \(W(\mu,M_d(\mu))\ge\operatorname{OPT}_d(\mu)/2\) for every admissible society and distance constraint.

Does there exist a randomized direct mechanism that is group-strategyproof under these mass deviations, satisfies the approximation requirement, and outputs its lottery in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), with \(m=2\), or is this guarantee unattainable?

For finite populations with private locations and public optional preferences, a randomized group-strategyproof mechanism achieves approximation ratio at most \(2\) for every \(d\in[0,1]\). Any randomized strategyproof mechanism in that finite-agent model has worst-case approximation ratio at least \(14/13\). These statements concern coalitions of agents rather than arbitrary rational portions of mass.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted input and reported locations to binary rationals so the computational input and deviations have finite encodings.
  • Replaced the finite-realization increment of 1/n by arbitrary positive rational coalition portions, allowing portions of a type to make different reports; no population-size parameter is part of the mass model.
  • Used the discrete definition of group-strategyproofness: a coalition deviation is prohibited only when every participating portion strictly improves its expected utility.
  • Required an explicitly output finite lottery with binary-rational probabilities and placements, and measured computation in the support size and total input bit length.
  • Expressed the ratio guarantee as a welfare inequality to avoid an undefined ratio when achieved welfare is zero.

Conclusion

Yes. A report-independent lottery achieves the required approximation factor \(2\) and is group-strategyproof under arbitrary rational mass deviations. It works for every admissible society and every \(d\in[0,1]\).

Mechanism and feasibility

For every reported society \(\nu\), define
\[ M_d(\nu)= \begin{cases} (0,1),&\text{with probability }1/2,\\ (1,0),&\text{with probability }1/2. \end{cases} \]

Both placements have facility separation \(1\), so both belong to \(Y_d\) for every \(d\in[0,1]\). Their coordinates and probabilities are binary rationals.

The rule is independent of the report. In particular, it is well-defined on every report distribution arising from the allowed rational mass deviations.

Group-strategyproofness

For any true type \(t=(x,p)\), where \(x\in[0,1]\), its expected utility is
\[ \begin{aligned} U_t(M_d(\nu)) &=\frac12\bigl(p_1x+p_2(1-x)\bigr) +\frac12\bigl(p_1(1-x)+p_2x\bigr)\\ &=\frac{p_1+p_2}{2}. \end{aligned} \]
Here we used \(|x-0|=x\) and \(|x-1|=1-x\).

Consider any permitted coalition and its resulting report distribution \(\mu'\). For every coalition portion \(k\), the displayed identity gives
\[ U_{t_k}(M_d(\mu'))=\frac{p_{k,1}+p_{k,2}}2 =U_{t_k}(M_d(\mu)). \]
Thus every portion has unchanged expected utility. In particular, no deviation makes every portion strictly better off, proving the required group-strategyproofness.

Approximation guarantee

Define the total preference weight
\[ A(\mu)=\sum_t\mu_t(p_1+p_2). \]
The utility calculation above implies
\[ W(\mu,M_d(\mu))=\frac{A(\mu)}2. \]

For any placement \(y\in Y_d\), each distance \(|x-y_i|\) is at most \(1\), because both coordinates lie in \([0,1]\). Consequently,
\[ p_1|x-y_1|+p_2|x-y_2|\le p_1+p_2. \]
Multiplying by the positive masses and summing yields
\[ \sum_t\mu_t\bigl(p_1|x-y_1|+p_2|x-y_2|\bigr)\le A(\mu). \]
Taking the maximum over \(Y_d\), we obtain
\[ \operatorname{OPT}_d(\mu)\le A(\mu) =2W(\mu,M_d(\mu)), \]
which is exactly the required guarantee.

The factor \(2\) is attained by this mechanism: if all mass has type \((0,(1,0))\), its expected utility is \(1/2\), whereas the feasible placement \((1,0)\) gives optimal utility \(1\), for every permitted \(d\).

Running time

The output contains two placements and two fixed probabilities, so its encoding has constant size. Constructing it takes constant time after reading the input; including input reading takes \(O(L)\) time. This is polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).
