The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) of complete reciprocal \(n\times n\) positive rational comparison matrices \(M^t\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a common nonempty reference set \(K\), and positive rational reference priorities \(r_k\), compute the HRE priority vector \(q\) and its ranking for \(\bar m_{ij}=\prod_{t\in T}(m^t_{ij})^{\mu_t}\), where \(q_k=r_k\) for \(k\in K\) and \(q_j^{n-1}=\prod_{i\ne j}\bar m_{ji}q_i\) for \(j\notin K\). When \(q_i<(n-1)\sum_{j\ne i}q_j\) for every \(i\), determine whether the returned vector minimizes \(e_{\bar M}(q)=\sum_{i,j}(\ln\bar m_{ij}-\ln(q_i/q_j))^2\).

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type specifies a complete positive rational matrix \(M^t\) satisfying \(m^t_{ij}m^t_{ji}=1\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Given \(\varnothing\ne K\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and positive rational reference priorities \(r_k\), set \(\bar m_{ij}=\prod_{t\in T}(m^t_{ij})^{\mu_t}\). The geometric HRE vector is the positive vector satisfying \(q_k=r_k\) for \(k\in K\) and \(q_j^{n-1}=\prod_{i\ne j}(\bar m_{ji}q_i)\) for \(j\notin K\).
Compute this vector exactly, with entries represented as finite products of positive rational bases raised to binary-rational exponents, and its ranking in decreasing priority, preserving exact ties. Priorities are not normalized.
Additionally, on instances promised to satisfy \(q_i<(n-1)\sum_{j\ne i}q_j\) for every \(i\), decide whether \(q\) globally minimizes \(e_{\bar M}(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(\ln\bar m_{ij}-\ln(x_i/x_j))^2\) over \(x\in\mathbb R_{>0}^{n}\) with \(x_k=r_k\) for \(k\in K\).

Is this exact computation problem, including the conditional optimality decision, solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard in the standard Turing model?

For a single comparison matrix, geometric HRE has an existence-and-uniqueness result and a logarithmic-system computation procedure; conditional optimality under the displayed inequality is asserted, without a polynomial bit-complexity theorem ([Kułakowski, Grobler-Dębska and Wąs](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10898-014-0253-4)).

1. Polynomial-time construction of the exact vector

Let
$$ U=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus K,\qquad u=|U|,\qquad h=|K|. $$
Thus $u,h\ge 1$ and $u+h=n$.

Define
$$ a_{ij}=\ln\bar m_{ij} =\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\ln m^t_{ij}. $$
Reciprocity implies $a_{ij}=-a_{ji}$. Positivity together with diagonal reciprocity gives $m^t_{ii}=1$, hence $a_{ii}=0$.

Put
$$ \alpha_j=\sum_{i\ne j}a_{ji},\qquad \rho=\sum_{k\in K}\ln r_k. $$

Solving the logarithmic equations

Write $y_i=\ln q_i$ and $S_U=\sum_{\ell\in U}y_\ell$. For $j\in U$, the defining equation becomes
$$ (n-1)y_j =\alpha_j+\sum_{\substack{i\in U\\i\ne j}}y_i+\rho, $$
or equivalently
$$ ny_j-S_U=\alpha_j+\rho. $$
Summing over $j\in U$ gives
$$ hS_U=\beta+u\rho, \qquad \beta=\sum_{\ell\in U}\alpha_\ell. $$
Therefore every solution must satisfy
$$ y_j=\frac{\rho}{h}+\frac{\alpha_j}{n}+\frac{\beta}{nh}, \qquad j\in U. $$

Conversely, these values have sum
$$ \frac{u\rho}{h}+\frac{\beta}{n} +\frac{u\beta}{nh} =\frac{u\rho+\beta}{h}, $$
because $u+h=n$. Substitution then gives $ny_j-S_U=\alpha_j+\rho$. This proves existence and uniqueness.

Exact rational-power representation

Define positive rational bases
$$ R=\prod_{k\in K}r_k,\qquad B_j^t=\prod_{i\ne j}m^t_{ji},\qquad C^t=\prod_{\ell\in U}\prod_{k\in K}m^t_{\ell k}. $$
The internal terms in $\sum_{\ell\in U}\alpha_\ell$ cancel in reciprocal pairs. Consequently,
$$ \alpha_j=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\ln B_j^t, \qquad \beta=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\ln C^t. $$
Setting
$$ H_j^t=(B_j^t)^hC^t $$
therefore yields
$$ \boxed{ q_j=R^{1/h}\prod_{t\in T}(H_j^t)^{\mu_t/(nh)} \quad(j\in U),\qquad q_k=r_k\quad(k\in K). } $$

Every unknown coordinate uses at most $\tau+1$ factors.

For bit complexity, multiplying input rationals adds their numerator and denominator bit lengths, up to an additive constant per factor. Thus $R,B_j^t,C^t$ have polynomial binary length. Raising $B_j^t$ to $h\le n$ increases its length by at most a polynomial factor. The exponents $\mu_t/(nh)$ also have polynomial binary length.

There are polynomially many factors across all coordinates, so ordinary integer and rational arithmetic constructs the entire exact output in time polynomial in $n,\tau,L$. No numerical logarithms are required.

2. Unconditional global optimality

The displayed promise is unnecessary for the objective in the question.

For logarithmic coordinates $z_i=\ln x_i$, define
$$ F(z)=\sum_{i,j=1}^n(a_{ij}-z_i+z_j)^2. $$
The logarithm map bijects the feasible positive vectors with the affine subspace
$$ z_k=\ln r_k\qquad(k\in K). $$

Let $y=\ln q$ and
$$ E_{ij}=a_{ij}-y_i+y_j. $$
These residuals satisfy $E_{ij}=-E_{ji}$. For each $j\in U$, the HRE equation gives
$$ \sum_iE_{ji} =\alpha_j-ny_j+\sum_i y_i =0. $$

Take any feasible perturbation $v$, meaning $v_k=0$ for $k\in K$. Expanding,
$$ F(y+v)-F(y) =-2\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}(v_i-v_j) +\sum_{i,j}(v_i-v_j)^2. $$
Antisymmetry gives
$$ \sum_{i,j}E_{ij}(v_i-v_j) =2\sum_i v_i\sum_jE_{ij}=0. $$
Here the terms for $i\in U$ vanish by the residual row equations, and those for $i\in K$ vanish because $v_i=0$.

Hence
$$ F(y+v)-F(y) =2n\sum_{i\in U}v_i^2 -2\left(\sum_{i\in U}v_i\right)^2. $$
Cauchy–Schwarz implies
$$ \left(\sum_{i\in U}v_i\right)^2 \le u\sum_{i\in U}v_i^2, $$
so
$$ F(y+v)-F(y)\ge 2h\sum_{i\in U}v_i^2. $$
Since $h\ge1$, this is strictly positive for every nonzero feasible perturbation.

Thus $q$ is the unique global minimizer on every valid instance, and the requested conditional decision always answers yes.

For $n=2$, the promise itself has no instances: it would require both $q_1<q_2$ and $q_2<q_1$. The meaningful promised case starts at $n=3$.

3. Ranking reduces to product-of-exponentials comparison

Define $\mathrm{PoE}_{\ge}$ to be the following decision problem: given binary-encoded positive integers $a_\ell,b_\ell,c_\ell,d_\ell$, decide whether
$$ A=\prod_{\ell=1}^{s}a_\ell^{b_\ell} \ge C=\prod_{\ell=1}^{t}c_\ell^{d_\ell}. $$
This is the inequality problem studied by [Etessami, Stewart and Yannakakis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.5429).

From Section 1, the ratio of any two priorities has a polynomial-size representation
$$ \frac{q_i}{q_j} =\prod_{\ell=1}^{v}(p_\ell/s_\ell)^{e_\ell}, $$
where $p_\ell,s_\ell$ are positive integers and $e_\ell$ are signed rational numbers.

Write $e_\ell=f_\ell/g_\ell$ with $g_\ell>0$, and take
$$ D=\prod_{\ell=1}^{v}g_\ell. $$
Its bit length is bounded by the sum of the denominator bit lengths. Each
$$ z_\ell=De_\ell $$
is consequently an integer of polynomial binary length.

Now
$$ \left(\frac{q_i}{q_j}\right)^D =\prod_{\ell=1}^{v}(p_\ell/s_\ell)^{z_\ell}. $$
Splitting numerators and denominators and moving negative powers to the other side produces two integer PoE expressions of polynomial encoding length. Delete zero-exponent factors and unit bases. If a side becomes empty, represent it by $1^1$.

Since $D>0$,
$$ q_i\ge q_j \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \left(\frac{q_i}{q_j}\right)^D\ge1. $$
This proves a polynomial-time many-one reduction of priority comparison to $\mathrm{PoE}_{\ge}$.

The numerical value of $D$ may be exponential. The reduction uses its binary encoding and does not expand the powers.

Exact ties are decidable in polynomial time

For completeness, equality of integer PoE expressions has an elementary cancellation algorithm.

Delete unit bases. If a left factor $a^b$ and a right factor $c^d$ have
$$ g=\gcd(a,c)>1, $$
replace them by
$$ (a/g)^b,\qquad(c/g)^d, $$
and retain $g^{b-d}$ on the left when $b>d$, or $g^{d-b}$ on the right when $d>b$. When $b=d$, retain neither. Delete newly created unit bases.

This operation divides both represented integers by the same positive integer $g^{\min(b,d)}$, so it preserves equality.

To bound the number of iterations, let $W$ be the product of all current bases, counting each factor once and ignoring its exponent. An iteration replaces the contribution $ac$ by at most
$$ (a/g)(c/g)g=ac/g. $$
Thus $W$ decreases by a factor of at least two. Initially $\log_2 W$ is bounded by the sum of the input base bit lengths, so only polynomially many iterations occur.

Each iteration increases the factor count by at most one. Bases never exceed the largest original base, and exponents never exceed the largest original exponent. Gcd computations, divisions, and scans therefore take polynomial total time.

At termination every left base is coprime to every right base. The represented integers are then coprime, so they are equal exactly when both are $1$.

Applying this algorithm after denominator clearing decides priority equality in polynomial time. All tie classes can therefore be identified in polynomial time.

Finally, polynomially many $\mathrm{PoE}_{\ge}$ queries suffice to sort these classes. Together with Sections 1 and 2, this proves a polynomial-time oracle algorithm for the full task.

4. Reverse reduction for every fixed number of alternatives

Fix any $n\ge2$ and any nonempty proper set $K$. Choose an alternative $v\notin K$. The following construction reduces arbitrary $\mathrm{PoE}_{\ge}$ instances to ranking with these fixed choices.

Take a PoE instance and set $P=A/C$. For a positive integer $a$, let $\operatorname{bl}(a)$ denote its binary length. Define
$$ S=\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}b_\ell+\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}d_\ell, $$
$$ H=1+ \sum_{\ell=1}^{s}b_\ell\operatorname{bl}(a_\ell) + \sum_{\ell=1}^{t}d_\ell\operatorname{bl}(c_\ell), $$
and
$$ D=2^{\operatorname{bl}(H)}. $$
Because every base has binary length at least one,
$$ D>H>S. $$
All these integers have polynomial binary length.

For each positive rational $z$, define a type using rational weights
$$ w_v=z,\qquad w_i=1\quad(i\ne v), $$
and matrix entries
$$ m_{ij}(z)=\frac{w_i}{w_j}. $$
These matrices are complete, positive, rational, reciprocal, and consistent.

Use the following types and masses:

  • For each left factor, use $z=a_\ell$ with mass $b_\ell/D$.
  • For each right factor, use $z=1/c_\ell$ with mass $d_\ell/D$.
  • Use $z=1$ with mass $(D-S)/D$.

All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. Merge duplicate matrices by adding their masses. This preserves the aggregate matrix and leaves support size at most $s+t+1$. Constructing and merging the types takes polynomial time.

Set every reference priority to $1$, and put
$$ p=P^{1/D}. $$
The aggregate matrix is the consistent matrix with weights
$$ \widetilde w_v=p,\qquad \widetilde w_i=1\quad(i\ne v). $$
Indeed, its entries involving $v$ are obtained from
$$ \prod_t z_t^{\mu_t}=P^{1/D}=p, $$
and all other entries are $1$.

The candidate
$$ q_v=p,\qquad q_i=1\quad(i\ne v) $$
satisfies every reference constraint. Moreover, for every unknown alternative $j$,
$$ \bar m_{ji}q_i =\frac{\widetilde w_j}{\widetilde w_i}\widetilde w_i =\widetilde w_j=q_j, $$
so
$$ \prod_{i\ne j}(\bar m_{ji}q_i)=q_j^{n-1}. $$
By uniqueness from Section 1, this candidate is precisely the HRE vector.

Choosing any $k\in K$, we obtain
$$ A\ge C \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q_v\ge q_k. $$
Equality corresponds exactly to a priority tie.

Promise preservation for every fixed $n\ge3$

Since $a<2^{\operatorname{bl}(a)}$,
$$ |\ln P| \le \sum_\ell b_\ell\ln a_\ell+ \sum_\ell d_\ell\ln c_\ell < H\ln2 < D\ln2. $$
Consequently,
$$ \frac12<p<2. $$

For alternative $v$ and $n\ge3$,
$$ q_v=p<2<(n-1)^2 =(n-1)\sum_{j\ne v}q_j. $$
For any $i\ne v$,
$$ (n-1)\sum_{j\ne i}q_j =(n-1)(p+n-2) >(n-1)(n-2)\ge2>q_i. $$
Thus every promised inequality holds.

This establishes PoE equivalence even with fixed $n\ge3$, any fixed nonempty proper reference set, consistent type matrices, dyadic masses, and the optimality promise. Without that promise, the ranking reduction also works for $n=2$.

Therefore the full computation task is polynomial-time Turing equivalent to $\mathrm{PoE}_{\ge}$. In particular,
$$ \boxed{ \text{The full task is polynomial-time solvable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{PoE}_{\ge}\in\mathrm P. } $$

5. An unconditional polynomial-space upper bound

Here is a direct proof that PoE comparison, and hence the full task, is computable in polynomial space.

Let $s$ denote the PoE input length. Repeated squaring constructs multiplication circuits for $A$ and $C$ with a polynomial number $g$ of vertices. Inputs are nonnegative integers of at most $s$ bits; an empty product is the constant $1$.

Multiplication adds operand bit lengths. Inducting through the vertices shows that every circuit value has bit length at most
$$ B=s2^g. $$
Although $B$ may be exponentially large, its binary encoding has polynomial length.

Computing one bit in polynomial space

Number bits starting from position $0$. For an input vertex, any requested bit can be read directly, returning zero beyond its encoded length.

For a multiplication vertex $w=uv$, let $u_j,v_j$ denote operand bits. Define
$$ c_r=\sum_{j=0}^{r}u_jv_{r-j}. $$
Binary multiplication gives the carry recurrence
$$ h_0=0,\qquad w_r=(c_r+h_r)\bmod2,\qquad h_{r+1}=\left\lfloor\frac{c_r+h_r}{2}\right\rfloor. $$
To compute $w_k$, iterate through $r=0,\ldots,k$, computing each operand bit recursively and retaining only the current counters, partial sum, and carry.

Since $c_r\le r+1$, induction gives $h_r\le r$. Thus each counter, sum, and carry needs only
$$ O(\log(k+2)) $$
bits. Calls descend strictly through the circuit, so recursion depth is at most $g$. For $k<B$, total working space is polynomial in $s$ and $g$.

The procedure may recompute bits many times, but it terminates because the circuit is acyclic and every loop is finite.

Comparison and full output

Scan bit positions from $B-1$ down to $0$, computing the corresponding bits of $A$ and $C$. At the first difference, the integer with bit $1$ is larger. If all bits agree, the integers are equal.

The scan counter has polynomial length, and the recursive procedure uses polynomial space. Therefore
$$ \mathrm{PoE}_{\ge}\in\mathrm{PSPACE}. $$
The denominator-clearing reduction produces polynomial-size PoE inputs. Reusing this comparison procedure while sorting, together with the polynomial-size exact representation, proves that the entire requested output is computable in polynomial space.

This is a space bound, not a polynomial-time bound.

6. Polynomial-time cases

A polynomially bounded common mass denominator

Suppose the masses admit a common denominator $d$ with polynomially bounded numerical value:
$$ \mu_t=\frac{a_t}{d},\qquad a_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad \sum_t a_t=d. $$
The least common denominator can be computed using gcd and lcm operations.

Section 1 gives
$$ q_j^{nhd}=R^{nd}\prod_{t\in T}(H_j^t)^{a_t} \qquad(j\in U), $$
while
$$ q_k^{nhd}=r_k^{nhd} \qquad(k\in K). $$
Because $a_t\le d$ and $d$ has polynomial numerical value, these rational powers can be expanded into rationals of polynomial binary length.

Comparing the expanded rationals gives the exact order and ties: raising positive numbers to the common positive power $nhd$ preserves both. Hence the entire task is polynomial-time solvable under this denominator bound.

Equal masses are a special case, with $d=\tau$.

Fixed support size

For fixed $\tau$, each priority uses at most $\tau+1$ rational bases. A ratio uses at most $2(\tau+1)$, so splitting numerators and denominators produces a bounded number of integer factors after denominator clearing.

The fixed-factor theorem for PoE inequality states that comparison is polynomial-time solvable when the number of factors on each side is fixed, with bases and exponents encoded in binary. Those hypotheses hold here. Thus the full task is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed $\tau$. See [Etessami, Stewart and Yannakakis, Proposition 3.7(2)](https://arxiv.org/html/1304.5429v3).

7. What remains open

The exact vector construction, exact tie detection, and global optimality decision are settled above. The remaining issue is unrestricted order comparison.

The reductions establish an equivalence; they do not establish that either equivalent problem belongs to $\mathrm P$ or is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard. Fixing the number of alternatives, imposing consistency on every type matrix, or imposing the optimality promise does not remove this obstacle.

A complete classification still requires an unconditional polynomial-time algorithm or a hardness proof for unrestricted PoE inequality. No such result is derived here.
