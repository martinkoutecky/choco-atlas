The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), committee \(W\subseteq C\) with \(|W|=k\), distinct approval types \(A_1,\ldots,A_\tau\subseteq C\), and rational masses \(\mu_i\ge0\) summing to \(1\), decide whether for every integer \(\ell\ge1\), witness set \(Q\subseteq C\), and type subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) satisfying \(\sum_{i\in I}\mu_i\ge|Q|/k\) and \(|A_i\cap Q|\ge\ell\) for all \(i\in I\), we have \(|W\cap\bigcup_{i\in I}A_i|\ge\ell\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set, \(k\) a positive integer, \(W\subseteq C\) with \(|W|=k\), and \(\mu\) a rational probability distribution, explicitly given by its nonzero masses, over approval sets \(A\subseteq C\). A mass coalition is a vector \(x\) with \(0\le x_A\le\mu(A)\).

The committee \(W\) satisfies mass-FPJR if, for every integer \(\ell\ge1\), every \(T\subseteq C\), and every mass coalition \(x\) such that \(\sum_Ax_A\ge |T|/k\) and \(|A\cap T|\ge\ell\) whenever \(x_A>0\), we have \(\left|W\capigcup_{\{A:x_A>0\}}A ight|\ge\ell\).

Target. Is deciding whether \(W\) satisfies mass-FPJR solvable in polynomial time, or is it coNP-hard?

Prover output (gpt-5.6-sol / xhigh, Bolzano prover prompt)

Resolution

Claim

Under the stated definition, no committee satisfies mass-FPJR. Consequently, verification is polynomial-time solvable: the algorithm always returns NO.

Proof

Fix any legal instance. Choose

  • \(\ell=1\),
  • \(T=\varnothing\),
  • \(x_A=0\) for every approval set \(A\).

This is a valid mass coalition because \(0\leq x_A\leq\mu(A)\). Moreover,

\[ \sum_A x_A=0=\frac{|T|}{k}. \]

Thus the required mass inequality holds. The condition

\[ |A\cap T|\geq\ell\quad\text{whenever }x_A>0 \]

holds vacuously, since no \(x_A\) is positive.

However, the union of the approval sets with positive coalition mass is empty. Therefore,

\[ \left|W\cap\bigcup_{\{A:x_A>0\}}A\right| =|W\cap\varnothing| =0 <1=\ell. \]

Hence the mass-FPJR conclusion fails.

Complexity consequence

Every valid input is a NO-instance. The decision logic therefore takes constant time, apart from parsing the input.

The finite-profile hardness result does not apply to this literal definition because the zero mass coalition is admitted. A nondegeneracy condition such as \(\sum_Ax_A>0\) would be needed to obtain the intended nontrivial verification problem.
