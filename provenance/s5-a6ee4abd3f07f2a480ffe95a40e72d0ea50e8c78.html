The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite hierarchy and \(\tau\) complete student types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), each with a matroid-rank valuation over \(m\) course categories, and rational per-enrollee seat supplies \(q_g\), find rational masses \(x_{t,S}\) assigning each type among integral bundles \(S\subseteq G\). Require that each type’s mass is preserved and total use of each category \(g\) is at most \(q_g\). At every internal node \(i\), among reallocations within its subtree that preserve type masses and use no more than its incoming seat vector \(b_i(x)\), the allocation must maximize descendant aggregate utility \(V_i\) and make the children’s aggregate-utility vector Lorenz-dominant. Determine whether such an allocation can be computed in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), hierarchy size, and rational input bit length \(L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The hierarchy is a finite rooted tree \(T\) with \(n\) nodes and root \(r\). Each type includes a fixed leaf affiliation \(\ell(t)\) and a matroid-rank valuation \(u_t\) on \(G\), where \(|G|=m\). Valuations are supplied as Boolean circuits evaluating their ranks, promised to represent matroid-rank functions; \(L\) is the total binary input length, including these circuits. Seat supplies are binary rationals \(q_g\geq0\).
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,S}\), for integral bundles \(S\subseteq G\), satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni g}x_{t,S}\leq q_g\). Empty bundles and unused seats are allowed; represent the allocation by listing its nonzero entries.
Let \(D(i)\) be the types affiliated with leaves below node \(i\), and \(C(i)\) its children. Define \(V_i(x)=\sum_{t\in D(i)}\sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{t,S}u_t(S)\). Set \(b_{r,g}(x)=q_g\) and, for \(i\ne r\), \(b_{i,g}(x)=\sum_{t\in D(i)}\sum_{S\ni g}x_{t,S}\).
For each internal node \(i\), compare \(x\) with every nonnegative real reallocation \(y\) of the types in \(D(i)\) that preserves each type's mass and uses at most \(b_{i,g}(x)\) seats of each category. Require \(V_i(x)\geq V_i(y)\) and that \((V_j(x))_{j\in C(i)}\) Lorenz-dominates \((V_j(y))_{j\in C(i)}\). Comparisons use unweighted aggregate utilities and impose no fairness constraints on the competing reallocations.

Can such an allocation always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,n,L\), or is this computation problem NP-hard?

For indivisible goods and polynomial-time rank evaluation, a polynomial-time sequential algorithm computes allocations satisfying utilitarian optimality and Lorenz-dominance at every internal node ([discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24105v2)).
Polynomial-time computation of Lorenz-dominant fractional utility profiles is known for pairwise kidney exchange ([fractional result](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2014/aamas/p445.pdf)); this concerns a different feasible set. No result settling the exact distributional hierarchy problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Complete types include fixed leaf affiliations; several types may belong to the same leaf, and reallocations preserve these affiliations.
  • The unspecified valuation representation is fixed as rank-evaluation Boolean circuits promised to represent matroid-rank functions, making evaluation polynomial in the encoded input length.
  • Seat supplies are nonnegative binary rationals; unused seats and empty bundles are allowed, consistent with incomplete goods allocations.
  • The root's incoming seat vector is the full supply vector. Each other node's incoming vector is the seat usage of its descendant types, since the graded allocation specifies no separate internal seat reservations.
  • Aggregate utilities are mass-weighted sums of bundle ranks, with unweighted Lorenz comparisons between children rather than comparisons of per-capita utilities.
  • Competing reallocations may use real masses and need not satisfy fairness at lower nodes, matching the unrestricted allocation comparisons in the discrete definition.
  • Allocations are represented by their nonzero rational entries, avoiding a compulsory exponentially long table.

1. Result and convention

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. An allocation satisfying both requirements at every internal node can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,n,L\), with at most \(\tau(m+1)\) nonzero entries.

For a vector \(v\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{p}\), let \(v_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq v_{(p)}\) denote its sorted coordinates, and define

\( \mathcal L_k(v)=\sum_{a=1}^{k}v_{(a)},\qquad \mathcal L_0(v)=0. \)

We use Lorenz dominance to mean \(\mathcal L_k(v)\geq\mathcal L_k(w)\) for every \(k\). This compares unnormalized aggregate utilities and allows unequal total utilities.

If the root is a leaf, assigning every type the empty bundle satisfies all requirements. Below, assume the root is internal.

The proof has two main ingredients: an exact polymatroid description of attainable child utilities, and a refinement procedure that preserves the seat vectors assigned at earlier levels.

2. Replacing bundle distributions by independent-set marginals

Write \(r_t=u_t\), and let \(M_t\) be the promised matroid with rank function \(r_t\). For any ground set \(E\) and normalized, nondecreasing, submodular function \(h\), define

\( P(h)=\{z\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{E}:z(A)\leq h(A)\text{ for every }A\subseteq E\}, \)

where \(z(A)=\sum_{e\in A}z_e\).

The matroid independence-polytope theorem states that

\( P(r_t)=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf1_I:I\text{ is independent in }M_t\}. \)

This is Edmonds’ theorem; see Theorem 4 in [Cunningham’s account of matroid polyhedra](https://ems.press/content/book-chapter-files/27359).

Every bundle \(S\) contains an independent set \(I_t(S)\subseteq S\) of cardinality \(r_t(S)\). Replacing mass assigned to \(S\) by the same mass assigned to \(I_t(S)\):

  • preserves that type’s utility;
  • preserves its total mass;
  • weakly decreases every category’s seat consumption.

Thus restricting allocations to independent bundles leaves the attainable utility vectors unchanged under upper bounds on seat use. This applies equally to rational allocations and real competing reallocations.

For distributions supported on independent bundles, define

\( z_{t,g}=\sum_{I\ni g}x_{t,I}. \)

The independence-polytope theorem gives the exact characterization

\( z_t\in\mu_tP(r_t). \)

Moreover, utility equals total seat consumption:

\( \sum_I x_{t,I}r_t(I)=\sum_I x_{t,I}|I|=z_t(G). \)

Conversely, every \(z_t\in\mu_tP(r_t)\) has a distribution over independent bundles of total mass \(\mu_t\), with marginals \(z_t\). Empty bundles are included.

Consequently, for types \(D\) facing a category-capacity vector \(b\), the relevant marginal feasible set is

\[ z_{t,g}\geq0,\qquad z_t(S)\leq\mu_t r_t(S)\quad(t\in D,\ S\subseteq G),\qquad \sum_{t\in D}z_{t,g}\leq b_g\quad(g\in G). \tag{1} \]

In this representation, the utility of any group of types is the sum of its marginal coordinates.

3. The exact polymatroid of attainable child utilities

Fix an internal node \(i\), a capacity vector \(b\), and write \(J=C(i)\). For each child \(j\), define

\( R_j(S)=\sum_{t\in D(j)}\mu_t r_t(S),\qquad S\subseteq G. \)

Each \(R_j\) is normalized, nonnegative, nondecreasing, and submodular.

Define a function on subsets of children by

\[ F(A)=\min_{S\subseteq G}\left[b(G\setminus S)+\sum_{j\in A}R_j(S)\right],\qquad A\subseteq J. \tag{2} \]

We prove that the attainable child utility vectors are exactly \(P(F)\), including those arising from real reallocations.

3.1. The function \(F\) is a polymatroid rank function

Nonnegativity is immediate. Taking \(S=G\) shows \(F(\emptyset)=0\). Since every \(R_j\) is nonnegative, enlarging \(A\) cannot decrease \(F(A)\).

For submodularity, choose minimizers \(S_A,S_B\) in (2). Monotonicity and submodularity of the \(R_j\) imply

\( \sum_{j\in A}R_j(S_A)+\sum_{j\in B}R_j(S_B) \geq \sum_{j\in A\cup B}R_j(S_A\cap S_B)+ \sum_{j\in A\cap B}R_j(S_A\cup S_B). \)

Indeed, a child appearing in only one of \(A,B\) contributes an inequality from monotonicity; a child appearing in both contributes its submodularity inequality.

Also,

\( b(G\setminus S_A)+b(G\setminus S_B) = b(G\setminus(S_A\cap S_B))+b(G\setminus(S_A\cup S_B)). \)

Adding these inequalities and using the definition of \(F\) gives

\( F(A)+F(B)\geq F(A\cup B)+F(A\cap B). \)

Hence \(F\) is normalized, nondecreasing, and submodular.

3.2. Every attainable vector belongs to \(P(F)\)

Let \(z\) satisfy (1), and let

\( v_j=\sum_{t\in D(j)}z_t(G). \)

For every \(A\subseteq J\) and \(S\subseteq G\), splitting consumption between \(S\) and its complement gives

\( v(A)\leq\sum_{j\in A}R_j(S)+b(G\setminus S). \)

Taking the minimum over \(S\) proves \(v(A)\leq F(A)\).

By the independent-bundle reduction, this also applies to the utility vector of every unrestricted real reallocation.

3.3. Every vector in \(P(F)\) is attainable

We use the polymatroid intersection theorem:

For normalized, nondecreasing, submodular functions \(h,k\) on a finite ground set \(E\),

\[ \max\{z(E):z\in P(h)\cap P(k)\} = \min_{X\subseteq E}\bigl[h(X)+k(E\setminus X)\bigr]. \tag{3} \]

The theorem applies to real-valued functions; see Theorem 7 in [Cunningham’s account](https://ems.press/content/book-chapter-files/27359).

Let \(v\in P(F)\). If \(D(i)\) is empty, then \(F\) is identically zero and the claim is immediate. Otherwise set \(E=D(i)\times G\). For \(X\subseteq E\), write \(X_t=\{g:(t,g)\in X\}\), and define

\( H_j(X)=\sum_{t\in D(j)}\mu_t r_t(X_t), \)

\( h_v(X)=\sum_{j\in J}\min\{v_j,H_j(X)\}, \)

\( k(X)=\sum_{\{g:\exists t,\ (t,g)\in X\}}b_g. \)

These functions satisfy the hypotheses of (3). For \(h_v\), this uses the fact that truncating a nondecreasing submodular function at a nonnegative constant preserves these properties. Explicitly, its marginal increment is the minimum of the original marginal increment and the remaining amount below the truncation threshold; both decrease as the underlying set grows. The function \(k\) is a nonnegative weighted coverage function.

Furthermore, \(P(h_v)\) is exactly the set of nonnegative marginals satisfying the type constraints in (1) and the additional bounds

\( \sum_{t\in D(j)}z_t(G)\leq v_j\quad(j\in J). \)

To verify the reverse inclusion, for each child the mass on any subset is bounded both by \(H_j(X)\) and by \(v_j\); summing gives the \(h_v\) inequalities. The forward inclusion follows by testing subsets involving one type and then the full ground set of one child. Similarly, \(P(k)\) is exactly the set satisfying the category-capacity constraints.

For \(X\subseteq E\), let

\( S(X)=\{g:(t,g)\in X\text{ for every }t\in D(i)\}. \)

Then \(k(E\setminus X)=b(G\setminus S(X))\), and monotonicity gives

\( h_v(X)\geq\sum_{j\in J}\min\{v_j,R_j(S(X))\}. \)

Equality is attained by taking \(X=D(i)\times S\). Thus (3) yields

\[ \max_{z\in P(h_v)\cap P(k)}z(E) = \min_{S\subseteq G}\left[b(G\setminus S)+\sum_{j\in J}\min\{v_j,R_j(S)\}\right]. \tag{4} \]

For a fixed \(S\), put \(A_S=\{j:v_j>R_j(S)\}\). Since \(v\in P(F)\),

\( v(A_S)\leq F(A_S)\leq b(G\setminus S)+\sum_{j\in A_S}R_j(S). \)

Rearranging gives

\( b(G\setminus S)+\sum_{j\in J}\min\{v_j,R_j(S)\}\geq v(J). \)

Hence the maximum in (4) is at least \(v(J)\). The child bounds make it at most \(v(J)\). A maximizer therefore attains equality in every child bound, realizing \(v\).

Combining the preceding parts proves the exact characterization

\[ \boxed{\text{Attainable child utility vectors under capacities }b=P(F).} \tag{5} \]

No fairness restriction was imposed on the competing allocations.

4. A computable vector that Lorenz-dominates an entire polymatroid

We give the necessary Lorenz argument directly.

Lemma. Let \(h\) be a normalized, nondecreasing, submodular function on a finite ground set \(E\). There exists \(v\in P(h)\) such that

\[ v(E)=h(E),\qquad \mathcal L_k(v)\geq\mathcal L_k(w)\quad(w\in P(h),\ 0\leq k\leq|E|). \tag{6} \]

For rational \(h\) with polynomial-time evaluation and polynomially bounded value encoding length, such a vector is computable in polynomial time.

4.1. Construction and feasibility

For nonempty \(E\), define

\( \lambda=\min_{\emptyset\neq A\subseteq E}\frac{h(A)}{|A|}. \)

Choose \(B\) to be the union of all minimizing sets. This union is itself minimizing: the function \(h(A)-\lambda|A|\) is nonnegative and submodular, so its zero sets are closed under union and intersection.

Assign \(v_e=\lambda\) for \(e\in B\). On \(E\setminus B\), continue recursively with the contracted function

\( (h/B)(A)=h(A\cup B)-h(B). \)

Contraction preserves normalization, monotonicity, and submodularity.

To verify feasibility inductively, suppose \(v'\in P(h/B)\). For any \(A\subseteq E\), submodularity gives

\( h(A)\geq h(A\cap B)+h(A\cup B)-h(B). \)

The definition of \(\lambda\) and feasibility of \(v'\) imply

\( h(A\cap B)\geq\lambda|A\cap B|,\qquad v'(A\setminus B)\leq h(A\cup B)-h(B). \)

Therefore \(v(A)\leq h(A)\). Its total is

\( v(E)=\lambda|B|+(h/B)(E\setminus B)=h(B)+h(E)-h(B)=h(E). \)

The empty ground set is the induction’s base case.

4.2. Increasing levels and tight prefixes

The successive assigned levels are strictly increasing. Indeed, for nonempty \(A\subseteq E\setminus B\),

\( (h/B)(A)=h(A\cup B)-h(B)\geq\lambda|A|. \)

Equality would make \(A\cup B\) another minimizing set, contradicting the maximality of \(B\). Thus the next minimum density is strictly greater than \(\lambda\).

Let the successive blocks be \(B_1,\ldots,B_s\), and put \(U_a=B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_a\). The construction gives

\[ v(U_a)=h(U_a). \tag{7} \]

Since levels increase, \(U_a\) consists of the \(|U_a|\) smallest coordinates of \(v\).

4.3. Lorenz dominance

For any \(w\in P(h)\), at each block boundary,

\[ \mathcal L_{|U_a|}(w)\leq w(U_a)\leq h(U_a)=\mathcal L_{|U_a|}(v). \tag{8} \]

The sequence \(\mathcal L_k(w)\) has nondecreasing increments, because those increments are the sorted coordinates of \(w\). Its linear interpolation is therefore convex and lies below the chord between any two block boundaries.

Between consecutive boundaries, \(\mathcal L_k(v)\) is precisely that linear chord, because \(v\) is constant on the intervening block. Inequality (8) consequently implies \(\mathcal L_k(w)\leq\mathcal L_k(v)\) at every intermediate integer \(k\).

This proves (6), including dominance over vectors with smaller totals.

4.4. Polynomial-time computation

For any current contracted function \(h\), compute \(\lambda\) by solving the scalar linear program

\[ \max\lambda\quad\text{subject to}\quad \lambda\geq0,\quad |A|\lambda\leq h(A)\quad(A\subseteq E). \tag{9} \]

Separation reduces to minimizing the submodular function \(h(A)-\lambda|A|\). Rational submodular minimization is polynomial-time computable from a value oracle, as established by [Iwata, Fleischer, and Fujishige](https://www.opt.mist.i.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~iwata/papers/sfm.pdf).

After obtaining \(\lambda\), determine whether an element \(e\) belongs to \(B\) by minimizing \(h(A)-\lambda|A|\) over sets containing \(e\). This is another submodular minimization, obtained by fixing \(e\). Its minimum is zero exactly when \(e\) lies in a minimizing set.

There are at most \(|E|\) stages and polynomially many minimizations. Exact solution of (9) follows from rational linear optimization with a strong separation oracle, using the [separation–optimization theorem](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf). Encoding bounds are verified below.

5. The sequential hierarchy algorithm

At an internal node \(i\) with capacities \(b_i\):

  • Form \(F_i\) using (2), with children \(C(i)\) and capacities \(b_i\).
  • Compute the vector \(\alpha^i\) from the lemma. It maximizes total utility and Lorenz-dominates every vector in \(P(F_i)\).
  • Find feasible marginals \(z^i\) realizing \(\alpha^i\), using (1) and the equalities

\( \sum_{t\in D(j)}z^i_t(G)=\alpha^i_j\quad(j\in C(i)). \)

  • For each child \(j\), define its capacities and retain its marginal witness:

\( b_{j,g}=\sum_{t\in D(j)}z^i_{t,g}. \)
Recurse if \(j\) is internal; if it is a leaf, keep the witness.

Start at the root with \(b_r=q\).

The crucial invariant is that every nonroot child receives a witness whose utility is exactly the total mass of its capacities:

\[ \alpha^i_j=\sum_{t\in D(j)}z^i_t(G)=b_j(G). \tag{10} \]

Under these capacities, no reallocation in that child can achieve utility greater than \(b_j(G)\), since \(r_t(S)\leq|S|\). The retained witness attains this bound.

Consequently, when an internal child is processed, its maximum total utility is \(b_j(G)\). Its newly computed independent-set marginals satisfy

\( \sum_{t\in D(j)}z_t(G)=b_j(G),\qquad \sum_{t\in D(j)}z_{t,g}\leq b_{j,g}. \)

Every category bound must therefore be tight: the nonnegative category slacks sum to zero. Thus refining the child preserves its entire capacity vector, as well as its total utility.

Inductively, all further refinements preserve the seat vectors and utility profiles already chosen at ancestors.

At termination, let \(z^\star\) be the retained marginals at all leaves. For every nonroot node \(i\),

\[ \sum_{t\in D(i)}z^\star_{t,g}=b_{i,g}. \tag{11} \]

The final child utilities at every internal node equal its selected vector \(\alpha^i\). Global seat use remains at most \(q\).

Now fix an internal node \(i\) and any competing real reallocation \(y\) allowed by the question. Its capacities are exactly those used when \(i\) was processed: this follows from (11), or from \(b_r=q\) at the root. By (5), its child utility vector belongs to \(P(F_i)\). Therefore

\( V_i(x)\geq V_i(y),\qquad \mathcal L_k\bigl((V_j(x))_{j\in C(i)}\bigr) \geq \mathcal L_k\bigl((V_j(y))_{j\in C(i)}\bigr) \)

for every applicable \(k\).

These are precisely the required conditions.

6. Exact computation, encoding bounds, and sparse output

6.1. Evaluation and marginal recovery

An evaluation of \(F_i(A)\) is a submodular minimization on \(G\), since its objective is

\( b_i(G)-b_i(S)+\sum_{j\in A}\sum_{t\in D(j)}\mu_t r_t(S). \)

The first two terms are modular in \(S\); the remaining terms are a nonnegative sum of matroid ranks. Each evaluation uses polynomially many circuit operations in the supplied input length. Hence \(F_i\), and every contraction used in Section 4, has a polynomial-time value oracle.

Recovering \(z^i\) is a rational feasibility problem in at most \(m\tau\) variables. Its only exponentially numerous constraints are

\( z_t(S)\leq\mu_t r_t(S). \)

For each type, separation is the submodular minimization of

\( \mu_t r_t(S)-z_t(S). \)

The category bounds, child utility equalities, and nonnegativity constraints are explicit. Thus exact rational linear optimization with separation computes a feasible vertex. The polytope is bounded because \(z_{t,g}\leq\mu_t r_t(\{g\})\leq\mu_t\).

6.2. Polynomial bit complexity throughout the tree

Maintain a common denominator \(D\) for the original masses, original supplies, and all capacity vectors already constructed. Initially, the product of the input denominators suffices, and its logarithm is bounded by the input length.

At a particular node, every value \(F_i(A)\) is a multiple of \(D^{-1}\): it is the minimum of finitely many numbers with that common denominator. The same holds for contracted values.

Each assigned Lorenz level has the form

\( \lambda_a=\frac{F_i(U_a)-F_i(U_{a-1})}{|B_a|}. \)

Therefore all coordinates of \(\alpha^i\) have common denominator dividing

\( D\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,|C(i)|). \)

For marginal recovery, the defining constraint coefficients belong to \(\{0,1,-1\}\). If there are \(d\leq m\tau\) variables, Cramer’s rule shows that a vertex denominator divides the current right-hand-side denominator times the determinant of a nonsingular active constraint matrix. Hadamard’s bound gives

\( |\det A|\leq d^{d/2}. \)

Thus each node increases the logarithm of a sufficient common denominator by at most

\( O\bigl(n\log(n+1)+m\tau\log(m\tau+1)\bigr). \)

There are at most \(n\) nodes. All constructed rational numbers therefore have polynomial encoding length. Numerators also have polynomial length: marginal coordinates are at most one, utilities are at most \(m\), and original supplies have their given encoding lengths.

These bounds verify the rational complexity hypotheses for all separation and optimization calls. In particular, the construction does not expand binary masses or supplies into individual agents or seats.

6.3. Constructing the listed bundle allocation

For each type \(t\), put \(p_t=z^\star_t/\mu_t\). Then \(p_t\in P(r_t)\).

A polynomial-time constructive decomposition into independent-set incidence vectors is available from an independence oracle; see [Cunningham, *Testing membership in matroid polyhedra*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0095895684900236). Here independence is tested by evaluating the supplied circuit and checking \(r_t(I)=|I|\).

Compute a rational convex decomposition

\( p_t=\sum_a\lambda_{t,a}\mathbf1_{I_{t,a}},\qquad \lambda_{t,a}\geq0,\qquad \sum_a\lambda_{t,a}=1. \)

Affine-dependence elimination reduces its support to at most \(m+1\) sets: whenever more remain, an affine dependence permits adjusting the coefficients while preserving the represented point and their sum, until one coefficient becomes zero. Repeating requires polynomially many rational linear-algebra operations.

Set

\( x_{t,I_{t,a}}=\mu_t\lambda_{t,a}, \)

merging duplicate entries and omitting zero entries. This preserves every marginal coordinate and, because all listed bundles are independent, every utility. The output has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) nonzero entries and polynomial total encoding length.

Thus the exact computation problem in the question is polynomial-time solvable.
