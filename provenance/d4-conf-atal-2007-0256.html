Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). Under the stated acyclicity assumption, the inventory balance inequalities are sufficient for a finite execution schedule. The allocation and revenue requirements therefore form an exact rational linear program.

1. Acyclicity makes the balance inequalities sufficient

Let \(T\) be the set of all listed transformation occurrences. For an occurrence \(v=(t,b,k)\), write \(I_v=I_{tbk}\), \(O_v=O_{tbk}\), and prescribe its total execution mass as \(y_v=x_{tb}\).

Lemma. Suppose \(y_v\ge 0\) for every occurrence and
\[ \text{} \]
\(F:=u_{\mathrm{in}}+\sum_{v\in T}y_v(O_v-I_v)\ge 0\).
Then these masses have a valid execution schedule containing at most \(K\) steps.

Proof. Take a topological ordering of the given directed bipartite graph, and list its occurrence nodes in their induced order as \(v_1,\ldots,v_K\). We will execute each occurrence of positive prescribed mass once, with that entire mass.

Proceed by induction through this list. Immediately before occurrence \(v_j\), the inventory is

\(M_j=u_{\mathrm{in}}+\sum_{i<j}y_{v_i}(O_{v_i}-I_{v_i})\).

The induction hypothesis says that preceding executions were valid, so \(M_j\ge 0\).

Fix a good \(g\) with \(I_{v_j}(g)>0\). Every occurrence \(v_i\) producing \(g\) has a directed path

\(v_i\longrightarrow g\longrightarrow v_j\).

Consequently, every such producer precedes \(v_j\). In particular, no occurrence with index \(i\ge j\) produces \(g\). This also excludes production of \(g\) by \(v_j\) itself, which would create a directed cycle.

All production of \(g\) is therefore already included in \(M_j(g)\), giving

\(M_j(g)=u_{\mathrm{in}}(g)+\sum_{i=1}^{K}y_{v_i}O_{v_i}(g)-\sum_{i<j}y_{v_i}I_{v_i}(g)\)

\(=F(g)+\sum_{i\ge j}y_{v_i}I_{v_i}(g)\ge y_{v_j}I_{v_j}(g)\).

For a good with \(I_{v_j}(g)=0\), the required inequality follows from \(M_j(g)\ge 0\). Thus, whenever \(y_{v_j}>0\), the whole prescribed mass is enabled. Its execution leaves a nonnegative inventory because

\(M_j-y_{v_j}I_{v_j}+y_{v_j}O_{v_j}\ge 0\).

When \(y_{v_j}=0\), omit the step. This completes the induction. Every occurrence receives exactly its prescribed mass, and the final inventory is \(F\). There are at most \(K\) positive-mass steps. \(\square\)

Conversely, summing the inventory changes in any valid schedule gives exactly \(F\). Hence, because \(u_{\mathrm{out}}\ge 0\), prescribed masses admit a schedule with final inventory at least \(u_{\mathrm{out}}\) if and only if

\(u_{\mathrm{in}}+\sum_{v\in T}y_v(O_v-I_v)\ge u_{\mathrm{out}}\).

The lemma applies directly to real masses; no discretization or limiting argument is required.

2. Exact linear-programming formulation

For each package and good, define the integer coefficient

\(a_{g,tb}:=\sum_k\bigl(O_{tbk}(g)-I_{tbk}(g)\bigr)\).

Consider the following rational linear feasibility problem, with one variable \(x_{tb}\) per listed package:

  • \(x_{tb}\ge 0\) for every package;
  • \(\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_t}x_{tb}\le\mu_t\) for every listed type \(t\);
  • \(\sum_{t,b}a_{g,tb}x_{tb}\ge u_{\mathrm{out}}(g)-u_{\mathrm{in}}(g)\) for every good \(g\);
  • \(\sum_{t,b}p_{tb}x_{tb}\ge R\).

This system has \(B\) variables and \(B+\tau+m+1\) inequalities, counting nonnegativity.

Exactness. Any allocation satisfying the original requirements satisfies these inequalities: the capacity and revenue inequalities are unchanged, and summing schedule updates gives the inventory inequalities.

Conversely, from any feasible point of this system, prescribe \(y_{tbk}=x_{tb}\) for every occurrence. The inventory inequalities and the lemma produce a valid finite schedule. Every occurrence of each purchased package executes exactly its required mass. The allocation also satisfies the type capacities and revenue threshold.

Thus the linear system is feasible exactly when the answer to the original decision problem is yes. Signed prices cause no difficulty: they are simply rational coefficients in the revenue inequality.

3. Running time and exact arithmetic

Set \(H=L+\lceil\log_2(K+2)\rceil\).

Each coefficient \(a_{g,tb}\) is a sum of at most \(K\) differences of binary-encoded integers, so its bit length is \(O(H)\). The rational right-hand sides, masses, prices, and threshold also have bit length \(O(H)\), including after the inventory subtraction.

A conservative dense encoding of the resulting linear system has length

\(N=O\bigl((B+m+\tau+1)(B+1)H\bigr)\).

The graph has \(m+K\) vertices and at most \(2mK\) arcs. Constructing and topologically sorting it takes \(O(m+K+mK)\) graph operations. Aggregating package coefficients takes \(O(mK)\) integer additions or subtractions on \(O(H)\)-bit integers. Including rational subtraction and encoding, a conservative preprocessing bound is

\(O\bigl((m+K+mK+\tau+B+1)H^2\bigr)\)

bit operations.

The standard rational linear-programming theorem decides feasibility exactly in time polynomial in the encoded system length, and can return a rational feasible point when one exists. Its hypotheses hold here: the system is finite, explicitly encoded, rational, and contains only non-strict linear inequalities. This includes feasibility on the exact revenue boundary. See [Khachiyan, *Polynomial algorithms in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

Consequently, the total decision time is bounded by

\(O\bigl((m+K+mK+\tau+B+1)H^2\bigr)+N^{O(1)}\),

and hence by

\((m+\tau+B+K+L+1)^{O(1)}\)

bit operations. A feasible allocation and a schedule of at most \(K\) steps can also be produced in polynomial time.

4. Why discrete XOR hardness does not transfer

The discrete XOR model permits at most one whole package per bidder; this is the model whose winner determination is NP-complete in [Ottens and Endriss](https://eprints.illc.uva.nl/id/eprint/287/1/PP-2008-13.text.pdf).

Here, different portions of a type may accept different packages. Its allocation restriction is therefore the linear capacity inequality \(\sum_bx_{tb}\le\mu_t\). Package coupling is retained by using the same variable \(x_{tb}\) for every occurrence in that package. Acyclicity then supplies the schedule proved above. These two facts give the exact polynomial-time formulation.
