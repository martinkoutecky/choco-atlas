The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite companies \(C\), complete applicant types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), rankings \(r_{\theta j}\), scores \(s_{\theta j}\), category map \(g(\theta)\), capacities \(u_j\), and category lower quotas \(\ell_j^g\) satisfying \(\sum_j\ell_j^g\le\mu_g\), \(\sum_g\ell_j^g\le u_j\), and \(\sum_j u_j\ge1\), find or report infeasibility of an aggregate assignment \(x_{\theta j}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_jx_{\theta j}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta j}\le u_j\), \(\sum_{\theta:g(\theta)=g}x_{\theta j}\ge\ell_j^g\), and, whenever \(g(\theta)=g(\phi)\), \(s_{\theta j}>s_{\phi j}\), and \(x_{\phi j}>0\), \(\sum_{k:r_{\theta k}\le r_{\theta j}}x_{\theta k}=\mu_\theta\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) complete applicant types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), where \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) companies \(C\) and \(p\) represented categories, with category map \(g:\Theta\to\{1,\ldots,p\}\) and \(\mu_g=\sum_{\theta:g(\theta)=g}\mu_\theta\). Each type accepts every company and has a strict ranking given by a permutation \((r_{\theta j})_{j\in C}\) of \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with smaller ranks preferred; company scores \(s_{\theta j}\in\mathbb Z\) are binary encoded, with larger scores preferred and ties allowed without prescribed tie-breaking.
Capacities \(u_j\) and category lower quotas \(\ell_j^g\) are nonnegative binary rationals satisfying \(\sum_j\ell_j^g\le\mu_g\), \(\sum_g\ell_j^g\le u_j\), and \(\sum_j u_j\ge1\).
An aggregate assignment consists of divisible masses \(x_{\theta j}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_jx_{\theta j}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta j}\le u_j\), and \(\sum_{\theta:g(\theta)=g}x_{\theta j}\ge\ell_j^g\).
It is within-category envy-free if, for every \(\theta,\phi,j\), \(g(\theta)=g(\phi)\), \(s_{\theta j}>s_{\phi j}\), and \(x_{\phi j}>0\) imply \(\sum_{k:r_{\theta k}\le r_{\theta j}}x_{\theta k}=\mu_\theta\).
Can a rational within-category envy-free aggregate assignment be found, or infeasibility reported, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,p,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, or is this computational problem NP-hard?
For indivisible applicants, a complete within-category envy-free matching exists and can be computed in polynomial time under these acceptability and quota assumptions, including sufficient total capacity ([Ágoston–Biró–Szántó](https://real.mtak.hu/74005/1/1_s2.0_S2214716018300022_main_u.pdf)).
For fractional assignments, a generalized probabilistic serial mechanism gives within-type envy-freeness in a model without company priorities, which differs from the score-based condition above ([Ashlagi–Saberi–Shameli](https://web.stanford.edu/~iashlagi/papers/final-paper.pdf)); no result settling the exact problem above was identified.

  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational capacities and lower quotas and a nonempty company set.

  • Specified rational assignment output for exact computation and defined L as total input bit length.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. Under the stated assumptions, a rational within-category envy-free aggregate assignment always exists.

The construction first fixes the total mass of each category at each company, then computes a stable allocation separately within each category.

1. Choose category capacities

We construct rational numbers \(q_j^g\) satisfying

\(\ell_j^g\le q_j^g,\qquad \sum_jq_j^g=\mu_g,\qquad \sum_gq_j^g\le u_j.\)

Start with \(q_j^g=\ell_j^g\). Define the unallocated category masses and residual company capacities by

\(a_g=\mu_g-\sum_j\ell_j^g,\qquad b_j=u_j-\sum_g\ell_j^g.\)

Both are nonnegative by hypothesis. Moreover,

\(\sum_jb_j=\sum_ju_j-\sum_{g,j}\ell_j^g\ge 1-\sum_{g,j}\ell_j^g=\sum_ga_g.\)

While some \(a_g>0\), choose a company with \(b_j>0\), set \(\delta=\min\{a_g,b_j\}\), and increase \(q_j^g\) by \(\delta\). Decrease both \(a_g\) and \(b_j\) by \(\delta\).

Such a company always exists: the inequality between the two residual totals is preserved, so positive residual demand implies positive residual capacity. Every iteration exhausts a category’s residual demand or a company’s residual capacity. Consequently, this procedure terminates after at most \(p+m\) iterations.

The resulting numbers \(q_j^g\) have all three required properties.

2. The stable allocation theorem used

A bipartite allocation instance has left vertices \(I\), right vertices \(J\), positive vertex quotas \(a_i,b_j\), edge capacities \(c_{ij}\), and strict preferences over incident neighbors.

An allocation \(y\) satisfies

\(0\le y_{ij}\le c_{ij},\qquad \sum_jy_{ij}\le a_i,\qquad \sum_iy_{ij}\le b_j.\)

Write \(k\succeq_i j\) when \(i\) ranks \(k\) at least as highly as \(j\), and analogously for preferences at right vertices. Stability means that every edge with \(y_{ij}<c_{ij}\) satisfies at least one of

\(\sum_{k:k\succeq_i j}y_{ik}=a_i,\qquad \sum_{h:h\succeq_j i}y_{hj}=b_j.\)

Otherwise, both endpoints could increase their allocation on that edge, using unused quota or replacing allocation to worse partners. This is the standard stable allocation condition. [Dean–Munshi, Section 2](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf)

Stable allocation theorem. Every such bipartite instance admits a stable allocation, computable by a strongly polynomial algorithm. We use Baïou–Balinski’s inductive algorithm; their corrected paper establishes its polynomial bound in Theorem 2 and existence for real-valued data in Corollary 1. [Baïou–Balinski, corrected paper, Section 2](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302)

3. Apply the theorem within each category

Fix a category \(g\), and let \(\Theta_g=\{\theta:g(\theta)=g\}\). Construct an instance as follows:

  • The left vertices are the supported types \(\theta\in\Theta_g\), with quotas \(\mu_\theta\).
  • The right vertices are the companies with \(q_j^g>0\), with quotas \(q_j^g\).
  • Every left vertex is connected to every right vertex, with edge capacity \(1\).
  • Each type uses its given company ranking, restricted to these companies.
  • Each company ranks types by decreasing \(s_{\theta j}\), breaking score ties by any fixed ordering of the supported types.

All theorem hypotheses hold. Left quotas are positive by assumption; right quotas are positive because zero-quota companies were omitted. The preferences are strict after tie refinement, and all edge capacities are positive. The edge capacity \(1\) imposes no additional restriction because \(\mu_\theta\le1\).

Compute a stable allocation \(y^g\). For omitted companies, set \(y^g_{\theta j}=0\).

Every type and every category capacity is fully allocated

The two sides have equal total quota:

\(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_g}\mu_\theta=\mu_g=\sum_jq_j^g.\)

Suppose some type \(\theta\) is underfilled. The total allocated mass is then less than \(\mu_g\). Since the total company quota also equals \(\mu_g\), some company \(j\) is underfilled.

This company has positive quota and hence is connected to \(\theta\). Furthermore,

\(y^g_{\theta j}\le\sum_ky^g_{\theta k}<\mu_\theta\le1,\)

so its edge capacity is not exhausted. But

\(\sum_{k:k\succeq_\theta j}y^g_{\theta k}<\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_{\phi:\phi\succeq_j\theta}y^g_{\phi j}<q_j^g,\)

because each sum is bounded above by the corresponding underfilled vertex’s total allocation. This contradicts stability.

Thus every type receives its full mass. The total allocated mass is consequently \(\mu_g\); because company allocations are bounded by quotas whose sum is also \(\mu_g\), every company quota is filled as well. Therefore,

\(\sum_jy^g_{\theta j}=\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_{\theta\in\Theta_g}y^g_{\theta j}=q_j^g.\)

4. Combine the allocations and prove envy-freeness

Define \(x_{\theta j}=y^{g(\theta)}_{\theta j}\).

The preceding equalities and the construction of \(q\) give

\(\sum_jx_{\theta j}=\mu_\theta,\)

\(\sum_\theta x_{\theta j}=\sum_gq_j^g\le u_j,\)

and

\(\sum_{\theta:g(\theta)=g}x_{\theta j}=q_j^g\ge\ell_j^g.\)

Hence \(x\) is a feasible aggregate assignment.

To prove the exact envy condition, suppose that

\(g(\theta)=g(\phi)=g,\qquad s_{\theta j}>s_{\phi j},\qquad x_{\phi j}>0.\)

Assume, for a contradiction, that

\(\sum_{k:r_{\theta k}\le r_{\theta j}}x_{\theta k}<\mu_\theta.\)

Since \(\theta\)’s total allocation is \(\mu_\theta\), it receives positive mass at a company worse than \(j\). In particular,

\(x_{\theta j}<\mu_\theta\le1,\)

so the edge from \(\theta\) to \(j\) is not saturated.

The strict score inequality ensures that \(j\)’s refined ranking places \(\theta\) above \(\phi\), regardless of how ties were broken. Because \(x_{\phi j}>0\) and the category quota at \(j\) is filled,

\(\sum_{\psi\in\Theta_g:\psi\succeq_j\theta}x_{\psi j} \le q_j^g-x_{\phi j}<q_j^g.\)

Thus neither endpoint satisfies the stability equality for the edge from \(\theta\) to \(j\). This contradicts stability of \(y^g\).

We conclude that

\(\sum_{k:r_{\theta k}\le r_{\theta j}}x_{\theta k}=\mu_\theta,\)

as required. Arbitrary tie refinement is therefore valid for the original condition, which compares only strictly different scores.

5. Polynomial bit complexity and rational output

The construction uses only the supported types. Across all category instances there are at most \(m\tau\) edges and \(\tau+pm\) vertices. Constructing the category capacities, sorting the company rankings, and running the strongly polynomial stable allocation algorithm therefore require polynomially many operations in \(m,\tau,p\).

For exact arithmetic, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses, capacities, and lower quotas. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). The greedy construction uses addition, subtraction, and minima, so every \(q_j^g\) is an integer multiple of \(1/D\).

Scale each stable allocation instance by \(D\). Its vertex quotas and edge capacities are now integers bounded by \(D\). Baïou–Balinski’s inductive algorithm uses sums, differences, comparisons, and minimum residual quantities, preserving integrality. [Corrected paper, Section 2](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302) Its allocation entries remain bounded by \(D\); sums over polynomially many entries also have polynomial bit length. Thus its polynomial arithmetic-operation bound yields polynomial bit complexity.

Dividing the computed allocation entries by \(D\) produces the required rational assignment, with polynomial output length.

Therefore the stated search problem is polynomial-time solvable, and no promised instance is infeasible.
