The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(g\) commodities, \(\tau\) complete economic types, rational masses \(\mu_t\ge0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational linear utilities \(u_{tj}\), and rational per-capita endowments \(e_{tj}\), compute a normalized exact equilibrium \(p\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^g\) and aggregate money flows \(f_{tj}\), where \(Q_j=\sum_t\mu_t e_{tj}\), \(\sum_t f_{tj}=p_jQ_j\), \(\sum_j f_{tj}=\mu_t\sum_j e_{tj}p_j\), and \(f_{tj}>0\) only when \(u_{tj}/p_j\) is maximal for type \(t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=g\ge1\) divisible commodities; each type specifies nonnegative rational linear-utility coefficients \(u_{tj}\) and per-capita endowments \(e_{tj}\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Assume \(\sum_j e_{tj}>0\) and \(\max_j u_{tj}>0\) for every type, and \(Q_j:=\sum_t\mu_t e_{tj}>0\) and \(\max_t u_{tj}>0\) for every commodity. For every nonempty proper subset \(S\) of types, require types \(t\in S\), \(s\notin S\) and a commodity \(j\) with \(u_{tj}>0\) and \(e_{sj}>0\).
Compute an exact equilibrium consisting of \(p\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^{m}\), normalized by \(\min_j p_j=1\), and aggregate money flows \(f\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{\tau\times m}\), satisfying \(\sum_t f_{tj}=p_jQ_j\), \(\sum_j f_{tj}=\mu_t\sum_j e_{tj}p_j\), and \(f_{tj}>0\Rightarrow u_{tj}/p_j=\max_k u_{tk}/p_k\). Spending may be split arbitrarily among maximizing commodities.

Is this exact-equilibrium computation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite markets with one unit of each good and one distinct owner per good, exact equilibrium prices can be computed in \(O(n^7\log^3(nU))\) time for integer utilities bounded by \(U\), including degenerate utilities ([Duan–Garg–Mehlhorn](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.02694)).
For finite markets with arbitrary nonnegative integral endowments, a polynomial-time exact-equilibrium algorithm is also known ([Chaudhury–Mehlhorn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.01237)); no separately formulated support-and-mass theorem was located.

Result

The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). The support representation reduces directly to a finite linear exchange market with exactly \(\tau\) agents. Clearing denominators preserves polynomial input length, and an exact equilibrium can then be computed using the arbitrary-endowment theorem.

1. Replace each type by one agent

Define the aggregate endowment of type \(t\) by
\[ w_{tj}:=\mu_t e_{tj}. \]

Consider a finite exchange market with \(\tau\) agents and \(m\) goods, where agent \(t\) has endowment \(w_t\) and utility coefficients \(u_{tj}\). Its total supply of commodity \(j\) is
\[ \sum_t w_{tj}=\sum_t\mu_t e_{tj}=Q_j. \]

At prices \(p\), agent \(t\)'s budget is
\[ \sum_j w_{tj}p_j=\mu_t\sum_j e_{tj}p_j. \]

Consequently, its equilibrium conditions in terms of money flows are exactly the conditions in the question. This replacement introduces no additional agents and imposes no restriction on splitting spending among maximizing commodities.

2. Clear denominators with polynomial bit length

Write the input rationals as
\[ \mu_t=\frac{a_t}{b_t},\qquad e_{tj}=\frac{c_{tj}}{d_{tj}},\qquad u_{tj}=\frac{r_{tj}}{h_{tj}}, \]
with positive integer denominators and nonnegative integer numerators. For zero entries, a denominator of one may be used.

Define
\[ D:=\left(\prod_t b_t\right) \left(\prod_{t,j}d_{tj}\right), \qquad A_t:=\prod_j h_{tj}. \]

Construct an integer market by setting
\[ \widehat w_{tj}:=D\mu_t e_{tj}, \qquad \widehat u_{tj}:=A_tu_{tj}. \]

These entries are nonnegative integers: \(b_td_{tj}\) divides \(D\), and \(h_{tj}\) divides \(A_t\).

The logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms of its factors. Since all these denominators are explicitly included in the input,
\[ \log_2 D=O(L), \qquad \log_2 A_t=O(L). \]
Every input numerator also has at most \(L\) bits. Thus every entry of \(\widehat w\) and \(\widehat u\) has \(O(L)\) bits. In particular, with
\[ W:=\max_{t,j}\widehat w_{tj}, \qquad U:=\max_{t,j}\widehat u_{tj}, \]
we have
\[ \log_2(1+W)+\log_2(1+U)=O(L). \]

The integer market therefore has polynomial encoding length and can be constructed using polynomial-time integer arithmetic. The numerical values of \(D,U,W\) may be large; their binary lengths are polynomial.

The two scalings have distinct, harmless effects:

  • Multiplying every endowment by \(D\) multiplies every budget and every commodity supply by \(D\).
  • Multiplying agent \(t\)'s utility coefficients by \(A_t>0\) preserves that agent's maximizing commodities at every positive price vector.

3. Apply the finite-market theorem

The known result used here is the following: for a finite linear exchange market with nonnegative integer utilities and endowments, positive endowment for every agent, positive supply of every good, every agent liking some good, every good liked by some agent, and the stated irreducibility condition, exact positive rational equilibrium prices can be computed in time polynomial in the numbers of agents and goods and in \(\log(1+U),\log(1+W)\). Chaudhury–Mehlhorn give the general-endowment algorithm, exact extraction for the original utilities, and a polynomial-bit implementation; thus degeneracy does not require an additional assumption. See Sections 1, 2.2.5, and 2.3 of [*Combinatorial Algorithms for General Linear Arrow-Debreu Markets*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.01237).

We verify the hypotheses for the constructed market.

First, for each agent \(t\),
\[ \sum_j\widehat w_{tj} =D\mu_t\sum_j e_{tj}>0, \qquad \max_j\widehat u_{tj} =A_t\max_j u_{tj}>0. \]

Second, for each commodity \(j\),
\[ \sum_t\widehat w_{tj}=DQ_j>0. \]
Since the factors \(A_t\) are positive, \(\max_tu_{tj}>0\) also implies \(\max_t\widehat u_{tj}>0\).

Finally, let \(S\) be any nonempty proper subset of agents. The input promise supplies \(t\in S\), \(s\notin S\), and \(j\) such that \(u_{tj}>0\) and \(e_{sj}>0\). Therefore
\[ \widehat u_{tj}=A_tu_{tj}>0, \qquad \widehat w_{sj}=D\mu_s e_{sj}>0. \]
This is precisely the required irreducibility condition.

All hypotheses hold. The theorem therefore computes exact positive rational equilibrium prices \(q\), of polynomial bit length, in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

4. Recover an exact rational money flow

For completeness, prices alone suffice to compute the required flows.

For the integer market at prices \(q\), define budgets, commodity values, and their common total by
\[ B_t:=\sum_j\widehat w_{tj}q_j,\qquad C_j:=q_j\sum_t\widehat w_{tj},\qquad V:=\sum_tB_t=\sum_jC_j. \]

Build a directed network with a source, one vertex per agent, one vertex per commodity, and a sink. Include:

  • A source-to-agent edge of capacity \(B_t\).
  • An agent-to-commodity edge of capacity \(V\) whenever

\[ \frac{\widehat u_{tj}}{q_j} =\max_k\frac{\widehat u_{tk}}{q_k}. \]

  • A commodity-to-sink edge of capacity \(C_j\).

Because \(q\) is an equilibrium price vector, there exists an equilibrium spending flow using these maximizing edges. Every individual edge flow is at most \(V\), so this gives a network flow of value \(V\). Conversely, the total capacity leaving the source is \(V\), so the maximum-flow value is exactly \(V\).

Compute such a maximum flow and let \(F_{tj}\) denote its agent-to-commodity flows. Since its value equals both the total source capacity and the total sink capacity, every source and sink edge is saturated. Hence
\[ \sum_jF_{tj}=B_t,\qquad \sum_tF_{tj}=C_j, \]
and positive \(F_{tj}\) occurs only on maximizing edges.

This computation is polynomial in bit complexity. The network has \(\tau+m+2\) vertices and at most \(\tau m+\tau+m\) edges. Its capacities are rationals of polynomial bit length. Multiplying by the product of their denominators produces integer capacities of polynomial bit length; an exact polynomial-time maximum-flow algorithm followed by division by that product produces rational \(F\) of polynomial bit length.

5. Convert and normalize the output

Set
\[ a:=\min_jq_j>0,\qquad p_j:=\frac{q_j}{a},\qquad f_{tj}:=\frac{F_{tj}}{Da}. \]

Then \(p\) is positive and rational, \(f\) is nonnegative and rational, and \(\min_jp_j=1\).

For each commodity,
\[ \sum_tf_{tj} =\frac{q_j\sum_t\widehat w_{tj}}{Da} =\frac{q_jDQ_j}{Da} =p_jQ_j. \]

For each type,
\[ \sum_jf_{tj} =\frac{\sum_j\widehat w_{tj}q_j}{Da} =\mu_t\sum_je_{tj}p_j. \]

Finally, if \(f_{tj}>0\), then \(F_{tj}>0\), so \(j\) maximizes \(\widehat u_{tk}/q_k\). For every \(k\),
\[ \frac{u_{tk}}{p_k} =\frac{a}{A_t}\frac{\widehat u_{tk}}{q_k}. \]
The multiplier \(a/A_t\) is positive and independent of \(k\). Thus \(j\) also maximizes \(u_{tk}/p_k\), as required.

All output conversions use polynomial-time rational arithmetic and preserve polynomial bit length. This proves the claimed polynomial-time algorithm for the full promised input class.
