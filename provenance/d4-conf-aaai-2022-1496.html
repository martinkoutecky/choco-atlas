The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), public budgets \(b_t\), per-unit values \(v_t\), reserve price \(v_s>0\), and per-capita supply \(q>0\), define \(D_\mu(p)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\,\mathbf{1}[p\le v_t]\left\lfloor b_t/p\right\rfloor\). Find the exact rational price \(p^\star\ge v_s\), if one exists, such that \(D_\mu(p^\star)>q\) and \(D_\mu(p^\star+\varepsilon)\le q\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\). Each unit of mass retains integral per-agent demand, while aggregate demand may be fractional.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T\): the list of \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a public budget \(b_t\) and a reported per-unit value \(v_t\), with \(0\le v_t\le b_t\). The reserve price \(v_s>0\) and per-capita supply \(q>0\) are inputs. All numerical inputs are binary rationals; \(L\) is their total encoding length together with the support list.
For real prices \(p\ge v_s\), define \(D_\mu(p)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\mathbf{1}[p\le v_t]\lfloor b_t/p\rfloor\). Individual demand is integral and uncapped; aggregate demand may be fractional. Equality with a valuation permits demand.
The computational problem is to return an exact rational \(p^\star\ge v_s\) satisfying \(D_\mu(p^\star)>q\) and \(D_\mu(p^\star+\varepsilon)\le q\) for every real \(\varepsilon>0\), or report that none exists.

Is this exact critical-price computation solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a finite society of \(n\) buyers and \(m\) identical units, with individual demand capped at \(m\), computing both kinds of critical prices has a stated \(O(mn^2)\) algorithm, polynomial in the numerical supply \(m\) ([Xiao, Song, and Khoussainov](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/20458/20458-13-24471-1-2-20220628.pdf)).
No result settling this exact rational-mass, uncapped-demand problem was verified.

  • Make budgets, values, reserve price, and supply binary rational inputs, and define L as the total input encoding length.
  • Interpret v_t as the reported valuation used in demand, impose the discrete model's restriction v_t <= b_t, and take budgets and valuations to be nonnegative.
  • Retain the graded question's explicitly uncapped individual demand. Per-capita supply q is the supply input; there is no separate total-unit parameter m in this normalized society.

Result

The exact critical-price computation is solvable in deterministic time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

The algorithm uses exact bisection to isolate the critical breakpoint, then recovers its rational value directly. The key is that, although there may be exponentially many breakpoints, their denominators have polynomially many bits.

1. Encoding and breakpoint bounds

Use the standard binary encoding of a rational as a numerator and a positive denominator written in binary. Write each numerical input as \(a/d\), and let \(h\ge 1\) be the maximum bit length of any such numerator or denominator. Thus \(h\le L\), every nonnegative input is less than \(2^h\), and every positive input is at least \(2^{-h}\).

Set \(U=2^h\). Then \(U>v_s\), \(U>v_t\) for every type, and \(D_\mu(U)=0\).

On the price domain \(p\ge v_s\), every demand change occurs at a point in the finite set

\(\displaystyle \mathcal C=\{v_t:t\in T,\ v_t\ge v_s\}\ \cup\ \bigcup_{t\in T}\left\{\frac{b_t}{k}:k\in\mathbb Z,\ 1\le k\le\left\lfloor\frac{b_t}{v_s}\right\rfloor\right\}.\)

Indeed, the valuation indicator changes immediately after \(v_t\), and the floor changes immediately after a price \(b_t/k\). Including points at which valuation restrictions suppress a floor change only enlarges this set.

For any integer \(k\) appearing here,

\(\displaystyle k\le \frac{b_t}{v_s}\le 2^{2h}.\)

If \(b_t=a_t/d_t\), the breakpoint \(b_t/k\) has the representation \(a_t/(d_tk)\), whose denominator is at most \(2^{3h}\). Valuation breakpoints have denominators at most \(2^h\). Consequently, every member of \(\mathcal C\) has a rational representation with denominator at most \(H=2^{3h}\).

For distinct rational numbers \(a/d\) and \(a'/d'\) with positive denominators at most \(H\),

\(\displaystyle \left|\frac ad-\frac{a'}{d'}\right|=\frac{|ad'-a'd|}{dd'}\ge\frac1{H^2}=2^{-6h},\)

because the nonzero integer \(ad'-a'd\) has absolute value at least \(1\).

Thus distinct candidate breakpoints are separated by at least \(\delta=2^{-6h}\). The algorithm will use this bound without enumerating \(\mathcal C\).

2. Existence and uniqueness

Each summand of \(D_\mu(p)\) is nonincreasing for positive prices. Therefore \(D_\mu\) is nonincreasing.

If \(D_\mu(v_s)\le q\), then \(D_\mu(p)\le q\) for every \(p\ge v_s\), so no requested price exists.

Suppose instead that \(D_\mu(v_s)>q\), and define

\(\displaystyle S=\{p\ge v_s:D_\mu(p)>q\},\qquad r=\sup S.\)

This set is nonempty and bounded above by \(U\).

We claim that \(D_\mu(r)>q\). If \(r=v_s\), this follows from the assumption. If \(r>v_s\), each individual demand is constant on some interval immediately to the left of \(r\), including \(r\) itself:

  • At a budget breakpoint \(r=b_t/k\), the floor equals \(k\) both at \(r\) and sufficiently close below it.
  • At a valuation breakpoint \(r=v_t\), the indicator equals \(1\) both at \(r\) and below it.
  • Away from these breakpoints, the corresponding factors are locally constant.

Since the support is finite, \(D_\mu\) is constant on \((r-\eta,r]\) for some \(\eta>0\). The definition of the supremum provides a point of \(S\) in this interval, so \(D_\mu(r)>q\).

Every price greater than \(r\) has demand at most \(q\), by the definition of \(r\). Hence \(r\) satisfies the requested conditions. Monotonicity also gives

\(\displaystyle S=[v_s,r].\)

Moreover, \(r\in\mathcal C\): otherwise every individual demand would be constant on a common interval immediately to its right, contradicting the definition of \(r\).

Finally, the requested price is unique. If two such prices satisfied \(p_1<p_2\), the condition at \(p_1\), with \(\varepsilon=p_2-p_1\), would imply \(D_\mu(p_2)\le q\).

Write this unique price as \(p^\star=r\). We have proved that it exists exactly when \(D_\mu(v_s)>q\).

3. Exact bisection

After the existence check, initialize \(\ell=v_s\) and \(u=U\). Maintain

\(\displaystyle D_\mu(\ell)>q,\qquad D_\mu(u)\le q.\)

At each iteration, evaluate demand exactly at \(z=(\ell+u)/2\). If \(D_\mu(z)>q\), replace \(\ell\) by \(z\); otherwise replace \(u\) by \(z\).

Since \(S=[v_s,p^\star]\), these invariants imply

\(\displaystyle \ell\le p^\star<u.\)

In particular, if a midpoint equals \(p^\star\), its demand is strictly greater than \(q\), so it becomes the lower endpoint.

Perform \(N=7h+1\) iterations. The resulting width satisfies

\(\displaystyle u-\ell=\frac{U-v_s}{2^N}<\frac{2^h}{2^{7h+1}}=2^{-6h-1}<\delta.\)

Therefore \([\ell,u)\) contains at most one distinct member of \(\mathcal C\), and that member is \(p^\star\).

4. Recovering the exact price

Let \(A=\{t\in T:\ell\le v_t\}\) be the types active at \(\ell\). For each \(t\in A\), compute

\(\displaystyle k_t=\left\lfloor\frac{b_t}{\ell}\right\rfloor,\qquad c_t=\min\left\{v_t,\frac{b_t}{k_t}\right\}.\)

These divisions are well-defined: \(\ell\le v_t\le b_t\) implies \(k_t\ge 1\). Also \(A\) is nonempty because \(D_\mu(\ell)>q>0\).

Return

\(\displaystyle c=\min_{t\in A}c_t.\)

We now prove that \(c=p^\star\).

First, \(c_t\ge\ell\) for every active type, so \(c\ge\ell\). For each active type, the definition of \(c\) gives \(c\le v_t\) and \(c\le b_t/k_t\). Hence

\(\displaystyle k_t\le\frac{b_t}{c}\le\frac{b_t}{\ell}<k_t+1,\)

so its demand at \(c\) remains exactly \(k_t\). Every type inactive at \(\ell\) remains inactive at \(c\), because \(c\ge\ell>v_t\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle D_\mu(c)=D_\mu(\ell)>q.\)

It follows that \(c\le p^\star<u\).

Furthermore, \(c\in\mathcal C\). The minimum defining \(c\) is attained at a valuation or a budget breakpoint \(b_t/k_t\); in the latter case, \(b_t/k_t\ge\ell\ge v_s\), so its index is within the range defining \(\mathcal C\).

Thus \(c\) and \(p^\star\) both belong to \(\mathcal C\cap[\ell,u)\). The separation bound makes this intersection contain at most one distinct point. Consequently, \(c=p^\star\), completing the correctness proof.

5. Bit complexity

It remains to show that exact arithmetic does not make the algorithm exponential.

Bisection prices. After \(j\) iterations, both endpoints lie on the grid

\(\displaystyle v_s+(U-v_s)\frac{r}{2^j},\qquad 0\le r\le 2^j.\)

Writing \(v_s=a_s/d_s\), a grid point has the representation

\(\displaystyle \frac{a_s2^j+(Ud_s-a_s)r}{d_s2^j}.\)

Its numerator and denominator have \(O(h+j)\) bits. Since \(j\le N=O(h)\), every evaluated price has \(O(L)\) bits. Maintaining these grid representations avoids denominator growth.

Individual demands. At any evaluated price \(p\ge v_s\),

\(\displaystyle \left\lfloor\frac{b_t}{p}\right\rfloor\le\frac{b_t}{v_s}\le 2^{2h}.\)

The quotient therefore has \(O(L)\) bits and can be computed by exact integer division on \(O(L)\)-bit operands.

Aggregate comparisons. Write \(\mu_t=a_{\mu,t}/d_{\mu,t}\) and \(q=a_q/d_q\). Precompute

\(\displaystyle Q=d_q\prod_{t\in T}d_{\mu,t},\qquad w_t=Q\mu_t,\qquad w_q=Qq.\)

All these quantities are integers. The bit length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\), since the total encoding length includes all denominator lengths. Then demand exceeds supply exactly when

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t:\,p\le v_t}w_t\left\lfloor\frac{b_t}{p}\right\rfloor>w_q.\)

The sum also has \(O(L)\) bits: using \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\),

\(\displaystyle QD_\mu(p)\le Q\sum_t\mu_t\frac{b_t}{v_s}\le Q\,2^{2h}.\)

Each demand evaluation uses \(O(\tau)\) integer operations on \(O(L)\)-bit operands. There are \(O(L)\) evaluations, followed by one pass over the support to recover \(c\). Standard integer arithmetic therefore gives a polynomial running time in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

The output itself has \(O(L)\) bits, since it is either an input valuation or \(b_t/k_t\) with \(k_t\le 2^{2h}\).

6. Relation to the cited algorithm

The cited paper computes critical prices by enumerating individual change-points under the finite supply cap and states an \(O(mn^2)\) running time. [Xiao, Song, and Khoussainov, “Multi-Unit Auction in Social Networks with Budgets,” Lemma 2](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/20458/20458-13-24471-1-2-20220628.pdf).

The proof above establishes the requested result independently. It neither expands rational masses into buyers nor enumerates uncapped demand levels. Polynomial bit bounds, breakpoint separation, and exact monotonic bisection suffice.
