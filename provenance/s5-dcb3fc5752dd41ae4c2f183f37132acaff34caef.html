The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item set (M), finite set of complete additive valuation types (T), rational type masses (\mu_t\ge 0) summing to (1), rational per-capita supplies (q_g\in[0,1]), and fixed (k\ge 1), does there exist a nonnegative mass (\lambda_{t,S}) for each type (t) and integral bundle (S\subseteq M) such that (\sum_S\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t) and (\sum_{t,S:g\in S}\lambda_{t,S}=q_g) for every item (g), with every pair of positive-mass outcomes ((t,S),(t',S')) satisfying both guarantees: some (D_E\subseteq S') with (|D_E|\le k) has (v_t(S)\ge v_t(S'\setminus D_E)), and some (D_Q\subseteq S') with (|D_Q|\le k) has (v_t(S)\ge v_{t'}(S'\setminus D_Q))?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) be a finite set of \(m\) goods. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) additive valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types.
Each type specifies a nonnegative binary-rational value \(v_t(g)\) for every good; valuations need not be normalized. Supplies \(q_g\in[0,1]\) are binary rational. Fix an integer \(k\ge1\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A feasible allocation assigns nonnegative real masses \(\lambda_{t,S}\) to types \(t\in T\) and integral bundles \(S\subseteq M\), allowing empty bundles, with \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}\lambda_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:g\in S}\lambda_{t,S}=q_g\) for every good.
For every ordered pair \((t,S),(t',S')\) of positive-mass outcomes, including outcomes of the same type, require subsets \(D_E,D_Q\subseteq S'\), each of cardinality at most \(k\), such that \(v_t(S)\ge v_t(S'\setminus D_E)\) and \(v_t(S)\ge v_{t'}(S'\setminus D_Q)\). The two removal sets may differ; goods valued at zero may be allocated or removed.

For each fixed \(k\ge1\), is deciding whether such an allocation exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents and indivisible goods, existence of a complete allocation satisfying both removal guarantees is NP-hard even for two agents, for every fixed \(k\ge1\), without normalization ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26410v2#A2)).
No result settling the exact support-specified mass problem above was located in the literature search.

  • The valuation encoding was unspecified; give every item value explicitly as a binary rational, and let L include the entire input.
  • Do not impose valuation normalization: the anchor explicitly permits unnormalized valuations.
  • Omit zero-mass types from the input support, preserving the given unit total population mass.
  • Interpret allocation masses as nonnegative real quantities; retain integral, possibly empty bundles and exact exhaustion of each prescribed supply.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Complexity classification

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for every fixed \(k\ge1\). Hardness holds even with exactly two supported types, equal masses, every supply equal to \(1/2\), and integer valuations.

The key step is to show that the supply equations force every positive-mass outcome to attain a pointwise bound on its dummy-good count. Consequently, splitting a type among several bundles cannot evade the reduction.

1. Removal residuals

For a type \(t\) and bundle \(S\), define
\[ \rho_t(S)=\min_{\substack{D\subseteq S\\ |D|\le k}}v_t(S\setminus D). \]
Because valuations are additive and nonnegative, \(\rho_t(S)\) is obtained by removing the \(\min\{k,|S|\}\) highest-valued goods, with arbitrary tie-breaking.

Thus the two required guarantees for an ordered pair \((t,S),(t',S')\) are exactly
\[ v_t(S)\ge \rho_t(S'), \qquad v_t(S)\ge \rho_{t'}(S'). \]
These minima are taken separately, as the problem permits different removal sets.

2. Reduction from Partition

Take an instance of \(\mathrm{Partition}\) consisting of positive integers \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), encoded in binary. Write \(W=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\) and \(H=W+1\).

Construct ordinary goods \(o_1,\ldots,o_n\) and a set \(\mathcal D\) of \(3k\) dummy goods. There are two types, \(A\) and \(B\), with \(\mu_A=\mu_B=1/2\). Set \(q_g=1/2\) for every good. Their values are:

| Type | Ordinary good \(o_i\) | Dummy good |
|---|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(x_i\) | \(H\) |
| \(B\) | \(x_i\) | \(0\) |

This valuation pattern comes from [Theorem 11 of the supplied paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26410v2#A2). The argument below establishes the additional implication needed for the mass model.

For a bundle \(S\), define \(d(S)=|S\cap\mathcal D|\) and \(s(S)=\sum_{i:o_i\in S}x_i\). Hence
\[ v_A(S)=H d(S)+s(S), \qquad v_B(S)=s(S), \qquad 0\le s(S)\le W<H. \]

For fixed \(k\), the construction has \(n+3k\) goods and polynomial encoding length.

3. Any feasible fair allocation yields a partition

Suppose the constructed instance admits an allocation satisfying both guarantees. Let \(\mathcal C_A\) and \(\mathcal C_B\) be the bundles receiving positive mass under the respective types. Both sets are nonempty because both type masses equal \(1/2\).

Pointwise bounds on dummy counts

First, every \(S\in\mathcal C_A\) satisfies \(d(S)\le k\).

Indeed, if \(d(S)\ge k+1\), removing at most \(k\) goods leaves a dummy, so \(\rho_A(S)\ge H\). Choose any \(S'\in\mathcal C_B\). The equitability guarantee for \((B,S'),(A,S)\) would require
\[ W\ge s(S')=v_B(S')\ge \rho_A(S)\ge H, \]
contradicting \(H=W+1\).

Next, for every \(S\in\mathcal C_A\) and \(S'\in\mathcal C_B\),
\[ d(S')\le d(S)+k. \]
Otherwise, after any admissible removal from \(S'\), at least \(d(S)+1\) dummies remain. Consequently,
\[ \rho_A(S')\ge (d(S)+1)H > d(S)H+W \ge v_A(S), \]
contradicting the envy guarantee for \((A,S),(B,S')\).

In particular, every \(S'\in\mathcal C_B\) satisfies \(d(S')\le2k\).

The supply equations force equality

Summing the supply equations over all dummy goods gives
\[ \sum_S\lambda_{A,S}d(S)+ \sum_S\lambda_{B,S}d(S) = \sum_{g\in\mathcal D}q_g = \frac{3k}{2}. \]
But the pointwise bounds and type-mass equations imply
\[ \sum_S\lambda_{A,S}d(S)+ \sum_S\lambda_{B,S}d(S) \le k\mu_A+2k\mu_B = \frac{3k}{2}. \]
Equivalently,
\[ \sum_S\lambda_{A,S}\bigl(k-d(S)\bigr)+ \sum_S\lambda_{B,S}\bigl(2k-d(S)\bigr)=0. \]
Every summand associated with a positive-mass outcome is nonnegative. Therefore every such summand is zero:
\[ d(S)=k\quad(S\in\mathcal C_A), \qquad d(S')=2k\quad(S'\in\mathcal C_B). \]

This is the step that prevents mass splitting from weakening the finite-agent construction.

Every supported bundle has the same ordinary value

Fix \(S\in\mathcal C_A\) and \(S'\in\mathcal C_B\).

Since each dummy has value \(H>x_i\) to \(A\), optimal removal under \(A\)'s valuation removes exactly \(k\) dummies. Thus
\[ \rho_A(S)=s(S), \qquad \rho_A(S')=kH+s(S'). \]

The equitability guarantee for \((B,S'),(A,S)\) gives \(s(S')\ge s(S)\). The envy guarantee for \((A,S),(B,S')\) gives
\[ kH+s(S)\ge kH+s(S'), \]
so \(s(S)\ge s(S')\). Hence \(s(S)=s(S')\).

Because these statements hold for every cross-type pair and both supports are nonempty, there is a common number \(c\) such that every positive-mass bundle has ordinary value \(c\).

The ordinary-good supply equations now yield
\[ \begin{aligned} c &=\sum_{t\in\{A,B\}}\sum_S\lambda_{t,S}s(S)\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i \sum_{t,S:o_i\in S}\lambda_{t,S}\\ &=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i q_{o_i} =\frac W2. \end{aligned} \]
The first equality uses total allocated mass \(\mu_A+\mu_B=1\).

Choose any \(S\in\mathcal C_A\). Its ordinary goods form a subset of the input integers with sum \(W/2\); their complement also has sum \(W/2\). Therefore the Partition instance is a yes-instance.

4. A partition yields a feasible fair allocation

Conversely, suppose the ordinary goods partition into \(X,Y\) with \(s(X)=s(Y)=W/2\). Partition the dummy goods into \(\mathcal D_A,\mathcal D_B\), with \(|\mathcal D_A|=k\) and \(|\mathcal D_B|=2k\).

Assign mass \(1/2\) to each of the two outcomes
\[ (A,X\cup\mathcal D_A), \qquad (B,Y\cup\mathcal D_B). \]
Every good occurs in exactly one outcome, so every supply is \(1/2\), and both type-mass constraints hold.

Write \(S_A=X\cup\mathcal D_A\) and \(S_B=Y\cup\mathcal D_B\). Their utilities are \(v_A(S_A)=kH+W/2\) and \(v_B(S_B)=W/2\).

For the ordered pair from \(A\) to \(B\):

  • Removing any \(k\) dummies from \(S_B\) leaves value \(kH+W/2\) under \(A\)'s valuation, establishing the envy guarantee.
  • The equitability guarantee holds without removal because \(v_A(S_A)\ge v_B(S_B)\).

For the ordered pair from \(B\) to \(A\):

  • The envy guarantee holds without removal because \(v_B(S_A)=v_B(S_B)=W/2\).
  • Removing all \(k\) dummies from \(S_A\) leaves value \(W/2\) under \(A\)'s valuation, establishing the equitability guarantee.

Each type has only one positive-mass outcome, so its self-comparison holds with empty removal sets. This proves the converse and completes the hardness reduction.

5. Membership in NP

It remains to justify polynomial certificates despite allowing real masses and exponentially many bundles.

Let \(r=m+\tau\). For each outcome \((t,S)\), define a column \(a_{t,S}\in\{0,1\}^r\): its type coordinates indicate \(t\), and its good coordinates indicate membership in \(S\). The feasibility equations are
\[ \sum_{t,S}\lambda_{t,S}a_{t,S}=b, \]
where \(b\) contains the type masses and supplies.

Suppose a fair feasible allocation exists. Restrict attention to its positive support. If its columns are linearly dependent, choose a nonzero dependence \(z\) with \(\sum_j z_j a_j=0\). Summing the type coordinates gives \(\sum_j z_j=0\), so \(z\) has both positive and negative entries.

Set \(\varepsilon=\min_{j:z_j>0}\lambda_j/z_j\) and replace \(\lambda_j\) by \(\lambda_j-\varepsilon z_j\). This preserves feasibility, keeps all masses nonnegative, and removes at least one supported outcome. Fairness persists because the support only shrinks.

Repeating produces a fair feasible allocation with linearly independent columns and at most \(r\) outcomes.

If there are \(s\le r\) remaining columns, select \(s\) rows forming an invertible integer matrix \(C\). Their masses solve \(C\lambda=b'\). Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators in \(b'\); its bit length is at most \(O(L)\). Cramer's rule expresses each mass with denominator \(Q\det C\).

Since \(C\) has binary entries, Hadamard's inequality gives \(|\det C|\le s^{s/2}\). The entries of \(Qb'\) have absolute value at most \(Q\), so the replacement determinants have absolute value at most \(Q s^{s/2}\). Thus every mass has a rational representation of bit length \(O(L+s\log(s+1))\).

A certificate can therefore list at most \(m+\tau\) bundles and polynomial-length rational masses. A verifier checks the feasibility equations and every ordered pair of supported outcomes. Each removal residual is computed by selecting the highest-valued goods, using exact rational arithmetic. All these checks take polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for every fixed \(k\ge1\).
