Theorem 6.5 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), per-mass weight densities \(\alpha_t>0\), a feasible current outcome represented by per-capita payoff densities \(b_t\), and prizes \(p_1\geq\cdots\geq p_k>0\), does there exist a mass vector \(z\) with \(0\leq z_t\leq\mu_t\) and an index \(j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) such that the deviating mass has current payoff \(B(z)=\sum_t b_tz_t<p_j\) and residual weight \(R(z)=\sum_t\alpha_t(\mu_t-z_t)<jW(z)\), where \(W(z)=\sum_t\alpha_tz_t\)? Ties between residual coalitions and the deviating coalition are resolved against the deviator, so \(R(z)<jW(z)\) means the residual mass cannot form \(j\) coalitions of at least the deviator’s weight.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T\): the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, with binary rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Mass is atomless and freely divisible.
The input also contains binary rational weight densities \(\alpha_t>0\), current payoff densities \(b_t\geq0\), and an explicitly listed sequence of prizes \(p_1\geq\cdots\geq p_k>0\), with \(k\geq1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; running times are measured in \(\tau,k,L\).
A coalition is a nonzero mass vector; its weight and current payoff are the corresponding density integrals. In any finite partition into positive-mass coalitions, the coalition ranked \(i\)-th by weight receives \(p_i\) if \(i\leq k\), and zero otherwise; fewer coalitions may leave prizes unclaimed.
Feasibility of the current payoff is promised: there exist finitely many nonzero vectors \(c^1,\ldots,c^q\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{T}\) summing to \(\mu\), ordered by nonincreasing weight, such that \(\sum_t b_tc^i_t=p_i\) for \(i\leq\min(q,k)\) and zero for \(i>k\). Equal-weight current coalitions may be ordered in either way.
A single-coalition deviation selects \(z\in\mathbb R^{T}\) with \(0\leq z_t\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_tz_t>0\). Residual mass may repartition arbitrarily into finitely many positive-mass coalitions; ties against the deviator are always lost. Write \(B(z)=\sum_t b_tz_t\), \(W(z)=\sum_t\alpha_tz_t\), and \(R(z)=\sum_t\alpha_t(\mu_t-z_t)\).
The decision problem asks whether some such \(z\) and \(j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) satisfy \(B(z)<p_j\) and \(R(z)<jW(z)\), so the coalition strictly improves its total payoff regardless of the residual partition.

Is this continuous single-coalition-deviation decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau,k,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite indivisible players, existence of a single-coalition deviation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and belongs to \(\Sigma_2^{\mathrm P}\), with polynomial-time coalition tie comparisons. [Source](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~eg/AAMAS2023/pdfs/p2049.pdf)
No result settling this exact atomless, support-encoded decision problem was located in the literature search.

  • Made atomlessness and unrestricted real-valued splitting explicit; coalitions must have positive mass, and residual responses are finite partitions.
  • Expanded feasible current outcome into a promise of a finite coalition partition whose coalition payoffs equal its awarded prizes; unclaimed prizes are permitted.

Result

The decision problem is solvable in polynomial time in $\tau,k,L$. An exact algorithm sorts types by payoff per unit of weight and performs a fractional-knapsack calculation for each prize.

The proof does not require the promised feasibility of the current payoff, so it applies in particular to all promised inputs.

1. Reduce the weight condition to a threshold

Let the total societal weight be
$$ A=\sum_{t\in T}\alpha_t\mu_t. $$
Since $\alpha_t,\mu_t>0$, we have $A>0$. For every admissible vector $z$,
$$ R(z)=A-W(z). $$
Consequently, for each $j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$,
$$ R(z)<jW(z) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W(z)>\frac{A}{j+1}. $$
Write
$$ h_j=\frac{A}{j+1}. $$
In particular, $0<h_j<A$.

For $0<h<A$, define the minimum payoff cost of exactly weight $h$ by
$$ F(h)= \min\left\{ B(z): 0\leq z_t\leq\mu_t,\quad W(z)=h \right\}. $$
This minimum exists: the feasible set is nonempty because it contains $(h/A)\mu$, and it is compact; the objective is continuous.

We will prove that the answer is YES exactly when
$$ F(h_j)<p_j $$
for at least one $j$.

2. Compute $F(h)$ explicitly

Introduce weight coordinates and payoff-to-weight ratios:
$$ y_t=\alpha_tz_t,\qquad a_t=\alpha_t\mu_t,\qquad r_t=\frac{b_t}{\alpha_t}. $$
Because $\alpha_t>0$, this is a bijective change of variables between the respective feasible sets. Thus
$$ F(h)= \min\left\{ \sum_{t\in T}r_ty_t: 0\leq y_t\leq a_t,\quad \sum_{t\in T}y_t=h \right\}. $$

Relabel the types so that
$$ r_1\leq r_2\leq\cdots\leq r_\tau. $$
Define prefix sums
$$ A_\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_i, \qquad C_\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}r_ia_i =\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_i\mu_i, \qquad A_0=C_0=0. $$
All $a_i$ are positive. Hence, for any $0<h<A$, there is a unique smallest index $m$ with $A_m\geq h$, and it satisfies
$$ A_{m-1}<h\leq A_m. $$

Consider the feasible vector
$$ y_i^*= \begin{cases} a_i,&i<m,\\ h-A_{m-1},&i=m,\\ 0,&i>m. \end{cases} $$
Its cost is
$$ F(h)=C_{m-1}+r_m(h-A_{m-1}). $$

To justify this equality, let $y$ be any feasible vector of total weight $h$. Since $\sum_i(y_i-y_i^*)=0$,
$$ \sum_i r_i(y_i-y_i^*) = \sum_i(r_i-r_m)(y_i-y_i^*). $$
Every summand on the right is nonnegative:

  • If $i<m$, then $r_i-r_m\leq0$ and $y_i-y_i^*=y_i-a_i\leq0$.
  • If $i=m$, the coefficient is zero.
  • If $i>m$, then $r_i-r_m\geq0$ and $y_i-y_i^*=y_i\geq0$.

Therefore $y^*$ has minimum cost. This proof also covers equal ratios and zero payoff densities.

3. Handle both strict inequalities exactly

Fix $j$, and put $h=h_j$.

Necessity. Suppose an admissible $z$ satisfies
$$ W(z)>h,\qquad B(z)<p_j. $$
Scale it down:
$$ z'=\frac{h}{W(z)}z. $$
The scaling factor belongs to $(0,1)$, so $z'$ remains within the mass bounds and satisfies $W(z')=h$. Since $b_t\geq0$,
$$ F(h)\leq B(z') =\frac{h}{W(z)}B(z) \leq B(z)<p_j. $$

Sufficiency. Suppose $F(h)<p_j$, and let $z^*$ be the minimizer constructed above, converted back to mass coordinates. Define
$$ \delta=p_j-F(h)>0, \qquad D=B(\mu)-F(h)=B(\mu-z^*)\geq0. $$
Choose the positive rational number
$$ \varepsilon= \min\left\{ \frac12,\frac{\delta}{2(D+1)} \right\}, $$
and set
$$ z^\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)z^*+\varepsilon\mu. $$
This convex combination satisfies all coordinate bounds. Its weight is
$$ W(z^\varepsilon) =h+\varepsilon(A-h)>h, $$
because $A>h$. Its payoff cost satisfies
$$ B(z^\varepsilon) =F(h)+\varepsilon D \leq F(h)+\frac{\delta}{2} <p_j. $$
Its positive weight ensures that it is a nonzero coalition. Thus it satisfies both required strict inequalities.

We have proved the exact criterion
$$ \boxed{ \text{YES} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}: F\!\left(\frac{A}{j+1}\right)<p_j. } $$
In particular, equality $F(h_j)=p_j$ must be rejected for that index.

4. Algorithm and bit complexity

The algorithm is:

  • Sort the types by $b_t/\alpha_t$.
  • Compute the prefix sums $A_\ell$ and $C_\ell$.
  • For each explicitly listed prize $p_j$, compute $h_j=A/(j+1)$, locate the smallest $m$ with $A_m\geq h_j$, and evaluate

$$ C_{m-1}+\frac{b_m}{\alpha_m}(h_j-A_{m-1}). $$

  • Return YES if any evaluated value is strictly below $p_j$; otherwise return NO.

Sorting and binary searches use
$$ O\!\left((\tau+k)\log(\tau+1)\right) $$
rational comparisons and arithmetic operations.

These operations have polynomial bit complexity. Products and ratios of input rationals have polynomial encoding length. Prefix sums also do: a common denominator can be chosen as the product of the constituent denominators, whose bit length is bounded by the sum of their bit lengths. Numerator summation adds at most a logarithmic overhead. Computing $h_j$ adds only the encoding length of $j+1$, and each evaluated cost uses a constant number of further rational operations. Exact comparisons can therefore be performed by integer cross multiplication with polynomially many bits.

Thus the algorithm runs in polynomial time in $\tau,k,L$. The construction in Section 3 also produces a rational deviating coalition of polynomial encoding length whenever the answer is YES.

Free divisibility enables the fractional selection used in the proof. The [cited finite-player paper](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~eg/AAMAS2023/pdfs/p2049.pdf) instead models coalitions as subsets of a finite player set; its complexity results do not obstruct this continuous algorithm.
