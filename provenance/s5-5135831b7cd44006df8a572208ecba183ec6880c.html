The policy treats each supported type as one aggregate buyer with budget \(B_t=\mu_tb_t\). Below, the multiplier guarantee follows from a stated dual-averaging theorem; the regret, envy, and bit-complexity claims are proved directly.

1. The aggregate model

The specified objective, allocation rule, prices, regret, and envy depend on the masses and per-capita budgets only through \(B_t=\mu_tb_t\). Thus the finite market with buyers indexed by \(T\), valuations \(v_t\), and budgets \(B_t\) has exactly the quantities required in the question.

This identification uses the stipulated aggregate comparisons. It requires neither expanding a type into individual buyers nor storing anything for types outside the support.

Write \(G=\max_{t,j}v_t(j)\). The normalization implies \(G\ge1\).

2. Location of the optimal multipliers

Define

\(f(\beta)=\sum_j s_j\max_t\beta_tv_t(j)\),
\(F(\beta)=f(\beta)-\sum_tB_t\log\beta_t\).

We first prove

\(B_t\le\beta_t^\star\le1\quad\text{for every }t.\)

Existence and uniqueness. Let \(R=\max_t\beta_t\). Normalization gives \(f(\beta)\ge\beta_t\) for every \(t\), hence \(f(\beta)\ge R\). Since \(\sum_tB_t=1\),

\(F(\beta)\ge R-\log R.\)

Consequently, every fixed sublevel set has bounded \(R\). Within such a bound, if any coordinate approaches zero, its term \(-B_t\log\beta_t\) tends to infinity, while the other logarithmic terms remain bounded below. Therefore a nonempty sublevel set is contained in a compact subset of the positive orthant, and continuity yields a minimizer. The function \(-\sum_tB_t\log\beta_t\) is strictly convex, so the minimizer is unique.

Bounds. The first-order optimality condition gives a subgradient \(q\in\partial f(\beta^\star)\) satisfying

\(q_t=B_t/\beta_t^\star.\)

For a finite maximum of linear functions, its subdifferential is the convex hull of the active coefficient vectors. Applying this fact to each summand of \(f\), there are numbers \(a_{tj}\ge0\) such that

\(\sum_ta_{tj}=1,\qquad a_{tj}>0\Longrightarrow \beta_t^\star v_t(j)=\max_k\beta_k^\star v_k(j),\qquad q_t=\sum_js_jv_t(j)a_{tj}.\)

Thus \(q_t\le\sum_js_jv_t(j)=1\), which implies \(\beta_t^\star\ge B_t\). Moreover,

\(f(\beta^\star) =\sum_t\beta_t^\star q_t =\sum_tB_t =1.\)

Since \(f(\beta^\star)\ge\beta_t^\star\), we also have \(\beta_t^\star\le1\).

In particular, \(\beta^\star\) lies strictly inside

\(K=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}[B_t/2,2],\)

with distance at least \(B_t/2\) from either endpoint of its coordinate interval.

Define the reference utilities

\(u_t^\star=B_t/\beta_t^\star.\)

The proved bounds imply \(B_t\le u_t^\star\le1\).

3. An exact rational policy

Maintain cumulative aggregate utilities \(A_t^h\), initialized by \(A_t^0=0\), and set \(\beta_t^1=2\).

At arrival \(h\):

  • Compute \(w_h=\min\arg\max_t\beta_t^hv_t(\theta_h)\).
  • Allocate the whole item to \(w_h\) and charge \(p_h=\beta_{w_h}^hv_{w_h}(\theta_h)\).
  • Update \(A_{w_h}^h=A_{w_h}^{h-1}+v_{w_h}(\theta_h)\); leave the other cumulative utilities unchanged.
  • For every type, set

\(\beta_t^{h+1} =\min\{2,\max\{B_t/2,hB_t/A_t^h\}\}\)

when \(A_t^h>0\), and set \(\beta_t^{h+1}=2\) when \(A_t^h=0\).

This specifies the division-by-zero case explicitly. The policy uses only past observations and the input, never \(s\). Its allocation and pricing rules are exactly those required.

4. The exact multiplier bound

We use the stochastic dual-averaging result stated as Theorem 2 of [Gao, Kroer, and Peysakhovich](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2021/file/e562cd9c0768d5464b64cf61da7fc6bb-Paper.pdf):

For independent identically distributed convex losses \(f_{\theta_h}\) and a closed, \(\sigma\)-strongly convex regularizer \(\Psi\), initialize \(w^1\in\arg\min\Psi\). With stochastic subgradients \(g^h\), update
\(w^{h+1}=\arg\min_w\{\langle h^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^h g^\ell,w\rangle+\Psi(w)\}\).
If \(\mathbb E\|g^h\|_2^2\le G^2\), then the minimizer \(w^\star\) of \(\mathbb Ef_\theta+\Psi\) satisfies
\(\mathbb E\|w^h-w^\star\|_2^2\le(6+\log h)G^2/(h\sigma^2)\).

Here, take

\(f_j(\beta)=\max_t\beta_tv_t(j),\qquad \Psi(\beta)=-\sum_tB_t\log\beta_t\quad(\beta\in K),\)

and extend \(\Psi\) by \(+\infty\) outside \(K\). We verify the hypotheses and identify the updates:

  • Each \(f_j\) is convex and subdifferentiable.
  • The selected winner gives

\(g^h=v_{w_h}(\theta_h)e_{w_h}\in\partial f_{\theta_h}(\beta^h)\).
Therefore \(\|g^h\|_2^2\le G^2\) pointwise.

  • Fresh arrivals are independent of the state, so this is a valid stochastic subgradient oracle for \(f=\mathbb E_sf_\theta\).
  • On \(K\), the logarithmic Hessian has diagonal entries

\(B_t/\beta_t^2\ge B_t/4\).
Hence \(\Psi\) is \(\sigma\)-strongly convex for
\(\sigma=\min_tB_t/4\).

  • The initialization \(\beta^1=(2,\ldots,2)\) minimizes \(\Psi\).
  • The average subgradient has coordinate \(A_t^h/h=\bar u_t^h\). Minimizing

\(\bar u_t^h\beta_t-B_t\log\beta_t\) over \([B_t/2,2]\) gives exactly the update in Section 3.

  • Section 2 shows that constraining the objective to \(K\) preserves its unique minimizer \(\beta^\star\).

The theorem therefore gives, with the question’s pre-arrival indexing,

\(\boxed{\mathbb E\|\beta^H-\beta^\star\|_2^2 \le\frac{(6+\log H)G^2}{H\sigma^2}.}\)

Every bound used here holds for every admissible \(s\).

5. Uniform utility estimates

Let

\(a_h=\frac{(6+\log h)G^2}{h\sigma^2},\qquad B_{\min}=\min_tB_t,\qquad \xi_t^H=|\bar u_t^H-u_t^\star|.\)

We prove the deterministic inequality

\(\xi_t^H\le\frac{2G}{B_t}|\beta_t^{H+1}-\beta_t^\star|.\)

If \(\beta_t^{H+1}\) is interior to its interval, the update implies
\(\bar u_t^H=B_t/\beta_t^{H+1}\). Thus

\(\xi_t^H =\frac{B_t|\beta_t^{H+1}-\beta_t^\star|} {\beta_t^{H+1}\beta_t^\star} \le\frac{2}{B_t}|\beta_t^{H+1}-\beta_t^\star|.\)

If the updated multiplier is an endpoint, Section 2 gives
\(|\beta_t^{H+1}-\beta_t^\star|\ge B_t/2\). Both \(\bar u_t^H\) and \(u_t^\star\) belong to \([0,G]\), so \(\xi_t^H\le G\), proving the claimed inequality also in this case.

Consequently,

\(\mathbb E(\xi_t^H)^2\le\frac{4G^2}{B_t^2}a_{H+1},\qquad \mathbb E\sum_t(\xi_t^H)^2 \le\frac{4G^2}{B_{\min}^2}a_{H+1}.\)

All constants are independent of \(s\).

We will also use

\(d_H=\frac{G}{H}\sum_{h=1}^H\|\beta^h-\beta^\star\|_2.\)

Cauchy–Schwarz and the multiplier bound yield

\(\mathbb E d_H^2 \le\frac{G^2}{H}\sum_{h=1}^H a_h \le\frac{G^4}{H\sigma^2}(6+\log H)(1+\log H),\)

where the last step uses \(\sum_{h=1}^Hh^{-1}\le1+\log H\).

6. Regret

Define the reference item price \(p_j^\star=\max_t\beta_t^\star v_t(j)\). The maximum operation gives

\(|p_h-p_{\theta_h}^\star| \le G\|\beta^h-\beta^\star\|_2.\)

For any vector \(z\) feasible in the definition of \(\widehat U_t^H\), nonnegativity and \(z_h\le1\) imply

\(\beta_t^\star\frac1H\sum_hv_t(\theta_h)z_h \le\frac1H\sum_hp_{\theta_h}^\star z_h \le\frac1H\sum_hp_hz_h+d_H \le B_t+d_H.\)

Taking the maximum over feasible \(z\),

\(\widehat U_t^H\le u_t^\star+d_H/\beta_t^\star.\)

Because \(\beta_t^\star\ge B_t\),

\(r_t^H\le\xi_t^H+d_H/B_t.\)

It follows that

\(\mathbb E(r_t^H)^2 \le2\mathbb E(\xi_t^H)^2+\frac{2}{B_t^2}\mathbb E d_H^2 =O((\log H)^2/H).\)

The constants depend only on the fixed input market, uniformly over admissible \(s\). As usual, this big-\(O\) statement is asymptotic.

7. Envy

Whenever arrival \(h\) is allocated to type \(k\), the winner rule implies

\(\beta_t^hv_t(\theta_h)\le\beta_k^hv_k(\theta_h).\)

Changing each multiplier to its reference value gives

\(\beta_t^\star v_t(\theta_h) \le\beta_k^\star v_k(\theta_h) +2G\|\beta^h-\beta^\star\|_2.\)

Multiply by \(x_k^h\) and average. Since \(0\le x_k^h\le1\),

\(\beta_t^\star\bar u_{tk}^H \le\beta_k^\star\bar u_k^H+2d_H \le B_k+\beta_k^\star\xi_k^H+2d_H.\)

Using \(u_t^\star/B_t=1/\beta_t^\star\), we obtain

\(\frac{\bar u_{tk}^H}{B_k}-\frac{\bar u_t^H}{B_t} \le\frac{\xi_t^H}{B_t} +\frac{\beta_k^\star\xi_k^H+2d_H}{\beta_t^\star B_k}.\)

Since \(\beta_k^\star\le1\) and \(\beta_t^\star\ge B_t\), maximization over \(k\) yields

\(\rho_t^H \le\frac1{B_t} \left[ (1+B_{\min}^{-1}) \left(\sum_k(\xi_k^H)^2\right)^{1/2} +\frac{2d_H}{B_{\min}} \right].\)

Squaring and taking expectations gives

\(\mathbb E(\rho_t^H)^2 \le\frac{2}{B_t^2} \left[ (1+B_{\min}^{-1})^2 \mathbb E\sum_k(\xi_k^H)^2 +\frac{4}{B_{\min}^2}\mathbb E d_H^2 \right] =O((\log H)^2/H).\)

Again, the constants are uniform over admissible \(s\).

8. Rational storage and running time

All policy operations are rational additions, multiplications, divisions, and comparisons. The implementation never evaluates a logarithm or computes \(\beta^\star\).

For a bit-size bound, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input valuations. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). This number is needed only for the analysis.

Each cumulative utility \(A_t^h\) is a sum of at most \(H\) input valuations. Its reduced denominator divides \(D\), and its numerator is at most \(HDG\). Since \(\log G=O(L)\), both numerator and denominator have \(O(L+\log H)\) bits.

Each \(B_t=\mu_tb_t\) has \(O(L)\) bits. The expression \(hB_t/A_t^h\), its clipped value, and each bid and price consequently have \(O(L+\log H)\) bits as well. Reduced rational arithmetic preserves these bounds.

The policy stores:

  • \(\tau\) cumulative utilities;
  • \(\tau\) multipliers and, optionally, \(\tau\) aggregate budgets;
  • a counter and a constant number of temporary registers.

Thus it uses \(O(\tau)\) rational registers. Each arrival requires \(O(\tau)\) arithmetic operations and comparisons on polynomial-size integers. Input access also costs polynomial time, even if implemented by scanning the read-only input.

The total construction and execution time is therefore

\(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,H).\)

Tiny binary-encoded budgets can make the convergence constants large, but they do not enlarge the registers beyond polynomial bit length. The measured running time includes \(H\), as required.
