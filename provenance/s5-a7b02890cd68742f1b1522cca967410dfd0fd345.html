The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of types \(T\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and an LPFD formula \(\varphi\) over \(T\), decide whether there exist a nonatomic population with those type masses, a set \(A\subseteq\prod_{t\in T}[0,\mu_t]\) of feasible aggregate states, a preorder \(\preceq_t\) on \(A\) for each type, predicate interpretations satisfying the paper’s dependence condition, and a state \(z\in A\) such that \(z\models\varphi\). Each coordinate \(z_t\) is the mass of type \(t\) choosing action \(1\), with every individual choosing either \(0\) or \(1\); interpret \(D_Xy\) and \(J_{X,Y,Z}\) over aggregate states using coordinate equality and the type preorders as in LPFD.

There are \(m=2\) individual actions, \(0\) and \(1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A realization is a nonatomic probability space partitioned into measurable type sets of masses \(\mu_t\); a measurable action profile induces \(z_t\), the mass of type \(t\) choosing \(1\).

1. Result and the external theorem used

Aggregate satisfiability is decidable. This includes fully supported formulas with $\tau\ge 2$.

The key step is a polynomial reduction to unrestricted LPFD satisfiability. Additional LPFD conditions allow us to construct a model in which equality on the entire support identifies states.

We use the following given result: unrestricted LPFD satisfiability is decidable for arbitrary feasible assignment sets, arbitrary object domains, and arbitrary preorders. Its syntax and semantics for dependence and $J_{X,Y,Z}$ coincide with those in the question. This is Theorem 5 of [Chen, Shi, and Wang, *Reasoning about Dependence, Preference and Coalitional Power*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.08213v1).

The reduction and the model construction below establish why that theorem applies despite the fixed-support restriction.

2. Relational notation and the reduction

Fix the nonempty support $T$. In a relational model, write $E_t$ for an equivalence relation representing equality of coordinate $t$, and $R_t$ for a preorder representing preference. Define

$$ E_X=\bigcap_{t\in X}E_t, $$

where $E_\varnothing$ is the universal relation, and define

$$ a<_t b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad aR_t b\ \wedge\ \neg(bR_t a). $$

Atomic valuations must be constant on the equivalence classes of $E_{\operatorname{set}(\vec t)}$. Dependence and modalities are interpreted exactly as in the question, using $E_t$ and $R_t$.

These relational models need not satisfy joint separation:

$$ aE_Tb\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=b. $$

This is the restriction we will recover.

Choose $t_0\in T$ and use the legitimate abbreviations

$$ \top=D_{\{t_0\}}t_0, \qquad \bot=\neg\top. $$

Also write

$$ [U]\psi=J_{\varnothing,\varnothing,\varnothing}\psi, \qquad [T]\psi=J_{T,\varnothing,\varnothing}\psi. $$

Thus $[U]$ quantifies over every state, and $[T]$ quantifies over states agreeing on all support coordinates.

Let $\operatorname{Sub}(\varphi)$ denote the set of subformulas of $\varphi$. Construct

$$ C_\varphi= \left( \bigwedge_{\psi\in\operatorname{Sub}(\varphi)} (\psi\rightarrow[T]\psi) \right) \wedge \left( \bigwedge_{t\in T} J_{T,\varnothing,\{t\}}\bot \right), $$

and

$$ \alpha_\varphi=\varphi\wedge[U]C_\varphi. $$

Implication and finite conjunctions are Boolean abbreviations, so this is an LPFD formula.

In every relational model satisfying $[U]C_\varphi$, the following two properties hold:

  • Every subformula of $\varphi$ has constant truth value on each $E_T$-class.
  • No strict preference comparison holds between two states in the same $E_T$-class.

For the first property, suppose $aE_Tb$. If $\psi$ holds at $a$, its corresponding conjunct forces $\psi$ at $b$. Conversely, if $\psi$ holds at $b$, symmetry of $E_T$ and the same conjunct at $b$ force $\psi$ at $a$. Hence

$$ aE_Tb \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \bigl(a\models\psi\ \Longleftrightarrow\ b\models\psi\bigr) $$

for every $\psi\in\operatorname{Sub}(\varphi)$.

For the second property, the corresponding conjunct at $a$ directly gives

$$ aE_Tb \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \neg(a<_t b) $$

for every $t\in T$.

We will prove

$$ \varphi\text{ is aggregate satisfiable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha_\varphi\text{ is unrestricted LPFD satisfiable}. $$

3. Forward implication

Suppose an aggregate model satisfies $\varphi$ at some state.

Interpret $E_t$ as equality of coordinate $t$ and $R_t$ as the given preorder. Equality on every support coordinate identifies aggregate states. Consequently, $[T]\psi$ is equivalent to $\psi$, and no $E_T$-related pair admits a strict comparison. Thus the model satisfies $\alpha_\varphi$.

We must also account for unrestricted LPFD’s common interpretation of each predicate symbol, whereas the task specifies a valuation for each atomic expression.

Encode coordinate values as tagged objects:

$$ z_t\longmapsto(t,z_t). $$

Associate with each aggregate state $z$ an assignment $a_z$ satisfying

$$ a_z(t)=(t,z_t) \qquad(t\in T). $$

Assign fixed objects to ambient variables outside $T$. The map $z\mapsto a_z$ is injective.

For each predicate $P$, interpret the argument tuples used in $\alpha_\varphi$ according to their aggregate valuations. This is well-defined. If two such object tuples are equal, their tags force the same ordered type tuple. Equality of the argument values then gives coordinate equality on that atomic expression’s support, and atomic locality forces equal truth values. For a nullary expression, locality makes its truth value constant.

Transport the support preorders along the injective assignment map and choose arbitrary preorders for other ambient variables. Induction on formulas preserves truth. Hence $\alpha_\varphi$ is unrestricted LPFD satisfiable.

4. Obtaining a countable relational model

Conversely, suppose $\alpha_\varphi$ is unrestricted LPFD satisfiable. Take the relational reduct of a satisfying model, retaining only:

  • the relations $E_t$ and $R_t$ for $t\in T$;
  • a unary predicate $B_a$ for each distinct atomic expression $a$ in $\varphi$;
  • a constant naming the satisfying state.

We may assume this relational model is at most countable. Here is the justification.

Truth has a first-order translation. Besides the Boolean clauses, its clauses are

$$ \operatorname{ST}_u(a)=B_a(u), $$

$$ \operatorname{ST}_u(D_Xy) = \forall v\bigl(E_X(u,v)\rightarrow E_y(u,v)\bigr), $$

and

$$ \operatorname{ST}_u(J_{X,Y,Z}\psi) = \forall v \left( \left[ E_X(u,v) \wedge \bigwedge_{t\in Y}R_t(u,v) \wedge \bigwedge_{t\in Z} \bigl(R_t(u,v)\wedge\neg R_t(v,u)\bigr) \right] \rightarrow\operatorname{ST}_v(\psi) \right). $$

Bound variables are renamed as necessary. Induction on syntax verifies that this translation preserves truth.

The reduct has a finite first-order language. By downward Löwenheim–Skolem, it has an at most countable elementary substructure containing the named state. Elementarity preserves $\alpha_\varphi$ and the equivalence, preorder, and locality axioms.

Write the resulting model as $M$, its domain as $W$, and its distinguished state as $a_0$. In particular,

$$ M,a_0\models\varphi, $$

and throughout $W$, subformula truth is constant on $E_T$-classes and no strict comparison occurs within an $E_T$-class.

5. A tree construction enforcing joint separation

5.1. States and relations

Let $\mathcal W$ consist of all finite sequences

$$ u=(a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_n) $$

such that

$$ \neg E_T(a_i,a_{i+1}) \qquad(0\le i<n). $$

The sequence $(a_0)$ is included. Since $W$ is at most countable, so is $\mathcal W$.

A node is adjacent to each sequence obtained by appending one state. This forms a rooted tree. Define the projection

$$ \pi(a_0,\ldots,a_n)=a_n. $$

For each $t\in T$, label an undirected tree edge $\{u,v\}$ with $t$ exactly when

$$ E_t(\pi(u),\pi(v)). $$

Let $E'_t$ be the equivalence relation generated by those labelled edges.

For adjacent nodes $u,v$, include the directed edge $u\to v$ for type $t$ exactly when

$$ R_t(\pi(u),\pi(v)). $$

The two orientations are determined independently. Let $R'_t$ be the reflexive-transitive closure of these directed edges, and let $<'_t$ be its strict part.

Finally, pull back atomic valuations:

$$ u\in B'_a \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \pi(u)\in B_a. $$

5.2. Relations along the unique tree path

Let

$$ u=u_0,u_1,\ldots,u_k=v $$

be the unique simple tree path from $u$ to $v$, and write $b_i=\pi(u_i)$.

Then

$$ uE'_tv \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E_t(b_i,b_{i+1})\text{ for every }i<k, $$

and

$$ uR'_tv \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_t(b_i,b_{i+1})\text{ for every }i<k. $$

To justify necessity, removing any edge of this simple path separates $u$ from $v$. Every walk between them must cross that edge; a directed walk must cross it in the path’s forward direction. Sufficiency follows by traversing the simple path itself. For $u=v$, the empty path agrees with reflexivity.

Therefore projection preserves coordinate equality and weak preference:

$$ uE'_tv\Longrightarrow E_t(\pi(u),\pi(v)), $$

$$ uR'_tv\Longrightarrow R_t(\pi(u),\pi(v)). $$

It also preserves strict preference:

$$ u<'_tv\Longrightarrow \pi(u)<_t\pi(v). $$

Here is the nontrivial strictness argument. If $u<'_tv$, every path edge admits its forward orientation, but at least one edge, say the edge from $u_i$ to $u_{i+1}$, lacks its reverse orientation. Thus

$$ R_t(b_j,b_{j+1})\text{ for every }j<k, \qquad \neg R_t(b_{i+1},b_i). $$

Transitivity gives $R_t(b_0,b_k)$. If $R_t(b_k,b_0)$ also held, transitivity along the suffix, this assumed comparison, and the prefix would give

$$ b_{i+1}R_tb_kR_tb_0R_tb_i, $$

contradicting $\neg R_t(b_{i+1},b_i)$. Hence $b_0<_tb_k$.

For adjacent nodes, these path characterizations give exact preservation and reflection of equality, weak preference, and strict preference.

5.3. Joint separation and atomic locality

The new equivalences jointly separate nodes:

$$ uE'_Tv\Longrightarrow u=v. $$

Indeed, if distinct nodes were $E'_t$-related for every $t\in T$, every edge on their nonempty tree path would have every coordinate label. Its projected endpoints would therefore be $E_T$-related, contrary to the definition of $\mathcal W$.

Atomic locality also holds. If $uE'_{\operatorname{set}(\vec t)}v$, projection preserves all the relevant coordinate equalities. Original locality then gives equal atomic truth at $\pi(u)$ and $\pi(v)$, hence at $u$ and $v$.

Thus the constructed structure is a legitimate relational model satisfying joint separation.

6. Preservation of the input formula

We prove, for every $\theta\in\operatorname{Sub}(\varphi)$ and every $u\in\mathcal W$, that

$$ M',u\models\theta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad M,\pi(u)\models\theta, $$

where $M'$ is the tree model just constructed.

Atomic and Boolean cases follow from the valuation definition and induction.

6.1. Dependence atoms

Suppose

$$ M,\pi(u)\models D_Xy. $$

Take any $v$ with $uE'_Xv$, and let $u_0,\ldots,u_k$ be their simple tree path, with $u_0=u$ and $b_i=\pi(u_i)$.

For each $x\in X$, every path edge has label $x$. Consequently,

$$ b_0E_Xb_i \qquad(0\le i\le k). $$

The original dependence atom gives $b_0E_yb_i$ for every $i$. Since $E_y$ is an equivalence relation, successive $b_i$ are $E_y$-related. Every path edge therefore has label $y$, so $uE'_yv$.

This proves $M',u\models D_Xy$.

Conversely, suppose $M,\pi(u)\not\models D_Xy$. There exists $b\in W$ such that

$$ \pi(u)E_Xb, \qquad \neg E_y(\pi(u),b). $$

Because $y\in T$, these states cannot be $E_T$-related. Thus appending $b$ produces an allowed child $v$ of $u$. Exact preservation on this edge gives

$$ uE'_Xv, \qquad \neg(uE'_yv). $$

Hence $M',u\not\models D_Xy$.

6.2. Modal formulas

Consider $\theta=J_{X,Y,Z}\psi$.

First suppose $M,\pi(u)\models\theta$. If $v$ is a corresponding successor of $u$ in the tree model, projection preserves all required equalities, weak comparisons, and strict comparisons. Thus $\pi(v)$ is a corresponding successor of $\pi(u)$ in $M$, so $M,\pi(v)\models\psi$. Induction gives $M',v\models\psi$. Therefore $M',u\models\theta$.

For the converse, suppose $M,\pi(u)\not\models\theta$. Choose $b\in W$ such that

$$ \pi(u)E_Xb, $$

$$ \pi(u)R_tb\qquad(t\in Y), $$

$$ \pi(u)<_tb\qquad(t\in Z), $$

and

$$ M,b\not\models\psi. $$

There are two cases.

Case 1: $\neg E_T(\pi(u),b)$. Appending $b$ gives a child $v$. Exact preservation on this edge makes $v$ a corresponding successor of $u$. Induction gives $M',v\not\models\psi$, so the modality fails at $u$.

Case 2: $E_T(\pi(u),b)$. No strict comparison is permitted within an $E_T$-class. Consequently, the witness conditions force $Z=\varnothing$.

Moreover, $\psi$ is a subformula of $\varphi$, so its truth is constant on this class. Since $\psi$ fails at $b$, it fails at $\pi(u)$, and therefore at $u$ by induction.

Reflexivity of every $E'_t$ and $R'_t$ makes $u$ itself a successor for $J_{X,Y,\varnothing}$. Thus the modality again fails at $u$.

This completes the induction. At the root,

$$ M',(a_0)\models\varphi. $$

7. Encoding the tree as realizable aggregate states

For each $t\in T$, the quotient $\mathcal W/E'_t$ is at most countable. Choose an injection

$$ c_t:\mathcal W/E'_t\longrightarrow\mathbb Q\cap(0,\mu_t). $$

For example, enumerate its classes and assign the class numbered $n$ the rational value

$$ \frac{\mu_t}{n+2}. $$

Define

$$ h(u)_t=c_t([u]_{E'_t}). $$

Then

$$ h(u)_t=h(v)_t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad uE'_tv. $$

Joint separation makes $h$ injective. Set

$$ A=h(\mathcal W)\subseteq\prod_{t\in T}[0,\mu_t]. $$

Transport the preorders and atomic valuations through $h$. The equivalence above gives exactly the required coordinate equalities, while injectivity makes the transported preorders well-defined. Atomic locality was proved in Section 5.3. Induction on formulas therefore preserves truth, giving an aggregate state satisfying $\varphi$.

For completeness, every vector in the aggregate box is realizable on a single nonatomic probability space. Enumerate $T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_\tau\}$ and put

$$ s_0=0, \qquad s_i=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mu_{t_j}. $$

Use $[0,1)$ with Lebesgue measure and type sets

$$ [s_{i-1},s_i). $$

For a vector $z$, let action $1$ be chosen precisely on

$$ \bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau} [s_{i-1},s_{i-1}+z_{t_i}). $$

This measurable profile realizes $z$. The question imposes no measurability requirement on $A$ itself, so it also realizes every state in the constructed aggregate model.

The reverse implication, and hence the reduction equivalence, is proved.

8. Decision procedure and running time

The decision procedure is:

  • Construct $\alpha_\varphi$ from the support and input formula.
  • Run the unrestricted LPFD satisfiability decider on $\alpha_\varphi$.
  • Return its answer.

Sections 3–7 prove correctness for every support size, including fully supported formulas.

The construction has polynomial size. There are at most $L$ subformula occurrences, their total copied length is polynomial in $L$, and the additional modal indices contain at most $\tau$ types each. After expanding Boolean abbreviations, a safe bound on both output length and construction time is

$$ p(L,\tau)=C(L+\tau)^3 $$

for a fixed encoding-dependent constant $C$.

Fix an unrestricted LPFD decider. Let $G(n)$ be its maximum running time over encoded inputs of length at most $n$, with invalid encodings rejected. Because the decider is total and there are finitely many such inputs, $G$ is a computable function.

Thus the aggregate decision procedure has the recursive running-time bound

$$ \operatorname{Time}(m,\tau,L) = O\!\left(p(L,\tau)+G(p(L,\tau))\right), \qquad m=2. $$

This establishes decidability and a polynomial reduction to LPFD; it does not assert an elementary complexity bound. The positive rational masses affect only the input-reading cost and the model embedding, not the satisfiability test.
