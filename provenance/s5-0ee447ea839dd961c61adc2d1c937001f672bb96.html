4. A repaired opportunity-quota lemma

Lemma. Under the resource-normalization assumption, fix the arrival order, mechanism randomness, and all reports other than those of a matching-phase mediator $b$. At every prefix after $b$’s arrival, there is a nonnegative quantity $K_b$, independent of $b$’s report, such that:

  • every report of $b$ produces completed matched mass at most $K_b$;
  • if its effective eligible supply has mass $s_b$, its completed matched mass is exactly

$$ \min\{K_b,s_b\}. $$

Its supply is taken in increasing reported-cost order. A symmetric statement holds for advertisers.

Proof. Immediately before $b$ arrives, let $S_0$ and $D_0$ be total residual eligible effective supply and capacity.

The greedy algorithm maintains
$$ S_0D_0=0. $$
Indeed, after any arrival, it exhausts either the arriving entity’s eligible mass or all waiting opposing mass. Starting with empty queues, induction proves that residual positive mass never remains on both sides.

Let $A(j)$ be total effective eligible capacity introduced after $b$’s arrival and through prefix $j$. Define
$$ K_b(j)=\max\{0,D_0+A(j)-S_0\}. $$

All $S_0$ units belong to earlier mediators and therefore precede $b$. All subsequent mediators follow $b$.

If $S_0>0$, then $D_0=0$. The first $S_0$ units of subsequent capacity serve those earlier mediators. Any additional capacity serves $b$ before later mediators while $b$ has eligible residual supply.

If $D_0>0$, then $S_0=0$. Mediator $b$ immediately receives up to $D_0$ units of matching opportunity, followed by subsequent capacity while it retains eligible supply.

These statements also cover $S_0=D_0=0$. Hence $b$ receives exactly
$$ \min\{K_b(j),s_b\}. $$
Its internal increasing-cost order determines which supply receives that mass.

The state before $b$ arrives is independent of its report. Crucially, normalization makes each other advertiser’s effective capacity fixed independently of $b$’s report. Thus $A(j)$, and therefore $K_b(j)$, are report-independent.

Interchanging supply and capacity proves the advertiser statement: if $D_0$ is earlier residual demand and $S(j)$ is subsequent effective eligible supply, its opportunity quota is
$$ J_a(j)=\max\{0,S_0+S(j)-D_0\}. $$
This is independent of the advertiser’s own report. $\square$

Both $K_b(j)$ and $J_a(j)$ are nondecreasing in $j$.

This proof covers arbitrary normalized opponent reports, including false costs, concealed resources, split records, relabeled records, and declarations that normalization reduces to zero. It uses fixed effective resources; a mere prohibition on fictitious completed matches would not establish that premise.

5. Payments and economic guarantees under normalization

5.1 Forwarding for an entire labeled user

For any effective eligible supply record $p$ of mediator $b$, let its reported cost be $d_p$, its effective mass be $w_p$, and the mediator’s current completed mass be $q_b$.

Define $X_p^q(z)$ by changing only this record’s cost to $z$ and taking up to $q$ units of eligible supply from the mediator in increasing reported-cost order. Set $X_p^q(z)=0$ when that report makes the record ineligible.

Recommend cumulative forwarding
$$ F_p=d_px_p+\int_{d_p}^{H}X_p^{q_b}(z)\,dz $$
for eligible records, and $F_p=0$ otherwise.

At the actual report,
$$ x_p=X_p^{q_b}(d_p). $$
For fixed $q$, increasing $z$ moves the record later in the order and eventually makes it ineligible. Thus $X_p^q(z)$ is nonincreasing in $z$. It follows that
$$ 0\le F_p \le d_px_p+(H-d_p)x_p =Hx_p. $$
Consequently, at every prefix,
$$ \sum_{p\text{ in }b}F_p\le Hq_b=T_b. $$

The integral is a finite sum: between consecutive other reported costs, the allocation is constant. No infinitesimal users need to be constructed.

5.2 User truthfulness

Fix a user whose mediator reports actual supply, transmits received cost reports, and follows forwarding recommendations. Other reports remain arbitrary.

Observed users always receive zero allocation and payment.

For a matching-phase user, its mass and label are unchanged by its cost report. Among eligible cost reports, the mediator’s total eligible mass is constant. The opportunity lemma therefore makes its completed quota $q$ independent of this user’s eligible report.

Put
$$ X(z)=X_p^q(z). $$
This is nonincreasing. For an eligible report $d$, allocation and payment are
$$ x(d)=X(d), \qquad F(d)=dX(d)+\int_d^H X(z)\,dz. $$

Suppose the true cost $c$ is eligible. Truthful utility is
$$ U(c;c)=\int_c^H X(z)\,dz. $$
For an eligible deviation $d\ge c$,
$$ U(c;c)-U(c;d) = \int_c^d\bigl(X(z)-X(d)\bigr)\,dz \ge0. $$
For an eligible deviation $d<c$,
$$ U(c;c)-U(c;d) = \int_d^c\bigl(X(d)-X(z)\bigr)\,dz \ge0. $$
Both inequalities follow from monotonicity. An ineligible deviation yields zero utility.

If $c>H$, truthful reporting is ineligible and gives zero. Every eligible deviation satisfies
$$ F(d)-cX(d)\le(H-c)X(d)\le0. $$

At $c=H$, truthful utility is zero regardless of boundary eligibility. The same payment bound shows that no deviation gives positive utility.

These arguments also cover $H=0$: payments are zero, and any positive-cost matched allocation has nonpositive utility.

Thus truthful cost reporting weakly maximizes terminal user utility.

5.3 Mediator truthfulness

Assume the mediator’s users report their actual costs. A matching-phase mediator’s terminal utility is
$$ Hq_b-\sum_{p\in P(b)}c_px_p = \sum_{p\in P(b)}(H-c_p)x_p. $$
Forwarding does not enter this expression.

Every deviation has total delivered mass at most $K_b$, by the opportunity lemma. Normalization also prevents duplication or delivery of supply belonging to another entity.

Among all selections of actual supply with total mass at most $K_b$, the displayed utility is maximized by taking the cheapest supply with cost below $H$, up to the quota. Supply of cost $H$ contributes zero; supply above $H$ contributes negatively.

A truthful report induces exactly this maximizing selection. Boundary tie rules may omit or include some zero-contribution supply, without changing the maximum.

Hence concealment, splitting, relabeling, or false costs cannot improve mediator utility.

5.4 Advertiser truthfulness

For a matching-phase advertiser with true value $v$, true capacity $u$, and report-independent opportunity quota $J_a$, every report produces feasible completed mass
$$ x\le\min\{J_a,u\}. $$
Its utility is
$$ (v-B)x. $$

If $v>B$, truthful reporting makes it eligible and obtains $\min\{J_a,u\}$, maximizing utility.

If $v<B$, truthful reporting obtains zero, whereas every eligible deviation gives nonpositive utility.

If $v=B$, every feasible allocation gives zero utility.

Observed advertisers always have zero allocation and payment. Thus truthful value and capacity reporting weakly maximize terminal utility.

All three arguments hold for every fixed arrival order and mechanism seed. They establish universal three-sided truthfulness under the additional normalization assumption.

5.5 Continuous individual rationality

For fixed reports, the mediator’s completed quota $q_b$ never decreases. For every $z$, increasing $q$ cannot decrease $X_p^q(z)$.

A truthful eligible user therefore has prefix utility
$$ F_p-c_px_p = \int_{c_p}^{H}X_p^{q_b}(z)\,dz, $$
which is nonnegative and nondecreasing. Ineligible truthful users have constant zero utility.

Every increment of truthful mediator allocation changes its utility by
$$ (H-c_p)\,dx_p\ge0. $$
Every increment of truthful advertiser allocation changes its utility by
$$ (v_a-B)\,dx_a\ge0. $$
Observed entities retain zero utility. All utilities start at zero.

5.6 Budget balance

Let $Z$ be total completed matched mass at a prefix. Then
$$ \sum_a C_a-\sum_b T_b=(B-H)Z\ge0. $$
The forwarding bound already proves that each mediator’s recommendations are funded at every prefix.

6. The weighted welfare bound

This section establishes the requested welfare bound for the truthful weighted allocation. It does not require normalization to change truthful reports.

6.1 Canonical weighted trade is optimal

Let $W$ be the smaller of total supply and capacity. Parametrize increasing-cost supply and decreasing-value capacity by mass, writing their respective step functions as $C(s)$ and $V(s)$ for $0<s\le W$.

For any feasible allocation of total mass $q$, its supply cost is at least
$$ \int_0^q C(s)\,ds, $$
and its advertiser value is at most
$$ \int_0^q V(s)\,ds. $$
The first inequality follows because selecting the cheapest $q$ units minimizes cost; the second follows because selecting the highest-valued $q$ units maximizes value. Both selections can be matched because there are no pair-specific feasibility constraints.

Thus the best gain at mass $q$ is
$$ G(q)=\int_0^q\bigl(V(s)-C(s)\bigr)\,ds. $$
The integrand is nonincreasing. Canonical trade includes all strictly positive-gain ranks and excludes all strictly negative-gain ranks. Its treatment of zero-gain ranks does not affect the objective. Therefore its gain equals $\mathrm{OPT}$.

$$ \left\lceil \left(1-\frac{2\alpha^{1/3}}r\right)M_T \right\rceil, $$

Only this allocation guarantee is used. The divisible-user payment arguments were proved independently in Section 5.

6.3 Exact finite subdivision

Expand the support into its $N$ separate entities for this proof.

Choose an integer $D>0$ such that every supply mass and capacity is an integer multiple of $1/D$. For an integer $j>0$, let
$$ \delta=\frac1{Dj}. $$
Replace each actual user $p$ by $w_p/\delta$ unit users of cost $c_p$, all belonging to its original mediator. Replace advertiser capacity $u_a$ by $u_a/\delta$ unit slots. Order these units consistently with their original blocks.

The resulting canonical assignment has size
$$ M=\frac Q\delta, $$
and its optimum is $\mathrm{OPT}/\delta$. Moreover,
$$ \frac{u_a/\delta}{M}\le\alpha, \qquad \frac{\sum_{p\in P(b)}w_p/\delta}{M}\le\alpha. $$
Since $Q>0$, an advertiser exists. Its positive integer capacity is at least one, so
$$ 1\le\frac{u_a}{\delta}\le\alpha M. $$
Hence $\alpha\ge1/M$. All discrete theorem hypotheses are satisfied.

Fix an observation set with $Q_T>0$. The discrete threshold rank corresponds to mass coordinate
$$ \delta\left\lceil\frac{\lambda Q_T}{\delta}\right\rceil. $$
If $\lambda Q_T$ lies strictly inside an original block, this coordinate lies in the same block for sufficiently small $\delta$.

If it lies exactly at an original block boundary, that boundary is an integer multiple of $\delta$. The ceiling therefore selects the block ending there, as required.

The threshold unit’s position inside its observed block cannot change eligibility of matching-phase entities: observed entities never trade, and numerical ties against a different entity are resolved by the fixed entity order.

Once threshold blocks agree, eligibility agrees. The FIFO matching quantities agree after multiplying the discrete quantities by $\delta$, because every original mass is an integer multiple of $\delta$. Thus, for this subdivision, equality of welfare holds for every arrival order and observation outcome.

Applying the discrete bound and rescaling gives
$$ \mathbb E[\mathrm{GFT}] \ge \left( 1-r-\frac{22\alpha^{1/3}}r -10\exp(-2/\alpha^{1/3}) \right)\mathrm{OPT}. $$

7. Polynomial computation and the remaining open step

$$ a\ge2^{-\ell}. $$
Use mesh width
$$ \eta=2^{-(\ell+3)} $$

$$ r^6\ge4^6\alpha. $$

$$ 4a\le r<4a+\eta \le\frac{33}{8}a<\frac92a. $$

7.2 Matching, forwarding, and termination

After normalization, every matching step transfers positive mass and exhausts a supply record or an advertiser’s residual capacity. Exhausted objects leave their queues. Consequently, there are at most as many matching steps as admitted supply records plus advertiser entities.

Unsupported records are removed during normalization and never enter a matching loop. Partial support and overlapping claims are resolved before matching, so there are no unspecified unsuccessful-transfer iterations.

Forwarding integrals are computed by scanning the finitely many other cost breakpoints. Even recomputing all recommendations at every arrival takes polynomial time.

On truthful inputs there are at most $Nm$ user records. Residual masses are sums and differences of input masses; payments are finite sums of mass-cost products. Their rational representations have polynomial bit length. Together with the assumed polynomial-time normalization, the mechanism runs in time polynomial in $N,m,\tau,L$.

For arbitrary reports of unrestricted length, the corresponding bound must additionally count the length of those reports and backing certificates.
