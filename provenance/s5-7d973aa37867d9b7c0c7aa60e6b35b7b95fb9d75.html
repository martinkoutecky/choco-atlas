The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite applicant and room type sets \(T\) and \(S\), binary-rational masses \(\mu_t,\nu_s\geq 0\) summing to \(1\), continuous monotone decreasing piecewise-linear utilities \(v_{t,s}\), and \(\varepsilon>0\), find nonnegative type-level prices \(p_s\) and flows \(x_{t,s}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_s x_{t,s}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{t,s}=\nu_s\), such that every \(x_{t,s}>0\) satisfies, for every \(q\in S\), \((1+\varepsilon)v_{t,s}(p_s)\geq v_{t,q}(p_q)\) when \(v_{t,s}(p_s)\geq 0\), and \(v_{t,s}(p_s)\geq(1+\varepsilon)v_{t,q}(p_q)\) otherwise?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given nonempty finite applicant and room supports \(T,S\), put \(\tau=|T|\) and \(m=|S|\). A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Room supplies \(\nu_s\) are likewise positive binary rationals, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=\sum_s\nu_s=1\).
For each \((t,s)\), the utility \(v_{t,s}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\) is continuous and piecewise linear, specified by a nonnegative rational base value, increasing rational breakpoints starting at zero, and strictly negative rational slopes; the last piece extends to infinity. The approximation parameter \(\varepsilon>0\) is binary rational, and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Find rational prices \(p_s\geq0\) and flows \(x_{t,s}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_s x_{t,s}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{t,s}=\nu_s\). Prices are shared by all rooms of a type; flows split applicant mass among room types. No total rent is prescribed.
For every \(x_{t,s}>0\) and every \(q\in S\), require \((1+\varepsilon)v_{t,s}(p_s)\geq v_{t,q}(p_q)\) if \(v_{t,s}(p_s)\geq0\), and \(v_{t,s}(p_s)\geq(1+\varepsilon)v_{t,q}(p_q)\) otherwise. Ties are allowed.

Does this mass allocation problem admit an FPTAS computing the stated guarantee in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\), or can such a scheme be ruled out under standard complexity assumptions?

For equally many individual applicants and rooms, with bijective allocation and these utility assumptions, an FPTAS computes the same multiplicative envy guarantee with nonnegative prices ([Arunachaleswaran–Barman–Rathi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04163)).
No result settling this precise formulation with binary-rational applicant masses, room supplies, and shared type prices was located in the literature search.

A fully polynomial-time approximation scheme exists

The stated problem admits an FPTAS. The construction uses the freedom to choose total rent: it returns sufficiently large nonnegative prices, at which all utilities are negative, and proves the required signed multiplicative guarantee.

The algorithm handles arbitrary binary-rational masses directly, without expanding them into individual applicants.

1. Reduction to the final linear pieces

For each pair \((t,s)\), let \(h_{t,s}\) be its last breakpoint and write its final piece as
\[ v_{t,s}(p)=a_{t,s}-b_{t,s}p \qquad\text{for }p\ge h_{t,s}, \]
where \(b_{t,s}>0\). The coefficients \(a_{t,s}\) are rational and can be computed exactly from the given base values, breakpoints, and slopes. Their encoding lengths are polynomial in \(L\).

Define
\[ B=\max_{t,s}h_{t,s},\qquad A=\max\{1,\max_{t,s}|a_{t,s}|\},\qquad \alpha=\min_{t,s}b_{t,s}>0. \]
All three quantities are rational and have polynomial encoding length.

Put
\[ \eta=\min\{\varepsilon,1\}, \qquad \beta=1+\frac{\eta}{2}. \]

We will construct rational numbers \(P_s\ge1\) and a feasible flow such that, whenever \(x_{t,s}>0\),
\[ b_{t,s}P_s\le\beta b_{t,q}P_q \qquad\text{for every }q\in S. \tag{1} \]
A common scaling of the \(P_s\) will then absorb all intercepts.

2. Round slopes and solve one transportation problem

For each pair \((t,s)\), choose the unique nonnegative integer \(k_{t,s}\) satisfying
\[ \beta^{k_{t,s}} \le \frac{b_{t,s}}{\alpha} <\beta^{k_{t,s}+1}. \tag{2} \]
Define
\[ \widetilde b_{t,s}=\alpha\beta^{k_{t,s}}. \]
Thus
\[ \widetilde b_{t,s}\le b_{t,s}<\beta\widetilde b_{t,s}. \tag{3} \]

Compute an exact optimal solution \(x\) of the rational transportation linear program
\[ \min\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{s\in S}k_{t,s}x_{t,s} \]
subject to
\[ x_{t,s}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{s\in S}x_{t,s}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t\in T}x_{t,s}=\nu_s. \tag{4} \]

This program is feasible: \(x_{t,s}=\mu_t\nu_s\) satisfies its constraints. Its feasible region is compact because every variable belongs to \([0,1]\). Consequently, it has an optimum.

We use the standard polynomial-time solvability of rational linear programming, including exact recovery of an optimal rational solution of polynomial encoding length. See the primary course notes on [LP algorithms](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/18/Scribe/s14-lpAlgorithms/s14-lpAlgorithms.html) and [rational encoding bounds](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n11-lp.html). The input-size bound needed for this application is verified in Section 5 below.

3. Construct rational room prices from shortest paths

Construct a directed multigraph on vertex set \(S\). For every supported allocation edge \(x_{t,s}>0\), and every room type \(q\), add an arc from \(s\) to \(q\) of weight
\[ k_{t,q}-k_{t,s}. \tag{5} \]

Lemma: this graph has no negative directed cycle

Suppose otherwise. A negative closed walk contains a negative simple directed cycle. Write such a cycle as
\[ s_1\longrightarrow s_2\longrightarrow\cdots \longrightarrow s_\ell\longrightarrow s_1, \]
where \(s_{\ell+1}=s_1\). For each arc, choose the applicant type \(t_i\) responsible for that arc. Then
\[ x_{t_i,s_i}>0, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \bigl(k_{t_i,s_{i+1}}-k_{t_i,s_i}\bigr)<0. \]

Choose a positive rational
\[ d\le\min_{1\le i\le\ell}x_{t_i,s_i}. \]
For each \(i\), subtract \(d\) from \(x_{t_i,s_i}\) and add \(d\) to \(x_{t_i,s_{i+1}}\).

Each operation preserves its applicant’s row sum. Each cycle room loses and gains exactly \(d\), so column sums also remain unchanged. The removed pairs are distinct because the cycle rooms are distinct; therefore the choice of \(d\) guarantees nonnegativity. These conclusions still hold if an applicant type occurs more than once.

The objective changes by
\[ d\sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \bigl(k_{t_i,s_{i+1}}-k_{t_i,s_i}\bigr)<0, \]
contradicting optimality of \(x\). This proves the lemma.

Integer potentials and prices

Add a new source with a zero-weight arc to every room vertex. Let \(w_s\) be the shortest-path distance from this source to \(s\).

The lemma guarantees finite shortest-path distances. All arc weights are integers, so every \(w_s\) is an integer. The direct source arcs give \(w_s\le0\). For every arc in (5), shortest-path distances satisfy
\[ w_q-w_s\le k_{t,q}-k_{t,s}. \tag{6} \]

Set
\[ P_s=\beta^{-w_s}. \tag{7} \]
These numbers are rational and at least one.

For every \(x_{t,s}>0\), inequality (6) implies
\[ k_{t,s}-w_s\le k_{t,q}-w_q. \]
Since \(\beta>1\), it follows that
\[ \widetilde b_{t,s}P_s =\alpha\beta^{k_{t,s}-w_s} \le\alpha\beta^{k_{t,q}-w_q} =\widetilde b_{t,q}P_q. \]
Together with (3), this gives
\[ b_{t,s}P_s <\beta\widetilde b_{t,s}P_s \le\beta\widetilde b_{t,q}P_q \le\beta b_{t,q}P_q. \]
In particular, the promised inequality (1) holds.

4. Scale prices and prove the stated guarantee

Define the rational scaling factor
\[ H=1+B+\frac{6A}{\eta\alpha}, \]
and output
\[ p_s=HP_s. \tag{8} \]

Because \(P_s\ge1\), every price satisfies \(p_s>B\). Hence every utility is evaluated on its final linear piece. Furthermore,
\[ b_{t,s}p_s\ge\alpha H>\frac{6A}{\eta}\ge6A. \]
Therefore
\[ v_{t,s}(p_s)=a_{t,s}-b_{t,s}p_s<0 \qquad\text{for every }(t,s). \tag{9} \]

Define the positive disutility
\[ D_{t,s}=-v_{t,s}(p_s)=b_{t,s}p_s-a_{t,s}. \]
Fix an allocated pair \(x_{t,s}>0\) and an arbitrary \(q\in S\). By (1) and (8),
\[ D_{t,s} \le\beta b_{t,q}p_q+A. \tag{10} \]
Also,
\[ (1+\eta)D_{t,q} \ge(1+\eta)b_{t,q}p_q-(1+\eta)A. \tag{11} \]

Subtracting the upper bound in (10) from the lower bound in (11) gives
\[ \begin{aligned} &(1+\eta)b_{t,q}p_q-(1+\eta)A -\bigl(\beta b_{t,q}p_q+A\bigr)\\ &\qquad=\frac{\eta}{2}b_{t,q}p_q-(2+\eta)A\\ &\qquad\ge3A-(2+\eta)A =(1-\eta)A \ge0. \end{aligned} \]
Here we used \(b_{t,q}p_q\ge6A/\eta\) and \(\eta\le1\). Consequently,
\[ D_{t,s}\le(1+\eta)D_{t,q} \le(1+\varepsilon)D_{t,q}. \]
Negating yields
\[ v_{t,s}(p_s)\ge(1+\varepsilon)v_{t,q}(p_q). \]

By (9), this is precisely the required negative-utility branch of the guarantee. The flow constraints hold exactly because the output flow is the solution of (4). Prices are shared by room type by construction.

5. Polynomial running time and output length

It remains to verify that none of the constructions hides dependence on mass denominators or on numerically large prices.

Let
\[ K=\max_{t,s}k_{t,s}. \]
Since each positive slope has binary encoding length at most \(L\),
\[ \log\!\left(\frac{\max_{t,s}b_{t,s}}{\alpha}\right)=O(L). \]
Moreover, for \(0<\eta\le1\),
\[ \log\beta=\log(1+\eta/2)\ge\eta/4. \]
Thus
\[ K=O(L/\eta) =O\bigl(L(1+1/\varepsilon)\bigr). \tag{12} \]

The integers \(k_{t,s}\) can be found using repeated rational multiplication by \(\beta\) and exact comparisons in (2). This takes polynomially many operations on rationals of polynomial encoding length; no numerical logarithm is required.

The transportation LP has \(m\tau\) variables and \(m+\tau\) equality constraints. Its coefficients have encoding length polynomial in the input length and in \(\log(K+1)\). Exact rational LP optimization therefore takes polynomial time and returns a flow of polynomial encoding length. Its running time depends on the binary encoding lengths of the masses and supplies.

The graph has at most \(\tau m^2\) arcs. Each room arc has weight between \(-K\) and \(K\). A shortest path can be chosen simple because there are no negative cycles. It uses at most \(m-1\) room arcs, so
\[ -(m-1)K\le w_s\le0. \]
Shortest paths can therefore be computed in polynomial time, and the exponents in (7) are bounded by \((m-1)K\).

If \(\beta\) is encoded with \(O(L)\) bits, then each rational power \(\beta^{-w_s}\) has encoding length
\[ O\bigl(mK L\bigr), \]
which is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\). The quantities \(A,B,\alpha,\eta,H\) have polynomial encoding length, so the final prices also have polynomial encoding length and can be computed exactly in polynomial time.

This proves the required FPTAS for the full stated formulation. The freedom to choose total rent and to return negative utilities is essential to this particular construction.
