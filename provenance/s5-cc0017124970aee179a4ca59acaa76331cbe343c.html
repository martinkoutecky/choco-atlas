The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite item classes \(I\), bidder types \(\Theta\), rational type masses \(\mu_\theta\), rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_i\), and bundle bids \(b_\theta(S)\), compute an efficient mass allocation \(y^\star_{\theta,S}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_S y^\star_{\theta,S}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,S:i\in S}y^\star_{\theta,S}\le \sigma_i\). Then compute type- and package-symmetric payment rates \(p_{\theta,S}\) minimizing \(P=\sum_{\theta,S}p_{\theta,S}y^\star_{\theta,S}\), subject to no feasible coalition transfer \(r_{\theta,S,R}\) with \(0\le\sum_R r_{\theta,S,R}\le y^\star_{\theta,S}\) and \(\sum_{\theta,S,R:i\in R}r_{\theta,S,R}\le \sigma_i\) obtaining seller revenue above \(P\) while preserving each selected agent's utility \(b_\theta(S)-p_{\theta,S}\); among minimizers, return a bidder-Pareto-optimal payment schedule.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) bidder types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), normalized by \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) item classes \(I\), with nonnegative rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_i\). Each type has an explicitly enumerated XOR bid table \(b_\theta:2^I\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), with \(b_\theta(\varnothing)=0\); bids determine quasilinear utilities. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including these tables.
Compute any efficient mass allocation \(y^\star\ge0\), maximizing \(\sum_{\theta,S}b_\theta(S)y_{\theta,S}\) subject to \(\sum_Sy_{\theta,S}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,S:i\in S}y_{\theta,S}\le\sigma_i\).
For this fixed allocation, choose payment rates identical for all agents of type \(\theta\) assigned package \(S\), with \(0\le p_{\theta,S}\le b_\theta(S)\), \(p_{\theta,\varnothing}=0\), and zero rates on cells of zero allocated mass. Write \(u_{\theta,S}=b_\theta(S)-p_{\theta,S}\) and \(P=\sum_{\theta,S}p_{\theta,S}y^\star_{\theta,S}\).
A blocking coalition selects masses \(r_{\theta,S,R}\ge0\) from current allocation cells and assigns them nonempty packages \(R\), with \(\sum_Rr_{\theta,S,R}\le y^\star_{\theta,S}\) and \(\sum_{\theta,S,R:i\in R}r_{\theta,S,R}\le\sigma_i\); the seller makes its entire supply available to the coalition.
For every positive selected mass, its alternative payment rate \(q_{\theta,S,R}\) must satisfy \(0\le q_{\theta,S,R}\le b_\theta(R)-u_{\theta,S}\). The coalition blocks exactly when \(\sum_{\theta,S,R}r_{\theta,S,R}q_{\theta,S,R}>P\); agents outside the coalition need not retain their allocations. An unblocked payment schedule is a core schedule.
Minimize \(P\) over core schedules and return a bidder-Pareto-optimal minimizer: no core schedule on the same allocation weakly lowers every occupied nonempty cell's payment rate and strictly lowers at least one.

Is this exact allocation-and-payment problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite bidders, [Day and Raghavan](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol05011/DagSemProc.05011.9/DagSemProc.05011.9.pdf) establish finite termination of constraint generation at minimum-total-payment, bidder-Pareto-optimal core payments; the anchor provides no polynomial running-time bound.
The [published version](https://www.grace.umd.edu/~raghavan/preprints/fpea.pdf) retains this guarantee. No complexity classification for exactly the mass problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total bidder mass to one so that supplies and revenue are per capita; allowed nonnegative rational supplies.
  • Specified explicitly enumerated nonnegative rational XOR bids for every bundle, with zero empty-bundle bids; the complete tables count toward input length.
  • Defined efficiency as maximization of total reported bid value and allowed any efficient allocation, which is then fixed for payment selection.
  • Restored individual rationality and nonnegative forward-auction payments, with zero payments for empty bundles and irrelevant zero-mass cells.
  • Made coalition payments and utility preservation explicit; coalitions may select arbitrary submasses, recruit previously unallocated agents, and use the seller's entire supply.
  • Restricted coalition recipients to nonempty packages, matching the discrete definition's coalition of item recipients.
  • Required a strict improvement for at least one positive-mass winning cell in Pareto domination; the literal weak-preference-only definition would exclude every outcome.
  • Interpreted bidder-Pareto optimality among symmetric payment schedules on the fixed efficient allocation, as in the payment-determination problem.

Result

The exact problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Two rational linear programs suffice: one computes an efficient allocation; the other computes minimum-total-payment core rates on that fixed allocation. Every such payment minimizer is bidder-Pareto-optimal in the stated sense.

The key is that coalition masses are divisible. Maximum blocking revenue is a linear program, whose dual gives a polynomial-size formulation of the core condition.

1. Computing and fixing an efficient allocation

Let \(N=\tau 2^m\), the number of explicitly listed type-package cells. Because every bid table is explicitly enumerated, \(N=O(L)\).

The allocation program in the question has \(N\) nonnegative variables, \(\tau\) mass equalities, and \(m\) supply inequalities. It is feasible: assign every type its entire mass to the empty package. It is bounded, since every feasible allocation satisfies \(0\le y_{\theta,S}\le\mu_\theta\).

Consequently, exact rational linear programming computes an optimal allocation \(y^\star\) in polynomial time, with polynomial encoding length. Here we use the standard bit-complexity theorem for rational linear programming, including exact recovery of an optimum; see [Vishnoi’s linear-programming notes](https://nisheethvishnoi.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/lecture71.pdf).

Fix this allocation throughout the remaining argument. Define its occupied cells by

\(C=\{(\theta,S):y^\star_{\theta,S}>0\}\).

For \(c=(\theta,S)\in C\), write \(y_c=y^\star_{\theta,S}\), \(v_c=b_\theta(S)\), and \(p_c=p_{\theta,S}\). Let \(\mathcal R=2^I\setminus\{\varnothing\}\).

The payment bounds become \(0\le p_c\le v_c\), with \(p_c=0\) whenever \(S=\varnothing\). All unoccupied cells receive rate zero.

2. Maximum coalition revenue is a linear program

For a payment vector satisfying these bounds, define

\(d_{c,R}(p)=b_\theta(R)-v_c+p_c\), for \(c=(\theta,S)\in C\) and \(R\in\mathcal R\).

This is precisely the maximum permissible alternative payment rate for selected mass from cell \(c\) receiving package \(R\).

Consider the linear program with value

\(F(p)=\displaystyle\max_r\sum_{c\in C}\sum_{R\in\mathcal R}d_{c,R}(p)r_{c,R}\),

subject to

  • \(r_{c,R}\ge0\) for every \(c,R\);
  • \(\sum_{R\in\mathcal R}r_{c,R}\le y_c\) for every \(c\in C\);
  • \(\sum_{c\in C}\sum_{R\in\mathcal R:i\in R}r_{c,R}\le\sigma_i\) for every \(i\in I\).

Its feasible region is nonempty, since it contains zero, and compact, since \(0\le r_{c,R}\le y_c\). Its maximum therefore exists.

Claim. \(F(p)\) equals the maximum revenue obtainable by a coalition under the question’s payment restrictions.

Proof. Every admissible coalition gives a feasible \(r\). For every positive selected mass, its payment satisfies \(q_{c,R}\le d_{c,R}(p)\). Thus its revenue is at most the objective value of that \(r\), hence at most \(F(p)\).

Conversely, take an optimal LP solution. Whenever \(d_{c,R}(p)<0\), replace \(r_{c,R}\) by zero. This preserves feasibility because every constraint is an upper bound with nonnegative coefficients. It cannot decrease the objective. Since the original solution was optimal, the modified solution is also optimal.

Every remaining positive coordinate has \(d_{c,R}(p)\ge0\). Setting \(q_{c,R}=d_{c,R}(p)\) therefore satisfies both alternative-payment bounds and realizes revenue \(F(p)\). This proves the claim.

Writing \(P(p)=\sum_{c\in C}y_cp_c\), we conclude that

\(p\text{ is core}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(p)\le P(p)\).

In fact, \(F(p)\ge P(p)\): the coalition can select every occupied nonempty cell and retain its current package, paying its current rate. This uses no more supply than \(y^\star\). Thus core schedules satisfy \(F(p)=P(p)\).

3. An exact linear formulation of core payments

Introduce a nonnegative dual variable \(\alpha_c\) for each cell-capacity constraint and a nonnegative dual variable \(\beta_i\) for each supply constraint. The dual of the coalition LP is

\(\displaystyle\min_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\sum_{c\in C}y_c\alpha_c+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_i\beta_i\right)\),

subject to

\(\alpha_c+\sum_{i\in R}\beta_i\ge b_\theta(R)-v_c+p_c\quad(c=(\theta,S)\in C,\ R\in\mathcal R)\),

and \(\alpha_c,\beta_i\ge0\).

The primal is feasible and bounded. The dual is also feasible: choose \(\beta_i=0\) and \(\alpha_c=\max\{0,\max_{R\in\mathcal R}d_{c,R}(p)\}\). Strong linear-programming duality therefore applies, with attained equal optimal values. See [MIT’s notes on LP duality](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/18/Notes/n12-duality.html).

It follows that \(p\) is core exactly when there exist dual-feasible \(\alpha,\beta\) satisfying

\(\sum_{c\in C}y_c\alpha_c+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_i\beta_i\le\sum_{c\in C}y_cp_c\).

Indeed, this inequality and weak duality imply \(F(p)\le P(p)\). Conversely, if \(F(p)\le P(p)\), an optimal dual solution satisfies the inequality.

Accordingly, solve the following payment LP:

\(\displaystyle\min_{p,\alpha,\beta}\sum_{c\in C}y_cp_c\),

subject to

  • \(0\le p_c\le v_c\) for every \(c\in C\);
  • \(p_c=0\) for every occupied empty-package cell;
  • \(\alpha_c\ge0\) and \(\beta_i\ge0\);
  • \(\alpha_c+\sum_{i\in R}\beta_i-p_c\ge b_\theta(R)-v_c\) for every \(c=(\theta,S)\in C\) and \(R\in\mathcal R\);
  • \(\sum_{c\in C}y_c\alpha_c+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_i\beta_i-\sum_{c\in C}y_cp_c\le0\).

The feasible payment vectors are exactly the core schedules. All these expressions are linear because the allocation masses \(y_c\) have already been fixed.

4. Feasibility of the payment LP

We verify that every efficient allocation admits a core schedule under the exact blocking definition given.

Set \(p_c=v_c\) on occupied cells. These rates satisfy all payment restrictions, including zero rates for empty packages. Every occupied agent then has utility zero, and seller revenue equals optimal welfare:

\(P=\sum_{c\in C}y_cv_c=W^\star\).

For any feasible coalition mass vector \(r\), define

\(z_{\theta,R}=\sum_{c\in C:c=(\theta,S)}r_{c,R}\quad(R\in\mathcal R)\),

and assign the remaining type mass to the empty package:

\(z_{\theta,\varnothing}=\mu_\theta-\sum_{R\in\mathcal R}z_{\theta,R}\).

The latter quantity is nonnegative because the coalition selects at most the total allocated mass of each type. The resulting \(z\) satisfies all allocation mass equalities and supply inequalities.

At the proposed payments, \(d_{c,R}(p)=b_\theta(R)\). Hence the coalition LP objective equals the welfare of \(z\), which is at most \(W^\star\) by efficiency of \(y^\star\). Therefore \(F(p)\le W^\star=P\), so these payments are core.

By Section 3, they extend to a feasible point of the payment LP. Its objective is bounded below by zero. Thus the payment LP has an attained optimum.

5. Polynomial size and exact computation

Let \(K=|C|\le N\). The payment LP has \(2K+m\) variables. Its package constraints number

\(K(2^m-1)\le\tau 4^m\le N^2=O(L^2)\).

The remaining constraints number \(O(K+m)\). Each package constraint contains at most \(m+2\) variable coefficients.

All coefficients are rational. The bids and supplies come from the input, bid differences have polynomial bit length, and the computed allocation \(y^\star\) has polynomial encoding length. The complete payment LP therefore has encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

An exact rational LP algorithm computes optimal payment rates in polynomial time, with polynomial output length. Return those rates on occupied cells and zero elsewhere.

6. Bidder-Pareto optimality

Let \(p^\star\) minimize total payment over core schedules on \(y^\star\). Suppose another core schedule \(\widehat p\) weakly lowers every occupied nonempty cell’s payment and strictly lowers at least one.

Every such cell has \(y_c>0\), so

\(P(p^\star)-P(\widehat p)=\sum_{c\in C:S\ne\varnothing}y_c(p^\star_c-\widehat p_c)>0\).

Empty-package payments are zero in both schedules. Thus \(\widehat p\) has strictly smaller total payment, contradicting minimality. Every minimum-total-payment core schedule is therefore bidder-Pareto-optimal as required.

The finite-bidder constraint-generation guarantee in [Day and Raghavan, Theorem 4.2](https://www.grace.umd.edu/~raghavan/preprints/fpea.pdf) is consistent with this result. The polynomial bound proved here follows independently from divisible coalition masses, LP duality, and the explicit bid-table encoding.
