The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), masses \(\mu_t>0\), line locations \(x_t\), reports \(r_t\), full predictions \(\widehat{x}_t\), and \(\delta\in[0,0.5]\), determine the minimum worst-case \(\beta\) for a randomized facility mechanism that is truthful in expectation for each type’s common report, achieves \(1+\delta\) approximation when \(\widehat{x}_t=x_t\) for all \(t\), and minimizes \(\beta\) for \(C(\Pi,\mu)=\mathbb{E}[\max_{t:\mu_t>0}|x_t-Y|]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support \(T\): the list of \(\tau=|T|\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Support size counts listed types, not possible types.
Each type has a private location \(x_t\in\mathbb R\), a common report \(r_t\in\mathbb R\), and an externally supplied prediction \(\widehat x_t\in\mathbb R\). Masses are public and fixed under deviations; locations may coincide.
A mechanism \(F(\mu,r,\widehat x)\) returns a probability distribution on facility locations \(Y\in\mathbb R\) with finite first moment; it observes reports and predictions, not private locations.
Require anonymity under simultaneous permutation of type records, unanimity for every prediction, and scale equivariance under simultaneous positive scaling of reports, predictions, and outcomes.
Truthfulness means that, for every \(\mu,x,\widehat x,t\) and \(a\in\mathbb R\), \(\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\mu,x,\widehat x)}|x_t-Y|\leq\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\mu,(x_{-t},a),\widehat x)}|x_t-Y|\); a deviation changes the entire type's common report while holding its mass and prediction fixed.
Define \(C_F(\mu,x,\widehat x)=\mathbb E_{Y\sim F(\mu,x,\widehat x)}[\max_{t\in T}|x_t-Y|]\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}(x)=\min_{y\in\mathbb R}\max_{t\in T}|x_t-y|\).
For \(\delta\in[0,\tfrac12]\), require \(C_F(\mu,x,x)\leq(1+\delta)\operatorname{OPT}(x)\); robustness \(\beta\) means \(C_F(\mu,x,\widehat x)\leq\beta\operatorname{OPT}(x)\) for every society, location profile, and prediction, including zero-optimum profiles.
Any running times should use \(\tau\), total binary input length \(L\), and \(\delta\)'s encoding length; there is no candidate-count parameter \(m\). Computational inputs are rational, while the mechanism guarantees range over real locations and reports.
What is the optimal worst-case robustness \(\beta^*(\delta)\), defined as the infimum over all such truthful mechanisms satisfying \((1+\delta)\)-consistency, for \(\delta\in[0,\tfrac12]\)?
For finite agents on the line, full predictions admit the tight consistency–robustness trade-off \((1+\delta,2-\delta)\). [Verified source](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/3ec7806669b4048cdba4d1defc76ace3-Paper-Conference.pdf)
No result explicitly treating the mass model with common-report deviations was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized positive masses to total mass one and made them public and fixed under deviations.
  • Interpreted a common-report deviation as changing the report of the entire type, with predictions fixed, rather than changing an infinitesimal individual's report.
  • Made consistency and robustness uniform over all finite supports, masses, profiles, and predictions rather than properties of a single supplied instance.
  • Used real line locations, reports, predictions, and outcomes; rational inputs and their binary lengths apply when discussing computation.
  • Carried over anonymity, unanimity, and scale independence; anonymity permutes complete type records, and scale independence scales predictions along with reports.
  • Included coincident locations and singleton supports, handling zero optimal cost through multiplicative inequalities rather than undefined ratios.
  • Used an infimum to define optimal robustness without assuming an optimizing mechanism exists, and required finite first moments.

Result

The optimal robustness is \(\boxed{\beta^*(\delta)=2-\delta}\) for every \(\delta\in[0,\tfrac12]\), and the infimum is attained.

This matches the finite-agent frontier in [Balkanski, Gkatzelis, and Shahkarami, Theorem 1 and Proposition 1](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/3ec7806669b4048cdba4d1defc76ace3-Paper-Conference.pdf). The following proof applies directly to the stated mass model.

A society with one listed type is trivial by unanimity. The lower bound already follows from two listed types, each having mass \(\tfrac12\).

1. An identity for the objective

For any nonempty location profile, define its extremes, midpoint, and radius by
\(\ell=\min_t x_t\), \(u=\max_t x_t\), \(h=(\ell+u)/2\), and \(R=(u-\ell)/2\).

Every location lies between the extremes, so for every \(y\in\mathbb R\),

\(\max_t|x_t-y|=\max\{|\ell-y|,|u-y|\}=R+|y-h|\).

Indeed, when \(y\leq h\), the right endpoint is farthest, and when \(y\geq h\), the left endpoint is farthest. Consequently,

\(\operatorname{OPT}(x)=R\), and \(C_F(\mu,x,\widehat x)=R+\mathbb E|Y-h|\).

These identities also hold when \(R=0\).

2. Lower bound: three profiles suffice

Fix two types with masses \(\mu_1=\mu_2=\tfrac12\), and fix predictions \(\widehat x=(0,2)\). For an arbitrary admissible mechanism, denote its output distributions at three report profiles by

\(P=F(\mu,(0,2),\widehat x)\),
\(Q=F(\mu,(0,1),\widehat x)\),
\(S=F(\mu,(-1,1),\widehat x)\).

Write \(Y_P,Y_Q,Y_S\) for random variables with these respective distributions.

At the first profile the predictions are correct, the midpoint is \(1\), and the optimum is \(1\). Consistency and the objective identity give

\(1+\mathbb E|Y_P-1|\leq1+\delta\), hence \(\mathbb E|Y_P-1|\leq\delta\).

Now consider true locations \((0,1)\), with the same predictions. The second type can report \(2\), producing distribution \(P\). Truthfulness therefore implies

\(\mathbb E|1-Y_Q|\leq\mathbb E|1-Y_P|\leq\delta\).

The pointwise triangle inequality \(|y|+|1-y|\geq1\) yields

\(\mathbb E|Y_Q|\geq1-\mathbb E|1-Y_Q|\geq1-\delta\).

At this same true profile, the first type can report \(-1\), producing distribution \(S\). Truthfulness gives

\(\mathbb E|Y_S|\geq\mathbb E|Y_Q|\geq1-\delta\).

Finally, interpret \(S\) as the truthful output for private locations \((-1,1)\) and predictions \((0,2)\). This is legitimate because the mechanism observes reports and predictions, not private locations. The midpoint is \(0\) and the optimum is \(1\), so

\(C_F(\mu,(-1,1),(0,2))=1+\mathbb E|Y_S|\geq2-\delta\).

Thus every admissible mechanism has robustness at least \(2-\delta\). Throughout both deviations, masses and predictions remain fixed.

3. An attaining mechanism

For a report profile \(r\), define

\(\ell=\min_t r_t\), \(u=\max_t r_t\), \(h=(\ell+u)/2\), and \(p=(\min_t\widehat x_t+\max_t\widehat x_t)/2\).

We use two mechanisms:

  • The deterministic mechanism \(D\) outputs the clipped predicted midpoint

\(d=\min\{u,\max\{\ell,p\}\}\).

  • The lottery \(H\) outputs \(\ell\) with probability \(\tfrac14\), \(h\) with probability \(\tfrac12\), and \(u\) with probability \(\tfrac14\).

Define \(F_\delta\) to run \(D\) with probability \(1-2\delta\) and \(H\) with probability \(2\delta\). These probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one throughout the required range.

The mechanism ignores masses. Its truthfulness must therefore be verified for each listed type’s common-report deviation; no replication argument is needed.

3.1. Truthfulness of the clipped midpoint

For one type, \(D\) always outputs its report, so truthful reporting gives cost zero.

Suppose there are at least two types. Fix the reports of all types except the deviating type, and let their extremes be \(b\leq c\). Predictions remain fixed, so \(p\) remains fixed. As a function of the deviating report \(q\), the output is:

  • If \(p<b\), the projection of \(q\) onto the fixed interval \([p,b]\).
  • If \(p>c\), the projection of \(q\) onto the fixed interval \([c,p]\).
  • If \(p\in[b,c]\), the constant \(p\).

These formulas follow by clipping \(p\) to \([\min\{q,b\},\max\{q,c\}]\).

For either projection case, reporting the true location \(a\) selects a point of the fixed interval closest to \(a\). Every other report selects another point of that interval and therefore cannot decrease the distance to \(a\). The constant case is also truthful. Hence \(D\) is truthful.

3.2. Truthfulness of the endpoint–midpoint lottery

For true location \(a\), the expected cost under lottery \(H\) with report extremes \(\ell,u\) is

\(G_a(\ell,u)=\tfrac14\bigl(|a-\ell|+|a-u|+|2a-\ell-u|\bigr)\).

The reverse triangle inequality gives

\(|2a-\ell-u|\geq\bigl||a-\ell|-|a-u|\bigr|\),

and consequently

\(G_a(\ell,u)\geq\tfrac12\max\{|a-\ell|,|a-u|\}\).

If \(a\in[\ell,u]\), equality holds: putting \(A=a-\ell\) and \(B=u-a\), both nonnegative, gives

\(G_a(\ell,u)=\tfrac14(A+B+|A-B|)=\tfrac12\max\{A,B\}\).

Now fix a type with true location \(a\) in a society with at least two types, and put

\(d_a=\max_{s\neq t}|a-x_s|\).

Under truthful reports, \(a\) belongs to the reported interval and contributes distance zero to the maximum. Thus its expected cost is exactly \(d_a/2\).

After any deviation, let the new extremes be \(\ell',u'\). Every unchanged report \(x_s\), for \(s\neq t\), still belongs to \([\ell',u']\). Therefore,

\(\max\{|a-\ell'|,|a-u'|\}\geq d_a\),

and the preceding inequality gives \(G_a(\ell',u')\geq d_a/2\). No deviation improves the type’s cost.

For one type, truthful cost is zero. Hence \(H\) is truthful for every support size, including profiles with coincident locations.

3.3. Truthfulness of the mixture

For fixed \(\delta\), the mixing probabilities do not depend on reports. Multiplying the truthfulness inequalities for \(D\) and \(H\) by \(1-2\delta\) and \(2\delta\), respectively, and adding proves truthfulness in expectation of \(F_\delta\).

4. Performance and structural guarantees

Evaluate the mechanism at truthful reports, and retain the midpoint \(h\) and radius \(R\) from Section 1.

The output of \(D\) belongs to \([\ell,u]\), so its maximum distance is at most \(2R\). If predictions are correct, then \(p=h\), and its cost is exactly \(R\).

For \(H\), either endpoint has maximum distance \(2R\), while the midpoint has maximum distance \(R\). Thus, for every prediction,

\(C_H=\tfrac14(2R)+\tfrac12R+\tfrac14(2R)=\tfrac32R\).

Under correct predictions, the mixture therefore satisfies

\(C_{F_\delta}=(1-2\delta)R+(2\delta)\tfrac32R=(1+\delta)R\).

For arbitrary predictions,

\(C_{F_\delta}\leq(1-2\delta)2R+(2\delta)\tfrac32R=(2-\delta)R\).

When \(R=0\), both components output the common location for every prediction. Thus unanimity and the required zero-optimum robustness guarantee hold exactly.

Simultaneous permutation of type records preserves all report and prediction extremes, proving anonymity. Positive scaling multiplies all four possible output locations by the same factor and preserves their probabilities, proving scale equivariance. The output has finite support, hence finite first moment.

Together with Section 2, these guarantees establish \(\beta^*(\delta)=2-\delta\).

5. Rational-input implementation

For rational inputs and rational \(\delta\), the distribution has at most four atoms: \(d,\ell,h,u\), with respective probabilities \(1-2\delta,\delta/2,\delta,\delta/2\); coincident atoms can be merged.

Let \(B\) denote \(\delta\)’s encoding length. Computing the distribution requires \(O(\tau)\) rational comparisons and a constant number of rational arithmetic operations. Its exact description has length \(O(L+B)\), and elementary integer arithmetic gives a polynomial bit-time bound, for example \(O(\tau(L+B)^2)\).

The same formulas define the mechanism for all real reports, locations, predictions, and parameters in the required range.
