The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 6 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two fixed families represented by finite supports of valuation types, with rational masses and normalized nonatomic valuations encoded by rational piecewise-constant densities on a common partition of \(C=[0,1]\), find a cut \(x\in[0,1]\) and assign the two resulting intervals to the families. For each family \(j\), require that the mass of its types \(t\) satisfying \(v_t(X_j)\ge 1/2\) be at least \(\rho_j/2\), where \(\rho_j\) is that family’s total mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

The cake is \(C=[0,1]\), and there are two fixed, nonempty families with equal entitlements \(1/2\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(w_t\), each assigned to exactly one family \(T_j\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(\sum_t w_t=1\) and \(\rho_j=\sum_{t\in T_j}w_t>0\).
The input includes a common partition \(0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_m=1\) with binary rational endpoints. Each type has a nonnegative rational density \(f_t\), constant on each partition interval, defining a normalized nonatomic valuation \(v_t(A)=\int_A f_t(s)\,ds\), with \(v_t(C)=1\). Zero densities are allowed.
Find an exact rational cut \(x\in[0,1]\) and assign \([0,x]\) and \((x,1]\) bijectively to the families, in either order. The resulting shares must satisfy \(\sum_{t\in T_j:\,v_t(X_j)\geq 1/2}w_t\geq\rho_j/2\) for both families; equality counts as approval.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

Can this search problem be solved in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite lists of agents, a connected allocation approved by at least half of each of two equally entitled families always exists and has an efficient protocol ([Segal-Halevi and Nitzan](https://hit-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2058166/files/070_hiasDP-E-79.pdf)).
No result settling the exact binary-encoded mass search problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the society's total mass to one, interpreting the stated distribution literally; both family masses are positive.
  • Counted family-labelled valuation types in the total support size, allowing the same valuation to occur in both families.
  • Required nonnegative densities, allowed zero densities, and made the common partition endpoints binary rationals.
  • Allowed either assignment order and assigned the cut point to the left interval to obtain disjoint shares.
  • Specified exact rational output and total binary input length for the computational model.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The search problem is polynomial-time solvable. An exact rational allocation can be found using \(O(m\tau+\tau\log\tau)\) arithmetic operations and \(O((m\tau+\tau\log\tau)L^2)\) bit operations with schoolbook integer arithmetic. The output cut has \(O(L)\) bits.

The construction uses weighted medians of half-value cuts. It extends the median construction in [Segal-Halevi and Nitzan, Section 5.1](https://hit-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2058166/files/070_hiasDP-E-79.pdf); the proof below handles binary-encoded masses directly.

1. Compute a half-value cut for every type

Write \(d_{t,k}\) for type \(t\)'s density on the interval with endpoints \(a_{k-1},a_k\), and define its cumulative valuation by

\(F_t(x)=v_t([0,x])\).

Nonnegative densities imply that \(F_t\) is nondecreasing. It is continuous, with \(F_t(0)=0\) and \(F_t(1)=1\). Nonatomicity and normalization give

\(v_t((x,1])=1-F_t(x)\).

Compute the rational prefix values

\(S_{t,0}=0,\qquad S_{t,k}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}d_{t,\ell}(a_\ell-a_{\ell-1})\).

Let \(k_t\) be the first index satisfying \(S_{t,k_t}\geq 1/2\). Such an index exists because \(S_{t,m}=1\). Its minimality gives \(S_{t,k_t-1}<1/2\), so \(d_{t,k_t}>0\). Define

\(h_t=a_{k_t-1}+\dfrac{1/2-S_{t,k_t-1}}{d_{t,k_t}}\).

The numerator is positive and at most \(d_{t,k_t}(a_{k_t}-a_{k_t-1})\). Hence \(h_t\) belongs to \((a_{k_t-1},a_{k_t}]\), and the affine formula for \(F_t\) on this interval gives \(F_t(h_t)=1/2\).

Consequently, monotonicity proves both implications:

  • If \(x\geq h_t\), then \(v_t([0,x])\geq 1/2\).
  • If \(x\leq h_t\), then \(v_t((x,1])=1-F_t(x)\geq 1/2\).

These implications remain valid when zero densities create an interval of half-value cuts. Uniqueness of \(h_t\) is unnecessary.

2. A weighted median gives majority approval on both sides

For family \(j\), define \(q_j\) to be the smallest mark among \(\{h_t:t\in T_j\}\) satisfying

\(\sum_{t\in T_j:\,h_t\leq q_j}w_t\geq \rho_j/2\).

This mark exists because the cumulative mass at the largest mark is \(\rho_j>0\).

Minimality also gives

\(\sum_{t\in T_j:\,h_t<q_j}w_t<\rho_j/2\).

Indeed, if there are smaller marks, their total mass is the cumulative mass at the largest smaller mark, which must be below the threshold. If there are none, that mass is zero. Therefore

\(\sum_{t\in T_j:\,h_t\geq q_j}w_t =\rho_j-\sum_{t\in T_j:\,h_t<q_j}w_t >\rho_j/2\).

Combining these inequalities with Section 1 establishes:

  • Every cut \(x\geq q_j\) gives family \(j\) a left piece approved by mass at least \(\rho_j/2\).
  • Every cut \(x\leq q_j\) gives family \(j\) a right piece approved by mass at least \(\rho_j/2\).

Repeated marks cause no difficulty: all types at the median mark count in both inequalities.

3. Construct and prove the allocation

Choose

\(x=(q_1+q_2)/2\).

If \(q_1\leq q_2\), assign \([0,x]\) to family \(1\) and \((x,1]\) to family \(2\). Since \(q_1\leq x\leq q_2\), Section 2 proves

\(\sum_{t\in T_1:\,v_t([0,x])\geq 1/2}w_t\geq\rho_1/2\)

and

\(\sum_{t\in T_2:\,v_t((x,1])\geq 1/2}w_t\geq\rho_2/2\).

If \(q_2<q_1\), reverse the family assignments and apply the same argument.

When \(q_1=q_2\), either assignment works at their common mark. Approval includes equality, exactly as required.

Thus the algorithm always produces a valid allocation on every input satisfying the stated premises.

4. Exact arithmetic and running time

Computing all half-value marks requires \(O(m\tau)\) arithmetic operations. Sorting the marks within each family requires \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) comparisons in total. Computing family masses and scanning the sorted marks requires \(O(\tau)\) further operations.

To justify a bit-complexity bound, let \(D\) be twice the product of all denominators appearing in the input rationals. Since their total encoding length is at most \(L\), the integer \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits.

Every interval mass \(d_{t,k}(a_k-a_{k-1})\) has denominator dividing \(D\): expand it as \(d_{t,k}a_k-d_{t,k}a_{k-1}\), and observe that each product denominator divides \(D\). Therefore all prefix values can be represented as integers divided by \(D\). These prefix values lie in \([0,1]\), so their scaled numerators have \(O(L)\) bits.

For completeness, if

\(a_{k_t-1}=p/r,\qquad d_{t,k_t}=u/v,\qquad S_{t,k_t-1}=A/D\),

then \(u>0\) and

\(h_t=\dfrac{p}{r}+\dfrac{(D-2A)v}{2Du}\).

All factors have \(O(L)\) bits, so this expression produces a rational numerator and denominator with \(O(L)\) bits. Comparing two marks by cross multiplication, and forming their midpoint, likewise uses integers with \(O(L)\) bits.

The masses can also be scaled to integers \(W_t=Dw_t\). If \(R_j=\sum_{t\in T_j}W_t\), the weighted-median test is the exact integer comparison

\(2\sum_{\text{current prefix}}W_t\geq R_j\).

All these integers have \(O(L)\) bits because the total input mass is one.

Building \(D\), computing the scaled values, comparing marks, and forming the output each use a constant number of operations on \(O(L)\)-bit integers per arithmetic step. Schoolbook multiplication and division therefore yield the stated bound

\(O((m\tau+\tau\log\tau)L^2)\).

No expansion of a type into individual agents is needed. The exact binary-encoded mass search problem is therefore resolved by this polynomial-time algorithm.
