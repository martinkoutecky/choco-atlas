The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite role types \(T\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and a succinct nonnegative rational worth function \(w:2^T\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), let \(\Omega\) be a union of intervals \(\Omega_t\) of lengths \(\mu_t\). For a measurable coalition \(A\), set \(F(A)=\{t:\lambda(A\cap\Omega_t)=\mu_t\}\) and \(W(A)=w(F(A))\). Does there exist a finite measurable partition \(\Pi\) of \(\Omega\) and an integrable payoff density \(x\), individually rational at each type’s reservation rate \(w(\{t\})/\mu_t\), such that \(\int_C x=W(C)\) for every \(C\in\Pi\) and \(\int_A x\ge W(A)\) for every measurable \(A\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains a Boolean circuit that, on the incidence vector of \(S\subseteq T\), outputs a binary-rational worth \(w(S)\). Promise \(w(S)\ge0\) for every \(S\) and \(w(\varnothing)=0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; there is no separate candidate parameter \(m\).
Let \(\Omega=[0,1)\) be partitioned into consecutive half-open intervals \(\Omega_t\) of lengths \(\mu_t\), and let \(\lambda\) denote Lebesgue measure. For every Lebesgue-measurable \(A\subseteq\Omega\), define \(F(A)=\{t\in T:\lambda(A\cap\Omega_t)=\mu_t\}\) and \(W(A)=w(F(A))\).
Call an outcome feasible if it consists of a finite partition \(\Pi\) of \(\Omega\) into measurable sets of positive measure and a real payoff density \(x\in L^1(\Omega,\lambda)\), with \(x\ge w(\{t\})/\mu_t\) almost everywhere on each \(\Omega_t\), such that \(\int_C x\,d\lambda=W(C)\) for every \(C\in\Pi\) and \(\int_A x\,d\lambda\ge W(A)\) for every measurable \(A\subseteq\Omega\).

Is deciding whether a feasible outcome exists solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite-player games with succinct polynomial-time evaluable worth functions, coalition-structure core nonemptiness is \(\Delta_2^P\)-complete ([Greco et al., 2011](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/11/Papers/047.pdf)); its hardness construction uses nonnegative rational worths and zero empty-coalition worth.
No result settling the exact continuous decision problem defined above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the unspecified succinct representation explicit as a Boolean circuit outputting binary-rational worths, so evaluation and output length are polynomially bounded by the input size.
  • Normalized the unspecified empty-coalition worth to zero, consistent with the discrete hardness construction and the usual coalitional-game convention.
  • Interpreted individual rationality as a pointwise lower bound almost everywhere within each type interval.
  • Used consecutive half-open intervals in [0,1) to specify the measure space without additional geometric input.
  • Required partition cells to have positive measure; zero-measure cells have zero worth under the normalization and may be absorbed into another cell.
  • Defined L as total input bit length and omitted m because this problem has no candidate set or corresponding parameter.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even when every singleton has worth zero and every coalition worth belongs to \(\{0,2,3\}\). The reduction uses positive dyadic masses and a polynomial-size Boolean circuit.

First, we establish that the measurable formulation is exactly equivalent to a finite coalition-structure core problem. We then give a direct reduction from Boolean satisfiability.

1. Equivalence with finite feasibility

For a vector \(y\in\mathbb R^T\), write \(y(S)=\sum_{t\in S}y_t\). Call a pair \((\mathcal P,y)\) finitely feasible if \(\mathcal P\) is a partition of \(T\) into nonempty blocks and

  • \(y_t\ge w(\{t\})\) for every \(t\in T\);
  • \(y(B)=w(B)\) for every \(B\in\mathcal P\);
  • \(y(S)\ge w(S)\) for every \(S\subseteq T\).

This is the finite coalition-structure core definition used in [Greco et al., Section 2](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/11/Papers/047.pdf).

Proposition. A continuous feasible outcome exists if and only if a finitely feasible pair exists.

Continuous feasibility implies finite feasibility

Let \((\Pi,x)\) be continuously feasible, and define
\(y_t=\int_{\Omega_t}x\,d\lambda\).
The density lower bound gives \(y_t\ge w(\{t\})\ge0\), and also \(x\ge0\) almost everywhere.

For \(S\subseteq T\), put \(U_S=\bigcup_{t\in S}\Omega_t\). Positivity of all masses implies \(F(U_S)=S\). Stability therefore gives
\(y(S)=\int_{U_S}x\,d\lambda\ge w(S)\).

For each cell \(C\in\Pi\), let \(S_C=F(C)\). By definition, \(U_{S_C}\setminus C\) has measure zero. Since \(x\) is integrable and nonnegative almost everywhere,

\(y(S_C)=\int_{C\cap U_{S_C}}x\,d\lambda\le\int_Cx\,d\lambda=w(S_C)\).

Together with the preceding stability inequality, this yields
\(y(S_C)=w(S_C)\).

The sets \(S_C\) are pairwise disjoint. Indeed, if a type belonged to both \(S_C\) and \(S_D\) for distinct cells, the disjoint sets \(C\cap\Omega_t\) and \(D\cap\Omega_t\) would each have measure \(\mu_t>0\), which is impossible inside \(\Omega_t\).

Let \(U=\bigcup_{C\in\Pi}S_C\) and \(Z=T\setminus U\). Summing the cell equalities gives

\(y(T)=\int_\Omega x\,d\lambda=\sum_{C\in\Pi}w(S_C)=\sum_{C\in\Pi}y(S_C)=y(U)\).

Thus \(y(Z)=0\). Since every coordinate is nonnegative, \(y_t=0\) for every \(t\in Z\); consequently \(w(\{t\})=0\) there.

Take as \(\mathcal P\) all nonempty sets \(S_C\), together with the singleton blocks \(\{t\}\) for \(t\in Z\). They partition \(T\), and the equalities above verify block efficiency. Individual rationality and all coalition inequalities were already proved. Hence \((\mathcal P,y)\) is finitely feasible.

Finite feasibility implies continuous feasibility

Conversely, let \((\mathcal P,y)\) be finitely feasible. Define
\(x(\omega)=y_t/\mu_t\) on \(\Omega_t\), and let
\(\Pi=\{U_B:B\in\mathcal P\}\).

Every cell has positive measure. The density is integrable and satisfies the required lower bound. Also,

\(\int_{U_B}x\,d\lambda=y(B)=w(B)=W(U_B)\).

For any measurable \(A\), let \(S=F(A)\). Then \(U_S\setminus A\) is null. Since \(x\ge0\),

\(\int_Ax\,d\lambda\ge\int_{A\cap U_S}x\,d\lambda=y(S)\ge w(S)=W(A)\).

Thus the constructed outcome is continuously feasible. This proves the proposition.

2. A direct reduction from satisfiability

Let \(\varphi\) be a Boolean formula with variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\). Introduce variable types \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) and three additional types \(a,b,c\). Set

\(T=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n,a,b,c\}\), \(G=\{a,b,c\}\), \(\tau=n+3\).

For \(S\subseteq T\), let \(\alpha_S\) be the assignment in which \(z_i\) is true exactly when \(v_i\in S\).

Define worths by the following ordered rules:

  • If \(G\subseteq S\) and \(\alpha_S\) satisfies \(\varphi\), set \(w(S)=3\).
  • Otherwise, if \(S\subseteq G\) and \(|S|\ge2\), set \(w(S)=2\).
  • Otherwise, set \(w(S)=0\).

These rules give nonnegative integer worths, \(w(\varnothing)=0\), and \(w(\{t\})=0\) for every type.

Satisfiability implies feasibility

Suppose \(\alpha\) satisfies \(\varphi\). Let \(H\) contain exactly the variable types assigned true by \(\alpha\).

Use the partition consisting of \(B=G\cup H\) and singleton blocks for the remaining variable types. Set

\(y_a=y_b=y_c=1\), \(y_{v_i}=0\) for every \(i\).

The block \(B\) has worth and total payoff both equal to \(3\). Every remaining singleton has worth and payoff zero.

For an arbitrary coalition \(S\):

  • If \(w(S)=0\), then \(y(S)\ge0=w(S)\).
  • If \(w(S)=2\), then \(S\) contains at least two members of \(G\), so \(y(S)\ge2\).
  • If \(w(S)=3\), then \(G\subseteq S\), so \(y(S)=3\).

Hence this pair is finitely feasible, and the proposition supplies a continuous feasible outcome.

Unsatisfiability implies infeasibility

Suppose \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable. The first worth rule never applies. Every positive-worth coalition is therefore a subset of \(G\) containing at least two members, and has worth \(2\).

Any two such coalitions intersect: two subsets of a three-element set, each of size at least two, cannot be disjoint. Consequently, any partition of \(T\) contains at most one positive-worth block. Block efficiency in a finitely feasible pair would therefore imply \(y(T)\le2\).

But stability for the three pair coalitions gives

\(y_a+y_b\ge2\), \(y_a+y_c\ge2\), \(y_b+y_c\ge2\).

Adding these inequalities yields \(2(y_a+y_b+y_c)\ge6\), hence \(y(G)\ge3\). Individual rationality gives nonnegative payoffs for all other types, so \(y(T)\ge y(G)\ge3\), a contradiction.

Thus no finite feasible pair exists, and the proposition excludes continuous feasibility.

3. Encoding and conclusion

Choose \(q=\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil\). In any fixed ordering of the types, assign mass \(2^{-q}\) to the first \(\tau-1\) types and mass \(1-(\tau-1)2^{-q}\) to the last.

All masses are dyadic, sum to one, and are positive: the last mass is at least \(2^{-q}\) because \(\tau\le2^q\). Their total encoding length is \(O(\tau\log\tau)\).

The worth circuit evaluates \(\varphi\) on the variable incidence bits and checks the two elementary set conditions above. It has polynomial size and outputs one of three two-bit integers. Thus the entire instance, including its input length \(L\), is constructed in polynomial time.

We have proved that \(\varphi\) is satisfiable exactly when the constructed society admits a feasible outcome. Therefore the requested decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(\tau\) and \(L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
