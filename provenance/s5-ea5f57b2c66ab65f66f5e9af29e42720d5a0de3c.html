The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_b\), initial opinion masses \(x_{b,o}(0)\) with \(\sum_o x_{b,o}(0)=\mu_b\), a rational \(\beta>0\), type-specific belief costs, a nondecreasing piecewise-constant pressure schedule \(\lambda(t)\), a tolerance \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), a horizon \(H\), and accuracy \(\delta\), compute a \(\delta\)-accurate first time \(t_\epsilon\) at which \(\max(q_0(t),q_1(t))\ge 1-\epsilon\), or report that it exceeds \(H\), under a margin promise at \(H\). Here \(q_o(t)=\sum_b x_{b,o}(t)\), \(p_b(o\mid q,t)\) is the logit probability for opinion \(o\) under per-capita cost \(f_b(\operatorname{dist}(o,b))+\lambda(t)g(1)(1-q_o(t))\), and &#92;dot{x}_{b,o}(t)=\mu_b p_b(o\mid q,t)-x_{b,o}(t)&#92;dot.

Partial resolution and new polynomial-time cases

I do not settle the unrestricted binary-input problem. I prove the following additional results:

  • A constant-pressure interval has at most $2\tau+1$ equilibria; this bound is sharp.
  • Every equilibrium other than possibly $1/2$ is transcendental. In particular, the rational threshold states are never equilibria.
  • Under uniformly subcritical pressure, the promised existence of a threshold crossing can be decided in polynomial time, independently of the numerical horizon.
  • Under constant, uniformly subcritical pressure, the first-hitting-time problem itself is polynomial-time solvable.
  • With two pressure intervals, crossing speed can be smaller than $\exp(-2^n)$ despite uniform contraction and a fixed final margin.

I also give a repaired, self-contained numerical construction supporting the exponential upper bound for unrestricted inputs.

1. Conventions and scalar reduction

I assume that $\gamma$ is a positive binary rational supplied in the input, or a fixed positive rational. Its encoding is included in the total input length $L$ when applicable. Every running-time bound below concerns $m=2$.

Write

$$ y(t)=q_1(t),\qquad \sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}},\qquad d_a=\beta(2b_a-1)c_a, $$

and, on pressure interval $j$, write

$$ A_j=\beta\gamma\lambda_j,\qquad A_{\max}=\max_j A_j. $$

For each type, its total mass satisfies

$$ \frac{d}{dt}(x_{a,0}+x_{a,1}) =\mu_a-(x_{a,0}+x_{a,1}). $$

The prescribed initial total is $\mu_a$, so this total remains $\mu_a$. Consequently, $q_0=1-y$.

The cost difference is

$$ C_{a,0}-C_{a,1} =(2b_a-1)c_a+\lambda_j\gamma(2y-1). $$

Therefore the aggregate equation closes exactly:

$$ \dot y=F_j(y),\qquad F_j(y)=\sum_{a=1}^{\tau}\mu_a \sigma\bigl(d_a+A_j(2y-1)\bigr)-y. $$

The initial allocations affect this equation only through the rational number

$$ y(0)=\sum_a x_{a,1}(0). $$

Every logistic probability lies strictly between zero and one. Thus

$$ F_j(0)>0,\qquad F_j(1)<0. $$

Smoothness, uniqueness, and these inward endpoint velocities give a unique trajectory in $[0,1]$ on each interval. Concatenating the finitely many interval flows gives the stipulated continuous trajectory.

Differentiation gives

$$ F_j'(y) = 2A_j\sum_a\mu_a \sigma\bigl(d_a+A_j(2y-1)\bigr) \bigl(1-\sigma\bigl(d_a+A_j(2y-1)\bigr)\bigr)-1. $$

Since $\sigma(z)(1-\sigma(z))\le 1/4$,

$$ -1\le F_j'(y)\le \frac{A_j}{2}-1. $$

In particular, $|F_j(y)|\le 1$, and every $F_j$ is Lipschitz with constant

$$ D=1+\frac{A_{\max}}2. $$

All logarithms in running-time bounds below are base two; $\ln$ denotes the natural logarithm.

2. The maximum majority share occurs at an endpoint

Define

$$ r(t)=\left|y(t)-\frac12\right|. $$

Then the majority share is $1/2+r(t)$.

Proposition. There is an $a\in[0,H]$ such that $r$ is nonincreasing on $[0,a]$ and nondecreasing on $[a,H]$. Hence

$$ R_H = \max\left\{ \max\{y(0),1-y(0)\}, \max\{y(H),1-y(H)\} \right\}. $$

Proof. Away from the center and pressure changes, let $v=\dot r$. On either side of the center,

$$ \dot v=F_j'(y)v. $$

Thus $v$ retains its sign on such a segment.

At a fixed state,

$$ \frac{\partial}{\partial A} \sum_a\mu_a\sigma(d_a+A(2y-1)) = (2y-1)\sum_a\mu_a\sigma'(d_a+A(2y-1)). $$

For $y>1/2$, increasing pressure increases $\dot r=F_j(y)$. For $y<1/2$, it increases $\dot r=-F_j(y)$. Therefore every pressure change gives a nonnegative jump in the outward velocity.

At the center,

$$ F_j(1/2)=\sum_a\mu_a\sigma(d_a)-\frac12, $$

independently of pressure. If this number is zero, the center is stationary on every interval; uniqueness prevents another trajectory from reaching it in finite time. Otherwise, every center crossing has the same direction and changes the radial velocity from negative to positive.

These observations exclude any transition from outward to inward motion. They prove the asserted shape and endpoint formula. $\square$

Put

$$ \theta=1-\epsilon,\qquad r_\theta=\frac12-\epsilon. $$

If the initial majority share is at least $\theta$, the answer is exactly zero. Otherwise, any first threshold crossing occurs on the outward portion. Its radial velocity is positive: a scalar autonomous trajectory cannot reach an equilibrium in finite time, and an upward pressure change preserves positive outward velocity. Thus the threshold predicate is false before the first crossing and true afterwards.

3. Equilibria: a sharp support-size bound

Fix one pressure value $A>0$ and suppress the interval index.

Proposition. The equation $F(y)=0$ has at most $2\tau+1$ distinct solutions in $(0,1)$. This bound is achievable.

Upper bound. Set

$$ z=e^{2Ay},\qquad t_a=e^{A-d_a}>0. $$

Then

$$ \sigma(d_a+A(2y-1))=\frac{z}{z+t_a}, $$

and

$$ F'(y)=2A\sum_a\mu_a\frac{zt_a}{(z+t_a)^2}-1. $$

Multiplication by the positive denominator $\prod_a(z+t_a)^2$ gives the polynomial

$$ P(z) = 2A\sum_a\mu_a zt_a\prod_{b\ne a}(z+t_b)^2 -\prod_a(z+t_a)^2. $$

Its degree is $2\tau$, with leading coefficient $-1$. It is therefore nonzero and has at most $2\tau$ distinct positive roots. Since $y\mapsto e^{2Ay}$ is strictly increasing, $F'$ has at most $2\tau$ distinct zeros. Rolle’s theorem now gives at most $2\tau+1$ distinct zeros of $F$.

Sharpness. Choose any admissible positive rational masses $\mu_i$ summing to one, and define

$$ M_i=\sum_{k\le i}\mu_k,\qquad M_0=0,\qquad s_i=\frac{M_{i-1}+M_i}{2}. $$

Set $\beta=\gamma=1$, $\lambda=A$, and

$$ d_i=A(1-2s_i). $$

These signed costs are realizable by choosing $b_i$ according to the sign of $d_i$ and setting $c_i=|d_i|$. The logistic centers are exactly $s_i$.

Let $\mu_{\min}=\min_i\mu_i$, and consider

$$ u_i=M_{i-1}+\frac{\mu_i}{4},\qquad v_i=M_{i-1}+\frac{3\mu_i}{4}. $$

Each test point is at distance at least $\mu_{\min}/4$ from every center. The logistic mixture differs from the corresponding staircase value by at most

$$ e^{-A\mu_{\min}/2}. $$

Choose rational $A$ sufficiently large that this is less than $\mu_{\min}/8$. At $u_i$, the staircase value is $M_{i-1}$, so $F(u_i)<0$. At $v_i$, it is $M_i$, so $F(v_i)>0$.

Together with $F(0)>0$ and $F(1)<0$, the ordered test points give $2\tau+1$ disjoint intervals with opposite endpoint signs. Each contains a zero. The upper bound proves that there are exactly $2\tau+1$. $\square$

Thus a support of size $\tau$ can already create linearly many equilibria. No enumeration of unlisted possible types is involved.

4. Algebraic equilibria and threshold transversality

The following result uses the rational encoding assumptions.

Theorem. Every equilibrium other than possibly $1/2$ is transcendental. Moreover, $1/2$ is an equilibrium if and only if, for every positive disagreement cost $c$,

$$ \sum_{\{a:b_a=1,\ c_a=c\}}\mu_a = \sum_{\{a:b_a=0,\ c_a=c\}}\mu_a. $$

I use the Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem: exponentials of distinct algebraic numbers are linearly independent over the algebraic numbers. In particular, $e^\alpha$ is transcendental for nonzero algebraic $\alpha$. A proof and this precise statement appear in [Popescu’s paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14352).

4.1 Rational equilibria

Suppose $y$ is rational. The numbers

$$ h_a=d_a+A(2y-1) $$

are rational. Choose a positive integer $Q$ such that $n_a=Qh_a$ are integers, and put $T=e^{1/Q}$. This $T$ is transcendental.

For each positive integer $n$ occurring, let $w_n^+$ and $w_n^-$ be the total masses with $n_a=n$ and $n_a=-n$. Let $w_0$ be the total mass with $n_a=0$, and let $w_-=\sum_n w_n^-$. Using $\sigma(-h)=1-\sigma(h)$,

$$ \sum_a\mu_a\sigma(h_a) = w_-+\frac{w_0}{2} +\sum_{n>0}(w_n^+-w_n^-)\frac{T^n}{1+T^n}. $$

If this expression equals rational $y$, the corresponding rational function of an indeterminate $X$ must be identically equal to $y$. Otherwise, clearing denominators would give a nonzero rational polynomial vanishing at transcendental $T$.

The functions

$$ 1,\qquad \frac{X^n}{1+X^n}\quad(n>0) $$

are linearly independent over the rationals for any finite set of distinct $n$. Indeed, choose the largest $n$ with nonzero coefficient and a primitive $2n$th root of unity $\zeta$. The term with index $n$ has a pole at $\zeta$. No smaller-index term does: $\zeta^k=-1$ would require $k\equiv n$ modulo $2n$, impossible for $0<k<n$.

Consequently, $w_n^+=w_n^-$ for every $n$. The mixture value is then

$$ w_-+\frac{w_0}{2}=\frac12. $$

An equilibrium therefore requires $y=1/2$.

At $y=1/2$, the exponents are $d_a=\beta(2b_a-1)c_a$. Since $\beta>0$ is common to all types, the required pairing is exactly the stated balance of masses at each positive cost. Conversely, this balance gives mixture value $1/2$, proving sufficiency.

4.2 Algebraic irrational equilibria

Suppose instead that $y$ is algebraic and irrational. Write $h_a=2Ay+d_a-A$ and multiply the putative equality

$$ \sum_a\mu_a\frac{e^{h_a}}{1+e^{h_a}}=y $$

by $\prod_a(1+e^{h_a})$. The result is

$$ \sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}} \left(\sum_{a\in S}\mu_a-y\right) e^{\alpha_S}=0, $$

where

$$ \alpha_S=2A|S|y+\sum_{a\in S}(d_a-A). $$

All exponents and coefficients are algebraic. The empty subset has exponent zero and coefficient $-y\ne0$. No nonempty subset has exponent zero, since that would express $y$ as a rational number.

After grouping coincident exponents, the coefficient of $e^0$ remains nonzero. Lindemann–Weierstrass therefore contradicts the displayed equality. This excludes algebraic irrational equilibria and completes the proof. $\square$

In particular, for every pressure interval,

$$ F_j(\epsilon)\ne0,\qquad F_j(1-\epsilon)\ne0. $$

Define the strictly positive, instance-dependent quantity

$$ \kappa= \min_{1\le j\le K} \min\{|F_j(\epsilon)|,\ |F_j(1-\epsilon)|\}. $$

This establishes exact transversality at the threshold states. It does not establish a polynomial lower bound on $\kappa$.

5. A certified short-step numerical method

This section supplies the quantitative details needed for the algorithms below, including the two numerical clarifications requested by the verifier.

5.1 Logistic evaluation

For a rational argument $z$ and integer $s\ge1$, $\sigma(z)$ can be approximated within $2^{-s}$ in time polynomial in the argument’s encoding length and $s$.

Put $x=|z|$. If $x\ge s+4$, use the appropriate limiting value zero or one. Its error is at most

$$ e^{-x}\le 2^{-(s+4)}. $$

Otherwise, approximate $e^{-x}$ by its Taylor polynomial of degree $N-1$, where

$$ N=8(s+4). $$

Taylor’s remainder theorem and $N!\ge(N/e)^N$ give

$$ \left|e^{-x}-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-x)^k}{k!}\right| \le\frac{x^N}{N!} \le\left(\frac{ex}{N}\right)^N \le\left(\frac38\right)^N <2^{-N}. $$

Here $e<3$ and $x<s+4$ were used. Clip the approximation to $[0,1]$; clipping cannot increase its error relative to $e^{-x}$.

On $[0,1]$, both maps

$$ E\longmapsto\frac{E}{1+E}, \qquad E\longmapsto\frac{1}{1+E} $$

have derivative of absolute value at most one. Thus rational division transfers the exponential error without amplification. Rounding the final result within $2^{-s-1}$ gives the claimed accuracy.

There are $O(s)$ rational operations, all on numbers of polynomial bit length.

5.2 Polynomial auxiliary system and explicit perturbed bounds

On an interval with pressure parameter $A$, introduce

$$ z_a=\sigma(d_a+A(2y-1)). $$

Along the exact trajectory,

$$ \dot y=\sum_a\mu_a z_a-y, $$

$$ \dot z_a = 2A z_a(1-z_a) \left(\sum_b\mu_bz_b-y\right). $$

This is a polynomial differential system of degree at most three.

Take a real initial vector in $[0,1]^{\tau+1}$ and its complex polydisc of radius one in the maximum norm. Within that polydisc,

$$ |y|\le2,\qquad |z_a|\le2,\qquad |1-z_a|\le2. $$

The vector field has maximum norm at most $32(A+1)$. Its Jacobian has maximum absolute row sum at most $56(A+1)$: the first row has sum two, and an auxiliary row has sum at most

$$ 2A\bigl(5\cdot4+4\cdot2\bigr)=56A. $$

Set

$$ R=\frac{1}{256(A+1)}. $$

Allow a perturbed initial vector at maximum distance $\rho\le1/4$ from the exact vector. Picard iteration on the complex time disc of radius $R$ has displacement from that perturbed initial vector at most

$$ 32(A+1)R=\frac18. $$

Its distance from the exact initial vector is consequently at most

$$ \rho+\frac18\le\frac38<1. $$

It therefore remains inside the same polydisc. Its contraction factor is at most

$$ 56(A+1)R=\frac{56}{256}<\frac14. $$

This proves existence of a holomorphic local solution with these explicit bounds.

Cauchy’s estimate, applied to its displacement from its initial vector, bounds the Taylor tail after degree $p$, for $\Delta\le R/2$, by

$$ \frac18\sum_{n=p+1}^{\infty}2^{-n} =2^{-p-3}. $$

The perturbed and exact solutions differ on this real step by at most

$$ \rho e^{56(A+1)\Delta}<2\rho. $$

Thus, for any $0<\xi\le1/8$, a scalar step of length

$$ \Delta\le\frac{1}{512(A+1)} $$

can be computed within error $\xi$: initialize the auxiliary coordinates within $\xi/8$, choose the Taylor order so that its tail is at most $\xi/4$, and round the scalar output within $\xi/4$. Projecting that output onto $[0,1]$ cannot increase its error.

5.3 Bit complexity

Coefficient comparison computes the Taylor coefficients using $\tau\operatorname{poly}(p)$ rational operations.

For completeness, let $Q$ be a common denominator for the polynomial coefficients and rounded initial coordinates. The $n$th time derivative has coefficient degree $n$ and initial-coordinate degree at most $2n+1$. Its evaluated denominator therefore divides $Q^{3n+1}$; division by $n!$ gives the Taylor coefficient. The Cauchy bounds above control its numerator size.

Hence the coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in $p$, the input length, and the requested precision. Together with logistic evaluation, this proves a short-step bit cost of the form

$$ \tau\,\operatorname{poly} \left(L+\log_2(1/\xi)\right), $$

with the rational step length’s encoding included when necessary.

6. Uniform contraction removes the numerical horizon from state evaluation

Assume now that

$$ A_{\max}\le2-2\alpha $$

for some known rational $0<\alpha\le1$. Then

$$ -1\le F_j'(y)\le-\alpha. $$

6.1 Contraction and equilibria

Each $F_j$ is strictly decreasing and has a unique equilibrium $e_j\in(0,1)$.

For two solutions on the same pressure interval, their difference satisfies a scalar linear equation whose coefficient lies in $[-1,-\alpha]$. Therefore the interval flow $\phi_j^s$ obeys

$$ |\phi_j^s(u)-\phi_j^s(v)| \le e^{-\alpha s}|u-v|. $$

Taking $v=e_j$ gives

$$ |\phi_j^s(u)-e_j|\le e^{-\alpha s}. $$

The equilibrium can be approximated to absolute error $z$ by bisection using approximate evaluations of $F_j$. Specifically, evaluate $F_j$ to error $\nu=\alpha z/4$. A certified positive or negative evaluation determines the root’s side. If the evaluation is ambiguous, then

$$ |F_j(y)|\le2\nu, $$

and the derivative bound gives

$$ |y-e_j|\le\frac{2\nu}{\alpha}=\frac z2. $$

Otherwise, continue until the bracket has length at most $z$. This uses $O(1+\log_2(1/z))$ evaluations, each at precision polynomial in

$$ L+\log_2(1/\alpha)+\log_2(1/z). $$

6.2 A state evaluator independent of numerical $H$

Fix a desired state error $0<w<1/4$. Put

$$ p=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{16K}{w}\right\rceil, \qquad T_{\mathrm{cut}}=\frac p\alpha. $$

Process the pressure intervals up to the queried time.

  • For an interval duration exceeding $T_{\mathrm{cut}}$, replace its output by an approximation to $e_j$ within $w/(16K)$. Its exact output is also within $w/(16K)$ of $e_j$, because $e^{-p}\le2^{-p}$.
  • For a shorter interval, use the short-step method to accuracy $w/(8K)$. Since $A_j<2$, the constant step bound $1/1536$ is valid.

Errors passed between intervals do not amplify, by contraction. Summing the local errors therefore gives total error at most $w$.

A short interval requires

$$ O\left(1+\frac{1+\log_2(K/w)}{\alpha}\right) $$

steps. For example, assigning every step an error budget equal to $w/(16K)$ divided by an upper bound on this step count gives the required interval accuracy.

Define

$$ P_w= L+\log_2 K+\log_2(1/\alpha)+\log_2(1/w). $$

The resulting state-evaluation bound is

$$ \mathcal T_{\mathrm{state}}(w) = O(m\tau+K) + \tau K \left( 1+\frac{1+\log_2(K/w)}{\alpha} \right) \operatorname{poly}(P_w). $$

There is no numerical dependence on $H$ or on $\max_a\beta c_a$. Large logistic arguments are handled by the saturation rule in Section 5.

6.3 Polynomial-time promised existence decision

Evaluate $y(H)$ within $\eta/8$ and use the endpoint formula from Section 2. The computed maximum differs from $R_H$ by at most $\eta/8$, so comparison with $\theta$ correctly distinguishes the two promise branches.

Thus, under uniform contraction, existence of a crossing is decidable in time

$$ O(m\tau+K) + \tau K \left( 1+\frac{1+\log_2(K/\eta)}{\alpha} \right) \operatorname{poly} \left( L+\log_2 K+\log_2(1/\alpha)+\log_2(1/\eta) \right). $$

In particular, this decision problem is polynomial-time solvable whenever $1/\alpha$ is polynomially bounded in $L$.

This conclusion concerns existence. Accurate first-hit timing requires a further argument.

7. A complete polynomial-time first-hit algorithm for constant subcritical pressure

Assume $K=1$ and

$$ A\le2-2\alpha. $$

The initial-threshold case is already settled. Otherwise, decide the promise branch using Section 6. In the low branch, report that the hitting time exceeds $H$.

In the high branch, choose $z=y$ or $z=1-y$ according to the opinion that exceeds the threshold at time $H$. The corresponding equation is

$$ \dot z=G(z),\qquad -1\le G'(z)\le-\alpha. $$

Its equilibrium $e$ satisfies

$$ e\ge z(H)\ge\theta+\eta. $$

Indeed, the initial value is below $\theta$, while the final value is above it. The scalar autonomous trajectory must therefore be increasing toward its unique equilibrium.

For every $z\le\theta$,

$$ G(z)\ge G(\theta) \ge\alpha(e-\theta) \ge\alpha\eta. $$

For $\theta\le z\le\theta+\eta/2$,

$$ G(z)\ge\alpha(e-z)\ge\frac{\alpha\eta}{2}. $$

These are quantitative crossing-speed bounds derived from the final promise.

Let

$$ T_0= \frac{\left\lceil\log_2(2/\eta)\right\rceil}{\alpha}, \qquad U=\min\{H,T_0\}. $$

If $T_0\le H$, contraction gives

$$ z(T_0)\ge e-e^{-\alpha T_0} \ge\theta+\frac{\eta}{2}. $$

If $H<T_0$, the high promise gives $z(H)\ge\theta+\eta$. Thus $[0,U]$ brackets the first crossing.

Use bisection, evaluating $z(t)$ within

$$ u=\min\left\{\frac{\eta}{16}, \frac{\alpha\eta\delta}{16}\right\}. $$

A value certified below or above $\theta$ updates the bracket. In an ambiguous comparison,

$$ |z(t)-\theta|\le2u. $$

Before the crossing, integration of $G\ge\alpha\eta$ gives

$$ \theta-z(t)\ge\alpha\eta(t_\epsilon-t). $$

After the crossing, the ambiguous value lies below $\theta+\eta/2$, so integration gives

$$ z(t)-\theta \ge\frac{\alpha\eta}{2}(t-t_\epsilon). $$

Consequently, an ambiguous query already satisfies

$$ |t-t_\epsilon| \le\frac{4u}{\alpha\eta} \le\frac{\delta}{4}. $$

Otherwise, terminate when the bracket has length at most $2\delta$ and return its midpoint.

To state the cost, put

$$ U_0=1+\frac{1+\log_2(1/\eta)}{\alpha}, $$

$$ S= L+\log_2(1/\alpha)+\log_2(1/\eta) +\log_2^+(1/\delta), $$

where $\log_2^+(x)=\max\{0,\log_2 x\}$. Each timing query can be integrated directly up to $U$ using $O(U_0)$ constant-size steps. Contraction allows the local errors to be summed without exponential amplification.

The complete running-time bound is therefore

$$ O(m\tau+K) + \tau U_0 \left(1+\log_2^+(U_0/\delta)\right) \operatorname{poly}(S), \qquad K=1. $$

Proven conclusion. The promised first-hitting-time problem is polynomial-time solvable for constant pressure whenever $1/\alpha$ is polynomially bounded in the encoded input length. In particular, any fixed bound $A\le2-c$ with $c>0$ gives polynomial time, even for exponentially large binary-encoded horizons and belief costs.

8. Several intervals: a horizon-independent conditioning bound

Here is an instance-sensitive improvement for arbitrary $K$.

Assume the initial majority share is below threshold and define

$$ d=r_\theta-r(0)>0. $$

Choose a certified rational lower bound $0<\kappa_0\le\kappa$, where $\kappa$ was defined in Section 4. Put

$$ h_*=\min\left\{\frac d2,\frac{\kappa_0}{2D}\right\}. $$

For the two possible majority sides, define the radial vector fields

$$ V_j^+(r)=F_j(1/2+r), \qquad V_j^-(r)=-F_j(1/2-r). $$

Both are $D$-Lipschitz, and their threshold values have absolute value at least $\kappa_0$.

Suppose a crossing exists and a queried time satisfies

$$ |r(t)-r_\theta|<h_*. $$

Then $r(t)>r(0)$, so Section 2 places this time on the outward portion. Inside this band, the active radial vector field differs from its threshold value by less than $\kappa_0/2$. Its sign must therefore match that threshold value, and its absolute value is at least $\kappa_0/2$. Since motion is outward, its value is positive.

$$ |r(t)-r_\theta| \ge\frac{\kappa_0}{2}|t-t_\epsilon|. $$

This local timing modulus has no factor $e^{-H}$.

A suitable bisection state tolerance is

$$ u= \min\left\{ \frac{\eta}{8}, \frac d{16}, \frac{\kappa_0}{16D}, \frac{\kappa_0\delta}{16} \right\}. $$

An ambiguous comparison gives $|r(t)-r_\theta|\le2u<h_*$ and hence time error at most $\delta/4$.

A lower bound $\kappa_0$ can be computed by adaptive certified evaluations of the finitely many threshold velocities. If an approximation $\widehat F$ with error $v$ satisfies $|\widehat F|>2v$, then

$$ |\widehat F|-v \le |F| <3(|\widehat F|-v). $$

Increasing precision until this occurs terminates because every threshold velocity is nonzero. Taking the minimum of these certified bounds gives

$$ \frac{\kappa}{3}<\kappa_0\le\kappa. $$

Doubling the precision at each unsuccessful stage gives cost polynomial in $L+\log_2(1/\kappa)$ per evaluation.

Under uniform contraction, $D$ can be replaced by one. Combining this modulus with Section 6 gives a first-hitting algorithm whose running time is polynomial in

$$ L,\quad \tau,\quad K,\quad \frac1\alpha,\quad \log_2(1/\eta),\quad \log_2^+(1/\delta),\quad \log_2(1/\kappa). $$

$$ B_\kappa= L+\log_2 K+\log_2(1/\alpha) +\log_2(1/\eta)+\log_2^+(1/\delta) +\log_2(1/\kappa), $$

$$ O(m\tau+K) + \tau K \left(1+\frac{B_\kappa}{\alpha}\right) \left(1+\log_2(H/\delta)\right) \operatorname{poly}(B_\kappa). $$

The remaining numerical obstacle is $\log_2(1/\kappa)$, not the numerical horizon. The next example shows that this quantity can be exponentially large even under uniform contraction.

9. Slow crossing with uniform contraction and a fixed margin

Take four types, with $\beta=\gamma=1$:

| Mass | Belief | Disagreement cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| $11/16$ | $1$ | $B$ |
| $3/16$ | $0$ | $B$ |
| $1/16$ | $0$ | $1/4$ |
| $1/16$ | $0$ | $5/4$ |

Let $B\ge8$, split each type equally initially between the opinions, and set

$$ y(0)=\frac12,\qquad \epsilon=\frac14,\qquad \eta=\frac1{2048},\qquad \delta=\frac1{16}. $$

Use pressure $3/2$ until time $8B$, then pressure $7/4$ until

$$ H=8B+1. $$

This pressure is positive and nondecreasing. Throughout,

$$ F_j'(y)\le\frac{7/4}{2}-1=-\frac18, $$

so the family has the fixed contraction parameter $\alpha=1/8$.

9.1 First-interval crossing speed

The threshold is $\theta=3/4$. At the first pressure, the two weak types have logistic arguments $1/2$ and $-1/2$. Their combined contribution is exactly $1/16$.

Set $u=e^{-B}$ and $a=e^{3/4}$. Direct substitution gives

$$ F_1(\theta) = \frac{u}{16} \left( \frac{3a}{1+ua} -\frac{11/a}{1+u/a} \right). $$

The elementary bounds

$$ a<3,\qquad a^2=e^{3/2}>4,\qquad u<\frac1{256} $$

imply

$$ \frac{3a}{1+ua}-\frac{11/a}{1+u/a} \ge 3a-3ua^2-\frac{11}{a} = \frac{3a^2-11}{a}-3ua^2 > \frac13-\frac{27}{256} >\frac18. $$

The same expression is less than $3a<9$. Therefore

$$ \frac{e^{-B}}{128} <F_1(\theta) <e^{-B}. $$

On this interval, $-1\le F_1'\le-1/4$. For $1/2\le y\le\theta$,

$$ F_1(\theta)+\frac{\theta-y}{4} \le F_1(y) \le F_1(\theta)+(\theta-y). $$

The field is positive throughout this interval, so separation of variables gives a finite crossing time and

$$ B-\ln4 <t_\epsilon \le 4\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{16F_1(\theta)}\right) < 4(B+\ln9) <8B. $$

Thus the first crossing precedes the pressure increase, with speed

$$ 0<\dot y(t_\epsilon)<e^{-B}. $$

9.2 Verification of the high promise

At the pressure increase, both weak-type arguments at $\theta$ increase by $1/8$, from $1/2,-1/2$ to $5/8,-3/8$.

For $|z|\le1$,

$$ \sigma'(z)=\frac1{2+e^z+e^{-z}}>\frac18, $$

using $e<3$. Each weak type’s logistic probability therefore increases by more than $1/64$. Including their masses, the increase in the mixture is more than $1/512$. The rigid types’ probabilities also increase. Hence

$$ F_2(\theta)>\frac1{512}. $$

Since $F_2'\ge-1$, throughout

$$ \theta\le y\le\theta+\frac1{1024} $$

we have

$$ F_2(y)>\frac1{1024}. $$

The state at time $8B$ is at least $\theta$. If below $\theta+1/1024$, it reaches that level within one time unit. If already above it, it cannot cross downwards through a level whose vector field is positive. Consequently,

$$ y(H)\ge\theta+\frac1{1024} >\theta+\eta. $$

Every member of this family satisfies the high promise.

Taking $B=2^n$ gives total encoding length $L=O(n)$, with fixed $\tau=4$, $K=2$, and fixed accuracy, margin, and contraction parameters. Nevertheless,

$$ \dot y(t_\epsilon)<\exp(-2^n), \qquad \log_2(1/\kappa)\ge\frac{2^n}{\ln2}. $$

This is a conditioning example, not a hardness proof. An algorithm exploiting the formula might avoid approximating the state at that absolute precision.

10. Unrestricted inputs: a certified exponential upper bound

For completeness, the short-step construction also proves an unconditional upper bound for the original problem.

Assume the initial state is below threshold, and keep

$$ d=r_\theta-r(0)>0. $$

On the outward portion, radial velocity satisfies

$$ \dot v\ge-v $$

between pressure changes, with nonnegative jumps at those changes. Thus, for outward times $w\le s$,

$$ v(w)\le e^{s-w}v(s). $$

If $a$ is the beginning of the outward portion, then $r(a)\le r(0)$. Integration gives, whenever $r(s)>r(0)$,

$$ r(s)-r(0) \le\int_a^s v(w)\,dw \le v(s)e^H. $$

Therefore

$$ v(s)\ge\bigl(r(s)-r(0)\bigr)e^{-H}. $$

If $|r(t)-r_\theta|<d/2$ and a crossing exists, the segment between $t$ and the crossing is outward and has radial value at least $r(0)+d/2$. Hence

$$ |r(t)-r_\theta| \ge\frac d2e^{-H}|t-t_\epsilon|. $$

Choose

$$ C=\lceil2H\rceil,\qquad u= \min\left\{ \frac{\eta}{8}, \frac d{16}, \frac{d\delta\,2^{-C}}{64} \right\}. $$

Because $2^{-C}\le e^{-H}$, an ambiguous state comparison at accuracy $u$ gives time error at most $\delta/16$. Final-state accuracy $u$ also distinguishes the promise branches by Section 2. Bisection therefore needs

$$ O\left(1+\log_2(H/\delta)\right) $$

trajectory evaluations.

For each evaluation, use steps bounded by

$$ h=\frac1{512(A_{\max}+1)} $$

and end steps at pressure changes. There are at most

$$ N=K+\left\lceil\frac Hh\right\rceil $$

steps. Set

$$ E=2^{\lceil2DH\rceil}\ge e^{DH} $$

and compute each step with local error at most $u/(4NE)$.

The exact scalar flow is $e^{D\Delta}$-Lipschitz over a step of duration $\Delta$. Summing the propagated local errors gives total error at most

$$ N\frac{u}{4NE}e^{DH}\le\frac u4. $$

The required arithmetic precision and Taylor order are polynomially bounded in

$$ \mathcal B= L+(1+A_{\max})H +\log_2(1/\eta)+\log_2^+(1/\delta) +\log_2(1/d). $$

The positive rational gap $d$ is computed from encoded rational data, so $\log_2(1/d)=O(L)$. Step endpoints and bisection times have polynomial encoding length: step counters require only $\log_2 N$ bits, and bisection adds $O(\log_2(H/\delta))$ bits.

A resulting running-time bound is

$$ O(m\tau+K) + \tau\left(K+1+(1+A_{\max})H\right) \left(1+\log_2(H/\delta)\right) \operatorname{poly}(\mathcal B). $$

Thus the problem is polynomial-time solvable when $(1+A_{\max})H$ is polynomially bounded in $L$, and is solvable in time $2^{\operatorname{poly}(L)}$ for unrestricted binary inputs.

11. What remains open

The unrestricted promised first-hitting-time problem remains unclassified.

The new contraction results remove numerical horizon dependence from state evaluation and promised existence decision. They also settle first-hit timing for constant pressure bounded sufficiently below $2$. For several intervals, the proved timing algorithm still depends on $\log_2(1/\kappa)$; the four-type example shows that this dependence need not be polynomial in $L$.

A complete resolution requires either an algorithm that handles such small threshold velocities using the encoded analytic structure, or a hardness reduction respecting scalar closure, nondecreasing pressure, and the final-margin promise.
