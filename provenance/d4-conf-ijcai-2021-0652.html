The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and nonnegative values \(v_{tg}\) with \(\sum_g q_gv_{tg}=1\), compute a finite-support rational assignment \(\lambda_{t,B}\), where each recipient receives an integral bundle \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{G}\). Require \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}\lambda_{t,B}B_g=q_g\), and, for every \((t,B)\) with \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\), \(v_t(B)+d_t(\lambda)\ge 1\), where \(v_t(B)=\sum_gv_{tg}B_g\) and \(d_t(\lambda)=\max_{C:\exists s,\lambda_{s,C}>0}\min_{g:C_g>0}v_{tg}\), with \(d_t(\lambda)=0\) if no nonempty bundle is assigned. Is such an assignment computable in time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(r\), the input bit length \(L\), and the output length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is atomless and given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
There are \(m=r\ge1\) good categories with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\ge0\). Values are binary rationals satisfying \(0\le v_{tg}\le1\) and \(\sum_gq_gv_{tg}=1\) for each type.
Each recipient receives an integral bundle \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\), valued additively as \(v_t(B)=\sum_gv_{tg}B_g\). An assignment is a finitely supported family of nonnegative rational masses \(\lambda_{t,B}\).
Require \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}\lambda_{t,B}B_g=q_g\).
Define \(\mathcal C(\lambda)=\{C\ne0:\exists s,\lambda_{s,C}>0\}\) and \(d_t(\lambda)=\max_{C\in\mathcal C(\lambda)}\min_{g:C_g>0}v_{tg}\), taking \(d_t=0\) when \(\mathcal C(\lambda)\) is empty. Bundles held by other recipients of the same type count.
Require \(v_t(B)+d_t(\lambda)\ge1\) whenever \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\). Let \(L\) be the input bit length and \(K\) the binary length of the listed positive assignment entries, including bundle coordinates.

Does every such instance admit an assignment satisfying these conditions?
Is finding one, or reporting infeasibility, possible in time polynomial in \(m=r,\tau,L,K\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite societies, the divider and every other agent are PROPm-satisfied by the constructive allocation; such allocations always exist and are computable in polynomial time in the numbers of agents and goods. [Verified discrete result](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0004.pdf)
No result settling this exact atomless, rational-support problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one, making the stated supplies per capita and the proportional benchmark one.
  • Identified the undefined parameter r with m, the number of good categories; supplies and values are binary rationals, and supplies are nonnegative.
  • Carried over the anchor construction's small-item assumption as v_{tg}≤1 after scaling the proportional benchmark to one.
  • Excluded empty bundles from the inner minimum; empty recipient bundles remain allowed.
  • Interpreted the society as atomless, so another recipient can hold the same bundle and have the same type; no type is excluded from the maximin comparison.
  • Specified binary encoding of bundle multiplicities and assignment masses to define output length.
  • Separated the anchor's existence guarantee from computational complexity and allowed reporting infeasibility without assuming continuous existence.

Result

Every instance admits a satisfying assignment. Such an assignment can be found in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), and its output length \(K\) is polynomially bounded in these parameters.

The construction uses positive, sufficiently small masses of monochromatic bundles to establish the correction terms. It allocates the remaining population by a rational rounding procedure.

1. A rounding lemma

Lemma. Let \(x\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{d}\), and let \(c\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{d}\) satisfy \(c_i\le a\) for every \(i\), where \(a>0\). In polynomial time, one can construct a rational probability distribution supported on at most \(2(d+1)\) integral vectors \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{d}\) such that

\[ \mathbb E[B]=x, \qquad c\cdot B>c\cdot x-a \]

for every vector with positive probability. All vectors and probabilities have polynomial encoding length.

Proof. Put \(\ell_i=\lfloor x_i\rfloor\) and \(u_i=\lceil x_i\rceil\). We first construct a distribution on rational vectors \(y\) satisfying

\[ \ell_i\le y_i\le u_i, \qquad c\cdot y=c\cdot x, \qquad \mathbb E[y]=x. \]

Start with probability one on \(x\). Apply the following transformations to each currently supported vector.

  • If \(y_i\) is fractional and \(c_i=0\), replace \(y\) by the two vectors obtained by setting coordinate \(i\) to \(\ell_i\) or \(u_i\), with probabilities preserving its expectation.
  • Otherwise, if two fractional coordinates \(i,j\) remain, their coefficients are positive. Move in direction \(h\), where \(h_i=c_j\), \(h_j=-c_i\), and all other coordinates vanish. Thus \(c\cdot h=0\). Move maximally in both directions while staying inside the box, obtaining \(y^+=y+\alpha h\) and \(y^-=y-\beta h\), where \(\alpha,\beta>0\). Replace \(y\) by these two vectors with conditional probabilities \(\beta/(\alpha+\beta)\) and \(\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)\).

Both transformations preserve the expectation and the weighted sum. In every resulting branch, at least one fractional coordinate becomes integral; no integral coordinate becomes fractional.

After each round, compress the distribution to at most \(d+1\) vectors. Explicitly, for the candidate vectors \(y^{(1)},\ldots,y^{(s)}\), solve the rational linear system

\[ p_k\ge0,\qquad \sum_{k=1}^{s}p_k=1,\qquad \sum_{k=1}^{s}p_k y^{(k)}=x. \]

It is feasible because the transformations preserved the expectation. A basic feasible solution has at most \(d+1\) positive entries. Finding such a solution is a standard polynomial-time rational linear-programming operation. Compression retains a subset of the candidate vectors, so it preserves the decrease in the number of fractional coordinates along every surviving branch.

After at most \(d\) rounds, every supported vector has all zero-coefficient coordinates integral and at most one fractional coordinate. Round that last coordinate to its two adjacent integers, preserving its expectation. This produces at most \(2(d+1)\) integral vectors with mean \(x\).

If \(y_i=\ell_i+f\), where \(0<f<1\), rounding downward loses \(c_i f<c_i\le a\). Rounding upward loses nothing. An already integral vector also loses nothing. Since \(c\cdot y=c\cdot x\), every resulting vector satisfies the asserted strict inequality.

For completeness, the arithmetic also has polynomial bit complexity. Choose integers \(D_0,H>0\) clearing the denominators of \(c\) and \(x\), respectively, and write \(A_i=D_0c_i\). Initially \(A_i y_i\) is an integer multiple of \(1/H\). This remains true throughout the transformations: when a two-coordinate move fixes one coordinate at an integer endpoint, its change in weighted value is an integer multiple of \(1/H\), and the other coordinate changes by the opposite amount. Hence, whenever \(A_i>0\), the denominator of \(y_i\) divides \(HA_i\). Zero-coefficient coordinates remain original coordinates or become integer endpoints.

All vectors stay inside the original box, so their numerators also have polynomial bit length. Each compression LP therefore has polynomial dimensions and coefficient length; its basic solution has polynomial bit length by determinant bounds. The final rounding probabilities have polynomial bit length as well. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

2. Choosing witness bundles and their masses

Delete categories with \(q_g=0\), restoring zero coordinates in the output. This is legitimate: nonnegativity and the supply equation force every positive assignment entry to have \(B_g=0\) in such a category. Write \(d\le m\) for the remaining number of categories.

For each type, define

\[ a_t=\max_{g:q_g>0}v_{tg}. \]

Normalization implies \(a_t>0\), since \(\sum_gq_gv_{tg}=1\).

Let \(G\) be the supplied categories valued positively by at least one type, and put \(h=|G|\). Thus \(h\ge1\). For each \(g\in G\), choose a supported type \(s(g)\) with \(v_{s(g),g}>0\), and define

\[ n_g=\left\lceil\frac1{v_{s(g),g}}\right\rceil, \qquad C^{(g)}=n_ge_g. \]

The monochromatic bundle \(C^{(g)}\) has value at least one to its designated recipient type:

\[ v_{s(g)}\!\left(C^{(g)}\right)=n_gv_{s(g),g}\ge1. \]

Let \(D\) be a positive integer clearing all valuation denominators, and put \(S=\sum_{g\in G}n_g\). Choose the positive rational number

\[ \delta= \min\left\{ \frac1{4h}, \min_t\frac{\mu_t}{2h}, \min_{g\in G}\frac{q_g}{2n_g}, \frac1{4DS} \right\}. \]

Assign mass \(\delta\) of type \(s(g)\) to \(C^{(g)}\), for each \(g\in G\).

Let \(z_t\) denote the total witness mass assigned to type \(t\), and let \(W=h\delta\). Then

\[ z_t\le h\delta\le\frac{\mu_t}{2}, \qquad W\le\frac14. \]

The witness bundles consume supply

\[ w_g= \begin{cases} \delta n_g,&g\in G,\\ 0,&g\notin G. \end{cases} \]

For \(g\in G\), we have \(w_g\le q_g/2\). Consequently every remaining supplied category still has positive residual supply.

3. Rounding the residual allocation

The remaining type masses are \(\beta_t=\mu_t-z_t>0\), and their sum is \(1-W\). Give every remaining type the same conditional mean bundle

\[ x_g=\frac{q_g-w_g}{1-W}. \]

This is a nonnegative rational vector. For a type \(t\), put

\[ A_t=\sum_{g\in G}n_gv_{tg}. \]

Using normalization,

\[ v_t(x) =\frac{1-\delta A_t}{1-h\delta} =1+\frac{\delta(h-A_t)}{1-W}. \]

Since \(0\le v_{tg}\le1\), we have \(A_t\le S\). Thus

\[ v_t(x) \ge1-\frac{\delta S}{1-W} \ge1-\frac43\delta S \ge1-\frac1{3D}. \]

Apply the rounding lemma to \(x\), with coefficients \(c_g=v_{tg}\) and bound \(a=a_t\). All remaining categories have positive original supply, so \(v_{tg}\le a_t\), as required.

We obtain a rational distribution \(p_{t,B}\), supported on at most \(2(d+1)\) integral bundles, with mean \(x\), such that every positively supported bundle satisfies

\[ v_t(B)>v_t(x)-a_t \ge1-a_t-\frac1{3D} >1-a_t-\frac1D. \]

Both \(v_t(B)\) and \(1-a_t\) are integer multiples of \(1/D\). Therefore this strict inequality implies

\[ v_t(B)\ge1-a_t. \]

Indeed, a grid value below \(1-a_t\) would be at most \(1-a_t-1/D\).

Assign mass \(\beta_t p_{t,B}\) of type \(t\) to each such bundle.

4. Verification of every requirement

Combine the witness entries with the residual entries, summing masses whenever their type and bundle coincide. All masses are nonnegative rational numbers, and there are finitely many entries.

For every type,

\[ \sum_B\lambda_{t,B} =z_t+\beta_t =\mu_t. \]

For every category, the total assigned supply is

\[ w_g+\sum_t\beta_t\,\mathbb E_{p_t}[B_g] =w_g+(1-W)x_g =q_g. \]

It remains to verify the correction term. For each type \(t\), a category attaining \(a_t\) belongs to \(G\), because \(a_t>0\). Its monochromatic witness bundle has positive assignment mass and minimum item value \(a_t\) from type \(t\)'s perspective. Consequently \(d_t(\lambda)\ge a_t\).

Conversely, all used categories have positive original supply, and every such category has value at most \(a_t\) to type \(t\). The minimum value in any nonempty used bundle is therefore at most \(a_t\). Hence

\[ d_t(\lambda)=a_t. \]

Every residual bundle assigned to type \(t\) satisfies

\[ v_t(B)+d_t(\lambda) \ge(1-a_t)+a_t =1. \]

Every witness bundle has value at least one to its designated recipient type, so it satisfies the same condition. If witness and residual entries coincide, both verifications still apply.

Thus the constructed assignment satisfies all requirements.

5. Running time and output length

The construction introduces at most

\[ h+2\tau(d+1)\le m+2\tau(m+1) \]

positive entries before coincidences are combined.

All preprocessing uses polynomial-time rational arithmetic. In particular:

  • \(D\) may be chosen as the product of the valuation denominators; its binary length is at most their total encoding length.
  • Each \(n_g\), and hence \(S\), has polynomial binary length.
  • The minimum defining \(\delta\), the residual masses \(\beta_t\), and the vector \(x\) have polynomial encoding length.
  • The rounding lemma runs in polynomial time and produces polynomial-length rational probabilities and integral coordinates.

Multiplying these probabilities by \(\beta_t\), and combining coincident entries, preserves polynomial encoding length. Therefore \(K\) is polynomially bounded in \(m,\tau,L\), and the entire search runs in polynomial time in those parameters—hence also in \(m,\tau,L,K\).

No promised instance is infeasible.
