The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods \(G\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) over bidder types with integer-valued monotone strong-gross-substitutes valuations, rational per-capita supply \(b\), and an integral starting price \(p^\circ \le p^*\), does the ascending process that repeatedly chooses an inclusion-minimal minimizer \(X\) of \(L_\mu(p+\chi_X)\) find the minimal clearing price \(p^*\) in exactly \(\lVert p^*-p^\circ\rVert_\infty\) updates, and yield a type-level clearing allocation?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) contain \(m\geq1\) goods. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) bidder types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Bundles belong to \(B=\{x\in\mathbb Z^m:0\leq x\leq u\}\), where \(u\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^m\) is an input. Per-capita supply is \(b\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^m\), with \(b\leq u\). Each valuation \(f_t:B\to\mathbb Z\) is given by its complete binary-encoded table and is monotone nondecreasing.
Strong gross substitutes means that replacing each good by \(u_i\) distinct copies and valuing a subset by its vector of copy counts gives a gross-substitutes set valuation.
For \(p\in\mathbb R^m\), define \(V_t(p)=\max_{x\in B}\{f_t(x)-p^\top x\}\), \(D_t(p)=\arg\max_{x\in B}\{f_t(x)-p^\top x\}\), and \(L_\mu(p)=\sum_t\mu_tV_t(p)+b^\top p\).
A type-level clearing allocation at a nonnegative price \(p\) consists of rational bundle masses \(a_{t,x}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_xa_{t,x}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,x}a_{t,x}x=b\), and \(a_{t,x}>0\Rightarrow x\in D_t(p)\). Thus a type may split its mass among tied demanded bundles. Let \(p^*\) denote the componentwise least nonnegative clearing price, if it exists.
Starting from an integral \(p^\circ\leq p^*\), the ascending process chooses an inclusion-minimal minimizer \(X\subseteq G\) of \(L_\mu(p+\chi_X)\), where \(\chi_X\) is the indicator vector; it stops if \(X=\varnothing\), and otherwise updates \(p\leftarrow p+\chi_X\). Include the empty set among the choices.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), and any additional numerical parameters used.

Is computing the least clearing price and a type-level clearing allocation polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard?
Does every such society admit an integral \(p^*\), and does the specified ascending process, for every permitted choice of \(X\), reach it in exactly \(\lVert p^*-p^\circ\rVert_\infty\) updates and permit recovery of a clearing allocation?

For finite bidder lists, integer supplies, and monotone integer-valued strong-gross-substitutes valuations, the corresponding ascending process reaches the minimal integral equilibrium price in exactly \(\lVert p^*-p^\circ\rVert_\infty\) updates. [Murota, Shioura, and Yang](https://www.keisu.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/data/2014/METR14-39.pdf).
No result settling this exact finite-support mass formulation was identified in the literature search.

Result and interpretation

Under the intended promise that every valuation satisfies strong gross substitutes:

  • Computing the componentwise least nonnegative clearing price and a rational type-level clearing allocation is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\).
  • Every society admits such a price \(p^*\), and \(p^*\) is integral.
  • For \(0\leq p^\circ\leq p^*\), the specified ascending process reaches \(p^*\) in exactly \(\lVert p^*-p^\circ\rVert_\infty\) updates, and a clearing allocation can be recovered in polynomial time.

There is one qualification to the literal statement: it permits negative initial prices. If \(b_i=u_i\), a negative initial price for good \(i\) can remain unchanged. Section 6 characterizes this exception precisely and gives a counterexample.

The polynomial-time algorithm below does not require expanding rational masses into individual bidders.

1. Clearing prices and a rational welfare program

Put \(N=|B|=\prod_{i=1}^m(u_i+1)\). Consider the welfare linear program

\[ \text{maximize }\sum_{t,x}a_{t,x}f_t(x) \]

subject to \(a_{t,x}\geq0\), \(\sum_xa_{t,x}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), and \(\sum_{t,x}a_{t,x}x=b\). Denote its optimal value by \(W\).

First consider the relaxation replacing the last equality by \(\sum_{t,x}a_{t,x}x\leq b\). It is feasible by allocating every type the zero bundle, and its feasible region is compact.

The relaxation has the same optimal value as the equality program. To prove this, take a feasible allocation with aggregate bundle \(s\leq b\). For a coordinate \(i\) with \(s_i<b_i\), let \(T_i(a)\) be the allocation obtained by replacing coordinate \(i\) of every bundle by \(u_i\). This preserves type masses and all other aggregate coordinates. Its aggregate coordinate \(i\) is \(u_i\), because \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).

The allocation
\(a'=(1-\lambda)a+\lambda T_i(a)\), where
\(\lambda=(b_i-s_i)/(u_i-s_i)\in(0,1]\),
has aggregate coordinate \(b_i\). Monotonicity implies that its welfare is at least that of \(a\). Applying this operation successively to all deficient coordinates produces an equality-feasible allocation without reducing welfare. Applying it to an optimal relaxed allocation proves the claim.

The dual of the relaxed program is

\[ \text{minimize }\sum_t\mu_tv_t+b^\top p \]

subject to \(p\geq0\), \(v_t\in\mathbb R\), and
\(v_t+p^\top x\geq f_t(x)\) for every \(t,x\).

For fixed \(p\), the smallest feasible \(v_t\) is \(V_t(p)\). Rational linear-programming duality therefore gives

\[ \min_{p\geq0}L_\mu(p)=W, \]

with the minimum attained.

Moreover, for any equality-feasible allocation \(a\) and any real price \(p\),

\[ L_\mu(p)-\sum_{t,x}a_{t,x}f_t(x) =\sum_{t,x}a_{t,x}\bigl(V_t(p)+p^\top x-f_t(x)\bigr)\geq0. \]

Each summand is nonnegative. Consequently:

  • A nonnegative price clears if and only if it minimizes \(L_\mu\) over the nonnegative orthant.
  • Every welfare-optimal allocation is supported on \(D_t(p)\) at every such minimizing price.

Indeed, when both objective values equal \(W\), every positive-mass summand in the displayed identity must vanish. Conversely, a demanded equality-feasible allocation makes the identity vanish, and weak duality proves optimality.

The same identity also shows that \(L_\mu(p)\geq W\) for every real \(p\). Thus its unrestricted minimum is also \(W\).

An optimal rational allocation exists: the equality program has rational coefficients and a nonempty compact feasible region, so an optimal vertex is rational.

2. Discrete convexity survives rational masses

We use two established discrete-convex facts: integer-valued strong-gross-substitutes valuations have integral polyhedral \(L^\natural\)-convex indirect utilities, and this class is closed under addition. These are Theorem 4.3 and Proposition 3.1 of [Murota, Shioura, and Yang](https://www.keisu.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/data/2014/METR14-39.pdf).

Here \(L^\natural\)-convexity means the translation-submodularity inequality

\[ h(p)+h(q)\geq h\bigl((p+\lambda\mathbf1)\wedge q\bigr) +h\bigl(p\vee(q-\lambda\mathbf1)\bigr) \]

for \(\lambda\geq0\), where \(\wedge,\vee\) denote coordinatewise minimum and maximum. Integrality means that every nonempty minimizer polyhedron of \(h(p)-c^\top p\), for real \(c\), is an integral polyhedron.

Positive scaling preserves both properties. For integrality, this follows directly from

\[ \operatorname*{argmin}_p\bigl(\alpha h(p)-c^\top p\bigr) = \operatorname*{argmin}_p\bigl(h(p)-(c/\alpha)^\top p\bigr), \qquad \alpha>0. \]

Hence every \(\mu_tV_t\) remains integral polyhedral \(L^\natural\)-convex. A linear function has the same properties, so \(L_\mu\) does as well. The weights need not be integers.

Define \(H(p)=L_\mu(p)\) for \(p\geq0\), and \(H(p)=+\infty\) otherwise. The indicator of the nonnegative orthant is integral polyhedral \(L^\natural\)-convex:

  • Translation submodularity holds because the two transformed vectors remain nonnegative whenever \(p,q\geq0\).
  • Every nonempty minimizer of a linear function over this orthant is a face of the orthant, hence an integral polyhedron.

Closure under addition therefore makes \(H\) integral polyhedral \(L^\natural\)-convex.

3. Existence and integrality of the least price

Let \(P=\operatorname*{argmin}H\). Section 1 proves that \(P\) is nonempty and is exactly the set of nonnegative clearing prices.

Define the integer

\[ M=\max_{\substack{t,\ i,\ x\in B\\x_i>0}} \bigl(f_t(x)-f_t(x-e_i)\bigr). \]

Monotonicity gives \(M\geq0\). Every clearing price satisfies \(p_i\leq M\): since \(b_i>0\), a clearing allocation gives positive mass to some demanded bundle \(x\) with \(x_i>0\). Comparing it with \(x-e_i\) gives

\[ p_i\leq f_t(x)-f_t(x-e_i)\leq M. \]

Thus \(P\subseteq[0,M]^m\); being closed, it is compact.

Translation submodularity with \(\lambda=0\) shows that \(P\) is closed under coordinatewise minimum and maximum. Indeed, if \(p,q\in P\), then

\[ 2W\geq H(p\wedge q)+H(p\vee q)\geq2W, \]

so both transformed vectors belong to \(P\).

For each coordinate \(i\), compactness supplies a point \(p^{(i)}\in P\) minimizing that coordinate. Their coordinatewise minimum belongs to \(P\) and simultaneously minimizes every coordinate. It is therefore the unique componentwise least element \(p^*\).

Finally, integrality of \(H\) makes \(P\) an integral polytope. Its least element is a vertex: if
\(p^*=(r+s)/2\) with \(r,s\in P\), then \(r,s\geq p^*\) coordinatewise, forcing \(r=s=p^*\). Vertices of an integral polytope are integral. Hence

\[ p^*\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m. \]

4. A polynomial-time algorithm

Two exact rational linear programs suffice.

First, solve the equality welfare program, obtaining \(W\) and an optimal allocation \(a\).

Second, minimize \(\sum_i p_i\) subject to

\[ p\geq0,\qquad v_t+p^\top x\geq f_t(x)\quad(t,x), \]

and

\[ \sum_t\mu_tv_t+b^\top p=W. \]

This program is feasible by Section 1. Its feasible price vectors are exactly \(P\). To check this, its constraints imply \(v_t\geq V_t(p)\), while

\[ W=\sum_t\mu_tv_t+b^\top p\geq L_\mu(p)\geq W. \]

Equality and \(\mu_t>0\) force \(v_t=V_t(p)\) and \(p\in P\). Conversely, every \(p\in P\), with \(v_t=V_t(p)\), is feasible.

Since every \(p\in P\) satisfies \(p\geq p^*\), the sum objective has the unique minimizing price \(p^*\). The allocation from the first program clears at this price by the gap identity in Section 1.

The first program has \(\tau N\) variables and \(\tau+m\) equality constraints. The second has \(\tau+m\) variables and \(\tau N\) bundle inequalities, besides its other constraints. Their binary encoding lengths are polynomial in \(m,\tau,N,L\), including the polynomial-length rational value \(W\). Standard exact rational linear programming therefore solves them in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,N,L)\) bit time.

Because the complete tables are input, \(\tau N=O(L)\). Thus the running time is

\[ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \]

An optimal vertex allocation has at most \(\tau+m\) positive entries. Otherwise its positive-support columns in the equality-constraint matrix would be linearly dependent, allowing small feasible perturbations in both directions and contradicting extremality. A rational optimal vertex therefore provides a sparse, polynomial-length output.

5. Exact update count for the ascending process

We prove the needed iteration statement directly from translation submodularity.

Let \(g=L_\mu\), let \(\ell\) be an integral lower bound, and suppose \(q\) is the integral componentwise least global minimizer satisfying \(q\geq\ell\). Start from \(\ell\). We show that every update preserves \(p\leq q\) and reduces \(\lVert q-p\rVert_\infty\) by exactly one.

For fixed integral \(p\), put \(F_p(X)=g(p+\chi_X)\). Submodularity of \(g\) makes \(F_p\) submodular. Its minimizers are closed under intersection: applying the submodular inequality to two minimizers forces their intersection and union also to minimize. Thus there is a unique inclusion-minimal minimizer.

No update overshoots

Assume \(\ell\leq p\leq q\), and let \(X\) be the inclusion-minimal minimizing set. Put \(A=\{i:p_i<q_i\}\). Submodularity gives

\[ g(p+\chi_X)+g(q) \geq g(p+\chi_{X\cap A})+g(q+\chi_{X\setminus A}). \]

Because \(q\) is a global minimizer, the last term is at least \(g(q)\). Hence \(X\cap A\) also minimizes \(F_p\). Inclusion minimality forces \(X\subseteq A\), so \(p+\chi_X\leq q\).

Every coordinate at maximum distance is raised

Suppose \(p\ne q\). Set

\[ d=\lVert q-p\rVert_\infty>0,\qquad I=\{i:q_i-p_i=d\}. \]

Write \(r=p+\chi_X\). Suppose \(J=I\setminus X\) were nonempty. Then \(q_i-r_i=d\) exactly on \(J\), and \(q_i-r_i\leq d-1\) elsewhere. Translation submodularity applied to \(r,q\) with \(\lambda=d-1\) therefore gives

\[ g(r)+g(q)\geq g(q-\chi_J)+g(r+\chi_J). \]

The point \(q-\chi_J\) is at least \(\ell\): on \(J\), its coordinate is \(r_i+d-1\geq r_i\geq\ell_i\). It cannot be a global minimizer, because it does not dominate the least such minimizer \(q\). Thus \(g(q-\chi_J)>g(q)\), yielding \(g(r+\chi_J)<g(r)\).

But \(J\cap X=\varnothing\), so \(r+\chi_J=p+\chi_{X\cup J}\). This contradicts the minimizing choice of \(X\). Therefore \(I\subseteq X\).

All gaps were nonnegative; every maximum gap is reduced by one; and every other gap was already at most \(d-1\). Consequently,

\[ \lVert q-(p+\chi_X)\rVert_\infty=d-1. \]

When \(p=q\), the empty set minimizes \(F_p\), so it is the unique inclusion-minimal minimizer and the process stops. These facts prove exactly \(\lVert q-\ell\rVert_\infty\) updates.

For \(0\leq p^\circ\leq p^*\), take \(\ell=p^\circ\) and \(q=p^*\). Section 1 identifies nonnegative global minimizers with clearing prices, so all hypotheses hold. Allocation recovery is polynomial-time by Section 4, or by solving the bundle-mass feasibility program restricted to \(D_t(p^*)\).

6. Precise treatment of negative initial prices

Let \(S=\{i:b_i=u_i\}\). For the literal hypothesis \(p^\circ\leq p^*\), define

\[ q_i= \begin{cases} p^\circ_i,&i\in S,\\ p^*_i,&i\notin S. \end{cases} \]

Then the specified process terminates at \(q\), in exactly \(\lVert q-p^\circ\rVert_\infty\) updates.

Here is a proof of the characterization.

Every unrestricted minimizer \(r\) of \(L_\mu\) supports a welfare-optimal equality allocation, by the gap identity. If \(i\notin S\), then \(r_i\geq0\): a negative price and monotonicity would force every demanded bundle to have coordinate \(u_i\), contradicting \(b_i<u_i\).

For \(i\in S\), every positively allocated bundle has coordinate \(u_i\). Replacing all these prices by zero preserves demand. To see this, compare an allocated bundle \(x\) with any \(y\), and let \(\widehat y\) replace all coordinates in \(S\) by \(u_i\). Demand at \(r\), followed by monotonicity, gives

\[ f_t(x)-r^\top x \geq f_t(\widehat y)-r^\top\widehat y. \]

The saturated-coordinate price terms cancel because both bundles have full coordinates there. At the modified price \(r^0\), this yields

\[ f_t(x)-(r^0)^\top x \geq f_t(\widehat y)-(r^0)^\top\widehat y \geq f_t(y)-(r^0)^\top y. \]

Thus \(r^0\) is a nonnegative clearing price and \(r^0\geq p^*\). In particular, lowering saturated coordinates of \(p^*\) to zero proves \(p_i^*=0\) on \(S\), so \(p_i^\circ\leq0\) there.

Lowering those coordinates further to \(p_i^\circ\) preserves the demand of allocated bundles: they contain the full quantity, and every competing bundle contains at most that quantity. Hence \(q\) is a global minimizer and \(q\geq p^\circ\).

Any other global minimizer \(r\geq p^\circ\) satisfies \(r_i\geq p_i^*\) outside \(S\), by the normalization argument, and \(r_i\geq p_i^\circ=q_i\) on \(S\). Therefore \(q\) is the least global minimizer above \(p^\circ\), and Section 5 applies.

It follows that the process reaches \(p^*\) if and only if \(p_i^\circ=0\) for all \(i\in S\). This includes every nonnegative permitted start.

For a concrete counterexample to the unrestricted-start assertion, take \(m=\tau=1\), \(\mu_1=u_1=b_1=1\), and \(f_1(0)=f_1(1)=0\). Then \(p^*=0\), while \(L_\mu(-1)=L_\mu(0)=0\). Starting at \(p^\circ=-1\), the inclusion-minimal minimizing set is empty, so the process stops immediately, despite \(\lVert p^*-p^\circ\rVert_\infty=1\).

7. Running time of the ascending implementation

Let \(H=\lVert q-p^\circ\rVert_\infty\), and let \(\ell^\circ\) be the binary encoding length of the initial vector.

A direct implementation enumerates all \(2^m\) subsets, evaluating every indirect utility by scanning its complete table. Maintaining the intersection of all minimizing subsets gives the unique inclusion-minimal minimizer. Across the process, including the stopping check, this uses

\[ O\bigl((H+1)2^m\tau Nm\bigr) \]

exact arithmetic operations on numbers of bit length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\ell^\circ\). Since \(u_i\geq1\), we have \(2^m\leq N\), so each iteration has polynomial bit complexity.

Thus the ascending process takes
\((H+1)\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\ell^\circ)\) bit time. For nonnegative starts, \(H\leq M\).

This bound is numerical rather than polynomial in binary input length. For example, with one good, \(\mu_1=u_1=1\), \(b_1=1/2\), and \(f_1(0)=0,f_1(1)=K\), the least clearing price is \(K\). Starting from zero requires exactly \(K\) updates, although \(K\) has only \(O(\log(K+1))\) bits. The two-LP algorithm avoids this dependence.
