The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over location types \(t\in[0,1]\), let \(P_{\alpha,p}\) put probabilities \(p,1-2p,p\) on \(1/2-\alpha,1/2,1/2+\alpha\), respectively. With \(u(y,t)=1-|y-t|\) and \(\mathrm{ER}(P,\mu)=\max_{s,t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P}[u(y,s)]}{\mathbb{E}_{y\sim P}[u(y,t)]}\), minimize over admissible \((\alpha,p)\) the worst-case ratio \(\sup_{\mu}\frac{\mathrm{ER}(P_{\alpha,p},\mu)}{\min_{z\in[0,1]}\mathrm{ER}(\delta_z,\mu)}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is a probability distribution \(\mu\) specified by its support: \(\tau\geq2\) distinct rational locations in \([0,1]\), each with positive binary rational mass, summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total encoding length. There is one facility and no candidate-count parameter \(m\); state any running times in \(\tau,L\) and other computational parameters.
For real parameters \((\alpha,p)\in[0,\tfrac12]^2\), the report-independent lottery \(P_{\alpha,p}\) assigns probabilities \(p,1-2p,p\) to \(\tfrac12-\alpha,\tfrac12,\tfrac12+\alpha\), respectively; parameters are chosen once for all societies.
Define \(u(y,t)=1-|y-t|\), \(U_P(t)=\mathbb E_{y\sim P}[u(y,t)]\), and \(\mathrm{ER}(P,\mu)=\max_{s,t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}U_P(s)/U_P(t)\). A positive numerator divided by zero is \(+\infty\); set \(0/0=1\).
Let \(D(\mu)=\min_{z\in[0,1]}\mathrm{ER}(\delta_z,\mu)\), where \(\delta_z\) places the facility deterministically at \(z\), and let \(R(\alpha,p)=\sup_\mu\mathrm{ER}(P_{\alpha,p},\mu)/D(\mu)\), over all societies just defined, with unrestricted finite support size.

What is the optimal worst-case approximation ratio \(\inf_{(\alpha,p)\in[0,\tfrac12]^2}R(\alpha,p)\), and which admissible parameter pairs attain it, if any?

For finite agent profiles, the corresponding three-point constant-lottery family has reported optimal approximation ratio \(1+2/\sqrt5\), attained at \((\alpha,p)=(\sqrt5/2-1,2/5)\); its analysis evaluates the expectation of the realized envy ratio. [Aziz et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11193v2)
That evaluation differs from the ratio of expected utilities specified here. No result settling this precise distributional objective was located.

Result

The optimal worst-case approximation ratio is \(1\), attained uniquely at \((\alpha,p)=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\).

The admissible boundary lottery puts equal probability on the two endpoints and gives every type the same expected utility. Beyond this observation, the entire family admits the exact formula
\(R(\alpha,p)=2-4\alpha p\).

Proof of the exact formula

Fix admissible parameters \((\alpha,p)\). For a type \(t\), write \(x=|t-\tfrac12|\), so \(x\in[0,\tfrac12]\). By symmetry, its expected distance from the facility is
\[ (1-2p)x+p\bigl(|x-\alpha|+|x+\alpha|\bigr) =(1-2p)x+2p\max\{x,\alpha\}. \]
Here the absolute-value identity follows by considering \(x\leq\alpha\) and \(x\geq\alpha\).

Both coefficients are nonnegative, so this expected distance is nondecreasing in \(x\). Its value at \(x=0\) is \(2p\alpha\), and its value at \(x=\tfrac12\) is \(\tfrac12\). Consequently, for every \(t\in[0,1]\),
\[ \tfrac12\leq U_{P_{\alpha,p}}(t)\leq1-2p\alpha. \]
In particular, all these expected utilities are positive. Thus every admissible society satisfies
\[ \mathrm{ER}(P_{\alpha,p},\mu)\leq \frac{1-2p\alpha}{1/2}=2-4p\alpha. \]

Every deterministic envy ratio is at least \(1\): its maximum includes a diagonal ratio, which equals \(1\), including under the stipulated zero-utility convention. Hence \(D(\mu)\geq1\). The benchmark is finite, because placing the facility at \(\tfrac12\) gives every type utility at least \(\tfrac12\). Therefore
\[ \frac{\mathrm{ER}(P_{\alpha,p},\mu)}{D(\mu)} \leq2-4p\alpha, \]
and taking the supremum gives \(R(\alpha,p)\leq2-4p\alpha\).

For the matching lower bound, take the admissible society
\[ \mu_*=\tfrac12\delta_{1/2}+\tfrac12\delta_1. \]
Its two distinct locations and its positive masses are rational. A deterministic facility at \(\tfrac34\) gives both types utility \(\tfrac34\), so \(D(\mu_*)=1\). Moreover,
\[ U_{P_{\alpha,p}}(\tfrac12)=1-2p\alpha, \qquad U_{P_{\alpha,p}}(1)=\tfrac12. \]
Since \(1-2p\alpha\geq\tfrac12\), this society satisfies
\[ \frac{\mathrm{ER}(P_{\alpha,p},\mu_*)}{D(\mu_*)} =2-4p\alpha. \]
Thus \(R(\alpha,p)=2-4p\alpha\), with the supremum attained by a society of support size \(2\).

Optimization and uniqueness

The constraints imply \(\alpha p\leq\tfrac14\). Therefore \(R(\alpha,p)\geq1\), with equality precisely when \(\alpha p=\tfrac14\). Because each parameter is at most \(\tfrac12\), equality forces \(\alpha=p=\tfrac12\).

At this pair,
\[ P_{\alpha,p}=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_1, \qquad U_{P_{\alpha,p}}(t)=\tfrac12(1-t)+\tfrac12t=\tfrac12 \]
for every type \(t\). This verifies the optimizer directly.

Why the two objectives differ

The expectation-of-realized-envy objective used in the [cited analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11193v2) does not permit the same conclusion. For example, on a society supported at \(0\) and \(1\), either realization of the endpoint lottery gives one type utility \(1\) and the other utility \(0\). Its realized envy ratio is therefore infinite almost surely, whereas its ratio of expected utilities is exactly \(1\).
