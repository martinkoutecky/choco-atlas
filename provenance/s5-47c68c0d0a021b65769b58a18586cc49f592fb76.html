Partial result: impossibility above $1-1/e$

Write
$$ \alpha=\frac{1}{d+1}+\varepsilon. $$

I prove that if $\alpha>1-1/e$, the requested guarantee is impossible with polynomially many queries. In fact, exponentially many queries are necessary even with one supported type whose valuation is submodular, and hence has supermodular width $0$.

This resolves a nontrivial range of the parameters. The range
$$ \frac{1}{d+1}<\alpha\le 1-\frac1e $$
remains unresolved here.

The proof directly controls arbitrary feasible mass allocations. It places no restriction on the number of bundles appearing in the output or on computation between queries.

1. A valuation construction

Fix any $\alpha>1-1/e$. Choose a power of two $h\ge2$ such that
$$ C_h:=1-\left(1-\frac1h\right)^h<\alpha. $$
Such a choice exists because $C_h$ converges to $1-1/e$.

For $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_h)\in[0,1]^h$, define
$$ z=\frac1h\sum_{i=1}^h y_i, \qquad r=\sum_{i=1}^h(y_i-z)^2, $$
and
$$ F(y)=1-\prod_{i=1}^h(1-y_i), \qquad G(y)=1-(1-z)^h, \qquad q(z)=2z-z^2. $$

The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality, applied to the nonnegative numbers $1-y_i$, gives
$$ \prod_{i=1}^h(1-y_i)\le(1-z)^h. $$
Consequently,
$$ H(y):=F(y)-G(y)\ge0. $$

We will modify $F$ so that it agrees exactly with a function of $z$ whenever $r$ is sufficiently small, while preserving monotonicity and diminishing marginal values.

1.1 Choosing the constants

Choose $0<\lambda<1/2$ sufficiently small that, with
$$ M=1-\lambda+\lambda q(1/h) $$
and
$$ B=(1-\lambda)C_h+\lambda q(1/h), $$
we have
$$ \gamma:=\alpha M-B>0. $$
This is possible because, as $\lambda$ tends to zero, $M$ tends to $1$ and $B$ tends to $C_h<\alpha$.

Set
$$ c=\frac{\lambda}{14h^3}, \qquad b=\frac{1-1/h}{2}. $$

We require a twice continuously differentiable function $\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]$ and a constant $a>0$ satisfying
$$ \phi(r)=0\quad\text{for }r\le a, \qquad \phi(r)=1\quad\text{for }r\ge b, $$
and, for $r>0$,
$$ |r\phi'(r)|\le c, \qquad |r^2\phi''(r)|\le c. $$

Here is an explicit construction. Let
$$ \psi(t)= \begin{cases} 0,&t\le0,\\ 6t^5-15t^4+10t^3,&0<t<1,\\ 1,&t\ge1. \end{cases} $$
This function is twice continuously differentiable, takes values in $[0,1]$, and satisfies
$$ |\psi'(t)|\le30, \qquad |\psi''(t)|\le360. $$
Choose
$$ T\ge\max\{1,390/c\}, \qquad a=be^{-T}, $$
and define, for $r>0$,
$$ \phi(r)=\psi\left(\frac{\log(r/a)}{T}\right), $$
with $\phi(0)=0$.

The required cutoff properties follow immediately. Differentiating gives
$$ r\phi'(r)=\frac{\psi'}{T}, \qquad r^2\phi''(r)=\frac{\psi''}{T^2}-\frac{\psi'}{T}. $$
Thus both derivative bounds hold. The function is constant near zero, so it is also twice continuously differentiable there.

Define
$$ \widehat F(y) =(1-\lambda)\bigl(G(y)+\phi(r)H(y)\bigr) +\lambda q(z). $$

2. Monotonicity and diminishing marginal values

We prove that every first partial derivative of $\widehat F$ is nonnegative and every second partial derivative is nonpositive.

For distinct $i,j$,
$$ \partial_iF=\prod_{\ell\ne i}(1-y_\ell)\ge0, \qquad \partial_{ii}F=0, \qquad \partial_{ij}F=-\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}(1-y_\ell)\le0. $$
Also,
$$ \partial_iG=(1-z)^{h-1}\ge0, \qquad \partial_{ij}G=-\frac{h-1}{h}(1-z)^{h-2}\le0 $$
for all $i,j$.

In particular, every entry of the Hessian of $H=F-G$ has absolute value at most $2$.

Let $\bar y=(z,\ldots,z)$. Then
$$ H(\bar y)=0, \qquad \nabla H(\bar y)=0. $$
Taylor’s formula along the segment from $\bar y$ to $y$ therefore gives
$$ |H(y)|\le h r, \qquad |\partial_iH(y)|\le2h\sqrt r. $$
For example, the first inequality follows from
$$ |H(y)| \le\left(\sum_i|y_i-z|\right)^2 \le h\sum_i(y_i-z)^2=hr. $$
The gradient estimate follows by integrating the Hessian along the same segment.

The derivatives of $r$ are
$$ \partial_i r=2(y_i-z), \qquad \partial_{ij}r=2\left(\mathbf1[i=j]-\frac1h\right). $$
Hence, whenever $r>0$,
$$ |\partial_i\phi(r)|\le\frac{2c}{\sqrt r}, \qquad |\partial_{ij}\phi(r)|\le\frac{6c}{r}. $$

Put
$$ K=G+\phi(r)H. $$
Its first derivatives are
$$ \partial_iK =(1-\phi)\partial_iG+\phi\partial_iF +H\partial_i\phi. $$
Its second derivatives are
$$ \partial_{ij}K =(1-\phi)\partial_{ij}G+\phi\partial_{ij}F+E_{ij}, $$
where
$$ E_{ij} =(\partial_i\phi)(\partial_jH) +(\partial_j\phi)(\partial_iH) +H\partial_{ij}\phi. $$
The estimates above imply
$$ |E_{ij}|\le14hc. $$

On the other hand,
$$ \partial_iq(z)=\frac{2(1-z)}h, \qquad \partial_{ij}q(z)=-\frac2{h^2}. $$
The convex combination of the Hessians of $F$ and $G$ has nonpositive entries. Therefore
$$ \partial_{ij}\widehat F \le(1-\lambda)14hc-\frac{2\lambda}{h^2} \le-\frac{\lambda}{h^2}<0. $$

For monotonicity, first observe that
$$ \sqrt r\le h(1-z). $$
Indeed, writing $t_i=1-y_i\ge0$,
$$ r=\sum_i(t_i-\bar t)^2 \le\sum_i t_i^2 \le\left(\sum_i t_i\right)^2 =h^2(1-z)^2. $$
Consequently,
$$ |H\partial_i\phi| \le2hc\sqrt r \le2h^2c(1-z). $$
The principal terms in $\partial_iK$ are nonnegative, so
$$ \partial_i\widehat F \ge\frac{2\lambda(1-z)}h-2h^2c(1-z) \ge0. $$
These conclusions also hold at $r=0$ by continuity.

Finally,
$$ \widehat F(0,\ldots,0)=0, \qquad \widehat F(1,\ldots,1)=1. $$
Monotonicity implies
$$ 0\le\widehat F(y)\le1. $$

3. Hidden partitions give promised oracle instances

Let $k$ be a positive integer and set $m=hk$. Consider a partition
$$ \mathcal R=(R_1,\ldots,R_h) $$
of $[m]$ into parts of size $k$. Define
$$ y_i(S)=\frac{|S\cap R_i|}{k}, \qquad f_{\mathcal R}(S)=\widehat F(y(S)). $$

Each such valuation is normalized and monotone.

It is also submodular. To verify this explicitly, suppose $A\subseteq B$ and $v\notin B$, with $v\in R_i$. Adding $v$ increases the $i$th coordinate by $1/k$. Because every second partial derivative of $\widehat F$ is nonpositive, $\partial_i\widehat F$ is nonincreasing in every coordinate. Integrating that derivative over the increment gives
$$ f_{\mathcal R}(v\mid A) \ge f_{\mathcal R}(v\mid B). $$

This diminishing-marginal inequality rules out every supermodular set in the task’s definition: if $v\notin A$, apply it to $A\cup T'\subseteq A\cup T$; if $v\in A$, both marginals are zero. Thus
$$ \mathrm{SMW}(f_{\mathcal R})=0\le d. $$

We use one supported type, of mass $1$, and supplies
$$ b_j=\frac1h\qquad(j\in[m]). $$
Because $h$ is a fixed power of two, these supplies are binary-rational, including under the dyadic interpretation.

Give bundle $R_i$ to mass $1/h$, for each $i$. Every item is consumed on mass exactly $1/h$, so this allocation is feasible. For $S=R_i$, the vector $y(S)$ is the $i$th unit vector, and
$$ z=\frac1h, \qquad r=1-\frac1h>b. $$
Thus $\phi(r)=1$ and
$$ f_{\mathcal R}(R_i)=M. $$
It follows that
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathcal R}\ge M. $$

4. A baseline oracle and its fractional welfare bound

Define
$$ f_0(S) =(1-\lambda)\left[1-\left(1-\frac{|S|}{m}\right)^h\right] +\lambda q\left(\frac{|S|}{m}\right). $$
This is normalized, monotone, and submodular: it is an increasing concave function of $|S|$.

For every feasible allocation $x=(x_S)_{S\subseteq[m]}$,
$$ \sum_Sx_S=1, \qquad \sum_Sx_S|S| =\sum_j\sum_{S:j\in S}x_S \le\frac mh. $$
The function
$$ J(z)=(1-\lambda)[1-(1-z)^h]+\lambda q(z) $$
is increasing and concave on $[0,1]$. Jensen’s inequality therefore gives
$$ \sum_Sx_Sf_0(S) \le J\left(\sum_Sx_S\frac{|S|}{m}\right) \le J(1/h)=B. $$

Moreover,
$$ f_{\mathcal R}(S)-f_0(S) =(1-\lambda)\phi(r(y(S)))H(y(S)). $$
This difference is nonnegative, at most $1$, and is exactly zero whenever $r(y(S))\le a$.

5. A fixed bundle rarely reveals the partition

Choose $\mathcal R$ uniformly at random among balanced partitions. We prove that, for every fixed $S\subseteq[m]$,
$$ \Pr\bigl[r(y(S))>a\bigr]\le p_k, $$
where
$$ p_k=2h(m+1)\exp\left(-\frac{2ka}{h^2}\right). $$

Write $s=|S|$ and $z=s/m$. If $r>a$, then for some $i$,
$$ |y_i-z|>\sqrt{a/h}. $$

The marginal distribution of $R_i$ is uniform among the $k$-subsets of $[m]$. It can be generated by independently including each item with probability $1/h$, conditioned on including exactly $k$ items.

Before conditioning, let $Z$ count included items in $S$. Then
$$ Z\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(s,1/h). $$
For completeness, its two-sided tail bound is
$$ \Pr[|Z-s/h|>u]\le2e^{-2u^2/s} $$
when $s>0$. To derive it, for a Bernoulli variable $X$ of mean $p$, the logarithm of
$$ \mathbb E e^{t(X-p)} $$
has value and first derivative zero at $t=0$, and second derivative at most $1/4$: that second derivative is the variance of a tilted Bernoulli distribution. Thus its logarithm is at most $t^2/8$. Independence and Markov’s inequality give the upper-tail bound by choosing $t=4u/s$, and the lower tail follows using negative $t$.

The conditioning event has probability at least $1/(m+1)$. Indeed, $k$ is a mode of $\mathrm{Binomial}(hk,1/h)$, as follows from the successive-probability ratio
$$ \frac{\Pr[N=\ell+1]}{\Pr[N=\ell]} =\frac{m-\ell}{(h-1)(\ell+1)}. $$
The ratio is greater than $1$ at $\ell=k-1$ and less than $1$ at $\ell=k$. A maximum among $m+1$ probabilities summing to $1$ is at least $1/(m+1)$.

Taking $u=k\sqrt{a/h}$ and using $s\le hk$ gives
$$ \Pr\left[|y_i-z|>\sqrt{a/h}\right] \le2(m+1)\exp\left(-\frac{2ka}{h^2}\right). $$
A union bound over $i$ proves the claim. The case $s=0$ is immediate.

6. Adaptive queries and unrestricted output distributions

Consider any randomized algorithm using at most $Q$ value queries on every promised input.

First fix its random bits and run it against the baseline oracle $f_0$. Its query list and output $x^0$ are then fixed independently of $\mathcal R$.

By Section 5, the probability that any query on this baseline list receives a different answer from $f_{\mathcal R}$ is at most
$$ Qp_k. $$
If none receives a different answer, the complete adaptive transcript and output agree with the baseline run: any first divergence would necessarily occur at a query on that list.

We next bound the welfare of the fixed output. If $x^0$ is infeasible, it cannot be a successful output when the transcripts agree. Otherwise, Section 4 gives
$$ \sum_Sx^0_Sf_0(S)\le B. $$
Furthermore, without imposing any bound on its support size,
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E_{\mathcal R} \left[\sum_Sx^0_S\bigl(f_{\mathcal R}(S)-f_0(S)\bigr)\right] &\le\sum_Sx^0_S\Pr[r(y(S))>a]\\ &\le p_k. \end{aligned} $$
Markov’s inequality implies that the probability this excess welfare is at least $\gamma$ is at most $p_k/\gamma$.

A successful output must satisfy
$$ W_{\mathcal R}(x^0) \ge\alpha\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathcal R} \ge\alpha M=B+\gamma. $$
Hence success, when transcripts agree, requires excess welfare at least $\gamma$. Combining the two exceptional events yields
$$ \Pr_{\mathcal R}[\text{success}] \le\left(Q+\frac1\gamma\right)p_k. $$
This bound is uniform in the fixed random bits, so averaging over those bits establishes the same bound for randomized algorithms.

If the algorithm succeeds with probability at least $2/3$ on every hidden-partition instance, its average success probability is also at least $2/3$. Therefore
$$ Q\ge \frac{1}{3h(m+1)} \exp\left(\frac{2am}{h^3}\right) -\frac1\gamma. $$

All of $h,a,\gamma$ are positive constants depending only on $\alpha$. Thus, for sufficiently large multiples of $h$,
$$ Q=\exp(\Omega(m)). $$

These instances have $\tau=1$ and $L=O(m)$, so this excludes $\mathrm{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ queries. The argument allows unrestricted computation between queries and unrestricted output support.

7. What is settled and what remains open

The preceding proof establishes impossibility whenever
$$ \varepsilon>1-\frac1e-\frac1{d+1}. $$
It applies to a subclass of the promised valuations, namely submodular valuations.

It does not settle whether polynomially many queries achieve the requested guarantee when
$$ 0<\varepsilon\le1-\frac1e-\frac1{d+1}. $$
In particular, it supplies no impossibility result for ratios arbitrarily close to $1/(d+1)$ from above.
