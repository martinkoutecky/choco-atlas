The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite chore kinds \(Q\), complete worker types \(\mathcal T\) with \(s_t(e)\in\{1,k\}\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_e\), compute masses \(\lambda_{t,B}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq Q\) satisfying \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:e\in B}\lambda_{t,B}=q_e\), such that for every \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\), \(\lambda_{u,D}>0\), and \(e\in B\), \(c_t(B\setminus\{e\})\le(2-1/k)c_t(D)\), where \(c_t(B)=\sum_{e\in B}s_t(e)\), and such that no feasible fractional reassignment weakly improves all positive-mass workers while strictly improving a positive-mass set; for \(k=2\), require the factor \(1\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(Q\) contain \(m\ge1\) chore kinds and let the common parameter \(k>1\) be a binary rational. Each worker type specifies all costs \(s_t(e)\in\{1,k\}\), with at least one cost equal to \(1\); write \(c_t(B)=\sum_{e\in B}s_t(e)\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Supplies are binary rationals \(0<q_e\le1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including \(k\).
Each worker receives at most one unit of each kind. An integral bundle is any \(B\subseteq Q\), including the empty bundle. An allocation is a sparse list of positive binary-rational masses \(\lambda_{t,B}\), with omitted entries zero, satisfying \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:e\in B}\lambda_{t,B}=q_e\).
Set \(\beta=2-1/k\), except that \(\beta=1\) when \(k=2\). Require \(c_t(B\setminus\{e\})\le\beta c_t(D)\) whenever \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\), \(\lambda_{u,D}>0\), and \(e\in B\).
Fractional Pareto optimality means there is no real array \(y_{t,B,e}\) satisfying \(0\le y_{t,B,e}\le\lambda_{t,B}\), \(\sum_{t,B}y_{t,B,e}=q_e\), and \(\sum_e s_t(e)y_{t,B,e}\le\lambda_{t,B}c_t(B)\) for every positive-mass group, with at least one strict inequality. Here \(y_{t,B,e}\) is the total fractional amount assigned to workers originally in group \((t,B)\); competing assignments need not satisfy fairness.

Is computing an allocation with these fairness factors and fractional Pareto optimality, or reporting that none exists, polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), or NP-hard?

Discrete: [Lin, Wu and Zhou](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0440.pdf) establish existence and claim polynomial-time computation at factor \(2-1/k\), and at factor \(1\) for \(k=2\); their stated initialization bound is \(O(kn^2m^2)\) for \(n\) agents.

Partial resolution

I do not resolve the classification for unrestricted support size. The following results advance the question beyond the supplied notes:

  • When $k=2$, the problem is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed support size $\tau$.
  • With unrestricted support size, a valid allocation can always be computed in polynomial time when $\sum_e q_e\le 1$.
  • More generally, if $\sum_e q_e=r$ is an integer and

$$ k(r-1)\le \beta r, $$
a valid allocation can always be computed in polynomial time. In particular, this covers total supply $2$ when $k=2$.

  • For an allocation satisfying exact EFX, fractional Pareto optimality can be characterized by a graph on types, rather than on positive-mass groups.

The general existence decision problem remains in $\mathrm{NP}$; I include a self-contained proof.

Throughout, a binary rational means a rational whose numerator and denominator are encoded in binary.

1. Fractional Pareto optimality: a group graph

Let $S$ be the positive support of an allocation. For $g\in S$, write $t_g$ for its type, $B_g$ for its bundle, and $\lambda_g>0$ for its mass. Define
$$ a_t(e)= \begin{cases} 0,&s_t(e)=1,\\ 1,&s_t(e)=k. \end{cases} $$

Construct a directed multigraph $G$ on $S$. Whenever $e\in B_g$ and $e\notin B_h$, include an edge $g\to h$, labelled $e$, of weight
$$ b(g,h,e)=a_{t_h}(e)-a_{t_g}(e). $$

Lemma 1. The allocation is fractionally Pareto optimal if and only if $G$ has no negative directed cycle.

Proof. First suppose that $G$ has a negative cycle. A negative closed walk contains a simple negative cycle, so take one with distinct vertices:
$$ g_1\xrightarrow{e_1}g_2\xrightarrow{e_2}\cdots \xrightarrow{e_{\ell-1}}g_\ell\xrightarrow{e_\ell}g_1. $$
Use cyclic indices and put
$$ D_i=s_{t_{g_i}}(e_i), \qquad I_i=s_{t_{g_i}}(e_{i-1}). $$
Group $g_i$ holds $e_i$ and misses $e_{i-1}$, so these two kinds are distinct. Moreover,
$$ \prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{I_i}{D_i} = k^{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b(g_i,g_{i+1},e_i)} <1. $$

Choose $\delta_1>0$ and recursively define
$$ \delta_i=\frac{I_i}{D_i}\delta_{i-1} \qquad (2\le i\le\ell). $$
Transfer $\delta_i$ units of kind $e_i$ from $g_i$ to $g_{i+1}$. All amounts are positive multiples of $\delta_1$. Taking $\delta_1$ sufficiently small ensures
$$ \delta_i\le \min\{\lambda_{g_i},\lambda_{g_{i+1}}\} $$
for every edge. Because the cycle is simple, each participating group has one incoming and one outgoing transfer, involving distinct kinds. Thus every coordinate remains within its required bounds. Every transfer preserves its kind’s supply.

For $2\le i\le\ell$, the cost change of $g_i$ is
$$ I_i\delta_{i-1}-D_i\delta_i=0. $$
The cost change of $g_1$ is
$$ I_1\delta_\ell-D_1\delta_1 = D_1\delta_1 \left(\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{I_i}{D_i}-1\right) <0. $$
This is a fractional Pareto improvement.

Conversely, suppose $G$ has no negative cycle. Add a source with zero-weight edges to all vertices, and let $d_g$ be shortest-path distances. They satisfy
$$ d_h\le d_g+a_{t_h}(e)-a_{t_g}(e) $$
on every edge. Setting $w_g=k^{-d_g}>0$ gives
$$ w_gs_{t_g}(e)\le w_hs_{t_h}(e) $$
whenever $g$ holds $e$ and $h$ misses $e$.

In the original assignment, each coordinate is either $\lambda_g$ or zero. Consequently, a competing assignment can only remove kind $e$ from its original holders and give it to its original nonholders. Supply conservation equates total removals and additions. Decompose these changes into transfers. Each transfer weakly increases weighted cost, by the displayed inequality. Therefore
$$ \sum_g w_g\sum_e s_{t_g}(e)y_{g,e} \ge \sum_g w_g\lambda_gc_{t_g}(B_g). $$
A Pareto improvement would strictly decrease this weighted sum, because all weights are positive. This contradiction proves the lemma. $\square$

In particular, fractional Pareto optimality depends only on positive support, and deleting groups preserves it.

2. Exact EFX permits a graph on types

For an allocation, define its type marginals by
$$ X_{t,e}=\sum_{B:e\in B}\lambda_{t,B}. $$
Thus $0\le X_{t,e}\le\mu_t$.

Construct a directed multigraph $H$ on the types. For distinct types $t,u$, include an edge $t\to u$, labelled $e$, whenever
$$ X_{t,e}>0, \qquad X_{u,e}<\mu_u. $$
Give it weight
$$ b(t,u,e)=a_u(e)-a_t(e). $$
The two conditions mean that some positive group of type $t$ holds $e$, and some positive group of type $u$ misses $e$.

We first isolate the support property needed for this compression:
$$ \text{For a fixed type, no two positive bundles }A\subsetneq B \text{ satisfy }|B\setminus A|\ge 2. $$

Lemma 2. Exact EFX implies this property.

Proof. Suppose positive bundles $A,B$ of the same type $t$ satisfy $A\subsetneq B$ and $|B\setminus A|\ge2$. Choose $e\in B\setminus A$. Since costs are positive,
$$ c_t(B\setminus\{e\}) = c_t(A)+c_t((B\setminus A)\setminus\{e\}) > c_t(A), $$
contradicting exact EFX. $\square$

Theorem 3. Under the support property above, the allocation is fractionally Pareto optimal if and only if $H$ has no negative directed cycle.

Proof. If $H$ has no negative cycle, then neither does $G$: projecting a group edge to its types preserves its weight; edges within one type have weight zero. A negative cycle in $G$ would therefore project to a negative closed walk in $H$, after removing zero-weight steps within types. Lemma 1 gives fractional Pareto optimality.

For the converse, suppose $H$ has a negative closed walk, and choose one with the fewest edges. It is a simple cycle: splitting a nonsimple negative closed walk produces a shorter negative closed walk.

Its incoming and outgoing labels at every vertex are distinct. Indeed, if two consecutive edges have the same label $e$, their weights add to
$$ (a_t(e)-a_v(e))+(a_u(e)-a_t(e)) = a_u(e)-a_v(e). $$
For a cycle of length at least three, the edge $v\to u$ labelled $e$ exists: its donor condition comes from the first edge and its recipient condition from the second. Contracting the two edges gives a shorter negative closed walk. For a cycle of length two, using the same label on both edges gives total weight zero.

We now lift the cycle to a closed walk in $G$. At a type $t$, let $e$ be its incoming label and $f$ its outgoing label, with $e\ne f$. Choose positive bundles $A,B$ of type $t$ such that
$$ e\notin A, \qquad f\in B. $$

There are three cases:

  • If $f\in A$, use group $(t,A)$ for both the incoming and outgoing edges.
  • If $e\notin B$, use group $(t,B)$ for both edges.
  • Otherwise, $B$ contains both $e,f$ and $A$ misses both. The support property implies $A\not\subseteq B$, since otherwise $|B\setminus A|\ge2$. Choose $h\in A\setminus B$. The group graph has a zero-weight edge

$$ (t,A)\xrightarrow{h}(t,B). $$

Thus the incoming recipient and outgoing donor can always be connected within the type using zero-weight edges. Between types, use the original cycle labels. The resulting group closed walk has the same negative total weight. It contains a negative cycle, so Lemma 1 rules out fractional Pareto optimality. $\square$

For the task’s exceptional value $k=2$, the required fairness is exact EFX. Therefore Theorem 3 applies to every candidate valid allocation.

There is also a small discrete set of type weights that suffices. If $H$ has no negative cycle, shortest-path distances from an added zero-edge source satisfy
$$ d_t\in\{-(\tau-1),\ldots,0\}. $$
This follows because shortest paths can be chosen simple, with at most $\tau-1$ edges between types, and every edge weight is at least $-1$.

The weights
$$ w_t=k^{-d_t} $$
then satisfy
$$ w_ts_t(e)\le w_us_u(e) \quad\text{whenever }X_{t,e}>0,\ X_{u,e}<\mu_u. $$
These type weights certify fractional Pareto optimality for every bundle decomposition having the same marginals.

3. Two algorithmic tools

Exact rational linear programming

I use the following standard bit-complexity theorem: an explicitly described rational linear program can be solved in time polynomial in its total encoding length. The algorithm detects infeasibility and, when a bounded feasible region is nonempty, produces an exact rational optimum whose encoding length is polynomial in the input length. Taking a zero objective produces an exact feasible rational point. This is the rational linear-programming theorem underlying [Khachiyan’s polynomial algorithms](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5239).

All linear programs below have explicit rational descriptions and variables bounded between zero and one. Thus they satisfy these hypotheses, including when the feasible region has lower dimension.

Decomposition with prescribed counts

Lemma 4. Let $Q$ be partitioned into cells $C_1,\ldots,C_J$. Suppose rational numbers $v_e$ satisfy
$$ 0\le v_e\le1, \qquad \sum_{e\in C_j}v_e=p_j, $$
where each $p_j$ is an integer. In polynomial time, one can express $v$ as a rational distribution on $O(m)$ bundles, each containing exactly $p_j$ kinds from cell $C_j$.

Proof. For each cell, order its kinds and partition $[0,p_j)$ into consecutive half-open intervals $I_e$ of lengths $v_e$. For $u\in[0,1)$, select $e\in C_j$ when $I_e$ contains one of
$$ u,u+1,\ldots,u+p_j-1. $$
If $p_j=0$, select nothing in that cell.

These $p_j$ points lie in the partition. An interval of length at most one cannot contain two of them, so exactly $p_j$ distinct kinds are selected. The measure of offsets selecting $e$ is
$$ \sum_{h=0}^{p_j-1}|I_e\cap[h,h+1)|=|I_e|=v_e. $$

Use the same offset $u$ for every cell. This preserves all marginals and all cell counts. The selected bundle changes only at fractional parts of interval endpoints. There are $O(m)$ such breakpoints, all rational. On each interval between consecutive breakpoints, assign its length as the mass of the constant bundle.

All rational calculations have polynomial encoding length: the endpoints are sums of input rationals, and the output masses are differences of their fractional parts. $\square$

4. Polynomial time for fixed support size when $k=2$

Theorem 5. When $k=2$, computing a valid allocation or reporting nonexistence is polynomial-time solvable for every fixed $\tau$.

Proof. Partition kinds by their cost vectors across the supported types. Let the nonempty cells be $C_1,\ldots,C_J$, where
$$ J\le 2^\tau. $$
Write $\sigma_j(t)\in\{1,2\}$ for type $t$’s cost of a kind in cell $C_j$.

A count profile is a vector
$$ p=(p_1,\ldots,p_J), \qquad 0\le p_j\le |C_j|, $$
with integer coordinates. Its cost to type $t$ is
$$ C_t(p)=\sum_j \sigma_j(t)p_j. $$
Define its maximum remaining cost after removing one kind by
$$ R_t(p)= \begin{cases} 0,&\sum_jp_j=0,\\ C_t(p)-\min\{\sigma_j(t):p_j>0\},&\sum_jp_j>0. \end{cases} $$
These quantities depend only on the count profile.

Enumerating fairness thresholds

Enumerate vectors
$$ A=(A_1,\ldots,A_\tau)\in\{0,\ldots,2m\}^\tau. $$
For a given vector, permit profile $p$ for workers of type $t$ exactly when
$$ C_u(p)\ge A_u\quad\text{for every type }u, \qquad R_t(p)\le A_t. $$

Every allocation using only permitted profiles is exact EFX: for a positive bundle $B$ of type $t$ and any positive bundle $D$,
$$ c_t(B\setminus\{e\}) \le R_t(p_B) \le A_t \le c_t(D). $$

Conversely, every exact EFX allocation is covered by one enumerated vector, namely
$$ A_u=\min_{D\text{ positive}}c_u(D). $$
The minima are integers between zero and $2m$. No requirement that a chosen threshold be attained is needed for the sufficiency direction.

Enumerating efficiency weights

Enumerate
$$ d\in\{-(\tau-1),\ldots,0\}^\tau $$
and put $w_t=2^{-d_t}$. For kind $e$, define its weighted type costs
$$ v_{t,e}=w_ts_t(e). $$

For each kind, sort types by $v_{t,e}$. Let $\theta_e$ be the smallest weighted cost whose cumulative type capacity is at least $q_e$:
$$ \sum_{t:v_{t,e}<\theta_e}\mu_t<q_e \le \sum_{t:v_{t,e}\le\theta_e}\mu_t. $$
Minimum weighted cost for this kind is characterized by
$$ X_{t,e}= \begin{cases} \mu_t,&v_{t,e}<\theta_e,\\ 0,&v_{t,e}>\theta_e, \end{cases} $$
with the types at cost $\theta_e$ sharing the remaining supply.

To justify this characterization, an assignment with a positive amount at a higher weighted cost and unused capacity at a lower weighted cost admits a strictly improving transfer. Conversely, under the displayed conditions, every original holder has weighted cost at most $\theta_e$, and every original nonholder has weighted cost at least $\theta_e$, so every transfer weakly increases weighted cost.

The linear program

For each type $t$ and each profile $p$ permitted for it, introduce:

  • $z_{t,p}$, its assigned mass;
  • $x_{t,p,e}$, the mass in that class receiving kind $e$.

Impose
$$ z_{t,p}\ge0, \qquad \sum_pz_{t,p}=\mu_t, $$
$$ 0\le x_{t,p,e}\le z_{t,p}, \qquad \sum_{t,p}x_{t,p,e}=q_e, $$
and
$$ \sum_{e\in C_j}x_{t,p,e}=p_jz_{t,p}. $$
Set
$$ X_{t,e}=\sum_px_{t,p,e} $$
and impose the zero or full constraints determined by $\theta_e$.

Every variable is bounded by one, so the linear program is bounded and has rational data.

If it is feasible, take an exact rational solution. For every class with $z_{t,p}>0$, apply Lemma 4 to
$$ v_e=\frac{x_{t,p,e}}{z_{t,p}}. $$
Multiply its output masses by $z_{t,p}$. The resulting bundles have profile $p$, preserve the type masses and supplies, and satisfy exact EFX.

They are also fractionally Pareto optimal. Their marginals minimize weighted cost separately for every kind, and the strictly positive weights $w_t$, assigned to all groups of type $t$, certify optimality by the weighted-transfer argument in Lemma 1.

Completeness

Suppose a valid allocation exists. Choose its actual minimum-cost vector $A$. By Theorems 2 and 3, its type graph has no negative cycle. Its shortest-path distance vector $d$ is among those enumerated.

For these distances, all holder-to-nonholder transfers satisfy
$$ w_ts_t(e)\le w_us_u(e). $$
Consequently its marginals minimize weighted cost for every kind and obey the threshold constraints above. Aggregating its masses and marginals by count profile therefore gives a feasible solution of the corresponding linear program.

Hence failure of every enumerated linear program proves nonexistence.

Running time

There are at most
$$ (2m+1)^\tau\tau^\tau $$
pairs of threshold and distance vectors. The number of count profiles is at most
$$ \prod_j(|C_j|+1)\le(m+1)^{2^\tau}. $$
Each linear program has $O(\tau(m+1)^{2^\tau+1})$ variables and polynomially many constraints relative to this bound, with polynomially encoded coefficients.

Thus the running time is bounded by
$$ \tau^{O(\tau)}(m+1)^{O(2^\tau+\tau)} \operatorname{poly}(L). $$
For every fixed $\tau$, this is polynomial in $m,L$. The decomposition also produces a polynomial-size rational output for fixed $\tau$. $\square$

5. A minimum-cost construction for unrestricted support

The next construction works for arbitrary $\tau$ and any common $k>1$.

Lemma 6. Fix rational type loads $h_t$ such that the following system is feasible:
$$ 0\le X_{t,e}\le\mu_t, \qquad \sum_tX_{t,e}=q_e, \qquad \sum_eX_{t,e}=h_t. $$
Minimize
$$ \sum_{t,e}s_t(e)X_{t,e} $$
over this system. Every optimal solution has a type graph with no negative cycle.

Proof. Suppose an optimal solution has a simple negative type cycle, with edge labels $e_i$. Transfer the same sufficiently small amount $\delta>0$ along each cycle edge.

Each donor coordinate is positive and each recipient coordinate is below its upper bound. Because the cycle is simple, taking $\delta$ smaller than all relevant positive margins preserves every bound. A coordinate that has both an incoming and outgoing transfer of the same kind has net change zero.

Every kind’s supply is conserved. Every type receives and gives the same total amount, so all row loads $h_t$ are conserved.

For every edge $t\to u$ labelled $e$,
$$ s_u(e)-s_t(e) = (k-1)(a_u(e)-a_t(e)). $$
Therefore the objective change is
$$ \delta(k-1)\sum_{\text{cycle}}b(t,u,e)<0, $$
contradicting optimality. $\square$

The absence of a negative type cycle is sufficient for fractional Pareto optimality of any bundle decomposition of these marginals. This sufficiency does not require fairness: shortest-path type weights certify all group transfers.

Theorem 7. Let
$$ r=\sum_eq_e. $$
A valid allocation can be computed in polynomial time for arbitrary support size in either of the following cases:

  • $r\le1$;
  • $r$ is an integer and $k(r-1)\le\beta r$.

Proof. Solve the bounded rational linear program
$$ \min\sum_{t,e}s_t(e)X_{t,e} $$
subject to
$$ 0\le X_{t,e}\le\mu_t, \qquad \sum_tX_{t,e}=q_e, \qquad \sum_eX_{t,e}=\mu_tr. $$
It is feasible because
$$ X_{t,e}=\mu_tq_e $$
satisfies all constraints. Its description has polynomial size in $m,\tau,L$, including the coefficients $\mu_tr$. Exact rational linear programming produces an optimum in polynomial time. Lemma 6 gives a type graph with no negative cycle.

If $r\le1$, assign
$$ \lambda_{t,\{e\}}=X_{t,e}, \qquad \lambda_{t,\varnothing}=\mu_t(1-r), $$
omitting zero entries. Every bundle is empty or a singleton, so every required removal leaves cost zero. Fairness follows. Fractional Pareto optimality follows from the type-weight certificate.

Now suppose $r$ is an integer. For each type, the normalized marginals satisfy
$$ 0\le\frac{X_{t,e}}{\mu_t}\le1, \qquad \sum_e\frac{X_{t,e}}{\mu_t}=r. $$
Apply Lemma 4 with one cell containing all kinds. This constructs bundles of cardinality exactly $r$ with the prescribed marginals.

For any positive bundles $B,D$, evaluating type $t$, and $e\in B$,
$$ c_t(B\setminus\{e\})\le k(r-1) \le\beta r \le\beta c_t(D). $$
The last inequality uses $|D|=r$ and the lower bound of one on each cost. Thus fairness holds. Fractional Pareto optimality again follows from the same type weights.

There are $O(m)$ bundles per type, and all masses have polynomial encoding length by Lemma 4 and the rational linear-programming theorem. $\square$

For $k=2$, the exceptional factor is $\beta=1$, and total supply $r=2$ satisfies
$$ k(r-1)=2=\beta r. $$
Thus every such instance admits a valid allocation, regardless of support size.

6. Polynomial certificates for the general problem

For completeness, the existence decision problem belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$.

Proposition 8. Whenever a valid allocation exists, one exists with at most $m+\tau$ positive groups and polynomially encoded rational masses.

Proof. Let $S$ be the support of any valid allocation. Form the matrix $A$ with one column for each $(t,B)\in S$: the column has a one in type row $t$, and a one in supply row $e$ exactly when $e\in B$. There are
$$ r_0=m+\tau $$
rows. Let
$$ b=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau,q_1,\ldots,q_m). $$

The polytope
$$ P=\{x\ge0:Ax=b\} $$
is nonempty and bounded, since a coordinate of type $t$ is at most $\mu_t$. Take a vertex $x^\star$.

Its positive columns are linearly independent. Otherwise, a nonzero vector $z$ supported on those columns would satisfy $Az=0$, and both $x^\star+\varepsilon z$ and $x^\star-\varepsilon z$ would be feasible for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, contradicting extremality. Hence its positive support has size
$$ h\le r_0. $$
Fairness is preserved by deleting groups. Fractional Pareto optimality is preserved because the new group graph is a subgraph of the original one.

Choose $h$ rows giving an invertible square matrix $M$ on the positive columns. Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of entries of $b$; its bit length is $O(L)$. Cramer’s rule gives
$$ x^\star_j=\frac{\det M_j}{D\det M}, $$
where $M_j$ replaces column $j$ by the relevant entries of $Db$. Hadamard’s inequality yields
$$ |\det M|\le h^{h/2}, \qquad |\det M_j|\le D h^{h/2}. $$
For the second bound, the replaced column has norm at most $D\sqrt h$, while every other column has norm at most $\sqrt h$.

Thus every mass has
$$ O\bigl(L+r_0\log(r_0+1)\bigr) $$
bits. $\square$

A certificate lists these groups and masses. Positivity, mass equations, supply equations, and fairness are checked by exact rational arithmetic. Lemma 1 checks fractional Pareto optimality through negative-cycle detection in a graph with integer weights and polynomially many vertices and edges. All verification therefore takes polynomial time.

7. What remains unresolved

The fixed-support algorithm above has an exponent depending on $\tau$. It does not establish polynomial dependence on unrestricted support size. The minimum-cost construction settles substantial supply regimes, but its fairness proof does not cover arbitrary total supply and arbitrary $k$.

Nonexistence still occurs outside those regimes. For example, take one type, two kinds with costs $1,k$, and supplies $1,\tfrac12$. Every positive bundle must contain the full-supply cheap kind, so the only possible allocation assigns masses $\tfrac12,\tfrac12$ to the cheap singleton and the two-kind bundle. Removing the cheap kind from the latter requires
$$ k\le\beta, $$
which fails: for $k\ne2$,
$$ k-\left(2-\frac1k\right)=\frac{(k-1)^2}{k}>0, $$
and for $k=2$ the stipulated $\beta$ equals one.

Accordingly, these results neither prove unrestricted polynomial-time solvability nor establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.

1. Lemma 1: the group graph characterization
