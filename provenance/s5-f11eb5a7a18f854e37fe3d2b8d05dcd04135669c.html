The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), and at most \(K\) rounds, each adaptive arrival reveals a good type and a rational supply \(q_r>0\) copies per capita. Goods must be allocated immediately, and each individual receives an integer number of copies. What is the supremal ratio \(\rho^*(\tau,K,L)\) that a deterministic online policy can guarantee, at every possible stopping round and for almost every individual, under \(B_a+U_a\geq \rho^* V_a\), and can a policy attaining that ratio be computed efficiently?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Represent type \(t\)'s individuals by an interval of length \(\mu_t\), with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\).
Fix \(K\geq1\). After observing previous allocations, an adaptive adversary may stop or reveal a batch with binary-rational supply \(q_r>0\) copies per capita and per-copy values \(v_{t,r}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\). Values are additive; copies within a batch are identical. The policy knows the society and the bounds \(K,L\), but no future values, supplies, or stopping round; \(L\) bounds the total binary encoding length of the society and arrival transcript.
Immediately and irrevocably, a deterministic policy chooses measurable counts \(c_r(a)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}\), finite almost everywhere, satisfying \(\int c_r(a)\,d\lambda(a)=q_r\). Individuals of the same type may receive different counts.
For an individual \(a\) of type \(t\), after round \(s\), define \(B_a^s=\sum_{r\leq s}c_r(a)v_{t,r}\) and the per-capita proportional benchmark \(V_a^s=\sum_{r\leq s}q_rv_{t,r}\). The one-copy correction is \(U_a^s=\max_{r\leq s}v_{t,r}\): every positive-supply batch has copies allocated outside any single individual's finite bundle. Empty maxima are zero.
A policy guarantees \(\rho\in[0,1]\) if \(B_a^s+U_a^s\geq\rho V_a^s\) for almost every individual at every possible stopping round, on every admissible adaptive instance. Let \(\rho^*(\tau,K,L)\) be the supremum of such guarantees over deterministic policies, uniformly over societies of support size \(\tau\).
What is \(\rho^*(\tau,K,L)\), and is this supremum attained by a deterministic online policy implementable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L\)?
Here \(m\leq K\) is the realized number of batches; an implementation represents allocations by finite partitions of the type intervals with binary-rational endpoints and binary-encoded integer counts.
For finite societies with \(n\geq2\) agents, unknown horizon, and unrestricted nonnegative additive values, every deterministic policy has an adaptive instance with sufficiently large \(m\) goods whose PROP1 factor is at most \(4n\log\log m/\log m\); a deterministic policy guarantees \(\Omega(1/\log(nm))\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13650v2).
No result settling this atomless, integer-count, per-capita model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive type masses to total mass one and modeled each type as an atomless interval; allocation may distinguish individuals within a type.
  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational per-copy values, additive valuations, identical copies within each arrival, and revelation of current values without advance knowledge of future values.
  • Required immediate, irrevocable allocation of the entire supply through measurable, almost-everywhere finite nonnegative integer counts.
  • Defined V as the per-capita proportional benchmark, weighting each arrival's per-copy value by its supply.
  • Interpreted U as the value of one arrived copy outside the individual's bundle; in the atomless model every positive-supply batch remains available outside any individual's finite bundle.
  • Restricted ratios to [0,1], retaining the discrete PROP1 convention, and defined the supremum uniformly over admissible societies and adaptive histories.
  • Made K and the total input-bit bound L known to the policy; the actual stopping round remains unknown.
  • Used m for the realized number of arrival batches and specified rational interval partitions with binary integer counts as the computational allocation representation.

Result

For every admissible choice of parameters,
\[ \boxed{\rho^*(\tau,K,L)=1.} \]
The supremum is attained by a deterministic online policy implementable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L\). After \(s\) rounds, the policy uses at most \(s+1\) intervals per type.

The key is to balance cumulative utility within each type, while giving each type an average of \(q_r\) copies in round \(r\).

1. The invariant

Let \(I_t\) be the interval representing type \(t\), with length \(\mu_t\). Write
\[ V_t^s=\sum_{r\le s}q_rv_{t,r}, \qquad U_t^s=\max_{r\le s}v_{t,r}. \]

The policy maintains a finite interval partition of \(I_t\), with cumulative utility constant on each cell. It maintains the following two invariants:

  • The mean utility within the type equals its proportional benchmark:

\[ \frac{1}{\mu_t}\int_{I_t}B_a^s\,d\lambda(a)=V_t^s. \]

  • Its largest and smallest cell utilities differ by at most \(U_t^s\).

These imply the required guarantee. Indeed, if \(b_{\min}\) and \(b_{\max}\) are the smallest and largest cell utilities, then
\[ V_t^s\le b_{\max}\le b_{\min}+U_t^s. \]
Consequently, every individual outside the partition endpoints satisfies
\[ B_a^s+U_a^s\ge V_a^s. \]

Initially, each type has one cell with utility zero, so both invariants hold. We now construct the online update.

2. A balancing lemma

Consider an interval of length \(\mu>0\), partitioned into cells of positive lengths \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_N\). Their current utilities are \(b_1,\ldots,b_N\), respectively. Let
\[ b_{\min}=\min_i b_i, \qquad W=\max_i b_i-b_{\min}. \]

Suppose a batch has per-copy value \(v>0\), and we must allocate a total of \(Q=\mu q\) copies on this interval.

Lemma. There is a deterministic allocation using nonnegative integer counts that:

  • allocates exactly \(Q\) copies;
  • splits at most one existing cell;
  • leaves the utility range at most \(\max\{W,v\}\);
  • has rational endpoints and counts when the input data are rational.

Proof. Choose a positive integer \(D\) such that
\[ \beta_i=Db_i \quad\text{and}\quad d=Dv \]
are integers. In particular, \(d>0\).

For an integer \(z\), define
\[ F(z)=\sum_{i=1}^N \ell_i\max\left\{0,\left\lfloor\frac{z-\beta_i}{d}\right\rfloor+1\right\}. \]

For each individual initially at utility \(b_i\), the successive levels at which a further copy would be assigned are
\[ b_i,\ b_i+v,\ b_i+2v,\ldots. \]
Thus \(F(z)\) counts, with cell lengths as weights, all such assignment levels at most \(z/D\).

The function \(F\) is nondecreasing and unbounded. Let \(z\) be the smallest integer satisfying \(F(z)\ge Q\). Since
\[ F\left(\min_i\beta_i-1\right)=0<Q, \]
we have \(z\ge\min_i\beta_i\), and minimality gives
\[ A:=F(z-1)<Q\le F(z). \]

First assign every individual in cell \(i\) the integer count
\[ k_i=\max\left\{0,\left\lfloor\frac{z-1-\beta_i}{d}\right\rfloor+1\right\}. \]
This allocates exactly \(A\) copies.

The cells whose next assignment level is exactly \(z/D\) are
\[ T=\{i:\beta_i\le z,\ d\text{ divides }z-\beta_i\}. \]
Since
\[ F(z)-F(z-1)=\sum_{i\in T}\ell_i, \]
the remaining quantity \(\delta=Q-A\) satisfies
\[ 0<\delta\le\sum_{i\in T}\ell_i. \]

Process the cells in \(T\) in their spatial order. Give one additional copy to every individual in successive whole cells, until the remaining quantity fits inside one cell. If necessary, give one additional copy to a prefix of that cell of precisely the remaining length. This splits at most one cell and allocates exactly \(Q\) copies. The construction is deterministic.

To prove the range bound, put \(\theta=z/D\). An initial utility \(b_i\le\theta\) becomes
\[ b_i+k_iv\in[\theta,\theta+v). \]
An additional copy is assigned only when this intermediate utility equals \(\theta\), so the resulting utility is \(\theta+v\).

An initial utility \(b_i>\theta\) receives no copies. Since \(\theta\ge b_{\min}\), its utility satisfies
\[ b_i\le b_{\min}+W\le\theta+W. \]
Therefore all final utilities lie in
\[ [\theta,\theta+\max\{W,v\}], \]
proving the claimed range bound.

Finally, all quantities defining a possible split are rational: they are obtained from \(Q\), the cell lengths, and integer counts by addition and subtraction. Thus the new endpoint is rational. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

3. The online policy and its guarantee

Upon observing round \(r\), process each type separately. On the interval \(I_t\), allocate exactly
\[ Q_t=\mu_tq_r \]
copies.

If \(v_{t,r}>0\), apply the balancing lemma to the current partition.

If \(v_{t,r}=0\), give everyone \(\lfloor q_r\rfloor\) copies, and give one additional copy to a prefix of \(I_t\) of length
\[ \mu_t(q_r-\lfloor q_r\rfloor). \]
This also splits at most one existing cell and leaves utilities unchanged.

Because the masses sum to one, the entire allocation satisfies
\[ \int c_r(a)\,d\lambda(a) =\sum_t\mu_tq_r=q_r. \]
All assigned counts are nonnegative integers and finite.

For type \(t\), its mean utility increases by exactly \(q_rv_{t,r}\). Hence the mean invariant is preserved.

For a positive-valued batch, the balancing lemma and the previous range invariant give a new utility range at most
\[ \max\{U_t^{r-1},v_{t,r}\}=U_t^r. \]
For a zero-valued batch, the range and \(U_t\) remain unchanged. Thus the range invariant is preserved as well.

It follows by induction that, after every round,
\[ B_a^s+U_a^s\ge V_a^s \]
for almost every individual. The argument applies to every revealed batch, regardless of how it was selected from previous allocations. It therefore covers adaptive adversaries and every possible stopping round.

The policy guarantees factor \(1\). Since the definition restricts guarantees to \([0,1]\), this proves
\[ \rho^*(\tau,K,L)=1. \]

4. Polynomial-time implementation

The balancing lemma does not require enumerating copies or successive assignment levels.

4.1 Finding the threshold

For each type, maintain
\[ D=\prod_{h\le r}\operatorname{den}(v_{t,h}), \]
using denominator \(1\) for a zero value. Every existing utility is an integer combination of previously observed values, so \(Db_i\) is an integer.

Let \(p=\lceil q_r\rceil\). An integer search interval for the threshold is
\[ \min_i\beta_i\le z\le H, \qquad H=\max_i\beta_i+dp. \]
Indeed, at \(H\), every cell contributes at least \(p+1\) assignment levels, so
\[ F(H)\ge\mu_t(p+1)>Q_t. \]
Binary search therefore finds the smallest suitable \(z\). Each evaluation of \(F\) uses one integer division and a bounded number of exact arithmetic operations per cell.

4.2 Partition size and endpoint encoding

Each round creates at most one new cell per type. Starting from one cell, type \(t\) therefore has at most \(s+1\) cells after round \(s\).

To bound endpoint encoding lengths, define
\[ E_s= \left(\prod_t\operatorname{den}(\mu_t)\right) \left(\prod_{r\le s}\operatorname{den}(q_r)\right). \]
All endpoints can be represented with denominators dividing \(E_s\).

This holds initially. In a positive-valued round, a split length is \(\mu_tq_r\) minus integer multiples of existing cell lengths. In a zero-valued round, the possible new endpoint uses the length \(\mu_t(q_r-\lfloor q_r\rfloor)\). Both constructions preserve the stated denominator property.

The total input encoding length is at most \(L\), so \(E_s\) and each type’s \(D\) have \(O(L)\) bits. Endpoints lie in \([0,1]\), and consequently have \(O(L)\)-bit representations. If “binary-rational” means dyadic rational, these constructions also preserve dyadic endpoints.

4.3 Utility and count encoding

Every input rational has magnitude at most \(2^L\); every positive input value is at least \(2^{-L}\). Hence
\[ V_t^s\le m2^{2L}, \qquad U_t^s\le2^L. \]
By the proved invariants, every cell utility satisfies
\[ 0\le b_i\le V_t^s+U_t^s\le(m+1)2^{2L}. \]

Since \(D\le2^L\), the integers \(\beta_i\), \(d\), and \(H\) have \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits. Binary search therefore takes \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) iterations.

For a positive-valued batch, each allocated count is at most
\[ \frac{\max_i b_i}{v_{t,r}}+\lceil q_r\rceil+2. \]
The bounds above show that this count also has \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits. Zero-valued batches satisfy the same encoding bound directly.

Using common denominators for cell lengths, every threshold evaluation operates on integers of polynomial bit length. There are \(O(\tau m)\) cells in the current state and polynomially many threshold evaluations. Recording all round allocations also requires only polynomial space. The complete policy is therefore implementable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

5. Why the finite-agent obstruction does not transfer

In the [cited finite-agent model](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13650v2), each arriving indivisible good must be assigned to a single agent. Here, an interval representing a type can be split at a rational endpoint. That operation lets the policy balance utilities within every type while assigning it exactly its prescribed average supply. The proof above depends explicitly on this atomless operation.
