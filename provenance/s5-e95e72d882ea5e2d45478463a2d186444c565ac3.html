The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given alternatives \(C\), a finite support \(S\subseteq\mathcal{L}(C)\) with rational masses \(\mu_V\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), an effective sample size \(\rho>0\), and fixed rational \(\varphi\in(0,1)\), define a posterior on ground-truth rankings by \(\pi_{\mu,\rho}(W)\propto\varphi^{\rho\sum_{V\in S}\mu_V\operatorname{Kendall}(V,W)}\). For each nonempty decision set \(D\subseteq C\), let \(R_{\mu,\rho}(D)=\sum_{W\in\mathcal{L}(C)}\pi_{\mu,\rho}(W)\left(1-\mathbf{1}[\operatorname{top}(W)\in D]/|D|\right)\). Determine a Bayes-risk-minimizing \(D\), or decide whether a given \(D\) is optimal and whether \(R_{\mu,\rho}(D_1)\le R_{\mu,\rho}(D_2)\).

Complexity classification

For every fixed rational \(\varphi\in(0,1)\), both decision problems are \(\Theta_2^p=\mathsf P_{\parallel}^{\mathsf{NP}}\)-hard, already for singleton decisions. Finding any minimizing set is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard. The search hardness holds even on instances having a unique minimizing set.

Consequently, none of these problems admits an algorithm polynomial in \((m,\tau,L)\) unless \(\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}\). These statements are hardness results, not completeness claims.

The proof below also gives an exact algorithm running in \(2^{O(m+L)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) bit operations, with the encoding of \(\rho\) included in \(L\).

1. Characterizing all minimizing sets

For each alternative \(c\), define its unnormalized posterior top mass by

\(Z_c=\sum_{W:\operatorname{top}(W)=c}\varphi^{\rho\sum_{V\in S}\mu_V\operatorname{Kendall}(V,W)}\).

Write \(Z=\sum_{c\in C}Z_c\), which is strictly positive. Directly from the definition of risk,

\(R_{\mu,\rho}(D)=1-\dfrac{\sum_{c\in D}Z_c}{|D|Z}\).

Let \(M=\max_{c\in C}Z_c\). Since every \(Z_c\le M\),

\(\dfrac{1}{|D|}\sum_{c\in D}Z_c\le M\),

with equality exactly when every member of \(D\) has top mass \(M\). Therefore:

Proven characterization. A nonempty set \(D\) minimizes risk if and only if

\(D\subseteq\{c\in C:Z_c=M\}\).

In particular, a minimizing singleton always exists. If precisely one alternative maximizes \(Z_c\), its singleton is the unique minimizing set.

For comparison, cancellation of the positive normalization gives

\(R_{\mu,\rho}(D_1)\le R_{\mu,\rho}(D_2)\iff |D_2|\sum_{c\in D_1}Z_c-|D_1|\sum_{c\in D_2}Z_c\ge0\).

When \(m=1\), all three tasks are immediate. The hardness concerns unbounded \(m\).

2. Exact transfer of finite-profile decision hardness

We use the finite-profile hardness theorem stated in the question: for every fixed dispersion, optimality and risk comparison under the uniform prior and this loss are \(\Theta_2^p\)-hard, already for singleton decisions. This is Theorem 3 of [Azari Soufiani, Parkes, and Xia](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2014/file/405bfe28e212b7e1d100f013aae9b2d1-Paper.pdf).

Let \(P\) be an explicitly listed profile of \(n\) votes. Aggregate identical rankings, and let \(n_V\) denote the multiplicity of ranking \(V\). Set

\(\mu_V=n_V/n,\qquad \rho=n\).

Then, for every ranking \(W\),

\(\rho\sum_{V\in S}\mu_V\operatorname{Kendall}(V,W)=\sum_{V\in S}n_V\operatorname{Kendall}(V,W)=\sum_{V\in P}\operatorname{Kendall}(V,W)\).

Thus the posterior distributions and all decision risks agree exactly.

Aggregation takes polynomial time in the explicit source input length. There are \(\tau\le n\) support types; each mass uses \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits, and \(\rho\) uses \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits. Hence this is a polynomial-time many-one reduction to the rational-mass formulation.

This already proves both decision-hardness claims. The next construction additionally proves search hardness and obtains hardness with dyadic masses.

3. A reduction with a unique minimizing set

We reduce from Kemeny Winner: given an explicit profile \(P\) over alternatives \(A\) and a designated \(c\in A\), decide whether some ranking minimizing total Kendall distance starts with \(c\). This problem is \(\Theta_2^p\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. See [Hemaspaandra, Spakowski, and Vogel](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397505005785).

We verify the margins-based construction underlying the low-dispersion reduction, including its constrained-score calculations. This verification is what ensures hardness for returning any minimizing set.

3.1. Expressing Kendall scores through margins

Let \(n\) be the number of votes in \(P\). Define

\(w_P(x,y)=\#\{V\in P:x\succ_V y\}-\#\{V\in P:y\succ_V x\}\).

For a ranking \(V\) of \(A\), let \(F(V)\) be the sum of the positive margins whose preferred orientations \(V\) reverses:

\(F(V)=\sum_{x,y:\,w_P(x,y)>0}|w_P(x,y)|\,\mathbf1[y\succ_V x]\).

The sum contains one orientation of each pair with nonzero margin.

For each unordered pair, agreeing with its majority orientation contributes the minority count to Kendall distance; reversing it adds the absolute margin. Consequently,

\(\operatorname{Kendall}(P,V)=B_P+F(V)\),

where \(B_P\) is independent of \(V\).

Define the nonnegative integers

\(k=\min_{V\in\mathcal L(A)}F(V),\qquad k_c=\min_{V:\operatorname{top}(V)=c}F(V)\).

Thus \(c\) is a Kemeny winner exactly when \(k_c=k\). Otherwise, integrality gives \(k_c\ge k+1\).

3.2. Constructing the enlarged profile

Introduce two alternatives \(a,b\), and put \(C=A\cup\{a,b\}\). Prescribe margins as follows:

  • For \(x,y\in A\), use \(w'(x,y)=6w_P(x,y)\).
  • For every \(x\in A\), use \(w'(a,x)=6\).
  • For every \(x\in A\setminus\{c\}\), use \(w'(b,x)=6\).
  • Use \(w'(c,b)=4\) and \(w'(b,a)=2\).

All prescribed margins are even.

Here is an explicit realization. To contribute margin \(2\) on \(x\succ y\) and zero on every other pair, list the remaining alternatives as \(z_1,\ldots,z_{m-2}\) and use the two votes

\(x\succ y\succ z_1\succ\cdots\succ z_{m-2}\),

\(z_{m-2}\succ\cdots\succ z_1\succ x\succ y\).

Every other pair occurs in opposite orientations in these two votes. Repeating this pair of votes realizes any positive even margin; reversing \(x,y\) realizes a negative margin. Combining the gadgets produces a profile \(P'\) with the prescribed margins.

For any ranking \(W\) of \(C\), let \(V=W|_A\). The same pairwise calculation as above gives

\(\operatorname{Kendall}(P',W)=B'+6F(V)+h(W)\),

where \(B'\) is independent of \(W\), and

\(h(W)=6\mathbf1[c\succ_W a]+4\mathbf1[b\succ_W c]+2\mathbf1[a\succ_W b]+6\sum_{x\in A\setminus\{c\}}\bigl(\mathbf1[x\succ_W a]+\mathbf1[x\succ_W b]\bigr)\).

3.3. Computing the constrained minima

Let \(s_d\) be the minimum Kendall score among rankings starting with \(d\).

Rankings starting with \(b\). The edge \(c\succ b\) is necessarily reversed, so \(h(W)\ge4\). The ranking \(b\succ a\succ V\), with \(F(V)=k\), attains \(h(W)=4\). Hence

\(s_b=B'+6k+4\).

Rankings starting with \(a\). There are three exhaustive cases.

  • If \(b\succ_W c\), both \(b\succ a\) and \(c\succ b\) are reversed. Thus \(h(W)\ge6\).
  • If \(c\succ_W b\) and some \(x\in A\setminus\{c\}\) precedes \(b\), the reversed edges \(b\succ a\) and \(b\succ x\) give \(h(W)\ge8\).
  • Otherwise, the ranking begins \(a\succ c\succ b\); its restriction to \(A\) starts with \(c\), and \(h(W)=2\).

The first case attains \(B'+6k+6\) using \(a\succ b\succ V\) with \(F(V)=k\). The third attains \(B'+6k_c+2\). The second cannot improve on the first. Therefore

\(s_a=B'+\min\{6k+6,\;6k_c+2\}\).

Rankings starting with an original alternative. If the first alternative is \(c\), the edge \(a\succ c\) is reversed, contributing \(6\). If it is \(x\ne c\), both \(a\succ x\) and \(b\succ x\) are reversed, contributing \(12\). Thus, for every \(x\in A\),

\(s_x\ge B'+6k+6\).

It follows that:

  • If \(k_c=k\), then \(s_a=B'+6k+2\), strictly below every other constrained minimum.
  • If \(k_c\ge k+1\), then \(s_a=B'+6k+6\), while \(s_b=B'+6k+4\), strictly below every other constrained minimum.

Thus \(a\) uniquely minimizes the constrained score on yes-instances, and \(b\) uniquely minimizes it on no-instances.

3.4. Turning the score gap into a strict posterior gap

Fix a positive integer \(s_\varphi\) satisfying \(\varphi^{s_\varphi}<1/2\). It is a constant because \(\varphi\) is fixed. Set

\(q=s_\varphi m^2\).

For \(m\ge2\),

\(\varphi^q<2^{-m^2}<1/m!\).

Consider posterior weights \(\varphi^{q\operatorname{Kendall}(P',W)}\). If \(s_d<s_e\), the integer-valued scores give \(s_e\ge s_d+1\). Therefore

\(Z_d\ge\varphi^{qs_d}\),

whereas

\(Z_e\le(m-1)!\varphi^{qs_e}\le(m-1)!\varphi^{q(s_d+1)}<\varphi^{qs_d}\).

Hence \(Z_d>Z_e\), with a strict, exact inequality.

By Section 1, the resulting instance has unique minimizing set \(\{a\}\) if \(c\) is a Kemeny winner, and unique minimizing set \(\{b\}\) otherwise.

4. Support size, mass encoding, and reduction size

The realization above has at most two ranking types per unordered pair, before merging duplicates. Its vote count \(N_0\) is even and satisfies

\(N_0=O(m^2(n+1))\).

To obtain dyadic masses, let \(N\) be the smallest power of two at least \(N_0\). Add \((N-N_0)/2\) copies of any ranking and its reverse. Such a pair contributes zero margin on every pair of alternatives. Thus all score comparisons proved above remain valid.

Aggregate this padded profile, with multiplicities \(n_V\), and set

\(\mu_V=n_V/N,\qquad \rho=qN\).

Then

\(\rho\sum_V\mu_V\operatorname{Kendall}(V,W)=q\operatorname{Kendall}(P',W)\),

where \(P'\) now denotes the padded profile.

The resulting instances satisfy

\(\tau\le m(m-1)+2,\qquad \rho=O_\varphi(m^4(n+1))\).

Every mass is positive and dyadic. The masses use \(O(\log(m(n+1)))\) bits each, and \(\rho\) uses \(O_\varphi(\log(m(n+1)))\) bits. With alternatives encoded by integers, total output length is

\(L=O_\varphi\bigl(m^3\log m+m^2\log(n+1)\bigr)\).

The reduction runs in polynomial time in the explicit source input length. It does not require expanding \(\rho\) copies of the electorate.

The same constructed society gives:

  • Optimality: \(\{a\}\) minimizes risk exactly on yes-instances.
  • Comparison: \(R(\{a\})\le R(\{b\})\) exactly on yes-instances.
  • Search: every correct output is \(\{a\}\) on yes-instances and \(\{b\}\) on no-instances.

This proves both \(\Theta_2^p\)-hardness claims and a polynomial reduction from Kemeny Winner using one call to the search problem. In particular, search is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard.

5. An exact upper bound that accounts for the encoding of \(\rho\)

This section supplies a bit-complexity upper bound, without assuming constant-time operations on algebraic numbers.

Write

\(\rho=a/b,\qquad \mu_i=c_i/d_i\),

with positive integer numerators and denominators. Define

\(H=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}d_i,\qquad Q=bH,\qquad t_i=ac_iH/d_i\).

These are integers, and

\(\rho\sum_i\mu_i\operatorname{Kendall}(V_i,W)=\dfrac{\sum_i t_i\operatorname{Kendall}(V_i,W)}{Q}\).

Because the masses sum to one, \(\sum_i t_i=aH\). Thus every numerator exponent is bounded by

\(E=\binom m2 aH\).

Set \(\alpha=\varphi^{1/Q}\). Each unnormalized weight is now an integer power of \(\alpha\).

5.1. Polynomial-valued subset dynamic programming

For distinct alternatives \(x,y\), define

\(e_{xy}=\sum_{i:\,y\succ_{V_i}x}t_i\).

When a ranking puts \(x\) above \(y\), this is its exponent contribution from that pair.

For each \(A\subseteq C\), let \(F_A(X)\) enumerate rankings of \(A\) by their numerator exponent. Then

\(F_\varnothing(X)=1\),

\(F_A(X)=\sum_{x\in A}X^{\sum_{y\in A\setminus\{x\}}e_{xy}}F_{A\setminus\{x\}}(X)\).

This recurrence partitions rankings according to their first alternative. Every pair contribution is included exactly once, when its earlier alternative is selected.

The polynomial for rankings of \(C\) starting with \(x\) is

\(T_x(X)=X^{\sum_{y\ne x}e_{xy}}F_{C\setminus\{x\}}(X)\),

and \(Z_x=T_x(\alpha)\).

All these polynomials have degree at most \(E\). Their coefficients count rankings, so each coefficient is at most \(m!\) and uses \(O(m\log(m+1))\) bits.

Computing the pair contributions and the dense polynomial dynamic program takes

\(\tau m^2\operatorname{poly}(L)+m2^m(E+1)\operatorname{poly}(m,L)\)

bit operations.

5.2. Exact signs and equality

Write the fixed rational dispersion as \(\varphi=u/v\). Then \(\alpha\) is the unique root in \((0,1)\) of

\(vX^Q-u\).

The endpoints are not roots, and the polynomial has exactly one positive root because it is strictly increasing on positive arguments.

Standard univariate algebraic sign algorithms determine the sign of an integer polynomial at an isolated real algebraic root in time polynomial in the degrees and coefficient bit lengths; they also detect zero exactly. See the univariate sign-determination discussion in [Basu’s survey](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final.pdf).

Applying these algorithms to differences \(T_x-T_y\) finds a maximizing alternative. Applying them to

\(|D_2|\sum_{x\in D_1}T_x-|D_1|\sum_{x\in D_2}T_x\)

decides the comparison problem. Section 1 then decides optimality or returns a minimizing singleton. Thus ties are handled exactly.

A common bound for all three tasks is

\(2^m\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,Q,E)\).

The binary encodings give \(Q\le2^{O(L)}\) and \(E\le m^2\,2^{O(L)}\). Consequently, this is

\(2^{O(m+L)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

Both the numerator and denominator of \(\rho\) enter these bounds through \(aH\) and \(bH\). Clearing denominators therefore provides an exact algorithm, but not a polynomial-time algorithm in the binary input length.
