The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given resident types \(T\), hospitals \(H\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), strict type rankings \(\succ_t\) over acceptable hospitals, strict hospital rankings \(\succ_h\) over types, and rational quotas \(\ell_h,u_h\), choose assignment masses \(y_{t,h}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_h y_{t,h}\le\mu_t\) and \(\ell_h\le\sum_t y_{t,h}\le u_h\). Define unmatched mass by \(y_{t,\bot}=\mu_t-\sum_h y_{t,h}\). Require that no positive masses \(y_{t,g},y_{t',h}\), for \(g\in H\cup\{\bot\}\), satisfy \(h\succ_t g\) and \(t\succ_h t'\); maximize \(\sum_{t,h}y_{t,h}\).

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) resident types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(H\) contain \(m\) hospitals, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Mutual acceptability is specified by \(E\subseteq T\times H\). Each type strictly ranks its acceptable hospitals, of which there are at most two; each hospital strictly ranks its acceptable types. Every acceptable hospital is preferred to being unmatched, denoted \(\bot\).
Each hospital has binary rational quotas \(0\leq\ell_h\leq u_h\). Assignment masses are real numbers \(y_{t,h}\geq0\), zero outside \(E\), satisfying \(\sum_{h\in H}y_{t,h}\leq\mu_t\) and \(\ell_h\leq\sum_{t\in T}y_{t,h}\leq u_h\). Define \(y_{t,\bot}=\mu_t-\sum_{h\in H}y_{t,h}\). Promise that such an assignment exists when preferences are ignored.
An assignment is envy-free if there are no \((t,h),(t',h)\in E\) and \(g\in H\cup\{\bot\}\) with \(y_{t,g}>0\), \(y_{t',h}>0\), \(h\succ_t g\), and \(t\succ_h t'\). Hospital rankings compare types only; members of the same type cannot have justified envy toward one another.
Given a binary rational threshold \(b\in[0,1]\), is deciding whether an envy-free assignment has total assigned mass \(\sum_{(t,h)\in E}y_{t,h}\geq b\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when all types have equal masses and \(b=1\). Consequently, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The key fact is that an envy-free fractional assignment can be rounded, after clearing denominators, while preserving feasibility and without decreasing its assigned mass.

1. Envy-freeness is preserved by restricting support

Let
\[ \]
\(\mathcal A=E\cup\{(t,\bot):t\in T\}\).
For an assignment \(y\), define its extended support by
\(\operatorname{supp}(y)=\{(t,g)\in\mathcal A:y_{t,g}>0\}\).

Lemma 1. If \(y\) is envy-free and \(z\) is a feasible assignment satisfying
\(\operatorname{supp}(z)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(y)\),
then \(z\) is envy-free.

Proof. Suppose \(z\) has an envy witness \(t,t',h,g\). Then
\(z_{t,g}>0\), \(z_{t',h}>0\), \(h\succ_t g\), and \(t\succ_h t'\).
Support containment gives \(y_{t,g}>0\) and \(y_{t',h}>0\). The same preferences therefore give an envy witness for \(y\), a contradiction. \(\square\)

The support here includes unmatched coordinates. In particular, if \(y_{t,\bot}=0\), support containment requires \(z_{t,\bot}=0\).

2. Integral rounding on a fixed support

Temporarily consider the same assignment problem with positive integer type masses \(a_t\) and integer hospital quotas \(p_h,q_h\). Write \(A=\sum_t a_t\); normalization to total mass \(1\) is unnecessary for this lemma.

Lemma 2. Given any feasible envy-free real assignment \(x\), there exists a feasible envy-free integer assignment \(z\) such that
\(\operatorname{supp}(z)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x)\)
and
\(\sum_{(t,h)\in E}z_{t,h}\geq\sum_{(t,h)\in E}x_{t,h}\).

Proof. Set \(S=\operatorname{supp}(x)\). Construct a minimum-cost circulation network with vertices \(s,r,\bot\), the types, and the hospitals:

| Arc | Lower and upper capacities | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| \(s\to t\) | \([a_t,a_t]\) | \(0\) |
| \(t\to h\), for \((t,h)\in S\cap E\) | \([0,a_t]\) | \(-1\) |
| \(t\to\bot\), for \((t,\bot)\in S\) | \([0,a_t]\) | \(0\) |
| \(h\to r\) | \([p_h,q_h]\) | \(0\) |
| \(\bot\to r\) | \([0,A]\) | \(0\) |
| \(r\to s\) | \([A,A]\) | \(0\) |

Flow conservation at each type requires its outgoing assignment and unmatched masses to sum to \(a_t\). Conservation at hospitals, together with their outgoing capacities, enforces their quotas. Thus feasible circulations correspond exactly to feasible assignments whose extended support is contained in \(S\). The assignment \(x\) supplies a feasible circulation.

The integral minimum-cost circulation theorem states that a feasible network with integer lower capacities, upper capacities, and costs has an integral optimum when its objective is bounded. All these hypotheses hold: the data are integers and every arc has finite capacity. Equivalently, subtracting the lower capacities leaves integer node balances and a totally unimodular directed incidence matrix, which gives integral vertices.

The circulation cost is precisely
\(-\sum_{(t,h)\in E}z_{t,h}\).
An integral optimum therefore has assigned mass at least that of \(x\). Its support is contained in \(S\), so Lemma 1 establishes envy-freeness. \(\square\)

This lemma does not require the bound of two acceptable hospitals.

3. Polynomial reduction from the discrete problem

We use the following known hardness result: deciding whether a discrete hospital–resident instance has a feasible envy-free matching covering every resident is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard with strict preferences and at most two acceptable hospitals per resident. This is the full-size threshold used in Lemma 11 and Theorem 4(I)(a) of [Krishnaa et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.07159v3#S2.SS1). Their instances admit a feasible matching when preferences are ignored, as required by the present promise.

Take such a discrete instance with \(n\) residents and integer quotas \(p_h,q_h\). Create one distinct type \(t_r\) for each resident \(r\), and set

\(\tau=n,\qquad \mu_{t_r}=1/n,\qquad \ell_h=p_h/n,\qquad u_h=q_h/n,\qquad b=1.\)

Keep all acceptability relations and strict rankings unchanged. Each type has at most two acceptable hospitals. The masses are positive binary-encoded rationals summing to \(1\). Scaling a preference-ignoring feasible matching by \(1/n\) verifies the feasibility promise.

If the discrete instance has a feasible envy-free matching covering every resident, its scaled assignment is feasible, envy-free, and has assigned mass \(1\).

Conversely, suppose the continuous instance has a feasible envy-free assignment of assigned mass at least \(1\). Its assigned mass must equal \(1\), since the total type mass is \(1\). Multiply all coordinates by \(n\). The resulting assignment has integer type masses \(a_t=1\), integer quotas \(p_h,q_h\), and assigned mass \(n\).

Apply Lemma 2. The resulting integral assignment has assigned mass at least \(n\), hence exactly \(n\). Each type has total mass \(1\), so its integral assignment places that unit at exactly one hospital. It therefore defines a discrete matching covering every resident. Feasibility and envy-freeness follow from the lemma.

Thus the reduction preserves yes and no answers. Distinct residents become distinct types, so the exemption concerning members of the same type introduces no additional possibilities.

The transformation takes polynomial time. If the discrete input has bit length \(L_{\mathrm d}\), the resulting bit length is
\(O(L_{\mathrm d}+(\tau+m)\log(\tau+1))\).

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators appearing in the input masses and hospital quotas. Then

\(D\mu_t,\quad D\ell_h,\quad Du_h\)

are integers, and \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits: the bit length of a product is bounded by the sum of its factors’ bit lengths.

Suppose a yes-instance has a real envy-free assignment \(y\) meeting threshold \(b\). Apply Lemma 2 to \(Dy\), then divide the resulting integral assignment by \(D\). This gives an envy-free assignment meeting the threshold whose acceptable-edge coordinates are

\(z_{t,h}=k_{t,h}/D,\qquad k_{t,h}\in\{0,\ldots,D\}.\)

Because \(|E|\leq2\tau\), these numerators form a certificate of \(O(\tau L)\) bits. The unmatched coordinates are determined by the type masses and these numerators.

A verifier checks nonnegativity, type constraints, quotas, the threshold, and every possible envy witness using exact rational arithmetic. There are \(O(\tau^2)\) potential envy witnesses under the degree bound: for each acceptable pair \((t,h)\), there are at most two worse destinations and at most \(\tau\) other acceptable types at \(h\). Verification therefore takes
\(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\)
bit time.

Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

5. An exact running-time bound

A deterministic exact algorithm runs in

\(2^{3\tau}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\).

Indeed, \(|\mathcal A|=|E|+\tau\leq3\tau\). Enumerate every subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal A\). Reject \(S\) if it contains two coordinates \((t,g)\) and \((t',h)\) that would constitute an envy witness whenever both were positive. For every remaining \(S\), solve the support-restricted minimum-cost circulation from Lemma 2 after scaling by \(D\), and check its optimum against \(Db\).

Every assignment supported on a remaining \(S\) is envy-free. Conversely, the extended support of every envy-free assignment appears among the remaining subsets. Hence the algorithm is exact.

Each network has \(O(m+\tau)\) vertices and arcs, with numerical data of \(O(L)\) bits. Polynomial-time minimum-cost circulation therefore gives the stated bound, without expanding binary-encoded masses into individual residents.
