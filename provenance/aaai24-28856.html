The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) rational-mass types with finite rational action and payoff descriptions, all copies of each type constrained to one unknown coalition block \(\Pi\), adaptively design factorized normal-form games and default type-action profiles, receive one bit indicating whether the profile is a Nash equilibrium under coalition aggregate payoff \(\sum_{t\in B}\mu_tu_t\), and recover \(\Pi\) with minimum worst-case queries.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) labeled types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. An unknown partition \(\Pi\) assigns each entire type to one nonempty coalition; singleton coalitions are allowed, and the number of coalitions is unknown.
Each query freely specifies finitely many explicitly tabulated normal-form factors with nonempty finite action sets and arbitrary signed rational payoffs. A type's action is a tuple of its factor actions, its payoff \(u_t\) is the sum of its factor payoffs, and the query specifies a pure default profile \(a\). All mass of a type uses the same action, including during deviations.
The oracle returns one exactly when no block \(B\in\Pi\) can strictly increase \(\sum_{t\in B}\mu_tu_t(a)\) by jointly changing the actions of any or all of its types, holding other blocks fixed. Equal-payoff deviations do not invalidate equilibrium. Queries may depend on previous oracle answers; oracle calls have unit cost.
Let \(Q(\mu)\) be the minimum worst-case query count of a deterministic adaptive strategy that identifies every possible \(\Pi\) exactly. The computational input is the mass list and a binary integer budget \(q\geq0\). Let \(L\) be the input bit length; when describing query construction, let \(m\) be the maximum factor action-set size, \(k\) the number of factors, and \(L_G\) their total binary description length. State running times in these quantities and \(\tau\).
Is deciding whether \(Q(\mu)\leq q\) polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For discrete agents, Iterative Grouping uses at most \(n\log_2 n+3n\) queries, while every exact single-bit learner needs at least \(\lceil\log_2 B_n\rceil=n\log_2 n-O(n\log_2\log_2 n)\), where \(B_n\) is the Bell number. These bounds concern query counts rather than computing the exact minimum. [Xu, Ling, and Fang](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09058v2).
No result settling this precise mass-based, minimum-query problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to total mass one, following the distribution interpretation.
  • Interpreted factorization as a product of explicitly tabulated normal-form games, with Cartesian-product action sets and additive payoffs.
  • Treated action sets and payoff tables as freely designed for each query, rather than immutable characteristics restricting the learner.
  • Used pure default profiles as specified by the graded type-action question, and required uniform actions within each type during defaults and deviations.
  • Included every joint deviation by a coalition, allowing any subset of its types to change actions; only strict payoff improvements invalidate equilibrium.
  • Made exact minimum worst-case queries precise using deterministic strategies, worst case over all partitions, and the binary-budget decision problem.
  • Separated unit-cost oracle queries from query-description construction costs and defined the relevant representation parameters.

1. Definitions and removal of the masses

Let
$$ T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}, $$
and let $\mathcal P_\tau$ be the set of partitions of $T$. Write $B_\tau=|\mathcal P_\tau|$ for the Bell number.

A coalition is profitable in a query if it has a permitted deviation whose mass-weighted payoff gain is strictly positive.

Consider two positive normalized rational mass vectors $\mu$ and $\nu$ on the same labeled support. Given a query for $\mu$, replace every factor payoff by
$$ u'_{t,f}=\frac{\mu_t}{\nu_t}u_{t,f}. $$
Keep all action sets and the default profile unchanged. For every coalition $C$ and every permitted deviation,
$$ \sum_{t\in C}\nu_t \bigl(u'_t(a'_C,a_{-C})-u'_t(a)\bigr) = \sum_{t\in C}\mu_t \bigl(u_t(a'_C,a_{-C})-u_t(a)\bigr). $$
Thus the transformation preserves strict profitability, including deviations in which some coalition members retain their default actions. It consequently preserves the oracle answer for every partition.

Applying this transformation at every node of an adaptive strategy preserves its answer paths and its query count. Transforming in both directions proves
$$ Q(\mu)=Q(\nu). $$
Relabeling the support also preserves the optimum. Hence there is a sequence $Q_\tau$, depending only on the support size, such that
$$ Q(\mu)=Q_\tau. $$

Positivity of all masses is necessary for this argument: it ensures that every multiplier is defined.

Running time. Rescaling preserves the number $k$ of factors and the maximum action-set size $m$. It takes time polynomial in $\tau,L,L_G$ and the encoding length of $\nu$, by exact rational arithmetic. No expansion of the product action space is needed.

2. Exact characterization of the available tests

2.1 Every query is a forbidden-block test

For a query $G$, define
$$ \mathcal F_G= \left\{ C\subseteq T: C\ne\varnothing \text{ and }C\text{ is profitable in }G \right\}. $$
Whether $C$ is profitable depends on its members, the query, and the default actions outside $C$. It does not depend on the partition of those outside types.

The oracle therefore answers one exactly when
$$ \Pi\cap\mathcal F_G=\varnothing. $$

2.2 Every forbidden-block family is realizable

Fix a nonempty subset $B\subseteq T$, and choose $r\in B$. Construct one factor in which $r$ has two actions, default and toggle, while every other type has one action.

All payoffs at the default profile are zero. At the toggle profile, set
$$ \mu_tu_t= \begin{cases} \frac32-|B|,&t=r,\\ 1,&t\in B\setminus\{r\},\\ -1,&t\notin B. \end{cases} $$
These are rational payoffs because every $\mu_t$ is positive and rational.

A coalition not containing $r$ cannot change this factor’s profile. If $r\in C$, its gain from toggling is
$$ \begin{aligned} \frac32-|B| +|C\cap(B\setminus\{r\})| -|C\setminus B| &= \frac12-|B\setminus C|-|C\setminus B|. \end{aligned} $$
For $C=B$, the gain is $\frac12$. For every other $C$ containing $r$, at least one of the two set differences is nonempty, so the gain is at most $-\frac12$. Thus this factor makes exactly $B$ profitable.

For an arbitrary family
$$ \mathcal F\subseteq 2^T\setminus\{\varnothing\}, $$
include one such factor for every $B\in\mathcal F$.

Factor actions can be chosen independently, and factor gains add. If a coalition has a positive-gain deviation in one factor, it can change only that factor. Conversely, a positive total gain must include a positive gain in at least one factor. Therefore the combined query has forbidden family exactly $\mathcal F$.

An all-zero factor implements the empty family.

Consequently, the available tests are exactly
$$ \Pi\longmapsto \mathbf 1[\Pi\cap\mathcal F=\varnothing], \qquad \mathcal F\subseteq 2^T\setminus\{\varnothing\}. $$

Construction costs. For nonempty $\mathcal F$, this construction has
$$ k=|\mathcal F|,\qquad m=2, $$
and only two profiles per factor. Put
$$ s=L+\log_2(\tau+1). $$
The description length is $O(k\tau s)$, and a conservative construction-time bound using elementary exact rational arithmetic is $O(k\tau s^3)$. The model permits $k$ to be exponential in $\tau$.

For completeness, the forbidden family of an arbitrary supplied query can also be computed by finite enumeration. For each factor, enumerate all nonempty coalitions and their factor deviations. The number of coalition-action pairs is at most
$$ \prod_{t\in T}(1+|A_{t,f}|) \le (m+1)^\tau. $$
The total time is therefore
$$ O\!\left(k(m+1)^\tau \operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,L_G)\right). $$
Checking factors separately suffices because a coalition can retain the default in every other factor, and a positive total gain requires some positive factor gain.

2.3 Tests on a restriction of the partition

The following extension will be needed for the six-type construction.

For $S\subseteq T$, define the restricted partition
$$ \Pi|_S= \{C\cap S:C\in\Pi,\ C\cap S\ne\varnothing\}. $$
Its members are nonempty, disjoint, and cover $S$.

Fix $\varnothing\ne B\subseteq S$ and $r\in B$. Modify the preceding factor by assigning mass-weighted toggle payoffs
$$ \mu_tu_t= \begin{cases} \frac32-|B|,&t=r,\\ 1,&t\in B\setminus\{r\},\\ -1,&t\in S\setminus B,\\ 0,&t\notin S. \end{cases} $$
For a coalition containing $r$, the gain is
$$ \frac12-|B\setminus C|-|(C\cap S)\setminus B|. $$
This is positive exactly when $C\cap S=B$. Coalitions excluding $r$ cannot change the profile.

Combining these factors realizes every test
$$ \mathbf 1[(\Pi|_S)\cap\mathcal F=\varnothing] $$
for a family of nonempty subsets of $S$, with the same construction bounds as above.

3. General lower and upper bounds

3.1 Information lower bound

A deterministic strategy using at most $q$ queries has at most $2^q$ answer leaves. Exact identification requires a distinct leaf for every partition. Hence
$$ Q_\tau\ge \left\lceil\log_2 B_\tau\right\rceil. $$

3.2 Pair tests and insertion

For distinct types $i,j$, give only $i$ a toggle action. At toggle, assign mass-weighted payoffs $-1$ to $i$, $2$ to $j$, and zero to every other type.

A coalition containing $i$ gains
$$ -1+2\mathbf 1[j\in C]. $$
Thus the oracle answers zero exactly when $i$ and $j$ belong to the same block. Testing all pairs gives
$$ Q_\tau\le h_\tau:=\binom{\tau}{2}. $$

A stronger bound follows by insertion. Suppose the restriction to the first $i-1$ types has been identified and has $r$ blocks. Choose one representative from each block.

Type $i$ either joins one of those $r$ represented blocks or belongs to a block containing no earlier type. There are $r+1$ possibilities. Assign them distinct binary codes of length
$$ c=\left\lceil\log_2(r+1)\right\rceil, $$
using the zero code for the last possibility.

For each bit, combine pair factors between $i$ and the representatives whose codes have that bit set. The oracle answers zero exactly when the correct representative has that bit. Indeed, the representatives belong to distinct actual blocks, so at most one can share a block with $i$.

The $c$ answers therefore identify the correct possibility. Induction gives
$$ Q_\tau\le U_\tau:= \sum_{i=2}^{\tau}\left\lceil\log_2 i\right\rceil \le (\tau-1)\left\lceil\log_2\tau\right\rceil. $$
Each insertion query has $m=2$ and at most $\tau-1$ factors. The whole strategy can be constructed and executed, excluding unit-cost oracle calls, in time polynomial in $\tau,L$.

5. Complexity consequences and the repaired sparse reduction

Let $A$ be the language of valid mass-list and budget encodings satisfying $Q(\mu)\le q$. Reject malformed encodings. Validity, including positivity and exact normalization, can be checked in polynomial time by rational arithmetic.

Here, NP-hardness means hardness under deterministic polynomial-time many-one reductions.

5.1 Reduction to a sparse language

Define
$$ S= \{\mathtt{YES}\} \cup \left\{ 1^\tau\#\operatorname{bin}(q): \tau\ge1,\ 0\le q<h_\tau,\ Q_\tau\le q \right\}, $$
where binary integer encodings are canonical.

Given a valid instance of $A$:

  • if $q\ge h_\tau$, output $\mathtt{YES}$;
  • otherwise, output $1^\tau\#\operatorname{bin}(q)$.

Malformed instances map to a fixed string outside $S$.

The pair-test bound and mass invariance prove that this reduction preserves membership. Its output length is polynomial because the explicit mass list implies $\tau\le L$.

A language is sparse if its number of members of length at most $N$ is bounded by a polynomial in $N$. Here that number is at most
$$ 1+\sum_{\tau=1}^{N}h_\tau=O(N^3). $$
Thus $S$ is sparse.

Mahaney’s theorem states that a sparse language cannot be NP-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions unless $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$. The sparse language need not itself belong to $\mathsf{NP}$. This formulation is given in [Grochow’s exposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05825).

If $A$ were NP-hard, composing its hardness reductions with the reduction to $S$ would make $S$ NP-hard. The theorem therefore applies and yields
$$ A\text{ is NP-hard}\Longrightarrow\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}. $$

5.2 Reverse reduction, including out-of-range budgets

The reverse reduction must treat the budget restriction as part of membership in $S$.

Map $\mathtt{YES}$ to the one-type instance with mass $1$ and budget zero. Map every string failing the required syntax, or satisfying $q\ge h_\tau$, to the two-type instance with masses $\frac12,\frac12$ and budget zero. This is a no-instance because there are two possible partitions.

For a remaining string $1^\tau\#\operatorname{bin}(q)$, put
$$ d=\left\lceil\log_2\tau\right\rceil $$
and construct
$$ \mu_t=2^{-d}\quad(1\le t<\tau),\qquad \mu_\tau=\frac{2^d-\tau+1}{2^d}. $$
All masses are positive, since $2^d\ge\tau$, and
$$ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t = \frac{\tau-1+2^d-\tau+1}{2^d}=1. $$
They are dyadic rationals with total description length $O(\tau\log_2(\tau+1))$. Mass invariance proves that the resulting instance is in $A$ exactly when the original string is in $S$.

Thus $A$ and $S$ are polynomial-time many-one equivalent. In particular, syntactically valid strings with $q\ge h_\tau$ map to no-instances, repairing the issue identified by the verifier.

5.3 Exact conditional hardness statement

Conversely, if $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$, satisfiability can be decided in polynomial time. Decide a satisfiability instance and output either the fixed yes-instance or the fixed no-instance above. This gives a polynomial-time many-one reduction from satisfiability to $A$.

Hence
$$ \boxed{ A\text{ is NP-hard} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}. } $$
This equivalence does not determine whether $A$ belongs to $\mathsf P$.

5.4 Polynomial-size advice

There is also an unconditional nonuniform upper bound:
$$ A\in\mathsf P/\mathsf{poly}. $$
For inputs of length $N$, provide the advice list
$$ Q_1,Q_2,\ldots,Q_N. $$
The bound $Q_\tau\le U_\tau$ shows that each entry needs $O(\log_2(\tau+1))$ bits, so the whole list has length $O(N\log_2(N+1))$.

After validating an input, compute $\tau$ and compare its budget with the advised value $Q_\tau$. This takes polynomial time. The missing ingredient for a uniform polynomial-time algorithm is computing the advice values uniformly.

6. A smaller exhaustive-search bound

The query characterization permits exact computation without enumerating all $2^{B_\tau}$ candidate families.

Let
$$ N_\tau=2^\tau-1,\qquad M_\tau=2^{N_\tau}. $$
There are $N_\tau$ nonempty possible blocks and $M_\tau$ forbidden-block families.

A query history consists of forbidden families
$$ \mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_\ell $$
and their answers. Let $H$ denote the partitions consistent with this history.

Define a recursive predicate $E(H,d)$, meaning that $H$ can be identified with at most $d$ additional queries:

  • If $|H|\le1$, return true.
  • If $|H|>1$ and $d=0$, return false.
  • Otherwise enumerate every forbidden family $\mathcal F$. Let

$$ H_1=\{\Pi\in H:\Pi\cap\mathcal F=\varnothing\}, \qquad H_0=H\setminus H_1. $$
Return true if, for some $\mathcal F$, both $E(H_1,d-1)$ and $E(H_0,d-1)$ are true.

This recursion is correct by induction on $d$. A successful choice supplies the root query and successful strategies for both branches. Conversely, the root query of any successful strategy has a forbidden family included in the enumeration, and its two continuations must satisfy the recursive conditions.

There is no need to store $H$ explicitly. Enumerate all partitions and check them against the stored history. Partition enumeration uses polynomial space in $\tau$: for example, assign each type its block’s least element, giving an injective representation among $\tau^\tau$ strings. Thus
$$ B_\tau\le\tau^\tau. $$

If $q\ge U_\tau$, answer yes immediately. Otherwise the recursive depth is at most $U_\tau$. At depth $d$, each forbidden family causes at most two calls of depth $d-1$. The work per call, apart from recursion, is bounded by
$$ 2^{O(\tau\log_2(\tau+1))} \operatorname{poly}(U_\tau). $$
This includes partition enumeration, history checking, and enumeration overhead.

The resulting deterministic running-time bound is
$$ \boxed{ 2^{O\!\left(2^\tau\,\tau\log_2(\tau+1)\right)} +\operatorname{poly}(L). } $$
A depth-first implementation stores at most $U_\tau$ forbidden-family bit vectors, each of length $N_\tau$, together with loop counters and polynomial-size partition-enumeration state. Its space bound is
$$ \boxed{ O\!\left(2^\tau\,\tau\log_2(\tau+1)\right) +\operatorname{poly}(L). } $$

These bounds prove uniform computability, but remain far from polynomial time.

7. What remains open

The numerical masses cannot encode an instance-specific source of hardness in this model: arbitrary rational payoff rescaling removes them exactly. The unresolved problem is the uniform computation of the sequence $Q_\tau$.

The finite constructions prove that the Bell-number lower bound is attained for support sizes five and six. They do not establish its attainability for general support size, nor a polynomial-time algorithm for the threshold decision problem.
