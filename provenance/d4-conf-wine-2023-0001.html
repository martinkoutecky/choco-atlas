The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a total seller mass \(N\), budget \(B\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) truthful types \((v_t,c_t)\), does there exist a deterministic direct mechanism, evaluable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the input bit length, that assigns each infinitesimal seller with public value \(v\), true cost \(c\), and report \(b\) an allocation \(x(v,b;\nu)\in[0,1]\) and payment \(p(v,b;\nu)\), where \(\nu\) is the fixed distribution of other sellers’ reports? Require dominant-strategy truthfulness and individual rationality for each seller, aggregate payments at most \(B\) for every report profile, and truthful aggregate value at least half of \(\max\{N\sum_t\mu_t v_t x_t: N\sum_t\mu_t c_t x_t\le B,\ x_t\in[0,1]\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An atomless seller society has total mass \(N>0\), budget \(B>0\), public values \(v_t\geq0\), and private unit costs \(0<c_t\leq B\); all input numbers are binary rationals.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Write its normalized distribution as \(\mu\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and let \(m\) count distinct public values.
Each seller may report any rational cost \(b\in[0,B]\). Admissible report distributions \(\nu\) have finite rational support and the same public-value marginal as the society; sellers of one truthful type may submit different reports.
A deterministic direct mechanism returns allocation and payment densities \(x(v,b;\nu)\in[0,1]\) and \(p(v,b;\nu)\geq0\), with utility density \(p-cx\). An individual seller's deviation leaves \(\nu\) fixed. The mechanism receives public information and reported costs, without separate access to truthful costs.
Require dominant-strategy truthfulness and individual rationality at truthful reporting for every seller and every admissible distribution of others' reports, and \(N\sum_{(v,b)\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)}\nu(v,b)p(v,b;\nu)\leq B\) for every admissible report distribution.
Define \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=\max\{N\sum_t\mu_t v_t y_t:N\sum_t\mu_t c_t y_t\leq B,\ 0\leq y_t\leq1\}\). Let \(L\) be the total binary encoding length of the public data, distribution being evaluated, and queried report; running times are measured in \(m,\tau,L\).

Does a uniform deterministic direct mechanism satisfying these requirements exist, evaluable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(N\sum_t\mu_t v_t x(v_t,c_t;\mu)\geq\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)/2\) for every admissible truthful society, or is this guarantee unattainable by such a mechanism?

For finitely many sellers with fractional allocations, linear values, and each full-service cost bounded by the budget, a polynomial-time deterministic truthful, individually rational, budget-feasible \(2\)-approximation exists ([Amanatidis et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.07385v3)).
For finite sellers under an asymptotic small-bidder assumption, the optimal approximation ratio is \(e/(e-1)\) ([Anari et al.](https://afshin-nikzad.com/pdfs/Procurement.pdf)); this concerns a different model. No result settling exactly the rational-support atomless problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored the full-service affordability restriction: truthful unit costs lie in (0,B], and reports lie in [0,B].
  • Specified positive rational total mass and budget, nonnegative rational public values, and normalized positive support weights; the actual mass of type t is N times its weight.
  • Interpreted allocations, payments, and utilities as densities per unit seller mass, with an individual deviation leaving the aggregate report distribution unchanged.
  • Restricted represented report profiles to finite rational-support distributions preserving the public-value marginal, while allowing each truthful type's mass to split among reports.
  • Required one uniform mechanism across all admissible societies, using reported costs rather than separate knowledge of the truthful cost distribution.
  • Used individual rationality at truthful reporting against arbitrary others' reports; the literal requirement of nonnegative utility under every own misreport would conflict with the intended mechanism model.
  • Defined m as the number of distinct public values, tau as the support size of the distribution being evaluated, and L to include the queried report.

A polynomial-time mechanism achieving the guarantee

The guarantee is attainable. We construct a uniform deterministic mechanism and prove truthfulness, individual rationality, budget feasibility, and the required approximation. The construction uses the reported distribution and public data only.

Throughout, write \(w_t=N\nu_t\) for the actual mass of a reported type. Ignore types with public value zero by assigning them allocation and payment zero.

1. Construction

Let \(M=N\sum_{t:v_t>0}\nu_t\) be the total mass with positive public value. This quantity depends only on the public-value marginal.

If \(M\leq1\), assign every positive-value seller allocation \(1\) and payment density \(B\), independently of their report. This branch will be verified below.

Suppose henceforth that \(M>1\). For \(r\geq0\), define

\[ F(r)=\sum_{t:v_t>0}w_t\min\{rv_t,B\}\mathbf{1}_{\{b_t\leq rv_t\}}. \]

Here and below, the mathematics uses the specified inline delimiters; equivalently, the defining expression is \(F(r)=\sum_{t:v_t>0}w_t\min\{rv_t,B\}\mathbf{1}_{\{b_t\leq rv_t\}}\).

The function \(F\) is nondecreasing and right-continuous. Indeed, each summand is zero before \(b_t/v_t\), jumps there if \(b_t>0\), subsequently increases linearly, and becomes constant at \(B/v_t\). The ordering of these two breakpoints follows from \(b_t\leq B\).

Moreover, \(F(0)=0\), and for all sufficiently large \(r\), \(F(r)=BM>B\). Define

\(r_*=\inf\{r\geq0:F(r)\geq B\}\).

This threshold is finite and strictly positive: with \(K=\sum_{t:v_t>0}w_tv_t>0\), we have \(F(r)\leq rK\), so \(r_*\geq B/K>0\).

Define

\(F_-=\sum_{t:v_t>0,\ b_t<r_*v_t}w_t\min\{r_*v_t,B\}\),

and

\(E=\sum_{t:v_t>0,\ b_t=r_*v_t}w_tb_t\).

The first expression is the left limit of \(F\) at \(r_*\), and \(F(r_*)=F_-+E\). Right-continuity and the definition of the first crossing imply

\(F_-\leq B\leq F_-+E\).

Set \(\alpha=(B-F_-)/E\) if \(E>0\), and set \(\alpha=1\) if \(E=0\). Consequently, \(0\leq\alpha\leq1\), and in both cases \(F_-+\alpha E=B\).

For any queried positive public value \(v\) and rational report \(b\in[0,B]\), define the allocation as follows:

  • If \(b<r_*v\), set \(x(v,b;\nu)=1\).
  • If \(b=r_*v\), set \(x(v,b;\nu)=\alpha\).
  • If \(b>r_*v\), set \(x(v,b;\nu)=0\).

Define the payment density by

\(p(v,b;\nu)=\min\{r_*v,B\}\,x(v,b;\nu)\).

These rules also define the mechanism at reports absent from the support of \(\nu\). All fractional allocations are deterministic allocation densities, as permitted in the question.

2. Dominant-strategy truthfulness and individual rationality

Fix any admissible distribution \(\nu\), any public value \(v\), and any true cost \(c\in(0,B]\). By atomlessness, an individual deviation leaves \(\nu\), and hence \(r_*\) and \(\alpha\), fixed. We therefore compare reports within one fixed menu.

The zero-value menu always gives utility zero. In the branch \(M\leq1\), every report by a positive-value seller gives utility \(B-c\geq0\), so truthfulness and individual rationality hold.

Consider the branch \(M>1\), and put \(s=r_*v\).

If \(s>B\), every admissible report satisfies \(b<s\). Every report therefore gives allocation \(1\), payment \(B\), and utility \(B-c\geq0\).

Suppose \(s\leq B\). Reports below \(s\) give utility \(s-c\); the report equal to \(s\) gives utility \(\alpha(s-c)\); and reports above \(s\) give utility zero.

There are three cases:

  • If \(c<s\), truthful reporting gives utility \(s-c>0\). Since \(0\leq\alpha\leq1\), neither reporting \(s\) nor reporting above \(s\) improves that utility.
  • If \(c=s\), every report gives utility zero.
  • If \(c>s\), truthful reporting gives utility zero. Reporting below \(s\) gives negative utility, and reporting \(s\) gives utility \(\alpha(s-c)\leq0\).

Thus truthful reporting maximizes utility and gives nonnegative utility for every possible true cost and every fixed admissible distribution. This includes the endpoint \(s=B\).

3. Budget feasibility for every report distribution

If \(M\leq1\), total payments equal \(BM\leq B\).

If \(M>1\), the positive-value support types with \(b_t<r_*v_t\) contribute exactly \(F_-\) to total payments. Types with \(b_t=r_*v_t\) contribute exactly \(\alpha E\), because their offered price satisfies \(r_*v_t=b_t\leq B\). All other types receive zero.

Hence total payments are

\(N\sum_t\nu_t p(v_t,b_t;\nu)=F_-+\alpha E=B\).

Budget feasibility therefore holds for every admissible report distribution, including distributions containing zero reports.

4. The approximation guarantee

Evaluate the mechanism at a truthful society, so \(\nu=\mu\) and \(b_t=c_t\).

If \(M\leq1\), purchasing every positive-value seller fully costs at most \(BM\leq B\). This allocation attains the maximum possible value, so the mechanism attains \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\).

Now suppose \(M>1\). Write \(w_t=N\mu_t\), and define

\(A=\{t:v_t>0,\ c_t<r_*v_t\}\),

\(T=\{t:v_t>0,\ c_t=r_*v_t\}\).

The mechanism’s value is

\(V=\sum_{t\in A}w_tv_t+\alpha\sum_{t\in T}w_tv_t\).

Let \(V_A=\sum_{t\in A}w_tv_t\). Since the mechanism spends exactly \(B\), and each payment density is at most \(r_*v_t\) times the corresponding allocation density,

\(B=\sum_t w_t p(v_t,c_t;\mu)\leq r_*V\).

Consequently, \(B/r_*\leq V\).

Next, take any feasible benchmark allocation \(y\). For every positive-value type outside \(A\), its cost satisfies \(c_t\geq r_*v_t\). Since \(0\leq y_t\leq1\),

\(\sum_t w_tv_ty_t\leq V_A+\sum_{t\notin A,\ v_t>0}w_tv_ty_t\).

Using the cost inequality for these remaining types and then benchmark budget feasibility gives

\(\sum_{t\notin A,\ v_t>0}w_tv_ty_t\leq \frac{1}{r_*}\sum_{t\notin A,\ v_t>0}w_tc_ty_t\leq B/r_*\).

It follows that every feasible benchmark allocation has value at most

\(V_A+B/r_*\leq V+V=2V\).

Taking the maximum over benchmark allocations proves

\(N\sum_t\mu_tv_tx(v_t,c_t;\mu)\geq\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)/2\).

The argument includes ties at the threshold and types whose offered prices are capped at \(B\).

5. Exact polynomial-time evaluation

We finally verify computational uniformity and rational output.

For each positive-value support type, form the rational breakpoints \(b_t/v_t\) and \(B/v_t\), together with zero. There are at most \(2\tau+1\) breakpoints.

Sort these breakpoints. On every open interval between consecutive distinct breakpoints, \(F\) has the affine form \(F(r)=ar+d\), with rational coefficients. Its endpoint values and jumps are also rational.

Scan the intervals and breakpoints in increasing order:

  • If the first crossing occurs at a jump, \(r_*\) is that rational breakpoint.
  • If it occurs inside an affine interval, its slope is positive and \(r_*=(B-d)/a\).
  • If the affine function first reaches \(B\) at an endpoint, that endpoint is \(r_*\).

This scan necessarily finds the crossing, because at the largest breakpoint every positive-value type is active and capped, giving \(F=BM>B\).

The coefficients, threshold, and \(\alpha\) are obtained using sums, products, comparisons, and divisions of input rationals. A direct implementation uses polynomially many such operations; even recomputing the coefficients on each interval takes only polynomial time in \(\tau\). Their binary encoding lengths remain polynomial in \(L\), since they involve sums of at most \(\tau\) products of input rationals and a bounded number of subsequent arithmetic operations.

Once \(r_*\) and \(\alpha\) are computed, a queried allocation and payment require only rational arithmetic and comparison. Thus evaluation is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), and all outputs are exact rationals.

The mechanism uses no separately supplied truthful costs: it performs this construction on whichever admissible report distribution it receives.
