The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of types \(t\), each specifying a strict school ranking and a priority tier at every school, rational masses \(\mu_t\), and rational school capacities, compute a student-proposing mass-SOSM baseline \(b_{t,s_0}\). At each proposal round, schools accept applicants in priority-tier order up to capacity, splitting a cutoff tier proportionally. Then find rational assignment masses \(x_{t,s_0,s}\) minimizing \(\sum_{t,s_0,s} x_{t,s_0,s}(\operatorname{rank}_t(s)-1)\), subject to assigning all mass from each baseline cohort, respecting school capacities, and allowing a cohort to move only to a school it ranks at least as highly as \(s_0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) schools. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each with binary rational mass \(\mu_t>0\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Normalize \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).
Each type specifies a strict complete ranking of \(S\) and a positive integer priority tier at each school; smaller tiers have higher priority. School capacities \(q_s\) are positive binary rationals with \(\sum_s q_s\geq1\). Every school is acceptable. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Define the student-proposing mass baseline by synchronous deferred acceptance: initially all mass proposes to its first choice; subsequently each rejected portion proposes to the school immediately following its rejecting school in its ranking.
Each school reconsiders its held mass together with new proposals, retaining higher tiers first. If a tier has applicant mass \(A\) and remaining capacity \(c<A\), retain the fraction \(c/A\) of every applicant portion in that tier and reject the remainder and all lower tiers. Define \(b_{t,s_0}\) as the terminal held masses, or their limits if the rounds continue indefinitely.
Compute this baseline and minimize \(\sum_{t,s_0,s}x_{t,s_0,s}(\operatorname{rank}_t(s)-1)\) over rational masses \(x_{t,s_0,s}\geq0\), subject to \(\sum_s x_{t,s_0,s}=b_{t,s_0}\), \(\sum_{t,s_0}x_{t,s_0,s}\leq q_s\), and \(x_{t,s_0,s}=0\) whenever \(\operatorname{rank}_t(s)>\operatorname{rank}_t(s_0)\). Priorities impose no additional constraints on reassignment; retaining the baseline is allowed.

Can this baseline-and-minimum-index assignment problem be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete students, every Pareto domination of the student-optimal stable matching is reachable by cycles of weakly beneficial trades, allowing cycles consisting entirely of indifferences; this is a reachability result, not a complexity classification. [Aksoy et al.](https://sinanaksoy.com/publication/aksoy-2015/aksoy-2015.pdf)
Nearby fractional deferred-acceptance work uses water-filling within priority ties and documents nontermination issues; its polynomial algorithm computes a fair stable fractional matching, without establishing equivalence to the proposal limit. These results do not classify the proportional-rationing optimization defined here. [Fairly Stable Two-Sided Matching with Indifferences](https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/comsoc2025/comsoc2025-papers/83.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total society mass to one and measure capacities in the same units.
  • Require nonempty school and type sets, positive capacities, complete rankings, universal acceptability, and sufficient total capacity so that the objective needs no undefined unmatched-school rank.
  • Use synchronous rounds, reconsider incumbents alongside new proposals, and interpret proportional cutoff splitting relative to current applicant mass.
  • Each rejected portion advances from its rejecting school to the immediately following school; rejection of one portion does not remove that school from every portion of its type.
  • Use the limit of held masses when proposal rounds do not terminate finitely.
  • Allow the unchanged baseline and impose no stability or priority constraints after baseline computation.

Status and main result

I prove a new obstruction to polynomial-time exact output in ordinary binary-rational form. It applies even to instances whose deferred-acceptance baseline terminates after polynomially many rounds and whose rational reassignment problem is feasible.

For every positive integer $n$, I construct an instance with
$$ m=n+2,\qquad \tau=n+1,\qquad L=O(n^2\log(n+2)), $$
such that representing the baseline, any feasible reassignment, or even the minimum objective value requires $\Omega(2^n)$ bits.

Consequently, the stated problem cannot have a polynomial-time algorithm that explicitly outputs these quantities as binary numerators and denominators. This is an output-length lower bound, not an $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness proof. A classification allowing succinct exact representations remains open here.

1. Construction

Fix $n\geq1$ and put
$$ c=\frac1{n+1}. $$

There are schools
$$ P_1,\ldots,P_n,X,Y $$
and types
$$ t_0,t_1,\ldots,t_n. $$

Every type has mass $c$. The capacities are
$$ q_{P_i}=c\quad(1\leq i\leq n), \qquad q_X=q_Y=\frac c2. $$
Thus total mass and total capacity both equal $1$.

Types $t_0,t_1$ have ranking
$$ P_1\succ\cdots\succ P_n\succ X\succ Y. $$
Every type $t_i$ with $2\leq i\leq n$ has ranking
$$ P_1\succ\cdots\succ P_n\succ Y\succ X. $$

Priorities are as follows:

  • At $P_i$, types $t_0,t_i$ have tier $1$; every other type has tier $2$.
  • At $X$, type $t_1$ has tier $1$; every other type has tier $2$.
  • At $Y$, every type has tier $1$.

These are strict complete school rankings and positive integer priority tiers. The explicitly listed rankings, tiers, masses, and capacities have total encoding length $O(n^2\log(n+2))$.

If “binary rational” is interpreted as requiring dyadic inputs, restrict to the infinite subfamily in which $n+1$ is a power of $2$. All masses and capacities are then dyadic, and the same lower bound applies.

2. Exact computation of the baseline

Define positive integers
$$ D_0=1,\qquad D_i=D_{i-1}(D_{i-1}+1)\quad(1\leq i\leq n). $$

I will prove that the remaining mass of $t_0$ after rejection by $P_i$ is
$$ r_i=\frac c{D_i}. $$

Processing the common prefix

All types share the first $n$ schools in the same order. School $P_i$ receives its proposals in round $i$.

This follows inductively. Initially, all mass proposes to $P_1$. After processing $P_i$, every rejected portion next proposes to $P_{i+1}$, or to its preferred terminal school when $i=n$. No portion can subsequently propose to an earlier prefix school. Consequently, held mass at a processed prefix school faces no later new proposals.

Suppose $P_1,\ldots,P_{i-1}$ have been processed and $t_0$ has remaining mass $r_{i-1}>0$. Type $t_i$ still has its full mass $c$: at every preceding $P_j$, it belonged to tier $2$, below the oversubscribed tier containing $t_0,t_j$.

At $P_i$, the tier-$1$ applicant mass is therefore
$$ r_{i-1}+c>c=q_{P_i}. $$
The school retains the fraction
$$ \frac c{r_{i-1}+c} $$
of each tier-$1$ applicant and rejects all tier-$2$ applicants.

The remaining mass of $t_0$ becomes
$$ r_i =r_{i-1}\left(1-\frac c{r_{i-1}+c}\right) =\frac{r_{i-1}^2}{r_{i-1}+c}. $$
Since $r_0=c$, substituting $r_{i-1}=c/D_{i-1}$ gives
$$ r_i =\frac c{D_{i-1}(D_{i-1}+1)} =\frac c{D_i}. $$
This proves the claimed recurrence by induction.

The masses retained at $P_i$ are
$$ b_{t_0,P_i} =\frac{cr_{i-1}}{r_{i-1}+c} =\frac c{D_{i-1}+1}, $$
and
$$ b_{t_i,P_i} =\frac{c^2}{r_{i-1}+c} =\frac{cD_{i-1}}{D_{i-1}+1}. $$
They sum to $c$. All other types receive zero at $P_i$.

Type $t_i$ has remaining mass
$$ c-b_{t_i,P_i}=\frac c{D_{i-1}+1}. $$
At subsequent prefix schools it belongs to tier $2$, so this remaining mass is rejected in full until it reaches a terminal school.

Processing the terminal schools

After $P_n$, the total remaining mass is $c$, because the prefix schools collectively hold $nc$.

The remaining mass of $t_1$ is
$$ \frac c{D_0+1}=\frac c2. $$
Write
$$ \delta_n=r_n=\frac c{D_n}=\frac1{(n+1)D_n}. $$

In round $n+1$:

  • Types $t_0,t_1$ propose their remaining masses to $X$.
  • Types $t_2,\ldots,t_n$ propose their remaining masses to $Y$.

At $X$, the tier-$1$ mass of $t_1$ equals its capacity $c/2$. Hence $X$ retains all that mass and rejects the entire $t_0$ mass $\delta_n$.

The initial proposals to $Y$ total
$$ c-\frac c2-\delta_n=\frac c2-\delta_n. $$
They are all accepted. In round $n+2$, the rejected $t_0$ mass proposes to $Y$, bringing its total to exactly $c/2$. All mass is then held, so the process terminates.

Thus the complete nonzero baseline entries are
$$ \begin{aligned} b_{t_0,P_i}&=\frac c{D_{i-1}+1} && (1\leq i\leq n),\\ b_{t_i,P_i}&=\frac{cD_{i-1}}{D_{i-1}+1} && (1\leq i\leq n),\\ b_{t_1,X}&=\frac c2,\\ b_{t_i,Y}&=\frac c{D_{i-1}+1} && (2\leq i\leq n),\\ b_{t_0,Y}&=\delta_n. \end{aligned} $$
Every other entry is zero.

All entries are rational. Retaining this baseline is therefore a feasible rational reassignment.

For an additional check on conservation, the identity
$$ \frac c{D_{i-1}+1} =\frac c{D_{i-1}}-\frac c{D_i} $$
telescopes to
$$ \sum_{i=1}^n\frac c{D_{i-1}+1}+\delta_n=c, $$
which is precisely the row sum for $t_0$.

3. Exponential output length

Since $D_1=2$ and
$$ D_i=D_{i-1}(D_{i-1}+1)\geq D_{i-1}^2, $$
induction gives
$$ D_n\geq 2^{\,2^{n-1}}. $$

The baseline entry
$$ b_{t_0,Y}=\frac1{(n+1)D_n} $$
is already in lowest terms. Its denominator therefore requires at least
$$ \log_2 D_n\geq 2^{n-1} $$
binary digits.

This lower bound also applies to every explicit feasible reassignment. Its row indexed by $(t_0,Y)$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{s\in S}x_{t_0,Y,s}=\delta_n>0. $$
At least one entry in that row is positive. Write such an entry as $a/d$, where $a,d$ are positive integers. Nonnegativity and the row sum imply
$$ \frac ad\leq\delta_n=\frac1{(n+1)D_n}. $$
Consequently,
$$ d\geq a(n+1)D_n\geq(n+1)D_n. $$
Its denominator requires $\Omega(2^n)$ bits.

This holds for every feasible output, independently of which optimizer an algorithm selects.

4. Exact minimum objective value

The minimum objective value is
$$ \boxed{\operatorname{OPT}_n=\frac n2+\frac1{(n+1)D_n}.} $$
In fact, every feasible reassignment has this value.

Prefix capacities prevent terminal mass from entering the prefix

Let
$$ \mathcal P=\{P_1,\ldots,P_n\}. $$

Baseline mass originating in $\mathcal P$ totals $nc$. Every type ranks every prefix school above both terminal schools. Thus the weak-improvement restriction requires all this baseline mass to remain within $\mathcal P$.

The total capacity of $\mathcal P$ is also $nc$. Therefore:

  • Each $P_i$ receives exactly $c$ in every feasible reassignment.
  • No baseline mass originating at $X$ or $Y$ can be reassigned into $\mathcal P$.

These conclusions use nonnegativity: any positive additional terminal-origin mass entering the prefix would exceed its total capacity.

Terminal masses are forced to stay

The baseline $t_1$ mass at $X$ is $c/2$. Its permitted destinations are $\mathcal P\cup\{X\}$. Since terminal-origin mass cannot enter $\mathcal P$, all of it must remain at $X$, filling that school.

For $i\geq2$, the baseline $t_i$ mass at $Y$ has permitted destinations $\mathcal P\cup\{Y\}$. It must therefore remain at $Y$.

Finally, the baseline $t_0$ mass $\delta_n$ at $Y$ cannot enter $\mathcal P$, and $X$ is already filled by the forced $t_1$ mass. It must remain at $Y$.

Calculating the objective

Every type ranks $P_i$ in position $i$. Since every prefix school receives exactly $c$, their objective contribution is
$$ c\sum_{i=1}^n(i-1)=\frac{cn(n-1)}2. $$

At the terminal schools:

  • The $t_1$ mass at $X$ has index $n$.
  • The $t_i$ masses at $Y$, for $i\geq2$, have index $n$.
  • The $t_0$ mass $\delta_n$ at $Y$ has index $n+1$.

Terminal-origin mass totals $c$. Its contribution is therefore
$$ n(c-\delta_n)+(n+1)\delta_n=nc+\delta_n. $$

Adding the contributions yields
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_n =\frac{cn(n-1)}2+nc+\delta_n =\frac{cn(n+1)}2+\delta_n =\frac n2+\delta_n. $$

The argument applies to every feasible reassignment, and retaining the baseline is feasible, so this proves the minimum.

It also proves uniqueness of the feasible reassignment. Indeed, row conservation gives
$$ \operatorname{cost}(b)-\operatorname{cost}(x) = \sum_{t,s_0,s} x_{t,s_0,s} \bigl(\operatorname{rank}_t(s_0)-\operatorname{rank}_t(s)\bigr). $$
Every summand is nonnegative. The costs are equal, so every positive reassignment entry must preserve rank. Because rankings are strict, this requires $s=s_0$.

5. Even the optimum value requires exponentially many bits

Put
$$ K_n=(n+1)D_n. $$
Suppose the exact optimum is represented as a rational $p/d$, with integers $p$ and $d>0$. Then
$$ \frac1{K_n} =\frac pd-\frac n2 =\frac{2p-nd}{2d}. $$
The numerator $2p-nd$ is a positive integer, so
$$ \frac1{K_n}\geq\frac1{2d}, \qquad d\geq\frac{K_n}{2}\geq D_n. $$
Thus even the minimum objective value requires $\Omega(2^n)$ denominator bits.

On this family, $m$, $\tau$, and $L$ are polynomially bounded in $n$. Writing $\Omega(2^n)$ output bits takes more than any polynomial in these input parameters. Hence no polynomial-time algorithm can explicitly output the exact binary-rational baseline, reassignment, or optimum value on all these instances.

6. What remains open

This lower bound concerns explicit exact output. A succinct representation can describe
$$ D_0=1,\qquad D_i=D_{i-1}(D_{i-1}+1) $$
using only $n$ arithmetic instructions. The construction therefore does not rule out polynomial-time algorithms using such representations.

Nor does the construction establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness of a threshold-decision problem. Indeed, threshold comparison is polynomial-time solvable on this particular family. To compare $\operatorname{OPT}_n$ with a rational $a/b$, where $b>0$:

  • If $a/b\leq n/2$, then $\operatorname{OPT}_n>a/b$.
  • Otherwise,

$$ \frac ab-\frac n2=\frac{2a-nb}{2b}\geq\frac1{2b}. $$
Compute the recurrence only until $(n+1)D_i>2b$. If that happens, then
$$ \delta_n\leq\frac1{(n+1)D_i}<\frac1{2b}, $$
proving $\operatorname{OPT}_n<a/b$.

  • If it never happens through $i=n$, the final denominator is at most $2b$, and ordinary exact comparison suffices.

Before the stopping condition, the integers have bit length polynomial in the threshold encoding length; the multiplication that first crosses it has at most twice that bit length. There are at most $n$ iterations.

The rigorously established advance is therefore an unconditional exclusion of polynomial-time explicit exact output, even for terminating rational instances. The general hardness classification under a succinct-output or decision formulation is not settled.
