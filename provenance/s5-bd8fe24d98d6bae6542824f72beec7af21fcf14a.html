The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite hypothesis set \(\Theta\), finitely many signal types \(A\) with rational masses \(\mu_a\), rational signal likelihood tables \(\ell_a(\cdot\mid\theta)\), and a rational fault-mass bound \(\rho<1/3\), consider an all-to-all society on \([0,1]\) whose type map gives type \(a\) mass \(\mu_a\). Honest agents of type \(a\) receive independent signals from \(\ell_a(\cdot\mid\theta^*)\). For each ordered hypothesis pair, each agent updates its log-likelihood ratio by taking the mean of received previous reports after trimming mass \(\rho\) from each end, splitting ties as needed, then adding its cumulative local log-likelihood ratio; a measurable Byzantine set of mass at most \(\rho\) may send arbitrary reports. Decide whether, for every true state and admissible Byzantine behavior, almost every honest agent’s ratios tend to \(+\infty\) for the true state against each alternative and to \(-\infty\) in the reverse direction; characterize this property by an effectively checkable necessary-and-sufficient condition and give a counterexample witness when it fails.

Let \(s=\sum_a|S_a|\) and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
The agent space \((I,\mathcal I,\lambda)\) extends the Lebesgue unit interval, with measurable type map \(a:I\to A\) satisfying \(\lambda(a^{-1}(a))=\mu_a\). Signals are modeled on a Fubini extension: the agent–probability product measure is extended so integrable measurable fields have measurable sections and their two iterated integrals agree. Conditional on each true state \(\theta^*\), each agent's signal sequence is i.i.d. with law \(\ell_{a(i)}(\cdot\mid\theta^*)\), and entire signal sequences are independent for almost every pair of distinct agents.
An execution has a fixed measurable Byzantine set \(F\subseteq I\), chosen before signals, with \(\lambda(F)\le\rho\). Byzantine agents may coordinate and send arbitrary finite real reports, differing by recipient and ordered hypothesis pair, measurably and adaptively using current and past information, without future signals. Missing reports are replaced by zero.
For each ordered pair \((u,v)\) of distinct hypotheses, initialize \(r_{i,0}(u,v)=0\). In synchronous round \(t\), every honest sender \(j\) reports \(r_{j,t-1}(u,v)\) to every recipient. The honest update is \(r_{i,t}(u,v)=\frac{1}{1-2\rho}\int_\rho^{1-\rho}Q_{i,t}^{u,v}(q)\,dq+\sum_{k=1}^t\log\frac{\ell_{a(i)}(s_{i,k}\mid u)}{\ell_{a(i)}(s_{i,k}\mid v)}\). 1. Recognition criterion

Recognition is in \(\mathrm P\). The exact threshold is strict.

For distinct hypotheses \(u,v\), define the distinguishing types and their total mass by

\(\displaystyle D_{u,v}=\{a\in A:\ell_a(\cdot\mid u)\ne\ell_a(\cdot\mid v)\},\qquad d_{u,v}=\sum_{a\in D_{u,v}}\mu_a.\)

Then the input is robustly learnable if and only if

\(\displaystyle d_{u,v}>2\rho\quad\text{for every distinct }u,v.\)

Equality fails. When the condition fails, there is a counterexecution with a fixed fault set of mass exactly \(\rho\) and deterministic, finite reports. A positive mass of honest agents has bounded reports in both directions for a failing pair.

The proof below works directly with the continuum update.

2. Quantile bounds

Write \(c=1-2\rho>0\). Fix a fault set \(F\), let \(f=\lambda(F)\le\rho\), and put \(H=I\setminus F\), with mass \(h=1-f\).

For a bounded measurable honest profile \(x:H\to\mathbb R\), define its mass quantile by

\(\displaystyle q_x(p)=\inf\{z:\lambda(\{j\in H:x(j)\le z\})\ge p\},\qquad 0<p<h.\)

Thus the quantile parameter measures sender mass, without normalizing \(H\) to mass one. Define the average of the lowest mass \(c\) by

\(\displaystyle K_H(x)=\frac1c\int_0^c q_x(p)\,dp.\)

Lemma 1: universal lower bound

Suppose a recipient’s report field \(y:I\to\mathbb R\) agrees with \(x\) on \(H\), and is arbitrary and finite on \(F\). Its trimmed average satisfies

\(\displaystyle \frac1c\int_\rho^{1-\rho}Q_y(q)\,dq\ge K_H(x).\)

Proof. Let \(G_y\) and \(G_x\) be the respective cumulative sender masses. For every real \(z\),

\(\displaystyle G_y(z)\le G_x(z)+f.\)

Consequently, for \(f<q<1\),

\(\displaystyle Q_y(q)\ge q_x(q-f).\)

Integrating over the retained interval gives

\(\displaystyle \frac1c\int_\rho^{1-\rho}Q_y(q)\,dq \ge\frac1c\int_{\rho-f}^{1-\rho-f}q_x(p)\,dp \ge\frac1c\int_0^c q_x(p)\,dp.\)

The last inequality uses monotonicity of \(q_x\), the nonnegative shift \(\rho-f\), and the fact that both integration intervals have length \(c\). Quantile endpoint choices do not affect these integrals. ∎

If \(x\) lies essentially in \([-M,M]\), every retained report quantile also lies in \([-M,M]\): mass below \(-M\) or above \(M\) can come only from \(F\), whose mass is at most \(\rho\). In particular, the trimmed integral is finite even when the Byzantine report field is not integrable.

Lemma 2: properties of the lowest-mass average

The functional has the variational representation

\(\displaystyle K_H(x)= \inf_{\substack{w:H\to[0,1]\ {\rm measurable}\\ \int_H w\,d\lambda=c}} \frac1c\int_H wx\,d\lambda. \)

Indeed, a minimizing weight takes all values below a threshold, none above it, and the required fraction of the threshold tie. If \(w_*\) is this weight and the threshold is \(z\), then every feasible \(w\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \int_H(w-w_*)x\,d\lambda =\int_H(w-w_*)(x-z)\,d\lambda\ge0.\)

Below the threshold both factors are nonpositive; above it both are nonnegative. When \(c=h\), the only feasible weight is one almost everywhere.

This representation proves:

  • Monotonicity: \(x\ge y\) almost everywhere implies \(K_H(x)\ge K_H(y)\).
  • Translation invariance: \(K_H(x+b)=K_H(x)+b\).
  • Positive homogeneity: \(K_H(tx)=tK_H(x)\) for \(t\ge0\).
  • The continuity bound

\(\displaystyle |K_H(x)-K_H(y)|\le\frac1c\int_H|x-y|\,d\lambda.\)

For the last assertion, the integrals for any fixed feasible weight differ by at most the displayed bound; taking infima in both directions proves the claim.

3. Local evidence and its limiting drift

Fix true state \(u\) and alternative \(v\). Define cumulative local evidence by

\(\displaystyle Z_{i,t}=\sum_{k=1}^t\log\frac{\ell_{a(i)}(s_{i,k}\mid u)}{\ell_{a(i)}(s_{i,k}\mid v)},\qquad Z_{i,0}=0.\)

Let

\(\displaystyle \Delta_a=\sum_{z\in S_a}\ell_a(z\mid u)\log\frac{\ell_a(z\mid u)}{\ell_a(z\mid v)}.\)

Strict positivity and finite alphabets make these quantities finite. Jensen’s inequality gives \(\Delta_a\ge0\), with equality exactly when the two probability vectors coincide: apply strict concavity of the logarithm to the ratios \(\ell_a(z\mid v)/\ell_a(z\mid u)\), whose weighted mean is one.

Hence \(\Delta_a>0\) precisely for \(a\in D_{u,v}\).

Choose a finite constant \(B\) bounding every absolute one-signal log likelihood ratio. Then

\(\displaystyle |Z_{i,t}|\le Bt.\)

The ordinary strong law, applied separately to each agent’s i.i.d. sequence, gives

\(\displaystyle Z_{i,t}/t\longrightarrow\Delta_{a(i)}\)

with probability one for almost every agent.

The stipulated Fubini property transfers this statement to: with probability one, convergence holds for almost every agent in the realized signal field. This is the Fubini property described in the supplied [continuum framework](https://arxiv.org/html/0803.0442). Since both \(Z_{i,t}/t\) and \(\Delta_{a(i)}\) are bounded by \(B\), dominated convergence on the agent space yields

\(\displaystyle \int_I\left|\frac{Z_{i,t}}t-\Delta_{a(i)}\right|\,d\lambda\longrightarrow0 \)

almost surely. In particular, Lemma 2 gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{K_H(Z_{\cdot,t})}{t}\longrightarrow K_H(\Delta_{a(\cdot)})=:\gamma_H \)

almost surely.

4. Sufficiency of the strict threshold

Assume \(d_{u,v}>2\rho\), and put

\(\displaystyle \delta=\min_{a\in D_{u,v}}\Delta_a>0.\)

This minimum exists because the supported type set is finite.

The total mass of agents with zero drift is \(1-d_{u,v}\). Thus every feasible weight in the definition of \(K_H\) places at least mass

\(\displaystyle c-(1-d_{u,v})=d_{u,v}-2\rho\)

on positive-drift honest agents. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \gamma_H\ge\frac{\delta(d_{u,v}-2\rho)}c>0. \)

This bound holds for every admissible fault set.

Define a common comparison sequence by

\(\displaystyle b_0=0,\qquad b_t=b_{t-1}+K_H(Z_{\cdot,t-1}).\)

For every Byzantine strategy, the honest forward reports satisfy

\(\displaystyle r_{i,t}(u,v)\ge b_t+Z_{i,t}\)

almost everywhere. The assertion holds at initialization. If it holds at the preceding round, Lemmas 1 and 2 give

\(\displaystyle \text{trimmed average at recipient }i \ge K_H(r_{\cdot,t-1}(u,v)) \ge b_{t-1}+K_H(Z_{\cdot,t-1})=b_t. \)

Adding the prescribed cumulative local evidence proves the induction.

Because \(K_H(Z_{\cdot,k})/k\to\gamma_H\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{b_t}{t^2} =\frac1{t^2}\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}K_H(Z_{\cdot,k}) \longrightarrow\frac{\gamma_H}{2}. \)

To justify this limit explicitly, write \(K_H(Z_{\cdot,k})=\gamma_H k+o(k)\); a finite initial segment contributes \(o(t^2)\), while any sufficiently late segment of the remainder is bounded in absolute value by \(\varepsilon\sum_{k<t}k\).

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle r_{i,t}(u,v)\ge b_t-Bt\longrightarrow+\infty.\)

For the reverse ordered pair, negate every report and the honest state variable. Symmetric trimming is odd:

\(\displaystyle T_\rho(-y)=-T_\rho(y).\)

This follows by reversing quantile order; \(Q_{-y}(q)=-Q_y(1-q)\) almost everywhere in the quantile parameter. The local evidence of the negated reverse update is again \(Z_{i,t}\). The same comparison therefore gives

\(\displaystyle -r_{i,t}(v,u)\ge b_t+Z_{i,t}\longrightarrow+\infty.\)

Thus \(r_{i,t}(v,u)\to-\infty\). For each true state, intersecting the probability-one events for its finitely many alternatives proves robust learnability.

5. Necessity and explicit counterexecutions

Suppose a pair \(u,v\) satisfies \(d:=d_{u,v}\le2\rho\). Take \(u\) as the true state.

Fixed fault allocation

Choose fault mass \(\min(\rho,d)\) from distinguishing types, and fill the remaining fault budget from nondistinguishing types. The fault set has mass exactly \(\rho\), while the remaining honest distinguishing mass is

\(\displaystyle q=d-\min(\rho,d)=\max(d-\rho,0)\le\rho.\)

The honest nondistinguishing mass is therefore

\(\displaystyle z=1-\rho-q\ge1-2\rho=c>0.\)

These fault masses can be realized measurably. For any measurable type set \(E\), the function

\(\displaystyle x\mapsto\lambda(E\cap[0,x])\)

is continuous: its increment between \(y\) and \(x\) is bounded by \(x-y\). It ranges from zero to \(\lambda(E)\), so any prescribed fraction of a type can be selected. This uses that the agent space extends the Lebesgue interval.

Deterministic reports

Every honest report, under any execution, obeys

\(\displaystyle |r_{i,t}(u,v)|\le\frac B2t(t+1).\)

Indeed, trimming keeps the aggregation between the honest essential extrema, and the cumulative local term has absolute value at most \(Bt\); induction proves the bound.

For the ordered pair \((u,v)\), let every Byzantine sender report to every recipient

\(\displaystyle -1-Bt^2\)

in round \(t\). This lies strictly below every honest report from round \(t-1\). All fault mass \(\rho\) is consequently removed by the lower trimming. Upper trimming removes the highest honest mass \(\rho\), leaving exactly the lowest honest mass \(c\).

All recipients now receive the same aggregation. Translation invariance gives the exact identity

\(\displaystyle r_{i,t}(u,v)=b_t+Z_{i,t},\qquad b_t=b_{t-1}+K_H(Z_{\cdot,t-1}).\)

Since honest nondistinguishing agents have \(Z_{i,k}=0\) and mass at least \(c\),

\(\displaystyle -\frac1c\int_H(-Z_{i,k})_+\,d\lambda \le K_H(Z_{\cdot,k})\le0. \)

The upper bound follows by choosing a weight supported on those zero-evidence agents. The lower bound follows from \(0\le w\le1\) in the variational representation.

The negative increments are summable

For a distinguishing type \(a\), define

\(\displaystyle \kappa_a=\sum_{z\in S_a}\sqrt{\ell_a(z\mid u)\ell_a(z\mid v)}<1. \)

Cauchy–Schwarz gives the upper bound one; equality would force the two probability vectors to coincide.

Temporal independence gives

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_u[e^{-Z_{i,k}/2}]=\kappa_a^k. \)

Markov’s inequality and the bound \(|Z_{i,k}|\le Bk\) imply

\(\displaystyle \mathbb P_u(Z_{i,k}<0)\le\kappa_a^k,\qquad \mathbb E_u[(-Z_{i,k})_+]\le Bk\kappa_a^k. \)

For nondistinguishing types the negative part is identically zero. Writing \(h_a=\lambda(H\cap a^{-1}(a))\), Fubini and summation of nonnegative expectations give

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_u\!\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_H(-Z_{i,k})_+\,d\lambda\right] \le B\sum_{a\in D_{u,v}}h_a\frac{\kappa_a}{(1-\kappa_a)^2}<\infty. \)

Thus the sum inside the expectation is finite almost surely. The preceding bounds on \(K_H\) show that \(b_t\) decreases to a finite limit \(b_\infty\).

Every honest nondistinguishing agent therefore satisfies

\(\displaystyle r_{i,t}(u,v)=b_t\longrightarrow b_\infty\in\mathbb R.\)

This happens on honest mass at least \(c>0\), contradicting robust learnability. If Byzantine reports for \((v,u)\) are the corresponding positive values \(1+Bt^2\), oddness of trimming gives \(r_{i,t}(v,u)=-r_{i,t}(u,v)\), so the reverse reports are bounded there as well.

This proves necessity, including equality. When \(\rho=0\), failure means \(d=0\); the same construction has no faults and all reports for the pair stay zero.

On rejection, a witness consists of:

  • Rational fault masses by supported type, totaling \(\rho\), allocated as above.
