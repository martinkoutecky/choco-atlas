Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite item set \([m]\), a distribution \(\mu\) over buyer types with support size \(\tau\), per-capita item supplies \(b_j\), and a population scale \(N\) with \(N\mu_t,Nb_j\in\mathbb Z\), let \(x^*_{t,S}\) be a feasible per-capita fractional allocation of bundle \(S\) to type \(t\). Given an efficient \(\alpha\)-integrality-gap verifier for the aggregate count-allocation polytope, find a finite lottery over feasible integer count allocations \(y_{t,S}\) such that \(\mathbb E[y_{t,S}/N]=\frac{\alpha}{1+4\varepsilon}x^*_{t,S}\) for every type and bundle. The decomposition should use at most \(s\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\ln s\rceil\) verifier calls, where \(s\) is the number of nonzero coordinates of \(Nx^*\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), \(m\ge1\), \(b_j\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), and \(N\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), encoded in binary, with \(N\mu_t,Nb_j\in\mathbb Z\).
Coordinates are pairs \((t,S)\) with \(S\subseteq[m]\), including the empty bundle. Define the count-allocation polytope \(Q=\{z\ge0:\sum_Sz_{t,S}\le N\mu_t\ \forall t,\ \sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}z_{t,S}\le Nb_j\ \forall j\}\); feasible integer allocations are \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^{\tau2^m}\). Buyers may remain unassigned and supplies unused.
The input includes a sparsely encoded rational allocation \(x^*\) with \(Nx^*\in Q\), rational parameters \(0<\alpha\le1\) and \(0<\varepsilon\le1/2\), and \(s=|\operatorname{supp}(Nx^*)|\ge2\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
An available verifier, on sparsely encoded rational inputs \(w\) of either sign and \(z\in Q\), returns a sparsely encoded \(y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^{\tau2^m}\) satisfying \(w^Ty\ge\alpha w^Tz\), in time polynomial in \(m,\tau\) and its input bit length.
The required output is a finite lottery with binary rational probabilities \(\lambda_k\ge0\), \(\sum_k\lambda_k=1\), and sparsely encoded feasible integer allocations \(y^k\), satisfying \(\sum_k\lambda_k y^k_{t,S}/N=\alpha x^*_{t,S}/(1+4\varepsilon)\) for every coordinate, using at most \(s\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\ln s\rceil\) verifier calls.

Is this lottery construction solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,s,L,\varepsilon^{-1}\), with the stated verifier-call bound, or is it NP-hard even with the promised efficient verifier?

Elbassioni, Mehlhorn, and Ramezani establish the corresponding decomposition for arbitrary packing polytopes, with the stated call bound and at most \(s(1+\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\ln s\rceil)\) lottery outcomes. Their result does not separately establish bit complexity for a society compressed into binary population counts. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.1577v4).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and specified nonnegative rational supplies and a positive binary-encoded integer population scale.
  • Defined aggregate feasibility by at-most-one-bundle constraints for each type population and item-supply constraints; included empty bundles and permitted unassigned buyers and unused supplies.
  • Specified sparse binary rational encodings for allocations, verifier inputs and outputs, and lottery probabilities so running time is measured in the compressed input rather than the expanded population or all possible bundles.
  • Carried over the verifier guarantee for arbitrary signed weights and every feasible fractional count allocation, rather than only optimal allocations.
  • Restricted the accuracy parameter to the range used in the underlying algorithm and restricted to support size at least two: the full version retains a zero-call, one-outcome bound at support size one that cannot generally represent a nonintegral target.

Result and encoding convention

The construction is polynomial-time solvable under the promised verifier, with at most \(s\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\ln s\rceil\) verifier calls. It can also achieve the cited bound of \(s(1+\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\ln s\rceil)\) outcomes.

Here, “binary rational” means a rational encoded by its numerator and denominator in binary. If probabilities were required to be dyadic, exact equality could fail even in elementary examples.

The [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.1577v4) supplies the corresponding decomposition theorem. The proof below gives a rational implementation and explicitly bounds its bit complexity, without expanding the population.

1. Restriction to the supported coordinates

Put \(q=Nx^*\), and enumerate its support as \(I=\{1,\ldots,s\}\). Thus \(q_i>0\) for every \(i\in I\). Define the required expectation in count units by

\(d_i=\alpha q_i/(1+4\varepsilon)\).

All coordinates outside \(I\) must have expectation zero.

The polytope \(Q\) is downward closed: if \(0\le u\le v\) coordinatewise and \(v\in Q\), then \(u\in Q\), because every constraint has nonnegative coefficients. Consequently, we can replace every verifier output by its restriction to \(I\). This preserves feasibility, integrality, and its scalar product with any weight vector supported on \(I\).

Also, every coordinate of every feasible allocation is at most \(N\mu_t\le N\). Hence every integer count has \(O(\log N)\) bits.

2. A rational stopping threshold

We first construct a rational threshold \(H\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \frac{19}{20}\frac{\ln s}{\varepsilon^2}\le H\le\frac{\ln s}{\varepsilon^2}.\)

Let \(M=\lceil20/\varepsilon\rceil\), and set

\(\displaystyle \ell=\sum_{k=M+1}^{sM}\frac1k,\qquad H=\frac{\ell}{\varepsilon^2}.\)

The right and left Riemann sums for the decreasing function \(1/x\) on \([1,s]\), with mesh \(1/M\), show that

\(\displaystyle \ell\le\ln s\le\ell+\frac1M-\frac1{sM}\le\ell+\frac{\varepsilon}{20}.\)

Since \(s\ge2\), we have \(\ln s\ge\ln2\ge1/2\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \ell\ge\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{10}\right)\ln s\ge\frac{19}{20}\ln s,\)

which proves the threshold bounds.

There are \(O(s\varepsilon^{-1})\) summands. Using the product of their denominators as a common denominator shows that \(\ell\), and hence \(H\), can be computed exactly with polynomially many bits and operations.

3. Rational covering algorithm

For each coordinate \(i\in I\), initialize a load \(h_i=0\) and a weight \(p_i=1\). A coordinate is *active* when \(h_i<H\); inactive coordinates have weight zero.

While an active coordinate remains, perform the following iteration.

  • Give the verifier the feasible point \(q\) and the sparse weight vector

\(\displaystyle w_i=\frac{p_i}{\alpha q_i}\)
on active coordinates, with all other weights zero.

  • Restrict its integer output \(y\) to \(I\), and put

\(\displaystyle a_i=\frac{y_i}{\alpha q_i}.\)

  • Set

\(\displaystyle \delta=\frac1{\max_{i\text{ active}}a_i}.\)
Record the pair \((\delta,y)\).

  • Increase every load by \(h_i\leftarrow h_i+\delta a_i\). For every coordinate active before this iteration, update

\(\displaystyle p_i\leftarrow p_i(1-\varepsilon\delta a_i).\)
Then set the weights of newly inactive coordinates to zero.

Every arithmetic operation is rational.

To verify that an iteration is well defined, let \(A\) denote its active set and \(P=\sum_{i\in A}p_i\). The verifier guarantee gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i\in A}p_i a_i=w^Ty\ge\alpha w^Tq=\sum_{i\in A}p_i=P.\)

Thus \(\max_{i\in A}a_i\ge1\), so \(0<\delta\le1\). Moreover,

\(\displaystyle 0\le\delta a_i\le1\quad(i\in A).\)

Every updated active weight therefore stays positive, since \(1-\varepsilon\delta a_i\ge1-\varepsilon\ge1/2\).

Verifier-call bound

Charge each iteration to an active coordinate attaining the maximum in the definition of \(\delta\). Its load increases by exactly one.

Loads never decrease. Immediately before a coordinate receives its \(r\)-th charge, its load is at least \(r-1\), and it must still be below \(H\). Hence it receives at most \(\lceil H\rceil\) charges. This also proves termination.

If \(K\) is the number of iterations, then

\(\displaystyle K\le s\lceil H\rceil\le s\left\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\ln s\right\rceil.\)

There is exactly one verifier call per iteration.

4. The recorded allocations dominate the target

Write the recorded pairs as \((\delta_r,y^r)\), and let

\(\displaystyle D=\sum_{r=1}^K\delta_r.\)

We prove \(D/H\le1+4\varepsilon\).

In any iteration, before removing newly inactive coordinates, the sum of updated active weights is

\(\displaystyle \widetilde P=P-\varepsilon\delta\sum_{i\in A}p_i a_i \le P(1-\varepsilon\delta)\le P e^{-\varepsilon\delta}.\)

Removing coordinates only decreases this sum. Starting from total weight \(s\), the updated weight sum in the final iteration is consequently at most \(s e^{-\varepsilon D}\).

Choose a coordinate \(i_0\) active immediately before the final iteration. Monotonicity of loads implies that it was active in every preceding iteration. Its final load satisfies \(h_{i_0}\le H+1\).

For \(0\le u\le1\), concavity of the logarithm gives

\(\displaystyle \ln(1-\varepsilon u)\ge u\ln(1-\varepsilon).\)

Applying this inequality to every weight update of \(i_0\), its final weight before removal satisfies

\(\displaystyle \widetilde p_{i_0}\ge(1-\varepsilon)^{h_{i_0}}\ge(1-\varepsilon)^{H+1}.\)

Combining the lower and upper bounds and taking logarithms yields

\(\displaystyle \frac DH\le\frac{\ln s}{\varepsilon H} +\left(1+\frac1H\right)\frac{-\ln(1-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}.\)

For \(0<\varepsilon\le1/2\), the convergent logarithm series gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{-\ln(1-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon} =1+\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\varepsilon^k}{k+1} \le1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1-\varepsilon)} \le1+\varepsilon.\)

The threshold bounds now imply

\(\displaystyle \frac{\ln s}{\varepsilon H}\le\frac{20}{19}\varepsilon\)

and, using \(\ln s\ge1/2\) and \((1+\varepsilon)\varepsilon\le3/4\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{1+\varepsilon}{H} \le\frac{40}{19}(1+\varepsilon)\varepsilon^2 \le\frac{30}{19}\varepsilon.\)

Therefore

\(\displaystyle \frac DH\le1+\frac{69}{19}\varepsilon<1+4\varepsilon.\)

Assign probability \(\lambda_r=\delta_r/D\) to \(y^r\). These are positive rational probabilities summing to one. At termination every load is at least \(H\), so

\(\displaystyle \sum_{r=1}^K\lambda_r y_i^r =\frac{\alpha q_i h_i}{D} \ge\frac{\alpha q_i H}{D} \ge d_i\qquad(i\in I).\)

Thus the resulting lottery dominates the target.

5. Exact correction by batched deletions

We now correct each coordinate in turn, preserving the expectations of all other coordinates.

For the current coordinate \(i\), let its surplus be

\(\displaystyle \Delta=\sum_k\lambda_k y_i^k-d_i\ge0.\)

Ignore outcomes with zero probability or zero \(i\)-th count. For an outcome \((\lambda,y)\) with \(\lambda>0\) and \(y_i>0\):

  • If \(\Delta\ge\lambda y_i\), set \(y_i=0\) and subtract \(\lambda y_i\) from \(\Delta\).
  • Otherwise, let \(a=\lfloor\Delta/\lambda\rfloor\). Delete \(a\) units from \(y_i\), leaving a surplus \(r=\Delta-\lambda a\), where \(0\le r<\lambda\).

If \(r>0\), replace the resulting outcome by two outcomes: \(y\) with probability \(\lambda-r\), and \(y-e_i\) with probability \(r\).

In the second case, \(a<y_i\), so the additional deletion is permissible. The split decreases the expectation in coordinate \(i\) by exactly \(r\), and preserves every other coordinate’s expectation and the total probability.

All modified allocations are nonnegative integer vectors dominated by their original allocations. Downward closure therefore guarantees feasibility.

There is sufficient count to remove: the required decrease is the current expectation minus the nonnegative target. Consequently, this procedure reaches \(\Delta=0\). Each coordinate requires at most one split, because a split completes its correction.

After all \(s\) corrections, the expectation is exactly \(d\), and the number of outcomes is at most

\(\displaystyle K+s\le s\left(1+\left\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\ln s\right\rceil\right).\)

Outside \(I\), every outcome is zero. Dividing the count expectations by \(N\) proves every required coordinate equality.

6. Bit complexity

It remains to check that the algorithms above are polynomial in the specified parameters.

Covering arithmetic. Each \(q_i\), \(\alpha q_i\), and verifier-output count has \(O(L)\) bits. Every coefficient \(a_i=y_i/(\alpha q_i)\), increment \(\delta\), and update factor \(1-\varepsilon\delta a_i\) therefore has \(O(L)\) bits.

Each load is a sum of at most \(K\) such rational increments, and each weight is a product of at most \(K\) such rational factors. Taking products of denominators bounds their bit lengths by \(O(KL+\log K)\). The sparse verifier inputs consequently have polynomial length.

The verifier’s promised running time bounds its output length polynomially. Reading that output and restricting it to \(I\) is therefore polynomial-time. Every retained outcome has at most \(s\) coordinates, each with count at most \(N\).

Initial probabilities. The sum \(D\) and all probabilities \(\delta_r/D\) have polynomial bit length by the same denominator-product bound.

Correction arithmetic. Choose a common denominator \(B\) for all initial probabilities and all target coordinates \(d_i\). Its bit length is polynomial, since there are polynomially many rationals of polynomial bit length.

Throughout correction, every probability remains an integer multiple of \(1/B\): surpluses are integer linear combinations of probabilities minus a target coordinate, and splitting uses only those surpluses and subtraction. Since probabilities remain between zero and one, their numerators require at most \(O(\log B)\) bits. Integer deletions and the divisions used to compute floors are also polynomial-time.

There are at most \(K+s\) outcomes and \(O(s(K+s))\) correction operations. None of these operations loops over \(N\) buyers or deletes counts one unit at a time.

Thus the complete construction runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,s,L,\varepsilon^{-1}\), with the stated verifier-call bound.
