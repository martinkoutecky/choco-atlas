The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of reviewer types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to one, a shared task distribution, and type-specific signal channels that are conditionally independent given ground truth, does the DMI payment rule—formed from two blocks of at least \(C\) tasks and averaged over peer-type masses—make truthful reporting dominant for each worker against arbitrary type-level reporting strategies, with strict preference over non-permutation strategies when informative truthful peers have positive mass?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is nonatomic and given by its support: \(\tau\) reviewer types \(\Theta\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_\theta\), with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
Let \(m=C\ge2\). Each of \(T\ge2C\) independent, identically distributed tasks has ground truth \(G\in[C]\) with distribution \(p\), and a type-\(\theta\) worker receives \(X_\theta\in[C]\) through channel \(K_\theta(g,a)=\Pr[X_\theta=a\mid G=g]\). Distinct workers' signals are conditionally independent given ground truth, including workers of the same type. The distribution and channels are nonnegative rational stochastic arrays; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A reporting strategy is any real row-stochastic \(C\times C\) matrix, applied to the current signal identically on every task, with independent private randomization across workers and tasks. Signals incur no effort cost, workers cannot communicate, and utility is ex ante expected payment. Truth uses \(I_C\); permutation strategies use permutation matrices.
Fix a partition into blocks \(B_1,B_2\) with \(|B_\ell|\ge C\), independently of signals and reports. For a focal worker and a distinct peer of type \(\eta\), define \(M_\ell^{\theta\eta}(a,b)=\sum_{t\in B_\ell}\mathbf1\{(\widehat X_\theta^t,\widehat X_\eta^t)=(a,b)\}\) and the normalized DMI pair payment \(Z_{\theta\eta}=\det(M_1^{\theta\eta})\det(M_2^{\theta\eta})/\bigl((C!)^2\binom{|B_1|}{C}\binom{|B_2|}{C}\bigr)\).
Against peer strategies \(S=(S_\eta)_{\eta\in\Theta}\), the mass-averaged expected payment for unilateral strategy \(R\) is \(U_\theta(R;S)=\sum_\eta\mu_\eta\mathbb E[Z_{\theta\eta}\mid R,S_\eta]\). Same-type peers are included; a unilateral deviation leaves peer strategies and masses fixed.
Define \(J_{\theta\eta}(a,b)=\sum_g p_gK_\theta(g,a)K_\eta(g,b)\). A peer type is informative for \(\theta\) precisely when \(\det J_{\theta\eta}\ne0\). Any running times are measured in \(m,\tau,L,T\).

Does this mass-averaged DMI rule satisfy \(U_\theta(I_C;S)\ge U_\theta(R;S)\) for every admissible instance, type, peer profile, and unilateral strategy, with strict inequality for every non-permutation \(R\) whenever \(\sum_{\eta:S_\eta=I_C,\,\det J_{\theta\eta}\ne0}\mu_\eta>0\), or is there a counterexample?

For finite populations, DMI is detail-free, minimal, individually rational, informed truthful, and dominantly truthful with at least two workers and \(T\ge2C\), under independent identically distributed tasks and consistent reporting strategies ([Kong](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.00272)).
Later work constructs additional finite-task dominantly truthful mechanisms ([Kong](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2022.95)); the search identified no result addressing exactly the mass-averaged problem defined here.

  • Restricted arbitrary reporting strategies to consistent signal-to-report stochastic matrices with independent private randomization, as required by the discrete model.
  • Specified independent identically distributed tasks, a fixed report-independent block partition, costless signals, no communication, and ex ante expected-payment utility.
  • Set ground truth, signals, and reports to the same finite choice alphabet; used rational task probabilities and signal channels, allowing zero entries.
  • Defined mass averaging over normalized pair payments before any aggregation of joint report matrices.
  • Interpreted types as masses of distinct nonatomic workers, allowing same-type peers and holding all peer behavior fixed during a unilateral deviation.
  • Defined informative peers by nonsingularity of their truthful joint signal matrix, rather than by an unspecified notion of information.
  • Used at least two choices to exclude the degenerate single-choice case and identified the generic size parameter with the number of choices.

Result

The stated weak and strict inequalities both hold. More precisely, for every admissible instance, type \(\theta\), peer profile \(S\), and unilateral strategy \(R\),

\[ U_\theta(R;S)=\det(R)^2U_\theta(I_C;S). \]

We prove this identity and the required strictness below. The argument directly establishes the specified mass-averaged rule; its finite-population counterpart is [Kong’s DMI theorem, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.00272).

1. Joint report distribution

Fix a focal type \(\theta\) and a distinct peer of type \(\eta\). Let \(R(a,u)\) be the probability that the focal worker reports \(u\) upon receiving signal \(a\), and interpret \(S_\eta(b,v)\) analogously.

Conditional independence of the two workers’ signals given ground truth gives their joint signal distribution as

\[ J_{\theta\eta}(a,b)=\sum_{g=1}^{C}p_gK_\theta(g,a)K_\eta(g,b). \]

Independent private randomization therefore gives the joint report distribution

\[ Q_{\theta\eta}(u,v) =\sum_{a,b=1}^{C}R(a,u)J_{\theta\eta}(a,b)S_\eta(b,v). \]

In matrix notation,

\[ Q_{\theta\eta}=R^{\mathsf T}J_{\theta\eta}S_\eta, \qquad \det Q_{\theta\eta} =\det R\,\det J_{\theta\eta}\,\det S_\eta. \]

The same calculation applies when \(\eta=\theta\): the peer is a distinct worker, and the assumed conditional independence explicitly includes workers of the same type.

Because tasks are independent and identically distributed, strategies are constant across tasks, and private randomization is independent across tasks, the reported pairs are independent and identically distributed with distribution \(Q_{\theta\eta}\).

2. Exact expectation of a block determinant

We first prove an identity for any joint probability matrix \(Q\) on \([C]\times[C]\).

Let \((A_t,D_t)\), indexed by a block \(B\) of size \(n\ge C\), be independent samples with distribution \(Q\). Define

\[ M(a,b)=\sum_{t\in B}\mathbf 1\{A_t=a,\ D_t=b\}. \]

Let \(\mathfrak S_C\) denote the permutations of \([C]\). The determinant expansion gives

\[ \det M =\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_C}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) \sum_{(t_1,\ldots,t_C)\in B^C} \prod_{a=1}^{C} \mathbf 1\{A_{t_a}=a,\ D_{t_a}=\sigma(a)\}. \]

If \(t_a=t_{a'}\) for two different rows \(a\ne a'\), the corresponding product is zero: it would require the same report \(A_{t_a}\) to equal both \(a\) and \(a'\). Thus only tuples of distinct task indices contribute.

For each such tuple, independence across tasks implies

\[ \mathbb E\!\left[ \prod_{a=1}^{C} \mathbf 1\{A_{t_a}=a,\ D_{t_a}=\sigma(a)\} \right] =\prod_{a=1}^{C}Q(a,\sigma(a)). \]

There are exactly \(n(n-1)\cdots(n-C+1)=C!\binom nC\) ordered tuples of distinct indices. Taking expectations and applying the determinant expansion once more yields

\[ \mathbb E[\det M] =C!\binom nC \sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_C} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{a=1}^{C}Q(a,\sigma(a)) =C!\binom nC\det Q. \]

Now apply this identity to the two stipulated blocks, with \(n_\ell=|B_\ell|\). Their count matrices are independent because they depend on disjoint collections of independent tasks and private randomizations. Hence

\[ \mathbb E[\det M_1^{\theta\eta}\det M_2^{\theta\eta}] =(C!)^2\binom{n_1}{C}\binom{n_2}{C} \bigl(\det Q_{\theta\eta}\bigr)^2. \]

Dividing by the normalization in the question proves

\[ \mathbb E[Z_{\theta\eta}\mid R,S_\eta] =\bigl(\det Q_{\theta\eta}\bigr)^2 =\det(R)^2\det(J_{\theta\eta})^2\det(S_\eta)^2. \]

If the partition is selected randomly but independently of signals and reports, the same argument holds conditional on every permitted partition. The resulting normalized expectation is independent of the partition, so the unconditional identity also holds.

3. Determinant bound and its equality case

Lemma. Every nonnegative row-stochastic \(C\times C\) matrix \(R\) satisfies \(|\det R|\le 1\), with equality if and only if \(R\) is a permutation matrix.

Proof. Write \(r_a\) for row \(a\). Since its entries are nonnegative and sum to one,

\[ \|r_a\|_2^2 =\sum_{u=1}^{C}R(a,u)^2 \le\left(\sum_{u=1}^{C}R(a,u)\right)^2 =1. \]

Equality holds precisely when at most one entry is positive: the difference between the squared sum and the sum of squares is \(2\sum_{u<v}R(a,u)R(a,v)\). Consequently, equality holds precisely when the row has one entry equal to one and all other entries zero.

Hadamard’s determinant inequality, applied to the real square matrix \(R\), now gives

\[ |\det R|\le\prod_{a=1}^{C}\|r_a\|_2\le 1. \]

If \(|\det R|=1\), every row norm must equal one. Thus every row is a coordinate vector. These coordinate vectors must be distinct, because repeated rows would make the determinant zero. Hence \(R\) is a permutation matrix.

Conversely, every permutation matrix has determinant equal to \(1\) or \(-1\). This proves the lemma.

4. Mass averaging and strictness

Define the nonnegative quantity

\[ A_\theta(S) =\sum_{\eta\in\Theta} \mu_\eta\det(J_{\theta\eta})^2\det(S_\eta)^2. \]

The exact pair-payment identity and the fixed peer masses imply

\[ U_\theta(R;S)=\det(R)^2A_\theta(S), \qquad U_\theta(I_C;S)=A_\theta(S). \]

Therefore,

\[ U_\theta(I_C;S)-U_\theta(R;S) =\bigl(1-\det(R)^2\bigr)A_\theta(S)\ge 0. \]

Both factors are nonnegative by the lemma and the definition of \(A_\theta(S)\). This proves weak dominance for every admissible peer profile.

Suppose now that

\[ \sum_{\eta:\,S_\eta=I_C,\ \det J_{\theta\eta}\ne0}\mu_\eta>0. \]

Since the support is finite, there is at least one listed type satisfying these conditions. Its contribution to \(A_\theta(S)\) is

\[ \mu_\eta\det(J_{\theta\eta})^2>0, \]

because \(\det(S_\eta)^2=\det(I_C)^2=1\). All other contributions are nonnegative, so \(A_\theta(S)>0\).

For every non-permutation \(R\), the lemma gives \(\det(R)^2<1\). Thus both factors in the utility difference are strictly positive, proving

\[ U_\theta(I_C;S)>U_\theta(R;S). \]

Indeed, the proof establishes the sharper condition: strict inequality holds exactly when \(R\) is not a permutation matrix and some peer type satisfies both \(\det J_{\theta\eta}\ne0\) and \(\det S_\eta\ne0\). Same-type peers remain included throughout, as required.
