1. Result. Yes. In fact, rational prices and bundles achieving \(\eta=B\) can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). No population expansion or rounding of masses is needed.

The threshold decomposition underlying the [explicit-population result of Yan and Liu](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06317v1) can be implemented directly with rational masses. We prove the required weighted-market algorithm and its approximation guarantee below.

2. Exact equilibria for weighted dichotomous markets. Consider types \(a\in A\) with positive rational masses \(w_a\), goods \(j\in J\) with positive rational supplies \(s_j\), and dichotomous utilities \(v_{aj}\in\{0,1\}\). Assume that every type likes at least one good and that

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\in A}w_a=\sum_{j\in J}s_j=1.\)

We will compute an exact equilibrium with nonnegative rational prices, minimum price zero, and unit budgets and bundle sizes.

For \(S\subseteq A\), let \(N(S)\) be its set of liked goods, and write

\(\displaystyle w(S)=\sum_{a\in S}w_a,\qquad s(H)=\sum_{j\in H}s_j.\)

We use the following weighted matching criterion: demands \(d_a\) can be assigned entirely along liked edges, without exceeding supplies, if and only if

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\in S}d_a\le s(N(S))\quad\text{for every }S\subseteq A.\)

Indeed, construct a network with source-to-type capacities \(d_a\), type-to-liked-good capacities \(D=\sum_a d_a\), and good-to-sink capacities \(s_j\). A cut crossing a type-to-good edge has capacity at least \(D\). Every other relevant cut has capacity at least \(D-d(S)+s(N(S))\). Thus the displayed inequalities imply, by max-flow/min-cut, a flow of value \(D\). Necessity follows by summing the assignments to types in \(S\).

We also need to find a subset maximizing the density

\(\displaystyle q=\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}\frac{w(S)}{s(N(S))}.\)

Here and below, neighborhoods are taken in the current remaining graph.

Computing the maximum density. Suppose every current type has a current neighbor. Solve the rational linear program

\(\displaystyle \text{maximize }\sum_{a\in A}w_a\xi_a\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j\in J}s_j\zeta_j=1,\qquad \xi_a,\zeta_j\ge0,\qquad \xi_a\le\zeta_j\ \text{whenever }v_{aj}=1.\)

This feasible region is bounded: \(\zeta_j\le 1/s_j\), and each \(\xi_a\) is bounded by one of its neighbors’ variables.

Its optimum equals \(q\). To prove the lower bound, for any nonempty \(S\), set \(\xi_a=1/s(N(S))\) on \(S\) and zero elsewhere, and set \(\zeta_j=1/s(N(S))\) on \(N(S)\) and zero elsewhere. This is feasible and has objective \(w(S)/s(N(S))\).

For the upper bound, take any feasible solution. List its distinct positive type-variable values as

\(\displaystyle 0=h_0<h_1<\cdots<h_\ell,\qquad S_i=\{a:\xi_a\ge h_i\}.\)

Then

\(\displaystyle \sum_a w_a\xi_a=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(h_i-h_{i-1})w(S_i).\)

For every good \(j\), its variable dominates all neighboring type variables. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(h_i-h_{i-1})s(N(S_i))\le\sum_j s_j\zeta_j=1.\)

Since \(w(S_i)\le q\,s(N(S_i))\), the objective is at most \(q\).

Moreover, an optimal solution yields a maximizing subset: enumerate its sets \(S_i\) and choose one with largest density. If every such density were smaller than \(q\), the preceding inequalities would make the objective smaller than \(q\), a contradiction. Thus the density and a maximizing subset are computable by a polynomial-size rational LP followed by polynomially many subset evaluations.

The equilibrium construction. Maintain the remaining types and goods. If there are remaining types, compute their maximum density \(q\).

When \(q>1\), choose a maximizing subset \(T\), put \(H=N(T)\), and:

  • Set the prices of all goods in \(H\) to \(q\).
  • Assign each type \(a\in T\) an aggregate amount \(w_a/q\) of liked goods from \(H\).
  • Remove \(T\) and \(H\), and repeat.

The assignment in step 2 exists. For every \(S\subseteq T\), maximality of \(q\) gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{w(S)}q\le s(N(S)).\)

All these neighbors belong to \(H\). The weighted matching criterion therefore applies. Furthermore,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\in T}\frac{w_a}{q}=s(H),\)

so the assignment completely exhausts the supplies in \(H\).

Every remaining type still has a remaining neighbor. Otherwise, a type \(a\notin T\) would have all its current neighbors in \(H\); adding it to \(T\) would leave the neighborhood unchanged and strictly increase the density.

The successive positive prices are nonincreasing. Indeed, for any nonempty subset \(S\) of the types remaining after removing \(T,H\), maximality of \(q\), applied before removal, gives

\(\displaystyle w(T)+w(S)\le q\bigl(s(H)+s(N_{\mathrm{remaining}}(S))\bigr).\)

Using \(w(T)=q\,s(H)\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle w(S)\le q\,s(N_{\mathrm{remaining}}(S)).\)

Thus the next maximum density is at most \(q\).

When the remaining maximum density is at most \(1\), assign every remaining type its full aggregate amount \(w_a\) along remaining liked edges. The matching criterion applies because \(w(S)\le s(N(S))\). If no types remain, this assignment is empty. Set all remaining goods’ prices to zero.

Finally, distribute the leftover zero-price goods to fill the removed types’ bundle deficits. To verify that this is possible, let \(f_{aj}\) denote the assignments made so far, and define

\(\displaystyle d_a=w_a-\sum_j f_{aj},\qquad e_j=s_j-\sum_a f_{aj}.\)

Both vectors are nonnegative, and equal total mass implies

\(\displaystyle E:=\sum_a d_a=\sum_j e_j.\)

If \(E>0\), add \(d_a e_j/E\) to each entry \(f_{aj}\). If \(E=0\), no addition is needed. This gives aggregate row sums \(w_a\) and column sums \(s_j\). Define the per-capita bundle \(y_{aj}=f_{aj}/w_a\).

There is at least one zero-price good. If no block was removed, all goods have price zero. Otherwise, each removed block satisfies \(w(T)>s(H)\); hence, after all removals,

\(\displaystyle s(J_{\mathrm{remaining}})-w(A_{\mathrm{remaining}})=\sum_{\text{removed blocks}}\bigl(w(T)-s(H)\bigr)>0.\)

The remaining goods therefore have positive total supply.

We now check individual optimality. A type removed at price \(q>1\) likes only goods removed at its own stage or earlier stages. All its liked goods therefore cost at least \(q\). Any affordable bundle has liked fraction at most \(1/q\). Its assigned bundle has exactly that liked fraction, costs exactly \(1\), and is completed with zero-price goods. The completion goods are disliked, because none of its liked goods remained after its removal. Thus its bundle is optimal.

Every remaining type receives a unit bundle consisting entirely of liked zero-price goods, attaining utility \(1\), which is optimal.

This proves the weighted dichotomous equilibrium lemma, including exact clearing, exact individual optimality, budgets at most \(1\), and minimum price zero.

3. The threshold market and its utility bound. Return to the market in the question. Fix a type \(r\), and abbreviate

\(\displaystyle a_k=u_r^{(k)},\qquad \Delta_k=a_k-a_{k-1},\qquad s=k_r^*,\qquad t=t_r.\)

The index \(s\) exists because \(\Delta_1/a_1=1\). Define

\(\displaystyle S_r=\sum_{k=s+1}^{t}\frac{\Delta_k}{a_k}.\)

Maximality of \(s\) gives \(S_r<1\): if \(s<t\), the defining inequality fails at \(s+1\); if \(s=t\), this is an empty sum.

Choose rational weights

\(\displaystyle \lambda_{rk}= \begin{cases} 0,&k<s,\\ 1-S_r,&k=s,\\ \Delta_k/a_k,&k>s. \end{cases}\)

They are nonnegative and sum to \(1\). The defining inequality at \(s\) also gives

\(\displaystyle \lambda_{rs}=1-S_r\le\frac{\Delta_s}{a_s}.\)

For every pair \((r,k)\) with positive weight, create one dichotomous type of mass

\(\displaystyle w_{rk}=\mu_r\lambda_{rk}\)

and utilities

\(\displaystyle v_{rk,j}=\mathbf 1\{u_{rj}\ge u_r^{(k)}\}.\)

Keep the original supplies \(\sigma_j\). These masses sum to \(1\), and every threshold type likes at least one good, because \(\max_j u_{rj}=1\). The weighted dichotomous lemma therefore applies. Let its equilibrium be \((p,y)\), with per-capita bundles \(y_{rk,j}\).

Return the same prices and average the threshold bundles:

\(\displaystyle x_{rj}=\sum_{k:\lambda_{rk}>0}\lambda_{rk}y_{rk,j}.\)

Bundle sizes and budgets satisfy

\(\displaystyle \sum_j x_{rj}=1,\qquad \sum_jp_jx_{rj}\le1.\)

Market clearing follows directly:

\(\displaystyle \sum_r\mu_rx_{rj}=\sum_{r,k:\lambda_{rk}>0}w_{rk}y_{rk,j}=\sigma_j.\)

The minimum price remains zero.

It remains to prove the utility guarantee. For all thresholds, including those assigned zero weight, define

\(\displaystyle W_{rk}(p)=\max\left\{\sum_jv_{rk,j}z_j:z_j\ge0,\ \sum_jz_j=1,\ \sum_jp_jz_j\le1\right\}.\)

These maxima exist because minimum price zero makes the feasible region nonempty, and that region is a closed subset of the simplex. Also, \(0\le W_{rk}(p)\le1\).

The threshold identity

\(\displaystyle u_{rj}=\sum_{k=1}^{t}\Delta_kv_{rk,j}\)

implies, by bounding each threshold utility separately on every feasible bundle,

\(\displaystyle V_r(p)\le\sum_{k=1}^{t}\Delta_kW_{rk}(p).\)

For every positive-weight threshold type, exact dichotomous optimality and nonnegative original utilities give

\(\displaystyle \sum_j u_{rj}y_{rk,j}\ge a_k\sum_jv_{rk,j}y_{rk,j}=a_kW_{rk}(p).\)

Therefore,

\(\displaystyle V_r(p)-\sum_j u_{rj}x_{rj}\le\sum_{k=1}^{t}\bigl(\Delta_k-\lambda_{rk}a_k\bigr)W_{rk}(p).\)

Every coefficient on the right is nonnegative. For \(k<s\), it equals \(\Delta_k\); for \(k>s\), it equals zero; and for \(k=s\), nonnegativity follows from \(\lambda_{rs}\le\Delta_s/a_s\). Using \(W_{rk}(p)\le1\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle V_r(p)-\sum_j u_{rj}x_{rj}\le\sum_{k=1}^{t}\bigl(\Delta_k-\lambda_{rk}a_k\bigr)=a_sS_r.\)

To bound this last quantity, put \(d=t-s+1\). If \(d=1\), then \(S_r=0\), so the bound is zero.

If \(d>1\), let \(b_k=a_{k-1}/a_k\) for \(k=s+1,\ldots,t\). Since \(a_t=1\),

\(\displaystyle a_s=\prod_{k=s+1}^{t}b_k,\qquad S_r=\sum_{k=s+1}^{t}(1-b_k).\)

The \(d\) nonnegative numbers \(S_r,b_{s+1},\ldots,b_t\) have sum \(d-1\). The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality yields

\(\displaystyle a_sS_r=S_r\prod_{k=s+1}^{t}b_k\le\left(\frac{d-1}{d}\right)^d.\)

Thus, for every original type,

\(\displaystyle \sum_j u_{rj}x_{rj}\ge V_r(p)-\left(1-\frac1{t_r-k_r^*+1}\right)^{t_r-k_r^*+1}\ge V_r(p)-B.\)

All required conditions hold with \(\eta=B\).

4. Polynomial running time and rational output. There are at most

\(\displaystyle K=\sum_r t_r\le\tau(m-1)\)

threshold types. Their weights have polynomial binary encoding length: they are formed by polynomially many rational additions, divisions and multiplications from the input.

The weighted algorithm removes at least one good at every positive-price stage, so there are at most \(m\) such stages. Each density LP has at most \(K+m\) variables and \(Km\) edge constraints. Every stage price is a ratio of sums of the original threshold masses and original supplies, and consequently has polynomial binary length.

We use the standard polynomial-time algorithms for rational linear programming and rational maximum flow, which return rational solutions of polynomial encoding length. Their instances here have polynomial size. The final filling and averaging involve only polynomially many rational arithmetic operations on these solutions. Hence the returned prices and bundles also have polynomial encoding length and are computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

In particular, this running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\delta\), and \(\eta=B\le B+\delta\).
