The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu\), type-count embedded rule templates with positive or negative weights, a maximum coalition size \(B\), and rational \(\varepsilon>0\), compute the supremum normalized welfare and return a feasible solution within \(\varepsilon\). Use variables \(\lambda_q\ge 0\) for coalition-composition frequencies and \(\delta_q\in\{0,1\}\) for compositions occurring only sublinearly often, with \(\sum_q q_t\lambda_q=\mu_t\) for every type \(t\); a rule applies to a focal composition when its focal condition matches and every external condition matches some other composition in the support \(\mathcal S=\{q:\lambda_q>0\text{ or }\delta_q=1\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Given a binary-encoded integer \(B\ge1\), let \(Q=\{q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\tau}:1\le\sum_tq_t\le B\}\); compositions are specified implicitly through this definition.
There are \(m\) embedded rule templates \(I_{r,0}\mid I_{r,1},\ldots,I_{r,k_r}\to w_r\), with \(k_r\ge0\) and nonzero binary-rational weights \(w_r\) of either sign. A condition \(I\) is specified by integer bounds \(0\le\ell_t\le u_t\le B\) and matches \(q\) exactly when \(\ell_t\le q_t\le u_t\) for every \(t\); require \(\sum_t\ell_t\ge1\).
Templates are in explicit form: no composition in \(Q\) matches both \(I_{r,0}\) and any \(I_{r,i}\) with \(i\ge1\).
A feasible solution has real frequencies \(\lambda_q\ge0\) and indicators \(\delta_q\in\{0,1\}\), with \(\delta_q=0\) whenever \(\lambda_q>0\), and \(\sum_{q\in Q}q_t\lambda_q=\mu_t\) for every \(t\). An indicator \(\delta_q=1\) records a composition present with zero limiting frequency; it contributes neither mass nor normalized welfare.
Let \(\mathcal S=\{q:\lambda_q>0\text{ or }\delta_q=1\}\). Rule \(r\) applies to \(q\) when \(I_{r,0}\) matches \(q\) and each external condition matches some \(p\in\mathcal S\setminus\{q\}\); different external conditions may share a witness.
Define \(v(q,\mathcal S)=\sum_{r\text{ applies to }q}w_r\), \(W(\lambda,\delta)=\sum_q\lambda_qv(q,\mathcal S)\), and \(W^*=\sup_{(\lambda,\delta)\text{ feasible}}W(\lambda,\delta)\). This is welfare per unit population; absent rules contribute zero.
Target. Given binary-rational \(\varepsilon>0\), is computing \(W^*\) and a rational feasible solution with \(W\ge W^*-\varepsilon\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,B,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, including \(B\), all conditions and \(\varepsilon\); polynomial time means polynomial in the encoded input length.
Nearest known results. For finite societies, a weighted partial MaxSAT encoding is proved correct for negative-weight embedded MC-net rules in explicit form, including the constant welfare offset ([Liao et al.](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/transinf/E97.D/7/E97.D_1781/_pdf)).
Without externalities, finite type-based MC-net coalition structure generation admits an \(O(n^{2\tau})\) algorithm for \(n\) agents ([Ueda et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/11/Papers/074.pdf)). No result settling the exact continuous problem above was identified in the searches.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized masses to sum to one and defined normalized welfare per unit population.
  • Defined compositions as nonempty integer type-count vectors of size at most B, with B binary encoded and compositions implicit.
  • Specified type-count conditions using coordinatewise integer intervals, following the verified type-based MC-net definition; required a positive lower bound somewhere to retain the positive-literal convention.
  • Carried over the anchor's explicit-form condition by requiring focal and external conditions to have disjoint matching sets. This makes the graded question's exclusion of the focal composition faithful to the exclusion of the focal coalition.
  • Allowed different external conditions to share a witness, as the discrete definition permits.
  • Made zero-frequency indicators contribute neither mass nor welfare and restricted them to compositions with zero frequency.
  • Specified rational output solutions, signed rational weights, zero contribution from absent rules, and binary encoding of the accuracy parameter.

Result

The task is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when there are no external conditions, every weight belongs to \(\{-1,1\}\), all masses are dyadic rationals, and \(B\) is linear in \(\tau\).

The hardness applies both to computing \(W^*\) exactly and to producing the requested additive approximation. Moreover, the supremum is always attained by a rational feasible solution with polynomial encoding length. Thus hardness persists even though a small exact solution always exists.

1. A reduction from satisfiability

Let \(\Phi\) be a Boolean formula in conjunctive normal form with variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_c\), each containing at most three literals. We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete satisfiability problem for such formulas.

We may assume \(n\ge1\), every clause is nonempty, and no clause contains both a variable and its negation: tautological clauses can be deleted, and repeated literals can be removed.

Society and composition bound

Introduce \(\tau=n+2\) types, indexed by \(0,1,\ldots,n,d\). Type \(0\) will mark compositions encoding assignments; type \(d\) absorbs the remaining population mass.

Set
\( h=\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil,\qquad \alpha=2^{-h},\qquad B=n+1. \)

Define the masses by
\( \mu_0=\mu_1=\cdots=\mu_n=\alpha, \qquad \mu_d=1-(n+1)\alpha. \)

Because \(2^h\ge n+2\), we have \(\mu_d\ge\alpha>0\). All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and their sum is \(1\).

Rules

Every rule has \(k_r=0\), so indicators and external witnesses have no effect on welfare.

First introduce a rule of weight \(+1\) whose condition is
\( q_0=1,\qquad 0\le q_i\le1\quad(1\le i\le n),\qquad q_d=0. \)

Write \(A\) for the compositions matching this condition. Its members are exactly
\( q(a)=(1,a_1,\ldots,a_n,0), \qquad a\in\{0,1\}^n. \)
Every such composition belongs to \(Q\), since its size lies between \(1\) and \(n+1=B\).

For each clause \(C_j\), introduce a rule of weight \(-1\). Start with the same bounds as the positive rule and additionally require:

  • \(q_i=0\) if the literal \(x_i\) occurs in \(C_j\);
  • \(q_i=1\) if the literal \(\neg x_i\) occurs in \(C_j\).

This is a valid box condition because the clause is not tautological. It matches \(q(a)\) precisely when assignment \(a\) falsifies \(C_j\).

Every condition has lower bound \(q_0\ge1\), so the required inequality \(\sum_t\ell_t\ge1\) holds. Explicit form is automatic because there are no external conditions.

If \(F(a)\) denotes the number of clauses falsified by \(a\), the resulting value function is
\( v(q(a))=1-F(a), \qquad v(q)=0\quad\text{for }q\notin A. \)

The construction has \(m=c+1\) rules. Writing their coordinate bounds explicitly takes polynomial time in the formula length.

2. Exact welfare gap

We prove
\( W^*= \begin{cases} \alpha,&\text{if }\Phi\text{ is satisfiable},\\ 0,&\text{if }\Phi\text{ is unsatisfiable}. \end{cases} \)

Universal upper bound

For every feasible solution, \(v(q)\le1\) on \(A\) and \(v(q)=0\) outside \(A\). Consequently,
\( W\le\sum_{q\in A}\lambda_q. \)

Every composition in \(A\) contains exactly one unit of type \(0\). The type-\(0\) population constraint therefore gives
\( \sum_{q\in A}\lambda_q \le\sum_{q\in Q}q_0\lambda_q =\mu_0=\alpha. \)
Thus \(W\le\alpha\).

Satisfiable formulas attain \(\alpha\)

Suppose \(a\) satisfies \(\Phi\). Give \(q(a)\) frequency \(\alpha\). For each variable with \(a_i=0\), give the singleton composition \(e_i\) frequency \(\alpha\). Give \(e_d\) frequency \(\mu_d\), and set all other frequencies and indicators to zero.

The assignment composition supplies all type-\(0\) mass. For each variable type, it supplies mass \(\alpha\) when \(a_i=1\), while its singleton supplies mass \(\alpha\) when \(a_i=0\). The dummy singleton supplies \(\mu_d\). Hence the solution is feasible.

Only \(q(a)\) has nonzero value, and its value is \(1\). Therefore \(W=\alpha\).

Unsatisfiable formulas have optimum zero

If \(\Phi\) is unsatisfiable, then \(F(a)\ge1\) for every assignment. Hence \(v(q)\le0\) for every composition, giving \(W\le0\).

Zero is attainable. Give composition \(2e_0\) frequency \(\alpha/2\), each \(e_i\) with \(1\le i\le n\) frequency \(\alpha\), and \(e_d\) frequency \(\mu_d\).

These frequencies meet every population constraint. Also, \(2e_0\in Q\), since \(B=n+1\ge2\). None of these compositions matches any rule: \(2e_0\) has marker coordinate \(2\), and the other singletons have marker coordinate \(0\). Thus their welfare is zero.

This proves the claimed gap.

3. Hardness of the requested approximation

Supply the constructed instance with
\( \varepsilon=\alpha/4. \)

A feasible solution satisfying \(W\ge W^*-\varepsilon\) must have:

  • \(W\ge3\alpha/4\) when \(\Phi\) is satisfiable;
  • \(W\le0\) when \(\Phi\) is unsatisfiable.

Thus computing such a solution decides satisfiability by comparing its welfare with \(\alpha/2\). Welfare can be evaluated exactly from an explicitly listed rational solution in polynomial time.

The gap is inverse-polynomial: \(2^h<2(n+2)\), so
\( \alpha>\frac{1}{2(n+2)}. \)
In particular, \(\varepsilon\) has only \(O(\log n)\) encoding bits.

Therefore both tasks in the question are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A polynomial-time algorithm for either would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

There is also hardness for the fixed accuracy \(\varepsilon=1/4\): multiply every rule weight by \(\alpha^{-1}=2^h\). The two optimum values become \(1\) and \(0\). These new integer weights still have polynomial encoding length.

4. Attainment and polynomial-size rational solutions

These properties hold for every instance, including instances with externalities.

Let
\( K=\sum_{r=1}^m k_r \)
be the number of external-condition occurrences, and let
\( N=|Q|=\binom{B+\tau}{\tau}-1. \)

The supremum is attained

For each subset \(S\subseteq Q\), consider the polytope
\( P_S= \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{S}: \sum_{q\in S}q_t\lambda_q=\mu_t \text{ for every }t \right\}. \)

Every point in \(P_S\) can realize exactly the presence set \(S\): assign indicator \(1\) to the members of \(S\) with zero frequency, and indicator \(0\) elsewhere.

For fixed \(S\), the values \(v(q,S)\) are fixed, so welfare is a linear function on \(P_S\). Moreover, summing the population equations yields
\( 1=\sum_{q\in S}\left(\sum_tq_t\right)\lambda_q \ge\sum_{q\in S}\lambda_q. \)
Thus \(P_S\) is bounded. It is also closed, so every nonempty \(P_S\) has a welfare maximizer.

There are finitely many subsets \(S\). At least one is feasible, because singleton frequencies \(\lambda_{e_t}=\mu_t\) satisfy the population equations. Taking the largest of the finitely many polytope maxima proves that \(W^*\) is attained.

At most \(\tau\) positive frequencies are needed

Fix an optimal presence set \(S\), and choose a welfare-maximizing point of \(P_S\) with the fewest positive coordinates.

Its positive composition columns must be linearly independent. Indeed, suppose there were a nonzero dependence vector \(d\) on those columns. Both \(\lambda+\eta d\) and \(\lambda-\eta d\) would be feasible for sufficiently small \(\eta>0\). Optimality in both directions forces
\( \sum_q v(q,S)d_q=0. \)

A nonzero dependence among nonzero nonnegative columns has both positive and negative coefficients. Increasing the perturbation in one direction until a coordinate first reaches zero therefore preserves feasibility and welfare while reducing the positive support, a contradiction.

Hence there are at most \(\tau\) positive frequencies.

At most \(K\) zero-frequency witnesses are needed

Explicit form implies the following useful equivalence: if \(I_{r,0}\) matches \(q\), then an external condition \(I_{r,i}\) matches some member of \(S\setminus\{q\}\) exactly when it matches some member of \(S\). The reason is that \(q\) itself cannot match \(I_{r,i}\).

For each external-condition occurrence that matches some member of \(S\), retain one such witness. Retain all positive-frequency compositions as well, and remove all other zero-frequency compositions.

Every external condition that was present remains present; every absent condition remains absent. By the equivalence above, rule applicability on every positive-frequency composition is unchanged. Welfare and feasibility are therefore preserved.

The resulting optimal solution has at most \(\tau+K\) present compositions.

Rational encoding length is polynomial

Let \(s\le\tau\) be the number of positive frequencies. Their composition columns are independent, so some \(s\) rows form a nonsingular integer matrix \(A\). Its entries lie between \(0\) and \(B\).

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Cramer’s rule expresses each positive frequency as
\( \lambda_j=\frac{\det A_j}{D\det A}, \)
where \(A_j\) replaces one column of \(A\) by the corresponding entries of \(D\mu\).

The determinant bounds
\( |\det A|\le s!B^s, \qquad |\det A_j|\le s!DB^{s-1} \)
show that every frequency has encoding length
\( O(L+s\log B+s\log s). \)

The compositions themselves require \(O(\tau\log(B+1))\) bits each. Thus an optimal rational solution, represented by its nonzero frequencies and indicators, has polynomial total encoding length. Its welfare \(W^*\) also has polynomial encoding length, since it is a sum of polynomially many products of these rationals and input weights.

5. Decision complexity and an exact running-time bound

The threshold problem “is \(W^*\ge a\)?” for binary-rational \(a\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Membership follows from the polynomial-size optimal certificate just proved. One can check its population equations, indicators, condition matches, rule applicability, and welfare comparison using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. Hardness follows from the reduction with threshold \(a=\alpha/2\).

An exact algorithm for the optimization problem has running time
\( 2^K\operatorname{poly}(N,m,\tau,L), \qquad N=\binom{B+\tau}{\tau}-1. \)

Here is a construction verifying that bound.

Index the external-condition occurrences by \(j=1,\ldots,K\). Enumerate vectors \(e\in\{0,1\}^K\), interpreting \(e_j=1\) as presence of a witness for condition \(j\). Define
\( A_e=\{q\in Q: q\text{ matches none of the external conditions with }e_j=0\}. \)

Reject a vector if some condition with \(e_j=1\) has no matching composition in \(A_e\). Otherwise choose one witness in \(A_e\) for each such condition.

For \(q\in A_e\), define
\( c_e(q)= \sum_{\substack{r:\ I_{r,0}\text{ matches }q\\ e_j=1\text{ for all external occurrences of }r}} w_r. \)

Solve the rational linear program
\( \max_{\lambda\ge0} \left\{ \sum_{q\in A_e}c_e(q)\lambda_q: \sum_{q\in A_e}q_t\lambda_q=\mu_t \text{ for every }t \right\}. \)

Every feasible presence set induces one enumerated vector and yields a feasible point of its linear program with the same welfare. Conversely, any feasible point of this linear program, together with the chosen witnesses, realizes exactly the vector \(e\). Explicit form ensures that its welfare equals the linear-program objective.

Therefore the largest feasible linear-program optimum is exactly \(W^*\). Each program is bounded by the population argument above, has rational coefficients of polynomial bit length, and can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its explicit input length.

Since \(K\le L\) and \(N<(B+1)^\tau\), a bound stated solely in the requested parameters is
\( 2^L\operatorname{poly}\!\left((B+1)^\tau,m,\tau,L\right). \)

With no externalities, this becomes \(\operatorname{poly}(N,m,\tau,L)\). That is polynomial in the number of explicitly enumerated compositions; it does not imply polynomial time in the encoded input length.
