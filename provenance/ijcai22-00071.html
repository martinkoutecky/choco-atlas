The continuous mirror question, as graded

Prover output (gpt-5.6-sol / xhigh, Bolzano prover prompt)

Complexity Classification

Under the standard support-list encoding—each positive-probability subset and its rational mass are listed—the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, allowing fractional transport plans does not remove the hardness.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate consists only of a candidate bijection \(\sigma:C\to D\).

For fixed \(\sigma\), the minimum-cost transport plan is the rational transportation linear program

\(\min \sum_{A,B} |\sigma(A)\triangle B|\pi_{A,B}\)

subject to the stated marginal constraints. Its number of variables is the product of the two listed support sizes, and it is solvable exactly in polynomial time by a min-cost-flow or rational linear-programming algorithm. The optimum can then be compared exactly with \(\delta\).

Thus the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Source Problem

We reduce from Balanced Complete Bipartite Subgraph:

Given a bipartite graph \(G=(L\mathbin{\dot\cup}R,E)\) and \(q\geq 1\), decide whether there are \(S\subseteq L\) and \(T\subseteq R\) with \(|S|=|T|=q\) and \(S\times T\subseteq E\).

This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; see Johnson’s [NP-completeness column](https://doi.org/10.1016/0196-6774(87)90021-6). Write \(n=|L|\) and \(m=|R|\), and assume \(q\leq\min\{n,m\}\). If \(|E|<q^2\), the instance is automatically negative and may be mapped to any fixed negative instance. Hence assume \(|E|\geq q^2\).

Construction

Let \(C\) be a copy of \(R\), and let \(D\) be a disjoint set of \(m\) candidates. Fix an arbitrary set \(T_0\subseteq D\) of size \(q\).

For every \(x\in L\), define its approval set

\(A_x=\{c_r\in C:\{x,r\}\in E\}\).

For each \(A\subseteq C\), let

\(a_A=|\{x\in L:A_x=A\}|\).

Define the first distribution by

\(\mu_A=a_A/n\)

for every \(A\) with \(a_A>0\). Thus \(\mu\) is the empirical distribution of the neighborhoods of the vertices in \(L\).

Define the second distribution by

\(\nu_{T_0}=q/n\), \(\nu_{\varnothing}=(n-q)/n\).

If \(q=n\), the zero-mass atom \(\varnothing\) is simply omitted. Finally, set

\(\delta=(|E|-q^2)/n\).

The construction has at most \(n\) distinct atoms in the support of \(\mu\), at most two atoms in the support of \(\nu\), and polynomial encoding length.

Exact Cost Identity

Fix a bijection \(\sigma:C\to D\) and a feasible transport plan \(\pi\). Since \(\nu\) is supported on \(T_0\) and \(\varnothing\), every other transport variable is zero.

Set

\(T=\sigma^{-1}(T_0)\).

Then \(|T|=q\). For each source atom \(A\), put

\(z_A=n\pi_{A,T_0}\).

The marginal constraints imply

\(0\leq z_A\leq a_A\)

and

\(\sum_A z_A=q\).

Because \(\sigma\) preserves cardinalities and intersections,

\(|\sigma(A)\triangle T_0|=|A|+q-2|A\cap T|\),

whereas

\(|\sigma(A)\triangle\varnothing|=|A|\).

Consequently, after multiplying the transport cost by \(n\), we obtain

\(n\,\operatorname{cost}(\sigma,\pi)=\sum_A z_A(|A|+q-2|A\cap T|)+\sum_A(a_A-z_A)|A|\).

Since \(\sum_A a_A|A|=|E|\) and \(\sum_A z_A=q\), this simplifies to

\(n\,\operatorname{cost}(\sigma,\pi)=|E|+q^2-2\sum_A z_A|A\cap T|\).

This identity holds for arbitrary fractional plans.

Forward Implication

Suppose \(G\) contains a \(K_{q,q}\) with sides \(S\subseteq L\) and \(T\subseteq R\).

Choose \(\sigma\) so that the copy of \(T\) in \(C\) is mapped onto \(T_0\). Transport the mass corresponding to the \(q\) vertices of \(S\) to \(T_0\), and transport all remaining mass to \(\varnothing\).

For the resulting \(z_A\),

\(\sum_A z_A|A\cap T|=q^2\),

because every vertex of \(S\) is adjacent to every vertex of \(T\). The cost identity therefore gives

\(\operatorname{cost}(\sigma,\pi)=(|E|-q^2)/n=\delta\).

Thus the constructed instance is positive.

Reverse Implication

Suppose some \(\sigma\) and transport plan \(\pi\) have cost at most \(\delta\). The cost identity gives

\(|E|+q^2-2\sum_A z_A|A\cap T|\leq |E|-q^2\),

and hence

\(\sum_A z_A|A\cap T|\geq q^2\).

On the other hand, \(|A\cap T|\leq q\) for every \(A\), so

\(\sum_A z_A|A\cap T|\leq q\sum_A z_A=q^2\).

Equality must therefore hold. Equivalently,

\(\sum_A z_A\bigl(q-|A\cap T|\bigr)=0\).

Every summand is nonnegative. Hence \(z_A>0\) implies \(|A\cap T|=q\), that is, \(T\subseteq A\).

Moreover,

\(\sum_{A\supseteq T}a_A\geq\sum_{A\supseteq T}z_A=q\).

The left-hand side is exactly the number of vertices \(x\in L\) whose neighborhood contains all of \(T\). Therefore at least \(q\) vertices of \(L\) are adjacent to every vertex of \(T\). Choosing any \(q\) of them produces a \(K_{q,q}\) in \(G\).

Thus the original Balanced Complete Bipartite Subgraph instance is positive.

Why Fractional Transport Does Not Help

The reverse implication did not require transport-polytope integrality: attaining the threshold forces all mass sent to \(T_0\) to come from neighborhoods containing all \(q\) selected candidates.

More generally, for two \(n\)-voter approval elections, let \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) be their empirical ballot distributions. For every fixed \(\sigma\),

\(n\min_{\pi}\sum_{A,B}|\sigma(A)\triangle B|\pi_{A,B}=\min_{\rho\in S_n}\sum_{i=1}^n|\sigma(A_i)\triangle B_{\rho(i)}|\).

Indeed, after scaling a transport plan by \(n\), its row and column margins are integral ballot multiplicities. The transportation constraint matrix is totally unimodular, so an optimal scaled plan is integral and can be expanded into a bijective voter matching. This formally validates the reduction route from the known finite-voter isomorphic Hamming problem, whose hardness is also stated by [Szufa et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0071.pdf).

Conclusion

The reduction is polynomial and preserves positive instances in both directions. Together with membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), the distributional decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when \(\nu\) has support size at most two and every probability is an integer multiple of \(1/n\).

If “explicitly listed” were instead intended to require a dense table containing all \(2^m\) zero entries, that would be a different encoding; the polynomial reduction above applies to the standard list-of-positive-atoms encoding.

Overall assessment

The cited source problem is indeed the standard Balanced Complete Bipartite Subgraph problem; the supplied DOI resolves to Johnson’s relevant NP-completeness column ([publisher entry](https://doi.org/10.1016/0196-6774(87)90021-6)).

Step-by-step verification

1. Membership in \( \mathrm{NP} \)

For a listed bijection \( \sigma \), the remaining optimization is a transportation linear program with one variable for each pair of listed support atoms. Thus its dimensions are polynomial in the support-list input length.

The coefficients of the objective are integers bounded by \( 2m \), while the marginal values and \( \delta \) are rational inputs. Exact rational linear programming, or min-cost flow after standard rational scaling, permits polynomial-time comparison of the optimum with \( \delta \). Therefore \( \sigma \) alone is a valid polynomial-size certificate.

This argument is correct under the convention that omitted subsets have probability zero.

2. Validity and size of the construction

The construction has \( |C|=|D|=m=|R| \), as required.

The first distribution is valid because

\( \sum_A a_A=n \),

so \( \sum_A\mu_A=1 \). The second is valid because \( q\leq n \) and

\( q/n+(n-q)/n=1 \).

After the stated preprocessing \( |E|\geq q^2 \), the threshold \( \delta=(|E|-q^2)/n \) is nonnegative, although nonnegativity is not required by the original definition.

Each neighborhood is represented by an \( m \)-bit subset. There are at most \( n \) distinct neighborhoods, so the construction has polynomial output length in a support-list representation.

3. Transportation variables

For fixed \( \sigma \), defining \( T=\sigma^{-1}(T_0) \) gives \( |T|=q \).

Since the target support consists only of \( T_0 \) and possibly \( \varnothing \), the definition

\( z_A=n\pi_{A,T_0} \)

correctly yields

\( 0\leq z_A\leq a_A \)

from the row marginal, and

\( \sum_Az_A=q \)

from the \( T_0 \)-column marginal. These statements remain valid when \( q=n \), in which case all source mass is transported to \( T_0 \).

4. Cost identity

The identities

\( |\sigma(A)\mathbin{\triangle}T_0|=|A|+q-2|A\cap T| \)

and

\( |\sigma(A)\mathbin{\triangle}\varnothing|=|A| \)

are correct because \( \sigma \) preserves cardinalities and intersections.

Substitution gives

\( n\,\operatorname{cost}(\sigma,\pi)=\sum_A z_A(|A|+q-2|A\cap T|)+\sum_A(a_A-z_A)|A| \).

The \( z_A|A| \) terms cancel. Using

\( \sum_Aa_A|A|=|E| \)

and \( \sum_Az_A=q \) gives exactly

\( n\,\operatorname{cost}(\sigma,\pi)=|E|+q^2-2\sum_Az_A|A\cap T| \).

No integrality of \( z_A \) is used.

5. Forward implication

Given a \( K_{q,q} \) with sides \( S \) and \( T \), the proposed \( \sigma \) exists because both \( T \) and \( T_0 \) have size \( q \).

Grouping the selected vertices of \( S \) by identical neighborhoods defines feasible values \( z_A \leq a_A \) with total \( q \). Every selected neighborhood contains \( T \), so each selected unit contributes \( q \), yielding

\( \sum_Az_A|A\cap T|=q^2 \).

The cost identity then gives \( \operatorname{cost}=\delta \). This implication is correct.

6. Reverse implication

From \( \operatorname{cost}\leq\delta \), the cost identity correctly implies

\( \sum_Az_A|A\cap T|\geq q^2 \).

Since \( |A\cap T|\leq q \) and \( \sum_Az_A=q \), the reverse inequality

\( \sum_Az_A|A\cap T|\leq q^2 \)

also holds. Hence equality is forced.

Because each term in

\( \sum_Az_A(q-|A\cap T|)=0 \)

is nonnegative, \( z_A>0 \) implies \( |A\cap T|=q \), equivalently \( T\subseteq A \).

All transported mass therefore comes from atoms containing \( T \), and

\( \sum_{A\supseteq T}a_A\geq\sum_{A\supseteq T}z_A=q \).

The left side counts precisely the vertices of \( L \) adjacent to every member of \( T \). Choosing any \( q \) such vertices gives the required \( K_{q,q} \). This reasoning is valid for fractional \( z_A \).

7. General integrality observation

The optional final observation about empirical distributions is also correct. Scaling by \( n \) produces integral row and column margins. The transportation constraint matrix is totally unimodular, so an integral optimal solution exists and can be expanded into a bijective matching of the individual voters.

This fact is not required for the preceding reduction.
