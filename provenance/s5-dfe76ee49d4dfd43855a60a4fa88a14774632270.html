Theorem 4 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given sampling access to a population law \(\mu\) over complete rankings \(\pi\in S_n\), with rational type masses and generated by an iid RUM with latent utilities \(\theta\) and minimum-advantage sensitivity \(c\), each round choose \(S\subseteq[n]\), \(|S|=k\), and observe a fresh respondent’s top-ranked item in \(S\). What is the minimax expected number of interviews needed to return an item \(i\) with \(\theta_i\ge\max_j\theta_j-\epsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\), and does Seq-PB attain the rate \(O\!\left(\frac{n}{c^2\epsilon^2}\log\frac{k}{\delta}\right)\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) strict complete rankings of \(m\) items with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; masses sum to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings, and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Assume \(m>2\), \(2\le k\le m\), and rational parameters \(0<\epsilon\le 1/2\), \(0<\delta<1/2\), and \(0<c<1/2\). The supplied law \(\mu\) is promised to arise by ranking \(X_i=\theta_i+\zeta_i\), where \(\theta\in\mathbb R^m\) is unknown and the noises are iid from an unknown distribution having a density; hence utility ties have probability zero.
Write \(p_i(S)=\mu\{\pi:i\text{ is highest in }S\}\). Every compatible latent representation must satisfy \(p_i(S)\ge[1+4c(\theta_i-\theta_j)/(1-2c)]p_j(S)\) whenever \(\theta_i>\theta_j\) and \(i,j\in S\), \(|S|=k\).
An interview chooses exactly \(k\) distinct items and observes only their highest-ranked item under an independent fresh draw from \(\mu\). An algorithm may also read the supplied support and masses, must stop almost surely, and must output \(i\) satisfying \(\theta_i\ge\max_j\theta_j-\epsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\), for every compatible latent representation.
The minimax interview cost is the infimum, over algorithms satisfying this requirement on every promised input, of their worst-case expected number of interviews; access to the supplied input does not count as an interview. State computational running times in \(m,\tau,L,k,c,\epsilon,\delta\).

Is finding such an item from the explicit support solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?
What is the minimax expected number of interviews when the support and masses are supplied?

For unknown iid RUMs with winner feedback, Seq-PB has the established upper bound \(O(m(c^2\epsilon^2)^{-1}\log(k/\delta))\), and a worst-case lower bound is reported up to a logarithmic factor. These are sampling results, rather than results for explicitly supplied ranking masses. The upper bound already covers fresh respondents sampled from any ranking law induced by the stipulated RUM. [Source](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/aadirupa-saha20a/aadirupa-saha20a.pdf).

Result

The task is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, the explicit support allows a deterministic algorithm to find an exact latent-utility maximizer for every compatible representation. The minimax expected number of interviews is \(0\).

The proof uses the iid noise assumption and the existence of a density. It does not require the additional advantage inequality.

1. Pairwise probabilities identify the latent order

For distinct items \(i,j\), define
\[ q_{ij}=\mu\{\pi:i\text{ precedes }j\}. \]
These probabilities can be computed directly from the supplied complete rankings, even when interviews are restricted to subsets of size \(k>2\).

Lemma. In every compatible representation,
\[ q_{ij}>\tfrac12\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\theta_i>\theta_j, \qquad q_{ij}=\tfrac12\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\theta_i=\theta_j. \]

Proof. Fix a compatible representation, and let \(U,V\) be independent draws from its noise distribution. Put \(D=V-U\). Exchangeability gives \(D\) and \(-D\) the same distribution. Moreover, \(D\) has no atoms: conditional on any value of \(U\), the density of \(V\) implies that \(V-U\) equals any specified real number with probability zero. Consequently,
\[ \Pr(D<0)=\Pr(D>0)=\tfrac12. \]

We next prove that \(\Pr(0<D<d)>0\) for every \(d>0\). Partition the real line into countably many half-open intervals of length \(d/2\). At least one interval \(I\) has positive noise probability \(a\), because their probabilities sum to one. Independence implies
\[ \Pr(U\in I,\ V\in I)=a^2>0. \]
On this event, \(|D|<d\). Since \(D\) has no atom at zero and is symmetric,
\[ \Pr(0<D<d)=\tfrac12\Pr(|D|<d)>0. \]
Thus \(\Pr(D<d)>\tfrac12\) for every \(d>0\). Symmetry and absence of atoms also give \(\Pr(D<-d)<\tfrac12\).

Finally, item \(i\) precedes item \(j\) exactly when
\[ \theta_i+U>\theta_j+V, \]
so
\[ q_{ij}=\Pr(D<\theta_i-\theta_j). \]
The preceding inequalities establish both equivalences. \(\square\)

In particular, all compatible latent representations have the same weak ordering of their utility coordinates.

2. A deterministic algorithm

Write the support as rankings \(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_\tau\), with masses \(a_1,\ldots,a_\tau\). Compute comparisons using
\[ q_{ij}=\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}a_r\,\mathbf 1\{i\text{ precedes }j\text{ in }\pi_r\}. \]

Use the following tournament:

  • Set the current candidate to item \(1\).
  • For each successive item \(j\), replace the current candidate \(b\) by \(j\) if \(q_{jb}>\tfrac12\).
  • Output the final candidate.

To verify correctness, fix any compatible representation. After processing the first item, the candidate maximizes utility among the processed items. Suppose this invariant holds before processing \(j\). By the lemma, replacement occurs exactly when \(\theta_j>\theta_b\). Whether replacement occurs or not, the resulting candidate therefore maximizes utility among all items processed so far. Induction proves that the final output \(b\) satisfies
\[ \theta_b=\max_i\theta_i. \]

The algorithm depends only on the supplied law. Since the representation fixed in this argument was arbitrary, its output is an exact maximizer simultaneously for every compatible representation. Hence it satisfies the required approximation guarantee with probability \(1\).

3. Computational running time

Here is a conservative bound in the standard bit-cost RAM model.

Precompute the position of every item in every supplied ranking. Express each mass as \(a_r=u_r/v_r\), and form the common denominator and integer weights
\[ B=\prod_{r=1}^{\tau}v_r, \qquad w_r=u_r(B/v_r). \]
Their bit lengths are \(O(L)\): the total encoding length of all denominators is at most \(L\), and \(0<w_r\le B\).

Each comparison is now the exact integer test
\[ 2\sum_{r:\,j\text{ precedes }b\text{ in }\pi_r}w_r>B. \]
There are \(m-1\) comparisons, each requiring at most \(\tau\) additions of \(O(L)\)-bit integers. Common-denominator preprocessing requires \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations using elementary multiplication and division. Including ranking preprocessing, a conservative total bound is \(O(m\tau L^2)\).

This is polynomial in the explicit input size. Its dependence on \(k,c,\epsilon,\delta\) is only through their contribution to \(L\); no factor involving their numerical reciprocals is needed.

4. Minimax interview cost

The algorithm performs no interviews and terminates deterministically. Its worst-case expected interview cost is therefore \(0\). Interview counts are nonnegative, so the minimax expected cost is exactly \(0\).

The stated Seq-PB sampling bounds concern acquiring information about an unknown law through winner observations. They do not impose a positive interview lower bound when complete ranking probabilities are supplied explicitly. See [Saha and Gopalan](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/aadirupa-saha20a/aadirupa-saha20a.pdf).
