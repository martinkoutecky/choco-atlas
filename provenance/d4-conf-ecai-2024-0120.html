The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each \(N\), let a society be a rational distribution \(\mu\) over finitely many locations \(x\in[0,1]\), with \(N\mu_x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\). Among deterministic rule families \(F_N\) that choose one facility location and remain strategyproof when a resident reports \(r\) instead of \(x\), changing the society to \(\mu+N^{-1}(\delta_r-\delta_x)\), determine the limiting Pareto frontier of worst-case approximation ratios for maximum distance and minimum utility as \(N\to\infty\). Does the pair \((2,3/2)\) remain optimal?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here types are locations in \([0,1]\), masses sum to \(1\), and there is one facility, \(m=1\).
For each integer \(N\geq1\), let \(\mathcal S_N\) contain these societies satisfying \(N\mu_x\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\). A deterministic rule \(F_N:\mathcal S_N\to[0,1]\) depends only on the distribution, hence is anonymous. Locations and reports may be real; for algorithmic claims use binary rational locations and state running times in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A resident at \(x\) has utility \(1-|x-y|\) from facility location \(y\). Require strategyproofness: \(|x-F_N(\mu)|\leq|x-F_N(\mu+N^{-1}(\delta_r-\delta_x))|\) for every \(\mu\in\mathcal S_N\), occupied \(x\), and report \(r\in[0,1]\), where \(\delta_z\) is unit mass at \(z\).
Define \(D(\mu,y)=\max_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu}|x-y|\), \(U(\mu,y)=1-D(\mu,y)\), \(D^*(\mu)=\min_{y\in[0,1]}D(\mu,y)\), and \(U^*(\mu)=\max_{y\in[0,1]}U(\mu,y)\).
Let \(R_D(F_N)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal S_N}D(\mu,F_N(\mu))/D^*(\mu)\) and \(R_U(F_N)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal S_N}U^*(\mu)/U(\mu,F_N(\mu))\); set \(0/0=1\) and positive-over-zero ratios to \(+\infty\).
Let \(\mathcal A\subseteq[1,+\infty]^2\) consist of pairs \((a,b)\) for which a strategyproof family satisfies \(\limsup_{N\to\infty}R_D(F_N)\leq a\) and \(\limsup_{N\to\infty}R_U(F_N)\leq b\). Its limiting Pareto frontier is the coordinatewise minimal part of \(\overline{\mathcal A}\), with closure in the extended-real product topology; impose no consistency or continuity between different \(N\).
Target. What is this limiting Pareto frontier? Does \((2,3/2)\) remain Pareto optimal, or can an asymptotic guarantee improve either coordinate without worsening the other?

Result

The limiting Pareto frontier is exactly \(\{(2,3/2)\}\). More precisely,
\[ \mathcal A=[2,+\infty]\times[3/2,+\infty]. \]
Thus neither coordinate of \((2,3/2)\) can be improved asymptotically, even if the other coordinate is unrestricted.

We prove the lower bounds directly in the resident-report model. The achieving rule is MIDORNEAREST, as identified in [Walsh, Theorem 1](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~twalsh/wecai24.pdf); we also verify its strategyproofness and guarantees here.

1. Optimal values and an outcome-preservation lemma

For a society \(\mu\), write \(\ell=\min\operatorname{supp}\mu\), \(h=\max\operatorname{supp}\mu\), and \(w=h-\ell\). For every facility location \(z\),
\[ D(\mu,z)=\max\{|\ell-z|,|h-z|\}. \]
The triangle inequality gives \(w\leq|\ell-z|+|h-z|\leq2D(\mu,z)\). The midpoint \(z=(\ell+h)/2\) attains equality. Consequently,
\[ D^*(\mu)=w/2,\qquad U^*(\mu)=1-w/2. \]

Lemma. Fix \(N\) and a strategyproof rule \(F_N\). If \(F_N(\mu)=y\), moving one resident from any occupied location \(x\) to \(y\) preserves the outcome.

Proof. Let \(\nu=\mu+N^{-1}(\delta_y-\delta_x)\). If \(x=y\), the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, \(\nu\) has a resident at \(y\), and that resident can report \(x\), returning the distribution to \(\mu\). Strategyproofness at \(\nu\) therefore gives
\[ |y-F_N(\nu)|\leq|y-F_N(\mu)|=0. \]
Hence \(F_N(\nu)=y\). Applying this argument successively permits any finite sequence of resident moves to the same outcome \(y\). No coalition-strategyproofness assumption is needed. \(\square\)

2. Lower bounds for every population size

Fix \(N\geq2\) and an arbitrary strategyproof rule \(F_N\). Begin with
\[ \mu_0=N^{-1}\delta_0+(N-1)N^{-1}\delta_1, \qquad y=F_N(\mu_0). \]

If \(y\geq1/2\), successively move all residents at \(1\) to \(y\). The lemma shows that the final society \(\nu\) still has outcome \(y\), and its support is \(\{0,y\}\).

If \(y<1/2\), move the resident at \(0\) to \(y\). Again the outcome remains \(y\), and the support is \(\{y,1\}\).

In either case, the facility is an endpoint of the occupied interval, whose width is
\[ s=\max\{y,1-y\}\geq1/2. \]
Both support points have positive mass, and every intermediate society has masses that are integer multiples of \(N^{-1}\). The possibly real report \(y\) is permitted by the hypotheses.

At the final society,
\[ D(\nu,F_N(\nu))=s,\qquad D^*(\nu)=s/2, \]
so the distance ratio is exactly \(2\).

For utility,
\[ U(\nu,F_N(\nu))=1-s,\qquad U^*(\nu)=1-s/2. \]
If \(s=1\), the utility ratio is \(+\infty\). Otherwise,
\[ \frac{1-s/2}{1-s}-\frac32 =\frac{s-1/2}{1-s}\geq0. \]
Thus the same two-type witness proves
\[ R_D(F_N)\geq2,\qquad R_U(F_N)\geq3/2 \quad\text{for every }N\geq2. \]

These inequalities hold separately for each rule. Therefore every strategyproof family, without any consistency between population sizes, satisfies
\[ \limsup_{N\to\infty}R_D(F_N)\geq2,\qquad \limsup_{N\to\infty}R_U(F_N)\geq3/2. \]

3. A family attaining both bounds

For every \(N\), define
\[ F_N(\mu)=\min\{h,\max\{\ell,1/2\}\}. \]
This projects \(1/2\) onto the occupied interval \([\ell,h]\). The formula depends only on the support and is therefore anonymous.

Strategyproofness

For \(N=1\), the rule returns the sole resident’s report, so truthful reporting gives distance zero.

For \(N\geq2\), fix the reports of all residents except one. Let \(a\) and \(b\) be the minimum and maximum of those fixed reports, and let \(t\) be the remaining resident’s report.

There are three exhaustive cases:

  • If \(a\leq1/2\leq b\), the outcome is always \(1/2\).
  • If \(b<1/2\), the outcome is \(\min\{1/2,\max\{b,t\}\}\), the projection of \(t\) onto \([b,1/2]\).
  • If \(a>1/2\), the outcome is \(\min\{a,\max\{1/2,t\}\}\), the projection of \(t\) onto \([1/2,a]\).

In the constant case, reporting cannot change utility. In either projection case, every possible outcome lies in a fixed closed interval, and truthful reporting selects the point of that interval closest to the resident’s true location. No alternative report can give a smaller distance. This proves the required resident-report strategyproofness.

Approximation guarantees

Let \(d=D(\mu,F_N(\mu))\). The outcome lies in \([\ell,h]\), so \(d\leq w\). If \(w>0\), this gives
\[ \frac{D(\mu,F_N(\mu))}{D^*(\mu)} =\frac{d}{w/2}\leq2. \]
If \(w=0\), the outcome is the sole occupied location, and the stipulated distance ratio is \(1\).

We also have \(d\leq1/2\). Indeed, when the occupied interval contains \(1/2\), the outcome is \(1/2\). When the interval lies entirely to one side of \(1/2\), its width is at most \(1/2\), and the outcome is its endpoint nearest \(1/2\).

Combining \(d\leq w\) with \(2d\leq1\) gives \(3d\leq1+w\). Therefore
\[ 1-\frac w2\leq\frac32(1-d). \]
Since \(1-d\geq1/2>0\), division yields
\[ \frac{U^*(\mu)}{U(\mu,F_N(\mu))} =\frac{1-w/2}{1-d}\leq\frac32. \]

For every \(N\geq2\), the society \(N^{-1}\delta_0+(N-1)N^{-1}\delta_{1/2}\) attains both bounds. Thus this family has \(R_D(F_N)=2\) and \(R_U(F_N)=3/2\) for every such \(N\).

Computation from the support

For binary rational locations, the rule requires only finding the two extreme occupied locations and comparing them with \(1/2\). It never expands the society into \(N\) resident records.

With \(m=1\), elementary rational comparisons give a conservative running time of \(O(\tau L^2+\log N)\) bit operations, including reading \(N\) if supplied separately.

4. Closure and the Pareto frontier

Section 2 excludes every guarantee pair having first coordinate below \(2\) or second coordinate below \(3/2\). Section 3 supplies one family satisfying every guarantee pair with coordinates at least those thresholds. Hence
\[ \mathcal A=[2,+\infty]\times[3/2,+\infty]. \]

Each coordinate interval is closed in the extended-real topology, so \(\overline{\mathcal A}=\mathcal A\). Its unique coordinatewise minimal point is \((2,3/2)\). This completely determines the limiting Pareto frontier.
