The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite bipartite graph \((I,J,E)\), arrival rates \(\lambda_j\), supply masses \(b_i\), and singleton groups with targets \(\mu_j>0\), let type \(j\) arrive as a deterministic mass flow of rate \(\lambda_j\) over \([0,1]\). Each arriving mass unit must be assigned immediately and irrevocably to a feasible supplier or rejected, subject to supplier capacities. What is the best worst-case competitive ratio of causal fluid policies against the clairvoyant fluid optimum for maximizing \(\min_{j\in J} X_j/(\Lambda\mu_j)\), where \(X_j\) is the mass served and \(\Lambda=\sum_j\lambda_j\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|J|\) demand types with positive binary-rational masses \(\nu_j\), where \(\sum_j\nu_j=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The remaining input comprises a nonempty supply set \(I\), \(m=|I|\), a compatibility graph \(E\subseteq I\times J\), positive binary-rational capacities \(c_i\), and an explicitly listed nonempty collection \(\mathcal G\) of nonempty, possibly overlapping subsets of \(J\), with binary-rational targets \(\mu_g\in(0,1)\). Targets need not sum to one. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length and \(k=|\mathcal G|\).
For each admissible integer \(N\ge1\), meaning \(Nc_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) for every \(i\), independent homogeneous Poisson processes generate type-\(j\) requests at rate \(N\nu_j\) on \([0,1]\). Each request has mass \(1/N\); serving it consumes one of the \(Nc_i\) slots at a compatible supply type.
An online policy knows the input and \(N\), and immediately and irrevocably rejects each arrival or assigns it wholly to one compatible supply type with a remaining slot; randomization is allowed. Set \(M_g=N^{-1}\sum_{j\in g}X_j\), where \(X_j\) counts served type-\(j\) requests, and \(V_N=\min_{g\in\mathcal G}\mathbb E[M_g]/\mu_g\), with expectations over arrivals and policy randomness.
Define \(\mathrm{OPT}_N\) as the supremum of this same value over feasible randomized policies allowed to observe the entire arrival sequence before allocating. The minimum remains outside the expectations.
Efficiency means polynomial preprocessing and expected computation per arrival in \(m,\tau,k,L,\log N\).

Does there exist a uniformly efficiently computable online policy family such that, for every fixed input and every \(\epsilon>0\), some \(N_0\) satisfies \(V_N\ge(1-\epsilon)\mathrm{OPT}_N\) for every admissible \(N\ge N_0\)?

For discrete requests, Xu and Xu establish an efficient policy with competitive ratio at least \(\max\{1-1/e,1-(2\pi b)^{-1/2}\}\), where \(b\) is the minimum supply capacity. [Published source](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/21234/21234-13-25247-1-2-20220628.pdf).
This guarantee applies to exactly the scaled model above; its implication is that the stated asymptotic approximation question is already settled affirmatively.

  • Specified that each request is indivisible, consumes one capacity slot, and requires an immediate irrevocable compatible assignment or rejection.

Conclusion

Yes. A uniformly efficiently computable online policy family exists.

The cited paper’s Theorem 1 applies to independent homogeneous Poisson arrivals, integer supply capacities, possibly overlapping groups, and the absolute-serving objective with the minimum outside expectations. Substituting \(\lambda_j=N\nu_j\) and \(b_i=Nc_i\) gives total arrival rate \(\lambda=N\), so its objective is exactly \(V_N\). Its guarantee therefore yields

\[ V_N\geq \max\left\{1-\frac1e,\, 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi N c_{\min}}}\right\}\mathrm{OPT}_N, \qquad c_{\min}:=\min_{i\in I}c_i>0. \]

No normalization of the targets is required. [Xu and Xu, *Equity Promotion in Online Resource Allocation*, model and Theorem 1](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/21234/21234-13-25247-1-2-20220628.pdf).

For completeness, the following direct proof establishes a slightly weaker bound sufficient for the requested conclusion, and verifies efficiency in the stated binary input model.

1. A fluid linear program bounds the offline optimum

Introduce a variable \(x_{ij}\) for every \((i,j)\in E\), representing allocated mass, and a variable \(s\). Solve the linear program

\[ \begin{gathered} \text{maximize }s,\\ \sum_{i:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}\leq\nu_j \quad(j\in J),\\ \sum_{j:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}\leq c_i \quad(i\in I),\\ \sum_{j\in g}\sum_{i:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}\geq s\mu_g \quad(g\in\mathcal G),\\ x_{ij}\geq0,\qquad s\geq0. \end{gathered} \]

Let its optimum be \(s^*\), and choose an optimal solution \(x^*\).

This program is feasible by taking all variables zero. Moreover, summing the demand constraints gives \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}\leq1\). Since \(\mathcal G\) is nonempty and each \(\mu_g>0\), the group constraints bound \(s\). Thus the feasible region is bounded and an optimum exists.

For any feasible randomized offline policy, let \(Y_{ij}\) count assignments from demand type \(j\) to supply type \(i\), and put \(x_{ij}=\mathbb E[Y_{ij}]/N\). Then:

  • A policy cannot serve more type-\(j\) requests than arrive, so \(\sum_i x_{ij}\leq\nu_j\).
  • Supply capacity holds on every realization, so \(\sum_jx_{ij}\leq c_i\).
  • If the policy’s objective value is \(w\), then \(\sum_{j\in g}\sum_i x_{ij}\geq w\mu_g\) for every group.

Consequently, \((x,w)\) is LP-feasible. Taking the supremum over offline policies proves

\[ \mathrm{OPT}_N\leq s^*. \]

This argument permits overlapping groups and arbitrary targets satisfying the given hypotheses.

2. The online policy

For each arrival of type \(j\), independently sample supply type \(i\) with probability

\[ p_{ij}:=\frac{x^*_{ij}}{\nu_j}. \]

Reject with the remaining probability \(1-\sum_i p_{ij}\). If supply \(i\) is sampled, assign the request there if a slot remains; otherwise reject.

The demand constraints and \(\nu_j>0\) ensure these are valid probabilities. The policy makes an immediate, irrevocable decision and always respects compatibility and capacity.

3. Expected service at each supply

Fix a supply type \(i\), and define

\[ B_i:=Nc_i,\qquad a_i:=\sum_jx^*_{ij},\qquad r_i:=Na_i\leq B_i. \]

Independent marking and superposition of the input Poisson processes imply that attempts directed to \(i\) form a homogeneous Poisson process of rate \(r_i\). If \(r_i>0\), their type labels are independent, with probabilities \(Nx^*_{ij}/r_i\), and are independent of the total attempt count.

Let \(T_i\) be that count. The policy serves precisely the first \(\min(T_i,B_i)\) attempts. Therefore, writing \(Y_{ij}\) for its assignment counts,

\[ \mathbb E[Y_{ij}] =\frac{Nx^*_{ij}}{r_i}\, \mathbb E[\min(\operatorname{Pois}(r_i),B_i)]. \]

If \(r_i=0\), all \(x^*_{ij}\) incident to \(i\) vanish and all corresponding assignment counts are zero.

For a positive integer \(B\), define \(F_B(r):=\mathbb E[\min(\operatorname{Pois}(r),B)]\). Its finite expression is

\[ F_B(r) =B-e^{-r}\sum_{\ell=0}^{B-1}(B-\ell)\frac{r^\ell}{\ell!}. \]

Differentiating this expression and cancelling consecutive terms gives

\[ F'_B(r)=e^{-r}\sum_{\ell=0}^{B-1}\frac{r^\ell}{\ell!} =\Pr(\operatorname{Pois}(r)<B). \]

Since \(F_B(0)=0\), for \(r>0\),

\[ \frac{F_B(r)}r =\int_0^1\Pr(\operatorname{Pois}(rt)<B)\,dt. \]

For \(r\leq B\), couple \(\operatorname{Pois}(Bt)\) as the sum of independent variables with means \(rt\) and \((B-r)t\). This proves

\[ \frac{F_B(r)}r\geq\frac{F_B(B)}B. \]

To bound the latter quantity, let \(Z\sim\operatorname{Pois}(B)\). Because \(\mathbb E[Z-B]=0\),

\[ B-F_B(B) =\mathbb E[(B-Z)_+] =\frac12\mathbb E[|Z-B|] \leq\frac12\sqrt{\mathbb E[(Z-B)^2]} =\frac{\sqrt B}{2}. \]

The inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz; the last equality uses the Poisson variance \(B\). Hence

\[ \mathbb E[Y_{ij}] \geq Nx^*_{ij} \left(1-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{B_i}}\right). \]

4. Groupwise guarantee and asymptotics

Set \(B_{\min}:=Nc_{\min}\). Summing the preceding inequality over each group and using its LP constraint gives

\[ \mathbb E[M_g] \geq \left(1-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{B_{\min}}}\right)s^*\mu_g. \]

Admissibility implies \(B_{\min}\geq1\), so the factor is nonnegative. Dividing by \(\mu_g\), taking the minimum, and applying the offline upper bound proves

\[ V_N\geq \left(1-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{Nc_{\min}}}\right)s^* \geq \left(1-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{Nc_{\min}}}\right)\mathrm{OPT}_N. \]

For any fixed input and any \(\epsilon>0\), choose

\[ N_0:= \max\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{1}{4c_{\min}\epsilon^2}\right\rceil\right\}. \]

Then every admissible \(N\geq N_0\) satisfies the requested inequality. The same policy family works for every \(\epsilon\); it does not require \(\epsilon\) as input. If \(s^*=0\), the offline upper bound gives \(\mathrm{OPT}_N=0\), so this case is also covered.

5. Uniform computational efficiency

The LP has \(|E|+1\leq m\tau+1\) variables and \(m+\tau+k\) nontrivial inequalities. Its rational encoding length is polynomial in the listed input length. Standard polynomial-time rational linear programming produces an exact optimal rational solution with polynomially bounded bit length.

Each probability \(p_{ij}\) consequently has polynomial bit length. Exact sampling is also efficient: for each demand type, express its probabilities and rejection probability over a common integer denominator \(D\). The bit length of \(D\) is polynomial. Generate a uniform integer below \(D\) by drawing \(\lceil\log_2D\rceil\) independent fair bits and retrying when the resulting integer is at least \(D\). Each trial succeeds with probability greater than one half, so the expected number of trials is less than two. The case \(D=1\) is deterministic.

Finally, supply counters require \(O(L+\log N)\) bits. Thus preprocessing and expected computation per arrival are polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L,\log N\), as required.
