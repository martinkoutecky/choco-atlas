Let \(P_N\in T^N\) be truthful finite profiles whose empirical frequencies converge coordinatewise to \(\mu\). The catalogue and its values stay fixed along each sequence; goods are neither replicated nor divided. Write \(D_t=S_t\) for singleton demands and \(D_t=M\) otherwise.
ARP treats each report as a bid for \(D_t\). For bidder \(i\), let \(A\) be the highest other grand-bundle bid and \(b_1\ge\cdots\ge b_m\) the highest other singleton bids, ordered by good; missing bids equal \(0\), and equal bids are ordered by a fixed ordering of goods.
The offered grand-bundle price is \(p_i(M)=\max\{A,b_1,2b_2\}\). The first-ranked singleton price is \(\max\{b_1,A/2\}\) if \(A<2b_2\), and \(\max\{b_1,A\}\) otherwise.
Every other singleton price is \(\max\{b_2,A/2\}\) if \(A<2b_1\), and \(\max\{b_1,A\}\) otherwise. Each report receives its effective demand or nothing, maximizing reported value minus its offered price; winners pay that price and losers pay \(0\).
Choose a feasible collection of these maximizing choices with at most two winners, choosing two whenever possible, then one whenever possible; resolve remaining ties by a fixed lexicographic ordering of goods and identifiers.
For the stipulated welfare benchmark, use effective demands in both quantities: \(s_{\mathrm{ARP}}(P_N)\) is the sum of winning values and \(s^*(P_N)=\max\{\sum_{i\in W}v_i:D_i\cap D_j=\varnothing\text{ for distinct }i,j\in W\}\). Require \(s^*(P_N)>0\).

What is \(R_m=\inf_{T,\mu,(P_N)}\liminf_{N\to\infty}s_{\mathrm{ARP}}(P_N)/s^*(P_N)\)? Is \(R_m=2/(m+1)\), or does it differ?

Result

\[ R_m=\frac{2}{m+1}. \]

This holds even if every catalogue type has positive mass and every value is strictly positive. The construction below keeps the catalogue fixed along each sequence.

1. Universal lower bound

Use the finite-profile guarantee stipulated in the question: for every finite single-minded profile \(P\) with \(s^*(P)>0\), under the specified effective-demand benchmark and allocation rule,
\[ s_{\mathrm{ARP}}(P)\ge \frac{2}{m+1}s^*(P). \]
This is the supplied finite ARP result; its grand-bundle/singleton restriction and preference for two feasible winners match the present mechanism. [Iwasaki et al., Section 5](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/worstcasefnpAAMAS10.pdf).

Every profile in every admissible sequence satisfies these hypotheses. Therefore,
\[ \liminf_{N\to\infty} \frac{s_{\mathrm{ARP}}(P_N)}{s^*(P_N)} \ge \frac{2}{m+1}. \]
Taking the infimum over catalogues, societies, and sequences gives
\[ R_m\ge \frac{2}{m+1}. \]

2. A fixed catalogue and a full-support society

Label the goods \(M=\{g_1,\ldots,g_m\}\). Fix a rational number \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\). Define a catalogue of \(K=m+1\) types:
\[ t_0=(M,2),\qquad t_1=(\{g_1\},2-\varepsilon),\qquad t_j=(\{g_j\},1-\varepsilon)\quad(2\le j\le m). \]
Every value is strictly positive. Moreover, each original demand already equals its effective demand.

Choose a power of two \(Q>m\), and define
\[ \mu(t_0)=\frac{Q-m}{Q}, \qquad \mu(t_j)=\frac1Q\quad(1\le j\le m). \]
These are positive binary-rational masses summing to \(1\), so the support size is \(\tau=K=m+1\).

For each \(N\ge1\), let \(P_N\) contain
\[ n_j(N)=\left\lfloor\frac NQ\right\rfloor \quad(1\le j\le m), \qquad n_0(N)=N-m\left\lfloor\frac NQ\right\rfloor \]
copies of the respective types. Their counts sum to \(N\), and
\[ \frac{n_j(N)}N\longrightarrow\frac1Q, \qquad \frac{n_0(N)}N\longrightarrow\frac{Q-m}{Q}. \]
Thus the empirical frequencies converge coordinatewise to \(\mu\).

Since \(Q>m\), there is at least one grand-bundle bidder for every \(N\); consequently \(s^*(P_N)\ge2>0\). For \(N\ge2Q\), every type has at least two copies.

3. Exact ARP outcome after replication

Fix \(N\ge2Q\). Removing any one bidder leaves at least one copy of every catalogue type. Hence every bidder faces the same highest other bids:
\[ A=2,\qquad b_1=2-\varepsilon,\qquad b_2=\cdots=b_m=1-\varepsilon. \]
The first-ranked good is uniquely \(g_1\).

Grand-bundle bidders. Their offered price is
\[ p_i(M)=\max\{2,2-\varepsilon,2-2\varepsilon\}=2. \]
Thus each grand-bundle bidder is indifferent between winning and receiving nothing: both choices give utility \(0\).

Bidders for \(g_1\). Because
\[ A=2>2(1-\varepsilon)=2b_2, \]
the first-ranked singleton price is
\[ p_i(\{g_1\})=\max\{2-\varepsilon,2\}=2. \]
Winning would give utility \(-\varepsilon<0\), so these bidders must lose.

Bidders for \(g_j\), where \(j\ge2\). Because \(\varepsilon<1/2\),
\[ A=2<2(2-\varepsilon)=2b_1. \]
Their offered singleton price is therefore
\[ p_i(\{g_j\})=\max\{1-\varepsilon,1\}=1. \]
Again, winning would give utility \(-\varepsilon<0\), so these bidders must lose.

Only grand-bundle bidders can win while making maximizing choices. Two such winners are infeasible, whereas one is feasible. The stipulated rule therefore chooses exactly one grand-bundle winner, independently of the remaining lexicographic tie-breaking. Consequently,
\[ s_{\mathrm{ARP}}(P_N)=2 \qquad(N\ge2Q). \]

4. Exact benchmark and limiting ratio

A feasible allocation containing a grand-bundle bidder has welfare at most \(2\). An allocation containing only singleton bidders can take one bidder for each good, achieving
\[ (2-\varepsilon)+(m-1)(1-\varepsilon) =m+1-m\varepsilon. \]
No singleton allocation has greater welfare, since each good can be allocated to at most one bidder.

Furthermore,
\[ m+1-m\varepsilon-2 =m-1-m\varepsilon >m-1-\frac m2 =\frac{m-2}{2}\ge0. \]
Thus the singleton allocation strictly dominates the grand-bundle allocation, including when \(m=2\). Therefore,
\[ s^*(P_N)=m+1-m\varepsilon \qquad(N\ge2Q). \]
The welfare ratio is eventually constant, giving
\[ \liminf_{N\to\infty} \frac{s_{\mathrm{ARP}}(P_N)}{s^*(P_N)} =\frac{2}{m+1-m\varepsilon}. \]

For every fixed rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)\), this is an admissible sequence with fixed catalogue and values. Hence
\[ R_m\le \frac{2}{m+1-m\varepsilon}. \]
Taking \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) through rational values yields
\[ R_m\le\frac{2}{m+1}. \]
Here \(\varepsilon\) varies between catalogues in the outer infimum, never along an individual profile sequence. Combining this bound with Section 1 proves the claimed equality.

5. Compatibility with exact finite false-name testing

The stipulated finite false-name-proofness theorem applies at every \(P_N\), rather than merely in the limit. For the constructed profiles with \(N\ge2Q\), this can also be checked directly.

Every truthful bidder has utility \(0\). After replacing one bidder by arbitrary finitely many reports, an external grand-bundle bid of \(2\) remains. Thus every winning grand-bundle identifier pays at least \(2\), and every winning singleton identifier pays at least \(1\), directly from the price formulas.

For an original grand-bundle bidder, obtaining all goods requires either a grand-bundle winner or at least two singleton winners. Either possibility costs at least \(2\), its original value.

For an original singleton bidder of value \(v\), an external bid of \(v\) for its desired good remains. Any identifier winning that good as a singleton pays at least \(v\): the first-ranked price is at least \(b_1\), and every other singleton price is at least \(b_2\), which bounds that good’s other bid. Obtaining it through a grand-bundle winner costs at least \(2>v\).

All payments are nonnegative. Hence no such deviation has positive utility, regardless of the values or demands of the false-name reports.
