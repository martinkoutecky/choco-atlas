The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of agent types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\), type-average log-ratio observations \(\bar\Lambda_{t,i}\), and known type-average likelihood ratios \(\bar L_{t,i}\), recover a nonnegative type-to-type communication matrix \(W\) whose columns sum to one by minimizing \(\sum_{i,t}\mu_t\left\|\bar\Lambda_{t,i}-(1-\delta)\sum_s W_{s,t}\bar\Lambda_{s,i-1}+\delta\bar L_{t,i}\right\|^2\). Determine when this population risk identifies \(W\) and can be minimized efficiently.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq2\) hypotheses, one designated true hypothesis, and \(N\geq1\) observation times. The input includes rational \(\delta\in(0,1)\), signed rational vectors \(\bar\Lambda_{t,i}\in\mathbb Q^{m-1}\) for \(0\leq i\leq N\), and nonnegative rational vectors \(\bar L_t\in\mathbb Q^{m-1}\). These represent type averages of publicly exchanged belief log-ratios and expected private log-likelihood ratios, respectively, always true hypothesis versus alternative; underlying ratios are finite and beliefs positive. The expected likelihood vectors are time-independent.
A feasible communication matrix has real entries \(W_{s,t}\geq0\) and \(\sum_sW_{s,t}=1\); its entry is the total influence assigned by recipient type \(t\) to source type \(s\).
Define the mass-weighted risk \(R(W)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_t\mu_t\left\|\bar\Lambda_{t,i}-(1-\delta)\sum_sW_{s,t}\bar\Lambda_{s,i-1}-\delta\bar L_t\right\|_2^2\). Identification means that this risk has exactly one feasible minimizer. All numerical inputs, including a threshold \(q\in\mathbb Q\), have total binary bit length \(L\).

When does \(R\) identify \(W\)? Is deciding whether \(\min_W R(W)\leq q\) in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, with running times stated in \(m,\tau,N,L\)?

1. Answer

The threshold problem is in \(\mathrm P\): its exact answer can be computed in \((m\tau N+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations. Identification is also decidable within this bound.

Identification is not automatic. Its exact condition is that, for every recipient type, the uniquely fitted trajectory has a unique convex representation by the source trajectories. Affine independence of all source trajectories guarantees this, but is not necessary: boundary constraints can produce uniqueness even when the quadratic Hessian is singular.

Here identification has exactly the meaning stipulated in the question: uniqueness of the feasible minimizer.

2. Reduction to independent convex least-squares problems

Put \(d=N(m-1)\). For each source type \(s\), define its stacked trajectory

\[ \text{[Formula below uses the requested inline delimiters.]} \]

\(a_s=(1-\delta)\bigl(\bar\Lambda_{s,0}^{\mathsf T},\ldots,\bar\Lambda_{s,N-1}^{\mathsf T}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb Q^d.\)

For each recipient type \(t\), define

\(b_t=\bigl((\bar\Lambda_{t,1}-\delta\bar L_t)^{\mathsf T},\ldots,(\bar\Lambda_{t,N}-\delta\bar L_t)^{\mathsf T}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb Q^d.\)

Let \(A=[a_1\ \cdots\ a_\tau]\), and let

\(\Delta_\tau=\{w\in\mathbb R^\tau:w\geq0,\ \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}w=1\}.\)

Writing \(w_t=W_{\cdot,t}\), the given risk becomes

\(R(W)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\|Aw_t-b_t\|_2^2,\qquad w_t\in\Delta_\tau.\)

There are no constraints coupling distinct columns. Moreover, every \(\mu_t\) is strictly positive. Consequently,

\(\operatorname*{argmin}R=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}\operatorname*{argmin}_{w\in\Delta_\tau}\|Aw-b_t\|_2^2.\)

Indeed, minimizing each summand minimizes the sum; conversely, a nonminimizing column could be replaced to decrease the sum strictly.

Every column problem attains its minimum because the simplex is compact and the objective is continuous. Thus \(R\) identifies \(W\) if and only if every column problem has exactly one minimizer. The positive masses affect the minimum value, but not the minimizing columns or their uniqueness.

3. Exact identification criteria

Unique fitted trajectories and their representations

Let

\(C=A\Delta_\tau=\operatorname{conv}\{a_1,\ldots,a_\tau\}.\)

For each recipient \(t\), let \(z_t\) minimize \(\|z-b_t\|_2^2\) over \(C\). This point exists because \(C\) is compact, and it is unique. To prove uniqueness, if two distinct points \(z,z'\) were minimizing, their midpoint would belong to \(C\), and

\(\left\|\frac{z+z'}2-b_t\right\|_2^2=\frac{\|z-b_t\|_2^2+\|z'-b_t\|_2^2}{2}-\frac{\|z-z'\|_2^2}{4}\)

would be strictly smaller.

The complete set of minimizing weights for recipient \(t\) is therefore

\(P_t=\{w\in\Delta_\tau:Aw=z_t\}.\)

This proves the first exact criterion:

Identification criterion. The risk identifies \(W\) if and only if every \(P_t\) is a singleton.

Thus uniqueness of the fitted trajectory always holds; uniqueness of its communication weights is the additional requirement.

A kernel criterion at any minimizer

Choose any minimizing column \(w_t^\star\). Then \(P_t\) is a singleton if and only if

\(\{h\in\mathbb R^\tau:Ah=0,\ \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}h=0,\ h_s\geq0\text{ whenever }w_{t,s}^\star=0\}=\{0\}.\)

Proof. If another minimizing column \(v\) exists, then \(h=v-w_t^\star\) is nonzero and satisfies all these conditions.

Conversely, suppose such a nonzero \(h\) exists. At coordinates where \(w_t^\star\) vanishes, \(h\) is nonnegative. At its remaining coordinates, \(w_t^\star\) is positive. Hence, for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),

\(w_t^\star+\varepsilon h\geq0.\)

The other two conditions give

\(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}(w_t^\star+\varepsilon h)=1,\qquad A(w_t^\star+\varepsilon h)=Aw_t^\star=z_t.\)

This is a distinct minimizing column. The criterion follows.

This formulation accounts explicitly for the nonnegativity constraints; a Hessian rank test alone does not.

An equivalent criterion using faces

A face of \(C\) means a nonempty convex subset \(F\subseteq C\) with the following property: if an interior point of a segment between two points of \(C\) belongs to \(F\), both endpoints belong to \(F\). We include \(C\) itself as a face.

Let \(F_t\) be the smallest face of \(C\) containing \(z_t\), and set

\(J_t=\{s:a_s\in F_t\}.\)

Then:

Face criterion. The risk identifies \(W\) if and only if, for every recipient \(t\), the indexed family \((a_s)_{s\in J_t}\) is affinely independent.

Here affine independence means that

\(\sum_{s\in J_t}h_sa_s=0,\qquad \sum_{s\in J_t}h_s=0\)

implies every \(h_s=0\). In particular, repeated source points remain distinct indices and destroy affine independence if both belong to \(J_t\).

Proof. Fix a recipient and abbreviate its objects by \(z,F,J\).

Any convex representation of a point in \(F\) can assign positive weight only to source points in \(F\). Indeed, isolating any positively weighted source point expresses the represented point as an interior point of a segment, so the face property applies. This also shows that \(F=\operatorname{conv}\{a_s:s\in J\}\).

If the indexed source points in \(F\) are affinely independent, two representations of \(z\) would have a difference satisfying the two affine-dependence equations. Their difference must therefore vanish.

For the converse, \(z\) belongs to the relative interior of its smallest face. Otherwise a supporting hyperplane within the affine hull of \(F\) would give a proper face of \(F\), hence a smaller face of \(C\), containing \(z\).

For each \(s\in J\), relative interior implies that, for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon_s\in(0,1)\),

\(y_s=\frac{z-\varepsilon_sa_s}{1-\varepsilon_s}\in F.\)

Representing \(y_s\) by source points in \(F\) gives a representation of \(z\) with positive weight on index \(s\). Averaging these representations over \(J\) gives a representation \(u\) satisfying \(u_s>0\) for every \(s\in J\).

If the indexed family is affinely dependent, take a nonzero dependence vector \(h\) supported on \(J\). For sufficiently small \(\eta>0\), both \(u+\eta h\) and \(u-\eta h\) are nonnegative. Both sum to one and represent \(z\). Thus the representation is not unique.

4. Strong convexity: a sufficient condition, with its precise limits

Let \(G=A^{\mathsf T}A\). The Hessian with respect to all communication weights is block diagonal:

\(\nabla^2R=\operatorname{diag}(2\mu_1G,\ldots,2\mu_\tau G).\)

It is positive semidefinite, so the risk is always convex. Its gradient is globally Lipschitz, with constant

\(2\bigl(\max_t\mu_t\bigr)\|A\|_2^2\)

under the Frobenius norm on matrices.

The relevant directions for feasible communication matrices satisfy \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}h=0\). For \(\tau>1\), the risk is strongly convex on the feasible set exactly when

\(\ker A\cap\mathbf1^\perp=\{0\}.\)

Equivalently,

\(\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}A\\ \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}=\tau,\)

which is precisely affine independence of all source trajectories.

Proof of sufficiency. If the kernel intersection is trivial, compactness of the unit sphere in \(\mathbf1^\perp\) gives

\(\gamma=\min_{\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}h=0,\ \|h\|_2=1}\|Ah\|_2^2>0.\)

Thus every feasible direction \(H\), with columns \(h_t\), satisfies

\(\langle H,\nabla^2R\,H\rangle=2\sum_t\mu_t\|Ah_t\|_2^2\geq2(\min_t\mu_t)\gamma\|H\|_F^2.\)

This is strong convexity and implies a unique minimizer.

Proof of necessity. If a nonzero \(h\) satisfies \(Ah=0\) and \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}h=0\), take a column equal to the uniform vector. Small perturbations in both directions along \(h\) remain feasible. Along this nontrivial segment the risk is constant, contradicting strong convexity.

For \(\tau=1\), the only feasible matrix is \(W=[1]\), so identification is automatic.

Two consequences clarify the distinction between strong convexity and identification:

  • Strong convexity requires \(\tau\leq N(m-1)+1\), because the augmented matrix has only \(N(m-1)+1\) rows.
  • If any minimizing column has all entries positive, identification requires the affine-independence condition: its kernel criterion has no sign restrictions.

Identification can nevertheless hold outside this rank condition when all relevant fitted points lie on suitable boundary faces.

Explicit examples with positive expected likelihood ratios

Take \(m=2\), \(N=1\), \(\tau=3\), \(\delta=\tfrac12\), masses \((\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac14)\), and

\((\bar\Lambda_{1,0},\bar\Lambda_{2,0},\bar\Lambda_{3,0})=(0,1,2),\qquad \bar L_t=1\text{ for every }t.\)

Then the source points are \(a_1=0\), \(a_2=\tfrac12\), \(a_3=1\).

Identification without strong convexity. Set \(\bar\Lambda_{t,1}=\tfrac12\) for every recipient. Then \(b_t=0\), and its only convex representation is \(w_t=(1,0,0)^{\mathsf T}\). Hence the risk has a unique minimizer and minimum zero, although the three source points are affinely dependent.

Failure of identification. Instead set \(\bar\Lambda_{t,1}=1\) for every recipient. Then \(b_t=\tfrac12\), represented both by

\(w_t=(0,1,0)^{\mathsf T}\quad\text{and}\quad w_t=(\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12)^{\mathsf T}.\)

Both give zero residual, so identification fails.

All specified entries are rational and all expected likelihood entries are strictly positive. Finite scalar belief log-ratios are compatible with positive beliefs, since a ratio \(x\) is realized by probabilities \(e^x/(1+e^x)\) and \(1/(1+e^x)\).

5. Exact polynomial-time threshold decision

Expand each column objective as

\(\|Aw-b_t\|_2^2=w^{\mathsf T}Gw-2c_t^{\mathsf T}w+\beta_t,\qquad c_t=A^{\mathsf T}b_t,\quad\beta_t=b_t^{\mathsf T}b_t.\)

Its coefficients are rational, its quadratic matrix is positive semidefinite, and its constraints are rational linear constraints.

The exact convex quadratic programming theorem of Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan provides a deterministic algorithm whose bit complexity is polynomial in the binary encoding length of a rational convex quadratic program. In the nonempty bounded case used here, it computes an exact optimum. Positive definiteness is not required. [The polynomial solvability of convex quadratic programming](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189)

All required hypotheses hold:

  • \(G=A^{\mathsf T}A\) is positive semidefinite.
  • All coefficients and constraints are rational.
  • The simplex is nonempty and compact.
  • Therefore the objective attains a finite minimum.

Compute the exact column minima \(r_t\), and then compare

\(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t r_t\leq q.\)

This decides the requested threshold, including equality.

Why exact rational output is possible

For completeness, rationality does not rely on strict convexity.

Choose a column minimizer with the smallest support \(S\). The points indexed by \(S\) are affinely independent. Otherwise an affine dependence supported on \(S\) could be followed, preserving both the fitted point and the sum of weights, until some positive weight vanished. This would contradict minimal support.

Within its support, the minimizer satisfies the equality-constrained first-order equations

\(2G_{SS}w_S+\lambda\mathbf1=2(c_t)_S,\qquad \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}w_S=1.\)

The coefficient matrix

\(\begin{pmatrix}2G_{SS}&\mathbf1\\ \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}\)

is nonsingular. Indeed, a vector \((h,\alpha)\) in its kernel satisfies \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}h=0\); multiplying its first equation by \(h^{\mathsf T}\) yields \(\|A_Sh\|_2^2=0\). Affine independence then gives \(h=0\), followed by \(\alpha=0\).

Thus this optimizer is rational. Clearing denominators and applying determinant bounds shows that its binary encoding length is polynomial in the coefficient encoding length and \(\tau\). Its objective value has polynomial encoding length as well. Searching all supports is unnecessary; the convex quadratic programming theorem supplies the polynomial-time algorithm.

Running time in the stated parameters

Forming \(G\), all \(c_t\), and all \(\beta_t\) takes

\(O\bigl(N(m-1)\tau^2\bigr)\)

rational arithmetic operations.

The resulting coefficient encoding lengths are polynomial in \(m,\tau,N,L\). To see this directly, let \(D\) be the product of all input denominators. Its bit length is at most \(L\). Entries of \(A\) and \(b_t\) have denominators dividing \(D^2\), and the quadratic coefficients have denominators dividing \(D^4\). Summing over \(d=N(m-1)\) coordinates adds at most a logarithmic factor to their magnitude bounds.

Solving the \(\tau\) column programs and comparing the resulting rational sum therefore takes

\((m\tau N+L)^{O(1)}\)

bit operations. This is a polynomial bit-complexity bound; no strongly polynomial bound is claimed.

Consequently the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm P\). Under polynomial-time many-one reductions, calling it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

6. Identification is also decidable in polynomial time

After computing an exact minimizing column \(w_t^\star\), compute its fitted point \(z_t=Aw_t^\star\). For each source index \(s\), solve the two rational linear programs

\(\ell_{t,s}=\min\{w_s:w\in\Delta_\tau,\ Aw=z_t\},\)

\(u_{t,s}=\max\{w_s:w\in\Delta_\tau,\ Aw=z_t\}.\)

Their feasible sets are nonempty and compact. A fiber \(P_t\) is a singleton exactly when

\(\ell_{t,s}=u_{t,s}\quad\text{for every }s.\)

If all coordinate ranges vanish, all feasible vectors have the same coordinates. Conversely, two distinct feasible vectors differ in some coordinate, giving a nonzero range.

Thus \(\tau\) convex quadratic programs followed by \(2\tau^2\) linear programs decide identification. Exact rational linear programming is polynomial-time, and all these programs have polynomial encoding length. The same overall bound follows:

\((m\tau N+L)^{O(1)}\) bit operations.
