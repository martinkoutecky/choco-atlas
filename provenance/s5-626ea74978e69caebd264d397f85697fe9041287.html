Theorem 3 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of types \(\Theta\), nonzero valuation vectors \(v^\theta\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^3\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta>0\) summing to \(1\), per-capita supplies \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^3\), and an integer bundle cap \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^3\), decide whether and construct a rational allocation table \(x_{\theta,b}\ge 0\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^3\) with \(b\le B\). Require \(\sum_b x_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,b}x_{\theta,b}b=q\), and any prescribed fixed masses receive their specified bundles. Require envy-freeness: whenever \(x_{\theta,b}>0\) and \(x_{\theta',b'}>0\), \(v^\theta\cdot b\ge v^\theta\cdot b'\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=3\) item kinds. Each type has a nonzero additive valuation vector \(v^\theta\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^3\). Supplies are per-capita quantities \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^3\), and the binary-encoded bundle cap is \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^3\); write \(\mathcal B=\{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^3:b\le B\}\), with coordinatewise inequalities.
Fixed subpopulations are specified by a sparse list of \(r\) pairs \((\theta,b)\) with prescribed binary-rational masses \(f_{\theta,b}>0\), where \(b\in\mathcal B\) and \(\sum_b f_{\theta,b}\le\mu_\theta\); unlisted entries are zero. These masses must receive their entire specified bundles.
Decide whether a rational allocation table \(x_{\theta,b}\ge0\) exists, and construct one when feasible, represented by its nonzero entries, satisfying \(\sum_b x_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,b}x_{\theta,b}b=q\), and \(x_{\theta,b}\ge f_{\theta,b}\).
Require envy-freeness: for every \(\theta,\theta'\in\Theta\) and \(b,b'\in\mathcal B\), if \(x_{\theta,b}>0\) and \(x_{\theta',b'}>0\), then \(v^\theta\cdot b\ge v^\theta\cdot b'\). This includes comparisons within a type and all fixed subpopulations; utility ties are permitted.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L,r\), including any dependence on the numerical cap \(B\).

Is feasibility for this continuous allocation problem in \(\mathsf P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,r\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

For \(n\) discrete agents and \(M\) items in three kinds, an exact envy-free allocation extending prescribed whole bundles can be found, or infeasibility reported, in \((nM)^{\mathcal O(\sqrt n)}\) time ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09327v1)).
No result settling the exact continuous problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified fixed masses are disjoint subpopulations within each type, aggregated by prescribed whole bundle; their total cannot exceed the type's mass. Additional free mass may receive the same bundle, so the aggregate constraints are lower bounds.
  • The bundle cap is encoded in binary and remains an independent input bound; it is not equated to normalized per-capita supply.
  • Allocation tables are represented sparsely by their nonzero entries, since the binary-encoded cap can describe exponentially many bundles.
  • Use m=3 for the number of item kinds in the continuous task and M for the discrete total item count; L includes all numerical input encodings.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. Feasibility is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete. This holds for \(m=3\), even with strictly positive valuation vectors, positive supplies, dyadic masses, and numerical caps bounded polynomially in \(\tau\).

The reduction below proves hardness. A sparse-certificate argument then proves membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\) and gives an exact algorithm that constructs an allocation when feasible.

1. Reduction construction.

Use the standard \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete positive-integer SUBSET SUM problem: given binary-encoded positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\) and an integer target \(T\), decide whether some subset sums to \(T\). This is the classical numerical problem in [Karp’s completeness theorem](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf). We may restrict to \(k\ge1\) and \(0\le T\le S:=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i\): targets outside this interval are immediately infeasible.

Let \(W\) be the smallest power of two strictly exceeding \(S+k\). Define

\(H=(2k+1)^2,\qquad B=(2k+1,H,1).\)

Construct \(k+1\) fixed types \(G_0,\ldots,G_k\). For \(j=0,\ldots,k\), set

\(t_j=2j+1,\qquad g_j=(t_j,H-t_j^2,1),\qquad v^{G_j}=(2t_j,1,1).\)

Their masses are

\(\beta_j=1/W\quad(0\le j<k),\qquad \beta_k=(W-S-k)/W.\)

Prescribe each fixed type’s entire mass at its bundle: \(f_{G_j,g_j}=\beta_j\). Consequently, every feasible allocation must give all of \(G_j\) exactly \(g_j\).

Construct \(k\) unfixed types \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\), with

\(c_i=2i,\qquad \alpha_i=a_i/W,\qquad v^{A_i}=(2c_i+\tfrac12,1,1).\)

For later use, define two candidate bundles

\(p_i(e)=(c_i,H-c_i^2+e,1),\qquad e\in\{0,1\}.\)

Finally, set the supply vector to

\(q=\sum_{j=0}^k\beta_jg_j+\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_ip_i(0)+(0,T/W,0).\)

All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and

\(\sum_{j=0}^k\beta_j+\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i=1.\)

Every valuation coordinate is strictly positive. All fixed and candidate bundles satisfy the cap. In particular, \(c_i\le2k\), and

\(H-c_i^2\ge H-(2k)^2=4k+1>0,\qquad H-c_i^2+1\le H.\)

All supply coordinates are positive; the third equals one. The constructed parameters are

\(m=3,\qquad \tau=2k+1,\qquad r=k+1,\qquad B_{\max}=(2k+1)^2.\)

Moreover, \(\log W=\mathcal O(\log(S+k))\), and the output length satisfies

\(L=\mathcal O\bigl(k(\log(S+k)+\log(k+1))\bigr).\)

Thus the construction is a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

2. Every feasible allocation encodes a subset.

Suppose the constructed instance has a feasible envy-free allocation \(x\). Write a bundle as \(b=(\xi,\eta,\zeta)\).

First, every positively allocated bundle has \(\zeta=1\). Indeed, the cap gives \(\zeta\le1\), while total mass and third-coordinate supply both equal one. Hence

\(\sum_{\theta,b}x_{\theta,b}(1-\zeta)=1-q_3=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative, so positive allocation mass requires \(\zeta=1\).

Because each fixed type receives \(g_j\), its non-envy towards any positively allocated bundle implies

\[2t_j\xi+\eta\le H+t_j^2.\tag{1}\]

Now fix an unfixed type \(A_i\), and abbreviate \(c=c_i\). Its neighboring fixed types have first coordinates \(c-1\) and \(c+1\); denote their bundles by \(g_-\) and \(g_+\).

We claim that every bundle positively allocated to \(A_i\) has \(\xi\ge c\). Suppose instead that \(\xi\le c-1\). Applying (1) at \(t=c-1\) gives

\(\eta\le H+(c-1)^2-2(c-1)\xi.\)

Therefore

\(v^{A_i}\cdot b\le H+(c-1)^2+\tfrac52\xi+1\le v^{A_i}\cdot g_-.\)

But direct calculation gives

\(v^{A_i}\cdot g_+-v^{A_i}\cdot g_-=1.\)

Thus \(A_i\) envies the fixed subpopulation receiving \(g_+\), a contradiction. Since \(\xi\) is integral, the claim follows.

The first-coordinate supply equality now forces every bundle allocated to \(A_i\) to have \(\xi=c_i\). Specifically, subtracting the fixed types’ contributions and using \(\sum_bx_{A_i,b}=\alpha_i\) yields

\(\sum_{i=1}^k\sum_bx_{A_i,b}(\xi-c_i)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative by the preceding claim. Hence each positively allocated bundle satisfies \(\xi=c_i\).

For such a bundle, (1) at either neighboring fixed type gives

\(\eta\le H-c_i^2+1.\)

Non-envy of \(A_i\) towards its right neighbor gives

\((2c_i+\tfrac12)c_i+\eta+1 \ge (2c_i+\tfrac12)(c_i+1)+H-(c_i+1)^2+1,\)

which simplifies to

\(\eta\ge H-c_i^2-\tfrac12.\)

Integrality therefore implies

\(\eta\in\{H-c_i^2,\ H-c_i^2+1\}.\)

We have proved that \(A_i\) can receive only \(p_i(0)\) and \(p_i(1)\). Furthermore,

\(v^{A_i}\cdot p_i(1)-v^{A_i}\cdot p_i(0)=1.\)

Both bundles cannot receive positive mass: the required within-type comparison would make recipients of \(p_i(0)\) envy recipients of \(p_i(1)\). Consequently, for some \(e_i\in\{0,1\}\), the entire mass \(\alpha_i\) receives \(p_i(e_i)\).

The second-coordinate supply equality is now exactly

\(\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_ie_i=T/W,\)

or equivalently,

\(\sum_{i=1}^ka_ie_i=T.\)

Thus every feasible allocation supplies a solution to SUBSET SUM.

3. Every subset solution gives an envy-free allocation.

Conversely, suppose \(e_1,\ldots,e_k\in\{0,1\}\) satisfy \(\sum_i a_ie_i=T\). Give each fixed type \(G_j\) its prescribed bundle \(g_j\), and give all of \(A_i\) the bundle \(p_i(e_i)\).

The masses, caps, prescribed allocations, and all three supply equalities hold by construction. It remains to verify every envy comparison.

For fixed types whose first coordinates are \(t\) and \(s\),

\(v^{G_t}\cdot g_t-v^{G_t}\cdot g_s=(t-s)^2\ge0,\)

where the subscript here identifies the fixed type by its first coordinate.

For a fixed type at \(t\) comparing its bundle with \(p_i(e_i)\),

\(v^{G_t}\cdot g_t-v^{G_t}\cdot p_i(e_i)=(c_i-t)^2-e_i\ge0.\)

The last inequality holds because \(c_i\) is even, \(t\) is odd, and \(e_i\le1\).

For \(A_i\) comparing its bundle with a fixed bundle at \(t\),

\(v^{A_i}\cdot p_i(e_i)-v^{A_i}\cdot g_t =(t-c_i)^2-\tfrac12(t-c_i)+e_i\ge\tfrac12.\)

Indeed, \(t-c_i\) is a nonzero integer, and for any such integer \(D\),

\(D^2-\tfrac12D\ge D^2-\tfrac12|D|\ge\tfrac12.\)

Finally, for distinct unfixed types \(A_i,A_h\), put \(D=c_h-c_i\). Then

\(v^{A_i}\cdot p_i(e_i)-v^{A_i}\cdot p_h(e_h) =D^2-\tfrac12D+e_i-e_h\ge2.\)

Here \(|D|\ge2\), so \(D^2-\tfrac12D\ge3\), while \(e_i-e_h\ge-1\).

Each type receives only one bundle, so its within-type comparisons also hold. These calculations cover every ordered comparison, including all fixed subpopulations. Equality can occur when a fixed type compares with a neighboring upper candidate, which is permitted.

Therefore the constructed instance is feasible if and only if the SUBSET SUM instance is feasible. This proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness under the stated restrictions.

4. Polynomial-size certificates.

The following argument applies to arbitrary \(m\). Let \(d=\tau+m\).

Suppose \(x\) is feasible, and subtract the prescribed lower bounds:

\(y_{\theta,b}=x_{\theta,b}-f_{\theta,b}\ge0.\)

For each pair \((\theta,b)\), define the integer column

\(a_{\theta,b}=(e_\theta,b)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m},\)

where \(e_\theta\) is the corresponding coordinate vector. The residual mass and supply equations are

\(\sum_{\theta,b}y_{\theta,b}a_{\theta,b}=h,\)

with

\(h=\left((\mu_\theta-\sum_bf_{\theta,b})_{\theta\in\Theta},\ q-\sum_{\theta,b}f_{\theta,b}b\right).\)

We can replace \(y\) by a nonnegative rational solution \(y'\), supported on a subset of its support, whose positive columns are linearly independent.

To see this, suppose its positive columns are dependent. Choose a nonzero rational dependence \(\sum_\ell z_\ell a_\ell=0\). Summing the first \(\tau\) coordinates shows \(\sum_\ell z_\ell=0\), so \(z\) has both positive and negative entries. Set

\(\lambda=\min_{z_\ell>0}y_\ell/z_\ell,\qquad y'_\ell=y_\ell-\lambda z_\ell.\)

This preserves the equations and nonnegativity, removes at least one positive entry, and introduces none. Repetition terminates with linearly independent positive columns, hence at most \(d\) residual entries.

Set \(x'=f+y'\). Every positively allocated pair in \(x'\) already had positive mass in \(x\). Thus every required envy comparison remains valid, and \(x'\) is feasible with at most

\(r+\tau+m\)

nonzero entries.

These entries also have polynomial binary encoding length. Let \(C=\max\{1,B_1,\ldots,B_m\}\). For \(s\le d\) independent residual columns, select \(s\) rows giving an invertible integer matrix. Its entries have absolute value at most \(C\), and its determinant has absolute value at most \(s!C^s\).

The vector \(h\) has a common denominator and cleared numerators of \(\mathcal O(L)\) bits. Cramer’s rule therefore bounds each residual coefficient’s encoding length by

\(\mathcal O\bigl(L+d\log(dC)\bigr).\)

Adding the prescribed masses preserves polynomial encoding length.

A verifier checks masses, supplies, caps, prescribed lower bounds, and every ordered comparison between positive entries. For \(s\le r+\tau+m\) entries, the envy checks require \(\mathcal O(ms^2)\) rational arithmetic operations, with polynomial-size operands. Verification consequently runs in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,r)\) bit time.

This proves membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\), completing the proof of \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness.

5. Exact construction algorithm and cap dependence.

Define

\(N=|\mathcal B|=\prod_{j=1}^m(B_j+1),\qquad d=\tau+m.\)

An exact deterministic algorithm enumerates every set of at most \(d\) residual pairs among the \(\tau N\) possible pairs. For each set:

  • Check envy-freeness of its union with the prescribed pairs.
  • Check that its columns \(a_{\theta,b}\) are linearly independent.
  • Solve the residual equations exactly, and check consistency and nonnegativity.
  • If successful, output \(f+y\), omitting zero entries.

Each accepted solution is feasible. Conversely, the sparse-certificate argument guarantees that some enumerated set succeeds whenever the instance is feasible.

The running time is bounded by

\(\left(1+\tau\prod_{j=1}^m(B_j+1)\right)^{\tau+m} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,r).\)

For the requested three-kind case, this becomes

\(\left(1+\tau(B_1+1)(B_2+1)(B_3+1)\right)^{\tau+3} \operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,r).\)

The output has at most \(r+\tau+m\) nonzero entries of polynomial encoding length.

A running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,r\) would imply \(\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}\). Even allowing polynomial dependence on the numerical caps does not avoid this consequence: the reduction already has \(B_{\max}=\mathcal O(\tau^2)\).

Finally, scaling the constructed masses by \(W\) produces \(W\) discrete agents, and \(W\) may be exponential in the source input length. Thus the supplied [discrete algorithm](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09327v1), whose bound depends on the number of discrete agents and items, does not imply polynomial time for this binary-encoded continuous problem.
