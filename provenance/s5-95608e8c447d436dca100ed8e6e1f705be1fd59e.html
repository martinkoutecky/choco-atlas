The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), utility functions \(U_t\) with continuously differentiable concave \(f_t(x)=U'_t(x)x\), and an explicitly represented strictly convex differentiable per-capita resource penalty \(\Psi_\mu\), compute an allocation \(x_t\geq 0\) within \(\varepsilon\) of maximizing \(V_\mu(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t f_t(x_t)-\Psi_\mu(x)\). The penalty may depend on per-capita loads such as \(\sum_t\mu_t a_{\ell t}x_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(m\) denotes the number of resource loads.
Each type has a nonnegative, nondecreasing, continuously differentiable concave utility \(U_t:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\), with \(f_t(z)=zU'_t(z)\) continuously differentiable and concave.
The per-capita penalty \(\Psi_\mu:[0,\infty)^\tau\to[0,\infty)\) is continuously differentiable and strictly convex. Any specified resource loads have the form \(s_\ell(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_{\ell t}x_t\), with binary-rational \(a_{\ell t}\geq0\) and \(1\leq\ell\leq m\).
Functions are explicitly represented by encoded evaluation routines returning values and first derivatives at rational arguments to absolute error \(2^{-k}\), in time bounded by a fixed polynomial in the representation length, argument bit length, and \(k\).
The input includes binary rationals \(B,G>0\), promising that the global maximum of \(V_\mu(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t f_t(x_t)-\Psi_\mu(x)\) over \(x\geq0\) is attained in \([0,B]^\tau\), and that \(\|\nabla V_\mu(x)\|_2\leq G\) throughout that box.
Given binary-rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), compute rational \(x\geq0\) with \(V_\mu(x)\geq\max_{y\geq0}V_\mu(y)-\varepsilon\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including function representations and numerical bounds.

Is this allocation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite user lists, multiplicative gradient dynamics are claimed to converge to a maximizer of penalized auction revenue under the corresponding concavity and strict-convexity assumptions; no polynomial bit-complexity bound is given ([Nguyen–Vojnović](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/MSR-TR-2010-145.pdf)). That revenue differs from the mass-weighted objective defined above.
No publication settling this precise mass-allocation problem was located in the literature search.

Proven result

The promised allocation problem is deterministically solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

The proof uses weak convex optimization with approximate evaluations. It does not require a convergence-rate bound for multiplicative gradient dynamics, or direct evaluation of the derivatives \(f'_t\).

1. Concavity and the promised box

Set \(n=\tau\), \(Q=[0,B]^n\), and \(V=V_\mu\).

Each \(f_t\) is concave, and each mass \(\mu_t\) is positive. Hence \(\sum_t\mu_t f_t(x_t)\) is concave. Since \(\Psi_\mu\) is strictly convex, \(V\) is strictly concave.

By the attainment promise,

\[ \max_{x\geq0}V(x)=\max_{x\in Q}V(x). \]

Moreover, the gradient bound implies that \(V\) is \(G\)-Lipschitz on \(Q\). Indeed, for \(x,y\in Q\), their joining segment lies in \(Q\), and

\[ |V(y)-V(x)| =\left|\int_0^1\nabla V(x+s(y-x))\cdot(y-x)\,ds\right| \leq G\|y-x\|_2. \]

Thus it suffices to optimize a concave Lipschitz function on the explicitly known box.

2. Normalization and evaluation complexity

Compute a rational \(q\) satisfying \(|q-V(0)|\leq1/4\). This is possible in polynomial time: \(f_t(0)=0\), so \(V(0)=-\Psi_\mu(0)\).

Define

\[ H=1+GBn,\qquad C=[0,1]^n,\qquad F(z)=\frac{V(Bz)-q}{H}. \]

The function \(F\) is concave. For every \(z\in C\),

\[ |V(Bz)-q| \leq GB\sqrt n+\frac14 \leq H, \]

so \(-1\leq F(z)\leq1\). Also,

\[ |F(z)-F(z')| \leq\frac{GB}{H}\|z-z'\|_2 \leq\|z-z'\|_2. \]

Consequently, \(F\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz on \(C\).

We can evaluate \(F\) to arbitrary prescribed absolute accuracy in polynomial time, even if only the routines for \(U_t\) and \(\Psi_\mu\) are supplied. At a rational point \(x=Bz\), use \(f_t(x_t)=x_tU'_t(x_t)\). If every evaluated derivative \(U'_t(x_t)\), and the value of \(\Psi_\mu(x)\), has absolute error at most \(2^{-k}\), then the resulting value of \(V(x)\) has error at most

\[ \sum_t\mu_t x_t2^{-k}+2^{-k} \leq(B+1)2^{-k}. \]

Here we used \(x_t\leq B\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Thus an approximation to \(F(z)\) with error at most \(\eta>0\) follows by choosing a nonnegative integer \(k\) such that

\[ \frac{(B+1)2^{-k}}{H}\leq\eta. \]

The required \(k\), and the bit lengths of all rational arithmetic operands, are polynomial in the input length, the query length, and \(\log^+(1/\eta)\), where \(\log^+(r)=\max\{0,\log r\}\).

3. The convex body and its weak membership oracle

Consider the truncated hypograph

\[ K=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:z\in C,\ -2\leq w\leq F(z)\}. \]

Concavity and continuity of \(F\) imply that \(K\) is convex and closed. Its coordinate bounds make it compact.

Let

\[ a=(1/2,\ldots,1/2,-3/2),\qquad r=1/4,\qquad R=n+3. \]

The closed Euclidean ball of radius \(r\) centered at \(a\) lies in \(K\). Indeed, every point in that ball has its first \(n\) coordinates in \(C\), and its last coordinate belongs to \([-7/4,-5/4]\), which lies between \(-2\) and \(F(z)\) because \(F(z)\geq-1\).

Furthermore, every point of \(K\) has norm at most \(\sqrt{n+4}\leq R\). Hence we have explicit rational guarantees

\[ \overline{\mathbb B}_2(a,r)\subseteq K \subseteq\overline{\mathbb B}_2(0,R). \]

In particular, \(K\) is a full-dimensional convex body.

For \(\rho>0\), define its enlargement and erosion by

\[ K_{+\rho}=\{y:\operatorname{dist}(y,K)\leq\rho\}, \qquad K_{-\rho}=\{y:y+\overline{\mathbb B}_2(0,\rho)\subseteq K\}. \]

A weak membership oracle may certify either \(y\in K_{+\rho}\) or \(y\notin K_{-\rho}\).

We implement this oracle at a rational query \(y=(z,w)\) as follows.

  • If \(z\notin C\) or \(w<-2\), certify \(y\notin K_{-\rho}\), since \(y\notin K\).
  • Otherwise, evaluate \(\widehat F(z)\) with error at most \(\rho/2\).
  • If \(w\leq\widehat F(z)+\rho/2\), certify \(y\in K_{+\rho}\). This is valid because \(w\leq F(z)+\rho\); lowering \(w\) to \(\min\{w,F(z)\}\) reaches \(K\) within distance \(\rho\).
  • Otherwise, \(w>\widehat F(z)+\rho/2\geq F(z)\). Thus \(y\notin K\), and we certify \(y\notin K_{-\rho}\).

Every answer is valid, and the oracle runs in polynomial time in its query length and requested precision.

4. Applying deterministic weak optimization

We use the following standard theorem: given a convex body with a weak membership oracle and explicit rational inner-ball and outer-ball guarantees, weak linear optimization is deterministic oracle-polynomial time. For rational \(c\) and \(\delta>0\), it returns a rational \(y\in K_{+\delta}\) satisfying \(c\cdot p\leq c\cdot y+\delta\) for every \(p\in K_{-\delta}\), or correctly asserts that \(K_{-\delta}\) is empty. Oracle queries and requested precisions have polynomial encoding length. This is [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Corollary 4.3.12, with Definition 2.1.10](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1988.pdf).

Apply the theorem with \(c=(0,\ldots,0,1)\) and

\[ \delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{16H}. \]

Since \(\delta<1/16<r\), the point \(a\) belongs to \(K_{-\delta}\). Therefore the algorithm must return a rational point \(y=(u,v)\), rather than an emptiness assertion.

We now account explicitly for both tolerances in its guarantee.

Let \(F^*=\max_{z\in C}F(z)\), choose a maximizer \(z^*\), and put \(p^*=(z^*,F^*)\). Define

\[ p_\delta=(1-4\delta)p^*+4\delta a. \]

For every \(h\) with \(\|h\|_2\leq\delta\),

\[ p_\delta+h =(1-4\delta)p^*+4\delta\left(a+\frac{h}{4\delta}\right)\in K. \]

This follows because the parenthesized point lies in the known inner ball and \(K\) is convex. Hence \(p_\delta\in K_{-\delta}\).

Its last coordinate satisfies

\[ (p_\delta)_{n+1} =F^*-4\delta(F^*+3/2) \geq F^*-10\delta, \]

using \(F^*\leq1\). Weak optimality therefore gives

\[ v\geq F^*-11\delta. \]

5. Recovering an exactly feasible allocation

Let \(\bar z\) be the coordinatewise projection of \(u\) onto \(C\). It is rational and computable in polynomial time.

Because \((u,v)\in K_{+\delta}\) and \(K\) is compact, there exists \((z',w')\in K\) within distance \(\delta\). Projection onto \(C\) cannot increase distance to \(z'\in C\), so

\[ \|\bar z-z'\|_2\leq\delta,\qquad v\leq w'+\delta. \]

Since \(w'\leq F(z')\) and \(F\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz,

\[ v\leq F(z')+\delta\leq F(\bar z)+2\delta. \]

Combining the inequalities yields

\[ F(\bar z)\geq F^*-13\delta. \]

Output \(x=B\bar z\). This is rational and belongs to \(Q\). By the definition of \(F\) and the global-attainment promise,

\[ \max_{y\geq0}V(y)-V(x) =H\bigl(F^*-F(\bar z)\bigr) \leq13H\delta =\frac{13}{16}\varepsilon <\varepsilon. \]

This proves the requested allocation guarantee.

6. Bit complexity

The dimension of \(K\) is \(\tau+1\). Its geometric guarantees have polynomial encoding length, and \(H\) has polynomial encoding length in \(L+\log\tau\). In particular,

\[ \log(1/\delta) =\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log H+\log16. \]

The weak-optimization theorem makes polynomially many calls with polynomial query lengths and precision requirements. Section 2 implements each call using the promised fixed-polynomial evaluation routines. Projection and rescaling also use polynomial-time rational arithmetic.

Thus the entire algorithm has polynomial bit complexity in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\). It works directly with the support and its masses; the resource-load data are already covered by the input length and any dependence of the encoded penalty on those loads.
