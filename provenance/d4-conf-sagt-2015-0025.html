The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given roommate types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), anonymous strict rankings of acceptable types, and forbidden type pairs \(P\), choose symmetric matching masses \(x_{st}\ge0\) with \(\sum_s x_{st}\le\mu_t\). Define \(D_t(s;x)=\mu_t-\sum_r x_{tr}+\sum_{r\succ_t s}x_{tr}\), require \(\min\{D_s(t;x),D_t(s;x)\}=0\) for every acceptable pair, and decide whether some such \(x\) satisfies \(\sum_{\{s,t\}\in P}x_{st}\le K\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
An undirected, loopless acceptability graph \((T,E)\) specifies mutually acceptable type pairs; let \(m=|E|\). Each type strictly ranks its neighbors, identically for all individuals of that type, and prefers every acceptable partner to being unmatched. Write \(s\succ_t r\) when type \(t\) prefers \(s\) to \(r\).
The input also contains forbidden pairs \(P\subseteq E\) and a positive binary-rational threshold \(K\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A matching consists of real masses \(x_{st}=x_{ts}\geq0\), with \(x_{st}=0\) outside \(E\) and \(\sum_{s\in T}x_{st}\leq\mu_t\) for every \(t\). Each cross-type pair mass consumes that mass at both endpoints; within-type matching is excluded.
For \(\{s,t\}\in E\), define the mass willing to switch to type \(s\) by \(D_t(s;x)=\mu_t-\sum_{r\in T}x_{tr}+\sum_{r:\,s\succ_t r}x_{tr}\). Require \(\min\{D_s(t;x),D_t(s;x)\}=0\) for every acceptable pair, including forbidden pairs.
The decision problem asks whether such a stable matching exists with \(\sum_{\{s,t\}\in P}x_{st}\leq K\), counting each unordered pair once.

Is this continuous forbidden-mass decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete strict-preference roommates, deciding whether a stable matching uses at most a given number of forbidden edges is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even with preference lists of length at most three ([Cseh–Manlove](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.0271)).
For unit-capacity fractional roommates, maximizing welfare under ordinal stability is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even with strict preferences; this uses a different objective ([Chen–Roy–Sorge](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0013.pdf)). No result settling this exact forbidden-mass problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one, interpreting the society as a distribution.
  • Specified mutual acceptability through an undirected loopless type graph, excluded within-type matching, and prohibited matching on unacceptable pairs.
  • Specified that all acceptable partners are strictly preferred to remaining unmatched and that rankings contain no ties.
  • Corrected the supplied willingness formula to count mass assigned to worse partners, rather than better partners, as required by the discrete blocking definition.
  • Specified that forbidden pairs remain eligible for matching and blocking; their matched mass is penalized rather than prohibited.
  • Made the threshold a positive binary rational, carrying over positivity from the discrete decision problem while allowing mass-valued thresholds.
  • Allowed real matching masses, counted each unordered forbidden pair once, and defined the edge count and total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete

The continuous forbidden-mass decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when all type masses are equal dyadic rationals and every preference list has length at most three.

The proof below treats real-valued matchings directly; it does not assume that fractional solutions can be rounded to discrete stable matchings.

1. Rewriting stability

For an acceptable pair \(\{s,t\}\), define
\(B_t(s)=\{s\}\cup\{r:r\succ_t s\}\).

Since preferences are strict, partitioning the neighbors of \(t\) into those better than \(s\), \(s\) itself, and those worse than \(s\) gives
\(D_t(s;x)=\mu_t-\sum_{r\in B_t(s)}x_{tr}\).

Capacity feasibility implies \(D_t(s;x)\geq 0\). Thus stability on \(\{s,t\}\) is exactly the disjunction
\(\sum_{r\in B_t(s)}x_{tr}=\mu_t\)
or
\(\sum_{r\in B_s(t)}x_{sr}=\mu_s\).

We will use this identity both in the reduction and in proving membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

2. Construction from cubic vertex cover

We reduce from vertex cover on simple cubic graphs: given \(G=(V,F)\) and an integer \(k\), decide whether there is \(C\subseteq V\) with \(|C|\leq k\) meeting every edge. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; it is also the source problem used in the proof of [Cseh–Manlove, Theorem 4.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.0271).

Write \(V=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}\). We may restrict to \(1\leq k\leq n\), since thresholds outside this range are trivially decidable and can be mapped to fixed yes or no instances within the range.

For each \(v_i\), introduce six types
\(p_i^1,q_i^1,p_i^2,q_i^2,p_i^3,q_i^3\).
Superscripts are interpreted cyclically modulo three.

Add the six internal edges
\(\{p_i^r,q_i^r\}\) and \(\{q_i^r,p_i^{r+1}\}\), for \(r\in\{1,2,3\}\).

Assign the three edges incident to \(v_i\) bijectively to its three types \(p_i^1,p_i^2,p_i^3\). For every source edge \(\{v_i,v_j\}\), add an external edge between its assigned types at the two endpoints. Consequently, each \(p_i^r\) has exactly one external neighbor; denote it by \(z_i^r\).

The preference lists are:

  • \(p_i^r:\ q_i^r\succ z_i^r\succ q_i^{r-1}\).
  • \(q_i^r:\ p_i^{r+1}\succ p_i^r\).

These lists are strict and have length at most three.

Choose the smallest power of two \(N\) satisfying \(N\geq 6n\), and add \(N-6n\) isolated types. Give every type mass \(c=1/N\). There are exactly \(N\) types, so their masses sum to one. All masses are positive dyadic rationals.

Finally, set
\(P=\{\{p_i^1,q_i^1\}:1\leq i\leq n\}\)
and \(K=kc\).

The construction has \(\tau=N<12n\) types and \(m=6n+|F|\) edges. Its size and construction time are polynomial in the source input length.

3. Every stable matching has a forced form

Fix any stable matching \(x\) in the constructed society. For each gadget, write
\(a_i^r=x_{p_i^rq_i^r}\),
\(b_i^r=x_{q_i^rp_i^{r+1}}\), and
\(h_i^r=x_{p_i^rz_i^r}\).

The capacity constraints are
\(a_i^r+b_i^r\leq c\)
at \(q_i^r\), and
\(a_i^r+b_i^{r-1}+h_i^r\leq c\)
at \(p_i^r\).

Internal stability saturates every \(q\)-type

Consider \(\{p_i^r,q_i^r\}\). Type \(p_i^r\) ranks \(q_i^r\) first, while \(q_i^r\) ranks \(p_i^r\) last. Therefore,
\(D_{p_i^r}(q_i^r;x)=c-a_i^r\)
and
\(D_{q_i^r}(p_i^r;x)=c-a_i^r-b_i^r\).

Both quantities are nonnegative, and the second is no larger than the first. Their minimum can therefore be zero only if
\(a_i^r+b_i^r=c\).

Thus every \(q_i^r\) is saturated.

All external edges carry zero mass

Summing the three capacity inequalities at the \(p\)-types of gadget \(i\) gives
\(\sum_{r=1}^3(a_i^r+b_i^{r-1})+\sum_{r=1}^3h_i^r\leq 3c\).

Cyclic reindexing and the preceding equalities yield
\(\sum_{r=1}^3(a_i^r+b_i^{r-1}) =\sum_{r=1}^3(a_i^r+b_i^r)=3c\).

Hence \(\sum_{r=1}^3h_i^r=0\). Nonnegativity implies \(h_i^r=0\) for every \(r\). Moreover, the three \(p\)-capacity inequalities must all be tight, so
\(a_i^r+b_i^{r-1}=c\).

Comparing this equality with \(a_i^r+b_i^r=c\) gives \(b_i^r=b_i^{r-1}\). Consequently, there is a single parameter \(a_i\in[0,c]\) such that, throughout gadget \(i\),
\(a_i^r=a_i\) and \(b_i^r=c-a_i\).

Thus every stable matching saturates all six types of every gadget, carries zero mass on every external edge, and has this one-parameter internal form.

External stability imposes vertex-cover constraints

Consider the external edge representing \(\{v_i,v_j\}\), joining \(p_i^r\) to \(p_j^s\).

Type \(p_i^r\) is saturated. Its only partner worse than its external neighbor is \(q_i^{r-1}\), with matching mass \(c-a_i\). Therefore,
\(D_{p_i^r}(p_j^s;x)=c-a_i\).

Likewise,
\(D_{p_j^s}(p_i^r;x)=c-a_j\).

Stability of this external edge is consequently equivalent to
\(a_i=c\) or \(a_j=c\).

In particular,
\(C_x=\{v_i:a_i=c\}\)
is a vertex cover of \(G\).

4. Exact correspondence of objective values

A feasible society matching gives a small vertex cover

Only one edge per gadget is forbidden, and its mass is \(a_i\). Thus the forbidden mass is
\(\sum_{\{s,t\}\in P}x_{st}=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\).

Every vertex in \(C_x\) contributes exactly \(c\), while all other contributions are nonnegative. Hence
\(c|C_x|\leq\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\).

If the matching satisfies the threshold, then
\(c|C_x|\leq kc\), and therefore \(|C_x|\leq k\).

A small vertex cover gives a feasible society matching

Conversely, let \(C\) be a vertex cover with \(|C|\leq k\). Set \(a_i=c\) when \(v_i\in C\), and \(a_i=0\) otherwise. For every \(r\), assign
\(x_{p_i^rq_i^r}=a_i\)
and
\(x_{q_i^rp_i^{r+1}}=c-a_i\).
Set every external edge mass to zero.

Every gadget type has total incident mass \(c\), so all capacity constraints hold. The isolated types remain unmatched.

We check every acceptable edge:

  • On \(\{p_i^r,q_i^r\}\), type \(q_i^r\) is saturated and ranks both its possible partners at least as highly as \(p_i^r\). Hence \(D_{q_i^r}(p_i^r;x)=0\).
  • On \(\{q_i^r,p_i^{r+1}\}\), type \(p_i^{r+1}\) is saturated and ranks \(q_i^r\) last. Hence \(D_{p_i^{r+1}}(q_i^r;x)=0\).
  • On an external edge representing \(\{v_i,v_j\}\), the cover property gives \(v_i\in C\) or \(v_j\in C\). At that endpoint, \(a_i=c\) or \(a_j=c\), so its willingness to switch is zero.

These checks include the forbidden edges. Thus the matching is stable, and its forbidden mass is \(c|C|\leq kc=K\).

We have proved the required equivalence: \(G\) has a vertex cover of size at most \(k\) if and only if the constructed society has a stable matching of forbidden mass at most \(K\). This establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

More precisely, the minimum forbidden mass in the constructed instance equals \(c\) times the minimum vertex-cover size. Fractional gadget parameters cannot improve this optimum.

5. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Although matching masses may be real, every yes instance has a rational witness of polynomial bit length.

Given a feasible stable real matching, choose, for every acceptable edge, an endpoint at which the willingness to switch is zero. Fixing these choices produces a rational linear system consisting of:

  • Nonnegativity and capacity inequalities.
  • The forbidden-mass threshold inequality.
  • One equality \(\sum_{r\in B_t(s)}x_{tr}=\mu_t\) for each chosen endpoint.

There are \(m\) variables and polynomially many constraints. Every coefficient is in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and every right-hand side is zero, a mass, or \(K\).

The resulting polytope is nonempty because it contains the original real matching. It is bounded because each edge variable lies between zero and one. Therefore it has a vertex.

For completeness, its vertex coordinates have polynomial bit length. Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators appearing in the masses and \(K\); its bit length is \(O(L)\). At a vertex, choose \(m\) linearly independent tight constraints, with coefficient matrix \(A\). The matrix has integer entries of absolute value at most one. Hadamard’s inequality bounds \(|\det A|\) by \(m^{m/2}\). Applying Cramer’s rule after multiplying the right-hand side by \(Q\) shows that each coordinate has numerator and denominator bit length \(O(L+m\log(m+1))\).

This rational vertex satisfies the chosen zero-willingness equalities and hence all original stability conditions. A verifier can check capacities, the threshold, and stability using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. The case \(m=0\) is immediate.

Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). Together with the reduction, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness, and rules out a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
