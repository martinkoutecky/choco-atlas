The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given item classes \(G\), at most four additive valuation types \(t\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), compute a complete allocation of mass to integral bundles. Represent a bundle by \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{G}\), and let \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\) be the mass of type \(t\) receiving \(B\). Require \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}B_g=q_g\) for each \(g\in G\), and \(v_t(B)\ge (2/3)v_t(B'-\mathbf{1}_h)\) whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\), \(\sum_s x_{s,B'}>0\), and \(h\) is a good copy in \(B'\). What is the complexity of computing such an allocation in the number of item classes and the input bit length?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a set of \(m\geq 1\) item classes. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(1\leq\tau\leq4\) distinct additive valuation types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Values \(v_t(g)\) and per-capita supplies \(q_g\) are nonnegative binary rationals; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Ties and zero values are permitted.
A bundle is \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{G}\), including the empty bundle; copies within a class are identical. An allocation is a finite list of positive binary rational masses \(x_{t,B}\), with bundle coordinates encoded in binary; unlisted masses are zero.
Completeness requires \(\sum_Bx_{t,B}=\mu_t\) for every type and \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}B_g=q_g\) for every class. Supplies constrain aggregate consumption, with no additional per-bundle cap.
The allocation is \(\frac23\)-EFX if \(v_t(B)\geq\frac23v_t(B'-\mathbf1_h)\) whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\), \(\sum_sx_{s,B'}>0\), and \(B'_h\geq1\). Removal includes copies having zero value to the comparing type; comparisons use weak inequalities.

Can a complete \(\frac23\)-EFX mass allocation always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite societies with at most four additive valuation types, a terminating algorithm constructs a complete \(\frac23\)-EFX allocation; its termination bound uses the number of allocation configurations and does not establish polynomial time. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15380v1).
No result settling this precise mass allocation problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative binary rational singleton values and supplies, following the discrete goods domain and making the input encoding explicit.
  • Specified a finite allocation representation with rational masses and binary integer bundle coordinates.
  • Allowed empty bundles, zero supplies, zero values, and valuation ties; the discrete treatment includes empty bundles and states an extension beyond nondegenerate instances.
  • Interpreted per-capita supplies as aggregate constraints without imposing a per-person bundle cap.
  • Specified that every present copy can be removed in the EFX comparison, including copies of zero value, with no tie-breaking.

The new result is a polynomial-time construction for a checkable regime that includes nonproportional types and nonintegral supplies. The construction guarantees the stronger property of $\frac23$-envy-freeness before removing any item.

For each type, define its total per-capita value by
$$ W_t=\sum_{g\in G}q_gv_t(g). $$
Let
$$ I=\{g\in G:q_g\notin\mathbb Z\}, \qquad n=|I|, $$
and let $r$ be the rank over $\mathbb Q$ of the matrix
$$ V_I=(v_t(g))_{t\in[\tau],\,g\in I}. $$
Thus $r\leq\tau\leq4$. Define $\Delta_t$ to be the sum of the $r$ largest numbers in $\{v_t(g):g\in I\}$, with $\Delta_t=0$ when $r=0$.

Proven theorem. If
$$ W_t\geq5\Delta_t \qquad\text{for every type }t, $$
then a complete $\frac23$-EFX mass allocation can be computed in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$. Every produced bundle satisfies
$$ B_g\in\{\lfloor q_g\rfloor,\lceil q_g\rceil\}. $$

listed type–bundle pairs and has polynomial encoding length.

The proof below gives an explicit rational algorithm. It does not expand the population or the item multiplicities.

1. A simultaneous rounding lemma

We first prove the rounding statement underlying the theorem.

Lemma. Given nonnegative rational valuations and rational supplies, one can compute, in polynomial time, bundles $B^1,\ldots,B^K$ and positive rational weights $p_1,\ldots,p_K$ satisfying
$$ K\leq(n+1)(r+1), \qquad \sum_{j=1}^Kp_j=1, \qquad \sum_{j=1}^Kp_jB^j=q, $$
such that
$$ B^j_g\in\{\lfloor q_g\rfloor,\lceil q_g\rceil\} $$
and, simultaneously for every type and every produced bundle,
$$ |v_t(B^j)-W_t|\leq\Delta_t. $$

1.1 A polytope preserving all type values

Write
$$ a_g=\lfloor q_g\rfloor, \qquad f_g=q_g-a_g. $$
Only coordinates in $I$ need rounding; on these coordinates, $0<f_g<1$.

Consider the rational polytope
$$ P=\{z\in[0,1]^I:V_Iz=V_If\}. $$
It is nonempty because $f\in P$.

For $z\in P$, let
$$ J(z)=\{g\in I:0<z_g<1\}. $$
A point $z$ is a vertex of $P$ if and only if the columns of $V_I$ indexed by $J(z)$ are linearly independent.

To prove the forward implication, suppose those columns are dependent. There is a nonzero vector $d$, supported on $J(z)$, with $V_Id=0$. Since every coordinate in $J(z)$ lies strictly between zero and one, sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$ gives two distinct feasible points $z+\varepsilon d$ and $z-\varepsilon d$. Their midpoint is $z$, contradicting the vertex property.

Conversely, suppose the columns are independent and
$$ z=\frac{u+w}{2}, \qquad u,w\in P. $$
If $z_g$ is zero or one, the bounds on $u_g,w_g$ force $u_g=w_g=z_g$. Therefore $u-w$ is supported on $J(z)$. Since $V_I(u-w)=0$, independence gives $u=w=z$. This proves the characterization.

In particular, every vertex has at most $r$ fractional coordinates.

1.2 Constructing a vertex in a specified face

Given $p\in P$, we can find a vertex $z\in P$ that agrees with every zero or one coordinate of $p$.

Start at $z=p$. If the columns indexed by $J(z)$ are dependent, compute a nonzero rational null vector $d$ supported on $J(z)$. Move in its positive direction by
$$ \varepsilon= \min\left( \left\{\frac{1-z_g}{d_g}:d_g>0\right\} \cup \left\{\frac{z_g}{-d_g}:d_g<0\right\} \right). $$
Every quantity in this minimum is positive. The new point $z+\varepsilon d$ remains in $P$, and at least one fractional coordinate becomes zero or one. Coordinates already at a boundary remain fixed.

After at most $n$ such moves, the fractional columns are independent. The resulting point is the required vertex.

1.3 Decomposing the fractional supply vector

We next construct a convex decomposition of $f$ into at most $n+1$ vertices of $P$.

Maintain a residual point $p\in P$ and its remaining weight $\rho>0$, initially $p=f$ and $\rho=1$. Obtain a vertex $z$ agreeing with the boundary coordinates of $p$.

If $p=z$, retain $z$ with weight $\rho$ and finish. Otherwise define
$$ \lambda= \min\left( \left\{\frac{p_g}{z_g}:z_g>0\right\} \cup \left\{\frac{1-p_g}{1-z_g}:z_g<1\right\} \right). $$
Then $0<\lambda<1$.

Indeed, every displayed ratio is positive: a zero numerator would mean that $p_g$ is a boundary coordinate on which $z_g$ must agree with it, contradicting the condition under which that ratio is included. Furthermore, because $p\neq z$, some coordinate satisfies either $p_g<z_g$, giving the first ratio below one, or $p_g>z_g$, giving the second ratio below one.

Retain $z$ with weight $\rho\lambda$ and replace
$$ p\longleftarrow p'=\frac{p-\lambda z}{1-\lambda}, \qquad \rho\longleftarrow\rho(1-\lambda). $$
The definition of $\lambda$ ensures $0\leq p'_g\leq1$. Also,
$$ V_Ip' = \frac{V_If-\lambda V_If}{1-\lambda} = V_If, $$
so $p'\in P$.

Every boundary coordinate of $p$ remains fixed. At least one previously nonboundary coordinate becomes a boundary coordinate: a ratio attaining the minimum gives $p'_g=0$ or $p'_g=1$. Such an attaining coordinate must previously have been nonboundary, since boundary coordinates contribute only ratios equal to one.

Consequently, at most $n$ nonterminal iterations occur. Including the terminal vertex, we obtain
$$ f=\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}\beta_\ell z^\ell, \qquad s\leq n+1, \qquad \beta_\ell>0, \qquad \sum_\ell\beta_\ell=1. $$

1.4 Rounding each vertex with at most $r+1$ outcomes

Fix a vertex $z$. List its distinct fractional coordinate values, together with zero and one:
$$ 0=b_0<b_1<\cdots<b_k=1. $$
Since $z$ has at most $r$ fractional coordinates, $k\leq r+1$.

For each $j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$, assign weight $b_j-b_{j-1}$ to the binary vector
$$ y^{(j)}_g= \begin{cases} 1,&z_g\geq b_j,\\ 0,&z_g<b_j. \end{cases} $$
For every coordinate,
$$ \sum_{j=1}^{k}(b_j-b_{j-1})y^{(j)}_g=z_g. $$
This follows because the intervals whose upper endpoint is at most $z_g$ have total length $z_g$.

Integral coordinates of $z$ are unchanged in every outcome. Hence
$$ \left|\sum_{g\in I}v_t(g)(y^{(j)}_g-z_g)\right| \leq \sum_{g\in J(z)}v_t(g) \leq\Delta_t. $$
The last inequality uses $|J(z)|\leq r$ and nonnegativity.

Form the bundle whose coordinates are $a_g+y^{(j)}_g$ on $I$ and $a_g$ elsewhere. Since $z\in P$,
$$ \sum_gv_t(g)a_g+\sum_{g\in I}v_t(g)z_g = \sum_gv_t(g)q_g = W_t. $$
Thus each rounded bundle has type-$t$ value within $\Delta_t$ of $W_t$.

Combine these distributions using the vertex weights $\beta_\ell$. The coordinate expectations are exactly $q$, and the number of outcomes is at most $(n+1)(r+1)$. This proves all algebraic assertions of the lemma.

1.5 Polynomial running time and rational encoding

Here are the bit-length details.

Multiply each row of $V_I$ by the product of the denominators in that row. This gives an integer matrix $A$ with the same constraints and rank. Its entries have polynomial bit length. Write
$$ P=\{z\in[0,1]^I:Az=s\}, \qquad s=Af. $$
The vector $s$ has polynomial rational encoding length.

The null directions used above can be chosen as integer vectors of polynomial bit length. To see this, take $k$ independent columns among a dependent set and another column in their span. Restrict to $k$ rows giving an invertible matrix $C$. Cramer’s rule expresses that additional column as a combination of the independent columns; multiplying by $\det C$ gives an integer dependence. If $H$ bounds the absolute matrix entries, its coefficients have magnitude at most $k!H^k$. When $k=0$, a unit vector suffices.

In a boundary move, if the current point has common denominator $P_0$ and the limiting coordinate has nonzero integer direction coefficient $d_g$, the new point admits common denominator $P_0|d_g|$. Therefore the at most $n$ moves in one vertex computation increase denominator bit length by only a polynomial amount.

Moreover, every vertex of $P$ has a common denominator of uniformly polynomial bit length, independently of the residual point from which it was found. Its fractional columns are independent. Selecting an invertible row restriction and applying Cramer’s rule to
$$ A_{J(z)}z_{J(z)} = s-\sum_{g\notin J(z)}A_gz_g $$
proves this assertion, because the coordinates outside $J(z)$ are zero or one.

For completeness, the outer decomposition also preserves polynomial bit lengths. Suppose $p$ has common denominator $P_0$ and the chosen vertex $z$ has common denominator $Z$. Write
$$ p_g=\frac{u_g}{P_0}, \qquad z_g=\frac{a_g}{Z}. $$
The selected value of $\lambda$ has the form
$$ \lambda=\frac{bZ}{P_0c}, $$
where either $(b,c)=(u_g,a_g)$ or $(b,c)=(P_0-u_g,Z-a_g)$. Thus $c$ is a positive integer at most $Z$. The residual point admits common denominator
$$ P_1=P_0c-bZ, $$
which is positive and smaller than $P_0c$. Its denominator bit length therefore increases by at most the bit length of $Z$. There are at most $n$ such updates.

The retained weights are products of polynomially many rational factors of polynomial bit length. Threshold rounding introduces only differences between vertex coordinates. Bundle coordinates are floors or ceilings of input supplies. All computations and outputs consequently have polynomial bit length.

Gaussian elimination, the boundary moves, and the decomposition require polynomially many exact rational operations. This completes the constructive lemma.

2. From rounding to a complete fair allocation

Apply the lemma and give every type the same bundle distribution:
$$ x_{t,B^j}=\mu_tp_j. $$
Combine duplicate pairs if necessary. Every listed mass is positive.

For each type,
$$ \sum_jx_{t,B^j} = \mu_t\sum_jp_j = \mu_t. $$
For each class,
$$ \sum_{t,j}x_{t,B^j}B^j_g = \left(\sum_t\mu_t\right)\sum_jp_jB^j_g = q_g. $$
Thus the allocation is complete.

For any comparing type $t$, any bundle $B$ assigned to that type, and any globally supported bundle $B'$, the lemma gives
$$ v_t(B)\geq W_t-\Delta_t, \qquad v_t(B')\leq W_t+\Delta_t. $$
The assumed condition yields
$$ W_t-\Delta_t \geq \frac23(W_t+\Delta_t), $$
because the difference between the two sides is $(W_t-5\Delta_t)/3$.

Therefore
$$ v_t(B)\geq\frac23v_t(B'). $$
Nonnegativity then gives, for every permitted removal,
$$ v_t(B) \geq \frac23v_t(B') \geq \frac23v_t(B'-\mathbf1_h). $$
This includes removal of zero-valued copies. Empty targets cause no difficulty. The theorem is proved.

More generally, the same construction guarantees $\alpha$-envy-freeness whenever $0<\alpha<1$ and
$$ W_t\geq\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}\Delta_t \qquad\text{for every type }t. $$

A nonproportional example covered by the theorem

Take $24$ classes, each with supply $\frac12$. Type $1$ values classes $1$ through $20$ at one and the others at zero. Type $2$ values classes $5$ through $24$ at one and the others at zero. Their positive masses may be arbitrary.

The two valuation rows are nonproportional, and $r=2$. For both types,
$$ W_t=10, \qquad \Delta_t=2. $$
Hence the theorem applies at equality. It constructs bundles whose value to either type lies between $8$ and $12$, and
$$ 8=\frac23\cdot12. $$
This example uses nonintegral supplies and zero values, so the result is not restricted to identical bundles or strictly positive proportional valuations.

3. General existence and short witnesses

The following results apply without the sufficient condition above. They explain why the remaining obstacle is finding a compatible bundle support efficiently.

3.1 Existence in the mass model

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the masses and supplies. Then
$$ N_t=D\mu_t, \qquad Q_g=Dq_g $$
are integers, with $N_t>0$ and $\sum_tN_t=D$.

Create $N_t$ finite agents of type $t$ and $Q_g$ separately labelled copies of class $g$. The finite-society theorem applies to at most four additive valuation types and guarantees a complete $\frac23$-EFX allocation. The cited paper combines correctness in Theorem 3 with termination in Lemma 7. [Prakash, Mehta and Nimbhorkar](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15380v1)

Ties and zeros can be handled explicitly. Number the finitely many labelled goods and add $\varepsilon2^j$ to every type’s value for labelled good $j$. For sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, originally unequal subset values remain unequal, while originally equal subset values are separated by the distinct binary subset sums. The perturbation is identical for agents of the same type, so there are still at most four types.

Apply finite existence along a sequence of such perturbations tending to zero. There are only finitely many complete allocations, so one allocation recurs along an infinite subsequence. Taking limits in its weak fairness inequalities proves fairness for the original valuations, including removal of originally zero-valued goods.

If $n_{t,B}$ finite agents of type $t$ receive class-count vector $B$, set
$$ x_{t,B}=\frac{n_{t,B}}D. $$
The resulting mass allocation is complete and satisfies the required fairness inequalities. Every supported bundle obeys
$$ B_g\leq Dq_g. $$
This is an existence argument, not a polynomial-time expansion.

3.2 Sparsification and encoding length

Given a complete fair allocation, associate to each listed pair $(t,B)$ the column
$$ a_{t,B}=(e_t,B)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m}. $$
Completeness is the system
$$ Ax=b, \qquad x\geq0, \qquad b=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau,q_1,\ldots,q_m). $$
Its feasible set is nonempty and compact: summing the type equations gives $\sum_jx_j=1$.

At an extreme point, the positive columns must be independent. Otherwise a nonzero dependence supported on positive coordinates permits small feasible perturbations in both directions, contradicting extremality. Hence at most $m+\tau$ pairs remain.

Deleting pairs and changing their positive masses preserves fairness: every retained comparison was required before, and its inequality does not depend on the numerical masses.

Apply this to the allocation obtained by finite expansion. Put
$$ R=m+\tau, \qquad H=\max\{1,D,\max_tD\mu_t,\max_gDq_g\}. $$
The input encoding implies $\log H=O(L)$.

For $k\leq R$ independent positive columns, choose $k$ rows forming a nonsingular integer matrix $C$. If $C_i$ replaces column $i$ with the selected integer vector $Db$, Cramer’s rule gives
$$ x_i=\frac{\det C_i}{D\det C}. $$
All entries of both matrices have absolute value at most $H$, so
$$ |\det C|,\ |\det C_i|\leq k!H^k. $$
Each mass therefore has bit length $O(R(L+\log R))$, and each bundle coordinate has bit length $O(L)$.

Thus every valid instance has a complete fair allocation of polynomial encoding length with at most $m+\tau$ listed pairs.

3.3 Verification and a limitation on hardness reductions

For a nonempty bundle define
$$ R_t(B)=v_t(B)-\min_{h:B_h>0}v_t(h), $$
and set $R_t(\varnothing)=0$. For each type define
$$ \ell_t=\min_{B:x_{t,B}>0}v_t(B). $$
The minima exist because $\mu_t>0$.

The allocation is $\frac23$-EFX exactly when
$$ \ell_t\geq\frac23R_t(B') $$
for every type and every globally supported bundle. Indeed, $R_t(B')$ is the largest value remaining after any permitted one-copy removal. Zero-valued copies are included in its minimum.

These inequalities and the completeness equations are polynomial-time verifiable by exact rational arithmetic. Consequently, restricting outputs to the polynomial witness bound gives a total, polynomially balanced search relation in $\mathrm{TFNP}$. Exhaustive witness enumeration gives a $2^{\operatorname{poly}(L)}$-time upper bound.

There is also a precise obstruction to a particular interpretation of “NP-hard.” Suppose polynomial-time maps $f,d$ satisfy:

  • For every Boolean formula $\varphi$, $f(\varphi)$ is a valid allocation instance.
  • For every valid solution $x$ of that instance,

$$ d(\varphi,x)=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \varphi\text{ is satisfiable}. $$

Then $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. A polynomial-length valid allocation with decoder output zero would certify unsatisfiability. Such certificates exist for unsatisfiable formulas by totality and the witness bound; they cannot exist for satisfiable formulas by condition 2. Thus $\mathrm{UNSAT}\in\mathrm{NP}$.

This statement concerns exactly the specified reduction conditions. It does not exclude every possible notion of search hardness.

4. Polynomial-time exact EFX for proportional types

For completeness, the one-type construction from the notes can be justified independently, and the verifier’s representational issue can be repaired.

4.1 One type

Scale the rational valuation to nonnegative integer weights $w_g$. Maintain groups $(p_i,B_i)$ with total mass one, initially one empty-bundle group.

Process positive-weight classes in nonincreasing weight order. Skip zero supplies.

For the current class of weight $c>0$ and supply $q>0$, let
$$ a_i=\sum_gw_g(B_i)_g, $$
and define, for integers $y\geq0$,
$$ K(y)=\sum_i p_i \max\left\{0,\left\lfloor\frac{y-a_i}{c}\right\rfloor+1\right\}. $$
Find the smallest $y$ with $K(y)\geq q$. This can be done by binary search, since $K$ is nondecreasing and
$$ U=\max_i a_i+c\lceil q\rceil $$
satisfies $K(U)>q$.

Set
$$ k_i=\max\left\{0,\left\lceil\frac{y-a_i}{c}\right\rceil\right\}, \qquad R=\sum_i p_ik_i, \qquad E=\{i:a_i+k_ic=y\}. $$
The base count $k_i$ counts additions with pre-addition utility strictly below $y$. Thus $R=K(y-1)$ when $y>0$, while $R=0$ when $y=0$. Minimality of $y$ and the additions at utility exactly $y$ give
$$ R\leq q\leq R+\sum_{i\in E}p_i. $$
Give group $i$ its $k_i$ copies, then give one further copy to total mass $q-R$ within $E$. Traversing those groups requires at most one split and consumes exactly $q$.

To prove exact EFX preservation, every resulting comparing bundle has utility at least $y$. Any changed target contains only items of weight at least $c$, because classes are processed in nonincreasing order and zero-weight classes have not yet been processed.

For a target receiving only its positive base count,
$$ v(B')-c=a_i+(k_i-1)c<y. $$
For a target receiving the extra copy,
$$ v(B')-c=a_i+k_ic=y. $$
Removing any item therefore leaves utility at most $y$, proving exact EFX toward changed targets. For unchanged targets, the previous inequalities remain true because comparing utilities never decrease. The initial empty allocation is exact EFX, completing the induction over positive classes.

For a zero-weight class, choose a minimum-utility group of mass $p$ and write
$$ q=kp+r, \qquad k=\left\lfloor\frac qp\right\rfloor, \qquad 0\leq r<p. $$
Give $k$ copies to mass $p-r$ and $k+1$ copies to mass $r$, omitting zero-mass parts. Utilities remain unchanged. Every modified target has minimum utility, and removing any item leaves no more than that minimum. Thus exact EFX is preserved, including removal of zero-weight copies.

Each class causes at most one split, so there are at most $m+1$ groups. Let $D_0$ be the product of supply denominators. Every group mass remains an integer multiple of $1/D_0$: each split mass is a supply minus an integer linear combination of current masses. Hence every positive mass is at least $1/D_0$, and completeness implies
$$ (B_i)_g\leq D_0q_g. $$
These bounds, together with the integer valuation scaling, give polynomial bit lengths for utilities, search bounds, and arithmetic. The algorithm is therefore polynomial-time.

4.2 An input-derived common valuation

Suppose the valuation vectors are proportional.

If every vector is zero, use any complete common bundle distribution, for example the floor-or-ceiling rounding construction above. Every fairness inequality is then zero against zero.

Otherwise choose a nonzero input valuation vector $v_*$ and a class $g_*$ with $v_*(g_*)>0$. The common valuation is the rational input vector $v_*$ itself. For each type, compute
$$ c_t=\frac{v_t(g_*)}{v_*(g_*)}. $$
Under proportionality,
$$ v_t(g)=c_tv_*(g) \qquad\text{for every }g. $$
These identities can also be checked directly in polynomial time. The common valuation and the constants have polynomial rational encoding length.

Apply the one-type algorithm to $v_*$, obtaining weights $p_i$ and bundles $B_i$, and set
$$ x_{t,B_i}=\mu_tp_i. $$
Completeness follows from $\sum_t\mu_t=1$. Multiplying the exact-EFX inequalities for $v_*$ by $c_t\geq0$ proves exact EFX for every type. This repairs the representational gap without assuming an externally supplied common valuation.

5. What remains open

The simultaneous rounding theorem gives a polynomial algorithm for nonproportional types whenever $W_t\geq5\Delta_t$ for every type. The proportional-types construction covers a different regime without that assumption.

Neither argument settles instances in which types are nonproportional and some type has $W_t<5\Delta_t$. In that regime, the rounding lemma still controls absolute value errors, but those errors may be too large to imply multiplicative fairness. No argument here repairs that failure or turns it into a hardness reduction.

1. Simultaneous rounding lemma

1.1 Definitions and boundary cases

The definitions of $I$, $n$, $r$, and $\Delta_t$ are valid. In particular, $r\leq n$, so selecting the $r$ largest values is well-defined whenever $r>0$.

If $n=0$, the polytope contains the unique empty coordinate vector, and the construction produces the single bundle $q$, which is integral. The decomposition terminates before evaluating any minimum over coordinates.

If $r=0$, every entry of $V_I$ is zero. Consequently, rounding coordinates in $I$ changes no type’s value, consistently with $\Delta_t=0$.

1.2 Vertex characterization

Both directions are correct.

  • A dependence among fractional columns gives a nonzero feasible direction supported entirely on interior coordinates. Sufficiently small perturbations in both directions remain feasible, contradicting extremality.
  • Conversely, coordinates at either boundary must agree in any midpoint representation. The difference between the two endpoints is therefore supported on the fractional coordinates, and independence forces that difference to vanish.

Thus every vertex has at most $r$ fractional coordinates.

1.3 Finding a vertex in the current face

The boundary-move formula is correct. Its candidate set is nonempty because $d$ is nonzero, and every included ratio is positive. The chosen step preserves the equality constraints and the box constraints, fixes existing boundary coordinates, and places at least one additional coordinate at a boundary.

The termination bound of at most $n$ moves is valid.

1.4 Decomposing $f$ into vertices

The assertions $0<\lambda<1$ are justified as written:

  • Boundary agreement excludes zero numerators in included ratios.
  • Since $p\neq z$, a differing coordinate supplies a ratio strictly below one.
