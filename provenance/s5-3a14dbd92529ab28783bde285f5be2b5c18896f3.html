The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix a finite public catalogue \(T\) of valuation vectors over item classes \(J\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), with \(\sum_j q_j\leq 1\). At every scale \(N\) for which the counts \(N\mu_t\) and \(Nq_j\) are integral, these counts specify the population and item copies; the mechanism knows the catalogue and aggregate counts but not individual types, and each bidder privately reports its valuation vector on arrival in a uniformly random permutation. Among mechanism families truthful in dominant strategies at each scale, what is the supremal worst-case asymptotic expected welfare ratio \(\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[W_N]/(N\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q,v))\), over instances with at most \(\tau\) types and \(m=|J|\), where \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q,v)=\max_{y\geq0}\sum_{t,j}\mu_t v_{tj}y_{tj}\) subject to \(\sum_j y_{tj}\leq1\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t y_{tj}\leq q_j\)?

Result

For all positive integers \(\tau\) and \(m\),

\[ R_{\tau,m}=1. \]

In fact, a deterministic mechanism achieves welfare exactly \(N\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q,v)\) at every admissible scale \(N\), for every arrival order. It satisfies dominant-strategy truthfulness after every report history.

The decisive information is the exact multiset of valuations: the mechanism initially knows that there are \(N\mu_t\) bidders of each type. Under truthful reporting, it can track the remaining multiset without knowing the correspondence between identities and types.

This is consistent with the dynamic-pricing result of Cohen-Addad et al., whose prices are computed before knowing the next buyer. The argument below proves directly the pricing property needed here. [Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05646)

1. The remaining-market linear program

Let \(r_t\) denote the number of remaining bidders of type \(t\), and let \(s_j\) denote the remaining inventory of class \(j\). These are nonnegative integers.

Introduce variables \(x_{tj}\) for allocations, \(a_t\) for unmatched bidders, and \(b_j\) for unused copies. Define

\(\displaystyle W(r,s)=\max \sum_{t,j}v_{tj}x_{tj}\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_jx_{tj}+a_t=r_t\quad\text{for every }t,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_tx_{tj}+b_j=s_j\quad\text{for every }j,\)

and \(x_{tj},a_t,b_j\geq0\).

This polytope is nonempty: take \(x=0\), \(a=r\), and \(b=s\). It is bounded because every variable is bounded by one of the counts.

Integrality. Every vertex of this polytope is integral. To verify this, negate the item equations in its constraint matrix. Each allocation column then has one entry \(1\) and one entry \(-1\); each slack column has just one nonzero entry, equal to \(1\) or \(-1\).

Every square submatrix has determinant in \(\{0,1,-1\}\): if a column has at most one nonzero entry, expand along that column and use induction; otherwise every column has two opposite nonzero entries, so its rows sum to zero and its determinant vanishes. Thus the matrix is totally unimodular. Its right-hand side is integral, so its vertices are integral.

Consequently, \(W(r,s)\) is also the maximum welfare of an actual allocation of the remaining bidders and copies.

Call an allocation variable \(x_{tj}\) allowed if it is positive in some optimal solution. Call unmatched choice \(a_t\) allowed if it is positive in some optimal solution. All other variables of these two kinds are forbidden.

Because the optimal face is the convex hull of integral optimal vertices, an allowed variable is at least \(1\) in some integral optimal solution. Hence:

  • An allowed \(x_{tj}\) means that assigning one bidder of type \(t\) to one copy of class \(j\) can be extended to an optimal allocation.
  • An allowed \(a_t\) means that leaving one bidder of type \(t\) unmatched can be extended to an optimal allocation.

2. A price menu admitting only allowed choices

We prove the following lemma.

Pricing lemma. For any remaining market, there are nonnegative rational class prices \(p_j\) such that, for every type \(t\) with \(r_t>0\), every utility-maximizing choice among available classes and being unmatched is allowed.

The outside option has utility \(0\); class \(j\) has utility \(v_{tj}-p_j\).

Constructing strict margins

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input valuations, written in lowest terms. Then \(Dv_{tj}\) is integral for every pair \(t,j\). At an integral vertex, welfare is therefore an integer multiple of \(1/D\).

Fix the original scale \(N\), and set

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\frac{1}{D(N+1)}.\)

Perturb the remaining-market objective by adding \(\varepsilon\) times the sum of its forbidden allocation and unmatched variables:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,j}v_{tj}x_{tj}+\varepsilon\left(\sum_{x_{tj}\text{ forbidden}}x_{tj}+\sum_{a_t\text{ forbidden}}a_t\right).\)

Write \(n=\sum_tr_t\leq N\). The parenthesized sum is at most \(n\), since \(\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}+\sum_ta_t=n\).

At every original optimal solution, the perturbation is zero. At any nonoptimal vertex, original welfare is at most \(W(r,s)-1/D\). Its perturbed value is therefore at most

\(\displaystyle W(r,s)-\frac1D+\varepsilon n<W(r,s).\)

Since the feasible polytope is bounded and is the convex hull of its vertices, the perturbed program has the same optimal face and the same optimal value \(W(r,s)\).

Dual prices

The perturbed dual is

\(\displaystyle \min \sum_tr_tu_t+\sum_js_jp_j\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle u_t+p_j\geq v_{tj}+\varepsilon\,\mathbf{1}_{\{x_{tj}\text{ forbidden}\}},\)

\(\displaystyle u_t\geq\varepsilon\,\mathbf{1}_{\{a_t\text{ forbidden}\}},\qquad p_j\geq0.\)

The primal is feasible and bounded, so linear-programming strong duality gives an optimal dual solution with objective \(W(r,s)\). All coefficients are rational, so a rational optimal solution can be selected.

These dual variables also satisfy the unperturbed dual constraints. For any original optimal primal solution,

\(\displaystyle 0=\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}(u_t+p_j-v_{tj})+\sum_ta_tu_t+\sum_jb_jp_j.\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Thus positivity of a variable in an optimal solution forces its corresponding dual slack to vanish. Together with the strict perturbed constraints, this proves

\(\displaystyle u_t+p_j=v_{tj}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_{tj}\text{ is allowed},\)

and

\(\displaystyle u_t=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_t\text{ is allowed}.\)

Verifying the demand property

Fix a type \(t\) with \(r_t>0\). Dual feasibility gives \(v_{tj}-p_j\leq u_t\) for every available class, and \(0\leq u_t\).

In any optimal primal solution, the equation \(\sum_jx_{tj}+a_t=r_t>0\) ensures that either \(a_t>0\) or some \(x_{tj}>0\).

If \(a_t>0\), then \(u_t=0\), and the outside option attains utility \(u_t\). Otherwise, some positive \(x_{tj}\) identifies an available class attaining \(v_{tj}-p_j=u_t\).

Therefore the maximum utility is exactly \(u_t\). Any maximizing class has \(u_t+p_j=v_{tj}\), so its allocation variable is allowed. If the outside option maximizes utility, then \(u_t=0\), so unmatched choice is allowed. This proves the lemma, including every tie case.

3. The mechanism and truthfulness after arbitrary histories

Initially, set \(r_t=N\mu_t\) and \(s_j=Nq_j\). Fix a public order on classes and the outside option for resolving utility ties.

Before reading each bidder’s report:

  • Compute the pricing lemma’s menu from the current \(r,s\).
  • Offer every class with positive actual inventory at its price \(p_j\), together with the outside option at payment zero.

For reported vector \(\widehat v\), choose a reported-utility maximizer using the fixed tie rule. Assign and charge immediately. A matched bidder pays the chosen class price; an unmatched bidder pays zero.

Afterward, if the report equals a support valuation whose remaining count is positive, decrement that type count. Otherwise, enter a permanent fallback mode: subsequent bidders receive the same kind of utility-maximizing menu, with every available class priced at zero. Actual inventory is updated after every allocation.

The fallback defines the mechanism after arbitrary reports. Under truthful reporting it is never entered.

Dominant-strategy truthfulness. Fix any history, including histories containing false reports. The current menu is determined before the current report. Every possible report produces one of that menu’s outcomes at its fixed payment. Truthful reporting selects an outcome maximizing the bidder’s actual quasilinear utility. No deviation can yield greater utility.

A bidder’s report may affect future menus, but the bidder’s own allocation and payment are already irrevocable, and the bidder appears only once. Thus this argument applies after every history. The mechanism is deterministic, so it also satisfies the requirement for every fixed realization of internal randomness.

All prices are nonnegative. The outside option guarantees nonnegative truthful utility, proving individual rationality. Inventory restrictions guarantee feasibility, and unmatched payment is always zero.

4. Exact welfare preservation

Under truthful reporting, the recorded counts always equal the true remaining type counts.

Suppose a bidder of type \(t\) receives class \(j\). The pricing lemma says \(x_{tj}\) is allowed. Hence some integral optimal allocation has \(x_{tj}\geq1\). Removing that assignment yields a feasible residual allocation with welfare \(W(r,s)-v_{tj}\). Conversely, adding this assignment to any residual allocation produces a feasible original allocation. Therefore

\(\displaystyle W(r,s)=v_{tj}+W(r-e_t,s-e_j).\)

If the bidder is unmatched, \(a_t\) is allowed. An integral optimum has \(a_t\geq1\), and removing one unmatched bidder preserves its welfare. Conversely, any residual allocation extends by leaving the current bidder unmatched. Thus

\(\displaystyle W(r,s)=W(r-e_t,s).\)

It follows inductively that accumulated welfare plus remaining optimal welfare always equals the initial optimal welfare. After the final arrival, remaining welfare is zero. The mechanism therefore achieves the initial optimum for every arrival order.

Finally, substitute \(x_{tj}=N\mu_ty_{tj}\) into the initial program. Its row and inventory constraints become precisely those defining \(\mathrm{OPT}\), and its objective becomes \(N\) times that objective. Hence

\(\displaystyle W(N\mu,Nq)=N\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q,v).\)

The mechanism’s welfare is exactly this quantity.

5. Running time

The construction operates on types and classes, without expanding the market into individual bidders or copies.

Let \(K=\tau m+\tau+m\). A remaining-market program has \(K\) variables and \(\tau+m\) equality constraints, besides nonnegativity.

To construct one menu:

  • Solve one LP for \(W(r,s)\).
  • For each of the \(\tau m+\tau\) allocation or unmatched variables, maximize that variable over the optimal face. Its optimum is positive exactly when it is allowed.
  • Solve the perturbed dual.

This requires at most \(\tau m+\tau+2\) rational LP solves, each with at most \(K\) variables and \(O(K)\) constraints.

The bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Counts require \(O(\log(N+1))\) bits each; \(W(r,s)\), \(\varepsilon\), and the constructed LPs have encoding lengths polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(N+1)\). Standard polynomial-time rational LP algorithms therefore give:

  • Preprocessing, including the first menu: \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(N+1))\).
  • Work per truthful arrival, including recomputation: \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(N+1))\).
  • Total work: \(N\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(N+1))\).

For arbitrary reports of bit length \(B\), processing takes \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(N+1),B)\). Dependence on \(B\) is unavoidable because reports have unrestricted rational encoding lengths.

6. The supremal ratio

Every realized feasible allocation has welfare at most \(N\mathrm{OPT}\), so every mechanism’s expected welfare ratio is at most \(1\).

The mechanism above achieves ratio \(1\) for every instance and every admissible scale. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle R_{\tau,m}=1.\)

The conclusion uses exact aggregate counts, rather than merely knowledge of a distribution from which private types are independently drawn.
