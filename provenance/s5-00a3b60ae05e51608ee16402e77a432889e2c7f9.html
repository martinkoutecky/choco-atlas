The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite course catalog \(G\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita course capacities \(q_g\), and \(\{1,c\}\)-valued submodular valuations \(v_t\) for integer \(c \ge 2\), choose masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) assigned to bundles \(S\subseteq G\), subject to \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) for every type \(t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:g\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_g\) for every course \(g\). Lexicographically maximize \(\left(\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S},\ \sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S)\right)\), where each individual receives an integral bundle and \(x\) records the mass assigned to each bundle.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a finite course catalog with \(m\ge 1\) courses and let \(c\ge 2\) be a common integer, encoded in binary. Each type has a normalized submodular valuation \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\) and \(v_t(S\cup\{g\})-v_t(S)\in\{1,c\}\) whenever \(g\notin S\); valuations are accessed through exact value oracles.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass \(\mu_t\) as a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible valuations. Course capacities \(q_g\ge0\) are binary rational per-capita quantities; let \(L\) be the total bit length of the numerical input.
An allocation assigns mass \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) of type \(t\) to each integral bundle \(S\subseteq G\), including the empty bundle, subject to \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni g}x_{t,S}\le q_g\). Unused capacity is allowed, and each individual receives at most one copy of each course.
Lexicographically maximize \(\bigl(\sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S},\ \sum_{t,S:v_t(S)>0}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S)\bigr)\), using the natural logarithm. Allocations are represented by their nonzero bundle masses; any optimizer is acceptable.

Is computing an exact optimizer for this mass-allocation problem polynomial-time solvable, or is it NP-hard? State running times and value-oracle query bounds in \(m\), \(\tau\), \(L\), and \(\log c\), accounting separately for oracle evaluation cost.

For discrete agents and indivisible goods with these valuations, maximizing the number of positive-utility agents and then their utility product is polynomial-time solvable ([Cousins, Viswanathan and Zick](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.03087v3)). No complexity classification of this exact mass-allocation problem was located in the literature search.

1. Effective capacities and the first objective

Every course is assigned to at most the entire population. Consequently, replacing $q_g$ by
$$ \widehat q_g=\min\{q_g,1\} $$
preserves the feasible allocations. Put
$$ Q=\sum_{g\in G}\widehat q_g. $$

Telescoping the stipulated valuation marginals along an ordering of a bundle gives
$$ |S|\le v_t(S)\le c|S|. $$
Thus positive utility is equivalent to a nonempty bundle.

For an allocation $x$, let
$$ P(x)=\sum_t\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}x_{t,S}. $$
The type-mass equations and capacity inequalities imply
$$ P(x)\le 1 $$
and
$$ P(x)\le \sum_t\sum_S |S|x_{t,S} \le \sum_g\widehat q_g=Q. $$

These bounds are attainable. Choose rational numbers $a_g$ with
$$ 0\le a_g\le\widehat q_g, \qquad \sum_g a_g=\min\{1,Q\}. $$
Such numbers can be chosen greedily. Assign
$$ x_{t,\{g\}}=\mu_ta_g, \qquad x_{t,\varnothing}=\mu_t\bigl(1-\min\{1,Q\}\bigr). $$
For each type, the assigned masses sum to $\mu_t$. Course $g$ is assigned mass $a_g$, so its capacity constraint holds. Therefore
$$ P^*=\min\{1,Q\} =\min\left\{1,\sum_gq_g\right\}. $$
The last equality holds because either no capacity exceeds one, or both minima equal one.

This construction uses polynomial rational arithmetic and no valuation queries.

2. Exact polynomial optimization when $Q\le 1$

Suppose $Q\le 1$ and an allocation attains $P^*=Q$. Then
$$ Q=P(x)\le \sum_{t,S}|S|x_{t,S}\le Q. $$
Hence
$$ \sum_{t,S} \bigl(|S|-1_{\{S\ne\varnothing\}}\bigr)x_{t,S}=0. $$
Every summand is nonnegative. Thus every positive-mass nonempty bundle is a singleton. Moreover, all effective capacities are exhausted.

Query every singleton value. Since
$$ v_t(\{g\})\in\{1,c\}, $$
the second objective becomes
$$ (\log c)\sum_{t,g:\,v_t(\{g\})=c}x_{t,\{g\}}. $$
It therefore suffices to maximize the mass assigned on pairs with singleton value $c$.

Construct a flow network with:

  • source-to-type capacity $\mu_t$;
  • course-to-sink capacity $\widehat q_g$;
  • a type-to-course arc of capacity one exactly when $v_t(\{g\})=c$.

Let $F$ be its maximum-flow value. Complete this flow to a singleton allocation as follows. Write $\beta_t$ for the remaining mass of type $t$ and $d_g$ for the remaining demand of course $g$. Then
$$ \sum_t\beta_t=1-F, \qquad \sum_gd_g=Q-F\le 1-F. $$
If $1-F>0$, assign additional singleton masses
$$ \frac{\beta_td_g}{1-F}. $$
These exhaust every remaining course demand without exceeding any remaining type mass. Assign each leftover type mass the empty bundle. If $1-F=0$, there is nothing to complete.

The completed allocation’s high-valued assignments form a feasible flow. Their total is at least $F$ because the original flow is retained, and at most $F$ by maximality. Thus the allocation is exactly optimal.

The bounds are
$$ A=\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log c), \qquad Q_{\mathrm{val}}\le\tau m. $$
The rational capacities are processed directly; no population expansion is involved.

3. An exact-optimization lemma for logarithmic objectives

The following lemma provides the new precision argument.

Lemma

Let
$$ \mathcal P=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{n}:Ax=\beta\} $$
be a nonempty bounded polytope, where $A$ is an integer matrix with $r$ rows and $\beta$ is rational. Suppose $\Delta\ge1$ is a known integer upper bound on the absolute values of all square subdeterminants of $A$.

Consider the objective
$$ \ell\cdot x, \qquad \ell_j=\log\left(\frac{p_j}{q_j}\right), $$
where $p_j,q_j$ are positive integers at most $B$, and $B\ge2$.

Put
$$ M=(r+1)\Delta, \qquad b=\lceil\log_2 B\rceil, \qquad J=Mb+\lceil\log_2(4M)\rceil+1. $$
Choose rational coefficients $\widetilde\ell_j$ satisfying
$$ |\widetilde\ell_j-\ell_j|\le 2^{-J}. $$

Every maximizer of $\widetilde\ell\cdot x$ over $\mathcal P$ is a maximizer of $\ell\cdot x$ over $\mathcal P$.

Consequently, exact optimization is possible in time polynomial in the rational input encoding length, $n$, $r$, $\log B$, and $J$.

Proof: bounded integer directions

Suppose $x,y\in\mathcal P$ and
$$ \ell\cdot(y-x)>0. $$
Set $w=y-x$. Then $Aw=0$.

For every coordinate with $w_j\ne0$, let $\sigma_j$ be its sign. Consider the compact polytope
$$ \mathcal C= \left\{ z\ge0: A\operatorname{diag}(\sigma)z=0,\ \sum_jz_j=1 \right\}, $$
restricted to those coordinates. It is nonempty because it contains
$$ \frac{|w|}{\sum_j|w_j|}. $$

At a vertex of $\mathcal C$ with support $T$, the nullspace of
$$ A_T\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_T) $$
has dimension one. Otherwise, there would be a nonzero perturbation in that nullspace whose coordinate sum is zero. A sufficiently small perturbation in either direction would preserve nonnegativity and contradict extremality.

It follows that
$$ |T|\le r+1. $$
Choose $|T|-1$ independent rows of this restricted matrix. The cofactor formula for its one-dimensional nullspace gives an integer generator. Every coordinate is a square subdeterminant of $A$, up to sign, and therefore has absolute value at most $\Delta$. Dividing by the common greatest divisor gives a primitive integer generator with the same bound.

Because a compact polytope is the convex hull of its vertices, $w$ is a nonnegative linear combination of integer vectors $h$ satisfying
$$ Ah=0, \qquad |\operatorname{supp}(h)|\le r+1, \qquad |h_j|\le\Delta, $$
whose signs agree coordinatewise with $w$.

Since $\ell\cdot w>0$, at least one such vector satisfies
$$ \ell\cdot h>0. $$
Furthermore, $x+\varepsilon h$ is feasible for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. Indeed, if $h_j<0$, sign agreement gives $w_j<0$, and hence $x_j>y_j\ge0$. All other coordinates cause no nonnegativity obstruction.

Proof: a gap bound for these directions

For any such integer vector $h$,
$$ \ell\cdot h = \log\left( \prod_j\left(\frac{p_j}{q_j}\right)^{h_j} \right) = \log\left(\frac{U}{V}\right), $$
where $U,V$ are positive integers satisfying
$$ U,V\le B^{\sum_j|h_j|}\le B^M. $$

If $U>V$, then
$$ \log(U/V)\ge\frac{U-V}{U}\ge B^{-M}. $$
The first inequality follows from $\log z\ge1-1/z$ for $z\ge1$. Interchanging $U,V$ handles the negative case. Therefore
$$ \ell\cdot h\ne0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |\ell\cdot h|\ge B^{-M}. $$

The coefficient approximation changes this direction’s objective by at most
$$ |(\widetilde\ell-\ell)\cdot h| \le M2^{-J} \le \frac18 B^{-M}. $$
Thus every strictly improving direction remains strictly improving.

If a maximizer $x$ of the rational surrogate were suboptimal for the true objective, the preceding argument would produce a feasible direction $h$ with
$$ \widetilde\ell\cdot h>0. $$
Moving a sufficiently small positive distance along $h$ would contradict surrogate optimality. This proves the lemma. Zero-cost directions may acquire either sign under rounding; the proof requires only preservation of strictly improving directions.

Algorithmic details

The rational coefficients can be computed in polynomial bit time in $J+\log B$. One explicit method uses range reduction and
$$ \log z = 2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2i+1} \left(\frac{z-1}{z+1}\right)^{2i+1}. $$
After writing an integer as $2^ez$ with $1\le z<2$, the series parameter has absolute value at most $1/3$. Truncating after $O(J+\log(b+1))$ terms gives the required error, including the factor $e$ multiplying $\log2$. Exact rational evaluation and final binary rounding have polynomial bit complexity.

For the remaining optimization, use the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly described rational LP can be optimized, and an optimal vertex returned when the feasible set is nonempty and bounded, in time polynomial in its encoding length. Here all constraints are rational and the surrogate coefficients have $O(J+\log B)$ bits. This is an application of the rational optimization framework of [Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf).

4. An elementary exact algorithm for arbitrary valuations

Enumerate all bundles and query each nonempty bundle for each type. This uses
$$ Q_{\mathrm{val}}=\tau(2^m-1) $$
queries. Every returned value has $O(\log m+\log c)$ bits because
$$ 1\le v_t(S)\le mc \qquad(S\ne\varnothing). $$

Introduce the configuration variables $x_{t,S}$ and one nonnegative slack variable $s_g$ per effective capacity. Impose
$$ \sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t \qquad(t=1,\ldots,\tau), $$
$$ \sum_t\sum_{S\ni g}x_{t,S}+s_g=\widehat q_g \qquad(g\in G), $$
and the first-objective equality
$$ \sum_t\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}x_{t,S}=P^*. $$

This is a nonempty bounded rational polytope. Nonemptiness was proved in Section 1. Every configuration variable is at most its type mass, and every slack is at most one.

There are
$$ n=\tau2^m+m $$
variables and
$$ d=\tau+m+1 $$
rows. The equality matrix has entries in $\{0,1\}$. For a square minor of size $k\le d$, Hadamard’s inequality gives
$$ |\det C|\le k^{k/2}\le d^d. $$
Thus $\Delta=d^d$ is a valid bound for the lemma.

Assign coefficient $\log v_t(S)$ to every nonempty bundle variable, and coefficient zero to empty-bundle variables and slacks. These have the lemma’s form with $B=mc$: coefficient zero is represented by $\log1$.

Put
$$ b=1+\lceil\log_2(mc)\rceil. $$
The lemma requires precision
$$ J=d^{O(d)}b. $$
Computing those rational coefficients and optimizing the explicit rational LP therefore takes
$$ A= 2^{O(m)}d^{O(d)}\operatorname{poly}(L,b) = 2^{O(m)}(\tau+m+1)^{O(\tau+m+1)} \operatorname{poly}(L,\log c) $$
bit operations.

The returned optimizer is exact for both original objectives. This argument uses only integer-product gap bounds on bounded circuit directions, rather than objective gaps between vertices.

Short rational output

An exact optimizer can be chosen with at most
$$ \tau+m $$
nonzero bundle masses.

To prove this, omit the first-objective equality temporarily. The configuration polytope with capacity slacks has $\tau+m$ equality rows. Maximizing the first objective selects a face; maximizing the second objective selects a face of that face. This final face contains a vertex of the original polytope.

At a vertex, the columns corresponding to positive variables must be linearly independent: a dependence would permit a sufficiently small feasible perturbation in both directions. Hence there are at most $\tau+m$ positive variables, counting slacks as well.

Let $D_{\mathrm{in}}$ be the product of the denominators in the numerical input. Its bit length is at most $L$. Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s inequality show that every bundle mass at such a vertex has bit length
$$ O\bigl(L+(\tau+m)\log(\tau+m+1)\bigr). $$
Thus exactness does not require irrational masses or an exponentially long output.

The algorithm may request an optimal vertex of the surrogate LP. Because its feasible set is the first-objective face, such a vertex is also a vertex of the original configuration polytope and satisfies these output bounds.

5. The size-dependent subclass

Assume now the additional promise that, for every type $t$, the valuation depends only on bundle size.

Write its value on a bundle of size $k$ as $u_t(k)$. Submodularity implies that
$$ u_t(k+1)-u_t(k) $$
is nonincreasing in $k$. These differences belong to $\{1,c\}$. Consequently, there is an integer $R_t\in\{0,\ldots,m\}$ such that
$$ u_t(k)=k+(c-1)\min\{k,R_t\}. $$
Equivalently, the associated rank function is that of a uniform matroid.

A single query to $G$ determines the entire valuation:
$$ R_t=\frac{v_t(G)-m}{c-1}. $$
Thus at most $\tau$ valuation queries suffice for this subclass.

If $Q\le1$, Section 2 applies, with all singleton values obtained from these queries. Hence it remains to handle $Q\ge1$.

Concavity of the interpolated logarithmic values

For $k=1,\ldots,m-1$, define
$$ d_{t,k}=\log\left(\frac{u_t(k+1)}{u_t(k)}\right). $$
These slopes are positive and nonincreasing. Indeed, writing
$$ a_{t,k+1}=u_t(k+1)-u_t(k), $$
the sequence $a_{t,k+1}$ is nonincreasing, whereas $u_t(k)$ is increasing. Therefore
$$ d_{t,k} = \log\left(1+\frac{a_{t,k+1}}{u_t(k)}\right) $$
is nonincreasing.

Let $F_t$ be the piecewise-linear interpolation of
$$ F_t(k)=\log u_t(k), \qquad k=1,\ldots,m. $$
It is concave on $[1,m]$.

Since $P^*=1$, all individuals receive nonempty bundles. We may restrict attention to allocations exhausting all effective capacities. To justify this, if course $g$ is used by mass $a_g<\widehat q_g$, the mass missing that course is $1-a_g$. Since $\widehat q_g\le1$,
$$ \widehat q_g-a_g\le1-a_g. $$
We can split off this much missing-course mass and add $g$ to its bundles. This preserves all other capacity usages and strictly increases its logarithmic utility. Repeating for each course proves the assertion.

For a saturated allocation, define
$$ y_{t,g}=\sum_{S\ni g}x_{t,S}, \qquad s_t=\sum_gy_{t,g}. $$
Its marginals satisfy
$$ 0\le y_{t,g}\le\mu_t, \qquad \sum_ty_{t,g}=\widehat q_g, \qquad \mu_t\le s_t\le m\mu_t. $$

For type $t$, the mean bundle size is $s_t/\mu_t$. Jensen’s inequality applied to $F_t$ gives
$$ \sum_Sx_{t,S}\log u_t(|S|) \le \mu_tF_t(s_t/\mu_t). $$

The next construction shows that this bound is attainable for every feasible marginal vector.

6. Balanced bundle distributions attain the concavity bound

Fix a type and feasible marginals $y_g$. Put
$$ p_g=\frac{y_g}{\mu}, \qquad a=\sum_gp_g\in[1,m]. $$
Choose an ordering $g_1,\ldots,g_m$, and let
$$ H_0=0, \qquad H_i=\sum_{j=1}^{i}p_{g_j}. $$

For $U$ uniformly distributed on $[0,1)$, include $g_i$ exactly when the half-open interval
$$ [H_{i-1},H_i) $$
contains a point of $\mathbb Z+U$.

Because its length is $p_{g_i}\le1$, each interval contains at most one such point. The resulting object is therefore an integral bundle with no repeated course.

Integration over $U$ shows that the probability of including $g_i$ equals the interval length $p_{g_i}$. Its course marginals are thus the prescribed ones.

The number of included courses equals the number of points of $\mathbb Z+U$ in $[0,a)$. It is always either $\lfloor a\rfloor$ or $\lceil a\rceil$. If $a=k+\alpha$ with $0\le\alpha<1$, their probabilities are respectively $1-\alpha$ and $\alpha$. In particular, every bundle is nonempty, and
$$ \mathbb E[\log u(|S|)]=F(a). $$

This distribution has a finite exact representation. The bundle changes only at fractional parts of the prefix sums $H_i$. Including the endpoints zero and one gives at most $m+1$ intervals on which it is constant. Assign each corresponding bundle mass $\mu$ times the interval length.

All endpoints and masses are rational. Normalization by positive rational $\mu$, prefix summation, and multiplication by $\mu$ preserve polynomial encoding length. The construction uses polynomial rational arithmetic and outputs at most $m+1$ bundles per type.

It follows that the optimal second objective in the size-dependent subclass is exactly
$$ \max_y\sum_t\mu_tF_t(s_t/\mu_t) $$
subject to
$$ 0\le y_{t,g}\le\mu_t, \qquad \sum_ty_{t,g}=\widehat q_g, \qquad s_t=\sum_gy_{t,g}\ge\mu_t. $$

7. Exact polynomial flow optimization for size-dependent types

Construct a directed network with source $a$, one node for each type, one node for each course, and sink $b$.

Its arcs are:

  • For each type $t$, a source-to-type arc fixed at flow $\mu_t$, with gain zero.
  • For each $t$ and $k=1,\ldots,m-1$, another source-to-type arc of capacity $\mu_t$, with gain $d_{t,k}$.
  • For each pair $(t,g)$, a type-to-course arc of capacity $\mu_t$, with gain zero.
  • For each course $g$, a course-to-sink arc fixed at flow $\widehat q_g$, with gain zero.

Require source supply and sink demand $Q$.

The network is feasible. For example,
$$ y_{t,g}=\mu_t\widehat q_g $$
satisfies all course totals and type-to-course bounds, and gives $s_t=\mu_tQ\in[\mu_t,m\mu_t]$. The optional source arcs can carry the remaining $s_t-\mu_t$.

Equality of the flow and allocation objectives

For fixed type total $s_t$, the optional source-arc flows $h_{t,k}$ satisfy
$$ 0\le h_{t,k}\le\mu_t, \qquad \sum_kh_{t,k}=s_t-\mu_t. $$
Since $d_{t,k}$ is nonincreasing, their maximum gain is obtained by filling earlier arcs before later arcs. Explicitly, if an earlier arc has room and a later arc has positive flow, transferring the smaller of those two amounts to the earlier arc cannot decrease gain.

The resulting maximum is
$$ \mu_tF_t(s_t/\mu_t)-\mu_t\log u_t(1). $$
Thus maximum network gain, plus the constant
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\log u_t(1), $$
equals the optimal allocation objective characterized in Section 6.

An exact maximum-gain flow therefore gives optimal marginals. Balanced bundle decomposition then gives an exact allocation optimizer.

Why the flow’s logarithmic costs permit polynomial exactness

There are
$$ V=\tau+m+2 $$
nodes and
$$ E=2\tau m+m $$
arcs before deleting fixed arcs.

Shift away arc lower bounds. Write flow conservation using the node–arc incidence matrix $H$, and introduce one nonnegative capacity slack per remaining arc. The resulting equality matrix is
$$ A= \begin{pmatrix} H&0\\ I&I \end{pmatrix}. $$

This matrix is totally unimodular. For completeness, every square minor of an incidence matrix is zero or has absolute value one: expand along a column with at most one nonzero entry; if every column has both its incidence entries, the column sums are zero and the minor is singular. For the displayed augmented matrix, expanding along slack columns and then the unit rows reduces every minor to an incidence-matrix minor.

Consequently, Section 3 applies with
$$ \Delta=1. $$
The nonzero arc gains are logarithms of rational numbers whose numerator and denominator are positive integers at most $mc$:
$$ d_{t,k}=\log\left(\frac{u_t(k+1)}{u_t(k)}\right). $$
There are $O(V+E)$ equality rows, so the required precision is only
$$ J=O\bigl((V+E)(\log m+\log c)+\log(V+E)\bigr). $$

All hypotheses of the lemma hold:

  • the shifted flow variables and capacity slacks are nonnegative;
  • the equality matrix is integer with subdeterminant bound one;
  • all right-hand sides are rational with polynomial encoding length;
  • feasibility was exhibited above;
  • arc capacities make the feasible set bounded.

Rational surrogate optimization therefore returns a flow that is exactly optimal for the original logarithmic gains.

Recovering its marginals and applying Section 6 gives an exact optimizer with at most
$$ \tau(m+1) $$
listed bundle masses.

The complete bounds for this subclass are
$$ A=\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log c), \qquad Q_{\mathrm{val}}\le\tau, $$
and total time is $A+\tau T_{\mathrm{val}}$.

8. What remains open

The unrestricted problem is still unclassified.

The general configuration formulation has an integer constraint matrix, but the determinant bound used here grows as
$$ (\tau+m+1)^{O(\tau+m+1)}. $$
The resulting exact algorithm is therefore not polynomial-time for unrestricted $m$ and $\tau$.

For size-dependent valuations, the flow reduction removes that growth because its matrix is totally unimodular. I have not proved a corresponding reduction, a polynomial bound on sufficient improving directions, or a hardness reduction for arbitrary allowed valuations.

The results proved here are:
