The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete ranking types on \(C\), a preferred candidate \(w\in C\), and \(k\), decide whether there exists \(D\subseteq C\setminus\{w\}\) with \(|D|\le k\) such that, after restricting every type to \(C\setminus D\), \(w\) is the unique Bucklin winner under \(\mu\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a nonempty candidate set with \(m=|C|\), let \(w\in C\), and let \(k\ge 0\) be a binary-encoded integer.
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete ranking types on \(C\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu(t)\), with \(\sum_t\mu(t)=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For a surviving set \(S\subseteq C\) containing \(w\), restrict every ranking to \(S\), preserving relative order and mass. Define \(s_i(c)=\sum_{t:\,c\text{ is among the first }i\text{ positions of }t|_S}\mu(t)\).
Use Bucklin voting with strict majority \(s_i(c)>1/2\): at the earliest level where any candidate has a strict majority, all candidates with maximum score at that level win. Retain all ties; no external tie-breaking applies.
The decision problem asks whether some \(D\subseteq C\setminus\{w\}\) with \(|D|\le k\) makes \(w\) the unique winner on \(C\setminus D\).

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite lists of unweighted voters, constructive control by deleting candidates under the unique-winner Bucklin convention is NP-hard and is \(\mathrm{W}[2]\)-hard parameterized by the deletion budget. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.2230v2).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the distribution normalization explicit: positive support masses sum to one.
  • Interpreted complete rankings as strict linear orders, matching the discrete definition.
  • Replaced the integer majority threshold by mass strictly greater than one half; equal mass at one half is not a majority.
  • Preserved the discrete Bucklin winner convention: maximize the level score at the earliest majority level and retain all tied winners.
  • Specified a nonempty candidate set, a nonnegative binary-encoded integer deletion budget, and total input bit length L.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) exists only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Here, binary-rational masses are understood as exact rationals with numerators and denominators encoded in binary.

The case \(k\ge m-1\) is trivially affirmative: delete every candidate except \(w\). The hardness concerns unrestricted instances with smaller budgets.

Polynomial-time reduction from finite electorates

We use the given hardness result: constructive control by deleting candidates for unweighted Bucklin voting, under the unique-winner convention, is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The source’s definitions use strict complete rankings, strict majority, and retention of all candidates tied for the maximum score at the earliest majority level, matching the present task. See Section 2 and Theorem 3.5 of [Control Complexity in Bucklin and Fallback Voting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.2230).

Take such a source instance with candidate set \(C\), distinguished candidate \(w\), deletion budget \(k\), and an explicit list of \(n>0\) voters.

Group identical rankings. For each distinct ranking \(t\), let \(a_t\) be its multiplicity, and assign

\(\displaystyle \mu(t)=\frac{a_t}{n}.\)

Keep \(C,w,k\) unchanged. Every mass is positive, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu(t)=\frac{\sum_t a_t}{n}=1.\)

The support contains at most \(n\) types. Grouping the explicitly listed rankings takes polynomial time. Each mass requires \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits for its numerator and denominator, so the resulting support representation has polynomial length in the source input.

Preservation after every deletion

Fix any permitted deletion set \(D\subseteq C\setminus\{w\}\), and write \(S=C\setminus D\). For \(c\in S\) and \(1\le i\le |S|\), let \(N_i^S(c)\) denote the number of original voters placing \(c\) among the first \(i\) positions after restriction to \(S\).

All voters of a given original type have the same restricted ranking. Therefore,

\(\displaystyle s_i^S(c)=\sum_{t:\,c\text{ is among the first }i\text{ positions of }t|_S}\frac{a_t}{n}=\frac{N_i^S(c)}{n}.\)

This equality remains valid when different original types become identical after restriction: their contributions simply add.

The majority conditions agree exactly:

\(\displaystyle s_i^S(c)>\frac12\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N_i^S(c)>\frac n2\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N_i^S(c)\ge\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor+1.\)

Moreover, division by the positive number \(n\) preserves every strict comparison and equality between candidates’ scores. Thus both elections have the same earliest majority level and precisely the same winners at that level, including all ties.

In particular, \(w\) is the unique winner after deleting \(D\) in the source instance if and only if it is the unique winner after deleting \(D\) in the constructed instance. Since the candidate set and budget are unchanged, the instances have the same answer.

This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. Crucially, the reduction lists only the observed ranking types; it never enumerates the \(m!\) possible rankings.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate is a deletion set \(D\), encoded using at most \(m-1\) candidate indicators. Its validity and the condition \(|D|\le k\) can be checked in polynomial time, even though \(k\) is binary encoded.

It remains to verify the winner using exact arithmetic. Write the input masses as \(\mu(t)=p_t/q_t\), with positive integer denominators. Set

\(\displaystyle Q=\prod_t q_t,\qquad A_t=p_t(Q/q_t).\)

The bit length of \(Q\) is at most \(1+\sum_t\log_2 q_t\), hence \(O(L)\). Because the masses sum to one, \(\sum_t A_t=Q\).

After restricting each ranking to \(S=C\setminus D\), compute

\(\displaystyle B_i(c)=\sum_{t:\,c\text{ is among the first }i\text{ positions of }t|_S}A_t.\)

Then \(s_i(c)=B_i(c)/Q\). Strict majority is tested exactly by \(2B_i(c)>Q\), and candidate scores are compared by comparing their integer numerators.

There are at most \(m\) levels and \(m\) surviving candidates. Direct computation uses \(O(\tau m^2)\) additions of integers having \(O(L)\) bits. Integer multiplication, exact division, addition, and comparison all take polynomial time in their bit lengths.

At level \(|S|\), every candidate has score one, so a majority level always exists. Find the earliest such level \(\ell\), and accept precisely when

\(\displaystyle 2B_\ell(w)>Q\quad\text{and}\quad B_\ell(w)>B_\ell(c)\ \text{for every }c\in S\setminus\{w\}.\)

This exactly checks that \(w\) is the unique Bucklin winner. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness proof.

Parameterized consequence

The same reduction preserves \(k\) exactly. Consequently, the given finite-electorate \(\mathrm{W}[2]\)-hardness also transfers to this support representation when parameterized by the deletion budget \(k\).
