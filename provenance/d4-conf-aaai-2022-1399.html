The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) of complete user types \(t=(q_t,\nu_t,\pi_t)\), with rational masses (\mu_t)_{t\in T} summing to one, define (U_t(s)) as type \(t\)’s expected reward when shown type \(s\)’s policy. Can an adaptive audit, using reward samples from target types and source policies drawn according to \mu, with confidence \(1-\delta\), certify that the mass of types \(t\) for which \sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\mathbf{1}[U_t(s)>U_t(t)+\epsilon]>\gamma is at most \lambda, or report evidence to the contrary, while ensuring that each audited type’s average expected reward at every horizon is at least (1-\alpha)U_t(t)\)? Characterize its duration and utility-loss guarantees.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the bit length of the support identifiers, masses, and rational audit parameters.
Each stationary type \(t=(q_t,\nu_t,\pi_t)\) has a context distribution, conditional reward distributions supported on \([0,1]\), and a recommendation policy on common nonempty finite context and item sets; write \(m\) for their combined cardinality.
Types identify sampling interfaces, not numerical descriptions of unknown rewards. Showing source policy \(s\) to target \(t\) produces an independent sample via \(x\sim q_t\), \(a\sim\pi_s(\cdot\mid x)\), \(r\sim\nu_t(\cdot\mid x,a)\), with mean \(U_t(s)=\mathbb E[r]\); each interface call has unit cost.
Targets and source policies can be drawn independently with replacement according to \(\mu\). The auditor may adaptively reuse sampled source policies and may always show each target its own baseline policy.
Take \(\epsilon,\alpha\in(0,1]\), \(\gamma,\lambda\in(0,1)\), \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\), and assume \(b=\min_{t\in T}U_t(t)>0\); baseline means are unknown.
Define \(E_t=\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\mathbf 1[U_t(s)>U_t(t)+\epsilon]\) and \(B=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\mathbf 1[E_t>\gamma]\).
With joint probability at least \(1-\delta\), the audit must terminate and either certify \(B\le\lambda\) or return a witnessed pair \((t,s)\) with \(U_t(s)>U_t(t)\). These alternatives may overlap.
For every audited target stream of type \(t\), with chosen sources \(s_{t,j}\), require \(\sum_{j=1}^{h}U_t(s_{t,j})\ge h(1-\alpha)U_t(t)\) at every horizon before stopping, on the same joint event.
If that stream stops at \(H_t\), its utility loss is \(C_t=H_tU_t(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{H_t}U_t(s_{t,j})\), which may be negative. Duration is the maximum stream length under parallel execution; total reward samples are counted separately.
Is this sample-access auditing problem solvable with polynomial computation and sample complexity in \(m,\tau,L,\epsilon^{-1},\gamma^{-1},\lambda^{-1},\alpha^{-1},b^{-1},\log(1/\delta)\), or is it NP-hard? What duration and utility-loss guarantees are achievable?
For a finite uniformly weighted user list, a conservative audit has population-size-independent target and source-policy counts, simultaneous duration and loss bounds, and a negative output witnessing strict pairwise envy. [Do et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14527v5)
No result specifically formulated for arbitrary rational type masses was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Complete types identify stationary sampling interfaces; unknown reward distributions and utilities are not supplied numerically, preserving the discrete audit's information model.
  • Specified common nonempty finite context and item domains, bounded rewards, independent sampling, and unit-cost interface access; defined m and L.
  • Specified independent mass-weighted sampling with replacement, permitted reuse of sampled policies, and retained the baseline-policy move.
  • Restricted audit parameters to nondegenerate domains compatible with the finite-sample guarantees.
  • Required positive baseline means and retained inverse dependence on their minimum b, which the discrete duration bound implicitly needs; b is not supplied as known baseline information.
  • Interpreted evidence to the contrary as strict pairwise envy, as explicitly clarified in the full version, rather than proof that the relaxed population condition fails.
  • Specified a joint confidence event, conservative constraints per target stream, parallel duration, separate total sample count, and signed cumulative utility loss.

Result

The auditing problem is solvable with polynomial computation and sample complexity in the stated sampling-interface model, for arbitrary rational type masses. The proof below gives simultaneous duration, utility-loss, and correctness guarantees.

The algorithm uses population-size-independent target and source-policy counts. Its bounds do not depend on \(b^{-1}\), and baseline means need not be known. The bounds are achievable bounds; no optimality claim is made.

For \(\epsilon=1\), every \(E_t=0\), so immediate certification is valid. The construction below also handles this case.

1. Parameters and guarantees

All logarithms in the construction have base \(2\). Define

\(K=\left\lceil\lambda^{-1}\left\lceil\log_2(8/\delta)\right\rceil\right\rceil,\qquad R=\left\lceil\gamma^{-1}\left\lceil\log_2(8K/\delta)\right\rceil\right\rceil.\)

Draw \(K\) targets independently from \(\mu\), and independently draw \(R\) source policies from \(\mu\). Reuse the same source-policy list for all targets. Repeated identifiers are allowed.

Set

\(J=\left\lceil\log_2(8/\epsilon)\right\rceil,\qquad \rho_\ell=2^{-\ell}\quad(0\le\ell\le J),\qquad \eta=\rho_J,\)

\(c=\left\lceil\log_2\!\left(\frac{8K[1+R(J+1)]}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil,\qquad n_\ell=\left\lceil\frac{c}{2\rho_\ell^2}\right\rceil,\qquad N=n_J.\)

In particular,

\(\epsilon/16<\eta\le\epsilon/8,\qquad N\le128c\epsilon^{-2}+1.\)

Run one target stream for each target draw. For every stream \(i\), write \(u_i=U_{t_i}(t_i)\), and let \(H_i\) and \(C_i\) denote its duration and utility loss.

The construction guarantees, simultaneously with probability at least \(1-\delta\):

  • The output is either a valid certificate \(B\le\lambda\), or a pair \((t,s)\) satisfying \(U_t(s)>U_t(t)\).
  • Every target stream satisfies the required utility constraint at every horizon.
  • Its utility loss satisfies \(C_i\le R(4c/\epsilon+1)\).

Moreover, termination and the following bounds hold deterministically in the supplied sampling-interface model:

\(D:=\max_i H_i\le RN+\max\!\left\{N,\left\lceil\frac{(1-\alpha)RN}{\alpha}\right\rceil\right\}\le N+\frac{RN}{\alpha}+1,\)

\(\text{total reward samples}\le KD.\)

Thus, with

\(c=O\!\left(\log\!\frac{KR[1+\log(1/\epsilon)]}{\delta}\right),\)

the achievable bounds are

\(D=O\!\left(\frac{Rc}{\alpha\epsilon^2}\right),\qquad \text{total reward samples}=O\!\left(\frac{KRc}{\alpha\epsilon^2}\right),\qquad C_i=O\!\left(\frac{Rc}{\epsilon}\right).\)

There are \(K\) target draws and \(R\) sampled source-policy draws, in addition to the targets’ own baseline policies.

2. A conservative schedule requiring no baseline estimate

Fix a target stream with baseline mean \(u\). Let \(p\) count baseline calls and \(e\) count calls using sampled source policies.

First make \(N\) baseline calls. Before each subsequent source-policy call, insert the minimum number of baseline calls needed to ensure

\(\alpha p\ge(1-\alpha)(e+1).\)

Then make that source-policy call and increment \(e\).

At every prefix, this schedule maintains

\(\alpha p\ge(1-\alpha)e.\)

All source means are nonnegative. Consequently, the cumulative mean utility at this prefix is at least \(pu\). The scheduling inequality gives

\(pu\ge(1-\alpha)(p+e)u.\)

This proves the required conservative constraint at every horizon, on every sample path. In particular, it does not depend on confidence intervals being correct.

After any completed source-policy call, the baseline count is exactly

\(p=\max\!\left\{N,\left\lceil\frac{(1-\alpha)e}{\alpha}\right\rceil\right\}.\)

Each source policy will receive at most \(N\) calls, so \(e\le RN\). This proves the stated duration and total-sample bounds.

3. Reward tests and deterministic termination

The tests can use bounded-precision observations. Put \(\kappa=\eta/2\), and replace each observed reward \(r\) by

\(r^{\mathrm q}=\kappa\lfloor r/\kappa\rfloor.\)

Then \(r^{\mathrm q}\in[0,1]\) and \(0\le r-r^{\mathrm q}\le\kappa\). Only \(O(\log(1/\epsilon))\) fractional bits are retained.

For target \(t_i\), let \(\widehat u_i\) be the average of its first \(N\) quantized baseline rewards.

Process its sampled source policies in order. For a source \(s_j\), progressively collect \(n_\ell\) cumulative source rewards at stages \(\ell=0,\ldots,J\), using the conservative schedule above. Let \(\widehat v_{ij,\ell}\) be their quantized average, and define

\(a_\ell=\rho_\ell+2\eta,\)

\(L_{ij,\ell}=\widehat v_{ij,\ell}-\widehat u_i-a_\ell,\qquad V_{ij,\ell}=\widehat v_{ij,\ell}-\widehat u_i+a_\ell.\)

At each stage:

  • If \(L_{ij,\ell}>0\), record \((t_i,s_j)\) as a witness and stop this stream.
  • Otherwise, if \(V_{ij,\ell}\le\epsilon\), discard this source and proceed to the next one.
  • Otherwise, advance to the next stage.

At stage \(J\),

\(V_{ij,J}-L_{ij,J}=6\eta\le3\epsilon/4.\)

Hence, if the witness condition fails, then

\(V_{ij,J}\le6\eta<\epsilon,\)

so the source is discarded. Thus every source test terminates, even if confidence intervals are incorrect.

Allow all streams to finish. If any stream recorded a witness, return one. Otherwise, certify \(B\le\lambda\).

4. Simultaneous confidence and witness validity

Condition on the target and source draws. For analysis, expose in advance independent reward sequences for every stream and source-list position, including sequences that the algorithm never queries. This realizes the stipulated sampling process and permits concentration bounds despite adaptive stopping.

For independent observations \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\in[0,1]\) with common mean \(w\), Hoeffding’s inequality states

\(\Pr\!\left(\left|n^{-1}\sum_{h=1}^nY_h-w\right|>r\right)\le2\exp(-2nr^2).\)

Apply this to the quantized baseline average with radius \(\eta\), and to each source-stage average with radius \(\rho_\ell\). By the definitions of the sample sizes, each failure probability is at most \(2e^{-c}\).

There are at most \(K[1+R(J+1)]\) such averages. Since \(c\ge\ln(8K[1+R(J+1)]/\delta)\), their union failure probability is at most \(\delta/4\).

Let \(\mathcal A\) denote the resulting simultaneous confidence event. Quantization changes each mean by at most \(\kappa\). On \(\mathcal A\),

\(\left|\widehat v_{ij,\ell}-\widehat u_i-\bigl(U_{t_i}(s_j)-U_{t_i}(t_i)\bigr)\right|\le\rho_\ell+\eta+2\kappa=a_\ell.\)

Therefore,

\(L_{ij,\ell}\le U_{t_i}(s_j)-U_{t_i}(t_i)\le V_{ij,\ell}.\)

Two conclusions follow:

  • Every recorded witness has strictly positive true improvement.
  • A source with true improvement greater than \(\epsilon\) can never be discarded.

These statements hold simultaneously for all streams and all stages.

5. Weighted sampling makes certification valid

Define the bad-target set

\(G=\{t:E_t>\gamma\}.\)

Its mass is \(\mu(G)=B\). Suppose \(B>\lambda\). The probability that all \(K\) target draws miss \(G\) is

\((1-B)^K\le e^{-\lambda K}\le\delta/8.\)

For any fixed target \(t\in G\), its set of sources satisfying

\(U_t(s)>U_t(t)+\epsilon\)

has mass \(E_t>\gamma\). Hence the probability that the shared list of \(R\) independently drawn sources misses this set is at most

\((1-E_t)^R\le e^{-\gamma R}\le\delta/(8K).\)

Conditioning on all target draws and taking a union bound, the probability that some sampled bad target lacks such a sampled source is at most \(\delta/8\). Independence between the different targets’ source-hit events is unnecessary.

It follows that, when \(B>\lambda\), with probability at least \(1-\delta/4\) the sampled lists contain an improvement greater than \(\epsilon\).

On this sampling event and \(\mathcal A\), its target stream either returns an earlier valid witness or reaches that source. The source cannot be discarded and its test must terminate, so the stream returns a witness.

Thus, on a joint event of probability at least \(1-\delta/2\), every witness is valid and every certificate is valid. This argument uses the masses directly; no replication into a uniformly weighted population is required.

6. Utility-loss bound

Fix a stream and work on \(\mathcal A\). Consider a sampled source with negative improvement. Write

\(\Delta=u-U_t(s)>0.\)

At stage \(\ell\), confidence validity gives

\(V_\ell\le-\Delta+2a_\ell=-\Delta+2\rho_\ell+4\eta.\)

Since \(4\eta\le\epsilon/2\), this source is discarded whenever

\(2\rho_\ell\le\Delta+\epsilon/2.\)

Let \(\ell_*\) be the first stage satisfying that inequality. It exists because \(\rho_J=\eta\le\epsilon/8\). Also, \(\ell_*\ge1\), since \(\Delta\le1\) and \(\epsilon\le1\). Minimality and the dyadic radii imply

\(\rho_{\ell_*}>(\Delta+\epsilon/2)/4.\)

Consequently, the number of calls to this source is at most

\(n_{\ell_*}\le\frac{8c}{(\Delta+\epsilon/2)^2}+1.\)

Its loss contribution is therefore at most

\(\Delta n_{\ell_*}\le\frac{8c\Delta}{(\Delta+\epsilon/2)^2}+\Delta\le\frac{4c}{\epsilon}+1.\)

The last inequality follows from

\((\Delta+\epsilon/2)^2\ge2\epsilon\Delta,\qquad \Delta\le1.\)

Baseline calls contribute zero loss. Sources with nonnegative improvement contribute nonpositive loss. Summing over at most \(R\) source-list positions proves

\(C_i\le R(4c/\epsilon+1).\)

This bound remains valid if the stream stops early upon finding another witness. Independently of confidence validity, the conservative constraint also gives

\(C_i\le\alpha H_i u_i.\)

Neither bound asserts that the loss is nonnegative.

7. Computation and rational masses

All test radii and quantized observations are dyadic rationals. Sample counts and thresholds can be computed with polynomial-bit arithmetic. Each observed reward is used only through comparisons with dyadic numbers having \(O(\log(1/\epsilon))\) bits.

Weighted draws are available by premise. They can also be implemented from the rational support without expanding the population. Take a common denominator \(Q\) equal to the product of the mass denominators; its bit length is at most \(O(L)\). Sampling an integer uniformly from \(\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}\), then locating its cumulative-mass interval, gives an exact \(\mu\)-draw.

For a bounded computation budget, cap rejection sampling for each draw at

\(M=\left\lceil\log_2(2(K+R)/\delta)\right\rceil\)

attempts. Each attempt accepts with probability at least \(1/2\), so the probability that any draw exhausts its cap is at most \(\delta/2\). Perform these draws before starting streams; on exhaustion, terminate with the certificate output and count this event as failure. Successful capped draws have exactly the required distribution.

Combining this additional failure probability with Sections 4–5 yields the promised joint success probability \(1-\delta\), with bounded polynomial computation.

The population-independent sampling principle matches the uniformly weighted audit in [Do et al., Section 4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.14527v5). The weighted argument and conservative bounds above are proved directly, rather than relying on an unverified arbitrary-mass extension.
