Theorem 2 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given goods \(j\in[L]\), buyer types \(t\in T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\in[0,1]\), and explicitly listed nonnegative monotone valuations \(v_t(B)\) for bundles \(B\subseteq[L]\), decide whether there are prices \(p\ge0\) and assignment masses \(z_{tB}\ge0\) such that \(\sum_B z_{tB}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B:j\in B}z_{tB}=s_j\), and every bundle assigned positive mass maximizes \(v_t(B)-\sum_{j\in B}p_j\) for its type.

Let there be \(m\ge1\) goods. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
For each type, all values \(v_t(B)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), for \(B\subseteq[m]\), are explicitly tabulated and monotone under inclusion. The empty bundle is available, and its value need not be zero. Supplies \(s_j\in[0,1]\) are binary rationals. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For \(q\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\), define \(W(q)=\max\{\sum_{t,B}y_{tB}v_t(B):y_{tB}\ge0,\ \sum_B y_{tB}=\mu_t,\ \sum_{t,B:j\in B}y_{tB}\le q_j\}\). Extra supply may be discarded in this auxiliary welfare definition. Promise \(W(2s)>W(s)\); coordinatewise weak monotonicity holds by definition.
An equilibrium consists of \(p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\setminus\{0\}\) and masses \(z_{tB}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_Bz_{tB}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:j\in B}z_{tB}=s_j\) for every good.
Whenever \(z_{tB}>0\), require \(v_t(B)-\sum_{j\in B}p_j=\max_{C\subseteq[m]}(v_t(C)-\sum_{j\in C}p_j)\). Mass may split among tied maximizers; there are no budget constraints.

For discrete bundle auctions whose aggregate welfare strictly increases when all unit supplies are doubled, nonzero-price equilibrium exists exactly when welfare at unit supply equals its upper concave envelope over \(\{0,1,2\}^m\). Conclusion

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. More strongly, every instance satisfying the promise admits an equilibrium. Thus the promised decision problem always answers YES. An equilibrium with rational prices and allocations can also be computed in polynomial time.

The essential feature is that type masses may split among bundles.

1. Welfare maximization and its dual

Write \(p(B)=\sum_{j\in B}p_j\). The linear program defining \(W(s)\) is feasible: assign every type entirely to the empty bundle. Its feasible region is compact, since \(0\le y_{tB}\le\mu_t\). Consequently, its optimum is finite and attained.

Its dual is

\(\displaystyle \min_{u,p}\left\{\sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_j s_jp_j:\ u_t+p(B)\ge v_t(B)\text{ for all }t,B,\quad p_j\ge0,\quad u_t\in\mathbb R\right\}.\)

Here the variables \(u_t\) are unrestricted because the type-mass constraints are equalities. This dual is feasible: take \(p=0\) and \(u_t=\max_Bv_t(B)\).

By finite-dimensional linear programming strong duality, a dual optimum \((u,p)\) exists and satisfies

\(\displaystyle W(s)=\sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_j s_jp_j.\)

Fix such an optimum.

2. An optimal allocation can clear every supply exactly

We first prove that the disposal allowed in the definition of \(W(s)\) does not prevent exact clearing.

Let \(y\) be a welfare-optimal allocation. Suppose good \(j\) currently has total usage

\(\displaystyle d_j=\sum_{t,B:j\in B}y_{tB}<s_j.\)

The mass assigned to bundles that do not contain \(j\) is exactly \(1-d_j\), because total society mass is \(1\). The additional usage required is

\(\displaystyle \delta=s_j-d_j\le1-d_j,\)

where the inequality follows from \(s_j\le1\).

Choose amounts \(a_{tB}\), for bundles \(B\) not containing \(j\), satisfying

\(\displaystyle 0\le a_{tB}\le y_{tB},\qquad \sum_{t,B:j\notin B}a_{tB}=\delta.\)

Such amounts exist because the available mass is \(1-d_j\ge\delta\); they can be selected greedily from the finitely many eligible entries. Move each amount \(a_{tB}\) from bundle \(B\) to bundle \(B\cup\{j\}\).

This operation preserves every type’s total mass. It increases good \(j\)’s usage to \(s_j\) and leaves every other good’s usage unchanged. Its welfare change is

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,B:j\notin B}a_{tB}\bigl(v_t(B\cup\{j\})-v_t(B)\bigr)\ge0,\)

by monotonicity.

The resulting allocation remains feasible for the program defining \(W(s)\). Since the original allocation was optimal, its welfare cannot increase strictly; hence it remains optimal.

Perform this operation successively for every good with slack. Previously cleared goods remain cleared. We obtain an allocation \(z\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \sum_Bz_{tB}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,B:j\in B}z_{tB}=s_j,\qquad \sum_{t,B}z_{tB}v_t(B)=W(s).\)

3. The dual prices support every allocated bundle

For every type and bundle, define the nonnegative dual slack

\(\displaystyle r_{tB}=u_t+p(B)-v_t(B)\ge0.\)

Using the type-mass equalities, exact market clearing, and equality of the primal and dual optimal values gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,B}z_{tB}r_{tB} =\sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_j s_jp_j-\sum_{t,B}z_{tB}v_t(B)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore, whenever \(z_{tB}>0\),

\(\displaystyle v_t(B)-p(B)=u_t.\)

Dual feasibility also gives, for every bundle \(C\),

\(\displaystyle v_t(C)-p(C)\le u_t.\)

Thus every positively allocated bundle maximizes the type’s utility over all bundles. This includes comparison with the empty bundle, without assuming its value is zero.

4. The promise forces nonzero prices

For any allocation \(y\) feasible at supply \(2s\), dual feasibility and nonnegativity of prices imply

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,B}y_{tB}v_t(B) \le\sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_jp_j\sum_{t,B:j\in B}y_{tB} \le\sum_t\mu_tu_t+2\sum_jp_js_j. \)

Maximizing over such allocations and using dual optimality at \(s\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle W(2s)\le W(s)+\sum_jp_js_j.\)

The promise \(W(2s)>W(s)\) therefore implies

\(\displaystyle \sum_jp_js_j>0.\)

In particular, \(p\ne0\). Together with Sections 2 and 3, this verifies every equilibrium requirement.

5. Polynomial-time construction

Let \(N=\tau 2^m\), the number of explicitly tabulated type-bundle values. Because the tables are part of the input, \(N\le L\), up to an encoding constant.

A constructive algorithm is:

  • Solve the dual program in Section 1 for an optimal rational pair \((u,p)\).
  • Solve the welfare program with every supply inequality replaced by an equality, obtaining an optimal rational allocation \(z\).

Section 2 proves that the second program is feasible and has optimum \(W(s)\). Sections 3 and 4 then prove that the computed pair \((p,z)\) is an equilibrium.

Both programs have polynomially many variables, constraints, and coefficient bits in \(m,\tau,L\). The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore supplies rational optimal solutions of polynomial encoding length in polynomial time.

The cited discrete auction framework concerns integer allocations. Here, splitting type masses permits the fractional welfare optimum itself to serve as the equilibrium allocation. [Referenced auction paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0603032).
