The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), ordered good categories \(g_1,\ldots,g_q\), binary utilities \(u_{tj}\), rational per-capita supplies \(\lambda_j\), and a queried type \(t^\star\), start all type mass at load \(0\). For each category \(j\), allocate supply \(\lambda_j\) by the Balanced Like least-load rule: at the least load \(r\) with eligible mass \(D=\sum_{t:u_{tj}=1}\nu_{t,r}>0\), transfer \(\delta=\min(s,D)\) mass, taking \(\delta\nu_{t,r}/D\) from each eligible type's load-\(r\) cohort to load \(r+1\), and continue with remaining supply \(s\). Compute exactly \(U_{t^\star}=\mu_{t^\star}^{-1}\sum_j u_{t^\star j}z_{t^\star j}\), where \(z_{tj}\) is the mass of type \(t\) served in category \(j\); establish that this equals the limit of finite-replica expected type-average utilities as population size grows through compatible multiples.

1. Exact update for one category

Consider an incoming state $x_{t,r}$ with finite load support and prescribed type masses
$$ \sum_r x_{t,r}=\mu_t. $$
Let $E$ be the category’s liking types and let $\lambda$ be its supply. If $E=\varnothing$ or $\lambda=0$, the state is unchanged.

Otherwise, put
$$ A=\sum_{t\in E}\mu_t>0, \qquad C_x(w)=\sum_{t\in E}\sum_r(w-r)_+x_{t,r}, $$
where $(a)_+=\max\{a,0\}$.

The function $C_x$ is continuous and piecewise affine. It is zero up to the smallest occupied eligible load, strictly increasing thereafter, and has eventual slope $A$. Thus there is a unique $w$ satisfying
$$ C_x(w)=\lambda. $$
Write
$$ h=\lfloor w\rfloor, \qquad p=w-h. $$
For each eligible type, define
$$ a_t(h)=\sum_{r\leq h}x_{t,r}, \qquad D(h)=\sum_{t\in E}a_t(h). $$
Because the incoming loads are integers,
$$ C_x(h+1)-C_x(h)=D(h). $$
Consequently,
$$ \lambda=C_x(h)+pD(h), \qquad 0\leq p<1, \qquad D(h)>0. $$

Lemma 1. The outgoing state $F(x)$ is unchanged for nonliking types. For $t\in E$, it is
$$ F(x)_{t,r}= \begin{cases} 0,&r<h,\\ (1-p)a_t(h),&r=h,\\ x_{t,h+1}+p\,a_t(h),&r=h+1,\\ x_{t,r},&r>h+1. \end{cases} $$
The type’s total transfer during the category is
$$ z_t=\sum_{r<h}(h-r)x_{t,r}+p\,a_t(h). $$

Proof. Raising every eligible cohort below $h$ to $h$ requires precisely $C_x(h)$ supply. Before this has happened, the rule always serves a cohort below $h$. Every such move is complete: the remaining cost of reaching $h$ is at most the remaining supply, so the process cannot stop with a partial move below $h$.

Complete moves relocate eligible mass without changing its type composition. Thus, after consuming $C_x(h)$ supply, the type-$t$ mass at load $h$ is $a_t(h)$. The remaining supply is $pD(h)$, so the proportional final move sends $p\,a_t(h)$ to load $h+1$. Since $p<1$, this ends the category. The displayed transfer formula counts all complete services and the final partial service. $\square$

Every transfer increases its recipient’s load by one. Since only liking types receive items, total utility equals final load. In particular, writing $\nu^{(j)}$ for the state after category $j$,
$$ U_{t^\star} =\frac{1}{\mu_{t^\star}} \sum_r r\,\nu^{(m)}_{t^\star,r}. $$

2. Each type occupies at most two consecutive loads

Lemma 2. After every category, each type’s positive-mass load support is contained in two consecutive integers.

Proof. Initially each type occupies only load zero. Suppose a type’s incoming support is contained in $\{k,k+1\}$.

A nonliking type is unchanged. For a liking type, use Lemma 1:

  • If $h<k$, the type is unchanged.
  • If $h=k$, its outgoing support remains contained in $\{k,k+1\}$.
  • If $h>k$, both incoming loads are at most $h$, so its outgoing support is contained in $\{h,h+1\}$.

This proves the induction. $\square$

Define the type’s total load mass and mean load by
$$ \ell_t=\sum_r r\,\nu_{t,r}, \qquad v_t=\frac{\ell_t}{\mu_t}. $$
Lemma 2 implies that its entire state is determined by $v_t$. Specifically, with
$$ k_t=\lfloor v_t\rfloor, \qquad f_t=v_t-k_t, $$
the only possibly nonzero cohorts are
$$ \nu_{t,k_t}=\mu_t(1-f_t), \qquad \nu_{t,k_t+1}=\mu_t f_t. $$

This gives a compact representation using at most $2\tau$ positive cohorts, independently of the supplies and number of categories.

Load-label bound

Suppose the incoming loads are at most an integer $R$. For a nonempty liking set,
$$ C_x\!\left(R+\frac{\lambda}{A}\right) = AR+\lambda-\sum_{t\in E}\sum_r r x_{t,r} \geq\lambda. $$
Hence
$$ w\leq R+\frac{\lambda}{A}. $$
Lemma 1 therefore bounds every outgoing load by
$$ R+\left\lceil\frac{\lambda}{A}\right\rceil. $$

For the complete instance, let
$$ A_j=\sum_{t:u_{tj}=1}\mu_t, \qquad R_0=0, $$
and define
$$ R_j= \begin{cases} R_{j-1},&A_j=0,\\ R_{j-1}+\left\lceil\lambda_j/A_j\right\rceil,&A_j>0. \end{cases} $$
All loads after category $j$ are at most $R_j$.

Every positive input mass is at least $2^{-L}$, and every supply is at most $2^L$. Thus
$$ R_m\leq m\,2^{2L}. $$
Load labels consequently have $O(L+\log(m+1))$ bits.

3. General exact simulation and its bit-complexity limit

Polynomially many arithmetic operations

For one category, list the occupied eligible loads as
$$ r_1<\cdots<r_k, \qquad k\leq 2\tau, $$
and define
$$ d_i=\sum_{t\in E}x_{t,r_i}, \qquad A_i=\sum_{\ell\leq i}d_\ell, \qquad B_i=\sum_{\ell\leq i}r_\ell d_\ell. $$
On the interval between consecutive occupied loads,
$$ C_x(w)=A_iw-B_i. $$
The same formula applies beyond the last occupied load. Scanning these intervals locates the water level, which is then computed exactly as
$$ w=\frac{\lambda+B_i}{A_i}. $$
Taking its floor and applying Lemma 1 completes the update.

Sorting at most $2\tau$ load labels and maintaining the two-cohort representation gives an exact simulation using
$$ O\!\left(m\tau\log(\tau+1)\right) $$
rational arithmetic operations, comparisons, and floor operations. This is an arithmetic-operation bound, not yet a polynomial binary running-time bound.

A denominator bound

Suppose all incoming cohort masses have a common denominator $Q$, and write the supply as $\lambda=a/b$.

Complete moves preserve denominator $Q$. At the final partial move, write
$$ D(h)=\frac dQ, \qquad a_t(h)=\frac{c_t}{Q}, \qquad \lambda-C_x(h)=\frac{v}{bQ}, $$
with integer numerators. The transferred mass is
$$ \frac{v c_t}{bQd}. $$
Thus $bQd$ is a valid outgoing common denominator. Since $0<D(h)\leq 1$,
$$ 1\leq d\leq Q. $$
Writing $q_j=\log_2 Q_j$, we may therefore choose denominators satisfying
$$ q_j\leq 2q_{j-1}+\log_2 b_j. $$
Initially, the product of the mass denominators is a valid common denominator. The total logarithm of that product and all supply denominators is at most $L$. It follows that
$$ q_j \leq 2^j q_0+\sum_{i=1}^j2^{j-i}\log_2 b_i \leq 2^jL. $$

Together with the load-label bound, this proves an exact binary algorithm whose running time is polynomial in
$$ m,\quad \tau,\quad 2^mL. $$
In particular, exact computation is polynomial-time for every fixed $m$.

This bound neither proves polynomial-time solvability for variable $m$ nor proves that exponential denominator growth is necessary.

4. Polynomial-time exact computation for two support types

Theorem 3. If $\tau\leq 2$, exact computation of $U_{t^\star}$ is polynomial-time in the binary input model, even when $m$ varies.

For one type, its mass is one, and
$$ U_{t^\star}=\sum_{j:u_{t^\star j}=1}\lambda_j. $$
It remains to prove the two-type case.

Let the types have masses
$$ a=\mu_1,\qquad b=\mu_2,\qquad a+b=1. $$
Write
$$ e=\ell_1, \qquad S=\ell_1+\ell_2. $$
Every category with a nonempty liking set spends its entire supply: repeated service imposes no capacity limit. Hence the aggregate load mass after category $j$ is the directly computable input quantity
$$ S_j=\sum_{\substack{i\leq j\\E_i\ne\varnothing}}\lambda_i. $$
Thus one scalar $e$ determines both type states, because
$$ \ell_2=S-e. $$

The key fact is that each category’s selected update has the form
$$ e'=\frac{\alpha e+\beta}{\gamma e+\delta}, $$
with coefficients of polynomial bit length.

Categories with at most one liking type

An unliked category leaves $e$ unchanged. If only type one likes it,
$$ e'=e+\lambda. $$
If only type two likes it,
$$ e'=e. $$
These are affine updates.

Categories liked by both types

Assume $\lambda>0$. The incoming base loads are
$$ k_1=\left\lfloor\frac ea\right\rfloor, \qquad k_2=\left\lfloor\frac{S-e}{b}\right\rfloor. $$
Compute the water level and its integer part $h$ using Section 3.

Because the supply is positive,
$$ h\geq\min\{k_1,k_2\}. $$
There are two cases.

Case A: $h<\max\{k_1,k_2\}$

Exactly one type has incoming base load at most $h$. The other type is unchanged by Lemma 1. Thus all supply goes to the lower type, and
$$ e'= \begin{cases} e+\lambda,&k_1\leq h,\\ e,&k_2\leq h. \end{cases} $$

Case B: $h\geq\max\{k_1,k_2\}$

Both outgoing type distributions are supported on $\{h,h+1\}$. Their combined mass at load $h$ is therefore
$$ \rho=h+1-(S+\lambda). $$
Indeed, total mass is one and outgoing aggregate load mass is $S+\lambda$.

The incoming cumulative masses through load $h$ are
$$ a_1(h)= \begin{cases} a,&k_1<h,\\ a(h+1)-e,&k_1=h, \end{cases} $$
and
$$ a_2(h)= \begin{cases} b,&k_2<h,\\ b(h+1)-(S-e),&k_2=h. \end{cases} $$
Each expression is affine in $e$. Put
$$ D=a_1(h)+a_2(h)>0. $$
Lemma 1 gives the outgoing type-one mass at load $h$ as
$$ (1-p)a_1(h)=\frac{\rho\,a_1(h)}{D}. $$
Consequently,
$$ e'=a(h+1)-\frac{\rho\,a_1(h)}{D}. $$

To verify the claimed fractional-linear form explicitly, write
$$ a_1(h)=c_1e+c_0, \qquad D=d_1e+d_0. $$
Then
$$ e' = \frac{ \bigl(a(h+1)d_1-\rho c_1\bigr)e +a(h+1)d_0-\rho c_0 }{ d_1e+d_0 }. $$
There is no product of two expressions involving $e$, because $\rho$ is determined by $h,S,\lambda$.

These cases exhaust every two-type update.

Polynomial bit growth

Let $B$ be the product of all input rational denominators. Its logarithm is at most $L$. Each aggregate quantity $S_j$ has denominator dividing $B$ and numerator of $O(L+\log(m+1))$ bits.

The masses, supplies, and load labels also have $O(L+\log(m+1))$ bits. The formulas above consequently give fractional-linear coefficients of that bit length. Clearing the four coefficient denominators produces integer coefficients with the same asymptotic bound.

Suppose the current scalar is represented in lowest terms as
$$ e=\frac PQ. $$
For an update with integer coefficients,
$$ e'=\frac{\alpha P+\beta Q}{\gamma P+\delta Q}. $$
If $P,Q$ have at most $H$ bits and the coefficients have at most $K$ bits, these new integers have at most $H+K+O(1)$ bits. Reducing the fraction cannot increase this bound. Since
$$ K=O(L+\log(m+1)), $$
after $m$ categories the scalar has
$$ O\!\left(m(L+\log(m+1))\right) $$
bits.

The branch selection depends on the current scalar, but every selected branch obeys this bound. The incoming cohorts and water level can be computed using at most four occupied loads and polynomial-length rational arithmetic.

Finally, the queried utility is either
$$ \frac{e}{a} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \frac{S_m-e}{b}, $$
which also has polynomial bit length and is computable in polynomial time. $\square$

5. A finite category is a uniform subset sample

Fix a compatible $N$ and condition on the incoming normalized cohort counts $x$. Consider a category with a nonempty liking set and positive supply. Compute $h,p,D(h)$ from this empirical state, and define
$$ M=ND(h), \qquad K=N\lambda-NC_x(h). $$
Both are integers: all cohort counts are integral, and $h$ is an integer. Moreover,
$$ K=pM, \qquad 0\leq K<M. $$

Initially, the total deficit of eligible agents below load $h$ is
$$ \sum_{\text{eligible agents}} (h-\text{current load})_+ =NC_x(h). $$
While this deficit is positive, every allocation goes below $h$ and decreases the deficit by one. Thus, after exactly $NC_x(h)$ items:

  • each eligible agent initially below $h$ has reached $h$;
  • each eligible agent initially at or above $h$ is unchanged.

This intermediate configuration is independent of the random allocation choices.

The remaining $K$ items go to distinct agents among the $M$ eligible agents now at load $h$. After selection, an agent moves to $h+1$ and cannot be selected again while unselected eligible agents remain at $h$. Uniform tie-breaking therefore selects a uniform $K$-element subset.

For eligible type $t$, let $B_t$ be the number of selected agents of that type, and let
$$ n_t=Na_t(h). $$
Then
$$ \mathbb E[B_t\mid x]=\frac{Kn_t}{M}. $$
Consequently, if $Y$ is the outgoing normalized random state,
$$ \mathbb E[Y\mid x]=F(x). $$

For $M>1$, the selection indicators satisfy
$$ \mathbb P(I_i=1)=\frac KM, \qquad \mathbb P(I_i=I_\ell=1)=\frac{K(K-1)}{M(M-1)} \quad(i\ne\ell). $$
Expanding the variance of their sum gives
$$ \operatorname{Var}(B_t\mid x) = K\frac{n_t}{M} \left(1-\frac{n_t}{M}\right) \frac{M-K}{M-1} \leq\frac M4 \leq\frac N4. $$
The case $K=0$ has zero variance. If $M=1$, necessarily $K=0$ as well.

Only the coordinates at loads $h,h+1$ fluctuate. Hence, by Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[\|Y-F(x)\|_1\mid x] &= 2\sum_{t\in E} \mathbb E\left[ \left| \frac{B_t-\mathbb E[B_t\mid x]}N \right| \,\middle|\,x \right]\\ &\leq \frac{2}{N}\sum_{t\in E} \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(B_t\mid x)} \leq\frac{\tau}{\sqrt N}. \end{aligned} $$
Zero-supply and unliked categories have zero discrepancy.

The load bounds $R_j$ also apply to every finite realization: the final subset selection creates mass only at the same loads allowed by Lemma 1.

6. A quantitative continuity bound

Fix a nonempty liking set, positive supply $\lambda$, and an incoming load bound $R$. Consider two states $x,y$ with the same prescribed type masses. Set
$$ B=R+\frac{\lambda}{A}. $$
Their water levels $w_x,w_y$ belong to $[0,B]$.

For every $v\in[0,B]$,
$$ |C_x(v)-C_y(v)|\leq B\|x-y\|_1. $$
Also, because $C_x(w_x)=\lambda$,
$$ \lambda \leq B\sum_{t\in E}\sum_{r<w_x}x_{t,r}. $$
For $v\geq w_x$, each cohort below $w_x$ contributes exactly $(v-w_x)x_{t,r}$ to the cost increase. Therefore
$$ C_x(v)-C_x(w_x) \geq \frac{\lambda}{B}(v-w_x). $$
If $w_y\geq w_x$, applying this at $v=w_y$ gives
$$ \frac{\lambda}{B}(w_y-w_x) \leq C_x(w_y)-C_y(w_y) \leq B\|x-y\|_1. $$
Swapping $x,y$ handles the opposite order, so
$$ |w_x-w_y| \leq\frac{B^2}{\lambda}\|x-y\|_1. $$

To bound the outgoing state, let $e_r$ denote unit mass at load $r$. For an eligible incoming load $r$, define
$$ K_w(r) = (1-p)e_{\max\{r,h\}} +p\,e_{\max\{r,h+1\}}, \qquad h=\lfloor w\rfloor,\quad p=w-h. $$
Lemma 1 says that the outgoing type-$t$ distribution is
$$ \sum_r x_{t,r}K_w(r). $$

On each interval between consecutive integers, this kernel is affine in $w$, with slope of $\ell^1$ norm at most two. At an integer $k$, both one-sided limits equal $e_{\max\{r,k\}}$. Subdividing an interval at its integer points therefore proves
$$ \|K_w(r)-K_v(r)\|_1\leq 2|w-v|. $$
Using the triangle inequality and the fixed eligible mass $A$, we obtain
$$ \begin{aligned} \|F(x)-F(y)\|_1 &\leq \|x-y\|_1+2A|w_x-w_y|\\ &\leq \left(1+\frac{2AB^2}{\lambda}\right)\|x-y\|_1. \end{aligned} $$
Thus each category map is Lipschitz on the relevant bounded state space. Zero-supply and unliked categories are identity maps.

7. Replica limit, with an explicit error bound

Let $X_N^{(j)}$ be the normalized random finite state after category $j$, and let $\nu^{(j)}$ be the deterministic mass state. Define
$$ \kappa_j= \begin{cases} 1,&A_j=0\text{ or }\lambda_j=0,\\ 1+\dfrac{2A_j}{\lambda_j} \left(R_{j-1}+\dfrac{\lambda_j}{A_j}\right)^2, &\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
Every incoming state has the prescribed type masses and loads bounded by $R_{j-1}$, so Section 6 applies with this constant.

Put
$$ E_j=\mathbb E\bigl[\|X_N^{(j)}-\nu^{(j)}\|_1\bigr]. $$
The initial states agree exactly, so $E_0=0$. Sections 5 and 6 imply
$$ \begin{aligned} E_j &\leq \mathbb E\bigl[ \|X_N^{(j)}-F_j(X_N^{(j-1)})\|_1 \bigr]\\ &\quad+ \mathbb E\bigl[ \|F_j(X_N^{(j-1)})-F_j(\nu^{(j-1)})\|_1 \bigr]\\ &\leq \frac{\tau}{\sqrt N}+\kappa_jE_{j-1}. \end{aligned} $$
Induction gives
$$ E_m \leq \frac{\tau}{\sqrt N} \sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=i+1}^{m}\kappa_j, $$
where an empty product equals one.

The random utility per queried-type agent is
$$ H_N = \frac{1}{\mu_{t^\star}} \sum_r r\,X_N^{(m)}{}_{t^\star,r}. $$
This identity holds because every allocated item contributes one utility unit to its recipient. By definition, $V_N=\mathbb E[H_N]$. Since all final loads are at most $R_m$,
$$ |H_N-U_{t^\star}| \leq \frac{R_m}{\mu_{t^\star}} \|X_N^{(m)}-\nu^{(m)}\|_1. $$
Consequently,
$$ |V_N-U_{t^\star}| \leq \frac{R_m\tau}{\mu_{t^\star}\sqrt N} \sum_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=i+1}^{m}\kappa_j. $$
For a fixed instance, the coefficient is finite and independent of $N$. Therefore
$$ \boxed{ \lim_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\ \mathrm{compatible}}} V_N=U_{t^\star}. } $$
Compatible integers are unbounded: every sufficiently large multiple of a common denominator of all input masses and supplies is compatible.

The displayed estimate is an instance-dependent convergence rate; no polynomial bound on its coefficient is asserted.

8. Open complexity question

The two-type proof uses the fact that the directly known aggregate load $S$ determines the other type’s load from the queried type’s load. This makes every selected update fractional-linear, and therefore limits bit growth to an additive amount per category.

With three or more support types, the total load of a proper liking subset is not determined by aggregate supply alone. The preceding fractional-linear argument consequently does not establish polynomial bit complexity in that case.

The general simulation provides an exponential upper bound, not an exponential lower bound. No $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness reduction is established here. Thus the unrestricted-support complexity question remains unresolved, while the replica-limit question and the case $\tau\leq 2$ are settled.
