Theorem 4 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many worker types \(t\), with population shares \(\mu_t\), response qualities \(q_t\in[1/2,1)\), costs \(c_t\ge 0\), prior \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and per-capita budget \(\beta\), choose recruited masses \(x_t\) satisfying \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t c_tx_t\le\beta\). For finite juries with type counts asymptotic to \(Nx_t\), find an allocation maximizing the BV error exponent \(E(x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}-N^{-1}\ln(1-\mathrm{JQ}(J_N,\mathrm{BV},\alpha))\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) worker types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has binary-rational quality \(q_t\in[1/2,1)\) and cost \(c_t\ge0\); the binary-rational inputs also include a prior \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and per-capita budget \(\beta\ge0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length and \(m=2\) the number of possible answers.
The ground truth is \(Y\in\{0,1\}\), with \(\Pr(Y=0)=\alpha\). Conditional on \(Y\), all workers vote independently, and a type-\(t\) worker satisfies \(\Pr(v=Y\mid Y)=q_t\) for either truth value.
Bayesian voting (BV) returns the answer with largest posterior probability, breaking ties in favor of \(0\); \(\mathrm{JQ}(J,\mathrm{BV},\alpha)=\Pr(\mathrm{BV}(J)=Y)\).
Choose real masses \(x_t\) with \(0\le x_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t c_tx_t\le\beta\). For each positive integer \(N\), let \(J_N(x)\) contain \(\lfloor Nx_t\rfloor\) workers of each type, with budget \(N\beta\); empty juries are allowed. The objective is to maximize \(E(x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}-N^{-1}\ln(1-\mathrm{JQ}(J_N(x),\mathrm{BV},\alpha))\).

Is finding an allocation maximizing \(E(x)\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

Discrete budget-constrained jury selection maximizing BV jury quality is NP-hard ([Zheng et al.](https://zhydhkcws.github.io/Jury/Jury.pdf)).
Sharp asymptotic misclassification exponents are known for prescribed worker populations under the Dawid–Skene model, subject to regularity conditions ([Gao, Lu, and Zhou](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/gaoa16.pdf)); this does not classify the budget-constrained mass-allocation problem. No classification of that exact problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized population shares to sum to one and encoded qualities, costs, prior, and budget as binary rationals.
  • Specified a nonempty support, a nonnegative budget, real recruited masses, and total input bit length L; m is the fixed number of answers, two.
  • Made independence conditional on the truth and specified symmetric conditional accuracy q_t for both truth values.
  • Defined BV by posterior maximization with ties favoring zero, following the intended rule and the surrounding derivation rather than the duplicated conditional likelihood in the printed definition.
  • Chose floor(Nx_t) type counts to make the jury sequence definite, with total budget N beta.
  • Allowed empty juries, consistent with selection from all budget-feasible subsets, and retained zero costs and quality one-half.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Classification

Finding an exact maximizing allocation is solvable in deterministic polynomial time for \(m=2\).

For every feasible allocation \(x\), the limit exists and equals

\(E(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}x_t d_t,\qquad d_t=-\ln\!\left(2\sqrt{q_t(1-q_t)}\right).\)

Thus the optimization is fractional knapsack. Exact comparisons between its logarithmic value-to-cost ratios are also polynomial-time computable in the binary input model.

1. Deriving the exponent

Fix a feasible allocation \(x\), and write \(n_t=\lfloor Nx_t\rfloor\). Its rounded jury satisfies the budget because

\(\sum_t c_t n_t\le N\sum_t c_tx_t\le N\beta.\)

Let \(P_{0,N}(v)\) and \(P_{1,N}(v)\) be the probabilities of the complete vote vector \(v\), conditional on the respective truth values. All these probabilities are positive since \(q_t\in[1/2,1)\).

Write \(p_N=1-\mathrm{JQ}(J_N(x),\mathrm{BV},\alpha)\). The Bayesian decision minimizes the error contribution at each vote vector, so

\(p_N=\sum_v\min\{\alpha P_{0,N}(v),(1-\alpha)P_{1,N}(v)\}.\)

This identity includes posterior ties: either decision has the same error contribution at a tie.

Define the affinity

\(H_N=\sum_v\sqrt{P_{0,N}(v)P_{1,N}(v)}.\)

Conditional independence gives

\(H_N=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}\left(2\sqrt{q_t(1-q_t)}\right)^{n_t}.\)

Indeed, for one type-\(t\) worker, either vote contributes \(\sqrt{q_t(1-q_t)}\) to the affinity.

Introduce the probability distribution

\(Q_N(v)=H_N^{-1}\sqrt{P_{0,N}(v)P_{1,N}(v)}\)

and the log likelihood ratio

\(W_N(v)=\ln\!\left(\frac{P_{0,N}(v)}{P_{1,N}(v)}\right).\)

Under \(Q_N\), all votes are independent and uniform on \(\{0,1\}\): each worker’s two unnormalized probabilities are equal. Consequently, with

\(w_t=\ln\!\left(\frac{q_t}{1-q_t}\right),\)

the random variable \(W_N\) is a sum of independent symmetric terms taking values \(w_t\) and \(-w_t\). Hence

\(\mathbb E_{Q_N}W_N=0,\qquad V_N:=\operatorname{Var}_{Q_N}(W_N)=\sum_t n_tw_t^2\le N\sum_t\mu_tw_t^2.\)

The last sum is finite because the support is finite and every \(q_t<1\).

From the definitions,

\(P_{0,N}(v)=H_NQ_N(v)e^{W_N(v)/2},\qquad P_{1,N}(v)=H_NQ_N(v)e^{-W_N(v)/2}.\)

Therefore

\(p_N=H_N\mathbb E_{Q_N}\!\left[\min\{\alpha e^{W_N/2},(1-\alpha)e^{-W_N/2}\}\right].\)

Let \(a=\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}>0\). For every real \(w\),

\(a e^{-|w|/2}\le\min\{\alpha e^{w/2},(1-\alpha)e^{-w/2}\}\le\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}.\)

The lower inequality follows by bounding each expression inside the minimum; the upper follows because a minimum is at most the geometric mean.

Set \(h_N=\sqrt{2(V_N+1)}\). Chebyshev’s inequality gives

\(Q_N(|W_N|\le h_N)\ge 1-\frac{V_N}{2(V_N+1)}\ge\frac12.\)

Thus

\(\frac a2 H_Ne^{-h_N/2}\le p_N\le\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}H_N.\)

Equivalently,

\(-\ln H_N-\ln\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)} \le-\ln p_N \le-\ln H_N+\frac{h_N}{2}-\ln(a/2).\)

Since \(V_N=O(N)\), we have \(h_N/N\to0\). Also,

\(-\frac1N\ln H_N=\sum_t\frac{\lfloor Nx_t\rfloor}{N}d_t\longrightarrow\sum_t x_td_t.\)

The sandwich proves the asserted exponent formula. It covers empty juries and types with \(q_t=1/2\), for which \(d_t=0\). In particular, the exponent is independent of the fixed prior \(\alpha\).

2. Constructing a maximizing allocation

Because \(q_t(1-q_t)\le1/4\), every \(d_t\ge0\), with equality precisely when \(q_t=1/2\). The problem is therefore

\(\max\left\{\sum_t d_tx_t:0\le x_t\le\mu_t,\ \sum_t c_tx_t\le\beta\right\}.\)

Use this algorithm:

  • For every type with \(c_t=0\), set \(x_t=\mu_t\).
  • For every type with \(c_t>0\) and \(d_t=0\), set \(x_t=0\).
  • Sort the remaining types by nonincreasing density \(\rho_t=d_t/c_t\), breaking equal-density ties by index.
  • Starting with remaining budget \(B=\beta\), process those types in order, setting

\(x_t=\min\{\mu_t,B/c_t\}\) and then \(B\leftarrow B-c_tx_t\).

This construction is feasible and has at most one coordinate strictly between its bounds.

Proof of optimality

Let \(x^*\) be the constructed allocation. We exhibit \(\lambda\ge0\) such that

\(d_t-\lambda c_t>0\implies x_t^*=\mu_t,\qquad d_t-\lambda c_t<0\implies x_t^*=0.\)

If every positive-cost, positive-value type is filled, choose \(\lambda=0\).

Otherwise, choose \(\lambda\) to be the density of the first such type not filled to capacity. All earlier types are filled and have density at least \(\lambda\); all later types receive zero and have density at most \(\lambda\). The displayed implications hold, including for the types handled in the first two steps. Moreover, in this case the algorithm exhausts the budget:

\(\sum_t c_tx_t^*=\beta.\)

For any feasible allocation \(y\),

\(\sum_t d_t(y_t-x_t^*) =\sum_t(d_t-\lambda c_t)(y_t-x_t^*)+\lambda\sum_t c_t(y_t-x_t^*).\)

Each term in the first sum is nonpositive: a positive coefficient corresponds to \(x_t^*=\mu_t\), and a negative coefficient corresponds to \(x_t^*=0\). The second sum contributes zero when \(\lambda=0\), and is nonpositive otherwise by budget feasibility and exhaustion. Therefore

\(\sum_t d_ty_t\le\sum_t d_tx_t^*.\)

Together with the exponent formula, this proves that \(x^*\) maximizes \(E\).

3. Exact density comparisons in polynomial bit time

The coefficients \(d_t\) involve logarithms, so an exact complexity argument must justify their comparisons.

For the types being sorted, define the positive rational number

\(R_t=\frac1{4q_t(1-q_t)}=\frac{p_t}{s_t}>1,\)

where \(p_t,s_t\) are positive integers. Then \(d_t=\frac12\ln R_t\).

Write two positive costs as \(c_i=u_i/v_i\) and \(c_j=u_j/v_j\), using positive integers. We have

\(\rho_i\ge\rho_j \iff c_j\ln R_i\ge c_i\ln R_j \iff A\ln R_i\ge B\ln R_j,\)

where \(A=u_jv_i\) and \(B=u_iv_j\). Exponentiating and clearing denominators gives

\(\rho_i\ge\rho_j \iff p_i^{A}s_j^{B}\ge p_j^{B}s_i^{A}.\)

Every base and exponent in this representation has \(O(L)\) bits.

We apply the following unconditional theorem: when the number of powers on each side is fixed, comparing two products of positive integer powers, with bases and exponents encoded in binary, is decidable in deterministic polynomial time in their total encoding length. This is Proposition 3.7(2) of [Etessami, Stewart, and Yannakakis](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/17370828/Etessami_Stewart_ET_AL_2014_A_note_on_the_complexity_of_comparing_succinctly_represented_integers_with_an_application_to_maximum_probability_parsing.pdf), using bounds on linear forms in logarithms.

Here there are exactly two powers on each side, independently of \(\tau\). Thus the theorem applies and each density comparison takes \(\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bit time. Testing both directions also detects equality exactly. The powers themselves need not be expanded.

4. Running time and output representation

For the stated case \(m=2\), sorting uses \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) exact comparisons. The remaining steps use \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations.

The remaining budget is always \(\beta\) minus a sum of products of input rationals. Its representation has polynomial bit length, as does the possible fractional coordinate \(B/c_t\). All other output coordinates are input masses or zero. Thus the algorithm outputs an exact rational maximizing allocation with polynomial encoding length.

The total deterministic bit running time is

\(T(2,\tau,L)=O\!\left(\tau\log(\tau+1)\operatorname{poly}(L)\right).\)

Here \(m=2\) is fixed; this statement makes no claim about a variable-\(m\) extension. The parameter \(\tau\) is exactly the supplied support size.

The [finite-jury hardness results of Zheng et al.](https://zhydhkcws.github.io/Jury/Jury.pdf) do not imply hardness for this objective: the proved limiting exponent is additive, and the allocation variables are divisible masses.
