The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite sets of male and female types, each assigned an ordered cell, a complete preference list, and a binary rational mass μ_p or ν_q summing to one on each side, with lists naming at most two types and ties only on the male side, does there exist a mass matching x_{pq} ≥ 0 on acceptable pairs? Require ∑_q x_{pq} ≤ μ_p and ∑_p x_{pq} ≤ ν_q; require that x_{pq} and x_{p'q'} cannot both be positive when p precedes p' but q follows q'; and require, for every acceptable pair (p,q), that B_p(q)=0 or B_q(p)=0, where B_p(q)=μ_p-∑_{q'}x_{pq'}+∑_{q':q≻_p q'}x_{pq'} and B_q(p)=ν_q-∑_{p'}x_{p'q}+∑_{p':p≻_q p'}x_{p'q}.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P,Q\) be nonempty finite sets of male and female types, each with a distinct position in its side's total order on two parallel lines. Each type ranks all its acceptable opposite-side types, at most two, allowing empty lists; acceptability is mutual, women's lists are strict, and men's lists may contain ties. Write \(\succ_p\) for strict preference; every acceptable partner is strictly preferred to remaining unmatched.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|P|+|Q|\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_p,\nu_q\), with \(\sum_p\mu_p=\sum_q\nu_q=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m=\max\{|P|,|Q|\}\) and \(L\) be the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A mass matching has real entries \(x_{pq}\geq0\) on acceptable pairs, zero elsewhere, satisfying \(\sum_qx_{pq}\leq\mu_p\) and \(\sum_px_{pq}\leq\nu_q\). Mass may be split among partners or left unmatched.
Noncrossing means \(x_{pq}x_{p'q'}=0\) whenever \(p\) precedes \(p'\) and \(q\) follows \(q'\).
Define \(B_p(q)=\mu_p-\sum_{q'}x_{pq'}+\sum_{q':q\succ_p q'}x_{pq'}\) and \(B_q(p)=\nu_q-\sum_{p'}x_{p'q}+\sum_{p':p\succ_q p'}x_{p'q}\).
Require \(B_p(q)=0\) or \(B_q(p)=0\) for every acceptable pair \((p,q)\), including pairs whose edge would cross positive matching edges.

Is deciding whether such a noncrossing mass matching exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For individual agents, existence of a noncrossing weakly stable matching is NP-complete even with lists of length at most two and ties only on the male side; it is polynomial-time solvable when every list on one side has length at most one. [Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-021-00832-9).
No result settling this exact mass formulation was located in the literature search.

  • Interpreted ordered cells as distinct positions in a total order on each side, matching the discrete placement convention.
  • Specified that all acceptable partners are strictly preferred to being unmatched; ties are permitted only among acceptable partners on the male side.

  • Allowed different support sizes on the two sides; equal total mass supplies the continuous balance condition.

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when every type has the same dyadic rational mass. The essential point is that a feasible mass matching can then be rounded to an individual matching while preserving stability and noncrossing.

1. What a zero blocking quantity means

Let \(G=(P\cup Q,E)\) be the bipartite acceptability graph. Its maximum degree is at most two. Write \(c_p=\mu_p\) and \(c_q=\nu_q\). For an allocation \(x\), define

\(d_x(v)=\sum_{u:\,vu\in E}x_{vu}.\)

At either endpoint \(v\) of an acceptable pair \(vu\), the blocking quantity is

\(B_v(u)=c_v-d_x(v)+\sum_{w:\,u\succ_v w}x_{vw}.\)

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Consequently,

\(B_v(u)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_x(v)=c_v\ \text{and}\ x_{vw}=0\ \text{for every }w\text{ with }u\succ_v w.\)

Thus a zero blocking quantity requires two things: the endpoint is saturated, and it receives no mass from a strictly worse partner.

It follows that stability is preserved by any feasible allocation \(z\) satisfying:

  • \(\operatorname{supp}(z)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x)\);
  • every vertex saturated by \(x\) remains saturated by \(z\).

Indeed, for each acceptable pair, choose an endpoint whose blocking quantity was zero under \(x\). Both conditions establishing that zero remain true under \(z\). This argument applies to every acceptable pair, including pairs crossing positive edges.

Noncrossing is also preserved when the positive support shrinks.

2. An integer rounding lemma

Lemma. Let \(G\) be a finite bipartite graph of maximum degree at most two, with nonnegative integer vertex capacities \(b_v\). Suppose real edge values \(y_e\geq0\) satisfy

\(\sum_{e\ni v}y_e\leq b_v\)

at every vertex. Then there are nonnegative integer edge values \(z_e\) satisfying the same capacities, with

\(\operatorname{supp}(z)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(y),\)

such that every vertex saturated by \(y\) remains saturated by \(z\).

Proof. Consider the graph consisting of the edges whose current values are nonintegers. Its nonempty connected components are paths or cycles. Every cycle has even length because \(G\) is bipartite.

Choose one component and assign alternating signs \(+1,-1\) to its edges. Replace their values by

\(y_e+\sigma_e\varepsilon,\qquad \sigma_e\in\{+1,-1\}.\)

At every internal vertex of a path, and every vertex of a cycle, the two changes cancel. Hence its total incident allocation remains unchanged.

A path endpoint has exactly one incident noninteger edge. All its other incident values are integers. Its total allocation therefore has the form \(k+f\), where \(k\) is an integer and \(0<f<1\). Since its capacity is an integer, feasibility implies

\(b_v-(k+f)\geq1-f.\)

Thus increasing its noninteger edge as far as the next integer preserves its capacity. Decreasing that edge as far as the preceding integer preserves nonnegativity. Such an endpoint cannot be saturated.

For each selected edge, let \(f_e=y_e-\lfloor y_e\rfloor\). Choose

\(\varepsilon=\min\bigl(\{1-f_e:\sigma_e=+1\}\cup\{f_e:\sigma_e=-1\}\bigr).\)

This number is positive. Every changed edge stays between its preceding and following integers, and at least one becomes an integer. The preceding observations establish feasibility and preservation of every currently saturated vertex.

Integer-valued edges are untouched. In particular, no zero edge becomes positive, and no new noninteger edge appears. Each iteration strictly decreases the number of noninteger edges. After at most \(|E|\) iterations, all values are integers. Throughout, support only shrinks and all originally saturated vertices remain saturated. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

3. Equal masses give an exact reduction from individuals

Consider an individual instance with \(N\) men and \(N\) women, and give every corresponding type mass \(1/N\).

An individual noncrossing weakly stable matching immediately gives a feasible mass matching: put mass \(1/N\) on each matched pair and zero elsewhere.

Conversely, suppose a feasible stable noncrossing mass matching \(x\) exists. Scale it by setting \(y=Nx\). Every vertex now has capacity one. Apply the lemma to obtain integer values \(z_e\). Feasibility forces

\(z_e\in\{0,1\},\)

so the positive edges of \(z\) form an individual matching.

The rounding preserves all saturated vertices and only removes positive edges. Section 1 therefore shows that \(z\) satisfies the scaled stability conditions and remains noncrossing.

To verify that these are exactly individual weak stability conditions, consider any acceptable pair \(vu\). Under an individual matching, its scaled blocking quantity is one precisely when \(v\) is unmatched or strictly prefers \(u\) to its assigned partner; otherwise it is zero. Hence both blocking quantities are positive precisely when both endpoints strictly prefer the pair to their current situations. This also handles ties correctly.

We have therefore proved the equivalence:

\(\text{an individual noncrossing weakly stable matching exists}\)

\(\Longleftrightarrow\)

\(\text{a feasible noncrossing mass matching exists with all masses }1/N.\)

The individual problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete with lists of length at most two and ties only in men's lists, by Theorem 1 of [Hamada, Miyazaki, and Okamoto](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-021-00832-9). The source calls this a *weak-SSNM*: weak stability in preferences, with blocking pairs checked even when they cross matching edges. Thus its stability requirement agrees with the one used here.

To ensure dyadic masses, start with the source instance having \(n\) agents on each side and let \(N\) be the least power of two satisfying \(N\geq n\). Append agents with empty lists to both orders until each side has \(N\) agents. These agents cannot participate in any acceptable pair, so padding preserves existence. Assign every type mass \(1/N\).

All hypotheses are satisfied: masses are positive dyadic rationals, each side's masses sum to one, acceptability remains mutual, list lengths remain at most two, and women's lists remain strict. Moreover,

\(m=N,\qquad \tau=2N,\qquad N\leq2n.\)

With indexed agents, the constructed instance has \(L=O(N\log(N+1))\), and the reduction takes polynomial time. The equivalence above proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) and running times

Real-valued feasibility does not automatically establish membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), so a certificate-size argument is needed.

Write all input masses as binary-encoded fractions and let \(D\) be the product of their denominators. Then \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits, and every scaled capacity \(Dc_v\) is an integer.

If a feasible stable noncrossing mass matching \(x\) exists, scale it to \(y=Dx\) and apply the integer rounding lemma. By Section 1, the resulting integer allocation \(z\) preserves stability and noncrossing. Consequently, \(z/D\) is another feasible solution.

Since every mass is at most one,

\(0\leq z_e\leq D.\)

Let \(e=|E|\). The list-length restriction gives

\(e\leq2\min\{|P|,|Q|\}\leq\tau.\)

Thus the integers \(z_e\) provide a certificate of \(O(\tau L)\) bits. A verifier computes the scaled capacities, checks all vertex capacities, evaluates both blocking quantities for every acceptable pair, and checks all pairs of positive edges for crossings. A conservative bound using elementary integer arithmetic is

\(O(\tau^2L^2)=O(m^2L^2)\)

bit operations. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

For an explicit deterministic upper bound, enumerate a proposed noncrossing allowed support and one zero-blocking endpoint for each acceptable pair. There are at most \(4^e\) choices. For each choice, forbid all edges strictly worse than the selected endpoint's partner and require every selected endpoint to be saturated. Feasibility is then a bipartite flow problem with lower bounds. After scaling by \(D\), all capacities are integers. Standard lower-bound reduction and Edmonds–Karp give the conservative total bound

\(O(4^\tau\tau^3L^2)=O(16^m m^3L^2).\)

Therefore the requested decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
