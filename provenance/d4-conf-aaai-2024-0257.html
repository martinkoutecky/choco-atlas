Fix a finite-support \(K\)-nary GBT root law and positive rational prior variance \(\sigma^2\). An instance gives \(m\) alternatives, a positive integer population scale \(N\), report types \(t=(a_t,b_t,r_t)\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and rational \(\varepsilon>0\). Output a rational vector within \(\ell_2\)-distance \(\varepsilon\) of the minimizer of \(J(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{a=1}^m\theta_a^2+N\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t[\Phi_f(\theta_{a_t}-\theta_{b_t})-r_t(\theta_{a_t}-\theta_{b_t})]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix an integer \(K\geq2\) and a rational \(\sigma^2>0\). The root law is uniform on \(S_K=\{2(k-1)/(K-1)-1:1\leq k\leq K\}\), with \(\Phi_f(x)=\log(K^{-1}\sum_{r\in S_K}e^{rx})\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) report types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
An instance specifies \(m\geq2\) alternatives, a binary positive integer population scale \(N\), types \(t=(a_t,b_t,r_t)\) with distinct \(a_t,b_t\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and \(r_t\in S_K\), and a binary rational \(\varepsilon>0\). Reversing a report replaces \((a,b,r)\) by \((b,a,-r)\); each listed mass contributes once.
Over \(\theta\in\mathbb R^m\), define \(J(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{a=1}^m\theta_a^2+N\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t[\Phi_f(\theta_{a_t}-\theta_{b_t})-r_t(\theta_{a_t}-\theta_{b_t})]\), and let \(\theta^*\) be its unique minimizer.
The computational problem is to output \(\widehat\theta\in\mathbb Q^m\) satisfying \(\|\widehat\theta-\theta^*\|_2\leq\varepsilon\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including \(N\), the masses, and \(\varepsilon\); \(K\) and \(\sigma^2\) are fixed constants.

Is this approximation problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite comparison data, the negative log-posterior is \((1/\sigma^2)\)-strongly convex and has a unique minimizer; the accompanying computational claim supplies no polynomial bound in binary input length. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.08644v2)
No result settling this exact rational-mass approximation problem was located in the literature search.

  • Restricted reports to the root-law support and comparisons between distinct alternatives; required at least two alternatives and nonempty society support.

Claim. The approximation problem is solvable by a deterministic algorithm in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), for every fixed \(K\geq2\) and fixed rational \(\sigma^2>0\).

The proof requires more than strong convexity: it must control the search radius, numerical evaluation, and the bit complexity of optimization. The [cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.08644v2) establishes strong convexity in its finite-data setting; the argument below supplies these additional controls for the stated rational-mass problem.

1. Convexity, existence, and quantitative bounds

Write \(v_t=e_{a_t}-e_{b_t}\), so that \(\|v_t\|_2=\sqrt2\). For real \(x\), define

\[ p_r(x)=\frac{e^{rx}}{\sum_{s\in S_K}e^{sx}}. \]

Then

\[ \Phi_f'(x)=\sum_{r\in S_K}r\,p_r(x),\qquad \Phi_f''(x)=\sum_{r\in S_K}(r-\Phi_f'(x))^2p_r(x)\geq0. \]

Because \(S_K\subseteq[-1,1]\), we also have \(|\Phi_f'(x)|\leq1\). Consequently,

\[ \nabla J(\theta)=\frac{\theta}{\sigma^2} +N\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t \bigl(\Phi_f'(v_t^\mathsf T\theta)-r_t\bigr)v_t, \]

and

\[ \nabla^2J(\theta)=\frac1{\sigma^2}I+ N\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\Phi_f''(v_t^\mathsf T\theta)v_tv_t^\mathsf T \succeq\frac1{\sigma^2}I. \]

Thus \(J\) is \((1/\sigma^2)\)-strongly convex.

For every listed report, the term corresponding to \(r_t\) in the exponential sum gives

\[ \Phi_f(x)-r_tx\geq-\log K. \]

Since the masses sum to \(1\),

\[ J(\theta)\geq\frac{\|\theta\|_2^2}{2\sigma^2}-N\log K. \]

Hence \(J\) is coercive and has a unique minimizer \(\theta^*\).

Symmetry of \(S_K\) gives \(\Phi_f'(0)=0\), whence

\[ \|\nabla J(0)\|_2 =\left\|-N\sum_t\mu_tr_tv_t\right\|_2 \leq\sqrt2N. \]

Strong convexity implies strong monotonicity of the gradient. Applying this to \(0\) and \(\theta^*\), using \(\nabla J(\theta^*)=0\), yields

\[ \frac{\|\theta^*\|_2^2}{\sigma^2} \leq-\nabla J(0)^\mathsf T\theta^* \leq\|\nabla J(0)\|_2\|\theta^*\|_2. \]

It follows, including the case \(\theta^*=0\), that

\[ \|\theta^*\|_2\leq\sqrt2\,\sigma^2N. \]

Set

\[ R=1+2\sigma^2N,\qquad D=\{\theta:\|\theta\|_2\leq R\},\qquad G=\frac R{\sigma^2}+3N. \]

These are rational numbers of bit length \(O(L+1)\), since \(\sigma^2\) is fixed. Moreover, \(\|\theta^*\|_2<R-1\), and for every \(\theta\in D\),

\[ \|\nabla J(\theta)\|_2 \leq\frac R{\sigma^2} +N\sum_t\mu_t\,2\sqrt2 \leq G. \]

Integrating the gradient along line segments in the convex set \(D\) shows that

\[ |J(x)-J(y)|\leq G\|x-y\|_2 \quad\text{for all }x,y\in D. \]

Finally, strong convexity gives the error conversion

\[ J(x)-J(\theta^*)\geq \frac{\|x-\theta^*\|_2^2}{2\sigma^2}. \]

2. Polynomial-time numerical evaluation

We next establish that \(J\) and its gradient can be approximated to any prescribed absolute precision in polynomial bit complexity. This must hold even when a rational score difference has exponentially large numerical magnitude.

For rational \(x\), let \(h=|x|\), and choose \(s=1\) when \(x\geq0\) and \(s=-1\) otherwise. Put

\[ q_r=e^{-(1-sr)h},\qquad Z=\sum_{r\in S_K}q_r,\qquad U=\sum_{r\in S_K}r q_r. \]

The endpoint \(r=s\) belongs to \(S_K\), so its term is exactly \(1\). Therefore

\[ 1\leq Z\leq K,\qquad \Phi_f(x)=h+\log Z-\log K,\qquad \Phi_f'(x)=\frac UZ. \]

All exponentials now have nonpositive arguments.

For completeness, \(e^{-u}\), with rational \(u\geq0\), admits an absolute-error \(2^{-p}\) approximation in time polynomial in the bit length of \(u\) and \(p\):

  • If \(u\geq p+2\), returning \(0\) has error smaller than \(2^{-p}\).
  • Otherwise \(u=O(p+1)\). Taylor expansion at \(0\), with \(O(p+1)\) terms, suffices. Indeed, its remainder after \(q-1\) terms has magnitude at most \(u^q/q!\), and \(q!\geq(q/e)^q\). Choosing \(q\geq8(p+2)\) makes this remainder smaller than \(2^{-p}\).

The Taylor terms are rational, and their exact numerators and denominators have polynomial bit length. Rounding and clipping to \([0,1]\), with a few extra precision bits, preserves the claimed accuracy. The endpoint term can be kept exactly equal to \(1\).

If each \(q_r\) is approximated within \(\zeta\), with nonnegative approximations, then the resulting \(\widetilde Z,\widetilde U\) satisfy

\[ \widetilde Z\geq1,\quad |Z-\widetilde Z|\leq K\zeta,\quad |U-\widetilde U|\leq K\zeta,\quad |\widetilde U|\leq\widetilde Z. \]

Consequently,

\[ \left|\frac UZ-\frac{\widetilde U}{\widetilde Z}\right| \leq2K\zeta, \qquad |\log Z-\log\widetilde Z|\leq K\zeta. \]

Logarithms of rational numbers \(z\in[1,K]\) are also polynomial-time approximable. Explicitly,

\[ \log z=2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{w^{2j+1}}{2j+1}, \qquad w=\frac{z-1}{z+1}. \]

Here \(0\leq w\leq(K-1)/(K+1)<1\). Since \(K\) is fixed, \(O(p+1)\) terms give absolute error \(2^{-p}\). The same construction approximates \(\log K\).

These procedures prove the following numerical fact:

Given rational \(\theta\) and positive rational tolerances \(\nu,\eta\), one can compute rational \(j,g\) satisfying

\[ > |j-J(\theta)|\leq\nu,\qquad > \|g-\nabla J(\theta)\|_2\leq\eta, > \]

in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), the bit length of \(\theta\), and the bit lengths of \(\nu,\eta\).

To check accumulation of errors, if every \(\Phi_f\) evaluation has error at most \(a\), its contribution to the objective error is at most \(Na\), because \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). If every derivative evaluation has error at most \(b\), its contribution to the gradient error is at most \(\sqrt2Nb\). The quadratic and report-linear terms are rational and can be computed exactly.

Thus the required extra precision depends on \(\log N\), not on \(N\). The sums range over the \(\tau\) listed reports; no expansion into individual voters is performed.

3. A finite-precision ellipsoid construction

We record the optimization machinery explicitly to ensure a bit-complexity bound.

Let

\[ E(c,A)=\{x:(x-c)^\mathsf TA^{-1}(x-c)\leq1\}, \]

where \(A\) is positive definite. For dimension \(n\geq2\), a nonzero central-cut normal \(g\) defines the retained halfspace

\[ H=\{x:g^\mathsf T(x-c)\leq0\}. \]

The exact central-cut update is

\[ d=c-\frac{Ag}{(n+1)\sqrt{g^\mathsf TAg}}, \]

\[ B=\frac{n^2}{n^2-1} \left(A-\frac{2}{n+1} \frac{Agg^\mathsf TA}{g^\mathsf TAg}\right). \]

It satisfies \(E(c,A)\cap H\subseteq E(d,B)\). Here is a direct verification. After transforming the original ellipsoid into the unit ball and rotating the cut normal to the first coordinate, the new center is \(-e_1/(n+1)\); the squared semiaxes are \(n^2/(n+1)^2\) in that direction and \(n^2/(n^2-1)\) in perpendicular directions. For a point in the retained half-ball, writing its first coordinate as \(t\in[-1,0]\), its new ellipsoid expression is at most

\[ \frac{(n+1)^2}{n^2} \left(t+\frac1{n+1}\right)^2 +\frac{n^2-1}{n^2}(1-t^2) =1+\frac{2(n+1)}{n^2}t(t+1)\leq1. \]

The exact volume ratio is

\[ q_n=\left(\frac{n^2}{n^2-1}\right)^{n/2} \sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n+1}}. \]

Expanding the logarithms in their convergent power series gives

\[ \log q_n =-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac1{2k(2k-1)n^{2k-1}} \leq-\frac1{2n}. \]

We now make the updates rational while retaining exponential volume decrease.

Fix in advance an iteration budget \(T\), start with \(c_0=0\), \(A_0=R^2I\), where \(R\geq1\), and set

\[ \alpha=\frac1{64n^2},\qquad \ell=4^{-(T+1)}. \]

Choose a dyadic number \(h\) satisfying

\[ 0<h\leq\frac{\alpha\ell}{4n}. \]

At each iteration:

  • Compute the rational matrix \(B\) above.
  • Approximate each coordinate of \(d\) by a multiple of \(h\), with error at most \(h\), obtaining \(\widehat c\).
  • Set \(C=(1+\alpha)^2B\), round its entries symmetrically to multiples of \(h\), with entrywise error at most \(h\), and call the result \(C_{\rm round}\).
  • Set \(\widehat A=C_{\rm round}+nhI\).

We verify all needed properties. The exact update obeys

\[ B\succeq\frac14A. \]

Since \(\widehat A\succeq C\succeq B\), induction gives \(\lambda_{\min}(A_i)\geq4^{-i}\). Thus every updated \(B\) has smallest eigenvalue at least \(\ell\).

The center error satisfies

\[ \|\widehat c-d\|_2\leq\sqrt n\,h \leq\alpha\sqrt\ell, \]

so its norm measured using \(B^{-1}\) is at most \(\alpha\). The triangle inequality in that norm proves

\[ E(d,B)\subseteq E(\widehat c,C). \]

For symmetric entrywise rounding, the operator-norm error is at most \(nh\). Hence

\[ C\preceq\widehat A\preceq C+2nhI \preceq(1+\alpha)C. \]

These inequalities prove containment and positive definiteness. They also bound the rounded-update volume ratio by

\[ q_n(1+\alpha)^{3n/2}. \]

Its logarithm is at most

\[ -\frac1{2n}+\frac{3n\alpha}{2} =-\frac1{2n}+\frac3{128n} \leq-\frac1{4n}. \]

Thus every rational update shrinks volume by a factor at most \(e^{-1/(4n)}\).

Finally, this construction has polynomial bit complexity. The grid denominator has \(O(T+\log n)\) bits. Furthermore,

\[ \lambda_{\max}(\widehat A) \leq(1+\alpha)^3\frac{n^2}{n^2-1}\lambda_{\max}(A) <2\lambda_{\max}(A). \]

Therefore matrix magnitudes and accumulated center magnitudes have logarithms bounded polynomially in \(T,\log R,n\). Every stored center and matrix consequently has polynomial bit length. Given cut normals of polynomial bit length, the exact intermediate rational operations also have polynomial bit complexity. The only square root is of a positive rational of polynomial bit length; rational bisection computes the center to the required precision in polynomial time.

We have therefore proved the precise implementation fact needed below: after \(T\) rational central cuts, all containment statements remain valid and

\[ \operatorname{vol}(E_T) \leq e^{-T/(4n)}\operatorname{vol}(E_0), \]

with polynomial arithmetic and bit complexity.

4. The approximation algorithm

Define the rational parameters

\[ \varepsilon_0=\min\{\varepsilon,1\},\qquad \Delta=\frac{\varepsilon_0^2}{4\sigma^2},\qquad \nu=\frac\Delta4,\qquad \eta=\frac{\Delta}{8R},\qquad \rho=\min\left\{1,\frac{\Delta}{4G}\right\}. \]

Each has polynomial bit length. The ball

\[ P=\{y:\|y-\theta^*\|_2\leq\rho\} \]

is contained in \(D\), because \(\|\theta^*\|_2<R-1\) and \(\rho\leq1\). The Lipschitz bound proves that every \(y\in P\) satisfies

\[ J(y)\leq J(\theta^*)+G\rho \leq J(\theta^*)+\frac\Delta4. \]

Compute an integer \(b\geq1\) such that \(R/\rho\leq2^b\), and set

\[ T=8m^2(b+1). \]

Because \(R/\rho\) has polynomial bit length, \(b\) and \(T\) are polynomially bounded in \(m,L\).

Run the rational ellipsoid construction in dimension \(m\), starting with \(E_0=D\). At its current rational center \(x\):

  • If \(\|x\|_2^2>R^2\), use the central-cut normal \(x\).
  • Otherwise compute \(j,g\) with the guarantees from Section 2, using tolerances \(\nu,\eta\), and record \(x,j\).
  • If \(g=0\), output \(x\) immediately.
  • Otherwise use the central-cut normal \(g\).

If all \(T\) iterations finish, output the recorded center with smallest recorded value \(j\). There is at least one recorded center, since the initial center \(0\) belongs to \(D\).

We prove correctness.

First, a cut at a center outside \(D\) retains all of \(D\). Indeed, for \(y\in D\),

\[ x^\mathsf T(y-x) \leq\|x\|_2R-\|x\|_2^2<0. \]

Second, if an evaluated center has \(g=0\), then \(\|\nabla J(x)\|_2\leq\eta\). Convexity and \(x,\theta^*\in D\) give

\[ J(x)-J(\theta^*) \leq\nabla J(x)^\mathsf T(x-\theta^*) \leq2R\eta=\frac\Delta4. \]

The strong-convexity error conversion already proves the required approximation in this case.

Now suppose no immediate return occurs. Consider any evaluated center \(x\) for which

\[ J(x)>J(\theta^*)+\Delta. \]

For \(y\in P\), convexity implies

\[ \nabla J(x)^\mathsf T(y-x) \leq J(y)-J(x)<-\frac{3\Delta}{4}. \]

Since \(x,y\in D\), the approximate-gradient error contributes at most

\[ |(g-\nabla J(x))^\mathsf T(y-x)| \leq2R\eta=\frac\Delta4. \]

Consequently,

\[ g^\mathsf T(y-x)<-\frac\Delta2<0. \]

Thus every cut at such a center retains the entire ball \(P\).

If every evaluated center had objective gap greater than \(\Delta\), all cuts would retain \(P\). The containment guarantees in Section 3 would then imply \(P\subseteq E_T\). But, writing \(v_m\) for the volume of the unit ball,

\[ \operatorname{vol}(E_T) \leq e^{-T/(4m)}v_mR^m <v_m\rho^m=\operatorname{vol}(P). \]

The strict inequality follows from \(T=8m^2(b+1)\) and \(\log(R/\rho)\leq b\log2\). This is a contradiction. Hence some recorded center \(x_{\rm good}\) satisfies

\[ J(x_{\rm good})-J(\theta^*)\leq\Delta. \]

The output \(\widehat\theta\) minimizes the recorded approximate objective values, each with absolute error at most \(\nu\). Therefore

\[ J(\widehat\theta) \leq J(x_{\rm good})+2\nu \leq J(\theta^*)+\frac{3\Delta}{2}. \]

Applying strong convexity,

\[ \|\widehat\theta-\theta^*\|_2^2 \leq2\sigma^2\bigl(J(\widehat\theta)-J(\theta^*)\bigr) \leq3\sigma^2\Delta =\frac34\varepsilon_0^2 \leq\varepsilon^2. \]

This establishes the required guarantee.

5. Complexity conclusion

There are polynomially many ellipsoid iterations. Section 3 bounds the bit lengths of their rational centers and matrices. Section 2 supplies polynomial-time objective and gradient evaluations at those centers, at the polynomial-bit tolerances used above. All remaining comparisons and arithmetic are rational operations of polynomial bit complexity.

The algorithm outputs a rational vector and runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Binary encoding of \(N\) does not invalidate this result: the iteration and precision bounds depend on \(\log N\).
