The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(T=\{s\}\cup V\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), a star communication graph \(H\), and a symmetric rational utility matrix \(U\), find a measurable partition into coalitions whose type supports are connected in \(H\), with every individual assigned to one coalition, such that no positive infinitesimal mass of type \(t\) can move between coalitions, strictly improve its utility, and be accepted by every destination neighbor \(r\) satisfying the marginal condition \(U(r,t)\ge0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(T=\{s\}\cup V\), the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, with binary rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\). Here \(m=|V|\) and \(\tau=m+1\); support size means listed types, not possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The communication graph is the star \(H\) with edges \(\{s,v\}\) for \(v\in V\). Utilities are given by a symmetric rational matrix \(U\), with \(U(t,t)=0\) and unrestricted signs off the diagonal, including between nonadjacent types.
A mass partition is a finite family of nonzero vectors \(x^1,\ldots,x^k\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{T}\) satisfying \(\sum_a x^a_t=\mu_t\). Types may split across coalitions; there is no prescribed bound on \(k\). Each support \(\operatorname{supp}(x^a)=\{t:x^a_t>0\}\) must induce a connected subgraph of \(H\); singleton supports are allowed. These vectors describe a measurable partition of atomless type populations, with every individual assigned.
A type-\(t\) individual in coalition \(x\) has utility \(u_t(x)=\sum_r U(t,r)x_r\).
An infinitesimal deviation from \(x^a\), where \(x^a_t>0\), may join another coalition \(y=x^b\), \(b\ne a\), or form a new coalition alone, represented by \(y=0\). It is profitable and admissible exactly when \(u_t(y)>u_t(x^a)\), \(\operatorname{supp}(y)\cup\{t\}\) is connected, and \(U(r,t)\ge0\) for every \(r\in\operatorname{supp}(y)\) adjacent to \(t\) in \(H\). Destination approval is vacuous for \(y=0\); no departure approval is required.
A partition is in-neighbor stable if it admits no such deviation. The computational problem is to find one, or determine that none exists.

Can this problem be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard as a search problem?

For finite individual players, finding an in-neighbor stable partition with symmetric additively separable utilities on a star is PLS-complete, and a stable partition always exists ([verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.05342)).
No result settling this exact mass problem was located in the literature search.

  • The measurable partition had no computational representation specified. I use finitely many rational mass vectors, with unrestricted finite coalition count and type splitting; coalitions have positive total mass, excluding a partition entirely into null individual blocks.
  • Utility aggregation was undefined. I use the mass-weighted additive sum, without division by coalition mass.
  • Within-type utilities were unspecified. I set the diagonal to zero, extending the discrete zero-diagonal convention to types.
  • I remove zero-mass types from the input support and retain the stated total-mass normalization of one.
  • The deviation definition omitted forming a new singleton coalition. I include this move with utility zero and vacuous destination approval.
  • I interpret infinitesimal improvement by a strict comparison of limiting utilities, require connected destination support after entry, and require nonnegative marginal utility for every destination neighbor.
  • Existence and failure handling were unspecified for the mass model. The search problem permits reporting nonexistence.
  • There is no candidate parameter in this model; I define m as the number of leaf types and L as total input bit length.

Proven progress and remaining scope

The polynomial-time versus $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness classification remains partial. The following results are proved below:

  • A four-type instance has no stable mass partition.
  • Replacing two zero-valued utilities in that instance by $\varepsilon>0$ yields instances whose minimum number of coalitions is exactly $\lceil 1/(2\varepsilon)\rceil+1$. Thus explicitly listing a stable partition can require exponential time, even with bounded utilities and four types.

These results do not establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness or classify search with compressed output.

1. Preliminary observations

Call a coalition central if its center coordinate is positive. Every noncentral coalition has singleton support: two distinct leaves induce a disconnected subgraph of the star.

Every type present in a stable coalition must have nonnegative utility. Indeed, forming a new singleton is admissible and gives utility zero.

If all center–leaf utilities are nonnegative, the center can admissibly join every existing coalition. Consequently, stability forces the center’s utility to have a common value $C$ in all central coalitions. For two distinct central coalitions, this follows by comparing deviations in both directions; with only one central coalition, define $C$ to be its center utility.

2. A bounded-utility family: nonexistence and exponential output size

Give each of $s,a,b,c$ mass $1/4$. For a rational parameter $\varepsilon\geq 0$, use the symmetric matrix
$$ U_\varepsilon= \begin{pmatrix} 0&1&\varepsilon&\varepsilon\\ 1&0&1&-3\\ \varepsilon&1&0&4\\ \varepsilon&-3&4&0 \end{pmatrix}, $$
in the order $s,a,b,c$.

All center–leaf utilities are nonnegative. Thus every leaf can admissibly join any central coalition, and the center can admissibly join any existing coalition.

Theorem. For $\varepsilon=0$, no stable partition exists. For $\varepsilon>0$, the minimum number of coalitions in a stable partition is
$$ \left\lceil\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\right\rceil+1. $$

2.1. Necessary properties of any stable partition

Assume a stable partition exists, and let $C$ be its common center utility.

The common center utility is positive

Individual rationality gives $C\geq 0$. In every central coalition $y$,
$$ C=y_a+\varepsilon(y_b+y_c). $$
If $C=0$, then $y_a=0$ in every central coalition. All $a$-mass therefore lies in singleton coalitions.

Choose such a singleton with mass $d>0$. A center individual can admissibly join it and obtain utility $d>0$, contradicting stability. Hence
$$ C>0. $$

All $b$-mass is central

In a central coalition $y$,
$$ u_b(y)=y_a+\varepsilon y_s+4y_c. $$
If $\varepsilon>0$, this is positive because $y_s>0$. If $\varepsilon=0$, it is positive because $y_a=C>0$.

A noncentral $b$-coalition gives utility zero, so its members would profitably join a central coalition. Thus all $b$-mass is central.

All $c$-mass is central

Suppose some $c$-mass lies in a singleton. Stability then requires
$$ u_c(y)=\varepsilon y_s-3y_a+4y_b\leq 0 $$
for every central coalition $y$.

Let $A$ be the total central $a$-mass. Summing over the finite family of central coalitions gives
$$ \sum_{y\text{ central}}u_c(y) = \frac{\varepsilon}{4}-3A+1 \geq \frac{\varepsilon}{4}+\frac14 >0, $$
where $A\leq 1/4$ and all $b$-mass is central. This contradicts the nonpositive summands. Therefore all $c$-mass is central.

Some central coalition contains no $a$

If every central coalition contained $a$, individual rationality would give $u_a(y)\geq 0$ in every such coalition. But
$$ u_a(y)=y_s+y_b-3y_c, $$
and hence
$$ \sum_{y\text{ central}}u_a(y) = \frac14+\frac14-\frac34 = -\frac14. $$
This is impossible. Thus some central coalition $z$ satisfies $z_a=0$.

2.2. Nonexistence when $\varepsilon=0$

For the coalition $z$ just obtained,
$$ C=z_a+\varepsilon(z_b+z_c)=0 $$
when $\varepsilon=0$. This contradicts $C>0$.

Therefore the instance with $\varepsilon=0$ has no stable partition. The argument applies to every finite partition, regardless of how types split.

2.3. Lower bound when $\varepsilon>0$

In the central coalition $z$ containing no $a$,
$$ C=\varepsilon(z_b+z_c)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. $$
For any central coalition $x$,
$$ x_a\leq x_a+\varepsilon(x_b+x_c)=C. $$

A noncentral coalition containing $a$ is a singleton. If its mass is $d$, the center can join it and receive utility $d$. Stability therefore requires $d\leq C$.

Thus every coalition contains at most $\varepsilon/2$ mass of $a$. Since total $a$-mass is $1/4$, at least
$$ \left\lceil\frac{1/4}{\varepsilon/2}\right\rceil = \left\lceil\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\right\rceil $$
coalitions contain $a$. The central coalition $z$ contains no $a$, so one additional coalition is necessary.

2.4. Matching stable construction

Set
$$ N=\left\lceil\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\right\rceil. $$
Use one central coalition
$$ x^0=\left(\frac14,0,\frac14,\frac14\right) $$
and $N$ singleton coalitions, each with vector
$$ x^j=\left(0,\frac{1}{4N},0,0\right), \qquad 1\leq j\leq N. $$
All coordinate sums equal the prescribed masses, and all supports are connected.

The central utilities are
$$ u_s(x^0)=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}, \qquad u_b(x^0)=u_c(x^0)=1+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}. $$

An $a$-individual receives zero in its singleton, while joining the central coalition gives
$$ u_a(x^0)=\frac14+\frac14-\frac34=-\frac14. $$
Joining another $a$-singleton or forming a new singleton gives zero.

For $b$ and $c$, joining an $a$-singleton would produce disconnected support. Forming a new singleton gives zero, below their current utilities.

The center receives
$$ \frac{1}{4N}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2} $$
by joining an $a$-singleton, where the inequality follows from $N\geq 1/(2\varepsilon)$. Forming a new singleton gives zero.

These exhaust the destinations, so the construction is stable and attains the lower bound.

2.5. Explicit-output consequence

Take $\varepsilon=2^{-n}$ with $n\geq 1$. The instance has fixed $m=3$ and $\tau=4$, all utility magnitudes are at most $4$, and its binary input length is $L=\Theta(n)$.

Nevertheless, every stable partition has at least
$$ 2^{n-1}+1=2^{\Omega(L)} $$
coalitions. Therefore an algorithm that explicitly writes one vector per coalition cannot run in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

This is an output-size obstruction, not an $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness proof. The matching construction has a short representation using one singleton vector and its binary multiplicity.

3. An exact polynomial-time existence test for a subclass

Consider instances satisfying the following hypotheses:

  • There is a distinguished leaf $a$ with $U(s,a)=\alpha>0$.
  • For every $i$ in $Z=V\setminus\{a\}$, $U(s,i)=0$.
  • For distinct $i,j\in Z$, $B_{ij}=U(i,j)\geq 0$.

Write
$$ \beta_i=U(a,i), \qquad i\in Z, $$
and set $B_{ii}=0$. The coefficients $\beta_i$ may have either sign.

Start with
$$ F_0=\{i\in Z:\beta_i>0\}. $$
For a current set $F$, define
$$ d_i(F)=\beta_i\mu_a+\sum_{j\in F}B_{ij}\mu_j. $$
Repeatedly add an element $i\notin F$ with $d_i(F)>0$, until no such element remains. Let $F$ be the resulting terminal set, and define
$$ D=\alpha\mu_s+\sum_{i\in F}\beta_i\mu_i. $$

Theorem. A stable partition exists if and only if $D\geq 0$. When $D\geq 0$, a stable partition can be found in polynomial time without splitting any type.

3.1. The distinguished leaf occurs in every central coalition

Assume a stable partition exists. All center–leaf utilities are nonnegative, so the common center utility is
$$ C=\alpha x_a $$
in every central coalition $x$.

If $C=0$, all $a$-mass is in singletons, and the center can profitably join any positive-mass $a$-singleton. Therefore $C>0$, and every central coalition has the same positive $a$-coordinate
$$ h=\frac{C}{\alpha}>0. $$

3.2. Every type added to $F$ must have all its mass central

If $\beta_i>0$, then in every central coalition $y$,
$$ u_i(y)=\beta_i h+\sum_{j\in Z}B_{ij}y_j>0. $$
Thus no $i$-mass can remain in a singleton. This proves the assertion for $F_0$.

Proceed inductively. Suppose every type in the current set $F$ has all its mass central, and let $i\notin F$ satisfy $d_i(F)>0$.

Because $F_0\subseteq F$, we have $\beta_i\leq 0$. Let $A\leq\mu_a$ be the total central $a$-mass, and let $z_j$ denote the total central mass of type $j$. Then
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{y\text{ central}}u_i(y) &=\beta_i A+\sum_{j\in Z}B_{ij}z_j\\ &\geq \beta_i\mu_a+\sum_{j\in F}B_{ij}\mu_j\\ &=d_i(F)>0. \end{aligned} $$
The inequality uses $\beta_i\leq 0$, $A\leq\mu_a$, $B_{ij}\geq 0$, and $z_j=\mu_j$ for $j\in F$.

If any $i$-mass remained in a singleton, stability would require $u_i(y)\leq 0$ for every central destination. The positive sum rules this out. Thus the assertion holds after the insertion.

3.3. Necessity of $D\geq 0$

Every central coalition contains $a$, so individual rationality gives $u_a(y)\geq 0$ in each one.

Every type in the terminal set $F$ has all its mass central. Every type outside $F$ has $\beta_i\leq 0$. Therefore
$$ \begin{aligned} 0 &\leq \sum_{y\text{ central}}u_a(y)\\ &=\alpha\mu_s+\sum_{i\in Z}\beta_i z_i\\ &=\alpha\mu_s+\sum_{i\in F}\beta_i\mu_i +\sum_{i\notin F}\beta_i z_i\\ &\leq D. \end{aligned} $$
Thus $D\geq 0$ is necessary.

3.4. Sufficiency and the algorithm

Suppose $D\geq 0$. Put all masses of $s,a$, and the types in $F$ into one central coalition. Put each remaining type into its own singleton.

The center receives $\alpha\mu_a>0$, and $a$ receives $D\geq 0$.

For each $i\in F$, its central utility is $d_i(F)>0$. For initial members this follows from $\beta_i>0$. For later members it follows from the positive score at insertion, since subsequent insertions cannot decrease scores.

For each $i\notin F$, terminality gives $d_i(F)\leq 0$. Consequently, it does not profitably join the central coalition from its singleton.

Distinct-leaf singleton destinations are disconnected. The center receives zero by joining any excluded singleton, because its center edge has value zero. Singleton formation offers no strict improvement to any present type. Hence the partition is stable.

The closure takes at most $|Z|$ insertions. Maintaining the scores requires polynomially many rational operations, and all scores are sums of input products with polynomial bit length. The construction therefore runs in polynomial time.

4. Existence whenever center–leaf utilities are nonzero

Theorem. If
$$ U(s,v)\neq 0 \qquad\text{for every }v\in V, $$
then a finite rational stable partition exists.

Moreover, one can construct such a partition with one central coalition and singleton coalitions, using at most $\tau$ distinct vectors when repetitions are represented by binary multiplicities.

4.1. Select the central coalition

Let
$$ P=\{v\in V:U(s,v)>0\}. $$
Leave every leaf with $U(s,v)<0$ in a singleton. Such a leaf cannot join a central coalition, and the center cannot join its singleton: destination approval fails in either direction.

If $P$ is empty, the all-singleton partition is stable.

Otherwise, for $S\subseteq P$, define
$$ \Phi(S)= \mu_s\sum_{i\in S}U(s,i)\mu_i + \sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}U(i,j)\mu_i\mu_j, $$
where the second sum is over unordered pairs of distinct elements.

Choose a set $S$ maximizing $\Phi$. It is nonempty, because
$$ \Phi(\{i\})=\mu_sU(s,i)\mu_i>0 $$
for every $i\in P$, whereas $\Phi(\varnothing)=0$.

Let $x$ contain all center mass and all masses of leaves in $S$.

For $i\in S$, maximality gives
$$ 0\leq\Phi(S)-\Phi(S\setminus\{i\})=\mu_i u_i(x), $$
so $u_i(x)\geq 0$.

For $i\in P\setminus S$, maximality gives
$$ 0\geq\Phi(S\cup\{i\})-\Phi(S)=\mu_i u_i(x), $$
so $u_i(x)\leq 0$.

These identities use the zero diagonal of $U$.

4.2. Split excluded positive-edge leaves sufficiently finely

The center’s utility in $x$ is
$$ C=\sum_{i\in S}U(s,i)\mu_i>0. $$
For each $i\in P\setminus S$, set
$$ n_i=\left\lceil\frac{U(s,i)\mu_i}{C}\right\rceil $$
and divide its mass into $n_i$ singleton coalitions, each of mass $\mu_i/n_i$.

The center’s utility from joining any such singleton is
$$ U(s,i)\frac{\mu_i}{n_i}\leq C. $$

Included leaves have nonnegative current utilities. Excluded positive-edge leaves have current utility zero and obtain nonpositive utility by joining $x$. Negative-edge leaves cannot join $x$ because approval fails. Distinct-leaf singleton destinations are disconnected, and same-type singleton destinations give zero. The center weakly prefers $x$ to every admissible singleton destination. Thus no profitable admissible deviation exists.

All coordinates are rational, and every $n_i$ is a finite positive integer.

Enumerating the subsets of $P$ gives a construction in time
$$ 2^{|P|}\operatorname{poly}(m,L) $$
for compressed output. The chosen vector, singleton vectors, and binary multiplicities all have polynomial encoding length. A polynomial-time search algorithm for this subclass is not established here.

In particular, a zero-valued center edge is necessary for nonexistence. The family in Section 2 shows that approaching such an edge from positive values can force the number of coalitions to diverge.
