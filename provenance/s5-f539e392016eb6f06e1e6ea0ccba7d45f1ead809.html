The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite patient-type and doctor-type sets \(P,D\), binary-rational masses \(\mu_i,\nu_j\) each summing to \(1\), and strict rankings of opposite-side types, compute an exact rational flow \(x_{ij}\geq 0\) with row and column sums \(\mu_i,\nu_j\) such that every pair \((p_i,d_j)\) satisfies \(A_{ij}=0\) or \(B_{ij}=0\), where \(A_{ij}=\sum_{j':\,d_j\succ_{p_i}d_{j'}}x_{ij'}\) and \(B_{ij}=\sum_{i':\,p_i\succ_{d_j}p_{i'}}x_{i'j}\).

Let \(P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_a\}\) and \(D=\{d_1,\ldots,d_b\}\) be nonempty patient-type and doctor-type sets. Each type has a strict total ranking of all opposite-side types; all pairs are acceptable.
A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Patient masses \(\mu_i\) and doctor masses \(\nu_j\) each sum to \(1\). Set \(\tau=a+b\), the total support size, not the number of possible types; set \(m=ab\), the number of possible pairs, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An admissible output is an exact rational flow \(x_{ij}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_jx_{ij}=\mu_i\) and \(\sum_ix_{ij}=\nu_j\).
Define \(A_{ij}=\sum_{j':\,d_j\succ_{p_i}d_{j'}}x_{ij'}\) and \(B_{ij}=\sum_{i':\,p_i\succ_{d_j}p_{i'}}x_{i'j}\). Require \(A_{ij}=0\) or \(B_{ij}=0\) for every pair, including pairs with \(x_{ij}>0\).

Is computing such a flow solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For equal finite populations with complete strict preferences, a stable matching is computable in \(O(n^2)\) time per category.
The continuous problem is already covered by the strongly polynomial stable-allocation result of [Baïou and Balinski, corrected version](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302); thus this complexity question is settled, rather than open.

Computing the required exact rational flow is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). The corrected Baïou–Balinski stable-allocation algorithm applies. The two points requiring verification are that its output exhausts all masses and that its stability condition applies even to pairs carrying positive flow.

1. The applicable stable-allocation theorem

For an allocation \(y\), define the amounts assigned to partners ranked at least as highly as the indicated partner:

\(R_{ij}(y)=y_{ij}+\sum_{\ell:\,d_\ell\succ_{p_i}d_j}y_{i\ell}\),

\(C_{ij}(y)=y_{ij}+\sum_{k:\,p_k\succ_{d_j}p_i}y_{kj}\).

The stable-allocation model has positive row capacities \(s_i\), positive column capacities \(t_j\), nonnegative pair capacities \(c_{ij}\), and strict preferences. Feasibility requires

\(\sum_jy_{ij}\leq s_i,\qquad \sum_iy_{ij}\leq t_j,\qquad 0\leq y_{ij}\leq c_{ij}\).

Its stability condition is

\(y_{ij}<c_{ij}\ \Longrightarrow\ R_{ij}(y)=s_i\ \text{or}\ C_{ij}(y)=t_j.\)

Baïou and Balinski’s corrected inductive algorithm computes such an allocation using polynomially many arithmetic operations and comparisons, independently of the numerical magnitudes. Its numerical operations are additions, subtractions, and minima, so integer input yields integer output. See the definition in Section 1 and the algorithm and Theorem 2 in Section 2 of the [corrected paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

2. Reduction and exhaustion of the masses

Construct a stable-allocation instance with the given types and preferences, setting

\(s_i=\mu_i,\qquad t_j=\nu_j,\qquad c_{ij}=2\)

for every pair.

All theorem hypotheses hold: the support masses are positive, the preferences are strict, and the pair capacities are positive. The constructed instance has \(\tau\) agents and \(m\) pairs.

Let \(x\) be the stable allocation returned by the theorem. Feasibility implies

\(x_{ij}\leq\sum_\ell x_{i\ell}\leq\mu_i\leq1<2=c_{ij}\).

Consequently, stability imposes its disjunction at every pair.

We now prove that every marginal constraint is an equality. Write

\(T=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}\leq1\).

Suppose \(T<1\). Since both sides have total capacity \(1\),

\(\sum_i\left(\mu_i-\sum_jx_{ij}\right)=1-T>0\),

\(\sum_j\left(\nu_j-\sum_ix_{ij}\right)=1-T>0\).

Each summand is nonnegative. Thus some row \(i\) and some column \(j\) have strictly positive deficits. At their pair,

\(R_{ij}(x)\leq\sum_\ell x_{i\ell}<\mu_i\),

\(C_{ij}(x)\leq\sum_kx_{kj}<\nu_j\).

Together with \(x_{ij}<c_{ij}\), these inequalities contradict stability. Hence \(T=1\).

The nonnegative row deficits now sum to zero, as do the column deficits. Therefore each deficit is zero:

\(\sum_jx_{ij}=\mu_i,\qquad \sum_ix_{ij}=\nu_j\).

This proves the required marginal equalities.

3. Exact equivalence of the stability conditions

Because each ranking is strict and total, the opposite-side types partition into those better than the indicated type, that type itself, and those worse. Using the marginal equalities established above gives

\(\mu_i=R_{ij}(x)+A_{ij},\qquad \nu_j=C_{ij}(x)+B_{ij}\).

It follows that

\(R_{ij}(x)=\mu_i\ \Longleftrightarrow\ A_{ij}=0\),

\(C_{ij}(x)=\nu_j\ \Longleftrightarrow\ B_{ij}=0\).

Since \(x_{ij}<c_{ij}\) for every pair, stable-allocation stability is therefore exactly

\(A_{ij}=0\ \text{or}\ B_{ij}=0\qquad\text{for every }(i,j)\).

There is no exception when \(x_{ij}>0\): the chosen pair capacity remains strictly larger than every feasible entry.

Conversely, any flow satisfying the task’s marginal equalities and disjunctions is feasible and stable for the constructed instance, by the same identities. Thus the two solution sets coincide.

4. Exact rational computation and bit complexity

To make exact computation explicit, write the input masses as fractions with positive integer denominators. Let \(Q\) be the product of all these denominators. Their total encoding length is at most \(L\), so

\(\log_2Q=O(L)\).

Compute the scaled instance with integer capacities

\(s_i'=Q\mu_i,\qquad t_j'=Q\nu_j,\qquad c_{ij}'=2Q\).

These integers have \(O(L)\) bits, and their construction takes polynomial time. Run the inductive algorithm on this instance and obtain an integer allocation \(z\). Output

\(x_{ij}=z_{ij}/Q\).

Dividing feasibility and stability relations by the positive number \(Q\) gives the original stable-allocation instance. Sections 2 and 3 consequently prove that this output meets every requirement.

For completeness, let \(K\) be the algorithm’s number of numerical operations, polynomial in \(m,\tau\). Initially, every numerical magnitude is at most \(2Q\). An addition or subtraction increases the largest absolute magnitude by at most a factor of two; comparisons and minima do not increase it. Hence intermediate integers have at most \(O(L+K)\) bits. Each operation therefore takes polynomial bit time, and so does the whole algorithm.

Finally, feasibility gives \(0\leq z_{ij}\leq Q\), so the output fractions have \(O(L)\) bits per entry and total encoding length \(O(mL)\). No expansion into individual patients or doctors is needed.
