The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) of valuation types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), integral bundle domains \(X_t\), and feasible rational per-capita supply \(q\), use a weighted aggregate-demand oracle returning \(d(p)\in\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\operatorname{conv}\{x:x\in D_t(p)\}\), where \(D_t(p)=\arg\max_{x\in X_t}(v_t(x)-p\cdot x)\), to find an exact rational price \(p\) whenever there exists a type-level allocation \(\lambda_{t,x}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{x\in X_t}\lambda_{t,x}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,x}\lambda_{t,x}x=q\), and positive mass assigned only to demanded bundles.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of the \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) goods and a binary-encoded capacity vector \(s\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\). Each type has bundle domain \(X_t=\prod_{j=1}^m\{0,\ldots,s_j\}\) and an otherwise unrestricted valuation \(v_t:X_t\to\mathbb Z\), with \(v_t(0)=0\) and \(|v_t(x)|\le M\) for a supplied integer \(M\ge1\); negative values are allowed.
The supplied per-capita supply is \(q\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^m\), with \(q\in\sum_t\mu_t\operatorname{conv}(X_t)\). Prices may have either sign, and \(D_t(p)=\arg\max_{x\in X_t}\bigl(v_t(x)-p\cdot x\bigr)\).
Valuations are accessible only through a weighted aggregate-demand oracle: at any rational query \(p\), it returns an arbitrary binary-rational vector \(d(p)\in\sum_t\mu_t\operatorname{conv}(D_t(p))\). All admissible tie choices are allowed; each reply has at most a supplied bound \(B\) bits and costs \(T_{\mathrm{AD}}\) time.
An equilibrium price supports masses \(\lambda_{t,x}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{x\in X_t}\lambda_{t,x}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,x}\lambda_{t,x}x=q\), and \(\lambda_{t,x}>0\Rightarrow x\in D_t(p)\). Assume such a price exists; the required output is only an exact rational equilibrium price.
Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the supplied support and numerical input, excluding oracle replies. State running times in \(m,\tau,L,B,T_{\mathrm{AD}}\).

Is this promised price-finding problem solvable in polynomial time in this oracle model, allowing randomization, or is it NP-hard?

For finite buyers with integer valuations and integer supply, exact Walrasian prices can be computed whenever an equilibrium exists in \(O(m^2T_{\mathrm{AD}}\log(SMm)+m^5\log^{O(1)}(SMm))\) time, where \(S=\max\{1,s_1,\ldots,s_m\}\); the general-valuation algorithm is randomized. [Published result](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-018-1334-9).
No result settling exactly the normalized mass problem with arbitrary convexified aggregate-demand replies was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to total mass one, matching a distribution over types and per-capita supply.
  • Specified the undefined integral bundle domains as the common nonnegative integer box used in the discrete model, encoded by an independent capacity vector s; rational per-capita supply q need not equal that vector.
  • Retained integer-valued, possibly negative valuations normalized at the zero bundle, and supplied a binary-encoded magnitude bound M.
  • Allowed prices of either sign, exact market clearing, and arbitrary demand ties, as in the discrete definition.
  • Kept valuation information hidden behind the aggregate-demand oracle rather than supplying valuation tables or individual value or demand oracles.
  • Required exact binary-rational oracle replies and introduced their bit-length bound B to make the computational model finite.
  • Treated equilibrium existence as a promise and required only prices, preserving the anchor's search problem.
  • Allowed randomized polynomial-time algorithms because the published version explicitly identifies the anchor's algorithm as randomized.

Result

The problem is deterministically polynomial-time solvable in the stated oracle model. A nonoptimized running-time bound is

\( (m+\tau+L+B)^{O(1)}(T_{\mathrm{AD}}+1). \)

The algorithm uses polynomially many oracle calls, all at rational prices of polynomial bit length, and works for every admissible sequence of convexified tie choices.

The key is to construct an exact separation oracle for the equilibrium price set. This requires two applications of the standard equivalence between optimization and separation for rational polytopes.

1. Equilibrium prices minimize a convex potential

Put \(S=\max\{1,s_1,\ldots,s_m\}\), and define

\( U_t(p)=\max_{x\in X_t}\bigl(v_t(x)-p\cdot x\bigr), \qquad F(p)=q\cdot p+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tU_t(p). \)

Also define the aggregate demand polytope

\( K(p)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\operatorname{conv}(D_t(p)). \)

A price \(p\) is an equilibrium price precisely when \(q\in K(p)\). Indeed, writing each component of this Minkowski sum as a convex combination gives the required masses \(\lambda_{t,x}\), and conversely any equilibrium masses give such a representation.

The subdifferential of \(F\) is

\( \partial F(p)=q-K(p). \)

To justify this formula, write \(F\) as the maximum of the finitely many affine functions

\( p\longmapsto \sum_t\mu_tv_t(x_t) + \left(q-\sum_t\mu_tx_t\right)\cdot p, \qquad x_t\in X_t. \)

Because every \(\mu_t\) is positive, a tuple is active at \(p\) exactly when \(x_t\in D_t(p)\) for every type. The subdifferential of a finite maximum of affine functions is the convex hull of the active gradients. Their convex hull is exactly \(q-K(p)\).

Consequently,

\( p\text{ is an equilibrium price} \iff 0\in\partial F(p) \iff F(p)=\min_{r\in\mathbb R^m}F(r). \)

Moreover, every oracle reply supplies the valid subgradient

\( g(p)=q-d(p)\in\partial F(p). \)

2. A bounded price box suffices

Let \(R=2M\) and \(C=[-R,R]^m\). Clipping any price coordinate into this interval cannot increase \(F\).

For the upper bound, if \(p_j>2M\) and \(x_j>0\), then

\( [v_t(x-e_j)-p\cdot(x-e_j)] - [v_t(x)-p\cdot x] = v_t(x-e_j)-v_t(x)+p_j >0. \)

Thus every demanded bundle has \(x_j=0\). Holding the other prices fixed, each \(U_t\) is constant throughout \(p_j>2M\), so \(F\) has slope \(q_j\ge0\) there. Decreasing \(p_j\) to \(2M\) cannot increase \(F\).

Similarly, if \(p_j<-2M\) and \(x_j<s_j\), adding \(e_j\) strictly improves utility. Therefore every demanded bundle has \(x_j=s_j\), and \(F\) has slope \(q_j-s_j\le0\) throughout that interval. Here we used normalization and feasibility, which imply \(0\le q_j\le s_j\).

Continuity handles the interval endpoints. Applying these operations coordinate by coordinate proves the clipping claim.

Hence the continuous function \(F\) attains a global minimum in \(C\). In particular, with the convexified allocation definition, the existence promise already follows from supply feasibility.

Define

\( P=C\cap\operatorname{argmin}F. \)

This is the nonempty, bounded set for which we will construct a separation oracle.

3. Rational-polytope tools and encoding bounds

We use the following established results.

For a rational polytope, a bound \(\nu\) on the encoding length of its vertices gives a facet-complexity bound \(3m^2\nu\). Given such a bound, strong optimization and strong separation are equivalent in oracle-polynomial time. These results apply without a full-dimensionality assumption. Strong optimization may return any rational optimal point. See Lemma 6.2.4 and Theorem 6.4.9 of [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1988.pdf).

Here “oracle-polynomial” includes polynomial bounds on query encodings and arithmetic complexity. We verify the necessary polytope encoding bounds next.

Aggregate demand polytopes

Let \(H\) be the product of the denominators of the supplied masses. Then \(H\mu_t\) is integral for every type and \(\log H=O(L)\).

The polytope \(K(p)\) is the convex hull of the vectors

\( \sum_t\mu_tx_t, \qquad x_t\in D_t(p). \)

Every such vector lies in \([0,s]\), and multiplying it by \(H\) gives an integer vector. Thus every vertex has coordinate denominators dividing \(H\), with numerators bounded by \(HS\).

A known vertex-encoding bound is therefore

\( \nu_K=O\bigl(m(1+\log H+\log S)\bigr). \)

The corresponding facet-complexity bound is polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\), independently of the number of demanded bundles.

The bounded equilibrium price set

For each tuple \(\mathbf{x}=(x_t)_t\), consider the possibly empty cell

\( C_{\mathbf{x}} = \{p\in C:x_t\in D_t(p)\text{ for every }t\}. \)

Its defining inequalities, besides the box constraints, are

\( p\cdot(x_t-y)\le v_t(x_t)-v_t(y), \qquad y\in X_t. \)

These inequalities have integer coefficients. Every coefficient in their price-variable matrix has absolute value at most \(S\).

At a vertex, choose \(m\) linearly independent active constraints. Cramer’s rule gives a common coordinate denominator at most

\( D=m!S^m\le(mS)^m. \)

The coordinates also have absolute value at most \(R\).

On each cell, \(F\) is affine. If the cell meets \(P\), its intersection with \(P\) is a face of that cell. These finitely many faces cover \(P\). Their union is convex, because it is the minimizer set intersected with \(C\), so \(P\) is a rational polytope.

Every vertex of \(P\) is a vertex of a cell: a vertex of \(P\) remains a vertex of any intersecting face containing it, and vertices of a face are vertices of the original cell. Hence a vertex-encoding bound for \(P\) is

\( \nu_P=O\bigl(m(1+\log R+\log D)\bigr). \)

Again, its facet complexity is polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\). No cells or valuation inequalities need to be enumerated.

4. Price perturbations give exact optimization over \(K(p)\)

Fix a rational price \(p\), and let \(N\) be a positive integer such that \(Np\) is integral.

Given any rational direction \(a\), set

\( \delta= \frac{1}{2N(1+mS\|a\|_\infty)} \)

and query the aggregate-demand oracle at \(p-\delta a\).

We claim that its reply is an exact maximizer of \(a\cdot z\) over \(z\in K(p)\).

For any type, utility differences at \(p\) are integer multiples of \(1/N\). Consequently, if \(x\notin D_t(p)\) and \(y\in D_t(p)\), then

\( [v_t(y)-p\cdot y]-[v_t(x)-p\cdot x]\ge\frac1N. \)

The perturbation changes this difference by at most

\( \delta|a\cdot(y-x)| \le \delta mS\|a\|_\infty < \frac{1}{2N}. \)

Thus no bundle outside \(D_t(p)\) becomes demanded at the perturbed price.

Among bundles already in \(D_t(p)\), utilities at the perturbed price differ only through the term \(\delta a\cdot x\). Therefore

\( D_t(p-\delta a) = \operatorname{argmax}_{x\in D_t(p)}a\cdot x. \)

It follows that every admissible convexified aggregate reply belongs to \(K(p)\) and maximizes \(a\cdot z\) over that polytope. Arbitrary tie choices cause no difficulty: all points in the exposed face have the same objective value.

This constructs a strong optimization oracle for \(K(p)\) using one aggregate-demand call per optimization query. The perturbed price has bit length polynomial in the encodings of \(p,a\), and the supplied input.

By the rational-polytope equivalence and the bound \(\nu_K\), we can therefore decide exactly whether \(q\in K(p)\) in polynomial time.

5. Exact separation of the equilibrium price set

We now construct a strong separation oracle for \(P\), at a rational query \(p\).

If \(p\notin C\), a violated box inequality separates it from \(P\).

Otherwise, use Section 4 to decide whether \(q\in K(p)\). If so, \(p\in P\), and the oracle reports membership.

If \(q\notin K(p)\), query aggregate demand at \(p\) and put \(g=q-d(p)\). Since \(p\) is not a minimizer, for every \(z\in P\) the subgradient inequality gives

\( g\cdot(z-p) \le F(z)-F(p) = \min F-F(p) <0. \)

Thus

\( g\cdot p>\max_{z\in P}g\cdot z, \)

which is a strong separator.

An explicit separating inequality is also available without knowing any function values. Let \(N_p,N_g\) be common coordinate denominators for \(p,g\). Every vertex \(z\) of \(P\) has a common denominator at most \(D\). Hence the strictly positive rational number \(g\cdot p-g\cdot z\) is at least \(1/(N_gN_pD)\). Since a linear functional attains its maximum over a polytope at a vertex, the inequality

\( g\cdot z \le g\cdot p-\frac{1}{2N_gN_pD} \)

contains \(P\) and excludes \(p\). Its encoding length is polynomial in the query length and \(m,L,B\).

This proves that \(P\) has a polynomial-time exact separation oracle.

6. Finding a price and accounting for time

Apply the rational-polytope separation-to-optimization algorithm to \(P\) with the zero objective. Since \(P\) is nonempty and bounded, it returns an exact rational point \(p^\star\in P\). Section 1 proves that this is an equilibrium price.

The algorithm consists of two nested oracle-polynomial reductions:

  • The outer reduction finds a point of \(P\).
  • Each outer separation query tests \(q\in K(p)\) using the inner reduction.
  • Each inner optimization query uses one aggregate-demand call at the explicitly constructed rational perturbation.

Both polytope encoding bounds are polynomial in \(m,L\). Oracle replies have at most \(B\) bits, and all additional arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity. Composing these fixed polynomial-time reductions therefore gives

\( T_{\mathrm{price}} = (m+\tau+L+B)^{O(1)}(T_{\mathrm{AD}}+1). \)

The number of oracle calls and every query and output encoding length are polynomial in the same parameters. The algorithm is deterministic and never expands rational masses into individual buyers.

This establishes polynomial-time solvability for the precise normalized-mass model in the question.
