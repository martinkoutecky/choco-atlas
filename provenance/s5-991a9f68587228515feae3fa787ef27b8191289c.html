The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete firm types \(T\), a rational mass distribution \(\mu\), and each type’s demand vector and setup, holding, backlogging, and production costs, define \(c(\nu)\) for every subpopulation \(0 \leq \nu \leq \mu\) as its pooled setup-inventory minimum cost, using aggregate demand \(\sum_{t \in T}\nu_t d_t\), the cheapest available technology, binary setup decisions, and real-valued aggregate flows. Compute per-firm type cost shares \(x_t\) such that \(\sum_{t \in T}\mu_t x_t = c(\mu)\) and \(\sum_{t \in T}\nu_t x_t \leq c(\nu)\) for every subpopulation \(\nu\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|R|\ge1\) outlet types with positive masses \(w_r=M\mu_r\), each a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types. Here \(M>0\), \(\mu_r>0\), and \(\sum_r\mu_r=1\). There are \(m\ge1\) periods. Each type has demands \(d^r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\) and nonnegative binary-rational setup, production, inventory, and backlogging cost vectors \(k^r,p^r,h^r,b^r\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A coalition is any real vector \(u\in\prod_{r\in R}[0,w_r]\). Its demand is \(D_t(u)=\sum_r u_rd_t^r\). For \(u\ne0\), define \(v_t(u)=\min_{r:u_r>0}v_t^r\) separately for \(v\in\{k,p,h,b\}\); any positive participation makes that type's costs available.
Set \(c(0)=0\). For \(u\ne0\), \(c(u)\) minimizes \(\sum_{t=1}^m[p_t(u)q_t+h_t(u)I_t+b_t(u)E_t+k_t(u)z_t]\), subject to \(I_0=I_m=E_0=E_m=0\), \(I_t-E_t=I_{t-1}-E_{t-1}+q_t-D_t(u)\), and \(q_t\le(\sum_{s=1}^mD_s(u))z_t\), with \(q_t,I_t,E_t\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) and \(z_t\in\{0,1\}\). Setup costs are paid per active production period, without scaling by coalition mass.
Find per-unit-mass charges \(a_r\), unrestricted in sign, satisfying \(\sum_rw_ra_r=c(w)\) and \(\sum_ru_ra_r\le c(u)\) for every coalition \(u\), or determine that none exist.

Is computing such charges exactly, or determining that none exist, polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this problem NP-hard?

For finite societies with integral outlet demands and production quantities, setup-inventory games are totally balanced: every subgame has a nonempty core ([verified result](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01901v1)).
An earlier result also establishes total balancedness under heterogeneous costs and backlogging ([verified result](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11750-021-00614-0)). No result settling this exact mass-coalition computational problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required a nonempty support, positive rational total mass, and positive rational proportions summing to one; represented the society by its positive type masses.
  • Allowed zero demands and zero costs; demands are nonnegative integers and all costs are nonnegative binary rationals.
  • Specified zero initial and terminal inventory and backlog, flow conservation, and a coalition-specific setup bound equal to total coalition demand.
  • Defined the empty coalition's cost as zero and retained fixed setup charges without mass scaling.
  • Quantified over real coalition masses and allowed charges of either sign, as in the discrete core definition.
  • Used m for the number of periods and L for total input bit length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Moreover, suitable charges exist for every input satisfying the stated hypotheses.

The algorithm solves one rational linear program with \(O(m^2)\) variables and constraints. Its optimal dual solution yields charges valid for every real coalition, including coalitions with arbitrarily small positive participation.

1. Demand and transportation costs

Write \(D_t^*=\sum_{r\in R}w_rd_t^r\) for grand-coalition demand. Write \(K_i,P_i,H_i,B_i\) for the componentwise minima of setup, production, inventory, and backlogging costs over all supported types.

If \(D_t^*=0\) for every period, positivity of all \(w_r\) and nonnegativity of all \(d_t^r\) imply that every type has zero demand. Then every coalition has zero cost, and \(a_r=0\) solves the problem. Henceforth assume that \(T_+=\{t:D_t^*>0\}\) is nonempty.

For any nonnegative production, inventory, and backlogging cost vectors \(p,h,b\), define the unit cost \(A_{it}\) of producing in period \(i\) to satisfy demand in period \(t\):

  • \(A_{it}=p_i+\sum_{\ell=i}^{t-1}h_\ell\) when \(i<t\).
  • \(A_{ii}=p_i\).
  • \(A_{it}=p_i+\sum_{\ell=t}^{i-1}b_\ell\) when \(i>t\).

Let \(A_{it}^*\) denote these costs calculated from \(P,H,B\). For a nonzero coalition \(u\), let \(A_{it}(u)\) denote the corresponding costs calculated from its available technologies.

Because a coalition uses a subset of the supported types, and because \(0\le u_r\le w_r\), we have

\(\displaystyle D_t(u)\le D_t^*,\qquad k_i(u)\ge K_i,\qquad A_{it}(u)\ge A_{it}^*.\)

These three inequalities will establish every coalition constraint.

2. An exact assignment reformulation

Fix an arbitrary nonnegative demand vector \(D\), with real entries, and nonnegative costs \(k,p,h,b\). Let \(x_{it}\) be the amount produced in period \(i\) for demand in period \(t\). Consider the formulation with objective

\(\displaystyle \min\ \sum_{i,t}A_{it}x_{it}+\sum_i k_i z_i,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_i x_{it}=D_t,\qquad 0\le x_{it}\le D_tz_i,\qquad z_i\in\{0,1\}.\)

Claim. This formulation has the same optimal value as the operational model in the question.

Proof. Given a feasible assignment, define

\(\displaystyle q_i=\sum_t x_{it},\qquad I_\ell=\sum_{i\le\ell<t}x_{it},\qquad E_\ell=\sum_{t\le\ell<i}x_{it}.\)

These quantities satisfy the required balances and boundary conditions. Furthermore,

\(\displaystyle q_i=\sum_t x_{it}\le\left(\sum_tD_t\right)z_i.\)

Their variable cost is exactly \(\sum_{i,t}A_{it}x_{it}\), by the definition of \(A_{it}\).

Conversely, interpret an operational solution as a flow network. Production \(q_i\) enters at node \(i\), demand \(D_t\) leaves at node \(t\), inventory \(I_\ell\) flows from \(\ell\) to \(\ell+1\), and backlogging \(E_\ell\) flows from \(\ell+1\) to \(\ell\). The operational balance equation is precisely flow conservation in this network.

Decompose the flow into production-to-demand paths and cycles. Discarding cycles cannot increase cost because all arc costs are nonnegative. A simple path between two nodes of the period line follows their unique connecting route, so its unit cost is \(A_{it}\). Let \(x_{it}\) be the total flow on paths from production node \(i\) to demand node \(t\).

Every demand is satisfied, giving \(\sum_i x_{it}=D_t\). If \(z_i=0\), then \(q_i=0\), hence \(x_{it}=0\). If \(z_i=1\), nonnegativity and the demand equality give \(x_{it}\le D_t\). Thus \(x_{it}\le D_tz_i\), and the assignment cost is at most the original operational cost.

The two constructions prove equality of optimal values. This argument applies to arbitrary real demands. \(\square\)

3. Why the grand-coalition LP is exact

For grand-coalition demand, consider the linear program

\(\displaystyle \min\ \sum_{i=1}^m\sum_{t\in T_+}D_t^*A_{it}^*\lambda_{it}+\sum_{i=1}^mK_i z_i,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_{it}=1\quad(t\in T_+),\)

\(\displaystyle 0\le\lambda_{it}\le z_i,\qquad z_i\ge0.\)

Here \(x_{it}=D_t^*\lambda_{it}\). Explicit upper bounds \(z_i\le1\) are unnecessary: the assignment equalities imply \(\lambda_{it}\le1\), so replacing \(z_i\) by \(\min\{z_i,1\}\) preserves feasibility and cannot increase cost.

We use the following established result: for uncapacitated lot sizing on a period line, with backlogging, integral nonnegative demands, and nonnegative setup, production, inventory, and backlogging costs, the facility-location assignment LP has an integral optimal solution. This is the strong reformulation of Pochet and Wolsey, stated in precisely this assignment form in Section 2 of the cited paper. [Pochet–Wolsey](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01580738), [assignment formulation and integrality statement](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01901v1#S2).

Its applicability to rational grand-coalition demand follows by scaling, rather than by assuming an extension to real demands.

Choose a positive integer \(Q\) such that every \(QD_t^*\) is integral. In the scaled instance, use demand \(QD_t^*\), keep setup costs \(K_i\), and replace variable costs by \(P_i/Q,H_i/Q,B_i/Q\). All costs remain nonnegative, and the demand is integral, so the theorem applies.

The scaled unit transportation costs are \(A_{it}^*/Q\). Consequently, its assignment LP has exactly the same objective coefficients:

\(\displaystyle (QD_t^*)(A_{it}^*/Q)=D_t^*A_{it}^*.\)

Its feasible region in \(\lambda,z\) is also identical. Therefore the displayed grand-coalition LP has an integral optimal solution. By Section 2, its optimum is exactly \(c(w)\).

4. Dual prices and the charges

A dual of the grand-coalition LP is

\(\displaystyle \max\ \sum_{t\in T_+}D_t^*y_t,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_+}D_t^*\beta_{it}\le K_i\quad(i=1,\ldots,m),\)

\(\displaystyle y_t-\beta_{it}\le A_{it}^*\quad(i=1,\ldots,m,\ t\in T_+),\)

with \(\beta_{it}\ge0\) and \(y_t\) unrestricted.

For completeness, take \(D_t^*y_t\) as the multiplier of the assignment equality and \(D_t^*\beta_{it}\) as the nonnegative multiplier of \(\lambda_{it}\le z_i\). The resulting Lagrangian coefficients are

\(\displaystyle D_t^*(A_{it}^*-y_t+\beta_{it})\)

for \(\lambda_{it}\), and

\(\displaystyle K_i-\sum_{t\in T_+}D_t^*\beta_{it}\)

for \(z_i\). Their nonnegativity gives exactly the displayed dual constraints.

The primal is feasible and has finite optimum. Thus strong LP duality supplies an optimal solution \((y,\beta)\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_+}D_t^*y_t=c(w).\)

Define the per-unit-mass charge of type \(r\) by

\(\displaystyle a_r=\sum_{t\in T_+}d_t^r y_t.\)

Budget balance follows immediately:

\(\displaystyle \sum_r w_ra_r=\sum_{t\in T_+}\left(\sum_r w_rd_t^r\right)y_t=\sum_{t\in T_+}D_t^*y_t=c(w).\)

5. Verification for every real coalition

Fix any nonzero coalition \(u\), and put \(D_t=D_t(u)\). There is no demand outside \(T_+\), since \(D_t\le D_t^*\).

Take any feasible assignment \(x,z\) for this coalition. Dual feasibility and cost monotonicity give

\(\displaystyle y_t\le A_{it}^*+\beta_{it}\le A_{it}(u)+\beta_{it}.\)

Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_+}D_ty_t =\sum_i\sum_{t\in T_+}x_{it}y_t \le\sum_i\sum_{t\in T_+}A_{it}(u)x_{it} +\sum_i\sum_{t\in T_+}\beta_{it}x_{it}.\)

For each production period \(i\), the assignment bound and nonnegativity of \(\beta\) imply

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T_+}\beta_{it}x_{it} \le z_i\sum_{t\in T_+}D_t\beta_{it} \le z_i\sum_{t\in T_+}D_t^*\beta_{it} \le z_iK_i \le z_i k_i(u).\)

Combining these inequalities shows that \(\sum_tD_ty_t\) is at most the cost of every feasible coalition assignment. Taking the minimum and using Section 2 yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_r u_ra_r=\sum_{t\in T_+}D_t(u)y_t\le c(u).\)

This argument also covers a nonzero coalition with zero demand: both its charge and its minimum cost are zero. For \(u=0\), the constraint follows from \(c(0)=0\).

Thus all real coalition constraints hold. No rationality assumption on coalition participation was used.

6. Exact polynomial-time computation

The algorithm is:

  • Compute \(D^*,K,P,H,B\) and the transportation costs \(A^*\).
  • Return zero charges if \(T_+\) is empty.
  • Otherwise solve the displayed rational dual LP exactly.
  • Return \(a_r=\sum_{t\in T_+}d_t^r y_t\).

There are \(m|T_+|+|T_+|=O(m^2)\) dual variables and \(m+m|T_+|=O(m^2)\) constraints, apart from sign restrictions. Every coefficient has bit length polynomial in the original input length: forming demands and transportation costs requires only products and sums of input rationals and integers.

The integer \(Q\) used in the proof can be chosen as the product of the denominators of the input masses. Its bit length is polynomial in \(L\); its numerical magnitude causes no expansion of the LP.

Standard exact rational linear programming therefore computes an optimal dual solution, and then all charges, in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). The output rationals have polynomial bit length.

Every promised instance is feasible, so the algorithm always returns charges.
