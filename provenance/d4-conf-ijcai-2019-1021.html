The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 3.3 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two complete additive types \(a,b\) with \(\mu_a=\mu_b=1/2\), a finite set \(G\) of indivisible good slots with \(q_g=1/2\) for every \(g\in G\), and mass variables \(y_{t,B}\ge 0\) for integral bundles \(B\subseteq G\), compute \(\mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)=\max_y \sum_{t,B}y_{t,B}v_t(B)\) subject to \(\sum_B y_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}y_{t,B}=q_g\), and population-EF1 for every pair of positive-support bundles; compare this optimum with the unrestricted welfare optimum and determine the worst-case ratio.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

An instance has \(T=\{a,b\}\), masses \(\mu_a=\mu_b=1/2\), and a finite set \(G\) of good slots, each with supply \(q_g=1/2\). Each type has a nonnegative additive rational valuation \(v_t\), normalized by \(v_t(G)=1\).
A population allocation is \(y_{t,B}\ge0\), for \(t\in T\) and \(B\subseteq G\), with \(\sum_B y_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}y_{t,B}=q_g\). Its welfare is \(\sum_{t,B}y_{t,B}v_t(B)\).
It is population-EF1 if, for all \(t,t'\in T\) and all \(B,C\) with \(y_{t,B},y_{t',C}>0\), some \(D\subseteq C\) with \(|D|\le1\) satisfies \(v_t(B)\ge v_t(C\setminus D)\). Let \(\mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)\) maximize welfare subject to these conditions, and let \(\mathrm{OPT}^{\infty}(I)\) be the same maximum without population-EF1.

Target

Is computing \(\mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)\) in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?
What is \(\sup_I \mathrm{OPT}^{\infty}(I)/\mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)\)?

Nearest known results

For two discrete agents with normalized additive valuations, a polynomial-time algorithm finds an EF1 allocation of welfare at least \(\sqrt{3}/2\) of optimum; the discrete EF1 price lies between \(8/7\) and \(2/\sqrt{3}\).
For arbitrary numbers of discrete agents, the EF1 price is \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\), with the upper bound obtained algorithmically.
For divisible goods, the price of envy-freeness is \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\); this does not impose supportwise EF1 over integral bundles.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

Results proved. Exact computation of \(\mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)\) is NP-hard. Its rational-threshold decision version is NP-complete. For the welfare ratio, I prove
\( \frac87\le \sup_I\frac{\mathrm{OPT}^{\infty}(I)}{\mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)} \le\frac2{\sqrt3}. \)
The exact supremum remains unresolved here. The upper bound uses the discrete approximation guarantee supplied in the question.

1. Marginals and a necessary EF1 inequality.

Write \(v_t(g)=v_t(\{g\})\). Given a feasible population allocation, define
\( x_g=2\sum_{B:g\in B}y_{a,B}. \)
Thus \(x_g\in[0,1]\) is the conditional probability that a type-\(a\) bundle contains \(g\). The supply constraints imply that the corresponding probability for type \(b\) is \(1-x_g\).

By additivity and normalization, its welfare \(W\) satisfies
\( 2W=\sum_gv_a(g)x_g+\sum_gv_b(g)(1-x_g) =1+\sum_g\bigl(v_a(g)-v_b(g)\bigr)x_g. \)

Consequently,
\( \mathrm{OPT}^{\infty}(I)=\frac12\sum_g\max\{v_a(g),v_b(g)\}. \)
Indeed, the marginal expression gives the upper bound, and assigning each good entirely to a type valuing it most attains it.

Let \(M_b=\max_gv_b(g)\). Population-EF1 implies, for every supported type-\(b\) bundle \(B\) and type-\(a\) bundle \(C\),
\( v_b(B)\ge v_b(C)-M_b. \)
Average this inequality over the conditional distributions of both bundles. Their expected values under \(v_b\) are respectively \(1-\sum_gv_b(g)x_g\) and \(\sum_gv_b(g)x_g\). Hence every population-EF1 allocation satisfies
\( \sum_gv_b(g)x_g\le\frac{1+M_b}{2}. \tag{1} \)

2. NP-hardness: construction from PARTITION.

Use the standard NP-complete problem PARTITION: given positive binary-encoded integers \(d_1,\ldots,d_n\), with \(D=\sum_i d_i\), decide whether some subset sums to \(D/2\).

Create goods \(A,P,h,g_1,\ldots,g_n\). Call \(h,g_1,\ldots,g_n\) the small goods. Define valuations by

| Good | Type \(a\) | Type \(b\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(A\) | \(19/30\) | \(1/2\) |
| \(P\) | \(0\) | \(1/6\) |
| \(h\) | \(11/60\) | \(1/6\) |
| \(g_i\) | \(11d_i/(60D)\) | \(d_i/(6D)\) |

The small goods have total value \(1/3\) for type \(b\), and type \(a\) values each of them at \(11/10\) times its type-\(b\) value. Therefore both valuations are nonnegative and normalized:
\( v_b(G)=\frac12+\frac16+\frac13=1, \qquad v_a(G)=\frac{19}{30}+\frac{11}{30}=1. \)
All numbers have polynomial encoding length. Give each good supply \(1/2\), as required.

I claim that
\( \mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)\ge\frac{139}{240} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the PARTITION instance is a yes-instance}. \tag{2} \)

3. Universal upper bound for the constructed instance.

For any population-EF1 allocation, put
\( z=x_A,\qquad r=x_P,\qquad s=\sum_{g\text{ small}}v_b(g)x_g. \)
Here \(M_b=1/2\). Inequality (1) gives
\( \frac12z+\frac16r+s\le\frac34. \tag{3} \)

The welfare identity becomes
\( 2W=1+\frac{2}{15}z-\frac16r+\frac1{10}s. \)
Substituting the bound on \(s\) from (3),
\( 2W \le 1+\frac3{40}+\frac1{12}z-\frac{11}{60}r \le 1+\frac3{40}+\frac1{12} =\frac{139}{120}. \)
Thus \(W\le139/240\).

Moreover, equality forces
\( z=1,\qquad r=0,\qquad s=\frac14. \tag{4} \)
The first two conclusions follow because the coefficient of \(z\) is strictly positive and that of \(r\) strictly negative. Equality in the preceding substitution then gives the value of \(s\).

4. Equality forces an actual partition.

Suppose a population-EF1 allocation attains \(139/240\). By (4), every supported type-\(a\) bundle contains \(A\) and excludes \(P\); every supported type-\(b\) bundle excludes \(A\) and contains \(P\).

For a supported type-\(a\) bundle \(C\), write
\( s(C)=\sum_{\substack{g\in C\\g\text{ small}}}v_b(g). \)
The good \(A\) has type-\(b\) value \(1/2\), while every small good has value at most \(1/6\). Therefore the minimum value remaining after deleting at most one good from \(C\) is exactly \(s(C)\).

Population-EF1 consequently requires
\( v_b(B)\ge s(C) \)
for every supported type-\(b\) bundle \(B\).

The conditional expected type-\(b\) value of a type-\(b\) bundle is, by (4),
\( 1-\left(\frac12z+\frac16r+s\right)=\frac14. \)
Averaging the preceding EF1 inequality over \(B\) shows that \(s(C)\le1/4\) for every supported \(C\). But the conditional average of \(s(C)\) is \(s=1/4\). Since every supported bundle has positive probability, every such bundle must satisfy \(s(C)=1/4\).

Choose one. The original goods \(g_i\) together have type-\(b\) value only \(1/6\), so its small-good subset must contain \(h\). Its remaining selected goods therefore satisfy
\( \sum_{i:g_i\in C}\frac{d_i}{6D} =\frac14-\frac16 =\frac1{12}. \)
Multiplying by \(6D\) yields
\( \sum_{i:g_i\in C}d_i=\frac D2. \)
Thus the original instance has a partition. This argument explicitly rules out attaining the threshold merely by mixing different subsets.

Conversely, suppose \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in J}d_i=D/2\). Assign the bundle
\( C=\{A,h\}\cup\{g_i:i\in J\} \)
to all of type \(a\), and its complement \(B=G\setminus C\) to all of type \(b\).

The small goods in \(C\) have type-\(b\) value \(1/4\), so
\( v_b(B)=\frac14=v_b(C\setminus\{A\}). \)
Thus type \(b\)'s EF1 condition holds. Type \(a\) does not envy \(B\): its own bundle contains \(A\), worth \(19/30\), whereas all goods outside \(A\) together are worth only \(11/30\). Comparisons within either type are comparisons of a bundle with itself and automatically satisfy EF1.

Finally,
\( W =\frac12\left(\frac{19}{30}+\frac{11}{10}\cdot\frac14+\frac14\right) =\frac{139}{240}. \)
This proves (2), and therefore NP-hardness.

5. Polynomial-size certificates.

Consider the decision problem with an additional rational threshold \(K\), asking whether \(\mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)\ge K\).

Let \(m=|G|\), and fix the set \(S\) of typed bundles appearing in any feasible population-EF1 allocation. Allow their weights to vary, subject to the original mass and supply equations, while keeping all other weights zero. This is a bounded linear-programming polytope with \(m+2\) equality constraints.

Every point of this polytope remains population-EF1: its positive support is a subset of the pairwise-compatible set \(S\). Maximizing welfare over this polytope therefore gives a vertex of at least the original welfare.

Such a vertex has at most \(m+2\) positive coordinates. To see this, if its positive columns were linearly dependent, a nonzero kernel direction supported on those coordinates could be added and subtracted by a sufficiently small amount, contradicting extremality.

These columns have entries in \(\{0,1\}\), and all right-hand sides equal \(1/2\). Select a nonsingular square subsystem determining the positive coordinates. Cramer's rule and the determinant bound for a matrix with entries in \(\{0,1\}\) show that their numerators and denominators have \(O(m\log(m+2))\) bits.

A certificate can thus list at most \(m+2\) bundles and their rational weights. In polynomial time one can verify:

  • the mass and supply equalities;
  • the welfare threshold;
  • every ordered EF1 comparison between listed typed bundles, by subtracting the largest singleton value from the compared bundle, or subtracting zero for an empty bundle.

Therefore the threshold problem belongs to NP and, by the reduction above, is NP-complete.

For completeness, the maximum is attained: the feasible EF1 set is a finite union of closed, bounded polytopes, one for each pairwise-compatible allowed support. Nonemptiness also follows from the supplied discrete EF1 guarantee.

6. A population lower bound of \(\boldsymbol{8/7}\).

Take three goods \(g_1,g_2,p\), and a rational parameter \(0<\varepsilon<1/6\). Set
\( (v_a(g_1),v_a(g_2),v_a(p)) =\left(\frac12,\frac12,0\right) \)
and
\( (v_b(g_1),v_b(g_2),v_b(p)) =\left(\frac13+\varepsilon,\frac13+\varepsilon,\frac13-2\varepsilon\right). \)
Both valuations are normalized. Write \(c=1/3+\varepsilon\) and \(d=1/3-2\varepsilon\).

I first show that every population-EF1 allocation satisfies
\( x_{g_1}+x_{g_2}\le1. \tag{5} \)

If no supported type-\(a\) bundle contains both \(g_1\) and \(g_2\), (5) follows by averaging their number in a type-\(a\) bundle.

Otherwise, choose a supported type-\(a\) bundle containing both. Deleting at most one good leaves type-\(b\) value at least \(c\). Every supported type-\(b\) bundle must therefore have value at least \(c\). A bundle containing neither \(g_1\) nor \(g_2\) has value at most \(d<c\), so every type-\(b\) bundle contains at least one of them. Averaging and using the supply constraints gives
\( (1-x_{g_1})+(1-x_{g_2})\ge1, \)
which again proves (5).

The welfare identity now gives
\( 2W =1+\left(\frac12-c\right)(x_{g_1}+x_{g_2})-d x_p \le1+\frac12-c =\frac76-\varepsilon. \)
The partition assigning \(\{g_1\}\) to type \(a\) and \(\{g_2,p\}\) to type \(b\) is EF1 and attains this bound. Hence
\( \mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)=\frac7{12}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. \)

The unconstrained optimum assigns both \(g_1,g_2\) to type \(a\) and \(p\) to type \(b\), giving
\( \mathrm{OPT}^{\infty}(I)=\frac23-\varepsilon. \)
Therefore
\( \frac{\mathrm{OPT}^{\infty}(I)}{\mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I)} =\frac{8-12\varepsilon}{7-6\varepsilon} \longrightarrow\frac87 \)
as positive rational \(\varepsilon\) tends to zero. This proves the claimed lower bound on the supremum.

7. Upper bound and remaining question.

Apply the discrete guarantee stated in the question to the same goods and valuations. Its hypotheses hold: there are two agents with nonnegative, normalized additive valuations.

The discrete unconstrained optimum is
\( \sum_g\max\{v_a(g),v_b(g)\}=2\mathrm{OPT}^{\infty}(I). \)
The guarantee supplies an EF1 partition \((A,B)\) satisfying
\( v_a(A)+v_b(B) \ge\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot2\mathrm{OPT}^{\infty}(I). \)
Assigning \(A\) to all of type \(a\) and \(B\) to all of type \(b\) is population-EF1 and halves that discrete welfare. Consequently,
\( \mathrm{EF1}^{\infty}(I) \ge\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\mathrm{OPT}^{\infty}(I). \)

Thus exact computation is NP-hard, but the arguments here determine the price only within
\( \left[\frac87,\frac2{\sqrt3}\right]. \)
