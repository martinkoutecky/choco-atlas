The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), masses \(\mu\), and a monotone bounded type-symmetric game \(v(A)\) on finite multisets \(A\), what is the best worst-case welfare ratio achievable by a causal, irrevocable, non-wasteful sharing policy when type mass arrives over time and follows the paper’s greedy choice rule, compared with \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,v)=\max_{\lambda_A\ge0}\sum_{1\le |A|\le\delta}\lambda_Av(A)\) subject to \(\sum_A a_t(A)\lambda_A=\mu_t\) for every \(t\in T\)?

Exact optimal ratio

The optimal worst-case welfare ratio is
$$ \boxed{ \rho^*(b,B)= \min\left\{ \frac{b+\sqrt{b^2+4bB}}{2B}, \frac{3b}{B} \right\}. } $$
Equivalently,
$$ \rho^*(b,B)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{b+\sqrt{b^2+4bB}}{2B},&3b\leq B\leq6b,\\[6pt] \dfrac{3b}{B},&B\geq6b. \end{cases} $$

The new step closing the gap in the notes is a three-type upper bound. It uses three different positive dyadic mass profiles with the same support and value table. Thus their relevant offers coincide even for policies depending arbitrarily on the packet parameter.

1. Preliminaries

Write
$$ q=\frac{B}{b}, \qquad f(q)=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4q}}{2q}. $$
The hypotheses imply $q\geq3$.

For every admissible policy, a packet of type $t$ receives at least its singleton value $v(\{t\})$ upon entry: starting a singleton offers exactly that value. Irrevocability preserves this lower bound. Consequently, every actual coalition $C$ satisfies
$$ v(C)\geq |C|b. $$
Since $v(C)\leq B<(\delta+1)b$, every actual coalition has at most $\delta$ members.

The benchmark program is feasible: take $\lambda_{\{t\}}=\mu_t$ and all other variables zero. Moreover, every feasible solution satisfies
$$ \sum_A |A|\lambda_A = \sum_t\mu_t = 1. $$
Its feasible region is therefore bounded and closed, so its maximum exists.

2. An admissible policy attaining the claimed lower bound

2.1. Threshold allocation

Fix a threshold $h\geq0$. A founder receives its singleton value. When a type $t$ is appended to a nonempty coalition with multiset $A$, let
$$ \Delta=v(A+\{t\})-v(A). $$
Give the newcomer
$$ \max\{0,\Delta-h\}, $$
and increase the preceding coalition member’s share by
$$ \min\{\Delta,h\}. $$
Leave all other shares unchanged.

Monotonicity gives $\Delta\geq0$. The two allocations sum to $\Delta$, so this defines nonnegative, budget-balanced, irrevocable shares recursively for every ordered history. It uses only permitted history values and the parameters $b,B$. This is the threshold allocation discussed in the [supplied discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11883v1#Thmtheorem6); its continuous guarantee is proved below directly.

Label the packets by $i$, and put
$$ s_i=v(\{t(i)\}). $$
Because the singleton option wins ties, an actual join requires
$$ \Delta-h>s_i. $$
Summing along a terminal coalition’s formation gives
$$ v(C)\geq \sum_{i\in C}s_i+(|C|-1)h. $$

Define analytical weights, rather than policy shares, by
$$ u_i = s_i+ \frac{v(C)-\sum_{j\in C}s_j}{|C|} \qquad(i\in C). $$
They satisfy
$$ \sum_{i\in C}u_i=v(C), \qquad u_i\geq s_i. $$
For a nonsingleton terminal coalition,
$$ u_i \geq s_i+\frac{|C|-1}{|C|}h \geq s_i+\frac h2. $$

2.2. Inequalities for subsets of labeled packets

Set
$$ \alpha(h)= \min\left\{ \frac{2b}{2b+h}, \frac{4b+h}{2B}, \frac{3b}{B} \right\}. $$
We prove that every nonempty subset $S$ of labeled packets with $|S|\leq\delta$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{i\in S}u_i\geq \alpha(h)v(S). $$
Here $v(S)$ denotes the value of its type multiset, including repeated types.

There are four exhaustive cases.

  • One packet. Since $\alpha(h)\leq1$,

$$ u_i\geq s_i=v(\{i\})\geq\alpha(h)v(\{i\}). $$

  • Two terminal singletons. Suppose $i$ arrived before $j$. The coalition containing $i$ was still a singleton when $j$ arrived. Since $j$ chose its singleton option,

$$ \max\{0,v(\{i,j\})-s_i-h\}\leq s_j. $$
Therefore
$$ v(\{i,j\})\leq s_i+s_j+h. $$
With $x=s_i+s_j\geq2b$,
$$ u_i+u_j=x \geq \frac{2b}{2b+h}(x+h) \geq \frac{2b}{2b+h}v(\{i,j\}). $$
The first inequality follows from
$$ \frac{x}{x+h}\geq\frac{2b}{2b+h}. $$

  • Two packets, at least one in a terminal nonsingleton coalition. Their weights sum to at least $2b+h/2$. Thus

$$ u_i+u_j \geq \frac{4b+h}{2B}v(\{i,j\}), $$
because $v(\{i,j\})\leq B$.

  • At least three packets. Each weight is at least $b$, so

$$ \sum_{i\in S}u_i\geq3b \geq\frac{3b}{B}v(S). $$

This proves the asserted subset inequality.

2.3. Transfer to the fractional benchmark

Fix an instance and an admissible packet parameter $k$. Write
$$ n_t=k\mu_t. $$
For all sufficiently large admissible $k$, every supported type satisfies $n_t\geq\delta$, because its mass is positive.

Define its average analytical weight by
$$ \overline u_t = \frac1{n_t}\sum_{i:t(i)=t}u_i. $$
For a table multiset $A$, choose uniformly a labeled subset containing exactly $a_t(A)$ packets of each type $t$. These choices exist because $n_t\geq\delta$. Every selected subset has value $v(A)$.

A packet of type $t$ is included with probability $a_t(A)/n_t$. Averaging the subset inequality therefore yields
$$ \sum_t a_t(A)\overline u_t\geq\alpha(h)v(A). $$
For any feasible benchmark allocation $(\lambda_A)_A$, multiply by $\lambda_A$ and sum:
$$ \begin{aligned} \alpha(h)\sum_A\lambda_Av(A) &\leq \sum_t\overline u_t\sum_Aa_t(A)\lambda_A\\ &= \sum_t\mu_t\overline u_t\\ &= \frac1k\sum_i u_i\\ &= \operatorname{SW}_{\varphi^{(k)}}(v,\pi). \end{aligned} $$
Taking the maximum over feasible allocations proves
$$ \operatorname{SW}_{\varphi^{(k)}}(v,\pi) \geq \alpha(h)\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,v) $$
for every arrival order and all sufficiently large admissible $k$.

The weights and the mass averages are used only in this proof. The policy itself uses neither final masses nor future arrivals.

2.4. Choosing the threshold

The first threshold-dependent term in $\alpha(h)$ decreases with $h$, and the second increases. They coincide when
$$ (2b+h)(4b+h)=4bB, $$
whose nonnegative solution is
$$ h_*=\sqrt{b^2+4bB}-3b. $$
It is positive because $B\geq3b$. At this threshold, both terms equal
$$ \frac{b+\sqrt{b^2+4bB}}{2B}=f(q). $$
Hence
$$ \rho^*(b,B)\geq \min\left\{f(q),\frac3q\right\}. $$
For $q\geq6$, the rational threshold $h=2b$ also attains $3/q$, since
$$ \alpha(2b)= \min\left\{\frac12,\frac{3b}{B},\frac{3b}{B}\right\} = \frac{3b}{B}. $$

3. A universal upper bound

Use one supported type of mass $1$, with
$$ v(A)= \begin{cases} b,&1\leq|A|\leq2,\\ B,&|A|\geq3. \end{cases} $$
This is an admissible monotone table and extension.

A singleton incumbent receives $b$. A pair also has value $b$, so budget balance and irrevocability force its newcomer’s offer to be zero. By induction, every packet starts a singleton under every policy. Terminal packet welfare is therefore $b$.

For the one-type fractional benchmark,
$$ \operatorname{OPT} = \max_{1\leq r\leq\delta}\frac{v(r)}r = \frac B3. $$
Indeed, the size-three column exists, $B/3\geq b$, and no other column has greater value per packet. Consequently,
$$ \rho^*(b,B)\leq\frac{3b}{B}. $$

4. The three-type upper bound

We now prove, for every binary rational $P$ satisfying
$$ 2b<P<B, $$
the bound
$$ \rho^*(b,B) \leq \max\left\{ \frac{2b}{P}, \frac{P+2b}{2B}, \frac{B+3b}{3B} \right\}. $$

4.1. A common support and value table

Take the support
$$ T=\{x,y,z\}. $$
For multisets of size at most $\delta$, define:

  • A pure $x$ singleton has value $b$, and a pure $x$ multiset of size at least two has value $P$.
  • Every nonempty pure $y$ or pure $z$ multiset has value $b$.
  • Every multiset containing at least two distinct types has value $B$.

For larger multisets, use the prescribed extension $v(A)=B$.

This table is monotone: a pure multiset’s value can increase only from $b$ to $P$ or $B$, and a mixed multiset already has value $B$.

Fix an arbitrary admissible policy family $\Phi$. For each $k$, let
$$ \xi_k = \text{the newcomer’s offer for history }(x,x), $$
and
$$ \eta_k = \text{the newcomer’s offer for history }(x,x,y). $$
These offers are identical across the mass profiles below. Their ordered histories, local values, support, and parameters coincide, and final masses are prohibited policy inputs.

Whenever all $x$ packets arrive first:

  • If $\xi_k\leq b$, every $x$ packet starts a singleton.
  • If $\xi_k>b$, the $x$ packets form pairs, with one singleton left over only when their number is odd.

For the second statement, a singleton offers $\xi_k>b$, whereas an existing $x$-pair offers a further $x$ packet zero: its size-three value is still $P$, so no additional value is available after preserving the incumbents’ total shares.

4.2. Profile A: refusing the third packet

Fix a positive dyadic $\varepsilon<1/4$ and take
$$ \mu_x=\mu_y=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}, \qquad \mu_z=\varepsilon. $$
Use the arrival order consisting of all $x$ packets, then all $y$ packets, then all $z$ packets.

Consider admissible $k$ for which the number of $x$ packets is even and
$$ \xi_k>b, \qquad \eta_k\leq b. $$
After the first phase, there are exactly
$$ \frac{(1-\varepsilon)k}{4} $$
$x$-pairs. Every $y$ packet starts a singleton: joining an $x$-pair offers at most $b$, and joining a pure $y$ singleton offers zero.

Welfare before the $z$ phase is
$$ (1-\varepsilon)\left(\frac P4+\frac b2\right). $$
Each later arrival increases welfare by at most $B$: it either starts a singleton or increases one existing coalition’s value by at most $B$. Thus
$$ \operatorname{SW} \leq (1-\varepsilon)\left(\frac P4+\frac b2\right)+\varepsilon B. $$

A feasible benchmark allocation uses mixed $xy$ pairs and $z$ singletons, giving
$$ \operatorname{OPT} \geq \frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}B+\varepsilon b. $$
The ratio is consequently at most
$$ H_\varepsilon(P)= \frac{ (1-\varepsilon)(P/4+b/2)+\varepsilon B }{ (1-\varepsilon)B/2+\varepsilon b }, $$
and
$$ \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}H_\varepsilon(P) = \frac{P+2b}{2B}. $$

4.3. Profile B: refusing the initial pair

With the same $\varepsilon$, take
$$ \mu_x=1-2\varepsilon, \qquad \mu_y=\mu_z=\varepsilon. $$
Again, all $x$ packets arrive first, then all $y$, then all $z$.

If $\xi_k\leq b$, the first phase consists entirely of singletons, with welfare $(1-2\varepsilon)b$. There are $2\varepsilon k$ later arrivals, each increasing welfare by at most $B$. Hence
$$ \operatorname{SW} \leq (1-2\varepsilon)b+2\varepsilon B. $$

The benchmark can use pure $x$-pairs and singleton $y$ and $z$ coalitions:
$$ \operatorname{OPT} \geq \frac{1-2\varepsilon}{2}P+2\varepsilon b. $$
Therefore the ratio is at most
$$ R_\varepsilon(P)= \frac{ (1-2\varepsilon)b+2\varepsilon B }{ (1-2\varepsilon)P/2+2\varepsilon b }, $$
with
$$ \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}R_\varepsilon(P)=\frac{2b}{P}. $$

4.4. Profile C: accepting both joins

Take a positive dyadic $a<1/6$ and set
$$ \mu_x=2a, \qquad \mu_y=a, \qquad \mu_z=1-3a. $$
All masses are positive dyadic numbers. Use the same type-block arrival order.

Suppose
$$ \xi_k>b, \qquad \eta_k>b. $$
For admissible $k$, the $2ak$ initial $x$ packets form exactly $ak$ pairs. Every subsequent $y$ packet joins an unfilled $x$-pair:

  • Such a pair offers $\eta_k>b$.
  • A completed $xxy$ coalition has value $B$, so it offers any further packet zero.
  • A pure $y$ singleton would offer another $y$ packet zero.

There are exactly $ak$ arriving $y$ packets. Thus every $x$-pair becomes an $xxy$ triple of value $B$.

Every later $z$ packet starts a singleton. Existing triples already have value $B$, and pure $z$ pairs have value $b$, so neither option offers a positive newcomer share. Terminal welfare is exactly
$$ \operatorname{SW}=aB+(1-3a)b. $$

A feasible benchmark allocation is
$$ \lambda_{\{x,z\}}=2a, \qquad \lambda_{\{y,z\}}=a, \qquad \lambda_{\{z\}}=1-6a. $$
These nonnegative coefficients use precisely the specified masses. They give
$$ \operatorname{OPT}\geq3aB+(1-6a)b. $$
Consequently the ratio is at most
$$ G_a= \frac{aB+(1-3a)b}{3aB+(1-6a)b}. $$
Taking dyadic $a$ approaching $1/6$ from below,
$$ \lim_{a\to(1/6)^-}G_a = \frac{B+3b}{3B}. $$

4.5. The quantifiers and dependence on the packet parameter

For fixed $\varepsilon$ and $a$, choose an infinite arithmetic progression of packet parameters admissible for all three profiles, additionally ensuring an even number of $x$ packets in Profile A. Such a progression exists because all masses are dyadic.

Every $k$ in this progression belongs to exactly one of the following classes:
$$ \begin{aligned} &\xi_k\leq b,\\ &\xi_k>b,\quad \eta_k\leq b,\\ &\xi_k>b,\quad \eta_k>b. \end{aligned} $$
At least one class is infinite.

If the first class is infinite, the fixed Profile B has limiting inferior ratio at most $R_\varepsilon(P)$. If the second is infinite, Profile A has limiting inferior ratio at most $H_\varepsilon(P)$. If the third is infinite, Profile C has limiting inferior ratio at most $G_a$.

Each conclusion follows because an infinite admissible subsequence bounds the limiting inferior over all admissible packet parameters, and the infimum over arrival orders is at most the ratio for the displayed order.

Thus, for every policy family, its worst-instance limiting inferior ratio is at most
$$ \max\{R_\varepsilon(P),H_\varepsilon(P),G_a\}. $$
Letting $\varepsilon$ tend to zero through positive dyadics and $a$ approach $1/6$ through dyadics from below gives
$$ \inf_{T,\mu,v} \liminf_{\substack{k\to\infty\\k\mu\in\mathbb Z^T}} \inf_\pi \frac{\operatorname{SW}_{\varphi^{(k)}}(v,\pi)} {\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,v)} \leq \max\left\{ \frac{2b}{P}, \frac{P+2b}{2B}, \frac{B+3b}{3B} \right\}. $$
This bound is uniform over policy families, so it also bounds $\rho^*(b,B)$.

5. Matching the lower bound

Put
$$ p=\sqrt{b^2+4bB}-b. $$
For $q\geq3$, one has $2b<p<B$. Indeed,
$$ \frac pb=\sqrt{1+4q}-1>2, $$
and
$$ \sqrt{1+4q}<q+1 $$
because squaring reduces the latter inequality to $q(q-2)>0$.

Also,
$$ p^2+2bp=4bB, $$
so
$$ \frac{2b}{p} = \frac{p+2b}{2B} = f(q). $$
Choose binary rationals $P$ approaching $p$ within $(2b,B)$. The three-type upper bound and continuity imply
$$ \rho^*(b,B) \leq \max\left\{ f(q),\frac{q+3}{3q} \right\}. $$

For $q\geq3$,
$$ f(q)\geq\frac{q+3}{3q} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 3\sqrt{1+4q}\geq2q+3. $$
Both sides of the last inequality are positive. Squaring therefore preserves equivalence and gives
$$ 9+36q\geq4q^2+12q+9 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\leq6. $$
Hence, when $3\leq q\leq6$,
$$ \rho^*(b,B)\leq f(q). $$
When $q\geq6$, Section 3 gives
$$ \rho^*(b,B)\leq\frac3q. $$

Finally,
$$ f(q)\leq\frac3q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sqrt{1+4q}\leq5 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\leq6. $$
These upper bounds match Section 2 in every permitted parameter range, proving the stated formula. The exhibited threshold policy family attains the optimum.

6. Running times and exact arithmetic

Benchmark computation

The benchmark is a rational linear program with $m$ variables and $\tau$ equality constraints. Every incidence coefficient belongs to $\{0,\ldots,\delta\}$. Its encoding length is bounded by
$$ O\!\left(L+m\tau\log(\delta+1)\right). $$
The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming computes an exact optimum in time polynomial in this encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here: all coefficients are rational, the feasible region is nonempty, and the objective has a finite attained maximum by Section 1. Thus the benchmark is computable in
$$ \operatorname{poly}\!\left(L+m\tau\log(\delta+1)\right) $$
bit operations.

Policy implementation

At each arrival, evaluate at most $k$ existing coalitions and the singleton option. Actual coalition histories have length at most $\delta$; an augmented multiset beyond that size has the prescribed value $B$.

Using linear searches through table keys, a conservative implementation takes
$$ O(k^2m\tau\delta) $$
arithmetic operations and comparisons. Its bit-operation bound is
$$ O\!\left( k^2m\tau\delta\, \operatorname{poly}\!\left( L+\log k+\log(\delta+1) \right) \right). $$
Only the requested history values enter the allocation decisions.

For $B\leq6b$, the threshold $h_*$ lies in a quadratic extension of the rationals. Store shares as rational expressions in $\sqrt{b^2+4bB}$. Exact sign comparisons reduce, after checking signs, to rational comparisons by squaring; they have polynomial bit complexity. Rational coefficient lengths remain polynomial in the displayed parameters because the input denominators have total bit length bounded by $L$, and accumulated multiplicities are bounded by $k$. The model permits these algebraic shares.

For $B\geq6b$, use the rational threshold $2b$. The optimal ratio itself has a rational or quadratic-radical representation computable in time polynomial in $L$.
