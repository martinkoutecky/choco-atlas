The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates with fixed positions in \(\mathbb{R}^2\), a norm from \(\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_\infty\}\), and a finite support \(S\) of realizable strict-ranking types with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), compute a ranking \(\pi\) minimizing \(\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\,\mathrm{KT}(\pi,\succ_t)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input consists of a nonempty candidate set \(C\) of size \(m\), fixed positions \(a_c\in\mathbb{Q}^2\) encoded as binary rationals, and a norm \(\ell_p\), where \(p\in\{1,2,\infty\}\). Positions are part of the input and cannot be changed.
A society is given by its support: a list \(S\) of \(\tau\) distinct strict total rankings \(\succ_t\) of \(C\), each with binary-rational mass \(\mu_t>0\), with \(\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
Each listed type is promised realizable: there exists \(x_t\in\mathbb{R}^2\) such that, for all distinct \(c,d\in C\), \(c\succ_t d\iff\lVert x_t-a_c\rVert_p<\lVert x_t-a_d\rVert_p\). Realizing points need not be supplied; distance ties are excluded.
Compute a strict total ranking \(\pi\) minimizing \(\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\,\mathrm{KT}(\pi,\succ_t)\), where \(\mathrm{KT}\) is Kendall tau distance. The output may be any minimizing ranking and need not be geometrically realizable. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

For each fixed \(p\in\{1,2,\infty\}\), is this mass-input Kemeny optimization problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite lists of voters, Kemeny optimization with two-dimensional Euclidean preferences is NP-hard under each of these three norms, even when a geometric representation is supplied. [Escoffier, Spanjaard, and Tydrichová](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.13054v2).
No published classification of exactly the mass-input formulation above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The coordinate encoding was unspecified; candidate positions are binary rationals, included in the total input length.
  • Fixed positions mean positions supplied with each instance and held fixed during optimization, rather than one universal configuration.
  • Realizability is a promise relative to the common supplied candidate positions; realizing voter points are not additional required inputs.
  • Realizability uses strict distance comparisons, excluding distance ties, as in the discrete definition.
  • The output ranges over all strict total rankings, without a geometric realizability restriction; any optimum is acceptable.
  • The candidate set is nonempty, and L measures the entire binary input.

Classification

For every fixed \(p\in\{1,2,\infty\}\), the stated mass-input optimization problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\). Consequently, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The rational-coordinate restriction requires some care for \(p=2\). Below, an exact rational construction closes that gap. Throughout, reductions construct candidate positions as part of their output instance; those positions remain fixed during optimization.

1. Aggregating voters preserves the minimizers

Consider an explicitly listed electorate with \(N>0\) voters and strict rankings \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_N\), realizable at supplied candidate positions.

Let \(S\) contain its distinct rankings. For each \(t\in S\), let \(n_t\) be its multiplicity and set \(\mu_t=n_t/N\). These are positive rational masses summing to one. For every output ranking \(\pi\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\,\mathrm{KT}(\pi,\succ_t)=\frac1N\sum_{v=1}^{N}\mathrm{KT}(\pi,\rho_v).\)

Thus the two objectives have exactly the same minimizing rankings.

Grouping identical rankings takes polynomial time, for example \(O(Nm\log(N+1))\) comparisons of candidate labels. Each mass needs \(O(\log(N+1))\) bits for its numerator and denominator, and \(\tau\leq N\). Candidate positions are copied unchanged, and every retained ranking remains realizable.

Therefore finite-voter hardness transfers whenever the hard instances have polynomial-size rational candidate coordinates. This reduction never expands binary-encoded masses into voters.

2. The cases \(p=1\) and \(p=\infty\)

We use the finite-voter hardness theorem supplied in the question: Kemeny optimization remains \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\) for strict two-dimensional preferences with a supplied representation. Its explicitly listed electorate has polynomial size in the source-instance length.

For \(\ell_1\), the paper’s construction already uses rational coordinates of polynomial binary length. With candidates indexed by \(i\), it takes \(\Delta=2^{m+1}\), places candidates at \((0,2^i)\) or \((\Delta,2^i)\), and uses voter coordinates obtained from integer bisector coordinates by perturbations of \(1/2\). All coordinates therefore have \(O(m)\) bits. Strictness is included in its definition of realizability. These facts are given in Definition 2, Theorem 1, and Section 3 of [Escoffier, Spanjaard, and Tydrichová](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.13054v2).

Applying Section 1 proves hardness for the exact rational mass-input problem under \(\ell_1\).

For \(\ell_\infty\), define the rational linear map \(T(x,y)=(x+y,x-y)\). For every vector \((u,v)\),

\(\displaystyle \|T(u,v)\|_\infty=\max\{|u+v|,|u-v|\}=|u|+|v|=\|(u,v)\|_1.\)

Indeed, when \(u,v\) have the same sign, \(|u+v|=|u|+|v|\); when they have opposite signs, \(|u-v|=|u|+|v|\). The other expression is at most that sum by the triangle inequality.

Consequently, transforming every candidate and realizing voter point preserves every distance and every strict ranking. Coordinates remain rational with polynomial binary length. Applying this transformation to the hard \(\ell_1\) instances proves hardness under \(\ell_\infty\).

3. An exact rational construction for \(p=2\)

We prove that any finite profile can be replaced, in polynomial time, by a profile realizable at rational planar candidate positions with exactly the same Kemeny minimizers.

Assume \(m\geq2\); for \(m=1\), every objective is zero.

3.1 Rational candidates with distinct bisectors

For \(i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), set

\(\displaystyle t_i=2^{i-3m},\qquad a_i=\left(\frac{1-t_i^2}{1+t_i^2},\frac{2t_i}{1+t_i^2}\right).\)

Each \(a_i\) has rational coordinates with \(O(m)\) bits, and direct calculation gives \(\|a_i\|_2^2=1\). The candidates are distinct because \(2t/(1+t^2)\) is strictly increasing for \(0<t<1\).

Since their squared norms coincide, the bisector of \(a_i,a_j\) is the line

\(\displaystyle D_{ij}=\{x:x\cdot(a_i-a_j)=0\}.\)

A direction vector for this line is

\(\displaystyle r_{ij}=(1-t_it_j,t_i+t_j).\)

To verify this, direct calculation shows that \(a_i+a_j\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(r_{ij}\), while

\(\displaystyle (a_i+a_j)\cdot(a_i-a_j)=\|a_i\|_2^2-\|a_j\|_2^2=0.\)

We next prove that distinct unordered candidate pairs have distinct bisector lines. Their slopes are

\(\displaystyle b_{ij}=\frac{t_i+t_j}{1-t_it_j}.\)

Let \(\delta=2^{-2m}\), so every \(t_i\leq\delta\). Then

\(\displaystyle 0<b_{ij}-(t_i+t_j)=\frac{(t_i+t_j)t_it_j}{1-t_it_j}<4\delta^3.\)

The sums \(t_i+t_j\), for \(i<j\), are distinct: after multiplication by \(2^{3m}\), their binary representations have ones precisely in positions \(i,j\). Moreover, distinct sums differ by at least \(h=2^{1-3m}\). Since

\(\displaystyle 4\delta^3=2^{2-6m}<2^{1-3m}=h,\)

each slope belongs to its corresponding interval \((t_i+t_j,t_i+t_j+h)\), and these intervals are disjoint. Hence all bisector lines are distinct.

3.2 Two voters affecting only one pairwise margin

Fix an ordered pair \((i,j)\), and write \(v=a_i-a_j\) and \(r=r_{ij}\). Thus \(r\cdot v=0\).

For any other unordered pair \(\{k,\ell\}\), put \(w=a_k-a_\ell\). Distinctness of the bisectors implies \(w\cdot r\neq0\): otherwise the nonzero vector \(r\) would belong to both bisector lines.

Choose the positive rational number

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\min\left(\{1\}\cup\left\{\frac{|w\cdot r|}{2|w\cdot v|}:\{k,\ell\}\neq\{i,j\},\ w\cdot v\neq0\right\}\right).\)

Define two voter points

\(\displaystyle f=r+\varepsilon v,\qquad g=-r+\varepsilon v.\)

For any candidate pair, equal squared candidate norms give

\(\displaystyle \|x-a_i\|_2^2-\|x-a_j\|_2^2=-2x\cdot(a_i-a_j).\)

Thus both voters strictly prefer \(i\) to \(j\), because

\(\displaystyle f\cdot v=g\cdot v=\varepsilon\|v\|_2^2>0.\)

For every other pair, the choice of \(\varepsilon\) gives

\(\displaystyle |\varepsilon w\cdot v|\leq\frac{|w\cdot r|}{2}.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle w\cdot f=w\cdot r+\varepsilon w\cdot v,\qquad w\cdot g=-w\cdot r+\varepsilon w\cdot v\)

are nonzero and have opposite signs. The two voters therefore rank that pair oppositely.

We have proved that these two strict rankings contribute margin \(2\) in favor of \(i\) over \(j\), and margin zero on every other pair.

All coordinates have \(O(m)\) bits. Each expression uses a constant number of arithmetic operations on \(O(m)\)-bit rationals; taking a minimum selects one such rational without increasing its length. Computing these gadgets for all pairs requires \(O(m^4)\) rational arithmetic operations. Rankings can be obtained exactly by sorting squared rational distances.

3.3 Preserving an arbitrary finite profile’s minimizers

Start with any explicitly listed profile of \(N>0\) voters. For distinct candidates define its signed margin

\(\displaystyle d_{ij}=\#\{v:i\succ_v j\}-\#\{v:j\succ_v i\}.\)

For each unordered pair with nonzero margin, take \(|d_{ij}|\) copies of the gadget favoring the candidate with positive margin. The resulting margin on that pair is \(2d_{ij}\).

Also add voters at \((1,0)\) and \((-1,0)\). Their rankings are strict and opposite: the first coordinate \((1-t_i^2)/(1+t_i^2)\) strictly decreases with \(i\), and the squared-distance comparison above determines their preferences. This extra pair contributes zero margins and ensures a nonempty electorate.

The new electorate has

\(\displaystyle N'=2+2\sum_{i<j}|d_{ij}|\leq2+2N\binom m2\)

voters. Its candidate positions are the rational points constructed above, and every ranking has an explicit strict realization.

Let \(K(\pi)\) and \(K'(\pi)\) be the original and new total Kendall costs. If \(\pi\) puts \(i\) before \(j\), the original disagreement count on that pair is \((N-d_{ij})/2\), and the new count is \((N'-2d_{ij})/2\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle K'(\pi)=2K(\pi)+\left(\frac{N'}2-N\right)\binom m2.\)

The additive term is independent of \(\pi\), and the multiplier is positive. Hence the minimizers coincide exactly.

Apply this construction to the hard finite Euclidean profiles from the supplied theorem, then aggregate rankings as in Section 1. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hardness}\) for \(p=2\) with binary-rational candidate positions.

No voter expansion is necessary in the implementation: each gadget ranking is stored with its multiplicity. After merging identical rankings,

\(\displaystyle \tau\leq2+2\binom m2,\)

and each mass has \(O(\log(Nm+1))\) bits. Thus the reduction’s output length and running time are polynomial in the original finite-profile input length.

4. Running times for the mass-input problem

These hardness results rule out a polynomial bound in \(m,\tau,L\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). An exact exponential algorithm nevertheless handles binary masses directly.

Write each mass as \(\mu_t=u_t/v_t\), and let \(D=\prod_t v_t\). The binary length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\). Define integer weights

\(\displaystyle w_{cd}=\sum_{t:c\succ_t d}u_t(D/v_t).\)

If an output ranking puts \(d\) before \(c\), this pair contributes \(w_{cd}\) to its cost multiplied by \(D\).

Let \(F(U)\) be the minimum scaled cost for ranking the subset \(U\). Choosing its last candidate gives

\(\displaystyle F(\varnothing)=0,\qquad F(U)=\min_{c\in U}\left(F(U\setminus\{c\})+\sum_{d\in U\setminus\{c\}}w_{cd}\right).\)

This recurrence is valid because all pairs involving the last candidate have determined orientations, while the remaining pairs contribute exactly the subproblem cost. Storing minimizing choices recovers a strict optimal ranking.

Precomputing subset sums yields \(O(m2^m)\) integer operations after \(O(\tau m^2)\) weight-accumulation operations. All integers have \(O(L+\log m)\) bits. A conservative bit-time bound is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left((\tau m^2+m2^m)L^2\right).\)

In particular, evaluating any proposed ranking takes \(O(\tau m^2L^2)\) bit time. These bounds apply under all three norms, since the objective depends only on the supplied rankings and masses.
