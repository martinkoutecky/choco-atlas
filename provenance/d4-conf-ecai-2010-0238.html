The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of categories \(G\), a finite support \(T\) of SCI-net types with strict rankings and rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and rational per-capita stocks \(b_g\), does there exist a bundle \(B_t\subseteq G\) for each type such that \(\sum_{t:g\in B_t}\mu_t=b_g\) for every category \(g\), and for every \(t,u\), either \(B_t=B_u\) or \(B_t\succ_t B_u\), where \(\succ_t\) is the partial bundle order induced by type \(t\)'s SCI-net?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a set of \(m\geq1\) categories. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq1\) distinct types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Each category has binary rational per-capita stock \(0<b_g\leq1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type supplies an SCI-net, meaning a strict linear ranking \(\triangleright_t\) of \(G\). Its bundle order \(\succ_t\) is the smallest strict partial order containing \(A\succ_t B\) whenever \(A\supsetneq B\), and \(S\cup\{x\}\succ_t S\cup\{y\}\) whenever \(x\triangleright_t y\) and \(S\subseteq G\setminus\{x,y\}\).
An allocation assigns one bundle \(B_t\subseteq G\) to the entire mass of each type; a type cannot be split among bundles. It is complete if \(\sum_{t\in T:g\in B_t}\mu_t=b_g\) for every \(g\in G\), and necessarily envy-free if, for every \(t,u\in T\), either \(B_t=B_u\) or \(B_t\succ_t B_u\).

Is deciding whether a complete, necessarily envy-free allocation exists solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents and distinct indivisible goods, existence of a complete necessarily envy-free allocation under SCI-net preferences is NP-complete, even with \(m=2n\), where \(n\) is the number of agents; it is in \(\mathrm{P}\) for two agents. [Source](https://recherche.noiraudes.net/resources/papers/COMSOC10.pdf)
No result settling the exact mass model defined above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The stock domain was unspecified: each listed category has strictly positive stock at most one, reflecting available goods and the restriction to at most one unit of each category per person.
  • The induced SCI-net bundle order is defined directly by monotonicity, singleton replacements, and transitive closure, retaining irreflexivity.
  • The continuous society may have a single supported type: the discrete requirement of at least two agents does not require two distinct preference types.
  • The input length L is the total binary encoding length of rankings, masses, and stocks.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when \(m=2\tau\) and every type mass and category stock equals \(1/\tau\).

The proof uses the finite-agent hardness result stated in the question, but explicitly handles the requirement that supported types be distinct.

1. Polynomial-time comparison of bundles

Fix a type \(t\), and write its ranking as \(g_1\triangleright_t\cdots\triangleright_t g_m\). Define \(P_k=\{g_1,\ldots,g_k\}\).

Lemma. For distinct bundles \(A,B\),
\[ A\succ_t B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |A\cap P_k|\geq |B\cap P_k| \text{ for every }k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}. \]

Proof. Adding goods never decreases any prefix count. Replacing a good by a higher-ranked good also never decreases any prefix count. Thus every generating comparison, and consequently every comparison in their transitive closure, satisfies the displayed inequalities.

Conversely, suppose the inequalities hold. Put \(C=A\setminus B\) and \(D=B\setminus A\). Cancelling the common goods gives \(|C\cap P_k|\geq|D\cap P_k|\) for every \(k\).

List \(C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_p\}\) and \(D=\{d_1,\ldots,d_q\}\) in decreasing preference order. The inequality for \(k=m\) gives \(p\geq q\). For each \(i\leq q\), consider the prefix ending at \(d_i\). It contains exactly \(i\) goods of \(D\), so it contains at least \(i\) goods of \(C\). Therefore \(c_i\) ranks at least as high as \(d_i\). Since \(C\cap D=\varnothing\), the ranking is strict: \(c_i\triangleright_t d_i\).

Starting from \(B\), replace each \(d_i\) by \(c_i\), and then add \(c_{q+1},\ldots,c_p\). Each replacement is an allowed improvement: its incoming good is absent, and its outgoing good is present. The final bundle is \(A\). Since \(A\neq B\), at least one improvement occurs. Hence \(A\succ_t B\). \(\square\)

The lemma gives a comparison algorithm taking \(O(m)\) time once the ranking positions are available.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate specifies the incidence bit of every category in every type’s bundle, using \(m\tau\) bits.

Completeness is verified by checking, for each \(g\),
\[ \sum_{t:g\in B_t}\mu_t=b_g. \]
Exact addition and comparison of the input rationals take polynomial time in \(L\): a common denominator obtained by multiplying their denominators has bit length at most the sum of their input bit lengths.

For every ordered pair \(t,u\), first check whether \(B_t=B_u\). Otherwise apply the lemma to check \(B_t\succ_t B_u\). All envy checks take \(O(\tau^2m)\) time after preprocessing the rankings.

Thus the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. Hardness reduction

Use the following known source problem: deciding whether \(n\) agents with SCI-net rankings over \(2n\) distinct indivisible goods admit a complete necessarily envy-free allocation. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete by Proposition 7 of [Bouveret, Endriss, and Lang](https://recherche.noiraudes.net/resources/papers/COMSOC10.pdf).

In the source problem, completeness means that the bundles partition the goods.

Handling distinct supported types

If two source agents have the same top-ranked good \(x\), the source instance is a no-instance. Indeed, completeness assigns \(x\) to exactly one agent. At least one of the two agents therefore lacks \(x\). For that agent, its own bundle has prefix count zero on \(\{x\}\), whereas the recipient’s bundle has prefix count one. The lemma rules out the required dominance.

Accordingly, when the source instance has repeated top-ranked goods, output this fixed mass-model no-instance:

  • \(G=\{a,b,c,d\}\) and \(\tau=2\);
  • rankings \(a\triangleright_1 b\triangleright_1 c\triangleright_1 d\) and \(a\triangleright_2 b\triangleright_2 d\triangleright_2 c\);
  • both masses and all stocks equal \(1/2\).

The two rankings are distinct. Completeness forces each category to belong to exactly one type’s bundle. Whichever type lacks \(a\) fails the prefix comparison against the type receiving \(a\). Hence this is a valid no-instance, with \(m=2\tau\).

Constructing the remaining instances

Now suppose all source agents have distinct top-ranked goods. Their rankings are consequently distinct.

Create one supported type per source agent, retaining its ranking, and set
\[ \tau=n,\qquad \mu_t=\frac1n\quad\text{for every }t,\qquad b_g=\frac1n\quad\text{for every }g. \]
Retain all \(2n\) goods as categories. These are valid positive rational masses and stocks, and the masses sum to one.

For any proposed allocation, completeness at category \(g\) is equivalent to
\[ \frac1n\,\bigl|\{t:g\in B_t\}\bigr|=\frac1n, \]
which is equivalent to exactly one type receiving \(g\). Thus complete allocations in the constructed instance are precisely partitions of the categories among the source agents.

The rankings and induced bundle orders are unchanged. Moreover, a complete necessarily envy-free partition has no empty bundle: an agent with an empty bundle cannot dominate any nonempty bundle, by the lemma at \(k=m\). Hence distinct agents’ bundles are nonempty and disjoint, so they cannot be equal. The mass model’s allowance for equal bundles therefore introduces no additional feasible allocation.

Consequently, the constructed mass instance admits a complete necessarily envy-free allocation if and only if the source instance does.

The construction has polynomial size: each added rational \(1/n\) uses \(O(\log n)\) bits. Together with the repeated-top case, this is a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

4. Conclusion

The exact, unsplittable mass model is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness already holds with \(m=2\tau\) and uniform masses and stocks \(1/\tau\). In this restriction, the completeness equations enforce the indivisibility needed by the source hardness result.
