The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an atomless probability space \((X,\mu)\) and a tagged individual \(h\) of zero population mass, choose a measurable undirected link relation \(E\) on \(X\cup\{h\}\). Let \(\deg_X(x)\) count \(x\)'s neighbors in \(X\), and require \(\int_X \deg_X(x)\,d\mu(x)<\infty\). Require every pair of distinct vertices to have a common neighbor, and minimize \(\ell(E)=\frac{1}{2}\int_X\deg_X(x)\,d\mu(x)+\int_X\mathbf{1}_{\{xh\in E\}}\,d\mu(x)\); return an optimal \(E\) and a proof of optimality.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(p_i\), where \(\sum_i p_i=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Realize it as \(X=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\times[0,1)\), with \(\mu(\{i\}\times A)=p_i\lambda(A)\), where \(\lambda\) is Lebesgue measure.
Add one tagged vertex \(h\notin X\) of zero mass. Types impose no link restrictions, and \(h\) is not required to be a hub.
An admissible link relation \(E\subseteq(X\cup\{h\})^2\) is Borel, symmetric and irreflexive. For every distinct \(u,v\), including pairs involving \(h\), there must exist \(w\notin\{u,v\}\) with \((u,w),(v,w)\in E\); this requirement holds pointwise, including on null sets.
Define \(d_X(x)=\#\{y\in X:(x,y)\in E\}\), interpreting every infinite cardinality as \(+\infty\). Require this function to be measurable and \(\int_X d_X\,d\mu<\infty\); infinite degrees on null sets are permitted.
Minimize \(\ell(E)=\frac12\int_X d_X(x)\,d\mu(x)+\int_X\mathbf1_{\{(x,h)\in E\}}\,d\mu(x)\). The associated threshold problem takes an additional binary rational \(B\geq0\) and asks whether an admissible \(E\) satisfies \(\ell(E)\leq B\).
Is this threshold problem in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and is the infimum of \(\ell(E)\) attained by an admissible relation? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(m=1\) counts tagged vertices and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
For finite simple graphs on \(n\geq3\) vertices with a common neighbor for every distinct pair, the sharp minimum edge count is \(3(n-1)/2\) for odd \(n\) and \(3n/2-1\) for even \(n\), attained by the windmill and its variant with one three-vertex sail. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05093v6).

Result

For every society in the question, the minimum is \(1\), and it is attained. Consequently, the threshold problem answers YES exactly when \(B\geq1\), and belongs to \(\mathrm P\).

The permitted infinite degrees on null sets are essential to the construction: an untagged point of \(X\) serves as the hub.

Lower bound

Let \(V=X\cup\{h\}\), and let \(N(v)\) denote the neighbors of \(v\).

Every vertex has at least two neighbors. Indeed, if \(N(v)\) is empty, no pair containing \(v\) has a common neighbor. If \(N(v)=\{u\}\), the pair \(u,v\) cannot have a common neighbor distinct from its endpoints.

For \(x\in X\), put \(H(x)=\mathbf1_{\{(x,h)\in E\}}\). Since \(h\) is the only vertex outside \(X\), the preceding observation implies
\(d_X(x)+H(x)\geq2\).
Therefore, pointwise, with the same inequality valid for infinite degrees,
\[ \frac12d_X(x)+H(x)\geq1+\frac12H(x). \]
Integrating and using \(\mu(X)=1\) gives
\[ \ell(E)\geq1+\frac12\mu\bigl(\{x\in X:(x,h)\in E\}\bigr)\geq1. \]

An admissible relation attaining the bound

Choose the distinct points \(a=(1,0)\) and \(b=(1,\tfrac12)\), which exist because \(\tau\geq1\). Both have measure zero. Put \(Y=X\setminus\{a,b\}\).

Define \(F:X\to X\) by
\[ F(i,t)= \begin{cases} (i,t+\tfrac12),&0\leq t<\tfrac12,\\ (i,t-\tfrac12),&\tfrac12\leq t<1. \end{cases} \]
This is a Borel involution without fixed points. It exchanges \(a,b\), so it restricts to an involution of \(Y\).

Define \(E\) by the following undirected edges:

  • Join \(a\) to every vertex of \(V\setminus\{a\}\).
  • Join \(y\) to \(F(y)\) for every \(y\in Y\).
  • Join \(b\) to \(h\).

These prescriptions give a symmetric, irreflexive Borel relation. To verify Borelness explicitly, the star consists of products involving the singleton \(\{a\}\); the pairing edges are the two Borel graphs specified by the displayed piecewise formula; and the remaining edge consists of two ordered pairs.

Every distinct pair has a common neighbor, pointwise:

  • Two vertices different from \(a\) share \(a\).
  • For \(a,y\) with \(y\in Y\), the vertex \(F(y)\) is a common neighbor distinct from both endpoints.
  • For \(a,b\), the vertex \(h\) is a common neighbor.
  • For \(a,h\), the vertex \(b\) is a common neighbor.

Thus the condition also holds at every exceptional null point.

The degrees into \(X\) are
\[ d_X(x)= \begin{cases} +\infty,&x=a,\\ 1,&x=b,\\ 2,&x\in Y. \end{cases} \]
This function is measurable. Since \(\mu(\{a,b\})=0\), its integral is \(2\), despite the infinite value at \(a\). Moreover, the neighbors of \(h\) in \(X\) are exactly \(a,b\), a null set. Hence
\[ \ell(E)=\frac12\cdot2+0=1. \]
The relation is admissible and attains the lower bound.

Threshold algorithm and running time

On valid inputs, compare the binary rational \(B\) with \(1\). If \(B=r/s\) with \(s>0\), this is the integer comparison \(r\geq s\).

Including an input scan, the running time is \(O(L+\tau+m)=O(L)\) bit operations in the stipulated setting \(m=1\). The masses require no optimization: the same construction works for every allowed support and every allowed choice of masses.

1. Lower bound

The assertion that every vertex has at least two neighbors is valid:

  • If \(N(v)=\varnothing\), pairing \(v\) with any other vertex violates the common-neighbor condition.
  • If \(N(v)=\{u\}\), a common neighbor of \(u,v\) would have to belong to \(N(v)\), hence equal \(u\), which the condition forbids.

There are other vertices available in each case because \(X\) is infinite.

Since the only vertex outside \(X\) is \(h\), the total degree of \(x\in X\) is \(d_X(x)+H(x)\), with infinite values interpreted as stipulated. Thus
\[ d_X(x)+H(x)\geq2 \]
and consequently
\[ \frac12d_X(x)+H(x)\geq1+\frac12H(x). \]
The latter inequality also holds when \(d_X(x)=+\infty\).

The function \(H\) is measurable: its support is a section of the Borel relation \(E\) at \(h\). Together with the required measurability of \(d_X\), this justifies integration. Using \(\mu(X)=1\) gives precisely the claimed bound
\[ \ell(E)\geq1+\frac12\mu(N(h)\cap X)\geq1. \]

2. Construction and Borelness

The points \(a=(1,0)\) and \(b=(1,\tfrac12)\) exist, are distinct, and have measure zero for every allowed input, including \(\tau=1\).

The displayed map \(F\) is Borel, has no fixed points, and satisfies \(F(F(i,t))=(i,t)\) on both branches. Since \(F(a)=b\) and \(F(b)=a\), it maps \(Y=X\setminus\{a,b\}\) onto itself.

Each prescribed edge family is Borel:

  • The star at \(a\) is the union of two products of Borel sets.
  • The pairing edges are the graph of the displayed piecewise translation restricted to \(Y\), together with its transpose.
  • The edge between \(b\) and \(h\) consists of two singleton ordered pairs.

Their union is symmetric and irreflexive. There is no hidden restriction imposed by the types.

3. Pointwise common-neighbor condition

The four cases listed by the prover exhaust all distinct pairs:

  • For two vertices different from \(a\), the vertex \(a\) is adjacent to both and distinct from both.
  • For \(a,y\) with \(y\in Y\), the vertex \(F(y)\) lies in \(Y\), differs from \(y\), and is adjacent to both endpoints.
  • For \(a,b\), the vertex \(h\) supplies both required edges.
  • For \(a,h\), the vertex \(b\) supplies both required edges.

This verification is pointwise and includes every exceptional null point.

4. Degrees, integrability, and attainment

The stated degrees into \(X\) are exact. The vertex \(a\) has infinitely many neighbors in \(X\); \(b\) has only \(a\) as an \(X\)-neighbor; and each \(y\in Y\) has exactly the two distinct \(X\)-neighbors \(a,F(y)\).

The degree function is measurable. An infinite value on the null singleton \(\{a\}\) contributes zero to its nonnegative Lebesgue integral. Therefore
\[ \int_X d_X\,d\mu=2. \]
The neighbors of \(h\) in \(X\) are exactly \(a,b\), so the tagged-edge term is zero. Hence \(\ell(E)=1\), and the admissible construction attains the lower bound.
