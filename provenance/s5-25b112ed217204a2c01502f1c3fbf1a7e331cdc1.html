The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu\), a symmetric block-affiliation matrix \(A\), positive rival-value parameters \(b\), and an initial informed-mass vector \(x^0\), determine whether there is a forecast \(F:[0,\mu]\to[0,\mu]\) satisfying the mass versions of Theorem 2’s consistency and incentive conditions with a finite exact semialgebraic description; if so, construct one and return \(F(x^0)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There is one affiliation block per supported type, so \(m=\tau\geq1\). Masses need not sum to one.
Input also includes a symmetric matrix \(A\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^{m\times m}\), parameters \(b\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^{m}\), and \(x^0\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}\) with \(0\leq x^0\leq\mu\) and \(\sum_t x_t^0>0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
States are \(D=\prod_{t=1}^{m}[0,\mu_t]\), ordered coordinatewise. An informed sender of type \(t\) evaluates a final state \(z\) by \(U_t(z)=b_t\sum_s A_{ts}z_s-\sum_s z_s\); communication history is irrelevant, and recipients cannot refuse information.
At state \(x\), a sender type is eligible exactly when \(x_t>0\). One eligible sender may inform any amount of any types, including its own, so its possible next states are all \(y\in D\) with \(y\geq x\), irrespective of affiliation links.
A forecast \(F:D\to D\) must satisfy, for every \(x\in D\), \(F(x)\geq x\) and \(F(F(x))=F(x)\).
If \(F(x)=x\), require \(U_t(x)\geq U_t(F(y))\) for every eligible \(t\) and every \(y\geq x\).
If \(F(x)\neq x\), require an eligible \(t\) and \(y\geq x\) such that \(F(y)=F(x)\), \(U_t(F(x))>U_t(x)\), and \(U_t(F(x))\geq U_t(F(z))\) for every \(z\geq x\). Thus indifference alone cannot initiate diffusion; maximizing ties are unrestricted.
A finite exact semialgebraic description means a quantifier-free Boolean formula of polynomial equalities and inequalities with rational coefficients defining the graph of \(F\), with coefficients and exponents encoded in binary.

Is deciding whether such a semialgebraically described forecast exists in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? What is the complexity of constructing one when it exists and computing \(F(x^0)\) exactly, with running times stated in \(m\), \(\tau\), \(L\), and output description length?

For finite societies, a forecast always exists for arbitrary preferences, and every forecast is obtainable by backward induction; this is an existence and characterization result, without a polynomial-time bound. [Source](https://roketskiy.net/docs/circles_of_trust.pdf)
A related continuum model analyzes fixed sharing probabilities under Poisson communication opportunities and a finite horizon, rather than the unrestricted forecast conditions above. [Source](https://evandsadler.com/Sharing%20Rival%20Information.pdf) No result settling this exact continuous problem was located.

The main new result is a complete construction for every admissible input with $m=\tau\leq2$, including arbitrary mixtures of positive and negative utility coefficients. Its graph uses only linear predicates, has length $O(L)$, and its value at the rational initial state is rational and polynomial-time computable.

I also give justified bit-complexity bounds for verifying and evaluating a supplied description, repairing the quantitative gap identified in the notes.

1. Definitions and the terminal-set characterization

Write
$$ c_{ts}=b_tA_{ts}-1,\qquad C=(c_{ts}), $$
so that
$$ U_t(z)=\sum_{s=1}^{m}c_{ts}z_s. $$
Every supported mass $\mu_t$ is positive, and throughout $m=\tau$. No normalization of the masses is used.

For an extensive, idempotent map $F:D\to D$, define
$$ T=\{w\in D:F(w)=w\},\qquad T_x=\{w\in T:w\geq x\}. $$

Lemma 1. For every $x\in D$,
$$ \{F(z):z\in D,\ z\geq x\}=T_x. $$

Proof. If $z\geq x$, extensiveness gives $F(z)\geq z\geq x$, and idempotence makes $F(z)$ terminal. Conversely, if $w\in T_x$, choosing $z=w$ gives $F(z)=w$. ∎

It follows that the forecast requirements are equivalent to the following conditions:

  • $F(x)\in T_x$ for every $x$.
  • If $x\in T$, then

$$ U_t(w)\leq U_t(x) $$
for every eligible $t$ and every $w\in T_x$.

  • If $x\notin T$, some eligible $t$ satisfies

$$ U_t(F(x))>U_t(x),\qquad U_t(F(x))\geq U_t(w)\quad\text{for every }w\in T_x. $$

These conditions are sufficient as well: the selected outputs are terminal, giving idempotence, and Lemma 1 supplies all comparisons with forecast outputs. The additional witness in the original transitory-state condition can be chosen as
$$ y=F(x). $$

Every forecast must satisfy
$$ F(0)=0,\qquad F(\mu)=\mu. $$
The first equality follows because the origin has no eligible sender; the second follows from extensiveness.

The constructions below explicitly prove attainment of the required maxima. They do not assume that an arbitrary terminal set is closed.

2. Complete solution for one type

Suppose $m=\tau=1$, and put $c=b_1A_{11}-1$.

If $c\leq0$, every increase weakly reduces utility, so strict improvement is impossible. Consequently, every state must be terminal, and the unique forecast is the identity.

If $c>0$, the terminal state $\mu_1$ rules out every positive smaller state as a terminal state. Extensiveness and idempotence therefore force
$$ F(0)=0,\qquad F(x)=\mu_1\quad\text{for }x>0. $$
This is a forecast because its positive-state output is the sender’s strict improvement and global utility maximum.

Both graphs have length $O(L)$ and are polynomial-time constructible and evaluable.

3. Complete construction for arbitrary two-type inputs

Theorem 2. Every admissible input with $m=\tau=2$ admits a rational-coefficient, quantifier-free semialgebraic forecast whose predicates are linear. Its graph has length $N=O(L)$. Construction and exact evaluation at $x^0$ take
$$ O\bigl((L+1)^2+N+H\bigr) $$
bit operations using elementary rational arithmetic, where $H$ is the written length of the exact value. The constructed value is rational and satisfies $H=O(L)$.

The proof works for an arbitrary rational $2\times2$ utility matrix $C$, and hence applies to the matrices arising from the task’s restrictions.

3.1. Choosing the geometric case

Call column $j$ positive if it has at least one positive entry. Define the cone
$$ W=\{r\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{2}:Cr\leq0\}. $$

There are three exhaustive cases.

Case I: neither column is positive. All coefficients are nonpositive. Use the identity forecast.

Case II: both columns are positive and $W=\{0\}$. Use the construction in Section 3.4.

Case III: all remaining cases. Choose a nonzero rational vector $v\geq0$ with $Cv\leq0$, as follows.

If exactly one column, say $j$, is not positive, take
$$ v=e_j. $$
The other column is positive.

If both columns are positive and $W\neq\{0\}$, every nonzero vector in $W$ has both coordinates positive: a vector supported on a single coordinate would contradict positivity of that column. Rescale such a vector to
$$ v=(1,k). $$
The admissible values of $k$ are given by
$$ k>0,\qquad c_{t1}+c_{t2}k\leq0\quad(t=1,2). $$
This is an intersection of two rational half-lines with the positive half-line. Its endpoints can be computed by rational comparisons and divisions; when nonempty, it contains a positive rational point of bit length $O(L)$.

Thus all case distinctions and the choice of $v$ are computable in polynomial time.

3.2. The ray construction when both types are eligible

Consider Case III. For a state $x$ with $x_1,x_2>0$, set
$$ r=\mu-x,\qquad \lambda(x)=\min_{j:v_j>0}\frac{r_j}{v_j}, $$
and define
$$ F(x)=\mu-\lambda(x)v. $$
Also set $F(0)=0$.

The terminal states with both coordinates positive are exactly
$$ T^{++}=\{\mu-\kappa v:\kappa\geq0,\ \mu-\kappa v>0\}. $$

First, $\lambda(x)v\leq r$, so $F(x)\geq x$ and $F(x)$ has both coordinates positive. Its residual vector is $\lambda(x)v$, so applying the formula again fixes it. Moreover,
$$ F(x)=x \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r=\lambda(x)v. $$

For a terminal point $w=\mu-\kappa v$ above $x$, the inequality $w\geq x$ is equivalent to
$$ 0\leq\kappa\leq\lambda(x). $$
Since $Cv\leq0$,
$$ U_t(\mu-\kappa v)=U_t(\mu)-\kappa(Cv)_t $$
is nondecreasing in $\kappa$ for each sender. Consequently, $F(x)$ maximizes both senders’ utilities among accessible terminal points.

Suppose $F(x)\neq x$. Then
$$ \delta=F(x)-x=r-\lambda(x)v $$
is nonnegative and nonzero.

If $v=e_j$, then $\delta_j=0$ and the other component is positive. The other column is positive, so some sender $t$ has
$$ U_t(F(x))-U_t(x)=c_{t,3-j}\delta_{3-j}>0. $$

If $v$ has both coordinates positive, the minimum defining $\lambda(x)$ makes at least one component of $\delta$ zero. Its other component is positive, and positivity of that column again supplies a sender with strict improvement.

Both senders are eligible at $x$. Thus the terminal-set characterization proves every required condition on states with both coordinates positive. Terminal states on the axes cannot be above such a state, so adding the axis rules below does not change this argument.

3.3. Completing the ray construction on the axes

Let $x=ae_t$, where $0<a\leq\mu_t$, and let $o=3-t$ denote the other coordinate. Only type $t$ is eligible. Write
$$ d=c_{tt},\qquad e=\mu_t e_t. $$

If $d>0$, define
$$ F(ae_t)=e. $$

This rule is valid because $v_o>0$. Otherwise $v$ would be supported on coordinate $t$, contradicting $(Cv)_t\leq0$ and $d>0$. Furthermore,
$$ dv_t+c_{to}v_o\leq0 $$
implies $c_{to}\leq0$. Therefore every state $w\in D$ satisfies
$$ U_t(w)=dw_t+c_{to}w_o\leq d\mu_t=U_t(e). $$
The endpoint $e$ is fixed by the rule. For $a<\mu_t$, it gives strict improvement
$$ U_t(e)-U_t(ae_t)=d(\mu_t-a)>0. $$
At $a=\mu_t$, it gives the required terminal inequality.

If $d\leq0$, define
$$ \lambda_a=\min_{j:v_j>0}\frac{\mu_j-a(e_t)_j}{v_j}, \qquad p=\mu-\lambda_a v. $$
Then $p\geq ae_t$. Set
$$ F(ae_t)= \begin{cases} p,&p_o>0\text{ and }U_t(p)>da,\\ ae_t,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$

To verify this rule, consider the accessible terminal points with both coordinates positive. Their ray parameter satisfies $\kappa\leq\lambda_a$, so their utilities are bounded above by $U_t(p)$.

If $p_o>0$, then $p$ itself belongs to $T^{++}$ and attains this bound.

If $p_o=0$, the bound may correspond to an excluded endpoint of the full-support ray. Nevertheless,
$$ U_t(p)=dp_t\leq da, $$
because $p_t\geq a$ and $d\leq0$. Hence no accessible full-support terminal strictly improves on the current state.

Finally, any accessible terminal on the same axis has the form $ue_t$ with $u\geq a$, and therefore
$$ U_t(ue_t)=du\leq da. $$
The other positive axis and the origin are inaccessible from $ae_t$.

Thus a fixed axis state weakly dominates every accessible terminal. Whenever the rule moves, it selects an attained full-support maximum that strictly exceeds both the current utility and every accessible axis-terminal utility. This verifies the forecast conditions and idempotence on the axes.

3.4. The singleton construction when $W=\{0\}$

Consider Case II. Define
$$ F(x)=\mu\qquad\text{when }x_1,x_2>0, $$
and set $F(0)=0$.

For a full-support state $x\neq\mu$, its nonzero residual $r=\mu-x$ does not belong to $W$. Thus some eligible sender satisfies
$$ U_t(\mu)-U_t(x)=(Cr)_t>0. $$
The only full-support terminal is $\mu$, so maximality is immediate.

It remains to define the positive axes. For $x=ae_t$, put
$$ d=c_{tt},\qquad e=\mu_t e_t,\qquad M=U_t(\mu). $$

If $d>0$, define
$$ F(ae_t)= \begin{cases} \mu,&M>d\mu_t,\\ e,&M\leq d\mu_t. \end{cases} $$
In the first branch there are no positive terminal states on that axis, and
$$ M>d\mu_t\geq da $$
gives strict improvement at every axis state. In the second branch, $e$ is terminal and maximizes utility among the accessible terminal states; it strictly improves on every smaller positive axis state.

If $d\leq0$, define
$$ F(ae_t)= \begin{cases} \mu,&M>da,\\ ae_t,&M\leq da. \end{cases} $$
Every accessible axis-terminal state $ue_t$ has utility $du\leq da$. Therefore the first branch selects a strict maximizing improvement, and the second branch satisfies terminal stability.

These rules complete Case II.

3.5. Description length and bit costs

In Case III, the minimum defining $\lambda(x)$ has at most two branches. Comparing its two arguments is a linear predicate:
$$ v_2(\mu_1-x_1)\leq v_1(\mu_2-x_2). $$
On each branch, $F(x)$ is rational affine in $x$. The axis tests are also linear after substituting the selected affine expression for $p$.

Consequently, the graph is a Boolean combination of a constant number of linear predicates, with rational coefficients of bit length $O(L)$. Its length is $O(L)$.

Choosing $v$, writing the graph, and evaluating its branches require a constant number of arithmetic operations on rational numbers of bit length $O(L)$. Elementary integer arithmetic gives the stated quadratic bound. This proves Theorem 2. ∎

4. Polynomial constructions for two general coefficient regimes

These results hold for arbitrary $m=\tau$.

4.1. All coefficients nonpositive

The identity is a forecast exactly when
$$ c_{ts}\leq0\quad\text{for every }t,s. $$

Sufficiency follows from
$$ U_t(y)-U_t(x)=\sum_s c_{ts}(y_s-x_s)\leq0 \qquad(y\geq x). $$

For necessity, suppose $c_{ts}>0$. Positive masses allow choosing a state with $x_t>0$ and $x_s<\mu_s$, including when $s=t$. Increasing coordinate $s$ by a sufficiently small positive amount strictly improves the eligible sender’s utility, contradicting the identity forecast’s terminal condition.

4.2. All coefficients nonnegative

Suppose
$$ c_{ts}\geq0\quad\text{for every }t,s. $$
Form the directed graph with edge $t\to s$ exactly when $c_{ts}>0$. Let $R(S)$ consist of vertices reachable from $S$ by a path of positive length, including paths returning to their starting vertex. Define
$$ S(x)=\{t:x_t>0\}, $$
and
$$ F_j(x)= \begin{cases} \mu_j,&j\in R(S(x)),\\ x_j,&j\notin R(S(x)). \end{cases} $$

This map is extensive. Its positive support becomes $S(x)\cup R(S(x))$, whose positive-length reachability set is still $R(S(x))$. Thus it is idempotent.

Every eligible sender has every positive-coefficient target saturated by $F(x)$, so
$$ U_t(F(x))=\max_{w\in D}U_t(w). $$

If $F(x)\neq x$, some initially eligible sender must have an unsaturated positive-coefficient target. Indeed, otherwise every immediate target would already be saturated and, because its mass is positive, initially eligible. Induction along paths would then show that all reachable coordinates were already saturated, contradicting $F(x)\neq x$.

For that sender, nonnegativity of all coefficients makes the total utility gain strictly positive. This proves the transitory condition. The displayed global maximality proves the terminal condition.

After computing the positive-length transitive closure, reachability of coordinate $j$ is expressed by
$$ Q_j(x)=\bigvee_{t:\,t\leadsto j}(x_t>0). $$
The graph includes, for each $j$, the predicate
$$ (Q_j(x)\land w_j=\mu_j) \lor (\neg Q_j(x)\land w_j=x_j). $$

Its length satisfies
$$ N=O\bigl(L+m^2\log(m+1)\bigr). $$
A conservative construction-and-evaluation bound for these regimes is
$$ O\bigl(m^3+m^2(L+1)^2+N+H\bigr), $$
where $H=O(L)$ for the rational constructed value. Since $m=\tau$, the same bound holds with $\tau$ substituted for $m$.

5. A supplied forecast can have irrational values

Polynomial-time rational constructions in the preceding cases do not imply that every valid forecast has rational values.

Take
$$ \mu=(1,1),\qquad b=(1,1),\qquad A= \begin{pmatrix} 2&0\\ 0&2 \end{pmatrix}. $$
Then
$$ U_1(z)=z_1-z_2,\qquad U_2(z)=z_2-z_1. $$

Let
$$ T=\{(a,a):0\leq a\leq1\}\cup\{(1,0),(0,1)\}. $$
Fix every point of $T$, send positive nonterminal axis states to their corresponding axis endpoint, and send a positive unequal-coordinate state to any diagonal point above it.

This is a forecast. At positive unequal-coordinate states, accessible terminal points are diagonal and give both utilities zero. The sender with negative current utility strictly improves and maximizes. At positive axis states, the axis endpoint gives the eligible sender utility $1$, while diagonal terminals give zero. Diagonal terminal states satisfy the terminal comparisons, as do the axis endpoints; the origin has no eligible sender.

Let $\alpha$ be the unique positive root of
$$ 2\alpha^2=1. $$
At positive unequal-coordinate states, choose
$$ F(x)=(h,h),\qquad h=\max(x_1,x_2,\alpha). $$
This choice has a rational-coefficient quantifier-free graph. For example, on $x_1>x_2>0$, impose $w_1=w_2$ and
$$ (2x_1^2\geq1\land w_1=x_1) \lor (2x_1^2<1\land 2w_1^2=1\land w_1>0). $$
The opposite branch is symmetric.

At
$$ x^0=(1/4,1/2), $$
this forecast gives
$$ F(x^0)=(\alpha,\alpha). $$
Choosing $h=\max(x_1,x_2)$ instead gives a rational value on the same input.

Thus exact evaluation of a supplied forecast must allow algebraic outputs, and the construction rule matters when several forecasts exist.

6. Verification and exact evaluation: justified bit bounds

Let $N$ be the length of a supplied quantifier-free graph formula $G(x,w)$. For the quantitative bounds, polynomials are encoded explicitly by monomial lists with binary rational coefficients and binary nonnegative exponents.

There is an absolute constant $C_0$ such that verification, and exact evaluation of a valid description, can be performed within
$$ B(m,L,N)= 2^{(L+N+1)^{C_0}(m+1)^3} $$
bit operations. Evaluation outputs each coordinate as an integer polynomial together with a rational isolating interval; the bound includes writing that output.

6.1. Exact first-order verification

First verify:

  • graph membership implies $x,w\in D$;
  • every $x\in D$ has an output;
  • outputs are unique;
  • $G(x,w)$ implies $w\geq x$;
  • $G(x,w)\land G(w,v)$ implies $v=w$.

These conditions are first-order polynomial sentences.

The incentive check can be written with three quantifier blocks and only $O(m)$ variables. Define
$$ R(x,z,v)\equiv z\in D\land z\geq x\land G(z,v), $$
$$ E(x,v)\equiv \bigwedge_{t=1}^{m} \bigl(x_t>0\Longrightarrow U_t(v)\leq U_t(x)\bigr), $$
$$ Q(q,x,w)\equiv \bigvee_{t=1}^{m} \bigl(q=t\land x_t>0\land U_t(w)>U_t(x)\bigr), $$
and
$$ M(q,w,v)\equiv \bigwedge_{t=1}^{m} \bigl(q=t\Longrightarrow U_t(v)\leq U_t(w)\bigr). $$
Here the real variable $q$ selects a sender index. Verify the sentence
$$ \forall x,w\ \exists q\ \forall z,v: $$
$$ G(x,w)\Longrightarrow \left[ \begin{aligned} &\bigl(w=x\land(R(x,z,v)\Longrightarrow E(x,v))\bigr)\\ &{}\lor \bigl(w\neq x\land Q(q,x,w) \land(R(x,z,v)\Longrightarrow M(q,w,v))\bigr). \end{aligned} \right] $$
The selector precedes the universally quantified comparison states, so one sender must maximize against every accessible forecast output. After idempotence is established, the omitted witness is $y=w$.

Hence these sentences check exactly the task’s conditions.

6.2. Quantifier-elimination theorem and parameter accounting

The quantitative theorem used is Theorem 2.18 of [Basu’s survey](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final.pdf). For integer polynomial predicates, it bounds both ring-operation counts and intermediate integer bit sizes. Its displayed bounds imply this consequence: with at most three quantified blocks, block sizes at most $k$, and at most one free variable, quantifier elimination uses
$$ s^{O((k+1)^3)}d^{O((k+1)^3)} $$
ring operations, with intermediate integer bit sizes bounded by
$$ h\,d^{O((k+1)^3)}. $$
Here $s$ bounds the polynomial count, $d\geq2$ the degrees, and $h$ the initial coefficient bit sizes.

For verification, let $M_0=L+N+1$. The formulas above have polynomial length in $M_0$, at most three blocks, and block sizes $O(m)$. Clearing rational denominators gives
$$ s\leq M_0^{O(1)},\qquad d\leq2^{O(M_0)},\qquad h\leq M_0^{O(1)}. $$
The degree estimate follows from the binary exponent encoding. Dense expansion costs at most $2^{O(mM_0)}$ bit operations and is covered by $B$.

Applying the stated theorem and implementing integer operations in time polynomial in their bounded bit sizes yields $B$. A closed sentence can, if necessary, be treated as a formula with one unused free variable and evaluated at zero.

Since $m\leq L$, this is a deterministic exponential-time verification bound in the combined encoded input length.

6.3. Rational specialization and algebraic output

Assume $G$ is valid. The formula $G(x^0,w)$ defines a singleton.

Specializing at rational $x^0$ requires separate coefficient accounting. Let $d$ bound the original degrees, and let $Q$ be the product of the denominators of the coordinates of $x^0$. Its bit length is at most $L$. Multiplying specialized polynomials by $Q^d$ and their coefficient denominators produces integer polynomials with coefficient bit sizes bounded by
$$ h_{\mathrm{sp}}\leq M_0^{O(1)}+O(dL). $$
Thus specialization can create exponentially large coefficient bit sizes, but their logarithms are still $O(M_0)$. This cost is covered by $B$.

For each coordinate $j$, eliminate the other output coordinates to obtain a quantifier-free formula $\Psi_j(a)$ defining exactly
$$ \{F_j(x^0)\}. $$
This uses one existential block of at most $m-1$ variables and one free variable. The same theorem bounds the formula length, polynomial degrees, and coefficient bit sizes by $B$, after enlarging $C_0$.

The singleton’s coordinate is algebraic. Otherwise every nonzero rational polynomial occurring in $\Psi_j$ would be nonzero there. Their finitely many signs would remain constant on some neighborhood, so the formula would hold throughout that neighborhood, contradicting the singleton property.

For completeness, an isolating-interval representation can be extracted within the claimed bound:

  • Multiply the nonconstant, nonzero integer polynomials in $\Psi_j$. The product has the target coordinate as a root.
  • Isolate its distinct real roots.
  • Evaluate the predicates of $\Psi_j$ at those roots and retain the unique satisfying root.

The required precise univariate bounds are available in [Emiris–Tsigaridas, Theorem 19, Theorem 8, and Lemma 24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0512072). With degree bound $D$ and coefficient-bit bound $h\geq D$, real-root isolation, including nonsquare-free inputs, takes
$$ \widetilde O(D^4h^2) $$
bit operations and gives interval endpoints of bit size $O(Dh)$. Square-free-part computation and sign evaluation at isolated algebraic roots also have polynomial bit bounds in these parameters. The restriction $h\geq D$ is met by padding the declared bound to $\max(h,D)$.

If $\Psi_j$ contains $K$ relevant polynomials, each of degree at most $D$ and coefficient bit size at most $h$, their product has degree at most $KD$. Its coefficient bit size is at most
$$ O\bigl(K(h+\log(D+1))+1\bigr), $$
because coefficient $\ell^1$ norms multiply submultiplicatively. All these parameters, root-isolation costs, sign-evaluation costs, and written output lengths are covered by $B$. This completes the quantitative evaluation argument.

7. Construction on promised yes instances

There is an algorithm that constructs a description whenever one exists:

  • Enumerate finite binary descriptions in increasing length.
  • Reject invalid syntax.
  • Apply the verification algorithm.
  • Return the first valid description and evaluate it at $x^0$.

Let $N_*$ be the shortest valid description length. Fewer than $2^{N_*+1}$ strings are tested before success. Consequently, after enlarging $C_0$, construction and exact evaluation take at most
$$ B(m,L,N_*)= 2^{(L+N_*+1)^{C_0}(m+1)^3} $$
bit operations. The returned description has length $N_*$, so this is a bound in input and actual output-description length. Equivalently, replace $m$ by $\tau$.

This proves recursive enumerability of existence and an explicit construction bound on promised yes instances. It does not give a decision algorithm: no stopping rule for no instances has been established.

8. What remains open

The proved construction settles every input with $m=\tau\leq2$ and the two general sign regimes. It does not extend here to arbitrary mixed-coefficient matrices in larger dimension.

The finite backward-induction result processes states after their strict extensions have been handled. The continuous box has infinite strictly increasing chains, so that finite induction does not itself establish the required continuous existence or description-length bound. See the algorithm preceding Theorem 2 in [*Circles of Trust*](https://roketskiy.net/docs/circles_of_trust.pdf).

The unresolved issue is existence for arbitrary support size, or a characterization that identifies failures. Verification of any fixed description cannot settle that issue without a justified bound on the descriptions that must be searched.
