The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a common binary attribute set \(V\), outcomes \(x,y\in\{0,1\}^{V}\), a finite support \(\{(C_i,\mu_i)\}_{i=1}^{\tau}\) of complete GCP-net types \(C_i=(V,R_i)\) with positive binary-rational masses summing to \(1\), and a rational threshold \(q\), with \(\operatorname{diam}(C_i)\le p(|V|)\) for a fixed polynomial \(p\), compute \(M_\mu(y,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i\mathbf{1}[y\succ_{C_i}x]\), or decide whether \(M_\mu(y,x)\ge q\); incomparable pairs contribute zero.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=|V|\ge1\). A GCP-net \(C=(V,R)\) is supplied as an explicit list of rules \(\varphi:\ell>\neg\ell\), where \(\varphi\) is a possibly empty conjunction of literals excluding the variable of \(\ell\). An improving flip changes only that variable from \(\neg\ell\) to \(\ell\) when \(\varphi\) holds.
Write \(y\succ_C x\) when a positive-length sequence of improving flips leads from \(x\) to \(y\); cycles and conflicting rules are permitted. “Complete” means the entire rule list is supplied, without imposing local completeness or consistency.
The preference graph has outcomes \(\{0,1\}^{V}\) as vertices and improving flips as arcs. Its diameter is the maximum shortest directed-path distance, assigning distance zero to unreachable pairs and identical endpoints.
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\ge1\) GCP-net types with positive masses \(\mu_i\) encoded as binary rationals, with \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Fix a positive integer-valued polynomial \(p\). Inputs promise \(\operatorname{diam}(C_i)\le p(m)\) for every type; checking this promise is outside the problem. Given outcomes \(x,y\) and a binary-rational threshold \(q\in[0,1]\), define \(M_\mu(y,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i\mathbf{1}[y\succ_{C_i}x]\). Incomparable pairs contribute zero; mutual dominance contributes to each direction separately. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including all rules and rationals.

Is deciding whether \(M_\mu(y,x)\ge q\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, under the stated diameter promise? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), treating \(p\) as fixed.

Single-net dominance with polynomially bounded diameter is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete ([Kronegger, Lackner, Pfandler and Pichler](https://martin.lackner.xyz/publications/kr-minimal.pdf)).
No result for exactly this rational-mass threshold problem was identified in the search.

1. Classification

For every fixed nonconstant positive integer-valued polynomial \(p\), the promised rational-mass threshold problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already for \(\tau=1\), \(\mu_1=1\), and \(q=1\).

If constant polynomials are allowed, there is an exception: for every fixed constant \(p(m)=K\), the problem is polynomial-time solvable. The intended polynomially growing diameter bound therefore gives \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness, and a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The cited single-net result is Theorem 5 of [Kronegger, Lackner, Pfandler and Pichler](https://martin.lackner.xyz/publications/kr-minimal.pdf). We give a direct reduction below to establish hardness for every fixed nonconstant \(p\), including bounds with small leading coefficients.

All claims concern promised inputs; no algorithm checks the diameter promise.

2. Short witnesses, including identical endpoints

Set \(H=p(m)+1\).

If \(x\ne y\) and \(y\succ_C x\), the diameter promise supplies an improving path from \(x\) to \(y\) of length at most \(p(m)\).

If \(x=y\), positive-length dominance requires a closed improving walk. Choose its first arc \(x\to z\). A flip changes a variable, so \(z\ne x\). Since \(x\) is reachable from \(z\), the promise supplies a path from \(z\) to \(x\) of length at most \(p(m)\). Prepending \(x\to z\) gives a positive-length witness of length at most \(H\).

Thus, for either choice of endpoints, every true dominance assertion has a witness containing between \(1\) and \(H\) flips.

3. The threshold problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate specifies a subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) and, for each \(i\in S\), an improving sequence from \(x\) to \(y\) of length between \(1\) and \(H\). The verifier checks all sequences and accepts exactly when \(\sum_{i\in S}\mu_i\ge q\).

A sequence can be encoded by its flipped-variable indices. Starting from \(x\), the verifier determines each flip’s direction and scans the supplied rules to check that some applicable rule sanctions it.

Soundness. Every verified type contributes its mass to \(M_\mu(y,x)\). Because all masses are positive, \(M_\mu(y,x)\ge\sum_{i\in S}\mu_i\ge q\).

Completeness. If \(M_\mu(y,x)\ge q\), choose \(S\) to contain all types satisfying \(y\succ_{C_i}x\). Section 2 provides the required short witnesses. If \(q=0\), the empty subset suffices.

No certificates of non-dominance are needed.

Exact rational arithmetic

Write \(\mu_i=a_i/b_i\) and \(q=c/d\), with positive denominators. Define \(D=d\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i\). The comparison is equivalent to the integer inequality

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i\in S}a_i(D/b_i)\ge c(D/d).\)

The bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\), because the sum of the encoded denominator lengths is at most \(L\). Every scaled mass and threshold lies between \(0\) and \(D\), and the selected masses sum to at most \(1\). Hence the calculation uses integers of \(O(L)\) bits. Schoolbook multiplication and division give an \(O(L^3)\) arithmetic bound.

The certificate length is \(O(\tau H\log(m+1))\). A conservative verification-time bound is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(\tau H(m+L)^2+L^3\right).\)

Since \(p\) is fixed and \(m,\tau\le L\), these bounds are polynomial in the input length.

4. A reduction with a globally bounded diameter

We reduce from \(\mathrm{3SAT}\). Let \(F\) have variables \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) and clauses \(B_1,\ldots,B_s\).

Introduce clause variables \(c_1,\ldots,c_s\) and two gate variables \(f,g\). The net has \(m_0=n+s+2\) variables and exactly the following rules:

  • For each assignment variable \(a_j\):

\(\neg f\land\neg g:a_j>\neg a_j\).

  • An unconditional rule:

\(\top:f>\neg f\).

  • For each literal \(\lambda\) occurring in clause \(B_k\):

\(f\land\neg g\land\lambda:c_k>\neg c_k\).

  • A rule recording that every clause has been certified:

\(f\land c_1\land\cdots\land c_s:g>\neg g\).

  • For every \(v\in\{a_1,\ldots,a_n,c_1,\ldots,c_s\}\):

\(g:\neg v>v\).

Here \(\top\) denotes the empty conjunction. Every condition excludes its effect variable, as required. The rule list has polynomial encoded size.

Let \(x\) set every variable to \(0\). Let \(y\) set \(f=g=1\) and every assignment and clause variable to \(0\).

4.1. Correctness

Suppose \(F\) has a satisfying assignment. While \(f=g=0\), set precisely its true assignment variables to \(1\). Set \(f=1\). For each clause, a true literal enables a rule setting its clause variable to \(1\). Once every \(c_k=1\), set \(g=1\). Finally, use the cleanup rules to reset every assignment and clause variable to \(0\). This reaches \(y\), so \(y\succ_C x\).

Conversely, suppose an improving sequence reaches \(y\) from \(x\). Both \(f\) and \(g\) can change only from \(0\) to \(1\). The flip setting \(g=1\) requires \(f=1\) and every \(c_k=1\).

Between the flip setting \(f=1\) and the flip setting \(g=1\), assignment variables cannot change: their upward rules require \(f=0\), and their downward rules require \(g=1\). They therefore represent one fixed assignment throughout this interval.

Every clause variable starts at \(0\), and its upward rules require \(f=1\). For \(c_k\) to be \(1\) when \(g\) is set, a literal of \(B_k\) must have been true under that fixed assignment. Consequently every clause is satisfied, and \(F\) is satisfiable.

Thus \(F\) is satisfiable if and only if \(y\succ_C x\).

4.2. Diameter bound for all outcomes

Consider any improving sequence, starting at an arbitrary outcome.

  • Each gate variable changes at most once.
  • Each assignment or clause variable can change upward only while \(g=0\).
  • Such a variable can change downward only while \(g=1\).
  • Since \(g\) never decreases, each assignment or clause variable changes at most twice.

Every improving sequence therefore has length at most \(2(n+s)+2=2m_0-2\). In particular, \(\operatorname{diam}(C)\le 2m_0-2\). This bound holds globally, independently of whether \(F\) is satisfiable.

4.3. Meeting any fixed nonconstant polynomial bound

Because \(p\) is nonconstant and positive on all positive integers, its leading coefficient is positive. There are constants \(b>0\) and \(t_0\), depending only on \(p\), such that \(p(t)\ge bt\) for every integer \(t\ge t_0\).

Choose

\(\displaystyle m=\max\!\left\{m_0,t_0,\left\lceil 2m_0/b\right\rceil\right\}.\)

Add \(m-m_0\) variables appearing in no rule, and set them to \(0\) in both endpoints. These variables never change. The enlarged preference graph consists of disjoint copies of the original graph, so its diameter is unchanged, and dominance between the chosen endpoints is unchanged.

Now \(\operatorname{diam}(C)\le 2m_0-2\le p(m)\). Also \(m=O(m_0)\), with constants depending only on the fixed \(p\), so the padding is polynomial.

Finally, use the singleton society \(\tau=1\), \(\mu_1=1\), with \(q=1\). Then

\(\displaystyle M_\mu(y,x)\ge1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y\succ_C x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F\text{ is satisfiable}.\)

The reduction always produces promised inputs. Together with Section 3, it proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for every fixed nonconstant \(p\).

5. Deterministic running times and constant bounds

For each type, enumerate all variable-index sequences of lengths \(1,\ldots,H\), simulate their flips, and test whether any valid sequence reaches \(y\). Section 2 proves this decides dominance, including when \(x=y\).

There are at most \(\sum_{j=1}^{H}m^j\le(m+1)^H\) candidates per type. After determining all dominance indicators, sum their masses exactly. A conservative deterministic bound is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(\tau H(m+1)^H(m+L)^2+L^3\right),\qquad H=p(m)+1.\)

For the constant polynomial \(p(m)=K\), this becomes

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(\tau(m+1)^{K+1}(m+L)^2+L^3\right),\)

where the hidden constant may depend on \(K\). This is polynomial in \(L\).

Hence the classification is complete: constant diameter bounds give polynomial-time solvability; every fixed nonconstant polynomial diameter bound gives \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.
