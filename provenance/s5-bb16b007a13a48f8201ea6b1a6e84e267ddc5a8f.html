The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given item classes \(G\), finitely supported valuation types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and additive valuations \(v_t(g)\), determine whether a finite rational distribution \(x_{t,b}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{G}\) exists that satisfies \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\), and continuous \((\phi-1)\)-GMMS; if it exists, compute one in time polynomial in \(|G|\), \(\tau\), and the input and output bit lengths.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) be a nonempty set of item classes, with \(m=|G|\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) valuation types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Thus \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Supplies \(q_g\) and additive values \(v_t(g)\) are nonnegative binary rationals; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation is a finitely supported rational array \(x_{t,b}\geq0\), indexed by integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{G}\), satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\). Empty bundles are allowed; write \(v_t(b)=\sum_gv_t(g)b_g\).
A coalition is any real array \(0\leq y_{t,b}\leq x_{t,b}\) with mass \(s=\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}>0\) and pooled supplies \(Q_g=\sum_{t,b}y_{t,b}b_g\).
Define its continuous maximin benchmark by \(\operatorname{MMS}_t(s,Q)=\sup_z\min_{c:z_c>0}v_t(c)\), where \(z\) ranges over finitely supported nonnegative real bundle masses satisfying \(\sum_cz_c=s\), \(\sum_cz_cc_g=Q_g\), and \(c\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{G}\).
The allocation is continuous \(\alpha\)-GMMS if every coalition satisfies \(v_t(b)\geq\alpha\operatorname{MMS}_t(s,Q)\) for every \((t,b)\) with \(y_{t,b}>0\). Set \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\) and \(\alpha=\phi-1\).

At the fixed guarantee \(\alpha=\phi-1\), is feasibility decidable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with a feasible allocation constructible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,L_{\mathrm{out}}\), where \(L_{\mathrm{out}}\) is its explicit binary encoding length, or is this approximation feasibility problem NP-hard?

For finite agents with nonnegative additive valuations, complete \((\phi-1)\)-GMMS allocations always exist and are computable in polynomial time ([Amanatidis et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04731v2)); an earlier polynomial algorithm guarantees \(4/7\)-GMMS ([Amanatidis et al., 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/5545/5401)).
No result settling this precisely defined continuous problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized society mass to one, so the stated per-capita supplies are also total supplies.
  • Specified nonnegative binary rational valuations and supplies, carrying over the goods domain; zero values, zero supplies, and empty bundles remain allowed.
  • Defined coalitions as arbitrary positive submasses of the allocated type-bundle cells, allowing coalitions to distinguish recipients of different bundles within a type.
  • Replaced the discrete number of coalition members by coalition mass, and defined the maximin benchmark through finite real mass distributions over integral bundles conserving the coalition's pooled supplies.
  • Required the fairness inequality for every type-bundle cell present with positive coalition mass, using weak inequalities.
  • Defined output length using the explicit finite list of positive rational allocation masses and their integral bundles.

Partial resolution: finite construction, short violation certificates, and polynomial special cases

Every valid instance is feasible. Thus the decision component is polynomial-time solvable: on valid inputs, the answer is always yes.

The unresolved part is constructing an allocation in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,L_{\mathrm{out}}$. Below I prove an explicit finite construction with exponential running time, strengthen the violation-witness argument to a polynomial certificate-size bound, and give a polynomial construction of exact continuous GMMS allocations for a nontrivial special case. I also prove hardness of computing the benchmark exactly, while distinguishing that problem from allocation search.

1. Normalization and dependence on support

Write
$$ \alpha=\phi-1=\frac1\phi. $$

For any $s>0$ and $Q\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^G$,
$$ \operatorname{MMS}_t(s,Q)=\operatorname{MMS}_t(1,Q/s). $$
Indeed, dividing every redistribution mass by $s$ preserves its supported bundles and their values, and gives a bijection between the two sets of admissible redistributions.

The benchmark is finite. Every admissible redistribution satisfies
$$ \min_{c:z_c>0}v_t(c) \leq \frac{\sum_cz_cv_t(c)}{s} = \frac{v_t(Q)}s. $$
Admissible redistributions exist: independently mixing floor and ceiling counts in each coordinate realizes any prescribed nonnegative mean vector with finite support.

Let $S$ be the positive, type-labelled support of an allocation, and write its atoms as $a=(t_a,b_a)$. Normalizing a coalition gives weights
$$ \lambda_a\geq0,\qquad \sum_{a\in S}\lambda_a=1, \qquad Q/s=\sum_{a\in S}\lambda_ab_a. $$
Conversely, any such weights are realizable by a coalition. Choose
$$ 0<s\leq \min_{\lambda_a>0}\frac{x_a}{\lambda_a} $$
and set $y_a=s\lambda_a$.

Consequently, continuous fairness depends only on the positive, type-labelled support. Changing positive masses preserves fairness; deleting support atoms also preserves fairness, provided the new masses satisfy the allocation equations.

2. A bounded integer certificate for every violation

The following strengthens the qualitative rational-witness lemma in the notes.

Proposition 1: bounded violation certificates

Suppose the allocated bundles satisfy
$$ 0\leq b_{a,g}\leq B_g $$
for integers $B_g\geq0$. Define
$$ U_t=\sum_gv_t(g)B_g, \qquad M_t=\max_gv_t(g), $$
and the integer
$$ J= \max\left( \{1\}\cup\{B_g:g\in G\} \cup \left\{ \left\lfloor \frac{2U_t+M_t}{v_t(g)} \right\rfloor: v_t(g)>0 \right\} \right). $$
Put
$$ r=m+2,\qquad W=r!J^r. $$

If an allocation violates continuous $\alpha$-GMMS, then there are:

  • a focal atom $a_0=(t,b)$;
  • nonnegative integer multiplicities $n_a$ of allocated atoms, with $n_{a_0}>0$;
  • nonnegative integer multiplicities $k_c$ of redistribution bundles;

such that
$$ 1\leq K=\sum_an_a=\sum_ck_c\leq W, $$
$$ \sum_an_ab_a=\sum_ck_cc, $$
and every redistribution bundle of positive multiplicity satisfies
$$ v_t(c)>\phi v_t(b). $$

At most $r$ allocated atoms and at most $r+1$ redistribution bundles are needed.

Proof

Let $u=v_t(b)$. A violation means
$$ \operatorname{MMS}_t(s,Q)>\phi u. $$
By the definition of supremum, some admissible finite redistribution has every supported bundle valued strictly above $\phi u$.

Normalize the coalition and this redistribution to mass one.

For every high-valued bundle $d$, repeatedly remove items while preserving value strictly above $\phi u$. This process terminates at a componentwise minimal bundle $c\leq d$ with
$$ v_t(c)>\phi u. $$
Such a bundle contains no zero-valued item. Whenever $c_g>0$, minimality gives
$$ v_t(c)-v_t(g)\leq\phi u. $$
It follows that
$$ v_t(c)\leq\phi u+M_t\leq2U_t+M_t, $$
because $u\leq U_t$ and $\phi<2$. Thus every coordinate of $c$ is at most $J$.

Let $C$ be the finite set of all these possible minimal high-valued bundles. Replacing each original redistribution bundle by a dominated member of $C$ leaves a nonnegative surplus mean $h$. Therefore the following linear constraints have a solution with $\lambda_{a_0}>0$:
$$ \lambda_a,\theta_c,h_g\geq0, $$
$$ \sum_a\lambda_a=1,\qquad \sum_{c\in C}\theta_c=1, $$
$$ \sum_a\lambda_ab_a-\sum_{c\in C}\theta_cc-h=0. $$

This feasible region is bounded: the normalized weights lie in simplices, and
$$ 0\leq h_g\leq B_g. $$
Maximize $\lambda_{a_0}$ and take an optimal vertex. Its objective is positive.

There are $r=m+2$ equality rows. At a vertex, the columns corresponding to positive variables are linearly independent; otherwise a sufficiently small perturbation in both directions would preserve feasibility. Hence at most $r$ variables are positive.

Select an invertible square submatrix determining these positive variables. Its dimension is at most $r$, its entries are integers of absolute value at most $J$, and its right-hand side is integral. By Cramer’s rule, the positive variables have a common denominator
$$ K\leq r!J^r=W. $$
Multiplying by $K$ yields integer multiplicities
$$ n_a=K\lambda_a,\qquad k_c=K\theta_c $$
and an integral surplus vector $Kh$.

There are exactly $K$ base redistribution bundles. Add the entire surplus vector $Kh$ to one of them. This preserves the total number of bundles, gives the exact supply identity, and preserves every strict value inequality by nonnegativity of valuations. Modifying one copy adds at most one distinct redistribution bundle to the support. This proves the proposition. $\square$

Corollary 1: fairness verification belongs to $\mathrm{coNP}$

Given an explicitly encoded rational allocation, a violation has a certificate of length polynomial in $m,\tau,L,L_{\mathrm{out}}$.

To see this, take $B_g$ to be the largest allocated count in coordinate $g$. The binary lengths of $J$ and $W$ above are polynomial in the input and allocation lengths. Moreover, every final redistribution bundle satisfies
$$ c_g\leq KB_g, $$
because the total pooled count in coordinate $g$ is at most $KB_g$. All certificate integers therefore have polynomial binary length.

The verifier checks the two mass identities, the supply identities, positive focal multiplicity, and the strict value inequalities. For nonnegative rational $a,u$,
$$ a>\phi u \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a^2-au-u^2>0. $$
Thus exact verification uses only rational arithmetic.

Conversely, an accepted certificate is a genuine continuous violation: normalize its multiplicities by $K$, then realize those normalized coalition weights using Section 1. Hence the certificate characterization is exact.

This is a verification result, not an efficient allocation-search algorithm.

3. Uniform bounds from the finite-agent theorem

For now, assume every item class is positively valued by at least one type. Universally worthless classes are restored in Section 5.

I use the following external theorem: every finite instance of indivisible goods with nonnegative additive valuations admits a complete $\alpha$-GMMS allocation computable in polynomial time. This is [Theorem 2 of Amanatidis et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04731v2). Repeated valuation functions and interchangeable copies of an item class satisfy its hypotheses.

Let $D$ be a positive integer clearing all denominators of $\mu_t$ and $q_g$. For any positive multiple $N$ of $D$, form a finite instance with
$$ N\mu_t\text{ agents of type }t, \qquad Nq_g\text{ copies of class }g. $$

We next bound every allocated bundle independently of $N$.

Lemma 1: a two-bundle bound

If a multiset of items has total value $V$ and every item has value at most $M$, it can be partitioned into two bundles whose minimum value is at least
$$ \frac{V-M}{2}. $$

Proof. Assign each successive item to a currently lower-valued bundle. If the previous difference is $d\leq M$ and the next item has value $w\leq M$, the new difference is $|d-w|\leq M$. Thus the final difference is at most $M$, which proves the bound. $\square$

For each type define
$$ A_t=\frac{v_t(q)}{\mu_t}, \qquad M_t=\max_gv_t(g). $$
In a complete finite $\alpha$-GMMS allocation, choose a minimum-value type-$t$ agent $i$, and write $u_t=v_t(B_i)$. Since $\mu_t>0$, such an agent exists. Nonnegativity and completeness give
$$ u_t \leq \frac{\sum_{j:\operatorname{type}(j)=t}v_t(B_j)}{N\mu_t} \leq \frac{Nv_t(q)}{N\mu_t} =A_t. $$

For any distinct agent $j$, apply GMMS to the coalition $\{i,j\}$ and use Lemma 1:
$$ u_t\geq \alpha\frac{u_t+v_t(B_j)-M_t}{2}. $$
Rearranging,
$$ v_t(B_j) \leq \left(\frac2\alpha-1\right)u_t+M_t = \sqrt5\,u_t+M_t \leq3A_t+M_t. $$
The same final bound holds for $j=i$ because $u_t\leq A_t$.

Consequently, every allocated bundle, regardless of its recipient, satisfies
$$ v_t(b)\leq3A_t+M_t \qquad\text{for every type }t. $$
Define
$$ B_g= \min_{t:v_t(g)>0} \left\lfloor \frac{3A_t+M_t}{v_t(g)} \right\rfloor. $$
The minimum is over a nonempty set. Since valuations are nonnegative,
$$ b_gv_t(g)\leq v_t(b), $$
so every finite allocated bundle satisfies $b_g\leq B_g$.

All $B_g$ have polynomial binary length: their definitions use rational sums, products, divisions and floors on the binary input.

4. One explicitly bounded replication suffices

The notes established existence through a convergent subsequence. The next proposition replaces that limiting argument by a single finite replication.

Let $J,W$ be the quantities in Proposition 1, using the bounds from Section 3. Define
$$ R=\tau\prod_g(B_g+1), $$
$$ d=m+\tau,\qquad H=\max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{B_g:g\in G\}\bigr), \qquad T=d!H^d. $$
There are at most $R$ possible type-labelled allocated atoms.

Choose the replication factor
$$ N=D\bigl(RW\,dH\,T+1\bigr). $$
This is a multiple of $D$ and has polynomial binary length.

Compute a complete finite $\alpha$-GMMS allocation. Let $n_a$ be the number of agents receiving atom $a$. Call an atom retained if
$$ n_a\geq W. $$

Proposition 2: the retained support is continuously fair and can realize the original marginals

Proof of fairness

Suppose an allocation supported on the retained atoms had a continuous violation. Proposition 1 would give an integer coalition of size at most $W$.

Each required atom occurs at least $W$ times in the finite allocation. Hence we can select exactly the certificate’s required agents. The certificate supplies a repartition of their pooled items into exactly as many bundles as selected agents, all worth strictly more than $\phi$ times the focal agent’s allocated value.

Class-count identities suffice to repartition the actual distinct item copies, because valuations depend only on their classes. This contradicts finite $\alpha$-GMMS. Therefore every allocation supported on retained atoms is continuously fair.

Proof that the marginals remain realizable

For an atom $a=(t,b)$ define its integer column
$$ a^\sharp=(e_t,b)\in\mathbb Z^{\tau+m}, $$
where $e_t$ is the type indicator vector. Every coordinate of $a^\sharp$ has absolute value at most $H$. Let
$$ p=(\mu,q). $$
The finite allocation gives
$$ p=\sum_a\frac{n_a}{N}a^\sharp. $$

Let $\varepsilon$ be the total mass of discarded atoms. There are at most $R$ atoms, each discarded atom has fewer than $W$ agents, and therefore
$$ \varepsilon\leq\frac{RW}{N}. $$

Suppose $p$ does not belong to the cone generated by retained columns. By Farkas’ lemma, there exists $w\in\mathbb R^d$ with
$$ w\cdot a^\sharp\geq0 \quad\text{for every retained atom}, \qquad w\cdot p<0. $$
Scale $w$ into the box $[-1,1]^d$, and minimize $w\cdot p$ over
$$ P= \left\{ w\in[-1,1]^d: w\cdot a^\sharp\geq0 \text{ for all retained atoms} \right\}. $$
This is a nonempty bounded rational polytope, and its minimum is negative. Choose a minimizing vertex.

At this vertex, $d$ independent active constraints determine $w$. Their integer coefficients have absolute value at most $H$, so their determinant has absolute value at most $T=d!H^d$. Since $Dp$ is integral, Cramer’s rule implies
$$ w\cdot p\leq-\frac1{DT}. $$

On the other hand, retained atoms contribute nonnegatively, while for every discarded atom
$$ w\cdot a^\sharp\geq-dH. $$
Thus
$$ w\cdot p\geq-\varepsilon dH \geq-\frac{RW\,dH}{N} >-\frac1{DT}, $$
where the last inequality follows from the definition of $N$. This is a contradiction.

Therefore $p$ lies in the cone generated by retained columns. Its nonnegative coefficients automatically sum to one: summing the type coordinates of each column gives one, and $\sum_t\mu_t=1$. Those coefficients are exactly an allocation satisfying the original type masses and supplies. $\square$

The resulting construction requires no fairness-testing oracle:

  • Compute the specified finite replication.
  • Obtain a finite $\alpha$-GMMS allocation.
  • Retain atoms occurring at least $W$ times.
  • Solve the allocation equations on retained atoms and take a vertex solution.

5. Rationality, encoding length, and running time

The support-restricted allocation polytope is nonempty by Proposition 2. Its variables are nonnegative and sum to one, so it is bounded.

A vertex has at most $m+\tau$ positive variables. Indeed, if its positive columns were dependent, a nonzero sufficiently small perturbation in both directions would preserve the allocation equations and nonnegativity.

The vertex masses are rational. A nonsingular square submatrix of dimension at most $m+\tau$ determines them. Its integer entries have absolute value at most $H$, and the right-hand-side denominators divide $D$. Cramer’s rule therefore bounds the mass denominators by
$$ D(m+\tau)!H^{m+\tau}. $$
Since each mass lies in $[0,1]$, its numerator also has polynomial binary length. Bundle coordinates have polynomial binary length by Section 3.

Thus the construction produces an allocation with polynomial total encoding length.

Restoring universally worthless classes

Deleting classes valued zero by every type leaves all benchmarks unchanged.

Projection of a full redistribution preserves values and gives an admissible projected redistribution. Conversely, any projected redistribution can be lifted by adding counts in the deleted coordinates with their required means. For each such coordinate, mix its floor and ceiling counts. A common real parameter in $[0,1)$, with thresholds at the fractional parts, realizes all these means using at most one more bundle pattern than the number of deleted classes. Values are unchanged.

Apply the same lifting to the constructed allocation. Every coalition projects to a coalition of the reduced allocation, and its benchmark agrees with the projected benchmark. Hence fairness is preserved.

Finally, take a vertex of the full support-restricted allocation polytope. Support deletion preserves fairness, and the same determinant argument gives at most $m+\tau$ positive entries of polynomial total encoding length. If all classes are universally worthless, the lifting construction alone suffices.

Computational conclusion

The quantities $D,R,H,J,W,T,N$ all have polynomial binary length. Consequently, the expanded finite instance has size
$$ 2^{\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)}. $$
The finite theorem and the subsequent rational linear program give an exact construction within that exponential bound.

This proves, without compactness:

  • universal rational feasibility;
  • a feasible allocation with at most $m+\tau$ positive entries and polynomial encoding length;
  • an explicit exponential-time construction.

It does not prove the requested polynomial construction bound. The constructed allocation has polynomial encoding length, while the expanded replication may have exponential size.

6. Polynomial construction of exact GMMS for uniform class values

Assume that each type values all classes equally:
$$ v_t(g)=a_t \qquad\text{for every }g\in G, $$
where $a_t\geq0$ may depend on the type. This includes every instance with $m=1$.

I first prove a rounding fact for arbitrary nonnegative real means.

Lemma 2: preserve coordinate means and round the total count

For any $r\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m$, there is a finite distribution of integral bundles with mean $r$, supported on bundles whose total counts belong to
$$ \left\{ \left\lfloor\sum_gr_g\right\rfloor, \left\lceil\sum_gr_g\right\rceil \right\}. $$
If $r$ is rational, such a distribution with at most $m+1$ supported bundles is computable in polynomial time.

Proof

Write
$$ r_i=\ell_i+f_i,\qquad \ell_i=\lfloor r_i\rfloor,\qquad 0\leq f_i<1, $$
and define
$$ F_0=0,\qquad F_i=\sum_{j=1}^if_j. $$

For $U\in[0,1)$ define
$$ e_i(U)= \#\left\{ k\in\mathbb Z: U+k\in[F_{i-1},F_i) \right\}. $$
Each interval has length less than one, so $e_i(U)\in\{0,1\}$. Uniformly sampling $U$ gives
$$ \mathbb E[e_i(U)]=f_i. $$
This identity follows by integrating the translated interval indicators over $[0,1)$; the translates tile the real line.

Also,
$$ \sum_ie_i(U) = \#\{k\in\mathbb Z:U+k\in[0,F_m)\}, $$
which is either $\lfloor F_m\rfloor$ or $\lceil F_m\rceil$. Therefore
$$ b_i(U)=\ell_i+e_i(U) $$
has mean $r$ and the required total count.

The bundle changes only at fractional parts of the endpoints $F_i$. These give at most $m+1$ intervals of constancy. Assign to each interval its length as probability and choose any interior representative.

For rational $r$, all endpoints, probabilities and representatives are rational of polynomial binary length. Sorting the endpoints and computing the bundles takes polynomial time. $\square$

Proposition 3: exact benchmark formula

Under uniform class values,
$$ \operatorname{MMS}_t(s,Q) = a_t\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_gQ_g}{s}\right\rfloor. $$

Proof. If $a_t=0$, both sides are zero. Otherwise, bundle value is $a_t$ times its integral total count. In any redistribution, the minimum supported total count is at most the floor of the mean total count. This proves the upper bound.

Apply Lemma 2 to $r=Q/s$. It realizes the prescribed coordinate means using only the floor and ceiling total counts, attaining the upper bound. $\square$

Exact allocation and fairness proof

Apply Lemma 2 to the rational supply vector $q$. Let its bundle probabilities be $p_b$, and assign
$$ x_{t,b}=\mu_tp_b. $$
This satisfies every allocation equation and has at most $\tau(m+1)$ positive entries, all of polynomial binary length.

Put
$$ K=\left\lfloor\sum_gq_g\right\rfloor. $$
Every allocated bundle has total count $K$ or $K+1$.

Consider any coalition and any participating focal bundle.

If the focal bundle has count $K$, its normalized coalition weight is positive. Since every other bundle has count at most $K+1$, the coalition’s mean total count is strictly below $K+1$. Proposition 3 gives
$$ \operatorname{MMS}_t(s,Q)\leq a_tK=v_t(b). $$

If the focal bundle has count $K+1$, the coalition’s mean total count is at most $K+1$, giving
$$ \operatorname{MMS}_t(s,Q)\leq a_t(K+1)=v_t(b). $$

Thus this allocation is continuous $1$-GMMS. It is constructible in polynomial time, proving the requested construction bound for this special case.

7. Exact benchmark computation remains hard

The continuous redistribution model does not make exact benchmark computation easy.

Proposition 4: exact benchmark evaluation is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard

Even with one valuation type, positive integral values, and every class supply equal to $1/2$, deciding whether
$$ \operatorname{MMS}(1,q)=v(q) $$
is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard.

Proof

Reduce from the standard binary PARTITION problem: given positive integers $w_1,\ldots,w_n$, decide whether some subset sums to half their total. Its $\mathrm{NP}$-completeness is established in [Karp’s original paper, reprinted here](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf). We may assume the total
$$ W_0=\sum_iw_i $$
is even: doubling all input integers enforces this without changing the answer.

Set
$$ B=2n(W_0+1)+2,\qquad H=B^{n+1}. $$
For each index $i$, introduce two classes with values
$$ v(g_i)=H+B^i+w_i, \qquad v(h_i)=H+B^i. $$
Give every class supply $1/2$. The mean value is the integer
$$ T=v(q)=nH+\sum_{i=1}^nB^i+\frac{W_0}{2}. $$
All numbers have polynomial binary length.

I claim that an integral bundle has value exactly $T$ if and only if it chooses exactly one class from each pair and its chosen $g_i$ classes correspond to a partition.

Let its counts be $c_i$ for $g_i$ and $d_i$ for $h_i$, and put
$$ \ell=\sum_i(c_i+d_i). $$
We have
$$ n(B^n+W_0)<H, $$
because $B>n+nW_0$. Also
$$ \sum_iB^i+\frac{W_0}{2}<H, $$
so $T<(n+1)H$.

Every item has value at least $H$. Therefore a value-$T$ bundle has $\ell\leq n$. If $\ell\leq n-1$, its value is at most
$$ (n-1)H+n(B^n+W_0)<nH\leq T, $$
a contradiction. Hence $\ell=n$.

Subtracting $nH$ from the value equation gives
$$ \sum_i(c_i+d_i)B^i+\sum_ic_iw_i = \sum_iB^i+\frac{W_0}{2}. $$
Here
$$ c_i+d_i\leq n<B, \qquad \sum_ic_iw_i\leq nW_0<B. $$
Thus there are no carries in this base-$B$ representation. Uniqueness of its digits implies
$$ c_i+d_i=1\quad\text{for every }i, \qquad \sum_ic_iw_i=\frac{W_0}{2}. $$
This proves the claim.

If a partition exists, choose one bundle accordingly and a second bundle using the complementary choice in every pair. Both have value $T$, and assigning each mass $1/2$ realizes $q$. The average-value upper bound then gives
$$ \operatorname{MMS}(1,q)=T. $$

If no partition exists, no integral bundle has value $T$. Every bundle value is integral. A redistribution whose minimum value exceeded $T-1$ would therefore have every supported value at least $T$. Its average value is exactly $T$, forcing every supported value to equal $T$, which is impossible. Hence
$$ \operatorname{MMS}(1,q)\leq T-1. $$
The reduction follows. $\square$

This hardness result concerns exact benchmark evaluation. It does not imply hardness of finding an $\alpha$-GMMS allocation: universal feasibility and a possible constructive algorithm can coexist with hard benchmark evaluation.
