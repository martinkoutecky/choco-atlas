Result

For every fixed rational \(\alpha\in[0,1)\), deciding clearing existence in the specified mass model is \(\exists\mathbb{R}\)-complete, and therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Hardness already holds when every mass equals \(1/\tau\).

An exact decision algorithm runs in \((\tau+1)^{O(\tau)}L^{O(1)}\) bit operations. Thus the problem is polynomial-time solvable for each fixed support size, but a polynomial-time algorithm for unrestricted support size would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Exact correspondence with a finite-bank network

Fix a valid mass-model instance. Construct a finite network with one bank for each \(t\in T\), external assets and notionals

\[ \widehat e_t=\mu_t e_t,\qquad \widehat d_{st}=\mu_s d_{st},\qquad \widehat h_{st\mid v}=\mu_s h_{st\mid v}. \]

Here and below these formulas use the given per-writer interpretation of the schedules.

For any recovery vector \(r\in[0,1]^T\), the finite network’s liability from bank \(s\) to bank \(t\) is

\[ \widehat q_{st}(r) =\widehat d_{st}+\sum_v(1-r_v)\widehat h_{st\mid v} =\mu_s q_{st}(r). \]

Consequently, its total liabilities and incoming payments satisfy

\[ \widehat L_t(r)=\mu_t L_t(r), \qquad \widehat I_t(r) =\sum_s r_s\widehat q_{st}(r) =\sum_s\mu_s r_s q_{st}(r) =\mu_t I_t(r). \]

Its assets before and after default costs therefore satisfy

\[ \widehat A_t(r)=\mu_t A_t(r), \qquad \widehat A'_t(r)=\mu_t A'_t(r), \]

where both models use the same \(\alpha\) and \(\beta=1\).

Since \(\mu_t>0\), we have

\[ \widehat A_t(r)\geq\widehat L_t(r) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A_t(r)\geq L_t(r). \]

On the default branch, \(A_t(r)<L_t(r)\) implies \(L_t(r)>0\), because assets are nonnegative. Hence

\[ \frac{\widehat A'_t(r)}{\widehat L_t(r)} =\frac{\mu_t A'_t(r)}{\mu_t L_t(r)} =\frac{A'_t(r)}{L_t(r)}. \]

These identities prove that the two instances have exactly the same clearing vectors. They also preserve equality on the solvent branch and zero liabilities.

All required restrictions survive the construction. Multiplication by positive masses preserves nonnegativity and all forbidden zero entries. Furthermore,

\[ \widehat e_t>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e_t>0, \qquad \sum_u\widehat d_{tu}>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_u d_{tu}>0. \]

Thus nondegeneracy is preserved. The construction uses exactly \(\tau\) banks and has polynomial bit complexity: multiplying binary rationals increases their encoding lengths by at most the sum of the operand lengths.

2. Hardness: equal masses reproduce individual banks

We use the supplied discrete theorem precisely as follows: for every fixed rational \(\alpha<1\), exact clearing existence in nondegenerate finite networks with \(\beta=1\) is \(\exists\mathbb{R}\)-complete. Its contract restrictions, nondegeneracy condition, and clearing branches agree with those in this question. This is Theorem 3 of [Dohn, Hansen, and Klinkby](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18717#S4).

Given such a finite network with \(\tau\) banks, create one type per bank and set

\[ \mu_t=\frac1\tau \]

for every type. Copy each bank’s external assets, debt notionals, and CDS notionals unchanged.

The masses are positive binary rationals and sum to \(1\). The copied data satisfy the distinctness and nondegeneracy requirements. For every pair of types, \(\mu_s/\mu_t=1\), so incoming payments in the mass model become

\[ I_t(r)=\sum_s r_s q_{st}(r), \]

exactly the incoming payments in the original finite network. Liabilities, external assets, and both clearing branches likewise coincide. Therefore, for every \(r\),

\[ r\text{ clears the finite network} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\text{ clears the mass-model instance}. \]

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction. The mass fields add only \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) bits; copying or explicitly padding the contract tables also takes polynomial time.

The cited theorem therefore establishes \(\exists\mathbb{R}\)-hardness, including \(\alpha=0\), already on equal-mass instances. Since \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\exists\mathbb{R}\), it establishes the requested \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

3. Membership and an explicit running-time bound

For completeness, we give a direct existential-real formulation that preserves the strict default condition.

Use the finite-network quantities from Section 1. Clearing existence is equivalent to the truth of

\[ \exists(r_t)_{t\in T}\; \bigwedge_{t\in T} \left[ 0\leq r_t\leq1 \;\land\; \left( \bigl(r_t=1\land\widehat A_t(r)-\widehat L_t(r)\geq0\bigr) \;\lor\; \bigl( \widehat A_t(r)-\widehat L_t(r)<0 \land r_t\widehat L_t(r)-\widehat A'_t(r)=0 \bigr) \right) \right]. \]

To verify the default branch, note that its strict inequality forces \(\widehat L_t(r)>0\). Dividing its polynomial equality by that positive quantity gives the required recovery ratio. Conversely, every clearing vector satisfies the corresponding branch. At equality, only the solvent branch applies. When liabilities vanish, nonnegative assets exclude the default branch and force \(r_t=1\).

Each \(\widehat q_{st}\) and \(\widehat L_t\) is affine in \(r\). Incoming payments are sums of products \(r_s\widehat q_{st}(r)\), so \(\widehat A_t\) and \(\widehat A'_t\) have degree at most \(2\). The polynomial \(r_t\widehat L_t-\widehat A'_t\) also has degree at most \(2\).

The formula therefore has exactly \(\tau\) variables, \(O(\tau)\) polynomial predicates, and maximum degree \(2\). Its rational coefficients can be computed and converted to integer coefficients in polynomial time. Multiplication by positive common denominators preserves every equality and inequality, including strict inequalities, and produces coefficient bit lengths polynomial in \(L\). This proves membership in \(\exists\mathbb{R}\).

The standard singly exponential decision bound for existential-real formulas with \(k\) variables, \(p\) polynomial predicates, maximum degree \(d\), and coefficient bit length \(B\) is \((pd)^{O(k)}B^{O(1)}\), with a polynomial factor for the Boolean formula’s length. See [Renegar’s decision theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(10)80003-3) and the complexity discussion in [Vorobjov’s notes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.00456).

Here all its hypotheses hold: the coefficients are integers, the formula is existential, and its predicates include permitted equalities and inequalities. Substituting \(k=\tau\), \(p=O(\tau)\), \(d=2\), and \(B=L^{O(1)}\) gives

\[ (\tau+1)^{O(\tau)}L^{O(1)} \]

bit operations, including polynomial preprocessing. There is no separate alternative-count parameter: the number of recovery variables is precisely the support size \(\tau\).
