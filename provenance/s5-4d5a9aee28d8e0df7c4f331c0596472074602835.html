The continuous mirror question, as graded

Input: a finite hospital set H; a finite type support T with rational masses μ_t>0 summing to 1; each type t has a strict ranking ≻_t over H and a binary acceptability vector a_ht; and each hospital h has a rational per-capita cap κ_h>0. Does there exist an assignment of mass x_th≥0 with ∑_h x_th≤μ_t, x_th=0 when a_ht=0, and ℓ_h=∑_t x_th≤κ_h, such that ℓ_h>0 for every hospital and the allocation is stable? Define q_th=μ_t-∑_g x_tg+∑_{g:h≻_t g}x_tg; stability requires that, for every acceptable pair (t,h), q_th>0 implies ℓ_h=κ_h. Equivalently, maximize z subject to these constraints and z≤ℓ_h for every h, and ask whether the optimum is positive.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H\) be a nonempty hospital set with \(m=|H|\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Each type has a strict complete ranking \(\succ_t\) over \(H\), with every hospital preferred to being unmatched. Each hospital has a binary acceptability indicator \(a_{ht}\in\{0,1\}\) for each type and a positive binary-rational capacity \(\kappa_h\). Hospitals value acceptable mass equally and seek only to increase their total assigned mass up to capacity.
An allocation consists of real masses \(x_{th}\geq0\), allowing each type to split across hospitals, with \(\sum_{h\in H}x_{th}\leq\mu_t\), \(x_{th}=0\) whenever \(a_{ht}=0\), and hospital loads \(\ell_h=\sum_{t\in T}x_{th}\leq\kappa_h\).
Define \(q_{th}=\mu_t-\sum_{g\in H}x_{tg}+\sum_{g\in H:\,h\succ_t g}x_{tg}\). An allocation is stable precisely when, for every pair with \(a_{ht}=1\), \(q_{th}>0\) implies \(\ell_h=\kappa_h\).

Is deciding whether a stable allocation exists with \(\ell_h>0\) for every \(h\in H\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For indivisible doctors, deciding whether a stable allocation gives every hospital at least one doctor is NP-complete, even with binary capped additive hospital valuations and strict complete doctor rankings. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19744v1)
No result settling this exact mass-allocation decision problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Completed the outside-option convention: every hospital is ranked above being unmatched, while the acceptability indicators describe hospital willingness to accept a type.
  • Made allocations real-valued and explicitly allowed splitting a type's mass across hospitals.
  • Specified a nonempty hospital set, used m for its size, and defined L as the total input bit length.

Proven result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. The reduction below permits arbitrary real splitting and uses only positive dyadic rational masses and capacities.

1. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

For a feasible allocation, every \(q_{th}\) is nonnegative: it is the sum of unmatched mass and mass assigned below \(h\) in type \(t\)’s ranking. Thus stability is equivalent to requiring, for every acceptable pair \((t,h)\), either \(\ell_h=\kappa_h\) or \(q_{th}=0\).

Guess a subset \(F\subseteq H\), intended to contain the full hospitals. Consider the rational linear program that maximizes \(\delta\), subject to allocation feasibility and

\[ \ell_h=\kappa_h\quad(h\in F),\qquad q_{th}=0\quad(h\notin F,\ a_{ht}=1), \]

together with \(\ell_h\geq\delta\) for every \(h\), and \(0\leq\delta\leq1\).

A feasible solution with \(\delta>0\) is stable and gives every hospital positive load. Conversely, any stable allocation with all loads positive satisfies this program for its actual set of full hospitals, with \(\delta=\min_{h\in H}\ell_h>0\).

The program has at most \(m\tau+1\) variables and \(O(m\tau+m+\tau)\) constraints, with encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Rational linear programming, including exact comparison of the optimum with zero, takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). The guessed subset uses \(m\) bits, proving membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

This also gives a deterministic algorithm taking \(2^m\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time.

2. Reduction from \(\mathrm{3SAT}\)

Let a \(\mathrm{3SAT}\) instance have variables \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_c\), where \(n,c\geq1\) and every clause has exactly three literal occurrences. Repeated literals are allowed.

Write

\[ R=3c,\qquad B=R+1,\qquad W=2n(B+1)+R. \]

Let \(D\) be the smallest power of two strictly greater than \(W\). Consequently, \(D-W>0\) and \(D\leq2W\).

For a sign \(s\in\{+,-\}\), let \(r_{ijs}\) be the number of occurrences of the corresponding literal of \(v_i\) in clause \(C_j\). Thus

\[ \sum_{i,s}r_{ijs}=3,\qquad \sum_{i,j,s}r_{ijs}=R. \]

Hospitals

Create the following hospitals:

| Hospital | Capacity |
|---|---:|
| Priority hospital \(P_i\), for each variable | \(B/D\) |
| Literal sinks \(S_i^+\) and \(S_i^-\), for each variable | \((B+1)/D\) each |
| Clause hospital \(K_j\), for each clause | \(3/D\) |
| Padding hospital \(Z\) | \((D-W)/D\) |

Types and acceptability

For each variable and each sign, create a switch type and a base type. For each positive multiplicity \(r_{ijs}\), create one occurrence type. Finally, create a padding type.

| Type | Mass | Hospitals accepting it | Ranking prefix |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Switch \(u_i^s\) | \(B/D\) | \(P_i,S_i^s\) | \(P_i\succ S_i^s\) |
| Base \(b_i^s\) | \(1/D\) | \(S_i^s\) | \(S_i^s\) first |
| Occurrence \(t_{ijs}\) | \(r_{ijs}/D\) | \(S_i^s,K_j\) | \(S_i^s\succ K_j\) |
| Padding \(f\) | \((D-W)/D\) | \(Z\) | \(Z\) first |

These are the complete acceptability lists. Complete every ranking strictly by appending the remaining hospitals in any fixed order, with unmatched last.

Aggregating repeated occurrences into \(t_{ijs}\) ensures that these are support entries rather than artificially distinguished copies of identical types.

The total type mass is

\[ \frac{2nB+2n+R+(D-W)}{D} =\frac{W+(D-W)}{D} =1. \]

All masses and capacities are positive dyadic rationals.

3. Properties of every stable allocation

The following arguments apply to arbitrary real allocations.

Lemma 1: Every priority hospital is full

Fix \(i\). Suppose \(\ell_{P_i}<B/D\).

Both switch types \(u_i^+\) and \(u_i^-\) rank \(P_i\) first, and \(P_i\) accepts both. Stability therefore requires

\[ q_{u_i^s,P_i}=0 \quad\text{for each }s\in\{+,-\}. \]

Because \(P_i\) is ranked first,

\[ q_{u_i^s,P_i}=\mu_{u_i^s}-x_{u_i^s,P_i}. \]

Thus each switch type assigns all its mass \(B/D\) to \(P_i\). Its load would be \(2B/D\), exceeding capacity \(B/D\). This contradiction proves \(\ell_{P_i}=B/D\).

Lemma 2: The two sinks of a variable cannot both be full

Let \(r_i=\sum_{j,s}r_{ijs}\). Only that variable’s switch, base, and occurrence types can enter \(P_i,S_i^+,S_i^-\). Their total mass is

\[ \frac{2B+2+r_i}{D} \leq\frac{2B+2+R}{D} =\frac{3B+1}{D}. \]

If both sinks were full, Lemma 1 would imply total load

\[ \ell_{P_i}+\ell_{S_i^+}+\ell_{S_i^-} =\frac{B+2(B+1)}{D} =\frac{3B+2}{D}. \]

This exceeds the entire mass available to those hospitals. Hence at most one sink is full.

Lemma 3: Positive occurrence mass at a clause requires its sink to be full

Suppose \(x_{t_{ijs},K_j}>0\). Type \(t_{ijs}\) ranks its acceptable sink \(S_i^s\) above \(K_j\). Consequently,

\[ q_{t_{ijs},S_i^s}\geq x_{t_{ijs},K_j}>0. \]

Stability requires \(\ell_{S_i^s}=(B+1)/D\).

In particular, even arbitrarily small positive clause mass triggers fullness of the corresponding sink.

4. Positive loads imply satisfiability

Suppose a stable allocation gives every hospital positive load.

Define a truth assignment as follows:

  • If \(S_i^+\) is full, set \(v_i\) true.
  • If \(S_i^-\) is full, set \(v_i\) false.
  • If neither is full, assign \(v_i\) arbitrarily.

Lemma 2 makes this consistent.

Every clause hospital \(K_j\) has positive load and accepts only its occurrence types. Therefore some \(t_{ijs}\) assigns positive mass to \(K_j\). Lemma 3 says \(S_i^s\) is full, so the corresponding literal is true under the assignment just defined.

Every clause is therefore satisfied.

5. Satisfiability implies a stable allocation with positive loads

Conversely, fix a satisfying truth assignment. For each variable, call the sign of its true literal the selected sign.

Allocate masses as follows:

  • Send the selected switch type entirely to its sink.
  • Send the other switch type entirely to the priority hospital.
  • Send every base type entirely to its sink.
  • Send an occurrence type to its clause hospital if its literal is true; otherwise send it to its sink.
  • Send the padding type entirely to \(Z\).

Feasibility and positive loads

Each priority hospital has load \(B/D\). Each selected sink has load

\[ \frac{B+1}{D}, \]

from its switch and base types, so it is full.

The other sink has load

\[ \frac{1+\sum_j r_{ijs}}{D} \leq\frac{1+R}{D} =\frac{B}{D} <\frac{B+1}{D}. \]

It also has positive load because it receives its base type.

A clause hospital receives exactly \(1/D\) per true literal occurrence, counting multiplicities. Its load is at most \(3/D\), and is positive because the assignment satisfies that clause. Finally, \(Z\) receives its full capacity.

All mass is assigned along acceptable edges, and every hospital has positive load.

Stability

We check every kind of acceptable pair.

  • A switch assigned to its sink prefers its priority hospital, which is full. It has no mass below its sink.
  • A switch assigned to its priority hospital receives its first choice, so its \(q\)-values at both acceptable hospitals are zero.
  • A base type receives all its mass at its sole acceptable hospital, so its corresponding \(q\)-value is zero.
  • A true occurrence type is assigned entirely to its clause hospital. Its preferred sink is full, and its \(q\)-value at the clause hospital is zero.
  • A false occurrence type is assigned entirely to its first-choice sink. Its \(q\)-values at both acceptable hospitals are zero.
  • The padding type is assigned entirely to its sole acceptable hospital.

Thus every acceptable pair with positive \(q\) has a full hospital. The allocation is stable.

6. Encoding size and conclusion

The construction has

\[ m=3n+c+1,\qquad \tau\leq4n+3c+1. \]

Since \(D\leq2W=O(nc+n+c)\), every numerical entry uses \(O(\log(n+c))\) bits. Explicitly listing all rankings and acceptability indicators gives total encoding length \(O((n+c)^2\log(n+c))\), and the reduction can be produced within that polynomial bound.

We have proved that the formula is satisfiable exactly when the constructed mass instance admits a stable allocation with every hospital load positive. Hence the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and Section 1 establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.
