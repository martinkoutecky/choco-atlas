Minimizing the specified objective to additive accuracy is possible in polynomial time. However, the malicious mass need not be identifiable, and the specified estimator’s error probability can tend to one.

Write \(S=\sum_r|Z_r|\), \(N=\sum_r n_r\), and \(\eta_0=\min\{\eta,1\}\). In the usual binary computational model, additive minimization takes

\(\operatorname{poly}\!\left(m,\tau,L,H,S,\log(1/\eta_0)\right)\)

bit operations. The batch sizes need not be expanded into individual observations, so no additional factor \(N\) is required. Here, as necessary for a binary running-time statement, the known numerical inputs and rounded observations have effective binary encodings counted in \(L\).

Under the assumptions in the question alone, the sharp uniform finite-sample upper bound is

\(\mathbb P\{|\widehat M-M^\star|>\varepsilon\}\leq 1.\)

Below, I prove both a more informative bound involving an explicit identification condition and a counterexample with pairwise distinct likelihood models and positive pairwise KL divergences.

2. Polynomial-time additive minimization

Affine residuals and convexity

Define normalized mixture weights and known coefficients by

\(p_{r\theta}=x_{r\theta}/\mu_r,\)

\(C_{r\theta j}=\sum_{z\in Z_r}L_r(z\mid\theta)\log\!\left(L_r(z\mid\theta_0)/L_r(z\mid\theta_j)\right).\)

Then \(p_r\) belongs to the probability simplex, and

\(G_{rj}(x)=\sum_\theta p_{r\theta}C_{r\theta j}.\)

The residual in role \(r\), coordinate \(j\), at time \(i\) is therefore

\(\widetilde\Lambda_{i,rj}-(1-\delta)\sum_{\ell=1}^{\tau}B_{\ell r}\widetilde\Lambda_{i-1,\ell j}-\delta\sum_\theta p_{r\theta}C_{r\theta j}.\)

This expression is affine jointly in \(p\) and \(B\). Thus \(F\) is a sum of squares of affine functions, with positive-semidefinite Hessian.

The feasible set is the product of \(\tau\) mixture simplices and \(\tau\) column simplices for \(B\). It is nonempty, compact, and defined by rational linear constraints. Moreover, the problem separates into \(\tau\) independent blocks: block \(r\) involves \(p_r\) and column \(r\) of \(B\), hence \(m+\tau\) variables and \(H(m-1)\) residuals.

Handling the logarithmic coefficients

Convexity alone does not address the irrational logarithms, so an approximation argument is needed.

Let \(D=H\tau(m-1)\), \(T=\max_{i,r,j}|\widetilde\Lambda_{i,rj}|\), and choose a polynomially bounded \(\beta\) satisfying \(|C_{r\theta j}|\leq\beta\). For example, \(\beta=L+1\) suffices: every positive probability in the rational input is at least \(2^{-L}\).

Set \(R=2T+\beta\). Every original residual has absolute value at most \(R\). Approximate each \(C_{r\theta j}\) by a rational coefficient \(C'_{r\theta j}\) with error at most \(\kappa\), and form \(F'\) using these coefficients. Because each \(p_r\) is a probability vector, each residual changes by at most \(\delta\kappa\leq\kappa\). Consequently, uniformly over the feasible set,

\(|F'-F|\leq D(2R\kappa+\kappa^2).\)

Choose

\(\kappa=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{\eta_0}{12D(R+1)}\right\}.\)

Then \(|F'-F|\leq\eta_0/4\).

These coefficient approximations are computable in polynomial time. For completeness, range-reduce a positive rational \(q\) as \(q=2^ku\), with \(1\leq u<2\), and use

\(\log u=2\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2h+1}\left(\frac{u-1}{u+1}\right)^{2h+1}.\)

The ratio has magnitude at most \(1/3\), so the series converges geometrically. The same series computes \(\log 2\). The required precision is polynomial in \(L+\log D+\log(1/\eta_0)\). Computing the coefficients requires \(O(m(m-1)S)\) summation terms.

Now apply the standard polynomial-time theorem for rational convex quadratic programming: a rational positive-semidefinite quadratic objective over a nonempty bounded rational polytope can be minimized to additive tolerance \(a>0\) in time polynomial in its encoding length and \(\log(1/a)\). Polynomial algorithms for convex quadratic programming are established, for example, by [Ye and Tse](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01587086).

All hypotheses hold for \(F'\). In particular, its Hessian remains positive semidefinite because \(F'\) is constructed as a sum of squares. If \(v'\) is feasible and satisfies \(F'(v')\leq\min F'+\eta_0/2\), then

\(F(v')\leq F'(v')+\eta_0/4\leq\min F+\eta_0\leq\min F+\eta.\)

This proves the stated polynomial running time.

The lexicographic selection

Continuity and compactness imply that the minimizer set is nonempty and compact. Successively minimizing each coordinate over the remaining set produces the unique lexicographically least minimizer.

The computational conclusion above concerns the requested additive objective accuracy. It does not assert exact computation of this real-valued lexicographic minimizer, or that a small objective gap guarantees a small malicious-mass error.

3. A finite-sample bound with explicit identification dependence

Here is a bound valid for every admissible true parameter pair.

Let \(q_\rho\) bound the entrywise rounding error:

\(|\widetilde\Lambda_{i,rj}-\Lambda_{i,rj}|\leq q_\rho.\)

For nearest-grid rounding, \(q_\rho=\rho/2\); taking \(q_\rho=\rho\) also covers rounding up or down. Define

\(d=\tau(m-1),\qquad n_{\min}=\min_r n_r,\)

\(K=b+q_\rho,\qquad c=(1-\delta)K+\delta b,\qquad e=(2-\delta)q_\rho.\)

For a feasible candidate \((x,B)\), define its prediction difference from the true parameters by

\(D_i(x,B)=(1-\delta)(B-B^\star)^{\mathsf T}\widetilde\Lambda_{i-1}+\delta\bigl(G(x)-G(x^\star)\bigr).\)

Define the empirical separation of incorrect malicious masses by

\(\gamma_H(\varepsilon)=\inf_{\substack{(x,B)\text{ feasible}\\|M(x)-M^\star|>\varepsilon}}\frac1H\sum_{i=1}^{H}\|D_i(x,B)\|_F^2,\)

with the infimum of the empty set equal to \(+\infty\).

For any \(0<\alpha<1\), set

\(t_\alpha=b\sqrt{\frac{2}{Hn_{\min}}\log\!\left(\frac{2d(\tau+1)}{\alpha}\right)},\)

\(v_\alpha=8\delta dc\,t_\alpha+4de^2.\)

Then

\(\boxed{\mathbb P\{|\widehat M-M^\star|>\varepsilon\}\leq\min\!\left\{1,\alpha+\mathbb P\{\gamma_H(\varepsilon)\leq v_\alpha\}\right\}.}\)

Thus a vanishing error bound requires an identification statement ensuring that incorrect malicious masses cannot have almost the same predictions.

Proof

Put \(W_i=Y_i-G(x^\star)\). The stationary recursion has the causal representation

\(\Lambda_i=\delta\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}(1-\delta)^s\bigl((B^\star)^{\mathsf T}\bigr)^sY_{i-s}.\)

The series converges absolutely because column-stochasticity makes multiplication by \((B^\star)^{\mathsf T}\) a contraction in the maximum-entry norm. Its weights sum to one, so \(|\Lambda_{i,rj}|\leq b\).

This representation also follows for any stationary solution by iterating the recursion: the initial term tends to zero in probability by stationarity and geometric contraction. Hence \(\Lambda_{i-1}\), and its rounding, are functions of past innovations and are independent of the new sampling at time \(i\).

Each \(W_{i,rj}\) is an average of \(n_r\) centered independent variables whose uncentered values lie in \([-b,b]\). Conditional Hoeffding bounds therefore give, for \(t>0\),

\(\mathbb P\!\left\{\left|\frac1H\sum_iW_{i,rj}\right|>t\right\}\leq2\exp\!\left(-\frac{Hn_rt^2}{2b^2}\right).\)

The predictable factor \(\widetilde\Lambda_{i-1,\ell j}\) has absolute value at most \(K\). The same conditional exponential-moment argument gives

\(\mathbb P\!\left\{\left|\frac1H\sum_i\widetilde\Lambda_{i-1,\ell j}W_{i,rj}\right|>Kt\right\}\leq2\exp\!\left(-\frac{Hn_rt^2}{2b^2}\right).\)

There are \(d(\tau+1)\) statistics in these two families. A union bound shows that, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), all satisfy their respective bounds with \(t=t_\alpha\).

On this event, for every feasible candidate simultaneously,

\(\left|\frac1H\sum_i\langle W_i,D_i(x,B)\rangle_F\right|\leq2dc\,t_\alpha.\)

Indeed, the difference between two stochastic columns has \(\ell_1\)-norm at most \(2\), and \(|G_{rj}(x)-G_{rj}(x^\star)|\leq2b\). Expanding the inner product and applying the preceding bounds yields exactly this inequality. Its simultaneous validity permits substituting the fitted parameters.

Write \(Q_i=\widetilde\Lambda_i-\Lambda_i\). The true-parameter residual is

\(E_i=\delta W_i+Q_i-(1-\delta)(B^\star)^{\mathsf T}Q_{i-1}.\)

The rounding contribution has entrywise magnitude at most \(e\). Optimality gives

\(\sum_i\|E_i-D_i(\widehat x,\widehat B)\|_F^2\leq\sum_i\|E_i\|_F^2.\)

Let \(A^2=H^{-1}\sum_i\|D_i(\widehat x,\widehat B)\|_F^2\). Expanding and using Cauchy–Schwarz yields

\(A^2\leq4\delta dc\,t_\alpha+2e\sqrt d\,A.\)

Since \(2e\sqrt d\,A\leq A^2/2+2de^2\), it follows that \(A^2\leq v_\alpha\).

If the malicious-mass error exceeds \(\varepsilon\), the fitted pair belongs to the set defining \(\gamma_H(\varepsilon)\), so \(\gamma_H(\varepsilon)\leq A^2\). This proves the probability bound.

For any feasible \(\eta\)-additive minimizer, the same argument adds \(2\eta/H\) to \(v_\alpha\).

4. Sharp failure of identification and consistency

Equal likelihoods already make the uniform bound \(1\) sharp: with one role and two identical state likelihoods, the objective is independent of the mixture, the lexicographic rule selects one endpoint, and choosing the opposite true endpoint gives error probability \(1\) for every \(H\) and \(\rho\).

The more substantive obstruction persists with distinct likelihoods.

Distinct models with identical mixture observations

Take \(\tau=1\), \(\mu_1=1\), \(m=3\), \(B^\star=[1]\), and \(Z=\{0,1\}\). Fix a rational \(0<h<1/2\), and specify

\(L(1\mid\theta_0)=1/2,\qquad L(1\mid\theta_1)=1/2-h,\qquad L(1\mid\theta_2)=1/2+h.\)

The probabilities at \(0\) are their complements. All tables are strictly positive and pairwise distinct. Their maximum absolute log-likelihood ratio is

\(\ell=\log\!\left(\frac{1/2+h}{1/2-h}\right).\)

For any prescribed positive \(b\), a sufficiently small positive rational \(h\) makes \(\ell\leq b\). Every pairwise KL divergence is strictly positive, because distinct strictly positive finite distributions have strictly positive KL divergence.

Consider the two true mixtures

\(p^{(0)}=(1,0,0),\qquad p^{(1)}=(0,1/2,1/2).\)

Their malicious masses are \(0\) and \(1\), respectively. Nevertheless,

\(L(\cdot\mid\theta_0)=\tfrac12L(\cdot\mid\theta_1)+\tfrac12L(\cdot\mid\theta_2).\)

Consequently, under either true mixture every sampled signal is an independent fair Bernoulli variable. Their batch processes \(Y_i\), stationary processes \(\Lambda_i\), and rounded observations have identical laws.

This is an information-theoretic obstruction. For any estimator and \(\varepsilon<1/2\), its success events for masses \(0\) and \(1\) are disjoint under this common observation law. Therefore at least one of the two error probabilities is at least \(1/2\).

Behavior of the specified lexicographic estimator

For a candidate mixture \(p\), let

\(q=\mathbb P_p\{\zeta=1\}=\tfrac12+h(p_2-p_1).\)

Define

\(a=\log\!\left(\frac{1/2}{1/2+h}\right),\qquad c_0=\log\!\left(\frac{1/2}{1/2-h}\right),\)

so \(c_0-a=\ell\). The two expected log-ratios are

\(G(p)=(a+\ell q,\ c_0-\ell q).\)

Let \(U_i\) be the fraction of ones in batch \(i\). Then

\(Y_i=(a+\ell U_i,\ c_0-\ell U_i).\)

Since \(B=[1]\) is the only feasible mixing matrix, least squares determines a unique fitted signal probability

\(\widehat q=\operatorname{proj}_{[1/2-h,\,1/2+h]}\!\left(\frac12+\frac{\overline R_1-\overline R_2}{2\ell}\right),\)

where

\(\overline R=\frac1H\sum_{i=1}^{H}\frac{\widetilde\Lambda_i-(1-\delta)\widetilde\Lambda_{i-1}}{\delta}.\)

This follows by expanding the quadratic objective in the scalar \(q\).

For a fixed \(q\), the remaining minimizers satisfy

\(p_0+p_1+p_2=1,\qquad p_2-p_1=(q-1/2)/h,\qquad p\geq0.\)

There are two cases, covering every fixed coordinate ordering; the coordinate of \(B\) is constant and irrelevant.

Case 1: \(p_0\) is the first mixture coordinate. Its lexicographic minimum is \(0\), since every feasible \(q\) can be represented using only states \(1\) and \(2\). Thus \(\widehat M=1\) for every observation sequence. Under the fixed benign truth \(p^{(0)}\),

\(\mathbb P_{p^{(0)}}\{|\widehat M-M^\star|>\varepsilon\}=1\)

for every \(0<\varepsilon<1\), every \(H\), and every rounding spacing.

Case 2: \(p_1\) or \(p_2\) is the first mixture coordinate. Minimizing either coordinate forces the smaller of \(p_1,p_2\) to zero. Hence

\(\widehat p_0=1-\frac{|\widehat q-1/2|}{h},\qquad \widehat M=\frac{|\widehat q-1/2|}{h}.\)

Choose the fixed malicious truth \(p^{(1)}\). Its observations are still independent fair Bernoulli signals. Put \(n=n_1\). The rounding contribution to each coordinate of \(\overline R\) is at most \(e/\delta\), where \(e=(2-\delta)q_\rho\). Projection cannot increase distance from \(1/2\), so

\(\left|\widehat q-\frac12\right|\leq\left|\frac1H\sum_iU_i-\frac12\right|+\frac{e}{\delta\ell}.\)

Whenever \(s=h(1-\varepsilon)-e/(\delta\ell)>0\), Hoeffding’s inequality for the \(Hn\) independent Bernoulli signals gives the finite-sample lower bound

\(\boxed{\mathbb P_{p^{(1)}}\{|\widehat M-1|>\varepsilon\}\geq1-2\exp(-2Hn s^2).}\)

For fixed \(h,\delta,n\), as \(H\to\infty\) and \(\rho\to0\), the right-hand side tends to \(1\).

Thus, for every coordinate ordering, this distinct-model instance has a fixed admissible true mixture whose error probability tends to one. Strong connectivity, a positive diagonal entry, and positive pairwise KL gaps all hold.

5. Relation to the cited finite-agent results

The first cited paper treats a true state for each individual agent, identifies its likelihood-equivalence class, and states a wrong-hypothesis bound involving batch size, covariance, KL gaps, adaptation, and a learning rate. That target differs from recovering continuous mixture weights. [Detection of Malicious Agents in Social Learning](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12619v4).

In the counterexample above, the two populations have identical expected log-ratios and even identical full observation laws. Therefore neither accurate estimation of those expectations nor positive pairwise KL gaps resolves the mixture ambiguity.
