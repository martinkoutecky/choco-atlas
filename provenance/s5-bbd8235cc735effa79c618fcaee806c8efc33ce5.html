The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item classes, finitely many cohorts \(h\in H\) with rational masses \(\mu_h\), finite valuation domains \(\mathcal V_h\), rational priors \(F_h\), and per-capita supplies \(b_j\), compute an anonymous direct mechanism that maps reported mass profiles to feasible allocations and gives each tagged agent a report-conditioned lottery over integral bundles and a payment. At the truthful mass profile \(\mu_hF_h(v)\), require ex-post individual rationality, \(\epsilon v_{\max}\)-BIC for every \(v,r\in\mathcal V_h\), and expected welfare per unit mass at least \(\frac12-\epsilon\) times first-best feasible welfare for subadditive values, or \(1-\frac1e-\epsilon\) for fractionally subadditive values; ask whether this can be done in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\) in the paper’s demand-query model.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\geq1\) item classes; an integral bundle is \(S\subseteq[m]\), with at most one unit of each class. Public cohorts \(h\in H\) have positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_h\) summing to \(1\), finite nonempty valuation domains \(\mathcal V_h\), and positive rational priors \(F_h(v)\) summing to \(1\) within each cohort. Supplies \(b_j\geq0\) are binary rationals.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((h,v)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(a_{h,v}=\mu_hF_h(v)\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible valuations. Valuations are normalized, monotone, nonnegative rational-valued set functions, all subadditive or all fractionally subadditive (XOS); let \(v_{\max}=\max_{h,v}v([m])\). Access is through demand queries and value evaluation; \(L\) includes the input bit length and a bound on oracle-value bit lengths, and demand ties may be resolved arbitrarily.
An anonymous direct mechanism depends on a reported mass profile \(\rho\), where \(\rho_{h,r}\geq0\) and \(\sum_r\rho_{h,r}=\mu_h\), and gives every tagged agent reporting \(r\in\mathcal V_h\) the same lottery \(Q_{h,r}(\rho)\) over integral bundles and payments. Cohorts are observable and cannot be misreported; a tagged agent has zero mass, so its report does not change \(\rho\). Lotteries must be defined even for reports of zero aggregate mass.
Feasibility means that, for every reported profile and every realization of any common mechanism randomness, the induced bundle probabilities satisfy \(\sum_{h,r}\rho_{h,r}\Pr_{Q_{h,r}(\rho)}(j\in S)\leq b_j\) for every \(j\). These probabilities describe the fractions of each reporting group receiving each integral bundle.
Ex-post individual rationality requires \(r(S)-p\geq0\) for every outcome under report \(r\), at every profile. At the truthful profile \(a\), require \(\mathbb E_{Q_{h,v}(a)}[v(S)-p]\geq\mathbb E_{Q_{h,r}(a)}[v(S)-p]-\epsilon v_{\max}\) for every \(h\) and \(v,r\in\mathcal V_h\).
Let \(W=\sum_{h,v}a_{h,v}\mathbb E_{Q_{h,v}(a)}v(S)\), and let \(W^*\) be the maximum of this welfare over feasible bundle distributions at \(a\), without incentive or payment constraints. Fix rational \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
Can such mechanisms be constructed and evaluated with polynomial time and oracle-query complexity in \(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon\), guaranteeing \(W\geq(1/2-\epsilon)W^*\) for subadditive valuations and \(W\geq(1-1/e-\epsilon)W^*\) for XOS valuations, or is achieving these guarantees computationally intractable in this oracle model?
For finitely many agents with independent finite-support priors, polynomial-time demand-query mechanisms attain these two ratios, \(\epsilon v_{\max}\)-BIC, and ex-post IR ([Bei–Huang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.4244)).
A related reduction treats continuous valuation spaces for finitely many agents and obtains approximate BIC; this is a different model from a nonatomic society ([Hartline–Kleinberg–Malekian](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~fiat/classauctions1314/Papers/bayesian-SODA-11.pdf)). No result settling exactly the mass-model question above was identified in the search.

Result

The mechanisms are polynomial-time constructible in the stated oracle model. In fact, they can attain \(W=W^*\), exact incentive compatibility, and ex-post individual rationality.

The construction works for every normalized, monotone, nonnegative rational valuation with the stipulated demand and value access. Subadditivity and XOS are unnecessary.

The decisive feature is the stated feasibility condition: population mass may be divided among integral bundles. Consequently, the configuration LP describes implementable allocations exactly.

1. The exact welfare program

Index supported types by \(t\in T\), where \(|T|=\tau\). Write \(a_t>0\) for their masses and \(v_t\) for their valuations. Because every domain valuation has positive prior probability, \(T\) includes every permissible cohort–report pair.

For each \(t\) and \(S\subseteq[m]\), let \(x_{t,S}\) denote the mass of type \(t\) receiving bundle \(S\). Consider the linear program \(P\):

  • Maximize \(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\).
  • Require \(\displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{t,S}=a_t\) for every \(t\).
  • Require \(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\leq b_j\) for every \(j\).
  • Require \(x_{t,S}\geq0\).

This program is feasible: assign every type the empty bundle. Its feasible region is compact, since \(0\leq x_{t,S}\leq a_t\).

A feasible bundle distribution at the truthful profile gives a feasible solution by setting \(x_{t,S}=a_t\Pr_t(S)\). Conversely, any feasible solution gives bundle probabilities \(x_{t,S}/a_t\). Both transformations preserve welfare. Thus the optimum of \(P\) is exactly \(W^*\).

Introduce item prices \(\pi_j\geq0\) and variables \(u_t\), and write \(\pi(S)=\sum_{j\in S}\pi_j\). The dual program \(D\) is:

  • Minimize \(\displaystyle \sum_t a_tu_t+\sum_j b_j\pi_j\).
  • Require \(u_t+\pi(S)\geq v_t(S)\) for every \(t,S\).
  • Require \(\pi_j\geq0\); the \(u_t\) are formally unrestricted.

The constraint for \(S=\varnothing\) implies \(u_t\geq0\). The dual is feasible, for example with \(u_t=v_{\max}\) and \(\pi_j=0\). Finite-dimensional LP strong duality therefore gives attained optimal solutions \(x\) and \((u,\pi)\) with equal objective values.

2. Polynomial-time computation, including primal recovery

The dual has only \(d=\tau+m\) variables. Its exponentially many bundle inequalities admit an exact separation oracle.

Given a rational candidate \((u,\pi)\):

  • If some \(\pi_j<0\), return that violated nonnegativity constraint.
  • Otherwise, for each \(t\), make a demand query returning any

\(\displaystyle S_t\in\arg\max_{S\subseteq[m]}\bigl(v_t(S)-\pi(S)\bigr)\),
and evaluate \(v_t(S_t)\).

  • If \(v_t(S_t)-\pi(S_t)>u_t\), return the corresponding violated bundle inequality. If this never occurs, every bundle inequality holds.

The last inference follows directly from maximization: for every \(S\), \(v_t(S)-\pi(S)\leq v_t(S_t)-\pi(S_t)\). Hence arbitrary demand ties cause no problem. Each separation call uses at most \(\tau\) demand queries and \(\tau\) value queries.

We use the standard exact rational-LP optimization and dual-recovery theorem: for a rational polyhedron in polynomial dimension, with bounded inequality encoding lengths and a strong separation oracle returning defining inequalities of bounded encoding length, one can obtain an exact optimum and an optimal solution of its standard dual represented on polynomially many returned inequalities, in oracle-polynomial time. The recovery part is essential; optimizing the oracle-described program alone does not produce its standard-dual solution. This is the Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver recovery result, discussed explicitly in [Dadush–Végh–Zambelli, Section 1.1](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35145/35145.pdf).

Here its hypotheses hold:

  • Every bundle inequality has coefficients in \(\{0,1\}\) and a rational right-hand side of the stipulated bounded bit length.
  • Every returned inequality is an actual defining inequality.
  • The objective coefficients \(a_t,b_j\) have bounded input bit length.
  • Both programs are feasible and have finite attained optima, as established above.

Applying this theorem to \(D\) recovers an optimal solution of \(P\), represented by polynomially many pairs \((t,S)\), together with optimal \((u,\pi)\). More explicitly, the recovered standard-dual variables for bundle inequalities are the \(x_{t,S}\); those for \(\pi_j\geq0\) are supply slacks. Their defining equations are precisely the type-mass equalities and item-supply inequalities of \(P\).

If desired, solve the recovered finite primal program for a basic optimum. After adding the \(m\) supply slacks, it has \(\tau+m\) equality constraints, so at most \(\tau+m\) bundle variables need be positive. Standard determinant bounds give polynomial encoding lengths for the resulting rational solutions.

Thus construction requires polynomial time and oracle-query complexity in \(m,\tau,L\). It does not require enumerating all bundles tied at the final prices.

3. Optimal lotteries supported by item prices

Fix the computed optimal primal–dual pair. For each type \(t\), define \(Q_t^0\) by assigning probability \(x_{t,S}/a_t\) to the outcome consisting of bundle \(S\) and payment \(\pi(S)\).

We now verify the property supporting incentives. The primal–dual objective gap equals

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\bigl(u_t+\pi(S)-v_t(S)\bigr) +\sum_j\pi_j\left(b_j-\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\right). \)

This identity uses \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=a_t\). Every summand is nonnegative by primal and dual feasibility. Optimality makes the gap zero. Therefore

\(\displaystyle x_{t,S}>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_t(S)-\pi(S)=u_t.\)

Since \(a_t>0\), each type has at least one positive-probability bundle. Combining this equality with all its dual inequalities yields

\(\displaystyle u_t=\max_{S\subseteq[m]}\bigl(v_t(S)-\pi(S)\bigr)\geq0.\)

Two consequences follow.

Ex-post IR. Every outcome of \(Q_t^0\) satisfies \(v_t(S)-\pi(S)=u_t\geq0\).

Truthfulness among the base lotteries. For any true type \(t\) and any permissible alternative report \(r\), every bundle satisfies \(v_t(S)-\pi(S)\leq u_t\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_{Q_t^0}[v_t(S)-p]=u_t \quad\text{and}\quad \mathbb E_{Q_r^0}[v_t(S)-p]\leq u_t. \)

This comparison applies, in particular, to every alternative report within \(t\)’s observable cohort.

4. A feasible mechanism at every reported profile

The base lotteries need not satisfy supplies when reporting-group masses differ from \(a\). We therefore extend them by a common thinning factor.

Precompute \(q_{t,j}=\Pr_{Q_t^0}(j\in S)\). At any permitted profile \(\rho\), define

\(\displaystyle D_j(\rho)=\sum_t\rho_tq_{t,j},\)

and

\(\displaystyle \lambda(\rho)= \min\left(\{1\}\cup \left\{\frac{b_j}{D_j(\rho)}:D_j(\rho)>0\right\}\right). \)

Thus \(0\leq\lambda(\rho)\leq1\), including when a zero-supply item has positive base demand.

For every report \(t\), define

\(\displaystyle Q_t(\rho)=\lambda(\rho)Q_t^0+ \bigl(1-\lambda(\rho)\bigr)\delta_{(\varnothing,0)}. \)

This formula defines the lottery even when \(\rho_t=0\).

Feasibility and ex-post IR

For each item \(j\), its assigned mass is

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\rho_t\Pr_{Q_t(\rho)}(j\in S) =\lambda(\rho)D_j(\rho)\leq b_j. \)

When \(D_j(\rho)=0\), the assigned mass is zero. Otherwise, the inequality follows from the definition of \(\lambda\).

The probability vectors are deterministic functions of \(\rho\); the mechanism uses no common randomness to choose them. Consequently, feasibility holds in precisely the required realization-wise sense for the bundle fractions.

Every retained base outcome satisfies IR for its report, and the added outcome has utility zero. Hence ex-post IR holds at every profile.

Exact incentive compatibility

A tagged agent cannot change \(\rho\), so changing its report leaves \(\lambda(\rho)\) unchanged. For true type \(t\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_{Q_t(\rho)}[v_t(S)-p]=\lambda(\rho)u_t. \)

For every permissible alternative report \(r\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_{Q_r(\rho)}[v_t(S)-p] =\lambda(\rho)\mathbb E_{Q_r^0}[v_t(S)-p] \leq\lambda(\rho)u_t. \)

Thus the mechanism is exactly incentive compatible at every profile, a stronger property than the requested approximate inequalities at \(a\).

5. Welfare and evaluation complexity

At the truthful profile, primal feasibility gives \(D_j(a)\leq b_j\) for every item. Therefore \(\lambda(a)=1\), and

\(\displaystyle W=\sum_ta_t\mathbb E_{Q_t^0}v_t(S) =\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S) =W^*. \)

Both requested approximation guarantees follow immediately.

The stored lotteries have polynomial total support and polynomial rational encoding length. Given \(\rho\), evaluation requires only computing the linear quantities \(D_j(\rho)\), taking the indicated minimum, and scaling the stored probabilities. It uses polynomially many arithmetic operations and no additional valuation queries. For rational profile inputs, bit complexity is polynomial in the stored mechanism size and the profile’s encoding length, as usual for evaluation. The same formulas mathematically define the mechanism for arbitrary real profiles.

The distinction from finite-agent auctions is substantive: here the configuration LP is implementable by splitting reporting-group mass among integral bundles. No rounding into a jointly feasible allocation of finitely many atomic agents is required.
