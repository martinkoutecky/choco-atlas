The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), a rational distribution \(\mu\) over approval ballots, an initial size-\(k\) committee \(W_0\), and the paper's candidate order, compute the complete trajectory of strict PAV-improving swaps under lexicographic better response through the first swap-free committee. Characterize the worst-case trajectory length in \(m\), \(\tau\), \(L\), and \(k\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Input consists of a candidate set \(C\) with \(|C|=m\geq1\), an integer \(1\leq k\leq m\), an initial committee \(W_0\subseteq C\) of size \(k\), and a strict total order \(<\) on \(C\).
A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) distinct approval types \(A_i\subseteq C\), each with positive binary rational mass \(\mu_i\), with \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval types; \(L\) is the total binary input length.
The mass-weighted PAV score is \(S_\mu(W)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i H_{|A_i\cap W|}\), where \(H_r\) is the harmonic number and \(H_0=0\).
At each committee \(W\), choose the lexicographically first pair \((b,a)\), ordered using \(<\), among \(b\in C\setminus W\), \(a\in W\) satisfying \(S_\mu((W\setminus\{a\})\cup\{b\})>S_\mu(W)\). Perform that swap and restart the search; stop when no such pair exists.
The computational problem is to produce the complete explicit trajectory \(W_0,\ldots,W_T\), including the first committee with no strictly improving swap; its length is \(T\).

Is the worst-case trajectory length quasipolynomial in \(m+\tau+L\), or can it be superquasipolynomial?
What are asymptotically tight trajectory-length bounds and corresponding bit running times for computing the complete trajectory, in \(m,\tau,L,k\)?

For finite electorates, lexicographic better response has a verified \(\Omega(k^{\log k})\) trajectory lower bound on instances with polynomially many voters and candidates. [Kraiczy–Elkind](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol308-esa2024/LIPIcs.ESA.2024.82/LIPIcs.ESA.2024.82.pdf)
These finite instances yield normalized rational-mass instances, so the same lower bound applies here as a direct consequence. No tight bound for the specified rational-mass trajectory was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and required each listed support mass to be a positive binary rational.
  • Interpreted the candidate order as an input strict total order, with the added candidate compared first and the removed candidate second; search restarts after every swap.
  • Carried over the domain \(1\leq k\leq m\) and allowed arbitrary approval subsets, including the empty set.
  • Extended PAV to masses by taking its expectation under the distribution; only strictly positive score changes permit swaps.

1. Proven results and scope

The trajectory can be superquasipolynomial in the total input size. There are instances with
$$ m=\Theta(h),\qquad k=m/2,\qquad \tau=\Theta(h),\qquad L=O(h^2), $$
and
$$ T=2^{\Omega(h)}. $$

The joint dependence on all four parameters remains unresolved. The following additional results narrow that gap.

Let
$$ r=\min\{k,m-k\}. $$

  • If support size and input length are unrestricted, the worst-case length satisfies

$$ \lfloor m/r\rfloor^r-1 \;\le\; \mathsf T_{m,k} \;\le\; \binom mr-1 \qquad(r\geq1). $$
Consequently,
$$ \log(\mathsf T_{m,k}+1) = \Theta\!\left(r\log(em/r)\right). $$
For every fixed positive $r$, this gives the multiplicatively tight bound
$$ \mathsf T_{m,k}=\Theta(m^r). $$

  • For support size two,

$$ T\leq5r, $$
and instances attaining $T=r$ exist for every admissible $m,k$ with $r\geq1$. Thus the worst-case length for $\tau=2$ is $\Theta(r)$.

  • The complete prescribed trajectory can be computed exactly with polynomial preprocessing and polynomial bit cost per output committee.

The proofs below are self-contained except for one explicitly stated signed Max-Cut lower-bound theorem.

2. General upper bounds

For approval type $A_i$, define
$$ \ell_i=\max\{0,k-(m-|A_i|)\}, \qquad u_i=\min\{k,|A_i|\}, \qquad h_i=u_i-\ell_i. $$

Every size-$k$ committee has intersection count between $\ell_i$ and $u_i$. Direct case analysis gives
$$ h_i=\min\{k,m-k,|A_i|,m-|A_i|\}\leq r. $$

2.1. Committee and count-vector bounds

Strict score improvement prevents repetition of a committee.

It also prevents repetition of the vector
$$ \bigl(|A_1\cap W|,\ldots,|A_\tau\cap W|\bigr), $$
because equal vectors have equal PAV scores. Therefore
$$ T+1\leq \min\left\{ \binom mk,\; \prod_{i=1}^{\tau}(h_i+1) \right\} \leq \min\left\{ \binom mr,\; (r+1)^\tau \right\}. $$

In particular, $T=0$ when $k=m$.

2.2. Rational score-lattice bound

Write each mass in lowest terms as $\mu_i=p_i/q_i$, and put
$$ Q=\operatorname{lcm}(q_1,\ldots,q_\tau). $$

Let
$$ J=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\{\ell_i+1,\ldots,u_i\}, \qquad D=\operatorname{lcm}(J), $$
with $D=1$ if $J$ is empty.

Subtract the fixed baseline
$$ S_{\mathrm{base}}=\sum_i\mu_iH_{\ell_i}. $$
Every residual score is a sum of terms $\mu_i/t$ with $t\in J$. Because $q_i$ divides $Q$ and $t$ divides $D$, every residual score belongs to
$$ \frac{1}{QD}\mathbb Z. $$
Thus every positive improvement is at least $1/(QD)$.

All scores lie in an interval of width
$$ \mathcal R=\sum_i\mu_i(H_{u_i}-H_{\ell_i}), $$
so
$$ T\leq\lfloor QD\mathcal R\rfloor. $$

Combining these arguments yields the instance-dependent bound
$$ T\leq \min\left\{ \binom mk-1,\; \prod_i(h_i+1)-1,\; \lfloor QD\mathcal R\rfloor \right\}. $$

For parameter-only estimates, $\log_2Q\leq L$, $\mathcal R\leq H_k$, and
$$ \log_2D \leq \min\left\{ 4k,\; \tau r\log_2(k+1) \right\}. $$

The second estimate follows from $|J|\leq\sum_i h_i\leq\tau r$ and
$$ D\leq\prod_{t\in J}t\leq k^{|J|}. $$

Here is an elementary justification of the first estimate. Write
$$ d_a=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,a). $$
Then
$$ d_{2a}/d_a\mid\binom{2a}{a}. $$
Indeed, if a prime acquires a new exponent in $d_{2a}$, its newly appearing prime power lies in $(a,2a]$. The corresponding summand in the prime valuation of $\binom{2a}{a}$ is one, while all other summands are nonnegative. Also, at most one new exponent appears for any prime. Since $\binom{2a}{a}\leq2^{2a}$, iteration over powers of two and rounding upward give $\log_2d_k\leq4k$.

In particular, for $r\geq1$,
$$ T+1\leq \min\left\{ \binom mr,\; (r+1)^\tau,\; 1+2^{L+4k}H_k \right\}, $$
and hence
$$ \log(T+1) = O\!\left( \min\left\{ r\log(em/r),\; \tau\log(r+1),\; L+k \right\} \right). $$

These are upper bounds; they are not jointly tight in general.

3. Linear trajectory length for two approval types

3.1. Upper bound

Suppose $\tau=2$, with masses $p,q>0$. At a committee, write
$$ a=|A_1\cap W|, \qquad b=|A_2\cap W|. $$

A swap changes each count by an element of $\{-1,0,1\}$. An improving swap cannot decrease a count without increasing the other: if both changes were nonpositive, the score could not increase.

Thus every improving swap is one of two kinds:

  • a dominance step, which increases at least one count and decreases neither;
  • a trade, whose count change is $(-1,1)$ or $(1,-1)$.

Let $N_{\mathrm{dom}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{tr}}$ denote their respective numbers.

Trades preserve $a+b$, while dominance steps increase it by at least one. Therefore
$$ N_{\mathrm{dom}}\leq h_1+h_2\leq2r. $$

To bound trades, define
$$ d=qa-pb, \qquad s=p+q. $$

A trade $(-1,1)$ improves precisely when
$$ \frac{q}{b+1}-\frac{p}{a}>0, $$
equivalently $d>p$. It changes $d$ to $d-s$.

A trade $(1,-1)$ improves precisely when
$$ \frac{p}{a+1}-\frac{q}{b}>0, $$
equivalently $d<-q$. It changes $d$ to $d+s$.

Define the nonnegative integer potential
$$ \Phi(d)= \begin{cases} \left\lceil(d-p)/s\right\rceil,&d>p,\\ 0,&-q\leq d\leq p,\\ \left\lceil(-q-d)/s\right\rceil,&d<-q. \end{cases} $$

Every improving trade decreases $\Phi$ by exactly one. For example, if $d>p$, then $d-s>-q$, so the trade cannot cross directly into the opposite improving-trade region. It either remains above $p$, reducing the displayed ceiling by one, or enters $[-q,p]$, in which case the previous potential was one.

A dominance step changes $d$ by one of
$$ q,\qquad -p,\qquad q-p. $$
Its absolute change is less than $s$. Distance to the interval $[-q,p]$ is a $1$-Lipschitz function, so a dominance step increases $\Phi$ by at most one.

We must also control the initial potential, which need not be bounded by $r$. Consider the trajectory before its first state with $d\in[-q,p]$, if such a state exists.

Neither a trade nor a dominance step can move directly from $d>p$ to $d<-q$, or conversely: the distance between these two open regions exceeds $s$, while every step changes $d$ by at most $s$. Hence, before the first visit to the interval, all trades have the same direction.

Their receiving count only increases during this initial portion, including during dominance steps. Its available range has width at most $r$. Consequently, this portion contains at most $r$ trades.

After the first visit to the interval, the potential starts at zero. Since every trade reduces it by one and every dominance step increases it by at most one, the remaining number of trades is at most the remaining number of dominance steps.

If the interval is never visited, all trades have the same direction throughout, so there are at most $r$ trades anyway. In both cases,
$$ N_{\mathrm{tr}}\leq r+N_{\mathrm{dom}}. $$
Therefore
$$ T=N_{\mathrm{tr}}+N_{\mathrm{dom}} \leq r+2N_{\mathrm{dom}} \leq5r. $$

This proof does not depend on the pivot rule or on the bit lengths of the masses.

3.2. Matching asymptotic lower bound

Assume $r\geq1$. Choose $A\subseteq C$ of size $r$ and a size-$k$ initial committee disjoint from $A$. Such a committee exists because $r\leq m-k$.

Use the two distinct approval types
$$ A,\qquad C\setminus A $$
with masses
$$ p=\frac{r+1}{r+2}, \qquad q=\frac1{r+2}. $$

At a state with $a=|A\cap W|<r$, replacing a candidate outside $A$ by one inside $A$ changes the score by
$$ \frac{p}{a+1}-\frac{q}{k-a}>0. $$
Indeed,
$$ \frac{p}{q}=r+1> \frac{a+1}{k-a}, $$
because $a+1\leq r$ and $k-a\geq1$.

The reverse swap has negative gain:
$$ \frac{q}{k-a+1}-\frac{p}{a}<0 \qquad(a>0). $$
Swaps within either approval class have zero gain.

Thus every improving swap increases $a$ by one, and the prescribed trajectory has exactly
$$ T=r $$
swaps. Together with the upper bound, this proves worst-case order $\Theta(r)$ for $\tau=2$.

For comparison, if $\tau=1$, the exact length is
$$ T=\min\{k,|A_1|\}-|A_1\cap W_0|. $$
Every improvement increases the sole count by one, and such a swap exists whenever that count is below its maximum.

4. Encoding arbitrary objectives on a fixed-size slice

The next lemma permits longer trajectories when support size is sufficiently large.

4.1. Encoding lemma

Fix $1\leq r\leq m$. Let $f$ be any integer-valued function on the $r$-subsets of $C$.

There is a positive rational-mass PAV instance whose score, on size-$r$ committees $U$, has the form
$$ S_\mu(U)=\alpha f(U)+\beta $$
for constants $\alpha>0,\beta$.

There is also such an instance satisfying
$$ S_\mu(C\setminus U)=\alpha f(U)+\beta $$
for every size-$r$ subset $U$.

Both constructions can use precisely all nonempty approval sets of size at most $r$, together with one empty approval type.

Harmonic expansions

For $0\leq t\leq d$,
$$ H_t=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}\binom tj. $$
To verify this, expand $(1-x)^t$ in
$$ H_t=\int_0^1\frac{1-(1-x)^t}{x}\,dx. $$

We also need
$$ H_{d-t} = H_d-\sum_{j=1}^{t} \frac{1}{j\binom dj}\binom tj. $$
The coefficient of $\binom tj$ in the finite-difference expansion of $H_{d-t}$ is, for $j\geq1$,
$$ -\int_0^1x^{d-j}(1-x)^{j-1}\,dx = -\frac{(d-j)!(j-1)!}{d!} = -\frac{1}{j\binom dj}. $$
The integral formula follows by applying $j$ forward differences to
$$ H_{d-t}=\int_0^1\frac{1-x^{d-t}}{1-x}\,dx. $$
The factorial evaluation follows by repeated integration by parts. Finite-difference expansion is exact here because $t$ ranges over the integers from zero to $d$.

For $B\subseteq C$, put
$$ I_B(U)=\mathbf1[B\subseteq U]. $$
Then
$$ \binom{|A\cap U|}{j} = \sum_{\substack{B\subseteq A\\|B|=j}}I_B(U). $$

Construction for selected subsets

Initially use unnormalized positive rational weights $w_A$ for every nonempty approval set $A$ of size at most $r$.

The coefficient of $I_B(U)$, where $|B|=j$, in the resulting score is
$$ \frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j} \sum_{A\supseteq B}w_A. $$

Let
$$ V=\max_{|U|=r}|f(U)|, \qquad K=V+1. $$
For every $r$-subset $A$, choose
$$ w_A=r\bigl(K+(-1)^{r+1}f(A)\bigr)>0. $$
Its degree-$r$ coefficient is
$$ (-1)^{r+1}K+f(A). $$

Proceed downward through $j=r-1,\ldots,1$. At degree $j$, all weights on larger approval sets have already been fixed. Define
$$ t_B=\sum_{A\supsetneq B}w_A, \qquad K_j=1+\left\lceil\max_{|B|=j}t_B\right\rceil, \qquad w_B=K_j-t_B>0. $$

Every degree-$j$ coefficient is now the same number,
$$ \gamma_j=\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}K_j. $$

For a size-$r$ subset $U$, there are exactly $\binom rj$ contained $j$-subsets. Also, its only contained $r$-subset is $U$ itself. Therefore the unnormalized score is
$$ \widehat S(U) = f(U)+(-1)^{r+1}K +\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\gamma_j\binom rj. $$
Everything except $f(U)$ is constant.

Construction for omitted subsets

Now evaluate the score on $C\setminus U$. An approval set $A$ of size $d$ contributes
$$ w_AH_{d-|A\cap U|}. $$
The coefficient of $I_B(U)$, where $|B|=j$, is
$$ -\frac1j \sum_{A\supseteq B} \frac{w_A}{\binom{|A|}{j}}. $$

Choose top-level weights
$$ w_A=r(K-f(A))>0 \qquad(|A|=r). $$
Their degree-$r$ coefficients are $-K+f(A)$.

At degree $j<r$, define
$$ t_B= \sum_{A\supsetneq B} \frac{w_A}{\binom{|A|}{j}}, \qquad K_j=1+\left\lceil\max_{|B|=j}t_B\right\rceil, \qquad w_B=K_j-t_B>0. $$
Every degree-$j$ coefficient then equals $-K_j/j$. Thus
$$ \widehat S(C\setminus U)=f(U)+\text{constant}. $$

Normalization

In either construction, clear a common denominator to obtain positive integer weights. Let their sum be $M$. Choose a power of two $Z>M$, add the empty approval type with weight $Z-M$, and divide all weights by $Z$.

All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. The empty type contributes zero. Clearing denominators and normalization multiply every score difference by a positive constant, proving the lemma.

4.2. Bit lengths

Write
$$ N_r=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\binom mj. $$

The selected-subset construction uses integer weights. In the omitted-subset construction, a common denominator divides
$$ E= \prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\prod_{d=j+1}^{r}\binom dj. $$
This follows inductively: at degree $j$, the only new divisions are by $\binom dj$ for larger approval sets of size $d$. Since $\binom dj\leq2^d$,
$$ \log_2E=O(r^3). $$

Let $R$ denote the sum of weights already constructed. A new degree-$j$ weight is at most $R+2$. Adding all degree-$j$ weights therefore satisfies
$$ R_{\mathrm{new}}+2 \leq \left(\binom mj+1\right)(R+2). $$
The top-level total is at most $\binom mr\,r(2V+1)$. Consequently,
$$ \log_2(R_{\mathrm{final}}+2) = O\!\left(\log(V+1)+r^2\log(m+1)\right). $$

After clearing denominators and dyadic normalization, every mass has
$$ O\!\left(r^3+r^2\log(m+1)+\log(V+1)\right) $$
bits.

These estimates also establish that the construction requires polynomial input length for each fixed $r$ when $V$ is polynomial in $m$.

5. Long trajectories for arbitrary committee sizes

5.1. A lexicographic Hamilton trajectory on a product

Consider $d$ coordinates, each taking values in $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. The local moves change one coordinate to any other value.

Use the rule: choose the smallest coordinate admitting an improvement, then the smallest improving alternative value.

We construct, from every starting vector $x$, a Hamilton path $P_d(x)$ that this rule follows when each vector's objective value is its position in the path.

For $d=1$, list the starting value first, followed by every other value in increasing order.

For $d>1$, order the last-coordinate values as follows: its starting value first, followed by the remaining values in increasing order. In each such fibre, run the recursively constructed path on the first $d-1$ coordinates. The first fibre starts at the given prefix; every subsequent fibre starts at the terminal prefix of the previous fibre.

This lists all $n^d$ vectors exactly once, with adjacent vectors differing in one coordinate.

To verify the pivot rule, consider a nonterminal state within a fibre. By induction, a move in one of the first $d-1$ coordinates advances to the next path state, and the prescribed rule chooses that move. Moves in coordinate $d$ have lower priority.

At the terminal state of a fibre, every vector in that fibre has already been visited. Hence no move in the first $d-1$ coordinates improves. Moves to earlier fibres decrease the objective, while moves to later fibres increase it. The smallest later last-coordinate value is exactly the next fibre in the construction.

At the last state of the last fibre, every possible destination has smaller objective value. Thus the path is the complete trajectory.

5.2. Transfer to PAV

Assume $r\geq1$ and put
$$ n=\lfloor m/r\rfloor\geq2. $$
Choose disjoint groups
$$ G_j=\{c_{j,1},\ldots,c_{j,n}\}, \qquad 1\leq j\leq r. $$
Order candidates by group first and value second; put any leftover candidates last.

Call an $r$-subset valid if it contains exactly one candidate from each group and no leftover candidate. Valid subsets correspond to vectors in $\{1,\ldots,n\}^r$.

Assign to each valid subset its position, starting at zero, in the path $P_r((1,\ldots,1))$. Assign value $-1$ to every invalid $r$-subset. This defines an integer function $f$ on all $r$-subsets.

If $k=r$, use the selected-subset encoding from Section 4. Starting from a valid committee:

  • a swap yielding an invalid committee decreases the score;
  • a valid swap changes one coordinate;
  • for a fixed inserted candidate, the only valid removal is the current candidate in that same group.

Therefore the lexicographic pair rule implements exactly the coordinate-priority rule of Section 5.1.

If $k=m-r$, use the omitted-subset encoding and start with the complement of the initial valid subset. A swap transforms the omitted set by removing $b$ and inserting $a$. For it to stay valid, these candidates must belong to the same group. The outer ordering on $b$ prioritizes the smallest group, and the inner ordering on $a$ prioritizes the smallest alternative value. Thus the same coordinate rule applies.

In either case, the complete trajectory has
$$ T=n^r-1=\lfloor m/r\rfloor^r-1. $$

The support size is
$$ \tau=N_r+1. $$
Here $V\leq n^r$, so incidence-vector encoding gives
$$ L= O\!\left( \tau\left[m+r^3+r^2\log(m+1)\right] \right). $$

5.3. Consequences

Let $\mathsf T_{m,k}$ be the maximum trajectory length over all supports, rational masses, orders, and initial committees, with $m,k$ fixed. The preceding construction and the committee-count upper bound prove
$$ \lfloor m/r\rfloor^r-1 \leq \mathsf T_{m,k} \leq \binom mr-1. $$

Because $m/r\geq2$, the quantities
$$ \log\lfloor m/r\rfloor \quad\text{and}\quad \log(em/r) $$
differ by at most universal constant factors. Combining this with
$$ \binom mr\leq(em/r)^r $$
gives
$$ \log(\mathsf T_{m,k}+1) = \Theta\!\left(r\log(em/r)\right). $$

For fixed $r$,
$$ \lfloor m/r\rfloor^r=\Theta(m^r), \qquad \binom mr=\Theta(m^r), $$
so the bounds are multiplicatively tight. In particular, both $k=2$ and $k=m-2$ admit worst-case length $\Theta(m^2)$.

This construction establishes the dependence on $m,k$ when $\tau,L$ are unrestricted. Its support can be large; the next construction establishes the superquasipolynomial claim with polynomial input length.

6. Exponential trajectories with small support

6.1. Signed Max-Cut encoding

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a simple graph with nonzero signed integer weights $w_e$. Put
$$ s=|V|, \qquad R=\sum_{e\in E}|w_e|, \qquad P=8R+1. $$

Use candidates $v^0,v^1$ for each vertex, and committee size $s$. A valid committee chooses exactly one candidate from each vertex pair:
$$ W(x)=\{v^{x_v}:v\in V\}. $$

Use these unnormalized approval weights:

  • $\{v^0,v^1\}$ has weight $P$ for every vertex;
  • for a positive edge $uv$ of weight $w$, the types $\{u^0,v^0\}$ and $\{u^1,v^1\}$ each have weight $2w$;
  • for a negative edge $uv$ of weight $-q$, the types $\{u^0,v^1\}$ and $\{u^1,v^0\}$ each have weight $2q$.

These approval types are distinct because the graph is simple.

For a positive edge, its two types contribute $3w$ when the endpoint states agree and $4w$ when they disagree. For a negative edge, they contribute $4q$ when the states agree and $3q$ when they disagree. Hence
$$ \widehat S(W(x)) = sP+ \sum_{w_e>0}3w_e+ \sum_{w_e<0}4|w_e| + \sum_{uv\in E}w_{uv}\mathbf1[x_u\neq x_v]. $$

Thus a within-pair swap improves exactly when the corresponding vertex flip improves the signed cut.

A swap between different vertex pairs changes the two vertex-type counts from $(1,1)$ to $(0,2)$, losing $P/2$. Each edge type can change its harmonic contribution by at most one, and all edge-type weights sum to $4R$. Therefore its total gain is at most
$$ -P/2+4R=-1/2<0. $$
Every improving swap from a valid committee remains valid.

Normalize dyadically by adding an empty type as in Section 4. This preserves all improvement signs. If the signed cut has a unique improving flip at each nonterminal trajectory state, the encoded PAV instance has a unique improving swap there. Its trajectory is then independent of candidate order.

6.2. Applying the external theorem

Michel and Scott construct simple signed integer-weighted graphs with $8h+4$ vertices, $13h+3$ edges, maximum absolute weight at most $2\cdot8^{h+1}$, and an initial cut whose unique improving flip trajectory has length $\Omega(3^h)$. These are the graphs in Section 2 and Theorem 2.1 of [Michel–Scott, *Superpolynomial smoothed complexity of 3-FLIP in Local Max-Cut*](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/scott/Papers/flip.pdf).

All encoding hypotheses hold: the graphs are simple, their edge weights are nonzero signed integers, and their improving trajectory is unique. The resulting parameters are
$$ m=16h+8,\qquad k=8h+4,\qquad \tau=34h+11, $$
with
$$ T=\Omega(3^h). $$

The integer weights used in the encoding and normalization have $O(h+\log h)$ bits. There are $O(h)$ approval types, each of size two except the empty type. Even incidence-vector encoding therefore gives
$$ L=O(h^2). $$

Let $N=m+\tau+L$. Then $N=O(h^2)$, while $T=2^{\Omega(h)}$. For every fixed $c>0$,
$$ (\log N)^c=o(h). $$
Consequently, these trajectories exceed every bound $2^{O((\log N)^c)}$ with fixed $c$. The superquasipolynomial alternative is proved.

Together with $T<2^m$, this also gives exponential order $2^{\Theta(m)}$ in the balanced regime, already with $\tau=\Theta(m)$ and polynomial input length.

6.3. Small-support padding

The construction also gives, for sufficiently large $r$, instances with
$$ T=2^{\Omega(r)},\qquad \tau=O(r), $$
and input length polynomial in $m$.

Choose a core with $s=8h+4\leq r$ vertex pairs, taking the largest admissible $h$. Add $d=k-s$ initially selected padding candidates and $m-k-s$ unapproved candidates.

Let $M$ be the total unnormalized core weight. If $d>0$, add one type approving exactly the selected padding candidates, with weight $d(M+1)$.

Removing a selected padding candidate loses
$$ d(M+1)(H_d-H_{d-1})=M+1, $$
while all core types together can gain at most $M$. Such a swap cannot improve.

Replacing a core candidate by an unapproved candidate loses its vertex-type contribution $P$, while edge types can gain at most $4R<P$. Cross-pair core swaps were already excluded. Thus padding membership stays fixed and the core trajectory is preserved.

All weights have polynomial bit length in $m$, and dyadic normalization completes the construction.

7. Exact bit running time

The following algorithm computes the prescribed trajectory, including its terminal committee.

Maintain the intersection counts
$$ c_i=|A_i\cap W|. $$
For a proposed swap $(b,a)$, type $i$ contributes
$$ \Delta_i= \begin{cases} \mu_i/(c_i+1),&b\in A_i,\ a\notin A_i,\\ -\mu_i/c_i,&a\in A_i,\ b\notin A_i,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$

A denominator is never zero in a nonzero contribution. Moreover, it belongs to $J$: the new intersection count must remain within $[\ell_i,u_i]$.

Precompute the integers
$$ g_{i,t}=(Q\mu_i)D/t, \qquad \ell_i<t\leq u_i. $$
There are at most $\tau r$ entries. The sign of a swap gain is exactly the sign of a sum of at most $\tau$ signed entries.

Set
$$ B= 2+\left\lceil\log_2(QD+1)\right\rceil +\left\lceil\log_2(m+1)\right\rceil. $$
Then
$$ B= O\!\left( L+\min\{k,\tau r\log(k+1)\}+\log(m+1) \right). $$
