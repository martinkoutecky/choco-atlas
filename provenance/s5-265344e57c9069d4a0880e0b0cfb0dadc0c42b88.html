The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(T_M,T_W\), rational masses \(\mu^M_a,\mu^W_b\geq 0\) with \(\sum_a\mu^M_a+\sum_b\mu^W_b=1\), direct match values \(p^a_b\) and \(p^b_a\), and per-capita externality coefficients \(e^z_{cd}\), does there exist a measurable one-to-one matching with no neutral blocking coalition of positive mass? A matching is represented by flows \(x_{ab}\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{ab}\leq\mu^M_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\leq\mu^W_b\); each type's utility is its direct value from its match, or zero if unmatched, plus \(\sum_{c,d}x_{cd}e^z_{cd}\). A blocking coalition may select fractions of types, rearrange its members' matches, and sever their matches to outsiders while outsider-to-outsider matches remain unchanged; almost every member must form or sever a match, members must weakly gain almost everywhere, and a positive mass must strictly gain.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: finite disjoint type sets \(T_M,T_W\), listing only types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu^M_a,\mu^W_b\), with \(\sum_a\mu^M_a+\sum_b\mu^W_b=1\). Let \(\tau=|T_M|+|T_W|\) be the support size, not the number of possible types, \(m=\max\{|T_M|,|T_W|\}\), and \(L\) the total input bit length. All direct values \(p^a_b,p^b_a\) and externality coefficients \(e^z_{cd}\), for \(z\in T_M\cup T_W\) and \((c,d)\in T_M\times T_W\), are signed binary rationals; utilities are nontransferable.
Represent each type by a disjoint atomless interval of its prescribed mass. A matching is a measurable, measure-preserving partial bijection between the two sides; its flows \(x_{ab}\) are matched masses, satisfying \(x_{ab}\geq0\), \(\sum_bx_{ab}\leq\mu^M_a\), and \(\sum_ax_{ab}\leq\mu^W_b\). Matching flows and coalition masses may be real.
An individual of type \(a\in T_M\) matched to type \(b\) receives \(p^a_b+\sum_{c,d}x_{cd}e^a_{cd}\); the other endpoint receives \(p^b_a+\sum_{c,d}x_{cd}e^b_{cd}\). An unmatched individual receives only the externality sum. Utilities are evaluated individually, not averaged within types; equality is indifference, with no tie-breaking.
A neutral blocking coalition is a measurable set of positive mass that changes the matching by forming new matches only between its members and severing existing matches with at least one member as endpoint. Severance is unilateral; outsider-to-outsider matches remain unchanged, and outsiders form no new matches. Almost every member must form or sever an actual match, weakly gain relative to their own initial utility, and a positive mass of members must strictly gain. Changing to a different individual of the same type counts as an action. A matching is neutral setwise stable if no such coalition exists.

Is deciding whether a neutral setwise stable matching exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents with signed additive match effects, neutral one-to-one setwise stable matching existence is NP-hard, and checking a given matching is coNP-complete ([source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2013/docs/p295.pdf)).
Distributional matching models have existence results for pairwise stability with aggregate externalities ([source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/ECTA16228)); these concern a different stability notion. No result settling the exact positive-mass coalitional decision problem above was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Discarded zero-mass types so that the input lists precisely the positive-mass support; retained the stipulated total-mass normalization.
  • Specified signed binary-rational direct values and externality coefficients, following the unrestricted signed discrete utility domain.
  • Realized each type as an atomless interval and matchings as measurable measure-preserving partial bijections; allowed real matching flows and coalition masses.
  • Made utility comparisons individual rather than type averages, and counted changes of actual partners within the same type as actions.
  • Specified unilateral severance, mutual coalition membership for new matches, nontransferable utilities, and indifference without tie-breaking.
  • Defined the otherwise absent size parameter m as the larger side's support size; L is the total input bit length.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\) under polynomial-time many-one reductions. This holds even when all direct values and externality coefficients are integers and all masses are dyadic rationals.

The proof below addresses the stated positive-mass coalitional notion directly. In particular, it allows arbitrary real matching flows and uses the rule that switching to another individual of the same type counts as an action.

1. A necessary condition for stability

We use the elementary fact that measurable subsets of atomless intervals of equal mass admit measurable, measure-preserving bijections modulo null sets. Such maps can be constructed using cumulative Lebesgue measure and generalized inverses. All matching constructions below may disregard null sets, consistently with the almost-everywhere requirements in the question.

Lemma. If a matching is neutral setwise stable, then every positive-flow edge has nonnegative direct value for both endpoints.

Proof. Suppose an edge \((a,b)\) has positive flow and an endpoint type \(z\) receives negative direct value \(p<0\) on this edge. Choose a measurable mass \(\varepsilon>0\) of these endpoints and let them unilaterally sever their matches.

The only flow change is a decrease of \(\varepsilon\) on \((a,b)\). Each coalition member therefore gains

\(-p-\varepsilon e^z_{ab}\).

Choose \(\varepsilon\) smaller than the available matched mass and sufficiently small that \(\varepsilon|e^z_{ab}|<-p\). Every member then strictly gains and severs an actual match. This is a neutral blocking coalition of positive mass, a contradiction. \(\square\)

2. Reduction construction

Reduce from the classical positive-integer Subset Sum problem: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) and a target \(K\), decide whether some subset sums to \(K\). Its \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness is classical; see [Karp’s original reductions](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Write \(A=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\). We restrict to \(n\ge1\) and \(1\le K\le A\). This restriction preserves hardness: excluded cases are immediately decidable and can be replaced by fixed yes or no instances satisfying the restriction.

Types and masses

For each item introduce male types \(u_i,v_i\) and female types \(s_i,t_i\). Also introduce male test types \(r_-,r_+\) and female test types \(w_-,w_+\). These are all distinct.

Let \(N=4n+4\), choose the smallest integer \(q\) with \(2^q\ge N\), and put \(h=2^{-q}\). Give every listed type mass \(h\).

If \(Nh<1\), add one dummy male type of mass \(1-Nh\). If \(Nh=1\), omit it. Thus every supported type has positive dyadic mass, and the total mass is exactly one.

Direct values

Set \(D=2A+2\). Initially give both endpoints of every type pair direct value \(-D\). Override these values inside each item gadget as follows:

| Male type | Female type | Direct values: male, female |
|---|---|---|
| \(u_i\) | \(s_i\) | \((2,1)\) |
| \(v_i\) | \(t_i\) | \((2,1)\) |
| \(u_i\) | \(t_i\) | \((1,2)\) |
| \(v_i\) | \(s_i\) | \((1,2)\) |

Also set

\((p^{r_-}_{w_-},p^{w_-}_{r_-})=(-2K-1,0)\),

\((p^{r_+}_{w_+},p^{w_+}_{r_+})=(-2(A-K)-1,0)\).

Call the four internal edges of each item gadget and these two test edges *designated edges*. Every other edge retains direct value \(-D\) for both endpoints.

Externalities

Set all externality coefficients to zero except the following.

For the test types, set

\(e^{r_-}_{u_i s_i}=-2a_i/h\), and \(e^{r_+}_{u_i s_i}=2a_i/h\).

For the item types, set:

| Affected types | Coefficient on \((r_-,w_-)\) | Coefficient on \((r_+,w_+)\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(u_i,v_i\) | \(1/h\) | \(-1/h\) |
| \(s_i,t_i\) | \(-1/h\) | \(1/h\) |

Each entry applies separately to both types in its row. The auxiliary types \(w_-,w_+\), and the dummy type if present, have no externalities.

All direct values and externality coefficients are integers.

3. Every stable matching encodes a subset

Let a neutral setwise stable matching be given.

By the lemma, no undesignated edge can have positive flow. Neither test edge can have positive flow either, because its male endpoint receives negative direct value. Consequently:

  • all test and auxiliary types, and the dummy type, are unmatched;
  • matches occur only inside item gadgets;
  • every item type receives zero externality.

Moreover, any deviation confined to an item gadget leaves its members’ externalities unchanged: their externalities depend only on the two test edges.

Item gadgets cannot split their populations

Consider any item type \(z\). Its possible initial direct-utility categories are \(0\) when unmatched and \(1\) or \(2\) according to its partner type.

Suppose two different categories have positive mass. Let \(y\) be a partner type occurring in the higher category. Choose equal positive masses \(\varepsilon\) of:

  • individuals of type \(z\) in the lower category;
  • individuals of type \(y\) currently matched to type \(z\).

Form new matches between these two sets, severing their previous matches.

Every selected individual of type \(z\) strictly improves their direct utility. Every selected individual of type \(y\) retains the same partner type and hence the same direct utility. Their externalities are unchanged.

Every member acts: the selected type-\(z\) individuals acquire a different partner type, while the selected type-\(y\) individuals acquire different individuals of the same type. In particular, their old partners cannot be among the selected lower-category individuals. Thus this is a valid positive-mass blocking coalition.

It follows that each item type occupies only one direct-utility category up to null sets. Therefore, an item type that has any positive matched mass must be fully matched, to one partner type only.

Since all four type masses are \(h\), every positive-flow edge inside the gadget must have flow \(h\), and positive-flow edges cannot share a type. If there were fewer than two such edges, there would be an entirely unmatched male type and an entirely unmatched female type in the gadget. They could form positive-mass matches, strictly improving both endpoints.

Hence each gadget has exactly one of these two configurations:

  • Diagonal: \(x_{u_i s_i}=x_{v_i t_i}=h\).
  • Off-diagonal: \(x_{u_i t_i}=x_{v_i s_i}=h\).

All other flows in that gadget are zero.

Let \(I\) be the set of diagonal gadgets, and write \(\sigma=\sum_{i\in I}a_i\). The initial utilities of the unmatched test types are then

\(U_{r_-}=-2\sigma\), and \(U_{r_+}=2\sigma\).

This argument establishes the binary encoding for arbitrary real flows.

4. Stability forces the encoded sum to equal the target

If \(\sigma>K\), the negative test blocks

Take a coalition containing the entire populations of \(r_-,w_-\), and all four types in every diagonal gadget.

The coalition:

  • forms mass \(h\) of matches on \((r_-,w_-)\);
  • switches every diagonal gadget to its off-diagonal configuration;
  • leaves all other matches unchanged.

Each switched gadget man loses one unit of direct utility and gains one unit of externality from the new test edge. Each switched gadget woman gains one unit of direct utility and loses one unit of externality. Thus every switched individual is exactly indifferent.

Members of \(w_-\) are also indifferent. Members of \(r_-\) gain

\(2\sigma-(2K+1)=2(\sigma-K)-1\).

Because \(\sigma\) and \(K\) are integers and \(\sigma>K\), this gain is at least one.

Every member forms a new actual match, and every switched gadget member also severs an old match. Outsider-to-outsider matches remain unchanged. A mass \(h>0\) of test members strictly gains. This is a neutral blocking coalition.

If \(\sigma<K\), the positive test blocks

Take instead the entire populations of \(r_+,w_+\), together with all four types in every off-diagonal gadget.

Form mass \(h\) on \((r_+,w_+)\), and switch every off-diagonal gadget to its diagonal configuration. The resulting diagonal sum is \(A\).

Each switched gadget man gains one unit of direct utility and loses one unit of externality. Each switched gadget woman loses one unit of direct utility and gains one unit of externality. Again, every switched individual is exactly indifferent, as are members of \(w_+\).

Members of \(r_+\) gain

\(2(A-\sigma)-\bigl(2(A-K)+1\bigr)=2(K-\sigma)-1\),

which is at least one.

This is another valid neutral blocking coalition with strictly gaining mass \(h\).

Therefore every stable matching satisfies \(\sigma=K\). In particular, stable existence implies that the Subset Sum instance has a solution.

5. A solution subset produces a stable matching

Conversely, suppose \(I\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=K\).

Construct a matching that is diagonal in gadgets indexed by \(I\), off-diagonal in all other gadgets, and leaves every test, auxiliary, and dummy individual unmatched. Equal-length intervals can be matched by translations, so this is a measurable, measure-preserving matching.

We prove stability against an arbitrary neutral coalition and arbitrary real deviation flows \(x'\).

Test types cannot participate with positive mass

Define

\(Q=\sum_{i=1}^n (a_i/h)x'_{u_i s_i}\).

Feasibility gives \(0\le x'_{u_i s_i}\le h\), hence \(0\le Q\le A\). Initially the test utilities are \(-2K\) and \(2K\).

A member of \(r_-\) newly matched to \(w_-\) would gain

\((-2K-1)-2Q-(-2K)=-1-2Q<0\).

A member of \(r_+\) newly matched to \(w_+\) would gain

\(\bigl(-2(A-K)-1\bigr)+2Q-2K=2Q-2A-1<0\).

If either test type instead acquires any other partner type, its direct value is \(-D\). Its gain is respectively

\(-D+2K-2Q\), or \(-D+2Q-2K\).

Both are at most \(2A-D=-2<0\).

These bounds use only flow feasibility; they allow deviations that mix gadgets or use undesignated edges.

Since the test types are initially unmatched, any member performing an allowed action must form a new match. Every such match strictly harms that member. Therefore a blocking coalition contains no positive mass of either test type. New matches require both endpoints to be coalition members, so both test-edge flows remain zero.

Auxiliary and dummy types cannot participate with positive mass

With no positive mass of test members, an auxiliary individual cannot form its designated zero-direct-value test match. Every other new match gives it direct value \(-D\), and it has no externalities. Thus it cannot act while weakly gaining.

The dummy type, if present, likewise has no externalities and strictly negative direct values for every partner.

Consequently, almost every member of a blocking coalition would have to belong to an item gadget.

Item members cannot supply strict gains

Because the test-edge flows remain zero, every item member’s externality remains zero.

Every item individual initially receives positive direct utility. Becoming unmatched therefore strictly harms them. Matching outside their own gadget gives direct value \(-D\), which also strictly harms them. Thus every acting item member who weakly gains must finish matched within their own gadget.

In an initially diagonal gadget, every man initially receives direct value \(2\). Weak improvement requires his final partner type to remain his diagonal partner type. Every newly formed edge there must therefore be diagonal, and its woman endpoint also retains her initial direct value \(1\).

In an initially off-diagonal gadget, every woman initially receives direct value \(2\). Weak improvement requires her final partner type to remain her off-diagonal partner type. Every newly formed edge there must therefore be off-diagonal, and its man endpoint retains his initial direct value \(1\).

Individuals whose original matches remain unchanged also have unchanged utilities. Hence almost every weakly gaining coalition member is indifferent. There is no positive mass of strictly gaining members, contradicting the blocking requirement.

The constructed matching is therefore neutral setwise stable.

6. Polynomial size and conclusion

The construction has at most \(4n+5\) supported types, with \(m\le2n+3\).

The exponent defining \(h\) has size \(O(\log(n+1))\). Every direct value and externality coefficient has bit length \(O(\log(A+1)+\log(n+1))\). Listing all direct values and the complete externality array requires only polynomial output size, since there are \(O(n^3)\) externality entries.

We have proved the equivalence:

\(\text{A neutral setwise stable matching exists}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{some subset of the }a_i\text{ sums to }K.\)

Thus the exact positive-mass coalitional existence problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\). An algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
