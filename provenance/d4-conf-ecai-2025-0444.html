The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), valuations \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^d\), and per-capita supplies \(q_j\), with cash supply \(\sigma=cv_{\max}\) for fixed \(c>1/2\), compute a finite-support plan \(\pi_t\) over integral bundles \(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^d\) and cash \(r\ge0\). Require \(\pi_t\) to have total mass \(\mu_t\), \(\sum_t\int k_j\,d\pi_t\le q_j\), and \(\sum_t\int r\,d\pi_t\le\sigma\); achieve \(W(\pi)=\exp(\sum_t\int\log(v_t\cdot k+r)\,d\pi_t)\ge(1-\epsilon)F/B(c)\) in time \(f(d)\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,1/\epsilon)\), where \(F\) is the fractional MNW optimum under the same per-capita supplies.

Result. Yes. The finite-agent theorem in the question, together with fixed-dimensional integer programming and exact oracle linear programming, gives the requested algorithm. The output can have at most \(\tau+m+1\) atoms in total.

The proof uses finite replication only to establish the existence of a bounded witness. The algorithm searches for a suitable plan through an implicit linear program.

1. Algorithmic tools

We use two standard results.

First, a bounded rational integer linear program with \(D\) variables can be optimized exactly in time \(g(D)S^{O(1)}\), where \(S\) is its binary encoding length, \(g\) is computable, and the polynomial exponent is independent of \(D\). Rational coefficients can be cleared; optimization follows from integer feasibility by binary search over the bounded objective range. This is the fixed-parameter form of the Lenstra–Kannan integer-programming theorem. [Kannan, *Minkowski’s Convex Body Theorem and Integer Programming*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/moor.12.3.415).

Second, we use the following consequence of exact rational oracle linear programming:

Consider a finite rational linear program with \(s\) rows and an implicitly represented set of columns. Suppose all column coefficients, objective coefficients, and right-hand sides have a known uniform binary encoding bound \(\beta\). If an exact separation oracle for its dual returns an original violated constraint, then a feasible, bounded primal can be optimized exactly, with an explicit optimal primal solution recovered, using polynomially many oracle calls and additional bit operations in \(s\) and \(\beta\).

This includes recovery of a polynomial number of primal columns; solving the resulting explicit restricted LP yields an optimal basic solution. The result applies also to unbounded or lower-dimensional dual polyhedra. [Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver, *Rational Polyhedra*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4_7).

2. A bounded witness from finite replication

If \(v_{\max}=0\), then \(\sigma=F=0\). Giving every type the single atom \((0,0)\), of mass \(\mu_t\), settles this case.

Henceforth suppose \(v_{\max}>0\), so \(\sigma>0\). In particular, giving everybody cash \(\sigma\) shows \(F\ge \sigma>0\).

Let \(N\) be the product of the denominators of all \(\mu_t\) and \(q_j\), in their input representations. Then

\(\displaystyle N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad K_j:=Nq_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\qquad \log N=O(L).\)

Consider a finite instance with \(N\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\), \(K_j\) separate copies of category \(j\), and total cash \(N\sigma\).

Its fractional MNW optimum equals \(F\). Indeed:

  • A feasible type allocation can be given to every agent of the corresponding type. For each category, the aggregate quantities can be distributed fractionally across its separate copies because their total demand is at most \(K_j\). The resulting finite-agent welfare equals the type welfare.
  • Conversely, average any finite fractional allocation within each type. These averages satisfy the original per-capita constraints. Concavity of \(\log\) shows that averaging does not decrease welfare.

These two directions establish equality of the optima.

There is a minor issue if categories attaining \(v_{\max}\) have zero supply. Define

\(\displaystyle V_0=\max_{\substack{t,j\\q_j>0}}v_{tj},\)

with \(V_0=0\) if no category has positive supply. If \(V_0=0\), supplied goods contribute no utility. Jensen’s inequality gives \(F=\sigma\), attained by uniform cash, so this case is also solved.

Otherwise, the finite instance has maximum item value \(V_0>0\), and its cash supply is

\(\displaystyle N\sigma=c_0NV_0,\qquad c_0:=\frac{cv_{\max}}{V_0}\ge c>\frac12.\)

The supplied finite-agent theorem applies. Rational valuations cause no difficulty: multiplying all valuations and cash by a common denominator makes valuations integral and preserves welfare ratios.

Also, \(B\) is nonincreasing on \((1/2,\infty)\). For every fixed \(\alpha\in[0,1]\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{2x}{2x-1+\alpha}=1+\frac{1-\alpha}{2x-1+\alpha}\)

is nonincreasing in \(x>1/2\). Raising to the nonnegative power \(\alpha\) and taking the maximum preserves that property.

Consequently, the finite-agent theorem supplies an allocation whose welfare is at least \(F/B(c_0)\ge F/B(c)\). Represent each agent by an atom of mass \(1/N\) in its type’s measure. This gives a feasible finite-support plan \(\pi^{(0)}\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle W(\pi^{(0)})\ge \frac{F}{B(c)},\qquad 0\le k_j\le K_j,\qquad 0\le r\le N\sigma.\)

Here we use exactly the finite-agent guarantee stated in the question. [Dementiev, Gravin and Ignatiev, Theorem 2](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.3233/FAIA251380).

The algorithm will not construct this expanded instance or this witness.

3. Discretizing cash while preserving feasibility

Set

\(\displaystyle \eta:=\frac{\epsilon}{8},\qquad a:=\eta\sigma,\qquad h:=\eta a=\eta^2\sigma.\)

At every atom of \(\pi^{(0)}\), first replace cash \(r\) by

\(\displaystyle r'=(1-\eta)r+\eta\sigma.\)

The total population mass is one, so total cash remains at most

\(\displaystyle (1-\eta)\sigma+\eta\sigma=\sigma.\)

Goods are unchanged. For an atom of type \(t\), writing its original utility as \(u=v_t\cdot k+r\), its new utility satisfies

\(\displaystyle u'=v_t\cdot k+r'\ge(1-\eta)u,\qquad u'\ge a.\)

Next round cash down to a multiple of \(h\):

\(\displaystyle r''=h\left\lfloor\frac{r'}h\right\rfloor.\)

This preserves feasibility. Since \(0\le r'-r''<h=\eta a\le\eta u'\),

\(\displaystyle u''=v_t\cdot k+r''\ge(1-\eta)u'.\)

The resulting plan \(\pi^{(2)}\) therefore satisfies

\(\displaystyle W(\pi^{(2)})\ge(1-\eta)^2\frac{F}{B(c)}.\)

Moreover, \(r'\le N\sigma\), because both \(r\) and \(\sigma\) are at most \(N\sigma\). Thus all its atoms have the form \((k,hz)\), where

\(\displaystyle 0\le k_j\le K_j,\qquad 0\le z\le Z:=\left\lfloor\frac{N\sigma}{h}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{N}{\eta^2}\right\rfloor.\)

Define

\(\displaystyle U_0:=\frac a2,\qquad M:=N\left(v_{\max}\sum_jq_j+\sigma\right).\)

Every atom of \(\pi^{(2)}\) has utility in \([U_0,M]\): the lower bound follows from \(u''\ge(1-\eta)a\ge a/2\), and the upper bound follows from the bounds on goods and cash.

All of \(N,K_j,Z,h,U_0,M\) have polynomial binary encoding length.

4. A polynomial number of utility thresholds

Let

\(\displaystyle U_\ell=U_0(1+\eta)^\ell,\)

and let \(H\) be the smallest integer such that \(U_H\ge M\). The thresholds can be constructed by exact rational multiplication and comparison.

Because

\(\displaystyle \frac{M}{U_0}=\frac{2N}{\eta}\left(1+\frac{\sum_jq_j}{c}\right)\)

and \(\log(1+\eta)\ge\eta/2\), we have

\(\displaystyle H=O\left(\frac{L+\log(1/\epsilon)}{\epsilon}\right).\)

Every threshold also has polynomial encoding length: it is a product of \(H\) rational factors of polynomial encoding length.

For each threshold, compute a rational coefficient \(b_\ell\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \log U_\ell-\eta\le b_\ell\le\log U_\ell.\)

These coefficients are computable in polynomial bit time. For completeness, range reduction writes a positive rational as \(2^ey\), with \(1\le y<2\). The identity

\(\displaystyle \log y=2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2i+1}}{2i+1},\qquad z=\frac{y-1}{y+1}\in[0,1/3)\)

has geometrically decreasing tails; the same series computes \(\log2\). Approximating both terms sufficiently accurately gives a rational approximation to \(\log U_\ell\) within \(\eta/2\). Subtracting \(\eta/2\) gives the required \(b_\ell\). The number of terms and the rational arithmetic lengths are polynomial in the input length and \(\log(1/\eta)\).

If \(u\in[U_0,M]\), choose the largest \(\ell\le H\) such that \(U_\ell\le u\). Then

\(\displaystyle b_\ell\ge\log u-\log(1+\eta)-\eta\ge\log u-2\eta.\)

Thus these finitely many labels approximate log utility from below.

5. The implicit linear program

For each type \(t\), threshold label \(\ell\), and integer vector \((k,z)\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle 0\le k_j\le K_j,\qquad 0\le z\le Z,\qquad v_t\cdot k+hz\ge U_\ell,\)

introduce a nonnegative variable \(y_{t,\ell,k,z}\). It represents population mass assigned the atom \((k,hz)\) with label \(\ell\).

Consider the finite, implicit LP

\(\displaystyle \max\sum_{t,\ell,k,z}b_\ell y_{t,\ell,k,z},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\ell,k,z}y_{t,\ell,k,z}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,\ell,k,z}k_jy_{t,\ell,k,z}\le q_j\quad\text{for every }j,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,\ell,k,z}hz\,y_{t,\ell,k,z}\le\sigma.\)

It has \(\tau+m+1\) rows. Its column set is finite, and every coefficient has a uniform polynomial encoding bound. It is feasible: label every atom of \(\pi^{(2)}\) by the threshold chosen in Section 4. It is bounded because its variables have total mass one and its objective coefficients belong to the finite list \(b_0,\ldots,b_H\).

Let its optimum be \(P^\star\). The labeled witness gives

\(\displaystyle P^\star\ge\log W(\pi^{(2)})-2\eta.\)

Conversely, every feasible LP solution specifies a feasible plan \(\pi\), after dropping labels and merging identical atoms. Column eligibility and the choice of \(b_\ell\) imply

\(\displaystyle \log(v_t\cdot k+hz)\ge\log U_\ell\ge b_\ell.\)

Therefore

\(\displaystyle \log W(\pi)\ge\sum_{t,\ell,k,z}b_\ell y_{t,\ell,k,z}.\)

6. Separation uses only \(m+1\) integer variables

The dual has unrestricted variables \(A_t\), nonnegative goods prices \(p_j\), and a nonnegative cash price \(\lambda\). Its objective is

\(\displaystyle \min\sum_t\mu_tA_t+\sum_jq_jp_j+\sigma\lambda,\)

and its column constraints are

\(\displaystyle A_t+p\cdot k+\lambda hz\ge b_\ell.\)

For a rational candidate dual point, first check \(p_j,\lambda\ge0\). For each pair \((t,\ell)\), solve exactly the bounded integer linear program

\(\displaystyle \min\;p\cdot k+\lambda hz,\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m},\quad z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad k_j\le K_j,\quad z\le Z,\quad v_t\cdot k+hz\ge U_\ell.\)

There are exactly \(m+1\) integer variables. All constraints and the objective are rational linear expressions.

If this problem is infeasible, that pair has no columns. Otherwise, its minimizing vector yields a violated dual constraint precisely when its minimum is less than \(b_\ell-A_t\). If no pair produces a violation, all dual constraints hold. This is an exact separation oracle returning original column constraints.

There are \(\tau(H+1)\) integer programs per oracle call. By the integer-programming theorem, the oracle runs in time

\(\displaystyle g(m+1)\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,1/\epsilon,\text{query length}).\)

The exact oracle-LP theorem now applies: the dual dimension is \(\tau+m+1\), and its inequalities have a uniform polynomial encoding bound. It recovers an explicit optimal primal solution in the desired parameterized running time.

Taking an optimal basic solution of the recovered restricted LP gives at most \(\tau+m+1\) positive atom variables. Its masses are binary rationals of polynomial length: each basic solution is determined by a rational system of at most \(\tau+m+1\) equations with polynomial-length coefficients, so determinant bounds give polynomial-length numerators and denominators. Its cash amounts \(hz\) likewise have polynomial encoding length.

7. Welfare guarantee and running time

Let \(\widehat\pi\) be the plan obtained from this optimal LP solution. Combining the proved inequalities gives

\(\displaystyle \log W(\widehat\pi)\ge P^\star\ge\log W(\pi^{(2)})-2\eta,\)

hence

\(\displaystyle W(\widehat\pi)\ge e^{-2\eta}(1-\eta)^2\frac{F}{B(c)}.\)

Since \(\eta=\epsilon/8<1/8\),

\(\displaystyle e^{-2\eta}(1-\eta)^2\ge(1-2\eta)^2\ge1-4\eta=1-\epsilon/2\ge1-\epsilon.\)

Thus

\(\displaystyle W(\widehat\pi)\ge(1-\epsilon)\frac{F}{B(c)}.\)

Feasibility holds exactly through the LP constraints. The algorithm uses polynomially many thresholds, polynomial-length rational data, and polynomially many oracle calls. Only integer optimization contributes a nonpolynomial parameter dependence, and its dimension is \(m+1\). Absorbing that dependence into a computable function \(f\), the total running time is

\(\displaystyle f(m)\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,1/\epsilon).\)
