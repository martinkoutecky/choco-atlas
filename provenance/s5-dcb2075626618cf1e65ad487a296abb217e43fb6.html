The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over rational locations on the line, a finite rational candidate set \(M\), a prediction \(\hat{o}\in M\), and rational \(\gamma\in[0,1)\), does there exist a deterministic anonymous mechanism \(F\) that is mass-group-strategy-proof and satisfies \(SC_\mu(F(\mu,M,\hat{o},\gamma))\leq \frac{3-\gamma}{1+\gamma}OPT_\mu\) whenever \(\hat{o}\) is optimal, and \(SC_\mu(F(\mu,M,\hat{o},\gamma))\leq \frac{3+\gamma}{1-\gamma}OPT_\mu\) for every prediction? Mass-group-strategy-proofness means no positive-mass submeasure of agents can replace its reports by another measure of equal mass and make almost all coalition members strictly better off. The candidate rule takes a weighted median of \(\mu+\gamma\delta_{\hat{o}}\) and returns a nearest site in \(M\), with fixed deterministic tie-breaking.

A society is a probability measure \(\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\delta_{x_i}\), with distinct \(x_i\in\mathbb Q\), positive binary-rational masses \(w_i\), and \(\sum_iw_i=1\). It is given by its support list; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(M\subset\mathbb Q\) be a nonempty finite candidate set, \(m=|M|\), \(\hat o\in M\), and \(\gamma\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1)\). Measure running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length, including \(\gamma\).
An agent at \(x\) has cost \(|x-y|\) when the facility is at \(y\in M\); define \(SC_\mu(y)=\int|x-y|\,d\mu(x)\) and \(OPT_\mu=\min_{y\in M}SC_\mu(y)\).
A deterministic anonymous mechanism \(F\) maps these inputs to a site in \(M\) and depends on agents only through their reported measure. During deviations, \(M,\hat o,\gamma\) stay fixed.
Mass-group-strategy-proofness means that, for every admissible \(\mu\), positive-mass finite-support rational submeasure \(\eta\leq\mu\), and finite-support rational replacement measure \(\rho\) with \(\rho(\mathbb Q)=\eta(\mathbb Q)\), it is impossible that \(|x-F(\mu-\eta+\rho,M,\hat o,\gamma)|<|x-F(\mu,M,\hat o,\gamma)|\) for \(\eta\)-almost every \(x\).
The specified median rule selects the smallest support point whose cumulative mass in \(\mu+\gamma\delta_{\hat o}\) is at least \((1+\gamma)/2\), then returns a nearest site in \(M\), choosing the smaller site in a tie.

Does there exist a deterministic anonymous mass-group-strategy-proof mechanism \(F\) satisfying, uniformly over all admissible inputs, \(SC_\mu(F(\mu,M,\hat o,\gamma))\leq\frac{3-\gamma}{1+\gamma}OPT_\mu\) whenever \(\hat o\in\arg\min_{y\in M}SC_\mu(y)\), and \(SC_\mu(F(\mu,M,\hat o,\gamma))\leq\frac{3+\gamma}{1-\gamma}OPT_\mu\) for every prediction, or is this pair of guarantees unattainable?

For finite equally weighted agents, adding \(\gamma n\) virtual agents at the prediction and selecting a nearest candidate to a median is deterministic, anonymous, group-strategy-proof, and achieves these two guarantees when \(\gamma n\) is integral. Result

The pair of guarantees is attainable. The specified median rule is deterministic, anonymous, and mass-group-strategy-proof, and it satisfies both bounds for rational masses.

The construction follows the virtual-voter median mechanism in the [cited finite-agent paper](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/40964/1/FL-predictions-accepted.pdf). The proof below works directly with measures and requires no replication into equally weighted agents.

1. Definition and median properties

Fix an input \((\mu,M,\hat o,\gamma)\). Write \(\nu=\mu+\gamma\delta_{\hat o}\) and \(H=(1+\gamma)/2\). Let \(z\) be the smallest support point of \(\nu\) whose cumulative mass is at least \(H\).

For any real \(t\), let \(q_M(t)\) be a nearest candidate in \(M\), choosing the smaller candidate in a tie. Define \(F(\mu,M,\hat o,\gamma)=q_M(z)\).

This formula also defines the mechanism on every finite-support rational probability measure, so it evaluates every replacement report appearing in the deviation definition.

Because \(z\) is the first support point at which cumulative mass reaches \(H\), for every real \(b\),

\[ z\leq b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \nu((-\infty,b])\geq H. \]

Consequently, \(z>b\) if and only if \(\nu((-\infty,b])<H\).

We also need the following consequence of the nearest-candidate rule. For candidates \(a<c\), put \(b=(a+c)/2\). Then:

  • If \(q_M(t)=a\), comparison with \(c\) gives \(|t-a|\leq |t-c|\), hence \(t\leq b\).
  • If \(q_M(t)=c\), then \(|t-c|<|t-a|\): equality would make the smaller candidate \(a\) preferable under the tie rule. Hence \(t>b\).

These statements hold even if other candidates lie between \(a\) and \(c\).

2. Mass-group-strategy-proofness

Fix any admissible society \(\mu\), any positive-mass submeasure \(\eta\leq\mu\), and any replacement measure \(\rho\) with \(\rho(\mathbb Q)=\eta(\mathbb Q)\). Set \(\mu'=\mu-\eta+\rho\), and let \(y\) and \(y'\) denote the outcomes before and after replacement. Let \(z,z'\) be the corresponding augmented medians.

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(\eta\)-almost every true location strictly prefers \(y'\) to \(y\). The outcomes must differ.

A move to the right

Suppose \(y<y'\), and put \(b=(y+y')/2\). On the line,

\[ |x-y'|<|x-y|\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x>b. \]

Thus \(\eta((-\infty,b])=0\). Since the original outcome is \(y\), the nearest-candidate property gives \(z\leq b\), so \(\nu((-\infty,b])\geq H\).

The augmented replacement measure is \(\nu'=\nu-\eta+\rho\). Therefore,

\[ \nu'((-\infty,b]) =\nu((-\infty,b])+\rho((-\infty,b]) \geq H. \]

Its median satisfies \(z'\leq b\). But selecting the larger candidate \(y'\) requires \(z'>b\), a contradiction.

A move to the left

Suppose \(y'<y\), and again put \(b=(y+y')/2\). Strict preference now means \(x<b\). Writing \(e=\eta(\mathbb Q)>0\), we have \(\eta((-\infty,b])=e\).

Since the original outcome is the larger candidate \(y\), its median satisfies \(z>b\), and hence \(\nu((-\infty,b])<H\). Since \(\rho((-\infty,b])\leq e\),

\[ \nu'((-\infty,b]) =\nu((-\infty,b])-e+\rho((-\infty,b]) \leq \nu((-\infty,b]) <H. \]

Thus \(z'>b\). Selecting the smaller candidate \(y'\), however, requires \(z'\leq b\), again a contradiction.

Both possible directions are impossible. This proves mass-group-strategy-proofness, including deviations that split atoms and redistribute their mass among multiple reports.

3. A cost comparison lemma

Let \(y\neq o\) be two sites, and define \(S=\{x:|x-y|\leq |x-o|\}\). Suppose \(s=\mu(S)\geq p>0\). Then

\[ SC_\mu(y)\leq \left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)SC_\mu(o). \]

To prove this, write \(d=|y-o|>0\). On \(S\), the difference \(|x-y|-|x-o|\) is nonpositive. Outside \(S\), the triangle inequality bounds that difference by \(d\). Integration therefore gives

\[ SC_\mu(y)\leq SC_\mu(o)+(1-s)d. \]

For every \(x\in S\), another application of the triangle inequality gives \(d\leq |x-y|+|x-o|\leq 2|x-o|\). Consequently, \(SC_\mu(o)\geq sd/2\). Substitution yields

\[ SC_\mu(y) \leq \left(1+\frac{2(1-s)}{s}\right)SC_\mu(o) =\left(\frac{2}{s}-1\right)SC_\mu(o) \leq \left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)SC_\mu(o). \]

The last inequality uses \(s\geq p\) and \(SC_\mu(o)\geq0\). No division by social cost occurs, so the argument also covers zero optimal cost.

4. Robustness for every prediction

Choose any \(o\in\arg\min_{a\in M}SC_\mu(a)\), and let \(y=F(\mu,M,\hat o,\gamma)\). If \(y=o\), the desired bound is immediate. Otherwise, put \(b=(y+o)/2\).

If \(y<o\), then \(z\leq b\), so \(\nu((-\infty,b])\geq H\). The virtual mass contributes at most \(\gamma\) to this half-line. Hence

\[ \mu((-\infty,b])\geq H-\gamma=\frac{1-\gamma}{2}. \]

This half-line is exactly the set of locations weakly preferring \(y\) to \(o\).

If \(y>o\), then \(z>b\), so \(\nu((-\infty,b])<H\). Since \(\nu\) has total mass \(1+\gamma=2H\), it follows that \(\nu((b,\infty))>H\). Subtracting at most \(\gamma\) of virtual mass gives

\[ \mu((b,\infty))>H-\gamma=\frac{1-\gamma}{2}. \]

Every location in this half-line prefers \(y\) to \(o\).

Thus, in either case, the cost comparison lemma applies with \(p=(1-\gamma)/2>0\). We obtain

\[ SC_\mu(y) \leq \left(\frac{4}{1-\gamma}-1\right)SC_\mu(o) =\frac{3+\gamma}{1-\gamma}OPT_\mu. \]

5. Consistency for every optimal prediction

Now suppose \(\hat o\in\arg\min_{a\in M}SC_\mu(a)\), and choose \(o=\hat o\). Again, only \(y\neq o\) needs consideration.

If \(y<o\), then \(z\leq b=(y+o)/2\), giving \(\nu((-\infty,b])\geq H\). The virtual atom is at \(o>b\), so it contributes no mass to this half-line. Therefore \(\mu((-\infty,b])\geq H\).

If \(y>o\), then \(z>b\), giving \(\nu((b,\infty))>H\). The virtual atom is at \(o<b\), so \(\mu((b,\infty))>H\).

In either case, at least \(p=H=(1+\gamma)/2\) of the true mass weakly prefers \(y\) to \(o\). Applying the cost comparison lemma gives

\[ SC_\mu(y) \leq \left(\frac{4}{1+\gamma}-1\right)SC_\mu(o) =\frac{3-\gamma}{1+\gamma}OPT_\mu. \]

This argument allows multiple optimal candidates: it uses only that the supplied prediction itself is optimal.

6. Determinism, anonymity, and running time

Both tie rules are fixed, so the mechanism is deterministic. Its outcome depends on reports only through their measure, so it is anonymous.

The mechanism can be computed directly from the support list:

  • Sort the support of \(\mu+\gamma\delta_{\hat o}\).
  • Accumulate exact rational masses until reaching \(H\).
  • Scan \(M\) to find the nearest candidate, applying the prescribed tie rule.

There are \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1)+m)\) rational arithmetic operations and comparisons. Under the standard binary numerator-and-denominator encoding, intermediate values have \(O(L)\) bits: a common denominator can be formed from the input denominators, whose total binary length is at most \(L\). Schoolbook arithmetic therefore gives a bit-time bound of \(O((\tau\log(\tau+1)+m)L^2)\).

Thus the specified rule attains both guarantees in polynomial time in the actual support size and input length.
