The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\) goods, a finite support of \(\tau\) CES buyer types \(\theta=(b_\theta,\rho_\theta,(a_{\theta j})_{j=1}^n)\), and rational masses \(\mu_\theta^r\) summing to \(1\) and per-capita supplies \(w_j^r>0\) for rounds \(r=0,\ldots,T\), compute an \(\epsilon\)-approximation to the final price vector. Define aggregate demand by \(D_j^r(p)=\sum_\theta \mu_\theta^r b_\theta (a_{\theta j})^{1-c_\theta}p_j^{c_\theta-1}/\sum_k(a_{\theta k})^{1-c_\theta}p_k^{c_\theta}\), where \(c_\theta=\rho_\theta/(\rho_\theta-1)\), and update \(p_j^{r+1}=p_j^r[1+\lambda\min\{(D_j^r(p^r)-w_j^r)/w_j^r,1\}]\). For fixed \(\rho_\theta=1/2\), rational type data and an assumed price bound \(P\) and contraction parameter \(\delta>0\), return a certified bound on \(\Phi^T=\sum_jp_j^T|D_j^T(p^T)-w_j^T|\), in particular whether \(\Phi^T\le(1-\delta)^T\Phi^0+\sum_{r=1}^T(1-\delta)^{T-r}[\sum_\theta b_\theta|\mu_\theta^r-\mu_\theta^{r-1}|+P\|w^r-w^{r-1}\|_1]\) can be certified with running time polynomial in \(n,\tau,T\), the input bit length, and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge1\) divisible goods and rounds \(r=0,\ldots,T\), supplied explicitly. Each round's society is given by its support: types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the size of the union of these supports, not the number of possible types. Missing types have mass zero, and \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta^r=1\).
Types have fixed binary-rational budgets \(b_\theta>0\), coefficients \(a_{\theta j}\ge0\) with \(\sum_j a_{\theta j}>0\), and CES exponent \(\rho_\theta=1/2\). Supplies \(w_j^r>0\), initial prices \(p_j^0>0\), and parameters \(0<\lambda<1\), \(P>0\), \(0<\delta\le1\), \(0<\epsilon<1\) are binary rationals.
Define \(D_j^r(p)=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta^r b_\theta a_{\theta j}^2p_j^{-2}/\bigl(\sum_{k=1}^m a_{\theta k}^2p_k^{-1}\bigr)\) and \(\Phi_r(p)=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j|D_j^r(p)-w_j^r|\).
For \(r=0,\ldots,T-1\), update \(p_j^{r+1}=p_j^r[1+\lambda\min\{(D_j^r(p^r)-w_j^r)/w_j^r,1\}]\); the market changes after this update.
Inputs promise \(p_j^r\le P\) throughout and \(\Phi_r(p^{r+1})\le(1-\delta)\Phi_r(p^r)\) for every update; these promises need not be checked.
Set \(U=(1-\delta)^T\Phi_0(p^0)+\sum_{r=1}^T(1-\delta)^{T-r}[\sum_\theta b_\theta|\mu_\theta^r-\mu_\theta^{r-1}|+P\|w^r-w^{r-1}\|_1]\).
The computational problem is to approximate \(p^T\) with absolute \(\ell_\infty\) error at most \(\epsilon\), compute rational enclosing intervals of width at most \(\epsilon\) for \(\Phi_T(p^T)\) and \(U\), and certify \(\Phi_T(p^T)\le U\) under the promises. Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the market data and parameters other than \(\epsilon\).

Is this promised computation solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,T,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many buyers, dynamic supply admits a misspending tracking bound with geometrically weighted recent perturbations and a geometric tail, conditional on bounded prices and static contraction; budget perturbations admit analogous bounds. The general tracking inequality includes perturbations through the final round. [Verified source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2019/pdfs/p1225.pdf)

Conclusion and precision convention

The promised computation is polynomial-time solvable. The key is to round prices after every update, using polynomially many bits of precision, rather than propagate exact rational prices through all rounds.

I use the usual accuracy-parameter interpretation: an accuracy request can be replaced by \(2^{-k}\), where \(k=\lceil\log_2(1/\epsilon)\rceil\). Binary fractional notation permits finding this tolerance from the first nonzero bit. If \(\epsilon\) is instead encoded as an unrestricted numerator–denominator pair, its input-reading cost must also be counted; the algorithm below is polynomial in that encoding length as well. This encoding qualification does not affect the substantive computation.

1. Expenditure shares and the tracking certificate

Index the union of the supplied supports by \(\theta=1,\ldots,\tau\), inserting zero masses where necessary. Define, for positive prices \(p\),

\[ c_{\theta j}=a_{\theta j}^2,\qquad S_\theta(p)=\sum_k\frac{c_{\theta k}}{p_k},\qquad s_{\theta j}(p)=\frac{c_{\theta j}/p_j}{S_\theta(p)}. \]

Here and below, these formulas use only rational operations. Each \(S_\theta(p)\) is positive because at least one coefficient of type \(\theta\) is positive. Moreover,

\[ s_{\theta j}(p)\ge0,\qquad \sum_j s_{\theta j}(p)=1. \]

Define aggregate expenditure on good \(j\) by

\[ E_j^r(p)=p_jD_j^r(p) =\sum_\theta\mu_\theta^r b_\theta s_{\theta j}(p). \]

Thus

\[ \Phi_r(p)=\sum_j\left|E_j^r(p)-p_jw_j^r\right|. \]

For consecutive markets at the same price vector, the reverse triangle inequality gives

\[ \left|\Phi_r(p)-\Phi_{r-1}(p)\right| \le \sum_j\left|E_j^r(p)-E_j^{r-1}(p)\right| +\sum_jp_j|w_j^r-w_j^{r-1}|. \]

The first term satisfies

\[ \sum_j\left|E_j^r(p)-E_j^{r-1}(p)\right| \le \sum_\theta b_\theta|\mu_\theta^r-\mu_\theta^{r-1}| \sum_js_{\theta j}(p) = \sum_\theta b_\theta|\mu_\theta^r-\mu_\theta^{r-1}|. \]

Consequently, whenever all prices are at most \(P\),

\[ \Phi_r(p)\le\Phi_{r-1}(p)+\Delta_r, \]

where

\[ \Delta_r= \sum_\theta b_\theta|\mu_\theta^r-\mu_\theta^{r-1}| +P\|w^r-w^{r-1}\|_1. \]

Apply this at \(p=p^r\). The promised contraction for update \(r-1\) yields

\[ \Phi_r(p^r) \le (1-\delta)\Phi_{r-1}(p^{r-1})+\Delta_r. \]

Induction therefore proves

\[ \Phi_T(p^T) \le (1-\delta)^T\Phi_0(p^0) +\sum_{r=1}^T(1-\delta)^{T-r}\Delta_r =U. \]

In particular, the perturbation at round \(T\) has coefficient \(1\). For \(\delta=1\), exponent-zero powers are interpreted as \(1\).

This proves the required certificate directly. It is the contraction-plus-perturbation mechanism studied for dynamic supply and budgets in the [supplied paper](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2019/pdfs/p1225.pdf).

2. A price domain with polynomially describable endpoints

For any positive price vector, demand is nonnegative. Hence every update multiplier lies between \(1-\lambda\) and \(1+\lambda\). It follows inductively that

\[ p_j^r\ge p_j^0(1-\lambda)^r. \]

Set

\[ \alpha=\left(\min_jp_j^0\right)(1-\lambda)^T, \qquad \mathcal C=[\alpha,P]^m. \]

The exact trajectory lies in \(\mathcal C\): the lower bound follows from the preceding inequality, and the upper bound is promised.

The rational number \(\alpha\) has bit length \(O((T+1)L)\). Although its numerical value may be exponentially small, its description is polynomially long.

Also define

\[ B=\max_\theta b_\theta, \qquad \underline w=\min_{r,j}w_j^r>0. \]

Normalization of the masses implies, in every round,

\[ \sum_\theta\mu_\theta^r b_\theta\le B. \]

3. Explicit Lipschitz bounds

Demand and the update map

For a single type, write

\[ x_{\theta j}(p)=\frac{b_\theta s_{\theta j}(p)}{p_j}. \]

Differentiating on the positive orthant gives

\[ \frac{\partial s_{\theta j}}{\partial p_k} = \frac{s_{\theta j}}{p_k} \left(s_{\theta k}-\mathbf1_{\{j=k\}}\right), \]

and therefore

\[ \frac{\partial x_{\theta j}}{\partial p_k} = \frac{x_{\theta j}}{p_k} \left(s_{\theta k}-2\mathbf1_{\{j=k\}}\right). \]

On \(\mathcal C\), the absolute row sum is bounded by

\[ \sum_k\left|\frac{\partial x_{\theta j}}{\partial p_k}\right| = x_{\theta j} \left( \frac{2-s_{\theta j}}{p_j} +\sum_{k\ne j}\frac{s_{\theta k}}{p_k} \right) \le\frac{3x_{\theta j}}{\alpha}. \]

Furthermore,

\[ D_j^r(p) =\sum_\theta\mu_\theta^r x_{\theta j}(p) \le\frac{B}{\alpha}. \]

Thus every row of the Jacobian of \(D^r\) has absolute sum at most \(3B/\alpha^2\). Integrating along the line segment between two points of the convex set \(\mathcal C\) proves

\[ \|D^r(p)-D^r(q)\|_\infty \le\frac{3B}{\alpha^2}\|p-q\|_\infty. \]

Let \(F_r\) denote the update map. The scalar function \(z\mapsto\min\{z,1\}\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz, including at its breakpoint. The update multiplier has absolute value at most \(1+\lambda\), so

\[ |F_{r,j}(p)-F_{r,j}(q)| \le (1+\lambda)|p_j-q_j| +\frac{\lambda P}{w_j^r}|D_j^r(p)-D_j^r(q)|. \]

Consequently, a common Lipschitz constant for all update maps on \(\mathcal C\) is

\[ K=2+\frac{3PB}{\underline w\,\alpha^2}\ge2. \]

This bound does not require deciding which side of a cap breakpoint the exact trajectory occupies.

Final misspending

The share derivative also gives

\[ \sum_{j,k} \left| \frac{\partial(b_\theta s_{\theta j})}{\partial p_k} \right| \le \frac{b_\theta}{\alpha} \sum_j s_{\theta j}\sum_k |s_{\theta k}-\mathbf1_{\{j=k\}}|. \]

For fixed \(j\), the inner sum equals \(2(1-s_{\theta j})\). Therefore the displayed expression is at most \(2b_\theta/\alpha\). Aggregating with the nonnegative masses and integrating along a line segment yields

\[ \|E^T(p)-E^T(q)\|_1 \le\frac{2B}{\alpha}\|p-q\|_\infty. \]

Using the reverse triangle inequality in the expression for \(\Phi_T\), we obtain

\[ |\Phi_T(p)-\Phi_T(q)| \le H\|p-q\|_\infty, \qquad H=\frac{2B}{\alpha}+\sum_jw_j^T. \]

All constants introduced so far are rational and have polynomial bit length.

4. A rounded simulation with certified error

The case \(T=0\) is handled by computing \(p^0\), \(\Phi_0(p^0)\), and \(U=\Phi_0(p^0)\) exactly. Assume henceforth that \(T\ge1\).

Choose a dyadic tolerance \(\epsilon_*=2^{-k}\le\epsilon\), with

\[ k=\lceil\log_2(1/\epsilon)\rceil. \]

Let

\[ M=\max\{1,H\},\qquad c=\lceil\log_2K\rceil,\qquad d=\lceil\log_2M\rceil, \]

and choose

\[ N=k+1+d+Tc,\qquad h=2^{-N}. \]

The integers \(c,d\) can be obtained by exact comparisons of rational numbers with powers of two.

Let \(\Pi\) be coordinatewise projection onto \(\mathcal C\), and let

\[ R_h(z)=h\lfloor z/h\rfloor \]

act coordinatewise. Initialize \(\widetilde p^0=p^0\), and perform

\[ \widetilde p^{r+1} = \Pi\!\left(R_h(F_r(\widetilde p^r))\right). \]

Each update is evaluated exactly at the stored rational prices; only its output is rounded.

Projection is nonexpansive in the infinity norm. Since \(p^{r+1}=F_r(p^r)\) lies in \(\mathcal C\), the errors

\[ e_r=\|\widetilde p^r-p^r\|_\infty \]

satisfy

\[ e_{r+1}\le h+Ke_r,\qquad e_0=0. \]

Hence, because \(K\ge2\),

\[ e_T\le h\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}K^i\le hK^T. \]

Using \(K\le2^c\), define the convenient dyadic bound

\[ \eta=2^{-(k+1+d)}. \]

Then

\[ e_T\le hK^T\le\eta \le\frac{\epsilon_*}{2M} \le\frac{\epsilon}{2M}. \]

Thus the output \(\widetilde p^T\) has the required price accuracy. Moreover,

\[ |\Phi_T(\widetilde p^T)-\Phi_T(p^T)| \le H\eta\le\frac{\epsilon}{2}. \]

5. Rational intervals and certification

Compute exactly

\[ \widehat\Phi=\Phi_T(\widetilde p^T), \qquad R=H\eta, \]

and compute \(U\) exactly from its defining formula. Output the rational intervals

\[ I_\Phi= \left[ \max\{0,\widehat\Phi-R\}, \ \min\{U,\widehat\Phi+R\} \right], \qquad I_U=[U,U]. \]

The error bound proves that \(\Phi_T(p^T)\) belongs to
\([\widehat\Phi-R,\widehat\Phi+R]\). Section 1 proves that it also belongs to \([0,U]\). Therefore \(I_\Phi\) is nonempty and encloses the true value.

Its width is at most \(2R\le\epsilon\), and \(I_U\) has width zero. Its upper endpoint is at most the exactly computed \(U\), furnishing the requested certification under the promises.

This remains valid when \(\Phi_T(p^T)=U\); numerical separation is unnecessary.

6. Bit complexity

For standard binary encodings, every positive input rational lies between \(2^{-L}\) and \(2^L\). In particular,

\[ \alpha\ge2^{-(T+1)L}, \qquad \underline w\ge2^{-L}, \qquad P,B\le2^L. \]

The formulas for \(K\) and \(H\) consequently imply

\[ c=O((T+1)L+1), \qquad d=O((T+1)L+\log(m+1)+1). \]

Therefore

\[ N= O\!\left( (T+1)^2L+\log(m+1)+\log(1/\epsilon) \right). \]

After rounding and projection, each stored price is either a dyadic grid point or one of the rational endpoints \(\alpha,P\). Its bit length is thus polynomially bounded uniformly across all rounds.

At such prices, one exact update has polynomial bit complexity:

  • Each \(S_\theta\) is a sum of \(m\) rational terms.
  • Each demand coordinate is a sum of at most \(\tau\) rational terms obtained from these sums.
  • The remaining operations are rational arithmetic, comparison, rounding, and projection.

For clarity, summing \(n\) rational numbers whose numerators and denominators have at most \(\ell\) bits produces a rational representation with \(O(n\ell+\log n)\) bits using a common denominator. Applying this bound to the explicitly described sums shows that all intermediate representations within one update have polynomial size. Rounding prevents those representations from being carried into the next round.

Finally, \(U\) uses only the initial rational prices, explicit market differences, and powers of \(1-\delta\) with exponents at most \(T\). These powers have polynomial bit length, and the subsequent sums and products also have polynomial bit complexity.

There are \(T\) updates. Thus the entire promised computation runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,T,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\), under the precision convention stated above.
