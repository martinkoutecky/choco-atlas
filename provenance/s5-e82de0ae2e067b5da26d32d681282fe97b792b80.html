The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and a directed friend-or-enemy template \(r:T\times T\to\{\mathrm{friend},\mathrm{enemy}\}\), an instance includes a current partition into \(s\) coalition profiles \(q^j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{T}\) satisfying \(\sum_j q^j=\mu\). Decide whether there is a positive-mass blocking coalition: variables \(x_{tj}\in[0,q^j_t]\) select mass from each current type-coalition cell, \(b_t=\sum_jx_{tj}\), and every cell with \(x_{tj}>0\) must strictly prefer the new profile \(b\) to its current profile \(q^j\) under the lexicographic comparison of friend mass and then enemy mass. Equivalently, decide whether no such blocker exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input gives a directed template \(r:T\times T\to\{\mathrm{friend},\mathrm{enemy}\}\) and \(s\geq1\) nonzero profiles \(q^j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^{T}\) with \(\sum_{j=1}^{s}q^j=\mu\). Types may be split among profiles; there are no neutrals or symmetry requirements.
For a profile \(z\), define \(F_t(z)=\sum_{u:r(t,u)=\mathrm{friend}}z_u\) and \(E_t(z)=\sum_{u:r(t,u)=\mathrm{enemy}}z_u\). The diagonal label governs other individuals of the same type; an individual's own mass is zero.
Write \(z\succ_t w\) exactly when \(F_t(z)>F_t(w)\), or \(F_t(z)=F_t(w)\) and \(E_t(z)<E_t(w)\); equal pairs give indifference.
A blocking coalition selects real masses \(0\leq x_{tj}\leq q_t^j\), forms \(b_t=\sum_{j=1}^{s}x_{tj}\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}b_t>0\), and satisfies \(b\succ_t q^j\) for every cell with \(x_{tj}>0\).
Let \(m\) be the number of ordered friend pairs in the template and \(L\) the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,s,L\).

Is deciding whether a positive-mass blocking coalition exists solvable in polynomial time, or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite agents, core verification is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete, even with a planar friendship graph and maximum degree \(4\); equivalently, existence of a coalition strictly improving every member is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. [Chen, Csáji, Roy, and Simola](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09655v2).
No prior result settling this exact continuous decision problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Used blocking-coalition existence as the decision polarity; absence is its complement, not an equivalent yes-instance question.
  • Required every current profile to have positive total mass, matching nonempty coalitions in a partition.
  • Allowed real selected masses because the society is divisible; only the input profiles and masses require rational encoding.
  • Interpreted diagonal template labels as relations between distinct individuals of the same type; an individual's own mass is zero.
  • Defined m as the number of ordered friend pairs and L as the total input bit length.

Result

Deciding whether a positive-mass blocking coalition exists is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. This holds even when every mass is dyadic and every type belongs entirely to one profile.

The reduction below permits arbitrary real contributions. Its reverse direction uses a weighted graph inequality to exclude fractional blocking coalitions.

1. A weighted graph inequality

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite simple undirected graph containing no clique of size \(k\), where \(k\geq3\). For nonnegative real weights \(y_v\), write \(Y=\sum_{v\in V}y_v\). Then

\( \displaystyle \sum_{v\in V}y_v\sum_{u:\{u,v\}\in E}y_u \leq \frac{k-2}{k-1}Y^2. \)

Proof. The assertion is immediate when \(Y=0\). Otherwise divide the weights by \(Y\), so their sum is \(1\).

Suppose two nonadjacent vertices \(a,b\) have positive weights. Keeping all other weights and \(y_a+y_b\) fixed, the quadratic expression depends on these two weights through

\( \displaystyle 2y_a\sum_{u:\{a,u\}\in E}y_u + 2y_b\sum_{u:\{b,u\}\in E}y_u. \)

The two neighbor sums remain fixed during this redistribution because \(a,b\) are nonadjacent and the graph has no loops. Transfer all their combined weight to whichever vertex has the larger neighbor sum. This does not decrease the expression and reduces its positive support.

Repeating this operation eventually leaves weights supported on a clique of size \(\ell\leq k-1\). On that clique, the expression equals

\( \displaystyle 1-\sum_v y_v^2 \leq 1-\frac1\ell \leq \frac{k-2}{k-1}, \)

where the first inequality follows from Cauchy–Schwarz. The original expression is no larger. Rescaling proves the claim. \(\square\)

2. Reduction from CLIQUE

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of CLIQUE. [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9)

We may restrict its instances to a simple graph \(G=(V,E)\), with \(n=|V|\) and \(3\leq k\leq n\). This restriction preserves hardness: instances outside that range can be decided directly and replaced by fixed yes or no instances within the range.

Set

\( \displaystyle H=n+1,\qquad \delta=2^{-\lceil\log_2(2k)\rceil},\qquad Q=H+k+1+\delta. \)

In particular,

\( \displaystyle 0<\delta\leq\frac1{2k}<\frac1{k-2}. \)

We first describe unnormalized masses. All of them will subsequently be multiplied by the same positive dyadic factor.

Types and profiles

For every vertex \(v\in V\), introduce three types \(v,d_v,e_v\), all assigned entirely to a profile \(q^v\), with masses

\( \displaystyle q^v_v=1,\qquad q^v_{d_v}=H+k-1,\qquad q^v_{e_v}=1+\delta. \)

Thus \(q^v\) has total mass \(Q\).

Introduce four further types \(g,h,d_0,e_0\), assigned entirely to a profile \(q^0\), with masses

\( \displaystyle q^0_g=H,\qquad q^0_h=1,\qquad q^0_{d_0}=k,\qquad q^0_{e_0}=\delta. \)

This profile also has total mass \(Q\).

The friend sets are exactly those in the following table. Every unspecified ordered pair is an enemy pair, including any unspecified diagonal pair.

| Type | Friend set |
|---|---|
| \(v\in V\) | \(\{g,d_v\}\cup\{u\in V:\{u,v\}\in E\}\) |
| \(d_v\) or \(e_v\) | \(\{v,d_v,e_v\}\) |
| \(g\) | \(\{h\}\) |
| \(h\) | \(\{g,d_0\}\cup V\) |
| \(d_0\) or \(e_0\) | \(\{g,h,d_0,e_0\}\) |

Normalization

Choose

\( \displaystyle p=\left\lceil\log_2\!\bigl(2(n+1)(n+k+3)\bigr)\right\rceil, \qquad c=2^{-p}. \)

Since \(Q<n+k+3\), the quantity

\( \displaystyle R=2^p-(n+1)Q \)

is positive. Add a padding type \(z\), assigned entirely to its own profile with unnormalized mass \(R\). Its only friend is itself.

Multiply all profile entries and type masses by \(c\). The resulting total mass is

\( \displaystyle c\bigl((n+1)Q+R\bigr)=1. \)

Every type mass is positive and dyadic. Every profile is nonzero, and the profiles sum to the type-mass vector.

Multiplication by a common positive factor preserves all friend comparisons, enemy comparisons, and capacity constraints. We therefore prove correctness using unnormalized masses.

3. Types that cannot participate

None of the types \(d_v,e_v,d_0,e_0,z\) can contribute positive mass to a blocking coalition.

For each such type, its friends are exactly all the types in its own profile. Those friends’ entire populations lie in that profile. Consequently, its friend mass in any candidate coalition is at most its current friend mass. Its current enemy mass is zero.

It therefore cannot obtain more friends; if its friend mass ties, it cannot obtain strictly fewer enemies than zero. This proves the assertion directly, regardless of which other types participate.

Hence every blocking coalition consists only of vertex types and the two types \(g,h\).

4. A clique produces a blocking coalition

Suppose \(G\) contains a clique \(C\) of size \(k\). Select:

  • all \(H\) units of type \(g\);
  • the single unit of type \(h\);
  • the single unit of each vertex type in \(C\).

The coalition has total mass \(H+k+1=Q-\delta\). Its members’ comparisons are:

| Participating type | Current friend mass | Coalition friend mass | Current enemy mass | Coalition enemy mass |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(v\in C\) | \(H+k-1\) | \(H+k-1\) | \(2+\delta\) | \(2\) |
| \(g\) | \(1\) | \(1\) | \(H+k+\delta\) | \(H+k\) |
| \(h\) | \(H+k\) | \(H+k\) | \(1+\delta\) | \(1\) |

For a vertex \(v\in C\), its coalition friends are type \(g\) and the other \(k-1\) selected vertices. The other two rows follow from the specified friend sets.

Every participating type keeps exactly its current friend mass and loses \(\delta>0\) enemy mass. Thus every participating cell strictly improves, and this is a positive-mass blocking coalition.

5. Every blocking coalition implies a clique

Conversely, suppose a blocking coalition exists. Divide its actual selected masses by \(c\), and denote its unnormalized masses by \(y_g,y_h,y_v\). Thus

\( \displaystyle 0\leq y_g\leq H,\qquad 0\leq y_h\leq1,\qquad 0\leq y_v\leq1. \)

Write \(Y=\sum_{v\in V}y_v\).

Both guard types must participate

First, \(y_g>0\). Indeed, if \(y_g=0\), an active vertex \(v\) would have friend mass at most \(n-1\), whereas its current friend mass is \(H+k-1=n+k\). It could not improve.

With no active vertex and \(y_g=0\), type \(h\) would have friend mass zero, below its current friend mass \(H+k\). Since all remaining types were excluded in Section 3, no positive coalition would remain.

Because \(g\) participates, its friend mass must be at least its current friend mass \(1\). Its only available friend is \(h\), so \(y_h=1\). Its friend mass consequently ties, and its strict improvement requires

\[ \displaystyle y_g+Y<H+k+\delta. \tag{1} \]

Type \(h\) now participates. Its available friends are \(g\) and all vertex types, so its improvement requires

\[ \displaystyle y_g+Y\geq H+k. \tag{2} \]

Define \(d=H-y_g\geq0\). Equations (1) and (2) give

\[ \displaystyle k+d\leq Y<k+d+\delta. \tag{3} \]

In particular, \(Y>0\).

Fractional vertex masses cannot evade the clique condition

For every vertex with \(y_v>0\), improvement requires its friend mass to be at least its current friend mass:

\( \displaystyle y_g+\sum_{u:\{u,v\}\in E}y_u\geq H+k-1. \)

Therefore

\( \displaystyle \sum_{u:\{u,v\}\in E}y_u\geq k-1+d. \)

Multiplying this inequality by \(y_v\) and summing gives

\[ \displaystyle \sum_{v\in V}y_v\sum_{u:\{u,v\}\in E}y_u \geq(k-1+d)Y. \tag{4} \]

Vertices of zero mass contribute zero, so they cause no difficulty in this sum.

If \(G\) had no clique of size \(k\), Section 1 would imply, together with (4),

\( \displaystyle (k-1+d)Y\leq\frac{k-2}{k-1}Y^2. \)

Since \(Y>0\),

\( \displaystyle Y\geq\frac{(k-1)(k-1+d)}{k-2} =k+d+\frac{1+d}{k-2}. \)

But \((1+d)/(k-2)\geq1/(k-2)>\delta\), contradicting (3).

Thus \(G\) contains a clique of size \(k\). This argument applies to arbitrary real masses, without any integrality assumption.

6. Size and running time of the reduction

The construction has

\( \displaystyle \tau=3n+5,\qquad s=n+2,\qquad m=2|E|+9n+12. \)

The count of \(m\) includes ordered pairs and friend diagonals.

Every nonzero profile entry and every type mass has \(O(\log n)\) bits. Under a conventional explicit encoding, the output length satisfies \(L=O(n^2\log n)\). Writing the template, profiles, and masses takes

\( \displaystyle O\bigl((\tau^2+\tau s)\log\tau\bigr) \)

bit operations. In particular, the reduction is polynomial in the specified parameters.

Sections 4 and 5 prove its equivalence, establishing \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

7. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Real-valued witnesses require a certificate-size argument.

Let \(N\leq\tau s\) be the number of cells with \(q_t^j>0\). Assign each such cell one of three labels:

  • \(0\): its selected mass is zero;
  • \(F\): it participates and strictly increases friend mass;
  • \(E\): it participates, ties friend mass, and strictly decreases enemy mass.

For a fixed labeling, introduce the cell masses \(x_{tj}\) and a common margin \(\varepsilon\). Impose the capacity constraints, \(0\leq\varepsilon\leq1\), and

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t,j}x_{tj}\geq\varepsilon. \)

For each cell, additionally impose the following constraints according to its label:

| Label | Constraints |
|---|---|
| \(0\) | \(x_{tj}=0\) |
| \(F\) | \(x_{tj}\geq\varepsilon\), and \(F_t(b)-F_t(q^j)\geq\varepsilon\) |
| \(E\) | \(x_{tj}\geq\varepsilon\), \(F_t(b)=F_t(q^j)\), and \(E_t(q^j)-E_t(b)\geq\varepsilon\) |

Here \(b_t=\sum_jx_{tj}\), so every constraint is linear.

A blocking coalition induces a labeling for which these constraints admit \(\varepsilon>0\): take a sufficiently small common margin below all positive selected masses and all relevant strict improvements. Conversely, any feasible solution with \(\varepsilon>0\) is a blocking coalition.

The feasible region is bounded: every cell mass lies in its capacity interval and \(\varepsilon\in[0,1]\). Thus, whenever a positive margin is feasible, maximizing \(\varepsilon\) has an optimal vertex with positive margin.

To bound its encoding length, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input profile entries. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Substitute

\( \displaystyle u_{tj}=Dx_{tj},\qquad \eta=D\varepsilon, \)

and multiply each constraint by \(D\). All coefficients are in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and all right-hand sides are integers of magnitude at most \(D\).

A vertex is determined by \(N+1\) linearly independent active constraints. Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s determinant bound therefore give coordinate bit lengths

\( \displaystyle O\bigl(L+N\log(N+1)\bigr). \)

Dividing by \(D\) preserves a polynomial bit-length bound. Hence every yes instance has a rational blocking coalition of polynomial encoding length.

Such a certificate is verified by checking capacities, positive total mass, and the lexicographic comparisons for all positive cells. Friend sums and profile totals can be computed using \(O(ms+m+\tau s)\) rational arithmetic operations. Enemy sums follow from

\( \displaystyle E_t(z)=\sum_u z_u-F_t(z), \)

because there are no neutrals. Including input reading, verification takes

\( \displaystyle O(L)+(ms+m+\tau s)\operatorname{poly}(L+\tau s) \)

bit operations. Therefore the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

For completeness, enumerating the three labels and solving the resulting rational linear programs gives a deterministic decision algorithm with running time

\( \displaystyle 3^N\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,s,L) \leq3^{\tau s}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,s,L). \)

Each program has \(N+1\) variables, \(O(N)\) constraints, and polynomial encoding length, satisfying the hypotheses of polynomial-time rational linear programming.

8. Conclusion

Positive-mass blocking-coalition existence is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even with dyadic masses and no splitting of types among input profiles. A polynomial-time algorithm for the general problem would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
