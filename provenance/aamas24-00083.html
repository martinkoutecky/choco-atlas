The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_w\), finite firms \(F\), capacities \(q_f\), worker-type and firm-type preferences, target type-firm pair \((w^\star,f^\star)\), target mass \(0<\delta\le\mu_{w^\star}\), and budget \(\ell\), decide whether there exist \(\Delta_f\ge0\) and a mass matching \(x\) that is stable under capacities \(q_f+\Delta_f\), satisfies \(\sum_f\Delta_f\le\ell\), and assigns at least \(\delta\) mass of type \(w^\star\) to \(f^\star\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

An instance has finite worker types \(A\) with rational masses \(\mu_a\), finite firms \(F\), rational capacities \(q_f\), and strict preferences of every type over \(F\cup\{\emptyset\}\) and of every firm over \(A\cup\{\emptyset\}\). A matching flow is a nonnegative matrix \(\pi\) with \(\sum_f\pi_{a,f}\le\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a\pi_{a,f}\le q_f+\delta_f\); residual type mass is unmatched.

A type--firm pair \((a,f)\) blocks \(\pi\) if some \(\varepsilon>0\) mass of \(a\) is unmatched or assigned below \(f\), and \(f\) can take \(\varepsilon\) mass of \(a\) by using vacant capacity that it prefers to leave empty or by replacing \(\varepsilon\) mass of types ranked below \(a\). A flow is stable if no such pair blocks it.

Target

Is the continuous Budgeted Add Capacity to Match Pair problem NP-hard, or solvable in polynomial time?

Nearest known results

For finite individual workers and integer capacities, the corresponding problem with a global seat-addition budget and per-firm addition budgets is NP-hard.

If only the global budget is imposed in that finite setting, the corresponding pair-matching problem is solvable in polynomial time.

The distinction in item 3 is substantive. Consider one type \(a\) of mass \(1\), one mutually acceptable firm \(f\) with \(q_f=0\), and both global and individual budgets equal to \(1/2\). The flow

\[ \delta_f=\pi_{a,f}=\frac12 \]

is stable: \(f\) is saturated and employs no type ranked below \(a\). Thus \((a,f)\) is matched with positive mass, but the entire type cannot be matched to \(f\).

I therefore analyze the following standard completion.

2. Standard formalization

The input additionally contains:

  • a designated pair \((a^\star,f^\star)\);
  • a rational global budget \(B\);
  • rational individual budgets \(b_f\).

We ask whether there are additions and a stable flow satisfying

\[ 0\leq\delta_f\leq b_f,\qquad \sum_f\delta_f\leq B,\qquad \pi_{a^\star,f^\star}>0. \]

Let

\[ L_f=\sum_{a\in A}\pi_{a,f} \]

be the load of firm \(f\), and regard residual mass as assignment to \(\emptyset\):

\[ \pi_{a,\emptyset}=\mu_a-\sum_{f\in F}\pi_{a,f}. \]

For a type-firm pair define

\[ W_{a,f} = \sum_{\substack{g\in F\cup\{\emptyset\}\\ f\succ_a g}} \pi_{a,g} \]

and

\[ D_{a,f} = \sum_{\substack{b\in A\\ a\succ_f b}} \pi_{b,f}. \]

Thus \(W_{a,f}\) is the amount of type \(a\) that would prefer \(f\), while \(D_{a,f}\) is the amount currently employed at \(f\) that \(f\) ranks below \(a\).

3. Exact blocking characterization

Lemma 1

At capacities \(c_f=q_f+\delta_f\), the pair \((a,f)\) blocks \(\pi\) if and only if

\[ W_{a,f}>0 \]

and at least one of the following holds:

\[ D_{a,f}>0, \]

or

\[ a\succ_f\emptyset \quad\text{and}\quad L_f<c_f. \]

Proof

If \(W_{a,f}>0\), some positive amount of type \(a\) is assigned below \(f\). If \(D_{a,f}>0\), choose

\[ 0<\varepsilon\leq\min\{W_{a,f},D_{a,f}\}. \]

Firm \(f\) can replace \(\varepsilon\) mass ranked below \(a\). Alternatively, if \(f\) has acceptable vacant capacity, choose

\[ 0<\varepsilon \leq \min\{W_{a,f},c_f-L_f\}. \]

In either case \((a,f)\) blocks.

Conversely, any blocking deviation needs positive type-\(a\) mass that prefers \(f\), giving \(W_{a,f}>0\). The firm must obtain room either by replacing positive mass ranked below \(a\), giving \(D_{a,f}>0\), or by using acceptable vacant capacity. These are exactly the stated alternatives. \(\square\)

Consequently, stability is equivalent, for every \((a,f)\), to the disjunction

\[ W_{a,f}=0 \]

or

\[ D_{a,f}=0 \quad\text{and}\quad \bigl(\emptyset\succ_f a\ \text{or}\ L_f=q_f+\delta_f\bigr). \]

4. Unused added capacity can be eliminated

Lemma 2

Suppose \((\pi,\delta)\) is feasible and stable. Define

\[ \widehat{\delta}_f = \max\{0,L_f-q_f\}. \]

Then \((\pi,\widehat{\delta})\) is feasible and stable, and

\[ 0\leq\widehat{\delta}_f\leq\delta_f \]

for every firm.

Proof

Feasibility of the original solution gives

\[ L_f\leq q_f+\delta_f. \]

Hence \(L_f-q_f\leq\delta_f\), so \(\widehat{\delta}_f\leq\delta_f\). Moreover,

\[ q_f+\widehat{\delta}_f=\max\{q_f,L_f\}\geq L_f, \]

so \(\pi\) remains capacity-feasible. All budget constraints remain satisfied because additions only decrease.

Suppose a pair blocked after replacing \(\delta\) by \(\widehat{\delta}\). Its worker-side condition and the set of replaceable lower-ranked masses are unchanged. If the block uses vacant capacity, then

\[ L_f<q_f+\widehat{\delta}_f\leq q_f+\delta_f, \]

so the same vacancy existed before the reduction. Therefore the pair would also have blocked the original solution, contradicting stability. \(\square\)

Corollary 3

The additions can be eliminated from the formulation. A flow \(\pi\) is realizable within the budgets if and only if

\[ L_f\leq q_f+b_f \]

for every \(f\),

\[ \sum_f\max\{0,L_f-q_f\}\leq B, \]

and, for every \((a,f)\),

\[ W_{a,f}=0 \]

or

\[ D_{a,f}=0 \quad\text{and}\quad \bigl(\emptyset\succ_f a\ \text{or}\ L_f\geq q_f\bigr). \]

Thus capacity addition is exactly controlled overfilling relative to the original capacities.

5. Complexity upper bound

Proposition 4

Under the standard rational formalization above, the continuous problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). More explicitly, it can be solved by at most \(2^{|A||F|}\) rational linear programs.

Proof

For every pair \((a,f)\), guess which side of the stability disjunction is used.

  • In the worker-side branch, impose \(W_{a,f}=0\).
  • In the firm-side branch, impose \(D_{a,f}=0\), and additionally impose \(L_f\geq q_f\) whenever \(a\succ_f\emptyset\).

All these are linear constraints.

Introduce variables \(y_f\) and impose

\[ y_f\geq0,\qquad y_f\geq L_f-q_f,\qquad y_f\leq b_f,\qquad \sum_f y_f\leq B. \]

Together with \(L_f\leq q_f+b_f\), these constraints are feasible exactly when the total canonical addition is at most \(B\).

For the guessed branch vector, maximize \(\pi_{a^\star,f^\star}\) subject to these constraints and the matching-flow constraints. This is a rational linear program. The branch is successful precisely when its optimum is positive.

There are \(2^{|A||F|}\) branch vectors. Moreover, if one branch has a point with positive target flow, maximizing the target coordinate yields a positive rational basic feasible solution whose encoding length is polynomially bounded in the input length. The branch vector together with this rational solution is therefore an \(\mathrm{NP}\) certificate. \(\square\)

This result also shows that allowing arbitrary real rather than rational solutions does not enlarge the set of yes-instances under this formalization.

6. Why the discrete hardness proof does not transfer

The known discrete result proves that the budgeted integer-capacity problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, while the global-budget-only version is polynomial-time solvable [Gokhale–Narang–Singla–Vaish](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04645). Its hardness reduction represents an addable man by a firm with capacity \(0\) and individual addition budget \(1\). Hence adding one integer seat is a binary activation.

That argument is invalid for divisible capacities.

For any \(m\), take mutually isolated pairs \((a_i,f_i)\), where:

  • \(\mu_{a_i}=1\);
  • \(q_{f_i}=0\) and \(b_{f_i}=1\);
  • \(a_i\) and \(f_i\) find one another acceptable and rank every other partner below \(\emptyset\);
  • the global budget is \(B=1\).

Set

\[ \delta_{f_i}=\pi_{a_i,f_i}=\frac1m \]

for every \(i\). Every \(f_i\) is saturated and holds no type ranked below \(a_i\), so the flow is stable. Thus all \(m\) zero-capacity firms are simultaneously active with positive flow under global budget \(1\). With integer additions, at most one of them could be activated.

Therefore the binary selection correspondence used by the discrete reduction is lost. This does not prove polynomial-time solvability, but it rules out the immediate hardness transfer.

For fixed capacities, the underlying divisible problem is the classical stable-allocation problem, which has a strongly polynomial algorithm [Baïou–Balinski](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.27.3.485.310). The unresolved issue is whether the special disjunctive system in Corollary 3 retains enough stable-allocation structure for polynomial optimization, or can encode an \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard selection problem despite divisibility.

7. Conclusion

No rigorous classification as either \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard or polynomial-time solvable follows from the premises given.

What is proved here is:

  • the problem as written is underspecified;
  • under the natural positive-flow interpretation, unused additions can be removed exactly;
  • the resulting decision problem is a finite union of rational linear systems and belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\);
  • there is an exact exponential-time linear-programming algorithm;
  • the known discrete \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness reduction does not extend to the continuous model.

A complete classification requires either a polynomial algorithm exploiting the nested prefix structure of \(W_{a,f}\) and \(D_{a,f}\), or a new hardness construction robust to arbitrarily small positive capacity additions.
