1. Definitions and interpretation

Write $\lambda$ for Lebesgue measure and
$$ V_t(B)=\int_B v_t(x)\,dx $$
for the true valuation of type $t$. Endpoints have zero measure and zero utility.

For a fixed compatible $q$, define
$$ \epsilon_q = \sup_i\sup_r \max\!\left\{ 0,\, q\left[V_{t(i)}(A_i^q(r))-V_{t(i)}(A_i^q(v_{t(i)}))\right] \right\}. $$
Thus
$$ \epsilon^*=\sup_{\substack{q\geq q_0\\q\text{ compatible}}}\epsilon_q. $$

For the allocation theorem, interpret a binary-rational mass as
$$ \mu_t=\frac{a_t}{2^S} $$
with integer $a_t>0$. If “binary-rational” instead means an arbitrary rational encoded in binary, the allocation theorem below establishes only the dyadic subclass. The finite-replica incentive theorem does not require dyadic masses.

Let $L_I$ denote the encoding length of the queried interval endpoints. For incentive computations, let $E$ denote the encoding length of $e$. These lengths must be included in any running-time bound if they are not already included in $L$.

2. Partition and proportionality properties

These properties are used below, so they are proved here.

Lemma 1. Both recursions partition their input interval, up to endpoints. Each representative in a call on $[a,b]$ with $k$ representatives receives reported value at least $1/k$ of its reported value of $[a,b]$.

Proof. Both statements follow by induction on $k$.

For $k=1$, the representative receives the entire interval.

For $k>1$, put
$$ h=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor. $$
The ordinary recursion partitions $[a,b]$ into $[a,d_h]$ and $[d_h,b]$. The modified recursion partitions it into
$$ [a,d_h],\qquad[d_h,d_{h+1}],\qquad[d_{h+1},b], $$
and applies a partitioning recursion to each interval.

For proportionality, let $R$ be a representative’s reported valuation. Its cut $c$ satisfies
$$ R([a,c])=\frac{h}{k}R([a,b]). $$
This equation also holds when the interval value is zero, because then $c=a$.

If the representative belongs to the left group, then $c\leq d_h$, hence
$$ R([a,d_h])\geq\frac{h}{k}R([a,b]). $$
Induction on the left child, which has $h$ representatives, gives value at least
$$ \frac{R([a,d_h])}{h}\geq\frac{R([a,b])}{k}. $$

For a right-group representative in the ordinary recursion, $d_h\leq c$, so
$$ R([d_h,b])\geq\frac{k-h}{k}R([a,b]). $$
The corresponding induction gives the same guarantee.

In the modified recursion, every right-group representative has $d_{h+1}\leq c$. Applying the same argument to $[d_{h+1},b]$ proves its guarantee. The middle allocation adds nonnegative value. $\square$

This directly proves the proportionality property stated for the modified rule in [Menon–Larson](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.06306). The instance-specific incentive results below do not use their worst-case incentive bound.

3. Exact truthful allocation invariance for dyadic masses

For truthful reporting, let
$$ U_t^q=\bigcup_{i:t(i)=t}A_i^q(v_t) $$
be the aggregate region allocated to type $t$.

By Lemma 1, individual pieces have disjoint interiors. Therefore, for every Borel set $B$,
$$ \overline K_t^q(B) = \frac{q}{N\mu_t}\lambda(U_t^q\cap B) = \frac{1}{n\mu_t}\lambda(U_t^q\cap B). $$

Lemma 2. In computing aggregate truthful regions, a recursive call containing only one type can be replaced by assigning its entire interval to that type.

Proof. Every descendant contains only that type, and Lemma 1 says that the descendants partition the input interval. Thus their aggregate region is the entire interval, up to endpoints. This remains true when its valuation is zero. $\square$

At every other truthful call, all representatives of one type have the same cut. Sorting by cut and identity consequently gives consecutive type blocks. The split can be computed from their counts, without listing representatives.

Theorem 3. Suppose all masses are dyadic. There are finite unions of rational intervals $W_t$, independent of compatible $q$, such that
$$ \overline K_t^q(B)=\frac{1}{n\mu_t}\lambda(W_t\cap B) $$
for every compatible $q$ and every Borel set $B$. In particular, the continuous type allocation exists.

Proof. Choose the smallest common dyadic denominator
$$ D=2^S,\qquad \mu_t=\frac{a_t}{D},\qquad \sum_t a_t=D. $$
If $D>1$, minimality implies that some $a_t$ is odd. Compatibility then implies
$$ D\mid N. $$
Indeed, integrality of $Na_t/D$, together with $\gcd(a_t,D)=1$, implies this divisibility.

Every compatible population therefore has
$$ N=Db,\qquad N\mu_t=ba_t $$
for an integer $b>0$. If $D=1$, positivity of the masses implies that there is only one type, and Lemma 2 already proves the theorem.

Run an auxiliary truthful population with $D$ representatives and $a_t$ representatives of type $t$. This auxiliary population is used only to define the aggregate regions; it need not itself be a compatible replica.

Compare its compressed execution with that of a compatible population. At corresponding calls, maintain:

  • identical input intervals;
  • actual type counts equal to $b$ times auxiliary type counts;
  • auxiliary population size a power of two.

These statements hold at the root. A call containing several types has auxiliary size
$$ g=2^j,\qquad j\geq1. $$
Its actual size is $bg$. Both sizes are even, so both calls request cuts at exactly half the reported interval value. Identical intervals and valuations imply identical type cuts, including the leftmost-cut convention.

Let the auxiliary blocks in sorted order have sizes
$$ c_1,c_2,\ldots. $$
The actual block sizes are $bc_1,bc_2,\ldots$, and the median ranks are respectively $g/2$ and $bg/2$.

If the auxiliary median rank ends a block, the actual median rank ends the corresponding block. Thus both median boundary coordinates coincide. If it lies strictly inside a block, scaling preserves that fact, and both adjacent median coordinates equal that block’s common cut in both populations.

Consequently, the two exterior intervals coincide and their type-count vectors remain scaled by $b$. Each auxiliary exterior child has size $g/2$. The middle ordinary call has auxiliary size $g$, and its children also have size $g/2$. Thus the same comparison applies throughout both recursion modes.

Whenever a call contains one type, Lemma 2 assigns the same interval to that type in both executions. Every group split halves the auxiliary size, so the comparison terminates after at most $S$ group splits along any path.

Let $W_t$ be the aggregate region recorded for type $t$ by the auxiliary execution. All its endpoints are rational, because cuts of rational piecewise-constant densities on rational intervals are rational. The comparison proves
$$ \lambda(U_t^q\mathbin{\triangle}W_t)=0. $$
Substituting this equality into the formula for $\overline K_t^q$ proves the result. The measures are identical for all compatible $q$, hence converge weakly to that same measure. $\square$

The argument includes zero densities, flat cumulative valuations, and coincident cuts: corresponding calls use exactly the same leftmost cuts and identity order.

4. Polynomial-time allocation computation

The auxiliary population has $2^S$ representatives, but it can be processed using binary type counts.

For a nonempty group containing $r$ types, define its potential to be $r-1$. At a group split, at most one type block is split between the children. If the children contain $r_L$ and $r_R$ types, then
$$ r_L+r_R\leq r+1, $$
and therefore
$$ (r_L-1)+(r_R-1)\leq r-1. $$

For the modified exterior recursion, the total potential at any depth is consequently at most $\tau-1$. Every call containing several types has potential at least one. There are thus at most $\tau-1$ such calls at each depth and at most $(\tau-1)S$ overall.

Each of those calls starts one middle ordinary recursion. Applying the same potential argument separately to every such recursion gives the conservative total call bound
$$ R=O\!\left(1+\tau^2(S+1)^2\right), $$
including terminal calls. This also bounds the number of recorded interval pieces.

Each call needs
$$ O\!\left(m\tau+\tau\log(\tau+1)\right) $$
rational operations: compute type cuts, sort the type blocks, and split their counts.

Here is a bit-length justification. On each common elementary interval, a cumulative valuation has the form
$$ F_t(x)=h_{tj}x+\beta_{tj}. $$
For a positive-value half cut lying in an interval with positive height,
$$ c= \frac{(F_t(a)+F_t(b))/2-\beta_{tj}}{h_{tj}}. $$
Thus each cut is an affine rational expression in its parent endpoints. Zero-value cuts are existing endpoints.

There is an integer $H$ with
$$ \log H=O(L) $$
such that all denominators of these affine coefficients divide $H$. To obtain one, take a sufficiently large fixed power of the product of the input denominators, positive-height numerators, and $2$. Cumulative-value coefficients introduce no additional prime factors.

An endpoint produced at affine-operation depth $d$ therefore has denominator dividing $H^d$. Since it lies in $[0,1]$, its numerator and denominator have bit length $O(L(d+1))$. In this execution, $d\leq S+1$: a path can follow exterior recursion and then enter one middle ordinary recursion, but cannot subsequently enter another middle recursion.

For a queried rational interval $I$, compute
$$ \overline K_t(I) = \frac{1}{n\mu_t} \sum_{\substack{J\text{ recorded}\\\text{for type }t}} \lambda(J\cap I). $$
Recorded intervals have disjoint interiors, so the sum has no positive-measure multiplicity.

Put
$$ p=\max\!\left\{0,\left\lceil\log_2(1/\delta)\right\rceil\right\}. $$
A conservative bit-operation bound is
$$ O\!\left( R\bigl(m\tau+\tau\log(\tau+1)\bigr) \bigl(L(S+1)+L_I+\log(n+1)+p+1\bigr)^3 +\log(q_0+1) \right). $$
Here rational numbers are reduced during arithmetic; their denominators divide the common powers described above, together with the queried endpoint denominators and the final normalization denominator.

Under an explicit binary rational encoding, $S\leq L$. The bound is therefore polynomial in the requested parameters and the query encoding length. The answer can be returned exactly or rounded to additive error $\delta$.

5. New result: polynomial-bit incentive witnesses at fixed replica size

Theorem 4. Fix a compatible $q$ given in binary. If
$$ \epsilon_q>e, $$
then some representative has a permitted rational report whose total encoding length is polynomial in
$$ m,\ \tau,\ L,\ \log(nq),\ E $$
and whose gain is strictly greater than $e$.

Such a report can be verified in polynomial time without expanding the representatives. Hence the fixed-replica predicate $\epsilon_q>e$ belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$, and $\epsilon_q\leq e$ belongs to $\mathrm{coNP}$.

This theorem applies to arbitrary rational masses.

5.1. The relevant execution has few queries

Distinguish the deviator from all truthful representatives. Follow its modified exterior path. At every nonterminal modified call, also follow its single path through that call’s middle ordinary recursion.

Put
$$ N=nq,\qquad d=\lceil\log_2 N\rceil. $$
The exterior path has at most $d$ nonterminal calls. At exterior depth $j$, its population size is at most
$$ \left\lceil\frac{N}{2^j}\right\rceil, $$
so the corresponding middle ordinary path has at most $d-j$ nonterminal calls. The number $Q$ of deviator cut queries is therefore at most
$$ Q\leq d+\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}(d-j) =\frac{d(d+3)}2. $$

The deviator’s final piece is a union of at most $d+1$ intervals: one ordinary terminal interval from each middle recursion and one exterior terminal interval.

Only these paths affect its allocation. Other branches contain no deviator and cannot affect these paths.

5.2. Compressed simulation preserves identities

At a relevant call, store the truthful count of each type. For the deviator’s true type, additionally distinguish truthful identities before and after the deviator. Initially these counts are its within-type ordinal minus one and the number of identities following it.

All truthful representatives of one type have a common cut. Sort these blocks and the deviator by coordinate and identity. If the deviator ties its true type’s cut, its earlier and later identity blocks lie on the stipulated sides of it. A split at rank $h$ requires only subtraction of binary counts, and updates the earlier and later counts directly.

This representation therefore reproduces exact group membership and both boundary cuts. It stores $O(\tau)$ counts per call, each using $O(\log N)$ bits.

Given a report with $J$ elementary intervals, its cuts can be computed by scanning those intervals. Since there are $O(d^2)$ relevant calls, verification uses polynomially many rational operations in
$$ m,\ \tau,\ J,\ L,\ d $$
and the report’s encoding length.

The same procedure computes the distinguished representative’s truthful baseline allocation.

5.3. A guessed execution is a rational linear system

Fix a deviator and guess the discrete data of its relevant execution:

  • coordinate equalities and strict orders;
  • elementary-interval locations of all endpoints and truthful cuts;
  • whether each queried interval has zero or positive reported value;
  • the resulting cut order, groups, and followed children.

There are
$$ P=O\!\left((\tau+1)(d+1)^2\right) $$
coordinate and cumulative-value variables. For a fixed guess, every condition below is linear.

For a truthful type $s$ at a call with population size $k$, write
$$ \alpha=\frac{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}{k}. $$
Its cut satisfies
$$ F_s(c_s)-F_s(a) = \alpha\bigl(F_s(b)-F_s(a)\bigr). $$
Once elementary intervals containing the coordinates have been guessed, every occurrence of $F_s$ is an affine function.

Leftmostness is also expressible linearly. If
$$ F_s(b)=F_s(a), $$
require $c_s=a$. Otherwise, require positive interval value and choose a positive-height elementary interval $[\ell,u]$ with
$$ \ell<c_s\leq u,\qquad a<c_s, $$
together with the cut equation.

These conditions are sufficient: the cumulative value strictly increases immediately before $c_s$, so no earlier point can reach the target. They are necessary: a positive leftmost target cut cannot have a zero-height interval immediately to its left, because that would provide an earlier point attaining the target.

For the deviator, collect $0,1$ and every queried coordinate $a,b,c$. Guess their equality pattern and order, and write their distinct coordinates as
$$ 0=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_J=1. $$
Introduce variables $z_j$ for reported cumulative values, imposing
$$ 0=z_0\leq z_1\leq\cdots\leq z_J=1. $$

Every deviator query imposes
$$ R(c)-R(a)=\alpha\bigl(R(b)-R(a)\bigr), $$
where $R$ at a collected point means its corresponding $z$ variable.

If $R(b)=R(a)$, require $c=a$. Otherwise, require $R(b)>R(a)$ and, for the collected point $x_\ell$ immediately preceding $c$, impose
$$ R(x_\ell)<R(c). $$
The queried endpoint $a$ was collected, so $x_\ell\geq a$.

These constraints construct a report: linearly interpolate the cumulative values between successive collected points, giving density
$$ r(x)=\frac{z_{j+1}-z_j}{x_{j+1}-x_j} \qquad\text{for }x_j<x<x_{j+1}. $$
It is nonnegative and normalized. For a positive target, cumulative values before $x_\ell$ are below $R(c)$, and the interpolation strictly increases from $x_\ell$ to $c$. Thus $c$ is exactly the leftmost cut. Zero-value queries obey the required convention.

There are at most $3Q+1$ report intervals.

Finally, suppose the terminal allocation intervals are $[u_\ell,v_\ell]$, and let $U_0$ be the already computed truthful baseline value. The desired violation is the strict linear inequality
$$ q\sum_\ell \bigl(F_{t(i)}(v_\ell)-F_{t(i)}(u_\ell)\bigr) -qU_0>e. $$
The cumulative functions are affine on the guessed elementary intervals.

The system contains $O(P)$ constraints. Its cut orders and rank counts enforce the guessed group assignments. Induction through the relevant calls consequently shows that every feasible report realizes the guessed allocation.

5.4. Coefficient and solution bit lengths

The truthful baseline has polynomial bit length. To see this explicitly, let $H$ cover the input-dependent affine coefficient denominators as in Section 4, and multiply it by the population sizes of all relevant baseline calls. There are $O(d^2)$ calls, each of size at most $N$, so the resulting integer $H_N$ satisfies
$$ \log H_N=O(L+d^3). $$
All baseline cut coefficients have denominators dividing $H_N$. The endpoint-operation depth is at most $d+1$, giving endpoint bit length
$$ O\!\left(L(d+1)+(d+1)^4\right). $$
The terminal utility sum has the same polynomial bound because these endpoints have a common bounded denominator.

Accordingly, every coefficient of the guessed linear system has bit length at most
$$ B=O\!\left(L(d+1)+(d+1)^4+E\right). $$

A feasible bounded rational linear system with strict inequalities has a polynomial-bit rational solution. Here is the argument needed for this application.

Write its strict inequalities as
$$ g_j(y)>0. $$
All coordinate and cumulative-value variables are bounded in $[0,1]$. Introduce $t$ and replace the strict inequalities by
$$ g_j(y)\geq t,\qquad 0\leq t\leq1. $$
If the original system is feasible, its finitely many strict margins have positive minimum, so the new system has a feasible point with $t>0$.

The new feasible set is a bounded rational polytope. Maximizing $t$ therefore gives a positive optimum at a vertex. For completeness, an optimal point with fewer than a full set of independent active constraints admits a direction preserving its active constraints. Small perturbations in both directions are feasible, so optimality forces the objective to be constant along that direction. Moving until another constraint becomes active, and repeating, produces an optimal vertex; boundedness prevents indefinite movement.

A vertex is determined by a nonsingular system of active rational constraints. Clearing denominators row by row gives integer coefficients of bit length $O(PB)$. Cramer’s rule and the determinant expansion then bound every vertex coordinate’s bit length by
$$ O\!\left(P^2(B+\log(P+1))\right). $$
The interpolated report heights are ratios of differences of these coordinates and cumulative values, so they have the same polynomial bit bound.

Thus the full report, together with the deviator’s identity, has encoding length at most
$$ O\!\left(P^3(B+\log(P+1))\right). $$

5.5. Completion of the certificate proof

If $\epsilon_q>e$, the definition of supremum implies that some permitted report gives a gain strictly greater than $e$. Choose that report’s actual execution data in Section 5.3. Its actual coordinates and cumulative values satisfy the resulting system.

Section 5.4 supplies a polynomial-bit rational feasible solution. Interpolation constructs a permitted rational report, and the execution argument proves that its gain remains strictly greater than $e$.

Conversely, any valid report certificate whose exact simulated gain exceeds $e$ proves $\epsilon_q>e$. The compressed simulation, report normalization checks, and rational arithmetic all take polynomial time in the input and certificate lengths. This proves Theorem 4. $\square$

An explicit, though exponential, fixed-replica decision procedure is obtained by enumerating all certificates up to the proved length bound and verifying them. Its running time is bounded by
$$ 2^{O(P^3(B+\log(P+1)))} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,d,E). $$
Each verification uses binary counts rather than an expanded population. This is not a polynomial-time algorithm for the target uniform decision.

6. Consequences and an exactly solved incentive case

Theorem 4 implies that violations of the uniform threshold are semidecidable.

Enumerate compatible $q\geq q_0$ and apply the finite-replica decision procedure to each. If $\epsilon^*>e$, some such $q$ has $\epsilon_q>e$, and the procedure eventually finds a certificate. If $\epsilon^*\leq e$, this enumeration need not terminate.

This gives no running-time bound in $\log q_0$: the size of a witnessing replica remains uncontrolled.

There is also a complete homogeneous incentive result.

Proposition 5. If every representative has the same true normalized valuation $V$, then
$$ \epsilon^*=0. $$

Proof. Under truthful reporting, Lemma 1 gives each representative value at least $1/N$. Since the allocations partition a cake of value one, every representative receives exactly $1/N$.

After one representative deviates, the other $N-1$ representatives still report $V$. Lemma 1 guarantees each of them true value at least $1/N$. Their pieces are disjoint up to endpoints, so the deviator’s true value is at most
$$ 1-\frac{N-1}{N}=\frac1N. $$
Its gain is therefore nonpositive for every report and every compatible replica. Taking positive parts and suprema proves the proposition. $\square$

In particular, when $\tau=1$, the continuous allocation measure is
$$ \overline K_1=\frac{\lambda}{n}, $$
and $\epsilon^*\leq e$ holds for every allowed $e\geq0$.

7. What remains open

For dyadic masses, continuous truthful type allocation existence and polynomial-time computation are proved completely.

For incentives, the new theorem controls report complexity and parameter bit length at each fixed replica size. It does not control the binary length of a replica witnessing $\epsilon^*>e$. Consequently, neither a polynomial-time algorithm nor an NP-hardness reduction for the precise uniform predicate has been established here.

Exact invariance of truthful type-average measures does not imply invariance of individual incentive opportunities: replacing one report breaks the scaled truthful block comparison used in Theorem 3.
