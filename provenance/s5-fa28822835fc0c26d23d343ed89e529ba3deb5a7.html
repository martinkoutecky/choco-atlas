The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a binary rational \(v\ge 0\), let \(\Omega=[0,1]\) have Lebesgue mass and let allocations be measurable \(x\in L^1_+(\Omega)\) with \(\int_\Omega x\,d\mu=1\). For every measurable coalition \(A\subseteq\Omega\), define \(\Pi(A,x)=\int_A(x(a)+v)\,d\mu(a)\). For allocations \(x,y\), let \(W=\{a:y(a)>x(a)\}\) and \(L=\{a:y(a)<x(a)\}\), and say \(y\) dominates \(x\) when \(\Pi(W,x)>\Pi(L,x)\). Decide whether there exists a set of allocations satisfying internal and external stability under this dominance relation.

Scope of the result

A stable set exists when $v=0$. In fact, there are continuum many pairwise disjoint stable sets.

For every $v>0$, I also construct an internally stable family that dominates every allocation taking at most two values outside that family. This settles existence in the two-valued subgame, but does not settle the full problem.

1. Dominance notation

For $x,y\in X$, define
$$ D_x(y)=\int_\Omega \operatorname{sgn}(y-x)(x+v)\,d\mu, $$
where $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$. Then
$$ y\succ x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D_x(y)>0. $$

The integral is finite because the absolute value of its integrand is bounded by $x+v$. It is unchanged by modifications on null sets.

Pointwise,
$$ \operatorname{sgn}(y-x)(x+v) + \operatorname{sgn}(x-y)(y+v) =-|y-x|. $$
Consequently,
$$ D_x(y)+D_y(x)=-\|y-x\|_1. $$

2. Proven: stable sets exist when $v=0$

Fix any real number $c>0$. Put
$$ Q_c=\{0\}\cup\{c2^k:k\in\mathbb Z\}, $$
and define
$$ S_c=\{x\in X:x(a)\in Q_c\text{ for almost every }a\}. $$

We prove that $S_c$ is stable when $v=0$.

2.1. Nonemptiness and internal stability

Choose $q\in Q_c$ with $q>1$ and a measurable set $A$ of measure $1/q$. Then $q\mathbf1_A\in S_c$, so $S_c$ is nonempty.

Let $x,y\in S_c$, and write
$$ W=\{y>x\},\qquad B=\{y<x\}. $$
On $W$, whenever $x>0$, the spacing of $Q_c$ implies $y\geq 2x$. When $x=0$, the inequality $y-x\geq x$ also holds. Thus
$$ \int_W x\,d\mu\leq\int_W(y-x)\,d\mu. $$

Since both allocations have integral one,
$$ \int_W(y-x)\,d\mu=\int_B(x-y)\,d\mu. $$
Nonnegativity of $y$ gives
$$ \int_B(x-y)\,d\mu\leq\int_Bx\,d\mu. $$
Therefore, when $v=0$,
$$ D_x(y)=\int_Wx\,d\mu-\int_Bx\,d\mu\leq0. $$
This applies to every ordered pair in $S_c$, proving internal stability.

2.2. External stability

Let $x\in X\setminus S_c$. Define
$$ E=\{a:x(a)\in Q_c\},\qquad U=\Omega\setminus E. $$
Up to null sets, $x>0$ on $U$. Moreover, $U$ has positive measure, so
$$ M=\int_Ux\,d\mu>0. $$

For $a\in U$, let $r(a)$ be the smallest positive element of $Q_c$ strictly greater than $x(a)$. Consecutive positive grid values differ by a factor of two. Because $x(a)$ is not itself a grid value,
$$ x(a)<r(a)<2x(a). $$
The function $r$ is measurable, since its level sets are intersections of $U$ with intervals between consecutive grid values. It is integrable on $U$, and
$$ M<\int_Ur\,d\mu<2M. $$

There is a measurable subset $A\subset U$ such that
$$ \int_Ar\,d\mu=M. $$
For completeness, the function
$$ F(t)=\int_{U\cap[0,t]}r\,d\mu $$
is continuous on $[0,1]$ by dominated convergence. Its endpoint values are $0$ and a number strictly greater than $M$. The intermediate value theorem therefore supplies such an $A$.

Define
$$ y=x\mathbf1_E+r\mathbf1_A. $$
Then $y$ takes values in $Q_c$ almost everywhere, and
$$ \int_\Omega y\,d\mu =\int_Ex\,d\mu+\int_Ar\,d\mu =\int_Ex\,d\mu+M=1. $$
Hence $y\in S_c$.

The winners are $A$, the losers are $U\setminus A$, and allocations agree on $E$. Since $r<2x$ on $A$ and $\int_Ar=M>0$,
$$ \int_Ax\,d\mu>\frac M2. $$
Therefore
$$ D_x(y) =\int_Ax\,d\mu-\int_{U\setminus A}x\,d\mu =2\int_Ax\,d\mu-M>0. $$
Thus $y\succ x$, proving external stability.

Conclusion. For every $c>0$, $S_c$ is a stable set when $v=0$.

2.3. Many distinct stable sets

If $c,d\in[1,2)$ are distinct, their positive grids are disjoint. Indeed,
$$ c2^k=d2^\ell $$
would imply $c/d=2^{\ell-k}$; the only power of two strictly between $1/2$ and $2$ is $1$, forcing $c=d$.

Consequently, $S_c\cap S_d=\varnothing$: an allocation belonging to both would have to vanish almost everywhere, contradicting its integral being one.

Each $S_c$ is itself uncountable. For a fixed grid value $q>1$, it contains
$$ q\mathbf1_{[t,t+1/q]}, \qquad 0\leq t\leq1-\frac1q, $$
and distinct parameters give allocations unequal on a set of positive measure.

3. The same grid construction fails for every positive $v$

This section excludes an extension of the construction, not existence of other stable sets.

Fix $v>0$ and $c>0$. Choose grid values $a,d\in Q_c$ such that
$$ 0<a<\min\{1,v/2\},\qquad d\geq2. $$
Choose pairwise disjoint measurable sets $W,B,C$ with
$$ \mu(W)=\frac14,\qquad \mu(B)=\frac18,\qquad \mu(C)=\frac{1-3a/4}{d}. $$
These choices are possible because the last quantity is positive and at most $1/2$, while $W\cup B$ has measure $3/8$.

Define
$$ x=a\mathbf1_W+4a\mathbf1_B+d\mathbf1_C, $$
$$ y=2a\mathbf1_W+2a\mathbf1_B+d\mathbf1_C. $$
Both allocations lie in $S_c$, and both have integral
$$ \frac{3a}{4}+d\mu(C)=1. $$
Their winner and loser sets are exactly $W$ and $B$. Hence
$$ D_x(y) =\frac{a+v}{4}-\frac{4a+v}{8} =\frac{v-2a}{8}>0. $$

Thus $S_c$ fails internal stability for every $v>0$.

4. Proven: a stable set in every positive-$v$ two-valued subgame

Let $X_2\subset X$ consist of allocations taking at most two values almost everywhere. We now construct a stable set for the dominance relation restricted to $X_2$, for every $v>0$.

Put
$$ C_v=\frac{1+v}{2}, $$
and define the admissible parameter set
$$ I_v= \left\{ p:0<p\leq\frac12,\quad \frac{C_v}{1-p}-v\geq0 \right\}. $$
For $p\in I_v$, put
$$ h(p)=\frac{C_v}{p}-v, \qquad \ell(p)=\frac{C_v}{1-p}-v. $$
For a measurable set $A$ with $\mu(A)=p$, define
$$ b_{p,A}=h(p)\mathbf1_A+\ell(p)\mathbf1_{\Omega\setminus A}. $$
Let
$$ \mathcal B_v=\{b_{p,A}:p\in I_v,\ \mu(A)=p\}. $$

These are feasible allocations because
$$ ph(p)+(1-p)\ell(p)=2C_v-v=1. $$
At $p=1/2$, the allocation is the constant $1$. For $p<1/2$,
$$ 0\leq\ell(p)<1<h(p). $$
Their defining power balance is
$$ p(h(p)+v)=(1-p)(\ell(p)+v)=C_v. $$

4.1. Internal stability

Consider $x=b_{p,A}$ and $y=b_{q,B}$.

First suppose $p<q$. Then
$$ h(p)>h(q),\qquad \ell(p)<\ell(q). $$
Thus $y<x$ everywhere on $A$ and $y>x$ everywhere outside $A$, irrespective of $B$. Consequently,
$$ D_x(y) =(1-p)(\ell(p)+v)-p(h(p)+v)=0. $$

In the reverse direction,
$$ D_y(x)=2\int_A(y+v)\,d\mu-(1+v). $$
Since $y+v\leq C_v/q$ everywhere,
$$ D_y(x) \leq2p\frac{C_v}{q}-2C_v =2C_v\left(\frac pq-1\right)<0. $$

If $p=q$, the sets $A\setminus B$ and $B\setminus A$ have equal measure. Therefore
$$ D_x(y)=-(h(p)-\ell(p))\mu(A\setminus B)\leq0, $$
and the same calculation with $A,B$ exchanged gives $D_y(x)\leq0$.

This proves internal stability of $\mathcal B_v$ on the full space $X$.

4.2. External stability within $X_2$

Every nonconstant allocation in $X_2$ can be written
$$ x=h\mathbf1_P+\ell\mathbf1_{\Omega\setminus P}, \qquad 0\leq\ell<1<h, $$
where
$$ \alpha=\mu(P)=\frac{1-\ell}{h-\ell}. $$
Let
$$ R=\alpha(h+v) $$
be the power of its high-allocation group.

The constant allocation satisfies
$$ D_x(1)=1+v-2R=2C_v-2R. $$

If $R<C_v$, it dominates $x$. If $R=C_v$, both groups have power $C_v$, and the defining formulas show $x\in\mathcal B_v$.

It remains to consider $R>C_v$. Put
$$ p_0=\frac{C_v}{h+v}<\alpha. $$
At this parameter, $h(p_0)=h$. The corresponding low value satisfies
$$ \ell(p_0)-\ell = \frac{(h-\ell)(\alpha-p_0)}{1-p_0}>0. $$

Choose $p<p_0$ sufficiently close to $p_0$ that
$$ h(p)>h,\qquad \ell(p)>\ell. $$
Such parameters remain admissible because $\ell(p)>\ell\geq0$. Choose $A\subset P$ with $\mu(A)=p$, and let $y=b_{p,A}$.

Its winners against $x$ are $A\cup(\Omega\setminus P)$; its losers are $P\setminus A$. Therefore
$$ D_x(y) =(1-\alpha)(\ell+v)+(2p-\alpha)(h+v). $$
As $p$ approaches $p_0$ from below, this expression approaches
$$ 2(1-\alpha)(\ell+v)>0, $$
where strict positivity uses $v>0$ and $\alpha<1$. Hence sufficiently close choices give $y\succ x$.

We have proved that every allocation in $X_2\setminus\mathcal B_v$ is dominated by a member of $\mathcal B_v$.

Conclusion. For every $v>0$, $\mathcal B_v$ is a stable set in the two-valued subgame $X_2$.

5. This family fails on the full space when $0\leq v<1$

The preceding subgame result cannot be promoted to unrestricted existence.

Fix $0\leq v<1$. Choose an integer $N\geq2$ and a real number $r>1$ such that
$$ \frac2N+\frac{1+v}{r}<1-v. $$
Put $H_j=r^j$ for $1\leq j\leq N$, and choose disjoint measurable sets $P_j$ with
$$ \mu(P_j)=\frac1{NH_j}. $$
Their total measure is less than $1$, so these choices are possible. Define
$$ x=\sum_{j=1}^NH_j\mathbf1_{P_j}. $$
Then $\int_\Omega x\,d\mu=1$. It takes at least three distinct values, including zero, and therefore lies outside $\mathcal B_v$.

Consider any $y=b_{p,A}\in\mathcal B_v$, and abbreviate its high and low values by $h$ and $\ell$. Here
$$ \ell\leq1,\qquad h\geq1,\qquad \mu(A)=\frac{C_v}{h+v}. $$
All positive values of $x$ exceed $1$. Thus any positive-wealth agent who wins or ties must belong to
$$ A\cap\{x\leq h\}. $$

If some $H_j\leq h$, let $j$ be the largest such index. The entire level $H_j$ carries wealth $1/N$. All lower levels are at most $H_j/r\leq h/r$. Writing $W=\{y>x\}$ and $T=\{y=x\}$, we obtain
$$ \int_{W\cup T}x\,d\mu \leq\frac1N+\frac hr\mu(A) \leq\frac1N+\frac{C_v}{r}. $$
If no $H_j\leq h$, the left side is zero, so the same bound holds.

Since winner, loser and tie sets partition $\Omega$,
$$ D_x(y) = 2\int_Wx\,d\mu+\int_Tx\,d\mu-1 +v\bigl(\mu(W)-\mu(B)\bigr). $$
Consequently,
$$ D_x(y) \leq 2\int_{W\cup T}x\,d\mu-1+v \leq \frac2N+\frac{1+v}{r}-1+v<0. $$

No member of $\mathcal B_v$ dominates $x$. Hence $\mathcal B_v$ is not externally stable on $X$ when $0\leq v<1$.
