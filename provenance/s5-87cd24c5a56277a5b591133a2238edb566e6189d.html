The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational initial mass distributions over finitely many user and provider learner types, rational finite-support reward kernels, and horizon H, where each type includes its preferences, reward parameters, and private UCB state, can one compute a sequence of feasible mass assignments x_t under balanced transfers such that each period is stable, has welfare at least half the maximum feasible welfare, and every positive-mass initial type has expected optimal regret O(log H) relative to its true-payoff stable mass assignment? Measure runtime in H, the reachable learner-state support τ_H, and input bit length; define fairness across a family of instances as the paper does, by comparing initial types’ regret growth as H increases.

  • Carried over positive reciprocal initial sample counts, strict true preferences, and distinct true balanced scores between different class pairs. Reciprocal counts are required initially; subsequent counts belong to individual learner states.

  • Replaced an anticipatory sequence with a reporting-based policy and its simulated prefixes, preserving the platform's information restriction despite kernels and private states being supplied to describe the instance.

3. The true-stable assignments and aggregate regret

Class masses remain constant throughout learning. Because total user mass and provider mass both equal \(1\), every feasible assignment uses all user mass: the sum of its row sums is \(1\), and each row sum is bounded above by its state's mass.

For an assignment \(x_t\), let \(d_t\) denote its total mass assigned from class \(A\) to class \(Q\). The class-level balance constraints imply that its four assigned masses are

\( \displaystyle x_t(A,P)=\frac12-d_t,\quad x_t(A,Q)=d_t,\quad x_t(B,P)=d_t,\quad x_t(B,Q)=\frac12-d_t. \)

Claim. An assignment belongs to \(S(\rho,q_t)\) exactly when it assigns all \(A\)-mass to \(P\) and all \(B\)-mass to \(Q\).

Indeed, if \(d_t>0\), some \(A\)-state receives positive mass at score \(1\), and some \(P\)-state receives positive mass at score \(2\). Their pair has score \(4\), so these submasses form a blocking pair. Conversely, under the stated diagonal assignments, the assigned scores are \(4\) and \(3\); neither cross-class pair improves both endpoints, and neither diagonal pair strictly improves an endpoint already receiving its diagonal score.

Thus every true-stable assignment has total expected payoff \(7\). Transfers cancel between endpoints, giving, for any feasible assignment \(x\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_i M_iG_{i,t}(x) =\sum_{s,r}x(s,r)\bigl(\mu_{a(s),a(r)}+\mu_{a(r),a(s)}\bigr). \)

In particular,

\( \displaystyle \sum_i M_iG_{i,t}(x_t) =2\left[4\left(\frac12-d_t\right)+d_t+2d_t +3\left(\frac12-d_t\right)\right] =7-8d_t. \)

Define

\( \displaystyle B_{i,t}:=M_i\max_{y\in S(\rho,q_t)}G_{i,t}(y), \qquad \Phi_t:=\sum_iB_{i,t}-7. \)

These maxima exist: the true-stable assignments are precisely the product of two transportation polytopes with rational margins, and each objective is linear. An optimal vertex is rational.

For any common true-stable assignment \(y\), each \(B_{i,t}\) is at least \(M_iG_{i,t}(y)\). Summing gives \(\Phi_t\geq0\).

Write

\( \displaystyle D_H:=\sum_{t=1}^Hd_t,\qquad \Gamma(H):=\sum_iM_iR_i(H). \)

The regret definition and the preceding cancellation yield the exact identity

\( \displaystyle \Gamma(H)=\sum_{t=1}^H\mathbb E[\Phi_t+8d_t]. \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \Gamma(H)\geq8\,\mathbb E D_H. \)

This already shows that keeping aggregate regret logarithmic requires keeping cumulative cross-class assigned mass logarithmic. We next show that this creates another regret cost.

4. Both \(A\)-cohorts can benchmark against the same best provider mass

For a current provider state \(r\) of class \(P\), define

\( f_t(r):=\nu_t(r,A). \)

Let the maximum report sum obtainable from provider mass \(1/4\) be

\( \displaystyle K_t:= \max_{\substack{0\leq\eta_r\leq q_t(r)\\ \sum_{r:a(r)=P}\eta_r=1/4}} \sum_{r:a(r)=P}\eta_rf_t(r), \)

and put

\( \displaystyle F_t:=K_t-\frac12\sum_{r:a(r)=P}q_t(r)f_t(r). \)

The choice \(\eta_r=q_t(r)/2\) is feasible, so \(F_t\geq0\).

Choose a reference true-stable assignment \(y^0\) that couples state masses proportionally within each diagonal class pair. Thus \(y^0(s,r)=2q_t(s)q_t(r)\) on the diagonal class pairs. In particular, each of the two \(A\)-cohorts receives half of every \(P\)-state's mass.

For either \(i\in\{A_-,A_+\}\), every true-stable assignment gives

\( \displaystyle M_iG_{i,t}(y) =4M_i-\frac12\sum_{s\in i}q_t(s)\nu_t(s,P) +\frac12\sum_{r:a(r)=P} \left(\sum_{s\in i}y(s,r)\right)f_t(r). \)

The first two terms are independent of the coupling. The cohort can obtain the last term's maximum by receiving the provider mass selected in \(K_t\); the other \(A\)-cohort receives the remainder. Such a coupling is feasible and true-stable.

Therefore, for each of the two cohorts,

\( \displaystyle B_{i,t}-M_iG_{i,t}(y^0)=\frac12F_t. \)

Every other cohort's benchmark is also at least its payoff under \(y^0\). Since the total payoff under \(y^0\) equals \(7\), we obtain

\( \displaystyle \Phi_t\geq F_t. \)

The two maxima here use separate comparison assignments, exactly as the regret definition permits. Both cohorts benchmark against the same highest-report provider mass.

5. Comparing provider reports with an always-sampled population

Fix any feasible assignment prefix. Introduce tagged trajectories for the provider cohort \(P\), with total mass \(1/2\). Their current-state marginals are the specified masses \(q_t(r)\).

At each round, choose a tagged provider's partner according to its assigned state masses. Independently generate a fresh symmetric sign \(\varepsilon_j\). If its partner has class \(A\), use reward \(4+\varepsilon_j\) for its actual update. If its partner has class \(B\), use the required deterministic reward \(2\), and retain \(\varepsilon_j\) only as a virtual draw.

This construction reproduces the actual provider-state evolution: whenever arm \(A\) is played, its update has the prescribed independent kernel law. The virtual signs remain independent symmetric signs because each is generated independently after the partner choice.

For a tagged trajectory, let \(\ell_t\) be the number of rounds before \(t\) in which its partner had class \(B\). Its actual count for arm \(A\) is

\( n=t-\ell_t. \)

Integrating over the tagged provider mass gives

\( \displaystyle \int\ell_t\,dq=D_{t-1}, \)

because provider \(P\)-mass assigned to \(B\) in round \(j\) is \(d_j\).

Set \(\beta_t:=\sqrt{6\log t}\). Define the virtual report that would result from sampling arm \(A\) in every preceding round:

\( \displaystyle \bar f_t =4-\frac1t+\frac{X_{t-1}}t+\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt t}, \qquad X_{t-1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{t-1}\varepsilon_j. \)

The term \(-1/t\) comes from the initial sample \(3\).

The actual sample list is obtained by deleting \(\ell_t\) entries from the virtual list of \(t\) samples. All these samples lie in \([3,5]\). Writing the full mean as the weighted average of the retained and deleted means therefore gives

\( \displaystyle |\widehat\mu_t-\widehat{\bar\mu}_t| \leq\frac{2\ell_t}{t}. \)

The confidence terms satisfy

\( \displaystyle \frac1{\sqrt n}-\frac1{\sqrt t} =\frac1{\sqrt n}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac nt}\right) \leq\frac1{\sqrt n}\left(1-\frac nt\right) =\frac{\ell_t}{t\sqrt n} \leq\frac{\ell_t}{t}. \)

Here \(n\geq1\), and \(\sqrt u\geq u\) for \(u\in[0,1]\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \int|f_t-\bar f_t|\,dq \leq(2+\beta_t)\frac{D_{t-1}}t. \)

6. A lower bound on the benchmark advantage

On any provider trajectory space of total mass \(1/2\), define

\( \displaystyle \mathcal F(g):= \sup_{\substack{0\leq\theta\leq1\\ \int\theta\,dq=1/4}} \int\left(\theta-\frac12\right)g\,dq. \)

This depends only on the distribution of report values. For the actual reports it equals \(F_t\): selecting trajectories with a given report value is equivalent to selecting a fraction of the corresponding state mass.

Since \(|\theta-1/2|\leq1/2\), every coupling of two report distributions satisfies

\( \displaystyle |\mathcal F(g)-\mathcal F(h)| \leq\frac12\int|g-h|\,dq. \)

The virtual variable \(X_{t-1}\) has a distribution symmetric about zero. Selecting its positive values, and half of any mass at zero, selects exactly half the provider population. This maximizes the selected report sum. Hence

\( \displaystyle \mathcal F(\bar f_t)=\frac1{4t}\mathbb E|X_{t-1}|. \)

Combining this with the preceding report comparison gives

\( \displaystyle \Phi_t\geq F_t \geq\frac1{4t}\mathbb E|X_{t-1}| -\frac{2+\beta_t}{2t}D_{t-1}. \)

We now bound the absolute moment explicitly. For \(n\geq1\), independence and symmetry give

\( \displaystyle \mathbb EX_n^2=n,\qquad \mathbb EX_n^4=n+6\binom n2=3n^2-2n. \)

Hölder's inequality, with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\), yields

\( \displaystyle n=\mathbb E\bigl[|X_n|^{2/3}|X_n|^{4/3}\bigr] \leq(\mathbb E|X_n|)^{2/3} (\mathbb E|X_n|^4)^{1/3}. \)

Rearranging,

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E|X_n| \geq\frac{n^{3/2}}{\sqrt{3n^2-2n}} \geq\sqrt{\frac n3}. \)

Thus, for \(t\geq2\),

\( \displaystyle \Phi_t\geq \frac1{4\sqrt6\,\sqrt t} -\frac{2+\sqrt{6\log t}}{2t}D_{t-1}. \)

All these inequalities hold for each fixed assignment prefix. For randomized policies, we average them over the policy's randomness.

7. The quantitative contradiction

For \(H\geq4\), consider rounds \(t=\lceil H/2\rceil,\ldots,H\). There are at least \(H/2\) such rounds, and

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t=\lceil H/2\rceil}^H\frac1{\sqrt t} \geq\frac{\sqrt H}{2}, \qquad \sum_{t=\lceil H/2\rceil}^H\frac1t\leq2. \)

Also, \(D_{t-1}\leq D_H\). Using \(\Phi_t\geq0\) outside this interval, we obtain

\( \displaystyle \Gamma(H) \geq\sum_{t=\lceil H/2\rceil}^H\mathbb E\Phi_t \geq\frac{\sqrt H}{8\sqrt6} -\bigl(2+\sqrt{6\log H}\bigr)\mathbb ED_H. \)

Together with \(\mathbb ED_H\leq\Gamma(H)/8\), this implies

\( \displaystyle \Gamma(H)\geq \frac{\sqrt H}{\sqrt6\bigl(10+\sqrt{6\log H}\bigr)}. \)

Suppose every cohort had the required upper bound with horizon-independent constants. Then

\( \displaystyle \Gamma(H)\leq \left(\sum_iM_iC_i\right)\log(1+H). \)

This contradicts the proved lower bound, because

\( \displaystyle \frac{\sqrt H} {\bigl(10+\sqrt{6\log H}\bigr)\log(1+H)} \longrightarrow\infty. \)

Therefore no policy can satisfy the logarithmic-regret requirement for every initial cohort in this fixed society.

8. Consequence for the question and the cited results

The obstruction arises from the full weak-stability comparator. True stability fixes the class-level assignments, while allowing arbitrary rearrangements of private states within those classes. The two \(A\)-cohorts can consequently benchmark against the same favorable provider states. Keeping cross-class exploration small leaves enough report dispersion to make these competing benchmarks jointly unattainable.

This distinction matters for the cited discrete result. Cen–Shah's Appendix F states that, when consistent tie-breaking is used to extend the result to ties, the stable benchmark set should be restricted to matchings obtainable under that rule. The present task explicitly retains the full weak-stability set. [Cen–Shah, Appendix F](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/cen22a/cen22a.pdf#page=25)

Thus the requested guaranteed construction is infeasible on allowed inputs, regardless of computational resources. This proof does not classify a different decision problem that asks whether a particular input admits such a policy.
