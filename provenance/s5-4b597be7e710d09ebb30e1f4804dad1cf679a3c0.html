2. A compressed round-robin algorithm

Write
$$ |b|=\sum_{h=1}^p b_h. $$

Consider either of the following additional promises on type $A$.

  • Cardinality ordering: for every two bundles $b,d$,

$$ |b|<|d|\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_A(b)\le C_A(d). $$
Costs among bundles of equal cardinality may differ.

  • Additivity with bounded ratio: there are positive rational weights $a_h$ such that

$$ C_A(b)=C_A(0)+\sum_{h=1}^p a_hb_h, \qquad \frac{\max_h a_h}{\min_h a_h}\le2. $$

These are promises about the entire oracle function. In particular, querying singleton bundles does not establish additivity.

Proven claim. Under either promise, an assignment can be found using polynomially many bit operations, with at most $p+2$ supported pairs. Under cardinality ordering, no oracle calls are needed. Under the additive promise, at most $p+1$ oracle calls are needed.

2.1 Fixed priorities

Give each type a fixed priority order over kinds.

For type $B$, sort kinds by increasing $w_h$, breaking ties by kind index.

Under cardinality ordering, type $A$ may use any fixed order. Under the additive promise, query $C_A(0)$ and $C_A(e_h)$, and compute
$$ a_h=C_A(e_h)-C_A(0). $$
Sort kinds by increasing $a_h$, again breaking ties consistently.

The construction simulates round robin over a parameter interval $[0,1)$: type $A$ occupies $[0,\mu_A)$ and type $B$ occupies $[\mu_A,1)$. During each round, every parameter position receives one copy of its type’s highest-priority available kind, unless all chores have already been assigned.

The interval description is implemented directly; no individual agents are created.

2.2 Skipping complete rounds

Let $R_h$ be the remaining per-capita supply of kind $h$. At a round boundary, suppose some supply remains. Let $h_A,h_B$ be the current preferred available kinds, and define
$$ v_h = \mu_A\mathbf 1_{\{h=h_A\}} + \mu_B\mathbf 1_{\{h=h_B\}}. $$
Thus $v_h$ is the supply of kind $h$ consumed by one complete round if these choices remain unchanged, and
$$ \sum_h v_h=1. $$

Compute
$$ r= \left\lfloor \min_{h:\,v_h>0}\frac{R_h}{v_h} \right\rfloor. $$
Perform these $r$ complete rounds in one operation:

  • add $r$ copies of $h_A$ to every type-$A$ bundle;
  • add $r$ copies of $h_B$ to every type-$B$ bundle;
  • replace $R_h$ by $R_h-rv_h$.

This batch is valid. Each chosen kind has enough supply for all its prescribed recipients throughout the batch. If its supply becomes zero, that happens at its last prescribed recipient in the batch. Removing kinds never makes a previously unavailable kind available, so a recipient’s prescribed choice remains its preferred kind while it has positive supply.

If the batch exhausts a kind, recompute the priorities at the next round boundary.

Otherwise, simulate the next round by intervals. At position $t$, let $u$ be the end of the current type interval and let $h$ be that type’s preferred available kind. Assign one copy of $h$ throughout an interval of length
$$ \ell=\min\{u-t,R_h\}. $$
Subtract $\ell$ from $R_h$, advance $t$ by $\ell$, and repeat until the round ends or every supply is zero.

If the batch exhausted no kind, this subsequent sweep must exhaust a kind. Indeed, the choices remain the same immediately after the batch, and
$$ \min_{h:\,v_h>0}\frac{R_h}{v_h}<1. $$
A full sweep with no exhaustion would consume $v_h$ of every chosen kind, contradicting the displayed inequality.

Consequently, every outer iteration exhausts at least one previously available kind, either in a batch or in the following sweep. There are at most $p$ such iterations.

2.3 Support size and bit complexity

Maintain a partition of $[0,1)$ into intervals on which both type and bundle are constant.

A batch changes the bundle uniformly within each type interval. A sweep introduces a new partition point only when a kind is exhausted inside a type interval. Each kind is exhausted once. Therefore there are at most $p$ additional partition points beyond $0,\mu_A,1$, and at most
$$ p+2 $$
final intervals.

For each final interval, output its length as the mass of its constant type-and-bundle pair. Merge identical pairs if necessary.

To bound encoding lengths, take $N$ to be the product of the input denominators of the type masses and supplies. Then
$$ \log N=O(L). $$
Every remaining supply and every partition point stays on the grid of multiples of $1/N$:

  • a batch subtracts an integer multiple of $\mu_A$, $\mu_B$, or $1$;
  • a sweep endpoint is a type boundary or the current position plus a remaining supply.

This is an invariant of the arithmetic; computing $N$ is unnecessary.

Let
$$ S=\sum_h\sigma_h. $$
Every complete round consumes total mass $1$ of chores. Thus the total number of complete rounds is at most $S$, and every final bundle has cardinality at most $\lceil S\rceil$. All repetition counts and bundle coordinates consequently have polynomial bit length.

There are polynomially many interval updates, involving only polynomial-length rational numbers. Hence the ordinary bit complexity is polynomial in $p$ and $L$. The oracle answers used to obtain the additive weights also have polynomial length by the answer-length promise.

Each interval remains inside its prescribed type block, so the type masses are correct. Every update increases assigned supply by exactly the amount subtracted from the corresponding $R_h$. Termination with all remaining supplies zero proves every supply equation.

2.4 The virtual finite interpretation

For correctness, choose a common denominator $N$ as above. The positions

2.5 Fairness for additive costs of ratio at most $2$

The following lemma applies to arbitrary finite round robin.

Lemma. Suppose agent $i$ has positive additive weights $u_h$ and always chooses a least-cost remaining chore. Let $b_i,b_j$ be its bundle and any other agent’s bundle. Then
$$ W_i(b_i)-W_i(b_j)\le u_{\max}, \qquad W_i(b)=\sum_hu_hb_h, \qquad u_{\max}=\max_hu_h. $$

Proof. Let $n_i,n_j$ be the numbers of picks made by the agents.

If $i$ precedes $j$ in the fixed round-robin order, then
$$ n_i\in\{n_j,n_j+1\}. $$
At $i$’s $r$-th pick, the chore eventually selected at $j$’s $r$-th pick is still available. Therefore, for every $r\le n_j$, the cost to $i$ of its own $r$-th chore is at most the cost to $i$ of $j$’s $r$-th chore. The only possible unmatched source chore costs at most $u_{\max}$.

If $i$ follows $j$, then
$$ n_j\in\{n_i,n_i+1\}. $$
When $n_i=0$, the claim follows from nonnegativity. Otherwise, for every $r<n_i$, the chore eventually selected at $j$’s $(r+1)$-st pick is available at $i$’s $r$-th pick. Pair these chores. The only unmatched source chore is $i$’s last chore; the unmatched target chores have nonnegative costs. Again the difference is at most $u_{\max}$. This proves the lemma.

If the weights have ratio at most $2$, then for every kind $h$ in $b_i$,
$$ \begin{aligned} W_i(b_i-e_h)-W_i(b_j+e_h) &=W_i(b_i)-W_i(b_j)-2u_h\\ &\le u_{\max}-2u_h\\ &\le0. \end{aligned} $$
Thus every such agent is tEFX-satisfied. Additive constants cancel in this calculation.

Type $B$ satisfies these hypotheses. Under the additional additive promise, type $A$ does as well.

2.6 Fairness under cardinality ordering

In round robin, any two final bundle cardinalities differ by at most one. Thus, for any type-$A$ source $b$, any target $d$, and any $h$ with $b_h>0$,
$$ |b-e_h|=|b|-1\le |d|<|d|+1=|d+e_h|. $$
Cardinality ordering gives
$$ C_A(b-e_h)\le C_A(d+e_h). $$
Type $B$ remains tEFX-satisfied by the preceding lemma.

Aggregation preserves these inequalities; self-comparisons also follow from monotonicity. This completes the proof of the restricted polynomial algorithm.

3. An exact capacity test for the previous compact distribution

This section uses no additional promise on $C_A$.

Sort kinds so that
$$ w_1\le\cdots\le w_p, $$
and define
$$ S_0=0, \qquad S_h=\sum_{j=1}^h\sigma_j. $$
For $0<t<1$, put
$$ F_h(t)=\lceil S_h-t\rceil, \qquad b_h(t)=F_h(t)-F_{h-1}(t). $$
Since $F_0(t)=0$ and the $S_h$ are nondecreasing, these are nonnegative integral bundles.

3.1 Conservation and small support

Write $S_h=q+r$, where $q$ is integral and $0\le r<1$. Except at endpoints, $F_h(t)$ equals $q+1$ on an interval of length $r$, and $q$ on the remainder. Hence
$$ \int_0^1F_h(t)\,dt=S_h, \qquad \int_0^1b_h(t)\,dt=\sigma_h. $$

The bundles change only at fractional parts of the cumulative supplies. Partition $[0,1]$ at those points. This produces at most $p+1$ positive-length intervals, with constant bundles $b^{(j)}$ and masses $y_j$, satisfying
$$ \sum_jy_j=1, \qquad \sum_jy_jb^{(j)}=\sigma. $$
The construction uses polynomial-length exact rational arithmetic.

3.2 Type $B$ is satisfied throughout

For $0<t<u<1$, define
$$ D_h=F_h(t)-F_h(u). $$
Then $D_h\in\{0,1\}$ and $D_0=0$. Summation by parts gives
$$ W_B(b(t))-W_B(b(u)) = w_pD_p-\sum_{h=1}^{p-1}(w_{h+1}-w_h)D_h. $$
Consequently,
$$ -(w_p-w_1) \le W_B(b(t))-W_B(b(u)) \le w_p. $$
Thus every two bundles in the distribution satisfy
$$ |W_B(b)-W_B(d)|\le w_p. $$
For every kind $h$ in a source bundle,
$$ W_B(b)-W_B(d) \le w_p \le2w_1 \le2w_h. $$
This is exactly the type-$B$ transfer inequality.

3.3 Computing the usable type-$A$ mass

For every kind $h$, define
$$ T_h=\min_j C_A(b^{(j)}+e_h). $$
Call bundle $b^{(j)}$ eligible for type $A$ if
$$ C_A(b^{(j)}-e_h)\le T_h \quad \text{for every }h\text{ with }b_h^{(j)}>0. $$
Let
$$ G=\{j:b^{(j)}\text{ is eligible for type }A\}, \qquad M_A=\sum_{j\in G}y_j. $$

Proven claim. Keeping the total mass of each bundle $b^{(j)}$ equal to $y_j$, a tEFX type assignment exists if and only if
$$ \mu_A\le M_A. $$

Necessity. Every original bundle retains positive total mass, so all of them remain targets in the fairness comparisons. Any bundle assigned positive type-$A$ mass must therefore satisfy exactly the eligibility inequalities. Type $A$ can receive at most the total mass $M_A$ of eligible bundles.

Sufficiency. Choose numbers $\beta_j$ satisfying
$$ 0\le\beta_j\le y_j, \qquad \beta_j=0\text{ for }j\notin G, \qquad \sum_j\beta_j=\mu_A. $$
Such numbers exist when $\mu_A\le M_A$: fill eligible bundles successively, splitting the last one if needed. Set
$$ x_{A,b^{(j)}}=\beta_j, \qquad x_{B,b^{(j)}}=y_j-\beta_j, $$
omitting zero entries.

The aggregate bundle distribution is unchanged, so conservation holds. Eligibility proves every type-$A$ inequality, and Section 3.2 proves every type-$B$ inequality.

This procedure splits at most one bundle between types, giving at most $p+2$ supported pairs. Computing the target minima and source values needs at most
$$ 2p(p+1) $$
oracle calls. The query and answer lengths, comparisons, and mass computations are polynomially bounded.

This test strengthens the condition in the notes. Indeed, any bundle minimizing $C_A$ over the distribution is eligible, because
$$ C_A(b-e_h)\le C_A(b)\le C_A(d)\le C_A(d+e_h). $$
Hence the earlier condition based on the smallest interval mass implies $\mu_A\le M_A$, whereas this test can also use the mass of other eligible bundles.

Failure of the test certifies only that this fixed aggregate distribution cannot accommodate type $A$. It does not certify infeasibility of the original instance.

4. A concrete obstruction to retaining that distribution

Take
$$ p=6, \qquad \mu_A=\frac34, \qquad \mu_B=\frac14, \qquad \sigma_h=\frac12, \qquad w_h=1 $$
for every kind, and use the kind order $1,\ldots,6$.

The distribution of Section 3 consists of
$$ b=(1,0,1,0,1,0), \qquad d=(0,1,0,1,0,1), $$
each with mass $1/2$.

Define the monotone binary-valued oracle
$$ C_A(z)= \begin{cases} 1,&z_3\ge1\text{ and }z_5\ge1,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
Bundle $d$ is eligible: its cost and every deletion cost are zero. Bundle $b$ is not eligible, since
$$ C_A(b-e_1)=1 \qquad\text{but}\qquad C_A(d+e_1)=0. $$
Thus
$$ M_A=\frac12<\frac34=\mu_A. $$
No splitting of these fixed bundle masses can produce a tEFX assignment.

The original instance nevertheless has a simple assignment. Use
$$ b'=(1,1,1,0,0,0), \qquad d'=(0,0,0,1,1,1), $$
and give each bundle type-$A$ mass $3/8$ and type-$B$ mass $1/8$.

Each kind then has total supply $1/2$. Both bundles have type-$A$ cost zero, so all type-$A$ deletion costs are zero. Both have type-$B$ cost $3$, so every type-$B$ comparison is
$$ 2\le4. $$

Therefore, a general algorithm must sometimes change the bundles themselves.

5. No assignment is universal over the type-$A$ oracle

The same explicit masses, supplies, and type-$B$ weights yield an information-theoretic obstruction.

Proven claim. For
$$ p=6,\qquad \mu_A=\frac34,\qquad \mu_B=\frac14,\qquad \sigma_h=\frac12,\qquad w_h=1, $$
there is no assignment that is tEFX for every coordinatewise monotone type-$A$ cost oracle.

5.1 Necessary coordinatewise comparisons

If an assignment worked for every such oracle, every supported type-$A$ source $b$, every supported target $d$, and every $h$ with $b_h>0$ would have to satisfy
$$ b-e_h\le d+e_h $$
coordinatewise.

To prove necessity, suppose this dominance fails and put $v=b-e_h$. The function
$$ C_v(z)= \begin{cases} 1,&z\ge v\text{ coordinatewise},\\ 0,&\text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$
is coordinatewise monotone and has constant-length answers. But
$$ C_v(b-e_h)=1, \qquad C_v(d+e_h)=0, $$
violating tEFX.

5.2 Every type-$A$ bundle would have at most two chores

Suppose an $A$ bundle $b$ has positive coordinates $j$ and $h$, with $j\ne h$. Apply the necessary dominance condition using source kind $h$ and any supported $A$ target $d$. Its $j$-th coordinate gives
$$ d_j\ge b_j\ge1. $$
Thus every type-$A$ bundle contains kind $j$, forcing
$$ \sigma_j \ge \sum_d x_{A,d}d_j \ge \mu_A = \frac34 > \frac12, $$
a contradiction.

Hence every $A$ bundle is empty or has the form $ke_h$.

For any kind $h$, some supported $A$ bundle $d$ must satisfy $d_h=0$; otherwise that kind’s supply would again be at least $\mu_A>1/2$. Comparing source $ke_h$ with this target gives
$$ k-1\le d_h+1=1. $$
Therefore $k\le2$, and every type-$A$ bundle has cardinality at most $2$.

5.3 Type-$B$ fairness contradicts total supply

Let $q$ be the smallest cardinality of any supported bundle. Since type $A$ has positive mass,
$$ q\le2. $$
For any nonempty type-$B$ bundle $b$, compare it with a bundle of cardinality $q$. Unit weights give
$$ |b|-1\le q+1, $$
so
$$ |b|\le q+2\le4. $$
Empty $B$ bundles also satisfy this bound.

Consequently, total assigned chores per capita would be at most
$$ 2\mu_A+4\mu_B = 2\cdot\frac34+4\cdot\frac14 = \frac52. $$
But the required total supply is
$$ \sum_{h=1}^6\sigma_h=3. $$
This contradiction proves the claim.

A deterministic algorithm making no oracle calls produces the same output for all type-$A$ oracles on this explicit input. Such an output cannot be correct for all of them. Therefore at least one oracle call is necessary in the unrestricted problem. This argument gives no superpolynomial lower bound.

6. What remains unresolved

The unrestricted problem is a total search problem: every promised input has a valid assignment.

The compressed algorithm above handles arbitrary masses, but its proof requires an additional structural promise on $C_A$. The capacity test applies to arbitrary monotone oracles, but can fail even on a simple feasible instance. The universal-assignment obstruction shows that oracle information matters, but does not establish that polynomially many calls are insufficient.

The remaining target is therefore to construct a general polynomial search algorithm, or to prove an appropriate search-hardness result or superpolynomial oracle lower bound.
