The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite catalogue of local bipartite multigraph types \(G_\theta=(V_\theta,E_\theta)\), rational copy masses \(\alpha_\theta\), and rational additive valuations \(v^\theta_u(e)\), where each copy contains one agent of every role \(u\in V_\theta\) and one indivisible item for every edge \(e\in E_\theta\), output nonnegative masses \(z_{\theta,X}\) over complete integral allocation patterns \(X\) such that \(\sum_X z_{\theta,X}=\alpha_\theta\) and every pattern with \(z_{\theta,X}>0\) is EFX for its local market.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

A rational bipartite multi-tree template is a finite multigraph \(G=(V,E)\) whose skeleton is a tree, with rational additive values \(v_u(e)>0\) exactly when \(u\) is an endpoint of \(e\). An integral orientation \(A\) assigns every edge to one of its endpoints; it is EFX under these additive endpoint valuations. A nonatomic society consists of independent copies of this template, and a measurable law \(q\) is a probability distribution over the finite set of complete orientations, applied independently to copies; every orientation in \(\operatorname{supp}(q)\) must be EFX. A finite description lists its supported orientations and probabilities.

Target

Is deciding whether such a law \(q\) exists, and producing a finite description when it does, solvable in polynomial time or NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For a discrete additive multi-tree instance, deciding whether an EFX orientation exists is NP-complete, even for symmetric instances with a constant number of agents (Afshinmehr et al., 2025, Theorem 3.6).

Conclusion

Under the stated definitions, existence of a law is equivalent to existence of a single EFX orientation. Consequently, the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and the accompanying production problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Randomization and nonatomic replication do not change feasibility.

1. Support-collapse lemma

Let \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{EFX}}(G)\) denote the set of complete EFX orientations of the template \(G\).

Lemma. A law \(q\) satisfying the definition exists if and only if \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{EFX}}(G)\neq\varnothing\).

Proof.

Suppose first that \(q\) exists. Because \(q\) is a probability distribution on a finite set,

\[ \sum_{A}q(A)=1. \]

Hence \(\operatorname{supp}(q)\neq\varnothing\). Choose \(A\in\operatorname{supp}(q)\). The definition of an admissible law requires every orientation in \(\operatorname{supp}(q)\) to be EFX. Therefore \(A\in\mathcal O_{\mathrm{EFX}}(G)\).

Conversely, suppose \(A\in\mathcal O_{\mathrm{EFX}}(G)\). Define the Dirac law

\[ q_A(A)=1,\qquad q_A(B)=0\quad\text{for every }B\neq A. \]

Then \(\operatorname{supp}(q_A)=\{A\}\), so every supported orientation is EFX. Applying this law independently to the copies therefore satisfies all the stated requirements. Its finite description consists of the single pair \((A,1)\). ∎

Thus the law-existence language is exactly

\[ \{G:\text{an admissible law exists}\} = \{G:\text{an EFX orientation exists}\}. \]

In particular, mixtures cannot repair a non-EFX orientation because fairness is imposed separately on every supported orientation, rather than only on an expected allocation.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

An orientation is specified by one endpoint choice for every edge, requiring \(O(|E|)\) bits.

For an orientation \(A\), write \(A_u\) for the bundle assigned to \(u\). Under the standard EFX definition, one must verify, for every \(u,w\in V\) and every \(e\in A_w\),

\[ v_u(A_u)\geq v_u(A_w\setminus\{e\}). \]

Because the valuations are additive, this is the rational inequality

\[ \sum_{f\in A_u}v_u(f) \geq \sum_{f\in A_w}v_u(f)-v_u(e). \]

There are polynomially many such inequalities. Sums and comparisons of binary-encoded rational numbers have polynomial bit complexity: summing at most \(|E|\) input rationals produces numerators and denominators whose bit lengths remain polynomial in the total input length. One can also check in polynomial time that every edge was assigned to one of its endpoints.

Therefore an EFX orientation is a polynomially verifiable certificate. By the support-collapse lemma, it is also a certificate for existence of an admissible law. Hence the law-existence problem lies in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness

Afshinmehr et al., Theorem \(3.6\), prove that deciding whether an additive multi-tree instance admits an EFX orientation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even for symmetric instances with eight agents. Their definitions match the present template restrictions:

  • items are edges and agents are vertices;
  • an edge has positive value exactly for its endpoints;
  • a multi-tree is a multigraph whose skeleton is a tree;
  • orientations assign each edge to an endpoint;
  • the hardness construction uses additive, finitely encoded numerical valuations.

These facts and the theorem are stated in the primary [AAMAS \(2025\) paper](https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p32.pdf).

Given an instance \(I\) from that restricted problem, construct the nonatomic-template instance \(T(I)\) by retaining exactly the same multigraph and valuations. This transformation merely changes the interpretation of the data and is computable in linear time. The support-collapse lemma gives

\[ I\text{ has an EFX orientation} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T(I)\text{ admits an admissible law}. \]

Thus the cited \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard problem reduces to law existence. Combined with membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), law existence is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. The hardness already holds for symmetric templates with a constant number of vertices.

4. Producing the law

Whenever the answer is positive, no large-support representation is necessary. From any EFX orientation \(A\), output

\[ q=\{(A,1)\}. \]

This description has polynomial size.

Conversely, any algorithm that decides feasibility and produces a finite law on positive instances would decide the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete existence problem. Moreover, from its nonempty support one may extract an EFX orientation directly. Therefore this combined decision-and-production task is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and cannot be solved in polynomial time unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
