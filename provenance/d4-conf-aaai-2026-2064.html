The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), fixed opinions \(x_t\), susceptibilities \(\lambda_t\), type-to-type influence rates \(w_{tu}\), a threshold \(\psi\), and \(K\) communities, define \(D_{tu}=\psi-|x_t-x_u|\), \(B_{tu}=\max\{D_{tu},0\}\), and \(Y_{tu}=(\lambda_tD_{tu}-B_{tu})w_{tu}+(\lambda_uD_{ut}-B_{ut})w_{ut}\). Choose shares \(q_{tk}\geq0\), with \(\sum_{k=1}^K q_{tk}=1\), to globally maximize \(\Phi(q)=\frac12\sum_{t,u\in T}\mu_t\mu_uY_{tu}\sum_{k=1}^Kq_{tk}q_{uk}\), and return a maximizing assignment.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational \(\mu_t>0\), with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a fixed rational opinion \(x_t\in[0,1]\) and susceptibility \(\lambda_t>1\). Influence rates \(w_{tu}\geq0\) are rational, \(w_{tt}=0\), and the directed graph with arcs where \(w_{tu}>0\) is strongly connected. The rational confidence threshold satisfies \(0<\psi<1\); the number of available community labels \(K\geq1\) is an input integer.
Define \(D_{tu}=\psi-|x_t-x_u|\), \(B_{tu}=\max\{D_{tu},0\}\), and \(Y_{tu}=(\lambda_tD_{tu}-B_{tu})w_{tu}+(\lambda_uD_{ut}-B_{ut})w_{ut}\). Negative values of \(D_{tu}\) are retained.
An assignment consists of real shares \(q_{tk}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{k=1}^{K}q_{tk}=1\); \(\mu_tq_{tk}\) is the mass of type \(t\) in community \(k\). Empty communities are allowed. The objective is \(\Phi(q)=\frac12\sum_{t,u\in T}\mu_t\mu_uY_{tu}\sum_{k=1}^{K}q_{tk}q_{uk}\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length and \(m=1\) the opinion dimension; state running times in \(m,\tau,K,L\).

Is computing an exact global maximizing assignment for \(\Phi\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,K,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents with indivisible memberships, sequential best responses are claimed to reach a pure Nash equilibrium that also globally maximizes welfare; the stated runtime depends on an unbounded expected iteration count. Result

Computing an exact global maximizing assignment is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, already when \(m=1\), \(K=2\), every susceptibility is \(\lambda_t=2\), and \(\psi=3/4\). This remains true with positive dyadic masses and symmetric influence rates whose graph is strongly connected.

The corresponding decision problem—whether the maximum is at least a given rational threshold—is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Consequently, an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,K,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

For \(K=1\), the assignment is forced. The hardness concerns the nontrivial case \(K=2\).

1. Splitting types does not increase the optimum

The definition gives \(Y_{tu}=Y_{ut}\) and \(Y_{tt}=0\). Put \(c_{tu}=\mu_t\mu_uY_{tu}\). Fixing any ordering of the types, the objective becomes

\(\displaystyle \Phi(q)=\sum_{t<u}c_{tu}\sum_{k=1}^{K}q_{tk}q_{uk}.\)

A pure assignment is a label map \(s:T\to\{1,\ldots,K\}\), represented by \(q_{tk}=\mathbf{1}\{s(t)=k\}\). Its objective is

\(\displaystyle \Phi(s)=\sum_{t<u}c_{tu}\mathbf{1}\{s(t)=s(u)\}.\)

Lemma. The maximum over all real-share assignments equals the maximum over pure assignments.

Proof. Given any feasible \(q\), independently choose a label \(S_t\) for each type, with \(\Pr(S_t=k)=q_{tk}\). For distinct types,

\(\displaystyle \Pr(S_t=S_u)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}q_{tk}q_{uk}.\)

Linearity of expectation therefore gives \(\mathbb{E}[\Phi(S)]=\Phi(q)\). At least one pure assignment has value at least this expectation. Since pure assignments themselves are feasible, the two maxima coincide. This also proves that a global maximum exists. \(\square\)

The absence of diagonal terms is essential: independence is used only for distinct types.

2. Source problem and connectivity

We reduce from unweighted Max-Cut. Its decision problem asks whether a simple undirected graph has a cut containing at least a specified number of edges. This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete by [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer, *Some simplified NP-complete graph problems*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1).

We can require the source graph to be connected. Indeed, if a graph has \(d\) connected components, choose one representative from each and add \(d-1\) edges joining consecutive representatives. The resulting graph \(G'\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{MaxCut}(G')=\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)+d-1.\)

The upper bound follows because only \(d-1\) edges were added. For the lower bound, take a maximum cut in each component. Complementing a component’s two sides preserves its internal cut size. Choose these complements successively so that every added edge crosses the cut. Adjusting the decision threshold by \(d-1\) proves the claimed restriction.

Henceforth let the source graph be connected, with vertices \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\), edge set \(E\), and \(r=|E|\). We may restrict to \(n\geq2\), since smaller instances are trivial. Set \(H=r+1\).

3. Constructing the society

For each source vertex \(i\), introduce three types \(a_i,b_i,d_i\), with opinions

\(x_{a_i}=0,\qquad x_{b_i}=1/2,\qquad x_{d_i}=1.\)

Set \(K=2\), \(\psi=3/4\), and \(\lambda_t=2\) for every type. Thus \(m=1\) and \(\tau=3n\).

To ensure that the masses are dyadic, enumerate the types as \(t_1,\ldots,t_\tau\), and let \(Q\) be the smallest power of two at least \(\tau\). Define

\(\displaystyle \mu_{t_j}=1/Q\quad(j<\tau),\qquad \mu_{t_\tau}=(Q-\tau+1)/Q.\)

All masses are positive, and their sum is one.

Define symmetric influence rates as follows:

  • Within each triple, set \(w_{a_i b_i}=w_{b_i a_i}=2H/(\mu_{a_i}\mu_{b_i})\) and \(w_{b_i d_i}=w_{d_i b_i}=2H/(\mu_{b_i}\mu_{d_i})\).
  • For each source edge \(\{i,j\}\), ordered so that \(i<j\), set \(w_{a_i d_j}=w_{d_j a_i}=1/(\mu_{a_i}\mu_{d_j})\).
  • Set every other rate, including every diagonal rate, to zero.

Computing the objective coefficients

For either internal pair, the opinion distance is \(1/2\). Therefore \(D=1/4\), \(B=1/4\), and \(\lambda D-B=1/4\). Symmetry of the rates gives

\(\displaystyle Y_{tu}=w_{tu}/2,\qquad c_{tu}=H.\)

For a pair encoding a source edge, the opinion distance is \(1\). Thus \(D=-1/4\), \(B=0\), and \(\lambda D-B=-1/2\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle Y_{tu}=-w_{tu},\qquad c_{tu}=-1.\)

All other coefficients vanish. For a pure assignment, the objective is exactly

\(\displaystyle \Phi(s)= H\sum_{i=1}^{n} \bigl( \mathbf{1}\{s(a_i)=s(b_i)\} +\mathbf{1}\{s(b_i)=s(d_i)\} \bigr) -\sum_{\substack{\{i,j\}\in E\\i<j}} \mathbf{1}\{s(a_i)=s(d_j)\}. \)

Checking the input requirements

Each triple is connected by its two internal influence edges. Each source edge connects its two corresponding triples. Since the source graph is connected, the undirected influence graph is connected. Every influence edge occurs in both directions, so the directed graph is strongly connected.

The construction uses only rational, nonnegative rates and has zero diagonal. Also, \(Q<2\tau\), and every nonzero rate has numerator and denominator of \(O(\log n)\) bits: after substituting the masses, its numerator is at most \(2HQ^2\), and its denominator is at most \(Q^2\). A dense encoding therefore has length \(O(n^2\log n)\), and the construction takes polynomial time.

4. Exact correspondence with Max-Cut

Call an assignment coherent if \(s(a_i)=s(b_i)=s(d_i)\) for every source vertex.

Lemma. Every globally maximizing pure assignment is coherent.

Proof. Assigning every type the same label gives value \(2nH-r\).

An incoherent assignment loses at least one of the \(2n\) positive contributions of size \(H\). All remaining terms are nonpositive, so its value is at most \((2n-1)H\). But

\(\displaystyle (2nH-r)-(2n-1)H=H-r=1>0.\)

Thus an incoherent assignment cannot maximize the objective. \(\square\)

For a coherent assignment, let \(z_i\in\{1,2\}\) be the common label of its triple. If \(C(z)\) is the number of source edges crossing these two labels, then

\(\displaystyle \Phi(s)=2nH-\bigl(r-C(z)\bigr)=2nH-r+C(z). \)

Every cut yields such a coherent assignment. Combining this fact with the preceding lemma and Section 1 proves

\(\displaystyle \boxed{\max_q\Phi(q)=2nH-r+\operatorname{MaxCut}(G).}\)

Hence, for any integer cut threshold \(b\),

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\geq b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \max_q\Phi(q)\geq 2nH-r+b. \)

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction proving decision hardness.

Recovering a cut from any exact maximizing assignment

There is no ambiguity caused by a solver returning fractional shares. In this construction, every maximizing real-share assignment is pure and coherent.

To prove this, independently round a maximizing \(q\) as in Section 1. Its expected value equals the global maximum, and every pure outcome has value at most that maximum. Therefore every outcome of positive probability must be maximizing, hence coherent.

In particular, adjacent types within a triple have equal random labels with probability one. Two independent label distributions can have this property only when both are concentrated on the same single label: any distinct labels in their supports would otherwise occur together with positive probability. Applying this to both internal pairs proves the assertion.

Reading the triple labels from an exact maximizing assignment consequently recovers a maximum cut. This establishes hardness of the requested assignment computation itself.

5. Decision complexity and running times

For an arbitrary valid instance, Section 1 ensures that a pure assignment witnesses any attainable threshold. Its certificate consists of \(\tau\) labels, requiring \(O(\tau\log(K+1))\) bits. Its objective can be evaluated exactly using polynomially many rational arithmetic operations, with polynomial bit complexity.

Thus the rational-threshold decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and the reduction proves it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

For an explicit exact upper bound, put \(K_0=\min\{K,\tau\}\). A pure assignment occupies at most \(\tau\) labels, and relabeling preserves its value. Enumerating all \(K_0^\tau\) label maps and evaluating each yields a running time of

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(K_0^\tau\,\tau^2\,\operatorname{poly}(m,L)+\tau K\right),\qquad m=1.\)

The final \(\tau K\) term accounts for writing a dense share matrix; it can be omitted when the output is given as one label per type.

The hardness holds with \(m=1\) and \(K=2\) fixed. Therefore allowing polynomial dependence on these parameters does not remove it.

6. Why best responses do not establish global optimality

[Zhang et al., Theorems 1–3](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/41312/45273) give the potential-game formulation, claim that the resulting equilibrium solves global welfare maximization, and express runtime using an expected iteration count. The potential property supports termination of strict improvement moves in a finite game; it does not imply global maximization.

Here is an explicit counterexample satisfying the finite-agent assumptions.

Take six agents in two triples, with opinions \(0,1/2,1\) in each triple. Set \(K=2\), \(\psi=3/4\), and every susceptibility to \(2\). Give each of the four internal adjacent pairs symmetric influence rate \(4\), and give the pair connecting the opinion-\(0\) agent of the first triple to the opinion-\(1\) agent of the second triple symmetric rate \(1\). All other rates are zero.

The influence graph is strongly connected. The four internal coefficients are \(Y=2\), and the connecting coefficient is \(Y=-1\).

Place all agents in community \(1\). Under the paper’s modified utility \(u_t^\star=\sum_{u\ne t}Y_{tu}\mathbf{1}\{s(t)=s(u)\}\):

  • The two agents incident to the negative edge have utility \(1\).
  • The other two endpoint agents have utility \(2\).
  • The two middle agents have utility \(4\).

Moving alone to the empty second community gives utility \(0\). Thus this assignment is a strict pure Nash equilibrium, even when both labels are available.

Its potential is \(4\cdot2-1=7\). Placing the two whole triples in different communities gives potential \(4\cdot2=8\). The equilibrium therefore fails to maximize welfare.
