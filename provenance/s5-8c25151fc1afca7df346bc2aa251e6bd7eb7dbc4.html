Conclusion

Exact minimum-cost stable allocation is solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\). The algorithm first finds one stable allocation by rational convex quadratic programming, then minimizes cost over an explicitly described rational polyhedron containing exactly all stable allocations.

1. A rational convex potential

Write \(D=D_\pi\), and define

\(H=DA=D+DKD,\qquad b=Dr,\qquad \Phi(x)=\frac12x^\top Hx-b^\top x.\)

Because \(K\) is symmetric, \(H\) is symmetric. The assumed weighted positive semidefiniteness gives

\(z^\top Hz=\langle z,Az\rangle_\pi\geq0\)

for every \(z\in\mathbb R^\tau\). Thus \(H\) is positive semidefinite and \(\Phi\) is convex. Its coefficients are rational, and

\(\nabla\Phi(x)=Hx-b=D(Ax-r).\)

The minimum of \(\Phi\) over \(x\geq0\) exists. Indeed, entrywise nonnegativity of \(K\) implies, for \(x\geq0\),

\(\Phi(x)\geq\frac12\sum_t\pi_tx_t^2-\sum_t\pi_tr_tx_t =\frac12\sum_t\pi_t(x_t-r_t)^2-\frac12\sum_t\pi_tr_t^2.\)

Since every \(\pi_t\) is positive, this lower bound tends to infinity as \(\|x\|\) tends to infinity. Consequently, the nonempty sublevel set \(\{x\geq0:\Phi(x)\leq\Phi(0)\}\) is compact. Continuity therefore guarantees a global minimizer on the nonnegative orthant.

This argument allows targets of either sign.

2. Stability is exactly potential minimization

We prove that \(x\geq0\) is stable and feasible if and only if it minimizes \(\Phi\) over the nonnegative orthant.

Suppose first that \(x\) minimizes \(\Phi\). For each coordinate \(t\), increasing \(x_t\) slightly is feasible, so the corresponding directional derivative satisfies \((\nabla\Phi(x))_t\geq0\). If \(x_t>0\), both increasing and decreasing that coordinate slightly are feasible, forcing \((\nabla\Phi(x))_t=0\).

Since \((\nabla\Phi(x))_t=\pi_t((Ax)_t-r_t)\) and \(\pi_t>0\), these conditions are precisely

\((Ax)_t\geq r_t,\qquad x_t>0\Longrightarrow(Ax)_t=r_t.\)

Conversely, suppose \(x\) is feasible and stable. Put \(g=Hx-b\). Then \(g\geq0\) and \(g^\top x=0\). For every \(y\geq0\), expansion of the quadratic gives

\(\Phi(y)-\Phi(x)=g^\top(y-x)+\frac12(y-x)^\top H(y-x) =g^\top y+\frac12(y-x)^\top H(y-x)\geq0.\)

Both terms are nonnegative, using \(g,y\geq0\) and positive semidefiniteness of \(H\). Hence \(x\) minimizes \(\Phi\).

In particular, a stable allocation always exists.

3. All stable allocations form an explicit polyhedron

Let \(x^0\) be any minimizer of \(\Phi\), and set

\(v=Hx^0,\qquad s=v-b.\)

By the preceding section, \(s\geq0\) and \(s^\top x^0=0\). For any \(x\geq0\),

\(\Phi(x)-\Phi(x^0) =s^\top x+\frac12(x-x^0)^\top H(x-x^0).\)

Both terms on the right are nonnegative. Thus equality holds exactly when

\(s^\top x=0,\qquad (x-x^0)^\top H(x-x^0)=0.\)

For a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix, \(d^\top Hd=0\) is equivalent to \(Hd=0\). To justify this, diagonalize \(H=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)U^\top\), where every \(\lambda_i\geq0\). The sum \(\sum_i\lambda_i(U^\top d)_i^2\) vanishes exactly when every coordinate with positive eigenvalue vanishes, which is exactly the condition \(Hd=0\).

Therefore the complete stable-allocation set is

\(\mathcal S=\{x\in\mathbb R^\tau:x\geq0,\ Hx=v,\ s^\top x=0\}.\)

This is a polyhedron. Once a rational \(x^0\) is obtained, its defining coefficients are rational.

The set is also bounded. If a stable allocation has \(x_t>0\), then entrywise nonnegativity of \(K\) gives

\(r_t=(Ax)_t=x_t+\sum_s\pi_sK_{ts}x_s\geq x_t.\)

If \(x_t=0\), the bound is automatic. Consequently every stable allocation satisfies

\(0\leq x_t\leq\max\{r_t,0\}.\)

Thus \(\mathcal S\) is nonempty and compact. The required minimum is exactly the attained optimum of the linear program

\(\min\{\pi^\top x:x\geq0,\ Hx=v,\ s^\top x=0\}.\)

4. Exact polynomial-time algorithm

We use the classical exact convex-quadratic-programming theorem: for rational symmetric positive semidefinite quadratic coefficients and rational linear constraints, an attained finite minimum and a rational minimizer can be computed in time polynomial in the binary encoding length. This is the theorem of [Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan, *The polynomial solvability of convex quadratic programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189). Its integer-data formulation extends to rational data by clearing denominators with positive multipliers; the resulting encoding length remains polynomial.

Apply it as follows:

  • Form \(H=D+DKD\) and \(b=Dr\).
  • Compute an exact rational minimizer \(x^0\) of \(\Phi(x)\) subject to \(x\geq0\).
  • Compute \(v=Hx^0\) and \(s=v-b\) exactly.
  • Solve the rational linear program above and return its optimizer.

For step 2, all theorem hypotheses have been checked: \(H\) is symmetric positive semidefinite, the constraints are rational and feasible, and the finite minimum is attained. Step 4 is also covered by the theorem, with zero quadratic term; its feasible set is nonempty and compact.

The encoding lengths remain polynomial throughout. Each entry

\(H_{ts}=\pi_t\delta_{ts}+\pi_tK_{ts}\pi_s,\qquad b_t=\pi_tr_t\)

has bit length polynomial in \(L\), and there are only \(\tau^2+\tau\) such entries. The polynomial-time exact algorithm outputs \(x^0\) with polynomial encoding length. Matrix-vector multiplication then produces \(v\) and \(s\) with polynomial encoding length. The final linear program has \(\tau\) variables, \(\tau\) nonnegativity inequalities, and \(\tau+1\) equalities, all of polynomial encoding length.

Its output is therefore an exact rational minimum-cost stable allocation, computable in \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\) time.

The finite-agent theorem is not needed for this argument. Weighted symmetry directly supplies the rational positive semidefinite matrix \(H=DA\), so the algorithm works on the given support without expanding the masses into individual agents.
