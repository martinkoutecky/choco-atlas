The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), disjoint labels and interval or dominance constraints, a reference committee \(X\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over a listed support of complete rankings, is there a feasible committee \(Y\) of size \(k\) whose SNTV or Bloc score under \(\mu\) is at least the score of \(X\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq1\) candidates, let \(1\leq K\leq m\), and let \(X\subseteq C\) be a reference committee with \(|X|=K\); \(X\) need not satisfy the constraints.
There are \(p\) explicitly listed, pairwise disjoint labels \(\lambda_i\subseteq C\), which need not cover \(C\). Each of the \(q\) constraints is either an interval constraint \(a\leq|Y\cap\lambda_i|\leq b\), with integer endpoints \(0\leq a\leq b\leq m\), or a dominance constraint \(|Y\cap\lambda_i|\geq|Y\cap\lambda_j|\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings of \(C\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
Fix SNTV or Bloc, with its usual scoring function \(\gamma\); for Bloc the approval cutoff is \(K\). Define \(s_\mu(Z)=\sum_{c\in Z}\sum_t\mu_t\gamma(\operatorname{pos}_t(c))\). Committees remain subsets of candidates, and score ties are accepted.
The decision problem asks whether some \(Y\subseteq C\) with \(|Y|=K\) satisfies every constraint and \(s_\mu(Y)\geq s_\mu(X)\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L,K,p,q\).

For each fixed rule, SNTV and Bloc, is this rational-mass constrained committee existence problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

With an explicitly listed finite electorate, constrained committee existence with disjoint labels is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for SNTV and for Bloc; Bloc remains \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete with a constant number of voters.

Result

For each fixed rule, SNTV and Bloc, the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. For Bloc, hardness persists when the support size \(\tau\) is bounded by an absolute constant. These conclusions hold even if every mass is dyadic.

Exact verification in polynomial time

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), with positive integers \(a_t,b_t\) encoded in binary. Define
\( Q=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t \)
and
\( A_t=a_t(Q/b_t). \)
Then \(A_t\) is a positive integer and, because the masses sum to one, \(\sum_t A_t=Q\).

For each candidate, compute the integer weight
\( w(c)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}A_t\gamma(\operatorname{pos}_t(c)). \)
Both rules have scores in \(\{0,1\}\), so \(0\leq w(c)\leq Q\). For every committee \(Z\),
\( s_\mu(Z)=Q^{-1}\sum_{c\in Z}w(c). \)
Consequently, the required comparison is exactly
\( \sum_{c\in Y}w(c)\geq\sum_{c\in X}w(c). \)
This includes equality, as required.

The binary length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the binary lengths of the input denominators, hence is \(O(L)\). Committee totals have at most \(O(L+\log K)=O(L)\) bits. Thus ordinary integer arithmetic suffices; there is no need to expand the masses into an electorate.

A certificate consists of the selected candidates. Verify that they form a subset of \(C\) of size \(K\), compute every label count, check the \(q\) constraints, and compare the integer scores. Because the labels are pairwise disjoint, their total listed membership is at most \(m\).

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, a conservative bound for the complete verification is
\( O\bigl((m\tau+m+p+q+K)L^2\bigr) \)
bit operations. The certificate has polynomial length, proving membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) for both rules.

Polynomial transfer of finite-electorate hardness

We use the finite-electorate hardness results supplied in the question. Their reference-committee formulation and the hardness statements appear in Definition 2.7 and Theorem 3.1 of [Ianovski, *Electing a committee with dominance constraints*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.05909).

The following transformation proves that those results apply to the present support representation, even with dyadic masses.

Consider a source instance with \(n\geq1\) explicitly listed voters. Let its distinct rankings be \(r_1,\ldots,r_h\), with positive multiplicities \(n_1,\ldots,n_h\), so \(\sum_{t=1}^{h}n_t=n\). Denote its unnormalized committee score by \(S(Z)\).

Choose the smallest power of two \(D\) strictly larger than \(n\). Thus \(n<D\leq2n\).

Candidates and constraints. Add a set \(F\) of exactly \(K\) new candidates. Add the label \(F\) and the interval constraint
\( 0\leq |Y\cap F|\leq0. \)
Retain every original label and constraint, and retain the original reference committee \(X\) and committee size \(K\).

The labels remain pairwise disjoint. All constraint endpoints remain within the permitted range for the enlarged candidate set. Every feasible committee avoids \(F\), so feasible committees correspond exactly to those of the source instance.

Support and masses. Extend each \(r_t\) by placing all candidates of \(F\) below all original candidates, and assign the extended ranking mass \(n_t/D\). Add one ranking \(\rho\) placing all candidates of \(F\) above all original candidates, with mass
\( (D-n)/D. \)

All masses are positive dyadic rationals, and their sum is
\( \sum_{t=1}^{h}n_t/D+(D-n)/D=1. \)
The extended original rankings remain distinct. Moreover, \(\rho\) starts with an auxiliary candidate, whereas every extended original ranking starts with an original candidate. Thus the support satisfies the distinctness requirement and has size \(\tau=h+1\leq n+1\).

Scores under SNTV. Each extended original ranking gives its point to the same original candidate as before. Ranking \(\rho\) gives its point to a candidate in \(F\). Hence, for every committee \(Z\) of original candidates,
\( s_\mu(Z)=S(Z)/D. \)

Scores under Bloc. The cutoff remains \(K\). Since the original candidate set has at least \(K\) members, appending \(F\) does not change the original rankings’ approved candidates. Ranking \(\rho\) approves precisely the \(K\) candidates in \(F\). Therefore, again,
\( s_\mu(Z)=S(Z)/D \)
for every committee \(Z\) of original candidates.

Every feasible target committee consists entirely of original candidates, as does \(X\). For either rule,
\( s_\mu(Y)\geq s_\mu(X) \)
holds exactly when
\( S(Y)\geq S(X). \)
Together with the correspondence of feasible committees, this proves equivalence of the source and target instances.

The construction adds only \(K\) candidates, one label, one constraint, and one support ranking. Each mass uses \(O(\log n)\) bits. Grouping explicitly listed rankings and producing the output takes polynomial time in the source’s binary input length. Hence this is a polynomial many-one reduction.

Applying the supplied hardness theorem separately to SNTV and Bloc establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for both. If the source Bloc instance has at most a constant \(c\) voters, the target has \(\tau\leq c+1\), proving hardness with constant support.

Running times and conclusion

For either rule, exact certificate verification takes
\( O\bigl((m\tau+m+p+q+K)L^2\bigr) \)
bit operations.

For completeness, preprocessing the integer scores once and then enumerating all size-\(K\) subsets gives a deterministic bound
\( O\!\left(\left[m\tau+\binom{m}{K}(m+p+q+K)\right]L^2\right). \)
This bound is polynomial for each fixed \(K\), but the unrestricted problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for both rules.
