Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\) on the stated input family. The exact value is

\(\displaystyle V=\begin{cases} 0,&\gamma=0\text{ and }(a=1\text{ or }b=0),\\ R_0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases}\)

One minimax rule is

\(\displaystyle f^*(q)=\begin{cases} \mathbf 1_{\{q=1\}},&\gamma=0,\ a=1,\\ \mathbf 1_{\{q>0\}},&\gamma=0,\ a<1,\ b=0,\\ f_{\mathrm{tr}}(q),&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases}\)

Thus the proposed rule and value are correct throughout \(\gamma>0\), and also when \(\gamma=0,\ a<1,\ b>0\). The permitted boundary cases matter: for example, \(\mu=1/2,\ a=1,\ b=1/2,\ \gamma=0\) satisfy every hypothesis, but \(V=0<R_0=1/4\).

The finite-population result appears as Theorem 4.2 in [the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08222v2). The following proof establishes the stated Borel-law model directly.

1. Notation and consequences of the hypotheses

Set

\(\displaystyle h=1-\gamma,\qquad d=1-2\gamma,\qquad C=\mu(1-a)+\bar\mu b.\)

Then \(d>0\) and \(R_0=hC/d\). Define the positive quantities

\(\displaystyle D=\mu a-\bar\mu b,\qquad E=\bar\mu(1-b)-\mu(1-a).\)

The four displayed bounds on \(\gamma\) are equivalent to

\(\displaystyle ha\ge\gamma,\qquad hb\le d,\qquad hD\ge\mu\gamma,\qquad hE\ge\bar\mu\gamma.\)

Indeed, the last two follow by multiplying the bounds \(\gamma\le D/(\mu+D)\) and \(\gamma\le E/(\bar\mu+E)\) by their positive denominators.

Consequently, the numbers

\(\displaystyle p_1=\frac{ha-\gamma}{d},\qquad p_0=\frac{hb}{d}\)

belong to \([0,1]\). They satisfy

\(\displaystyle \mu p_1-\bar\mu p_0=\frac{hD-\mu\gamma}{d}\ge0,\)

\(\displaystyle \bar\mu(1-p_0)-\mu(1-p_1)=\frac{hE-\bar\mu\gamma}{d}\ge0.\)

Also,

\(\displaystyle 1-p_1=\frac{h(1-a)}d,\qquad \mu(1-p_1)+\bar\mu p_0=R_0.\)

2. Upper bound for every admissible Borel law

The truncated mean is continuous and nondecreasing. For every truthful mass \(x\in[0,h]\) and adversarial mass \(u\in[0,\gamma]\),

\(\displaystyle 1-f_{\mathrm{tr}}(x+u)\le1-f_{\mathrm{tr}}(x)\le\frac{h-x}{d},\)

\(\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{tr}}(x+u)\le f_{\mathrm{tr}}(x+\gamma)\le\frac{x}{d}.\)

These inequalities hold pointwise, so they also hold after integration against any admissible law and Borel kernel. Using the prescribed conditional means gives

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_{\theta,\sigma}[|f_{\mathrm{tr}}(Q)-\omega|] \le\frac{\mu(h-ha)+\bar\mu hb}{d} =R_0. \)

The benchmark loss is nonnegative. Therefore

\(\displaystyle R(f_{\mathrm{tr}})\le R_0,\qquad V\le R_0.\)

This argument covers arbitrary laws, including nonatomic laws.

3. Matching lower bound when \(\gamma>0\)

Write \(\delta_x\) for unit probability mass at \(x\).

3.1. The case \(\gamma\ne1/3\)

Choose an admissible law with conditional distributions

\(\displaystyle \mathcal L(Q_T\mid\omega=1)=(1-p_1)\delta_\gamma+p_1\delta_h, \)

\(\displaystyle \mathcal L(Q_T\mid\omega=0)=(1-p_0)\delta_0+p_0\delta_d. \)

Its conditional means are exactly

\(\displaystyle \gamma+dp_1=ha,\qquad dp_0=hb.\)

Because \(0<\gamma<1/2\) and \(\gamma\ne1/3\), the sets \(\{\gamma,h\}\) and \(\{0,d\}\) are disjoint. In particular, their only possible collision in this range, \(\gamma=d\), would require \(\gamma=1/3\). Thus \(Q_T\) determines the state, and the benchmark loss is zero.

Let adversaries choose \(Q_A=0\) in state \(1\) and \(Q_A=\gamma\) in state \(0\). This is a deterministic Borel kernel. The observed report lies in \(\{\gamma,h\}\), with conditional probability \(p_1\) of \(h\) in state \(1\) and \(p_0\) in state \(0\).

For any Borel rule \(f\), its regret against this construction equals

\(\displaystyle R_0+ \bigl[\bar\mu(1-p_0)-\mu(1-p_1)\bigr]f(\gamma) +\bigl[\mu p_1-\bar\mu p_0\bigr](1-f(h)). \)

Both coefficients are nonnegative by Section 1. Hence \(R(f)\ge R_0\) for every rule. Together with the upper bound, this proves \(V=R_0\).

3.2. The collision case \(\gamma=1/3\)

Here \(d=\gamma\), so the preceding truthful supports can overlap.

If \(b=0\), the state-\(0\) distribution is concentrated at \(0\); the preceding construction still has zero benchmark loss and proves the result.

Suppose \(b>0\). Choose any

\(\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\min\{\gamma,hb\}.\)

Keep the state-\(1\) distribution unchanged, and replace the state-\(0\) distribution by

\(\displaystyle (1-p_{0,\varepsilon})\delta_\varepsilon+ p_{0,\varepsilon}\delta_{d+\varepsilon}, \qquad p_{0,\varepsilon}=\frac{hb-\varepsilon}{d}. \)

These are valid probabilities, and the conditional mean remains

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon+dp_{0,\varepsilon}=hb.\)

Moreover,

\(\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\gamma=d<d+\varepsilon<h.\)

Thus the conditional truthful supports are disjoint, and the benchmark loss is zero.

Choose \(Q_A=0\) in state \(1\) and \(Q_A=\gamma-\varepsilon\) in state \(0\). Both states again produce observed reports only at \(\gamma,h\).

Since \(p_{0,\varepsilon}\le p_0\), the two posterior inequalities from Section 1 remain valid after replacing \(p_0\) by \(p_{0,\varepsilon}\). Therefore every rule has regret at least

\(\displaystyle \mu(1-p_1)+\bar\mu p_{0,\varepsilon} =R_0-\frac{\bar\mu\varepsilon}{d}. \)

Taking \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) yields \(R(f)\ge R_0\) for every Borel rule. This limit concerns only the numerical lower bound; it requires no continuity of \(f\).

Hence \(V=R_0\) for every permitted \(\gamma>0\).

4. The case \(\gamma=0\)

Now \(Q=Q_T\). Because the benchmark minimizes conditional expected absolute loss using this same observation, every rule has nonnegative regret against every admissible law. In particular, \(V\ge0\).

4.1. Boundary cases: \(a=1\) or \(b=0\)

Suppose \(a=1\). Since \(Q_T\in[0,1]\) and its conditional mean in state \(1\) is \(1\), it equals \(1\) almost surely in that state.

Let \(t=\Pr[Q_T=1\mid\omega=0]\). Then \(t\le b\), because \(Q_T\ge\mathbf1_{\{Q_T=1\}}\). At report \(1\), the state masses satisfy

\(\displaystyle \mu>\bar\mu b\ge\bar\mu t.\)

The benchmark therefore selects \(1\) at report \(1\), and selects \(0\) almost surely elsewhere. The rule \(\mathbf1_{\{q=1\}}\) matches it for every admissible law, proving \(V=0\).

Suppose instead \(b=0\). Then \(Q_T=0\) almost surely in state \(0\). Let \(s=\Pr[Q_T=0\mid\omega=1]\). The inequality \(1-Q_T\ge\mathbf1_{\{Q_T=0\}}\) gives \(s\le1-a\), and hence

\(\displaystyle \mu s\le\mu(1-a)<\bar\mu.\)

Thus the benchmark selects \(0\) at report \(0\), and selects \(1\) almost surely elsewhere. The rule \(\mathbf1_{\{q>0\}}\) matches it universally, again proving \(V=0\).

4.2. Interior cases: \(a<1\) and \(b>0\)

The informative-signal inequalities also imply \(a>0\) and \(b<1\). Choose

\(\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\min\{a,1-a,b,1-b,D,E,1/2\}, \)

and define

\(\displaystyle p_a=\frac{a-\varepsilon}{1-2\varepsilon},\qquad p_b=\frac{b-\varepsilon}{1-2\varepsilon}.\)

Both probabilities lie in \((0,1)\). Consider two separate admissible laws:

| Law | Conditional law given \(\omega=1\) | Conditional law given \(\omega=0\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(\theta_A\) | \((1-a)\delta_0+a\delta_1\) | \((1-p_b)\delta_\varepsilon+p_b\delta_{1-\varepsilon}\) |
| \(\theta_B\) | \((1-p_a)\delta_\varepsilon+p_a\delta_{1-\varepsilon}\) | \((1-b)\delta_0+b\delta_1\) |

Their means are correct because \(\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)p_c=c\) for \(c=a,b\). Each law has disjoint conditional supports, so each benchmark has zero loss.

The weighted state masses at the two interior reports obey

\(\displaystyle \mu p_a-\bar\mu p_b =\frac{D-(2\mu-1)\varepsilon}{1-2\varepsilon}>0, \)

\(\displaystyle \bar\mu(1-p_b)-\mu(1-p_a) =\frac{E+(2\mu-1)\varepsilon}{1-2\varepsilon}>0. \)

Positivity follows from \(|2\mu-1|<1\) and \(\varepsilon<\min\{D,E\}\).

For nonnegative masses \(A,B\) and any prediction \(y\in[0,1]\),

\(\displaystyle A(1-y)+By\ge\min\{A,B\}.\)

Apply this inequality at each of the four reports to the average of the two losses. The preceding inequalities identify state \(0\) as the majority at \(0,\varepsilon\), and state \(1\) as the majority at \(1-\varepsilon,1\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle R(f)\ge \frac{R(f,\theta_A)+R(f,\theta_B)}2 \ge \frac C2+\frac{\mu(1-p_a)+\bar\mu p_b}{2} =\frac C2+\frac{C-\varepsilon}{2(1-2\varepsilon)}. \)

Here the average is taken over regrets against two separate laws, each with its own zero-loss benchmark.

Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves \(R(f)\ge C\) for every Borel rule. Section 2 gives the matching upper bound. Since \(R_0=C\) when \(\gamma=0\), this proves \(V=R_0\) in the interior cases.

5. When the truncated mean fails

In fact, \(R(f_{\mathrm{tr}})=R_0\) throughout the promised family.

Only the nonadversarial boundary cases remain to verify this statement. There, choose the conditional truthful reports deterministically as \(Q_T=a\) in state \(1\) and \(Q_T=b\) in state \(0\). The reports are distinct: \(a=1>b\), or \(a>b=0\). The benchmark consequently has zero loss, while \(f_{\mathrm{tr}}(q)=q\) has loss \(C=R_0\).

Thus \(f_{\mathrm{tr}}\) fails to be minimax precisely when

\(\displaystyle \gamma=0,\quad (a=1,\ b>0)\ \text{or}\ (a<1,\ b=0).\)

If \(a=1,b=0\), both \(V\) and \(R_0\) are zero.

6. Exact decision algorithm and running time

Compute \(V\) using the proved piecewise formula, then compare it with \(r\) by exact rational arithmetic.

The computation uses a constant number of arithmetic operations on integers of \(O(L)\) bits. With schoolbook arithmetic, validation, value computation, and decision take \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. Here \(m=2\), while \(\tau=1\) when \(\gamma=0\) and \(\tau=2\) otherwise; equivalently, a bound in the requested parameters is \(O((m+\tau)L^2)\) for this promised family.

The minimax rule has an \(O(L)\)-bit symbolic description. Evaluation at a rational report of bit length \(B\) takes \(O((L+B)^2)\) bit operations.
