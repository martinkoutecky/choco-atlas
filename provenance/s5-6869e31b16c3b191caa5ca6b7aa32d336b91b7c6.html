The continuous mirror question, as graded

Fix rational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). Given a finite support \(T\) of valuation types, positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and an explicitly encoded piecewise-constant probability measure \(\nu_t\) for each type, together with a cut bound \(K\), decide whether the interval \([0,1]\) can be partitioned into alternating \(+\) and \(-\) intervals using at most \(K\) cuts so that \(\nu_t(A_+)=\alpha\) for every \(t\in T\). Equivalently, compute the minimum number of cuts.

Reduction to a support instance

Fix \(\alpha\). Let a finite-agent cut-bound instance consist of probability valuations \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n\) and an integer budget \(K\). Its feasibility requirement is

\(\lambda_i(A_+)=\alpha\) for every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\).

First remove duplicate valuations. This preserves feasibility because repeated equalities impose the same constraint. Equality of two encoded step functions is decidable in polynomial time: refine their breakpoint lists jointly and compare their rational heights on every resulting interval. Endpoint values do not affect their measures.

Let the distinct remaining valuations be \(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_\tau\), where \(1\leq\tau\leq n\). These become the support types.

To assign positive masses, put \(h=\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil\) and define

\(\mu_t=2^{-h}\) for \(1\leq t<\tau\), and \(\mu_\tau=(2^h-\tau+1)2^{-h}\).

Every mass is positive, since \(2^h\geq\tau\), and

\(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=((\tau-1)+(2^h-\tau+1))2^{-h}=1\).

These masses are even dyadic rationals. Their total encoding length is \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\). Copy the cut budget \(K\) unchanged.

The valuation encodings are copied from the finite-agent instance. If a hardness construction is initially presented on an interval \([0,H]\), its conversion to \([0,1]\) preserves the required encoding: a density \(f\) becomes \(g(x)=Hf(Hx)\). Indeed, substitution gives

\(\int_B g(x)\,dx=\int_{HB}f(y)\,dy\).

Thus all valuation equalities and the number of cuts are preserved. Rational breakpoints and heights remain rational, with polynomial encoding length.

Correctness and cut conventions

For every candidate positive set \(A_+\),

\(\lambda_i(A_+)=\alpha\) for all original agents if and only if \(\nu_t(A_+)=\alpha\) for all support types.

The forward implication follows because each support valuation occurs among the original agents. For the reverse implication, every original valuation equals one of the retained support valuations. The masses play no role in either implication because feasibility is required separately for every type.

The alternating-label convention also preserves cut-bound feasibility. In a partition with arbitrary interval labels, delete each boundary between adjacent intervals bearing the same label. The remaining labels alternate, the positive set is unchanged up to endpoints, and the number of cuts cannot increase. Conversely, an alternating partition is an admissible labeled partition in the finite-agent formulation.

Endpoint cuts and zero-length pieces can likewise be removed. All measures are atomless, so endpoint assignments do not affect feasibility. Consequently, the resulting partition uses distinct interior cuts and satisfies exactly the convention in the question.

The reduction therefore preserves yes-instances and no-instances, preserves \(K\), and has polynomial running time and output length. Composing it with the cited finite-agent reduction proves the claimed hardness. A running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
