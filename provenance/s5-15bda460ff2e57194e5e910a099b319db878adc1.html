Theorem 3 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given worker types \(\mathcal A=\{A_1,\ldots,A_p\}\) and opening types \(\mathcal B=\{B_1,\ldots,B_q\}\), positive rational masses \(\mu_i,\nu_j\) summing to \(1\) on each side, eligibility edges \(E\), strict rankings over eligible opposite-side types with \(\bot\) last, and weights \(w_{ij}\in\{1,2\}\), find a maximum-weight feasible mass flow \(x\). The flow has \(x_{ij},x_{i\bot},x_{\bot j}\ge0\), \(\sum_{j:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}+x_{i\bot}=\mu_i\), and \(\sum_{i:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}+x_{\bot j}=\nu_j\); its objective is \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}\). Each unit of mass receives one counterpart type or is unmatched, while the mass assigned to each option may be fractional. For a type \(A_i\), let \(\sigma_i(k,\ell)\) be \(1\), \(-1\), or \(0\) according as it prefers \(k\) to \(\ell\), prefers \(\ell\) to \(k\), or is indifferent, and set \(v_i(k\mid x)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal B\cup\{\bot\}}(x_{i\ell}/\mu_i)\sigma_i(k,\ell)\); define \(v_j(k\mid x)\) analogously for each \(B_j\). Require every feasible challenger flow \(y\) to have margin \(D(y,x)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\sum_{j\in\mathcal B\cup\{\bot\}}y_{ij}v_i(j\mid x)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}\sum_{i\in\mathcal A\cup\{\bot\}}y_{ij}v_j(i\mid x)\le0\). Masses are encoded in binary.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are worker types \(\mathcal A=\{A_1,\ldots,A_p\}\) and opening types \(\mathcal B=\{B_1,\ldots,B_q\}\), with \(p,q\ge1\). A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau=p+q\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(\sum_i\mu_i=\sum_j\nu_j=1\). Eligibility is a bipartite edge set \(E\), with \(m=|E|\), and each type strictly ranks its eligible opposite-side types above the unmatched option \(\bot\). Edge weights satisfy \(w_{ij}\in\{1,2\}\).
A feasible flow has nonnegative real coordinates \(x_{ij},x_{i\bot},x_{\bot j}\), with \(x_{ij}=0\) outside \(E\), satisfying \(\sum_jx_{ij}+x_{i\bot}=\mu_i\) and \(\sum_ix_{ij}+x_{\bot j}=\nu_j\). Mass may split among options; unmatched mass contributes zero objective weight.
Let \(S_i=\{j:(i,j)\in E\}\cup\{\bot\}\) and \(T_j=\{i:(i,j)\in E\}\cup\{\bot\}\). For each type \(u\), let \(\sigma_u(k,\ell)\) equal \(1\) if it prefers \(k\) to \(\ell\), \(-1\) for the reverse preference, and \(0\) if \(k=\ell\). Define \(v_{A_i}(k\mid x)=\mu_i^{-1}\sum_{\ell\in S_i}x_{i\ell}\sigma_{A_i}(k,\ell)\) and \(v_{B_j}(k\mid x)=\nu_j^{-1}\sum_{\ell\in T_j}x_{\ell j}\sigma_{B_j}(k,\ell)\).
Call \(x\) popular if every feasible flow \(y\) satisfies \(D(y,x)=\sum_i\sum_{k\in S_i}y_{ik}v_{A_i}(k\mid x)+\sum_j\sum_{k\in T_j}y_{kj}v_{B_j}(k\mid x)\le0\). Both sides vote by mass, including unmatched mass, and a zero margin is permitted.
Given also a binary rational threshold \(K\), the decision problem asks whether a popular feasible flow \(x\) satisfies \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}\ge K\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including masses, rankings, eligibility, weights, and \(K\).

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete bipartite matchings with strict preferences, maximum-weight popular matching is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard even with edge weights in \(\{1,2\}\). [Kavitha](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07440)
Maximum-weight popular mixed matchings in the unit-capacity model are polynomial-time computable; two-sided popular fractional matchings are also efficiently computable. These results concern unit capacities, rather than the arbitrary normalized masses specified here. [Kavitha](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2019.4), [Kavitha–Mestre–Nasre](https://cse.iitm.ac.in/~meghana/papers/TCS11-2.pdf)

Result

The decision problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). For arbitrary positive rational masses, popularity has an exact characterization by a polynomial-size system of rational linear inequalities.

1. The challenger’s margin is linear

Let \(P\) denote the set of feasible flows, retaining only the \(m\) eligible edge coordinates and the \(\tau\) unmatched coordinates. This set is nonempty: assigning every type entirely to \(\bot\) is feasible. It is compact, since its defining constraints are closed and every coordinate is bounded by its incident type’s mass.

For a fixed \(x\in P\), define

\(c_{ij}(x)=v_{A_i}(j\mid x)+v_{B_j}(i\mid x)\quad ((i,j)\in E),\)

\(c_{i\bot}(x)=v_{A_i}(\bot\mid x),\qquad c_{\bot j}(x)=v_{B_j}(\bot\mid x).\)

Grouping the two endpoint contributions of each eligible edge gives

\(D(y,x)=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}c_{ij}(x)y_{ij}+\sum_i c_{i\bot}(x)y_{i\bot}+\sum_j c_{\bot j}(x)y_{\bot j}.\)

Every coefficient \(c(x)\) is a linear function of \(x\): the denominators \(\mu_i,\nu_j\) are fixed positive input numbers.

We also have the crucial identity

\(D(x,x)=0.\)

Indeed, for any type \(u\), write \(h_u>0\) for its mass and \(x_{uk}\) for its option coordinates. Its contribution to \(D(x,x)\) is

\(\frac{1}{h_u}\sum_{k,\ell}x_{uk}x_{u\ell}\sigma_u(k,\ell)=0.\)

The diagonal terms vanish, while the terms indexed by \((k,\ell)\) and \((\ell,k)\) cancel because \(\sigma_u(k,\ell)=-\sigma_u(\ell,k)\). Summing over all types proves the identity, including unmatched options.

Consequently, if

\(M(x)=\max_{y\in P}D(y,x),\)

then \(M(x)\ge 0\), by choosing \(y=x\). Thus \(x\) is popular exactly when \(M(x)=0\).

2. An exact dual characterization of popularity

For fixed \(x\), the maximization defining \(M(x)\) is a linear program. Introduce unrestricted real dual variables \(a_i\) for the worker conservation equalities and \(b_j\) for the opening conservation equalities. Its dual minimizes

\(\sum_i\mu_i a_i+\sum_j\nu_j b_j\)

subject to

\(a_i+b_j\ge c_{ij}(x)\quad ((i,j)\in E),\)

\(a_i\ge c_{i\bot}(x)\quad (1\le i\le p),\)

\(b_j\ge c_{\bot j}(x)\quad (1\le j\le q).\)

These constraints follow directly from the conservation equations: an eligible edge coordinate appears in both endpoint equations, whereas an unmatched coordinate appears in only its own type’s equation.

The primal is feasible and has a finite attained optimum, because \(P\) is nonempty and compact. Linear-programming strong duality therefore supplies a dual optimum with the same value.

It follows that \(x\in P\) is popular if and only if there exist \(a,b\) satisfying the displayed dual constraints and

\(\sum_i\mu_i a_i+\sum_j\nu_j b_j\le 0.\)

For completeness, both implications are explicit:

  • If \(x\) is popular, then \(M(x)=0\). An optimal dual solution therefore satisfies all dual constraints and has objective value zero.
  • Conversely, suppose these constraints hold. For every \(y\in P\), multiplying each dual inequality by its nonnegative coordinate of \(y\), and then summing, gives

\(D(y,x)\le\sum_{(i,j)\in E}(a_i+b_j)y_{ij}+\sum_i a_i y_{i\bot}+\sum_j b_j y_{\bot j}.\)

Conservation of \(y\) makes the right-hand side equal to \(\sum_i\mu_i a_i+\sum_j\nu_j b_j\le0\). Hence \(x\) is popular.

This characterization contains only linear constraints in the joint variables \(x,a,b\).

3. The decision algorithm and its encoding size

Construct the following linear feasibility system:

  • The original nonnegativity and conservation constraints for \(x\).
  • The dual inequalities from Section 2, with each \(c(x)\) expanded using the given rankings and masses.
  • The inequality \(\sum_i\mu_i a_i+\sum_j\nu_j b_j\le0\).
  • The threshold inequality \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}w_{ij}x_{ij}\ge K\).

By Section 2, this system is feasible exactly when the answer to the original decision problem is yes.

There are \(m+\tau\) flow variables and \(\tau\) dual variables, for a total of \(m+2\tau\). The number of constraints is \(O(m+\tau)\). Unrestricted dual variables may, if required by the solver, be represented as differences of two nonnegative variables.

To verify the coefficient encoding, the eligible-edge expressions are explicitly

\(c_{ij}(x)=\mu_i^{-1}\sum_{\ell\in S_i}x_{i\ell}\sigma_{A_i}(j,\ell)+\nu_j^{-1}\sum_{k\in T_j}x_{kj}\sigma_{B_j}(i,k).\)

Because every eligible option ranks above \(\bot\), the unmatched expressions are

\(c_{i\bot}(x)=-\mu_i^{-1}\sum_{j:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij},\)

\(c_{\bot j}(x)=-\nu_j^{-1}\sum_{i:(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}.\)

All ranking signs belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). If a positive input mass is represented as \(r/s\), its reciprocal is \(s/r\), with the same order of binary encoding length. Thus even an exponentially small mass causes only a polynomial increase in coefficient bit length. Every coefficient in the constructed system has \(O(L)\) bits.

The system has a matrix with \(O((m+\tau)^2)\) entries and hence total encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). It can also be constructed in polynomial time by recording each type’s ranks and expanding the indicated sums.

The standard rational linear-programming theorem decides feasibility exactly in time polynomial in the system’s binary encoding length, including systems with equalities and lower-dimensional feasible sets. Its hypotheses hold here because all coefficients are explicitly represented rationals of polynomial total length. See [Khachiyan, *A polynomial algorithm in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319). Therefore the original decision problem is polynomial-time solvable, including when the optimum equals \(K\).

4. Relation to discrete popular matchings

The cited hardness result concerns flows constrained to represent discrete matchings. [Kavitha’s result](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07440) establishes hardness for that discrete optimization problem even with weights in \(\{1,2\}\).

Here, both candidates and challengers range over the continuous flow polytope \(P\). Linear-programming duality therefore characterizes popularity exactly, and fixed reciprocal masses preserve linearity. No expansion into unit-capacity copies is required.

The proof also works for arbitrary rational edge weights; the restriction \(w_{ij}\in\{1,2\}\) is unnecessary for tractability.
