The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given item categories with per-capita supplies \(b_i\) and bidder types \(t\) with masses \(\mu_t\), bundles \(S_t\), values \(v_t\), and cohort-withdrawal probabilities \(p_t\), compute an initial allocation and contingent repairs maximizing expected social welfare when at most one type cohort withdraws.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) item categories with positive per-capita supplies \(b_i\), and \(\tau\geq2\) known single-minded types with nonempty bundles \(S_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and values \(v_t>0\). Serving one unit of type mass consumes one unit of each category in its bundle; mass may be split between served and unserved agents.
All numerical inputs are binary rationals; \(L\) is their total encoding length together with the bundle descriptions. Exogenous probabilities satisfy \(0\leq p_t<1/2\) and \(\sum_t p_t\leq1\): event \(k\), of probability \(p_k\), removes the entire type cohort \(k\); otherwise no cohort withdraws, and no further withdrawal occurs.
An allocation specifies initially served masses \(0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t\) and additional served masses \(r_{kt}\geq0\) following event \(k\), with \(r_{kk}=0\) and \(x_t+r_{kt}\leq\mu_t\) for \(t\neq k\). Nonwithdrawn initial recipients retain their bundles; unused supplies may be discarded, and there are no repair costs or minimum-welfare requirements.
Feasibility requires \(\sum_{t:i\in S_t}x_t\leq b_i\) and \(\sum_{t\neq k:i\in S_t}(x_t+r_{kt})\leq b_i\) for every category \(i\) and event \(k\).
Expected welfare is \(W(x,r)=\sum_t(1-p_t)v_tx_t+\sum_k p_k\sum_{t\neq k}v_tr_{kt}\); let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be its maximum over feasible allocations and repairs.

Does this continuous allocation problem admit an \((m+1)\)-approximation in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is achieving this approximation ratio NP-hard?

For discrete single-minded bidders with unit item supplies and irrevocable assignments, a polynomial-time \((m+1)\)-approximation for expected welfare under single-bid withdrawal is reported by [Holland and O’Sullivan](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/07/Papers/212.pdf).
No result settling this approximation question for the specified continuous cohort-withdrawal model was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one, interpreting supplies and welfare per capita.
  • Specified divisible served mass, with each served agent receiving its entire requested bundle.
  • Required nonempty bundles and positive values, following the intended single-minded domain; retained at least two types to match the fallback repair selection.
  • Took per-capita supplies to be positive binary rationals and made all other numerical input encodings explicit.
  • Retained the withdrawal bound p_t<1/2 and made cohort events mutually exclusive, requiring their probabilities to sum to at most one.
  • Interpreted cohort withdrawal as removal of the entire type, including initially unserved members; contingent allocations are defined for every such event.
  • Carried over irrevocability, free disposal, additional allocations only to previously unserved surviving mass, and the absence of repair costs or welfare floors.
  • Used the approximation ratio asserted by the anchor rather than the exact optimization target suggested by the graded question.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is exactly solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Consequently, it admits an \((m+1)\)-approximation.

Exact linear-programming formulation. Let \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), define \(a_{it}=1\) when \(i\in S_t\) and \(a_{it}=0\) otherwise, and put \(q=1-\sum_{k\in T}p_k\). By hypothesis, \(q\geq0\).

For each ordered pair \(k,t\in T\) with \(t\neq k\), introduce \(y_{kt}\), the total mass of type \(t\) served after event \(k\). Consider the following linear program in the variables \(x_t\) and \(y_{kt}\):

\[ \text{maximize}\quad q\sum_{t\in T}v_tx_t+ \sum_{k\in T}p_k\sum_{t\neq k}v_ty_{kt}, \]

subject to

\[ 0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t \qquad(t\in T), \]

\[ x_t\leq y_{kt}\leq\mu_t \qquad(k,t\in T,\ t\neq k), \]

\[ \sum_{t\in T}a_{it}x_t\leq b_i \qquad(i=1,\ldots,m), \]

\[ \sum_{t\neq k}a_{it}y_{kt}\leq b_i \qquad(i=1,\ldots,m,\ k\in T). \]

The lower bounds \(y_{kt}\geq x_t\) express retention of every nonwithdrawn initial recipient’s bundle.

Proof of equivalence. Given any feasible allocation and repairs, set \(y_{kt}=x_t+r_{kt}\) for \(t\neq k\). The original mass bounds, nonnegative repair bounds, and capacity constraints imply every displayed LP constraint.

Conversely, given any feasible LP solution, set \(r_{kt}=y_{kt}-x_t\) for \(t\neq k\), and \(r_{kk}=0\). These repairs are nonnegative, satisfy \(x_t+r_{kt}\leq\mu_t\), and meet every original capacity constraint. Thus the two feasible sets correspond bijectively.

Their objective values also agree. Indeed,

\[ \begin{aligned} q\sum_t v_tx_t+\sum_kp_k\sum_{t\neq k}v_ty_{kt} &=\sum_t\left(q+\sum_{k\neq t}p_k\right)v_tx_t +\sum_kp_k\sum_{t\neq k}v_tr_{kt}\\ &=\sum_t(1-p_t)v_tx_t +\sum_kp_k\sum_{t\neq k}v_tr_{kt}\\ &=W(x,r). \end{aligned} \]

The second equality uses \(q+\sum_{k\neq t}p_k=1-p_t\). Therefore the LP optimum equals \(\mathrm{OPT}\) exactly.

Polynomial bit complexity. The LP has \(\tau+\tau(\tau-1)=\tau^2\) variables and \(2\tau^2+m(\tau+1)\) scalar inequalities. In particular, its size depends on the listed support, with no enumeration of other possible types.

All constraint coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and all right-hand sides are supplied rational numbers. The objective coefficients are \(qv_t\) and \(p_kv_t\). Computing \(q\) and these products requires polynomially many bit operations and produces rational numbers of polynomial encoding length. For example, a common denominator for the sum defining \(q\) can be the product of the input denominators; its binary length is at most their total binary length. Consequently, the entire LP has encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The feasible region is nonempty because \(x=y=0\) is feasible. It is closed and bounded because every variable lies between zero and its corresponding \(\mu_t\). Hence the linear objective attains a finite maximum.

The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming computes an exact rational optimum in time polynomial in the LP’s encoding length; exact optimization, including rational recovery, is discussed in [Vishnoi’s linear-programming lecture notes](https://nisheethvishnoi.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/lecture71.pdf). All its required hypotheses hold here. Recovering \(r_{kt}=y_{kt}-x_t\) also takes polynomial time.

The resulting allocation satisfies \(W(x,r)=\mathrm{OPT}\geq\mathrm{OPT}/(m+1)\). This settles the specified continuous model for all admissible inputs.
