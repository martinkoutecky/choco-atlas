The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite buyer types \(\Theta\), seller types \((q,\sigma)\), positive rational masses \(\beta_\theta\) and \(\eta_{q,\sigma}\) normalized to one on each side, and rational valuations \(v_\theta\), costs \(c_\sigma\), and public qualities \(q\), choose matched mass \(y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\ge 0\) and per-agent transfers \(p^B_\theta,p^S_{q,\sigma}\). Require \(\sum_{q,\sigma}y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\le\beta_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\le\eta_{q,\sigma}\). Define \(a_\theta=\sum_{q,\sigma}q y_{\theta,q,\sigma}/\beta_\theta\) and \(b_{q,\sigma}=\sum_\theta y_{\theta,q,\sigma}/\eta_{q,\sigma}\). Impose buyer and seller incentive compatibility across reports, individual rationality, and mass-weighted budget balance. Find a feasible mechanism maximizing \(\sum_{\theta,q,\sigma}q(v_\theta-c_\sigma)y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\). A prover should also establish whether these truthful-mass variables admit a feasible extension to other aggregate report distributions.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) in the standard bit model. Every feasible truthful outcome admits an extension as defined in the question.

Both conclusions hold for valuations and costs of either sign. The proof uses the stated divisible-mass model and the requirement that incentive compatibility, individual rationality, and budget balance hold at the truthful aggregate distribution.

1. A rational linear program

Write \(t=|\Theta|\), \(s=|S|\), so that \(\tau=t+s\). For each quality \(q\), define \(S_q=\{\sigma:(q,\sigma)\in S\}\) and \(s_q=|S_q|\).

Introduce the following real variables:

  • \(y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\), for every \(\theta\in\Theta\) and \((q,\sigma)\in S\);
  • \(a_\theta,p^B_\theta\), for every \(\theta\in\Theta\);
  • \(b_{q,\sigma},p^S_{q,\sigma}\), for every \((q,\sigma)\in S\).

Impose the following linear conditions.

Matching feasibility:

\(y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\geq 0\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{(q,\sigma)\in S}y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\leq\beta_\theta\quad(\theta\in\Theta)\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\leq\eta_{q,\sigma}\quad((q,\sigma)\in S)\).

Allocation definitions:

\(\displaystyle \beta_\theta a_\theta=\sum_{(q,\sigma)\in S}q\,y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\quad(\theta\in\Theta)\),

\(\displaystyle \eta_{q,\sigma}b_{q,\sigma}=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\quad((q,\sigma)\in S)\).

Buyer incentive compatibility:

\(\displaystyle v_\theta(a_\theta-a_{\hat\theta})-p^B_\theta+p^B_{\hat\theta}\geq 0\quad(\theta,\hat\theta\in\Theta)\).

Seller incentive compatibility:

\(\displaystyle p^S_{q,\sigma}-p^S_{q,\hat\sigma}-qc_\sigma(b_{q,\sigma}-b_{q,\hat\sigma})\geq 0\quad(q\in Q,\ \sigma,\hat\sigma\in S_q)\).

Only reports with the same public quality are compared.

Individual rationality:

\(\displaystyle v_\theta a_\theta-p^B_\theta\geq 0\quad(\theta\in\Theta)\),

\(\displaystyle p^S_{q,\sigma}-qc_\sigma b_{q,\sigma}\geq 0\quad((q,\sigma)\in S)\).

Exact budget balance:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\beta_\theta p^B_\theta=\sum_{(q,\sigma)\in S}\eta_{q,\sigma}p^S_{q,\sigma}\).

Welfare threshold:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{(q,\sigma)\in S}q(v_\theta-c_\sigma)y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\geq W\).

All coefficients are rational input values or products and differences of such values. In particular, the signs of valuations and costs cause no difficulty.

Equivalence with the original problem

Suppose first that feasible truthful outcomes achieve welfare at least \(W\). Retain their matching masses and transfers, and assign \(a_\theta,b_{q,\sigma}\) their specified values. Every displayed LP condition then follows directly from the corresponding condition in the question.

Conversely, suppose the LP is feasible. Since every truthful mass is positive, its allocation equalities imply

\(\displaystyle a_\theta=\beta_\theta^{-1}\sum_{(q,\sigma)\in S}q\,y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\),

\(\displaystyle b_{q,\sigma}=\eta_{q,\sigma}^{-1}\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\).

Thus these variables have exactly the required meanings. The remaining constraints give matching feasibility, both families of incentive inequalities, individual rationality, exact budget balance, and welfare at least \(W\). Consequently, the LP is feasible if and only if the requested truthful outcomes exist.

For \(W\leq 0\), zero matching and zero transfers already provide a feasible witness. The same LP decides every threshold.

2. Polynomial running time

The LP has \(ts+2\tau=O(\tau^2)\) variables.

There are \(t(t-1)\) nontrivial buyer incentive inequalities and \(\sum_q s_q(s_q-1)\) nontrivial seller incentive inequalities. Because \(\sum_q s_q=s\), we have \(\sum_q s_q^2\leq s^2\). Including all other conditions, the LP therefore has \(O(\tau^2)\) scalar constraints.

Its sparse matrix also has \(O(\tau^2)\) nonzero entries:

  • matching capacities and allocation definitions each use \(O(ts+\tau)\) entries;
  • each incentive inequality uses at most four entries;
  • the remaining conditions use \(O(ts+\tau)\) entries.

Each coefficient has bit length \(O(L)\): it is obtained using a bounded number of rational additions or multiplications. Including variable indices, the sparse LP encoding has length \(O(\tau^2(L+\log(\tau+1)))\), and can be constructed in polynomial time.

Apply the standard rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility of a finite system of rational linear equalities and non-strict inequalities is decidable in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here. Unrestricted transfer variables can, if necessary, be represented as differences of nonnegative variables, and equalities as pairs of inequalities; these conversions preserve the polynomial bounds.

It follows that the decision problem has running time \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\). The LP size depends on the support size \(\tau\), rather than on the number of possible unsupported types. For an explicitly encoded quality list, \(m=O(L)\), so the bound can also be written \((\tau+L)^{O(1)}\).

This is a bit-polynomial classification; no strongly polynomial running time is claimed. In particular, polynomial-time many-one \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The continuous-information characterization in [Widmer, Karaenke, and Sugumaran, Section 4.1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mde.3256) is not needed for this argument.

3. Extension of every feasible truthful outcome

Fix any feasible truthful outcome, allowing its matching masses and transfers to be arbitrary real numbers.

Let \(d^0=(\beta,\eta)\) denote the truthful aggregate report distribution. An admissible aggregate distribution \(d=(\widetilde\beta,\widetilde\eta)\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \widetilde\beta_\theta\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\widetilde\beta_\theta=1\),

and

\(\displaystyle \widetilde\eta_{q,\sigma}\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{\sigma\in S_q}\widetilde\eta_{q,\sigma}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_q}\eta_{q,\sigma}\quad(q\in Q)\).

Define the rule as follows.

At the truthful aggregate distribution

At \(d=d^0\), a buyer reporting \(\theta\) receives fractional matching with seller report \((q,\sigma)\) equal to

\(\displaystyle x^B_{\theta,q,\sigma}=\frac{y_{\theta,q,\sigma}}{\beta_\theta}\),

and pays \(p^B_\theta\). A seller reporting \((q,\sigma)\) receives fractional matching with buyer report \(\theta\) equal to

\(\displaystyle x^S_{q,\sigma,\theta}=\frac{y_{\theta,q,\sigma}}{\eta_{q,\sigma}}\),

and receives \(p^S_{q,\sigma}\).

These assignments are anonymous: they depend only on the agent’s report and the aggregate distribution. Their per-agent capacity bounds follow from

\(\displaystyle \sum_{(q,\sigma)\in S}x^B_{\theta,q,\sigma}\leq 1,\qquad \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}x^S_{q,\sigma,\theta}\leq 1\).

They describe consistent divisible matching, because for every report pair,

\(\displaystyle \beta_\theta x^B_{\theta,q,\sigma}=\eta_{q,\sigma}x^S_{q,\sigma,\theta}=y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\).

The resulting quality-weighted buyer allocation is \(a_\theta\), and the seller’s supplied fraction is \(b_{q,\sigma}\). Hence the rule agrees with all given truthful outcomes.

At every other aggregate distribution

At every \(d\neq d^0\), assign zero matching and zero transfer to every report on both sides, including zero-mass reports.

This specifies all outcomes without dividing by any zero mass. Matching capacities hold everywhere: at \(d^0\) by feasibility of the given outcome, and elsewhere because all matching masses vanish.

Incentives at the truthful distribution

By hypothesis, an individual’s report does not change aggregate masses. Therefore a unilateral buyer deviation at \(d^0\) still uses the \(d^0\) branch of the rule. Reporting \(\hat\theta\) gives that buyer utility

\(\displaystyle v_\theta a_{\hat\theta}-p^B_{\hat\theta}\).

The given buyer incentive inequalities make truthful reporting optimal.

Likewise, a seller of public quality \(q\) and true cost \(c_\sigma\), reporting \(\hat\sigma\in S_q\), obtains utility

\(\displaystyle p^S_{q,\hat\sigma}-qc_\sigma b_{q,\hat\sigma}\).

The given seller incentive inequalities make truthful reporting optimal among all permitted reports.

Individual rationality and exact budget balance at \(d^0\) hold because the extension reproduces the original outcomes there. Thus the constructed rule satisfies every requirement of the extension definition.

Its possible discontinuity at \(d^0\) is permitted by the stated absence of a continuity requirement. This completes both proofs.
