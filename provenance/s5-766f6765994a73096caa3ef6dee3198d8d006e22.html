The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of researcher types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and rational effort weights \(w_t>0\), with reward \(F(w)=w^2\) and equal credit \(\phi=\tfrac12\), find a rational allocation of mass to solo projects and pairs that is pairwise stable under the paper’s deviation rule. A pair of types \(t,u\) gives each participant utility \(\tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2\); a solo participant of type \(t\) gets \(w_t^2\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) researcher types, with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\) and positive binary-rational effort weights \(w_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each researcher undertakes exactly one solo or two-person project, using their entire effort; distinct researchers of the same type may collaborate.
An allocation consists of nonnegative rational solo masses \(s_t\) and symmetric pair masses \(p_{tu}=p_{ut}\), where \(p_{tt}\) counts pairs, satisfying \(s_t+2p_{tt}+\sum_{u\ne t}p_{tu}=\mu_t\). Define occupied-role masses \(r_{t0}=s_t\), \(r_{tt}=2p_{tt}\), and \(r_{tu}=p_{tu}\) for \(u\ne t\), with \(0\notin T\).
With reward \(F(w)=w^2\) and equal credit \(\phi=\tfrac12\), role utilities are \(U_{t0}=w_t^2\) and \(U_{tu}=\tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2\). Author-order ties do not affect utility.
Pairwise stability means \(U_{ta}\ge w_t^2\) whenever \(r_{ta}>0\), and, for every \(t,u\in T\) and \(a,b\in T\cup\{0\}\) with \(r_{ta}>0\) and \(r_{ub}>0\), either \(U_{ta}\ge\tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2\) or \(U_{ub}\ge\tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2\). This includes \(t=u\): deviations use distinct researchers, abandon their current projects, and require both participants to improve strictly. Stability concerns occupied roles, not average utility across a type.
Is finding a rational pairwise-stable allocation, or reporting that none exists, polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau\) and the total input bit length \(L\), or is it NP-hard? There is no separate \(m\) parameter in this model.
For finite lists of researchers with indivisible effort and equal credit, a pairwise-stable allocation always exists and is computable in polynomial time ([Ackerman–Brânzei](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.3391v2)).
Nearby continuous work studies one-sided matching of type masses with equal sharing and strict blocking pairs ([Wu–Zhang](https://hanzhezhang.github.io/research/2603EvoEqual.pdf)); no result settling this precise rational-input computational question was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted mass allocation as an aggregate matching of a nonatomic population, with each researcher using their entire effort in exactly one project, matching the anchor's indivisible-budget setting.
  • Allowed collaborations between distinct researchers of the same type; diagonal pair mass consumes twice that mass of researchers, and self-collaboration is excluded.
  • Defined stability separately for every positive-mass occupied role, including same-type blocking pairs, rather than using average type utilities.
  • Included unilateral departures to solo projects and required strict improvement by both members of a blocking pair; utility ties do not block.
  • Made the search problem total by allowing a report of nonexistence, without asserting continuous existence.
  • Used support size and total binary input length as the running-time parameters because this model has no separate alternative-count parameter.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. Every input admits a rational pairwise-stable allocation: set \(s_t=0\), \(p_{tt}=\mu_t/2\), and \(p_{tu}=0\) for distinct types.

The explicit permission for distinct researchers of the same type to collaborate is decisive. Beyond this construction, the stability conditions give a complete characterization of stable allocations.

Characterization of stability

Proposition. A feasible allocation is pairwise stable if and only if it has no positive solo masses and every positive pair mass joins types with equal effort weights.

Necessity. Fix a type \(t\) and an occupied role \(a\), so \(r_{ta}>0\). In the stated stability condition, take \(u=t\) and \(b=a\). Both alternatives in its disjunction are then identical, giving

\(U_{ta}\ge \tfrac12(w_t+w_t)^2=2w_t^2.\)

This application is permitted by the specified same-type deviation condition, including two distinct researchers occupying the same role.

A solo role has utility \(U_{t0}=w_t^2<2w_t^2\), because \(w_t>0\). Consequently, \(s_t=0\) for every \(t\).

Now suppose \(p_{tu}>0\) for distinct types \(t,u\). Both corresponding roles are occupied. Applying the inequality just proved to each endpoint gives

\(\tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2\ge 2w_t^2\quad\text{and}\quad\tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2\ge 2w_u^2.\)

Since the weights are positive, the first inequality implies \(w_t+w_u\ge 2w_t\), hence \(w_u\ge w_t\). The second implies \(w_t\ge w_u\). Therefore \(w_t=w_u\).

Sufficiency. Suppose a feasible allocation has no solos and only pairs between equal-weight types. Every occupied role of type \(t\) then has utility \(2w_t^2\), which exceeds its solo utility \(w_t^2\).

Consider any types \(t,u\) and any occupied roles \(a,b\) of those types. If \(w_t\ge w_u\), then

\(U_{ta}=2w_t^2=\tfrac12(2w_t)^2\ge\tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2.\)

Thus the first alternative in the blocking-pair condition holds. If \(w_u\ge w_t\), the same calculation at \(u\) establishes the second alternative. Every required stability condition follows. This proves the proposition.

Construction and feasibility

For every \(t\in T\), output \(s_t=0\) and \(p_{tt}=\mu_t/2\); output zero for all off-diagonal pair masses.

These masses are nonnegative and rational, and the balance equation for each type is

\(s_t+2p_{tt}+\sum_{u\ne t}p_{tu}=0+2(\mu_t/2)+0=\mu_t.\)

Every occupied pair joins equal-weight researchers. The proposition therefore proves stability for arbitrary support size, masses, and positive weights.

When all effort weights are distinct, the proposition also shows that this is the unique stable allocation: all off-diagonal pair masses and all solo masses must vanish, leaving \(2p_{tt}=\mu_t\).

Bit complexity

If an input mass is represented as \(\mu_t=n_t/d_t\), its diagonal pair mass can be represented as \(n_t/(2d_t)\). Doubling the denominator increases its binary length by at most one bit. Thus the total length of the nonzero output entries is \(O(L+\tau)\).

A full matrix representation additionally contains \(O(\tau^2)\) zero entries. The construction takes polynomial time in \(\tau\) and \(L\), using only exact rational arithmetic. It does not expand the masses into individual researchers, and no separate population-size parameter is needed.

Step-by-step verification

1. Same-type deviations impose the claimed utility bound

Fix an occupied role, so \(r_{ta}>0\). The task’s stability condition explicitly quantifies over \(t=u\) and permits choosing \(b=a\). Both occupancy hypotheses are then satisfied, and the two alternatives become the same inequality:

\(U_{ta}\ge \tfrac12(w_t+w_t)^2=2w_t^2.\)

This is a direct application of the supplied definition. It does not introduce an additional assumption about the number of individual researchers. The task expressly includes deviations by distinct researchers of the same type, and its occupied-role formulation imposes this condition whenever the role has positive mass.

2. Positive solo masses are impossible

A positive solo mass would give an occupied role with utility \(U_{t0}=w_t^2\). Since \(w_t>0\), this is strictly less than \(2w_t^2\), contradicting the bound above.

Thus every stable allocation has \(s_t=0\) for every type. The positivity hypothesis is stated in the task and used correctly.

3. Positive pairs must have equal endpoint weights

If \(p_{tu}>0\) for distinct types, both endpoint roles are occupied. Applying the established bound at each endpoint gives

\(\tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2\ge 2w_t^2\)

and

\(\tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2\ge 2w_u^2.\)

All weights are positive, so taking square roots preserves the inequalities and yields \(w_u\ge w_t\) and \(w_t\ge w_u\). Hence \(w_t=w_u\).

Diagonal pairs automatically have equal endpoint weights. This proves the entire necessity direction.

4. The stated conditions suffice for stability

With no solos and only equal-weight pairs, every occupied role of type \(t\) has utility \(2w_t^2\). This satisfies individual rationality because \(2w_t^2>w_t^2\).

For any two occupied roles, at least one of \(w_t\ge w_u\) or \(w_u\ge w_t\) holds. In the first case, positivity gives \(2w_t\ge w_t+w_u\), and therefore

\(U_{ta}=2w_t^2\ge \tfrac12(w_t+w_u)^2.\)

The second case establishes the other alternative in the stability disjunction. This covers equal weights and same-type deviations as well. The characterization is proved in both directions.

5. The construction is feasible and stable

The proposed masses are nonnegative rationals. For each type,

\(s_t+2p_{tt}+\sum_{u\ne t}p_{tu}=\mu_t.\)

Every occupied role belongs to a diagonal pair, so the proved characterization establishes stability. The construction also covers the singleton support case.

If all weights are distinct, the characterization forces every off-diagonal mass and every solo mass to vanish. Feasibility then uniquely determines \(p_{tt}=\mu_t/2\), verifying the uniqueness claim.

6. The complexity bound is valid

Representing \(\mu_t=n_t/d_t\), the output \(n_t/(2d_t)\) increases the denominator’s binary length by exactly one bit. Reduction is unnecessary for a valid rational representation; requiring reduction would still permit polynomial-time computation.

The nonzero entries have total encoding length \(O(L+\tau)\). Explicitly writing all matrix zeros requires \(O(\tau^2)\) additional entries and polynomial time. No enumeration of individual researchers is required.
