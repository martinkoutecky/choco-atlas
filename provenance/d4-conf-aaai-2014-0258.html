The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite horizon \(T\), a finite support \(\Theta\) of types \(\theta=(a_\theta,d_\theta,v_\theta)\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), and shared discount functions \(F,D\), each arriving type mass is revealed at its arrival slot while future arrivals remain unknown. With per-capita capacity \(q\) per slot, does there exist an online allocation policy assigning mass \(x_{\theta,t}\) only for \(a_\theta\le t\le d_\theta\), with \(\sum_t x_{\theta,t}\le\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta,t}\le q\), whose welfare \(\sum_{\theta,t}x_{\theta,t}\max\{v_\theta F(t)+D(t),0\}\) is at least half the offline optimum for every arrival sequence?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types \(\theta=(a_\theta,d_\theta,v_\theta)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(T\ge1\) slots (write \(m=T\)); \(1\le a_\theta\le d_\theta\le T\) are integers and \(v_\theta\ge0\) is rational. Each unit of society mass demands at most one unit of service.
Shared discount functions are given as binary-rational tables: \(0<F(t)\le1\) and \(D(t)\le0\), both nonincreasing. Service at slot \(t\) has per-unit value \(w_\theta(t)=\max\{v_\theta F(t)+D(t),0\}\).
The horizon, discount tables, and rational per-slot capacity \(q>0\) are known initially. Each type and its entire mass are revealed at the beginning of slot \(a_\theta\); future support entries are unknown.
A deterministic online policy irrevocably assigns nonnegative rational mass \(x_{\theta,t}\) using only information revealed through slot \(t\), with \(x_{\theta,t}=0\) outside \([a_\theta,d_\theta]\), \(\sum_t x_{\theta,t}\le\mu_\theta\), and \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta,t}\le q\).
Its welfare is \(\mathrm{ALG}=\sum_{\theta,t}x_{\theta,t}w_\theta(t)\); \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is the maximum welfare under the same constraints with the entire sequence known. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; measure running times in \(m=T\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

Does there exist a single online policy running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) that guarantees \(\mathrm{ALG}\ge\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}\) for every admissible arrival sequence, or is such a guarantee impossible?

For indivisible unit-demand agents with time-discounting values and a fixed integer supply per slot, a polynomial-time online allocation rule is known to guarantee \(\mathrm{ALG}\ge\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}\). [Source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/8801/8660)
No result settling this precise rational-mass online formulation was identified in the search.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Proven result. Yes. A deterministic policy that greedily serves the available mass with the highest current per-unit value guarantees \(\mathrm{ALG}\ge \tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}\) and runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

This extends the highest-current-value allocation rule in the [cited paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/8801/8660). The proof below works directly with rational masses.

1. Policy and feasibility. Let \(g_{\theta,t}\) denote the policy’s allocation. At the beginning of slot \(t\), define the remaining mass of each revealed type by

\(\displaystyle r_{\theta,t}=\mu_\theta-\sum_{s<t}g_{\theta,s}.\)

Consider the types satisfying \(a_\theta\le t\le d_\theta\), \(r_{\theta,t}>0\), and \(w_\theta(t)>0\). Sort them by nonincreasing \(w_\theta(t)\), breaking ties deterministically. Start with remaining capacity \(c=q\). For each type in this order, assign

\(\displaystyle g_{\theta,t}=\min\{r_{\theta,t},c\},\)

and subtract this allocation from \(c\). All other allocations are zero.

Every assignment respects both the remaining type mass and the remaining slot capacity. The policy uses only revealed types and the initially known discount tables, so it is deterministic and online.

For each type, its value is nonincreasing in time. Indeed, if \(s\le t\), then \(v_\theta\ge0\) and the assumed monotonicity give

\(\displaystyle v_\theta F(s)+D(s)\ge v_\theta F(t)+D(t).\)

Taking the maximum with zero preserves this inequality, hence \(w_\theta(s)\ge w_\theta(t)\).

2. A single-slot optimality lemma. At slot \(t\), the greedy allocation maximizes \(\sum_\theta u_\theta w_\theta(t)\) over all allocations to currently available types satisfying

\(\displaystyle 0\le u_\theta\le r_{\theta,t},\qquad \sum_\theta u_\theta\le q.\)

Here is a direct proof. If the total available positive-value mass is at most \(q\), greedy takes all of it, which maximizes the objective.

Otherwise, greedy fills capacity \(q\). Let \(\lambda>0\) be the value of the last type receiving positive allocation. Greedy takes all available mass with value greater than \(\lambda\), takes no mass with value less than \(\lambda\), and possibly takes some mass with value equal to \(\lambda\). Thus, for every feasible \(u\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta w_\theta(t)(g_{\theta,t}-u_\theta) = \sum_\theta (w_\theta(t)-\lambda)(g_{\theta,t}-u_\theta) +\lambda\left(q-\sum_\theta u_\theta\right) \ge0. \)

Each summand in the first sum is nonnegative: above \(\lambda\), \(g_{\theta,t}=r_{\theta,t}\ge u_\theta\); below \(\lambda\), \(g_{\theta,t}=0\le u_\theta\); and at \(\lambda\), the coefficient vanishes. The final term is nonnegative by feasibility. This proves the lemma.

3. Charging an offline optimum. Fix an optimal offline allocation \(o_{\theta,t}\). Such an optimum exists because its feasible set is a nonempty compact polytope and welfare is continuous.

Define cumulative allocations, with \(O_{\theta,0}=G_{\theta,0}=0\), by

\(\displaystyle O_{\theta,t}=\sum_{s\le t}o_{\theta,s},\qquad G_{\theta,t}=\sum_{s\le t}g_{\theta,s}.\)

For the proof, label each type’s mass by the interval \([0,\mu_\theta)\). Represent the offline and greedy allocations at slot \(t\) by the respective intervals

\(\displaystyle I_{\theta,t}=[O_{\theta,t-1},O_{\theta,t}),\qquad J_{\theta,t}=[G_{\theta,t-1},G_{\theta,t}). \)

Their lengths are \(o_{\theta,t}\) and \(g_{\theta,t}\). This labeling is legitimate because mass within a type has identical availability and per-unit values.

Partition each offline interval into two parts. Let

\(\displaystyle z_{\theta,t} =\left|I_{\theta,t}\cap[0,G_{\theta,t-1})\right|, \qquad y_{\theta,t}=o_{\theta,t}-z_{\theta,t}, \)

where \(|\cdot|\) denotes interval length. Thus \(z_{\theta,t}\) is offline mass whose labels greedy served strictly before \(t\), while \(y_{\theta,t}\) consists of the remaining labels.

We bound these two contributions separately.

The remaining contribution. The labels counted by \(y_{\theta,t}\) lie inside \([G_{\theta,t-1},\mu_\theta)\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle 0\le y_{\theta,t}\le\mu_\theta-G_{\theta,t-1}=r_{\theta,t}.\)

Also, \(y_{\theta,t}\le o_{\theta,t}\), so

\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta y_{\theta,t}\le q.\)

Whenever \(y_{\theta,t}>0\), offline feasibility implies \(a_\theta\le t\le d_\theta\); hence this type has already been revealed and is currently available. Therefore \(y_{\theta,t}\) forms a feasible comparison allocation for the single-slot lemma, including any zero-value components. It follows that

\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta y_{\theta,t}w_\theta(t) \le \sum_\theta g_{\theta,t}w_\theta(t). \)

Summing over slots gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta,t}y_{\theta,t}w_\theta(t)\le\mathrm{ALG}.\)

The previously served contribution. For \(s<t\), define

\(\displaystyle c_{\theta,s,t}=|J_{\theta,s}\cap I_{\theta,t}|.\)

The greedy intervals with \(s<t\) partition \([0,G_{\theta,t-1})\), so

\(\displaystyle z_{\theta,t}=\sum_{s<t}c_{\theta,s,t}.\)

For fixed \(\theta,s\), the offline intervals are disjoint. Their intersections with \(J_{\theta,s}\) therefore have total length at most \(g_{\theta,s}\):

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t>s}c_{\theta,s,t}\le g_{\theta,s}.\)

Using the proved monotonicity \(w_\theta(t)\le w_\theta(s)\) whenever \(s<t\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta,t}z_{\theta,t}w_\theta(t) = \sum_{\theta,s}\sum_{t>s}c_{\theta,s,t}w_\theta(t) \le \sum_{\theta,s}w_\theta(s)\sum_{t>s}c_{\theta,s,t} \le \sum_{\theta,s}g_{\theta,s}w_\theta(s) = \mathrm{ALG}. \)

Because \(o_{\theta,t}=y_{\theta,t}+z_{\theta,t}\), the two bounds yield

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT} =\sum_{\theta,t}o_{\theta,t}w_\theta(t) \le2\mathrm{ALG}. \)

Thus the policy guarantees \(\mathrm{ALG}\ge\tfrac12\mathrm{OPT}\) on every admissible sequence.

4. Exact rational running time. At each slot, the policy evaluates and sorts at most \(\tau\) values and performs at most \(\tau\) allocation updates. Across all slots, this requires \(O(m\tau\log(\tau+1))\) comparisons and \(O(m\tau)\) additional arithmetic operations.

To check bit complexity, let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of all masses and of \(q\). This is only an analysis device; the online policy need not know future denominators. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). Every remaining mass, remaining capacity, and allocation is an integer multiple of \(1/Q\), because the policy uses only subtraction and minimum on these quantities. Remaining masses are at most \(1\), and remaining capacities are at most \(q\); consequently their numerators and denominators have \(O(L)\) bits.

Each current value \(\max\{v_\theta F(t)+D(t),0\}\) also has \(O(L)\) bits, since its calculation involves a constant number of operations on input rationals. Exact comparisons and allocation updates therefore take polynomial time in \(L\). The overall running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), as required.
