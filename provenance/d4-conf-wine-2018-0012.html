The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite downward-closed family \(\mathcal B\) of integral bundle patterns, type masses \(\mu\), per-capita supplies \(q\), and valuations \(v_t\), define \(M_t=\max\{\lambda:\exists z_b\ge0,\ \sum_b z_b=1,\ \sum_b b_jz_b\le q_j,\ z_b>0\Rightarrow v_t(b)\ge\lambda\}\). Run the paper's increasing-cardinality minimum-bundle greedy rule on mass, allowing legal tie-breaking, and determine the largest \(\alpha\) guaranteed to exhaust all type mass using bundles with \(v_t(b)\ge\alpha M_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge1\) be the number of goods categories and let \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\) be an explicitly listed, nonempty, finite downward-closed family of feasible bundle patterns. Supplies \(q_j\) and item values \(w_{t,j}\) are nonnegative binary rationals; on feasible patterns, \(v_t(b)=\sum_j w_{t,j}b_j\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. State any running times in \(m,\tau,L,|\mathcal B|\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length, and any additional parameters.
For each type, define \(M_t=\max\{\lambda\ge0:\exists(z_b)_{b\in\mathcal B}\ge0,\ \sum_bz_b=1,\ \sum_bb_jz_b\le q_j\ \forall j,\ z_b>0\Rightarrow v_t(b)\ge\lambda\}\). These benchmarks remain fixed throughout allocation; types with \(M_t=0\) receive the empty pattern.
For a threshold \(\alpha\in(0,1]\), initialize remaining type masses \(a_t=\mu_t\) for positive-benchmark types and remaining supplies \(r=q\). A pair \((t,b)\) is eligible when \(a_t>0\), \(b\in\mathcal B\), \(v_t(b)\ge\alpha M_t\), and \(r_j>0\) whenever \(b_j>0\).
Repeatedly choose any eligible pair minimizing \(|b|=\sum_jb_j\), allocate pattern \(b\) to mass \(d=\min\{a_t,\min_{j:b_j>0}r_j/b_j\}\), and update \(a_t\leftarrow a_t-d\), \(r\leftarrow r-db\). Stop when no pair is eligible; unused supply is permitted. Every such choice among minimum-cardinality pairs is legal tie-breaking.

What is the supremum \(\alpha_*\in[0,1]\) of thresholds for which this mass greedy rule exhausts every type's mass on every admissible instance under every legal tie-breaking, and is the supremum itself guaranteed?

For finite agents with hereditary valuations and the usual MMS benchmark, minimum-cardinality greedy guarantees \(4/11\), while its universal guarantee is at most \(40/107\), even for identical agents and partition matroids. [Li–Vetta](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.09561v2)
A different discrete procedure establishes existence of \(1/2\)-MMS allocations for hereditary valuations; this does not settle the specified greedy rule. [Hummel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11582)
No result settling the precise mass greedy guarantee above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one so that q is both per-capita supply and the aggregate supply available to the mass allocation.
  • Restricted feasible-pattern valuations to additive nonnegative rational item values, as required by the hereditary valuation model, rather than allowing arbitrary functions on patterns.
  • Specified an explicit binary encoding of the finite bundle family and all numerical input.
  • Interpreted integral pattern coordinates as counts of interchangeable goods categories and cardinality as the sum of those counts.
  • Defined a mass allocation move as assigning one integral pattern to a divisible portion of a type, consuming that portion times the pattern.
  • Used maximal feasible batches, ending when the selected type mass or a used supply is exhausted, to make the mass execution finite.
  • Interpreted guaranteed performance universally over legal tie-breaking, matching the discrete upper bound's use of an adverse legal execution.
  • Kept the original benchmarks fixed, allowed unused supply, and assigned empty patterns to zero-benchmark types without renormalizing the remaining society.
  • Used a supremum and asked about endpoint attainment because the graded question's largest guaranteed threshold need not a priori exist.

3. A sharper upper bound

Define
$$ T_n=1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k_i-1}, \qquad c_n=\frac{1}{T_n}. $$
The first terms of the integer sequence are
$$ 2,\ 3,\ 7,\ 43,\ 1807,\ 3263443. $$

$$ \left(c_n,\ c_n\frac{k_n}{k_n-1}\right]\cap(0,1]. $$

3.1 Reciprocal identities

The recurrence gives
$$ k_{i+1}-1=k_i(k_i-1), $$
and hence
$$ \frac{1}{k_i-1}-\frac{1}{k_{i+1}-1} =\frac{1}{k_i}. $$
Summing and using $k_1-1=1$ yields
$$ S_n:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k_i} =1-\frac{1}{k_{n+1}-1}<1. $$

Also,
$$ \frac{1/(k_{i+1}-1)}{1/(k_i-1)}=\frac{1}{k_i}\leq\frac12. $$
Thus the series defining $C_\infty$ converges, and $c_n$ decreases strictly to $C_\infty$.

3.2 The instance

$$ A_1,\ldots,A_n,F. $$
Set the supplies to
$$ q_{A_i}=1,\qquad q_F=h. $$

The feasible family is
$$ \mathcal B= \left\{ b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{n+1}: b_{A_i}\leq k_i\text{ for every }i,\quad b_F\leq h \right\}. $$
The input lists every vector in this rectangle. Its size is
$$ K_n=(h+1)\prod_{i=1}^{n}(k_i+1). $$
Thus the construction uses an explicitly listed finite downward-closed family, although its list can be large.

Give the type weights
$$ w_{A_i}=\frac{c_n}{k_i-1}, \qquad w_F=\frac{c_n}{h}. $$
All input data are nonnegative rationals.

3.3 The benchmark is exactly $1$

The pattern

$$ v(p) =c_n\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k_i-1}+1\right) =c_nT_n=1. $$
The deterministic distribution on $p$ proves $M\geq1$.

On the other hand, $w\cdot q=1$. Any benchmark-feasible distribution supported on patterns worth at least $\lambda$ satisfies

The last inequality uses nonnegative weights. Hence

$$ k_1A_1,\ k_2A_2,\ \ldots,\ k_nA_n. $$

$$ w_{A_i}\geq w_{A_{i+1}}\geq\cdots\geq w_{A_n}=w_F. $$

$$ (k_i-1)w_{A_i}=c_n<\alpha. $$

Here $k_i\leq k_n$, and $x/(x-1)$ decreases for $x>1$. Thus each proposed choice has minimum eligible cardinality.

The mass allocated at step $i$ is exactly $1/k_i$. Indeed, its category supply is $1$, and before that step the remaining mass is
$$ 1-S_{i-1} =\frac{1}{k_i}+\frac{1}{k_{i+1}-1} >\frac{1}{k_i}. $$
Consequently the supply constraint determines the allocation and exhausts $A_i$.

$$ 1-S_n=\frac{1}{k_{n+1}-1}>0. $$
Only $F$ has positive supply. Every feasible pattern using only $F$ has value at most
$$ hw_F=c_n<\alpha. $$
The execution therefore stops with positive mass unallocated.

4. The supremum itself is guaranteed

This argument applies to any finite explicitly listed family in the question.

Suppose an execution fails at a positive threshold $\alpha_0$. Before each selected pair $(t,b)$, consider every pair $(u,e)$ whose type has positive remaining mass, whose used categories have positive remaining supply, and whose cardinality is smaller than $|b|$. Minimum cardinality implies
$$ \frac{v_u(e)}{M_u}<\alpha_0. $$
Only positive-benchmark types are considered.

At the terminal state, consider every pair satisfying the remaining-mass and positive-supply conditions, without a cardinality restriction. Termination likewise implies
$$ \frac{v_u(e)}{M_u}<\alpha_0. $$

There are finitely many states, types, and patterns. Let $\gamma$ be the maximum of these ratios together with $0$. Then
$$ \gamma<\alpha_0. $$

Replay the selected sequence at any threshold
$$ \gamma<\alpha\leq\alpha_0. $$
Inductively, the states remain unchanged:

  • Every selected pair still qualifies, because it qualified at $\alpha_0$.
  • Every available smaller pattern remains below threshold, by the definition of $\gamma$.
  • The maximal allocated mass depends only on the state and chosen pattern, so the update is unchanged.

At the terminal state every available pair remains below threshold. Thus the same execution fails throughout
$$ (\gamma,\alpha_0]. $$

Section 2 shows that $G$ is nonempty and $\alpha_*>0$. If $\alpha_*\notin G$, a failing execution at $\alpha_*$ would exclude some interval $(\gamma,\alpha_*]$ from $G$. But the definition of supremum supplies an element of $G$ greater than $\gamma$, necessarily at most $\alpha_*$. This is a contradiction.

Hence
$$ \boxed{\alpha_*\in G.} $$
This proves attainment of the actual supremum; it does not assert that the upper bound $C_\infty$ is guaranteed.

If $k=0$, all benchmarks are zero. Otherwise, every positive-benchmark type has the same minimum qualifying size

$$ \frac{r}{h} =\frac{q}{h}-D \geq1-D \geq P-D>0. $$

Thus the unresolved universal constant concerns instances with multiple categories.

Benchmarks can be computed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,K$. Compute and sort each type’s $K$ pattern values, then binary-search feasibility of the linear system in Section 1. This uses
$$ O\bigl(\tau\log(K+1)\bigr) $$
rational LP feasibility tests, each with at most $K$ variables, $m$ supply constraints, one normalization constraint, and nonnegativity constraints.

For a rational threshold of bit length $A$, a specified execution can be implemented by scanning all type-pattern pairs at each step. Since there are at most $m+\tau$ steps, this takes
$$ O\bigl((m+\tau)\tau mK\bigr) $$
rational arithmetic operations after preprocessing, with polynomial bit complexity in $m,\tau,L,K,A$.

To check the bit bound, start with a common denominator for supplies and masses. Each update introduces at most one additional positive integer pattern-coordinate factor into that denominator. There are at most $m+\tau$ updates, and every coordinate has input-bounded bit length. All states therefore retain polynomial bit length.

Exploring every legal tie-breaking gives an exhaustive verification algorithm for a supplied rational-threshold instance in time
$$ (\tau K+1)^{m+\tau}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,K,A). $$
This follows from branching over at most $\tau K$ eligible minimum-cardinality choices at each state and the execution-length bound. It does not compute the universal constant over all instances.
