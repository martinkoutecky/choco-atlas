Result

The exact lexicographic optimization problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\), already with \(N=2\) and \(\tau=3\). The hardness construction uses explicit rational linear-inequality descriptions of the polytopes.

The nonatomic formulation also has stronger existence and attainment properties than the finite-publisher model:

  • Every valid instance has a feasible aggregate equilibrium.
  • The budget infimum is always \(0\), attained at \(\mathbf t=\mathbf 0\).
  • The welfare supremum among budget minimizers is always attained and is rational.

An exact algorithm for all requested outputs has running time

\(2^{O((m+\tau)(m+\log(\tau+1)))}L^{O(1)}\)

in bit operations. Its dependence on \(N\) is through its binary encoding, already included in \(L\).

The proofs follow.

1. Equilibrium forces globally maximal retrieval scores

Write \(a_\theta=N\mu_\theta>0\), and define

\(M_j=\max_{\theta}\max_{d\in D_\theta}f_\theta(d_j)\).

These maxima exist because every \(D_\theta\) is nonempty and compact, and every \(f_\theta\) is continuous.

Lemma 1. Every feasible aggregate equilibrium satisfies \(w_j=M_j\) for every query \(j\).

Proof. Every document has score at most \(M_j\), so \(w_j\le M_j\).

By the definition of essential supremum, almost every allocated document satisfies \(f_\theta(d_j)\le w_j\), simultaneously for all queries. Since feasibility requires \(H_j>0\), its utility is finite and bounded above by \(\sum_j1/H_j\).

Suppose \(w_j<M_j\). Some supported type \(\theta\) has a document \(d\in D_\theta\) with \(f_\theta(d_j)>w_j\). Feasibility gives \(t_j\le w_j\), so this deviation also satisfies \(f_\theta(d_j)\ge t_j\). Its utility is therefore \(+\infty\).

Almost every publisher of this type currently has finite utility and hence fails to maximize utility. Because \(a_\theta>0\), this contradicts aggregate equilibrium. Thus \(w_j=M_j\). \(\square\)

Consequently, once equilibrium is feasible, thresholds have no effect on incentives. For every \(\mathbf t\) satisfying \(0\le t_j\le M_j\), utility is exactly

\(U_\theta(d;H)=\sum_{j:\,f_\theta(d_j)=M_j}1/H_j\).

There are no attainable strict overtakes.

2. A feasible equilibrium always exists

For each type and document, define its winning set

\(S_\theta(d)=\{j:f_\theta(d_j)=M_j\}\),

and let \(\mathcal A_\theta=\{S_\theta(d):d\in D_\theta\}\). This is a finite, nonempty family of subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\). Write \(\chi_S\) for the incidence vector of \(S\).

The possible vectors of winning masses form the compact convex set

\(C=\left\{\sum_\theta a_\theta z_\theta:z_\theta\in\operatorname{conv}\{\chi_S:S\in\mathcal A_\theta\}\right\}\subseteq[0,N]^m\).

Every point of \(C\) can be realized by assigning each type’s mass to finitely many representative documents.

Moreover, \(C\) contains a vector with every coordinate positive. For each query, a document attains \(M_j\), and its type has positive mass. Distributing each type’s mass positively over representatives of all its available winning sets therefore covers every query with positive mass.

Consider

\(\Phi(H)=\sum_{j=1}^m\log H_j\)

on the positive part of \(C\). It has a maximizer \(H^*\) with all coordinates positive. Indeed, \(C\) is compact, a positive point exists, and approaching a boundary point with a zero coordinate sends \(\Phi\) to \(-\infty\), while every other coordinate is bounded above by \(N\).

Choose a finite allocation realizing \(H^*\). Suppose it assigns positive mass to a winning set \(S\) of type \(\theta\). Replacing a sufficiently small amount of that mass by any \(T\in\mathcal A_\theta\) changes the potential with derivative

\(\nabla\Phi(H^*)\cdot(\chi_T-\chi_S)=\sum_{j\in T}1/H_j^*-\sum_{j\in S}1/H_j^*\).

This derivative cannot be positive, because \(H^*\) maximizes \(\Phi\). Thus every positively used winning set maximizes that type’s utility.

All allocated scores are at most \(M_j\), and the positive winning masses ensure \(w_j=M_j\). The allocation is consequently a feasible aggregate equilibrium at \(\mathbf t=\mathbf0\).

Atoms in this document distribution represent positive masses of publishers choosing identical documents; they do not require atomic publishers.

Corollary. Every valid instance is feasible. Its minimum threshold budget is \(0\), attained at \(\mathbf t=\mathbf0\). Since thresholds are nonnegative, this is the only budget-minimizing threshold vector.

3. Welfare optimization always attains its optimum

Call \(S\in\mathcal A_\theta\) inclusion-maximal if no strictly larger set belongs to \(\mathcal A_\theta\).

At equilibrium, only inclusion-maximal winning sets can have positive mass: a strict superset would add at least one strictly positive term \(1/H_j\) to utility.

For each inclusion-maximal set, define

\(K_{\theta,S}=\{d\in D_\theta:f_\theta(d_j)=M_j\text{ for every }j\in S\}\).

This set is nonempty and compact. Every document in it has winning set exactly \(S\): an additional winner would contradict maximality.

Define its minimum possible worst winning distance by

\(c_{\theta,S}=\min_{d\in K_{\theta,S}}\max_{j\in S}|p_\theta-d_j|\),

with \(c_{\theta,\varnothing}=0\). The minimum is attained, since the objective is continuous on a compact set.

Now take any equilibrium. Group its allocation by type and winning set, and replace each positive group by a document attaining \(c_{\theta,S}\). This preserves:

  • all type masses;
  • every winning mass \(H_j\);
  • every publisher’s utility and best-response status.

It weakly improves welfare. The resulting welfare is exactly

\(-\max\{c_{\theta,S}:\text{the group }(\theta,S)\text{ has positive mass}\}\).

There are finitely many possible group supports, and at least one supports an equilibrium by Section 2. For each feasible support, the preceding replacement attains its best possible welfare. Taking the best of these finitely many supports proves attainment of the global welfare optimum.

Finally, each \(c_{\theta,S}\) is rational. A level set \(f_\theta(x)=M_j\) is a finite union of rational closed intervals and points. Thus \(K_{\theta,S}\) is a finite union of rational polytopes. On each component, minimizing the worst distance is a rational linear program, using a variable \(z\) and constraints \(-z\le p_\theta-d_j\le z\) for \(j\in S\). Its attained optimum is rational.

Therefore the welfare optimum is rational and attained.

4. Hardness with three types and two units of publisher mass

We reduce from Independent Set: given a graph \(G=(V,E)\), with \(n=|V|\ge2\), and \(2\le k\le n\), decide whether its independence number satisfies \(\alpha(G)\ge k\). This is the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem, equivalently Clique on the complement graph. [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

4.1 Queries, masses, and retrieval functions

Set \(N=2\), \(m=2n+1\), and \(\tau=3\). Partition the queries into one anchor \(a\), vertex queries \(v\in V\), and padding queries \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\). Let \(b=1/n\).

Choose a power of two \(Q>2k+1\), with \(Q\le2(2k+1)\), and set

\(\mu_A=2/Q,\qquad \mu_B=(2k-1)/Q,\qquad \mu_C=(Q-2k-1)/Q\).

These are positive dyadic rational masses summing to \(1\). Denote their total publisher masses by \(A=N\mu_A\), \(B=N\mu_B\), and \(C=N\mu_C\). Their relevant ratio is

\(B/A=k-\tfrac12\).

Type \(A\) has peak \(p_A=1\) and function \(f_A(x)=x\).

Types \(B\) and \(C\) have peak \(p_B=p_C=b\). Their common function equals \(x\) on \([0,b]\) and \((1-x)/(n-1)\) on \([b,1]\). It is continuous, rational piecewise linear, strictly increasing before its unique maximum at \(b\), and strictly decreasing afterward.

The high-peak requirement holds for all three types, since

\(\frac1{\lfloor m/N\rfloor+1}=\frac1{n+1}<\frac1n=b\le p_\theta\).

Also \(2=N<m\).

4.2 Document polytopes

Type \(B\) has a singleton document polytope: its document assigns \(b\) to every vertex query and zero elsewhere.

Type \(C\) likewise has a singleton polytope: its document assigns \(b\) to every padding query and zero elsewhere.

Both documents have total coordinate sum \(nb=1\).

For type \(A\), write the anchor coordinate as \(s\), the vertex coordinates as \(x_v\), and the padding coordinates as \(y_i\). Its polytope is given by

\(0\le s\le1,\qquad 0\le x_v\le b(1-s),\qquad y_i=0\),

and, for every edge \(\{u,v\}\in E\),

\(x_u+x_v\le b(1-s)\).

This is a nonempty rational polytope. Every document satisfies

\(s+\sum_vx_v+\sum_i y_i\le s+nb(1-s)=1\),

so it lies in the required document simplex.

The global maximum scores are

\(M_a=1,\qquad M_v=b\text{ for }v\in V,\qquad M_{z_i}=b\).

4.3 All possible winning sets of type \(A\)

A type \(A\) document wins the anchor exactly when \(s=1\). The coordinate bounds then force every \(x_v=0\), so its winning set is exactly \(\{a\}\).

A type \(A\) document wins a vertex query exactly when \(x_v=b\). The bound \(x_v\le b(1-s)\) forces \(s=0\). If two adjacent vertices both had coordinate \(b\), their edge constraint would require \(2b\le b\), which is impossible. Its winning vertices therefore form an independent set.

Conversely, every independent set \(S\subseteq V\) is realized by setting \(s=0\), \(x_v=b\) for \(v\in S\), and all other coordinates to zero.

Type \(A\) cannot win a padding query. Hence its complete family of winning sets consists of the anchor singleton and the independent vertex sets, including the empty set.

At equilibrium, its empty winning set cannot receive positive mass: it has utility zero, whereas the anchor document has positive utility.

4.4 Zero welfare encodes the absence of a large independent set

Every winning coordinate of types \(B\) and \(C\) equals their peak \(b\), so their winning distances are zero.

A type \(A\) anchor winner also has distance zero. Every type \(A\) vertex winner, however, has distance \(1-b\). Consequently every equilibrium has welfare either \(0\) or \(-(1-b)\).

An equilibrium has welfare zero exactly when all type \(A\) mass chooses the anchor document. For this allocation, winning masses are \(A\) at the anchor, \(B\) at every vertex query, and \(C\) at every padding query.

Type \(A\)’s anchor utility is \(1/A\). Its utility from an independent vertex set \(S\) is \(|S|/B\). Thus the allocation is an equilibrium exactly when

\(1/A\ge\alpha(G)/B\),

equivalently,

\(\alpha(G)\le B/A=k-\tfrac12\).

Because \(\alpha(G)\) is an integer, this is equivalent to \(\alpha(G)\le k-1\). Types \(B\) and \(C\) have only one document each and automatically maximize utility.

It follows that:

  • If \(\alpha(G)\le k-1\), optimal welfare is \(0\).
  • If \(\alpha(G)\ge k\), no zero-welfare equilibrium exists. Equilibria nevertheless exist by Section 2, and every one has welfare \(-(1-1/n)\).

Therefore a single exact optimal-value call decides Independent Set: its answer is yes exactly when optimal welfare is negative. More specifically, deciding whether optimal welfare is strictly negative is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\) under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

The construction has three supported types, constant \(N=2\), and constant-size piecewise-linear function descriptions. Even with dense inequality encoding, it has \(L=O(n^3\log n)\) and is constructible in polynomial time. This proves hardness of the exact optimization problem.

5. Exact algorithm and running time

Here polytopes are explicitly described by rational linear constraints.

5.1 Enumerate maximal winning sets and their costs

For each type and query, two rational linear programs compute the coordinate projection of \(D_\theta\), a closed interval. Maximizing \(f_\theta\) over this interval means evaluating it at the peak if the peak belongs to the interval, and otherwise at the endpoint nearest the peak. Taking the maximum over types computes every \(M_j\) in polynomial time.

For any level \(M_j\), the set \(f_\theta(x)=M_j\) has at most two connected components: at most one point below the peak and at most one interval or point above it. Their endpoints are rational and computable from the function description.

For every \(\theta\) and \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), test joint attainability of its winning equalities by enumerating at most \(2^{|S|}\) component choices and solving rational linear programs. Retain only inclusion-maximal jointly attainable sets. These are precisely the maximal winning sets used in Section 3.

For each retained set, compute \(c_{\theta,S}\) by the distance linear program from Section 3 over all its components. A sufficient preprocessing bound is

\(\tau4^m\operatorname{poly}(m,L)\).

Let \(K\le\tau2^m\) be the number of retained type-set pairs. Optimal worst winning distance belongs to the finite list of their costs.

5.2 At most \(m+\tau\) pairs suffice

Consider an equilibrium using masses \(\lambda_{\theta,S}\) on retained pairs. Its type masses and winning masses satisfy

\((a,H)=\sum_{\theta,S}\lambda_{\theta,S}(e_\theta,\chi_S)\in\mathbb R^{\tau+m}\),

where \(e_\theta\) is the type’s unit vector.

If more than \(m+\tau\) coefficients are positive, their vectors are linearly dependent. Any dependence has coefficients summing to zero, by summing its first \(\tau\) coordinates, and therefore has both positive and negative coefficients. Moving the allocation coefficients along this dependence until one becomes zero preserves \((a,H)\) and nonnegativity.

Repeating reduces the support to at most \(m+\tau\) pairs. The remaining pairs retain their best-response status because \(H\) is unchanged. Deleting pairs cannot worsen welfare.

5.3 Test a candidate distance exactly

For a candidate distance \(\delta\), enumerate supports of at most \(m+\tau\) pairs satisfying \(c_{\theta,S}\le\delta\).

For each support, use variables \(\lambda_{\theta,S}>0\), \(q_j>0\), and \(u_\theta\). Impose the type-mass equations

\(\sum_{S:\,(\theta,S)\text{ selected}}\lambda_{\theta,S}=a_\theta\),

the reciprocal-winning-mass equations

\(q_j\sum_{\text{selected }(\theta,S):\,j\in S}\lambda_{\theta,S}=1\),

and the best-response conditions

\(u_\theta\ge\sum_{j\in T}q_j\quad\text{for every retained }T\text{ of type }\theta\),

\(u_\theta=\sum_{j\in S}q_j\quad\text{for every selected pair }(\theta,S)\).

The reciprocal equations enforce positive winning masses and \(q_j=1/H_j\). The inequalities cover every possible deviation, because every available winning set is contained in a maximal one and all \(q_j\) are positive. The equalities make every allocated document a best response.

Thus this system is feasible exactly when the selected support yields an equilibrium of welfare at least \(-\delta\), using its minimum-distance representative documents.

The system has \(O(m+\tau)\) variables, \(O(K+m+\tau)\) polynomial constraints, degree at most two, and rational coefficients of polynomial bit length. Standard algorithms for existential real-algebraic feasibility give running time \(K^{O(m+\tau)}L^{O(1)}\) for these systems. The required single-block bounds, including coefficient-bit bounds, are stated in [Basu, *Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry: A Survey*, Theorem 2.27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.1534).

Enumerating supports and candidate costs preserves the total bound

\((\tau2^m)^{O(m+\tau)}L^{O(1)}=2^{O((m+\tau)(m+\log(\tau+1)))}L^{O(1)}\).

The smallest feasible candidate is the attained optimal worst distance; its negative is optimal welfare. Exact algebraic sampling can also provide allocation masses if an optimizing allocation is requested.

The budget value and its attainment can be reported in \(O(L)\) time, because Section 2 proves that every promised valid instance has minimum budget zero.
