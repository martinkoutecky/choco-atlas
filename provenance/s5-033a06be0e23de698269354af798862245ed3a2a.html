The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational agent-type masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_e\) for recurring chore classes \(e\), and each type's capacity \(c_t\) and size profile \(s_{t,e}\), let \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\) be an integral bundle and \(p_t(b)\) its minimum bin count. Define \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)=\inf_{\lambda}\max_{\lambda_b>0}p_t(b)\), subject to \(\lambda_b\ge0\), \(\sum_b\lambda_b=1\), and \(\sum_b\lambda_b b=q\). Find masses \(\pi_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_b\pi_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}\pi_{t,b}b_e=q_e\), and \(p_t(b)\le2\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(\pi_{t,b}>0\), or compute the minimum achievable factor.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) recurring chore classes \(E\), with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_e>0\). Each type has binary-rational capacity \(c_t>0\) and sizes \(0\le s_{t,e}\le c_t\), and unlimited identical bins of capacity \(c_t\).
A bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\) contains \(b_e\) indivisible copies of class \(e\). Its cost \(p_t(b)\) is the minimum number of bins packing those copies; \(p_t(0)=0\), and every nonempty bundle requires at least one bin, including bundles of zero-size copies.
Define \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)=\inf_{\lambda}\max_{b:\lambda_b>0}p_t(b)\), where \(\lambda\) ranges over finitely supported distributions satisfying \(\lambda_b\ge0\), \(\sum_b\lambda_b=1\), and \(\sum_b\lambda_b b=q\).
An allocation is a finitely supported family of nonnegative rational masses \(\pi_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_b\pi_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}\pi_{t,b}b_e=q_e\). It is \(\alpha\)-MMS fair, for \(\alpha\ge1\), if \(p_t(b)\le\alpha\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(\pi_{t,b}>0\). Encode supported bundles by binary multiplicities and their masses as binary rationals; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Can a \(2\)-MMS allocation always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this factor-two guarantee impossible on some instances or NP-hard to achieve?

Discrete bin-packing chores admit a polynomial-time \(2\)-MMS allocation algorithm, and some instances admit no factor below \(2\). [Li, Wang and Zhou](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/aa5d22c77b380e2261332bb641b3c2e3-Paper-Conference.pdf)
Later discrete work establishes allocations with cost at most \(\frac43\mathrm{MMS}_i+\frac43\) for each agent. [Li, Sun, Wang and Zhou](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04425)
No result settling this precise continuous allocation problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified positive rational capacities and rational sizes between zero and capacity, preserving the discrete requirement that every individual chore fits in a bin.
  • Listed only chore classes with positive supply; classes with zero supply can be omitted.
  • Specified that nonempty bundles of zero-size chores still require one bin, while the empty bundle costs zero.
  • Used finitely supported distributions for the benchmark and finitely supported rational allocations, with binary bundle multiplicities, to make the computational representation explicit.
  • Selected the factor-two approximation branch of the graded question and omitted exact computation of the minimum factor, as required for an approximation anchor.

A polynomial-time \(2\)-MMS allocation algorithm

The answer is affirmative. A \(2\)-MMS allocation can always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The algorithm outputs only \(O(\tau m)\) supported type–bundle pairs, with polynomial-length binary encodings.

Existence alone is easier: the specified continuous model always admits an exact \(1\)-MMS allocation. The substantive issue is computing suitable distributions efficiently. We resolve it using compressed next-fit packing.

1. A lower bound on the benchmark

Fix a type \(t\). Normalize its capacity to one by defining

\(a_e=s_{t,e}/c_t\), and \(v=\sum_{e\in E}a_eq_e\).

Thus \(0\le a_e\le1\), and \(v\) is the expected total normalized size.

First, \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) is a finite positive integer, and its infimum is attained. Indeed, independently rounding every coordinate \(q_e\) to its floor or ceiling gives a finitely supported distribution with mean \(q\). Every supported bundle has finite packing cost because each copy fits individually into one bin. Consequently, the attainable objective values form a nonempty subset of the nonnegative integers. Since \(q\ne0\), every feasible distribution assigns positive probability to some nonempty bundle, so all these objective values are positive.

Write \(k=\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\). For every bundle \(b\), the capacity constraint gives

\(\sum_e a_eb_e\le p_t(b)\).

Taking expectations in a benchmark-attaining distribution yields \(v\le k\). Hence

\(k\ge\max\{1,\lceil v\rceil\}\).

We will construct a distribution with mean \(q\) whose supported bundles each require at most

\(K=\max\{1,\lceil2v\rceil\}\)

bins. Since \(v\le k\) and \(k\ge1\) is an integer, \(K\le2k\).

The construction uses \(v\), but never computes \(k\).

2. Next-fit packing and its rounding term

Consider a finite multiset of positive-size copies, each of size at most one. Next fit maintains one open bin: it puts the next copy there if it fits, and otherwise closes that bin and opens another.

Let \(B\) be the number of bins produced, let their loads be \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_B\), and let \(V=\sum_i\ell_i>0\).

Whenever bin \(i+1\) is opened, its first copy did not fit into bin \(i\). Therefore

\(\ell_i+\ell_{i+1}>1\), for every \(1\le i<B\).

If \(B=2h\), summing over the disjoint pairs of bins gives \(V>h\). If \(B=2h+1\), the first \(2h\) bins have total load greater than \(h\), and the last bin has positive load. This also gives \(V>h\), including the case \(h=0\).

In both cases,

\(B<2V+1\).

We must account for the additive term before grouping bins into individual bundles.

Write \(v=A/D\) in lowest terms, with \(D\ge1\). Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of the supplies \(q_e\), and choose an integer \(r\ge0\) such that \(2^r\ge D\). Set

\(N=Q2^r\).

Then every \(M_e=Nq_e\) is an integer, and \(N\ge D\). Consider the virtual multiset containing \(M_e\) copies of each class. Its total normalized size is \(Nv\).

Suppose \(v>0\). Pack its positive-size copies by next fit. We claim that the resulting bin count satisfies \(B\le NK\).

There are two cases.

  • If \(2v\) is an integer, then \(K=2v\), and the next-fit inequality gives \(B<NK+1\). Since \(B,NK\) are integers, \(B\le NK\).
  • If \(2v\) is not an integer, then \(K=\lceil2v\rceil\). Because \(v\) has denominator \(D\), the positive rational number \(K-2v\) is at least \(1/D\). Thus \(N(K-2v)\ge1\), and

\(B<2Nv+1\le NK\).

Zero-size copies cause no difficulty. When \(v>0\), append all of them to the first packed bin; this changes neither its load nor the bin count. When \(v=0\), put all copies into one bin. This bin is required by the stipulated cost convention, and its count satisfies \(B=1\le NK\), since \(K=1\).

Thus, in every case, the virtual multiset has a packing into at most \(NK\) bins.

3. Computing the packing without expanding the multiset

The numbers \(N\) and \(M_e\) may be exponentially large in value. We therefore represent the packing by runs.

A bin pattern is a vector \(d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\) satisfying \(\sum_ea_ed_e\le1\). A run \((d,R)\) represents \(R\) consecutive bins with pattern \(d\), with \(R\) encoded in binary.

Process the positive-size classes consecutively, putting all copies of a class next to one another in the next-fit order. The following operations implement next fit exactly.

For a class of size \(a_e>0\), let \(n\) be its remaining multiplicity and let \(\ell\) be the current bin load. The number of copies that fit into the current bin is

\(h=\min\{n,\lfloor(1-\ell)/a_e\rfloor\}\).

Insert those \(h\) copies. If none remain, proceed to the next class.

Otherwise, the current bin is nonempty and is closed. Put

\(d_e=\lfloor1/a_e\rfloor\ge1\).

If \(n'\) copies remain after closing the current bin, emit a run of

\(w=\lfloor(n'-1)/d_e\rfloor\)

pure bins, each containing \(d_e\) copies of class \(e\). Leave the remaining \(n'-wd_e\) copies in the new current bin. This remaining number lies between \(1\) and \(d_e\), so this precisely reproduces next fit while leaving its last bin open.

Each class emits at most one previously open bin and one run of pure bins. Finally, emit the last open bin. Thus there are \(O(m)\) runs.

Appending zero-size copies to the first bin requires at most splitting the first run into that modified bin and its remaining unchanged bins. In the all-zero case there is just one run.

Consequently, the entire virtual packing is computable using \(O(m)\) exact arithmetic operations on integers and rationals of polynomial bit length.

4. Compressing the grouping into bundles

Append \(NK-B\) empty bin slots to the run representation. There are now exactly \(NK\) slots. Partition them consecutively into \(N\) blocks of \(K\) slots.

Every block defines a bundle that can be packed into at most \(K\) bins: retain its nonempty bin slots as its packing. We now compute the distribution of these bundles without enumerating the \(N\) blocks.

Let the padded run representation have \(R=O(m)\) runs, with patterns \(d^1,\ldots,d^R\), and cumulative endpoints

\(0=H_0<H_1<\cdots<H_R=NK\).

Run \(i\) occupies the slot interval from \(H_{i-1}\) to \(H_i\), excluding the upper endpoint.

Call a block exceptional if an internal run boundary lies strictly inside it. Each internal boundary belongs to at most one such block, so there are at most \(R-1\) exceptional blocks. Their indices are precisely the distinct integers

\(\lfloor H_i/K\rfloor\)

arising from boundaries \(H_i\) not divisible by \(K\), for \(1\le i<R\).

For an exceptional block of index \(j\), its number of slots from run \(i\) is

\(w_{i,j}=\max\{0,\min(H_i,(j+1)K)-\max(H_{i-1},jK)\}\).

Its bundle is therefore

\(b^j=\sum_{i=1}^{R}w_{i,j}d^i\),

and it receives probability \(1/N\).

Every other block lies wholly inside one run. The number of complete blocks contained in run \(i\) is

\(C_i=\max\{0,\lfloor H_i/K\rfloor-\lceil H_{i-1}/K\rceil\}\).

When \(C_i>0\), output the bundle \(Kd^i\) with probability \(C_i/N\).

These two kinds of blocks exhaust the partition:

  • a block with an internal run boundary is exceptional;
  • a block without such a boundary lies entirely inside one run.

Thus the resulting distribution \(\lambda^{(t)}\) has total probability one and at most \(2R-1=O(m)\) supported bundles. Its expectation is exactly \(q\), because the blocks partition the virtual multiset:

\(\sum_b\lambda^{(t)}_b b_e=M_e/N=q_e\), for every \(e\).

Moreover, every supported bundle has the explicitly constructed packing into at most \(K\) bins. Hence

\(p_t(b)\le K\le2\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\)

whenever \(\lambda^{(t)}_b>0\).

5. Combining types and checking bit complexity

Perform this construction independently for every supported type, and set

\(\pi_{t,b}=\mu_t\lambda^{(t)}_b\).

Then

\(\sum_b\pi_{t,b}=\mu_t\),

and, for every chore class \(e\),

\(\sum_{t,b}\pi_{t,b}b_e =\sum_t\mu_tq_e =q_e\).

All positive allocation masses satisfy the \(2\)-MMS bound established above.

For the running time and output size:

  • Each normalized size \(a_e\) and the rational number \(v\) can be computed exactly with polynomial bit length. For example, a common denominator obtained by multiplying the denominators of the finitely many summands already has polynomial bit length.
  • The integers \(Q,D,N,K,M_e\), and \(NK\) consequently have polynomial bit length.
  • The packing uses \(O(m)\) runs. Their patterns, multiplicities, and cumulative endpoints have polynomial bit length.
  • There are \(O(m)\) exceptional blocks. Computing their overlaps with the \(O(m)\) runs takes \(O(m^2)\) arithmetic operations.
  • Every output bundle uses a subset of the virtual copies, so its coordinate \(b_e\) is at most \(M_e\). Every output probability, and its product with \(\mu_t\), has polynomial bit length.

Integer arithmetic, exact rational arithmetic, and sorting polynomial-length integers all take polynomial time. Therefore the full algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and outputs \(O(\tau m)\) supported type–bundle pairs.

6. Why exact fairness exists, but was not needed computationally

For completeness, benchmark-attaining distributions can be chosen rational.

Fix a type and any finite support attaining its benchmark. The probabilities on this support satisfy the rational linear system

\(\sum_b\lambda_b=1\), and \(\sum_b\lambda_b b=q\),

together with nonnegativity. If its positive-support columns \((1,b)\) are linearly dependent, take a nonzero dependence vector. Its coordinates sum to zero, so it has both positive and negative coordinates. Moving the probability vector along this dependence until one positive probability becomes zero preserves feasibility and reduces support. Repeating leaves linearly independent columns.

On those remaining columns the solution is unique and rational: select an invertible square subsystem and apply exact rational linear algebra. The support still consists entirely of benchmark-admissible bundles.

Multiplying each type’s resulting distribution by \(\mu_t\) therefore gives an exact \(1\)-MMS allocation. Finding those benchmark distributions efficiently is unnecessary for our \(2\)-MMS algorithm.

Accordingly, the factor-two nonexistence obstruction from the [discrete model](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/aa5d22c77b380e2261332bb641b3c2e3-Paper-Conference.pdf) does not transfer to this formulation. Here type mass can be split among bundles, and the construction above also resolves the requested computational guarantee.

Step-by-step verification

1. Benchmark attainment and lower bound

This section is correct.

Independent floor-or-ceiling rounding gives a finitely supported distribution with expectation \(q\), including when some coordinates are integers. Every resulting bundle has finite packing cost because each copy fits individually into a bin.

Every feasible distribution has a nonempty supported bundle because \(q_e>0\). Its maximum packing cost is therefore a positive integer. The set of attainable objective values is a nonempty subset of the positive integers, so its minimum exists and is attained by a feasible distribution.

The capacity inequality
\[ \sum_e a_e b_e\le p_t(b) \]
also holds for bundles containing zero-size copies. Taking expectations establishes \(v\le k\). Consequently,
\[ K=\max\{1,\lceil 2v\rceil\}\le 2k. \]

No computation of the benchmark is required.

2. Next-fit bound and choice of virtual population

The next-fit analysis is correct.

When a new bin opens, its first copy failed to fit in the preceding bin. The final loads of those two bins therefore sum to more than one. Pairing consecutive bins proves \(B<2V+1\), including the one-bin case.

The rounding argument correctly yields \(B\le NK\):

  • If \(2v\) is integral and \(v>0\), then \(K=2v\), and integrality converts \(B<NK+1\) into \(B\le NK\).
  • Otherwise, writing \(v=A/D\) shows that \(K-2v\) is a positive integer multiple of \(1/D\). Thus \(N\ge D\) implies \(N(K-2v)\ge1\), which absorbs the additive term.

The zero-size cases are handled correctly. If \(v>0\), zero-size copies can share the first occupied bin. If \(v=0\), positivity of all supplies implies that every class has zero size; the virtual multiset is nonempty and fits in one bin under the stated convention.

Minor issue 1: The instruction to choose any \(r\) satisfying \(2^r\ge D\) does not itself guarantee polynomial bit length. An unnecessarily large choice could make \(N\) enormous even in binary representation.

Minimum clarification: Specify \(r=\lceil\log_2 D\rceil\), or explicitly require a choice bounded by the bit length of \(D\).

3. Compressed next-fit implementation

The compression correctly reproduces next fit.

For a positive-size class, the formula for \(h\) gives exactly the number of remaining copies that fit in the current bin. If copies remain afterward, that bin is nonempty: an empty bin would accommodate at least one copy because \(a_e\le1\).

For \(d_e=\lfloor1/a_e\rfloor\), the formula
\[ w=\left\lfloor\frac{n'-1}{d_e}\right\rfloor \]
leaves between one and \(d_e\) copies in the final open bin. It correctly handles exact multiples of \(d_e\), retaining the last full bin as the open bin.

Each class creates at most one closed mixed bin and one pure-bin run, followed by one final open bin. The number of runs is therefore \(O(m)\). Appending zero-size copies requires at most one additional split.

Minor issue 2: A computed run multiplicity \(w\) can be zero, and the padding length \(NK-B\) can also be zero. Including such runs would contradict the strictly increasing cumulative endpoints assumed in Section 4.

Minimum clarification: Omit every zero-length run, including zero-length padding.

4. Grouping and distribution compression

The grouping formulas are correct once runs have positive lengths.

A boundary strictly inside a block identifies exactly one exceptional block, with index \(\lfloor H_i/K\rfloor\). Boundaries divisible by \(K\) do not create exceptional blocks. Deduplicating these indices correctly handles several boundaries in the same block.

The overlap formula counts the slots belonging to each run. The formula
\[ C_i=\max\{0,\lfloor H_i/K\rfloor-\lceil H_{i-1}/K\rceil\} \]
correctly counts complete blocks contained in run \(i\), including endpoints aligned with block boundaries.

Exceptional blocks and complete blocks inside runs exhaust the partition without overlap. Hence their weights sum to one, and counting the virtual copies gives expectation exactly \(q\). Each block inherits a packing using at most \(K\) bins. Empty blocks have cost zero; blocks containing only zero-size copies still have the required one-bin packing.

Minor issue 3: Different block categories can produce the same bundle vector. The write-up does not explicitly say to add their probabilities when forming the indexed distribution \(\lambda_b^{(t)}\).

For example, with one class, \(a_1=1\) and \(q_1=3/2\), the smallest permitted choice gives \(N=4\) and \(K=3\). The described unmerged run representation produces two exceptional blocks with the same bundle of three copies, each assigned probability \(1/4\).

Minimum clarification: Aggregate identical bundle vectors by summing their weights. This preserves the support bound and all stated identities.

5. Combining types and polynomial complexity

The allocation identities are correct:
\[ \sum_b\pi_{t,b}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,b}\pi_{t,b}b_e=\sum_t\mu_tq_e=q_e. \]

Multiplication by positive rational \(\mu_t\) preserves rationality and the established fairness bound.

With the bounded choice of \(r\) specified above, the bit-complexity argument is sound. Products of input denominators have polynomial bit length; exact normalized sizes, volumes, multiplicities, endpoints, and bundle coordinates likewise have polynomial bit length. The potentially large numerical values are never enumerated.

The \(O(m^2)\) overlap computation per type and aggregation of polynomially many bundle vectors take polynomial time. The resulting support bound remains \(O(\tau m)\).

6. Exact \(1\)-MMS existence

The additional existence claim is correct.

Starting from an attained benchmark distribution, a dependence among its positive-support columns has coefficients summing to zero and therefore has both signs. Moving along that dependence until a probability first reaches zero preserves feasibility and nonnegativity while reducing support.

After finitely many reductions, the remaining columns are independent. Selecting an invertible square subsystem shows that their unique feasible probabilities are rational. No bundle outside the original benchmark-admissible support is introduced.

Combining these distributions across types therefore establishes exact \(1\)-MMS existence. This argument makes no unsupported claim that those distributions can be found efficiently.

The target is resolved subject only to the three routine clarifications above. The construction supplies both existence and a polynomial-time algorithm for the requested factor-two allocation; no hardness or nonexistence obstruction remains within this formulation.
