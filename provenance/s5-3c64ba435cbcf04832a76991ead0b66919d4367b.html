The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite sets of national buyer types, international buyer types, and seller/lot types with rational masses, national gains from trade, international rankings, and seller spot prices, together with a feasible international type-to-seller flow \(F\), determine whether there is a quasi-stable rational cohort allocation realizing \(F\) whose vector of mean seller payoffs is componentwise least among all such allocations; if so, compute one, and otherwise report that no least vector exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) national buyer, international buyer, and seller types with positive binary-rational masses. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m\geq 1\) be the number of seller types and \(L\) the total input bit length. Masses need not sum to one, and the two sides may have unequal total mass.
Each buyer can acquire one lot and each seller supplies one lot. National type \(i\) has rational gain \(g_{ij}\geq 0\) from seller type \(j\); seller type \(j\) has rational international spot price \(b_j\geq 0\). International types strictly rank seller types and the unmatched option \(0\); only types ranked above \(0\) are acceptable. Seller reservation payoffs are zero.
The input includes a nonnegative rational international flow \(F\), supported on acceptable pairs, whose row sums do not exceed international buyer masses and whose column sums do not exceed seller masses.
A rational cohort allocation partitions every type's mass into finitely many positive rational cohorts, each with a constant assignment and constant rational payoff. Matched buyer and seller cohorts have equal mass; every remaining cohort is unmatched. Cohorts of the same type may have different assignments and payoffs.
All monetary payoffs are nonnegative. A national match between types \(i,j\) has buyer payoff \(u\) and seller payoff \(v\) satisfying \(u+v=g_{ij}\); an international match gives the seller \(b_j\) and the buyer the assigned seller type. Unmatched national buyers receive zero; unmatched sellers may have positive payoff.
An allocation is quasi-stable if, for every positive-mass national buyer cohort of type \(i\) with payoff \(u\) and every seller cohort of type \(j\) with payoff \(v\), \(u+v\geq g_{ij}\); and, for every international buyer cohort with assignment \(k\), including \(k=0\), and every seller cohort of type \(j\), either \(k\succeq_i j\) or \(v\geq b_j\). Thus blocking requires strict improvement by both agents; no tie-breaking is imposed.
Realizing \(F\) means that the total mass of international matches on each pair \((i,j)\) equals \(F_{ij}\). For seller mass \(s_j\), define its mean payoff by \(\bar v_j=s_j^{-1}\sum_{c:\,\operatorname{type}(c)=j}w_cv_c\), including unmatched cohorts. A least vector is attained by an allocation and is componentwise no larger than the mean vector of every quasi-stable rational cohort allocation realizing \(F\).

Is deciding whether such a least vector exists, computing an allocation attaining it when it does, and reporting nonexistence otherwise solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

Known discrete result: a finite ascending-price mechanism produces an allocation whose seller payoff vector is componentwise least among quasi-stable allocations preserving its international assignments; its statement does not establish a polynomial bound in binary input length. [Sotomayor (2003)](https://doi.org/10.12660/bre.v23n22003.2727)
No result settling the exact rational-cohort, prescribed-flow problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The mass normalization was unspecified; masses are arbitrary positive binary rationals, with potentially unequal totals across sides.
  • Rational cohort allocation was undefined; it means a finite rational partition with constant assignments and payoffs, allowing heterogeneous outcomes within a type and equal masses on matched cohorts.
  • Quasi-stability was undefined for masses; the discrete pairwise inequalities apply to every pair of positive-mass cohorts, including unmatched cohorts, rather than to type means.
  • Domains and outside options were unspecified; national gains and spot prices are nonnegative rationals, international rankings are strict and include the unmatched option, and seller reservation payoffs are zero.
  • Flow feasibility means capacity feasibility on acceptable international pairs, without promising a quasi-stable extension.
  • Mean seller payoffs include all seller mass, including unmatched cohorts whose nonnegative payoff may be positive under quasi-feasibility; leastness is attained and compares all rational cohort allocations realizing the prescribed flow.

Result

The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\).

More precisely, whenever any quasi-stable rational cohort allocation realizing \(F\) exists, a least mean seller-payoff vector exists. Two rational linear programs suffice to compute an allocation attaining it or to certify nonexistence.

The proof must account for unmatched sellers receiving positive payoffs. We do this explicitly, without imposing a market-clearing condition on arbitrary allocations.

1. Prices and the prescribed international flow

Let \(N\) and \(I\) be the sets of national and international buyer types. Write their positive masses as \(a_i\) and \(h_\ell\), respectively.

For each seller type \(j\), define its internationally assigned mass and residual mass by

\[ \text{ } \]

\(f_j=\sum_{\ell\in I}F_{\ell j},\qquad t_j=s_j-f_j.\)

For an international type \(\ell\), the assignments occurring with positive mass are precisely

\(K_\ell=\{k:F_{\ell k}>0\}\ \cup\ \{0:h_\ell-\sum_jF_{\ell j}>0\}.\)

Thus these assignment sets are determined by the input.

Define a nonnegative price floor \(d_j\) as follows:

  • Set \(d_j=b_j\) if \(t_j=0\), or if some \(\ell\in I\) and \(k\in K_\ell\) satisfy \(j\succ_\ell k\).
  • Otherwise set \(d_j=0\).

The second condition records exactly when international blocking requires every seller cohort of type \(j\) to receive at least \(b_j\). The first condition records that, when \(t_j=0\), every seller of type \(j\) is internationally matched and therefore receives \(b_j\).

Rigidity of national matches

Consider any quasi-stable allocation realizing \(F\). Because its cohort partitions are finite, the following minima are attained:

\(u_i=\min\{\text{payoff of a national buyer cohort of type }i\},\)

\(p_j=\min\{\text{payoff of a seller cohort of type }j\}.\)

National quasi-stability implies

\(u_i+p_j\geq g_{ij}\qquad\text{for every }i\in N,\ j.\)

Suppose a national match between types \(i,j\) gives payoffs \(u,v\). Then

\(g_{ij}=u+v\geq u_i+p_j\geq g_{ij}.\)

Consequently,

\(u=u_i,\qquad v=p_j,\qquad u_i+p_j=g_{ij}.\)

Thus every nationally matched cohort receives its type’s minimum payoff. If a national type has any unmatched mass, its minimum payoff is zero.

Let \(x_{ij}\) be the total national matching mass on pair \(i,j\). We therefore have

\(\sum_jx_{ij}\leq a_i,\qquad \sum_ix_{ij}\leq t_j,\qquad x_{ij}\geq0,\)

\(x_{ij}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u_i+p_j=g_{ij},\)

\(u_i>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_jx_{ij}=a_i.\)

Moreover,

\(p_j\geq d_j,\qquad f_j>0\ \Longrightarrow\ p_j\leq b_j.\)

The upper bound follows because positive international matching mass supplies seller cohorts whose payoff is exactly \(b_j\).

Define the national utility function at prices \(p\) by

\(U_i(p)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{g_{ij}-p_j:1\leq j\leq m\}\bigr).\)

We claim that \(u_i=U_i(p)\). The blocking inequalities give \(u_i\geq U_i(p)\). If type \(i\) has a national match, its equality on that pair gives the reverse inequality. If it has no national match, all its cohorts are unmatched, so \(u_i=0\); again equality follows.

Call \(p\geq d\) a supporting price vector if there is a nonnegative rational matrix \(x\) satisfying the capacity inequalities above and

\(x_{ij}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ U_i(p)+p_j=g_{ij},\)

\(U_i(p)>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_jx_{ij}=a_i.\)

Every quasi-stable rational allocation therefore supplies supporting prices satisfying the international upper bounds.

Finally, its mean seller payoffs obey

\(\bar v_j\geq \dfrac{f_jb_j+t_jp_j}{s_j}.\)

Indeed, international seller mass receives exactly \(b_j\), while all remaining seller cohorts receive at least \(p_j\).

2. A least supporting price vector

We first omit the international upper bounds. The floors \(d\) remain imposed.

Consider the fractional assignment linear program:

\(W=\max_x\sum_{i\in N}\sum_j(g_{ij}-d_j)x_{ij},\)

subject to

\(x_{ij}\geq0,\qquad \sum_jx_{ij}\leq a_i,\qquad \sum_ix_{ij}\leq t_j.\)

Its feasible set is nonempty and compact: the zero matrix is feasible, and every coordinate is bounded by the corresponding buyer mass. Hence its finite optimum is attained.

Its dual, writing the seller dual variables as \(p_j-d_j\), is

\(\min_{u,p}\ \sum_i a_iu_i+\sum_jt_j(p_j-d_j),\)

subject to

\(u_i\geq0,\qquad p_j\geq d_j,\qquad u_i+p_j\geq g_{ij}.\)

Strong linear-programming duality gives dual optimum \(W\).

For fixed \(p\geq d\), the smallest feasible buyer variables are \(u_i=U_i(p)\). Since every \(a_i\) is positive, every dual optimum uses these smallest variables. Thus the optimal dual price vectors are exactly the minimizers, over \(p\geq d\), of

\(H(p)=\sum_i a_iU_i(p)+\sum_jt_j(p_j-d_j).\)

We establish two properties of this function.

2.1. Closure of minimizers under componentwise minimum

For two price vectors \(p,q\), write

\(r=p\wedge q,\qquad z=p\vee q,\)

where minimum and maximum are taken componentwise.

For each national type,

\(U_i(r)=\max\{U_i(p),U_i(q)\},\)

because \(g_{ij}-\min\{p_j,q_j\}=\max\{g_{ij}-p_j,g_{ij}-q_j\}\).

Also, \(z\geq p,q\), so

\(U_i(z)\leq\min\{U_i(p),U_i(q)\}.\)

Consequently,

\(U_i(r)+U_i(z)\leq U_i(p)+U_i(q).\)

The linear seller term satisfies equality under componentwise minimum and maximum. Therefore,

\(H(p\wedge q)+H(p\vee q)\leq H(p)+H(q).\)

In particular, if \(p,q\) minimize \(H\), then both their componentwise minimum and maximum minimize \(H\): neither can have value below \(W\), and their sum is at most \(2W\).

Choose a dual optimum \((u^*,p^*)\) minimizing \(\sum_jp_j\) among all dual optima. Such an optimum exists: this is a nonempty rational linear program with objective bounded below by \(\sum_jd_j\).

Then \(p^*\) is componentwise least among all minimizers of \(H\). Indeed, if another minimizer \(q\) had \(q_j<p_j^*\) in any coordinate, \(p^*\wedge q\) would also minimize \(H\) and would have strictly smaller price sum, a contradiction.

2.2. Comparison with arbitrary supporting prices

This step handles allocations that leave sellers unmatched at positive prices.

Let \(p\geq d\) be any supporting price vector, and let \(x\) support it. Write \(c_j=\sum_ix_{ij}\), so \(c_j\leq t_j\).

The support conditions imply

\(\sum_i a_iU_i(p)=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}(g_{ij}-p_j).\)

To justify this equality, every used pair has \(g_{ij}-p_j=U_i(p)\); any unassigned buyer mass has \(U_i(p)=0\).

For any \(q\geq p\), the definition of \(U_i(q)\) gives \(U_i(q)\geq g_{ij}-q_j\). Nonnegativity and the buyer capacity inequalities therefore imply

\(\sum_i a_iU_i(q)\geq\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}(g_{ij}-q_j).\)

Subtracting yields

\(H(q)-H(p)\geq\sum_j(t_j-c_j)(q_j-p_j)\geq0.\)

Thus \(H(q)\geq H(p)\) whenever \(q\geq p\).

Now apply the inequality from Section 2.1 to \(p^*,p\). Since \(p^*\vee p\geq p\),

\(H(p^*\wedge p)+H(p^*\vee p)\leq W+H(p),\)

and

\(H(p^*\vee p)\geq H(p).\)

Hence \(H(p^*\wedge p)\leq W\). It must equal \(W\), so \(p^*\wedge p\) is a minimizer of \(H\). The least-minimizer property proved above gives

\(p^*\leq p^*\wedge p\leq p.\)

We have proved:

Price lemma. The vector \(p^*\) is componentwise no larger than every supporting price vector, including those supported by allocations with unused seller capacity at positive prices.

3. The algorithm and its correctness

Solve the assignment linear program to obtain an optimal matrix \(x^*\) and its value \(W\).

Next solve the following second linear program:

\(\min_{u,p}\sum_jp_j,\)

subject to

\(u_i\geq0,\qquad p_j\geq d_j,\qquad u_i+p_j\geq g_{ij},\)

\(\sum_i a_iu_i+\sum_jt_j(p_j-d_j)=W.\)

Its optimal prices are exactly the vector \(p^*\) constructed above.

Nonexistence test

If \(p_j^*>b_j\) for some \(j\) with \(f_j>0\), report nonexistence.

This conclusion is rigorous. Any quasi-stable allocation realizing \(F\) would yield supporting prices \(p\) with

\(p_j^*\leq p_j\leq b_j,\)

contradicting \(p_j^*>b_j\).

Allocation construction

Suppose instead that

\(p_j^*\leq b_j\qquad\text{whenever }f_j>0.\)

For the optimal primal matrix \(x^*\) and optimal dual pair \((u^*,p^*)\), the duality gap decomposes as

\(0=\sum_i u_i^*\bigl(a_i-\sum_jx_{ij}^*\bigr)+\sum_j(p_j^*-d_j)\bigl(t_j-\sum_ix_{ij}^*\bigr)+\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}^*(u_i^*+p_j^*-g_{ij}).\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Thus

\(x_{ij}^*>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u_i^*+p_j^*=g_{ij},\)

and

\(a_i-\sum_jx_{ij}^*>0\ \Longrightarrow\ u_i^*=0.\)

Construct cohorts as follows, omitting every zero-mass cohort:

  • For each \(x_{ij}^*>0\), nationally match buyer and seller cohorts of that mass, with payoffs \(u_i^*\) and \(p_j^*\).
  • Leave residual national buyer mass unmatched, with payoff zero.
  • Realize each international match with its prescribed mass \(F_{\ell j}\), giving the seller payoff \(b_j\); leave residual international buyer mass unmatched.
  • Leave residual seller mass \(t_j-\sum_ix_{ij}^*\) unmatched, with payoff \(p_j^*\).

All payoffs are nonnegative, all type masses are accounted for, and every national match satisfies its payoff equation.

Every national cohort of type \(i\) receives \(u_i^*\), including unmatched cohorts because their existence forces \(u_i^*=0\). Every seller cohort of type \(j\) receives at least \(p_j^*\): internationally matched cohorts receive \(b_j\geq p_j^*\). Therefore national quasi-stability follows from

\(u_i^*+p_j^*\geq g_{ij}.\)

For international quasi-stability, consider an occurring assignment \(k\in K_\ell\). If \(j\succ_\ell k\), then the definition of \(d_j\) gives

\(p_j^*\geq d_j=b_j.\)

Every seller cohort of type \(j\) consequently receives at least \(b_j\). If \(j\) is not strictly preferred to \(k\), the other clause of international quasi-stability holds. This verifies all international blocking conditions.

The constructed allocation is therefore quasi-stable and realizes \(F\).

Leastness of its mean vector

Its mean seller payoffs are exactly

\(\bar v_j^*=\dfrac{f_jb_j+t_jp_j^*}{s_j}.\)

Any other quasi-stable rational cohort allocation realizing \(F\) yields supporting prices \(p\). By the price lemma, \(p^*\leq p\), and by Section 1,

\(\bar v_j\geq\dfrac{f_jb_j+t_jp_j}{s_j}\geq\dfrac{f_jb_j+t_jp_j^*}{s_j}=\bar v_j^*.\)

Thus the constructed allocation attains a componentwise least mean vector.

4. Polynomial bit complexity

Let \(n=|N|\) and \(r=|I|\), so \(n+r+m=\tau\).

The first linear program has \(nm\) variables and \(n+m\) capacity inequalities, besides nonnegativity. The second has \(n+m\) variables and \(nm\) buyer–seller inequalities, besides bounds and one equality. Computing the masses \(f_j,t_j\), the sets \(K_\ell\), and the floors \(d_j\) requires polynomially many rational operations and ranking comparisons.

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: exact optimization takes polynomial time in the program’s dimensions and binary encoding length, and attained optima admit rational optimal solutions of polynomial encoding length. Polynomial-time linear programming is established by [Khachiyan’s theorem](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Both programs have polynomial encoding length in \(m,\tau,L\); the optimal value \(W\) used in the second program also has polynomial encoding length.

The output construction uses at most \(nm+rm+n+r+m\) positive matching or unmatched cohort records, with polynomially encoded rational masses and payoffs. It therefore has polynomial size and can be produced in polynomial time.

This proves the claimed polynomial-time decision and construction algorithm.
