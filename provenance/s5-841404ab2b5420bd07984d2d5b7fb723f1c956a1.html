The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) with support \(T\) of size \(\tau\), and a bounded non-atomic rational step-function valuation density \(v_t\) for each \(t\in T\), decide whether there is a labelled partition of \([0,1]\) using \(\tau-1\) cuts such that every type satisfies \(D_t \le \epsilon_0\), where \(D_t=\left|\int_{O^+}v_t(x)\,dx-\int_{O^-}v_t(x)\,dx\right|\) and \(\epsilon_0\) is the constant from Theorem 3.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq 1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu(t)\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has an explicitly encoded, bounded, nonnegative rational step-function density \(v_t\) on \([0,1]\), with binary rational breakpoints and heights. Valuations are non-atomic and need not integrate to \(1\); no common bound independent of the input is imposed. Let \(m\) be the total number of encoded step intervals and \(L\) the total input bit length.
Fix a positive rational constant \(\epsilon_0\), independent of the input. A labelled partition uses at most \(\tau-1\) distinct real cut positions; each resulting interval independently receives a label in \(\{+,-\}\). Let \(O^+\) and \(O^-\) be the unions with the respective labels; endpoint assignments do not affect valuations.
Decide whether such a partition exists with \(D_t=\left|\int_{O^+}v_t(x)\,dx-\int_{O^-}v_t(x)\,dx\right|\leq\epsilon_0\) for every \(t\in T\). Masses do not weight these individual constraints.

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite lists of \(n\) agents with nonnegative step-function valuations, existence of an approximate consensus-halving partition with \(n-1\) cuts is known to be \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for a positive constant discrepancy. [Published result](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2018.24).
The distribution formulation retains exactly these feasibility constraints and the corresponding cut budget; consequently, it does not supply a distinct open complexity question.

Result

For every fixed positive rational \(\epsilon_0\), the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Consequently, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Published hardness result

We use the hardness result supplied in the task: for a suitable fixed positive rational discrepancy \(\delta\), deciding whether \(n\) agents with explicitly encoded nonnegative rational step-function densities admit an approximate consensus-halving partition with \(n-1\) cuts is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This is Theorem 5 of Filos-Ratsikas, Frederiksen, Goldberg, and Zhang, *Hardness Results for Consensus-Halving*. Their decision formulation uses the inequalities \(\left|u_i(O^+)-u_i(O^-)\right|\leq\delta\), independently labelled intervals, and identifies coincident cut positions. [Published paper, Sections 2 and 4](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/paul.goldberg/papers/LIPIcs-MFCS-2018-24.pdf).

The distinction between exactly \(n-1\) and at most \(n-1\) cuts does not affect feasibility here. Given fewer cuts, insert additional distinct interior cuts and give both resulting subintervals their original label. This preserves every valuation.

Reduction to a society

Let \(f_1,\ldots,f_n\) be the densities of an instance of that hard problem. Construct distinct indexed types \(T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_n\}\), with \(\tau=n\), and set
\[ \mu(t_i)=\frac1n,\qquad v_{t_i}(x)=\frac{\epsilon_0}{\delta}f_i(x). \]

Here and below, these formulas mean \(\mu(t_i)=1/n\) and \(v_{t_i}(x)=(\epsilon_0/\delta)f_i(x)\). The masses are positive binary-encoded rationals and sum to \(1\).

Each constructed density is nonnegative and bounded because its source density has these properties and the scaling factor is positive and finite. Its breakpoints remain unchanged, and its heights remain rational. Thus the construction satisfies the required valuation representation. Scaling also preserves non-atomicity.

For any labelled partition \(P=(O^+,O^-)\), define the source discrepancy by \(d_i(P)=\left|\int_{O^+}f_i(x)\,dx-\int_{O^-}f_i(x)\,dx\right|\). Linearity of integration and positivity of the scaling factor give
\[ D_{t_i}(P)=\frac{\epsilon_0}{\delta}d_i(P). \]

Equivalently, \(D_{t_i}(P)=(\epsilon_0/\delta)d_i(P)\). Since \(\epsilon_0\) and \(\delta\) are positive, \(D_{t_i}(P)\leq\epsilon_0\) holds if and only if \(d_i(P)\leq\delta\).

Moreover, the target cut budget is \(\tau-1=n-1\). Therefore the same partition is feasible in the source instance if and only if it is feasible in the constructed society. The masses do not enter this equivalence because the individual discrepancy constraints are unweighted.

Encoding size and conclusion

The reduction preserves the number of encoded step intervals. Multiplication by the fixed rational \(\epsilon_0/\delta\) increases each height’s encoding length by only a constant amount. Encoding all masses and type identifiers requires \(O(n\log n)\) bits. Hence the construction has polynomial size and is computable in polynomial time.

This proves the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. The absence of normalization and of a common input-independent density bound permits the scaling for every fixed positive rational tolerance. The distribution formulation therefore retains the known hard feasibility problem.
