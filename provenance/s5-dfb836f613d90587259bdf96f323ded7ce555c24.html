The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(k\) latent task types, \(m\) workers with known response probabilities \(\pi_i\), and exact observed profile masses \(\eta_z\) for \(z\in\{0,\ldots,k\}^m\), generated by \(\eta_z=\sum_{t=1}^k\mu_t\prod_{i=1}^m q_{i,t}(z_i)\), where \(q_{i,t}(0)=1-\pi_i\) and \(q_{i,t}(c)=\pi_i(C_i)_{tc}\), compute \(\widehat\mu\), worker confusion matrices \(\widehat C_i\), and a classifier \(a(z)\) with small population loss \(R(a)=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_z\prod_i q_{i,t}(z_i)\mathbf{1}[a(z)\ne t]\), under the paper's positivity, rank, diagonal-gap, and separation assumptions.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) observed profile types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible profiles. Masses sum to \(1\); omitted profiles have mass \(0\).
Given \(k\ge2\), \(m\ge3\), known binary-rational response probabilities \(0<\pi_i\le1\), and profile masses \(\eta_z\) for \(z\in\{0,\ldots,k\}^m\), promise that \(\eta_z=\sum_{t=1}^k\mu_t\prod_{i=1}^m q_{i,t}(z_i)\), where \(q_{i,t}(0)=1-\pi_i\), \(q_{i,t}(c)=\pi_i(C_i)_{tc}\), \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and each unknown \(C_i\) is row-stochastic.
A partition of the workers into three nonempty groups \(G_1,G_2,G_3\) is supplied. Define \(A_g=|G_g|^{-1}\sum_{i\in G_g}\pi_i C_i\); each \(A_g\) is nonsingular and \(\kappa=\min_{g,t,c\ne t}((A_g)_{tt}-(A_g)_{tc})>0\).
For a supplied binary rational \(0<\rho<1/k\), promise \((C_i)_{tc}\ge\rho\) and \(D=\min_{t\ne u}m^{-1}\sum_i\pi_i D_{\mathrm{KL}}((C_i)_{t,:},(C_i)_{u,:})>0\).
Given binary-rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), compute rational probability estimates satisfying \(\|\widehat\mu-\mu\|_\infty\le\varepsilon\) and \(\max_{i,t,c}|(\widehat C_i)_{tc}-(C_i)_{tc}|\le\varepsilon\), with class indices fixed by the diagonal-gap condition.
Also compute a deterministic classifier \(a:\{0,\ldots,k\}^m\to\{1,\ldots,k\}\), represented by its labels on the supplied support and label \(1\) elsewhere, satisfying \(R(a)\le R^*+\varepsilon\), where \(R(a)=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_z\prod_i q_{i,t}(z_i)\mathbf1[a(z)\ne t]\) and \(R^*=\min_b R(b)\). Ties may be resolved arbitrarily. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including \(\rho\) and \(\varepsilon\).

Is this population recovery and classification problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,k,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite sampled items, accurate initialization followed by EM yields exact recovery of all sampled labels with high probability and confusion-row squared error of order \(\log(mk/\delta)/(\pi_i\mu_t n)\), under sufficient worker and sample sizes. [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.3824v3)

1. Claim

The promised recovery and classification problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,k,\tau,L\), in the standard binary Turing model.

The proof uses exact rational moments, a deterministic spectral separation procedure, and certified computations with univariate real algebraic numbers. It requires no supplied numerical lower bounds on the diagonal gap, singular values, or class masses.

Write \(S\) for the supplied support and \([k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}\). All computations below use the masses on \(S\).

The positivity promise actually determines which profiles have positive mass. Each positive response symbol has positive conditional probability in every class; response \(0\) has positive probability exactly when \(\pi_i<1\). Thus, if \(r\) workers have \(\pi_i<1\), then \(\tau=k^{m-r}(k+1)^r\). The algorithm below scans the supplied support directly.

2. Exact observable moments

Introduce a latent variable \(Y\) with \(\Pr(Y=t)=\mu_t\). Conditional on \(Y=t\), the worker responses are independent and have probabilities \(q_{i,t}\), as in the promise.

Let \(e_c\) be the \(c\)-th standard basis vector of \(\mathbb R^k\), and define

\(X_i(z)=e_{z_i}\) when \(z_i\in[k]\), and \(X_i(z)=0\) when \(z_i=0\).

For each supplied group, put

\(X_g=|G_g|^{-1}\sum_{i\in G_g}X_i,\qquad s_g=|G_g|^{-1}\sum_{i\in G_g}\pi_i,\qquad U_g=A_g^{\mathsf T}.\)

Write \(a_{g,t}=U_ge_t\). Then

\(\mathbb E[X_g\mid Y=t]=a_{g,t}.\)

Because each row of every \(C_i\) sums to one,

\(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}a_{g,t}=s_g>0.\)

Let \(D_\mu=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_k)\). Define the observable quantities

\(v=\mathbb E[X_1],\qquad M_{gh}=\mathbb E[X_gX_h^{\mathsf T}]\quad(g\ne h),\)

and the third-order tensor

\(\mathcal T_{abc}=\mathbb E[(X_1)_a(X_2)_b(X_3)_c].\)

The group vectors depend on disjoint sets of responses, so they are conditionally independent given \(Y\). Consequently,

\(v=U_1\mu,\qquad M_{gh}=U_gD_\mu U_h^{\mathsf T},\)

and

\(\mathcal T_{abc}=\sum_{t=1}^k\mu_t(U_1)_{at}(U_2)_{bt}(U_3)_{ct}.\)

Every one of these quantities is computable exactly from the support. For example,

\(M_{gh}=\sum_{z\in S}\eta_zX_g(z)X_h(z)^{\mathsf T}.\)

Since every \(U_g\) is nonsingular and every \(\mu_t\) is positive, every cross-group matrix \(M_{gh}\) is nonsingular.

3. Rational maps recovering individual workers

For each worker \(i\), choose a group excluding that worker by setting

\(g(i)=2\) if \(i\notin G_2\), and \(g(i)=3\) if \(i\in G_2\).

Compute

\(H_i=\mathbb E[X_iX_{g(i)}^{\mathsf T}],\qquad F_i=H_iM_{1,g(i)}^{-1}.\)

These are observable rational matrices. With \(W_i=\pi_iC_i^{\mathsf T}\), conditional independence gives

\(H_i=W_iD_\mu U_{g(i)}^{\mathsf T}.\)

Using the cross-group moment identity and its nonsingularity,

\(F_i=W_iD_\mu U_{g(i)}^{\mathsf T}(U_1D_\mu U_{g(i)}^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=W_iU_1^{-1}.\)

Thus recovering the columns of \(U_1\), with their class weights, will recover every worker through a known rational linear map.

4. Deterministically separating the latent components

For \(h\in\mathbb Q^k\), contract the tensor in its third coordinate:

\(\mathcal T(h)_{ab}=\sum_{c=1}^kh_c\mathcal T_{abc}.\)

The moment identities imply

\(\mathcal T(h)=U_1D_\mu\operatorname{diag}(U_3^{\mathsf T}h)U_2^{\mathsf T}.\)

Therefore the observable rational matrix

\(B(h)=\mathcal T(h)M_{12}^{-1}\)

satisfies

\(B(h)=U_1\operatorname{diag}(U_3^{\mathsf T}h)U_1^{-1}.\)

We now find a suitable \(h\) without randomness.

For an integer \(s\), let

\(h(s)=(1,s,s^2,\ldots,s^{k-1})^{\mathsf T}.\)

The eigenvalue corresponding to class \(t\) is

\(\lambda_t(s)=\sum_{c=1}^k(A_3)_{tc}s^{c-1}.\)

For \(t\ne u\), the polynomial \(\lambda_t(s)-\lambda_u(s)\) is nonzero: otherwise the two corresponding rows of \(A_3\) would coincide, contradicting nonsingularity. Its degree is at most \(k-1\), so it has at most \(k-1\) real roots.

It follows that at most

\(N=(k-1)\binom{k}{2}\)

integers can cause any eigenvalue collision. Among the \(N+1\) integers \(0,\ldots,N\), at least one therefore yields pairwise distinct eigenvalues.

For each candidate, compute

\(p_s(x)=\det(xI-B(h(s))).\)

Test whether \(\gcd(p_s,p_s')=1\), using exact polynomial arithmetic. This test succeeds exactly when the eigenvalues are distinct.

Choose the first successful candidate, and abbreviate its matrix and characteristic polynomial by \(B\) and \(p\). Then:

  • \(p\) has degree \(k\);
  • all its roots are real and simple;
  • its roots correspond bijectively to the latent classes.

The search uses only polynomially many candidates.

5. Exact algebraic recovery and class alignment

Define the rational-coefficient polynomial vector and polynomial

\(r(x)=\operatorname{adj}(xI-B)v,\qquad d(x)=\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}r(x).\)

Their degrees are at most \(k-1\).

Let \(\lambda_t\) denote the root corresponding to class \(t\). We first establish the identity that recovers its component.

For \(x\) outside the spectrum,

\(\operatorname{adj}(xI-B)=p(x)(xI-B)^{-1}.\)

Since \(B=U_1\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)U_1^{-1}\), this becomes

\(\operatorname{adj}(xI-B)=U_1\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\prod_{u\ne1}(x-\lambda_u),\ldots,\prod_{u\ne k}(x-\lambda_u)\right)U_1^{-1}.\)

Both sides are polynomial matrices, so the identity holds for every \(x\). Evaluating at the simple root \(\lambda_t\) gives

\(\operatorname{adj}(\lambda_tI-B)=p'(\lambda_t)U_1e_te_t^{\mathsf T}U_1^{-1}.\)

Since \(v=U_1\mu\),

\(r(\lambda_t)=p'(\lambda_t)\mu_ta_{1,t},\qquad d(\lambda_t)=s_1p'(\lambda_t)\mu_t.\)

In particular, \(d(\lambda_t)\ne0\). Therefore

\(\mu_t=\dfrac{d(\lambda_t)}{s_1p'(\lambda_t)},\qquad a_{1,t}=\dfrac{s_1r(\lambda_t)}{d(\lambda_t)}.\)

Using \(F_i=W_iU_1^{-1}\), we also obtain

\(\bigl((C_i)_{t,1},\ldots,(C_i)_{t,k}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}=\dfrac{s_1F_ir(\lambda_t)}{\pi_i d(\lambda_t)}.\)

Indeed, \(F_ir(\lambda_t)=p'(\lambda_t)\mu_t\pi_iC_i^{\mathsf T}e_t\), and substitution proves the formula.

Thus each recovered parameter is a rational function of a single root of \(p\). No arithmetic in a joint splitting field is required.

Fixing the class names

For each root \(\alpha\), compute the unique largest coordinate of

\(\dfrac{s_1r(\alpha)}{d(\alpha)}.\)

When \(\alpha=\lambda_t\), this vector is \(a_{1,t}\), whose coordinates form row \(t\) of \(A_1\). The diagonal-gap promise says that its unique largest coordinate is coordinate \(t\).

Hence this procedure assigns each root its correct class index. The assignment is bijective.

These formulas also prove uniqueness of the parameters under the promises: their values and class names are determined by observable rational moments.

6. Polynomial bit complexity and rational probability estimates

Sizes of the exact expressions

Let

\(\Delta=\left(\prod_{z\in S}\operatorname{den}(\eta_z)\right)|G_1||G_2||G_3|.\)

Its bit length is at most \(L+3\log_2m+O(1)\). Every entry of \(v\), the required cross moments, the worker moments \(H_i\), and \(\mathcal T\) has denominator dividing \(\Delta\). These entries lie in \([0,1]\), so their numerator bit lengths are polynomially bounded as well.

Exact matrix inversion preserves polynomial bit length here. To see this, clear a common denominator and use the adjugate formula. For an \(n\times n\) integer matrix with entries of absolute value at most \(2^H\), the Leibniz bound gives

\(|\det M|\le n!2^{nH}.\)

The same bound applies to its minors, with smaller dimensions. Thus inverse entries have polynomially bounded numerator and denominator lengths, regardless of how small the nonzero determinant is.

The separator coordinates have bit length \(O(k\log k)\), because \(s\le N=O(k^3)\). Consequently:

  • \(B\) and all \(F_i\) have polynomial bit length;
  • \(p,r,d\) have polynomial coefficient bit length;
  • every rational function in the recovery formulas has degree at most \(k-1\) and polynomial coefficient bit length.

All these expressions can be constructed in polynomial time. For example, characteristic polynomials and adjugate entries can be computed by evaluating the corresponding determinants at polynomially many integers and interpolating.

Certified algebraic computations

We use the following standard univariate computational result.

Given an integer polynomial of degree at most \(d\) and coefficient bit length at most \(H\), its real roots can be isolated deterministically in polynomial time, with polynomial-length rational interval endpoints. Given a square-free defining polynomial, an isolating interval for a root \(\alpha\), and another integer polynomial \(G\), the sign of \(G(\alpha)\), including whether it is zero, can also be determined deterministically in polynomial time in the degrees and bit lengths. These results are established in Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 6.1 of [Emiris, Mourrain, and Tsigaridas, *Real Algebraic Numbers: Complexity Analysis and Experimentation*](https://who.paris.inria.fr/Elias.Tsigaridas/files/emt-lncs-2006.pdf).

The hypotheses apply: clearing denominators gives an integer defining polynomial; our \(p\) is square-free; and all degrees and coefficient lengths are polynomially bounded. Irreducibility is unnecessary. If a sign query has a common root with the defining polynomial, polynomial gcd computation and root counting in the isolating interval detect that case.

Every comparison needed for class alignment is therefore an exact polynomial-time sign query. In particular, no prescribed lower bound on the diagonal gap is needed.

The same machinery gives rational approximations without any conditioning assumption. Suppose a recovered probability is \(u=f(\alpha)/g(\alpha)\), with \(g(\alpha)\ne0\). For integers \(b,Q\) with \(Q>0\),

\(\operatorname{sgn}(u-b/Q)=\operatorname{sgn}(Qf(\alpha)-bg(\alpha))\,\operatorname{sgn}(g(\alpha)).\)

Binary search over \(b\in\{0,\ldots,Q\}\) therefore computes \(\lfloor Qu\rfloor\) using \(O(\log Q)\) exact sign queries.

Rounding while preserving probability sums

Set

\(\delta=\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2k(m+1)}.\)

Choose a power of two \(Q\) satisfying \(Q\ge k/\delta\). Its bit length is

\(O(\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log m+\log k),\)

and hence polynomial in the input parameters.

For any recovered probability vector \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\), define

\(\widehat x_j=\dfrac{\lfloor Qx_j\rfloor}{Q}\quad(1\le j<k),\qquad \widehat x_k=1-\sum_{j<k}\widehat x_j.\)

This is a rational probability vector. For \(j<k\),

\(0\le x_j-\widehat x_j<1/Q.\)

For its last coordinate,

\(0\le\widehat x_k-x_k=\sum_{j<k}(x_j-\widehat x_j)<(k-1)/Q.\)

Thus every coordinate error is at most \(\delta\).

Apply this construction to \(\mu\) and to every row of every \(C_i\), after exact class alignment. The resulting estimates satisfy

\(\|\widehat\mu-\mu\|_\infty\le\delta\le\varepsilon,\qquad \max_{i,t,c}|(\widehat C_i)_{tc}-(C_i)_{tc}|\le\delta\le\varepsilon.\)

There are polynomially many coordinates, each requiring polynomially many polynomial-time sign queries.

7. Classification with the required excess-risk guarantee

Define estimated response probabilities by

\(\widehat q_{i,t}(0)=1-\pi_i,\qquad \widehat q_{i,t}(c)=\pi_i(\widehat C_i)_{tc}\quad(c\in[k]).\)

These are probability distributions. Write the true and estimated joint masses as

\(J(t,z)=\mu_t\prod_{i=1}^mq_{i,t}(z_i),\qquad \widehat J(t,z)=\widehat\mu_t\prod_{i=1}^m\widehat q_{i,t}(z_i).\)

We bound their total \(\ell_1\) difference.

For probability distributions \(p_i,\widetilde p_i\), telescoping the difference of their products and summing absolute values gives

\(\left\|\prod_i p_i-\prod_i\widetilde p_i\right\|_1\le\sum_i\|p_i-\widetilde p_i\|_1.\)

Indeed, each telescoping term contains one factor \(p_i-\widetilde p_i\); all other factors are nonnegative probability distributions and sum to one.

For each worker and class,

\(\|q_{i,t}-\widehat q_{i,t}\|_1=\pi_i\sum_{c=1}^k|(C_i)_{tc}-(\widehat C_i)_{tc}|\le k\delta.\)

Let \(P_t\) and \(\widehat P_t\) denote the corresponding product distributions. Using

\(\mu_tP_t-\widehat\mu_t\widehat P_t=\mu_t(P_t-\widehat P_t)+(\mu_t-\widehat\mu_t)\widehat P_t,\)

we obtain

\(\|J-\widehat J\|_1\le\|\mu-\widehat\mu\|_1+\sum_t\mu_t\|P_t-\widehat P_t\|_1\le k\delta+mk\delta=\varepsilon/2.\)

Now, for each supplied profile, choose

\(a(z)\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{t\in[k]}\widehat J(t,z).\)

Set \(a(z)=1\) outside \(S\), as required. The scores are rational, so these maximizations use exact rational comparisons.

For a profile \(z\in S\), let \(t^*(z)\) maximize \(J(t,z)\). If \(a(z)=t^*(z)\), its regret is zero. Otherwise, since \(\widehat J(a(z),z)\ge\widehat J(t^*(z),z)\),

\(J(t^*(z),z)-J(a(z),z)\le|J(t^*(z),z)-\widehat J(t^*(z),z)|+|J(a(z),z)-\widehat J(a(z),z)|.\)

The two indices are distinct, so the right-hand side is at most

\(\sum_{t=1}^k|J(t,z)-\widehat J(t,z)|.\)

For omitted profiles, every \(J(t,z)\) is zero: the nonnegative joint masses sum to the observed mass \(\eta_z=0\). Hence their labels have no effect on risk.

Finally, Bayes optimization separates over profiles, giving

\(R(a)-R^*=\sum_{z\in S}\bigl(\max_tJ(t,z)-J(a(z),z)\bigr)\le\|J-\widehat J\|_1\le\varepsilon/2\le\varepsilon.\)

Computing all support labels requires polynomially many rational products and comparisons. Their bit lengths are polynomial because each product has only \(m\) factors of polynomial bit length.

Thus both the probability estimates and the specified classifier are produced deterministically in polynomial time.
