Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of customer cohorts with binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), a shared feasible product space and feature map, a context stream for each cohort, and typewise choice fractions, can one compute cohort query sets and Choice Perceptron updates in polynomial time while attaining the mass-weighted expected average-regret guarantee?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), normalized by \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The shared product space \(Y\) is a nonempty bounded set described by rational mixed-integer linear constraints on \(m\) attributes. Each cohort supplies a context stream \((x_{\theta t})_{t=1}^T\); the supplied feature map \(\phi(x,\cdot):Y\to\mathbb R^d\) is rational affine for each context, with \(\|\phi(x,y)\|_2\le R\). True utilities are \(u_\theta^*(x,y)=\langle w_\theta^*,\phi(x,y)\rangle\), with fixed unknown \(w_\theta^*\in\mathbb R^d\), allowing either sign.
Maintain separate estimates \(w_{\theta1}=0\), with constant positive rational step size \(\eta\) and query size \(k\ge2\). Write \(v_{\theta t}(y)=\phi(x_{\theta t},y)\) and \(u_{\theta t}(y)=\langle w_{\theta t},v_{\theta t}(y)\rangle\).
At each round compute an exact maximizing tuple \((y_1,\ldots,y_k)\in Y^k\) for \(t^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^k\|v_{\theta t}(y_1)-v_{\theta t}(y_i)\|_1+(1-t^{-1})\sum_{i=2}^k u_{\theta t}(y_i)\), subject to \(y_1\in\arg\max_{y\in Y}u_{\theta t}(y)\) and pairwise distinct feature vectors. Promise that these constrained maxima exist at every round; any rational maximizing tuple is allowed, with ties resolved deterministically. Set \(Q_{\theta t}=\{y_1,\ldots,y_k\}\).
After issuing the query, receive exact binary-rational choice fractions \(p_{\theta t}(y)\ge0\), summing to one on \(Q_{\theta t}\). They satisfy \(p_{\theta t}(y)\ge p_{\theta t}(z)\iff u_\theta^*(x_{\theta t},y)\ge u_\theta^*(x_{\theta t},z)\). Update \(w_{\theta,t+1}=w_{\theta t}+\eta D_{\theta t}\), where \(D_{\theta t}=\frac{k}{k-1}\sum_{y\in Q_{\theta t}}p_{\theta t}(y)v_{\theta t}(y)-\frac1{k-1}\sum_{y\in Q_{\theta t}}v_{\theta t}(y)\).
Define \(r_{\theta t}=\max_{y\in Y}u_\theta^*(x_{\theta t},y)-\max_{y\in Q_{\theta t}}u_\theta^*(x_{\theta t},y)\), \(h_{\theta t}=\max_{y\in Y}u_\theta^*(x_{\theta t},y)-\min_{y\in Q_{\theta t}}u_\theta^*(x_{\theta t},y)\), and \(M_\theta=\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1[\langle w_\theta^*,D_{\theta t}\rangle=0]\).
The guarantee is conditional on constants \(\alpha_\theta\in(0,1]\) and \(\beta_\theta\in\mathbb R\) satisfying \(\langle w_\theta^*,D_{\theta t}\rangle\ge\alpha_\theta h_{\theta t}-2\alpha_\theta R\|w_\theta^*\|_2\mathbf1[\langle w_\theta^*,D_{\theta t}\rangle=0]\) at every round and \(T^{-1}\sum_t\langle w_{\theta t},D_{\theta t}\rangle\le\beta_\theta\), with \(2\beta_\theta/\eta+4R^2\ge0\).
The required mass-weighted expected average-regret bound is \(\mathbb E[T^{-1}\sum_{t,\theta}\mu_\theta r_{\theta t}]\le\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\bigl(\frac{\|w_\theta^*\|_2\sqrt{2\beta_\theta/\eta+4R^2}}{\alpha_\theta\sqrt T}+\frac{2R\|w_\theta^*\|_2M_\theta}{T}\bigr)\). Exact cohort fractions and deterministic tie choices make this trajectory deterministic.

Can these cohort queries and Choice Perceptron updates be computed while attaining the stated conditional regret bound in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,d,k,T,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard? Here \(L\) is the total bit length of the supplied constraints, feature maps, contexts, numerical parameters, masses, and received fractions; response generation is external.

For an individual user with sampled choice feedback, Choice Perceptron has the corresponding conditional expected-regret bound; this is not a polynomial-time query-selection theorem. Exact query selection is formulated using mixed-integer optimization ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.07875)).

Result

The computational problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, already for \(\tau=d=T=1\) and \(k=2\). The hardness persists on instances satisfying every stated promise and both conditional regret assumptions.

The stated regret bound nevertheless holds for the exact-fraction updates. Thus the statistical guarantee and the difficulty of exact query selection are compatible.

1. A reduction from satisfiability

Let \(F\) be a Boolean formula in conjunctive normal form with three literals per clause, on variables \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\). We use the standard fact that deciding satisfiability of such formulas is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

For a literal \(\ell\), define its affine truth indicator by

\(\lambda_{a_i}(a)=a_i,\qquad \lambda_{\neg a_i}(a)=1-a_i.\)

Construct products \(y=(a,z,s)\), with \(m=n+2\) attributes, and define \(Y\) by the rational mixed-integer linear constraints

\(a_i\in\{0,1\},\qquad z\in\{0,1\},\qquad s\in\mathbb Z,\qquad 0\le s\le2,\qquad s\le1+z,\)

together with, for every clause \(C\) of \(F\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\ell\in C}\lambda_\ell(a)\ge z.\)

Take one cohort of mass \(\mu_1=1\), one constant context, and

\(\tau=d=T=1,\qquad k=2,\qquad \eta=1,\qquad \phi(x,(a,z,s))=\frac{s}{2},\qquad R=1.\)

This construction has encoding length polynomial in the length of \(F\).

Feasibility and the promises

For every Boolean assignment \(a\), choosing \(z=0\) makes every clause constraint hold, because each literal indicator is nonnegative. Both \(s=0\) and \(s=1\) are then feasible. Consequently, \(Y\) is nonempty and contains two distinct feature vectors.

The set \(Y\) is finite and bounded, and all its products have integer coordinates. Hence every constrained query maximum exists and has a rational maximizing tuple. Also,

\(\displaystyle |\phi(x,y)|=\frac{s}{2}\le1=R.\)

Finally, a product with \(s=2\) exists if and only if \(F\) is satisfiable:

  • If \(s=2\), the constraint \(s\le1+z\) forces \(z=1\). Every clause then has at least one true literal.
  • If \(a\) satisfies \(F\), the product \((a,1,2)\) satisfies every constraint.

Thus the attainable feature set is precisely

\(\displaystyle \phi(x,Y)= \begin{cases} \{0,\frac12\},&F\text{ is unsatisfiable},\\ \{0,\frac12,1\},&F\text{ is satisfiable}. \end{cases}\)

What the first exact query reveals

At the first round, \(w_{11}=0\), so every product maximizes the estimated utility. With \(T=1\) and \(k=2\), the query objective reduces to

\(\displaystyle |\phi(x,y_1)-\phi(x,y_2)|.\)

Its maximum is therefore

\(\displaystyle \begin{cases} \frac12,&F\text{ is unsatisfiable},\\ 1,&F\text{ is satisfiable}. \end{cases}\)

Every maximizing tuple uses the smallest and largest attainable features. Hence, regardless of deterministic tie resolution,

\(\displaystyle F\text{ is satisfiable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \max\{s(y_1),s(y_2)\}=2.\)

A polynomial-time algorithm returning the required exact query would therefore decide satisfiability in polynomial time. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness of query selection, and consequently of the requested combined computation.

2. The reduction satisfies the conditional regret assumptions

The hardness does not rely on violating the feedback model or the conditions attached to the guarantee.

Choose the fixed, nonzero true weight \(w_1^*=1\). For the issued pair, let \(y^+\) have the larger feature and \(y^-\) the smaller feature. Supply the exact binary-rational fractions

\(p_{11}(y^+)=1,\qquad p_{11}(y^-)=0.\)

Since the features are distinct and true utility equals the feature, these fractions satisfy the stipulated ordering equivalence. Generating them only requires comparing the returned coordinates.

Write

\(\delta=\phi(x,y^+)-\phi(x,y^-)\in\{\frac12,1\}.\)

For \(k=2\), the update gives

\(\displaystyle D_{11} =2\phi(x,y^+)-\phi(x,y^+)-\phi(x,y^-) =\delta.\)

Because an exact maximizing pair contains both extreme features,

\(\langle w_1^*,D_{11}\rangle=\delta,\qquad h_{11}=\delta,\qquad r_{11}=0,\qquad M_1=0.\)

Take \(\alpha_1=1\) and \(\beta_1=0\). The first conditional inequality holds with equality. The second holds because

\(\langle w_{11},D_{11}\rangle=0=\beta_1.\)

The remaining numerical condition also holds:

\(2\beta_1/\eta+4R^2=4\ge0.\)

Thus every instance produced by the reduction satisfies all promises, with fixed conditional constants and a nonzero true utility weight. The first query already reveals satisfiability before any response is needed.

3. Proof of the stated conditional regret bound

Fix a cohort and suppress its index. Write \(v_{ti}=v_t(y_i)\) and \(p_{ti}=p_t(y_i)\).

Bounding the update norm

The update can be rewritten as

\(\displaystyle D_t=\sum_{i=1}^k p_{ti} \left(v_{ti}-\frac1{k-1}\sum_{j\ne i}v_{tj}\right). \)

Each vector in parentheses has norm at most \(2R\): the first term has norm at most \(R\), and the second is an average of vectors of norm at most \(R\). Since the fractions are nonnegative and sum to one, the triangle inequality yields

\(\|D_t\|_2\le2R.\)

Bounding the final estimate

Expanding \(w_{t+1}=w_t+\eta D_t\) gives

\(\displaystyle \|w_{t+1}\|_2^2 =\|w_t\|_2^2+2\eta\langle w_t,D_t\rangle+\eta^2\|D_t\|_2^2. \)

Summing over rounds, using \(w_1=0\), the update norm bound, and the assumed average bound on \(\langle w_t,D_t\rangle\), gives

\(\displaystyle \|w_{T+1}\|_2^2 \le2\eta\beta T+4\eta^2R^2T =\eta^2T\left(\frac{2\beta}{\eta}+4R^2\right). \)

The assumed nonnegativity of the expression in parentheses therefore implies

\(\displaystyle \|w_{T+1}\|_2 \le\eta\sqrt T\sqrt{\frac{2\beta}{\eta}+4R^2}. \)

Converting alignment into regret

By their definitions, \(0\le r_t\le h_t\). Summing the assumed conditional informativeness inequality therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \alpha\sum_{t=1}^T r_t \le\sum_{t=1}^T\langle w^*,D_t\rangle +2\alpha R\|w^*\|_2M. \)

The update recurrence and \(w_1=0\) imply

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^T\langle w^*,D_t\rangle =\frac{\langle w^*,w_{T+1}\rangle}{\eta} \le\frac{\|w^*\|_2\|w_{T+1}\|_2}{\eta}, \)

where the last inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz. Substituting the norm bound and dividing by \(\alpha T\) yields

\(\displaystyle \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T r_t \le \frac{\|w^*\|_2\sqrt{2\beta/\eta+4R^2}}{\alpha\sqrt T} +\frac{2R\|w^*\|_2M}{T}. \)

Apply this argument separately to each cohort, multiply by its nonnegative mass, and sum. This proves

\(\displaystyle \frac1T\sum_{t,\theta}\mu_\theta r_{\theta t} \le \sum_\theta\mu_\theta \left( \frac{\|w_\theta^*\|_2\sqrt{2\beta_\theta/\eta+4R^2}}{\alpha_\theta\sqrt T} +\frac{2R\|w_\theta^*\|_2M_\theta}{T} \right). \)

The trajectory is deterministic under the stated feedback and tie choices, so taking the indicated expectation leaves this inequality unchanged.

4. Computational conclusion

Given encoded rational query tuples and fractions, the updates require only rational arithmetic and are computable in polynomial time in those encoding lengths. The obstacle is obtaining the exact maximizing queries: the reduction proves hardness even when all returned coordinates have constant bit length.

The cited [Choice Perceptron paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.07875) formulates query selection through mixed-integer optimization. The reduction above independently establishes hardness for this precise cohort formulation.

Therefore, the conditional regret bound is valid, but computing the prescribed trajectory is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A general polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,d,k,T,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
