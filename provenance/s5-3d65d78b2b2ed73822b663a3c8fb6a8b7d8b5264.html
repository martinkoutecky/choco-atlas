The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given common finite variables \(V\) and domains, a finite support \(\Theta\) of conditionally acyclic TCP-net types, rational masses \(\mu_\theta>0\) summing to \(1\), and outcomes \(a,b\in\mathcal{D}(V)\), compute the exact value \(D_\mu(a,b)=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta\,\mathbf{1}[a\succ_\theta b]\), where \(a\succ_\theta b\) means that type \(\theta\) entails \(a\) is strictly preferred to \(b\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list \(\Theta\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not all possible types.
All types share \(m=|V|\) variables with explicitly listed nonempty finite domains; outcomes belong to \(\mathcal D(V)=\prod_{X\in V}\mathcal D(X)\). Let \(d=\max_X|\mathcal D(X)|\) and \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A TCP-net type specifies directed preference-dependency arcs, a complete explicit conditional preference table giving a total preorder on each variable's domain for every assignment to its parents, unconditional directed importance arcs, and conditional importance edges. Each conditional edge \(\{X,Y\}\) has selectors \(S(X,Y)\subseteq V\setminus\{X,Y\}\) and an explicit, possibly partial table specifying which endpoint is more important. Missing entries impose no importance comparison; importance endpoints are not parents of one another.
Conditional acyclicity is promised: the directed graph containing preference and unconditional importance arcs, plus arcs from each selector to both endpoints of its conditional edge, is acyclic; every graph containing preference and unconditional importance arcs and conditional edges oriented by a complete selector assignment is also acyclic, with unspecified edges omitted.
A permitted improvement changes either exactly one variable to a strictly better value in its conditional table, or exactly two variables, strictly improving the currently more important one and strictly worsening the other. In the latter move, both parent assignments and the importance-selector assignment remain unchanged. Local ties impose no strict comparison.
A satisfying ranking is a total preorder on outcomes that strictly prefers the destination of every permitted improvement to its source. Write \(a\succ_\theta b\) when every satisfying ranking of type \(\theta\) strictly prefers \(a\) to \(b\).
Given outcomes \(a,b\), compute the exact rational value \(D_\mu(a,b)=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta\mathbf 1[a\succ_\theta b]\).

Is exact computation of \(D_\mu(a,b)\) possible in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,d,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete dominance is equivalent to existence of a nonempty sequence of permitted improvements ([Brafman–Domshlak](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.0558)). Dominance is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for binary acyclic CP-nets having at most one directed path between any two variables ([Boutilier et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.0023)).
No verified complexity result was located for this exact explicit-support mass-weighted query.

Result

Exact computation of \(D_\mu(a,b)\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=1\), \(d=2\), all local preferences are strict, and there are no importance arcs or conditional importance edges.

Consequently, an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,d,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). The proof uses a singleton support, which is permitted by the stated input model.

1. The hard source problem

We use the following established result: deciding dominance in binary CP-nets is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when the preference-dependency graph is acyclic and has at most one directed path between any two variables. The hardness construction has at most six parents per variable and fully specified conditional preference tables. See [Boutilier et al., Theorem 15 and its Appendix A proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.0023).

Thus the source problem has precisely the explicit-table representation needed here. In particular, its hardness does not depend on a succinct encoding of exponentially large conditional tables.

A source instance consists of such a CP-net \(N\) and outcomes \(a,b\), with the question whether \(N\) entails that \(a\) is strictly preferred to \(b\).

2. Agreement of dominance semantics

We verify the relevant semantic equivalence directly for the subclass used in the reduction.

Let \(N\) be a binary acyclic CP-net with strict conditional preferences. Define its improvement graph \(G_N\) to have all outcomes as vertices and an arc \(o\to o'\) exactly when \(o'\) is obtained from \(o\) by strictly improving one variable according to its conditional preference table.

Lemma. Every satisfying total preorder strictly prefers \(a\) to \(b\) if and only if \(G_N\) contains a nonempty directed path from \(b\) to \(a\).

Proof. First, we show that \(G_N\) is acyclic and that satisfying rankings exist.

Choose a topological ordering \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\) of the preference-dependency graph. For each outcome \(o\), let \(r_i(o)\in\{0,1\}\) equal \(1\) when \(o[X_i]\) is the preferred binary value under the parent assignment in \(o\), and equal \(0\) otherwise. Define \(U(o)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}2^{m-i}r_i(o)\).

Suppose \(o\to o'\) improves \(X_i\). Every parent of \(X_i\) precedes it in the topological ordering, so its parent assignment is unchanged and \(r_i(o')-r_i(o)=1\). For every \(j<i\), neither \(X_j\) nor any of its parents changes, so \(r_j(o')=r_j(o)\). The remaining terms can decrease by at most \(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}2^{m-j}=2^{m-i}-1\). Hence \(U(o')-U(o)\ge 1\).

Therefore every improvement strictly increases \(U\). This rules out directed cycles, and the total preorder defined by numerical comparison of \(U\) is a satisfying ranking.

If a nonempty path from \(b\) to \(a\) exists, each arc is a strict preference in every satisfying ranking. Transitivity of the strict part of a total preorder therefore gives strict preference for \(a\) over \(b\).

Conversely, suppose there is no such path. If \(a=b\), no satisfying ranking strictly prefers \(a\) to itself, so dominance is false.

If \(a\ne b\), add the arc \(a\to b\) to \(G_N\). The resulting graph remains acyclic: any newly created cycle would have to use that arc and would therefore contain an original path from \(b\) to \(a\), contrary to the assumption. Because the graph is finite, it has a topological ordering. Prefer outcomes occurring later in that ordering. This defines a strict total order satisfying every original improvement, while strictly preferring \(b\) to \(a\). Thus \(a\) does not dominate \(b\). This proves the lemma.

For a CP-net with strict tables, its ceteris paribus requirements are exactly the one-variable improvements defining \(G_N\). The lemma therefore applies both to ordinary CP-net dominance and to the satisfying-ranking definition in the question.

3. Reduction to the weighted query

Given a source instance \((N,a,b)\), construct a society with support \(\Theta=\{\theta_N\}\) and mass \(\mu_{\theta_N}=1\). The type \(\theta_N\) copies the variables, binary domains, preference-dependency arcs, and explicit conditional preference tables of \(N\). Give it no unconditional importance arcs and no conditional importance edges. Keep the queried outcomes \(a,b\).

We check all relevant input conditions:

  • Masses and support. The sole mass is a positive binary-rational number, and the masses sum to \(1\). Thus \(\tau=1\).
  • Domains and tables. Every domain has size \(2\), so \(d=2\). The copied tables are complete and specify strict total orders, which are allowed total preorders.
  • Conditional acyclicity. With no importance edges or selectors, both graphs in the promise reduce to the acyclic preference-dependency graph of \(N\).
  • Importance endpoint restrictions. These hold vacuously because there are no importance edges.
  • Permitted improvements. No variable pair has a specified importance comparison, so no two-variable improvement is permitted. The one-variable improvements are exactly those of \(N\).

It follows from the semantic agreement proved above that \(a\succ_{\theta_N}b\) holds exactly when the source dominance instance is a yes-instance. Consequently, \(D_\mu(a,b)=\mathbf 1[a\succ_{\theta_N}b]\): the exact answer is \(1\) for a yes-instance and \(0\) for a no-instance.

The construction copies an explicitly encoded instance and adds only the singleton-support and mass information. Its output length is polynomial in the source input length; in particular, no table expansion occurs.

Therefore, deciding whether \(D_\mu(a,b)=1\) is already \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions on this restricted subclass. An exact-value algorithm solves that decision problem with one computation and a comparison with \(1\). This proves the claimed hardness of exact computation, without asserting an upper complexity classification for the full TCP-net model.
