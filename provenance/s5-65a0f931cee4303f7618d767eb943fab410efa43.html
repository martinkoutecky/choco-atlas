The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) interval islands, a piece limit \(k\), a rational population scale \(N\), and \(\tau\) types with rational proportions \(\mu_t\) and rational piecewise-constant valuations \(V_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) and \(V_t(C)=1\), assign mass \(N\mu_t\) of each type to bundles that are unions of at most \(k\) intervals. Represent the assignment by a measure \(\pi_t\) over bundles, with \(\pi_t\) having total mass \(N\mu_t\) and aggregate cake use at most one at almost every point. Find the largest \(\alpha\) such that \(\pi_t\)-almost every bundle assigned to each type \(t\) has value at least \(\alpha\); characterize the sharp worst-case guarantee over such instances and determine whether it equals \(\min\{1/N,k/(m+N-1)\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m,k\geq1\) be integers, \(N\geq1\) a binary rational, and \(C\) the union of \(m\) pairwise-disjoint unit intervals with rational endpoints.
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational proportions \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); type \(t\) has mass \(N\mu_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a nonnegative piecewise-constant density with rational breakpoints and heights, inducing an additive valuation \(V_t\) normalized by \(V_t(C)=1\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Let \(\mathcal B_k\) be the space of subsets of \(C\) that are unions of at most \(k\) intervals, equipped with the Borel structure induced by interval endpoints; endpoints are immaterial modulo Lebesgue-null sets.
An assignment consists of finite nonnegative Borel measures \(\pi_t\) on \(\mathcal B_k\), with \(\pi_t(\mathcal B_k)=N\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\int_{\mathcal B_k}\mathbf1_B(x)\,d\pi_t(B)\leq1\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\in C\). Free disposal is allowed.
A threshold \(a\in[0,1]\) is feasible if such an assignment satisfies \(V_t(B)\geq a\) for \(\pi_t\)-almost every \(B\), for every type. Define \(\alpha^*(I)\) as the supremum of feasible thresholds and \(g(m,k,N)=\inf_I\alpha^*(I)\), over all societies and valuations above with these parameters.

Is deciding feasibility of a binary-rational threshold \(a\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, with running times measured in \(m,\tau,k,L\), including the binary encodings of \(N\) and \(a\)?
What is the sharp guarantee \(g(m,k,N)\), and does it equal \(\min\{1/N,k/(m+N-1)\}\)?

For a finite list of \(n\geq1\) agents, a polynomial-time algorithm guarantees \(\min\{1/n,k/(m+n-1)\}\), and this worst-case guarantee is sharp; this is not an algorithm for the instance-specific optimum ([Fair Multi-Cake Cutting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08150)).

2. Measure-theoretic conventions

Let $\lambda$ denote Lebesgue measure. For an assignment $(\pi_t)_t$, define its occupation function by
$$ \rho(x)=\sum_t\int_{\mathcal B_k}\mathbf 1_B(x)\,d\pi_t(B). $$
The capacity condition is $\rho(x)\leq1$ for $\lambda$-almost every $x\in C$.

Interval membership is jointly Borel measurable in the point and interval endpoints. Consequently, Tonelli’s theorem applies to all the nonnegative integrands used below. Endpoints do not affect valuation or length.

In all upper-bound and hardness constructions, the unit islands have positive gaps between them. Thus every nondegenerate interval contained in the cake lies in one island.

3. Exact feasibility is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard

We reduce from the standard $\mathrm{NP}$-complete problem PARTITION: given positive binary-encoded integers $b_1,\ldots,b_r$, decide whether some subset sums to half their total. This is one of the problems in [Karp’s NP-completeness theorem](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Set
$$ S=\sum_{i=1}^r b_i, \qquad H=2r+S. $$
Construct an instance with
$$ m=2r,\qquad k=r,\qquad \tau=1,\qquad N=2,\qquad a=\frac12. $$
Use islands $[2j,2j+1]$ for $1\leq j\leq2r$. Give the first $r$ islands constant densities
$$ \frac{1+b_i}{H}, $$
and the remaining $r$ islands constant density $1/H$. These densities are strictly positive, rational, and normalized because
$$ \frac{\sum_{i=1}^r(1+b_i)+r}{H}=1. $$
The construction has polynomial binary length and is computable in polynomial time.

Feasibility implies a partition

Suppose a feasible assignment measure $\pi$ exists. Its mass is $2$. Let $f$ be the constructed density and set
$$ \rho(x)=\int_{\mathcal B_r}\mathbf1_B(x)\,d\pi(B). $$
The threshold and capacity conditions give
$$ 1 =\frac12\,\pi(\mathcal B_r) \leq\int_{\mathcal B_r}V(B)\,d\pi(B) =\int_C f(x)\rho(x)\,dx \leq\int_C f(x)\,dx =1. $$
Every inequality is therefore an equality.

Since $V(B)\geq1/2$ almost surely and its integral equals $1$, we obtain
$$ V(B)=\frac12 \qquad\text{for $\pi$-almost every $B$}. $$
Since $f>0$ almost everywhere and $\rho\leq1$ almost everywhere, equality of the last integral implies
$$ \rho(x)=1 \qquad\text{for $\lambda$-almost every $x\in C$}. $$
Hence Tonelli’s theorem gives
$$ \int_{\mathcal B_r}\lambda(B)\,d\pi(B) =\int_C\rho(x)\,dx =2r. $$

Every admissible bundle has at most $r$ interval components, each contained in a unit island, so $\lambda(B)\leq r$. Because $\pi$ has mass $2$, equality in the preceding integral forces $\lambda(B)=r$ almost surely.

A union of at most $r$ intervals has length $r$ here only if it consists of exactly $r$ whole islands, modulo endpoints. Choose a bundle satisfying this and $V(B)=1/2$; such bundles form a set of positive measure.

If $A$ indexes its selected original islands, its $r$ selected islands have total value
$$ \frac{r+\sum_{i\in A}b_i}{H}=\frac12. $$
Therefore
$$ \sum_{i\in A}b_i=\frac S2. $$

A partition implies feasibility

Conversely, suppose $\sum_{i\in A}b_i=S/2$. Select the original islands indexed by $A$ and any $r-|A|$ dummy islands. Their union $B$ consists of $r$ islands and has value $1/2$.

Its complement also consists of $r$ islands and has value $1/2$. Thus
$$ \pi=\delta_B+\delta_{C\setminus B} $$
is feasible: it has mass $2$ and occupation $1$ almost everywhere.

This proves a polynomial-time many-one reduction. Exact feasibility is therefore $\mathrm{NP}$-hard in the required input model; a polynomial-time algorithm would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. Membership of the general measure-assignment problem in $\mathrm{NP}$ is not asserted.

4. General lower bound

Let
$$ n=\lceil N\rceil, \qquad h_n=\min\left\{\frac1n,\frac{k}{m+n-1}\right\}. $$

We use the supplied finite-agent theorem: for any list of $n$ agents with normalized valuations induced by nonnegative integrable densities, there are pairwise-disjoint bundles, each containing at most $k$ intervals, that give every agent value at least $h_n$. The present piecewise-constant valuations satisfy these hypotheses. See [*Fair Multi-Cake Cutting*, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.08150v6).

Introduce a dummy type with any normalized rational piecewise-constant valuation. Independently sample $n$ labels with probabilities
$$ p_t=\frac{N\mu_t}{n} \quad\text{for genuine types}, \qquad p_{\mathrm d}=\frac{n-N}{n}. $$
These probabilities are nonnegative and sum to $1$.

There are finitely many sampled lists. For each list, choose a finite-agent allocation supplied by the theorem, and discard the dummy agents’ bundles. Define
$$ \pi_t= \mathbb E\left[ \sum_{\substack{1\leq j\leq n\\\text{label }j=t}} \delta_{B_j} \right]. $$
This is a finite nonnegative Borel measure, and
$$ \pi_t(\mathcal B_k)=np_t=N\mu_t. $$
Every bundle in its support has type-$t$ value at least $h_n$.

For each sampled list, the retained bundles are disjoint almost everywhere. The exceptional null sets can be united over the finitely many lists. Outside this union, taking expectations preserves capacity:
$$ \sum_t\int\mathbf1_B(x)\,d\pi_t(B)\leq1. $$
Thus
$$ \boxed{g(m,k,N)\geq h_n.} $$

This is an existence proof. Enumerating the sampled lists does not give a polynomial-time algorithm in the binary encoding of $N$.

5. A stronger upper bound for every $k$

We prove
$$ \boxed{ g(m,k,N)\leq \min\left\{\frac1N,\frac{k}{m+\lceil N\rceil-1}\right\}. } $$

For any one-type instance and any feasible threshold $a$, Tonelli and capacity imply
$$ Na \leq\int_{\mathcal B_k}V(B)\,d\pi(B) =\int_C f(x)\rho(x)\,dx \leq1. $$
Thus every one-type instance has optimum at most $1/N$.

It remains to construct a one-type instance with optimum at most $k/(m+n-1)$.

Construction

Put
$$ s=\frac1{m+n-1}. $$
Give one distinguished unit island uniform value $ns$, and give each of the other $m-1$ islands uniform value $s$. The total value is
$$ ns+(m-1)s=1. $$
All densities are rational.

If $n=1$, then $N=1$ and all islands have value $s=1/m$. Any bundle has value at most $ks$, so the desired bound follows.

Assume henceforth that $n\geq2$.

Piercing all qualifying bundles

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that a threshold
$$ a>ks $$
is feasible. Identify the distinguished island with $[0,1]$, and let $\pi$ be its assignment measure, of mass $N$.

Let $E\subseteq[0,1]$ be a Lebesgue-null set outside which capacity holds. Define
$$ \varepsilon=\frac{a-ks}{ns}>0. $$
Choose $\theta$ satisfying
$$ |\theta|< \min\left\{\frac1{2n},\frac{\varepsilon}{k}\right\} $$
and such that
$$ p_j=\frac jn+\theta\notin E \qquad(1\leq j\leq n-1). $$
Such a choice exists: the forbidden choices lie in the finite union of null sets
$$ \bigcup_{j=1}^{n-1}(E-j/n). $$

Every gap between consecutive points of
$$ 0,p_1,\ldots,p_{n-1},1 $$
has length at most
$$ d=\frac1n+|\theta| < \frac1n+\frac{\varepsilon}{k}. $$

Consider a qualifying bundle $B$. Let $r$ be the number of its nondegenerate interval components in the distinguished island.

If $r=0$, its value is at most $ks<a$, impossible. Thus $1\leq r\leq k$. Its remaining components contribute at most $(k-r)s$, so
$$ ns\,\lambda(B\cap[0,1]) \geq a-(k-r)s. $$
Equivalently,
$$ \lambda(B\cap[0,1]) \geq \frac rn+\varepsilon. $$

If none of the $p_j$ belonged to the interior of $B$, each of these $r$ components would lie between consecutive test points, possibly with test points as endpoints. Its length would therefore be at most $d$. Consequently,
$$ \lambda(B\cap[0,1]) \leq rd < \frac rn+\frac{r\varepsilon}{k} \leq\frac rn+\varepsilon, $$
a contradiction.

Every qualifying bundle therefore contains at least one test point in its interior. Integrating this fact gives
$$ N =\pi(\mathcal B_k) \leq \sum_{j=1}^{n-1} \int_{\mathcal B_k}\mathbf1_B(p_j)\,d\pi(B) \leq n-1. $$
The last inequality uses capacity at the chosen points. But
$$ N>\lceil N\rceil-1=n-1. $$
This contradiction proves that no threshold exceeding $ks$ is feasible.

Together with the value-conservation bound, this proves the stated upper bound.

6. Exact consequences and counterexamples

The full geometric range

The condition
$$ n(k-1)\leq m-1 $$
is equivalent to
$$ \frac{k}{m+n-1}\leq\frac1n. $$
In this range, the lower bound from Section 4 equals $k/(m+n-1)$. Since $N\leq n$, we also have $1/N\geq1/n$, so the upper bound from Section 5 equals the same quantity. Therefore
$$ \boxed{ g(m,k,N)=\frac{k}{m+\lceil N\rceil-1} \qquad\text{if }\lceil N\rceil(k-1)\leq m-1. } $$

In particular, $k=1$ always satisfies this condition:
$$ \boxed{g(m,1,N)=\frac1{m+\lceil N\rceil-1}.} $$

Integer population mass

If $N$ is an integer, then $n=N$, and the general lower and upper bounds coincide:
$$ \boxed{ g(m,k,N)= \min\left\{\frac1N,\frac{k}{m+N-1}\right\}. } $$

The proposed formula fails

Taking $m=k=1$ and $N=3/2$ gives
$$ g(1,1,3/2)=\frac12, $$
whereas the proposed expression equals $2/3$.

For a concrete instance, use one uniform type on $[0,1]$. Threshold $1/2$ is feasible by assigning mass $3/4$ to each half-interval. For $a>1/2$, every qualifying interval contains the positive-length interval $(1-a,a)$. Its occupation would be $3/2$ there, violating capacity.

The stronger upper bound also yields a counterexample with $k>1$. For
$$ m=4,\qquad k=2,\qquad N=\frac52, $$
we have $n=3$ and $n(k-1)=m-1$. Hence
$$ g(4,2,5/2)=\frac13, $$
while the proposed expression is
$$ \min\left\{\frac25,\frac{2}{4+5/2-1}\right\} =\frac4{11} >\frac13. $$

7. An additional exact case: two islands and two intervals

We prove
$$ \boxed{ g(2,2,N)=\frac23 \qquad\left(1<N\leq\frac32\right). } $$

The upper bound follows from Section 5: here $n=2$, so
$$ g(2,2,N)\leq\frac2{2+2-1}=\frac23. $$

For the lower bound, we prove a partition lemma separately for each type.

Partition lemma

For any normalized nonatomic valuation $V$ on two islands, there is a partition
$$ C=D_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}D_2\mathbin{\dot\cup}D_3 $$
modulo endpoints such that
$$ V(D_i)=\frac13 $$
and every complement $C\setminus D_i$ is a union of at most two intervals.

Let the island values be $w_1,w_2$, with $w_1+w_2=1$.

If both island values are at least $1/3$, take $D_1$ to be a prefix of the first island of value $1/3$, and $D_2$ to be a suffix of the second island of value $1/3$. Let $D_3$ be the remainder. These cuts exist because cumulative valuation on each island is continuous.

Then:

  • $C\setminus D_1$ consists of the remaining suffix of the first island and the whole second island.
  • $C\setminus D_2$ consists of the whole first island and the remaining prefix of the second island.
  • $C\setminus D_3=D_1\cup D_2$.

Each complement has at most two intervals, and $V(D_3)=1/3$.

Otherwise, relabel the islands so that $w_1<1/3$. Then $w_2>2/3$. In the second island, choose a prefix $D_1$ and a suffix $D_2$, each of value $1/3$. Their interiors are disjoint because their combined value is less than $w_2$. Let $D_3$ consist of the first island and the middle interval remaining in the second island.

Again $V(D_3)=1/3$. The complements of $D_1$ and $D_2$ each consist of the first island and one interval in the second; the complement of $D_3$ consists of the prefix and suffix in the second island. This proves the lemma.

Assignment construction

Apply the lemma to each type $t$, obtaining sets $D_{t,1},D_{t,2},D_{t,3}$. Set
$$ B_{t,i}=C\setminus D_{t,i}, \qquad \pi_t=\frac{N\mu_t}{3}\sum_{i=1}^3\delta_{B_{t,i}}. $$
Every bundle is admissible and has type-$t$ value $2/3$. The measure has mass $N\mu_t$.

Almost every point belongs to exactly two of the three complements for each type. Therefore total occupation is
$$ \sum_t\frac{2N\mu_t}{3}=\frac{2N}{3}\leq1. $$
Thus $2/3$ is feasible for every society in the stated range, proving the exact formula.

8. Exact guarantee when $k\geq m+1$

We prove
$$ \boxed{g(m,k,N)=\frac1N\qquad(k\geq m+1).} $$

The upper bound follows from the one-type conservation argument. For the lower bound, fix an arbitrary society and concatenate its $m$ islands into $[0,m]$, preserving length and valuations. Define
$$ F_t(x)=V_t([0,x]). $$
This function is continuous, nondecreasing, and piecewise affine, with $F_t(0)=0$ and $F_t(m)=1$.

Put $a=1/N$. If $N=1$, assign the whole cake to every type with measure $\mu_t\delta_C$. The total occupation is $1$, and the whole cake has at most $m$ intervals.

Assume $N>1$. Choose $s$ uniformly in $[0,1)$ and let $A_s$ be the circular arc of length $a$ beginning at $s$. Initially define
$$ B_{t,s} = \{x\in[0,m]:F_t(x)\bmod1\in A_s\}. $$

Except when an arc endpoint equals $0$ or a flat value of $F_t$, this set is either one interval or a prefix together with a suffix. There are only finitely many exceptional $s$. At these exceptional parameters, replace the bundle by $C$.

Returning to the original cake splits an interval at island boundaries. A nonwrapping preimage therefore uses at most $m$ intervals. A wrapping preimage uses at most $m+1$: at most one island can meet both the prefix and suffix. Thus every chosen bundle is admissible.

The distribution is Borel. Indeed, away from the finite exceptional set, its endpoints are obtained from generalized inverses of the continuous piecewise-affine function $F_t$; these inverse endpoint functions are Borel.

The pushforward of valuation measure under $F_t$ is uniform on $[0,1]$. To verify this, on a positive-density piece of height $c$, the substitution $y=F_t(x)$ gives
$$ dy=c\,dx. $$
The image intervals of the positive-density pieces partition $[0,1]$ modulo endpoints. Zero-density pieces carry no valuation. Hence
$$ V_t(B_{t,s})=a $$
for almost every $s$.

For every fixed $x$, a uniformly shifted circular arc contains $F_t(x)\bmod1$ with probability $a$. The finite exceptional modification does not change this probability. Thus
$$ \mathbb P_s(x\in B_{t,s})=a. $$

Let $\pi_t$ be $N\mu_t$ times the distribution of the resulting bundle. It has the required mass and meets threshold $a$ almost surely. Its total occupation is
$$ \sum_tN\mu_t a=Na=1. $$
This proves feasibility at $1/N$.

9. What remains open

The complexity question is settled by the hardness reduction, and the proposed universal guarantee is disproved.

The remaining undetermined parameter range is contained in
$$ N\notin\mathbb Z,\qquad 2\leq k\leq m,\qquad \lceil N\rceil(k-1)>m-1, $$
excluding the exact case from Section 7. In this range, the proved general bounds reduce to
$$ \frac1{\lceil N\rceil} \leq g(m,k,N) \leq \min\left\{\frac1N,\frac{k}{m+\lceil N\rceil-1}\right\}, $$
and can differ.

For example, the present arguments only show
$$ \frac12\leq g(2,2,8/5)\leq\frac58. $$
Determining the sharp guarantee in these remaining cases requires an additional lower-bound construction or a stronger obstruction.
