The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite two-goal interval geometry \(S\), per-capita slot capacities \(\kappa_s\), and a fixed support of at most \(\tau\) uniform-value MAXDISTANCE types \(\theta=(g,r)\) arriving in two successive nonatomic cohorts with rational masses, what is \(\sup_{\pi}\inf_{\mu}\mathrm{SW}(\pi,\mu)/\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{frac}}(\mu)\)? Here \(\pi\) is a causal posted-price policy that knows the geometry and capacities but not future cohort masses; each infinitesimal arrival chooses an available slot maximizing \(\phi\mathbf{1}[d(g,s)\le r]-p_s\), with fixed tie-breaking; and \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{frac}}\) is the offline maximum-welfare mass matching.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) contain \(m\geq1\) distinct rational slot locations in \([0,R]\), with rational \(R>0\), goals \(g_1=0,g_2=R\), distance \(d(x,y)=|x-y|\), and positive rational capacities \(\kappa_s\).
A society is given by its support: the list \(T\) of its \(\tau\) types with positive total mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Types are \(\theta=(g,r)\), with \(g\in\{g_1,g_2\}\), rational \(r\geq0\), and common rational value \(\phi>0\).
Masses and capacities use a fixed reference-population unit; total mass need not equal \(1\). An arrival instance \(\mu\) specifies two successive positive-mass cohorts, each an ordered finite list of homogeneous nonatomic blocks of positive rational mass; their union has support exactly \(T\). The adversary fixes masses and block orders before policy randomization.
A causal, possibly randomized posted-price policy knows \(S,\kappa,T,\phi\), observes cumulative arrivals, purchases, rejections and the cohort boundary, and may update prices using only this history. Types, block boundaries and unarrived masses are not announced; prices are anonymous and lie in \([0,\infty]\).
Each infinitesimal arrival irrevocably consumes equal capacity mass from an available slot maximizing \(\phi\mathbf1[d(g,s)\leq r]-p_s\), provided this maximum is nonnegative; otherwise it takes the outside option, of value and utility \(0\). Utility ties prefer smaller distance, then smaller slot label; a slot is preferred to the outside option at zero utility. Exhausted capacity is unavailable and never replenished.
Restrict policies to those inducing well-defined measurable allocations. Welfare is the integral of allocated values, excluding payments. Writing \(a_\theta\) for total type mass, \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{frac}}(\mu)\) is the maximum of \(\phi\sum_{\theta,s}x_{\theta s}\) subject to \(x\geq0\), \(\sum_sx_{\theta s}\leq a_\theta\), \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta s}\leq\kappa_s\), and \(x_{\theta s}=0\) whenever \(d(g,s)>r\).
State any running times in \(m,\tau,L,B\), where \(L\) is the total binary input length and \(B\) is the number of arrival blocks.

What is the optimal competitive ratio \(C(S,\kappa,T)=\sup_\pi\inf_{\mu:\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{frac}}(\mu)>0}\mathbb E[\mathrm{SW}(\pi,\mu)]/\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{frac}}(\mu)\) for this two-cohort posted-price problem?

Result and information model

For the policy class specified in the question, every nonvacuous society satisfies
$$ \boxed{C(S,\kappa,T)=1.} $$

Here the masses $a_\theta$ supplied with $T$ are known and fixed. Admissible arrival instances vary their block decomposition, order, and cohort split.

The proof uses a substantive feature of the stated model: arbitrary measurable price changes and exact observations of cumulative purchases are permitted. Diagnostic menus can occupy arbitrarily little arrival mass while occurring with positive mass in every interval. Because there are finitely many homogeneous blocks, these observations reveal the current acceptable slots almost everywhere, using only strictly preceding history. I prove this carefully below.

If every supported type accepts no slot, there is no instance with positive fractional optimum. The defining infimum then has an empty domain; under the extended-real convention, its value is $+\infty$.

1. Normalization and the universal upper bound

Write
$$ N_\theta=\{s\in S:|g-s|\leq r\}, \qquad A=\sum_{\theta\in T}a_\theta. $$
Parameterize arrivals by cumulative mass $t\in[0,A]$. For each admissible instance, the type function $\theta(t)$ is constant on finitely many intervals. Block endpoints form a finite set of Lebesgue measure zero.

Let $Z$ denote the maximum acceptable mass in the fractional allocation problem:
$$ Z=\max\left\{ \sum_{\theta,s}x_{\theta s}: x\geq0,\ \sum_sx_{\theta s}\leq a_\theta,\ \sum_\theta x_{\theta s}\leq\kappa_s,\ x_{\theta s}=0\text{ if }s\notin N_\theta \right\}. $$
Because the type totals are fixed,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{frac}}(\mu)=\phi Z $$
for every admissible arrival instance.

Any policy’s positive-value allocations, after discarding zero-value allocations, give a feasible vector in this optimization problem. Thus, for every realization of policy randomness,
$$ \mathrm{SW}(\pi,\mu)\leq\phi Z. $$
Consequently,
$$ C(S,\kappa,T)\leq1. $$

Henceforth assume $Z>0$. This holds precisely when some supported type accepts some slot: the reverse implication follows by allocating positive mass on one such edge, using the positive mass and capacity hypotheses.

2. Reserve a nearly optimal fractional allocation

Fix a rational number $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$. Choose an acceptable pair $(\theta_0,s_0)$ and put
$$ z_0=\min\{a_{\theta_0},\kappa_{s_0}\}>0. $$
Then $Z\geq z_0$.

Choose a positive rational $\delta$ satisfying
$$ \delta< \min\left\{ \min_{\theta\in T}a_\theta,\ \min_{s\in S}\kappa_s,\ \frac{\varepsilon z_0}{m+\tau} \right\}. $$
Consider the reduced allocation problem with type masses $a_\theta-\delta$ and capacities $\kappa_s-\delta$. Let $x^\delta$ be an optimal allocation and let
$$ Z_\delta=\sum_{\theta,s}x^\delta_{\theta s}. $$

Claim.
$$ Z_\delta\geq Z-(m+\tau)\delta>(1-\varepsilon)Z. $$

Proof. Start with an optimal allocation for the original problem. For each type, delete any mass exceeding the reduced row bound $a_\theta-\delta$. Each row requires deletion of at most $\delta$, so these deletions total at most $\tau\delta$.

Next, for each slot, delete any mass exceeding its reduced capacity $\kappa_s-\delta$. Each column requires deletion of at most $\delta$, because its mass before these deletions is at most $\kappa_s$. Column deletions preserve all reduced row bounds and total at most $m\delta$.

The resulting allocation is feasible for the reduced problem and has mass at least $Z-(m+\tau)\delta$. Finally,
$$ (m+\tau)\delta<\varepsilon z_0\leq\varepsilon Z. $$
This proves the claim. $\square$

Define the active slots
$$ J=\left\{s\in S:\sum_\theta x^\delta_{\theta s}>0\right\}. $$
Since $Z_\delta>0$, the set $J$ is nonempty. Slots outside $J$ will always have price $\infty$.

Group types according to their acceptable sets restricted to $J$. For each occurring set $H\subseteq J$, define
$$ T_H=\{\theta\in T:N_\theta\cap J=H\}, \qquad a_H=\sum_{\theta\in T_H}a_\theta, $$
and initialize reservation quantities
$$ q^0_{Hs}=\sum_{\theta\in T_H}x^\delta_{\theta s}, \qquad Q_H=\sum_{s\in J}q^0_{Hs}. $$
Feasibility gives
$$ q^0_{Hs}=0\quad\text{when }s\notin H. $$
Also, since $T_H$ is nonempty,
$$ Q_H \leq\sum_{\theta\in T_H}(a_\theta-\delta) \leq a_H-\delta. $$
For each active slot,
$$ \sum_Hq^0_{Hs}\leq\kappa_s-\delta. $$

These reservations specify which normal purchases the policy will permit.

3. Diagnostic sets of arbitrarily small total mass

Let $\lambda$ denote Lebesgue measure.

Lemma. There are pairwise disjoint Borel sets $E_s\subseteq(0,A)$, one for each $s\in J$, such that
$$ \lambda\left(\bigcup_{s\in J}E_s\right)<\delta $$
and, for every nonempty open interval $I\subseteq(0,A)$,
$$ \lambda(E_s\cap I)>0 \qquad\text{for every }s\in J. $$

Proof. Enumerate all pairs $(s,I)$ where $s\in J$ and $I$ is an open interval with rational endpoints contained in $(0,A)$.

At step $n$, choose a compact nowhere dense set $F_n$ of positive measure inside the interval specified by that pair, avoiding all previously chosen sets, and with
$$ \lambda(F_n)<\delta\,2^{-n-1}. $$
This choice is possible. A finite union of compact nowhere dense sets leaves an open subinterval of the specified interval. Inside a sufficiently short closed subinterval, remove open neighborhoods of a countable dense set whose total length is less than half the subinterval’s length. The remaining set is compact, nowhere dense, and has positive measure.

Let $E_s$ be the union of the sets $F_n$ assigned to $s$. These sets are Borel and pairwise disjoint, and their total measure is less than $\delta$.

Every nonempty open interval contains an interval with rational endpoints. The construction places a positive-measure set for each slot inside that smaller interval. $\square$

Put
$$ E=\bigcup_{s\in J}E_s, \qquad E_0=[0,A]\setminus E. $$
Times in $E$ will use diagnostic menus; times in $E_0$ will use reservation menus.

4. Causal recovery of the current acceptable set

At every diagnostic time $t\in E_s$, post price $\phi/2$ at $s$ and price $\infty$ everywhere else.

Whenever $s$ remains available, the arriving mass purchases precisely when $s$ is acceptable: an acceptable slot offers utility $\phi/2>0$, whereas an unacceptable slot offers utility $-\phi/2<0$.

Let $Y_s(t)$ be the observed cumulative mass purchased at slot $s$. The policy can extract from its recorded history the cumulative diagnostic purchases
$$ D_s(t)=\int_{[0,t)\cap E_s}dY_s(u). $$
It also knows the deterministic function
$$ e_s(t)=\lambda([0,t)\cap E_s). $$

For $t>0$, set $h_n=t/2^n$ and define
$$ b_s(t)= \limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{D_s(t)-D_s(t-h_n)} {e_s(t)-e_s(t-h_n)}. $$
Every denominator is positive by the diagnostic-set lemma. Define
$$ \widehat H(t)=\{s\in J:b_s(t)>1/2\}. $$
At $t=0$, use an arbitrary default estimate.

This estimate is causal. Every integral used in its definition concerns mass strictly preceding $t$. Taking a limit of these historical quantities uses no future observation.

Recovery claim. If the active slots remain available, then, outside the finite set of block endpoints,
$$ \widehat H(t)=N_{\theta(t)}\cap J. $$

Proof. Fix a time $t$ strictly inside a homogeneous block. For all sufficiently large $n$, the entire interval $(t-h_n,t)$ belongs to that block.

Throughout such an interval, a diagnostic arrival at $s$ purchases exactly when $s\in N_{\theta(t)}$. Therefore
$$ D_s(t)-D_s(t-h_n) = \mathbf1[s\in N_{\theta(t)}]\, \bigl(e_s(t)-e_s(t-h_n)\bigr). $$
The displayed ratio is eventually exactly zero or exactly one. Its limsup therefore recovers acceptability for every $s\in J$. $\square$

Crucially, the policy need not know whether $t$ is inside a block. The historical limit gives the correct estimate at every interior time automatically.

5. The posted-price policy

Maintain reservation balances $q_{Hs}(t)$, initialized to $q^0_{Hs}$.

The deterministic policy is:

  • At $t\in E_s$, offer only slot $s$, at price $\phi/2$. Diagnostic purchases do not decrease reservations.
  • At $t\in E_0$, compute $\widehat H(t)$. If this is an occurring group $H$ with some $q_{Hs}(t)>0$, offer only the smallest-label such slot $s$, at price $\phi/2$. Otherwise, post $\infty$ everywhere.
  • A purchase under a reservation menu decreases the corresponding balance by the purchased mass. Upon its depletion, the next positive reservation is used.

All prices are anonymous. Each menu offers at most one finite-price slot, so the prescribed utility tie rules introduce no additional choices.

Capacity safety

At any slot $s\in J$, reservation purchases total at most its initial reservations:
$$ \text{reservation purchases at }s \leq\sum_Hq^0_{Hs} \leq\kappa_s-\delta. $$
Diagnostic purchases there total at most $\lambda(E_s)$, which is less than $\delta$. Hence, at every point in the process,
$$ Y_s(t) \leq\sum_Hq^0_{Hs}+\lambda(E_s) <\kappa_s. $$
Thus every active slot remains available throughout. Slots outside $J$ receive no purchases.

This verifies the availability hypothesis in the recovery claim.

6. Well-definedness and measurability

I give an explicit allocation construction to justify the feedback rule.

For a fixed admissible arrival instance, let
$$ H(t)=N_{\theta(t)}\cap J. $$
This is a measurable function, constant on finitely many intervals.

At diagnostic times in $E_s$, assign the arriving mass to $s$ exactly when $s\in H(t)$.

For each group $H$, define its cumulative non-diagnostic arrival mass
$$ v_H(t)= \int_0^t \mathbf1[u\in E_0]\, \mathbf1[H(u)=H]\,du. $$
Order that group’s positive reservations by slot label. Assign its non-diagnostic arrivals consecutively to those reservations, using $v_H(t)$ to determine the current reservation. Once $v_H(t)$ reaches $Q_H$, reject further non-diagnostic arrivals of that group.

This allocation is measurable: the diagnostic sets are Borel, the type function is measurable, and each $v_H$ is absolutely continuous. Reservation choices are determined by finitely many thresholds of these functions.

It also respects capacities, by the capacity bound just proved. Consequently, its diagnostic purchase history gives
$$ \widehat H(t)=H(t) $$
outside block endpoints. Its reservation balances therefore produce exactly the menus specified by the causal policy, and buyers accept the intended reservation slot because it belongs to $H(t)$.

At block endpoints, apply the stipulated buyer choice to the menu computed from past history. Altering allocations at these finitely many points changes no mass, reservation balance, or welfare.

This establishes existence of a measurable allocation consistent with the policy. It also determines the allocation almost everywhere: capacity safety forces the diagnostic purchase history, that history determines the group estimate almost everywhere, and the fixed reservation order then determines normal purchases.

The dense diagnostic schedule therefore induces a well-defined measurable allocation, despite its unrestricted price variation.

7. Welfare guarantee

For every occurring group $H$, its total diagnostic arrival mass is less than $\delta$. Its non-diagnostic arrival mass consequently satisfies
$$ v_H(A)>a_H-\delta\geq Q_H. $$
Thus every reservation of every group is fully consumed.

Every reservation purchase has value $\phi$, because its assigned slot belongs to the correctly inferred acceptable set almost everywhere. Diagnostic purchases also have value $\phi$. Therefore
$$ \frac{\mathrm{SW}(\pi_\varepsilon,\mu)}{\phi} \geq\sum_HQ_H =\sum_{\theta,s}x^\delta_{\theta s} =Z_\delta >(1-\varepsilon)Z. $$
For every admissible arrival instance,
$$ \frac{\mathrm{SW}(\pi_\varepsilon,\mu)} {\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{frac}}(\mu)} >1-\varepsilon. $$

The diagnostic budget covers the whole known mass horizon. This bound is uniform in the number $B$ of blocks, their orders, and the cohort boundary.

For every positive rational $\varepsilon$, we have constructed one admissible deterministic policy with this guarantee. Taking the supremum over policies yields
$$ C(S,\kappa,T)\geq1. $$
Together with the universal upper bound,
$$ C(S,\kappa,T)=1. $$

This proves the value of the supremum. It does not assert that a single policy attains welfare exactly equal to the optimum on every arrival instance.

8. Computation and scope

Checking whether the society is nonvacuous takes at most $m\tau$ rational distance comparisons, hence polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$. Once nonvacuity is established, the exact competitive-ratio value is $1$; computing that value does not depend on $B$.

For each fixed rational $\varepsilon$, the reservation allocation can be computed in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$. Its reduced problem is a rational maximum-flow network with $m+\tau+2$ vertices and at most $m\tau+m+\tau$ arcs. Clearing denominators gives integer capacities of polynomial bit length, so a polynomial-time maximum-flow algorithm applies.

The witnessing online policy uses countable Borel diagnostic sets and limits of exact historical observations. No finite online computation-time or finite price-update bound is asserted. The measurability restriction in the question is verified explicitly above.

The proof actually applies to any finite family of acceptable sets; the endpoint geometry is unnecessary for this construction. Its essential assumptions are known type totals, finitely many homogeneous arrival blocks, and the permitted measurable policies.

The cited unknown-population obstruction concerns different information: [Meir–Chen–Feldman, Proposition 10](https://cs.tau.ac.il/~mfeldman/papers/MCFc13.pdf) varies the population.
