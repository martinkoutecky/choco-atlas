Theorem — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite Boolean vocabulary \(\mathcal X\), consistent knowledge bases \(K_1,K_2\), and a consistent observed result \(R\), decide whether there are positive rational masses \(\mu_1,\mu_2\) with \(\mu_1+\mu_2=1\) such that \(\operatorname{Mod}(R)=\arg\min_{I\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal X|}}\bigl(\mu_1d_H(I,K_1)+\mu_2d_H(I,K_2)\bigr)\), where \(d_H(I,K)=\min_{J\models K}|\{x\in\mathcal X:I(x)\ne J(x)\}|\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a finite Boolean vocabulary \(\mathcal X\), let \(m=|\mathcal X|\). The input consists of three satisfiable propositional formulas \(K_1,K_2,R\) over \(\mathcal X\), represented syntactically, with total encoding bit length \(L\).
Define \(d_H(I,K)=\min_{J\models K}|\{x\in\mathcal X:I(x)\ne J(x)\}|\). All interpretations belong to \(\{0,1\}^{m}\).
Call the input obtainable if there exist \(\mu_1,\mu_2\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\), with \(\mu_1+\mu_2=1\), such that \(\operatorname{Mod}(R)=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{I\in\{0,1\}^{m}}(\mu_1d_H(I,K_1)+\mu_2d_H(I,K_2))\). Every minimizing interpretation is retained, including ties; the masses are existential variables, not input data.
Is this obtainability problem in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For positive integer weights and two satisfiable bases, weighted Hamming obtainability is \(\Pi_2^p\)-complete. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.5340)
The same integer-weight problem is polynomial-time solvable when both bases are conjunctions of literals and the observed formula is Horn or Krom. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.5340)

Result

The problem is \(\Pi_2^p\)-complete, and therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. In particular, a running time polynomial in \(m\) and \(L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The key point is that positive rational masses summing to one give exactly the same obtainable model sets as positive integer weights.

Equivalence of the weight domains

Fix any input satisfying the stated hypotheses, and write \(d_i(I)=d_H(I,K_i)\). These distances are well-defined because both bases are satisfiable.

Suppose positive integers \(w_1,w_2\) witness integer-weight obtainability. Define
\[ \]
\( \displaystyle \mu_i=\frac{w_i}{w_1+w_2}\qquad(i=1,2). \)

Then \(\mu_1,\mu_2\) are positive rationals summing to one, and for every interpretation \(I\),
\( \displaystyle \mu_1d_1(I)+\mu_2d_2(I) =\frac{w_1d_1(I)+w_2d_2(I)}{w_1+w_2}. \)

Multiplication of an objective by a positive constant preserves every comparison and equality between objective values. Hence it preserves the entire minimizing set, including all ties. These masses therefore witness obtainability in the question.

Conversely, suppose positive rational masses \(\mu_1,\mu_2\), summing to one, witness obtainability. Choose a positive integer \(D\) divisible by both denominators, and set \(w_i=D\mu_i\). Then \(w_1,w_2\) are positive integers, and
\( \displaystyle w_1d_1(I)+w_2d_2(I) =D\bigl(\mu_1d_1(I)+\mu_2d_2(I)\bigr) \)
for every interpretation \(I\). Again, the entire minimizing set is preserved.

Thus, on every promised input, rational-mass obtainability holds if and only if positive-integer-weight obtainability holds.

Complexity transfer

Theorem 3 of Liberatore’s *Belief revision by examples* establishes \(\Pi_2^p\)-completeness for obtainability using the weighted sum of Hamming distances from exactly two bases. The paper assumes satisfiable bases and a satisfiable observed formula, uses existential positive integer weights, and retains exactly all interpretations of minimum weighted distance. These are precisely the hypotheses and semantics needed here, apart from the equivalent choice of weight domain. [Source: Sections 2–3 and Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.5340).

The equivalence proved above leaves the input formulas and vocabulary unchanged. Consequently, the identity map is a polynomial-time reduction in both directions; no weights need to be computed or included in the input. Both membership in \(\Pi_2^p\) and \(\Pi_2^p\)-hardness therefore transfer.

Finally, \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\Pi_2^p\): an existential polynomially checkable certificate can be preceded by an unused universal quantifier. Thus \(\Pi_2^p\)-hardness implies \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Step-by-step verification

  • Distances are well-defined.

Each \(K_i\) is satisfiable, so its model set is nonempty. The interpretation space is finite, so the minimum defining \(d_H(I,K_i)\) exists for every \(I\). The weighted objective also attains its minimum.

  • Positive integer weights yield admissible rational masses.

Given \(w_1,w_2\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), the proposed definition
\[ \mu_i=\frac{w_i}{w_1+w_2} \]
produces positive rational numbers with sum one. The claimed objective identity is correct:
\[ \mu_1d_1(I)+\mu_2d_2(I) =\frac{w_1d_1(I)+w_2d_2(I)}{w_1+w_2}. \]
The divisor is strictly positive and independent of \(I\), so all comparisons and equalities of objective values are preserved. Consequently, the entire minimizing set, including ties, is preserved.

  • Admissible rational masses yield positive integer weights.

Both masses have positive integer denominators. A positive common multiple \(D\) exists, and \(w_i=D\mu_i\) is a positive integer. The second objective identity is also correct:
\[ w_1d_1(I)+w_2d_2(I) =D\bigl(\mu_1d_1(I)+\mu_2d_2(I)\bigr). \]
Again, multiplication by a common positive constant preserves exactly the minimizing set. There is no approximation or loss of ties.

  • The cited theorem matches the problem.

Theorem 3 states completeness for obtainability using the weighted sum of Hamming distances from two knowledge bases. Sections 2–3 assume consistent propositional bases and a consistent observed formula, define distance to a base by minimizing over its models, require positive integer weights, and select the models of minimum weighted distance. Thus the prover’s description of the theorem and its hypotheses is accurate. The theorem applies to syntactically represented formulas, rather than requiring explicitly enumerated model sets. [Liberatore, *Belief revision by examples*, Sections 2–3 and Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.5340).

The paper uses \(m\) for the number of bases; the task uses \(m\) for the number of Boolean variables. This notation difference does not affect the application: the cited theorem concerns exactly two bases.

  • The complexity transfer is valid.

The equivalence holds for every input satisfying the promises and leaves all input data unchanged. Hence the identity transformation preserves both affirmative and negative answers and is polynomial-time computable.

No bound on the denominators or on \(D\) is needed for this reduction: neither masses nor weights are input data or outputs of the reduction. Membership transfers from the cited integer-weight decision problem because the two decision problems have identical answers on the same promised inputs.

  • The final hardness consequence follows.

The inclusion \(\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\Pi_2^p\) is correctly justified by adding an unused universal quantifier before an existential certificate. Therefore, completeness under polynomial-time many-one reductions implies \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m\) and \(L\) would consequently imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
