The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), \(q\) indivisible good categories with rational per-capita supplies \(b_g\), binary values \(v_{tg}\), and a rational threshold \(c\), decide whether there is a nonnegative allocation measure \(\lambda_{t,z}\) over integral bundles \(z \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{q}\) such that \(\sum_z \lambda_{t,z}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,z}\lambda_{t,z}z_g\le b_g\) for each category \(g\), and every bundle assigned positive mass to type \(t\) satisfies \(\sum_g v_{tg}z_g \ge c\sum_g v_{tg}b_g\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=q\ge1\) good categories, nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(b_g\), additive binary values \(v_{tg}\in\{0,1\}\), and a nonnegative binary-rational threshold \(c\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation measure consists of real masses \(\lambda_{t,z}\ge0\), indexed by \(t\in T\) and integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\), satisfying \(\sum_z\lambda_{t,z}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,z}\lambda_{t,z}z_g\le b_g\) for every category \(g\). Unused supply is permitted; integral bundles have no additional coordinate bounds.
Define the proportional share \(p_t=\sum_g v_{tg}b_g\). Assume \(p_t>0\) for every type and promise that some allocation measure gives strictly positive utility to every bundle assigned positive mass.
The decision problem asks whether an allocation measure exists such that \(\lambda_{t,z}>0\) implies \(\sum_g v_{tg}z_g\ge c p_t\), for every \(t,z\).

Is this decision problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents and explicitly listed indivisible goods with additive binary values, deciding whether every agent can receive at least a specified multiple of her proportional share is polynomial-time solvable ([Nguyen, Nguyen, and Rothe](https://ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2017/pdfs/p262.pdf)). No result settling exactly the allocation-measure problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Identified m with the graded question's number q of good categories; required at least one category and defined L as total input bit length.
  • Specified nonnegative supplies and a nonnegative threshold, consistent with goods and proportional-share multipliers.
  • Carried over the positive proportional-share assumption and the existence of a supply-feasible allocation giving positive utility almost everywhere.
  • Specified allocation masses as nonnegative real numbers, without requiring finite support or rational witnesses.
  • Retained unused supply and unrestricted integral bundle multiplicities as stated in the graded question; supplies constrain aggregate consumption rather than individual bundles.

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable in $m,\tau,L$. It reduces exactly to a maximum-flow problem with rational capacities. Moreover, every feasible instance admits a rational allocation measure supported on at most $m\tau$ type–bundle pairs.

The essential fact is that bundles may contain arbitrarily many copies of a category, while supply constraints apply to aggregate consumption.

1. Integer thresholds

Write $G=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and define
$$ A_t=\{g\in G:v_{tg}=1\},\qquad k_t=\left\lceil c p_t\right\rceil,\qquad d_t=\mu_t k_t. $$
For every integral bundle $z$, its utility to type $t$ is the nonnegative integer
$$ u_t(z)=\sum_{g\in A_t}z_g. $$
Consequently,
$$ u_t(z)\ge c p_t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u_t(z)\ge k_t. $$

Consider the following finite system, with a real variable $x_{tg}$ for each pair satisfying $g\in A_t$:
$$ x_{tg}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{g\in A_t}x_{tg}=d_t\quad(t\in T),\qquad \sum_{t:g\in A_t}x_{tg}\le b_g\quad(g\in G). $$
Here $x_{tg}$ represents aggregate consumption of category $g$ by type $t$.

We prove that this system is feasible exactly when the requested allocation measure exists.

2. From an allocation measure to the finite system

Suppose $\lambda$ is a feasible allocation measure satisfying the thresholds. For each valued pair, define
$$ y_{tg}=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}}\lambda_{t,z}z_g. $$
These sums are finite: nonnegativity and the supply constraint imply $y_{tg}\le b_g$. Also,
$$ \sum_{t:g\in A_t}y_{tg}\le b_g. $$

For each type, let
$$ r_t=\sum_{g\in A_t}y_{tg} =\sum_z\lambda_{t,z}u_t(z). $$
The equality follows by interchanging a finite sum with a series of nonnegative terms. Every bundle of positive mass has utility at least $k_t$, so
$$ r_t\ge k_t\sum_z\lambda_{t,z} =k_t\mu_t=d_t. $$

If $d_t>0$, then $r_t>0$, and we set
$$ x_{tg}=\frac{d_t}{r_t}y_{tg}. $$
If $d_t=0$, set $x_{tg}=0$. In both cases, the row sum is $d_t$ and $0\le x_{tg}\le y_{tg}$. Thus every column sum remains at most $b_g$, proving feasibility of the finite system.

3. From the finite system to an allocation measure

Conversely, suppose $x$ satisfies the finite system. Let $e_g$ denote the $g$th unit vector.

For each type with $k_t>0$, assign masses
$$ \lambda_{t,k_t e_g}=\frac{x_{tg}}{k_t} \qquad(g\in A_t), $$
and assign zero mass to every other bundle. Then
$$ \sum_z\lambda_{t,z} =\frac{1}{k_t}\sum_{g\in A_t}x_{tg} =\frac{d_t}{k_t} =\mu_t. $$
Every assigned bundle consists of $k_t$ copies of a valued category, so its utility is exactly $k_t$, which is at least $c p_t$.

For each type with $k_t=0$, assign its entire mass $\mu_t$ to the zero bundle. Since $c p_t$ is nonnegative and has ceiling zero, this bundle satisfies the threshold.

For every category $g$, aggregate consumption is
$$ \sum_{t,z}\lambda_{t,z}z_g =\sum_{t:g\in A_t}x_{tg} \le b_g. $$
The equality also covers types with $k_t=0$: their consumption is zero, and their nonnegative row variables all vanish.

This proves the equivalence. Each type uses at most $m$ bundles, since a type with zero threshold uses one bundle and $m\ge1$.

4. Maximum-flow algorithm

Let
$$ D=\sum_{t\in T}d_t. $$
If $D=0$, the zero-bundle allocation is feasible, so return YES.

Otherwise, construct a directed network with source $s$, sink $h$, one vertex for each type, and one vertex for each category. Its edges and capacities are:

| Edge | Condition | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| $s\to t$ | $t\in T$ | $d_t$ |
| $t\to g$ | $g\in A_t$ | $D$ |
| $g\to h$ | $g\in G$ | $b_g$ |

A feasible $x$ yields a flow of value $D$: send $d_t$ into each type vertex, $x_{tg}$ along each type–category edge, and the corresponding column sum into the sink. The middle-edge capacities suffice because
$$ x_{tg}\le d_t\le D. $$

Conversely, a flow of value $D$ saturates every source edge, since their capacities sum to $D$. Conservation at each type vertex therefore gives
$$ \sum_{g\in A_t}x_{tg}=d_t, $$
where $x_{tg}$ is the flow along $t\to g$. Conservation and capacity constraints at the category vertices give the required column inequalities.

Hence the algorithm returns YES exactly when the maximum-flow value equals $D$.

5. Bit complexity and conclusion

The network has
$$ V=m+\tau+2,\qquad E\le m\tau+m+\tau. $$
The quantities $p_t$, $c p_t$, $k_t$, $d_t$, and $D$ can all be computed exactly with polynomially many bit operations. Indeed, the denominator of a sum of input rationals divides the product of their denominators; that product has bit length bounded by the sum of the input denominator lengths. Multiplication by $c$ and taking the ceiling preserve polynomial bit length. In particular, a numerically large $k_t$ is stored in binary and is never expanded into individual units.

For an explicit integer-capacity implementation, write the input masses and supplies as
$$ \mu_t=\frac{a_t}{r_t},\qquad b_g=\frac{u_g}{s_g}, $$
and set
$$ Q=\prod_{t\in T}r_t\prod_{g\in G}s_g. $$
This integer has polynomial bit length. Multiplying every network capacity by $Q$ produces integer capacities, because $d_t=k_t\mu_t$ and $D=\sum_t d_t$. All scaled capacities have polynomial bit length.

The standard Edmonds–Karp theorem gives a maximum-flow algorithm using $O(VE^2)$ arithmetic operations, independently of the numerical capacity magnitudes. Its hypotheses hold here: the network is finite and all capacities are nonnegative integers after scaling. Every flow and residual-capacity value has polynomial bit length, so this is a polynomial-time algorithm in the bit model. Dividing the resulting flow by $Q$, and then using the construction in Section 3, also produces a rational allocation measure of polynomial encoding length.

The cited finite-agent tractability result concerns explicitly listed goods; the equivalence proved above directly settles the allocation-measure formulation. [Nguyen, Nguyen, and Rothe](https://ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2017/pdfs/p262.pdf) establish the stated binary-utility tractability result for their finite-agent model.

Thus the requested decision problem belongs to $\mathrm P$. The proof also works without the promise that a positive-utility allocation already exists.
