Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for Borda and for \(\mathrm{Copeland}_{\alpha}\) for every fixed rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). Hardness holds even when all masses are dyadic rationals.

The proof uses the unweighted hardness result supplied in the question and an explicit reduction preserving every possible manipulation.

1. Reduction from unweighted manipulation

Use the following given result: beneficial manipulation by one designated voter under the specified \(T\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for Borda and for each fixed rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\) under \(\mathrm{Copeland}_{\alpha}\). These are Theorems 5.2 and 5.5 of [Obraztsova, Elkind, and Hazon](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2011/papers/BP2_B42.pdf).

Let an instance of that problem consist of an explicitly listed profile \(P=(R_1,\ldots,R_n)\), with designated manipulating voter \(i\). Set \(R^{\star}=R_i\). Preserve the candidate set and its label order.

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two with \(N\geq n\). Choose any ranking \(A\), and let \(\overline A\) be its reverse. Define

\[ \text{} \]

\(b=\dfrac1N,\qquad \eta=\dfrac{N-n}{2N},\qquad \mu=\dfrac1N\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{R_j}+\eta\delta_A+\eta\delta_{\overline A}.\)

Merge identical ranking types and omit zero-mass entries.

All masses are nonnegative, and their sum is \(n/N+2\eta=1\). Moreover, \(\mu(R^{\star})\geq1/N=b>0\). Thus this is a valid target instance. Its support size satisfies \(\tau\leq n+2\).

All masses have denominator dividing \(2N\), so they are dyadic and have \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits. The output length is

\(O\bigl((n+2)m\log(m+1)+(n+2)\log(n+1)\bigr).\)

Sorting the explicit rankings and merging equal ones takes polynomial time.

For any replacement ballot \(L'\), let \(P^{L'}\) denote the unweighted profile obtained by replacing \(R_i\) with \(L'\). The manipulated rational society is exactly

\(\mu^{L'}=\mu-b\delta_{R^{\star}}+b\delta_{L'} =\dfrac1N\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{P^{L'}_j} +\eta\delta_A+\eta\delta_{\overline A}.\)

Consequently, the strategic mass corresponds to precisely one source voter and submits precisely one replacement ballot.

Borda preservation

Write \(s_P(c)\) for the unweighted Borda score of candidate \(c\), using scores \(m-1,\ldots,0\).

If \(c\) occupies position \(r\) in \(A\), it occupies position \(m+1-r\) in \(\overline A\). Its two Borda scores therefore sum to

\((m-r)+(r-1)=m-1.\)

It follows that, for every candidate \(c\),

\(s_{\mu^{L'}}(c)=\dfrac1N s_{P^{L'}}(c)+\eta(m-1).\)

Multiplication by the positive constant \(1/N\) and addition of a candidate-independent constant preserve all score comparisons and equalities. Hence

\(W_{\mathrm{Borda}}(\mu^{L'})=W_{\mathrm{Borda}}(P^{L'}).\)

The same argument applies to the sincere profile, giving \(W_{\mathrm{Borda}}(\mu)=W_{\mathrm{Borda}}(P)\).

Copeland preservation

For distinct candidates \(a,c\), define the unweighted majority margin

\(d_P(a,c)=|\{j:a\text{ precedes }c\text{ in }R_j\}| -|\{j:c\text{ precedes }a\text{ in }R_j\}|.\)

Define the rational majority margin by replacing voter counts with masses. The rankings \(A\) and \(\overline A\) have opposite orientations on every pair, so their equal masses cancel. Therefore

\(d_{\mu^{L'}}(a,c)=\dfrac1N d_{P^{L'}}(a,c).\)

Every pairwise win, loss, and tie is preserved. Thus each candidate has exactly the same Copeland score in both elections, for every \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). In particular,

\(W_{\mathrm{Copeland}_{\alpha}}(\mu^{L'}) =W_{\mathrm{Copeland}_{\alpha}}(P^{L'}),\)

and the sincere winning sets are also equal.

Preservation of beneficial manipulation

For either rule, both the sincere and manipulated winning sets are preserved. The candidate labels are unchanged, so applying the fixed \(T\) gives identical outcomes in the source and target instances.

The sincere preference order is also unchanged. Consequently, for every \(L'\),

\(T(W_F(\mu^{L'}))\succ T(W_F(\mu)) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T(W_F(P^{L'}))\succ T(W_F(P)).\)

This proves a polynomial-time many-one reduction and establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for both rules.

2. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

A certificate is a permutation \(L'\) of the candidates, of length \(O(m\log(m+1))\).

First, \(T\) is polynomial-time computable. Test the possible integers \(1\leq s\leq m\) for the required candidate-count equation. If its exceptional conditions hold, enumerate the \(\ell=\binom{2s}{3}\leq m\) literal triples in lexicographic order and evaluate the selected clauses under the assignment specified by the winning set. This evaluates a supplied assignment; it requires no satisfiability search.

All election calculations can also be performed exactly in polynomial bit complexity. Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses and \(b\). Their total encoding length is at most \(L\), so \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Define the integers

\(q_j=D\mu(R_j),\qquad B=Db.\)

For Borda, the manipulated score multiplied by \(D\) is

\(\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}q_j\bigl(m-\operatorname{rank}_{R_j}(c)\bigr) -B\bigl(m-\operatorname{rank}_{R^{\star}}(c)\bigr) +B\bigl(m-\operatorname{rank}_{L'}(c)\bigr).\)

For Copeland, put \(\sigma_R(a,c)=1\) when \(a\) precedes \(c\), and \(\sigma_R(a,c)=-1\) otherwise. The manipulated majority margin multiplied by \(D\) is

\(\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}q_j\sigma_{R_j}(a,c) -B\sigma_{R^{\star}}(a,c)+B\sigma_{L'}(a,c).\)

Taking its sign determines the pairwise result exactly. Since \(\alpha\) is fixed rational, Copeland scores can subsequently be compared using its fixed denominator.

These integers have \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits. Compute both outcomes, apply \(T\), and compare their positions in \(R^{\star}\). This verifies the certificate in polynomial time, proving membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

3. Running times

Let \(H=L+\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil+1\). Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, the following conservative bit-operation bounds hold.

| Rule | Verification of one certificate | Deterministic decision by enumerating all ballots |
|---|---:|---:|
| Borda | \(O((L+\tau m)H^2)\) | \(O((L+\tau m+m!\,m)H^2)\) |
| \(\mathrm{Copeland}_{\alpha}\) | \(O((L+\tau m^2)H^2)\) | \(O((L+\tau m^2+m!\,m^2)H^2)\) |

For enumeration, compute the original Borda scores or majority margins once, then update them for each ballot. The bounds include exact rational preprocessing and evaluation of \(T\).

Thus both classifications are resolved: both problems are \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
