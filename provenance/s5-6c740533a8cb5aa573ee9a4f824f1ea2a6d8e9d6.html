The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over binary-rational locations on the line, with binary-rational masses summing to \(1\), apply Mechanism 1 to the occupied locations. Does the resulting facility rule achieve a maximum-cost approximation ratio at most \(2\) relative to \(\min_y \max_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)} \min\{|x|,|x-y|\}\), while remaining strategyproof against moving any rational mass \(0<\varepsilon\leq\mu_x\) from a true location \(x\) to a reported location \(r\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(S\subset\mathbb{Q}\) of \(\tau\) distinct locations with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_x\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length. State any running times in \(\tau,L\); the number of facilities is fixed at \(m=2\).
One facility is fixed at \(0\); the new facility may be placed anywhere in \(\mathbb{R}\). Define \(c(x,y)=\min\{|x|,|x-y|\}\), \(MC(\mu,y)=\max_{x\in S}c(x,y)\), and \(OPT(\mu)=\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}}MC(\mu,y)\).
For \(a=\min S\) and \(b=\max S\), define the deterministic rule \(f(\mu)=\begin{cases}\max\{b,-2a\},&|b|\geq|a|,\\\min\{a,-2b\},&|b|<|a|.\end{cases}\) Thus equal endpoint distances select the right-hand branch, and masses affect the rule only through positive support.
A deviation moves binary-rational mass \(0<\varepsilon\leq\mu_x\) from \(x\in S\) to any binary-rational report \(r\in\mathbb{Q}\), producing \(\mu'=\mu-\varepsilon\delta_x+\varepsilon\delta_r\); remove zero-mass locations and merge coincident reports. The moved mass retains true location \(x\). Strategyproofness means \(c(x,f(\mu))\leq c(x,f(\mu'))\) for every such deviation.

Does this rule satisfy \(MC(\mu,f(\mu))\leq2OPT(\mu)\) for every society, including those with \(OPT(\mu)=0\), while remaining strategyproof against every permitted mass deviation?

For finite lists of agents on the line, this rule is strategyproof and achieves maximum-cost approximation ratio at most \(2\); every deterministic strategyproof rule has worst-case ratio at least \(2\). [Qin, Fang and Liu](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30292v1).
The literature search found no result explicitly treating this distribution model with rational mass deviations.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Expanded the mechanism's symmetric branch explicitly and retained its prescribed right-hand tie-breaking when endpoint distances are equal.
  • Restricted reported locations and moved masses to binary rationals, preserving the input domain after deviations.
  • Defined the deviating mass's cost at its true location, with all remaining reports held fixed; coincident reports merge and zero masses disappear.
  • Expressed the approximation guarantee as an inequality so that zero-optimum societies remain included without an undefined ratio.

1. Verdict and counterexample

No, as written. The rule fails both requirements for a negative singleton. However, for every initial society with \(\tau\geq 2\), it satisfies the approximation bound and admits no profitable permitted deviation, including deviations that leave a singleton support. I prove these nondegenerate results below.

Let \(S=\{-1\}\) and \(\mu_{-1}=1\). Then \(a=b=-1\), so the specified tie convention gives
\[ f(\mu)=\max\{-1,2\}=2. \]
Consequently, \(MC(\mu,f(\mu))=1\), whereas placing the new facility at \(-1\) gives \(OPT(\mu)=0\). Thus the requested approximation inequality fails.

Strategyproofness also fails. Move mass \(\varepsilon=\tfrac12\) from the true location \(-1\) to the report \(r=-\tfrac12\). The resulting support has endpoints \(a'=-1\) and \(b'=-\tfrac12\), so
\[ f(\mu')=\min\{-1,1\}=-1. \]
The moved mass’s cost therefore decreases from \(c(-1,2)=1\) to \(c(-1,-1)=0\). All masses and the report are binary-rational.

This exceptional input falls outside the two right-hand cases displayed in [Mechanism 1 of Qin, Fang and Liu](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30292v1). Thus the singleton convention matters when translating that presentation into the formula in the question.

2. A corrected rule and its relation to the stated rule

For any nonempty finite support \(S\), define its left and right extents by \(A=\max\{0,-\min S\}\) and \(B=\max\{0,\max S\}\). Define \(g\) by
\[ g(S)= \begin{cases} \max\{B,2A\},&B\geq A,\\ -\max\{A,2B\},&A>B. \end{cases} \]

The rules \(f\) and \(g\) agree except when \(S=\{x\}\) with \(x<0\):

  • If \(S\) is nonnegative, both rules output \(\max S\).
  • If \(S\) is nonpositive and contains at least two locations, then \(\min S<\max S\), so both output \(\min S\).
  • If \(S\) contains both negative and positive locations, substituting \(A=-a\) and \(B=b\) makes the formulas identical.
  • For \(S=\{0\}\), both output \(0\). For a negative singleton \(S=\{x\}\), however, \(f(S)=-2x\) and \(g(S)=x\).

Thus \(g\) changes only the negative-singleton outcome. The next two sections prove that \(g\) satisfies both requirements universally.

3. Proven approximation bound for \(g\)

Fix a support \(S\). I prove \(MC(S,g(S))\leq 2MC(S,z)\) for every candidate location \(z\in\mathbb R\). Taking the minimum over \(z\) then gives the desired bound, without dividing by \(OPT\).

First suppose \(B\geq A\), and put \(R=MC(S,z)\).

3.1. The endpoint lower bound

We have \(R\geq A\). This is immediate if \(A=0\). Otherwise, both \(-A\) and \(B\) belong to \(S\), and \(B\geq A>0\).

If \(z\geq0\), then \(c(-A,z)=A\). If \(z<0\), then \(c(B,z)=B\geq A\). Either way, \(R\geq A\).

3.2. The case \(B\leq2A\)

Here \(g(S)=2A\).

Every nonpositive support point has cost at most \(A\), using the facility at \(0\). Every nonnegative support point \(t\) satisfies \(0\leq t\leq B\leq2A\), and hence
\[ c(t,2A)=\min\{t,2A-t\}\leq A. \]
Therefore \(MC(S,g(S))\leq A\leq R\).

3.3. The case \(B>2A\)

Here \(g(S)=B\). Nonpositive support points again have cost at most \(A\leq R\).

Consider a positive support point \(t\). If \(t\leq R\), then \(c(t,B)\leq t\leq R\). Otherwise \(B\geq t>R\). Since both points have cost at most \(R\) under the candidate \(z\), neither can obtain that bound from the facility at \(0\). Thus
\[ |t-z|\leq R,\qquad |B-z|\leq R. \]
The triangle inequality gives \(B-t\leq2R\), and therefore \(c(t,B)\leq B-t\leq2R\).

Taking the maximum over the support yields \(MC(S,g(S))\leq2R\).

3.4. The left-hand branch and zero optimum

If \(A>B\), reflect the support through \(0\). The reflected extents are \(A_{\mathrm{ref}}=B\) and \(B_{\mathrm{ref}}=A\), so its right-hand branch outputs \(-g(S)\). Reflection preserves costs because \(c(-x,-z)=c(x,z)\). The preceding proof therefore applies.

We have proved, for every society,
\[ MC(\mu,g(\mu))\leq2OPT(\mu). \]
In particular, \(OPT(\mu)=0\) forces \(MC(\mu,g(\mu))=0\).

4. Proven strategyproofness of \(g\) under mass deviations

The following support-level argument includes partial-mass deviations.

Let \(T\) be any finite residual support, possibly empty. For real \(x,r\), compare the initial support \(T\cup\{x\}\) with the changed support \(T\cup\{r\}\). Define
\[ \alpha=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{-t:t\in T\}\bigr),\qquad \beta=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup T\bigr). \]
Write \(y=g(T\cup\{x\})\) and \(y'=g(T\cup\{r\})\). The changed extents always satisfy \(A'\geq\alpha\) and \(B'\geq\beta\).

I prove \(c(x,y)\leq c(x,y')\).

4.1. Positive true location

Suppose \(x>0\). Initially, \(A=\alpha\) and \(B=\max\{\beta,x\}\).

If \(A>B\), then \(y<0\) and the initial cost is \(x\). A changed outcome \(y'\leq0\) also gives cost \(x\). If \(y'>0\), its defining branch gives
\[ y'=\max\{B',2A'\}\geq2\alpha>2x, \]
so its cost is again \(x\).

Now suppose \(B\geq A\). Put \(K=\max\{\beta,2\alpha\}\), so \(y=\max\{x,K\}\).

If \(K\leq x\), then \(y=x\), and the initial cost is zero. If \(K>x\), then \(y=K\). Every changed positive outcome satisfies \(y'\geq K\), and hence
\[ c(x,y')=\min\{x,y'-x\}\geq\min\{x,K-x\}=c(x,y). \]
A changed nonpositive outcome gives cost \(x\), which also cannot improve upon the initial cost.

4.2. Negative true location

Suppose \(x=-u<0\), where \(u>0\). Initially, \(A=\max\{\alpha,u\}\) and \(B=\beta\).

If \(B\geq A\), then \(y>0\) and the initial cost is \(u\). A changed outcome \(y'\geq0\) also gives cost \(u\). If \(y'<0\), then
\[ |y'|=\max\{A',2B'\}\geq2\beta\geq2u, \]
so \(|x-y'|=|y'|-u\geq u\), and its cost remains \(u\).

Now suppose \(A>B\). Put \(H=\max\{\alpha,2\beta\}\), so \(y=-\max\{u,H\}\).

If \(H\leq u\), the initial outcome is \(x\), giving cost zero. If \(H>u\), the initial outcome is \(-H\). Every changed negative outcome satisfies \(|y'|\geq H\), and hence
\[ c(x,y')=\min\{u,|y'|-u\}\geq\min\{u,H-u\}=c(x,y). \]
A changed nonnegative outcome gives cost \(u\), which cannot improve upon the initial cost.

For \(x=0\), every outcome gives cost zero. This completes the support-level proof, including the specified endpoint tie.

4.3. Application to the permitted deviations

For a permitted deviation, let \(T=\operatorname{supp}(\mu-\varepsilon\delta_x)\). Positivity of the remaining masses gives exactly
\[ \operatorname{supp}(\mu)=T\cup\{x\},\qquad \operatorname{supp}(\mu')=T\cup\{r\}. \]
If mass remains at \(x\), then \(x\in T\); if all its mass moves, then \(x\notin T\). Coincident reports are automatically covered by set union.

The proved inequality therefore applies to every permitted deviation. In fact, it holds for arbitrary real reports, so the binary-rational restriction causes no additional difficulty.

5. What remains true for the original rule

For every initial society with \(\tau\geq2\), we have \(f(\mu)=g(\mu)\). Section 3 therefore proves the approximation inequality for all such societies, including those with zero optimum.

For strategyproofness, Section 4 applies whenever the changed support is not a negative singleton. It remains to check precisely that exceptional changed support.

Suppose an initial support of size at least two becomes \(\{t\}\), where \(t=-u<0\). A single permitted deviation can do this only from the initial support \(\{t,x\}\), by moving all mass at \(x\neq t\) to \(t\). The changed original-rule outcome is \(f(\mu')=2u\).

If \(x\leq0\), the changed cost is \(|x|\), which is at least the initial cost.

If \(0<x<u\), the initial rule selects a negative outcome, so its cost is \(x\). The changed outcome \(2u>2x\) also gives cost \(x\).

If \(x\geq u\), the initial outcome is \(\max\{x,2u\}\). When \(x\leq2u\), this equals the changed outcome. When \(x>2u\), the initial cost is zero. Neither case permits an improvement.

Thus every initial society with \(\tau\geq2\) is strategyproof against every permitted deviation under the original rule, even when the deviation leaves a negative singleton.

6. Tightness and computation

The factor \(2\) is attained on the nondegenerate support \(S=\{1,2\}\), with masses \(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\). Both rules output \(2\), giving maximum cost \(1\). The optimum is \(\tfrac12\), attained at \(\tfrac32\): a smaller maximum cost would require both points to lie within a distance smaller than \(\tfrac12\) of the new facility, contradicting their separation of \(1\).

Both rules can be evaluated by scanning the \(\tau\) support locations. Using schoolbook rational arithmetic gives the conservative bound \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations. No expansion into individual agents is required.
