The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite rational mass distributions \(\mu^B\) over buyer values and \(\mu^S\) over seller costs in \([0,1]\), does the rule that trades when \(\bar v>\bar c\) and sets the shared per-member payment and receipt to \((\bar v+\bar c)/2\) achieve group IR and first-best gains from trade in the exact-mass population, while remaining IC and SBB?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Two nonatomic societies, buyers and sellers, each have total mass \(1\) and trade one indivisible item non-excludably. Their publicly known exact type distributions \(\mu^B,\mu^S\) have nonempty finite supports of rational values and costs in \([0,1]\).
A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Let \(\tau\) be the combined support size, not the number of possible types, and \(L\) the total input bit length; state any running times in \(m,\tau,L\), with \(m=1\) item.
Each member privately knows its type and may report any number in \([0,1]\); the public distributions are fixed independently of reports. Write \(\bar v=\int v\,d\mu^B\) and \(\bar c=\int c\,d\mu^S\).
The rule ignores reports and uses shared allocation \(x=\mathbf 1\{\bar v>\bar c\}\) and shared payment and receipt \(p=r=x(\bar v+\bar c)/2\). Buyer and seller utilities are respectively \(xv-p\) and \(r-xc\); utility ties favor trade.
Group IR means each society's integrated utility is nonnegative, with strictly positive utilities required when trade occurs. IC means dominant-strategy truthfulness for individual members; SBB means equality of total payments and receipts. GFT is \(x(\bar v-\bar c)\), and FB is its maximum over the two possible shared allocations.

Does this rule achieve group IR and GFT equal to FB for every such exact-mass population, while satisfying IC and SBB, or can any of these guarantees fail?

For equal finite groups with independent draws from known bounded priors, the corresponding report-independent rule is IC and SBB; under a strictly positive mean gap, group IR holds with probability \(1-e^{-\Omega(n)}\) and its expected GFT/FB ratio is \(1-e^{-\Omega(n)}\). [Xu, Zhang, and Conitzer](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11800v1)
No result settling precisely this exact-mass formulation was identified in the literature search.

  • Interpreted the input distributions as public, fixed exact population distributions, corresponding to the known priors used by the discrete rule; they are not distributions computed from strategic reports.
  • Represented each society as nonatomic; types with positive mass describe populations of individual members rather than single strategic agents controlling those masses.
  • Allowed individual reports throughout [0,1] and used dominant-strategy IC with utility ties favoring trade.

  • Used nonnegative integrated utilities for group IR, requiring strict positivity when trade occurs; literal strict positivity without trade would contradict the intended participation condition.

Conclusion and definitions

All four guarantees hold for every population in the question. Strict positivity when trade occurs holds for each society’s integrated utility, as required by the stated definition of group IR.

Write the buyer distribution as \(\mu^B=\sum_{i=1}^{b}\alpha_i\delta_{v_i}\) and the seller distribution as \(\mu^S=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\beta_j\delta_{c_j}\), where all masses are positive and \(\sum_i\alpha_i=\sum_j\beta_j=1\). Thus \(\tau=b+s\),

\(\bar v=\sum_i\alpha_i v_i,\qquad \bar c=\sum_j\beta_j c_j.\)

Define \(\Delta=\bar v-\bar c\). The rule sets \(x=\mathbf 1\{\Delta>0\}\) and \(p=r=x(\bar v+\bar c)/2\).

Group individual rationality

Since every buyer pays the same amount and the buyer society has total mass \(1\), its integrated utility is

\(U^B=\int(xv-p)\,d\mu^B=x\bar v-p=x\Delta/2.\)

Similarly, the seller society’s integrated utility is

\(U^S=\int(r-xc)\,d\mu^S=r-x\bar c=x\Delta/2.\)

There are two cases:

  • If \(\Delta>0\), the rule trades, and \(U^B=U^S=\Delta/2>0\).
  • If \(\Delta\leq0\), the rule sets \(x=p=r=0\), and \(U^B=U^S=0\).

These cases exhaust all populations and establish precisely the required group IR.

Individual members can have negative utility. For example, if buyers have types \(0\) and \(1\), each with mass \(1/2\), and every seller has cost \(0\), then \(p=1/4\). Buyers of type \(0\) obtain utility \(-1/4\), while each society’s integrated utility is \(1/4\). This does not violate the specified group IR.

First-best gains from trade

For either possible shared allocation \(y\in\{0,1\}\), aggregate gains from trade are

\(\mathrm{GFT}(y)=\int yv\,d\mu^B-\int yc\,d\mu^S=y\Delta.\)

Consequently,

\(\mathrm{FB}=\max_{y\in\{0,1\}}y\Delta=\max\{0,\Delta\}.\)

The rule obtains

\(\mathrm{GFT}=x\Delta=\mathbf 1\{\Delta>0\}\Delta=\max\{0,\Delta\}=\mathrm{FB}.\)

This equality also covers \(\Delta=0\): both allocations yield zero gains, and the stated rule chooses no trade. No approximation ratio is needed when \(\mathrm{FB}=0\).

Incentive compatibility and strong budget balance

Fix any buyer’s true type \(v\), every other member’s reports, and any alternative report \(a\in[0,1]\). The public distributions are fixed independently of reports, so reporting either \(v\) or \(a\) produces exactly the same allocation and payment. Hence

\(u^B(v;\text{report }v)=xv-p=u^B(v;\text{report }a).\)

Truthful reporting therefore weakly dominates every alternative report. The same argument gives, for every seller’s true cost \(c\) and alternative report \(a\),

\(u^S(c;\text{report }c)=r-xc=u^S(c;\text{report }a).\)

Thus the rule is dominant-strategy IC. The convention that utility ties favor trade cannot distinguish these reports: they produce the same trade decision as well as the same monetary utility.

Finally, total payments and receipts are

\(P=\int p\,d\mu^B=p,\qquad R=\int r\,d\mu^S=r.\)

Because \(p=r\), strong budget balance holds for every report profile.

Equality, exact masses, and computation

At \(\bar v=\bar c\), the explicit allocation formula prescribes no trade. If one instead changed the formula to require trade at equality, SBB would imply \(U^B+U^S=\bar v-\bar c=0\), preventing both integrated utilities from being strictly positive. The strict inequality in the given rule therefore handles this boundary consistently with strict group IR.

The finite-sample setting in [Xu, Zhang, and Conitzer](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11800v1) involves independent draws from priors. Here, the public distributions specify the actual population masses exactly, so the integrated utilities above are deterministic; no concentration argument is required.

For \(m=1\), the rule can be evaluated using \(O(\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations. Computing the means with unreduced common denominators keeps intermediate integers at \(O(L)\) bits: each denominator is a product of input denominators whose total bit length is \(O(L)\). Ordinary integer arithmetic therefore gives a conservative running time of \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations.
