The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given atomless left and right populations with finitely many interchangeable types, rational type masses, degree parameters satisfying \(d_L\ell=d_Rr\), common rankings of counterpart types, and additive utilities consistent with those rankings, construct a compactly represented simple measurable \(d_L\)-/\(d_R\)-regular matching whose every individual bundle satisfies the pointwise SD-DEF1 prefix inequalities on both sides; in the degree-two variant, also require every individual to receive at least their continuous maximin-share value.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, each mass encoded as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Write \(\tau=\tau_L+\tau_R\), \(m=\max\{\tau_L,\tau_R\}\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Realize the populations as disjoint Lebesgue intervals \(X=[0,\ell)\) and \(Y=[0,r)\), partitioned into half-open type intervals of the specified masses; \(\ell,r>0\) are not normalized. Input positive rational degree masses satisfying \(d_L\le r\), \(d_R\le\ell\), and \(d_L\ell=d_Rr\).
Each side has a common linear ranking of counterpart types. Each type has nonnegative binary-rational utility densities over counterpart types, weakly decreasing along that ranking; equal values use the supplied tie order. Utilities of measurable bundles are their density integrals.
A simple matching is a jointly measurable indicator \(W:X\times Y\to\{0,1\}\) with measurable sections \(B_x=\{y:W(x,y)=1\}\) and \(C_y=\{x:W(x,y)=1\}\). Validity means \(\lambda(B_x)\le d_L\) and \(\lambda(C_y)\le d_R\) for every individual; regularity means equality everywhere.
Pointwise SD-DEF1 means \(\lambda(B_x\cap P)\ge\lambda(B_{x'}\cap P)-1\) for all \(x,x'\in X\) and every union \(P\) of an initial segment of ranked right types, with the analogous inequalities for all \(y,y'\in Y\) and left-type prefixes.
For a left type \(a\), define \(\operatorname{MMS}_a=\sup_{V\text{ valid}}\inf_{x'\in X}\int_{B_{x'}^V}u_a(y)\,dy\), allowing all simple measurable valid matchings; define right-type benchmarks symmetrically. In the variant \(d_L=d_R=2\), require every individual's utility to reach their type's benchmark.
A compact output represents \(\{W=1\}\) by a finite Boolean formula of rational affine inequalities in the two population coordinates, including its boundary conventions.

Is feasibility of a regular pointwise SD-DEF1 matching in \(\mathrm{P}\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, both generally and with the degree-two MMS requirement? In any tractable case, can a compact matching be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?

For finite populations with balanced total degrees and common ordinal rankings on each side, a complete SD-DEF1 matching exists and is computable in polynomial time; the degree-two case also admits simultaneous DMMS [Freeman–Micha–Shah](https://www.rupertfreeman.com/Matching-Fair-Division-Full.pdf).
No result settling exactly the atomless problem specified here was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Realized atomless populations as Lebesgue intervals partitioned by type, with arbitrary positive total masses rather than probability normalization.
  • Interpreted degrees as positive rational neighborhood masses and imposed the counterpart-mass bounds required by simplicity.
  • Specified nonnegative rational utility densities, permitted cardinal differences between types sharing the common ordinal ranking, and fixed utility ties by the supplied type order.
  • Interpreted prefixes as unions of whole ranked types and retained the SD-DEF1 allowance of one unit of mass.
  • Required degree, fairness, and MMS conditions for every individual, including boundary points, rather than almost everywhere.
  • Defined continuous MMS using a supremum over all valid simple measurable matchings and an ordinary infimum over all same-side individuals; validity retains both degree bounds.
  • Made compact representation precise as a finite Boolean formula of rational affine inequalities with explicit boundary conventions.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Both feasibility problems are in \(\mathrm{P}\). Every instance satisfying the stated hypotheses admits a regular matching that is pointwise SD-envy-free, and every individual simultaneously attains their type’s MMS benchmark. A compact matching can be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The construction works for arbitrary positive rational degree masses satisfying the hypotheses, including the degree-two variant.

1. A binary band with constant section lengths

Set
\[ p=\frac{d_L}{r}=\frac{d_R}{\ell}. \]
The equality follows from degree balance, and the degree bounds imply \(0<p\le 1\).

For \(s,t\in[0,1)\), define
\[ K_p(s,t)=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0\le s+t<p \ \text{ or }\ 1\le s+t<1+p. \]

Every horizontal and vertical section of this indicator has Lebesgue measure exactly \(p\), including sections at boundary coordinates.

To verify the horizontal assertion, fix \(s\in[0,1)\). If \(s\le p\), the permitted \(t\)-values are
\[ [0,p-s)\ \cup\ [1-s,1), \]
whose total length is \((p-s)+s=p\). Empty intervals at the endpoints cause no difficulty. If \(s>p\), the permitted interval is
\[ [1-s,1+p-s), \]
which lies inside \([0,1)\) and has length \(p\). Symmetry proves the vertical assertion. These calculations also cover \(p=1\).

2. Constructing the matching

Write the supplied left-type intervals as \(I_a=[\alpha_a,\alpha_a+A_a)\), where \(A_a>0\), and the right-type intervals as \(J_b=[\beta_b,\beta_b+B_b)\), where \(B_b>0\). Thus \(\sum_a A_a=\ell\) and \(\sum_b B_b=r\).

On each rectangle \(I_a\times J_b\), define
\[ W(x,y)=K_p\!\left(\frac{x-\alpha_a}{A_a}, \frac{y-\beta_b}{B_b}\right). \]

The half-open partitions assign every individual to exactly one interval. The formula therefore defines \(W\) everywhere on \(X\times Y\).

It is a binary indicator given by finitely many rational affine inequalities. Consequently it is jointly Borel measurable and has measurable sections.

By the section calculation in Section 1 and the change of scale, for every \(x\in X\) and every right type \(b\),
\[ \lambda(B_x\cap J_b)=pB_b. \]
Similarly, for every \(y\in Y\) and every left type \(a\),
\[ \lambda(C_y\cap I_a)=pA_a. \]

Summing over the counterpart types gives
\[ \lambda(B_x)=pr=d_L \quad\text{and}\quad \lambda(C_y)=p\ell=d_R. \]
Thus the matching is regular everywhere.

If \(P\) is any union of right-type intervals, then every left individual receives exactly \(p\lambda(P)\) mass from \(P\). Hence
\[ \lambda(B_x\cap P)=\lambda(B_{x'}\cap P) \]
for all \(x,x'\). The corresponding assertion holds on the right.

In particular, every ranked prefix has identical mass in every bundle on its side. This proves pointwise SD-envy-freeness, and therefore pointwise SD-DEF1. The common-ranking assumption is unnecessary for this construction.

3. Computing the MMS benchmarks exactly

Fix a left type \(a\), with utility density \(u_a\). I claim that
\[ \operatorname{MMS}_a=p\int_Y u_a(y)\,dy. \]

First, let \(V\) be any valid simple measurable matching, and put
\[ U_a^V(x)=\int_Y V(x,y)u_a(y)\,dy. \]
The density is bounded, nonnegative, and measurable because it is constant on finitely many types. The populations have finite measure. Fubini’s theorem therefore applies and yields
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac1\ell\int_X U_a^V(x)\,dx &=\frac1\ell\int_Y u_a(y)\left(\int_X V(x,y)\,dx\right)\,dy\\ &=\frac1\ell\int_Y u_a(y)\lambda(C_y^V)\,dy\\ &\le \frac{d_R}{\ell}\int_Y u_a(y)\,dy\\ &=p\int_Y u_a(y)\,dy. \end{aligned} \]
The inequality uses validity for every \(y\), together with nonnegativity of \(u_a\).

An infimum cannot exceed the average: integrating the pointwise inequality \(U_a^V(x)\ge\inf_{x'\in X}U_a^V(x')\) proves this directly. Hence every valid \(V\) satisfies
\[ \inf_{x'\in X}U_a^V(x') \le p\int_Y u_a(y)\,dy. \]
Taking the supremum over \(V\) gives the claimed upper bound.

For the constructed matching, let \(u_{ab}\) denote type \(a\)’s density on \(J_b\). For every \(x'\in X\),
\[ U_a^W(x') =\sum_b u_{ab}\lambda(B_{x'}\cap J_b) =p\sum_b u_{ab}B_b =p\int_Y u_a(y)\,dy. \]
The matching is valid, so it supplies the reverse inequality in the MMS definition. Thus the claimed equality holds, and its supremum is attained.

Repeating the argument with the sides exchanged gives, for every right type \(b\) with density \(v_b\),
\[ \operatorname{MMS}_b=p\int_X v_b(x)\,dx. \]

Consequently, every individual simultaneously receives utility exactly equal to their type’s benchmark.

In the degree-two variant, balance implies \(\ell=r\), and the bounds imply \(\ell=r\ge2\). Taking \(p=2/\ell\) in the same construction proves feasibility with all required MMS guarantees.

4. Compact representation and polynomial construction

The definition can be written without division in its affine inequalities. For each type pair, set
\[ H_{ab}(x,y)=B_b(x-\alpha_a)+A_a(y-\beta_b). \]
Its matching region is specified by the rectangle bounds
\[ \alpha_a\le x<\alpha_a+A_a, \qquad \beta_b\le y<\beta_b+B_b, \]
together with
\[ 0\le H_{ab}(x,y)<pA_aB_b \quad\text{or}\quad A_aB_b\le H_{ab}(x,y)<(1+p)A_aB_b. \]

Disjoin these formulas over all type pairs. This is precisely the required finite Boolean formula of rational affine inequalities. The displayed strict and non-strict inequalities specify all boundary conventions.

There are \(\tau_L\tau_R\le m^2\) blocks, each containing a constant number of inequalities. All interval endpoints and \(p\) are computed by rational sums and divisions. Their bit lengths are \(O(L)\): a common denominator for a sum can be chosen as the product of its input denominators, whose total bit length is bounded by the input length. The additional coefficients involve only a constant number of products and sums of these rationals, so they also have \(O(L)\) bit length.

The output therefore has \(O(\tau_L\tau_R L)\) bits, and exact rational arithmetic constructs it in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). No expansion into unit-mass individuals is needed.

5. Conclusion

Both decision problems always answer yes on admissible inputs, and both have polynomial-time compact construction algorithms.

The [finite-population results of Freeman–Micha–Shah](https://www.rupertfreeman.com/Matching-Fair-Division-Full.pdf) concern indivisible counterparts. Here, subdivision inside each positive-mass type interval permits the identical type-mass profiles proved above.
