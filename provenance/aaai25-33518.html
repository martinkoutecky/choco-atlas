The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_t\) for finitely many complete agent types, rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\) of finitely many chore classes, and positive rational factored costs \(v_t(j)\), define \(\theta_t\) as the minimum \(\theta\) admitting a finite rational distribution \(y\) over integral bundles \(b\) in \(\mathbb{Z}_+^r\) with \(\sum_b y_b=1\), \(\sum_b b_j y_b=q_j\), and \(v_t(b)\le\theta\) on its support. Compute, or decide feasibility of, a finite rational distribution \(x_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b} b_j x_{t,b}=q_j\), and \(v_t(b)\le\theta_t\) whenever \(x_{t,b}>0\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) complete types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) chore classes with positive binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\). Type \(t\) specifies positive rational additive costs \(v_t(j)\); its distinct costs, sorted increasingly as \(a_{t,1}<\cdots<a_{t,k_t}\), satisfy \(a_{t,\ell+1}/a_{t,\ell}\in\mathbb Z_{\geq2}\). These integer ratios may vary by level and type.
Bundles are \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\), including the empty bundle, with \(v_t(b)=\sum_jv_t(j)b_j\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; bundle coordinates are encoded in binary.
Define \(\theta_t\) as the minimum \(\theta\geq0\) admitting a finitely supported rational distribution \(y_b\geq0\) with \(\sum_b y_b=1\), \(\sum_b b_jy_b=q_j\) for every \(j\), and \(v_t(b)\leq\theta\) whenever \(y_b>0\).
The allocation problem is to compute finitely supported rational masses \(x_{t,b}\geq0\), or decide their feasibility, subject to \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}b_jx_{t,b}=q_j\), and \(v_t(b)\leq\theta_t\) whenever \(x_{t,b}>0\). The thresholds are defined by the input, not supplied separately.
Is this distributional allocation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite societies with explicitly listed chores and factored additive costs, HFFD at the agents’ MMS thresholds allocates all chores, and an exact MMS allocation is computable in polynomial time ([Garg–Huang–Segal-Halevi](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04391v1)). Factored-cost MMS values and optimal partitions are also polynomial-time computable ([Ebadian–Peters–Shah](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2022/pdfs/p372.pdf)).
No result settling this exact distributional formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one, so the per-capita supplies are also the aggregate supplies appearing in the allocation constraints.
  • Defined factored rational costs by integer ratios between consecutive distinct costs, allowing different ratios at different levels and for different types, as in the substantive definition in the full version.
  • Used m for the number of chore classes, replacing r; required positive supplies for represented classes, with zero-supply classes omitted.
  • Allowed empty bundles and required all distribution weights and allocation masses to be nonnegative rationals with finite support.
  • Specified binary encoding of bundle coordinates and total input bit length L; the thresholds must be derived from the input.

Polynomial-time solution

The problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Every valid instance is feasible. Moreover, all thresholds can be computed exactly, and an allocation can be output using at most \(\tau(2m+1)\) nonzero type–bundle masses, each of polynomial binary encoding length.

The exact threshold formula is

\( \displaystyle \theta_t= \max_{1\leq \ell\leq k_t} a_{t,\ell} \left\lceil \sum_{\{j:\,v_t(j)\geq a_{t,\ell}\}} \frac{v_t(j)}{a_{t,\ell}}\,q_j \right\rceil . \)

We prove the formula and give a constructive algorithm.

1. Feasibility versus computation

Once threshold-attaining distributions \(y^t\) have been computed, define

\( \displaystyle x_{t,b}=\mu_t y^t_b. \)

Then

\( \displaystyle \sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t,b}b_jx_{t,b} =\sum_t\mu_t q_j=q_j, \)

and every positive mass respects its type’s threshold.

Thus feasibility follows directly from the threshold definitions, provided the minima are attained. The substantive computational task is to find threshold-attaining distributions with polynomially bounded representations. The following lemma establishes both attainment and efficient construction under the factored-cost hypothesis.

2. An exact convex-hull lemma for divisible weights

Let \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) be positive integers satisfying

\( \displaystyle 1=w_1\leq w_2\leq\cdots\leq w_m, \qquad w_i\mid w_{i+1}\quad(1\leq i<m). \)

Equal consecutive weights are allowed. For an integer \(C\geq0\), define

\( \displaystyle B_C=\left\{ b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m: \sum_{j=1}^m w_jb_j\leq C \right\}, \qquad d_i=w_i\left\lfloor\frac{C}{w_i}\right\rfloor, \)

and

\( \displaystyle Q_C=\left\{ z\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m: \sum_{j=i}^m w_jz_j\leq d_i \text{ for every }i \right\}. \)

Lemma. We have \(\operatorname{conv}(B_C)=Q_C\). Every rational point of \(Q_C\) can be decomposed into a rational convex combination of at most \(2m+1\) elements of \(B_C\), in polynomial time in its binary input length.

Necessity

For \(b\in B_C\), the integer \(\sum_{j=i}^m w_jb_j\) is a multiple of \(w_i\), because \(w_i\) divides every later weight. It is also at most \(C\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{j=i}^m w_jb_j \leq w_i\left\lfloor\frac{C}{w_i}\right\rfloor=d_i. \)

These linear inequalities also hold for convex combinations. Hence \(\operatorname{conv}(B_C)\subseteq Q_C\).

Integer bundles associated with a support

The sequence \(d_1,\ldots,d_m\) is nonincreasing: \(d_{i+1}\) is a multiple of \(w_i\) at most \(C\), whereas \(d_i\) is the largest such multiple.

For a nonempty index set \(S=\{p_1<\cdots<p_s\}\), construct \(b(S)\) by

\( \displaystyle b(S)_{p_h}= \begin{cases} (d_{p_h}-d_{p_{h+1}})/w_{p_h},&h<s,\\ d_{p_s}/w_{p_s},&h=s, \end{cases} \)

and set all other coordinates to zero. Also define \(b(\varnothing)=0\).

These coordinates are nonnegative integers. Indeed, the numerators are nonnegative, and \(w_{p_h}\) divides both terms in each difference.

If \(p_h\) is the first member of \(S\) at least \(i\), telescoping gives

\( \displaystyle \sum_{j=i}^m w_jb(S)_j=d_{p_h}\leq d_i. \)

If no such member exists, the sum is zero. Therefore \(b(S)\in Q_C\); its total weighted cost is at most \(d_1=C\), so \(b(S)\in B_C\).

Constructive decomposition

Start with \(z\in Q_C\), and initialize a remaining mass \(\alpha=1\) and a residual vector \(r=z\). Maintain

\( \displaystyle \alpha\geq0,\qquad r_j\geq0,\qquad s_i:=\alpha d_i-\sum_{j=i}^m w_jr_j\geq0. \)

Suppose \(\alpha>0\). Let \(S=\{j:r_j>0\}\), choose \(b=b(S)\), and define

\( \displaystyle h_i=d_i-\sum_{j=i}^m w_jb_j\geq0. \)

We first establish the property that makes the procedure terminate: every currently zero coordinate or slack remains zero when a mass of \(b\) is removed.

If \(r_j=0\), then \(j\notin S\), so \(b_j=0\).

If \(s_i=0\) and \(p_h\) is the first member of \(S\) at least \(i\), then the tails of \(r\) starting at \(i\) and at \(p_h\) coincide. Thus feasibility at \(p_h\) gives

\( \displaystyle \alpha d_i =\sum_{j=p_h}^m w_jr_j \leq\alpha d_{p_h} \leq\alpha d_i. \)

Since \(\alpha>0\), it follows that \(d_i=d_{p_h}\). The telescoping identity for \(b(S)\) now gives \(h_i=0\). If there is no member of \(S\) at least \(i\), then \(s_i=\alpha d_i=0\), so again \(h_i=0\).

Choose

\( \displaystyle \delta= \min\left( \{\alpha\} \cup \left\{\frac{r_j}{b_j}:b_j>0\right\} \cup \left\{\frac{s_i}{h_i}:h_i>0\right\} \right). \)

All included ratios are strictly positive: a positive denominator cannot correspond to a zero numerator, by the property just proved. Record the mass \(\delta\) on bundle \(b\), and update

\( \displaystyle \alpha\leftarrow\alpha-\delta, \qquad r\leftarrow r-\delta b. \)

The new upper-constraint slacks are \(s_i-\delta h_i\). The choice of \(\delta\) therefore preserves all invariants.

If the new \(\alpha\) is zero, the first upper constraint and nonnegativity imply \(r=0\). Otherwise, at least one previously positive coordinate \(r_j\) or slack \(s_i\) becomes zero. All zero coordinates and slacks remain zero in subsequent iterations.

There are only \(2m\) coordinates and upper-constraint slacks. Hence the procedure terminates after at most \(2m+1\) iterations. Writing its recorded outputs as \((\delta_h,b^h)\), telescoping the updates yields

\( \displaystyle \sum_h\delta_h=1, \qquad z=\sum_h\delta_h b^h. \)

Every \(b^h\) belongs to \(B_C\). This proves \(Q_C\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(B_C)\), and all recorded masses are rational when \(z\) is rational.

Binary encoding bounds

The case \(C=0\) has only the point \(z=0\), so assume \(C\geq1\).

Choose an initial common denominator \(D_0\) for the coordinates of \(z\), for example the product of their input denominators. Its binary length is polynomial in the input length.

If \(\alpha\) and all coordinates of \(r\) have common denominator \(D\), so do all slacks \(s_i\). The denominators \(b_j\) and \(h_i\) appearing in the ratios above are integers between \(1\) and \(C\). Thus, after an update, a common denominator can be chosen as \(Dg\) for some integer \(1\leq g\leq C\); choosing \(\delta=\alpha\) allows \(g=1\).

After at most \(2m+1\) updates, a common denominator is therefore bounded by

\( \displaystyle D_0C^{\,2m+1}. \)

Its binary length is polynomial. Also, \(\alpha\leq1\), \(r_j\leq C\), and \(s_i\leq C\), so the corresponding numerators have polynomial binary length.

Each iteration uses a polynomial number of exact arithmetic operations and comparisons. This proves the algorithmic assertion of the lemma.

3. Computing the exact thresholds

Fix a type \(t\). Reorder its chore classes by nondecreasing cost, and write \(a=a_{t,1}\). Define

\( \displaystyle w_j=\frac{v_t(j)}a. \)

The factored-cost hypothesis implies that these are positive integers satisfying the lemma’s divisibility conditions, with \(w_1=1\). Supplies are reordered by the same permutation.

Every bundle cost is an integer multiple of \(a\). Consequently, a threshold \(\theta\) allows exactly the bundles in \(B_C\), where

\( \displaystyle C=\left\lfloor\frac{\theta}{a}\right\rfloor. \)

By the lemma, a rational distribution over these bundles with mean \(q\) exists exactly when

\( \displaystyle \sum_{j=i}^m w_jq_j \leq w_i\left\lfloor\frac C{w_i}\right\rfloor \quad\text{for every }i. \)

For each \(i\), this is equivalent to

\( \displaystyle C\geq w_i\left\lceil \sum_{j=i}^m\frac{w_j}{w_i}q_j \right\rceil. \)

Indeed, the first inequality says that the integer \(\lfloor C/w_i\rfloor\) is at least the displayed rational sum, and therefore at least its ceiling.

It follows that the least feasible integer capacity is

\( \displaystyle C_t= \max_{1\leq i\leq m} w_i\left\lceil \sum_{j=i}^m\frac{w_j}{w_i}q_j \right\rceil. \)

Applying the lemma at \(C_t\) constructs a rational distribution with mean \(q\) and maximum cost at most \(aC_t\). Conversely, every admissible threshold \(\theta\) has \(\lfloor\theta/a\rfloor\geq C_t\), and hence \(\theta\geq aC_t\). Therefore

\( \displaystyle \theta_t=aC_t. \)

Within a block of equal costs, the suffix beginning at the first class in that block has the largest sum. All other suffix conditions in the block are redundant. Grouping these equal-cost blocks gives precisely

\( \displaystyle \theta_t= \max_{1\leq \ell\leq k_t} a_{t,\ell} \left\lceil \sum_{\{j:\,v_t(j)\geq a_{t,\ell}\}} \frac{v_t(j)}{a_{t,\ell}}q_j \right\rceil. \)

This establishes the minimum in the question, including its attainment by a finitely supported rational distribution.

4. Constructing the allocation in polynomial time

For each listed type \(t\):

  • Sort its costs and compute the normalized integer weights.
  • Compute \(C_t\) by the formula above.
  • Apply the decomposition algorithm to \(q\in Q_{C_t}\).
  • Restore the original class order in every output bundle.

This produces masses \(y^t_b\) on at most \(2m+1\) bundles, satisfying

\( \displaystyle \sum_b y^t_b=1,\qquad \sum_b b_jy^t_b=q_j,\qquad y^t_b>0\Longrightarrow v_t(b)\leq\theta_t. \)

Output \(x_{t,b}=\mu_t y^t_b\), merging repeated bundles for a type if necessary. Section 1 verifies all allocation constraints.

Finally, normalization, ceiling calculations, and decomposition all have polynomial bit complexity. To see explicitly that the capacities have polynomial binary length, observe that

\( \displaystyle C_t\leq \sum_jw_jq_j+\max_jw_j. \)

This follows from \(\lceil u\rceil\leq u+1\). Each normalized weight and supply has polynomial binary length, so the bound has polynomial binary length as well. The decomposition’s denominator bound then applies, and multiplying its masses by the input masses \(\mu_t\) preserves polynomial encoding length.

The algorithm processes only the \(\tau\) listed types. It never expands binary supplies into an explicit list of chores or constructs a finite society.
