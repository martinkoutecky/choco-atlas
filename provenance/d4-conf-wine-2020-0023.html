The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) divisible goods, \(\rho\in(0,1]\), a common homogeneity degree \(r\), and \(\tau\) valuation types with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compute an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal allocation and an \(\varepsilon\)-accurate dual certificate for maximizing \(\frac{1}{\rho}\sum_t\mu_t v_t(x_t)^\rho\), subject to \(x_t\ge 0\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\le 1\) for every good \(j\), assuming each valuation has an efficient finite-precision gradient-oracle representation.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) divisible goods, each of supply \(1\), and binary-rational parameters \(r,\rho\in(0,1]\) and \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\).
Each valuation \(v_t:\mathbb R_{\geq0}^m\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}\) is continuous, nonzero, coordinatewise nondecreasing, concave, normalized by \(v_t(0)=0\), homogeneous of common degree \(r\), and differentiable on \(\mathbb R_{>0}^m\).
Its finite description provides a gradient oracle: at any positive rational bundle \(z\) and precision \(k\), it returns a rational vector within \(2^{-k}\) of \(\nabla v_t(z)\) in maximum norm, in time polynomial in the description length, the query bit length, and \(k\), with a uniform polynomial bound.
An allocation assigns a common per-capita bundle \(x_t\geq0\) to each type, with \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\leq1\) for every good; bundles have no additional individual upper bound.
Write \(F(x)=\rho^{-1}\sum_t\mu_t v_t(x_t)^\rho\) and let \(F^*\) be its feasible maximum. Define the resource-price dual by \(D(q)=\sum_jq_j+\sum_t\mu_t\sup_{z\geq0}\{\rho^{-1}v_t(z)^\rho-q\cdot z\}\), for \(q\geq0\).
The computational problem is to return rational feasible \(x\) and rational \(q\geq0\) satisfying \(F^*-F(x)\leq\varepsilon\) and \(D(q)-F^*\leq\varepsilon\).

Is this problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is it NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total binary description length of the masses, valuation representations, \(r\), and \(\rho\)?

For a finite list of agents, Goel and Plaut establish polynomial-time optimization using valuation-gradient queries and recovery of optimal resource multipliers from an exact optimum ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.09351)).
The later thesis retains these ideal-oracle claims but does not specify finite-precision guarantees for the mass formulation and its approximate dual certificate ([source](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/bplaut/BenPlautThesisFinal.pdf)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored the omitted nonzero, monotonicity, normalization, concavity, and differentiability assumptions on valuations.
  • Restricted the common homogeneity degree to (0,1], excluding the degenerate zero-degree case, and encoded r, rho, and epsilon as binary rationals.
  • Required continuity on the closed orthant and differentiability only on its interior, accommodating the intended diminishing-return valuations whose boundary gradients can be undefined.
  • Specified a uniform polynomial-time finite-precision gradient oracle with absolute coordinatewise error, queried at positive rational bundles.
  • Interpreted each type's allocation as its common per-capita bundle, retaining the graded mass constraints without an extra individual unit cap.
  • Defined epsilon-optimality and dual accuracy as additive objective errors, with the dual certificate consisting of nonnegative resource multipliers evaluated through the unrestricted Lagrangian dual.
  • Specified rational outputs and running-time dependence on total representation length and logarithmic accuracy.

Result and interpretation

The problem is \(\text{NP-hard}\) under the stated assumptions on valuation representations, even with one good, two distinct linear valuation types, equal masses, and fixed accuracy \(\varepsilon=1/4096\).

Here a valuation representation may be any finite description satisfying the uniform polynomial-time gradient-oracle promise. The reduction below uses a fixed representation scheme satisfying that promise. Its coefficients have succinct descriptions; writing them explicitly as binary rationals would require exponentially many bits.

The hardness already holds for returning the approximate primal allocation, so requiring the dual certificate cannot remove it.

1. Construction from satisfiability

Let \(\varphi\) be an instance of Boolean satisfiability. Put

\[ N=\max\{2,|\varphi|\},\qquad B=2^N. \]

Construct a society with

\[ m=1,\qquad \tau=2,\qquad \mu_1=\mu_2=\frac12,\qquad r=1,\qquad \rho=\frac1B,\qquad \varepsilon=\frac1{4096}. \]

Define linear valuations \(v_t(z)=a_tz\), where

\[ a_1= \begin{cases} 2^{-B},&\varphi\text{ is satisfiable},\\ 2^{-2B},&\varphi\text{ is unsatisfiable}, \end{cases} \qquad a_2=2^{-(B+1)}. \]

The first representation consists of \(\varphi\) and a fixed algorithm described below. The second consists of \(N\) and another fixed algorithm. Constructing these descriptions does not require deciding satisfiability.

Both coefficients are strictly positive and, since \(B\geq4\), distinct. Thus the support genuinely contains two distinct valuation types. Both valuations are continuous, nonzero, nondecreasing, concave, normalized at zero, homogeneous of common degree \(1\), and differentiable on the positive domain.

The binary encoding of \(\rho=2^{-N}\) has length \(O(N)\). Consequently, the total instance length is \(L=O(N)\).

2. Verification of the gradient-oracle promise

For these valuations, \(\nabla v_t(z)=a_t\), independently of the positive rational query \(z\).

The first oracle operates as follows:

  • If \(k<B\), return \(0\).
  • If \(k\geq B\), enumerate all assignments to \(\varphi\), determine satisfiability, and return the corresponding coefficient exactly.

In the first case,

\[ |0-a_1|\leq2^{-B}\leq2^{-k}, \]

so the answer has the required accuracy.

In the second case, \(\varphi\) has at most \(N\) variables. Exhaustive satisfiability testing therefore takes time at most \(2^N\operatorname{poly}(N)=B\operatorname{poly}(N)\). Since \(k\geq B\), this is polynomial in \(N\) and \(k\). The exact coefficient has binary length at most \(2B+O(1)\leq2k+O(1)\), so producing the output also takes polynomial time.

The second oracle returns \(0\) when \(k<B\) and returns \(2^{-(B+1)}\) exactly otherwise. Its error and runtime satisfy the same bounds.

Comparing \(k\) with \(B\) only requires constructing the \(N+1\)-bit binary representation of \(B\). Hence both oracles have a uniform runtime bound polynomial in the description length, query bit length, and \(k\), exactly as required.

3. Optimal allocations distinguish satisfiability

Write

\[ c_t=a_t^\rho. \]

Then

\[ c_1= \begin{cases} \frac12,&\varphi\text{ is satisfiable},\\ \frac14,&\varphi\text{ is unsatisfiable}, \end{cases} \qquad c_2=2^{-1-\rho}. \]

In particular, \(c_1,c_2\geq1/4\) and \(0<\rho\leq1/4\).

Feasibility means \(x_1+x_2\leq2\). Since both valuations are strictly increasing, an optimum exhausts supply. On allocations exhausting supply, define

\[ H(s)=F(s,2-s) =\frac{c_1s^\rho+c_2(2-s)^\rho}{2\rho}, \qquad 0\leq s\leq2. \]

For \(0<s<2\),

\[ H'(s)=\frac12\left(c_1s^{\rho-1}-c_2(2-s)^{\rho-1}\right). \]

This derivative tends to positive infinity at the left endpoint and negative infinity at the right endpoint. Moreover, \(H\) is strictly concave. Its unique maximizer \(s^*\) is therefore interior and satisfies

\[ s^*=\frac{2}{1+(c_2/c_1)^{1/(1-\rho)}}. \]

If \(\varphi\) is satisfiable, \(c_2/c_1=2^{-\rho}<1\), giving

\[ s^*>1. \]

If \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable, \(c_2/c_1=2^{1-\rho}\), giving the exact value

\[ s^*=\frac{2}{1+2}=\frac23. \]

Thus the two cases have a constant separation in their optimal first-type allocations.

4. Every sufficiently accurate allocation reveals the answer

We next prove that the specified additive accuracy preserves this separation.

For \(0<s<2\),

\[ -H''(s) =\frac{1-\rho}{2} \left(c_1s^{\rho-2}+c_2(2-s)^{\rho-2}\right). \]

Because \(\rho-2<0\) and both arguments lie in \((0,2)\),

\[ s^{\rho-2}\geq2^{\rho-2}\geq\frac14, \qquad (2-s)^{\rho-2}\geq\frac14. \]

Together with \(c_1,c_2\geq1/4\) and \(1-\rho\geq3/4\), this yields

\[ -H''(s)\geq \frac{3}{8}\left(\frac1{16}+\frac1{16}\right) =\frac3{64}. \]

Consequently,

\[ H(s^*)-H(s)\geq\frac3{128}(s-s^*)^2 \qquad\text{for every }s\in[0,2]. \]

To justify this inequality, the function \(H(s)+(3/128)s^2\) is concave on the interior. Its supporting-tangent inequality at \(s^*\), together with \(H'(s^*)=0\), gives the stated bound. Continuity extends it to the endpoints.

Now let \(x\) be any feasible output satisfying \(F^*-F(x)\leq\varepsilon\). Set \(s=x_1\). Feasibility gives \(x_2\leq2-s\), so monotonicity implies

\[ F(x)\leq H(s). \]

Since \(F^*=H(s^*)\), we obtain

\[ H(s^*)-H(s)\leq\varepsilon. \]

If \(|s-s^*|\geq1/6\), however, the curvature bound gives

\[ H(s^*)-H(s)\geq \frac3{128}\left(\frac16\right)^2 =\frac1{1536} >\frac1{4096}. \]

Therefore every valid output satisfies \(|x_1-s^*|<1/6\). It follows that:

  • If \(\varphi\) is satisfiable, then \(x_1>5/6\).
  • If \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable, then \(x_1<5/6\).

Comparing the returned rational coordinate with \(5/6\) decides satisfiability.

5. The dual requirement is well defined on these instances

The constructed instances also admit rational approximate dual certificates.

Define

\[ q^*=c_1(s^*)^{\rho-1} =c_2(2-s^*)^{\rho-1}>0. \]

The equality follows from \(H'(s^*)=0\). For each type, the function

\[ z\longmapsto \frac{c_tz^\rho}{\rho}-q^*z \]

is strictly concave, and its derivative vanishes at the corresponding optimal bundle. Those bundles therefore attain the suprema defining \(D(q^*)\). As their weighted sum is \(1\), substitution gives \(D(q^*)=F^*\).

For any \(q>0\), direct maximization gives

\[ D(q)=q+\frac{1-\rho}{2\rho} \left(c_1^{1/(1-\rho)}+c_2^{1/(1-\rho)}\right) q^{-\rho/(1-\rho)}. \]

This expression is continuous on the positive domain. Rational positive prices sufficiently close to \(q^*\) therefore satisfy \(D(q)-F^*\leq\varepsilon\). Likewise, continuity of \(H\) gives rational feasible allocations with the required primal accuracy. Thus the reduction uses valid instances with nonempty sets of acceptable outputs.

6. Complexity conclusion and relation to the cited results

A polynomial-time solver for the requested problem would decide satisfiability by constructing this instance, making one solver call, and comparing \(x_1\) with \(5/6\). All construction and decoding steps take polynomial time, while \(m,\tau,\log(1/\varepsilon)\) are constant and \(L=O(N)\). This proves \(\text{NP-hardness}\). In particular, a general polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The exact-gradient calculations in [Goel and Plaut, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.09351#S5), also presented in [Plaut’s thesis, Section 4.5](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/bplaut/BenPlautThesisFinal.pdf#page=105), recover objective information from valuation gradients. They do not provide the finite-precision guarantee needed here. In this construction, exponentially small coefficients become constant-sized welfare coefficients after taking the input-dependent power \(\rho\). The absolute-error gradient promise permits precisely this obstruction.
