Here \(Q=N\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tq_t\), \(Q_\nu=\sum_{t\in T}\nu_tq_t\), and \(v(0)=0\).

Result and exact construction

Computing a core allocation is polynomial-time solvable. For the stipulated \(m=1\), an exact allocation can be output in \(O(\tau L)\) bit operations, with total expression length \(O(\tau L)\).

Define the payment per unit mass of type \(t\) by

\(y_t=pq_t\log Q,\qquad Q=N\sum_{s\in T}\mu_sq_s.\)

Because \(T\) is nonempty and \(N,\mu_s,q_s\) are positive, \(Q>0\), so these expressions are well-defined.

This is the continuous counterpart of the production-proportional allocation in Theorem 1 of the [cited paper](https://pure.hw.ac.uk/ws/files/7446774/ccrjVPPcoop.pdf). The proof below verifies the constraints directly for every real submass coalition.

Proof of core membership

A core allocation must distribute the grand coalition’s entire value and pay every coalition at least its value.

First, the proposed payments satisfy the required efficiency equality:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}N\mu_ty_t =p\log Q\sum_{t\in T}N\mu_tq_t =pQ\log Q.\)

Next, fix any coalition \(\nu\) satisfying \(0\leq\nu_t\leq N\mu_t\) for every \(t\in T\). Since each \(q_t>0\), these coordinatewise inequalities imply

\(\displaystyle 0\leq Q_\nu=\sum_{t\in T}\nu_tq_t \leq\sum_{t\in T}N\mu_tq_t=Q.\)

If \(Q_\nu=0\), positivity of the \(q_t\) and nonnegativity of the \(\nu_t\) imply \(\nu_t=0\) for every \(t\). Thus the coalition’s payment and its stipulated value are both zero.

If \(Q_\nu>0\), monotonicity of the natural logarithm gives \(\log Q_\nu\leq\log Q\). Multiplying by the positive number \(pQ_\nu\) yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\nu_ty_t =pQ_\nu\log Q \geq pQ_\nu\log Q_\nu =v(\nu).\)

This proves every coalition constraint, including those for arbitrary real submasses.

The argument also covers \(Q<1\): the payments are then negative, as permitted by the problem, and the same inequality holds. For \(Q=1\), every proposed payment is zero and every coalition value is nonpositive.

Bit complexity and expression length

The permitted output representation makes numerical evaluation of logarithms unnecessary. Indeed, even the rational arithmetic inside their arguments can remain unevaluated.

Construct the expression

\(\displaystyle E=N\left(\sum_{s\in T}\mu_sq_s\right)\)

using the input’s binary-rational constants, multiplication symbols, addition symbols, and parentheses. Its encoded length is \(O(L+\tau)=O(L)\), since the input explicitly lists \(\tau\) types and consequently \(\tau=O(L)\).

For each \(t\), output the expression \(pq_t\log(E)\), inserting a complete copy of \(E\). Each payment expression has length \(O(L)\), so all \(\tau\) expressions together have length \(O(\tau L)\). These are standalone finite expressions of precisely the allowed form. Every logarithm has the positive argument \(E=Q\).

A direct algorithm scans and copies the input constants to construct each expression. It therefore takes \(O(\tau L)\) bit operations, including writing the output. This bound includes the binary encoding of \(N\); the algorithm never iterates over a population of size \(N\).

Thus, in terms of the requested parameters, the proved bound is \(T(1,\tau,L)=O(\tau L)\) for \(m=1\). No enumeration of coalitions or of possible unsupported types is required.
