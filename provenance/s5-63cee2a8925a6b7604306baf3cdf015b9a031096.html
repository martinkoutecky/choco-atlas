2. Totality, rational cuts, and a finite algorithm

Existence over the reals. The Hobby–Rice theorem states that, for \(n\) real integrable functions on an interval, there is a sign function with at most \(n\) sign changes whose integral against every function is zero. Here the \(\tau\) step densities are integrable, so its hypotheses hold. Hence an exact solution exists with at most \(\tau\) real cuts. The connection between this theorem and consensus-halving is recalled in [the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.11437).

We next prove that rational cuts suffice and give an algorithm.

Let \(0=b_0<b_1<\cdots<b_q=1\) be the common refinement of all density breakpoints. There are \(q=O(m)\) refinement intervals. Define the cumulative functions

\(F_t(x)=\int_0^x f_t(u)\,du.\)

Every \(F_t\) is continuous and affine with rational coefficients on each closed interval \([b_{r-1},b_r]\). These coefficients can be computed with polynomial bit length using rational arithmetic.

Fix a number of cuts \(k\leq\tau\). Consecutive intervals carrying the same label can be merged. Exchanging the two labels preserves exact balance. It therefore suffices to consider alternating labels beginning with \(+1\).

For every cut \(x_j\), choose a refinement interval indexed by \(r_j\), and impose

\(b_{r_j-1}\leq x_j\leq b_{r_j},\qquad x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_k.\)

Set \(x_0=0\) and \(x_{k+1}=1\). Exact balance is expressed by the \(\tau\) equations

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\bigl(F_t(x_{j+1})-F_t(x_j)\bigr)=0,\qquad t\in T.\)

For the chosen refinement intervals, every occurrence of \(F_t(x_j)\) has a fixed affine formula. Thus these equations and inequalities define a bounded rational polytope in \(k\) variables.

Enumerate all choices of \(k\) and all refinement-interval assignments, and solve each resulting linear feasibility problem. The real solution guaranteed above belongs to at least one enumerated polytope, so some feasibility test succeeds.

A nonempty bounded rational polytope has a rational vertex. Its coordinates have polynomial bit length: a vertex is determined by \(k\) independent active constraints, and clearing denominators followed by Cramer’s rule bounds the numerator and denominator lengths polynomially in the constraint encoding length. Polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore returns polynomially encoded cuts.

Repeated cuts and cuts at endpoints create only zero-length intervals. Removing these intervals, and then merging adjacent intervals with equal labels, preserves every integral and leaves at most \(k\) cuts.

There are at most \(\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}q^k\leq(\tau+1)\max\{1,q\}^{\tau}\) feasibility problems. Each has polynomial size in \(m,\tau,L\). This proves the asserted running time and establishes exact rational totality. Exact balance also satisfies every positive input tolerance.

Finally, a proposed rational solution is verifiable in polynomial time: integrate each rational step density over its assigned intervals and compare the resulting rational discrepancy with \(\varepsilon\).
