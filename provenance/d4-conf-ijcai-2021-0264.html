Given a candidate set \(C\) with \(m\geq 1\), let \(\mathcal R(C)\) be the strict complete rankings of \(C\); \(\operatorname{pos}_\pi(c)\) is the position of \(c\), with the top position numbered \(1\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) ranking types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with \(\sum_\pi\mu_\pi=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(m!\).
The resolution \(n\geq1\) is an integer encoded in binary; \(L\) is the total input bit length. Define \(X^\mu_{i,c}=\sum_{\pi:\operatorname{pos}_\pi(c)=i}\mu_\pi\).
Output a list of distinct rankings \(\rho\in\mathcal R(C)\) with positive binary integer multiplicities \(k_\rho\), summing to \(n\); output rankings need not belong to the input support. Their position matrix is \(p_{i,c}=\sum_{\rho:\operatorname{pos}_\rho(c)=i}k_\rho\).
Minimize \(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{c\in C}|nX^\mu_{i,c}-p_{i,c}|\), subject to \(|nX^\mu_{i,c}-p_{i,c}|\leq1\) for every position and candidate. Any minimizer is acceptable.

Conclusion

The problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), including \(\log n\), with the requested compressed output.

The cited paper’s Proposition 2 states the corresponding optimization result for bistochastic matrix inputs, and Proposition 1 provides recovery with at most \(m^2-2m+2\) distinct rankings. [Boehmer et al., *Putting a Compass on the Map of Elections*, Section 3 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.07815).

Below is a direct proof that explicitly handles binary encoding and all integer values permitted by the error constraints.

1. Computing the matrix

Write \(X=X^\mu\) and \(a_{i,c}=nX_{i,c}\). Each input ranking places exactly one candidate in each position and places each candidate exactly once. Therefore,

\[ \sum_{c\in C}X_{i,c}=1,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i,c}=1,\qquad X_{i,c}\geq 0. \]

Consequently, every row and column of \(A=(a_{i,c})\) sums to \(n\).

For exact arithmetic, write each input mass as \(\mu_\pi=v_\pi/d_\pi\), and take \(Q=\prod_\pi d_\pi\). The bit length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the input denominator bit lengths, hence is \(O(L)\). Every entry of \(X\) can be computed with denominator \(Q\), and every entry of \(A\) has numerator at most \(nQ\). Thus computing \(A\) takes polynomial time, with intermediate bit lengths polynomial in \(L\).

Any permitted output induces a nonnegative integer matrix \(P\) whose rows and columns sum to \(n\). Section 3 proves the converse constructively. It therefore suffices to optimize over such matrices.

2. Optimizing the integer matrix by minimum-cost flow

For each entry, define

\[ \ell_{i,c}=\max\{0,\lceil a_{i,c}-1\rceil\}, \qquad u_{i,c}=\lfloor a_{i,c}+1\rfloor, \qquad w_{i,c}=u_{i,c}-\ell_{i,c}. \]

These are exactly the bounds for a nonnegative integer entry satisfying \(|a_{i,c}-p_{i,c}|\leq 1\). Moreover,

\[ \ell_{i,c}\leq a_{i,c}\leq u_{i,c}, \qquad 0\leq a_{i,c}-\ell_{i,c}\leq 1, \qquad w_{i,c}\leq 2. \]

In particular, these bounds retain both possible deviations at entries where \(a_{i,c}\) is integral.

Define integer residual margins

\[ r_i=n-\sum_{c\in C}\ell_{i,c}, \qquad s_c=n-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell_{i,c}. \]

Using the margins of \(A\), we obtain

\[ r_i=\sum_{c\in C}(a_{i,c}-\ell_{i,c}), \qquad s_c=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i,c}-\ell_{i,c}). \]

Hence \(0\leq r_i,s_c\leq m\), and

\[ B:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_i=\sum_{c\in C}s_c\leq m^2. \]

Construct a directed network with a source, one vertex for each position, one vertex for each candidate, and a sink:

  • The source-to-position arc for \(i\) has capacity \(r_i\) and cost zero.
  • The candidate-to-sink arc for \(c\) has capacity \(s_c\) and cost zero.
  • For every pair \((i,c)\), introduce \(w_{i,c}\) parallel position-to-candidate arcs, each of capacity one. Arc \(j\) has cost

\[ \gamma_{i,c,j} = |a_{i,c}-(\ell_{i,c}+j)| - |a_{i,c}-(\ell_{i,c}+j-1)|. \]

We seek a minimum-cost flow of value \(B\).

Feasibility. The quantities \(d_{i,c}=a_{i,c}-\ell_{i,c}\) have row sums \(r_i\), column sums \(s_c\), and satisfy \(0\leq d_{i,c}\leq w_{i,c}\). Distributing \(d_{i,c}\) over the corresponding unit-capacity arcs gives a feasible fractional flow of value \(B\).

We use the standard minimum-cost-flow theorem: with integer capacities and a prescribed integer flow value, fractional feasibility implies the existence of an integral minimum-cost flow; an optimum can be computed in time polynomial in the network size and the bit lengths of capacities and rational costs. All its hypotheses hold here.

Exact correspondence with the objective. Since absolute value is convex, the incremental costs satisfy \(\gamma_{i,c,1}\leq\gamma_{i,c,2}\) whenever both arcs exist. Thus an integral optimum can be chosen to use the first \(t_{i,c}\) parallel arcs for every pair: replacing a used later arc by an unused earlier arc preserves feasibility and cannot increase cost.

Set \(p_{i,c}=\ell_{i,c}+t_{i,c}\). Because the total source capacity and total sink capacity both equal \(B\), a flow of value \(B\) saturates those arcs. Consequently, \(P\) has every row and column sum equal to \(n\). Its entries satisfy the required bounds.

The costs telescope:

\[ \sum_{j=1}^{t_{i,c}}\gamma_{i,c,j} = |a_{i,c}-p_{i,c}|-|a_{i,c}-\ell_{i,c}|. \]

Conversely, every feasible integer matrix \(P\) defines such a prefix flow by setting \(t_{i,c}=p_{i,c}-\ell_{i,c}\). Therefore minimum flow cost equals the minimum requested objective minus the fixed constant \(\sum_{i,c}|a_{i,c}-\ell_{i,c}|\). The recovered matrix is globally optimal.

Bit complexity. The network has \(2m+2\) vertices and at most \(2m^2+2m\) arcs. Its required flow value is at most \(m^2\), independently of \(n\). Each cost has denominator dividing \(Q\) and absolute value at most one, by the triangle inequality. Multiplying all costs by \(Q\) produces integer costs of \(O(L)\) bits without changing the optimum. Thus this optimization takes polynomial time in the specified parameters.

3. Recovering rankings with binary multiplicities

Let \(R=P\). Maintain the invariant that \(R\) is nonnegative and integral, with every row and column summing to a common integer \(N\), initially \(N=n\).

When \(N>0\), form the bipartite graph whose vertices are positions and candidates, with edge \((i,c)\) exactly when \(R_{i,c}>0\).

For any set \(S\) of position vertices, let \(\Gamma(S)\) be its candidate neighbors. All mass in the rows of \(S\) lies in columns \(\Gamma(S)\). Therefore,

\[ N|S| = \sum_{i\in S}\sum_{c\in\Gamma(S)}R_{i,c} \leq \sum_{c\in\Gamma(S)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}R_{i,c} = N|\Gamma(S)|. \]

Since \(N>0\), Hall’s condition holds. Hall’s theorem gives a perfect matching \(M\), which defines a ranking \(\rho\): candidate \(c\) occupies position \(i\) precisely when \((i,c)\in M\).

Let

\[ k=\min_{(i,c)\in M}R_{i,c}. \]

Record \(\rho\) with multiplicity \(k\), and subtract \(k\) from every matched entry. Here \(k\) is a positive integer; the new matrix remains nonnegative and integral, and its common margin becomes \(N-k\).

At least one positive entry becomes zero in every iteration, and no zero entry ever becomes positive. Hence there are at most \(m^2\) iterations. Furthermore, a recorded matching cannot recur: one of its edges becomes permanently zero when it is recorded. Thus the recorded rankings are distinct.

Upon termination, \(R=0\). Since each iteration decreases the common margin by its recorded multiplicity, the multiplicities sum to \(n\). The resulting rankings reproduce \(P\) exactly and therefore attain the optimum.

Each matching is computable in polynomial time. Every multiplicity is at most \(n\) and needs \(O(\log(n+1))\) bits. The total output length is

\[ O\!\left(m^2\bigl(m\log(m+1)+\log(n+1)\bigr)\right). \]

This establishes polynomial running time and polynomial output length even when \(n\) is encoded in binary.

4. Resolved classification

The distribution-to-matrix computation, exact optimization, and compressed ranking recovery all run in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). They apply to every \(m\geq 1\) and \(n\geq 1\), and they do not require output rankings to belong to the input support.
