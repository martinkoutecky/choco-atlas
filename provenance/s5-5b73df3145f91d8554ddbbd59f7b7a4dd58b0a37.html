Theorem 4.1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(M\) goods, \(\tau\) buyer types with rational masses \(\mu_\theta>0\) summing to \(1\), a horizon \(T\), and an unknown stationary distribution \(P\) over a finite set of positive valuation matrices \(V=(v_{\theta j})\), each shared within its type, find an online policy for mass bids \(z_{\theta j,t}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_j z_{\theta j,t}=\mu_\theta\). Prices are \(p_{j,t}=\sum_\theta z_{\theta j,t}\), and per-person allocations are \(x_{\theta j,t}=z_{\theta j,t}/(\mu_\theta p_{j,t})\). Against \(z^\star\in\arg\min_z\Phi_\mu(z)\), where \(\Phi_\mu(z)=\mathbb E_{V\sim P}[-\sum_{\theta,j}z_{\theta j}\log v_{\theta j}+\sum_j p_j(z)\log p_j(z)]\), does the type-level proportional response initialized at \(z_{\theta j,1}=\mu_\theta/M\) achieve expected cumulative fairness regret \(\sum_{t=1}^T(\mathbb E[\phi_\mu(z_t;V_t)]-\Phi_\mu(z^\star))\le\log(M\tau)\) while clearing every good each round?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_\theta\) encoded as binary rationals, with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) divisible goods, each replenished to unit supply in every round, and a known horizon \(T\geq1\). Each person has budget \(1\) per round, so type \(\theta\) has aggregate budget \(\mu_\theta\).
Valuation matrices \(V_t=(v_{\theta j,t})\) are drawn independently from the same unknown distribution \(P\) on a finite nonempty set of strictly positive rational matrices; valuations are shared within each type. Each type privately observes its current valuation row before bidding and its own allocations and the prices afterward.
Let \(K_\mu=\{z\geq0:\sum_jz_{\theta j}=\mu_\theta\}\), \(p_j(z)=\sum_\theta z_{\theta j}\), and \(x_{\theta j,t}=z_{\theta j,t}/(\mu_\theta p_j(z_t))\) whenever the price is positive.
Define type-level online proportional response by \(z_{\theta j,1}=\mu_\theta/m\) and, for \(t\geq2\), \(z_{\theta j,t}=\mu_\theta v_{\theta j,t}x_{\theta j,t-1}/\sum_kv_{\theta k,t}x_{\theta k,t-1}\).
Set \(\phi_\mu(z;V)=-\sum_{\theta,j}z_{\theta j}\log v_{\theta j}+\sum_jp_j(z)\log p_j(z)\), with \(0\log0=0\), \(\Phi_\mu(z)=\mathbb E_{V\sim P}[\phi_\mu(z;V)]\), and \(z^\star\in\arg\min_{z\in K_\mu}\Phi_\mu(z)\). Logarithms are natural. Expectations involving \(z_t\) use the actual sampled history, including \(V_t\).
State any running times in \(m,\tau,T,L\), where \(L\) is the total binary encoding length of the masses, horizon, and observed rational valuation matrices; \(P\) is not supplied to the policy.

Does this policy satisfy \(\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(\mathbb E[\phi_\mu(z_t;V_t)]-\Phi_\mu(z^\star)\bigr)\leq\log(m\tau)\) and \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta j,t}=1\) for every good and round, for every admissible instance, or can either guarantee fail?

Conclusion

The regret guarantee can fail; market clearing always holds. Although failure is already possible when \(T=1\), the counterexample below violates the bound for every horizon \(T\), with two types, two goods, and deterministic strictly positive rational valuations.

The valid general bound is
\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\mathbb E[\phi_\mu(z_t;V_t)]-\Phi_\mu(z^\star)\right) \leq \Phi_\mu(z_1)-\Phi_\mu(z^\star)+\log m. \)
The first-round term cannot be bounded using only \(m\) and \(\tau\).

1. Positivity and market clearing

Initially, \(z_{\theta j,1}=\mu_\theta/m>0\), and each row sums to \(\mu_\theta\).

Suppose every entry of \(z_{t-1}\) is positive. Then every price \(p_j(z_{t-1})\) and allocation \(x_{\theta j,t-1}\) is positive. Strict positivity of the valuations implies
\(\displaystyle \sum_k v_{\theta k,t}x_{\theta k,t-1}>0. \)
Consequently, the update defines positive bids whose row sums are
\(\displaystyle \sum_j z_{\theta j,t} =\mu_\theta \frac{\sum_jv_{\theta j,t}x_{\theta j,t-1}} {\sum_kv_{\theta k,t}x_{\theta k,t-1}} =\mu_\theta. \)
Induction proves positivity and membership in \(K_\mu\) in every round.

In particular, every price is positive, so the allocation formula always applies. For every good \(j\) and round \(t\),
\(\displaystyle \sum_\theta\mu_\theta x_{\theta j,t} =\sum_\theta\frac{z_{\theta j,t}}{p_j(z_t)} =\frac{p_j(z_t)}{p_j(z_t)} =1. \)
This identity holds for every sampled history.

2. A counterexample for every horizon

Set \(m=\tau=2\) and \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\). Let \(P\) concentrate on the single matrix
\(\displaystyle V= \begin{pmatrix} 1&1/256\\ 1/2&1/512 \end{pmatrix}. \)
Constant random matrices are independent and identically distributed. All entries are strictly positive rationals lying in \([1/512,1]\), and the two valuation rows are distinct.

Write \(v_{\theta j}=a_\theta r_j\), where
\(\displaystyle (a_1,a_2)=(1,1/2), \qquad (r_1,r_2)=(1,1/256). \)
For any feasible \(z\), its price vector \(p=p(z)\) satisfies \(p_1+p_2=1\). Using the row constraints,
\(\displaystyle \phi_\mu(z;V) =C+\sum_{j=1}^{2}p_j\log\frac{p_j}{r_j}, \qquad C=-\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\log a_\theta=\frac12\log2. \)

Let
\(\displaystyle S=r_1+r_2=\frac{257}{256}, \qquad q=\frac{r}{S}=\left(\frac{256}{257},\frac1{257}\right). \)
Define \(D(p\|q)=\sum_jp_j\log(p_j/q_j)\), with zero numerator terms interpreted as zero. Then
\(\displaystyle \phi_\mu(z;V)=C-\log S+D(p\|q). \)
Relative entropy is nonnegative, with equality at \(p=q\). The bids \(z^\star_{\theta j}=\mu_\theta q_j\) are feasible and have prices \(q\). Thus
\(\displaystyle \Phi_\mu(z^\star)=C-\log S. \)

First round. The prescribed initialization gives \(z_{\theta j,1}=1/4\), prices \(p(z_1)=(1/2,1/2)\), and \(x_{\theta j,1}=1\). Its objective gap is
\(\displaystyle D\left((1/2,1/2)\middle\|q\right) =\frac12\log\frac{257}{512} +\frac12\log\frac{257}{2} =\log\frac{257}{32}. \)

Every subsequent round. Whenever \(x_{\theta j,t-1}=1\), the update gives
\(\displaystyle z_{\theta j,t} =\mu_\theta\frac{a_\theta r_j}{a_\theta(r_1+r_2)} =\mu_\theta q_j. \)
These bids have prices \(q_j\) and allocations \(x_{\theta j,t}=1\). Starting from round one, induction therefore shows that every round \(t\geq2\) attains the optimum.

All randomness is degenerate, so for every \(T\geq1\),
\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left(\mathbb E[\phi_\mu(z_t;V_t)]-\Phi_\mu(z^\star)\right) =\log\frac{257}{32} >\log4 =\log(m\tau), \)
where the strict inequality follows from \(257>128\).

Moreover, replacing \(r_2\) by any positive rational \(\varepsilon\) gives total regret
\(\displaystyle \log\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}. \)
This tends to infinity as \(\varepsilon\) tends to zero. Hence no finite bound depending only on \(m\) and \(\tau\) holds under the stated first-round convention.

3. A valid bound from round two onward

The following argument identifies precisely what the proportional-response update does guarantee.

For \(u,y\in K_\mu\), with \(y\) strictly positive, define
\(\displaystyle D(u\|y)=\sum_{\theta,j}u_{\theta j}\log\frac{u_{\theta j}}{y_{\theta j}}. \)
Both arrays have total mass one, so this relative entropy is nonnegative.

Fix a round \(t\geq2\), and write \(y=z_{t-1}\), \(w=z_t\), and \(f(z)=\phi_\mu(z;V_t)\). Substituting the allocation formula into the update gives
\(\displaystyle w_{\theta j} =\mu_\theta \frac{y_{\theta j}v_{\theta j,t}/p_j(y)} {\sum_k y_{\theta k}v_{\theta k,t}/p_k(y)}. \)
Set
\(\displaystyle A_\theta=\sum_k\frac{y_{\theta k}v_{\theta k,t}}{p_k(y)}, \qquad g_{\theta j}=1-\log v_{\theta j,t}+\log p_j(y). \)
The update implies
\(\displaystyle g_{\theta j} =1+\log\mu_\theta-\log A_\theta -\log\frac{w_{\theta j}}{y_{\theta j}}. \)
Because \(w\) and any comparator \(u\in K_\mu\) have the same row sums, the row-constant terms cancel in the inner product. Therefore
\(\displaystyle \langle g,w-u\rangle =\sum_{\theta,j}(u_{\theta j}-w_{\theta j}) \log\frac{w_{\theta j}}{y_{\theta j}} =D(u\|y)-D(u\|w)-D(w\|y). \)

Expanding the price-entropy terms directly yields
\(\displaystyle f(w)-f(u) =\langle g,w-u\rangle +D(p(w)\|p(y))-D(p(u)\|p(y)). \)
Also, aggregation over types cannot increase relative entropy. Indeed,
\(\displaystyle D(w\|y)-D(p(w)\|p(y)) =\sum_jp_j(w) D\left( \left(\frac{w_{\theta j}}{p_j(w)}\right)_\theta \middle\| \left(\frac{y_{\theta j}}{p_j(y)}\right)_\theta \right) \geq0. \)
Combining these identities and dropping nonnegative terms proves, for every sampled history,
\(\displaystyle \phi_\mu(z_t;V_t)-\phi_\mu(u;V_t) \leq D(u\|z_{t-1})-D(u\|z_t). \)
Summing over \(t=2,\ldots,T\) gives
\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=2}^{T} \left(\phi_\mu(z_t;V_t)-\phi_\mu(u;V_t)\right) \leq D(u\|z_1). \)

A minimizer \(z^\star\) exists: \(K_\mu\) is compact, and \(\Phi_\mu\) is continuous because the valuation support is finite and \(p\log p\) extends continuously to zero. Choose a fixed such minimizer. Writing \(q_{\theta j}=z^\star_{\theta j}/\mu_\theta\), each row \(q_\theta\) is a probability vector, and
\(\displaystyle D(z^\star\|z_1) =\log m+\sum_\theta\mu_\theta\sum_jq_{\theta j}\log q_{\theta j} \leq\log m. \)
Taking expectations, using \(\mathbb E[\phi_\mu(z^\star;V_t)]=\Phi_\mu(z^\star)\), proves
\(\displaystyle \sum_{t=2}^{T} \left(\mathbb E[\phi_\mu(z_t;V_t)]-\Phi_\mu(z^\star)\right) \leq\log m. \)
This proof keeps the actual dependence between \(z_t\) and \(V_t\); it never replaces the expected sampled objective by \(\mathbb E[\Phi_\mu(z_t)]\).

Adding the first-round gap gives the valid full-horizon bound stated above.

4. Relation to the cited result

[Yang et al., Theorem 4.1 and Appendix C.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00160v1#Thmtheorem4.1) state the cited bound. Their proof uses an inequality involving consecutive iterates, then sums from the first round. Under the convention in this task, that update inequality applies only from round two: the first bid is prescribed without an update using \(V_1\). The explicit counterexample proves that the missing first-round term cannot be absorbed into \(\log(m\tau)\).
