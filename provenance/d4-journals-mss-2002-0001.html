Theorem 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given demand types \(d_1,\ldots,d_\tau\in\mathbb{N}\) with atomless masses \(\mu_j>0\), \(\sum_j\mu_j=1\), and rational per-capita supply \(b\le D:=\sum_j\mu_jd_j\), find a symmetric law over measurable allocations that gives each type-\(j\) individual an integer bundle in \(\{0,\ldots,d_j\}\), allocates exactly \(b\) units per capita in every realization, and gives each type-\(j\) individual expected allocation \(a_j:=bd_j/D\). Minimize the population-weighted sum of individual variances over such laws.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

The society is given by its support: \(\tau\ge1\) distinct demands \(d_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_j\), where \(\sum_j\mu_j=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible demands. Let \(m=\max_j d_j\), \(D=\sum_j\mu_jd_j\), and let the binary-rational supply satisfy \(0\le b\le D\). Set \(a_j=bd_j/D\) when \(D>0\), and \(a_j=0\) otherwise.
Represent type \(j\) by an atomless copy of \([0,1)\), with population measure \(\mu_j\,ds\). A law is a jointly measurable random allocation \(X_j(s,\omega)\in\{0,\ldots,d_j\}\). Symmetry means that all individuals of a given type have the same marginal distribution; no independence is required.
Require \(\mathbb E[X_j(s,\cdot)]=a_j\) for every \(j,s\), and \(\sum_j\mu_j\int_0^1X_j(s,\omega)\,ds=b\) in every realization. Minimize \(V=\sum_j\mu_j\int_0^1\operatorname{Var}(X_j(s,\cdot))\,ds\).
For a binary-rational threshold \(v\ge0\), consider the decision problem of whether such a law exists with \(V\le v\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the demands, masses, supply, and threshold.

Is this continuous minimum-variance decision problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), with polynomial time measured in \(\tau,L\) under binary encoding.

For finitely many agents with integer demands and integer total supply, feasible prescribed expectations admit a law minimizing every individual's variance simultaneously; such laws are exactly those whose realized shares differ from their expectations by less than one ([Tasnádi, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0165-4896(02)00014-8)). This characterization does not assert a polynomial running time. No result settling the atomless problem defined here was located in the search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative integer demands and nonnegative rational supply, retaining zero demands and the boundary cases permitted by the discrete definition.
  • When aggregate demand is zero, supply and prescribed expectations are zero, following the proportional rule's explicit zero-demand convention.
  • Represented each atomless type population by a unit interval with its mass as a measure weight.
  • Interpreted undefined symmetry as equality of individual marginal distributions within each type, without imposing independence or invariance of the entire law under every relabeling.
  • Required joint measurability to make individual expectations and population integrals well-defined.
  • Introduced a rational variance threshold to give the optimization question a finite-input decision formulation.
  • Defined m as the largest supported demand and included all numerical inputs in L; polynomial time refers to binary input length.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). Its exact minimum is
\(\displaystyle V_{\min}=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j r_j(1-r_j)\),
where \(r_j=a_j-\lfloor a_j\rfloor\).

Under ordinary binary encoding, the decision can be made in \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations using schoolbook integer arithmetic. No iteration over the \(m\) possible allocation levels is needed.

1. A lower bound for each individual

Fix an integer demand \(d\), an expectation \(a\in[0,d]\), and a random variable \(Y\in\{0,\ldots,d\}\) with \(\mathbb E[Y]=a\). Write \(k=\lfloor a\rfloor\) and \(r=a-k\).

For every integer \(n\), the product \((n-k)(n-k-1)\) is nonnegative: its two factors cannot have opposite signs. Therefore
\(\mathbb E[(Y-k)(Y-k-1)]\ge0\).
Expanding this expectation gives
\[ \mathbb E[(Y-k)(Y-k-1)] =\operatorname{Var}(Y)+r^2-r. \]
Consequently, \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)\ge r(1-r)\).

Equality holds precisely when \(Y\) is supported on \(\{k,k+1\}\), intersected with its allowed range. Its expectation then forces probabilities \(1-r\) and \(r\), respectively. When \(r=0\), equality means \(Y=k\) almost surely.

Apply this bound to \(X_j(s,\cdot)\) for every \(j,s\). Integrating and summing yields
\(\displaystyle V\ge\sum_j\mu_j r_j(1-r_j)\).
This lower bound does not require symmetry or the realization-by-realization supply constraint.

2. An atomless construction attaining the bound

First suppose \(D>0\). Since \(0\le b\le D\), every prescribed expectation satisfies \(0\le a_j\le d_j\). Put \(k_j=\lfloor a_j\rfloor\) and \(r_j=a_j-k_j\).

Take the probability space \(\Omega=[0,1)\) with Lebesgue probability measure. For \(s,u\in[0,1)\), define
\(\displaystyle X_j(s,u)=k_j+\mathbf 1_{\{\{s+u\}<r_j\}}\),
where \(\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor\) denotes fractional part.

This is jointly Borel measurable. It also takes values in \(\{0,\ldots,d_j\}\): if \(r_j>0\), then \(k_j+1\le d_j\); if \(r_j=0\), the indicator is always zero.

For every fixed \(s\), the map \(u\mapsto\{s+u\}\) preserves Lebesgue measure. Indeed, splitting its domain at \(1-s\) makes it a translation onto each of the two intervals partitioning \([0,1)\). Thus
\(\mathbb P(X_j(s,\cdot)=k_j+1)=r_j\).
Every individual of type \(j\) has the same marginal distribution, with expectation \(k_j+r_j=a_j\) and variance \(r_j(1-r_j)\).

Likewise, for every fixed realization \(u\), the map \(s\mapsto\{s+u\}\) preserves Lebesgue measure. Hence
\(\displaystyle \int_0^1X_j(s,u)\,ds=k_j+r_j=a_j\)
for every \(u\). It follows that the realized total allocation is
\(\displaystyle \sum_j\mu_j\int_0^1X_j(s,u)\,ds =\sum_j\mu_j a_j =\frac bD\sum_j\mu_jd_j=b\).

The construction therefore satisfies all constraints and attains the lower bound simultaneously for every individual. Atomlessness supplies the crucial feature: each type can have exactly the required proportion \(r_j\) receiving its upper allocation in every realization.

If \(D=0\), positivity of the masses and nonnegativity of the demands imply \(d_j=0\) for every \(j\). Also \(b=0\), so the identically zero allocation is feasible and has minimum variance zero.

3. Exact decision algorithm and running time

The preceding proof reduces the decision to comparing an explicitly computable rational number with \(v\). The following integer calculation establishes the claimed bit complexity.

Write the masses, supply, and threshold as binary-encoded fractions. Let \(Q\) be the product of all their denominators, and define integers \(c_j,B,C\) by
\(\mu_j=c_j/Q\), \(b=B/Q\), and \(v=C/Q\).
Set \(N=\sum_jc_jd_j\), so \(D=N/Q\).

If \(N=0\), answer yes, since the minimum is zero and \(v\ge0\). Otherwise, for each supported type perform Euclidean division
\(\displaystyle Bd_j=k_jN+R_j,\qquad 0\le R_j<N\).
Because \(a_j=Bd_j/N\), this gives \(r_j=R_j/N\). Therefore
\(\displaystyle V_{\min}=\frac{S}{QN^2}\),
where
\(\displaystyle S=\sum_jc_jR_j(N-R_j)\).
The decision is exactly the integer comparison \(S\le CN^2\).

All integers in this calculation have \(O(L)\) bits:

  • The bit length of \(Q\) is bounded by the sum of the input denominator bit lengths.
  • Since \(\sum_jc_j=Q\), one has \(N\le Qm\).
  • Each remainder satisfies \(R_j<N\), and \(S\le QN^2\).
  • The scaled threshold \(C\) also has \(O(L)\) bits.

There are \(O(\tau)\) additions, multiplications, and divisions of integers having \(O(L)\) bits. Schoolbook arithmetic therefore gives \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

The dependence on demand magnitude enters through its binary length, at most \(O(\log(m+1))\) per demand, rather than through \(m\) allocation levels. Thus this is polynomial time in \(\tau,L\) under binary encoding. In particular, an \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness classification under polynomial-time reductions would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
