The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) chores, types \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) with masses \(\mu_t=1/\tau\), positive rational linear disutilities \(D_t(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}d_{tj}y_j\), and accuracy \(\eta>0\), compute type bundles \(x_t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}\) and prices \(p\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}\) such that \((1-\eta)\langle p,x_t\rangle\leq\langle p,x_{t'}\rangle\), \(D_t(x_t)\leq D_t(y)\) whenever \(\langle p,y\rangle\geq\langle p,x_t\rangle\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}=1\) for every chore \(j\).

Result

The problem admits an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\eta)\). The finite-agent FPTAS applies through an exact rescaling: represent each supported type by one agent, then multiply that agent’s bundle by \(\tau\).

1. The finite-agent theorem

We use the following published result of Boodaghians, Chaudhury, and Mehta. For \(n\) agents with finite, strictly positive rational linear disutility coefficients \(c_{ij}\), and rational accuracy \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), their linear-case FPTAS computes rational allocations \(z_i\) and rational payment prices \(q\) satisfying:

  • \((1-\varepsilon)\langle q,z_i\rangle\leq\langle q,z_{i'}\rangle\) for all \(i,i'\).
  • \(D_i(z_i)\leq D_i(v)\) for every \(v\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^m\) satisfying \(\langle q,v\rangle\geq\langle q,z_i\rangle\).
  • \(\sum_i z_{ij}=1\) for every \(j\).

The running time is polynomial in \(n,m,1/\varepsilon\), the coefficient encoding length, and \(\log(\max_{ij}c_{ij}/\min_{ij}c_{ij})\). These are the stronger linear-case guarantees established by Theorems 4 and 15. The construction uses \(q_j=\min_i a_i c_{ij}\), with every \(a_i>0\); hence every \(q_j>0\). The exact rational implementation is discussed in Lemma 14 and the allocation recovery in Theorem 4. [Verified full version, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.06649#S4).

2. Constructing the output

Given the society instance, construct a finite-agent instance with exactly \(n=\tau\) agents. Agent \(t\) has coefficients

\(c_{tj}=d_{tj}\).

Keep the supply of each chore equal to \(1\), and run the preceding algorithm with \(\varepsilon=\eta\).

Its hypotheses hold: \(m,\tau\geq1\), every coefficient is a finite positive rational, and \(\eta\in(0,1)\). No types outside the listed support are introduced.

Let its output be \(z_t\) and \(q\). Define

\(S=\sum_{j=1}^m q_j,\qquad p_j=q_j/S,\qquad x_t=\tau z_t.\)

Since every \(q_j>0\), we have \(S>0\). Thus \(p\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^m\), \(\sum_jp_j=1\), and \(x_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^m\).

3. Verification of all three requirements

Approximately equal payments

For every \(t,t'\), the finite-agent guarantee gives

\((1-\eta)\langle q,z_t\rangle\leq\langle q,z_{t'}\rangle.\)

Multiplying by the positive number \(\tau/S\) yields

\((1-\eta)\langle p,x_t\rangle\leq\langle p,x_{t'}\rangle,\)

because \(\langle p,x_t\rangle=(\tau/S)\langle q,z_t\rangle\).

Exact bundle optimality

Fix a type \(t\) and any \(y\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^m\) with

\(\langle p,y\rangle\geq\langle p,x_t\rangle.\)

Substituting the definitions and multiplying by \(S>0\) gives

\(\langle q,y\rangle\geq\tau\langle q,z_t\rangle.\)

Consequently, \(v=y/\tau\) satisfies \(\langle q,v\rangle\geq\langle q,z_t\rangle\). Exact finite-agent optimality therefore implies

\(D_t(z_t)\leq D_t(y/\tau).\)

Linearity gives \(D_t(\tau z_t)=\tau D_t(z_t)\) and \(\tau D_t(y/\tau)=D_t(y)\). Hence

\(D_t(x_t)=\tau D_t(z_t)\leq D_t(y).\)

This proves the required optimality for every admissible real bundle \(y\).

Exact clearing

For each chore \(j\), using \(\mu_t=1/\tau\) and finite-agent clearing gives

\(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t x_{tj} =\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}(1/\tau)(\tau z_{tj}) =\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}z_{tj} =1.\)

4. Bit complexity

The constructed coefficient matrix is the original matrix, and the number of agents is the support size \(\tau\). Its encoding length is therefore polynomial in \(L\).

For ordinary explicit binary-rational encoding, each positive coefficient is bounded between \(2^{-O(L)}\) and \(2^{O(L)}\). Thus

\(\log(\max_{t,j}d_{tj}/\min_{t,j}d_{tj})=O(L).\)

The finite-agent running time is consequently \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\eta)\). Its rational outputs have polynomial total encoding length. Summing the \(m\) prices, dividing by their positive sum, and multiplying bundles by the integer \(\tau\) all require polynomial-time rational arithmetic and produce polynomial-size outputs.

Therefore the support-and-mass formulation inherits the FPTAS. In particular, when \(1/\eta\) is polynomially bounded in the input size, this search problem is solvable in polynomial time.
