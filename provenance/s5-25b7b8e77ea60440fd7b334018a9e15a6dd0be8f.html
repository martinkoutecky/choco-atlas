Proposition 4 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item categories, \(\tau\) valuation types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and value oracles for monotone valuations \(v_t:2^{[m]}\to\mathbb{R}_+\) satisfying \(\ell_t(S)\le v_t(S)\le(1+\epsilon)\ell_t(S)\) for some affine-linear \(\ell_t(S)=c_t+\sum_{j\in S}a_{tj}\), define \(W^*=\max\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_j\), and \(x_{t,S}\ge0\). Characterize the worst-case number of type-value queries needed to estimate \(W^*\), in particular whether Proposition 4's lower bound against subexponential-query algorithms achieving better-than-polynomial approximation in \(m,\tau\) persists when each type has arbitrarily many identical copies and supplies scale with the population.

Partial characterization of the mass problem

The full dependence of $Q(m,\tau,L,\epsilon,\rho)$ remains unresolved. However, the following bounds settle several additional regimes and clarify the qualitative obstruction.

All lower bounds below concern value queries and permit unrestricted computation between queries.

  • Polynomial-query approximation. At most $\tau(m+1)$ queries suffice for either of the factors

$$ \tau(1+\epsilon) \qquad\text{and}\qquad 1+m\epsilon(2+\epsilon). $$
Both algorithms run in polynomial time. In particular, $\epsilon=0$ permits exact optimization.

  • A sharp threshold for one type with fixed parameters. If $\tau=1$, a factor of $1+\epsilon$ uses at most $m+1$ queries. For every fixed $\epsilon>0$ and fixed $1\le\rho<1+\epsilon$, there are polynomially encoded instances requiring $2^{\Omega(m)}$ queries.
  • Exact estimation with positive error. For even $m\ge4$, power-of-two $\tau$, and every $\epsilon>0$, suitable polynomially encoded instances require

$$ \frac{\tau}{3}\binom{m}{m/2} \ge \frac{\tau\,2^m}{3(m+1)} $$
queries for exact estimation. Thus the exhaustive upper bound $\tau2^m$ is optimal within a polynomial factor in these regimes.

  • General approximation tradeoff. For $\rho\ge2$, monotonicity alone gives

$$ Q(m,\tau,L,\epsilon,\rho) \le \min\left\{\tau2^m,\; \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\,2^{O(m/\rho)}\right\}. $$
For $m$ a power of two, $2\le\rho\le m/8$, and $\epsilon\ge2\rho$, one type already requires
$$ Q(m,1,L,\epsilon,\rho) \ge \frac{2^{m/(8\rho)}}{24\rho}. $$

  • An improved vanishing-error obstruction. For power-of-two $m,n$ with $2\le n\le m$, there are instances with

$$ \tau=n,\qquad \epsilon=\frac1n, $$
on which every factor $\rho<n/2$ requires
$$ Q\ge \frac16\exp\left(\frac{m}{64n^4}\right). $$
Consequently, every fixed budget $2^{O(m^\gamma)}$, where $\gamma<1$, faces a polynomial approximation barrier along a family with $\epsilon\to0$.

These statements are proved below. Bounds involving lower-bound families assume that $L$ is large enough to encode the displayed inputs and oracle answers; their encoding lengths are explicitly discussed.

1. Feasibility and affine welfare

Categories with $q_j=0$ cannot occur in any bundle assigned positive mass: their supply constraint is a sum of nonnegative allocation variables bounded by zero. We may discard them. We may also replace $q_j$ by $\min(q_j,1)$, since total population mass is $1$.

For nonnegative affine valuations
$$ \ell_t(S)=c_t+\sum_{j\in S}a_{tj}, $$
write $A(\ell)$ for their optimal welfare. Then
$$ A(\ell) = \sum_t\mu_tc_t+ \max\left\{ \sum_{t,j}a_{tj}y_{tj}: 0\le y_{tj}\le\mu_t,\quad \sum_ty_{tj}\le q_j \right\}. $$

To prove this identity, any feasible bundle allocation yields feasible marginals
$$ y_{tj}=\sum_{S:j\in S}x_{t,S}, $$
and affine expected welfare depends only on these marginals. Conversely, every feasible marginal vector is realizable: for type $t$, independently include category $j$ with probability $y_{tj}/\mu_t$, and multiply the resulting bundle probabilities by $\mu_t$. Positivity of $\mu_t$ makes these probabilities well defined.

Therefore the near-affine promise implies
$$ A(\ell)\le W^*\le(1+\epsilon)A(\ell) $$
for every witnessing affine profile $\ell$.

The original welfare program is a finite linear program. Its feasible region is nonempty, since everyone can receive the empty bundle, and compact, since each variable lies between $0$ and its type mass. Querying every type on every bundle thus permits exact optimization with $\tau2^m$ queries.

2. Polynomial-query upper bounds

2.1 A factor of $\tau(1+\epsilon)$ from supply chains

For each type $t$, define
$$ r_{tj}=\min\left\{1,\frac{q_j}{\mu_t}\right\}. $$
Let $U$ be uniform on $[0,1]$, and set
$$ S_t(U)=\{j:r_{tj}\ge U\}. $$
This distribution has at most $m+1$ distinct bundles and satisfies
$$ \Pr(j\in S_t(U))=r_{tj}. $$

Consider the allocation that gives type $t$ this distribution and gives every other type the empty bundle. It is feasible because category $j$ is used with mass
$$ \mu_tr_{tj}=\min(\mu_t,q_j)\le q_j. $$
Let $F_t$ be its actual welfare, computed by querying the chain bundles and the empty-bundle values.

For a witnessing affine profile, put
$$ C=\sum_s\mu_sc_s, \qquad D_t=\sum_j a_{tj}\min(\mu_t,q_j). $$
The promise gives
$$ F_t\ge C+D_t. $$
Moreover, every feasible marginal vector satisfies $y_{tj}\le\min(\mu_t,q_j)$, so
$$ A(\ell)\le C+\sum_tD_t. $$
Hence
$$ \max_tF_t \ge C+\frac1\tau\sum_tD_t \ge \frac{A(\ell)}{\tau}. $$

Outputting
$$ \widehat W=\max_tF_t $$
therefore gives
$$ \widehat W\le W^* \le(1+\epsilon)A(\ell) \le\tau(1+\epsilon)\widehat W. $$

The empty bundle can be included among each type’s queried chain bundles, so the query count is at most $\tau(m+1)$. Sorting the rational thresholds and evaluating the resulting finite distributions takes polynomial time.

For $\tau=1$, this proves the factor $1+\epsilon$.

2.2 A factor of $1+m\epsilon(2+\epsilon)$ from empty and singleton queries

Query
$$ B_t=v_t(\varnothing), \qquad V_{tj}=v_t(\{j\}). $$
Consider all nonnegative affine profiles consistent with these answers:
$$ \mathcal C= \left\{ (c,a): \frac{B_t}{1+\epsilon}\le c_t\le B_t,\quad \frac{V_{tj}}{1+\epsilon}\le c_t+a_{tj}\le V_{tj},\quad c_t,a_{tj}\ge0 \right\}. $$
The promise ensures that $\mathcal C$ is nonempty. It is compact because $c_t\le B_t$ and $a_{tj}\le V_{tj}$.

Define
$$ A_{\min}=\min_{\ell\in\mathcal C}A(\ell). $$
I claim that
$$ A_{\min}\le W^* \le \bigl(1+m\epsilon(2+\epsilon)\bigr)A_{\min}. $$

First compare any two profiles $\ell,\ell'\in\mathcal C$ for a fixed type, suppressing the type index. The singleton constraints imply
$$ c+a_j\le V_j\le(1+\epsilon)(c'+a'_j), $$
while the empty-bundle constraints imply
$$ \frac{c'}{1+\epsilon}\le c\le(1+\epsilon)c'. $$

For a nonempty bundle $S$ of cardinality $r$,
$$ \begin{aligned} \ell(S) &=\sum_{j\in S}(c+a_j)-(r-1)c\\ &\le(1+\epsilon)\ell'(S) +(r-1)\bigl((1+\epsilon)c'-c\bigr)\\ &\le(1+\epsilon)\ell'(S) +(r-1)\frac{\epsilon(2+\epsilon)}{1+\epsilon}c'. \end{aligned} $$
Because $c'\le\ell'(S)$ and $r\le m$, this gives
$$ \ell(S)\le K_m\ell'(S), \qquad K_m=(1+\epsilon) +(m-1)\frac{\epsilon(2+\epsilon)}{1+\epsilon}. $$
For the empty bundle, the same conclusion follows from $c\le(1+\epsilon)c'$.

Choose an actual witnessing profile $\ell$ and a minimizing profile $\ell_0\in\mathcal C$. The pointwise comparison gives
$$ A(\ell)\le K_mA(\ell_0)=K_mA_{\min}. $$
Since the witnessing profile belongs to $\mathcal C$,
$$ A_{\min}\le A(\ell)\le W^*. $$
Combining these inequalities with the promise proves
$$ W^*\le(1+\epsilon)K_mA_{\min} =\bigl(1+m\epsilon(2+\epsilon)\bigr)A_{\min}. $$

It remains to justify polynomial-time computation of $A_{\min}$. The dual of the affine marginal program gives the following single linear program:
$$ \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad& \sum_t\mu_tc_t+ \sum_{t,j}\mu_tz_{tj}+ \sum_jq_jp_j,\\ \text{subject to}\quad& (c,a)\in\mathcal C,\\ &p_j,z_{tj}\ge0,\\ &z_{tj}+p_j\ge a_{tj}. \end{aligned} $$
For fixed $(c,a)$, minimizing over $(z,p)$ gives exactly $A(\ell)$ by finite linear-programming duality. Thus its overall optimum is $A_{\min}$. The program has polynomially many variables and constraints, with polynomial coefficient bit lengths.

This algorithm uses exactly $\tau(m+1)$ queries. When $\epsilon=0$, the consistent affine profile is unique and the estimate is exact.

3. The general monotone upper bound

This section proves the bound
$$ Q\le\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\,2^{O(m/\rho)} \qquad(\rho\ge2). $$

3.1 A covering lemma for nonnegative prices

Fix rational prices $p_j\ge0$ and $\alpha>1$. Write $p(S)=\sum_{j\in S}p_j$.

There is a randomized family of
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,H,\log(1/\delta)) \exp\left(\frac{4m}{\alpha-1}\right) $$
bundles, where $H$ bounds the price bit lengths, such that with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every $S$ there is a sampled $T$ satisfying
$$ S\subseteq T, \qquad p(T)\le\alpha p(S). $$

To prove this, include the bundle of all zero-price categories. It handles every $S$ with $p(S)=0$.

Otherwise, let $p_{\min}$ be the smallest positive price and $P_{\max}=\sum_jp_j$. Use a doubling grid spanning
$$ \frac{(\alpha-1)p_{\min}}{4m} \quad\text{through}\quad \frac{(\alpha-1)P_{\max}}{2m}. $$
For every positive $P=p(S)$, some grid point $h$ satisfies
$$ \frac{(\alpha-1)P}{4m}\le h\le \frac{(\alpha-1)P}{2m}. $$

At scale $h$, independently include category $j$ with probability $h/(h+p_j)$. Then
$$ \Pr(S\subseteq T) =\prod_{j\in S}(1+p_j/h)^{-1} \ge\exp(-P/h) \ge\exp\left(-\frac{4m}{\alpha-1}\right). $$
Conditional on containing $S$, the selections outside $S$ remain independent, and
$$ \mathbb E[p(T\setminus S)\mid S\subseteq T] \le mh \le\frac{(\alpha-1)P}{2}. $$
Markov’s inequality gives conditional probability at least $1/2$ that the added price is at most $(\alpha-1)P$.

Thus a sample at the appropriate scale succeeds for a fixed $S$ with probability at least
$$ \beta=\frac12\exp\left(-\frac{4m}{\alpha-1}\right). $$
Taking at least
$$ \beta^{-1}\bigl(m\log2+\log(1/\delta)\bigr) $$
samples at every scale and applying a union bound over $2^m$ bundles proves the assertion. The number of scales is polynomial in $m$ and $H$.

3.2 Valid cuts and approximate acceptance

After discarding zero-supply categories, the full welfare dual is
$$ \begin{aligned} W^*=\min\quad& \sum_t\mu_tu_t+\sum_jq_jp_j,\\ \text{subject to}\quad& u_t+p(S)\ge v_t(S)\quad\text{for all }t,S,\\ &p_j\ge0. \end{aligned} $$
The empty-bundle constraint implies $u_t\ge0$.

At a candidate $(u,p)$, query every type on the sampled covering family.

If a queried inequality is violated, it is a genuine full-dual constraint and provides a valid separating cut.

If every queried inequality holds and the covering event succeeds, then for every $S$ a sampled superset $T$ gives
$$ v_t(S)\le v_t(T)\le u_t+p(T)\le u_t+\alpha p(S). $$
Consequently, $(u,\alpha p)$ is full-dual feasible, and its objective is at most $\alpha$ times the candidate objective.

This is an approximate acceptance test, not an exact separation oracle.

3.3 A bounded number of rejected iterations

Fix a budget $B>0$, and let $K_B$ be the set of nonnegative full-dual feasible points with objective at most $B$. Put
$$ d=\tau+m, \qquad b_{\min}=\min\bigl(\{\mu_t\}_t\cup\{q_j\}_j\bigr)>0. $$
Every coordinate of a point in $K_B$ is at most $B/b_{\min}$, so $K_B$ lies in a ball of radius
$$ R=\frac{dB}{b_{\min}} $$
centered at the origin.

Fix $\eta>0$. If $W^*\le B/(1+\eta)$, take an optimal dual solution and increase every coordinate by
$$ h=\frac{\eta B}{4(1+\eta)(m+1)}. $$
The objective increases by at most $h(m+1)$, since
$$ \sum_t\mu_t+\sum_jq_j\le m+1. $$
Every valuation inequality gains slack at least $h$, and every coordinate becomes at least $h$.

The ball of radius
$$ r=\frac{h}{2d} $$
around the shifted solution lies in $K_B$. Indeed, coordinate perturbations are at most $h/2$; the norm of a valuation constraint’s coefficient vector is at most $\sqrt d$, so its slack decreases by at most $\sqrt d\,r\le h/2$; and the objective perturbation is at most $(m+1)r$, smaller than the remaining budget slack.

Here is the precise ellipsoid property used. For a prescribed number $N$ of iterations, rounded rational central-cut updates preserve containment of the intersection with each supplied halfspace, contract volume by at least $\exp(-c/d)$ per update for an absolute $c>0$, and keep center and matrix bit lengths polynomial in $d,N$, the initial-radius encoding, and the cut-normal encoding. These are the update properties proved in Section 2, Lemmas 2.1–2.3, with the polynomial encoding analysis in Section 3 of [Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf).

We use these properties only while centers are rejected. First check nonnegativity and the budget constraint; then check the sampled valuation inequalities. Every rejection supplies a halfspace containing $K_B$, which may be weakened to a central cut. Its normal has polynomial bit length.

After
$$ O\bigl(d^2\log(R/r)+1\bigr) $$
rejected iterations, the enclosing ellipsoid has volume smaller than an $r$-ball. Therefore $K_B$ cannot contain the ball established above, and rejection certifies
$$ W^*>\frac{B}{1+\eta}. $$
The center of that hypothetical ball need not be known to the algorithm.

Finally,
$$ \frac Rr= \frac{8d^2(1+\eta)(m+1)}{\eta b_{\min}}, $$
so for fixed $\eta$ the number of iterations and center bit lengths are polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

3.4 Producing the one-sided estimate

Let $M$ be the remaining category set. If $M$ is empty, empty-bundle queries determine the optimum.

Otherwise query $v_t(M)$ for every type and define
$$ U=\sum_t\mu_tv_t(M), $$
$$ D=\max_t\left[ \min\left(\mu_t,\min_{j\in M}q_j\right)v_t(M) \right]. $$
If all full-bundle values vanish, monotonicity implies $W^*=0$. Otherwise,
$$ 0<D\le W^*\le U, \qquad \frac UD\le\frac1{b_{\min}}. $$
The lower bound allocates $M$ to the indicated feasible amount of one type and gives everyone else the empty bundle.

For an integer target $\lambda\ge2$, set
$$ \eta=\frac1{10}, \qquad \alpha=\frac{\lambda}{(1+\eta)^2}. $$
Test budgets
$$ D,\ D(1+\eta),\ D(1+\eta)^2,\ldots $$
until the first acceptance. There are polynomially many tests, since a budget at least $(1+\eta)U$ cannot be rejected.

If $D$ is accepted, output $D$. Otherwise, for the first accepted budget $B$, output
$$ \widehat W=\frac{B}{(1+\eta)^2}. $$
Rejection of the preceding budget proves $\widehat W<W^*$, while successful acceptance proves
$$ W^*\le\alpha B=\lambda\widehat W. $$

Allocate total failure probability $1/3$ across the predetermined polynomial bound on covering tests. Conditional on each preceding history, the fresh covering samples have their stated guarantee. A union bound therefore makes all acceptance certificates valid with probability at least $2/3$.

For $\lambda\ge2$,
$$ \alpha-1\ge\frac{79\lambda}{242}. $$
The query exponent is consequently $O(m/\lambda)$. For $m\ge2$, use $\lambda=\min(m,\lfloor\rho\rfloor)$; for $m=1$, enumerate. This gives the asserted bound for every $\rho\ge2$, with the constant overhead absorbed into the polynomial factor. Exact rational Bernoulli sampling yields the same bound for expected running time, up to polynomial overhead.

4. An indistinguishability lemma

The lower bounds use the following elementary coupling argument.

Suppose a reference oracle has welfare $B$, while every oracle in a random hidden family has welfare at least $G>\rho B$. Assume that each fixed type-value query differs from the reference answer with probability at most $p$ over the hidden instance.

For an algorithm making at most $T$ queries, couple the reference and hidden executions using the same random seed. For a fixed seed, the queries on the reference transcript are fixed independently of the hidden instance. Any first differing answer must occur at one of these queries. Thus
$$ \Pr(\text{transcripts differ})\le Tp. $$

The reference execution succeeds with probability at least $2/3$. Averaging the success guarantee over hidden instances gives the same bound for the hidden execution. Hence both executions succeed simultaneously with probability at least $1/3$.

Their successful output intervals are disjoint, because
$$ \frac{W_{\mathrm{hidden}}^*}{\rho}\ge\frac G\rho>B. $$
On simultaneous success their transcripts must therefore differ. Consequently,
$$ T\ge\frac1{3p}. $$

No restriction on intervening computation is used.

5. One-type lower bounds and the fixed-parameter threshold

Let $m,n$ be powers of two with $2\le n\le m$, and set $k=m/n$. There is one type of mass $1$, with supplies $q_j=1/n$.

Choose a uniformly random equipartition
$$ A_1,\ldots,A_n, \qquad |A_i|=k. $$
For a positive dyadic $e\le\epsilon$, define
$$ w(S)=1+e\min(2|S|/m,1), $$
$$ v_A(S)=1+e\max\left\{ \min(2|S|/m,1),\ \mathbf1\{\text{some }A_i\subseteq S\} \right\}. $$
Both are monotone, and both lie between the constant affine witness $1$ and $1+e\le1+\epsilon$.

Every feasible allocation satisfies $\mathbb E|S|\le m/n$. Therefore
$$ W_w^*\le1+\frac{2e}{n}. $$
Choosing each block of any equipartition with probability $1/n$ attains equality, so
$$ W_w^*=1+\frac{2e}{n}. $$

For the hidden instance, choosing each hidden block with probability $1/n$ gives everyone value $1+e$ and meets every supply constraint. Hence
$$ W_A^*=1+e. $$

For $|S|\ge m/2$, the answers agree. For $|S|<m/2$, a difference requires a hidden block to lie in $S$. A specified block is a uniform $k$-subset, so
$$ \Pr(A_i\subseteq S) =\frac{\binom{|S|}{k}}{\binom mk} \le\left(\frac{|S|}{m}\right)^k \le2^{-k}. $$
A union bound gives difference probability at most $n2^{-k}$.

Thus, whenever
$$ \rho<\frac{1+e}{1+2e/n}, $$
the coupling lemma proves
$$ T\ge\frac{2^{m/n}}{3n}. $$

5.1 Matching the general exponent for large $\epsilon$

Assume $2\le\rho\le m/8$ and $\epsilon\ge2\rho$. Let $n$ be the smallest power of two at least $4\rho$, and choose the integer
$$ e=\lfloor2\rho\rfloor. $$
Then
$$ 4\rho\le n<8\rho,\qquad n\mid m,\qquad e\le\epsilon. $$
Also $e>2\rho-2$, so
$$ \rho\left(1+\frac{2e}{n}\right) \le\rho+\frac e2 <1+e. $$
The lower bound becomes
$$ T\ge\frac{2^{m/(8\rho)}}{24\rho}. $$

The explicit encoding length is polynomial in $m$ and the encoding length of $\epsilon$; the displayed oracle answers have polynomial bit length. This qualifies the encoding claim even when $\epsilon$ itself has a long encoding.

5.2 The one-type threshold

Fix $\epsilon>0$ and $1\le\rho<1+\epsilon$. Choose a dyadic $e\le\epsilon$ such that $1+e>\rho$. Then choose a fixed power-of-two $n$ sufficiently large that
$$ \rho<\frac{1+e}{1+2e/n}. $$
For all sufficiently large power-of-two $m$, the construction gives
$$ T\ge\frac{2^{m/n}}{3n}=2^{\Omega(m)}. $$
Padding with zero-supply categories extends the exponential lower bound to arbitrary sufficiently large $m$.

Together with Section 2.1, this establishes the fixed-parameter dichotomy:
$$ \begin{aligned} \rho\ge1+\epsilon&:\quad Q\le m+1,\\ 1\le\rho<1+\epsilon&:\quad Q=2^{\Theta(m)} \end{aligned} $$
in the worst case for $\tau=1$ and fixed $\epsilon,\rho$, with polynomially bounded $L$. The constants in the exponential statement may depend on those fixed parameters.

6. A stronger hidden-partition lower bound

This construction adapts the hidden-partition approach in Proposition 4 of [Roughgarden, Talgam-Cohen, and Vondrák](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol081-approx-random2017/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2017.22/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2017.22.pdf). The following proof establishes the mass-welfare gap and improves the concentration exponent used in the notes.

Let $m,n$ be powers of two satisfying
$$ 2\le n\le m, \qquad a=m/n. $$
There are $n$ types, each of mass $1/n$, and $q_j=1/n$. Choose a uniformly random equipartition $A_1,\ldots,A_n$, with $|A_t|=a$.

For positive dyadic parameters $b$ and $e$ satisfying $e\le\min(1,\epsilon)$, define
$$ \ell_t(S)=b+\frac{|S\cap A_t|}{a}, $$
$$ w(S)=\left(1+\frac e2\right) \left(b+\frac{|S|}{m}\right), $$
$$ v_t(S)= \max\left\{ \ell_t(S),\ \min\bigl(w(S),(1+e)\ell_t(S)\bigr) \right\}. $$

All constituent functions are monotone. Minimum and maximum preserve monotonicity, and
$$ \ell_t(S)\le v_t(S)\le(1+e)\ell_t(S)\le(1+\epsilon)\ell_t(S). $$
The reference instance gives every type the affine valuation $w$ and therefore also satisfies the promise.

6.1 Welfare separation

In the reference instance, total attainable expected cardinality is $m/n$. It is attained by giving the full bundle to mass $1/n$ and empty bundles to everyone else. Hence
$$ B=W_{\mathrm{ref}}^* =\left(1+\frac e2\right)\left(b+\frac1n\right). $$

In the hidden instance, give every person of type $t$ the bundle $A_t$. Each category is used by exactly mass $1/n$, so the allocation is feasible. Its welfare is at least
$$ G=1+b. $$

6.2 When an answer agrees with the reference

Fix a query $(t,S)$ and write
$$ s=\frac{|S|}{m}, \qquad \Delta=\frac{|S\cap A_t|}{a}-s. $$
Then
$$ \ell_t(S)=b+s+\Delta. $$
If
$$ |\Delta|\le\frac{e(b+s)}{2(1+e)}, $$
we have both
$$ \ell_t(S)\le w(S) \qquad\text{and}\qquad w(S)\le(1+e)\ell_t(S). $$
Indeed, these inequalities are respectively equivalent to
$$ \Delta\le\frac e2(b+s), \qquad \Delta\ge-\frac{e(b+s)}{2(1+e)}. $$
Thus the hidden answer equals $w(S)$.

Since $e\le1$, the simpler condition
$$ |\Delta|\le\frac{e(b+s)}4 $$
also guarantees agreement.

6.3 A self-contained hypergeometric concentration bound

Let $Z=|S\cap A_t|$. Then $\mathbb EZ=as$. For every real $\lambda$,
$$ \mathbb E e^{\lambda Z} \le(1-s+se^\lambda)^a. $$

Here is a justification. For nonnegative numbers $z_1,\ldots,z_m$, their elementary symmetric polynomial satisfies
$$ \frac1{\binom ma}\sum_{|A|=a}\prod_{j\in A}z_j \le\left(\frac1m\sum_jz_j\right)^a. $$
For $a=1$ this is equality. For $a\ge2$, maximize the left-hand numerator at fixed sum over the compact nonnegative simplex. A maximizer has at least $a$ positive coordinates, since otherwise its value is zero whereas equal coordinates give a positive value. If two coordinates differ, replacing them by their average increases their product while preserving their sum. 8. What the budget distinction means

The mass problem retains a strong value-query obstruction. In particular, for every fixed $\gamma<1$, polynomially encoded instances with vanishing error defeat subpolynomial approximation using $2^{O(m^\gamma)}$ queries.

$$ \rho=\lceil\log_2m\rceil $$

$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\,2^{O(m/\log m)} $$
queries. When $\tau,L$ are polynomial in $m$, this budget is literally $2^{o(m)}$, and the approximation factor is $m^{o(1)}$.

The additional polynomial-query bounds show why support and error must also enter the answer. One type admits factor $1+\epsilon$, while all support sizes admit factor $1+m\epsilon(2+\epsilon)$. Conversely, exact estimation becomes exponentially difficult for every positive error allowance, and growing support can conceal polynomial welfare gaps even as that allowance vanishes.
