The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), and approval types \(t\) with approval sets \(A_t\) and positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), find an integral committee \(W\subseteq C\) of size \(k\) such that, for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and every cohort of submasses \(0\leq\nu_t\leq\mu_t\), if \(\sum_t\nu_t\geq\ell/k\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{t:\nu_t>0} A_t\right|\geq\ell\), then \(\sum_{t:\,|A_t\cap W|\geq\ell}\nu_t>0\). What is the complexity of finding such a committee as a function of \(m\), support size \(\tau\), and mass bit length \(L\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a candidate set of size \(m\geq1\), and let \(k\) be an integer with \(1\leq k\leq m\).
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) distinct approval types with positive masses \(\mu_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\), encoded in binary, satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Each type specifies an approval set \(A_t\subseteq C\), possibly empty. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets; \(L\) is the total bit length of the encoded masses.
A cohort is any vector of real submasses \(\nu_t\in[0,\mu_t]\). An integral committee \(W\subseteq C\), \(|W|=k\), satisfies mass EJR if, for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and every cohort,
\(\sum_t\nu_t\geq\ell/k\ \text{and}\ \left|\bigcap_{t:\nu_t>0}A_t\right|\geq\ell\ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_{t:\,|A_t\cap W|\geq\ell}\nu_t>0.\)

Is finding an integral committee satisfying mass EJR solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,k\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

For an explicit list of \(n\) voters, MaxSwapPAV finds an EJR committee in polynomial time in \(n,m,k\). [Aziz and Huang](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~haziz/ejrcomp.pdf)
No complexity result for exactly the binary-rational support model above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified the committee-size domain as an integer between 1 and m, as required by the discrete definition.
  • Allowed empty approval sets, as permitted by the discrete ballot domain.
  • Specified that cohort submasses are real, while input masses are binary rationals.
  • Defined L as the total bit length of the encoded masses.

Result

The search problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L,k\).

A mass EJR violation always yields a committee swap that increases normalized PAV score by at least \(1/k^2\). This bound is independent of the smallest mass. It gives a polynomial number of swaps, and all comparisons can be performed exactly with integers of polynomial bit length.

The algorithm uses the PAV swap framework of [Aziz and Huang](https://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~haziz/ejrcomp.pdf). The argument below proves the required bound directly for real cohorts and binary-rational masses.

1. The potential function

Define \(H_0=0\) and \(H_j=\sum_{i=1}^{j}1/i\) for \(j\geq1\). For a committee \(W\) of size \(k\), write \(r_t=|A_t\cap W|\) and define its normalized PAV score by

\(P(W)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t H_{r_t}.\)

Since \(0\leq r_t\leq k\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), we have

\(0\leq P(W)\leq H_k\leq k.\)

2. A uniform improvement bound

Lemma. If \(W\) violates mass EJR, there exist \(c\in C\setminus W\) and \(w\in W\) such that

\(P((W\setminus\{w\})\cup\{c\})-P(W)\geq1/k^2.\)

Proof. A violation supplies an integer \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and a cohort \((\nu_t)_t\) with total mass \(s=\sum_t\nu_t\geq\ell/k\), at least \(\ell\) common approved candidates, and

\(\sum_{t:r_t\geq\ell}\nu_t=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Consequently, \(\nu_t>0\) implies \(r_t\leq\ell-1\).

Choose \(\ell\) candidates approved by every type with positive cohort mass. At least one of these candidates, say \(c\), lies outside \(W\): otherwise every such type would approve at least \(\ell\) members of \(W\).

For each \(w\in W\), define

\(\Delta_w=P((W\setminus\{w\})\cup\{c\})-P(W).\)

We average these gains over all \(k\) possible removals. For each type \(t\), let

\(f_t=\sum_{w\in W}\bigl(H_{|A_t\cap((W\setminus\{w\})\cup\{c\})|}-H_{r_t}\bigr).\)

The following calculation gives \(f_t\) exactly:

  • If \(c\in A_t\), removing any of the \(r_t\) approved committee members and adding \(c\) leaves the approval count unchanged. Each of the other \(k-r_t\) removals increases it by one. Thus \(f_t=(k-r_t)/(r_t+1)\).
  • If \(c\notin A_t\) and \(r_t>0\), exactly \(r_t\) removals decrease the approval count, each contributing \(-1/r_t\). Thus \(f_t=-1\).
  • If \(c\notin A_t\) and \(r_t=0\), then \(f_t=0\).

In particular, \(f_t\geq-1\) for every type.

If \(\nu_t>0\), then \(c\in A_t\) and \(r_t\leq\ell-1\). Therefore

\(f_t=\frac{k+1}{r_t+1}-1\geq\frac{k+1}{\ell}-1=\frac{k-\ell+1}{\ell}.\)

Using \(0\leq\nu_t\leq\mu_t\), split each type’s mass into its cohort portion and its remaining portion. Interchanging the finite sums gives

\(\sum_{w\in W}\Delta_w=\sum_t\mu_t f_t=\sum_t\nu_t f_t+\sum_t(\mu_t-\nu_t)f_t.\)

The preceding bounds imply

\(\sum_{w\in W}\Delta_w\geq s\frac{k-\ell+1}{\ell}-(1-s)=s\frac{k+1}{\ell}-1.\)

Because \(s\geq\ell/k\), we obtain

\(\sum_{w\in W}\Delta_w\geq\frac{k+1}{k}-1=\frac1k.\)

There are \(k\) terms in this sum, so at least one satisfies \(\Delta_w\geq1/k^2\). This proves the lemma.

3. The algorithm and its correctness

Use the following algorithm:

  • Start with any committee \(W\subseteq C\) of size \(k\).
  • Examine every pair \(w\in W\), \(c\in C\setminus W\).
  • If some pair increases \(P(W)\) by at least \(1/k^2\), perform such a swap and repeat.
  • If no such pair exists, return \(W\).

Every successful swap increases the potential by at least \(1/k^2\). If \(q\) swaps have occurred, the potential bound implies

\(\frac{q}{k^2}\leq P(W_{\mathrm{final}})-P(W_{\mathrm{initial}})\leq H_k.\)

Thus \(q\leq k^2H_k\leq k^3\), so the algorithm terminates after polynomially many swaps.

At termination, every swap has gain strictly below \(1/k^2\). By the lemma, the returned committee cannot violate mass EJR.

The argument includes \(k=1\). If \(k=m\), the only committee is \(C\), which satisfies mass EJR: every type in a cohort with at least \(\ell\) common approvals itself approves at least \(\ell\) members of \(C\).

4. Exact arithmetic and binary encoding

It remains to verify polynomial bit complexity, without expanding the masses into individual voters.

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), where \(a_t,b_t\) are positive integers. Set

\(Q=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t,\qquad K=k!,\qquad p_t=a_t(Q/b_t).\)

Each \(p_t\) is an integer, and normalization gives \(\sum_t p_t=Q\). Define the integers

\(h_j=K H_j\quad\text{for }0\leq j\leq k.\)

They are integral because every integer from \(1\) through \(k\) divides \(K\). Consequently,

\(P(W)=\frac{N(W)}{QK},\qquad N(W)=\sum_t p_t h_{r_t}.\)

The swap threshold can therefore be checked exactly by the integer comparison

\(k^2\bigl(N((W\setminus\{w\})\cup\{c\})-N(W)\bigr)\geq QK.\)

The bit length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\), and that of \(K\) is \(O(k\log(k+1))\). Moreover,

\(0\leq N(W)\leq QK H_k\leq QKk.\)

Thus all integers needed for score calculations and threshold comparisons have bit length \(O(L+k\log(k+1))\).

Store approval membership and the current counts \(r_t\). For a proposed swap, the new count is

\(r'_t=r_t-\mathbf{1}_{w\in A_t}+\mathbf{1}_{c\in A_t}.\)

Its score numerator takes \(O(\tau)\) integer arithmetic operations to compute. Each round examines at most \(k(m-k)\) swaps, and there are at most \(k^3\) successful rounds plus one final round. Hence the algorithm uses \(O(k^4m\tau)\) integer arithmetic operations, together with polynomial preprocessing, on integers of polynomial bit length.

This proves polynomial-time solvability in the specified support model.

The prover’s claim is correct. The argument establishes a polynomial-time algorithm in the specified binary-rational support model. No major or minor mathematical issues were found.

  • Potential function and bounds.

Each representation count satisfies \(0\leq r_t\leq k\). Because the masses are positive and sum to one, \(P(W)\) is a weighted average of harmonic numbers between \(0\) and \(H_k\). Thus \(0\leq P(W)\leq H_k\leq k\). These bounds also hold when some approval sets are empty.

  • Extraction of a violating cohort.

Negating mass EJR gives an integer \(\ell\) and a cohort satisfying the two antecedent conditions while its represented submass is zero. This last conclusion is valid because represented submass is always nonnegative.

Since all summands in \(\sum_{t:r_t\geq\ell}\nu_t\) are nonnegative, every type with \(\nu_t>0\) has \(r_t\leq\ell-1\). Furthermore, \(s\geq\ell/k>0\), so the cohort has nonempty support; no convention for an intersection over empty support is needed.

Among any \(\ell\) common approved candidates, at least one lies outside \(W\). Otherwise every positive-cohort type would have representation at least \(\ell\), contradicting the preceding bound.

  • Exact calculation of summed swap gains.

All three cases for \(f_t\) are correct:

  • If \(c\in A_t\), the \(r_t\) approved removals preserve the count, and the other \(k-r_t\) removals contribute \(1/(r_t+1)\) each.
  • If \(c\notin A_t\) and \(r_t>0\), the \(r_t\) approved removals contribute \(-1/r_t\) each.
  • If \(c\notin A_t\) and \(r_t=0\), every contribution is zero.

Consequently \(f_t\geq-1\) universally. For a positive-cohort type, \(c\in A_t\) and \(r_t+1\leq\ell\), giving precisely the stated lower bound \(f_t\geq(k-\ell+1)/\ell\). No division by zero occurs.

  • Uniform improvement bound.

Splitting each mass into \(\nu_t\) and \(\mu_t-\nu_t\) is legitimate because both portions are nonnegative. Their total masses are \(s\) and \(1-s\), respectively. The finite-sum identity and both lower bounds therefore give
\[ \sum_{w\in W}\Delta_w \geq s\frac{k-\ell+1}{\ell}-(1-s) =s\frac{k+1}{\ell}-1 \geq\frac1k. \]
Averaging over the \(k\) removals yields a gain of at least \(1/k^2\). The constant and its inclusive inequality are correct, and the argument applies to arbitrary real cohort submasses.

  • Termination and correctness.

Every accepted swap increases \(P(W)\) by at least \(1/k^2\). Applied to any finite execution prefix, the potential bound limits the number of successful swaps to \(k^2H_k\leq k^3\), establishing termination without assuming it beforehand.

At termination, every possible swap has gain strictly less than \(1/k^2\). This contradicts the lemma whenever mass EJR is violated, so the returned committee satisfies mass EJR. The inclusive acceptance threshold is essential and is implemented correctly.

The cases \(k=1\) and \(k=m\) are covered. When \(k=m\), every positive-cohort type meeting the common-approval condition has representation at least \(\ell\), and the cohort’s positive total mass ensures the required conclusion.

The constructions of \(Q\), \(K\), \(p_t\), and \(h_j\) are valid without requiring reduced input fractions. Normalization gives \(\sum_t p_t=Q\), and every denominator occurring in \(H_j\), for \(j\leq k\), divides \(k!\).

The score numerator and integer threshold comparison are exact. The bounds on the bit lengths of \(Q\), \(K\), \(N(W)\), and the comparison intermediates are correct. In particular, multiplying a score difference by \(k^2\) adds only \(O(\log(k+1))\) bits.

Computing each proposed score requires \(O(\tau)\) integer operations. The number of swap examinations is polynomial, and the stated \(O(k^4m\tau)\) arithmetic-operation bound is valid. Preprocessing and approval membership storage also have polynomial size and cost. Polynomial bit lengths therefore yield polynomial running time without expanding masses into voters.
