The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a group map \(g:T\to\{1,\ldots,k\}\), nonnegative rational contact intensities \(\lambda_{st}\), a deadline \(D\), and a seed-mass budget \(b\), choose \(y_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_t y_t\le b\). Set \(a_t^0=x_t^0=y_t\), and for \(r=0,\ldots,D-1\) define \(a_t^{r+1}=(\mu_t-x_t^r)(1-\exp(-\sum_s\lambda_{st}a_s^r))\) and \(x_t^{r+1}=x_t^r+a_t^{r+1}\). For \(F_i(y)=\sum_{t:g(t)=i}x_t^D\), find a rational feasible seed-mass vector whose \(\sum_i\sqrt{F_i(y)}\) value is within additive \(\varepsilon\) of the maximum.

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types. Let \(m=k\) be the number of groups, with a surjective map \(g:T\to\{1,\ldots,k\}\).
The input also gives finite nonnegative binary-rational intensities \(\lambda_{st}\), a unary-encoded integer deadline \(D\ge0\), a binary-rational budget \(0<b\le1\), and binary-rational accuracy \(0<\varepsilon<1\). Let \(L\) be the total bit length of the binary-encoded input data.
A feasible allocation satisfies \(0\le y_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t y_t\le b\). Set \(a_t^0=x_t^0=y_t\), and for \(r=0,\ldots,D-1\) define \(a_t^{r+1}=(\mu_t-x_t^r)(1-\exp(-\sum_{s\in T}\lambda_{st}a_s^r))\) and \(x_t^{r+1}=x_t^r+a_t^{r+1}\).
Here \(a_t^r\) is newly activated mass and \(x_t^r\) is cumulative activated mass; seeds count toward reach, activation persists, and each newly activated cohort transmits only in the next round. Define \(F_i(y)=\sum_{t:g(t)=i}x_t^D\), \(W(y)=\sum_{i=1}^k\sqrt{F_i(y)}\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_{y\text{ feasible}}W(y)\), with the maximum over real allocations. Group reach is unnormalized.

Can a rational feasible allocation satisfying \(W(y)\ge\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,D,L,1/\varepsilon\), or is obtaining this additive approximation NP-hard even when \(1/\varepsilon\) is polynomially bounded in \(m+\tau+D+L\)?

For finite independent-cascade networks, the discrete anchor states that greedy maximization of square-root group reach yields total expected reach at least \((1-1/e)\sqrt{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{total}}}\), where \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mathrm{total}}\) is the maximum total expected reach under the same seed budget. Result

Obtaining the requested additive approximation is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\), already when \(m=k=1\) and \(D=1\). The reduction uses only positive dyadic masses and finite nonnegative dyadic intensities, with \(1/\varepsilon=O(\tau^8)\). Consequently, the proposed polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof accounts for fractional allocations and permits seeding every supported type.

1. A quadratic characterization of clique number

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a simple graph with \(n\) vertices and clique number \(\omega\). For nonnegative numbers \(z_v\), write \(B=\sum_{v\in V}z_v\) and define

\( Q_G(z)=\sum_{v\in V}z_v^2+ 2\sum_{\{u,v\}\notin E,\ u\ne v}z_uz_v, \)

where the second sum runs over unordered pairs. Then

\( \min_{\substack{z_v\ge0\\ \sum_vz_v=B}}Q_G(z)=\dfrac{B^2}{\omega}. \)

Here is a proof. The assertion is immediate for \(B=0\), so suppose \(B>0\). Define \(H_G(z)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}z_uz_v\). Expanding \(B^2\) gives

\( Q_G(z)=B^2-2H_G(z). \)

If two nonadjacent vertices \(u,v\) have positive coordinates, keep their sum fixed and transfer mass between them. Because \(\{u,v\}\notin E\), the function \(H_G\) is affine in this transfer. One endpoint therefore does not decrease \(H_G\) and eliminates a positive coordinate. Repeating this operation produces a vector whose positive support is a clique, without increasing \(Q_G\).

If that clique has size \(r\), then \(r\le\omega\), and the resulting vector satisfies

\( Q_G(z)=\sum_vz_v^2\ge\dfrac{B^2}{r}\ge\dfrac{B^2}{\omega}. \)

Thus the original vector also satisfies this lower bound. Equality is attained by putting mass \(B/\omega\) on each vertex of a maximum clique.

2. Construction of the society

We reduce from deciding whether \(\omega(G)\ge\ell\), the classical \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete CLIQUE problem. [Karp’s original paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf) establishes its completeness. We may restrict to \(n\ge2\) and \(2\le\ell\le n\), since the excluded cases are decided directly.

Let \(P\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(P\ge4n\), and set

\( h=\dfrac1P,\qquad b=\dfrac1{4P},\qquad \eta=\dfrac1{16P^4},\qquad c=nh. \)

Create a vertex type \(v\) and an auxiliary type \(d_v\) for every \(v\in V\), together with one filler type \(f\). Assign masses

\( \mu_v=\mu_{d_v}=h,\qquad \mu_f=1-2nh. \)

These masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one; indeed, \(\mu_f\ge1/2\). There are \(\tau=2n+1\) supported types. Put every type in the sole group and set \(D=1\).

For each vertex \(v\), let

\( N_v=\{v\}\cup\{u\ne v:\{u,v\}\notin E\},\qquad d_v^{\,\mathrm{count}}=|N_v|,\qquad p_v=n-d_v^{\,\mathrm{count}}. \)

The notation \(d_v^{\,\mathrm{count}}\) denotes an integer, distinct from the auxiliary type \(d_v\). Define the intensities by

\( \lambda_{uv}=\eta\quad\text{when }u\in N_v, \qquad \lambda_{v,d_v}=\eta p_v, \)

and set all other intensities to zero. In particular, \(\lambda_{vv}=\eta\), which is permitted by the stated model. Every intensity is finite, nonnegative, and dyadic.

3. Uniform bounds for every feasible allocation

For any feasible allocation \(y\), define

\( z_v=y_v,\qquad w_v=y_{d_v},\qquad B=\sum_vz_v,\qquad Y=\sum_ty_t. \)

Thus \(0\le B\le Y\le b\). Write

\( q_t=\sum_s\lambda_{st}y_s. \)

Because \(D=1\), total reach is exactly

\( R(y)=\sum_tx_t^1 =Y+\sum_t(\mu_t-y_t)(1-e^{-q_t}). \)

For every \(q\ge0\),

\( 0\le q-(1-e^{-q})\le\dfrac{q^2}{2}. \)

Indeed, \(1-e^{-u}=\int_0^u e^{-v}\,dv\le u\), and hence

\( q-(1-e^{-q})=\int_0^q(1-e^{-u})\,du \)

lies between zero and \(\int_0^q u\,du=q^2/2\).

Consider the rational linearization

\( \widehat R(y)=Y+\sum_t(\mu_t-y_t)q_t. \)

The construction gives

\( q_v=\eta\sum_{u\in N_v}z_u,\qquad q_{d_v}=\eta p_vz_v,\qquad q_f=0. \)

Since nonadjacency is symmetric, each vertex coordinate contributes to exactly \(d_v^{\,\mathrm{count}}\) vertex targets. Consequently,

\( \sum_t\mu_tq_t =\eta h\sum_v(d_v^{\,\mathrm{count}}+p_v)z_v =\eta cB, \)

and

\( \sum_ty_tq_t =\eta Q_G(z)+\eta\sum_vp_vz_vw_v. \)

Therefore,

\( \widehat R(y) =Y+\eta cB-\eta Q_G(z)-\eta\sum_vp_vz_vw_v. \)

Also, every \(q_t\le\eta nb\). The exponential bound and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) imply

\( 0\le\widehat R(y)-R(y) \le\frac12\sum_t\mu_tq_t^2 \le\mathcal E, \qquad \mathcal E:=\frac{\eta^2n^2b^2}{2}. \)

In particular, the following rational expression bounds reach from above:

\( R(y)\le U(y):=b+\eta cB-\eta Q_G(z). \)

By Section 1,

\( U(y)\le b+\eta cB-\frac{\eta B^2}{\omega}. \)

The right-hand side is maximized over \(0\le B\le b\) at \(B=b\). Explicitly, its value at \(b\) minus its value at \(B\) is

\( \eta(b-B)\left(c-\frac{b+B}{\omega}\right)\ge0, \)

because \(c=n/P>2b\). Define, for positive integers \(j\),

\( V_j=b+\eta cb-\frac{\eta b^2}{j}. \)

We have proved the uniform bound

\( R(y)\le U(y)\le V_\omega. \)

This bound includes allocations that seed auxiliary or filler types.

For the converse bound, seed only a maximum clique, with \(z_v=b/\omega\) on its vertices. This allocation is feasible because \(b/\omega\le b<h\). It satisfies \(Y=B=b\), \(Q_G(z)=b^2/\omega\), and \(w_v=0\). Thus \(\widehat R(y)=V_\omega\), giving

\( V_\omega-\mathcal E\le R^*\le V_\omega, \qquad R^*:=\max_{y\text{ feasible}}R(y). \)

The maximum exists because the feasible region is compact and the recurrence is continuous.

4. Accuracy and the distinguishing gap

Set

\( \delta=\frac{\eta b^2}{P^2}=\frac1{256P^8}, \qquad \varepsilon=\frac{\delta}{64}=\frac1{16384P^8}. \)

Both are dyadic, and

\( \frac{\mathcal E}{\delta} =\frac{\eta n^2P^2}{2} =\frac{n^2}{32P^2} \le\frac1{32}. \)

Moreover,

\( V_\ell-V_{\ell-1} =\frac{\eta b^2}{\ell(\ell-1)} \ge\delta, \)

since \(\ell(\ell-1)\le n^2\le P^2\).

Suppose an algorithm returns a rational feasible allocation satisfying the requested guarantee. With one group,

\( W(y)=\sqrt{R(y)},\qquad \mathrm{OPT}=\sqrt{R^*}. \)

Every type’s one-round reach lies between zero and its mass, so \(0\le W(y)\le\mathrm{OPT}\le1\). Hence

\( R^*-R(y) =(\mathrm{OPT}-W(y))(\mathrm{OPT}+W(y)) \le2\varepsilon. \)

Compute \(U(y)\) exactly from the returned rational coordinates and compare it with the rational threshold

\( \theta=\frac{V_\ell+V_{\ell-1}}2. \)

If \(\omega\ge\ell\), the established bounds yield

\( U(y)\ge R(y)\ge R^*-2\varepsilon \ge V_\ell-\mathcal E-2\varepsilon \ge V_\ell-\frac{\delta}{16} >\theta. \)

The last strict inequality follows from \(V_\ell-V_{\ell-1}\ge\delta\).

If \(\omega\le\ell-1\), the uniform upper bound instead gives

\( U(y)\le V_\omega\le V_{\ell-1}<\theta. \)

Thus one call to the approximation algorithm decides CLIQUE. The comparison uses only rational arithmetic; no exponential evaluation is required.

5. Encoding and conclusion

Since \(4n\le P<8n\), all constructed input rationals have \(O(\log n)\) bits, and the complete intensity matrix has polynomial encoding length. Furthermore,

\( \frac1\varepsilon=16384P^8=O(n^8)=O(\tau^8). \)

A runtime polynomial in \(m,\tau,D,L,1/\varepsilon\) would therefore be polynomial in the graph input size. Its rational output also has polynomial length, so the exact computation and comparison of \(U(y)\) take polynomial time.

This proves the requested additive approximation is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\), even with polynomially bounded inverse accuracy, one group, and one transmission round.
