The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite firm and worker type sets \(F\) and \(W\), rational type masses \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\), rankings of counterpart types and \(\bot\), an initial mass matching \(\lambda\), and a target stable mass matching \(\mu\), where a state is \(x=(x_{fw},x_{f\bot},x_{\bot w})\) with prescribed row and column masses, decide whether \(\lambda\) can reach \(\mu\) by finitely many moves transferring \(q>0\) mass from currently assigned \(f\)- and \(w\)-types to one another whenever \(w\succ_f a\) and \(f\succ_w b\), sending the displaced masses to \(\bot\); characterize when every unstable \(\lambda\) reaches every stable \(\mu\) in terms of non-trivial positive-mass fragments.

Let \(F,W\) be nonempty finite type sets with strict rankings of counterpart types, all ranked above the unmatched option \(\bot\). Positive binary-rational masses satisfy \(\sum_f\rho_f=\sum_w\sigma_w=1\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|F|+|W|\) positive-mass types and their masses, not the universe of possible types. Put \(m=\max\{|F|,|W|\}\); state running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A state has nonnegative real coordinates \(x_{fw},x_{f\bot},x_{\bot w}\), satisfying \(\sum_w x_{fw}+x_{f\bot}=\rho_f\) and \(\sum_f x_{fw}+x_{\bot w}=\sigma_w\). Input states \(\lambda,\mu\) have binary-rational coordinates, and \(\mu\) is stable.
A move chooses \(f,w,a\in W\cup\{\bot\},b\in F\cup\{\bot\}\) with \(w\succ_f a\), \(f\succ_w b\), and real \(0<q\leq\min\{x_{fa},x_{bw}\}\): subtract \(q\) from both source coordinates, add \(q\) to \(x_{fw}\), and, when \(a\neq\bot\) or \(b\neq\bot\), add \(q\) to \(x_{\bot a}\) or \(x_{b\bot}\), respectively. All other coordinates stay fixed.
A state is stable precisely when no move is available; reachability means exact attainment by finitely many moves, allowing zero moves.
A positive-mass fragment consists of submasses \(0\leq r\leq\rho\), \(0\leq s\leq\sigma\) with \(\sum_f r_f=\sum_w s_w=t\in(0,1)\), admitting a stable internal state \(z\) with these margins and no unmatched mass, such that every \(z_{fw}>0\) satisfies \(w\succ_f v\) for all \(v\) with \(\sigma_v-s_v>0\), and \(f\succ_w u\) for all \(u\) with \(\rho_u-r_u>0\).
To define non-triviality, split each type into insider and outsider portions of masses \(r_f,\rho_f-r_f\) or \(s_w,\sigma_w-s_w\); comparisons use original counterpart types, with no preference between copies of one type. A fragment is non-trivial if two stable states of this split society give different partner-type mass distributions to its insiders.

Is deciding whether \(\lambda\) reaches \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
Is the absence of non-trivial positive-mass fragments necessary and sufficient for every unstable state to reach every stable state?

For finite balanced markets with strict preferences and universal acceptability, universal unstable-to-stable reachability is equivalent to absence of non-trivial fragments, even with best blocking pairs. [Rudov](https://krudov.com/papers/fragile_stable.pdf)
For continuous capacitated allocations, a polynomial path to some stable allocation is known under better-response dynamics; this does not decide reachability of a prescribed stable target or the fragment characterization above. [Cseh–Skutella](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.2169)

2. Prescribed-target reachability is polynomial-time decidable

2.1. Exact continuous-Petri-net encoding

A continuous Petri net has nonnegative real markings on finitely many places. A transition with input and output vectors $A_t,B_t$ can fire by an amount $q>0$ when
$$ qA_t\leq x, $$
and changes the marking to $x+q(B_t-A_t)$.

Create one place for every state coordinate:
$$ P=(F\times W)\cup(F\times\{\bot\})\cup(\{\bot\}\times W). $$

For each quadruple $(f,w,a,b)$ satisfying
$$ w\succ_f a,\qquad f\succ_w b, $$
create a transition consuming one unit from each of $(f,a)$ and $(b,w)$, and producing one unit at $(f,w)$, together with:

  • one unit at $(\bot,a)$ if $a\neq\bot$;
  • one unit at $(b,\bot)$ if $b\neq\bot$.

The two input places are distinct. Equality would force $b=f$ and $a=w$, contradicting the strict improvements. Thus its firing condition is precisely
$$ 0<q\leq\min\{x_{fa},x_{bw}\}. $$
Its effect is exactly the specified move.

Each transition preserves every margin. To see this, the chosen firm $f$ loses and gains $q$; the chosen worker $w$ loses and gains $q$. If $a\neq\bot$, worker $a$ loses matched mass and gains the same unmatched mass. If $b\neq\bot$, firm $b$ does likewise. The improvement conditions ensure $a\neq w$ and $b\neq f$.

Hence markings reached from a valid state remain valid states, and there is an exact equivalence:
$$ \lambda\text{ reaches }\mu \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mu\text{ is finitely reachable from }\lambda \text{ in the constructed continuous Petri net}. $$
This includes the empty sequence.

3. Non-trivial fragments are a necessary obstruction

3.1. A fragment contains whole types

Lemma. Every positive-mass fragment satisfies
$$ r_f\in\{0,\rho_f\},\qquad s_w\in\{0,\sigma_w\}. $$

Proof. Suppose $r_f>0$. Since the internal state $z$ has no unmatched mass, some $w$ has $z_{fw}>0$. If $r_f<\rho_f$, the fragment condition applies to outsider type $u=f$, requiring
$$ f\succ_w f, $$
which contradicts strictness.

Similarly, if $s_w>0$, some $f$ has $z_{fw}>0$. If $s_w<\sigma_w$, the condition with outsider type $v=w$ requires
$$ w\succ_f w, $$
another contradiction. $\square$

Thus a fragment is described by nonempty proper type subsets
$$ I=\{f:r_f>0\},\qquad J=\{w:s_w>0\}, $$
with
$$ \sum_{f\in I}\rho_f=\sum_{w\in J}\sigma_w=t\in(0,1). $$

Every type has exactly one positive-mass portion in the split society. Removing zero-mass portions identifies its states and preferences with those of the original society. Consequently, non-triviality is witnessed by two stable states of the original society that differ on insider partner-type distributions.

3.2. A fragment can freeze an unstable state

Proposition. A non-trivial positive-mass fragment implies failure of universal unstable-to-stable reachability.

Proof. Fix its subsets $I,J$ and inducing internal state $z$. Define $\lambda$ by assigning insiders according to $z$, putting no mass on cross edges, and leaving every outsider unmatched.

This is a valid state. Both outsider sides have total mass $1-t>0$, so an unmatched outsider firm and an unmatched outsider worker can move together. Thus $\lambda$ is unstable.

We prove that all states reachable from $\lambda$ preserve $z$ exactly and have no cross edges. Suppose this holds before a move.

  • If both chosen types are insiders, their positive sources are internal. The move would contradict stability of $z$.
  • If the chosen firm $f$ is an insider and worker $w$ is an outsider, its positive source $z_{fa}$ satisfies $a\succ_f w$. The required improvement $w\succ_f a$ is impossible.
  • If the chosen worker $w$ is an insider and firm $f$ is an outsider, its positive source $z_{bw}$ satisfies $b\succ_w f$. Again the required improvement is impossible.
  • If both chosen types are outsiders, their positive sources and all resulting coordinates involve only outsiders.

These cases prove the invariant by induction over any finite sequence.

Non-triviality supplies two stable states with different insider distributions. At least one differs from the distribution fixed by $z$; choose it as $\mu$. The invariant prevents reaching $\mu$. $\square$

Therefore
$$ \text{universal unstable-to-stable reachability} \ \Longrightarrow\ \text{absence of non-trivial positive-mass fragments}. $$

3.3. Rational witnesses suffice

The obstruction can be witnessed by rational $\lambda,\mu$.

For completeness, fix the support of a real state with rational margins. Its supported coordinates satisfy a consistent rational linear system. Gaussian elimination supplies a rational particular solution and a rational basis of the homogeneous solution space. Approximating the real basis coefficients by rationals gives rational solutions arbitrarily close to the original state.

Because there are finitely many positive coordinates, sufficiently close approximations preserve positivity on the support; coordinates outside it remain zero. They therefore preserve stability.

The fragment margins are rational by the whole-type lemma. Apply this approximation to $z$ and to a stable non-triviality witness differing from $z$. Choose the approximations close enough to preserve a differing coordinate. The fragment inequalities remain valid because its support is unchanged. The construction above then gives rational unstable and stable witnesses.

4. An exact finite-reachability lemma

The following lemma will handle the case with two types on each side, including targets with zero coordinates.

Lemma. Consider a finite continuous Petri net with incidence matrix $C$. Let $x,y$ be markings. Suppose:

  • $y=x+Cv$ for a vector $v$ strictly positive on every transition;
  • $x$ can finitely reach a marking positive on every place;
  • in the reversed net, $y$ can finitely reach a marking positive on every place.

Then $x$ finitely reaches $y$.

Proof. Let the forward sequence in assumption 2 have cumulative firing vector $a$, ending at
$$ h=x+Ca>0. $$
Let the reverse sequence in assumption 3 have cumulative firing vector $b$, ending at
$$ \ell=y-Cb>0. $$

Scaling either entire firing sequence by a factor in $(0,1]$ remains feasible from its original marking. Indeed, at every step the scaled marking is the corresponding convex combination of the original initial marking and the unscaled intermediate marking; the input requirements scale by the same factor.

Choose $\varepsilon,\eta\in(0,1]$ sufficiently small that
$$ v'=v-\varepsilon a-\eta b\geq0. $$
Such choices exist because $v$ is strictly positive and there are finitely many transitions.

The scaled sequences end at strictly positive markings
$$ h_\varepsilon=x+\varepsilon Ca,\qquad \ell_\eta=y-\eta Cb, $$
and
$$ \ell_\eta=h_\varepsilon+Cv'. $$

It remains to realize this last state equation by finitely many firings. Let
$$ d=\min_p\{h_\varepsilon[p],\ell_\eta[p]\}>0. $$
Fix an ordering of the transitions. Choose an integer $N$ so large that, for every place $p$,
$$ \frac1N\sum_t \operatorname{Pre}[p,t]v'[t]<\frac d2. $$

Execute $N$ rounds, firing each transition $t$ by $v'[t]/N$ in every round, omitting zero amounts. At the start of round $j+1$, for $0\leq j<N$, the marking is
$$ h_\varepsilon+\frac jN Cv' = \left(1-\frac jN\right)h_\varepsilon+\frac jN\ell_\eta. $$
Every coordinate is at least $d$. Within that round, total possible consumption at a place is less than $d/2$. Thus before every firing, all its input requirements are available. The rounds are feasible and end exactly at $\ell_\eta$.

Finally, reversing the scaled reverse-net sequence gives a forward sequence from $\ell_\eta$ to $y$. Concatenating these three finite sequences proves the result. $\square$

We also need a support observation.

Support observation. Starting from a marking, repeatedly add a transition’s output places whenever all its input places have already been included. The resulting support closure is attainable by a finite sequence that never makes a previously positive coordinate zero.

To justify this, realize each newly enabled transition by an amount strictly smaller than its enabling degree. Its input coordinates remain positive and its outputs become positive. Every successful enlargement adds a place, so finitely many enlargements suffice.

5. Universal reachability for small supports

Theorem. Every unstable state reaches every stable state if either one side has only one type, or $|F|=|W|=2$. These statements allow real initial and target coordinates.

5.1. One type on one side

Suppose $F=\{f\}$. Every stable state is fully matched, hence necessarily
$$ x_{fw}=\sigma_w $$
for all $w$. It is unique.

For any initial state,
$$ x_{f\bot}=\sum_w x_{\bot w}. $$
Matching $f$ successively with each worker type’s unmatched mass reaches that unique state in finitely many moves. The argument with the sides reversed is identical.

There are no positive-mass fragments: the whole-type lemma would require the sole type’s entire mass, giving $t=1$.

5.2. Two types on each side with a mutually top-ranked pair

Suppose $f,w$ rank each other first. Every stable state must satisfy
$$ x_{fw}=\min\{\rho_f,\sigma_w\}. $$
Otherwise both endpoints have positive mass assigned elsewhere or unmatched, giving a blocking move between them.

From any initial state, this equality can be established by repeatedly choosing positive sources at $f,w$ outside their shared edge. Each move increases $x_{fw}$, and those source coordinates only decrease during this procedure. Choosing the maximum feasible amount exhausts at least one source coordinate, so the procedure is finite.

Once equality holds, one endpoint is entirely assigned to the other. The residual society therefore has only one firm type or only one worker type. Direct moves between unmatched portions finish matching it.

Moreover, fixing $x_{fw}$ determines the entire fully matched two-by-two matrix from the margins. Thus the stable state is unique, and the procedure reaches every stable target.

Uniqueness also rules out non-trivial fragments.

5.3. The remaining preference profile

If there is no mutually top-ranked pair, the firms must have different top choices: otherwise their common favorite worker’s favorite firm would form such a pair.

After relabeling, the preferences are therefore
$$ w_1\succ_{f_1}w_2,\qquad w_2\succ_{f_2}w_1, $$
$$ f_2\succ_{w_1}f_1,\qquad f_1\succ_{w_2}f_2. $$
All counterpart types remain above $\bot$.

Use coordinates
$$ (A,B,C,D,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta) = (x_{11},x_{12},x_{21},x_{22}, x_{1\bot},x_{2\bot},x_{\bot1},x_{\bot2}). $$

The net has four direct matching transitions
$$ E_{11}:\alpha+\gamma\longrightarrow A,\qquad E_{12}:\alpha+\delta\longrightarrow B, $$
$$ E_{21}:\beta+\gamma\longrightarrow C,\qquad E_{22}:\beta+\delta\longrightarrow D, $$
and four exchange transitions
$$ S_1:B+\gamma\longrightarrow A+\delta, $$
$$ S_2:D+\alpha\longrightarrow B+\beta, $$
$$ S_3:A+\beta\longrightarrow C+\alpha, $$
$$ S_4:C+\delta\longrightarrow D+\gamma. $$

These exhaust the admissible moves. At every pair, one endpoint ranks the other last among counterpart types; that endpoint’s only worse source is unmatched. The other endpoint can use either its other partner type or unmatched mass.

Every fully matched allocation is stable: for each pair, the endpoint ranking it last has no worse positive source. Conversely, Section 1 shows that every stable allocation is fully matched. Thus an unstable $\lambda$ has
$$ M=\alpha+\beta=\gamma+\delta>0. $$

We verify all three hypotheses of Section 4’s lemma.

5.4. Forward support becomes positive everywhere

Let $K$ be the forward support closure of $\lambda$. By the support observation, it is realized by a reachable valid state retaining all initially positive coordinates. In particular, $K$ contains a firm unmatched coordinate and a worker unmatched coordinate.

Suppose $\beta\notin K$. Then $\alpha\in K$. Transition $S_2$ forces $D\notin K$, since otherwise it would produce $\beta$. The positive margin of $f_2$ then forces $C\in K$.

Transition $S_4$ forces $\delta\notin K$, since otherwise it would produce $D$. The positive margin of $w_2$ forces $B\in K$, and the existence of a worker unmatched coordinate forces $\gamma\in K$. But $S_1$ then produces $\delta$, a contradiction.

Hence $\beta\in K$. Simultaneously exchanging the indices $1,2$ preserves this preference profile, so the same argument gives $\alpha\in K$.

If $\delta\notin K$, then $\gamma\in K$. Transition $S_1$ forces $B\notin K$, so the positive margin of $w_2$ gives $D\in K$. Transition $S_2$, using $\alpha\in K$, then produces $B$, a contradiction.

If $\gamma\notin K$, then $\delta\in K$. Transition $S_4$ forces $C\notin K$, so the positive margin of $w_1$ gives $A\in K$. Transition $S_3$, using $\beta\in K$, produces $C$, again a contradiction.

All four unmatched places therefore belong to $K$. The direct matching transitions produce all four matched places. Thus $\lambda$ finitely reaches a marking positive everywhere.

5.5. Reverse support becomes positive everywhere

Let $\mu$ be any stable target. It is fully matched.

In the reverse net, the reverse direct matching transitions unmatch positive edges of $\mu$. Every type has positive mass and hence an incident positive edge. Firing sufficiently small amounts on these edges makes all four unmatched coordinates positive while retaining the original positive edges.

With these unmatched coordinates available, the reverse exchange transitions propagate positivity through the cycle
$$ A\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow A. $$
There is at least one initially positive matched coordinate, so all four become positive. Therefore $\mu$ also satisfies the reverse positivity hypothesis.

5.6. A strictly positive state-equation solution

Let $s_i$ denote the incidence vector of $S_i$, and $e_{ij}$ that of $E_{ij}$.

Differences of valid states lie in the linear space $H$ given by
$$ y_A+y_B+y_\alpha=0,\qquad y_C+y_D+y_\beta=0, $$
$$ y_A+y_C+y_\gamma=0,\qquad y_B+y_D+y_\delta=0. $$
These four equations are independent, because each unmatched coordinate occurs in exactly one equation. Hence $\dim H=4$.

Define the matched-mass functional
$$ \ell(y)=y_A+y_B+y_C+y_D. $$
It is nonzero on $H$, since $\ell(e_{11})=1$. Therefore
$$ \dim(H\cap\ker\ell)=3. $$

Every $s_i$ lies in this three-dimensional space, and
$$ s_1+s_2+s_3+s_4=0. $$
Moreover, $s_1,s_2,s_3$ are independent: in a linear relation, the $\delta$ coordinate first forces the coefficient of $s_1$ to vanish, the $D$ coordinate then forces that of $s_2$ to vanish, and the $C$ coordinate forces that of $s_3$ to vanish. Thus the exchange vectors span $H\cap\ker\ell$.

Since $\mu$ is fully matched,
$$ \ell(\mu-\lambda)=M. $$
Assign the positive firing count $M/4$ to each direct matching transition. The remaining difference
$$ r=\mu-\lambda-\frac M4(e_{11}+e_{12}+e_{21}+e_{22}) $$
belongs to $H\cap\ker\ell$. Hence
$$ r=\sum_{i=1}^4 c_i s_i $$
for real coefficients $c_i$.

Choose $R$ sufficiently large that $c_i+R>0$ for every $i$. Because the exchange vectors sum to zero,
$$ r=\sum_{i=1}^4(c_i+R)s_i. $$
Together with the four positive direct matching counts, these give a state-equation solution strictly positive on all eight transitions.

All hypotheses of Section 4’s lemma now hold. Therefore $\lambda$ reaches $\mu$ by a finite sequence, with exact attainment. No positivity assumption on the target’s matched coordinates was used.

5.7. Fragments in this case

A proper whole-type fragment in a two-by-two society must consist of one firm type and one worker type. Its inequalities require those two types to rank each other first. The cyclic profile has no such pair, so it has no fragments.

Combining the cases proves universal reachability, and absence of non-trivial fragments, whenever one side has one type or both sides have two types.
