The continuous mirror question, as graded

Under type-uniform smoothness and moment bounds, a mixing condition on the kernel, and a uniformly positive continuum analogue of global identifiability, does the conditional prediction error admit a uniform-in-type bound with an exponential transient and steady-state order \(O(\delta)+O(\eta)+O(\widetilde{\eta})\), as in Theorem 1 and Corollary 1?

Conclusion

The bound holds uniformly under the stated assumptions, including the fixed constants \(M,\zeta\) in the mixing hypothesis. No lower bound on the masses \(\nu_s\) or stationary weights \(\pi_s\) is needed.

We give explicit constants below. In particular, \(C_1,C_2\) can be chosen proportional to \(1/\delta\), while \(C_3,\ldots,C_6\) are independent of all three adaptation parameters. This is a direct proof of uniformity, rather than an application of the network-dependent constants in [the cited finite-network theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17370v1).

1. Notation and averaging inequalities

Write \(S=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) and define the matrix \(P\) by

\(P_{rs}=\nu_s a(s,r)\).

Then \(P_{rs}\geq0\), \(\sum_sP_{rs}=1\), and \(Tf=Pf\). Every power \(P^n\), including \(P^0=I\), has probability vectors as its rows.

For any square-integrable random vector \(F=(F_s)_{s\in S}\), Jensen’s inequality gives

\(\mathbb E|(P^nF)_r|^2\leq\sum_s(P^n)_{rs}\mathbb E|F_s|^2\leq\sup_s\mathbb E|F_s|^2.\)

This requires no independence across types. Also, \(\|P^nf\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_\infty\) for deterministic \(f\).

Choose uniform bounds

\(\mathbb E\|X'_{s,t}\|^2\leq K_{\rm tr},\qquad \mathbb E\|X_{s,t}\|^2\leq K_{\rm pr},\)

and let \(\sigma_w^2,\sigma_u^2\) bound the conditional second moments of the two gradient noises. Let

\(\|w_{s,0}-w_s^o\|\leq R_w,\qquad \|u_{s,0}-u_s^o\|\leq R_u.\)

All parameter norms are Euclidean norms of the stacked nonpivot blocks. If the given feature bounds are coordinatewise, one may take \(K_{\rm tr},K_{\rm pr}\) to be \(d\) times the respective coordinatewise bounds.

Set \(\rho=1-\delta\). The assumed upper bound on \(\delta\) implies \(0<\rho<1\).

2. Uniform bounds on the optimal decision statistics

For the regularized multiclass logistic loss, the nonpivot part of the gradient of the unregularized loss is

\((p_i(w,x)-\mathbf1_{\{y=\theta_i\}})_{i<m}\otimes x.\)

The full probability residual has squared norm at most \(2\): indeed,

\(\|p-e_y\|^2=\|p\|^2+1-2p_y\leq2.\)

Removing the pivot coordinate cannot increase its norm. The quadratic penalty has zero gradient at the origin, so

\(\|\nabla J_s(0)\|\leq\sqrt{2K_{\rm tr}},\qquad \|\nabla\widetilde J_s(0)\|\leq\sqrt2.\)

Differentiation under expectation is justified here by the integrable gradient bound \(\sqrt2\|X'_s\|\), together with the deterministic penalty gradient.

Strong convexity and \(\nabla J_s(w_s^o)=0\) imply

\(h\|w_s^o\|^2\leq-\langle\nabla J_s(0),w_s^o\rangle\leq\|\nabla J_s(0)\|\|w_s^o\|.\)

Consequently, with

\(W=\sqrt{2K_{\rm tr}}/h,\qquad U=\sqrt2/h,\)

we have \(\|w_s^o\|\leq W\) and \(\|u_s^o\|\leq U\), uniformly in \(s\).

Fix a false hypothesis \(\theta\neq\theta_0\). For any stacked parameter \(v\), with pivot block zero,

\(\|v(\theta_0)-v(\theta)\|^2\leq2\|v\|^2.\)

Thus, defining the oracle evidence

\(Z^o_{s,t}=\alpha^{\rm pr}_{s,t}\bigl[d_s^o(X_{s,t};\theta_0)-d_s^o(X_{s,t};\theta)\bigr],\)

we obtain

\(\mathbb E|Z^o_{s,t}|^2\leq4(K_{\rm pr}W^2+U^2)=:D^2.\)

Here we used \(\alpha^{\rm pr}_{s,t}\leq1\) and \((a-b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2\).

Let \(v_s=\mathbb EZ^o_{s,t}\) and \(G_\theta=\sum_s\pi_sv_s\). Independence of the prediction indicator and data gives exactly the quantity appearing in the gap assumption. Therefore

\(G_\theta\geq g,\qquad \|v\|_\infty\leq D,\qquad |G_\theta|\leq D.\)

3. Uniform SGD estimates

Define

\(\varphi_s=1-2\eta q_s^{\rm tr}h+\eta^2q_s^{\rm tr}b^2.\)

The step-size assumption implies \(\eta<h/b^2\). It also implies

\(\varphi_s-\rho=q_s^{\rm tr}b^2\eta^2-2q_s^{\rm tr}h\eta+\delta>0,\)

because \(\eta\) lies below the smaller root of this quadratic. Hence

\(\rho<\varphi_s<1,\qquad \rho<\varphi<1.\)

The same conclusions hold for \(\widetilde\varphi_s,\widetilde\varphi\).

Put \(e_{s,t}=w_{s,t}-w_s^o\). The Hessian bounds imply

\(\langle e,\nabla J_s(w_s^o+e)\rangle\geq h\|e\|^2,\qquad \|\nabla J_s(w_s^o+e)\|\leq b\|e\|.\)

Conditioning on the past, using unbiased gradient noise and independence of the sampling indicator, therefore gives

\(\mathbb E[\|e_{s,t}\|^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\leq\varphi_s\|e_{s,t-1}\|^2+\eta^2q_s^{\rm tr}\sigma_w^2.\)

Since

\(1-\varphi=\min_s q_s^{\rm tr}(2\eta h-\eta^2b^2)\geq\eta q_*h,\)

iteration yields

\(\mathbb E\|w_{s,t}-w_s^o\|^2\leq R_w^2\varphi^t+V_w\eta,\qquad V_w=\sigma_w^2/(q_*h).\)

Identically,

\(\mathbb E\|u_{s,t}-u_s^o\|^2\leq R_u^2\widetilde\varphi^t+V_u\widetilde\eta,\qquad V_u=\sigma_u^2/(q_*h).\)

These estimates are uniform over the support and network.

4. Exact log-belief recursion

For the fixed false hypothesis, define

\(\beta_{r,t}=\log\bigl(\mu_{r,t}(\theta_0)/\mu_{r,t}(\theta)\bigr)\)

and

\(Z_{s,t}=\alpha^{\rm pr}_{s,t}\bigl[d_{s,t}(X_{s,t};\theta_0)-d_{s,t}(X_{s,t};\theta)\bigr].\)

Taking ratios cancels both normalization constants in the belief updates. Consequently,

\(\beta_t=\rho P\beta_{t-1}+PZ_t,\)

and iteration gives

\(\beta_t=\rho^tP^t\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^t\rho^{t-j}P^{t-j+1}Z_j.\)

For each \(r\), decompose

\(\delta\beta_{r,t}-G_\theta=I_{r,t}+B_{r,t}+N_{r,t}+A_{r,t},\)

where

\(I_{r,t}=\rho^t\bigl[\delta(P^t\beta_0)_r-G_\theta\bigr],\)

\(B_{r,t}=\delta\sum_{j=1}^t\rho^{t-j}\bigl[(P^{t-j+1}v)_r-G_\theta\bigr],\)

\(N_{r,t}=\delta\sum_{j=1}^t\rho^{t-j}(P^{t-j+1}(Z_j^o-v))_r,\)

\(A_{r,t}=\delta\sum_{j=1}^t\rho^{t-j}(P^{t-j+1}(Z_j-Z_j^o))_r.\)

This identity uses \(\delta\sum_{j=1}^t\rho^{t-j}=1-\rho^t\).

5. Bounds on the four terms

Initial-state term

The initial-belief assumption gives

\(\|\beta_0\|_\infty\leq B_0,\qquad B_0=\log(1/\epsilon).\)

Therefore

\(|I_{r,t}|\leq(B_0+D)\rho^t.\)

Mixing bias

Apply the assumed mixing inequality to the deterministic vector \(v\). Setting

\(H=M\zeta/(1-\zeta),\)

we obtain

\(|B_{r,t}|\leq\delta MD\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^k\zeta^{k+1}\leq\delta DH.\)

This is where the fixed, support-independent mixing constants enter.

Oracle fluctuations

The oracle evidence vectors \(Z_j^o\) are independent over time. Thus the centered summands defining \(N_{r,t}\) have zero cross moments at distinct times.

By the averaging inequality from Section 1,

\(\mathbb E|(P^{k+1}(Z_j^o-v))_r|^2\leq\sup_s\operatorname{Var}(Z^o_{s,j})\leq D^2.\)

Consequently,

\(\mathbb E|N_{r,t}|^2\leq D^2\delta^2\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\rho^{2k}\leq D^2\delta/(2-\delta)\leq D^2\delta.\)

Contemporaneous dependence across types causes no difficulty.

Model-learning error

Training and prediction independence, the contrast inequality from Section 2, and the SGD estimates imply

\(\|Z_{s,j}-Z^o_{s,j}\|_{L^2}\leq\sqrt{2K_{\rm pr}}\bigl(R_w\varphi^{j/2}+\sqrt{V_w\eta}\bigr)+\sqrt2\bigl(R_u\widetilde\varphi^{j/2}+\sqrt{V_u\widetilde\eta}\bigr).\)

For example, the feature-dependent part is bounded using

\(\mathbb E|X_{s,j}^{\top}(e_{s,j}(\theta_0)-e_{s,j}(\theta))|^2\leq2K_{\rm pr}\mathbb E\|e_{s,j}\|^2.\)

For any \(a\in(\rho,1)\),

\(\delta\sum_{j=1}^t\rho^{t-j}a^{j/2}\leq\frac{\delta a^{t/2}}{1-\rho/\sqrt a}\leq\frac{\delta a^{t/2}}{1-\sqrt\rho}=(1+\sqrt\rho)a^{t/2}\leq2a^{t/2}.\)

Importantly, this bound does not involve \(1/(a-\rho)\).

Using the averaging inequality, the \(L^2\) triangle inequality, and \(\delta\sum_{j=1}^t\rho^{t-j}\leq1\), we conclude

\(\|A_{r,t}\|_{L^2}\leq2\sqrt{2K_{\rm pr}}R_w\varphi^{t/2}+2\sqrt2R_u\widetilde\varphi^{t/2}+\sqrt{2K_{\rm pr}V_w\eta}+\sqrt{2V_u\widetilde\eta}.\)

Squaring and applying \((a_1+a_2+a_3+a_4)^2\leq4\sum_i a_i^2\) gives

\(\mathbb E|A_{r,t}|^2\leq32K_{\rm pr}R_w^2\varphi^t+32R_u^2\widetilde\varphi^t+8K_{\rm pr}V_w\eta+8V_u\widetilde\eta.\)

No independence between successive model-learning errors was used.

6. Error probability and explicit uniform constants

Applying the same four-term square inequality to the decomposition in Section 4, and using \(\delta^2\leq\delta\), gives

\(\mathbb E|\delta\beta_{r,t}-G_\theta|^2\leq128K_{\rm pr}R_w^2\varphi^t+128R_u^2\widetilde\varphi^t+4(B_0+D)^2\rho^{2t}+4D^2(1+H^2)\delta+32K_{\rm pr}V_w\eta+32V_u\widetilde\eta.\)

If the false hypothesis \(\theta\) ties or exceeds the true hypothesis, then \(\beta_{r,t}\leq0\). Since \(G_\theta\geq g>0\), this event implies

\(|\delta\beta_{r,t}-G_\theta|\geq g.\)

Markov’s inequality applied to the squared deviation therefore bounds its probability by the preceding second-moment estimate divided by \(g^2\). Taking the union over the \(m-1\) false hypotheses proves the requested inequality, uniformly in \(r\), with

\(C_1=\dfrac{128(m-1)K_{\rm pr}R_w^2}{g^2\delta},\qquad C_2=\dfrac{128(m-1)R_u^2}{g^2\delta},\)

\(C_3=\dfrac{4(m-1)(B_0+D)^2}{g^2},\qquad C_4=\dfrac{4(m-1)D^2(1+H^2)}{g^2},\)

\(C_5=\dfrac{32(m-1)K_{\rm pr}V_w}{g^2},\qquad C_6=\dfrac{32(m-1)V_u}{g^2}.\)

Thus, for every \(t\geq0\),

\(\sup_r p_{r,t}\leq\delta(C_1\varphi^t+C_2\widetilde\varphi^t)+C_3(1-\delta)^{2t}+C_4\delta+C_5\eta+C_6\widetilde\eta.\)

These constants contain no dependence on \(\tau\), the masses, a minimum stationary weight, or the rational-input bit length \(L\). The coefficients \(C_3,\ldots,C_6\) are independent of the adaptation parameters, and the dependence of \(C_1,C_2\) on \(1/\delta\) is expressly permitted by the question.
