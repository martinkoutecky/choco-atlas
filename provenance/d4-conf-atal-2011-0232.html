5. Succinct evaluation and exact comparison upper bounds.

Define $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ as follows: given a variable-free integer arithmetic circuit using constants $0,1$ and operations $+,-,\times$, decide whether its output is positive.

A rational quantity can be maintained as a pair of integer circuits
$$ \frac{N}{D}, \qquad D>0. $$
For example,
$$ \frac{N_1}{D_1}+\frac{N_2}{D_2} = \frac{N_1D_2+N_2D_1}{D_1D_2}, $$
and
$$ \frac{N_1}{D_1}-\frac{N_2}{D_2} = \frac{N_1D_2-N_2D_1}{D_1D_2}. $$
Multiplication similarly adds only constantly many integer gates.

The only divisions needed by the cohort algorithm are by strictly positive tied-group masses. If $N_2/D_2>0$, then
$$ \frac{N_1/D_1}{N_2/D_2} = \frac{N_1D_2}{D_1N_2}, $$
whose denominator is positive.

Input integers written in binary can be constructed from $0,1$ using a number of gates linear in their bit length, by repeated doubling and addition. Thus the polynomial arithmetic-operation algorithm produces polynomial-size shared circuits for all rational quantities.

For two represented rationals,
$$ \frac{N_1}{D_1}>\frac{N_2}{D_2} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N_1D_2-N_2D_1>0. $$
This is one $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ query. Equality can be decided by testing both this integer and its negative for positivity.

Consequently, active-mass comparisons, cumulative-demand comparisons, zero-mass tests, and cutoff choices can all be performed using polynomially many adaptive $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ queries. Infinite cutoffs are handled as a separate symbol. Price comparisons otherwise involve input marginal values or zero.

It follows that polynomial-size circuit descriptions of the exact outputs can be constructed in
$$ \mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{PosSLP}}. $$
The construction remains causal because each circuit and oracle query uses only quantities available at that slot.

For a precise decision formulation, consider either of the following tasks:

  • decide whether a specified cutoff exceeds a supplied rational threshold;
  • decide whether a specified cohort mass, tagged outcome probability, or aggregate payment exceeds a supplied rational threshold.

The coordinate indices and threshold are part of the input. Constructing the relevant circuits and performing the final comparison places each task in
$$ \mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{PosSLP}}. $$

A known theorem of Allender, Bürgisser, Kjeldgaard-Pedersen, and Miltersen states that $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ belongs to the counting hierarchy. Its hypotheses apply here: every oracle query is a variable-free, division-free integer circuit using $0,1,+,-,\times$. [On the Complexity of Numerical Analysis](https://www3.math.tu-berlin.de/algebra/work/focs7.pdf)

For completeness, let
$$ \mathcal C_0=\mathrm P, \qquad \mathcal C_{j+1}=\mathrm{PP}^{\mathcal C_j}, \qquad \mathrm{CH}=\bigcup_{j\geq0}\mathcal C_j. $$
The cited theorem places $\mathrm{PosSLP}$ in some fixed level $\mathcal C_j$. A deterministic polynomial-time oracle computation is a special probabilistic computation, so
$$ \mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{PosSLP}} \subseteq \mathrm{P}^{\mathcal C_j} \subseteq \mathcal C_{j+1} \subseteq \mathrm{CH}. $$

Each fixed level of this hierarchy lies in $\mathrm{PSPACE}$. Inductively, enumerate the polynomially many random bits of a probabilistic oracle computation, count its accepting branches with a polynomial-bit counter, and answer each oracle query using the preceding level's polynomial-space simulation. The fixed nesting depth preserves polynomial space.

Thus the stated comparison problems lie in $\mathrm{CH}$ and in $\mathrm{PSPACE}$. These are upper bounds, not hardness reductions.

6. Explicit bit complexity and a tractable special case.

$$ 0<\alpha_t<1. $$

$$ \log_2 D_0\leq L. $$

Suppose current cohort masses have common denominator $D$, and write a splitting slot's quantities as
$$ s_t=\frac{u_t}{w_t}, \qquad B_{>}=\frac{a_t}{D}, \qquad B_{=}=\frac{h_t}{D}. $$

$$ \alpha_t = \frac{u_tD-w_ta_t}{w_th_t}. $$

$$ D'=Dw_th_t. $$

$$ \log_2 D' \leq 2\log_2 D+\log_2 w_t \leq 2\log_2 D+L. $$

After $b$ splitting slots, the recurrence therefore gives
$$ \log_2 D=O(2^bL). $$
