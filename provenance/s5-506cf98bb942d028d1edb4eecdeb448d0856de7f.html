The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a single half-hour, the input is rational type masses \(\mu_t\), explicit contract menus \(J_t\), each offer's bid \(b_j\) and bundle size \(\ell_j\), rational per-capita demand \(D\), and nonnegative spot price \(m\). Choose accepted offer mass \(x_{tj}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{j\in J_t}x_{tj}\le\mu_t\), so each EV receives at most one whole contract. Let \(q=\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}\ell_j\); maximize \(\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}(m-b_j)\ell_j-m\max\{0,q-D\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

For one half-hour, a society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) EV types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
Each type has an explicit contract menu \(J_t\). An EV offers one indivisible energy bundle, and distinct offers for that bundle must concern distinct half-hours; thus \(|J_t|\leq1\) in this single-period problem. Each offer specifies binary-rational bundle size \(\ell_j\geq0\) and bid \(b_j\geq0\) per unit of energy.
The remaining inputs are binary-rational per-capita demand \(D\geq0\), spot price \(s\geq0\), and savings threshold \(K\in\mathbb{Q}\). Let \(m=\sum_t|J_t|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Choose real accepted masses \(x_{tj}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_{j\in J_t}x_{tj}\leq\mu_t\). Mass may be divided among acceptance and rejection, but every accepted EV exports its whole bundle. Set \(q=\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}\ell_j\) and \(F(x)=\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}(s-b_j)\ell_j-s\max\{0,q-D\}\); excess supply is permitted.

Is deciding whether some feasible allocation satisfies \(F(x)\geq K\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Finite-agent optimal contract selection is reported NP-hard, already for one half-hour, and has a demand-indexed dynamic program for bounded integer bundle sizes. [Shamash and Fan (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07308v1) establish no complexity result for the distribution-over-types version defined here.

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). An optimal allocation can be computed exactly by sorting offers by increasing bid and accepting mass until demand is met, using only offers whose bids are below the spot price.

The proof uses the stated permission to divide each type’s mass between acceptance and rejection. Every accepted EV still exports its whole bundle.

1. Expressing the problem in energy variables

Because \(|J_t|\leq1\), enumerate the offers by \(i=1,\ldots,m\), and write \(\mu_i\) for the mass of the type owning offer \(i\). The constraints are independently

\(0\leq x_i\leq\mu_i.\)

An offer with \(\ell_i=0\) contributes neither energy nor savings, so its accepted mass may be set to zero. For every remaining offer define

\(y_i=\ell_i x_i,\qquad c_i=\mu_i\ell_i.\)

Here \(y_i\) is the aggregate accepted energy and \(c_i\) its upper bound. Since \(\ell_i>0\), the transformation is reversible:

\(0\leq y_i\leq c_i \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_i=y_i/\ell_i\in[0,\mu_i].\)

Thus every feasible energy vector corresponds to an allocation permitted by the question.

Writing \(q=\sum_i y_i\), the objective becomes

\(F(y)=sq-\sum_i b_i y_i-s\max\{0,q-D\}=s\min\{q,D\}-\sum_i b_i y_i.\)

The last equality follows by considering \(q\leq D\) and \(q>D\).

2. Excess supply can be removed without loss

Lemma. Every feasible allocation with \(q>D\) is dominated by a feasible allocation supplying exactly \(D\).

Proof. Let \(\alpha=D/q\), and set \(y_i'=\alpha y_i\). Since \(0\leq\alpha<1\), the box constraints remain satisfied, and \(\sum_i y_i'=D\).

Let \(B=\sum_i b_i y_i\). The assumptions \(b_i,y_i\geq0\) give \(B\geq0\). Therefore

\(F(y')-F(y)=(sD-\alpha B)-(sD-B)=(1-\alpha)B\geq0.\)

This also covers \(D=0\). \(\square\)

Consequently, imposing \(\sum_i y_i\leq D\) does not change the optimal value. On this restricted feasible set,

\(F(y)=\sum_i(s-b_i)y_i.\)

If \(b_i\geq s\), setting \(y_i=0\) preserves feasibility and cannot decrease this objective. Hence such offers may also be discarded.

Let \(r\leq m\) be the number of remaining offers. They satisfy \(c_i>0\) and \(v_i:=s-b_i>0\). The original problem now has exactly the same optimal value as

\(\displaystyle \max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_i y_i:\ 0\leq y_i\leq c_i,\ \sum_{i=1}^{r}y_i\leq D\right\}.\)

3. Greedy construction and proof of optimality

Sort the remaining offers so that

\(b_1\leq b_2\leq\cdots\leq b_r,\)

equivalently \(v_1\geq v_2\geq\cdots\geq v_r>0\). Ties may be ordered arbitrarily.

Define \(C_0=0\) and \(C_i=\sum_{h=1}^{i}c_h\). Choose

\(\displaystyle y_i^*=\min\{c_i,\max\{0,D-C_{i-1}\}\}.\)

This accepts each offer up to its capacity, in order, until demand is met. Its total energy is \(\min\{D,C_r\}\), so it is feasible.

If \(r=0\) or \(D=0\), the reduced optimum is zero. Assume henceforth that \(r>0\) and \(D>0\).

Case 1: \(D\geq C_r\). The construction gives \(y_i^*=c_i\) for every \(i\). For any feasible \(y\), positivity of \(v_i\) and the bounds \(y_i\leq c_i\) imply

\(\displaystyle \sum_i v_i y_i\leq\sum_i v_i c_i=\sum_i v_i y_i^*.\)

Thus \(y^*\) is optimal.

Case 2: \(0<D<C_r\). Let \(p\) be the smallest index with \(C_p\geq D\). Then

\(y_i^*=c_i\text{ for }i<p,\qquad y_p^*=D-C_{p-1},\qquad y_i^*=0\text{ for }i>p,\)

and \(\sum_i y_i^*=D\).

Set \(\lambda=v_p>0\). For any feasible \(y\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_i v_i(y_i-y_i^*)=\sum_i(v_i-\lambda)(y_i-y_i^*)+\lambda\left(\sum_i y_i-D\right).\)

Every term on the right is nonpositive:

  • For \(i<p\), \(v_i-\lambda\geq0\) and \(y_i-y_i^*=y_i-c_i\leq0\).
  • For \(i=p\), \(v_i-\lambda=0\).
  • For \(i>p\), \(v_i-\lambda\leq0\) and \(y_i-y_i^*=y_i\geq0\).
  • Finally, \(\sum_i y_i\leq D\).

Therefore \(\sum_i v_i y_i\leq\sum_i v_i y_i^*\), proving optimality. This argument also handles tied bids and the boundary \(D=C_p\).

The original optimal allocation is obtained by setting \(x_i^*=y_i^*/\ell_i\) for retained offers and setting all discarded offers’ accepted masses to zero. At most one type has accepted mass strictly between zero and its full mass.

4. Exact arithmetic and polynomial bit complexity

The optimal value is the rational number

\(\displaystyle V^*=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(s-b_i)\min\{c_i,\max\{0,D-C_{i-1}\}\}.\)

The decision algorithm returns yes exactly when \(V^*\geq K\).

It remains to check that exact rational arithmetic has polynomial cost. Let \(Q\) be the product of the positive denominators in the input representations, including the denominator of \(K\). Their total encoded length is at most \(L\), so \(Q\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Every input rational can be represented with denominator \(Q\) and an \(O(L)\)-bit numerator.

It follows that:

  • Capacities \(c_i=\mu_i\ell_i\) have denominator \(Q^2\).
  • Prefix sums \(C_i\), demand differences, and accepted energies \(y_i^*\) can all use denominator \(Q^2\).
  • Margins \(s-b_i\) can use denominator \(Q\).
  • Objective terms, their sum \(V^*\), and the final comparison with \(K\) can use denominator \(Q^3\).

Products involve only a fixed number of input-scale factors. Summing at most \(m\) terms increases numerator length by at most \(O(\log(m+1))\). Thus all these integers have \(O(L+\log(m+1))\) bits. Recovering \(x_i^*=y_i^*/\ell_i\) likewise produces rational numbers of polynomial bit length.

Reading the support takes \(O(\tau+m)\) operations, sorting takes \(O(m\log(m+1))\) rational comparisons, and the subsequent scan takes \(O(m)\) arithmetic operations. Integer addition, multiplication, and comparison on the stated bit lengths take polynomial time. Hence the entire algorithm runs in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), without a demand-indexed state space.

This includes empty menus, zero bundle sizes, zero demand, zero spot price, and arbitrary rational \(K\).

5. Relation to finite-agent contract selection

The cited paper formulates finite-agent allocation as choosing a subset of offered contracts and reports NP-hardness in Proposition 1. [Shamash and Fan (2025), Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07308v1#S3).

Here, accepting an arbitrary fraction of a type’s mass makes every aggregate energy \(y_i\in[0,c_i]\) attainable. That permission justifies both the excess-supply scaling and the possible partial acceptance at the demand boundary. The proof therefore establishes polynomial-time solvability for the distribution-over-types problem as defined.
