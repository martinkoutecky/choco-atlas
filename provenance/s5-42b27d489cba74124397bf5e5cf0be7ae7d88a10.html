The continuous mirror question, as graded

For \(C\) with \(|C|=4\), let \(T\) be its 24 strict rankings and let \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\) be a society distribution. Can a synthesis procedure produce a margin-invariant rule \(F:\Delta(T)\to 2^C\setminus\{\emptyset\}\) that selects the Condorcet winner uniquely, excludes every Condorcet loser, is neutral, and satisfies strict positive involvement: whenever \(x\in F(\mu)\), \(F((\mu+\lambda\nu)/(1+\lambda))=\{x\}\) for every \(\lambda>0\) and every distribution \(\nu\) supported on rankings with \(x\) uniquely first?

Fix a candidate set \(C\) with \(m=|C|=4\), and let \(T\) be its \(24\) strict rankings. Let \(\Delta(T)=\{\mu\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{T}:\sum_{t\in T}\mu(t)=1\}\).
For computational inputs, a society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and any other input parameters.
Define \(M_\mu(x,y)=\sum_{t:x\succ_t y}\mu(t)-\sum_{t:y\succ_t x}\mu(t)\). A rule \(F:\Delta(T)\to2^C\setminus\{\emptyset\}\) is margin-invariant if equal margin matrices give equal winning sets.
Require neutrality, unique selection of every Condorcet winner, and exclusion of every Condorcet loser, with both Condorcet notions using strict positive margins. Winning sets may contain ties.
Require strict positive involvement: for every \(\mu,\nu\in\Delta(T)\), every real \(\lambda>0\), and every \(x\in F(\mu)\), if every ranking in the support of \(\nu\) places \(x\) first, then \(F((\mu+\lambda\nu)/(1+\lambda))=\{x\}\).

The verified construction also settles the distribution version affirmatively; this is an inference from its real-margin verification and homogeneous defining inequalities. Answer

Such a rule exists. The cited construction extends to all real-valued societies because its verification covers real margins and its defining inequalities are homogeneous. The normalization in the question therefore preserves its strict positive involvement property.

The proof below uses that verified construction as a known result and explicitly checks the passage to probability distributions.

1. The verified construction used

Let $\mathcal S_4$ be the space of real skew-symmetric matrices indexed by $C$, with zero diagonal. Identify it with $\mathbb R^6$.

The real-margin verification underlying Lemma 12 and Theorem 13 of the cited paper supplies a map
$$ H:\mathcal S_4\longrightarrow 2^C\setminus\{\emptyset\} $$
with these properties:

  • $H$ is neutral and satisfies the strict Condorcet winner and loser criteria.
  • If $B$ is the margin matrix of a nonzero, nonnegative real-weight profile whose supported rankings all put $x$ first, then

$$ x\in H(A)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H(A+B)=\{x\} $$
for every $A\in\mathcal S_4$.

  • $H$ selects the leximin-maximal candidates among those passing fixed exclusion predicates. Those predicates are finite Boolean combinations of homogeneous linear inequalities.

These are properties of the real-margin verification, rather than merely an extension inferred from finite electorates. See the paper’s logical specification and verification preceding Theorem 13. [Holliday, *Voting Method Synthesis on an Infinite Domain*](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08897v1)

Here leximin compares each candidate’s vector of margins against the other candidates, sorted in ascending order, lexicographically.

2. Positive homogeneity

We first prove the property needed for normalization:
$$ H(rA)=H(A)\qquad(A\in\mathcal S_4,\ r>0). $$

Every atomic test in an exclusion predicate compares a homogeneous linear form $\ell(A)$ with zero. Since
$$ \ell(rA)=r\ell(A), $$
multiplication by $r>0$ preserves its sign and whether it equals zero. Thus every exclusion predicate has the same truth value at $A$ and $rA$, so the eligible candidates are identical.

For each candidate $x$, let $v_x(A)$ be its ascending vector of margins. Positive scaling preserves their order, giving
$$ v_x(rA)=r\,v_x(A). $$
Lexicographic comparisons are also preserved: equality of coordinates remains equality, and the first unequal coordinates retain their strict order. Hence the leximin-maximal eligible candidates remain identical. This proves positive homogeneity.

3. Definition and verification on distributions

Define
$$ F(\mu):=H(M_\mu). $$

This is defined for every $\mu\in\Delta(T)$, including societies with irrational masses, because $M_\mu\in\mathcal S_4$.

Nonemptiness and margin invariance. Nonemptiness follows from the codomain of $H$. If $M_\mu=M_{\mu'}$, then
$$ F(\mu)=H(M_\mu)=H(M_{\mu'})=F(\mu'). $$

Neutrality. For a permutation $\pi$ of $C$, define the relabeled society by
$$ (\pi\mu)(\pi t)=\mu(t). $$
Directly from the margin definition,
$$ M_{\pi\mu}(\pi x,\pi y)=M_\mu(x,y). $$
Neutrality of $H$ therefore gives
$$ F(\pi\mu)=\pi(F(\mu)). $$

Condorcet criteria. If $M_\mu(x,y)>0$ for every $y\ne x$, the Condorcet winner property of $H$ gives $F(\mu)=\{x\}$. If $M_\mu(x,y)<0$ for every $y\ne x$, its Condorcet loser property gives $x\notin F(\mu)$. Both statements use precisely the strict margins stipulated in the question.

Strict positive involvement. Fix arbitrary $\mu,\nu\in\Delta(T)$, $\lambda>0$, and $x\in F(\mu)$. Assume every supported ranking of $\nu$ puts $x$ first.

The real-weight profile
$$ w(t):=\lambda\nu(t) $$
has total weight $\lambda>0$, and all its supported rankings put $x$ first. Its margin matrix is
$$ B=\lambda M_\nu. $$
Consequently, the verified additive property applies:
$$ H(M_\mu+\lambda M_\nu)=\{x\}. $$

Linearity of margins gives
$$ M_{(\mu+\lambda\nu)/(1+\lambda)} = \frac{M_\mu+\lambda M_\nu}{1+\lambda}. $$
Since $1/(1+\lambda)>0$, positive homogeneity yields
$$ \begin{aligned} F\left(\frac{\mu+\lambda\nu}{1+\lambda}\right) &=H\left(\frac{M_\mu+\lambda M_\nu}{1+\lambda}\right)\\ &=H(M_\mu+\lambda M_\nu)\\ &=\{x\}. \end{aligned} $$
This verifies the required quantifiers over arbitrary real masses and arbitrary real $\lambda>0$.

4. Computation from rational support

For rational inputs, this rule is polynomial-time computable. The exclusion predicates are fixed parts of the rule.

Write the $\tau$ positive masses as $p_t/q_t$, where $q_t>0$, and set
$$ Q:=\prod_{t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}q_t, \qquad n_t:=p_t(Q/q_t). $$
These are integers, and
$$ \sum_t n_t=Q. $$
The total input length $L$ bounds the bit length of $Q$ by $O(L)$. Thus each $n_t$ also has $O(L)$ bits.

Compute the integer matrix
$$ A(x,y):=\sum_t n_t \begin{cases} 1,&x\succ_t y,\\ -1,&y\succ_t x. \end{cases} $$
Then $A=QM_\mu$, and homogeneity shows that evaluating $H(A)$ returns $F(\mu)$ exactly.

Using ordinary quadratic-time integer arithmetic, forming the common denominator and integer weights costs $O(\tau L^2)$ bit operations. Accumulating margins costs $O(m^2\tau L)$; the fixed predicates cost $O(L^2)$; sorting and comparing margin vectors costs $O(m^2\log m\,L)$.

A convenient combined bound, for the stipulated $m=4$, is
$$ O\!\left((m^2\tau+m^2\log m+1)L^2\right) = O\!\left((\tau+1)L^2\right). $$
Only the $\tau$ supported rankings are processed.
