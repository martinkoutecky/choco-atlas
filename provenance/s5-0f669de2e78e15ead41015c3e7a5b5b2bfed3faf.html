The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational finite-state data \(p^0\), \(P\), and \(\delta\), role masses \(\mu_q\), coalition masses \(0\leq\lambda_q\leq\mu_q\), per-member reward vectors \(K_q\), and risk coefficients \(b_q\), where \(P\) and \(K_q\) are induced by a supplied stationary policy, compute the exact mean and variance of \(Z_\lambda=\sum_q\lambda_q\xi_q\) and the value \(\max_{q:\lambda_q>0}\bigl(E[Z_\lambda]+b_q\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Z_\lambda)}\bigr)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_q\), each a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Assume \(\sum_q\mu_q=1\).
There are \(m\geq1\) states. Input consists of a rational initial probability vector \(p^0\), a rational row-stochastic matrix \(P\), a rational discount \(0<\delta<1\), reward vectors \(K_q\in\mathbb Q^m\), risk coefficients \(b_q\in\mathbb Q\), and rational coalition masses \(0\leq\lambda_q\leq\mu_q\) with \(\sum_q\lambda_q>0\). Rewards and risk coefficients may have either sign.
The matrix and rewards are the explicitly supplied data induced by a fixed deterministic stationary policy. All members share the same Markov trajectory \((S_t)_{t\geq0}\), with initial law \(p^0\) and transition matrix \(P\); define \(\xi_q=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\delta^tK_q(S_t)\) and \(Z_\lambda=\sum_q\lambda_q\xi_q\).
Evaluate exactly \(M=\mathbb E[Z_\lambda]\), \(V=\operatorname{Var}(Z_\lambda)\), and \(U=\max_{q:\lambda_q>0}(M+b_q\sqrt V)\). Exact values may use binary rational numbers and symbolic nonnegative square roots. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Is this exact evaluation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite lists of players, explicit formulas for fixed-policy coalition expectations, variances, and pairwise covariances are known ([Parilina–Akimochkin](https://doi.org/10.3390/math9030230)); this is a formula result, without a stated complexity classification.
Formulas for variance and higher moments of discounted rewards in finite Markov decision processes are also known ([Sobel](https://doi.org/10.2307/3213832)). No result explicitly treating the distribution-over-types evaluation problem above was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized society masses to sum to one, as a distribution over types; coalition masses remain unnormalized in the aggregate payoff.
  • Defined all type payoffs on one shared Markov trajectory, preserving the dependence between players' payoffs in the discrete model.
  • Required positive total coalition mass because the maximum over coalition members is undefined for an empty coalition.
  • Specified a deterministic stationary policy and treated its induced transition matrix and state rewards as explicitly supplied evaluation data.
  • Carried over a strictly positive discount below one and unrestricted signed rewards and risk coefficients; made all numerical inputs binary rational.
  • Allowed symbolic nonnegative square roots for exact values because the utility need not be rational.

Conclusion

The exact evaluation problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Both \(M\) and \(V\) are rational, and \(U\) has the permitted exact representation

\( \displaystyle U=M+b_*\sqrt V,\qquad b_*=\max_{q:\lambda_q>0}b_q. \)

The proof below includes polynomial bounds on arithmetic bit lengths, so the conclusion concerns binary computation rather than unit-cost rational arithmetic.

1. Aggregate the rewards along the common trajectory

Treat reward vectors as columns, and define

\( \displaystyle g=\sum_{q=1}^{\tau}\lambda_qK_q\in\mathbb Q^m. \)

Let \(C=\max_{q,i}|K_q(i)|\). Since \(0\leq\lambda_q\leq\mu_q\) and \(\sum_q\mu_q=1\), we have \(\sum_q\lambda_q\leq1\), hence \(\|g\|_\infty\leq C\).

Each discounted return converges absolutely, because

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\delta^t|K_q(S_t)|\leq\frac{C}{1-\delta}. \)

The sum over types is finite. Consequently, on every trajectory,

\( \displaystyle Z_\lambda=\sum_{q=1}^{\tau}\lambda_q\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\delta^tK_q(S_t)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\delta^tg(S_t). \)

Thus the coalition return is exactly the discounted return for the single reward vector \(g\). This identity accounts for all correlations between types arising from their shared trajectory.

Moreover,

\( \displaystyle |Z_\lambda|\leq\frac{C}{1-\delta}, \)

so its first and second moments are finite.

2. Compute the first and second moments

For each state \(i\), let \(\mathbb E_i\) denote expectation for the chain started deterministically at \(i\). Define

\( \displaystyle v_i=\mathbb E_i\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\delta^tg(S_t)\right],\qquad w_i=\mathbb E_i\!\left[\left(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\delta^tg(S_t)\right)^2\right]. \)

These definitions apply even when \(p_i^0=0\).

First moment

Write the return as \(g(i)+\delta Y\), where \(Y\) is the discounted return starting at time \(1\), with its discount exponent reset to zero. By the Markov property,

\( \displaystyle \mathbb E_i[Y]=\sum_jP_{ij}v_j=(Pv)_i. \)

Therefore

\( \displaystyle v=g+\delta Pv,\qquad (I-\delta P)v=g. \)

Second moment

The same conditioning gives \(\mathbb E_i[Y^2]=(Pw)_i\). Expanding the square,

\( \displaystyle w_i=g(i)^2+2\delta g(i)(Pv)_i+\delta^2(Pw)_i. \)

For vectors \(a,c\), write \(a\circ c\) for their componentwise product. The second system is therefore

\( \displaystyle (I-\delta^2P)w=g\circ g+2\delta\,g\circ(Pv). \)

Both systems are nonsingular

Since \(P\) is row-stochastic, its induced infinity norm is \(\|P\|_\infty=1\). For either \(\alpha=\delta\) or \(\alpha=\delta^2\), we have \(0<\alpha<1\), and the matrix series

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\alpha^tP^t \)

converges in that norm. Multiplying its partial sums by \(I-\alpha P\) gives \(I-\alpha^{n+1}P^{n+1}\), which converges to \(I\). Thus

\( \displaystyle (I-\alpha P)^{-1}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\alpha^tP^t. \)

Both systems consequently have unique rational solutions.

Apply the initial distribution

Regard \(p^0\) as a column vector. Conditioning on the initial state yields

\( \displaystyle M=(p^0)^{\mathsf T}v,\qquad \mathbb E[Z_\lambda^2]=(p^0)^{\mathsf T}w. \)

Hence

\( \displaystyle \boxed{V=(p^0)^{\mathsf T}w-\bigl((p^0)^{\mathsf T}v\bigr)^2.} \)

This includes randomness in the initial state. Since the expression equals a variance, \(V\geq0\), including for signed rewards.

3. Compute the maximum exactly

The set \(A=\{q:\lambda_q>0\}\) is nonempty because all coalition masses are nonnegative and their sum is positive. Compute the rational number

\( \displaystyle b_*=\max_{q\in A}b_q. \)

For every \(q\in A\),

\( \displaystyle \bigl(M+b_*\sqrt V\bigr)-\bigl(M+b_q\sqrt V\bigr)=(b_*-b_q)\sqrt V\geq0. \)

Therefore

\( \displaystyle \boxed{U=M+b_*\sqrt V.} \)

The argument remains valid when all risk coefficients are negative or when \(V=0\). No comparison between distinct algebraic radicals is needed, and the square root need not be simplified.

4. Polynomial bit complexity

The preceding formulas require aggregation, two linear-system solves, and a scan of the supported types. We now bound the sizes of their exact arithmetic operands.

Clear the first system to integers

Write every input rational with a positive integer denominator, and let \(D\) be the product of all these denominators. Because \(L\) is the total input bit length,

\( \displaystyle \log_2D=O(L). \)

Every input denominator divides \(D\). Each coordinate of \(g\) has denominator dividing \(D^2\), as does every entry of \(I-\delta P\). Thus

\( \displaystyle A_1=D^2(I-\delta P),\qquad a_1=D^2g \)

are integral, and \(A_1v=a_1\).

Every input reward has absolute value at most \(2^L\). Since \(\sum_q\lambda_q\leq1\), also \(\|g\|_\infty\leq2^L\). Entries of \(I-\delta P\) have absolute value at most \(1\). Consequently, all entries of \(A_1\) and \(a_1\) have \(O(L)\) bits.

For an integer matrix of order \(k\) whose entries have absolute value at most \(2^H\), the determinant expansion gives

\( \displaystyle |\det A|\leq k!\,2^{kH}. \)

Its determinant therefore has \(O(kH+k\log(k+1))\) bits. Applying this bound to \(A_1\) and to its column-replacement matrices, Cramer’s rule shows that the coordinates of \(v\) have a common denominator

\( \displaystyle h=|\det A_1|>0, \)

with numerators and denominator of \(O(m(L+\log(m+1)))\) bits.

Clear the second system to integers

Let

\( \displaystyle f=g\circ g+2\delta\,g\circ(Pv). \)

The denominators of \(g\), \(P\), \(\delta\), and \(v\) divide \(D^2,D,D,h\), respectively. Hence every coordinate of \(f\) has denominator dividing

\( \displaystyle T=D^4h. \)

Thus \(d=Tf\) is an integer vector.

We also need a numerator bound. Writing \(\delta=a/e\) with positive integers \(a<e\), its input encoding implies \(e\leq2^L\). Hence

\( \displaystyle \frac1{1-\delta}=\frac e{e-a}\leq2^L,\qquad \|v\|_\infty\leq\frac{\|g\|_\infty}{1-\delta}\leq2^{2L}. \)

Because \(\|Pv\|_\infty\leq\|v\|_\infty\),

\( \displaystyle \|f\|_\infty\leq2^{2L}+2^{3L+1}. \)

Together with the bound on \(h\), this shows that the entries of \(d\) have \(O(m(L+\log(m+1)))\) bits.

Now set

\( \displaystyle A_2=D^3(I-\delta^2P),\qquad x=Tw. \)

The second moment system is equivalent to the integer system

\( \displaystyle A_2x=D^3d. \)

Its coefficient entries have \(O(L)\) bits, and its right-hand side has \(O(m(L+\log(m+1)))\) bits. The determinant bound and Cramer’s rule therefore give polynomial-size rational coordinates for \(x\), and hence for \(w=x/T\).

In particular, the conservative bound

\( \displaystyle B=O\!\left(m^2(L+\log(m+1))\right) \)

suffices for the bit lengths of all moment outputs.

The final dot products can use their common denominators: \(M\) has denominator dividing \(Dh\), while \((p^0)^{\mathsf T}w\) has denominator dividing \(DT|\det A_2|\). Forming \(V\) and recording \(b_*\) therefore preserves polynomial output size.

Running time

For completeness, an explicit implementation can solve each integer system by Cramer’s rule, computing its \(m+1\) required determinants by pivoted fraction-free elimination. Fraction-free elimination uses \(O(m^3)\) integer arithmetic operations per determinant; its exact divisions and minor representation follow from the determinantal identity underlying the elimination recurrence. The determinant bound above controls its intermediate bit lengths by \(O(B)\).

Thus this implementation uses \(O(\tau m+m^4)\) arithmetic operations, apart from input preprocessing, which is also polynomial. With schoolbook integer arithmetic and rational reduction, a conservative total bit bound is

\( \displaystyle O\!\left((\tau m+m^4)\,[m^2(L+\log(m+1))]^3\right). \)

This bound is deliberately loose but explicit and polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). Standard exact linear-system solvers can reduce the arithmetic-operation count for the moment calculation to \(O(\tau m+m^3)\).

5. Complexity classification

The algorithm returns rational \(M,V\) and the exact symbolic expression \(M+b_*\sqrt V\), all with polynomial-size encodings. Therefore the evaluation function belongs to \(\mathrm{FP}\).

This settles the polynomial-time side of the question for the explicitly supplied support and fixed policy. Under the usual polynomial-time reductions, an \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness classification would consequently imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
