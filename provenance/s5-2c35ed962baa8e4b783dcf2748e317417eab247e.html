The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite buyer-type support \(\{(\theta_a,\mu_a)\}_{a=1}^{\tau}\) with \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\), item-feature vectors \(\psi_\ell\), rates \(\kappa_\ell\) items per buyer, and supply \(s\) per physical item, set \(v_{a\ell}=\theta_a^\top\psi_\ell\). Compute the mass-level optimizer \(z\ge0\) of \(\max \sum_a\mu_a\log(\sum_\ell\kappa_\ell v_{a\ell}z_{a\ell})\), subject to \(\sum_a\mu_a z_{a\ell}\le s\). Interpret \(z_{a\ell}/N\) as each type member’s allocation of each physical \(\ell\)-item, and characterize when the resulting prices support CEEI and how the finite-\(N\) AMO-EG markets converge to this mass solution.

1. Result

Exact computation of a mass optimizer and its supply multipliers is polynomial-time solvable in the stated input parameters. Every mass optimizer, paired with its supply multipliers and the induced physical-item prices, is a mass CEEI.

Moreover, write \(\mu_{\min}=\min_a\mu_a\). For every compatible integer satisfying

\(\displaystyle N>\frac{s}{\mu_{\min}},\)

the set of averaged finite optimizer–price pairs equals the mass optimizer–price solution set exactly. Thus convergence is deterministic and becomes exact after a finite threshold. Allocations need not converge to a single point when the mass optimizer is nonunique.

Below, finite CEEI uses the same exact supply-clearing requirement as in the question.

2. Reduction to an exact rational Fisher market

Set \(c_{a\ell}=\kappa_\ell v_{a\ell}\) and introduce aggregate allocations \(y_{a\ell}=\mu_a z_{a\ell}\). Then

\(\displaystyle U_a(z)=\frac{1}{\mu_a}\sum_\ell c_{a\ell}y_{a\ell},\qquad \sum_a y_{a\ell}\leq s.\)

Consequently, the mass objective is

\(\displaystyle \sum_a\mu_a\log\!\left(\sum_\ell c_{a\ell}y_{a\ell}\right)-\sum_a\mu_a\log\mu_a.\)

The second term is constant. The remaining program is precisely the linear Fisher Eisenberg–Gale program with buyer budgets \(\mu_a\), utility coefficients \(c_{a\ell}\), and supplies \(s\).

All budgets, supplies, and coefficients are positive rationals. Their encoding lengths are polynomial in the original input length: computing each dot product and multiplying by \(\kappa_\ell\) require only rational arithmetic of polynomial bit complexity. Supplies can alternatively be normalized to one by writing \(y_{a\ell}=s t_{a\ell}\), which replaces the utility coefficients by \(s c_{a\ell}\).

The exact rational Fisher algorithm therefore computes one rational optimal allocation and its rational supply prices in polynomial time. Its hypotheses hold because every buyer values every good positively. Dividing \(y_{a\ell}\) by \(\mu_a\) gives the required mass optimizer. The cited theorem also provides polynomial-size rational outputs. [Devanur, Papadimitriou, Saberi, and Vazirani](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/market.pdf).

No enumeration of possible buyer types is involved: the Fisher instance has exactly \(\tau\) buyers and \(m\) goods. The fixed-dimension assumption is unnecessary for this reduction.

3. Mass optimality, CEEI, and uniqueness of prices

Let \(F(z)=\sum_a\mu_a\log U_a(z)\). Every feasible allocation satisfies

\(\displaystyle 0\leq z_{a\ell}\leq\frac{s}{\mu_a}.\)

Thus the feasible set is compact. It contains strictly positive allocations with positive utilities. Maximizing the continuous extension of \(\prod_a U_a(z)^{\mu_a}\) establishes existence of an optimizer with every utility positive.

A sufficiently small strictly positive allocation satisfies all supply inequalities strictly. Hence Slater’s condition holds, and the KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient.

Let \(q_\ell\) be the supply multipliers. Differentiating the objective and accounting for the coefficient \(\mu_a\) in the supply constraint gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{c_{a\ell}}{U_a(z)}\leq q_\ell,\qquad z_{a\ell}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{c_{a\ell}}{U_a(z)}=q_\ell.\)

Because \(c_{a\ell}>0\), every \(q_\ell>0\). Complementary slackness therefore gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_a\mu_a z_{a\ell}=s.\)

Multiplying the stationarity equalities by allocated quantities yields, for every type,

\(\displaystyle \sum_\ell q_\ell z_{a\ell}=\frac{\sum_\ell c_{a\ell}z_{a\ell}}{U_a(z)}=1.\)

For any bundle \(w\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_\ell q_\ell w_\ell\leq1\),

\(\displaystyle U_a(w)=\sum_\ell c_{a\ell}w_\ell\leq U_a(z)\sum_\ell q_\ell w_\ell\leq U_a(z).\)

Thus \(z_a\) maximizes utility at budget one. Since \(q_\ell=\kappa_\ell p_\ell\), this proves exactly the mass CEEI conditions in the question.

Conversely, a mass CEEI has positive prices: a zero price would make demand unbounded because valuations are positive. Each buyer exhausts its budget, since otherwise it could purchase more of a positively valued good. Comparing its chosen utility with the affordable bundle concentrated on any one good gives \(c_{a\ell}/U_a\leq q_\ell\). Budget exhaustion then forces equality on every positively allocated coordinate. Together with clearing, these are the mass KKT conditions. Hence mass CEEI and mass optimality with supply multipliers are equivalent.

The optimal utility vector \(U^*\) is unique. Indeed, if two optimizers had different utility vectors, their midpoint would be feasible and strict concavity of \(\sum_a\mu_a\log U_a\) would give a strictly larger objective.

Every good clears and therefore has a positive allocation to some type. The KKT inequalities and equality on that allocation imply

\(\displaystyle q_\ell^*=\max_a\frac{c_{a\ell}}{U_a^*},\qquad p_\ell^*=\max_a\frac{v_{a\ell}}{U_a^*}.\)

Thus the supply multipliers and induced prices are unique, although allocations can be nonunique. In particular,

\(\displaystyle s\sum_\ell q_\ell^*=1.\)

Write \(\mathcal Z^*\) for the mass optimizer set. It is the compact polytope

\(\displaystyle \mathcal Z^*=\left\{z\geq0:\ \sum_a\mu_a z_{a\ell}=s\ \text{for all }\ell,\quad U_a(z)=U_a^*\ \text{for all }a\right\}.\)

The mass allocation–price solution set is \(\mathcal S=\mathcal Z^*\times\{p^*\}\).

4. Exact aggregation of the finite AMO program

Average a finite allocation over permutations of buyers within each buyer type and physical items within each item type. Feasibility is preserved. Concavity of the objective implies that averaging cannot decrease its value.

The resulting allocation has the form \(\bar x_{ij}=z_{a\ell}/N\). Its buyer utility and physical-item consumption are

\(\displaystyle \sum_\ell N\kappa_\ell v_{a\ell}\frac{z_{a\ell}}N=U_a(z),\qquad \sum_a N\mu_a\frac{z_{a\ell}}N=\sum_a\mu_a z_{a\ell}.\)

The AMO bounds become \(z_{a\ell}\leq N\), and the finite objective becomes \(N F(z)\). Therefore the averaged finite optimizer set is exactly the optimizer set of

\(\displaystyle \max F(z)\quad\text{subject to}\quad z\geq0,\quad \sum_a\mu_a z_{a\ell}\leq s,\quad z_{a\ell}\leq N.\)

Denote this reduced program by \(P_N\). Every feasible point of \(P_N\) lifts to a feasible finite allocation by setting \(x_{ij}=z_{a\ell}/N\).

The multiplier correspondence also holds for arbitrary choices of optimal finite multipliers. Here is the compatibility argument needed to justify averaging them.

Let \(x^o\) be any finite optimizer and \(\bar x\) its symmetrization. Strict concavity in buyer utilities implies that the utility vector is unique across optimizers. Permuting equal-type buyers then shows that each buyer of a given type has the same optimal utility. Thus \(x^o\) and \(\bar x\) give every buyer identical utility.

Let \(\lambda_j,\rho_{ij},\eta_{ij}\geq0\) be finite KKT multipliers for supply, upper bounds, and nonnegativity, respectively. Stationarity reads

\(\displaystyle g_{ij}:=\frac{v_{ij}}{u_i^*}=\lambda_j+\rho_{ij}-\eta_{ij}.\)

Equal utilities and complementary slackness at \(x^o\) give

\(\displaystyle 0=\sum_{ij}g_{ij}(\bar x_{ij}-x^o_{ij})=\sum_j\lambda_j\left(\sum_i\bar x_{ij}-s\right)+\sum_{ij}\rho_{ij}(\bar x_{ij}-1)-\sum_{ij}\eta_{ij}\bar x_{ij}.\)

Every summand on the right is nonpositive. Consequently, every summand vanishes, proving complementary slackness at \(\bar x\) for the original multipliers.

Now average \(\lambda_j\) within item types to obtain \(p_\ell^{(N)}\), and average \(\rho_{ij}\) within buyer–item type pairs to obtain \(r_{a\ell}\). Averaged stationarity and complementary slackness give

\(\displaystyle \frac{v_{a\ell}}{U_a(z^{(N)})}\leq p_\ell^{(N)}+r_{a\ell},\)

with equality when \(z_{a\ell}^{(N)}>0\), and

\(\displaystyle r_{a\ell}(N-z_{a\ell}^{(N)})=0.\)

These are precisely the KKT conditions of \(P_N\), with supply multipliers \(q_\ell^{(N)}=\kappa_\ell p_\ell^{(N)}\) and upper-bound multipliers \(\mu_a\kappa_\ell r_{a\ell}\).

5. When finite supply prices support CEEI

Under the averaged prices, a finite buyer’s demand problem is exactly

\(\displaystyle \max_{0\leq w_\ell\leq N}U_a(w)\quad\text{subject to}\quad\sum_\ell\kappa_\ell p_\ell^{(N)}w_\ell\leq1.\)

Indeed, any such aggregate bundle can be realized by spreading its allocation equally across physical items of each type.

Define the expenditure of type \(a\) by

\(\displaystyle E_a=\sum_\ell\kappa_\ell p_\ell^{(N)}z_{a\ell}^{(N)}.\)

Multiplying averaged stationarity equalities by allocations gives

\(\displaystyle E_a=1-\sum_\ell\kappa_\ell r_{a\ell}z_{a\ell}^{(N)}\leq1.\)

The following is an exact criterion: an averaged finite optimal pair supports finite CEEI if and only if supplies clear and, for each type, either \(E_a=1\) or \(z_{a\ell}^{(N)}=N\) for every \(\ell\).

To prove necessity, suppose a row is not completely capped and \(E_a<1\). Some coordinate has room to increase. A sufficiently small increase respects both its cap and the remaining budget and strictly improves utility.

For sufficiency, a completely capped row already maximizes utility over all AMO-feasible bundles. If \(E_a=1\), the expenditure identity forces all its upper-bound multipliers to vanish: a positive \(r_{a\ell}\) would imply \(z_{a\ell}^{(N)}=N>0\) and a positive deduction from expenditure. The mass argument from Section 3 then proves utility maximization.

For \(N\geq s\), supplies always clear at an optimizer of \(P_N\). Otherwise a slack column has weighted consumption less than \(s\leq N\), so some entry in that column is below its cap and can be increased profitably. For \(N<s\), exact clearing is impossible because a physical item’s total AMO capacity is only \(N\).

Failure at smaller market sizes occurs even with unique supply multipliers. Take

\(\displaystyle N=2,\quad \mu_1=\mu_2=\kappa_1=\kappa_2=\frac12,\quad s=\frac32,\quad (v_{a\ell})=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&100\end{pmatrix}.\)

These valuations have the required representation in dimension two: choose \(\theta_1=(1,0)\), \(\theta_2=(0,1)\), \(\psi_1=(1,1)\), and \(\psi_2=(1,100)\).

There is one buyer of each type and one physical item of each type. The allocation

\(\displaystyle x=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{pmatrix}\)

has utilities \(3/2\) and \(201/2\). A KKT certificate is

\(\displaystyle p_1=\frac2{201},\qquad p_2=\frac23,\qquad \rho_{11}=\frac23-\frac2{201},\qquad \rho_{22}=\frac{200}{201}-\frac23,\)

with all other bound multipliers zero. These multipliers are nonnegative and satisfy stationarity and complementary slackness, proving optimality. The interior allocations \(x_{21}=x_{12}=1/2\) force the displayed supply prices; compatibility of optimal primal and dual solutions proves their uniqueness.

Yet \(p_1+p_2=136/201<1\). Both buyers can afford the completely capped bundle \((1,1)\), which uniquely maximizes their positive additive utilities. Demand therefore exceeds supply, so these supply prices support no finite CEEI.

This agrees with the general obstruction from AMO multipliers identified by [Kroer–Peysakhovich](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10925).

6. Eventual exact agreement and convergence

Set \(B=s/\mu_{\min}\). Every mass-feasible allocation satisfies \(z_{a\ell}\leq B\). Therefore, for \(N>B\), the upper bounds in \(P_N\) are strictly slack at every feasible allocation. Its feasible set and optimizer set equal those of the mass program.

By Section 4, the averaged finite supply multipliers are supply multipliers for \(P_N\). Strict slackness forces every \(r_{a\ell}=0\). Mass price uniqueness consequently gives

\(\displaystyle p^{(N)}=p^*,\qquad z^{(N)}\in\mathcal Z^*.\)

Conversely, every mass optimizer lifts to a finite optimizer. Its mass KKT certificate lifts by assigning price \(p_\ell^*\) to each physical item of type \(\ell\), zero upper-bound multipliers, and nonnegativity multipliers \(p_\ell^*-v_{a\ell}/U_a^*\). Thus every mass allocation–price pair is realized.

Hence, if \(\mathcal S_N\) denotes all averaged finite optimal allocation–price pairs,

\(\displaystyle \mathcal S_N=\mathcal S\qquad\text{for every compatible }N>B.\)

This proves exact eventual equality of solution sets, and therefore Hausdorff convergence. Every selected sequence has eventually constant prices and allocation accumulation points in \(\mathcal Z^*\). If \(\mathcal Z^*\) is a singleton, allocations also become exactly constant after this threshold.

Nonunique allocations can prevent pointwise convergence. For example, take two types \(\theta_1=1,\theta_2=2\), two features \(\psi_1=1,\psi_2=2\), rates \(\kappa_1=1,\kappa_2=1/2\), masses \(1/2\), and supply \(s=2\). Both allocations

\(\displaystyle z=\begin{pmatrix}4&0\\0&4\end{pmatrix},\qquad \widetilde z=\begin{pmatrix}0&4\\4&0\end{pmatrix}\)

give utilities \((4,8)\) and satisfy the mass KKT conditions with \(q_1=q_2=1/4\). Alternating their finite lifts along compatible even integers \(N>4\) produces a nonconvergent allocation sequence.

Finally, the threshold can be exponentially large in the input bit length when \(\mu_{\min}\) is small. The exact mass computation nevertheless remains polynomial-time.
