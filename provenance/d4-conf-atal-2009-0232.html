The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 3 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a finite support of complete rankings \(t\) with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), a target \(c^\star\), and a rational threshold \(\delta\), define \(P^\mu_{ab}=1\) if \(a\) has greater pairwise majority mass than \(b\), \(P^\mu_{ab}=0\) if it has less, and \(P^\mu_{ab}=\tfrac12\) on a tie. Is there a balanced binary agenda and seeding for which the paper's tournament-winning probability \(q(c^\star,KT;P^\mu)\ge\delta\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m=2^h\) candidates, with \(h\ge1\), and let \(c^\star\in C\) and \(\delta\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q\).
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings of \(C\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For distinct \(a,b\in C\), put \(M_{ab}=\sum_{t:a\succ_t b}\mu_t\), and set \(P^\mu_{ab}=1\), \(0\), or \(\tfrac12\) according as \(M_{ab}>\tfrac12\), \(M_{ab}<\tfrac12\), or \(M_{ab}=\tfrac12\).
An agenda \(KT\) is a full binary tree whose \(m\) leaves all have depth \(h\), together with a bijection from candidates to leaves. At each internal node, the winners of its two child subtrees compete, and candidate \(a\) defeats candidate \(b\) with probability \(P^\mu_{ab}\); tied comparisons use independent fair coins. Let \(q(c^\star,KT;P^\mu)\) be the probability that \(c^\star\) reaches the root.
Is deciding whether some agenda \(KT\) satisfies \(q(c^\star,KT;P^\mu)\ge\delta\) solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
Vu, Altman, and Shoham established NP-completeness for balanced schedule control with an explicitly supplied pairwise probability matrix taking values in \(\{0,\tfrac12,1\}\). [Source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas09/pdf/01_Full%20Papers/04_23_FP_0767.pdf)
Aziz et al. established NP-completeness even for explicitly supplied deterministic pairwise outcomes. [Source](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/8805) No result explicitly treating this exact rational-support society formulation was located.

  • Defined the tournament-winning probability through the knockout process, with independent fair coins for tied majority comparisons.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when \(\delta=1\) and every pairwise majority is strict. The reduction uses only polynomially many distinct rankings with positive dyadic masses.

For \(\delta=0\), every instance is trivially a yes-instance. The hardness result concerns the general input family, with an unbounded number of candidates.

1. The known problem used in the reduction

We use the following established theorem.

Deterministic balanced tournament fixing is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. An instance consists of a candidate set \(C\), with \(|C|=2^h\), a distinguished candidate \(c^\star\), and deterministic pairwise outcomes \(D_{ab}\in\{0,1\}\), satisfying \(D_{ab}+D_{ba}=1\) for distinct candidates. The question is whether some assignment of candidates to a complete balanced knockout tree makes \(c^\star\) its winner.

This is Theorem 1 of [Aziz et al., *Fixing a Balanced Knockout Tournament*](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/8805/8664). Their definition explicitly requires a power-of-two candidate count and permits arbitrary assignments to the leaves. Thus its agendas have exactly the shape required here.

The missing step is to realize an arbitrary deterministic outcome matrix by a society whose rational-support encoding has polynomial size. We prove that step directly.

2. A polynomial-size majority realization

Lemma. Let \(C\) have \(m\ge2\) candidates, and let \(D\) specify deterministic outcomes for every distinct pair. One can construct, in polynomial time, a society of distinct strict complete rankings with positive dyadic masses such that \(P^\mu_{ab}=D_{ab}\) for all distinct \(a,b\). Its support size is \(O(m^2)\), and its total encoding length is \(O(m^3\log m)\), using candidate labels of \(O(\log m)\) bits.

Proof. For each unordered pair, write its desired orientation as \(a\to b\), meaning \(D_{ab}=1\). Choose any ordering \(r_1,\ldots,r_{m-2}\) of \(C\setminus\{a,b\}\), and introduce the two rankings

\[ a\succ b\succ r_1\succ\cdots\succ r_{m-2}, \]

\[ r_{m-2}\succ\cdots\succ r_1\succ a\succ b. \]

Here and below, an empty list is simply omitted.

For a multiset \(\mathcal V\) of rankings, define its count margin by

\[ \Delta_{\mathcal V}(x,y) = \#\{R\in\mathcal V:x\succ_R y\} - \#\{R\in\mathcal V:y\succ_R x\}. \]

The displayed pair contributes \(2\) to \(\Delta_{\mathcal V}(a,b)\) and zero to every other unordered-pair margin:

  • Both rankings put \(a\) above \(b\).
  • For any remaining candidate \(r_i\), the first ranking puts both \(a\) and \(b\) above \(r_i\), while the second puts \(r_i\) above both.
  • The two rankings reverse every comparison between remaining candidates.

Introduce such a pair of rankings for every unordered candidate pair. The resulting multiset has

\[ B=2\binom m2=m(m-1) \]

rankings, counting multiplicities. Each desired orientation \(a\to b\) has margin exactly \(2\): its own pair contributes \(2\), and every other pair contributes zero.

To ensure dyadic masses, let

\[ N=2^{\lceil\log_2 B\rceil}. \]

Both \(B\) and \(N\) are even. Consequently, \((N-B)/2\) is a nonnegative integer. Choose a fixed complete ranking \(R\), and add \((N-B)/2\) copies of the pair consisting of \(R\) and its reversal. Each such pair contributes zero to every margin. We now have exactly \(N\) rankings, still with margin \(2\) for every desired orientation.

Merge repeated rankings. If distinct ranking \(R_t\) occurs \(k_t\) times, give it mass

\[ \mu_t=\frac{k_t}{N}. \]

These masses are positive dyadic rationals, and

\[ \sum_t\mu_t=\frac{\sum_t k_t}{N}=1. \]

For every desired orientation \(a\to b\), the normalized margin is

\[ M_{ab}-M_{ba}=\frac{2}{N}. \]

Because the rankings are strict and complete, \(M_{ab}+M_{ba}=1\). Solving these two equations gives

\[ M_{ab}=\frac12+\frac1N>\frac12. \]

Thus \(P^\mu_{ab}=1=D_{ab}\), and the reverse comparison has probability zero.

Finally, \(B\le N<2B\), so the number of generated rankings and the final support size are \(O(m^2)\). Each ranking requires \(O(m\log m)\) bits. Each numerator \(k_t\) and the denominator \(N\) require \(O(\log m)\) bits. Hence the total encoding length is \(O(m^3\log m)\). Generating, padding, and merging these polynomially many rankings all take polynomial time. This proves the lemma.

3. The hardness reduction

Take an instance \((C,D,c^\star)\) of deterministic balanced tournament fixing. Apply the lemma to produce its society, retain \(C\) and \(c^\star\), and set \(\delta=1\).

All hypotheses of the target problem hold:

  • The candidate count remains \(m=2^h\).
  • The support contains distinct strict complete rankings.
  • Every mass is positive and dyadic, and the masses sum to one.
  • The support size and total bit length are polynomial in the source-instance size.
  • Every comparison satisfies \(P^\mu_{ab}=D_{ab}\).

For any agenda, the two instances therefore have the same winner. Formally, their winners agree at each leaf; if they agree at both children of an internal node, the identical deterministic comparison gives the same winner at that node. Induction up the tree proves agreement at the root.

In particular,

\[ q(c^\star,KT;P^\mu) = \begin{cases} 1,&\text{if }c^\star\text{ wins }KT\text{ under }D,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

It follows that

\[ \exists KT:\ c^\star\text{ wins under }D \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \exists KT:\ q(c^\star,KT;P^\mu)\ge1. \]

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

The same reduction works for any fixed rational threshold \(\delta\in(0,1]\), since these constructed instances have winning probabilities only in \(\{0,1\}\).

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

We show that a proposed agenda can be verified in polynomial bit complexity, including when majority comparisons tie.

Computing the majority matrix

Precompute each candidate’s position in each ranking. For every distinct pair, sum the masses of rankings placing the first candidate above the second, and compare that sum exactly with \(\tfrac12\).

There are \(O(\tau m^2)\) summand inspections. Under the ordinary binary encoding of rational numerators and denominators, these calculations have polynomial bit complexity in \(L\): the product of all input denominators is a common denominator whose bit length is at most the sum of their bit lengths. Thus every entry of \(P^\mu\) can be computed exactly in polynomial time.

Encoding the agenda

A full binary tree whose leaves all have depth \(h\) must be the perfect binary tree of depth \(h\): every node at smaller depth must have two children, and no node at depth \(h\) can have children.

Consequently, a certificate is just a permutation assigning the \(m\) candidates to the leaves of one canonical perfect tree. Its length is \(O(m\log m)\).

Computing the winning probability exactly

For a tree node \(v\), let \(C_v\) be its descendant candidates, let \(s_v=|C_v|\), and let \(Q_v(a)\) denote the probability that \(a\in C_v\) wins its subtree.

A subtree with \(s_v\) leaves has \(s_v-1\) matches. Preassign an independent fair bit to each internal node, using that bit if its comparison ties and otherwise ignoring it. These bits reproduce the stipulated independent tie-breaking. Therefore

\[ Q_v(a)=\frac{A_v(a)}{2^{s_v-1}} \]

for an integer \(A_v(a)\) satisfying \(0\le A_v(a)\le2^{s_v-1}\).

We compute these numerators directly. At a leaf occupied by \(a\), set \(A_v(a)=1\). At an internal node with children \(u,w\), define \(r_{ab}=2P^\mu_{ab}\in\{0,1,2\}\). For \(a\in C_u\), independence of the two child subtrees and the current comparison gives

\[ Q_v(a) = Q_u(a)\sum_{b\in C_w}Q_w(b)P^\mu_{ab}. \]

Since \(s_v=s_u+s_w\), substituting the common-denominator representations yields

\[ A_v(a) = A_u(a)\sum_{b\in C_w}A_w(b)r_{ab}. \]

For \(a\in C_w\), use the same formula with the children interchanged.

Every stored numerator has at most \(m\) bits. At each internal node the computation uses \(O(|C_u||C_w|)\) arithmetic operations. Each unordered candidate pair lies in opposite children at exactly one node, its lowest common ancestor. Hence the total number of arithmetic operations is \(O(m^2)\), all on polynomially bounded integers.

Finally, write \(\delta=d/e\) with \(e>0\). At the root \(\rho\), verify the threshold by the exact integer comparison

\[ eA_\rho(c^\star)\ge d\,2^{m-1}. \]

This comparison also has polynomial bit complexity in \(m+L\). The decision problem therefore belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Conclusion

The rational-support formulation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. The explicit majority realization transfers the deterministic hardness theorem without increasing the input size superpolynomially, and exact agenda verification establishes membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\). Thus a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
