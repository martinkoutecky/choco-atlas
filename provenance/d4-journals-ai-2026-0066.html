The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many roles \(T\), rational role masses \(\mu_{t,q}\) for each initial opinion state \(q\subseteq\{a,b\}\), a symmetric nonnegative rational exposure matrix \(K\), rational thresholds \(\theta_{t,1},\theta_{t,2}\), and a four-round horizon, choose rational seed masses \(x_{t,q,r}\ge 0\), where \(r\subseteq\{a,b\}\setminus q\) and \(\sum_r x_{t,q,r}=\mu_{t,q}\). At each round, role \(t\) receives opinion-\(c\) exposure \(E^i_{t,c}=\sum_{u\in T}K_{tu}M^i_{u,c}\), where \(M^i_{u,c}\) is the mass of role \(u\) holding \(c\); unopinionated agents adopt \(c\) at threshold \(\theta_{t,1}\), and agents already holding the other opinion adopt \(c\) at threshold \(\theta_{t,2}\). Minimize \(\sum_{t,q,r}|r|x_{t,q,r}\) subject to stabilization by round \(4\) and zero mass holding exactly one opinion.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((t,q)\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_{t,q}\) a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(T\) is a nonempty set of roles, \(m=|T|\), \(q\subseteq\{a,b\}\), omitted masses are zero, and \(\sum_{t,q}\mu_{t,q}=1\).
Input also includes a symmetric matrix \(K\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{T\times T}\), thresholds \(0\le\theta_{t,1}\le\theta_{t,2}\) in \(\mathbb Q\), and a binary rational budget \(B>0\). Diagonal entries of \(K\) represent exposure within a role and may be positive. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Choose rational masses \(x_{t,q,r}\ge0\) for every \(r\subseteq\{a,b\}\setminus q\), including \(r=\varnothing\), with \(\sum_r x_{t,q,r}=\mu_{t,q}\). This assigns mass \(x_{t,q,r}\) initial state \(q\cup r\), at cost \(\sum_{t,q,r}|r|x_{t,q,r}\); seeding occurs only at initialization.
Let \(y^i_{t,s}\) be the mass in role \(t\) holding exactly state \(s\) after round \(i\), so \(y^0_{t,s}=\sum_{q,r:q\cup r=s}x_{t,q,r}\). Define \(M^i_{t,c}=\sum_{s:c\in s}y^i_{t,s}\) and \(E^i_{t,c}=\sum_u K_{tu}M^i_{u,c}\).
Updates are deterministic and synchronous: each entire mass \(y^i_{t,s}\) retains its opinions and adds each missing \(c\) when \(E^i_{t,c}\ge\theta_{t,1}\) if \(s=\varnothing\), or \(E^i_{t,c}\ge\theta_{t,2}\) otherwise. Both opinions can be acquired simultaneously from the empty state, using the first threshold for both; equality triggers adoption.
A feasible allocation has cost at most \(B\), satisfies \(y^4_{t,s}=y^5_{t,s}\) for every role and state, and satisfies \(y^4_{t,\{a\}}=y^4_{t,\{b\}}=0\) for every role.

Is deciding whether a feasible rational allocation exists in \(\mathsf P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete two-opinion target set selection is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard; it is also \(\mathsf{W[1]}\)-hard for budget, treedepth, feedback vertex number, and four-path vertex cover number combined, even with ordered thresholds and successful processes stabilizing within four rounds. Result

The decision problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when every role has exactly one supported type, initially holding \(\{a\}\), and \(\theta_{t,1}=\theta_{t,2}\) for every role.

The reduction below explicitly allows arbitrary fractional seeding. Positive diagonal entries amplify sufficiently seeded roles, while quantitative bounds prevent smaller fractional seeds from bypassing the reduction.

1. Reduction from Vertex Cover

We use the standard \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness of Vertex Cover: given a simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(k\), decide whether some \(C\subseteq V\) with \(|C|\le k\) meets every edge. See [Karp’s original paper](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Write \(n=|V|\) and \(h=|E|\). We may restrict to \(h\ge1\) and \(1\le k<n\). The excluded cases are immediately decidable and can be replaced by fixed yes or no instances satisfying these restrictions.

Construction

Introduce a selector role \(s_v\) for every \(v\in V\), an edge role \(d_e\) for every \(e\in E\), and one completion role \(z\). Thus \(m=n+h+1\).

Define

\(W=4(k+1),\qquad \eta=\dfrac{1}{16hW(k+1)},\qquad R_0=64hnW(k+1)+16(k+1)^2.\)

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\ge(n+h)W+R_0\), and set

\(R=N-(n+h)W.\)

In particular, \(R\ge R_0\). Assign the supported masses

\(\mu_{s_v,\{a\}}=\dfrac{W}{N},\qquad \mu_{d_e,\{a\}}=\dfrac{W}{N},\qquad \mu_{z,\{a\}}=\dfrac{R}{N}.\)

All other masses are zero. These masses sum to one, are dyadic rationals, and give \(\tau=m\).

Define the symmetric exposure matrix by the following entries:

  • \(K_{s_v,s_v}=1\) for every vertex.
  • \(K_{s_v,d_e}=K_{d_e,s_v}=\eta\) when \(v\) is an endpoint of \(e\).
  • \(K_{s_v,z}=K_{z,s_v}=1/R\) for every vertex.
  • \(K_{d_e,z}=K_{z,d_e}=\eta\) for every edge.

All unspecified entries are zero.

For convenience, define unnormalized thresholds

\(\Theta_{s_v}=1,\qquad \Theta_{d_e}=\dfrac{\eta W}{2},\qquad \Theta_z=\eta\left(h-\dfrac12\right)W.\)

The actual thresholds and budget are

\(\theta_{t,1}=\theta_{t,2}=\dfrac{\Theta_t}{N},\qquad B=\dfrac{k}{N}.\)

Every threshold is positive, the ordered-threshold requirement holds, and \(B>0\).

All constructed numbers have bit length polynomial in the Vertex Cover input length. The number of roles and the explicitly written matrix size are also polynomial. Hence this is a polynomial-time construction.

2. Dynamics and quantitative bounds

Every agent initially holds \(a\), so the only seeding choice is whether to add \(b\). Every state throughout the process is therefore either \(\{a\}\) or \(\{a,b\}\).

Let \(p_t\) be the unnormalized mass seeded with \(b\) in role \(t\). The budget condition becomes

\(p_t\ge0,\qquad \sum_{t\in T}p_t\le k.\)

Let \(w_t=W\) for selector and edge roles, and \(w_z=R\). Both \(W>k\) and \(R>k\), so no role can be fully seeded.

Define the unnormalized \(b\)-mass and exposure by

\(A_t^i=N M_{t,b}^i,\qquad F_t^i=\sum_{u\in T}K_{tu}A_u^i.\)

Adoption occurs exactly when \(F_t^i\ge\Theta_t\). Before a role’s unseeded mass adopts \(b\), its \(b\)-mass remains \(p_t\); afterward, its \(b\)-mass is \(w_t\). This follows because all its remaining mass has the same state \(\{a\}\) and undergoes the same deterministic update.

We say that a role activates in the round when this remaining mass adopts \(b\).

Two bounds will control fractional seeding. First,

\(h\eta W=\dfrac{1}{16(k+1)},\qquad \dfrac{k}{R}\le\dfrac{k}{16(k+1)^2}<\dfrac{1}{16(k+1)}.\)

Consequently,

\(h\eta W+\dfrac{k}{R}<\dfrac{1}{8(k+1)}.\) \[\tag{1}\]

Second,

\(\dfrac{nW}{R}\le\dfrac{1}{64h(k+1)}=\dfrac{\eta W}{4}.\) \[\tag{2}\]

3. Completeness: a cover gives a feasible allocation

Suppose \(C\subseteq V\) is a vertex cover with \(|C|\le k\). Seed unnormalized mass one with \(b\) in each selector \(s_v\) for \(v\in C\), and seed nothing elsewhere.

The actual cost is \(|C|/N\le B\).

The following statements concern adoption by each indicated round:

  • Round one: Every selected selector activates. Its diagonal contribution to exposure is exactly one, which meets \(\Theta_{s_v}=1\). Equality triggers adoption.
  • Round two: Every edge role activates. Since \(C\) covers every edge, each edge role has a selected endpoint whose \(b\)-mass is already \(W\). Its exposure is therefore at least \(\eta W>\eta W/2=\Theta_{d_e}\).
  • Round three: The completion role activates. All edge roles now have \(b\)-mass \(W\), providing exposure at least \(\eta hW>\Theta_z\).
  • Round four: Every remaining selector activates. The completion role alone contributes \((1/R)R=1=\Theta_{s_v}\).

Thus all mass holds \(\{a,b\}\) by round four. There are no singleton states, and no further change is possible in round five. The allocation is feasible.

4. Soundness: every feasible allocation gives a cover

Consider any feasible allocation, allowing arbitrary rational fractional seeds.

Because every role always holds \(a\), the requirement \(y^4_{t,\{a\}}=0\) forces every role to activate by round four. Let \(j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) be the activation round of \(z\).

Define

\(S=\{v\in V:s_v\text{ activates strictly before round }j\}.\)

We prove that \(|S|\le k\) and that \(S\) covers every edge.

At most \(k\) selectors activate before \(z\)

Take \(v\in S\), and suppose its selector activates in round \(\ell<j\). In the preceding exposure round, \(z\) has not activated, so its \(b\)-mass is \(p_z\). The selector’s own \(b\)-mass is still \(p_{s_v}\), and every incident edge role has \(b\)-mass at most \(W\).

Its successful threshold comparison therefore implies

\(1\le p_{s_v}+h\eta W+\dfrac{p_z}{R} \le p_{s_v}+h\eta W+\dfrac{k}{R}.\)

Using (1),

\(p_{s_v}>1-\dfrac{1}{8(k+1)}.\)

If \(|S|\ge k+1\), then

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}p_t \ge\sum_{v\in S}p_{s_v} >(k+1)\left(1-\dfrac{1}{8(k+1)}\right) =k+\dfrac78, \)

contradicting the budget. Hence \(|S|\le k\).

Every edge role activates before \(z\)

The successful comparison activating \(z\) uses exposures from round \(j-1\).

Suppose some edge role has not activated by that round. Its \(b\)-mass is its initial seed, which is at most \(k\). Each other edge role has \(b\)-mass at most \(W\), and each selector also has \(b\)-mass at most \(W\). Therefore, by (2),

\(\displaystyle F_z^{j-1} \le \eta\bigl((h-1)W+k\bigr)+\dfrac{nW}{R} \le \eta\left((h-1)W+k+\dfrac W4\right). \)

But \(W/4=k+1>k\), so

\(\displaystyle (h-1)W+k+\dfrac W4 < \left(h-\dfrac12\right)W. \)

Thus \(F_z^{j-1}<\Theta_z\), contradicting activation of \(z\) in round \(j\).

Every edge role must consequently activate strictly before round \(j\). This step uses synchronous updating: activation of \(z\) cannot depend on edge activations in the same round.

Every edge has an endpoint in \(S\)

Take an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), and let \(\ell<j\) be its edge role’s activation round.

If neither endpoint selector had activated by round \(\ell-1\), then their \(b\)-masses would still be \(p_{s_u}\) and \(p_{s_v}\). The completion role would also still have \(b\)-mass \(p_z\). These are the edge role’s only neighbors, so

\(\displaystyle F_{d_e}^{\ell-1} =\eta(p_{s_u}+p_{s_v}+p_z) \le\eta k <\dfrac{\eta W}{2} =\Theta_{d_e}. \)

This contradicts its activation.

At least one endpoint selector therefore activates before the edge role, hence before \(z\). That endpoint belongs to \(S\). Since \(e\) was arbitrary, \(S\) is a vertex cover of size at most \(k\).

Together with completeness, this proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness.

5. Membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\)

It remains to justify polynomial-size rational certificates; rational feasibility alone does not establish this.

Consider a feasible allocation and record, for every round \(i\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), role \(t\), opinion \(c\), and threshold index \(j\in\{1,2\}\), whether

\(E^i_{t,c}\ge\theta_{t,j}.\)

There are \(20m\) such Boolean comparisons.

For a fixed comparison pattern, every state’s destination in each synchronous update is fixed. The constraints then form a rational linear system:

  • Nonnegative allocation variables and the support-mass conservation equations.
  • The initialization equations and five rounds of linear update equations.
  • The budget inequality.
  • The required round-four singleton equalities and round-four/round-five equality.
  • A weak inequality \(E^i_{t,c}\ge\theta_{t,j}\) for every successful comparison.
  • A strict inequality \(E^i_{t,c}<\theta_{t,j}\) for every failed comparison.

This system has polynomially many variables and constraints: at most \(4\tau\) allocation variables and \(24m\) state-mass variables. Its coefficients have polynomial bit length.

Introduce a variable \(\delta\), with \(0\le\delta\le1\), and replace every failed comparison by

\(E^i_{t,c}\le\theta_{t,j}-\delta.\)

Also bound every allocation and state-mass variable between zero and one; these bounds hold for every genuine trajectory.

The original feasible allocation realizes this augmented system with some \(\delta>0\): take the minimum of one and the finitely many positive failure margins. If there are no failed comparisons, take \(\delta=1\).

The augmented feasible region is a nonempty bounded rational polytope. Maximizing \(\delta\) therefore has a vertex optimum with positive objective value. Such a vertex has polynomial bit length: after clearing denominators, its coordinates are determined by a square nonsingular subsystem of active constraints; Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound give polynomial-size numerators and denominators.

The allocation coordinates of this vertex satisfy every original strict comparison because \(\delta>0\). They therefore produce the recorded trajectory and form a polynomial-size feasible rational allocation.

A verifier can check such an allocation by exact rational arithmetic, simulate five synchronous rounds, and check the budget and terminal conditions, all in polynomial time. Thus the problem belongs to \(\mathsf{NP}\).

Conclusion

The exact mass-allocation problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, even with \(\tau=m\), only initial type \(\{a\}\), and equal first and second thresholds. Consequently, a running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}\).
