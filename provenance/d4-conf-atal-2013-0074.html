The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), finite action sets \(A_t\), and rational multilinear payoff functions of aggregate action shares, decide whether there is a type-symmetric mixed profile \(\sigma_t\) that is a Nash equilibrium and admits no positive-mass coalition deviation that strictly benefits every deviating type; if one exists, return it.

Each type has a nonempty finite action set \(A_t\); put \(m=\max_t|A_t|\). For every \(t,a\), the input specifies a multilinear polynomial \(u_{t,a}(z)\) by an explicit list of monomials with binary-rational coefficients, with degree at most one in each coordinate. Its values lie in \([0,1]\) on feasible aggregate vectors; coefficients may have either sign. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A type-symmetric profile assigns every individual of type \(t\) the same independent mixed strategy \(\sigma_t\in\Delta(A_t)\), with real probabilities. Aggregate shares are indexed by type and action: \(z_{t,a}=\mu_t\sigma_{t,a}\). The payoff to a type-\(t\) individual using \(q\in\Delta(A_t)\) at aggregate \(z\) is \(V_t(q,z)=\sum_{a\in A_t}q_a u_{t,a}(z)\).

1. Result

Deciding existence is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard for the exact nonatomic model in the question. This holds even under either restriction:

  • $\tau=2$, with payoff polynomials of total degree at most $3$;
  • $m=2$, with payoff polynomials of total degree at most $4$.

All masses and coefficients in the reductions are dyadic rationals.

Put $N=\sum_t|A_t|\le m\tau$. There are deterministic algorithms with the following bit-operation bounds:

| Task | Running time |
|---|---|
| Decide existence | $L^{O(1)}(N+2)^{O(N^2)}$ |
| Decide existence and output an exact strong profile when one exists | $L^{O(1)}(N+2)^{O(N^3)}$ |

In particular, the second bound is at most

$$ L^{O(1)}(m\tau+2)^{O((m\tau)^3)}. $$

The output probabilities are represented by integer polynomials and rational isolating intervals. Rational probabilities are not assumed.

Thus fixing both $m$ and $\tau$ gives polynomial-time solvability. Fixing only either parameter retains $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness. For $m=1$, the unique profile is trivially strong.

2. A guard gadget that controls all partial-type coalitions

Consider any collection of encoding types, all of whose action payoffs are identically $1$. Add a guard type $g$ of positive mass $\mu_g$, with actions $C,D$.

Let $F$ depend only on encoding aggregate coordinates, and suppose

$$ 0\le F(z)\le1 $$

on feasible aggregates. Choose a rational $\theta\in[0,1]$, write $x=z_{g,C}/\mu_g$, and define

$$ u_{g,C}(z)=\frac{1+(1+\theta-F(z))x}{4}, \qquad u_{g,D}(z)=\frac{2+(1+\theta-F(z))x}{4}. $$

These payoffs satisfy the input promise: $0\le x\le1$ and $0\le1+\theta-F\le2$, so both lie in $[0,1]$.

Lemma. A profile is strong exactly when the guard plays $D$ purely and the encoding strategies satisfy $F\ge\theta$.

Proof. At every feasible aggregate,

$$ u_{g,D}(z)-u_{g,C}(z)=\frac14. $$

Because an individual leaves the aggregate unchanged, Nash equilibrium requires the guard to play $D$ purely. Conversely, every profile with a pure-$D$ guard is Nash, since all encoding types are indifferent.

An encoding type cannot participate in a profitable coalition: its payoff remains $1$ after every deviation, whereas profitability requires strict improvement for every participating type. Hence any profitable coalition consists exclusively of guard individuals, and $F$ remains unchanged.

At a Nash profile the guard’s payoff is $1/2$. For a guard-only deviation, let

$$ \lambda=\frac{c_g}{\mu_g}\in(0,1], \qquad p=\rho_{g,C}\in[0,1]. $$

The new normalized $C$-share is $x'=\lambda p$. Coalition members receive

$$ V_g(\rho_g,z') = \frac{2-p+(1+\theta-F)\lambda p}{4}. $$

Their gain is therefore

$$ V_g(\rho_g,z')-\frac12 = \frac{p\bigl((1+\theta-F)\lambda-1\bigr)}4. $$

If $F\ge\theta$, then $0\le1+\theta-F\le1$, so this gain is nonpositive for every $\lambda,p$. This includes every permitted partial-type and mixed deviation.

If $F<\theta$, the entire guard type can switch to pure $C$: taking $\lambda=p=1$ gives gain $(\theta-F)/4>0$.

This proves the characterization, including the equality case $F=\theta$. $\square$

If $F$ is multilinear in encoding coordinates, the guard payoffs remain multilinear: multiplication introduces only the distinct coordinate $z_{g,C}$.

3. Hardness with two types

We reduce from CLIQUE on a simple graph $G=(\{1,\ldots,n\},E)$ and an integer $2\le k\le n$.

3.1. The graph optimization identity

For $w$ in the probability simplex, define

$$ F_G(w)=2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_iw_j. $$

Its range satisfies

$$ 0\le F_G(w) \le2\sum_{i<j}w_iw_j =1-\sum_iw_i^2 \le1. $$

Let $\omega(G)$ be the maximum clique size. We prove

$$ \max_{w\in\Delta(\{1,\ldots,n\})}F_G(w) = 1-\frac1{\omega(G)}. $$

Continuity and compactness give a maximizer. Choose one having minimum support size.

Suppose nonadjacent vertices $i,j$ both belong to its support. Hold all other coordinates fixed and preserve $w_i+w_j=s$. The terms involving these coordinates are

$$ 2w_iA+2w_jB, $$

where

$$ A=\sum_{\ell:\{i,\ell\}\in E}w_\ell, \qquad B=\sum_{\ell:\{j,\ell\}\in E}w_\ell. $$

Both sums remain fixed: the absence of the edge $\{i,j\}$ means neither involves either changing coordinate. Moving all mass $s$ to $i$ when $A\ge B$, and to $j$ otherwise, cannot decrease the objective. Since the original point was a global maximizer, the resulting point is another maximizer with smaller support, a contradiction.

Consequently, the support is a clique of some size $r\le\omega(G)$. On that support,

$$ F_G(w)=1-\sum_iw_i^2 \le1-\frac1r \le1-\frac1{\omega(G)}. $$

Here $\sum_iw_i^2\ge1/r$ follows from Cauchy–Schwarz. Conversely, the uniform distribution on a maximum clique attains $1-1/\omega(G)$. The identity follows.

3.2. A threshold with polynomial encoding length

Set

$$ \alpha=1-\frac1{k-1}, \qquad \beta=1-\frac1k. $$

Choose the specific power of two

$$ q=2^{\lceil\log_2(k(k-1))\rceil+1}, \qquad \theta=\frac{\lfloor q\alpha\rfloor+1}{q}. $$

Then $q>k(k-1)$ and $q\le4k(k-1)$, so $\theta$ has $O(\log k)$ bits. Moreover,

$$ \alpha<\theta \le\alpha+\frac1q <\alpha+\frac1{k(k-1)} =\beta. $$

3.3. The society and reduction equivalence

Introduce exactly two types, each of mass $1/2$:

  • an encoding type $e$ with actions $1,\ldots,n$, all having payoff $1$;
  • the two-action guard.

Set $w_i=2z_{e,i}$ and use $F=F_G(w)$ in the gadget. Feasible encoding aggregates correspond exactly to simplex points $w$.

The gadget implies that a strong profile exists exactly when some $w$ satisfies $F_G(w)\ge\theta$.

If $\omega(G)\ge k$, then

$$ \max F_G = 1-\frac1{\omega(G)} \ge\beta>\theta. $$

If $\omega(G)\le k-1$, then

$$ \max F_G \le\alpha<\theta. $$

Thus a strong profile exists exactly when the CLIQUE instance is positive.

3.4. Input promises and size

In aggregate coordinates,

$$ F(z)=8\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}z_{e,i}z_{e,j}. $$

The explicit guard payoffs are

$$ u_{g,C}(z) = \frac14+\frac{1+\theta}{2}z_{g,C} -4\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}z_{g,C}z_{e,i}z_{e,j}, $$

$$ u_{g,D}(z) = \frac12+\frac{1+\theta}{2}z_{g,C} -4\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}z_{g,C}z_{e,i}z_{e,j}. $$

Each cubic monomial uses distinct coordinates. The payoffs are multilinear, have degree at most $3$, and satisfy the promised range by the gadget lemma.

The construction has

$$ \tau=2,\qquad m=n,\qquad L=O((n+|E|)\log(n+2)). $$

It is a polynomial-time reduction. This proves $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness with two types.

4. Hardness with two actions per type

Start with a $3$-CNF formula. Remove tautological clauses and repeated literals within clauses. Append a positive unit clause on a fresh variable. Appending this clause preserves satisfiability and guarantees at least one remaining clause.

Write $n$ for the resulting number of variables and $M\ge1$ for the number of clauses. Each clause has at most three literals on distinct variables.

For each variable $i$, introduce an encoding type with actions $0,1$, both having payoff $1$. Put

$$ w_i=\frac{z_{i,1}}{\mu_i}\in[0,1]. $$

For clause $j$, define its failure polynomial

$$ B_j(w)= \prod_{\text{positive literals }i\text{ in }j}(1-w_i) \prod_{\text{negative literals }i\text{ in }j}w_i. $$

Every $B_j$ is nonnegative on the cube and multilinear of degree at most $3$. Set

$$ h=\lceil\log_2 M\rceil, \qquad F(w)=1-2^{-h}\sum_{j=1}^M B_j(w). $$

Since $0\le B_j\le1$ and $M\le2^h$,

$$ 0\le F(w)\le1. $$

We claim

$$ \exists w\in[0,1]^n:\ F(w)=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the formula is satisfiable}. $$

A satisfying Boolean assignment makes every clause failure vanish, proving one direction.

Conversely, $F(w)=1$ forces every $B_j(w)=0$, because these polynomials are nonnegative. In each clause, some failure factor is zero. For a positive literal this requires $w_i=1$; for a negative literal it requires $w_i=0$. Assign variables with integral coordinates accordingly, and assign all other variables arbitrarily. Every clause retains a true literal. The assignments are consistent because they come from the same vector $w$.

Add the guard with threshold $\theta=1$. Choose

$$ b=\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil. $$

Give every variable type and the guard mass $2^{-b}$. Add a one-action dummy type of mass

$$ 1-(n+1)2^{-b}>0 $$

and constant payoff $1$. All masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one. The dummy cannot participate profitably.

By the gadget lemma, a strong profile exists exactly when $F\ge1$, equivalently when $F=1$, equivalently when the formula is satisfiable.

Each clause failure expands into at most eight monomials. Normalizing by the dyadic masses preserves rational coefficients, and multiplication by the guard coordinate preserves multilinearity and increases degree by at most one. Therefore

$$ m=2,\qquad \tau=n+2, $$

the payoff degree is at most $4$, and

$$ L=O((n+M)\log(n+M+2)). $$

This proves $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness with two actions per type.

5. An exact first-order formulation

Let $N=\sum_t|A_t|$. Introduce candidate variables

$$ X=(\sigma,z,v), \qquad \dim X=K=2N+\tau. $$

Let $P(X)$ be the conjunction, over the appropriate types and actions, of

$$ \sigma_{t,a}\ge0,\qquad \sum_a\sigma_{t,a}=1,\qquad z_{t,a}=\mu_t\sigma_{t,a}, $$

$$ v_t=\sum_a\sigma_{t,a}u_{t,a}(z), \qquad v_t\ge u_{t,a}(z). $$

These conditions express feasibility and Nash equilibrium exactly. Indeed, the payoff against a fixed aggregate is linear in the individual’s strategy, so dominating every pure-action payoff is equivalent to dominating every mixed-strategy payoff.

Introduce deviation variables

$$ W=(c,\rho,y), \qquad \dim W=2N+\tau. $$

Let $D(X,W)$ assert

$$ 0\le c_t\le\mu_t,\qquad \sum_tc_t>0, $$

$$ \rho_{t,a}\ge0,\qquad \sum_a\rho_{t,a}=1, $$

$$ y_{t,a}=(\mu_t-c_t)\sigma_{t,a}+c_t\rho_{t,a}, $$

and, for every type,

$$ c_t=0 \quad\lor\quad \sum_a\rho_{t,a}u_{t,a}(y)>v_t. $$

Thus $D$ expresses exactly an admissible profitable coalition. Strategies for nonparticipating types cause no restriction: their coalition masses are zero.

Define

$$ Q(X)=P(X)\land\forall W\,\neg D(X,W). $$

The existence question is exactly

$$ \exists X\,\forall W\, \bigl(P(X)\land\neg D(X,W)\bigr). $$

There are $O(N)$ distinct polynomial atoms, each of total degree at most $N+1$. The formula can be constructed with sparse bit length polynomial in $L$. In particular, multiplying a listed payoff monomial by a strategy variable adds one factor without expanding substituted aggregate expressions.

Multiplying each polynomial by a positive common denominator gives integer coefficients of bit size $L^{O(1)}$ and preserves every sign condition.

6. Compactness supplies a canonical exact output

The following property avoids the multivariate sampling issue identified in the verifier report.

Lemma. The set of strong profiles is compact.

Proof. The feasible strategy space is the compact product $\prod_t\Delta(A_t)$. Nash equilibrium is a closed condition, since it consists of finitely many weak inequalities between continuous functions.

The set of profiles admitting a profitable coalition is relatively open in the feasible strategy space. To prove this, fix a profitable deviation at a profile $\sigma$, including its masses $c$ and strategies $\rho$. Its participating types form a fixed finite set. For each participating type, the gain

$$ V_t\bigl(\rho_t,y(\sigma)\bigr) - V_t\bigl(\sigma_t,z(\sigma)\bigr) $$

is continuous in $\sigma$ and strictly positive at the given profile. All these gains therefore remain positive in a sufficiently small neighborhood. The same $c,\rho$ remain admissible throughout that neighborhood, because their admissibility does not depend on $\sigma$.

Consequently, having no profitable coalition is relatively closed. Intersecting it with the Nash conditions gives a closed subset of a compact space. $\square$

Let

$$ S=\{X:Q(X)\}. $$

For each feasible $\sigma$, the coordinates $z$ and $v$ are uniquely determined continuous functions of $\sigma$. Hence $S$ is compact as well.

Fix an ordering of the $K$ coordinates. Every nonempty compact subset of $\mathbb R^K$ has a unique lexicographically least point: minimize the first coordinate; within the resulting nonempty compact set minimize the second; continue through all coordinates.

Write $X'\prec X$ for strict lexicographic comparison, expressed as

$$ X'\prec X \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \bigvee_{j=1}^K \left( X'_j<X_j \land \bigwedge_{i<j}X'_i=X_i \right). $$

The lexicographically least point of $S$ is characterized by

$$ R(X)= Q(X)\land \neg\exists X'\bigl(Q(X')\land X'\prec X\bigr). $$

Using a fresh copy $W'$ of the deviation variables, define the quantifier-free formula

$$ \begin{aligned} E(X,W,X',W')={}&P(X)\land\neg D(X,W)\\ &{}\land \bigl( \neg(X'\prec X) \lor\neg P(X') \lor D(X',W') \bigr). \end{aligned} $$

Then

$$ R(X) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \forall(W,X')\,\exists W'\,E(X,W,X',W'). $$

To check this equivalence, the first two conjuncts enforce $Q(X)$. For every earlier feasible candidate $X'$, the last conjunct requires a profitable deviation, which is exactly the assertion that $X'$ does not satisfy $Q$. For an infeasible or non-earlier $X'$, any $W'$ suffices.

For coordinate $j$, define

$$ \Phi_j(x)= \exists X_{-j}\, \forall(W,X')\, \exists W'\, E(X,W,X',W') \bigm|_{X_j=x}. $$

When $S$ is nonempty, $\Phi_j$ defines the singleton containing coordinate $j$ of its lexicographically least point. Crucially, all these singleton formulas describe coordinates of the same point.

Each formula has three quantifier blocks of size $O(N)$, one free variable, $O(N)$ distinct polynomials, and Boolean length $O(N^2)$.

7. Bit complexity and ordinary real algebraic output

7.1. Quantifier elimination

We use the following conservative consequence of the block quantifier-elimination theorem. For a fixed number $r$ of quantifier blocks of sizes $k_1,\ldots,k_r$, $\ell$ free variables, $s$ integer polynomials of degree at most $d$, and coefficient bit size at most $B$, its arithmetic-operation count and output length are bounded by

$$ (s+d+2)^{C_r(\ell+1)\prod_i(k_i+1)}. $$

Intermediate integer bit sizes are bounded by

$$ B(d+2)^{C_r(\ell+1)\prod_i(k_i+1)}. $$

Here $C_r$ depends only on $r$. These estimates follow directly from [Basu’s survey, Theorem 2.16](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011.pdf), including its explicit integer-size bound. Polynomial-time integer arithmetic converts them to bit-operation bounds.

The dense input representation required by such algorithms also fits the claimed running times. In $O(N)$ variables and degree at most $N+1$, the number of monomial slots per polynomial is at most

$$ (N+2)^{O(N)}. $$

Filling these slots from our sparse lists costs

$$ L^{O(1)}(N+2)^{O(N)}, $$

which is absorbed below.

For existence, there are two quantifier blocks of size $O(N)$ and no free variables. Therefore decision takes

$$ L^{O(1)}(N+2)^{O(N^2)} $$

bit operations.

For each coordinate formula $\Phi_j$, there are three blocks of size $O(N)$ and one free variable. Quantifier elimination produces an equivalent univariate Boolean formula $\Psi_j(x)$ whose length, polynomial degrees, and coefficient bit sizes are all bounded by

$$ L^{O(1)}(N+2)^{O(N^3)}. $$

Its computation satisfies the same bit-time bound.

7.2. Extracting the unique coordinate

We explain why a singleton univariate formula gives ordinary real algebraic output in polynomial time in its explicit size, degrees, and coefficient bit sizes.

Remove constant polynomial atoms by evaluating them. Let $p_1,\ldots,p_s$ be the remaining nonzero, nonconstant integer polynomials, and put

$$ H=\prod_{i=1}^s p_i. $$

If $\Psi$ defines $\{\alpha\}$, then $H(\alpha)=0$. Otherwise, all polynomial signs would be constant on some open neighborhood of $\alpha$, making $\Psi$ true throughout that neighborhood—a contradiction.

Construct the square-free part $H_{\mathrm{sf}}$ and isolate its real roots. Integer-polynomial root isolation produces rational isolating intervals in polynomial bit time in degree and coefficient bit size, including ordinary real output. Relevant explicit bounds are in [Emiris–Mourrain–Tsigaridas, Theorem 5.1 and Remark 5.2](https://who.paris.inria.fr/Elias.Tsigaridas/files/emt-lncs-2006.pdf). Their Theorems 2.1–2.4 and Corollary 2.1 also provide polynomial bit bounds for square-free parts, gcds, and Sturm root counts.

For completeness, signs at each isolated root $\beta$ can be determined as follows. If $\beta$ is a rational endpoint, evaluate each $p_i$ directly. Otherwise, use an isolating interval $(a,b)$ whose endpoints are not roots of $H_{\mathrm{sf}}$. Compute

$$ G_i=\gcd(p_i,H_{\mathrm{sf}}). $$

A Sturm count determines whether $G_i$ has a root in $(a,b)$. Since this interval contains exactly one root of $H_{\mathrm{sf}}$, a positive count is equivalent to $p_i(\beta)=0$. If the count is zero, $p_i$ has no root in the interval, so its sign at $\beta$ equals its sign at any rational interior point.

Thus every atom of $\Psi$ can be evaluated at every root. Exactly one root satisfies $\Psi$. Return that root using $H_{\mathrm{sf}}$ and its isolating interval.

All product-polynomial sizes remain polynomial in the univariate input parameters. Indeed, if the $p_i$ have degree at most $d$ and coefficient bit size at most $B$, then

$$ \deg H\le sd, \qquad \log_2\|H\|_1 \le s\bigl(B+\log_2(d+1)\bigr), $$

using multiplicativity of the coefficient $\ell^1$-norm bound. Therefore the extraction stage is polynomial in the quantifier-elimination output parameters.

Apply this procedure to the $N$ strategy-coordinate formulas. Their roots are coordinates of one common lexicographically least strong profile, so assembling them produces a valid exact profile. The total bit time is

$$ L^{O(1)}(N+2)^{O(N^3)}. $$

8. Conclusion

The two reductions establish hardness directly for the specified nonatomic deviations, including partial-type coalitions and strict profitability. The exact algorithm uses compactness, quantifier elimination with controlled integer sizes, and univariate root isolation.

Therefore existence is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard, while decision and exact computation are polynomial in $L$ when both $m$ and $\tau$ are fixed. No membership claim in $\mathrm{NP}$ is needed or asserted.
