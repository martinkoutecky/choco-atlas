The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite loopless weighted type graph \(G=(T,E;w)\), with positive rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\) and positive rational edge weights, define each coalition by a mass vector \(q\in[0,\mu]\) and its profit by \(v(q)=\max\{\sum_{e\in E} w_e y_e : y_e\ge 0,\ \sum_{e\ni t}y_e\le q_t\text{ for every }t\}\). Does there exist a per-capita allocation function \(a_t(q)\), for every nonzero \(q\), such that \(\sum_{t:q_t>0}q_ta_t(q)=v(q)\) and \(a_t(q)\le a_t(q')\) whenever \(q\le q'\) and \(q_t>0\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains a simple undirected loopless graph \(G=(T,E;w)\) with positive binary rational edge weights. Set \(m=|E|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; express running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Coalitions are all real vectors \(q\in\prod_{t\in T}[0,\mu_t]\), with profit \(v(q)=\max\{\sum_{e\in E}w_ey_e:y\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E},\ \sum_{e\ni t}y_e\le q_t\text{ for every }t\in T\}\).
A population monotonic per-capita allocation scheme assigns a real number \(a_t(q)\) whenever \(q\ne0\) and \(q_t>0\), satisfying \(\sum_{t:q_t>0}q_ta_t(q)=v(q)\) and \(a_t(q)\le a_t(q')\) whenever \(q\le q'\) coordinatewise and \(q_t>0\). No continuity or measurability is required.

Is deciding whether such a scheme exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite players whose coalition profit is the maximum weight of an ordinary matching in the induced graph, existence of a population monotonic allocation scheme is decidable in polynomial time. [Xiao and Fang](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10878-021-00804-3).
No result settling this exact continuous decision problem was located in the literature search.

  • Specified a simple undirected graph, as in the discrete definition; isolated types and an empty edge set remain allowed.
  • Specified real coalition masses and real allocation values, with allocations defined only for types present in the coalition and no additional regularity requirement.
  • Defined m as the number of edges and L as the total input bit length.

Result

Under the monotonicity condition as stated, a scheme exists if and only if \(m=0\). Consequently, the decision problem is solvable in \(O(L+m+\tau)\) time, including reading the input.

The decisive requirement is monotonicity when a type’s own mass increases. The impossibility proof below uses only four coalitions supported on the endpoints of one edge.

1. Homogeneity forces constant allocations along rays

For every coalition \(q\) and scalar \(c>0\) such that \(cq\) is within the prescribed box, the matching linear program satisfies

\(v(cq)=c\,v(q).\)

Indeed, multiplication by \(c\) gives a bijection between the feasible edge vectors for \(q\) and those for \(cq\), multiplying their objective values by \(c\).

Suppose a scheme exists. Fix a nonzero coalition \(q\) and \(0<c<1\). Monotonicity gives

\(a_t(cq)\le a_t(q)\qquad\text{for every }t\text{ with }q_t>0.\)

Efficiency at \(cq\), divided by \(c\), and efficiency at \(q\) give

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t:q_t>0}q_ta_t(cq)=\frac{v(cq)}{c}=v(q)=\sum_{t:q_t>0}q_ta_t(q).\)

Thus

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t:q_t>0}q_t\bigl(a_t(q)-a_t(cq)\bigr)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative, and every coefficient \(q_t\) in this sum is strictly positive. Therefore

\(a_t(cq)=a_t(q)\qquad\text{whenever }q_t>0.\)

In particular, if another coalition \(z\) satisfies \(cq\le z\le q\), monotonicity yields

\(a_t(cq)\le a_t(z)\le a_t(q),\)

so \(a_t(z)=a_t(q)\) for every type in the support of \(q\).

2. Any edge gives a contradiction

Assume that \(ij\in E\), and write \(w=w_{ij}>0\). Set

\(r=\min\{\mu_i,\mu_j\}>0.\)

Consider the following four coalitions, with every coordinate other than \(i,j\) equal to zero:

| Coalition | Coordinate \(i\) | Coordinate \(j\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(q\) | \(r\) | \(r\) |
| \(p\) | \(r/2\) | \(r/2\) |
| \(u\) | \(r\) | \(r/2\) |
| \(z\) | \(r/2\) | \(r\) |

All four are in the allowed box. Because any edge with an endpoint outside \(\{i,j\}\) has capacity zero, and the graph is simple and loopless, their profits are

\(v(q)=wr,\qquad v(p)=v(u)=v(z)=wr/2.\)

Let \(A=a_i(q)\) and \(B=a_j(q)\). Since \(p=q/2\), the preceding result gives the same allocations \(A,B\) at \(p\). Moreover,

\(p\le u\le q,\qquad p\le z\le q.\)

The sandwich conclusion therefore forces allocations \(A,B\) at both \(u\) and \(z\) as well.

Efficiency at \(q,u,z\), respectively, now gives

\(A+B=w,\)

\(A+\tfrac12 B=\tfrac12 w,\)

\(\tfrac12 A+B=\tfrac12 w.\)

Subtracting half the first equation from the second yields \(A/2=0\). Subtracting half the first equation from the third yields \(B/2=0\). Hence \(A=B=0\), contradicting \(A+B=w>0\).

Thus no graph containing an edge admits the required scheme. This proof permits arbitrary real allocations and uses no continuity or measurability assumption.

3. Sufficiency and running time

If \(m=0\), then \(v(q)=0\) for every coalition. Defining

\(a_t(q)=0\qquad\text{whenever }q\ne0\text{ and }q_t>0\)

satisfies efficiency and monotonicity.

The algorithm therefore accepts exactly when the edge list is empty. Reading the input and checking this condition takes \(O(L+m+\tau)\) time; no linear programs need to be solved.

The [finite-player result of Xiao and Fang](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10878-021-00804-3) concerns changes in coalition membership. The obstruction here arises from comparisons between different positive masses on the same support, which the stated continuous model explicitly requires.

Verdict

The prover fully resolves the stated continuous decision problem. The characterization “a scheme exists if and only if \(m=0\)” is correct, and the claimed \(O(L+m+\tau)\) running time is valid. No mathematical gaps or false steps were found.

Step-by-step verification

  • Homogeneity. For every \(c>0\) with \(cq\) in the allowed box, scaling an edge vector by \(c\) maps feasibility at \(q\) to feasibility at \(cq\). Scaling by \(1/c\) gives the inverse map, and the objective scales by \(c\). Thus \(v(cq)=cv(q)\), as claimed.
  • Constancy along rays. For nonzero \(q\) and \(0<c<1\), both coalitions are admissible and have identical support. Consequently, every allocation appearing in the argument is defined. Monotonicity gives \(a_t(cq)\le a_t(q)\). Efficiency and homogeneity imply

\[ \sum_{t:q_t>0}q_t\bigl(a_t(q)-a_t(cq)\bigr)=0. \]
This finite sum has nonnegative terms and strictly positive coefficients. Therefore every allocation difference vanishes. This reasoning remains valid when allocations are arbitrary real numbers.

  • Sandwich conclusion. If \(cq\le z\le q\), every type in the support of \(q\) has positive mass at \(z\), since \(z_t\ge cq_t>0\). Both monotonicity comparisons are therefore legitimate. Their equal endpoints force \(a_t(z)=a_t(q)\).
  • Admissibility of the four coalitions. Positive support masses ensure \(r=\min\{\mu_i,\mu_j\}>0\). Every listed coordinate is at most its corresponding population mass. All four coalitions are nonzero, with support exactly \(\{i,j\}\).
  • Profit calculations. Nonnegativity and zero capacity at every other vertex force all edges incident to those vertices to carry zero flow. Simplicity and looplessness leave exactly the edge \(ij\) available. Its feasible flow is bounded by the smaller endpoint mass, and its positive weight makes that bound optimal. Hence the four stated profits are correct.
  • Contradiction. Ray constancy identifies allocations at \(p\) and \(q\); the sandwich conclusion identifies those at \(u\) and \(z\). Dividing the three efficiency equations by \(r>0\) gives precisely

\[ A+B=w,\qquad A+\tfrac12B=\tfrac12w,\qquad \tfrac12A+B=\tfrac12w. \]
The stated subtractions correctly yield \(A=B=0\), contradicting \(w>0\). Thus every graph containing an edge is excluded.

  • Sufficiency and edge cases. When \(m=0\), the profit is zero for every coalition, and the proposed zero allocation satisfies both requirements on its entire required domain. The zero coalition requires no allocation. The case \(\tau=1\) is covered because a simple loopless graph on one vertex has no edges.
  • Complexity and scope. Checking whether the input edge list is empty requires no optimization or rational arithmetic. Including input reading, \(O(L+m+\tau)\) is a valid bit-time upper bound. The argument uses exactly the stated monotonicity requirement, including increases in a type’s own positive mass; it introduces no continuity or measurability assumption.

The comparison with finite matching games is consistent with the cited paper’s definition of coalitions as sets of vertices. No theorem from that paper is used in the continuous proof.
