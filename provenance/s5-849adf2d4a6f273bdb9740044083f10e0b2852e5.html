The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 3 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of expert roles \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\), represent each role by an interval \(I_t\) of length \(\mu_t\). Each role has an independent, homogeneous signal measure \(W_t\) with \(\operatorname{Var}(W_t(A))=(v_t/\mu_t)\lambda(A)\) for measurable \(A\subseteq I_t\), where \(v_t>0\). The target is \(Y=\sum_t W_t(I_t)\). Before observing signal realizations, choose measurable subsets \(A_t\subseteq I_t\) with \(\sum_t\lambda(A_t)\le\delta\), observe their reports \(W_t(A_t)\), and estimate \(Y\). What is the minimum, over randomized selection policies and estimators based on the reports, of the worst-case normalized squared error \(\sup_{v\ne0}\mathbb{E}[(\hat Y-Y)^2]/\sum_t v_t\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains binary rationals \(0\leq\delta<1\) and \(0\leq r\leq1\); \(L\) is the total input bit length. State running times in \(\tau,L\); there is no separate universe-size parameter \(m\).
Represent type \(t\) by a disjoint interval \(I_t\) of length \(\mu_t\). For each \(v\in(0,\infty)^T\), let \(\mathcal D(v)\) contain all laws of mutually independent, centered, homogeneous, independently scattered random measures \(W_t\), countably additive in \(L^2\), with \(\operatorname{Var}(W_t(B))=(v_t/\mu_t)\lambda(B)\). Homogeneity means increment laws depend only on Lebesgue measure; disjoint increments are independent.
Set \(Y=\sum_tW_t(I_t)\). A policy draws a random seed \(Z\), independent of the signals, and chooses measurable \(A_t\subseteq I_t\) before observing any reports, with \(\sum_t\lambda(A_t)\leq\delta\) almost surely.
It receives exactly the reports \(W_t(A_t)\) and outputs an arbitrary measurable estimator \(f_Z((W_t(A_t))_{t\in T})\). The selections and estimator may depend on the input and seed, but not on \(v\) or the signal law.
Define \(E(\mu,\delta)=\inf_{\pi,f}\sup_{v\in(0,\infty)^T}\sup_{\mathcal D\in\mathcal D(v)}\mathbb E_{Z,\mathcal D}[(f_Z((W_t(A_t))_t)-Y)^2]/\sum_tv_t\).

What is the sharp minimax normalized error \(E(\mu,\delta)\)? Is deciding whether \(E(\mu,\delta)\leq r\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For \(n\) discrete experts observing overlapping independent centered signals, the optimal worst-case normalized error under an ex-ante randomized budget of \(d<n\) DAG-elicitable queries is exactly \(1-d/n\). [Frongillo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05271v1)
No result settling this exact random-measure observation problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one and restricted the rational mass budget to 0<=delta<1, mirroring the discrete restriction d<n.
  • Interpreted independent homogeneous signal measures as centered, independently scattered random measures, homogeneous in increment law and countably additive in L2.
  • Added a supremum over all admissible signal laws because variances alone do not specify the risk of an arbitrary nonlinear estimator; this preserves the discrete distribution-robust model without imposing Gaussianity.
  • Made both selection and estimator independent of the unknown variance vector and signal law, while allowing dependence on the realized ex-ante random seed.
  • Required the budget in every realization of the policy, rather than merely in expectation.
  • Used an infimum without presupposing attainment, and introduced a binary-rational error threshold to define the computational decision problem.
  • Used tau and total input bit length L as size parameters because this model has no separate universe parameter m.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The sharp minimax normalized error is
\[ E(\mu,\delta)=1-\delta. \]
Consequently, deciding whether \(E(\mu,\delta)\leq r\) is in \(\mathrm P\). With ordinary binary numerator–denominator encodings, it takes \(O(\tau+L^2)\) bit operations.

The essential feature is that each type’s interval can be partially observed: a policy can observe exactly the fraction \(\delta\) of every type.

1. Error decomposition for any policy

Fix an admissible policy, a variance vector \(v\), and a law in \(\mathcal D(v)\). Write
\[ a_t(Z)=\lambda(A_t(Z)),\qquad X_t=W_t(A_t(Z)),\qquad R_t=W_t(I_t\setminus A_t(Z)). \]
By additivity,
\[ Y=S+R,\qquad S=\sum_tX_t,\qquad R=\sum_tR_t. \]

Condition on the seed \(Z=z\). The selected sets are now deterministic. Independent scattering within each type, together with independence across types, implies that the residual vector \((R_t)_t\) is independent of the entire report vector \((X_t)_t\). Each residual is centered, and
\[ \operatorname{Var}(R_t\mid Z=z) =\frac{v_t}{\mu_t}\bigl(\mu_t-a_t(z)\bigr). \]
Therefore,
\[ \mathbb E[R\mid Z,(X_t)_t]=0, \qquad \mathbb E[R^2\mid Z] =\sum_t v_t\left(1-\frac{a_t(Z)}{\mu_t}\right). \]

Let \(F=f_Z((X_t)_t)\). If its squared-error risk is infinite, the lower bounds below hold automatically. Otherwise \(F-S\) is square-integrable, and the displayed conditional mean gives \(\mathbb E[(F-S)R]=0\). Expanding the square yields the exact identity
\[ \mathbb E[(F-Y)^2] = \mathbb E[(F-S)^2] + \sum_t v_t\left(1-\frac{\mathbb E[a_t(Z)]}{\mu_t}\right). \]

Thus, for every selection policy, summing the reports minimizes squared error simultaneously for every allowed variance vector and signal law. An arbitrary nonlinear estimator cannot improve on this sum.

2. Achievable upper bound

Choose deterministic measurable sets satisfying
\[ \lambda(A_t)=\delta\mu_t \quad\text{for every }t, \]
for example initial subintervals of the prescribed lengths. Their total mass is
\[ \sum_t\lambda(A_t)=\delta\sum_t\mu_t=\delta, \]
so the policy is admissible. Output the sum of the reports.

The error decomposition then gives, for every \(v\) and every law in \(\mathcal D(v)\),
\[ \mathbb E[(S-Y)^2]=(1-\delta)\sum_t v_t. \]
Hence
\[ E(\mu,\delta)\leq1-\delta. \]

3. Matching lower bound

Consider any admissible policy and estimator. The adversary may choose
\[ v_t=\mu_t \quad\text{for every }t. \]
This vector is strictly positive and satisfies \(\sum_t v_t=1\).

The corresponding class of signal laws is nonempty. For example, independent centered Gaussian random measures with control measure \(\lambda\) satisfy the requirements: their covariance is \(\lambda(B\cap C)\), disjoint increments are independent because they are jointly Gaussian with zero covariance, and increment distributions depend only on set length. Countable additivity in \(L^2\) follows from convergence of the indicators of finite disjoint unions in \(L^2(\lambda)\).

Applying the error decomposition under this variance vector gives
\[ \mathbb E[(F-Y)^2] \geq \sum_t\mu_t\left(1-\frac{\mathbb E[a_t(Z)]}{\mu_t}\right) = 1-\mathbb E\left[\sum_t a_t(Z)\right]. \]
The almost-sure budget constraint implies
\[ \mathbb E\left[\sum_t a_t(Z)\right]\leq\delta. \]
Since the normalization denominator is \(1\), every admissible policy has worst-case normalized error at least \(1-\delta\). Consequently,
\[ E(\mu,\delta)\geq1-\delta. \]

Together with the upper bound, this proves the stated formula.

4. Exact decision complexity

The decision condition is simply
\[ E(\mu,\delta)\leq r \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \delta+r\geq1. \]

For encodings \(\delta=p/q\) and \(r=a/b\), with positive denominators, test
\[ pb+aq\geq qb. \]
All operands have \(O(L)\) bits. Schoolbook integer arithmetic therefore gives an \(O(\tau+L^2)\) bit-time algorithm, including reading the input. No universe-size parameter is required.
