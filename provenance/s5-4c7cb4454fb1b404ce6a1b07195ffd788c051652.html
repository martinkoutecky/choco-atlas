The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_a,\mu_b,\mu_c>0\) summing to \(1\), and rational intrinsic powers \(v_a\ge v_b\ge v_c\ge0\), let an atomless population \((\Omega,\lambda)\) be partitioned into three types of those masses. Feasible allocations are measurable \(x:\Omega\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) with \(\int_\Omega x\,d\lambda=1\); coalition \(A\) has power \(\Pi(A,x)=\int_A(x(\omega)+v_{\tau(\omega)})\,d\lambda\). Using the paper’s gainers-versus-losers dominance relation for measurable coalitions, does this game admit a von Neumann–Morgenstern stable set?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here \(\tau=3\), there is \(m=1\) resource, and \(L\) is the total input bit length.
The input consists of \(\mu_a,\mu_b,\mu_c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) with \(\mu_a+\mu_b+\mu_c=1\), and \(v_a,v_b,v_c\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) with \(v_a\ge v_b\ge v_c\).
Use the Lebesgue probability space \(\Omega=[0,1]\), partitioned into consecutive intervals of these masses, with type map \(t:\Omega\to\{a,b,c\}\).
Let \(X=\{x\in L^1(\Omega):x\ge0\text{ almost everywhere},\ \int_\Omega x\,d\lambda=1\}\), identifying allocations that agree almost everywhere; allocations may vary within types.
For any measurable coalition \(A\), define \(\Pi(A,x)=\int_A(x(\omega)+v_{t(\omega)})\,d\lambda\).
For \(x,y\in X\), put \(W=\{\omega:y(\omega)>x(\omega)\}\) and \(D=\{\omega:x(\omega)>y(\omega)\}\); define \(y\succ x\) exactly when \(\Pi(W,x)>\Pi(D,x)\). Equal power gives no dominance, and indifferent individuals belong to neither coalition.
A von Neumann–Morgenstern stable set is an arbitrary subset \(S\subseteq X\) with no dominance between its members and with every \(x\in X\setminus S\) dominated by some \(y\in S\).

Is deciding whether this game admits a von Neumann–Morgenstern stable set in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, with running time measured in \(m,\tau,L\) (here \(m=1\) and \(\tau=3\))?

For three discrete agents with \(v_a\ge v_b\ge v_c\ge0\) and empty core, equivalently \(v_a<v_b+v_c-1\), existence is decided by \(v_a-v_c\le\tfrac12\); when it exists, the unique stable set consists of the three allocations splitting the resource equally between two agents. This gives a polynomial-time decision procedure on rational inputs. [Verified later version](https://www.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/Common/publication/DP/DPS-A738.pdf).
No result settling the exact atomless decision problem above was found in the literature search.

  • Allocations are identified almost everywhere; feasible allocations are all nonnegative integrable densities of unit integral, with no requirement of constancy within types.
  • Dominance uses the entire strict gainer and loser coalitions, evaluated at the allocation being dominated; indifferent individuals are excluded and power ties do not dominate.
  • Stable sets may be arbitrary subsets of the allocation space, without additional finiteness, closure, or measurability requirements.

Partial results for the exact atomless game

The decision question remains unresolved. The main additional result below concerns the boundary
$$ V:=\mu_a v_a+\mu_b v_b+\mu_c v_c=1. $$
At this boundary, any stable set must exhibit concentration of squared allocations on arbitrarily small sets. Consequently, it cannot be bounded in $L^p$ for any $p>2$, or compact in $L^2$.

I also construct, for every permitted input, an uncountable internally stable family that fails uniform integrability. This demonstrates why restrictions on finite or uniformly integrable families do not alone settle existence.

1. Dominance and the empty core

Write $v(\omega)=v_{t(\omega)}$. For $x,y\in X$, define
$$ \Delta(y,x)=\int_\Omega \operatorname{sgn}(y-x)(x+v)\,d\lambda, $$
where $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$. Since $x$ is integrable and $v$ is bounded, this integral is finite. The specified winning, losing, and indifferent coalitions give
$$ y\succ x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Delta(y,x)>0. $$
Moreover,
$$ \Delta(y,x)+\Delta(x,y) =\int_\Omega\operatorname{sgn}(y-x)(x-y)\,d\lambda =-\|y-x\|_1. $$

For completeness, every allocation is dominated. Fix $x\in X$, put $H=\{x>0\}$, and define
$$ \nu(A)=\int_A(x+v)\,d\lambda. $$
We have $\nu(H)\geq1$. The function
$$ u\longmapsto \nu(H\cap[0,u]) $$
is continuous, by absolute continuity of the integral, and has endpoint values $0$ and $\nu(H)$. Hence there exists $B\subseteq H$ with
$$ \nu(B)=\frac14\nu(H). $$
Both $B$ and $H\setminus B$ have positive Lebesgue measure. Since $x>0$ on $H$, also $\int_Bx>0$. Set
$$ r=\frac12\int_Bx\,d\lambda,\qquad y=x-\frac12x\mathbf1_B+ \frac{r}{\lambda(H\setminus B)}\mathbf1_{H\setminus B}. $$
Then $y\geq0$ and $\int y=1$. Its winning and losing coalitions against $x$ are respectively $H\setminus B$ and $B$, up to null sets. Therefore
$$ \Delta(y,x)=\nu(H)-2\nu(B)=\frac12\nu(H)>0. $$

Thus the core is empty for every permitted input. This does not by itself preclude a stable set.

2. A quantitative concentration requirement

Let $T$ be one of the three type intervals, and let its intrinsic power be $c\geq0$. For measurable $B\subseteq T$ of measure $\beta>0$, define
$$ x_B=\frac1\beta\mathbf1_B. $$

Proposition. Suppose $V\leq1$ and $S$ is a stable set. Then
$$ \sup_{y\in S}\int_B y\,d\lambda \geq \frac{1-V+2c\beta}{2(1+c\beta)} $$
and
$$ \sup_{y\in S}\int_B y^2\,d\lambda \geq \frac{1-V+2c\beta}{2\beta(1+c\beta)}. $$
The squared integrals are allowed to be infinite.

Proof. Put
$$ q=\frac{1-V+2c\beta}{2(1+c\beta)}. $$
Because $0\leq V\leq1$, we have $0\leq q<1$.

If $x_B\in S$, its integrals over $B$ are $1$ and $1/\beta$, which establish both bounds.

Otherwise, external stability supplies $y\in S$ with $\Delta(y,x_B)>0$. Define
$$ C=B\cap\{y\geq\beta^{-1}\},\qquad z=\frac{\lambda(C)}{\beta}. $$
Including equality points in $C$ ensures that the losing coalition is exactly $B\setminus C$. The winning coalition intersects $B$ only inside $C$. Thus its resource power is at most $z$, while losing resource power is $1-z$.

Winning intrinsic power is at most
$$ \int_{\Omega\setminus B}v\,d\lambda+c\lambda(C) =V-c\beta+c\beta z. $$
Losing intrinsic power is exactly
$$ c\lambda(B\setminus C)=c\beta(1-z). $$
Consequently,
$$ \Delta(y,x_B) \leq V-1-2c\beta+2(1+c\beta)z. $$
Strict dominance implies $z>q$. On $C$, we have $y\geq1/\beta$, so
$$ \int_B y\,d\lambda\geq z>q, \qquad \int_B y^2\,d\lambda\geq\frac{z}{\beta}>\frac q\beta. $$
This proves the proposition. $\square$

Consequence below the boundary

Call $S$ uniformly integrable if
$$ \lim_{\delta\downarrow0} \sup_{\substack{y\in S\\ B\text{ measurable}\\\lambda(B)\leq\delta}} \int_B y\,d\lambda=0. $$
When $V<1$, choose arbitrarily small sets $B$ inside a fixed type interval. The first bound in the proposition tends to
$$ \frac{1-V}{2}>0. $$
Thus no stable set is uniformly integrable.

In particular, no stable set is compact in $L^1$. Indeed, a compact subset of $L^1$ has, for every $\varepsilon>0$, a finite $\varepsilon/2$-net. Absolute continuity of the integrals of its finitely many net points, together with
$$ \int_B y\,d\lambda \leq \|y-y_i\|_1+\int_B y_i\,d\lambda, $$
establishes uniform integrability.

3. A boundary obstruction when $V=1$

Suppose now that $V=1$. At least one type interval has intrinsic power $c>0$, because all masses are positive and all intrinsic powers are nonnegative.

For every measurable $B$ in such a type interval, the second bound becomes
$$ \sup_{y\in S}\int_B y^2\,d\lambda \geq\frac{c}{1+c\lambda(B)}. $$
The right-hand side tends to $c$, rather than to zero, as $\lambda(B)$ tends to zero.

More precisely, for every $\delta>0$,
$$ \sup_{\substack{y\in S\\B\subseteq T\\0<\lambda(B)\leq\delta}} \int_B y^2\,d\lambda\geq c. $$
To verify this, take sets $B\subseteq T$ of positive measures tending to zero and apply the preceding bound.

Therefore, when $V=1$, the family $\{y^2:y\in S\}$ cannot be uniformly integrable on any type interval of positive intrinsic power.

This gives two further consequences.

No uniform $L^p$ bound for $p>2$

Suppose, to the contrary, that for some $p>2$ and finite $K$,
$$ \|y\|_p\leq K\qquad\text{for every }y\in S. $$
Hölder’s inequality gives, for every measurable $B$,
$$ \int_B y^2\,d\lambda \leq \left(\int_B y^p\,d\lambda\right)^{2/p} \lambda(B)^{1-2/p} \leq K^2\lambda(B)^{1-2/p}. $$
Since $1-2/p>0$, this bound tends uniformly to zero as $\lambda(B)$ tends to zero. It contradicts the established boundary obstruction.

Hence, at $V=1$, no stable set is bounded in $L^p$ for any $p>2$.

No compact stable set in $L^2$

Suppose $S\subseteq L^2$ were compact in the $L^2$ norm. Fix $\eta>0$ and choose a finite net $y_1,\ldots,y_N\in S$ with radius less than $\sqrt{\eta/4}$.

Absolute continuity of the finitely many integrals $\int_B y_i^2$ supplies $\delta>0$ such that each is less than $\eta/4$ when $\lambda(B)\leq\delta$. For any $y\in S$, choosing an appropriate net point and using $y^2\leq2y_i^2+2(y-y_i)^2$ yields
$$ \int_B y^2\,d\lambda \leq2\int_B y_i^2\,d\lambda+2\|y-y_i\|_2^2 <\eta. $$
Thus the squares would be uniformly integrable, again a contradiction.

This argument does not rule out stable sets merely bounded in $L^2$.

4. No local uniform pointwise bound when $V\leq1$

Proposition. Suppose $V\leq1$ and $S$ is stable. For every measurable $E$ of positive measure and every finite $K\geq0$, some $y\in S$ satisfies
$$ \lambda(E\cap\{y>K\})>0. $$

Proof. Assume instead that every $y\in S$ satisfies $y\leq K$ almost everywhere on $E$. Choose a measurable $B\subseteq E$ with
$$ 0<\beta=\lambda(B)<\frac1{K+1}, $$
and set $x=x_B$. Then $x>K$ throughout $B$, so $x\notin S$.

For every $y\in S$, the losing coalition against $x$ is $B$, while the winning coalition lies outside $B$. Winning resource power is zero, and losing resource power is one. Therefore
$$ \Delta(y,x) \leq V-1-2\int_Bv\,d\lambda \leq0. $$
No member of $S$ dominates $x$, contradicting external stability. $\square$

In particular, for $V\leq1$, no finite stable set exists: if $S=\{y_1,\ldots,y_n\}$ and $K>n$, then
$$ \lambda\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^n\{y_i>K\}\right) \leq\frac nK<1, $$
using $\int y_i=1$. On the positive-measure complement, all members have the forbidden common bound $K$.

5. Uncountable internally stable families exist for every input

The preceding restrictions do not imply that internally stable families must be small or uniformly integrable.

Choose a type interval of intrinsic power $c$, and an interval $H$ inside it of measure $\alpha>0$ such that
$$ \alpha<1,\qquad c\alpha<1. $$
This is possible because the type has positive mass and $c$ is finite. For each integer $n\geq0$, put
$$ \beta_n=\frac{\alpha}{2^n}, $$
and define
$$ \mathcal F= \left\{ \frac1{\beta_n}\mathbf1_B: n\geq0,\ B\subseteq H\text{ measurable},\ \lambda(B)=\beta_n \right\}. $$
Every member belongs to $X$.

Proposition. The family $\mathcal F$ is internally stable.

Proof. First consider allocations at the same level:
$$ x=k\mathbf1_C,\qquad y=k\mathbf1_B,\qquad \lambda(B)=\lambda(C)=\frac1k. $$
Writing $d=\lambda(B\cap C)$, the winning and losing coalitions have equal intrinsic power, and
$$ \Delta(y,x)=-k\left(\frac1k-d\right)\leq0. $$
The reverse direction has the same value.

Next consider different levels, with
$$ x=l\mathbf1_C,\qquad y=k\mathbf1_B,\qquad \gamma=\lambda(C)=\frac1l,\qquad \beta=\lambda(B)=\frac1k, $$
where $k>l$. The dyadic choice implies $\beta\leq\gamma/2$. Put $d=\lambda(B\cap C)$.

Against $x$, the allocation $y$ wins on $B$ and loses on $C\setminus B$. Hence
$$ \Delta(y,x)=2ld-1+c(\beta-\gamma+d). $$
Since $d\leq\beta$,
$$ \Delta(y,x) \leq2l\beta-1+c(2\beta-\gamma)\leq0. $$
For the reverse comparison, $x$ wins on $C\setminus B$ and loses on all of $B$. Thus
$$ \Delta(x,y)=-1+c(\gamma-\beta-d) \leq-1+c\alpha<0. $$
Neither allocation dominates the other. $\square$

The family is uncountable: already at level $n=1$, translating an interval of length $\alpha/2$ inside $H$ gives uncountably many distinct allocations modulo null sets.

It also fails uniform integrability. Indeed, $\beta_n$ tends to zero, while for every level-$n$ member supported on $B$,
$$ \int_B\frac1{\beta_n}\mathbf1_B\,d\lambda=1. $$

However, $\mathcal F$ is not externally stable. Choose a positive-measure set $A\subseteq\Omega\setminus H$ and let
$$ x=\frac1{\lambda(A)}\mathbf1_A. $$
For $y=\beta_n^{-1}\mathbf1_B\in\mathcal F$, the supports are disjoint, so
$$ \Delta(y,x)=c\beta_n-1-\int_Av\,d\lambda \leq c\alpha-1<0. $$
Thus $x\notin\mathcal F$ is dominated by no member of $\mathcal F$.

6. What remains unresolved

The boundary $V=1$ now has a concentration obstruction beyond those established in the notes: stable sets must fail uniform integrability of their squares, and cannot be bounded in $L^p$ for $p>2$ or compact in $L^2$.

Nevertheless, none of these arguments decides existence of arbitrary infinite stable sets. The construction in Section 5 confirms that large, non-uniformly-integrable internally stable families are available for every input; achieving external stability remains the missing step.

No polynomial-time decision procedure or hardness reduction follows from these results.
