The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) alternatives, a finite support \(T\) of strict rankings with rational masses \(\mu_t\geq 0\) summing to \(1\), and rational thresholds \(\theta_c^k\), each type assigns its mass to its highest-ranked surviving alternative at round \(k\). Let \(q_c^k\) be the total mass assigned to \(c\), and eliminate each surviving \(c\) with \(q_c^k<\theta_c^k\). Under the condition that the surviving thresholds sum to more than \(1\) at every nonterminal round, compute the exact sequence of survivor sets and vote masses through termination, including the number of rounds.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq2\) alternatives. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) fixed strict rankings of \(C\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
The input also contains an explicit table of nonnegative binary-rational thresholds \(\theta_c^k\) for \(c\in C\) and \(1\leq k\leq m\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Start with \(S_1=C\). Whenever \(|S_k|\geq2\), each type assigns its entire mass to its highest-ranked member of \(S_k\); denote the resulting mass at \(c\) by \(q_c^k\). Simultaneously set \(S_{k+1}=\{c\in S_k:q_c^k\geq\theta_c^k\}\).
Promise that at every executed round, \(\sum_{c\in S_k}\theta_c^k>1\) and \(\min_{c\in S_k}\theta_c^k\leq1\). Stop as soon as the survivor set has size at most one, including the possibility of no survivors.
The required output is the exact survivor sequence through the terminal set, every executed round's vote masses, and the number \(K\) of executed rounds.

Is computing this exact trajectory possible in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite voters with positive integer vote weights, thresholds whose sum exceeds the total vote weight force an elimination at every executed round and termination within \(m-1\) rounds ([discrete termination result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06189)); this is a termination result, not a stated complexity classification.
No result settling the exact mass-input problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Threshold encoding was unspecified; use an explicit binary-rational table for the first m rounds, rather than an oracle or an implicit updating rule.
  • Threshold signs were unspecified; interpret minimum required vote masses as nonnegative rational thresholds.
  • Carry over the discrete model's exclusion of the trivially forced empty outcome by requiring at least one surviving threshold to be at most total mass at every executed round.
  • Termination was unspecified; stop with zero or one survivors, count only executed voting rounds, and report vote masses only for those rounds.

Conclusion

The exact trajectory is computable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). The proof requires both a bound on the number of rounds and a bound on the bit length of the exact arithmetic. Both hold for the explicitly listed support.

1. Every executed round eliminates an alternative

Fix an executed round \(k\). Since \(S_k\) is nonempty and every ranking is strict, each type has exactly one highest-ranked alternative in \(S_k\). Its entire mass is assigned to that alternative. Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{c\in S_k}q_c^k=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1. \)

Suppose no alternative were eliminated. Then \(q_c^k\geq\theta_c^k\) for every \(c\in S_k\), implying

\( \displaystyle 1=\sum_{c\in S_k}q_c^k\geq\sum_{c\in S_k}\theta_c^k>1, \)

a contradiction. Thus \(S_{k+1}\) is a proper subset of \(S_k\).

Before the \(k\)-th executed round, therefore, \(|S_k|\leq m-k+1\). Since execution requires \(|S_k|\geq2\), every executed round satisfies \(k\leq m-1\). Hence the number of executed rounds obeys

\( \displaystyle K\leq m-1. \)

This proof allows the terminal set to be empty. The additional promise \(\min_{c\in S_k}\theta_c^k\leq1\) is not needed for the bound.

The argument is the mass-valued counterpart of the counting argument in Proposition 1 of the cited [discrete termination paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06189). The complexity analysis below is separate.

2. Exact arithmetic has polynomial bit length

Use the standard explicit binary encoding of rationals: write each mass as \(\mu_t=p_t/d_t\), with positive integers \(p_t,d_t\) represented in binary. Finite binary expansions can also be converted to this representation with polynomial overhead.

Define a common denominator and integer weights by

\( \displaystyle D=\prod_{t\in T}d_t,\qquad w_t=p_t(D/d_t). \)

The division is exact because \(d_t\) divides \(D\). Moreover,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}w_t=D\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=D. \)

The binary length of \(D\) is at most one plus the sum of the binary lengths of the denominators, hence is \(O(L)\). Each \(w_t\) is positive and at most \(D\), so it also has \(O(L)\) bits.

For any executed round and active alternative, define

\( \displaystyle W_c^k=\sum_{\substack{t\in T:\\ c\text{ is highest in }S_k\text{ for }t}}w_t. \)

Then \(q_c^k=W_c^k/D\), and \(0\leq W_c^k\leq D\). Thus every vote mass has an exact representation using \(O(L)\) bits, regardless of the round.

Write a threshold as \(\theta_c^k=a_c^k/b_c^k\), with \(a_c^k\geq0\) and \(b_c^k>0\). Its comparison with the vote mass is exactly

\( \displaystyle q_c^k\geq\theta_c^k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W_c^k b_c^k\geq a_c^kD. \)

Both products have \(O(L)\) bits. Integer addition, multiplication, exact division, and comparison on these operands take polynomial time. Therefore all required arithmetic is exact and has polynomial cost.

3. Algorithm and correctness

First compute \(D\) and the weights \(w_t\). Initialize \(S_1=C\) and the executed-round counter to zero.

For each round \(k\) with \(|S_k|\geq2\):

  • Initialize \(W_c^k=0\) for every \(c\in S_k\).
  • For each listed type \(t\), scan its ranking from the top until finding the first member \(c\) of \(S_k\), and add \(w_t\) to \(W_c^k\).
  • Record every mass \(q_c^k=W_c^k/D\).
  • Compute a fresh survivor set using the old round’s masses:

\( \displaystyle S_{k+1}=\{c\in S_k:W_c^k b_c^k\geq a_c^kD\}. \)

  • Record \(S_{k+1}\) and increment the executed-round counter.

Stop when the current survivor set has size at most one. Return the recorded sequence \(S_1,\ldots,S_{K+1}\), the recorded vote masses, and \(K\). If masses for eliminated alternatives are also desired, record zero for them.

Correctness follows by induction on the round index. The initialization gives the required \(S_1\). Assuming the algorithm has the correct \(S_k\), scanning a strict ranking finds precisely that type’s highest-ranked member of \(S_k\). Summing its integer weight therefore gives precisely \(Dq_c^k\). The cross-product comparison is equivalent to the prescribed threshold comparison. Since all comparisons use the completed mass vector before updating the survivor set, elimination is simultaneous. Hence the computed \(S_{k+1}\) is correct. The stopping condition and round counter then give the required terminal set and \(K\).

4. Running time and output size

Each round examines at most \(m\tau\) ranking entries, performs \(\tau\) weight additions, and makes at most \(m\) threshold comparisons. There are at most \(m-1\) rounds. Thus the algorithm examines \(O(m^2\tau)\) ranking entries and performs \(O(m\tau+m^2)\) arithmetic operations, in addition to polynomial-time preprocessing.

Every arithmetic operand has \(O(L)\) bits, and membership in the current survivor set can be maintained using an indexed Boolean array. These bounds yield polynomial total running time in \(m,\tau,L\).

There are at most \(m\) recorded survivor sets and \(m(m-1)\) recorded vote masses. Each mass requires \(O(L)\) bits, so the exact output also has polynomial length. If reduced fractions are required, reducing each recorded fraction by its greatest common divisor takes polynomial time.

Therefore the full requested trajectory, not merely its terminal survivor, is polynomial-time computable.
