The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(X=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), a finite set \(T\) of complete valuation types, rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), rational item supplies \(b_j\ge 0\), and capped-additive valuations \(v_t(S)=\min\{B_t,\sum_{j\in S}w_{tj}\}\), compute the maximum welfare over configuration masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq X}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T,\;S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le b_j\), where \(W(x)=\sum_{t\in T,S\subseteq X}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\); equivalently, decide whether some feasible \(x\) has \(W(x)\ge K\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions. Let \(X=\{1,\ldots,m\}\). Each type \(t\in T\) has rational mass \(\mu_t\), budget \(B_t\), and item weights \(w_{tj}\), with \(v_t(S)=\min\{B_t,\sum_{j\in S}w_{tj}\}\). Given rational supplies \(b_j\) and threshold \(K\), choose \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) such that \(\sum_{S\subseteq X}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le b_j\). Its welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\).

Target. Is deciding whether a feasible \(x\) with \(W(x)\ge K\) exists solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results. In the discrete indivisible-item setting, welfare maximization is NP-hard even for two budget-additive valuations. This problem instead permits divisible masses of each valuation type and rational item supplies.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with a single valuation type of mass \(1\) and every item supply equal to \(\tfrac12\).

Reduction. We reduce from the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem Partition: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\), encoded in binary, decide whether some subset has sum exactly half their total. Its \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness is a classical result of [Karp](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Write \(A=\sum_{j=1}^m a_j\). Construct an instance with one type \(t\), setting

\(\mu_t=1,\qquad w_{tj}=a_j,\qquad B_t=A/2,\qquad b_j=1/2,\qquad K=A/2.\)

These parameters have polynomial encoding length and are computable in polynomial time. Suppress the type index, and write \(B=A/2\), \(a(S)=\sum_{j\in S}a_j\), and \(x_S=x_{t,S}\).

A partition gives a feasible allocation reaching the threshold. Suppose \(S\subseteq X\) satisfies \(a(S)=B\). Its complement also satisfies \(a(X\setminus S)=B\). Assign mass \(\tfrac12\) to each of these two bundles and zero to all other bundles.

The total assigned mass is \(1\). Every item belongs to exactly one of the two bundles, so its consumption is exactly \(\tfrac12\), meeting its supply constraint. Both bundles have value \(B\), hence welfare is \(B=K\).

An allocation reaching the threshold gives a partition. Conversely, suppose a feasible allocation satisfies \(W(x)\ge B\). Since every bundle has value at most \(B\) and \(\sum_S x_S=1\), we have

\(W(x)=\sum_S x_S\min\{B,a(S)\}\le B.\)

Thus \(W(x)=B\). Consequently,

\(\sum_S x_S\bigl(B-\min\{B,a(S)\}\bigr)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore every bundle with \(x_S>0\) satisfies \(a(S)\ge B\).

On the other hand, the supply constraints and positivity of the weights give

\(\sum_S x_Sa(S)=\sum_{j=1}^m a_j\sum_{S\ni j}x_S\le \sum_{j=1}^m a_j/2=B.\)

Because all positive-mass bundles have weight at least \(B\), their average weight is at least \(B\). The preceding inequality forces that average to equal \(B\). Explicitly,

\(\sum_{S:x_S>0}x_S\bigl(a(S)-B\bigr)=0.\)

Again, every summand is nonnegative, so every positive-mass bundle has weight exactly \(B\). At least one such bundle exists because the total mass is \(1\). It supplies the required partition.

Conclusion. The constructed instance reaches its welfare threshold if and only if the original Partition instance is a yes-instance. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under polynomial-time many-one reductions, and rules out a polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). Fractional masses do not remove this obstruction: attaining the threshold forces exact equality in the weight of every bundle used.
