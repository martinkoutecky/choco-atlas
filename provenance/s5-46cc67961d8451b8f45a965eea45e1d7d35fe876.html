The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a campaign of \(N\) contributors with rational type masses \(\mu_t\), type \(t\) has maximum precision \(u_t\) and maximum-effort cost \(c_t(u_t)\). Given prior precision \(\xi_x\) and a target global precision \(\xi_x+N\delta\), choose recruited masses \(q_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) to minimize \(N\sum_t q_t c_t(u_t)\), subject to \(N\sum_t q_t u_t\geq N\delta\); determine whether a peer-payment mechanism can support truthful reporting and maximum effort for recruited contributors in equilibrium.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Each type is an atomless population; \(N\ge2\) is a binary-encoded integer scaling its mass.
Type \(t\) has binary-rational maximum precision \(u_t>0\) and a known, densely encoded rational polynomial cost \(c_t\) on \([0,u_t]\), with \(c_t(0)=0\), \(c_t\ge0\), and \(c_t'>0\). Let \(m\) be the maximum polynomial degree and \(L\) the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\). Assume \(-2c_t'(e)-(\xi_x+e)c_t''(e)\ge0\) for some type and effort.
Given binary-rational \(\xi_x>0\) and \(\delta>0\), minimize \(N\sum_tq_tc_t(u_t)\) over real recruited masses \(0\le q_t\le\mu_t\), subject to \(N\sum_tq_tu_t\ge N\delta\). Global precision is defined by the mass accounting rule \(\xi_x+N\sum_tq_tu_t\).
For incentives, \(X\sim\mathcal N(0,\xi_x^{-1})\); before observing data, each recruited individual chooses hidden effort \(e\in[0,u_t]\), then privately observes \(Y=X+V\), with \(V\sim\mathcal N(0,e^{-1})\), independently across individuals and of \(X\). Zero effort means no observation. Individuals may report arbitrary real measurement and local-estimate values; truthful values are \(Y\) and \(\mathbb E[X\mid Y]\).
An allowed payment is \(p=\gamma_t-\beta_t(\hat x^r-y_J^r)^2\), with rational \(\beta_t>0\) and \(\gamma_t\in\mathbb Q\); \(J\) is a distinct individual sampled from normalized recruited mass independently of reports and signals, possibly of the same type. Transfers may be negative; no ground truth or actual effort is observable.
Support means a Bayesian Nash equilibrium in which recruited individuals exert \(u_t\), report both values truthfully, and receive expected payment \(c_t(u_t)\), hence satisfy ex ante individual rationality with outside option zero. Truthfulness need not be strict or unique; unrecruited individuals exert zero effort and receive zero payment.

Is exact minimum-cost mass recruitment in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and does every feasible instance admit a minimizing recruitment supported by an allowed peer-payment mechanism?

For finite populations, maximum-effort peer payments are claimed to support truthful reporting and cost reimbursement in equilibrium; the associated binary recruitment problem is NP-hard and has pseudo-polynomial dynamic programming algorithms. Result

Exact minimum-cost mass recruitment is in \(\mathrm P\). Every feasible instance admits a rational minimizing recruitment supported by an allowed peer-payment mechanism.

The proof uses the stated mass-accounting definition of global precision. It applies even without the assumed failure of the curvature condition.

1. Exact optimal recruitment

Write
\[ \]
For each supported type, define \(C_t=c_t(u_t)\), \(b_t=\mu_tu_t\), and \(r_t=C_t/u_t\). Because \(c_t(0)=0\), \(c_t'>0\), and \(u_t>0\), we have \(C_t>0\).

Set \(x_t=q_tu_t\). Since \(N>0\), the recruitment problem becomes

\(\displaystyle \min N\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}r_tx_t,\qquad 0\le x_t\le b_t,\qquad \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}x_t\ge\delta.\)

Consequently, feasibility is equivalent to \(\delta\le\sum_t b_t\). Necessity follows from the upper bounds; sufficiency follows by recruiting every type fully.

Assume feasibility. Relabel the types so that \(r_1\le\cdots\le r_\tau\), breaking ties arbitrarily. Let \(k\) be the smallest index with \(\sum_{t=1}^{k}b_t\ge\delta\). Define

\(\displaystyle x_t^*=\begin{cases} b_t,&t<k,\\ \delta-\sum_{s<k}b_s,&t=k,\\ 0,&t>k, \end{cases} \qquad q_t^*=\frac{x_t^*}{u_t}.\)

This vector is feasible and satisfies \(\sum_t x_t^*=\delta\). All its entries are rational, and at most one type is partially recruited.

To prove optimality, put \(\lambda=r_k>0\). For any feasible vector \(x\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_t r_t(x_t-x_t^*) = \lambda\left(\sum_t x_t-\delta\right) +\sum_t(r_t-\lambda)(x_t-x_t^*). \)

Every contribution on the right is nonnegative:

  • The first term is nonnegative by feasibility.
  • For \(t<k\), both \(r_t-\lambda\le0\) and \(x_t-x_t^*=x_t-b_t\le0\).
  • For \(t=k\), the coefficient \(r_t-\lambda\) is zero.
  • For \(t>k\), both \(r_t-\lambda\ge0\) and \(x_t-x_t^*=x_t\ge0\).

Thus \(q^*\) minimizes the original objective. Its exact minimum value is

\(\displaystyle N\sum_t q_t^*C_t.\)

2. Rational peer-payment coefficients

Fix this rational minimizing recruitment. Define its total recruited mass and its average peer-noise variance by

\(\displaystyle Q=\sum_tq_t^*,\qquad A=\frac{1}{Q}\sum_t\frac{q_t^*}{u_t}.\)

Since \(\delta>0\), we have \(Q>0\). A sampled peer has type \(s\) with probability \(q_s^*/Q\), so \(A\) is precisely the expected variance of its measurement noise at maximum effort. Both \(Q\) and \(A\) are rational.

Write \(c_t(e)=\sum_{j=0}^{d_t}a_{tj}e^j\), where \(d_t\le m\), and define

\(\displaystyle H_t=\max\{1,u_t\},\qquad D_t=\sum_{j=1}^{d_t}j|a_{tj}|H_t^{j-1}.\)

For every \(e\in[0,u_t]\), the triangle inequality gives \(c_t'(e)\le D_t\). Choose

\(\displaystyle \beta_t=(\xi_x+u_t)^2D_t+1,\qquad \gamma_t=C_t+\beta_t\left(A+\frac{1}{\xi_x+u_t}\right).\)

These coefficients satisfy \(\beta_t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) and \(\gamma_t\in\mathbb Q\). Use the allowed payment

\(\displaystyle p_i=\gamma_t-\beta_t(\hat x_i^r-y_{J_i}^r)^2\)

for a recruited individual \(i\) of type \(t\).

Because the recruited population is atomless, every individual has zero mass. Excluding \(i\) from its peer draw therefore leaves the normalized peer distribution unchanged. A distinct peer exists even if recruitment uses only one supported type or \(NQ<1\).

3. Reporting and effort incentives

Consider the proposed profile in which every recruited individual exerts maximum effort and reports truthfully. Fix one recruited individual \(i\) of type \(t\), and hold every other individual’s strategy fixed at this profile.

Optimal reporting after arbitrary effort

Suppose \(i\) chooses effort \(e>0\) and observes \(Y_i=y\). Independence and the Gaussian densities imply that the posterior density of \(X\) is proportional to

\(\displaystyle \exp\left(-\frac12\bigl[\xi_xx^2+e(y-x)^2\bigr]\right).\)

Completing the square yields

\(\displaystyle h_e(y):=\mathbb E[X\mid Y_i=y]=\frac{e}{\xi_x+e}y,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X\mid Y_i=y)=\frac{1}{\xi_x+e}.\)

The truthful peer measurement is \(Y_{J_i}=X+V_{J_i}\). Its noise has conditional mean zero and average variance \(A\), independently of \(i\)’s signal. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[Y_{J_i}\mid Y_i=y]=h_e(y),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y_{J_i}\mid Y_i=y)=\frac{1}{\xi_x+e}+A. \)

For any reported local estimate \(z\in\mathbb R\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[(z-Y_{J_i})^2\mid Y_i=y] =(z-h_e(y))^2+\frac{1}{\xi_x+e}+A. \)

Thus reporting \(h_e(y)\) minimizes conditional expected squared loss. Randomizing the report cannot improve this minimum, because the additional squared term remains nonnegative.

At \(e=0\), the individual has no observation. The same conclusion holds with posterior mean zero and posterior variance \(1/\xi_x\).

The individual’s own measurement report does not enter its own payment: its peer is distinct, and peer selection is independent of reports. Consequently, truthful measurement reporting is a best response, although it is not strict.

Maximum effort is optimal

After optimal local-estimate reporting, the individual’s expected utility at effort \(e\in[0,u_t]\) is

\(\displaystyle F_t(e)=\gamma_t-\beta_t\left(A+\frac{1}{\xi_x+e}\right)-c_t(e).\)

Its derivative satisfies

\(\displaystyle F_t'(e) =\frac{\beta_t}{(\xi_x+e)^2}-c_t'(e) \ge \frac{(\xi_x+u_t)^2D_t+1}{(\xi_x+e)^2}-D_t \ge \frac{1}{(\xi_x+e)^2}>0. \)

Here the last inequality uses \(e\le u_t\). Hence \(F_t\) is strictly increasing and has its unique maximum at \(u_t\).

This also rules out arbitrary joint effort-and-report deviations. For each chosen effort, the reporting calculation bounds utility by \(F_t(e)\); mixing over efforts cannot exceed \(F_t(u_t)\). Reports with infinite expected squared loss have expected payment negative infinity and cannot improve utility.

Reimbursement and equilibrium

At maximum effort and truthful reporting,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E[p_i] =\gamma_t-\beta_t\left(A+\frac{1}{\xi_x+u_t}\right) =C_t. \)

Thus \(F_t(u_t)=0\), giving exact cost reimbursement and ex ante individual rationality. Declining participation also yields zero utility.

Unrecruited individuals receive zero payment. Since \(c_t(0)=0\) and \(c_t(e)>0\) for \(e>0\), zero effort is their best response.

These best-response checks establish the required Bayesian Nash equilibrium.

4. Bit complexity

The algorithm uses \(\mathrm O(\tau(m+1))\) rational operations to evaluate the costs and construct the payment coefficients, \(\mathrm O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) comparisons to sort the ratios, and \(\mathrm O(\tau)\) further operations for recruitment and reimbursement.

For an explicit conservative bit bound, put \(B=(m+1)(\tau+1)(L+1)\). All integers appearing in reduced rational computations have \(\mathrm O(B)\) bits:

  • Evaluating a degree-\(m\) polynomial at an input rational requires powers of exponent at most \(m\) and coefficient denominators whose total encoding length is at most \(L\).
  • Recruitment requires sums of at most \(\tau\) rational capacities and one division.
  • All but at most one recruited mass equal either zero or their input mass. Computing \(Q\), \(A\), and the objective therefore also has polynomially bounded rational encoding length.
  • The formulas for \(D_t\), \(\beta_t\), and \(\gamma_t\) use the same bounded powers and rational sums.

Using elementary integer arithmetic and fraction reduction gives the conservative running-time bound

\(\displaystyle \mathrm O\!\left( \bigl[\tau(m+1)+\tau\log(\tau+1)\bigr] \bigl[(m+1)(\tau+1)(L+1)\bigr]^3 \right). \)

This bound covers the exact minimizing masses, minimum value, and supporting rational payment coefficients. The binary-encoded \(N\) only scales the objective; its encoding length is already included in \(L\).

The cited finite-population formulation imposes binary recruitment decisions. Its stated knapsack hardness therefore does not transfer to the divisible mass problem proved here. [Discrete formulation, Section III](https://arxiv.org/html/1908.04780v1)
