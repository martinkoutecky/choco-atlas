The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\), \(\epsilon,\delta>0\), and a finite-support unit-mass society \(\mu\) over feasible metric voter types, can an algorithm estimate every fractional-matching tournament weight \(f_\mu(A,B)\) from samples of types so that \(\Pr[\max_{A\ne B}|\hat f(A,B)-f_\mu(A,B)|\le\epsilon]\ge 1-\delta\)? Each type fixes a complete ranking and its distances to the candidates; \(f_\mu\) uses the paper’s fractional matching score for opposing preference mass.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge2\) candidates. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type supplies a strict complete ranking and nonnegative binary-rational distances \(d_t(A)\), jointly realizable in one metric space, allowing co-location; rankings respect weak distance comparisons. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An independent sample draws a type according to \(\mu\) and reveals its ranking and distances. Repeated draws of the same type represent distinct portions of voter mass. Fix \(w=\sqrt3-1\) and a candidate order for resolving deliberation ties.
For distinct \(A,B\), let \(P_{AB}\) be the types ranking \(A\) above \(B\), and put \(p_{AB}=\sum_{t\in P_{AB}}\mu_t\). For \(u\in P_{AB},v\in P_{BA}\), let \(D_{AB}(u,v)\) indicate that \(d_u(A)+d_v(A)<d_u(B)+d_v(B)\), with equality awarded to the earlier candidate in the fixed order.
Define \(q_{AB}=\frac{\sum_{u\in P_{AB},v\in P_{BA}}\mu_u\mu_vD_{AB}(u,v)}{p_{AB}(1-p_{AB})}\) when \(0<p_{AB}<1\), and \(q_{AB}=0\) otherwise. The fractional-matching tournament weight is \(f_\mu(A,B)=\frac{p_{AB}+w\min\{p_{AB},1-p_{AB}\}q_{AB}}{1+w\min\{p_{AB},1-p_{AB}\}}\), with \(f_\mu(B,A)=1-f_\mu(A,B)\).
For binary-rational \(0<\epsilon,\delta<1\), is estimating all weights from independent type samples with \(\Pr[\max_{A\ne B}|\hat f(A,B)-f_\mu(A,B)|\le\epsilon]\ge1-\delta\) possible in randomized time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\epsilon^{-1},\log(\delta^{-1})\), including sampling costs, or is this estimation problem NP-hard?
For finite electorates, simultaneous additive estimation is known using \(O(\epsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta))\) sampled voters per candidate pair, sampled without replacement, and \(O(m^2\epsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta))\) samples overall. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00986v2#S6.SS2).
No separate result explicitly treating this independent-type sampling formulation was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The omitted deliberation weight is fixed to sqrt(3)-1, the sampling implementation's prescribed value.
  • Type distances and accuracy parameters use binary-rational encodings; metric feasibility is promised, and co-location is allowed as in the discrete model.
  • Complete rankings resolve individual distance ties; a fixed candidate order resolves deliberation ties consistently across reverse comparisons.
  • When either preference side has zero mass, its conditional pair score is set to zero; its matching contribution vanishes.
  • Sampling a mass society means independent draws with replacement, revealing each sampled type's ranking and distances.
  • Accuracy and failure parameters are restricted to the nontrivial interval (0,1), and polynomial complexity includes inverse accuracy and logarithmic inverse failure probability.

Result

The estimation problem is solvable in randomized polynomial time. In fact, \(O(\epsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta))\) independent type samples suffice *overall*, because each sample reveals all rankings and distances and can be reused across candidate pairs.

If the entire support is explicitly accessible, direct summation already gives a deterministic polynomial-time approximation. Below we prove the stronger result using only sampled type records. The cited [Section 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00986v2#S6.SS2) concerns sampling without replacement; our proof directly handles independent draws.

1. A stable reformulation

Fix an orientation \((A,B)\) for each unordered candidate pair. Write \(p=p_{AB}\), and define the unconditional probability

\[ r=r_{AB}:=\Pr[X\in P_{AB},\,Y\in P_{BA},\,D_{AB}(X,Y)=1], \]

where \(X,Y\) are independent draws from \(\mu\). Thus

\[ r=\sum_{u\in P_{AB},\,v\in P_{BA}}\mu_u\mu_vD_{AB}(u,v), \qquad 0\le r\le p(1-p). \]

Define \(h(p)=\min\{p,1-p\}\) and \(M(p)=\max\{p,1-p\}\). When \(0<p<1\), the identity \(p(1-p)=h(p)M(p)\) gives

\[ h(p)q_{AB}=\frac{r}{M(p)}. \]

When \(p\in\{0,1\}\), both sides are zero under the stipulated definition of \(q_{AB}\). Consequently, for every \(p\in[0,1]\),

\[ f_\mu(A,B)=F_w(p,r), \qquad F_c(p,r):=\frac{p+c\,r/M(p)}{1+c\,h(p)}. \]

Crucially, \(M(p)\ge\tfrac12\), including when one preference group has arbitrarily small mass.

If \(c\ge0\) and \(0\le r\le p(1-p)\), then \(0\le r/M(p)\le h(p)\). Hence

\[ 0\le F_c(p,r)\le1. \]

2. Uniform stability bounds

We first prove a bound that permits empirical estimates of \(p\) and \(r\) to be computed separately.

Lemma. Suppose \(p,s\in[0,1]\), \(0\le r\le p(1-p)\), \(t\in[0,1]\), and \(c\in[0,1]\). Then

\[ |F_c(s,t)-F_c(p,r)| \le 3|s-p|+2|t-r|. \]

Proof. Both \(h\) and \(M\) are \(1\)-Lipschitz: they equal \(\tfrac12-|p-\tfrac12|\) and \(\tfrac12+|p-\tfrac12|\), respectively. Since \(M(s),M(p)\ge\tfrac12\) and \(r\le\tfrac14\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \left|\frac{t}{M(s)}-\frac{r}{M(p)}\right| &\le \frac{|t-r|}{M(s)} +\frac{r|M(s)-M(p)|}{M(s)M(p)}\\ &\le 2|t-r|+|s-p|. \end{aligned} \]

Let \(N=p+c\,r/M(p)\), \(D=1+c\,h(p)\), and let \(N',D'\) denote their counterparts using \(s,t\). The preceding inequality implies

\[ |N'-N|\le(1+c)|s-p|+2c|t-r|, \qquad |D'-D|\le c|s-p|. \]

Using \(D'\ge1\) and \(F_c(p,r)\in[0,1]\), we obtain

\[ \begin{aligned} |F_c(s,t)-F_c(p,r)| &=\frac{|N'-N-F_c(p,r)(D'-D)|}{D'}\\ &\le(1+2c)|s-p|+2c|t-r|\\ &\le3|s-p|+2|t-r|. \end{aligned} \]

This proves the lemma.

We also need to approximate the irrational constant \(w\). For feasible \(p,r\) and nonnegative \(a,w\), direct subtraction gives

\[ |F_a(p,r)-F_w(p,r)| = \frac{|a-w|\,|r/M(p)-p\,h(p)|} {(1+a\,h(p))(1+w\,h(p))} \le \frac{|a-w|}{2}. \]

Indeed, both \(r/M(p)\) and \(p\,h(p)\) belong to \([0,h(p)]\), and \(h(p)\le\tfrac12\).

3. The estimator

Let \(K=\binom m2\), and choose the integers

\[ b=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{8K}{\delta}\right\rceil, \qquad n=\left\lceil32\epsilon^{-2}b\right\rceil. \]

Compute a rational \(a\in[0,1]\) satisfying \(|a-w|\le\epsilon/4\). This can be done by bisecting \([1,2]\) for \(\sqrt3\), comparing rational squares exactly with \(3\), and subtracting \(1\). It takes \(O(\log(\epsilon^{-1}))\) iterations.

Draw \(2n\) independent types, grouped into pairs \((X_i,Y_i)\), for \(1\le i\le n\). For every chosen orientation \((A,B)\), compute

\[ \widehat p_{AB} =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1[X_i\in P_{AB}], \]

and

\[ \widehat r_{AB} =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf1[X_i\in P_{AB},\,Y_i\in P_{BA},\,D_{AB}(X_i,Y_i)=1]. \]

The second indicator is zero unless the samples have the indicated opposing rankings. Otherwise, its value is obtained by exact rational distance comparison, including the specified deliberation tie rule.

Let \(\Pi(z)=\min\{1,\max\{0,z\}\}\). Output

\[ \widehat f(A,B)=\Pi\!\left(F_a(\widehat p_{AB},\widehat r_{AB})\right), \qquad \widehat f(B,A)=1-\widehat f(A,B). \]

Repeated occurrences of a type remain separate observations. No deduplication or conditioning on a preference group occurs.

4. Simultaneous accuracy

We use the following concentration bound: if \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\) are independent Bernoulli variables with common mean \(\theta\), then

\[ \Pr\!\left[\left|\frac1n\sum_iZ_i-\theta\right|>\alpha\right] \le2e^{-2n\alpha^2}. \]

For completeness, let \(\psi(\lambda)=\log(1-\theta+\theta e^\lambda)\). Its second derivative is \(v(1-v)\le\tfrac14\), where \(v=\theta e^\lambda/(1-\theta+\theta e^\lambda)\). Therefore

\[ \log\mathbb E[e^{\lambda(Z_i-\theta)}]\le\lambda^2/8. \]

Independence and Markov’s inequality bound the upper tail by \(\exp(-\lambda n\alpha+n\lambda^2/8)\). Choosing \(\lambda=4\alpha\) gives \(e^{-2n\alpha^2}\); applying the same argument to the lower tail proves the stated bound.

For a fixed candidate pair, the summands defining \(\widehat p_{AB}\) are independent Bernoulli variables of mean \(p_{AB}\). The summands defining \(\widehat r_{AB}\) are independent Bernoulli variables of mean \(r_{AB}\), because the sample pairs \((X_i,Y_i)\) are independent. The two estimators need not be independent of each other.

Set \(\alpha=\epsilon/8\). A union bound over the two estimates for all \(K\) candidate pairs yields

\[ \begin{aligned} \Pr[\text{some estimate has error greater than }\alpha] &\le4K e^{-2n\alpha^2}\\ &\le4K e^{-b}\\ &\le\delta/2. \end{aligned} \]

The last inequality follows from \(b\ge\ln(8K/\delta)\). Independence across candidate pairs is unnecessary.

On the complementary event, the stability bounds imply, simultaneously for every chosen orientation,

\[ \begin{aligned} |F_a(\widehat p_{AB},\widehat r_{AB})-f_\mu(A,B)| &\le3|\widehat p_{AB}-p_{AB}| +2|\widehat r_{AB}-r_{AB}| +\tfrac12|a-w|\\ &\le5\epsilon/8+\epsilon/8\\ &=3\epsilon/4. \end{aligned} \]

Projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase distance to a point of \([0,1]\), so this also bounds the error of \(\widehat f(A,B)\). Defining the reverse estimate by complementation preserves the same error because \(f_\mu(B,A)=1-f_\mu(A,B)\).

Thus ideal independent sampling gives simultaneous error at most \(\epsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta/2\).

5. Sampling costs and bit complexity

We now implement sampling with a worst-case polynomial running-time bound, including for rational masses whose denominators are not powers of two.

Write \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\) with positive integers encoded in binary. Set

\[ Q=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t, \qquad N_t=a_t(Q/b_t). \]

These integers satisfy \(\sum_tN_t=Q\). The bit length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). The integers \(N_t\) and their cumulative sums likewise have \(O(L)\) bits and are computable in polynomial time.

A uniform integer from \(\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}\), assigned to the cumulative intervals of lengths \(N_t\), produces exactly the distribution \(\mu\). For \(Q>1\), generate \(\lceil\log_2Q\rceil\) independent fair bits and reject values at least \(Q\). Each attempt succeeds with probability greater than \(\tfrac12\). The case \(Q=1\) is deterministic.

Cap each of the \(2n\) sampling calls at

\[ J=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{4n}{\delta}\right\rceil \]

attempts. The probability that any call exceeds its cap is at most

\[ 2n\,2^{-J}\le\delta/2. \]

On such a failure, the algorithm returns any fixed tournament. Couple this implementation to the uncapped exact sampler using the same random bits: their outputs agree whenever no cap is exceeded. Combining the sampling-failure bound with the concentration-failure bound therefore gives

\[ \Pr\!\left[ \max_{A\ne B}|\widehat f(A,B)-f_\mu(A,B)|\le\epsilon \right]\ge1-\delta. \]

The capped sampler uses \(O(nJL)\) random bits and polynomial work per attempt. Processing the samples takes \(O(nm^2)\) indicator evaluations. Each distance comparison is an exact comparison of sums of binary-rational inputs and has polynomial bit complexity in \(L\). The empirical averages have denominator \(n\); evaluating \(F_a\), projecting, and complementing use rational arithmetic with polynomially bounded bit lengths.

Finally,

\[ n=O(\epsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta)), \qquad J=O(\log n+\log(\delta^{-1})). \]

Consequently, the total worst-case running time, including sampling and record access, is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\epsilon^{-1},\log(\delta^{-1})\). Metric realizability is a promise on the input; the estimator does not need to construct or verify a realizing metric.
