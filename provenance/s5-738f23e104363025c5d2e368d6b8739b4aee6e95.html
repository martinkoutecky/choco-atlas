The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(A\) and \(B\), rational type masses \((\mu_a)_{a\in A}\) and \((\nu_b)_{b\in B}\), and a rational initial perfect matching flow \(x^0\) with row and column sums equal to those masses, decide whether a finite sequence of mass-divorce moves reaches a stable flow. Each type has a strict ranking over acceptable types on the other side. A move chooses types \(a\in A\), \(b\in B\) and current partner types \(b'\in B\), \(a'\in A\) such that \(b\succ_a b'\), \(a\succ_b a'\), \(x_{ab'}>0\), \(x_{a'b}>0\), and \(a'\) and \(b'\) are mutually acceptable; for some rational \(\delta>0\), it transfers mass \(\delta\) from \((a,b')\) and \((a',b)\) to \((a,b)\) and \((a',b')\), leaving a nonnegative flow. A flow is stable if for every acceptable pair \((a,b)\), either no mass of \(a\) is matched below \(b\), or no mass of \(b\) is matched below \(a\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be nonempty disjoint type sets with symmetric acceptability relation \(E\subseteq A\times B\); each type strictly ranks its acceptable opposite-side types, allowing incomplete lists.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Require \(\sum_a\mu_a=\sum_b\nu_b=1\). Let \(m=|E|\) and \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A perfect flow is a nonnegative rational matrix \(x\), zero outside \(E\), with row sums \(\mu\) and column sums \(\nu\). The input includes such an initial flow \(x^0\).
A mass-divorce move chooses \((a,b),(a',b')\in E\) with \(b\succ_a b'\), \(a\succ_b a'\), and \(x_{ab'},x_{a'b}>0\). For any rational \(0<\delta\leq\min\{x_{ab'},x_{a'b}\}\), subtract \(\delta\) from those two entries and add it to \(x_{ab},x_{a'b'}\), leaving all other entries unchanged.
A flow is stable if every \((a,b)\in E\) satisfies \(\sum_{c:\,b\succ_a c}x_{ac}=0\) or \(\sum_{d:\,a\succ_b d}x_{db}=0\).

Is deciding whether a stable flow is reachable from \(x^0\) by a finite sequence of mass-divorce moves, possibly empty, in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For indivisible agents with strict incomplete preferences, divorce reachability is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, including instances with a perfect initial matching, and belongs to \(\mathrm{PSPACE}\). [Chen](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.12274v5)
No result settling this exact rational mass-divorce reachability problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made acceptability symmetric and required every flow, including the initial flow, to assign mass only to mutually acceptable pairs.
  • Required nonempty sides and positive supported masses, and normalized the common total mass on each side to one.
  • Defined tau as the combined support size, m as the number of mutually acceptable type pairs, and L as the total input bit length.
  • Explicitly allowed an empty sequence and imposed no bound on sequence length or on the denominators of rational move amounts.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. The reduction below allows arbitrary rational move amounts; it does not assume that moves transfer an entire type’s mass. All masses in the construction are dyadic rationals.

The construction compresses the selector gadgets in [Chen’s reduction](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.12274v5) into types with larger masses. The additional argument establishes invariants that remain valid under arbitrary fractional moves.

1. Source problem and normalization

We reduce from \(\mathrm{CLIQUE}\). Let \(G\) be a simple graph with vertices \(1,\ldots,n\), edges \(g_1,\ldots,g_q\), and requested clique size \(h\). Put \(K=\binom{h}{2}\).

We may restrict to inputs satisfying \(2\leq h<n\) and \(q\geq K\). Indeed, excluded inputs can be decided directly. They can then be replaced by fixed admissible yes- or no-instances: a triangle plus an isolated vertex, or a three-edge star, respectively, both with \(h=3\).

Initially describe masses in unnormalized units. Each vertex or edge gadget type will have mass \(1\); each vertex-selector type will have mass \(h\); each edge-selector type will have mass \(K\). The total on each side is

\(N=2n+2q+2h+2K.\)

Choose the smallest power of two \(P>N\). Add an isolated acceptable pair \((\alpha_*,\beta_*)\), each of mass \(P-N\), and divide every mass and flow entry by \(P\).

Thus both sides have total mass \(1\), and every supported type has positive dyadic mass. Throughout the proof, write \(X_{uv}=P x_{uv}\), so calculations use the unnormalized units. A unit move in \(X\) means a legal move of amount \(1/P\) in the actual input.

2. Types, preferences, and initial flow

The two sides are

\(A=\{v_i,x_i:1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{f_j,p_j:1\leq j\leq q\}\cup\{t,a,c,r,\alpha_*\}\)

and

\(B=\{w_i,y_i:1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{e_j,q_j:1\leq j\leq q\}\cup\{s,b,d,z,\beta_*\}.\)

The symbols \(e_j\) and \(q_j\) here denote types.

All \(v_i,x_i,w_i,y_i,f_j,p_j,e_j,q_j\) have mass \(1\). Types \(t,a,s,b\) have mass \(h\), and types \(c,r,d,z\) have mass \(K\).

Lists below run from best to worst. Unlisted pairs are unacceptable. Every indexed block has a fixed strict order.

| Type | Preference list |
|---|---|
| \(v_i\) | \(w_i\succ [e_j:i\in g_j]\succ s\succ y_i\) |
| \(x_i\) | \(y_i\succ b\succ w_i\) |
| \(w_i\) | \(x_i\succ v_i\succ t\) |
| \(y_i\) | \(v_i\succ x_i\succ a\) |
| \(t\) | \(b\succ s\succ w_1\succ\cdots\succ w_n\) |
| \(a\) | \(s\succ b\succ y_1\succ\cdots\succ y_n\) |
| \(s\) | \(t\succ v_1\succ\cdots\succ v_n\succ a\) |
| \(b\) | \(a\succ x_1\succ\cdots\succ x_n\succ t\) |
| \(f_j\) | \(q_j\succ d\succ e_j\) |
| \(p_j\) | \(e_j\succ q_j\) |
| \(e_j\), where \(g_j=\{i,i'\}\), \(i<i'\) | \(f_j\succ v_i\succ v_{i'}\succ r\succ p_j\succ c\) |
| \(q_j\) | \(p_j\succ c\succ f_j\) |
| \(c\) | \(z\succ e_1\succ\cdots\succ e_q\succ d\succ q_1\succ\cdots\succ q_q\) |
| \(r\) | \(d\succ z\succ e_1\succ\cdots\succ e_q\) |
| \(d\) | \(c\succ f_1\succ\cdots\succ f_q\succ r\) |
| \(z\) | \(r\succ c\) |
| \(\alpha_*\) | \(\beta_*\) |
| \(\beta_*\) | \(\alpha_*\) |

These lists define symmetric acceptability.

The initial positive entries are

\(X^0_{v_i y_i}=X^0_{x_i w_i}=1,\qquad X^0_{f_j e_j}=X^0_{p_j q_j}=1,\)

\(X^0_{tb}=X^0_{as}=h,\qquad X^0_{cz}=X^0_{rd}=K,\qquad X^0_{\alpha_*\beta_*}=P-N.\)

All other entries are zero. This is a perfect flow with the stated masses.

3. Structural facts for every reachable flow

3.1. Incidence pairs always carry zero mass

Define the vertex block by

\(A_V=\{v_i,x_i:1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{t,a\},\qquad B_V=\{w_i,y_i:1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{s,b\}.\)

Both blocks have total mass \(2n+2h\). No type outside \(A_V\) accepts a type in \(B_V\). Consequently every perfect flow sends all mass of \(A_V\) into \(B_V\): their row and column totals are equal, and no outside row can supply those columns.

Therefore, for every perfect flow,

\(X_{v_i e_j}=0\quad\text{whenever }i\in g_j.\)

These incidence pairs affect stability but cannot carry mass. In particular, moves can be analyzed inside the vertex and edge blocks separately.

3.2. At most \(h\) vertices can be fully improved

We prove that every reachable flow satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^n X_{v_iw_i}\leq h.\)

Three facts about possible moves suffice.

First, \(X_{as}\) never increases. The pair \((a,s)\) cannot be the improving pair because \(a\) is last on \(s\)’s list.

If it were added as the complementary pair, the removed pairs would be \((a,\beta)\) and \((\alpha,s)\), where

\(\beta\in\{b,y_i:1\leq i\leq n\},\qquad \alpha\in\{t,v_i:1\leq i\leq n\}.\)

The improving pair would be \((\alpha,\beta)\). For \(\beta=b\), type \(b\) already has its top type \(a\), so improvement is impossible. For \(\beta=y_i\), the only acceptable possibility is \(\alpha=v_i\), but \(v_i\) prefers \(s\) to \(y_i\). Thus complementary addition is also impossible.

Second, every increase of \(X_{v_i s}\) consumes the same amount of \(X_{as}\). If \((v_i,s)\) is the improving pair, \(v_i\)’s removed partner must be \(y_i\). The complementary pair must then match \(y_i\) with a former partner of \(s\); the only possibility is \(a\).

If \((v_i,s)\) were complementary, the improving pair would join a former partner of \(s\), among \(t,a,v_\ell\), to a former partner of \(v_i\), among \(w_i,y_i\). The only acceptable possibilities are \((t,w_i)\), \((a,y_i)\), and \((v_i,y_i)\). In each, the first type prefers its removed partner \(s\), so none is an improving pair.

Third, every increase of \(X_{v_iw_i}\) consumes the same amount of \(X_{v_i s}\). If \((v_i,w_i)\) is the improving pair, \(w_i\)’s removed partner must be \(t\): its other possible partner \(x_i\) is preferred to \(v_i\). Acceptability of the complementary pair then forces \(v_i\)’s removed partner to be \(s\).

If \((v_i,w_i)\) is complementary, the improving pair must be one of

\((t,s),\ (t,y_i),\ (x_i,s),\ (x_i,y_i).\)

The middle two are unacceptable. The last cannot improve \(y_i\), which prefers its removed partner \(v_i\). Only \((t,s)\) remains, again consuming \((v_i,s)\).

Now consider any finite sequence, with arbitrary move amounts. Initially \(X_{as}=h\), and all \(X_{v_i s}\) and \(X_{v_iw_i}\) are zero. Since \(X_{as}\) never increases, at most \(h\) units in total can be introduced into the entries \(X_{v_i s}\). Nonnegativity bounds their total consumption by their total introduction. Every introduction into \(X_{v_iw_i}\) requires such consumption.

This proves the asserted bound.

Call vertex \(i\) fully improved when \(X_{v_iw_i}=1\). Since each such entry contributes one unit, every reachable flow has at most \(h\) fully improved vertices.

3.3. An edge-selection invariant

For every reachable flow,

\(\displaystyle X_{rd}+\sum_{j=1}^q\bigl(1-X_{f_je_j}\bigr)=K.\)

We establish this by checking all moves that can affect these entries.

The entry \(X_{f_je_j}\) cannot increase. It cannot be added as the improving pair because \(e_j\) is last on \(f_j\)’s list.

For complementary addition, \(f_j\)’s removed partner is either \(q_j\) or \(d\). If it is \(q_j\), the improving pair must be \((p_j,q_j)\) or \((c,q_j)\); respectively, \(p_j\) or \(c\) prefers its removed partner \(e_j\). If it is \(d\), the improving pair must be \((r,d)\) or \((c,d)\). The former fails because \(d\) prefers its removed partner \(f_j\) to \(r\); the latter fails because \(c\) prefers its removed partner \(e_j\) to \(d\).

The entry \(X_{f_je_j}\) can decrease only through

\((f_j,e_j),(r,d)\longrightarrow(f_j,d),(r,e_j).\)

Indeed, \(e_j\) has its top type \(f_j\), so \(f_j\) must initiate the improvement. It cannot improve to \(q_j\), which ranks \(f_j\) last. If it improves to \(d\), that column’s removed partner cannot be \(c\), its top type. Another \(f_\ell\) cannot form the complementary pair with \(e_j\). The only possibility is \(r\).

The entry \(X_{rd}\) cannot increase. Direct addition fails because \(r\) is last on \(d\)’s list. For complementary addition, the improving pair must join a former partner of \(d\), among \(c,f_j\), to a former partner of \(r\), among \(z,e_j\). The acceptable possibilities are:

  • \((c,z)\), rejected because \(z\) prefers its removed partner \(r\);
  • \((c,e_j)\), rejected because \(e_j\) prefers its removed partner \(r\);
  • \((f_j,e_j)\), rejected because \(f_j\) prefers its removed partner \(d\).

Finally, \(X_{rd}\) can decrease only through the displayed move. Type \(r\) already has its top type \(d\), so \(d\) must initiate the improvement. An improvement to \(c\) would require \(c\) to leave some \(q_j\), but the complementary pair \((r,q_j)\) is unacceptable. An improvement to \(f_j\) forces \(f_j\) to leave \(e_j\), giving exactly the displayed move.

That move decreases both \(X_{rd}\) and \(X_{f_je_j}\) by its amount, preserving the expression. Initially the expression is \(K\), proving the invariant.

4. Consequences of stability

Suppose a reachable flow \(X\) is stable.

4.1. The edge selector has no external mass

First,

\(X_{ce_j}=0\quad\text{for every }j.\)

Otherwise the unit column total of \(e_j\) gives \(X_{p_je_j}<1\). Since \(p_j\) accepts only \(e_j,q_j\), it follows that \(X_{p_jq_j}>0\). Then \((p_j,e_j)\) violates stability: \(p_j\) prefers \(e_j\) to \(q_j\), and \(e_j\) prefers \(p_j\) to \(c\).

Likewise,

\(X_{cq_j}=0\quad\text{for every }j.\)

If some such entry were positive, the mass-\(K\) row of \(c\) would imply \(X_{cd}<K\). The mass-\(K\) column of \(d\) would therefore contain positive mass outside \(c\). Since \(d\) ranks \(c\) first and \(c\) ranks \(d\) above every \(q_j\), the pair \((c,d)\) would violate stability.

The row of \(c\) and column of \(z\) now give

\(X_{cz}+X_{cd}=K,\qquad X_{rz}+X_{cz}=K,\)

so \(X_{rz}=X_{cd}\).

Comparing the row of \(r\) with the column of \(d\) consequently gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_jX_{re_j}=\sum_jX_{f_jd}.\)

If \(X_{cz}>0\), stability of \((r,z)\) forces \(\sum_jX_{re_j}=0\), because \(z\) prefers \(r\) to \(c\), and \(r\) prefers \(z\) to every \(e_j\).

If \(X_{cz}=0\), then \(X_{cd}=K\); the column total of \(d\) forces \(\sum_jX_{f_jd}=0\), and the displayed equality again gives \(\sum_jX_{re_j}=0\).

Thus, in either case,

\(X_{re_j}=X_{f_jd}=0\quad\text{for every }j.\)

4.2. At least \(K\) distinct edges are selected

Define

\(\alpha_j=1-X_{f_je_j}.\)

Each satisfies \(0\leq\alpha_j\leq1\). By the invariant,

\(\displaystyle \sum_j\alpha_j=K-X_{rd}.\)

We claim \(X_{rd}=0\). If \(X_{rd}>0\), stability of every \((f_j,d)\) forces \(X_{f_je_j}=0\): type \(f_j\) prefers \(d\) to \(e_j\), and \(d\) prefers \(f_j\) to \(r\). Hence all \(\alpha_j=1\), and

\(q=K-X_{rd}<K,\)

contradicting \(q\geq K\).

Therefore

\(\displaystyle \sum_j\alpha_j=K.\)

Let \(J=\{j:\alpha_j>0\}\). Since each \(\alpha_j\leq1\), we have \(|J|\geq K\).

The selector has no external mass, so the row of \(f_j\), column of \(q_j\), and row of \(p_j\) yield

\(X_{f_jq_j}=\alpha_j,\qquad X_{p_jq_j}=1-\alpha_j,\qquad X_{p_je_j}=\alpha_j.\)

Thus every \(j\in J\) has positive mass on \((p_j,e_j)\).

4.3. Every selected edge has fully improved endpoints

Fix \(j\in J\) and an endpoint \(i\in g_j\). Type \(e_j\) prefers \(v_i\) to \(p_j\), and \(X_{p_je_j}=\alpha_j>0\).

The incidence entries all vanish. On \(v_i\)’s list, \(w_i\) is above \(e_j\), while its remaining possible positive-flow partners \(s,y_i\) are below \(e_j\). Stability of \((v_i,e_j)\) therefore requires

\(X_{v_i s}+X_{v_i y_i}=0.\)

The unit row total of \(v_i\) gives \(X_{v_iw_i}=1\). Hence both endpoints of every edge indexed by \(J\) are fully improved.

Let \(H\) be the set of fully improved vertices. Section 3.2 gives \(|H|\leq h\), and all edges indexed by \(J\) lie in \(G[H]\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle K\leq |J|\leq |E(G[H])|\leq\binom{|H|}{2}\leq\binom h2=K.\)

Every inequality is an equality. Since \(h\geq2\), this implies \(|H|=h\) and \(G[H]\) is complete.

We have proved: a stable reachable flow implies an \(h\)-clique in \(G\), irrespective of how the move amounts were split.

5. A clique gives a legal finite sequence

Conversely, suppose \(H\) is an \(h\)-clique.

For each \(i\in H\), perform the following four unit moves, in order:

| Improving pair | Removed entries | Added entries |
|---|---|---|
| \((v_i,s)\) | \((v_i,y_i),(a,s)\) | \((v_i,s),(a,y_i)\) |
| \((x_i,b)\) | \((x_i,w_i),(t,b)\) | \((x_i,b),(t,w_i)\) |
| \((t,s)\) | \((t,w_i),(v_i,s)\) | \((t,s),(v_i,w_i)\) |
| \((a,b)\) | \((a,y_i),(x_i,b)\) | \((a,b),(x_i,y_i)\) |

All complementary pairs are acceptable. The required strict improvements are, respectively,

\(s\succ_{v_i}y_i,\ v_i\succ_s a;\qquad b\succ_{x_i}w_i,\ x_i\succ_b t;\)

\(s\succ_t w_i,\ t\succ_s v_i;\qquad b\succ_a y_i,\ a\succ_b x_i.\)

Before processing the next vertex, the remaining entries \(X_{as},X_{tb}\) each equal \(h\) minus the number already processed, so the needed unit amounts are available.

After processing all \(h\) vertices,

\(X_{v_iw_i}=X_{x_iy_i}=1\quad(i\in H),\qquad X_{ts}=X_{ab}=h.\)

Unselected vertex gadgets retain their initial entries.

There are exactly \(K\) clique edges. For each such edge \(g_j\), perform:

| Improving pair | Removed entries | Added entries |
|---|---|---|
| \((f_j,d)\) | \((f_j,e_j),(r,d)\) | \((f_j,d),(r,e_j)\) |
| \((r,z)\) | \((r,e_j),(c,z)\) | \((r,z),(c,e_j)\) |
| \((p_j,e_j)\) | \((p_j,q_j),(c,e_j)\) | \((p_j,e_j),(c,q_j)\) |
| \((c,d)\) | \((c,q_j),(f_j,d)\) | \((c,d),(f_j,q_j)\) |

Again every complementary pair is acceptable. The strict improvements are

\(d\succ_{f_j}e_j,\ f_j\succ_d r;\qquad z\succ_r e_j,\ r\succ_z c;\)

\(e_j\succ_{p_j}q_j,\ p_j\succ_{e_j}c;\qquad d\succ_c q_j,\ c\succ_d f_j.\)

The remaining entries \(X_{rd},X_{cz}\) each equal \(K\) minus the number of edges already processed, ensuring availability.

At the end,

\(X_{f_jq_j}=X_{p_je_j}=1\quad(g_j\in E(G[H])),\qquad X_{cd}=X_{rz}=K.\)

Other edge gadgets retain their initial entries. The padding pair is untouched.

Verification of final stability

It suffices to inspect every type in \(B\), since every acceptable pair has one endpoint there.

  • An unselected \(w_i\) or \(y_i\) has its top partner. A selected \(w_i\) has \(v_i\) and only prefers \(x_i\), which has its top partner \(y_i\). A selected \(y_i\) has \(x_i\) and only prefers \(v_i\), which has its top partner \(w_i\).
  • Types \(s,b,d,z,\beta_*\) have their top partners.
  • An unselected \(e_j\) has its top partner \(f_j\). A selected \(e_j\) has \(p_j\); the types it prefers are \(f_j\), its two endpoint types, and \(r\). The first three have their top partners, and \(r\) prefers its current partner \(z\) to \(e_j\).
  • An unselected \(q_j\) has its top partner \(p_j\). A selected \(q_j\) has \(f_j\) and prefers only \(p_j,c\). Type \(p_j\) has its top partner \(e_j\), while \(c\) prefers its current partner \(d\) to every \(q_j\).

Thus every acceptable pair satisfies the stated stability condition. The sequence has \(4h+4K\) moves, each of actual amount \(1/P\).

6. Size and complexity conclusion

Every listed type has positive mass, so the supported-type count is exactly

\(\tau=4n+4q+10.\)

The acceptability-edge count is

\(m=8n+10q+9.\)

Because \(h<n\) and \(K\leq q\), we have \(N=O(n+q)\) and \(P=O(n+q)\). Every rational parameter has \(O(\log\tau)\) bits.

The construction takes \(O((m+\tau^2)\log\tau)\) bit operations when the initial matrix is written explicitly, or \(O((m+\tau)\log\tau)\) with a sparse encoding. Its total input length satisfies \(L=O((m+\tau^2)\log\tau)\) under the explicit encoding. In particular, the reduction is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), without expanding masses into indivisible agents.

We have established the equivalence

\(G\text{ has an }h\text{-clique}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^0\text{ reaches a stable flow by finitely many mass-divorce moves}.\)

Therefore the exact rational mass-divorce reachability problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
