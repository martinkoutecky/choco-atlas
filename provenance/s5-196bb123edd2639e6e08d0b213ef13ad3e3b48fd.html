Theorem 8.2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite candidate set \(C\), strict-ranking types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), Snowveil's local parameters, quorum \(Q>1/2\), horizon \(H\), and precision \(\varepsilon\), initialize \(y_{t,j}(0)=0\), where \(y_{t,j}\) is the mass of type \(t\) locked on candidate \(j\). Let \(u_t=\mu_t-\sum_j y_{t,j}\), and let \(q_j(y)\) be the probability that the paper's Voter Update Process returns \(\mathrm{LOCK}(j)\) when its samples are drawn from the current reported-ranking distribution. Evolve \(\frac{dy_{t,j}}{ds}=\frac{u_t}{\sum_r u_r}q_j(y)\). Determine which candidate first reaches \(\sum_t y_{t,j}\ge Q\) by time \(H\), and return its hitting time within \(\varepsilon\), or report that none reaches quorum by \(H\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\ge2\), ordered by index for lexicographic tie-breaking. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) full strict-ranking types \(R_t\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings.
Inputs are positive integers \(k,\gamma\), an integer \(1\le\tau_{\min}\le\gamma\), and binary rationals \(\alpha,\beta,\lambda\in[0,1]\), \(Q\in(1/2,1]\), \(H>0\), and \(\varepsilon>0\). Set \(\tau_{\max}=\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor+1\); let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including the integers.
For a sample of \(k\) rankings, let \(B_j\) be candidate \(j\)'s Borda score, \(a_j\) its first-place count, and \(b\) the lexicographically selected Borda winner. CHB returns \(b\) if \(a_b\ge\lceil\alpha k\rceil\); otherwise it selects the lexicographically selected maximizer of \((1-\lambda)B_j/[k(m-1)]+\lambda a_j/k\) among candidates satisfying \(a_j\ge\lceil\alpha k\rceil\) and \(B_j\ge\beta\max_h B_h\), returning \(b\) if that set is empty.
At state \(y\), put \(u_t=\mu_t-\sum_j y_{t,j}\). The reported-ranking distribution assigns mass \(u_t\) to \(R_t\) and mass \(y_{t,j}\) to the ranking obtained by moving \(j\) to the top of \(R_t\), preserving all other relative positions; coincident rankings have their masses added.
A local update freezes this distribution and starts candidate win counters at zero. Each of at most \(\gamma\) independent rounds draws \(k\) independent rankings from it, applies CHB, and increments the winner's counter; it immediately returns \(\mathrm{LOCK}(j)\) when \(j\)'s counter reaches \(\tau_{\max}\). If no early exit occurs, it returns NO-LOCK when the maximum counter is below \(\tau_{\min}\), and otherwise locks on the counter maximizer whose most recent round win occurred latest.
Let \(q_j(y)\) be this update's probability of returning \(\mathrm{LOCK}(j)\). Starting from \(y_{t,j}(0)=0\), evolve \(\frac{dy_{t,j}}{ds}=\frac{u_t}{\sum_r u_r}q_j(y)\) while unlocked mass is positive; if it vanishes, continue from the limiting state with all masses constant. Locks are irreversible.
The computational problem is to identify the first candidate with \(\sum_t y_{t,j}(s)\ge Q\) at some \(s\in[0,H]\), and approximate its first hitting time with absolute error at most \(\varepsilon\), or report that no candidate reaches quorum by \(H\). Equality at the horizon counts as reaching quorum.

Is this continuous quorum-and-hitting-time problem solvable in deterministic polynomial time, or is it NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,k,\gamma,H,\varepsilon\), with polynomial time measured in the binary input length.

Discrete Snowveil has a stated expected \(O(n)\) bound on local-update calls for single-winner convergence, with other parameters fixed and assuming convergence and a positive lock-probability bound independent of \(n\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18444)
No result settling this exact continuous quorum-and-hitting-time problem was located.

  • Defined reported rankings by promoting the locked candidate while preserving the remaining order, with unlocked mass reporting its original ranking.
  • Used independent mass-weighted sampling within and across rounds, with the distribution frozen during a local update; individual self-exclusion has zero mass in the continuous society.

Status

I do not settle the unrestricted quorum problem. I prove a new hardness result that survives the feedback in the differential equation:

Deciding whether a designated candidate has positive locked mass at a fixed positive time is NP-complete, already for $\gamma=1$ and $\alpha=0$.

The construction also gives a polynomial-bit separation between zero and positive mass. Consequently, approximating a designated locked-mass coordinate to a supplied binary accuracy is NP-hard.

These results concern positive mass and small thresholds, rather than the required quorum $Q>1/2$. I distinguish that remaining gap explicitly below.

1. Exact reduction to candidate coordinates

Let
$$ x_j(s)=\sum_t y_{t,j}(s),\qquad X(s)=\sum_jx_j(s),\qquad U(s)=1-X(s). $$
Write
$$ r(y)=\sum_jq_j(y). $$
While $U>0$, summing the given equations yields
$$ u_t'=-\frac{u_t}{U}r(y), \qquad U'=-r(y). $$
Therefore
$$ \left(\frac{u_t}{U}\right)' =\frac{u_t'U-u_tU'}{U^2}=0. $$
Since $u_t(0)=\mu_t$ and $U(0)=1$,
$$ u_t=\mu_tU. $$
It follows that
$$ x_j'=q_j(y), \qquad y_{t,j}'=\mu_tq_j(y)=\mu_tx_j'. $$
The zero initial conditions imply
$$ y_{t,j}=\mu_tx_j. $$

Let $P_0$ be the truthful-ranking distribution, and let $P_j$ be the distribution obtained by promoting $j$ in every truthful ranking, with the original type masses. The reported distribution is exactly
$$ P(x)=(1-X)P_0+\sum_jx_jP_j. $$
Consequently the dynamics reduce to
$$ x_j'=q_j(x),\qquad x(0)=0, $$
on
$$ S=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^m:x_j\ge0,\ \sum_jx_j\le1\right\}, $$
until $X=1$, followed by the prescribed constant continuation.

This reduction preserves all type information relevant to reported rankings; coincident rankings simply have their masses added.

2. Probability formulas, regularity, and completion

Put
$$ h=\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma}{2}\right\rfloor+1. $$
Let $p_j(x)$ denote the probability that one round elects $j$.

Exact probability evaluation

Use $N=\tau(m+1)$ labeled components, consisting of each truthful type and each of its possible promotions. Their weights are
$$ w_{t,0}(x)=\mu_t(1-X), \qquad w_{t,a}(x)=\mu_tx_a. $$
For a count vector $v$ over these components,
$$ p_j(x)= \sum_{\substack{|v|=k\\\mathrm{CHB}(v)=j}} \frac{k!}{\prod_i v_i!}\prod_iw_i(x)^{v_i}. $$
Thus $p_j$ is a rational-coefficient polynomial of degree at most $k$.

For the local update, generate all $\gamma$ round winners in advance. Let $n_j$ be their final counts, and define
$$ M(n)=\max_i n_i,\qquad T(n)=\{i:n_i=M(n)\}. $$
Since $2h>\gamma$, at most one candidate has count at least $h$. If one does, it is precisely the early-exit winner. Otherwise, conditional on a positive-probability count vector, all compatible winner sequences are equiprobable. Permuting tied maximizers shows that each has probability $1/|T(n)|$ of having the latest most recent win.

Hence, with
$$ \omega_j(n)= \begin{cases} 1,&n_j\ge h,\\ 1/|T(n)|,&M(n)<h,\ M(n)\ge\tau_{\min},\ j\in T(n),\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} $$
we have
$$ q_j(x)= \sum_{|n|=\gamma} \frac{\gamma!}{\prod_i n_i!} \prod_i p_i(x)^{n_i}\,\omega_j(n). $$
Zero-probability count vectors contribute zero, so no conditional-distribution assertion is needed for them. In particular, $q_j$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k\gamma$.

Define
$$ A_k=\binom{k+N-1}{N-1}, \qquad A_\gamma=\binom{\gamma+m-1}{m-1}. $$
At a rational state with coordinate bit length at most $b$, multinomial enumeration evaluates all probabilities in bit time
$$ O(A_kNm+A_\gamma m)\, \operatorname{poly}(L,m,\tau,k,\gamma,b). $$
For each sample histogram, Borda scores, thresholds, and tie-breaking are computable exactly in polynomial bit time. Probability products admit common denominators whose bit lengths are polynomial in the displayed numerical parameters. This is an exact evaluation bound, not a polynomial bound in binary input length.

Existence and finite completion

Set
$$ D=k\gamma,\qquad c=m^{1-h}. $$
For $x,z\in S$, total variation satisfies
$$ \operatorname{TV}(P(x),P(z)) \le\|x-z\|_1, $$
because
$$ P(x)-P(z)=\sum_j(x_j-z_j)(P_j-P_0) $$
and $\operatorname{TV}(P_j,P_0)\le1$.

Coupling all $D$ pre-generated ranking draws gives
$$ \|q(x)-q(z)\|_1\le2D\|x-z\|_1, \qquad |r(x)-r(z)|\le D\|x-z\|_1. $$
The polynomial field therefore has a unique trajectory until the boundary $X=1$.

The event that the first $h$ round winners are all $j$ guarantees a lock on $j$. Thus
$$ q_j(x)\ge p_j(x)^h. $$
Since $\sum_jp_j(x)=1$, convexity gives
$$ c=m^{1-h} \le\sum_jp_j(x)^h \le r(x)\le1. $$
Nonnegative derivatives preserve nonnegative coordinates. Boundedness permits continuation until $X=1$, and the completion time satisfies
$$ 1\le T_{\mathrm{end}}\le c^{-1}=m^{h-1}. $$

Let $a_j$ be the truthful first-place mass of $j$. Its reported first-place mass is
$$ Ua_j+x_j. $$
If all sampled rankings put $j$ first, then $j$ is the unique Borda winner and passes the first-place threshold. Hence, writing $e=kh$,
$$ p_j(x)\ge(Ua_j+x_j)^k, \qquad q_j(x)\ge(Ua_j+x_j)^e\ge x_j^e. $$
An interior quorum crossing therefore has strictly positive speed:
$$ x_j'\ge Q^e. $$

Finally, all coordinates are nondecreasing. Since $2Q>1$, at most one candidate can ever reach quorum.

3. An exact characterization of positive locked mass

For a set $A$ of candidates, let $\mathcal W(A)$ consist of all truthful rankings and all rankings obtained by promoting a member of $A$ in a truthful ranking.

Define
$$ A_0=\varnothing $$
and recursively
$$ A_{\ell+1} = A_\ell\cup \left\{ j:\text{some multiset of $k$ rankings from }\mathcal W(A_\ell) \text{ elects }j \right\}. $$
This increasing sequence stabilizes after at most $m$ strict enlargements. Denote its fixed point by $A_*$. Possible samples may equivalently be represented by histograms over labeled components; coincident rankings do not affect existence.

Proposition. For every $H>0$,
$$ x_j(H)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad j\in A_*. $$

Proof. First,
$$ q_j(x)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_j(x)>0. $$
The forward implication holds because a lock on $j$ requires at least one round win by $j$: early exit requires $h$ wins, and fallback locking requires a positive maximal count because $\tau_{\min}\ge1$. The reverse implication follows from $q_j\ge p_j^h$.

Consider the face of $S$ on which $x_j=0$ for every $j\notin A_*$. Every reported ranking there belongs to $\mathcal W(A_*)$. By the fixed-point definition, no sample of these rankings elects a candidate outside $A_*$. Thus every corresponding derivative is zero. Restricting the polynomial field to this face gives a solution of the full equation; uniqueness shows that the trajectory starting at zero stays in the face. Therefore
$$ j\notin A_*\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_j(s)=0 $$
for all times.

Conversely, all truthful labeled components have positive mass throughout $0<s<T_{\mathrm{end}}$, because their masses are $\mu_tU(s)$.

Every candidate in $A_1$ consequently has positive round-win probability, and hence positive derivative, at every such time. Integrating over any nonempty subinterval of $(0,s)$ gives $x_j(s)>0$.

Inductively, suppose all members of $A_\ell$ have positive coordinates throughout $(0,T_{\mathrm{end}})$. Every labeled component used by $\mathcal W(A_\ell)$ then has positive mass throughout that interval. A witnessing sample for any newly added candidate therefore has positive probability, so that candidate also has positive derivative and positive coordinate throughout the interval.

All members of $A_*$ thus have positive coordinates at every positive time before completion. Monotonicity and constant continuation preserve positivity afterward. ∎

This proposition controls feedback exactly: it determines which candidates become active, rather than merely which candidates can win initially.

Certificate and enumeration costs

Membership in $A_*$ has a polynomial-size certificate. List at most $m$ candidates in an activation order, with a winning sample histogram for each. Each histogram may use truthful components and promotions of previously listed candidates. Its counts require $O(N\log(k+1))$ bits. Exact CHB verification is polynomial in binary input length.

A direct deterministic closure algorithm has bit running time bounded by
$$ O\!\left(mA_kNm\right)\, \operatorname{poly}(L,m,\tau,k). $$
The activation predicate does not otherwise depend on $\gamma$ or $\tau_{\min}$.

4. NP-completeness of continuous activation

Theorem. Given a designated candidate $J$, deciding whether
$$ x_J(H)>0 $$
is NP-complete, already with
$$ H=\frac12,\qquad \gamma=\tau_{\min}=1,\qquad \alpha=0. $$

Membership in NP follows from the preceding activation certificate. I prove hardness by constructing a SAT encoding whose infeasibility survives every promotion other than promotion of $J$ itself.

4.1 Encoding a satisfying assignment by sample counts

Start with a $3$-CNF formula on $n\ge1$ variables, with $c_{\mathrm{cl}}$ clauses. There are $2n$ variable ranking types, indexed by truth choices
$$ t=(i,T),\qquad t=(i,F). $$
Let their sample counts be $v_t$, temporarily assuming
$$ \sum_t v_t=n. $$

For each variable $i$, introduce two coefficient rows
$$ A_{i,+,t} = n\,\mathbf1_{\{t\text{ belongs to variable }i\}}-1, \qquad A_{i,-,t}=-A_{i,+,t}. $$
Their row sums are
$$ n(v_{i,T}+v_{i,F}-1) $$
and its negative. Both are nonnegative exactly when
$$ v_{i,T}+v_{i,F}=1. $$

For each clause, introduce a row
$$ A_{\mathrm{clause},t} = n\,\mathbf1_{\{t\text{ makes a literal in that clause true}\}}-1. $$
Once each variable has exactly one selected truth choice, the row sum is $n(s-1)$, where $s$ counts selected choices satisfying a literal in that clause. It is nonnegative exactly when the clause is satisfied.

Let
$$ d=2n+c_{\mathrm{cl}}. $$
Every coefficient has absolute value at most $n$.

4.2 Realizing the inequalities as Borda comparisons

Set
$$ h_0=2n+1,\qquad b_{\max}=d+1, $$
$$ m_0=h_0(d+2)+n+1, \qquad k=n(h_0+1). $$
Use named candidates $J,L_0,L_1$, and one candidate $G_b$ for each row, indexed by $b=2,\ldots,d+1$. Give $J$ the smallest candidate index. The remaining candidates are fillers.

Specify rankings by their distinct Borda scores.

In a baseline ranking, assign
$$ B_J=m_0-2,\qquad B_{L_0}=m_0-1,\qquad B_{L_1}=m_0-3, $$
$$ B_{G_b}=m_0-2-b. $$
These named candidates occupy the top $d+3$ positions. Fill all remaining positions with fillers.

In variable ranking $t$, assign
$$ B_J=h_0,\qquad B_{L_0}=0,\qquad B_{L_1}=2h_0, $$
$$ B_{G_b}=h_0(b+1)-A_{b,t}. $$
These scores are valid and distinct:

  • The smallest row-candidate score is at least $3h_0-n>2h_0$.
  • Consecutive row-candidate scores differ by at least $h_0-2n=1$.
  • The largest row-candidate score is at most $h_0(d+2)+n=m_0-1$.

Fill the unused positions with fillers. There are
$$ m_0-(d+3)=2n(d+2)+n $$
fillers, so there are at least $2n$ of them. If two variable types have identical named-score patterns, use distinct cyclic orders of the fillers in their available positions. This makes all variable types distinct.

Let
$$ r_0=\left\lceil\log_2(2n+1)\right\rceil. $$
Give every variable type mass $2^{-r_0}$, and the baseline type mass
$$ 1-2n\,2^{-r_0}. $$
Every mass is positive and at least $2^{-r_0}$.

4.3 Correctness of the unmodified encoding

Suppose a sample contains $z$ baseline rankings and $v=\sum_tv_t$ variable rankings. Its score differences against the balancing candidates are
$$ B_J-B_{L_0}=-z+h_0v, \qquad B_J-B_{L_1}=z-h_0v. $$
For $J$ to beat both, necessarily
$$ z=h_0v. $$
Since $z+v=k=n(h_0+1)$, this forces
$$ v=n,\qquad z=h_0n. $$

Against row candidate $G_b$, the score difference is
$$ B_J-B_{G_b} = bz+\sum_t(A_{b,t}-h_0b)v_t = \sum_tA_{b,t}v_t. $$
Thus beating every named candidate is equivalent to selecting one truth value per variable and satisfying every clause.

For completeness, fillers cannot obstruct a satisfying witness. In the baseline ranking, $J$ beats every filler by at least $b_{\max}+1$. In a variable ranking, its disadvantage against any filler is at most
$$ m_0-1-h_0=h_0b_{\max}+n. $$
For the forced sample composition, its total advantage is at least
$$ h_0n(b_{\max}+1)-n(h_0b_{\max}+n) = n(h_0-n)>0. $$
Ties against named candidates favor $J$.

We have proved:

  • If a sample beats all the named opponents of $J$, the formula is satisfiable.
  • If the formula is satisfiable, some sample elects $J$.

4.4 Making infeasibility survive feedback

The following elementary fact is the key.

Promotion lemma. In one ranking, promoting a candidate other than $J$ increases the Borda-score difference of $J$ against any fixed opponent by at most one.

Proof. If the opponent is the promoted candidate, its score increases and $J$'s score does not increase, so the difference cannot increase. Otherwise, neither compared candidate is promoted. Each of their scores either stays unchanged or decreases by one, so their difference increases by at most one. ∎

Let
$$ g=k+1. $$
Replace every original candidate $a$ by a block
$$ a^1,a^2,\ldots,a^g. $$
In every ranking, replace $a$ by this consecutive block, in the displayed fixed order. The final number of candidates is
$$ m=gm_0. $$
Call $a^1$ the representative. If $a$ originally has Borda score $s_a$, its representative now has score
$$ gs_a+g-1. $$
Therefore representative-to-representative score differences are multiplied by $g$. Give $J^1$ the smallest index in the enlarged election.

If the formula is satisfiable, its original winning sample elects $J^1$ in the enlarged election. Indeed, it beats every representative, and each nonrepresentative is below its own representative in every sampled ranking.

Now suppose a sample made from enlarged truthful rankings and arbitrary single promotions of candidates other than $J^1$ elects $J^1$. Each sampled ranking has an underlying truthful type. For every named opponent representative $a^1$, the promotion lemma implies
$$ \Delta_{\mathrm{modified}}(J^1,a^1) \le g\,\Delta_{\mathrm{original}}(J,a)+k. $$
Winning implies the left side is nonnegative. Hence
$$ g\,\Delta_{\mathrm{original}}(J,a)\ge-k. $$
The original difference is an integer, and $g=k+1$. Therefore
$$ \Delta_{\mathrm{original}}(J,a)\ge0. $$
This holds for both balancing candidates and every row candidate. Section 4.3 then recovers a satisfying assignment.

Thus, if the formula is unsatisfiable, $J^1$ cannot win from any combination of truthful rankings and promotions of other candidates.

On the face $x_{J^1}=0$, every reported component is of precisely this form. Consequently
$$ q_{J^1}(x)=0 $$
throughout that face. Uniqueness of the reduced dynamics makes the face invariant, and
$$ x_{J^1}(s)=0 $$
for all times.

If the formula is satisfiable, a truthful sample elects $J^1$ with positive probability, so $q_{J^1}(0)>0$. Continuity gives positive locked mass at every positive time.

For $\alpha=0$, CHB always returns the Borda winner. For $\gamma=1$, the local update immediately locks on its single round winner. We have therefore obtained the required reduction.

Construction size

The construction satisfies
$$ \tau=2n+1,\qquad k=O(n^2), $$
$$ m_0=O\!\left(n(n+c_{\mathrm{cl}})\right), \qquad m=O\!\left(n^3(n+c_{\mathrm{cl}})\right). $$
All full rankings and masses can be written in polynomial time and have polynomial total binary length. This completes the NP-completeness proof.

5. A quantitative hardness gap for continuous mass approximation

The preceding reduction gives more than a zero-versus-positive distinction.

For $\gamma=1$, every update locks, so
$$ r=1,\qquad X(s)=s,\qquad U(s)=1-s $$
before completion at time one.

In a satisfiable instance, fix a truthful winning histogram for $J^1$. Its initial probability is at least
$$ 2^{-r_0k}, $$
because every type mass is at least $2^{-r_0}$ and its multinomial coefficient is at least one.

At time $s\le1/2$, the labeled event of drawing this same histogram entirely from truthful components has probability at least
$$ (1-s)^k2^{-r_0k}. $$
This is a lower bound on the winning-sample probability; coincident reported rankings can only add further probability. Thus
$$ x_{J^1}\!\left(\frac12\right) \ge 2^{-r_0k}\int_0^{1/2}(1-s)^k\,ds $$
$$ = \frac{2^{-r_0k}}{k+1} \left(1-2^{-(k+1)}\right) \ge \frac{2^{-r_0k}}{2(k+1)}. $$

Define the dyadic rational
$$ \delta= 2^{-\left(r_0k+\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil+2\right)}. $$
Its binary description has polynomial length. The two cases satisfy
$$ \begin{cases} x_{J^1}(1/2)=0,&\text{if the formula is unsatisfiable},\\ x_{J^1}(1/2)\ge2\delta,&\text{if the formula is satisfiable}. \end{cases} $$

It follows that:

  • Deciding whether $x_{J^1}(1/2)\ge\delta$ is NP-hard, even under this gap promise.
  • Producing an approximation to $x_{J^1}(1/2)$ with absolute error at most $\delta/4$ is NP-hard.

For the second statement, compare the returned approximation with $\delta$. The error guarantee separates the two cases.

In particular, unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, a general coordinate-approximation algorithm cannot run in time polynomial in the binary input length, including $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. This conclusion does not rule out algorithms polynomial in the numerical value $1/\varepsilon$.

Limitation. The threshold $\delta$ is below $1/2$. These arguments do not establish hardness for quorum $Q>1/2$, nor do they show that activation of $J^1$ creates or prevents a majority quorum.

6. Exact polynomial-time cases of the requested problem

Unanimity quorum

Suppose $Q=1$.

If two distinct candidates appear first in truthful supported rankings, each can win a homogeneous truthful sample. Both belong to $A_1$, so both receive positive irreversible locked mass. No candidate can reach mass one.

If every truthful ranking has the same first candidate $j$, consider
$$ x_j(s)=\min\{s,1\}, \qquad x_i(s)=0\quad(i\ne j). $$
Promoting $j$ changes no truthful ranking. Every sample elects $j$, and every local update locks on $j$. The displayed trajectory therefore satisfies the reduced dynamics, and uniqueness identifies it as the actual trajectory.

Hence quorum is reached precisely when the truthful support has a common first candidate and $H\ge1$. Its hitting time is exactly one. This is decidable in $\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bit time, independently of the numerical sizes of $k$ and $\gamma$.

The same unanimous-support argument handles every allowed $Q$: the hitting time is exactly $Q$.

One sampled ranking

For $k=\gamma=1$, CHB elects the sampled ranking's first candidate, and the update locks immediately. Thus
$$ q_j(x)=Ua_j+x_j,\qquad r=1. $$
The solution is
$$ x_j(s)=a_js\quad(0\le s\le1), \qquad x_j(s)=a_j\quad(s\ge1), $$
because before completion,
$$ Ua_j+x_j=(1-s)a_j+a_js=a_j. $$

A candidate reaches quorum exactly when
$$ a_j\ge Q \quad\text{and}\quad H\ge\frac{Q}{a_j}. $$
Its hitting time is the rational number $Q/a_j$. Exact rational comparisons handle equality at the horizon in $\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bit time.

The same conclusion holds for
$$ k=1,\qquad \gamma=2,\qquad \tau_{\min}=1. $$
If the two round winners coincide, the second round triggers the lock. If they differ, the latest-win tie rule selects the second winner. The update therefore always returns the second-round winner, giving the same probabilities.

Earliest possible horizon

Since $X'\le1$, no candidate can reach quorum before time $Q$. Thus $H<Q$ is an immediate exact NO instance.

At $H=Q$, reaching quorum requires unanimous truthful first place. To see this, suppose truthful first places are not unanimous. For every candidate $j$, some truthful type has another first candidate $i$. Homogeneous samples of that type give $p_i(0)>0$, hence $q_i(0)>0$, so $q_j(0)<1$. By continuity, integrating $q_j\le1$ gives
$$ x_j(Q)<Q. $$
Here the trajectory exists through time $Q$ because $Q\le1\le T_{\mathrm{end}}$.

7. A certified algorithm with explicit separation assumptions

The following algorithm approximates the trajectory and solves the quorum problem under additional promises. Those promises are not part of the original task.

Assume a supplied rational $\delta_{\mathrm{sep}}>0$ such that:

  • Every terminal coordinate differs from $Q$ by at least $\delta_{\mathrm{sep}}$.
  • If an eventual quorum exists, its hitting time differs from $H$ by at least $\delta_{\mathrm{sep}}$.

Mass parameterization and field bounds

Parameterize by total locked mass $z=X$. Since $r\ge c>0$,
$$ \frac{dx}{dz}=\frac{q(x)}{r(x)}, \qquad \frac{ds}{dz}=\frac1{r(x)}, \qquad 0\le z\le1. $$
In the combined $\ell_1$ norm, this field has magnitude at most
$$ B=1+c^{-1}. $$
Its Lipschitz constant is at most
$$ K=\frac{3D}{c}+\frac{D}{c^2}. $$
Indeed,
$$ \left\|\frac{q(x)}{r(x)}-\frac{q(y)}{r(y)}\right\|_1 \le \frac{2D}{c}\|x-y\|_1 +\frac{|r(x)-r(y)|}{r(x)} \le \frac{3D}{c}\|x-y\|_1, $$
and
$$ \left|\frac1{r(x)}-\frac1{r(y)}\right| \le\frac{D}{c^2}\|x-y\|_1. $$

Finite Euler implementation

For a desired node error $0<\eta\le1/2$, use the explicitly computable integer
$$ E_0=2^{\lceil2K\rceil}. $$
It satisfies
$$ e^K\le E_0\le2\,2^{2K}. $$
Choose a power-of-two mesh size $J$ with
$$ J\ge\frac{BE_0}{\eta}, \qquad \Delta=\frac1J. $$
Set
$$ \nu=\frac{K\eta}{2E_0}. $$

At each step, evaluate the field exactly at the rational numerical state, then round it with combined error at most $\nu$. Round the candidate derivative vector to nonnegative dyadic coordinates summing exactly to one. This can be done by rounding down to a sufficiently fine dyadic grid and distributing the missing grid units according to the fractional remainders. Each coordinate error is at most one grid unit. Round the time derivative on the same sufficiently fine grid.

Consequently, numerical candidate states stay in $S$ and satisfy
$$ \sum_j\widehat x_j(z_i)=z_i. $$

If $e_i$ is the combined node error, the Euler truncation bound gives
$$ e_{i+1}\le(1+K\Delta)e_i+\frac{KB\Delta^2}{2}+\nu\Delta. $$
Summing the recurrence yields
$$ e_i \le \left(\frac{B\Delta}{2}+\frac{\nu}{K}\right)(e^K-1) \le\eta. $$
The mesh and rounding use
$$ O\!\left(K+\log(m/\eta)\right) $$
fractional bits. In particular, the implementation does not require exact evaluation of an irrational exponential.

Quorum identification and hitting time

Put
$$ a=(Q/2)^e $$
and choose
$$ \eta\le \min\left\{ \frac{\delta_{\mathrm{sep}}}{4}, \frac Q4, \frac12, \frac{ca}{6}\min\{\varepsilon,\delta_{\mathrm{sep}}\} \right\}. $$
The terminal approximation identifies whether an eventual quorum exists and, if it does, its unique candidate.

For that candidate, let $z_i$ be the first numerical node whose approximate coordinate reaches $Q$. Since a numerical coordinate increases by at most $\Delta$ in one step, its true coordinate at $z_i$ differs from $Q$ by at most $\eta+\Delta$.

Let $z_*$ be the true crossing. Between $z_i$ and $z_*$ the coordinate is at least $Q-\eta\ge Q/2$. Therefore
$$ \frac{dx_j}{dz}=\frac{q_j}{r}\ge q_j\ge x_j^e\ge a, $$
and
$$ |z_i-z_*|\le\frac{\eta+\Delta}{a}. $$
Since $ds/dz\le1/c$ and $\Delta\le\eta$, the approximate hitting time has error at most
$$ \eta+\frac{\eta+\Delta}{ca} \le\frac{3\eta}{ca} \le\frac12\min\{\varepsilon,\delta_{\mathrm{sep}}\}. $$
The horizon-separation promise then makes comparison with $H$ unambiguous.

$$ O\!\left( \frac{B\,2^{2K}}{\eta} (A_kNm+A_\gamma m) \right) \operatorname{poly} \!\left( L,m,\tau,k,\gamma,K,\ell_\eta,\ell_{\mathrm{sep}} \right). $$
Here
