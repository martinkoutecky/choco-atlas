The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite customer and supplier type sets \(A\) and \(B\), rational type masses \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\), nonnegative rational MNL weights \(v_{ab}\) and \(w_{ba}\), and fixed per-agent caps \(K_C,K_S\), let population size \(N\) range over values for which all type counts are integral. Each customer of type \(a\) assigns weight \(v_{ab}\) to each individual supplier of type \(b\), and each supplier of type \(b\) assigns weight \(w_{ba}\) to each individual customer of type \(a\); both outside options have weight \(1\). Over simple bipartite display graphs whose edges are reciprocal displays and whose degrees obey the caps, determine whether the limit exists and compute \(\limsup_{N\to\infty} N^{-1}\max_G \mathbb{E}[M(G)]\), where \(M(G)\) is the number of mutual selections.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be nonempty customer and supplier type sets. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) positive-mass types, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Normalize \(\sum_a\mu_a+\sum_b\nu_b=1\); write \(m=|B|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The input includes nonnegative rational weights \(v_{ab},w_{ba}\). Fix positive integer caps \(K_C,K_S\), independent of population size. For each positive integer \(N\) making all counts integral, there are \(N\mu_a\) customers of type \(a\) and \(N\nu_b\) suppliers of type \(b\).
A feasible display is a simple bipartite graph \(G\), with customer degrees at most \(K_C\) and supplier degrees at most \(K_S\). Each edge displays its endpoints to each other; all displays are chosen before any selections occur. Empty displays are allowed.
Agents independently make one MNL selection from their displayed neighbors or the outside option. Each individual supplier of type \(b\) has weight \(v_{ab}\) for a customer of type \(a\), and conversely each individual customer has weight \(w_{ba}\); both outside-option weights are \(1\). A match is a mutual selection.
Write \(F(G)=\mathbb E[M(G)]\), \(\mathrm{OPT}_N=\max_G F(G)\), and \(V=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathrm{OPT}_N/N\), along admissible integers.
An asymptotic display algorithm produces a finite recipe for feasible, possibly randomized graphs \(G_N\). Its preprocessing time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) for each fixed pair \(K_C,K_S\); expanding the recipe may additionally take polynomial time in \(N\).
Does \(\mathrm{OPT}_N/N\) converge, and is there an asymptotic display algorithm with \(\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb E[F(G_N)]/N\geq 0.067V\), or is achieving this approximation factor NP-hard?
For finite explicitly listed agents, cardinality-constrained fully static MNL assortment optimization admits a polynomial-time \(0.067\)-approximation ([El Housni, Hennebelle, and Torrico](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08929v6)). No result settling this support-encoded, fixed-cap mass-limit approximation question was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize the combined customer and supplier masses to one, making N the total population size while permitting unequal side sizes.
  • Require nonempty supports and positive integer caps, as in the cardinality-constrained discrete model; interpret fixed caps as constants independent of N.
  • Specify independent, simultaneous, single selections and allow empty displays, following the discrete fully static model.
  • Define m as the supplier support size and L as the total input bit length.
  • Specify a finite graph-generation recipe with preprocessing polynomial in the support input and expansion polynomial in N, so polynomial time does not mean merely running an explicit-agent algorithm on an arbitrarily large population.

Result

The sequence \(\mathrm{OPT}_N/N\) converges along admissible integers. For every fixed pair \((K_C,K_S)\), its limit is exactly computable in time polynomial in \((m,\tau,L)\). Moreover, there is a deterministic asymptotic display algorithm satisfying

\(\displaystyle \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{F(G_N)}{N}=V.\)

Thus the requested \(0.067\)-approximation is achievable. The proof uses a polynomial-size linear program and an explicit realization of its rational solutions.

1. Local profiles determine edge contributions

Write \(n=|A|\). Define the customer and supplier profile sets

\(\displaystyle \mathcal C=\left\{c\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}^{B}:\sum_{b\in B}c_b\leq K_C\right\},\qquad \mathcal D=\left\{d\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}^{A}:\sum_{a\in A}d_a\leq K_S\right\}.\)

A customer has profile \(c\) when it has exactly \(c_b\) displayed neighbors of supplier type \(b\). Supplier profiles are defined analogously. These profiles record multiplicities of types, while the displayed neighbors remain distinct individuals.

For customer type \(a\) and profile \(c\), and supplier type \(b\) and profile \(d\), define

\(\displaystyle C_{a,c}=1+\sum_{b'\in B}c_{b'}v_{ab'},\qquad S_{b,d}=1+\sum_{a'\in A}d_{a'}w_{ba'}.\)

Both denominators are positive because the outside-option weight is \(1\). Define the edge coefficient

\(\displaystyle h_{a,c,b,d}=\frac{v_{ab}w_{ba}}{C_{a,c}S_{b,d}}.\)

Consider an edge joining a customer of type \(a\), profile \(c\), to a supplier of type \(b\), profile \(d\). The customer selects that particular supplier with probability \(v_{ab}/C_{a,c}\); the supplier selects that particular customer with probability \(w_{ba}/S_{b,d}\). Independence of the agents’ selections therefore gives mutual-selection probability \(h_{a,c,b,d}\).

Consequently, linearity of expectation gives

\(\displaystyle F(G)=\sum_{(i,j)\in E(G)}h_{a(i),c(i),b(j),d(j)}.\)

No independence between different edge-match events is needed.

2. A linear program over profile and edge masses

Introduce nonnegative variables:

  • \(x_{a,c}\): customer mass of type \(a\) assigned profile \(c\);
  • \(y_{b,d}\): supplier mass of type \(b\) assigned profile \(d\);
  • \(z_{a,c,b,d}\): edge mass joining those two labeled profiles.

Consider the following linear program, with optimum denoted by \(H\):

\(\displaystyle H=\max\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\sum_{b\in B}\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}h_{a,c,b,d}z_{a,c,b,d},\)

subject to the type-mass constraints

\(\displaystyle \sum_{c\in\mathcal C}x_{a,c}=\mu_a\quad(a\in A),\qquad \sum_{d\in\mathcal D}y_{b,d}=\nu_b\quad(b\in B),\)

and the incidence-balance constraints

\(\displaystyle \sum_{d\in\mathcal D}z_{a,c,b,d}=c_bx_{a,c} \quad(a\in A,\ c\in\mathcal C,\ b\in B),\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{c\in\mathcal C}z_{a,c,b,d}=d_ay_{b,d} \quad(b\in B,\ d\in\mathcal D,\ a\in A).\)

These equations count the incidences required by each profile, separately for every opposite-side type.

The program is feasible: put every agent in the zero profile and set every edge variable to zero. It is bounded: the mass constraints bound \(x\) and \(y\), while nonnegativity and the first incidence equations give

\(\displaystyle 0\leq z_{a,c,b,d}\leq c_bx_{a,c}\leq K_C.\)

Thus an optimal solution exists, and \(H\geq 0\).

Every finite display gives a feasible LP solution

For an admissible population \(N\), take any feasible graph \(G\). Set \(x_{a,c}\) and \(y_{b,d}\) equal to the respective numbers of agents with those labeled profiles, divided by \(N\). Set \(z_{a,c,b,d}\) equal to the corresponding number of edges, divided by \(N\).

Counting incidences proves every LP constraint. The formula from Section 1 proves that its objective is \(F(G)/N\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{OPT}_N}{N}\leq H\)

for every admissible \(N\).

The remaining issue is whether the LP’s aggregate incidences can be realized by simple graphs. The next two sections prove that every rational feasible solution can be realized after finite replication.

3. Integer realization as a multigraph

Fix a rational feasible LP solution \((x,y,z)\). Choose a positive integer \(D\) clearing the denominators of all its coordinates and all input masses. Thus

\(\displaystyle Dx_{a,c},\quad Dy_{b,d},\quad Dz_{a,c,b,d},\quad D\mu_a,\quad D\nu_b\)

are nonnegative integers.

Create \(Dx_{a,c}\) customer vertices labeled \((a,c)\), and \(Dy_{b,d}\) supplier vertices labeled \((b,d)\). Their total number is \(D\), by population normalization.

Give every customer vertex labeled \((a,c)\) exactly \(c_b\) distinguishable stubs labeled \(b\), for each \(b\in B\). Give every supplier vertex labeled \((b,d)\) exactly \(d_a\) distinguishable stubs labeled \(a\), for each \(a\in A\).

For each \((a,c,b)\), the available customer stubs number \(Dc_bx_{a,c}\). The first incidence equation gives

\(\displaystyle Dc_bx_{a,c}=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}Dz_{a,c,b,d}.\)

Partition these stubs into groups indexed by \(d\), with the indicated sizes. Likewise, the second incidence equation permits the supplier stubs for \((b,d,a)\) to be partitioned into groups indexed by \(c\), of sizes \(Dz_{a,c,b,d}\).

For each quadruple \((a,c,b,d)\), pair its customer and supplier stub groups by any bijection. This constructs a bipartite multigraph \(Q\) having:

  • the prescribed vertex counts;
  • the prescribed neighbor-type multiplicities, counting parallel edges;
  • exactly \(Dz_{a,c,b,d}\) edges of each labeled-profile pair.

This multigraph is only a construction scaffold; it is not used as a feasible display.

Every customer degree is at most \(K_C\), and every supplier degree is at most \(K_S\). Hence the multiplicity of any pair of endpoints is at most

\(\displaystyle r=\min\{K_C,K_S\}.\)

4. A constant-size lift makes the graph simple

Replace every vertex \(u\) of \(Q\) by copies \((u,k)\), indexed by \(k\in\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z\).

For each pair of base endpoints \(u,v\) having \(t\) parallel edges, assign those edges distinct shifts from \(\{0,\ldots,t-1\}\). This is possible because \(t\leq r\). Replace a base edge with assigned shift \(s\) by the \(r\) edges

\(\displaystyle (u,k)(v,k+s),\qquad k\in\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z.\)

Call the resulting graph \(\widetilde Q\).

Simplicity. If two lifted edges had the same endpoints, their base endpoints would also agree. The customer-copy index and supplier-copy index determine the shift modulo \(r\). Distinct parallel base edges have distinct shifts, so they cannot produce the same lifted edge. Thus \(\widetilde Q\) is simple.

Profile preservation. Each customer copy receives exactly one lifted edge for each incident base edge. Each supplier copy also receives exactly one: for supplier-copy index \(\ell\) and shift \(s\), the corresponding customer-copy index is uniquely \(k=\ell-s\). Neighbor types are inherited from the base vertices. Hence every copy has exactly its prescribed profile and satisfies its degree cap.

Population and value. Put \(T=rD\). There are exactly \(T\mu_a\) customers of type \(a\) and \(T\nu_b\) suppliers of type \(b\). The number of edges between labeled profiles \((a,c)\) and \((b,d)\) is \(Tz_{a,c,b,d}\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle \frac{F(\widetilde Q)}{T} =\sum_{a,c,b,d}h_{a,c,b,d}z_{a,c,b,d}.\)

In particular, a rational optimal LP solution produces an admissible population block with

\(\displaystyle F(\widetilde Q)=TH.\)

5. Convergence and an asymptotically optimal recipe

Choose a rational optimal LP solution and construct its block size \(T\) as above.

For every admissible \(N\), let \(q=\lfloor N/T\rfloor\). Define \(G_N\) to consist of \(q\) disjoint copies of \(\widetilde Q\), with all remaining agents isolated.

The remaining number of customers of each type is

\(\displaystyle N\mu_a-qT\mu_a=(N-qT)\mu_a.\)

This is a nonnegative integer because both \(N\) and \(T\) are admissible. The same holds for suppliers. Thus \(G_N\) uses exactly the required population and is feasible.

Disjoint copies preserve all displayed neighborhoods, so Section 1 gives

\(\displaystyle F(G_N)=qTH.\)

Combining this construction with the LP upper bound yields

\(\displaystyle \frac{qT}{N}H =\frac{F(G_N)}{N} \leq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}_N}{N} \leq H.\)

Since \(0\leq N-qT<T\), we have \(qT/N\to 1\) along admissible integers. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{OPT}_N}{N} =\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{F(G_N)}{N} =H.\)

Therefore \(V=H\). The recipe is deterministic and asymptotically optimal, so in particular

\(\displaystyle \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E[F(G_N)]}{N} =V\geq 0.067V.\)

6. Polynomial preprocessing and expansion

The profile counts are

\(\displaystyle |\mathcal C|=\binom{m+K_C}{K_C},\qquad |\mathcal D|=\binom{n+K_S}{K_S}.\)

These follow by introducing a slack coordinate and counting nonnegative integer vectors with a fixed total. For fixed caps, both counts are polynomial in \(\tau=n+m\).

The LP has

\(\displaystyle n|\mathcal C|+m|\mathcal D|+nm|\mathcal C||\mathcal D|\)

variables, and a polynomial number of constraints. Each objective coefficient is rational with polynomial encoding length: its denominators are sums of input rationals multiplied by integers bounded by the fixed caps, followed by a product of two such denominators.

The standard exact rational linear-programming theorem provides, in time polynomial in the encoded LP length, a rational optimal solution of polynomial encoding length for a nonempty bounded rational LP. Section 2 verified nonemptiness and boundedness. Thus computing an optimal \((x,y,z)\) takes polynomial preprocessing time.

The integer \(D\) can be the least common multiple of the coordinate and mass denominators. Its binary length is at most the sum of their binary lengths, hence polynomial in the input length. Computing \(D\), and then \(T=rD\), also takes polynomial time. Their numerical values may be exponentially large; the recipe stores them in binary.

The finite recipe stores:

  • the profile lists and rational optimal solution;
  • the integers \(D\) and \(T\);
  • the deterministic stub-pairing, lifting, and replication instructions.

It does not expand the population block during preprocessing.

To expand the recipe at population \(N\):

  • If \(N<T\), output the empty display.
  • If \(N\geq T\), build the scaffold and its lift, whose sizes are at most \(N\), then output the required copies and isolated agents.

With fixed degree caps, these constructions involve a polynomial number of operations in \(N\) and the input length. Grouping parallel base edges and assigning shifts can also be done in polynomial time.

Thus both preprocessing and expansion meet the stated requirements. There is no asserted polynomial bound on the population needed to approach the limit; the definition requires asymptotic performance, and the binary-encoded block construction supplies it.

7. Conclusion

The profile LP exactly characterizes the mass-limit optimum. Its rational solutions are realizable after finite replication because a constant-size cyclic lift removes all parallel edges while preserving every local profile.

The cited finite-agent \(0.067\) guarantee is stated in [Theorem \(6.6\) of El Housni, Hennebelle, and Torrico](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08929v6#S6). The construction here proves the stronger asymptotic guarantee under the task’s fixed-cap, support-encoded assumptions.
