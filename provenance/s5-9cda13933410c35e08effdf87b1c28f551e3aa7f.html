The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\alpha_t\) summing to \(1\), rational assignment bids \(b_{tj}\), and rational per-capita position supplies \(\kappa_j\) with \(\sum_j\kappa_j\leq 1\), compute maximal type-uniform bicore feedback. The aggregate assignment maximizes \(\sum_{t,j} b_{tj}x_{tj}\) subject to \(\sum_j x_{tj}\leq\alpha_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{tj}\leq\kappa_j\). Feedback must preserve every optimal aggregate assignment under every admissible combination of discount and raise updates to type submasses.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Input consists of masses \(\alpha_t>0\) with \(\sum_t\alpha_t=1\), nonnegative rational bids \(b_{tj}\), and positive rational supplies \(\kappa_j\) for \(m\geq1\) positions, with \(\sum_j\kappa_j\leq1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length. Every type has positive value for every position, including positions with zero bids; being unassigned yields zero bid and value.
An aggregate assignment is \(x\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_jx_{tj}\leq\alpha_t\) and \(\sum_tx_{tj}\leq\kappa_j\). Let \(X^*\) be the assignments maximizing \(\sum_{t,j}b_{tj}x_{tj}\). Represent each type by an atomless interval of measure \(\alpha_t\); a realization of \(x\) measurably assigns its points to at most one position each, with assigned masses \(x_{tj}\).
Type-uniform feedback consists of nonnegative scalars \((\pi_t,\mu_t)\). Writing \(q_t(x)=\sum_jx_{tj}\), validity requires \(\mu_t=0\) if some \(x\in X^*\) has \(q_t(x)>0\), \(\pi_t=0\) if some \(x\in X^*\) has \(q_t(x)<\alpha_t\), and \(\pi_t\leq b_{tj}\) whenever some \(x\in X^*\) has \(x_{tj}>0\); all applicable conditions hold simultaneously.
An update selects arbitrary disjoint measurable submasses to discount or raise, leaving other points unchanged. A discounted point of type \(t\) bids \(\max\{b_{tj}-\pi_t,0\}\) at every position. A raised point bids \(b_{tj}+\mu_t\) where \(b_{tj}>0\), and any measurable choice in \([0,\mu_t]\) where \(b_{tj}=0\); its unassigned bid remains zero. These are hypothetical updates with no valuation cap.
Define the continuous bicore as the valid feedback for which, under every such update and every permitted choice of raised zero bids, every realization of every \(x\in X^*\) remains optimal among feasible measurable assignments, evaluated by integrated updated bids. Compute feedback maximizing \(\sum_t\alpha_t(\pi_t+\mu_t)\).

Is this continuous bicore feedback optimization solvable in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite unit-demand assignment auctions, the bicore is exactly the projection of the optimal assignment-dual solutions, giving polynomial-time optimization of total feedback. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17058v1)
No result settling this exact continuous formulation was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified nonnegative bids and positive supplies; zero-supply positions are removed, and at least one position remains, ensuring nonempty feasible winning support.
  • Specified positive valuation support at every position, even for zero bids, following the unrestricted assignment formulation; being unassigned has zero value.
  • Interpreted type-uniform validity across all optimal assignments: any potentially assigned type receives zero raise, any potentially unassigned type receives zero discount, and discounts cannot exceed any optimally assigned position bid.
  • Defined submass updates using atomless type intervals and arbitrary disjoint measurable subsets, including every permitted raise at a zero bid.
  • Interpreted preservation as preservation of every measurable realization of every optimal aggregate assignment, so identities of updating submasses are retained.
  • Interpreted maximal feedback using the total-feedback-maximizing bicore policy, with the discrete sum replaced by the mass-weighted sum.
  • Used hypothetical uncapped raises because valuations are absent from the input and the assignment feedback characterization has no valuation-cap parameter.
  • Retained optional assignment and disposal as stated in the continuous question, rather than imposing allocation of all supply.

Result

The continuous bicore feedback optimization is solvable in polynomial time in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).

An exact linear programming characterization follows below. The proof directly handles arbitrary measurable submass updates and all permitted choices of raised zero bids. It does not require expanding rational masses into individual bidders.

The related finite assignment characterization appears in [Theorem 3 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17058v1#S5). The continuous characterization is proved here independently.

1. The original assignment problem and its dual

Write \(q_t(x)=\sum_jx_{tj}\) and \(c_j(x)=\sum_tx_{tj}\). Let \(V\) be the optimal value of the original assignment linear program:

\[ \displaystyle V=\max_{x\geq0}\left\{\sum_{t,j}b_{tj}x_{tj}:q_t(x)\leq\alpha_t,\ c_j(x)\leq\kappa_j\right\}. \]

Its dual has variables \(u_t\geq0\) and \(p_j\geq0\), and is

\[ \displaystyle \min\left\{\sum_t\alpha_tu_t+\sum_j\kappa_jp_j:u_t+p_j\geq b_{tj}\text{ for every }t,j\right\}. \]

The primal is nonempty and compact: the zero assignment is feasible, and every coordinate satisfies \(0\leq x_{tj}\leq\alpha_t\). Thus its optimum is attained and finite. Finite-dimensional linear programming duality gives an attained dual optimum of value \(V\).

For any primal-feasible \(x\) and dual-feasible \((u,p)\), the duality gap has the following decomposition:

\[ \displaystyle \sum_t\alpha_tu_t+\sum_j\kappa_jp_j-\sum_{t,j}b_{tj}x_{tj} = \sum_tu_t\bigl(\alpha_t-q_t(x)\bigr) +\sum_jp_j\bigl(\kappa_j-c_j(x)\bigr) +\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}(u_t+p_j-b_{tj}). \tag{1} \]

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Consequently, every original optimal assignment and every original optimal dual solution satisfy complementary slackness with each other.

2. Exact characterization of the continuous bicore

Theorem. Nonnegative feedback \((\pi,\mu)\) belongs to the continuous bicore if and only if there exist nonnegative \(u,p\) satisfying

\[ u_t\geq\pi_t\qquad\text{for every }t, \tag{2} \]

\[ u_t+p_j\geq b_{tj}+\mu_t\qquad\text{for every }t,j, \tag{3} \]

and

\[ \displaystyle \sum_t\alpha_tu_t+\sum_j\kappa_jp_j=V. \tag{4} \]

In particular, these constraints also imply every validity requirement in the question.

2.1. The constraints imply validity

Assume (2)–(4). Because \(\mu_t\geq0\), condition (3) makes \((u,p)\) feasible for the original dual. Equation (4) makes it optimal.

Fix any \(x\in X^*\). By (1), complementary slackness gives:

  • If \(x_{tj}>0\), then \(u_t+p_j=b_{tj}\). Combining this equality with (3) gives \(\mu_t=0\), and with (2) gives \(\pi_t\leq u_t\leq b_{tj}\).
  • If \(q_t(x)<\alpha_t\), then \(u_t=0\), so (2) gives \(\pi_t=0\).
  • If \(c_j(x)<\kappa_j\), then \(p_j=0\).

Since \(x\) was arbitrary, these conclusions establish all the required validity conditions simultaneously.

2.2. Sufficiency for every measurable update

Fix an arbitrary \(x\in X^*\), an arbitrary realization \(A\) of \(x\), and an arbitrary permitted update.

Let \(I_t\) be the interval representing type \(t\). For a point \(a\in I_t\), define

\[ h(a)= \begin{cases} u_t-\pi_t,&\text{if }a\text{ is discounted},\\ u_t,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

Condition (2) ensures \(h(a)\geq0\). Let \(B_j(a)\) denote the updated bid of point \(a\) for position \(j\). We claim that

\[ B_j(a)\leq h(a)+p_j. \tag{5} \]

There are three cases.

  • Unchanged point. Original dual feasibility gives \(b_{tj}\leq u_t+p_j\).
  • Discounted point. Original dual feasibility and \(u_t-\pi_t+p_j\geq0\) give

\[ \max\{b_{tj}-\pi_t,0\}\leq u_t-\pi_t+p_j. \]

  • Raised point. Every permitted raised bid, including a raised zero bid, satisfies

\[ B_j(a)\leq b_{tj}+\mu_t\leq u_t+p_j \]
by (3).

Thus (5) holds pointwise.

For any feasible measurable assignment \(Y\), integrate (5) over its assigned points. Since \(h\geq0\), \(p\geq0\), and its assigned mass at position \(j\) is at most \(\kappa_j\), its updated objective satisfies

\[ \displaystyle \operatorname{Bid}(Y) \leq \int h(a)\,da+\sum_j\kappa_jp_j. \tag{6} \]

We now show that the original realization \(A\) attains this bound.

Suppose \(A\) assigns a point of type \(t\) to position \(j\). Apart from null sets, this implies \(x_{tj}>0\), hence

\[ u_t+p_j=b_{tj},\qquad \mu_t=0,\qquad \pi_t\leq b_{tj}. \]

At that point, an unchanged or raised bid equals \(u_t+p_j\), while a discounted bid equals \(u_t-\pi_t+p_j\). In every case, its updated bid equals \(h(a)+p_j\).

If \(A\) leaves a positive mass of type \(t\) unassigned, then \(q_t(x)<\alpha_t\). Hence \(u_t=\pi_t=0\), so \(h=0\) on that unassigned mass.

Finally, complementary slackness gives \(p_j(\kappa_j-c_j(x))=0\). Therefore

\[ \displaystyle \operatorname{Bid}(A) = \int h(a)\,da+\sum_jp_jc_j(x) = \int h(a)\,da+\sum_j\kappa_jp_j. \]

Together with (6), this proves that \(A\) remains optimal. All the choices were arbitrary, establishing sufficiency for the exact universal quantifiers in the question.

2.3. Necessity: splitting types into discounted and unchanged halves

Now suppose \((\pi,\mu)\) belongs to the continuous bicore. In particular, it is valid.

Define the set of never-assigned types by

\[ N=\{t:q_t(x)=0\text{ for every }x\in X^*\}. \]

Validity implies \(\pi_t=0\) for \(t\in N\), and \(\mu_t=0\) for \(t\notin N\).

Choose any \(x^0\in X^*\) and any realization \(A^0\). Construct the following single permitted update.

  • For each \(t\notin N\), partition every assigned cell of \(A^0\), and its unassigned cell, into two measurable halves. Discount one half and leave the other unchanged.
  • For each \(t\in N\), raise its entire interval, choosing the raised bid \(b_{tj}+\mu_t\) at every position. This includes choosing \(\mu_t\) wherever \(b_{tj}=0\), which is permitted by the assumptions.

The required halves exist because the intervals, and their measurable subsets, are atomless.

For \(t\notin N\), denote the unchanged group by \(U_t\) and the discounted group by \(D_t\). Each has mass \(\alpha_t/2\), with position bids

\[ b_{tj}\quad\text{and}\quad \max\{b_{tj}-\pi_t,0\}, \]

respectively. For \(t\in N\), denote its raised group by \(R_t\), of mass \(\alpha_t\) and bids \(b_{tj}+\mu_t\).

Updated bids are constant within each group. Hence the updated measurable assignment problem is exactly a finite assignment linear program on these groups: every feasible group-level assignment can be realized by measurable partitions of the groups.

The original realization induces the group assignment

\[ y^0_{U_tj}=y^0_{D_tj}=x^0_{tj}/2\quad(t\notin N), \qquad y^0_{R_tj}=0\quad(t\in N). \]

Bicore robustness makes this assignment optimal for the updated group problem. Take an optimal dual of that problem, with nonnegative group variables \(r_{U_t},r_{D_t},r_{R_t}\) and nonnegative position variables \(p_j\).

Define

\[ u_t= \begin{cases} r_{U_t},&t\notin N,\\ 0,&t\in N. \end{cases} \]

We verify (2)–(4).

Verification of (3). For \(t\notin N\), unchanged-group dual feasibility gives

\[ u_t+p_j\geq b_{tj}=b_{tj}+\mu_t. \]

For \(t\in N\), its raised group is entirely unassigned in the optimal \(y^0\). Its row constraint is strictly slack, so complementary slackness gives \(r_{R_t}=0\). Raised-group dual feasibility therefore gives

\[ p_j\geq b_{tj}+\mu_t. \]

Thus (3) holds for all types.

Verification of (2). For \(t\in N\), both \(u_t\) and \(\pi_t\) are zero. Consider \(t\notin N\).

If \(q_t(x^0)=0\), the unchanged group is entirely unassigned, so \(u_t=r_{U_t}=0\). Validity also gives \(\pi_t=0\).

Otherwise choose \(j\) with \(x^0_{tj}>0\). Both corresponding group edges are used. Validity gives \(\pi_t\leq b_{tj}\), and complementary slackness gives

\[ r_{U_t}+p_j=b_{tj}, \qquad r_{D_t}+p_j=b_{tj}-\pi_t. \]

Subtracting yields \(u_t=r_{D_t}+\pi_t\geq\pi_t\). This proves (2).

Verification of (4). The constructed \((u,p)\) is feasible for the original dual and complementary to \(x^0\):

  • Every used original edge is an unchanged-group edge, hence \(u_t+p_j=b_{tj}\).
  • If \(q_t(x^0)<\alpha_t\), the unchanged-group row is slack when \(t\notin N\), giving \(u_t=0\); for \(t\in N\), this holds by definition.
  • Group splitting preserves each position’s assigned mass, so updated complementary slackness gives \(p_j(\kappa_j-c_j(x^0))=0\).

Every term on the right of (1) is therefore zero. Since \(x^0\) has value \(V\), equation (4) follows. This completes the characterization.

3. Polynomial-time optimization

First compute \(V\) by solving the original assignment linear program. Then solve the following rational linear program:

\[ \displaystyle \max\sum_t\alpha_t(\pi_t+\mu_t) \]

subject to

\[ u_t,p_j,\pi_t,\mu_t\geq0,\qquad u_t\geq\pi_t,\qquad u_t+p_j\geq b_{tj}+\mu_t, \]

and

\[ \displaystyle \sum_t\alpha_tu_t+\sum_j\kappa_jp_j=V. \]

The characterization proves that its projection onto \((\pi,\mu)\) is exactly the continuous bicore.

This second program has \(3\tau+m\) variables and \(\tau m+\tau\) inequalities besides nonnegativity and one equality. No separate computation of the optimal face or validity conditions is needed.

It is nonempty: take any original optimal dual and set \(\pi=\mu=0\). It is bounded because positivity of every mass and supply gives

\[ u_t\leq V/\alpha_t,\qquad p_j\leq V/\kappa_j. \]

Also \(\pi_t\leq u_t\), and, using any position \(j\), condition (3) gives \(\mu_t\leq u_t+p_j\). Thus the maximum is attained.

Both programs have polynomially many variables and constraints. Their rational coefficients, including the computed optimum \(V\), have polynomial encoding length. Polynomial-time algorithms for rational linear programming therefore compute an exact optimal feedback vector in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\).
