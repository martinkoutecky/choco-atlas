The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) of complete types \(t=(x,y,c)\), a rational society distribution \(\mu\) with \(\mu_t=n_t/N\), a sparse-regression model class, and target accuracy and incentive parameters, find the minimum expected total payment of a type-mass mechanism. The mechanism maps reported-response mass to an estimator and payment rates; require error at most \(\eta_{\mathrm{acc}}\), \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-joint differential privacy under a one-participant mass change of \(1/N\), truthful reporting as an \(\eta_{\mathrm{IC}}\)-Bayesian Nash equilibrium for at least \(1-\alpha\) of the mass, and individual rationality for that mass.

Positive supplied bounds satisfy \(\|\Sigma w\|_\infty\ge\kappa_\infty\|w\|_\infty\), \(\|\Sigma^{-1/2}x\|_{\psi_2}\le\kappa_x\), and \(\max_i\sum_j|\Sigma_{ij}|^q\le s\), with \(0\le q<1\); at \(q=0\), count nonzero entries. Also assume \(\Pr_\mu(c>u\mid x,y)\le e^{-\lambda u}\) for every supported \((x,y)\) and \(u\ge0\), with supplied \(\lambda>0\).

Result

Under the intended finite-input interpretation, with the supplied scalar parameters encoded as binary rationals, the decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\).

Two features of the formulation matter: the supplied society determines \(\theta^*\), and payments have no upper bound. These simplify estimation and privacy, but leave a nontrivial minimum-budget problem. Below, I solve that problem exactly, including the continuum of higher-cost reporting profiles.

1. Exact estimation and private implementation of expected payments

Put \(\gamma=(1+\delta)\epsilon^3\).

First, the covariance assumption implies that \(\Sigma\) is invertible: if \(\Sigma w=0\), then \(0\ge\kappa_\infty\|w\|_\infty\), so \(w=0\).

The conditional-mean assumption gives

\(\mathbb E_\mu[xy]=\mathbb E_\mu[x(x^\top\theta^*+\zeta)]=\Sigma\theta^*\),

because \(\mathbb E[x\zeta]=\mathbb E[x\,\mathbb E[\zeta\mid x]]=0\). Consequently,

\(\theta^*=\Sigma^{-1}\sum_t\mu_t x_t y_t.\)

This is a rational vector computable from the input. Its norm is at most one by the promise. Thus the mechanism can release this constant vector on its entire domain, obtaining zero estimation error. The supplied society is fixed when defining the mechanism; changing its reported input profile does not change this hardwired vector.

Next, consider any deterministic, nonnegative proposed expected-payment rule \(f(x,r;\rho)\). Implement it as follows:

  • With probability \(1-\delta\), pay everyone zero.
  • With probability \(\delta\), pay each participant \(f(x,r;\rho)/\delta\).

Always release the constant estimator \(\theta^*\). Each participant’s expected payment is exactly \(f(x,r;\rho)\).

For any two adjacent inputs and any measurable event \(E\) in the protected output, its distribution has the form

\(P_\rho=(1-\delta)Q+\delta R_\rho,\)

where \(Q\) is the same point mass on the constant estimator and zero payments. Hence

\(P_\rho(E)-P_{\rho'}(E)=\delta\bigl(R_\rho(E)-R_{\rho'}(E)\bigr)\le\delta.\)

This proves \((0,\delta)\)-joint differential privacy, and therefore \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-joint differential privacy, throughout the mechanism domain. The shared lottery preserves anonymity.

Thus privacy imposes no additional restriction on achievable expected-payment rules here. The construction uses the absence of an upper bound on realized payments.

2. Reporting profiles for a fixed threshold

Fix a threshold \(h\). Let \(\mathcal X\) be the set of listed feature vectors. For \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(r\in\mathcal Y\), define

\(\ell_{xr}=\sum_{t:x_t=x,\ y_t=r,\ c_t\le h}\mu_t,\qquad F_x=\sum_{t:x_t=x}\mu_t.\)

Write

\(A_x=\{r\in\mathcal Y:\ell_{xr}>0\},\qquad n_{xr}=N\ell_{xr},\qquad K_x=N\left(F_x-\sum_r\ell_{xr}\right).\)

The numbers \(n_{xr}\) and \(K_x\) are nonnegative integers by the input integrality promise. For \(r\in A_x\), \(n_{xr}\ge1\).

The admissible higher-cost reporting profiles are exactly the polytope

\(\mathcal P_h=\left\{\rho:\rho_{xr}\ge\ell_{xr},\ \sum_{r\in\mathcal Y}\rho_{xr}=F_x\text{ for every }x\in\mathcal X\right\},\)

with zero mass outside these features.

Indeed, reporting preserves each feature’s total mass. Conversely, any excess masses satisfying these conditions can be produced by splitting the higher-cost mass at that feature among reports.

For \(\rho\in\mathcal P_h\), put

\(v_{xr}=N(\rho_{xr}-\ell_{xr}).\)

Then \(v_{xr}\ge0\) and \(\sum_r v_{xr}=K_x\). These variables may be fractional.

Let \(f_{xr}(\rho)\) denote the expected payment of any proposed mechanism. For a truthful tagged type at \((x,r)\), changing its report to \(s\) changes the profile to

\(\rho'=\rho+(e_{xs}-e_{xr})/N.\)

Because the privacy-cost term is identical for every report, the incentive condition is exactly

\(f_{xs}(\rho')-f_{xr}(\rho)\le\eta_{\mathrm{IC}}.\)

3. The pointwise minimum expected-payment rule

For \(r\in A_x\), define

\(d_{xr}=\gamma\max\{c_t:x_t=x,\ y_t=r,\ c_t\le h\}.\)

Individual rationality requires \(f_{xr}(\rho)\ge d_{xr}\) at every \(\rho\in\mathcal P_h\). Taking the maximum is necessary because costs are unreported and all these types receive the same expected payment.

For features with \(A_x\ne\varnothing\), put

\(D_x=\max_{r\in A_x}d_{xr},\qquad e_{xr}=\max\{d_{xr},D_x-\eta_{\mathrm{IC}}\},\qquad t_{xr}=e_{xr}-d_{xr}.\)

Define the following rule on \(\mathcal P_h\):

\(f^*_{xr}(\rho)=d_{xr}\) if \(r\in A_x\) and \(v_{xr}<1\);

\(f^*_{xr}(\rho)=e_{xr}\) if \(r\in A_x\) and \(v_{xr}\ge1\);

\(f^*_{xr}(\rho)=0\) if \(r\notin A_x\).

Set all payments to zero outside \(\mathcal P_h\).

Necessity

Individual rationality already gives the lower bound \(d_{xr}\).

Suppose \(v_{xr}\ge1\), and choose \(s\in A_x\) with \(d_{xs}=D_x\). If \(s=r\), individual rationality gives \(f_{xr}(\rho)\ge D_x\).

Otherwise, removing one participant’s mass from report \(r\) and adding it to report \(s\) leaves the profile inside \(\mathcal P_h\). Individual rationality at that new profile gives \(f_{xs}(\rho')\ge D_x\). The incentive condition therefore gives

\(f_{xr}(\rho)\ge D_x-\eta_{\mathrm{IC}}.\)

Combining the bounds proves \(f_{xr}(\rho)\ge e_{xr}\). At cells outside \(A_x\), nonnegativity gives the lower bound zero.

Thus every feasible mechanism has expected payments at least \(f^*\), pointwise on all admissible profiles.

Sufficiency

Individual rationality follows directly from \(f^*_{xr}\ge d_{xr}\).

Consider a truthful tagged participant changing report from \(r\in A_x\) to \(s\).

If \(s=r\), the gain is zero. If the new profile is outside \(\mathcal P_h\), or \(s\notin A_x\), its deviation payment is zero, so the incentive condition holds.

In the remaining case, \(s\ne r\), \(s\in A_x\), and \(\rho'\in\mathcal P_h\). Necessarily \(v_{xr}\ge1\); also \(v'_{xs}=v_{xs}+1\ge1\). The truthful and deviation payments are therefore \(e_{xr}\) and \(e_{xs}\). Since

\(e_{xr}\ge D_x-\eta_{\mathrm{IC}}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{xs}\le D_x,\)

their difference is at most \(\eta_{\mathrm{IC}}\).

This proves all incentive conditions. Pointwise validity also implies validity after averaging over randomized higher-cost reporting.

Accordingly, \(f^*\) is the pointwise minimum feasible expected-payment rule for this threshold.

4. Exact worst-case budget

Fix a feature \(x\) with \(A_x\ne\varnothing\). Define

\(C_x^0=\sum_{r\in A_x}n_{xr}d_{xr},\qquad J_{xr}=n_{xr}t_{xr}+e_{xr}-D_x.\)

List the strictly positive scores \(J_{xr}\) in decreasing order as

\(J_{x,(1)}\ge\cdots\ge J_{x,(u_x)}>0.\)

I claim that the exact worst-case expected total payment at this feature is

\(C_x(h)=C_x^0+K_xD_x+\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{K_x,u_x\}}J_{x,(j)}.\)

If \(A_x=\varnothing\), define \(C_x(h)=0\).

Upper bound for every fractional allocation

For an allocation \(v\), let \(S=\{r\in A_x:v_{xr}\ge1\}\). Under \(f^*\), the feature’s contribution to expected total payment is

\(W_x(v)=C_x^0+\sum_{r\in A_x}v_{xr}d_{xr}+\sum_{r\in S}(n_{xr}+v_{xr})t_{xr}.\)

Reports outside \(A_x\) contribute zero. Because all payment rates are at most \(D_x\),

\(W_x(v)\le C_x^0+K_xD_x+\sum_{r\in S}\bigl(n_{xr}t_{xr}+v_{xr}(e_{xr}-D_x)\bigr).\)

For \(r\in S\), we have \(v_{xr}\ge1\) and \(e_{xr}-D_x\le0\). Thus each summand is at most \(J_{xr}\).

Moreover, \(|S|\le K_x\), since every member of \(S\) consumes at least one unit of excess mass. Discarding nonpositive scores and selecting the largest remaining scores gives \(W_x(v)\le C_x(h)\).

This argument applies to all real-valued allocations \(v\).

Attainment

Let \(S\) contain the \(\min\{K_x,u_x\}\) largest positive scores. Assign one unit of excess mass to each report in \(S\).

Choose \(b\in A_x\) with \(d_{xb}=D_x\), and assign all remaining \(K_x-|S|\) units to \(b\). Here \(e_{xb}=D_x\), \(t_{xb}=0\), and \(J_{xb}=0\), so \(b\notin S\).

Every selected report contributes exactly its score above the baseline \(C_x^0+K_xD_x\). The resulting budget is exactly \(C_x(h)\).

Thus an integral allocation attains the maximum over the entire fractional domain. Allocations at different features are independent, so the exact minimum worst-case budget for threshold \(h\) is

\(C(h)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}C_x(h).\)

5. Decision algorithm and correctness

Enumerate the thresholds in \(\{0\}\cup\{c_t:t\in T\}\). These represent every possible set of types satisfying \(c_t\le h\).

For each candidate:

  • Check \(\sum_{t:c_t\le h}\mu_t\ge1-\alpha\).
  • Compute the quantities above and \(C(h)\).
  • Accept if \(C(h)\le B\).

Reject if no candidate passes.

If a candidate passes, use the constant estimator and implement \(f^*\) with the probability-\(\delta\) payout branch. The preceding proofs establish accuracy, joint privacy, individual rationality, incentives, and the budget bound.

Conversely, any feasible mechanism’s threshold induces one of the enumerated truthful sets. Its expected payments dominate \(f^*\) at every admissible profile, so its worst-case budget is at least \(C(h)\). Therefore its candidate passes.

This proves the algorithm decides the stated problem exactly.

6. Running time and scope

There are at most \(\tau+1\) candidate thresholds and at most \(\tau\) nonempty truthful feature-response cells.

Grouping the listed types takes \(O(m\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational comparisons and operations. For each threshold, forming the quantities and sorting scores takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational operations. Consequently, the decision algorithm uses

\(O\!\left(m\tau\log(\tau+1)+\tau^2\log(\tau+1)\right)\)

rational operations, on numbers of bit length polynomial in \(L\). Its bit running time is therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In particular, it does not enumerate participants: dependence on \(N\) is through its binary encoding, rather than its numerical value.

Constructing the hardwired estimator additionally takes \(O(\tau m^2+m^3)\) rational operations, with polynomial bit complexity. The constructed payment rule has a polynomial-size description. Dependence on \(\epsilon,\delta,\alpha,B,\eta_{\mathrm{IC}}\) is through their encoding lengths; no numerical dependence on \(1/\delta\) is needed for the decision algorithm. The parameters \(k,\eta_{\mathrm{acc}}\) and the other statistical bounds impose promises but require no additional optimization.

The [cited paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/7b871f53ba2ee540ef76e6220b462e16-Paper-Conference.pdf) constructs a mechanism for independently sampled agents. The result above instead uses the task’s supplied fixed society and unrestricted realized payments to solve its exact minimum-budget decision problem.
