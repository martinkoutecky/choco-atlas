The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given public buyer classes \(a\), a finite support of private types \(t=(a,v,b)\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), class caps \(\kappa_a\), and supply \(q\) per buyer, optimize expected revenue per buyer over direct menus with deterministic allocations \(x_{a,r}\in[0,\kappa_a]\) and payment lotteries \(P_{a,r}\), where reports \(r\) are valuation-budget pairs. Require voluntary participation and dominant-strategy truthfulness for each type, treating utility as \(-\infty\) whenever a payment exceeds its true budget with positive probability. Require supply feasibility for every report distribution, equivalently \(\sum_a \rho_a\max_{r}x_{a,r}\le q\), where \(\rho_a=\sum_{t:a(t)=a}\mu_t\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types \(t=(a,v,b)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). There are \(m\) public buyer classes; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Values and budgets are nonnegative binary rationals; class quantity caps \(\kappa_a\ge0\) and divisible supply per buyer \(q>0\) are binary rationals. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Let \(V\) and \(B\) contain the supported valuation and budget coordinates, respectively, together with \(0\); the private type and report domain in every class is \(R=V\times B\), including pairs of zero mass. Classes cannot be misreported.
A direct menu assigns each \((a,r)\) a deterministic quantity \(x_{a,r}\in[0,\kappa_a]\) and a finite payment lottery \(P_{a,r}\) with rational outcomes and probabilities. Payments may be negative, but require \(\mathbb E[P_{a,r}]\ge0\) and \(P_{a,(v,b)}\le b\) almost surely.
A true type \((a,v,b)\) reporting \(r\) has expected utility \(v x_{a,r}-\mathbb E[P_{a,r}]\) if \(\Pr(P_{a,r}>b)=0\), and \(-\infty\) otherwise. Require voluntary participation and dominant-strategy truthfulness for every type in \(R\), with expectations only over payment randomness; truth-telling may tie with other reports.
Write \(\rho_a=\sum_{t:a(t)=a}\mu_t\). Require feasibility for every report distribution preserving class masses: \(\sum_a\rho_a\max_{r\in R}x_{a,r}\le q\). Revenue is \(\sum_t\mu_t\mathbb E[P_{a(t),(v(t),b(t))}]\); let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be its supremum over feasible menus.

Result

The problem is exactly solvable in polynomial time. In particular, it admits a polynomial-time \(3+2\sqrt2\)-approximation.

The proof uses both features specified in the question: negative payment outcomes are permitted provided the expected payment is nonnegative, and robust supply is expressed through the maximum allocation in each class. The supremum \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is attained by a menu with rational quantities and at most two payment outcomes per report.

1. An exact linear program

Write \(s=|V|\), \(h=|B|\), and \(d=sh\). Introduce variables \(x_{a,v,b}\) for quantities, \(p_{a,v,b}\) for expected payments, and \(z_a\) for class allocation bounds.

Maximize

\[ \sum_{t=(a,v,b)}\mu_t p_{a,v,b} \]

subject to the following constraints, imposed on every \((a,v,b)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\times V\times B\), including pairs of zero mass:

\[ 0\le x_{a,v,b}\le z_a\le\kappa_a, \]

\[ 0\le p_{a,v,b}\le b,\qquad p_{a,v,b}\le v x_{a,v,b}, \]

\[ \sum_a\rho_a z_a\le q, \]

and, for all \(v,v'\in V\) and \(b,b'\in B\) with \(b'\le b\),

\[ v x_{a,v,b}-p_{a,v,b} \ge v x_{a,v',b'}-p_{a,v',b'}. \]

The last family expresses truthfulness against reports whose budgets do not exceed the true budget. Denote the optimal value by \(Z\).

The zero vector is feasible. All variables are bounded by the finite input caps and budgets, so the feasible region is compact. Consequently, an optimum exists. Since all coefficients are rational, there is a rational optimal solution.

2. Every admissible menu gives a feasible LP solution

Consider any admissible menu and set \(p_{a,v,b}=\mathbb E[P_{a,(v,b)}]\).

The requirement of nonnegative expected payments gives \(p_{a,v,b}\ge0\). Since truthful payment outcomes satisfy \(P_{a,(v,b)}\le b\), their mean satisfies \(p_{a,v,b}\le b\). Voluntary participation gives \(p_{a,v,b}\le v x_{a,v,b}\).

Now consider a true type \((a,v,b)\) and a report \((v',b')\) with \(b'\le b\). Every payment outcome at this report is at most \(b'\), hence at most \(b\). The report is therefore affordable, and dominant-strategy truthfulness implies precisely the displayed incentive inequality.

Finally, take \(z_a=\max_{r\in R}x_{a,r}\). The quantity caps and stipulated robust-supply condition give all remaining constraints.

The objective equals the menu’s revenue. Thus every admissible menu has revenue at most \(Z\), and

\[ \mathrm{OPT}\le Z. \]

This argument does not assume independence of values and budgets.

3. Every rational LP solution can be implemented

Fix a rational feasible LP solution. Assign deterministic quantity \(x_{a,v,b}\). For a report with \(b=0\), charge zero; the LP already forces \(p_{a,v,0}=0\).

For a report with \(b>0\), abbreviate \(p=p_{a,v,b}\), and use the following two-outcome lottery:

  • Charge \(b\) with probability \(\alpha=(b+p)/(2b)\).
  • Charge \(-b\) with probability \(1-\alpha=(b-p)/(2b)\).

Because \(0\le p\le b\), both probabilities are valid, and \(\alpha\ge1/2\). All outcomes and probabilities are rational. The expected payment is

\[ \alpha b+(1-\alpha)(-b) = b(2\alpha-1) = p. \]

Every outcome is at most the reported budget. Negative outcomes are allowed by the question, and the expected payment remains nonnegative. When \(p=b\), omit the outcome of probability zero.

We verify truthfulness for an arbitrary true type \((a,v,b)\) against any report \((v',b')\).

If \(b'\le b\): every payment outcome at the report is affordable. Its expected utility is \(v x_{a,v',b'}-p_{a,v',b'}\), which is at most truthful utility by the LP incentive constraint.

If \(b'>b\): the report charges \(b'\) with probability at least \(1/2\). It therefore gives utility \(-\infty\) under the specified hard-budget rule. Truthful utility is finite and nonnegative.

These two cases cover every report. Thus the menu is truthful on the entire grid, including unsupported types. Voluntary participation follows from \(p_{a,v,b}\le v x_{a,v,b}\).

Moreover,

\[ \sum_a\rho_a\max_{r\in R}x_{a,r} \le \sum_a\rho_a z_a \le q, \]

so robust feasibility holds. The menu’s revenue is exactly the LP objective.

Applying this construction to a rational optimal LP solution proves \(Z\le\mathrm{OPT}\). Together with the preceding upper bound,

\[ \boxed{\mathrm{OPT}=Z.} \]

The positive probability of charging the reported budget persists even when the expected payment is zero. This is what allows upward budget reports to be excluded uniformly.

4. Polynomial size and running time

The LP above has \(2msh+m\) variables and \(O(ms^2h^2)\) constraints. Its coefficients have bit length \(O(L)\), including the class masses obtained by summing supported masses. Thus even this direct formulation gives a polynomial-time exact algorithm.

A smaller equivalent formulation uses only \(O(msh)\) constraints. Here is a proof of that reduction.

Sort the coordinate sets as \(V=\{v_0<\cdots<v_{s-1}\}\) and \(B=\{b_0<\cdots<b_{h-1}\}\). Retain the allocation, payment, participation, and supply constraints. Replace the full incentive family by:

  • Both incentive inequalities between adjacent valuations at each fixed budget.
  • The inequality against the immediately lower budget at each fixed valuation.

To verify equivalence, first fix a class and budget, suppress these indices, and write \(\Delta x_i=x_{i+1}-x_i\) and \(\Delta p_i=p_{i+1}-p_i\). The two adjacent valuation inequalities say

\[ v_i\Delta x_i\le\Delta p_i\le v_{i+1}\Delta x_i. \]

Since \(v_i<v_{i+1}\), these bounds imply \(\Delta x_i\ge0\). For any \(k<i\), summing the bounds therefore gives

\[ v_k(x_i-x_k)\le p_i-p_k\le v_i(x_i-x_k). \]

The right inequality is exactly truthfulness of valuation \(v_i\) against report \(v_k\); the left inequality is truthfulness of valuation \(v_k\) against report \(v_i\). Hence all same-budget valuation inequalities follow.

Next define \(U_{i,j}=v_i x_{a,v_i,b_j}-p_{a,v_i,b_j}\). Adjacent-budget inequalities imply \(U_{i,j}\ge U_{i,\ell}\) whenever \(\ell\le j\). Combining this with same-budget valuation truthfulness gives

\[ U_{i,j} \ge U_{i,\ell} \ge v_i x_{a,v_k,b_\ell}-p_{a,v_k,b_\ell}. \]

This recovers every original downward-budget incentive inequality. Conversely, all retained inequalities belong to the original family, proving equivalence. The argument also covers singleton coordinate sets, where the corresponding adjacent families are empty.

The resulting running-time bounds are:

  • Preprocessing: \(O(\tau+s\log s+h\log h)\) rational additions and comparisons.
  • LP construction: \(O(msh)\) rational operations after preprocessing.
  • Exact optimization: \(\mathrm{poly}(msh,L)\) bit operations, by polynomial-time rational linear programming.
  • Menu construction: \(O(msh)\) rational operations, producing at most \(2msh\) payment outcomes.

The optimal rational solution and constructed lotteries have polynomial encoding length, as follows from the standard bit-size bounds for rational LP vertices and rational arithmetic.

Since \(s,h\le\tau+1\), the overall bit running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\); with the coordinate counts explicit, it is \(\mathrm{poly}(m|V||B|,L)\).

5. Relation to the cited approximation theorem

Section 5 of the [cited finite-bidder paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.3501) likewise uses payment threats to prevent budget overstatement. Its approximation step repairs supply feasibility for an LP based on expected allocations. Here, quantities depend only on class and report, and the class bounds encode robust supply exactly. The LP therefore needs no approximation rounding.

The stated formulation admits exact optimization, and hence the requested approximation ratio.
