The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), finite approval types \(T\), approval sets \(A_t\subseteq C\), and rational population distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), compute a fractional committee \(p\in[0,1]^C\) and a lottery \(X\) over integral \(k\)-committees with \(p_c=\Pr_{W\sim X}[c\in W]\), such that for every submass vector \(z\le\mu\), \(\sum_{c\in\bigcup_{z_t>0}A_t}p_c\ge\sum_t z_t\min\{k,|A_t|\}\); for every type \(t\) and \(q\in[0,\mu_t]\), \(\sum_{c\in A_t}p_c\ge\min\{qk,|A_t|\}\); and every supported committee \(W\) satisfies continuous EJR: for every \(\ell\in[k]\) and every \(z\le\mu\) with \(\sum_tz_t\ge\ell/k\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{z_t>0}A_t\right|\ge\ell\), some type \(t\) with \(z_t>0\) has \(|A_t\cap W|\ge\ell\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\ge1\) candidates and let \(k\) be an integer with \(1\le k\le m\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) distinct nonempty approval sets \(A_t\subseteq C\), with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible approval sets; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Compute a rational fractional committee \(p\in[0,1]^C\), with \(\sum_cp_c=k\), and an explicit finite lottery \(X=((\lambda_j,W_j))_{j=1}^s\), where \(\lambda_j\) are positive binary rationals summing to one, \(W_j\subseteq C\), \(|W_j|=k\), and \(p_c=\sum_{j:c\in W_j}\lambda_j\).
For every real submass vector \(z\) with \(0\le z_t\le\mu_t\), require \(\sum_{c\in\bigcup_{t:z_t>0}A_t}p_c\ge\sum_tz_t\min\{k,|A_t|\}\); the empty union is empty.
For every \(t\in T\) and real \(q\in[0,\mu_t]\), require \(\sum_{c\in A_t}p_c\ge\min\{qk,|A_t|\}\).
Every supported \(W_j\) must satisfy continuous EJR: for every integer \(1\le\ell\le k\) and every such \(z\) with \(\sum_tz_t\ge\ell/k\) and \(|\bigcap_{t:z_t>0}A_t|\ge\ell\), some \(t\) with \(z_t>0\) satisfies \(|A_t\cap W_j|\ge\ell\).

Result and encoding convention

The search problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L,k\).

Here, a binary rational means a rational whose numerator and denominator are encoded in binary. The construction below is a weighted implementation of the BW-MES approach of [Aziz et al., Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.03642v3). We give a self-contained proof for the population-support setting, including real submasses, polynomial bit complexity, and an explicit lottery.

1. Reducing the continuous quantifiers

For \(S\subseteq T\), write
\(U(S)=\bigcup_{t\in S}A_t\), \(\mu(S)=\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\), and \(d_t=\min\{k,|A_t|\}\).
For \(B\subseteq C\), write \(p(B)=\sum_{c\in B}p_c\).

The first requirement is equivalent to
\(p(U(S))\geq\sum_{t\in S}\mu_td_t\) for every \(S\subseteq T\).

Indeed, necessity follows by taking \(z_t=\mu_t\) on \(S\) and zero elsewhere. Conversely, for an arbitrary submass vector with support \(S\), its right-hand side is at most \(\sum_{t\in S}\mu_td_t\), and its union is \(U(S)\).

The second requirement is equivalent to
\(p(A_t)\geq\min\{\mu_tk,|A_t|\}\) for every \(t\), because its right-hand side is nondecreasing in \(q\).

Similarly, continuous EJR is equivalent to the following condition: for every nonempty \(S\subseteq T\) and integer \(1\leq\ell\leq k\) satisfying
\(\mu(S)\geq\ell/k\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{t\in S}A_t\right|\geq\ell\),
some \(t\in S\) has \(|A_t\cap W|\geq\ell\).

For the converse direction of this last equivalence, the support \(S\) of any qualifying \(z\) satisfies \(\mu(S)\geq\sum_tz_t\geq\ell/k\).

These equivalences are used only in the proof; the algorithm does not enumerate subsets of \(T\).

2. Weighted equal shares

Maintain a balance per unit mass \(b_t\), initially \(b_t=k\), and a selected set \(M\), initially empty. Thus type \(t\)'s aggregate remaining budget is \(\mu_tb_t\), and the total initial budget is \(k\).

For each unselected candidate \(c\), define
\(F_c(\rho)=\sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_t\min\{b_t,\rho\}\).

A candidate is affordable precisely when
\(\sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_tb_t\geq1\).
For every affordable candidate, compute the least \(\rho_c\) satisfying \(F_c(\rho_c)=1\). Select a candidate minimizing \(\rho_c\), breaking ties by a fixed order. If its price is \(\rho\), charge every approving type the aggregate amount
\(\mu_t\min\{b_t,\rho\}\),
and update
\(b_t\leftarrow b_t-\min\{b_t,\rho\}\)
for those types.

Record all aggregate payments as \(y_{tc}\). Initially every \(y_{tc}=0\).

Each selection costs exactly one. Consequently, there are at most \(k\) selections. Stop when no unselected candidate is affordable, and denote the resulting balances by \(\bar b_t\). Then

\( \displaystyle \sum_t\mu_t\bar b_t=k-|M|, \qquad \sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_t\bar b_t<1\quad(c\notin M). \)

Every selected candidate has total recorded payment one.

3. The selected set already satisfies continuous EJR

We prove the EJR condition for \(M\), allowing \(|M|<k\). Any committee containing \(M\) will therefore satisfy it as well.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that some \(S\subseteq T\) and integer \(\ell\) satisfy

\( \displaystyle \alpha:=\mu(S)\geq\ell/k,\qquad \left|\bigcap_{t\in S}A_t\right|\geq\ell, \qquad |A_t\cap M|\leq\ell-1\quad(t\in S). \)

There is a candidate \(c^\ast\in\bigcap_{t\in S}A_t\) outside the final set \(M\): otherwise every type in \(S\) would approve at least \(\ell\) selected candidates.

We show inductively that every selection price is at most \(1/\alpha\). Assume this holds for all preceding selections. Each type in \(S\) has paid for at most \(\ell-1\) preceding candidates, with each payment per unit mass at most \(1/\alpha\). Its current balance therefore satisfies

\( \displaystyle b_t\geq k-\frac{\ell-1}{\alpha}\geq\frac1\alpha, \)

where the last inequality follows from \(k\alpha\geq\ell\). This reasoning also establishes the initial step, when no payments have occurred.

The candidate \(c^\ast\) is still unselected. Hence

\( \displaystyle F_{c^\ast}(1/\alpha) \geq\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\min\{b_t,1/\alpha\} =\frac{\alpha}{\alpha}=1. \)

By continuity of \(F_{c^\ast}\), its least affordable price is at most \(1/\alpha\). The algorithm selects a candidate of minimum price, proving the induction step.

Applying the same balance bound after the last selection gives

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t:c^\ast\in A_t}\mu_t\bar b_t \geq\sum_{t\in S}\frac{\mu_t}{\alpha}=1. \)

This contradicts termination. Thus \(M\) satisfies the required representation condition. Since approval counts cannot decrease when candidates are added, every size-\(k\) superset of \(M\) satisfies continuous EJR.

4. Completing to a fractional committee

First, for every type \(t\) with \(A_t\setminus M\neq\varnothing\), choose one candidate in \(A_t\setminus M\) and assign its entire residual aggregate budget \(\mu_t\bar b_t\) to that candidate, adding this amount to \(y_{tc}\).

This cannot exceed any candidate's capacity. For \(c\notin M\), the total assigned to \(c\) is at most
\(\sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_t\bar b_t<1\).
Selected candidates already have total payment one and receive no payment in this step.

The remaining unspent budgets belong to types with \(A_t\subseteq M\). Allocate these budgets greedily to any candidates with unused capacity, updating \(y\).

This allocation is always possible. If the current total allocation is \(v\), the remaining budget is \(k-v\), while the total unused candidate capacity is \(m-v\geq k-v\).

Define
\(p_c=\sum_ty_{tc}\).
Every budget is now fully allocated, so

\( \displaystyle 0\leq p_c\leq1,\qquad \sum_cp_c=k,\qquad p_c=1\quad(c\in M). \)

5. Proof of the ex-ante guarantees

A payment bound for each type

We claim that
\(\sum_{c\in A_t}y_{tc}\geq\mu_td_t\)
for every \(t\).

If \(A_t\setminus M\neq\varnothing\), type \(t\) spends its entire initial budget \(\mu_tk\) on approved candidates, proving the bound.

If \(A_t\subseteq M\) and \(\bar b_t=0\), it likewise spent its entire budget on approved candidates during equal shares.

Finally, suppose \(A_t\subseteq M\) and \(\bar b_t>0\). Every selection price \(\rho\) satisfies

\( \displaystyle 1=\sum_{u:c\in A_u}\mu_u\min\{b_u,\rho\} \leq\rho\sum_{u:c\in A_u}\mu_u\leq\rho. \)

Thus \(\rho\geq1\). Since type \(t\) retains a positive final balance, it was never exhausted by a payment. It therefore paid exactly \(\mu_t\rho\geq\mu_t\) for each candidate in \(A_t\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \sum_{c\in A_t}y_{tc}\geq\mu_t|A_t|=\mu_td_t, \)

where \(|A_t|\leq|M|\leq k\). This proves the claim.

Group fair share

For any \(S\subseteq T\), nonnegativity of payments and \(A_t\subseteq U(S)\) for \(t\in S\) give

\( \displaystyle p(U(S)) =\sum_{c\in U(S)}\sum_ty_{tc} \geq\sum_{t\in S}\sum_{c\in A_t}y_{tc} \geq\sum_{t\in S}\mu_td_t. \)

By Section 1, this proves the requirement for every real submass vector.

Strong unanimous fair share

If \(A_t\subseteq M\), then \(p(A_t)=|A_t|\), which satisfies the required bound.

Otherwise, type \(t\) allocated its entire budget to approved candidates, so

\( \displaystyle p(A_t)\geq\sum_{c\in A_t}y_{tc} =\mu_tk\geq\min\{\mu_tk,|A_t|\}. \)

Section 1 then gives the requirement for every \(q\in[0,\mu_t]\).

6. Constructing an explicit lottery

We give an elementary decomposition that preserves \(M\).

Let \(r=k-|M|\). If \(r=0\), return the single committee \(M\) with probability one.

Otherwise, order \(C\setminus M\) as \(c_1,\ldots,c_n\), and put

\( \displaystyle a_0=0,\qquad a_i=\sum_{h=1}^{i}p_{c_h}. \)

Thus \(a_n=r\), and each interval \([a_{i-1},a_i)\) has length at most one.

For \(u\in[0,1)\), define

\( \displaystyle W(u)=M\cup \left\{c_i:\ [a_{i-1},a_i) \text{ contains }u+h\text{ for some integer }0\leq h<r\right\}. \)

The intervals partition \([0,r)\), so each of the \(r\) points \(u,u+1,\ldots,u+r-1\) belongs to exactly one interval. An interval of length at most one, with its right endpoint excluded, contains at most one such point. Therefore \(|W(u)|=|M|+r=k\).

For a uniformly distributed \(u\), the probability that \(c_i\) is selected equals \(p_{c_i}\). To see this, intersect \([a_{i-1},a_i)\) with each interval \([h,h+1)\) and translate each intersection by \(-h\). These translated sets describe exactly the values of \(u\) selecting \(c_i\); they are disjoint and have total length \(a_i-a_{i-1}=p_{c_i}\).

The committee \(W(u)\) can change only at fractional parts of the cumulative sums \(a_i\). Sort these breakpoints together with zero and one, deleting duplicates. For each consecutive pair, take its rational midpoint \(u_j\), output \(W_j=W(u_j)\), and assign probability equal to the interval length.

There are at most \(n+1\leq m+1\) such intervals. Their lengths are positive rational numbers summing to one, and the resulting lottery implements \(p\). Every \(W_j\) contains \(M\), so Section 3 proves continuous EJR for every supported committee.

7. Polynomial bit complexity

It remains to establish polynomial time in the binary input length, rather than merely a polynomial number of rational operations.

Choose a common denominator \(D\) for the input masses, for example the product of their denominators, and write
\(\mu_t=a_t/D\).
Then \(\sum_ta_t=D\), and the bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\).

Suppose the current per-unit balances share denominator \(Q\), so \(b_t=B_t/Q\). On a linear segment of \(F_c\), let \(V\) be the approving types whose balances are already capped, and let \(U\) be the remaining approving types. Solving \(F_c(\rho)=1\) gives

\( \displaystyle \rho=\frac{DQ-\sum_{t\in V}a_tB_t} {Q\sum_{t\in U}a_t}. \)

For the segment on which the least affordable price is reached, \(U\) is nonempty. This includes a price at the final breakpoint, by using the preceding segment.

Thus the selected price has denominator dividing \(QH\), where
\(H=\sum_{t\in U}a_t\) satisfies \(1\leq H\leq D\).
After updating balances, they share denominator \(QH\). Starting from \(Q=1\), at most \(k\) selections yield a common denominator of at most \(D^k\).

All balances lie between zero and \(k\). Hence balances, prices, and intermediate arithmetic have bit length polynomial in \(k,L,\log k\). Candidate prices are computed by sorting at most \(\tau\) balance breakpoints and solving the resulting linear segments. Only polynomially many candidates and rounds are processed.

All aggregate payments and completion allocations can use the final common denominator \(DQ\). Greedy filling uses only additions, subtractions, and comparisons. The cumulative sums and lottery probabilities therefore also have polynomial bit length. Sorting their breakpoints and explicitly constructing at most \(m+1\) committees takes polynomial time.

This proves the claimed polynomial-time solvability with an explicit, polynomial-size output.
