The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two answer types \(A\) and \(B\), rational masses \(\mu_A \ge \mu_B > 0\) with \(\mu_A+\mu_B=1\), self-confidences \(\alpha_X \in (0,1]\), and cross-routing shares \(\lambda_X \in [0,1]\), determine the absorbed mass of each answer under the paper’s two-block PPV process and report the winner or a tie. In particular, determine whether \(B\) wins exactly when \(r_A\lambda_A-r_B\lambda_B > \mu_A-\mu_B\), where \(r_X=(1-\alpha_X)/\alpha_X\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Here there are \(m=2\) answers and \(\tau=2\) types, \(A,B\), with \(\mu_A\geq\mu_B>0\) and \(\mu_A+\mu_B=1\). Each type \(X\) has binary-rational confidence \(\alpha_X\in(0,1]\) and routing share \(\lambda_X\in[0,1]\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Initially the transient type states carry masses \(\mu_A,\mu_B\), and the two absorbing answer states carry zero mass. At each step, mass at type \(X\) sends fraction \(\alpha_X\) to its own absorbing answer, fraction \((1-\alpha_X)\lambda_X\) to the other type, and fraction \((1-\alpha_X)(1-\lambda_X)\) back into its own type. Absorbed mass stays absorbed.
Types represent nonatomic populations; routing within a type is permitted. Define \(M_X\) as the limiting mass absorbed at answer \(X\); the answer with greater absorbed mass wins, and equality is reported as a tie. Define \(r_X=(1-\alpha_X)/\alpha_X\).

Is deciding the winning answer or tie in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and can the exact absorbed masses be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?
Does \(B\) win exactly when \(r_A\lambda_A-r_B\lambda_B>\mu_A-\mu_B\)?

For finite populations with block-constant confidence and routing, positive confidences, and block sizes \(n_A\geq n_B>0\), the established flip condition is \((n_A+n_B)(r_A\lambda_A-r_B\lambda_B)>n_A-n_B\); singleton blocks must route their entire peer budget across blocks.
That result also establishes exact lumping into two transient type states and two absorbing answer states. No separate continuous complexity classification is supplied here.

Result

The winning answer or tie is decidable in \(\mathrm{P}\), and both absorbed masses can be computed exactly in polynomial time. The proposed condition is correct:

\(B\) wins exactly when
\(r_A\lambda_A-r_B\lambda_B>\mu_A-\mu_B\).

More precisely, writing \(a=r_A\lambda_A\) and \(b=r_B\lambda_B\), the absorbed masses are

\(M_A=\dfrac{\mu_A+b}{1+a+b},\qquad M_B=\dfrac{\mu_B+a}{1+a+b}.\)

1. Convergence and complete absorption

Let \(p_X(t)\) denote the transient mass at type \(X\) after \(t\) steps, so \(p_X(0)=\mu_X\). Set \(\varepsilon=\min(\alpha_A,\alpha_B)>0\).

At every step, at least an \(\varepsilon\) fraction of the total transient mass is absorbed. Consequently,

\(p_A(t+1)+p_B(t+1)\leq(1-\varepsilon)\bigl(p_A(t)+p_B(t)\bigr).\)

Induction gives \(p_A(t)+p_B(t)\leq(1-\varepsilon)^t\). Thus both transient masses converge to zero. Because total mass is conserved, all initial mass is eventually absorbed, and

\(M_A+M_B=1.\)

Moreover, the cumulative transient occupancies

\(U_X=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}p_X(t)\)

are finite, since \(U_A+U_B\leq1/\varepsilon\). By summing the absorption increments, we obtain \(M_X=\alpha_XU_X\).

These facts justify the infinite sums used below.

2. Exact absorbed masses and winning condition

The transient update at \(A\) is

\(p_A(t+1)=(1-\alpha_A)(1-\lambda_A)p_A(t)+(1-\alpha_B)\lambda_Bp_B(t).\)

Recall that \(a=(1-\alpha_A)\lambda_A/\alpha_A\) and \(b=(1-\alpha_B)\lambda_B/\alpha_B\). Subtracting \(p_A(t)\) from the update gives

\(p_A(t+1)-p_A(t)=-\alpha_A(1+a)p_A(t)+\alpha_Bb\,p_B(t).\)

Sum this identity over all \(t\). The left side telescopes to \(-\mu_A\), because \(p_A(t)\) converges to zero. Using \(M_X=\alpha_XU_X\), the right side becomes \(-(1+a)M_A+bM_B\). Hence

\((1+a)M_A-bM_B=\mu_A.\)

Substituting \(M_B=1-M_A\) yields

\((1+a+b)M_A=\mu_A+b.\)

Since \(a,b\geq0\), the denominator \(1+a+b\) is strictly positive. Therefore

\(M_A=\dfrac{\mu_A+b}{1+a+b},\qquad M_B=1-M_A=\dfrac{\mu_B+a}{1+a+b}.\)

Subtracting these expressions gives

\(M_B-M_A=\dfrac{a-b-(\mu_A-\mu_B)}{1+a+b}.\)

The positive denominator establishes all three cases:

  • \(B\) wins if \(a-b>\mu_A-\mu_B\).
  • The outcome is a tie if \(a-b=\mu_A-\mu_B\).
  • \(A\) wins if \(a-b<\mu_A-\mu_B\).

The proof includes confidence equal to \(1\) and routing shares equal to \(0\) or \(1\).

3. Exact computational complexity

For the stated problem, \(m=\tau=2\). Computing \(a,b,M_A,M_B\) uses a constant number of additions, subtractions, multiplications, and divisions of binary-encoded rational numbers.

Represent each rational by an integer numerator and a positive integer denominator. Because there are only constantly many arithmetic operations, every intermediate numerator and denominator has \(O(L)\) bits. Standard integer arithmetic therefore computes the masses exactly in time polynomial in \(L\). Reduction to lowest terms, if required, also takes polynomial time by the Euclidean algorithm.

To determine the outcome, compute the sign of the rational number

\(a-b-(\mu_A-\mu_B).\)

This requires only exact integer arithmetic and establishes membership in \(\mathrm{P}\). Under polynomial-time many-one reductions, an additional claim of \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

This algorithm solves the limiting equations directly. It does not require waiting for a simulation to converge.

General finite-support extension

The same conclusion holds for an explicitly specified rational system with \(\tau\) transient states and \(m\) absorbing states, provided every transient state has positive one-step absorption probability.

Let \(Q\) be its \(\tau\times\tau\) transient transition matrix and \(R\) its \(\tau\times m\) absorption matrix. Assume their entries are nonnegative and each row of the combined matrix sums to \(1\). Define

\(\varepsilon=\min_i\sum_{j=1}^{m}R_{ij}>0.\)

Every row sum of \(Q\) is at most \(1-\varepsilon\), so the matrix series \(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}Q^t\) converges and equals \((I-Q)^{-1}\). For the initial transient row vector \(\mu\), the exact absorbed-mass vector is therefore

\(M=\mu(I-Q)^{-1}R.\)

For completeness, this formula admits a polynomial bit-complexity algorithm. Let \(L'\) be the total encoding length of \(Q,R,\mu\), and let \(D\) be the product of their positive denominators. Then \(D\) has \(O(L')\) bits, and

\(K=D(I-Q),\qquad C=DR\)

are integer matrices with entries of \(O(L')\) bits. The matrix \(K\) is nonsingular by the convergence argument above.

Cramer’s rule expresses each entry of \(K^{-1}C\) as a ratio of two determinants of integer matrices of order \(\tau\). If their entries have absolute value at most \(2^B\), the determinant expansion bounds their determinants by \(\tau!2^{\tau B}\), giving bit length \(O(\tau(B+\log\tau))\). Standard fraction-free Gaussian elimination computes each determinant in polynomial time; its intermediate entries are bounded by minors of the input matrix. Only \(1+\tau m\) determinants are needed. Multiplication by \(\mu\) then uses polynomially many rational operations with polynomial bit lengths.

Thus exact absorbed masses are computable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L'\). This applies in terms of the original length \(L\) whenever the transition matrices can be constructed from the support description in polynomial time with polynomial encoding length. The stated two-type model satisfies this requirement directly.

4. Agreement with the finite-population condition

Normalize the block sizes by setting

\(\mu_A=\dfrac{n_A}{n_A+n_B},\qquad \mu_B=\dfrac{n_B}{n_A+n_B}.\)

Then \(\mu_A-\mu_B=(n_A-n_B)/(n_A+n_B)\). Multiplying the proved winning inequality by the positive number \(n_A+n_B\) gives exactly

\((n_A+n_B)(r_A\lambda_A-r_B\lambda_B)>n_A-n_B.\)

Thus the conditions agree on parameters admissible for the finite-population model. Its singleton routing restriction limits those admissible parameters; the nonatomic model permits within-type routing and imposes no such restriction.
