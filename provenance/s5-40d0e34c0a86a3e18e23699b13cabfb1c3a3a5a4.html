The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), a finite item-slot catalogue \(G\) with rational per-capita capacities \(q_g\), additive values \(v_{tg}\), and welfare densities \(\lambda_t\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t=1\), choose finite-support \(y_{t,S}\ge0\) with \(\sum_S y_{t,S}=1\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t\sum_{S\ni g}y_{t,S}\le q_g\), and output \(y\) whose log welfare \(\sum_{t,S}\mu_t\lambda_t y_{t,S}\ln(\sum_{g\in S}v_{tg})\) is within \(\ln(1+ε)\) of the optimum.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|G|\) item slots, with binary-rational capacities \(q_g\ge0\), additive values \(v_{tg}\ge0\), and welfare densities \(\lambda_t\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_t=1\). A bundle is any subset \(S\subseteq G\); each slot occurs at most once in a bundle. Write \(v_t(S)=\sum_{g\in S}v_{tg}\).
A feasible allocation consists of probabilities \(y_{t,S}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}y_{t,S}=1\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t\sum_{S\ni g}y_{t,S}\le q_g\) for every \(g\). Its log welfare is \(F(y)=\sum_{t:\lambda_t>0}\mu_t\lambda_t\sum_{S:y_{t,S}>0}y_{t,S}\ln v_t(S)\), with \(\ln 0=-\infty\); zero-density types contribute zero.
Promise that some feasible allocation has finite log welfare. Given binary-rational \(\varepsilon>0\), the approximation problem is to output a list of nonzero rational coordinates of a feasible \(y\) with \(F(y)\ge\operatorname{OPT}-\ln(1+\varepsilon)\), where \(\operatorname{OPT}=\max_y F(y)\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Does this approximation problem admit an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\), or is obtaining the stated approximation NP-hard?

For finitely many agents with nonnegative weights summing to one and unit item capacities, the configuration LP admits a rational solution within additive error \(\ln(1+\varepsilon)\) in time polynomial in input size and \(1/\varepsilon\). [Feng–Li](https://theoretics.episciences.org/16348/pdf)
No result settling this exact formulation with explicit type masses and arbitrary rational per-capita capacities was identified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified values, capacities, welfare densities, and accuracy as binary rationals; values and capacities are nonnegative.
  • Specified bundles as arbitrary subsets of the catalogue, with at most one occurrence of each slot, following the discrete configuration domain.
  • Required output probabilities to be rational and represented by their nonzero coordinates, following the anchor.
  • Excluded instances with no finite-log-welfare feasible allocation, carrying over the positive-welfare condition under which the anchor operates.
  • Ignored zero-density types in the objective and assigned negative infinite log welfare to positive-density types receiving zero-value bundles with positive probability.
  • Defined L as total binary input length, including the accuracy parameter.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

A polynomial-time approximation algorithm

The problem admits an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\). It outputs an exactly feasible rational allocation in the required list representation.

The proof constructs a polynomial-size flow LP. Bundle values are rounded relative to their largest item, while item consumption is preserved exactly. This largest-item rounding idea also appears in [Feng–Li, Section 3](https://theoretics.episciences.org/16348/pdf); the flow formulation below handles explicit masses and arbitrary rational capacities directly.

1. Preprocessing and error parameters

Write \(w_t=\mu_t\lambda_t\) and \(T_+=\{t:\lambda_t>0\}\). Then \(w_t>0\) for \(t\in T_+\), and \(\sum_{t\in T_+}w_t=1\).

Assign the empty bundle to every type outside \(T_+\). For a type in \(T_+\), remove zero-valued items from every assigned bundle. These operations preserve welfare and weakly decrease every item’s consumption. Therefore we may restrict attention to positive-density types receiving nonempty bundles consisting entirely of positively valued items.

A finite-welfare allocation cannot give positive probability to a zero-value bundle for a positive-density type. After excluding those coordinates, the feasible region is a nonempty compact polytope, by the promise. Its objective is a finite linear function. Consequently, an optimum \(y^*\) exists. The preprocessing above lets us choose \(y^*\) with the stated restrictions.

Set

\(e=\min\{\varepsilon,1\},\qquad \delta=e/8,\qquad \eta=e/16.\)

In particular, \(0<\delta\le 1/8\). The promise and weight normalization imply \(m\ge 1\).

2. Rounding bundles relative to their largest item

Fix \(t\in T_+\) and an item \(j\) with \(v_{tj}>0\). We call \(j\) the anchor and define

\(a_{tj}=v_{tj},\qquad h_{tj}=\delta a_{tj}/m,\qquad H_{tj}=\{g:0<v_{tg}\le a_{tj}\}.\)

For \(g\in H_{tj}\), define the integer

\(r_{tjg}=\left\lfloor v_{tg}/h_{tj}\right\rfloor.\)

The bundles represented by this anchor are exactly those satisfying \(j\in S\subseteq H_{tj}\). Their rounded values are

\(V_{tj}(S)=h_{tj}\sum_{g\in S}r_{tjg}.\)

For every such bundle, rounding down gives

\(0\le v_t(S)-V_{tj}(S)<|S|h_{tj}\le mh_{tj}=\delta a_{tj}\le\delta v_t(S).\)

Thus

\((1-\delta)v_t(S)\le V_{tj}(S)\le v_t(S).\)

The rounded value is positive: the anchor contributes

\(r_{tjj}=\lfloor m/\delta\rfloor\ge 1.\)

Taking logarithms, we obtain the uniform bound

\(\ln v_t(S)+\ln(1-\delta)\le\ln V_{tj}(S)\le\ln v_t(S).\) (1)

Every nonempty bundle consisting of positively valued items is represented by at least one anchor: choose any item attaining its maximum value.

Let

\(R=\lfloor m/\delta\rfloor,\qquad K=mR.\)

Every eligible item has \(r_{tjg}\le R\), so every represented bundle has rounded integer sum at most \(K=O(m^2/\delta)\).

3. A polynomial-size network representing the bundles

Order the items as \(1,\ldots,m\). For each pair \((t,j)\), construct a layered directed graph with vertices

\((i,k),\qquad 0\le i\le m,\quad 0\le k\le K.\)

The state records the number of processed items and their rounded integer sum. At stage \(i\), processing item \(g=i+1\), use these arcs:

  • If \(g=j\), allow only an inclusion arc from \((i,k)\) to \((i+1,k+r_{tjg})\).
  • If \(g\in H_{tj}\setminus\{j\}\), allow an exclusion arc to \((i+1,k)\) and an inclusion arc to \((i+1,k+r_{tjg})\).
  • If \(g\notin H_{tj}\), allow only the exclusion arc.

Arcs whose destination exceeds \(K\) are omitted. Inclusion and exclusion arcs remain distinct even when their endpoints coincide because \(r_{tjg}=0\).

For each type \(t\), introduce a common source and sink. Connect its source to \((0,0)\) in every anchor graph. Connect each terminal state \((m,k)\), for \(k\ge 1\), to the sink. All anchor graphs otherwise have disjoint vertices.

Every source-to-sink path selects a bundle \(S\) with \(j\in S\subseteq H_{tj}\), and its terminal index satisfies

\(k=\sum_{g\in S}r_{tjg}.\)

Conversely, every such bundle determines a path: its successive rounded sums never exceed \(K\), and its terminal sum is positive. Each item is processed once, so a path includes each item at most once.

For one anchor there are \(O(mK)\) vertices and arcs. Across all types and anchors, the network size is

\(O(\tau m^2K)=O(\tau m^4/\delta).\)

All rounded integers and graph data can be computed by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

4. Flow constraints preserve feasibility exactly

For each \(t\in T_+\), place a nonnegative flow \(x_{t,b}\) on its network arcs. Require flow conservation at every internal vertex and total source outflow and sink inflow equal to one.

Let \(\mathcal I_{tg}\) denote the inclusion arcs for item \(g\) in type \(t\)’s network. Impose the capacity constraints

\(\sum_{t\in T_+}\mu_t\sum_{b\in\mathcal I_{tg}}x_{t,b}\le q_g,\qquad g\in G.\) (2)

These constraints have exactly the masses and capacities from the input.

Because each network is acyclic, every feasible unit flow decomposes into source-to-sink paths with nonnegative coefficients summing to one. To see this constructively, follow positive-flow arcs from the source; conservation supplies a positive outgoing arc at every internal vertex. Acyclicity ensures that the path reaches the sink. Subtract the minimum flow on its arcs and repeat. Each subtraction eliminates at least one positive arc, so at most the number of arcs many paths are needed.

Interpret the path coefficients as bundle probabilities and merge paths selecting the same bundle. For the resulting allocation,

\(\sum_S y_{t,S}=1,\qquad \sum_{S\ni g}y_{t,S}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal I_{tg}}x_{t,b}.\)

Therefore (2) is precisely the original capacity constraint. All positive-density types receive positive-value bundles.

Conversely, any allocation satisfying the preprocessing restrictions lifts to a feasible flow: choose a largest-valued anchor for each supported bundle and send its probability along the corresponding path. Inclusion-arc flows preserve its item marginals exactly. In particular, \(y^*\) lifts to a feasible flow \(x^*\).

5. A rational objective and its approximation guarantee

Let \(c_{tjk}\) denote the flow on the terminal arc from anchor \(j\) and terminal index \(k\). Define the real rounded objective

\(H(x)=\sum_{t\in T_+}w_t\sum_{j,k}c_{tjk}\ln(kh_{tj}).\)

For each type, \(\sum_{j,k}c_{tjk}=1\). Equation (1) consequently gives two useful inequalities.

First, decomposing any feasible flow \(x\) into an allocation \(y\) gives

\(F(y)\ge H(x).\) (3)

Second, lifting \(y^*\) gives

\(H(x^*)\ge\operatorname{OPT}+\ln(1-\delta),\) (4)

because the weights sum to one.

To obtain a rational LP, compute rational numbers \(\ell_{tjk}\) satisfying

\(\left|\ell_{tjk}-\ln(kh_{tj})\right|\le\eta.\)

This computation takes polynomial time. Here is an explicit justification. For a positive rational \(z\), write \(z=2^p r\), where \(p\) is an integer and \(1\le r<2\). With \(u=(r-1)/(r+1)\),

\(\ln r=2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{2i+1}}{2i+1},\qquad 0\le u<1/3.\)

The same series with \(u=1/3\) computes \(\ln 2\). Truncating either series after \(N\) terms has error at most

\(2\sum_{i=N}^{\infty}(1/3)^{2i+1}=\frac34\,9^{-N}\le 9^{-N}.\)

Thus rational approximations to \(\ln z=p\ln 2+\ln r\) have error at most \((|p|+1)9^{-N}\). If \(z\) has bit length \(B\), then \(|p|=O(B)\); choosing \(N=O(\log(B+1)+\log(1/\eta))\) suffices. The rational partial sums have polynomial bit length. Here every \(z=kh_{tj}\) has bit length polynomial in the input length and the encoding lengths of the graph parameters.

Now maximize the rational objective

\(C(x)=\sum_{t\in T_+}w_t\sum_{j,k}c_{tjk}\ell_{tjk}\)

subject to the flow and capacity constraints. For every feasible flow,

\(|C(x)-H(x)|\le\eta\sum_{t\in T_+}w_t\sum_{j,k}c_{tjk}=\eta.\) (5)

This is an explicitly constructed rational LP of polynomial size. It is feasible by the lift of \(y^*\). It is bounded: acyclicity and the unit-flow conditions imply every arc flow lies between zero and one. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming therefore supplies a rational optimum \(\widehat x\).

Decompose \(\widehat x\) into an allocation \(\widehat y\). By (3)–(5) and optimality for \(C\),

\(F(\widehat y)\ge H(\widehat x)\ge C(\widehat x)-\eta\ge C(x^*)-\eta\ge H(x^*)-2\eta\ge\operatorname{OPT}+\ln(1-\delta)-2\eta.\)

Finally,

\(-\ln(1-\delta)\le\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\le\frac e7,\qquad 2\eta=\frac e8.\)

Hence the total loss is at most \(15e/56<e/2\). Since \(0<e\le 1\) and \(e\le\varepsilon\),

\(\ln(1+\varepsilon)\ge\ln(1+e)\ge\frac{e}{1+e}\ge\frac e2.\)

It follows that

\(F(\widehat y)\ge\operatorname{OPT}-\ln(1+\varepsilon).\)

6. Running time and output representation

The network size is \(O(\tau m^4/e)\), and all rational LP coefficients have polynomial bit length. Constructing the LP, approximating its logarithmic rewards, and solving it therefore take time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/e\). Because \(1/e=\max\{1,1/\varepsilon\}\), this is the required running time.

Flow decomposition uses at most one path per network arc. Its probabilities are rational with polynomial bit length: all input arc flows have polynomial bit length, and a common denominator for them has polynomial bit length and remains a denominator throughout the subtraction procedure.

Merge duplicate bundles, omit zero coordinates, and add \(y_{t,\varnothing}=1\) for each zero-density type. The resulting list has polynomial length, satisfies the original constraints exactly, and attains the required welfare guarantee. No expansion of the type masses or capacities is needed.
