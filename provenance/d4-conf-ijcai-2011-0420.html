The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C\), a rational distribution \(\nu\) over nonmanipulator rankings with total mass \(1-\varepsilon\), a strategic type of mass \(\varepsilon>0\) with true ranking \(R^\star\) and utility \(u\), rational \(q\), and rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), does there exist a ranking \(L\) for the strategic type such that the average utility of the Copeland\(_\alpha\) winners under the resulting society \(\mu_L=\nu+\varepsilon\delta_L\) is at least \(q\)? Pairwise contests are decided by comparing ranking masses, with equal masses counted as ties.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a nonempty candidate set with \(m=|C|\); all rankings are strict linear orders. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible rankings.
The input consists of a nonmanipulator society \(\nu\) of total mass \(1-\varepsilon\), rational \(0<\varepsilon\leq1\), a strategic type with true ranking \(R^\star\), binary-encoded utilities \(u:C\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\), rational \(q\geq0\), and rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). Require \(a\succ_{R^\star}b\Rightarrow u(a)\geq u(b)\); equal utilities are allowed.
The strategic type submits one strict ranking \(L\) for its entire mass, producing \(\mu_L=\nu+\varepsilon\delta_L\), where \(\delta_L\) is unit mass at \(L\). Pairwise contests compare the masses ranking either candidate above the other, with equal masses counted as ties.
Use Copeland\(_\alpha\), and let \(W(L)\) be all candidates attaining the maximum score. Select uniformly from \(W(L)\), giving expected utility \(\frac{1}{|W(L)|}\sum_{c\in W(L)}u(c)\).
Decide whether some \(L\) gives expected utility at least \(q\). Here \(\tau=|\operatorname{supp}\nu|\), and \(L_{\mathrm{bits}}\) is the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L_{\mathrm{bits}}\).

Is this decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L_{\mathrm{bits}}\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite unweighted electorates with one manipulating voter and uniform selection among Copeland\(_\alpha\) winners, the corresponding decision problem is NP-complete for every fixed rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). [Discrete result](https://comsoc-community.org/assets/proceedings/comsoc-2012/Obraztsova.pdf)
No result for exactly the rational-mass problem stated above was identified in the literature search.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, both when \(\alpha\) is part of the input and for every fixed rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). Consequently, it has no polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L_{\mathrm{bits}}\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof uses the discrete hardness theorem supplied in the question and an exact, polynomial-size reduction to the stated support representation.

1. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Write \(B=L_{\mathrm{bits}}\). A certificate is a strict ranking \(L\), represented as a permutation of \(C\), using \(O(m\log m)\) bits.

Suppose the nonmanipulator support consists of rankings \(R_1,\ldots,R_\tau\) with masses \(w_1,\ldots,w_\tau\). For distinct candidates \(a,b\), define \(\sigma_R(a,b)=1\) if \(a\succ_R b\), and \(\sigma_R(a,b)=-1\) otherwise. The pairwise margin after submitting \(L\) is
\[ \Delta_L(a,b)=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\sigma_{R_i}(a,b)+\varepsilon\sigma_L(a,b). \]
Its sign determines whether \(a\) wins, ties, or loses against \(b\).

All these signs can be computed exactly in polynomial time. Specifically, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the support masses and \(\varepsilon\). Its bit length is \(O(B)\), because the sum of those denominator bit lengths is bounded by the input length. Multiplying each margin by \(D\) reduces its sign computation to integer arithmetic on \(O(B)\)-bit integers. There are \(O(m^2)\) candidate pairs.

Write \(\alpha=h/k\), with integers \(k>0\) and \(0\leq h\leq k\). If candidate \(c\) has \(v_c\) pairwise victories and \(t_c\) pairwise ties, its Copeland score is \((kv_c+ht_c)/k\). Thus \(W(L)\) is obtained by comparing the integers \(kv_c+ht_c\).

Finally, write \(q=p/r\), with integers \(p\geq0\) and \(r>0\). Since \(C\) is nonempty, \(W(L)\) is nonempty. The required utility comparison is exactly
\[ r\sum_{c\in W(L)}u(c)\ \geq\ p\,|W(L)|. \]
This also uses polynomial-bit-length integers.

Using ordinary quadratic-time integer arithmetic, a conservative verification bound is \(O((\tau+1)m^2B^2)\) bit operations. Hence the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), including when \(\alpha\) is an input rational.

2. Exact reduction from the discrete problem

Fix any rational \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). The supplied discrete result states that manipulation by one voter, with expected utility under uniform selection from the Copeland winners, is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for this fixed \(\alpha\). This is Theorem 4.8 of [Obraztsova, Zick, and Elkind, *On the Complexity of Voting Manipulation under Randomized Tie-Breaking*](https://comsoc-community.org/assets/proceedings/comsoc-2012/Obraztsova.pdf).

Take an instance of that discrete problem with \(n\geq1\) voters, where the last voter manipulates. Denote the nonmanipulator rankings by \(R_1,\ldots,R_{n-1}\).

Retain the candidate set, utilities, threshold, and Copeland parameter. Define
\[ \varepsilon=\frac1n, \qquad \nu=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\delta_{R_i}. \]
Choose \(R^\star\) by sorting candidates in nonincreasing utility order, breaking equal-utility ties arbitrarily. This gives a strict ranking satisfying the required utility consistency condition. Its particular choice does not affect the evaluation of a submitted ranking \(L\).

Validity and encoding size

The constructed instance has \(0<\varepsilon\leq1\) and nonmanipulator mass \((n-1)/n=1-\varepsilon\).

To produce its support representation, group identical nonmanipulator rankings. If ranking \(R\) occurs \(k_R>0\) times, give it mass \(k_R/n\). Therefore \(\tau\leq n-1\), and every mass has a binary numerator and denominator of \(O(\log n)\) bits.

Grouping and writing these entries takes polynomial time in the explicitly listed discrete input. The reduction lists only rankings that occur; it never enumerates all possible rankings.

Preservation of every possible outcome

For every submitted strict ranking \(L\) and every distinct \(a,b\), the rational-mass margin is
\[ \Delta_L(a,b) = \frac1n \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{R_i}(a,b)+\sigma_L(a,b) \right). \]
The parenthesized expression is exactly the discrete pairwise margin after the manipulating voter submits \(L\).

Multiplication by the positive number \(1/n\) preserves positive, zero, and negative margins. Hence every pairwise victory, tie, and loss is identical in the two instances. Their Copeland scores and winner sets consequently coincide.

The utilities are unchanged, so uniform selection from these identical winner sets gives identical expected utility. Thus, for every \(L\), the discrete instance meets its threshold precisely when the constructed instance does. This proves a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

3. If “binary rational” means dyadic rational

The reduction also works if masses must have denominators that are powers of two.

Let \(N\) be the least power of two satisfying \(N\geq n\). Set \(\varepsilon=1/N\) and give every original nonmanipulator vote mass \(1/N\). Choose any strict ranking \(H\), and let \(\overline H\) be its reverse. Add to \(\nu\) mass \((N-n)/(2N)\) at each of \(H\) and \(\overline H\), omitting these entries when their mass is zero and merging duplicate rankings.

The resulting nonmanipulator mass is
\[ \frac{n-1}{N}+2\frac{N-n}{2N}=1-\frac1N. \]
All masses are dyadic. For each distinct pair \(a,b\), the added rankings cancel because \(\sigma_H(a,b)+\sigma_{\overline H}(a,b)=0\). Thus the final margin is exactly the original discrete margin divided by \(N\). The outcome-preservation proof remains unchanged.

There are at most \(n+1\) support entries, each with \(O(\log n)\)-bit masses, so this construction is also polynomial.

4. Running times and conclusion

The reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for every fixed rational \(\alpha\); the verifier proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\). Allowing \(\alpha\) as input preserves membership and includes any fixed-\(\alpha\) hard subclass.

For completeness, an exact deterministic algorithm can enumerate all \(m!\) submitted rankings. Precomputing the nonmanipulator margins once gives the conservative running-time bound
\[ O\!\left((\tau+1+m!)m^2L_{\mathrm{bits}}^2\right). \]
The polynomial verification bound is \(O((\tau+1)m^2L_{\mathrm{bits}}^2)\). A polynomial deterministic bound in all three stated parameters exists if and only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
