Status and main result

The proposed lower bound does hold for the full mechanism class. In fact, the nonatomic model admits a stronger lower bound:

$$ \boxed{\alpha\ge \max\left\{1,\frac{3(\tau-1)}{128\beta}\right\}.} $$

This holds already on a graph consisting of one edge of cost one, with the entire population at its nonroot endpoint. The proof allows arbitrary partial service and uses only individual rationality, budget balance, and the incentive constraints obtained by treating a positive-mass population block as a coalition.

Together with a construction proved below, this determines the optimal rate on general graphs in the range

$$ 2\le\beta\le\sqrt{\tau}: \qquad \boxed{\alpha_{\mathrm{optimal}}=\Theta(\tau/\beta).} $$

In particular, for every fixed $\beta\ge2$, the optimal dependence on support size is linear. Thus an $\Omega((\log\tau)^2/\beta)$ lower bound cannot be beaten in this model.

I do not determine the optimal approximation for all budget parameters. The general-graph range $1\le\beta<2$, and the optimal tradeoff when $\beta>\sqrt{\tau}$, remain unresolved here.

1. An incentive inequality for a population block

Fix a measurable population block of mass $\delta>0$, all at one location. Fix every outsider’s report. Suppose all members of this block report the same value.

When their common report is $z/\delta$, let $q(z)$ be their service probability and let

$$ t(z)=\delta q(z)p(z) $$

be the block’s total expected payment. Set $t(z)=0$ when $q(z)=0$.

If the block’s true per-person value is $z/\delta$, its total expected utility is

$$ zq(z)-t(z). $$

Every member has the same expected utility. Consequently, group-strategyproofness implies, for every pair of permitted reports $z,z'$,

$$ zq(z)-t(z)\ge zq(z')-t(z'). $$

Indeed, a strict violation would make every member of this positive-mass coalition strictly better off by changing their common report.

Consider increasing values

$$ 0<z_0<z_1<\cdots<z_k, $$

and write $q_j=q(z_j)$ and $t_j=t(z_j)$. Applying the incentive inequality in both directions between adjacent values gives

$$ z_{j-1}(q_j-q_{j-1}) \le t_j-t_{j-1} \le z_j(q_j-q_{j-1}). $$

Since $z_j>z_{j-1}$, these inequalities imply

$$ q_j\ge q_{j-1}. $$

Individual rationality also gives $t_0\le z_0q_0$. Iterating the upper payment inequality therefore yields

$$ t_j\le z_0q_0+ \sum_{\ell=1}^{j}z_\ell(q_\ell-q_{\ell-1}). $$

These conclusions require no continuity assumption and permit genuinely fractional service.

Now let

$$ z_j=a2^j, \qquad 0\le j\le k, $$

and let a random index $J$ satisfy

$$ \Pr(J=j)= \begin{cases} 2^{-j-1},&0\le j<k,\\ 2^{-k},&j=k. \end{cases} $$

For every $\ell\in\{0,\ldots,k\}$,

$$ \Pr(J\ge\ell)=2^{-\ell}. $$

Taking expectations in the payment bound gives

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[t_J] &\le aq_0+ \sum_{\ell=1}^{k} a2^\ell(q_\ell-q_{\ell-1})\Pr(J\ge\ell)\\ &=a\left(q_0+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(q_\ell-q_{\ell-1})\right)\\ &=aq_k\\ &\le a. \end{aligned} $$

We have proved the following finite-distribution revenue bound:

With outsiders’ reports fixed, a population block whose total value follows this distribution contributes expected payment at most $a$.

The technique is related to the public-good revenue lower bound in [Dobzinski, Mehta, Roughgarden, and Sundararajan, Theorem 5.1](https://timroughgarden.org/papers/IsShapley.pdf). The construction below ensures exactly $\tau$ aggregate types and supplies the nonatomic incentive argument explicitly.

2. A full-class lower bound linear in support size

2.1. Rational instances with exactly $\tau$ types

Fix $\tau\ge2$ and $\beta\ge1$. Put

$$ k=\tau-1, \qquad N=2^{k+2}. $$

Choose the dyadic number

$$ c=2^{-\lceil\log_2(4\beta)\rceil}. $$

Then

$$ \frac1{8\beta}\le c\le\frac1{4\beta}. $$

The graph has two vertices, $r$ and $a$, joined by an edge of cost one. The public mass at $a$ is one.

For the proof, partition this population into $N$ measurable blocks, each of mass $1/N$. These blocks are bookkeeping devices; the mechanism receives only the aggregate distribution of bids.

Use precisely the following $\tau=k+1$ possible per-person values:

$$ v_j=c2^j, \qquad 0\le j\le k. $$

Assign one fixed anchor block value $v_j$ for each $j$. Assign each of the remaining $N-\tau$ blocks an independent random value $v_J$, using the distribution from Section 1.

Every resulting truthful aggregate profile has exactly $\tau$ types:

  • all types are at $a$;
  • the values $v_0,\ldots,v_k$ are distinct;
  • every value has positive mass because its anchor block is present;
  • all masses are positive integer multiples of $1/N$.

Thus every profile is a valid binary-rational input with support size exactly $\tau$. Its aggregate encoding length is

$$ L=O\bigl(\tau(\tau+\log\beta+1)\bigr). $$

The exponentially many conceptual blocks do not appear individually in the input.

2.2. Expected revenue is small

For a nonanchor block, condition on every other block’s report. Its total value at level $j$ is

$$ z_j=\frac{v_j}{N}=\frac cN2^j. $$

The block revenue bound from Section 1, with $a=c/N$, gives conditional expected payment at most $c/N$. Independence allows us to average this conditional bound. Summing over the nonanchor blocks gives expected payment at most

$$ \frac{N-\tau}{N}c. $$

An anchor block at value $v_j$ pays at most its total value, by individual rationality. Hence the total payment from anchor blocks is at most

$$ \frac cN\sum_{j=0}^{k}2^j = \frac cN(2^{k+1}-1) < \frac c2. $$

Writing $R$ for total expected payment at the realized profile, we obtain

$$ \mathbb E[R]<\frac{3c}{2}. $$

Let $A$ be the event that the mechanism serves some positive mass. On this single-edge graph, $A$ forces the chosen subgraph to have cost one. Budget balance therefore implies, pointwise,

$$ R\ge\frac1\beta\mathbf 1_A. $$

Consequently,

$$ \Pr(A)\le\beta\mathbb E[R] < \frac{3\beta c}{2} \le\frac38. $$

Let $B$ be the event that no positive mass is served. In particular,

$$ p:=\Pr(B)\ge\frac12. $$

This argument applies equally when positive service consists of very small probabilities: any positive served mass still requires the unit-cost edge.

2.3. Total truthful value is large and concentrated

Let $W$ be total truthful value. For one nonanchor block’s per-person value,

$$ \mathbb E[v_J] = c\left( \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}2^j2^{-j-1} + 2^k2^{-k} \right) = c\left(1+\frac k2\right). $$

Its second moment is

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[v_J^2] &= c^2\left( \frac12\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}2^j+2^k \right)\\ &= c^2\frac{3\cdot2^k-1}{2}\\ &< \frac32c^2 2^k. \end{aligned} $$

Because $k+1\le2^k$ for $k\ge1$,

$$ \frac{\tau}{N} = \frac{k+1}{2^{k+2}} \le\frac14. $$

Ignoring the nonnegative anchor contribution gives

$$ \mathbb E[W] \ge \frac{N-\tau}{N}c\left(1+\frac k2\right) \ge \frac34c\left(1+\frac k2\right). $$

Only nonanchor values are random, and they are independent. Thus

$$ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(W) &= \frac{N-\tau}{N^2}\operatorname{Var}(v_J)\\ &\le \frac1N\mathbb E[v_J^2]\\ &\le \frac38c^2. \end{aligned} $$

The variance bound controls the possible correlation between total value and the mechanism’s decision not to serve:

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[W\mathbf 1_B] &= p\mathbb E[W] + \operatorname{Cov}(W,\mathbf 1_B)\\ &\ge \frac12\mathbb E[W] - \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(W)}\sqrt{p(1-p)}\\ &\ge \frac12\mathbb E[W] - \frac12\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(W)}\\ &\ge c\left( \frac38+\frac{3k}{16} -\frac12\sqrt{\frac38} \right)\\ &\ge \frac{3ck}{16}. \end{aligned} $$

The last inequality holds because $\sqrt{3/8}<3/4$.

2.4. The approximation lower bound

When $B$ occurs, nobody is served, so social cost is $W$. At other profiles social cost is nonnegative. Therefore

$$ \mathbb E[\operatorname{SC}] \ge \mathbb E[W\mathbf 1_B] \ge \frac{3ck}{16} \ge \frac{3(\tau-1)}{128\beta}. $$

At every profile in this construction, serving everyone costs one, and hence

$$ \operatorname{OPT}\le1. $$

Thus an approximation guarantee $\operatorname{SC}\le\alpha\operatorname{OPT}$ on all support-$\tau$ profiles would imply

$$ \mathbb E[\operatorname{SC}]\le\alpha. $$

Combining the inequalities proves

$$ \boxed{\alpha\ge\frac{3(\tau-1)}{128\beta}.} $$

Also, every feasible outcome has social cost at least $\operatorname{OPT}$: its chosen subgraph costs at least the minimum connecting cost for its allocation. Since the constructed instances have positive optimum, every valid approximation factor satisfies $\alpha\ge1$.

We have therefore established

$$ \boxed{\alpha\ge \max\left\{1,\frac{3(\tau-1)}{128\beta}\right\}.} $$

All profiles used in the proof are rational and have exactly $\tau$ types. Coalition deviations in the payment argument move a single block between the existing value levels, so the anchors preserve that support size throughout.

Since $\tau-1$ eventually dominates $(\log\tau)^2$, the requested all-class $\Omega((\log\tau)^2/\beta)$ lower bound follows.

3. A general construction lemma

To compare the lower bound with achievable guarantees, I give a self-contained version of the construction from the notes.

For a measurable population subset $A$, let $C(A)$ be the minimum cost of connecting its positive-mass locations to $r$.

First, an optimum exists that serves whole locations. If an allocation serves positive mass at a location, completing service at that location leaves its required terminal set unchanged and weakly reduces excluded value. Minimizing over the finitely many public location subsets proves attainment.

Let $O$ be such an optimal population, and let $V_{\mathrm{out}}$ denote total value outside $O$. Then

$$ \operatorname{OPT}=C(O)+V_{\mathrm{out}}. $$

Suppose a nonnegative price rule $\chi_a(A)$, depending on location and active population, has the following properties:

  • Cross-monotonicity: if $A\subseteq B$ and $a$ has positive mass in $A$, then $\chi_a(B)\le\chi_a(A)$.
  • Writing

$$ R(A)=\sum_a w_A(a)\chi_a(A), $$
there are constants $\lambda\ge1$ and $\gamma\ge\lambda$ such that
$$ R(A)\le\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}C(A). $$

  • A fixed deterministic construction produces a connecting subgraph of cost

$$ C'(A)\le\gamma R(A). $$

Assume also $\gamma\le\beta$. The parameters are fixed across reports.

Start with the full reported population active. Repeatedly remove every reported type whose bid is strictly below its current price. When no further deletion occurs, serve all remaining types fully at their final prices. Serve nobody else. Accept equality, and use the empty subgraph for an empty outcome.

There are finitely many reported types, so this procedure terminates on every finite-support real profile.

3.1. Individual rationality and budget balance

Acceptance ensures that served bids are at least their nonnegative prices. Moreover,

$$ \frac{C'(A)}{\beta} \le \frac{\gamma}{\beta}R(A) \le R(A), $$

and, since $\gamma\ge\lambda$,

$$ R(A)\le\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}C(A)\le C(A). $$

Thus every outcome satisfies the required budget inequalities.

3.2. Group-strategyproofness under splitting

Consider an initial profile at which coalition members are truthful and outsiders’ reports are arbitrary and fixed. Let $S$ be the initially served population, and let $T$ be the served population after a coalition deviation.

Suppose almost every coalition member weakly gains.

I claim that $T\subseteq S$, up to null sets. Otherwise, consider the earliest initial deletion round that removes positive mass from $T$. Let $A$ be the population active immediately before that round. By its choice, $T\subseteq A$ up to null sets.

Some initially reported type of bid $b$ and location $a$ is deleted in that round and has positive-mass intersection with $T$. Its deletion implies

$$ b<\chi_a(A)\le\chi_a(T). $$

An outsider in this intersection still reports $b$ and cannot satisfy the deviating outcome’s acceptance condition. A coalition member in the intersection has true value $b$, was initially unserved, and is now served at a price strictly exceeding $b$. That member strictly loses. These observations contradict the positive-mass intersection and the proposed weak improvement.

Hence $T\subseteq S$. Cross-monotonicity now implies that coalition members served in both outcomes face weakly higher prices. A member losing service receives zero utility, which cannot exceed their initially nonnegative truthful utility. Initially unserved members remain unserved.

No positive-mass subset can strictly gain. This proves the specified group-strategyproofness, including measurable coalitions that split types into finitely many reports.

3.3. Approximation guarantee

Let a truthful optimal type $t$ be deleted while population $A$ is active. Its whole mass is still in $A\cap O$. Therefore

$$ \begin{aligned} \mu_t v_t &<\mu_t\chi_{a_t}(A)\\ &\le\mu_t\chi_{a_t}(A\cap O)\\ &\le R(A\cap O)\\ &\le\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}C(O). \end{aligned} $$

The second inequality uses cross-monotonicity; the third uses nonnegative revenue contributions; the last uses $C(A\cap O)\le C(O)$.

If $N_O$ optimal types are deleted, their excluded value $D_O$ consequently satisfies

$$ D_O\le\frac{\lambda N_O}{\gamma}C(O). $$

For the final population $S$, let $V_{\mathrm{served,out}}$ be served value outside $O$. Cross-monotonicity and truthful individual rationality give

$$ R(S)\le R(S\cap O)+V_{\mathrm{served,out}} \le\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}C(O)+V_{\mathrm{served,out}}. $$

Hence

$$ C'(S)\le\lambda C(O)+\gamma V_{\mathrm{served,out}}. $$

If some optimal type survives, $N_O\le\tau-1$. Adding excluded value, and using $\gamma\ge1$, bounds social cost by

$$ \left(\lambda+\frac{\lambda(\tau-1)}{\gamma}\right)C(O) +\gamma V_{\mathrm{out}}. $$

If no optimal type survives, final revenue comes entirely from outside $O$. Thus

$$ C'(S)\le\gamma V_{\mathrm{served,out}}, $$

and social cost is at most

$$ \frac{\lambda\tau}{\gamma}C(O)+\gamma V_{\mathrm{out}}. $$

Because $\gamma\ge1$,

$$ \frac{\lambda\tau}{\gamma} \le \lambda+\frac{\lambda(\tau-1)}{\gamma}. $$

Both cases therefore prove the guarantee

$$ \boxed{ \alpha= \max\left\{ \gamma,\, \lambda+\frac{\lambda(\tau-1)}{\gamma} \right\}.} $$

This is an approximation inequality without division by $\operatorname{OPT}$, so it also proves zero social cost whenever $\operatorname{OPT}=0$.

4. Applying the construction on general graphs

Let $d(a,b)$ be shortest-path distance in the root component. Nonnegative costs make this a finite pseudometric on the public locations and the root.

For an active population $A$, form the complete distance graph on its positive-mass locations together with $r$. At threshold $h\ge0$, retain edges of length at most $h$. Let $B_A(a,h)$ be the threshold component containing $a$.

Define

$$ f_a(A,h)= \begin{cases} 0,&r\in B_A(a,h),\\ 1/w_A(B_A(a,h)),&r\notin B_A(a,h), \end{cases} $$

and, for a fixed parameter $2\le\gamma\le\beta$, set

$$ \chi_a(A)=\frac1\gamma\int_0^\infty f_a(A,h)\,dh. $$

Every nonroot component has positive active mass, so its denominator is positive. The integrand vanishes beyond the largest distance threshold, and the integral is a finite sum.

4.1. Cross-monotonicity and total revenue

If $A\subseteq B$, the threshold component containing an active location $a$ can only enlarge. If the enlarged component contains $r$, its integrand is zero. Otherwise its mass weakly increases. Thus

$$ f_a(B,h)\le f_a(A,h), \qquad \chi_a(B)\le\chi_a(A). $$

Let $K(A)$ be the minimum spanning-tree weight in the complete distance graph, and let $\kappa_A(h)$ be its threshold component count. Each nonroot component contributes exactly one to the mass-weighted sum of the integrands. Hence

$$ R(A)=\frac1\gamma\int_0^\infty(\kappa_A(h)-1)\,dh =\frac{K(A)}{\gamma}. $$

For the last equality, apply Kruskal’s algorithm. After edges of length at most $h$ have been processed, its forest has $\kappa_A(h)$ components. Its completed spanning tree therefore has exactly $\kappa_A(h)-1$ edges longer than $h$. Integrating that count gives its total edge weight. Threshold ties affect only finitely many integration points.

4.2. Comparison with connecting cost

The metric spanning-tree weight satisfies

$$ C(A)\le K(A)\le2C(A). $$

For the first inequality, expand each metric tree edge into a shortest path in the original graph. Their union is feasible and costs at most $K(A)$.

For the second inequality, take a minimum connecting subgraph. Removing cycles and irrelevant edges produces a tree of the same minimum cost. Double its edges to obtain a closed walk of length $2C(A)$, then shortcut between successive distinct required vertices using shortest-path distance. The resulting metric tour has length at most $2C(A)$; deleting one edge gives a spanning tree of no greater weight.

If the required vertex set contains only the root, both $C(A)$ and $K(A)$ are zero. This separately covers the empty-population and root-only cases.

Choose a metric minimum spanning tree using fixed ties and expand its edges into fixed shortest paths. The union has cost

$$ C'(A)\le K(A)=\gamma R(A). $$

Also,

$$ R(A)\le\frac2\gamma C(A). $$

The construction lemma applies with $\lambda=2$, proving a group-strategyproof, $\beta$-budget-balanced mechanism with

$$ \boxed{ \alpha_\gamma(\tau)= \max\left\{ \gamma,\, 2+\frac{2(\tau-1)}{\gamma} \right\}, \qquad 2\le\gamma\le\beta.} $$

The parameter $\gamma$ remains fixed under all reporting and splitting deviations.

4.3. Running time

For rational inputs, shortest-path distances, threshold components, price sums, and the final connecting subgraph can all be computed exactly in polynomial time. There are at most $\tau$ deletion rounds and polynomially many rational operations per round.

Every active mass is a sum of input masses. Each price is a sum of polynomially many ratios involving these masses and input-derived distances. Their encoding lengths are polynomial in the input length.

For $\gamma=2$ or $\gamma=\beta$, the running time is

$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L), $$

with the encoding of $\beta$ included in $L$. There is no iteration over the numerical magnitude of $\beta$ or inverse masses.

5. Trees admit the construction for every $\beta\ge1$

There is also a useful extension to the budget range below two on tree graphs.

Root the graph tree at $r$. For an edge $e$, let $w_e(A)$ be active mass in its descendant subtree. At an active location $a$, define

$$ \chi_a(A)= \frac1\gamma \sum_{e\text{ on the path from }r\text{ to }a} \frac{c_e}{w_e(A)}, \qquad 1\le\gamma\le\beta. $$

Every denominator on this path is positive because $a$ has positive active mass. Enlarging $A$ weakly increases each denominator, so these prices are cross-monotone.

Summing payments edge by edge gives

$$ R(A) = \frac1\gamma\sum_{e:w_e(A)>0}c_e = \frac{C(A)}{\gamma}. $$

The unique union of required root paths is a minimum connecting subgraph. Thus

$$ C'(A)=C(A)=\gamma R(A). $$

The construction lemma applies with $\lambda=1$, yielding

$$ \boxed{ \alpha_\gamma^{\mathrm{tree}}(\tau) = \max\left\{ \gamma,\, 1+\frac{\tau-1}{\gamma} \right\}, \qquad 1\le\gamma\le\beta.} $$

In particular, $\gamma=1$ gives an exactly budget-balanced, group-strategyproof, $\tau$-approximate mechanism on every tree. Its rational-input running time is $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$.

6. Consequences and remaining gaps

For general graphs, choose the fixed parameter $\gamma=\beta$ when $\beta\ge2$. If additionally $\beta\le\sqrt{\tau}$, then

$$ \beta\le\frac{\tau}{\beta}, $$

and the upper bound becomes

$$ \alpha_\beta(\tau)\le2+\frac{2\tau}{\beta}. $$

The full-class lower bound from Section 2 therefore matches this up to universal constants:

$$ \boxed{ \alpha_{\mathrm{optimal}}=\Theta(\tau/\beta) \quad\text{for general graphs when }2\le\beta\le\sqrt{\tau}.} $$

On trees, the same argument with $\lambda=1$ establishes

$$ \boxed{ \alpha_{\mathrm{optimal}}^{\mathrm{tree}}=\Theta(\tau/\beta) \quad\text{when }1\le\beta\le\sqrt{\tau}.} $$

The lower bound applies to trees because its graph is a single edge.

The important distinction from the discrete setting is that a population can contain exponentially many equal-mass blocks while its aggregate distribution has only $\tau$ value levels. Coalition incentives still constrain each block separately. The anchor construction makes this distinction rigorous while preserving exactly $\tau$ positive-mass types.

Thus the lower-bound alternative in the question is settled affirmatively, with the stronger rate $\Omega(\tau/\beta)$. Determining the optimal approximation over the full parameter range requires additional arguments.
