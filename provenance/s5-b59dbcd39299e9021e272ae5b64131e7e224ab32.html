The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite alternative set \(A\), a finite catalogue of preference types \(\theta=(U_\theta,\delta_\theta)\), and a rational uncertainty set \(\mathcal{M}\) for their masses, find an adaptive policy that queries pairs of alternatives within response-history cohorts. Each query returns the exact masses answering \(x\succ y\), \(y\succ x\), or indifference. The policy must identify, for every society \(\mu\in\mathcal{M}\) consistent with the answers, exactly the alternatives acceptable to at least an \(\alpha\)-fraction of society, while minimizing worst-case total mass queried.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) alternatives. The known catalogue \(\Theta\) contains \(\tau\) types \(\theta=(U_\theta,\delta_\theta)\), with explicitly tabulated \(U_\theta:A\to\mathbb{Q}\) and \(\delta_\theta\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\).
Type \(\theta\) answers \(x\succ y\) precisely when \(U_\theta(x)>U_\theta(y)+\delta_\theta\), answers the reverse symmetrically, and otherwise answers indifference. It accepts \(x\) precisely when \(U_\theta(x)\ge\max_{y\in A}U_\theta(y)-\delta_\theta\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses sum to \(1\). Here the support is known and the mass vector is hidden, belonging to \(\mathcal M=P\cap\mathbb Q^\tau\), where the input specifies a nonempty rational polytope \(P\subseteq\{\mu\in\mathbb R_{>0}^\tau:\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\}\) by linear inequalities.
Initially everyone belongs to one cohort. A query selects an entire current cohort and two distinct alternatives, returns the exact three response masses, and partitions that cohort by response; cohorts are distinguished only by their accumulated query-response histories. Individual types and arbitrary fractions of cohorts cannot be selected directly.
A deterministic adaptive policy depends on all observed masses and histories. Querying cohort \(C\) costs its mass \(\sum_{\theta\in C}\mu_\theta\), charged again for each subsequent query; total cost is the sum over queries.
Given binary rationals \(0<\alpha\le1\) and \(B\ge0\), a policy must terminate for every \(\mu\in\mathcal M\) and output exactly \(F_\alpha(\mu)=\{x\in A:\sum_{\theta:\,x\text{ accepted by }\theta}\mu_\theta\ge\alpha\}\). At termination this same output must hold for every mass vector consistent with the entire transcript.
Is deciding whether such a policy exists with worst-case total queried mass at most \(B\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), the number of defining inequalities, and total input bit length \(L\), including \(\alpha\) and \(B\).
For a single decision maker whose true utility belongs to the assumed model, adaptive comparison of possibly acceptable alternatives terminates with exactly all truly acceptable alternatives; this is a correctness result, without an optimal-policy complexity classification. [Branke et al.](https://pure.port.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/7427577/Efficient_Pairwise_Preference_Elicitation.pdf)
No result settling the exact cohort-mass policy problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The utility representation was unspecified; use explicit rational utility tables with unrestricted signs, and nonnegative rational thresholds, as permitted by the formal preference definitions.
  • Acceptability and boundary responses were undefined; use utility within the threshold of the maximum, strict preference beyond the threshold, and indifference at equality.
  • Normalize total mass to one and take a nonempty alternative set and a rational fraction threshold strictly greater than zero and at most one.
  • The uncertainty-set encoding was unspecified; use rational points of a polytope supplied by rational linear inequalities, with all points having positive mass on the same listed support.
  • Cohort operations were unspecified; start with the whole society, query an entire current cohort, and split it by response without direct selection by type or arbitrary fractional selection.
  • Interpret total mass queried as cumulative cohort mass, charging repeated participation each time, and use deterministic policies terminating for every feasible society.
  • Express exact minimization through the decision problem with a binary-rational budget; policies may depend on exact mass observations, and worst-case cost means the supremum over feasible rational societies.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. This holds even when every indifference threshold equals \(\delta_\theta=1\), every utility belongs to \(\{0,1,2\}\), and the mass polytope is defined by coordinate bounds and normalization.

The proof below applies to fully adaptive policies that observe exact response masses.

1. Reduction from Vertex Cover

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete Vertex Cover problem: given a simple undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\) and an integer \(k\), decide whether there is a set \(S\subseteq V\) with \(|S|\le k\) meeting every edge. See [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Write \(n=|V|\) and \(q=|E|\). We may restrict to \(q\ge1\) and \(1\le k\le n\): excluded cases are directly decidable and can be mapped to fixed yes or no instances satisfying these restrictions.

Alternatives and types

Create the alternatives
\(\displaystyle A=\{x\}\cup\{a_v:v\in V\}\).

There is one distinguished type \(\theta_0\), and one type \(\theta_e\) for each edge \(e\in E\). Thus \(m=n+1\) and \(\tau=q+1\).

Every type has threshold \(1\). Define the distinguished type by
\(\displaystyle U_0(x)=0,\qquad U_0(a_v)=1\quad(v\in V).\)

For an edge type, define
\(\displaystyle U_e(x)=0,\qquad U_e(a_v)= \begin{cases} 2,&v\in e,\\ 1,&v\notin e. \end{cases}\)

These are distinct types: different edges in a simple graph have different endpoint sets, and every edge type differs from \(\theta_0\).

Mass polytope and budget

Set
\(\displaystyle \alpha=1-\frac{1}{2(k+1)},\qquad B=k,\qquad c=\frac{1}{2q(k+1)}.\)

Define
\(\displaystyle P=\left\{\mu:\ \mu_0+\sum_{e\in E}\mu_e=1,\quad \frac c2\le\mu_e\le\frac{3c}{2}\ \text{for every }e\in E\right\}.\)

The equality can be encoded by two inequalities, giving \(h=2q+2\) defining inequalities.

Every edge coordinate is positive. Moreover,
\(\displaystyle \mu_0=1-\sum_e\mu_e \ge1-\frac{3qc}{2} =1-\frac{3}{4(k+1)}>0.\)

Thus \(P\) satisfies the required strict positivity condition. It is a bounded rational polytope and contains the rational vector
\(\displaystyle \mu^\star_0=\alpha,\qquad \mu^\star_e=c\quad(e\in E),\)
because \(qc=1-\alpha\).

2. Comparisons and acceptance in the constructed instance

We first establish the exact behavior of every available comparison.

Comparisons between vertex alternatives. For any type and any vertices \(u,v\), the utility difference between \(a_u\) and \(a_v\) has absolute value at most \(1\). Consequently, every type answers indifference to every comparison between vertex alternatives.

Comparisons involving \(x\). In the comparison of \(a_v\) with \(x\):

  • The distinguished type answers indifference.
  • An edge type \(\theta_e\) answers \(a_v\succ x\) exactly when \(v\in e\).
  • All other edge types answer indifference.

Indeed, the relevant utility difference is \(2>1\) for incident edges and \(1\le1\) otherwise. Reversing the order of the alternatives only reverses the response labels.

Acceptance. Every type accepts every vertex alternative. For the distinguished type, the maximum utility is \(1\), so the acceptance threshold is \(0\). For an edge type, the maximum is \(2\), so the acceptance threshold is \(1\), and every vertex alternative has utility at least \(1\).

The distinguished type also accepts \(x\), whereas every edge type rejects \(x\). Therefore
\(\displaystyle F_\alpha(\mu)=\{a_v:v\in V\}\cup \begin{cases} \{x\},&\mu_0\ge\alpha,\\ \varnothing,&\mu_0<\alpha. \end{cases}\)

The only unknown output membership is that of \(x\).

3. A vertex cover gives a feasible policy

Suppose \(S=\{v_1,\ldots,v_s\}\) is a vertex cover with \(s\le k\).

For each \(i=1,\ldots,s\), query the current cohort containing the distinguished type with the pair \(a_{v_i},x\). This is an allowed cohort selection: the cohort is identified by its accumulated history of indifference responses to these queries.

Each query separates the remaining edge types incident to \(v_i\) from that cohort. Since \(S\) meets every edge, after the final query the cohort contains exactly \(\theta_0\).

Its observed mass is therefore exactly \(\mu_0\). Output all vertex alternatives and include \(x\) precisely when this mass is at least \(\alpha\). Every mass vector consistent with the transcript has the same distinguished-type mass, so the output satisfies the required terminal certification condition.

There are at most \(k\) queries, each on a cohort of mass at most \(1\). Hence, for every \(\mu\in\mathcal M\),
\(\displaystyle \operatorname{cost}(\mu)\le s\le k=B.\)

Thus a vertex cover of size at most \(k\) gives a feasible policy.

4. Every feasible policy yields a vertex cover

Suppose a feasible deterministic adaptive policy exists. Run it at \(\mu^\star\).

We first prove that its terminal cohort containing \(\theta_0\) must contain no edge type.

Terminal separation is necessary

Suppose instead that an edge type \(\theta_e\) remains in the same terminal cohort as \(\theta_0\). Choose the rational number \(\varepsilon=c/4\), and define
\(\displaystyle \mu'_0=\alpha-\varepsilon,\qquad \mu'_e=c+\varepsilon,\qquad \mu'_f=c\quad(f\ne e).\)

Normalization is preserved, and
\(\displaystyle \frac c2<c+\varepsilon=\frac{5c}{4}<\frac{3c}{2}.\)
Thus \(\mu'\in\mathcal M\).

Cohort partitions only refine. Therefore, because \(\theta_0\) and \(\theta_e\) share a terminal cohort, they shared a cohort throughout the run and gave the same response whenever that cohort was queried.

For every query in the run at \(\mu^\star\), each response class consequently contains both of these types or neither. The changes \(-\varepsilon\) and \(+\varepsilon\) cancel in every observed response mass. All other coordinates are unchanged.

Inductively, determinism implies that the policy takes exactly the same actions and receives exactly the same transcript at \(\mu'\). In particular, it terminates with the same output.

But
\(\displaystyle x\in F_\alpha(\mu^\star),\qquad x\notin F_\alpha(\mu'),\)
since \(\mu^\star_0=\alpha\) and \(\mu'_0<\alpha\). This contradicts correctness, as well as terminal certification.

Hence every edge type must be separated from \(\theta_0\) before termination.

Separation forces a cover

Consider the first query that separates a particular edge type \(\theta_e\) from \(\theta_0\). It must query their shared cohort.

By Section 2, a comparison between vertex alternatives cannot separate them. A comparison involving \(x\) separates them only if its other alternative is \(a_v\) for an endpoint \(v\in e\).

Let \(r\) be the number of comparisons involving \(x\) made on the distinguished type’s cohort during this run, counting repetitions. The vertices appearing in these comparisons meet every edge, so they form a vertex cover after repetitions are removed.

At \(\mu^\star\), every such queried cohort has mass at least \(\mu^\star_0=\alpha\). Therefore
\(\displaystyle \operatorname{cost}(\mu^\star)\ge r\alpha.\)

If \(r\ge k+1\), then
\(\displaystyle \operatorname{cost}(\mu^\star) \ge(k+1)\alpha =(k+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{2(k+1)}\right) =k+\frac12>B,\)
a contradiction.

Thus \(r\le k\), and the extracted vertex cover has size at most \(k\).

This argument allows arbitrary mass-dependent choices, queries on other cohorts, and repeated queries. Such additional queries cannot invalidate the lower bound.

5. Encoding size and running times

The construction has
\(\displaystyle m=n+1,\qquad \tau=q+1,\qquad h=2q+2=2\tau.\)

Utilities and thresholds have constant bit length. The numbers \(\alpha,B,c/2,3c/2\) have bit length \(O(\log(q(k+1)))\), including the threshold and budget encodings explicitly.

With a dense inequality representation, the total output bit length satisfies
\(\displaystyle L=O\!\left(m\tau+h\tau+\tau\log(m\tau)\right).\)

The reduction can be constructed in
\(\displaystyle O\!\left((m\tau+h\tau)\log^{O(1)}(m\tau)\right)\)
bit operations, and therefore in polynomial time.

Consequently, a general decision algorithm running in
\(\displaystyle (m+\tau+h+L)^{O(1)}\)
time would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

For the constructed family, exhaustive enumeration of vertex subsets decides policy existence in \(O(2^m\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,h,L))\) time, by the proved equivalence. This is a bound for this family, not a claimed general upper bound or a proof of general membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

The complete equivalence is
\(\displaystyle G\text{ has a vertex cover of size at most }k \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed instance admits the required policy}.\)

It settles the requested hardness classification.
