Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq 1\) candidates. A society is given by its support: an explicit list \(T\) of \(\tau\geq 1\) distinct partial orders on \(C\), each with a positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
For partial orders \(\alpha,\beta\), define \(\operatorname{KT}(\alpha,\beta)=|\{(x,y)\in C^2:x<_\alpha y\text{ and }y<_\beta x\}|\); incomparable pairs contribute zero. For a linear order \(\pi\), set \(K_\mu(\pi)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\operatorname{KT}(\pi,t)\).
Given binary-encoded integers \(r\geq1\) and \(d,s\geq0\), decide whether there are \(r\) distinct linear orders \(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_r\), each globally minimizing \(K_\mu\), such that \(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\operatorname{KT}(\pi_i,\pi_j)\geq d\) and \(\operatorname{KT}(\pi_i,\pi_j)\geq s\) for every \(i<j\). All minimizing orders are eligible, without tie-breaking.
Let \(\rho=\bigcap_{t\in T}t\), and let \(w\) be the pathwidth of the graph on \(C\) whose edges join distinct candidates incomparable in \(\rho\). State running times in \(m,\tau,L,r,d,s,w\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? If it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, is it fixed-parameter tractable parameterized by \((w,r,s)\)?

For explicit lists of partial votes, the diverse aggregation problem is FPT parameterized by unanimity width, number of solutions, solution imperfection, and scatteredness; this includes exact optima. The score-threshold version is NP-hard, including its single-solution restriction. [Arrighi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.09413)
Computing a single optimal Kemeny order is NP-hard even for four complete votes. [Dwork et al.](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~naor/PAPERS/rank_www10.html) No verified classification was found for the exact mass-based diversity decision problem stated here.

  • Selected the decision formulation from 'compute or decide whether'.
  • Specified explicit partial-order relations and total binary input length.
  • Required distinct output orders, following the discrete set formulation even when the scatteredness threshold is zero.
  • Used unordered pairs for total diversity, following the algorithm's accumulation over indices i<j; the displayed diversity definition double-counts pairs, and the later author-hosted version retains this inconsistency.
  • Set r to a positive integer and d,s to nonnegative integers; allowed any nonempty candidate set.
  • Made all global minimizers eligible without tie-breaking.
  • Defined unanimity width from the positive-mass support and selected (w,r,s) as the parameter tuple, with solution imperfection fixed at zero.

Classification

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=1\), \(r=2\), \(s=1\), and the minimum score is zero. Nevertheless, it is fixed-parameter tractable parameterized by \((w,r,s)\), including with binary-rational masses and binary-encoded \(d\).

For \(r=1\), the answer is simply yes if and only if \(d=0\): an optimal order always exists, and both diversity conditions involve empty collections of pairs.

1. Hardness for one partial vote

We use the following established result: given a finite poset \(P\) and an integer \(k\), deciding whether two linear extensions of \(P\) disagree on at least \(k\) unordered pairs is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. This is Theorem 5 of [Brightwell and Massow, *Diametral Pairs of Linear Extensions*](https://arxiv.org/html/0809.1828). We may restrict to \(k\geq1\): instances with \(k=0\) are always yes-instances and can be replaced by a fixed positive-threshold yes-instance.

Given such an instance \((P,k)\), construct a society on the ground set of \(P\) by setting
\[ T=\{P\},\qquad \mu_P=1,\qquad r=2,\qquad d=k,\qquad s=1. \]
Here and below, displayed formulas use the requested delimiters:
\(T=\{P\},\ \mu_P=1,\ r=2,\ d=k,\ s=1\).

For every linear order \(\pi\), its score is \(\operatorname{KT}(\pi,P)\). A linear extension of \(P\) has score zero. Conversely, score zero means that no comparable pair of \(P\) is reversed, so \(\pi\) is a linear extension. Thus the globally minimizing orders are exactly all linear extensions of \(P\).

For two linear orders, the specified Kendall distance counts each disagreeing unordered pair exactly once. Consequently, the constructed instance is yes precisely when \(P\) has two linear extensions at distance at least \(k\). Since \(k\geq1\), these orders are distinct and satisfy scatteredness at least one.

The construction is polynomial-time and proves the claimed hardness.

2. Every optimum respects unanimity

We first justify restricting the parameterized algorithm to linear extensions of \(\rho\).

Lemma. Every globally minimizing order extends \(\rho=\bigcap_{t\in T}t\).

Proof. Suppose \(a<_\rho b\), but a linear order \(\pi\) puts \(b\) before \(a\). Obtain \(\pi'\) by exchanging their positions.

Fix a vote \(t\). Because \(a<_\rho b\), we have \(a<_t b\). Reordering the pair \(\{a,b\}\) decreases its contribution by one.

For a candidate \(z\) between \(b\) and \(a\) in \(\pi\), the decrease from the two pairs involving \(z\) is

\( [\ z<_t b\ ]-[\ z<_t a\ ]+ [\ a<_t z\ ]-[\ b<_t z\ ], \)

where brackets denote indicator values. Both differences are nonnegative: transitivity gives \(z<_t a\Rightarrow z<_t b\) and \(b<_t z\Rightarrow a<_t z\). All remaining pair contributions are unchanged. Therefore

\( \operatorname{KT}(\pi,t)-\operatorname{KT}(\pi',t)\geq1. \)

Weighting and summing yields \(K_\mu(\pi)-K_\mu(\pi')\geq\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Hence \(\pi\) cannot be optimal. Applying this argument to every violated relation proves the lemma. \(\square\)

3. Polynomial-size integer encoding of the masses

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), with positive integers encoded in binary. Define

\( D=\prod_{t\in T}b_t,\qquad W_t=a_t(D/b_t). \)

These are positive integers satisfying \(\sum_tW_t=D\). Furthermore,

\( \log_2D=\sum_t\log_2b_t=O(L). \)

Thus their binary encodings have polynomial length; no voter replication is required.

Define a nonnegative integer pair cost by

\( c(x,y)=\sum_{t\in T}W_t[\ y<_t x\ ]. \)

For every linear order \(\pi\),

\( \sum_{x<_\pi y}c(x,y) =\sum_tW_t\operatorname{KT}(\pi,t) =D K_\mu(\pi). \)

Also, \(c(x,y)=0\) whenever \(x<_\rho y\), because every vote puts \(x\) before \(y\). Consequently, for a linear extension \(\pi\) of \(\rho\), its completion cost is

\( c(\pi\setminus\rho) =\sum_{x<_\pi y}c(x,y) =D K_\mu(\pi). \)

By the lemma, every global optimum belongs to this restricted domain. Positive scaling preserves all score comparisons and ties. Therefore the minimum-cost completions of \((\rho,c)\) are exactly the globally minimizing orders in the original problem.

The construction takes \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) bit operations, including computing transitive closures if needed. Every cost is at most \(D\).

4. Applying the parameterized completion theorem

We use the integer-cost version of the cited result, rather than an algorithm requiring an explicitly replicated vote list.

A zero-safe consequence of Theorem 14 and the state count in Remark 5 of [Arrighi et al., *Diversity in Kemeny Rank Aggregation*](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.09413v1) is the following: for a poset on \(n\) elements, incomparability-graph pathwidth \(p\), and integer pair costs bounded by \(M\), exact-optimum diversity with \(r\) orders, scatteredness \(h\), and total threshold \(q\) is decidable in time

\( 2^{O(r(p+1)\log(p+2))} (h+1)^{O(r^2)} (q+1)\, \operatorname{poly}(n,\log(M+2),\text{threshold encoding lengths}). \)

This uses solution imperfection zero. The factors remain positive at zero thresholds: Remark 5 counts \(\delta+1\) cost values per structural state. The theorem constructs the required decomposition; one need not supply it.

All hypotheses hold here:

  • \(\rho\) is a partial order, being an intersection of partial orders.
  • Its incomparability graph has the specified pathwidth \(w\).
  • The costs are nonnegative integers bounded by \(D\), whose bit length is \(O(L)\).
  • Section 3 proves exact correspondence of all optimal solutions.

For \(r\geq2\), replace scatteredness by \(h=\max\{s,1\}\). This is equivalent to combining the original scatteredness requirement with distinctness, because two linear orders have distance zero exactly when they are identical.

Applying the theorem therefore gives the running-time bound

\( 2^{O(r(w+1)\log(w+2))} (s+2)^{O(r^2)} (d+1)\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \)

5. Removing numeric dependence on the binary threshold

The preceding dependence on \(d\) must be bounded to establish FPT for the stated binary encoding.

Let \(N=\binom m2\). Every pair of linear orders has distance at most \(N\), so every collection of \(r\) orders has total diversity at most

\( H=\binom r2 N. \)

For \(r\geq2\), reject immediately if \(d>H\) or \(s>N\). These comparisons use polynomial-time binary arithmetic. One may also reject if \(r>m!\).

On every remaining instance, \(d+1\leq1+\binom r2\binom m2\). Hence a valid overall bound is

\( 2^{O(r(w+1)\log(w+2))} (s+2)^{O(r^2)} \left(1+\binom r2\binom m2\right) \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). \)

Absorbing the factors depending only on \(r\) into the parameter function, this is \(f(w,r,s)\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), with a polynomial exponent independent of the parameters. This proves fixed-parameter tractability for the exact mass-based problem.
