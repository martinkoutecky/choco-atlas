The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given ordered item types \(a\in M\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_a\), and an explicit blacklist \(\mathcal F\) of pairs of intervals, decide whether a unit-mass population can each receive an integral interval bundle. Writing \(x_S\geq 0\) for the mass receiving interval \(S\), require \(\sum_S x_S=1\), \(\sum_{S:a\in S}x_S=q_a\) for every item type \(a\), and \(x_Bx_{B'}=0\) for every \((B,B')\in\mathcal F\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with \(m\geq1\), be ordered item types, and let \(\mathcal I=\{\emptyset\}\cup\{\{\ell,\ell+1,\ldots,r\}:1\leq\ell\leq r\leq m\}\). An integral bundle contains one copy of each included item type.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here the population is atomless and homogeneous, with one type of mass \(1\), so \(\tau=1\).
The input consists of binary rational per-capita supplies \(q_a\in[0,1]\) and an explicit blacklist \(\mathcal F\) of pairs \((B,B')\in\mathcal I^2\) satisfying \(B\cap B'=\emptyset\). The blacklist is independent of population type and has no closure requirement.
Feasibility means that real masses \(x_S\geq0\), indexed by \(S\in\mathcal I\), satisfy \(\sum_{S\in\mathcal I}x_S=1\), \(\sum_{S\in\mathcal I:a\in S}x_S=q_a\) for every \(a\in M\), and \(x_Bx_{B'}=0\) for every \((B,B')\in\mathcal F\). Empty bundles are allowed, and supplies must be exhausted exactly; the supplies need not sum to \(1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Is this feasibility problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|\mathcal F|,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents, deciding whether a complete contiguous allocation avoids forbidden tuples \((i,B,B')\)—prohibiting agent \(i\) from receiving \(B\) while another receives \(B'\)—is NP-complete, even with interval bundles; the hardness construction is symmetric across agents. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31457v3#A3)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Included the empty bundle among intervals, as permitted by the discrete allocation definitions.
  • Required blacklisted bundles to be disjoint, carrying over the discrete forbidden-tuple domain.
  • Specified supplies as binary rationals in [0,1], since a person receives at most one copy of each item type; imposed no normalization on their sum.
  • Used an atomless homogeneous population with one input type of mass 1, since the graded question has no type-dependent constraints or agent index.
  • Allowed real allocation masses and splitting the population among bundles, while keeping each person's bundle integral.
  • Interpreted blacklist pairs as type-independent prohibitions on simultaneous positive bundle masses, retaining the graded product constraints and imposing no monotonicity closure.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The exact mass-feasibility problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already for \(\tau=1\) and identical positive dyadic supplies. The reduction below uses the empty bundle explicitly; it does not require transferring the finite-agent hardness result.

1. Reduction from graph coloring.

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem of deciding whether a finite simple graph admits a proper coloring with three colors. See [Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(76)90059-1).

Let \(G=(V,E)\), where \(V=\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and \(n\geq1\). Set
\[ \]
\(m=4n,\qquad t=\lceil\log_2(8n)\rceil,\qquad c=2^{-t}.\)

Give every item supply \(q_a=c\). In particular,
\(4nc\leq\frac12\).

For each vertex \(v\), write \(b_v=4(v-1)\) and define six intervals:
\[ \]
\(P_{v,k}=\{b_v+1,\ldots,b_v+k\},\qquad Q_{v,k}=\{b_v+k+1,\ldots,b_v+4\},\qquad k\in\{1,2,3\}.\)

Thus \(P_{v,k}\) and \(Q_{v,k}\) partition the four-item block belonging to \(v\). Let \(\mathcal A\) denote the collection of these \(6n\) intervals.

The blacklist consists precisely of the following pairs:

  • \((\emptyset,S)\), for every nonempty \(S\in\mathcal I\setminus\mathcal A\).
  • \((P_{v,k},Q_{v,\ell})\), for every vertex \(v\) and every \(1\leq k<\ell\leq3\).
  • \((P_{u,k},P_{v,k})\), for every edge \(\{u,v\}\in E\), ordered with \(u<v\), and every \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\).

All these pairs satisfy the required disjointness condition. The empty bundle is disjoint from every interval. In the second family, the prefix ends at \(b_v+k\), whereas the suffix starts at \(b_v+\ell+1\). In the third family, the intervals belong to different four-item blocks.

There are \(O(n^2+|E|)\) blacklist entries. Each interval can be specified by its endpoints, and each supply has \(O(\log n)\) bits. Even if intervals are explicitly listed by their items, the construction still has polynomial size and can be produced in polynomial time.

2. Every feasible solution chooses exactly one color per vertex.

Suppose \(x\) is feasible. Summing all item-supply equations gives
\[ \]
\(\displaystyle \sum_{a=1}^{m}q_a=\sum_{\substack{S\in\mathcal I\\S\neq\emptyset}}|S|x_S.\)

Every nonempty interval contains at least one item. Therefore
\[ \]
\(\displaystyle 1-x_{\emptyset}=\sum_{S\neq\emptyset}x_S\leq\sum_{S\neq\emptyset}|S|x_S=4nc\leq\frac12.\)

Consequently \(x_{\emptyset}\geq\frac12>0\). The first blacklist family now forces
\(x_S=0\) for every nonempty \(S\notin\mathcal A\).

Fix a vertex \(v\), and abbreviate
\(p_k=x_{P_{v,k}}\) and \(s_k=x_{Q_{v,k}}\).
Only these six intervals can contribute to the supplies in its block. The four item equations are
\[ \]
\(\begin{aligned} p_1+p_2+p_3&=c,\\ p_2+p_3+s_1&=c,\\ p_3+s_1+s_2&=c,\\ s_1+s_2+s_3&=c. \end{aligned}\)

Subtracting consecutive equations yields
\[ \]
\(s_1=p_1,\qquad s_2=p_2,\qquad s_3=p_3.\)

For each \(k<\ell\), the second blacklist family gives
\(p_ks_\ell=0\), hence \(p_kp_\ell=0\).
Thus at most one of \(p_1,p_2,p_3\) is positive. Their sum is \(c>0\), so exactly one is positive, and it equals \(c\).

Define \(\chi(v)\) to be its index. We have proved
\[ \]
\(x_{P_{v,\chi(v)}}=x_{Q_{v,\chi(v)}}=c,\)
with the other four masses zero.

For an edge \(\{u,v\}\), if \(\chi(u)=\chi(v)=k\), then the third blacklist family would require
\[ \]
\(0=x_{P_{u,k}}x_{P_{v,k}}=c^2>0,\)
a contradiction. Hence \(\chi\) is a proper three-coloring of \(G\).

3. Every proper three-coloring gives a feasible solution.

Conversely, suppose \(\chi:V\to\{1,2,3\}\) is a proper coloring. Assign
\[ \]
\(x_{P_{v,\chi(v)}}=x_{Q_{v,\chi(v)}}=c\)
for each vertex \(v\), set every other nonempty-bundle mass to zero, and set
\[ \]
\(x_{\emptyset}=1-2nc.\)

Since \(2nc\leq\frac14\), the empty-bundle mass is nonnegative. The total mass is exactly one.

Within each block, its two selected intervals partition the block, so every item receives mass exactly \(c\). Intervals from other blocks contribute nothing to its supplies.

Every blacklist condition holds:

  • In the first family, the nonempty interval has zero mass.
  • In the second family, both intervals could have positive mass only if \(k=\chi(v)=\ell\), contradicting \(k<\ell\).
  • In the third family, both intervals could have positive mass only if adjacent vertices had the same color.

Thus the constructed instance is feasible.

Together, the two directions prove
\[ \]
\(G\text{ is three-colorable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{the constructed mass instance is feasible}.\)

This establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under all the stated restrictions.

4. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), despite allowing real masses.

We prove that every feasible instance has a polynomial-length rational certificate.

For a bundle \(S\), define its column
\[ \]
\(a_S=(1,\mathbf1_{\{1\in S\}},\ldots,\mathbf1_{\{m\in S\}})^{\mathsf T},\)
and write \(b=(1,q_1,\ldots,q_m)^{\mathsf T}\). The linear constraints are
\(\sum_S x_Sa_S=b\).

Among feasible solutions, choose one with the smallest number of positive coordinates, and let \(T\) be its support. Such a minimum exists because there are finitely many bundles.

The columns indexed by \(T\) are linearly independent. Indeed, suppose
\(\sum_{S\in T}z_Sa_S=0\) for a nonzero real vector \(z\). The first row gives \(\sum_{S\in T}z_S=0\), so \(z\) has both positive and negative coordinates. Set
\[ \]
\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\min_{\substack{S\in T\\z_S>0}}\frac{x_S}{z_S}>0,\qquad x'_S=x_S-\varepsilon z_S\quad(S\in T),\)
and leave all other coordinates zero.

Then \(x'\) is nonnegative, preserves every linear equality, and eliminates at least one positive coordinate. Its support is a subset of \(T\), so it preserves every blacklist condition as well. This contradicts minimality.

Consequently \(r=|T|\leq m+1\). Choose \(r\) rows on which these columns form an invertible \(r\times r\) matrix \(D\). Its entries are zero or one.

Write each supply as a rational with positive integer denominator, and let \(Q\) be the product of those denominators. Then \(\log_2 Q\leq L\), and \(Qb\) is integral. By Cramer’s rule, each positive coordinate has the form
\[ \]
\(\displaystyle x_S=\frac{\det D_S}{Q\det D},\)
where \(D_S\) replaces the corresponding column of \(D\) by the selected entries of \(Qb\).

Because every entry of \(b\) lies in \([0,1]\), the determinant expansion gives
\[ \]
\(1\leq|\det D|\leq r!,\qquad |\det D_S|\leq Qr!.\)

Thus each mass has a rational representation with \(O(L+r\log r)\) bits. Listing the at most \(m+1\) bundles and their masses therefore gives a polynomial-length certificate. A verifier checks nonnegativity, normalization, exact supplies, and every blacklist condition using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

The problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the proof of \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,|\mathcal F|,L\) would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
