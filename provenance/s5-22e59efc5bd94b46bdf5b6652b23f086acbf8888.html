The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_p\) summing to one over pair types \(p=(X_p,Y_p)\), with \(X_p,Y_p\in\{0,1\}^3\), define \(D=\sum_{p:X_p\le Y_p}\mu_p\). Let \(\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E\) exactly when \(p\ne q\), both pair types are incomparable in their own patient-donor relation, and \(X_p\le Y_q\) and \(X_q\le Y_p\). Choose exchange masses \(x_{pq}\ge0\) subject to \(\sum_{q:\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}x_{pq}\le\mu_p\) for every \(p\). What is the maximum of \(D+2\sum_{\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}x_{pq}\), when cohorts may be split among exchanges but each patient-donor pair receives one whole outcome?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An individual type is a vector in \(T=\{0,1\}^3\); write \(m=3\) for the fixed dimension. A pair type \(p=(X_p,Y_p)\) specifies its patient and donor; a donor of type \(Y\) can donate to a patient of type \(X\) exactly when \(X\le Y\) coordinatewise.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) distinct pair types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_p\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_p\mu_p=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Only left-lobe donation, direct transplants, and two-way exchanges are allowed. Pairs prefer a feasible direct transplant to any exchange, are indifferent among exchanges, and prefer an exchange to remaining unmatched; every directly compatible pair receives a direct transplant.
Let \(D=\sum_{p:X_p\le Y_p}\mu_p\). An unordered pair \(\{p,q\}\) belongs to \(\mathcal E\) exactly when \(p\ne q\), \(X_p\not\le Y_p\), \(Y_p\not\le X_p\), \(X_q\not\le Y_q\), \(Y_q\not\le X_q\), \(X_p\le Y_q\), and \(X_q\le Y_p\).
Each cohort is nonatomic and may be partitioned among outcomes, but each individual pair receives one whole outcome. An exchange mass \(x_{pq}=x_{qp}\ge0\) uses that mass from each endpoint, with \(\sum_{q:\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}x_{pq}\le\mu_p\); unused incompatible mass remains unmatched.
Define \(V(\mu)=\max_x\bigl(D+2\sum_{\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}x_{pq}\bigr)\) over these allocations. The decision input additionally includes a binary rational threshold \(b\in[0,1]\); \(L\) is the total input bit length.

Is deciding whether \(V(\mu)\ge b\) in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies with two sizes and only left-lobe donation, a sequential algorithm maximizes exchanges and transplants among individually rational matchings, giving polynomial-time optimization ([Ergin–Sönmez–Ünver](https://www.tayfunsonmez.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/ic-liver-exchange-2018-05-31.pdf)).

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\). In fact, the optimization reduces exactly to maximum flow in a network with \(\mathcal O(\tau)\) vertices and \(\mathcal O(\tau^2)\) arcs. The argument works for arbitrary dimension \(m\)\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)\).

1. Constructing the network

Index the supported pair types by \(p\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\)\). Compute \(D\)\) and construct \(\mathcal E\)\) using exactly the compatibility conditions in the question. Each coordinatewise comparison takes \(\mathcal O(m)\) time, so this requires \(\mathcal O(m\tau^2)\) comparisons of coordinates.

Construct a directed network with source \(s\)\), sink \(t\)\), and two copies \(p_L,p_R\)\) of every supported type. Its arcs and capacities are:

  • \(s\to p_L\)\), with capacity \(\mu_p\)\);
  • \(p_R\to t\)\), with capacity \(\mu_p\)\);
  • for every \(\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E\)\), both \(p_L\to q_R\)\) and \(q_L\to p_R\)\), each with capacity \(1\)\).

Let \(F^*\)\) be its maximum flow value. We prove

\(\displaystyle V(\mu)=D+F^*.\)

The two copies are an algorithmic construction; we do not assume that the exchange graph itself is bipartite.

2. Exact correspondence between flows and exchange allocations

From allocations to flows

Take any feasible exchange allocation \(x\)\). For each \(\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E\)\), send flow \(x_{pq}\)\) on each of the two corresponding middle arcs. On both \(s\to p_L\)\) and \(p_R\to t\)\), send

\(\displaystyle \sum_{q:\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}x_{pq}.\)

Flow conservation holds at every copy. The endpoint capacities hold by allocation feasibility. The middle capacities also hold because \(x_{pq}\le\mu_p\le1\)\).

The resulting flow has value

\(\displaystyle F=\sum_p\sum_{q:\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}x_{pq} =2\sum_{\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}x_{pq}.\)

Consequently, every feasible allocation satisfies

\(\displaystyle D+2\sum_{\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}x_{pq}\le D+F^*.\)

From flows to allocations

Conversely, take any feasible flow, and write \(f_{pq}\)\) for the flow on \(p_L\to q_R\)\). For every exchange edge, define

\(\displaystyle x_{pq}=x_{qp}=\frac{f_{pq}+f_{qp}}2.\)

These numbers are nonnegative. At each type \(p\)\), flow conservation and the endpoint capacities give

\(\displaystyle \sum_q f_{pq}=f(s,p_L)\le\mu_p,\qquad \sum_q f_{qp}=f(p_R,t)\le\mu_p,\)

where the sums range over exchange neighbors. Therefore,

\(\displaystyle \sum_qx_{pq} =\frac12\left(\sum_qf_{pq}+\sum_qf_{qp}\right) \le\mu_p.\)

Thus \(x\)\) is a feasible exchange allocation. Moreover, its exchanged patient mass equals the flow value:

\(\displaystyle 2\sum_{\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}x_{pq} =\sum_{\{p,q\}\in\mathcal E}(f_{pq}+f_{qp}) =\sum_p\sum_qf_{pq} =F.\)

Applying this construction to a maximum flow proves the reverse inequality and hence \(V(\mu)=D+F^*\)\).

Whole outcomes for individual pairs

The averaging above splits cohort mass, as permitted by the question. It does not assign fractional outcomes to individuals.

Explicitly, represent cohort \(p\)\) by an interval of length \(\mu_p\)\). Partition it into disjoint pieces of lengths \(x_{pq}\)\), one for each exchange neighbor, and an unmatched remainder. For each edge \(\{p,q\}\)\), identify the two equal-length pieces by their interval coordinates and exchange the corresponding pairs. Every participating individual receives exactly one exchange outcome.

Directly compatible cohorts receive their direct transplants. They have no incident exchange edges under the stated definition of \(\mathcal E\)\), so the construction respects their prescribed outcome.

3. Polynomial bit complexity and exact threshold comparison

Write each input mass as \(\mu_p=a_p/d_p\)\) and the threshold as \(b=c/d_b\)\), with positive integer denominators. Set

\(\displaystyle Q=d_b\prod_{p=1}^{\tau}d_p.\)

Although its numerical value can be large, its binary length satisfies

\(\displaystyle \log_2 Q=\log_2 d_b+\sum_p\log_2d_p=\mathcal O(L).\)

Multiplying every network capacity by \(Q\)\) produces integer capacities: endpoint capacities \(Q\mu_p\)\) and middle capacities \(Q\)\). All have \(\mathcal O(L)\) bits. Computing these integers takes polynomial time by ordinary binary arithmetic.

Use the Edmonds–Karp maximum-flow algorithm. Its standard bound is \(\mathcal O(NA^2)\) operations for a directed network with \(N\)\) vertices and \(A\)\) arcs, independently of capacity magnitudes. This follows from choosing shortest augmenting paths; see the [Cornell analysis of Edmonds–Karp](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs4820/2017sp/lectures/edmondskarp.pdf).

Our network satisfies the theorem’s hypotheses and has

\(\displaystyle N=2\tau+2,\qquad A=2\tau+2|\mathcal E|\le2\tau+\tau(\tau-1).\)

Thus the algorithm uses \(\mathcal O(\tau^5)\) operations. Starting with zero flow, all augmentations are integral. Every arc flow and residual capacity stays bounded by \(Q\)\), and the total flow is bounded by \(\sum_pQ\mu_p=Q\)\). Hence numerical operations involve only \(\mathcal O(L)\)-bit integers, giving polynomial bit complexity.

Let \(H\)\) be the maximum flow value in the scaled network, and compute the integers

\(\displaystyle D_Q=\sum_{p:X_p\le Y_p}Q\mu_p,\qquad B=Qb.\)

Scaling preserves feasible flows and their values, so the proved identity yields

\(\displaystyle V(\mu)=\frac{D_Q+H}{Q}.\)

The exact decision rule is therefore

\(\displaystyle V(\mu)\ge b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D_Q+H\ge B.\)

This includes equality without numerical approximation. If \(\mathcal E\)\) is empty, the same construction gives \(H=0\)\).

4. Conclusion

The construction, maximum-flow computation, and final integer comparison all run in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)\). No enumeration of unsupported types or expansion into a finite population is required. The result applies to the specified case \(m=3\)\) and also to variable dimension.
