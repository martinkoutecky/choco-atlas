The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) indivisible good kinds, rational \(0<a<b\), a finite type set \(T\subseteq\{a,b\}^{m}\) with rational mass distribution \(\mu\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\rho\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{m}\), compute a polynomial-size lottery or compact sampler over measurable assignments of integral bundles to a unit-mass population. Type-level expected bundles must satisfy ex-ante \(\mathrm{EF}\), while almost every realization must satisfy ex-post \(\mathrm{EFX}\) and continuum \(\mathrm{fPO}\), in time polynomial in \(m\), \(|T|\), and input length \(L\), rather than in the denominator-cleared population \(Q\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\geq1\), let \(0<a<b\) be binary rationals shared by all types, and let supplies be \(\rho\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^{m}\); utilities are additive.
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types \(T\subseteq\{a,b\}^{m}\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Represent the population by disjoint intervals \(I_t\) of lengths \(\mu_t\), with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\); write \(t(x)\) for the type of person \(x\).
A feasible realization is a measurable assignment \(z(x)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\) satisfying \(\int z_g\,d\lambda=\rho_g\) for every kind \(g\); multiple copies of a kind are allowed.
For a lottery over realizations, define \(\bar z_t=\mu_t^{-1}\mathbb E[\int_{I_t}z\,d\lambda]\). Type-level ex-ante EF means \(t\cdot\bar z_t\geq t\cdot\bar z_s\) for all \(t,s\in T\).
Ex-post EFX means that, for almost every pair \((x,y)\), \(t(x)\cdot z(x)\geq t(x)\cdot(z(y)-e_g)\) whenever \(z_g(y)>0\), where \(e_g\) is the unit vector for kind \(g\).
Continuum fPO means there is no measurable fractional assignment \(w(x)\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{m}\) with the same supplies, weakly improving utility almost everywhere and strictly improving it on a set of positive measure.
Compute a lottery whose realizations satisfy EFX and fPO with probability \(1\), and whose type-level expectations satisfy EF. Encode realizations by rational interval partitions with binary-encoded integral bundles; permit either an explicit rational lottery or an exact compact sampler producing such encodings.
Is this construction problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard, when output size and expected sampling time must also be polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?
For explicitly listed finite agents and individual goods with shared bi-valued utilities, a polynomial-time algorithm samples from a lottery satisfying ex-ante EF, ex-post EFX, and ex-post fPO ([verified result](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06877v2#S5)).
No result settling this exact continuum construction problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive type masses to sum to one and represented each type by a Lebesgue interval of its prescribed mass.
  • Interpreted good kinds as permitting multiple integral copies per person, with exact supply conservation in every realization.
  • Interpreted type-level expected bundles as per-person averages within each type, including expectation over the lottery.
  • Interpreted continuum EFX using almost-everywhere pairwise comparisons and removal of one copy of any kind present in the compared bundle.
  • Defined continuum fPO against all measurable fractional reallocations, with weak improvement almost everywhere and strict improvement on a positive-measure set, excluding improvements confined to null sets.
  • Specified rational interval partitions and binary-encoded bundle counts as realization encodings; an exact compact sampler must produce these encodings with polynomial expected sampling time.
  • Required at least one good kind; retained zero supplies and empty bundles because the discrete definition permits their counterparts.

Status and new partial results

I do not settle the general polynomial-time-versus-hardness question. I prove two additional results:

  • Continuum fPO has an exact characterization by the absence of negative cycles in a graph whose edge weights belong to $\{-1,0,1\}$. This gives a combinatorial verification algorithm and polynomial-size rational supporting prices.
  • The full construction problem is polynomial-time solvable, for arbitrary $m$ and $\tau$, whenever

$$ \sum_g\rho_g\leq \frac ab. $$
In this regime, a single deterministic realization suffices.

I also give a polynomial-time test extending the second construction to some larger-supply instances, and an explicit example showing why that test does not resolve the general problem.

1. Aggregating continuum Pareto improvements

For a feasible realization $z$, define
$$ A_{tg}=\int_{I_t}z_g\,d\lambda, \qquad U_t=\sum_g t_gA_{tg}. $$
These quantities are finite: all coordinates are nonnegative, supplies are finite, and utility coefficients are bounded.

Lemma 1. The realization $z$ is continuum fPO if and only if there is no matrix $Y\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{T\times m}$ satisfying
$$ \sum_tY_{tg}=\rho_g $$
for every $g$, and
$$ t\cdot Y_t\geq U_t $$
for every $t$, with strict inequality for at least one type.

Proof. Suppose a measurable fractional assignment $w$ is a continuum Pareto improvement. Set
$$ Y_{tg}=\int_{I_t}w_g\,d\lambda. $$
Integration gives the supply equalities and weak utility inequalities. The utility difference is a nonnegative integrable function almost everywhere. If it is positive on a set of positive measure, its integral is positive. Because there are finitely many types, at least one type inequality is strict.

Conversely, suppose such a matrix $Y$ exists. Write
$$ u(x)=t(x)\cdot z(x), \qquad V_t=t\cdot Y_t. $$
For a type with $U_t>0$, define, on $I_t$,
$$ w_g(x)=\frac{u(x)}{U_t}Y_{tg}. $$
Then $\int_{I_t}w_g\,d\lambda=Y_{tg}$, and its utility is
$$ t\cdot w(x)=\frac{V_t}{U_t}u(x)\geq u(x). $$
If $V_t>U_t$, this improvement is strict wherever $u(x)>0$, a set of positive measure since $\int_{I_t}u\,d\lambda=U_t>0$.

For a type with $U_t=0$, nonnegativity implies $u(x)=0$ almost everywhere on $I_t$. Define
$$ w_g(x)=\frac{Y_{tg}}{\mu_t}. $$
Its aggregate allocation is $Y_t$, and its utility is $V_t/\mu_t$. Thus it weakly improves utility and strictly improves it throughout that type when $V_t>0$.

These definitions produce a measurable fractional assignment with the required supplies and a strict improvement on a set of positive measure. $\square$

2. An exact graph characterization of fPO

Set
$$ c=\frac ba>1, \qquad \varepsilon_{tg}= \begin{cases} 0,&t_g=a,\\ 1,&t_g=b. \end{cases} $$
Thus $t_g=a c^{\varepsilon_{tg}}$.

Given a feasible aggregate matrix $A$, construct a directed multigraph with vertex set $T$. For every incidence $(t,g)$ with $A_{tg}>0$, and every type $s$, insert an edge
$$ t\longrightarrow s $$
labelled by $g$, with weight
$$ d(t,s;g)=\varepsilon_{tg}-\varepsilon_{sg}\in\{-1,0,1\}. $$

Theorem 2. A feasible realization with aggregate matrix $A$ is continuum fPO if and only if this graph has no negative directed cycle.

Moreover, when it is fPO, there are integers
$$ h_t\in\{0,\ldots,\tau-1\} $$
such that the rational weights and prices
$$ \beta_t=c^{h_t}, \qquad p_g=\max_{s\in T}\beta_s s_g $$
satisfy
$$ p_g\geq\beta_t t_g $$
for every $t,g$, with equality whenever $A_{tg}>0$.

2.1 A negative cycle produces a Pareto improvement

Suppose there is a negative directed cycle. We may choose one with distinct vertices: a negative closed walk with a repeated vertex splits into shorter closed walks, at least one of which is negative.

Write the cycle as
$$ t_1\longrightarrow t_2\longrightarrow\cdots \longrightarrow t_k\longrightarrow t_1, $$
and let $g_j$ label its $j$-th edge. Indices are cyclic. In particular,
$$ A_{t_jg_j}>0, \qquad \sum_{j=1}^k d(t_j,t_{j+1};g_j)<0. $$

Transfer an amount $q_j>0$ of kind $g_j$ from type $t_j$ to type $t_{j+1}$. Starting with $q_1>0$, choose
$$ q_j= \frac{t_j(g_{j-1})}{t_j(g_j)}q_{j-1}, \qquad 2\leq j\leq k. $$
Every type $t_j$ with $j\geq2$ then has zero net utility change:
$$ t_j(g_{j-1})q_{j-1}-t_j(g_j)q_j=0. $$

For type $t_1$, the ratio of gained utility to lost utility is
$$ \frac{t_1(g_k)q_k}{t_1(g_1)q_1} = \prod_{j=1}^k \frac{t_{j+1}(g_j)}{t_j(g_j)} = c^{-\sum_{j=1}^k d(t_j,t_{j+1};g_j)} >1. $$
Its utility therefore strictly increases.

All $q_j$ are fixed positive multiples of $q_1$. Choose $q_1$ sufficiently small that
$$ q_j\leq A_{t_jg_j} $$
for every $j$. The resulting aggregate matrix is nonnegative and preserves every supply. Types outside the cycle are unchanged.

It is consequently an aggregate Pareto improvement. Lemma 1 converts it into a continuum Pareto improvement.

2.2 Absence of negative cycles produces supporting prices

Now suppose there is no negative directed cycle. Add a source vertex with a zero-weight edge to every type, and let $\ell_t$ be the shortest-path distance from the source to $t$.

These distances are finite. A shortest path can be chosen without repeated vertices, because every directed cycle has nonnegative weight. Such a path has at most $\tau-1$ edges between types. Since those edges have weights in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and there is a direct zero-weight source edge,
$$ -(\tau-1)\leq\ell_t\leq0. $$
Every original edge satisfies
$$ \ell_s\leq\ell_t+d(t,s;g). $$

Set
$$ h_t=\ell_t-\min_{r\in T}\ell_r. $$
Then $h_t$ is an integer in $\{0,\ldots,\tau-1\}$. For every edge arising from $A_{tg}>0$,
$$ h_s-h_t\leq\varepsilon_{tg}-\varepsilon_{sg}. $$
Exponentiating with base $c$ gives
$$ c^{h_s}s_g\leq c^{h_t}t_g. $$
Thus, with $\beta_t=c^{h_t}$ and $p_g=\max_s\beta_s s_g$,
$$ p_g=\beta_t t_g \qquad\text{whenever }A_{tg}>0. $$

For any feasible fractional assignment $w$,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_t\beta_t\int_{I_t}t\cdot w\,d\lambda &\leq \sum_gp_g\rho_g\\ &=\sum_{t,g}\beta_t t_gA_{tg}\\ &=\sum_t\beta_tU_t. \end{aligned} $$
A continuum Pareto improvement would make the left side strictly larger than the last expression: every $\beta_t$ is positive, and a nonnegative integrable utility difference that is positive on a set of positive measure has positive integral. This is impossible. $\square$

2.3 Algorithmic consequences

The graph has $\tau$ vertices and at most $m\tau^2$ edges. Negative-cycle detection and shortest paths therefore take polynomial time.

For an encoded realization, determining whether $A_{tg}>0$ requires only checking whether some positive-length cell of type $t$ contains kind $g$.

The supporting weights and prices have polynomial binary length. Indeed, each exponent $h_t$ is at most $\tau-1$, and
$$ p_g\in\{a c^j:0\leq j\leq\tau\}. $$
Hence fPO admits a rational certificate using at most $\tau+1$ geometric price levels.

This characterizes and verifies fPO, but it does not find bundle patterns that simultaneously satisfy EFX and expected EF.

3. A polynomial-time construction when total supply is small

Write
$$ R=\sum_g\rho_g. $$

Theorem 3. If $R\leq a/b$, the requested construction is deterministically polynomial-time solvable in $m,\tau,L$. It produces one realization with at most $\tau(m+1)$ interval cells, each carrying either the empty bundle or one item.

If $R=0$, assign the empty bundle everywhere. Feasibility forces every nonnegative fractional alternative to be zero almost everywhere, so all required properties hold.

Assume henceforth that $R>0$, and let
$$ G_+=\{g:\rho_g>0\}. $$
Kinds outside $G_+$ receive zero allocation.

3.1 Computing a rational Fisher equilibrium

Consider a linear Fisher market with one buyer per supported type:

  • buyer $t$ has budget $\mu_t$;
  • kind $g\in G_+$ has divisible supply $\rho_g$;
  • buyer $t$ values one unit of kind $g$ at $t_g$.

We use the established algorithmic theorem that a linear Fisher market with positive rational budgets and rational utilities admits an exact rational equilibrium computable in polynomial time. [Orlin’s algorithm](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol10171/DagSemProc.10171.2/DagSemProc.10171.2.pdf) provides such a computation.

Here the hypotheses hold: budgets are positive, and every buyer values every retained kind positively. To match the unit-supply formulation, replace kind $g$ by one unit whose value to buyer $t$ is $t_g\rho_g$. Clearing denominators of these coefficients and the budgets gives integer data of polynomial binary length. Rescaling the resulting equilibrium recovers prices per physical unit and allocations in the original quantities.

Let $p_g>0$ and $A_{tg}\geq0$ be the resulting prices and allocations. They satisfy
$$ \sum_tA_{tg}=\rho_g, \qquad \sum_gp_gA_{tg}=\mu_t. $$
For
$$ \alpha_t=\max_{g\in G_+}\frac{t_g}{p_g}, $$
equilibrium optimality gives
$$ A_{tg}>0\ \Longrightarrow\ t_g=\alpha_t p_g. $$

Define the per-person mean bundle
$$ x_t=\frac{A_t}{\mu_t}. $$
Then
$$ p\cdot x_t=1, \qquad t\cdot x_t=\alpha_t. $$
For every pair of types $t,s$,
$$ t\cdot x_s \leq\alpha_t p\cdot x_s =\alpha_t =t\cdot x_t. $$
Thus these means satisfy exactly the required type-level EF inequalities.

3.2 Bounding each type’s mean item count

Let
$$ p_{\min}=\min_{g\in G_+}p_g, \qquad p_{\max}=\max_{g\in G_+}p_g. $$
Choose a kind $h$ with price $p_{\max}$. Since $\rho_h>0$, some type $t$ receives a positive amount of it. For a kind $k$ with price $p_{\min}$, equilibrium optimality implies
$$ \frac{t_h}{p_{\max}}\geq\frac{t_k}{p_{\min}}. $$
Consequently,
$$ \frac{p_{\max}}{p_{\min}} \leq\frac{t_h}{t_k} \leq\frac ba=c. $$

The budgets sum to one, so
$$ 1=\sum_gp_g\rho_g \leq p_{\max}R \leq c p_{\min}R. $$
Therefore
$$ p_{\min}\geq\frac1{cR}. $$
For every type,
$$ \sum_gx_{tg} \leq\frac{p\cdot x_t}{p_{\min}} =\frac1{p_{\min}} \leq cR \leq1. $$
Equivalently,
$$ \sum_gA_{tg}\leq\mu_t. $$

3.3 Implementing the means with singleton bundles

Inside $I_t$, assign a cell of length $A_{tg}$ the bundle $e_g$, for each positive $A_{tg}$. Assign the empty bundle to the remaining length
$$ \mu_t-\sum_gA_{tg}\geq0. $$
All endpoints are rational.

The resulting realization has aggregate matrix $A$, so it is feasible and its type-level means are $x_t$. The preceding calculation proves EF.

Every bundle is empty or a singleton. Removing any present item from another person’s bundle leaves the empty bundle. Since utilities are nonnegative, EFX holds.

Finally, set $\beta_t=1/\alpha_t>0$. Then
$$ \beta_t t_g\leq p_g, $$
with equality on every positive allocation incidence. The weighted-utility argument in Section 2.2 proves continuum fPO.

The construction uses at most $\tau(m+1)$ cells. All rational data have polynomial binary length, and the equilibrium computation and interval construction take polynomial time. A lottery concentrated on this realization is the requested output. $\square$

4. A computable extension beyond the small-supply regime

The numerical condition $R\leq a/b$ is sufficient, but the construction can apply more broadly.

Compute a Fisher equilibrium as above and retain its prices $p$ and values $\alpha_t$. Solve the following rational linear feasibility problem for $Y$:
$$ Y_{tg}\geq0, \qquad \sum_tY_{tg}=\rho_g, $$
$$ \sum_gp_gY_{tg}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_gY_{tg}\leq\mu_t, $$
and
$$ Y_{tg}=0 \qquad\text{whenever }t_g<\alpha_t p_g. $$

This system has polynomial size. If it is feasible, implement $Y$ using singleton and empty bundles.

The spending equalities and allocation only on maximum-ratio incidences give
$$ t\cdot\frac{Y_t}{\mu_t}=\alpha_t, \qquad t\cdot\frac{Y_s}{\mu_s}\leq\alpha_t. $$
Hence EF holds. Singleton bundles give EFX, and weights $\beta_t=1/\alpha_t$ give fPO.

Thus this is a polynomial-time sufficient test for a deterministic solution. Its infeasibility is not a certificate that the original construction problem is difficult or infeasible.

5. Why the Fisher implementation does not settle larger supplies

Consider
$$ a=1,\qquad b=2, $$
with two types
$$ t^0=(1,1),\qquad t^1=(2,1), $$
masses
$$ \mu_{t^0}=\frac14,\qquad \mu_{t^1}=\frac34, $$
and supplies
$$ \rho_1=\rho_2=\frac38. $$
Here $R=3/4>a/b$.

5.1 Every Fisher equilibrium violates the singleton capacity

Let $p_1,p_2$ be equilibrium prices.

If $p_1<p_2$, both types strictly prefer kind $1$ per unit of money, so kind $2$ cannot clear.

If $p_1=p_2$, type $t^1$ buys only kind $1$. Its budget forces
$$ p_1\frac38=\frac34, $$
so $p_1=p_2=2$. Clearing kind $2$ would then require expenditure $3/4$ from type $t^0$, whose budget is only $1/4$. This is impossible.

Thus $p_1>p_2$, and type $t^0$ buys only kind $2$.

If $p_1<2p_2$, type $t^1$ buys only kind $1$. Clearing both kinds then gives
$$ p_1=2,\qquad p_2=\frac23, $$
contradicting $p_1<2p_2$.

If $p_1>2p_2$, both types buy only kind $2$, so kind $1$ cannot clear.

Consequently,
$$ p_1=2p_2. $$
Total expenditure is one, whence
$$ \frac38(p_1+p_2)=1, \qquad p_2=\frac89,\qquad p_1=\frac{16}{9}. $$
Type $t^0$ must therefore receive
$$ A_{t^0,2}=\frac{1/4}{8/9}=\frac9{32}>\frac14=\mu_{t^0}. $$
Its mean item count is $9/8$. The linear feasibility test in Section 4 is therefore infeasible at every equilibrium.

5.2 Nevertheless, a deterministic target solution exists

Assign the entire type-$t^0$ interval one item of kind $2$ per person. Within the type-$t^1$ interval, use cells of lengths
$$ \frac38,\qquad\frac18,\qquad\frac14 $$
carrying, respectively,
$$ e_1,\qquad e_2,\qquad0. $$
These cell lengths sum to $3/4$. Kind $1$ has total allocation $3/8$, and kind $2$ has total allocation
$$ \frac14+\frac18=\frac38. $$

The type-level means are
$$ \bar z_{t^0}=(0,1), \qquad \bar z_{t^1}=\left(\frac12,\frac16\right). $$
The two nontrivial EF inequalities are
$$ 1\geq\frac12+\frac16=\frac23 $$
and
$$ 2\cdot\frac12+\frac16=\frac76\geq1. $$
EFX holds because all bundles are empty or singletons.

Prices $(2,1)$ and weights $\beta_{t^0}=\beta_{t^1}=1$ satisfy the supporting inequalities, with equality on all positive allocation incidences. Hence the realization is fPO.

This example identifies the limitation precisely: insisting on the Fisher equilibrium’s equal per-person expenditure can prevent a singleton implementation even when the required deterministic EF, EFX, and fPO realization exists.

The graph characterization removes linear programming from fPO verification and gives explicit rational supporting certificates. The small-supply theorem resolves a regime with unrestricted numbers of kinds and supported types.

Neither result supplies a general method for finding compatible EFX bundle patterns and lottery probabilities when $R>a/b$. No hardness reduction is established here.
