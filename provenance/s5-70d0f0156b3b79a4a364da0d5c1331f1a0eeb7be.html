Lemma A3 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of voter types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), a prior \(\pi\) over states \(\theta\), type-specific signal laws \(P_t(s\mid\theta)\), and fixed strategies \(\sigma_t\) mapping signals to vote, oppose, or abstain, compute the probability that the mass majority selects the true state in an atomless society, under a specified tie rule.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list of \(\tau\) voter types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=2\) states and voting alternatives, blue and green. The input gives a positive binary-rational prior \(\pi\), conditional signal tables \(P_t(s\mid\theta)\) on these two colors, and deterministic strategies \(\sigma_t(s)\in\{\text{vote},\text{oppose},\text{abstain}\}\). Each conditional table sums to \(1\), and \(P_t(\theta\mid\theta)>1/2\); perfect signals are allowed.
Voting supports the signal's color, opposing supports the other color, and abstaining contributes no vote. The electorate is atomless, partitioned into measurable type classes of masses \(\mu_t\).
Conditional on the state, signals have the specified type-dependent laws and are independent for almost every pair of voters. Assume a measurable signal process on an extended voter–outcome product probability space where both iterated integrals exist and equal the joint integral for every integrable function.
The color receiving strictly greater voting mass is selected; a tie, including universal abstention, selects neither color and counts as failure. Compute the exact probability of selecting the true state. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Can this exact probability be computed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is its computation NP-hard?

The finite-electorate analogue has an explicit success-probability formula combining multinomial action counts, hypergeometric signals from sampling without replacement, and random exclusion of an expert; no complexity classification is asserted. [Schlangenotto–Schnedler–Vadovič](https://doi.org/10.3390/g11030031)
Conditional exact laws of large numbers are known for continuum signal processes on Fubini extensions; this is a probabilistic result rather than a complexity classification of this voting task. [Qiao–Sun–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.1147)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted states and signals to blue and green, following the discrete model, so the number of alternatives is m=2.
  • Normalized positive type masses to total mass one and treated them as describing the participating electorate.
  • Made the prior and signal tables explicit binary-rational inputs, with both states having positive prior probability and signals informative conditional on either state; perfect signals remain admissible.
  • Interpreted the graded question's fixed signal-to-action maps as deterministic strategies, although the discrete formula also permits randomized behavior.
  • Specified that opposing means voting for the other signal color and that abstentions contribute no voting mass.
  • Fixed the unspecified tie rule to failure, including universal abstention, matching the discrete success condition of a strictly positive correct-state vote margin.
  • Marginal signal laws do not specify dependence. Chose conditional pairwise independence with a Fubini extension as the continuum convention; this replaces finite sampling-without-replacement dependence.
  • Absorbed participation and expert composition into the supplied masses rather than retaining a randomly excluded finite voter, which has no positive-mass counterpart in an atomless electorate.

Result

The exact success probability is polynomial-time computable. For the specified model with \(m=2\), an algorithm uses \(O(m\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations and \(O(m\tau L^2)=O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations under ordinary binary encoding of rational numbers.

The key fact is that, conditional on either state, the aggregate vote margin is deterministic almost surely. We prove this directly from the stated assumptions.

1. The expected vote margin

Write the colors as \(b\) and \(g\). Let \(\pi_b\) and \(\pi_g\) denote their prior probabilities. If the input supplies a single prior \(\pi\) for blue, then \(\pi_b=\pi\) and \(\pi_g=1-\pi\).

Represent each voter’s contribution to the blue-minus-green vote margin by \(1\) for a blue vote, \(-1\) for a green vote, and \(0\) for abstention.

Set \(r_b=1\) and \(r_g=-1\). For type \(t\) receiving signal \(s\), its deterministic contribution is

\( c_t(s)=r_s\bigl(\mathbf{1}\{\sigma_t(s)=\text{vote}\}-\mathbf{1}\{\sigma_t(s)=\text{oppose}\}\bigr). \)

Thus \(c_t(s)\in\{-1,0,1\}\), with opposing correctly reversing the signal’s color.

For state \(\theta\in\{b,g\}\), define the expected contribution of a type-\(t\) voter and the expected aggregate margin by

\( e_{t,\theta}=\sum_{s\in\{b,g\}}P_t(s\mid\theta)c_t(s), \qquad D_\theta=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t e_{t,\theta}. \)

Both quantities are rational numbers computable directly from the input.

2. Exact aggregation: a self-contained proof

Let \((I,\mathcal I,\lambda)\) be the voter probability space. Let its measurable type classes be \(I_1,\ldots,I_\tau\), with \(\lambda(I_t)=\mu_t\). Let \(\Theta\) be the random state, and let \(X(i,\omega)\in\{-1,0,1\}\) be voter \(i\)’s realized contribution.

The process \(X\) is measurable on the given extension: it is obtained from the measurable signal process using finitely many measurable type classes and deterministic strategies. It is bounded, hence integrable.

Fix a state \(\theta\). Because \(\pi_\theta>0\), conditional probability on \(\{\Theta=\theta\}\) is well-defined. Write \(\mathbb E_\theta\) for expectation under this probability.

For every bounded measurable process \(H\), the assumed Fubini property gives

\( \mathbb E_\theta\!\left[\int_I H(i,\omega)\,d\lambda(i)\right] = \int_I\mathbb E_\theta[H(i,\omega)]\,d\lambda(i). \)

Indeed, apply the original Fubini property to \(H(i,\omega)\mathbf{1}\{\Theta(\omega)=\theta\}\) and divide by \(\pi_\theta\). This also supplies the required measurable sections and aggregate integrals, up to null sets.

For \(i\in I_t\), define

\( Z(i,\omega)=X(i,\omega)-e_{t,\theta}, \qquad A(\omega)=\int_I Z(i,\omega)\,d\lambda(i). \)

Choose measurable versions on exceptional null sets. We have \(|Z|\leq 2\), \(|A|\leq 2\), and \(\mathbb E_\theta[Z_i]=0\) for almost every voter \(i\).

Conditional pairwise independence of signals implies conditional pairwise independence of their deterministic vote contributions. Consequently, for almost every \(i\) and almost every \(j\),

\( \mathbb E_\theta[Z_iZ_j] = \mathbb E_\theta[Z_i]\mathbb E_\theta[Z_j] = 0. \)

Now fix such a voter \(i\). The process \((j,\omega)\mapsto Z_i(\omega)Z(j,\omega)\) is measurable on the extension: the first factor is an outcome-measurable random variable and the second is an extension-measurable process. It is bounded. Applying conditional Fubini yields

\( \mathbb E_\theta[Z_iA] = \mathbb E_\theta\!\left[\int_I Z_iZ_j\,d\lambda(j)\right] = \int_I\mathbb E_\theta[Z_iZ_j]\,d\lambda(j) = 0. \)

Apply conditional Fubini again, this time to the bounded measurable process \((i,\omega)\mapsto A(\omega)Z(i,\omega)\). Since \(A=\int_I Z_i\,d\lambda(i)\),

\( \mathbb E_\theta[A^2] = \mathbb E_\theta\!\left[\int_I AZ_i\,d\lambda(i)\right] = \int_I\mathbb E_\theta[AZ_i]\,d\lambda(i) = 0. \)

A nonnegative random variable with expectation zero vanishes almost surely. Therefore \(A=0\) almost surely conditional on \(\Theta=\theta\). It follows that

\( \int_I X(i,\omega)\,d\lambda(i) = \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\lambda(I_t)e_{t,\theta} = D_\theta \)

almost surely conditional on that state.

This argument uses the voter–outcome Fubini property twice; it does not assume a Fubini extension involving two voter coordinates. The resulting identity is also a special case of the conditional exact law of large numbers in [Qiao–Sun–Zhang, Corollary 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.1147).

3. Exact success probability

The realized blue-minus-green margin is almost surely \(D_b\) in the blue state and \(D_g\) in the green state.

Therefore blue is selected in the blue state exactly when \(D_b>0\), and green is selected in the green state exactly when \(D_g<0\). A zero margin is a tie and contributes no success probability.

Hence the exact answer is

\( p_{\mathrm{success}} = \pi_b\,\mathbf{1}\{D_b>0\} + \pi_g\,\mathbf{1}\{D_g<0\}. \)

In particular, the output belongs to \(\{0,\pi_b,\pi_g,1\}\). Universal abstention gives both margins zero and thus success probability zero.

4. Exact algorithm and bit complexity

The algorithm computes \(c_t(s)\), evaluates \(D_b\) and \(D_g\) exactly, tests their signs, and returns the formula above.

To establish a bit-complexity bound, write the input masses and signal probabilities as

\( \mu_t=\frac{a_t}{b_t}, \qquad P_t(s\mid\theta)=\frac{u_{t,s,\theta}}{v_{t,s,\theta}}, \)

with positive denominators. Define the common denominator

\( Q= \left(\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t\right) \left(\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}\prod_{\theta\in\{b,g\}}\prod_{s\in\{b,g\}}v_{t,s,\theta}\right). \)

Its binary length is \(O(L)\), because the total length of all input denominators is at most \(L\). Each \(b_tv_{t,s,\theta}\) divides \(Q\), so

\( N_\theta = \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{s\in\{b,g\}} c_t(s)a_tu_{t,s,\theta} \frac{Q}{b_tv_{t,s,\theta}} \)

is an integer satisfying \(D_\theta=N_\theta/Q\). Since \(Q>0\), the signs of \(D_\theta\) and \(N_\theta\) agree.

All these calculations involve \(O(L)\)-bit integers. In particular, the sum of the absolute values of the summands for a fixed state is at most

\( Q\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\sum_{s\in\{b,g\}}P_t(s\mid\theta)=Q, \)

so intermediate partial sums also have \(O(L)\) bits.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic:

  • Constructing \(Q\) takes \(O(L^2)\) bit operations. Sequential multiplication has total cost bounded by the square of the total factor length.
  • Each summand requires a constant number of multiplications and one exact division, costing \(O(L^2)\) bit operations.
  • There are \(2m\tau\) summands, with \(m=2\).

The total running time is therefore \(O(m\tau L^2)\), polynomial in the requested parameters. No enumeration of possible voter types or signal realizations is required.

The signal-accuracy inequalities and allowance of perfect signals require no additional cases: the proof uses only the specified conditional laws, conditional pairwise independence, and the Fubini property.
