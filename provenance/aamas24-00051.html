The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite left and right type sets \(A\) and \(B\), rational masses \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\), and contract types \(e\in\mathcal E\) with endpoints \(a(e),b(e)\), preference values \(p_a(e),p_b(e)\), unmatched values, and thresholds \(0<\gamma_{v,e}<\delta_{v,e}\), find a mass matching \(x\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{e:a(e)=a}x_e\le\mu_a\) and \(\sum_{e:b(e)=b}x_e\le\nu_b\), maximizing \(\sum_e x_e\), subject to no contract \(e=(a,b)\) having positive masses on statuses \(f,g\in\mathcal E\cup\{\varnothing\}\) such that either \(p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\gamma_{a,e}\) and \(p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\delta_{b,e}\), or \(p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\delta_{a,e}\) and \(p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\gamma_{b,e}\). The approximation version asks for a \(c\gamma\)-stable matching with matched mass at least \(\frac{2}{3}\) of the optimum.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: disjoint finite left and right type sets \(A,B\), with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_a,\nu_b\) summing jointly to \(1\). Let \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) be the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m=|\mathcal E|\) and \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each listed acceptable contract \(e\in\mathcal E\) has endpoints \(a(e)\in A,b(e)\in B\); parallel contracts are allowed. Endpoint values \(p_v(e)\ge0\), unmatched values \(p_v(\varnothing)\le0\), and thresholds \(0<\gamma_{v,e}<\delta_{v,e}\) are finite binary rationals. Preferences may have ties; no tie-breaking enters stability. There are no critical types or critical contracts.
Write \(q_a=\mu_a,q_b=\nu_b\) and let \(\mathcal E(v)\) denote incident contracts. A mass matching is \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\mathcal E}\) with \(\sum_{e\in\mathcal E(v)}x_e\le q_v\). Status masses are \(s_v(f)=x_f\) for \(f\in\mathcal E(v)\) and \(s_v(\varnothing)=q_v-\sum_{f\in\mathcal E(v)}x_f\).
Call \(x\) \(c\gamma\)-stable if no contract \(e=(a,b)\) has statuses \(f\in\mathcal E(a)\cup\{\varnothing\}\), \(g\in\mathcal E(b)\cup\{\varnothing\}\) with \(s_a(f)>0,s_b(g)>0\) satisfying either \(p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\gamma_{a,e}\) and \(p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\delta_{b,e}\), or \(p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\delta_{a,e}\) and \(p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\gamma_{b,e}\).
Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the maximum of \(\sum_e x_e\) over such stable mass matchings.

Does this problem admit an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) returning a rational \(c\gamma\)-stable mass matching of mass at least \(\frac23\mathrm{OPT}\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?

The discrete maximum-size \(c\gamma\)-stable matching problem, including critical vertices and contracts, admits a polynomial-time \(\frac32\)-approximation. [Discrete result](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p409.pdf)
For unit vertex capacities, the corresponding threshold-stable fractional matching problem admits a polynomial-time \(\frac32\)-approximation even on arbitrary graphs; this covers equal type masses after normalization. No result settling the stated arbitrary-rational-mass problem was located. [Fractional result](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16173v2)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Mass normalization was unspecified; normalize the combined left and right society to total mass one, allowing unequal totals on the two sides.
  • The graded question supplies no critical sets and explicitly forbids every threshold-blocking status pair; interpret it as the specialization with empty critical-type and critical-contract sets.
  • Preference domains were unspecified; use nonnegative acceptable-contract values and nonpositive unmatched values, as in the discrete definition, with ties allowed.
  • For finite input encoding, require all preference values and thresholds to be finite binary rationals.
  • Positive status masses were undefined; define contract-status masses by incident matching coordinates and unmatched-status masses by residual capacity, restricting statuses to incident contracts.
  • Define m as the number of listed contract types, tau as the combined support size, and L as total input bit length; require a rational output while allowing real feasible mass matchings.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

A polynomial-time algorithm exists

The arbitrary-rational-mass problem admits the requested approximation: one can compute a rational \(c\gamma\)-stable mass matching \(x\), in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), satisfying

\( \displaystyle \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}x_e\ge \frac23\mathrm{OPT}. \)

The algorithm combines the four-copy preference construction with a polynomial-time stable-allocation algorithm. Capacity expansion is used only in the approximation proof.

1. The stable-allocation primitive

For a bipartite contract graph with strict preferences, vertex quotas, and edge upper bounds, an allocation \(y\) is ordinarily stable if there is no edge \(h\) below its upper bound such that both endpoints either have unused quota or have positive allocation on an edge ranked below \(h\).

We use the established theorem that such a stable allocation can be found in strongly polynomial time. The augmenting-path algorithms preserve integrality when quotas and upper bounds are integers. See [Baïou–Balinski’s corrected paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302) and [Dean–Munshi, Section 3](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

These algorithms also handle parallel contracts: preference pointers and residual capacities are attached to individual edges. Path and cycle augmentations remain defined on the resulting multigraph, including two-edge cycles.

Give every edge upper bound \(1\). If \(m>0\), both sides are nonempty, and positivity and joint normalization imply \(q_v<1\) for every vertex. Consequently every feasible edge allocation is strictly below its upper bound. Thus ordinary stability becomes precisely the absence of an edge strictly preferred to positive-mass statuses at both endpoints, with unused quota treated as the worst status.

2. The algorithm

If \(m=0\), return the empty matching. Henceforth assume \(m>0\).

Replace each original contract \(e\) by four parallel contracts

\( \alpha(e),\ \beta_0(e),\ \beta_1(e),\ \chi(e). \)

Keep the original vertex quotas.

The following is the no-critical-types specialization of [Csáji’s four-copy construction](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p409.pdf), expressed as explicit sorting rules.

At a left type \(a\), rank the copies of types \(\alpha,\beta_0,\beta_1\) by decreasing score:

| Copy | Score at \(a\) |
|---|---|
| \(\alpha(e)\) | \(p_a(e)\) |
| \(\beta_0(e)\) | \(p_a(e)-\gamma_{a,e}\) |
| \(\beta_1(e)\) | \(p_a(e)-\delta_{a,e}\) |

At equal scores, prioritize \(\beta_1\), then \(\beta_0\), then \(\alpha\). Resolve remaining ties arbitrarily. Place all \(\chi\)-copies below these copies, ordered by decreasing original value.

At a right type \(b\), rank the copies of types \(\chi,\beta_1,\beta_0\) by decreasing score:

| Copy | Score at \(b\) |
|---|---|
| \(\chi(e)\) | \(p_b(e)\) |
| \(\beta_1(e)\) | \(p_b(e)-\gamma_{b,e}\) |
| \(\beta_0(e)\) | \(p_b(e)-\delta_{b,e}\) |

At equal scores, prioritize \(\beta_0\), then \(\beta_1\), then \(\chi\). Resolve remaining ties arbitrarily. Place all \(\alpha\)-copies below these copies, ordered by decreasing original value.

Every copy is acceptable and ranks above unused quota. These auxiliary strict preferences are computational devices; the returned matching is evaluated using the original thresholds without tie-breaking.

Compute an ordinarily stable allocation \(y\) in this expanded instance. Return its projection

\( \displaystyle x_e=y_{\alpha(e)}+y_{\beta_0(e)}+y_{\beta_1(e)}+y_{\chi(e)}. \)

Summing at each endpoint shows that \(x\) satisfies the original quota constraints.

The sorting rules give the following exact equivalences for incident original contracts:

\( \beta_0(e)\succ_a\alpha(f) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\gamma_{a,e}, \)

\( \beta_1(e)\succ_a\alpha(f) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\delta_{a,e}, \)

\( \beta_0(e)\succ_b\chi(g) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\delta_{b,e}, \)

\( \beta_1(e)\succ_b\chi(g) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\gamma_{b,e}. \)

The specified priorities at equal scores account for the non-strict threshold inequalities.

3. The projection is \(c\gamma\)-stable

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that \(e=(a,b)\) blocks \(x\) through positive-mass statuses \(f,g\).

First suppose

\( p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\gamma_{a,e}, \qquad p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\delta_{b,e}. \)

If \(f\ne\varnothing\), then at least one copy \(h\) of \(f\) has \(y_h>0\). At \(a\), the scores of the \(\beta\)-copies of \(f\) are strictly below \(p_a(f)\), while \(\chi(f)\) lies in the bottom group. The left inequality and the equality priorities therefore imply

\( \beta_0(e)\succ_a h. \)

If \(f=\varnothing\), the unused quota at \(a\) is positive in \(y\), and \(\beta_0(e)\) ranks above that status.

Likewise, if \(g\ne\varnothing\), choose a positive-allocation copy \(k\) of \(g\). Its score in the right upper group is at most \(p_b(g)\), while an \(\alpha\)-copy lies in the bottom group. The right inequality gives

\( \beta_0(e)\succ_b k. \)

If \(g=\varnothing\), \(b\) instead has positive unused quota.

Thus \(\beta_0(e)\) ordinarily blocks \(y\), a contradiction.

For the other permitted blocking pattern,

\( p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\delta_{a,e}, \qquad p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\gamma_{b,e}, \)

the same argument uses \(\beta_1(e)\). Hence \(x\) is \(c\gamma\)-stable.

4. A support-preserving integrality lemma

We next justify using integral comparison matchings even though \(\mathrm{OPT}\) ranges over real allocations.

Lemma. Suppose all quotas \(Q_v\) are integers. For every real \(c\gamma\)-stable allocation \(z\), there is an integral \(c\gamma\)-stable allocation \(w\) such that

\( \displaystyle \sum_e w_e\ge\sum_e z_e. \)

Moreover, \(w_e>0\) implies \(z_e>0\), and every vertex saturated by \(z\) remains saturated by \(w\).

Proof. Consider the polytope defined by

\( w_e\ge0,\qquad w_e=0\ \text{whenever }z_e=0, \)

\( \displaystyle \sum_{e\in\mathcal E(v)}w_e\le Q_v, \)

and, at every vertex saturated by \(z\),

\( \displaystyle \sum_{e\in\mathcal E(v)}w_e=Q_v. \)

It is nonempty because it contains \(z\), and it is bounded.

The vertex-edge incidence matrix of a bipartite multigraph is totally unimodular: negating the rows on one side gives a directed incidence matrix. Deleting columns, imposing selected quota equalities, and adding nonnegativity preserve the integrality conclusion. Since the right-hand sides are integers, this polytope has integral vertices.

Maximizing \(\sum_e w_e\) therefore yields an integral optimum of value at least \(\sum_e z_e\).

Every positive contract status of \(w\) was positive in \(z\). Also, if \(w\) has positive unmatched mass at a vertex, then \(z\) had positive unmatched mass there, because all vertices saturated by \(z\) were kept saturated. Thus every pair of positive statuses that could witness a block of \(w\) would already witness the same block of \(z\). Stability of \(z\) rules this out. \(\square\)

5. The approximation bound

Choose a positive integer \(D\) clearing the denominators of the original quotas. Run the stable-allocation primitive on integer quotas \(Q_v=Dq_v\) and edge bounds \(D\), and divide its integral output by \(D\). Write

\( Y_h=Dy_h,\qquad X_e=Dx_e. \)

These quantities are integers.

Let \(z\) be any original stable mass matching. Scaling masses by \(D\) preserves which statuses have positive mass and hence preserves stability. By the lemma, there is an integral stable allocation \(W\), with quotas \(Q_v\), satisfying

\( \displaystyle \sum_e W_e\ge D\sum_e z_e. \)

For the following argument only, replace type \(v\) by \(Q_v\) individual copies.

Realize \(Y\) as an ordinary matching \(M\) on these individuals: for each expanded contract \(h\), connect \(Y_h\) distinct pairs of endpoint individuals, respecting all quotas. Record the label \(h\) on each edge.

Independently realize \(W\) as a matching \(N\) on the same individual sets, recording the original contract labels. Both realizations exist because their incident totals do not exceed the numbers of individuals.

Their sizes are

\( \displaystyle |M|=D\sum_e x_e,\qquad |N|=\sum_e W_e. \)

Keep the two edge colors distinct, even if their endpoints coincide. Their union has degree at most two, and its nontrivial components are alternating paths or alternating cycles.

We show that an alternating path with one more \(N\)-edge than \(M\)-edge cannot have length one or three.

5.1. A one-edge path is impossible

Such a path consists of an \(N\)-edge labeled \(f=(a,b)\), with both endpoint individuals unmatched in \(M\).

Therefore both types \(a,b\) have positive unused quota in \(Y\). Any expanded copy of \(f\) would ordinarily block \(Y\), contradicting its stability.

5.2. A three-edge path is impossible

Write such a path as

\( b'\ \xleftrightarrow{\ N:f\ }\ a\ \xleftrightarrow{\ M:h\ }\ b\ \xleftrightarrow{\ N:g\ }\ a'. \)

Here \(h\) is a copy of an original contract \(e=(a,b)\). The endpoint individuals of types \(b'\) and \(a'\) are unmatched in \(M\), so those types have positive unused quota in \(Y\). Also,

\( Y_h>0,\qquad W_f>0,\qquad W_g>0. \)

Since \(\alpha(f)\) does not ordinarily block \(Y\), the positive status \(h\) at \(a\) cannot rank below \(\alpha(f)\). Strictness of the auxiliary order gives either \(h=\alpha(f)\) or

\( h\succ_a\alpha(f). \)

Similarly, nonblocking of \(\chi(g)\) gives either \(h=\chi(g)\) or

\( h\succ_b\chi(g). \)

Every \(\chi\)-copy ranks below every \(\alpha\)-copy at a left type. Thus \(h\) cannot be a \(\chi\)-copy. Every \(\alpha\)-copy ranks below every \(\chi\)-copy at a right type. Thus \(h\) cannot be an \(\alpha\)-copy.

Consequently \(h\) is either \(\beta_0(e)\) or \(\beta_1(e)\). Neither equality alternative above is now possible.

If \(h=\beta_0(e)\), the exact ranking equivalences give

\( p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\gamma_{a,e}, \qquad p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\delta_{b,e}. \)

Since \(W_f,W_g>0\), these inequalities make \(e\) block \(W\).

If \(h=\beta_1(e)\), they give

\( p_a(e)-p_a(f)\ge\delta_{a,e}, \qquad p_b(e)-p_b(g)\ge\gamma_{b,e}, \)

again making \(e\) block \(W\).

Both conclusions contradict stability of \(W\).

The argument permits repeated underlying types: it uses only positive allocation and unused quota at those types.

5.3. Counting the components

An alternating cycle has equally many edges of each color. An alternating path either has no more \(N\)-edges than \(M\)-edges, or has \(k+1\) \(N\)-edges and \(k\) \(M\)-edges.

In the latter case, Sections 5.1–5.2 imply \(k\ge2\). Therefore

\( \displaystyle k+1\le\frac32k. \)

Summing over all components yields

\( \displaystyle |N|\le\frac32|M|. \)

Combining the inequalities,

\( \displaystyle D\sum_e z_e \le\sum_e W_e =|N| \le\frac32|M| =\frac32D\sum_e x_e. \)

Since \(D>0\), this proves

\( \displaystyle \sum_e x_e\ge\frac23\sum_e z_e \)

for every stable mass matching \(z\).

6. Encoding size and attainment of the optimum

One may take \(D\) to be the product of the denominators of the input masses. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). The scaled quotas and edge bounds consequently have polynomial encoding length.

The constructed instance has \(\tau\) original vertices and \(4m\) edges. Its preference lists can be sorted using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. The stable-allocation primitive runs directly on the encoded quotas; it does not create \(Q_v\) individuals. Its integral output divided by \(D\) has polynomial encoding length. Thus the complete algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

Finally, the feasible matching polytope is compact. For every contract and every status pair satisfying a blocking threshold pattern, stability imposes the closed condition

\( s_a(f)=0\quad\text{or}\quad s_b(g)=0. \)

There are finitely many such conditions. The stable set is therefore closed, and Section 3 proves it nonempty. The continuous mass objective attains a maximum on this set.

Taking \(z\) to be an optimum in Section 5 establishes the claimed guarantee.
