Proposition 5 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a GAI attribute structure, monotonicity conditions, fixed background constraints, a focal report \(P^\star\), and a society with rational type masses \(\mu_t\) over optional reports \(P_t\), find a subset \(Q\) of report types minimizing \(\sum_{t\in Q}\mu_t\) such that the background constraints, \(P^\star\), the monotonicity conditions, and every constraint \(T(P_t,\hat u)\ge 0\) for \(t\in Q\) are infeasible.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge 1\) attributes. Each domain \(X_i\) is either an explicitly listed finite set with a given total preorder \(\succeq_i\), or a bounded interval with larger values preferred and an explicitly listed rational grid containing its endpoints. A nonempty family \(\mathcal S\) of nonempty attribute subsets specifies the GAI structure.
For each \(S\in\mathcal S\), the unknown table \(\hat u_S\) has one nonnegative real entry per local grid point. Require coordinatewise monotonicity under \(\succeq_i\), including equality for indifferent levels. Define \(U(x)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}u_S(x_S)\), using table lookup on finite coordinates and piecewise multilinear interpolation on interval coordinates; impose no utility normalization or upper bound.
A report is a weak comparison \(x\succeq y\), a lower bound \(U(x)\ge\alpha\), or an upper bound \(U(x)\le\alpha\), with rational interval coordinates and \(\alpha\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). Its constraint \(T(P,\hat u)\ge0\) has \(T=U(x)-U(y)\), \(T=U(x)-\alpha\), or \(T=\alpha-U(x)\), respectively.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible report types; require \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Type \(t\) supplies one optional report \(P_t\).
The input also contains \(b\) compulsory background reports \(B\) and a focal report \(P^\star\). Promise that monotonicity, nonnegativity, \(B\), and all optional reports are jointly feasible, but become infeasible after adding \(P^\star\).
Choose whole types \(Q\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) minimizing \(\sum_{t\in Q}\mu_t\), subject to infeasibility of monotonicity, nonnegativity, \(B\), \(P^\star\), and the reports indexed by \(Q\); the empty subset is allowed.
Let \(d=\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}\prod_{i\in S}p_i\), where \(p_i\) is the number of retained levels. Local grid cells are explicitly indexed in the input. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,d,b,L\).

Is deciding whether such an explanation has mass at most a given binary rational \(r\in[0,1]\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite report lists, a mixed integer formulation based on Farkas certificates is proposed for minimum-size explanations, without a complexity classification. [Labreuche–Grabisch](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/8388.pdf)

Classification

The decision problem is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard, even when every domain is binary and finite, every GAI scope is a singleton, there are no background reports, and all types have equal mass.

The reduction below always satisfies both feasibility promises. It settles the requested hardness question without assuming that the number $d$ of table entries is polynomial in the binary input length $L$.

1. Source problem

We use the decision version of Set Cover: given a nonempty universe
$$ E=\{1,\ldots,n\}, $$
sets $C_1,\ldots,C_s\subseteq E$ satisfying
$$ \bigcup_{t=1}^s C_t=E, $$
and an integer $0\le k\le s$, decide whether at most $k$ of these sets cover $E$.

Set Cover is $\mathrm{NP}$-complete; see [Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9). The restrictions above preserve hardness. Indeed, an instance whose sets do not cover its universe is immediately a no-instance, and one with an empty universe is immediately a yes-instance. These cases can be replaced by fixed instances satisfying the restrictions: respectively, $E=\{1\}$, $C_1=\{1\}$ with $k=0$ or $k=1$. For the remaining instances, duplicate and empty sets can be removed without changing the minimum cover cardinality, and the threshold can be replaced by its minimum with the resulting number of sets. Thus we may also assume that the sets are distinct and nonempty. In particular, $n,s\ge1$.

2. Construction of the GAI instance

Given such a Set Cover instance, construct the following instance.

  • There are $m=n$ attributes, with $X_i=\{0,1\}$ and strict preference $1\succ_i0$.
  • The GAI structure is

$$ \mathcal S=\{\{i\}:i\in E\}. $$
Hence $d=2n$.

  • There are no compulsory background reports, so $b=0$.
  • There are $\tau=s$ types, each with mass $\mu_t=1/s$.
  • For $C\subseteq E$, let $x^C\in\{0,1\}^n$ be its indicator vector. Type $t$ supplies

$$ P_t:\quad U(x^{C_t})\le0. $$

  • The focal report is

$$ P^\star:\quad U(x^E)\ge1. $$

  • The decision threshold is $r=k/s$.

These are permitted reports: their bound constants are the nonnegative rationals $0$ and $1$. The masses are positive, sum to $1$, and $r\in[0,1]$. Distinct sets give distinct optional reports.

Write the entries of the singleton table for attribute $i$ as
$$ a_i=u_{\{i\}}(0), \qquad c_i=u_{\{i\}}(1). $$
Nonnegativity and monotonicity are exactly
$$ 0\le a_i\le c_i. $$
For every $C\subseteq E$, additive table lookup gives
$$ U(x^C)=\sum_{i\in C}c_i+\sum_{i\notin C}a_i. $$

The construction has polynomial size. The optional alternatives contain $ns$ binary coordinates; domain and scope data have polynomial size; and the masses and threshold require $O(s\log(s+1))$ bits. Any required indexing for these singleton finite tables is also polynomial in size.

3. Exact correspondence between explanations and covers

We prove that, for every $Q\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}$,
$$ \text{the selected system including }P^\star\text{ is infeasible} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \bigcup_{t\in Q}C_t=E. $$

A cover gives an infeasible system

Suppose the selected sets cover $E$. Each selected optional report requires
$$ \sum_{i\in C_t}c_i+\sum_{i\notin C_t}a_i\le0. $$
Every summand is nonnegative. A finite sum of nonnegative real numbers can be at most zero only when every summand is zero. Consequently,
$$ c_i=0\qquad\text{for every }i\in C_t. $$
Since the selected sets cover $E$, this forces $c_i=0$ for every $i\in E$. Therefore
$$ U(x^E)=\sum_{i\in E}c_i=0, $$
contradicting the focal report $U(x^E)\ge1$. The system is infeasible.

A noncover gives a feasible system

Suppose the selected sets do not cover $E$. Choose
$$ j\in E\setminus\bigcup_{t\in Q}C_t. $$
Assign
$$ a_i=0\quad\text{for every }i, \qquad c_j=1, \qquad c_i=0\quad\text{for }i\ne j. $$
These assignments satisfy $0\le a_i\le c_i$.

For each $t\in Q$, the set $C_t$ excludes $j$. Its utility therefore equals zero: every selected high-level entry is zero, and every low-level entry is zero. Thus every selected optional upper bound holds. Moreover,
$$ U(x^E)=\sum_{i\in E}c_i=1, $$
so the focal report holds as well. The selected system is feasible.

This argument includes $Q=\varnothing$, because $E$ is nonempty.

4. Promise and decision threshold

Without the focal report, setting every table entry to zero satisfies all optional reports simultaneously. Thus the first feasibility promise holds.

Selecting all optional reports covers $E$. By the equivalence proved above, adding the focal report makes this system infeasible. Thus the second promise also holds.

Finally, equal masses give
$$ \sum_{t\in Q}\mu_t=\frac{|Q|}{s}. $$
Since $s>0$,
$$ \sum_{t\in Q}\mu_t\le r \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{|Q|}{s}\le\frac{k}{s} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |Q|\le k. $$
Combining this with the infeasibility equivalence, the constructed instance has an explanation of mass at most $r$ exactly when the source instance has a cover of cardinality at most $k$.

We have therefore obtained a polynomial-time reduction that maps both yes-instances and no-instances to inputs satisfying the promise. This proves $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness of the promised decision problem.

5. Running times in the stated parameters

For completeness, a proposed explanation can be checked in
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,d,b,L) $$
bit operations. This bound does not require $d$ to be polynomial in $L$.

To justify it, let $z\in\mathbb R^d$ collect the unknown table entries. Every utility evaluation is a rational linear expression in $z$.

For an interval coordinate lying in a grid segment with endpoints $\ell_i<h_i$, the interpolation weights are
$$ w_i^-=\frac{h_i-x_i}{h_i-\ell_i}, \qquad w_i^+=\frac{x_i-\ell_i}{h_i-\ell_i}. $$
They are rational, nonnegative, and sum to $1$. At a grid endpoint, a segment incident to that endpoint gives the corresponding weight $1$ and the other weight $0$. If a singleton interval is permitted, its sole level is evaluated directly with weight $1$, without division.

Each local interpolation coefficient is a product of at most $m$ such weights. Its binary length is $O(m(L+1))$. Finite coordinates select table entries directly. Utility differences and bound reports consequently yield rational linear inequalities with coefficients of the same asymptotic bit-length bound.

Nonnegativity contributes $d$ inequalities. For each finite total preorder, order its retained levels from least to most preferred, with indifferent levels consecutive. Adjacent-level inequalities enforce monotonicity by transitivity; between indifferent adjacent levels, impose both directions to enforce equality. For interval grids, impose adjacent-level monotonicity inequalities.

For a scope $S$, the number of adjacent pairs, summed over coordinates, is
$$ \sum_{i\in S}(p_i-1)\prod_{j\in S\setminus\{i\}}p_j \le |S|\prod_{i\in S}p_i. $$
Allowing two inequalities for an equality, the total number of intrinsic inequalities is at most $d+2md$. Including the background reports, focal report, and selected optional reports gives
$$ N=O(md+b+\tau+1). $$

The resulting dense rational system has encoding length bounded conservatively by
$$ H= O\!\left( N(d+1)\bigl(m(L+1)+\log(d+1)\bigr)+L \right). $$
Constructing it takes time polynomial in $m,\tau,d,b,L$. Feasibility of an explicitly represented rational linear inequality system is decidable in polynomial bit time in its encoding length, by the polynomial-time linear-programming theorem; see [Khachiyan, *A polynomial algorithm in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319). Its hypotheses apply because the constructed system is finite and has rational coefficients. No boundedness assumption on the feasible region is required.

Exact addition and comparison of the binary rational masses also take polynomial time. Thus the stated verification bound follows. Enumerating all subsets, including the empty subset, gives an exact decision algorithm—and an exact minimum-mass algorithm—in
$$ 2^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,d,b,L) $$
bit operations.

6. Scope of the conclusion

Explicit indexing of interpolation cells alone does not imply that $d$ is polynomial in $L$. For example, one scope containing all $m$ attributes, with interval grids $\{0,1\}$, has a single interpolation cell but $2^m$ table entries. Consequently, the verification bound above does not establish general $\mathrm{NP}$ membership under an encoding that specifies cells without enumerating their corners.

On the binary additive subclass used in the reduction, however, $d=2m$, so verification is polynomial in $L$. That subclass is therefore $\mathrm{NP}$-complete.

For the full problem as stated, the proved classification answering the question is $\mathrm{NP}$-hard. A polynomial-time algorithm in the binary input length would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.
