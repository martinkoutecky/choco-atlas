The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_1,\mu_2>0\) with \(\mu_1+\mu_2=1\), and rational thresholds \(0\le\alpha\le\mu_1\), \(0\le\beta\le\mu_2\), a coalition is winning when its yes-mass vector \((x_1,x_2)\in[0,\mu_1]\times[0,\mu_2]\) satisfies \(x_1\ge\alpha\) and \(x_1+x_2\ge\alpha+\beta\). Decide whether there exist type weights \(w_1\ge w_2\ge0\) and quota \(q\) such that every feasible coalition wins exactly when \(w_1x_1+w_2x_2\ge q\).

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. A weighted representation consists of real weights \(w_1\ge w_2\ge0\) and a real quota \(q>0\), such that every feasible coalition wins exactly when \(w_1x_1+w_2x_2\ge q\); equality is winning.

We prove nonexistence using two losing coalitions whose midpoint is winning.

Proof

Let \(R=[0,\mu_1]\times[0,\mu_2]\) be the feasible rectangle. By hypothesis, all four numbers \(\alpha\), \(\mu_1-\alpha\), \(\beta\), and \(\mu_2-\beta\) are strictly positive. Define

\( \varepsilon=\frac14\min\{\alpha,\mu_1-\alpha,\beta,\mu_2-\beta\}>0. \)

Consider the coalitions

\( p=(\alpha,\beta),\qquad u=(\alpha-\varepsilon,\beta+2\varepsilon),\qquad v=(\alpha+\varepsilon,\beta-2\varepsilon). \)

Feasibility. The definition of \(\varepsilon\) gives \(\varepsilon<\alpha\), \(\varepsilon<\mu_1-\alpha\), \(2\varepsilon<\beta\), and \(2\varepsilon<\mu_2-\beta\). Consequently, \(p,u,v\) all belong to \(R\).

Winning and losing status. The coalition \(p\) is winning because both defining inequalities hold with equality:

\(p_1=\alpha,\qquad p_1+p_2=\alpha+\beta.\)

The coalition \(u\) is losing because \(u_1=\alpha-\varepsilon<\alpha\). The coalition \(v\) is losing because

\(v_1+v_2=\alpha+\beta-\varepsilon<\alpha+\beta.\)

Their midpoint is exactly the winning coalition:

\( \frac{u+v}{2}=p. \)

Now suppose a weighted representation exists, and write \(f(x)=w_1x_1+w_2x_2\). Since equality with the quota is winning, every losing coalition must have score strictly below the quota. Therefore

\(f(u)<q,\qquad f(v)<q.\)

Linearity then implies

\( f(p)=f\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)=\frac{f(u)+f(v)}2<q. \)

This contradicts the fact that \(p\) is winning, which requires \(f(p)\ge q\).

Thus no weighted representation exists. Indeed, this argument excludes arbitrary real weights, even without the restrictions \(w_1\ge w_2\ge0\).

Algorithm and bit complexity

On inputs promised to satisfy the stated hypotheses, the algorithm simply returns NO. Its decision time is constant, hence polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

If promise validation is required, positivity, the threshold inequalities, and \(\mu_1+\mu_2=1\) can all be checked by exact rational arithmetic in time polynomial in \(L\). The explicit witness above also has polynomial encoding length and can be constructed and verified in polynomial time.

Why the discrete result differs

The discrete setting in [Freixas–Kurz](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.6113) restricts coalitions to integer counts. Our proof relies on fractional perturbations that remain feasible however close the thresholds are to the rectangle’s edges. Those coalitions need not be available on an integer grid. Thus the discrete weighted cases do not contradict the continuous obstruction proved here.
