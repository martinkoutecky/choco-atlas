The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many role types \(T\), masses \(\mu_t=1/|T|\), finite owned graphs \(H_t\), rational-shift cross-role conflict rules on \(\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\), and a palette bound \(q\), decide whether \(q\) is the minimum number of colors in a legal measurable coloring and whether such a \(q\)-coloring exists with \(\left|\{c_{t,p}(\theta):p\in P_t\}\right|=\chi(H_t)\) for every role \(t\) and almost every \(\theta\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive masses, encoded as binary rationals; here \(\mu_t=1/\tau\). Thus \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type owns a nonempty finite simple graph \(H_t=(P_t,E_t)\). Agents are \((t,\theta)\), with \(\theta\in\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), distributed according to \(\mu_t\,d\theta\), where \(d\theta\) is normalized Lebesgue measure; agent \((t,\theta)\) owns vertices \((t,p,\theta)\) for \(p\in P_t\).
Input also includes a finite list \(R\) of undirected conflict rules \((t,p,s,r,\delta)\), where \(t\ne s\), \(p\in P_t\), \(r\in P_s\), and \(\delta\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,1)\), encoded in binary. Such a rule joins \((t,p,\theta)\) to \((s,r,\theta+\delta)\), with addition modulo \(1\). Internal edges are exactly copies of \(E_t\) at each \(\theta\).
A legal measurable \(k\)-coloring consists of Lebesgue measurable maps \(c_{t,p}:\mathbb T\to\{1,\ldots,k\}\), satisfying \(c_{t,p}(\theta)\ne c_{t,r}(\theta)\) for every internal edge and \(c_{t,p}(\theta)\ne c_{s,r}(\theta+\delta)\) for every conflict rule, almost everywhere.
Let \(\chi_{\mathrm{meas}}\) be the least positive integer \(k\) admitting a legal measurable coloring, without any individual rationality requirement. A coloring is individually rational when \(\left|\{c_{t,p}(\theta):p\in P_t\}\right|=\chi(H_t)\) for every \(t\), almost everywhere.
Given a positive binary integer \(q\), the decision problem asks whether both \(q=\chi_{\mathrm{meas}}\) and an individually rational legal measurable \(q\)-coloring exists. Set \(m=\sum_{t\in T}|P_t|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,|R|,q,L\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete societies, deciding whether a partition of an odd chordless cycle admits a globally minimum coloring using exactly each agent's induced chromatic number is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when the number of agents can grow linearly with the number of vertices ([Blum–Rosenschein, 2008](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2008/AAAI08-004.pdf)).
No result settling exactly the measurable decision problem defined above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified the continuum of agents as type–circle pairs with uniform conditional normalized Lebesgue measure, retaining the prescribed uniform type masses.
  • Specified owned graphs as nonempty finite simple graphs, copied internally at each circle coordinate, consistent with agents owning induced subgraphs.
  • Interpreted rational-shift cross-role conflict rules as an explicitly listed finite family of undirected translated edges between distinct roles, with binary rational shifts modulo one.
  • Interpreted measurable legality almost everywhere, matching the almost-everywhere individual rationality condition.
  • Defined the global measurable chromatic minimum independently of individual rationality and interpreted the two requested conditions as a conjunction.
  • Specified a positive binary palette bound and defined the size parameters and total input bit length.

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. This holds even when:

  • \(q=3\) and every conflict shift is zero;
  • every local graph \(H_t\) is nonempty and edgeless, so \(\chi(H_t)=1\);
  • the graph at each fixed \(\theta\) is an odd chordless cycle.

Thus hardness persists when the global measurable chromatic number is known in advance. The difficulty comes entirely from individual rationality.

1. Zero shifts preserve the finite coloring problem

Suppose every conflict shift is zero. Define a finite graph \(F\) with vertex set \(\{(t,p):t\in T,\ p\in P_t\}\), containing the internal edges and the edges specified by the conflict rules.

Lemma. For every positive integer \(k\):

  • A legal measurable \(k\)-coloring exists exactly when \(F\) has a proper \(k\)-coloring.
  • An individually rational legal measurable \(k\)-coloring exists exactly when \(F\) has a proper \(k\)-coloring using exactly \(\chi(H_t)\) colors on each set \(\{t\}\times P_t\).

Proof. A finite coloring gives measurable coloring functions constant in \(\theta\), preserving both legality and the number of colors used by each type.

Conversely, take a legal measurable coloring. Each edge constraint fails only on a null set. There are finitely many constraints, so their exceptional sets have a null union. Choose \(\theta\) outside that union. Because all shifts are zero, the assignment \((t,p)\mapsto c_{t,p}(\theta)\) is a proper coloring of \(F\).

If the measurable coloring is individually rational, include the finitely many exceptional sets for individual rationality in the union before choosing \(\theta\). The resulting finite coloring uses exactly \(\chi(H_t)\) colors on each type. This proves both assertions. Consequently, \(\chi_{\mathrm{meas}}=\chi(F)\). ∎

The uniform positive masses cause no obstruction: null sets for each type are precisely Lebesgue null sets.

2. A reduction with known global minimum

We reduce from graph three-colorability: given a finite simple graph \(G\), decide whether it has a proper coloring with at most three colors. This is the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem used as the starting problem in [Blum–Rosenschein, Theorem 7](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2008/AAAI08-004.pdf). The construction below is independent of their cycle construction.

Construction

Form \(G'\) by adjoining a disjoint triangle to \(G\), then deleting isolated vertices. These operations preserve three-colorability: isolated vertices can always be colored, and the added triangle can be colored with three colors.

Enumerate the edges of \(G'\) as \(e_i=\{u_i,v_i\}\), for \(1\leq i\leq s\). Here \(s\geq3\). Orient each edge arbitrarily to choose the notation \(u_i,v_i\).

Create distinct vertices \(a_i,b_i,z_i\) for each edge. Set \(\varepsilon=0\) when \(s\) is odd and \(\varepsilon=1\) when \(s\) is even. If \(\varepsilon=1\), create one additional vertex \(w\).

Make these vertices into a chordless cycle in the order

\(a_1,b_1,z_1,a_2,b_2,z_2,\ldots,a_s,b_s,z_s\),

inserting \(w\) between \(z_s\) and \(a_1\) when it exists. The cycle length is \(m=3s+\varepsilon\), which is odd.

Partition the cycle vertices into types as follows:

  • For each vertex \(v\) of \(G'\), create a type \(t_v\) owning all \(a_i\) with \(u_i=v\) and all \(b_i\) with \(v_i=v\).
  • Give each \(z_i\) its own singleton type.
  • If \(w\) exists, give it its own singleton type.

Every type is nonempty because \(G'\) has no isolated vertices.

Every cycle edge has endpoints in different types. Indeed, \(a_i\) and \(b_i\) belong to different types because \(u_i\neq v_i\); all remaining edges involve singleton separator types.

Let every \(H_t\) be the edgeless graph on its assigned vertices. Encode every cycle edge as one conflict rule with shift zero. Set every mass to \(1/\tau\), and set \(q=3\).

This is a valid instance of the stated measurable problem.

Its measurable chromatic number is always three

The finite graph \(F\) from the lemma is exactly the constructed odd chordless cycle.

A two-coloring of a cycle must alternate colors around the cycle, which contradicts the closing edge when its length is odd. Thus \(\chi(F)\geq3\).

For the reverse inequality, color the first \(m-1\) vertices alternately with colors \(1,2\), and color the last vertex with color \(3\). This is proper, so \(\chi(F)=3\).

By the lemma, \(\chi_{\mathrm{meas}}=3=q\), regardless of whether \(G\) is three-colorable.

Three-colorability implies individual rationality

Suppose \(h\) is a proper three-coloring of \(G'\). Color every vertex belonging to \(t_v\) with \(h(v)\). Each edge \(a_ib_i\) is properly colored because it represents the edge \(\{u_i,v_i\}\) of \(G'\).

For each separator \(z_i\) lying between two already colored vertices, choose a color different from those two endpoint colors. At most two colors are forbidden, so a choice exists.

If \(w\) exists, the remaining segment is \(b_s,z_s,w,a_1\). First choose the color of \(z_s\) different from that of \(b_s\). Then choose the color of \(w\) different from those of \(z_s\) and \(a_1\). Again, at most two colors are forbidden.

This gives a proper three-coloring of the cycle. Every type is monochromatic. Since each nonempty \(H_t\) is edgeless, \(\chi(H_t)=1\), so the coloring is individually rational.

Its constant measurable extension is therefore an individually rational legal measurable \(q\)-coloring.

Individual rationality implies three-colorability

Conversely, suppose the constructed society has an individually rational legal measurable three-coloring. By the lemma, there is a finite proper three-coloring of the cycle that uses exactly one color on each type.

Let \(h(v)\) be the common color of type \(t_v\). For every edge \(\{u_i,v_i\}\) of \(G'\), the cycle edge \(a_ib_i\) gives \(h(u_i)\neq h(v_i)\). Hence \(h\) is a proper three-coloring of \(G'\), and therefore \(G\) is three-colorable.

We have proved that \(G\) is three-colorable exactly when the constructed measurable instance is accepted. This establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

3. Size and running time of the reduction

Write \(n'=|V(G')|\). Since \(G'\) has no isolated vertices, \(n'\leq2s\). The output satisfies

\(m=3s+\varepsilon,\quad \tau=n'+s+\varepsilon,\quad |R|=m,\quad q=3.\)

In particular, \(\tau\leq m\) and \(\tau=\Theta(m)\). All shifts are zero, all internal edge sets are empty, and each mass \(1/\tau\) requires \(O(\log(\tau+1))\) bits.

Under ordinary explicit graph encodings, the output length is \(L=O((m+\tau+|R|)\log(m+\tau+1))\). Given an input graph with \(n\) vertices and \(e\) edges, the reduction can be implemented in \(O((n+e)\log(n+e+2))\) bit operations. Its output construction takes \(O((m+\tau+|R|)\log(m+\tau+1))\) bit operations.

Thus a polynomial-time algorithm for the stated decision problem would yield a polynomial-time algorithm for graph three-colorability. The conclusion is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness; exclusion from \(\mathrm P\) is conditional on \(\mathrm P\neq\mathrm{NP}\).

4. A finite decision procedure for arbitrary rational shifts

For completeness, the general measurable problem also admits an explicit finite reduction, although its size can be exponential in \(L\).

Let \(D\) be the least common multiple of the reduced denominators of all shifts, with \(D=1\) if there are no rules. Write each shift as \(\delta=a_\delta/D\).

Construct a finite graph \(F_D\) on vertices \((t,p,j)\), where \(0\leq j<D\). Include:

  • each internal edge at every index \(j\);
  • for each rule, the edges \((t,p,j)(s,r,j+a_\delta\bmod D)\).

A measurable coloring restricts to a coloring of \(F_D\) by evaluating at the finitely many points \(x+j/D\), choosing \(x\in[0,1/D)\) outside all translated exceptional null sets. Such an \(x\) exists because a finite union of null sets cannot cover that interval.

Conversely, a coloring \(g\) of \(F_D\) yields measurable functions by setting \(c_{t,p}(\theta)=g(t,p,\lfloor D\theta\rfloor)\) for the representative \(\theta\in[0,1)\). Addition of \(a_\delta/D\) changes the interval index by \(a_\delta\) modulo \(D\), so every rule is satisfied. Both directions preserve individual rationality on each set \(\{(t,p,j):p\in P_t\}\).

Therefore \(\chi_{\mathrm{meas}}=\chi(F_D)\), and individual rationality reduces to the corresponding finite constraints.

Also, \(\chi_{\mathrm{meas}}\leq m\): assign a distinct constant color to every pair \((t,p)\). Hence inputs with \(q>m\) can immediately be rejected.

For \(q\leq m\), exhaustive enumeration computes the local chromatic numbers, tests for an individually rational \(q\)-coloring of \(F_D\), and tests the absence of a proper \((q-1)\)-coloring. For \(q=1\), the last test is unnecessary.

A valid, deliberately loose bit-time bound is

\(\operatorname{poly}(L)\bigl[\tau m^{m+2}+D(m^2+|R|+\tau)q^{mD}\bigr].\)

Here \(D\leq2^L\), since the least common multiple is at most the product of the encoded denominators. This gives a finite decision procedure, without asserting a tighter complexity classification.
