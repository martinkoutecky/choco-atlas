The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) of owner types and \(\nu_\phi\) of viewer types, complete type data, and a rational relationship kernel \(w_{\theta\phi}\), choose a policy \(a\) maximizing \(\sum_\theta \mu_\theta u_{\theta,a}v_{\theta,a}\). The finite candidate set contains policies preferred by positive-mass owner types, a specified compromise policy, and the distinguished uploader’s preferred policy if the uploader is represented separately; each \(u_{\theta,a}\) is computed from the type-level allowed, extra, excluded, and excluded-extra viewer masses, and \(v_{\theta,a}\) is the paper’s value-promotion score. Return a maximizing policy and its score.

Result

The stated exact policy-selection problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), including the required tie-break and the exact rational score.

The proof uses the supplied support representation and the specified candidate set. Rational masses affect score evaluation; they do not introduce additional decision variables.

1. Constructing the candidate audiences

Let \(\Theta\) and \(\Phi\) be the owner and viewer supports, with \(n=|\Theta|=\tau_O\) and \(t=|\Phi|=\tau_V\). Thus \(n+t=\tau\). Represent every supplied audience by its membership vector on \(\Phi\). This requires only polynomial preprocessing of the explicit input.

Let \(\mathcal P=\{p_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}\) be the set of distinct preferred policies. For every \(p\in\mathcal P\), compute its collective audience \(S_p=\bigcap_{\theta\in\Theta}H_{\theta,p}\) by intersecting the supplied membership vectors. Also compute \(P_\theta=H_{\theta,p_\theta}\) and \(U=\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}P_\theta\).

Every preferred-policy collective audience lies in \(U\). Indeed, because \(p\in\mathcal P\), some owner \(\theta_0\) satisfies \(p_{\theta_0}=p\). Consequently,
\(S_p\subseteq H_{\theta_0,p}=P_{\theta_0}\subseteq U\).
The compromise audience lies in \(U\) by hypothesis. Hence every candidate audience \(S_a\) lies in \(U\).

There are \(m=|\mathcal P|+1\) base candidates and exactly \(2m\) actions, one for each candidate and sharing mode. In particular, \(m\leq n+1\). Distinct policies with identical collective audiences can remain separate candidates: their supplied valuation labels may differ.

Audience construction takes \(O(mnt)\) Boolean operations after preprocessing.

2. Computing each action’s scores

For each owner \(\theta\) and action \(a\), compute the supplied value score
\(v_{\theta,a}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}(5-i)\operatorname{prom}_{\theta,a}(o_{\theta,i})\).
It is an integer satisfying
\(|v_{\theta,a}|\leq 4+3+2+1=10\).

Because \(P_\theta,S_a\subseteq U\), the sets \(A,B,C,D\) in the question form a disjoint partition of \(U\). For \(\phi\in U\), define

| Membership of \(\phi\) | \(c_{\theta,a,\phi}\) |
|---|---|
| \(\phi\in A\) | \(1\) |
| \(\phi\in B\) | \(r_\theta\) |
| \(\phi\in C\) | \(-\alpha_a\) |
| \(\phi\in D\) | \(r_\theta\beta_a\) |

Expanding the definition of \(W_\theta\) gives
\(u_{\theta,a}=\sum_{\phi\in U}\nu_\phi w_{\theta\phi}c_{\theta,a,\phi}\).
Therefore the primary score is exactly
\(F(a)=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{\phi\in U}\mu_\theta\nu_\phi w_{\theta\phi}v_{\theta,a}c_{\theta,a,\phi}\).

Also compute the secondary score
\(T(a)=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta v_{\theta,a}\).

For one action, these computations require \(O(nt+n)\) arithmetic operations. Over all actions, they require \(O(mnt+mn)\) arithmetic operations. It remains to verify that these are operations on integers of polynomial bit length.

3. Exact arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity

Write every supplied rational scalar as an integer numerator divided by a positive integer denominator. Let \(Q\) be the product of all these denominator occurrences. This includes the denominators of the masses, relationship weights, appreciation parameters, and modified-mode parameters.

Because the sum of their encoded lengths is at most \(L\), the binary length of \(Q\) is \(O(L)\). Every supplied rational scalar \(x\) can therefore be represented exactly as \(N_x/Q\), where
\(N_x=\operatorname{num}(x)\bigl(Q/\operatorname{den}(x)\bigr)\).
The division is exact. Each \(N_x\) has \(O(L)\) bits, since both the original numerator and \(Q\) have \(O(L)\) bits.

Each summand in the expanded formula for \(F(a)\) is an integer coefficient of absolute value at most \(10\), multiplied by at most five supplied rational scalars:

  • The factors \(\mu_\theta,\nu_\phi,w_{\theta\phi}\) contribute three.
  • The audience coefficient contributes at most two more, namely \(r_\theta\beta_M\).

The constants used by original-only actions contribute no additional input factors. Thus a summand containing \(k\leq 5\) rational factors can be represented over the common denominator \(Q^5\) with integer numerator
\(\varepsilon v_{\theta,a}Q^{5-k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}N_{x_j}\),
where \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\) accounts for its sign. Zero summands can be omitted.

Each such numerator has \(O(L)\) bits: it multiplies only a constant number of integers of \(O(L)\) bits. Summing at most \(nt\) of them produces an integer \(J_a\) of bit length \(O(L+\log(\tau+1))\), satisfying
\(F(a)=J_a/Q^5\).
The same bound applies to intermediate partial sums, because their absolute values are bounded by the sum of the absolute values of the summands.

Likewise, the secondary score has the exact representation
\(T(a)=K_a/Q\), where \(K_a=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}N_{\mu_\theta}v_{\theta,a}\).
Its numerator also has \(O(L+\log(\tau+1))\) bits.

Constructing these representations requires polynomially many additions, multiplications, and exact divisions on polynomial-length integers. Elementary binary arithmetic performs each operation in polynomial time. Thus the arithmetic-operation count from Section 2 yields a polynomial bit-time bound, rather than merely a unit-cost arithmetic bound.

4. Selection, correctness, and output

Enumerate all \(2m\) actions and retain an action whose integer pair \((J_a,K_a)\) is lexicographically maximal: first maximize \(J_a\), and among equal values maximize \(K_a\). Any remaining tie may be resolved arbitrarily.

All primary scores share the positive denominator \(Q^5\), so comparing their numerators compares the primary scores exactly. All secondary scores share the positive denominator \(Q\), so the same holds for the tie-break.

For completeness, after processing any initial segment of the action list, the retained pair is lexicographically maximal among that segment. This follows by induction: retaining the larger of the previous maximum and the next pair preserves that property. After the final action, the retained action therefore satisfies both required optimization criteria.

Return that action and \(J_a/Q^5\). If a reduced fraction is desired, divide numerator and denominator by \(\gcd(|J_a|,Q^5)\), using the polynomial-time Euclidean algorithm. The output fraction has polynomial bit length.

The algorithm evaluates every allowed action, computes both scores exactly, and implements the specified tie-break. This completes the proof.
