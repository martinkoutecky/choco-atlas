The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given an interval \([a,b]\) and a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over residents’ ideal locations, design a deterministic mechanism that takes the reported distribution \(\nu\), chooses a facility location \(x(\nu)\), and assigns per-capita payments \(p(r,\nu)\le 0\). Require that the mechanism be OSP with monitoring on every finite equal-mass refinement of \(\mu\), where an agent of true location \(t\) who reports \(r\) incurs cost \(\max\{|t-x(\nu)|,|r-x(\nu)|\}-p(r,\nu)\). On truthful reports, the facility must minimize \(\int |t-x|\,d\mu(t)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Q\) with \(a\le b\). There is one facility, so \(m=1\); true and reported locations lie in \(D=[a,b]\cap\mathbb Q\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational, with total mass \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total binary input length, including endpoints and locations.
Seek a uniform deterministic direct-revelation mechanism, depending only on the public interval and reported distribution \(\nu\), that returns \(x(\nu)\in D\) and rational per-capita payments \(p(r,\nu)\le0\) for reported types \(r\). Negative payments are charges; voluntary participation is not required.
For every truthful society \(\mu\), require \(x(\mu)\) to be the leftmost minimizer of \(\int_D|t-x|\,d\mu(t)\) over \(x\in[a,b]\).
An equal-mass refinement has \(n\ge1\) agents of mass \(1/n\), with empirical true distribution \(\mu\). Each agent reports once without observing others' reports; a report profile \(\mathbf r\in D^n\) induces \(\nu_{\mathbf r}=n^{-1}\sum_j\delta_{r_j}\).
The monitored per-capita cost is \(C(t,r;\nu)=\max\{|t-x(\nu)|,|r-x(\nu)|\}-p(r,\nu)\).
Require OSP on every such refinement, with each agent's domain equal to \(D\): for all \(n,i,t,r\) with \(t\ne r\), and all other-agent report profiles \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\in D^{n-1}\), require \(C(t,t;\nu_{(t,\mathbf u)})\le C(t,r;\nu_{(r,\mathbf v)})\).

Can such a mechanism be evaluated in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), independently of the refinement size \(n\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finite equally weighted agents on a bounded interval, an optimal deterministic direct-revelation OSP mechanism with monitoring and nonpositive payments is computable in polynomial time. [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/html/1702.05640).
No result settling this precise distribution-input formulation was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass 1, so a refinement replaces each mass by an integer number of agents of mass 1/n.
  • Used binary rational endpoints and ideal/report locations to give the computational problem a finite encoding; retained the entire rational interval as every agent's domain.
  • Required a uniform mechanism for all societies, with the true distribution private and only the interval public.
  • Specified simultaneous direct revelation and quantified OSP over arbitrary other-agent reports, rather than only reports in the truthful support.
  • Required rational facility locations and payments and interpreted payments and costs per capita, without scaling them by refinement size.
  • Carried over leftmost-minimizer tie-breaking and imposed no voluntary-participation requirement.
  • Set m=1 for the single facility and required compressed evaluation time independent of the potentially exponentially large refinement size.

Result

Such a mechanism is evaluable in deterministic polynomial time, independently of the refinement size \(n\). The interval mechanism’s payment formula depends only on the reported location and facility location, so it extends directly to distribution inputs. The finite-agent formula appears in [Section 5.1 of the verified source](https://arxiv.org/html/1702.05640#S5.SS1). Below is a complete proof for the stated formulation.

1. Define the mechanism

Write the reported distribution, with its support sorted, as

\( \displaystyle \nu=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}w_j\delta_{z_j},\qquad a\le z_1<\cdots<z_\tau\le b,\qquad w_j>0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}w_j=1. \)

Define

\( \displaystyle S_h=\sum_{j=1}^{h}w_j,\qquad k=\min\{h:S_h\ge\tfrac12\},\qquad x(\nu)=z_k. \)

For every reported type \(r\), set

\( \displaystyle p(r,\nu)=|r-x(\nu)|-(b-a). \)

These rules depend only on the public interval and reported distribution. The facility location and payments are rational. Because \(r,x(\nu)\in[a,b]\),

\( \displaystyle |r-x(\nu)|\le b-a,\qquad p(r,\nu)\le0. \)

When \(a=b\), the same construction places the facility at \(a\) and assigns zero payments. We now verify the nondegenerate case as well.

2. Prove that the facility is the leftmost minimizer

Fix any distribution \(\nu\) as above, and put

\( \displaystyle q=z_k,\qquad F(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}w_j|z_j-y|,\qquad S_0=0. \)

The definition of \(k\) gives \(S_{k-1}<\tfrac12\) and \(S_k\ge\tfrac12\).

For \(y<q\), every \(j\ge k\) satisfies \(z_j\ge q\), so

\( \displaystyle |z_j-y|-|z_j-q|=q-y. \)

For \(j<k\), the triangle inequality gives

\( \displaystyle |z_j-y|-|z_j-q|\ge-(q-y). \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle F(y)-F(q)\ge\bigl(1-2S_{k-1}\bigr)(q-y)>0. \)

For \(y>q\), every \(j\le k\) satisfies \(z_j\le q\), giving

\( \displaystyle |z_j-y|-|z_j-q|=y-q. \)

For \(j>k\), the triangle inequality gives a lower bound of \(-(y-q)\). Hence

\( \displaystyle F(y)-F(q)\ge(2S_k-1)(y-q)\ge0. \)

Thus \(q\) minimizes \(F\), and every feasible point strictly to its left has strictly larger objective value. Therefore \(q\) is the leftmost minimizer over \([a,b]\). Applying this argument to each truthful society \(\mu\) proves the required optimality.

3. Prove OSP on every equal-mass refinement

Fix arbitrary \(n\ge1\), agent \(i\), distinct types \(t,r\in D\), and other-agent report profiles \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\in D^{n-1}\). Let

\( \displaystyle \nu_T=\nu_{(t,\mathbf u)},\qquad \nu_R=\nu_{(r,\mathbf v)},\qquad B=b-a. \)

With a truthful report, the monitored cost is exactly

\( \displaystyle C(t,t;\nu_T)=|t-x(\nu_T)|-\bigl(|t-x(\nu_T)|-B\bigr)=B. \)

With report \(r\), it is

\( \displaystyle C(t,r;\nu_R)=B+\max\{|t-x(\nu_R)|,\ |r-x(\nu_R)|\}-|r-x(\nu_R)|\ge B. \)

The final inequality follows because a maximum is at least its second argument. Therefore

\( \displaystyle C(t,t;\nu_{(t,\mathbf u)})\le C(t,r;\nu_{(r,\mathbf v)}), \)

exactly as required.

This calculation holds separately for every profile and every refinement size. It uses neither the agent’s mass nor \(n\), so the same distribution-based mechanism satisfies all the refinement requirements simultaneously.

4. Prove polynomial-time evaluability

Given the support representation, the algorithm sorts the rational locations, finds the first cumulative mass at least \(\tfrac12\), and computes one payment for each support type.

Here are explicit bit-length bounds. Write each mass as \(w_j=c_j/d_j\), with positive integer denominator, and let

\( \displaystyle Q=\prod_{j=1}^{\tau}d_j. \)

The bit length of \(Q\) is bounded by the sum of the input denominator lengths, hence is \(O(L)\). Each cumulative mass can be represented as \(A_h/Q\), where

\( \displaystyle A_h=\sum_{j=1}^{h}c_j(Q/d_j). \)

Since the masses are positive and total one, \(0<A_h\le Q\). Thus these integers also have \(O(L)\) bits, and the median test is the exact integer comparison \(2A_h\ge Q\).

Sorting requires \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) rational comparisons, each implemented by integer cross multiplication on numbers of \(O(L)\) bits. Computing the cumulative masses takes polynomially many integer operations on numbers of \(O(L)\) bits.

Finally, every payment combines only the reported location, the selected support location, and the two endpoints. Its rational representation has \(O(L)\) bits, so producing all \(\tau\) payments also takes polynomial time and output space.

No step expands the distribution into individual agents or computes a refinement size. The running time is therefore polynomial in \(\tau,L\), and hence in \(m,\tau,L\) with \(m=1\), independently of \(n\).
