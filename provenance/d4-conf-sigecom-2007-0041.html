The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a directed series-parallel graph with common source \(s\), rational edge costs, and rational masses on finitely many sink types, does there exist a path-mass assignment with no positive-mass measurable coalition whose members can all reroute to strictly lower their costs?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

An instance consists of a two-terminal directed series-parallel graph \(G=(V,E)\), oriented from its source \(s\) to its terminal \(t\), with nonnegative rational edge costs \(c_e\); type destinations may be internal vertices.
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) distinct sink types \(t_i\in V\), each with positive mass \(a_i\) as a binary rational, with \(\sum_i a_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Put \(m=|V|+|E|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Represent type \(i\) by an atomless interval of Lebesgue measure \(a_i\). A path-mass assignment is a measurable assignment of one directed \(s\)-to-\(t_i\) path to each individual; arbitrary real path masses and splitting of each type among paths are allowed. All such paths are available; the empty path is allowed when \(t_i=s\).
If \(x_e\) is the total mass using edge \(e\), an individual using path \(P\) incurs cost \(C(P,x)=\sum_{e\in P}c_e/x_e\). At zero load, an edge contributes \(+\infty\) if \(c_e>0\), and zero if \(c_e=0\).
A profitable deviation is a measurable coalition of positive mass measurably assigning its members new paths to their original destinations, while outsiders retain their paths, such that almost every coalition member has strictly lower cost at the resulting loads. A strong equilibrium admits no profitable deviation; assignments are identified up to null sets.

Is deciding whether such a path-mass assignment exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many indivisible players, single-source fair connection games on directed series-parallel graphs always admit a strong equilibrium ([Epstein–Feldman–Mansour](https://cs.tau.ac.il/~mfeldman/papers/EFMj07.pdf)).
This discrete existence guarantee extends to arbitrary nonincreasing per-user edge costs ([Holzman–Monderer](https://holzman.technion.ac.il/files/2015/08/strongeq.pdf)). No result settling the exact atomless decision problem above was located.

Result

Every instance in the question admits a strong equilibrium. Consequently, the existence decision problem is polynomial-time solvable: on the specified domain, the algorithm always returns YES.

The proof transfers the finite-player existence theorem to the atomless model by compactness. The essential step is to show that a profitable coalition in a limiting assignment would yield a profitable coalition in sufficiently fine finite-player approximations.

1. The finite-player theorem and its applicability

We use the following established result:

Finite-player existence theorem. Every finite single-source fair connection game on a directed series-parallel graph, with nonnegative edge costs and all source-to-destination paths available, has a strong equilibrium. Destinations may be internal vertices. This is Theorem 3.4 of [Epstein–Feldman–Mansour](https://cs.tau.ac.il/~mfeldman/papers/EFMj07.pdf).

The graph and path-availability hypotheses are exactly those in the question. We will apply this theorem with finitely many players having the prescribed destinations and appropriately scaled edge costs.

A type whose destination is the source has only the empty path, because the graph is acyclic. Such individuals always pay zero and cannot strictly improve. Corresponding finite players can therefore be omitted when applying the theorem and added back afterward. If every destination is the source, the all-empty assignment already proves the result.

2. Path masses and coalitions

For each type \(i\), let \(\mathcal P_i\) be the set of directed paths from \(s\) to \(t_i\), including the empty path when appropriate. These sets are nonempty and finite: every vertex of the given graph is reachable from its source, and the graph is finite and acyclic.

Represent an assignment by its path masses \(z_{iP}\), satisfying

\(z_{iP}\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{P\in\mathcal P_i}z_{iP}=a_i.\)

Every such vector is realizable by a measurable assignment: partition the interval representing type \(i\) into subintervals of these measures and assign the corresponding paths.

Its edge loads are

\(x_e(z)=\sum_i\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_i:\,e\in P}z_{iP}.\)

Write \(C(P,x)\) for the path cost under the convention in the question. If \(z_{iP}>0\), then every edge of \(P\) has load at least \(z_{iP}\). Hence \(C(P,x(z))\) is finite. Since there are finitely many paths, almost every individual in any assignment has finite cost.

A measurable coalition and its reassignment can be described by finitely many numbers

\(b_{iPQ}\geq 0,\qquad P,Q\in\mathcal P_i,\)

where \(b_{iPQ}\) is the mass of type \(i\) coalition members whose original path is \(P\) and whose new path is \(Q\). Put

\(B_{iP}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal P_i}b_{iPQ}.\)

These numbers necessarily satisfy

\(B_{iP}\leq z_{iP}.\)

Conversely, these inequalities suffice to realize the specified reassignment: atomlessness allows the individuals originally assigned \(P\) to be partitioned into measurable subsets of the required measures.

The resulting edge loads are

\(y_e=x_e(z)+\sum_i\sum_{P,Q\in\mathcal P_i}b_{iPQ}\bigl(\mathbf 1_{\{e\in Q\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{e\in P\}}\bigr).\)

Equivalently, these loads consist of the nonnegative outsider loads plus the coalition’s new loads. In particular, if \(b_{iPQ}>0\), then

\(x_e(z)\geq z_{iP}>0\quad\text{for every }e\in P,\)

and

\(y_e\geq b_{iPQ}>0\quad\text{for every }e\in Q.\)

Thus both the old and new costs of every positive-mass path pair are finite.

A profitable coalition has positive total mass and satisfies

\(C(Q,y)<C(P,x(z))\qquad\text{whenever }b_{iPQ}>0.\)

Indeed, all individuals in a given path pair have the same old and new costs. If that pair had positive mass and failed this inequality, profitability almost everywhere would fail.

3. A convergent sequence of finite strong equilibria

Because the masses are rational, choose a positive integer \(D\) such that \(Da_i\) is an integer for every type. For each positive integer \(k\), set \(N_k=kD\).

Construct a finite fair connection game with \(N_ka_i\) players of type \(i\), each choosing a path in \(\mathcal P_i\), and edge costs

\(\widehat c_e^{(k)}=N_kc_e.\)

For bookkeeping, give each finite player mass \(1/N_k\). If \(n_e\) finite players use edge \(e\), their empirical load is \(x_e=n_e/N_k\). A player using that edge pays

\(\dfrac{\widehat c_e^{(k)}}{n_e}=\dfrac{N_kc_e}{n_e}=\dfrac{c_e}{x_e}.\)

Consequently, finite-player costs agree exactly with the costs evaluated at empirical loads. This equality holds both before and after every finite coalition deviation.

All hypotheses of the finite-player theorem hold: the graph and available paths are unchanged, all players share the source, and the scaled edge costs are nonnegative. Choose a finite strong equilibrium and denote its normalized path masses by \(z^{(k)}\). Each coordinate is an integer multiple of \(1/N_k\), and

\(\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_i}z^{(k)}_{iP}=a_i.\)

The feasible path-mass vectors form the compact, finite-dimensional set

\(\mathcal Z=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}\left\{(z_{iP})_{P\in\mathcal P_i}:z_{iP}\geq0,\ \sum_{P\in\mathcal P_i}z_{iP}=a_i\right\}.\)

Pass to a convergent subsequence and relabel it, retaining \(N_k\to\infty\). Thus

\(z^{(k)}\longrightarrow z\in\mathcal Z.\)

We prove that a measurable assignment realizing \(z\) is a strong equilibrium.

4. A limiting profitable coalition would contradict finite stability

Suppose, for contradiction, that such an assignment admits a profitable coalition. Let \(b_{iPQ}\), \(B_{iP}\), and \(y\) describe it as above.

Approximating the coalition by whole finite players

The initial finite equilibrium may have slightly less mass on a particular path than the limit does. To respect every available supply, define

\(\alpha_k=\min\left(\{1\}\cup\left\{\dfrac{z^{(k)}_{iP}}{B_{iP}}:B_{iP}>0\right\}\right).\)

The coalition has positive mass, so there is at least one positive \(B_{iP}\). For each such row,

\(\dfrac{z^{(k)}_{iP}}{B_{iP}}\longrightarrow\dfrac{z_{iP}}{B_{iP}}\geq1.\)

There are finitely many rows. Therefore \(0\leq\alpha_k\leq1\) and \(\alpha_k\to1\).

Define integer numbers of deviating players by

\(q^{(k)}_{iPQ}=\left\lfloor N_k\alpha_kb_{iPQ}\right\rfloor,\)

and normalized reassignment masses by

\(b^{(k)}_{iPQ}=q^{(k)}_{iPQ}/N_k.\)

For every original type-path pair,

\(\sum_Qq^{(k)}_{iPQ}\leq N_k\alpha_kB_{iP}\leq N_kz^{(k)}_{iP}.\)

The right-hand side is exactly the number of available finite players of that type using that path. Thus we can select disjoint groups of the prescribed sizes, reassign each group to its specified new path, and leave all other players fixed.

Moreover,

\(0\leq\alpha_kb_{iPQ}-b^{(k)}_{iPQ}<1/N_k,\)

so \(b^{(k)}_{iPQ}\to b_{iPQ}\) for every pair. A zero limiting pair never recruits players. Every positive limiting pair recruits at least one player for all sufficiently large \(k\). In particular, the resulting finite coalition is eventually nonempty.

Preserving every strict improvement

Let \(x^{(k)}=x(z^{(k)})\), and let \(y^{(k)}\) be the empirical loads after the constructed finite deviation. Their load formulas give

\(x^{(k)}\longrightarrow x(z),\qquad y^{(k)}\longrightarrow y.\)

Fix a pair with \(b_{iPQ}>0\). As established in Section 2, every edge of \(P\) has positive limiting old load, and every edge of \(Q\) has positive limiting new load.

For an edge of positive cost, the function \(u\mapsto c_e/u\) is continuous at every positive load. For an edge of zero cost, its contribution is identically zero, including at zero load. Hence

\(C(P,x^{(k)})\longrightarrow C(P,x(z)),\)

and

\(C(Q,y^{(k)})\longrightarrow C(Q,y).\)

The limiting inequality is strict:

\(C(Q,y)<C(P,x(z)).\)

It follows that, for all sufficiently large \(k\),

\(C(Q,y^{(k)})<C(P,x^{(k)}).\)

There are only finitely many positive-mass pairs, so one sufficiently large \(k\) makes these inequalities hold simultaneously for every pair that recruits players.

Every member of the constructed finite coalition therefore strictly improves. The equality between finite costs and empirical-load costs proved in Section 3 makes this a profitable deviation in the finite fair connection game. This contradicts the choice of its initial profile as a strong equilibrium.

The contradiction proves that \(z\) admits no profitable atomless coalition. Its measurable realization is the required strong equilibrium.

5. Decision complexity

All valid instances are therefore YES instances. Under the stated instance promise, returning YES takes \(O(1)\) time, independently of \(m,\tau,L\); reading the entire encoding first takes \(O(L)\) time.

The potentially large denominator, the number of paths, and the limiting sequence are used only in the existence proof. The decision algorithm does not construct them.
