The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given two agent types with rational masses μ_t, integral bundles of indivisible good types, rational per-capita supplies q_g, and additive valuations and social impacts, does there exist a distribution of each type’s mass over bundles that uses every supply, maximizes total social impact, and is EF1 between every pair of bundles receiving positive mass?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here \(\tau=2\), with \(\mu_1+\mu_2=1\).
There are \(m\geq1\) good types, with binary rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\geq0\). Type \(t\) has binary-encoded nonnegative integer additive valuations \(v_{tg}\) and social impacts \(s_{tg}\).
An integral bundle is \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\), including the empty bundle; write \(v_t(b)=\sum_g v_{tg}b_g\) and \(s_t(b)=\sum_g s_{tg}b_g\). Supplies constrain aggregate consumption, not individual bundle sizes.
An allocation assigns real masses \(x_{t,b}\geq0\), with \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\) for every good type. Countably supported distributions are allowed.
Its total social impact is \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}s_t(b)\); social impact maximization is over all supply-feasible allocations, without imposing fairness.
Require EF1 for every ordered pair \((t,b),(u,c)\) with \(x_{t,b}>0\) and \(x_{u,c}>0\), including \(t=u\): either \(v_t(b)\geq v_t(c)\), or some \(g\) with \(c_g>0\) satisfies \(v_t(b)\geq v_t(c-e_g)\), where \(e_g\) is the unit vector for one copy of good type \(g\).

Is deciding whether such an EF1, globally social impact maximizing allocation exists in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau=2\), and the total input bit length \(L\).

For finite societies, this existence problem is NP-complete even with two agents and binary social impacts; a pseudopolynomial algorithm exists for every fixed number of agents. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08160v2)
No result settling this exact mass-distribution problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the two positive masses to sum to one, so supplies are measured per capita.
  • Used nonnegative integer valuation and social impact coefficients, matching the discrete domains; all numerical input is binary encoded.
  • Allowed nonnegative rational supplies, empty bundles, and arbitrary nonnegative integral copy counts; imposed supplies only through aggregate consumption.
  • Allowed real allocation masses on the countable integral-bundle domain, without imposing an unspecified finite support bound.
  • Interpreted social impact maximization globally over supply-feasible allocations, before imposing EF1.
  • Applied EF1 to all ordered positive-mass bundle pairs, including pairs within one type; removal deletes one copy, and an empty comparison bundle requires no removal.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for \(\tau=2\). Hardness holds even when \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\), every supply is \(q_g=1/2\), and all social impacts belong to \(\{0,1\}\).

The proof below handles unrestricted bundle multiplicities and countably supported allocations explicitly.

1. Global optimality and EF1

For each good \(g\), let \(S_g=\max\{s_{1g},s_{2g}\}\). Every supply-feasible allocation satisfies

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{SI}(x)\leq\sum_g S_gq_g.\)

This bound is attainable. Assign each good to a type attaining \(S_g\). Within that type, realize its required conditional mean \(q_g/\mu_t\) by mixing the floor and ceiling of this number. Taking the product of these finitely supported coordinate distributions gives a supply-feasible allocation attaining the bound.

Moreover, the difference from this bound equals

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,b,g}x_{t,b}b_g(S_g-s_{tg}).\)

All summands are nonnegative. Consequently, an allocation is globally social impact maximizing if and only if every supported bundle uses only goods for which its recipient type maximizes social impact. These conclusions also hold for countable support: the sums are nonnegative, and finite supplies bound the objective.

For later use, define

\(\displaystyle h_t(c)=\max\bigl(\{v_{tg}:c_g>0\}\cup\{0\}\bigr).\)

The EF1 condition for an ordered pair \((t,b),(u,c)\) is exactly

\(\displaystyle v_t(b)\geq v_t(c)-h_t(c).\)

For a nonempty bundle, removing a copy with maximum value gives the smallest possible remaining value. For the empty bundle, the displayed inequality holds automatically.

2. An encoding that excludes repeated copies

We reduce from the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete PARTITION problem, also used in the supplied [finite-agent reduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08160v2). Its input consists of positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), with

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^n a_i=2A,\)

and asks whether some subset sums to \(A\). Restricting to even total preserves hardness: an odd-total instance can be mapped to the fixed no-instance \(1,3\).

Put \(a_{\max}=\max_i a_i\), and define

\(\displaystyle B=2n(a_{\max}+1)+2,\qquad H=B^{n+1}.\)

For each index \(i\), introduce two positive integer weights

\(\displaystyle w_i^+=H+B^i+a_i,\qquad w_i^-=H+B^i,\)

and set

\(\displaystyle T=nH+\sum_{i=1}^n B^i+A.\)

Their total is

\(\displaystyle \sum_i(w_i^++w_i^-)=2T.\)

Encoding lemma. Nonnegative integers \(c_i^+,c_i^-\) satisfy

\(\displaystyle \sum_i(w_i^+c_i^++w_i^-c_i^-)=T\)

if and only if

\(\displaystyle c_i^++c_i^-=1\quad\text{for every }i,\qquad \sum_i a_ic_i^+=A.\)

Proof. Write

\(\displaystyle N=\sum_i(c_i^++c_i^-),\quad k_i=c_i^++c_i^-,\quad P=\sum_i a_ic_i^+.\)

The assumed equation becomes

\(\displaystyle NH+\sum_i k_iB^i+P=nH+\sum_i B^i+A.\)

Because \(A<B\), the target’s terms below \(H\) form a valid base-\(B\) expansion and satisfy

\(\displaystyle \sum_i B^i+A<H.\)

Each weight is at least \(H\), so \(N\leq n\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle k_i\leq n<B,\qquad P\leq na_{\max}<B,\qquad N<B.\)

Thus the left side also has a base-\(B\) expansion with no carries: its leading digit is \(N\), its indexed digits are \(k_i\), and its units digit is \(P\). Uniqueness of base-\(B\) expansion gives \(N=n\), every \(k_i=1\), and \(P=A\).

Conversely, these conditions give the required weighted sum directly. \(\square\)

In particular, an integral bundle of encoded value \(T\) determines a PARTITION solution, even though repeated copies were initially permitted.

3. Reduction to the mass-distribution problem

Create \(m=2n+2\) goods: common goods \(g_i^+,g_i^-\), and two anchor goods \(d_1,d_2\). Set

\(\displaystyle \mu_1=\mu_2=\frac12,\qquad q_g=\frac12\quad\text{for every good }g,\qquad C=2T+1.\)

The valuations and social impacts are:

| Good | \(v_{1g}\) | \(v_{2g}\) | \(s_{1g}\) | \(s_{2g}\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(g_i^+\) | \(w_i^+\) | \(w_i^+\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| \(g_i^-\) | \(w_i^-\) | \(w_i^-\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |
| \(d_1\) | \(0\) | \(C\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \(d_2\) | \(C\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(1\) |

Since every common weight is positive and their total is \(2T\), each common weight is strictly smaller than \(C\).

3.1. A fair optimal allocation yields a partition

Suppose an EF1, globally maximizing allocation exists.

By Section 1, only type \(1\) receives \(d_1\), and only type \(2\) receives \(d_2\). For any bundle \(b\), write its common-good value as

\(\displaystyle z(b)=\sum_i(w_i^+b_{g_i^+}+w_i^-b_{g_i^-}).\)

For a supported type-\(1\) bundle \(b\), let \(k_1(b)=b_{d_1}\). For a supported type-\(2\) bundle \(c\), let \(k_2(c)=c_{d_2}\). Anchor supplies imply

\(\displaystyle \sum_bx_{1,b}k_1(b)=\frac12,\qquad \sum_cx_{2,c}k_2(c)=\frac12.\)

Thus each type has at least one supported bundle containing its anchor.

Take any supported \(b,c\) with \(k_1(b)\geq1\) and \(k_2(c)\geq1\). From type \(1\)’s perspective, the most valuable removable copy in \(c\) is its anchor, worth \(C\). EF1 therefore gives

\(\displaystyle z(b)\geq z(c)+(k_2(c)-1)C.\)

The reverse ordered comparison gives

\(\displaystyle z(c)\geq z(b)+(k_1(b)-1)C.\)

Adding yields

\(\displaystyle 0\geq(k_1(b)+k_2(c)-2)C.\)

Both anchor counts are positive integers, so both equal \(1\). Pairing any bundle containing an anchor with an anchor-containing bundle of the other type shows that no supported bundle contains two or more anchors.

Each anchor count is consequently either \(0\) or \(1\). Its conditional mean within its owner type is \(1\), because both anchor supply and owner mass equal \(1/2\). Hence every supported bundle contains exactly one copy of its own anchor.

For arbitrary supported bundles \(b\) of type \(1\) and \(c\) of type \(2\), the two EF1 inequalities now reduce to

\(\displaystyle z(b)\geq z(c),\qquad z(c)\geq z(b).\)

All supported bundles therefore have one common value, say \(\lambda\). Supply feasibility gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}z(b)=\sum_i\frac{w_i^++w_i^-}{2}=T.\)

Since total population mass is \(1\), the left side equals \(\lambda\). Thus every supported bundle has common-good value \(T\).

The encoding lemma applies to any such bundle and yields a subset of the original numbers summing to \(A\).

This argument uses no finite-support assumption.

3.2. A partition yields a fair optimal allocation

Conversely, suppose \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in J}a_i=A\).

Let \(p\) contain one copy of \(g_i^+\) for \(i\in J\), and one copy of \(g_i^-\) otherwise. Let \(\bar p\) make the opposite choice at every index. Then

\(\displaystyle z(p)=z(\bar p)=T,\)

and together these bundles contain exactly one copy of every common good.

Assign mass \(1/4\) to each of

\(\displaystyle (1,p+e_{d_1}),\quad(1,\bar p+e_{d_1}),\quad(2,p+e_{d_2}),\quad(2,\bar p+e_{d_2}).\)

Each type has mass \(1/2\), and every good has aggregate consumption \(1/2\).

Within a type, all bundles have own value \(T\), so there is no envy. Across types, the compared bundle has value \(T+C\); removing its anchor leaves value \(T\). Thus every ordered comparison is EF1.

Every good is allocated to a social-impact maximizing type, so the allocation is globally maximizing.

The reduction is therefore correct in both directions.

4. Polynomial-size certificates

We now prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), including for general inputs admitting countably supported solutions.

Define

\(\displaystyle W_t=\sum_gv_{tg}q_g,\qquad M_t=\max_gv_{tg},\qquad U_t=\left\lfloor\frac{W_t}{\mu_t}\right\rfloor+M_t.\)

Suppose a fair optimal allocation exists. The own-type conditional mean valuation is at most \(W_t/\mu_t\). Since supported valuations are nonnegative integers, their minimum \(a_t\) exists and satisfies

\(\displaystyle a_t\leq\left\lfloor W_t/\mu_t\right\rfloor.\)

Comparing a type-\(t\) bundle attaining this minimum against any supported bundle \(c\), including bundles of the same type, gives

\(\displaystyle v_t(c)-h_t(c)\leq a_t.\)

Because \(h_t(c)\leq M_t\), every supported bundle satisfies

\(\displaystyle v_t(c)\leq U_t.\)

Consequently, if some type values good \(g\) positively, then its multiplicity in every supported bundle is at most \(\max\{U_1,U_2\}\).

4.1. Goods valued zero by both types

Let \(Z=\{g:v_{1g}=v_{2g}=0\}\). Project the allocation onto goods outside \(Z\), aggregating masses with identical projected bundles. The multiplicity bounds make this projected support finite.

For each \(g\in Z\), choose a social-impact maximizing owner \(t(g)\). Within that type, independently append either

\(\displaystyle \left\lfloor q_g/\mu_{t(g)}\right\rfloor\quad\text{or}\quad\left\lceil q_g/\mu_{t(g)}\right\rceil\)

copies, with probabilities giving conditional mean \(q_g/\mu_{t(g)}\). Append zero copies to the other type.

This restores supplies and preserves optimality. Both types’ valuations and removable maximum values remain unchanged, so EF1 is preserved.

We have obtained a finite fair optimal allocation whose bundle coordinates are bounded by

\(\displaystyle K=\max\left\{1,U_1,U_2,\max_{g,t}\left\lceil q_g/\mu_t\right\rceil\right\}.\)

Under standard binary encoding of rational numerators and denominators,

\(\displaystyle \log K=O(L+\log(m+1)).\)

Indeed, \(\mu_t\geq2^{-L}\), while each valuation and supply is at most \(2^L\), giving \(U_t\leq m2^{3L}+2^L\).

4.2. Sparsification and rational masses

Represent each supported pair by the integer column

\(\displaystyle a(t,b)=(\mathbf 1_{t=1},\mathbf 1_{t=2},b_1,\ldots,b_m).\)

Its masses satisfy

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}a(t,b)=(\mu_1,\mu_2,q_1,\ldots,q_m).\)

If these columns are linearly dependent, choose a nonzero dependence \(\delta\). The first two rows imply \(\sum_i\delta_i=0\), so \(\delta\) has both signs. Subtracting

\(\displaystyle \left(\min_{\delta_i>0}x_i/\delta_i\right)\delta\)

preserves all balances and nonnegativity while eliminating at least one positive mass. Repeating leaves at most \(m+2\) linearly independent columns. Restricting support preserves both EF1 and optimality.

Let their number be \(r\leq m+2\). Select \(r\) rows forming a nonsingular integer matrix \(A'\). Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses and supplies; then \(D\leq2^L\).

Cramer’s rule shows that all remaining masses are rational, with reduced denominators dividing \(D|\det A'|\). Since matrix entries are at most \(K\),

\(\displaystyle |\det A'|\leq r!K^r.\)

Each mass is at most \(1\), so its numerator and denominator require

\(\displaystyle O\bigl(L+r(\log K+\log r)\bigr)=O\bigl(m(L+\log(m+1))\bigr)\)

bits.

Thus a yes-instance has a certificate consisting of at most \(m+2\) bundles and rational masses, with total length

\(\displaystyle O\bigl(m^2(L+\log(m+1))\bigr).\)

A verifier checks exact mass and supply balances, social-impact eligibility of every consumed good, and every ordered EF1 comparison in polynomial time. Hence the problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

5. Running times and conclusion

For the reduction, let \(\ell\) be the PARTITION input length. The constructed instance has

\(\displaystyle \tau=2,\qquad m=2n+2,\qquad L=O(n^2\log B).\)

Computing successive powers of \(B\) and writing the instance takes polynomial time; schoolbook arithmetic gives, for example, \(O(\ell+n^2(\log B)^2)\) bit operations.

For general instances with \(\tau=2\):

  • Certificate verification and nondeterministic decision take \(\operatorname{poly}(m,L)\) time.
  • Exhaustively enumerating the bounded certificates gives deterministic time \(2^{O(m^2(L+\log(m+1)))}\operatorname{poly}(m,L)\).
  • A deterministic algorithm polynomial in \(m,L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

Therefore the stated mass-distribution existence problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.
