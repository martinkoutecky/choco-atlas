The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given applicant types \(I\), position types \(J\), rational masses \(\alpha_i,\beta_j\), strict rankings over opposite-side types and being unmatched, and a feasible baseline mass matching \(q^0\), form the auxiliary market whose acceptable pairs \(E(q^0)\) are exactly the type pairs whose members prefer one another to their baseline assignments, preserving their rankings among those pairs. For feasible mass matchings \(x\) with \(\sum_j x_{ij}\leq\alpha_i\), \(\sum_i x_{ij}\leq\beta_j\), and \(x_{ij}=0\) outside \(E(q^0)\), determine the minimum and maximum of \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E(q^0)}x_{ij}\) over stable \(x\), and return flows attaining both extrema. Stability means there is no pair in \(E(q^0)\) for which positive mass on each side is unmatched or assigned to a less-preferred type.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I,J\) be disjoint, nonempty sets of applicant and position types, with strict rankings \(\succ_i,\succ_j\) over opposite-side types and the unmatched option \(\varnothing\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau=|I|+|J|\) types with positive mass, each mass \(\alpha_i,\beta_j\) encoded as a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Masses need not sum to one or have equal totals across sides. Put \(m=\max\{|I|,|J|\}\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The input includes a nonnegative rational baseline \(q^0\) with \(\sum_jq^0_{ij}\leq\alpha_i\), \(\sum_iq^0_{ij}\leq\beta_j\), and positive entries only on mutually individually rational pairs.
For any feasible flow \(q\), define \(a_{ij}(q)=\alpha_i-\sum_kq_{ik}+\sum_{k:j\succ_i k}q_{ik}\) and \(b_{ij}(q)=\beta_j-\sum_hq_{hj}+\sum_{h:i\succ_j h}q_{hj}\).
Set \(E(q^0)=\{(i,j):j\succ_i\varnothing,\ i\succ_j\varnothing,\ a_{ij}(q^0)>0,\ b_{ij}(q^0)>0\}\), and require \(E(q^0)\neq\varnothing\). Auxiliary preferences preserve the rankings on these pairs and place every such partner above being unmatched.
An auxiliary flow has real entries \(x_{ij}\geq0\), row sums at most \(\alpha_i\), column sums at most \(\beta_j\), and zero entries outside \(E(q^0)\). It is stable exactly when \(a_{ij}(x)b_{ij}(x)=0\) for every \((i,j)\in E(q^0)\).
Is computing the minimum and maximum of \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E(q^0)}x_{ij}\) over stable auxiliary flows, together with rational flows attaining both, possible in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this problem NP-hard?
Discrete: permissible collections are precisely stable matchings in the auxiliary market; all have equal cardinality, computable by Gale–Shapley. [Biermann](https://ratio.huji.ac.il/files/dp575.pdf).
Continuous: the exact auxiliary problem is already covered by stable allocation: a strongly polynomial algorithm exists, and every stable allocation gives each type the same total matched mass, so both extrema are polynomial-time computable. [Baïou–Balinski, corrected version](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Moreover, the minimum and maximum coincide: every stable auxiliary flow gives each supported type the same total matched mass. A single rational stable flow therefore attains both extrema.

The proof uses the corrected Baïou–Balinski stable-allocation theorem, with an explicit reduction and a bit-complexity check.

1. Exact reduction to stable allocation

Write \(E=E(q^0)\). For each auxiliary feasible flow \(x\), define the cumulative allocations

\( \displaystyle R_{ij}(x)=x_{ij}+\sum_{k:\,k\succ_i j}x_{ik},\qquad C_{ij}(x)=x_{ij}+\sum_{h:\,h\succ_j i}x_{hj}. \)

Strictness of the rankings partitions each row into partners better than, equal to, and worse than \(j\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle a_{ij}(x)=\alpha_i-R_{ij}(x),\qquad b_{ij}(x)=\beta_j-C_{ij}(x). \)

Both quantities are nonnegative by feasibility. Thus the required stability condition is equivalent to

\[ \displaystyle R_{ij}(x)=\alpha_i\quad\text{or}\quad C_{ij}(x)=\beta_j,\qquad (i,j)\in E. \tag{1} \]

We now construct a stable-allocation instance. Its row quotas are \(\alpha_i\), its column quotas are \(\beta_j\), and its edge capacities are

\( \displaystyle u_{ij}=\begin{cases}\alpha_i+\beta_j,&(i,j)\in E,\\0,&(i,j)\notin E.\end{cases} \)

Preferences on positive-capacity edges are the prescribed auxiliary preferences.

The stable-allocation definition requires, on each edge, that

\[ \displaystyle x_{ij}<u_{ij}\ \Longrightarrow\ R_{ij}(x)=\alpha_i\ \text{or}\ C_{ij}(x)=\beta_j. \tag{2} \]

This is the definition in Section 1 of the [corrected Baïou–Balinski paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

The feasible sets coincide. Indeed, zero capacities force zero entries outside \(E\), while on \(E\) the added capacity constraint is redundant because

\( \displaystyle x_{ij}\leq\min\{\alpha_i,\beta_j\}<\alpha_i+\beta_j=u_{ij}. \)

The strict inequality follows from positivity of both supported masses. Hence the antecedent in (2) always holds on \(E\), making (2) exactly (1). On zero-capacity edges its antecedent never holds.

Therefore the stable allocations of the constructed instance are precisely the stable auxiliary flows. The baseline \(q^0\) enters only through the construction of \(E\).

2. Existence and invariance of matched mass

We use two results from the corrected paper:

  • Section 2 gives a strongly polynomial inductive algorithm producing a stable allocation for arbitrary positive real row and column quotas and nonnegative real edge capacities.
  • Lemma 8 states that any two stable allocations of the same instance have identical row totals and identical column totals.

Both results apply here: the supported masses are positive, the edge capacities are nonnegative, and preferences are strict. Neither result assumes equal aggregate quotas or any nondegeneracy condition. See [Baïou–Balinski, Section 2 and Lemma 8](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

In particular, stable auxiliary flows exist. If \(x,y\) are any two such flows, the reduction and Lemma 8 give

\( \displaystyle \sum_jx_{ij}=\sum_jy_{ij}\quad(i\in I),\qquad \sum_ix_{ij}=\sum_iy_{ij}\quad(j\in J). \)

Summing the row equalities yields

\( \displaystyle \sum_{(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}y_{ij}. \)

Thus the objective is constant over the entire set of stable auxiliary flows, including flows with irrational entries.

3. Polynomial bit complexity and rational witnesses

First compute \(E\) exactly. There are at most \(|I||J|\leq m^2\) candidate pairs. Each membership test uses preference comparisons and rational sums from \(q^0\). A direct implementation requires only polynomially many operations. Exact rational arithmetic is polynomial in \(L\): a product of the input denominators provides a common denominator whose bit length is at most their total encoding length.

For the stable-allocation computation, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all masses. Define integers

\( \displaystyle A_i=D\alpha_i,\qquad B_j=D\beta_j. \)

Their bit lengths are \(O(L)\). Construct the scaled instance with quotas \(A_i,B_j\) and capacities

\( \displaystyle U_{ij}=\begin{cases}A_i+B_j,&(i,j)\in E,\\0,&(i,j)\notin E.\end{cases} \)

Positive scaling preserves feasibility and all stability equalities, so dividing a stable allocation of this instance by \(D\) gives a stable auxiliary flow.

Run the inductive algorithm on the scaled instance. Its numerical updates use sums, differences, minima, and comparisons, as specified in [Section 2 of the corrected paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302). Starting from integer data, these operations produce integer allocations.

For completeness, its strongly polynomial arithmetic bound also gives polynomial bit complexity here. Let \(P\) be its polynomially bounded number of elementary operations. Starting from integers of \(O(L)\) bits, each addition or subtraction increases the maximum bit length by at most one; minima and comparisons do not increase it. Hence every intermediate integer has \(O(L+P)\) bits. Every operation therefore takes polynomial bit time.

The instance has \(\tau\) agents and at most \(m^2\) positive-capacity edges, so the complete computation takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). No expansion into individual units of mass is needed.

Let \(X\) be the resulting integer stable allocation and put \(x^*_{ij}=X_{ij}/D\). This is a rational stable auxiliary flow of polynomial encoding length.

4. Computing both extrema

Compute

\( \displaystyle T=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}x^*_{ij}. \)

By the invariance proved above,

\( \displaystyle \min_{x\text{ stable auxiliary}}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}=T=\max_{x\text{ stable auxiliary}}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}x_{ij}. \)

Output \(T\) as both extremal values and \(x^*\) as both attaining flows. This settles the stated problem, including unequal side totals, arbitrary binary rational masses, and potentially degenerate instances.
