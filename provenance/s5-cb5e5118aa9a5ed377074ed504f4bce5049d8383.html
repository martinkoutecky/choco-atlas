The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given public rational masses \(\mu_t\) for \(\tau\) bidder cohorts, public item-type supplies \(q_e\) per unit population, and item eligibility sets \(I_e\subseteq T\) of size at most \(k\), each cohort representative privately holds its cohort’s XOS valuation \(v_t\). What is the communication complexity, as a function of \(\tau\), \(|E|\), and the input bit length, of outputting an optimal mass allocation \(x_{t,S}\), where each unit of cohort mass receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq E_t\), \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t:e\in E_t}\sum_{S\ni e}x_{t,S}\le q_e\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a public list \(T\) of \(\tau\) cohorts with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible valuations.
There are \(m=|E|\ge1\) item types, public binary-rational supplies \(q_e\ge0\) per unit population, and public eligibility sets \(\varnothing\ne I_e\subseteq T\), with \(|I_e|\le k\) and \(2\le k\le\tau\). Write \(E_t=\{e:t\in I_e\}\).
Representative \(t\) privately knows a normalized, monotone XOS valuation \(v_t:2^{E_t}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), shared by its entire cohort. Let \(L\) be the public input's total bit length plus the maximum bit length of any valuation value; no polynomial bound on the valuation's full description is assumed.
A feasible allocation consists of rational masses \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), for \(S\subseteq E_t\), satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq E_t}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\subseteq E_t:e\in S}x_{t,S}\le q_e\). Each individual receives an integral bundle, including possibly the empty bundle.
Define \(W(x)=\sum_t\sum_{S\subseteq E_t}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_xW(x)\) over feasible mass allocations. A factor \(\rho\ge1\) means \(W(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}/\rho\).
Use deterministic number-in-hand blackboard protocols with unrestricted local computation and no truthfulness requirement. The auctioneer outputs an allocation, represented by its nonzero entries, using only public data and the transcript; communication is the worst-case total number of transmitted bits. Express communication and any running-time bounds in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

What is the best welfare approximation factor achievable with communication polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\)? In particular, for fixed \(k\ge2\) and \(0<\varepsilon<\rho_k-1\), where \(\rho_k=[1-(1-1/k)^k]^{-1}\), is factor \(\rho_k-\varepsilon\) achievable with polynomial communication, or does it require exponential communication?

For discrete XOS auctions with at most \(k\) eligible bidders per item, factor \(\rho_k\) is achievable with polynomial communication, whereas factor \(\rho_k-\varepsilon\) requires exponential communication, even for randomized and nondeterministic protocols. [Source](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol364-stacs2026/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.27/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.27.pdf)
For the related fractional problem with unit bidder and item capacities, an optimal configuration-LP allocation can be obtained with polynomial communication using demand queries. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06585v1)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total cohort mass to one because supplies are specified per unit population.
  • Specified nonempty eligibility sets, nonnegative rational supplies, and the nontrivial parameter range 2 <= k <= tau.
  • Carried over normalized, monotone, nonnegative valuations and imposed binary-rational values to make bit communication well-defined.
  • Defined L using public input length and maximum valuation-value precision, rather than the potentially exponential full private valuation description.
  • Specified total mass-weighted social welfare and measured approximation against the optimal feasible mass allocation.
  • Adopted deterministic blackboard communication with unrestricted local computation and no truthfulness requirement, matching the discrete upper-bound setting.

Result

The best achievable factor is \(1\): exact welfare maximization admits a deterministic protocol with communication polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\). Consequently, factor \(\rho_k-\varepsilon\) is achievable with polynomial communication for every \(k,\varepsilon\) in the question.

The reason is that the permitted allocation is already a configuration-LP solution. Each cohort may split its mass among several integral bundles; no rounding to one bundle per cohort is required. The following proof also establishes exact rational recovery and polynomial output size.

1. The primal and dual programs

Put \(d=m+\tau\). We may replace each supply by \(\min\{q_e,1\}\): every feasible allocation consumes at most \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) units of any item.

The welfare problem is exactly the finite linear program

\( \displaystyle \max_x \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq E_t}v_t(S)x_{t,S}, \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq E_t}x_{t,S}=\mu_t \quad(t\in T), \)

\( \displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq E_t\\e\in S}}x_{t,S}\le q_e \quad(e\in E), \qquad x_{t,S}\ge0. \)

Although this program can have exponentially many variables, it has only \(d\) mass and supply constraints. It is feasible by allocating every cohort the empty bundle. Its feasible region is compact, since \(0\le x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\). Thus its optimum is attained.

Introduce a dual variable \(\alpha_t\) for each cohort equality and \(p_e\ge0\) for each supply inequality. The dual is

\( \displaystyle \min_{\alpha,p}\sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+\sum_e q_ep_e, \)

subject to

\( \displaystyle \alpha_t+\sum_{e\in S}p_e\ge v_t(S) \quad(t\in T,\ S\subseteq E_t). \)

Initially \(\alpha_t\) is unrestricted. The empty-bundle constraint, together with normalization \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\), implies \(\alpha_t\ge0\). Finite-dimensional LP strong duality applies, so the primal and dual optimal values coincide.

For later use, let \(M=2^L\), which bounds every valuation value. The dual can be restricted to the public box

\( \displaystyle 0\le\alpha_t\le M,\qquad 0\le p_e\le M \)

without changing its optimum. Indeed, clip every coordinate of a feasible dual solution to \(M\). A bundle constraint involving a clipped coordinate remains satisfied because that coordinate alone contributes \(M\ge v_t(S)\); every other constraint is unchanged. The objective cannot increase because all its coefficients are nonnegative.

The same clipping argument applies if only a subset of the bundle constraints is retained.

2. Exact separation uses polynomial communication

At a candidate dual point satisfying the public bounds, representative \(t\) computes a demand bundle

\( \displaystyle S_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{S\subseteq E_t} \left(v_t(S)-\sum_{e\in S}p_e\right). \)

Use a fixed public ordering to break ties. The representative transmits \(S_t\) and the exact rational value \(v_t(S_t)\).

The auctioneer then checks whether

\( \displaystyle \alpha_t+\sum_{e\in S_t}p_e\ge v_t(S_t). \)

If this fails, the reply supplies a violated dual inequality. If it holds, maximality of \(S_t\) proves that every bundle constraint belonging to cohort \(t\) holds. Querying all representatives therefore implements an exact separation oracle.

Demand computation is local and may be expensive, but unrestricted local computation is explicitly permitted. No succinct XOS representation or efficient demand algorithm is needed.

Encode \(S_t\) by its characteristic vector in the publicly ordered set \(E_t\). Since

\( \displaystyle \sum_t|E_t|=\sum_e|I_e|\le km, \)

one complete separation round communicates

\( \displaystyle O\bigl(km+\tau(L+1)\bigr) \)

bits of replies.

Moreover, the candidate point and query schedule can be computed by everyone from public data and the preceding transcript, using a fixed deterministic LP algorithm. Consequently, communicating the price vectors is unnecessary.

This is the demand-separation mechanism used for configuration LPs in the cited fractional work; here the cohort masses and supplies simply change the dual objective coefficients. [Qiu–Weinberg–Zhang, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06585v1#S3.SS2)

3. Exact recovery of a sparse primal optimum

Finding a dual optimum alone would not meet the requested output condition. We therefore justify recovery of an actual allocation.

Rational LP oracle theorem used

We use the standard exact rational ellipsoid theorem, including its certificate version: a rational LP in a known bounded box can be optimized exactly using polynomially many exact separation calls, polynomial-size rational queries, and polynomial public computation, measured in dimension and inequality encoding length. An infeasible system can likewise be certified using polynomially many inequalities returned by the oracle, together with its explicit constraints.

Recording those inequalities permits primal recovery by solving a restricted primal LP. This is the standard configuration-LP recovery method described in [Roughgarden, Section 8.3](https://www.nowpublishers.com/article/DownloadSummary/TCS-085).

Here the dimension is \(d\); every bundle inequality has coefficients in \(\{0,1\}\) and a right-hand side of at most \(L\) bits. The box and objective also have polynomial encoding length. Thus the theorem’s hypotheses hold.

For completeness, the following argument explains why the recovered allocation can be exactly optimal.

A uniform denominator bound

Consider any restriction of the primal that includes every empty-bundle variable. It remains feasible and compact.

Add one slack variable for each supply constraint. Its constraint matrix has \(d\) rows, entries in \(\{0,1\}\), and full row rank: the empty-bundle columns provide the cohort identity columns, while the slack columns provide the item identity columns.

An optimal basic feasible solution therefore uses at most \(d\) positive variables, including slacks. Let \(B\) be its nonsingular basis matrix. Hadamard’s inequality gives

\( \displaystyle 1\le|\det B|\le d^{d/2}. \)

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of the public masses and capped supplies. Because their total encoding length is at most \(L\), we have \(Q\le2^L\). Cramer’s rule shows that all basic coordinates have a common denominator dividing \(Q|\det B|\).

At most \(d\) bundle variables contribute to welfare. Multiplying additionally by the denominators of their valuation values yields a welfare denominator at most

\( \displaystyle 2^{(d+1)L}d^{d/2}. \)

Hence there is a publicly computable integer

\( \displaystyle H=O\bigl(d(L+\log(d+1))\bigr) \)

such that the optimal welfare of every such restricted primal, including the full primal, has reduced denominator at most \(2^H\).

Two distinct rational numbers with denominators at most \(2^H\) differ by at least \(2^{-2H}\).

Recovering sufficient bundle variables

First optimize the boxed dual exactly, obtaining its value \(z^*=\mathrm{OPT}\). Set

\( \displaystyle \delta=2^{-2H-2}. \)

Run the certificate version of exact LP feasibility on the boxed dual with the additional inequality

\( \displaystyle \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t+\sum_e q_ep_e\le z^*-\delta. \)

This system is infeasible by the definition of \(z^*\). Its certificate uses polynomially many bundle inequalities.

Let \(J\) contain the corresponding bundle variables and every empty-bundle variable. Form the primal restricted to \(J\), and denote its optimum by \(z_J\).

The certificate proves that the restricted boxed dual has optimum strictly greater than \(z^*-\delta\). Clipping preserves the optimum of this restricted dual, as established above. Strong duality for the restricted primal therefore gives

\( \displaystyle z^*-\delta<z_J\le z^*. \)

Both values have denominators at most \(2^H\). The denominator gap bound forces \(z_J=z^*\).

Thus the auctioneer can solve the polynomial-size restricted primal and output an optimal basic feasible solution. Its nonzero allocation entries satisfy

\( \displaystyle |\{(t,S):x_{t,S}>0\}|\le m+\tau. \)

Cramer’s rule also bounds each output mass’s encoding length by \(O(L+d\log(d+1))\). Every selected bundle and its valuation value is known from public data or the transcript. The final allocation therefore satisfies the auctioneer’s information restriction.

4. Communication and running time

Let \(R(d,L)\) denote the polynomial number of separation rounds needed for exact optimization and the recovery certificate above. Total communication is bounded by

\( \displaystyle O\!\left(\tau\log(L+1) +R(m+\tau,L)\bigl(km+\tau(L+1)\bigr)\right) =\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L). \)

The first term handles a valuation-length bound if none is initially public: each representative can announce the maximum encoding length among its finitely many valuation values. This costs \(O(\tau\log(L+1))\) bits and can be computed locally.

The output itself has polynomial encoding length and at most \(m+\tau\) nonzero entries. Counting transmission of that output would preserve the communication bound.

The auctioneer’s computation is polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\), and the protocol makes polynomially many exact demand queries. With efficient demand access, the whole computation is polynomial. Without a specified valuation representation, no polynomial local running-time claim follows.

With unit-cost value access, representatives can instead enumerate bundles. This gives the explicit bound

\( \displaystyle \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L)\sum_t2^{|E_t|} \le \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,k,L)\,2^m \)

for value evaluations and associated arithmetic. Polynomial communication therefore does not depend on polynomial local computation.

5. Why the discrete lower bound does not transfer

Set \(\mu_t=1/\tau\) and \(q_e=1/\tau\), and define \(y_{t,S}=\tau x_{t,S}\). The resulting constraints are

\( \displaystyle \sum_S y_{t,S}=1,\qquad \sum_t\sum_{S\ni e}y_{t,S}\le1. \)

These are the unit-capacity configuration-LP constraints. They permit fractional \(y_{t,S}\). A discrete allocation additionally requires each bidder to select a single bundle.

The cited discrete approximation barrier concerns this latter requirement. Its proof therefore cannot establish the proposed barrier for cohort-mass allocations. [Christodoulou–Koutsoupias–Kovács–Vlachos, Section 5](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol364-stacs2026/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.27/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.27.pdf)

A small XOS example makes the distinction concrete. Take two cohorts of mass \(1/2\), four items \(a,b,c,d\), supply \(1/2\) for each item, and eligibility for both cohorts. Define

\( \displaystyle v_1(S)=\max\{|S\cap\{a,b\}|,\ |S\cap\{c,d\}|\}, \)

\( \displaystyle v_2(S)=\max\{|S\cap\{a,c\}|,\ |S\cap\{b,d\}|\}. \)

Both valuations are normalized, monotone XOS: each displayed term is an additive clause.

Allocate mass \(1/4\) to each of cohort \(1\)’s two displayed pairs and mass \(1/4\) to each of cohort \(2\)’s two pairs. Every item consumes exactly \(1/2\), and welfare is \(2\). This is optimal because every individual’s value is at most \(2\).

In the corresponding discrete auction, welfare \(4\) would require one full row pair and one full column pair, which always intersect. The discrete optimum is \(3\), attained, for example, by giving \(\{a,b\}\) to bidder \(1\) and \(\{c,d\}\) to bidder \(2\).

Thus integral bundles for individuals do not force integral configuration variables for cohorts.

The protocol above actually works for arbitrary normalized nonnegative rational valuations; XOS is unnecessary. Since approximation factors are restricted to be at least \(1\), exact optimization settles the best-factor question.
