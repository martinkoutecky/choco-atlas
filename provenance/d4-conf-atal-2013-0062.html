The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates, a target \(c\), a rational coalition mass \(\alpha\), and a distribution \(\nu\) over \(\tau\) nonmanipulator rankings with total mass \(1-\alpha\), does there exist a rational assignment \(y_\pi \geq 0\) of coalition mass to rankings \(\pi\), with \(\sum_\pi y_\pi=\alpha\), such that \(c\) is the unique Schulze winner under the resulting pairwise margins and path strengths?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) candidates, \(c\in C\), and \(\mathcal R(C)\) the set of strict total rankings of \(C\). The input includes a binary rational \(0<\alpha\leq1\) and nonmanipulator masses \(\nu_\pi\geq0\) totaling \(1-\alpha\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here types are rankings, and \(\tau\) is the support size, not \(m!\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The coalition may assign rational masses \(y_\pi\geq0\) to any rankings \(\pi\in\mathcal R(C)\), subject to \(\sum_\pi y_\pi=\alpha\). Set \(q_\pi=\nu_\pi+y_\pi\), taking unlisted nonmanipulator masses as zero.
For distinct candidates define the margin \(w(a,b)=\sum_{\pi:a\succ_\pi b}q_\pi-\sum_{\pi:b\succ_\pi a}q_\pi\). Use the complete directed graph, retaining zero and negative margins.
Define \(S(a,b)=\max_{p:a\leadsto b}\min_{(u,v)\in p}w(u,v)\), where the maximum ranges over simple directed paths. The winning set is \(W=\{a\in C:\forall b\neq a,\ S(a,b)\geq S(b,a)\}\); success means \(W=\{c\}\), with no tie-breaking.

Is deciding whether such a rational coalition assignment exists in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete unique-winner unweighted coalitional manipulation is polynomial-time solvable for any positive number of manipulators ([Gaspers et al., 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0844)).
Later work proves polynomial-time solvability for positive integer voter weights, in both winner models with unbounded candidates and manipulators ([Müller–Kosub, 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.09226)).
No result explicitly treating this divisible rational-mass decision problem was located.

  • Allowed any nonempty candidate set, including the one-candidate boundary case.

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The key fact is stronger than polynomial solvability of integer weighted manipulation: whenever manipulation succeeds, it also succeeds with all manipulators using the same ranking. This implies that allowing rational division of the coalition does not enlarge the set of successful instances.

1. The integer weighted result used

We use the following established result.

Müller–Kosub theorem and unanimity property. For Schulze elections with positive integer voter weights, constructive coalitional manipulation in the unique-winner model is polynomial-time solvable. Moreover, if a coalition with prescribed positive integer weights can make a candidate the unique winner, then it can do so with every coalition member submitting the same ranking.

The decision statement is Theorem 2; the unanimity property follows from Algorithm 2 and is explicitly stated in footnote 5 of [Müller–Kosub, *A Note on the Complexity of Manipulating Weighted Schulze Voting*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.09226).

Their election definition matches the present question: the graph is complete, edge weights are pairwise margins including zero and negative values, path strengths are maximum bottleneck strengths, and winners satisfy the weak comparisons defining \(W\). Thus their unique-winner model means exactly \(W=\{c\}\). [Source, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.09226).

Their formulation of paths causes no discrepancy with the requirement of simple paths here. Removing a cycle from a directed walk preserves its endpoints and cannot decrease its minimum edge weight. Consequently, allowing walks does not increase the maximum bottleneck strength.

2. Positive scaling preserves the winning set

For a margin function \(w\), write \(S_w(a,b)\) for its strongest-path value and \(W_w\) for its winning set.

Lemma 1. For every positive scalar \(D\) and distinct candidates \(a,b\),

\(S_{Dw}(a,b)=D S_w(a,b)\).

Consequently, \(W_{Dw}=W_w\).

Proof. For each simple directed path \(p\),

\(\min_{(u,v)\in p}Dw(u,v)=D\min_{(u,v)\in p}w(u,v)\),

because \(D>0\). Taking the maximum over the finitely many simple paths gives the asserted equality. Multiplication by \(D>0\) preserves every comparison \(S_w(a,b)\geq S_w(b,a)\), so it preserves the winning set. \(\square\)

3. Rational manipulation implies unanimous manipulation

Lemma 2. A successful rational coalition assignment exists if and only if there is a ranking \(\rho\in\mathcal R(C)\) such that assigning the entire mass \(\alpha\) to \(\rho\) makes \(c\) the unique winner.

Proof. The reverse implication is immediate: setting \(y_\rho=\alpha\) and all other coalition masses to zero is an admissible rational assignment.

For the forward implication, suppose a successful rational assignment \(y\) exists. Its positive support is finite because \(\mathcal R(C)\) is finite. Let \(D\) be a positive common multiple of the denominators of all positive nonmanipulator masses and all positive coalition masses.

Construct an integer weighted election as follows:

  • For each listed nonmanipulator ranking \(\pi\), create one voter of weight \(D\nu_\pi\).
  • For each ranking \(\pi\) with \(y_\pi>0\), create one manipulator of weight \(Dy_\pi\), initially voting \(\pi\).

Every listed weight is a positive integer. The coalition is nonempty because \(\alpha>0\), and its total weight is

\(\sum_{\pi:y_\pi>0}Dy_\pi=D\alpha\).

The resulting margin function is exactly \(Dw\), where \(w\) is the margin function under the assumed successful rational assignment. Lemma 1 therefore shows that \(c\) is the unique winner of this integer weighted election.

Apply the integer unanimity property. There is a ranking \(\rho\) such that all these manipulators can vote \(\rho\) while retaining \(c\) as unique winner. Since their weights sum to \(D\alpha\), this unanimous integer profile is precisely the positive scaling by \(D\) of the rational profile with nonmanipulator masses \(\nu\) and coalition mass \(\alpha\) concentrated on \(\rho\). Lemma 1 again preserves the winning set upon scaling down. \(\square\)

The denominator \(D\) in this proof can depend on the hypothetical successful assignment. The algorithm does not construct \(D\); it is used only to establish the equivalence.

4. Reduction using only input denominators

First handle the elementary cases. If \(m=1\), the answer is yes. If \(\alpha=1\), there are no positive nonmanipulator masses; putting all coalition mass on any ranking with \(c\) first gives \(w(c,b)=1\) and \(w(b,c)=-1\) for every \(b\neq c\). Every path ending at \(c\) then has strength at most \(-1\), while \(S(c,b)\geq1\), so the answer is yes.

Now suppose \(m\geq2\) and \(0<\alpha<1\). Write the input fractions as

\(\alpha=a_0/b_0,\qquad \nu_i=a_i/b_i\quad(1\leq i\leq\tau)\),

with positive integer denominators. Set

\(D_0=\prod_{i=0}^{\tau}b_i\).

Create an integer weighted manipulation instance with:

  • The same candidates and preferred candidate \(c\).
  • One nonmanipulator of weight \(D_0\nu_i\) for each supported ranking.
  • One manipulator, of weight \(D_0\alpha\).

These weights are positive integers.

For any ranking \(\rho\) of the single manipulator, the resulting integer margins are \(D_0\) times the margins obtained by assigning rational mass \(\alpha\) to \(\rho\). Lemma 1 shows that the two profiles have identical winning sets. Together with Lemma 2, this proves

\(\text{successful rational assignment exists}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{the constructed integer instance admits successful manipulation}.\)

We therefore run the integer weighted unique-winner algorithm on this instance.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

The product \(D_0\) has polynomial bit length: indeed,

\(\log_2 D_0=\sum_{i=0}^{\tau}\log_2 b_i\leq L\).

Since every input mass is at most one, every constructed weight is at most \(D_0\), hence has \(O(L)\) bits. Computing the product and the scaled integer weights uses polynomial-time integer arithmetic.

The constructed instance has exactly \(\tau\) nonmanipulators and one manipulator. Rankings are copied directly; neither voters nor the full ranking space are expanded. Its encoding length is therefore polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The weighted algorithm uses polynomially many arithmetic operations, with finite bounds selected from margins plus total coalition weight. These quantities have \(O(L)\) bits on the constructed instance, so its arithmetic also has polynomial bit complexity. [Müller–Kosub, Algorithms 1–2 and Lemma 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.09226).

Thus the reduction and decision algorithm run in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). On a yes-instance, a successful assignment can also be returned compactly: one ranking carrying mass \(\alpha\).
