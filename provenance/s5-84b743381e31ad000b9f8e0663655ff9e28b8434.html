The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite catalogue \(T\) of complete atomless valuation oracles \(V_t\), rational masses \(\mu\), and \(c\ge1\), issue shared exact Robertson–Webb \(\operatorname{Eval}_t\) and \(\operatorname{Cut}_t\) queries and output pairwise-disjoint measurable type bundles \(A_t\) satisfying \(V_t(A_t)\ge\mu_t/c\) for every \(\mu_t>0\); minimize worst-case query complexity.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T\): a list of \(\tau\ge2\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The cake is \([0,1]\). Each type has an unknown nonnegative, complete atomless probability measure \(V_t\); access is exclusively through its oracle, shared by all mass of that type.
An exact query \(\operatorname{Eval}_t(x,y)\) returns \(V_t([x,y])\), for \(0\le x\le y\le1\). A query \(\operatorname{Cut}_t(x,\alpha)\) returns the leftmost \(y\ge x\) with \(V_t([x,y])=\alpha\), or reports infeasibility; \(\alpha\ge0\).
Given binary-rational \(c\ge1\), a deterministic adaptive protocol must output pairwise-disjoint bundles \(A_t\), each a finite union of intervals whose endpoints are \(0\), \(1\), or endpoints of cuts made by the protocol, satisfying \(V_t(A_t)\ge\mu_t/c\). Boundary points may be assigned individually; unallocated cake is allowed.
Each Eval or Cut costs one query; other computation is free. Let \(L\) be the total binary encoding length of the masses and \(c\), and let \(Q(\tau,L,c)\) be the minimum worst-case query count over admissible mass vectors and valuation oracles. There is no finite-item parameter \(m\).

What is the asymptotically optimal deterministic query complexity \(Q(\tau,L,c)\) as a function of \(\tau\), \(L\), and \(c\)?

For finite equal-share societies, [Edmonds–Pruhs](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/TOPICS/cake/lbprop.pdf) prove a deterministic lower bound \(\Omega(n(\log n-\log c))\), even for disconnected bundles and exact queries.
For unequal rational entitlements and \(c=1\), [Cseh–Fleiner](https://hpi.de/friedrich/docs/publications/2020/TALG.pdf) give an \(O(\tau\log D)\) upper bound and a matching worst-case lower bound, where \(D\) is the least common denominator of the masses. No result settling the full dependence on \(c\) was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized positive masses to sum to one and valuations to be nonnegative complete atomless probability measures on the unit interval.
  • Restricted protocols to deterministic adaptive protocols, matching the anchor; shared access means one oracle per supported type.
  • Replaced unspecified measurable output representations with finite unions of intervals bounded by cut endpoints, following the discrete output convention; assigned boundary points individually and permitted leftover cake.
  • Specified leftmost exact cuts, nonnegative cut requests, and an infeasibility response, following the explicit convention in the unequal-entitlement literature.
  • Made c a binary rational, included its encoding in L, and defined the worst case over mass vectors and valuation oracles; only queries are charged.
  • Used the intended fairness threshold 1/(cn), whose mass analogue is μ_t/c, rather than the inconsistent c/n formula in the formal statement.

1. Extending bounded protocols to arbitrary atomless measures

The following observation repairs the main issue identified by the verifier.

Lemma 1. Suppose a deterministic protocol in the stated query model is correct for absolutely continuous probability measures and has a uniform finite query bound on that domain. Suppose its output bundles have endpoints among its cut endpoints and the cake boundaries. Then the same protocol, with the same query bound, is correct for arbitrary atomless probability measures.

Proof. First consider any finite transcript generated by atomless measures $V_t$.

Let
$$ 0=z_0<z_1<\cdots<z_m=1 $$
contain all query endpoints: the endpoints of evaluation intervals, the starting points of cuts, and the returned cut points. For each type define an absolutely continuous probability measure $\widetilde V_t$ by giving it constant density
$$ \frac{V_t((z_{j-1},z_j))}{z_j-z_{j-1}} $$
on $(z_{j-1},z_j)$.

Atomlessness implies that all endpoints have value zero. Therefore $\widetilde V_t$ agrees with $V_t$ on every interval whose endpoints belong to this partition. In particular, every evaluation answer is preserved.

Consider a feasible query $\operatorname{Cut}_t(x,\alpha)$ returning $y$.

If $\alpha=0$, the leftmost answer is $x$ for both measures.

If $\alpha>0$, then $y>x$. Let $z$ be the partition point immediately preceding $y$. Since $x$ is a partition point, $z\ge x$. The leftmost-cut condition implies
$$ V_t([x,z])<\alpha=V_t([x,y]). $$
Thus
$$ V_t((z,y))>0. $$
The constructed density is strictly positive on $(z,y)$. Its cumulative value reaches $\alpha$ at $y$ and is strictly smaller at every earlier point. Hence $y$ remains the leftmost answer.

For an atomless measure, continuity of the cumulative interval value implies that a cut is infeasible exactly when
$$ \alpha>V_t([x,1]). $$
This condition is also preserved.

Thus every finite transcript has an absolutely continuous realization with exactly the same answers, including leftmost cuts and infeasibility reports.

Now let $q$ be the protocol’s uniform query bound for absolutely continuous measures. If a run on atomless measures attempted query $q+1$, an absolutely continuous realization of that finite transcript would force the same deterministic protocol to exceed its bound. This is impossible.

Finally, at termination, every output bundle is a union of partition intervals, up to endpoints. Its value is therefore preserved by the construction. An incorrect atomless output would produce the same incorrect output on an absolutely continuous realization. This is also impossible. $\square$

Applying the integer-entitlement theorem

We use the following established result: for positive integer demands $d_1,\ldots,d_r$ summing to $N>r$, absolutely continuous valuations admit a deterministic proportional allocation in $O(r\log N)$ queries, with bundles formed from cut intervals. This is the content of [Cseh–Fleiner, Theorems 4.2–4.3](https://hpi.de/friedrich/docs/publications/2020/TALG.pdf).

Here are the required model and hypothesis checks:

  • Multiplying probability measures by $N$ gives the theorem’s normalization.
  • A proportional cut of $[a,b]$ at fraction $\rho$ uses an evaluation of $v=V_t([a,b])$, followed by $\operatorname{Cut}_t(a,\rho v)$.
  • Recursive copies of a type use its shared oracle.
  • If $N=r$, replace every demand by $2d_i$. The total becomes $2N>r$, while all entitlement ratios remain unchanged.
  • Lemma 1 extends the resulting bounded exact-query protocol to arbitrary atomless measures.

Consequently, for every $N\ge r$, integer entitlements $d_i/N$ can be satisfied in $O(r\log N)$ permitted queries.

2. Uniform upper bounds

Let $D$ be the least common denominator of the masses. The positive integers
$$ d_t=D\mu_t $$
sum to $D$. Exact proportional allocation therefore gives an instancewise upper bound
$$ O(\tau\log D) $$
for every $c\ge1$.

For $c>1$, set
$$ N=\left\lceil\frac{\tau}{\eta}\right\rceil, \qquad d_t=\left\lceil\frac{N\mu_t}{c}\right\rceil. $$
Each demand is positive, and
$$ \sum_t d_t \le \frac{N}{c}+\tau =N(1-\eta)+\tau \le N. $$
Increase one demand by the nonnegative difference between $N$ and this sum. The resulting positive integer demands $d'_t$ satisfy
$$ \sum_t d'_t=N, \qquad \frac{d'_t}{N}\ge\frac{\mu_t}{c}. $$
The integer-entitlement protocol gives the required allocation in
$$ O(\tau\log N) = O\!\left(\tau\left[\log\tau+\log\frac1\eta\right]\right) $$
queries.

Combining the two protocols yields
$$ O\!\left( \tau\min\left\{ \log D,\ \log\tau+\log\frac1\eta \right\} \right). $$

Under explicit binary numerator–denominator encoding, write each reduced mass as $p_t/q_t$. Then
$$ D\le\prod_t q_t, \qquad \log D\le\sum_t\log q_t\le L. $$
This proves the announced uniform upper bound. The same argument permits replacing $L$ by the encoding length of the masses alone.

3. An adversary respecting exact leftmost cuts

The next lemma supplies the stronger lower bound. Its partition strategy is related to the crumble construction in [Cseh–Fleiner, Section 6.1](https://hpi.de/friedrich/docs/publications/2020/TALG.pdf). The uniform background below explicitly ensures that every feasible cut is unique.

Let $\lambda$ denote Lebesgue measure.

Lemma 2. Fix $0<\varepsilon<1$. There is an adversary for one probability-measure oracle with the following properties.

After $q$ queries, its transcript has an absolutely continuous realization of the form
$$ V=\varepsilon\lambda+W, \qquad W([0,1])=1-\varepsilon. $$
Moreover, for every measurable set $A$ satisfying
$$ \lambda(A)<3^{-q}, $$
the transcript has such a realization with
$$ W(A)=0. $$
All reported cuts are exact leftmost cuts in every realization used.

Proof. The adversary maintains a finite measurable partition $\mathcal C$ and nonnegative weights $w_C$ summing to $1-\varepsilon$. These specify the residual-measure totals
$$ W(C)=w_C. $$
Initially $\mathcal C=\{[0,1]\}$.

After $q$ queries, maintain the invariant
$$ w_C>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \lambda(C)\ge3^{-q}. $$
All previously queried intervals are unions of partition cells, up to endpoints. Refinement always preserves each parent cell’s total weight.

Evaluation queries

For an evaluation interval $I$, split every cell into $C\cap I$ and $C\setminus I$. Place all of $w_C$ on a part of greater or equal length, assigning zero to the other part.

Every positive-weight descendant has at least half its parent’s length. Return
$$ \varepsilon\lambda(I)+\sum_{C'\subseteq I}w_{C'}. $$
This preserves the invariant with the weaker factor $1/3$.

Cut queries

For $\operatorname{Cut}(x,0)$, return $x$ without changing the partition.

Now suppose $\alpha>0$, and put $I=[x,1]$. First split each cell into its parts inside and outside $I$.

If
$$ \lambda(C\cap I)\ge\frac23\lambda(C), $$
place all residual weight on $C\cap I$. Otherwise place it on $C\setminus I$.

Positive-weight outside parts have length greater than one third of the parent’s length. Positive-weight inside parts have length at least two thirds of it.

List the positive-weight inside parts as $B_j$, with weights $w_j$. For each choose a length median $z_j$, so that
$$ \lambda(B_j\cap[x,z_j]) = \lambda(B_j\setminus[x,z_j]) = \frac12\lambda(B_j). $$
Such a point exists because interval length varies continuously with the endpoint.

If
$$ \alpha>\varepsilon(1-x)+\sum_j w_j, $$
report infeasibility. The first-stage partition and weights already realize that answer.

Otherwise define
$$ H_-(y)=\varepsilon(y-x)+\sum_{z_j<y}w_j, \qquad H_+(y)=\varepsilon(y-x)+\sum_{z_j\le y}w_j. $$
There exists $y\in[x,1]$ with
$$ H_-(y)\le\alpha\le H_+(y). $$
Indeed, the cumulative function increases continuously with slope $\varepsilon$ between its finitely many jumps, and these inequalities fill each jump.

Refine every $B_j$ at $y$. Assign its residual weight as follows:

  • If $z_j<y$, place all its weight on the left.
  • If $z_j>y$, place all its weight on the right.
  • Among parts with $z_j=y$, distribute total left weight $\alpha-H_-(y)$, respecting each part’s available weight.

The last distribution is possible because
$$ 0\le\alpha-H_-(y)\le\sum_{z_j=y}w_j. $$
Each positive-weight descendant contains at least half its intermediate part’s length. It therefore has at least one third of its original parent’s length.

Return $y$. The prescribed weights give
$$ V([x,y])=\alpha. $$
Every eventual realization contains the background $\varepsilon\lambda$. Its cumulative interval value is strictly increasing. Thus $y$ is its unique, and hence leftmost, cut answer.

Realization and the terminal set

All refinements preserve parent totals. Therefore an atomless residual measure assigning each final cell its prescribed weight preserves every earlier evaluation, cut equality, and infeasibility answer. The fixed uniform background preserves uniqueness of all feasible cuts.

Now suppose $\lambda(A)<3^{-q}$. Every positive-weight final cell satisfies
$$ \lambda(C)\ge3^{-q}>\lambda(A), $$
and consequently $\lambda(C\setminus A)>0$.

On each such cell, realize its residual weight by constant density on $C\setminus A$:
$$ W(B)= \sum_{C:w_C>0} w_C\, \frac{\lambda(B\cap(C\setminus A))} {\lambda(C\setminus A)}. $$
This defines an absolutely continuous residual measure of total mass $1-\varepsilon$, preserves all prescribed cell weights, and gives $W(A)=0$.

Completing the resulting measure gives an admissible complete atomless probability measure. $\square$

4. A universal lower bound independent of $c$

Theorem 3. For every positive mass vector and every finite $c\ge1$, every correct deterministic protocol has worst-case query complexity at least
$$ \tau\log_3\tau. $$

Proof. Choose
$$ 0<\varepsilon<\min_t\frac{\mu_t}{c}. $$
Run independent copies of Lemma 2 for the $\tau$ type oracles. Let $q_t$ count queries addressed to type $t$.

Every finite transcript has simultaneous admissible realizations. Thus a protocol with a finite worst-case bound must terminate on this adversarial run.

At termination, correctness requires
$$ \lambda(A_t)\ge3^{-q_t} $$
for every type. Otherwise Lemma 2 provides a consistent realization for that type with
$$ V_t(A_t)=\varepsilon\lambda(A_t) \le\varepsilon <\frac{\mu_t}{c}. $$
Keeping consistent realizations for all other types produces an admissible instance on which the unchanged deterministic transcript ends with an incorrect allocation.

Disjointness gives
$$ \sum_t3^{-q_t} \le\sum_t\lambda(A_t) \le1. $$
By the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality,
$$ \sum_t3^{-q_t} \ge \tau\,3^{-\left(\sum_tq_t\right)/\tau}. $$
Therefore
$$ \sum_tq_t\ge\tau\log_3\tau. $$
$\square$

This proof permits disconnected bundles and unallocated cake. It does not use the restriction on output endpoints.

Matching regime

If $c\ge1+\tau^{-a}$ for fixed $a>0$, then
$$ \frac1\eta=\frac{c}{c-1} =1+\frac1{c-1} \le1+\tau^a. $$
The rounding upper bound is consequently $O(\tau\log\tau)$. Theorem 3 gives the matching lower bound.

Thus large approximation factors do not reduce the asymptotic deterministic worst-case query complexity in this valuation model.

5. Matching precision–slack bounds for an explicit family

Fix
$$ h=\tau-1, \qquad D=2^k\ge2\tau, $$
and consider the masses
$$ \mu_1=\cdots=\mu_h=\frac1D, \qquad \mu_\tau=1-\frac hD. $$

Theorem 4. For this fixed mass vector, the optimal worst-case query complexity is
$$ \boxed{ \Theta\!\left( \tau\log\min\left\{D,\frac{\tau}{\eta}\right\} \right). } $$
At $\eta=0$, the minimum is interpreted as $D$.

Proof of the lower bound. Give type $\tau$ Lebesgue valuation. For each of the first $h$ types, run Lemma 2 with
$$ 0<\varepsilon<\frac1{cD}. $$
As in Theorem 3, correctness forces
$$ \lambda(A_i)\ge3^{-q_i}, \qquad 1\le i\le h. $$

The last type must receive length at least
$$ \frac{1-h/D}{c}. $$
Hence disjointness implies
$$ \sum_{i=1}^h3^{-q_i} \le 1-\frac{1-h/D}{c} = \eta+\frac{h}{cD}. $$
Applying the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives the explicit bound
$$ \boxed{ \sum_{i=1}^h q_i \ge h\log_3 \left( \frac{ch}{c-1+h/D} \right). } $$

To compare this with the claimed order, put
$$ s=\min\left\{D,\frac h\eta\right\}, $$
again taking $s=D$ when $\eta=0$. Then
$$ \eta+\frac{h}{cD} \le\eta+\frac hD \le\frac{2h}{s}. $$
Consequently,
$$ \sum_{i=1}^h q_i\ge h\log_3(s/2). $$
Also, since $h\ge\tau/2$,
$$ \min\left\{D,\frac{\tau}{\eta}\right\}\le2s. $$
Thus this lower bound is at least
$$ h\left( \log_3\min\left\{D,\frac{\tau}{\eta}\right\} -\log_3 4 \right). $$
When the logarithm is sufficiently large, this is the required order. When it is bounded, Theorem 3 supplies the required linear lower bound. Together these establish the claimed lower bound uniformly.

Proof of the upper bound. Section 2 gives
$$ O\!\left( \tau\min\left\{ \log D,\ \log\tau+\log\frac1\eta \right\} \right) = O\!\left( \tau\log\min\left\{D,\frac{\tau}{\eta}\right\} \right). $$
$\square$

Encoding consequence

Under explicit reduced numerator–denominator encoding, the mass-list length $L_{\mathrm{mass},k}$ for this family satisfies
$$ L_{\mathrm{mass},k}=\Theta(\tau k). $$
The first $h$ denominators already require $\Omega(\tau k)$ bits, and every mass has an $O(k)$-bit representation.

Therefore its optimal complexity can also be written as
$$ \Theta\!\left( \min\left\{ L_{\mathrm{mass},k}, \ \tau\left[\log\tau+\log\frac1\eta\right] \right\} \right). $$
In particular, at $c=1$ this family requires
$$ \Theta(L_{\mathrm{mass},k}) $$
queries. The encoding of $c$ is additional and should not be counted as mass precision.

6. Remaining gap in the target question

The results above settle all $c\ge1+\tau^{-a}$ for fixed $a>0$, and determine the entire precision–slack tradeoff on the explicit family in Section 5.

They do not determine the sharp worst-case dependence on total mass encoding length for arbitrary mass vectors. At $c=1$, the general upper bound remains $O(\tau L)$, while the displayed families establish linear dependence on their mass-list encoding length. When $c$ is extremely close to $1$, the same encoding-dependent gap can remain.

For clarity, under the usual at-most encoding-budget interpretation, let $B$ be the budget for the masses, excluding the encoding of $c$. For $B\ge C\tau\log\tau$, with a sufficiently large fixed encoding-dependent constant $C$, the proved results imply
$$ \Omega\!\left( \min\left\{ B,\ \tau\left[\log\tau+\log\frac1\eta\right] \right\} \right) \le Q \le O\!\left( \min\left\{ \tau B,\ \tau\left[\log\tau+\log\frac1\eta\right] \right\} \right). $$
For the lower bound, choose $k$ of order $B/\tau$, with a sufficiently small constant so that the family in Section 5 fits the budget. Its matching bound then gives the displayed expression.

Closing this gap requires an additional upper-bound argument or a stronger encoding-sensitive lower bound.
