The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite alternative set \(C\) and a binary-rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) complete rankings, construct a finite derivation of \(W(\mu)=\arg\max_{c\in C}\sum_t\mu_t s(t,c)\) from a rational-mass extension of \(J_B\). Intermediate profiles are finite nonzero rational measures over rankings, with the paper’s \(\mathrm{Elem}\), \(\mathrm{Cycl}\), \(\mathrm{Canc}\), and reinforcement axioms interpreted on those measures.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) be a nonempty alternative set, \(m=|C|\), with a fixed reference order. Types are strict complete rankings. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. The input \(\mu\) has total mass \(1\); let \(L\) be its total input bit length.
Intermediate profiles \(P\) are finite nonzero nonnegative rational measures over rankings, without normalization; addition and positive rational scaling act on masses. All profiles appearing in proofs are encoded explicitly by support and binary-rational masses.
An atom \([P\mapsto A]\), for \(\varnothing\ne A\subseteq C\), asserts that the complete winner set is \(A\). Background axioms require exactly one such set for each profile; derivations use classical propositional natural deduction, with earlier lines reusable.
For each nonempty \(A\subseteq C\), let \(E_A\) assign unit mass to the ranking listing \(A\) then \(C\setminus A\) in reference order, and to the ranking reversing both blocks while keeping \(A\) first. Coincident rankings have their masses added. For each cycle \(S\) visiting every alternative once, let \(D_S\) assign unit mass to each cyclic rotation of its ranking.
The rational-mass axiom system \(J_B^{\mathbb Q}\) contains \(\mathrm{Elem}: [qE_A\mapsto A]\) and \(\mathrm{Cycl}: [qD_S\mapsto C]\) for every \(q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\); \(\mathrm{Canc}: [P\mapsto C]\) whenever, for every distinct pair, the mass preferring either alternative to the other is equal.
It also contains \(\mathrm{Reinf}: ([P\mapsto A]\land[Q\mapsto B])\to[P+Q\mapsto A\cap B]\) when \(A\cap B\ne\varnothing\); \(\mathrm{Reinf\text{-}sub}: ([P+Q\mapsto A]\land[Q\mapsto C])\to[P\mapsto A]\); and \(\mathrm{Simp}: [qP\mapsto A]\to[P\mapsto A]\) for \(q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\).
The computational task is to construct a finite derivation of \([\mu\mapsto W(\mu)]\) from these axioms and the background axioms, where \(W(\mu)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{c\in C}\sum_t\mu_t s(t,c)\), \(s\) is the standard Borda score, and all tied maximizers are included.

Can this derivation be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), counting the entire encoded output, or is this search problem NP-hard?

For finite electorates, Cailloux and Endriss give an algorithm constructing Borda justifications from the six discrete axioms, without an explicit polynomial-time bound ([source](https://eprints.illc.uva.nl/id/eprint/552/)). Peters et al. prove an \(O(m^2)\) bound on explanation steps for discrete profiles using these axioms plus a multiplication shortcut ([source](https://zixinzhou.com/pdf/explain.pdf)).
No result settling the stated rational-mass proof-generation problem was located.

  • Interpreted s as the standard Borda score and retained the entire set of tied winners.
  • Normalized the input distribution to total mass 1, while allowing arbitrary positive total mass in intermediate profiles; signed masses and null-profile atoms are excluded.
  • Made the rational extension explicit: elementary and cyclic profiles may have any common positive rational mass, and simplification permits any positive rational scaling factor.
  • Restored Reinf-sub and Simp, which belong to the six-axiom discrete system but were omitted from the abbreviated continuous question.
  • Used the two elementary rankings with each block reversed in the second ranking, resolving the lost reversal marks in the supplied text.
  • Retained the nonempty alternative domain, including the single-alternative case, and added masses when elementary rankings coincide.
  • Specified natural deduction with reusable earlier lines and explicit support encodings for intermediate profiles; polynomial time counts all output bits rather than only proof steps.

Result

The derivation can be constructed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), including the entire encoded output. In fact, \(O(m^2)\) natural-deduction lines suffice, with polynomially bounded support sizes and rational bit lengths.

The construction uses the pairwise-margin viewpoint of [Peters et al., Appendix C.1](https://zixinzhou.com/pdf/explain.pdf). Below, an explicit identity supplies the required cyclic profiles, and the encoding bounds are proved directly.

1. Pairwise margins and Borda scores

Index alternatives by their fixed reference order. For any profile \(P\), write \(n(P)\) for its total mass and define the antisymmetric margin matrix by

\[ \delta(P)_{ab} = \sum_{t:a\succ_t b}P_t-\sum_{t:b\succ_t a}P_t, \qquad \delta(P)_{aa}=0. \]

Define its row sums by \(\beta(P)_a=\sum_{b\ne a}\delta(P)_{ab}\). If \(b(P)_a\) denotes the Borda score of \(a\), then

\[ \beta(P)_a=2b(P)_a-(m-1)n(P). \]

Indeed, the Borda score counts, for each other alternative, the mass ranking \(a\) above that alternative. The two directional masses for each pair sum to \(n(P)\). Consequently, maximizing \(\beta(P)\) gives exactly the complete Borda winner set.

Both \(\delta\) and \(\beta\) are additive and rational-linear. Antisymmetry also gives \(\sum_a\beta(P)_a=0\).

If \(m=1\), cancellation applies vacuously to \(\mu\), proving \([\mu\mapsto C]\). Henceforth assume \(m\ge 2\).

2. Transfer between profiles with equal margins

Lemma. Given nonzero profiles \(R,M\) with \(\delta(R)=\delta(M)\), a derivation of \([R\mapsto A]\) can be extended by a constant number of lines to derive \([M\mapsto A]\).

Proof. Let \(\overline R\) reverse every ranking of \(R\), preserving its mass. Then \(\delta(\overline R)=-\delta(R)\). Define

\[ U=M+\overline R, \qquad V=R+\overline R. \]

Both have zero margins. Cancellation therefore supplies \([U\mapsto C]\) and \([V\mapsto C]\).

Reinforcement applied to \([R\mapsto A]\) and \([U\mapsto C]\) gives \([R+U\mapsto A]\). As measures,

\[ R+U=M+V. \]

Thus reinforcement-subtraction, using \([V\mapsto C]\), gives \([M\mapsto A]\).

Each reinforcement application expands into its axiom instance, conjunction introduction, and implication elimination. All profiles are nonzero and nonnegative. When supports are aggregated and canonically ordered, \(R+U\) and \(M+V\) have identical encodings; no additional equality principle is needed. \(\square\)

3. An elementary profile matching the Borda row sums

Put \(\beta_a=\beta(\mu)_a\), and order the alternatives as \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) so that

\[ \beta_{a_1}\ge\beta_{a_2}\ge\cdots\ge\beta_{a_m}. \]

For \(1\le k<m\), define \(A_k=\{a_1,\ldots,a_k\}\) and

\[ q_k=\frac{\beta_{a_k}-\beta_{a_{k+1}}}{2m}. \]

These coefficients are nonnegative. Form the nonzero profile

\[ E=E_C+\sum_{\substack{1\le k<m\\q_k>0}}q_kE_{A_k}. \]

The rankings defining every \(E_{A_k}\) use the prescribed reference order.

For a subset \(A\) of size \(k\), the two rankings in \(E_A\) give zero margins within each block and margin \(2\) from every member of \(A\) to every member of its complement. Therefore

\[ \beta(E_A)_a= \begin{cases} 2(m-k),&a\in A,\\ -2k,&a\notin A. \end{cases} \]

In particular, \(\delta(E_C)=0\). Between consecutive alternatives in the score order, only the cut \(A_k\) separates \(a_k\) and \(a_{k+1}\). Hence

\[ \beta(E)_{a_k}-\beta(E)_{a_{k+1}} = 2mq_k = \beta_{a_k}-\beta_{a_{k+1}}. \]

Thus \(\beta(E)-\beta(\mu)\) is constant across alternatives. Both vectors sum to zero, so

\[ \beta(E)=\beta(\mu). \]

The winner set of \(E\) is formally derivable without assuming this equality. Elem supplies \([E_C\mapsto C]\) and, for each positive coefficient, \([q_kE_{A_k}\mapsto A_k]\). Reinforcement combines them.

If every coefficient is zero, all Borda scores tie and the resulting set is \(C=W(\mu)\). Otherwise, let \(k_0\) be the first index with \(q_{k_0}>0\). The positive-coefficient sets are nested, and their intersection is \(A_{k_0}\), precisely the set of all tied maximizers. Every intermediate intersection is nonempty. We therefore obtain

\[ [E\mapsto W(\mu)] \]

in \(O(m)\) proof lines.

4. Explicit cyclic profiles for the residual margins

Set \(x=\delta(\mu)-\delta(E)\). The preceding section proves that every row sum of \(x\) is zero. We now construct a nonzero profile \(T\) with

\[ \delta(T)=x, \qquad [T\mapsto C] \]

derivable in \(O(m^2)\) lines.

4.1 A projection onto zero-row-sum matrices

For an antisymmetric matrix \(z\), let \(d_a(z)=\sum_bz_{ab}\), and define

\[ (\Pi z)_{ab}=z_{ab}-\frac{d_a(z)-d_b(z)}m. \]

Antisymmetry implies \(\sum_ad_a(z)=0\). Consequently,

\[ \sum_b(\Pi z)_{ab} = d_a(z)-\frac{md_a(z)-\sum_bd_b(z)}m = 0. \]

Moreover, if \(z\) already has zero row sums, then \(\Pi z=z\).

For reference-ordered indices \(i<j\), let \(e^{ij}\) have entry \(1\) at \((i,j)\), entry \(-1\) at \((j,i)\), and zero elsewhere. Thus every antisymmetric matrix satisfies

\[ z=\sum_{i<j}z_{ij}e^{ij}. \]

4.2 Adjacent swaps of cyclic profiles

For a ranking \(r\), let \(\varepsilon(r)\) be its unit-mass margin matrix and \(p_r(a)\) the position of \(a\), numbered from \(1\). Then

\[ d_a(\varepsilon(r))=m+1-2p_r(a). \]

Write \(D_r\) for the cyclic profile generated by the ranking \(r\). If \(a\) precedes \(b\) and their positional distance is \(d\), exactly \(m-d\) rotations rank \(a\) above \(b\), while \(d\) rank \(b\) above \(a\). Applying this observation in both positional orders gives

\[ \delta(D_r)_{ab} = m\varepsilon(r)_{ab} + 2\bigl(p_r(a)-p_r(b)\bigr). \]

Combining the last two identities yields

\[ \delta(D_r)=m\Pi\varepsilon(r). \]

For each \(i<j\), choose \(r_{ij}\) to list \(i,j\) first, followed by the remaining alternatives in reference order. Let \(r'_{ij}\) exchange its first two alternatives. Because they are adjacent, only their mutual comparison changes:

\[ \varepsilon(r_{ij})-\varepsilon(r'_{ij})=2e^{ij}. \]

Therefore

\[ \delta(D_{r_{ij}})-\delta(D_{r'_{ij}}) = 2m\Pi e^{ij}. \]

Since \(x\) has zero row sums, linearity now proves the explicit decomposition

\[ x = \sum_{i<j} \frac{x_{ij}}{2m} \left( \delta(D_{r_{ij}})-\delta(D_{r'_{ij}}) \right). \]

4.3 Realizing the decomposition with positive masses

Reversing every ranking in a cyclic profile produces another cyclic profile and negates its margins.

For each pair with \(x_{ij}\ne0\), set \(\alpha_{ij}=|x_{ij}|/(2m)>0\). Define \(T_{ij}\) as follows:

  • If \(x_{ij}>0\), use \(T_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}(D_{r_{ij}}+\overline{D_{r'_{ij}}})\).
  • If \(x_{ij}<0\), use \(T_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}(\overline{D_{r_{ij}}}+D_{r'_{ij}})\).

In both cases its margin matrix is the corresponding summand in the decomposition. Define

\[ T=E_C+\sum_{\substack{i<j\\x_{ij}\ne0}}T_{ij}. \]

Then \(T\) is nonzero and \(\delta(T)=x\). Elem proves the full winner set for the initial \(E_C\). Cycl proves the full winner set for each positively scaled cyclic summand. Repeated reinforcement therefore proves \([T\mapsto C]\).

There are at most \(2\binom m2\) cyclic summands, so this takes \(O(m^2)\) lines. No signed measure is used as a profile.

5. Completing the derivation

Let \(H=E+T\). Reinforcement gives \([H\mapsto W(\mu)]\), and

\[ \delta(H) = \delta(E)+\delta(T) = \delta(\mu). \]

The transfer lemma, with \(R=H\) and \(M=\mu\), completes the derivation of

\[ [\mu\mapsto W(\mu)]. \]

The construction uses only Elem, Cycl, Canc, Reinf, and Reinf-sub. It requires neither Simp nor the background uniqueness axioms.

6. Polynomial bounds for the entire encoding

We bound both support sizes and rational bit lengths.

Support sizes and proof length

The profile \(E\) contains at most \(m\) elementary summands, each supported on at most two rankings. The profile \(T\) contains at most \(2\binom m2\) cyclic summands, each supported on \(m\) rankings, plus \(E_C\). Thus

\[ |\operatorname{supp}(H)|=O(m^3). \]

Reversal preserves support size, and addition takes unions of supports. Every profile in the construction and transfer therefore has support size \(O(\tau+m^3)\).

There are \(O(m^2)\) proof lines. Every axiom instance and logical inference used contains only a constant number of atoms, even when every profile occurrence is encoded in full.

Rational bit lengths

Write the input masses as \(p_t/d_t\), with positive integer denominators, and let \(D=\prod_td_t\). The binary length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\).

Every input margin and row sum has denominator dividing \(D\). Consequently:

  • Every \(q_k\) has denominator dividing \(2mD\).
  • Every residual entry \(x_{ij}\) has denominator dividing \(mD\).
  • Every \(\alpha_{ij}\) has denominator dividing \(2m^2D\).

All masses in every constructed profile can therefore be represented over the single denominator \(B=2m^2D\). Additions and reversals introduce no larger denominator.

It remains to bound the numerators. Because \(\mu\) has total mass \(1\), \(|\delta(\mu)_{ab}|\le1\) and \(|\beta_a|\le m-1\). Telescoping gives

\[ \sum_{k=1}^{m-1}q_k = \frac{\beta_{a_1}-\beta_{a_m}}{2m} \le\frac{m-1}{m}<1. \]

Thus \(n(E)<4\). The initial \(E_C\) has zero margins, so \(|\delta(E)_{ab}|<2\), whence \(|x_{ab}|<3\).

Each pair contributes two cyclic profiles, giving total mass \(2m\alpha_{ij}=|x_{ij}|<3\). Hence

\[ n(T)\le2+3\binom m2, \qquad n(H)<6+3\binom m2. \]

All transfer profiles also have total mass \(O(m^2)\). Every individual mass consequently has numerator at most \(O(m^2)B\), and its complete rational encoding uses \(O(L+\log m)\) bits.

Running time and output size

After indexing alternatives by reference order, a ranking uses \(O(m\log m)\) bits. The entire expanded proof therefore has size

\[ O\!\left( m^2(\tau+m^3) \bigl(m\log m+L+\log m\bigr) \right). \]

Computing margins, sorting row sums, generating the specified rankings, performing the rational arithmetic, aggregating supports, and printing the proof all require polynomial time: their counts and operand bit lengths are polynomially bounded as above.

This proves polynomial-time construction with the full output counted.
