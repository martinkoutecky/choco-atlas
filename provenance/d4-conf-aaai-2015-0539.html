The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many atomic centers \(I\), each period center \(i\) receives a rational mass vector over a finite support of complete pair types, with expected total mass \(k_i\). Types encode center ownership, compatibility, and internal-match behavior. A center may report any submass of its arrivals. Define the clearinghouse allocation by maximum-total fractional packing into legal cycles and chains; in a random center order, subject to earlier centers’ selected matched masses, choose the current center’s maximum attainable matched mass when its credit is nonnegative and its minimum otherwise, then update its credit by the corresponding best-to-worst swing. Is there a polynomially bounded coefficient \(\lambda_i\) for the update \(c_i \leftarrow c_i + \lambda_i(\text{reported mass}-k_i)\) such that full disclosure dominates every underreporting strategy with probability \(1\) and guarantees long-term individual rationality? Determine a sufficient coefficient and prove the guarantees, or give a counterexample.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(n\geq2\) atomic centers. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible types. Masses are nonnegative and need not sum to one.
Each type specifies its owner, donor–patient compatibility, and whether it is an altruist. The input explicitly lists \(m\) permitted cycle and altruist-initiated chain templates, including type multiplicities; repeated types represent distinct individuals. Chains terminate within the period, with the final donation going to the waiting list; an employed altruist contributes one matched unit to its owner.
Arrivals are independent across periods with a common, explicitly given finite rational distribution over joint mass vectors \(a^t\), allowing dependence between centers. The known rational means satisfy \(k_i>0\), \(\mathbb E\lVert a_i^t\rVert_1=k_i\), and \(\lVert a_i^t\rVert_1\leq2k_i\). All arrivals expire after one period.
For every center, with positive probability an arriving type has positive mass that cannot be matched internally, and its matched mass differs between two globally maximum fractional packings of the full society.
A center uses a measurable policy \(S_i(a_i,c_i)=r_i\) with \(0\leq r_i\leq a_i\), and internally maximizes matched mass using the withheld vector \(a_i-r_i\) and templates entirely owned by it. Fractional packing assigns nonnegative real weights to templates, consuming their type multiplicities subject to available masses; \(u_i\) denotes the resulting matched mass owned by center \(i\).
Starting from any finite rational credit vector, first update \(c_i\leftarrow c_i+\lambda_i(\lVert r_i\rVert_1-k_i)\). Restrict to maximum-total packings of the reports, and draw an independent uniform center permutation. At center \(i\), subject to all earlier selected utilities, let \(b_i\) and \(w_i\) be its maximum and minimum attainable utilities: select \(b_i\) and subtract \(b_i-w_i\) from its credit when \(c_i\geq0\); otherwise select \(w_i\) and add \(b_i-w_i\). Fix any deterministic tie-breaking among final packings.
Let \(Q_i^S(T)\) be cumulative clearinghouse plus internal matched mass through period \(T\). Couple comparisons using identical arrivals and permutations, fixing other centers’ policies. Almost-sure truthful dominance means \(\liminf_{T\to\infty}(Q_i^F(T)-Q_i^S(T))\geq0\) almost surely from every initial state, with strict inequality almost surely on paths where \(S\) actually withholds positive mass; \(F\) reports everything. Long-term IR means \(\liminf_{T\to\infty}(Q_i^F(T)-B_i(T))/T\geq0\) almost surely, where \(B_i(T)\) is cumulative maximum internal matching of all arrivals.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. Coefficients must be positive rationals fixed before play and bounded by one universal polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,n,\max_i k_i\); state any running times in these quantities.
Does such a coefficient rule \((\lambda_i)\) guarantee almost-sure truthful dominance and long-term IR for every admissible arrival law, or is there an admissible counterexample to every polynomially bounded rule?
For discrete arrivals bounded by \(2k_i\), truthful dominance and long-term IR are claimed with coefficient \(4k_i\). [Hajaj et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/9322/9181)
No result settling this exact mass-valued mechanism was located in the literature search.

Result

There is an admissible counterexample to every positive coefficient rule, even without a polynomial bound. The same fixed instance violates both almost-sure truthful dominance and long-term IR, from every initial credit vector.

The counterexample has genuine cross-center utility variation, two stochastic arrival states, and a deviating center that continues reporting positive mass.

1. Types, compatibility, and arrivals

There are \(n=2\) centers and \(\tau=9\) types. Every type is a donor–patient pair; there are no altruists.

In each period, independently, draw \(s\in\{1,2\}\), each with probability \(1/2\). Arrivals are:

| Types | Owner | Mass of each type |
|---|---|---|
| \(a,b\) | Center \(1\) | \(s\) |
| \(c,d\) | Center \(2\) | \(s\) |
| \(p,u,v\) | Center \(1\) | \(s/4\) |
| \(h,q\) | Center \(2\) | \(s/4\) |

The input permits exactly \(m=4\) cycle templates, each using its listed types once:

| Template | Directed cycle |
|---|---|
| \(I\) | \(a\to b\to a\) |
| \(E\) | \(a\to c\to d\to a\) |
| \(G_1\) | \(p\to h\to q\to p\) |
| \(G_2\) | \(u\to h\to v\to u\) |

Specify donor–patient compatibility by precisely these directed edges. Thus every listed template is compatible. Template \(I\) belongs entirely to center \(1\); each other template uses both centers.

The arriving masses satisfy

\(\displaystyle \lVert a_1^t\rVert_1=\frac{11s}{4},\qquad \lVert a_2^t\rVert_1=\frac{5s}{2}.\)

Since \(\mathbb E s=3/2\), the known means are

\(\displaystyle k_1=\frac{33}{8}>0,\qquad k_2=\frac{15}{4}>0.\)

The required bounds hold in both arrival states:

\(\displaystyle \lVert a_1^t\rVert_1\leq\frac{11}{2}<2k_1=\frac{33}{4},\qquad \lVert a_2^t\rVert_1\leq5<2k_2=\frac{15}{2}.\)

The arrival distribution is finite and rational. Periods are independent, while arrivals of the two centers are dependent, as permitted.

2. Exact characterization of full-society maximum packings

Fix either arrival state \(s\). Let \(x,y,z,w\) denote the nonnegative packing weights of \(I,E,G_1,G_2\), respectively.

The type-capacity constraints are equivalent to

\(\displaystyle x+y\leq s,\qquad z+w\leq\frac{s}{4},\qquad x,y,z,w\geq0.\)

Indeed, type \(a\) gives the first inequality and type \(h\) gives the second. Every other type appears in exactly one template, and its capacity constraint follows from these inequalities and nonnegativity.

Total matched mass is

\(\displaystyle U=2x+3y+3z+3w =3(x+y)-x+3(z+w)\leq\frac{15s}{4}.\)

The upper bound is attainable: take \(x=0\), \(y=s\), and any nonnegative \(z,w\) with \(z+w=s/4\).

Moreover, equality holds if and only if

\(\displaystyle x=0,\qquad y=s,\qquad z+w=\frac{s}{4}.\)

To verify necessity, the objective’s difference from its upper bound is

\(\displaystyle \frac{15s}{4}-U =3(s-x-y)+x+3\left(\frac{s}{4}-z-w\right).\)

All three terms are nonnegative, so equality forces each to vanish.

Center utilities are

\(\displaystyle u_1=2x+y+z+2w,\qquad u_2=2y+2z+w.\)

Consequently, every full-society maximum packing satisfies

\(\displaystyle u_1=\frac{5s}{4}+w\in\left[\frac{5s}{4},\frac{3s}{2}\right],\qquad u_2=\frac{5s}{2}-w\in\left[\frac{9s}{4},\frac{5s}{2}\right].\)

These bounds apply regardless of credits, permutation, and final tie-breaking.

3. Verification of the participation assumption

Consider the two globally maximum packings

\(\displaystyle M^{(1)}=(x,y,z,w)=\left(0,s,\frac{s}{4},0\right),\qquad M^{(2)}=\left(0,s,0,\frac{s}{4}\right).\)

For center \(1\), type \(u\) has positive arriving mass \(s/4\), cannot be matched internally, and has matched mass \(0\) in \(M^{(1)}\) and \(s/4\) in \(M^{(2)}\).

For center \(2\), type \(q\) has positive arriving mass \(s/4\), cannot be matched internally, and has matched mass \(s/4\) in \(M^{(1)}\) and \(0\) in \(M^{(2)}\).

Both centers’ aggregate utilities also differ between these packings. Thus the required participation assumption holds in every arrival state, hence with probability one.

4. Long-term IR fails under truthful reporting

Let both centers report everything.

The only entirely center-\(1\) template is \(I\). Its maximum feasible weight on center \(1\)’s full arrivals is \(s\), so its maximum internal matched mass is exactly \(2s\).

Write \(s_t\) for the scale in period \(t\). Therefore,

\(\displaystyle B_1(T)=2\sum_{t=1}^T s_t.\)

Truthful reporting withholds nothing, so it produces no internal matches. Section 2 gives, in every period,

\(\displaystyle u_1^F(t)\leq\frac{3s_t}{2}.\)

Hence, on every arrival and permutation path,

\(\displaystyle Q_1^F(T)-B_1(T) \leq-\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T s_t \leq-\frac{T}{2}.\)

It follows that

\(\displaystyle \liminf_{T\to\infty} \frac{Q_1^F(T)-B_1(T)}{T}\leq-\frac12<0.\)

Thus long-term IR fails pathwise, from every initial credit vector and for every choice of positive coefficients.

5. Truthful dominance also fails

Fix center \(2\)’s policy to report everything, independently of its credits.

Define center \(1\)’s deviating policy \(S\) to withhold all arriving mass of \(a,b\), while reporting all arriving mass of \(p,u,v\). This coordinate-wise policy is measurable and satisfies \(0\leq r_1\leq a_1\). It withholds positive mass \(2s\) in every period and reports positive mass \(3s/4\).

The withheld vector admits template \(I\) with weight \(s\), giving exactly \(2s\) internal matched mass.

In the reported society, templates \(I\) and \(E\) are unavailable because type \(a\) is absent. The clearinghouse therefore maximizes

\(\displaystyle 3z+3w\quad\text{subject to}\quad z+w\leq s/4,\quad z,w\geq0.\)

Every maximum packing has \(z+w=s/4\). Center \(1\)’s clearinghouse utility consequently satisfies

\(\displaystyle z+2w=\frac{s}{4}+w\geq\frac{s}{4}.\)

Including internal matches, its utility under \(S\) is therefore at least

\(\displaystyle 2s+\frac{s}{4}=\frac{9s}{4}.\)

Under truthful reporting its utility is at most \(3s/2\), as already proved. Coupling the two plays using identical arrivals and permutations gives, on every path,

\(\displaystyle Q_1^F(T)-Q_1^S(T) \leq\left(\frac32-\frac94\right)\sum_{t=1}^T s_t =-\frac34\sum_{t=1}^T s_t \leq-\frac{3T}{4}.\)

In particular,

\(\displaystyle \liminf_{T\to\infty}\bigl(Q_1^F(T)-Q_1^S(T)\bigr)=-\infty.\)

Even weak truthful dominance fails; the deviation withholds positive mass on every path.

6. Why no coefficient rule can repair this instance

Credits only determine which maximum-total packing is selected. Every full-society maximum packing gives center \(1\) strictly less than its internal benchmark. Changing coefficients cannot alter that bound.

The instance is fixed: \(n=2\), \(m=4\), \(\tau=9\), and all numerical data have constant binary length. Its construction and the displayed certificate require a constant number of rational arithmetic operations. It defeats every positive coefficient vector simultaneously, and therefore every polynomially bounded rule.

The cited discrete dominance and IR claims appear as Corollaries 1 and 2 of [Hajaj et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/9322/9181). The counterexample above directly analyzes the mechanism specified in this question. Its obstruction also survives multiplying every mass by four, which makes all arrival masses integral: it arises from restricting allocations to global optima while permitting internal withholding.
