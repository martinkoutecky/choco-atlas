The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite applicant and position type sets \(P,Q\), allowed pairs \(E\subseteq P\times Q\), rational type masses \(\mu_p,\nu_q\) summing to \(1\) on each side, strict rankings of compatible opposite-side types, and a rational threshold \(\kappa\), partition two atomless unit-measure populations into those types. A feasible matching \(M\) is a measurable, measure-preserving partial bijection using only pairs in \(E\); its size \(|M|\) is its matched mass. For each person, compare their partner type (or unmatched status \(\bot\)) in \(M\) and \(N\), assigning vote \(+1,-1,\) or \(0\) for preferring \(M\), preferring \(N\), or being indifferent. Decide whether there is a feasible \(M\) with \(|M|\ge\kappa\) such that \(\Delta(M,N)\ge0\) for every feasible \(N\) with \(|N|\ge\kappa\), where \(\Delta\) is the sum of the two populations’ integrated votes; if so, return \(M\).

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even when all types have the same dyadic mass.

The key step is a rounding theorem: for equal type masses and an integer cardinality threshold after scaling, existence of a popular continuous matching is equivalent to existence of a popular finite matching. The proof uses the actual individual comparisons in the question.

We use the known finite hardness theorem: given a bipartite graph with strict preferences and an integer threshold \(k\), deciding whether some matching of size at least \(k\) is popular among all matchings of size at least \(k\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This is [Theorem 5 of Csáji, Király, Takazawa, and Yokoi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.09798).

1. Type flows and the exact challenger problem

Write \(V=P\sqcup Q\). For a type \(v\), let \(h_v\) be its mass, and let \(D_v\) consist of its compatible opposite-side types together with \(\bot\).

A matching has a type-flow vector \(x=(x_e)_{e\in E}\), where \(x_e\) is the measure matched along \(e\). Its feasibility conditions are

\(x_e\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{e\ni v}x_e\le h_v\quad(v\in V).\)

Define its outcome masses by

\(x_{v,a}=x_{\{v,a\}}\quad(a\ne\bot),\qquad x_{v,\bot}=h_v-\sum_{e\ni v}x_e.\)

Thus \(\sum_{a\in D_v}x_{v,a}=h_v\), and its size is \(|x|=\sum_e x_e\).

Every feasible flow is realizable by finitely many interval cuts and translations: partition each type interval into pieces of lengths \(x_e\) for its incident edges and one unmatched remainder. For each edge, translate the corresponding applicant piece onto the corresponding position piece.

For outcomes \(a,b\in D_v\), define

\(c_v(a,b)=1\) if \(b\succ_v a\), \(c_v(a,b)=-1\) if \(a\succ_v b\), and \(c_v(a,b)=0\) if \(a=b\).

This is the vote for the challenger when the original outcome is \(a\) and the challenger’s outcome is \(b\).

Fix a feasible matching \(M\) with flow \(x\) and \(|x|\ge\kappa\). Introduce variables \(t^v_{ab}\ge0\), representing the measure of type-\(v\) people whose outcomes are \(a\) under \(M\) and \(b\) under a challenger, and variables \(y_e\ge0\) for the challenger’s edge flows. The challenger’s best possible vote margin is the following linear program:

  • Maximize \(\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{a,b\in D_v}c_v(a,b)t^v_{ab}\).
  • Require \(\sum_{b\in D_v}t^v_{ab}=x_{v,a}\) for every \(v,a\).
  • Require \(\sum_{a\in D_v}t^v_{ab}=y_{\{v,b\}}\) for every \(v\) and every \(b\ne\bot\).
  • Require \(\sum_e y_e\ge\kappa\).

There is no separate constraint for the challenger’s unmatched outcome: its mass is determined by the first two families of equalities.

This LP describes actual challengers exactly. Every matching \(N\) supplies these variables by measuring the intersections of its outcome classes with those of \(M\), and the objective equals \(\Delta(N,M)\).

Conversely, any feasible LP solution can be realized against the fixed \(M\). Partition each measurable old-outcome class into pieces of measures \(t^v_{ab}\). For every edge, the people assigned that new edge on its two sides have the same measure \(y_e\), so they can be joined by a measure-preserving bijection modulo null sets. Here we use the standard measure-space isomorphism theorem: a positive-measure measurable subset of a Lebesgue interval, with its normalized restricted measure, is a standard atomless probability space and is isomorphic modulo null sets to an interval. These hypotheses hold for every piece being joined.

Consequently, popularity depends only on \(x\), although the vote margin between two particular matchings can depend on their individual assignments.

The LP is feasible, since \(N=M\) is allowed, and its feasible set is bounded: each \(t^v_{ab}\le h_v\), and every \(y_e\) is a marginal of such variables. Its maximum is therefore attained and is at least zero. The candidate is popular precisely when this maximum is zero.

2. An exact popularity certificate

For each \(v,a\), introduce a free dual variable \(\alpha_{v,a}\). For each \(v,b\ne\bot\), introduce a free variable \(\beta_{v,b}\), and put \(\beta_{v,\bot}=0\). Introduce also \(\lambda\ge0\).

The dual LP minimizes

\(\sum_{v,a}\alpha_{v,a}x_{v,a}-\lambda\kappa\)

subject to

\(\alpha_{v,a}-\beta_{v,b}\ge c_v(a,b)\quad(v\in V,\ a,b\in D_v),\)

and

\(\beta_{p,q}+\beta_{q,p}\ge\lambda\quad((p,q)\in E).\)

For clarity, these signs can be checked directly. Every primal feasible solution and dual feasible solution satisfy

\(\sum_{v,a,b}c_v(a,b)t^v_{ab}\le \sum_{v,a}\alpha_{v,a}x_{v,a} -\sum_{(p,q)\in E}y_{pq}(\beta_{p,q}+\beta_{q,p}) \le\sum_{v,a}\alpha_{v,a}x_{v,a}-\lambda\kappa.\)

These are exactly the standard LP dual constraints. Since the primal is feasible and bounded, strong duality applies.

Certificate theorem. A feasible flow \(x\) with \(|x|\ge\kappa\) is popular if and only if there are \(\lambda\ge0\) and numbers \(\beta_{v,a}\), with \(\beta_{v,\bot}=0\), satisfying:

  • For every positive original outcome \(x_{v,a}>0\) and every \(b\in D_v\),

\(\beta_{v,a}-\beta_{v,b}\ge c_v(a,b).\)

  • For every edge \((p,q)\),

\(\beta_{p,q}+\beta_{q,p}\ge\lambda,\)

with equality whenever \(x_{pq}>0\).

  • \(\lambda(|x|-\kappa)=0.\)

Necessity. Suppose \(x\) is popular. Its challenger LP has value zero, so choose an optimal dual solution of value zero. Its objective can be rewritten as

\(\sum_{v,a}x_{v,a}(\alpha_{v,a}-\beta_{v,a}) +\sum_{(p,q)\in E}x_{pq}(\beta_{p,q}+\beta_{q,p}-\lambda) +\lambda(|x|-\kappa).\)

Every term is nonnegative. Indeed, the diagonal dual constraint gives \(\alpha_{v,a}-\beta_{v,a}\ge c_v(a,a)=0\); the edge constraints give the second family of nonnegative terms; and \(\lambda\ge0\), \(|x|\ge\kappa\) give the last one.

Since their sum is zero, every positive \(x_{v,a}\) satisfies \(\alpha_{v,a}=\beta_{v,a}\), every positive edge has \(\beta_{p,q}+\beta_{q,p}=\lambda\), and \(\lambda(|x|-\kappa)=0\). Substituting \(\alpha_{v,a}=\beta_{v,a}\) into the other dual constraints proves item 1.

Sufficiency. Suppose the certificate exists. For any challenger \(N\), let \(y,t\) be its variables from Section 1. A row with \(x_{v,a}=0\) has all \(t^v_{ab}=0\). Hence item 1 gives

\(\Delta(N,M)\le \sum_{v,a}x_{v,a}\beta_{v,a} -\sum_{v,b}\left(\sum_a t^v_{ab}\right)\beta_{v,b}.\)

Because \(\beta_{v,\bot}=0\), the first sum equals
\(\sum_{(p,q)}x_{pq}(\beta_{p,q}+\beta_{q,p})=\lambda|x|\).
The second sum equals
\(\sum_{(p,q)}y_{pq}(\beta_{p,q}+\beta_{q,p})\ge\lambda|y|\).
Therefore

\(\Delta(N,M)\le\lambda(|x|-|y|).\)

If \(\lambda=0\), this is nonpositive. If \(\lambda>0\), item 3 gives \(|x|=\kappa\), so it is nonpositive for every allowed challenger \(|y|\ge\kappa\). This proves popularity.

A useful consequence is that a popular flow has at most two positive outcomes at each type, counting \(\bot\). For two positive outcomes \(a\succ_v b\), item 1 in both directions forces \(\beta_{v,b}-\beta_{v,a}=1\). Three positive outcomes \(a\succ_v b\succ_v c\) would then force both \(\beta_{v,c}-\beta_{v,a}=2\) and \(\beta_{v,c}-\beta_{v,a}=1\), a contradiction.

3. Integrality with a cardinality threshold

For a finite bipartite graph and integer \(k\), define

\(\mathcal F_k=\{z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}: \sum_{e\ni v}z_e\le1\text{ for every }v,\ \sum_e z_e\ge k\}.\)

Lemma. Every vertex of \(\mathcal F_k\) is integral.

Proof. Let \(A\) be the unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix, augmented by one all-ones row for cardinality. We check the Ghouila-Houri criterion for total unimodularity.

For any selected subset of rows:

  • If the cardinality row is absent, give selected applicant rows sign \(+1\) and selected position rows sign \(-1\). Every signed column sum belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\).
  • If the cardinality row is present, give it sign \(+1\) and every selected vertex row sign \(-1\). Each column sum is \(1-r\), where \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\) is the number of its selected endpoint rows, again belonging to \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

Thus \(A\) is totally unimodular. Negating the cardinality row and adjoining the rows for nonnegativity preserve total unimodularity. All right-hand sides defining \(\mathcal F_k\) are integers, so every vertex is integral. Explicitly, a vertex is determined by a nonsingular square subsystem of active constraints; its determinant is \(1\) or \(-1\), and Cramer’s rule gives integer coordinates.

Finally, the degree bounds make every integral coordinate either zero or one. Thus integral points are precisely finite matchings of size at least \(k\). \(\square\)

The polytope is bounded. Consequently, whenever it is nonempty, every affine objective attains a maximum at an integral vertex. Every nonempty face likewise contains an integral vertex.

4. Equivalence for equal masses

Suppose each side has \(n\) types, every type has mass \(1/n\), and \(\kappa=k/n\) for an integer \(k\).

We prove:

There is a popular continuous matching above threshold \(\kappa\) if and only if there is a popular finite matching above threshold \(k\) on the type graph.

From a finite matching to a continuous matching

Let \(F\) be a finite matching popular among finite matchings of size at least \(k\). Realize it by matching whole type intervals. Every person of type \(v\) then has the same original outcome \(F(v)\).

For any continuous challenger with flow \(y\), put \(z_e=ny_e\). Then \(z\in\mathcal F_k\), and its vote margin satisfies

\(n\Delta(N,M_F)= \sum_{v\in V}\left[ \sum_{e\ni v}z_e\,c_v(F(v),\operatorname{opp}_v(e)) +\left(1-\sum_{e\ni v}z_e\right)c_v(F(v),\bot) \right],\)

where \(\operatorname{opp}_v(e)\) is the other endpoint type.

The right-hand side is affine in \(z\). By Section 3, its maximum over \(\mathcal F_k\) is attained at an integral finite matching. At every such matching it is nonpositive, by the assumed popularity of \(F\). Therefore every continuous challenger also has nonpositive margin, and \(M_F\) is popular.

From a continuous matching to a finite matching

Let \(x\) be a popular continuous flow and take its certificate \((\beta,\lambda)\). Put \(z=nx\), so \(z\in\mathcal F_k\).

Consider the subset \(\mathcal R\) of \(\mathcal F_k\) defined by:

  • \(w_e=0\) whenever \(x_e=0\).
  • \(\sum_{e\ni v}w_e=1\) whenever \(x_{v,\bot}=0\).
  • If \(\lambda>0\), also require \(\sum_e w_e=k\).

The vector \(z\) belongs to \(\mathcal R\). For the last condition, this follows from the certificate identity \(\lambda(|x|-\kappa)=0\).

Each added equality makes an existing defining inequality of \(\mathcal F_k\) tight. Hence \(\mathcal R\) is a nonempty face of \(\mathcal F_k\), and Section 3 supplies an integral point \(f\in\mathcal R\). Regard \(f\) as a finite matching.

Every outcome used by \(f\) was positive under \(x\): a matched edge must have \(x_e>0\), and an unmatched vertex must have \(x_{v,\bot}>0\). Therefore the certificate’s outcome inequalities remain valid for \(f\). Every used edge remains tight. If \(\lambda>0\), \(f\) has size exactly \(k\); if \(\lambda=0\), no additional cardinality equality is needed.

Thus the same \((\beta,\lambda)\) is a popularity certificate for the whole-interval realization of \(f\), with threshold \(k/n\). That realization is popular against all continuous challengers, and therefore against all finite challengers. This proves the equivalence.

5. Polynomial reduction and dyadic masses

Take an instance of the finite threshold problem. Choose \(n\) to be the smallest power of two at least the larger side size, padding each side to \(n\) vertices with isolated vertices.

Isolated vertices are always unmatched, so they contribute zero to every vote comparison and do not change matching sizes or the answer.

Create one continuous type for each padded vertex, preserve all original edges and preferences, assign every type mass \(1/n\), and set \(\kappa=k/n\). Both population masses sum to one. All masses are dyadic.

The construction has \(\tau=2n\), preserves \(m\), and has polynomial input bit length: \(n\) is less than twice the larger original side size, except for the immaterial smallest cases.

Section 4 proves that the finite and continuous instances have the same answer. Therefore the continuous decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, already with uniform dyadic masses.

6. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\) and finite construction

We finally prove that arbitrary rational masses do not require infinitely described witnesses.

Guess an edge set \(T\subseteq E\), a set \(U\subseteq V\) of types allowed to have unmatched mass, and one of two branches. Define

\(S_v=\{a\ne\bot:\{v,a\}\in T\}\cup\{\bot:v\in U\}.\)

Use variables \(x_e,\beta_{v,a},\lambda\), and impose these linear constraints:

  • \(x_e\ge0\), \(\sum_{e\ni v}x_e\le h_v\), and \(\sum_e x_e\ge\kappa\).
  • \(x_e=0\) outside \(T\), and \(\sum_{e\ni v}x_e=h_v\) for \(v\notin U\).
  • \(\beta_{v,\bot}=0\), \(\lambda\ge0\), and

\(\beta_{v,a}-\beta_{v,b}\ge c_v(a,b)\) for every \(a\in S_v\), \(b\in D_v\).

  • \(\beta_{p,q}+\beta_{q,p}\ge\lambda\) on every edge, with equality on every edge of \(T\).
  • In the first branch impose \(\lambda=0\); in the second impose \(\sum_e x_e=\kappa\).

These systems are sound: every positive candidate outcome lies in \(S_v\), every positive edge lies in \(T\), and the chosen branch ensures the certificate’s complementarity condition. Hence every feasible solution gives a popular matching by Section 2.

They are complete: from any popular matching, choose \(T\) to be its positive edges and \(U\) its types with positive unmatched mass. Its certificate makes the first branch feasible if \(\lambda=0\), and the second feasible otherwise.

Each system has polynomially many variables and constraints and rational coefficients of polynomial bit length. A nonempty rational linear system has a rational feasible solution of polynomial bit length. To recall why, convert it to standard form with nonnegative variables; a feasible point of minimal support uses linearly independent columns. Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound then give polynomial bit length.

Thus a yes-instance has a polynomial-length rational certificate, verifiable by exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time. The decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), and hence is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

For construction, enumerate \(T,U\) and the two branches and solve the corresponding rational LPs. On finding a feasible system, realize its flow by the interval construction in Section 1. It uses at most \(2m+\tau\) interval pieces across both populations, with rational endpoints and measure-preserving translations. This supplies the requested finite description whenever a solution exists, although the search is exponential in general. A polynomial-time decision-and-construction algorithm would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
