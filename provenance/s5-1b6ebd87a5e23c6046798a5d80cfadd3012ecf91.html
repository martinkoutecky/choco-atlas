The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), supported initial rankings \(T\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a designated candidate \(p\), and an attainable rational per-capita swap radius \(\rho\), let \(x_{t,\pi}\ge 0\) be the mass moving from initial ranking \(t\) to final ranking \(\pi\in\Pi(C)\). Require \(\sum_{\pi}x_{t,\pi}=\mu_t\) for each \(t\), and \(\sum_{t,\pi}x_{t,\pi}d_K(t,\pi)=\rho\). Define \(h(x)=-\sum_{t,\pi}x_{t,\pi}\ln(x_{t,\pi}/\mu_t)\), with zero terms interpreted as \(0\); let \(A\) maximize \(h\) over all feasible plans and \(A_p\) maximize it over plans where \(p\)'s final Plurality score is at least every other candidate’s. Compute an \(\epsilon\)-additive approximation to \(I_p=A-A_p\), returning \(+\infty\) if no feasible plan makes \(p\) a winner.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a nonempty candidate set \(C\) of size \(m\), let \(\Pi(C)\) be all strict complete rankings of \(C\), and designate \(p\in C\).
A society is given by its support: a list \(T\subseteq\Pi(C)\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number \(m!\) of possible rankings.
The remaining inputs are binary rationals \(0\leq\rho\leq\binom{m}{2}\) and \(0<\epsilon<1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; destination rankings are implicit.
Write \(d_K\) for Kendall distance with unit swap costs. A plan has real masses \(x_{t,\pi}\geq0\), with \(\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(C)}x_{t,\pi}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), and \(\sum_{t\in T,\pi\in\Pi(C)}x_{t,\pi}d_K(t,\pi)=\rho\).
The final mass of ranking \(\pi\) is \(\sum_{t\in T}x_{t,\pi}\). Use mass-weighted Plurality, with every candidate tied for highest score a winner.
Define \(h(x)=-\sum_{t\in T,\pi\in\Pi(C)}x_{t,\pi}\ln(x_{t,\pi}/\mu_t)\), interpreting zero terms as \(0\). Let \(A\) be its maximum over all plans and \(A_p\) its maximum over plans making \(p\) a winner.
The computational problem is to approximate \(I_p=A-A_p\) within additive error \(\epsilon\), returning \(+\infty\) exactly when no plan makes \(p\) a winner.

Is this problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete counting of final elections at an exact unit-cost swap distance that make a designated Plurality candidate a winner is \(\#\mathrm{P}\)-hard and \(\#\mathrm{W}[1]\)-hard parameterized by swap radius ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09678)).
Continuous Plurality swap bribery with an upper cost budget is polynomial-time solvable; this concerns winning feasibility, not the exact-radius entropy quantity above ([source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25444v1)). No result settling this entropy approximation problem was located.

  • Replaced the undefined attainable-radius promise by the explicit range from zero to the maximum Kendall distance per voter, including both endpoints.
  • Specified that plan masses may be arbitrary nonnegative reals and that all destination rankings are implicit.
  • Specified a binary rational accuracy parameter between zero and one, total input bit length L, and polynomial dependence on log(1/epsilon).

Result

The problem is solvable in deterministic time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\). Infeasibility of making \(p\) a winner at the exact prescribed cost can also be decided exactly.

The proof has two ingredients: an exact compression of the destination rankings, followed by entropy optimization with explicit control of numerical precision.

Throughout, logarithms in entropy expressions are natural logarithms.

1. Counting rankings with a specified top candidate and distance

Set \(D=\binom{m}{2}\). For a source ranking \(t\) and candidate \(c\), let \(r_t(c)\) be the number of candidates preceding \(c\) in \(t\).

For \(j\geq 0\), let \(a_{j,\ell}\) count permutations of \(j\) elements with exactly \(\ell\) inversions relative to a fixed reference order. Put \(a_{0,0}=1\), and interpret coefficients outside their possible range as zero. Inserting the largest element into a permutation of \(j-1\) elements creates any number of inversions from \(0\) through \(j-1\), exactly once for each insertion position. Consequently,

\(a_{j,\ell}=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}a_{j-1,\ell-i}\).

This recurrence computes all coefficients needed below in polynomial time. Every coefficient is at most \(j!\), so its binary encoding length is \(O(m\log m)\).

Define

\(N_{t,c,k}=\left|\{\pi\in\Pi(C):\operatorname{top}(\pi)=c,\ d_K(t,\pi)=k\}\right|\).

Claim. For every \(t,c,k\),

\(N_{t,c,k}=a_{m-1,k-r_t(c)}\).

Proof. If \(c\) is first in the destination ranking, exactly the \(r_t(c)\) pairs consisting of \(c\) and a candidate preceding it in \(t\) are inverted. The inversions among the remaining candidates are precisely the inversions of their destination order relative to their order in \(t\). Every permutation of those remaining candidates gives exactly one destination ranking with \(c\) first. Thus the total distance is \(r_t(c)\) plus its residual inversion count. ∎

Let

\(J=\{(t,c,k):t\in T,\ c\in C,\ 0\leq k\leq D,\ N_{t,c,k}>0\}\),

and write \(n=|J|\). Then \(n\leq\tau m(D+1)=O(\tau m^3)\). All class sizes indexed by \(J\) are available exactly in polynomial time.

2. Exact compression of feasibility and entropy

For a plan \(x\), define its aggregated masses by

\(y_{t,c,k}=\sum_{\pi:\operatorname{top}(\pi)=c,\ d_K(t,\pi)=k}x_{t,\pi}\).

Let \(P\) be the rational polytope defined by

\(y_{t,c,k}\geq 0\),

\(\sum_{c,k:(t,c,k)\in J}y_{t,c,k}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t\in T\),

\(\sum_{(t,c,k)\in J}k\,y_{t,c,k}=\rho\).

For candidate \(c\), its final Plurality score is

\(s_c(y)=\sum_{t,k:(t,c,k)\in J}y_{t,c,k}\).

Thus define the winning polytope

\(P_p=\{y\in P:s_p(y)\geq s_c(y)\text{ for every }c\in C\setminus\{p\}\}\).

These are polynomial-size rational descriptions, and both polytopes lie in \([0,1]^n\).

Every original plan projects into \(P\). Conversely, every \(y\in P\) lifts to a plan: divide \(y_{t,c,k}\) equally among the \(N_{t,c,k}\) destination rankings in its class. This preserves source masses, exact cost, and all Plurality scores. In particular,

\(P_p\neq\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{some plan of exact cost }\rho\text{ makes }p\text{ a winner}\).

Therefore exact rational linear programming decides the required infeasibility condition in polynomial time.

Now consider a class of size \(N\), associated with source mass \(\mu\), whose total allocated mass is \(y\). Concavity of \(u\mapsto-u\ln(u/\mu)\), continuously extended at zero, gives

\(-\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i\ln(x_i/\mu)\leq-y\ln\!\left(y/(\mu N)\right)\)

whenever the nonnegative \(x_i\) sum to \(y\). Equality holds when every \(x_i=y/N\), including when \(y=0\).

Consequently define

\(H(y)=-\sum_{(t,c,k)\in J}y_{t,c,k}\ln\!\left(\frac{y_{t,c,k}}{\mu_tN_{t,c,k}}\right)\).

Uniform allocation within each class attains this expression. Hence the compression is exact:

\(A=\max_{y\in P}H(y),\qquad A_p=\max_{y\in P_p}H(y)\)

when \(P_p\) is nonempty.

Both maxima exist: their feasible sets are compact, and \(H\) is continuous under the prescribed zero convention.

3. Uniformly approximating the entropy objective

Index the coordinates by \(j\in J\), and put \(\alpha_j=\mu_tN_{t,c,k}\) for \(j=(t,c,k)\). Define the convex objective

\(F(y)=-H(y)=\sum_{j\in J}y_j\ln(y_j/\alpha_j)\).

The numbers \(\alpha_j\) are positive rationals of polynomial encoding length. Moreover,

\(2^{-L}\leq\alpha_j\leq m!\).

Thus the integer \(B=L+m^2+2\) satisfies \(|\ln\alpha_j|\leq B\).

The derivative of \(F\) is unbounded near zero, so we first approximate it uniformly. For a dyadic rational \(\delta=2^{-s}\leq 1/2\), let

\(F_\delta(y)=\sum_{j\in J}(y_j+\delta)\ln\!\left(\frac{y_j+\delta}{\alpha_j}\right)\).

This changes only the objective; the feasible sets retain their exact cost equalities and winning inequalities.

For \(g(u)=u\ln u\), with \(g(0)=0\), and \(0\leq u\leq1\),

\(|g(u+\delta)-g(u)|\leq\int_u^{u+\delta}|\ln v+1|\,dv\).

On \(0<v\leq3/2\), we have \(|\ln v+1|\leq(-\ln v)_++2\). Because \((-\ln v)_+\) is decreasing, its integral over an interval of length \(\delta\) is at most its integral over \((0,\delta)\). Therefore

\(|g(u+\delta)-g(u)|\leq\delta\bigl(\ln(1/\delta)+3\bigr)\).

It follows that, throughout \([0,1]^n\),

\(|F_\delta(y)-F(y)|\leq n\delta\bigl(\ln(1/\delta)+3+B\bigr)\leq n2^{-s}(s+B+3)\).

We can make this bound at most \(\epsilon/8\) with polynomially many precision bits. Explicitly, put

\(q=\lceil\log_2(1/\epsilon)\rceil,\qquad v=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\),

\(s=\max\{2B+16,\ 2(v+q+4)\}\).

Here \(s+B+3\leq2s\leq2^{s/2}\), using \(s\geq16\). Hence

\(n2^{-s}(s+B+3)\leq n2^{-s/2}\leq2^{-q-4}\leq\epsilon/16\).

In particular, the desired uniform error bound holds, and \(s\) is polynomially bounded in the input parameters and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\).

The smoothed objective is differentiable and convex, since

\(\frac{\partial F_\delta}{\partial y_j}=\ln\!\left(\frac{y_j+\delta}{\alpha_j}\right)+1,\qquad \frac{\partial^2 F_\delta}{\partial y_j^2}=\frac{1}{y_j+\delta}>0\).

On \([0,1]^n\), every derivative has absolute value at most \(s+B+2\). Thus \(F_\delta\) has the rational Euclidean Lipschitz bound \(G=n(s+B+2)\).

Its values and gradients are computable to arbitrary prescribed accuracy in polynomial time. To verify this without assuming exact transcendental arithmetic, scale a positive rational argument as \(2^e w\), where \(1\leq w<2\), and use

\(\ln w=2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2i+1}}{2i+1},\qquad z=\frac{w-1}{w+1},\quad 0\leq z<1/3\).

The same series with \(z=1/3\) computes \(\ln2\). Its tail decays geometrically, so polynomially many rational operations give any requested number of accuracy bits. The multiplications and additions needed for objective and gradient evaluation preserve this polynomial bound.

4. Convex value optimization, including boundary cases

For completeness, the following lemma supplies the required dependence on accuracy and handles feasible sets of lower dimension.

Lemma. Let \(Q\subseteq[0,1]^n\) be a nonempty polytope given by rational linear constraints. Let \(f\) be convex and differentiable on a neighborhood of \(Q\), with a known rational Lipschitz bound \(G\geq1\). Suppose objective values and gradients at rational points of \(Q\) can be approximated to error \(2^{-b}\) in time polynomial in the query encoding length and \(b\). Then \(\min_Q f\) can be approximated within additive error \(\eta\), for \(0<\eta<1\), in polynomial time in these data and \(\log(1/\eta)\).

We use two standard bit-complexity results: exact rational linear programming, and the rounded central-cut ellipsoid method. The latter maintains containment after each rational central cut and shrinks volume by a factor at most \(\exp(-1/(4(d+1)))\) per iteration in dimension \(d\); polynomially many iterations with polynomial-bit cut normals require polynomial bit complexity. These are standard ellipsoid-method results; see [Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf).

Proof of the lemma.

First reduce to the affine hull of \(Q\). For each inequality \(a_i^\top y\leq b_i\), use exact linear programming to maximize its slack \(b_i-a_i^\top y\). An optimum of zero identifies an inequality that holds with equality everywhere on \(Q\).

For every other inequality, obtain a rational feasible witness with positive slack. Averaging these witnesses gives a rational point \(z\) strictly satisfying every inequality not forced to equality. All witnesses, and their average, have polynomial encoding length.

Let \(\mathcal H\) be the affine space defined by the supplied equalities and all inequalities identified as forced equalities. A sufficiently small neighborhood of \(z\) within \(\mathcal H\) satisfies every remaining inequality. Therefore \(\mathcal H=\operatorname{aff}(Q)\).

Rational Gaussian elimination gives a parametrization

\(y=z+Vu\),

where \(V\) has full column rank and polynomial encoding length. We may choose the coordinates of \(u\) to be free coordinates of \(y-z\), so \(V\) has an identity submatrix. If the affine dimension is zero, evaluate \(f(z)\) and stop.

Otherwise, in dimension \(d\geq1\), the transformed polytope \(Q'\) lies in a ball of rational radius \(R=d+1\), centered at zero. It contains a ball centered at zero of positive rational radius

\(r=\min\left\{1,\ \min_i\frac{b_i-a_i^\top z}{1+\|a_i^\top V\|_1}\right\}\),

where the inner minimum ranges over inequalities not forced to equality. Indeed, each numerator is positive, and this radius prevents any such inequality from being violated.

Both \(r\) and \(R\) have polynomial encoding length. In particular, \(\log(1/r)\) is polynomially bounded. The transformed objective

\(\widetilde f(u)=f(z+Vu)\)

has a rational Lipschitz upper bound \(M\geq1\) of polynomial encoding length, obtained from \(G\) and the entries of \(V\). Its gradient is \(V^\top\nabla f(z+Vu)\), so its value and gradient approximation oracles remain polynomial-time.

Let \(u^*\) minimize \(\widetilde f\), and write \(f^*=\widetilde f(u^*)\). Set

\(\lambda=\frac{\eta}{8MR}\).

For every \(w\) in the radius-\(r\) ball, convexity and Lipschitz continuity imply

\(\widetilde f((1-\lambda)u^*+\lambda w)\leq f^*+\lambda M\|w-u^*\|\leq f^*+\eta/4\).

Thus the set

\(K=\{u\in Q':\widetilde f(u)\leq f^*+\eta/4\}\)

contains a ball of radius \(\lambda r\).

Run the rounded central-cut ellipsoid method from the radius-\(R\) ball. At an infeasible center, a violated rational inequality provides a central cut retaining \(Q'\).

At a feasible center \(u\), record an objective approximation with error at most \(\eta/8\), and compute a rational gradient approximation \(\widehat g\) satisfying

\(\|\widehat g-\nabla\widetilde f(u)\|\leq\kappa,\qquad \kappa=\eta/(16R)\).

When \(\widehat g\neq0\), supply the central cut

\(\widehat g^\top(w-u)\leq0\).

Suppose \(\widetilde f(u)>f^*+\eta/2\). For every \(w\in K\), convexity gives

\(\nabla\widetilde f(u)^\top(w-u)\leq\widetilde f(w)-\widetilde f(u)<-\eta/4\).

Since both points lie in the radius-\(R\) ball, the gradient approximation changes this inner product by at most \(2R\kappa=\eta/8\). Therefore this cut retains all of \(K\).

If \(\widehat g=0\), convexity instead gives

\(f^*\geq\widetilde f(u)-2R\kappa=\widetilde f(u)-\eta/8\),

so the recorded objective value already approximates \(f^*\) within \(\eta\).

Otherwise, if every queried feasible center had objective greater than \(f^*+\eta/2\), every cut would retain \(K\). After

\(O\!\left(d^2\log\frac{R}{\lambda r}\right)\)

iterations, however, the ellipsoid volume becomes smaller than that of the radius-\(\lambda r\) ball contained in \(K\), a contradiction.

Hence a feasible center with objective at most \(f^*+\eta/2\) must have been queried. The smallest recorded approximate value lies between \(f^*-\eta/8\) and \(f^*+5\eta/8\), and therefore has error less than \(\eta\).

Finally, the iteration count, query encoding lengths, and required oracle precision are polynomially bounded: \(\log(1/r)\), \(\log M\), and \(\log R\) are polynomially bounded, and the accuracy enters through \(\log(1/\eta)\). This proves the lemma. ∎

This argument requires neither a positive optimum in every coordinate nor strict winning inequalities.

5. The algorithm and its error guarantee

If \(m=1\), the input necessarily has \(\rho=0\). There is only one ranking, \(p\) wins, and \(A=A_p=0\). Return zero.

Assume \(m\geq2\).

  • Compute the class sizes \(N_{t,c,k}\), and construct \(P\) and \(P_p\).
  • Decide feasibility of \(P_p\) by exact rational linear programming. If it is empty, return \(+\infty\).
  • Choose \(\delta\) as in Section 3.
  • Apply the lemma to minimize \(F_\delta\) over each of \(P\) and \(P_p\), with value accuracy \(\epsilon/8\).
  • If the resulting rational values are \(\widehat v\) and \(\widehat v_p\), return \(\widehat I=\widehat v_p-\widehat v\).

The unconstrained polytope \(P\) is always nonempty. To see this, let \(\operatorname{rev}(t)\) reverse \(t\), and send fractions \(1-\rho/D\) and \(\rho/D\) of each source mass to \(t\) and \(\operatorname{rev}(t)\), respectively. Their distances are zero and \(D\), so the total cost is exactly \(\rho\).

Uniform approximation of the objective implies, for either feasible polytope \(Q\),

\(\left|\min_Q F_\delta-\min_Q F\right|\leq\epsilon/8\).

Combining this with optimization error gives

\(|\widehat v-\min_P F|\leq\epsilon/4,\qquad |\widehat v_p-\min_{P_p}F|\leq\epsilon/4\).

By the exact compression,

\(I_p=A-A_p=\min_{P_p}F-\min_P F\).

Therefore

\(|\widehat I-I_p|\leq\epsilon/2<\epsilon\).

The class counts, rational polytope descriptions, smoothing precision, and optimization oracles all have polynomial encoding length or running time in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\). This proves the claimed deterministic polynomial-time bound.

6. Why discrete counting hardness does not transfer

The cited discrete problem counts entire elections subject to exact total distance and winner conditions, and is hard even for Plurality. [Boehmer et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09678)

Here, only the individual ranking-class sizes must be counted. Those sizes have the polynomial-time recurrence proved above. Within each class, uniform allocation maximizes entropy exactly, leaving a polynomial-dimensional convex optimization problem over real masses.
