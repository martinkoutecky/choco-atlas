The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of weight types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), integer weights \(a_t\), and a rational quota \(\theta\) with \(0<\theta\leq\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t a_t\), let \(D\) be the positive integers \(d\) for which every \(d\mu_t\) and \(d\theta\) is integral. For each \(d\in D\), form the original weighted voting game with \(d\mu_t\) players of type \(t\), weight \(a_t\), and threshold \(d\theta\). Let \(x_t^{(d)}\) be each type’s per-player award in that game’s nucleolus and set \(z_t^{(d)}=d x_t^{(d)}\). Determine whether \(z^{(d)}\) converges as \(d\) grows through \(D\), and, if it does, compute its limit to any requested precision.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is specified by its support \(T\), with \(\tau=|T|\) types of positive binary rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Each type has a binary encoded weight \(a_t\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\). Put \(A=\sum_t\mu_ta_t\), and require a binary rational quota \(0<\theta\leq A\).
Let \(D=\{d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:d\theta\in\mathbb Z,\ d\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\text{ for every }t\}\). For each \(d\in D\), the player set \(N_d\) contains \(d\mu_t\) players of weight \(a_t\); a coalition has value one exactly when its total weight is at least \(d\theta\), and zero otherwise.
Use the nucleolus over nonnegative allocations totaling one, without additional singleton lower bounds. Symmetry gives a common per-player award \(x_t^{(d)}\) within each type; set \(z_t^{(d)}=d x_t^{(d)}\).
The computational problem is to decide convergence as \(d\to\infty\) through \(D\) and, when convergent, compute the limit with absolute error at most \(2^{-p}\) in every coordinate, for an input precision \(p\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Let \(m=\max_t a_t\) and \(L\) be the total binary input length excluding precision; state running times in \(m,\tau,L,p\), with polynomial time measured in \(\tau,L,p\).

Does \(z^{(d)}\) converge for every admissible input, and is the convergence-and-limit computation problem in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The discrete nucleolus admits a pseudo-polynomial algorithm polynomial in the player count, integer weights and quota. [Pashkovich](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02670)
For interior normalized quotas, sufficiently many replicas have nucleolus equal to normalized weights; this applies directly to the rational-support construction above. [Kurz–Napel–Nohn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.0004)
At the endpoint quota, the known rule for games with veto players assigns equal shares to veto players and zero to others. Consequently, these existing results already settle the stated limit problem; it is not an open target. [Elkind et al.](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/leslieann.goldberg/papers/wvg.pdf)

Result

Convergence holds for every admissible input, and the limit can be computed exactly in polynomial time. In fact, the sequence is eventually constant.

Define the total mass of positive-weight types by
\(M_+=\sum_{t:a_t>0}\mu_t\).
Then, for every supported type \(t\),

\( \displaystyle \lim_{\substack{d\to\infty\\d\in D}}z_t^{(d)} = \begin{cases} a_t/A,&0<\theta<A,\\ 1/M_+,&\theta=A\text{ and }a_t>0,\\ 0,&\theta=A\text{ and }a_t=0. \end{cases} \)

Thus the convergence-and-limit computation problem belongs to \(\mathrm P\), with polynomial time measured in \(\tau,L,p\).

1. Admissible replication sizes and normalization

Since \(0<\theta\le A\), we have \(A>0\), so at least one type has positive weight and \(M_+>0\).

Let \(d_0\) be the least common multiple of the reduced denominators of \(\theta\) and all \(\mu_t\). A positive integer makes each of these rationals integral upon multiplication exactly when it is divisible by every reduced denominator. Consequently,
\(D=\{kd_0:k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\}\),
which is unbounded.

For \(d\in D\), the total player count is
\(\sum_t d\mu_t=d\),
and the total weight is
\(\sum_t d\mu_ta_t=dA\).
Therefore the normalized quota is \(q=\theta/A\), and a type-\(t\) player has normalized weight \(a_t/(dA)\).

We use excess \(e(S,x)=v(S)-\sum_{i\in S}x_i\). The nucleolus lexicographically minimizes the decreasingly sorted vector of all coalition excesses over nonnegative allocations totaling one.

2. Interior quota: eventual exact equality

Assume \(0<\theta<A\), and put
\(\delta=\min\{\theta/A,1-\theta/A\}>0\)
and
\(\eta=\min_t\mu_t>0\).

The theorem we use is Proposition 2 of [Kurz–Napel–Nohn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.0004): for an integer-weight voting representation with normalized quota \(q\), largest weight \(w_{\max}\), \(s\) distinct weight values, and minimum multiplicity \(n_{\min}\) among those values, the nucleolus equals normalized weights whenever
\(\min\{q,1-q\}n_{\min}>2s w_{\max}^2\).

We verify its hypotheses for our games:

  • All player weights are nonnegative integers, with largest weight \(m\).
  • There are at most \(\tau\) distinct weight values, including zero if present.
  • The multiplicity of a weight value \(b\) is

\(d\sum_{t:a_t=b}\mu_t\), which is at least \(d\eta\).

  • The normalized quota is the fixed interior value \(\theta/A\).

Hence the theorem’s inequality holds whenever
\(\delta d\eta>2\tau m^2\).

There is also a convention issue to check: the cited paper imposes singleton lower bounds. If \(d>m/\theta\), then every singleton has weight at most \(m<d\theta\), so every singleton is losing. Its imputation domain then coincides exactly with the nonnegative simplex required here.

Thus, for every \(d\in D\) satisfying

\( \displaystyle d>\max\left\{\frac m\theta,\frac{2\tau m^2}{\delta\eta}\right\}, \)

the theorem applies under our allocation convention and gives

\( \displaystyle x_t^{(d)}=\frac{a_t}{dA}, \qquad z_t^{(d)}=d x_t^{(d)}=\frac{a_t}{A}. \)

This proves eventual constancy and the asserted interior limit. Types sharing the same numerical weight cause no difficulty: their multiplicities are aggregated in the hypothesis check.

3. Endpoint quota: equality for every replication size

Assume \(\theta=A\). Fix \(d\in D\), and let \(V_d\) be the set of positive-weight players. Its cardinality is \(dM_+>0\).

The quota equals the total weight \(dA\). Omitting any player in \(V_d\) strictly decreases coalition weight below the quota; including all of \(V_d\) achieves the quota. Therefore

\( \displaystyle v(S)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V_d\subseteq S. \)

In particular, the positive-weight players are precisely the veto players, while zero-weight players are null.

For completeness, we prove the allocation rule directly. Any nonnegative allocation totaling one and supported on \(V_d\) gives every winning coalition allocation one. Its winning-coalition excesses are zero, and its losing-coalition excesses are nonpositive. Since the empty and grand coalitions always have excess zero, the minimum possible largest excess is exactly zero.

The nucleolus must attain that minimum. Applying its nonpositive-excess condition to the winning coalition \(V_d\) yields \(x(V_d)\ge1\). Efficiency and nonnegativity then imply \(x(V_d)=1\) and zero awards outside \(V_d\).

Every permutation of \(V_d\) preserves the game, because winning depends only on containing all of \(V_d\). Uniqueness and symmetry of the nucleolus therefore give the same award to every member of \(V_d\). Hence

\( \displaystyle x_t^{(d)} = \begin{cases} 1/(dM_+),&a_t>0,\\ 0,&a_t=0, \end{cases} \qquad z_t^{(d)} = \begin{cases} 1/M_+,&a_t>0,\\ 0,&a_t=0. \end{cases} \)

This holds for every \(d\in D\). It also agrees with the veto-player rule in Theorem 2 of [Elkind et al.](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/leslieann.goldberg/papers/wvg.pdf).

4. Exact computation and bit complexity

Write the input masses as \(\mu_t=u_t/v_t\), with positive integer denominators. Set

\( \displaystyle C=\prod_t v_t,\qquad U=\sum_t u_ta_t(C/v_t),\qquad B=\sum_{t:a_t>0}u_t(C/v_t). \)

Then \(A=U/C\) and \(M_+=B/C\). Compare \(\theta\) with \(U/C\) by exact cross multiplication. The algorithm outputs:

  • For \(\theta<A\), the rational coordinate \(a_tC/U\).
  • For \(\theta=A\), the rational coordinate \(C/B\) when \(a_t>0\), and zero otherwise.

These are exact answers, so their error is zero for every requested precision \(p\).

The integers \(C,U,B\), and all output numerators and denominators, have \(O(L)\) bits. Indeed, the bit length of the denominator product is bounded by the sum of the input denominator lengths; the products in each summand have \(O(L)\) bits, and summation adds at most \(O(\log\tau)\) bits.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, the exact rational computation takes \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations, without requiring fractions to be reduced. If dyadic approximations are desired, output

\( \displaystyle \widehat z_t=2^{-p}\left\lfloor2^p z_t\right\rfloor. \)

Integer division computes these in \(O(\tau(L+p)^2)\) bit operations, and
\(0\le z_t-\widehat z_t<2^{-p}\).

Thus the running time is polynomial in \(\tau,L,p\); its dependence on \(m\) is through its binary encoding, already included in \(L\). The potentially large replication threshold need never be reached computationally. [Pashkovich’s pseudo-polynomial algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02670) concerns computing a finite game’s nucleolus; the formulas proved above compute this limit directly.
