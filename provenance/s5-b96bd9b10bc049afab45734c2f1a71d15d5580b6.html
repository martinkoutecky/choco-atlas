The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over recurring types \(t=(v_t,x_t,w_t)\), per-capita capacities \(\beta\), a designated true type \(t_0\), a compact report domain \(\mathcal R\), and accuracy \(\epsilon\), compute an \(\epsilon\)-accurate maximum utility gain and a witnessing report for a zero-mass user. Assume every PF resource price is positive and reports can choose values proportional to the price vector and any demand cap up to \(\bar x\). The continuum PF allocation uses per-type bundles \(a_t\) and maximizes \(\sum_t\mu_t w_t\log(v_t^\top a_t)\), subject to \(0\le a_t\le x_t\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t a_{t,m}\le\beta_m\). If \(\lambda^\star\) is its resource-price vector, a report \((v',x')\) gives the tagged user \(q_{\lambda^\star}(v',x')\in\arg\max_{0\le q\le x'}\{w_{t_0}\log(v'^\top q)-(\lambda^\star)^\top q\}\). Maximize \(v_{t_0}^\top\min(q_{\lambda^\star}(v',x'),x_{t_0})-v_{t_0}^\top\min(q_{\lambda^\star}(v_{t_0},x_{t_0}),x_{t_0})\) over \((v',x')\in\mathcal R\).

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types \(t=(v_t,x_t,w_t)\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) divisible resources, positive rational per-capita capacities \(\beta\), and a zero-mass tagged user with true type \(t_0=(v_0,x_0,w_0)\). All true values, demands, and weights are positive rationals; weights are fixed and cannot be reported.
Continuum proportional fairness (PF) maximizes \(\sum_t\mu_t w_t\log(v_t^\top a_t)\) subject to \(0\leq a_t\leq x_t\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t a_{t,j}\leq\beta_j\) for every resource \(j\). Define \(\lambda^\star\) as the minimum-Euclidean-norm optimal multiplier vector for these resource constraints; assume every optimal resource-price vector is strictly positive.
The compact report domain \(\mathcal R\) is an input rational polytope described by linear inequalities, contained in \([\underline v,\overline v]^m\times[0,\bar x]^m\), where \(\underline v,\overline v,\bar x>0\) are rational, and containing \((v_0,x_0)\). Write \(U(q)=v_0^\top\min(q,x_0)\), with componentwise minimum, and \(G=\max_{(v',x')\in\mathcal R}\{U(q_{\lambda^\star}(v',x'))-U(q_{\lambda^\star}(v_0,x_0))\}\). Given rational \(0<\epsilon<1\), the computational problem is to approximate \(G\) within additive \(\epsilon\) and obtain a feasible report whose gain is at least \(G-\epsilon\).

Result

Under the stated promises, the problem is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, the optimal gain and an optimal feasible report can be computed exactly as rational numbers in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The decisive assumption is that the report domain contains an entire demand box at some valuation vector proportional to \(\lambda^\star\).

1. The global reporting problem reduces to a linear program

Write \(\lambda=\lambda^\star\) and \(w=w_0\). By assumption, every coordinate of \(\lambda\) is positive.

Every induced allocation has expenditure at most the user’s weight

Consider any report \((v',x')\), and let \(q\) be a maximizer of its reported objective.

If \(x'=0\), then \(q=0\). Otherwise, \(v'^\top q>0\): a bundle with positive reported utility exists, whereas a bundle with zero reported utility has logarithmic objective equal to negative infinity.

For \(0\leq h<1\), the bundle \((1-h)q\) remains feasible. The change in reported objective is

\[ w\log(1-h)+h\lambda^\top q. \]

Optimality of \(q\) implies that its right derivative at \(h=0\) is nonpositive. Consequently,

\[ \lambda^\top q\leq w. \]

This conclusion holds for every maximizer, including the prescribed minimum-norm maximizer.

Every bundle within that budget can be induced uniquely

Let \(z\in[0,\bar x]^m\) satisfy \(\lambda^\top z\leq w\). Choose the scale \(c>0\) supplied by the report-domain promise, and report \((c\lambda,z)\).

If \(z=0\), the induced allocation is \(0\). Otherwise, for a feasible bundle \(q\leq z\), the reported objective depends only on its expenditure \(s=\lambda^\top q\):

\[ w\log(cs)-s. \]

Its derivative is \(w/s-1\), so it is strictly increasing on \(0<s\leq w\). Moreover, positivity of \(\lambda\) implies that \(q\neq z\) and \(q\leq z\) give \(\lambda^\top q<\lambda^\top z\). Thus \(z\) is the unique maximizing allocation.

Exact characterization of maximum attainable utility

Since the true report belongs to \(\mathcal R\), we have \(x_0\leq\bar x\) componentwise. Define

\[ H=\max\{v_0^\top z:0\leq z\leq x_0,\ \lambda^\top z\leq w\}. \]

For any induced allocation \(q\), put \(z=\min(q,x_0)\). Then \(0\leq z\leq x_0\), and positivity of \(\lambda\) gives

\[ \lambda^\top z\leq\lambda^\top q\leq w. \]

Therefore \(U(q)=v_0^\top z\leq H\).

Conversely, any optimizer \(z^\star\) defining \(H\) is induced uniquely by an aligned report \((c\lambda,z^\star)\). Hence

\[ G=H-U\bigl(q_\lambda(v_0,x_0)\bigr). \]

Thus, once the specified prices are known, global optimization is a rational linear program. The remaining issue is computing those prices with a polynomial bit-complexity guarantee.

2. The society’s optimal utilities are rational with polynomial bit length

Let \(a^\star\) be a society-optimal allocation, and define

\[ u_t^\star=v_t^\top a_t^\star,\qquad s_t=\frac{w_t}{u_t^\star}. \]

A strictly feasible allocation with positive utilities exists because all capacities and demands are positive. Therefore the optimum has positive utilities, Slater’s condition holds, and optimal resource multipliers exist.

The optimal utility vector is unique: the feasible utility vectors form a convex set, and \(\sum_t\mu_t w_t\log u_t\) is strictly concave on positive utility vectors.

Choose any optimal multiplier vector \(\lambda\). It is strictly positive by the promise. Complementary slackness therefore gives

\[ \sum_t\mu_t a_{t,j}^\star=\beta_j \]

for every resource \(j\).

A graph of coordinates strictly between their bounds

Construct a bipartite graph whose vertices are types and resources, with an edge \((t,j)\) precisely when

\[ 0<a_{t,j}^\star<x_{t,j}. \]

Stationarity on such an edge gives

\[ \lambda_j=s_t v_{t,j}. \]

Consider a connected component containing an edge. By choosing a root and following a spanning tree, we can construct positive rational numbers \(r_t,r_j\) and a positive scalar \(\kappa\) such that

\[ s_t=\kappa r_t,\qquad \lambda_j=\kappa r_j,\qquad r_j=r_t v_{t,j} \]

on its edges.

Each \(r_t\) or \(r_j\) is a product of at most \(m+\tau\) input valuations or their reciprocals. Consistency follows from the actual positive numbers \(s_t,\lambda_j\).

For each type in the component, define its utility from coordinates at their upper bounds:

\[ d_t=\sum_{j:a_{t,j}^\star=x_{t,j}}v_{t,j}x_{t,j}. \]

For each resource in the component, define its capacity remaining after those coordinates:

\[ e_j=\beta_j-\sum_{t:a_{t,j}^\star=x_{t,j}}\mu_t x_{t,j}. \]

Both quantities are rational. Resource clearing implies

\[ e_j=\sum_{t:0<a_{t,j}^\star<x_{t,j}}\mu_t a_{t,j}^\star. \]

Multiply each type’s utility equation by \(\mu_t r_t\), sum over types in the component, and use \(r_t v_{t,j}=r_j\) on every edge. This gives

\[ \frac{\sum_t\mu_t w_t}{\kappa} = \sum_t\mu_t r_t d_t+\sum_j r_j e_j. \]

All sums here range over the component. Its right-hand side is positive: every resource vertex has an incident edge with positive allocation. Thus

\[ \kappa= \frac{\sum_t\mu_t w_t} {\sum_t\mu_t r_t d_t+\sum_j r_j e_j} \]

is rational, and so is \(u_t^\star=w_t/(\kappa r_t)\).

A type with no incident edge has every allocation coordinate equal to zero or its demand bound. Its utility is therefore rational directly.

Uniform bit bound

These formulas also give a polynomial bit bound independent of the unknown graph.

Each path product uses at most \(m+\tau\) input factors. Expanding the denominator in the displayed formula for \(\kappa\) gives at most \(2\tau m+m\) terms, each involving at most \(m+\tau+3\) input factors. Addition and multiplication of that many rationals have polynomial bit complexity.

For example, the conservative bound

\[ B=20(\tau m+m+\tau+1)(m+\tau+4)(L+1) \]

bounds the numerator and denominator bit lengths of every reduced \(u_t^\star\). Indeed, a sum of \(k\) rationals whose numerators and denominators have at most \(h\) bits has numerator and denominator lengths at most \(kh+\lceil\log_2 k\rceil+2\); applying this estimate to the formulas above yields the stated bound.

In particular, every reduced denominator is at most \(Q=2^B\).

3. Computing those utilities exactly in polynomial time

We use two standard algorithmic facts: rational linear programming is exactly solvable in polynomial time, and the bit-complexity version of the ellipsoid method permits additive optimization of a convex objective over a bounded rational polytope when separation and first-order evaluation are polynomial-time computable and the geometric bounds have polynomial encoding length. These are classical results in [Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver, *Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-97881-4).

The logarithmic singularity must be excluded before applying the second fact. We now do that explicitly.

A truncation containing the optimum

Set

\[ \theta=\min\left\{\frac12,\ \min_j\frac{\beta_j}{2\sum_t\mu_t x_{t,j}}\right\}, \qquad b_{t,j}=\theta x_{t,j}, \]

and write \(u_t^b=v_t^\top b_t\) and \(W=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\).

The allocation \(b\) is strictly inside every demand box and uses at most half of every resource capacity.

The feasible direction from \(a^\star\) towards \(b\), together with optimality, gives

\[ \sum_t\mu_t w_t\left(\frac{u_t^b}{u_t^\star}-1\right)\leq0. \]

Every term in \(\sum_t\mu_t w_t u_t^b/u_t^\star\) is positive, so each is at most \(W\). Consequently,

\[ u_t^\star\geq\frac{\mu_t w_t u_t^b}{W}. \]

Define

\[ \ell_t=\frac{\mu_t w_t u_t^b}{2W}. \]

We may therefore restrict optimization to the rational polytope

\[ K=\left\{a:0\leq a_t\leq x_t,\ \sum_t\mu_t a_{t,j}\leq\beta_j,\ v_t^\top a_t\geq\ell_t\right\}. \]

It contains the optimum and has an explicit interior point \(b\).

For completeness, an inner-ball radius is

\[ r=\frac14\min\left\{ \min_{t,j}b_{t,j},\ \min_j\beta_j,\ \min_t\frac{u_t^b}{\sum_jv_{t,j}} \right\}. \]

The upper-box margins at \(b\) are at least \(b_{t,j}\); resource margins are at least \(\beta_j/2\); and utility margins are at least \(u_t^b/2\). The resource constraint normals have Euclidean norm at most \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and utility constraint normals have norm at most \(\sum_jv_{t,j}\). These observations verify that this ball lies in \(K\).

An outer radius is \(R=1+\sum_{t,j}x_{t,j}\). Both radii have polynomial encoding length.

Objective accuracy implies utility accuracy

Put

\[ M_t=\sum_jv_{t,j}x_{t,j},\qquad \alpha=\min_t\frac{\mu_t w_t}{M_t^2}>0. \]

As a function of utilities, the negative PF objective has Hessian entries \(\mu_t w_t/u_t^2\), which are at least \(\alpha\). Strong convexity and the first-order optimality condition therefore imply that an allocation with PF objective gap at most \(\delta\) satisfies

\[ \|u(a)-u^\star\|_2\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\delta}{\alpha}}. \]

The ellipsoid hypotheses hold on \(K\):

  • Linear inequalities provide exact rational separation.
  • The objective gradient in allocation coordinates is the rational vector with entries \(\mu_t w_t v_{t,j}/u_t(a)\).
  • Utilities are bounded below by \(\ell_t>0\), giving polynomially encoded bounds on gradient magnitudes.
  • Logarithms of positive rationals can be evaluated to any requested precision in time polynomial in their encoding length and the precision length.

Choose

\[ \sigma=\frac{1}{8Q^2},\qquad \delta=\frac{\alpha\sigma^2}{4}. \]

All these numbers have polynomial encoding length. Polynomial-time convex optimization then produces a rational feasible allocation \(\widehat a\) satisfying

\[ |u_t(\widehat a)-u_t^\star|<\sigma \]

for every type.

Rational reconstruction

Two distinct reduced rationals with denominators at most \(Q\) differ by at least \(1/Q^2\). Thus each interval of radius \(\sigma\) around \(u_t(\widehat a)\) contains at most one such rational, and the true \(u_t^\star\) lies in it.

That rational can be recovered in polynomial time by continued fractions: the error is less than \(1/(2q^2)\) for its denominator \(q\leq Q\), so it is a convergent of the rational approximation. Enumerating convergents and checking the interval identifies it uniquely.

We have therefore computed the entire optimal utility vector exactly, in polynomial time.

4. Computing the specified minimum-norm prices

Using the recovered utilities, solve the rational linear feasibility problem consisting of the original allocation constraints and

\[ v_t^\top a_t=u_t^\star \]

for every type. Any resulting rational allocation \(a^\star\) is PF-optimal.

Define

\[ d_{t,j}=\frac{w_t v_{t,j}}{u_t^\star}. \]

For this fixed optimal allocation, the optimal resource-price vectors are exactly the nonnegative vectors satisfying

\[ a_{t,j}^\star=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda_j\geq d_{t,j}, \]

\[ 0<a_{t,j}^\star<x_{t,j}\ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda_j=d_{t,j}, \]

\[ a_{t,j}^\star=x_{t,j}\ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda_j\leq d_{t,j}. \]

Necessity follows from the saddle-point conditions: any optimal primal allocation and any optimal dual vector form a saddle point. Conversely, these conditions make each \(a_t^\star\) maximize its type’s concave Lagrangian objective over its demand box. All resource constraints are tight, so complementary slackness holds, making the price vector dual-optimal.

These conditions impose independent closed intervals or singletons on the price coordinates. Their minimum-norm point is their coordinatewise smallest nonnegative point. Explicitly,

\[ \lambda_j^\star= \max\left(\{0\}\cup \{d_{t,j}:a_{t,j}^\star<x_{t,j}\}\right). \]

If a coordinate strictly between its bounds exists, its equality fixes the price, and the zero-allocation lower bounds cannot exceed it. Otherwise, the displayed maximum is the lower endpoint of the feasible price interval. Feasibility guarantees that all upper bounds hold.

This computes the precise selector required in the question. Its coordinates are rational with polynomial bit length and, by the promise, positive.

5. Computing the truthful baseline

The truthful allocation satisfies \(q\leq x_0\), so its true utility is simply \(v_0^\top q\). We only need this scalar, which is the same for all truthful maximizers.

For a desired utility \(y\), define its minimum expenditure

\[ C(y)=\min\{\lambda^{\star\top}q: 0\leq q\leq x_0,\ v_0^\top q=y\}. \]

Group resources by their distinct expenditure-per-utility ratios

\[ d_h=\frac{\lambda_j^\star}{v_{0,j}}, \qquad d_1<\cdots<d_k. \]

Let \(Y_0=0\), and let \(Y_h\) be the total available utility in the first \(h\) groups.

Minimum expenditure fills cheaper groups first. To verify this, whenever an expensive group is used while a cheaper group has unused capacity, shifting a small equal amount of utility to the cheaper group strictly decreases expenditure. Thus, on \(Y_{h-1}\leq y\leq Y_h\), \(C(y)\) is affine with slope \(d_h\).

The truthful utility is the unique maximizer of

\[ w_0\log y-C(y). \]

This function is strictly concave. Inside group \(h\), its derivative is \(w_0/y-d_h\), and derivatives jump downwards at group boundaries.

If there is an index satisfying \(w_0/d_h\leq Y_h\), take the first such index. Then

\[ y_{\mathrm{true}}=\max\{Y_{h-1},w_0/d_h\}. \]

Earlier groups have positive derivatives through their right endpoints; in the selected group, the derivative reaches zero or is already nonpositive at its left endpoint. If no such index exists, the function is increasing throughout and \(y_{\mathrm{true}}=Y_k\).

Sorting and rational comparisons compute this value exactly in polynomial time. In particular,

\[ U\bigl(q_{\lambda^\star}(v_0,x_0)\bigr)=y_{\mathrm{true}}. \]

No allocation tie-breaking needs to be computed for this baseline.

6. Exact optimal report and gain

Solve the rational linear program

\[ H=\max\{v_0^\top z: 0\leq z\leq x_0,\ \lambda^{\star\top}z\leq w_0\}, \]

and obtain a rational optimizer \(z^\star\). This is also the usual fractional-knapsack problem.

Next, substitute \(v'=c\lambda^\star\) and \(x'=z^\star\) into the inequalities defining \(\mathcal R\). This leaves a one-variable rational linear feasibility problem in \(c\). Add the bounds

\[ \frac{\underline v}{\lambda_j^\star}\leq c \leq\frac{\overline v}{\lambda_j^\star} \]

for every coordinate. The promised aligned slice ensures feasibility. A rational feasible \(c>0\) can therefore be found in polynomial time.

The report

\[ (v',x')=(c\lambda^\star,z^\star) \]

belongs to \(\mathcal R\) and induces \(z^\star\) uniquely by Section 1. Its gain is exactly

\[ G=H-y_{\mathrm{true}}. \]

Every intermediate rational number has polynomial bit length, and every optimization or reconstruction step takes polynomial time. Thus the algorithm returns an exact gain and an exact optimal feasible report in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time, which satisfies both requested guarantees for every positive \(\epsilon\).
