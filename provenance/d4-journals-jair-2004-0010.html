The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rational finite-support society \{(N_t,\mu_t)\}_{t=1}^{\tau} of binary-valued, directed-path singly connected CP-net types, two outcomes o,o'\in\{0,1\}^n, and a rational quota \theta\in[0,1], decide whether \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\mathbf{1}[N_t\models o\succ o']\ge\theta.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

A binary CP-net is an acyclic dependency DAG whose conditional preference table assigns a strict preference between the two values of each variable for every assignment to its parents. An improving flip changes one variable to its locally preferred value, keeping all other variables fixed; write \(N\models o\succ o'\) iff an improving-flip sequence leads from \(o'\) to \(o\).
A dependency DAG is directed-path singly connected if every ordered pair of vertices has at most one directed path between them. A society is a rational finite-support distribution \(\{(N_t,\mu_t)\}_{t=1}^{\tau}\), where \(\mu_t\ge0\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).

Target

Is deciding whether \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\mathbf{1}[N_t\models o\succ o']\ge\theta\) for binary-valued, directed-path singly connected CP-net types in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For a single binary-valued, directed-path singly connected CP-net, dominance testing is NP-complete.
That result concerns one CP-net rather than a rational weighted society of CP-net types.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

The problem is NP-complete, using the stated single-type NP-completeness result as a premise. In particular, allowing rational weighted societies preserves NP-hardness.

NP-hardness. Consider an instance \((N,o,o')\) of single-net dominance testing, where \(N\) is binary-valued and its dependency DAG is directed-path singly connected. Construct the society consisting of the single type \(N_1=N\), with weight \(\mu_1=1\), and set \(\theta=1\). Then
\[ \sum_{t=1}^{1}\mu_t\mathbf{1}[N_t\models o\succ o'] =\mathbf{1}[N\models o\succ o']. \]
Consequently, the society satisfies the threshold precisely when \(N\models o\succ o'\).

The construction copies the original instance and adds only constant-size rational data. It is therefore a polynomial-time many-one reduction, and it preserves both structural restrictions on the CP-net. The assumed NP-hardness of single-net dominance thus proves NP-hardness of the target problem, even for singleton societies.

Membership in NP. Assume the society is explicitly listed and rational numbers use binary numerator–denominator encodings. By the stated single-net NP-completeness premise, dominance on the prescribed class admits a polynomial-time verifier with polynomial-size certificates.

A certificate for the society problem specifies a subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) and, for every \(t\in S\), a certificate that \(N_t\models o\succ o'\). The verifier checks these certificates and checks
\[ \sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\ge\theta. \]
There are at most as many selected types as explicitly listed types, so the total certificate length and verification time are polynomial in the input length. Exact rational summation and comparison also take polynomial time: a product of the input denominators has bit length at most the sum of their bit lengths.

For soundness, all certified types truly satisfy dominance, and nonnegativity of every weight gives
\[ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\mathbf{1}[N_t\models o\succ o'] \ge\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\ge\theta. \]
For completeness, whenever the target inequality holds, choose \(S\) to contain exactly the types satisfying dominance and provide their certificates.

Thus the problem is NP-complete under the usual explicit encoding, and a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
