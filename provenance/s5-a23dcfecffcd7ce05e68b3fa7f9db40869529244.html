The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given registries with rational pair-type masses \(\mu_t\), type-determined compatibility and match-quality weights, and cycle bounds \(b_k\) and \(B\) with \(B\geq\max_k b_k\), compute each registry’s standalone-optimal allocation and its resulting transplant mass \(r_k\). Let \(\mathcal C\) be the allowable discrete cycle patterns, including repeated types when sufficient mass is available; for each pattern let \(a_{tC}\) count its uses of type \(t\), \(a_{kC}\) count its participants from registry \(k\), and \(q_C\) be its total quality. Find cycle masses \(x_C\geq 0\) maximizing \(\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}q_Cx_C\), subject to \(\sum_C a_{tC}x_C\leq\mu_t\) for every type and \(\sum_C a_{kC}x_C\geq r_k\) for every registry.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) pair types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq2\) nonempty registries; each type belongs to one registry \(k(t)\).
The input specifies a directed compatibility relation on types and nonnegative binary-rational arc-quality weights \(w_{ts}\), applying uniformly between distinct pairs of those types. Only closed exchanges involving at least two distinct pairs are allowed; there are no chains or direct donations.
The integer bounds \(b_k\geq2\) and \(B\geq\max_k b_k\) are binary encoded. An allowable cycle pattern \(C=(t_1,\ldots,t_\ell)\) has compatible consecutive arcs, including the closing arc, \(2\leq\ell\leq B\), and at most \(b_k\) positions belonging to registry \(k\). Types may repeat, representing distinct pairs.
Let \(\mathcal C\) comprise all such patterns, specified implicitly by the input. Define \(a_{tC}\) as the multiplicity of type \(t\), \(a_{kC}=\sum_{t:k(t)=k}a_{tC}\), and \(q_C=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w_{t_i t_{i+1}}\), where \(t_{\ell+1}=t_1\). A cycle mass \(x_C\geq0\) consumes \(a_{tC}x_C\) mass of type \(t\); repeated-type availability is governed solely by these mass capacities.
For each registry \(k\), let \(\mathcal C_k\) contain its internal patterns. Compute a standalone allocation maximizing \(\sum_{C\in\mathcal C_k}q_Cy_C\) subject to \(y_C\geq0\) and \(\sum_{C\in\mathcal C_k}a_{tC}y_C\leq\mu_t\). Among quality-optimal allocations maximize transplant mass, and denote this mass by \(r_k=\sum_{C\in\mathcal C_k}a_{kC}y_C\).
Then maximize \(\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}q_Cx_C\) subject to \(x_C\geq0\), \(\sum_Ca_{tC}x_C\leq\mu_t\) for every type, and \(\sum_Ca_{kC}x_C\geq r_k\) for every registry. The threshold decision version asks whether this optimum is at least an input binary rational \(Q\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including bounds and \(Q\); state running times in \(m,\tau,L\) and the cycle bounds.

Is this exact continuous optimization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is its threshold decision version NP-hard when the cycle bounds are part of the input?

Known discrete results: standalone-optimal allocations can simultaneously meet all registries’ transplant-count benchmarks in the pooled system ([discrete feasibility result](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.06647v3)). Separately, perfect directed cycle cover with any fixed maximum cycle length at least three is NP-complete ([Abraham–Blum–Sandholm](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/kidneyExchange.EC07.withGrantInfo.pdf)).

1. Classification

The threshold decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when the number of registries is part of the input. This holds even under all the following restrictions:

  • Every registry bound is \(b_k=2\).
  • The total bound is \(B=\tau\).
  • Each registry contains at most three supported types.
  • All masses are equal positive dyadic rationals.
  • Exactly one compatible arc has quality weight one; every other arc has weight zero.
  • Every standalone benchmark is \(r_k=0\).

Consequently, exact optimization is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and has no algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

There is an important parameter distinction: for every fixed \(m\), the problem is polynomial-time solvable, even with binary-encoded cycle bounds. It is also polynomial-time solvable for every fixed total bound \(B\).

The proof below treats repeated types and the standalone quality-then-mass rule explicitly.

2. Reducing repeated-type patterns

Write \(\tau_k\) for the number of supported types belonging to registry \(k\). Since every registry is nonempty, \(\tau\geq m\geq2\).

Call a pattern *primitive* if it is either:

  • A directed cycle of length at least two whose types are all distinct; or
  • The length-two pattern \((t,t)\), where the compatibility relation contains the self-arc \(t\to t\).

The second pattern represents two distinct pairs of the same type, as permitted by the question.

Lemma. Every feasible allocation can be replaced by an allocation using only allowable primitive patterns, preserving every type’s consumed mass and total quality.

Proof. Regard a pattern as a directed closed walk on types. Its arc occurrences decompose into simple directed cycles, allowing one-arc self-loops as components. One obtains this decomposition by splitting a walk at a repeated vertex and continuing until each component has no repeated vertex except its closing endpoint.

For a component of length at least two, its length and every registry’s multiplicity are no larger than those of the original pattern. It therefore satisfies the original bounds.

If an original pattern has mass \(x\), assign mass \(x\) to each such component. A one-arc self-loop at type \(t\) would contribute consumption \(x\) of type \(t\) and quality \(w_{tt}x\). Replace it by the allowable pattern \((t,t)\) with mass \(x/2\). This has exactly the same consumption and quality, since its coefficients are \(a_{tC}=2\) and \(q_C=2w_{tt}\). It is allowable because \(b_{k(t)}\geq2\) and \(B\geq2\).

The decomposition partitions the original arc occurrences. Counting outgoing arc occurrences also counts positions of each type. Consequently, these replacements preserve all type consumptions and quality. Applying them to every allocated pattern proves the lemma. \(\square\)

Registry transplant masses are sums of type consumptions, so they are preserved too. The same argument applies within each standalone registry and preserves both objectives in its lexicographic optimization.

Define the effective bounds

\( D=\min\{B,\tau\}, \qquad d_k=\min\{b_k,\max\{\tau_k,2\}\}. \)

Every primitive pattern satisfies these effective bounds. Conversely, every pattern satisfying the effective bounds satisfies the original bounds. The lemma therefore shows that replacing \(B,b_k\) by \(D,d_k\) leaves all attainable consumption-quality vectors unchanged.

Thus large numerical bounds do not themselves require exponentially long patterns: patterns of length at most \(\tau\) suffice.

3. Exact algorithms and their running times

Standalone benchmarks are polynomial-time computable

I use the standard rational linear programming theorem: an explicitly represented rational LP can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its dimensions and total coefficient bit length. The formulations below have rational coefficients, are feasible, and have bounded objectives.

For registry \(k\), consider each possible starting type \(v\) separately. Construct an acyclic path network with states \((t,j)\), where \(t\) is an internal type and \(1\leq j\leq d_k\).

A source enters \((v,1)\), recording consumption of one occurrence of \(v\). A compatible transition from \((t,j)\) to \((s,j+1)\):

  • Records consumption of one occurrence of \(s\); and
  • Earns quality \(w_{ts}\).

If \(j\geq2\) and \(t\to v\) is compatible, an edge from \((t,j)\) to the sink earns the closing quality \(w_{tv}\), without recording another occurrence of \(v\).

Source-to-sink paths correspond exactly to internal patterns of lengths between two and \(d_k\), including repeated types. Nonnegative flows decompose into path flows because the network is acyclic. Conversely, each allocation induces such flows.

Combine these networks over starting types, impose the type mass capacities, and maximize their total quality. Then impose equality to this optimal quality and maximize their total recorded consumption. This computes precisely \(r_k\).

The formulation uses \(O(\tau_k^3d_k)\) variables. Across all registries,

\( \sum_k\tau_k^3d_k=O(\tau^4). \)

All edge weights are input rationals, and all resource coefficients are zero or one. Thus all benchmarks, including their exact rational values, are computable in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time. Their bit lengths are polynomial in these parameters by the usual rational LP determinant bounds.

The pooled problem is always feasible: take the union of standalone allocations attaining these benchmarks. Their type supports are disjoint, and every internal pattern is pooled-allowable because \(B\geq b_k\). This union achieves exactly \(r_k\) for every registry.

A count-state formulation for pooled optimization

Let

\( S=\prod_{k=1}^{m}(d_k+1). \)

For each starting type \(v\), construct a network whose states are \((t,c)\), where \(c\) is an integer vector satisfying

\( 0\leq c_k\leq d_k, \qquad 1\leq\sum_k c_k\leq D. \)

The vector records registry occurrence counts. Let \(e_k\) denote the \(k\)-th unit vector.

The source enters \((v,e_{k(v)})\), recording one occurrence of \(v\). A compatible transition from \((t,c)\) to \((s,c+e_{k(s)})\) records one occurrence of \(s\) and quality \(w_{ts}\). A closing edge to the sink exists when \(t\to v\) is compatible and \(\sum_kc_k\geq2\); it records quality \(w_{tv}\) and no further consumption.

Every transition increases the total count, so the network is acyclic. The same path-flow correspondence proves that the combined formulation represents exactly the patterns under the effective bounds. Impose type capacities and the registry lower bounds, then maximize quality.

There are \(O(\tau^3S)\) variables and \(O(\tau^2S+\tau+m)\) constraints. Therefore exact optimization takes

\( \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\,S^{O(1)} \)

bit operations. Since \(S\leq(\tau+1)^m\), this gives a bound of

\( \tau^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,L), \)

which is polynomial for each fixed \(m\).

Alternatively, enumerate all type sequences of lengths two through \(D\), discard those violating compatibility or registry bounds, and solve the explicit cycle LP. There are at most \(\sum_{\ell=2}^{D}\tau^\ell=O(\tau^D)\) sequences, giving running time

\( \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\,\tau^{O(D)}. \)

This is polynomial for every fixed \(B\).

4. A direct reduction from satisfiability

We now prove hardness for the exact continuous model.

Take an instance of \(3\text{-SAT}\) with variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\) and clauses \(K_1,\ldots,K_h\), where \(h\geq1\). Treat the three literal positions in each clause as separate occurrences. Let \(\mathcal O\) be the occurrence set, so \(R=|\mathcal O|=3h\).

Types and registries

For each occurrence \(o\), create three types \(p_o,f_o,c_o\), all belonging to a distinct occurrence registry \(\rho_o\):

  • \(p_o\) is a mandatory prefix type.
  • \(f_o\) will be visited exactly when occurrence \(o\)’s literal is false under the chosen assignment.
  • \(c_o\) represents selecting that occurrence to satisfy its clause.

Create connector types \(u_0,\ldots,u_n\) and \(v_1,\ldots,v_h\), each in its own singleton registry. For notation, set \(v_0=u_n\).

Set every registry bound to two.

Compatibility graph

First build a directed acyclic graph.

  • Prefix. Order the occurrences and connect all their prefix types in a directed path:

\( p_{o_1}\to p_{o_2}\to\cdots\to p_{o_R}\to u_0. \)

  • Assignment gadgets. For variable \(z_i\), create two paths from \(u_{i-1}\) to \(u_i\), one for each truth value. The path for a truth value has as its interior vertices exactly the types \(f_o\) whose occurrences involve \(z_i\) and whose literals that truth value makes false. Use any fixed order for these vertices. An empty interior gives a direct arc.

The two interiors are disjoint: each literal occurrence is false under exactly one of the two truth values. There are no edges allowing a switch between the two paths.

  • Clause gadgets. For each occurrence \(o\) in clause \(K_j\), add

\( v_{j-1}\to c_o\to v_j. \)
A path through this gadget selects one occurrence of the clause.

Finally, add the single feedback arc

\( v_h\to p_{o_1}. \)

There are no other arcs. Give the feedback arc weight one and every other arc weight zero.

Removing the feedback arc leaves a directed acyclic graph. Hence every simple directed cycle consists of one feedback arc together with a path through the entire prefix, all assignment gadgets, and all clause gadgets.

Registry bounds enforce satisfaction

Such a simple cycle chooses a truth value for every variable and an occurrence for every clause. Its multiplicity in occurrence registry \(\rho_o\) is exactly

\( 1+ \mathbf 1\{\text{occurrence }o\text{ is false under the chosen assignment}\} + \mathbf 1\{\text{occurrence }o\text{ is selected in its clause}\}. \)

The initial one comes from the mandatory prefix type \(p_o\). This multiplicity exceeds two exactly when a selected occurrence is false.

Connector registries are visited once. Therefore:

An allowable simple cycle exists if and only if the formula is satisfiable.

For the forward implication, each selected occurrence must be true, so every clause is satisfied. For the reverse implication, follow a satisfying assignment’s paths and select a true occurrence in each clause; all registry multiplicities are at most two.

There are no self-arcs. By the decomposition lemma, an allowable repeated-type pattern would contain an allowable simple-cycle component. Consequently, allowing repeated types does not change this equivalence.

Masses, bounds, and benchmarks

Before padding, the number of types is

\( N_0=3R+n+h+1. \)

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two with \(N\geq N_0\). Add \(N-N_0\) isolated types, each in its own singleton registry. Set

\( \tau=N, \qquad \mu_t=\frac1N\ \text{for every type}, \qquad B=N, \qquad Q=\frac1N. \)

These masses are positive dyadic rationals summing to one. Padding adds no cycles, and every simple cycle has length at most \(B\). Also, \(N<2N_0\), so the construction has polynomial size.

Every directed cycle visits all \(R\geq3\) occurrence registries through its prefix. Thus no registry has any internal closed exchange, and

\( r_k=0 \)

for every registry.

The pooled optimum encodes satisfiability exactly

If the formula is satisfiable, allocate mass \(1/N\) to the allowable simple cycle constructed above. Each type on that cycle is consumed once, so every capacity is respected. The cycle has quality one, yielding objective \(1/N\).

For every pattern \(C\), the only positive-quality arc is the unique outgoing arc of \(v_h\). Hence

\( q_C=a_{v_hC}. \)

Every allocation therefore satisfies

\( \sum_Cq_Cx_C = \sum_Ca_{v_hC}x_C \leq\mu_{v_h} = \frac1N. \)

Thus a satisfiable formula gives optimum exactly \(1/N\).

If the formula is unsatisfiable, there are no allowable patterns, so the optimum is zero. We have proved

\( \operatorname{OPT}= \begin{cases} 1/N,&\text{if the formula is satisfiable},\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \)

The threshold question with \(Q=1/N\) therefore decides \(3\text{-SAT}\). This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under all the restrictions stated in Section 1.

5. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

First compute all \(r_k\) deterministically in polynomial time, using Section 3.

By Section 2, the pooled optimum is attained using primitive patterns of length at most \(\tau\). The LP on these patterns is finite and nonempty. It is bounded because every pattern consumes at least two occurrences and

\( \sum_C |C|x_C = \sum_t\sum_Ca_{tC}x_C \leq1. \)

It therefore has an optimal vertex.

There are \(\tau+m\) type-capacity and registry-benchmark inequalities. At a vertex, at most \(\tau+m\) pattern variables can be positive. Otherwise, their columns would have a nonzero linear dependence preserving all resource rows; a sufficiently small perturbation in either direction would preserve nonnegativity and feasibility, contradicting extremality.

The positive variables satisfy an invertible subsystem of active resource constraints. Its integer coefficients have absolute value at most \(\tau\), and its right-hand sides are input masses or polynomial-bit benchmark values. Cramer’s rule and determinant bounds therefore give polynomial bit lengths for the positive masses.

A certificate consists of at most \(\tau+m\) primitive patterns and their rational masses. A verifier checks compatibility, all cycle bounds, capacities, registry benchmarks, and objective at least \(Q\), using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

Thus the threshold problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness proof.

6. What causes the hardness

The cited [Abraham–Blum–Sandholm result](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/kidneyExchange.EC07.withGrantInfo.pdf) concerns integral cycle covers. Its hardness does not transfer directly to this continuous model: here, every fixed total bound \(B\) permits a polynomial-size explicit LP.

The reduction above instead makes the existence of even one allowable pattern encode satisfiability. Fractional allocation cannot bypass that obstruction. Nevertheless, the count-state formulation solves the problem in polynomial time for every fixed number of registries.
