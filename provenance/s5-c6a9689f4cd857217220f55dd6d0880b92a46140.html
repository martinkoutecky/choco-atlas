The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supports \(T_D,T_H\) of complete doctor and hospital types, rational masses \(\mu_t,\nu_s\) summing to \(1\) on each side, doctor rankings over hospital types, a proto-metric on doctor types, and strict hospital-type rankings over doctor clusters, can an algorithm running in time polynomial in \(K_D,K_H,L,1/\varepsilon\) output a finite lottery over feasible couplings \(X\) such that the expected doctor-type prospects satisfy PIIF and, with probability at least \(1-\varepsilon\), no realized coupling admits a positive-mass blocking swap?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Each society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; support size counts listed types, not possible types. Let the nonempty doctor and hospital supports be \(T_D,T_H\), with sizes \(K_D,K_H\), positive masses satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=\sum_s\nu_s=1\), and total input bit length \(L\). Write \(\tau=K_D+K_H\) and \(m=K_H\).
Each doctor type \(t\) has a strict complete ranking \(\succ_t\) of hospital types. A proto-metric is a pseudometric \(d:T_D^2\to\{0,1\}\); its zero-distance classes are doctor clusters \(C(t)\). Each hospital type \(s\) strictly ranks all clusters by \(\succ_s\) and is indifferent between doctors within a cluster.
A feasible coupling is a nonnegative matrix \(X\) with \(\sum_sX_{ts}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_tX_{ts}=\nu_s\); masses are divisible and everyone is matched.
A finite lottery consists of feasible rational couplings \(X^a\) and positive rational probabilities \(\lambda_a\) summing to \(1\). Its doctor prospects are \(p_t(s)=\sum_a\lambda_aX^a_{ts}/\mu_t\).
These prospects satisfy preference-informed individual fairness (PIIF) when \(p_t\) stochastically dominates \(p_u\) under \(\succ_t\) for every ordered pair \(t,u\) with \(d(t,u)=0\); different clusters impose no constraints.
A realized coupling has a positive-mass blocking swap if some \(t,t',s,s'\) satisfy \(X_{ts'}>0\), \(X_{t's}>0\), \(s\succ_t s'\), and \(C(t)\succ_s C(t')\): for some \(0<\delta\le\min\{X_{ts'},X_{t's}\}\), transfer \(\delta\) from these two cells to \((t,s)\) and \((t',s')\). Neither displaced party must improve, and the modified coupling need not satisfy PIIF.
Given rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), the required lottery has PIIF expected prospects and \(\Pr[X\text{ has no positive-mass blocking swap}]\ge1-\varepsilon\).
Is finding such a lottery on every admissible input solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\) (equivalently \(K_D,K_H,L,1/\varepsilon\)), or is this computational problem NP-hard?
For finite equal-size populations under the same preference and metric restrictions, a polynomial-time algorithm in population size and \(1/\varepsilon\) produces exact PIIF prospects and a lottery with blocking probability at most \(\varepsilon\). [Karni–Rothblum–Yona](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/opus/volltexte/2022/15688/pdf/LIPIcs-ITCS-2022-92.pdf)
No result settling this precise support-encoded mass problem was identified in the literature search.

Partial result: one stable, exactly PIIF coupling always exists

The unrestricted complexity question remains unresolved here. I prove a stronger existence result than the one in the notes:

Every admissible input admits a single rational coupling that is both exactly PIIF and free of positive-mass blocking swaps. Such a coupling has polynomial encoding length.

Thus randomization is unnecessary for existence in the divisible-mass model. I also give a polynomial-time construction, with unrestricted support sizes, under the additional hypothesis that all hospitals rank the doctor clusters identically.

The existence proof uses a weighted version of the eating process underlying the hospital-first algorithm of [Karni–Rothblum–Yona](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.10885). All properties needed for the weighted process are proved below; no running-time guarantee for an expanded population is invoked.

1. Definitions and the claims proved

Let $\mathcal C$ be the partition of $T_D$ into zero-distance clusters, and write $c=|\mathcal C|$. For $C\in\mathcal C$, define
$$ M_C=\sum_{t\in C}\mu_t. $$
For a doctor type $t$, let $B_t(k)$ be the set of its $k$ most preferred hospital types.

It is useful to consider a partial coupling $A$, meaning a nonnegative matrix satisfying
$$ \sum_s A_{ts}\leq\mu_t, \qquad \sum_t A_{ts}\leq\nu_s. $$
For partial couplings, use the following prefix inequalities:
$$ \frac{\sum_{s\in B_t(k)}A_{ts}}{\mu_t} \geq \frac{\sum_{s\in B_t(k)}A_{us}}{\mu_u} \quad \text{whenever }C(t)=C(u). $$
For a feasible coupling, these are exactly the task’s PIIF conditions.

I prove:

  • There exists a feasible coupling satisfying these inequalities and having no positive-mass blocking swap.
  • There exists such a coupling with rational entries of polynomial total encoding length.
  • Enumerating $c^m$ cutoff profiles and solving rational linear programs finds such a coupling in time $\operatorname{poly}(c^m,\tau,L)$.
  • If all hospitals have the same cluster ranking, such a coupling can be found in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$, independently of $\varepsilon$.

The first two claims strengthen the existence and certificate results in the notes. They do not, by themselves, settle the unrestricted running-time question.

2. A weighted eating procedure

Fix a cluster $C$ and a vector of nonnegative hospital supplies $a=(a_s)_{s\in T_H}$.

Run the following continuous-time procedure on the time interval $[0,1]$. Every doctor type $t\in C$ consumes its most preferred hospital type whose supply remains positive, at rate $\mu_t$. An exhausted hospital is removed. If all supplies are exhausted, consumption stops.

Let $E_C(a)_{ts}$ be the amount consumed by type $t$ from hospital $s$, and put
$$ b_s=\sum_{t\in C}E_C(a)_{ts}. $$
Then
$$ \sum_s E_C(a)_{ts}\leq\mu_t, \qquad 0\leq b_s\leq a_s. $$
For rational supplies, the procedure has rational output: between exhaustion events its rates are fixed rational numbers, and the next event time is obtained by rational division. There are at most $m$ exhaustion events.

For each hospital $s$, define its exhaustion time $e_s$ as follows:

  • $e_s=0$ if $a_s=0$;
  • $e_s$ is its actual exhaustion time if it exhausts by time $1$;
  • $e_s=1$ if it remains available through time $1$.

Thus, except at event instants, $s$ is available precisely at times $x<e_s$.

Lemma 1: the eating output satisfies the prefix inequalities

For every ordered pair $t,u\in C$ and every $k$,
$$ \frac{\sum_{s\in B_t(k)}E_C(a)_{ts}}{\mu_t} \geq \frac{\sum_{s\in B_t(k)}E_C(a)_{us}}{\mu_u}. $$

Proof. Set
$$ e=\max_{s\in B_t(k)}e_s. $$
At every time $x<e$, at least one hospital in $B_t(k)$ is available. Since $t$ prefers every hospital in this prefix to every hospital outside it, $t$ consumes from the prefix throughout $[0,e)$. After time $e$, none of its hospitals remains available. Hence
$$ \sum_{s\in B_t(k)}E_C(a)_{ts}=\mu_t e. $$
Type $u$ can consume from this same set only before time $e$, and its consumption rate is $\mu_u$. Therefore
$$ \sum_{s\in B_t(k)}E_C(a)_{us}\leq\mu_u e. $$
Dividing by the positive masses proves the inequality. $\square$

Lemma 2: removing uneaten supply leaves the output unchanged

With $b_s=\sum_{t\in C}E_C(a)_{ts}$,
$$ E_C(b)=E_C(a). $$

Proof. Hospitals exhausted in the original process have $b_s=a_s$, so their initial supplies are unchanged.

Suppose instead that $s$ has uneaten supply at time $1$. Once a doctor type starts consuming $s$, it continues consuming $s$ until time $1$: $s$ never exhausts, and no new hospital becomes available. Consequently, if $b_s>0$, the original cumulative consumption of $s$ is strictly less than $b_s$ at every time before $1$. Replacing $a_s$ by $b_s$ therefore leaves $s$ available throughout the original trajectory before time $1$.

If $b_s=0$, nobody chooses $s$ in the original trajectory, so deleting it cannot alter any original choice.

The original trajectory thus remains valid after replacing $a$ by $b$. The procedure’s choices and event evolution are determined by the supplies and strict preferences, so it produces the same amounts. $\square$

Lemma 3: increasing supplies improves every doctor’s prefixes

If $a'_s\geq a_s$ for every $s$, then
$$ \sum_{s\in B_t(k)}E_C(a')_{ts} \geq \sum_{s\in B_t(k)}E_C(a)_{ts} $$
for every $t\in C$ and every $k$.

Proof. Write $e_s$ and $e'_s$ for the two sets of exhaustion times. I first prove
$$ e'_s\geq e_s \quad\text{for every }s. $$

Suppose otherwise. Choose a hospital $s$ with $e'_s<e_s$ for which $e'_s$ is smallest among all such violations, and put $e=e'_s$.

Consider any doctor type consuming $s$ in the new process at a time $x<e$, away from event instants. Every hospital $q\succ_t s$ has already exhausted in the new process, so
$$ e'_q\leq x<e. $$
By the minimal choice of $e$, such a hospital cannot have $e'_q<e_q$. Therefore
$$ e_q\leq e'_q\leq x. $$
Meanwhile $s$ is still available in the old process, since $x<e<e_s$. Hence this doctor type also consumes $s$ in the old process at time $x$.

The new consumption rate of $s$ is consequently at most its old consumption rate throughout $[0,e)$, apart from finitely many event instants. Since $s$ exhausts at time $e$ in the new process,
$$ a'_s = \text{new consumption of }s\text{ by time }e \leq \text{old consumption of }s\text{ by time }e < a_s. $$
The strict inequality follows because $s$ has not exhausted in the old process by time $e$. This contradicts $a'_s\geq a_s$.

Finally, the calculation in Lemma 1 gives
$$ \sum_{s\in B_t(k)}E_C(a)_{ts} = \mu_t\max_{s\in B_t(k)}e_s. $$
The coordinatewise exhaustion-time inequalities give the desired prefix inequalities. $\square$

Combining Lemmas 2 and 3 yields the form needed below:

If $b$ is the vector consumed in a previous eating process, and a new supply vector satisfies $a'\geq b$, then every doctor’s new prefix amounts are at least its previous prefix amounts.

3. Hospital proposals and permanent rejection certificates

For every hospital $s$, maintain a list of clusters not yet rejected, initially its complete cluster ranking.

At the end of round $r$, maintain:

  • retained inventories $b^r_{Cs}$;
  • free hospital masses $f^r_s$;
  • a partial coupling $A^r$.

Initialize
$$ b^0_{Cs}=0,\qquad f^0_s=\nu_s,\qquad A^0=0. $$

A round proceeds as follows.

  • Every hospital $s$ with $f^{r-1}_s>0$ offers all this mass to its highest-ranked cluster still on its list.
  • For each cluster $C$, combine its retained inventories with its new offers, obtaining supplies $a^r_{Cs}$.
  • Run the weighted eating procedure in $C$ on these supplies. Its output forms the rows of $A^r$ belonging to $C$. Set

$$ b^r_{Cs}=\sum_{t\in C}A^r_{ts}. $$

  • Every uneaten amount

$$ a^r_{Cs}-b^r_{Cs}>0 $$
is returned to hospital $s$, and $C$ is deleted from $s$’s list. Thus
$$ f^r_s=\sum_C(a^r_{Cs}-b^r_{Cs}). $$

Previously retained inventory can be rejected in a later round even if its cluster was already deleted. Deletion prevents new offers to that cluster; it does not prevent reconsideration of retained inventory.

Lemma 4: conservation and monotonicity

At every completed round,
$$ \sum_C b^r_{Cs}+f^r_s=\nu_s, $$
and $A^r$ is a partial coupling satisfying the prefix inequalities. Moreover,
$$ \sum_{s\in B_t(k)}A^r_{ts} \geq \sum_{s\in B_t(k)}A^{r-1}_{ts} $$
for every doctor type $t$ and every $k$.

Proof. Offering free mass preserves each hospital’s total inventory. Eating divides its proposed inventory into consumed and returned amounts, proving conservation. Doctor consumption is bounded by its rate times the unit time horizon, proving the row bounds. Lemma 1 proves the prefix inequalities.

For monotonicity, cluster $C$’s new supplies are coordinatewise at least its previously consumed inventories $b^{r-1}_{Cs}$. Lemmas 2 and 3 therefore apply. $\square$

Lemma 5: rejection permanently rules out worse assignments

If cluster $C$ rejects a positive amount of hospital $s$ in round $r$, then, for every $t\in C$ and every later round $j\geq r$,
$$ \sum_{q:\ q\succeq_t s}A^j_{tq}=\mu_t. $$
In particular,
$$ A^j_{tq}=0 \quad\text{whenever }s\succ_t q. $$

Proof. Positive uneaten supply means $s$ remains available throughout round $r$’s eating interval. Every $t\in C$ therefore consumes for the entire interval and consumes only hospitals it ranks at least as highly as $s$. The displayed prefix sum is consequently $\mu_t$ in round $r$.

Lemma 4 says this prefix sum cannot decrease. The row bound says it cannot exceed $\mu_t$. It therefore remains equal to $\mu_t$. $\square$

This also proves that the proposal process is well-defined. If a hospital had positive free mass and an empty remaining list, every cluster would previously have rejected it. Lemma 5 would then imply that every doctor row is full. Conservation would imply that all free mass is zero, a contradiction.

Lemma 6: every partial coupling produced has no blocking swap

No $A^r$ contains two positive cells satisfying the task’s blocking conditions.

Proof. Suppose $A^r_{t's}>0$, and put $C'=C(t')$. Positive retained inventory in $C'$ must originate from an offer of $s$ to $C'$ in some round at or before $r$.

Before making its first offer to $C'$, hospital $s$ must have had every cluster it ranks above $C'$ deleted from its list. Thus, if
$$ C(t)\succ_s C', $$
cluster $C(t)$ previously rejected $s$. Lemma 5 gives
$$ A^r_{tq}=0 \quad\text{for every }q\text{ with }s\succ_t q. $$
The two positive cells required for a blocking swap therefore cannot coexist. $\square$

Notice that this argument uses exactly the task’s blocking definition. No improvement by either displaced party is required.

4. Convergence proves existence of a single stable PIIF coupling

Define the total free mass after round $r$ by
$$ F_r=\sum_s f^r_s. $$

Lemma 7: the total offered mass over all rounds is bounded

The proposal process satisfies
$$ \sum_{r\geq1}F_{r-1}\leq c. $$
Here the sum is finite if the process terminates.

Proof. Fix a hospital $s$ and a cluster $C$.

Before $C$ first rejects $s$, all mass offered by $s$ to $C$ remains retained there. Hence the cumulative fresh offers to $C$ equal its retained inventory, including any offer in the current round. This inventory is at most $\nu_s$ by conservation.

Once $C$ rejects $s$, it receives no further fresh offers from $s$. If it never rejects $s$, every finite cumulative total is still at most $\nu_s$. In either case, the total mass freshly offered along the pair $(s,C)$ is at most $\nu_s$.

Summing over all pairs gives
$$ \text{total offered mass} \leq \sum_s\sum_C\nu_s = c. $$
Round $r$ offers exactly $F_{r-1}$, proving the claim. $\square$

Theorem 1: one stable, exactly PIIF coupling exists

There exists a feasible real coupling $X^*$ satisfying PIIF and having no positive-mass blocking swap.

Proof. If some $F_r=0$, conservation and the row bounds show that $A^r$ is feasible. Lemmas 4 and 6 prove the conclusion.

Otherwise, the process runs indefinitely. Lemma 7 implies
$$ F_r\longrightarrow0. $$

For each doctor type $t$ and each prefix length $k$, Lemma 4 makes
$$ \sum_{s\in B_t(k)}A^r_{ts} $$
a nondecreasing sequence bounded above by $\mu_t$. Every such sequence converges. Each entry is the difference of two successive prefix sums in its doctor’s ranking, so every $A^r_{ts}$ converges. Let $X^*$ be the entrywise limit.

Conservation gives
$$ \sum_t A^r_{ts}=\nu_s-f^r_s. $$
Since $0\leq f^r_s\leq F_r$, the limiting column sums are $\nu_s$.

The total doctor-row deficit is
$$ \sum_t\left(\mu_t-\sum_s A^r_{ts}\right) = 1-\sum_{t,s}A^r_{ts} = F_r. $$
Every deficit is nonnegative and bounded by $F_r$, so every limiting row sum is $\mu_t$. Thus $X^*$ is feasible.

The prefix inequalities pass to the limit, proving PIIF.

Finally, suppose $X^*$ had a blocking swap witnessed by positive entries $X^*_{tq}$ and $X^*_{t's}$. Entrywise convergence would make both corresponding entries of $A^r$ positive for every sufficiently large $r$. The preference conditions are fixed, so this would contradict Lemma 6. Hence $X^*$ is stable. $\square$

This proof starts directly from the mass-encoded input. It does not expand masses into individual agents.

5. Rationality and a polynomial encoding bound

The limiting argument produces a real coupling. The next theorem supplies the rationality required by the task.

Theorem 2: a polynomial-size rational single-realization solution exists

There exists a rational feasible coupling $\widehat X$ that is exactly PIIF and stable, and whose total encoding length is polynomial in $\tau,L$.

Proof. Let
$$ I=\{(t,s):X^*_{ts}>0\}. $$
Consider the polytope $R$ of matrices $Y$ satisfying:

  • nonnegativity and the prescribed row and column sums;
  • $Y_{ts}=0$ for $(t,s)\notin I$;
  • all PIIF inequalities, written without division as

$$ \mu_u\sum_{s\in B_t(k)}Y_{ts} - \mu_t\sum_{s\in B_t(k)}Y_{us} \geq0. $$

Theorem 1 makes $R$ nonempty. Its marginal equations and nonnegativity make it bounded.

Every member of $R$ is stable. Indeed, a blocking swap in $Y$ would use two positive cells belonging to $I$. Those same cells are positive in $X^*$, and the preference conditions would give a blocking swap in $X^*$.

It remains to bound the rational encoding of a vertex of $R$.

Let $Q$ be the least common multiple of the input mass denominators. Then
$$ \log_2 Q\leq L. $$
Let
$$ n=K_Dm $$
be the number of matrix coordinates.

After multiplying marginal equations and PIIF inequalities by $Q$, the defining system has integer coefficients and right-hand sides of absolute value at most $Q$. Nonnegativity and prescribed zero coordinates also have integer coefficients bounded by $Q$.

Choose a vertex $\widehat X$ of $R$. There are $n$ linearly independent active defining equations at that vertex. Otherwise, a nonzero vector orthogonal to all active normals would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions while preserving all inactive inequalities, contradicting extremality.

These equations form an invertible integer system. Cramer’s rule expresses each coordinate as a ratio of integer determinants. The elementary determinant bound gives
$$ |\det A|\leq n!Q^n, $$
and the same bound applies when one column is replaced by the right-hand side. Thus each coordinate has encoding length
$$ O\!\left(n(L+\log n)\right). $$
There are $n$ coordinates, so the total length is polynomial in $\tau,L$.

The matrix $\widehat X$ is rational, feasible, PIIF, and stable. $\square$

Taking a lottery with the single realization $\widehat X$ and probability $1$ satisfies the task for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$.

This is an existence and certificate theorem. The support $I$ used in its proof has not been found in polynomial time.

6. An exact algorithm by cutoff enumeration

For completeness, the strengthened existence theorem gives an exact algorithm using ordinary linear programs, without mixture variables.

For a profile
$$ w=(w_s)_{s\in T_H}\in\mathcal C^m, $$
let $P_w$ be the transportation polytope with these additional zero constraints:
$$ X_{ts}=0 \quad\text{if }w_s\succ_s C(t), $$
and
$$ X_{tq}=0 \quad\text{if there exists }s \text{ such that }C(t)\succ_s w_s \text{ and }s\succ_t q. $$

Lemma 8: stable couplings are exactly the union of these faces

If $S$ denotes the set of stable feasible couplings, then
$$ S=\bigcup_{w\in\mathcal C^m}P_w. $$

Proof. First suppose $X\in P_w$ and there is a purported blocking swap using positive cells $X_{tq}$ and $X_{t's}$. The first zero constraints imply that $C(t')$ is ranked at least as highly as $w_s$. Therefore
$$ C(t)\succ_s C(t') \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(t)\succ_s w_s. $$
Together with $s\succ_t q$, the second zero constraints force $X_{tq}=0$, a contradiction.

Conversely, let $X$ be stable. Since $\nu_s>0$, every column contains positive mass. Choose $w_s$ as its least preferred positively occupied cluster. The first zero constraints hold by construction.

If a second zero constraint were violated, there would be positive $X_{tq}$ with
$$ C(t)\succ_s w_s,\qquad s\succ_t q. $$
Some $t'$ in $w_s$ has $X_{t's}>0$. These two cells would form a blocking swap, contradicting stability. $\square$

Now enumerate all $c^m$ profiles and, for each one, test feasibility of the rational linear system
$$ X\in P_w, \qquad X\text{ satisfies PIIF}. $$
Theorem 1 and Lemma 8 guarantee that at least one system is feasible. Rational linear programming returns a rational solution, which can be output with probability $1$.

Each system has size polynomial in $\tau,L$, so the running time is
$$ \operatorname{poly}(c^m,\tau,L). $$
This is polynomial for fixed $m$, but does not establish the requested bound when $m$ varies.

7. A polynomial-time case with unrestricted support sizes

Assume additionally that all hospitals have the same strict cluster ranking:
$$ C_1\succ_s C_2\succ_s\cdots\succ_s C_c \quad\text{for every }s. $$
Doctor rankings may remain arbitrary, including within a cluster.

Theorem 3: common hospital priorities permit a polynomial-time solution

Under this additional hypothesis, a single rational stable PIIF coupling can be found in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$.

Construction. Begin with remaining supplies $\nu_s$. Process clusters in the order $C_1,\ldots,C_c$. In cluster $C_i$, run the weighted eating procedure for time $1$ on all remaining supplies. Record its allocation and subtract its consumption before proceeding to $C_{i+1}$.

Feasibility. Before processing $C_i$, total remaining supply is
$$ 1-\sum_{j<i}M_{C_j} = \sum_{j\geq i}M_{C_j} \geq M_{C_i}. $$
It cannot run out before time $1$: by time $x<1$, the cluster can have consumed at most $M_{C_i}x<M_{C_i}$. Consequently, every doctor in $C_i$ consumes its full mass.

All doctor rows are therefore full. The total consumed mass is $1$, so all hospital columns are full as well.

PIIF. Every cluster’s rows are the output of one weighted eating procedure. Lemma 1 proves all required inequalities.

Stability. Suppose a purported blocking swap uses positive cells $X_{tq}$ and $X_{t's}$, where $t\in C_i$ and $t'\in C_j$. Hospital $s$’s strict preference implies $i<j$.

Because $s$ supplies positive mass to the later cluster $C_j$, some of its supply remains after stage $i$. It is therefore available throughout stage $i$. Type $t$ consumes only hospitals it ranks at least as highly as $s$, making
$$ X_{tq}>0,\qquad s\succ_t q $$
impossible.

Running time and rational encoding. Each stage has at most $m$ exhaustion events and one terminal event. Choices, rates, and the next event time can be computed by scanning the explicitly listed preferences. There are polynomially many arithmetic operations.

For bit complexity, use the common denominator $Q$ from Theorem 2. Every positive consumption rate is an integer divided by $Q$, with numerator at most $Q$. At an event, division by the selected rate and the subsequent inventory updates enlarge a common denominator for the state by a factor at most $Q^2$. There are at most $c(m+1)$ events. Thus all intermediate rational numbers have polynomial bit length. The construction consequently runs in polynomial bit time. $\square$

This case allows both $m$ and $c$ to vary and allows exponentially small positive input masses.

8. What remains open

The unrestricted problem is not settled.

The weighted proposal process proves existence because its total offered mass is bounded and its partial allocations converge. That argument does not supply a finite polynomial-time method for finding the limiting support or an exact feasible coupling.

In particular, a small remaining mass deficit is not the task’s bound on the probability of an unstable realization. The partial matrices still have deficient marginals, and completing them arbitrarily is not justified by the stability proof.

The remaining target is therefore a polynomial-time construction for arbitrary hospital cluster rankings, or an appropriate hardness reduction for the stated search problem. Neither is proved here.

1. Definitions and scope

The partial-coupling inequalities correctly extend the task’s PIIF constraints. For a feasible coupling, dividing each row by its positive mass produces a probability distribution, and the stated prefix inequalities are precisely stochastic dominance under the observing doctor’s ranking.

Restricting these inequalities to ordered pairs within the same zero-distance cluster matches the task. The subsequent stability arguments also use the specified blocking-swap definition without imposing improvement requirements on either displaced party.

2. Weighted eating procedure

The procedure is well-defined with strict complete preferences and finitely many hospital types. Between exhaustion events, consumption rates are fixed. Each exhaustion removes at least one hospital, so there are at most $m$ exhaustion events.

The row and supply bounds follow directly from the consumption rates and the time horizon. Rational inputs give rational event times and allocations. This initial rationality observation does not claim a polynomial encoding bound for an indefinitely repeated proposal process.

3. Lemma 1: prefix inequalities

Correct.

For a nonempty preference prefix, its latest exhaustion time is exactly the length of time during which some hospital in that prefix remains available. Doctor type $t$ consumes from the prefix throughout that interval, because all hospitals outside it are less preferred.

Another type $u$ can consume from the same set only during that interval, at rate at most $\mu_u$. The resulting normalized inequality is valid, including when supplies run out before time $1$ or remain available until the horizon.

4. Lemma 2: removing uneaten supply

Correct.

For an exhausted hospital, the initial supply is unchanged. For a hospital with uneaten supply, any type that begins consuming it continues until time $1$, because that hospital remains available and no new alternatives appear.

Consequently, a positively consumed, unexhausted hospital reaches its total consumed amount only at the horizon. Reducing its initial supply to that amount does not change its availability before the horizon. Deleting a hospital never selected in the original trajectory does not change any original choice.

These observations justify preservation of the trajectory and output.

5. Lemma 3: supply monotonicity

Correct.

The earliest-violation argument is valid. If a doctor consumes $s$ before its allegedly earlier exhaustion in the enlarged-supply process, every hospital preferred to $s$ has already exhausted there. Minimality of the violating exhaustion time ensures that those hospitals have also exhausted in the original process.

Thus every such doctor also consumes $s$ in the original process. Integrating the consumption rates gives the stated contradiction: the enlarged initial supply cannot exhaust while the original process has consumed strictly less than its smaller initial supply.

The convention assigning exhaustion time $1$ to a hospital still available at the horizon causes no problem: a violating time is strictly earlier than that horizon. A zero-time violation likewise contradicts coordinatewise supply dominance.

The prefix conclusion then follows from the formula already established in Lemma 1.

6. Proposal process and Lemma 4

Correct.
