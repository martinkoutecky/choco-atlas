Polynomial-time solvability under the stated transfer condition

The decision problem is in $\mathrm P$ as stated. Its exact criterion is
$$ q\le S_{\mathrm{eff}} := \int_0^1\int_0^v(v-c)f(v)g(c)\,dc\,dv. $$

The essential point is the transfer domain: finite interim expectations for every report do not require joint absolute integrability. Under the stated condition, efficient trade admits a common transfer satisfying all the incentive and participation constraints. I prove this below for the full nonuniform density domain.

This argument does not invoke the generic posted-price result in [Crawford (2021)](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~vcrawfor/LkBilateralTrading27January21.pdf). Adding joint absolute integrability would change the problem and invalidate the construction used here.

1. The two relevant beliefs

Define the belief densities
$$ f_0(v)=g_0(c)=1,\qquad f_1(v)=f(v),\qquad g_1(c)=g(c). $$

Level $1$ faces uniform reports. If truth is selected as a best response at that level, level $2$ faces the opposite side’s true value density. Inductively, every higher level faces that same truthful-report density. Independence of values and levels ensures that these densities are $f$ and $g$, regardless of level proportions.

Consequently, to construct a feasible mechanism, it suffices to make truth a best response and truthful utility nonnegative for buyers under both $g_0,g_1$ and for sellers under both $f_0,f_1$. The construction below makes truth the unique best response under each belief.

The main issue is realizing the required four interim transfer schedules with one common transfer.

2. A transfer construction without aggregate compatibility requirements

I first prove a general lemma.

Lemma. Let $f,g$ be the densities in the task. Given any bounded measurable functions
$$ r_0,r_1,z_0,z_1:[0,1]\longrightarrow\mathbb R, $$
there exists a measurable, finite-valued function $x$ such that, for every $v,c\in[0,1]$,
$$ \int_0^1x(v,c)g_j(c)\,dc=r_j(v), \qquad j\in\{0,1\}, $$
and
$$ \int_0^1x(v,c)f_i(v)\,dv=z_i(c), \qquad i\in\{0,1\}. $$
Furthermore, every horizontal and vertical section of $x$ is absolutely integrable.

The lemma imposes no equality between the aggregate buyer and seller transfer integrals. That is possible because $x$ need not be jointly absolutely integrable.

2.1. Disjoint functions with identical density moments

Let $d$ be either $f$ or $g$. Since $d$ integrates to $1$ and is not identically $1$, it is a nonconstant polynomial. Its derivative is therefore not identically zero. Choose an interior point $a$ and $\varepsilon>0$ such that $d$ is strictly monotone on
$$ [a-\varepsilon,a+\varepsilon]\subset(0,1). $$

For every integer $n\ge1$, define the open intervals
$$ I_n^-=(a-\varepsilon2^{-n},a-\varepsilon2^{-(n+1)}), $$
$$ I_n^+=(a+\varepsilon2^{-(n+1)},a+\varepsilon2^{-n}). $$
These intervals are pairwise disjoint, including across signs and indices.

Let $\phi_n^\pm$ be the normalized indicator of $I_n^\pm$, and let
$$ \mu_n^\pm=\int_0^1d(u)\phi_n^\pm(u)\,du. $$
Strict monotonicity implies that $d(a)$ lies strictly between $\mu_n^-$ and $\mu_n^+$. Hence there exists $\theta_n\in(0,1)$ such that
$$ \theta_n\mu_n^-+(1-\theta_n)\mu_n^+=d(a). $$

Set
$$ A_n=\theta_n\phi_n^-+(1-\theta_n)\phi_n^+. $$
Each $A_n$ is nonnegative, bounded, and measurable, and
$$ \int_0^1A_n(u)\,du=1, \qquad \int_0^1d(u)A_n(u)\,du=d(a). $$
The supports are pairwise disjoint. In particular, every point belongs to the support of at most one $A_n$.

2.2. A function with zero buyer moments and nonzero seller moments

Apply the preceding construction to $f$ at a center $a$, obtaining functions $A_n$, and to $g$ at a center $b$, obtaining functions $B_n$. Thus
$$ \int_0^1f_i(v)A_n(v)\,dv=f_i(a), \qquad \int_0^1g_j(c)B_n(c)\,dc=g_j(b). $$

Define
$$ h_{a,b}(v,c) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n(v)\bigl(B_n(c)-B_{n+1}(c)\bigr). $$

This function is measurable and finite-valued. For a fixed $v$, at most one summand is nonzero. For a fixed $c$, at most two summands are nonzero. Therefore every horizontal and vertical section is absolutely integrable. These statements include interval boundaries and accumulation points, where the relevant indicator functions vanish.

For every $v$ and each $j$,
$$ \int_0^1h_{a,b}(v,c)g_j(c)\,dc=0, $$
because $B_n$ and $B_{n+1}$ have identical $g_j$ moments.

For a fixed $c$ in the support of $B_1$, the vertical section is $A_1(v)B_1(c)$. For $c$ in the support of $B_n$, with $n\ge2$, it is
$$ \bigl(A_n(v)-A_{n-1}(v)\bigr)B_n(c). $$
The identical $f_i$ moments of the $A_n$ therefore give, for every $c$,
$$ \int_0^1h_{a,b}(v,c)f_i(v)\,dv=f_i(a)B_1(c). $$

In particular,
$$ \int_0^1g_j(c) \left[\int_0^1h_{a,b}(v,c)f_i(v)\,dv\right]dc =f_i(a)g_j(b). $$

There is no application of Fubini’s theorem here: each section is integrated separately. Indeed, under uniform weights, the two iterated integrals are $0$ and $1$. Thus $h_{a,b}$ is not jointly absolutely integrable.

2.3. Correcting an arbitrary matrix of aggregate discrepancies

Choose centers $a_0,a_1$ for $f$ such that
$$ f(a_0)\ne f(a_1), $$
and centers $b_0,b_1$ for $g$ such that
$$ g(b_0)\ne g(b_1). $$
Such choices exist within intervals where the respective derivatives have a fixed nonzero sign.

Define
$$ U= \begin{pmatrix} 1&1\\ f(a_0)&f(a_1) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad V= \begin{pmatrix} 1&1\\ g(b_0)&g(b_1) \end{pmatrix}. $$
Both matrices are invertible.

For the prescribed schedules, define
$$ M^R_{ij}=\int_0^1f_i(v)r_j(v)\,dv, \qquad M^Z_{ij}=\int_0^1g_j(c)z_i(c)\,dc, $$
and let
$$ \Delta=M^Z-M^R. $$
These are finite because the schedules and densities are bounded.

Set
$$ \Lambda=U^{-1}\Delta(V^{\mathsf T})^{-1}, \qquad h=\sum_{s,t=0}^1\Lambda_{st}h_{a_s,b_t}. $$
The function $h$ has absolutely integrable sections, and its buyer moments vanish for every report.

Let
$$ H_i(c)=\int_0^1h(v,c)f_i(v)\,dv. $$
These functions are bounded: each is a finite linear combination of the bounded functions $B_1$ appearing in the construction. Moreover,
$$ \int_0^1g_j(c)H_i(c)\,dc = (U\Lambda V^{\mathsf T})_{ij} =\Delta_{ij}. $$

Consequently, the adjusted seller schedules
$$ \widetilde z_i=z_i-H_i $$
satisfy the compatibility identities
$$ \int_0^1g_j(c)\widetilde z_i(c)\,dc = \int_0^1f_i(v)r_j(v)\,dv =M^R_{ij}. $$

2.4. Realizing the compatible schedules by a bounded transfer

The functions $f_0,f_1$ are linearly independent. Otherwise $f$ would be constant and, by normalization, identically $1$. Their Gram matrix
$$ \Gamma^f_{\ell i}=\int_0^1f_\ell(v)f_i(v)\,dv $$
is positive definite: a vanishing quadratic form would make a nontrivial linear combination vanish almost everywhere, and continuity would then make it vanish everywhere.

Inverting this matrix gives bounded continuous functions $\alpha_0,\alpha_1$, each a linear combination of $f_0,f_1$, satisfying
$$ \int_0^1f_\ell(v)\alpha_i(v)\,dv = \begin{cases} 1,&\ell=i,\\ 0,&\ell\ne i. \end{cases} $$
The same argument gives bounded functions $\beta_0,\beta_1$ with
$$ \int_0^1g_\ell(c)\beta_j(c)\,dc = \begin{cases} 1,&\ell=j,\\ 0,&\ell\ne j. \end{cases} $$

Write $M_{ij}=M^R_{ij}$ and define
$$ J_i(c)=\sum_{j=0}^1M_{ij}\beta_j(c), $$
$$ y(v,c) = \sum_{j=0}^1r_j(v)\beta_j(c) + \sum_{i=0}^1\alpha_i(v) \bigl(\widetilde z_i(c)-J_i(c)\bigr). $$
This function is bounded and measurable.

The compatibility identities imply
$$ \int_0^1g_j(c) \bigl(\widetilde z_i(c)-J_i(c)\bigr)\,dc=0. $$
Thus, for every $v$,
$$ \int_0^1y(v,c)g_j(c)\,dc=r_j(v). $$
The dual identities for the $\alpha_i$ similarly give, for every $c$,
$$ \int_0^1y(v,c)f_i(v)\,dv = J_i(c)+\widetilde z_i(c)-J_i(c) =\widetilde z_i(c). $$

Finally, set
$$ x=y+h. $$
Its buyer moments are $r_j$ and its seller moments are $\widetilde z_i+H_i=z_i$. Every section is absolutely integrable because $y$ is bounded and $h$ has absolutely integrable sections. This proves the lemma.

3. Efficient trade is feasible

Take the efficient allocation
$$ p^*(v,c)=\mathbf1\{c\le v\}. $$

Prescribe buyer transfer schedules
$$ r_j(v)=\int_0^v c\,g_j(c)\,dc $$
and seller transfer schedules
$$ z_i(c)=\int_c^1v\,f_i(v)\,dv. $$
All four schedules are bounded and measurable. The lemma supplies one common transfer $x$ realizing them, with absolutely integrable sections for every report.

Buyer incentives and participation

Under belief $g_j$, a buyer of value $v$ reporting $r$ has expected utility
$$ u_j(v,r) = v\int_0^r g_j(c)\,dc-r_j(r) = \int_0^r(v-c)g_j(c)\,dc. $$

If $r\le v$, then
$$ u_j(v,v)-u_j(v,r) = \int_r^v(v-c)g_j(c)\,dc\ge0. $$
If $r\ge v$, then
$$ u_j(v,v)-u_j(v,r) = \int_v^r(c-v)g_j(c)\,dc\ge0. $$
Since $g_j$ is strictly positive, either inequality is strict whenever $r\ne v$.

Truthful utility is
$$ u_j(v,v)=\int_0^v(v-c)g_j(c)\,dc\ge0. $$
Thus buyer truthfulness and interim individual rationality hold under both beliefs, for every value.

Seller incentives and participation

Under belief $f_i$, a seller of cost $c$ reporting $r$ has expected utility
$$ w_i(c,r) = z_i(r)-c\int_r^1f_i(v)\,dv = \int_r^1(v-c)f_i(v)\,dv. $$

If $r\le c$, then
$$ w_i(c,c)-w_i(c,r) = \int_r^c(c-v)f_i(v)\,dv\ge0. $$
If $r\ge c$, then
$$ w_i(c,c)-w_i(c,r) = \int_c^r(v-c)f_i(v)\,dv\ge0. $$
Strict positivity of $f_i$ again makes truth the unique best response.

Truthful utility is
$$ w_i(c,c)=\int_c^1(v-c)f_i(v)\,dv\ge0. $$

Hence level $1$ reports truthfully under uniform beliefs, and induction gives truthful reporting at every subsequent level. All required interim expectations are finite. Using the single transfer $x$ with opposite signs in the two agents’ utilities supplies budget balance at every report pair.

This proves feasibility of $p^*$ for every admissible input.

Why joint absolute integrability would prevent this construction

Under uniform beliefs, the prescribed schedules are
$$ r_0(v)=\frac{v^2}{2}, \qquad z_0(c)=\frac{1-c^2}{2}. $$
Their aggregate integrals differ:
$$ \int_0^1r_0(v)\,dv=\frac16, \qquad \int_0^1z_0(c)\,dc=\frac13. $$

A jointly absolutely integrable transfer would make these integrals equal by Fubini’s theorem. Thus the efficient mechanism constructed above necessarily uses the broader transfer class explicitly permitted in the task.

4. Optimal surplus and an exact decision algorithm

For every allocation probability $p(v,c)\in[0,1]$,
$$ (v-c)p(v,c)\le\max(v-c,0). $$
For $v\ge c$, this follows from $p\le1$; for $v<c$, it follows from $p\ge0$.

Multiplication by the positive densities and integration therefore yield
$$ S(p)\le S_{\mathrm{eff}}. $$
The feasible allocation $p^*$ attains equality. Consequently,
$$ \exists\text{ feasible common mechanism with }S(p)\ge q \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\le S_{\mathrm{eff}}. $$
In particular, the optimum is attained, so equality thresholds present no approximation issue.

Exact formula

Pad both density coefficient lists to degree $m$ and write
$$ f(v)=\sum_{i=0}^m a_i v^i, \qquad g(c)=\sum_{j=0}^m b_j c^j. $$
For each $j$,
$$ \int_0^v(v-c)c^j\,dc = \frac{v^{j+2}}{(j+1)(j+2)}. $$
Integrating the resulting polynomial gives
$$ S_{\mathrm{eff}} = \sum_{i=0}^m\sum_{j=0}^m \frac{a_i b_j} {(j+1)(j+2)(i+j+3)}. $$
This is an explicitly computable rational number.

Bit complexity

Let $Q_f$ and $Q_g$ be the products of the encoded coefficient denominators of $f$ and $g$. Their total bit length is $O(L)$. Write
$$ A_i=Q_fa_i,\qquad B_j=Q_gb_j, $$
so that $A_i,B_j$ are integers.

Set
$$ T=(m+2)!(2m+3)!. $$
Each denominator
$$ (j+1)(j+2)(i+j+3) $$
divides $T$: the first two factors divide $(m+2)!$, and the third divides $(2m+3)!$. Hence
$$ S_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{N}{Q_fQ_gT}, $$
where
$$ N= \sum_{i,j=0}^m A_iB_j \frac{T}{(j+1)(j+2)(i+j+3)} $$
is an integer.

All intermediate integers have bit length
$$ B=O\bigl(L+(m+1)\log(m+2)\bigr). $$
There are $O((m+1)^2)$ terms. Using ordinary integer arithmetic and exact cross multiplication to compare with $q$, a conservative bit-operation bound is
$$ O\!\left( K+L+(m+1)^2 \bigl[L+(m+1)\log(m+2)\bigr]^2 \right). $$

The level proportions affect only input reading; they do not affect the criterion. No finite point-type support or support-size parameter is used.

For example, the admissible nonuniform densities
$$ f(v)=\frac12+v,\qquad g(c)=\frac32-c $$
satisfy
$$ F(t)-t=-\frac{t(1-t)}2,\qquad G(t)-t=\frac{t(1-t)}2, $$
so they meet the stated interior exclusion. The formula above gives their attained efficient surplus as
$$ S_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{31}{120}. $$
