The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite category set \(O\), a policy word \(\Pi\) over types \(\{A,B\}\), masses \(\mu_A=\mu_B=1/2\), supply \(q_o=1/2\) for each \(o\in O\), rational additive valuations \(v_A,v_B\), and a feasible baseline mass flow with bundle distributions \(\beta_A,\beta_B\), does there exist a feasible alternative flow under the same policy and supplies with distributions \(\alpha_A,\alpha_B\) such that, for each type \(\theta\), there is a coupling \(\kappa_\theta\) of \(\beta_\theta\) and \(\alpha_\theta\) supported on pairs \((S,S')\) satisfying \(v_\theta(S')\ge v_\theta(S)\), with positive mass on strictly improving pairs? Each unit of mass receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq O\); at a policy position, the called cohort's mass picks one available category, with mass tracked by bundle history and cumulative category use bounded by supply.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here the support is \(\{A,B\}\), with \(\mu_A=\mu_B=1/2\), so total mass is one.
Input consists of a nonempty category set \(O\), with \(m=|O|\), a policy \(\Pi\in\{A,B\}^{m}\), supplies \(q_o=1/2\), nonnegative rational additive valuations \(v_A,v_B\), and an explicitly listed feasible rational baseline flow. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the baseline's nonzero transitions and their bundle labels.
A flow starts each type's entire mass with the empty bundle. At position \(t\), every unit of type \(\Pi_t\) must add exactly one category absent from its current bundle; the other type's bundles stay unchanged.
Mass at each bundle history may split arbitrarily among such additions, with conservation of mass. Cumulative consumption of each category at every position is at most its supply; flows may have nonnegative real masses. There are no non-manipulators, subsequent trades, or monetary payments.
Write \(\beta_\theta\) and \(\alpha_\theta\) for the baseline and alternative terminal bundle measures, each of total mass \(1/2\). An alternative is an improvement if, for each \(\theta\), a coupling \(\kappa_\theta\) of these measures is supported on pairs \((S,S')\) with \(v_\theta(S')\ge v_\theta(S)\), and for at least one type the coupling assigns positive mass to pairs satisfying strict inequality. Utility ties are allowed; no sincere-picking requirement is imposed.

Is deciding whether such an alternative flow exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The discrete problem of finding a Pareto-improving joint picking strategy without trades or payments is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even with two manipulators, additive valuations, and no non-manipulator ([verified full version](https://recherche.noiraudes.net/resources/papers/ECAI14-full.pdf)).
No result settling this exact continuous mass-flow and bundle-coupling problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The policy length was unspecified; set it equal to the number of categories, as in the discrete definition, and require a nonempty category set.
  • The valuation signs were unspecified; use nonnegative rational item values, allowing zero values and ties, consistent with the full version's hardness construction.
  • Interpret bundle distributions as mass measures of total mass 1/2 for each type, rather than conditional probability distributions.
  • Specify that the entire called cohort picks exactly once at each position, cannot repeat a category in its bundle, and may split its mass among choices while conserving bundle-history mass.
  • Specify an explicit sparse rational encoding of the baseline flow and include all of it in the input bit length; allow nonnegative real masses in alternative flows and couplings.
  • Resolve the strict-improvement quantifier in favor of the discrete Pareto definition: everyone weakly improves, and positive mass strictly improves for at least one type.
  • The baseline may be any supplied feasible flow, consistent with comparison against an input joint strategy; no sincerity or tie-breaking rule is required.
  • Explicitly exclude subsequent trades and monetary payments, as required by the anchor.

1. Classification. The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, even with the specified support size \(\tau=2\), masses and supplies equal to \(1/2\), and a baseline that never splits mass.

The hardness construction adapts the numerical gadget in Proposition 5 of [Bouveret and Lang’s full version](https://recherche.noiraudes.net/resources/papers/ECAI14-full.pdf). The crucial additional argument below proves that arbitrary fractional splitting cannot create an improvement in a negative partition instance.

2. A terminal-measure characterization. Let \(r_\theta\) denote the number of occurrences of type \(\theta\) in the policy. Every terminal bundle of that type has cardinality \(r_\theta\): each occurrence adds exactly one previously absent category.

For terminal measures \(\alpha_A,\alpha_B\), define category consumption by
\(C_o=\sum_{\theta\in\{A,B\}}\sum_{S\ni o}\alpha_\theta(S)\).
Because categories are never removed or repeated within a bundle, this equals cumulative consumption at the terminal position. Thus any feasible flow satisfies \(C_o\le 1/2\). Moreover,
\(\sum_{o\in O}C_o=(r_A+r_B)/2=m/2\).
Consequently, every category’s terminal consumption equals \(1/2\).

Conversely, suppose each \(\alpha_\theta\) has total mass \(1/2\), is supported on bundles of cardinality \(r_\theta\), and satisfies the category capacities. For each terminal bundle, order its categories and route its assigned mass along the path that adds them at successive occurrences of its type. Superposing these paths gives a flow with conservation and the required additions. At every position, consumption of each category is at most its terminal consumption, so all intermediate capacities hold.

Therefore, these terminal conditions characterize feasible flows.

3. The reduction. Start with positive integers \(s_1,\ldots,s_n\), encoded in binary, with
\(\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_i=2K\), where \(K\) is a positive integer. The question is whether some index subset sums to \(K\). This even-total restriction of \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\) remains \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard: doubling every integer in an unrestricted instance preserves its answer and makes its total even.

Create the categories
\(O=\{z,g_1,\ldots,g_n,d_1,\ldots,d_n\}\),
so \(m=2n+1\). Use the policy \(\Pi=AB^nA^n\), where the powers denote repetitions. Set all supplies and both type masses to \(1/2\).

The additive valuations are:

| Category | \(v_A\) | \(v_B\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(g_i\) | \(s_i\) | \(s_i\) |
| \(d_i\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(z\) | \(K-1/2\) | \(K+1/2\) |

These valuations are nonnegative because \(K\ge 1\).

Let \(G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_n\}\) and \(D=\{d_1,\ldots,d_n\}\). The baseline sends all of type \(A\)’s mass to \(\{z\}\cup D\) and all of type \(B\)’s mass to \(G\). Explicitly, \(A\) first adds \(z\), \(B\) adds the categories \(g_1,\ldots,g_n\), and \(A\) then adds \(d_1,\ldots,d_n\). Every addition carries mass \(1/2\).

This baseline is feasible: each category is consumed exactly once by mass \(1/2\). Its terminal measures are
\(\beta_A=\tfrac12\delta_{\{z\}\cup D}\) and
\(\beta_B=\tfrac12\delta_G\),
where \(\delta_S\) is the unit point mass at \(S\). The baseline utilities are respectively \(K-1/2\) and \(2K\).

4. A partition gives an improvement. Suppose \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in J}s_i=K\), and put \(j=|J|\). Positivity of the integers and the total \(2K\) imply \(1\le j\le n-1\).

Choose \(D_A\subseteq D\) with \(|D_A|=n+1-j\), which is possible because \(1\le j\le n-1\). Set
\(S_A=\{g_i:i\in J\}\cup D_A\)
and
\(S_B=\{z\}\cup\{g_i:i\notin J\}\cup(D\setminus D_A)\).

These bundles partition \(O\), and their cardinalities are
\(|S_A|=n+1\) and
\(|S_B|=1+(n-j)+(j-1)=n\).
They therefore match the two types’ numbers of picks. Sending mass \(1/2\) to each bundle is feasible by Section 2.

Their utilities are \(v_A(S_A)=K>K-1/2\) and
\(v_B(S_B)=2K+1/2>2K\).
For each type, coupling its baseline point mass to its new point mass strictly improves all its mass. Hence the constructed instance is positive.

5. A fractional improvement gives a partition. Now suppose an improving flow exists, with arbitrary nonnegative real masses.

Because each baseline measure is concentrated on one bundle, its coupling condition implies that every positive-mass alternative bundle satisfies
\(v_A(S)\ge K-1/2\) for type \(A\), or
\(v_B(S)\ge 2K\) for type \(B\).

Normalize the alternative measures to probability distributions:
\(P_\theta(S)=2\alpha_\theta(S)\).
Define
\(w(S)=\sum_{i:g_i\in S}s_i\),
\(x_\theta=P_\theta(z\in S)\), and
\(W_\theta=\sum_S P_\theta(S)w(S)\).
Every \(w(S)\) is an integer.

For every positive-probability type-\(A\) bundle:

  • If \(z\notin S\), then \(w(S)\ge K-1/2\), and integrality gives \(w(S)\ge K\).
  • If \(z\in S\), nonnegativity gives \(w(S)\ge 0\).

Thus \(W_A\ge K(1-x_A)\).

For every positive-probability type-\(B\) bundle:

  • If \(z\notin S\), then \(w(S)\ge 2K\).
  • If \(z\in S\), then \(w(S)+K+1/2\ge 2K\), so integrality gives \(w(S)\ge K\).

Thus \(W_B\ge 2K-Kx_B\).

The supply of \(z\), after normalization, gives \(x_A+x_B\le 1\). The supplies of the integer-valued categories give
\(W_A+W_B=\sum_i s_i\bigl(P_A(g_i\in S)+P_B(g_i\in S)\bigr)\le 2K\).
Combining these inequalities yields

\(\displaystyle 2K\ge W_A+W_B \ge 3K-K(x_A+x_B) \ge 2K. \)

Every inequality must therefore be an equality. In particular,
\(W_A=K(1-x_A)\) and \(W_B=2K-Kx_B\).

The corresponding bundlewise lower bounds have nonnegative slack. Their expected slack is zero, so every positive-probability bundle has zero slack. This proves the following exhaustive table:

| Type | Contains \(z\)? | Forced \(w(S)\) | Utility |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| \(A\) | Yes | \(0\) | \(K-1/2\) |
| \(A\) | No | \(K\) | \(K\) |
| \(B\) | Yes | \(K\) | \(2K+1/2\) |
| \(B\) | No | \(2K\) | \(2K\) |

Strict improvement has positive mass for at least one type. Because its baseline is a point mass, this requires a positive-mass alternative bundle with utility strictly above that type’s baseline utility. By the table, any such bundle has \(w(S)=K\).

Hence its index subset \(\{i:g_i\in S\}\) solves the original partition instance. This proves soundness for arbitrary real splitting.

The construction has polynomial bit length. There are \(2n+1\) categories, the valuation numerators have polynomial encoding length, and the baseline contains only linearly many transitions with polynomial-size bundle labels. Thus the reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under the stated encoding.

6. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\). It remains to justify polynomial-size certificates despite permitting real masses.

Aggregate the given baseline into terminal atoms \(S_{\theta,i}\) with positive rational masses \(b_{\theta,i}\). Let \(R\) be their total number. Both \(R\) and the bit lengths of these aggregated masses are polynomially bounded by the explicit input length.

Introduce a variable \(y_{\theta,i,T}\) for every bundle \(T\) satisfying
\(|T|=r_\theta\) and
\(v_\theta(T)\ge v_\theta(S_{\theta,i})\).
It represents coupling mass from baseline atom \(S_{\theta,i}\) to terminal bundle \(T\). Impose nonnegativity and the equations

\(\displaystyle \sum_T y_{\theta,i,T}=b_{\theta,i}\)

for every baseline atom, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta,i,T:\,o\in T}y_{\theta,i,T}=\tfrac12\)

for every category \(o\).

By Section 2, this system exactly describes feasible terminal couplings with weak improvement. Strict improvement is equivalent to positivity of the linear objective

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\theta,i,T:\,v_\theta(T)>v_\theta(S_{\theta,i})}y_{\theta,i,T}. \)

Although there can be exponentially many variables, this is a finite, nonempty, bounded rational polytope: the baseline supplies a feasible point, and each variable is bounded by its baseline row mass. A linear objective attains its maximum at a vertex.

There are \(R+m\) equality rows. Any vertex has at most \(R+m\) positive variables. Indeed, if the corresponding columns were linearly dependent, a sufficiently small positive and negative perturbation along that dependence would preserve the equations and nonnegativity, contradicting extremality.

Those positive coordinates have polynomial bit length. Select a nonsingular square subsystem determining them. Its coefficients belong to \(\{0,1\}\), its dimension is at most \(R+m\), and its right-hand side has polynomial bit length. Cramer’s rule gives polynomial-bit rational coordinates: the integer determinants have bit length bounded by a polynomial in \(R+m\), and clearing the rational right-hand-side denominators also requires only polynomially many bits.

Consequently, whenever an improvement exists, one exists with a polynomial-size list of rational coupling entries. A verifier checks bundle cardinalities, utility comparisons, all mass equations, and positive strict-improvement mass using exact rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

Together with the reduction, this establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness. In particular, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
