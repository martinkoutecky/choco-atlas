Conclusion

Every admissible run satisfies the stated inequality. The proof is deterministic and permits both error terms to have either sign. The corresponding individual guarantee appears as Proposition 2 in [Teso, Dragone, and Passerini](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/10929/10788); below is a direct proof for the precise update rule in the question.

1. Revealing a coordinate preserves the current scores

Fix a type \(h\), and suppress its index temporarily. Work throughout in the fixed ambient space \(\mathbb R^m\), padding unknown coordinates and their learned weights with zeros.

Let \(K_t\) be the coordinates known before round \(t\)’s critique, and \(J_t\) those known afterward. For a coordinate set \(S\), let \(P_S\) be the coordinate projection that zeros coordinates outside \(S\). Thus

\(\psi_t(x)=P_{K_t}\phi^*(x)\), and \(\phi_t(x)=P_{J_t}\phi^*(x)\).

The revelation and retention rules give \(K_t\subseteq J_t\) and \(K_{t+1}=J_t\).

We first prove that \(w_t\) is zero outside \(K_t\). This holds initially because \(w_1=0\). If it holds at round \(t\), then both \(w_t\) and the update difference are supported on \(J_t\). Consequently, \(w_{t+1}\) is supported on \(J_t=K_{t+1}\), establishing the induction.

In particular, every coordinate newly revealed during round \(t\) has weight zero in \(w_t\). Hence, for every \(x\in X\),

\(\langle w_t,\phi_t(x)\rangle=\langle w_t,\psi_t(x)\rangle\).

Define the update difference by

\(\Delta_t=\phi_t(\bar x_t)-\phi_t(x_t)\).

Because \(x_t\) maximizes the pre-critique score and \(\bar x_t\in X\), it follows that

\(\langle w_t,\Delta_t\rangle =\langle w_t,\psi_t(\bar x_t)\rangle-\langle w_t,\psi_t(x_t)\rangle \le 0\).

This reasoning allows arbitrary maximizing ties. It also covers rounds in which no coordinate is revealed.

2. Bounding the learned weight norm

Coordinate projection cannot increase the Euclidean norm. Therefore, for every feasible \(x\),

\(\|\phi_t(x)\|_2\le\|\phi^*(x)\|_2\le R\).

The triangle inequality gives

\(\|\Delta_t\|_2\le\|\phi_t(\bar x_t)\|_2+\|\phi_t(x_t)\|_2\le 2R\).

Using the update \(w_{t+1}=w_t+\Delta_t\) and the nonpositive inner product proved above,

\(\|w_{t+1}\|_2^2 =\|w_t\|_2^2+2\langle w_t,\Delta_t\rangle+\|\Delta_t\|_2^2 \le\|w_t\|_2^2+4R^2\).

Summing over the \(T\) rounds, with \(w_1=0\), yields

\(\|w_{T+1}\|_2^2\le 4R^2T\), hence \(\|w_{T+1}\|_2\le 2R\sqrt T\).

3. The per-type regret inequality with signed errors

Write \(r_t=u^*(x^*)-u^*(x_t)\). By the assumed feedback identity and the definition of the post-revelation missing-feature error,

\(\langle w^*,\Delta_t\rangle =u^*(\bar x_t)-u^*(x_t)-\eta_t =\alpha r_t-\xi_t-\eta_t\).

The updates telescope in the fixed ambient space:

\(\sum_{t=1}^T\Delta_t=w_{T+1}-w_1=w_{T+1}\).

Consequently,

\(\alpha T\,\mathrm{REG}_T =\langle w^*,w_{T+1}\rangle+\sum_{t=1}^T(\xi_t+\eta_t)\).

Cauchy–Schwarz and the norm bound imply

\(\langle w^*,w_{T+1}\rangle \le\|w^*\|_2\|w_{T+1}\|_2 \le 2R\|w^*\|_2\sqrt T\).

Dividing by the positive quantity \(\alpha T\) proves

\(\mathrm{REG}_T \le \dfrac{2R\|w^*\|_2}{\alpha\sqrt T} +\dfrac{1}{\alpha T}\sum_{t=1}^T(\xi_t+\eta_t)\).

No step replaces an error by its absolute value or assumes an error is nonnegative.

4. Aggregating over the listed support

The preceding proof applies separately to every listed type \(h\). Since each \(\mu_h>0\), multiplication by \(\mu_h\) preserves its inequality. Summing over the \(\tau\) listed types gives exactly

\(\displaystyle \sum_{h=1}^{\tau}\mu_h\mathrm{REG}_{T,h} \le \sum_{h=1}^{\tau}\mu_h \left[ \frac{2R_h\|w_h^*\|_2}{\alpha_h\sqrt T} +\frac{1}{\alpha_h T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\xi_{h,t}+\eta_{h,t}) \right]. \)

Thus there is no admissible counterexample.

The historical strict-ranking test does not require an additional lemma for this guarantee: the proof works for any revelation schedule that retains coordinates and initializes newly revealed weights at zero.

5. Computational scope

This establishes a regret guarantee, without asserting a running-time bound. The premises do not specify computational representations or costs for feature evaluation, exact maximization over \(X\), or historical strict-ranking feasibility. In particular, the finite support and input length \(L\) alone do not establish polynomial-time implementation.
