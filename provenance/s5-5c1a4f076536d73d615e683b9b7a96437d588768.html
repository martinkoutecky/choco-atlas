The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), per-institution endowments \(z_t\), and per-institution liabilities \(\ell_{ts}\) owed uniformly across type-\(s\) mass, compute the greatest proportional clearing profile. Equivalently, solve for \(\lambda_t\in[0,1]\) maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\lambda_t\sum_{s\in T}\ell_{ts}\), subject for every \(s\in T\) to \(z_s+\frac{1}{\mu_s}\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\lambda_t\ell_{ts}-\lambda_s\sum_{u\in T}\ell_{su}\geq 0\); set \(\lambda_t=0\) by convention if type \(t\) has no liabilities.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type represents a mass of identical institutions. Input endowments \(z_t\) and liabilities \(\ell_{ts}\) are nonnegative binary rationals, with \(\ell_{tt}=0\). Each type-\(t\) institution owes a total of \(\ell_{ts}\) to type-\(s\) institutions, distributed uniformly across their mass.
Write \(b_t=\sum_{s\in T}\ell_{ts}\). A proportional payment profile is \(\lambda\in[0,1]^T\): each type-\(t\) institution pays \(\lambda_t\ell_{ts}\) to type \(s\). Its available assets are \(a_t(\lambda)=z_t+\mu_t^{-1}\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\lambda_s\ell_{st}\).
A profile clears if \(\lambda_tb_t=\min\{b_t,a_t(\lambda)\}\) for every \(t\), with \(\lambda_t=0\) when \(b_t=0\). The greatest clearing profile dominates every clearing profile coordinatewise.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. Running times are measured in \(\tau\) and \(L\); this model has no separate \(m\) parameter.

Is exact computation of the greatest proportional clearing profile polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many individually listed institutions, the greatest proportional clearing payment matrix is the unique payment matrix selected by a linear program, yielding polynomial-time computation for rational inputs. [Csóka–Herings](https://www.mechanism-design.org/arch/v007-1/p_02.pdf)
No result addressing exactly this support-input mass problem was located in the literature search.

  • Imposed zero diagonal liabilities at the type level, carrying over the finite model's zero-diagonal restriction; this excludes liabilities between institutions of the same type.
  • Made priority of creditors explicit through the clearing equation: an institution pays all liabilities or exhausts its available assets.

Result

Exact computation of the greatest proportional clearing profile is polynomial-time solvable in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

The proof uses a rational linear program with \(\tau\) variables. It applies directly to the given support input, without expanding masses into individually listed institutions.

1. A linear program for the support model

For each \(t\in T\), compute \(b_t=\sum_{s\in T}\ell_{ts}\). Define the polytope \(P\) by the following constraints on \(x\in\mathbb R^T\):

  • \(0\le x_t\le 1\) for every \(t\in T\);
  • \(x_t=0\) whenever \(b_t=0\);
  • \(\mu_t b_t x_t\le \mu_t z_t+\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\ell_{st}x_s\) for every \(t\in T\).

Because \(\mu_t>0\), the last constraint is exactly \(b_tx_t\le a_t(x)\). Thus every clearing profile belongs to \(P\), although feasibility alone does not require clearing.

Consider the linear program

\(\displaystyle \max_{x\in P}\sum_{t\in T}x_t.\)

The zero vector is feasible: every right-hand side in the asset constraints is nonnegative. Moreover, \(P\) is closed and contained in \([0,1]^T\), hence compact. Therefore an optimum exists.

2. The optimum uniquely dominates every feasible profile

Lemma. If \(x,y\in P\), then their coordinatewise maximum \(w\), defined by \(w_t=\max\{x_t,y_t\}\), belongs to \(P\).

Proof. The bounds and zero-liability constraints are preserved. Fix \(t\in T\), and choose \(v\in\{x,y\}\) with \(w_t=v_t\). Since \(v\) is feasible and all incoming-payment coefficients are nonnegative,

\(\displaystyle \mu_t b_t w_t =\mu_t b_t v_t \le \mu_t z_t+\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\ell_{st}v_s \le \mu_t z_t+\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\ell_{st}w_s. \)

Thus every asset constraint holds. This proves the lemma.

Let \(x^*\) be an optimum. For any \(y\in P\), the lemma makes \(\max\{x^*,y\}\) feasible. If some coordinate of \(y\) exceeded the corresponding coordinate of \(x^*\), then

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t\in T}\max\{x_t^*,y_t\}>\sum_{t\in T}x_t^*, \)

contradicting optimality. Consequently \(x^*\) dominates every vector in \(P\).

In particular, any two optima dominate one another, so the optimum is unique.

3. The optimum clears

The convention \(x_t^*=0\) already holds when \(b_t=0\). Fix a type \(t\) with \(b_t>0\).

Feasibility gives both \(b_tx_t^*\le b_t\) and \(b_tx_t^*\le a_t(x^*)\). Suppose its clearing equation failed. Then

\(\displaystyle b_tx_t^*<\min\{b_t,a_t(x^*)\}.\)

Hence \(x_t^*<1\), and the asset constraint has strictly positive slack

\(\displaystyle d_t=\mu_t z_t+\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\ell_{st}x_s^*-\mu_t b_t x_t^*>0. \)

Choose

\(\displaystyle \varepsilon=\frac12\min\left\{1-x_t^*,\frac{d_t}{\mu_t b_t}\right\}>0, \)

and increase only coordinate \(t\) by \(\varepsilon\).

This change remains feasible:

  • The bound \(x_t\le 1\) is preserved.
  • In type \(t\)'s asset constraint, the right-hand side is unchanged because \(\ell_{tt}=0\). The left-hand side increases by \(\mu_t b_t\varepsilon\le d_t/2\).
  • For every other type \(r\), its left-hand side is unchanged, while its right-hand side increases by the nonnegative amount \(\mu_t\ell_{tr}\varepsilon\).

The objective increases by \(\varepsilon\), contradicting optimality. Therefore

\(\displaystyle b_tx_t^*=\min\{b_t,a_t(x^*)\}\)

for every type with positive liabilities. Thus \(x^*\) clears.

Every clearing profile lies in \(P\), and Section 2 proves that \(x^*\) dominates every profile in \(P\). Therefore \(x^*\) is the greatest clearing profile.

4. Exact polynomial-time computation

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a linear program with binary-encoded rational coefficients and a nonempty bounded feasible region admits computation of an exact rational optimal solution in time polynomial in its encoding length. Polynomial-time solvability in the binary bit model is discussed in [Adler–Beling, *Polynomial Algorithms for Linear Programming over the Algebraic Numbers*](https://adler.ieor.berkeley.edu/ilans_pubs/lp_algebraic_stoc_1992.pdf).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • Polynomial dimensions. The program has \(\tau\) variables, at most \(4\tau\) scalar constraints, and at most \(O(\tau^2)\) coefficient entries.
  • Polynomial coefficient length. Its coefficients are formed from the input using the sums \(b_t=\sum_s\ell_{ts}\) and products \(\mu_tb_t\), \(\mu_tz_t\), and \(\mu_s\ell_{st}\). For binary-encoded rationals, these operations produce rational coefficients of polynomial bit length and can be performed in polynomial time. For example, if each input numerator and denominator has at most \(L\) bits, a common-denominator calculation bounds each \(b_t\)'s encoding length by \(O(\tau L+\log\tau)\).
  • Existence and boundedness. Section 1 established that the feasible region is nonempty and compact.

There is also no exponential output-length obstruction. The unique optimum is a vertex: otherwise it would be a strict convex combination of two distinct feasible points, and linearity would force both points to be optimal, contradicting uniqueness. At a vertex, \(\tau\) linearly independent active constraints determine the coordinates. Clearing denominators gives an integer system with polynomial coefficient bit length; Cramer's rule and the determinant bound \(|\det A|\le\tau!H^\tau\), when all entries have magnitude at most \(H\), give polynomial bit length for every coordinate.

The algorithm is therefore to construct this rational linear program and solve it exactly. Its unique output is the greatest clearing profile, and its running time is polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

The connection with the individually listed model is explicit: aggregate liabilities \(B_{ts}=\mu_t\ell_{ts}\) and aggregate endowments \(E_t=\mu_tz_t\) satisfy

\(\displaystyle x_t\sum_{s\in T}B_{ts} =\min\left\{\sum_{s\in T}B_{ts},\,E_t+\sum_{s\in T}x_sB_{st}\right\}. \)

Thus the support model itself gives a finite proportional clearing network. This explains why the proportional LP structure in [Csóka–Herings, Theorem 4.2](https://www.mechanism-design.org/arch/v007-1/p_02.pdf) carries over; the preceding argument proves the result directly for the stated mass model.
