Yes. There is a deterministic algorithm that returns a binary rational $x\in K$ satisfying

in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon$, independently of $N$. Its success probability is therefore $1$.

The essential repair to the previous argument is to restrict every finite-difference probe by the remaining budget. At allocation $x$, the probe length in coordinate $i$ will be
$$ r_i=\min\left\{h,\mu_i-x_i,\kappa-\sum_jx_j\right\}. $$
Thus both the probe and the actual update remain in $K$. This makes the curvature bound using $\mathrm{OPT}$ valid throughout every probe.

We prove all ingredients below. Rational numbers are represented by binary-encoded numerators and denominators.

2. Existence of the limit and its branching-process representation

Define the coordinate box
$$ Q=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}[0,\mu_t]. $$
We first establish the limiting influence on all of $Q$, without imposing a budget. Denote this extension by $\Phi$; its restriction to $K$ is $F$.

For $x\in Q$, put
$$ s_t=\frac{x_t}{\mu_t}, \qquad B_{tu}=\lambda_{ut}\mu_u. $$
Consider a genealogical branching tree rooted at type $t$. A type-$t$ vertex has, independently for each $u$, a Poisson number of type-$u$ children with mean $B_{tu}$. Independently of this tree, mark each type-$u$ vertex with probability $s_u$.

The orientation of $B$ follows from reverse exploration: a type-$u$ predecessor of a type-$t$ agent supplies an incoming edge at rate $\lambda_{ut}/N$, and there are asymptotically $N\mu_u$ possible predecessors.

We claim that
$$ \Phi(x)=\sum_t\mu_t \mathbb P_t(\text{the genealogical tree contains a mark}). $$

2.1 Bounded explorations converge

By permutation invariance within each type, randomizing the seed identities uniformly within each type leaves expected influence unchanged.

For any fixed exploration bound, reverse exploration in the finite graph converges to the branching exploration described above. Indeed:

  • Fresh predecessor counts converge from binomial distributions to the stated independent Poisson distributions.
  • Collisions among a bounded number of exposed agents have probability tending to zero.
  • Seed indicators on finitely many distinct agents converge from sampling without replacement to independent marks with probabilities $s_u$.

To accommodate possibly unbounded offspring counts, first truncate the total exposed offspring at a fixed bound. Under this truncation, collision probabilities tend to zero and all required binomial and hypergeometric limits apply. The truncation can then be removed because finitely many offspring counts form a tight family.

It remains to justify passage from bounded exploration to unrestricted reachability.

2.2 Infinite relevant trees contain a mark almost surely

Let
$$ S=\{t:x_t>0\}, $$
and let $R$ consist of types reachable from $S$ in the directed type graph with an arc $u\to t$ whenever $\lambda_{ut}>0$.

If $S$ is empty, there are no seeds and $\Phi(x)=0$. Suppose otherwise.

Types outside $R$ cannot be activated. For a root in $R$, restricting reverse exploration to types in $R$ preserves every possible seed-to-root path.

For each type in $R$, choose a reverse type path to some type in $S$, of length at most $\tau-1$. Starting at a fresh vertex, test whether children exist along this prescribed path and whether its endpoint is marked. Each required offspring mean is positive, as is the endpoint marking probability. Since there are finitely many starting types, these tests have a uniform positive lower bound $p$ on their success probability.

The tests can be performed successively from a frontier of vertices with unexamined randomness. Each test examines at most $\tau$ vertices. Reveal their offspring, retaining all unexamined children on the frontier. Conditional on preceding unsuccessful tests, the next test succeeds with probability at least $p$.

Every offspring count is finite almost surely. Consequently, on an infinite tree, finitely many such tests cannot exhaust the frontier. The event that the tree is infinite and contains no mark therefore has probability at most $(1-p)^j$ for every positive integer $j$. Its probability is zero.

2.3 The exploration sandwich

Fix a root type in $R$. Let $D_{N,H}$ be the event that its restricted reverse exploration terminates after at most $H$ vertices and finds no seed. Let $A_{N,H}$ be the event that its first $H$ explored vertices contain no seed, with an earlier seed-free termination also counted.

Then
$$ \mathbb P(D_{N,H}) \leq \mathbb P(\text{root is unactivated}) \leq \mathbb P(A_{N,H}). $$
For fixed $H$, the bounding probabilities converge to their branching-process counterparts.

As $H$ tends to infinity, the lower bound converges to the probability of a finite unmarked tree. The upper bound converges to the probability of an unmarked tree, finite or infinite. These probabilities are equal by Section 2.2. This proves existence of the limit.

In the full genealogical tree, any path from a marked vertex to the root uses only types in $R$. Pruning vertices outside $R$ therefore preserves the event of containing a mark. This establishes the claimed full-tree representation of $\Phi$.

Averaging the activation probability over root types, with weights $\mu_t$, proves that $\Phi(x)$ equals the limiting normalized expected influence.

2.4 Positive allocations

Suppose every $x_t>0$. An infinite genealogical tree contains a mark almost surely: if $s_{\min}=\min_t s_t>0$, the probability that any specified $H$ distinct vertices are all unmarked is at most $(1-s_{\min})^H$.

Let $q_t$ be the probability that a tree rooted at type $t$ contains no mark. Branching independence gives
$$ q_t=(1-s_t)\exp\left(\sum_uB_{tu}(q_u-1)\right), \qquad \Phi(x)=1-\sum_t\mu_tq_t. $$

For later differentiation, let $Z_u$ count the type-$u$ vertices in a finite underlying genealogical tree before any marks are assigned. Its distribution is independent of $x$. Conditional on this tree, activation has probability
$$ h_Z(x)=1-\prod_u(1-x_u/\mu_u)^{Z_u}. $$
Infinite trees contribute the constant activation probability $1$ throughout the positive coordinate box.

3. Monotonicity, diminishing returns, and curvature

Marking vertices using independent uniform random variables shows that $\Phi$ is coordinatewise nondecreasing on $Q$.

For a finite genealogical tree, differentiation gives useful probabilistic identities:
$$ x_i\partial_i h_Z = \mathbb P(\text{exactly one mark, of type }i), $$
$$ x_i^2(-\partial_{ii}h_Z) = 2\mathbb P(\text{exactly two marks, both of type }i), $$
and, for distinct $i,j$,
$$ x_ix_j(-\partial_{ij}h_Z) = \mathbb P(\text{exactly two marks, one of each type}). $$
For example, the first identity follows because
$$ x_i\partial_i h_Z = Z_i s_i(1-s_i)^{Z_i-1} \prod_{u\ne i}(1-s_u)^{Z_u}. $$
The second and third identities follow by choosing respectively an unordered pair of type-$i$ vertices, or one vertex of each of types $i,j$.

Each event on the right entails activation. Hence, at positive coordinates,
$$ 0\leq\partial_i h_Z\leq\frac{h_Z}{x_i}, \qquad 0\leq-\partial_{ij}h_Z\leq\frac{2h_Z}{x_ix_j}. $$

On any neighborhood where the variable coordinates are bounded below by a positive constant, these derivatives are uniformly bounded independently of the tree size. Dominated differentiation is therefore valid when averaging over finite trees. Infinite trees contribute a constant. We obtain
$$ \partial_i\Phi(x)\geq0, \qquad \partial_{ij}\Phi(x)\leq0, \qquad -\partial_{ii}\Phi(x)\leq\frac{2\Phi(x)}{x_i^2}. $$
Coordinates already at capacity may be held fixed. At a capacity endpoint, the statements needed below follow by one-sided limits, using the same uniform bounds.

In particular, each marginal derivative decreases when any coordinate increases.

Now let $x\in K$ have all coordinates positive, and suppose some coordinate is below capacity. For any $y\in K$, monotonicity gives
$$ F(y)\leq\Phi(x\vee y), $$
where $x\vee y$ denotes the coordinatewise maximum. Although this maximum can lie outside $K$, it belongs to $Q$, where $\Phi$ is defined.

Integrating along the segment from $x$ to $x\vee y$, and using diminishing marginal derivatives, gives
$$ F(y)-F(x) \leq \sum_{i:x_i<\mu_i}(y_i-x_i)_+\partial_i\Phi(x). $$
Since
$$ \sum_i(y_i-x_i)_+\leq\sum_i y_i\leq\kappa, $$
taking the supremum over $y\in K$ yields
$$ \mathrm{OPT}-F(x) \leq \kappa\max_{i:x_i<\mu_i}\partial_i\Phi(x). $$
This statement is only needed at nonterminal allocations, where the maximum is over a nonempty set. No attainment of $\mathrm{OPT}$ is assumed.

4. A polynomial-time value oracle

We prove the following computational claim.

Value-oracle claim. Given a positive rational allocation $x\in Q$ and a rational $\xi\in(0,1)$, one can deterministically compute a rational $V(x)$ satisfying
$$ |V(x)-\Phi(x)|\leq\xi $$
in time polynomial in the input and query bit lengths and $\log(1/\xi)$.

4.1 A unique fixed point

Let
$$ J=\{t:x_t<\mu_t\}. $$
For $t\notin J$, we have $q_t=0$. If $J$ is empty, return $1$.

For $t\in J$, define
$$ c_t=-\log(1-s_t)+\sum_{u\notin J}B_{tu}, $$
and
$$ T_t(r)=c_t+\sum_{u\in J}B_{tu}(1-e^{-r_u}). $$
Every $c_t$ is strictly positive. Substituting $r_t=-\log q_t$ transforms the no-mark equations into
$$ r=T(r). $$
The substitution is legitimate because, for $t\in J$,
$$ 0<(1-s_t)e^{-\sum_u B_{tu}}\leq q_t\leq1-s_t<1. $$

Set
$$ R_t=c_t+\sum_{u\in J}B_{tu}. $$
The map $T$ is coordinatewise nondecreasing, and $T(R)\leq R$. Its iteration from $R$ therefore decreases to a nonnegative fixed point.

For uniqueness, suppose $r,z$ are nonnegative fixed points. Both are strictly positive because $T(r)\geq c$. If
$$ \alpha=\max_t\frac{r_t}{z_t}>1, $$
monotonicity and concavity of $T$ imply
$$ T(r)\leq T(\alpha z) \leq\alpha T(z)-(\alpha-1)c. $$
The second inequality follows by applying concavity to
$$ z=\frac1\alpha(\alpha z)+\left(1-\frac1\alpha\right)0. $$
At a coordinate attaining the maximum ratio, the displayed inequality contradicts $r_t=\alpha z_t$. Thus $r\leq z$, and interchanging them proves equality. Denote the unique fixed point by $r^*$.

4.2 A convex optimization formulation

Write
$$ n=|J|, \qquad d=\min_{t\in J}s_t, \qquad B_{\max}=\max_t\sum_uB_{tu}, \qquad A=1+B_{\max}. $$
Choose a polynomial-bit integer $H\geq1$ such that
$$ 1-s_t\geq2^{-H}\quad(t\in J), $$
and put
$$ U=H+B_{\max}+1. $$
Such an $H$ can be obtained from the binary lengths of the positive rationals $1-s_t$. Since $-\log(1-s_t)\leq H\log2<H$, we have $R_t<U$.

Define
$$ g_t(r)=r_t-c_t-\sum_{u\in J}B_{tu}(1-e^{-r_u}), $$
and the compact convex set
$$ \mathcal C= \{r\in[0,U]^n:g_t(r)\leq0\text{ for every }t\in J\}. $$
Each $g_t$ is convex because it is an affine function plus a nonnegative linear combination of exponentials.

Every $r\in\mathcal C$ satisfies
$$ r\leq T(r)\leq R. $$
Comparison with the decreasing iteration from $R$ then gives
$$ r\leq r^*\quad\text{coordinatewise}. $$
Indeed, if $r\leq R^{(k)}$, then
$$ r\leq T(r)\leq T(R^{(k)})=R^{(k+1)}. $$
Taking the limit proves the assertion.

Thus $r^*$ is the unique maximizer of $\sum_t r_t$ over $\mathcal C$. If a feasible $\widehat r$ has objective gap at most $\xi/2$, then
$$ 0\leq \sum_{t\in J}\mu_t(e^{-\widehat r_t}-e^{-r_t^*}) \leq \sum_{t\in J}(r_t^*-\widehat r_t) \leq\xi/2. $$
Here we used that $e^{-a}$ is $1$-Lipschitz for $a\geq0$ and that $\mu_t\leq1$.

4.3 Explicit radius bounds

On the nonnegative orthant,
$$ \|\nabla g_t(r)\|_2 \leq1+\sum_{u\in J}B_{tu}\leq A. $$
At the rational center $a_0=(d/2)\mathbf1$,
$$ g_t(a_0)\leq d/2-c_t\leq-d/2. $$
Consequently, the Euclidean ball with center $a_0$ and radius
$$ \rho=\frac{d}{4A} $$
lies in $\mathcal C$. Its coordinates lie between $d/4$ and $3d/4$, so it also lies in the box. The nonlinear constraints retain slack at least $d/4$ by the gradient bound.

An outer ball, with the same center and radius $nU$, contains $\mathcal C$. Both radii and the center have polynomial binary encoding length.

4.4 A rational weak separation oracle

Given a rational point $r$ and a distance tolerance $\zeta\in(0,1)$, first separate exactly if $r$ lies outside $[0,U]^n$. Otherwise set
$$ \eta=\frac{d\zeta}{4nU} $$
and evaluate every $g_t(r)$ within error $\eta/4$.

If all computed values are at most $\eta$, every true value is at most $2\eta$. Convexity and $g_t(0)=-c_t\leq-d$ imply that
$$ \widetilde r=\left(1-\frac{2\eta}{d}\right)r $$
belongs to $\mathcal C$. The scaling factor lies in $[0,1]$, and
$$ \|\widetilde r-r\|_2 \leq\frac{2\eta U\sqrt n}{d} \leq\zeta. $$
The oracle can therefore certify distance at most $\zeta$ from $\mathcal C$.

Otherwise, some constraint satisfies $g_t(r)>3\eta/4$. Compute a rational approximation $v$ to its gradient with coordinate errors at most
$$ \frac{\eta}{8nU}. $$
For every $w\in\mathcal C$, convexity gives
$$ \nabla g_t(r)^{\mathsf T}(w-r) \leq g_t(w)-g_t(r)<-3\eta/4. $$
Since each coordinate of $w-r$ has absolute value at most $U$, gradient approximation changes this expression by at most $\eta/8$. Hence
$$ v^{\mathsf T}(w-r)<-5\eta/8. $$
The rational halfspace
$$ v^{\mathsf T}(w-r)\leq-\eta/2 $$
contains $\mathcal C$ and excludes $r$. This supplies the required separation.

All computations have polynomial bit complexity. For completeness, logarithms of positive rationals can be evaluated using binary range reduction and
$$ \log v= 2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2j+1} \left(\frac{v-1}{v+1}\right)^{2j+1}, \qquad 1\leq v\leq2. $$
The series converges geometrically. To approximate $e^{-a}$ within $2^{-p}$, one may return zero if $a\geq p+1$. Otherwise, range reduction and the exponential power series require only polynomially many bit operations. Multiplication by the encoded coefficients $B_{tu}$ requires only an additional polynomial amount of precision.

4.5 Weak optimization and exact feasibility

We use the following standard ellipsoid-method theorem: for a compact convex body with an explicitly encoded contained ball and containing ball, a polynomial-time weak separation oracle yields polynomial-time weak linear optimization. Given rational tolerance $\zeta>0$, the latter can return a rational $z$ with distance at most $\zeta$ from the body and objective at least the exact optimum minus $\zeta$. Complexity depends polynomially on the encodings, including the logarithms of the radii and tolerance. This is the separation-to-optimization result of [Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf).

The required hypotheses hold here: $\mathcal C$ is compact and convex, Section 4.3 supplies the encoded balls, and Section 4.4 supplies the oracle. The one-dimensional case is also covered, or can be obtained by adjoining an independent interval coordinate with zero objective coefficient.

Apply weak optimization to the objective $\sum_t r_t$, with
$$ \zeta=\frac{\xi d}{100nA(1+U)}. $$
It returns a rational $z$ satisfying
$$ \operatorname{dist}(z,\mathcal C)\leq\zeta, \qquad \sum_tz_t\geq\sum_tr_t^*-\zeta. $$

Clip $z$ coordinatewise to $[0,U]^n$, obtaining $\bar z$. Projection onto the box preserves the distance bound to $\mathcal C$. Moreover, its displacement has Euclidean norm at most $\zeta$, so clipping loses at most $\sqrt n\,\zeta$ in the objective. The Lipschitz bound gives
$$ g_t(\bar z)\leq A\zeta. $$
Set
$$ \theta=\frac{2A\zeta}{d}, \qquad \widehat r=(1-\theta)\bar z. $$
We have $0<\theta<1$. Convexity gives
$$ g_t(\widehat r) \leq(1-\theta)A\zeta-\theta d \leq0. $$
Thus $\widehat r$ is exactly feasible, despite being produced from a weakly feasible point.

Its objective gap is at most
$$ \sum_t(r_t^*-\widehat r_t) \leq (1+\sqrt n)\zeta+\theta nU <\xi/2. $$
For the last inequality, use $1+\sqrt n\leq2n$, $d\leq1$, and $A\geq1$: the two terms are each at most $\xi/50$.

Section 4.2 now bounds the induced influence error by $\xi/2$. Evaluating the final weighted exponentials with total error at most $\xi/2$ proves the value-oracle claim. Every required precision has polynomial binary length.

5. The allocation algorithm

If $\kappa=0$, return $x=0$. If $\kappa=1$, return $x=\mu$. These allocations are optimal. Henceforth assume
$$ 0<\kappa<1. $$
Because seed mass contributes to influence and an allocation of total mass $\kappa$ exists,
$$ \kappa\leq\mathrm{OPT}\leq1. $$

Define
$$ \delta=\epsilon/4, \qquad a=\frac{\delta\kappa}{\tau}, \qquad b_i=\min\{\mu_i,a\}, \qquad h=\frac{\epsilon a^2}{16\kappa}. $$
Start with $x=b$. Every coordinate is positive, and
$$ \sum_i b_i\leq\delta\kappa. $$
Any coordinate below capacity starts at $a$ and remains at least $a$.

While $\sum_i x_i<\kappa$, do the following:

  • For each $i$ with $x_i<\mu_i$, define

$$ r_i=\min\left\{h,\mu_i-x_i,\kappa-\sum_jx_j\right\}. $$
This length is strictly positive.

  • Evaluate $F(x+r_ie_i)$ and $F(x)$, each within error $\epsilon r_i/64$, using the value oracle. Form the rational estimate

$$ \widehat D_i= \frac{V(x+r_ie_i)-V(x)}{r_i}. $$

  • Choose a coordinate maximizing $\widehat D_i$ and replace $x$ by $x+r_ie_i$.

Every probe segment and every update lies in $K$. An available coordinate always exists while the budget is unfilled, since total capacity is $1$.

Each iteration either adds mass $h$, saturates a coordinate, or finishes the budget. Therefore the number of iterations is at most
$$ \left\lceil\frac{\kappa}{h}\right\rceil+\tau+1 = \left\lceil\frac{256\tau^2}{\epsilon^3}\right\rceil+\tau+1. $$

6. Approximation guarantee

Throughout any probe segment, its changing coordinate is at least $a$, and the segment lies in $K$. Section 3 therefore gives the valid uniform bound
$$ 0\leq-\partial_{ii}\Phi \leq\frac{2F}{a^2} \leq\frac{2\mathrm{OPT}}{a^2}. $$

Let
$$ D_i=\frac{F(x+r_ie_i)-F(x)}{r_i} $$
be the exact finite-difference quotient. Integrating the curvature bound yields
$$ 0\leq\partial_i\Phi(x)-D_i \leq\frac{\mathrm{OPT}\,r_i}{a^2} \leq\frac{\epsilon\,\mathrm{OPT}}{16\kappa}. $$
The two value errors contribute at most $\epsilon/32$ to the quotient. Since $\mathrm{OPT}\geq\kappa$,
$$ |\widehat D_i-\partial_i\Phi(x)| \leq \frac{3\epsilon\,\mathrm{OPT}}{32\kappa}. $$

If the algorithm chooses coordinate $i$, comparison with a coordinate of largest marginal derivative gives
$$ \partial_i\Phi(x) \geq \max_{j:x_j<\mu_j}\partial_j\Phi(x) -\frac{3\epsilon\,\mathrm{OPT}}{16\kappa}. $$

Write $d=r_i$ for the update length. A second integration of the curvature bound gives
$$ F(x+de_i) \geq F(x)+d\partial_i\Phi(x) -\frac{\mathrm{OPT}\,d^2}{a^2}. $$
Combining this with the optimality-gap inequality from Section 3, and using $d\leq h$, gives
$$ F(x+de_i) \geq F(x)+\frac d\kappa\bigl(\mathrm{OPT}-F(x)\bigr) -\frac{\epsilon\,\mathrm{OPT}}{4\kappa}d. $$
Indeed, the marginal-estimation loss has coefficient $3\epsilon/16$, and the quadratic loss has coefficient at most $\epsilon/16$.

Set
$$ A_0=(1-\epsilon/4)\mathrm{OPT}. $$
For the successive update lengths $d_1,\ldots,d_q$, the preceding inequality is
$$ F(x^{(\ell)}) \geq \left(1-\frac{d_\ell}{\kappa}\right)F(x^{(\ell-1)}) +\frac{d_\ell}{\kappa}A_0. $$
All coefficients are nonnegative. Iteration and $F(b)\geq0$ therefore yield
$$ F(x^{(q)}) \geq A_0\left( 1-\prod_{\ell=1}^{q}\left(1-\frac{d_\ell}{\kappa}\right) \right). $$

The algorithm fills the budget exactly, so
$$ \sum_{\ell=1}^{q}d_\ell = \kappa-\sum_i b_i \geq(1-\delta)\kappa. $$
Using $1-z\leq e^{-z}$ gives
$$ F(x^{(q)}) \geq (1-\epsilon/4)\bigl(1-e^{-(1-\delta)}\bigr)\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Finally,
$$ 1-e^{-(1-\delta)}\geq1-1/e-\delta. $$
To verify this explicitly, write
$$ e^{-(1-\delta)}-e^{-1} = \int_0^\delta e^{-1+t}\,dt\leq\delta, $$
because $\delta<1$.

With $\delta=\epsilon/4$, it follows that
$$ F(x^{(q)}) \geq (1-1/e-\epsilon/2)\mathrm{OPT} \geq (1-1/e-\epsilon)\mathrm{OPT}. $$

There are polynomially many iterations, each requiring at most $2\tau$ value-oracle calls.

The rationals $a,b_i,h$ have polynomial encoding length. A common denominator for these numbers, the capacities, and the budget can be chosen with polynomial encoding length.
