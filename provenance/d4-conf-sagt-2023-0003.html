The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(J\) of voucher categories with rational costs \(c_j\), a rational per-capita supply vector \(b\) with \(\sum_{j\in J} b_j=1\), and a rational population distribution \(\mu\) over a finite support of approval types, define \(u_t(z)=\sum_{j\in t}c_jz_j\) for integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{J}\). For each type \(t\), let \(M_t(b,c)\) be the supremum of \(v\) such that some distribution \(\pi\) over integral bundles has \(\mathbb E_\pi[z]=b\) and \(\pi(u_t(z)\ge v)=1\). Does there exist a direct mechanism, polynomial in the input bit length, that outputs polynomially represented bundle lotteries \((\pi_r^\mu)_{r\in\mathcal T}\) whose aggregate use is \(b\), gives every truthful type \(t\) its benchmark \(M_t(b,c)\) with probability one, is Pareto efficient among feasible measurable assignments, and satisfies \(\mathbb E_{\pi_t^\mu}[u_t(z)]\ge\mathbb E_{\pi_r^\mu}[u_t(z)]\) for every pair of types \(t,r\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(J\) be a nonempty set of \(m\) voucher categories, with public costs \(c_j\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) and supply \(b\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{J}\), where \(\sum_j b_j=1\). The approval and report domain is \(\mathcal T=2^{\{j:c_j>0\}}\), including the empty type; bundles are \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{J}\), with utility \(u_t(z)=\sum_{j\in t}c_jz_j\).
A society is given by its support \(S\subseteq\mathcal T\): the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Define \(M_t(b,c)=\sup\{v\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}:\exists\pi,\ \mathbb E_\pi[z]=b,\ \Pr_\pi[u_t(z)\ge v]=1\}\), allowing arbitrary probability distributions on integral bundles with finite first moments.
A direct mechanism provides a lottery \(\pi_r^\mu\) for every report \(r\in\mathcal T\). Its representation is a succinct menu evaluator that, given a report, returns a finite list of integral bundles and rational probabilities; description size, list size, and numerical bit lengths must be polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
Require \(\sum_{t\in S}\mu_t\mathbb E_{\pi_t^\mu}[z]=b\), \(\Pr_{\pi_t^\mu}[u_t(z)\ge M_t(b,c)]=1\) for every \(t\in\mathcal T\), and \(\mathbb E_{\pi_t^\mu}[u_t(z)]\ge\mathbb E_{\pi_r^\mu}[u_t(z)]\) for every \(t,r\in\mathcal T\). An individual deviation leaves \(\mu\) fixed.
Model the population as an atomless probability space with type masses \(\mu\). Require Pareto efficiency in expected utility against all measurable assignments of bundle lotteries with aggregate expected supply \(b\): no alternative weakly improves almost everyone's utility and strictly improves it on a set of positive population mass.

Is there a direct mechanism satisfying these requirements for every input, with construction and report evaluation polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the associated feasibility/construction problem NP-hard?

For finite societies with \(n\) agents and \(n+2\) goods, a polynomial-time deterministic strategyproof mechanism produces a Pareto-efficient MMS allocation; with two agents and five goods, such a deterministic mechanism need not exist. [Botan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13171v1)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Costs and supplies are nonnegative binary rationals; population mass is normalized to one.
  • Approval reports range over all subsets of positive-cost categories, including the empty set, matching approval as strictly positive singleton utility.
  • Benchmark and incentive requirements quantify over the entire type domain, including types outside the population support; only positive-mass types consume aggregate supply.
  • Polynomial representation means a succinct report evaluator returning explicit finite rational lotteries, avoiding enumeration of the exponentially large report domain.
  • Pareto efficiency compares expected individual utilities against arbitrary measurable lottery assignments, with strict improvement required on positive population mass.
  • Aggregate feasibility means equality of expected category use; individual deviations leave the atomless population distribution fixed.
  • The graded question's unit per-capita supply and unbounded integral category bundles are retained. No finite agent count or additional relation between category count and population mass is imposed.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

A polynomial-time mechanism exists

The distributional problem as stated is polynomial-time solvable. The construction below satisfies aggregate feasibility, the almost-sure benchmark guarantee for every type, strategyproofness for every pair of reports, and Pareto efficiency against arbitrary measurable lottery assignments.

The construction uses two features of the stated model: total expected supply is normalized to \(1\), and an individual deviation leaves \(\mu\) fixed.

1. Exact characterization of the benchmark

Let
\[ B=\{j\in J:b_j>0\}. \]
Because \(\sum_j b_j=1\), the set \(B\) is nonempty.

Lemma 1. For every \(t\in\mathcal T\),
\[ M_t(b,c)= \begin{cases} \min_{j\in B}c_j,&B\subseteq t,\\ 0,&B\not\subseteq t. \end{cases} \]
Both values are attained.

Proof. Consider any integral-bundle lottery with mean \(b\), and write
\[ N_t(z)=\sum_{j\in t}z_j. \]
If it guarantees a strictly positive utility, then \(N_t(z)\ge 1\) almost surely. Consequently,
\[ 1\le \mathbb E[N_t(z)] =\sum_{j\in t}b_j \le 1. \]
Equality forces \(B\subseteq t\). Thus, when \(B\not\subseteq t\), no strictly positive guarantee is possible. The guarantee \(0\) is attained, for example, by giving the singleton bundle \(e_j\) with probability \(b_j\).

Suppose now that \(B\subseteq t\). Every category in \(B\) has positive cost, since reports contain only positive-cost categories. The singleton lottery just described guarantees
\[ a=\min_{j\in B}c_j>0. \]

Conversely, any lottery guaranteeing a positive utility satisfies
\[ \sum_j z_j\ge 1 \quad\text{almost surely}, \qquad \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_j z_j\right]=1. \]
Hence \(\sum_j z_j=1\) almost surely. Moreover, \(z_j=0\) almost surely whenever \(b_j=0\). The lottery must therefore consist of singleton bundles, with probability \(b_j\) on \(e_j\). Every supplied category occurs with positive probability, so its guaranteed utility cannot exceed \(a\). This proves the formula and attainment. \(\square\)

Thus the nonzero benchmark requires an almost-surely nonempty bundle, consisting of supplied categories.

2. Constructing a fixed depletion schedule

Define the categories that some positive-mass type values:
\[ G=\{j\in J:b_j>0,\ c_j>0,\ \text{and }j\in s \text{ for some }s\in S\}. \]
Let
\[ q=\sum_{j\in G}b_j, \qquad d_j= \begin{cases} 0,&j\in G,\\ b_j,&j\notin G. \end{cases} \]
The vector \(d\) will be included in the expected bundle of every report. No supported type derives utility from its positive coordinates.

Fix a public ordering of categories to break cost ties. For a report \(r\) and a set \(A\subseteq G\), let \(i_r(A)\) be a category of maximum cost in \(r\cap A\), using that ordering. If \(r\cap A=\varnothing\), set \(i_r(A)=\bot\), and define \(e_\bot=0\).

Construct a schedule using only the fixed society \(\mu\):

  • Initially, category \(j\in G\) has residual supply \(b_j\).
  • Let \(A\) be the categories with positive residual supply. Each supported type \(s\) consumes \(i_s(A)\) at unit speed, or waits if \(i_s(A)=\bot\).
  • The aggregate consumption rate of category \(j\in A\) is

\[ \rho_j=\sum_{\substack{s\in S\\i_s(A)=j}}\mu_s. \]

  • If the residual supply is \(R_j\), run this phase for

\[ h=\min_{\substack{j\in A\\\rho_j>0}}\frac{R_j}{\rho_j}. \]
Subtract \(h\rho_j\) from each residual supply and repeat.

Whenever \(A\ne\varnothing\), some consumption rate is positive: every remaining category has a supported approver, and that type selects some remaining approved category. Each phase exhausts at least one category. Thus the schedule ends after \(K\le m\) phases.

Write its available sets and durations as
\[ (A_1,h_1),\ldots,(A_K,h_K), \qquad T=\sum_{k=1}^K h_k. \]
When \(G=\varnothing\), take \(K=0\) and \(T=0\).

In phase \(k\), the total consumption rate is
\[ \lambda_k =\sum_{\substack{s\in S\\s\cap A_k\ne\varnothing}}\mu_s \le 1. \]
Since the schedule consumes exactly the supply in \(G\),
\[ q=\sum_{k=1}^K h_k\lambda_k \le \sum_{k=1}^K h_k=T. \tag{1} \]

For every report \(r\in\mathcal T\), define its expected bundle by
\[ x_r=d+\sum_{k=1}^K h_k e_{i_r(A_k)}. \tag{2} \]
This evaluates the report against the already constructed schedule. The schedule is not recomputed after an individual deviation.

3. Aggregate feasibility and strategyproofness

Lemma 2. The expected bundles in (2) satisfy aggregate feasibility and all expected-utility incentive constraints.

Proof of feasibility. For \(j\in G\), the schedule exhausts exactly \(b_j\), so
\[ \sum_{s\in S}\mu_s x_{s,j} =\sum_{k=1}^K h_k \sum_{\substack{s\in S\\i_s(A_k)=j}}\mu_s =b_j. \]
For \(j\notin G\), every report receives expected quantity \(d_j=b_j\), and the masses sum to \(1\). Hence
\[ \sum_{s\in S}\mu_s x_s=b. \]

Proof of strategyproofness. Fix arbitrary \(t,r\in\mathcal T\). Extend the utility formula linearly to nonnegative real vectors.

In each phase \(k\), truthful selection achieves
\[ u_t(e_{i_t(A_k)}) =\max\bigl(\{c_j:j\in t\cap A_k\}\cup\{0\}\bigr). \]
Any category selected by report \(r\) either belongs to \(t\cap A_k\), or gives type \(t\) utility zero. Therefore
\[ u_t(e_{i_t(A_k)}) \ge u_t(e_{i_r(A_k)}). \]
The common vector \(d\) contributes the same utility under both reports. Multiplying the phase inequalities by \(h_k\) and summing gives
\[ u_t(x_t)\ge u_t(x_r). \tag{3} \]
This holds for every pair of types, including types outside \(S\). \(\square\)

4. Finite lotteries preserving nonemptiness

The following rounding construction implements every expected bundle while controlling the total number of vouchers.

Lemma 3. Every rational vector \(x\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^J\) has a rational lottery on at most \(m+1\) integral bundles with mean \(x\). Every bundle in its support has total size at least
\[ \left\lfloor\sum_j x_j\right\rfloor. \]

Proof. Enumerate \(J\) as \(1,\ldots,m\), and set
\[ s_0=0, \qquad s_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^j x_\ell. \]
For a uniform random variable \(\alpha\in[0,1)\), define
\[ z_j(\alpha) =\lfloor s_j+\alpha\rfloor -\lfloor s_{j-1}+\alpha\rfloor. \tag{4} \]
These differences are nonnegative integers because the cumulative sums are nondecreasing.

For every nonnegative real \(a\),
\[ \mathbb E[\lfloor a+\alpha\rfloor]=a: \]
writing \(a=n+f\), where \(n\) is integral and \(0\le f<1\), the floor equals \(n+1\) with probability \(f\) and \(n\) otherwise. Consequently,
\[ \mathbb E[z_j(\alpha)]=s_j-s_{j-1}=x_j. \]
Telescoping also gives
\[ \sum_j z_j(\alpha)=\lfloor s_m+\alpha\rfloor \ge \lfloor s_m\rfloor. \tag{5} \]

The bundle changes only at the points
\[ 1-\{s_j\} \quad\text{with}\quad \{s_j\}>0, \]
where \(\{s_j\}\) denotes the fractional part. There are at most \(m\) such points. Partitioning \([0,1)\) at them gives at most \(m+1\) half-open intervals. Output the constant bundle on each interval with probability equal to its length. All endpoints and probabilities are rational. \(\square\)

For report \(r\), let \(\pi_r^\mu\) be the finite lottery obtained by applying Lemma 3 to \(x_r\).

Its mean is exactly \(x_r\), so aggregate feasibility and (3) give the required feasibility and strategyproofness for these lotteries.

Lemma 4. Every report’s lottery satisfies its almost-sure benchmark guarantee.

Proof. When \(B\not\subseteq t\), Lemma 1 gives \(M_t(b,c)=0\), and the guarantee follows from nonnegative utility.

Suppose \(B\subseteq t\). Type \(t\) approves every category in every nonempty \(A_k\), so it consumes at unit speed throughout the schedule. Therefore
\[ \sum_j x_{t,j} =\sum_j d_j+T =1-q+T \ge 1, \]
by (1).

Furthermore, \(x_{t,j}=0\) outside \(B\). Formula (4) then gives \(z_j=0\) outside \(B\) in every output bundle. By (5), every output bundle is nonempty. Each contains at least one category in \(B\), and therefore
\[ u_t(z)\ge \min_{j\in B}c_j=M_t(b,c). \]
Thus the benchmark is satisfied in every outcome of positive probability. \(\square\)

5. Pareto efficiency on the atomless population

Let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,P)\) be the population probability space, and let \(t(\omega)\) denote an individual’s type. The set of individuals whose type lies outside \(S\) has measure zero.

Define
\[ W=\sum_{j\in G}c_jb_j. \]

Lemma 5. Every measurable alternative lottery assignment with aggregate expected supply \(b\) has total expected utility at most \(W\). The constructed assignment attains \(W\).

Proof. For an alternative assignment, let \(y_j(\omega)\) be individual \(\omega\)’s expected quantity of category \(j\). Aggregate feasibility says
\[ \int_\Omega y_j(\omega)\,dP(\omega)=b_j. \]
By nonnegativity and finite aggregate supply, these quantities and the corresponding utilities are integrable.

Total expected utility is
\[ \int_\Omega \sum_{j\in t(\omega)}c_jy_j(\omega)\,dP(\omega) = \sum_j c_j \int_{\{\omega:j\in t(\omega)\}} y_j(\omega)\,dP(\omega). \tag{6} \]
For \(j\in G\), its contribution is at most \(c_jb_j\). For \(j\notin G\), its contribution is zero: either its cost is zero, its supply is zero, or its set of approvers has population measure zero. Thus (6) is at most \(W\).

In the constructed assignment, every unit consumed from \(G\) is assigned to a supported type that approves it. The schedule consumes all of \(b_j\) for each \(j\in G\). Hence its total expected utility is exactly \(W\). \(\square\)

If an alternative weakly improved almost everyone and strictly improved a set of positive measure, the utility difference would be an integrable, nonnegative function positive on a positive-measure set. Its integral would be strictly positive. That would make the alternative’s total utility exceed \(W\), contradicting Lemma 5.

Therefore the constructed assignment is Pareto efficient against the full class of measurable alternatives required in the question.

6. Polynomial construction and report evaluation

Only the listed support types are used to construct the schedule. Each of at most \(m\) phases requires finding their preferred remaining categories, summing rates, and updating at most \(m\) residual supplies. The number of rational operations is polynomial in \(m\) and \(\tau\).

It remains to verify polynomial numerical bit lengths.

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses, and let \(E\) be the product of the denominators of the input supplies. Both have bit length bounded by \(O(L)\). Every phase rate has the form
\[ \rho_j=\frac{a_j}{D}, \qquad 0\le a_j\le D, \]
with integral \(a_j\).

Initially, all residual supplies have common denominator \(E\). Suppose a phase’s residual supplies have common denominator \(EP\), and category \(\ell\), with positive rate \(a_\ell/D\), determines the phase duration. Writing its residual numerator as \(N_\ell\), the duration is
\[ h=\frac{DN_\ell}{EPa_\ell}. \]
If category \(j\) has residual numerator \(N_j\), its updated residual is
\[ \frac{N_ja_\ell-a_jN_\ell}{EPa_\ell}. \]
Thus each phase adds at most one factor bounded by \(D\) to the common denominator. After at most \(m\) phases, all durations and expected bundles have a common denominator dividing
\[ Q=E\prod_{k=1}^K a_{\ell_k}, \]
whose bit length is \(O((m+1)L)\).

Numerators also have polynomial bit length. Let
\[ \mu_{\min}=\min_{s\in S}\mu_s. \]
Every nonempty phase has at least one active supported type, so \(\lambda_k\ge\mu_{\min}\). Consequently,
\[ q=\sum_k h_k\lambda_k \ge \mu_{\min}T, \qquad T\le \frac{1}{\mu_{\min}}. \]
The reciprocal of \(\mu_{\min}\) has polynomial bit length, and every report’s total expected quantity is at most \(T+1\).

The menu evaluator stores the costs, tie order, vector \(d\), and at most \(m\) pairs \((A_k,h_k)\). Given a report, it:

  • computes \(x_r\) from (2);
  • forms its cumulative sums;
  • constructs the at most \(m+1\) rational intervals from Lemma 3;
  • returns their integral bundles and rational probabilities.

All these operations, the menu description, the output list, and all numerical representations have size polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). No enumeration of the full report domain is needed.

7. Conclusion

The construction gives a qualifying direct mechanism for every input. The benchmark is attained almost surely for every possible type, all reports satisfy the incentive inequalities with \(\mu\) fixed, and the supported population receives a Pareto-efficient allocation. Construction and report evaluation are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
