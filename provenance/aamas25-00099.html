1. Pareto optimality depends only on positive support

Let a feasible law have positive support
$$ S=\{(\theta_i,b_i):1\leq i\leq s\}, $$
with masses $y_i>0$. For each source $i$, define
$$ C_i=\{c\in B_K:u_{\theta_i}(c)\geq u_{\theta_i}(b_i)\}. $$
For $c\in C_i$, let
$$ d_{i,c}=c-b_i, \qquad a_{i,c}=\mathbf 1_{\{u_{\theta_i}(c)>u_{\theta_i}(b_i)\}}. $$

Consider the finite linear program
$$ \max \sum_{i,c\in C_i}a_{i,c}\lambda_{i,c} $$
subject to
$$ \lambda_{i,c}\geq0, \qquad \sum_{i,c\in C_i}\lambda_{i,c}=1, \qquad \sum_{i,c\in C_i}d_{i,c}\lambda_{i,c}=0. $$
It is feasible because diagonal transitions $c=b_i$ are permitted.

Lemma 1. The supplied law is population Pareto optimal if and only if this linear program has optimum zero.

Proof. Suppose first that a Pareto-improving reassignment exists. Write its entries as $z_{i,c}$. The source-mass equations imply
$$ \sum_{i,c}z_{i,c}=\sum_i y_i=1. $$
Supply preservation implies
$$ \sum_{i,c}(c-b_i)z_{i,c}=0. $$
Every positive entry is weakly improving, and strictly improving entries have positive total mass. Thus $\lambda_{i,c}=z_{i,c}$ is feasible and has positive objective.

Conversely, suppose a feasible $\lambda$ has positive objective. Put
$$ o_i=\sum_c\lambda_{i,c}. $$
Choose $\delta>0$ sufficiently small that $\delta o_i\leq y_i$ for every $i$. Define
$$ z_{i,c} = \delta\lambda_{i,c} + (y_i-\delta o_i)\mathbf 1_{\{c=b_i\}}. $$
These entries are nonnegative and have source totals $y_i$. Their total supply change is
$$ \delta\sum_{i,c}(c-b_i)\lambda_{i,c}=0. $$
All positive transitions are weakly improving, and the strictly improving transitions have positive total mass. This is a Pareto improvement. $\square$

In particular, Pareto optimality depends only on the positive support, rather than on its positive masses. Deleting support entries preserves Pareto optimality whenever the remaining entries support a feasible law. Deleting entries also preserves support-wise EF1.

Short certificates of nonoptimality

The normalized transition polytope above is compact. Its objective therefore attains a maximum at a vertex.

At a vertex, the columns corresponding to positive variables are linearly independent. Otherwise, a nonzero dependence would give sufficiently small feasible perturbations in both directions, contradicting vertexhood. There are only $m+1$ equality rows, so a vertex has at most $m+1$ positive transitions.

The equality coefficients are integers of absolute value at most $K$, except for the normalization coefficients, which are $1$. Choosing independent rows and applying Cramer's rule gives rational coefficients of bit length
$$ O\!\left((m+1)\bigl(\log(K+1)+\log(m+2)\bigr)\right). $$
For example, the denominator bound follows from Hadamard's determinant bound on a square submatrix of order at most $m+1$.

Consequently, non-Pareto-optimality has a polynomial-size certificate consisting of at most $m+1$ transitions and their rational coefficients. One checks admissibility, weak utility improvement, normalization, zero supply change, and positive coefficient on strict transitions using exact rational arithmetic.

Thus checking Pareto optimality of an explicitly supplied law belongs to $\mathrm{coNP}$.

2. A hardness construction for verification

The starting hardness fact is NP-completeness of positive-integer SUBSET SUM: given positive integers $s_1,\ldots,s_r,S$, determine whether
$$ \sum_{i=1}^r s_i x_i=S, \qquad x_i\in\{0,1\}. $$
This is the standard exact-sum problem in [Karp's NP-completeness results](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

We first encode it as an integer-sum equation whose nonnegative integer solutions automatically have bounded multiplicities.

2.1 An encoding that prevents repeated choices

Let $s_{\max}=\max_i s_i$ and choose the integer
$$ B=2+r+r s_{\max}+S. $$
Then
$$ B>r+1,\qquad B>r s_{\max},\qquad B>S. $$
Put $J=B^{r+1}$. For each $i$, introduce two denominations
$$ a_{i,0}=J+B^i, \qquad a_{i,1}=J+B^i+s_i. $$
The target is
$$ T=rJ+\sum_{i=1}^r B^i+S. $$

Lemma 2. Every nonnegative integer solution of
$$ \sum_{i=1}^r \bigl(a_{i,0}q_{i,0}+a_{i,1}q_{i,1}\bigr)=T $$
satisfies
$$ q_{i,0}+q_{i,1}=1 \quad\text{for every }i, \qquad \sum_i s_iq_{i,1}=S. $$
Conversely, every SUBSET SUM solution gives such an integer solution.

Proof. Let
$$ \ell_{\max}=B^r+s_{\max}, \qquad U=\sum_{i=1}^r B^i+S. $$
The choice of $B$ gives
$$ r\ell_{\max} < rB^r+B \leq (r+1)B^r < B^{r+1}=J, $$
and likewise
$$ U<rB^r+B\leq(r+1)B^r<J. $$

Every denomination lies between $J$ and $J+\ell_{\max}$. If at most $r-1$ coins were used, their sum would be less than $rJ$. If at least $r+1$ coins were used, their sum would exceed
$$ rJ+U=T. $$
Hence exactly $r$ coins are used.

Subtracting their common contribution $rJ$ leaves an equality of lower parts. The coefficient of every positional digit $B^i$ is at most $r<B$, while the sum in the units position is at most $r s_{\max}<B$. Thus no carries occur. Uniqueness of base-$B$ representation forces every positional coefficient to equal $1$, and the units sum to equal $S$. These are precisely the asserted equations.

The converse follows by choosing denomination $a_{i,1}$ when $x_i=1$ and $a_{i,0}$ when $x_i=0$. $\square$

List the resulting $n=2r$ denominations as $a_1,\ldots,a_n$. We have proved that
$$ \sum_{i=1}^n a_iq_i=T,\qquad q_i\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}, $$
is solvable exactly when the original SUBSET SUM instance is solvable. Moreover, every solution has $q_i\in\{0,1\}$ and uses exactly $r$ coins.

All these integers have polynomial binary encoding length: their bit lengths are $O(r\log B)$.

2.2 The society and supplied law

Introduce $n+5$ classes:
$$ v,h,d,c,f,g_1,\ldots,g_n. $$
There are two types, $\alpha$ and $\beta$, with
$$ \mu_\alpha=\mu_\beta=\frac12, $$
and masks
$$ A_\alpha=\{v,h,d,f,g_1,\ldots,g_n\}, $$
$$ A_\beta=\{v,h,d,c,g_1,\ldots,g_n\}. $$
Thus $\alpha$ excludes only $c$, and $\beta$ excludes only $f$. Every class belongs to a mask.

Set
$$ K=n+3,\qquad D=K-2,\qquad M=4K,\qquad H=MT-D. $$
Because $T\geq1$ and $D<K$, we have
$$ H>\frac{MT}{2}>0. $$
Define
$$ P_{\max}=\max\{H,D,1,Ma_1,\ldots,Ma_n\}, \qquad V=KP_{\max}+1, $$
and choose the positive dyadic rational
$$ \varepsilon=2^{-\lceil\log_2(K+1)\rceil}. $$
In particular,
$$ K\varepsilon<1. $$

The inherent values are:

| Class | Inherent value |
|---|---:|
| $v$ | $V$ |
| $h$ | $H$ |
| $d$ | $1$ |
| $c$ | $D$ |
| $f$ | $\varepsilon$ |
| $g_i$ | $Ma_i$ |

For the proof, introduce an auxiliary integer-valued linear function
$$ p(b)=Vb_v+Hb_h+b_d+Db_c+\sum_i Ma_i b_{g_i}. $$
The bonus class $f$ has coefficient zero in this function. The utilities satisfy
$$ u_\alpha(b)=p(b)-Db_c+\varepsilon b_f, \qquad u_\beta(b)=p(b). $$

The supplied law has three positive entries:
$$ b^\alpha=e_v+e_h+D e_d, \qquad y_{\alpha,b^\alpha}=\frac12, $$
$$ b^I=e_v+\sum_i e_{g_i}, \qquad y_{\beta,b^I}=\frac14, $$
$$ b^C=e_v+e_c+e_f, \qquad y_{\beta,b^C}=\frac14. $$
Their sizes are $K$, $K-2$, and $3$, respectively.

Define the supplies from this law:
$$ \rho_v=1,\quad \rho_h=\frac12,\quad \rho_d=\frac D2,\quad \rho_c=\rho_f=\frac14,\quad \rho_{g_i}=\frac14. $$
They are positive dyadic rationals, and the law is feasible by construction.

Here
$$ m=n+5,\qquad \tau=2,\qquad K=m-2,\qquad t\leq m. $$
The construction, including the supplied law, has polynomial binary encoding length and is computable in polynomial time.

2.3 The supplied law is support-wise EF1

Every support bundle contains exactly one unit of $v$, which both types like. Consequently, every recipient has utility at least $V$.

After removing that unit from any support bundle, either type's evaluation is at most
$$ KP_{\max}+K\varepsilon < KP_{\max}+1 = V. $$
Thus, for every ordered pair of support entries, removing the common class $v$ from the compared bundle eliminates envy. The supplied law is support-wise EF1.

2.4 A Pareto improvement forces an exact integer sum

Suppose a Pareto-improving reassignment exists.

For a transition from the $\alpha$ source, weak improvement implies
$$ p(c)-Dc_c+\varepsilon c_f \geq V+MT. $$
Since $c_f\leq K$ and $K\varepsilon<1$, this gives
$$ p(c)>V+MT-1. $$
The function $p$ is integer-valued on bundles, so
$$ p(c)\geq V+MT=p(b^\alpha). $$

For either $\beta$ source, weak improvement directly implies
$$ p(c)\geq p(b). $$
Supply preservation gives
$$ \sum_{\theta,b,c}z_{\theta,b,c}\bigl(p(c)-p(b)\bigr)=0. $$
Every summand with positive mass has a nonnegative price change. Therefore every positive transition preserves $p$ exactly.

A $\beta$ transition consequently preserves utility exactly. Hence some positive $\alpha$ transition must be strict. For its destination $c$,
$$ p(c)=V+MT, \qquad -Dc_c+\varepsilon c_f>0. $$
Because $\varepsilon c_f<1\leq D$, these conditions force
$$ c_c=0,\qquad c_f\geq1. $$

We now classify such a destination.

First,
$$ MT=H+D\leq2P_{\max}<V. $$
A bundle without $v$ has price at most $KP_{\max}<V$, while a bundle containing at least two units of $v$ has price at least $2V>V+MT$. Thus
$$ c_v=1. $$
Its remaining price must equal $MT$.

Since $2H>MT$, it contains at most one unit of $h$. If $c_h=1$, the remaining price is $D$. Every ordinary class costs at least $M>D$, and $c_c=0$, so this remaining price requires exactly $D$ filler units. Together with $v$, $h$, and at least one bonus unit, that would give size at least
$$ 1+1+D+1=K+1, $$
which violates the cap. Therefore $c_h=0$.

The price equation is now
$$ M\sum_i a_i c_{g_i}+c_d=MT. $$
Because $0\leq c_d\leq K<M$, reducing modulo $M$ forces $c_d=0$. Hence
$$ \sum_i a_i c_{g_i}=T. $$
Lemma 2 turns this into a solution of the original SUBSET SUM instance.

This necessity argument used only nonnegativity and supply conservation of the real reassignment masses. Fractional population splitting therefore cannot circumvent the integer equation.

2.5 An exact integer sum gives a Pareto improvement

Conversely, suppose
$$ \sum_i a_iq_i=T $$
has a solution. Lemma 2 guarantees $q_i\in\{0,1\}$.

Use these destinations:
$$ c^\alpha=e_v+\sum_iq_i e_{g_i}+e_f, $$
$$ c^I=e_v+\sum_i(1-q_i)e_{g_i}+e_h+e_c, $$
$$ c^C=e_v+D e_d. $$
They respect the cap because
$$ |c^\alpha|=2+\sum_iq_i\leq n+2<K, $$
$$ |c^I|=3+n-\sum_iq_i\leq n+3=K, \qquad |c^C|=D+1=K-1. $$

Type $\alpha$ strictly gains:
$$ u_\alpha(c^\alpha)=V+MT+\varepsilon > V+MT=u_\alpha(b^\alpha). $$
The two $\beta$ sources preserve utility:
$$ u_\beta(c^I) = V+M\sum_i a_i-MT+H+D = u_\beta(b^I), $$
$$ u_\beta(c^C)=V+D=u_\beta(b^C). $$
Finally,
$$ b^\alpha+b^I+b^C=c^\alpha+c^I+c^C. $$
Reassigning mass $1/4$ from each of the three sources to its corresponding destination therefore preserves every supply. Leave the remaining mass $1/4$ at $b^\alpha$.

This is a Pareto improvement.

Combining both directions, the supplied law is non-Pareto-optimal exactly when the original SUBSET SUM instance is solvable. Nonoptimality is therefore NP-hard, even under all the stated restrictions. Section 1 proves its membership in NP.

Theorem 3. Checking population Pareto optimality of a supplied feasible, support-wise EF1 law is $\mathrm{coNP}$-complete, even with $\tau=2$, three positive support entries, and $K=m-2$.

3. Why this does not prove hardness of the requested construction

Every society in the preceding reduction has a simple valid law, independently of the SUBSET SUM answer.

Starting from the supplied law, exchange one filler unit for the bonus unit on equal masses $1/4$:
$$ b^{\alpha\prime}=b^\alpha-e_d+e_f, \qquad b^{C\prime}=b^C-e_f+e_d. $$
The new law gives mass $1/4$ to each of
$$ (\alpha,b^\alpha),\quad (\alpha,b^{\alpha\prime}),\quad (\beta,b^I),\quad (\beta,b^{C\prime}). $$
All bundles respect the cap, and the exchange preserves supplies.

Every bundle still contains exactly one unit of $v$. The argument in Section 2.3 therefore proves support-wise EF1.

For any feasible law $y$, its aggregate utility is
$$ \sum_{\theta,b}y_{\theta,b}u_\theta(b) = \sum_g p(e_g)\rho_g - D\sum_b y_{\alpha,b}b_c + \varepsilon\sum_b y_{\alpha,b}b_f. $$
Consequently,
$$ \sum_{\theta,b}y_{\theta,b}u_\theta(b) \leq \sum_g p(e_g)\rho_g+\varepsilon\rho_f. $$
The modified law attains this upper bound: all bonus supply goes to $\alpha$, and no currency supply goes to $\alpha$.

It therefore maximizes aggregate utility over all feasible real laws. A population Pareto improvement would strictly increase aggregate utility, contradicting this maximality. The modified law is population Pareto optimal.

Thus the reduction establishes a verification barrier, while its underlying construction instances are all yes-instances. It cannot serve as a hardness reduction for existence.

4. Small valid laws and polynomial encoding length

Theorem 4. Every yes-instance has a valid law with at most $m+\tau$ positive entries and polynomial binary encoding length.

Proof. Start with a valid law and restrict attention to its positive support $S$. The mass and supply equations have the form
$$ Ay=h,\qquad y\geq0, $$
with $m+\tau$ rows. This polytope is nonempty and bounded because each variable is bounded by its type's mass.

Choose a vertex. Its positive columns must be linearly independent: a dependence would permit sufficiently small feasible perturbations in both directions. Hence at most
$$ \operatorname{rank}(A)\leq m+\tau $$
entries are positive.

Deleting the other entries preserves both EF1 and Pareto optimality by Section 1.

Let $d=m+\tau$. The matrix coefficients are integers bounded by $K$. Clearing a common denominator of the input vector $h$ costs $O(L)$ bits. Selecting independent rows and applying Cramer's rule and Hadamard's determinant bound shows that each positive mass has bit length
$$ O\!\left( L+d\bigl(\log(K+1)+\log(d+1)\bigr) \right). $$
Each bundle requires $O(m\log(K+1))$ bits. The complete law therefore has polynomial encoding length. $\square$

Explicit vertex selection

The minor vertex-selection issue identified in the notes has a direct repair. For a nonempty bounded rational polytope with an explicit inequality description, successively minimize its first coordinate, then its second coordinate over the preceding optimal face, and so on. After all coordinates have been fixed, the remaining face is a singleton.

That singleton is a vertex: if it were the midpoint of distinct feasible points, optimality of the first coordinate would force both points into the first optimal face; repeating coordinate by coordinate would force both to equal the singleton.

This uses one exact rational LP per coordinate. Each intermediate set is a face of the original polytope, so its coordinate optimum occurs at an original vertex. Standard determinant bounds therefore keep the added optimal-value equalities at polynomial bit length. The same procedure applies to the minimal face containing a specified point, obtained by imposing its tight inequalities as equalities.

5. A finite exact algorithm and an existence upper bound

Write
$$ R=\sum_g\rho_g. $$
Every feasible law satisfies
$$ R=\sum_{\theta,b}|b|y_{\theta,b}\leq K. $$
Conversely, when $R\leq K$, feasibility alone is witnessed by
$$ y_{\theta,Ke_g}=\mu_\theta\frac{\rho_g}{K}, \qquad y_{\theta,0}=\mu_\theta\left(1-\frac RK\right). $$
These masses have the prescribed type totals and supplies. Thus $R>K$ is an immediate obstruction.

For the simultaneous requirements, let
$$ M_K=|B_K|=\binom{K+m}{m}, \qquad N=\tau M_K, \qquad d=m+\tau. $$

An exact algorithm is:

  • Enumerate every nonempty support $S\subseteq\Theta\times B_K$ of size at most $d$.
  • Check EF1 on every ordered pair in $S$.
  • Check Pareto optimality using the transition LP of Section 1.
  • Solve the mass and supply equations using nonnegative masses on $S$.
  • If feasible, output its positive entries; otherwise continue.

If no candidate succeeds, report that no valid law exists.

For step 2, EF1 is equivalent to
$$ u_\theta(b)\geq u_\theta(c)- \max\bigl(\{w_g:g\in A_\theta,\ c_g>0\}\cup\{0\}\bigr). $$
Thus each comparison is computable by exact rational arithmetic.

The transition LP has at most $dM_K$ variables. Both LPs have rational coefficients of polynomial bit length in $m,\tau,L,\log(K+1)$. Standard polynomial-time rational linear programming therefore gives the running-time bound
$$ (d+1)(\tau M_K)^d \operatorname{poly}\!\left( dM_K,m,\tau,L,\log(K+1) \right). $$
There is no additional dependence on $t$ beyond the values already encoded in $L$.

Correctness follows from Theorem 4: every yes-instance has a support among those enumerated. Conversely, every output is feasible and has both properties. Any zero entries produced by the feasibility LP can be deleted without losing either property.

Finally, existence belongs to $\Sigma_2^p$. Existentially guess the polynomial-size law guaranteed by Theorem 4, check feasibility and EF1, and universally exclude the polynomial-size nonoptimality certificates from Section 1.
