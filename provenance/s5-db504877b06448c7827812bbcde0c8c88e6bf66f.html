The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a matroid \(\mathcal M=(E,\mathcal I)\), a marked set \(S\subseteq E\), and a rational inventory \(\lambda\) in the base polytope with \(\sum_{e\in E}\lambda_e=r\), find a distribution \(\pi\) over integral bases \(B\in\mathcal B(\mathcal M)\) satisfying \(\sum_{B\ni e}\pi_B=\lambda_e\) for every \(e\), such that every \(B\) with \(\pi_B>0\) has \(|B\cap S|\in\{\lfloor\lambda(S)\rfloor,\lceil\lambda(S)\rceil\}\).

Result

The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), with polynomially many independence queries. Every promised inventory is feasible. The output can have at most \(\max\{1,\tau\}\) supported bases, with exact rational probabilities of polynomial encoding length.

I use the standard bit-complexity convention: rational numbers are represented by numerators and denominators encoded in binary.

1. Encode the quota by a partition matroid

For a matroid \(\mathcal N\), let \(P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal N)\) denote the convex hull of incidence vectors of its independent sets.

Write
\[ T=\{e\in E:\lambda_e>0\},\qquad s=\lambda(S),\qquad a=\lfloor s\rfloor,\qquad b=\lceil s\rceil. \]

Every basis occurring with positive probability in any marginal-preserving distribution must lie in \(T\): if it contained an element outside \(T\), that element would have positive marginal.

The promise implies that the restriction \(\mathcal M_T=\mathcal M|_T\) has rank \(r\). Indeed, express \(\lambda\) as a convex combination of bases, using the definition of the base polytope. Nonnegativity and the zero coordinates outside \(T\) force every positively weighted basis in this combination to lie in \(T\).

If \(r=0\), the promise forces \(\lambda=0\). Return the empty basis with probability one. Henceforth assume \(r>0\).

Define a partition matroid \(\mathcal N\) on \(T\) by declaring \(I\subseteq T\) independent precisely when
\[ |I\cap S|\le b,\qquad |I\setminus S|\le r-a. \]

These are nonnegative integer capacities because \(0\le s\le r\). Independence in \(\mathcal N\) is tested simply by counting elements in its two parts.

For any basis \(B\) of \(\mathcal M_T\), independence in \(\mathcal N\) is equivalent to the required quota:
\[ B\in\mathcal I(\mathcal N) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\le |B\cap S|\le b. \]
To justify the lower bound, use \(|B|=r\):
\[ |B\setminus S|\le r-a \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |B\cap S|\ge a. \]
Since \(b-a\le1\), the interval contains exactly the permitted integer values.

2. Prove that the inventory lies in the intersection polytope

The promise immediately gives
\[ \lambda|_T\in P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal M_T). \]

We also have
\[ \lambda|_T\in P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal N). \]
Here is an explicit verification of all rank inequalities for the latter assertion.

For \(A\subseteq T\), the partition-matroid rank is
\[ r_{\mathcal N}(A) =\min\{|A\cap S|,b\} +\min\{|A\setminus S|,r-a\}. \]
Because every coordinate of \(\lambda\) lies between zero and one,
\[ \lambda(A\cap S)\le |A\cap S|, \qquad \lambda(A\setminus S)\le |A\setminus S|. \]
Nonnegativity, together with the definitions of \(a,b\), also gives
\[ \lambda(A\cap S)\le s\le b, \qquad \lambda(A\setminus S)\le r-s\le r-a. \]
Adding the resulting bounds proves
\[ \lambda(A)\le r_{\mathcal N}(A). \]
Together with nonnegativity, these are precisely the independent-set-polytope inequalities.

Now apply the matroid-intersection polytope theorem: for two matroids on the same ground set, the intersection of their independent-set polytopes is the convex hull of their common independent sets. See [Schrijver, Corollary 41.12b](https://page.math.tu-berlin.de/~felsner/Lehre/SemMatS/Literatur/Schrijver-41:MatIntersection.pdf).

Consequently, there exist common independent sets \(I_1,\ldots,I_q\) and positive coefficients \(\alpha_i\) such that
\[ \lambda|_T=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\alpha_i\mathbf1_{I_i}, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{q}\alpha_i=1. \]

Every \(I_i\) has size at most \(r\), whereas
\[ r=\lambda(T)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\alpha_i|I_i|. \]
Thus
\[ 0=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\alpha_i(r-|I_i|). \]
Every summand is nonnegative, and every coefficient is positive. Therefore \(|I_i|=r\) for every \(i\).

Each \(I_i\) is consequently a basis of the original matroid. Its independence in \(\mathcal N\) gives the quota proved in Section 1. This establishes existence with exact marginals.

3. Construct the decomposition in polynomial time

Existence alone would not establish the requested algorithm. We use the following constructive theorem.

Exact matroid-intersection decomposition theorem. Given two matroids through independence-testing oracles and a rational vector, one can test membership in their common independent-set polytope and, when membership holds, construct a convex combination of incidence vectors of common independent sets. The procedure is strongly polynomial; in particular, its exact rational implementation has polynomial bit complexity and makes polynomially many independence queries. This is [Schrijver, Theorem 41.13](https://page.math.tu-berlin.de/~felsner/Lehre/SemMatS/Literatur/Schrijver-41:MatIntersection.pdf), based on Cunningham’s algorithm.

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • Both matroids have the same ground set \(T\), of size \(\tau\).
  • Independence in \(\mathcal M_T\) requires one query to the given oracle.
  • Independence in \(\mathcal N\) requires only the two cardinality checks.
  • The input vector \(\lambda|_T\) is rational and has encoding length bounded by \(L\).

Apply the theorem to \(\mathcal M_T,\mathcal N,\lambda|_T\). Discard zero coefficients and merge duplicate sets. Section 2 proves that every remaining set is an admissible basis.

Thus the algorithm already produces an explicit distribution of polynomial size. No expansion into a number of copies equal to a common denominator is needed.

4. Bound the support and probability encodings

We can further compress the constructed distribution by solving a finite rational linear program over its polynomially many returned bases.

Let its bases be \(B_1,\ldots,B_q\). Find an extreme point of the nonempty polytope
\[ \sum_{i=1}^{q}p_i\mathbf1_{B_i}=\lambda|_T, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{q}p_i=1, \qquad p_i\ge0. \]

The equality matrix has columns \((\mathbf1_{B_i},1)\). Since every basis has size \(r\), the sum of its first \(\tau\) rows equals \(r\) times its last row. Its rank is therefore at most \(\tau\).

At an extreme point, the columns corresponding to positive variables are linearly independent. Otherwise, a nonzero dependence supported on those variables would permit sufficiently small perturbations in both directions while preserving feasibility, contradicting extremality. Hence at most \(\tau\) probabilities are positive.

For completeness, their bit lengths also admit an explicit bound. Suppose the final support size is \(t\le\tau\). Select \(t\) independent equality rows on these columns, obtaining a nonsingular matrix \(C\) with entries zero or one. The corresponding right-hand side \(d\) consists of coordinates of \(\lambda\) and possibly the number one.

Let \(D\) be the product of the input denominators. Then \(Dd\) is integral and \(\log D=O(L)\). Cramer’s rule gives
\[ p_j=\frac{\det C_j}{\det C}, \]
where \(C_j\) replaces column \(j\) by \(d\). Hadamard’s inequality yields
\[ 1\le|\det C|\le t^{t/2}, \qquad |D\det C_j|\le D\,t^{t/2}. \]
The second bound uses the fact that every entry of \(d\) lies between zero and one.

Thus each probability has numerator and denominator encoding length
\[ O\bigl(L+\tau\log(\tau+1)\bigr). \]
Discarding zero probabilities gives precisely the requested positive support.

5. Feasibility and scope

Under the stated promise, infeasibility never occurs.

If the promise is removed, first check nonnegativity and total mass \(r\), then perform the same common-independent-set-polytope membership test. Membership is equivalent to existence of the required distribution: necessity follows from every admissible basis being common independent, and sufficiency follows from the cardinality argument in Section 2.

The construction uses classical matroid intersection and exact decomposition. It does not require the newer equitability results, which impose the additional requirement of a partition into disjoint bases.
