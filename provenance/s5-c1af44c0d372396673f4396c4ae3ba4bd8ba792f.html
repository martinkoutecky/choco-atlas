The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a supplier network with \(\tau\) occupied service locations, nonnegative edge costs, a rational mass \(\mu_t>0\) at each location type \(t\), and rational weights \(a_t\), compute an optimal nonnegative per-resident charge \(p_t\). Require that every proper measurable coalition \(Q\), with mass \(q_t=\mu(Q\cap t)\) and occupied locations \(U(q)=\{t:q_t>0\}\), satisfies \(\sum_t q_t p_t\le c(U(q))\), where \(c(U)\) is the supplier's minimum spanning tree cost to locations in \(U\). Equivalently, optimize \(\sum_t a_t y_t\) subject to \(y_t=\mu_t p_t\ge0\) and \(\sum_{t\in U}y_t\le c(U)\) for every \(U\subseteq T\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is an atomless probability space partitioned into location types \(T\), given by its support: the list of \(\tau=|T|\ge1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The supplier network is a complete undirected graph on \(T\cup\{0\}\), where \(0\) is the supplier, with nonnegative binary rational edge costs. Put \(m=\tau+1\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including the masses, edge costs, and objective weights \(a_t\in\mathbb Q\).
For \(U\subseteq T\), let \(c(U)\) be the minimum spanning tree cost in the subgraph induced by \(U\cup\{0\}\), with \(c(\varnothing)=0\); locations outside \(U\) cannot serve as Steiner vertices.
Choose a constant per-resident charge \(p_t\ge0\) at each type to maximize \(\sum_t a_t\mu_t p_t\), without requiring budget balance. Every measurable coalition \(Q\) with \(\mu(Q)<1\) must satisfy \(\sum_t q_t p_t\le c(U(q))\), where \(q_t=\mu(Q\cap t)\) and \(U(q)=\{t:q_t>0\}\).
Equivalently, maximize \(\sum_t a_t y_t\) subject to \(y_t=\mu_t p_t\ge0\) and \(\sum_{t\in U}y_t\le c(U)\) for every \(U\subseteq T\), including \(U=T\).

Is exact optimization of this continuous charge problem in \(\mathsf P\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

For finite-agent minimum spanning tree games, arbitrary linear optimization over allocations constrained by all proper coalitions, with unrestricted signs, admits no polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}\); maximizing total charges remains \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard with nonnegative allocations. Conclusion

Exact optimization is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. Consequently, an algorithm running in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}\).

This holds even with positive integer edge costs, positive dyadic masses, and nonnegative objective weights. The qualification concerning Turing reductions is explicit: the proof uses the equivalence between optimization and separation.

There is an important easy special case: when every \(a_t=1\), the optimum equals \(c(T)\) and is computable in polynomial time.

1. The atomless constraints give exactly the stated polytope

Write \(y(U)=\sum_{t\in U}y_t\), and define

\( P_w=\{y\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}:y(U)\le c(U)\text{ for every }U\subseteq T\}. \)

For any proper subset \(U\subsetneq T\), the coalition consisting of all residents of its types has measure less than \(1\). Its constraint gives \(y(U)\le c(U)\).

To obtain the constraint for \(T\), fix a type \(s\). Atomlessness permits removing a measurable subset of type \(s\) of any measure \(\varepsilon\) with \(0<\varepsilon<\mu_s\). The remaining coalition has measure \(1-\varepsilon\), support \(T\), and charge

\( y(T)-\varepsilon p_s\le c(T). \)

Letting \(\varepsilon\) tend to zero gives \(y(T)\le c(T)\).

Conversely, suppose \(y\in P_w\) and put \(p_t=y_t/\mu_t\). For any measurable coalition with quantities \(q_t\) and support \(U\), nonnegativity gives

\( \sum_tq_tp_t\le\sum_{t\in U}\mu_tp_t=y(U)\le c(U). \)

Thus the continuous problem is precisely linear optimization over \(P_w\). Since every mass is positive, conversion between \(p\) and \(y\) is an invertible rational change of variables.

2. A hardness theorem that already respects nonnegativity

The relevant finite problem is core nonmembership, rather than maximization of total charges over proper-coalition constraints.

Faigle, Kern, Fekete, and Hochstättler prove the following problem \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete: given a complete undirected supplier graph and an allocation \(x\) satisfying \(x(T)=c(T)\), decide whether some coalition satisfies \(x(U)>c(U)\). Their construction already has positive integer edge costs and a nonnegative integer allocation. Specifically, its allocation assigns \(q+2\) to element players, \(q\) to set players, and zero to two auxiliary players. These restrictions follow directly from the construction proving Theorem 2.1. [Original paper, Theorem 2.1 and its construction](https://www.ibr.cs.tu-bs.de/users/fekete/hp/publications/PDF/1997-On_the_Complexity_of_Testing-Membership_in_the_Core_of_Min-Cost_Spanning_Tree_Games.pdf)

For these instances, \(x\ge0\) and \(x(T)=c(T)\). Therefore,

\( x\notin P_w \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{there exists }U\subseteq T\text{ with }x(U)>c(U). \)

Consequently, deciding nonmembership in \(P_w\) is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard, already for positive integer edge costs and nonnegative candidate points. The grand-coalition inequality causes no difficulty: it holds with equality for the candidate points.

3. Optimization would decide this hard membership problem

We use the exact optimization–separation theorem: for a well-described rational polyhedron, strong separation can be solved in oracle-polynomial time using a strong linear-optimization oracle. A description is “well-described” when the dimension and a bound on the encoding length of each defining inequality are supplied; the number of inequalities may be exponential. [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, *Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization*, Theorem 6.4.9](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4)

Here are the required hypothesis checks. Put \(n=|T|\), and let \(W\) be the largest edge cost in one of the positive-integer instances above.

Every induced spanning tree has at most \(n\) edges, so

\( 0\le c(U)\le nW. \)

Each coalition inequality has coefficients in \(\{0,1\}\) and an integer right-hand side of binary length at most \(O(\log(nW))\). The nonnegativity inequalities also have constant-size coefficients. Thus each row has encoding length \(O(n+\log(nW))\), polynomial in the graph input length.

The polytope is nonempty and bounded: zero is feasible, and singleton inequalities give

\( 0\le y_t\le w_{0t}\le W. \)

It is even full-dimensional. Positive integer costs imply \(c(U)\ge1\) for every nonempty \(U\). Hence

\( [0,1/n]^n\subseteq P_w, \)

because any point in this cube satisfies \(y(U)\le |U|/n\le1\le c(U)\). These facts also provide explicit inner and outer bounds of polynomial encoding length.

Now suppose that the continuous charge problem had a polynomial-time exact optimizer for arbitrary rational objective weights.

For any such graph, choose positive dyadic masses as follows. Set \(k=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) and take

\( \mu_i=2^{-k}\quad(1\le i<n), \qquad \mu_n=1-(n-1)2^{-k}. \)

These masses are positive, sum to one, and have total encoding length polynomial in \(n\). They can be realized by a partition of the atomless probability space \([0,1]\).

By Section 1, calling the hypothesized charge optimizer with objective vector \(a\) optimizes \(a\cdot y\) over exactly \(P_w\). Returning charges \(p\) instead of \(y\) makes no difference, since \(y_t=\mu_tp_t\).

The optimization–separation theorem therefore supplies a polynomial-time separation algorithm for \(P_w\). Applied to the candidate \(x\), this algorithm decides whether \(x\in P_w\), and hence decides the \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete nonmembership problem from Section 2.

The theorem makes only polynomially many oracle calls, with objective vectors of polynomial encoding length. Accordingly, every charge instance queried has \(m=n+1\), \(\tau=n\), and polynomial input length \(L\).

This proves the claimed polynomial-time Turing hardness.

Nonnegative weights suffice

The polytope \(P_w\) is downward closed: if \(0\le z\le y\) coordinatewise and \(y\in P_w\), then \(z\in P_w\).

For an arbitrary rational objective \(a\), define \(a_t^+=\max(a_t,0)\). Then

\( \max_{y\in P_w}a\cdot y = \max_{y\in P_w}a^+\cdot y. \)

Indeed, the left-hand side cannot exceed the right-hand side. Conversely, from any optimizer for \(a^+\), set every coordinate with \(a_t<0\) to zero. Downward closure preserves feasibility, and the resulting \(a\)-objective equals the original \(a^+\)-objective.

Thus an optimizer restricted to nonnegative weights would also provide the arbitrary-objective oracle used above.

4. Why maximizing total charges is nevertheless easy

For completeness, the total-charge special case has a direct proof.

Let \(H\) be a full-network minimum spanning tree, rooted at the supplier. For each type \(t\), let \(e_t\) be its edge to its parent, and put \(b_t=w(e_t)\). Then

\( b\ge0, \qquad b(T)=w(H)=c(T). \)

We prove \(b(U)\le c(U)\) for every \(U\).

Remove from \(H\) the parent edges \(e_t\) for all \(t\in U\). Each resulting component contains exactly one vertex of \(U\cup\{0\}\): following parent edges stops at either the supplier or the first vertex whose parent edge was removed.

Let \(F\) be an induced MST on \(U\cup\{0\}\). Its edges connect those components into a spanning tree \(H'\) of the full network. Therefore,

\( w(H')=w(H)-b(U)+c(U). \)

Since \(H\) is a minimum spanning tree,

\( w(H)\le w(H'), \)

which gives \(b(U)\le c(U)\). Hence \(b\in P_w\).

The grand-coalition inequality bounds every feasible total charge by \(c(T)\), and \(b\) attains this bound. Thus

\( \max_{y\in P_w}\sum_{t\in T}y_t=c(T). \)

The hardness established above concerns varying objective weights; it does not transfer the proper-coalition total-charge hardness to this atomless formulation.
