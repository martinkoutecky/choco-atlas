1. Classification and running times

With an explicit list, both exact coalition-density optimization and exact pricing are polynomial-time solvable. With circuits, both are $\mathrm{NP}$-hard. Under either representation, the two computational problems are polynomial-time Turing equivalent.

The reduction from density optimization to pricing includes recovery of an optimal density evaluated with the original coalition values. A homogeneous cone supplies the missing recovery argument identified in the verifier’s report.

Define
$$ b=\left\lceil\log_2(m+1)\right\rceil, \qquad s=|\mathcal K|, \qquad N(m,\tau)=\binom{m+\tau}{\tau}-1. $$

The following are bit-operation upper bounds. For pricing, $L$ includes the prices.

| Representation | Exact density optimization, including infeasibility detection | Exact pricing |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit list | $\operatorname{poly}(s,\tau,L,b)$ | $O\!\left(s\,\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,b)\right)$ |
| Circuits | $O\!\left(N(m,\tau)\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,b)\right)$ | $O\!\left(N(m,\tau)\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,b)\right)$ |

Every feasible instance has an optimal rational density with at most $\tau$ nonzero entries and polynomial encoding length.

For circuits, the displayed bounds are polynomial-time bounds when $m$ is fixed. When $\tau$ is fixed, they are polynomial in the numerical value of $m$, which can be exponential in its binary encoding length. Hardness holds even for $\tau=1$. It also holds when $m=\tau/2$, so a uniform $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ algorithm would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.

2. The linear program and its output size

Let $A$ have column $k$ for each $k\in\mathcal K$, and let $v_k=V(k)$. The density problem is the finite rational linear program
$$ \max\{v^{\mathsf T}y:Ay=\mu,\ y\geq0\}. $$

2.1. Feasibility implies boundedness and attainment

Write $s(k)=\sum_t k_t$. Summing the mass equations gives
$$ \sum_{k\in\mathcal K}s(k)y_k = \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t = 1. $$
Since $1\leq s(k)\leq m$,
$$ \frac1m\leq\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}y_k\leq1. $$
In particular, every coordinate satisfies $0\leq y_k\leq1$.

The feasible set is therefore closed and bounded in a finite-dimensional space, hence compact. If it is nonempty, its linear objective attains a finite maximum. This argument applies to arbitrary signed values.

2.2. An optimum has at most $\tau$ nonzero entries

Choose an optimum $y$ whose support $S$ has minimum cardinality. Suppose the columns indexed by $S$ are dependent. There is then a nonzero vector $d$, supported on $S$, such that
$$ Ad=0. $$
Because $y_k>0$ on $S$, both $y+\varepsilon d$ and $y-\varepsilon d$ are nonnegative for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. Both satisfy the mass equations. Optimality in both directions forces
$$ v^{\mathsf T}d=0. $$

Moreover, summing the coordinates of $Ad=0$ gives
$$ \sum_{k\in S}s(k)d_k=0. $$
All $s(k)$ are positive, so the nonzero vector $d$ must have both positive and negative entries.

Set
$$ \alpha=\min_{k:d_k>0}\frac{y_k}{d_k}. $$
Then $y-\alpha d$ is nonnegative, feasible, and has the same objective value. At least one previously positive entry becomes zero, and no entry outside $S$ becomes positive. This contradicts minimality of $S$.

Thus the supported columns are independent, and
$$ |S|\leq\operatorname{rank}(A)\leq\tau. $$

2.3. The sparse optimum has polynomial encoding length

Let $r=|S|$. Choose $r$ rows on which the supported columns form an invertible integer matrix $B$. Its entries lie between zero and $m$, and
$$ By_S=\mu_I. $$

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the input masses. Its binary length is at most their total input length, hence $O(L)$. Write $\mu_I=u/D$, where $u$ is integral and $0<u_i\leq D$.

Cramer’s rule gives
$$ (y_S)_j=\frac{\det B_j(u)}{D\det B}, $$
where $B_j(u)$ replaces column $j$ of $B$ by $u$. Expanding determinants,
$$ |\det B|\leq r!m^r, \qquad |\det B_j(u)|\leq r!Dm^{r-1}. $$
Consequently, each nonzero density entry has encoding length
$$ O\!\left(L+r\log(r+1)+r\log(m+1)\right). $$

Each supported coalition takes $\tau b$ bits to describe. Each corresponding value has encoding length bounded by the explicit input or the circuit output widths, which are included in $L$. The entire sparse optimum, and its exact objective value, therefore have polynomial encoding length in $\tau,L,b$.

3. Explicit inputs

The explicit density problem is a rational linear program with $s$ variables and $\tau$ equality constraints. Polynomial-time rational linear programming determines infeasibility or returns an exact basic optimal solution. Section 2 proves that such a solution has at most $\tau$ nonzero entries and polynomial output length.

Thus density optimization takes
$$ \operatorname{poly}(s,\tau,L,b) $$
bit operations.

For pricing, compute
$$ V(k)-p^{\mathsf T}k $$
for every listed coalition and retain a maximizer. Exact rational arithmetic takes
$$ O\!\left(s\,\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,b)\right) $$
bit operations.

Neither algorithm needs to enumerate coalitions outside the supplied list. Their dependence on $m$ is through its binary encoding length.

4. Density optimization using exact pricing

We prove that a pricing oracle for the given coalition family and values suffices to determine infeasibility and return a sparse exact optimum.

4.1. Oracle results used

A rational polyhedron is *well described* when its dimension $d$ and a bound $\varphi$ on each defining inequality’s encoding length are known.

We use two results from [Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver, Chapter 6](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1988.pdf):

  • Theorem 6.4.9: strong separation permits exact optimization, including infeasibility and unboundedness detection, in oracle-polynomial time.
  • Lemma 6.5.15: if oracle-returned normals also have encoding length at most $\varphi$, a nonempty problem with finite optimum $\gamma$ admits computable oracle normals $a_i$, valid inequalities $a_i^{\mathsf T}x\leq\beta_i$, and positive rational multipliers $\lambda_i$ satisfying

$$ \sum_i\lambda_i a_i=c, \qquad \sum_i\lambda_i\beta_i=\gamma. $$
The normals can be chosen independent.

These guarantees are uniform polynomial bounds in $d,\varphi$, and the objective encoding length, counting oracle calls and additional bit work. Query lengths are polynomially bounded. Neither boundedness nor full dimensionality is required.

The distinction between the returned normals and the right-hand sides $\beta_i$ is essential below.

4.2. Obtain the optimal value or determine infeasibility

The density LP has dual
$$ \min\left\{\mu^{\mathsf T}p: k^{\mathsf T}p\geq V(k) \text{ for every }k\in\mathcal K\right\}, $$
with unrestricted $p\in\mathbb R^\tau$. Denote its feasible polyhedron by $D_{\mathcal K}$.

At a rational query $p$, pricing returns
$$ R(p)=\max_{k\in\mathcal K}\bigl(V(k)-p^{\mathsf T}k\bigr) $$
and a maximizing coalition.

If $R(p)\leq0$, the query belongs to $D_{\mathcal K}$. Otherwise, the maximizing coalition supplies the violated valid inequality
$$ -k^{\mathsf T}p\leq-V(k). $$
This is a strong separation oracle.

Each defining inequality and each returned normal has encoding length bounded by a polynomial in $\tau,L,b$. Thus $D_{\mathcal K}$ is well described.

Set $B=2^L$. Every value satisfies $|V(k)|\leq B$, because its numerator width is included in $L$ and its denominator is positive. Hence $B\mathbf1$ is dual feasible:
$$ (B\mathbf1)^{\mathsf T}k = B\,s(k) \geq B \geq V(k). $$

If the density equations are infeasible, Farkas’ lemma gives a vector $d$ such that
$$ k^{\mathsf T}d\geq0\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathcal K, \qquad \mu^{\mathsf T}d<0. $$
Starting from any dual feasible $p_0$, every $p_0+\lambda d$, for $\lambda\geq0$, remains feasible, while its objective tends to $-\infty$.

Conversely, if a density $y$ is feasible, every dual feasible price satisfies
$$ \mu^{\mathsf T}p = \sum_k y_k k^{\mathsf T}p \geq \sum_k V(k)y_k, $$
so the dual is bounded below.

Theorem 6.4.9 therefore detects density infeasibility as dual unboundedness. Otherwise, finite-dimensional LP strong duality identifies the computed dual minimum with the density maximum. Write this exact value as $\gamma$. Section 2 shows that $\gamma$ has polynomial encoding length.

4.3. A homogeneous cone for recovering the density

Define the rational polyhedral cone
$$ C= \left\{(u,q)\in\mathbb R^\tau\times\mathbb R: q\geq0,\quad -k^{\mathsf T}u+V(k)q\leq0 \text{ for every }k\in\mathcal K \right\}. $$

Its defining normals are
$$ a_k=(-k,V(k)), \qquad a_0=(0,\ldots,0,-1). $$
Their right-hand sides are all zero, and all these inequalities are tight at the origin.

We next give a strong separation oracle for $C$ using pricing on the original values.

Case $q<0$. Return $a_0$: its scalar product with $(u,q)$ is $-q>0$.

Case $q>0$. Price at $u/q$. The maximum violation is
$$ \max_k\bigl(-k^{\mathsf T}u+V(k)q\bigr) = q\max_k\left(V(k)-k^{\mathsf T}\frac uq\right). $$
A positive result returns the corresponding original normal $a_k$. A nonpositive result certifies membership.

Case $q=0$. We must decide whether some $k$ satisfies $k^{\mathsf T}u<0$.

Write the coordinates of $u$ as rational fractions, and let $W$ be the product of their positive denominators. Then $Wk^{\mathsf T}u$ is integral for every integer coalition $k$. Thus
$$ k^{\mathsf T}u<0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k^{\mathsf T}u\leq-\frac1W. $$
Set
$$ H=(2B+1)W $$
and price at $Hu$.

For any coalition with $k^{\mathsf T}u\geq0$,
$$ V(k)-Hk^{\mathsf T}u\leq B. $$
For any coalition with $k^{\mathsf T}u<0$,
$$ V(k)-Hk^{\mathsf T}u \geq -B+\frac HW = B+1. $$
Therefore, if a violating coalition exists, every pricing maximizer is violating. Inspect the returned coalition: if $k^{\mathsf T}u<0$, return $a_k$; otherwise certify membership.

The binary length of $W$, hence of $H$, is polynomial in the query length and $L$. All pricing inputs consequently have polynomial length. Every returned normal is an original $a_k$ or $a_0$, with a query-independent polynomial encoding bound.

4.4. The oracle certificate is an optimal density

Apply Lemma 6.5.15 to maximizing the objective with normal
$$ c=(-\mu,\gamma) $$
over $C$.

To verify boundedness and the optimum, let $y^\star$ be any optimal density, whose existence was established in Section 2. Then
$$ c=\sum_k y^\star_k(-k,V(k)). $$
Consequently, for every $(u,q)\in C$,
$$ c^{\mathsf T}(u,q) = \sum_k y^\star_k\bigl(-k^{\mathsf T}u+V(k)q\bigr) \leq0. $$
The origin belongs to $C$ and attains zero. The cone optimization therefore has optimum exactly zero.

The lemma returns positive multipliers and valid inequalities satisfying
$$ \sum_i\lambda_i a_i=c, \qquad \sum_i\lambda_i\beta_i=0. $$
Because the origin belongs to $C$, validity implies $\beta_i\geq0$ for every returned inequality. Positivity of the multipliers then forces
$$ \beta_i=0 \quad\text{for every }i. $$
Thus the recovery cannot replace any coalition right-hand side by a different value.

Aggregate the multipliers on coalition normals as $y_k\geq0$, and let $\alpha\geq0$ be the multiplier on $a_0$, if present. Comparing coordinates in
$$ (-\mu,\gamma) = \sum_k y_k(-k,V(k))+\alpha a_0 $$
gives
$$ \sum_k k y_k=\mu, \qquad \sum_k V(k)y_k-\alpha=\gamma. $$
The first equation makes $y$ a feasible density. Since $\gamma$ is the density maximum,
$$ \sum_k V(k)y_k\leq\gamma. $$
The second equation gives the reverse inequality, because $\alpha\geq0$. Hence
$$ \alpha=0, \qquad \sum_k V(k)y_k=\gamma. $$
The recovered density is optimal for the original values.

The basic certificate contains at most $\tau+1$ normals. Solving the explicit density LP restricted to its returned coalitions yields a basic optimum with at most $\tau$ nonzero entries, in polynomial additional time.

This completes the reduction: density optimization uses polynomially many exact pricing calls on the same coalition family and values, with polynomial query lengths and polynomial additional bit work.

5. Exact pricing using density optimization

We now prove the converse direction. This reduction uses one density-optimization instance with one additional supported type.

Given a pricing instance, define
$$ r(k)=V(k)-p^{\mathsf T}k, \qquad R=\max_{k\in\mathcal K}r(k). $$
Set
$$ h=\left\lceil\log_2(1+\tau m)\right\rceil, \qquad \varepsilon=2^{-h}. $$

Add a type $\star$, with masses
$$ \mu'_\star=\varepsilon, $$
$$ \mu'_1= 1-\bigl(1+(\tau-1)m\bigr)\varepsilon, \qquad \mu'_t=m\varepsilon \quad(2\leq t\leq\tau). $$
They sum to one. Since $2^h\geq1+\tau m$,
$$ \mu'_1-m\varepsilon = 1-(1+\tau m)\varepsilon \geq0. $$
Thus every original-type mass is at least $m\varepsilon>0$, and all masses are dyadic.

Use the bound $m'=m+1$ and include exactly:

  • $(k,1)$, for each $k\in\mathcal K$, with value $r(k)$;
  • the singleton $e_t$ of each original type, with value zero.

Every coalition satisfies the new size bound, and the family is nonempty.

For any feasible new density $z$, the $\star$ equation gives
$$ \sum_{k\in\mathcal K}z_{(k,1)}=\varepsilon. $$
Its objective therefore satisfies
$$ \sum_{k\in\mathcal K}r(k)z_{(k,1)} \leq \varepsilon R. $$

Choose a maximizing coalition $k^\star$. The entries
$$ z_{(k^\star,1)}=\varepsilon, \qquad z_{e_t}=\mu'_t-\varepsilon k^\star_t $$
are nonnegative because $k^\star_t\leq m$ and $\mu'_t\geq m\varepsilon$. They satisfy all mass equations and attain $\varepsilon R$. Hence
$$ \operatorname{OPT}'=\varepsilon R. $$

In any optimum, the augmented entries have total density $\varepsilon>0$. Moreover,
$$ 0 = \varepsilon R-\operatorname{OPT}' = \sum_k\bigl(R-r(k)\bigr)z_{(k,1)}. $$
Every summand is nonnegative. Thus every augmented coalition with positive density satisfies $r(k)=R$. Reading any such coalition returns a pricing maximizer, and dividing the optimal value by $\varepsilon$ returns $R$.

The construction has polynomial encoding length:

  • Explicit lists gain $\tau$ singleton entries.
  • Circuit membership tests the two specified forms, including the original coordinate and size bounds.
  • The new value circuit performs exact rational subtraction. Binary arithmetic has polynomial-size circuits, and the required numerator and denominator widths are polynomial in the original pricing input length.

Therefore pricing reduces to one density-optimization call, under either representation. Together with Section 4, this proves polynomial-time Turing equivalence.

6. Circuit hardness

Both reductions below use positive dyadic masses, nonempty coalition families, nonnegative integer values, and always-feasible density instances.

6.1. Hardness with one supported type

Let $\varphi$ be a Boolean formula on $n\geq1$ variables. Set
$$ \tau=1, \qquad \mu_1=1, \qquad m=2^{n+1}-1, $$
and
$$ \mathcal K=\{2^n,\ldots,2^{n+1}-1\}. $$
A coalition coordinate uses $n+1$ bits. Membership is determined by its leading bit; the trailing $n$ bits encode an assignment $x(k)$.

Define
$$ V(k)= \begin{cases} k,&\varphi(x(k))\text{ is true},\\ 0,&\varphi(x(k))\text{ is false}. \end{cases} $$
The membership and value circuits have polynomial size, and the value denominator is one.

Every density satisfies
$$ \sum_k k y_k=1. $$
Thus the numbers $ky_k$ are nonnegative weights summing to one, and
$$ \sum_k V(k)y_k = \sum_k\frac{V(k)}k(ky_k) \leq \max_k\frac{V(k)}k. $$
Equality is attained by setting $y_{k^\star}=1/k^\star$ for a maximizing coalition. Therefore
$$ \operatorname{OPT}= \begin{cases} 1,&\varphi\text{ is satisfiable},\\ 0,&\varphi\text{ is unsatisfiable}. \end{cases} $$
Exact density optimization, or the threshold question with threshold $1/2$, decides satisfiability.

For pricing, take $p_1=0$. Then
$$ \max_k V(k)>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \varphi\text{ is satisfiable}. $$

Both problems are $\mathrm{NP}$-hard even when $\tau=1$. The reduction is polynomial in encoded length: the exponentially large numerical bound $m$ uses only $n+1$ bits.

6.2. Hardness with numerical $m$ polynomially bounded

Choose a power of two $q$ satisfying
$$ n\leq q<2n. $$
Pad the assignment to $q$ variables, with $\varphi$ ignoring the extra variables.

Introduce types $(i,0),(i,1)$ for $1\leq i\leq q$, and set
$$ \tau=2q, \qquad m=q, \qquad \mu_{i,a}=\frac1{2q}. $$
These masses are dyadic because $q$ is a power of two.

For every assignment $x\in\{0,1\}^q$, include
$$ k(x)_{i,1}=x_i, \qquad k(x)_{i,0}=1-x_i. $$
Membership is exactly the condition
$$ k_{i,0}+k_{i,1}=1 \quad\text{for every }i, $$
which has a polynomial-size circuit. Every coalition has size $q$.

Let $\bar x$ be the coordinatewise complement and define
$$ V(k(x))= \begin{cases} q,&\varphi(x)\lor\varphi(\bar x),\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} $$
The value circuit also has polynomial size.

For every assignment,
$$ k(x)+k(\bar x)=\mathbf1. $$
Assigning density $1/(2q)$ to each complementary coalition therefore satisfies every mass equation. Thus every instance is feasible.

Since all coalitions have size $q$,
$$ q\sum_k y_k=1. $$
All values are at most $q$, so every feasible density has objective at most one.

If $\varphi$ is satisfiable, extend a satisfying assignment to $x$. Both $k(x)$ and $k(\bar x)$ have value $q$; their complementary feasible density attains one. If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, all values are zero. Hence again
$$ \operatorname{OPT}= \begin{cases} 1,&\varphi\text{ is satisfiable},\\ 0,&\varphi\text{ is unsatisfiable}. \end{cases} $$
At zero prices, the pricing maximum is respectively $q$ or zero.

This proves hardness with
$$ m=\tau/2, $$
while $m,\tau,L$ are all polynomially bounded in the satisfiability input length. Consequently, a uniform $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)$ algorithm for either circuit problem would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.

7. Enumeration bounds and threshold decisions

Introduce a slack coordinate
$$ k_0=m-\sum_{t\in T}k_t. $$
Nonnegative vectors with total size at most $m$ correspond to nonnegative integer solutions of
$$ k_0+\sum_{t\in T}k_t=m. $$
Stars and bars gives $\binom{m+\tau}{\tau}$ such vectors. Removing the zero coalition leaves
$$ N(m,\tau)=\binom{m+\tau}{\tau}-1. $$

A recursive enumeration of coordinate prefixes visits at most a polynomial in $\tau$ times this number of vectors. Coordinate arithmetic uses $O(b)$-bit integers. Evaluating membership and values, then computing and comparing rational surpluses, adds polynomial work per vector. Thus circuit pricing takes
$$ O\!\left(N(m,\tau)\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,b)\right). $$

Section 4 uses polynomially many pricing calls, all on the original coalition family and with polynomial price encoding lengths. Substituting the enumeration algorithm proves the same form of bound for density optimization, including sparse optimal output.

The parameter distinctions follow from
$$ N(m,\tau)<(m+1)^\tau $$
and
$$ N(m,\tau)<(\tau+m)^m. $$
Thus fixed $\tau$ gives polynomial dependence on numerical $m$, and fixed $m$ gives polynomial dependence on $\tau$ and encoded input length. Neither is a uniform polynomial bound in the binary input length when both parameters vary.

For completeness, the natural circuit threshold problems are $\mathrm{NP}$-complete:

  • Pricing asks whether an admissible coalition has surplus at least a supplied rational threshold. A coalition is a polynomial-size certificate.
  • Density optimization asks whether a feasible density has value at least a supplied rational threshold. A yes-instance has a sparse rational optimal density of polynomial encoding length by Section 2.

Membership, mass equations, nonnegativity, and objective thresholds can all be verified exactly in polynomial time. Section 6 supplies hardness.

8. Relation to discrete coalition structure generation

ELIXIR concerns partitions of a finite set of agents. Its optimality and $O(3^n)$ running-time statements are Theorems 5 and 6 of [Taguelmimt et al., 2023](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0035.pdf).

The present problem permits fractional coalition densities and is a rational linear program. Its explicit-input polynomial solvability and circuit-input hardness follow from the arguments above. The discrete coalition-value arithmetic bound does not supply a bit-complexity bound for this continuous problem, and no literature-novelty assertion is needed for the classification.
