The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational group masses \(\lambda_j>0\) summing to \(1\), a finite rational cost domain \(K\), and truthful cost masses \(\mu_{j,c}\) summing to \(\lambda_j\), determine the tight worst-case approximation ratio for a computationally efficient truthful, individually rational, budget-feasible mechanism. The mechanism receives reported masses \(\nu_{j,b}\), assigns selection rates \(q_j(b;\nu)\) and expected per-person transfers \(p_j(b;\nu)\), and must satisfy \(\sum_{j,b}\nu_{j,b}p_j(b;\nu)\le B\) for every report measure, interpreted as exact aggregate expenditure over selected mass. For each infinitesimal agent of true cost \(c\), require \(p_j(c;\nu)-c q_j(c;\nu)\ge p_j(b;\nu)-c q_j(b;\nu)\) for every report \(b\), holding the other agents' mass fixed. Compare its truthful representation \(\min_j \sum_c\mu_{j,c}q_j(c;\mu)/\lambda_j\) with \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=\max_{0\le y_{j,c}\le\mu_{j,c}}\min_j\sum_c y_{j,c}/\lambda_j\), subject to \(\sum_{j,c}c y_{j,c}\le B\). In particular, determine whether every such mechanism has a worst-case approximation lower bound of order \(\Omega(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min})\), or whether fractional selection beats it, with running time measured in \(m,\tau,L\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge1\) publicly known, disjoint groups in an atomless society, with positive binary rational masses \(\lambda_j\) summing to \(1\). Public inputs also include a positive binary rational budget \(B\) and a nonempty finite cost/report domain \(K\subset\mathbb Q_{>0}\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((j,c)\) with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Thus \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Truthful masses satisfy \(\mu_{j,c}\ge0\) and \(\sum_{c\in K}\mu_{j,c}=\lambda_j\); they are private.
An anonymous mechanism receives only reported masses \(\nu_{j,b}\ge0\), with \(\sum_{b\in K}\nu_{j,b}=\lambda_j\), and returns rational selection rates \(q_j(b;\nu)\in[0,1]\) and expected per-person transfers \(p_j(b;\nu)\ge0\), defined even for reports of zero mass. Require \(p_j(b;\nu)=0\) whenever \(q_j(b;\nu)=0\).
For every report measure, require exact aggregate expenditure \(\sum_{j,b}\nu_{j,b}p_j(b;\nu)\le B\). For every \(j,c,b,\nu\), require individual rationality \(p_j(c;\nu)-c q_j(c;\nu)\ge0\) and truthfulness \(p_j(c;\nu)-c q_j(c;\nu)\ge p_j(b;\nu)-c q_j(b;\nu)\). An individual's deviation has zero mass and leaves \(\nu\) unchanged; group membership cannot be misreported.
Define \(\mathrm{ALG}_M(\mu)=\min_j\lambda_j^{-1}\sum_{c\in K}\mu_{j,c}q_j(c;\mu)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)=\max\{\min_j\lambda_j^{-1}\sum_{c\in K}y_{j,c}:0\le y_{j,c}\le\mu_{j,c},\ \sum_{j,c}c y_{j,c}\le B\}\).
The approximation factor is \(\mathrm{OPT}/\mathrm{ALG}_M\), interpreted as \(+\infty\) when \(\mathrm{ALG}_M=0<\mathrm{OPT}\). Computational efficiency means polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|K|,L\), where \(\tau\) is the reported support size and \(L\) is the total binary input length.

What is the tight worst-case approximation factor achievable by such computationally efficient mechanisms over all admissible inputs? In particular, must every such mechanism incur an \(\Omega(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min})\) factor, where \(\lambda_{\max}=\max_j\lambda_j\) and \(\lambda_{\min}=\min_j\lambda_j\), or can fractional selection improve this dependence?

For finite agents with indivisible selection, an \(\Omega(n_{\max}/n_{\min})\) approximation lower bound is established; a polynomial-time mechanism achieves \(3+n_{\max}/n_{\min}\) when it selects at least one agent from every group ([discrete results](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0044.pdf)).
The expanded [journal version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370223001212) also reports a matching discrete lower bound. No result settling precisely the atomless model above was located.

Result

The tight worst-case approximation factor is \(\displaystyle \frac{e}{e-1}\approx 1.58198\), independent of \(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}\).

A polynomial-time mechanism attains this guarantee. Conversely, every mechanism satisfying the stated incentive and budget constraints has worst-case factor at least \(\frac{e}{e-1}\), even for any fixed choice of group masses.

The argument uses the stipulated fact that an individual deviation leaves the reported measure unchanged. The cited discrete bounds concern indivisible selection and therefore do not establish a lower bound for this model. [IJCAI paper](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0044.pdf)

1. Truthful menus reduce to mixtures of posted prices

Write \(K=\{c_1,\ldots,c_h\}\), where \(0<c_1<\cdots<c_h\). Fix a reported measure \(\nu\) and a group, and abbreviate its menu by \(q_i,p_i\).

Adding the truthfulness inequalities for types \(c_i<c_k\) gives

\((c_k-c_i)(q_i-q_k)\ge 0.\)

Thus \(q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_h\). Set \(q_{h+1}=0\) and \(x_i=q_i-q_{i+1}\). Then

\(x_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^h x_i=q_1\le1,\qquad q_i=\sum_{k=i}^h x_k.\)

Truthfulness for type \(c_i\) against report \(c_{i+1}\) gives

\(p_i\ge p_{i+1}+c_i(q_i-q_{i+1}).\)

Together with individual rationality \(p_h\ge c_hq_h\), backward induction yields

\[p_i\ge \sum_{k=i}^h c_kx_k. \tag{1}\]

Conversely, for any nonnegative weights satisfying \(\sum_kx_k\le1\), the menu

\[q_i=\sum_{k=i}^h x_k,\qquad p_i=\sum_{k=i}^h c_kx_k \tag{2}\]

is truthful and individually rational. To see this, interpret weight \(x_k\) as offering price \(c_k\) and selecting precisely reports at most \(c_k\). For an individual of cost \(c\), participating in that offer contributes \(c_k-c\) to utility. Truthful reporting accepts every offer with nonnegative contribution and rejects every offer with negative contribution. Hence no alternative report improves utility.

Also, \(q_i=0\) forces every corresponding \(x_k=0\), so \(p_i=0\).

Consequently, every feasible truthful menu can be replaced by a mixture of posted prices preserving its selection rates and weakly decreasing every payment.

2. A polynomial-time mechanism

For every reported measure, define the normalized cumulative masses

\(f_{j,i}=\lambda_j^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^i\nu_{j,c_k}.\)

In particular, \(f_{j,h}=1\). Offering price \(c_i\) in group \(j\) selects fraction \(f_{j,i}\) and costs \(\lambda_jc_if_{j,i}\).

Solve the following linear program:

\(\text{maximize }t,\)

subject to

\(x_{j,i}\ge0,\qquad \sum_i x_{j,i}\le1\quad\text{for every }j,\)

\(\sum_i f_{j,i}x_{j,i}\ge t\quad\text{for every }j,\)

\[\sum_j\lambda_j\sum_i c_if_{j,i}x_{j,i}\le B,\qquad 0\le t\le1. \tag{3}\]

Return

\[q_j(c_k;\nu)=\sum_{i=k}^h x_{j,i},\qquad p_j(c_k;\nu)=\sum_{i=k}^h c_ix_{j,i}. \tag{4}\]

Section 1 proves truthfulness, individual rationality, and the required zero-payment property, including at reports of zero mass. The budget constraint in (3) is exactly the aggregate expenditure of (4).

Crucially, an individual's deviation leaves \(\nu\), and therefore the entire computed menu, unchanged.

For binary-rational reported masses, (3) is a rational linear program with \(mh+1\) variables and polynomially many constraints. Its coefficients have binary lengths polynomial in the input length. An exact rational optimal solution can therefore be computed in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|K|,L\).

Moreover, this mechanism is pointwise optimal among feasible truthful mechanisms: applying (1) to any such mechanism produces feasible weights in (3), with the same selection rates and no greater expenditure.

3. The key single-group inequality

Consider a normalized group of mass \(1\). Let its cumulative masses at the costs be \(f_i\), and let \(c(z)\), for \(0<z\le1\), be its nondecreasing cost quantile function.

Define

\(C(t)=\int_0^t c(z)\,dz.\)

This is the minimum actual cost of selecting fraction \(t\): any allocation selecting higher-cost mass while leaving lower-cost mass unselected can be improved by exchanging equal amounts of that mass.

Define the minimum posted-price-mixture expenditure for selecting exactly fraction \(s\):

\[T(s)=\min\left\{\sum_i c_if_ix_i:\ x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i\le1,\ \sum_i f_ix_i=s\right\}. \tag{5}\]

Geometrically, \(T\) is the lower boundary of the convex hull of \((0,0)\) and the points \((f_i,c_if_i)\). It is finite and convex on \([0,1]\), and \(T(0)=0\).

Lemma. With \(\rho=1-e^{-1}\), for every \(0<t\le1\),

\[T(\rho t)<C(t). \tag{6}\]

Proof. Put \(s=\rho t\). Since all costs are positive, \(T(s)\ge c_1s>0\).

Because \(s\in(0,1)\), the finite convex polygon defining \(T\) has a supporting line at \(s\). Write it as

\(az-d,\qquad T(z)\ge az-d,\qquad T(s)=as-d.\)

Evaluating the supporting inequality at \(0\) gives \(d\ge0\). Since \(as-d=T(s)>0\), we also have \(a>0\) and \(d<as<at\).

Each threshold point satisfies

\(c_if_i\ge T(f_i)\ge af_i-d.\)

For any quantile \(z>0\), choose its cost index \(i\), so \(c(z)=c_i\) and \(f_i\ge z\). Therefore

\(c(z)\ge a-\frac{d}{f_i}\ge a-\frac{d}{z}.\)

Using positivity of costs,

\[c(z)\ge\max\left\{0,a-\frac{d}{z}\right\}. \tag{7}\]

If \(d=0\), integration gives \(C(t)\ge at>as=T(s)\).

Suppose \(d>0\), and set \(r=d/(at)\in(0,1)\). Integrating (7) gives

\[C(t)>\int_{d/a}^t\left(a-\frac{d}{z}\right)\,dz =at(1-r+r\log r). \tag{8}\]

The inequality is strict because the actual costs are positive on the nonempty interval \((0,d/a)\), where the lower bound in (7) is zero.

The function \(r\log r\) has derivative \(1+\log r\), second derivative \(1/r>0\), and minimum \(-e^{-1}\) at \(r=e^{-1}\). Hence

\(1-r+r\log r\ge \rho-r.\)

Combining this with (8),

\(C(t)>at(\rho-r)=as-d=T(s).\)

This proves (6). ∎

4. Approximation guarantee

At a truthful society, let \(C_j,T_j\) denote the normalized functions from Section 3 for group \(j\), and write \(t^*=\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)\).

The cheapest way to obtain representation fraction \(t\) in every group costs

\(\sum_j\lambda_jC_j(t).\)

Indeed, selection beyond fraction \(t\) can be discarded, and the remaining selection can be replaced by the cheapest mass in its group. Therefore

\[\sum_j\lambda_jC_j(t^*)\le B. \tag{9}\]

Also, \(t^*>0\): selecting a sufficiently small common fraction uniformly across all types is feasible because \(B>0\) and \(K\) is finite.

Apply the lemma group by group:

\(\sum_j\lambda_jT_j(\rho t^*)<\sum_j\lambda_jC_j(t^*)\le B.\)

Thus posted-price mixtures selecting fraction \(\rho t^*\) in every group are feasible in (3). Its optimum consequently satisfies

\(\mathrm{ALG}_M(\mu)\ge \rho\,\mathrm{OPT}(\mu).\)

It follows that

\[\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{OPT}(\mu)}{\mathrm{ALG}_M(\mu)} \le\frac1\rho=\frac{e}{e-1}. \tag{10}\]

The mechanism computes the rational LP optimum; it does not need to represent \(e\) or \(\rho\).

Arbitrary real report measures. If “every report measure” includes masses without finite binary encodings, a rational-output extension also exists. The strict budget slack above and continuity of the finite convex functions \(T_j\) permit a common fraction slightly greater than \(\rho t^*\). Scale its weights down slightly, retaining representation greater than \(\rho t^*\), to make every weight-sum constraint strict. Sufficiently close nonnegative rational weights then preserve these strict inequalities and budget feasibility. Define the extension by choosing the first suitable rational tuple in a fixed enumeration. Section 1 still proves all incentive properties. Polynomial-time computation applies to the binary-encoded inputs specified in the question.

5. Matching lower bound using finite rational instances

Fix any admissible group masses \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\). We construct a sequence of finite rational societies with those same masses.

Let \(n\) run through powers of two with \(n\ge4\), and put

\(r_n=\frac{\lfloor n/e\rfloor}{n},\qquad c_i=\max\left\{\frac1n,\ 1-\frac{r_n}{i/n}\right\}, \quad i=1,\ldots,n.\)

These are positive rational costs. Let \(K_n\) contain their distinct values. In each group, assign mass \(\lambda_j/n\) to each listed index, aggregating indices with identical costs.

Define

\(\overline c_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n c_i,\qquad B_n=\frac{\lceil n\overline c_n\rceil}{n}.\)

The group masses, type masses, and budget are valid binary-rational inputs. Selecting everybody costs \(\overline c_n\le B_n\), so

\[\mathrm{OPT}=1. \tag{11}\]

For any threshold cost \(c\in K_n\), let \(i\) be the last index with \(c_i=c\). Its normalized cumulative mass is \(f=i/n\), and the construction gives

\[cf=c_i(i/n)\ge i/n-r_n=f-r_n. \tag{12}\]

Now consider any truthful mechanism on this society. By Section 1, in group \(j\) its allocation has threshold weights \(x_{j,k}\ge0\) with \(\sum_kx_{j,k}\le1\), and its expenditure is at least the corresponding posted-price-mixture expenditure.

Write \(A_j\) for its selected fraction in group \(j\). From (12), its group expenditure is at least

\(\lambda_j\sum_k(f_k-r_n)x_{j,k} =\lambda_j\left(A_j-r_n\sum_kx_{j,k}\right) \ge\lambda_j(A_j-r_n).\)

Summing, using \(\sum_j\lambda_j=1\), and applying budget feasibility gives

\(B_n\ge\sum_j\lambda_jA_j-r_n \ge\min_j A_j-r_n.\)

Consequently, every truthful mechanism satisfies

\[\mathrm{ALG}_M\le B_n+r_n. \tag{13}\]

It remains to compute the limit. Define

\(g_r(z)=\max\{0,1-r/z\}\quad(z>0),\qquad g_r(0)=0.\)

This function is nondecreasing and bounded by \(1\). Its right-endpoint Riemann sum differs from its integral by at most \(1/n\). Replacing \(g_{r_n}(i/n)\) by \(c_i\) increases each summand by at most \(1/n\); rounding the budget adds less than another \(1/n\). Thus

\(\int_0^1g_{r_n}(z)\,dz \le B_n \le\int_0^1g_{r_n}(z)\,dz+\frac3n.\)

The integral is

\(\int_0^1g_r(z)\,dz =\int_r^1(1-r/z)\,dz =1-r+r\log r.\)

Since \(r_n\to e^{-1}\),

\[B_n+r_n\longrightarrow 1-e^{-1}=\rho. \tag{14}\]

Combining (11)–(14), every mechanism has approximation factor at least \(1/(B_n+r_n)\) on these instances, with the usual infinite-factor interpretation if its selection is zero. These lower bounds converge to \(1/\rho=e/(e-1)\).

Therefore no smaller uniform approximation factor is possible, regardless of computational efficiency.

Conclusion

The exact uniform worst-case factor is \(\frac{e}{e-1}\). Fractional selection under the stated zero-mass deviation model removes the dependence on \(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}\) entirely.
