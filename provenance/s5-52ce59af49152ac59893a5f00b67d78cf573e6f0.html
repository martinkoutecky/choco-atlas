The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite supported applicant types \(A\), recipient types \(B\), positive masses \(\mu_a\) with \(\sum_{a\in A}\mu_a=1\), complete preference preorders, and a fixed-support clone-symmetric scaled laminar quota function \(q\) on \(E\subseteq A\times B\), choose \(x_e\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_{b:(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\le\mu_a\) and \(x(S)\le q(S)\) for every \(S\subseteq E\). For \(e=(a,b)\), let \(W_e\) be type \(a\)'s unmatched mass plus its mass on other assignments weakly below \(e\), and let \(S_e\) be the mass strictly below \(e\). The recipient side weakly accepts \(e\) if, for some \(\epsilon>0\), either \(x+\epsilon\mathbf{1}_e\) is feasible for its quota constraints or \(x+\epsilon\mathbf{1}_e-\epsilon\mathbf{1}_f\) is feasible for some occupied \(f\) with \(e\succeq_H f\). It strictly accepts if direct addition is feasible or such an exchange exists with \(e\succ_H f\), matching the paper's addition and exchange cases. Does there exist a feasible \(x\) with no edge \(e\) for which \(W_e>0\), the recipient side weakly accepts, and either \(S_e>0\) or the recipient side strictly accepts; if so, construct such an \(x\)?

2. Recipient acceptance

Let $x$ be feasible. A quota $P$ is tight when $x(P)=c_P$. For $e\in E$, define
$$ \mathcal T_e(x)=\{P\in\mathcal P:e\in P,\ x(P)=c_P\}. $$
If this family is nonempty, its members form a chain: any two intersect at $e$, so laminarity makes them comparable. Write $C_e(x)$ for its smallest member.

Lemma 1. Direct addition at $e$ is possible exactly when $\mathcal T_e(x)$ is empty. If $\mathcal T_e(x)$ is nonempty, an exchange from $f\ne e$ with $x_f>0$ is possible exactly when $f\in C_e(x)$.

Proof. A tight quota containing $e$ prevents direct addition. If none is tight, choose a positive addition smaller than every relevant quota slack. There are finitely many quotas, so such an addition exists.

For an exchange, every tight quota containing $e$ must contain $f$: otherwise that quota increases above capacity. Because these quotas form a chain, this condition is equivalent to $f\in C_e(x)$.

Conversely, suppose $f\in C_e(x)$. Every tight quota containing $e$ also contains $f$, so its mass remains unchanged. Quotas containing $f$ but not $e$ decrease. Every quota that increases has positive slack. An exchange amount smaller than $x_f$ and all increasing quotas’ slacks preserves quotas and nonnegativity. $\square$

Thus, when $C_e(x)$ exists, weak recipient acceptance is equivalent to
$$ \exists f\in C_e(x)\setminus\{e\}: \quad x_f>0,\quad e\succeq_H f. $$
Strict acceptance replaces $\succeq_H$ by $\succ_H$.

We will also use the following immediate consequence: every feasible exchange at $e$ must remove mass from every tight quota containing $e$, even if that quota is not the smallest tight one.

$$ e\succ_H f\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \rho(e)<\rho(f). $$

| State | Constraints imposed on $x$ |
|---|---|
| Unselected | None. |
| Ordinary state $r$, where $r$ is a class meeting $P$ | $x(P)=c_P$, and $x_f=0$ for every $f\in P$ with $\rho(f)>r$. |
| Exceptional state $g$, where $g\in P$ | $x(P)=c_P$, and $x_f=0$ for every $f\in P\setminus\{g\}$ with $\rho(f)\ge\rho(g)$. |

The rank of an ordinary state is $r$; the rank of an exceptional state $g$ is $\rho(g)$.

The ordinary state allows several positive coordinates in its recorded rank. The exceptional state allows only $g$ in that rank. Neither state requires positive mass in the recorded rank. This deliberate relaxation keeps all constraints linear; the proof below shows that it is sound.

  • If $e$ belongs to no selected quota, require $W_e=0$.

  • if $\rho(e)=r$ and the state is exceptional $g\ne e$, require $S_e=0$;
  • in the remaining cases, impose no additional applicant constraint.

Theorem 2.
$$ \{x:x\text{ is strongly stable}\} = \bigcup_\sigma F_\sigma. $$

Sufficiency

Suppose $x\in F_\sigma$, and fix $e$.

If $W_e=0$ is imposed, $e$ cannot block. This covers edges without selected containing quotas and edges ranked better than their recorded quota rank.

Otherwise, $C=C^\sigma_e$ is tight. Direct addition at $e$ is therefore impossible, and every feasible exchange must remove a positive coordinate from $C$.

Let $r$ be its recorded rank.

If $\rho(e)>r$, every positive coordinate in $C$ has rank at most $r$ and is therefore strictly preferred to $e$. No weakly acceptable exchange exists.

If $\rho(e)=r$ and the state is ordinary, every positive coordinate in $C$ is at least as preferred as $e$. Consequently, strict recipient acceptance is impossible. The imposed equality $S_e=0$ prevents blocking.

If $\rho(e)=r$ and the state is exceptional $g\ne e$, the same argument rules out strict recipient acceptance, and again $S_e=0$ prevents blocking.

Finally, if the state is exceptional $g=e$, every positive coordinate in $C\setminus\{e\}$ is strictly preferred to $e$. Weak recipient acceptance is impossible.

These cases cover every edge, so $x$ is strongly stable.

Necessity

Suppose $x$ is strongly stable. Select exactly its tight quotas.

Each tight quota has positive mass because its capacity is positive. For each such $P$, define
$$ r_P=\max\{\rho(f):f\in P,\ x_f>0\}. $$
If exactly one coordinate $g$ of rank $r_P$ is positive, choose the exceptional state $g$. Otherwise choose the ordinary state $r_P$. All corresponding quota constraints hold.

Fix $e$. If no containing quota is tight, direct addition is possible. Since direct addition gives strict recipient acceptance, stability requires $W_e=0$.

Otherwise, $C^\sigma_e=C_e(x)$. Let $r=r_{C_e(x)}$.

If $\rho(e)<r$, a positive coordinate $f$ of rank $r$ is distinct from $e$. By Lemma 1, it permits a strictly acceptable exchange. Stability therefore requires $W_e=0$.

If $\rho(e)=r$ and the state is ordinary, at least two coordinates of rank $r$ are positive. At least one is distinct from $e$, so weak recipient acceptance holds. If $S_e>0$, then $W_e>0$ as well, and $e$ would block. Hence $S_e=0$.

If $\rho(e)=r$ and the state is exceptional $g\ne e$, exchanging from its positive coordinate $g$ gives weak recipient acceptance. The same reasoning requires $S_e=0$.

No applicant constraint was imposed in the remaining cases. Thus $x\in F_\sigma$. $\square$

$$ \mu_a=\mu_d=\frac14, \qquad \mu_b=\frac12. $$

$$ P_j=\{(a,j),(d,j),(b,j)\}, \qquad c_{P_j}=\frac12. $$

$$ (a,1)\succ_a(a,2), \qquad (d,1)\succ_d(d,2), \qquad (b,2)\succ_b(b,1). $$

$$ (b,1)\succ_H(a,1)\succ_H(d,1) \succ_H(a,2)\succ_H(d,2)\succ_H(b,2). $$

| Coordinate | $x$ | $y$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| $(a,1)$ | $1/4$ | $0$ |
| $(a,2)$ | $0$ | $1/4$ |
| $(d,1)$ | $1/4$ | $0$ |
| $(d,2)$ | $0$ | $1/4$ |
| $(b,1)$ | $0$ | $1/2$ |
| $(b,2)$ | $1/2$ | $0$ |

Both are feasible, and both quotas are tight.

At $x$, every applicant receives its entire mass at its first choice. Consequently, $W_e=0$ for every edge, and $x$ is strongly stable.

At $y$, the only edges with $W_e>0$ are the three applicants’ first choices. At $(a,1)$ and $(d,1)$, the only positive coordinate in $P_1$ is $(b,1)$, which the recipient strictly prefers. At $(b,2)$, both positive coordinates in $P_2$ are strictly preferred to it. Lemma 1 therefore rules out weak recipient acceptance at all three edges. Hence $y$ is strongly stable.
