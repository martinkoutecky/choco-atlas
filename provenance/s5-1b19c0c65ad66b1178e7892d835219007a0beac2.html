The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 4 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational potential levels \(z_j\) with conditional masses \(q_j\), disadvantaged share \(p\), bias factor \(\beta\), and voucher budget \(\hat c\), choose voucher masses \(x_j\) with \(0 \le x_j \le p q_j\) and \(\sum_j x_j \le p\hat c\). Advantaged students at level \(z_j\) are ranked by \(z_j\); disadvantaged students are ranked by \(z_j\) if vouchered and \(\beta z_j\) otherwise. Using expected ranks under uniform tie-breaking, find an allocation minimizing the maximum positive rank displacement of any disadvantaged type from its unbiased rank.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here there are \(m\) distinct potential levels \(1=z_1<\cdots<z_m\), and \(\tau=2m\) group–potential types.
Both groups have conditional masses \(q_j>0\), with \(\sum_j q_j=1\): advantaged and disadvantaged masses at level \(z_j\) are \((1-p)q_j\) and \(pq_j\). All inputs are rational, with \(0<\beta<1\), \(0<p<\sum_{j:\beta z_j<1}q_j\), and \(0\le\hat c\le1\).
Schools have unit total capacity and common rank order \([0,1]\), with smaller ranks better; all students share this order. Choose real voucher masses \(0\le x_j\le pq_j\), with \(\sum_jx_j\le p\hat c\). At level \(z_j\), masses \((1-p)q_j+x_j\) and \(pq_j-x_j\) have perceived scores \(z_j\) and \(\beta z_j\), respectively.
Let \(H_x\) be this perceived-score mass distribution. Uniform tie-breaking gives expected rank \(r_x(s)=H_x((s,\infty))+\tfrac12H_x(\{s\})\); the unbiased expected rank at level \(z_j\) is \(u_j=\sum_{k:z_k>z_j}q_k+\tfrac12q_j\).
Define \(M(x)\) as the maximum of \(\max\{0,r_x(s)-u_j\}\) over disadvantaged subpopulations of positive mass: include \(s=z_j\) when \(x_j>0\), and \(s=\beta z_j\) when \(pq_j-x_j>0\). Expectations concern tie-breaking only.
Given a rational threshold \(d\in[0,1]\), decide whether some feasible allocation satisfies \(M(x)\le d\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length, including \(d\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

The anchor characterizes the unique optimal deterministic voucher interval, up to null sets, for atomless \(\operatorname{Pareto}(1,\alpha)\) potentials under \(p<1-\beta^\alpha\); it establishes no discrete complexity classification. [Verified characterization](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.10846v5).

Result

The decision problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\). A forcing algorithm uses at most \(m\) iterations and \(O(m^2)\) exact arithmetic operations on numbers of polynomial bit length.

The proof treats the finite-support model directly and does not rely on the [cited atomless Pareto characterization](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.10846v5).

1. Two rank inequalities

For scores \(s,t\), define
\[ w(s,t)=\mathbf{1}_{\{t>s\}}+\tfrac12\mathbf{1}_{\{t=s\}}. \]
Thus, for fixed \(s\), the function \(w(s,t)\) is nondecreasing in \(t\), taking values \(0,\tfrac12,1\). The prescribed tie-breaking rule gives
\[ r_x(s)=\sum_{k=1}^m\left[((1-p)q_k+x_k)w(s,z_k)+(pq_k-x_k)w(s,\beta z_k)\right]. \]

Lemma 1: Voucher recipients never have positive mistreatment. For every allocation satisfying the coordinate bounds and every \(j\),
\[ r_x(z_j)\le u_j. \]

Indeed, \(\beta z_k<z_k\), so \(w(s,\beta z_k)\le w(s,z_k)\). Both masses in the \(k\)-th summand are nonnegative and sum to \(q_k\). Consequently,
\[ r_x(s)\le\sum_{k=1}^m q_k w(s,z_k). \]
At \(s=z_j\), the right-hand side equals \(u_j\), because the potential levels are distinct. This proves the lemma, including cases where some biased score equals another potential level.

Lemma 2: Ranks at fixed scores are nondecreasing in voucher masses. Define
\[ a_k(s)=w(s,z_k)-w(s,\beta z_k). \]
Then \(a_k(s)\in\{0,\tfrac12,1\}\), and
\[ r_x(s)=r_0(s)+\sum_{k=1}^m a_k(s)x_k. \]
Hence, if \(x_k\le y_k\) for every \(k\), then \(r_x(s)\le r_y(s)\) for every \(s\).

Because \(d\ge0\), Lemma 1 makes every constraint concerning voucher recipients automatic. Therefore the exact threshold condition is
\[ M(x)\le d \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r_x(\beta z_j)\le u_j+d \ \text{for every }j\text{ with }x_j<pq_j. \]
The strict condition \(x_j<pq_j\) is precisely the requirement that the unvouchered subpopulation have positive mass.

2. Partial vouchers are unnecessary

Suppose \(x\) satisfies the coordinate bounds, budget, and threshold. Define \(y_j=pq_j\) when \(x_j=pq_j\), and \(y_j=0\) otherwise.

Then \(y\le x\) coordinatewise, so its voucher cost does not increase. If \(y_j=0\), then \(x_j<pq_j\), and feasibility of \(x\) together with Lemma 2 gives
\[ r_y(\beta z_j)\le r_x(\beta z_j)\le u_j+d. \]
If \(y_j=pq_j\), the type has only voucher recipients, whose constraint holds by Lemma 1.

Thus \(y\) is feasible. In particular, every yes-instance has a witness that vouchers either all or none of the disadvantaged mass at each potential level.

This observation alone leaves exponentially many subsets. The next argument eliminates that search.

3. The forcing algorithm

For a subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), let \(x^S_j=pq_j\) when \(j\in S\), and \(x^S_j=0\) otherwise.

Use the following algorithm:

  • Initialize \(S=\varnothing\).
  • If some \(j\notin S\) satisfies \(r_{x^S}(\beta z_j)>u_j+d\), add one such \(j\) to \(S\) and repeat.
  • When no such index remains, accept exactly when \(\sum_{j\in S}pq_j\le p\hat c\).

Each iteration adds an index, so there are at most \(m\) additions. All tests use the stated midpoint tie-breaking rule. Equality with the threshold does not trigger an addition.

4. Correctness

We prove the following invariant:

Every allocation \(y\) satisfying the coordinate bounds and \(M(y)\le d\) must satisfy \(y_k=pq_k\) for every \(k\in S\).

The invariant does not assume a budget bound.

Initially it holds because \(S\) is empty. Suppose it holds for the current \(S\), and the algorithm selects \(j\notin S\) with
\[ r_{x^S}(\beta z_j)>u_j+d. \]
Consider any allocation \(y\) satisfying the coordinate bounds and threshold. By the invariant, \(y_k=x^S_k=pq_k\) on \(S\); outside \(S\), \(y_k\ge0=x^S_k\). Thus \(y\ge x^S\) coordinatewise, and Lemma 2 implies
\[ r_y(\beta z_j)\ge r_{x^S}(\beta z_j)>u_j+d. \]
If \(y_j<pq_j\), the unvouchered subpopulation at level \(z_j\) has positive mass, so this inequality contradicts \(M(y)\le d\). Therefore \(y_j=pq_j\), establishing the invariant after the addition.

Let \(S_*\) be the terminal set.

For every \(j\notin S_*\), termination gives
\[ r_{x^{S_*}}(\beta z_j)\le u_j+d. \]
For every \(j\in S_*\), all disadvantaged students receive vouchers, and Lemma 1 gives \(r_{x^{S_*}}(z_j)\le u_j\). Consequently, \(M(x^{S_*})\le d\).

If the terminal cost is at most \(p\hat c\), this allocation is a valid witness, so acceptance is correct.

If the terminal cost exceeds \(p\hat c\), the invariant says every threshold-satisfying allocation \(y\) must voucher all types in \(S_*\). Since all other coordinates are nonnegative,
\[ \sum_{j=1}^m y_j\ge\sum_{j\in S_*}pq_j>p\hat c. \]
No allocation can satisfy both the threshold and budget, so rejection is correct.

In fact, the algorithm constructs the coordinatewise least allocation satisfying the coordinate bounds and threshold.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

Precompute
\[ R_j=r_0(\beta z_j), \qquad a_{jk}=w(\beta z_j,z_k)-w(\beta z_j,\beta z_k), \]
and all benchmarks \(u_j\). This takes \(O(m^2)\) score comparisons and arithmetic operations.

Maintain \(R_j=r_{x^S}(\beta z_j)\). When index \(k\) is added, update every rank by
\[ R_j\leftarrow R_j+pq_k a_{jk}. \]
Each addition requires \(O(m)\) updates. Scanning all remaining indices after each addition also costs \(O(m)\). Since there are at most \(m\) additions, the total is \(O(m^2)\) arithmetic operations and comparisons.

These operations can be performed exactly with polynomial bit complexity. To see this explicitly, let \(D\) be twice the product of the input denominators of \(p,\hat c,d,q_1,\ldots,q_m\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Every rank, benchmark, threshold, voucher cost, and rank increment used by the algorithm is an integer multiple of \(D^{-1}\). The ranks lie in \([0,1]\), the thresholds \(u_j+d\) lie in \([0,2]\), and the costs lie in \([0,1]\), so their scaled integer numerators also have \(O(L)\) bits.

Score comparisons, including equalities \(\beta z_j=z_k\), can be decided by exact rational multiplication and cross-multiplication using integers of \(O(L)\) bits. Thus the total running time is \(O(m^2\operatorname{poly}(L))\), polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), since \(\tau=2m\).

The additional restriction \(p<\sum_{j:\beta z_j<1}q_j\) is not needed for this proof; the algorithm applies in particular to all instances specified in the question.
