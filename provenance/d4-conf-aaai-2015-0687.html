The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(K\), a rational mass vector \(\mu\) with \(\sum_{j\in K}\mu_j=1\), and a rational polynomial \(f_\mu(x)\) in dense or unary-degree encoding with \(f_\mu(0)=0\), where \(f_\mu(x)\) is the worth of a coalition of type-mass \(x\) against its complement as one rival coalition, compute the exact continuous McQuillin allocation \(A_j=\mu_j a_j\), where \(a_j=\int_0^1\partial_j f_\mu(t\mu)\,dt\). Equivalently, compute the limit of the type allocations in replica games with \(q\mu_j\) identical agents of each type, as \(q\) ranges over multiples of the common denominator of \(\mu\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(K\) of \(\tau\) types with positive masses \(\mu_j\) encoded as binary rationals, with \(\sum_{j\in K}\mu_j=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; the number of mass coordinates is \(m=\tau\).
The input also contains a polynomial \(f_\mu\in\mathbb{Q}[x_j:j\in K]\) of total degree \(d\), with \(f_\mu(0)=0\), represented either by a dense coefficient table or by an explicit monomial list with unary exponents. Coefficients are binary rationals of either sign; no monotonicity or nonnegativity is assumed. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A coalition with type masses \(0\leq x_j\leq\mu_j\) has worth \(f_\mu(x)\) when its complement forms one rival coalition; at \(x=\mu\), the rival is absent. Its continuous McQuillin allocation is the vector of exact rationals \(A_j=\mu_j a_j\), where \(a_j=\int_0^1\partial_j f_\mu(t\mu)\,dt\).
For the replica interpretation, each of the \(q\mu_j\) agents of type \(j\) has mass \(1/q\), and the induced coalition worth is \(w_q(S)=f_\mu((|S\cap N_j|/q)_{j\in K})\). McQuillin payoffs are the ordinary Shapley payoffs of this induced worth function; type allocations sum the payoffs of all agents of that type.

Is computing the exact continuous McQuillin allocation solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,d,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite games represented by partition decision trees of total size \(B\), McQuillin values are computable in \(O(nB)\) time, where \(n\) is the number of agents. [Skibski et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9306)
The continuous formula is the Aumann–Shapley allocation; for explicitly represented multilinear functions with \(s\) nonzero monomials, each coordinate is computable in \(O(\tau^2s)\) arithmetic operations. This restricted result does not cover arbitrary polynomial inputs as stated here. [Sun and Sundararajan](https://arxiv.org/html/1102.0989)

Result

Computing the exact continuous McQuillin allocation is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,d,L\), for both input representations in the question. The algorithm handles arbitrary exponents and coefficients of either sign. Its running time is polynomial in the bit model, not merely in the number of arithmetic operations.

1. Exact formula for arbitrary monomials

Index the support by \(K=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), where \(m=\tau\). Write the explicitly represented polynomial as

\( \displaystyle f_\mu(x)=\sum_{r=1}^{s}c_r x^{\alpha^{(r)}},\qquad x^\alpha=\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_i^{\alpha_i}. \)

Discard zero coefficients and constant terms. This preserves the polynomial because its total constant coefficient is zero by \(f_\mu(0)=0\). For every remaining term, let

\( \displaystyle k_r=|\alpha^{(r)}|=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_i^{(r)},\qquad 1\leq k_r\leq d. \)

Consider one monomial \(c_r x^{\alpha^{(r)}}\). If \(\alpha_j^{(r)}=0\), its contribution to \(A_j\) is zero. Otherwise, differentiation and substitution give

\( \displaystyle \mu_j\partial_j\!\left(c_r x^{\alpha^{(r)}}\right)\big|_{x=t\mu} =c_r\alpha_j^{(r)}t^{k_r-1}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mu_i^{\alpha_i^{(r)}}. \)

Since \(k_r\geq1\), integrating \(t^{k_r-1}\) from zero to one gives \(1/k_r\). Differentiation and integration distribute over the finite monomial sum. Therefore

\( \displaystyle \boxed{A_j=\sum_{r=1}^{s}c_r\frac{\alpha_j^{(r)}}{k_r}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mu_i^{\alpha_i^{(r)}}.} \)

This calculation does not require multilinearity, monotonicity, or nonnegative coefficients.

As a consistency check, summing over coordinates yields

\( \displaystyle \sum_{j=1}^{m}A_j =\sum_{r=1}^{s}c_r\left(\frac{\sum_j\alpha_j^{(r)}}{k_r}\right)\mu^{\alpha^{(r)}} =f_\mu(\mu). \)

2. An exact integer algorithm

If no nonzero terms remain, return the zero vector. Otherwise \(d\geq1\). Write the input rationals as

\( \displaystyle \mu_i=\frac{p_i}{q_i},\qquad c_r=\frac{u_r}{b_r}, \)

with positive denominators. Define the common denominator

\( \displaystyle T=\left(\prod_{r=1}^{s}b_r\right)d!\prod_{i=1}^{m}q_i^d. \)

For each monomial, compute the integers

\( \displaystyle P_r=\prod_i p_i^{\alpha_i^{(r)}},\qquad Q_r=\prod_i q_i^{\alpha_i^{(r)}},\qquad V_r=b_rk_rQ_r. \)

The integer \(V_r\) divides \(T\): \(b_r\) divides \(\prod_\ell b_\ell\), \(k_r\) divides \(d!\), and \(Q_r\) divides \(\prod_i q_i^d\) because every exponent is at most \(d\). Multiplying these three divisibilities establishes \(V_r\mid T\).

Initialize \(N_j=0\) for all coordinates. For each monomial, update

\( \displaystyle N_j\leftarrow N_j+u_r\alpha_j^{(r)}P_r(T/V_r). \)

The quotient \(T/V_r\) is exact. Furthermore,

\( \displaystyle \frac{u_r\alpha_j^{(r)}P_r(T/V_r)}{T} =c_r\frac{\alpha_j^{(r)}}{k_r}\mu^{\alpha^{(r)}}. \)

Thus the final answer is \(A_j=N_j/T\). Reduce each fraction using a greatest common divisor, with zero represented canonically.

3. Bit complexity

Let \(\ell(z)\) denote the binary length of an integer, including a sign bit when needed. Because the rational data are part of the input,

\( \displaystyle \sum_r\ell(b_r)\leq O(L),\qquad \sum_i\ell(q_i)\leq O(L), \)

and each input numerator has length \(O(L)\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \ell(T)=O\!\left((d+1)L+d\log(d+1)\right). \)

Also, since \(\sum_i\alpha_i^{(r)}=k_r\leq d\),

\( \displaystyle \ell(P_r),\ell(Q_r)=O(dL). \)

Each integer summand added to \(N_j\) therefore has bit length at most

\( \displaystyle O\!\left((d+1)L+d\log(d+1)\right). \)

Summing at most \(s\) signed integers increases this bound by at most \(O(\log(s+1))\): the absolute value of a sum is bounded by the sum of the absolute values. Thus cancellation causes no difficulty, and all intermediate integers and final output fractions have polynomial bit length.

Using repeated multiplication, constructing \(T\) takes \(O(s+md+d)\) integer operations. Computing all monomial products and updating all coordinates takes \(O(s(m+d))\) additional integer operations. Integer addition, multiplication, exact division, and greatest common divisor computation all have polynomial running time in their operand bit lengths. Hence the entire algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(m,d,L,s\).

For an explicit monomial list, \(s\) is at most the number of listed entries and hence \(O(L)\). For a dense coefficient table, scanning its explicitly stored entries takes polynomial time in \(L\), and again \(s=O(L)\). Neither representation requires expanding a succinctly encoded polynomial.

This proves polynomial bit complexity in \(m,\tau,d,L\).

4. Relation to the stated background

The cited [Sun–Sundararajan paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1102.0989) studies efficient attribution for explicitly represented multilinear functions. The calculation above directly covers the arbitrary polynomial inputs here: each monomial contributes according to its exponent vector divided by its total degree.

The finite-replica interpretation does not require constructing a replica game for this computation. The target allocation is given by the continuous integral, which the formula above evaluates exactly.
