The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(\Theta\) with rational arrival masses \(\mu_{\theta,t}\), where each type fixes its timing constraints, rate limit, maximum integral quantity, and valuation \(v_\theta(k)\), does there exist a causal allocation policy and a constant \(p>0\) such that \(p\,SW_{\mathrm{online}}\ge SW_{\mathrm{offline}}\) on every instance? The policy may split each type's mass among schedules, but each individual receives an integral number of units per period and past allocations cannot be revoked. At time \(t\), it observes the actual nondecreasing marginal procurement cost curve \(c_t(q)\); future curves are unknown but subject to the instance's stated forecasts and upper bounds. Procurement of mass \(q\) costs \(\int_0^q c_t(z)\,dz\), and the offline benchmark has the same feasibility constraints and knows all future arrivals and costs.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Time consists of \(m\) discrete periods. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Masses need not sum to one. Each type \(\theta\) specifies arrival \(a_\theta\), confirmation deadline \(d_\theta\), receiving interval \([\alpha_\theta,\beta_\theta]\), positive integral rate \(r_\theta\le r_{\max}\), positive integral capacity \(Q_\theta\), and an explicitly tabulated nonnegative rational valuation \(v_\theta(k)\), nondecreasing with \(v_\theta(0)=0\). Require \(1\le a_\theta\le d_\theta\le\beta_\theta\le m\) and \(a_\theta\le\alpha_\theta\le\beta_\theta\). Its mass \(\mu_\theta=\mu_{\theta,a_\theta}>0\) arrives together.
An individual schedule \(s\) has integral deliveries \(s_t\), with \(0\le s_t\le r_\theta\), zero outside the receiving interval, and \(\sum_t s_t\le Q_\theta\). The policy may split a type's mass among schedules. Past deliveries are irrevocable, and each individual's final total must be irrevocably fixed by \(d_\theta\); future delivery times may still change. Procurement is unlimited and must be allocated immediately.
Initially the policy knows \(m\), \(r_{\max}\), and nonnegative rational piecewise constant forecast and upper-bound curves \(\widetilde c_t\le\overline c_t\). At period \(t\), it learns that period's arriving types and masses and the actual nonnegative, nondecreasing rational piecewise constant curve \(c_t\le\overline c_t\), before delivering. Forecasts impose no further accuracy or probabilistic promise. Future arrivals and actual curves remain unknown.
If final schedule masses are \(x_{\theta,s}\ge0\), with \(\sum_s x_{\theta,s}=\mu_\theta\), define \(q_t=\sum_{\theta,s}x_{\theta,s}s_t\) and \(SW=\sum_{\theta,s}x_{\theta,s}v_\theta(\sum_t s_t)-\sum_t\int_0^{q_t}c_t(z)\,dz\). The offline optimum knows all arrivals and costs and obeys the same feasibility and commitment constraints. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; express running times in \(m,\tau,L,Q_{\max},r_{\max}\), where \(Q_{\max}=\max_\theta Q_\theta\).

Does there exist a deterministic causal allocation policy and a universal finite constant \(p>0\) such that \(p\,SW_{\mathrm{online}}\ge SW_{\mathrm{offline}}\) on every admissible instance, or is every such policy without a bounded competitive ratio?

For finite individual agents, no deterministic online allocation mechanism has a bounded welfare competitive ratio under uncertain future procurement costs ([Hayakawa et al., 2018](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2018/pdfs/p309.pdf)).
Related procurement allocation work analyzes competitive guarantees when the increasing procurement cost function is known in advance ([Ray et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12457)); no result settling the exact mass-and-uncertain-cost model above was identified.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Every deterministic causal allocation policy has an unbounded welfare competitive ratio.

This holds already with \(m=3\), \(\tau=1\), \(Q_{\max}=3\), and \(r_{\max}=1\), with arrival at the start of the receiving interval and confirmation deadline at its end. Both instances in the construction have strictly positive offline optimum.

Two indistinguishable instances

Fix any deterministic causal policy \(A\) and any proposed finite constant \(p>0\). Choose an integer \(N\ge 1\) such that \(2^N>2p\), and set \(\varepsilon=2^{-N}\). Thus \(0<\varepsilon<1\) and \(2p\varepsilon<1\).

There is one supported type, of mass \(1\), with parameters

\(a_\theta=\alpha_\theta=1,\qquad d_\theta=\beta_\theta=3,\qquad r_\theta=1,\qquad Q_\theta=3.\)

Its explicitly tabulated valuation is

\(v_\theta(0)=0,\qquad v_\theta(1)=v_\theta(2)=\varepsilon,\qquad v_\theta(3)=2.\)

This valuation is nonnegative and nondecreasing. All required timing inequalities hold.

The initial forecasts are identically zero. The upper-bound curves are constant in procurement quantity:

\(\overline c_1(z)=0,\qquad \overline c_2(z)=1,\qquad \overline c_3(z)=3\quad\text{for every }z\ge 0.\)

Consider two actual-cost instances:

| Instance | \(c_1(z)\) | \(c_2(z)\) | \(c_3(z)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(I_H\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(3\) |
| \(I_L\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |

Every curve is nonnegative, rational, piecewise constant, nondecreasing in quantity, and bounded by its announced upper-bound curve.

The policy receives identical information through its period-two deliveries. Determinism and causality therefore imply that its period-two procurement is the same on both instances. Denote this quantity by \(x\). Because the total mass is \(1\) and each individual can receive at most one unit per period, \(0\le x\le 1\).

Welfare bounds that allow arbitrary mass splitting

Consider any feasible final allocation on either instance. Write \(y=q_3\), and let \(h\) be the mass receiving a final total of three units.

An individual receiving three units must receive one unit in each period: there are exactly three periods, and its rate is one. Consequently,

\(h\le x\qquad\text{and}\qquad h\le y.\)

Every individual outside this mass has valuation at most \(\varepsilon\). Hence aggregate value is at most

\(2h+\varepsilon(1-h)=\varepsilon+(2-\varepsilon)h.\)

These inequalities apply to arbitrary fractional schedule masses.

On \(I_H\), procurement costs are \(x+3y\). Thus

\(SW_H\le\varepsilon+(2-\varepsilon)h-x-3y \le\varepsilon-x-(1+\varepsilon)y \le\varepsilon-x.\)

Here the second inequality uses \(h\le y\) and \(2-\varepsilon>0\).

On \(I_L\), procurement costs are \(x\). Using \(h\le x\) gives

\(SW_L\le\varepsilon+(2-\varepsilon)h-x \le\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)x.\)

Exact offline optima

The preceding bounds also apply to offline allocations.

On \(I_H\), welfare is at most \(\varepsilon\). Giving every individual the schedule \((1,0,0)\) achieves value \(\varepsilon\) at zero procurement cost. Therefore,

\(SW_{\mathrm{offline}}(I_H)=\varepsilon>0.\)

On \(I_L\), since \(x\le 1\) and \(1-\varepsilon>0\), welfare is at most

\(\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)x\le 1.\)

Giving every individual the schedule \((1,1,1)\) achieves value \(2\) and procurement cost \(1\). Therefore,

\(SW_{\mathrm{offline}}(I_L)=1.\)

Both schedules satisfy the delivery constraints. The offline policy can fix every individual's final total upon arrival, so the confirmation constraint is also satisfied.

Failure of every proposed constant

Apply the bounds to the common period-two quantity \(x\) chosen by \(A\).

  • If \(x\ge\varepsilon\), then \(SW_A(I_H)\le 0\). Consequently, \(p\,SW_A(I_H)\le 0<\varepsilon=SW_{\mathrm{offline}}(I_H)\).
  • If \(x<\varepsilon\), then \(SW_A(I_L)\le\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)x<2\varepsilon\). Consequently, \(p\,SW_A(I_L)<2p\varepsilon<1=SW_{\mathrm{offline}}(I_L)\).

Thus at least one of the two admissible instances violates the proposed guarantee. The two complete instances are specified in advance; the argument only selects which one witnesses failure for the fixed deterministic policy.

Because \(A\) and \(p>0\) were arbitrary, no deterministic causal allocation policy admits a universal finite welfare competitive ratio.

Input size and computation

The construction fixes \(m=3\), \(\tau=1\), \(Q_{\max}=3\), and \(r_{\max}=1\). Under ordinary binary rational encoding, its input length is \(L=\Theta(N)\), and either instance can be written in \(O(L)\) bit operations.

The impossibility holds without any restriction on the policy's running time.
