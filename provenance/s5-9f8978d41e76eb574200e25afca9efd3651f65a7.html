The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite complete type sets \(T_A,T_B\), a finite outcome set \(O\), rational masses \(\mu_A,\mu_B\), utilities \(u_A,u_B\), and threshold \(G\), consider a high-multiplicity population of independent two-role units with pair mass \(\mu_A(t)\mu_B(s)\). Does there exist a deterministic mechanism \(f:T_A\times T_B\to O\) satisfying the Bayes–Nash truthfulness inequalities \(\sum_{s\in T_B}\mu_B(s)u_A(t,f(t,s))\ge\sum_{s\in T_B}\mu_B(s)u_A(t,f(t',s))\) and the analogous inequalities for role \(B\), while achieving \(\sum_{t,s}\mu_A(t)\mu_B(s)(u_A(t,f(t,s))+u_B(s,f(t,s)))\ge G\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here the two roles have finite nonempty supports \(T_A,T_B\), with \(\tau=|T_A|+|T_B|\), and each role's masses sum to \(1\). These supports are also the complete report domains.
There is a finite nonempty outcome set \(O\), with \(m=|O|\). Utilities \(u_A:T_A\times O\to\mathbb Q\), \(u_B:T_B\times O\to\mathbb Q\), and threshold \(G\in\mathbb Q\) are explicitly encoded in binary and may have either sign. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length. There are no side payments.
Independent two-role units have pair mass \(\mu_A(t)\mu_B(s)\). One deterministic mechanism \(f:T_A\times T_B\to O\) applies to every unit; every unit with the same report pair receives the same outcome.
Truthfulness requires, for all \(t,t'\in T_A\), \(\sum_{s\in T_B}\mu_B(s)u_A(t,f(t,s))\ge\sum_{s\in T_B}\mu_B(s)u_A(t,f(t',s))\), and, for all \(s,s'\in T_B\), \(\sum_{t\in T_A}\mu_A(t)u_B(s,f(t,s))\ge\sum_{t\in T_A}\mu_A(t)u_B(s,f(t,s'))\). Indifference permits truthful reporting.
Feasibility also requires \(\sum_{t\in T_A,s\in T_B}\mu_A(t)\mu_B(s)[u_A(t,f(t,s))+u_B(s,f(t,s))]\ge G\).

Is deciding the existence of such a mechanism solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Known results: Two-agent deterministic Bayes–Nash mechanism design without payments is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even for expected social welfare and independent rational type distributions; allowing randomized outcomes gives a polynomial-time algorithm. [Conitzer and Sandholm (2002)](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/amdUAI02.pdf).
The stated mass formulation has exactly the same input, mechanism choices, inequalities, and objective as the deterministic problem already covered by that result; it therefore supplies no separate open continuous problem.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized each role's masses to sum to one, matching the independent probability distributions in the discrete definition.
  • Specified nonempty supports and outcome set, and identified the supported types with the complete report domains.
  • Specified explicitly encoded binary rational utilities and threshold, allowing negative values as in the discrete setting.
  • Made the absence of side payments explicit and retained weak truthfulness and welfare inequalities.
  • Defined m as the outcome count, tau as the total support size across roles, and L as the total input bit length.

Conclusion

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Thus it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) exists if and only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Applying the known hardness theorem

We use the known result supplied in the question: two-agent deterministic mechanism design without payments, with independent rational type distributions, Bayes–Nash truthfulness, and an expected social-welfare threshold, is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This is Theorem 2 of [Conitzer and Sandholm (2002)](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~conitzer/amdUAI02.pdf).

The reduction to the present problem is the identity transformation:

  • The two agents become roles \(A,B\), with the same supported types, probabilities, and utilities.
  • The outcome set and threshold remain unchanged.
  • An outcome function becomes the same table \(f:T_A\times T_B\to O\).

We verify that this transformation preserves feasibility. Independence makes the conditional distribution of the other role’s type equal to its marginal distribution. Consequently, the expected utility of true type \(t\) reporting \(t'\), when role \(B\) reports truthfully, is exactly
\[ \sum_{s\in T_B}\mu_B(s)u_A(t,f(t',s)). \]
Comparing this expression with truthful reporting gives precisely the first family of inequalities in the question. The same calculation for role \(B\) gives the second family. Both comparisons are weak inequalities, so indifference is treated identically.

Likewise, expected social welfare is exactly
\[ \sum_{t\in T_A,\ s\in T_B} \mu_A(t)\mu_B(s) \bigl[u_A(t,f(t,s))+u_B(s,f(t,s))\bigr]. \]
Thus the welfare thresholds agree. The transformation preserves yes-instances and no-instances and has linear encoding cost.

The positive-support restriction is also compatible with the cited hardness construction: its first-role probabilities are \(w_j/\sum_i w_i\), and its second-role probabilities are \(1/2\). One may restrict the source knapsack problem to positive integer weights. That restriction remains \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard: positive-integer subset sum reduces to it by taking each item’s weight and value equal to its subset-sum integer, and taking capacity and value threshold both equal to the target. All constructed types then have strictly positive mass.

Therefore the present problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Write \(n=|T_A|\) and \(k=|T_B|\), so \(n+k=\tau\). A certificate specifies an outcome index for every report pair. Its length is
\[ O\bigl(nk\log(m+1)\bigr) = O\bigl(\tau^2\log(m+1)\bigr), \]
which is polynomial in the explicit input length.

Given this table, the verifier checks all \(n^2+k^2\) truthfulness inequalities and the welfare inequality. The total number of summands examined is
\[ O(n^2k+k^2n+nk), \]
which is polynomial in \(\tau\).

These checks can use exact arithmetic. Let \(D\) be the product of all positive denominators appearing in the input rationals, including the threshold. Its binary length is \(O(L)\), and every input denominator divides \(D\).

A truthfulness summand multiplies a mass by a utility, so its denominator divides \(D^2\). A welfare summand multiplies two masses by a utility, so its denominator divides \(D^3\). Multiplying the respective inequalities by these positive integers therefore converts them into exact integer comparisons without changing their direction. The resulting integers have polynomial bit length, including after summation. Hence verification takes polynomial time.

The problem consequently belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\), completing the proof of \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

Interpretation of the mass formulation

The rational masses are fixed input data. They introduce no additional continuous decision variables: the mechanism still chooses one outcome for each of finitely many report pairs. The stated formulation is therefore covered by the deterministic hardness result.
