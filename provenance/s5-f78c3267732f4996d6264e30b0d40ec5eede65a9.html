Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result. The problem is polynomial-time solvable. An exact optimum is a weighted median of the combined distribution. Under the standard encoding of a rational as a binary numerator and denominator, the algorithm below takes \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2)\) bit operations. Its running time therefore is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

1. Construct the combined distribution exactly.

For each location \(t\), define
\[ w_t=\lambda\alpha_t+(1-\lambda)\mu_t, \]
interpreting an unlisted mass as zero. These weights are nonnegative and satisfy
\[ \sum_t w_t =\lambda\sum_t\alpha_t+(1-\lambda)\sum_t\mu_t =1. \]
Only locations in the combined support need to be considered.

Write the input rationals as
\[ \lambda=\frac{a}{b},\qquad \alpha_t=\frac{A_t}{B_t},\qquad \mu_t=\frac{U_t}{V_t}, \]
where denominators are positive and \(0\le a\le b\). Fractions need not be reduced. Set
\[ D=b \prod_{t\in\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)}B_t \prod_{t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}V_t. \]
For each location in the combined support, compute the integer
\[ N_t= \begin{cases} aA_t\,\dfrac{D}{bB_t},&t\in\operatorname{supp}(\alpha),\\ 0,&t\notin\operatorname{supp}(\alpha) \end{cases} + \begin{cases} (b-a)U_t\,\dfrac{D}{bV_t},&t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu),\\ 0,&t\notin\operatorname{supp}(\mu). \end{cases} \]
Every displayed division is exact because its denominator divides \(D\). Consequently,
\[ N_t=Dw_t,\qquad N_t\ge0,\qquad \sum_tN_t=D. \]

Sort the distinct combined-support locations as
\[ s_1<s_2<\cdots<s_\tau. \]
Define \(P_0=0\) and \(P_j=\sum_{i=1}^jN_{s_i}\). Choose
\[ k=\min\{j:2P_j\ge D\}, \]
and output \(y=s_k\). Such an index exists because \(P_\tau=D\). By its definition,
\[ 2P_{k-1}<D\le2P_k. \]
The construction also covers \(\lambda=0\) and \(\lambda=1\); some combined weights may simply be zero.

2. Prove global optimality over the real line.

Put \(x=s_k\). We show that \(C(y)\ge C(x)\) for every real \(y\).

First suppose \(y\ge x\), and let \(h=y-x\). For \(t\le x\),
\[ |t-y|-|t-x|=h. \]
For \(t>x\), the triangle inequality gives
\[ |t-y|-|t-x|\ge-h. \]
Multiplying by the nonnegative weights and summing therefore yields
\[ C(y)-C(x) \ge h\left(\sum_{t\le x}w_t-\sum_{t>x}w_t\right) =h\left(\frac{2P_k}{D}-1\right) \ge0. \]

Now suppose \(y\le x\), and let \(h=x-y\). For \(t\ge x\),
\[ |t-y|-|t-x|=h, \]
while for \(t<x\), the triangle inequality gives
\[ |t-y|-|t-x|\ge-h. \]
Hence
\[ C(y)-C(x) \ge h\left(\sum_{t\ge x}w_t-\sum_{t<x}w_t\right) =h\left(1-\frac{2P_{k-1}}{D}\right) \ge0. \]
These two cases cover every real facility location, proving that the output is an exact minimizer.

3. Verify the binary-input running-time bound.

The two support lists contain at most \(2\tau\) records. Sorting their locations and merging equal locations requires \(O(\tau\log(\tau+1))\) rational comparisons. To compare locations \(r/s\) and \(u/v\), with \(s,v>0\), compare the integers \(rv\) and \(us\). Each operand has \(O(L)\) bits, so schoolbook arithmetic performs a comparison in \(O(L^2)\) bit operations.

The bit length of \(D\) is \(O(L)\): the logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms of its factors, and all denominator encodings together occupy at most \(L\) bits. Thus forming \(D\) requires \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

Each contribution to \(N_t\) uses a constant number of multiplications and exact divisions on integers of \(O(L)\) bits, including intermediate products. All contributions therefore take \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations. Moreover, every prefix sum lies between \(0\) and \(D\), so the final scan also uses integers of \(O(L)\) bits.

The total bound is consequently
\[ O\!\left(\tau\log(\tau+1)L^2\right). \]
The output is an input location, so its encoding has at most \(L\) bits. This establishes the required bound directly for rational support input, independently of the cited quantile-based result.
