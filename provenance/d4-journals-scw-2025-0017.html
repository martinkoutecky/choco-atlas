The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational masses \(\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3>0\) summing to \(1\) and the five options \(X_1,X_2,X_3,Y,Z\), with type \(t\) valuing \(X_t\) at \(1\), the other favorite options at \(0\), \(Z\) at \(v_Z<1\), and \(Y\) at \(v_Y<1\) for types \(1,2\) and \(0\) for type \(3\), determine whether weighted MaxParC has a strong Nash equilibrium with \(p_Z=1\), under Proposition 2’s parameter assumptions. Each faction controls a measurable rating assignment over its mass, and the weighted rule replaces voter counts by approval masses and rating sums by integrals.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here \(\tau=3\), \(m=5\), and \(L\) is the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Let \(C=\{X_1,X_2,X_3,Y,Z\}\). Input masses satisfy \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and \(\mu_1\geq\mu_2\). Input utilities satisfy \(v_Y\in\mathbb Q\), \(v_Y<1\), and \(v_Z\in\mathbb Q\), \(\max_t\mu_t<v_Z<1\), \(v_Z>(\mu_1+\mu_2)v_Y\); negative \(v_Y\) is allowed.
Type \(t\) has \(u_t(X_t)=1\), \(u_t(X_j)=0\) for \(j\ne t\), and \(u_t(Z)=v_Z\); also \(u_1(Y)=u_2(Y)=v_Y\), \(u_3(Y)=0\).
Represent the society by disjoint intervals \(F_t\) of Lebesgue mass \(\mu_t\), with union \(E\) and measure \(\lambda\). Faction \(t\) chooses an arbitrary measurable pure rating assignment \(r:F_t\to[0,1]^C\); assignments are identified almost everywhere.
Weighted MaxParC uses the largest measurable set \(A_x\subseteq E\), modulo null sets, satisfying \(\lambda(A_x)+r_x(a)>1\) for almost every \(a\in A_x\). Put \(s_x=\lambda(A_x)+\int_E r_x\,d\lambda\), \(D=\bigcup_x A_x\), and \(W(a)=\arg\max_{x:a\in A_x}s_x\) for \(a\in D\).
If \(\lambda(D)>0\), output \(p_x=\lambda(D)^{-1}\int_D\mathbf1_{\{x\in W(a)\}}/|W(a)|\,d\lambda(a)\). Otherwise output the uniform lottery on \(\arg\max_{x\in C}s_x\).
Faction \(t\)'s payoff is \(\pi_t(p)=\mu_t\sum_{x\in C}p_xu_t(x)\). A strong Nash equilibrium permits no nonempty coalition of whole factions to change their rating assignments so that every participating faction strictly improves; outsiders' assignments stay fixed and transfers are unavailable.

Is deciding whether this weighted MaxParC factional game has a pure strong Nash equilibrium with \(p_Z=1\) solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

For finite electorates, MaxParC has a pure strong Nash equilibrium selecting \(Z\) surely under the corresponding utility and faction-share assumptions. [Heitzig, Simmons and Constantino](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00355-024-01524-3).
No result settling this exact atomless decision problem was located in the literature search.

  • Restored the contextual assumptions mu_1>=mu_2, v_Z>max_t mu_t, and v_Z>(mu_1+mu_2)v_Y. The last strict inequality already implies the proposition's additional weak inequality.

Partial results for the atomless game

The polynomial-time versus NP-hardness classification remains unresolved. However, the infinite-valued case admits substantial progress:

  • No equilibrium selecting $Z$ surely exists if $\max_t\mu_t\geq 2/3$.
  • No such equilibrium exists if $v_Y>v_Z$ and $\mu_1+\mu_2\geq 2/3$.
  • An explicit infinite-valued strong Nash equilibrium exists whenever

$$ v_Z\geq \frac12,\qquad v_Y\leq v_Z, $$
and, writing $b=\max_t\mu_t$, either
$$ b\leq\frac12 $$
or
$$ b>\frac12,\qquad v_Z\geq\frac{b}{2(1-b)}. $$

All these statements concern arbitrary measurable deviations, rather than a restricted strategy space.

1. Approval sets and notation

Write
$$ U_t(p)=\sum_{x\in C}p_xu_t(x). $$
Because $\mu_t>0$, strict improvement of $\pi_t(p)=\mu_tU_t(p)$ is equivalent to strict improvement of $U_t(p)$.

We first justify the approval rule. For a measurable rating function $r$, call $B$ feasible if
$$ \lambda(B)+r(a)>1 $$
for almost every $a\in B$. Let $q$ be the supremum of feasible-set measures, and choose feasible sets $B_n$ whose measures tend to $q$. Their union $B$ is feasible: on each $B_n$, outside a null set,
$$ \lambda(B)+r(a)\geq\lambda(B_n)+r(a)>1. $$
Consequently, $\lambda(B)=q$. For any other feasible $H$, the same argument makes $B\cup H$ feasible, so $\lambda(H\setminus B)=0$. Thus $B$ is the largest feasible set modulo null sets.

If $A$ is this largest set, then
$$ A=\{a:r(a)>1-\lambda(A)\} $$
modulo null sets. Indeed, feasibility gives one containment. The threshold set on the right has measure at least $\lambda(A)$, hence is itself feasible, proving the reverse containment.

Increasing a candidate’s ratings enlarges its approval set and increases its score weakly: every previously feasible set remains feasible, and the rating integral increases weakly.

All set statements below are understood modulo null sets.

2. Universal approval of $Z$ is necessary

Proposition 1. At any Nash equilibrium selecting $Z$ surely,
$$ A_Z=E,\qquad r_Z>0\text{ almost everywhere}. $$
Moreover,
$$ s_Z>1,\qquad \Delta:=s_Z-\max_{x\ne Z}s_x>0. $$

Proof. If $\lambda(D)=0$, faction $t$ can rate $X_t$ by $1$ throughout $F_t$, leaving every other rating unchanged. Then $F_t$ approves $X_t$, every other approval set remains null, and the positive-participation branch selects $X_t$ surely. This improves faction $t$ because $v_Z<1$.

Hence $\lambda(D)>0$. Certain $Z$ means $W(a)=\{Z\}$ almost everywhere on $D$, so $D=A_Z$. Every other candidate with positive approval mass therefore has score strictly below $s_Z$.

If $S\subseteq F_t\setminus D$ has positive measure, faction $t$ can set its $X_t$-ratings on $S$ to $1$. The new approval set of $X_t$ contains $S$; other candidates’ approval sets and scores stay fixed. On the old participation set, only $Z$ and $X_t$ can win. Every newly participating voter approves only $X_t$. The resulting lottery is supported on $\{Z,X_t\}$ and gives $X_t$ positive probability, again improving faction $t$.

Thus $D=A_Z=E$. Feasibility implies $r_Z>0$ almost everywhere, so
$$ s_Z=1+\int_Er_Z\,d\lambda>1. $$
Other candidates with positive approval mass have smaller scores. Candidates with null approval sets have scores at most $1$. Since $C$ is finite, $\Delta>0$. $\square$

3. A stronger necessary condition on outsiders’ low ratings

The following removes the alternative involving the *total* low-rating mass from the previous round’s necessary condition.

For faction $t$, define
$$ h_{-t}(d)= \lambda\bigl(\{a\in E\setminus F_t:r_Z(a)\leq d\}\bigr) $$
and
$$ w_t=\min_{x\in C}u_t(x). $$
Thus
$$ w_1=w_2=\min\{0,v_Y\},\qquad w_3=0. $$

Proposition 2. At any Nash equilibrium selecting $Z$ surely, for every faction $t$ and every
$$ 0<d<\min\{\mu_t,\Delta/2\}, $$
one has
$$ h_{-t}(d)\geq d\,\frac{1-v_Z}{1-w_t}. $$

Proof. Suppose instead that
$$ h:=h_{-t}(d)< d\,\frac{1-v_Z}{1-w_t}. $$
Because $0<v_Z<1$ and $w_t\leq0$, the fraction lies strictly between $0$ and $1$. Consequently,
$$ \delta:=d-h $$
satisfies $0<\delta<\mu_t$.

Choose $S\subseteq F_t$ of measure $\delta$. Faction $t$ makes the following deviation:

  • rate $X_t$ by $1$ throughout $F_t$;
  • rate $Z$ by $0$ on $S$ and by $1$ on $F_t\setminus S$;
  • rate every other candidate by $0$ throughout $F_t$.

Let $H$ be the outsiders whose unchanged $Z$-rating is at most $d$. The set
$$ B=E\setminus(S\cup H) $$
has measure $1-d$ and is feasible for $Z$: outsiders in $B$ have rating strictly greater than $d$, and faction members in $B$ have rating $1>d$. Thus, writing $q=\lambda(A'_Z)$,
$$ q\geq1-d. $$
No voter in $S$ approves $Z$.

The rating integral for $Z$ decreases by at most $\delta$, because ratings increase weakly on $F_t\setminus S$. Therefore
$$ s'_Z\geq s_Z-d-\delta\geq s_Z-2d. $$
Every candidate other than $Z,X_t$ has nonincreasing score, hence
$$ s'_x\leq s_x\leq s_Z-\Delta<s'_Z. $$

On $A'_Z$, local winner lotteries give faction $t$ utility at least $v_Z$. On $S$, only $X_t$ is approved, giving utility $1$. All remaining participating voters give utility at least $w_t$.

Let $I$ denote the integral of faction $t$’s local expected utility over the new participation set. Since $w_t\leq0$,
$$ I\geq qv_Z+\delta+(1-q-\delta)w_t. $$
Using $q\geq1-d$,
$$ \begin{aligned} I-v_Z &\geq-dv_Z+\delta+(d-\delta)w_t\\ &=d(1-v_Z)-h(1-w_t)\\ &>0. \end{aligned} $$
The participation mass is positive and at most $1$. Since $I>v_Z>0$, normalization preserves strict improvement. This contradicts Nash equilibrium. $\square$

In particular, every faction must face outsiders with positive $Z$-ratings arbitrarily close to zero. Therefore, defining
$$ R_{-t}=\int_{E\setminus F_t}r_Z\,d\lambda, $$
we obtain the strict inequality
$$ R_{-t}<1-\mu_t. $$
Indeed, Proposition 2 supplies a positive-measure outsider set with rating at most some $d<1$.

4. Two nonexistence results

4.1 A faction of mass at least $2/3$

Proposition 3. If $\max_t\mu_t\geq2/3$, no Nash equilibrium selecting $Z$ surely exists.

Proof. Suppose such an equilibrium exists, and choose a faction of mass $b\geq2/3$. Put $a=1-b$ and
$$ R=\int_{E\setminus F_t}r_Z\,d\lambda. $$
Proposition 2 gives
$$ R<a\leq2b-1. $$

Choose $\eta>0$ sufficiently small that
$$ 1+R+b\eta<2b. $$
Faction $t$ rates $X_t$ by $1$, $Z$ by $\eta$, and every other candidate by $0$ throughout its faction.

All $Z$-ratings remain positive, so $A'_Z=E$ and
$$ s'_Z=1+R+b\eta<2b. $$
Meanwhile,
$$ s'_{X_t}\geq2b, $$
because $F_t$ approves $X_t$ and contributes rating integral $b$.

Every other candidate has approval mass and rating integral at most $a$, because its ratings are zero on $F_t$. Hence
$$ s'_x\leq2a<1<s'_Z $$
for $x\notin\{Z,X_t\}$.

The output is therefore supported on $\{Z,X_t\}$, with positive probability on $X_t$. This strictly improves faction $t$, a contradiction. $\square$

4.2 A sufficiently large coalition sharing a preferred $Y$

Proposition 4. If
$$ v_Y>v_Z,\qquad \mu_1+\mu_2\geq2/3, $$
no strong Nash equilibrium selecting $Z$ surely exists.

Proof. Suppose such an equilibrium exists. Put
$$ G=F_1\cup F_2,\qquad b=\lambda(G),\qquad a=\mu_3, $$
and define
$$ h_3(d)=\lambda(\{a\in F_3:r_Z(a)\leq d\}). $$

First we prove that, for
$$ 0<d<\min\{b,\Delta/2\}, $$
necessarily
$$ h_3(d)\geq d\,\frac{v_Y-v_Z}{v_Y}. $$

If this fails, put $\delta=d-h_3(d)>0$ and choose $S\subseteq G$ of measure $\delta$. The coalition rates $Y$ by $1$ throughout $G$, rates $Z$ by $1$ on $G\setminus S$ and $0$ on $S$, and rates all other candidates by $0$ throughout $G$.

Exactly as in Proposition 2,
$$ \lambda(A'_Z)\geq1-d,\qquad s'_Z\geq s_Z-2d, $$
and every candidate other than $Z,Y$ has score below $s'_Z$. For each coalition member, utility on $A'_Z$ is at least $v_Z$, utility on $S$ is $v_Y$, and utility elsewhere is nonnegative because $v_Y>v_Z>0$. Its utility integral is therefore at least
$$ (1-d)v_Z+\delta v_Y = v_Z+d(v_Y-v_Z)-h_3(d)v_Y > v_Z. $$
Normalization preserves strict improvement for both factions, which is impossible.

This necessary low-rating condition implies
$$ R:=\int_{F_3}r_Z\,d\lambda<a\leq2b-1. $$
Choose $\eta>0$ with
$$ 1+R+b\eta<2b. $$
The coalition now rates $Y$ by $1$, $Z$ by $\eta$, and all other candidates by $0$ throughout $G$.

Then
$$ A'_Z=E,\qquad s'_Z=1+R+b\eta<2b\leq s'_Y. $$
Every other candidate has score at most $2a<1<s'_Z$. Thus the output is supported on $\{Y,Z\}$ with positive probability on $Y$. Since $v_Y>v_Z$, both factions strictly improve. $\square$

5. An infinite-valued equilibrium construction

We now prove a sufficient condition that handles arbitrary measurable deviations.

Theorem 5. Assume
$$ v_Z\geq\frac12,\qquad v_Y\leq v_Z. $$
Suppose numbers $\ell_t$ can be chosen with $0\leq\ell_t\leq\mu_t$ such that, for each $t$, writing
$$ H_{-t}=\sum_{j\ne t}\ell_j, $$
one has
$$ \mu_t\frac{1-v_Z}{v_Z} \leq H_{-t}\leq2-3\mu_t. $$
Then a pure strong Nash equilibrium selecting $Z$ surely exists.

Construction. The lower bounds imply $H_{-t}>0$. Choose
$$ 0<\varepsilon<\min_t H_{-t}. $$
Within each $F_t$, choose a subinterval $L_t$ of measure $\ell_t$. Assign $Z$-ratings uniformly over $(0,\varepsilon)$ on $L_t$, using an affine function, and assign rating $1$ on $F_t\setminus L_t$. Null endpoints can be assigned any positive value.

Each faction rates its own $X_t$ by $1$ and all other non-$Z$ candidates by $0$.

All $Z$-ratings are positive, so $A_Z=E$. Also,
$$ A_{X_t}=F_t,\qquad s_{X_t}=2\mu_t,\qquad s_Y=0. $$
For each $t$, the outsiders’ $Z$-rating integral equals
$$ R_{-t}=(1-\mu_t)-H_{-t}+\frac{\varepsilon H_{-t}}2. $$
Consequently,
$$ 1+R_{-t} = 2-\mu_t-H_{-t}+\frac{\varepsilon H_{-t}}2 > 2\mu_t. $$
Here the strict inequality uses $H_{-t}\leq2-3\mu_t$ and $\varepsilon H_{-t}>0$. Thus $s_Z>2\mu_t$ for every $t$, and the constructed profile selects $Z$ surely.

5.1 No unilateral deviation improves payoff

Fix faction $t$, and abbreviate
$$ b=\mu_t,\qquad a=1-b,\qquad H=H_{-t}. $$
Consider an arbitrary measurable unilateral deviation.

If $A'_Z=E$, then
$$ s'_Z\geq1+R_{-t}>2b. $$
The candidates $X_t$ and $Y$ have unchanged zero ratings outside $F_t$, so each has approval mass and rating integral at most $b$. Their scores are at most $2b$, and neither can win anywhere. Every possible winner gives faction $t$ utility at most $v_Z$. Thus this deviation cannot improve its payoff.

Now suppose $q=\lambda(A'_Z)<1$, and put $d=1-q>0$. We claim that $d\geq\varepsilon$.

If $0<d<\varepsilon$, the approval-set threshold characterization shows that precisely
$$ H\frac d\varepsilon $$
outsiders have $Z$-ratings at most $d$ and cannot belong to $A'_Z$. Therefore
$$ q\leq b+a-H\frac d\varepsilon =1-H\frac d\varepsilon. $$
Since $q=1-d$, this implies $\varepsilon\geq H$, contrary to our choice of $\varepsilon$.

Hence $d\geq\varepsilon$, and all $H$ outsiders in the low-rating intervals are excluded from $A'_Z$. They still approve their respective private candidates. They cannot approve $X_t$ or $Y$, whose outsider ratings remain zero. Their local winner lotteries therefore give faction $t$ utility $0$.

All other outsiders give utility at most $v_Z$. Every outsider participates, because its own private candidate still has rating $1$ throughout its original faction.

Let $c\leq b$ be the participation mass inside $F_t$. Local utility there is at most $1$. Thus
$$ U_t(p') \leq \frac{c+(a-H)v_Z}{a+c}. $$
This expression increases with $c$, since its derivative is
$$ \frac{a-(a-H)v_Z}{(a+c)^2}>0. $$
Consequently,
$$ \begin{aligned} U_t(p') &\leq b+(a-H)v_Z\\ &=v_Z+b(1-v_Z)-Hv_Z\\ &\leq v_Z, \end{aligned} $$
where the last inequality is exactly the assumed lower bound on $H$.

This proves resistance to every unilateral measurable deviation.

5.2 No two-faction coalition improves both members

For a pair of distinct factions $i,j$, consider the sum of their normalized utilities.

At every private candidate this sum is at most $1$, hence at most $2v_Z$. At $Y$ it is either $2v_Y$ or $v_Y$, both at most $2v_Z$. At $Z$ it equals $2v_Z$.

Every lottery therefore satisfies
$$ U_i(p)+U_j(p)\leq2v_Z. $$
At certain $Z$, equality holds. Strict improvement for both factions would violate this inequality. This argument applies to every possible output lottery, regardless of reachability.

5.3 The grand coalition cannot improve all members

For any lottery,
$$ \sum_t\pi_t(p) = \sum_xp_x\sum_t\mu_tu_t(x). $$
The aggregate utilities of $X_t,Y,Z$ are respectively
$$ \mu_t,\qquad(\mu_1+\mu_2)v_Y,\qquad v_Z. $$
The input hypotheses make every non-$Z$ value strictly smaller than $v_Z$. Thus
$$ \sum_t\pi_t(p)\leq v_Z. $$
Certain $Z$ attains this bound. Strict improvement for all three factions is impossible.

Together, these three arguments prove strong Nash equilibrium. $\square$

6. Explicit parameter ranges and running times

The feasibility conditions in Theorem 5 have the following explicit solutions.

If $b=\max_t\mu_t\leq1/2$, choose
$$ \ell_t=\mu_t. $$
Then $H_{-t}=1-\mu_t$, and
$$ 1-\mu_t> \mu_t\frac{1-v_Z}{v_Z} $$
follows from $v_Z>\mu_t$. Also,
$$ 1-\mu_t\leq2-3\mu_t $$
follows from $\mu_t\leq1/2$.

If $b>1/2$, let $k$ be the unique largest faction and put
$$ a=1-b,\qquad B=b\frac{1-v_Z}{v_Z}. $$
Choose
$$ \ell_k=b,\qquad \ell_j=\frac{\mu_j}{a}B\quad(j\ne k). $$
Since $v_Z>b$, one has $0<B<a$, so these choices respect the interval bounds.

For faction $k$, the lower bound holds with equality. Its upper bound is equivalent to
$$ B\leq2-3b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_Z\geq\frac{b}{2(1-b)}. $$
For every other faction, $H_{-j}\geq b>\mu_j$ supplies the lower bound, while
$$ H_{-j}\leq1-\mu_j\leq2-3\mu_j $$
supplies the upper bound.

Thus Theorem 5 proves the existence range stated at the beginning. For example,
$$ (\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)=\left(\frac35,\frac14,\frac3{20}\right), \qquad v_Z=\frac45,\qquad v_Y=\frac7{10} $$
satisfies this sufficient condition.

All predicates above can be checked by rational comparisons, including the division-free comparison
$$ 2(1-b)v_Z\geq b. $$
For the specified dimensions $m=5,\tau=3$, input validation, these sufficient and necessary tests, and the rational description of the constructed profile take $O(m\tau)$ rational arithmetic operations and $O(m\tau L^2)$ bit operations using schoolbook arithmetic. Thus their bit complexity is $O(L^2)$ here. The construction uses finitely many rational breakpoints and affine functions, although its ratings take infinitely many values.

These running times concern the proved parameter tests and construction. They do not establish a running-time bound for deciding all permitted inputs.

7. What remains open

The results prove that infinite-valued profiles can sustain equilibrium, and they exclude substantial parameter ranges. They leave cases outside both the existence criterion and the nonexistence criteria unresolved. For instance,
$$ (\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)=\left(\frac35,\frac14,\frac3{20}\right), \qquad v_Z=\frac7{10},\qquad v_Y=\frac35 $$
satisfies the input assumptions but is not classified by these results.

A complete characterization in the remaining ranges, or a hardness reduction exploiting them, is still needed to settle the target complexity question.
