The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given worker types \(A\) with rational masses \(\mu_a\), job types \(Q\) with rational per-worker supplies \(\sigma_q\), and additive valuations \(v_a(q)\), let \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{Q}\) range over bundles schedulable by earliest-deadline-first. Define \(\mathrm{MMS}_a(\sigma)=\max_{\lambda}\min_{B:\lambda_B>0}v_a(B)\), where \(\lambda\) is a distribution over feasible integral bundles with mean supply \(\sum_B\lambda_B B_q=\sigma_q\) for every \(q\). Find the minimum \(\delta\ge 0\) and masses \(x_{a,B}\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_B x_{a,B}=\mu_a\), \(\sum_{a,B}x_{a,B}B_q=\sigma_q\) for every \(q\), and every bundle with \(x_{a,B}>0\) satisfies \(v_a(B)\ge\mathrm{MMS}_a(\sigma)-\delta\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(A\) of \(\tau\ge 1\) worker types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), normalized by \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=|Q|\ge 1\) job types, with positive binary-rational per-worker supplies \(\sigma_q\), positive integer processing times and deadlines \(p_q,d_q\), and signed integer additive valuations \(v_a(q)\). All numerical input is binary encoded; \(L\) is its total bit length.
All jobs are available at time zero and run sequentially without preemption on identical workers. Let \(\mathcal B\) contain exactly the bundles \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{Q}\) satisfying \(\sum_{q:d_q\le D}p_qB_q\le D\) for every distinct deadline \(D\); the empty bundle is allowed. Write \(v_a(B)=\sum_qv_a(q)B_q\).
Let \(\Lambda(\sigma)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\mathcal B}:\sum_B\lambda_B=1,\ \sum_B\lambda_BB_q=\sigma_q\text{ for every }q\}\), and promise \(\Lambda(\sigma)\ne\varnothing\). Define \(\mathrm{MMS}_a(\sigma)=\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\sigma)}\min_{B:\lambda_B>0}v_a(B)\); these benchmarks must be computed from the input.
Find the minimum \(\delta\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) and allocation masses \(x_{a,B}\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) such that \(\sum_Bx_{a,B}=\mu_a\), \(\sum_{a,B}x_{a,B}B_q=\sigma_q\), and \(x_{a,B}>0\) implies \(v_a(B)\ge\mathrm{MMS}_a(\sigma)-\delta\). Conservation is exact, and each recipient receives an integral bundle.

Is this continuous optimal additive maximin-share allocation problem solvable exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

The discrete scheduling counterpart has NP-hardness for constant values of the remaining parameters whenever a parameter is omitted from a minimal FPT parameter set. [Jansen–Lassota–Tutas–Vetta](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04225v1)

Result

The problem as stated is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with one worker type, strictly positive valuations, and every supply equal to \(\tfrac12\).

With one worker type, the optimal deficit is always \(\delta^*=0\). The hard part is computing the required benchmark and producing an allocation that attains it. The reduction below establishes hardness of both tasks, including allocation computation when the benchmark is not explicitly returned.

1. Source problem and construction

We reduce from PARTITION: given positive binary-encoded integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) with total \(2T\), decide whether some subset sums to \(T\). This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Restricting the total to be even preserves hardness: doubling every weight of an arbitrary instance preserves whether an equal-sum partition exists. [MIT lecture notes on NP-completeness](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-046j-design-and-analysis-of-algorithms-spring-2015/f2829e57caade7e7f21d765b8e7b9058_MIT6_046JS15_lec16.pdf).

Create one worker type \(a\), with mass \(\mu_a=1\), and \(m=n\) job types. For each \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), set

\[ p_i=2^{i-1},\qquad d_i=2^i-1,\qquad \sigma_i=\tfrac12,\qquad v_a(i)=w_i. \]

Each processing time and deadline has at most \(n\) bits. Thus the construction has total encoding length \(O(n^2+\sum_i\operatorname{bits}(w_i))\) and is computable in polynomial time. All required positivity and integrality conditions hold.

2. The feasible bundles are exactly the subsets

Lemma. For the constructed instance, \(\mathcal B=\{0,1\}^n\).

Proof. The deadlines are strictly increasing. The constraint at deadline \(d_i\) is

\[ \sum_{j=1}^{i}2^{j-1}B_j\le 2^i-1. \]

Since every term is nonnegative, this implies

\[ 2^{i-1}B_i\le 2^i-1<2\cdot2^{i-1}. \]

As \(B_i\) is a nonnegative integer, \(B_i\le1\).

Conversely, if every \(B_j\in\{0,1\}\), then for each \(i\),

\[ \sum_{j=1}^{i}2^{j-1}B_j \le \sum_{j=1}^{i}2^{j-1} =2^i-1=d_i. \]

Hence every subset is feasible, and no other integral bundle is feasible. \(\square\)

The feasibility promise holds independently of the PARTITION answer: placing mass \(\tfrac12\) on the empty bundle and mass \(\tfrac12\) on the full bundle gives exactly \(\sigma_i=\tfrac12\) for every job type.

3. Exact computation of the benchmark

For a subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), write \(w(S)=\sum_{i\in S}w_i\), and define

\[ K=\max\{w(S):S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\ w(S)\le T\}. \]

The empty subset guarantees that this maximum is defined.

Lemma. The constructed instance satisfies \(\mathrm{MMS}_a(\sigma)=K\).

Proof: upper bound. Let \(\lambda\in\Lambda(\sigma)\) be arbitrary. Exact conservation and additivity imply

\[ \sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\lambda_Bv_a(B) =\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_i\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}\lambda_BB_i =\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_i=T. \]

Consequently, some positively supported bundle \(B\) has \(v_a(B)\le T\): otherwise the finite weighted average would exceed \(T\).

By the preceding lemma, \(B\) represents a subset. The definition of \(K\) therefore gives \(v_a(B)\le K\). Thus

\[ \min_{B:\lambda_B>0}v_a(B)\le K. \]

This holds for every \(\lambda\in\Lambda(\sigma)\), proving \(\mathrm{MMS}_a(\sigma)\le K\).

Proof: lower bound. Choose a subset \(S\) attaining \(K\). Give mass \(\tfrac12\) to its indicator bundle and mass \(\tfrac12\) to the indicator bundle of its complement.

Every coordinate has mean \(\tfrac12\), so this distribution belongs to \(\Lambda(\sigma)\). Its two bundle values are \(K\) and \(2T-K\). Since \(K\le T\), its minimum supported value is \(K\). Therefore \(\mathrm{MMS}_a(\sigma)\ge K\). \(\square\)

In particular,

\[ \mathrm{MMS}_a(\sigma)=T \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the PARTITION instance is a yes-instance}. \]

Thus computing the required benchmark exactly is already \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

4. Producing an optimal allocation is also hard

The complementary pair constructed above is an allocation attaining the benchmark with \(\delta=0\). Because deficits must be nonnegative, \(\delta^*=0\).

Suppose a polynomial-time algorithm returns an optimal allocation as a list of its nonzero masses and associated integral bundles. We can decide PARTITION from that output:

  • Yes-instance. Here \(K=T\). Optimality forces \(\delta=0\), so every positively supported bundle has value at least \(T\). Their weighted average is exactly \(T\), by conservation. Hence every positively supported bundle has value exactly \(T\).
  • No-instance. No subset has value \(T\). Since feasible bundles are exactly subsets, no positively supported bundle can have value \(T\).

The allocation has total mass one, so its support is nonempty. Reading any positively supported bundle and checking whether its value equals \(T\) therefore decides PARTITION. This check uses polynomial-time binary arithmetic.

The hardness is not caused by an unavoidable large output: every constructed instance has an optimal allocation supported on just two bundles, with masses \(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\).

5. Conclusion and scope

The requested exact task—computing the benchmarks, the minimum deficit, and an optimal allocation—is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Consequently, it has no algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

This proof directly handles continuous masses and exact conservation. It does not transfer hardness from the discrete scheduling counterpart. It also distinguishes the full requested task from computing only the scalar deficit: on this restricted family that scalar is always zero, while computing the benchmark or producing an optimal allocation remains hard.
