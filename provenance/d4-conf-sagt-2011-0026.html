The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite product set \(P\), cohort types \(i\in\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) with rational masses \(\mu_i\), thresholds \(\theta_i\), initial adopted products or available-product sets \(S_i\), and rational influence coefficients \(q_{ji}\), let \(x_{j,p}\) be the fraction of type \(j\) that has adopted product \(p\), with exposure \(E_{i,p}=\sum_j q_{ji}x_{j,p}\). A legal move transfers any positive fraction of a type's undecided mass to an available product \(p\) when \(E_{i,p}\ge\theta_i\), and adopted mass cannot switch. Find the minimum \(\sum_i\mu_i x_{i,\mathrm{top}}\) over finite legal sequences ending in a state where no undecided mass can adopt any available product.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\), normalized by \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(P\) contain \(m\ge3\) products, with designated product \(\mathrm{top}\).
Input also includes a directed graph on the types without parallel edges or self-loops; write \(N(i)\) for the incoming neighbours. Edge coefficients are binary rationals \(q_{ji}\in[0,1]\), with \(\sum_{j\in N(i)}q_{ji}\le1\), and product-independent thresholds are binary rationals \(\theta_i\in(0,1]\).
Each type has a nonempty set \(S_i\subseteq P\). If \(S_i=\{p\}\), its entire mass initially adopts \(p\); otherwise its entire mass is initially undecided.
A state records adopted fractions \(x_{i,p}\ge0\), with \(\sum_{p\in P}x_{i,p}\le1\); its undecided fraction is \(u_i=1-\sum_{p\in P}x_{i,p}\). Exposure is \(E_{i,p}=\sum_{j\in N(i)}q_{ji}x_{j,p}\).
A legal move increases one \(x_{i,p}\) by any real \(\delta\in(0,u_i]\), for \(p\in S_i\), provided either \(N(i)=\varnothing\) or \(E_{i,p}\ge\theta_i\) before the move. Adopted mass never switches; the undecided remainder retains all choices in \(S_i\).
A terminal state permits no legal move. Define \(\mathrm{OPT}\) as the infimum of \(\sum_i\mu_i x_{i,\mathrm{top}}\) over terminal states reachable by finite legal sequences. A multiplicative \(\rho\)-approximation returns such a sequence with terminal cost at most \(\rho\mathrm{OPT}\), including when \(\mathrm{OPT}=0\).

Does continuous MIN-ADOPTION admit a polynomial-time multiplicative approximation with ratio \(o(\tau)\), or is achieving such a ratio NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.

Discrete MIN-ADOPTION is NP-hard to approximate within \(o(n)\), already with three products and product-independent thresholds; with two products it has an \(O(n^2)\) algorithm ([verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.5636)).
No result settling this precise continuous approximation problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize positive masses to sum to one; adopted variables are fractions within each type, and masses weight only the objective.
  • Restore the explicit directed graph, including possible zero-weight edges, so having no incoming neighbours is distinguishable from having incoming edges of total weight zero. Exclude self-loops and parallel edges and impose the original incoming-weight bound.
  • Restore unconditional adoption for types with no incoming neighbours, which the graded question omitted.
  • Require nonempty available-product sets and rational thresholds in (0,1]; singleton sets mean full initial adoption, while larger sets mean full initial indecision.
  • Retain product-independent thresholds and consider at least three products, matching the verified inapproximability setting.
  • Allow arbitrary positive real fractional transfers and preserve the full available-product set for the undecided remainder; eligibility is evaluated before each move.
  • Use the infimum over finite reachable terminal sequences because the continuous formulation does not guarantee attainment of a minimum; retain multiplicative approximation, with its usual zero-optimum requirement.
  • Use support size tau as the continuous counterpart of the discrete node-count parameter in the approximation ratio.

Proven result

Achieving a multiplicative approximation ratio \(o(\tau)\) is NP-hard. In fact, a polynomial-time multiplicative \(\tau\)-approximation would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

This holds already with three products, an acyclic graph, product-independent thresholds, and dyadic coefficients and masses. The reduction has positive optimum in every instance, so the hardness does not depend on the zero-cost requirement.

The discrete result in the [provided paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.5636) concerns indivisible adoption. The following reduction directly handles the continuous model.

Construction from satisfiability

Let \(\Phi\) be a three-literal conjunctive normal form formula with \(v\) variables and \(c\) clauses. We reduce from the standard NP-complete satisfiability problem for such formulas. Treat repeated literal occurrences as separate occurrences.

Use products \(P=\{A,B,\mathrm{top}\}\). Interpret complete adoption of \(A\) by a variable type as true, and complete adoption of \(B\) as false.

Create the following types.

  • One seed \(z\). Set \(S_z=\{\mathrm{top}\}\), so it initially adopts \(\mathrm{top}\) completely.
  • One variable type \(V_i\) for each variable, and one dummy type \(d\). Each has no incoming neighbours, choice set \(\{A,B\}\), and threshold \(1\).
  • One sensor \(H_{C,k}\) for each literal occurrence in each clause. Its only incoming edge is from the corresponding variable type, with coefficient \(1\). Its threshold is \(1\).
  • For a positive literal, its choice set is \(\{A,\mathrm{top}\}\).
  • For a negative literal, its choice set is \(\{B,\mathrm{top}\}\).
  • One clause type \(W_C\) for each clause. Its choice set is \(\{A,B,\mathrm{top}\}\), and its threshold is \(1/4\). It has four incoming edges: one from \(z\) and one from each of its three occurrence sensors. Every coefficient is \(1/4\).

Give \(z\) threshold \(1\); its threshold does not affect its initial adoption.

The support size is

\(\tau=v+4c+2.\)

All thresholds are product-independent. The graph is acyclic, has no self-loops or parallel edges, and the incoming coefficient sum is \(1\) at each sensor and clause type. Separate occurrence sensors ensure that repeated literals do not create parallel edges.

Masses

Let \(K\) be the least power of two satisfying \(K\ge\tau\), and set \(\varepsilon=1/K^2\). Thus \(\tau\le K<2\tau\).

Assign masses

\(\mu_z=\varepsilon,\qquad \mu_i=1/K\quad\text{for every }i\notin\{z,d\},\)

and

\(\mu_d=1-\varepsilon-(\tau-2)/K.\)

These masses sum to \(1\). Moreover,

\(\mu_d\ge 2/K-1/K^2>0,\)

because \(\tau\le K\). Every mass is dyadic and has \(O(\log\tau)\) encoding length.

Sensor and terminal-state properties

We establish properties valid for arbitrary real move sizes.

Sensors certify complete variable adoption

Consider a positive-occurrence sensor \(H\) for variable \(V_i\). At every reachable state,

\(E_{H,A}=x_{V_i,A},\qquad E_{H,\mathrm{top}}=0.\)

The second equality holds because \(V_i\) cannot adopt \(\mathrm{top}\). Consequently, \(H\) can never adopt \(\mathrm{top}\), and it can start adopting \(A\) only when \(x_{V_i,A}=1\).

Once \(x_{V_i,A}=1\), the variable has no undecided remainder and can never change. In a terminal state, \(H\) must then adopt \(A\) completely: otherwise its positive undecided remainder would permit another legal \(A\)-move.

Conversely, if the final value satisfies \(x_{V_i,A}<1\), monotonicity implies that it was less than \(1\) throughout the sequence. Hence \(H\) could never make any move.

Therefore, in every reachable terminal state,

\(x_{H,A}=1\quad\text{if }x_{V_i,A}=1,\qquad x_{H,A}=0\quad\text{otherwise}.\)

The identical argument for a negative-occurrence sensor gives complete adoption of \(B\) exactly when its variable completely adopts \(B\).

Thus a variable may split fractionally between \(A\) and \(B\), but such a split activates neither kind of sensor.

Every clause type is completely allocated

At every reachable state, a clause type has

\(E_{W_C,\mathrm{top}}=1/4.\)

Indeed, \(z\) supplies this exposure, and its sensors never adopt \(\mathrm{top}\). Hence any positive undecided remainder at \(W_C\) permits a legal \(\mathrm{top}\)-move.

It follows that every reachable terminal state satisfies

\(x_{W_C,A}+x_{W_C,B}+x_{W_C,\mathrm{top}}=1.\)

Satisfiable formulas have optimum \(\varepsilon\)

Suppose \(\Phi\) has a satisfying assignment.

First, completely allocate each variable type to \(A\) or \(B\) according to that assignment, and allocate \(d\) to \(A\). These moves are legal because these types have no incoming neighbours.

Next, completely allocate each sensor whose literal is true to its corresponding product. Its exposure is \(1\), so the move is legal. Leave every false-literal sensor undecided; its exposure to every available product is \(0\).

Finally, for each clause choose a true literal. Its sensor supplies exposure \(1/4\) to either \(A\) or \(B\), allowing complete allocation of the clause type to that product.

The resulting state is terminal:

  • Every variable, dummy, and clause type is completely allocated.
  • Every activated sensor is completely allocated.
  • Every remaining sensor has zero exposure to its available products.

This construction uses at most \(\tau-1\) moves. Only \(z\) adopts \(\mathrm{top}\), so its cost is \(\varepsilon\).

Every reachable state already incurs the seed’s cost \(\varepsilon\). Consequently, for satisfiable formulas,

\(\mathrm{OPT}=\varepsilon=1/K^2,\)

and this optimum is attained.

Unsatisfiable formulas force an additional clause cost

Suppose \(\Phi\) is unsatisfiable, and consider any reachable terminal state.

Define a Boolean assignment as follows:

  • If \(x_{V_i,A}=1\), assign variable \(i\) true.
  • If \(x_{V_i,B}=1\), assign it false.
  • Otherwise, assign it arbitrarily.

The first two cases cannot both occur because the adopted fractions sum to at most \(1\).

Since \(\Phi\) is unsatisfiable, some clause \(C\) is false under this assignment. Each sensor of \(C\) must be inactive:

  • A positive literal is false, so its variable cannot have \(x_{V_i,A}=1\).
  • A negative literal is false, so its variable cannot have \(x_{V_i,B}=1\).

By the sensor property, all three sensors have zero adopted fractions. Thus the final exposures of \(W_C\) to \(A\) and \(B\) are both zero.

These exposures are nondecreasing throughout a legal sequence. Therefore, they were zero throughout the sequence, and \(W_C\) could never legally adopt either \(A\) or \(B\). Since every clause type is completely allocated at terminality, this implies

\(x_{W_C,\mathrm{top}}=1.\)

Its mass is \(1/K\). Therefore every reachable terminal state has cost at least

\(\varepsilon+1/K=(K+1)\varepsilon.\)

Taking the infimum preserves this lower bound:

\(\mathrm{OPT}\ge(K+1)\varepsilon\quad\text{when }\Phi\text{ is unsatisfiable}.\)

This argument covers arbitrary fractional variable choices and arbitrary finite real-valued move sequences.

Approximation consequence and running times

Suppose an algorithm returns a legal finite sequence ending in a terminal state and guarantees a multiplicative \(\tau\)-approximation.

On a satisfiable formula, its returned cost must satisfy

\(\mathrm{cost}\le\tau\varepsilon\le K\varepsilon=1/K<\varepsilon+1/K.\)

Consequently, no clause type in its output can completely adopt \(\mathrm{top}\): such a clause, together with the seed, would already cost \(\varepsilon+1/K\).

A satisfying assignment can be extracted from the returned sequence without evaluating its real move sizes. Whenever a positive sensor makes a move, assign its variable true; whenever a negative sensor makes a move, assign its variable false. These assignments cannot conflict, because each sensor move requires complete adoption of the corresponding variable product. Assign all remaining variables arbitrarily.

Every clause has a positive non-\(\mathrm{top}\) fraction, since it is completely allocated and is not completely assigned to \(\mathrm{top}\). Its first non-\(\mathrm{top}\) move therefore required positive exposure from a sensor representing a true literal under the extracted assignment. Hence every clause is satisfied.

We can consequently decide satisfiability by running the approximation algorithm, extracting this assignment, and checking it against \(\Phi\). An unsatisfiable formula cannot pass that check.

For a formula of input length \(B\), the reduction has:

  • \(m=3\);
  • \(\tau=v+4c+2\);
  • \(O(\tau)\) edges;
  • total output bit length \(L=O(\tau\log\tau)\) using an explicit edge-list encoding.

The reduction takes \(O(B+\tau\log\tau)\) bit time. If the approximation algorithm runs in time \(T(m,\tau,L)\), the resulting satisfiability algorithm runs in

\(O\!\left(B+\tau\log\tau+T(3,\tau,L)\right),\)

including inspection of its output sequence. Thus polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

Finally, any ratio \(o(\tau)\) is at most \(\tau\) for all sufficiently large support sizes. The finitely many smaller sizes can be handled by exhaustive satisfiability search. Therefore achieving \(o(\tau)\) is NP-hard as well.
