The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of household market-situation strata, positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), per-household prices \(p_t\), aggregate bundles \(q_t\), and assignable lower bounds \(q_t^1,q_t^2\) with \(q_t^1+q_t^2\le q_t\), choose \(\mathcal A\in\{\mathrm{GARP},\mathrm{SARP}\}\). Do there exist per-situation splits \(x_t^1,x_t^2\in\mathbb R_+^m\) satisfying \(x_t^1+x_t^2=q_t\) and \(x_t^\ell\ge q_t^\ell\), such that for each role \(\ell\), the data \(\{(p_t,x_t^\ell):t\in T\}\) satisfy \(\mathcal A\) under one shared role-specific preference relation across the cohort’s market situations?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) household market-situation types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) privately consumed goods, two household roles, and no consumption externalities. Each type specifies binary rational vectors \(p_t\in\mathbb Q_{++}^m\) and \(q_t,q_t^1,q_t^2\in\mathbb Q_+^m\), with \(q_t^1+q_t^2\le q_t\) componentwise. Quantities are per household; \(q_t\) aggregates the two roles' consumption within a household.
For a fixed choice \(\mathcal A\in\{\mathrm{GARP},\mathrm{SARP}\}\), feasibility means choosing one pair \(x_t^1,x_t^2\in\mathbb R_+^m\) per type, satisfying \(x_t^1+x_t^2=q_t\) and \(x_t^\ell\ge q_t^\ell\). Each role's entire support data set \(\{(p_t,x_t^\ell):t\in T\}\) must satisfy \(\mathcal A\); its preference relation is shared across all types. Use the standard weak affordability comparisons, with identical bundles permitted under SARP. Masses specify frequencies and do not scale these comparisons.

For each fixed \(\mathcal A\in\{\mathrm{GARP},\mathrm{SARP}\}\), is this feasibility problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total binary input length?

For a finite list of observations, both corresponding two-role feasibility problems are NP-complete, even when all assignable lower bounds vanish. [Source](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.2016.1527). No separate result for the distribution formulation is verified here.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified strictly positive rational prices and nonnegative rational quantities and lower bounds, using binary encoding for all numerical inputs.
  • Interpreted aggregate bundles as aggregating the two household roles per household, rather than aggregating consumption over a stratum's mass.
  • Specified private consumption without externalities and one common split per type, matching the underlying two-member model.
  • Specified that masses record frequencies and do not weight affordability comparisons or impose additional aggregate constraints.
  • Defined L as total binary input length and allowed zero quantities and identical personalized bundles.

Result

For each fixed \(\mathcal A\in\{\mathrm{GARP},\mathrm{SARP}\}\), the support feasibility problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when all assignable lower bounds vanish and all support masses are equal. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof uses the finite-list complexity result supplied in the question and explicitly establishes its transfer to the support formulation.

1. The finite-list premise

Fix \(\mathcal A\). Let \(F_{\mathcal A}\) denote the corresponding finite-list problem: its input consists of observations \((p_i,q_i,q_i^1,q_i^2)\), and it asks for allocations satisfying exactly the allocation constraints and role-specific axiom in the question. Each occurrence in the list has its own allocation variables, including repeated occurrences.

We use the stated premise that \(F_{\mathcal A}\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, with hardness already when \(q_i^1=q_i^2=0\) for every observation. This is the finite-list result attributed to [Talla Nobibon et al. (2016)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.2016.1527). No separate distribution complexity theorem is assumed.

The point requiring proof is that repeated observations can be consolidated into support types without changing feasibility.

2. Revealed-preference definitions

For one role, write its allocated bundle at observation \(i\) as \(x_i\). Define weak direct revealed preference by

\(i\,D\,j \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_i\cdot x_i\ge p_i\cdot x_j.\)

Let \(R\) be the reflexive transitive closure of \(D\). Under the conventions in the question:

  • \(\mathrm{GARP}\) requires that \(i\,R\,j\) imply \(p_j\cdot x_j\le p_j\cdot x_i\).
  • \(\mathrm{SARP}\) requires that \(i\,R\,j\) and \(j\,D\,i\) imply \(x_i=x_j\).

Thus identical bundles are permitted under \(\mathrm{SARP}\).

Both axioms are inherited by restriction to a sublist. Indeed, every direct comparison and every revealed-preference path in the sublist also occurs in the original list. A violation in the sublist would therefore be a violation in the original list.

3. Consolidating repeated types preserves feasibility

Lemma. Partition a nonempty finite list into classes of identical tuples \((p_i,q_i,q_i^1,q_i^2)\). Retain one representative per class. The original list is feasible for \(\mathcal A\) if and only if the representative list is feasible.

Proof. Suppose first that the original list is feasible. Restrict its allocations to the representatives. The allocation constraints remain satisfied, and both roles retain \(\mathcal A\) by the restriction property proved above.

Conversely, suppose the representative list is feasible. Let \(c(i)\) be the representative of the class containing \(i\), and define, for each role \(\ell\),

\(x_i^\ell=x_{c(i)}^\ell.\)

Because all input vectors agree within a class, these copied allocations satisfy

\(x_i^1+x_i^2=q_i,\qquad x_i^\ell\ge q_i^\ell,\qquad x_i^\ell\in\mathbb R_+^m.\)

Fix either role and suppress its superscript. Its direct comparisons satisfy

\(i\,D\,j \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c(i)\,D\,c(j),\)

because the corresponding prices and allocated bundles coincide. Consequently, every revealed-preference path in the expanded list projects to a revealed-preference path among representatives.

For \(\mathrm{GARP}\), if \(i\,R\,j\), then \(c(i)\,R\,c(j)\). The representative data therefore give

\(p_j\cdot x_j=p_{c(j)}\cdot x_{c(j)}\le p_{c(j)}\cdot x_{c(i)}=p_j\cdot x_i.\)

For \(\mathrm{SARP}\), if \(i\,R\,j\) and \(j\,D\,i\), projection gives \(c(i)\,R\,c(j)\) and \(c(j)\,D\,c(i)\). Representative \(\mathrm{SARP}\) implies \(x_{c(i)}=x_{c(j)}\), hence \(x_i=x_j\).

This proves the required axiom for each role on the expanded list, completing the proof.

4. Polynomial reduction to the support problem

Take an instance of \(F_{\mathcal A}\) from its hard subclass with vanishing lower bounds. Consolidate identical observations as in the lemma. If there are \(k\) distinct types, create a society whose support consists of those types and assign

\(\mu_t=\frac1k\qquad\text{for every }t\in T.\)

The output satisfies every input requirement:

  • \(k\ge1\), and its support types are distinct.
  • The price and quantity vectors retain their required signs and rational encodings.
  • The lower bounds remain zero.
  • Every mass is a positive binary-encoded rational, and \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=k(1/k)=1\).

Exact equality of rational vectors can be checked in polynomial time, so consolidation is polynomial-time computable. Each mass has encoding length \(O(\log(k+1))\), and all other output vectors are copied from the input. Thus the output length is polynomial in the finite-list input length.

By hypothesis, masses enter neither allocation constraints nor affordability comparisons. Hence the output society is feasible precisely when its representative list is feasible. The lemma then gives

\(\text{original finite list feasible}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\text{output society feasible}.\)

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from the hard subclass of \(F_{\mathcal A}\). Therefore the support problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for each fixed axiom, already with uniform masses and zero lower bounds.

5. Membership and conclusion

Conversely, given a support instance, discard its masses and regard its support entries as a finite list. There is one allocation pair per entry in either formulation, and all allocation constraints and role-specific comparisons are identical. This polynomial-time transformation preserves feasibility exactly.

Because \(F_{\mathcal A}\) belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\) by the supplied finite-list premise, the support problem also belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\). If input validity needs checking, positivity, componentwise bounds, and the rational identity \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) can all be checked in polynomial time.

Combining membership with the reduction proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for both axioms. The argument uses the explicit support size \(\tau=k\); it requires neither enumerating possible types nor expanding masses into households.
