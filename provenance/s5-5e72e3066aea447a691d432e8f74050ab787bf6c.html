The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a rational quota \(q\), additive feature contributions \(a_j(t)\), and a DNF strict partial order \(C\) over profiles, describe exactly all allocations \(y\) with \(0\le y_t\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t y_t=q\) for which no other feasible allocation \(z\) satisfies \(C(p(z),p(y))\), where \(p_j(y)=\sum_{t\in T}y_ta_j(t)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The input also contains \(m\geq1\) features with arbitrary signed binary-rational contributions \(a_j(t)\), a binary-rational quota \(0<q<1\), and an explicitly encoded DNF formula \(C(u,v)=\bigvee_i\bigwedge_h(u_{j_{ih}}\,\theta_{ih}\,v_{j_{ih}})\), where \(\theta_{ih}\in\{=,\neq,<,>,\leq,\geq\}\). Comparisons use the same feature on both sides; \(C\) is promised to define a strict partial order on \(\mathbb R^m\).
Allocations are real vectors in \(P=\{y\in\mathbb R^\tau:0\leq y_t\leq\mu_t,\ \sum_t y_t=q\}\), with profiles \(p_j(y)=\sum_t y_ta_j(t)\). Define \(U=\{y\in P:\nexists z\in P\ C(p(z),p(y))\}\); incomparable profiles are retained without tie-breaking.
An exact description means a finite quantifier-free Boolean formula in \(y\), using rational linear comparisons, whose real solution set is precisely \(U\). Let \(s\) be the encoded length of \(C\) and \(L\) the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,s,L\).

Can an exact description of \(U\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,s,L\), or is this computation NP-hard?

Throughout, a formula is an explicitly written Boolean expression: repeated subexpressions count repeatedly. A representation with shared subexpressions is a Boolean circuit.

1. Dominance depends only on the active allocation bounds

Let $A\in\mathbb Q^{m\times\tau}$ have entries $A_{jt}=a_j(t)$, so that $p(y)=Ay$.

Every permitted comparison between matching features depends only on their difference. Consequently, for all $u,v\in\mathbb R^m$ and all $\lambda>0$,
$$ C(u,v)\iff C(u-v,0), \qquad C(\lambda u,0)\iff C(u,0). $$
These statements hold for each of the six comparison operators and therefore for their Boolean combinations.

For $y\in P$, define
$$ K(y)= \left\{ h\in\mathbb R^\tau: \sum_t h_t=0,\quad h_t\geq0\text{ when }y_t=0,\quad h_t\leq0\text{ when }y_t=\mu_t \right\}. $$

Lemma 1. An allocation $y\in P$ is dominated if and only if
$$ \exists h\in K(y)\quad C(Ah,0). $$

Proof. If $z\in P$ dominates $y$, take $h=z-y$. The quota constraints give $\sum_t h_t=0$. A coordinate at its lower bound has $h_t\geq0$, and a coordinate at its upper bound has $h_t\leq0$. Translation invariance gives $C(Ah,0)$.

Conversely, suppose $h\in K(y)$ and $C(Ah,0)$. For each $h_t>0$, the sign constraints imply $y_t<\mu_t$, so
$$ \frac{\mu_t-y_t}{h_t}>0. $$
For each $h_t<0$, they imply $y_t>0$, so
$$ \frac{y_t}{-h_t}>0. $$
Choose a positive $\varepsilon$ smaller than every applicable ratio. Then $z=y+\varepsilon h$ satisfies the box constraints and
$$ \sum_t z_t=q+\varepsilon\sum_t h_t=q. $$
Positive homogeneity gives $C(Az-Ay,0)$, so $z$ dominates $y$. $\square$

The direction search may additionally impose $-1\leq h_t\leq1$: any direction can be multiplied by a sufficiently small positive scalar without changing the relevant comparisons.

A convexity lemma for disequalities

The following formulation also covers identically zero forms.

Lemma 2. Let $S$ be a nonempty convex set, and let $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ be real affine functions on its ambient space. Then
$$ \exists x\in S\quad \bigwedge_{i=1}^r f_i(x)\neq0 $$
if and only if, for every $i$, there exists $x_i\in S$ with $f_i(x_i)\neq0$.

Proof. Necessity is immediate. For sufficiency, suppose $p\in S$ avoids the first $k$ zero sets. If $f_{k+1}(p)\neq0$, retain $p$. Otherwise choose $q\in S$ with $f_{k+1}(q)\neq0$.

On the segment
$$ p_\lambda=(1-\lambda)p+\lambda q, \qquad 0<\lambda<1, $$
the function $f_{k+1}$ is nonzero. Each earlier $f_i(p_\lambda)$ is affine in $\lambda$ and is nonzero at $\lambda=0$, so it vanishes at at most one parameter value. Choose $\lambda$ outside these finitely many exceptional values. Convexity ensures $p_\lambda\in S$. Induction proves the claim. $\square$

In particular, an identically zero disequality makes the conjunction infeasible; the lemma does not require its zero set to be a proper hyperplane.

Polynomial-time membership

For a specified pattern of active bounds, Lemma 1 reduces dominance to a bounded direction search. Distribute the existential quantifier over the explicitly encoded DNF.

For one term, remove its disequalities. The remaining constraints, together with the direction constraints, define a convex set $S$ using rational linear equalities, weak inequalities, and strict inequalities.

Strict feasibility is decidable by exact rational linear programming. Orient the strict inequalities as $\ell_i(h)>0$, introduce $0\leq\delta\leq1$, replace them by $\ell_i(h)\geq\delta$, and maximize $\delta$. The original system is feasible exactly when this bounded LP is feasible with positive optimum. Indeed, a strict feasible point has a positive minimum strict slack; conversely, positive $\delta$ certifies every strict inequality. If no strict inequalities occur, ordinary LP feasibility suffices.

The theorem used here is that feasibility and optimization for rational linear programs are decidable in time polynomial in their binary encoding length. Our LPs have polynomially many variables and constraints, polynomial coefficient bit lengths, and bounded feasible regions. Thus its hypotheses apply. See [Khachiyan’s polynomial-time linear programming theorem](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

For each removed disequality $\ell(h)\neq0$, test feasibility of
$$ S\cap\{\ell>0\} \quad\text{and}\quad S\cap\{\ell<0\}. $$
After checking that $S$ is nonempty, Lemma 2 proves that these individual tests suffice for simultaneous satisfaction of all disequalities.

A term with $r$ disequalities requires at most $1+2r$ LP tests. Summed over the DNF, this is $O(s+1)$ tests.

Proposition 3. A specified active-bound pattern can be classified in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,s,L)$ time. Membership in $U$ for a rational allocation of bit length $b$ is decidable in
$$ \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,s,L+b). $$

These arguments do not require the strict-partial-order promise.

2. A general exact construction by enumerating faces

For $\sigma\in\{0,*,1\}^{\tau}$, let
$$ R_\sigma= \left\{ y\in P: y_t=0\text{ if }\sigma_t=0,\quad 0<y_t<\mu_t\text{ if }\sigma_t=*,\quad y_t=\mu_t\text{ if }\sigma_t=1 \right\}. $$
The positive masses ensure that these sets partition $P$. The cone $K(y)$ is constant on each nonempty $R_\sigma$, so Lemma 1 makes that entire set either dominated or undominated.

Nonemptiness is easily checked. Put
$$ a_\sigma=\sum_{\sigma_t=1}\mu_t, \qquad b_\sigma=\sum_{\sigma_t=*}\mu_t. $$
If there are no starred coordinates, nonemptiness means $q=a_\sigma$. Otherwise it means
$$ a_\sigma<q<a_\sigma+b_\sigma. $$
Necessity follows by summing the strict coordinate bounds. For sufficiency, assign each starred coordinate the value
$$ y_t=\mu_t\frac{q-a_\sigma}{b_\sigma}, $$
which satisfies all the strict bounds and the quota.

Call a nonempty pattern *good* when $R_\sigma$ is undominated, and define its closed face
$$ F_\sigma= \left\{ y\in P: y_t=0\text{ if }\sigma_t=0,\quad y_t=\mu_t\text{ if }\sigma_t=1 \right\}. $$
For $x\in F_\sigma$, all endpoint constraints of $\sigma$ remain active; additional coordinates may also reach endpoints. Hence
$$ K(x)\subseteq K_\sigma. $$
If $\sigma$ is good, this inclusion and Lemma 1 imply $x\in U$. Conversely, every point of $U$ belongs to a good pattern. Therefore
$$ U=\bigcup_{\sigma\text{ good}}F_\sigma. $$

An exact formula is
$$ \left( \bigwedge_t(0\leq y_t\leq\mu_t) \ \land\ \sum_t y_t=q \right) \land \bigvee_{\sigma\text{ good}} \left( \bigwedge_{\sigma_t=0}y_t=0 \ \land\ \bigwedge_{\sigma_t=1}y_t=\mu_t \right). $$
An empty disjunction means false.

Proposition 4. An exact description can be computed in
$$ 3^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,s,L) $$
time. In particular, the task is polynomial-time solvable for fixed $\tau$. Moreover, $U$ is a finite union of closed faces of $P$ and is therefore closed.

3. A polynomial construction for fixed affine contribution dimension

This section proves the additional theorem stated at the beginning.

Let
$$ d=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{a(t):t\in T\}. $$
If $d=0$, all contribution vectors are identical. Every feasible allocation then has the same profile, and irreflexivity gives $U=P$.

The construction below handles this case as well as all positive dimensions.

3.1. The feasible profiles form a slice of a zonotope

Define augmented vectors
$$ b_t=(a(t),1)\in\mathbb Q^{m+1} $$
and the zonotope
$$ Z=\left\{\sum_t z_tb_t:0\leq z_t\leq\mu_t\right\}. $$
Then the feasible profile set $Q=A(P)$ satisfies
$$ x\in Q\iff (x,q)\in Z. $$
Indeed, the last coordinate of $\sum_t z_tb_t$ is $\sum_t z_t$.

Let
$$ r=\dim\operatorname{span}\{b_t:t\in T\}. $$
For any fixed reference type $t_0$, this span is generated by $b_{t_0}$ and the vectors
$$ b_t-b_{t_0}=(a(t)-a(t_0),0). $$
The latter span has dimension $d$, and $b_{t_0}$ lies outside it because its last coordinate is $1$. Thus
$$ r=d+1. $$

Choose a rational basis matrix $J\in\mathbb Q^{(m+1)\times r}$ for this span, and write
$$ b_t=Jg_t,\qquad g_t\in\mathbb Q^r. $$
These data are computable by rational linear algebra in polynomial time, with polynomial coefficient bit lengths. In these coordinates,
$$ Z'=\left\{\sum_t z_tg_t:0\leq z_t\leq\mu_t\right\} $$
is full dimensional in $\mathbb R^r$: the $g_t$ span $\mathbb R^r$, and all segment lengths are positive.

3.2. Enumerating a small inequality description of the zonotope

For a nonzero $c\in\mathbb R^r$, its supporting face is
$$ \operatorname{argmax}_{\xi\in Z'}c\cdot\xi = \sum_{c\cdot g_t>0}\mu_tg_t + \sum_{c\cdot g_t=0}[0,\mu_t]g_t. $$
To see this, maximize each independent scalar contribution $z_t(c\cdot g_t)$ over its interval.

Its dimension is consequently
$$ \dim\operatorname{span}\{g_t:c\cdot g_t=0\}. $$
The equality follows because the free segments have positive lengths and their affine hull is exactly the span of their generators.

In particular, a facet has a normal annihilating $r-1=d$ linearly independent generators. Enumerate every independent subset of $d$ generators. Its orthogonal complement is one dimensional; choose a nonzero rational vector $c$ in it and include both orientations. For each orientation include
$$ c\cdot\xi\leq\beta(c), \qquad \beta(c)=\sum_t\mu_t\max\{c\cdot g_t,0\}. $$

Every included inequality is valid, and every facet inequality occurs, up to positive rescaling. The elementary facet representation theorem says that a full-dimensional polytope equals the intersection of its facet halfspaces. It applies here because $Z'$ is a bounded full-dimensional polytope. Thus the enumerated inequalities describe $Z'$ exactly.

Their number satisfies
$$ N\leq2\binom{\tau}{d}\leq2(\tau+1)^d. $$
For $d=0$, the empty subset yields the two orientations in $\mathbb R^1$.

Independence tests, nullspace computations, and the sums defining $\beta(c)$ use rational arithmetic of polynomial bit length. For example, a normal can be obtained from minors of a $d\times(d+1)$ matrix; determinant bit lengths are polynomial in the input bit lengths and dimensions. Summing the resulting rational terms also has polynomial bit length, since the bit length of a product of denominators is the sum of their bit lengths. The entire inequality list is therefore constructible in
$$ (\tau+1)^d\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time.

3.3. Using only $d$ profile variables

Let $\ell$ be the last row of $J$. Then
$$ \ell g_t=1 $$
for every type. Hence $\ell$ is nonzero.

Choose
$$ \xi_0=q\sum_t\mu_tg_t, $$
so $\ell\xi_0=q$, and choose a rational matrix $R\in\mathbb Q^{r\times d}$ whose columns form a basis of $\ker\ell$. Every point of the quota hyperplane has a unique representation
$$ \xi=\xi_0+Rw,\qquad w\in\mathbb R^d. $$
Let $x(w)$ be the first $m$ coordinates of $J(\xi_0+Rw)$.

Substituting this parameterization into the $N$ zonotope inequalities produces a conjunction $H(w)$ of rational weak linear inequalities. The preceding constructions prove
$$ Q=\{x(w):H(w)\}. $$
For completeness, a point satisfying $H$ lies in $Z'$ and has last coordinate $q$, so any representation by the bounded coordinates $z_t$ has $\sum_t z_t=q$. Conversely, every allocation in $P$ produces such a point.

3.4. Eliminating strict inequalities exactly

We need the following elementary form of Fourier–Motzkin elimination.

Lemma 5. A system of rational weak and strict linear inequalities in $(w,y)$ can have one coordinate of $w$ existentially eliminated while remaining a conjunction of rational weak and strict linear inequalities in the remaining variables.

If the original system has $M$ inequalities, the resulting system has at most $M^2+M$ inequalities.

Proof. Fix the remaining variables and write constraints involving the eliminated coordinate $u$ as lower or upper bounds:
$$ u\geq a_i,\quad u>a_i, \qquad u\leq b_j,\quad u<b_j. $$
Retain all constraints with zero coefficient of $u$. For every lower–upper pair require $a_i\leq b_j$, making this comparison strict if either original bound is strict.

These pairwise conditions are necessary. They are also sufficient. If both bound families are present, let $a$ be the largest lower endpoint and $b$ the smallest upper endpoint. When $a<b$, any point strictly between them works. When $a=b$, the pairwise conditions ensure that all bounds attaining these extrema are weak, so $u=a$ works. If one family is absent, choose $u$ sufficiently far in the permitted direction.

All endpoints are rational affine functions of the remaining variables, because the eliminated coordinate has a constant rational coefficient. The resulting comparisons are therefore rational linear comparisons. $\square$

Equalities can first be replaced by two weak inequalities. After eliminating $d$ variables, the number of inequalities is at most
$$ (M+1)^{2^d}. $$
Indeed, if $M_k$ is the count after $k$ eliminations, then
$$ M_{k+1}+1\leq(M_k+1)^2. $$

Coefficient lengths remain controlled. Clear denominators separately in each initial row. Each elimination combines two rows by multiplying them by constant coefficients and adding them, so a maximum coefficient bit length $B$ grows to at most $2B+O(1)$ in one step. After $d$ steps it is $O(2^d(B+1))$.

3.5. Disequalities do not require sign expansion

Write the DNF as
$$ C(u,v)=\bigvee_i T_i(u,v). $$
For each term, remove its disequality literals and define
$$ S_i(y)= \{w:H(w)\text{ and all remaining literals of }T_i(x(w),Ay)\}. $$
For fixed $y$, this is a convex set.

A removed disequality using feature $j$ is
$$ f_{ih}(w,y)=x_j(w)-(Ay)_j\neq0. $$
Define the quantified statements
$$ G_i(y)\iff\exists w\in S_i(y), $$
and
$$ G_{ih}^{+}(y)\iff \exists w\in S_i(y)\quad f_{ih}(w,y)>0, $$
$$ G_{ih}^{-}(y)\iff \exists w\in S_i(y)\quad f_{ih}(w,y)<0. $$
Each statement involves only a conjunction of rational linear equalities and weak or strict inequalities, and only $d$ quantified variables. Lemma 5 converts each into a quantifier-free conjunction in $y$.

By Lemma 2, the original term has a dominating feasible profile exactly when
$$ D_i(y)= G_i(y)\land \bigwedge_h\bigl(G_{ih}^{+}(y)\lor G_{ih}^{-}(y)\bigr) $$
holds. The explicit factor $G_i$ supplies the lemma’s nonemptiness hypothesis. Identically zero disequalities are handled correctly because both sign tests fail.

Consequently,
$$ D(y)=\bigvee_i D_i(y) $$
expresses existence of a dominating feasible profile, and an exact description of $U$ is
$$ \left( \bigwedge_t(0\leq y_t\leq\mu_t) \land \sum_t y_t=q \right) \land\neg D(y). $$

There are $O(s+1)$ elimination calls. Each starts with at most $N+2s+O(1)$ inequalities after splitting equalities, and its initial coefficient lengths are polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. The enumeration, elimination, and explicit formula assembly therefore take
$$ \bigl(s+(\tau+1)^d+2\bigr)^{O(2^d)} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time.

This proves the fixed affine dimension theorem. Its proof does not require transitivity or irreflexivity, except for the direct observation that $d=0$ gives $U=P$ under the promised preference relation.

4. Why the unrestricted formula representation remains difficult

The distinction between a Boolean circuit and an explicitly written formula is substantive here. I now prove the conversion equivalence, including the promised-order and arithmetic details.

4.1. Polynomial-time construction with sharing

For $y\in P$, the bits
$$ b_t^0=[y_t=0], \qquad b_t^1=[y_t=\mu_t] $$
determine $K(y)$. Proposition 3 gives a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm deciding dominance from these bits and the fixed society data. Define it arbitrarily on inconsistent bit patterns.

A time-$T$ deterministic computation can be converted into a Boolean circuit of polynomial size, constructible in polynomial time: encode its configurations using polynomially many bits and create a circuit layer for each transition. For a fixed machine, the local transition rules have constant-size Boolean implementations. Polynomially many layers and configuration bits therefore suffice.

Here $T=\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,s,L)$, and the society data can be hardwired. Substitute the endpoint comparisons for the circuit inputs, negate the dominance output, and conjoin the constraints defining $P$.

Thus an exact circuit description is computable in $\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,s,L)$ time.

4.2. Encoding an arbitrary Boolean circuit in one preference term

Let $B$ be a Boolean circuit with $n\geq1$ inputs, $k$ noninput gates, and one output. Use binary AND and OR gates, unary NOT gates, and optional constant gates.

Create
$$ \tau=2n+k+2 $$
types: input pairs $a_i,b_i$, one type per noninput gate, a scale type $\rho$, and a reservoir type $h$.

Let $M$ be the smallest power of two satisfying
$$ M\geq4(\tau-1), $$
and put $\eta=1/M$. This choice has $O(\log\tau)$ bits. Every type except $h$ has mass $\eta$, while
$$ \mu_h=1-(\tau-1)\eta\geq\frac34. $$
Set $q=1/2$.

For a Boolean assignment $x$, define
$$ y_{a_i}(x)=\eta(1-x_i), \qquad y_{b_i}(x)=\eta x_i. $$
Give every gate type and the scale type allocation $\eta/2$, and set the reservoir coordinate to make the total $1/2$. The allocation outside the reservoir is at most $1/4$, so
$$ \frac14\leq y_h(x)\leq\frac12<\mu_h. $$
Thus $y(x)\in P$; all gate, scale, and reservoir coordinates are interior.

For a direction $v$, write
$$ r=v_\rho,\qquad X_i=v_{a_i},\qquad X_g=v_g. $$
Impose the homogeneous linear constraints
$$ r>0, $$
$$ v_{a_i}\geq0,\quad v_{b_i}\geq0,\quad v_{a_i}+v_{b_i}=r $$
for each input, and
$$ 0\leq X_g\leq r $$
for each noninput gate. Gate constraints are:
$$ \begin{aligned} \text{AND: }& X_g\leq X_u,\quad X_g\leq X_v,\quad X_g\geq X_u+X_v-r,\\ \text{OR: }& X_g\geq X_u,\quad X_g\geq X_v,\quad X_g\leq X_u+X_v,\\ \text{NOT: }& X_g=r-X_u. \end{aligned} $$
A constant gate has $X_g=0$ or $X_g=r$, according to its value. Finally impose
$$ X_{\mathrm{out}}=r. $$

For each homogeneous constraint, introduce a feature equal to its linear form. For example, $r-X_g\geq0$ becomes a comparison $u_j\geq v_j$ on that feature. Let $C$ be the conjunction of all these comparisons.

This relation is transitive on all of $\mathbb R^m$: every conjunct is an equality, weak order comparison, or strict order comparison on one coordinate, and each is transitive. The strict comparison on the scale feature makes the conjunction irreflexive. Therefore the strict-partial-order promise is satisfied.

The numbers of types, features, and comparisons are $O(n+k)$, and the entire society has encoding length polynomial in the circuit encoding.

Lemma 6. For every Boolean assignment $x$,
$$ y(x)\notin U\iff B(x)=1. $$

Proof. Suppose $y(x)$ is dominated. By Lemma 1, there is a direction satisfying the displayed constraints and the active-bound signs.

If $x_i=0$, the coordinate $a_i$ is at its upper bound. Hence $v_{a_i}\leq0$; its imposed nonnegativity gives $X_i=0$.

If $x_i=1$, the coordinate $b_i$ is at its upper bound. Its direction is therefore zero, and the pair equality gives $X_i=r$.

Thus $X_i=rx_i$. Inductively, every gate direction is $r$ times its Boolean value. For AND, a zero input forces the output to zero using the upper inequalities and nonnegativity; two inputs equal to $r$ force the output to $r$ using the lower inequality and the upper bound. For OR, an input equal to $r$ forces the output to $r$; two zero inputs force it to zero. NOT and constants follow from their equalities.

Since $X_{\mathrm{out}}=r>0$, the Boolean output is one.

Conversely, suppose $B(x)=1$. Set $r=1$, every wire direction to its Boolean value, and $v_{b_i}=1-x_i$. Set
$$ v_h=-\sum_{t\neq h}v_t. $$
All preference constraints hold, and the direction sums to zero. At every input upper bound the direction is zero, and at every input lower bound it is nonnegative. All other allocation coordinates are interior, so they impose no sign constraints. Hence the direction belongs to $K(y(x))$, and Lemma 1 proves dominance. $\square$

4.3. Rational affine atoms on Boolean inputs have polynomial-size formulas

Suppose an exact formula $F(y)$ is obtained for the constructed society. Substitute the affine map $y(x)$. Every atom becomes a rational affine comparison in the Boolean inputs.

Here is a quantitative justification that such an atom can be converted to an ordinary Boolean formula of polynomial size.

Clear denominators using their positive product. The resulting integer coefficients have polynomial bit length in the substituted atom’s encoding length. Write the comparison as
$$ c_0+\sum_{i=1}^n c_ix_i\ \theta\ 0. $$
Move negative terms to the opposite side. Each side is a sum of at most $n+1$ nonnegative integers, each a constant or a constant multiplied by one input bit.

If every coefficient magnitude is smaller than $2^B$, a sufficient width for every resulting sum is
$$ W=B+\left\lceil\log_2(n+2)\right\rceil+2. $$
Indeed, each sum is smaller than $(n+1)2^B<2^W$. This explicitly includes space for constants and accumulated carries.

Carry-save addition reduces the collection to two $W$-bit integers in $O(\log(n+2))$ stages. A full adder computes its sum and carry from three bits by fixed-size Boolean functions, so each stage has constant bounded-fan-in depth.

The final addition has bounded-fan-in depth $O(\log(W+2))$: carry propagation is obtained by a binary prefix computation on pairs $(g,p)$ with associative composition
$$ (g,p)\circ(g',p') = \bigl(g\lor(p\land g'),\,p\land p'\bigr). $$
Comparison also has logarithmic depth. Combining the equality and greater-than flags of a more significant block with those of a less significant block uses the associative rule
$$ (G,E)\circ(G',E') = \bigl(G\lor(E\land G'),\,E\land E'\bigr). $$
Equality, disequality, and all order comparisons follow from these flags.

Therefore the atom has a bounded-fan-in circuit of depth
$$ O(\log(n+2)+\log(W+2)). $$
Unfolding its output into a formula gives at most exponentially many nodes in this depth, which is polynomial in $n+W$. This construction uses only the original inputs and constants.

4.4. The equivalence

Consider the conversion task:

Given a Boolean circuit, output an equivalent Boolean formula over its original input variables.

Theorem 7. Polynomial-time exact formula construction for the allocation problem exists if and only if polynomial-time circuit-to-formula conversion exists.

Proof.

For the forward implication, construct the society of Section 4.2 from $B$, obtain its exact formula $F$, and substitute $y(x)$. Lemma 6 gives
$$ F(y(x))=\neg B(x) $$
on Boolean assignments. Replace each rational affine atom using Section 4.3 and negate the resulting formula. A polynomial-time description algorithm produces only polynomially many bits, so both the number of atoms and their coefficient lengths are polynomially bounded.
