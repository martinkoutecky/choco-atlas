The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(T\), mass vector \(\mu\), and an oracle-accessible class \(\mathcal V_L\) of nonnegative typed coalition-value functions \(v:D_\mu\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), where \(D_\mu=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\tau}:x\le\mu\}\), \(v(0)=0\), and \(v(x)\le L\lVert x\rVert_1\), a query is a finite partition \(X=(x^1,\ldots,x^r)\) with \(\sum_jx^j=\mu\) revealing \(v(x^j)\). After \(q\) queries, determine the smallest \(K\) for which an output \(\widehat X\) can certify \(\max_Y V_{v'}(Y)\le K V_{v'}(\widehat X)\) for every \(v'\in\mathcal V_L\) consistent with the transcript.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m=\sum_t\mu_t\), without requiring \(m=1\).
Fix a known positive binary rational \(C\). Let \(D_\mu=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\tau}:x\le\mu\}\), with componentwise inequalities. Let \(\mathcal V_C\) contain all functions \(v:D_\mu\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) satisfying \(v(0)=0\) and \(v(x)\le C\lVert x\rVert_1\), with rational values at rational arguments. No continuity, monotonicity, or superadditivity is assumed.
A mass partition is a finite sequence \(X=(x^1,\ldots,x^r)\) of nonzero vectors summing to \(\mu\); identical vectors may occur repeatedly. Its welfare is \(V_v(X)=\sum_jv(x^j)\), and \(\operatorname{OPT}(v)=\sup_Y V_v(Y)\), over all finite mass partitions with real coordinates.
A query specifies a mass partition with binary-rational coordinates and reveals every constituent value exactly. The input is a feasible transcript of \(q\ge1\) queries; consistency means agreement with every revealed value. The output \(\widehat X\) must be one of the queried partitions.
A finite factor \(K\ge1\) is certified when \(\operatorname{OPT}(v')\le K V_{v'}(\widehat X)\) for every consistent \(v'\in\mathcal V_C\). The optimal certificate is \(+\infty\) if no finite factor is possible; the inequality also governs zero-welfare cases.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length and \(N\) the total number of listed coalition vectors. State running times in \(m,\tau,L,C,q,N\), counting the full query descriptions.

What is the smallest certifiable approximation factor for a given transcript, and can any finite \(q\) guarantee a finite factor uniformly over \(\mathcal V_C\), or is that impossible?

For unrestricted nonnegative coalition values on \(a\ge3\) discrete agents, establishing any uniform finite approximation factor requires \(2^{a-1}\) partition queries; the unique minimum query set consists of all partitions into one or two coalitions and gives the tight factor \(a\). [Sandholm et al.](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/coalstruct.aij.pdf)
Later discrete work characterizes minimum search sets for prescribed approximation factors. [Rahwan, Michalak, and Jennings](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/cs15-892F11/minimum%20coalition%20structure%20search.ijcai11.pdf)
No result settling this precise mass-partition oracle problem was identified in the literature search.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Exact certificate and impossibility of a uniform guarantee

Let the queried partitions be \(X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(q)}\). Write \(W_i\) for the revealed welfare of \(X^{(i)}\), counting repeated coalition vectors with their multiplicities, and let \(B=\max_{1\le i\le q}W_i\).

The smallest certifiable factor is \(Cm/B\) when \(B>0\), and \(+\infty\) when \(B=0\). When \(B>0\), any queried partition attaining \(B\) is an optimal output. The factor can be computed exactly in polynomial time in the full transcript length.

No finite number of queries guarantees a finite certificate uniformly over \(\mathcal V_C\). This holds already for \(\tau=1\), including adaptive query strategies.

1. A universal welfare upper bound

For every \(v\in\mathcal V_C\) and every finite mass partition \(Y=(y^1,\ldots,y^r)\), the defining bound gives

\(V_v(Y)=\sum_{j=1}^r v(y^j)\le C\sum_{j=1}^r\lVert y^j\rVert_1=C\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j=1}^r y_t^j=C\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=Cm.\)

The equalities use nonnegative coordinates and \(\sum_j y^j=\mu\). Taking the supremum over all permitted partitions yields \(\operatorname{OPT}(v)\le Cm\).

In particular, feasibility of the transcript implies \(0\le W_i\le Cm\) for every \(i\). Since \(C>0\) and every \(\mu_t>0\), we have \(Cm>0\).

2. Two unqueried coalitions always suffice

Let \(S\) be the set of distinct coalition vectors occurring anywhere in the transcript, and put \(n=|S|\le N\).

Lemma. There is a binary-rational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) such that both \(\alpha\mu\) and \((1-\alpha)\mu\) lie outside \(S\).

Proof. Choose a power of two \(D\ge 2n+2\), and consider the \(2n+1\) distinct candidates \(\alpha_k=k/D\), where \(1\le k\le 2n+1\). Every candidate lies strictly between zero and one.

For each fixed \(s\in S\), the equation \(\alpha\mu=s\) excludes at most one candidate: its first coordinate requires \(\alpha=s_1/\mu_1\), and \(\mu_1>0\). Likewise, \((1-\alpha)\mu=s\) excludes at most one candidate.

Thus all vectors in \(S\) together exclude at most \(2n\) candidates. At least one candidate remains. Its two coalition vectors are binary-rational, nonzero, componentwise bounded by \(\mu\), and sum to \(\mu\). This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

The two vectors may coincide; the definition explicitly permits repeated vectors.

3. The worst consistent optimum is exactly \(Cm\)

For \(s\in S\), let \(a(s)\) be its revealed value. Feasibility ensures that repeated occurrences have the same value and that \(0\le a(s)\le C\lVert s\rVert_1\).

Define a completion \(v^+\) on \(D_\mu\) by assigning:

  • \(v^+(0)=0\);
  • \(v^+(s)=a(s)\) for \(s\in S\);
  • \(v^+(x)=C\lVert x\rVert_1\) at every other argument.

This function is nonnegative, satisfies the required upper bound, and agrees with every revealed value. At rational arguments its values are rational: the observed values are rational by the premises, and \(C\lVert x\rVert_1\) is rational whenever \(x\) is rational. Consequently, \(v^+\in\mathcal V_C\).

Apply the lemma and let \(Y=(\alpha\mu,(1-\alpha)\mu)\). Neither constituent belongs to \(S\), so

\(V_{v^+}(Y)=C\lVert\alpha\mu\rVert_1+C\lVert(1-\alpha)\mu\rVert_1=C\alpha m+C(1-\alpha)m=Cm.\)

Together with Section 1, this proves \(\operatorname{OPT}(v^+)=Cm\). Hence

\(\max\{\operatorname{OPT}(v'):v'\in\mathcal V_C\text{ consistent with the transcript}\}=Cm.\)

The maximum is attained, and the attaining completion has an optimal partition consisting of two binary-rational coalitions. Thus this sharpness argument does not depend on using irrational coordinates.

4. The smallest certifiable factor

Fix a queried output \(X^{(i)}\). Its welfare is \(W_i\) under every consistent completion.

If \(W_i>0\), Section 1 shows that \(K=Cm/W_i\) is certified. This factor satisfies \(K\ge1\), since \(W_i\le Cm\). Conversely, the completion \(v^+\) from Section 3 forces every certified factor to satisfy \(Cm\le K W_i\). Therefore the smallest factor for this output is exactly \(Cm/W_i\).

If \(W_i=0\), that same completion has positive optimum \(Cm\), whereas \(K W_i=0\) for every finite \(K\). No finite factor is certified.

Minimizing over the permitted outputs therefore gives:

  • If \(B>0\), the optimal certificate is \(K_{\min}=Cm/B\), attained by any queried partition of welfare \(B\).
  • If \(B=0\), the optimal certificate is \(+\infty\).

In particular, an all-zero transcript cannot certify finite approximation even if the actual function happens to be identically zero: the certificate must also cover the consistent completion \(v^+\).

5. No finite query strategy guarantees a finite certificate

Here is a single counterexample function, independent of the query strategy. Define \(v_0(x)=0\) when \(x\in\mathbb Q^\tau\), and \(v_0(x)=C\lVert x\rVert_1\) otherwise.

This function belongs to \(\mathcal V_C\): it satisfies the required bounds, vanishes at zero, and has rational values at rational arguments. Every permitted query consists of rational vectors, so every answer is zero.

Nevertheless, choose an irrational \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). Because each \(\mu_t\) is positive and rational, both \(\alpha\mu\) and \((1-\alpha)\mu\) have irrational coordinates. Their partition has welfare \(Cm\), and Section 1 therefore gives \(\operatorname{OPT}(v_0)=Cm>0\).

Thus every permitted queried output has zero welfare while the optimum is positive. No finite factor is possible. This applies to every finite query count and survives adaptivity or randomization because the same fixed function answers every permitted query with zero.

The obstruction is also stronger than a zero-welfare example. For any rational \(0<\varepsilon\le C\), define \(v_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon\lVert x\rVert_1\) at rational vectors and \(v_\varepsilon(x)=C\lVert x\rVert_1\) elsewhere. This again belongs to \(\mathcal V_C\).

Every queried partition then has welfare \(\varepsilon m>0\), while the irrational split above attains \(Cm\). Every finite transcript consequently has exact optimal certificate \(C/\varepsilon\). Given any prescribed finite \(K\), choosing rational \(0<\varepsilon<C/K\) makes this certificate exceed \(K\), although every revealed constituent value is strictly positive.

6. Exact computation and running time

On the promised feasible input, the algorithm:

  • Reads the full query descriptions and revealed values.
  • Computes \(m=\sum_t\mu_t\) and each \(W_i\), preserving multiplicities.
  • Selects a queried partition of maximum welfare \(B\).
  • Returns \(+\infty\) if \(B=0\), and otherwise returns the exact rational \(Cm/B\) together with that partition.

There are \(O(N+\tau+q)\) rational arithmetic operations. Using standard binary numerator-denominator encodings, the intermediate numbers have \(O(L)\) bits: denominators in a sum can be represented by their product, whose bit length is bounded by the total input bit length. The numerators also have \(O(L)\) bits. Schoolbook arithmetic therefore gives the conservative bit-time bound \(O\!\left(L+(N+\tau+q)L^2\right)\), which is \(O(L^3)\), with \(O(L)\) space.

This bound counts the full query descriptions. It has no dependence on the numerical magnitudes of \(m\) or \(C\) beyond their binary encoding lengths already included in \(L\).

The cited discrete results study partitions of a finite agent set and their search guarantees. [Sandholm et al.](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/www/coalstruct.aij.pdf), [Rahwan, Michalak, and Jennings](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/cs15-892F11/minimum%20coalition%20structure%20search.ijcai11.pdf). Here, Section 2 proves that every finite transcript leaves an entirely unqueried two-coalition mass partition, which establishes the precise continuous certificate above.
