The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 2 (Complexity) — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support distribution μ over role-level agent types ρ, bundles \(B\), and payment balances π, a role graph \(H\), per-capita copies of the indivisible goods, encoded valuations, and a rational threshold \(K\), decide whether nonnegative rational masses \(x_d\) of finitely encoded deal templates fit within each source type’s mass and induce a distribution μ' with \(E(μ)-E(μ')\ge K\). Each template involves a clique of roles, conserves its participants’ goods, balances payments, and makes every participant strictly better off; \(E(ν)=∑_{z,z':(ρ(z),ρ(z'))\in E_H}ν_zν_{z'}e(z,z')\), using the paper’s pairwise envy notion.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(H\) be a simple undirected graph on \(r\) roles and let \(m\) be the number of good kinds. A type is \(z=(\rho,B,\pi)\), where \(\rho\) is a role, \(B\subseteq[m]\), and \(\pi\in\mathbb Q\) is cumulative money paid. Each role has a valuation \(v_\rho:2^{[m]}\to\mathbb Q\), with \(v_\rho(\varnothing)=0\); negative values are allowed. Valuations are encoded by Boolean circuits taking bundle indicators and outputting binary signed rational values with nonzero denominators.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Require \(\sum_z\mu_z=1\) and \(\sum_z\mu_z\pi(z)=0\). The per-capita inventory is \(q_g=\sum_{z:g\in B(z)}\mu_z\); copies of each kind are interchangeable, but each individual holds at most one copy of each kind.
A deal template chooses source types \(z_i=(\rho_i,B_i,\pi_i)\) from the initial support, with distinct roles forming a clique, and specifies destination bundles \(B'_i\subseteq[m]\) and rational payments \(p_i\). Require \(\sum_i\mathbf1_{g\in B_i}=\sum_i\mathbf1_{g\in B'_i}\) for every good kind, \(\sum_i p_i=0\), at least one changed bundle, and \(v_{\rho_i}(B'_i)-v_{\rho_i}(B_i)>p_i\) for every participant. Its destination types are \(z'_i=(\rho_i,B'_i,\pi_i+p_i)\); nonparticipants remain unchanged.
Choose any finite collection of such templates and rational masses \(x_d\ge0\), with \(\sum_d x_d>0\) and \(\sum_{d,i:z_i=z}x_d\le\mu_z\) for every source type. Apply them simultaneously, without reusing destination mass: \(\mu'=\mu+\sum_d x_d\sum_i(\delta_{z'_i}-\delta_{z_i})\), where \(\delta_z\) is unit mass at \(z\). Templates and their payments are existentially chosen, not supplied as an input catalogue.
For \(z=(\rho,B,\pi)\) and \(w=(\sigma,C,\eta)\), define \(e(z,w)=\max\{v_\rho(C)-\eta-v_\rho(B)+\pi,0\}\). Set \(E(\nu)=\sum_{z,w:\{\rho(z),\rho(w)\}\in E(H)}\nu_z\nu_w e(z,w)\), counting both directions of every role edge.
Given these data and \(K\in\mathbb Q\), the decision problem asks whether such masses induce \(E(\mu)-E(\mu')\ge K\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,r,L\).

Is this continuous envy-reducing deal problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

The discrete threshold problem is NP-complete for valuation languages for which welfare improvement is NP-complete ([2007 result](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2007/AAAI07-111.pdf)). A later result establishes NP-completeness of discrete strict envy reduction even for single-minded valuations ([full version](https://eprints.illc.uva.nl/id/eprint/571/1/PP-2016-35.text.pdf)).
No result settling exactly the mass-distribution problem defined above was located.

  • Normalized the distribution to total mass one and required mass-weighted payment balances to sum to zero, reflecting cumulative balanced payments.
  • Interpreted per-capita goods as interchangeable copies of good kinds, with bundles containing at most one copy of each kind and template-level conservation of multiplicities.
  • Used a simple undirected role graph, one participant per selected role, and visibility only across role edges.
  • Interpreted templates as existentially chosen rather than an explicitly supplied catalogue, and masses as simultaneous trades using only initial source mass.
  • Required a nonempty trading operation and at least one changed bundle per template, reflecting the discrete requirement that a deal changes the allocation.
  • Applied strict improvement to participants and left nonparticipants unchanged, using the stated individual-rationality exception for uninvolved agents rather than the decision display's unconditional strict inequalities.
  • Restricted payments to template participants, following the graded question's clique-local templates; imposed no sign or liquidity restrictions on payments or balances.
  • Allowed any rational threshold, as in the discrete threshold definition, and counted both directed envy comparisons across each undirected edge.

Result

The problem is NP-hard, even when \(r=\tau=2\), the role graph consists of a single edge, all valuations are nonnegative integer-valued, and \(K=\tfrac14\).

The proof uses a direct reduction from Boolean satisfiability and handles arbitrary finite collections of simultaneous fractional deals.

Construction

Let \(\varphi\) be a Boolean formula on variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\). Introduce the goods

\(G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_n,h,d\}\),

so \(m=n+2\). For a bundle \(B\), interpret the indicators of \(g_1,\ldots,g_n\) as a Boolean assignment, denoted \(a_B\).

There are two roles, \(A\) and \(B\), joined by an edge. Define their valuations as follows:

  • \(v_A(G)=1\).
  • For any bundle \(S\) containing \(h\) and excluding \(d\), set \(v_A(S)=4\) if \(\varphi(a_S)\) is true.
  • Set \(v_A(S)=0\) in all other cases.
  • Set \(v_B(G)=1\), and \(v_B(S)=0\) for every \(S\ne G\).

The first two rules cannot overlap because the second excludes \(d\). Both valuations satisfy \(v_A(\varnothing)=v_B(\varnothing)=0\).

These valuations have Boolean circuits of polynomial size: evaluate \(\varphi\) on the relevant bundle indicators, test the marker conditions and whether the bundle is \(G\), and output one of the constants \(0,1,4\), with denominator \(1\).

The initial support consists of

\(z_A=(A,\varnothing,0)\), \(\qquad z_B=(B,G,0)\),

each with mass \(\tfrac12\). Set \(K=\tfrac14\).

The masses sum to \(1\), cumulative money sums to \(0\), and every good has per-capita inventory \(\tfrac12\). Thus the instance satisfies all input requirements.

Initial envy

The only ordered pairs counted by the role graph are the two directions between the support types. Their envy values are

\(e(z_A,z_B)=\max\{v_A(G)-v_A(\varnothing),0\}=1\),

\(e(z_B,z_A)=\max\{v_B(\varnothing)-v_B(G),0\}=0\).

Consequently,

\(E(\mu)=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12\cdot(1+0)=\tfrac14\).

Since every summand defining \(E\) is nonnegative, meeting the chosen threshold requires \(E(\mu')=0\).

Satisfiable formulas yield a feasible deal

Suppose \(a\) satisfies \(\varphi\). Define

\(S=\{h\}\cup\{g_j:a(x_j)=1\}\), \(\qquad T=G\setminus S\).

The bundle \(S\) contains \(h\) and excludes \(d\), while \(T\) excludes \(h\). Hence

\(v_A(S)=4,\quad v_A(T)=0,\quad v_B(S)=v_B(T)=0\).

Here \(S\) and \(T\) are both proper subsets of \(G\): \(h\in S\) and \(d\in T\).

Choose the two-participant template

\(B'_A=S,\quad B'_B=T,\quad p_A=\tfrac32,\quad p_B=-\tfrac32\).

The roles form a clique. The destination bundles partition \(G\), so every good is conserved. Payments sum to zero, and both bundles change. Strict individual improvement holds because

\(v_A(S)-v_A(\varnothing)=4>\tfrac32=p_A\),

\(v_B(T)-v_B(G)=-1>-\tfrac32=p_B\).

Apply this single template with mass \(\tfrac12\). This uses exactly the available mass of each source. The final society has mass \(\tfrac12\) at each of

\(z'_A=(A,S,\tfrac32)\), \(\qquad z'_B=(B,T,-\tfrac32)\).

Its two directed envy values are

\(e(z'_A,z'_B)=\max\{0+\tfrac32-4+\tfrac32,0\}=0\),

\(e(z'_B,z'_A)=\max\{0-\tfrac32-0-\tfrac32,0\}=0\).

Therefore \(E(\mu')=0\), and the reduction equals \(K=\tfrac14\). All payments and masses are rational.

Unsatisfiable formulas admit no template

Suppose \(\varphi\) is unsatisfiable. Then both roles have the same valuation:

\(v_A(U)=v_B(U)=1\) when \(U=G\), and \(0\) otherwise.

A singleton template is impossible. Goods conservation forces its destination bundle to equal its source bundle, and payment balance forces its payment to be \(0\); its strict improvement inequality would therefore be \(0>0\).

Every possible template must consequently involve both roles. Because each role has exactly one initial support type, its sources must be \(z_A,z_B\).

Let its destination bundles be \(S,T\). Initially, the two participants jointly hold exactly one copy of every good. Conservation therefore implies that \(S,T\) partition \(G\).

For every such partition,

\(v_A(S)+v_B(T)\le 1\).

Indeed, if either bundle equals \(G\), the other is empty and the total value is \(1\). Otherwise neither bundle equals \(G\), and the total value is \(0\).

On the other hand, summing the two required strict improvement inequalities gives

\(v_A(S)+v_B(T)-v_A(\varnothing)-v_B(G)>p_A+p_B=0\).

Since the initial total valuation is \(1\), this requires

\(v_A(S)+v_B(T)>1\),

a contradiction.

Thus an unsatisfiable instance has no admissible template at all. In particular, arbitrary mixtures, fractional masses, and existentially chosen rational payments cannot produce a feasible collection with positive total mass.

Complexity and conclusion

The construction proves the equivalence

\(\varphi\text{ is satisfiable}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{the constructed continuous instance is a yes-instance}\).

If the formula encoding has length \(s\), the reduction takes \(\operatorname{poly}(s+n)\) time and produces \(m=n+2\), \(\tau=r=2\), and \(L=\operatorname{poly}(s+n)\).

Consequently, an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,r,L)\) time would decide Boolean satisfiability in polynomial time. The continuous problem is therefore NP-hard, and has no polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

This settles the requested hardness question. It does not assert membership of the general problem in \(\mathrm{NP}\).
