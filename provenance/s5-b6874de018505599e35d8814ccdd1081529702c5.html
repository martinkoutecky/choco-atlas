The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) roles with rational masses \(\mu_i>0\) summing to \(1\), a symmetric rational exposure matrix \(W\), and rational \(q\in(1/2,1]\), decide whether there are colour masses \(x_{i,b},x_{i,r}\ge0\) with \(x_{i,b}+x_{i,r}=\mu_i\) such that at least mass \(q\) belongs to roles whose strict local majority opposes the strict global majority. Here \(B=\sum_i x_{i,b}\), \(R=\sum_i x_{i,r}\), and \(\ell_{i,c}=\sum_j W_{ij}x_{j,c}\); a tie at either level counts as no winner.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) roles with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_i\), where \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=2\) colours, \(b,r\).
The input also contains a symmetric matrix \(W\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{\tau\times\tau}\) with \(W_{ii}=0\), and a binary rational threshold \(q\in(1/2,1]\). Exposure weights need not be normalized; zero rows are allowed. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A colouring splits each role into real masses \(x_{i,b},x_{i,r}\ge0\) with \(x_{i,b}+x_{i,r}=\mu_i\). Define \(B=\sum_i x_{i,b}\), \(R=\sum_i x_{i,r}\), and \(\ell_{i,c}=\sum_j W_{ij}x_{j,c}\).
Every member of role \(i\) has these local exposures, regardless of its own colour. Role \(i\) is under illusion precisely when \(B>R\) and \(\ell_{i,r}>\ell_{i,b}\), or \(R>B\) and \(\ell_{i,b}>\ell_{i,r}\). A tie at either level yields no illusion.
The decision problem asks whether a colouring exists with \(\sum_{i\text{ under illusion}}\mu_i\ge q\).

Is this continuous majority-illusion decision problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m=2\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), including the encoding of \(q\).

For finite undirected simple networks, deciding whether a binary colouring puts at least a fraction \(q\) of vertices under strict majority illusion is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete for every fixed rational \(q\in(1/2,1]\); this holds even on bipartite networks ([verified result](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/191897/7/17441_pub.pdf)).
No result settling the exact mass formulation above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The exposure matrix had no sign domain; require nonnegative rational weights, consistent with neighbourhood exposure.
  • The diagonal was unspecified; require zero diagonal to carry over open neighbourhoods, interpreting this as no exposure within a role.
  • Weights have no imposed upper bound or row normalization; allow arbitrary nonnegative rational weights and zero rows.
  • Colour masses may be real, and roles may split between colours; all members of a role share its local exposure and its entire mass counts when that role is under illusion.
  • Treat the rational threshold as part of the input, and define L as the total binary input length; the number of colours is fixed at m=2.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

For \(m=2\), the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete when \(q\) is part of the input. This remains true with dyadic rational masses and threshold, and with \(W_{ij}\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\).

An exact deterministic algorithm runs in \(2^\tau(\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) time. The endpoint \(q=1\) is solvable in \((\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) time. Thus the hardness for every fixed threshold in the cited [binary network result](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/191897/7/17441_pub.pdf) does not transfer to this continuous formulation.

The reduction below establishes hardness for the stated problem with encoded \(q\); it does not assert hardness for each fixed \(q<1\).

1. Signed formulation and scaling

Define \(y_i=x_{i,b}-x_{i,r}\). The colouring constraints become \(-\mu_i\le y_i\le\mu_i\), and

\[ B-R=\sum_i y_i,\qquad \ell_{i,b}-\ell_{i,r}=(Wy)_i. \]

Swapping the two colours preserves the set of illuded roles. Consequently, a successful colouring may be assumed to satisfy \(\sum_i y_i>0\). Its illuded roles are then precisely those satisfying \((Wy)_i<0\).

The bounds on \(y_i\) do not restrict which strict sign patterns are realizable. Indeed, for any \(y\in\mathbb R^\tau\), let

\[ a=\min_i\frac{\mu_i}{1+|y_i|}>0. \]

Then \(|ay_i|<\mu_i\), and multiplication by \(a\) preserves all strict signs. The masses

\[ x_{i,b}=\frac{\mu_i+ay_i}{2}, \qquad x_{i,r}=\frac{\mu_i-ay_i}{2} \]

therefore define a valid colouring with the same global and local signs.

Hence the decision problem is equivalent to asking whether there exists \(y\in\mathbb R^\tau\) such that

\[ \sum_i y_i>0, \qquad \sum_{\{i:(Wy)_i<0\}}\mu_i\ge q. \]

This equivalence uses the positivity of every \(\mu_i\).

2. Polynomial verification and the exact algorithm

For a prescribed subset \(S\) of roles, consider the rational linear program

\[ \begin{array}{ll} \text{maximize}&\varepsilon\\ \text{subject to} &-\mu_i\le y_i\le\mu_i\quad\text{for every }i,\\ &\displaystyle\sum_i y_i\ge\varepsilon,\\ &(Wy)_i\le-\varepsilon\quad\text{for every }i\in S,\\ &0\le\varepsilon\le1. \end{array} \]

Its optimum is positive exactly when a colouring exists in which blue is the strict global majority and every role in \(S\) is illuded.

To verify this equivalence, a positive optimum directly gives the required strict inequalities. Conversely, a colouring satisfying them has a positive minimum among its global margin and the finitely many required local margins; choosing \(\varepsilon\) smaller than that minimum and at most \(1\) makes the linear program feasible with positive objective.

The program has \(\tau+1\) variables and \(O(\tau)\) constraints, with encoding length polynomial in \(\tau+L\). Exact rational linear programming therefore tests it in \((\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) time.

A certificate for the decision problem is a subset \(S\), encoded by \(\tau\) bits. A verifier checks \(\sum_{i\in S}\mu_i\ge q\) exactly and then solves this linear program. This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Enumerating all subsets gives the running time

\[ 2^\tau(\tau+L)^{O(1)}. \]

Here \(L\) includes the encoding of \(q\); all threshold comparisons use exact rational arithmetic.

When \(q=1\), positivity of the masses means every role must be illuded. Only the program with \(S=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) is needed, proving the polynomial-time endpoint claim.

3. Reduction from INDEPENDENT SET

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem INDEPENDENT SET: given a finite undirected simple graph \(G\) with \(n\ge1\) vertices and an integer \(1\le k\le n\), determine whether it has an independent set of size at least \(k\).

Write \(e=|E(G)|\). Construct two sets of roles:

  • \(U\): a vertex role \(v_i\) for each graph vertex, and an edge role \(h_{uv}\) for each graph edge;
  • \(V\): coordinate roles \(z_1,\ldots,z_n,a,c\).

Thus \(|U|=n+e\) and

\[ \tau=2n+e+2. \]

The vertex roles will be optional; all edge and coordinate roles will be compulsory.

Exposure matrix

Set all weights within \(U\) and within \(V\) to zero. Specify the cross weights below and impose symmetry.

For a vertex role \(v_i\), set

\[ W_{v_i,z_j}=3-\mathbf1_{\{i=j\}}, \qquad W_{v_i,a}=4, \qquad W_{v_i,c}=3. \]

For an edge role \(h_{uv}\), set

\[ W_{h_{uv},z_j}=3+\mathbf1_{\{j\in\{u,v\}\}}, \qquad W_{h_{uv},a}=1, \qquad W_{h_{uv},c}=3. \]

Every cross weight is strictly positive and belongs to \(\{1,2,3,4\}\). The resulting matrix is symmetric, nonnegative, and has zero diagonal.

For any signed vector \(y\), define

\[ T=\sum_{j=1}^n y_{z_j}+y_a+y_c, \qquad \xi_j=y_{z_j}-y_a. \]

Direct expansion gives the identities

\[ (Wy)_{v_i}=-\xi_i+3T, \qquad (Wy)_{h_{uv}}=\xi_u+\xi_v+3T, \qquad (Wy)_c=3\sum_{u\in U}y_u. \]

Masses and threshold

There are \(M=e+n+2\) compulsory roles. Let

\[ D=n+(n+1)M, \]

and let \(H\) be the least power of two satisfying \(H\ge D\).

Assign each vertex role mass \(1/H\). Assign each compulsory role other than \(c\) mass \((n+1)/H\), and assign \(c\) mass

\[ \mu_c=\frac{n+1+H-D}{H}. \]

All masses are positive, and their sum is

\[ \frac{n+(n+1)M+H-D}{H}=1. \]

Set

\[ q=1-\frac{n-k}{H}. \]

Since \(H\ge D>2n\), this threshold lies in \((1/2,1]\). All masses and \(q\) are dyadic rationals.

Each compulsory role has mass at least \((n+1)/H\). Missing even one such role would leave illuded mass at most

\[ 1-\frac{n+1}{H} < 1-\frac{n-k}{H} =q. \]

Thus any successful colouring must put every compulsory role under illusion. Once they are all illuded, reaching \(q\) requires at least \(k\) illuded vertex roles.

4. Soundness

Suppose the constructed society has a successful colouring. Swap colours if necessary, obtaining a signed vector satisfying \(\sum_i y_i>0\).

Role \(c\) is compulsory, so

\[ (Wy)_c=3\sum_{u\in U}y_u<0. \]

Therefore \(\sum_{u\in U}y_u<0\). Combining this with the strict global majority gives

\[ T=\sum_i y_i-\sum_{u\in U}y_u>0. \]

If vertex role \(v_i\) is illuded, its exposure identity implies

\[ -\xi_i+3T<0, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \xi_i>3T>0. \]

Every edge role is compulsory. Consequently, for every graph edge \(\{u,v\}\),

\[ \xi_u+\xi_v+3T<0. \]

Both endpoint vertex roles cannot be illuded: that would give \(\xi_u+\xi_v>6T>0\), contradicting the edge inequality.

Thus the illuded vertex roles form an independent set. The mass argument shows that at least \(k\) of them are illuded, so \(G\) has an independent set of size at least \(k\).

5. Completeness

Conversely, let \(S\) be an independent set of size at least \(k\). Put \(p=n+e\) and \(t=1/12\), and define

\[ \xi_i= \begin{cases} 1,&i\in S,\\ -2,&i\notin S. \end{cases} \]

Choose the signed coordinates

\[ y_{z_i}=\xi_i,\qquad y_a=0,\qquad y_c=t-\sum_i\xi_i, \qquad y_u=-\frac{t}{2p}\quad(u\in U). \]

Then \(T=t\), while

\[ \sum_i y_i=t-\frac t2=\frac t2>0. \]

Every coordinate role in \(V\) has strictly negative exposure: all its neighbours lie in \(U\), every corresponding weight is positive, and every \(y_u\) is negative.

For \(i\in S\),

\[ (Wy)_{v_i}=-1+3t=-\frac34<0. \]

Because \(S\) is independent, an edge has at most one endpoint in \(S\), so \(\xi_u+\xi_v\le-1\). Every edge role therefore satisfies

\[ (Wy)_{h_{uv}} =\xi_u+\xi_v+3t \le-\frac34<0. \]

Hence all compulsory roles and all vertex roles indexed by \(S\) are illuded. The scaling argument from Section 1 converts this signed vector into a valid colouring without changing these signs. Its illuded mass is at least

\[ 1-\frac nH+\frac{|S|}{H} \ge 1-\frac{n-k}{H} =q. \]

This proves completeness.

6. Encoding and complexity

The reduction has \(\tau=2n+e+2\) roles. Since \(D=O(\tau^2)\) and \(H<2D\), each mass and the threshold have \(O(\log\tau)\) encoding length. Every matrix entry is a constant-size integer. The whole output has polynomial length and can be constructed in polynomial time.

In particular, hardness does not require a threshold with exponentially many precision bits. It already holds with \(q\) having \(O(\log\tau)\) bits and dyadic denominator \(O(\tau^2)\).

Combining the reduction with polynomial verification proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for the stated input problem. For \(m=2\), the resulting complexity statements are:

  • General encoded \(q\): \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; exact algorithm \(2^\tau(\tau+L)^{O(1)}\).
  • Fixed support size \(\tau\): polynomial time in \(L\).
  • Endpoint \(q=1\): \((\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) time.
