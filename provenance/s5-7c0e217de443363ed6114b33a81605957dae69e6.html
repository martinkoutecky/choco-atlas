The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many contract classes and a support \(T\) of types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), where each type specifies its bounded fully substitutable valuation, contract roles, and compatible local network position, define feasible aggregate allocations \(x_{t,\Psi}\geq 0\) over integral bundles \(\Psi\), with \(\sum_\Psi x_{t,\Psi}=\mu_t\) and contract balance respecting the stated compatibility structure. Let \(P^*_\mu\) be the prices supporting a competitive equilibrium of this aggregate market. Does the process that samples type \(t\) with probability \(\mu_t\), selects a demanded bundle, and updates prices on its un demanded incident contracts produce average prices \(\bar p\) with \(\mathbb{E}[d_\infty(\bar p,P^*_\mu)]\leq\varepsilon\); what is its running-time dependence on \(m,\tau,L,R,\Delta,\) and \(\varepsilon\), especially when masses are small?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
There are \(m\geq1\) ordered contract classes. Type \(t\) has disjoint buying and selling sets \(B_t,S_t\), incident set \(\Omega_t=B_t\cup S_t\), and signs \(\chi_{t\omega}=1,-1,0\) for buying, selling, and nonincident classes. Each class has both buyer and seller types; every opposite-role pair within a class is compatible, with no additional matching constraints. Put \(\Delta=\max_t|\Omega_t|\).
Each valuation is given by its complete table \(v_t:2^{\Omega_t}\to\mathbb Z\), with \(v_t(\emptyset)=0\) and \(|v_t(\Psi)|\leq R\), where \(R\geq1\). Negative values are allowed; no bundle is prohibited. Define \(D_t(p)=\arg\max_{\Psi\subseteq\Omega_t}\{v_t(\Psi)-\sum_{\omega\in\Psi}\chi_{t\omega}p_\omega\}\), for unrestricted real prices.
Assume full substitutability: writing \(Z_t(p)=\{(\chi_{t\omega}\mathbf1_{\omega\in\Psi})_\omega:\Psi\in D_t(p)\}\), whenever \(p'\geq p\), each \(z\in Z_t(p)\) has some \(z'\in Z_t(p')\) with \(z'_\omega\geq z_\omega\) on every unchanged-price coordinate.
An aggregate allocation satisfies \(x_{t,\Psi}\geq0\), \(\sum_\Psi x_{t,\Psi}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t\sum_{\Psi\ni\omega}\chi_{t\omega}x_{t,\Psi}=0\) for every class. Let \(P^*_\mu\) be the prices admitting such an allocation with \(x_{t,\Psi}>0\) only for \(\Psi\in D_t(p)\), and define \(d_\infty(p,P)=\inf_{q\in P}\|p-q\|_\infty\).
For a positive rational step size \(\eta\), start at an arbitrary rational \(p^0\in[-R,R]^m\). At each step independently sample \(t\) with probability \(\mu_t\), select the lexicographically smallest maximum-cardinality bundle \(d_t(p^k)\in D_t(p^k)\), and set \(p^{k+1}_\omega=p^k_\omega-\eta\chi_{t\omega}\mathbf1_{\omega\in\Omega_t\setminus d_t(p^k)}\), without clipping. Return \(\bar p=N^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}p^k\).

Is finding random rational prices satisfying \(\mathbb E[d_\infty(\bar p,P^*_\mu)]\leq\varepsilon\) polynomial-time solvable or NP-hard?
Does the specified clock process attain this guarantee for every input and initialization with suitable \(\eta,N\), and what is its running-time dependence on \(m,\tau,L,R,\Delta,\varepsilon^{-1}\), and \(\mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t\), including whether it is polynomial in the encoded mass lengths?

For finite bilateral trading networks, uniform agent sampling has a stated expected distance convergence rate \(O(N^{-1/2})\) with the prescribed horizon-dependent step size. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20868v1)
A divisible extension of the finite market also has a welfare LP and an indirect-utility price dual; this does not establish a rate for the mass-sampled process above. No result settling that exact process was identified in the search. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20868v1)

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Proven conclusion. Exact rational equilibrium prices are computable in polynomial time in the explicit input length \(L\). However, the specified clock process fails on some admissible inputs for every choice of step size and horizon—even when \(m=1\), \(\tau=2\), \(R=\Delta=1\), and \(\mu_{\min}=1/4\).

1. The welfare LP yields exact equilibrium prices. For each type and bundle, define its signed incidence vector by \(a_{t,\Psi,\omega}=\chi_{t\omega}\mathbf1_{\omega\in\Psi}\). Consider the rational linear program

\[ \max_{x\geq0}\ \sum_t\sum_{\Psi\subseteq\Omega_t}v_t(\Psi)x_{t,\Psi}, \qquad \sum_\Psi x_{t,\Psi}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_t\sum_\Psi a_{t,\Psi,\omega}x_{t,\Psi}=0 \quad(\omega=1,\ldots,m). \]

This is exactly welfare maximization over the aggregate allocations in the question. Its feasible set is nonempty: assign every type its empty bundle. It is closed and bounded, since \(0\leq x_{t,\Psi}\leq\mu_t\). Its optimum \(W^*\) therefore exists, and \(0\leq W^*\leq R\).

Introducing unrestricted dual variables \(u_t\) for the mass equalities and \(p_\omega\) for the balance equalities gives the dual

\[ \min_{u,p}\ \sum_t\mu_tu_t, \qquad u_t+\sum_\omega a_{t,\Psi,\omega}p_\omega\geq v_t(\Psi) \quad(t,\Psi\subseteq\Omega_t). \]

The empty-bundle inequalities imply \(u_t\geq0\). The dual is feasible, for example with \(p=0\) and \(u_t=R\). By finite-dimensional LP strong duality, it has an attained optimum equal to \(W^*\).

Write \(U_t(p)=\max_{\Psi\subseteq\Omega_t}\{v_t(\Psi)-a_{t,\Psi}\cdot p\}\). Dual feasibility is equivalent to \(u_t\geq U_t(p)\). Because every \(\mu_t\) is positive, every dual optimum satisfies \(u_t=U_t(p)\): otherwise lowering that coordinate strictly improves its objective.

We now verify that the optimal dual prices are precisely \(P^*_\mu\). For any primal-feasible \(x\) and dual-feasible \((u,p)\), the mass and balance equalities give

\[ \sum_t\mu_tu_t-\sum_{t,\Psi}v_t(\Psi)x_{t,\Psi} = \sum_{t,\Psi}x_{t,\Psi} \bigl(u_t+a_{t,\Psi}\cdot p-v_t(\Psi)\bigr). \]

Every summand on the right is nonnegative.

If both solutions are optimal, the left side is zero. Consequently, \(x_{t,\Psi}>0\) implies \(v_t(\Psi)-a_{t,\Psi}\cdot p=u_t=U_t(p)\). Thus every positively allocated bundle belongs to \(D_t(p)\), proving \(p\in P^*_\mu\).

Conversely, suppose \(p\in P^*_\mu\), witnessed by an aggregate allocation \(x\). Set \(u_t=U_t(p)\). This is dual-feasible, and every positively weighted summand in the displayed identity is zero. Hence the primal and dual objectives agree, so both solutions are optimal.

Therefore \(P^*_\mu\) is nonempty and is exactly the price projection of the dual optimal set. This argument does not require full substitutability.

2. Complexity and mass encoding. Let \(K=\sum_t2^{|\Omega_t|}\) be the number of explicitly supplied valuation entries. The dual has \(m+\tau\) variables and \(K\) inequalities. Its constraint coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\); its right-hand sides are the given integer valuations; its objective coefficients are the given rational masses.

The standard exact rational-LP theorem states that a rational LP can be solved, including producing an exact rational optimum when one exists, in time polynomial in its binary description length. Polynomial-time exact primal and dual solution algorithms are discussed in this [primary research source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-023-01956-2). Here all its hypotheses hold: the LP is finite, rational, feasible, and has an attained finite optimum.

Constructing its explicit matrix requires only polynomially many bits in \(L\): the complete tables imply \(K=O(L)\), and the explicitly listed types and incidences imply \(m,\tau=O(L)\). Thus an exact rational \(p^*\in P^*_\mu\), with polynomial output bit length, is computable in \(\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bit operations.

Returning \(p^*\) deterministically, viewed as a constant random variable, gives \(\mathbb E[d_\infty(p^*,P^*_\mu)]=0\).

The dependence can equivalently be expressed as \(\operatorname{poly}(m+\tau,K,L)\), with \(K\leq\tau2^\Delta\). In particular:

  • The table-size dependence is already included in \(L\).
  • Valuations enter through their binary lengths, bounded by \(O(\log(1+R))\) per entry.
  • No approximation dependence on \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) is needed.
  • Masses enter through their encoded lengths; no numerical factor \(\mu_{\min}^{-1}\) is needed.

3. A counterexample to the specified clock. Take one class \(\omega\) and two types \(b,s\), with

\[ B_b=\{\omega\},\quad S_b=\emptyset,\quad B_s=\emptyset,\quad S_s=\{\omega\},\qquad \mu_b=\frac34,\quad\mu_s=\frac14. \]

Set \(v_b(\{\omega\})=1\) and \(v_s(\{\omega\})=0\), with both empty-bundle values zero. All bundles are allowed, and \(R=\Delta=1\).

These valuations satisfy the stated full-substitutability condition. In one dimension, if the price increases strictly, there are no unchanged-price coordinates, so the required coordinate inequalities are vacuous and demand remains nonempty. If the price is unchanged, choose the original demand vector.

At scalar price \(p\), the buyer’s contract utility is \(1-p\), and the seller’s is \(p\). We claim \(P^*_\mu=\{1\}\).

If \(p<1\), all buyer mass \(3/4\) strictly demands the contract. Seller supply is at most \(1/4\), so balance is impossible. If \(p>1\), buyer demand is zero and all seller mass \(1/4\) strictly demands the contract, again making balance impossible.

At \(p=1\), allocate buyer mass \(1/4\) to the contract and buyer mass \(1/2\) to the empty bundle, and allocate all seller mass \(1/4\) to the contract. Both buyer bundles are demanded, the seller demands the contract, and balance holds. This proves the claim.

Now initialize \(p^0=1/2\), which belongs to \([-R,R]\). Both types uniquely demand \(\{\omega\}\). Hence \(\Omega_t\setminus d_t(p^0)=\emptyset\) for either sampled type, and the update is zero.

Inductively, for every sampling sequence, every positive rational \(\eta\), and every positive integer \(N\),

\[ p^k=\frac12\quad\text{for all }k, \qquad \bar p=\frac12, \qquad d_\infty(\bar p,P^*_\mu)=\frac12. \]

Thus the required guarantee fails whenever \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\). There is no universally sufficient horizon, and therefore no guarantee-producing running-time bound for this clock process.

4. Why the finite bilateral argument does not extend. Define \(z_t(p)=a_{t,d_t(p)}\), \(F(p)=\sum_t\mu_tU_t(p)\), and \(c=\sum_t\mu_t\chi_t\). Since \(d_t(p)\) maximizes indirect utility, \(-z_t(p)\in\partial U_t(p)\): this follows directly by evaluating that same bundle at any other price.

The specified update is \(p^{k+1}=p^k-\eta(\chi_t-z_t(p^k))\). Its conditional expected descent vector therefore satisfies

\[ \mathbb E[\chi_t-z_t(p)\mid p] = c-\sum_t\mu_tz_t(p) \in\partial\bigl(F(p)+c\cdot p\bigr). \]

It is a stochastic subgradient update for the tilted objective \(F(p)+c\cdot p\). Equilibrium prices instead minimize \(F\), by the dual characterization above.

In the finite bilateral model, each contract has exactly one buyer and one seller. Uniform agent weights make their incidence terms cancel, so \(c=0\); the cited paper uses uniform sampling in its clock and subgradient argument. [Source, Section 4.2 and Appendix A.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20868v1)

That cancellation is absent from the present hypotheses. In the counterexample, \(c=1/2\), and the clock freezes at a minimizer of the tilted objective that is not an equilibrium price.
