The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given atomless doctor and hospital populations with rational masses on finitely many complete types, finite contract forms, and type-specific choice rules satisfying the paper's assumptions, find a measurable matching with integral portfolios for individual agents and at most one contract per doctor–hospital pair. Stability requires individual rationality and the absence of any nonempty finite blocking set of individual contracts. Find a doctor-greatest stable matching \(M^D\) such that, for every stable matching \(M\), \(C_d(M^D_d \cup M_d)=M^D_d\) for almost every doctor \(d\); give it a finite type or configuration representation.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Doctors and hospitals occupy disjoint unions of Lebesgue intervals, one per type, of prescribed positive mass. A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary-rational masses; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Masses need not sum to one. There are \(m\) contract forms, with permitted forms specified for each doctor-type–hospital-type pair; an individual contract \(x=(d,h,f)\) names both individuals and its form.
Each type has an integer portfolio bound \(q_t\), with \(q=\max_t q_t\). Its deterministic choice rule \(C_a(S)\subseteq S\) is specified by an explicit finite table for menus of size at most \(2q\): entries record partner types, forms, equality of partner identities, and the order of partners' interval coordinates. Thus repeated partner types remain distinct individuals, and all ties are resolved by the table. The tables are promised to extend to choices on all finite menus, always selecting at most \(q_t\) contracts, with the following properties.
Every choice is unitary: it contains at most one contract per doctor–hospital pair. Every rule satisfies IRC; doctor rules are substitutable. Hospital rules satisfy unilateral substitutability: for finite \(Y\) and contracts \(x,z\) incident to \(h\), if \(d(z)\notin d(Y)\), then \(z\in C_h(Y\cup\{x,z\})\Rightarrow z\in C_h(Y\cup\{z\})\), where \(d(Y)\) denotes the doctors named in \(Y\).
A matching \(M\) is a measurable set of individual contracts with portfolios \(M_a\) of size at most \(q_t\) and at most one contract per pair. Require balanced incidence: for every form and measurable endpoint subsets, the doctor-side and hospital-side integrals counting their connecting contracts agree.
Individual rationality means \(C_a(M_a)=M_a\) for every individual. A nonempty finite \(Z\subseteq X\setminus M\) blocks when \(Z_a\subseteq C_a(M_a\cup Z_a)\) for every incident individual; stability excludes every such \(Z\), including deviations by measure-zero sets of individuals.
A doctor-greatest matching \(M^D\) is stable and satisfies \(C_d(M^D_d\cup M_d)=M^D_d\) for almost every doctor, for each stable \(M\). A finite representation consists of finitely many rational interval pieces and rational piecewise-affine, measure-preserving partner bijections, each labeled by a contract form; stability is evaluated for the represented individuals themselves.
Is the problem of producing such a finite representation of a doctor-greatest stable matching, or determining that none exists, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,q,L\), or is it NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total binary input length?
In finite markets, cumulative offers reach the unique doctor-greatest stable allocation after at most \(|X|\) proposals; this gives polynomial time when choice evaluation is polynomial-time ([Yang](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10756v1)).
Continuum pairwise matching has distributional existence and finite-market limit results ([Greinecker–Kah](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.3982/ECTA16228)); these do not establish the computational classification above. No result settling this exact problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Realized each positive-mass type as a Lebesgue interval; retained arbitrary positive rational masses without normalizing either side.
  • Specified permitted contract forms by endpoint types and retained individual partner identities, including multiple distinct partners of the same type.
  • Added finite individual portfolio bounds because integral portfolios alone do not specify a finitely encodable domain.
  • Encoded choice rules by explicit tables on menus of size at most twice the maximum portfolio bound, recording identity equality and coordinate order; promised extensions satisfying the discrete assumptions on all finite menus. Every menu queried by individual rationality, a possible blocking set, or a comparison of two matching portfolios has this bounded size.
  • Used deterministic table entries to resolve within-type identity ties rather than replacing identity-level choice by choice over aggregate masses.
  • Required balanced incidence so that doctor and hospital contract masses describe the same relationships.
  • Applied individual rationality and finite blocking pointwise, including null sets; retained the stated almost-everywhere quantifier only for doctor dominance.
  • Made finite output mean rational interval pieces and measure-preserving piecewise-affine partner bijections.
  • Allowed a negative answer when no doctor-greatest matching with the specified finite representation exists, since finite-market existence does not itself guarantee continuum existence or representability.

1. Bounded blocking witnesses

I use substitutability in the following form: for finite menus $S\subseteq T$,

$$ C_d(T)\cap S\subseteq C_d(S). $$

Lemma 1. If a finite set $Z$ blocks a matching $M$, then a nonempty subset of $Z$ blocks $M$, involves just one hospital, and contains at most $q$ contracts with distinct doctors.

Proof. Choose a hospital $h$ incident to $Z$ and put $W=Z_h$. The blocking condition gives

$$ W\subseteq C_h(M_h\cup W). $$

Since hospital choice is unitary and selects at most $q_h$ contracts, the contracts in $W$ involve distinct doctors and

$$ 1\leq |W|\leq q_h\leq q. $$

For each $z\in W$, the original blocking condition and doctor substitutability imply

$$ z\in C_{d(z)}(M_{d(z)}\cup Z_{d(z)}) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z\in C_{d(z)}(M_{d(z)}\cup\{z\}). $$

Each doctor incident to $W$ receives exactly one proposed contract. Thus every incident doctor accepts its proposed contract, and the hospital accepts all of $W$. Consequently, $W$ blocks. $\square$

It follows that individual rationality and stability depend only on the supplied tables: individual rationality uses menus of size at most $q$, and these blocking witnesses use hospital menus of size at most $2q$ and doctor menus of size at most $q+1$.

When $q=0$, the empty matching is the only matching, is stable, and is doctor-greatest. Henceforth assume $q\geq 1$.

2. A useful dichotomy for hospital types

Call a hospital type *single-form per doctor type* if, for every doctor type, at most one form is permitted with that hospital type.

2.1 Single-form hospital types have substitutable choices

Lemma 2. For a hospital type that is single-form per doctor type, unilateral substitutability implies ordinary substitutability.

Proof. Fix a hospital $h$ of that type, finite menus $S\subseteq T$, and $z\in C_h(T)\cap S$. There is only one permitted contract between $h$ and the doctor named by $z$. Thus no contract in $T\setminus\{z\}$ names that doctor.

Remove the contracts of $T\setminus S$ one at a time. At any removal step, write the current menu as

$$ Y\cup\{x,z\}, $$

where $x$ is being removed. Since $d(z)\notin d(Y)$, unilateral substitutability preserves acceptance of $z$:

$$ z\in C_h(Y\cup\{x,z\}) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z\in C_h(Y\cup\{z\}). $$

After all removals, $z\in C_h(S)$. This is substitutability. $\square$

Consequently, any hospital-centered block involving such a hospital contains a singleton block. Indeed, hospital substitutability preserves acceptance of each proposed contract when the other proposals are removed, and Lemma 1 supplies the corresponding doctor acceptance.

2.2 Multiple forms force a large explicit table

Lemma 3. Suppose a hospital type permits two distinct forms with some positive-mass doctor type. Its explicit table contains at least $2^{2q}$ distinct menu-pattern rows. In particular,

$$ 2^{2q}\leq L. $$

Proof. Fix a hospital of that type. Choose $2q$ distinct doctors of the specified doctor type, with strictly increasing interval coordinates. This is possible because the type occupies an interval of positive mass.

For each binary word of length $2q$, offer one contract with each doctor, using the first or second permitted form according to the word. These menus have distinct recorded patterns: their ordered sequences of forms differ. All are permitted menus of size $2q$.

The explicit table must contain their $2^{2q}$ distinct rows, regardless of whether some rows have the same choice output. Each row requires at least one bit of input. Therefore $L\geq 2^{2q}$. $\square$

This lemma uses the explicit, exhaustive table encoding stipulated in the question. It would not hold for a succinct encoding that represents many rows by one rule.

3. Ordered-coordinate feasibility

The main algorithmic ingredient avoids choosing a representation cell separately for every proposed doctor.

Lemma 4. Let $h$ range over a bounded rational interval, with specified endpoint inclusion. For each of $k$ positions, suppose the allowable pairs $(h,d)$ have the following description:

  • membership is constant whenever the signs of a given collection of rational affine expressions are fixed;
  • membership at a rational pair can be evaluated in polynomial time.

Allow additional requirements that some positions equal specified rational affine functions of $h$. Then existence of coordinates satisfying

$$ d_1<d_2<\cdots<d_k $$

and all these requirements can be decided in polynomial time in the description length and $k$. A feasible instance has a rational solution of polynomial encoding length.

Proof. Each affine boundary in the $(h,d)$ plane is either vertical, of the form $h=c$, or can be written

$$ d=a h+b. $$

Include the graphs of all required affine values among these boundaries.

Partition the $h$-interval at its endpoints, all vertical boundary values, and all intersections of distinct nonvertical boundary graphs. With $N$ boundary lines, there are at most a polynomial number of partition cells. Treat every included partition point as a singleton cell.

On an open $h$-cell, the order of the nonvertical boundary values is fixed. Identical graphs can be merged. For each fixed $h$ in this cell, these values partition the $d$-axis into:

  • point levels at boundary values;
  • open interval levels between consecutive boundary values.

On each level, all affine signs are fixed. Hence the allowability of each position is constant throughout the corresponding strip or graph over the open $h$-cell. A prescribed affine value permits only its point level.

Choose a rational sample $h$ in the cell and evaluate the allowable levels. We must choose a level for each position, in increasing order. Consecutive positions may use the same level precisely when that level is an open interval. They cannot use the same point level.

This condition is checked by a finite dynamic program. If $R_i$ is the set of levels reachable after placing position $i$, then a level $j$ is in $R_{i+1}$ exactly when it is allowable for position $i+1$ and some $\ell\in R_i$ satisfies

$$ \ell<j, $$

or satisfies $\ell=j$ with $j$ an open interval level. The number of positions and levels is polynomially bounded.

The dynamic program is exact. Any increasing coordinate tuple determines such a level sequence. Conversely, for a feasible level sequence, all positions assigned to the same open interval can be placed in strictly increasing order inside that interval. Point levels determine their coordinates uniquely.

For a singleton $h$-cell, evaluate and sort the boundary values at that rational $h$, and use the same dynamic program.

All partition values are rational intersections of rational lines, so their encoding lengths are polynomially bounded. Rational midpoints provide samples for open $h$-cells. If an open $d$-interval must contain $r$ positions, place them at fractions

$$ \frac{1}{r+1},\frac{2}{r+1},\ldots,\frac{r}{r+1} $$

of its length. This yields a rational feasible tuple with polynomial encoding length. Unbounded outer levels, if needed, admit equally simple rational choices. $\square$

The same proof accommodates a menu pattern in which distinct partner identities have equal coordinates. Combine identities with equal coordinates into a single coordinate block, intersect their allowable predicates, and impose strict order between the blocks. Distinct individuals of the same type cannot have the same interval coordinate; such a pattern is discarded.

4. Polynomial verification for variable $q$

Theorem 5. Let $M$ be supplied as a valid finite representation of binary length $B$, including its exact endpoint membership conventions. Under the promises and explicit-table encoding in the question:

  • Individual rationality and stability of $M$ are decidable in time polynomial jointly in $m,\tau,q,L,B$.
  • If a finite blocking set exists, one can produce a hospital-centered block of at most $q$ contracts at rational coordinates, with polynomial total encoding length.

Here validity means that the representation describes a matching with the required incidence balance, portfolio bounds, and unitarity. The theorem concerns the additional individual-rationality and stability checks.

4.1 Decompose the entire populations

Start with every doctor and hospital population interval, not merely the domains of represented partner maps.

Subdivide these intervals at:

  • every endpoint of a represented affine piece;
  • every coordinate where two active partner-coordinate functions coincide.

Include singleton cells for included subdivision points. Regions without any represented contracts remain in the decomposition and have empty portfolios.

The inverse of each affine partner bijection is rational affine on its image piece. Consequently, on every resulting cell, an individual's current portfolio is given by a fixed list of rational affine partner functions with fixed types and forms. Equality and order among current partners are fixed on the cell.

There are polynomially many cells: the representation has at most $B$ described pieces, and pairs of affine functions contribute at most one nonidentical crossing each. All subdivision coordinates have polynomial encoding length.

For individual rationality, evaluate the appropriate table row on each cell. The menu pattern is constant there. This includes singleton cells and cells with empty portfolios.

4.2 Doctor acceptance is determined by planar affine signs

Fix a hospital type and a permitted proposed form $f$. Define the predicate

$$ A_{u,f}(h,d) $$

for a doctor of type $u$ to mean that the proposed contract $z=(d,h,f)$ is outside $M$ and satisfies

$$ z\in C_d(M_d\cup\{z\}). $$

On a doctor cell, write the current hospital-coordinate functions as

$$ p_1(d),\ldots,p_r(d), \qquad r\leq q. $$

The augmented menu pattern is determined by the current fixed pattern, the proposed type and form, and the comparisons between $h$ and the values $p_j(d)$. Identity equality additionally requires the same partner type and equal coordinate.

Thus $A_{u,f}$ is constant on sign cells cut out by doctor-cell boundaries and lines

$$ h=p_j(d). $$

Its value at a rational pair is obtained by evaluating a table menu of size at most $q+1\leq 2q$. Exclusion of an already represented contract is checked at the same time.

Taking all doctor cells together gives polynomially many rational boundary lines for all relevant doctor-acceptance predicates.

4.3 Hospitals with one form per doctor type

For these hospital types, Lemmas 1 and 2 reduce every block to a singleton.

Fix a hospital cell. Its current doctor-coordinate functions are

$$ g_1(h),\ldots,g_r(h). $$

Add the lines

$$ d=g_j(h) $$

to the planar arrangement used above. They determine the proposed doctor's order and identity relative to the hospital's current partners.

On every resulting sign cell, both augmented menu patterns are fixed. Evaluate the doctor and hospital choices on a rational sample. A singleton blocks exactly when the contract is outside $M$ and both endpoints select it.

There are polynomially many sign cells, types, and forms, so this case is polynomial-time checkable.

4.4 Hospitals permitting alternative forms

Fix a hospital type permitting two forms with some doctor type. By Lemma 3,

$$ 2^{2q}\leq L. $$

Fix a hospital cell with current portfolio size $r$. Enumerate a hospital table row whose menu size is

$$ n=r+k\leq 2q, \qquad 1\leq k\leq q_h. $$

Mark $r$ of its contract positions as current contracts; all other positions are proposed contracts. There are at most

$$ 2^n\leq 2^{2q}\leq L $$

markings per row.

Discard a marking unless:

  • the marked current contracts have the types and forms of the actual current portfolio;
  • no identity receives two current contracts;
  • no identity receives two proposed contracts;
  • the row selects every proposed contract;
  • each proposed contract is permitted.

The restrictions on current and proposed identity multiplicities are necessary because both the current matching and the selected proposals are unitary.

The alignment of current contracts with their marked positions is determined by their coordinate order and types. More explicitly, use coordinate order and, for a coordinate tie, type index as a canonical ordering. Within a type, distinct individuals have distinct coordinates. Once the current positions are marked, their ordered list must match the ordered actual current portfolio, including forms. There is therefore no additional factorial enumeration.

Each current identity is now pinned to its actual affine coordinate $g_j(h)$. Each proposed contract imposes its doctor's acceptance predicate $A_{u,f}(h,d)$. The row specifies all partner-identity equalities and coordinate orders.

Combine equal-coordinate identities into coordinate blocks. For each block, intersect the acceptance predicates of its proposed contracts and impose all current-coordinate pins in that block. Distinct coordinate blocks must occur in strict increasing order.

This is exactly the problem in Lemma 4. Its boundary lines consist of:

  • the polynomially many doctor-acceptance boundaries;
  • the current hospital partner functions;
  • the population and cell boundaries.

Hence each row and marking can be checked in polynomial time.

There are at most $L$ table rows and at most $L$ markings per row. Multiplying these counts by the polynomial number of hospital cells and the polynomial cost of Lemma 4 still gives a polynomial in $m,\tau,q,L,B$.

4.5 Correctness and rational witnesses

Suppose the algorithm finds a feasible row and marking. The current contracts are exactly $M_h$; the proposed contracts are outside $M$; the hospital selects all proposals; and every proposed doctor accepts its proposal against its current portfolio. The proposed identities are distinct. Thus these contracts form a blocking set.

Conversely, suppose $M$ has a block. Lemma 1 supplies a hospital-centered block $W$ with distinct doctors and at most $q$ contracts.

For a single-form hospital type, a singleton sub-block is found by Section 4.3. Otherwise, the actual menu $M_h\cup W$ has a supplied table row, and the enumeration includes its correct current-versus-proposed marking. Its real coordinates satisfy the corresponding ordered feasibility instance. Lemma 4 therefore finds that instance feasible.

The rational-coordinate construction in Lemma 4 preserves all required strict inequalities, equalities, endpoint memberships, and acceptance predicates. It produces a rational block with polynomial encoding length. $\square$

The two important ingredients are different: coordinate ordering removes the earlier enumeration of one representation cell per doctor, while the explicit-table size absorbs the enumeration of current-versus-proposed positions.

5. Comparing two supplied representations

Proposition 6. Given two valid finite representations $M$ and $N$, the condition

$$ C_d(M_d\cup N_d)=M_d $$

for almost every doctor is polynomial-time checkable jointly in the input and representation lengths.

Proof. Decompose every doctor population interval using endpoints and crossings of partner functions from both representations. On each positive-length cell, the union menu has at most $2q$ contracts and a fixed pattern, after identifying duplicate contracts.

Evaluate its supplied table row and compare the selected contracts with $M_d$. Equality either holds throughout that cell or fails throughout it. Singleton cells can be ignored because the condition is almost everywhere. $\square$

This proposition checks one supplied comparison. It does not check dominance over all stable matchings.

6. Alternative forms can require a genuine two-contract block

The following instance both explains why singleton verification is insufficient in general and shows that hospital capacity exceeding one is compatible with a finite doctor-greatest representation.

6.1 Populations and choices

There are two doctor types and one hospital type:

$$ D_A=(0,1),\qquad D_B=(1,2),\qquad H=(2,3). $$

All masses are one. Both forms $0$ and $1$ are permitted for every doctor–hospital pair. Doctor capacities are one, and hospital capacity is two.

Write a doctor's local coordinate as $t$:

$$ d=t\quad\text{for type }A, \qquad d=1+t\quad\text{for type }B. $$

Write a hospital's coordinate as $h=2+s$.

Each doctor chooses the contract with the smallest hospital coordinate, preferring form $1$ to form $0$ when the hospital identity is the same. This is choice of the best contract under a strict total order. It is unitary, substitutable, and satisfies IRC.

Each hospital first selects the lowest-coordinate doctor represented in the menu from each doctor type, if that type is present. Call these its selected identities. It chooses one contract per selected identity as follows:

  • If every selected identity has form $1$ available, choose form $1$ for every selected identity.
  • Otherwise, choose form $0$ wherever available, and form $1$ for any selected identity having only form $1$ available.

This defines choices on every finite menu and selects at most two contracts.

6.2 Hospital IRC and unilateral substitutability

The hospital rule is unitary by construction.

To prove IRC, let $C_h(S)\subseteq T\subseteq S$. The selected minimum-coordinate identity of each present doctor type is retained in $T$, so the selected identities do not change.

If all selected identities had form $1$ available in $S$, their chosen form-$1$ contracts remain in $T$, and the same branch applies.

Otherwise, some selected identity lacked form $1$ in $S$ and still lacks it in $T$. The second branch therefore still applies. Every chosen form-$0$ contract is retained, and every selected identity originally having only form $1$ retains that chosen contract. Hence

$$ C_h(T)=C_h(S). $$

For unilateral substitutability, suppose $d(z)\notin d(Y)$ and

$$ z\in C_h(Y\cup\{x,z\}). $$

The doctor named by $z$ is a minimum-coordinate identity of its type in that menu. Removing $x$ cannot introduce a lower-coordinate doctor. Thus this identity remains selected in $Y\cup\{z\}$.

In the reduced menu, $z$ is the only contract naming its doctor, because $d(z)\notin d(Y)$. The rule must therefore choose $z$. This proves the promised unilateral substitutability.

6.3 An individually rational matching with no singleton block

Define $M^0$ by matching, for every $t\in(0,1)$, both doctors with local coordinate $t$ to hospital $2+t$, using form $0$.

The two partner maps are translations:

$$ d\longmapsto d+2 \quad\text{on }D_A, \qquad d\longmapsto d+1 \quad\text{on }D_B. $$

They preserve Lebesgue measure. Their labeled graphs give balanced incidence. All individuals choose their current portfolios, so $M^0$ is individually rational.

Consider a missing contract between a doctor with local coordinate $t$ and hospital $2+s$.

  • If $s>t$, the doctor rejects the worse hospital.
  • If $s<t$, the hospital rejects that doctor: its current doctor of the same type has local coordinate $s<t$.
  • If $s=t$, the only missing form is $1$. The doctor accepts it, but the hospital rejects it because its other selected doctor still has only form $0$ available. The hospital remains in the second branch of its form rule.

Thus $M^0$ has no singleton block.

Nevertheless, at any hospital $2+t$, let $W$ consist of the two form-$1$ contracts with its current doctors. Both doctors accept their respective proposal. Both selected identities now have form $1$ available, so the hospital chooses both proposals. Therefore $W$ blocks $M^0$.

6.4 A finite doctor-greatest stable matching

Define $M^1$ using the same two translations but labeling every contract with form $1$.

This matching is balanced and individually rational.

To prove stability, suppose a finite block existed and use Lemma 1 to obtain a hospital-centered block at hospital $2+s$. A doctor with local coordinate $t$ accepts a missing contract against $M^1$ only if

$$ s<t. $$

Indeed, a hospital with larger coordinate is rejected, and at the current hospital the only missing contract has the inferior form $0$.

But the hospital's current doctor of each type has local coordinate $s$. Every proposed doctor of that type has larger coordinate $t>s$. The hospital therefore retains its current minimum-coordinate identity of each type and selects no proposal. This contradicts blocking. Hence $M^1$ is stable.

It remains to prove doctor-greatestness against arbitrary stable matchings, not just finite representations.

Let $N$ be any balanced stable matching. Individual rationality implies that each doctor has at most one contract and each hospital has at most one doctor of each type.

Fix a doctor type $u$. Let $T_u$ be its total incidence, summed over both forms. Balanced incidence gives

$$ T_u\leq 1. $$

If $T_u<1$, the unmatched type-$u$ doctors have positive measure, and the hospitals lacking a type-$u$ doctor have positive measure. Choose one of each. Their form-$0$ contract is accepted by both: the doctor is unmatched, and the hospital selects the new identity for its missing type, with its only offered form. This blocks. Therefore

$$ T_u=1. $$

Almost every type-$u$ doctor is matched, and almost every hospital has one type-$u$ doctor.

For measurable local-coordinate sets $E,F\subseteq(0,1)$, let $N_u(E,F)$ denote the incidence between type-$u$ doctors in $E$ and hospitals in $F$, summed over forms. For every $c\in(0,1)$, balance and almost-everywhere fullness imply

$$ N_u((0,c),(0,1))=c = N_u((0,1),(0,c)). $$

Subtracting the common internal incidence gives

$$ N_u((0,c),[c,1)) = N_u([c,1),(0,c)). $$

Suppose these equal crossing incidences were positive. Then there would be positive-measure sets of:

  • doctors with local coordinate $t<c$ matched to hospitals with local coordinate at least $c$;
  • hospitals with local coordinate $s<c$ matched to type-$u$ doctors with local coordinate at least $c$.

Choose an unlinked doctor–hospital pair from these sets. This is possible because a fixed doctor has only one current partner, whereas the second set has positive measure.

Their form-$0$ contract blocks: the doctor prefers the strictly lower hospital coordinate, and the hospital selects the strictly lower type-$u$ doctor. The proposed identity has only its form-$0$ contract available, so the hospital accepts that contract regardless of the other type's forms. This contradiction shows that both crossing incidences are zero.

Apply this conclusion to all rational $c\in(0,1)$. Each cut has a null set of doctors incident to crossing contracts. Outside the union of these countably many null sets, every contract must have equal local coordinates at its endpoints: any unequal coordinates can be separated by a rational cut.

Thus, for almost every doctor with local coordinate $t$, $N_d$ consists of one contract with hospital $2+t$, in either form. Since $M^1_d$ uses form $1$ with that same hospital,

$$ C_d(M^1_d\cup N_d)=M^1_d $$

for almost every doctor. Therefore $M^1$ is doctor-greatest and has a finite rational affine representation. $\square$

7. What remains open

The verification theorem removes a genuine computational obstacle from the earlier partial result: stability of a supplied representation is polynomial-time checkable even when $q$ varies. It also distinguishes two necessary verification regimes, as the example demonstrates.

It does not bound the length of a representation that must be produced. Nor does checking dominance against one supplied representation settle dominance against every balanced stable matching, which may have no finite representation.

The [finite cumulative-offer theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10756v1) concerns a finite contract set. An additional continuum construction argument is still needed to obtain the algorithm requested here.
