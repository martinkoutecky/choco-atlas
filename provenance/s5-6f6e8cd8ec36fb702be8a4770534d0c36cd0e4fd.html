1. Result

The problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), for both utility models. The dependence on the requested tolerance can be made polynomial in \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

The support encoding reduces exactly to a finite Fisher market with \(\tau\) buyers. Below, I prove this reduction, give explicit conditioning bounds, and establish:

  • a rational ellipsoid algorithm with polynomial iteration and bit complexity;
  • an accelerated price algorithm with an explicit real-arithmetic iteration bound of order \(\widetilde O(\varepsilon^{-1/2})\) for each fixed instance.

The second bound has numerical conditioning factors that can be exponentially large in the input length. It therefore does not, by itself, establish polynomial bit complexity.

2. Exact reduction to a finite buyer list

Define the effective budgets and their sum by
\( w_t=\rho_t B_t,\qquad W=\sum_{t\in T}w_t. \)

Replace each type \(t\) by one buyer with budget \(w_t\), the same valuations, and aggregate bundle
\( y_t=\rho_t x_t. \)

Because \(\rho_t>0\), this is a bijection between bundles satisfying
\( p^\top x_t\le B_t \)
and bundles satisfying
\( p^\top y_t\le w_t. \)
Under this bijection, either utility is multiplied by the positive constant \(\rho_t\). Thus optimal bundles correspond exactly, including all tied optima. Market clearing becomes
\( \sum_t y_{tj}=1. \)

Moreover, the finite market’s price objective is exactly the given objective:
\( F_\mu(z)=\sum_j e^{z_j}+\sum_t w_t h_t(z). \)

The reduction uses \(\tau\) buyers and polynomial-length rational arithmetic. It does not require replicating buyers according to a common denominator of the masses.

Consequently, the continuum-of-goods framework of [Gao–Kroer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03025) is unnecessary for this encoding. The finite-market framework of [Chen–Jiang–So](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07759v1) applies after this exact aggregation.

3. A convex formulation with rational gradients

Write \(F=F_\mu\). Introduce positive variables \(\beta_t\) and consider the convex program
\( f(p,\beta)=\sum_j p_j-\sum_t w_t\log\beta_t \)
subject to
\( p_j\ge v_{tj}\beta_t\quad\text{for every }t,j. \)
For quasi-linear utilities, additionally impose
\( \beta_t\le1. \)

For fixed positive \(p\), the objective decreases as each \(\beta_t\) increases. Its minimizing choice is therefore
\( \beta_t(p)=\min_{j:v_{tj}>0}\frac{p_j}{v_{tj}} \)
in the linear model, and
\( \beta_t(p)=\min\left\{1,\min_{j:v_{tj}>0}\frac{p_j}{v_{tj}}\right\} \)
in the quasi-linear model. Every minimum is over a nonempty set because there is no zero valuation row.

It follows directly that
\( f(p,\beta(p))=F(\log p). \)
In particular, every feasible pair satisfies
\( F(\log p)\le f(p,\beta). \)

Attainment of the minimum

For fixed positive \(\beta\), minimizing over \(p\) gives
\( p_j=\max_t v_{tj}\beta_t. \)
These prices are positive because there is no zero column.

Let
\( r_t=\max_j v_{tj}>0. \)
The resulting objective in \(\beta\) satisfies
\( \sum_j\max_t v_{tj}\beta_t-\sum_t w_t\log\beta_t \ge \sum_t\left(\frac{r_t}{\tau}\beta_t-w_t\log\beta_t\right). \)

To justify the inequality, the first sum is at least \(r_t\beta_t\) for each \(t\), and hence at least their average.

Each summand on the right is bounded below and tends to infinity as its argument tends either to zero or to infinity. Consequently, every nonempty sublevel set of the objective is contained in a compact box bounded away from zero. In the quasi-linear model, intersect this box with \(\beta_t\le1\). Continuity then gives an attained minimum in either model.

Denote its value by \(f^*\). The preceding identities prove
\( f^*=\min_{z\in\mathbb R^m}F(z). \)

The oracle uses rational arithmetic

At a positive rational pair,
\( \nabla f(p,\beta)= \left(\mathbf 1_m,-\left(\frac{w_t}{\beta_t}\right)_{t\in T}\right) \)
is rational.

All feasibility constraints are rational linear inequalities. Thus feasibility cuts and objective-gradient cuts can be computed exactly with rational arithmetic. Logarithms are needed only to select the best candidate at the end.

4. Explicit bounds on optimal prices and auxiliary variables

Define
\( V_t=\sum_j v_{tj},\qquad c_t=\frac{w_t}{V_t+w_t},\qquad c=\min_t c_t, \)
and
\( a=\min_j\max_t v_{tj}c_t. \)
Also define
\( v_{\min}=\min_{t,j:v_{tj}>0}v_{tj},\qquad v_{\max}=\max_{t,j}v_{tj},\qquad C=\frac{W}{v_{\min}}. \)

All these quantities are positive rationals; furthermore, \(c<1\).

Claim. Every optimum satisfies
\( a\le p_j^*\le W,\qquad c\le\beta_t^*\le C. \)

Proof. The program is convex and differentiable on its positive domain. Slater’s condition holds: choose positive \(\beta\), strictly below \(1\) where required, and choose every price sufficiently large.

Let \(\lambda_{tj}\ge0\) be the multipliers for \(v_{tj}\beta_t-p_j\le0\). Let \(\alpha_t\ge0\) be the multipliers for \(\beta_t\le1\), with \(\alpha_t=0\) in the linear model. The KKT conditions give
\( \sum_t\lambda_{tj}=1 \)
and
\( w_t=\beta_t^*\sum_jv_{tj}\lambda_{tj}+\alpha_t\beta_t^*. \)

Complementary slackness yields
\( W=\sum_jp_j^*+\sum_t\alpha_t\beta_t^*. \)
Thus every \(p_j^*\le W\).

Since \(0\le\lambda_{tj}\le1\), in the linear model,
\( \beta_t^*\ge w_t/V_t. \)
In the quasi-linear model, either \(\beta_t^*=1\), or complementary slackness gives \(\alpha_t=0\) and the same inequality. In both cases,
\( \beta_t^*\ge c_t. \)

Hence
\( p_j^*\ge\max_t v_{tj}c_t\ge a. \)
Finally, using any positive valuation in row \(t\),
\( \beta_t^*\le p_j^*/v_{tj}\le W/v_{\min}=C. \)
This proves the claim.

Bounds in terms of input length

Under ordinary binary numerator–denominator encoding, the following conservative bounds hold:
\( W\le2^L,\qquad v_{\min}\ge2^{-L},\qquad v_{\max}\le2^L,\qquad w_t\ge2^{-2L}. \)
Therefore,
\( c\ge\frac{2^{-3L}}{m+1},\qquad a\ge\frac{2^{-4L}}{m+1},\qquad C\le2^{2L}. \)

The displayed rational parameters have bit lengths \(O(L+\log(m+\tau))\). Their numerical values may be exponentially small or large, but their logarithms have polynomial size.

5. An explicit polynomial-time ellipsoid algorithm

Let
\( d=m+\tau. \)
Because both \(m\) and \(\tau\) are positive, \(d\ge2\).

Restrict the convex program to the rational polytope \(K\) defined by
\( a/2\le p_j\le2W,\qquad c/4\le\beta_t\le2C, \)
together with the market inequalities and, in the quasi-linear model, \(\beta_t\le1\).

Section 4 shows that \(K\) contains an optimum of the unrestricted program.

An explicit interior ball and Lipschitz bound

Take the rational point
\( p_j^0=3W/2,\qquad \beta_t^0=c/2. \)
Its market-constraint slack is at least \(W\), since
\( v_{tj}c/2\le v_{tj}c_t/2\le w_t/2\le W/2. \)

Define
\( s=\min\left\{ \frac W4,\frac c8,\frac14, \frac{W}{2(1+v_{\max})} \right\}. \)
The closed Euclidean ball of radius \(s\) about this point lies in \(K\):

  • the price-bound slacks are at least \(W/2\);
  • the auxiliary-variable lower-bound slacks equal \(c/4\);
  • the quasi-linear upper-bound slacks are at least \(1/2\);
  • each market-constraint normal has norm at most \(1+v_{\max}\).

Choose the power of two
\( R=2^{\left\lceil\log_2\!\left(2d(1+W+C)\right)\right\rceil}. \)
Then \(K\) lies in the ball of radius \(R\) about the origin.

On \(K\),
\( \|\nabla f\|_2\le\|\nabla f\|_1 \le m+\frac{4W}{c}. \)
Define
\( G=m+\frac{4W}{c}. \)
Integration along line segments in \(K\) proves that \(f\) is \(G\)-Lipschitz there.

A full-dimensional nearly optimal ball

Let \(x^*\) be an optimum in \(K\), and let \(x^0\) be the interior point above. Set
\( \theta=\frac{\varepsilon}{8GR},\qquad r=\theta s=\frac{\varepsilon s}{8GR}. \)

The ball of radius \(r\) about
\( (1-\theta)x^*+\theta x^0 \)
lies in \(K\). Indeed, every point in it can be written as
\( (1-\theta)x^*+\theta u \)
with \(u\) in the radius-\(s\) ball about \(x^0\).

For such a point, convexity and the Lipschitz bound give
\( f((1-\theta)x^*+\theta u) \le f^*+\theta(f(u)-f^*) \le f^*+2\theta GR =f^*+\varepsilon/4. \)

Thus \(K\cap\{f\le f^*+\varepsilon/4\}\) contains a full-dimensional ball of radius \(r\), even when the optimum lies on the boundary of \(K\).

Cuts and iteration count

Start with the radius-\(R\) ball.

At each rational center \(x\):

  • If \(x\notin K\), use a violated linear inequality to make a central cut containing \(K\).
  • If \(x\in K\), record \(x\) and make the central cut

\( \nabla f(x)^\top(y-x)\le0. \)

The second cut contains every \(y\) with \(f(y)\le f(x)\), because convexity implies
\( f(y)\ge f(x)+\nabla f(x)^\top(y-x). \)

Use a rounded rational ellipsoid update with volume reduction at least
\( \exp\!\left(-\frac1{4(d+1)}\right) \)
per cut. Its construction is verified in Section 6.

It suffices to make
\( N= 4d(d+1)\left( 1+\left\lceil\log_2\frac{8GR^2}{\varepsilon s}\right\rceil \right) \)
cuts.

Suppose that every feasible queried center had value greater than \(f^*+\varepsilon/2\). Every cut would then preserve the radius-\(r\) ball constructed above. But after \(N\) cuts the ellipsoid’s volume is less than the volume of that ball, since
\( e^{-N/(4(d+1))}R^d<r^d. \)
This is a contradiction. Therefore, a recorded center satisfies
\( f(x)\le f^*+\varepsilon/2. \)

Evaluate each recorded objective with absolute error at most \(\varepsilon/8\), and select the smallest approximate value. The selected feasible rational pair \((\widehat p,\widehat\beta)\) satisfies
\( f(\widehat p,\widehat\beta)\le f^*+3\varepsilon/4. \)
Consequently,
\( F(\log\widehat p)-\min F \le f(\widehat p,\widehat\beta)-f^* \le3\varepsilon/4<\varepsilon. \)

This proves correctness with positive rational output prices.

Numerical dependence of the iteration bound

The parameters above satisfy
\( s\ge\frac{2^{-3L}}{8(m+1)},\qquad R\le12d\,2^{2L},\qquad G\le5(m+1)2^{4L}. \)
Hence
\( \frac{8GR^2}{\varepsilon s} \le \frac{46080\,d^2(m+1)^2\,2^{11L}}{\varepsilon}. \)

In particular,
\( N= O\!\left( d^2\left(L+\log d+\log(1/\varepsilon)\right) \right). \)
The preceding explicit formula for \(N\) displays all instance-dependent conditioning.

6. Rational rounding and bit complexity

This section verifies that the volume argument can be implemented with polynomial-length rational numbers.

Rounded ellipsoid updates

Represent an ellipsoid by center \(x\) and positive definite shape matrix \(A\). For a nonzero cut normal \(g\), the ideal central-cut update is
\( x'=x-\frac{Ag}{(d+1)\sqrt{g^\top Ag}}, \)
\( A'=\frac{d^2}{d^2-1} \left( A-\frac{2}{d+1}\frac{Agg^\top A}{g^\top Ag} \right). \)

It contains the retained half-ellipsoid. Its volume ratio is
\( \left(\frac{d^2}{d^2-1}\right)^{d/2} \sqrt{\frac{d-1}{d+1}} \le e^{-1/(2(d+1))}. \)
These are the standard central-cut formulas; see [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Section 3.2](https://www.mpi-inf.mpg.de/fileadmin/inf/d1/ellipsoid-lovasz.pdf).

Here is a sufficient rounding scheme. Define
\( \alpha=\frac1{64d(d+1)},\qquad \ell=4^{-(N+1)}. \)
Choose a dyadic grid spacing \(\gamma\) satisfying
\( \gamma\le\frac{\alpha\ell}{2d}. \)

Approximate \(x'\), then round it to that grid, so that its rounded value \(\widehat x\) satisfies
\( \|\widehat x-x'\|_2\le\sqrt d\,\gamma\le\alpha\sqrt\ell. \)

Set
\( M=(1+\alpha)^2A'. \)
Round each entry of \(M\) to the nearest grid point, obtaining a symmetric matrix \(\overline M\), and use
\( \widehat A=\overline M+d\gamma I. \)

The entrywise rounding error has operator norm at most \(d\gamma/2\). Thus
\( M\preceq\widehat A \preceq M+\frac{3d\gamma}{2}I. \)

To check the required eigenvalue bound, the ideal update satisfies
\( A'\succeq\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^2A\succeq A/4. \)
Starting from \(R^2I\succeq I\), induction gives \(A'\succeq\ell I\) throughout the prescribed \(N\) updates. Therefore,
\( M\preceq\widehat A\preceq(1+\alpha)M. \)

The center error has norm at most \(\alpha\) in the metric of \(A'\). The inflation from \(A'\) to \(M\) therefore absorbs that error, and the matrix inequality absorbs matrix rounding. The rounded ellipsoid contains the ideal updated ellipsoid.

Its volume increase relative to the ideal update is at most
\( (1+\alpha)^{3d/2}\le e^{3d\alpha/2}. \)
Combining this with the ideal decrease gives a volume ratio at most
\( e^{-1/(4(d+1))}. \)
This proves the rounded volume guarantee used in Section 5.

Sizes of numbers and arithmetic cost

Define
\( b= 1+L+\left\lceil\log_2(d+1)\right\rceil +\left\lceil\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\right\rceil. \)
The tolerance is already included in \(L\), so \(b=O(L+\log d)\).

The grid needs \(O(N+\log d)\) fractional bits. Also,
\( \widehat A\preceq2A \)
at each update, so matrix entries and centers need only \(O(N+b)\) integer and fractional bits altogether.

At a rational center, each gradient coordinate has \(O(N+b)\) bits. Clearing denominators produces an integer cut normal with
\( O(d(N+b)) \)
bits per coordinate. The exact rational expressions for the shape update, and the square-root approximation required for the center update, consequently use polynomially many bits.

Each iteration requires \(O(m\tau+d^2)\) rational arithmetic operations, apart from the polynomial-cost square-root approximation. The output prices have \(O(N+b)\) bits each.

For objective evaluation, approximate every \(\log\beta_t\) with error at most
\( \min\{1,\varepsilon/(8W)\}. \)
The total weighted error is then at most \(\varepsilon/8\). These logarithms are computable in polynomial bit complexity: scale the argument into \([1,2)\), and use the geometrically convergent series
\( \log u= 2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{((u-1)/(u+1))^{2k+1}}{2k+1}. \)
Here the series ratio is at most \(1/9\); fixed-point interval arithmetic with guard bits gives the stated error.

For example, a conservative implementation using elementary arithmetic has total bit complexity
\( \widetilde O\!\left( (m\tau+d^2)\,N\,[d(N+b)]^4 \right). \)
Since \(N=O(d^2b)\), this is at most
\( \widetilde O\!\left( (m\tau+d^2)d^{14}b^5 \right). \)

These deliberately loose bounds establish polynomial Turing complexity. They contain no unbounded numerical-conditioning factor.

7. An accelerated price bound with explicit conditioning

For comparison, a substantially cheaper iteration is available in log-price coordinates. This section concerns exact real arithmetic; the polynomial rational implementation has already been established above.

Let
\( Z=[\log a,\log W]^m,\qquad D=\log(m+1). \)
For each type, replace its maximum by log-sum-exp smoothing with parameter
\( \delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{4WD}. \)
Omit zero valuations from the sum, and include the constant zero option for quasi-linear utilities.

Call the resulting objective \(F_\delta\). The elementary maximum/log-sum-exp inequalities give
\( F\le F_\delta\le F+\delta WD=F+\varepsilon/4. \)

Smoothness and strong convexity

Each smoothed maximum is formed from affine functions whose gradients are either \(-e_j\), or additionally \(0\). Its Hessian is \(1/\delta\) times their covariance matrix. Since all these vectors have norm at most \(1\), that covariance has operator norm at most \(1\).

On \(Z\), the exponential Hessian is between \(aI\) and \(WI\). Consequently, \(F_\delta\) is \(a\)-strongly convex and has gradient Lipschitz constant
\( S=W+\frac W\delta =W+\frac{4W^2D}{\varepsilon}. \)

To apply an accelerated proximal-gradient theorem without assumptions on extrapolated points, extend each exponential outside \([\log a,\log W]\) by its matching quadratic at the corresponding endpoint. The extension is globally convex and smooth, has curvature between \(a\) and \(W\), and agrees with the exponential on \(Z\). Add the indicator function of \(Z\); its proximal map is coordinatewise clipping.

The standard accelerated proximal-gradient bound is
\( F_\delta(z^{(k)})-\min_ZF_\delta \le \frac{2S\|z^{(0)}-z_\delta^*\|_2^2}{(k+1)^2}. \)
Its hypotheses hold for the global smooth extension and the closed convex box indicator. See [Tseng, *On accelerated proximal gradient methods for convex-concave optimization*](https://www.mit.edu/~dimitrib/PTseng/papers/apgm.pdf).

Strong convexity and constrained optimality imply
\( \|z^{(0)}-z_\delta^*\|_2^2 \le \frac2a \left(F_\delta(z^{(0)})-\min_ZF_\delta\right). \)
Therefore, restarting after
\( K=\left\lceil\sqrt{\frac{8S}{a}}\right\rceil \)
iterations reduces the smoothed objective gap by at least a factor of two.

Explicit iteration count

On \(Z\), \(F\) is \((m+1)W\)-Lipschitz in the maximum norm. Thus any initial point in \(Z\) has smoothed gap at most
\( H=(m+1)W\log(W/a)+\varepsilon/4. \)

It suffices to use
\( J=\max\left\{ 0,\left\lceil\log_2(4H/\varepsilon)\right\rceil \right\} \)
restart blocks. The resulting original objective gap is at most \(\varepsilon/2\).

The explicit iteration bound is consequently
\( N_{\mathrm{acc}} \le \left\lceil \sqrt{\frac8a \left(W+\frac{4W^2\log(m+1)}{\varepsilon}\right)} \right\rceil \max\left\{ 0,\left\lceil\log_2(4H/\varepsilon)\right\rceil \right\}. \)
Each iteration costs \(O(m\tau)\) elementary real operations and function evaluations. For a fixed instance, this gives the stated \(\widetilde O(\varepsilon^{-1/2})\) rate, with all conditioning factors displayed.

Rationalizing the final prices

Suppose the final real iterate is \(z\in Z\), and put
\( \eta=\min\left\{1,\frac{\varepsilon}{4(2m+1)W}\right\}. \)
Choose rational prices satisfying
\( |\widehat p_j-e^{z_j}|\le a\eta/2. \)
They are positive and satisfy
\( \|\log\widehat p-z\|_\infty\le\eta. \)

Each maximum term changes by at most \(\eta\), while the exponential sum changes by at most \(mW(e^\eta-1)\). Since \(e^\eta-1\le2\eta\) for \(0\le\eta\le1\),
\( |F(\log\widehat p)-F(z)| \le(2m+1)W\eta\le\varepsilon/4. \)
Thus these rational prices satisfy the required tolerance. Their bit lengths can be \(O(L+\log m+\log(1/\varepsilon))\).

This final rounding observation does not substitute for an error analysis of accelerated iterations performed at finite precision. The independent rational ellipsoid algorithm supplies the complete polynomial bit guarantee.

Finally, the conditioning issue is real. With one linear buyer of effective budget \(1\), two goods, and valuations \((1,2^{-k})\), the optimal prices are
\( p^*=\left( \frac1{1+2^{-k}}, \frac{2^{-k}}{1+2^{-k}} \right). \)
Thus an equilibrium price can be exponentially small in the input length. A rate containing \(a^{-1/2}\) can therefore have exponential instance dependence, despite its accelerated dependence on \(\varepsilon\).
