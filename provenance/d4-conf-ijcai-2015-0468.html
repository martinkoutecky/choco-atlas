The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational role masses \(w_i\), a symmetric nonnegative contact matrix \(W_{ij}\), and parameters \(\mu,\delta\), the adversary chooses red-expert and blue-expert masses \(a_i,b_i\) with \(0\leq a_i+b_i\leq w_i\), \(\sum_i a_i=(1/2+\delta)\mu\), and \(\sum_i b_i=(1/2-\delta)\mu\). Each nonexpert of role \(i\) follows the sign of \(q_i=\sum_j W_{ij}(a_j-b_j)\), splitting evenly on a tie. Determine whether every feasible adversary choice leaves strictly more than half the population believing the truth.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) roles with positive binary rational masses \(w_i\), where \(\sum_i w_i=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=2\) opinions, red (truth) and blue.
The input also contains a symmetric matrix \(W\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^{\tau\times\tau}\) and binary rational parameters \(0<\mu,\delta<1/2\). Diagonal entries are allowed and represent contact within a role; no regularity or spectral promise is imposed. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An adversary chooses real masses \(a_i,b_i\geq0\) satisfying \(a_i+b_i\leq w_i\), \(\sum_i a_i=(1/2+\delta)\mu\), and \(\sum_i b_i=(1/2-\delta)\mu\). These expert masses retain their assigned opinions.
There is one propagation round: the remaining mass of role \(i\) becomes red if \(q_i=\sum_jW_{ij}(a_j-b_j)>0\), blue if \(q_i<0\), and splits equally if \(q_i=0\), including when there is no expert contact.
Define \(h(x)=1\) for \(x>0\), \(h(x)=0\) for \(x<0\), and \(h(0)=1/2\). The final truthful mass is \(T(a,b)=\sum_i a_i+\sum_i(w_i-a_i-b_i)h(q_i)\). Robustness means \(T(a,b)>1/2\) for every feasible adversary choice.

Is deciding robustness in \(\mathrm{P}\), with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions?

For finite societies, a verified sufficient condition guarantees robustness on regular graphs: \(d^2>\lambda^2/[\delta^2\mu(1-\mu+2\delta\mu)]\), where \(d\) is the degree and \(\lambda\) bounds the absolute nonprincipal adjacency eigenvalues. This is a robustness guarantee, not a general decision algorithm. [Source](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/291.pdf)
Discrete minimization of the expected truthful population is reported \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even on disjoint unions of cliques; this concerns optimization rather than the fixed majority threshold. [Source](https://edoc.ub.uni-muenchen.de/22969/1/Daknama_Rami.pdf)
No result settling the exact mass-based decision problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one and required every listed role to have positive mass.
  • Restricted the input parameters to binary rationals with 0 < mu, delta < 1/2, matching the intended expert fraction and truth bias.
  • Required contact weights to be binary rationals; allowed diagonal entries as contact between distinct individuals of the same role.
  • Allowed arbitrary real expert allocations, with nonnegative red and blue masses separately.
  • Made experts retain their opinions and specified a single propagation round with exact equal splitting on every tie.
  • Treated mu and delta as input parameters and imposed no spectral promise, preserving the graded question's unrestricted contact matrix.
  • Set m = 2 for the two opinions and defined L as the total binary input length.
  • Specified polynomial-time Turing reductions for hardness to accommodate the universal robustness formulation.

Result

Deciding robustness is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions. More precisely, robustness is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions.

The proof below treats arbitrary real adversary allocations and the specified tie rule exactly. The hardness construction uses strictly positive role masses and a symmetric matrix whose entries belong to \(\{1/2,3/4,1,5/4,3/2\}\). All constructed numerical inputs are dyadic rationals.

1. A bound accounting for experts placed anywhere

Write \(\mathrm{NONROBUST}\) for the existence of a feasible allocation satisfying \(T(a,b)\leq1/2\)\). Define

\(A=(1/2+\delta)\mu,\qquad B=(1/2-\delta)\mu,\qquad g(x)=1-h(x).\)

Thus \(g(x)\)\) equals \(1\)\) for negative \(x\)\), \(0\)\) for positive \(x\)\), and \(1/2\)\) at zero. For any feasible allocation, define its nominal blue mass

\(F(a,b)=\sum_i w_i g(q_i).\)

This counts the blue outcome of each entire role, including its experts. The actual blue mass is

\(1-T(a,b)=B+\sum_i(w_i-a_i-b_i)g(q_i) =F(a,b)+B-\sum_i(a_i+b_i)g(q_i).\)

Because \(0\leq g(q_i)\leq1\)\) and \(\sum_i(a_i+b_i)=\mu\)\), this gives

\(1-F(a,b)-B\leq T(a,b)\leq1-F(a,b)+A.\)

In particular,

\[1-F(a,b)-\mu\leq T(a,b)\leq1-F(a,b)+\mu. \tag{1}\]

Consequently, every nonrobust allocation satisfies

\[F(a,b)\geq1/2-\mu. \tag{2}\]

These inequalities apply even when the adversary puts experts on the roles used as tests in the construction.

2. Source problem and parameters

We reduce from weighted maximum cut:

  • The input is an explicitly represented undirected graph \(G=(V,E)\)\), with positive integer edge weights \(\ell_e\)\) encoded in binary.
  • The question is whether some cut has weight at least the integer \(K\)\).

This decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; it is the weighted MAX CUT problem in [Karp’s *Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Let \(n=|V|\)\), \(r=|E|\)\), and \(M=\sum_e\ell_e\)\). We may restrict to \(n\geq2\)\), \(r\geq1\)\), and \(1\leq K\leq M\)\): inputs outside these conditions are immediately decidable.

Set \(p=n+1\)\). Choose powers of two \(N,H,J\)\), each the smallest satisfying its respective inequality,

\(N\geq8n,\qquad H\geq4(M+1),\qquad J\geq64p.\)

Define

\(C=\frac1N,\qquad D=\frac{C}{H},\qquad v=\frac{D}{J},\qquad \mu=\frac{D}{16},\qquad \delta=\frac2J.\)

The fixed total red-minus-blue expert mass is therefore

\[s=A-B=2\delta\mu=\frac v4. \tag{3}\]

We will use the following bounds:

\[nC\leq\frac18,\qquad MD<\frac C4,\qquad D\leq\frac C8\leq\frac1{64},\qquad pv\leq\frac D{64}. \tag{4}\]

In particular,

\[\mu+pv\leq\frac{5D}{64}<\frac D2,\qquad \mu+pv<\frac C4,\qquad \mu+pv<\frac3{32}. \tag{5}\]

For the middle inequality, use \(D\leq C/8\)\); for the last, use \(D\leq1/64\)\).

The parameters satisfy \(0<\mu,\delta<1/2\)\).

3. Construction of the society

Roles and masses

Construct the following distinct roles.

| Roles | Number | Mass of each |
|---|---:|---:|
| Signal roles \(S_0\)\) and \(S_u\)\), \(u\in V\)\) | \(p\)\) | \(v\)\) |
| Vertex tests \(P_u,N_u\)\), \(u\in V\)\) | \(2n\)\) | \(C\)\) |
| Edge tests \(P_e,N_e\)\), \(e\in E\)\) | \(2r\)\) | \(\ell_eD\)\) |
| Guard role \(Z\)\) | \(1\)\) | \(\Gamma\)\) |
| Reservoir role \(R\)\) | \(1\)\) | \(\omega\)\) |

Define

\(\Gamma=\frac12-nC-\left(K-\frac12\right)D,\)

and

\(\omega=1-pv-2nC-2MD-\Gamma.\)

The masses sum to one. They are all strictly positive. Indeed,

\(\Gamma>\frac12-\frac18-\frac C4\geq\frac{11}{32},\qquad \Gamma<\frac12.\)

Also,

\(\omega=\frac12-nC-\left(2M-K+\frac12\right)D-pv >\frac12-\frac18-2MD-pv >\frac5{16}-pv>\frac14.\)

Here the first strict inequality uses \(K\geq1\)\), and the remaining inequalities follow from (4).

The support size is

\(\tau=3n+2r+3.\)

Contact matrix

Initially set every entry of \(W\)\), including every diagonal entry, equal to \(1\)\). Make the following changes, always changing the transposed entry as well.

For each vertex \(u\)\), set

\(W_{P_u,S_u}=\frac32,\qquad W_{N_u,S_u}=\frac12.\)

For each edge \(e=\{u,v\}\)\), choose either ordering of its endpoints and set

\(W_{P_e,S_u}=\frac54,\qquad W_{P_e,S_v}=\frac34,\)

\(W_{N_e,S_u}=\frac34,\qquad W_{N_e,S_v}=\frac54.\)

Finally, set

\(W_{Z,S_0}=\frac32.\)

The resulting matrix is symmetric and strictly positive.

Exact test equations

For a feasible allocation, put \(x_i=a_i-b_i\)\). Feasibility and (3) imply

\(\sum_i x_i=s,\qquad |x_{S_u}|\leq v,\qquad |x_{S_0}|\leq v.\)

The all-ones baseline contributes exactly \(s\)\) to every row. Hence the test equations are

\[q_{P_u}=s+\frac{x_{S_u}}2,\qquad q_{N_u}=s-\frac{x_{S_u}}2, \tag{6}\]

\[q_{P_e}=s+\frac{x_{S_u}-x_{S_v}}4,\qquad q_{N_e}=s-\frac{x_{S_u}-x_{S_v}}4, \tag{7}\]

and

\[q_Z=s+\frac{x_{S_0}}2,\qquad q_R=s>0. \tag{8}\]

These equations hold regardless of where the adversary places its experts. The symmetric modifications also affect signal-role rows, but those roles have total mass only \(pv\)\).

4. Exact interpretation of the tests, including ties

Define

\(r_u=g(q_{P_u})+g(q_{N_u}),\qquad t_e=g(q_{P_e})+g(q_{N_e}),\qquad z=g(q_Z).\)

Since \(s=v/4\)\), equation (6) gives:

  • \(r_u=1\)\) if \(|x_{S_u}|>v/2\)\);
  • \(r_u=1/2\)\) if \(|x_{S_u}|=v/2\)\);
  • \(r_u=0\)\) if \(|x_{S_u}|<v/2\)\).

Similarly, equation (7) gives:

  • \(t_e=1\)\) if \(|x_{S_u}-x_{S_v}|>v\)\);
  • \(t_e=1/2\)\) if \(|x_{S_u}-x_{S_v}|=v\)\);
  • \(t_e=0\)\) if \(|x_{S_u}-x_{S_v}|<v\)\).

In particular, \(0\leq r_u,t_e\leq1\)\). Equation (8) gives \(z=1\)\) exactly when \(x_{S_0}<-v/2\)\); otherwise \(z\leq1/2\)\).

Let \(F_{\mathrm{sig}}\)\) denote the signal roles’ contribution to nominal blue mass. Because the reservoir is red, we have the exact decomposition

\[F=\Gamma z+C\sum_u r_u+D\sum_e\ell_e t_e+F_{\mathrm{sig}}, \qquad 0\leq F_{\mathrm{sig}}\leq pv. \tag{9}\]

5. Correctness of the reduction

We prove

\[\text{there is a cut of weight at least }K \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed society is nonrobust}. \tag{10}\]

A qualifying cut gives a feasible nonrobust allocation

Suppose a cut has weight \(\kappa\geq K\)\). Set \(x_{S_u}=v\)\) on one side and \(x_{S_u}=-v\)\) on the other, and set \(x_{S_0}=-v\)\).

Realize these choices by making each signal role entirely red experts or entirely blue experts. All other test roles receive no experts. Put the remaining red and blue expert masses in the reservoir.

This allocation is feasible. To check the quotas, note that

\(\mu=\frac D{16}\geq4pv,\qquad B=\frac\mu2-\frac v8\geq2pv-\frac v8>pv,\)

and \(A>B\)\). Neither color uses more than \(pv\)\) among signal roles, so both remaining quotas are nonnegative. Their sum is \(\mu-pv\leq\mu<\omega\)\), so they fit in the reservoir.

Every vertex-test pair contributes \(r_u=1\)\), and the guard has \(z=1\)\). An edge-test pair contributes \(t_e=1\)\) exactly when its edge crosses the chosen cut: its signal difference is then \(2v\)\), whereas a noncrossing edge has difference zero.

Thus, by (9),

\(F\geq\Gamma+nC+\kappa D \geq\Gamma+nC+KD =\frac12+\frac D2.\)

Using (1),

\(T\leq1-F+\mu \leq\frac12-\frac D2+\frac D{16} =\frac12-\frac{7D}{16}<\frac12.\)

The society is nonrobust.

A nonrobust allocation must activate the guard

Conversely, suppose an arbitrary feasible real allocation is nonrobust. Equation (2) gives \(F\geq1/2-\mu\)\).

If \(z\leq1/2\)\), then (9) and (4) give

\(F\leq\frac{\Gamma}{2}+nC+MD+pv \leq\frac14+\frac18+\frac1{32}+pv =\frac{13}{32}+pv.\)

By (5), this is strictly less than \(1/2-\mu\)\), a contradiction. Therefore \(z=1\)\).

Every vertex signal must have magnitude greater than half its capacity

Suppose some vertex \(u\)\) satisfies \(|x_{S_u}|\leq v/2\)\). Its test pair then has \(r_u\leq1/2\)\), including the equality case. Equation (9) gives

\(F\leq\Gamma+\left(n-\frac12\right)C+MD+pv\)

\(=\frac12-\frac C2+\left(M-K+\frac12\right)D+pv <\frac12-\frac C4+pv.\)

The strict inequality follows from \(K\geq1\)\) and \(MD<C/4\)\). By (5), the last expression is strictly less than \(1/2-\mu\)\), again a contradiction.

Consequently,

\[|x_{S_u}|>v/2\quad\text{for every }u\in V. \tag{11}\]

The signs define a qualifying cut

Partition the vertices according to the sign of \(x_{S_u}\)\); these signs are nonzero by (11).

For endpoints with opposite signs, (11) implies

\(|x_{S_u}-x_{S_v}|>v,\)

so \(t_e=1\)\).

For endpoints with the same sign, their values lie either in \((v/2,v]\)\) or in \([-v,-v/2)\)\). Thus

\(|x_{S_u}-x_{S_v}|<v/2<v,\)

so \(t_e=0\)\). In particular, no edge test is tied.

Let \(\kappa\)\) be this cut’s integer weight. Since every \(r_u=1\)\) and \(z=1\)\), equation (9) becomes

\(F=\Gamma+nC+\kappa D+F_{\mathrm{sig}}.\)

If \(\kappa<K\)\), integrality gives \(\kappa\leq K-1\)\), whence

\(F\leq\Gamma+nC+(K-1)D+pv =\frac12-\frac D2+pv <\frac12-\mu,\)

where the last inequality uses (5). This contradicts (2). Hence \(\kappa\geq K\)\), completing the proof of (10).

Polynomial encoding length

There are \(3n+2r+3\)\) roles. The binary lengths of \(N,H,J\)\) are bounded by \(O(\log n+\log M)\)\). Every constructed mass and parameter therefore has polynomial encoding length, even when the integer edge weights are large. No edge weight is expanded into multiple roles.

The matrix has polynomially many entries, each drawn from the fixed five-element set stated above. Thus (10) is a polynomial-time many-one reduction to \(\mathrm{NONROBUST}\).

6. Real allocations still give polynomial certificates

It remains to prove \(\mathrm{NONROBUST}\in\mathrm{NP}\)\) for the general input problem.

Suppose a real feasible allocation satisfies \(T\leq1/2\)\). Fix its sign pattern \(\sigma_i\in\{-1,0,1\}\)\). Let \(c_i\)\) be the corresponding value of \(h(q_i)\)\), namely \(0\)\), \(1/2\)\), or \(1\)\).

Consider the rational linear program with variables \(a_i,b_i,\varepsilon\)\), containing:

  • all original feasibility constraints;
  • \(0\leq\varepsilon\leq1\)\);
  • \(q_i\geq\varepsilon\)\) when \(\sigma_i=1\)\), \(q_i\leq-\varepsilon\)\) when \(\sigma_i=-1\)\), and \(q_i=0\)\) when \(\sigma_i=0\)\);
  • the linear inequality

\(\sum_i a_i+\sum_i(w_i-a_i-b_i)c_i\leq1/2\)\).

Maximize \(\varepsilon\)\).

The given real allocation extends to a feasible point with positive \(\varepsilon\)\): take the minimum of \(1\)\) and its finitely many nonzero \(|q_i|\)\). If every \(q_i=0\)\), take \(\varepsilon=1\)\).

This linear program has a bounded feasible polytope, because \(0\leq a_i,b_i\leq w_i\)\) and \(0\leq\varepsilon\leq1\)\). Its optimum is positive and is attained at a vertex. That vertex has rational coordinates of polynomial binary length: after clearing denominators, its coordinates are obtained from a nonsingular system of at most \(2\tau+1\)\) active constraints, and Cramer’s rule bounds their lengths polynomially in the constraint encoding length.

At this vertex, positive \(\varepsilon\)\) ensures the prescribed signs are exact. Its rational allocation therefore satisfies the original objective inequality \(T\leq1/2\)\).

A verifier can check these rational masses, all contact sums, their exact signs, and the resulting truthful mass in polynomial time. Thus \(\mathrm{NONROBUST}\in\mathrm{NP}\)\). This argument does not assume that the discontinuous objective attains a global minimum.

7. Complexity conclusion

The reduction proves that \(\mathrm{NONROBUST}\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Section 6 proves membership, so \(\mathrm{NONROBUST}\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, and robustness is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-complete.

A robustness oracle decides weighted maximum cut with one query to the constructed society, followed by negating the answer. Therefore robustness is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, as requested. A polynomial-time algorithm in the stated input parameters would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\)\).
