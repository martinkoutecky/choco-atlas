The continuous mirror question, as graded

For each \(N\ge2\), let \(D_N\) contain finite-support distributions \(\mu\) on rational locations in \([0,1]\), with \(N\mu_x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}\) and total mass \(1\). A randomized mechanism \(M_N\) maps \(\mu\) to a facility location \(Y\in[0,1]\); require that, whenever \(N\mu_x\ge1\), for every report \(y\in[0,1]\), \(\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim M_N(\mu)}|x-Y|\le\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim M_N(\mu-\frac1N\delta_x+\frac1N\delta_y)}|x-Y|\). Determine the minimum, over truthful mechanism families \((M_N)_{N\ge2}\), of \(\sup_{N\ge2,\,\mu\in D_N}\left[\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim M_N(\mu)}|x-Y|-\frac{\max\operatorname{supp}(\mu)-\min\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}2\right]\), and give an attaining rule computable from the support.

Result and mechanism

The infimum is \(0\), and a mechanism computable directly from the rational support attains it for every society.

For every \(N\ge 2\) and \(\mu\in\widehat D_N\), let \(a=\min\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\) and \(b=\max\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\). Define

\(M_N(\mu)=\frac14\delta_a+\frac12\delta_{(a+b)/2}+\frac14\delta_b.\)

This is the left-right-center mechanism, described in [Golomb–Tzamos, Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.00529). We prove its truthfulness and its performance for the precise objective in the question below. The definition depends only on the measure; coincident atoms are combined, so singleton societies receive the facility at their sole location.

Truthfulness on the full domain

For any \(u\le v\), define the expected distance of a location \(x\) under the left-right-center distribution with extremes \(u,v\) by

\(C_x(u,v)=\frac14|x-u|+\frac14|x-v|+\frac12\left|x-\frac{u+v}{2}\right| =\frac14\bigl(|x-u|+|x-v|+|2x-u-v|\bigr).\)

We first establish the inequality

\(C_x(u,v)\ge\frac12\max\{|x-u|,|x-v|\},\)

with equality whenever \(x\in[u,v]\). Indeed, setting \(p=x-u\) and \(q=x-v\), the reverse triangle inequality gives

\(|p+q|\ge\bigl||p|-|q|\bigr|.\)

Consequently,

\(|p|+|q|+|p+q|\ge |p|+|q|+\bigl||p|-|q|\bigr|=2\max\{|p|,|q|\}.\)

If \(x\in[u,v]\), then \(p\) and \(q\) have opposite signs or one is zero, so equality holds.

Now fix an arbitrary \(\mu\in\widehat D_N\), an occupied location \(x\), and a report \(y\in[0,1]\). Remove the reporting agent’s mass and write

\(\nu=\mu-\frac1N\delta_x.\)

This is a nonnegative finite-support measure: the positive integer \(N\mu_x\) is at least one. Its total mass is \((N-1)/N>0\), so its support has well-defined extremes \(\ell,r\).

Under truthful reporting the extremes are

\(a=\min\{x,\ell\},\qquad b=\max\{x,r\}.\)

Since \(x\in[a,b]\), the equality established above gives

\(C_x(a,b)=\frac12\max\{|x-\ell|,|x-r|\}.\)

To justify the last equality, the farthest point from \(x\) in the set \(\{x,\ell,r\}\) is one of \(\ell,r\), and its distance is also the maximum distance to that set’s extremes \(a,b\).

After reporting \(y\), the extremes become

\(a'=\min\{y,\ell\},\qquad b'=\max\{y,r\}.\)

The interval \([a',b']\) contains both \(\ell\) and \(r\). For every \(z\in[a',b']\),

\(|x-z|\le\max\{|x-a'|,|x-b'|\},\)

as follows by expressing \(z\) as a convex combination of the endpoints and applying the triangle inequality. Thus

\(C_x(a',b')\ge\frac12\max\{|x-a'|,|x-b'|\} \ge\frac12\max\{|x-\ell|,|x-r|\} =C_x(a,b).\)

This is exactly the required truthfulness inequality. The proof covers arbitrary real reports, changes in support, and singleton societies.

Exact performance and optimality

For the mechanism above and every \(x\in[a,b]\), the equality from the preceding section yields

\(\mathbb E|x-Y|=\frac12\max\{x-a,b-x\}\le\frac{b-a}{2}.\)

At either occupied extreme, equality holds. Therefore

\(\max_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mathbb E|x-Y|=\frac{b-a}{2}.\)

This also holds when \(a=b\), when both sides vanish. Hence the constructed family satisfies \(A((M_N))=0\).

Conversely, for any facility distribution, the triangle inequality gives

\(|a-Y|+|b-Y|\ge b-a.\)

Taking expectations shows

\(\max_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mathbb E|x-Y| \ge\max\{\mathbb E|a-Y|,\mathbb E|b-Y|\} \ge\frac{b-a}{2}.\)

Thus every family, truthful or otherwise, has nonnegative additive error. Together, these bounds prove that the requested infimum is \(0\) and is attained.

The order of maximum and expectation is decisive. For example, on support \(\{0,1\}\), this mechanism has maximum expected distance \(1/2\), but expected maximum distance \(3/4\).

Computation from the rational support

Scan the \(\tau\) listed locations to find \(a,b\), compute their rational midpoint, and output the distribution’s at most three atoms. This uses \(O(\tau)\) rational comparisons and a constant number of rational arithmetic operations.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, a conservative bit-operation bound is \(O(\tau L^2+\log N)\). The output has \(O(L)\) bits. The mechanism ignores the positive masses and requires no expansion into \(N\) individual reports. An exact sample uses two independent fair bits: one outcome selects \(a\), two select the midpoint, and one selects \(b\).
