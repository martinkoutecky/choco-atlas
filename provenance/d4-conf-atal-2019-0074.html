The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-domain assignment space \(\mathcal F\), a finite support \(T\) of preference types with rational masses \(\mu_t\ge0\) summing to \(1\), and type-specific nonnegative cost tables with entries that can be elicited for free, let \(F_t(x)\) be type \(t\)'s completed cost and \(\Phi_\mu(x)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tF_t(x)\). For inputs \(w\ge1\) and \(\epsilon\ge0\), can DFBnB with a mass-weighted LUC lower bound return a feasible assignment \(x\), eliciting enough entries to evaluate its true cost, with \(\Phi_\mu(x)\le w\min_{y\in\mathcal F}\Phi_\mu(y)+\epsilon\)?

Explore children in increasing current estimate, break ties by fixed domain orders, update the incumbent at complete assignments, and terminate when search finishes. Result

The mass-weighted LUC DFBnB always terminates and returns a fully evaluated assignment \(x\) satisfying
\[ \Phi_\mu(x)\le w\min_{y\in\mathcal F}\Phi_\mu(y)+\epsilon. \]

Here “incumbent” has its usual branch-and-bound meaning: a stored, fully evaluated assignment of minimum true cost among the complete assignments accepted so far. The search may start without an incumbent or with any fully evaluated feasible assignment.

The proof below directly handles the support-and-mass formulation. It does not require reducing rational masses to replicated types or assuming polynomial-time computation of the LUC estimate.

1. The estimate is always a valid lower bound

Fix a partial assignment \(p\) and the cache state when its estimate is evaluated. Let
\(A_t(p)=\{j:S_{t,j}\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(p)\}\)
be the indices of type \(t\)'s tables whose scopes are fully assigned.

By the elicitation rule, every entry contributing to
\[ g_\mu(p)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\sum_{j\in A_t(p)} c_{t,j}(p|_{S_{t,j}}) \]
has been revealed.

Fix any completion \(y\supseteq p\). Its true cost decomposes as
\[ \Phi_\mu(y)=g_\mu(p)+ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\sum_{j\notin A_t(p)} c_{t,j}(y|_{S_{t,j}}). \]

Among the terms in the second sum, exactly \(u_t(p,y)\) entries for type \(t\) are currently unknown. Each such entry has true cost at least \(\ell\). Every other term is nonnegative, and every mass is positive. Consequently,
\[ \Phi_\mu(y)\ge g_\mu(p)+\ell\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tu_t(p,y). \]

The set of completions of \(p\) is finite and nonempty. Since \(\ell\ge0\),
\[ b_\mu(p) =g_\mu(p)+\ell\min_{z\supseteq p}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tu_t(p,z) \le g_\mu(p)+\ell\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_tu_t(p,y) \le\Phi_\mu(y). \]

Thus at every cache state, \(b_\mu(p)\) is a lower bound on the true cost of every completion of \(p\).

This argument uses the weighted sum inside the stated minimum; it does not interchange minimization and summation. It also remains valid when queries reveal entries used by other branches. No monotonicity of the estimates is required.

At a complete assignment \(x\), all table scopes are fully assigned and their used entries have been elicited. Therefore \(u_t(x,x)=0\) for every type and
\[ b_\mu(x)=g_\mu(x)=\Phi_\mu(x). \]

2. Termination and existence of an incumbent

Write \(d_i=|D_i|\), numbering variables in their fixed search order. The assignment tree has
\[ N=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}d_i \]
nodes, where the empty product is \(1\). In particular, it is finite.

Every estimate can be computed by enumerating the finitely many completions of its partial assignment. All relevant sums and comparisons involve finite binary rationals, so exact evaluation terminates.

There are also only finitely many table entries. Because queries are cached, each initially unknown entry is queried at most once. Ordinary DFBnB expands each node at most once, or discards its subtree by pruning. Hence the search terminates.

If the search starts without an incumbent, it cannot prune any node until one exists. Since every domain is nonempty, depth-first exploration reaches a complete assignment. All entries used by that assignment are elicited before its evaluation, and it supplies the first incumbent.

Every later incumbent is likewise fully evaluated. Querying has zero cost and reveals fixed values, so the true cost of a stored assignment never changes. Updating to a better incumbent makes its cost \(B\) nonincreasing.

For \(m=0\), the root itself is the unique complete assignment; the same reasoning applies, including elicitation of any empty-scope entries.

3. Approximation guarantee

Because \(\mathcal F\) is finite and nonempty, choose an optimal assignment \(x^\star\), and write
\[ \operatorname{OPT}=\Phi_\mu(x^\star). \]
Let \(B_{\mathrm{final}}\) be the returned incumbent's true cost.

There are two exhaustive cases.

Case 1: The optimal leaf is considered for an incumbent update.

At that point, the incumbent cost becomes at most \(\operatorname{OPT}\). Since incumbent costs are nonincreasing,
\[ B_{\mathrm{final}}\le\operatorname{OPT} \le w\operatorname{OPT}+\epsilon, \]
where the second inequality follows from \(\operatorname{OPT}\ge0\), \(w\ge1\), and \(\epsilon\ge0\).

Case 2: The optimal leaf is not considered for an incumbent update.

Because the finite search finishes, some node \(p\) on the path to \(x^\star\), possibly the leaf itself, must have been pruned. Let \(B_p\) be the incumbent cost at that moment. The prescribed pruning condition gives
\[ B_p\le w b_\mu(p)+\epsilon. \]

The lower-bound result applies to the cache state at that moment and to the completion \(x^\star\), giving \(b_\mu(p)\le\operatorname{OPT}\). Since \(w\ge0\) and the incumbent cost is nonincreasing,
\[ B_{\mathrm{final}} \le B_p \le w b_\mu(p)+\epsilon \le w\operatorname{OPT}+\epsilon. \]

Both cases establish the claimed guarantee. Child ordering and tie-breaking affect the work performed, but not this argument.

The corresponding unweighted pruning argument appears in Theorem 7.1 of [Tabakhi, Yeoh and Yokoo (2019)](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2019/pdfs/p476.pdf); the lower-bound proof above supplies the required justification for the mass-weighted formulation.

4. Explicit finite running-time bounds

Let \(k_t\) be the number of tables for type \(t\), and define
\[ C=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}k_t,\qquad E=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{k_t} \prod_{x_i\in S_{t,j}}d_i,\qquad P=\prod_{i=1}^{m}d_i,\qquad D=\max(\{1\}\cup\{d_i:1\le i\le m\}). \]

Here \(C\) counts tables, \(E\) counts their indexed entries, and \(P\) counts complete assignments. Empty scopes contribute one entry. The total number of queries is at most the number of initially unknown entries, hence at most \(E\).

A straightforward exact estimate computation enumerates at most \(P\) completions and scans the \(C\) tables for each completion. Table access, scope restriction, rational arithmetic, and comparisons take time polynomial in \(m,\tau,C,L\). Including query answers in \(L\) bounds the encodings of all revealed values used in these calculations.

To maintain increasing current estimates, one implementation recomputes the estimates of all remaining children whenever it selects the next child. Required queries are completed before comparing estimates in the resulting cache state. A parent with \(d\) children needs at most \(O(d^2)\) child-estimate evaluations. Summing over parents gives at most \(O(DN)\) such evaluations; node pruning checks add \(O(N)\).

Thus a conservative bit-time bound is
\[ O\!\left( L+E+(D+1)NP\,\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,C,L) \right). \]
Since \(N\le(m+1)P\), this is also bounded by
\[ O\!\left( L+E+(D+1)(m+1)P^2\,\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,C,L) \right). \]

These are finite exhaustive-search bounds, not polynomial-time guarantees. They depend on the explicitly supplied support size \(\tau\), without introducing the number of possible types.
