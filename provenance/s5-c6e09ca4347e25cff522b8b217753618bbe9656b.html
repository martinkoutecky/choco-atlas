The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), schools \(J\) with rational per-capita capacities \(\kappa_j\), and positive rational utilities \(u_{tj}\) that are distinct across type-school pairs, compute the feasible assignment \(x_{tj}\ge 0\), with \(\sum_j x_{tj}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{tj}\le\kappa_j\), that is stable. Stability means there is no pair \((t,j)\) for which positive mass of type \(t\) is unassigned or assigned to a school \(k\) with \(u_{tk}<u_{tj}\), while school \(j\) has spare capacity or holds positive mass of a type \(s\) with \(u_{sj}<u_{tj}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq 1\) schools \(J\), with nonnegative binary rational capacities \(\kappa_j\), and positive binary rational utilities \(u_{tj}\), distinct across all type-school pairs. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
Mass is divisible. A feasible assignment has \(x_{tj}\geq 0\), \(\sum_j x_{tj}\leq\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t x_{tj}\leq\kappa_j\). Unassigned students have utility \(-\infty\); school \(j\)'s utility is \(\sum_t u_{tj}x_{tj}\).
An assignment is stable if no \((t,j)\) satisfies both: \(\mu_t-\sum_k x_{tk}>0\) or some \(k\) has \(x_{tk}>0\) and \(u_{tk}<u_{tj}\); and \(\kappa_j-\sum_s x_{sj}>0\) or some \(s\) has \(x_{sj}>0\) and \(u_{sj}<u_{tj}\).

Can a stable assignment be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computation \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For discrete students with strictly aligned preferences, the stable allocation is unique and maximizes the descending vector of student utilities lexicographically. [Galichon, Ghelfi and Henry](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.06587).
The stated mass problem is already covered by the real-valued stable allocation model, for which a strongly polynomial algorithm is known; consequently this complexity target is settled. [Baïou and Balinski, corrected version](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302).

  • Make the unmatched utility normalization explicit as negative infinity and extend aligned school utility additively to masses.
  • Interpret masses as divisible and request a stable assignment without assuming continuous uniqueness.

Conclusion

A stable assignment can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, under the stated global distinctness of utilities, the stable assignment is unique.

An explicit algorithm uses \(O(m\tau\log(m\tau+1)+m+\tau)\) arithmetic operations and comparisons. With elementary exact integer arithmetic, its bit running time is

\(O\bigl((m\tau\log(m\tau+1)+m+\tau)L^2\bigr)\).

The proof below works directly with divisible masses.

1. An equivalent stability condition

For a feasible assignment \(x\), define

\(A_{tj}(x)=\sum_{k:\,u_{tk}\geq u_{tj}}x_{tk}\),

\(B_{tj}(x)=\sum_{s:\,u_{sj}\geq u_{tj}}x_{sj}\).

Feasibility implies \(A_{tj}(x)\leq\mu_t\) and \(B_{tj}(x)\leq\kappa_j\).

The identity

\(\mu_t-A_{tj}(x)=\bigl(\mu_t-\sum_kx_{tk}\bigr)+\sum_{k:\,u_{tk}<u_{tj}}x_{tk}\)

expresses the left side as a sum of nonnegative quantities. Consequently, the type-side blocking condition in the question holds exactly when \(A_{tj}(x)<\mu_t\).

Similarly,

\(\kappa_j-B_{tj}(x)=\bigl(\kappa_j-\sum_sx_{sj}\bigr)+\sum_{s:\,u_{sj}<u_{tj}}x_{sj}\),

so the school-side blocking condition holds exactly when \(B_{tj}(x)<\kappa_j\).

Thus a feasible assignment is stable if and only if, for every pair \((t,j)\),

\(A_{tj}(x)=\mu_t\quad\text{or}\quad B_{tj}(x)=\kappa_j.\)

2. Algorithm

Let \(N=m\tau\). Since all utilities are distinct, order all pairs as \(e_1,\ldots,e_N\), where \(e_r=(t_r,j_r)\), with

\(u_{t_1j_1}>u_{t_2j_2}>\cdots>u_{t_Nj_N}.\)

Initialize residual type masses \(a_t=\mu_t\), residual school capacities \(b_j=\kappa_j\), and every assignment entry to zero.

For each \(r=1,\ldots,N\):

  • Set \(d_r=\min\{a_{t_r},b_{j_r}\}\).
  • Set \(x_{t_rj_r}=d_r\).
  • Replace \(a_{t_r}\) by \(a_{t_r}-d_r\) and \(b_{j_r}\) by \(b_{j_r}-d_r\).

Each residual remains nonnegative. The maintained identities

\(a_t=\mu_t-\sum_{\text{processed }(t,k)}x_{tk}\),

\(b_j=\kappa_j-\sum_{\text{processed }(s,j)}x_{sj}\)

therefore prove feasibility.

3. Proof of stability

Fix a pair \(e_r=(t,j)\). Immediately after its processing, at least one of its endpoint residuals is zero, because \(d_r\) is their minimum.

If \(a_t=0\), the algorithm has already assigned all mass \(\mu_t\) of type \(t\). Every assignment made so far has utility at least \(u_{tj}\). Moreover, the zero residual prevents any subsequent positive assignment involving \(t\). Hence the final assignment satisfies \(A_{tj}(x)=\mu_t\).

If \(b_j=0\), the same reasoning gives \(B_{tj}(x)=\kappa_j\): the school is filled entirely through pairs of utility at least \(u_{tj}\), and receives no subsequent positive assignment.

Thus every pair satisfies the stability condition established in Section 1. This includes pairs involving schools of zero capacity.

4. Proof of uniqueness

Let \(y\) be any stable assignment. We prove by induction on \(r\) that \(y_{e_r}=x_{e_r}\).

Assume agreement on every pair preceding \(e_r=(t,j)\). Define the amounts already assigned at its endpoints through those preceding pairs:

\(H_t=\sum_{q<r:\,t_q=t}x_{e_q}\),

\(H_j=\sum_{q<r:\,j_q=j}x_{e_q}.\)

The induction hypothesis makes these sums identical for \(y\). Before processing \(e_r\), the algorithm therefore has residuals

\(a_t=\mu_t-H_t,\qquad b_j=\kappa_j-H_j.\)

Feasibility of \(y\) implies

\(y_{tj}\leq\min\{\mu_t-H_t,\kappa_j-H_j\}=x_{tj}.\)

Suppose this inequality were strict. Then both

\(H_t+y_{tj}<\mu_t\quad\text{and}\quad H_j+y_{tj}<\kappa_j.\)

By the strict global utility ordering, the preceding pairs incident to each endpoint are exactly its pairs with utility greater than \(u_{tj}\). Consequently,

\(A_{tj}(y)=H_t+y_{tj}<\mu_t\),

\(B_{tj}(y)=H_j+y_{tj}<\kappa_j.\)

Section 1 then shows that \((t,j)\) blocks \(y\), contradicting stability. Therefore \(y_{tj}=x_{tj}\), completing the induction and proving uniqueness.

5. Exact arithmetic and running time

Sorting the \(N=m\tau\) utilities requires \(O(N\log(N+1))\) comparisons. Comparing rational utilities by cross multiplication takes \(O(L^2)\) bit operations using elementary multiplication.

To implement mass updates exactly, let \(Q\) be the product of the input denominators of all masses and capacities. Their total encoding length is at most \(L\), so \(Q\) has \(O(L)\) bits. Scale masses and capacities by \(Q\), obtaining integers.

Every update is then an integer minimum and subtraction. Residuals remain between zero and their initial values, so all intermediate integers have \(O(L)\) bits. Every output entry has the form \(D_{tj}/Q\), also with \(O(L)\) bits.

Constructing the scaled integers costs at most \(O((m+\tau)L^2)\) bit operations. Combining this with sorting, updates, and output gives the stated bound

\(O\bigl((m\tau\log(m\tau+1)+m+\tau)L^2\bigr)\).

No expansion into individual students is required: the algorithm operates on the given \(\tau\) support types.

6. Relation to the cited general model

The corrected [Baïou–Balinski paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302) establishes a strongly polynomial algorithm for real-valued stable allocation. Here its row supplies are \(\mu_t\), column capacities are \(\kappa_j\), and both endpoint preference orders come from decreasing \(u_{tj}\). Zero-capacity schools can be deleted.

Its entry upper bounds can be set to \(1\), since feasibility already implies \(x_{tj}\leq\mu_t\leq1\). If \(x_{tj}=1\), type \(t\) is necessarily saturated entirely at \(j\), so that entry cannot block under the question’s definition. Otherwise, its blocking condition agrees exactly with Section 1.

Thus the general theorem applies, and the direct proof above supplies a simpler algorithm for the aligned preferences assumed here.
