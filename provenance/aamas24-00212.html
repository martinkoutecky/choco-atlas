Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(N\in\mathbb{N}\), rational public coefficients \(\alpha_g\), and rational finite-support masses \(\mu_{g,x}\) with \(N\mu_{g,x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) and \(\sum_{g,x}\mu_{g,x}=1\), compute the RGA-M facility \(F_N(\mu)\) using weighted group medians, weights \(c_g=\mu_g(1+\alpha_g(N\mu_g-1))\), and \(\lambda=2\sqrt{3}-3\), together with \(\max_{z\in[0,1]}SU_N(z;\mu)\) and the achieved ratio, where \(SU_N(y;\mu)=\sum_g[1+\alpha_g(N\mu_g-1)]\sum_x\mu_{g,x}(1-|y-x|)\). Prove uniformly that \(F_N\) is unanimous, no one-clone location report \(\mu\mapsto\mu+N^{-1}(e_{g,x'}-e_{g,x})\) improves the true type's utility, and the ratio is at most \(\sqrt{3}\); the design variant asks for the smallest achievable \(\rho\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((g,x)\) with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Locations are rational \(x\in[0,1]\), groups \(g\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) are public and nonempty, and public coefficients satisfy \(\alpha_g\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q\).
The input includes a binary integer \(N\ge1\), with \(N\mu_{g,x}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) and \(\sum_{g,x}\mu_{g,x}=1\); put \(\mu_g=\sum_x\mu_{g,x}\). State algorithmic running times in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.
The true utility of one clone of type \((g,x)\) at facility \(y\in[0,1]\) is \(U_{g,x}(y;\mu)=(1-\alpha_g)(1-|y-x|)+\alpha_gN\sum_t\mu_{g,t}(1-|y-t|)\).
A deterministic mechanism family \(f_{N,\alpha}\) is unanimous if it outputs \(x\) whenever all mass is at \(x\), and location-strategyproof if \(U_{g,x}(f_{N,\alpha}(\mu);\mu)\ge U_{g,x}(f_{N,\alpha}(\mu');\mu)\) for every occupied type and rational report \(x'\in[0,1]\), where \(\mu'=\mu+N^{-1}(e_{g,x'}-e_{g,x})\) and \(e_{g,t}\) denotes unit mass at \((g,t)\).
Define \(SU_N(y;\mu)=\sum_g[1+\alpha_g(N\mu_g-1)]\sum_x\mu_{g,x}(1-|y-x|)\), and \(\rho(f)=\sup_{N,m,\alpha,\mu}\max_{z\in[0,1]}SU_N(z;\mu)/SU_N(f_{N,\alpha}(\mu);\mu)\), taking a zero denominator as infinite.
For comparison, RGA-M uses lower group medians \(q_g=\min\{t:\sum_{x\le t}\mu_{g,x}\ge\mu_g/2\}\), weights \(c_g=\mu_g[1+\alpha_g(N\mu_g-1)]\), and \(\lambda=2\sqrt3-3\).
Write \(C=\sum_gc_g\), \(B=\sum_{q_g\le1/2}c_g\), and \(Q(a)=\min\{t:\sum_{q_g\le t}c_g\ge a\}\). Its output is \(Q((1+\lambda)C/2)\) if \(B>(1+\lambda)C/2\), \(1/2\) if \((1-\lambda)C/2\le B\le(1+\lambda)C/2\), and \(Q((1-\lambda)C/2)\) otherwise.

What is \(\rho^*=\inf_f\rho(f)\) over deterministic unanimous, location-strategyproof mechanism families on this domain: can the \(\sqrt3\) guarantee be improved, or is it optimal?

For finite societies, RGA-M is unanimous and location-strategyproof with approximation ratio at most \(\sqrt3\); every deterministic unanimous, location-strategyproof mechanism has worst-case ratio at least \(3/2\). [Source](https://ifmas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p1883.pdf)
These bounds also cover the stated integral-clone mass domain; the optimal ratio between them remains unresolved in the verified sources. The expanded treatment retains the same bounds. [Source](https://lbms03.cityu.edu.hk/theses/c_ftt/phd-cs-194502506.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted public coefficients to rational values in [0,1], as required by the discrete model.
  • Specified rational locations and reports in [0,1] to give the support input a finite binary encoding.
  • Required N to be positive and omitted empty groups, whose medians would be undefined.
  • Defined a deviating clone's utility using the true society, including group externalities and excluding its own contribution from the externality term.
  • Used lower weighted medians to preserve leftmost median tie-breaking; aggregate cumulative weights also settle coincident group medians and the case with no median left of 1/2.
  • Selected the expressly offered design variant, with an infimum over deterministic mechanism families and a uniform worst-case ratio over all admissible inputs.
  • Assigned infinite approximation ratio when achieved social utility is zero.

Result

The optimal ratio on the stated domain is
$$ \boxed{\rho^*=\frac32}. $$

Thus the $\sqrt3$ guarantee can be improved. The construction below replaces RGA-M’s two fixed quantile thresholds by a continuously varying threshold. It closes the gap between the bounds stated in the [AAMAS paper](https://ifmas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p1883.pdf) and its [expanded treatment](https://lbms03.cityu.edu.hk/theses/c_ftt/phd-cs-194502506.pdf).

The proof is self-contained: it establishes unanimity, individual location-strategyproofness, the $3/2$ welfare guarantee, and the matching universal lower bound.

1. Counts, weights, and group medians

Write
$$ n_{g,x}=N\mu_{g,x}, \qquad n_g=\sum_xn_{g,x}, \qquad \beta_g=1+\alpha_g(n_g-1). $$

Every $n_g$ is a positive integer, so $\beta_g\ge1$. Define
$$ c_g=\mu_g\beta_g, \qquad C=\sum_gc_g, \qquad w_g=\frac{c_g}{C}. $$
These weights are positive and sum to one. Crucially, they remain unchanged by a location report, because such a report preserves all group sizes.

Let $P_g$ be the conditional location distribution within group $g$:
$$ P_g(\{x\})=\frac{n_{g,x}}{n_g}. $$
Then normalized social utility is
$$ W(y)=\frac{SU_N(y;\mu)}{C} =\sum_gw_g\int(1-|y-t|)\,dP_g(t). $$
Normalization preserves approximation ratios.

The lower group median $q_g$ satisfies
$$ P_g([0,q_g])\ge\frac12, \qquad P_g([q_g,1])\ge\frac12. $$
Indeed, the mass strictly below the first support point reaching half the group mass is less than half.

For later use, put
$$ S_g(y)=\sum_tn_{g,t}(1-|y-t|). $$
The individual utility in the question is exactly
$$ U_{g,x}(y;\mu) =(1-\alpha_g)(1-|y-x|)+\alpha_gS_g(y). $$

The median inequalities imply that $S_g$ is nondecreasing on $[0,q_g]$ and nonincreasing on $[q_g,1]$. For example, if $q_g\le u\le v$, every clone at or below $q_g$ loses exactly $v-u$ when the facility moves from $u$ to $v$, while every remaining clone gains at most $v-u$. Since at least half the clones are at or below $q_g$,
$$ S_g(v)-S_g(u)\le0. $$
The argument on the left of $q_g$ is symmetric.

2. The mechanism

Define, for $p\in[0,1]$,
$$ R(p)=1+2p-2p^2, \qquad F(p)=\frac{1-p^2}{1+2p-2p^2}. $$

The denominator is positive, and
$$ 1\le R(p)\le\frac32. $$
Moreover,
$$ F(0)=1, \qquad F(1)=0, \qquad F'(p)= -\frac{2(p^2-p+1)}{(1+2p-2p^2)^2}<0. $$
Thus $F$ is a continuous, strictly decreasing bijection from $[0,1]$ onto $[0,1]$. Write $H=F^{-1}$.

Sort the group medians, retaining their weights:
$$ q_{(1)}\le\cdots\le q_{(m)}, \qquad p_i=\sum_{j=1}^iw_{(j)}. $$

Define the mechanism by
$$ \boxed{ f_{N,\alpha}^{\star}(\mu) = \min_{1\le i\le m} \max\{q_{(i)},F(p_i)\}. } $$

Equal medians may be ordered arbitrarily. Within a block of equal medians, $p_i$ increases and $F(p_i)$ decreases, so only the last index of the block can improve the displayed minimum.

Although $H$ is useful for the proof, the mechanism evaluates only $F$ at rational numbers. Its output is therefore rational.

The threshold characterization

For any $y\in[0,1]$, define
$$ A_<(y)=\sum_{q_g<y}w_g, \qquad A_\le(y)=\sum_{q_g\le y}w_g. $$

The mechanism’s output is the unique $y$ satisfying
$$ \boxed{ A_<(y)\le H(y)\le A_\le(y). } $$

To prove existence, let $y$ be the output in the explicit formula. If there are medians strictly below $y$, let $k$ be their last index. Since every candidate in the minimum is at least $y$,
$$ \max\{q_{(k)},F(p_k)\}\ge y. $$
Because $q_{(k)}<y$, this implies $F(p_k)\ge y$, hence
$$ A_<(y)=p_k\le H(y). $$
If there are no such medians, the inequality is immediate.

Next, take an index $i$ attaining the minimum. Then
$$ q_{(i)}\le y, \qquad F(p_i)\le y. $$
Consequently,
$$ A_\le(y)\ge p_i\ge H(y). $$

For uniqueness, suppose $y_1<y_2$ both satisfied the characterization. Then
$$ H(y_1) \le A_\le(y_1) \le A_<(y_2) \le H(y_2), $$
contradicting strict decrease of $H$.

This characterization includes atoms at the output and outputs at either endpoint or at the midpoint.

Unanimity

If all mass is at $x$, every group median equals $x$. Every candidate is at least $x$, and the last candidate is
$$ \max\{x,F(1)\}=x. $$
Therefore the mechanism outputs $x$.

3. Individual location-strategyproofness

We first establish two properties of the aggregation rule.

Changing one group median

Fix the weights and all other group medians. Suppose one median increases from $q$ to $q'$, with $q'>q$. Let the corresponding outputs be $y$ and $y'$.

At every location $t$, increasing that median weakly decreases cumulative median weight:
$$ A'_\le(t)\le A_\le(t). $$
If $y'<y$, the threshold characterizations would give
$$ H(y') \le A'_\le(y') \le A_\le(y') \le A_<(y) \le H(y), $$
contradicting $H(y')>H(y)$. Hence
$$ y'\ge y. $$

Furthermore, if $y<q$, both the old and new median lie strictly above $y$. Thus $A_<(y)$ and $A_\le(y)$ are unchanged. The old output still satisfies the new threshold characterization, so uniqueness implies $y'=y$.

It follows that
$$ q'>q,\quad y'>y \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y\ge q. $$

Reversing the comparison proves the corresponding statement for a decrease:
$$ q'<q,\quad y'<y \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y\le q. $$

Which clone can change its group median?

Suppose a clone at true location $x$ reports $x'$, changing its group’s lower median from $q$ to $q'$.

If $q'>q$, then necessarily $x\le q$. Otherwise, the report removes no clone at or below $q$, so the reported profile still has at least half its clones at or below $q$, forcing $q'\le q$.

If $q'<q$, then necessarily $x\ge q$. Otherwise, the report removes one clone strictly below $q$ and adds at most one there. The number strictly below $q$ cannot increase, so it remains less than half, forcing $q'\ge q$.

Comparing true utilities

A report changes only the reporting group’s median; all aggregation weights remain fixed.

If the group median is unchanged, the output is unchanged. Suppose it increases. The output weakly increases. If it increases strictly, the preceding facts give
$$ x\le q\le y\le y'. $$
Both the clone’s own proximity utility and its true group proximity sum are nonincreasing on $[q,1]$. Their coefficients $1-\alpha_g$ and $\alpha_g$ are nonnegative. Therefore
$$ U_{g,x}(y;\mu)\ge U_{g,x}(y';\mu). $$

If the group median decreases and the output changes, then
$$ y'\le y\le q\le x. $$
Both components of true utility are nondecreasing on $[0,q]$, again giving
$$ U_{g,x}(y;\mu)\ge U_{g,x}(y';\mu). $$

These cases cover every permitted report. Hence $f^\star$ is location-strategyproof.

4. The $3/2$ welfare guarantee

Fix a profile and let
$$ y=f^\star_{N,\alpha}(\mu), \qquad p=H(y), \qquad r=R(p). $$
Thus $y=F(p)$ and $1\le r\le3/2$.

We prove, for every $z\in[0,1]$,
$$ W(z)\le rW(y). $$

Splitting the median weight at the output

The threshold characterization gives
$$ A_<(y)\le p\le A_\le(y). $$

Assign all groups with median below $y$ to a left part and all groups with median above $y$ to a right part. Split the weights of groups whose median equals $y$, if necessary, so that the left part has total weight exactly $p$.

Every distribution assigned to the left part has at least half its mass in $[0,y]$. Every distribution assigned to the right part has at least half its mass in $[y,1]$. Splitting a group’s weight does not change its conditional distribution or the resulting normalized welfare distribution.

A comparison lemma

Define
$$ \phi(t)=(1-|z-t|)-r(1-|y-t|), \qquad M=\max\{\phi(0),\phi(1)\}. $$

The function $\phi$ is nonincreasing on $[0,y]$. Indeed, for $0\le s\le t\le y$, proximity to $y$ increases by exactly $t-s$, while proximity to $z$ increases by at most $t-s$. Hence
$$ \phi(t)-\phi(s)\le(1-r)(t-s)\le0. $$

Similarly, $\phi$ is nondecreasing on $[y,1]$: proximity to $y$ decreases by exactly $t-s$, and proximity to $z$ decreases by at most $t-s$, so
$$ \phi(t)-\phi(s)\ge(r-1)(t-s)\ge0. $$

Consequently, $\phi(t)\le M$ everywhere. If a probability distribution $P$ has mass $a\ge1/2$ in $[0,y]$, then
$$ \int\phi\,dP \le a\phi(0)+(1-a)M \le\frac{\phi(0)+M}{2}. $$
The last inequality uses $\phi(0)\le M$.

Likewise, if $P([y,1])\ge1/2$, then
$$ \int\phi\,dP\le\frac{\phi(1)+M}{2}. $$

Applying these bounds to the left and right parts yields
$$ W(z)-rW(y) \le \frac p2\phi(0)+\frac{1-p}{2}\phi(1)+\frac12M. $$

Set
$$ t_-=\frac p2, \qquad t_+=\frac{1+p}{2}. $$
The right-hand side is exactly
$$ \max\left\{ t_-\phi(0)+(1-t_-)\phi(1), \; t_+\phi(0)+(1-t_+)\phi(1) \right\}. $$

Thus it remains to check two endpoint distributions.

The two endpoint distributions

For a distribution with mass $t$ at $0$ and $1-t$ at $1$, its normalized welfare is
$$ W_t(v)=t(1-v)+(1-t)v. $$

Because $t_-\le1/2$ and $t_+\ge1/2$,
$$ \max_vW_{t_-}(v)=1-t_-=\frac{2-p}{2}, $$
and
$$ \max_vW_{t_+}(v)=t_+=\frac{1+p}{2}. $$

Our choice of $F$ gives the identities
$$ p+2(1-p)F(p)=\frac{2-p}{R(p)}, $$
and
$$ 1+p-2pF(p)=\frac{1+p}{R(p)}. $$
For verification, multiplying the first identity by $R(p)$ gives
$$ p(1+2p-2p^2)+2(1-p)(1-p^2)=2-p, $$
and multiplying the second gives
$$ (1+p)(1+2p-2p^2)-2p(1-p^2)=1+p. $$

Since $y=F(p)$ and $r=R(p)$, these identities imply
$$ rW_{t_-}(y)=\max_vW_{t_-}(v), \qquad rW_{t_+}(y)=\max_vW_{t_+}(v). $$

Therefore, for either $t=t_-$ or $t=t_+$,
$$ t\phi(0)+(1-t)\phi(1) = W_t(z)-rW_t(y) \le0. $$
The comparison lemma now gives
$$ W(z)\le rW(y)\le\frac32W(y). $$

There is no zero-denominator exception: every location has proximity at least $1/2$ to the midpoint, so $W(1/2)\ge1/2$. The proved inequality consequently implies
$$ W(y)\ge\frac13. $$

We have established
$$ \rho(f^\star)\le\frac32. $$

The ratio is attained by a small admissible example: take $N=4$, two groups of size two, and $\alpha_1=\alpha_2=0$. Put group $1$’s clones at $0$ and $1$, and both group $2$ clones at $1$. The medians are $0$ and $1$ with equal weights, so the mechanism outputs $F(1/2)=1/2$. Optimal social utility is $3/4$, while the output gives $1/2$, producing ratio $3/2$.

5. Universal lower bound

For completeness, we prove that every location-strategyproof mechanism family has ratio at least $3/2$. Unanimity is unnecessary for this argument.

Choose
$$ n=2^s\ge4, \qquad N=2n, \qquad k=\frac n2-1. $$
There are two groups, each containing $n$ clones, with
$$ \alpha_1=\alpha_2=1. $$

Initially, group $1$ is entirely at $0$ and group $2$ entirely at $1$. Let the mechanism output $y_0$.

Case 1: $y_0\ge1/2$

For $j=0,\ldots,k$, move $j$ group-$2$ clones from $1$ to $0$, and denote the resulting output by $y_j$.

At profile $j$, every group-$2$ clone has true utility
$$ U_2(y)=j(1-y)+(n-j)y=j+(n-2j)y. $$

For each $j\ge1$, an occupied group-$2$ clone at $0$ can report $1$, recovering profile $j-1$. Strategyproofness gives
$$ j+(n-2j)y_j \ge j+(n-2j)y_{j-1}. $$
Since $n-2j>0$ throughout the chain,
$$ y_j\ge y_{j-1}. $$
In particular, $y_k\ge1/2$.

The groups have equal welfare weights. At the final profile, the normalized welfare distribution places mass
$$ t=\frac{n+k}{2n}>\frac12 $$
at $0$, and the remainder at $1$. Therefore
$$ W(y)=t+(1-2t)y. $$
Its optimum is $W(0)=t$, while $y_k\ge1/2$ implies $W(y_k)\le1/2$. The approximation ratio is at least
$$ 2t=1+\frac kn=\frac32-\frac1n. $$

Case 2: $y_0<1/2$

Instead move $j$ group-$1$ clones from $0$ to $1$. At profile $j$, their common true utility is
$$ U_1(y)=n-j-(n-2j)y. $$
A clone at $1$ can report $0$ to recover profile $j-1$. Strategyproofness gives $y_j\le y_{j-1}$, hence $y_k<1/2$.

The final normalized welfare distribution now places mass $t=(n+k)/(2n)$ at $1$. Its optimum is $1$, and its welfare at the mechanism output is at most $1/2$. The same lower bound follows.

Every profile has dyadic masses and at most three occupied types; every report uses a rational endpoint. Taking arbitrarily large powers of two proves
$$ \rho(f)\ge\frac32 $$
for every admissible family.

Together with the construction,
$$ \boxed{\rho^*=\rho(f^\star)=\frac32}. $$

6. Computation from the compressed support

The mechanism requires no expansion into clones.

Put
$$ K=L+\left\lceil\log_2(N+1)\right\rceil. $$
Using elementary integer arithmetic, its output can be computed in
$$ O\!\left( \bigl[ \tau\log(\tau+1)+m\log(m+1)+\tau+m \bigr]K^2 \right) $$
bit operations.

Here is an exact implementation.

  • Compute the integer counts $n_{g,x}=N\mu_{g,x}$ and group counts $n_g$. Sort locations within each group and accumulate counts to find its lower median.
  • Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of the public coefficients. Use integer weights

$$ h_g=D\,n_g[1+\alpha_g(n_g-1)]. $$
These are a common positive scaling of the mechanism weights $c_g$.

  • Sort the group medians with their weights. Write

$$ T=\sum_gh_g, \qquad S_i=\sum_{j=1}^ih_{(j)}. $$
Compute each candidate using
$$ F(S_i/T) = \frac{T^2-S_i^2}{T^2+2TS_i-2S_i^2}. $$
Its denominator is
$$ T^2+2S_i(T-S_i)\ge T^2>0. $$
Compare these rational values exactly with the corresponding medians, then take the minimum of the resulting maxima.

The bit length of $D$ is $O(L)$. The counts, scaled weights, cumulative weights, and candidate numerators and denominators have $O(K)$ bits; here $m\le N$ because groups are nonempty. Rational comparisons and arithmetic therefore cost $O(K^2)$ each. The sorting and arithmetic counts give the stated bound.
