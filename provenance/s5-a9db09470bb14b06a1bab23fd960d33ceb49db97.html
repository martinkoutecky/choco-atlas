The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given supported applicant types \(\mathcal M\) and position types \(\mathcal W\), with rational masses \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\) each summing to \(1\), and weak rankings of mutually acceptable opposite-side types, choose matching masses \(x_{ab}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{ab}\le\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\le\nu_b\). Define unmatched masses \(u_a=\mu_a-\sum_b x_{ab}\) and \(v_b=\nu_b-\sum_a x_{ab}\); for each acceptable pair \((a,b)\), require \(u_a+\sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}x_{ab'}=0\) or \(v_b+\sum_{a':\,a\succ_b a'}x_{a'b}=0\). Compute the maximum \(\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}\), and classify its complexity in the treewidth \(t\) of the type graph, support size \(\tau\), and input bit length \(L\).

Masses satisfy \(\sum_a\mu_a=\sum_b\nu_b=1\). Each type has a weak ranking, with ties allowed, of its acceptable opposite-side types; acceptability is mutual, lists may be incomplete, and every acceptable partner is strictly preferred to being unmatched. Ties are not broken.
Let \(E\subseteq\mathcal M\times\mathcal W\) be the acceptable pairs. Choose real masses \(x_{ab}\ge0\) on \(E\), with \(x_{ab}=0\) outside \(E\), satisfying \(\sum_bx_{ab}\le\mu_a\) and \(\sum_ax_{ab}\le\nu_b\).

The objective is to compute the exact maximum \(\sum_{(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\). Let \(t\) be the treewidth of the bipartite graph \((\mathcal M\cup\mathcal W,E)\); a tree decomposition of width \(t\) is supplied.

Classification

The problem is NP-hard. It is also \(\mathrm{W}[1]\)-hard parameterized by \(t\), even with equal positive dyadic masses and a supplied tree decomposition. Consequently, an algorithm with running time \(f(t)(m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) would imply \(\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W}[1]\).

The essential point is that, under the stated stability condition, fractional splitting cannot improve total matched mass when all vertex quotas are equal. I prove this below, including the normalization needed for the reduction.

1. Stability defines a union of faces

Consider the same acceptable graph and preferences, temporarily with arbitrary positive quotas \(c_z\) at vertices \(z\). Its feasible matching polytope is

\( P(c)=\left\{y\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{E}:\sum_{e\ni z}y_e\le c_z\text{ for every }z\right\}. \)

For an acceptable pair \((a,b)\), define the two dissatisfaction expressions

\( d_{ab}^{a}(y)=c_a-\sum_{b'}y_{ab'}+\sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}y_{ab'}, \qquad d_{ab}^{b}(y)=c_b-\sum_{a'}y_{a'b}+\sum_{a':\,a\succ_b a'}y_{a'b}. \)

Each expression is nonnegative on \(P(c)\): it is the sum of unused quota and assignments to strictly worse partners. The required stability condition is precisely

\( d_{ab}^{a}(y)=0\quad\text{or}\quad d_{ab}^{b}(y)=0 \)

for every acceptable pair.

For each edge, select one of its endpoints. Write \(\sigma(ab)\) for that selection, and define

\( F_\sigma=\left\{y\in P(c):d_{ab}^{\sigma(ab)}(y)=0\text{ for every }(a,b)\in E\right\}. \)

Whenever \(F_\sigma\) is nonempty, it is a face of \(P(c)\). Indeed, it is exactly the set where the nonnegative affine function

\( H_\sigma(y)=\sum_{(a,b)\in E}d_{ab}^{\sigma(ab)}(y) \)

attains its minimum value zero. Nonnegativity of the summands ensures that \(H_\sigma(y)=0\) is equivalent to all the selected equalities.

Every point of \(F_\sigma\) is stable. Conversely, every stable point belongs to some \(F_\sigma\), by choosing an endpoint whose dissatisfaction expression vanishes on each edge. Therefore, the stable feasible set is the finite union

\( \bigcup_{\sigma}F_\sigma. \)

No ties have been broken in this argument.

2. Integer quotas give integral stable optima

Lemma. If every quota \(c_z\) is an integer, every stable feasible \(y\) is a convex combination of stable feasible vectors with integer coordinates. In particular, there is such an integer vector with total matched mass at least that of \(y\).

Proof. First, \(P(c)\) has only integral vertices. To verify this, let \(A\) be the vertex-edge incidence matrix of the bipartite acceptable graph. Multiply the rows corresponding to one side by minus one. Each resulting column has one positive and one negative entry, both of absolute value one.

This signed matrix is totally unimodular. Here is the determinant argument. In any square submatrix, a column with at most one nonzero entry permits induction by determinant expansion. If every column has two nonzero entries, those entries have opposite signs, so the sum of the rows is zero and the determinant vanishes. Thus every square subdeterminant belongs to \(\{0,1,-1\}\).

Row sign changes preserve total unimodularity, and adjoining the negative identity matrix for the nonnegativity constraints also preserves it, by expansion along identity rows. Hence the constraint matrix of \(P(c)\) is totally unimodular. At any vertex, choose a nonsingular square system of active constraints. Its determinant is \(1\) or \(-1\), and its right-hand side is integral; Cramer's rule therefore makes every vertex coordinate integral.

Now let \(y\) be stable and choose \(\sigma\) with \(y\in F_\sigma\). The polytope \(P(c)\) is bounded, so its face \(F_\sigma\) is the convex hull of its vertices. Vertices of a face are vertices of the original polytope; consequently, all vertices of \(F_\sigma\) are integral. They are also stable by Section 1.

Write

\( y=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_i y^{(i)}, \qquad \lambda_i\ge 0,\qquad \sum_i\lambda_i=1, \)

using these stable integral vertices. Then

\( \sum_{e\in E}y_e = \sum_i\lambda_i\sum_{e\in E}y_e^{(i)}. \)

At least one summand has objective value at least the weighted average. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

For unit quotas, integral feasible vectors are precisely incidence vectors of ordinary matchings. For such a vector, \(d_{ab}^{a}=0\) means that \(a\) is matched to a partner it weakly prefers to \(b\). Thus the stated stability condition becomes exactly discrete weak stability.

It follows that, for unit quotas,

\( \max_{\text{stable fractional }y}\sum_{e\in E}y_e = \max_{\text{weakly stable discrete }M}|M|. \)

This identity concerns total matched mass, directly addressing the objective in the question.

3. A polynomial reduction satisfying all mass requirements

Start with a discrete maximum-cardinality weakly stable marriage instance having \(p\) applicants, \(q\) positions, and target cardinality \(K\).

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying

\( N\ge\max\{p,q,1\}. \)

Add applicants and positions with empty acceptable lists until there are exactly \(N\) vertices on each side. These isolated vertices cannot participate in either matchings or blocking pairs, so the maximum stable cardinality is unchanged.

Make every vertex a supported type and assign every type mass \(1/N\). Then:

  • Every supported mass is positive and dyadic.
  • Both side totals equal one.
  • Preferences, acceptability, and ties on the original vertices are unchanged.
  • \(m=N\) and \(\tau=2N\).
  • The construction has polynomial input length, since \(N\le 2\max\{p,q,1\}\).

For a feasible mass assignment \(x\), put \(y=Nx\). This produces unit quotas. Each dissatisfaction expression is multiplied by \(N\), so feasibility and stability are preserved in both directions. Section 2 therefore gives

\( \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}} = \frac{\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{discrete}}}{N}. \)

In particular,

\( \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{discrete}}\ge K \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{mass}}\ge K/N. \)

Maximum-cardinality weakly stable marriage with ties and incomplete lists is NP-hard, as proved in [Manlove et al., *Hard Variants of Stable Marriage*, Section 2.1](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/15/1/hvsm.pdf). The construction above is therefore a polynomial reduction proving NP-hardness of the supported-mass problem.

4. Treewidth hardness survives

The reduction changes the acceptable graph only by adding isolated vertices. Its treewidth is unchanged. A supplied decomposition extends by attaching a singleton bag for each new isolated vertex.

The discrete source problem is \(\mathrm{W}[1]\)-hard parameterized by the treewidth of this same acceptable graph. The source reduction also supplies decompositions of width bounded by a function of its source parameter; see Theorem 1 and the constructive proof of Lemma 4.19 in [Gupta, Saurabh, and Zehavi, *On Treewidth and Stable Marriage*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.05404).

Thus the normalization in Section 3 preserves the parameter and the supplied-decomposition requirement. It is a parameterized reduction to the supported-mass problem, proving \(\mathrm{W}[1]\)-hardness.

More explicitly, an exact algorithm running in \(f(t)(m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) would, on these constructed instances, compute the discrete optimum by multiplying its output by \(N\). The constructed input length is polynomial in the discrete input length, and treewidth is preserved. This would give an FPT algorithm for the discrete source problem.

Hence the requested FPT running time is ruled out unless \(\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W}[1]\).

5. The rational-threshold decision problem is NP-complete

For completeness, arbitrary binary rational masses do not require certificates involving arbitrary real numbers.

Let \(D\) be the least common multiple of all mass denominators. Its binary length is polynomial in \(L\), because

\( \log_2 D\le\sum_{\text{mass denominators }q}\log_2 q. \)

Multiplying assignments and quotas by \(D\) produces integer quotas. By Section 2, whenever a stable assignment reaches a given rational threshold, some stable assignment reaching that threshold has coordinates

\( x_{ab}=z_{ab}/D, \qquad z_{ab}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}. \)

Since all original vertex masses are at most one, \(z_{ab}\le D\). These integers form a polynomial-length certificate. Exact rational arithmetic verifies quota constraints, the stability disjunction on every acceptable pair, and the objective threshold in polynomial time.

Therefore, deciding whether the maximum stable mass is at least an input rational threshold is in NP and, by Section 3, NP-complete.
