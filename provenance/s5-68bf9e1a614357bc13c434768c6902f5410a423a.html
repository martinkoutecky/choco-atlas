The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite ranking set \(S\subseteq\mathcal L(A)\) and an explicit support of \(\tau\) voter types \(t\), each with a binary-rational mass \(\mu_t>0\), vote \(v_t\in S\), and binary-rational prediction distribution \(q_t\in\Delta(S)\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), compute the ranking maximizing \(f_\mu(\sigma)\sum_{\rho\in S} h_\mu(\rho\mid\sigma)/h_\mu(\sigma\mid\rho)\). Here \(f_\mu(\sigma)=\sum_{t:v_t=\sigma}\mu_t\) and \(h_\mu(\rho\mid\sigma)=\sum_{t:v_t=\sigma}\mu_tq_t(\rho)/f_\mu(\sigma)\); assume the required vote masses and denominators are positive.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) contain \(m\geq1\) alternatives, and let \(S\subseteq\mathcal L(A)\) be a nonempty, explicitly listed set of strict complete rankings; write \(s=|S|\).
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type reports a vote \(v_t\in S\) and an explicitly listed prediction vector \(q_t\), with nonnegative binary-rational entries \(q_t(\rho)\) summing to \(1\) over \(\rho\in S\).
Define \(f_\mu(\sigma)=\sum_{t:v_t=\sigma}\mu_t\) and \(h_\mu(\rho\mid\sigma)=f_\mu(\sigma)^{-1}\sum_{t:v_t=\sigma}\mu_tq_t(\rho)\).
Require \(f_\mu(\sigma)>0\) and \(h_\mu(\sigma\mid\rho)>0\) for every \(\sigma,\rho\in S\).
The computational problem is to output any ranking in \(\operatorname*{argmax}_{\sigma\in S} f_\mu(\sigma)\sum_{\rho\in S}h_\mu(\rho\mid\sigma)/h_\mu(\sigma\mid\rho)\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Is this exact maximization problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,s,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite sampled electorates, SP-voting has a conditional ground-truth recovery guarantee under a mixture of Mallows models sharing a central ranking: under sufficient separation, the stated sample requirement is \(n\geq O(m!\sqrt{m\log(m/\delta)})\), with success probability at least \(1-\delta\), for \(0<\delta<1\). Result

The exact maximization problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,s,L\). This includes the cost of exact rational arithmetic and exact score comparisons.

We use the standard encoding of rational numbers by binary numerators and positive binary denominators. The proof also applies when the inputs are restricted to dyadic rationals.

1. Replace the rational input by integer aggregates

Write the explicitly listed input numbers as

\( \displaystyle \mu_t=\frac{a_t}{d_t},\qquad q_t(\rho)=\frac{b_{t\rho}}{e_{t\rho}}, \)

where \(a_t,d_t,e_{t\rho}\) are positive integers and \(b_{t\rho}\) is a nonnegative integer. Define the common denominator

\( \displaystyle D=\left(\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}d_t\right)\left(\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}\prod_{\rho\in S}e_{t\rho}\right). \)

Because all these denominators are explicitly encoded within the input, their combined binary lengths are at most \(L\). Consequently, \(\log_2 D\leq L\), so \(D\) has at most \(L+1\) bits.

For \(\sigma,\rho\in S\), define integers

\( \displaystyle F_\sigma=\sum_{t:v_t=\sigma}a_t\frac{D}{d_t}, \)

\( \displaystyle J_{\sigma\rho}=\sum_{t:v_t=\sigma}a_tb_{t\rho}\frac{D}{d_te_{t\rho}}. \)

Every displayed quotient is an integer: the factors \(d_t\) and \(e_{t\rho}\) both occur in the product defining \(D\). These aggregates satisfy

\( \displaystyle F_\sigma=Df_\mu(\sigma),\qquad J_{\sigma\rho}=D\sum_{t:v_t=\sigma}\mu_tq_t(\rho). \)

Since the masses sum to \(1\), and each prediction entry lies in \([0,1]\),

\( \displaystyle 0\leq J_{\sigma\rho}\leq F_\sigma\leq D. \)

The promised positivity gives \(F_\sigma>0\) and \(J_{\sigma\rho}>0\) for every pair. Indeed,

\( \displaystyle h_\mu(\rho\mid\sigma)=\frac{J_{\sigma\rho}}{F_\sigma}, \)

and the positivity assumption covers every ordered pair.

Thus all integer aggregates have \(O(L)\) bits. They can be computed with \(O(\tau s)\) integer arithmetic operations, after identifying each vote’s position in the explicit list \(S\).

2. Express each score as an exact fraction

Let \(V_\sigma\) denote the objective value of \(\sigma\). Substituting the aggregates gives

\( \displaystyle V_\sigma =\frac{F_\sigma}{D}\sum_{\rho\in S} \frac{J_{\sigma\rho}/F_\sigma}{J_{\rho\sigma}/F_\rho} =\frac{1}{D}\sum_{\rho\in S}\frac{J_{\sigma\rho}F_\rho}{J_{\rho\sigma}}. \)

All cancellations are valid because the relevant integers are positive.

For each \(\sigma\), define

\( \displaystyle P_\sigma=\prod_{\rho\in S}J_{\rho\sigma}, \)

\( \displaystyle N_\sigma=\sum_{\rho\in S}J_{\sigma\rho}F_\rho\frac{P_\sigma}{J_{\rho\sigma}}. \)

Here \(P_\sigma>0\), and every quotient \(P_\sigma/J_{\rho\sigma}\) is an exact integer quotient. By construction,

\( \displaystyle V_\sigma=\frac{N_\sigma}{DP_\sigma}. \)

These fractions need not be reduced.

The bounds from Section 1 imply

\( \displaystyle P_\sigma\leq D^s. \)

Each summand defining \(N_\sigma\) contains two factors bounded by \(D\), multiplied by a product of \(s-1\) factors bounded by \(D\). Hence

\( \displaystyle N_\sigma\leq sD^{s+1},\qquad DP_\sigma\leq D^{s+1}. \)

Therefore every score has an exact representation whose numerator and denominator have

\( \displaystyle O\bigl((s+1)(L+1)+\log(s+1)\bigr) \)

bits. In particular, summing the rational terms does not require exponentially many bits relative to the explicit input parameters.

3. Find a maximizer by exact comparisons

For any \(\sigma,\eta\in S\), positivity of the denominators yields

\( \displaystyle V_\sigma\geq V_\eta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N_\sigma P_\eta\geq N_\eta P_\sigma. \)

The common factor \(D\) cancels. The cross products have \(O(s(L+1)+\log(s+1))\) bits.

The algorithm computes all \(P_\sigma,N_\sigma\), then scans \(S\), retaining a ranking with the greatest score seen so far. An induction on the number of scanned rankings proves that the retained ranking is always a maximizer among those already scanned. After the final comparison, it is therefore a member of the required argmax. Ties can be resolved by retaining the first ranking.

Computing all products and numerators takes \(O(s^2)\) integer arithmetic operations; the final scan takes \(s-1\) comparisons. Together with the aggregates, the arithmetic operation count is \(O(\tau s+s^2)\). Every operand has polynomially bounded bit length, and integer addition, multiplication, exact division, and comparison admit polynomial-time binary algorithms.

Identifying votes in the explicit ranking list also takes polynomial time: even direct comparison against every listed ranking uses only \(O(\tau sm)\) alternative-label comparisons. Returning the retained ranking takes time polynomial in its explicit encoding length.

This establishes the claimed deterministic polynomial-time bound, including bit complexity.

4. Why factorial ranking spaces do not change this conclusion

If \(S=\mathcal L(A)\), then \(s=m!\), so this algorithm need not run in polynomial time in \(m\) alone. But the task explicitly lists \(S\) and every prediction vector: the factorial-sized data are already part of the input.

The cited paper uses this normalized score in Equation (3) and observes that eliciting full distributions over all rankings requires information exponential in \(m\). Its statistical recovery analysis does not alter the complexity conclusion for the explicit input representation here. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.08367v1#S2)

No statistical or model assumptions are needed for the algorithm—only the stated rational encoding and positivity promises.
