The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) candidates \(C\), a target \(p\), a finite support \(S\) of complete rankings with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and a rational budget \(\beta\), does there exist a transfer \(x_{t,u}\geq 0\) for distinct \(t,u\in S\), with \(\sum_{u\ne t}x_{t,u}\leq\mu_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_{t\ne u}x_{t,u}\leq\beta\), such that \(p\) is a 3-Veto winner under the resulting masses \(\nu_u=\mu_u-\sum_{v\ne u}x_{u,v}+\sum_{t\ne u}x_{t,u}\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a candidate set \(C\) with \(m\geq 3\), a target \(p\in C\), and a nonnegative binary rational budget \(\beta\), consider continuous conformant bribery under 3-Veto.
A society is given by its support \(S\): the list of \(\tau\) distinct strict complete rankings with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings; \(L\) is the total input bit length. State running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
A transfer consists of real quantities \(x_{t,u}\geq 0\) for distinct \(t,u\in S\), subject to \(\sum_{u\in S\setminus\{t\}}x_{t,u}\leq\mu_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_{t\in S}\sum_{u\in S\setminus\{t\}}x_{t,u}\leq\beta\).
Each unit of transferred mass costs one unit of budget; destination rankings must belong to the initial support, even if their original mass is entirely transferred away.
The resulting masses are \(\nu_u=\mu_u-\sum_{v\in S\setminus\{u\}}x_{u,v}+\sum_{t\in S\setminus\{u\}}x_{t,u}\). Apply 3-Veto with scores weighted by these masses; every candidate tied for highest score is a winner.

Is deciding whether such a transfer makes \(p\) a winner solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For discrete unweighted voters, conformant bribery under 3-Veto is NP-complete, with a unit cost per changed voter and destinations restricted to initially occurring rankings. [Fitzsimmons and Hemaspaandra](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.11689v1).
No result settling this exact continuous transfer problem was located in the literature search.

Result

The decision problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). We prove this by constructing an exact rational linear feasibility problem with \(2\tau\) variables.

For \(m=3\), every candidate receives score zero, so the answer is always yes. The argument below also covers this case.

1. Minimum cost of realizing a mass vector

Index the initial support by \(S=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\). Let \(\nu\) be any nonnegative mass vector on \(S\) with \(\sum_t\nu_t=1\). Define

\[ D(\mu,\nu)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\max\{\mu_t-\nu_t,0\}. \]

Lemma. The minimum transfer cost required to obtain \(\nu\) is exactly \(D(\mu,\nu)\).

Proof. For any transfer realizing \(\nu\), write its outgoing and incoming mass at ranking \(t\) as

\[ a_t=\sum_{u\ne t}x_{t,u}, \qquad b_t=\sum_{u\ne t}x_{u,t}. \]

The mass-balance equation gives \(\mu_t-\nu_t=a_t-b_t\). Since \(a_t,b_t\geq0\),

\[ \max\{\mu_t-\nu_t,0\}\leq a_t. \]

Summing proves that every realizing transfer costs at least \(D(\mu,\nu)\).

For the converse, let \(s_t=\max\{\mu_t-\nu_t,0\}\) and \(d_t=\max\{\nu_t-\mu_t,0\}\). Since both mass vectors sum to one,

\[ \sum_t s_t=\sum_t d_t=D(\mu,\nu). \]

If this quantity is zero, the vectors coincide and no transfer is needed. Otherwise, put

\[ x_{t,u}=\frac{s_t d_u}{D(\mu,\nu)} \qquad(t\ne u). \]

A ranking cannot have both positive surplus and positive deficit, so \(s_td_t=0\). Consequently, the outgoing mass at \(t\) is exactly \(s_t\), and the incoming mass is exactly \(d_t\). Moreover, \(s_t\leq\mu_t\), so every source constraint holds. The resulting mass is

\[ \mu_t-s_t+d_t=\nu_t, \]

and the total cost is \(\sum_t s_t=D(\mu,\nu)\). Every destination belongs to the initial support, as required. This proves the lemma.

2. Linear winner conditions

For each candidate \(c\) and support ranking \(t\), define \(v_{c,t}=1\) if \(c\) appears among the bottom three positions of \(t\), and \(v_{c,t}=0\) otherwise.

Under resulting masses \(\nu\), candidate \(c\)'s weighted veto count and score are respectively

\[ V_c(\nu)=\sum_t v_{c,t}\nu_t, \qquad \operatorname{score}_c(\nu)=1-V_c(\nu). \]

The score identity uses \(\sum_t\nu_t=1\). Thus, with ties counted as wins, \(p\) is a winner exactly when

\[ \sum_t(v_{p,t}-v_{c,t})\nu_t\leq0 \qquad\text{for every }c\ne p. \]

These are non-strict linear inequalities.

3. Exact linear-programming formulation

Introduce real variables \(\nu_t,r_t\) for every \(t\in S\), and impose:

  • \(\nu_t\geq0\) and \(r_t\geq0\) for every \(t\).
  • \(\sum_t\nu_t=1\).
  • \(r_t\geq\mu_t-\nu_t\) for every \(t\).
  • \(\sum_t r_t\leq\beta\).
  • \(\sum_t(v_{p,t}-v_{c,t})\nu_t\leq0\) for every \(c\ne p\).

Legal successful transfer implies feasibility. Such a transfer produces nonnegative masses summing to one: the source constraints ensure nonnegativity, and each transfer contributes equally to total outgoing and incoming mass. Set \(r_t=\max\{\mu_t-\nu_t,0\}\). The lemma's lower bound gives \(\sum_t r_t\leq\beta\), and the winner inequalities hold by the preceding section.

Feasibility implies a legal successful transfer. The constraints on \(r_t\) imply

\[ D(\mu,\nu)\leq\sum_t r_t\leq\beta. \]

The lemma constructs a transfer realizing \(\nu\), respecting all source constraints and costing at most \(\beta\). The remaining inequalities ensure that \(p\) is a winner.

Hence the linear system is feasible if and only if the given bribery instance is a yes-instance.

4. Encoding size and running time

There are \(2\tau\) variables. Replacing the mass-sum equality by two inequalities gives \(m+3\tau+2\) inequalities.

All matrix coefficients belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). The right-hand sides use only the input masses, the budget, and constant-size integers. Under a standard dense encoding, the resulting system has binary length

\[ B=O\bigl(L+\tau(m+\tau)\bigr). \]

The veto indicators can be obtained by scanning the explicitly supplied rankings. Constructing the system therefore takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\); no absent ranking is enumerated.

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: feasibility over the reals of a finite system with binary rational coefficients is decidable exactly in time polynomial in its binary encoding length. This theorem applies to equalities represented by opposite inequalities and does not require a full-dimensional feasible region. See [Khachiyan, *A polynomial algorithm in linear programming*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Thus the overall decision running time is

\[ \bigl(L+\tau(m+\tau)\bigr)^{O(1)} \]

bit operations. This settles the stated continuous problem. Indivisible voters would impose additional integrality requirements, which this linear-programming algorithm does not enforce.

Their non-strict form correctly permits ties. For \(m=3\), all candidates are vetoed in every ranking, so all scores are zero and all winner inequalities hold identically.
