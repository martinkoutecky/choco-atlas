The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of weight types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), positive integer weights \(w_t\), a per-capita quota \(q\), and accuracy \(\varepsilon>0\), compute an \(\varepsilon\)-accurate value of \(\Psi_\mu(q)\) and an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal profile \(x\), where \(\Psi_\mu(q)=\max\{\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t h(x_t/\mu_t): 0\le x_t\le\mu_t,\ \sum_{t\in T}w_tx_t=q\}\) and \(h(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) weight types, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a binary-encoded positive integer weight \(w_t\). Input also includes binary rationals \(0<q\le\sum_t\mu_tw_t\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1\). Let \(m=\max_t w_t\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A profile \(x_t\) specifies the mass of type \(t\) included in a coalition. Define \(F_\mu(x)=\sum_t\mu_t h(x_t/\mu_t)\), where \(h(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)\), using natural logarithms and \(0\log0=0\).
Define \(\Psi_\mu(q)=\max\{F_\mu(x):0\le x_t\le\mu_t,\ \sum_t w_tx_t=q\}\). The computational problem is to return rational \(v,x\) with \(|v-\Psi_\mu(q)|\le\varepsilon\), exactly feasible \(x\), and \(F_\mu(x)\ge\Psi_\mu(q)-\varepsilon\).

Is this computational problem solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau,L,\log(1+m),\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?

For a discrete list of \(n\) positive integer weights, all nonzero coefficients of \(\prod_{i=1}^n(1+z^{w_i})\), counting coalitions of each total weight, can be computed in \(O(nC)\) arithmetic operations and \(O(C)\) storage, where \(C\) is the number of distinct coalition weights ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.6543v2)).
No result settling this exact continuous computational problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the undefined quota domain to positive binary rationals at most the total per-capita weight, preserving the discrete requirements that the empty coalition loses and the grand coalition wins.
  • Specified natural logarithms and the endpoint convention 0 log 0 = 0.
  • Interpreted accuracy as additive value error and additive objective suboptimality, with exact feasibility and rational outputs.
  • Specified binary-rational accuracy 0 < epsilon < 1 and polynomial dependence on its precision rather than its reciprocal.
  • Defined m as the maximum weight because this society has no candidate or alternative count.

Result

The stated continuous computational problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(\tau,L,\log(1+m),\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

The proof below gives a rational-output algorithm, including an exact repair of the equality constraint and explicit control of transcendental approximation errors.

1. Reduction and characterization of the optimizer

Write \(W=\sum_t\mu_tw_t\). Since the weights are positive integers and the masses sum to one, \(1\le W\le m\).

If \(q=W\), the only feasible profile is \(x=\mu\), and its entropy is zero. Return this profile and \(v=0\).

Otherwise, complementation gives

\(F_\mu(\mu-x)=F_\mu(x)\), and \(\sum_tw_t(\mu_t-x_t)=W-\sum_tw_tx_t\).

Consequently, it suffices to solve the problem for

\(r=\min\{q,W-q\}\in(0,W/2]\).

If \(r=W-q\), complement the resulting profile at the end.

For \(\beta\ge0\), define

\(p_t(\beta)=\dfrac{1}{1+\exp(\beta w_t)}\), \(\quad a_t(\beta)=\mu_tp_t(\beta)\), \(\quad g(\beta)=\sum_tw_ta_t(\beta)\).

The function \(g\) is continuous and strictly decreasing, because

\(g'(\beta)=-\sum_t\mu_tw_t^2p_t(\beta)(1-p_t(\beta))<0\).

Moreover, \(g(0)=W/2\), and \(g(\beta)\) tends to zero. Thus there is a unique \(\beta_*\ge0\) satisfying \(g(\beta_*)=r\).

The profile \(a_*=a(\beta_*)\) maximizes entropy at weight \(r\). Indeed, \(h\) is concave and

\(h'(p_t(\beta_*))=\log\!\left(\dfrac{1-p_t(\beta_*)}{p_t(\beta_*)}\right)=\beta_*w_t\).

The supporting-line inequality therefore gives, for every feasible profile \(z\),

\(F_\mu(z)\le F_\mu(a_*)+\beta_*\sum_tw_t(z_t-a_{*,t})=F_\mu(a_*)\).

This proves optimality without assuming a numerical optimization oracle.

Two estimates will be useful. First,

\(\lvert g'(\beta)\rvert\le m^2/4\).

Second, all coordinates of \(a(\beta)-a(\beta_*)\) have the same sign. Since every \(w_t\ge1\),

\(\displaystyle \|a(\beta)-a_*\|_1\le\sum_tw_t|a_t(\beta)-a_{*,t}|=|g(\beta)-r|.\)

This second estimate avoids any division by a potentially tiny derivative.

2. A quantitative entropy continuity bound

For \(u,v\in[0,1]\),

\(\lvert h(u)-h(v)\rvert\le h(|u-v|)\).

To prove this, fix \(d\in(0,1)\). The function \(h(u+d)-h(u)\), for \(0<u<1-d\), has negative derivative because \(h'\) is strictly decreasing. Its endpoint values, obtained by continuity, are \(h(d)\) and \(-h(d)\). The cases \(d=0,1\) follow directly.

For profiles \(x,y\) in the box, let \(D=\sum_t|x_t-y_t|\). Applying this inequality coordinatewise and then applying concavity of \(h\), with weights \(\mu_t\), gives

\(\displaystyle |F_\mu(x)-F_\mu(y)|\le\sum_t\mu_t h\!\left(\frac{|x_t-y_t|}{\mu_t}\right)\le h(D).\)

Here \(D\le1\), since both profiles lie in the box and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).

We also have the elementary bound

\(h(d)\le2\sqrt d\quad\text{for }0\le d\le1.\)

For \(0<d<1\), the inequality \(-\log(1-d)\le d/(1-d)\) yields \(-(1-d)\log(1-d)\le d\). Also, setting \(u=\sqrt d\),

\(-d\log d=2u^2\log(1/u)\le(2/e)u\le u\),

because the maximum of \(u\log(1/u)\) on \([0,1]\) is \(1/e\). Since \(d\le\sqrt d\), the bound follows; endpoints follow by continuity.

Hence, if \(\|x-y\|_1\le\delta\), then

\(|F_\mu(x)-F_\mu(y)|\le2\sqrt\delta.\)

3. Polynomial-time rational evaluation of logistic coordinates

We first give a uniform numerical primitive.

Given a nonnegative rational \(y\) and rational \(0<\alpha<1/2\), one can compute a rational \(\widehat p\in[0,1]\) such that

\(\displaystyle \left|\widehat p-\frac{1}{1+e^y}\right|\le\alpha\)

in time polynomial in the encoding length of \(y,\alpha\) and \(\log(1/\alpha)\), even when the numerical value of \(y\) is enormous.

Let \(K\ge1\) be the least integer satisfying \(2^{-K}\le\alpha\).

If \(y\ge K\), return \(\widehat p=0\). This is valid because

\(\displaystyle \frac1{1+e^y}\le e^{-y}\le e^{-K}\le2^{-K}\le\alpha.\)

If \(y<K\), set \(N=6K\) and compute exactly the rational number

\(\displaystyle S_N(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{y^j}{j!}.\)

For the exponential tail, consecutive terms starting at index \(N\) have ratio at most \(K/(N+1)<1/6\). Using \(N!\ge(N/e)^N\) and \(e<3\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle 0\le e^y-S_N(y)\le2\frac{y^N}{N!}\le2\left(\frac{ey}{N}\right)^N\le2^{1-N}\le2^{-K}\le\alpha.\)

The factorial inequality follows, for example, by comparing \(\sum_{j=1}^N\log j\) with \(\int_1^N\log s\,ds\).

Return \(\widehat p=1/(1+S_N(y))\). Then

\(\displaystyle \left|\widehat p-\frac1{1+e^y}\right|=\frac{e^y-S_N(y)}{(1+S_N(y))(1+e^y)}\le\alpha.\)

This computation has polynomial bit complexity. There are \(O(K)\) terms. If \(y=A/D\), their denominators divide \(D^{N-1}(N-1)!\), whose bit length is polynomial in \(N\) and the encoding length of \(y\). The numerators also have polynomial bit length. Thus the exact rational arithmetic uses only polynomially many bits.

4. Certified bisection

Set

\(\delta=\varepsilon^2/16\), \(\qquad \alpha=\delta/(8m)\).

At any rational parameter \(\beta\), use the primitive above with \(y=\beta w_t\) to obtain \(\widehat p_t\). Define the rational profile and its exact weight by

\(y_t=\mu_t\widehat p_t\), \(\qquad s=\sum_tw_ty_t\).

They satisfy

\(\displaystyle \|y-a(\beta)\|_1\le\alpha\), \(\qquad |s-g(\beta)|\le W\alpha\le\delta/8.\)

For an initial bracket, choose the least integer \(B\ge1\) satisfying \(W2^{-B}\le r\). Since

\(\displaystyle g(B)\le\sum_t\mu_tw_te^{-Bw_t}\le We^{-B}<W2^{-B}\le r,\)

the interval \([0,B]\) contains \(\beta_*\). The integer \(B\) can be found by exact rational comparisons; its value is polynomially bounded in the input length.

Maintain a bracket \([\ell,u]\) containing \(\beta_*\). At its midpoint \(\beta\), compute \(y,s\) as above.

  • If \(|s-r|\le\delta/4\), stop.
  • If \(s>r+\delta/4\), replace \(\ell\) by \(\beta\).
  • If \(s<r-\delta/4\), replace \(u\) by \(\beta\).

Every update preserves the bracket. In the second case, for example,

\(g(\beta)\ge s-\delta/8>r\),

so strict decrease of \(g\) implies \(\beta<\beta_*\). The third case is analogous.

The stopping condition must occur after polynomially many iterations. Once the bracket width is at most \(\delta/(2m^2)\), the derivative bound and the fact that \(\beta_*\) lies in the bracket imply

\(\displaystyle |g(\beta)-r|\le\frac{m^2}{4}(u-\ell)\le\delta/8.\)

Together with the evaluation error, this gives \(|s-r|\le\delta/4\). Thus the number of midpoint evaluations is at most

\(\displaystyle 1+\left\lceil\log_2\!\left(\frac{2Bm^2}{\delta}\right)\right\rceil.\)

At termination, the monotonicity estimate from Section 1 gives

\(\displaystyle \|y-a_*\|_1\le\|y-a(\beta)\|_1+\|a(\beta)-a_*\|_1\le\alpha+|g(\beta)-r|\)

\(\displaystyle \le\alpha+|s-r|+\delta/8\le\delta/2.\)

The final inequality uses \(m\ge1\), hence \(\alpha\le\delta/8\).

5. Exact rational feasibility repair

The terminating profile \(y\) is rational and lies in the box. Repair its weight using exact rational arithmetic.

If \(s=r\), set \(z=y\).

If \(s>r\), set

\(\displaystyle z_t=\frac{r}{s}y_t.\)

If \(s<r\), set

\(\displaystyle \theta=\frac{r-s}{W-s},\qquad z_t=(1-\theta)y_t+\theta\mu_t.\)

The denominators are nonzero: in the scaling case \(s>r>0\); in the interpolation case \(s<r\le W/2<W\). The scaling factor belongs to \((0,1)\), and so does \(\theta\).

Therefore, in all cases, \(z\) is rational, lies in the box, and satisfies

\(\sum_tw_tz_t=r\)

exactly.

All coordinate changes in the repair have the same sign. Since \(w_t\ge1\),

\(\displaystyle \|z-y\|_1\le\sum_tw_t|z_t-y_t|=|r-s|\le\delta/4.\)

Consequently,

\(\|z-a_*\|_1\le3\delta/4\le\delta.\)

Section 2 now gives

\(\displaystyle F_\mu(z)\ge\Psi_\mu(r)-2\sqrt\delta=\Psi_\mu(r)-\varepsilon/2.\)

If complementation was used, return \(x=\mu-z\); otherwise return \(x=z\). In either case, \(x\) is exactly feasible for the original quota, and

\(F_\mu(x)\ge\Psi_\mu(q)-\varepsilon/2.\)

6. Computing the rational value and bounding bit complexity

It remains to compute a rational \(v\) approximating \(F_\mu(x)\).

For completeness, logarithms of positive rationals can be approximated in polynomial time by an explicit series. Given a positive rational \(a\), choose an integer \(k\) by exact comparisons so that

\(a=2^ku,\qquad 1\le u<2.\)

With \(z=(u-1)/(u+1)\in[0,1/3)\),

\(\displaystyle \log u=2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}.\)

After \(N\) terms, the tail has magnitude at most

\(\displaystyle \frac{2z^{2N+1}}{1-z^2}\le3\cdot3^{-(2N+1)}.\)

The same series with \(z=1/3\) computes \(\log2\). To approximate \(\log a=k\log2+\log u\) within \(\rho\), approximate each constituent logarithm within \(\rho/(|k|+1)\). This needs

\(O(\log(|k|+1)+\log(1/\rho))\)

terms. The integer \(|k|\) is bounded by the encoding length of \(a\), and all series calculations have polynomial bit complexity.

For each rational \(p_t=x_t/\mu_t\) strictly between zero and one, approximate \(\log p_t\) and \(\log(1-p_t)\) within \(\varepsilon/2\). Then the resulting rational approximation to \(h(p_t)\) has error at most

\(p_t\varepsilon/2+(1-p_t)\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon/2.\)

At the endpoints, use \(h(0)=h(1)=0\) exactly. Because the masses sum to one, the weighted sum \(v\) satisfies

\(|v-F_\mu(x)|\le\varepsilon/2.\)

Exact feasibility gives \(F_\mu(x)\le\Psi_\mu(q)\), while Section 5 gives the reverse bound within \(\varepsilon/2\). Hence

\(|v-\Psi_\mu(q)|\le\varepsilon.\)

Finally, all encoding lengths remain polynomial:

  • Exact calculation of \(W,r,\delta,\alpha\) has polynomial bit complexity.
  • The integer \(B\) has value \(O(L+\log(1+m))\), because the positive rational \(r\) has polynomial encoding length.
  • Bisection uses \(O(\log B+\log(1+m)+\log(1/\varepsilon))\) iterations, and its midpoint encodings grow linearly with this count.
  • Logistic evaluation uses \(O(\log(1+m)+\log(1/\varepsilon))\) series terms per type.
  • Summation, the single feasibility repair, and the final logarithm calculations involve polynomially encoded rationals.

Thus both runtime and output length are polynomial in the parameters specified in the question.

7. Relation to discrete coalition counting

The supplied [generating-function reference](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6543v2) concerns discrete weighted-voting computations. A bound depending on the number \(C\) of distinct coalition weights can still be exponential: for weights \(w_i=2^{i-1}\), uniqueness of binary expansions gives \(C=2^n\).

The continuous algorithm proved above does not enumerate coalition weights or compute generating-function coefficients. Its polynomial bound depends on the explicitly supplied support and the requested precision.
