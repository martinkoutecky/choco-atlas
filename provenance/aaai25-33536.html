The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite item kinds \(G\), agent types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), entitlements \(w_t>0\), binary values \(a_{t,g}\in\{0,1\}\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_g\), where a bundle is an indivisible-copy vector \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{G}\), find a rational sparse configuration \(x_{t,b}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_gx_{t,b}=\rho_g\). Require that every pair of supported type-bundle pairs satisfies WEFX after removing any one copy, and that the configuration is PO under identity-preserving measurable reallocations; determine whether such a configuration can be computed in time polynomial in \(|T|+|G|\) and the input bit length, independently of the common denominator.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types \(T\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|G|\) item kinds, binary-rational supplies \(\rho_g\ge0\), binary-rational entitlements \(w_t>0\), and binary additive valuations \(v_t(b)=\sum_g a_{t,g}b_g\), where \(a_{t,g}\in\{0,1\}\) and \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A configuration is a finite list of positive rational entries \(x_{t,b}\), with all unlisted entries zero, satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_gx_{t,b}=\rho_g\) for every \(g\); all supply must be allocated.
Require WEFX: for every supported \((t,b),(s,c)\) and every \(g\) with \(c_g>0\), \(v_t(b)/w_t\ge v_t(c-e_g)/w_s\), where \(e_g\) is the unit vector. This includes removal of copies valued zero by the evaluating type.
For identity-preserving Pareto optimality, realize each entry as an interval of agents of length \(x_{t,b}\), with fixed type \(t\) and initial bundle \(b\). No measurable reassignment of integer bundles preserving the supply integrals may weakly improve utility almost everywhere and strictly improve it on a set of positive measure; competing assignments need not satisfy WEFX.

Is finding a rational configuration satisfying WEFX and Pareto optimality, or reporting that none exists, solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), independently of the numerical common denominator except through \(L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finitely many explicitly listed agents and goods, binary additive valuations and positive entitlements always admit a complete WEFX and Pareto-optimal allocation computable in polynomial time ([Neoh and Teh, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03951v1)).
No result settling this exact atomless configuration problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total agent mass to one so that supplies have the stated per-capita interpretation.
  • Specified entitlements as positive binary rationals and supplies as nonnegative binary rationals; included all input encodings in L.
  • Interpreted sparse configuration as an explicitly encoded finite list of positive rational entries, with integer bundle coordinates encoded in binary.
  • Made WEFX quantify over all supported pairs, including pairs of the same type, and removal of every present copy, including zero-valued copies.
  • Defined identity-preserving measurable reallocations on atomless intervals, with weak improvement almost everywhere and strict improvement on positive mass; competitors preserve complete supply allocation and need not satisfy fairness.
  • Included reporting nonexistence because the continuous question supplied no existence promise.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. Every valid instance admits a rational configuration satisfying WEFX and the stated identity-preserving Pareto optimality. A configuration can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with at most \(\tau(m+1)\) positive entries and polynomial total encoding length.

The proof uses an exactly optimizable discrete potential and a short, explicit decomposition of rational averages into integer bundles.

1. Pareto optimality is equivalent to allocating valued goods to valuers

Define
\[ \]
\(G^+=\{g\in G:\text{some }t\in T\text{ has }a_{t,g}=1\}\),
\(G^0=G\setminus G^+\), and
\(S=\sum_{g\in G^+}\rho_g\).

Call a configuration *nonwasteful* if every supported \((t,b)\) satisfies \(b_g=0\) whenever \(g\in G^+\) and \(a_{t,g}=0\).

Lemma 1. A configuration is Pareto optimal in the sense of the question if and only if it is nonwasteful.

Proof. For every feasible measurable assignment, integrated utility is at most \(S\): each copy of a kind in \(G^+\) contributes at most one unit of utility, and kinds in \(G^0\) contribute zero. A nonwasteful configuration attains integrated utility exactly \(S\).

If a competing assignment weakly improves utility almost everywhere and strictly improves it on a set of positive measure, its integrated utility is strictly larger. Indeed, utilities are integer-valued, so every strict improvement increases utility by at least one. This contradicts the upper bound \(S\).

Conversely, suppose a supported entry \((t,b)\) wastes a copy of \(g\in G^+\). Choose a type \(s\) valuing \(g\), and a positive-measure entry of that type. On equal, sufficiently small portions of the two entries, remove one copy of \(g\) from the first portion and add one copy to the second. Supply integrals are preserved, donors lose no utility, and recipients strictly improve. Thus the original configuration is not Pareto optimal. \(\square\)

We initially allocate only \(G^+\), nonwastefully. Section 5 allocates \(G^0\).

2. A potential whose minimizers satisfy WEFX

For an integer utility \(k\geq 0\), define
\(f_t(k)=k(k-1)/(2w_t)\).

For a nonwasteful configuration on \(G^+\), define its potential by
\(\Phi(x)=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}f_t(v_t(b))\).

Its marginal increments satisfy
\(f_t(k+1)-f_t(k)=k/w_t\).

Lemma 2. Every global minimizer of \(\Phi\) among rational nonwasteful configurations on \(G^+\) satisfies WEFX.

Proof. Suppose supported entries \((t,b)\) and \((s,c)\) violate WEFX for some \(g\) with \(c_g>0\). Put \(k=v_t(b)\) and \(h=v_s(c)\). Nonwastefulness implies that every copy in \(c\) is valued by \(s\), so \(h=\sum_r c_r\). Consequently,
\(k/w_t<v_t(c-e_g)/w_s\leq(h-1)/w_s\).

The violating right-hand side is positive. Hence \(c\) contains a copy of some kind \(r\) valued by \(t\). That copy is also valued by \(s\), by nonwastefulness.

Choose equal portions of rational mass \(\varepsilon>0\) from the two supported entries. Transfer one copy of \(r\) from every donor in the second portion to a recipient in the first. The entries must be distinct, since an entry cannot violate WEFX against itself. Thus the portions can be chosen disjoint.

The transfer preserves supplies and nonwastefulness. Its potential change is
\(\varepsilon\bigl(f_t(k+1)-f_t(k)+f_s(h-1)-f_s(h)\bigr) =\varepsilon\bigl(k/w_t-(h-1)/w_s\bigr)<0\).

This contradicts global minimality. \(\square\)

The next two sections establish that such a minimizer can be constructed in polynomial time.

3. Realizing rational averages with few integer bundles

Let \(F_t\) be the piecewise-linear interpolation of \(f_t\) between consecutive nonnegative integers. For \(q\geq0\), writing \(k=\lfloor q\rfloor\), we have
\(F_t(q)=\bigl(kq-k(k+1)/2\bigr)/w_t\).

We need the following explicit decomposition.

Lemma 3. Given a nonnegative rational vector \(r=(r_1,\ldots,r_d)\), one can compute a rational distribution on at most \(d+1\) nonnegative integer vectors \(b\) such that:

  • the coordinate averages are \(r_j\);
  • every supported vector has total size either \(\lfloor q\rfloor\) or \(\lceil q\rceil\), where \(q=\sum_j r_j\).

The computation and output encoding length are polynomial in the encoding length of \(r\) and in \(d\).

Proof. Define cumulative sums \(C_0=0\) and \(C_j=\sum_{i=1}^j r_i\). For \(\theta\in[0,1)\), set
\(b_j(\theta)=\lfloor C_j+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor C_{j-1}+\theta\rfloor\).

Because cumulative sums are nondecreasing, these are nonnegative integers. Telescoping gives
\(\sum_j b_j(\theta)=\lfloor q+\theta\rfloor\),
which has the required two possible values.

For every nonnegative real \(u\),
\(\int_0^1\lfloor u+\theta\rfloor\,d\theta=u\).
Indeed, writing \(u=n+\delta\) with \(0\leq\delta<1\), the integrand equals \(n\) on length \(1-\delta\) and \(n+1\) on length \(\delta\). Therefore,
\(\int_0^1 b_j(\theta)\,d\theta=C_j-C_{j-1}=r_j\).

Each cumulative floor changes at most once inside \([0,1)\). Thus at most \(d\) distinct internal breakpoints partition the interval into at most \(d+1\) constant-bundle intervals. Their rational lengths give the distribution weights.

Cumulative sums, breakpoint sorting, floor computations, and interval-length calculations require polynomial time and produce rationals and integers of polynomial encoding length. For \(d=0\), use the empty vector with probability one. \(\square\)

In particular, if all coordinates correspond to goods valued by type \(t\), this distribution has expected potential exactly \(F_t(q)\).

4. Exact polynomial-time minimization

Aggregate variables and the exact relaxation

For every \(g\in G^+\) and type \(t\) valuing it, introduce a rational aggregate allocation variable \(z_{t,g}\geq0\). Require
\(\sum_{t:a_{t,g}=1}z_{t,g}=\rho_g\).

The average utility of type \(t\) is
\(q_t=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_{g:a_{t,g}=1}z_{t,g}\).

Consider minimizing
\(\sum_t\mu_tF_t(q_t)\)
over these aggregate allocations.

This is an exact relaxation of potential minimization. To prove the lower bound, fix a type \(t\), let \(k=\lfloor q_t\rfloor\), and observe that for every nonnegative integer \(h\),
\(f_t(h)-\bigl(kh-k(k+1)/2\bigr)/w_t =(h-k)(h-k-1)/(2w_t)\geq0\).

The last inequality holds because \(h-k\) is an integer: its product with the preceding integer is nonnegative. Multiplying this inequality by the configuration masses and summing within type \(t\) gives
\(\sum_b x_{t,b}f_t(v_t(b))\geq\mu_tF_t(q_t)\).

Conversely, any rational feasible aggregate allocation can attain this lower bound. Apply Lemma 3 within each type to the averages \(r_g=z_{t,g}/\mu_t\), using only kinds valued by that type. Multiply the distribution weights by \(\mu_t\).

The resulting configuration has the required type masses and kind supplies. Its utilities within each type are consecutive integers surrounding \(q_t\), so its potential equals \(\sum_t\mu_tF_t(q_t)\).

A linear program with an efficient separation oracle

Set
\(K_t=\lceil S/\mu_t\rceil\)
and
\(H_t=(K_t+1)^2/w_t\).

Every feasible aggregate allocation satisfies \(0\leq q_t\leq K_t\). On this interval,
\(F_t(q)=\max_{0\leq k\leq K_t,\;k\in\mathbb Z} \bigl(kq-k(k+1)/2\bigr)/w_t\).

To verify the maximum formula, the difference between the expressions indexed by \(k+1\) and \(k\) is \((q-k-1)/w_t\). Thus a maximizing index is \(\lfloor q\rfloor\), with a possible adjacent tie at integer \(q\).

Introduce epigraph variables \(y_t\). The linear program minimizes
\(\sum_t\mu_ty_t\)
subject to the aggregate allocation constraints, the defining equations for \(q_t\), and
\(0\leq q_t\leq K_t\), \(0\leq y_t\leq H_t\), together with
\(w_ty_t\geq kq_t-k(k+1)/2\)
for every integer \(k\in\{0,\ldots,K_t\}\).

The upper bounds \(H_t\) do not alter the optimum, because
\(F_t(q)\leq f_t(K_t)\leq H_t\)
throughout the allowed interval.

The linear program is nonempty: assign each kind in \(G^+\) entirely to any type valuing it, and set \(y_t=F_t(q_t)\). It is bounded, since supplies bound every \(z_{t,g}\), and explicit bounds constrain \(q_t,y_t\).

Although there may be exponentially many epigraph inequalities, separation is polynomial-time:

  • Check all explicit constraints.
  • For each type, compute \(k_t=\lfloor q_t\rfloor\).
  • Check the epigraph inequality indexed by \(k_t\).

If that inequality holds, all epigraph inequalities for that type hold, because its right-hand side is maximal.

We apply the standard strong separation–optimization theorem for rational polyhedra: a rational linear program can be optimized exactly in polynomial time given a polynomial-time strong separation oracle and a polynomial bound on inequality encoding lengths. The theorem also applies to lower-dimensional polyhedra. See [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, Chapter 6, Theorem 6.4.9](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-78240-4_7).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • There are at most \(\tau m+2\tau\) variables.
  • Every inequality has polynomial encoding length. In particular, \(K_t\) may be numerically large, but its binary encoding length is polynomial in the input length; the same holds for \(k(k+1)/2\) when \(k\leq K_t\).
  • The oracle uses only exact rational arithmetic and integer floor computations.
  • The feasible polyhedron is nonempty and bounded.

Therefore an exact rational optimum of polynomial encoding length can be computed in polynomial time. Since every \(\mu_t\) is positive, optimal epigraph values satisfy \(y_t=F_t(q_t)\).

Apply Lemma 3 to this optimum. The resulting rational nonwasteful configuration attains the global minimum of \(\Phi\). By Lemma 2, it satisfies WEFX.

5. Allocating globally worthless kinds

Let the constructed configuration on \(G^+\) be \(x\). Choose a supported entry \((s,c)\) minimizing normalized utility, and write
\(\lambda=v_s(c)/w_s=\min_{(t,b):x_{t,b}>0}v_t(b)/w_t\).
Let its mass be \(\gamma=x_{s,c}>0\).

Allocate every kind in \(G^0\) within this entry. Specifically, apply Lemma 3 to the vector of averages \((\rho_g/\gamma)_{g\in G^0}\), and replace the chosen entry by the resulting entries with bundles \(c+d\). Their masses sum to \(\gamma\), and their aggregate allocations of worthless kinds are exactly the required supplies.

All agents’ utilities remain unchanged. For any evaluating type \(t\),
\(v_t(c+d)=v_t(c)\leq v_s(c)\),
because all copies in \(c\) are valued by \(s\), valuations are binary, and every copy in \(d\) is globally worthless.

Hence, for every kind \(g\) present in a modified target bundle and every supported evaluating bundle \(b'\),
\(v_t(c+d-e_g)/w_s \leq v_s(c)/w_s =\lambda \leq v_t(b')/w_t\).

This proves WEFX for every modified target bundle, including removal of a copy valued zero by the evaluator. For unmodified target bundles, the original inequalities continue to hold because evaluating utilities are unchanged.

The completed configuration is nonwasteful, so Lemma 1 proves its identity-preserving Pareto optimality against arbitrary feasible measurable reassignment.

6. Output size and denominator independence

Write \(d=|G^+|\) and \(d_0=|G^0|\). The initial construction uses at most \(\tau(d+1)\) entries. Replacing one entry to allocate worthless kinds adds at most \(d_0\) entries. Thus the final support size is at most
\(\tau(d+1)+d_0\leq\tau(m+1)\).

All masses and bundle counts have polynomial encoding length. This follows from the polynomial encoding length of the exact linear-program optimum and the explicit rational operations in Lemma 3. The division by \(\gamma\) when allocating worthless kinds also preserves polynomial encoding length because \(\gamma\) is a positive rational of polynomial encoding length. Duplicate entries can be merged.

The construction never expands rational masses or supplies into grid agents or individual copies. Its bit complexity is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), independently of the numerical common denominator except through its contribution to \(L\).
