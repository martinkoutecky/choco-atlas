The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(T\) of demand-record types with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), chosen bundles \(x_t\), tariff costs \(c_{ts}\) for each supported bundle, and a kernel \(\kappa\), find values \(v_t\), feature-space slopes \(g_t\), multipliers \(\lambda_t\ge 1\), and slacks \(\epsilon_{ts},\bar\epsilon_{ts}\) minimizing \(\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{t,s\in T}\mu_t\mu_s(\epsilon_{ts}^2+\bar\epsilon_{ts}^2)+\frac12\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\|g_t\|_{\mathcal H_\kappa}^2\), subject to \(v_t-\langle g_t,\phi(x_t)\rangle+\epsilon_{ts}\ge v_s-\langle g_t,\phi(x_s)\rangle\) and \(\langle g_t,\phi(x_t)\rangle-\lambda_t c_{tt}+\bar\epsilon_{ts}\ge\langle g_t,\phi(x_s)\rangle-\lambda_t c_{ts}\) for every \(t,s\in T\); return the induced concave utility \(\hat u(x)=\min_{t\in T}\{v_t+\langle g_t,\phi(x)-\phi(x_t)\rangle\}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) demand-record types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type records a chosen bundle \(x_t\in\mathbb Q_+^m\), with \(m\ge1\), and costs \(c_{ts}\in\mathbb Q\) for every supported bundle. Costs are promised to be restrictions of strictly monotone tariffs \(p_t:\mathbb R_+^m\to\mathbb R\), with \(c_{ts}=p_t(x_s)\); the observed budget is \(c_{tt}\). Repeated chosen bundles are allowed.
Input also includes \(\rho\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) and a positive-semidefinite kernel \(\kappa\), with feature map \(\phi\) into a Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_\kappa\). Its rational support Gram matrix is supplied, together with an exact rational kernel evaluator running in polynomial time in its description and argument lengths. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Over \(v_t,\epsilon_{ts},\bar\epsilon_{ts}\in\mathbb R\), \(g_t\in\mathcal H_\kappa\), and \(\lambda_t\ge1\), minimize \(\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{t,s\in T}\mu_t\mu_s(\epsilon_{ts}^2+\bar\epsilon_{ts}^2)+\frac12\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\|g_t\|_{\mathcal H_\kappa}^2\).
For every \(t,s\in T\), require \(v_t-\langle g_t,\phi(x_t)\rangle+\epsilon_{ts}\ge v_s-\langle g_t,\phi(x_s)\rangle\) and \(\langle g_t,\phi(x_t)\rangle-\lambda_t c_{tt}+\bar\epsilon_{ts}\ge\langle g_t,\phi(x_s)\rangle-\lambda_t c_{ts}\).
Recover an exact optimum and its induced utility \(\hat u(x)=\min_{t\in T}\{v_t+\langle g_t,\phi(x)-\phi(x_t)\rangle\}\), represented by finite kernel expressions. Concavity is in feature space; global monotonicity is not an additional optimization constraint.

Conclusion

The problem is solvable exactly in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). This holds for every rational \(\rho>0\), including singular support Gram matrices and repeated chosen bundles.

The proof reduces the Hilbert-space problem to a rational convex quadratic program of polynomial encoding length, then applies the exact polynomial-time quadratic-programming theorem.

1. Reduction to the support span

Index the support by \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), and write \(K_{rs}=\kappa(x_r,x_s)=\langle\phi(x_r),\phi(x_s)\rangle\). By hypothesis, \(K\) is rational and positive semidefinite.

Let \(S=\operatorname{span}\{\phi(x_r):r\in T\}\). This finite-dimensional subspace of \(\mathcal H_\kappa\) is closed, so it has an orthogonal projection \(P_S\).

For any feasible solution, decompose \(g_t=P_Sg_t+h_t\), where \(h_t\perp S\). For every supported bundle \(x_s\),

\(\langle g_t,\phi(x_s)\rangle=\langle P_Sg_t,\phi(x_s)\rangle.\)

Thus replacing each \(g_t\) by \(P_Sg_t\) preserves every constraint. Orthogonality also gives

\(\|g_t\|_{\mathcal H_\kappa}^2=\|P_Sg_t\|_{\mathcal H_\kappa}^2+\|h_t\|_{\mathcal H_\kappa}^2.\)

Consequently, this replacement weakly decreases the objective. Because \(\mu_t>0\), it strictly decreases the objective whenever any \(h_t\ne0\).

It therefore suffices to optimize over \(g_t\in S\). Every such vector has a representation

\(g_t=\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}a_{tr}\phi(x_r)\)

with real coefficients \(a_{tr}\). No linear independence assumption is needed. Writing \(a_t=(a_{t1},\ldots,a_{t\tau})^{\mathsf T}\), we obtain

\(\langle g_t,\phi(x_s)\rangle=\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}a_{tr}K_{rs},\qquad \|g_t\|_{\mathcal H_\kappa}^2=a_t^{\mathsf T}Ka_t.\)

2. An equivalent rational convex quadratic program

Substitution produces the finite-dimensional objective

\(F(a,v,\lambda,\epsilon,\bar\epsilon)=\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{t,s=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\mu_s(\epsilon_{ts}^2+\bar\epsilon_{ts}^2)+\frac12\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t a_t^{\mathsf T}Ka_t.\)

For every \(t,s\in T\), its constraints are

\(v_t-v_s+\epsilon_{ts}-\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}a_{tr}(K_{rt}-K_{rs})\ge0,\)

\(\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}a_{tr}(K_{rt}-K_{rs})-\lambda_t(c_{tt}-c_{ts})+\bar\epsilon_{ts}\ge0,\)

together with \(\lambda_t\ge1\).

We may additionally impose \(v_1=0\): subtracting \(v_1\) from every \(v_t\) preserves all constraints and the objective.

There are \(3\tau^2+2\tau\) scalar variables and \(2\tau^2+\tau\) inequalities, plus this normalization equality.

All coefficients are rational. The objective Hessian is block diagonal:

  • each \(a_t\) block is \(\mu_tK\);
  • each slack coordinate has coefficient \(\rho\mu_t\mu_s\);
  • the \(v\) and \(\lambda\) blocks are zero.

Since \(K\) is positive semidefinite and all masses and \(\rho\) are positive, this Hessian is positive semidefinite. The resulting problem is therefore a rational convex quadratic program.

To verify equivalence, let \(I_{\mathcal H}\) and \(I_Q\) denote the respective infima. Every feasible coefficient vector defines a feasible Hilbert-space solution of identical objective value, so \(I_{\mathcal H}\le I_Q\). Conversely, projecting any feasible Hilbert-space solution onto \(S\), representing the projected vectors by coefficients, and normalizing \(v\) produces a feasible quadratic-program solution of no larger objective value. Hence \(I_Q\le I_{\mathcal H}\), and the infima coincide.

3. Feasibility, boundedness, and exact optimization

The quadratic program is feasible. An explicit rational feasible point is

\(a_{tr}=0,\quad v_t=0,\quad \lambda_t=1,\quad \epsilon_{ts}=0,\quad \bar\epsilon_{ts}=\max\{c_{tt}-c_{ts},0\}.\)

Indeed, every first inequality holds with equality, and every second inequality reduces to \(\bar\epsilon_{ts}\ge c_{tt}-c_{ts}\).

The objective is nonnegative, because its slack terms have positive coefficients and \(a_t^{\mathsf T}Ka_t\ge0\). Thus it is bounded below on its nonempty feasible polyhedron.

We now use the following established result:

Exact convex quadratic-programming theorem. Given rational data defining a quadratic objective with positive-semidefinite Hessian and finitely many linear constraints, feasibility and boundedness can be decided in polynomial time. If feasible and bounded below, an exact rational minimizer and the exact minimum value can be computed in polynomial time in the binary encoding length. This is the theorem of [Kozlov–Tarasov–Khachiyan (1980)](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189); their definition of exact solution includes an attaining point.

Their integer-data formulation applies to rational data by clearing denominators. Multiplying the objective by a positive common denominator preserves its minimizers and positive semidefiniteness; clearing each constraint’s denominators by a positive multiplier preserves its feasible set. These operations increase encoding length only polynomially.

All hypotheses have now been checked: our program has rational data, a positive-semidefinite Hessian, a nonempty feasible set, and an objective bounded below. The theorem therefore supplies an exact rational optimum

\((a^*,v^*,\lambda^*,\epsilon^*,\bar\epsilon^*).\)

Defining \(g_t^*=\sum_r a_{tr}^*\phi(x_r)\) gives an exact optimum of the original problem, by the equivalence proved above. In particular, possible unbounded directions in the coefficient, utility-level, or multiplier variables do not obstruct attainment or exact recovery.

4. Polynomial encoding length

Every coefficient of the quadratic program is obtained from input rationals using a fixed number of additions and multiplications, such as \(\rho\mu_t\mu_s\), \(\mu_tK_{rs}\), \(K_{rt}-K_{rs}\), and \(c_{tt}-c_{ts}\). Each therefore has binary length polynomial in \(L\).

The numbers of variables, constraints, and matrix entries are polynomial in \(\tau\). Consequently, the entire quadratic program has encoding length polynomial in \(\tau,L\), and can be constructed in polynomial time. Exact quadratic programming then takes polynomial time in that length.

This argument never expands a mass into multiple observations and never enumerates unsupported types. Small binary-encoded masses affect coefficient bit lengths, rather than the number of variables. Likewise, singularity of \(K\) requires neither an inverse nor a square-root factorization.

Together with the input bundle descriptions, this establishes the requested polynomial bound in \(m,\tau,L\).

5. Exact recovery of the utility

The recovered vectors are represented by the finite expressions

\(g_t^*=\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}a_{tr}^*\phi(x_r).\)

Their induced utility is exactly

\(\hat u(x)=\min_{1\le t\le\tau}\left\{v_t^*+\sum_{r=1}^{\tau}a_{tr}^*\bigl(\kappa(x_r,x)-K_{rt}\bigr)\right\}.\)

All stored coefficients are rational and have polynomial total output length, because the exact optimization algorithm runs in polynomial time.

For a rational query bundle \(x\), compute the \(\tau\) kernel values \(\kappa(x_r,x)\), reuse them across the \(\tau\) expressions, and take their minimum. The promised exact polynomial-time evaluator and polynomially many rational arithmetic operations give exact evaluation in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and the query’s binary length.

Finally, define on feature space

\(U(y)=\min_t\{v_t^*+\langle g_t^*,y-\phi(x_t)\rangle\}.\)

Writing each expression inside the minimum as \(A_t(y)\), its affinity implies, for \(y,z\in\mathcal H_\kappa\) and \(\theta\in[0,1]\),

\(U(\theta y+(1-\theta)z)=\min_t\{\theta A_t(y)+(1-\theta)A_t(z)\}\ge\theta\min_t A_t(y)+(1-\theta)\min_t A_t(z).\)

Thus \(U\) is concave in feature space and \(\hat u=U\circ\phi\), as required.

The tariff promise adds no optimization constraints beyond the supplied costs. The argument also requires no large-regularization assumption: every rational \(\rho>0\) is covered.
