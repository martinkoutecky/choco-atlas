The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), personalized \(2\)-value data, and complete valuation patterns on a finite-horizon scenario tree, goods arrive sequentially in per-capita blocks and a policy sees only revealed history plus the next block. Assign each individual an integral bundle through mass transports \(y_{\theta,B,q}\) from type/bundle states, irrevocably and nonanticipatorily, while enforcing pairwise \(\rho\)-\(\mathrm{EF2}\) at every prefix and \(\rho\)-\(\mathrm{EF1}\) at block boundaries. Compute the maximum achievable \(\rho\), in particular deciding whether \(\rho=1\) is achievable for every allowed arrival path.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is nonatomic and given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Each type has additive valuations with binary-rational \(\alpha_\theta\geq\beta_\theta\geq0\), \(\alpha_\theta>0\), and every good's value belongs to \(\{\alpha_\theta,\beta_\theta\}\).
The input explicitly lists a finite scenario tree with \(S\) nodes and horizon \(H\), including all valuation patterns. Each node specifies an ordered block of distinct good labels \(q\), with positive binary-rational supplies \(s_q\) summing to \(1\); labels do not recur along a path. Let \(m\) count labels in the input and \(L\) be its total bit length.
A supply is a mass of indivisible copies: an individual may receive at most one copy of each label, and holds an integral bundle \(B\) of labels. Initially \(x_{\theta,\varnothing}=\mu_\theta\). On arrival of \(q\), choose real transports \(0\leq y_{\theta,B,q}\leq x_{\theta,B}\) with \(\sum_{\theta,B}y_{\theta,B,q}=s_q\), moving that mass from \((\theta,B)\) to \((\theta,B\cup\{q\})\); goods cannot be discarded or reassigned.
The current block's complete ordered supplies and valuations are revealed before its first arrival. A deterministic policy may use the known scenario set, revealed history, current allocation, and current block, but must make identical decisions on identical observations; future branch choices are hidden.
At every prefix after a supply arrival, every ordered pair of positive-mass states \((\theta,B),(\theta',C)\) must satisfy \(v_\theta(B)\geq\rho\min_{D\subseteq C,\,|D|\leq2}v_\theta(C\setminus D)\). At each block boundary require the same inequality with \(|D|\leq1\). These requirements hold on every root-to-leaf path; no restriction limits the number of different goods an individual receives in a block.

For an input binary-rational \(\rho\in[0,1]\), is existence of such a policy in \(\mathrm P\), or is it NP-hard, including the case \(\rho=1\)? What is the complexity of computing the maximum achievable \(\rho\)? State running times in \(m,\tau,L,S,H\).

For finite societies of \(n\) agents, a polynomial-time algorithm with foresight of \(n-1\) goods guarantees EF2 after every arrival and EF1 every \(n\) arrivals. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22174v1)
No result settling this precise mass-transport problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total population mass to one and each complete block's total supply to one, mirroring one good per agent per discrete round.
  • Represent sequential arrivals within a block by ordered positive rational supply packets; prefixes are evaluated after each packet, and the horizon consists of complete blocks.
  • Interpret copies as mass-replicated distinct good labels, with at most one copy of each label per individual and no repeated label along a path; allow multiple distinct goods per individual per block.
  • Use an explicitly encoded scenario tree known for policy synthesis, while hiding the realized future branch; require identical decisions for identical observation histories.
  • Reveal the entire current block at its start, matching the blockwise foresight used by the discrete algorithm.
  • Require complete immediate allocation of each supply, conserve type masses through real nonnegative transports, and start with empty bundles.
  • Interpret pairwise fairness as applying to every ordered pair of positive-mass type/bundle states, including states of the same type.
  • Allow nonnegative rational low values, equal high and low values, and zero low values; exclude identically zero valuation types as in the discrete setup.
  • Restrict the optimization factor to the unit interval and include zero as the fallback value.

Result

Every valid input admits a deterministic policy with \(\rho=1\). Consequently, the existence problem is in \(\mathrm P\), and the maximum achievable value is always \(\rho_{\max}=1\).

The essential point is that each block has total supply exactly \(1\). After a rational expansion, it becomes a batch containing exactly as many copies as virtual agents. The finite-agent algorithm supplied in the question allocates such a batch by a perfect matching.

1. The finite-agent result used

We use the following properties of Priority-Matching, Algorithm 3 in the cited paper. For \(N\) agents with personalized two-value additive valuations, starting from empty bundles, it:

  • assigns exactly one good to each agent in every complete batch of \(N\) goods;
  • maintains EF2 after every individual arrival and EF1 after each complete batch;
  • chooses the batch’s matching using only the current allocation and that batch’s goods and valuations.

The first and third properties follow from Algorithm 3, lines 6–13; the fairness guarantees are Theorem 5.4. Fix deterministic tie-breaking for every topological sort and maximum-weight matching. The hypotheses permit both \(\beta_i=0\) and \(\alpha_i=\beta_i>0\). [Algorithm 3 and Theorem 5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22174v1#S5.SS2)

The third property matters: although the theorem is phrased using rolling foresight, its algorithm only consults the preview when a batch begins. Thus revealing the whole current batch suffices.

2. Exact rational expansion

Choose a positive integer \(N\) divisible by the denominators of every type mass and every supply occurring anywhere in the scenario tree. For example, their product suffices. Consequently,

\(N\mu_\theta\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\quad\text{and}\quad Ns_q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.\)

For every supported type \(\theta\), create \(N\mu_\theta\) virtual agents of that type. Since the type masses sum to \(1\), there are exactly \(N\) virtual agents. Each represents a population cell of mass \(1/N\).

When a block is revealed, replace its arrival of label \(q\) by \(Ns_q\) distinct virtual copies, each having the value of \(q\) for every type. Order these copies consecutively, respecting the block’s label order. Because the block’s supplies sum to \(1\), the expanded block contains exactly

\(\sum_{q\text{ in the block}}Ns_q=N\)

virtual goods.

Run Priority-Matching on these virtual agents, treating each expanded block as one complete batch. All its valuation hypotheses hold: a virtual agent of type \(\theta\) values every copy in \(\{\alpha_\theta,\beta_\theta\}\).

This is an exact finite construction, with no limiting or approximation argument.

3. The construction respects integral bundles of labels

Priority-Matching gives each virtual agent exactly one copy per expanded block.

Within a block, a virtual agent therefore cannot receive two copies of the same label. Across blocks on a path, the input promises that labels never recur. Hence no virtual agent ever receives two copies with the same label.

Let \(A_i\) denote virtual agent \(i\)’s bundle of copies, and let \(B_i\) be its bundle after replacing each copy by its label. The replacement map is injective on \(A_i\). In particular, if \(i\) has type \(\theta\), then

\(v_i(A_i)=v_\theta(B_i).\)

More generally, deleting any subset \(E\subseteq A_i\) corresponds to deleting a subset \(D\subseteq B_i\) with \(|D|=|E|\), and preserves the resulting valuation.

Give the population cell represented by \(i\) the integral label bundle \(B_i\). Choosing one label per individual per block is a property of this construction; it imposes no additional restriction on the problem’s feasible policies.

4. The virtual allocation defines legal transports

Immediately before an actual arrival \(q\), define

\(x_{\theta,B}=\frac{1}{N}\bigl|\{i:\operatorname{type}(i)=\theta,\ B_i=B\}\bigr|.\)

The matching for the current block has already been selected. Let \(R_q\) be the virtual agents matched to the copies of \(q\), and set

\(y_{\theta,B,q}=\frac{1}{N}\bigl|\{i\in R_q:\operatorname{type}(i)=\theta,\ B_i=B\}\bigr|.\)

These transports satisfy

\(0\leq y_{\theta,B,q}\leq x_{\theta,B}\)

because their counted agents form a subset of the agents in the corresponding state. Moreover,

\(\sum_{\theta,B}y_{\theta,B,q}=\frac{|R_q|}{N}=\frac{Ns_q}{N}=s_q.\)

Every recipient lacks label \(q\), by the preceding section. Moving these cells from \(B\) to \(B\cup\{q\}\) therefore performs exactly the permitted transport.

The resulting aggregate allocation is precisely the virtual allocation after all copies of \(q\) have arrived. No supply is discarded, and no previous allocation is reassigned.

5. Fairness survives aggregation

Consider any actual prefix immediately after a supply arrival, and any two positive-mass states \((\theta,B)\) and \((\theta',C)\). Positivity ensures that there are virtual representatives \(i,j\) with these respective types and bundles.

At the corresponding virtual prefix, EF2 gives a subset \(E\subseteq A_j\), with \(|E|\leq2\), such that

\(v_i(A_i)\geq v_i(A_j\setminus E).\)

Let \(D\subseteq C\) be the labels corresponding to \(E\). The injectivity established above gives \(|D|=|E|\), and the valuation identity yields

\(v_\theta(B)\geq v_\theta(C\setminus D)\geq\min_{\substack{D'\subseteq C\\|D'|\leq2}}v_\theta(C\setminus D').\)

Thus the actual allocation satisfies the required inequality with \(\rho=1\).

At a block boundary, the virtual prefix is a complete-batch boundary. Applying EF1 instead gives the identical argument with \(|D|\leq1\). If both states coincide, the inequality also follows directly from nonnegative valuations.

This proves both fairness requirements on every path.

6. Hidden branches and identical observations

Assign fixed identifiers to virtual agents initially, and use fixed identifiers for the copies generated from each revealed block. Fix all algorithmic tie-breaking.

At the beginning of a block, compute its matching from the virtual allocation reconstructed from the revealed history and the newly revealed block. Subsequently execute the corresponding transports as its labels arrive.

Inductively, identical revealed histories produce identical virtual allocations: they start from the same virtual population, and each next matching is a deterministic function of the previous virtual allocation and the revealed block. The resulting transports are therefore identical on identical observations.

The construction never uses the realized identity of a hidden branch. Its choice of the common denominator uses the known scenario set, which the problem explicitly allows.

For any particular root-to-leaf path, its execution is exactly Priority-Matching on that path’s expanded sequence. The finite-agent guarantee consequently applies on every path, proving the existence of one admissible deterministic policy for the entire tree.

7. Complexity

For every promised valid input and every permitted \(\rho\in[0,1]\), the policy above works: nonnegative residual valuations make the inequalities for \(\rho=1\) imply those for smaller \(\rho\).

Therefore:

  • Existence: return YES.
  • Maximum achievable value: return \(1\).

Both computations take \(O(1)\) time once the promised input is available, or \(O(L)\) including reading it. These bounds have no further dependence on \(m,\tau,S,H\).

For completeness, explicitly constructing a policy by the expansion has a different bound. The product-denominator choice satisfies \(\log_2N=O(L)\), so \(N=2^{O(L)}\). Each node requires one matching on \(N\) virtual agents and \(N\) virtual goods. Exact matching weights have polynomial bit length in \(N\). Thus direct simulation throughout the explicit tree takes

\(S\cdot\operatorname{poly}(N,m,\tau,H,L)=2^{O(L)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,S,H,L)\)

bit operations.

This exponential construction bound does not affect the requested decision and optimum-value computations, which are polynomial-time.
