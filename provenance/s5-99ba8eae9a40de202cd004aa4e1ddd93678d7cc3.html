The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_\theta\), per-capita task-category supplies \(q_j\), rewards \(r_j\), success probabilities \(p_{\theta j}\), and costs \(c_{\theta j}\), choose task shares \(\alpha_j\), masses \(\lambda_{\theta B}\) assigned to integral bundles \(B\), and subsidies \(s_{\theta B}\) for occupied type-bundle states. Subject to \(\sum_B\lambda_{\theta B}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,B:j\in B}\lambda_{\theta B}=q_j\), effort feasibility, and envy-freeness between every pair of occupied states, determine the maximum revenue per unit population, or decide whether it is at least a given rational threshold \(R\). What is the computational complexity as a function of the number of categories \(K\), type-support size \(\tau\), and input bit length \(L\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m=K\geq1\) task categories. Inputs are binary rationals \(q_j\in(0,1]\), \(r_j,p_{\theta j}\in[0,1]\), \(c_{\theta j}\geq0\), and a threshold \(R\); \(L\) is their total encoding length, including the society. Assume each category has some supported type with \(p_{\theta j}r_j-c_{\theta j}\geq0\).
An integral bundle is any \(B\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}\), including the empty bundle: a person receives at most one task of each category. Choose real shares \(\alpha_j\in[0,1]\), assignment masses \(\lambda_{\theta B}\geq0\), and nonnegative per-person subsidies \(s_{\theta B}\) for occupied states, meaning \(\lambda_{\theta B}>0\).
Require \(\sum_B\lambda_{\theta B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B:j\in B}\lambda_{\theta B}=q_j\): all supplied tasks must be allocated.
Write \(u_{\theta j}=\alpha_jp_{\theta j}r_j-c_{\theta j}\). An occupied state must satisfy \(u_{\theta j}\geq0\) for every \(j\in B\); effort is chosen at indifference. Shirking gives zero success probability and zero cost, and subsidies are unconditional.
For every ordered pair of occupied states \((\theta,B),(\eta,C)\), including states of the same type, require \(\sum_{j\in B}u_{\theta j}+s_{\theta B}\geq\sum_{j\in C}\max\{u_{\theta j},0\}+s_{\eta C}\). Thus an agent evaluating another bundle may shirk on any of its tasks.
Revenue per unit population is \(\operatorname{Rev}=\sum_{\theta,B:\lambda_{\theta B}>0}\lambda_{\theta B}\bigl[\sum_{j\in B}(1-\alpha_j)p_{\theta j}r_j-s_{\theta B}\bigr]\). The decision problem asks whether a feasible choice has \(\operatorname{Rev}\geq R\); infeasible instances are no-instances.
Is this continuous revenue-threshold problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m=K\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
For finite agents and individually listed indivisible tasks, the corresponding revenue-threshold problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, while optimal revenue is computable in polynomial time when the number of tasks is constant. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25431v2). No result settling this precise continuous formulation was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one so supplies and revenue are per unit population.
  • Interpreted integral bundles as subsets of categories, allowing empty bundles and at most one task per category per person; consequently restricted positive category supplies to at most one.
  • Specified binary-rational input domains: probabilities and rewards lie in [0,1], costs are nonnegative, and the threshold may be any rational.
  • Carried over the category-wise usefulness assumption without assuming it guarantees feasibility under prescribed mass supplies; infeasible instances are no-instances.
  • Specified real decision variables, category-wide shares in [0,1], and unconditional nonnegative per-person subsidies attached to occupied type-bundle states.
  • Restored task-wise weak effort feasibility, effort at indifference, and the option to shirk when evaluating an envied bundle.
  • Applied envy-freeness to all occupied states, including distinct bundles occupied by the same type, and weighted both revenue and subsidies by assignment mass.
  • Selected the revenue-threshold decision formulation and identified the category count K with m for reporting running times.

Result

The continuous revenue-threshold problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with support size \(\tau=2\), equal type masses, and dyadic input data. Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(K,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The construction adapts the special-task gadget in [Appendix A.3 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25431v2#A3). The proof below handles arbitrary real assignment masses, including splitting the special-task supply between types.

1. Source problem and construction

We reduce from PARTITION: given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) with \(\sum_{i=1}^n a_i=2B\), decide whether some subset has sum \(B\). This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; see [Karp’s original paper](https://www2.seas.gwu.edu/~simhaweb/champalg/tsp/papers/KarpOriginal.pdf). Restricting the total to be even preserves hardness: multiplying every integer of an arbitrary instance by two gives an equivalent instance with even total.

Let \(D\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(D\geq16B\), and put \(h=B/D\). Thus \(0<h\leq1/16\).

Construct two supported types, denoted \(E\) and \(N\), with masses \(\mu_E=\mu_N=1/2\). There are \(K=n+1\) categories: item categories \(1,\ldots,n\), and a special category \(g=n+1\). All rewards are \(r_j=1\). The remaining parameters are:

| Category | Supply | \(p_{Ej}\) | \(c_{Ej}\) | \(p_{Nj}\) | \(c_{Nj}\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Item \(i\) | \(1/4\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(a_i/D\) | \(0\) |
| Special \(g\) | \(1/2\) | \(1\) | \(1/2\) | \(2h\) | \(0\) |

Set the revenue threshold to \(R=1/4\).

Every probability lies in \([0,1]\): indeed, \(a_i\leq2B\) gives \(a_i/D\leq2h\leq1/8\). Every category satisfies the required effort-feasibility assumption.

All constructed numbers are dyadic. Moreover, \(D<32B\), so their encoding lengths are polynomial in the source encoding length. The construction is therefore a polynomial-time reduction.

2. A partition gives a threshold-achieving solution

Suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=B\). Its complement also has sum \(B\).

Use the three occupied states

\( \lambda_{E,\{g\}}=1/2,\qquad \lambda_{N,I}=1/4,\qquad \lambda_{N,I^c}=1/4. \)

Set \(\alpha_g=1/2\), set \(\alpha_i=1\) for every item category, and give every occupied state subsidy zero.

Each item category is assigned with mass \(1/4\), and the special category with mass \(1/2\). Effort constraints hold.

Type \(E\) obtains utility zero from its own state and evaluates both item bundles at zero. Type \(N\) obtains utility \(B/D=h\) from either assigned bundle and evaluates \(\{g\}\) at \((1/2)(2h)=h\). Thus every ordered envy constraint holds, including those between states of type \(N\).

Item categories contribute zero revenue. The special category contributes \((1/2)(1-1/2)=1/4\). Hence the constructed instance is a yes-instance.

3. A revenue bound for arbitrary continuous assignments

Consider any feasible solution of the constructed instance. Write \(\alpha=\alpha_g\), and let

\( x=\sum_{C:g\in C}\lambda_{EC}. \)

Because the special-category supply is \(1/2\), its assignment mass to type \(N\) is \(1/2-x\), with \(0\leq x\leq1/2\).

For each item category, let \(z_i=\sum_{C:i\in C}\lambda_{NC}\). Define its total gross success value by

\( W=\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{a_i}{D}z_i. \)

Supply constraints give \(z_i\leq1/4\), and therefore

\( W\leq\frac14\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{a_i}{D}=\frac h2. \)

Let \(A\) be the total task utility received by type \(N\), and let \(S_E,S_N\) be the total subsidies paid to the respective types:

\( A=\sum_{C:\lambda_{NC}>0}\lambda_{NC}\sum_{j\in C}\alpha_jp_{Nj},\qquad S_\theta=\sum_{C:\lambda_{\theta C}>0}\lambda_{\theta C}s_{\theta C}. \)

All costs of type \(N\) are zero, so \(A\geq0\). Directly separating special-category revenue from type \(E\), and gross value minus payments from type \(N\), gives the exact identity

\( \operatorname{Rev}=x(1-\alpha)+W+2h(1/2-x)-A-S_N-S_E. \qquad (1) \)

Case \(x=0\)

Equation \((1)\), nonnegative payments and subsidies, and \(W\leq h/2\) imply

\( \operatorname{Rev}\leq W+h\leq3h/2\leq3/32<1/4. \)

Thus any threshold-achieving solution must have \(x>0\).

Case \(x>0\)

Choose an occupied type-\(E\) state whose bundle contains \(g\), and denote its subsidy by \(\sigma\geq0\). Its effort constraint implies \(\alpha\geq1/2\).

Type \(N\) evaluates that state at least at \(2h\alpha+\sigma\): the special category contributes \(2h\alpha\), and every item category contributes a nonnegative amount. Therefore every occupied type-\(N\) state satisfies

\( \sum_{j\in C}\alpha_jp_{Nj}+s_{NC}\geq2h\alpha+\sigma. \)

Multiplying by the corresponding masses and summing, using \(\mu_N=1/2\), yields

\( A+S_N\geq h\alpha+\sigma/2. \qquad (2) \)

Substituting \((2)\) into \((1)\), and rearranging, gives

\( \operatorname{Rev}\leq\frac14-(1/2-x)(1/2-2h)-(x+h)(\alpha-1/2)-(h/2-W)-\sigma/2-S_E. \qquad (3) \)

For verification, the terms involving \(x,\alpha,h\) satisfy

\( x(1-\alpha)+3h/2-2hx-h\alpha=\frac14-(1/2-x)(1/2-2h)-(x+h)(\alpha-1/2). \)

Every term subtracted on the right of \((3)\) is nonnegative. In particular, \(1/2-2h>0\) and \(x+h>0\).

4. Reaching the threshold forces a partition

Suppose now that \(\operatorname{Rev}\geq1/4\). By \((3)\), every subtracted term must vanish. Consequently,

\( x=1/2,\qquad \alpha_g=1/2,\qquad W=h/2,\qquad S_E=0. \)

We derive the consequences explicitly.

All item supply goes to type \(N\). Since

\( \frac h2-W=\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{a_i}{D}(1/4-z_i)=0, \)

and each coefficient \(a_i/D\) is positive, every \(z_i=1/4\). Hence type \(E\) receives no item category in any occupied state.

The only occupied type-\(E\) state is \(\{g\}\), with subsidy zero. The equality \(x=\mu_E=1/2\) means every occupied type-\(E\) state contains \(g\). The preceding conclusion excludes all item categories. Finally, \(S_E=0\), together with positive occupied-state masses and nonnegative subsidies, forces its subsidy to be zero.

Every occupied type-\(N\) state has subsidy zero. Type \(E\)’s utility from \(\{g\}\) is \(\alpha_g-1/2=0\). It evaluates every type-\(N\) bundle at zero, because those bundles contain only item categories. Its envy constraint against such a state therefore gives \(0\geq s_{NC}\). Nonnegativity forces \(s_{NC}=0\).

Every occupied type-\(N\) bundle has task utility exactly \(h\). Its envy constraint against \(\{g\}\) gives

\( \sum_{i\in C}\alpha_i\frac{a_i}{D}\geq h. \)

Summing over type-\(N\) masses yields \(A\geq h/2\). Conversely,

\( A=\frac14\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i\frac{a_i}{D}\leq\frac14\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{a_i}{D}=\frac h2. \)

Thus \(A=h/2\). Every occupied bundle must attain its lower bound \(h\): otherwise its positive mass would make the aggregate strictly larger than \(h/2\).

Every item share equals one. The equality in the preceding upper bound gives

\( \frac14\sum_{i=1}^n(1-\alpha_i)\frac{a_i}{D}=0. \)

Each summand is nonnegative and \(a_i/D>0\), so \(\alpha_i=1\) for every item category.

It follows that every occupied type-\(N\) bundle \(C\) satisfies

\( \sum_{i\in C}\frac{a_i}{D}=h=\frac BD, \)

and hence \(\sum_{i\in C}a_i=B\). At least one such bundle exists because \(\mu_N=1/2>0\). That bundle, together with its complement, is a valid partition.

5. Conclusion

The source instance admits a partition if and only if the constructed continuous instance admits revenue at least \(R=1/4\). The reverse implication allows arbitrary real shares, masses, and subsidies; it makes no integrality assumption on population assignments.

This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness for the precise formulation in the question, already with \(\tau=2\).
