The continuous mirror question, as graded

For α∈[0,1], let \(k_N=\lfloor α N\rfloor\). Draw \(N\) binary opinions independently and uniformly, choose a hidden coalition of \(k_N\) advisors uniformly, and let non-colluders report truthfully. Over all joint stochastic reporting kernels \(Q_N(r_{K_N}\mid o_{K_N})\) for the coalition, determine whether \(V(α)=\lim_{N→\infty}N^{-1}\min_{Q_N} I(O^N;R^N)\) exists, its value, and an asymptotically optimal family of kernels.

A society is represented by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, with masses given as binary rationals summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Here the opinion alphabet has size \(m=2\), and the support is \(\{0,1\}\), each with mass \(1/2\). State any running times in \(N,m,\tau,L\) and any precision parameter, where \(L\) is the input bit length.
Fix \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). For each positive integer \(N\), put \(k_N=\lfloor\alpha N\rfloor\), draw \(O_1,\ldots,O_N\) independently from this distribution, and independently choose \(K_N\) uniformly among the \(k_N\)-element subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\).
An admissible attack is an arbitrary stochastic kernel \(Q_N\) from \(\{0,1\}^{k_N}\) to \(\{0,1\}^{k_N}\), used identically for every coalition after ordering its members by index. It may jointly randomize all coalition reports using only their opinions; reporting truthfully is permitted. Set \(R_i=O_i\) outside \(K_N\). For \(k_N=0\), use the unique empty-vector kernel.
The observer receives the full indexed vector \(R^N\), knows \(k_N\) and the kernel, and does not observe \(K_N\). Mutual information uses base-two logarithms and the joint law with \(K_N\) marginalized out. Write \(a_N(\alpha)=\min_{Q_N}I(O^N;R^N)\). A kernel family is asymptotically optimal when \(\bigl(I_{Q_N}(O^N;R^N)-a_N(\alpha)\bigr)/N\to0\).

1. Result

Define the binary entropy function by
\(h_2(p)=-p\log_2 p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)\), with \(h_2(0)=h_2(1)=0\).

The limit exists for every \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), and
\[ V(\alpha)=1-h_2\!\left(\min\{\alpha,1/2\}\right) = \begin{cases} 1-h_2(\alpha),&0\leq\alpha\leq1/2,\\ 0,&1/2\leq\alpha\leq1. \end{cases} \]

In fact, the exact finite minimum is
\[ a_N(\alpha)=N-\log_2\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k_N}\binom Nj\right). \]

Here is a characterization of all asymptotically optimal admissible kernel families. For a given family, let \(E^N=O^N\oplus R^N\) be its error vector, and let
\(T_N=|\{i\in K_N:R_i=O_i\}|\) count coalition members reporting truthfully.

  • If \(\alpha=0\), the unique admissible family is optimal.
  • If \(0<\alpha\leq1/2\), the family is asymptotically optimal if and only if \(\mathbb E T_N=o(N)\).
  • If \(1/2<\alpha\leq1\), the family is asymptotically optimal if and only if

\(N-H(E^N)=o(N)\) and \(I(E^N;R^N)=o(N)\).

All entropies below use base-two logarithms, and coalition membership is marginalized out.

2. Exact finite minimum and an attaining admissible kernel

Fix \(N\) and \(k\in\{0,\ldots,N\}\), and write
\(B_{N,k}=\sum_{d=0}^k\binom Nd\).

Lower bound

Every admissible attack satisfies \(|E^N|\leq k\) almost surely, because reports outside the coalition are truthful.

For each report vector \(r\) of positive probability, the conditional support of \(O^N\) therefore lies in the Hamming ball
\(\{o:|o\oplus r|\leq k\}\), which has exactly \(B_{N,k}\) elements. The entropy of a distribution supported on \(B_{N,k}\) points is at most \(\log_2 B_{N,k}\). Consequently,
\[ H(O^N\mid R^N)\leq\log_2 B_{N,k}. \]
Since the opinions are independent fair bits, \(H(O^N)=N\), giving
\[ I(O^N;R^N)\geq N-\log_2 B_{N,k}. \]

Construction and normalization

Use the following kernel, identically for every ordered coalition:

  • Independently of its opinions, draw \(D\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\) with

\(\Pr(D=d)=\binom Nd/B_{N,k}\).

  • Choose uniformly a \(d\)-element subset of the \(k\) coalition positions.
  • Flip exactly those opinions and report all remaining opinions truthfully.

These instructions use only coalition opinions and internal randomness. The probabilities for \(D\) sum to one, so this defines an admissible stochastic kernel. Explicitly, for coalition vectors \(u,v\) at Hamming distance \(d\),
\[ Q_{N,k}(v\mid u)= \frac{\binom Nd}{B_{N,k}\binom kd}. \]

To verify its induced global error law, fix a subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\) of size \(d\leq k\). Exactly \(\binom{N-d}{k-d}\) possible coalitions contain \(S\). Thus
\[ \Pr(E^N=\mathbf 1_S) = \frac{\binom Nd}{B_{N,k}}\, \frac{\binom{N-d}{k-d}}{\binom Nk}\, \frac1{\binom kd} = \frac1{B_{N,k}}, \]
where the final equality uses
\(\binom Nk\binom kd=\binom Nd\binom{N-d}{k-d}\).

Hence \(E^N\) is uniform over all binary vectors of weight at most \(k\). It is also independent of \(O^N\), since coalition selection and all flip choices are independent of the opinions.

Because \(O^N\) is uniform and \(R^N=O^N\oplus E^N\), for every \(e,r\),
\[ \Pr(E^N=e,R^N=r)=\Pr(E^N=e)\,2^{-N}. \]
Therefore \(E^N\) and \(R^N\) are independent. Given \(R^N\), the correspondence between \(O^N\) and \(E^N\) is bijective, so
\[ H(O^N\mid R^N)=H(E^N\mid R^N)=\log_2 B_{N,k}. \]
The lower bound is attained, proving the asserted finite minimum. This includes \(k=0\) and \(k=N\).

Relation to the cited claim

This kernel agrees with equation (3) of [Wang et al., Theorem 5](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/023.pdf). Its normalization and attainment have been checked directly above. The displayed lower endpoint \(2^k/B_{N,k}\) in that theorem is inconsistent with the entropy calculation; the correct endpoint is \(N-\log_2 B_{N,k}\). For example, at \(N=4,k=2\), the minimum is \(4-\log_2 11\), rather than \(4/11\).

3. The normalized limit and a simpler optimal family

Put \(d=\min\{k,\lfloor N/2\rfloor\}\). The largest binomial coefficient among indices \(0,\ldots,k\) is \(\binom Nd\). Hence
\[ \binom Nd\leq B_{N,k}\leq(N+1)\binom Nd. \]

We also have the elementary bounds
\[ \frac{2^{Nh_2(d/N)}}{N+1}\leq\binom Nd\leq2^{Nh_2(d/N)}. \]
For completeness, when \(0<d<N\), set \(p=d/N\). The binomial probability
\(\binom Nd p^d(1-p)^{N-d}\) is a mode of the distribution with parameters \(N,p\): its successive probability ratios decrease, and the ratios immediately before and after \(d\) are respectively
\((N-d+1)/(N-d)>1\) and \(d/(d+1)<1\).
A mode has probability at least \(1/(N+1)\), and every probability is at most one. Substituting
\(p^d(1-p)^{N-d}=2^{-Nh_2(p)}\) proves the bounds. The endpoint cases follow directly.

Combining these inequalities gives
\[ \left| \frac{N-\log_2 B_{N,k}}N- \left(1-h_2(d/N)\right) \right| \leq\frac{\log_2(N+1)}N. \]
For \(k=k_N\), we have \(d/N\to\min\{\alpha,1/2\}\). Continuity of \(h_2\) proves the claimed limit.

A simpler asymptotically optimal kernel is:

Choose uniformly exactly \(d_N=\min\{k_N,\lfloor N/2\rfloor\}\) coalition positions and flip their opinions.

The same counting argument shows that its global error vector is uniform over all vectors of weight exactly \(d_N\), independently of the opinions. Its leakage is therefore
\[ I(O^N;R^N)=N-\log_2\binom N{d_N}. \]
Its excess over the exact minimum satisfies
\[ 0\leq I(O^N;R^N)-a_N(\alpha) =\log_2\frac{B_{N,k_N}}{\binom N{d_N}} \leq\log_2(N+1). \]
Thus this family is asymptotically optimal for every \(\alpha\). For \(\alpha\leq1/2\), it simply flips every coalition opinion.

4. Characterization of all asymptotically optimal families

The following arguments apply to arbitrary admissible kernels, including kernels whose randomness depends jointly on the coalition opinions.

A general entropy identity

For fixed \(R^N\), the transformation \(O^N\mapsto E^N=O^N\oplus R^N\) is bijective. Consequently,
\[ I(O^N;R^N) =N-H(E^N\mid R^N) =N-H(E^N)+I(E^N;R^N). \]
Both terms on the right are nonnegative, because \(E^N\) is a binary vector of length \(N\).

The regime \(0<\alpha\leq1/2\)

Let \(q_N=\mathbb E|E^N|/N\). Entropy subadditivity and concavity of \(h_2\) give
\[ H(E^N)\leq\sum_{i=1}^N h_2(\Pr(E_i=1)) \leq Nh_2(q_N). \]
Thus
\[ \frac{I(O^N;R^N)}N\geq1-h_2(q_N). \]
Since errors occur only inside the coalition,
\[ q_N=\frac{k_N-\mathbb E T_N}{N}\leq\frac{k_N}N\leq\alpha. \]

If the family is asymptotically optimal, its normalized leakage converges to \(1-h_2(\alpha)\). The preceding inequality, together with the strict increase of \(h_2\) on \([0,1/2]\), forces \(q_N\to\alpha\). Since \(k_N/N\to\alpha\), this implies \(\mathbb E T_N/N\to0\). This proves necessity.

For sufficiency, couple the given reports with the reports \(R^{*,N}\) obtained by flipping every member of the same coalition. Define
\(Z^N=R^N\oplus R^{*,N}\). Binary reporting implies \(|Z^N|=T_N\) exactly.

The all-flip channel has leakage
\[ I(O^N;R^{*,N})=N-\log_2\binom N{k_N}. \]
Furthermore, the mutual-information chain rule gives
\[ I(O^N;R^N) \leq I(O^N;R^{*,N})+H(R^N\mid R^{*,N}) \leq I(O^N;R^{*,N})+H(Z^N). \]
Again using subadditivity and concavity,
\[ H(Z^N)\leq Nh_2(\mathbb E T_N/N). \]
Because \(k_N\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), Section 3 bounds the all-flip channel’s excess leakage by \(\log_2(N+1)\). Hence
\[ 0\leq I(O^N;R^N)-a_N(\alpha) \leq\log_2(N+1)+Nh_2(\mathbb E T_N/N). \]
If \(\mathbb E T_N=o(N)\), the right side is \(o(N)\). This proves sufficiency, including the boundary \(\alpha=1/2\).

The regime \(1/2<\alpha\leq1\)

Here \(a_N(\alpha)/N\to0\), so asymptotic optimality is equivalent to \(I(O^N;R^N)=o(N)\).

The general identity above expresses this leakage as the sum of two nonnegative terms. Therefore asymptotic optimality holds exactly when
\[ N-H(E^N)=o(N), \qquad I(E^N;R^N)=o(N). \]
Equivalently, the first condition is
\(D(P_{E^N}\Vert U_N)=o(N)\), where \(U_N\) is uniform on \(\{0,1\}^N\) and relative entropy uses base-two logarithms.

These conditions imply \(\mathbb E|E^N|/N\to1/2\), since
\(H(E^N)\leq Nh_2(q_N)\) and \(h_2\) has its unique maximum at \(1/2\). The converse does not follow from the average flip count alone.

To see this, for sufficiently large \(N\), flip the entire coalition with probability \(t_N=N/(2k_N)\), and otherwise report truthfully. Then \(q_N=1/2\) exactly. Nevertheless, its opinion-independent error vector has entropy
\[ H(E^N)=h_2(t_N)+t_N\log_2\binom N{k_N}. \]
It is independent of \(R^N\), by the uniform-input argument from Section 2. Consequently,
\[ \frac{I(O^N;R^N)}N \longrightarrow 1-\frac{h_2(\alpha)}{2\alpha}>0, \]
where strict positivity follows from \(h_2(\alpha)\leq1<2\alpha\). This family has the required average flip count but is not asymptotically optimal.
