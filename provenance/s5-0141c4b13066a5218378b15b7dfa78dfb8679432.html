The continuous mirror question, as graded

For fixed dimension \u(\(d\)\), an instance consists of rational ideal points \u(\(x_t \in \mathbb{Q}^d\)\) for \u(\(t \in T\)\), positive rational masses \u(\(\mu_t\)\) summing to \u(\(1\)\), and rational \u(\(\varepsilon \in (0,1/2]\)\). Let \u(\(L\)\) be the total input bit length and \u(\(\tau=|T|\)\). Define \u(\(W_{\mu,\beta}(p,q)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\mathbf{1}[\beta\|p-x_t\|_2<\|q-x_t\|_2]\)\) and \u(\(D_{\mu,\beta}(p,q)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\mathbf{1}[\beta\|p-x_t\|_2>\|q-x_t\|_2]\)\), with ties contributing to neither. Set \u(\(\beta_\mu(p)=\sup\{\beta: W_{\mu,\beta}(p,q)\ge D_{\mu,\beta}(p,q)\text{ for every }q\}\)\) and \u(\(\beta(\mu)=\sup_p\beta_\mu(p)\)\). Does there exist an algorithm, polynomial in \u(\(\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\)\) for fixed \u(\(d\)\), that outputs a point \u(\(p\)\) with an explicit exact representation of polynomial bit length and \u(\(\beta_\mu(p)\ge(1-\varepsilon)\beta(\mu)\)\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Fix a dimension \(d\geq2\). A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) ideal-point types \(x_t\in\mathbb Q^d\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including a rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]\).
For \(p,q\in\mathbb R^d\) and \(\beta\in(0,1]\), define \(W_{\mu,\beta}(p,q)=\sum_t\mu_t\mathbf1[\beta\|p-x_t\|_2<\|q-x_t\|_2]\) and \(D_{\mu,\beta}(p,q)=\sum_t\mu_t\mathbf1[\beta\|p-x_t\|_2>\|q-x_t\|_2]\); ties abstain.
Define \(\beta_\mu(p)=\sup(\{0\}\cup\{\beta\in(0,1]:W_{\mu,\beta}(p,q)\geq D_{\mu,\beta}(p,q)\text{ for every }q\in\mathbb R^d\})\) and \(\beta(\mu)=\sup_{p\in\mathbb R^d}\beta_\mu(p)\).
An exact output point may have real algebraic coordinates, each represented by an integer polynomial and a rational isolating interval.

Does there exist, for every fixed \(d\geq2\), an algorithm using \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\) bit operations that outputs an exactly represented point \(p\) of polynomial representation length satisfying \(\beta_\mu(p)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\beta(\mu)\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?

For an explicitly listed multiset of \(n\) voters, a \((1-\varepsilon)\)-approximation is computable in \(O(n^2\varepsilon^{-(3d-2)}\log(n/\varepsilon^{d-1})\log^2(1/\varepsilon))\) geometric running time ([Aronov et al.](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SoCG.2020.7)).
No result settling the stated binary-rational-mass approximation problem was located in the literature search.

Result

The requested algorithm exists for every fixed \(d\geq2\). In fact, its bit complexity and output representation length can be bounded by \(\operatorname{poly}_d(\tau,L)\).

The proof uses cylindrical algebraic decomposition (CAD). The key observation is that the vote margin can be evaluated exactly on each cell without expanding the binary-rational masses into individual voters.

1. Encode comparisons by polynomial signs

Introduce a real variable \(b\), representing the factor \(\beta\), and define
\(F_t(b,p,q)=\|q-x_t\|_2^2-b^2\|p-x_t\|_2^2\).

For \(b\geq0\), both distances being compared are nonnegative. Consequently,
\(\operatorname{sgn}F_t(b,p,q)\) equals \(1\) when type \(t\) votes for \(p\), equals \(-1\) when it votes for \(q\), and equals \(0\) when it abstains. Thus, for \(b>0\),
\(W_{\mu,b}(p,q)-D_{\mu,b}(p,q)=\sum_t\mu_t\operatorname{sgn}F_t(b,p,q)\).

There are \(2d+1\) variables: \(b,p_1,\ldots,p_d,q_1,\ldots,q_d\). Each \(F_t\) has total degree at most \(4\). Explicitly,
\(F_t=\sum_{i=1}^d(q_i-x_{t,i})^2-b^2\sum_{i=1}^d(p_i-x_{t,i})^2\).

Clear its rational denominators by multiplying by a positive integer. Denote the resulting integer polynomial by \(P_t\). This preserves its sign. Since \(d\) is fixed, the number of coefficients of each polynomial is bounded in terms of \(d\), and their bit lengths are \(O_d(L)\).

We also clear the mass denominators. Write \(\mu_t=a_t/c_t\), with positive integers \(a_t,c_t\), and put
\(C=\prod_t c_t\) and \(m_t=C\mu_t\).

Then each \(m_t\) is a positive integer, \(\sum_t m_t=C\), and
\(W_{\mu,b}(p,q)\geq D_{\mu,b}(p,q)\) if and only if
\(\sum_t m_t\operatorname{sgn}P_t(b,p,q)\geq0\).

The bit length of \(C\) is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Every signed sum above has absolute value at most \(C\). Its sign can therefore be computed in polynomially many bit operations.

2. The real-algebraic algorithmic fact

We use the following standard consequence of effective CAD.

Fixed-dimension CAD theorem. Fix a number of variables \(k\) and a degree bound \(D\). Given \(s\) integer polynomials in those variables, with coefficient bit length at most \(h\), one can construct a sign-invariant CAD, in any prescribed variable order, in \(\operatorname{poly}_{k,D}(s,h)\) bit operations. The total number of cells is polynomially bounded. The construction supplies the projection relationships, polynomial-size algebraic sample points, and polynomial-size representations of finite endpoints at the first level. Sample coordinates can be converted, in polynomial time, to integer defining polynomials with rational isolating intervals.

These are standard properties of the CAD and algebraic-number algorithms in [Basu–Pollack–Roy, *Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-662-05355-3). The usual CAD bound is doubly exponential in the number of variables, which gives a polynomial bound when that number is fixed; see also [Basu’s survey, Theorem 2.4](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final.pdf).

Here the hypotheses hold with \(k=2d+1\), \(D=4\), \(s=\tau+2\), and \(h=O_d(L)\).

3. Compute exactly which factors are feasible

Define a predicate, also allowing the auxiliary value \(b=0\), by
\(H(b,p,q)\) if and only if
\(0\leq b\leq1\) and \(\sum_t m_t\operatorname{sgn}P_t(b,p,q)\geq0\).

Define the feasible-factor set
\(S=\{b\in[0,1]:\exists p\in\mathbb R^d\ \forall q\in\mathbb R^d\ H(b,p,q)\}\).

Construct a sign-invariant CAD for
\(P_1,\ldots,P_\tau,b,1-b\), with successive coordinate levels ordered as
\(b,p_1,\ldots,p_d,q_1,\ldots,q_d\).

On every cell at the full level, all signs in \(H\) are constant. Label that cell true exactly when \(H\) holds there. Compute its label using the integer weighted sum from Section 1.

Now propagate the labels through the CAD:

  • Eliminate \(q_d,\ldots,q_1\) universally: a parent cell is true exactly when every child in its stack is true.
  • Eliminate \(p_d,\ldots,p_1\) existentially: a parent cell is true exactly when some child in its stack is true.

Here a *stack* consists of the cells above a given parent cell when one more coordinate is introduced.

To justify the propagation, fix a point of a parent cell. Every child in its stack has a nonempty fiber above that point, and these fibers partition the real line in the next coordinate. Therefore universal quantification corresponds exactly to requiring all child labels to be true; existential quantification corresponds exactly to requiring at least one true child. Induction through the coordinate levels proves the stated rules.

The resulting true cells on the \(b\)-axis have union exactly \(S\). All cells, including those where comparison polynomials vanish, are retained. Thus the construction treats abstention exactly.

There are polynomially many cells, and evaluating each full-level label requires polynomially many bit operations. The entire computation has bit complexity \(\operatorname{poly}_d(\tau,L)\).

4. Identify the correct supremum

For fixed \(p,q\), each function
\(b\mapsto F_t(b,p,q)=\|q-x_t\|_2^2-b^2\|p-x_t\|_2^2\)
is nonincreasing on \([0,1]\). Its sign is therefore nonincreasing as well. Since every mass is positive, the vote margin is nonincreasing in \(b\).

It follows that feasibility is downward closed: if \(b\in S\) and \(0\leq a\leq b\), then \(a\in S\).

Moreover, \(0\in S\). At \(b=0\), every \(F_t\) is nonnegative, so there are no negative contributions.

Put \(B=\sup S\). We claim that \(B=\beta(\mu)\).

Indeed, for any fixed \(p\), its feasible positive factors are precisely those in the definition of \(\beta_\mu(p)\); adjoining \(0\) does not change their supremum. Each such factor belongs to \(S\), so \(\beta_\mu(p)\leq B\). Taking the supremum over \(p\) gives \(\beta(\mu)\leq B\).

Conversely, every positive \(b\in S\) has a witnessing point \(p\), and then \(b\leq\beta_\mu(p)\leq\beta(\mu)\). Taking the supremum over \(S\) gives \(B\leq\beta(\mu)\), proving equality.

The first CAD level partitions the real line into points and open intervals with algebraic endpoints. Since \(S\) is a nonempty union of these cells contained in \([0,1]\), its supremum \(B\) is the right endpoint of its rightmost true cell. Hence we can obtain an exact algebraic representation of \(B\) of polynomial length.

No attainment of this supremum is required. Downward closure implies that every \(b\) satisfying \(0<b<B\) belongs to \(S\): by the definition of supremum, some feasible factor exceeds \(b\), and downward closure then makes \(b\) feasible.

5. Choose a rational factor sufficiently close to \(B\)

If \(B=0\), output the origin. Since \(\beta_\mu(p)\geq0\) for every point, the required inequality holds.

Suppose \(B>0\). Refine its algebraic representation to obtain rational bounds satisfying
\(0<a\leq B\leq c\) and \(c-a\leq\varepsilon a/4\).

This refinement needs only polynomially many bits of precision. To verify that assertion, let \(f\) be a nonzero integer defining polynomial for \(B\), and remove any factor that is a power of its variable. The remaining polynomial still vanishes at \(B\) and has nonzero constant term. If its coefficients have absolute value at most \(H\), the Cauchy root bound applied to its reciprocal polynomial gives
\(B\geq1/(1+H)\).

The degree and \(\log(1+H)\) are polynomially bounded by the CAD representation. An absolute interval width at most
\(\varepsilon/(8(1+H))\)
therefore suffices: its lower endpoint is at least \(B/2>0\), and its width is at most \(\varepsilon a/4\). Standard exact root refinement achieves this with polynomial bit complexity. Also, \(\log(1/\varepsilon)=O(L)\), because \(\varepsilon\) is part of the binary-rational input.

Set the rational factor
\(b=(1-\varepsilon/2)a\).

Then \(0<b<B\). Furthermore, \(c\leq(1+\varepsilon/4)a\), so
\((1-\varepsilon)B\leq(1-\varepsilon)c\leq(1-\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon/4)a<(1-\varepsilon/2)a=b\).

The last strict inequality follows from
\((1-\varepsilon/2)-(1-\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon/4)=\varepsilon/4+\varepsilon^2/4>0\).

Thus \(b\) is feasible and satisfies \(b>(1-\varepsilon)B\). Its rational encoding length is polynomial in \(\tau,L\).

6. Extract an exactly represented point

Substitute this rational \(b\) into the comparison polynomials and construct another sign-invariant CAD, now in the \(2d\) variables ordered as
\(p_1,\ldots,p_d,q_1,\ldots,q_d\).

Label the full-level cells by the weighted vote-margin condition, and eliminate the \(q\)-coordinates universally as before. The true cells at the level containing all \(p\)-coordinates consist exactly of the points that satisfy
\(W_{\mu,b}(p,q)\geq D_{\mu,b}(p,q)\) for every \(q\in\mathbb R^d\).

At least one such cell exists because \(b\in S\). Output its algebraic sample point \(p\), converting each coordinate to an integer polynomial and rational isolating interval.

After substitution, the comparison polynomials have degree at most \(2\); their coefficient bit lengths remain polynomially bounded. The fixed-dimension CAD theorem therefore guarantees polynomial bit complexity and polynomial output representation length.

For the resulting point,
\(\beta_\mu(p)\geq b>(1-\varepsilon)B=(1-\varepsilon)\beta(\mu)\).

This proves the requested approximation guarantee with bit complexity \(\operatorname{poly}_d(\tau,L)\), and hence also with the stated bound \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\).
