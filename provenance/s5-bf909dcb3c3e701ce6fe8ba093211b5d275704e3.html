Exact value

For every finite real \(p,q\ge 1\) and every integer \(T\ge 2\),

\[ \boxed{A_{p,q}(T)=2^{\,1-1/\max(p,q)}}. \]

In fact, the supremum is attained by a society with exactly two support locations and masses \(1/2,1/2\).

1. Upper bound for rational masses

We use the stated finite-profile theorem: in the plane, coordinate-wise median with lower-median tie-breaking has approximation ratio at most \(K=2^{1-1/\max(p,q)}\) for the \(p\)-norm social cost of \(\ell_q\) distances. This is [Hastings, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12187v2#Thmtheorem2); Section 2.2 explicitly specifies lower-median tie-breaking.

Let \(\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}w_i\delta_{x_i}\) be any admissible society. Since its masses are rational, choose a positive integer \(N\) such that \(n_i=Nw_i\) is a positive integer for every \(i\). Then \(\sum_i n_i=N\). Form a finite agent profile \(z\) containing \(n_i\) copies of location \(x_i\).

For any coordinate \(j\) and any support coordinate \(a\), the number of entries of \(z\) whose \(j\)-th coordinate is at most \(a\) equals

\[ N\sum_{i:x_{i,j}\le a}w_i. \]

Consequently, this count reaches \(N/2\) exactly when the corresponding mass reaches \(1/2\). The lower coordinate-wise median of \(z\) is therefore precisely \(M(\mu)\).

Define the unnormalized profile cost by

\[ S_{p,q}(f;z)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|z_k-f\|_q^p\right)^{1/p}. \]

For every facility \(f\),

\[ S_{p,q}(f;z)=N^{1/p}C_{p,q}(f;\mu). \]

The same factor relates the optimal costs, so the two approximation ratios coincide. Hastings’s theorem applies because \(z\) is a finite planar profile, repetitions are allowed, the exponents satisfy \(p,q\ge1\), and the median conventions agree. Hence

\[ \frac{C_{p,q}(M(\mu);\mu)}{\operatorname{OPT}_{p,q}(\mu)} \le K. \]

Taking the supremum proves \(A_{p,q}(T)\le K\). Replication is only a proof device; no running-time claim is involved.

2. Optimal cost for two equal masses

Let \(u\ne v\), let \(\nu=\tfrac12\delta_u+\tfrac12\delta_v\), and put \(D=\|u-v\|_q>0\). We claim that

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_{p,q}(\nu)=D/2. \]

Indeed, for any facility \(f\), write \(a=\|u-f\|_q\) and \(b=\|v-f\|_q\). Convexity of \(t\mapsto t^p\) on the nonnegative reals, followed by the triangle inequality, gives

\[ C_{p,q}(f;\nu) =\left(\frac{a^p+b^p}{2}\right)^{1/p} \ge\frac{a+b}{2} \ge\frac D2. \]

At \(f=(u+v)/2\), homogeneity of the norm gives \(a=b=D/2\), attaining this bound.

3. Matching admissible examples

Case \(p\ge q\)

Take

\[ \mu=\tfrac12\delta_{(0,0)}+\tfrac12\delta_{(1,0)}. \]

The lower coordinate-wise median is \((0,0)\). Thus

\[ C_{p,q}(M(\mu);\mu)=2^{-1/p}, \qquad \operatorname{OPT}_{p,q}(\mu)=\tfrac12, \]

and the ratio is \(2^{1-1/p}=K\).

Case \(p<q\)

Take

\[ \mu=\tfrac12\delta_{(1,0)}+\tfrac12\delta_{(0,1)}. \]

In each coordinate, mass \(1/2\) lies at zero, so lower-median tie-breaking gives \(M(\mu)=(0,0)\). Both support locations have distance one from this median, whereas their mutual distance is \(2^{1/q}\). Therefore,

\[ C_{p,q}(M(\mu);\mu)=1, \qquad \operatorname{OPT}_{p,q}(\mu)=2^{1/q-1}, \]

and the ratio is \(2^{1-1/q}=K\).

Both examples have distinct rational locations, positive binary-rational masses, support size two, and strictly positive optimal cost. They are admissible for every \(T\ge2\), completing the proof.
