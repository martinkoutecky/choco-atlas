Theorem 1 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given discrete update periods, a finite type set \(A\), rational type masses \(\mu_a^r\), finite-support contact laws, and a hyperparameter set \(\Theta\) with transmission function \(H\), each period generates a multitype configuration-model network. For a population-fraction budget \(\kappa\), compute feasible seed masses \(z_a^r\) with \(0\le z_a^r\le\mu_a^r\) and \(\sum_a z_a^r\le\kappa\) that maximize the worst-case limiting expected activated fraction \(\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\Phi_{\theta,r}(z^r)\); determine whether an additive-\(\varepsilon\) approximation can be maintained with useful amortized update time in the type and model parameters.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society at period \(r\) is given by its support \(A^r\): the \(\tau_r\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a^r\), normalized by \(\sum_a\mu_a^r=1\). Support size means the number of listed positive-mass types, not the number of possible types.
Each type has rational features \(x_a\in[-1,1]^{d/2}\), with even \(d\), and an explicitly listed rational probability law on nonnegative integer vectors \((i_b,o_b)_{b\in A^r}\) of incoming and outgoing contacts by neighbor type. Require \(\mu_a^r\mathbb E_a[o_b]=\mu_b^r\mathbb E_b[i_a]\) for every pair \(a,b\).
For each compatible population size \(N\), realize these type and contact frequencies exactly and match outgoing \(a\)-to-\(b\) stubs uniformly to incoming stubs from \(a\) at type \(b\); loops and parallel edges are allowed. Networks are sampled independently at each period.
Let \(\Theta=[-B,B]^d\), where \(B>0\) is rational, and \(H(\theta,x)=h(\theta^{\mathsf T}x)\), where the input link \(h:[-Bd,Bd]\to[0,1]\) is continuous, nondecreasing, \(1\)-Lipschitz, and piecewise linear with rational breakpoints and coefficients. An edge from type \(a\) to type \(b\) has IC transmission probability \(H(\theta,(x_a,x_b))\).

Partial resolution with new algorithmic and hardness results

The full classification remains open. The following results substantially narrow it.

Write
$$ \rho=1-\frac1e, \qquad \gamma_\beta=1-e^{-1/\beta}. $$

Proved results.

  • For the general configuration model, there is a deterministic algorithm returning

$$ F_r(z)\ge\gamma_\beta\mathrm{OPT}_r-\varepsilon $$
within the expanded budget. Its running time is polynomial in the listed input parameters, $1/\kappa$, and $1/\varepsilon$ for every fixed $d$, and exponential in $d$. Explicit rebuilding bounds appear below.

  • For every fixed rational triple satisfying

$$ \beta(\alpha-\varepsilon)>\rho, $$
finding an $(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)$ approximation is NP-hard. This holds already for $d=2$, deterministic contact laws, and constant transmission probability one.

  • Consequently, strict-budget additive approximation is NP-hard for every fixed rational

$$ 0<\varepsilon<\frac1e. $$
The inverse-polynomial additive hardness from the notes also holds, with a complete reduction given below.

  • In the regime where $d$ is fixed and $D$ and $1/\kappa$ are polynomially bounded in the encoded input length, strict-budget guarantees are achievable whenever

$$ \alpha-\varepsilon<\rho, $$
and NP-hard whenever
$$ \alpha-\varepsilon>\rho. $$
This does not settle the boundary, unrestricted dimension, or exponentially small binary-encoded budgets.

All algorithmic parameters supplied as input are assumed to have finite binary rational encodings. Let $b$ be their total encoding length.

1. Elementary guarantees

Suppress the period index. Since seeds are activated and influence is at most one, the finite-population bounds and promised limits give
$$ \sum_a z_a\le F(z)\le1. $$

Put
$$ c=\min\{1,\kappa/\beta\}. $$
The rational vector $z_a=c\mu_a$ is feasible for the expanded budget and satisfies $F(z)\ge c$. Therefore it provides the requested guarantee whenever
$$ c\ge\alpha-\varepsilon. $$

If $\kappa\ge\beta$, seeding the entire population solves every requested approximation guarantee.

Two further exact cases hold.

  • If $h(0)=0$, the admissible parameter $\theta=0$ suppresses every transmission. Hence

$$ F(z)=\sum_a z_a,\qquad \mathrm{OPT}=\kappa. $$
Proportional seeding $z_a=\kappa\mu_a$ is optimal.

  • If there is one listed type, influence is nondecreasing in its seed mass. To prove this, couple uniform seed sets as prefixes of one uniformly random ordering. Seed-set containment implies activation-set containment. Limits and the parameter infimum preserve the inequality. Thus $z=\kappa$ is optimal.

Conservative bit-operation bounds for these elementary algorithms are
$$ O((L+b+\tau)^3) $$
for preprocessing and
$$ O((L+s+b+\tau)^3) $$
per update. They are deterministic.

2. Independent seeding and logarithmic coordinates

For a seed vector $z$, define the within-type fractions
$$ q_a=\frac{z_a}{\mu_a}. $$

2.1 Fixed-count and Bernoulli seeding have the same limit

Preassign independent live-edge indicators with the IC transmission probabilities. A vertex activates exactly when its reverse reachable set contains a seed: a live path from a seed produces activation, and every nonseed activation has such a path.

Let a reverse reachable set contain $c_a$ vertices of type $a$. Write
$$ n_a=N\mu_a,\qquad s_a=\lfloor n_aq_a\rfloor. $$
Under uniform fixed-count seeding, the probability that these vertices avoid seeds is
$$ A_{n_a}(c_a)= \frac{\binom{n_a-c_a}{s_a}}{\binom{n_a}{s_a}}, $$
with value zero if the numerator is impossible. Under independent Bernoulli seeding, it is $(1-q_a)^{c_a}$.

For fixed $q_a$, these quantities become uniformly close over all $c_a$.

At $q_a=0$ or $q_a=1$, they agree exactly. For $0<q_a<1$, convergence holds uniformly for bounded $c_a$, by the finite product formula for the hypergeometric probability. Moreover,
$$ A_{n_a}(c_a) \le(1-s_a/n_a)^{c_a}. $$
For sufficiently large $N$, $s_a/n_a\ge q_a/2$. Thus, for $c_a>K$, both avoidance probabilities are bounded respectively by
$$ (1-q_a/2)^K \quad\text{and}\quad (1-q_a)^K. $$
First take $N$ large for bounded counts, then take $K$ large.

For numbers in $[0,1]$, the difference between two products is bounded by the sum of the differences between corresponding factors. Since the support is finite, the total avoidance probabilities also become uniformly close. Averaging over networks, live edges, and target vertices proves equality of the limiting influence under the two seeding mechanisms.

This comparison is for each fixed seed vector; it asserts no uniform convergence as the seed vector varies.

2.2 Concavity

For finite $y_a\ge0$, put
$$ z_a(y)=\mu_a(1-e^{-y_a}), \qquad G_\theta(y)=\Phi_\theta(z(y)), \qquad G(y)=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}G_\theta(y). $$

A reverse reachable set with count vector $c$ has Bernoulli activation probability
$$ 1-e^{-c^{\mathsf T}y}. $$
Its Hessian is
$$ -e^{-c^{\mathsf T}y}cc^{\mathsf T}, $$
which is negative semidefinite. Expectations and pointwise limits preserve concavity, so $G_\theta$ is concave. Furthermore,
$$ \begin{aligned} G(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)w) &\ge \inf_\theta\bigl[\lambda G_\theta(y)+(1-\lambda)G_\theta(w)\bigr]\\ &\ge\lambda G(y)+(1-\lambda)G(w) \end{aligned} $$
for $0\le\lambda\le1$. Thus $G$ is concave.

2.3 The bicriteria value comparison

Let $C=1/\beta$. Given any strict-budget seed vector, choose $y=Cq$. Then
$$ \sum_a\mu_ay_a=C\sum_a z_a\le C\kappa. $$

For reverse reachable counts $c$, set $t=\sum_a c_aq_a$. The union bound gives
$$ 1-\prod_a(1-q_a)^{c_a}\le\min\{1,t\}. $$
Also,
$$ 1-e^{-Ct}\ge(1-e^{-C})\min\{1,t\}. $$
For $0\le t\le1$, this follows from concavity and the endpoint values; for $t\ge1$, it follows from monotonicity.

Taking expectations, limits, and parameter infima yields
$$ G(Cq)\ge\gamma_\beta F(z). $$
Consequently,
$$ \sup_{\substack{y\ge0\\ \sum_a\mu_ay_a\le C\kappa}}G(y) \ge\gamma_\beta\mathrm{OPT}. $$

The remaining sections turn this structural comparison into an effective algorithm.

3. Positive seeding gives uniform approximation bounds

3.1 Lipschitz dependence on transmission probabilities

Suppose every type has Bernoulli seed probability at least $q_{\min}>0$. Consider two transmission matrices $p,p'$ satisfying
$$ \max_{a,b}|p_{ab}-p'_{ab}|\le\zeta. $$

Couple the network, seeds, and edge uniforms. Explore reverse reachability from a target vertex, stopping immediately upon finding a seed. Skip previously visited vertices.

Conditionally on the exploration history, each newly inspected vertex is seeded with probability at least $q_{\min}$. Hence the expected number of inspected vertices is at most $1/q_{\min}$. Each inspected vertex requires testing at most $D$ incoming edges.

Before the first disagreement, the two coupled explorations coincide. At each tested edge, the conditional probability of a disagreement is at most $\zeta$. Summing these probabilities gives
$$ \left| \mathbb E[\text{activated fraction under }p] - \mathbb E[\text{activated fraction under }p'] \right| \le\frac{D\zeta}{q_{\min}}. $$
The same argument applies to depth-truncated exploration. Passing to the population limits proves the corresponding bound for limiting influence.

In particular, on $y_a\ge a$ with $0<a\le1$,
$$ q_{\min}=1-e^{-a}\ge a/2, $$
so the sensitivity bound is $2D\zeta/a$.

3.2 Uniform depth truncation

Let activation be counted only when a seed reaches the target by a live path of length at most $T$.

If full activation occurs but truncated activation does not, reverse exploration has reached depth $T+1$ without encountering a seed. Its depth-$T$ portion therefore contains at least $T+1$ distinct vertices. Under $y_a\ge a$, the probability that all these vertices avoid seeds is at most $e^{-a(T+1)}$.

Thus, at every finite population size under Bernoulli seeding,
$$ 0\le \text{full expected influence} - \text{depth-}T\text{ expected influence} \le e^{-a(T+1)}. $$

3.3 Explicit limiting recursion

Let $\pi_a(\omega)$ be the probability of contact outcome $\omega$, and let
$$ e_{ab}=\mathbb E_a[o_b]. $$
When $e_{ab}>0$, a predecessor of type $a$ reached through an outgoing stub to type $b$ has contact law
$$ \pi_{a\mid b}(\omega) = \frac{\pi_a(\omega)o_b(\omega)}{e_{ab}}. $$
This is a probability law because its numerator sums to $e_{ab}$.

Define messages
$$ u_{a\mid b}^{(0)}=e^{-y_a} $$
and recursively
$$ u_{a\mid b}^{(t+1)} = e^{-y_a} \sum_\omega\pi_{a\mid b}(\omega) \prod_c \left(1-p_{ca}+p_{ca}u_{c\mid a}^{(t)}\right)^{i_c(\omega)}. $$
The depth-$T$ influence is, for $T\ge1$,
$$ f_{\theta,T}(y) = 1-\sum_a\mu_ae^{-y_a} \sum_\omega\pi_a(\omega) \prod_c \left(1-p_{ca}+p_{ca}u_{c\mid a}^{(T-1)}\right)^{i_c(\omega)}. $$
For $T=0$, it is $\sum_a\mu_a(1-e^{-y_a})$.

Pairs with $e_{ca}=0$ never occur as incoming contacts with positive probability: this follows from balance and positive masses. Their messages need not be computed.

To justify the recursion, fix $T$. The reverse neighborhood contains at most
$$ 1+D+\cdots+D^T $$
vertices. Every nonempty stub pool has size proportional to $N$. With bounded degrees, the probability of a repeated vertex or previously exposed stub in this fixed exploration tends to zero. Sampling an outgoing stub gives exactly the displayed size-biased law. Sampling finitely many distinct stubs without replacement converges to independent sampling from these laws.

Thus the finite reverse neighborhood converges to the branching exploration described by the recursion. No incoming count is subtracted when a predecessor is reached: the selected stub is outgoing from that predecessor.

Together with Sections 2.1 and 3.2, this proves
$$ 0\le G_\theta(y)-f_{\theta,T}(y)\le e^{-a(T+1)} \qquad(y_a\ge a). $$

3.4 Concavity and derivative bounds for the oracle

The recursion represents an expectation of $1-e^{-c^{\mathsf T}y}$ over a finite-depth tree. Therefore $f_{\theta,T}$ is smooth and concave in $y$.

Writing $S=\sum_a c_a$, on $y_a\ge a$ its gradient satisfies
$$ \|\nabla f_{\theta,T}(y)\|_1 = \mathbb E\!\left[S e^{-c^{\mathsf T}y}\right] \le \sup_{S\ge0}S e^{-aS} = \frac1{ea}. $$
The same expectation argument gives, for full limiting influence,
$$ |G_\theta(y)-G_\theta(w)| \le\frac{\|y-w\|_\infty}{ea} $$
whenever both vectors have coordinates at least $a$. Taking infima preserves this bound.

4. Deterministic general-model algorithm

The algorithm below achieves
$$ F(z)\ge\gamma_\beta\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon. $$

If $\kappa/\beta\ge1$, seed the whole population. Otherwise set
$$ C=1/\beta,\qquad c=C\kappa<1, $$
and choose
$$ a=\frac{\varepsilon c}{8}, \qquad M=\left\lceil\log_2(16/\varepsilon)\right\rceil, \qquad T=\left\lceil M/a\right\rceil. $$

Optimize on the rational polytope
$$ Y= \left\{ y:\ a\le y_a\le M,\quad \sum_a\mu_ay_a\le c \right\}. $$
It is nonempty because the constant vector $a$ belongs to it.

4.1 Restricting to this polytope loses little value

Take any original feasible $z$, with fractions $q$. The vector $Cq$ has linear cost at most $c$ and value at least $\gamma_\beta F(z)$.

Mix it with the constant vector $c$:
$$ \bar y=(1-\varepsilon/8)Cq+(\varepsilon/8)c\mathbf1. $$
It remains feasible for the linear budget and has every coordinate at least $a$. Concavity and nonnegativity give
$$ G(\bar y)\ge(1-\varepsilon/8)G(Cq). $$

Now replace each coordinate exceeding $M$ by $M$. For any reverse reachable set, if a changed coordinate occurs, its new no-seed probability is at most $e^{-M}$; otherwise nothing changes. Thus clipping loses at most $e^{-M}\le\varepsilon/16$, uniformly over parameters.

Taking the supremum over original feasible vectors proves
$$ \sup_{y\in Y}G(y) \ge \gamma_\beta\mathrm{OPT}-\frac{3\varepsilon}{16}. $$

4.2 A parameter net whose size depends on encoded $B$

Set
$$ J=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{32D}{a\varepsilon}\right\rceil\right\}. $$

For the univariate link, list its breakpoints and one rational preimage of every level $j/J$ lying in its range. These points partition its domain into intervals on which its value varies by at most $1/J$.

For every type pair, insert the corresponding hyperplanes
$$ \theta^{\mathsf T}(x_a,x_b)=t, $$
and include the box facets $\theta_i=\pm B$. Their number is at most
$$ P=2d+\tau^2(L+J+2). $$

Enumerate all intersections of $d$ linearly independent listed hyperplanes and retain those inside $\Theta$. Let $V$ be the resulting rational set; then
$$ |V|\le P^d. $$

For any $\theta$, take the closure of an arrangement cell containing it. This is a nonempty bounded polytope, hence has a vertex. Every such vertex is an intersection of $d$ independent listed hyperplanes. At that vertex $v$, each transmission probability differs from its value at $\theta$ by at most $1/J$.

Section 3.1 consequently gives
$$ |G_\theta(y)-G_v(y)| \le\frac{2D}{aJ} \le\frac{\varepsilon}{16} \qquad(y\in Y). $$

This construction avoids a grid with size proportional to the numerical magnitude of $B$. Its vertices have bit lengths polynomial in $d$, $L$, and $\log J$, by rational Gaussian elimination.

Define
$$ \widehat G(y)=\min_{v\in V}f_{v,T}(y). $$
It is concave, and the net and truncation bounds imply
$$ |\widehat G(y)-G(y)|\le\varepsilon/16 \qquad(y\in Y). $$

4.3 Concave optimization with rational computations

Here is an explicit optimization argument.

Let
$$ R=\tau M,\qquad Q=2/a. $$
The Euclidean diameter of $Y$ is at most $R$. Each oracle gradient has Euclidean norm at most $1/a$.

At a rational point $y$, evaluate every $f_{v,T}(y)$ to absolute error at most
$$ \nu=\varepsilon/128. $$
Choose an index with minimum evaluated value, and approximate its gradient in Euclidean norm to error at most
$$ \xi=\frac{\varepsilon}{128\max\{R,1\}}. $$
The resulting rational vector $g$ has norm at most $Q$ and satisfies
$$ \widehat G(w) \le \widehat G(y)+g^{\mathsf T}(w-y)+E \qquad(w\in Y), $$
where
$$ E=2\nu+R\xi\le3\varepsilon/128. $$
Indeed, the selected function has true value within $2\nu$ of the minimum; its concavity supplies the tangent inequality, and the gradient error contributes at most $R\xi$.

Choose
$$ n=\left\lceil16RQ/\varepsilon\right\rceil, \qquad H=n^2, \qquad \lambda=\frac{R}{Qn}. $$
Starting at any rational point in $Y$, iterate
$$ y_{t+1}=\operatorname{Proj}_Y(y_t+\lambda g_t). $$

Projection is exactly computable using rational arithmetic. First clamp to the coordinate interval. If the weighted budget is violated, the projection has coordinates
$$ \min\{M,\max\{a,w_a-\ell\mu_a\}\}, $$
where $\ell\ge0$ is determined by the weighted-budget equality. The left side is piecewise linear in $\ell$ with rational breakpoints, so sorting these breakpoints finds the rational solution.

For a maximizer $y^\star$ of the continuous function $\widehat G$ on compact $Y$, projection and the approximate tangent inequality give
$$ \widehat G(y^\star)-\widehat G(y_t) \le \frac{\|y_t-y^\star\|_2^2-\|y_{t+1}-y^\star\|_2^2}{2\lambda} +\frac{\lambda Q^2}{2}+E. $$
Summing, dividing by $H$, and using concavity at the average $\bar y$ proves
$$ \max_Y\widehat G-\widehat G(\bar y) \le\frac{RQ}{n}+E <\varepsilon/8. $$

The oracle values and gradients have polynomial bit complexity. In fact, the recursion and differentiation use
$$ O\!\left(Tm\tau^2(D+1)\right) $$
scalar arithmetic operations per parameter point. A depth-$T$ tree has at most $(D+1)^{T+1}$ vertices, so intermediate derivative magnitudes have logarithms polynomial in $T\log(D+1)$. Induction through the product recursions shows that sufficient working precision is polynomial in
$$ T,\ L,\ d,\ \log(m+\tau+D+1),\ \log(1/\nu),\ \log(1/\xi). $$
Products have factors in $[0,1]$, and the size-biased coefficients form probability laws; accumulated rounding errors are bounded by an exponential in $T$ with polynomially bounded logarithm.

Finally, $e^{-y_a}$ can be approximated with certified rational bounds in polynomial time: the Taylor series for $e^{y_a}$ has remainder at most
$$ e^M\frac{M^{r+1}}{(r+1)!}, $$
and $r=O(M+p)$ suffices for $p$ bits of accuracy, after which reciprocation gives $e^{-y_a}$. Thus this oracle does not assume exact real arithmetic.

4.4 Rational seed output

Approximate $1-e^{-\bar y_a}$ from below by a dyadic rational $\tilde q_a$ so accurately that
$$ 0\le \bar y_a+\log(1-\tilde q_a) \le a\varepsilon/32. $$
This requires only polynomial precision because $\bar y_a\le M$.

Output
$$ z_a=\mu_a\tilde q_a. $$
It is rational and obeys
$$ \sum_a z_a \le \sum_a\mu_a(1-e^{-\bar y_a}) \le \sum_a\mu_a\bar y_a \le c. $$
Its logarithmic coordinates remain at least $a/2$. Section 3.4 bounds the influence loss from rounding by $\varepsilon/16$.

Combining the restriction loss, the two oracle comparisons, optimization error, and output rounding gives
$$ F(z)\ge\gamma_\beta\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon. $$
The sum of the displayed losses is at most $\varepsilon/2$, leaving room within the stated error.

5. Preprocessing and update bounds

The construction uses at most $P^d$ parameter points and
$$ H= O\!\left(\frac{\tau^2M^2}{a^2\varepsilon^2}\right) $$
optimization iterations. Its main scalar-arithmetic count is bounded by
$$ O\!\left( P^d H \left[Tm\tau^2(D+1)+\tau\log(\tau+1)\right] \right), $$
plus parameter-net construction and input processing. The preceding precision argument converts this into a bit-operation bound polynomial in the same quantities and encoded input lengths.

A simpler conservative summary is obtained by defining
$$ W= L+b+m+\tau+D+d+ \kappa^{-1}+\beta^{-1}+\varepsilon^{-1}+2. $$
Preprocessing takes
$$ W^{O(d+1)} $$
bit operations, with an absolute implicit constant.

After each update, reconstruct the snapshot and rerun the algorithm. Replacing $L+b$ by $L+s+b$ in $W$ gives the same sufficient bound per update and hence amortized per update.

These algorithms are deterministic. Their guarantees hold simultaneously over every valid sequence of updates, with probability one. Therefore no dependence on $\log(U/\delta)$ is needed.

Scope of polynomiality. This is polynomial time for fixed $d$ in the numerical reciprocals $1/\kappa$ and $1/\varepsilon$, and in $D$. It is not a polynomial bound in the encoded input length when these numerical quantities are exponentially large.

More generally, if
$$ \alpha-\varepsilon<\gamma_\beta, $$
run the algorithm with a positive internal accuracy smaller than
$$ \gamma_\beta-(\alpha-\varepsilon). $$
Since $\mathrm{OPT}\le1$, this supplies the requested $(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)$ guarantee. The runtime then depends on the reciprocal of that internal accuracy.

6. A weighted coverage construction

This construction proves fixed-additive-error hardness and handles fractional seeding explicitly.

Let $S_1,\ldots,S_t$ be sets on a nonempty universe of size $u$, and let $1\le k\le t$. Choose a power of two $n\ge t+u$, and put
$$ v=\left\lfloor\frac{n-t}{u}\right\rfloor, \qquad w=\frac vn, \qquad \kappa=\frac kn. $$

Create:

  • root types $a_j$, each of mass $1/n$;
  • one terminal type $b_e$ per element, each of mass $w$;
  • one isolated type of mass $(n-t-uv)/n$, if this mass is positive.

All listed masses are positive dyadic rationals and sum to one.

For each incidence $e\in S_j$, every root vertex has $v$ outgoing stubs to $b_e$, and every terminal vertex has one incoming stub from $a_j$. The balance equation is
$$ \frac1n\,v=w\cdot1. $$
All contact laws are deterministic. Roots have no incoming contacts, and terminals have no outgoing contacts.

Take $d=2$, $B=1$, zero features, and $h\equiv1$.

For compatible $N$, a terminal vertex’s predecessor from root type $a_j$ is uniform in that root type. Matchings for distinct pairs are independent. Hence, writing $q_a=z_a/\mu_a$, limiting influence is
$$ F(z)= \frac1n\sum_jq_{a_j} + w\sum_e \left[ 1-(1-q_{b_e})\prod_{j:e\in S_j}(1-q_{a_j}) \right] + \mu_{\mathrm{iso}}q_{\mathrm{iso}}, $$
with the last term omitted when there is no isolated type. Floors converge to these fractions, so every required limit exists.

Define root-only fractional coverage
$$ C(q)= \sum_e\left[1-\prod_{j:e\in S_j}(1-q_j)\right], $$
and let $C_k^\star$ be maximum integral coverage by $k$ sets. The influence formula implies
$$ F(z)\le\sum_a z_a+wC(q_{\mathrm{roots}}). $$

6.1 Fractional coverage under an integer budget

The function $C$ is coordinatewise nondecreasing and multilinear. For distinct $i,j$,
$$ \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial q_i\partial q_j} = -\sum_{e:i,j\in\{j:e\in S_j\}} \prod_{\ell:e\in S_\ell,\ \ell\ne i,j}(1-q_\ell) \le0. $$

Along $q+\lambda e_i-\lambda e_j$, the second derivative is therefore nonnegative. One endpoint of the feasible exchange interval has value at least the current value and makes at least one coordinate integral.

Fill the root-coordinate sum to $k$, then repeat these exchanges. At most one fractional coordinate remains; the integer coordinate sum excludes that possibility. Consequently,
$$ \sum_jq_j\le k \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(q)\le C_k^\star. $$

Under strict budget, $\sum_jq_{a_j}\le k$. Thus
$$ F(z)\le\kappa+wC_k^\star. $$
Seeding $k$ optimal roots completely attains this bound. Therefore
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=\kappa+wC_k^\star. $$

6.2 Fractional coverage with budget augmentation

For every $K\ge k$,
$$ \sum_jq_j\le K \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(q)\le\frac Kk C_k^\star. $$

To prove this, first consider an integral collection of $\ell\ge k$ sets. A uniformly random $k$-subset covers each element covered by the collection with probability at least $k/\ell$: fix one set covering that element and use its inclusion probability. Thus
$$ C(\text{collection})\le\frac{\ell}{k}C_k^\star. $$

If $K\le t$, fill the fractional sum to $K$ and perform the preceding exchanges. At most one coordinate remains fractional. By multilinearity, its value is a convex combination of integral solutions of sizes $\lfloor K\rfloor$ and $\lceil K\rceil$. Both sizes are at least $k$, and their weighted average is $K$, proving the bound.

If $K\ge t$, monotonicity and the integral bound for all $t$ sets prove it directly.

An expanded-budget output consequently satisfies
$$ F(z)\le\frac{\kappa}{\beta} +\frac{w}{\beta}C_k^\star. $$

7. Hardness for fixed approximation parameters

The external theorem used is the perfect-completeness maximum-coverage gap: for every fixed $\eta>0$, it is NP-hard to distinguish instances in which $k$ sets cover the whole universe from instances in which every $k$ sets cover at most a $\rho+\eta$ fraction. This is Feige’s maximum-coverage theorem; its precise gap formulation is also stated in Section 4.5 of [Feige and Vondrák](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v006a011/v006a011.pdf).

$$ \beta(\alpha-\varepsilon)>\rho. $$
Choose a fixed rational $\rho'$ with
$$ \rho<\rho'<\beta(\alpha-\varepsilon), $$
and use the gap theorem with soundness at most $\rho'$. Set
$$ g=\alpha-\varepsilon-\rho'/\beta>0. $$

In the weighted construction, choose $n$ as the smallest power of two satisfying
$$ n\ge \max\left\{ t+u,\, \frac{4(t+u+k/\beta)}g \right\}. $$
This is polynomially bounded because the approximation parameters and $g$ are fixed constants. The construction has at most $t+u+1$ types and $D\le\max\{n,t\}$.

Also,
$$ wu = \frac{u\lfloor(n-t)/u\rfloor}{n} \ge1-\frac{t+u}{n}. $$

In a yes instance,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=\kappa+wu, $$
so a successful approximation must have value at least
$$ A=\alpha(\kappa+wu)-\varepsilon. $$
In a no instance, every expanded-budget output has value at most
$$ B=\frac{\kappa}{\beta}+\frac{\rho'}{\beta}wu. $$

Their separation is
$$ \begin{aligned} A-B &=(\alpha-\rho'/\beta)wu-\varepsilon -(1/\beta-\alpha)\kappa\\ &\ge g-\frac{t+u+k/\beta}{n}\\ &\ge3g/4>0. \end{aligned} $$
All thresholds are rational. Output feasibility and its influence can be checked exactly from the displayed polynomial. Comparing with $(A+B)/2$ decides the NP-hard gap problem.

Thus the requested approximation is NP-hard under the stated condition. Fractional root seeding, direct terminal seeding, random matching, and normalized masses are all accounted for.

For randomized algorithms with success probability at least $2/3$, the same check has one-sided error: no feasible output on a no instance crosses the threshold. Such an algorithm would imply $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{RP}$.

Taking $\beta=\alpha=1$ proves fixed additive hardness for every rational $\varepsilon<1/e$.

Every incidence has one outgoing root stub and one incoming terminal stub. Again take $h\equiv1$ and zero features.

$$ \mathrm{OPT}=\frac{k+C_k^\star}{n}. $$

$$ T_0=\frac{k+|E|}{n}. $$
A yes instance has optimum $T_0$; a no instance has optimum at most $T_0-1/n$.

With
$$ \varepsilon=\frac1{4n}=\frac1{4\tau}, $$
a strict-budget additive approximation has value greater than $T_0-1/(2n)$ on yes instances, whereas every feasible vector has smaller value on no instances. Exact evaluation decides Vertex Cover.

Here $m=\tau$, $d=2$, and all construction sizes are polynomial.
