The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item categories, a finite support \(T\) of types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and a fixed rational-weight ReLU network \(N_t:\{0,1\}^m\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\) for each type, choose masses \(x_{t,b}\ge 0\) assigned to integral bundles \(b\in\{0,1\}^m\) to maximize \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^m}x_{t,b}N_t(b)\), subject to \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) for each \(t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_j\le q_j\) for each item \(j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) item categories with nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\). Each individual receives an integral bundle \(b\in\{0,1\}^m\), including the empty bundle.
Each type has an explicitly supplied, fully connected feed-forward network \(N_t:\{0,1\}^m\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), with arbitrary finite depth and width, signed rational weights and biases, and ReLU activation at every hidden layer and the scalar output. Its parameters are fixed during allocation; no monotonicity or empty-bundle normalization is assumed.
Masses may split between bundles. Define \(W^*:=\max_{x_{t,b}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}}\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^m}x_{t,b}N_t(b)\), subject to \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_j\le q_j\) for every \(j\).
The input also contains a binary-rational threshold \(h\). Let \(S\) be the total network size, counting nodes and connections, and \(L\) the total binary input length, including architectures, coefficients, masses, supplies, and \(h\). State running times in \(m,\tau,S,L\).

Is deciding whether \(W^*\ge h\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finitely many bidders and unit item supplies, maximizing the sum of these network valuations over feasible integral allocations has an equivalent mixed integer linear formulation ([Weissteiner–Seuken, 2020](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/5606/5606-13-8831-1-10-20200512.pdf)); this formulation alone supplies no polynomial-time guarantee.
No result settling the exact mass-allocation problem with explicitly supplied rational ReLU networks was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted fixed networks as explicitly supplied input networks whose parameters remain fixed during allocation; architectures and rational coefficients contribute to input length.
  • Specified feed-forward networks with ReLU activation at every hidden layer and the scalar output, matching the discrete valuation model; weights and biases may have either sign.
  • Required nonnegative supplies and positive numbers of categories and supported types; supplies need not sum to one.
  • Retained the empty bundle and imposed neither monotonicity nor zero value at the empty bundle, because the discrete network model imposes neither restriction.
  • Made divisibility explicit by allowing real nonnegative assignment masses while retaining integral bundles.
  • Introduced a binary-rational welfare threshold to state the exact optimization question as a decision complexity target.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Hardness holds already when \(\tau=1\), every supply is \(q_j=1\), and the valuation network has one hidden layer and only integer coefficients of constant magnitude.

Thus a polynomial-time algorithm exists if and only if \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. A direct reduction from satisfiability

Let \(F=C_1\land\cdots\land C_k\) be a 3-CNF formula on \(m\) Boolean variables, with \(m,k\ge1\). A bundle \(b\in\{0,1\}^m\) represents a truth assignment. For a literal \(\ell\), define its truth value by

\(\ell(b)=b_j\) if \(\ell\) is the positive literal on variable \(j\), and \(\ell(b)=1-b_j\) if it is the negative literal.

Write \(\rho(z)=\max\{0,z\}\). Construct one hidden neuron for each clause:

\[ \]
\(u_i(b)=\rho\!\left(1-\sum_{\ell\in C_i}\ell(b)\right).\)

The preactivation is affine in the inputs, so it is implemented directly by rational weights and a bias. Since each clause has at most three literals, all these coefficients are integers of constant magnitude. Every input is connected to every hidden neuron; connections not used by a clause have weight zero.

For a Boolean input, the sum of literal truth values is an integer. Consequently,

\(u_i(b)=1\) when \(C_i\) is unsatisfied, and \(u_i(b)=0\) when \(C_i\) is satisfied.

Use the scalar ReLU output

\(N(b)=\rho\!\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_i(b)\right).\)

This gives exactly

\(N(b)=1\) if \(b\) satisfies \(F\), and \(N(b)=0\) otherwise.

The output is connected to every hidden neuron with weight \(-1\), and its bias is \(1\). The resulting network satisfies all the architectural requirements and has size \(S=O(mk+m+k)\). Its explicit binary encoding has length polynomial in the formula length.

Construct the allocation instance with one supported type, mass \(\mu_1=1\), supplies \(q_j=1\) for all \(j\), and threshold \(h=1\).

For every nonnegative allocation satisfying \(\sum_b x_{1,b}=1\), each supply constraint holds automatically:

\(\sum_b x_{1,b}b_j\le\sum_b x_{1,b}=1=q_j.\)

Therefore the feasible allocations are precisely probability distributions over bundles. Their welfare is a weighted average of the bundle values, so

\(W^*=\max_{b\in\{0,1\}^m}N(b).\)

The upper bound follows because an average cannot exceed its largest constituent value. Equality follows by assigning the entire mass to a maximizing bundle. Hence

\(W^*\ge1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F\text{ is satisfiable}.\)

This is a polynomial-time reduction from the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem 3-SAT. It proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness and explicitly handles arbitrary splitting of mass.

2. Exact network evaluation has polynomial bit complexity

To establish membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), we first bound the cost of evaluating a supplied network on a specified bundle.

Every coefficient has absolute value at most \(2^L\), and each neuron has at most \(S\) incoming connections. Starting from Boolean inputs, a topological induction bounds the absolute value of every preactivation and activation by

\(M=\bigl((S+1)2^L\bigr)^S.\)

Indeed, an affine combination of at most \(S\) preceding values, followed by a ReLU, increases the bound by at most the factor \((S+1)2^L\).

Let \(D_N\) be the product of the denominators of all coefficient occurrences in the network. Its binary length is \(O(L)\). For a fixed input, replace each ReLU by either the identity or zero according to its evaluated preactivation. Expanding the resulting affine computation expresses each node value as a sum of terms corresponding to directed paths. Each term uses each coefficient occurrence at most once, because the network is feed-forward. Its denominator therefore divides \(D_N\), and so does the denominator of the sum.

Combining this denominator bound with the magnitude bound shows that every node value has numerator and denominator of bit length at most

\(B=O\!\left(L+S\bigl(L+\log(S+1)\bigr)\right).\)

The same argument applies to intermediate affine partial sums. Exact rational arithmetic, reducing fractions after each operation, therefore evaluates a specified bundle in time polynomial in \(m,S,L\). In particular, arbitrary depth does not cause exponentially long evaluation results.

3. Polynomial-size allocation certificates

Put \(r=\tau+m\), and introduce nonnegative supply slacks \(s_j\). The allocation constraints become

\(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) for every \(t\), and

\(\sum_{t,b}b_jx_{t,b}+s_j=q_j\) for every \(j\).

Write this system as \(Ay=d\), \(y\ge0\), where \(y\) contains the allocation variables and slacks. Every entry of \(A\) is zero or one.

The system is feasible: assign every type its mass on the empty bundle and set \(s_j=q_j\). Its feasible set is compact, since

\(0\le x_{t,b}\le\mu_t\) and \(0\le s_j\le q_j.\)

Thus the welfare objective attains a maximum.

A maximizing solution uses at most \(r\) positive variables

Choose an optimal solution \(y\) having the fewest positive coordinates. Suppose that the columns of \(A\) corresponding to those coordinates are linearly dependent. Then there is a nonzero vector \(z\), supported on those coordinates, with \(Az=0\).

For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), both \(y+\varepsilon z\) and \(y-\varepsilon z\) remain nonnegative and satisfy the equality constraints. If \(c\) is the welfare coefficient vector, optimality forces \(c^\mathsf{T}z=0\); otherwise one of these perturbations would improve welfare.

After changing the sign of \(z\) if necessary, it has a positive coordinate. Set

\(\varepsilon_*=\min_{i:z_i>0}y_i/z_i.\)

Then \(y-\varepsilon_*z\) is feasible, has the same welfare, and has fewer positive coordinates. This contradicts the choice of \(y\).

The supported columns are therefore linearly independent. Since \(A\) has \(r\) rows, there are at most \(r\) positive coordinates, including slacks. In particular, at most \(r\) type–bundle pairs receive positive mass.

The masses have polynomial-length rational encodings

The matrix \(A\) has row rank \(r\). Indeed, the empty-bundle column for each type and the \(m\) slack columns together form an identity matrix.

Extend the independent columns supporting \(y\) to an invertible \(r\)-by-\(r\) submatrix \(A_0\). Give the added coordinates value zero. The selected coordinates are then the unique solution of

\(A_0y_0=d.\)

Let \(D\) be the product of the denominators in \(d\), and put \(a=Dd\). The integer \(D\) has bit length \(O(L)\), and every entry of \(a\) has absolute value at most \(2^{2L}\).

Cramer’s rule gives each selected coordinate as

\((y_0)_i=\det(A_0^{(i)})/(D\det(A_0)),\)

where \(A_0^{(i)}\) replaces column \(i\) of \(A_0\) by \(a\).

By the determinant expansion,

\(|\det(A_0)|\le r!\) and \(|\det(A_0^{(i)})|\le r!\,2^{2L}.\)

Thus every positive allocation mass has numerator and denominator of bit length

\(O\!\left(L+r\log(r+1)\right).\)

These witness masses may be general rationals, even if the input masses and supplies are dyadic.

Verification

For every yes-instance, the optimal allocation supplies a certificate listing at most \(r\) type–bundle pairs and their rational masses. Its total encoding length is at most

\(O\!\left(r\left[m+\log(\tau+1)+L+r\log(r+1)\right]\right).\)

A verifier checks nonnegativity, every type-mass equality, every supply inequality, and the welfare threshold. It evaluates the listed networks exactly using Section 2.

All checks take time polynomial in \(m,\tau,S,L\). Conversely, any accepted certificate specifies a feasible allocation of welfare at least \(h\). Therefore the decision problem belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Together with Section 1, this proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

4. Running times and interpretation

Enumerating all bundles produces an explicit rational linear program with \(\tau2^m\) allocation variables and \(\tau+m\) allocation constraints. Section 2 bounds every generated welfare coefficient by polynomial bit length. Applying polynomial-time exact rational linear programming therefore gives a deterministic running time of

\(2^{O(m)}\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,S,L).\)

In particular, the problem is polynomial-time solvable when \(m\) is fixed. When \(m\) is part of the input, the hardness reduction rules out a polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\), even for fixed support size \(\tau=1\).

The related [Weissteiner–Seuken mixed-integer formulation](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/5606/5606-13-8831-1-10-20200512.pdf) concerns discrete winner determination. The reduction above establishes hardness directly for the stated mass-allocation problem.
