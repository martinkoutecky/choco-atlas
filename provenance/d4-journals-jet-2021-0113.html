The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite worker and firm type sets with rational masses, explicit contract menus, strict rankings, and initial partnership cohorts with matched mass on both sides, find a feasible matching flow and final commitment masses such that the resulting state has no blocking positive-mass chain or cycle and no implementable measurable coalition can weakly improve for all its members while strictly improving a positive mass. Types record all relevant preferences, initial contracts, partner cohorts, and commitment status; each unit of mass receives at most one contract.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Both worker and firm sides are nonempty; their combined mass is \(1\), with no requirement that their masses coincide. Let \(m\) count explicitly listed contract labels, including being single, and \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Each type specifies its side, strict ranking of incident contract labels, initial contract, initial partner cohort, and initial commitment status. Bilateral labels specify worker and firm types and fixed terms; different individuals offering the same label are indifferent. No additional payments or contracts are available.
Represent types by atomless intervals with Lebesgue measure. Initial partnership cohorts are disjoint equal-length interval pairs, paired by translation; remaining individuals are single. Write \(\mu_0\), \(a_0(x)\), and \(C_0\) for initial partner map, contract, and committed set, with singles fixed by \(\mu_0\) and excluded from \(C_0\). Require \(\operatorname{single}(x)\succ_x a_0(x)\Rightarrow\mu_0(x)\notin C_0\).
A state specifies a measurable measure-preserving matching involution \(\mu\), bilateral contracts agreed by their endpoints, and a measurable committed set \(C\subseteq C_0\cap\{x:\mu(x)\ne x\}\); every individual has exactly one bilateral or single contract \(a(x)\). Its computational representation is a finite rational interval subdivision with partner translations, contract labels, and commitment flags; lengths give matching flows and commitment masses.
Call \(a\) acceptable to \(x\) when \(a\succeq_x\operatorname{single}(x)\), and relatively acceptable when \(a\succeq_x a_0(x)\). Feasibility requires each assigned contract to be acceptable or relatively acceptable, acceptable for every \(x\notin C_0\), and both acceptable and relatively acceptable whenever \(\mu_0(x)\in C_0\).
A positive-mass chain consists of disjoint measurable cohorts \(X_1,Y_1,\ldots,X_K,Y_K\) of equal positive mass, \(K\ge1\), with \(X_1,Y_K\) free under \(C\) and \(\mu(Y_k)=X_{k+1}\) for \(k<K\). A cycle instead requires these partner equalities cyclically, with \(X_{K+1}=X_1\). It blocks if each \(X_k\) can be measurably paired with \(Y_k\) through mutually acceptable contracts strictly improving every participating individual. A state is strongly stable if free individuals receive acceptable contracts and no such chain or cycle blocks.
A positive-measure measurable coalition \(S\) is implementable when \(\mu_0(S\cap C_0)\subseteq S\). It improves a matching if a measurable matching using only members of \(S\) and listed contracts weakly improves every member and strictly improves a positive mass. A feasible matching is in the strict core if no implementable coalition improves it. All conditions hold almost everywhere; blocking coalitions and pairings need not have rational subdivisions.

Is constructing a feasible, strongly stable strict-core state, or determining that none exists, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finite individual agents, a hybrid procedure constructs a strongly stable strict-core matching state with final commitments contained in initial commitments; the published version retains this existence result, calling the state stable. [Sun–Yang, 2018](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/economics/documents/discussionpapers/2018/1820.pdf), [Sun–Yang, 2021](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/175719/1/Senior_Level_Job_Matching_Sun_Yang_Complete.pdf).
No result settling this precise atomless, support-encoded computational problem was located.

  • Carried over the initial-state condition that an individual who prefers being single to the initial contract has an initially free partner.
  • Restored the exact feasibility conditions involving acceptability, relative acceptability, initial freedom, and an initial partner's commitment.
  • Required final commitments to be a measurable subset of initial commitments and to concern currently partnered individuals.
  • Defined positive-mass chains and cycles using disjoint equal-mass measurable cohorts, current partner links, free chain endpoints, and mutually acceptable strict improvements.
  • Defined coalition implementability using initial partner links and initial commitments; coalition improvements may assign single contracts and are not additionally required to satisfy grand-coalition feasibility.

Partial resolution: arbitrary masses without initial commitments

I do not settle the problem for arbitrary initial commitments. I prove a stronger tractable case than the equal-mass case in the notes:

Theorem. If $C_0=\varnothing$, constructing a feasible, strongly stable strict-core state, or determining that none exists, is solvable in polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$, for arbitrary positive binary-rational type masses.

The proof gives an explicit linear-programming algorithm and handles parallel contract labels. I also give two examples explaining why the reduction does not extend directly to initial commitments.

1. Definitions and consequences of having no commitments

Throughout Sections 1–5, assume $C_0=\varnothing$.

Let $W$ and $F$ be the supported worker and firm types. Write $q_t>0$ for the mass of type $t$, and $s_t$ for its single label. For a bilateral label $b$, write $w(b)$ and $f(b)$ for its endpoint types.

Because every individual lies outside $C_0$, feasibility requires every assigned contract to be acceptable. Conversely, an assignment using acceptable contracts satisfies all the task’s local feasibility conditions: the condition concerning an initially committed partner is vacuous. Also, every measurable coalition is implementable, and the final committed set must be empty.

Call a type uniformly assigned if almost all its members receive the same label.

Lemma 1: uniformity in the strict core

Every feasible strict-core matching assigns each supported type uniformly.

Proof. Suppose a type $t$ receives distinct labels $b,d$ on positive masses, with $b\succ_t d$.

Both labels are acceptable. Therefore $b$ cannot be $s_t$: otherwise $d\prec_t s_t$, contradicting acceptability of $d$. Thus $b$ is bilateral.

Choose a positive number $\varepsilon$ no larger than either of the following masses:

  • the mass of type $t$ currently receiving $d$;
  • the mass of type $t$ currently receiving $b$.

Choose a measurable cohort $A$ of mass $\varepsilon$ from the first population. Choose a measurable cohort $B$ of mass $\varepsilon$ from the current partners of the second population. Measure preservation guarantees that the latter partner population has the requisite mass.

The cohorts $A,B$ lie on opposite sides and are disjoint. Match them through label $b$. Members of $A$ strictly improve, while members of $B$ retain their current label and are indifferent to the replacement individuals. Equal-mass measurable subsets of atomless intervals admit a measure-preserving pairing modulo null sets.

Since $C_0=\varnothing$, the coalition $A\cup B$ is implementable. It contradicts strict-core membership.

There are finitely many incident labels. Hence, if a type is not uniform almost everywhere, at least two distinct labels occur on positive masses, which we have excluded. $\square$

Lemma 2: uniform bilateral assignments require equal masses

If a feasible strict-core matching uniformly assigns label $b$ to type $w(b)$, then type $f(b)$ is also uniformly assigned $b$, and
$$ q_{w(b)}=q_{f(b)}. $$

Proof. Positive mass of type $w(b)$ is matched through $b$, so positive mass of type $f(b)$ receives $b$. Lemma 1 makes the latter type uniform, necessarily with label $b$. Both entire type populations therefore participate in $b$, and measure preservation gives equality of their masses. The argument is symmetric between the sides. $\square$

2. Exact reduction to matching supported types

Construct a bipartite multigraph $G$ with vertex classes $W,F$. Each listed bilateral label acceptable to both endpoint types becomes a distinct edge. Parallel labels remain distinct edges, ranked according to the given strict preferences.

A type matching $M$ selects edges with no shared vertices. A matched type receives its selected edge label; an unmatched type receives its single label. Denote this label by $a_t(M)$.

Call $M$ stable if there is no edge $b$ satisfying
$$ b\succ_{w(b)}a_{w(b)}(M) \qquad\text{and}\qquad b\succ_{f(b)}a_{f(b)}(M). $$

Define the forbidden edge set
$$ P=\{b\in E(G):q_{w(b)}\ne q_{f(b)}\}. $$

Edges in $P$ remain potential blocking edges; they are forbidden only as assignments.

Proposition 3: equivalence

A requested state exists if and only if $G$ has a stable type matching avoiding $P$.

Moreover, every such type matching yields a requested state by assigning its labels uniformly.

Proof: necessity. Lemmas 1 and 2 turn any feasible strict-core matching into a type matching avoiding $P$.

If some edge $b$ strictly improved both endpoint types over their uniform assignments, choose equal positive masses from those types and match them through $b$. Both cohorts strictly improve, and the coalition is implementable. This contradicts strict-core membership. Thus the type matching is stable.

Proof: sufficiency. Let $M$ be stable and avoid $P$. For every selected edge, its endpoint types have equal masses. Match their intervals by translation, assigning the selected label. Leave unmatched types single. All assigned labels are acceptable, so the resulting matching is feasible.

Suppose an improving measurable coalition existed. Since there are finitely many types and labels, some positive-mass category of its strictly improved members has one type $t$ and one new label $b$.

Their new label cannot be single, because their current assignment is acceptable. Thus $b$ is bilateral. Let $u$ be its other endpoint type. Weak improvement for their new partners gives
$$ b\succeq_u a_u(M). $$

If $b=a_u(M)$, then type $u$ is uniformly assigned $b$ in the current matching. Since $b$ specifies endpoint types $t,u$, positive mass of type $t$ currently receives $b$. Uniformity would give $a_t(M)=b$, contradicting strict improvement of the selected type-$t$ members.

Consequently $b\ne a_u(M)$. Strict rankings imply
$$ b\succ_u a_u(M). $$
Together with strict improvement for type $t$, this contradicts stability of $M$. Therefore no improving coalition exists, including one with an arbitrary measurable representation.

Finally, all individuals are free and receive acceptable contracts. Any blocking chain or cycle would use a bilateral label strictly improving both endpoint types over their uniform assignments. No such label exists. The state is strongly stable. $\square$

This reduction leads to stable marriage with forbidden edges, a known polynomially solvable problem; see [Cseh–Matuschke, Section 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3701). The following construction supplies a direct proof for the multigraph formulation needed here.

3. An auxiliary perfect-matching instance

Write
$$ n_W=|W|,\qquad n_F=|F|. $$

Add a private dummy firm $\widehat w$ for each real worker type $w$, and a private dummy worker $\widehat f$ for each real firm type $f$. The auxiliary bipartition is
$$ U=W\cup\{\widehat f:f\in F\}, \qquad V=F\cup\{\widehat w:w\in W\}. $$
Both classes have size $\tau=n_W+n_F$.

Add the following edges:

  • Every original edge of $G$, including those in $P$.
  • A private edge $w\widehat w$ for each $w\in W$.
  • A private edge $\widehat f f$ for each $f\in F$.
  • Every dummy–dummy edge $\widehat f\widehat w$.

Each real vertex retains its ordering of original acceptable labels and ranks its private edge last. Each dummy vertex ranks its private edge first and ranks its dummy–dummy edges in an arbitrary strict order.

Private edges represent single assignments. Dummy edges are solely computational devices; they will not be contracts in the output society.

Lemma 4: perfect completion

The original graph has a stable matching avoiding $P$ if and only if the auxiliary graph has a stable perfect matching avoiding $P$.

Proof: restriction. Restrict an auxiliary perfect matching to its original edges. A real vertex not matched through an original edge is matched to its private dummy, and hence represents a single type.

If an original edge blocked the restriction, both real endpoints would prefer it to their auxiliary assignments, because private edges occupy the position of single. It would therefore block the auxiliary matching. Avoidance of $P$ is preserved.

Proof: extension. Start with a stable original matching $M$. Match each unmatched real vertex to its private dummy.

If $M$ contains $k$ original edges, exactly $k$ dummy workers and $k$ dummy firms remain unmatched. Their induced graph is complete bipartite. Run worker-proposing deferred acceptance on that graph.

For completeness, this procedure terminates because each edge is proposed at most once. It leaves a perfect matching: an unmatched worker who exhausted its complete list would imply that every firm holds a proposal, accounting for all $k$ workers and contradicting that worker’s unmatched status. It is stable because a worker preferring another firm to its final partner proposed to that firm, and the firm’s retained proposals only improved thereafter.

Combine these dummy matches with $M$ and the private matches. The result is perfect. It has no blocking edge:

  • An original blocking edge would block $M$.
  • A private edge is either selected or rejected by its real endpoint, whose original assigned label is preferred to single.
  • A dummy–dummy edge incident to a dummy privately matched to its owner is rejected by that dummy.
  • The remaining dummy–dummy edges cannot block by the deferred-acceptance argument.

Only original edges belong to $P$, so avoidance is preserved. $\square$

4. A linear program with direct support extraction

Let $H$ be the auxiliary multigraph. Write $\delta(v)$ for its incident edges, and $\succ_v$ for its strict edge ranking.

Introduce one real variable $x_e$ per edge. Consider the following feasibility system:
$$ x_e\ge 0 \qquad(e\in E(H)), $$
$$ \sum_{e\in\delta(v)}x_e=1 \qquad(v\in U\cup V), $$
$$ x_e+ \sum_{\substack{g\in\delta(u)\\g\succ_u e}}x_g+ \sum_{\substack{g\in\delta(v)\\g\succ_v e}}x_g \ge 1 \qquad(e=uv\in E(H)), $$
$$ x_e=0 \qquad(e\in P). $$

For parallel edges, the two preference sums are separate: an edge preferred at both endpoints contributes twice. This is intentional.

Lemma 5: every stable perfect matching supplies a feasible point

The characteristic vector of a stable perfect matching avoiding $P$ satisfies the system.

Proof. Nonnegativity, degree equations, and forbidden-edge equations are immediate.

For the stability inequality of edge $e$, if $e$ is selected then $x_e=1$. Otherwise, stability and strict rankings imply that at least one endpoint is matched to an edge strictly preferred to $e$. The corresponding preference sum therefore contributes $1$. $\square$

Lemma 6: every feasible point supplies a stable perfect matching

Given any feasible $x$, let each vertex $u\in U$ select its most-preferred edge $e_u$ having $x_{e_u}>0$. These selected edges form a stable perfect matching avoiding $P$.

Proof. The degree equation ensures that every $u$ has a positive incident edge.

Fix $u$, and write $e_u=uv$. By its definition,
$$ \sum_{\substack{g\in\delta(u)\\g\succ_u e_u}}x_g=0. $$
The stability inequality for $e_u$ gives
$$ x_{e_u}+ \sum_{\substack{g\in\delta(v)\\g\succ_v e_u}}x_g \ge 1. $$
The degree equation at $v$ is
$$ \sum_{g\in\delta(v)}x_g=1. $$
Nonnegativity now implies that every edge strictly worse than $e_u$ at $v$ has zero weight. Since $x_{e_u}>0$, the edge $e_u$ is exactly the least-preferred positive edge at $v$.

Two different vertices of $U$ cannot select edges incident to the same $v$: each selected edge would have to be the unique least-preferred positive edge at $v$. Thus the selected edges match all vertices of $U$ to distinct vertices of $V$. Because $|U|=|V|$, they form a perfect matching $N$. Its edges have positive weight, so none belongs to $P$.

It remains to prove stability. Suppose an edge $e=uv$ blocked $N$. Then
$$ e\succ_u e_u. $$
Because $e_u$ is the most-preferred positive edge at $u$, both $x_e$ and every weight on an edge preferred to $e$ at $u$ are zero. The stability inequality for $e$ therefore becomes
$$ \sum_{\substack{g\in\delta(v)\\g\succ_v e}}x_g\ge 1. $$
The degree equation at $v$ forces all its positive edges to be strictly preferred to $e$. In particular, the edge assigned to $v$ by $N$ is strictly preferred to $e$. This contradicts blocking. Hence $N$ is stable. $\square$

The extraction requires neither an integral LP solution nor a polyhedral integrality theorem.

5. Algorithm, running time, and output representation

The algorithm is now explicit:

  • Retain the bilateral labels acceptable to both supported endpoint types.
  • Mark unequal-mass edges as forbidden.
  • Build the auxiliary graph and feasibility system.
  • Solve the system by exact rational linear programming.
  • If it is infeasible, report that no requested state exists.
  • Otherwise, apply Lemma 6, restrict the resulting matching to original edges, and realize it uniformly on the type intervals.

Correctness follows from Proposition 3 and Lemmas 4–6.

The auxiliary graph has $2\tau$ vertices and at most
$$ m+\tau+n_Wn_F $$
edges. The linear program therefore has polynomially many variables and constraints. Its coefficients have bounded bit length, and exact comparisons of the input rational masses take polynomial time.

We use the standard rational-linear-programming theorem: feasibility of a system with rational coefficients can be decided in time polynomial in its encoding length, and a rational feasible point can be returned with polynomial encoding length. Its hypotheses hold here. In addition, the degree equations and nonnegativity imply $x_e\le 1$, so the feasible region is bounded.

The extracted type matching uses only equal-mass original edges. Each matched pair of type intervals can consequently be paired by translation. If the input represents a type through several interval pieces, split and pair those pieces greedily; each pairing exhausts at least one piece. This produces polynomially many pieces.

All lengths and translations are rational combinations of input interval coordinates, with polynomial bit length. Set $C=\varnothing$. Thus the algorithm constructs the required computational representation in polynomial time.

This proves the announced theorem for arbitrary type masses, without requiring equal total masses on the two sides.

6. Commitments can permit nonuniform strict-core states

The uniformity lemma does not extend to arbitrary initial commitments.

Consider one worker type $w$ and one firm type $f$, each of mass $1/2$. There are three bilateral labels $r,b,c$ between them, with rankings
$$ b\succ_w c\succ_w r\succ_w s_w, $$
$$ c\succ_f b\succ_f r\succ_f s_f. $$

Initially, the entire populations are paired through $r$, and everyone is committed. Divide the initial parameter interval into two pieces of length $1/4$. Retain the initial partners, assigning $b$ on one piece and $c$ on the other. Set $C=C_0$.

The state has a rational interval representation. It is feasible because both $b$ and $c$ are acceptable and relatively acceptable to both types.

Strict-core proof

Let $S$ be an implementable coalition. Since both endpoints of every initial pair are committed, $S$ selects either both endpoints of an initial pair or neither, modulo null sets.

Let $\alpha$ be the selected mass on each side from the $b$ piece, and $\beta$ the selected mass on each side from the $c$ piece.

In any weakly improving coalition matching:

  • The $\alpha$ selected workers currently receiving $b$ must retain $b$, their top label.
  • The $\beta$ selected firms currently receiving $c$ must retain $c$, their top label.
  • No selected individual can receive $r$ or single, since both are strictly worse than its current assignment.

If $B,D$ are the new matching masses through $b,c$, respectively, these observations give
$$ B\ge\alpha,\qquad D\ge\beta,\qquad B+D=\alpha+\beta. $$
Therefore
$$ B=\alpha,\qquad D=\beta. $$

The selected workers originally receiving $b$ exhaust all new $b$ matches, so the other workers retain $c$. The selected firms originally receiving $c$ exhaust all new $c$ matches, so the other firms retain $b$. Everyone retains their current label, and nobody strictly improves.

Thus the state belongs to the strict core.

Strong-stability proof

No available label strictly improves both endpoints of a prospective pair:

  • For $b$, a firm currently receiving $b$ is indifferent, and a firm receiving $c$ rejects it.
  • For $c$, a worker currently receiving $c$ is indifferent, and a worker receiving $b$ rejects it.
  • Label $r$ is worse than both current labels.

Consequently no blocking chain or cycle exists. The free-individual condition is vacuous because everyone is committed.

This is a feasible, strongly stable strict-core state in which both supported types receive two labels on positive masses.

7. Commitments can protect a mutually improving pair

Even restricting attention to uniform states, ordinary pairwise stability is not necessary once commitments are present.

Take worker types $w_1,w_2$ and firm types $f_1,f_2$, each of mass $1/4$. The bilateral labels are
$$ r_1:w_1\text{--}f_1,\qquad r_2:w_2\text{--}f_2,\qquad b:w_1\text{--}f_2. $$
Rankings are
$$ b\succ_{w_1}r_1\succ_{w_1}s_{w_1}, \qquad r_2\succ_{w_2}s_{w_2}, $$
$$ r_1\succ_{f_1}s_{f_1}, \qquad b\succ_{f_2}r_2\succ_{f_2}s_{f_2}. $$

Initially, the populations are paired through $r_1,r_2$, and everyone is committed. Retain the initial matching, labels, and commitments. This state is feasible.

Strict-core proof

An implementable coalition selects equal masses $\alpha$ from each endpoint of the first initial cohort and equal masses $\beta$ from each endpoint of the second.

Every selected member of $f_1$ must retain $r_1$ to weakly improve. This consumes all $\alpha$ selected workers of type $w_1$. Every selected member of $w_2$ must retain $r_2$, consuming all $\beta$ selected firms of type $f_2$.

Hence the coalition cannot use $b$, and all selected members retain their labels. No improving coalition exists.

Strong-stability proof

There are no free individuals, so no chain can block. The only bilateral label strictly improving both of its endpoints is $b$.

A blocking cycle containing an $f_2$ cohort would also contain its current $w_2$ partners: the cyclic partner equalities include every participant’s current partner. But $w_2$ has no strictly improving label. Thus no cycle blocks.

Nevertheless, $b$ strictly improves both $w_1$ and $f_2$ over their assignments. The state therefore fails ordinary pairwise stability.

Indeed, every feasible matching has the same assigned labels: feasibility requires members of $f_1$ and $w_2$ to receive contracts relatively acceptable to their initial contracts, forcing all of $f_1$ to receive $r_1$ and all of $w_2$ to receive $r_2$. Equal type masses then exhaust $w_1$ and $f_2$. Thus this instance has a requested state but no feasible ordinarily pairwise-stable matching.
