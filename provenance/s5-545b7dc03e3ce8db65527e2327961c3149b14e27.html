The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of complete pair types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), hospital ownership, type-level compatibility and weights, fixed opponents' internal mass matchings, a player \(p\), a threshold \(\rho\), and \(\sigma\in\{\mathrm{pessimistic},\mathrm{optimistic}\}\), decide whether \(p\) has a feasible internal mass matching \(x^p\) such that the worst-case or best-case utility over all maximum-cardinality residual external mass matchings \(y\) is at least \(\rho\). Internal and external matching variables must satisfy the type-mass capacities \(\sum_u x^p_{tu}+\sum_u y_{tu}\le\mu_t\), and utility is the paper's weighted internal-plus-external matched-patient benefit.

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) complete incompatible patient–donor pair types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has one owner among \(m\geq2\) hospitals; ownership, reciprocal compatibility, and transplant benefits are constant within types. Compatibility is an explicitly supplied undirected loop-free graph \((T,E)\), and only two-way exchanges are allowed.
Let \(E^q\) contain compatible edges whose endpoints both belong to hospital \(q\), and let \(E^I\) contain compatible edges with different owners. Each edge has a nonnegative binary-rational benefit \(w_e^q\) for every incident hospital: an internal edge's benefit includes both patients, while an external edge's benefit includes only that hospital's patient; benefits for nonincident hospitals are zero.
The input specifies a hospital \(p\), a positive binary-rational threshold \(\rho\), feasible binary-rational internal mass matchings \(\bar x^q\) for all \(q\ne p\), and \(\sigma\in\{\mathrm{pessimistic},\mathrm{optimistic}\}\). Write \(\delta_F(t)=\{e\in F:t\in e\}\).
Hospital \(p\) chooses any real vector \(x^p\geq0\) on \(E^p\) with \(\sum_{e\in\delta_{E^p}(t)}x_e^p\leq\mu_t\) for its types. Set \(x^q=\bar x^q\) for opponents and \(r_t(x)=\mu_t-\sum_{e\in\delta_{E^{h(t)}}(t)}x_e^{h(t)}\), where \(h(t)\) is the owner of \(t\). Unused mass remains available externally; no additional withholding move is allowed.
Define \(Y(x)=\{y\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{E^I}:\sum_{e\in\delta_{E^I}(t)}y_e\leq r_t(x)\ \forall t\}\) and \(Y^*(x)=\arg\max_{y\in Y(x)}\sum_{e\in E^I}y_e\).
Utility is \(U_p(x,y)=\sum_{e\in E^p}w_e^p x_e^p+\sum_{e\in E^I}w_e^p y_e\). Define \(V_{\mathrm{pessimistic}}(x)=\min_{y\in Y^*(x)}U_p(x,y)\) and \(V_{\mathrm{optimistic}}(x)=\max_{y\in Y^*(x)}U_p(x,y)\).
For each fixed \(\sigma\), is deciding whether some feasible \(x^p\) satisfies \(V_\sigma(x)\geq\rho\) in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and total input bit length \(L\).
The discrete pessimistic and optimistic best-response decision problems are both \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already with two hospitals; the pessimistic result also holds with a bipartite internal graph. [Carvalho–Lodi](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.09207v3).

1. Classification

For each fixed tie-breaking rule, the divisible-mass best-response decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness already holds with:

  • two hospitals;
  • a star as the choosing hospital’s internal graph;
  • a forest as the entire compatibility graph;
  • benefits belonging to \(\{0,1,2\}\);
  • dyadic masses and thresholds.

Both problems admit an exact algorithm with running time
\(2^{O(\tau\log(\tau+1))}(m+L)^{O(1)}\).
Thus fixed support size gives polynomial-time solvability, but unrestricted support size is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even when \(m=2\).

The reductions below apply directly to real-valued internal allocations.

2. Common reduction framework

We reduce from positive-integer Subset Sum: given binary-encoded positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n,K\), decide whether some \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in J}a_i=K\). This is the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete numerical problem. [Karp, *Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Write \(S=\sum_i a_i\). Hospital \(1\), the choosing hospital, owns a center \(c\) of unnormalized mass \(K\) and leaves \(t_i\) of unnormalized mass \(a_i\). Its only internal edges are \(\{c,t_i\}\).

Let \(\xi_i\) denote the unnormalized internal amount on \(\{c,t_i\}\). Internal feasibility is exactly

\(\displaystyle 0\leq \xi_i\leq a_i\quad\text{for every }i,\qquad \sum_i\xi_i\leq K.\)

The leaf’s residual mass is \(r_i=a_i-\xi_i\). The center has no external edges. Hospital \(2\) has no internal edges, so its prescribed internal matching is the empty vector.

Both constructions below have total unnormalized mass
\(C=K+\frac52S\). Choose a power of two \(D>C\), divide every mass by \(D\), and add an isolated type owned by hospital \(2\), of mass \((D-C)/D\). All resulting masses are positive dyadic rationals summing to one.

Multiplication of every matching coordinate by \(D\) gives a bijection between the normalized and unnormalized feasible allocations. It multiplies cardinality and utility by \(D\), preserving primary optimality and either secondary tie-breaking rule. We therefore calculate in unnormalized units and divide the threshold by \(D\).

The external gadgets are disjoint. Consequently, a global cardinality optimum must maximize cardinality in every gadget separately; secondary utility optimization then also separates.

3. Pessimistic rule: hardness

Construction and follower optimum

For each \(i\), hospital \(2\) owns two additional types:

| Type | Unnormalized mass |
|---|---:|
| \(u_i\) | \(a_i/2\) |
| \(v_i\) | \(a_i\) |

The external edges and hospital \(1\)’s benefits are:

| Edge | Benefit |
|---|---:|
| \(\{t_i,u_i\}\) | \(0\) |
| \(\{t_i,v_i\}\) | \(2\) |

Every internal edge \(\{c,t_i\}\) has benefit \(2\). All benefits for hospital \(2\) are zero.

Because \(v_i\) alone can accommodate the entire residual \(r_i\), maximum external cardinality in gadget \(i\) is \(r_i\). Every primary optimum therefore externally matches all of \(t_i\)’s residual mass.

At most \(a_i/2\) can use the zero-benefit edge. Hence pessimistic external utility is exactly

\(\displaystyle 2\max\{r_i-a_i/2,0\}=2\max\{a_i/2-\xi_i,0\}.\)

This lower bound is attainable by assigning \(\min\{r_i,a_i/2\}\) to \(\{t_i,u_i\}\) and the remaining mass to \(\{t_i,v_i\}\).

Exact utility bound

Including internal utility, gadget \(i\) contributes

\(\displaystyle f_i(\xi_i)=2\xi_i+2\max\{a_i/2-\xi_i,0\} =a_i+\xi_i-\min\{\xi_i,a_i-\xi_i\}.\)

To verify the identity: for \(\xi_i\leq a_i/2\), both sides equal \(a_i\); for \(\xi_i\geq a_i/2\), both equal \(2\xi_i\).

Define \(d_i=\min\{\xi_i,a_i-\xi_i\}\). Since \(0\leq\xi_i\leq a_i\), we have \(d_i\geq0\), with equality exactly when \(\xi_i\in\{0,a_i\}\). Thus the unnormalized pessimistic value is

\(\displaystyle \widehat V_{\mathrm{pessimistic}}(\xi)=S+\sum_i\xi_i-\sum_i d_i.\)

Set the normalized threshold to \(\rho=(S+K)/D\). Its unnormalized gap is exactly

\(\displaystyle (S+K)-\widehat V_{\mathrm{pessimistic}}(\xi) =\left(K-\sum_i\xi_i\right)+\sum_i d_i.\)

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Therefore the threshold is reached if and only if

\(\displaystyle \sum_i\xi_i=K\qquad\text{and}\qquad \xi_i\in\{0,a_i\}\ \text{for every }i.\)

These conditions hold exactly when the original Subset Sum instance is a YES instance: choose \(J=\{i:\xi_i=a_i\}\). Conversely, any subset summing to \(K\) supplies such an internal allocation.

This proves pessimistic \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness, including against arbitrary real-valued choices.

4. Optimistic rule: hardness

Construction and primary follower optimum

Keep the same center, leaves, and internal star, but give every internal edge benefit \(1\).

For each \(i\), add the following types:

| Type | Owner | Unnormalized mass |
|---|---|---:|
| \(s_i\) | Hospital \(1\) | \(a_i/2\) |
| \(u_i\) | Hospital \(2\) | \(a_i/2\) |
| \(v_i\) | Hospital \(2\) | \(a_i/2\) |

Their external edges are:

| Edge | Hospital \(1\)’s benefit |
|---|---:|
| \(\{t_i,u_i\}\) | \(0\) |
| \(\{s_i,u_i\}\) | \(2\) |
| \(\{s_i,v_i\}\) | \(0\) |

Again, all benefits for hospital \(2\) are zero.

Put \(b_i=a_i/2\). Denote the amounts on these three edges by \(\ell_i,h_i,z_i\), respectively. Their constraints are

\(\displaystyle \ell_i\leq r_i,\qquad \ell_i+h_i\leq b_i,\qquad h_i+z_i\leq b_i,\qquad z_i\leq b_i,\qquad \ell_i,h_i,z_i\geq0.\)

Since \(\ell_i\leq\min\{r_i,b_i\}\) and \(h_i+z_i\leq b_i\), cardinality is at most \(b_i+\min\{r_i,b_i\}\). This is attained by

\(\displaystyle \ell_i=\min\{r_i,b_i\},\qquad h_i=0,\qquad z_i=b_i.\)

Thus every primary optimum must satisfy

\(\displaystyle \ell_i=\min\{r_i,b_i\},\qquad h_i+z_i=b_i.\)

Optimistic follower utility

The capacity of \(u_i\) then implies

\(\displaystyle h_i\leq b_i-\min\{r_i,b_i\}=\max\{b_i-r_i,0\}.\)

Equality is attainable: choose \(h_i=b_i-\min\{r_i,b_i\}\) and \(z_i=\min\{r_i,b_i\}\). These choices satisfy every constraint above.

Therefore optimistic external utility is exactly

\(\displaystyle 2\max\{b_i-r_i,0\}=2\max\{\xi_i-a_i/2,0\}.\)

Including internal utility, gadget \(i\) contributes

\(\displaystyle g_i(\xi_i)=\xi_i+2\max\{\xi_i-a_i/2,0\} =2\xi_i-\min\{\xi_i,a_i-\xi_i\}.\)

For \(\xi_i\leq a_i/2\), both expressions equal \(\xi_i\); for \(\xi_i\geq a_i/2\), both equal \(3\xi_i-a_i\).

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \widehat V_{\mathrm{optimistic}}(\xi)=2\sum_i\xi_i-\sum_i d_i.\)

Set \(\rho=2K/D\). The unnormalized threshold gap is

\(\displaystyle 2K-\widehat V_{\mathrm{optimistic}}(\xi) =2\left(K-\sum_i\xi_i\right)+\sum_i d_i.\)

Again, all terms are nonnegative. Reaching the threshold is equivalent to \(\sum_i\xi_i=K\) and \(\xi_i\in\{0,a_i\}\) for every \(i\), which is equivalent to a YES answer to Subset Sum.

This proves optimistic \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

Encoding and structural restrictions

The pessimistic construction has \(\tau=3n+2\); the optimistic construction has \(\tau=4n+2\). Each has a tree consisting of the center and its gadget branches, together with the isolated padding type. Both internal graphs are stars.

The binary length of \(D\) is \(O(\log(K+S))\). Every mass and threshold therefore has polynomial encoding length, and each construction is computable in polynomial time in the source input length. These are polynomial many-one reductions.

5. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Real-valued leader strategies require a rational-witness argument.

Write \(x=x^p\), and let \(A\) be the type-edge incidence matrix of the external graph. Let \(a\) and \(b\) be hospital \(p\)’s internal and external benefit vectors. The residual vector \(r(x)\) is an affine rational function of \(x\), because opponents’ allocations are fixed rational input.

If there are no external edges, the decision problem is simply rational linear-program feasibility with \(a^\top x\geq\rho\). Hence assume \(E^I\neq\varnothing\).

Primary optimality certificates

For fixed feasible \(x\), the primary follower LP and its dual are

\(\displaystyle F(x)=\max\{\mathbf1^\top y:Ay\leq r(x),\ y\geq0\},\)

\(\displaystyle F(x)=\min\{r(x)^\top z:A^\top z\geq\mathbf1,\ z\geq0\}.\)

The primal is nonempty, since \(y=0\) is feasible, and compact: every edge amount is bounded by its endpoint residuals. Strong LP duality therefore applies and both optima are attained.

Define \(P=\{z\geq0:A^\top z\geq\mathbf1\}\). This rational polyhedron is nonempty and contains no affine line, because it lies in the nonnegative orthant. Thus an optimal dual solution can be chosen as a vertex of \(P\).

For feasible \(x,y,z\), the equality

\(\displaystyle \mathbf1^\top y=r(x)^\top z\)

certifies that \(y\in Y^*(x)\), by weak duality.

Optimistic certificates

An optimistic certificate consists of rational \(x,y,z\) satisfying:

  • internal feasibility of \(x\);
  • \(y\geq0\) and \(Ay\leq r(x)\);
  • \(z\geq0\) and \(A^\top z\geq\mathbf1\);
  • \(\mathbf1^\top y=r(x)^\top z\);
  • \(a^\top x+b^\top y\geq\rho\).

These conditions directly imply an optimistic YES answer.

To establish polynomial certificate size, start with any real YES witness and fix an optimal vertex \(z\) of \(P\). With \(z\) fixed, all displayed conditions form a nonempty rational polytope in \((x,y)\), hence have a polynomial-size rational solution.

Here and below, the size bound follows from the standard rational-polyhedron bound: a vertex is determined by linearly independent tight constraints, and Cramer’s rule bounds its coordinate encoding lengths polynomially in the constraint-system encoding length.

Pessimistic certificates

For fixed \(x\), pessimistic external utility is

\(\displaystyle \min\{b^\top y:Ay\leq r(x),\ y\geq0,\ \mathbf1^\top y=F(x)\}.\)

Its dual is

\(\displaystyle \max\{\alpha F(x)-\lambda^\top r(x): \lambda\geq0,\ \alpha\mathbf1-A^\top\lambda\leq b\},\)

where \(\alpha\) is unrestricted.

The primal feasible set is the nonempty compact primary-optimal face, so strong duality applies. Define

\(\displaystyle Q=\{(\lambda,\alpha):\lambda\geq0,\ \alpha\mathbf1-A^\top\lambda\leq b\}.\)

This polyhedron is nonempty: \(\lambda=0\) and \(\alpha=\min_e b_e\) are feasible. It contains no affine line. Indeed, nonnegativity forces any line direction to have zero \(\lambda\)-component, and any external-edge inequality then forces its \(\alpha\)-component to be zero. Thus an optimal dual solution can be chosen as a vertex of \(Q\).

A pessimistic certificate consists of rational \(x,y,z,\lambda,\alpha\), satisfying internal and external feasibility, primary dual feasibility and equality, secondary dual feasibility, and

\(\displaystyle a^\top x+\alpha\,\mathbf1^\top y-\lambda^\top r(x)\geq\rho.\)

To check sufficiency explicitly, every \(y'\in Y^*(x)\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle b^\top y'\geq\alpha\,\mathbf1^\top y'-\lambda^\top Ay' \geq\alpha F(x)-\lambda^\top r(x).\)

The first inequality uses secondary dual feasibility and \(y'\geq0\); the second uses \(Ay'\leq r(x)\) and \(\lambda\geq0\). Hence the certificate guarantees utility at least \(\rho\) for every primary optimum.

For polynomial certificate size, start from a real pessimistic YES witness and fix optimal vertices \(z\in P\) and \((\lambda,\alpha)\in Q\). Strong duality makes the certificate inequality hold at that witness. With these vertices fixed, the remaining conditions are a nonempty rational polytope in \((x,y)\), so admit a polynomial-size rational solution.

All certificate conditions are verifiable by exact rational arithmetic in \((m+\tau+L)^{O(1)}\) time. Both problems therefore belong to \(\mathrm{NP}\).

6. Exact running time

Let \(e=|E^I|\) and \(N=\tau+e\). The polyhedra \(P\) and \(Q\) have respectively \(\tau\) and \(\tau+1\) variables, and each has \(N\) inequalities.

Enumerating sets of independent tight inequalities lists all vertices using at most \(N^\tau\) candidate systems for \(P\), and \(N^{\tau+1}\) for \(Q\). Their coordinate bit lengths are polynomial in \(\tau+L\).

For the optimistic rule, enumerate vertices \(z\in P\) and solve the corresponding certificate-feasibility LP in \((x,y)\). For the pessimistic rule, enumerate pairs of vertices from \(P\) and \(Q\) and solve the corresponding certificate-feasibility LP. Section 5 proves that these enumerations are complete, and that every accepted LP supplies a valid witness.

Thus a common deterministic running-time bound is

\(\displaystyle N^{2\tau+1}(m+\tau+L)^{O(1)} =2^{O(\tau\log(\tau+1))}(m+L)^{O(1)},\)

because the explicitly supplied loop-free graph has \(e\leq\binom{\tau}{2}\).

Together with the two reductions, this establishes the claimed \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness for both rules. The reductions use binary-encoded Subset Sum and establish ordinary \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness; no claim of strong \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness is needed.
