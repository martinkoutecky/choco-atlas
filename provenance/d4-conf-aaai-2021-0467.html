The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), community labels \(c(t)\), rational transmission rates \(\kappa_{st}\), a seed budget \(\beta\), and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), let \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_t x_t\le\beta\). Define limiting reached fractions by \(y_t=1-(1-x_t/\mu_t)\exp(-\sum_s\mu_s\kappa_{st}y_s)\), taking the least fixed point, and set \(\mu_c=\sum_{t:c(t)=c}\mu_t\), \(u_c(x)=\sum_{t:c(t)=c}\mu_t y_t/\mu_c\), and \(W_\alpha(x)=\sum_c\mu_c u_c(x)^\alpha/\alpha\). Does this typed-population welfare objective retain a monotone diminishing-returns property that yields a polynomial-time \(1-1/e\)-approximation to its maximum, with running time measured in \(\tau=|T|\) and the input bit length?

The remaining inputs are binary-rational transmission intensities \(\lambda_{st}\geq0\), a binary-rational seed budget \(\beta\in[0,1]\), and binary-rational accuracy \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\); \(L\) is the total input bit length. Intensities may be asymmetric and may have nonzero diagonal entries.
A feasible allocation has real coordinates \(0\leq x_t\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_t\leq\beta\). Set \(q_t=x_t/\mu_t\), \(a_t^{(0)}=q_t\), and \(a_t^{(r+1)}=q_t+(1-q_t)\left(1-\exp\left(-\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s\lambda_{st}a_s^{(r)}\right)\right)\). Define \(a_t=\lim_{r\to\infty}a_t^{(r)}\), using this initialization even when other fixed points exist.

Does an algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\epsilon)\) return a feasible binary-rational allocation \(x\) satisfying \(W(x)\geq(1-1/e)\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon\) for every instance?

Result

Yes. There is a polynomial-time algorithm returning a feasible binary-rational allocation \(x\) with the stronger guarantee

\(W(x)\geq \sqrt{1-1/e}\,\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon.\)

The proof uses a convex relaxation and a rounding procedure that ensures every type receives a strictly positive seed whenever \(\beta>0\). Strict positivity is what makes the relaxation compatible with the specified initialization.

1. Fixed points and a comparison lemma

Write \(B_{ts}=\mu_s\lambda_{st}\), so that \((Ba)_t=\sum_s\mu_s\lambda_{st}a_s\). For \(q\in[0,1]^\tau\), define

\(F_q(a)_t=1-(1-q_t)\exp(-(Ba)_t).\)

The prescribed iteration starts at \(a^{(0)}=q\). Since \(B\) has nonnegative entries, \(F_q\) is coordinatewise nondecreasing. Also, \(F_q(q)\geq q\), and \(F_q\) maps \([0,1]^\tau\) into itself. Consequently, the iterates increase coordinatewise to a limit \(a(q)\). Continuity gives \(F_q(a(q))=a(q)\).

For every fixed point \(d\in[0,1]^\tau\), we have \(d=F_q(d)\geq q\). Induction then gives \(a^{(r)}\leq d\), so \(a(q)\) is the least fixed point.

We need a stronger comparison when every seed coordinate is positive.

Lemma. Suppose \(q_t>0\) for every \(t\). If \(b\in[0,1]^\tau\) satisfies \(b\leq F_q(b)\), then \(b\leq a(q)\).

Proof. Put \(a=a(q)\). Since \(a\geq q\), every coordinate of \(a\) is positive. Suppose, contrary to the claim, that

\(r=\max_t b_t/a_t>1.\)

Choose \(i\) attaining this maximum. Each coordinate of \(F_q\) is concave as a function of its activation argument, and the same formula defines a nondecreasing map on the entire nonnegative orthant. Concavity applied to \(a=r^{-1}(ra)+(1-r^{-1})0\) gives

\(F_q(ra)\leq rF_q(a)-(r-1)F_q(0)=ra-(r-1)q.\)

Because \(b\leq ra\), we obtain

\(b_i\leq F_q(b)_i\leq F_q(ra)_i\leq ra_i-(r-1)q_i<ra_i=b_i,\)

a contradiction. This proves the lemma.

2. A convex relaxation

For \(y,b\in[0,1]^\tau\), define the exponential-hazard map

\(H_y(b)_t=1-\exp(-y_t-(Bb)_t).\)

Introduce variables \(v_c\in[0,1]\), one per community, and let \(K\) be the set of triples \((y,b,v)\) satisfying

  • \(0\leq y_t,b_t\leq1\) and \(0\leq v_c\leq1\);
  • \(\sum_t\mu_ty_t\leq\beta\);
  • \(b_t-1+\exp(-y_t-(Bb)_t)\leq0\), for every \(t\);
  • \(v_c^2-\mu_c^{-1}\sum_{t:c(t)=c}\mu_tb_t\leq0\), for every \(c\).

Every constraint is convex: the exponential has an affine argument, and the last constraint is a convex quadratic minus a linear function. Thus \(K\) is compact and convex.

Consider the linear objective

\(C(y,b,v)=2\sum_c\mu_cv_c,\qquad V=\max_{(y,b,v)\in K}C(y,b,v).\)

Since \(v_c\leq1\) and \(\sum_c\mu_c=1\), we have \(V\leq2\).

If \((y,b,v)\in K\) has every \(y_t>0\), then \(x_t=\mu_ty_t\) is feasible for the original problem. Moreover, \(1-e^{-y_t}\leq y_t\), so

\(b\leq H_y(b)\leq F_y(b).\)

The lemma implies \(b\leq a(y)\). Hence

\(W(x)\geq2\sum_c\mu_c\sqrt{\mu_c^{-1}\sum_{t:c(t)=c}\mu_tb_t}\geq C(y,b,v).\)

The qualification \(y_t>0\) matters: at \(y=0\), the convex inequalities can admit positive activation vectors although the prescribed iteration remains identically zero. Section 4 ensures strict positivity.

3. The relaxation has a sufficient approximation value

Let \(\alpha=1-1/e\). We prove

\(V\geq\sqrt{\alpha}\,\mathrm{OPT}.\)

Take any feasible original allocation, with seed fractions \(q\) and prescribed limit \(a=a(q)\). Concavity of \(z\mapsto1-e^{-z}\) on \([0,1]\) gives

\(1-e^{-q_t}\geq\alpha q_t.\)

For a fixed coordinate \(t\), put \(h=(Ba)_t\geq0\), and consider

\(f(s)=F_{sq}(sa)_t=1-(1-sq_t)e^{-sh},\qquad 0\leq s\leq1.\)

Its second derivative is

\(f''(s)=-e^{-sh}\bigl(h^2(1-sq_t)+2hq_t\bigr)\leq0.\)

Since \(f(0)=0\), concavity implies

\(F_{\alpha q}(\alpha a)_t=f(\alpha)\geq\alpha f(1)=\alpha a_t.\)

The map \(F_q\) is nondecreasing in its seed argument. Therefore,

\(H_q(\alpha a)=F_{1-e^{-q}}(\alpha a)\geq F_{\alpha q}(\alpha a)\geq\alpha a.\)

Thus the triple defined by

\(y=q,\qquad b=\alpha a,\qquad v_c=\sqrt{\alpha u_c(x)}\)

belongs to \(K\), and its objective is \(\sqrt{\alpha}W(x)\). Taking the supremum over feasible original allocations proves the claimed bound on \(V\). No attainment of \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is needed.

4. Polynomial-time optimization and exact feasibility

We now justify the computational step, including its dependence on small masses and budgets.

If \(\beta=0\), return \(x=0\). The prescribed iteration is then zero, so \(\mathrm{OPT}=0\). Assume henceforth that \(\beta>0\).

Let

\(n=2\tau+m,\qquad H=1+\max_t\sum_sB_{ts},\qquad G=8H,\qquad \sigma=\beta/32.\)

Represent all the inequalities defining \(K\) as \(g_j(z)\leq0\), where \(z=(y,b,v)\). Define the rational point \(z_0\) by

\(y_t=\beta/2,\qquad b_t=\beta/8,\qquad v_c=\beta/16.\)

Every defining inequality has slack at least \(\sigma\) at \(z_0\). For the exponential constraints, use

\(1-e^{-\beta/2}\geq\beta/2-\beta^2/8\geq3\beta/8,\)

which gives

\(g_t(z_0)\leq\beta/8-1+e^{-\beta/2}\leq-\beta/4.\)

For the community constraints,

\(g_c(z_0)=\beta^2/256-\beta/8\leq-31\beta/256.\)

The box and budget constraints also have slack at least \(\sigma\).

On the box \(\Omega=[-1/H,1+1/H]^n\), every \(g_j\) is \(G\)-Lipschitz in Euclidean norm. Indeed, \(y_t+(Bb)_t\geq-1\) there, so the exponential is at most \(e<3\). The exponential constraint’s gradient has norm at most \(1+3H\), the community constraint’s gradient has norm at most \(5\), and the affine constraint gradients have norm at most \(1\).

It follows that

\(B_2(z_0,\sigma/G)\subseteq K\subseteq B_2(z_0,n).\)

Both radii and the center have polynomial encoding length.

A polynomial-time weak separation oracle

For completeness, the transcendental constraints permit a weak separation oracle in the ordinary binary model.

Given a rational query \(z\) and tolerance \(\delta>0\), first separate any violated box constraint exactly. Otherwise, \(z\in[0,1]^n\). Set

\(\nu=\min\{\sigma/6,\delta\sigma/(6n)\}.\)

Approximate every \(g_j(z)\) within \(\nu\), and every gradient coordinate within \(\nu/(2n)\).

If an approximate constraint value exceeds \(2\nu\), its true value exceeds \(\nu\). Convexity gives, for every \(w\in K\),

\(\nabla g_j(z)\cdot(w-z)\leq-g_j(z)<-\nu.\)

Replacing the gradient by its rational approximation changes this inner product by at most \(\nu/2\), because \(\|w-z\|_1\leq n\). The approximate gradient therefore supplies a strict rational separating hyperplane.

If no approximate value exceeds \(2\nu\), every true value is at most \(3\nu\). With \(\theta_0=3\nu/\sigma\leq1/2\), convexity shows that

\((1-\theta_0)z+\theta_0z_0\in K.\)

Its distance from \(z\) is at most \(3\nu n/\sigma\leq\delta\), so the oracle may certify approximate membership.

These computations take polynomial time. At queries inside the unit box, the only transcendental quantity is \(e^{-h}\) for a nonnegative rational \(h\). To obtain absolute error \(2^{-p}\), sufficiently large \(h\) can be replaced by zero; otherwise, range reduction, Taylor approximation on \([0,1]\), and repeated squaring use polynomially many bit operations. Approximating gradients merely requires extra precision proportional to the encoding length of \(B\).

The standard weak separation-to-optimization theorem now applies: for a compact convex body with known inner and outer balls and a polynomial-time weak separation oracle, linear weak optimization is polynomial-time. In the form used here, it returns a rational point within distance \(\eta\) of \(K\), whose objective is within \(\eta\) of the maximum. This is the formulation in Sections 1–3 of [Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver, *The ellipsoid method and its consequences in combinatorial optimization*](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf).

Choose

\(\eta=\epsilon\sigma/(64G),\qquad \theta=2G\eta/\sigma=\epsilon/32.\)

Obtain a rational \(z\) with

\(\operatorname{dist}(z,K)\leq\eta,\qquad C(z)\geq V-\eta.\)

Because \(\eta<1/H\), this point lies in \(\Omega\). Lipschitz continuity gives \(g_j(z)\leq G\eta\). Set

\(\widehat z=(1-\theta)z+\theta z_0.\)

For every defining constraint, convexity yields

\(g_j(\widehat z)\leq(1-\theta)G\eta-\theta\sigma\leq-G\eta.\)

Thus \(\widehat z=(\widehat y,\widehat b,\widehat v)\) is exactly feasible and has positive seed coordinates. Also, since \(C(z_0)\geq0\) and \(V\leq2\),

\(C(\widehat z)\geq(1-\theta)(V-\eta)\geq V-2\theta-\eta\geq V-\epsilon.\)

5. Binary-rational output and the guarantee

To ensure a dyadic output even if intermediate rational denominators are arbitrary, let \(\mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t\). Choose a positive dyadic number \(h\) satisfying

\(h\leq\mu_{\min}\eta/(4n),\)

and return

\(x_t=h\left\lfloor\mu_t\widehat y_t/h\right\rfloor.\)

Rounding down preserves \(x_t\leq\mu_t\) and \(\sum_tx_t\leq\beta\). Put \(q_t=x_t/\mu_t\). Then

\(0\leq\widehat y_t-q_t<h/\mu_t\leq\eta/(4n).\)

Consequently, replacing \(\widehat y\) by \(q\) changes the full variable vector by Euclidean norm at most \(\eta/4\). The constraint margins and the Lipschitz bound imply that \((q,\widehat b,\widehat v)\) remains in \(K\).

The lower-box constraint margin gives \(\widehat y_t\geq G\eta\), so every rounded \(q_t\) is still strictly positive. The comparison lemma therefore applies, giving

\(W(x)\geq C(q,\widehat b,\widehat v)=C(\widehat z)\geq V-\epsilon\geq\sqrt{1-1/e}\,\mathrm{OPT}-\epsilon.\)

All precision parameters have polynomial encoding length: the logarithms of \(H\), \(1/\beta\), and \(1/\mu_{\min}\) are polynomially bounded by the input length. Hence optimization, repair, and rounding take time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\), and therefore satisfy the requested polynomial bound.

Finally, \(\sqrt{1-1/e}\geq1-1/e\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}\geq0\), proving the requested guarantee for every instance.
