Theorem 7 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given item classes \(J=[d]\) with rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\ge 0\), a finite set of additive valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and comparison-oracle access to each type’s valuation \(v_t\), compute a finite-support rational assignment \(x_{t,b}\ge 0\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^d\). Require \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_j=q_j\) for every class \(j\); every bundle with positive assigned mass must satisfy \(v_t\cdot(b+e_j)\ge v_t\cdot q\) for some class with positive supply, and \(v_t\cdot b\ge\frac12\mathrm{MMS}_t(q)\). Here \(\mathrm{MMS}_t(q)\) is the maximum \(z\) for which a distribution over integral bundles has expectation \(q\) and every bundle in its support has value at least \(z\) under \(v_t\). Determine the comparison-query and running-time complexity in \(d\), \(|T|\), and the input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Each type has a hidden additive valuation vector \(v_t\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^d\). A comparison query presents two binary-encoded integral bundles \(a,b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d\) and returns one of greater value, choosing arbitrarily on equality; no numerical values or fractional-bundle queries are available.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Polynomial-time construction and stronger query lower bounds

The assignment is computable deterministically in polynomial bit time, with
$$ O\!\left(\tau m\log(m+1)\right) $$
comparison queries.

The optimal query complexity remains unresolved. The lower bounds can, however, be strengthened substantially beyond those in the notes: for $m\ge2$, the worst case requires
$$ \Omega\!\left(m+\tau\log(m+1)\right) $$
comparisons. These bounds hold even when queries may use arbitrarily large integral coefficients.

The exceptional cases are completely settled: $m=1$ requires no comparisons, and $m=2$ has worst-case complexity $\Theta(\tau)$.

All arguments below concern the mass-society model directly; they do not apply the [cited finite-society theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18133v2).

1. Reduction to a single-type lottery

Write
$$ J=\{j:q_j>0\}. $$
This set is nonempty by hypothesis.

Suppose that, for each type $t$, we construct a finite rational probability distribution $p_t$ on integral bundles with mean $q$, satisfying both required inequalities. Setting
$$ x_{t,b}=\mu_t p_t(b) $$
then gives
$$ \sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_j =\sum_t\mu_t q_j=q_j. $$
Thus the types may be handled independently.

For the single-type construction, fix a valuation $v\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m$, and define
$$ V=v\cdot q, \qquad M=\max_{j\in J}v_j. $$
The PROP1 requirement is equivalent to
$$ v\cdot b+M\ge V. $$
Indeed, a class attaining $M$ belongs to $J$ and supplies the required hypothetical copy.

We will sometimes describe comparisons between nonnegative rational vectors. These are legal abbreviations: multiplying both vectors by a common positive denominator gives integral query bundles with the same comparison outcome. Each returned side certifies a weak inequality, including under arbitrary equality responses.

2. A threshold bounding distributional MMS

For nonnegative rational supplies $s$ and positive rational population mass $r$, define
$$ U(s,r)=\frac{v\cdot s}{r} $$
and
$$ \mathcal T(s,r)= \max\left\{ z\ge0: \sum_j s_j\min(v_j,z)\ge rz \right\}. $$

The maximum exists. The feasible set contains zero and is closed, and every feasible threshold satisfies
$$ rz\le\sum_j s_j\min(v_j,z)\le v\cdot s. $$
It is therefore contained in the compact interval $[0,U(s,r)]$.

Set
$$ \Theta=\mathcal T(q,1). $$

Lemma 1: MMS upper bound

For every valuation $v$,
$$ \mathrm{MMS}_v(q)\le\Theta. $$

Proof. Suppose a distribution with mean $q$ guarantees value at least $z$ on its support. Every supported integral bundle satisfies
$$ \sum_j b_j\min(v_j,z)\ge z. $$
If the bundle contains a copy worth at least $z$, that copy contributes $z$ to the truncated sum. Otherwise truncation leaves its value unchanged.

Taking expectations gives
$$ \sum_j q_j\min(v_j,z)\ge z. $$
The expectations are legitimate because there are finitely many coordinates, each with finite prescribed mean. Hence $z\le\Theta$. Taking the supremum over feasible $z$ proves the claim. $\square$

Singleton removal

Sort the active classes by nonincreasing unit value, using comparisons of unit bundles. Start with $s=q$ and $r=1$.

While supplies remain, let $h$ be a maximum-valued remaining class.

  • If $s_h\ge r$, stop.
  • Otherwise compare $e_h$ with $s/r$.
  • If the oracle returns $s/r$, stop.
  • If it returns $e_h$, assign singleton $e_h$ to population mass $s_h$, replace $r$ by $r-s_h$, and set $s_h=0$.

Every removal leaves $r>0$. There are at most $m$ removals.

Lemma 2: Removal preserves the threshold

Every removal preserves $\mathcal T(s,r)$.

Proof. At a removal,
$$ s_h<r, \qquad v_h\ge U(s,r). $$
For the new supplies $s'$ and mass $r'=r-s_h$,
$$ U(s',r') = \frac{rU(s,r)-s_hv_h}{r-s_h} \le U(s,r). $$
For every $z\in[0,U(s,r)]$, we have $\min(v_h,z)=z$, so
$$ \sum_j s_j\min(v_j,z)\ge rz \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_j s'_j\min(v_j,z)\ge r'z. $$
Both feasible sets lie in this interval: the old one by its average bound, and the new one because its average does not increase. Thus the feasible sets coincide. $\square$

At termination, every remaining class satisfies
$$ v_j\le U(s,r). $$
If a comparison caused termination, this follows because $h$ was maximum-valued. If $s_h\ge r$, nonnegativity gives
$$ U(s,r)\ge\frac{s_hv_h}{r}\ge v_h. $$
If no supplies remain, the assertion is vacuous.

Consequently, truncation changes nothing at $z=U(s,r)$, and Lemma 2 yields
$$ \Theta=U(s,r), \qquad v\cdot s=r\Theta, \qquad v_j\le\Theta\quad\text{whenever }s_j>0. $$
Every removed singleton has value at least $\Theta$, since its value was at least the current average, which bounds the preserved threshold.

Finally, if any removal occurred, its first removed class satisfied $v_h\ge V$. Therefore
$$ M\ge V. $$
In that situation every nonnegative bundle automatically satisfies PROP1.

3. A finite rational rounding lemma

Lemma 3

Let $a\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^k$, with corresponding values
$$ w_1\ge\cdots\ge w_k\ge0. $$
One can construct, without further valuation queries, a rational distribution on at most $k+1$ integral bundles with mean $a$, every supported bundle having value at least
$$ \sum_{j=1}^k a_jw_j-w_1. $$

Proof. Put
$$ C_0=0, \qquad C_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^j a_\ell. $$
Let $g$ equal $w_j$ on $[C_{j-1},C_j)$ and zero on $[C_k,\infty)$. It is nonincreasing.

For $u\in[0,1)$, place one copy in the bundle for each point $u+\ell$, with $\ell$ a nonnegative integer, lying in a class interval. The resulting counts are
$$ b_j(u)= \left\lceil C_j-u\right\rceil - \left\lceil C_{j-1}-u\right\rceil. $$
These counts are nonnegative integers and can be computed directly.

Averaging over $u$ gives
$$ \int_0^1 b_j(u)\,du=C_j-C_{j-1}=a_j. $$
For example, this follows by integrating the indicators of the points $u+\ell$ lying in $[C_{j-1},C_j)$; together, their integration domains cover that interval.

By monotonicity,
$$ \sum_{\ell\ge0}g(u+\ell) \ge\int_u^\infty g(x)\,dx \ge\int_0^\infty g(x)\,dx-w_1. $$
This is the claimed value bound.

The bundle changes only at fractional parts of the prefix sums $C_j$. Partitioning $[0,1)$ at these rational breakpoints gives at most $k+1$ intervals. On each interval, evaluate the bundle at a rational midpoint and assign probability equal to the interval length. This produces the required finite rational distribution. $\square$

For $k=0$, the analogous construction is the empty bundle with probability one.

4. Completing the polynomial-time construction

Consider the residual supplies $s$ and population mass $r$ after singleton removal.

For each remaining class, compare $2e_j$ with $s/r$. Let $A$ contain those classes for which the oracle returns $2e_j$. Then
$$ j\in A\Longrightarrow v_j\ge\frac{\Theta}{2}, \qquad j\notin A\Longrightarrow v_j\le\frac{\Theta}{2}. $$
Either classification is valid on equality. Put
$$ B=\sum_{j\in A}s_j. $$

Case 1: $B\ge r$

Choose rational quantities $c_j$, supported on $A$, with
$$ 0\le c_j\le s_j, \qquad \sum_jc_j=r. $$
A greedy choice suffices. Assign singleton $e_j$ to population mass $c_j$. Every recipient thereby gets value at least $\Theta/2$.

Distribute the leftover supplies $s-c$ to these same recipients as follows. Parametrize them by $u\in[0,1)$, with population mass $r\,du$. Give singleton labels on intervals of lengths $c_j/r$. Write
$$ \frac{s_j-c_j}{r}=k_j+f_j, \qquad k_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}, \quad 0\le f_j<1. $$
Give everyone $k_j$ additional copies of class $j$, and one more when $u<f_j$.

The singleton-label boundaries and the thresholds $f_j$ give only $O(m)$ intervals. Their bundles and rational masses consume exactly $s$. Nonnegative valuations preserve the singleton value guarantee.

Case 2: $B<r$

Assign singleton $e_j$ to mass $s_j$ for every $j\in A$. The remaining mass is
$$ R=r-B>0, $$
with supplies consisting of the classes outside $A$.

Its average value is
$$ U_{\mathrm{small}} = \frac{\sum_{j\notin A}s_jv_j}{R} = \frac{r\Theta-\sum_{j\in A}s_jv_j}{r-B} \ge\Theta, $$
because $v_j\le\Theta$ for every residual class.

Apply Lemma 3 to the small-class supplies divided by $R$, in their known value order. Their maximum value $w$ satisfies $w\le\Theta/2$. Hence every resulting bundle has value at least
$$ U_{\mathrm{small}}-w\ge\frac{\Theta}{2}. $$
If no small classes remain, the preceding inequality forces $\Theta=0$, and the empty bundle is valid.

Fairness verification

If singleton removal occurred, then $M\ge V$, so every constructed bundle satisfies PROP1.

Otherwise $\Theta=V$. A bundle containing an assigned singleton from $A$ satisfies
$$ v\cdot b+M \ge v_j+M \ge2v_j \ge\Theta=V. $$
A rounded small-class bundle satisfies
$$ v\cdot b+M \ge U_{\mathrm{small}}-w+M \ge U_{\mathrm{small}} \ge\Theta=V, $$
since $M\ge w$.

Every bundle also has value at least $\Theta/2$, including the initially removed singletons. Lemma 1 therefore gives
$$ v\cdot b\ge\frac{\Theta}{2} \ge\frac12\mathrm{MMS}_v(q). $$

The assigned masses sum to one, and their consumption is exactly $q$. Applying this construction independently to every type completes the assignment.

Query and bit complexity

For each type, the algorithm uses:

  • $O(m\log(m+1))$ comparisons to sort unit values;
  • at most $m$ comparisons during singleton removal;
  • at most $m$ comparisons to classify residual classes.

Sorting remains valid under arbitrary equality responses: every comparison agrees with the weak numerical ordering of the actual values.

There are $O(m)$ output bundles per type. To check their encoding lengths, let $D$ be the product of the input supply denominators. Its bit length is $O(L)$. Every positive residual population mass $r$ or $R$ is an integer multiple of $1/D$, and thus is at least $1/D$. All supplies divided by these masses have polynomial encoding length.

The remaining operations—prefix sums, ceilings, fractional parts, interval lengths, and multiplication by $\mu_t$—preserve polynomial encoding length. Integral query bundles are obtained by denominator clearing. Bundle multiplicities are computed in binary without enumerating copies.

Thus the total query count is
$$ O\!\left(\tau m\log(m+1)\right), $$
and all bit operations, query encoding, and output encoding take polynomial time in $m,\tau,L$.

5. A stronger lower bound: $\Omega(\tau\log m)$

Fix an integer $k$ with $2\le k\le m$, and take
$$ q_j=\frac1k\quad\text{for every }j, \qquad \mu_t=\frac1\tau\quad\text{for every }t. $$
Each hidden valuation will be the indicator of a $k$-element set $H_t$:
$$ v_{t,j}= \begin{cases} 1,&j\in H_t,\\ 0,&j\notin H_t. \end{cases} $$

The MMS of these valuations

For every such valuation,
$$ \mathrm{MMS}_t(q)=1. $$
The expected value is exactly $k/k=1$, giving the upper bound.

For attainability, choose one class uniformly from $H_t$, and independently include each class outside $H_t$ with probability $1/k$. This finite distribution has mean $q$ and value exactly one on every bundle.

Consequently, every qualifying bundle must satisfy
$$ \sum_{j\in H_t}b_j\ge1, $$
because its value is integral and must be at least $1/2$.

Independent adversaries for the types

For each type maintain a set $S_t$, initially all $m$ indices, such that every $k$-element subset of $S_t$ remains a possible valued set.

For a query to type $t$, let $c=a-b$.

If $|S_t|\ge2k$, retain whichever of
$$ \{j\in S_t:c_j\ge0\}, \qquad \{j\in S_t:c_j<0\} $$
is larger. Answer in favor of $a$ for the first set, and $b$ for the second. Every $k$-element subset of the retained set is consistent with this answer.

If $|S_t|<2k$, fix any $k$-element subset of $S_t$ and answer this and subsequent queries truthfully for that fixed valuation.

Each query reduces the maintained set size by at most a factor of two. If $K_t$ queries have been directed to type $t$, then
$$ |S_t|\ge m\,2^{-K_t}. $$
The possible valuations for different types remain independent.

Resource conservation forces many queries

At termination, every positive-mass bundle assigned to type $t$ must intersect every $k$-element subset of $S_t$. Otherwise a possible hidden valuation would give it value zero.

Thus the bundle has at most $k-1$ zero coordinates within $S_t$, and integrality implies
$$ \sum_j b_j\ge |S_t|-k+1. $$
The total supply is $m/k$. Therefore
$$ \frac{m}{k} \ge \frac1\tau\sum_t\bigl(|S_t|-k+1\bigr), $$
or
$$ \frac1\tau\sum_t|S_t| \le\frac{m}{k}+k-1. $$

Let $N=\sum_tK_t$ be the total number of queries. Convexity of the exponential gives
$$ \frac1\tau\sum_t|S_t| \ge \frac{m}{\tau}\sum_t2^{-K_t} \ge m\,2^{-N/\tau}. $$
Combining the inequalities yields the explicit lower bound
$$ N\ge \tau\log_2\left( \frac{m}{m/k+k-1} \right). $$

Choose $k=\lceil\sqrt m\rceil$. Then
$$ \frac{m}{k}+k-1<2\sqrt m, $$
so
$$ N> \tau\left(\frac12\log_2m-1\right). $$
In particular, for $m\ge16$,
$$ N\ge\frac{\tau}{4}\log_2m. $$
This proves $\Omega(\tau\log m)$.

The proof places no restriction on query coefficient sizes.

6. A linear lower bound, even with identical valuations

This bound uses the same supplies $q_j=1/k$, but all types have the same unknown valued set $H$, of size $k$.

For a fixed output, aggregate its positive masses into a probability distribution $p$ on bundles. It has mean $q$.

If the output is valid for $H$, every supported bundle contains at least one valued copy. Its expected number of valued copies is
$$ \sum_{j\in H}q_j=1. $$
Hence every positive-mass bundle must contain exactly one valued copy:
$$ \sum_{j\in H}b_j=1. $$

We next bound how many valued sets can be compatible with one output.

Lemma 4: Counting compatible valued sets

A finite distribution with mean $(1/k,\ldots,1/k)$ can satisfy
$$ \sum_{j\in H}b_j=1 $$
on every supported bundle for at most
$$ \left(\frac{m}{k}\right)^k $$
different $k$-element sets $H$.

Proof. Let $\mathcal F$ be the family of compatible sets. If it is empty, there is nothing to prove.

Let $E$ consist of those coordinates satisfying $b_j\le1$ on every supported bundle. Every $H\in\mathcal F$ is contained in $E$, since a valued coordinate with multiplicity at least two would violate the displayed equality.

For each supported bundle define
$$ A_b=\{j\in E:b_j=1\}. $$
Every compatible set intersects $A_b$ in exactly one coordinate. Moreover, for each $j\in E$,
$$ \sum_b p(b)\mathbf 1_{\{j\in A_b\}} =\mathbb E_p[b_j]=\frac1k. $$

Choose $H$ uniformly from $\mathcal F$, and let $X_j$ be its membership indicators on $E$. Write $\mathsf H$ for Shannon entropy in bits.

We use three elementary entropy facts: the chain rule, reduction of conditional entropy under additional conditioning, and the bound $\mathsf H(Y)\le\log_2 r$ for a variable with at most $r$ possible outcomes. The chain rule follows by factoring joint probabilities. Conditioning reduction follows from concavity of entropy; the outcome bound follows from concavity of the logarithm.

Order the coordinates of $E$. For every subset $A\subseteq E$, the chain rule and conditioning reduction give
$$ \mathsf H(X_A) \ge \sum_{j\in A}\mathsf H(X_j\mid X_{<j}), $$
where $X_{<j}$ includes all earlier coordinates of $E$.

Multiply this inequality for $A=A_b$ by $kp(b)$ and sum over bundles. The covering identity above gives
$$ \begin{aligned} k\sum_b p(b)\mathsf H(X_{A_b}) &\ge \sum_{j\in E} \left(k\sum_b p(b)\mathbf 1_{\{j\in A_b\}}\right) \mathsf H(X_j\mid X_{<j})\\ &= \sum_{j\in E}\mathsf H(X_j\mid X_{<j})\\ &=\mathsf H(X_E). \end{aligned} $$

Since $X_{A_b}$ has exactly one nonzero coordinate,
$$ \mathsf H(X_{A_b})\le\log_2|A_b|. $$
Every $A_b$ is nonempty because $\mathcal F$ is nonempty. Concavity of the logarithm now gives
$$ \begin{aligned} \log_2|\mathcal F| =\mathsf H(X_E) &\le k\sum_b p(b)\log_2|A_b|\\ &\le k\log_2\left(\sum_b p(b)|A_b|\right)\\ &=k\log_2\left(\frac{|E|}{k}\right)\\ &\le k\log_2\left(\frac{m}{k}\right). \end{aligned} $$
Exponentiation proves the lemma. $\square$

Decision-tree lower bound

Fix a permissible deterministic tie rule, such as returning the first bundle on equality. There are
$$ \binom{m}{k} $$
possible identical valuations. An algorithm using at most $Q$ binary comparisons has at most $2^Q$ leaves.

By Lemma 4, each leaf’s output can be valid for at most $(m/k)^k$ of these valuations. Therefore
$$ Q\ge \log_2\binom{m}{k} - k\log_2\left(\frac{m}{k}\right). $$

For $m=2k$, this becomes
$$ Q\ge\log_2\binom{2k}{k}-k. $$
The central binomial coefficient is the largest of the $2k+1$ binomial coefficients whose sum is $2^{2k}$, so
$$ Q\ge k-\log_2(2k+1)=\Omega(m). $$
For odd $m$, use $m-1$ active classes and give the remaining class zero supply. This proves the same asymptotic bound.

Thus $\Omega(m)$ comparisons are necessary even with identical valuations and any number of supported types.

7. Exact edge cases and the remaining gap

One class

For $m=1$, let $q>0$ and $v\ge0$. Then
$$ \mathrm{MMS}_v(q)=v\lfloor q\rfloor. $$
The floor-and-ceiling lottery attains this value. If $v>0$, any strictly larger guaranteed threshold would require every bundle to contain at least $\lfloor q\rfloor+1$ copies, contradicting its mean. For $v=0$, the identity is immediate.

Give every type this lottery. Each bundle has at least $\lfloor q\rfloor$ copies and satisfies
$$ b+1\ge q. $$
Both fairness requirements follow without any comparison queries.

Two classes

The upper bound already gives $O(\tau)$ comparisons.

For a matching lower bound, take equal type masses and
$$ q=\left(\frac9{10},\frac9{10}\right). $$
Consider the two possible valuations
$$ v^{(1)}=(1,9), \qquad v^{(2)}=(9,1). $$
Both have MMS equal to $9$. For $v^{(1)}$, a lottery assigning $e_2$ with probability $9/10$ and $9e_1$ with probability $1/10$ has mean $q$ and constant value $9$. The expected-value upper bound is also $9$. The other valuation is symmetric.

Answer every query consistently with $v^{(1)}$. An unqueried type could have either valuation. Every bundle assigned to it must therefore satisfy both half-MMS inequalities. A bundle with fewer than two copies cannot do so: a singleton has value one under one of the valuations.

Every queried type must receive a nonempty bundle. If $U$ types remain unqueried, total consumption is therefore at least
$$ \frac{2U+(\tau-U)}{\tau} =1+\frac U\tau. $$
The actual total supply is $9/5$, forcing
$$ U\le\frac45\tau. $$
At least $\tau/5$ types must be queried. Hence the worst-case complexity for $m=2$ is $\Theta(\tau)$.

Established bounds

For $m\ge2$, combining the linear lower bound with the product lower bound gives
$$ \boxed{ \Omega\!\left(m+\tau\log(m+1)\right) \ \le\ \text{optimal worst-case query complexity} \ \le\ O\!\left(\tau m\log(m+1)\right). } $$
For bounded $m$, the two-class construction supplies the needed $\Omega(\tau)$ lower bound by adding zero-supply classes.

The upper bound holds for every explicit input length $L$. The lower bounds use input families of length
$$ O\!\left(m\log(m+1)+\tau\log(\tau+1)\right). $$
Thus they establish hardness already for polynomial-size rational inputs, without relying on large query encodings.

The positive polynomial-time classification is fully proved. The optimal dependence of query complexity on jointly growing $m$ and $\tau$ remains open.
