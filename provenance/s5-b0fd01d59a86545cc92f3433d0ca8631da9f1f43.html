The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\) over finite \(T\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\) for item classes \(j\in J\), and each type's personalized bivalued additive goods values \(v_{tj}\), compute finite-support masses \(x_{t,z}\ge 0\) on integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{J}\) such that \(\sum_z x_{t,z}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_j=q_j\), and \(x_{t,z}>0\) implies \(\sum_jv_{tj}z_j\ge\beta_t^G(q)\), where \(\beta_t^G(q)=\max_{\pi\in\Pi(q)}\min_{z\in\operatorname{supp}(\pi)}\sum_jv_{tj}z_j\) and \(\Pi(q)\) is the set of finite-support distributions over integral bundles with expected bundle vector \(q\).

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\ge1\) types \(t\in T\) with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|J|\ge0\) item classes with binary-rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\ge0\). Type \(t\) has additive values \(v_{tj}\in\{a_t,b_t\}\), where \(0\le a_t<b_t\) are binary rationals; either value may be unrealized. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Bundles are vectors \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{J}\). Let \(\Pi(q)\) contain all finite-support probability distributions \(\pi\) on these bundles, with real probabilities and \(\mathbb E_\pi[z]=q\), and define \(\beta_t^G(q)=\max_{\pi\in\Pi(q)}\min_{z\in\operatorname{supp}(\pi)}\sum_jv_{tj}z_j\).
Compute a finite list of bundles and binary-rational masses \(x_{t,z}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_zx_{t,z}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_j=q_j\), and \(x_{t,z}>0\Rightarrow\sum_jv_{tj}z_j\ge\beta_t^G(q)\), or report infeasibility. The benchmarks are defined by the input, not supplied separately; bundle coordinates are encoded in binary.

Is this exact allocation problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents and explicitly listed indivisible goods, personalized bivalued valuations always admit exact MMS allocations computable in polynomial time ([Zhang, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15822)).

Result and encoding convention

The exact allocation problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The algorithm computes the benchmarks from the input and returns an allocation of polynomial total encoding length, or correctly reports infeasibility.

Here “binary-rational” means a rational number represented by a numerator and denominator encoded in binary. Thus output denominators need not be powers of two.

The proof uses a polynomial-size description of each type’s acceptable-bundle convex hull. It does not require the finite-agent MMS theorem cited in the question.

1. Two standard algorithmic results

We use the following established results.

  • Integer hulls in fixed dimension. For fixed \(d\), given a bounded rational polytope \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), one can enumerate the vertices of \(\operatorname{conv}(P\cap\mathbb Z^d)\) in time polynomial in the binary encoding length of \(P\). This includes empty and lower-dimensional hulls. The polynomial vertex bound and enumeration algorithm are given in Cook–Hartmann–Kannan–McDiarmid, particularly Remark 2.4. [On integer points in polyhedra](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~bico/papers/ippoints.pdf)
  • Exact rational linear programming. An explicitly represented rational linear program can be tested for feasibility in polynomial time; when feasible, a rational feasible solution of polynomial encoding length can be computed.

All integer hulls below have dimension at most two. All linear programs below have polynomially many explicitly listed variables and constraints.

2. Adding surplus while retaining integer bundles

We first record a construction that will convert resource inequalities into equalities.

Lemma 1. Let \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), let \(\theta>0\), and let \(s\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\). An atom of mass \(\theta\) at \(z\) can be replaced by at most \(m+1\) atoms, each at an integer bundle coordinatewise greater than or equal to \(z\), whose total resource vector is \(\theta z+s\).

If \(\theta,s\) are rational, the replacement is computable with rational masses in polynomial time and has polynomial encoding length.

Proof. For each coordinate, write
\(s_j/\theta=h_j+f_j\), where \(h_j=\lfloor s_j/\theta\rfloor\) and \(0\le f_j<1\).

Sort the distinct numbers among \(0,1,f_1,\ldots,f_m\). They partition the unit interval into at most \(m+1\) intervals of positive length. On each interval choose an interior point \(u\), and use the bundle
\(z+h+(\mathbf 1_{\{u<f_j\}})_{j=1}^m\)
with mass \(\theta\) times the interval length.

The intervals below \(f_j\) have total length \(f_j\). Consequently, the total contribution in coordinate \(j\) is
\(\theta(z_j+h_j+f_j)=\theta z_j+s_j\).
All increments are nonnegative integers.

For rational data, sorting, taking floors, and forming interval lengths use polynomially many exact rational operations. The resulting numbers have polynomial encoding length. \(\square\)

Because valuations are nonnegative, these replacements preserve every lower bound on bundle value.

The same construction, starting from the empty bundle with mass one, proves that \(\Pi(q)\) is nonempty for every nonnegative \(q\).

3. A polynomial candidate list for one type and threshold

Fix a type \(t\). Choose a positive integer \(D_t\) clearing the denominators of \(a_t,b_t\), and define the integer-valued utility
\(w_t(z)=D_t\sum_j v_{tj}z_j\).

The binary lengths of \(D_t\) and the scaled values are polynomial in \(L\).

List the distinct positive, realized scaled values as
\(c_1,\ldots,c_d\), where \(0\le d\le2\). Let
\(I_i=\{j:D_tv_{tj}=c_i\}\).
Thus each \(I_i\) is nonempty; classes of value zero are excluded.

For an integer threshold \(k\ge0\), define
\(S_t(k)=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:w_t(z)\ge k\}\).

We will compute a polynomial-size list \(C_t(k)\subseteq S_t(k)\) satisfying
\(\operatorname{conv}S_t(k)=\operatorname{conv}C_t(k)+\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\).
Here convex hull means finite convex combinations, without taking a closure.

For \(k=0\), take \(C_t(0)=\{0\}\); the identity follows from Lemma 1.

Now suppose \(k>0\). If \(d=0\), then \(S_t(k)\) is empty, and we take \(C_t(k)\) empty. Otherwise, put
\(M_i=\lceil k/c_i\rceil\)
and define the bounded polytope
\(P_t(k)=\{u\in\mathbb R^d:0\le u_i\le M_i,\ \sum_i c_i u_i\ge k\}\).

Enumerate the vertices \(V_t(k)\) of its integer hull. Every vertex is an integer count vector. For every \(r\in V_t(k)\), construct bundles by independently choosing one item class \(j_i\in I_i\) for every coordinate with \(r_i>0\), and placing all \(r_i\) items of that value in class \(j_i\). Let \(C_t(k)\) contain all bundles obtained this way.

Each such bundle has scaled value \(\sum_i c_i r_i\ge k\). There are at most \((m+1)^2\) bundles per count vertex. Moreover, \(P_t(k)\) has dimension at most two, at most five inequalities, and coefficients of encoding length polynomial in \(L+\log(k+1)\). The fixed-dimensional integer-hull theorem therefore computes \(C_t(k)\) in polynomial time and guarantees polynomial output length.

Proof of the convex-hull identity

Take any \(z\in S_t(k)\). Delete all zero-valued items. In each group \(I_i\), retain only
\(u_i=\min\{\sum_{j\in I_i}z_j,M_i\}\)
items, producing an integer bundle \(z'\le z\).

This deletion preserves the threshold. If no positive-valued count was truncated, the value is unchanged. If group \(i\) was truncated, its retained items alone have value
\(c_iM_i\ge k\).
Thus \(u\in P_t(k)\cap\mathbb Z^d\).

Write \(u=\sum_{r\in V_t(k)}\lambda_r r\), with \(\lambda_r\ge0\) and \(\sum_r\lambda_r=1\).

For every group with \(u_i>0\), define
\(p_{ij}=z'_j/u_i\) for \(j\in I_i\).
Conditional on choosing count vertex \(r\), choose the representative class in group \(i\) according to these probabilities and put all \(r_i\) items there.

If \(u_i=0\), every vertex with positive \(\lambda_r\) has \(r_i=0\), because the coordinates are nonnegative and their weighted average is zero.

This is a finite distribution on \(C_t(k)\). For \(j\in I_i\) with \(u_i>0\), its expected coordinate is
\(\sum_r\lambda_r r_i p_{ij}=u_i p_{ij}=z'_j\).
The other expected coordinates are zero. Therefore
\(z'\in\operatorname{conv}C_t(k)\), and
\(z\in\operatorname{conv}C_t(k)+\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m\).

The latter set is convex, so it contains \(\operatorname{conv}S_t(k)\).

Conversely, take a finite convex combination of bundles from \(C_t(k)\), and any nonnegative surplus vector. Apply Lemma 1 to one positive-mass atom to add that surplus. Every resulting bundle remains in \(S_t(k)\). This proves the reverse inclusion and hence the identity. \(\square\)

4. Computing each benchmark exactly

Define the integer upper bound
\(U_t=\lfloor\sum_j D_tv_{tj}q_j\rfloor\).

For every finite-support distribution with mean \(q\), its minimum scaled utility is an integer and is at most its expected scaled utility. Hence every attainable minimum belongs to
\(\{0,\ldots,U_t\}\).
The set of attainable minima is nonempty by Lemma 1, so it has a largest element, which is attained. This also justifies the maximum in the benchmark definition.

For any integer \(k\in\{0,\ldots,U_t\}\), test the following explicit linear program:

\(p_z\ge0\quad(z\in C_t(k)),\)

\(\sum_{z\in C_t(k)}p_z=1,\)

\(\sum_{z\in C_t(k)}p_z z_j\le q_j\quad(j\in J).\)

This program is feasible if and only if a finite distribution of mean exactly \(q\) can guarantee scaled utility at least \(k\).

Indeed, such a distribution has mean in \(\operatorname{conv}S_t(k)\). The identity proved above then supplies a convex combination of candidates whose mean is coordinatewise at most \(q\), establishing LP feasibility. Conversely, a feasible LP gives a candidate distribution; Lemma 1 adds its resource surplus and produces mean exactly \(q\) while preserving the threshold.

Feasibility is monotone in \(k\), since \(S_t(k+1)\subseteq S_t(k)\). Binary search therefore computes the largest feasible integer \(K_t\), and
\(\beta_t^G(q)=K_t/D_t\).

The upper bound \(U_t\) has polynomial binary length: it is computed by rational arithmetic from polynomial-length scaled values and the input supplies. Thus binary search makes only polynomially many LP calls, each with polynomial encoding length.

In particular, the benchmarks are computed exactly, rather than assumed to be supplied.

5. The allocation linear program

For each type, compute the candidate list
\(C_t=C_t(K_t)\).
Solve the explicit rational linear program

\(x_{t,z}\ge0\quad(t\in T,\ z\in C_t),\)

\(\sum_{z\in C_t}x_{t,z}=\mu_t\quad(t\in T),\)

\(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{z\in C_t}x_{t,z}z_j\le q_j\quad(j\in J).\)

There are polynomially many columns: the input lists only \(\tau\) types, and each list \(C_t\) is polynomial in \(m,L\). No enumeration of possible types is involved.

Necessity. Suppose an allocation satisfying the task exists, even with real masses. For each positive-mass acceptable bundle, the convex-hull identity provides a finite mixture of candidates whose expected bundle is coordinatewise at most the original bundle. Replacing each atom by its candidate mixture preserves every type’s mass and weakly decreases total resource use. Thus the LP is feasible.

Sufficiency. If the LP is feasible, compute a rational feasible solution of polynomial encoding length. Every positive-mass candidate meets its type’s benchmark. It remains only to allocate the unused supplies, as follows.

Consequently, if this LP is infeasible, reporting infeasibility is correct.

6. Recovering exact supplies and bounding the output

Let
\(s_j=q_j-\sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_j\ge0\)
be the unused supply.

Because \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), some atom \((t_0,z_0)\) has positive mass \(\theta=x_{t_0,z_0}\). Apply Lemma 1 to this atom and the entire surplus vector \(s\).

The replacement preserves its total mass \(\theta\), adds exactly \(s\), and gives only bundles coordinatewise greater than or equal to \(z_0\). Therefore the resulting allocation satisfies

\(\sum_zx_{t,z}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,z}x_{t,z}z_j=q_j,\)

and every positive-mass bundle meets its recipient type’s exact benchmark.

The replacement introduces at most \(m+1\) atoms. Its coordinates may be large, but their binary lengths remain polynomial: \(\theta\) and \(s_j\) have polynomial rational encoding length, so \(\lfloor s_j/\theta\rfloor\) has polynomial binary length. All output masses likewise have polynomial encoding length.

If \(m=0\), every benchmark is zero and the immediate output assigns mass \(\mu_t\) to the empty bundle for each type. Zero supplies, zero-valued types, and unrealized valuation levels are already covered by the constructions above.

7. Conclusion

The benchmark computation, candidate enumeration, allocation LP, and surplus redistribution all run in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Their correctness holds for the finite-support distributions with real probabilities allowed in the benchmark definition, while the produced allocation uses rational masses of polynomial encoding length.

Thus the exact allocation problem is polynomial-time solvable under the stated binary encoding convention.
