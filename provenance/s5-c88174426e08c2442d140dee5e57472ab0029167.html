The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite directed graph of firm types with terminal seller and buyer types, rational masses \(\mu_v\) satisfying \(\sum_v \mu_v=1\), contract types \(e=(u,v)\) with rational per-capita capacities, and strict upstream and downstream rankings for each nonterminal type, does there exist an individually rational balanced mass flow with no blocking path or cycle of positive feasible mass? Each type accepts equal total mass on both sides, choosing its most-preferred offered contract types first and allowing partial mass at the cutoff; a blocking path or cycle uses unused capacity and added contract mass that every participating type would accept alongside its current flow.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) firm types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_v\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. Let \(m\) be the number of contract types and \(L\) the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).
The contract graph is loopless, with exactly one terminal seller \(s\) having no incoming contracts and one terminal buyer \(t\) having no outgoing contracts; every other type has both. Each contract \(e=(u,v)\) has positive binary-rational seller-per-capita capacity \(c_e\), giving aggregate capacity \(U_e=\mu_u c_e\). There is no separate throughput quota.
Each nonterminal type has strict rankings of its incoming and outgoing contract types. Given offered quantities, it chooses quantity \(k=\min\{\text{total incoming offer},\text{total outgoing offer}\}\) on each side, taking preferred types first and splitting at the cutoff. Existing mass precedes added mass within the same contract type; terminals accept every offer.
An outcome is a real vector \(f\) with \(0\le f_e\le U_e\), balanced at every nonterminal type and individually rational under these choices.
A block is a nonempty simple directed path or simple directed cycle, carrying the same added quantity \(\varepsilon>0\) on every constituent contract, with \(f_e+\varepsilon\le U_e\). At each participating type, all its added mass must be chosen when offered alongside its current incident flow; existing contracts may be rejected. Path endpoints need not be terminal. A cycle repeats only its starting vertex.

Is deciding whether such an outcome exists with no blocking path or cycle in \(\mathsf{P}\), or is it \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard?

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete, including for the stated continuous model. Thus it is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard, and a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\).

The proof below applies the published continuous hardness result, verifies its correspondence with the stated choices, and independently establishes polynomial-size certificates.

1. Characterizing blocking paths and cycles

Write \(U_e=\mu_u c_e\) for the aggregate capacity of \(e=(u,v)\). Let \(f\) satisfy the capacity constraints and balance at each nonterminal.

Such an \(f\) is automatically individually rational. Indeed, when offered only its current incident flow, a nonterminal has equal incoming and outgoing totals. Its choice therefore accepts everything offered.

Define the residual graph \(R_f\) to contain precisely the arcs \(e\) satisfying \(f_e<U_e\).

Internal vertices and cycles

Suppose a participating nonterminal has current incoming and outgoing totals both equal to \(q\). Offering one additional incoming quantity \(\varepsilon\) and one additional outgoing quantity \(\varepsilon\) makes both totals \(q+\varepsilon\). Consequently, the type accepts all offered quantities, regardless of its rankings.

It follows that a simple directed cycle blocks if and only if all its arcs belong to \(R_f\). For sufficiency, choose \(\varepsilon>0\) no larger than the minimum residual capacity on that cycle. Necessity follows from the capacity constraint in the definition of a block.

Path endpoints

For an outgoing arc \(e\) of a nonterminal \(v\), define
\[ W_v^{\mathrm{out}}(e) =\sum_{\substack{a\text{ outgoing from }v\\ e\succ_v^{\mathrm{out}}a}} f_a . \]

Here and below, the displayed expressions use the same mathematical notation as inline expressions: \(W_v^{\mathrm{out}}(e)=\sum_{a:\,e\succ_v^{\mathrm{out}}a}f_a\).

At a path’s initial nonterminal vertex, only an outgoing quantity \(\varepsilon\) is added. The chosen quantity on each side remains \(q\). Because existing mass precedes added mass within \(e\), all added mass on \(e\) is chosen exactly when
\(f_e+\varepsilon+\sum_{a:\,a\succ_v^{\mathrm{out}}e}f_a\le q\).
Using \(q=\sum_{a\text{ outgoing}}f_a\), this is equivalent to
\(\varepsilon\le W_v^{\mathrm{out}}(e)\).

Thus a residual arc can initiate a blocking path exactly when its seller is \(s\), or its seller has positive current flow on some strictly less preferred outgoing arc.

The analogous calculation at the final vertex shows that a residual arc can terminate a blocking path exactly when its buyer is \(t\), or its buyer has positive current flow on some strictly less preferred incoming arc.

Therefore a simple path blocks precisely when:

  • every constituent arc is residual;
  • its first arc satisfies the initial-endpoint condition;
  • its last arc satisfies the final-endpoint condition.

For sufficiency, take \(\varepsilon\) smaller than all positive residual capacities and all applicable endpoint quantities. There are finitely many quantities, so such an \(\varepsilon>0\) exists.

These characterizations show, in particular, that stability depends only on the sets
\(Z(f)=\{e:f_e=0\}\) and \(S(f)=\{e:f_e=U_e\}\), together with the graph and rankings.

2. Applying the continuous hardness theorem

Theorem 2 of Fleiner, Jankó, Schlotter, and Teytelboym establishes \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness of path-or-cycle-stable outcome existence in flow networks. Immediately following its proof, the authors explicitly extend the result to real-valued completely stable flows. Their construction uses unit capacities, a loopless graph, one terminal seller, one terminal buyer, and strict rankings at the remaining vertices. [Complexity of stability in trading networks, Section 3.2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00182-022-00833-0).

In Fleiner’s continuous formulation, complete stability excludes unsaturated directed cycles and unsaturated blocking walks whose endpoints are terminal or can replace positive flow on less preferred arcs. There is no separate throughput quota. [On Stable Matchings and Flows, Sections 2–3](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4893/7/1/1).

Section 1 proves that these endpoint and cycle conditions coincide with the task’s quantity-choice definition. Moreover, when there is no residual directed cycle, every residual directed walk is a simple path: a repeated vertex would contain a directed cycle. Thus excluding blocking paths and cycles is exactly complete stability.

It remains to encode the published hard instances as societies with positive masses.

Let such an instance have \(n\) vertices and unit aggregate capacities. Set \(\tau=n\), and let \(q\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(q\ge n\). Assign:

  • mass \(2/q\) to \(q-n\) types;
  • mass \(1/q\) to \(2n-q\) types.

These counts are nonnegative and sum to \(n\). The masses sum to
\((2(q-n)+(2n-q))/q=1\).
Every mass is positive and dyadic.

For each arc \(e=(u,v)\), set \(c_e=1/\mu_u\). This is a positive integer, and its aggregate capacity is exactly \(\mu_u c_e=1\). Keep the graph and rankings unchanged.

The transformation preserves feasible flows and every blocking path or cycle. Hence it preserves existence of a stable outcome. Its numerical additions use \(O((m+\tau)\log\tau)\) bits and can be constructed in that many bit operations, besides copying the original instance. This proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness under all the stated restrictions.

3. Polynomial-size rational certificates

Allowing arbitrary real flows does not obstruct membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\). We prove that every yes-instance has a rational stable flow of polynomial encoding length.

Suppose a real stable flow \(f\) exists. Fix its sets \(Z=Z(f)\) and \(S=S(f)\). Consider the bounded polytope \(P\) defined by balance, capacity constraints, and the additional equalities \(x_e=0\) for \(e\in Z\) and \(x_e=U_e\) for \(e\in S\).

Choose an integer \(D>0\) such that every \(DU_e\) is integral. One suitable choice is the product of all denominators appearing in the input masses and seller-per-capita capacities. Because their total encoding length is at most \(L\), \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits.

Scale the polytope by \(D\), obtaining \(P'\) with integer bounds.

Every vertex of \(P'\) is integral

Add an auxiliary arc from \(t\) to \(s\), with capacity \(H=\sum_e DU_e\). Give it the flow equal to the total flow leaving \(s\).

Summing the nonterminal balance equations shows that the total flow leaving \(s\) equals the total entering \(t\). Therefore the augmented vector is a circulation. The auxiliary flow lies between \(0\) and \(H\). This construction is an affine bijection between \(P'\) and a bounded circulation polytope with integer lower and upper bounds.

Suppose a vertex of that circulation polytope had a nonintegral coordinate. In the undirected multigraph consisting of its nonintegral arcs, no incident vertex can have degree one: conservation would then equate one signed nonintegral quantity plus an integer to zero.

A finite nonempty graph with this property contains an undirected cycle. Perturb the flow around that cycle, using signs according to the arcs’ directions. This preserves conservation. Every affected coordinate is strictly between its integer bounds, so sufficiently small perturbations in both directions remain feasible. Their midpoint is the original vector, contradicting extremality.

Thus every vertex of the augmented polytope, and consequently every vertex of \(P'\), is integral.

Preserving the stability pattern

Let \(I=E\setminus(Z\cup S)\) and \(r=|I|\). If \(r=0\), the original flow already has rational coordinates of polynomial encoding length.

Otherwise, for every \(e\in I\), the point \(Df\in P'\) satisfies \(0<(Df)_e<DU_e\). Maximizing and minimizing coordinate \(e\) over the bounded polytope therefore yields vertices \(p^{e,+}\) and \(p^{e,-}\) satisfying \(p^{e,+}_e>0\) and \(p^{e,-}_e<DU_e\).

Average these \(2r\) integral vertices and divide by \(D\):
\(\widehat f=(2rD)^{-1}\sum_{e\in I}(p^{e,+}+p^{e,-})\).

The vector \(\widehat f\) is feasible. Each coordinate in \(I\) is positive because one summand is positive, and is strictly below capacity because one summand is strictly below capacity. All other coordinates retain their fixed values.

Hence \(Z(\widehat f)=Z(f)\) and \(S(\widehat f)=S(f)\). By Section 1, \(\widehat f\) is stable.

Its common denominator \(2rD\) has \(O(L+\log m)\) bits. Its numerators have the same asymptotic bit bound, since every summand is bounded by \(DU_e\). Thus the entire certificate has polynomial length.

4. Verification and running time

Given a rational candidate flow, first check capacities and balance. Next construct \(R_f\) and test for a directed cycle.

If a cycle exists, Section 1 supplies a blocking cycle. Otherwise \(R_f\) is acyclic. Mark residual arcs satisfying the initial-endpoint condition and those satisfying the final-endpoint condition. These marks can be computed by scanning each preference list and recording whether a less preferred arc carries positive flow.

Check separately for an arc satisfying both endpoint conditions. For longer paths, start a reachability search at the heads of all initially eligible arcs. A blocking path exists exactly when the tail of some finally eligible arc is reachable. Acyclicity ensures that each resulting concatenation is a simple path.

The graph operations and arithmetic checks require \(O(m+\tau)\) operations. Using the common-denominator certificates above and schoolbook integer arithmetic gives the conservative bit-time bound
\(O((m+\tau)(L+\log(m+\tau))^2)\).

Consequently, existence is in \(\mathsf{NP}\). Together with Section 2, this proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness for the precise continuous problem stated.
