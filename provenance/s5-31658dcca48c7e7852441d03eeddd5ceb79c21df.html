The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a candidate set \(C\), committee size \(k<|C|\), fault fraction \(\beta=t/n<1/3\), a public tie order, and a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings and Byzantine status for the submitted quorum, compute the exact committee and round-by-round transfers using quota \(1/(k+1)\) in quorum-normalized units. Determine whether, for every \(0<j\le s\le k\), a group of quorum mass greater than \(j/(k+1)\) that ranks the same \(s\) candidates in its top \(s\) positions has at least \(j\) of those candidates elected. If \(\mu\) is normalized over the \(n-t\) quorum ballots, allow Byzantine mass up to \(\beta/(1-\beta)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\) candidates, disjoint from the voters, with \(1\le k<m\), a public total order on \(C\), and binary rational \(0\le\beta<1/3\).
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(a_i\). Each type consists of a strict complete ranking of \(C\) and a Byzantine-status bit; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Require \(\sum_i a_i=1\) and Byzantine mass at most \(\beta/(1-\beta)\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Use the following mass interpretation of BFT-STV, with quota \(q=1/(k+1)\). Initially every type points to its first candidate, and each candidate's tally is the sum of the masses pointing to it. Elected and eliminated candidates are ineligible.
In each round, if an eligible candidate has tally strictly greater than \(q\), elect the first such candidate in the public order and transfer its surplus; otherwise eliminate an eligible candidate of minimum tally, breaking ties by that order, and transfer its entire tally.
For a removed candidate \(c\), let \(I_c\) be the types currently pointing to it and \(M_c=\sum_{i\in I_c}a_i\). Advance those pointers to their highest-ranked eligible candidate. Transfer amount \(T_c=v_c-q\) on election or \(T_c=v_c\) on elimination, giving destination \(z\) the amount \(T_c\sum_{i\in I_c:\,\text{new pointer}=z}a_i/M_c\). Thus transfer shares use original type masses, not previous transferred weights; when \(M_c=0\), transfer nothing. Remove \(c\)'s tally from further counting.
Stop when \(k\) candidates are elected; if the eligible candidates exactly fill the remaining seats, elect them all and stop. Byzantine status does not affect counting. All arithmetic is exact.
Output the committee \(W\), each round's election or elimination and destination transfers, and whether the following condition holds: for every pair of integers \(1\le j\le s\le k\) and every \(A\subseteq C\) with \(|A|=s\), if the original mass whose top \(s\) candidates are exactly \(A\) exceeds \(j/(k+1)\), then \(|W\cap A|\ge j\).

Is this combined computation and decision problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite electorates, BFT-STV satisfies the stated proportionality criterion with threshold \(j(n-t)/(k+1)\). [Tennakoon–Gramoli](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.02711)
Classical STV has the corresponding unrounded Droop proportionality guarantee, and positive rational vote masses are explicitly allowed; this does not establish the complexity of the transfer rule specified here. [Janson](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.06377)

Result

The combined computation and decision problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,k,L\).

Two facts are decisive: exact tally denominators have polynomial bit length, and the proportionality condition needs to be checked only for sets appearing as prefixes of supported rankings.

1. Integer representation of the original masses

Write each input mass as \(a_i=u_i/d_i\), with positive integers \(u_i,d_i\). Define
\[ D=\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}d_i, \qquad b_i=u_i(D/d_i). \]
Then \(a_i=b_i/D\), and normalization gives
\[ \sum_{i=1}^{\tau}b_i=D. \]
Consequently, every sum of original type masses has the form \(B/D\), where \(B\) is an integer satisfying \(0\le B\le D\).

The binary length of \(D\) is at most \(1+\sum_i\log_2 d_i\), hence \(O(L)\). All \(b_i\) therefore also have \(O(L)\) bits. These integers are computable in polynomial time.

The Byzantine-status bits and the promised bound on their total mass do not affect either counting or the stated proportionality condition.

2. Termination and tally invariants

Let \(e\) be the number of elected candidates and \(E\) the number of eligible candidates. Throughout an unfinished execution,
\[ E\ge k-e. \]

Initially this holds because \(m>k\). If equality holds, the stopping rule elects precisely the remaining \(k-e\) candidates. Otherwise, an elimination reduces \(E\) by one and preserves the inequality; an ordinary election reduces both \(E\) and \(k-e\) by one.

Thus the procedure produces exactly \(k\) winners. Every ordinary round removes one candidate, so there are at most \(m\) ordinary rounds. Moreover, an ordinary round starts with \(E>k-e\ge1\), so at least one eligible destination remains after removal. Every advancing pointer is therefore well-defined.

We also need two tally invariants:

  • An eligible candidate with no types pointing to it has tally zero.
  • Before terminal completion, all tallies are nonnegative and their sum is \(1-eq\), where \(q=1/(k+1)\).

The first holds initially. For a retained eligible candidate \(z\), its pointing types consist of its previous pointing types and any newly arriving types. If this collection is empty, its previous tally was zero by induction, and its incoming transfer is zero.

For the second, suppose a removed candidate has at least one pointing type. The destination shares are nonnegative and sum to one, because every such type advances to exactly one destination. Hence an elimination transfers its entire tally, preserving total active tally. An election transfers its tally minus \(q\), decreasing total active tally by exactly \(q\). All transferred amounts are nonnegative.

If no type points to the removed candidate, its tally is zero by the first invariant. It cannot be elected and its elimination changes no tally. This completes the induction.

In particular, every active tally lies in \([0,1]\).

3. Exact simulation has polynomial bit complexity

Maintain all active tallies with one common denominator:
\[ v_z=\frac{N_z}{H}, \]
where \(H\) is a positive integer divisible by \(k+1\).

Initially choose \(H_0=(k+1)D\). The initial tally numerator of candidate \(z\) is then
\[ N_z=(k+1)\sum_{i:\,\text{first choice of }i=z}b_i. \]

Consider an ordinary round removing \(c\). Define
\[ B=\sum_{i\in I_c}b_i, \qquad B_z=\sum_{\substack{i\in I_c\\\text{new pointer}=z}}b_i. \]
If \(B>0\), then \(1\le B\le D\), and the specified transfer share is exactly
\[ \frac{\sum_{i\in I_c:\,\text{new pointer}=z}a_i}{M_c} =\frac{B_z}{B}. \]

Define the integer
\[ S= \begin{cases} N_c-H/(k+1),&\text{on election},\\ N_c,&\text{on elimination}. \end{cases} \]
The transfer to \(z\) is therefore
\[ \Delta_z=\frac{S B_z}{H B}. \]

Use the new common denominator \(H'=HB\). For every retained eligible candidate, set
\[ N'_z=B N_z+S B_z. \]
Indeed,
\[ \frac{N'_z}{H'} =\frac{N_z}{H}+\frac{S B_z}{HB}, \]
which is precisely the required tally update. Remove \(c\)'s tally and advance the corresponding pointers.

If \(B=0\), the tally of \(c\) is zero by Section 2. Transfer nothing and retain the denominator \(H\).

After \(r\) ordinary rounds,
\[ H_r\le(k+1)D^{r+1}. \]
Since \(r\le m\),
\[ \log_2 H_r\le\log_2(k+1)+(m+1)\log_2 D. \]
Thus every denominator has \(O(mL+\log(k+1))\) bits. Since each tally lies in \([0,1]\), its maintained numerator satisfies \(0\le N_z\le H_r\) and has the same bit-length bound. Intermediate products and transfer numerators also have polynomial bit length.

All round decisions use exact integer comparisons:

  • Eligibility for election is \(N_z>H/(k+1)\).
  • Minimum-tally elimination compares the numerators \(N_z\), since their denominator is common.
  • The public order resolves the prescribed choices and ties.

There are at most \(m\) ordinary rounds. Each requires only polynomially many pointer operations, sums, products, and comparisons on integers of polynomial bit length. Exact integer arithmetic therefore gives a polynomial-time simulation. Destination transfers can be output in the displayed rational form, or reduced using the polynomial-time Euclidean algorithm.

There are at most \(m\) destination-transfer entries per ordinary round, so the complete transfer transcript also has polynomial length. The terminal completion rule can be recorded as the election of all remaining eligible candidates followed by immediate stopping.

4. Polynomial-time verification of proportionality

For each \(s\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), let \(P_{i,s}\) be the unordered set of the first \(s\) candidates in type \(i\)'s ranking. For a set \(A\) of size \(s\), write
\[ \mu_s(A)=\sum_{i:\,P_{i,s}=A}a_i. \]

If \(A\) occurs in no supported ranking prefix, then \(\mu_s(A)=0\). Its antecedent cannot hold for any \(j\ge1\). Consequently, for each \(s\), only the at most \(\tau\) distinct sets \(P_{i,s}\) need checking.

Fix one such set \(A\), and define
\[ B_A=\sum_{i:\,P_{i,s}=A}b_i, \qquad h=|W\cap A|. \]
Then \(\mu_s(A)=B_A/D\).

If \(h=s\), every required conclusion holds. Suppose instead that \(h<s\). A violation for \(A\) occurs exactly when
\[ \mu_s(A)>\frac{h+1}{k+1}. \]

To prove this equivalence, a violating integer \(j\) must satisfy \(h<j\le s\). Thus \(j\ge h+1\), and
\[ \mu_s(A)>\frac{j}{k+1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu_s(A)>\frac{h+1}{k+1}. \]
Conversely, if the latter inequality holds, choosing \(j=h+1\) gives a valid violating pair because \(1\le h+1\le s\).

Hence the exact violation test is simply
\[ (k+1)B_A>(h+1)D. \]

An algorithm can therefore:

  • For each \(s\), group supported types by their top-\(s\) set.
  • Sum their integers \(b_i\) to obtain each \(B_A\).
  • Compute \(h=|W\cap A|\).
  • Reject the criterion if \(h<s\) and the displayed integer inequality holds.

Representing candidate sets as \(m\)-bit vectors makes grouping and intersection counting polynomial-time operations. There are at most \(\tau k\) groups across all prefix lengths, and \(B_A\le D\), so every arithmetic comparison has polynomial bit complexity.

5. Conclusion

The committee, full round transcript, and proportionality truth value are all computable in polynomial time. This proof evaluates the exact transfer rule given in the question; it does not require applying the proportionality guarantees discussed in [Tennakoon–Gramoli](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.02711) or [Janson](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.06377).
