The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many complete utility types \(T\), a rational population distribution \(\mu\), indivisible item types with rational per-capita supplies \(q_i\), and additive utilities \(u_t(i)\), decide whether there exists a finitely supported mass allocation \(x_{t,b}\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\) satisfying type-mass and supply constraints, envy-freeness across all positive-mass bundles, and Pareto efficiency under type-preserving mass couplings; construct such an allocation when one exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) complete utility types with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) item types, binary rational per-capita supplies \(q_i>0\), and additive utilities \(u_t(i)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length. Fix a computable function \(g:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N\) satisfying \(g(L)=o(\log L)\), and impose the promise \(\max_{t,i}u_t(i)\le2^{g(L)}\).
A feasible allocation is a finitely supported family \(x_{t,b}\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), indexed by integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), satisfying \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_i\le q_i\). Bundles have no additional size or coordinate bounds; unused supply is permitted.
Write \(u_t(b)=\sum_i u_t(i)b_i\). Envy-freeness requires \(u_t(b)\ge u_t(c)\) whenever \(x_{t,b}>0\) and \(x_{s,c}>0\), including comparisons within a type; utility ties are allowed.
An allocation \(x\) is Pareto-dominated if there exist a feasible finitely supported allocation \(y\) and nonnegative real coupling masses \(\gamma_{t,b,c}\) with \(\sum_c\gamma_{t,b,c}=x_{t,b}\), \(\sum_b\gamma_{t,b,c}=y_{t,c}\), and \(\gamma_{t,b,c}>0\Rightarrow u_t(c)\ge u_t(b)\), such that \(\sum_{t,b,c:\,u_t(c)>u_t(b)}\gamma_{t,b,c}>0\). The competitor need not be envy-free. Pareto efficiency means absence of such domination.

Is deciding whether an envy-free Pareto-efficient allocation exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard? If NP-hard, can it nevertheless be solved in \(f(m+\tau)\operatorname{poly}(L)\) time? In either algorithmic case, can such an allocation also be constructed when it exists?

The discrete problem admits an \(f(m+n)\operatorname{poly}(L)\)-time algorithm under the same subpolynomial utility bound, where \(n\) is the number of agents ([Bredereck et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.04907)).
Without that bound, discrete existence is \(\Sigma_2^P\)-complete for additive preferences ([de Keijzer et al.](https://recherche.noiraudes.net/resources/papers/ADT09.pdf)); with unary utilities it is W[1]-complete parameterized by \(n\) even for identical preferences ([Bliem et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/022.pdf)).

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one, so supplies are measured per capita.
  • Specified nonnegative integer utilities, as allowed by the discrete model; specified positive supplies and nonempty sets of item and utility types.
  • Preserved the anchor's subpolynomial maximum-utility condition using a fixed computable bounding function g, rather than silently extending its guarantees to arbitrary binary utilities.
  • Used supply inequalities, allowing unused items, as in the discrete allocation constraints.
  • Allowed every nonnegative integral bundle without a per-person supply bound, because per-capita supply constrains aggregate consumption.
  • Allowed real allocation and coupling masses, finite allocation supports, weak utility comparisons, and envy comparisons within a type.
  • Defined type-preserving Pareto couplings to permit splitting mass, require individual weak improvement, and require strict improvement on positive mass.
  • Allowed Pareto competitors to violate envy-freeness, retaining the discrete definition of Pareto efficiency.
  • Used m plus support size tau as the continuous counterpart of the anchor's combined parameter.

2. Two structural lemmas.

Write $u_t$ for the utility vector of type $t$.

Lemma 1: envy-freeness fixes one integer utility level per type.
In an envy-free allocation, there is a vector
$$ U=(U_1,\ldots,U_\tau)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\tau} $$
such that every bundle assigned positive mass to type $t$ has utility $U_t$.

Indeed, comparing two positively assigned bundles within type $t$ in both directions gives equality. Every type has nonempty support because $\mu_t>0$.

Moreover, every bundle assigned to any type satisfies
$$ u_s(b)\le U_s \qquad\text{for every type }s. $$
This follows by comparing that bundle with a positively assigned bundle of type $s$. Consequently, define
$$ B_t(U)= \left\{ b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}: u_t(b)=U_t,\ u_s(b)\le U_s\text{ for every }s \right\}. $$
An allocation is envy-free with utility vector $U$ exactly when type $t$ uses only bundles in $B_t(U)$.

Feasibility also implies
$$ 0\le U_t\le \frac{u_t\cdot q}{\mu_t}. $$
To see the upper bound, the aggregate utility assigned to type $t$ is $\mu_tU_t$, and its item consumption is coordinatewise at most $q$.

Lemma 2: a characterization of Pareto efficiency for binary utilities.
Suppose all utilities belong to $\{0,1\}$ and every item type is valued by at least one utility type. A feasible allocation is Pareto-efficient if and only if:

  • it uses every supply completely; and
  • every allocated item is valued by its recipient.

For necessity, suppose item $i$ has positive unused supply. Choose a type valuing $i$ and a positively assigned bundle $b$ of that type. For a sufficiently small positive mass $\varepsilon$, replace $b$ by $b+e_i$ on that mass. This is feasible and strictly improves those recipients.

Next, suppose positive mass of a bundle $b$ assigned to type $t$ contains item $i$, but $u_t(i)=0$. Choose a type $s$ with $u_s(i)=1$ and a positively assigned bundle $c$ of type $s$. Necessarily $s\ne t$. On an equal sufficiently small positive mass, replace
$$ b\longmapsto b-e_i, \qquad c\longmapsto c+e_i. $$
Consumption is unchanged, type $t$ loses no utility, and type $s$ strictly gains. Both modifications give finite allocations and couplings of the kind required in the question.

For sufficiency, let
$$ W(x)=\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}u_t(b). $$
Every feasible allocation satisfies
$$ W(x)\le\sum_iq_i, $$
because each item contributes at most one unit of utility. The two stated conditions give equality. Any domination coupling would strictly increase $W$, contradicting this upper bound. This proves the lemma.

3. NP-hardness with binary utilities and dyadic data.

Reduce from graph $4$-colorability. This problem is NP-hard: adjoining one vertex adjacent to every vertex of a graph reduces $3$-colorability to $4$-colorability.

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a nonempty graph and write $N=|V|$. Construct a graph $H$ whose vertices are
$$ V(H)=V\times\{1,2,3,4\}. $$
Its edges are:

  • all pairs $(v,a),(v,b)$ with $a\ne b$; and
  • all pairs $(v,a),(w,a)$ with $\{v,w\}\in E$.

An independent set in $H$ contains at most one vertex from each set
$$ \{(v,1),(v,2),(v,3),(v,4)\}. $$
Thus an independent set of size $N$ is exactly a proper $4$-coloring of $G$.

Let $C=|E(H)|$, and let $T$ be the smallest power of two satisfying $T\ge C+1$. Set
$$ \mu=\frac1T. $$

The society has the following $T$ distinct utility types, each of mass $\mu$:

  • one main type, denoted $0$;
  • one constraint type for every edge $e\in E(H)$;
  • $T-C-1$ filler types.

There is one vertex item $a_z$ for every $z\in V(H)$, with supply
$$ q_{a_z}=\frac{\mu}{4}. $$
There is also one private item $p_t$ for every non-main type $t$, with supply
$$ q_{p_t}=\mu. $$

All unspecified utilities are zero. The positive utilities are:

  • the main type values every vertex item at one;
  • the constraint type for $e=\{z,z'\}$ values $a_z$, $a_{z'}$, and its private item $p_e$ at one;
  • each filler type values only its private item at one.

All masses and supplies are dyadic. There are
$$ \tau=T,\qquad m=4N+T-1, $$
and $T<2(C+1)$, so the construction has polynomial encoding length. The utility rows are distinct because every non-main type has its own private item.

Necessity. Suppose an envy-free Pareto-efficient allocation exists.

Every item is valued by some type, so Lemma 2 applies. All supplies are consumed, and items are assigned only to types valuing them.

In particular, all of private item $p_t$ is assigned to type $t$. Let $U_t$ be the integer utility level from Lemma 1.

For a constraint type $e=\{z,z'\}$, private-item consumption gives
$$ \mu U_e\ge q_{p_e}=\mu. $$
Its total utility cannot exceed the total supply of its three valued items:
$$ \mu U_e \le q_{p_e}+q_{a_z}+q_{a_{z'}} =\mu+\frac{\mu}{4}+\frac{\mu}{4} =\frac32\mu. $$
Therefore
$$ 1\le U_e\le\frac32. $$
Since $U_e$ is integral, $U_e=1$.

Its private item already accounts for aggregate utility $\mu$. Hence it receives no positive aggregate quantity of either endpoint vertex item. Filler types value no vertex items. Consequently, all vertex items are assigned to the main type.

The main type’s aggregate utility is therefore
$$ \sum_{z\in V(H)}q_{a_z} =4N\frac{\mu}{4} =N\mu. $$
Its common utility level is $U_0=N$.

Consider any positively assigned main-type bundle $b$. Pareto efficiency excludes private items from $b$, since the main type does not value them. For each edge $e=\{z,z'\}$ of $H$, envy-freeness of constraint type $e$ requires
$$ b_{a_z}+b_{a_{z'}}\le U_e=1. $$
The clique edges within each original vertex’s four choices imply that these coordinates are binary and that at most one choice is present for each $v$. Since
$$ \sum_zb_{a_z}=U_0=N, $$
exactly one choice is present for every $v$. The remaining edge constraints exclude equal colors on adjacent vertices. Hence $b$ determines a proper $4$-coloring of $G$.

Sufficiency. Conversely, suppose $G$ has a proper $4$-coloring.

Apply the four cyclic permutations of the colors to this coloring. Each resulting coloring determines a bundle of $N$ vertex items. Assign each bundle main-type mass $\mu/4$, merging coincident bundles if necessary.

For every $v$ and color $a$, exactly one of these four bundles contains $a_{(v,a)}$. Thus all vertex supplies are consumed exactly.

Give every non-main type its private singleton bundle on its entire mass $\mu$. This consumes every private supply exactly.

The allocation is envy-free:

  • every main-type bundle has main-type utility $N$, while private singletons have main-type utility zero;
  • each constraint type obtains utility one and values every main-type bundle at most one, because that bundle is an independent set in $H$;
  • each filler type obtains utility one and values every other type’s bundle at zero.

Every allocated item is valued by its recipient, and every supply is exhausted. Lemma 2 proves Pareto efficiency.

We have proved the equivalence
$$ G\text{ is }4\text{-colorable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed society admits an envy-free Pareto-efficient allocation}. $$
Since $\Delta=1\le 2^{g(L)}$, this proves NP-hardness under the stated promise for every permitted $g$.

4. Exact integer-hull formulation for general utilities.

For an integer utility vector $U$, define
$$ C_t(U)= \left\{ c\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}:u_t(c)\ge U_t \right\}, $$
and the integer hulls
$$ H_t^{=}(U)=\operatorname{conv}B_t(U), \qquad H_t^{\ge}(U)=\operatorname{conv}C_t(U). $$
Here convex hull means finite convex combinations.

Envy-free feasibility with utility vector $U$ is equivalent to
$$ \exists z_1,\ldots,z_\tau: \quad z_t\in H_t^{=}(U), \qquad \sum_t\mu_tz_t\le q. $$
The forward direction takes conditional mean bundles. For the reverse direction, a finite convex representation of each $z_t$, scaled by $\mu_t$, gives the required allocation.

Such an allocation is Pareto-dominated exactly when
$$ \exists w_1,\ldots,w_\tau: \quad w_t\in H_t^{\ge}(U), \qquad \sum_t\mu_tw_t\le q, \qquad \sum_t\mu_tu_t(w_t)>\sum_t\mu_tU_t. $$

For the forward direction, a domination coupling assigns each type only bundles of utility at least $U_t$. Its positive mass of strict improvements makes the displayed inequality strict.

Conversely, finite convex representations of the $w_t$ give an allocation $y$ supported on bundles of utility at least $U_t$. Couple it to the original allocation $x$ using
$$ \gamma_{t,b,c}=\frac{x_{t,b}y_{t,c}}{\mu_t}. $$
The marginals are $x$ and $y$. Every positive coupling entry is a weak improvement, and the strict aggregate inequality implies positive coupling mass of strict improvements.

In particular, Pareto efficiency of an envy-free allocation depends only on its utility vector $U$, rather than on its particular bundle distribution.

5. A constructive algorithm parameterized also by the utility bound.

Set
$$ k=m+\tau,\qquad \Delta_0=\max(1,\Delta). $$
The following argument proves a computable
$$ F(k,\Delta_0)\operatorname{poly}(L) $$
bound.

The integer-hull theorem of Eisenbrand and Rothvoss states that, for an integer matrix $A$ with bounded dimension and entries, one can compute a modulus $D$ and matrices $B,C$ such that, for every residue $r$, a computable vector $a_r$ satisfies
$$ \operatorname{conv}\{v\in\mathbb Z^d:Av\le b\} = \{v\in\mathbb R^d:Bv\le a_r+Cb\} \quad\text{when }b\equiv r\pmod D. $$
The computation depends only on the dimensions and coefficient bound; their Remark 1 allows repeated rows. See [Theorem 1 and Remark 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.02347).

For $B_t(U)$, the defining inequalities are
$$ -b_i\le0,\qquad u_s(b)\le U_s\quad(s=1,\ldots,\tau),\qquad -u_t(b)\le-U_t. $$
For $C_t(U)$, they are
$$ -c_i\le0,\qquad -u_t(c)\le-U_t. $$
Thus every coefficient matrix has dimensions bounded by $k$ and entries bounded in absolute value by $\Delta_0$. Every right-hand side is an integer linear function of $U$. These check all the theorem’s hypotheses.

Take a common multiple of the finitely many resulting moduli, and enumerate the residue vector of $U$ modulo this common modulus. The number of branches depends only on $k,\Delta_0$.

Within each branch, all the hulls have descriptions
$$ H_t^{=}(U)=\{z:B_t^{=}z\le a_t^{=}+E_t^{=}U\}, $$
$$ H_t^{\ge}(U)=\{w:B_t^{\ge}w\le a_t^{\ge}+E_t^{\ge}U\}. $$
These are linear inequalities jointly in the mean bundles and $U$.

Substitute them into the two systems in Section 4. Eliminate their real mean-bundle variables by Fourier–Motzkin elimination, retaining strict inequalities where they occur. This gives:

  • a conjunction $\mathcal E(U)$ of rational linear inequalities expressing envy-free feasibility;
  • a conjunction $\mathcal D(U)$ of rational weak or strict linear inequalities expressing domination.

The number of variables eliminated and all resulting numbers of inequalities are bounded by functions of $k,\Delta_0$. Their coefficient encoding lengths are at most a parameter-dependent factor times a polynomial in $L$.

We seek an integer $U$ in the selected residue class satisfying
$$ 0\le U_t\le\frac{u_t\cdot q}{\mu_t}, \qquad \mathcal E(U), \qquad \neg\mathcal D(U). $$
The negation of a conjunction is a disjunction of negated individual inequalities. Branch on one violated inequality of $\mathcal D(U)$. Writing
$$ U=r+Dv,\qquad v\in\mathbb Z^\tau, $$
each resulting branch is an integer linear feasibility problem with $\tau$ variables.

After clearing denominators, strict inequalities can be replaced exactly by weak integer inequalities. For example, with integral data,
$$ a\cdot v<b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\cdot v\le b-1. $$
[Lenstra’s fixed-dimension integer-programming algorithm](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1983i/art.pdf) decides these systems and returns a feasible integer vector when one exists. The number of branches and their parameter-dependent sizes yield the claimed running time.

Construction. Once a suitable $U$ is found, delete item types valued by nobody. This preserves all utilities and all domination possibilities after projection. If no items remain, assigning empty bundles is sufficient.

Otherwise every coordinate is valued by some type. Hence $B_t(U)$ is bounded: if $u_s(i)>0$, then
$$ b_i\le\frac{U_s}{u_s(i)}. $$
Each $H_t^{=}(U)$ is therefore a rational polytope whose vertices are integral bundles. Enumerate its vertices and solve the finite allocation linear program over those bundles. Section 4 guarantees feasibility.

A basic feasible solution has at most $m+\tau$ positive allocation masses: after adding the $m$ supply slacks, the system has $\tau+m$ equations. Its masses are rational. Every positively used bundle satisfies the envy constraints, and the absence of $\mathcal D(U)$ proves Pareto efficiency. This construction has the same parameterized running-time bound.

6. What this does and does not settle.

For every fixed utility bound, the algorithm above proves constructive fixed-parameter tractability in $m+\tau$. This includes the binary-utility instances used in the hardness reduction.

For the full promise, however, the theorem supplies an unrestricted computable dependence on $\Delta$. Such a dependence cannot automatically be absorbed into $f(k)\operatorname{poly}(L)$. For example, a dependence $2^\Delta$ is already superpolynomial when
$$ \Delta=2^{\lfloor\sqrt{\log_2L}\rfloor}, $$
even though this utility bound satisfies the promise.

Thus the missing result is an algorithm whose dependence on the utility bound is sufficiently controlled—or another argument removing that parameter. The NP-hardness proof is complete; the requested parameterized classification for the full promise is not settled here.
