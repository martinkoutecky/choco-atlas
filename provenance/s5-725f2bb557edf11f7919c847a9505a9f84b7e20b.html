The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a catalogue \(G\), at most two complete valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and rational additive values \(v_t(g)\), find a partition \(G=B_0\,\dot\cup\,B_1\) and assignment masses \(z_{tb}\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_b z_{tb}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_t z_{tb}=1/2\), and \(v_t(B_b)\ge v_t(B_{b'})-\bar v_t(B_{b'})\) for every supported type \(t\) and \(b,b'\in\{0,1\}\). Each unit of mass receives one integral bundle; equivalently, an even finite population has \(N/2\) copies of each catalogue good and receives \(N/2\) copies of each bundle template.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A catalogue \(G\) contains \(m\) indivisible goods. A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\in\{1,2\}\) complete additive valuation types, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). The support size \(\tau\) counts listed types, not all possible types.
Each type specifies binary-rational values \(v_t(g)\in[0,1]\) for every good. Write \(\bar v_t(B)=\max_{g\in B}v_t(g)\), with \(\bar v_t(\varnothing)=0\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Find an integral partition \(G=B_0\,\dot\cup\,B_1\), allowing empty bundles, and rational assignment masses \(z_{tb}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}z_{tb}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}z_{tb}=1/2\).
Every unit of assigned mass receives the entire corresponding bundle template. Require \(v_t(B_b)\ge v_t(B_{b'})-\bar v_t(B_{b'})\) for every \(t\in T\) and \(b,b'\in\{0,1\}\), even when \(z_{tb}=0\).

Is finding such a partition and assignment solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For two discrete agents with nonnegative additive valuations, a symmetrically EF1 partition always exists and can be found in polynomial time; it can also have bundle cardinalities differing by at most one. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13824v1)
No separate result for this exact distribution-over-types model was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restricted the unspecified rational valuation domain to [0,1], as in the discrete definition; no normalization of total bundle value is imposed.
  • Allowed empty bundles and defined their maximum item value as zero, consistent with the discrete formulation permitting removal of at most one good and its use of empty partial bundles.
  • Specified rational output assignment masses and defined L as the total binary input bit length.
  • Kept the number of bundle templates fixed at two, independently of support size and population mass, as stipulated by the continuous question.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable for \(\tau\in\{1,2\}\). Every valid instance admits a solution. The two-agent symmetric EF1 theorem applies directly once the assignment-mass constraints are handled.

1. Assignment masses impose no additional restriction

For any partition, define

\(z_{t0}=z_{t1}=\mu_t/2\qquad(t\in T).\)

These numbers are nonnegative rationals. For each type,

\(z_{t0}+z_{t1}=\mu_t,\)

and, for each bundle index \(b\in\{0,1\}\),

\(\sum_{t\in T}z_{tb}=\frac12\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=\frac12.\)

Thus the assignment constraints are always feasible, independently of the partition. Because the fairness inequalities are required even for zero assigned mass, the remaining problem is exactly to construct a partition satisfying those inequalities for every listed valuation.

2. Apply the two-agent symmetric EF1 theorem

We use the following supplied result, whose symmetric fairness definition is precisely the inequality in the question:

Two-agent symmetric EF1 theorem. For any finite set of indivisible goods and two nonnegative additive valuations \(w_1,w_2\), one can find a partition \((A_0,A_1)\) in polynomial time such that, for every \(i\in\{1,2\}\) and \(b,b'\in\{0,1\}\),

\(w_i(A_b)\geq w_i(A_{b'})-\max_{g\in A_{b'}}w_i(g),\)

with the maximum of an empty bundle interpreted as zero. The partition can have bundle cardinalities differing by at most one. See Definition 1 and the construction proving Corollary 13 in [Johnston and Kazachkov, *Symmetrically Fair Allocations of Indivisible Goods*](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13824v1).

All hypotheses hold here: the catalogue is finite, every valuation is additive, and every good has nonnegative value.

If \(\tau=2\), label the listed types \(t_1,t_2\) and apply the theorem with \(w_i=v_{t_i}\). Its conclusion gives every required fairness inequality.

If \(\tau=1\), let \(t\) be the sole listed type and apply the theorem with \(w_1=w_2=v_t\). These are two auxiliary agents used by the algorithm; the original support still contains just one listed type. The theorem again gives every required inequality for that type.

Set \(B_b=A_b\), and use the masses constructed in Section 1. This proves feasibility of the complete output. If \(m=0\), both bundles are empty and all fairness inequalities hold directly.

3. Polynomial bit complexity

The cited construction sorts goods by each auxiliary valuation, pairs consecutive goods, and colors the graph formed by the two collections of pairs. An odd number of goods is handled by adding a good valued zero and subsequently deleting it. The graph has linearly many edges, so sorting and graph traversal require polynomially many operations. [Source, Section 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13824v1#S4.SS2).

This operation bound also gives polynomial bit complexity for the present input. Rational values can be compared exactly by cross multiplication. Their numerators and denominators have at most \(L\) bits, so each comparison takes polynomial time in \(L\). Dividing each input mass by two increases its encoding length by at most a constant before optional reduction.

Consequently, both the partition and rational assignment masses are computable and representable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
