The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) item types, a support \(T\) of \(\tau\) valuation types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita item supplies \(q_j\), and a rational-parameter MVNN \(v_t\) for each type, compute a welfare-maximizing mass assignment \(x_{t,S}\): maximize \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le q_j\), and \(x_{t,S}\ge0\). Each unit of mass receives an integral bundle; ask for the exact computational complexity and algorithms as functions of \(m\), \(\tau\), network size, and input bit length.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m\ge1\). A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge1\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible valuations.
Each type has an explicitly encoded rational MVNN: starting with the bundle indicator \(z^0=\mathbf1_S\), hidden layers apply \(z^k=\phi(W^kz^{k-1}+b^k)\) coordinatewise, where \(\phi(u)=\min\{1,\max\{0,u\}\}\), all weights are nonnegative and hidden biases nonpositive. The scalar output is a nonnegative linear readout with zero bias. Thus \(v_t(S)\ge0\) is monotone and \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\); network depths and widths are unrestricted inputs.
Supplies \(q_j\ge0\) are binary rationals. An assignment splits each type's mass among integral bundles \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), including the empty bundle, with no additional bundle restrictions. Its variables satisfy \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), \(\sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le q_j\).
The computational problem is to maximize \(\sum_t\sum_Sx_{t,S}v_t(S)\) exactly and compute an optimal assignment, represented by its nonzero bundle-mass entries. Let \(H\) be the total explicitly encoded network size and \(L\) the total input bit length, including a rational threshold \(B\).

Is this exact welfare-maximization problem polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,H,L\), or is it NP-hard, already for deciding whether the optimum is at least \(B\)?

For finitely many agents and one copy of each item, MVNN welfare maximization has an equivalent succinct MILP formulation; this establishes neither polynomial-time solvability nor a hardness classification ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.15117)).
No result settling this exact mass-assignment problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified MVNNs using the standard cutoff 1, nonnegative rational weights, nonpositive rational hidden biases, and a linear output with zero bias, preserving monotonicity and empty-bundle normalization.
  • Required nonnegative rational supplies and positive support masses, with at least one item and one supported type; retained the stated total-mass normalization of 1.
  • Allowed every integral subset, including the empty bundle, and unused supply, as in the discrete feasible-allocation definition.
  • Used explicit network encodings with unrestricted input depths and widths, defined total network size H and input bit length L, and added a binary-rational welfare threshold B for the decision version.
  • Represented optimal assignments by nonzero bundle-mass entries so that the computational question does not require printing all exponentially many zero entries.

Result

The exact welfare-maximization problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Its threshold decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.

Hardness holds already with:

  • One listed type, of mass \(1\).
  • Supply \(\tfrac12\) for every item.
  • A valuation taking only the values \(0\) and \(1\).
  • Two hidden layers, all weights in \(\{0,1\}\), and integer nonpositive biases.
  • Threshold \(B=1\).

The proof explicitly allows arbitrary splitting of the type’s mass among bundles.

1. Reduction from Boolean satisfiability

We reduce from satisfiability of conjunctive normal form formulas with at most three literals per clause, a standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem. See [Karp’s original paper](https://www2.seas.gwu.edu/~simhaweb/champalg/tsp/papers/KarpOriginal.pdf).

Let the input formula be \(F=C_1\land\cdots\land C_r\), on Boolean variables \(y_1,\ldots,y_n\). We may assume \(n\ge1\) and \(r\ge1\): adding a fresh variable and a tautological clause enforces these conditions without changing satisfiability.

Introduce two items \(u_i^+,u_i^-\) for each variable, and two selector items \(a,b\). Thus the item set is

\(\mathcal M=\{u_i^+,u_i^-:1\le i\le n\}\cup\{a,b\},\qquad m=2n+2.\)

There is one type, with mass \(1\). Set \(q_j=\tfrac12\) for every item, and set \(B=1\).

For each clause \(C_k\), define its literal-item set

\(P_k=\{u_i^+:y_i\text{ occurs in }C_k\}\cup\{u_i^-:\neg y_i\text{ occurs in }C_k\}.\)

Let \(Q_k\) be obtained from \(P_k\) by exchanging \(u_i^+\) and \(u_i^-\) for every variable.

Consider the following list of \(K=n+1+2r\) item sets:

  • \(\{u_i^+,u_i^-\}\), for every \(1\le i\le n\).
  • \(\{a,b\}\).
  • \(P_k\cup\{b\}\), for every \(1\le k\le r\).
  • \(Q_k\cup\{a\}\), for every \(1\le k\le r\).

Define \(v(S)=1\) precisely when \(S\) intersects every set in this list; otherwise define \(v(S)=0\).

This valuation is monotone. We next give its explicit permitted MVNN encoding.

2. Exact two-layer MVNN encoding

Write the listed sets as \(R_1,\ldots,R_K\). For a bundle indicator \(z^0=\mathbf1_S\), the first hidden layer has one neuron per listed set:

\(h_\ell=\phi\!\left(\sum_{j\in R_\ell}z^0_j\right),\qquad 1\le\ell\le K.\)

Its weights belong to \(\{0,1\}\), and its biases are zero. Since the inputs are Boolean,

\(h_\ell=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ S\cap R_\ell\ne\varnothing,\)

and otherwise \(h_\ell=0\).

The second hidden layer consists of one neuron:

\(g=\phi\!\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}h_\ell-(K-1)\right).\)

Its weights are all \(1\), and its bias is the nonpositive integer \(-(K-1)\). Because the first-layer outputs are Boolean, \(g=1\) exactly when all of them equal \(1\); otherwise \(g=0\).

Finally, use the scalar readout \(v(S)=g\), with weight \(1\) and zero bias. This realizes the required valuation exactly and gives \(v(\varnothing)=0\).

The first hidden layer has width \(K\), and the second has width \(1\). Even when every matrix entry, including zeros, is explicitly encoded, the network uses \(O((n+r)(n+1))\) entries. Its largest bias has magnitude \(K-1\). Consequently, the entire constructed instance has polynomial encoding length and is computable in polynomial time.

3. Characterization of minimum-size valuable bundles

Lemma. Every bundle \(S\) with \(v(S)=1\) has \(|S|\ge n+1\). Moreover, a bundle with \(v(S)=1\) and \(|S|=n+1\) exists if and only if \(F\) is satisfiable.

Proof. A valuable bundle must intersect all \(n\) variable pairs and the selector pair. These \(n+1\) pairs are disjoint, so it contains at least \(n+1\) items.

If it contains exactly \(n+1\) items, it contains exactly one item from each pair. There are two selector cases.

If \(a\in S\), then \(b\notin S\). The requirements involving \(P_k\cup\{b\}\) therefore force \(S\cap P_k\ne\varnothing\) for every clause. Define \(y_i\) to be true exactly when \(u_i^+\in S\). Since exactly one of \(u_i^+,u_i^-\) is selected, intersection with \(P_k\) means that clause \(C_k\) has a true literal. Hence this assignment satisfies \(F\).

If \(b\in S\), then \(a\notin S\). Now the requirements involving \(Q_k\cup\{a\}\) force \(S\cap Q_k\ne\varnothing\) for every clause. Define \(y_i\) to be true exactly when \(u_i^-\in S\). By the definition of \(Q_k\), these intersections again mean that every clause has a true literal.

Conversely, if \(\sigma\) satisfies \(F\), select \(a\) and, for every variable, select \(u_i^+\) when \(\sigma(y_i)\) is true and \(u_i^-\) when it is false. This bundle has size \(n+1\). It intersects every variable pair and the selector pair, every \(P_k\) because \(\sigma\) satisfies \(F\), and every \(Q_k\cup\{a\}\) because it contains \(a\). Its value is therefore \(1\). This proves the lemma.

4. Satisfiable formulas attain welfare one

Suppose \(\sigma\) satisfies \(F\). Let \(S_\sigma\) be the bundle constructed in the lemma, and let \(S_\sigma^c=\mathcal M\setminus S_\sigma\).

We already know \(v(S_\sigma)=1\). Its complement contains \(b\), exactly one item from each variable pair, and intersects every \(Q_k\): exchanging the variable items exchanges \(P_k\) with \(Q_k\). It also intersects every \(P_k\cup\{b\}\) because it contains \(b\). Thus \(v(S_\sigma^c)=1\).

Assign mass \(\tfrac12\) to each of these complementary bundles. Every item appears in exactly one of them, so each item’s consumption is exactly \(\tfrac12\). The assignment is feasible and has welfare \(1\).

No assignment can have welfare greater than \(1\), since total mass is \(1\) and every bundle value is at most \(1\). Therefore, when \(F\) is satisfiable,

\(\operatorname{OPT}=1.\)

5. Unsatisfiable formulas have a strict welfare gap

Suppose \(F\) is unsatisfiable. By the lemma, every bundle of value \(1\) has at least \(n+2\) items.

For any feasible mass assignment, write its bundle masses as \(x_S\). Summing all item-supply constraints gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal M}x_S|S|=\sum_{j\in\mathcal M}\sum_{S\ni j}x_S\le\sum_{j\in\mathcal M}q_j=n+1.\)

Because \(v(S)\) is Boolean, the welfare equals the total mass assigned to valuable bundles. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle (n+2)\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal M}x_Sv(S)\le\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal M}x_S|S|\le n+1.\)

It follows that

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{OPT}\le\frac{n+1}{n+2}<1.\)

This inequality applies to every fractional assignment, regardless of how many bundles receive positive mass.

Combining the two cases proves

\(F\text{ is satisfiable}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \operatorname{OPT}\ge B,\qquad B=1.\)

Thus the threshold decision problem, and hence exact welfare maximization, is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

6. Polynomial-size certificates for the general problem

We now prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), allowing all networks and rational inputs specified in the question.

Sparse optimal assignments

Introduce one nonnegative slack variable for each item-supply constraint. The assignment problem becomes a finite linear program

\(\max c^\top y\quad\text{subject to}\quad Ay=b,\quad y\ge0,\)

with \(d=\tau+m\) equality rows. All entries of \(A\) belong to \(\{0,1\}\). Its columns comprise the bundle-mass variables and the supply slacks.

The matrix has row rank \(d\): the empty-bundle column for each type is its type-row unit vector, and each slack column is its item-row unit vector.

The feasible set is nonempty, since assigning each type entirely to the empty bundle is feasible. It is bounded: each bundle mass is at most its type mass, and each slack is at most its item supply. It is also closed. Thus it is a nonempty compact polytope, and a linear objective attains its maximum at a vertex.

At a vertex, the columns corresponding to positive coordinates are linearly independent. Indeed, a dependence would give a nonzero vector \(h\), supported on those coordinates, with \(Ah=0\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both \(y+\varepsilon h\) and \(y-\varepsilon h\) would remain nonnegative and feasible. Their midpoint would be \(y\), contradicting that \(y\) is a vertex.

Therefore some optimal assignment has at most \(d=\tau+m\) positive bundle-mass entries.

Polynomial bit length of the masses

Extend the positive-coordinate columns of this vertex to a basis of \(d\) columns of \(A\). The resulting square matrix \(A_0\) is nonsingular and has entries in \(\{0,1\}\). The vertex coordinates on these columns solve \(A_0y_0=b\).

Let \(D_b\) be the product of the denominators of the entries of \(b\). Its bit length is \(O(L)\), and the integer entries of \(D_bb\) also have bit length \(O(L)\).

By Cramer’s rule, each coordinate is a ratio with denominator \(D_b\det(A_0)\) and numerator a determinant obtained by replacing one column of \(A_0\) with \(D_bb\). Determinant expansion bounds their bit lengths by \(O(L+d\log d)\). Hence the nonzero bundle masses admit polynomial-length rational encodings.

Polynomial-time verification

A certificate lists at most \(\tau+m\) entries, each specifying a type, an \(m\)-bit bundle indicator, and a rational mass. The type-mass equalities and supply inequalities can be checked by exact rational arithmetic.

Each network can also be evaluated exactly in polynomial time. To justify the bit bound, fix the clipping outcomes during an evaluation. Each remaining neuron expression expands into sums of products along directed paths. A path uses each encoded parameter occurrence at most once. Thus every intermediate denominator divides the product of all network-parameter denominators, whose bit length is \(O(L)\).

Hidden outputs lie in \([0,1]\), and preactivations and the final readout have magnitudes bounded by the sums of the relevant encoded coefficient magnitudes. Their numerators therefore also have polynomial bit length. Exact evaluation uses polynomially many operations on polynomial-length integers.

The verifier can consequently compute the certificate’s exact welfare and compare it with \(B\) in polynomial time. Whenever \(\operatorname{OPT}\ge B\), the sparse optimal assignment above supplies such a certificate.

The threshold decision problem therefore belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\) and is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. In particular, a polynomial-time exact optimization algorithm in \(m,\tau,H,L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
