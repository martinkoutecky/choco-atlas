The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) options \(C\) and a list of \(\tau\) strict partial-order types \(t\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), compute the exact limit \(q^\infty\) from the uniform initial distribution under \(q^{(k+1)}=q^{(k)}P\), where \(P_{ij}=\frac{1}{m-1}\sum_{t:\,x_i\prec_t x_j}\mu_t\) for \(i\ne j\) and \(P_{ii}=1-\sum_{j\ne i}P_{ij}\). Return the induced ranking and winner set, including when the chain has multiple closed classes.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C=\{x_1,\ldots,x_m\}\), with \(m\geq2\). A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct strict partial-order types, each represented by its comparison matrix and a positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
The relation \(x_i\prec_t x_j\) means that type \(t\) prefers \(x_j\) to \(x_i\); incomparable options contribute no preference in either direction.
Define \(P_{ij}=\frac{1}{m-1}\sum_{t:x_i\prec_t x_j}\mu_t\) for \(i\ne j\), and \(P_{ii}=1-\sum_{j\ne i}P_{ij}\). Starting from \(q^{(0)}=(1/m,\ldots,1/m)\), let \(q^{(k+1)}=q^{(k)}P\).
The computational problem is to compute \(q^\infty=\lim_{k\to\infty}q^{(k)}\) exactly as rational coordinates, together with the ranking by decreasing coordinates, retaining ties, and the winner set \(\{x_i:q_i^\infty=\max_j q_j^\infty\}\). Multiple closed communication classes are allowed; the uniform initial distribution determines the output.

Is this exact computation problem solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For an explicit list of \(n\) voters, convergence voting has a deterministic polynomial-time ranking algorithm, with stated arithmetic-operation bound \(O(m^2n+m^3)\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.01995v3)
A weighted-voter formulation using a convex combination of individual transition matrices is also known, but it supplies no complexity bound for exact computation with binary-rational masses given by support. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.01995v3)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Excluded fewer than two options because the transition formula divides by m-1.

  • Specified that the induced ranking retains equal-score ties and that all maximum-score options belong to the winner set.

Result

The exact computation problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). This includes computing every rational coordinate, retaining all ranking ties, and identifying the complete winner set.

The supplied paper gives the explicit-voter arithmetic bound in Proposition 3.8 and discusses weighted transition matrices in Section 4.2. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.01995v3). Below, we establish convergence and polynomial bit complexity directly for the stated support representation.

1. The transition matrix has polynomial-size rational entries

Write each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), where the positive integers \(a_t,b_t\) are encoded in binary. Set

\(B=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t,\qquad D=(m-1)B.\)

Then \(P=A/D\), where the integer matrix \(A\) is given by

\(A_{ij}=\sum_{t:x_i\prec_t x_j}a_t(B/b_t)\quad(i\ne j),\qquad A_{ii}=D-\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}.\)

For every row, the sum of its off-diagonal entries in \(P\) is at most \(1\): each type contributes its mass multiplied by at most \((m-1)/(m-1)\). Consequently, \(P\) is a stochastic matrix and

\(0\le A_{ij}\le D.\)

Moreover,

\(\log_2D=\log_2(m-1)+\sum_t\log_2b_t=O(L+\log m).\)

Thus, constructing \(A\) requires \(O(m^2\tau)\) arithmetic operations on integers of polynomial bit length. No expansion into an explicit voter list is needed.

2. Convergence holds, including with multiple closed classes

Consider the directed graph with an edge \(i\to j\) whenever \(P_{ij}>0\). Its strongly connected components with no outgoing edges are precisely its closed communication classes. Denote them by \(H_1,\ldots,H_c\), and let \(T\) contain all remaining states.

Positive self-loops in every closed class

Let \(d_t(i)\) be the number of options strictly preferred to \(x_i\) by type \(t\). Then

\(P_{ii}=\sum_t\mu_t\left(1-\frac{d_t(i)}{m-1}\right).\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Because every mass is positive, \(P_{ii}=0\) implies \(d_t(i)=m-1\) for every type: all types prefer every other option to \(x_i\).

A strict partial order is asymmetric. Therefore, for every \(j\ne i\), no type has \(x_j\prec_t x_i\), and hence \(P_{ji}=0\).

Such a state cannot belong to a strongly connected component containing another state, because it has no incoming edge from another state. Its singleton component is not closed either, since its outgoing off-diagonal probabilities sum to \(1\). Thus,

\(i\in H_a\ \Longrightarrow\ P_{ii}>0.\)

Convergence within a closed class

Fix a closed class \(H_a\) of size \(s\), and write \(S=P_{H_a,H_a}\). This matrix is stochastic and irreducible, and every diagonal entry is positive.

Between any two states there is a directed simple path of length at most \(s-1\). Positive self-loops allow padding that path to length \(s\). Consequently, every entry of \(S^s\) is positive.

Let \(\varepsilon=\min_{i,j}(S^s)_{ij}>0\), and let \(J\) be the all-ones matrix. If \(s\varepsilon<1\), then

\(S^s=\varepsilon J+(1-s\varepsilon)U\)

for a stochastic matrix \(U\). For every row vector \(z\) whose coordinates sum to zero, \(zJ=0\), and therefore

\(\|zS^s\|_1\le(1-s\varepsilon)\|z\|_1.\)

If \(s\varepsilon=1\), then \(zS^s=0\), giving an even stronger conclusion.

A stationary probability vector exists: for any probability row vector \(v\), the averages

\(v_N=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}vS^k\)

have a convergent subsequence in the compact probability simplex, and

\(v_NS-v_N=(vS^N-v)/N\longrightarrow0.\)

Any subsequential limit is therefore stationary. The contraction just proved shows that this stationary probability vector, denoted \(\pi_a\), is unique, and that every probability vector on \(H_a\) converges to it under iteration of \(S\).

The same contraction also shows that any signed stationary vector with coordinate sum zero must be zero. We will use this to justify uniqueness of the linear systems below.

The remaining states are transient

Suppose \(T\) is nonempty, and set \(Q=P_{T,T}\). Every state in \(T\) has a directed path to a closed class: follow the finite acyclic graph of strongly connected components to a component with no outgoing edge.

Choose one such path from each state in \(T\). These paths can be taken to have length at most \(m-1\), and each has positive transition-probability product. Let \(\delta>0\) be the minimum of those products.

From every state in \(T\), the probability of reaching a closed class by time \(m\) is at least \(\delta\). Once reached, a closed class cannot be left. Thus, using the maximum absolute row-sum matrix norm,

\(\|Q^m\|_\infty\le1-\delta,\qquad \|Q^{km}\|_\infty\le(1-\delta)^k.\)

It follows that \(Q^k\to0\), the series \(\sum_{k\ge0}Q^k\) converges, and

\((I-Q)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^k.\)

These arguments establish convergence without assuming a unique closed class. The quantities \(\varepsilon,\delta\) are used only in the proof; the algorithm does not simulate convergence.

3. An exact formula determined by the uniform initial distribution

For each closed class define

\(S_a=P_{H_a,H_a},\qquad R_a=P_{T,H_a}.\)

Compute its stationary probability row vector by solving

\(\pi_aS_a=\pi_a,\qquad \pi_a\mathbf1=1.\)

When \(T\) is nonempty, also solve

\((I-Q)h_a=R_a\mathbf1.\)

The vector \(h_a\) gives the probabilities of eventual absorption into \(H_a\) from states in \(T\). Indeed, its series representation is

\(h_a=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}Q^rR_a\mathbf1,\)

whose term indexed by \(r\) is the probability of spending the first \(r\) transitions in \(T\) and entering \(H_a\) on the next transition.

For the uniform initial distribution, define

\(w_a=\frac{|H_a|+\sum_{i\in T}(h_a)_i}{m}.\)

Then the exact answer is

\(q_i^\infty=0\quad(i\in T),\qquad q_{H_a}^\infty=w_a\pi_a.\)

To verify this formula, let \(u_T\) and \(u_a\) be the restrictions of the uniform initial row vector to \(T\) and \(H_a\). Direct induction on the iteration count gives

\(q_{H_a}^{(k)}=u_aS_a^k+\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}u_TQ^rR_aS_a^{k-1-r}.\)

For each fixed \(r\), convergence within \(H_a\) sends the corresponding summand to

\((u_TQ^rR_a\mathbf1)\pi_a.\)

Furthermore, its norm is at most \(\|u_TQ^r\|_1\), and these bounds are summable by the preceding geometric estimate. We may therefore pass to the limit in the sum, obtaining

\(q_{H_a}^\infty=(u_a\mathbf1+u_Th_a)\pi_a=w_a\pi_a.\)

Also \(q_T^{(k)}=u_TQ^k\to0\). If \(T\) is empty, the absorption systems are omitted and \(w_a=|H_a|/m\).

4. Exact linear algebra has polynomial bit complexity

The communication classes are found by standard strongly connected component algorithms on a graph with at most \(m^2\) edges.

All required linear systems can be written with integer coefficients bounded in absolute value by \(D\).

For the absorption systems, multiply by \(D\):

\((DI-A_{T,T})h_a=A_{T,H_a}\mathbf1.\)

Every right-hand-side entry is at most \(D\), because it sums part of a row of \(A\). The coefficient matrix is nonsingular by Section 2.

For a stationary system, use the column unknown \(y=\pi_a^{\mathsf T}\). Start with

\((DI-A_{H_a,H_a}^{\mathsf T})y=0,\)

and replace one equation by \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}y=1\). The omitted stationary equation follows from the others: the sum of all stationary residual coordinates is zero because \(S_a\mathbf1=\mathbf1\).

This square system is nonsingular. A vector in its homogeneous kernel would be stationary with coordinate sum zero, and the contraction in Section 2 forces that vector to vanish.

Set \(b=\lceil\log_2(D+1)\rceil\). For any square minor of order \(r\le m\) of one of these coefficient matrices or its right-hand-side augmentation, Hadamard’s determinant inequality gives

\(|\det M|\le r^{r/2}D^r.\)

Hence every such determinant has bit length

\(O(m(b+\log m))=O(m(L+\log m)).\)

Cramer’s rule consequently bounds the numerator and denominator lengths of all solution coordinates by this polynomial quantity.

The systems can also be computed in polynomial bit time. For example, fraction-free Gaussian elimination with pivoting uses polynomially many integer operations, with intermediate entries controlled by minors of the input matrix. Equivalently, reduced rational Gaussian elimination has Schur-complement entries given by ratios of such minors. The determinant bound therefore controls intermediate bit lengths; ordinary binary arithmetic and fraction reduction take polynomial time.

There are at most \(m\) absorption right-hand sides and at most \(m\) stationary systems, each of dimension at most \(m\). Computing the weights \(w_a\) and products \(w_a\pi_a\) uses polynomially many further rational operations on numbers of polynomial bit length.

Finally, compare rational coordinates exactly by cross multiplication, sort them, group equal coordinates, and select the largest group. These operations also take polynomial bit time.

Conclusion

The complete exact output belongs to \(\mathrm{FP}\): it is computable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Multiple closed classes are handled by their absorption weights from the specified uniform initial distribution.
