Corollary 1.2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite chore catalogue \(M\), finite agent-type set \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), restricted-additive costs \(c_t(e)\in\{0,c(e)\}\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_e\), compute a mass allocation \(\lambda_{t,B}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq M\) satisfying \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:e\in B}\lambda_{t,B}=\rho_e\). Require support-wise EFX: for every \(t\), every \(B\) with \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\), every supported bundle \(B'\), and every \(e\in B\), \(c_t(B\setminus\{e\})\le c_t(B')\). Define \(z_t^\star\) as the minimum, over distributions \(\pi\) on integral bundles with item marginals \(\rho\), of \(\max_{B:\pi_B>0}c_t(B)\), require \(c_t(B)\le(1+\varepsilon)z_t^\star\) whenever \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\), and require \(\mathrm{SC}(\lambda)=\sum_{t,B}\lambda_{t,B}c_t(B)\le2\,\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\rho)\); determine whether this can be computed in time polynomial in the compressed input.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) be a finite chore catalogue with \(m=|M|\). A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \(T\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_t\) a binary rational, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Costs are restricted additive: global costs \(c(e)\) are nonnegative binary rationals and \(c_t(e)\in\{0,c(e)\}\). Supplies \(\rho_e\in[0,1]\) are binary rationals; bundles \(B\subseteq M\) may be empty and contain at most one copy of each chore. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A mass allocation has nonnegative rational masses \(\lambda_{t,B}\) satisfying \(\sum_{B\subseteq M}\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:e\in B}\lambda_{t,B}=\rho_e\). Its supported bundles are \(\mathcal S(\lambda)=\{B:\sum_t\lambda_{t,B}>0\}\).
Support-wise EFX requires \(c_t(B\setminus\{e\})\le c_t(B')\) whenever \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\), \(B'\in\mathcal S(\lambda)\), and \(e\in B\), including chores of zero cost to \(t\).
Define \(z_t^\star=\min_{\pi}\max_{B:\pi_B>0}c_t(B)\), where \(\pi\) ranges over probability distributions on \(2^M\) satisfying \(\sum_{B:e\in B}\pi_B=\rho_e\) for every \(e\).
Define \(\mathrm{SC}(\lambda)=\sum_{t,B}\lambda_{t,B}c_t(B)\), and let \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\rho)\) be its minimum over all mass allocations satisfying the mass and supply equations, without fairness constraints.
The required allocation is support-wise EFX and satisfies \(c_t(B)\le(1+\varepsilon)z_t^\star\) whenever \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\), together with \(\mathrm{SC}(\lambda)\le2\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\rho)\).

For every fixed rational \(\varepsilon>0\), is finding an allocation with these simultaneous guarantees, or determining that none exists, polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this problem NP-hard?

For finite agents, a polynomial-time algorithm achieves EFX, \((1+\varepsilon)\)-MMS, and social cost at most \(2\mathrm{OPT}\) for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08864v1)
Earlier discrete work establishes simultaneous EFX and exact MMS with the same social-cost bound. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17270v1) No result settling precisely the mass-allocation problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one so that supplies and the benchmark distribution use the same per-capita units.
  • Restricted each rational supply to [0,1], as bundles contain at most one copy of each catalogue item.
  • Specified nonnegative binary-rational global costs; retained zero costs and empty bundles, as permitted by the discrete definitions.
  • Defined OPT over all mass allocations satisfying conservation, without fairness constraints, matching the discrete unrestricted social-cost benchmark.
  • Treated epsilon as a fixed positive rational, with running time polynomial in m, tau, and total input bit length L; no polynomial dependence on inverse epsilon is required.
  • Made the computational problem include determining nonexistence because arbitrary catalogue supplies come without an existence promise.

Result

For every fixed rational \(\varepsilon>0\), the problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with one supported type, strictly positive integer costs, and supplies belonging to \(\{1/2,1\}\).

The reduction uses support-wise EFX to force equal costs across all supported bundles. On yes-instances, the constructed allocation also satisfies exact MMS and achieves optimal social cost.

1. Reduction from PARTITION

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem PARTITION: given positive binary integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), determine whether some \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i\in I}a_i=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n a_i.\)

Its \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness is established in [Karp’s “Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems”](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Write \(W=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\). Construct the following instance:

  • There is one type \(t\), with \(\mu_t=1\).
  • The catalogue is \(M=\{d,e_1,\ldots,e_n\}\).
  • Costs are \(c_t(d)=c(d)=1\) and \(c_t(e_i)=c(e_i)=2a_i\).
  • Supplies are \(\rho_d=1\) and \(\rho_{e_i}=1/2\).

These are restricted additive costs, and every chore has strictly positive cost. The construction has \(m=n+1\), \(\tau=1\), and encoding length polynomial in the PARTITION input length. It is independent of \(\varepsilon\).

2. EFX forces an exact partition

Suppose the constructed instance admits a support-wise EFX allocation. Since there is only one type, abbreviate \(\lambda_{t,B}\) to \(\lambda_B\).

The mass and supply equations give

\(\displaystyle \sum_B\lambda_B=1=\sum_{B:d\in B}\lambda_B.\)

Consequently, \(\sum_{B:d\notin B}\lambda_B=0\). Nonnegativity implies that every supported bundle contains \(d\).

For each supported bundle, define the integer

\(\displaystyle s(B)=\sum_{i:e_i\in B}a_i.\)

Its cost is \(c_t(B)=1+2s(B)\). For any two supported bundles \(B,B'\), apply EFX to removal of \(d\) from \(B\):

\(\displaystyle 2s(B)=c_t(B\setminus\{d\})\le c_t(B')=1+2s(B').\)

Thus \(2(s(B)-s(B'))\le1\). Since \(s(B)-s(B')\) is an integer, this implies \(s(B)\le s(B')\). Applying the same argument with \(B,B'\) exchanged proves equality.

Hence all supported bundles have a common value \(s(B)=q\). The marginal equations then imply

\(\displaystyle q=\sum_B\lambda_Bs(B) =\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\sum_{B:e_i\in B}\lambda_B =\frac W2.\)

At least one bundle is supported because total mass is \(1\). Choosing any such bundle yields a subset \(I=\{i:e_i\in B\}\) with \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=W/2\).

Therefore, existence of a support-wise EFX allocation already implies that the PARTITION instance is a yes-instance.

3. A partition yields all three guarantees

Conversely, suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=W/2\). Define

\(\displaystyle B_1=\{d\}\cup\{e_i:i\in I\},\qquad B_2=\{d\}\cup\{e_i:i\notin I\},\)

and assign mass \(1/2\) to each bundle, with all other masses zero.

The masses sum to \(1\). Chore \(d\) appears in both bundles, while each \(e_i\) appears in exactly one. Thus every supply equation holds. Moreover,

\(\displaystyle c_t(B_1)=c_t(B_2)=W+1.\)

Support-wise EFX

For either supported bundle \(B\), every \(e\in B\), and either supported comparison bundle \(B'\), nonnegativity gives

\(\displaystyle c_t(B\setminus\{e\})\le c_t(B)=W+1=c_t(B').\)

Thus the allocation is support-wise EFX.

Exact MMS

For every probability distribution \(\pi\) satisfying the prescribed marginals, additivity gives the same expected cost:

\(\displaystyle \sum_B\pi_Bc_t(B) =\sum_{e\in M}\rho_ec_t(e) =1+\sum_{i=1}^n\frac12(2a_i) =W+1.\)

Its maximum supported cost is at least this expectation. Therefore \(z_t^\star\ge W+1\).

The distribution assigning probability \(1/2\) to each of \(B_1,B_2\) has maximum supported cost \(W+1\). Hence

\(\displaystyle z_t^\star=W+1.\)

Both allocated bundles therefore satisfy exact MMS, and consequently satisfy the required \((1+\varepsilon)\)-MMS guarantee.

Optimal social cost

Likewise, every feasible mass allocation has social cost

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{SC}(\lambda)=\sum_{e\in M}\rho_ec_t(e)=W+1.\)

Unrestricted feasibility holds for every constructed instance: assign mass \(1/2\) to \(\{d\}\) and mass \(1/2\) to \(M\). Thus \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\rho)=W+1\).

The allocation obtained from the partition achieves \(\mathrm{SC}(\lambda)=\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\rho)\), so it satisfies the required social-cost bound.

4. Complexity conclusion

We have proved the equivalence:

\(\displaystyle \text{PARTITION is a yes-instance} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed instance admits all three guarantees}. \)

This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction to the existence decision problem. An algorithm that finds a qualifying allocation or correctly determines nonexistence would therefore decide PARTITION.

Accordingly, the requested task is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard for every fixed rational \(\varepsilon>0\), and cannot have a polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The reduction does not depend on empty bundles or zero-cost chores. Its essential feature is supply \(1\) for the inexpensive chore \(d\): every supported bundle must contain it, and EFX for its removal forces exact equality because all other bundle costs differ by multiples of \(2\). This constraint differs from the ordinary discrete model, where a single chore belongs to only one agent’s bundle.
