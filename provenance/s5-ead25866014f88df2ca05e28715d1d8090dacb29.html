Theorem 4.1 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a mass-one society with finitely many additive valuation types of rational masses and a fixed finite online stream of cohorts, where cohort \(r\) supplies mass \(q_r\) of identical indivisible goods, determine whether every randomized nonanticipating policy has worst-case expected population EFX score zero. A policy moves mass among type-and-integral-bundle states, assigning cohort \(r\)'s goods to mass \(q_r\) of agents without knowing later cohorts or the stopping horizon. Equivalently, for every policy and \(\alpha,p>0\), is there a fixed finite cohort stream with probability less than \(p\) of terminal EFX score at least \(\alpha\), where the score is the minimum EFX comparison over positive-mass agent states?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is atomless and given by its support: \(\tau\geq1\) additive valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(w_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Only type labels and masses are initially known; future values are not.
A non-adaptive adversary fixes a finite stream \(I=((q_r,(v_{tr})_{t=1}^{\tau}))_{r=1}^{m}\), with binary-rational \(0<q_r\leq1\) and \(v_{tr}\geq0\). Cohort \(r\) supplies mass \(q_r\) of identical copies, each worth \(v_{tr}\) to type \(t\). There is no normalization of total values.
Initially all bundles are empty. On arrival, the cohort's mass and valuation vector are revealed. A randomized nonanticipating policy immediately and irrevocably assigns one whole copy to each agent in a selected mass \(q_r\); it may split any type-and-bundle state's mass into arbitrary real submasses. Goods cannot be discarded, and neither future cohorts nor the stopping horizon are announced.
A bundle \(B\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) records the cohorts received, with \(v_t(B)=\sum_{r\in B}v_{tr}\). For positive-mass states \((t,B),(u,C)\), put \(d_t(C)=v_t(C)-\min_{r\in C}v_{tr}\) when \(C\neq\varnothing\), and \(d_t(\varnothing)=0\).
Define \(h(x,0)=1\) and \(h(x,d)=\min\{1,x/d\}\) for \(d>0\). The realized population EFX score is \(\rho=\min_{(t,B),(u,C)}h(v_t(B),d_t(C))\), including comparisons within the same state and deletion of zero-valued copies.
For fixed support masses \(w\), define \(R_A(w)=\inf_I\mathbb E_A[\rho(A,I)]\), over all admissible finite streams, independently of the policy's randomness. Any running times are measured in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the total input bit length \(L\).

Does \(R_A(w)=0\) hold for every support-mass vector \(w\) and every randomized nonanticipating policy \(A\), or do some \(w\) and \(A\) satisfy \(R_A(w)>0\)?
Equivalently, for every \(w,A,\alpha\in(0,1],p\in(0,1)\), does some fixed finite stream \(I\) satisfy \(\Pr_A(\rho(A,I)\geq\alpha)<p\)?

For discrete societies with \(n\geq2\), every randomized fully online algorithm has arbitrarily small success probability for every positive EFX factor, even with identical valuations, and worst-case expected EFX score zero. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23577v2#S4.SS1)

  • Resolved the conflict between a given fixed stream and a worst-case guarantee by quantifying over all finite streams, with the support masses held fixed.
  • Preserved the fully online information restriction: type labels and masses are known initially, but valuation vectors are revealed only upon cohort arrival.
  • Made the population atomless, allowing arbitrary real splits of state masses while keeping each individual's bundle integral.
  • Interpreted assignment to mass q_r of agents as at most one copy per agent per cohort, requiring binary-rational 0<q_r<=1 with no uniform positive lower bound.

Conclusion

For every support-mass vector \(w\) and every randomized nonanticipating policy \(A\), we have \(R_A(w)=0\). The impossibility holds even when all types value every cohort identically and every cohort has mass \(1/2\).

The proof adapts the rapidly increasing values and unannounced stopping prefixes used in the cited [discrete impossibility theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23577v2#S4.SS1). In the atomless model, the relevant bounded resource is the mass of agents whose bundles remain empty.

1. A fixed family of admissible inputs

Fix \(w,A,\alpha\in(0,1]\), and \(p\in(0,1)\). Choose \(0<\varepsilon<\alpha\), an integer \(K>2/p\), and a power of two \(b\) such that \(1/(b-1)<\varepsilon\).

For each \(r\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), define cohort \(r\) by

\(q_r=1/2,\qquad v_{tr}=s_r:=b^{r-1}\quad\text{for every type }t.\)

Let \(I_T\) be the stream consisting of the first \(T\) cohorts. These are admissible finite streams: the masses and values are positive binary rationals. The construction uses no normalization of total values.

Write \(P_{r-1}:=\sum_{h<r}s_h\). For \(r\geq2\), the geometric-sum formula gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{P_{r-1}}{s_r}=\frac{1-b^{-(r-1)}}{b-1}<\varepsilon.\)

In particular, the current value \(s_r\) exceeds the sum of all earlier values.

2. Receiving a new cohort in a nonempty bundle forces a small score

Consider any realized allocation immediately after cohort \(r\). Suppose a positive mass of its recipients had nonempty bundles immediately before its arrival.

There are finitely many type-and-bundle states at this prefix. Consequently, some positive-mass resulting state \((u,C)\) contains cohort \(r\) and at least one earlier cohort. Because all types have the common values \(s_h\), the least-valued cohort in \(C\) is an earlier one. Deleting it leaves cohort \(r\), so, for every type \(t\),

\(d_t(C)\geq s_r.\)

The mass of agents who did not receive cohort \(r\) is \(1/2\). Thus some positive-mass state \((t,B)\) satisfies \(r\notin B\). Each agent receives at most one copy from any cohort, hence

\(v_t(B)\leq P_{r-1}.\)

The comparison from \((t,B)\) to \((u,C)\) therefore yields

\(\displaystyle \rho_r\leq h(v_t(B),d_t(C))\leq\frac{P_{r-1}}{s_r}<\varepsilon<\alpha,\)

where \(\rho_r\) denotes the score after cohort \(r\). Such an overlap cannot occur at \(r=1\), since all bundles are initially empty.

It follows that success at threshold \(\alpha\) requires the entire recipient mass \(1/2\) to have had empty bundles, up to sets of mass zero.

3. At most two prefixes can succeed on any realization

Let \(f_r\) be the mass of cohort \(r\)'s recipients whose bundles were empty immediately before its arrival. Let \(E_r\) be the empty-bundle mass immediately before that arrival.

Irrevocability gives

\(E_1=1,\qquad E_{r+1}=E_r-f_r.\)

Since empty-bundle mass is nonnegative, telescoping gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{r=1}^{K}f_r=1-E_{K+1}\leq1.\)

Call an arrival *fresh* if \(f_r=1/2\). Every fresh arrival consumes mass \(1/2\) from the initially empty population. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \frac12\,\#\{r:f_r=1/2\}\leq\sum_{r=1}^{K}f_r\leq1,\)

so there are at most two fresh arrivals. By Section 2, every successful prefix ends at a fresh arrival. Thus, on every realization,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{r=1}^{K}\mathbf 1\{\rho_r\geq\alpha\}\leq2.\)

This bound permits arbitrary real splitting of states and uses no restriction on the support masses.

4. Selecting a fixed bad prefix against a randomized policy

Couple the runs on \(I_1,\ldots,I_K\) using the same internal randomness. Because the policy is nonanticipating and the horizon is unannounced, its allocation on \(I_T\) agrees with its allocation after \(T\) arrivals on \(I_K\).

Taking expectations in the preceding pathwise bound gives

\(\displaystyle \frac1K\sum_{T=1}^{K}\Pr_A\!\bigl(\rho(A,I_T)\geq\alpha\bigr)\leq\frac2K<p.\)

Hence some \(T\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \Pr_A\!\bigl(\rho(A,I_T)\geq\alpha\bigr)\leq\frac2K<p.\)

This selects a fixed finite stream using the policy's probability law, without observing its realized randomness. It proves the requested small-success-probability statement against a non-adaptive adversary.

5. Worst-case expected score

The same construction directly bounds the expected score. At nonfresh arrivals, Section 2 gives \(\rho_r<\varepsilon\); at fresh arrivals, \(\rho_r\leq1\). There are at most two fresh arrivals, so

\(\displaystyle \frac1K\sum_{r=1}^{K}\rho_r\leq\varepsilon+\frac2K.\)

Taking expectations and selecting a prefix yields some fixed \(I_T\) with

\(\displaystyle \mathbb E_A[\rho(A,I_T)]\leq\varepsilon+\frac2K.\)

For any \(\eta>0\), choose \(0<\varepsilon<\min\{1,\eta/2\}\) and an integer \(K>4/\eta\), and construct the family above. Then some prefix has expected score less than \(\eta\). Since scores are nonnegative, their worst-case infimum is zero:

\(R_A(w)=0.\)

Thus no support-mass vector and randomized nonanticipating policy in the stated model have a positive worst-case expected EFX score.

\[ \rho_r \leq h(v_t(B),d_t(C)) \leq \frac{P_{r-1}}{s_r} < \varepsilon < \alpha. \]

The argument correctly excludes \(r=1\), when no recipient can have a previously nonempty bundle. Thus success implies that all recipient mass was previously empty, modulo null sets. No converse is needed or claimed.

3. Empty-bundle mass accounting

The recurrence \(E_{r+1}=E_r-f_r\) follows from irrevocability: precisely the previously empty recipients cease to be empty, and no agent becomes empty.

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{K}f_r=1-E_{K+1}\leq1. \]

\[ \sum_{r=1}^{K}\mathbf{1}\{\rho_r\geq\alpha\}\leq2 \]

Consequently, at least one fixed prefix has success probability below \(p\). Selecting it from the policy’s probability law does not make the adversary adaptive: the selected stream is independent of realized random choices.
