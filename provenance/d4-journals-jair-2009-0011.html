The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite \(\Theta\), rational prior \(\pi\), rational signal probabilities \(p_\theta=\Pr[1\mid\theta]\), controlled mass \(\kappa\in[0,1]\), margin \(\Lambda>0\), and a rational breakpoint grid \(G\subseteq[0,1]\), choose nonnegative continuous piecewise-linear payment functions \(\tau_0,\tau_1\) with breakpoints \(G\) to minimize \(B=\sum_\theta\pi_\theta((1-p_\theta)\tau_0(p_\theta)+p_\theta\tau_1(p_\theta))\), subject to honest Bayesian incentive inequalities and \(W_\theta(\kappa p_\theta)\ge W_\theta(r)+\Lambda\) for every \(\theta\) and \(r\in[0,\kappa]\setminus\{\kappa p_\theta\}\), where \(W_\theta(r)=r\tau_1((1-\kappa)p_\theta+r)+(\kappa-r)\tau_0((1-\kappa)p_\theta+r)\); decide feasibility and compute the optimum.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Normalize total mass to \(1\).
Here \(\Theta\) indexes a common latent quality state with prior masses \(\pi_\theta>0\), \(\sum_\theta\pi_\theta=1\); conditional on state \(\theta\), the signal-\(1\) fraction is \(p_\theta\). Assume \(\tau=|\Theta|\ge2\) and pairwise distinct rational \(p_\theta\in(0,1)\).
Input also includes rational controlled mass \(0<\kappa<1\), rational margin \(\Lambda>0\), and a finite rational grid \(G\subseteq[0,1]\) containing \(0,1\); write \(m=|G|\) and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Choose finite-valued nonnegative continuous functions \(\tau_0,\tau_1:[0,1]\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), affine between consecutive grid points; \(\tau_i(q)\) pays report \(i\) when the aggregate positive-report fraction is \(q\).
For \(i\in\{0,1\}\), set \(a_{\theta,1}=p_\theta\), \(a_{\theta,0}=1-p_\theta\), and \(q_i=\sum_\theta\pi_\theta a_{\theta,i}\). Honest Bayesian incentives require \(\sum_\theta(\pi_\theta a_{\theta,i}/q_i)(\tau_i(p_\theta)-\tau_{1-i}(p_\theta))\ge\Lambda\) for both signals.
One controller pools the signals of mass \(\kappa\), can transfer utility, and chooses any positive-report mass \(r\in[0,\kappa]\); the remaining mass reports honestly. Define \(W_\theta(r)=r\tau_1((1-\kappa)p_\theta+r)+(\kappa-r)\tau_0((1-\kappa)p_\theta+r)\).
Require \(W_\theta(\kappa p_\theta)\ge W_\theta(r)+\Lambda\) for every \(\theta\in\Theta\) and every real \(r\in[0,\kappa]\setminus\{\kappa p_\theta\}\), and minimize \(B=\sum_\theta\pi_\theta((1-p_\theta)\tau_0(p_\theta)+p_\theta\tau_1(p_\theta))\).

Is feasibility and minimum-budget computation polynomial-time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is the decision problem asking whether a feasible mechanism has \(B\le b\), for an additional binary-rational threshold \(b\ge0\), NP-hard?

Answer for the stated problem

Every admissible instance is infeasible. Consequently, feasibility and budget decision are polynomial-time decidable, and minimum-budget computation always reports infeasibility.

The obstruction is the fixed positive margin against arbitrarily small deviations. To distinguish this from an impossibility of truthful reporting itself, a nontrivial example under a weaker controller condition is given below.

Proof of infeasibility

Fix any state \(\theta\), and write \(p=p_\theta\) and \(r^*=\kappa p\). Because \(0<p<1\) and \(\kappa>0\), we have \(0<r^*<\kappa\).

For every \(r\in[0,\kappa]\), the payment argument lies in its prescribed domain:
\[ 0\le (1-\kappa)p+r\le (1-\kappa)p+\kappa\le1. \]
Thus continuity of \(\tau_0,\tau_1\), together with continuity of products and sums, implies that \(W_\theta\) is continuous on \([0,\kappa]\).

For each positive integer \(n\), set \(r_n=r^*+(\kappa-r^*)/(n+1)\). Then \(r_n\in(r^*,\kappa)\) and \(r_n\to r^*\). The required controller inequality applies to every \(r_n\), giving
\[ W_\theta(r^*)-W_\theta(r_n)\ge\Lambda. \]
Continuity makes the left-hand side converge to \(0\). Taking limits therefore gives \(0\ge\Lambda\), contradicting \(\Lambda>0\).

This contradiction uses just one state. It applies regardless of the grid, prior, Bayesian incentive constraints, or budget.

Computational consequences

On inputs satisfying the stated promises, the algorithm simply returns infeasible, and the budget decision returns NO for every threshold \(b\). Checking the rational input promises, if required, also takes polynomial time.

There is no minimum over feasible mechanisms because the feasible set is empty. Under the extended-real convention, its budget infimum is \(\inf\varnothing=+\infty\).

The decision language has no yes-instances, so it is not NP-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions: a reduction from any language with a yes-instance would have to map that instance to a yes-instance of the empty language.

A nontrivial comparison: strict controller truthfulness

For this section only, replace the controller requirement by \(W_\theta(\kappa p_\theta)>W_\theta(r)\) for every \(r\ne\kappa p_\theta\), while retaining the honest Bayesian margin \(\Lambda=1\). This modified condition can be satisfied.

Take \(\pi_1=\pi_2=1/2\), \(p_1=1/4\), \(p_2=3/4\), \(\kappa=1/4\), and
\[ G=\{0,3/16,7/16,9/16,13/16,1\}. \]
The attainable aggregate intervals are \(I_1=[3/16,7/16]\) and \(I_2=[9/16,13/16]\). Define payments there by

| Interval | \(\tau_0(q)\) | \(\tau_1(q)\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(I_1\) | \(4+12(q-1/4)\) | \(2-4(q-1/4)\) |
| \(I_2\) | \(4+4(q-3/4)\) | \(6-12(q-3/4)\) |

Interpolate affinely across \([7/16,9/16]\), and extend constantly to \(0\) and \(1\). These functions are continuous and affine between consecutive grid points. Their values on the two specified intervals satisfy
\[ \tau_0(I_1)\subseteq[13/4,25/4],\quad \tau_1(I_1)\subseteq[5/4,9/4], \]
and
\[ \tau_0(I_2)\subseteq[13/4,17/4],\quad \tau_1(I_2)\subseteq[21/4,33/4]. \]
Interpolation between positive endpoint values preserves nonnegativity.

At the truthful aggregates, the payment differences are \(d_1=-2\) and \(d_2=2\), where \(d_j=\tau_1(p_j)-\tau_0(p_j)\). Since \(q_0=q_1=1/2\), the honest signal-\(1\) incentive equals
\[ \frac{\frac12(\frac14)(-2)+\frac12(\frac34)(2)}{1/2}=1, \]
and the honest signal-\(0\) incentive equals
\[ \frac{\frac12(\frac34)(2)+\frac12(\frac14)(-2)}{1/2}=1. \]

To verify controller incentives, put \(h=r-\kappa p_j\). The aggregate payment argument is \(p_j+h\), always within \(I_j\). If \(b_{0,j},b_{1,j}\) denote the slopes in the table, expansion gives
\[ W_j(r)-W_j(\kappa p_j) =h\bigl[d_j+\kappa((1-p_j)b_{0,j}+p_jb_{1,j})\bigr] +h^2(b_{1,j}-b_{0,j}). \]
For the first state, the linear coefficient is \(-2+\frac14(\frac34\cdot12+\frac14\cdot(-4))=0\). For the second, it is \(2+\frac14(\frac14\cdot4+\frac34\cdot(-12))=0\). Both quadratic coefficients equal \(-16\). Hence
\[ W_j(\kappa p_j)-W_j(r)=16(r-\kappa p_j)^2>0 \]
for every nontruthful report. The mechanism has budget \(B=9/2\).

Thus strict truthful optimality is possible, but its gap approaches zero near truth. The [discrete source, Section 4.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.3451), compares finitely many integer report counts; a finite set of strictly positive gaps can be scaled to a common positive margin. Here continuity prevents that step.
