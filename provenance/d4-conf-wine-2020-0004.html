The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) items, a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) positive-mass symmetric-threshold utility types with values \(u_t(j)\) and caps \(c_{tj}\), and the fixed constant \(\varepsilon\), find \(x\in[0,1]^m\) with \(\kappa(x)=|\{j:0<x_j<1\}|\leq\tau\) such that every type \(t\) satisfies \(\left|\sum_j f_{tj}(x_j)u_t(j)-\sum_j f_{tj}(1-x_j)u_t(j)\right|\leq\varepsilon\sum_j u_t(j)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) distinct utility types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) unordered, divisible items. Type \(t\) has binary-rational values \(u_t(j)\geq0\) and caps \(0\leq c_{tj}<1/2\). Define \(f_{tj}(z)=\max\{0,\min\{1,(z-c_{tj})/(1-2c_{tj})\}\}\) and \(U_t(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_t(j)f_{tj}(x_j)\).
Fix a sufficiently small positive rational constant \(\varepsilon\) in the finite-agent hardness regime. The search problem asks for a rational vector \(x\in[0,1]^m\) such that \(\kappa(x)=|\{j:0<x_j<1\}|\leq\tau\) and \(|U_t(x)-U_t(\mathbf{1}-x)|\leq\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_t(j)\) for every support type \(t\), independently of its mass. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is this search problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For a sufficiently small fixed positive error, the finite-agent version is PPAD-hard even with symmetric-threshold utilities, and belongs to PPA. [Verified source](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/full/10.1287/moor.2021.1249).
The support formulation is computationally equivalent to the version with one agent per distinct type, so these bounds apply here as well; they do not establish NP-hardness or a polynomial-time algorithm.

  • Normalized the positive rational masses to sum to one and retained the requirement for every support type without mass weighting.
  • Interpreted the unspecified fixed error as a sufficiently small positive rational constant in the anchor's hardness regime.
  • Specified rational output coordinates, positive item and support counts, and total binary input length L; zero-total-value types remain allowed.

2. Normalization and exact totality

Write
$$ W_t=\sum_{j=1}^m u_t(j). $$
If $W_t=0$, nonnegativity implies that every $u_t(j)$ is zero, so this type imposes no restriction. Define
$$ w_{tj}= \begin{cases} u_t(j)/W_t,&W_t>0,\\ 0,&W_t=0. \end{cases} $$
Thus each row of $w$ sums to either $1$ or $0$.

Let
$$ d_{tj}(z)=2f_{tj}(z)-1. $$
The two unclipped affine expressions defining $f_{tj}(z)$ and $f_{tj}(1-z)$ sum to $1$. Clipping to $[0,1]$ preserves this complementary identity, so
$$ f_{tj}(1-z)=1-f_{tj}(z). $$
Consequently, with
$$ A_t(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m w_{tj}d_{tj}(x_j), $$
the required utility condition for a positive-value type is exactly
$$ |A_t(x)|\leq\varepsilon. $$
Moreover,
$$ d_{tj}(0)=-1,\qquad d_{tj}(1)=1,\qquad d_{tj}(1-z)=-d_{tj}(z). $$

If $\tau\geq m$, setting every coordinate to $1/2$ is an exact solution. The algorithm below therefore only needs to address $\tau<m$.

Lemma 1: a sparse exact solution exists

Every instance has a real vector $x$ satisfying
$$ A_t(x)=0\quad\text{for all }t, \qquad \kappa(x)\leq\tau. $$

Proof. Use the following form of the Borsuk–Ulam theorem: every continuous odd map from $S^\tau$ to $\mathbb R^\tau$ has a zero.

For $a=(a_0,\ldots,a_\tau)\in S^\tau$, define
$$ p_a(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{\tau}a_i s^i, \qquad x_j(a)=\lambda\{s\in[j-1,j]:p_a(s)\geq0\}, $$
where $\lambda$ denotes length. Since $a$ is a unit vector, $p_a$ is a nonzero polynomial.

Each coordinate $x_j(a)$ depends continuously on $a$. Indeed, if $a^{(n)}\to a$, then the sign indicators converge pointwise wherever $p_a(s)\neq0$. The exceptional set is finite, and the indicators are bounded by $1$. Dominated convergence on the item interval proves continuity.

The zero set of $p_a$ has length zero. Since $p_{-a}=-p_a$,
$$ x(-a)=\mathbf1-x(a). $$
Therefore
$$ F(a)=\bigl(A_1(x(a)),\ldots,A_\tau(x(a))\bigr) $$
is continuous and odd. Borsuk–Ulam supplies $a^*$ with $F(a^*)=0$.

If $0<x_j(a^*)<1$, the polynomial takes both positive and negative values in the interior of that item interval. The intermediate value theorem gives a root in its interior. Distinct split items give distinct roots, while a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $\tau$ has at most $\tau$ roots. Hence $\kappa(x(a^*))\leq\tau$. $\square$

Lemma 2: polynomial-length rational exact solutions exist

An exact solution from Lemma 1 can be replaced by a rational exact solution of polynomial encoding length without increasing its number of split items.

Proof. Let $S$ be the split coordinates of a real exact solution, with $r=|S|\leq\tau$. Freeze all coordinates outside $S$ at their endpoint values.

For each $j\in S$, sort the distinct breakpoints
$$ \{0,1\}\cup\{c_{tj},1-c_{tj}:t\in[\tau]\}. $$
Choose a closed interval between consecutive breakpoints containing the real coordinate. Every $d_{tj}$ is affine on that interval; adjacent formulas agree at the breakpoints.

The exact balance equations and these interval bounds define a nonempty bounded rational polytope in $r$ variables. It has a vertex. At a vertex, the active constraints have rank $r$: otherwise a nonzero direction preserving every active constraint would permit sufficiently small feasible perturbations in both directions, contradicting extremality.

Choose $r$ independent active constraints. Their rational coefficients have polynomial bit length. This follows because they are formed from the input rationals by sums, products, and division by the nonzero rationals $1-2c_{tj}$. $$ |\det B|\leq r!M^r $$
for an $r$ by $r$ matrix with entries of absolute value at most $M$ show that its solution has polynomial bit length.

3. Improved approximation algorithm

Theorem

For fixed rational $0<\varepsilon<1$, the required allocation can be found deterministically in time
$$ 2^{O(\tau\log(2+\tau/\varepsilon))} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L). $$

3.1. Separate heavy and light items

Set
$$ \delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\tau}. $$
Call an item heavy if $w_{tj}>\delta$ for at least one type. Let $H$ be the heavy-item set, let $K=[m]\setminus H$, and write $h=|H|$.

For a fixed positive-value type, fewer than $1/\delta$ items can have weight greater than $\delta$, because its weights sum to $1$. Taking a union over types gives
$$ h<\frac{\tau}{\delta} =\frac{2\tau^2}{\varepsilon}. $$
Every light item satisfies
$$ w_{tj}\leq\delta \quad\text{for every }t. $$

For the existence argument, replace all light-item caps by zero. The resulting normalized balance functions are
$$ A_t^H(x)= \sum_{j\in H}w_{tj}d_{tj}(x_j) +\sum_{j\in K}w_{tj}(2x_j-1). $$
This modified system is still a system of symmetric-threshold utilities. Lemma 1 therefore gives an exact allocation $x^*$ with
$$ A_t^H(x^*)=0,\qquad \kappa(x^*)\leq\tau. $$
The algorithm does not need to compute $x^*$.

3.2. Enumerate fractional heavy items

Enumerate every set $S\subseteq H$ with $|S|\leq\tau$. These are the heavy items allowed to remain fractional.

For each $j\in S$, enumerate one interval between consecutive breakpoints from
$$ \{0,1\}\cup\{c_{tj},1-c_{tj}:t\in[\tau]\}. $$
There are at most $2\tau+1$ choices per coordinate. On a chosen interval, write the rational affine formula for $d_{tj}$ as
$$ d_{tj}(z_j)=a_{tj}z_j+b_{tj}. $$

It remains to handle endpoint assignments on $T=H\setminus S$ without enumerating all of them.

3.3. Compress endpoint assignments by dynamic programming

Define
$$ \eta=\frac{\varepsilon}{4\max\{1,h\}}, \qquad q_{tj}=\left\lfloor\frac{w_{tj}}{\eta}\right\rfloor. $$
An endpoint assignment on $T$ is specified by the subset $B\subseteq T$ assigned endpoint $1$; all other items in $T$ receive endpoint $0$.

Associate with $B$ the integer vector
$$ v(B)=\left(\sum_{j\in B}q_{tj}\right)_{t=1}^{\tau}. $$
A standard subset-sum dynamic program retains one representative subset for each reachable vector. Initially, the zero vector has representative $\varnothing$. When processing item $j$, each existing state has an exclusion transition and an inclusion transition adding $(q_{tj})_t$. If transitions collide, retain either representative.

Induction over processed items proves that every reachable vector remains represented.

For every coordinate,
$$ \sum_{j\in B}q_{tj} \leq \frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{j\in B}w_{tj} \leq\frac1\eta. $$
Thus the number of states is at most
$$ N=\left(1+\left\lfloor\frac1\eta\right\rfloor\right)^\tau. $$

The compression has a controlled error. Write
$$ w_{tj}=\eta q_{tj}+r_{tj}, \qquad 0\leq r_{tj}<\eta. $$
If $v(B)=v(B')$, their integer contributions cancel, and each remainder sum lies between $0$ and $h\eta$. Hence
$$ \left| \sum_{j\in B}w_{tj}-\sum_{j\in B'}w_{tj} \right| \leq h\eta. $$
Their signed endpoint contributions therefore differ by at most
$$ \left| \sum_{j\in T}w_{tj}(2\mathbf1_B(j)-1) - \sum_{j\in T}w_{tj}(2\mathbf1_{B'}(j)-1) \right| \leq2h\eta \leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. $$

3.4. Solve a linear program for each retained assignment

For every enumerated $S$, interval choice, and retained representative $B$, introduce variables $z_j$ for $j\in S$ and $y_j$ for $j\in K$. Define
$$ E_t(z,y)= \sum_{j\in T}w_{tj}(2\mathbf1_B(j)-1) +\sum_{j\in S}w_{tj}(a_{tj}z_j+b_{tj}) +\sum_{j\in K}w_{tj}(2y_j-1). $$
Solve the rational linear feasibility problem consisting of the chosen interval bounds,
$$ 0\leq y_j\leq1\quad(j\in K), $$
and
$$ |E_t(z,y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2} \quad(t\in[\tau]). $$

At least one branch is feasible. Choose $S$ to be the fractional heavy coordinates of the sparse exact allocation $x^*$ for the modified system. Its size is at most $\tau$. Choose intervals containing its coordinates, and let $B^*\subseteq T$ describe its heavy endpoint assignments.

The dynamic program retains a representative $B$ with $v(B)=v(B^*)$. At $z_j=x_j^*$ and $y_j=x_j^*$, the expression using $B^*$ would equal $A_t^H(x^*)=0$. Replacing $B^*$ by $B$ changes it by at most $\varepsilon/2$, by the preceding calculation. Thus this branch is feasible.

3.5. Obtain a vertex and round the light items

Obtain a vertex $(z,y)$ of any feasible branch.

At most $\tau$ light coordinates can be fractional. To prove this, suppose more than $\tau$ coordinates $y_j$ lie strictly between $0$ and $1$. Their columns $(w_{tj})_{t=1}^{\tau}$ are linearly dependent. Therefore there is a nonzero vector $v$, supported on those coordinates, such that
$$ \sum_{j\in K}w_{tj}v_j=0 \quad\text{for every }t. $$
Holding $z$ fixed, both $y+\alpha v$ and $y-\alpha v$ satisfy the coordinate bounds for sufficiently small $\alpha>0$. Every $E_t$ remains unchanged. Both points are feasible, contradicting extremality.

Round each light coordinate to a nearest endpoint $\widehat y_j\in\{0,1\}$. Each changed coordinate contributes an error bounded by
$$ |2w_{tj}(\widehat y_j-y_j)| \leq w_{tj}\leq\delta. $$
At most $\tau$ coordinates change, so
$$ |E_t(z,\widehat y)-E_t(z,y)| \leq\tau\delta =\frac{\varepsilon}{2}. $$

Define the final allocation by keeping the heavy coordinates from this branch and using $\widehat y$ on the light items. At endpoints, original threshold utilities and linear utilities agree. Consequently its original normalized imbalance is exactly $E_t(z,\widehat y)$, and
$$ |A_t(\widehat x)| \leq |E_t(z,y)| +|E_t(z,\widehat y)-E_t(z,y)| \leq\varepsilon. $$
All light items and all heavy items outside $S$ are endpoints. Thus
$$ \kappa(\widehat x)\leq|S|\leq\tau. $$

3.6. Running time and bit complexity

The number of sets $S$ is at most $(h+1)^\tau$. This bound follows by encoding each subset of size at most $\tau$ as a sorted list padded to length $\tau$ with a sentinel.

There are at most $(2\tau+1)^\tau$ interval choices per set, and at most $N$ retained endpoint assignments. Since
$$ h=O(\tau^2/\varepsilon), \qquad \frac1\eta =O(\tau^2/\varepsilon^2), $$
the total number of linear programs is bounded by
$$ (h+1)^\tau(2\tau+1)^\tau \left(1+\left\lfloor\frac1\eta\right\rfloor\right)^\tau = 2^{O(\tau\log(2+\tau/\varepsilon))}. $$
The dynamic programs have the same bound up to polynomial factors.

All linear programs have polynomially many variables and constraints and polynomial-bit rational coefficients. Exact rational linear programming decides feasibility and optimizes rational linear objectives in polynomial time.

A vertex can be obtained by minimizing coordinates successively, fixing each optimum before minimizing the next coordinate. Each optimization selects a nonempty face of the preceding polytope. After all coordinates are fixed, the remaining face is a singleton, hence a vertex of the original polytope. Its encoding length is polynomial by the determinant argument in Lemma 2.

All rounding and reconstruction operations use exact rational arithmetic. This completes the proof of the theorem.

4. A polynomial-time case with arbitrary cap sizes

$$ U_t(x)-U_t(\mathbf1-x) = \sum_j u_t(j)(2y_j-1)=0. $$
Furthermore, $x_j$ is fractional exactly when $y_j$ is fractional. Hence $\kappa(x)\leq\tau$. Rational linear programming and the displayed reconstruction both take polynomial time.

This settles a substantial restricted case, including caps arbitrarily close to $1/2$.

There is also a total-search obstruction to conventional $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness, which can be proved directly here.

By Lemma 2, every instance has a valid allocation of polynomial encoding length. Such an allocation is verifiable in polynomial time: check the coordinate bounds, count split items, and evaluate the rational utility differences. Restricting outputs to a sufficiently large polynomial length bound therefore gives a total, polynomially verifiable search relation.

Now suppose a deterministic polynomial-time oracle reduction decides satisfiability using this search relation, correctly for every valid oracle. An unsatisfiability certificate can consist of a complete rejecting oracle transcript:

  • Recompute every query from the preceding transcript.
  • Verify every returned allocation.
  • Require repeated identical queries to receive identical replies.
  • Check that the simulated reduction rejects.

The transcript has polynomial length, and all checks take polynomial time. For an unsatisfiable formula, totality supplies a valid oracle and hence such a rejecting transcript. For a satisfiable formula, any verified consistent transcript extends to a valid oracle: assign its recorded replies on queried inputs and choose valid replies elsewhere. Correctness excludes rejection for that oracle.

Thus unsatisfiability belongs to $\mathrm{NP}$, implying
$$ \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$
This is a conditional consequence of the specified reduction notion, not an unconditional exclusion of $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.
