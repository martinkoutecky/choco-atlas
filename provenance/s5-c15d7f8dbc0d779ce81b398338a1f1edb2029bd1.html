Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. In fact, one can compute rational prices and bundles satisfying exact capacity feasibility and attaining \(U_\mu^\star\), so the algorithm needs no approximation parameter.

The key is to represent each supported type by one Fisher buyer with budget \(\mu_k w_k\), then rescale its allocation to obtain the per-capita bundle.

1. Exact Fisher equilibrium theorem

We use the following known result: a linear Fisher market with rational positive budgets, unit supplies, and rational nonnegative utility coefficients admits an exact equilibrium computable in polynomial time, provided every buyer values some good and every good is valued by some buyer. Equilibrium prices and a suitable equilibrium allocation are rational with polynomial encoding length. This follows from [Devanur et al., Sections 2 and 8, especially Theorem 8.12](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/market.pdf).

Here an equilibrium consists of nonnegative prices \(q\) and allocations \(t_k\) such that each \(t_k\) maximizes its buyer’s utility subject to its budget, and every positively priced good clears.

All prices are strictly positive under the stated hypotheses. Indeed, if \(q_j=0\) and buyer \(k\) values good \(j\), its budget-feasible utility is unbounded: arbitrarily many units of that good cost nothing. Thus that buyer could have no utility-maximizing bundle, contradicting equilibrium.

2. Constructing the market and recovering the output

Construct a market with \(\tau\) buyers and \(m\) goods, each good having supply one. Set

\(b_k=\mu_k w_k,\qquad a_{kj}=d_j u_{kj}.\)

Buyer \(k\) has budget \(b_k\) and linear utility \(a_k^\top t_k\).

Every budget is positive. Since every \(d_j\) is positive, the positivity pattern of \(a\) is exactly that of \(u\). Consequently, every buyer values some good and every good has a potential buyer. The theorem applies.

Compute an exact equilibrium \((q,t)\), and output

\(p_j=\dfrac{q_j}{d_j},\qquad x_{kj}=\dfrac{d_jt_{kj}}{\mu_k}.\)

These numbers are rational, and \(p_j>0\).

Demand optimality. For each fixed type \(k\), the correspondence

\(s_j=\dfrac{\mu_k x_j}{d_j}\)

is a bijection between nonnegative bundles. It satisfies

\(q^\top s=\mu_k p^\top x,\qquad a_k^\top s=\mu_k u_k^\top x.\)

Thus \(p^\top x\le w_k\) holds exactly when \(q^\top s\le b_k\). Since multiplication by \(\mu_k>0\) preserves utility comparisons, the equilibrium optimality of \(t_k\) implies

\(x_k\in\arg\max\{u_k^\top x:p^\top x\le w_k,\ x\ge0\}.\)

Membership in the required convex hull follows immediately.

Capacity feasibility. Strict positivity of \(q\) implies that all unit-supply goods clear. Therefore, for every good \(j\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{\tau}\mu_kx_{kj}=d_j\sum_{k=1}^{\tau}t_{kj}=d_j.\)

The output satisfies the capacity constraints exactly.

3. Direct proof of welfare optimality

We prove that the recovered allocation attains the specified mass-weighted objective.

Define the maximum utility per unit of expenditure for type \(k\) by

\(\displaystyle \alpha_k=\max_{1\le j\le m}\frac{u_{kj}}{p_j}>0.\)

For every nonnegative bundle \(z_k\),

\(u_k^\top z_k\le\alpha_k p^\top z_k.\)

A bundle spending all of \(w_k\) on a good attaining this maximum has utility \(\alpha_k w_k\). Hence the demand optimality already proved gives

\(v_k:=u_k^\top x_k=\alpha_k w_k>0.\)

Moreover, \(v_k\le\alpha_k p^\top x_k\le\alpha_k w_k=v_k\), so

\(p^\top x_k=w_k.\)

Combining full expenditure with exact capacity clearing yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_k\mu_kw_k=\sum_k\mu_kp^\top x_k=p^\top d.\)

Now take any capacity-feasible allocation \(z\) with positive utility for every type. The elementary inequality \(\log r\le r-1\), valid for \(r>0\), gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_k\mu_kw_k\log\frac{u_k^\top z_k}{v_k}\le\sum_k\mu_kw_k\left(\frac{u_k^\top z_k}{v_k}-1\right).\)

Using \(v_k=\alpha_kw_k\), the right-hand side equals

\(\displaystyle \sum_k\mu_k\frac{u_k^\top z_k}{\alpha_k}-\sum_k\mu_kw_k.\)

The utility-per-expenditure inequality bounds this by

\(\displaystyle p^\top\left(\sum_k\mu_kz_k\right)-p^\top d\le0.\)

The last inequality follows from capacity feasibility and \(p>0\).

Thus every feasible allocation with positive utilities has objective at most that of \(x\). An allocation giving some type zero utility cannot improve the objective, since that type has positive weight and its logarithmic term is \(-\infty\). Since \(x\) itself is feasible and has positive utilities, it attains the maximum:

\(\displaystyle U_\mu^\star=\sum_k\mu_kw_k\log(u_k^\top x_k).\)

Consequently, the welfare gap is exactly zero.

4. Encoding length, runtime, and ties

The transformed market contains exactly \(\tau\) buyers. There is no expansion into a population whose size depends on the denominators of the masses.

Each transformed input is a product of two input rationals. Numerator and denominator bit lengths therefore add, so the transformed market has encoding length polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The exact Fisher algorithm runs in polynomial time on this encoding and produces prices and allocations of polynomial encoding length. Recovering \(p\) and \(x\) requires only rational multiplication and division, preserving polynomial encoding length and runtime.

Thus the computation is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\); apart from reading the input, its work is independent of \(\varepsilon\). No logarithm needs to be numerically evaluated.

Ties create no additional obstruction. The exact equilibrium algorithm chooses a market-clearing allocation among utility-maximizing bundles, and the recovery map preserves optimality. This uses precisely the algorithm’s permitted freedom to split ties.

5. Why the stochastic lower bound does not contradict this

The stochastic lower bound concerns realized arrivals, whose empirical type frequencies fluctuate around their probabilities. Jalota–Ye explicitly distinguish this issue from their finite-support certainty-equivalent program, whose dual variables give static expected equilibrium prices. See [Jalota–Ye, Corollary 1 and Section 4.2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.00825v5).

Here the capacity constraints and welfare objective both use the given masses \(\mu_k\) exactly. There are no realized arrival frequencies to accommodate. The exact deterministic equilibrium constructed above therefore settles the stated task.
