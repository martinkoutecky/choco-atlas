The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 3.12 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given ordered types \(t=1,\ldots,\tau\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), \(m\) identical resources, a common strictly increasing continuous latency \(f\), and a specified family \(C\) of intervals of types, find an allocation \(x_{t,i}\geq 0\) with \(\sum_i x_{t,i}=\mu_t\) such that no \(I\in C\) can reassign its mass among the resources, with outsiders fixed, so that almost every coalition member weakly lowers their latency and positive mass strictly lowers it.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: ordered types \(t=1,\ldots,\tau\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Each type consists of atomless mass; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) identical resources, available to everyone, and an explicitly listed family \(C\) of nonempty intervals \(\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\}\) of types. An interval coalition contains all mass of its types.
The common latency \(f:[0,1]\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}\) is continuous and strictly increasing, with \(f(0)=0\). It is an arbitrary fixed function, not encoded input; the problem must be handled uniformly for every such \(f\).
An allocation has \(x_{t,i}\geq0\), \(\sum_i x_{t,i}=\mu_t\), and resource loads \(\ell_i=\sum_t x_{t,i}\); members on resource \(i\) incur latency \(f(\ell_i)\).
A deviation by \(I\in C\) consists of real masses \(d_{t,i,j}\geq0\) for \(t\in I\), with \(\sum_j d_{t,i,j}=x_{t,i}\); this moves mass from resource \(i\) to resource \(j\), allowing \(i=j\). Outsiders stay fixed, giving \(\ell'_j=\sum_{t\notin I}x_{t,j}+\sum_{t\in I,i}d_{t,i,j}\).
The deviation is profitable if \(f(\ell'_j)\leq f(\ell_i)\) whenever \(d_{t,i,j}>0\), and \(\sum_{t,i,j:\,f(\ell'_j)<f(\ell_i)}d_{t,i,j}>0\). An allocation is \(C\)-stable if no listed coalition has a profitable deviation.

Is finding a rational \(C\)-stable allocation, or determining that none exists, polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,|C|,L\), or is it NP-hard, where \(L\) is the total binary input length?

Discrete: with identical resources and a supplied ordering making every viable coalition contiguous, a stable allocation always exists and can be constructed in linear time in the number of agents. [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.04648).
Continuous: no computational result for this exact support-encoded problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted each type as atomless mass and each listed interval as containing all mass of its types.
  • Specified the interval family by an explicit list of endpoint pairs; an empty family is allowed.
  • Restored the latency normalization f(0)=0 and specified its nonnegative range and load domain [0,1].
  • Resolved the unspecified encoding of an arbitrary continuous latency by treating it as a fixed common function and requiring uniformity over all functions satisfying the promise.
  • Defined deviations through mass transfers indexed by original and destination resources, including members who stay, so improvements are compared individually rather than through average type latency.
  • Specified rational allocation output, allowed real-valued deviations, and included reporting nonexistence in the computational problem.
  • Defined L as total binary input length and included the explicitly listed coalition family in the running-time parameters.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, uniformly for every latency function satisfying the stated hypotheses. A rational \(C\)-stable allocation always exists.

The key is that the model permits each type’s mass to be split among resources. Consequently, exact equal loading is always feasible.

Equal loading implies stability

Lemma. Any allocation with \(\ell_i=1/m\) for every resource \(i\) is stable against every coalition of types.

Proof. Fix such an allocation and any coalition \(I\). Consider a deviation satisfying the no-worsening condition. Write \(q=1/m\), and define the outsider mass and incoming coalition mass on resource \(j\) by

\(o_j=\sum_{t\notin I}x_{t,j}\), and \(y_j=\sum_{t\in I}\sum_i d_{t,i,j}\).

Thus \(\ell'_j=o_j+y_j\). Since outsiders are a subset of the original occupants, \(o_j\leq\ell_j=q\).

For each resource \(j\), there are two cases:

  • If \(y_j=0\), then \(\ell'_j=o_j\leq q\).
  • If \(y_j>0\), at least one summand \(d_{t,i,j}\) is positive. The no-worsening condition gives \(f(\ell'_j)\leq f(\ell_i)=f(q)\). Strict monotonicity of \(f\) therefore gives \(\ell'_j\leq q\).

Hence every final resource load is at most \(q\).

The deviation preserves total mass: summing \(\sum_j d_{t,i,j}=x_{t,i}\) over coalition types and original resources, and adding the unchanged outsider mass, gives \(\sum_j\ell'_j=1=mq\). Since all \(m\) final loads are at most \(q\), every final load must equal \(q\). Indeed, a single load strictly below \(q\) would make their sum strictly below \(mq\).

Every positive transfer therefore has the same original and final latency, namely \(f(q)\). The mass experiencing a strict improvement is zero, so the deviation is not profitable. This proves the lemma.

Construction and complexity

For every type \(t\) and resource \(i\), output

\(x_{t,i}=\mu_t/m\).

These entries are nonnegative and satisfy \(\sum_i x_{t,i}=\mu_t\). Moreover,

\(\ell_i=\sum_t x_{t,i}=(1/m)\sum_t\mu_t=1/m\).

The lemma proves that this allocation is \(C\)-stable. In fact, it is stable against every coalition of types, so the interval restriction imposes no additional difficulty.

To check the encoding bound, write each input mass as \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), with positive integers \(a_t,b_t\) represented in binary. Each output entry can be represented as \(a_t/(mb_t)\). Its numerator has the original bit length, and its denominator has at most the original denominator bit length plus \(O(\log(m+1))\) bits.

There are \(m\tau\) entries, with total representation length \(O(mL+m\tau\log(m+1))\). Computing the denominators and writing the entries therefore takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|C|,L\).

The algorithm does not evaluate \(f\); strict monotonicity alone justifies the proof. Thus it handles every admissible fixed \(f\) uniformly.

Step-by-step verification

  • Equal loading is feasible.

The construction \(x_{t,i}=\mu_t/m\) is nonnegative and satisfies
\[ \sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{t,i}=\mu_t, \qquad \ell_i=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}x_{t,i}=\frac1m. \]
Splitting each type’s atomless mass this way is permitted by the task. All output entries are rational.

  • The lemma covers every permitted coalition.

The proof fixes an arbitrary coalition of types and an arbitrary deviation satisfying the no-worsening condition. This includes every listed interval coalition. Proving that such a deviation cannot strictly improve positive mass suffices to establish stability.

  • The outsider-load bound is valid.

With \(q=1/m\), the outsider mass \(o_j\) is a sum of a subset of the nonnegative original allocations on resource \(j\). Therefore \(0\leq o_j\leq\ell_j=q\).

  • Both cases for incoming coalition mass are justified.

If \(y_j=0\), then \(\ell'_j=o_j\leq q\). If \(y_j>0\), the defining sum is finite and has nonnegative summands, so some \(d_{t,i,j}>0\). The deviation condition then gives
\[ f(\ell'_j)\leq f(\ell_i)=f(q). \]
Strict increase implies \(\ell'_j\leq q\). The arguments lie in the domain of \(f\): final loads are nonnegative and, by mass conservation, sum to \(1\), so each belongs to \([0,1]\).

  • Mass conservation forces exact final equality.

The constraints \(\sum_jd_{t,i,j}=x_{t,i}\), together with unchanged outsiders, give \(\sum_j\ell'_j=1=mq\). Since every final load is at most \(q\), a single strict inequality would force the total below \(mq\). Thus every final load equals \(q\).

  • Profitability is excluded under the exact definition.

Every positive transfer starts on a resource with load \(q\) and ends on a resource with load \(q\). Consequently, no transferred mass experiences strict latency improvement. The strict-improvement sum is zero, so the deviation is not profitable.

  • The encoding and time bounds are sufficient.

For binary-encoded \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), the representation \(a_t/(mb_t)\) is exact. Its denominator requires at most the original denominator length plus \(O(\log(m+1))\) bits. Summing over the \(m\tau\) entries gives the claimed bound
\[ O\!\left(mL+m\tau\log(m+1)\right). \]
Integer multiplication and output writing take polynomial time in the requested parameters. The task permits rational output; it does not require the output denominators to be powers of two.

  • Uniformity and edge cases are covered.

The algorithm never evaluates \(f\). Its correctness uses only strict increase, which holds for every admissible function. The reasoning also applies when \(m=1\), when \(\tau=1\), and when \(C\) is empty. No cited theorem or external computational claim is needed.
