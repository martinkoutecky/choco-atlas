The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) with \(|T|=\tau\), a symmetric binary affinity matrix \(A\in\{0,1\}^{\tau\times\tau}\), and rational masses \(\mu\in\Delta_\tau\), choose coalition compositions \(p^j\in\Delta_\tau\) and masses \(s_j\ge0\) with \(\sum_j s_j=1\) and \(\sum_j s_jp^j=\mu\), maximizing \(\sum_j s_j(p^j)^\mathsf{T}Ap^j\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\), where \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types.
The input also contains a symmetric matrix \(A\in\{0,1\}^{\tau\times\tau}\) and a binary-rational threshold \(b\in[0,1]\). Entry \(A_{ik}\) is the affinity between distinct individuals of types \(i,k\), including when \(i=k\). Individual self-valuations are zero; individuals have zero mass.
Mass is divisible. An outcome consists of any finite number \(r\ge1\) of coalitions, with real masses \(s_j>0\) and compositions \(p^j\in\Delta_\tau\), satisfying \(\sum_{j=1}^r s_j=1\) and \(\sum_{j=1}^r s_jp^j=\mu\). A type may occur in several coalitions; there are no stability constraints.
A type-\(i\) individual in composition \(p\) has utility \((Ap)_i\); total utilitarian welfare is \(W=\sum_{j=1}^r s_j(p^j)^\mathsf{T}Ap^j\). The decision problem asks whether an outcome with \(W\ge b\) exists. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; there is no separate candidate parameter \(m\).

Is this continuous utilitarian-welfare decision problem solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite, indivisible agents, maximizing utilitarian welfare is NP-hard even with symmetric binary affinities and zero self-valuations ([Aziz et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/071.pdf)). No result settling the exact continuous problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Converted exact welfare maximization into its threshold decision problem, with a binary-rational threshold included in the input length.
  • Required every listed type to have strictly positive mass, while retaining the stated total-mass normalization of one.
  • Allowed any finite number of positive-mass coalitions, real coalition masses and compositions, and splitting each type across coalitions; omitted zero-mass coalitions.
  • Retained the graded question's unrestricted binary diagonal: diagonal entries describe affinities between distinct individuals of the same type. Zero individual self-valuation does not restrict these entries in an atomless society.
  • Specified that welfare maximization imposes no stability constraint.
  • Used the affinity dimension and support size as the size parameter; this problem has no separate candidate parameter m.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even when \(A_{ii}=0\) for every type and all input masses and thresholds are dyadic rationals.

We prove this by a direct reduction from \(\mathrm{CLIQUE}\). The key construction allows us to insert a small coalition supported on a large clique and compensate for its population consumption exactly.

1. A quadratic bound for graph affinities

For a nonempty simple graph \(H\), let \(A_H\) be its adjacency matrix and let \(\omega(H)\) denote its maximum clique size. Then every composition \(p\) satisfies

\( p^{\mathsf T}A_Hp\le 1-\frac{1}{\omega(H)}. \)

Here is a self-contained proof of this standard Motzkin–Straus bound.

Suppose two nonadjacent vertices \(u,v\) have positive coordinates in \(p\). Hold all other coordinates fixed and write \(p_u=x\), \(p_v=t-x\), where \(t=p_u+p_v\). Because the diagonal entries and \(A_{uv}\) are zero, the quadratic objective is affine in \(x\):

\( p^{\mathsf T}A_Hp =C+2x\alpha_u+2(t-x)\alpha_v, \)

where \(\alpha_u=\sum_{w\notin\{u,v\}}A_{uw}p_w\), and similarly for \(\alpha_v\).

Moving all mass \(t\) to whichever endpoint has the larger coefficient does not decrease the objective and reduces the support size. Repeating this operation produces a composition supported on a clique, with objective at least the original value.

For a composition supported on a clique of size \(d\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\( p^{\mathsf T}A_Hp =1-\sum_i p_i^2 \le 1-\frac1d \le 1-\frac1{\omega(H)}. \)

This proves the bound. A uniform composition on a clique of size \(d\) has welfare exactly \(1-1/d\).

2. A gadget with an explicit population-repair identity

Fix an integer \(k\ge2\), and put \(m=2k+1\). Let \(F_k\) be the complement of the cycle on vertices indexed modulo \(m\).

An independent set in that cycle is disjoint from its set of successors. Thus its size is at most \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor=k\), so \(\omega(F_k)\le k\).

For each index \(j\), define

\( S_j=\{j,j+2,\ldots,j+2(k-1)\}\pmod m. \)

Each \(S_j\) is an independent set of size \(k\) in the cycle, hence a clique in \(F_k\). Consequently, \(\omega(F_k)=k\).

Let \(p^j\) be the uniform composition on \(S_j\), and let \(B\) be the matrix with columns \(p^j\). Every vertex belongs to exactly \(k\) of these sets, so

\( B\mathbf1=\mathbf1. \)

Moreover, \(S_{a+1}\) and \(S_{a+2}\) partition all gadget vertices except \(a\). Therefore

\( k(p^{a+1}+p^{a+2})=\mathbf1-e_a. \)

These identities give an explicit way to express any vector through the gadget compositions. For a vector \(z\in\mathbb R^m\), set \(t=\sum_a z_a\) and define

\( x_j=t-k(z_{j-1}+z_{j-2}), \)

with indices interpreted modulo \(m\). Then

\( Bx =t\mathbf1-k\sum_a z_a(p^{a+1}+p^{a+2}) =t\mathbf1-\sum_a z_a(\mathbf1-e_a) =z. \)

Also,

\( \sum_jx_j=(m-2k)t=t. \)

If \(z\) is coordinatewise nonnegative, then \(x_j\le t\). The coefficients \(x_j\) may be negative; this is harmless because we will subtract a small multiple of them from positive baseline coalition masses.

3. Constructing the reduction

Take an instance \((G,t)\) of \(\mathrm{CLIQUE}\), where \(G\) has \(n\) vertices and \(3\le t\le n\). This restricted problem remains \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard: excluded cases are directly decidable and can be mapped to fixed yes- or no-instances. We use the standard hardness of \(\mathrm{CLIQUE}\) established in [Karp’s *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

Padding the clique threshold

Let \(k\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(k\ge t-1\). Add

\( h=k+1-t \)

new vertices that form a clique and are adjacent to every original vertex. Call the resulting graph \(G'\), and write \(n'=n+h\).

Every maximum clique of \(G'\) contains all the added vertices, so

\( \omega(G')=\omega(G)+h. \)

In particular,

\( \omega(G)\ge t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \omega(G')\ge k+1. \)

Using a power of two for \(k\) will ensure dyadic input values.

Attaching the gadgets

For each vertex \(v\) of \(G'\), take a separate copy of \(F_k\), identifying its vertex indexed \(0\) with \(v\). Its other \(2k\) vertices are private to that gadget. Retain the edges of \(G'\), and add no other edges between gadgets. Denote the resulting graph by \(H\).

A clique containing a private vertex must lie entirely within its gadget, because that private vertex has no neighbors outside the gadget. A clique containing only distinguished vertices is a clique of \(G'\). Hence

\( \omega(H)=\max\{k,\omega(G')\}. \)

The society has one type per vertex of \(H\), and \(A=A_H\). Its number of types is

\( \tau=N=n'(2k+1). \)

Let \(p^{v,j}\) denote the uniform composition on the clique \(S_j\) in gadget \(v\). There are exactly \(N\) such compositions. Each has welfare

\( c=1-\frac1k. \)

Masses and threshold

Let \(D\) be the smallest power of two with \(D\ge N\). Assign baseline coalition masses \(\lambda_{v,j}\) as follows: initially assign \(1/D\) to every composition, then replace one chosen mass with \(1-(N-1)/D\).

Thus

\( \sum_{v,j}\lambda_{v,j}=1, \qquad \lambda_{v,j}\ge\frac1D. \)

Define the society’s prescribed masses by

\( \mu=\sum_{v,j}\lambda_{v,j}p^{v,j}. \)

Every type belongs to exactly \(k\) gadget compositions. Therefore

\( \mu_i\ge k\cdot\frac1D\cdot\frac1k=\frac1D>0. \)

Also, \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). Since \(k\) and \(D\) are powers of two, every \(\mu_i\) is dyadic.

Finally, set

\( \varepsilon=\frac1{2D}, \qquad b=c+\frac{\varepsilon}{2k^2} =1-\frac1k+\frac1{4Dk^2}. \)

This threshold is dyadic and satisfies \(c<b<1\).

4. Correctness: no-instances

Suppose \(\omega(G)<t\). Then \(\omega(G')\le k\), and consequently \(\omega(H)=k\).

By the quadratic bound, every composition \(p\) satisfies

\( p^{\mathsf T}Ap\le c. \)

For any admissible finite outcome, its coalition masses sum to one. Therefore

\( W=\sum_j s_j(p^j)^{\mathsf T}Ap^j \le\sum_j s_jc =c<b. \)

Thus the constructed welfare instance is a no-instance, regardless of how many coalitions are used or how types are divided among them.

5. Correctness: yes-instances

Suppose \(\omega(G)\ge t\). Then \(G'\) contains a clique of size \(k+1\). Let \(q\) be the uniform composition on such a clique, viewed as a composition on \(H\). Its welfare is

\( q^{\mathsf T}Aq=1-\frac1{k+1}. \)

We will insert a coalition of composition \(q\) and mass \(\varepsilon\), repairing the remaining population exactly.

For each gadget \(v\), restrict \(q\) to that gadget, obtaining \(z^v\). Let \(t_v\) be the sum of its coordinates. Applying the population-repair identity gives coefficients \(x_{v,j}\) satisfying

\( \sum_jx_{v,j}p^{v,j}=z^v, \qquad \sum_jx_{v,j}=t_v, \qquad x_{v,j}\le t_v\le1. \)

Because the gadgets partition the type set,

\( \sum_{v,j}x_{v,j}p^{v,j}=q, \qquad \sum_{v,j}x_{v,j}=1. \)

Define the repaired baseline masses by

\( y_{v,j}=\lambda_{v,j}-\varepsilon x_{v,j}. \)

They are strictly positive, since

\( y_{v,j}\ge\frac1D-\varepsilon=\frac1{2D}>0. \)

Their total mass is \(1-\varepsilon\), and their aggregate population, together with the new coalition, is exactly

\( \sum_{v,j}y_{v,j}p^{v,j}+\varepsilon q =\mu-\varepsilon q+\varepsilon q =\mu. \)

Thus these \(N+1\) coalitions form an admissible finite outcome. Its welfare is

\( W=(1-\varepsilon)c+\varepsilon\left(1-\frac1{k+1}\right) =c+\frac{\varepsilon}{k(k+1)}. \)

Since \(k+1\le2k\),

\( W\ge c+\frac{\varepsilon}{2k^2}=b. \)

Therefore the constructed welfare instance is a yes-instance.

6. Encoding size and conclusion

We have \(k<2(t-1)\), hence \(n'=O(n)\) and \(N=O(n^2)\). The affinity matrix has \(N^2=O(n^4)\) binary entries.

Every population mass has denominator dividing \(kD\), and the threshold has denominator dividing \(4Dk^2\). Because \(D<2N\), all these values require \(O(\log n)\) bits each. The complete construction therefore takes polynomial time and produces an input of polynomial length.

We have proved

\( \omega(G)\ge t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the constructed society admits an outcome with }W\ge b. \)

This settles the target question: the exact continuous problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. An algorithm polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
