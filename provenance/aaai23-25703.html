The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given explicit finite facilities F, finite complete client types T with rational distances d(t,A), rational mass distribution μ, and rational 0<λ<ρ≤1, output a facility A minimizing the maximum of Cλμ(A)/OPTλ and Cρμ(A)/OPTρ, where Cθμ(A) is the total distance of the worst θ mass and OPTθ is its minimum over F; determine the tight worst-case guarantee f(ρ/λ).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

Let \(T\cup F\) be a finite metric space with an explicitly given rational distance matrix, and let \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\cap\mathbb{Q}^{T}\). For rational \(0<\lambda_1<\lambda_2<\lambda_3\leq1\), define \(C^\mu_\lambda(A)=\max\{\sum_{t\in T}z_t d(t,A):0\leq z_t\leq\mu_t,\ \sum_{t\in T}z_t=\lambda\}\) and \(C^{\mu,*}_\lambda=\min_{B\in F}C^\mu_\lambda(B)\).
Restrict to instances with \(C^{\mu,*}_{\lambda_i}>0\), and put \(R_I(A)=\max_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}C^\mu_{\lambda_i}(A)/C^{\mu,*}_{\lambda_i}\).

Target

Is computing a facility minimizing \(R_I(A)\) polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard?
Is \(\sup_I\min_{A\in F}R_I(A)=eta_3\), where \(eta_3= rac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\)?

Nearest known results

For unit-mass discrete clients, equivalently \(\mu_t=1/n\) and \(\lambda_i=k_i/n\), the worst-case simultaneous ratio for three centrum objectives is exactly \(eta_3\): a \(eta_3\) upper bound holds, and an instance forces ratio at least \(eta_3\) for every facility.
No result for the exact rational-mass formulation above was verified.

Resolution

Let \(m=|T|\) and \(q_t(A)=d(t,A)\). The facility minimizing \(R_I(A)\) is computable exactly in polynomial time. Moreover,

\[ \sup_I\min_{A\in F}R_I(A)=\beta_3=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}. \]

The supremum need not be attained by any rational instance.

1. Exact evaluation of one upper-tail objective

Fix \(A\in F\) and \(0<\lambda\leq1\). Order the clients as \(t_1,\ldots,t_m\) so that

\[ q_{t_1}(A)\geq q_{t_2}(A)\geq\cdots\geq q_{t_m}(A). \]

Write \(S_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^j\mu_{t_\ell}\), \(S_0=0\), and let \(r\) be the least index satisfying \(S_r\geq\lambda\). Then

\[ C^\mu_\lambda(A) = \sum_{\ell<r}\mu_{t_\ell}q_{t_\ell}(A) + \bigl(\lambda-S_{r-1}\bigr)q_{t_r}(A). \]

Indeed, for every feasible \(z\),

\[ \sum_{\ell=1}^m z_{t_\ell}q_{t_\ell}(A) = \lambda q_{t_r}(A) + \sum_{\ell<r}z_{t_\ell}\bigl(q_{t_\ell}(A)-q_{t_r}(A)\bigr) + \sum_{\ell>r}z_{t_\ell}\bigl(q_{t_\ell}(A)-q_{t_r}(A)\bigr). \]

For \(\ell<r\), the coefficient is nonnegative, so \(z_{t_\ell}\leq\mu_{t_\ell}\) gives an upper bound. For \(\ell>r\), the coefficient is nonpositive, so the corresponding sum is at most zero. This proves that every feasible \(z\) has value at most the displayed formula.

Equality is attained by taking \(z_{t_\ell}=\mu_{t_\ell}\) for \(\ell<r\), \(z_{t_r}=\lambda-S_{r-1}\), and \(z_{t_\ell}=0\) for \(\ell>r\). Thus the formula is exact.

2. Polynomial-time optimization

For each \(A\in F\), sort the values \(d(t,A)\) once. The three values \(C^\mu_{\lambda_i}(A)\) can then be obtained by scanning the resulting cumulative masses.

After computing these quantities for all \(A\in F\), compute

\[ D_i=C^{\mu,*}_{\lambda_i} =\min_{B\in F}C^\mu_{\lambda_i}(B) \]

for each \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\). By hypothesis, every \(D_i\) is positive. Hence exact rational comparison determines

\[ R_I(A)=\max_{i\in\{1,2,3\}} \frac{C^\mu_{\lambda_i}(A)}{D_i} \]

and then an \(A\in F\) minimizing this value.

The algorithm uses \(O(|F||T|\log|T|)\) rational-arithmetic operations. Products and sums of polynomially many binary-encoded rationals have polynomially bounded bit length, and ratios are compared by exact cross-multiplication. Therefore the algorithm runs in polynomial bit complexity. In particular, the search problem belongs to \(\mathrm{FP}\).

3. Transfer of the unit-mass upper bound

Fix an arbitrary rational-mass instance \(I\). Choose a positive integer \(N\) clearing all denominators of the numbers \(\mu_t\) and \(\lambda_i\). Thus

\[ n_t=N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}, \qquad k_i=N\lambda_i\in\mathbb Z, \]

and \(\sum_t n_t=N\). Since the \(\lambda_i\) are strictly increasing, \(1\leq k_1<k_2<k_3\leq N\).

Create \(n_t\) unit-mass client copies of every \(t\). To avoid using a pseudometric with zero distance between copies, fix \(\varepsilon>0\), attach every copy of \(t\) to the original point \(t\) by an edge of length \(\varepsilon\), and use the induced shortest-path metric on the copies and \(F\). Explicitly,

\[ d_\varepsilon((t,j),A)=d(t,A)+\varepsilon. \]

This is a genuine metric: it is the restriction of the metric obtained by attaching distinct leaves to the original metric space.

Give every copy mass \(1/N\). Aggregating the variables of all copies of \(t\) shows that, for every \(A\in F\),

\[ C^{\mathrm{copy}}_{k_i/N}(A) = C^\mu_{\lambda_i}(A)+\varepsilon\lambda_i. \]

The aggregation is exact: copy-level feasible variables sum to some \(z_t\leq n_t/N=\mu_t\), while every such \(z_t\) can be distributed among the \(n_t\) copies, each having capacity \(1/N\). The additional distance \(\varepsilon\) contributes exactly \(\varepsilon\sum_tz_t=\varepsilon\lambda_i\).

Consequently,

\[ C^{\mathrm{copy},*}_{k_i/N} = C^{\mu,*}_{\lambda_i}+\varepsilon\lambda_i. \]

Applying the supplied unit-mass three-centrum theorem gives

\[ \min_{A\in F} \max_i \frac{C^\mu_{\lambda_i}(A)+\varepsilon\lambda_i} {C^{\mu,*}_{\lambda_i}+\varepsilon\lambda_i} \leq\beta_3. \]

Every denominator is positive at \(\varepsilon=0\). Because \(F\) and the set of levels are finite, the left-hand side is a continuous function of \(\varepsilon\). Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) yields

\[ \min_{A\in F}R_I(A)\leq\beta_3. \]

Thus

\[ \sup_I\min_{A\in F}R_I(A)\leq\beta_3. \]

The potentially exponential numerical value of \(N\) causes no computational problem: cloning is used only in this existential proof, while the algorithm in Section 2 operates directly on the binary-encoded rational masses.

4. Rational metrics retain the matching lower bound

Take the supplied tight unit-mass instance \(I_0\), with its three positive optimal costs, for which every facility has simultaneous ratio at least \(\beta_3\). Its masses \(1/n\) and levels \(k_i/n\) are rational. It remains only to ensure that its distance matrix may be required to be rational and a genuine metric.

For a fixed mass vector and fixed levels, define

\[ G(d)=\min_{A\in F}\max_i \frac{C^{\mu,d}_{\lambda_i}(A)} {C^{\mu,d,*}_{\lambda_i}}. \]

If two client-facility distance matrices differ entrywise by at most \(\eta\), then, for every \(A\) and \(i\),

\[ \left| C^{\mu,d}_{\lambda_i}(A) - C^{\mu,d'}_{\lambda_i}(A) \right| \leq\lambda_i\eta. \]

This follows because every feasible \(z\) has total mass \(\lambda_i\). Taking minima over \(A\) gives the same bound for the optimal costs. Since the three optimal costs of \(I_0\) are positive, all relevant ratios, finite maxima, and finite minima are continuous in a neighbourhood of its distance matrix. Hence \(G\) is continuous there.

Rational genuine metrics are dense among finite real metrics, and also among finite pseudometrics after an arbitrarily small perturbation. To see this, let \(\delta(x,y)=1\) for \(x\neq y\) and \(\delta(x,x)=0\). For \(\alpha>0\),

\[ d^\alpha=d+\alpha\delta \]

has positive distance between distinct labels and triangle-inequality slack at least \(\alpha\) on every triple of pairwise distinct points. Approximate each off-diagonal entry of \(d^\alpha\), symmetrically, by a rational number with error less than \(\alpha/4\). Every triangle inequality remains valid because its slack changes by less than \(3\alpha/4\), and all off-diagonal entries remain positive. Letting \(\alpha\downarrow0\) produces rational genuine metrics converging to \(d\).

Therefore, for every \(\xi>0\), there is an admissible rational-metric instance \(I_\xi\) with

\[ \min_{A\in F}R_{I_\xi}(A)>\beta_3-\xi. \]

It follows that

\[ \sup_I\min_{A\in F}R_I(A)\geq\beta_3. \]

Together with the upper bound,

\[ \boxed{ \sup_I\min_{A\in F}R_I(A) = \beta_3 = \frac{3+\sqrt5}{2} }. \]

Finally, every individual rational instance has rational tail costs and hence a rational value \(\min_A R_I(A)\). Since \(\beta_3\) is irrational, the supremum is not attained by any instance having entirely rational input data.
