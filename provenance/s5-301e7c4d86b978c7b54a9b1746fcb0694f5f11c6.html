The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 21 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) with integer entitlements \(w_t>0\), binary item-category values \(a_{tk}\in\{0,1\}\), rational society masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_k\), and integral bundle vectors \(B\) from a specified family \(\mathcal B\), where \(v_t(B)=\sum_k a_{tk}b_k(B)\), compute masses \(x_{tB}\) and nonnegative subsidies \(p_{tB}\) satisfying \(\sum_B x_{tB}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}b_k(B)x_{tB}=\rho_k\), weighted envy-freeness \(\frac{v_t(B)+p_{tB}}{w_t}\geq\frac{v_t(C)+p_{uC}}{w_u}\) for every pair of occupied cells \((t,B)\) and \((u,C)\), \(p_{tB}\leq w_t/w_{\min}\), and \(\sum_{t,B}x_{tB}p_{tB}\leq(\sum_t\mu_t w_t)/w_{\min}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) item categories, nonnegative binary-rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_k\), positive binary-encoded integer entitlements \(w_t\), and values \(a_{tk}\in\{0,1\}\). Each category is valued by at least one supported type.
The admissible bundle family \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{m}\) is an explicitly supplied finite list containing the empty bundle; write \(q=|\mathcal B|\), \(b_k(B)=B_k\), \(v_t(B)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{tk}b_k(B)\), and \(w_{\min}=\min_t w_t\).
A feasible outcome consists of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{tB}\) and per-recipient subsidies \(p_{tB}\), with \(\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}x_{tB}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}b_k(B)x_{tB}=\rho_k\) for every category.
A cell is occupied when \(x_{tB}>0\). For every ordered pair of occupied cells \((t,B),(u,C)\), require weighted envy-freeness: \(\frac{v_t(B)+p_{tB}}{w_t}\geq\frac{v_t(C)+p_{uC}}{w_u}\).
Require \(0\leq p_{tB}\leq w_t/w_{\min}\) and \(\sum_{t,B}x_{tB}p_{tB}\leq\frac{\sum_t\mu_t w_t}{w_{\min}}\); unoccupied cells have zero subsidy. These are comparisons of realized bundles and subsidies, including comparisons within one type.

Is deciding whether a feasible outcome exists in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? In the polynomial-time case, can a feasible outcome also be computed in polynomial time? State running times in \(m,\tau,q,L\), where \(L\) is the total input bit length.

For finite agents with binary additive valuations and unrestricted bundles, a polynomial-time algorithm finds a complete weighted envy-free outcome with subsidies at most \(w_i/w_{\min}\) per agent and \(W/w_{\min}-1\) in total, where \(W=\sum_iw_i\). [Verified discrete result](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09006v5#S7).
No result settling exactly the supported-mass problem defined above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize society masses to sum to one, so supplies and the total subsidy bound are per capita.
  • Resolve the unspecified bundle-family representation as an explicit finite list of nonnegative integer vectors encoded in binary; include the empty bundle, which the discrete model permits.
  • Interpret m as the number of item categories and include the bundle-list size q and total input bit length L in complexity bounds.
  • Allow nonnegative rational supplies, and carry over the assumption that every category is positively valued by some supported type.
  • Interpret subsidies as amounts per recipient, masses and subsidies as rational outputs, and envy comparisons as applying to all occupied cells, including cells of the same type.
  • Set subsidies on unoccupied cells to zero and formulate feasibility without an existence promise, since admissible bundle restrictions can prevent an outcome.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete. Hardness holds even when all entitlements equal \(1\), all masses are uniform dyadic rationals, and every bundle coordinate belongs to \(\{0,2,8\}\).

An exact algorithm can decide feasibility and compute an outcome in time \(2^{\tau q}\mathrm{poly}(m,\tau,q,L)\). A polynomial-time algorithm for general instances would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

1. Two preliminary facts

First, the total subsidy bound follows automatically from the individual bounds and the type-mass equalities:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,B}x_{tB}p_{tB}\leq \sum_{t,B}x_{tB}\frac{w_t}{w_{\min}}=\frac{\sum_t\mu_t w_t}{w_{\min}}.\)

Consequently, we may omit the total subsidy constraint when testing feasibility.

Second, if \((t,B)\) and \((t,C)\) are both occupied, envy-freeness in both directions gives

\(\displaystyle v_t(B)+p_{tB}=v_t(C)+p_{tC}.\)

The individual subsidy bounds therefore imply

\(\displaystyle |v_t(B)-v_t(C)|\leq \frac{w_t}{w_{\min}}.\)

In particular, when all entitlements equal \(1\), a type cannot occupy two bundles whose values differ by more than \(1\).

2. Reduction from exact cover

We reduce from Exact Cover by 3-Sets: given a universe \(U\) of cardinality \(r=3h\) and three-element subsets \(S_1,\ldots,S_s\), decide whether some subcollection covers every element exactly once. This is the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem [Garey–Johnson, entry SP2](https://perso.limos.fr/~palafour/PAPERS/PDF/Garey-Johnson79.pdf). We use instances with \(h,s\geq1\).

Let \(N\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(N\geq s\).

Construction

Introduce \(N\) types and \(N+r\) categories. The categories are:

  • A private category \(h_i\) for each \(i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\).
  • An element category for each \(e\in U\).

Every type has mass \(\mu_i=1/N\) and entitlement \(w_i=1\). Type \(i\) values its private category and every element category:

\(\displaystyle a_{i,h_j}=\begin{cases}1,&i=j,\\0,&i\neq j,\end{cases}\qquad a_{i,e}=1\quad(e\in U).\)

Thus every category is valued by a supported type.

Writing \(\mathbf e_k\) for the unit vector of category \(k\), define

\(\displaystyle B_i^0=8\mathbf e_{h_i}\qquad(1\leq i\leq N),\)

and

\(\displaystyle B_i^1=8\mathbf e_{h_i}+2\sum_{e\in S_i}\mathbf e_e\qquad(1\leq i\leq s).\)

The explicitly listed family is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal B=\{\mathbf0\}\cup\{B_i^0:1\leq i\leq N\}\cup\{B_i^1:1\leq i\leq s\}.\)

Set the supplies to

\(\displaystyle \rho_{h_i}=\frac8N,\qquad \rho_e=\frac2N.\)

All required masses and supplies are dyadic rationals. The construction has

\(\displaystyle \tau=N,\qquad m=N+r,\qquad q=N+s+1,\qquad s\leq N<2s.\)

Its explicitly listed arrays have polynomial size in \(s+r\); their entries have at most \(O(\log(N+1))\) bits. Hence the reduction is computable in polynomial time.

The relevant valuations are

\(\displaystyle v_i(B_i^0)=8,\qquad v_i(B_i^1)=14\quad(i\leq s).\)

For every other bundle \(B\in\mathcal B\), we have \(v_i(B)\leq6\): such a bundle contains none of type \(i\)'s private category and contains at most six element-category items.

Every feasible outcome uses only each type’s own bundles

Fix a type \(i\). Its private category has positive supply \(8/N\). Only \(B_i^0\), and \(B_i^1\) when present, contain that category. Therefore some occupied cell \((u,C)\) has \(C\) among those bundles.

For any occupied cell \((i,B)\), envy-freeness and nonnegative subsidies imply

\(\displaystyle v_i(B)+p_{iB}\geq v_i(C)+p_{uC}\geq8.\)

But if \(B\) is outside type \(i\)'s designated bundles, then

\(\displaystyle v_i(B)+p_{iB}\leq6+1=7,\)

a contradiction. Thus every occupied cell of type \(i\) uses its own designated bundles.

For \(i\leq s\), both designated bundles cannot be occupied: the comparison from \(B_i^0\) to \(B_i^1\) would require

\(\displaystyle 8+p_{iB_i^0}\geq14+p_{iB_i^1},\)

where the left side is at most \(9\) and the right side is at least \(14\).

Consequently, each type puts its entire mass \(1/N\) on exactly one designated bundle. Types \(i>s\) necessarily receive \(B_i^0\).

Feasibility implies an exact cover

Let

\(\displaystyle I=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}:x_{iB_i^1}=1/N\}.\)

For each element category \(e\), its supply equality becomes

\(\displaystyle \frac2N=\sum_{i\in I:e\in S_i}\frac2N.\)

Multiplying by \(N/2\) shows that exactly one selected set contains \(e\). Therefore \(\{S_i:i\in I\}\) is an exact cover of \(U\).

An exact cover implies feasibility

Conversely, suppose \(I\) indexes an exact cover. Give type \(i\) its entire mass on \(B_i^1\) if \(i\in I\), and on \(B_i^0\) otherwise. Set every subsidy to zero.

Each private category receives mass-weighted supply \(8/N\). Each element belongs to exactly one selected set, so its supply is \(2/N\). Type-mass equalities also hold.

For envy-freeness, each type values its own assigned bundle at least \(8\), whereas it values every other type’s assigned bundle at most \(6\). Thus every required comparison holds with zero subsidies. All subsidy constraints hold as well.

This proves equivalence and establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness.

3. Membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\)

Let \(n=\tau q\). Guess the occupied-cell set

\(\displaystyle S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\times\mathcal B.\)

Because the total subsidy bound is redundant, feasibility for this support separates into two rational linear programs.

Mass feasibility

Use variables \(x_{tB}\) for cells in \(S\), and a variable \(\delta\). Maximize \(\delta\), subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_{B:(t,B)\in S}x_{tB}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{(t,B)\in S}b_k(B)x_{tB}=\rho_k\quad\text{for every }k,\)

and

\(\displaystyle x_{tB}\geq\delta\quad((t,B)\in S),\qquad 0\leq\delta\leq1.\)

The support admits strictly positive masses exactly when this program is feasible and its optimum is positive. Indeed, any positive mass assignment has a positive minimum over the finite set \(S\); conversely, a solution with \(\delta>0\) occupies every cell in \(S\).

This program has at most \(n+1\) variables and \(O(m+\tau+n)\) constraints. Its feasible region is bounded: nonnegative masses satisfying a type-mass equality are at most \(1\), and \(\delta\leq1\).

Subsidy feasibility

Use variables \(p_{tB}\) for cells in \(S\), impose the individual bounds, and impose, for every ordered pair in \(S\),

\(\displaystyle w_u p_{tB}-w_t p_{uC}\geq w_t v_t(C)-w_u v_t(B).\)

These are exactly the weighted envy inequalities after multiplication by the positive number \(w_tw_u\).

This program has at most \(n\) variables and \(O(n^2)\) constraints. All coefficients are rational with bit length polynomial in \(L\); bundle valuations are computed by summing at most \(m\) input integers.

The polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming applies to both programs. They admit a polynomial-time exact feasibility test, and feasible rational solutions can be computed with polynomial bit length. Therefore the guessed support can be verified in \(\mathrm{poly}(m,\tau,q,L)\) time, proving membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

Together with the reduction, this establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

4. Running time and outcome computation

The valuation table can be computed using \(O(m\tau q)\) integer additions. For any prescribed occupied set, the two linear programs above decide feasibility and compute an outcome in \(\mathrm{poly}(m,\tau,q,L)\) bit operations.

Enumerating all at most \(2^{\tau q}\) occupied sets therefore gives an exact decision and construction algorithm with running time

\(\displaystyle 2^{\tau q}\mathrm{poly}(m,\tau,q,L).\)

A polynomial-time algorithm that always computes a feasible outcome or reports infeasibility would decide this \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem.

The supplied finite-agent theorem concerns unrestricted bundles. Its algorithm may use bundles outside the explicitly prescribed family, so it does not settle feasibility under the bundle restrictions used here. See [Theorem 7.12 of the cited paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09006v5#S7).
