The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a known poll, target candidate \(\omega\), and a finite distribution of positive mass over voter types that specify strict rankings and bounded integer counts of initial neighbors by type, find a finite split into cohorts and an integer balanced table of added links so that the paper’s update process reaches a stable profile in which \(\omega\) wins. Consider societies where each candidate has positive mass of first-round supporters and more than \(1/m\) of the population does not rank \(\omega\) last.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq2\) candidates, with target \(\omega\), a fixed lexicographic tie-breaking order, and known poll scores \(q_c\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\). Write \(W(x)\) for the tie-broken Plurality winner of score vector \(x\).
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Each type specifies a strict ranking and integers \(k_{tu}\geq0\), its initial neighbor counts by type, with \(\sum_u k_{tu}\leq d\) and \(\mu_tk_{tu}=\mu_uk_{ut}\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Assume every candidate has positive mass ranking it first, and the mass not ranking \(\omega\) last exceeds \(1/m\).
An intervention chooses finitely many cohorts \(i\), each inheriting a type \(t(i)\), with positive rational masses \(w_i\) satisfying \(\sum_{i:t(i)=t}w_i=\mu_t\), and nonnegative integer tables \(B,A\) satisfying \(\sum_{j:t(j)=u}B_{ij}=k_{t(i)u}\), \(w_iB_{ij}=w_jB_{ji}\), and \(w_iA_{ij}=w_jA_{ji}\).
The tables specify initial and added neighbors per individual; diagonal entries mean distinct neighbors within the same cohort, never self-loops. There is no bound on added links or cohort count. All additions occur before voting and remain fixed.
Initially each cohort votes for its ranking's first candidate. At round \(r\), its perceived scores are \(x_{i,c}(r)=q_c+\sum_j(B_{ij}+A_{ij})\mathbf1\{b_j(r)=c\}\).
Updates are simultaneous: among candidates \(a\neq b_i(r)\) preferred to \(W(x_i(r))\) for which \(W(x_i(r)+e_a)=a\), choose the most preferred; if none exists, retain the current vote. Here \(e_a\) is one unit vote, even though individuals have zero population mass.
A profile is stable when every cohort retains its vote. Its global winner is \(W((\sum_iw_i\mathbf1\{b_i=c\})_{c\in C})\). Feasibility means some intervention reaches a stable profile with winner \(\omega\) after finitely many rounds.

Is this feasibility problem in \(\mathrm P\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), displaying any additional dependence on \(d\) and poll size \(s=\sum_cq_c\); \(L\) is the total input bit length, including binary-encoded counts and poll scores.

For finite societies, known-poll influence has a polynomial-time construction under quantitative bounded-degree, supporter-reserve, and preference-fraction conditions; it stabilizes by round three ([Sina et al., 2015](https://u.cs.biu.ac.il/~sarit/data/articles/fp195-Sina.pdf)).
No result settling this exact mass-and-integer-cohort formulation was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total mass to one; require at least two candidates, nonnegative integer poll scores, and a fixed lexicographic order for both perceived and global ties.
  • Interpret bounded initial counts as a common bound on total initial degree, with mass balance required for undirected links.
  • Because counts by original type do not determine routing after splitting, allow the intervention to choose an integer initial-link refinement B preserving every original neighbor count and satisfying mass balance.
  • Require cohort masses to be positive rationals and added-link counts to be nonnegative integers; impose no added-link budget, cohort bound, or convergence deadline.
  • Allow within-cohort links between distinct individuals; diagonal table entries do not represent self-loops.
  • Retain a unit pivotal vote in the local update despite atomless population mass: test a candidate by adding one vote to the neighbor-plus-poll score vector, as in the stated pivotal construction.
  • Use truthful initialization, simultaneous deterministic updates, retention when no improving switch exists, and the same tie-breaking order for the final mass-weighted Plurality outcome.
  • Interpret positive first-round supporter mass and bounded initial degree as the continuous reserve conditions; do not introduce a finite population size into normalized masses.

Classification

The problem is in \(\mathrm P\). In fact, every instance satisfying the stated assumptions is feasible.

More strongly, an intervention with exactly \(m\tau\) cohorts and polynomial encoding length suffices. Starting from truthful votes, it reaches a stable profile with strict global winner \(\omega\) after one simultaneous update.

The construction uses arbitrarily small rational reserve cohorts and unbounded added degrees, both expressly permitted in the question.

1. Choosing integral reciprocal link counts

Write the input masses over a common denominator as \(\mu_t=n_t/D\), where every \(n_t\) is a positive integer and \(\sum_t n_t=D\). Taking the product of the input denominators gives such a \(D\) with \(O(L)\) bits.

Let \(p=\sum_{t:\,\omega\text{ is not last for }t}\mu_t\). By assumption, \(p>1/m\). Define

\[ g=p-\frac{1-p}{m-1}=\frac{mp-1}{m-1}>0. \]

Choose the positive integers

\[ P=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}n_t,\qquad K=\left\lceil\frac1g\right\rceil+1,\qquad H=(m-1)PK. \]

Then \(Hg>1\). Moreover, for every pair of types \(t,u\),

\[ \frac{H\mu_t}{(m-1)\mu_u} =PK\frac{n_t}{n_u} \]

is a positive integer, because \(n_u\) divides \(P\). This divisibility will make every added reciprocal count integral.

Set \(\varepsilon=1/(H+1)\), and enumerate the candidates other than \(\omega\) as \(c_1,\ldots,c_{m-1}\).

2. Cohorts and the initial table

For each type \(t\), create \(m\) cohorts, indexed by \(r\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), with masses

\[ w_{t,0}=\varepsilon\mu_t,\qquad w_{t,r}=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{m-1}\mu_t \quad(1\leq r\leq m-1). \]

All masses are positive rational numbers, and their sum for each type is \(\mu_t\).

The cohorts with \(r=0\) form the reserve. Every reserve cohort will retain its truthful vote.

Define the initial table by

\[ B_{(t,r),(u,r)}=k_{tu},\qquad B_{(t,r),(u,r')}=0\quad(r\ne r'). \]

Thus each cohort has exactly its prescribed number of initial neighbors of every type. Within each copy, all masses have the same scaling factor, so the input identity \(\mu_tk_{tu}=\mu_uk_{ut}\) gives

\[ w_{t,r}B_{(t,r),(u,r)} =w_{u,r}B_{(u,r),(t,r)}. \]

The initial table therefore satisfies every required constraint.

For each candidate \(c\), select a reserve cohort \(a(c)\) whose type ranks \(c\) first. Such a cohort exists by the positive first-choice-mass assumption. Different candidates have different selected cohorts.

For a working cohort \((t,r)\), where \(r\geq1\), prescribe its eventual vote as

\[ z(t,r)= \begin{cases} \omega,&\omega\text{ is not last for }t,\\ c_r,&\omega\text{ is last for }t. \end{cases} \]

Every prescribed vote is different from that type’s last-ranked candidate.

3. Keeping truthful cohorts fixed

Call a cohort *fixed* if it is in the reserve or its prescribed vote is already its first choice.

First construct all off-diagonal additions as described in the next section. For each fixed cohort \(i\), then set

\[ A_{ii}=s+\sum_jB_{ij}+\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}+2. \]

These diagonal additions satisfy mass balance automatically.

Suppose \(i\) currently votes for its first choice \(f_i\). Its diagonal added neighbors contribute \(A_{ii}\) votes to \(f_i\). Every other candidate’s perceived score is at most

\[ s+\sum_jB_{ij}+\sum_{j\ne i}A_{ij}=A_{ii}-2. \]

Consequently, \(W(x_i)=f_i\). No candidate is preferred to this winner, so the update rule retains \(f_i\).

Starting from truthful voting, induction shows that every fixed cohort keeps its first-choice vote forever, regardless of the votes outside that cohort. In particular, every selected reserve cohort \(a(c)\) supplies fixed votes for \(c\).

The diagonal entries here have exactly the interpretation allowed in the question: distinct neighbors within the same cohort.

4. Making every remaining cohort switch once

Let \(d_0=\max_t\sum_u k_{tu}\), so \(d_0\leq d\), and put

\[ T=s+2d_0+2. \]

Consider a nonfixed working cohort \(i=(t,r)\). Let \(z=z(t,r)\), and let \(\ell\) be the last-ranked candidate of type \(t\). Thus \(z\ne\ell\), and \(z\) differs from \(i\)’s initial vote.

Before additions, its truthful perceived score for candidate \(c\) is

\[ h_{t,c}=q_c+\sum_{u:\,u\text{ ranks }c\text{ first}}k_{tu}. \]

Every \(h_{t,c}\) is at most \(s+d_0\).

Define \(\alpha=1\) if the tie-breaking order favors \(z\) over \(\ell\), and \(\alpha=0\) otherwise. Add neighbors according to

\[ A_{i,a(z)}=T-h_{t,z},\qquad A_{i,a(\ell)}=T+\alpha-h_{t,\ell}. \]

Both counts are positive integers. All other off-diagonal additions incident from \(i\) are zero.

For either selected reserve endpoint \(j=a(c)\), define the reciprocal count by

\[ A_{ji}=\frac{w_i}{w_j}A_{ij}. \]

If \(j\) inherits type \(u\), then

\[ \frac{w_i}{w_j} =\frac{H\mu_t}{(m-1)\mu_u}, \]

which is an integer by Section 1. Thus the reciprocal count is a nonnegative integer and \(w_iA_{ij}=w_jA_{ji}\). All off-diagonal additions satisfy the required balance constraints.

The first update

At the truthful profile, the resulting perceived scores are \(x_{i,z}=T\) and \(x_{i,\ell}=T+\alpha\). Every other candidate has score at most \(s+d_0<T\).

There are two cases:

  • If \(\alpha=0\), the two scores are equal and tie-breaking selects \(\ell\). Adding one vote to \(z\) makes \(z\) strictly win.
  • If \(\alpha=1\), \(\ell\) leads by one. Adding one vote to \(z\) creates a tie that selects \(z\).

Hence \(W(x_i)=\ell\) and \(W(x_i+e_z)=z\). The cohort prefers \(z\) to \(\ell\).

No candidate outside \(\{z,\ell\}\) can win after receiving one additional vote, because its score is at most \(s+d_0+1<T\). Therefore \(z\) is the unique eligible alternative, and cohort \(i\) switches to \(z\).

All fixed cohorts retain their votes during this simultaneous update.

Stability after the update

The added neighbors of \(i\) keep voting for \(z\) and \(\ell\). Changes among its initial neighbors can decrease either candidate’s score by at most \(d_0\). Therefore, after the update,

\[ x_{i,z}\geq T-d_0,\qquad x_{i,\ell}\geq T+\alpha-d_0. \]

Both lower bounds are at least \(s+d_0+2\). Any candidate outside this pair has perceived score at most \(s+d_0\), even if all initial neighbors vote for it. Such a candidate cannot win after one additional vote.

The perceived winner must consequently be \(z\) or \(\ell\):

  • If it is \(z\), the only other potentially pivotal candidate is \(\ell\), which the cohort ranks last.
  • If it is \(\ell\), the only other potentially pivotal candidate is \(z\), which is already the cohort’s current vote and is excluded by the update rule.

In either case, the cohort retains \(z\). Together with Section 3, this proves that the profile after the first update is stable.

5. Strict global victory

Let \(f_c\) be the original mass ranking candidate \(c\) first. The reserve contributes \(\varepsilon f_c\) votes to \(c\).

All working mass of types not ranking \(\omega\) last votes for \(\omega\). The remaining working mass is divided equally among the other \(m-1\) candidates. Consequently, the stable global scores are

\[ v_\omega=(1-\varepsilon)p+\varepsilon f_\omega, \]

and, for every \(c\ne\omega\),

\[ v_c=(1-\varepsilon)\frac{1-p}{m-1}+\varepsilon f_c. \]

Since \(f_\omega-f_c\geq-1\),

\[ v_\omega-v_c =(1-\varepsilon)g+\varepsilon(f_\omega-f_c) \geq(1-\varepsilon)g-\varepsilon =\frac{Hg-1}{H+1}>0. \]

Thus \(\omega\) strictly defeats every rival. Global tie-breaking is immaterial.

This completes the feasibility proof.

6. Running time and encoding length

The construction uses exactly \(m\tau\) cohorts. Its arithmetic remains polynomial in the binary input length.

Indeed, \(D\) and each \(n_t\) have \(O(L)\) bits, so \(P\) has \(O(\tau L)\) bits. If \(S=\sum_{t:\,\omega\text{ is not last}}n_t\), then

\[ g=\frac{mS-D}{(m-1)D}. \]

The numerator is a positive integer, implying \(g\geq1/((m-1)D)\). Hence \(K\) has \(O(L+\log m)\) bits, and \(H\) has \(O(\tau L+\log m)\) bits.

A nonfixed working cohort receives at most \(2T+1=O(s+d+1)\) added neighbors. For a selected reserve cohort of type \(u\), its total off-diagonal added degree is at most \(H(T+1)/\mu_u\): summing the mass ratios of all working cohorts to this reserve cohort gives \(H/\mu_u\), and each incident working count is at most \(T+1\). This bound also controls the diagonal additions.

Therefore all masses and counts in the output have

\[ \beta=O\!\left(\tau L+\log(m+d+s+2)\right) \]

bits. Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, a conservative bound for constructing and writing both dense tables is

\[ O\!\left((L+m^2\tau^2) \left(\tau L+\log(m+d+s+2)\right)^2\right) \]

bit operations. The dependence on \(d\) and \(s\) is logarithmic; no iteration runs up to either numerical value.

For the decision problem on the promised domain, the algorithm simply returns yes, taking \(O(L)\) time if it reads the input. The construction above additionally supplies a polynomial-size witness.

Unlike the quantitative finite-society construction of [Sina et al. (2015)](https://u.cs.biu.ac.il/~sarit/data/articles/fp195-Sina.pdf), this proof obtains its reserve from rational subdivision and protects it using unbounded internal added degrees.
