The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite seller and buyer type sets \(S\) and \(B\), binary-rational type masses \(\mu_i\) and \(\nu_j\), and the paper's valuation functions and integer price bounds for each type pair, compute a stable mass matching. Represent it by \(\lambda_{ijp}\ge 0\), the mass of type-\(i\) sellers matched to type-\(j\) buyers at integer price \(p\), with \(\sum_{j,p}\lambda_{ijp}\le\mu_i\) and \(\sum_{i,p}\lambda_{ijp}\le\nu_j\); require individual rationality and no positive-mass seller and buyer subgroups that can both strictly improve by trading at any feasible price.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: finite disjoint seller and buyer type sets \(S,B\), each type having positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_i\) or \(\nu_j\). Let \(\tau=|S|+|B|\) be the support size, not the number of possible types, and \(m=|S||B|\). Mass totals need not equal each other or be normalized.
For every \((i,j)\in S\times B\), input signed binary integers \(a_{ij}\le b_{ij}\) and explicit tables of signed rational utilities \(s_{ij}(p),t_{ij}(p)\) for all \(p\in P_{ij}:=[a_{ij},b_{ij}]\cap\mathbb Z\). Seller utility \(s_{ij}\) is strictly increasing in \(p\); buyer utility \(t_{ij}\) is strictly decreasing. Positive prices mean payment from buyer to seller. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length, including these tables.
A mass matching assigns rational masses \(\lambda_{ijp}\ge0\), with \(\sum_{j,p}\lambda_{ijp}\le\mu_i\) and \(\sum_{i,p}\lambda_{ijp}\le\nu_j\). Each matched portion receives its contract utility; unmatched portions receive \(0\).
Individual rationality requires \(s_{ij}(p),t_{ij}(p)\ge0\) whenever \(\lambda_{ijp}>0\). Utilities may tie across contracts, and no tie-breaking is imposed.
Stability requires that there is no \((i,j,p)\), with \(p\in P_{ij}\), for which positive mass of type-\(i\) sellers currently receives utility strictly below \(s_{ij}(p)\) and positive mass of type-\(j\) buyers currently receives utility strictly below \(t_{ij}(p)\). These portions may leave existing trades; comparisons use their individual contract utilities, including unmatched utility, rather than type averages.

Is computing a stable mass matching polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

The discrete unit-agent version has an algorithm proved to terminate finitely and return a pairwise-stable outcome; no polynomial running-time bound is established. [Ali–Javaid](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/3259/pdf)
For the related real-capacity ordinal stable allocation problem, a strongly polynomial algorithm is known; the verified result does not explicitly address this type-and-price formulation. [Baïou–Balinski, corrected version](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/moor.27.4.662.302)

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Computing a stable mass matching is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). The proof is a reduction to a polynomially solvable stable-flow problem. It handles utility ties, binary-encoded masses, and multiple prices between the same types.

1. Contract preprocessing

Let \(K=\sum_{i,j}|P_{ij}|\), the number of explicitly listed contracts. Because the utility tables are part of the input, \(K=O(L)\).

Retain the contract set
\[ \mathcal C=\{(i,j,p):s_{ij}(p)>0,\ t_{ij}(p)>0\}. \]
Here and below, the displayed formulas use the same mathematical notation as the inline formulas.

We will assign zero mass to every discarded contract. This cannot leave a blocking contract among those discarded: in the outcome constructed below, every individual receives nonnegative utility, whereas a discarded contract gives at least one endpoint utility at most \(0\). That endpoint has no portion strictly worse than the discarded contract.

For each seller, order its incident retained contracts by decreasing seller utility, resolving equalities arbitrarily. For each buyer, do the same using buyer utility. Write \(\succ_i\) and \(\succ_j\) for these strict orders. They satisfy
\[ s_{ij}(p)>s_{ij'}(p')\ \Longrightarrow\ (i,j,p)\succ_i(i,j',p'), \]
and the analogous implication for buyers.

These orders are auxiliary algorithmic choices. The output will be proved stable under the original utilities, with their original ties.

If \(\mathcal C\) is empty, the zero matching is already stable by the preceding argument. Hence assume \(\mathcal C\ne\varnothing\), and let \(N=|\mathcal C|\).

2. The stable-flow theorem used

A flow in a directed network respects arc capacities and conserves flow at every vertex except its source and sink. Each nonterminal vertex strictly ranks its outgoing arcs and, separately, its incoming arcs.

A *blocking walk* is a directed walk whose arcs all have unused capacity and which satisfies both endpoint conditions:

  • Its starting vertex is the source, or it prefers the walk’s first arc to some outgoing arc carrying positive flow.
  • Its ending vertex is the sink, or it prefers the walk’s last arc to some incoming arc carrying positive flow.

A flow is *stable* if it has no blocking walk.

Cseh and Matuschke’s Theorem 2 gives an algorithm computing a stable flow using \(O(|V||E|)\) arithmetic operations. Their model permits nonnegative real capacities; its strict preference orders concern incoming and outgoing arcs separately. Their algorithm starts at zero and augments by minimum residual capacities. Consequently, integer capacities produce integer flow values. See [*New and simple algorithms for stable flow problems*, Sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3701).

We now construct a simple directed network satisfying these hypotheses.

3. Network construction and feasibility

Introduce a source \(\sigma\), a sink \(\omega\), and one vertex \(w_e\) for every retained contract \(e=(i,j,p)\). Include seller and buyer vertices incident to retained contracts. Types with no retained contracts remain entirely unmatched.

Set
\[ C=1+\sum_{i\in S}\mu_i+\sum_{j\in B}\nu_j. \]

Add the following arcs:

  • \(\sigma\to i\), of capacity \(\mu_i\), for each included seller.
  • \(j\to\omega\), of capacity \(\nu_j\), for each included buyer.
  • \(i\to w_e\) and \(w_e\to j\), both of capacity \(C\), for each \(e=(i,j,p)\in\mathcal C\).

Seller \(i\) ranks its outgoing contract arcs according to \(\succ_i\). Buyer \(j\) ranks its incoming contract arcs according to \(\succ_j\). Each other incoming or outgoing list has only one arc, so its order is immediate.

There are no parallel arcs: different contracts have different intermediate vertices. Every nonterminal vertex has incoming and outgoing arcs. All capacities are positive.

For any feasible flow \(f\), conservation at \(w_e\) gives
\[ f(iw_e)=f(w_ej). \]
Define
\[ \lambda_{ijp}=f(iw_e)\qquad\text{for }e=(i,j,p)\in\mathcal C, \]
and set all other \(\lambda_{ijp}\) to zero.

Conservation at seller and buyer vertices gives
\[ \sum_{j,p}\lambda_{ijp}=f(\sigma i)\le\mu_i, \qquad \sum_{i,p}\lambda_{ijp}=f(j\omega)\le\nu_j. \]
Thus the resulting assignment is a mass matching. It is individually rational because every used contract belongs to \(\mathcal C\).

Crucially, each contract arc is always unsaturated. Indeed,
\[ f(iw_e)=f(w_ej)=\lambda_{ijp}\le\min\{\mu_i,\nu_j\}<C. \]
Therefore, the artificial contract capacities cannot conceal a blocking contract.

4. A stable flow yields the required stability

Compute a stable flow \(f\) and extract \(\lambda\) as above. Suppose, for contradiction, that an original contract \(e=(i,j,p)\) blocks \(\lambda\).

By individual rationality, all current individual utilities are nonnegative. Strict improvement at both endpoints therefore implies
\[ s_{ij}(p)>0,\qquad t_{ij}(p)>0. \]
Hence \(e\in\mathcal C\), and its contract path exists.

Define the unmatched masses
\[ u_i=\mu_i-\sum_{j',p'}\lambda_{ij'p'}, \qquad v_j=\nu_j-\sum_{i',p'}\lambda_{i'jp'}. \]

Because positive seller mass receives utility strictly below \(s_{ij}(p)\), at least one of the following holds:

  • \(u_i>0\).
  • Some retained contract \(e'=(i,j',p')\) carries positive mass and satisfies \(s_{ij'}(p')<s_{ij}(p)\).

This follows because the seller’s mass is partitioned among finitely many contracts and an unmatched portion. Likewise, either \(v_j>0\), or a positive-mass contract \(e''=(i',j,p')\) gives buyer utility strictly below \(t_{ij}(p)\).

Construct a directed path containing \(i\to w_e\to j\), choosing its endpoints as follows.

If \(u_i>0\), start at \(\sigma\) and prepend \(\sigma\to i\). This arc is unsaturated, since
\[ \mu_i-f(\sigma i)=u_i>0. \]
Otherwise, start at \(i\). The positive-mass contract \(e'\) supplies an outgoing arc \(i\to w_{e'}\) carrying positive flow, and strict utility improvement implies that \(i\) prefers \(i\to w_e\) to that arc.

If \(v_j>0\), end at \(\omega\) and append \(j\to\omega\). This arc is unsaturated, since
\[ \nu_j-f(j\omega)=v_j>0. \]
Otherwise, end at \(j\). The positive-mass contract \(e''\) supplies an incoming arc carrying positive flow, and strict utility improvement implies that \(j\) prefers \(w_e\to j\) to that arc.

Both contract arcs are unsaturated by Section 3. Thus every arc of the constructed path is unsaturated, and both endpoint conditions for a blocking walk hold. This contradicts stability of \(f\).

Therefore \(\lambda\) is stable under the original individual-utility definition. In particular, the proof compares individual portions, including unmatched portions, and never uses type-average utilities.

5. Bit complexity and output size

The network has at most
\[ |V|\le\tau+N+2,\qquad |E|\le\tau+2N. \]
Since \(N\le K=O(L)\), its size is polynomial in the stated parameters.

Exact utility comparisons and sorting take polynomial bit time: comparing two input rationals requires cross-multiplying their integer numerators and positive denominators, whose bit lengths are bounded by the input length.

To handle capacities exactly, write all input masses as fractions with positive integer denominators, and let \(D\) be the product of those denominators. Its bit length is at most the sum of their bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Multiply every network capacity by \(D\). All capacities are then integers of polynomial bit length, including
\[ DC=D+\sum_iD\mu_i+\sum_jD\nu_j. \]

Apply the cited stable-flow algorithm to this integer network. Its augmentations preserve integrality. Its arithmetic consists of residual-capacity calculations and integer flow updates; capacities and feasible flow values have polynomial bit length. Together with its polynomial operation count, this gives polynomial bit complexity.

Finally, divide every flow value by \(D\). Positive scaling preserves feasibility, saturation, positivity, and every blocking-walk condition, so the divided flow is stable in the original network. The extracted matching is rational, and each output mass has polynomial bit length. Writing all \(K\) contract masses therefore also takes polynomial time.

This proves the claimed polynomial-time solvability. The proof actually needs only explicitly listed contract utilities; their monotonicity in price is not needed.
