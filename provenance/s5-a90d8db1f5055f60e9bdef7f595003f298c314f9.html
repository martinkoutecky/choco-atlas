Each \(S_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\) has size \(\kappa_t\geq2\), and \(\mathcal G_t\) is an explicitly represented strict partial order on \(S_t\); incomparability does not mean indifference.
A separator \(s\) is comparable with every other item and has at least one item strictly below it. Write \(A_t^+(s)\) and \(A_t^-(s)\) for its strictly better and strictly worse items; the former may be empty.
Use all separators \(s_{t,1},\ldots,s_{t,\ell_t}\), with \(\ell_t\geq1\), ordered by increasing position \(p_{t,a}=|A_t^+(s_{t,a})|+1\). Define \(E_{t,a}=\{(i,s_{t,a}):i\in A_t^-(s_{t,a})\}\), so \(1\leq p_{t,a}<\kappa_t\).

Conclusion and precision convention

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. No sampling assumption, comparison-graph connectivity, or strict concavity is needed.

I use the standard precision interpretation: the requested accuracy can be specified by an integer \(q\) with \(2^{-q}\leq\varepsilon\), taking \(q=\lceil\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\rceil\). If \(\varepsilon\) is supplied as an arbitrarily long rational encoding, reading that encoding adds its input-length cost. The optimization algorithm itself has running time polynomial in \(d,\tau,L,q\).

The proof below addresses the two issues that concavity alone does not settle: polynomial-time evaluation when \(b\) is numerically enormous, and production of a rational point satisfying the constraints exactly.

1. Reduction to a convex logistic-loss problem

For each listed comparison \(e=(t,a,i,s_{t,a})\), define

\(w_e=\mu_t/(\kappa_t-p_{t,a})\), \(z_e(\theta)=\theta_i-\theta_{s_{t,a}}\), \(h(z)=\log(1+e^z)\).

Then the objective in the question satisfies

\(F(\theta)=-f(\theta)\), \(f(\theta)=\sum_e w_e h(z_e(\theta))\).

All weights are positive rational numbers. Transitive closure of each explicitly represented order identifies its separators and comparison sets in polynomial time. There are at most \(\tau d(d-1)\) comparison occurrences, so constructing this weighted list takes polynomial time and produces coefficients of polynomial encoding length.

Every separator has exactly \(\kappa_t-p_{t,a}\) items below it. Consequently, the total comparison weight is

\(W:=\sum_e w_e=\sum_t\mu_t\ell_t\leq d-1.\)

Indeed, a survey can have at most \(\kappa_t-1\leq d-1\) separators.

The derivatives of the scalar loss are

\(h'(z)=e^z/(1+e^z)\), \(h''(z)=e^z/(1+e^z)^2>0.\)

Thus \(f\) is convex and continuously differentiable. Because \(\Omega_b\) is nonempty and compact, \(f\) attains its minimum there.

Two uniform bounds will be useful. First, since \(0<h'(z)<1\), the mean-value theorem gives

\(\lvert f(\theta)-f(\phi)\rvert\leq 2W\lVert\theta-\phi\rVert_\infty.\)

Second, for \(\theta\in\Omega_b\), every \(z_e(\theta)\leq2b\), and hence

\(0<f(\theta)\leq W(2b+\log 2)\leq d(2b+1).\)

These bounds require no statistical assumptions.

2. Polynomial-time numerical evaluation

For any rational \(z\) and rational tolerance \(0<\eta<1\), both \(h(z)\) and \(h'(z)\) can be approximated by rationals to absolute error at most \(\eta\), in time polynomial in the encoding length of \(z\) and \(\log(1/\eta)\).

Here is an explicit justification. Write

\(h(z)=\max(z,0)+\log(1+e^{-|z|}).\)

Choose an integer \(H\) such that \(2^{-H}\leq\eta/8\), with \(H=O(1+\log(1/\eta))\).

If \(|z|\geq H\), then

\(0\leq\log(1+e^{-|z|})\leq e^{-|z|}\leq2^{-H}.\)

Also, approximating \(h'(z)\) by \(1\) when \(z\geq0\), and by \(0\) when \(z<0\), incurs error at most \(e^{-|z|}\). This case therefore requires no enormous exponential.

If \(|z|<H\), compute \(u=e^{-|z|}\) by a Taylor polynomial. Its degree can be chosen polynomial in \(H+\log(1/\eta)\): the degree-\(N\) remainder has absolute value at most

\(e^H H^{N+1}/(N+1)!.\)

The bound \((N+1)!\geq((N+1)/e)^{N+1}\) shows that a sufficiently large constant multiple of \(H+\log(1/\eta)+1\) suffices. All intermediate rational numbers have polynomial encoding length. Clipping the approximation to \([0,1]\) cannot increase its error.

For rational \(u\in[0,1]\), set \(v=u/(2+u)\), so \(0\leq v\leq1/3\). Then

\(\log(1+u)=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}v^{2k+1}/(2k+1).\)

The tail decreases geometrically, so polynomially many rational terms suffice. Errors in \(u\) propagate by at most their absolute magnitude, since the derivative of \(\log(1+u)\) is at most \(1\). The derivative \(h'(z)\) is obtained from \(u/(1+u)\) or \(1/(1+u)\); these functions also have derivatives of absolute value at most \(1\) on \([0,1]\).

This proves the claimed scalar evaluation complexity.

Summing over the polynomially many comparison occurrences therefore yields polynomial-time rational approximations to \(f\) and its gradient at any rational query point. The next section specifies the coordinates and error bounds needed for optimization.

3. A full-dimensional convex epigraph with explicit bounds

Eliminate the sum constraint using \(x\in\mathbb R^{d-1}\):

\(T(x)=(x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1},-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}x_j).\)

Define

\(C=\{x:|x_j|\leq b\text{ for every }j,\ |\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}x_j|\leq b\}\),

and \(g(x)=f(T(x))\). Thus \(T(C)=\Omega_b\).

Each linear form \(z_e(T(x))\) has coefficients of absolute value at most \(2\). Consequently, approximating every scalar loss and derivative to error \(\eta\) gives errors at most \(W\eta\) in \(g(x)\) and at most \(2W\eta\) in each coordinate of \(\nabla g(x)\). Since \(W\leq d-1\), both can be approximated to prescribed absolute error \(\alpha\) in polynomial time.

Set

\(U=4d(1+b)\),

and introduce the convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\)

\(K=\{(x,z):x\in C,\ 0\leq z\leq U,\ g(x)\leq z\}.\)

Minimizing the linear coordinate \(z\) over \(K\) is equivalent to minimizing \(f\) over \(\Omega_b\).

Here are explicit inner and outer balls. Let

\(a_0=(0,U/2)\), \(r=\min\{1,b/(2d)\}\), \(R=U+db+1.\)

The closed Euclidean ball of radius \(r\) around \(a_0\) lies in \(K\). To verify this, a point in that ball has \(|x_j|\leq r\) and

\(|\sum_jx_j|\leq(d-1)r\leq b/2.\)

Thus \(x\in C\). Its last coordinate satisfies

\(z\geq U/2-1=2d(1+b)-1\geq d(1+2b)\geq g(x),\)

where the middle inequality uses \(d\geq2\). Its last coordinate also lies in \([0,U]\).

Every point of \(K\) satisfies

\(\lVert(x,z)\rVert_2\leq\sqrt{(d-1)b^2+U^2}<R.\)

The same \(R\) bounds distance from \(a_0\). All of \(a_0,r,R,U\) are rational and have encoding lengths polynomial in \(L+\log d\). In particular, a numerically large or small \(b\) does not invalidate the binary complexity bound.

4. A rigorous weak separation oracle

Given a rational query \((x,z)\) and a rational tolerance \(\delta>0\), the oracle will either certify that its Euclidean distance to \(K\) is at most \(\delta\), or return a rational separating direction.

First check the rational linear inequalities defining \(C\) and \(0\leq z\leq U\). Any violated inequality gives an exact separating hyperplane. Hence suppose these inequalities hold.

Set

\(Q=1+2(d-1)b\), \(\alpha=\min\{1,\delta/(4Q)\}\), \(\beta=Q\alpha\leq\delta/4.\)

Compute rational approximations \(\widetilde g,\widetilde v\) satisfying

\(|\widetilde g-g(x)|\leq\alpha\), \(\lVert\widetilde v-\nabla g(x)\rVert_\infty\leq\alpha.\)

For every \(y\in C\), convexity implies

\(g(y)\geq g(x)+\nabla g(x)\cdot(y-x).\)

Since \(\lVert y-x\rVert_1\leq2(d-1)b\), the approximation errors give the valid lower bound

\(g(y)\geq\widetilde g+\widetilde v\cdot(y-x)-\beta.\)

There are two cases.

Separating case. If \(\widetilde g-z>\beta\), every \((y,u)\in K\) satisfies

\(\widetilde v\cdot(y-x)-(u-z)\leq z-\widetilde g+\beta<0.\)

Thus \((\widetilde v,-1)\) strictly separates the query from \(K\). It is rational, nonzero, and can be normalized to infinity norm \(1\) by rational arithmetic.

Near-membership case. If \(\widetilde g-z\leq\beta\), then

\(g(x)-z\leq\alpha+\beta\leq\delta/2.\)

The point \((x,\max\{z,g(x)\})\) belongs to \(K\), because \(g(x)\leq d(2b+1)<U\). Its distance from the query is at most \(\delta/2\). This certifies the required near-membership; the oracle need not compute that point exactly.

All oracle calculations take polynomial time in the input length, query encoding length, and tolerance encoding length.

5. Polynomial-time weak optimization

Apply the rational ellipsoid weak separation-to-optimization theorem for a convex body with known inner and outer balls. It gives, for any rational \(\delta>0\), a rational point \((\bar x,\bar z)\) satisfying

\(\operatorname{dist}((\bar x,\bar z),K)\leq\delta\), \(\bar z\leq f_*+\delta,\)

where \(f_*=\min_{\theta\in\Omega_b}f(\theta)\). Its running time and query encoding lengths are polynomial in the dimension, the ball-data encoding lengths, and the accuracy encoding length. This is the standard [Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver weak separation/optimization theorem](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf).

For completeness, versions of weak optimization that compare only with interior points give the stated guarantee as well. If \(p\in K\) and \(0<\xi<r\), convexity and the inner-ball inclusion imply that

\(p_\xi=(1-\xi/r)p+(\xi/r)a_0\)

has a radius-\(\xi\) ball inside \(K\). Its last coordinate differs from that of \(p\) by at most \(2R\xi/r\). Choosing

\(\xi=\min\{r/2,\delta r/[2(r+2R)]\}\)

makes the sum of this loss and the weak optimization error at most \(\delta\). The encoding length of \(\xi\) is polynomial in those of \(r,R,\delta\). Thus the required formulation applies with polynomial overhead.

The body and oracle constructed above meet every hypothesis.

6. Exact rational feasibility and the objective guarantee

Let \(\varepsilon_0=2^{-q}\leq\varepsilon\), and run weak optimization with

\(\delta=\varepsilon_0/(16d^2).\)

Set \(\bar\theta=T(\bar x)\). By the distance guarantee and compactness of \(K\), there is some \((y,u)\in K\) with

\(\lVert(\bar x,\bar z)-(y,u)\rVert_2\leq\delta.\)

Write \(\theta_y=T(y)\in\Omega_b\). The definition of \(T\) gives

\(\lVert\bar\theta-\theta_y\rVert_\infty\leq d\delta,\)

so \(\lVert\bar\theta\rVert_\infty\leq b+d\delta\).

Return the rational vector

\(\widehat\theta=\dfrac{b}{b+d\delta}\bar\theta.\)

Its coordinates sum to zero, and every coordinate has absolute value at most \(b\). Therefore \(\widehat\theta\in\Omega_b\) exactly. Moreover,

\(\lVert\widehat\theta-\bar\theta\rVert_\infty\leq d\delta,\)

and hence

\(\lVert\widehat\theta-\theta_y\rVert_\infty\leq2d\delta.\)

Since \((y,u)\in K\) and \(|u-\bar z|\leq\delta\),

\(f(\theta_y)\leq u\leq\bar z+\delta\leq f_*+2\delta.\)

Using the Lipschitz bound from Section 1,

\(f(\widehat\theta)\leq f(\theta_y)+4Wd\delta\leq f_*+(2+4d^2)\delta<f_*+\varepsilon_0.\)

Negating this inequality proves

\(F(\widehat\theta)\geq\max_{\theta\in\Omega_b}F(\theta)-\varepsilon.\)

The oracle, ellipsoid computation, and final rational scaling all have polynomial bit complexity in \(d,\tau,L,q\). This completely establishes the polynomial-time result under the stated precision convention, independently of finite-sample estimation guarantees.
