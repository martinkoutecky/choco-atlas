The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(M\) goods, \(\tau\) buyer types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, XoS demand and valuation oracles, and rational piecewise-linear convex per-capita cost curves \(C_i\), can a polynomial-time algorithm in \(M,\tau,L\) compute item prices \(p_i\) and mass supply caps \(q_i\in[0,1]\) such that an on-the-fly posted-price mechanism achieves at least \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}/4\) for every measurable arrival order with type frequencies \(\mu\)? Each infinitesimal buyer chooses a utility-maximizing integral bundle from the available goods, and \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}\) maximizes \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)-\sum_i C_i(y_i)\), where \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(y_i=\sum_{t,S\ni i}x_{t,S}\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(I\) be a set of \(M\geq1\) goods. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) denotes support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a normalized, monotone XoS valuation \(v_t:2^I\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\), with \(v_t(\varnothing)=0\). Access consists of valuation and demand oracles, and an XoS oracle returning an additive supporting clause attaining \(v_t(S)\) on a queried bundle \(S\); demand queries may restrict the available goods.
Each explicitly encoded cost \(C_i:[0,1]\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\) is finite, continuous, nondecreasing, convex and piecewise linear with rational breakpoints and coefficients, and \(C_i(0)=0\). Costs and welfare are measured per unit of total population. Let \(L\) count explicit input bits and bound rational oracle-answer lengths; running time counts bit operations and oracle queries.
Define \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}=\max_{x\geq0}\{\sum_{t,S\subseteq I}x_{t,S}v_t(S)-\sum_i C_i(y_i):\sum_{S\subseteq I}x_{t,S}=\mu_t,\ y_i=\sum_{t,S\ni i}x_{t,S}\}\).
A static on-the-fly mechanism specifies rational prices \(p_i\geq0\) and caps \(q_i\in[0,1]\). An arrival order is a measurable map \(a:[0,1]\to\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) with \(\lambda(a^{-1}(t))=\mu_t\), where \(\lambda\) is Lebesgue measure.
For a measurable bundle choice \(B(s)\), put \(z_i(s)=\int_0^s\mathbf{1}_{\{i\in B(u)\}}\,du\). Almost everywhere, \(B(s)\) maximizes \(v_{a(s)}(S)-\sum_{i\in S}p_i\) over \(S\subseteq\{i:z_i(s)<q_i\}\); each buyer receives an integral bundle, including possibly the empty bundle, and ties may be resolved arbitrarily. Welfare is \(\int_0^1v_{a(s)}(B(s))\,ds-\sum_i C_i(z_i(1))\), charging only actual production.

Can an algorithm using \(\operatorname{poly}(M,\tau,L)\) time and oracle queries compute static prices and caps guaranteeing welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}/4\) for every measurable arrival order and every measurable utility-maximizing tie resolution, or can this polynomial-time approximation guarantee be ruled out?

For finite independent Bayesian XoS buyers and convex production costs, polynomial-time computation of static prices and supply caps guarantees one-fourth of expected optimal welfare for every arrival order, using demand and XoS oracles; exact distribution processing depends on the support of the product distribution ([Sekar, 2017](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0055.pdf)).
No result settling this precise atomless, fixed-frequency, every-order formulation was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored normalized, monotone, nonnegative valuations and the option to receive the empty bundle.
  • Interpreted XoS oracle access as access to a maximizing additive supporting clause, alongside valuation and demand access; allowed demand queries restricted to available goods.
  • Specified finite, continuous, nonnegative, nondecreasing convex costs on the mass domain [0,1], with zero cost at zero production; costs are per unit of total population.
  • Made benchmark allocation variables nonnegative and allowed each type's mass to split among integral bundles.
  • Formalized measurable arrival orders by Lebesgue time with the prescribed type frequencies, and depletion by cumulative purchased mass.
  • Required the guarantee for arbitrary measurable utility-maximizing tie resolutions, since no favorable tie-breaking rule is specified.
  • Specified nonnegative rational output prices and rational caps, and an oracle bit model in which L covers explicit input and rational answer lengths.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

Yes. Under the stated oracle and encoding assumptions, a polynomial-time algorithm computes rational static prices and caps guaranteeing welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}/2\), hence at least \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}/4\), for every measurable arrival order and every measurable utility-maximizing tie resolution.

The proof uses an optimal fractional allocation directly. Splitting a type’s mass between bundles is allowed by the benchmark and does not require giving any individual buyer a fractional bundle.

1. Computing an optimal fractional allocation

For each good \(i\), write its piecewise-linear convex cost as

\(\displaystyle C_i(y)=\max_{1\leq k\leq K_i}\{c_{ik}y+b_{ik}\},\qquad 0\leq y\leq1.\)

Here the affine functions are the extensions of its linear pieces. Convexity makes each extension a supporting line, so this representation is exact. Nondecreasing costs give \(c_{ik}\geq0\). All coefficients have polynomial encoding length, and \(K=\sum_iK_i\) is bounded by the explicit input size.

Introduce unrestricted epigraph variables \(w_i\). The welfare problem becomes the rational linear program

\(\displaystyle \max\left\{\sum_{t,S}v_t(S)x_{t,S}-\sum_iw_i\right\},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_Sx_{t,S}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t,\qquad x_{t,S}\geq0,\)

and

\(\displaystyle w_i\geq c_{ik}\sum_{t,S:\,i\in S}x_{t,S}+b_{ik}\quad\text{for every }i,k.\)

Every feasible allocation has total mass one, so its good quantities belong to \([0,1]\). Consequently, minimizing each epigraph variable produces exactly the prescribed production cost. This LP therefore has optimal value \(\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}\). It is feasible by assigning everybody the empty bundle, and its optimum is finite and attained.

Its dual has variables \(\alpha_t\), unrestricted, and \(\theta_{ik}\geq0\). Define

\(\displaystyle \pi_i=\sum_k c_{ik}\theta_{ik}.\)

The dual minimizes

\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_t\alpha_t-\sum_{i,k}b_{ik}\theta_{ik},\)

subject to

\(\displaystyle \sum_k\theta_{ik}=1\quad\text{for every }i,\)

and

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t+\sum_{i\in S}\pi_i\geq v_t(S)\quad\text{for every }t,S.\)

The equality constraints arise because each \(w_i\) is unrestricted.

Separation. First check the explicit constraints on \(\theta\). If they hold, every \(\pi_i\) is nonnegative. For each type \(t\), a demand query at prices \(\pi\) returns a bundle maximizing

\(\displaystyle v_t(S)-\sum_{i\in S}\pi_i.\)

A valuation query on that bundle determines whether the corresponding dual constraint is violated. Thus a strong separation call needs at most one demand query and one valuation query per type, together with polynomial-time rational arithmetic. Arbitrary oracle tie-breaking causes no difficulty: every returned maximizer has the same objective value.

We use the standard exact rational oracle-LP theorem with primal recovery: a feasible LP with polynomially many rows, bounded coefficient encoding lengths, and a bounded optimum admits polynomial-time exact solution with polynomial-size primal support when its dual has a strong separation oracle returning violated original constraints. Primal recovery retains polynomially many columns and solves the resulting explicit LP; this is the standard [ellipsoid and column-recovery construction](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~15850/hws/h4.pdf).

All hypotheses hold here. There are \(\tau+K\) primal rows, all coefficients have encoding length polynomial in \(M,\tau,L\), and the separation oracle above returns an actual bundle constraint. For completeness, the dual also has a bounded optimal representative: each \(\theta_i\) lies in a simplex, and, for fixed \(\theta\), one can choose

\(\displaystyle \alpha_t=\max_S\left\{v_t(S)-\sum_{i\in S}\pi_i\right\}\in[0,v_t(I)].\)

It follows that we can compute an exact optimal allocation \(x^*\), represented by polynomially many positive variables, in \(\operatorname{poly}(M,\tau,L)\) bit operations and oracle queries. Its rational coordinates have polynomial encoding length by the usual determinant bounds for basic LP solutions.

2. Supporting clauses and nonnegative contributions

For every pair \((t,S)\) with \(x^*_{t,S}>0\), query the XoS oracle for a supporting additive clause \(a_{t,S}\). Its defining properties are

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i\in T}a_{t,S}(i)\leq v_t(T)\quad\text{for every }T\subseteq I,\)

and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i\in S}a_{t,S}(i)=v_t(S).\)

Define benchmark quantities and additive value contributions by

\(\displaystyle q_i=\sum_{t,S:\,i\in S}x^*_{t,S},\qquad V_i=\sum_{t,S:\,i\in S}x^*_{t,S}a_{t,S}(i).\)

Then

\(\displaystyle \sum_iV_i=\sum_{t,S}x^*_{t,S}v_t(S),\qquad \mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}=\sum_i\bigl(V_i-C_i(q_i)\bigr).\)

We next prove the crucial itemwise inequality

\(\displaystyle V_i\geq C_i(q_i).\)

Delete good \(i\) from every benchmark bundle, merging masses when bundles coincide. This remains a feasible allocation with the same type masses. Quantities of other goods remain unchanged, while the quantity of \(i\) becomes zero. The supporting-clause inequality gives

\(\displaystyle v_t(S\setminus\{i\})\geq v_t(S)-a_{t,S}(i)\qquad(i\in S).\)

Thus total valuation decreases by at most \(V_i\), and production cost decreases by exactly \(C_i(q_i)\). The modified allocation has welfare at least

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}-V_i+C_i(q_i).\)

Optimality of \(x^*\) implies the claimed inequality.

Write

\(\displaystyle W_i=V_i-C_i(q_i)\geq0.\)

If \(q_i=0\), its definition implies \(V_i=0\), hence \(W_i=0\).

3. The static mechanism

Use the benchmark quantities \(q_i\) as caps. Set

\(\displaystyle p_i=\frac{V_i+C_i(q_i)}{2q_i}\quad\text{when }q_i>0,\)

and set \(p_i=0\) when \(q_i=0\).

These prices are nonnegative rationals of polynomial encoding length. For each positive cap,

\(\displaystyle p_iq_i-C_i(q_i)=\frac{W_i}{2},\qquad V_i-p_iq_i=\frac{W_i}{2}.\)

Furthermore, convexity and \(C_i(0)=0\) imply, for \(0\leq r\leq q_i\),

\(\displaystyle C_i(r)\leq\frac{r}{q_i}C_i(q_i).\)

Since \(V_i\geq C_i(q_i)\), we have \(p_i\geq C_i(q_i)/q_i\). Consequently,

\(\displaystyle p_ir-C_i(r)\geq0\qquad(0\leq r\leq q_i).\)

Thus every good produces nonnegative seller profit at every permitted final production quantity; selling its entire cap produces profit \(W_i/2\).

4. Welfare for every measurable execution

Fix any admissible measurable arrival map \(a\) and any measurable maximizing bundle choice \(B\). Let

\(\displaystyle r_i=z_i(1),\qquad u(s)=v_{a(s)}(B(s))-\sum_{i\in B(s)}p_i,\qquad U=\int_0^1u(s)\,ds.\)

First, actual production respects every cap: \(r_i\leq q_i\). Indeed, \(z_i\) is continuous and nondecreasing. If it reaches \(q_i\), feasibility makes its derivative zero almost everywhere thereafter, because the good is unavailable whenever \(z_i(s)\geq q_i\). It therefore cannot exceed the cap. This argument also covers \(q_i=0\).

Partition positive-cap goods into

\(\displaystyle E=\{i:q_i>0,\ r_i=q_i\},\qquad F=\{i:q_i>0,\ r_i<q_i\}.\)

Every good in \(F\) remains available throughout the execution, since

\(\displaystyle z_i(s)\leq r_i<q_i\quad\text{for every }s\in[0,1].\)

Buyer utility from goods remaining available

Consider almost any arrival \(s\) of type \(t\). For every benchmark bundle \(S\) of that type, \(S\cap F\) is available. Utility maximization and the supporting clause imply

\(\displaystyle u(s)\geq v_t(S\cap F)-\sum_{i\in S\cap F}p_i\geq\sum_{i\in S\cap F}\bigl(a_{t,S}(i)-p_i\bigr).\)

Average these inequalities with weights \(x^*_{t,S}/\mu_t\), which sum to one because \(\mu_t>0\). We obtain

\(\displaystyle u(s)\geq\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_Sx^*_{t,S}\sum_{i\in S\cap F}\bigl(a_{t,S}(i)-p_i\bigr).\)

Integrating over the type-\(t\) arrivals multiplies the right-hand side by exactly \(\mu_t\). Summing over types therefore yields

\(\displaystyle U\geq\sum_{i\in F}(V_i-p_iq_i)=\frac12\sum_{i\in F}W_i.\)

This averaging is solely a comparison of utilities against feasible integral bundles. It imposes no fractional-bundle requirement on buyers.

Seller profit from exhausted goods

For every good, the preceding convexity bound gives nonnegative profit. For \(i\in E\), the entire cap is sold, so its profit equals \(W_i/2\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \sum_i\bigl(p_ir_i-C_i(r_i)\bigr)\geq\frac12\sum_{i\in E}W_i.\)

Combining the bounds

By the definition of \(r_i\), integrated payments equal \(\sum_i p_ir_i\). Welfare therefore decomposes exactly as

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{Welfare}=U+\sum_i\bigl(p_ir_i-C_i(r_i)\bigr).\)

The utility and profit bounds give

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{Welfare}\geq\frac12\sum_{i\in F}W_i+\frac12\sum_{i\in E}W_i=\frac12\sum_iW_i=\frac{\mathrm{OPT}_{\mu}}2.\)

The penultimate equality uses \(W_i=0\) for zero-cap goods. Every inequality used only measurability, the prescribed type masses, feasibility, and utility maximization. Thus the bound holds for every arrival order and every measurable maximizing tie resolution.
