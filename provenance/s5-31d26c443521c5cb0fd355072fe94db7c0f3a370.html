The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of objects and facts, and a finite support of complete claim-pattern types \(t\) with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), define \(r_1(t)=1\), \(b_1(f)=\sum_{t:f\in A_t}\mu_t\), \(r_{k+1}(t)=\sum_{f\in A_t}b_k(f)\), and \(b_{k+1}(f)=\sum_{t:f\in A_t}\mu_t r_{k+1}(t)\). Compute the eventual total preorders of types by \(r_k\) and facts by \(b_k\), or determine whether those preorders fail to stabilize.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(O\) and \(F\) be finite nonempty sets of objects and facts, with an input map \(\operatorname{obj}:F\to O\); put \(m=|F|\). A type \(t\) specifies its entire claim set \(A_t\subseteq F\), containing at most one fact for each object; abstention is allowed, including \(A_t=\varnothing\), but at least one supported type makes a claim.
A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) distinct types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For \(k\ge1\), use the unnormalized updates \(r_1(t)=1\), \(b_1(f)=\sum_{t:f\in A_t}\mu_t\), \(r_{k+1}(t)=\sum_{f\in A_t}b_k(f)\), and \(b_{k+1}(f)=\sum_{t:f\in A_t}\mu_t r_{k+1}(t)\).
At iteration \(k\), rank supported types by \(r_k\) and all facts, including facts belonging to different objects, by \(b_k\); equal scores remain tied. Stabilization means that both total preorders are identical for every \(k\ge K\), for some finite \(K\).
The computational problem is to compute these eventual total preorders, or report that they fail to stabilize.

Can this problem be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|O|,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite lists of sources with unit contribution per claim, UnboundedSums always has eventually constant source and fact rankings; no computational complexity bound is supplied by this convergence result ([Singleton and Booth, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10458-022-09569-3)). No result settling the computational complexity of the exact rational-mass problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted 'complete claim-pattern' as a full specification of claims and abstentions, rather than requiring a claim for every object: types may claim at most one fact per object and may make no claims.
  • Included the fact-to-object map as input and required nonempty object and fact domains and at least one claim, following the discrete network domain.
  • Defined m as the number of facts and L as the total input bit length; included the number of objects explicitly in running-time parameters.
  • Specified that rankings compare all facts across objects, preserve exact ties, and stabilize only when both total preorders remain constant at every subsequent iteration.

Polynomial-time solvability

The problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial bit time. Moreover, every valid input stabilizes. Thus the requested function belongs to \(\mathrm{FP}\).

The proof computes eventual comparisons exactly, using rational functions and univariate real-root algorithms.

1. Integer matrix representation

Index the supported types and facts, and define the incidence matrix \(C\in\{0,1\}^{m\times\tau}\) by \(C_{ft}=1\) exactly when \(f\in A_t\).

Write each mass as \(\mu_t=p_t/q_t\), with positive integers \(p_t,q_t\). Set

\(\displaystyle Q=\prod_t q_t,\qquad a_t=Q\mu_t,\qquad a=(a_t)_t.\)

Then all \(a_t\) are positive integers and \(\sum_t a_t=Q\). The bit length of \(Q\) is at most the sum of the input denominator bit lengths, hence at most \(L\).

Define

\(\displaystyle A=C\operatorname{diag}(a)C^{\mathsf T},\qquad v=Ca.\)

Both are integral, and every entry of \(A\) and \(v\) lies between \(0\) and \(Q\). The update equations give

\(\displaystyle b_1=\frac{v}{Q},\qquad b_{k+1}=\frac{A}{Q}b_k,\qquad r_{k+1}=C^{\mathsf T}b_k.\)

Consequently, by induction,

\(\displaystyle b_k=Q^{-k}A^{k-1}v\quad(k\ge1),\)

and

\(\displaystyle r_k=Q^{-(k-1)}C^{\mathsf T}A^{k-2}v\quad(k\ge2).\)

For facts \(f,g\), put \(u=e_f-e_g\). For types \(s,t\), put \(u=C_{\cdot s}-C_{\cdot t}\). Their respective score differences are

\(\displaystyle b_k(f)-b_k(g)=Q^{-k}u^{\mathsf T}A^{k-1}v,\)

and

\(\displaystyle r_k(s)-r_k(t)=Q^{-(k-1)}u^{\mathsf T}A^{k-2}v.\)

Since these scaling factors are positive, every required eventual comparison reduces to finding the eventual sign of

\(\displaystyle S_u(n)=u^{\mathsf T}A^n v,\qquad n\ge0.\)

In both cases, \(u\in\{-1,0,1\}^m\).

2. Proof that stabilization always occurs

The matrix \(A\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite: for every \(x\in\mathbb R^m\),

\(\displaystyle x^{\mathsf T}Ax=\sum_t a_t\bigl(C_{\cdot t}^{\mathsf T}x\bigr)^2\ge0.\)

Moreover, positivity of every \(a_t\) implies

\(\displaystyle \ker A=\ker C^{\mathsf T}.\)

Indeed, if \(Ax=0\), the displayed sum is zero, so every \(C_{\cdot t}^{\mathsf T}x=0\); the converse follows directly from the definition of \(A\).

If \(x\in\ker A\), then

\(\displaystyle x^{\mathsf T}v=x^{\mathsf T}Ca=(C^{\mathsf T}x)^{\mathsf T}a=0.\)

Thus \(v\) has no component in the zero eigenspace of \(A\).

By the spectral theorem, write \(P_\lambda\) for the orthogonal projector onto the eigenspace of a distinct eigenvalue \(\lambda\). All eigenvalues are nonnegative, and the preceding observation gives

\(\displaystyle S_u(n)=\sum_{\lambda>0}c_\lambda\lambda^n,\qquad c_\lambda=u^{\mathsf T}P_\lambda v.\)

If every \(c_\lambda\) is zero, then \(S_u(n)=0\) for all \(n\ge0\).

Otherwise, let \(\rho\) be the largest eigenvalue with \(c_\rho\ne0\). Then

\(\displaystyle \frac{S_u(n)}{\rho^n} =c_\rho+\sum_{0<\lambda<\rho}c_\lambda\left(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\right)^n \longrightarrow c_\rho.\)

To make the sign implication explicit, the sum of the absolute values of the latter terms tends to zero. For all sufficiently large \(n\), it is smaller than \(|c_\rho|/2\). Therefore \(S_u(n)\) eventually has the strict sign of \(c_\rho\).

There are finitely many fact and type pairs. Taking the maximum of their eventual-sign thresholds yields one finite iteration after which every comparison is constant. Hence both total preorders stabilize.

It remains to compute those signs in polynomial time.

3. An exact eventual-sign algorithm

Fix a comparison vector \(u\). Define integer polynomials

\(\displaystyle p(z)=\det(zI-A),\qquad g_u(z)=u^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{adj}(zI-A)v.\)

Here \(p\) is monic of degree \(m\), and \(g_u\) has degree at most \(m-1\).

For \(z\) outside the spectrum of \(A\), the adjugate identity and spectral decomposition give

\(\displaystyle \frac{g_u(z)}{p(z)} =u^{\mathsf T}(zI-A)^{-1}v =\sum_{\lambda>0}\frac{c_\lambda}{z-\lambda}.\)

This equality is an identity of rational functions.

If \(g_u=0\), the rational function is zero. Its residue at each positive eigenvalue is therefore zero, so every \(c_\lambda=0\). The comparison is a permanent tie.

Now suppose \(g_u\ne0\). Compute the monic greatest common divisor over \(\mathbb Q[z]\),

\(\displaystyle d_u=\gcd(p,g_u),\qquad q_u=p/d_u,\qquad h_u=g_u/d_u.\)

Because \(p\) is monic and integral, its monic divisor \(d_u\) is integral by Gauss’s lemma. Division by a monic integer polynomial then shows that \(q_u,h_u\) are also integral.

The fraction \(h_u/q_u\) is reduced. Its poles are exactly those positive eigenvalues for which \(c_\lambda\ne0\): the displayed partial-fraction expansion has a simple pole at such an eigenvalue and no pole when its coefficient is zero. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle q_u(z)=\prod_{\lambda:\,c_\lambda\ne0}(z-\lambda).\)

In particular, \(q_u\) is square-free, has positive real roots only, and has positive degree.

Let \(\rho_u\) be its largest root. Taking the residue there gives

\(\displaystyle c_{\rho_u} =\lim_{z\to\rho_u}(z-\rho_u)\frac{h_u(z)}{q_u(z)} =\frac{h_u(\rho_u)}{q_u'(\rho_u)}.\)

Because \(q_u\) is monic with distinct real roots,

\(\displaystyle q_u'(\rho_u) =\prod_{\substack{q_u(\lambda)=0\\\lambda<\rho_u}}(\rho_u-\lambda)>0.\)

Also, \(h_u(\rho_u)\ne0\), because numerator and denominator are coprime. Therefore

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{sign}_{\mathrm{eventual}}S_u =\operatorname{sign}h_u(\rho_u).\)

This proves the following exact algorithm:

  • Compute \(g_u\).
  • If \(g_u=0\), return a tie.
  • Otherwise compute \(q_u,h_u\) by gcd cancellation.
  • Isolate the largest real root \(\rho_u\) of \(q_u\).
  • Compute the exact sign of \(h_u(\rho_u)\).

Cancellation is essential: when the contribution of a larger eigenvalue vanishes in a particular comparison, the algorithm automatically proceeds to the largest eigenvalue whose contribution survives.

4. Polynomial bit complexity

We check both computation costs and coefficient sizes.

Computing the initial polynomials

The rank-one determinant identity gives

\(\displaystyle g_u(z)=\det(zI-A+vu^{\mathsf T})-\det(zI-A).\)

Thus \(p\) and \(g_u\) can be computed using determinants and interpolation, without computing eigenvalues or eigenvectors.

For example, evaluate the two determinants at \(z=0,1,\ldots,m\), and interpolate their degree-at-most-\(m\) polynomials. Every evaluated matrix entry has absolute value at most \(m+2Q\). Integer determinant computation and exact rational interpolation take polynomial bit time; Hadamard’s bound ensures that the determinant values have polynomial bit length.

More explicitly, if an integer matrix \(B\) has entries of absolute value at most \(2Q\), the coefficient of \(z^{m-j}\) in \(\det(zI-B)\) is a signed sum of its principal minors of order \(j\). Its absolute value is at most

\(\displaystyle \binom mj j!(2Q)^j.\)

This applies to \(B=A\) and \(B=A-vu^{\mathsf T}\). Hence the coefficients of \(p\) and \(g_u\) have bit lengths

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(m\bigl(L+\log(m+1)\bigr)\right).\)

Gcd cancellation and reduced coefficient sizes

Exact univariate polynomial gcd computation over \(\mathbb Q\) takes polynomial bit time, for example by subresultant algorithms.

Every eigenvalue of \(A\) is at most \(mQ\). To see this directly, choose an eigenvector coordinate of maximum absolute value and apply the eigenvalue equation; each row contains \(m\) entries bounded by \(Q\).

Both \(d_u\) and \(q_u\) are monic divisors of \(p\), so their roots, with multiplicity, lie in \([0,mQ]\). Expanding their products of linear factors bounds each coefficient in absolute value by \((1+mQ)^m\). Their coefficient bit lengths are therefore

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(m\bigl(L+\log(m+1)\bigr)\right).\)

Finally, compute \(h_u=g_u/d_u\) by monic long division. There are at most \(m\) subtraction steps. If the coefficients of \(d_u\) are bounded by \(D\), each step increases the maximum absolute coefficient by at most a factor \(1+D\). Thus the coefficients of \(h_u\) have bit lengths at most

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(m^2\bigl(L+\log(m+1)\bigr)\right).\)

All reduced polynomials consequently have polynomial-sized representations.

Exact root and sign computation

We use the standard univariate algorithmic result: for integer polynomials of degree at most \(d\) and coefficient bit length at most \(H\), real-root isolation is deterministic polynomial bit time and produces rational isolating endpoints of polynomial bit length. The sign of another integer polynomial at a root specified by such an interval can also be determined in polynomial bit time. These results are established, for example, in Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 6.1 of [Emiris, Mourrain and Tsigaridas, *Real Algebraic Numbers: Complexity Analysis and Experimentation*](https://who.paris.inria.fr/Elias.Tsigaridas/files/emt-lncs-2006.pdf).

The hypotheses hold here: \(q_u,h_u\) are integer polynomials of degrees at most \(m\), with the coefficient bounds just proved. Root isolation identifies the largest root of \(q_u\), and exact sign evaluation determines \(h_u(\rho_u)\). Each comparison therefore costs polynomial bit time.

5. Constructing the two eventual total preorders

Run the comparison algorithm for every unordered fact pair and every unordered supported-type pair. There are

\(\displaystyle \binom m2+\binom\tau2\)

such comparisons.

Their results are consistent total preorders: by Section 2, there is a common sufficiently large iteration at which every returned comparison agrees with the actual scores. Consequently, ties form equivalence classes, and those classes can be ordered using the computed comparisons.

The construction includes unsupported facts, empty claim sets, and comparisons between facts belonging to different objects. The object constraint requires no additional algorithmic step.

The total running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,|O|,L\), and the algorithm always returns the eventual preorders. No nonstabilizing valid input exists.
