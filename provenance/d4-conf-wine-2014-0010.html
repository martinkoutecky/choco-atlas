The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type sets \(A,B\), rational masses \(\mu_a,\nu_b\) summing to \(1\) on each side, allowed pairs \(E\subseteq A\times B\), positive rational shared benefits \(w_{ab}\), and rational initial and target mass matchings \(x^0,x^\star\), decide whether a finite sequence of legal blocking steps takes \(x^0\) exactly to \(x^\star\). A mass matching satisfies \(\sum_b x_{ab}\leq\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\leq\nu_b\). A step selects an allowed pair \((a,b)\) with positive mass on both sides of agents who are unmatched or strictly prefer that pair to their current partner, then matches the same positive rational mass \(\delta\) from those pools, dissolving their prior matches. The target must be stable, meaning no allowed pair has positive willing mass on both sides.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A,B\) be disjoint finite type supports with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a,\nu_b\), summing to \(1\) on each side. A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass and their binary-rational masses. Set \(\tau=|A|+|B|\), the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(m=|E|\) and \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Allowed pairs are \(E\subseteq A\times B\), with shared benefits \(w_{ab}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\). Types rank partners by these benefits; equal benefits are ties, with no tie-breaking, and unmatched agents receive \(0\).
A mass matching is \(x\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}^{E}\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{ab}\leq\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\leq\nu_b\); entries outside \(E\) are zero. Write \(u_a=\mu_a-\sum_b x_{ab}\) and \(v_b=\nu_b-\sum_a x_{ab}\).
For \((a,b)\in E\), its willing pools consist of unmatched mass and mass currently assigned to strictly worse partners: their sizes are \(P_{ab}=u_a+\sum_{c:w_{ac}<w_{ab}}x_{ac}\) and \(Q_{ab}=v_b+\sum_{d:w_{db}<w_{ab}}x_{db}\), summing only over allowed pairs.
A legal step selects \((a,b)\in E\) and rational portions totaling the same \(\delta\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) from each willing pool. It dissolves exactly the selected portions of their previous matches, leaves their former partners unmatched, and increases \(x_{ab}\) by \(\delta\). Portions may be split arbitrarily; an already positive entry may be increased.
The input contains feasible \(x^0,x^\star\), with \(x^\star\) stable: for every \((a,b)\in E\), \(P_{ab}=0\) or \(Q_{ab}=0\).

Is deciding whether a finite sequence of legal steps takes \(x^0\) exactly to \(x^\star\) solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard? The sequence may have length zero.

Discrete prescribed-stable-matching reachability is NP-complete for ordinary bipartite matching with shared positive benefits and ties. [Hoefer–Wagner](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2073063/component/file_2073062/content)
For divisible stable allocations with strict preferences, a polynomial-time better-response construction reaches some stable allocation; this concerns an unspecified endpoint. [Cseh–Skutella](https://real.mtak.hu/83554/1/MTDP1820.pdf)
No result settling the exact mass reachability problem above was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made mass matchings explicitly nonnegative and supported only on allowed pairs, following the discrete matching domain.
  • Specified that shared benefits determine preferences, equal benefits remain ties without tie-breaking, and unmatched utility is zero.
  • Resolved pool selection by allowing arbitrary rational portions from eligible current-partner classes; only the selected portions of prior matches dissolve, and their former partners become unmatched.
  • Allowed increasing an already positive pair entry when additional willing mass exists, since the discrete exclusion concerns already matched individuals rather than an entire type pair.
  • Defined m as the number of allowed pairs, tau as the total support size on both sides, and L as total input bit length.
  • Allowed the empty sequence, so an initial state equal to the target is reachable.

Result

The problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). In fact, the same conclusion holds for any prescribed feasible rational target, without assuming that it is stable.

The proof gives an exact reduction to reachability in rational continuous Petri nets.

1. The reachability theorem used

A rational continuous Petri net has a finite place set \(\mathcal P\), a finite transition set \(T\), and nonnegative integer matrices \(\mathrm{Pre},\mathrm{Post}\). A marking is a vector \(h\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^{\mathcal P}\). Firing transition \(t\) by a positive rational amount \(\lambda\) is permitted when

\(\displaystyle h(p)\geq\lambda\,\mathrm{Pre}(p,t)\quad\text{for every }p\in\mathcal P,\)

and produces

\(\displaystyle h'=h+\lambda\bigl(\mathrm{Post}(\cdot,t)-\mathrm{Pre}(\cdot,t)\bigr).\)

We use the following known theorem:

Continuous reachability theorem. Given such a net and two rational markings encoded in binary, deciding whether a finite sequence of rational firings takes the first marking exactly to the second is polynomial-time solvable.

This is the rational reachability result of Fraca–Haddad, stated explicitly in Proposition 2.4 of [Blondin–Finkel–Haase–Haddad, *The Logical View on Continuous Petri Nets*](https://michaelblondin.com/papers/BFHH17.pdf). That paper uses binary encodings of integers and rationals and defines reachability through finite rational firing sequences.

We now verify that the matching dynamics have precisely this form.

2. Encoding allocations as markings

Create distinct places:

  • \(M_{ab}\) for every allowed pair \((a,b)\in E\);
  • \(U_a\) for every \(a\in A\);
  • \(V_b\) for every \(b\in B\).

Thus \(|\mathcal P|=m+\tau\).

For a feasible mass matching \(x\), define its marking \(h(x)\) by

\(\displaystyle h(x)(M_{ab})=x_{ab},\qquad h(x)(U_a)=u_a,\qquad h(x)(V_b)=v_b.\)

These markings satisfy the type-mass identities

\(\displaystyle h(U_a)+\sum_{b:(a,b)\in E}h(M_{ab})=\mu_a,\)

\(\displaystyle h(V_b)+\sum_{a:(a,b)\in E}h(M_{ab})=\nu_b.\)

Conversely, any nonnegative rational marking satisfying these identities determines a feasible mass matching by setting \(x_{ab}=h(M_{ab})\). The unmatched coordinates are then forced by the identities.

Consequently, this encoding is a bijection between feasible mass matchings and nonnegative rational markings satisfying the type-mass identities.

3. Encoding elementary legal steps

Fix an allowed pair \(e=(a,b)\). Define its possible source places on the two sides:

\(\displaystyle S_e^A=\{U_a\}\cup\{M_{ac}:(a,c)\in E,\ w_{ac}<w_{ab}\},\)

\(\displaystyle S_e^B=\{V_b\}\cup\{M_{db}:(d,b)\in E,\ w_{db}<w_{ab}\}.\)

Their total markings are exactly \(P_{ab}\) and \(Q_{ab}\).

For each \(s\in S_e^A\) and \(r\in S_e^B\), introduce a transition \(t_{e,s,r}\). Its inputs are one unit from \(s\) and one unit from \(r\). Its outputs are:

  • one unit at \(M_{ab}\);
  • additionally, one unit at \(V_c\) if \(s=M_{ac}\);
  • additionally, one unit at \(U_d\) if \(r=M_{db}\).

All unspecified matrix entries are zero.

The two input places are distinct. Indeed, unmatched places have distinct labels, and equality \(M_{ac}=M_{db}\) would imply \(d=a\) and \(c=b\), contradicting the strict benefit inequalities defining the source sets. Also, \(c\neq b\) and \(d\neq a\). Thus all arc coefficients belong to \(\{0,1\}\).

Firing this transition by amount \(\lambda\) takes precisely \(\lambda\) mass from one willing source on each side, dissolves any selected previous matches, leaves their former partners unmatched, and adds \(\lambda\) to \(x_{ab}\).

Every transition preserves the type-mass identities. For type \(a\), it removes \(\lambda\) either from \(U_a\) or from an incident matching place and adds \(\lambda\) to \(M_{ab}\). The same reasoning applies to \(b\). If a former partner \(c\) exists, its loss of \(\lambda\) at \(M_{ac}\) is offset by a gain of \(\lambda\) at \(V_c\); the analogous statement holds for \(d\). Every other type is unchanged.

It follows that all markings reachable from \(h(x^0)\) encode feasible mass matchings. Every positive rational firing is a legal matching step. In particular, the construction permits increasing an already positive entry, and equal-benefit partners never appear among its sources.

4. Every legal step decomposes into firings

We must also show that a legal step using several source places can be simulated.

Consider a legal step forming \(e=(a,b)\) with total amount \(\delta\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\). Let \(\alpha_s\) be the amount selected from each \(s\in S_e^A\), and let \(\beta_r\) be the amount selected from each \(r\in S_e^B\). Legality gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_s\alpha_s=\sum_r\beta_r=\delta,\qquad 0\leq\alpha_s\leq h(s),\qquad 0\leq\beta_r\leq h(r).\)

Define rational amounts

\(\displaystyle \lambda_{s,r}=\frac{\alpha_s\beta_r}{\delta}.\)

Their row and column sums are

\(\displaystyle \sum_r\lambda_{s,r}=\alpha_s,\qquad \sum_s\lambda_{s,r}=\beta_r.\)

Fire each transition \(t_{e,s,r}\) for which \(\lambda_{s,r}>0\), by that amount, in any order.

To verify enabling, observe that none of these transitions produces mass at any source place in \(S_e^A\cup S_e^B\). Their matching output is \(M_{ab}\), which is excluded from both source sets. Any unmatched output is \(V_c\) with \(c\neq b\), or \(U_d\) with \(d\neq a\).

For any fixed source place, its total consumption over the whole sequence is therefore its selected amount, which is at most its initial marking. Immediately before any particular firing, its remaining marking is at least the sum of its remaining scheduled consumptions, hence at least the amount required by that firing. Every firing is enabled.

The sequence consumes exactly \(\alpha_s\) and \(\beta_r\) from the respective sources. It creates

\(\displaystyle \sum_{s,r}\lambda_{s,r}=\delta\)

at \(M_{ab}\), and releases exactly the selected portions to their former partners’ unmatched places. Its final marking is therefore precisely the marking produced by the original legal step.

This decomposition is finite and rational.

Combining both directions proves the exact equivalence:

\(\displaystyle x^\star\text{ is reachable from }x^0\text{ by finitely many legal steps} \iff h(x^\star)\text{ is reachable from }h(x^0)\text{ by finitely many rational firings}. \)

The empty sequence is allowed on both sides.

5. Polynomial size and the decision algorithm

For each \(e=(a,b)\),

\(\displaystyle |S_e^A|\leq |B|+1,\qquad |S_e^B|\leq |A|+1.\)

Hence the number of transitions is at most

\(\displaystyle |T|=\sum_{e\in E}|S_e^A||S_e^B| \leq m(|B|+1)(|A|+1) =O(m\tau^2). \)

Each transition has two input arcs and at most three output arcs, all with coefficient one. The net therefore has polynomial encoding length.

Its construction requires only comparisons of input rational benefits and enumeration of source pairs. The markings \(h(x^0)\) and \(h(x^\star)\) require rational sums and differences of input quantities. These computations have polynomial bit complexity: denominators can be combined using products whose bit lengths are bounded by the sum of the operand bit lengths.

The algorithm is therefore:

  • Construct the net above.
  • Compute \(h(x^0)\) and \(h(x^\star)\).
  • Apply the polynomial-time rational continuous reachability algorithm.

All hypotheses of the cited theorem hold: the net is finite, its incidence matrices contain nonnegative integers, and both markings are nonnegative binary-encoded rational vectors. Section 4 proves that its answer is exactly the answer to the specified finite-sequence matching problem.

The construction uses only the listed support types. It never expands a mass into individual agents. Target stability is unnecessary for the reduction.
