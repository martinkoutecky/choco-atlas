The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite binary feature set \(F\), a support of \(\tau\) distinct acyclic binary CP-net types \(N_t\) over \(F\), and positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, decide whether some outcome \(\alpha \in \{0,1\}^F\) is not max-dominated by any other outcome. For outcomes \(\beta,\alpha\), define \(P^+_\mu(\beta,\alpha)\), \(P^-_\mu(\beta,\alpha)\), and \(P^?_\mu(\beta,\alpha)\) as the masses of types that respectively prefer \(\beta\), prefer \(\alpha\), or find them incomparable; max-dominance holds when \(P^+_\mu(\beta,\alpha) > \max(P^-_\mu(\beta,\alpha),P^?_\mu(\beta,\alpha))\). Use the paper's polynomial-connectivity restriction, with indegree at most three for the hardness subcase.

Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each CP-net has explicitly listed conditional preference tables specifying a strict order on \(\{0,1\}\) for every parent assignment; no common topological order is required. Write \(\beta\succ_t\alpha\) when a nonempty sequence of improving flips leads from \(\alpha\) to \(\beta\). Distinct outcomes are incomparable when neither dominates the other.
The nets belong to a polynomially connected class: one fixed polynomial \(p\), independent of the instance, bounds the number of directed paths between any two features of every \(N_t\) by \(p(\|N_t\|)\), where \(\|N_t\|\) is its encoding length. Include the subcase in which every net has indegree at most three.
For distinct \(\beta,\alpha\), define \(P^+_\mu(\beta,\alpha)=\sum_{t:\beta\succ_t\alpha}\mu_t\), \(P^-_\mu(\beta,\alpha)=\sum_{t:\alpha\succ_t\beta}\mu_t\), and \(P^?_\mu(\beta,\alpha)=\sum_{t:\beta\not\succ_t\alpha,\,\alpha\not\succ_t\beta}\mu_t\). Outcome \(\beta\) max-dominates \(\alpha\) iff \(P^+_\mu(\beta,\alpha)>\max\{P^-_\mu(\beta,\alpha),P^?_\mu(\beta,\alpha)\}\); ties give no dominance. A max-optimal outcome has no distinct max-dominator.
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Result

The problem is \(\Sigma_3^P\)-complete, hence \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when every net has indegree at most three, the support size satisfies \(\tau\leq 8\), and every mass is a positive multiple of \(1/8\). Thus a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

The hardness proof transfers the given discrete theorem to distinct rational-mass support. The upper bound handles the masses directly, without expanding them into agent copies.

1. The discrete theorem used

The published reduction establishes \(\Sigma_3^P\)-hardness of max-optimal-outcome existence for lists of exactly eight equally weighted agents. Its nets are acyclic and binary, have explicitly represented preference tables and indegree at most three, and belong to a fixed polynomially connected family. These are precisely the structural hypotheses needed here. See [Lemma 6.5 and Theorem 6.6 of the full treatment](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/5b7f4550-209d-4418-8357-05127a8b0322/aij2202-ComplexityMCP-NetsMaxRank.pdf).

We use this established theorem; the transfer to rational-mass support is proved next.

2. Merging repeated agents preserves every comparison

Let \(M=(N_1,\ldots,N_8)\) be an instance produced by that reduction. Group identical CP-nets together. Denote the distinct nets by \(T_1,\ldots,T_\tau\), and let \(k_t\) be the number of occurrences of \(T_t\). Set

\(\displaystyle \mu_t=\frac{k_t}{8}.\)

Then \(\tau\leq 8\), every mass is positive, and \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). These masses are dyadic rationals, so the construction also satisfies the interpretation of “binary-rational” as requiring finite binary expansions.

Fix distinct outcomes \(\beta,\alpha\). Define the discrete vote counts

\(\displaystyle c^+(\beta,\alpha)=|\{i:\beta\succ_{N_i}\alpha\}|,\qquad c^-(\beta,\alpha)=|\{i:\alpha\succ_{N_i}\beta\}|,\)

and let \(c^?(\beta,\alpha)\) count agents for whom the outcomes are incomparable.

All occurrences of the same net give the same comparison. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle P_\mu^+(\beta,\alpha)=\frac{c^+(\beta,\alpha)}8,\qquad P_\mu^-(\beta,\alpha)=\frac{c^-(\beta,\alpha)}8,\qquad P_\mu^?(\beta,\alpha)=\frac{c^?(\beta,\alpha)}8.\)

Multiplication by the positive number \(8\) therefore gives

\(\displaystyle P_\mu^+>\max\{P_\mu^-,P_\mu^?\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c^+>\max\{c^-,c^?\}.\)

This equivalence preserves strict inequalities and ties. Hence the discrete instance and the constructed society have exactly the same max-dominance relation, and therefore exactly the same max-optimal outcomes.

The transformation is polynomial-time: canonicalize the explicitly listed representations, compare the eight nets, and record their multiplicities. Equality here is equality of the CP-net objects—their graphs and tables—not equivalence testing of their induced dominance relations.

No graph or preference table is changed. Thus acyclicity, binary domains, indegree at most three, and the fixed polynomial connectivity bound are preserved. This proves \(\Sigma_3^P\)-hardness for the rational-mass formulation.

3. Polynomial certificates for individual dominance

We prove the certificate bound needed for membership.

Fix a net \(N\) and any finite improving-flip sequence. For each feature \(v\), let \(f_v\) count its flips in that sequence.

Between two consecutive flips of \(v\), at least one parent of \(v\) must flip. Indeed, with its parent assignment fixed, a binary feature has one strictly preferred value. After improving to that value, it cannot improve again until its parent assignment changes. Therefore

\(\displaystyle f_v\leq 1+\sum_{u\in\operatorname{Pa}(v)}f_u.\)

Because the dependency graph is acyclic, define recursively, in topological order,

\(\displaystyle h_v=1+\sum_{u\in\operatorname{Pa}(v)}h_u.\)

Induction gives \(f_v\leq h_v\). Moreover, \(h_v\) counts all directed paths ending at \(v\), including the length-zero path at \(v\): every nonempty such path has a unique final edge.

The connectivity hypothesis consequently implies

\(\displaystyle h_v\leq 1+(m-1)p(\|N\|).\)

Thus every improving sequence has length at most

\(\displaystyle \sum_v f_v\leq m+m(m-1)p(\|N\|).\)

Choose a fixed nondecreasing polynomial \(q\) dominating \(p\). Since \(\|N\|\leq L\), a uniform certificate-length bound is

\(\displaystyle B=m+m(m-1)q(L).\)

A certificate specifies the flipped features. Starting from the claimed initial outcome, each flip can be checked against the explicit preference table, and the final outcome can be checked against the claimed target. Verification takes \(O((B+1)(L+m))\) time by straightforward table scanning. Therefore individual dominance belongs to \(\mathrm{NP}\) on the promised class.

The same bound also rules out cycles in the improving-outcome graph: repeating a cycle would produce finite improving sequences of arbitrarily large length. Thus, for distinct outcomes, the two dominance directions cannot both hold.

4. Rational weights retain the upper bound

For fixed distinct outcomes \(\beta,\alpha\), ask an \(\mathrm{NP}\) oracle the two dominance questions for each support net. This uses \(2\tau\) nonadaptive oracle calls. Their answers determine all three comparison categories exactly.

The mass calculations require only polynomial time. To see this explicitly, write \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), and set

\(\displaystyle D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}b_t,\qquad w_t=a_t(D/b_t).\)

The total encoded length of the numerators and denominators is at most \(L\), so these integers have \(O(L)\) bits. Comparing the three masses is equivalent to comparing the corresponding sums of integer weights \(w_t\). Computing the weights and comparisons takes \(O(\tau L^2)\) time using elementary integer arithmetic.

Hence pairwise max-dominance is in \(\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{NP}}\), with \(2\tau\) oracle calls and polynomial additional time in \(m,\tau,L\).

An outcome \(\alpha\) fails to be max-optimal exactly when some distinct outcome \(\beta\) max-dominates it. Guessing \(\beta\) and performing the preceding oracle computation puts non-optimality in \(\Sigma_2^P\). Consequently, checking max-optimality belongs to \(\Pi_2^P\).

Finally, guess an outcome \(\alpha\), represented by \(m\) bits, and check its max-optimality with a \(\Pi_2^P\) oracle. This proves that existence belongs to \(\Sigma_3^P\). Together with Section 2, it establishes the claimed completeness.

5. A deterministic running-time bound

A direct exhaustive algorithm also gives a running time explicitly in \(m,\tau,L\).

For each support net, construct its improving-outcome graph, which has \(2^m\) vertices and at most \(m2^m\) edges. Run graph reachability from every outcome to obtain all individual dominance comparisons. Then inspect every ordered pair of distinct outcomes, calculate its weighted max-dominance status, and mark its dominated outcome. A max-optimal outcome exists exactly when some outcome remains unmarked.

With elementary arithmetic and straightforward graph representations, a conservative bound is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(\tau(m+1)^2\,4^m\,L^2\right).\)

For \(m=0\), there is just one outcome, which is automatically max-optimal. This degenerate case does not affect the hardness result.
