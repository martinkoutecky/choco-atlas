The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a finite set \(T\) of complete rankings of \(C\), a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\), integers \(\tau\) and \(k\), and a rational threshold \(\eta\), decide whether there exists an ordered sequence \(W_1,\ldots,W_\tau\) of \(k\)-candidate committees with \(f=1\) such that the committees are pairwise disjoint and \(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\max_{c\in W_i}(m-\operatorname{pos}_t(c))\ge\eta\).

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The distributional decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete, already when \(k=3\). For every fixed \(k\ge 3\), it is deterministically \(\mathrm{FPT}\) parameterized by \(r\), with running time

\[ O^\star\!\left(2.851^{(k-0.5501)r}\right), \]

where the suppressed factors are polynomial in the distributional input length \(L\), with \(k\) fixed. No expansion into an explicit electorate is required.

Here, binary-encoded rationals mean numerator–denominator representations. The hardness conclusion also holds if only dyadic fractions are permitted, as proved below.

1. Feasibility and exact arithmetic

If \(rk>m\), return no: disjoint committees would require \(rk\) distinct candidates. Henceforth assume \(rk\le m\). In particular, a feasible outcome has a polynomial-length description, even though \(r\) is binary encoded.

Write \(\mu_t=a_t/d_t\) and \(\eta=a_0/d_0\), with positive denominators. Define

\[ D=d_0\prod_{t\in T}d_t,\qquad b_t=a_t(D/d_t),\qquad H=a_0(D/d_0). \]

These are integers, and normalization gives \(\sum_t b_t=D\). For each committee \(W\), define its integer weight

\[ w(W)=\sum_{t\in T}b_t\max_{c\in W}\bigl(m-\operatorname{pos}_t(c)\bigr). \]

Because \(D>0\), the original threshold condition is equivalent to

\[ \sum_{i=1}^{r}w(W_i)\ge H. \]

The bit length of \(D\) is at most the sum of the denominator bit lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Moreover,

\[ 0\le w(W)\le D(m-1),\qquad 0\le\sum_{i=1}^{r}w(W_i)\le Dr(m-1). \]

Thus all weights and accumulated scores have \(O(L+\log(rm+2))\) bits. Clearing denominators can create large *values*, but only polynomial-length integers.

A verifier checks committee sizes and disjointness, computes their integer weights, and compares their sum with \(H\). This proves membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

For example, setting \(B=L+\lceil\log_2(rm+2)\rceil+2\), schoolbook arithmetic gives a conservative verification bound of \(O((L+m\tau+r\tau k)B^2)\).

2. Hardness by normalizing an explicit electorate

Use the known \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness of explicit-electorate utilitarian Borda–Chamberlin–Courant successive committee election with \(k=3\) and \(f=1\). Its score and frequency restriction agree with those here; the additional consecutiveness condition in that model is automatic when \(f=1\). [Bredereck–Kaczmarczyk–Niedermeier, Theorem 6](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/5552/5552-13-8777-1-10-20200512.pdf)

Let an explicit instance have \(n>0\) voters and integer score threshold \(P\). Group identical rankings. If ranking \(t\) occurs \(n_t>0\) times, set

\[ \mu_t=\frac{n_t}{n},\qquad \eta=\frac{P}{n}. \]

The resulting support contains distinct strict complete rankings, its masses are positive, and their sum is one.

For every feasible outcome \(\mathcal W=(W_1,\ldots,W_r)\), its distributional score is exactly its explicit-electorate score divided by \(n\):

\[ \sum_{i,t}\mu_t\max_{c\in W_i}(m-\operatorname{pos}_t(c)) = \frac1n\sum_{i,t}n_t\max_{c\in W_i}(m-\operatorname{pos}_t(c)). \]

Consequently, the new score reaches \(P/n\) exactly when the old score reaches \(P\). Grouping rankings and encoding these rational numbers takes polynomial time in the explicit input length. This proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness at \(k=3\), and therefore \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.

3. Hardness also holds for dyadic masses and thresholds

If “binary-rational” is intended to mean a terminating binary fraction, the preceding normalization may produce inadmissible denominators. The integer gap in the source problem repairs this.

Assume the source instance is feasible; infeasible instances can be mapped to a fixed no-instance. Put \(M=r(m-1)\), and choose a power of two \(Q\) satisfying

\[ Q>16n\tau(M+1). \]

For every support type, initially set \(x_t=\lfloor Qn_t/n\rfloor\). Let \(\delta=Q-\sum_t x_t\), and add \(\delta\) to one \(x_t\). Define \(\widehat\mu_t=x_t/Q\). Since \(Q>n\), every \(x_t\) is positive. The masses sum to one, and

\[ \sum_t\left|\widehat\mu_t-\frac{n_t}{n}\right| \le \frac{2\tau}{Q}. \]

Each type contributes a total score between zero and \(M\). Hence every outcome’s new score differs from its old normalized score by at most

\[ \varepsilon=\frac{2\tau M}{Q}<\frac1{8n}. \]

Choose the dyadic threshold

\[ \widehat\eta= \frac{\left\lceil Q(P-\tfrac12)/n\right\rceil}{Q}. \]

It satisfies

\[ \frac{P-\tfrac12}{n}\le\widehat\eta < \frac{P-\tfrac12}{n}+\frac1Q. \]

An outcome with integer source score at least \(P\) has new score at least \(P/n-\varepsilon>\widehat\eta\). An outcome with source score at most \(P-1\) has new score at most \((P-1)/n+\varepsilon<\widehat\eta\). Thus the reduction preserves yes- and no-instances.

Finally, \(\log Q=O(\log n+\log\tau+\log(M+1))\), so this is also a polynomial-time reduction.

4. Exact reduction to weighted set packing

Let

\[ \mathcal F=\{W\subseteq C:|W|=k\},\qquad N=|\mathcal F|=\binom{m}{k}. \]

Construct a weighted set-packing instance with universe \(C\), family \(\mathcal F\), weights \(w(W)\), requested packing size \(r\), and threshold \(H\).

The equivalence is exact:

  • A feasible outcome gives \(r\) pairwise disjoint members of \(\mathcal F\) with the same integer total weight.
  • Conversely, any such packing can be ordered arbitrarily to give a feasible outcome. Committee order does not affect the objective.

Every weight is computable directly from the \(\tau\) support rankings. After building the position table, constructing all weights takes \(O(N\tau kB^2)\) bit operations under schoolbook arithmetic. For fixed \(k\), \(N\le m^k\) is polynomial in the input size.

5. FPT algorithm and running time

The weighted set-packing theorem of Goyal, Misra, Panolan, and Zehavi provides a deterministic algorithm for packing \(p\) disjoint sets from a \(d\)-uniform family. Its running time is

\[ O\!\left( 2.851^{(d-0.5501)p} |\mathcal F|\,|U|\log^2|U|\,\log W_{\max} \right), \]

where \(W_{\max}\) is the maximum integer weight. The dependence on weight magnitude is logarithmic. [Goyal–Misra–Panolan–Zehavi](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/140981290)

Its hypotheses hold here: the family is explicitly listed and \(k\)-uniform, the requested number of sets is \(r\), and weights are binary-encoded integers. They are positive: every size-\(k\) committee has Borda–CC score at least \(k-1\) under every ranking. Also, \(\log W_{\max}=O(B)\).

Applying the theorem with \(d=k\), \(p=r\), and \(|U|=m\) therefore gives the claimed FPT bound. A conservative bound including construction and polynomial overhead for exact arithmetic is

\[ O\!\left( \left[ L+m\tau+N\tau k+ 2.851^{(k-0.5501)r}Nm\log^2(m+1) \right]B^2 \right). \]

This also verifies the transfer of the cited successive-election algorithm: its proof reduces to precisely this weighted set-packing problem. [Efficient Algorithms for Electing Successive Committees, Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18287v1#A2)

For every fixed \(k\), the displayed bound is a function of \(r\) times a polynomial in \(L\). Here \(\tau\) always denotes the supplied support size; neither all possible rankings nor an electorate of size proportional to \(D\) is enumerated.
