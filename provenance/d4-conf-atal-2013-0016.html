The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support of paired rankings \(h=(r_0,r_1)\) with rational masses \(\eta_h\), where the initial and final marginal societies both have unique Borda winner \(a\), does there exist a piecewise-linear evolution of mass in which each history’s mass moves only along adjacent-swap paths of length \(\kappa(r_0,r_1)\), and \(a\) remains a Borda co-winner in the aggregate society at every point?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Given a candidate set \(C\) of size \(m\geq 1\) and \(a\in C\), a society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq 1\) distinct types \(h=(r_0,r_1)\), each consisting of two strict complete rankings and a positive binary rational mass \(\eta_h\), with \(\sum_h\eta_h=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Let \(\kappa(r,s)\) be the minimum number of adjacent swaps transforming \(r\) into \(s\). For each history \(h\), retain rankings \(r\) satisfying \(\kappa(r_0,r)+\kappa(r,r_1)=\kappa(r_0,r_1)\); permit a directed edge \(r\to s\) precisely when one adjacent swap produces \(s\) and \(\kappa(s,r_1)=\kappa(r,r_1)-1\).
An evolution consists of nonnegative continuous piecewise-linear masses \(x_{h,r}(t)\), for \(t\in[0,1]\), initially concentrated at \(r_0\) and finally at \(r_1\), with total mass \(\eta_h\) for every history. On each of finitely many time intervals, constant nonnegative transfer rates along permitted edges give each coordinate's derivative as inflow minus outflow. Mass may split and transfers may occur simultaneously; history labels remain fixed.
The aggregate mass at ranking \(r\) is \(\sum_h x_{h,r}(t)\); use mass-weighted Borda scores, allowing all candidates tied for the maximum to win. Both endpoint societies are promised to have unique winner \(a\). Call an evolution feasible if \(a\) is a Borda co-winner at every time.

Is deciding whether a feasible evolution exists solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For explicitly listed, individually paired voters, deciding whether a shortest adjacent-swap sequence preserves the common endpoint winner as a co-winner is NP-complete for Borda. [Source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2013/docs/p383.pdf)
No result settling this exact divisible-mass problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive rational history masses to total mass one, interpreting the society as a distribution.
  • Made rankings strict complete orders and defined κ as adjacent-swap distance, following the discrete model.
  • Specified the undefined mass evolution through continuous piecewise-linear coordinates and nonnegative directed transfer rates, allowing splitting, waiting, and simultaneous transfers.
  • Preserved each history's identity and mass throughout, and restricted its transfers to directed edges lying on shortest paths between its paired endpoints.
  • Specified mass-weighted Borda aggregation and retained all score ties as co-winners, without tie-breaking.
  • Used the time interval [0,1] with finitely many pieces and no speed bound; neither durations nor transfer rates are additional inputs.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable: every instance satisfying the endpoint promise admits a feasible evolution. In fact, \(a\) can remain the unique Borda winner throughout.

The stated equations allow simultaneous through-flow at rankings containing zero mass, which gives a direct construction. To address the more restrictive interpretation of transfers as moving mass already present, I also prove feasibility using only one edge at a time.

1. Permitted shortest paths

For each history \(h\), choose a shortest adjacent-swap path

\(r_{h,0}=r_0,\ r_{h,1},\ldots,r_{h,D_h}=r_1,\qquad D_h=\kappa(r_0,r_1).\)

Such a path exists because adjacent swaps can transform any complete ranking into any other.

Every prefix and suffix of a shortest path is shortest: replacing either by a shorter path would shorten the whole path. Consequently, for every \(j\),

\(\kappa(r_0,r_{h,j})=j,\qquad \kappa(r_{h,j},r_1)=D_h-j.\)

Thus every path vertex satisfies the required geodesic equality, and every path edge decreases distance to \(r_1\) by one. All these vertices and edges are permitted. Shortest paths have no repeated vertices.

2. Direct construction under the stated equations

Define, for every history and ranking,

\(x_{h,r}(t)=\eta_h\bigl((1-t)\mathbf 1_{\{r=r_0\}}+t\mathbf 1_{\{r=r_1\}}\bigr).\)

For a history with \(D_h>0\), assign constant transfer rate \(\eta_h\) to every edge of its chosen path. Assign zero rate to all other edges.

The resulting derivatives are exactly those of the displayed coordinates:

  • At \(r_0\), outflow minus inflow is \(\eta_h\).
  • At \(r_1\), inflow minus outflow is \(\eta_h\).
  • At every internal path vertex, inflow and outflow both equal \(\eta_h\), so the derivative is zero.

When \(D_h=0\), keep the history stationary. These coordinates are nonnegative, conserve each history’s mass, and satisfy both endpoint conditions. One time interval suffices.

Let \(S_c^i\) denote candidate \(c\)’s Borda score at endpoint \(i\). Linearity of mass-weighted Borda scores gives

\(S_a(t)-S_c(t)=(1-t)(S_a^0-S_c^0)+t(S_a^1-S_c^1).\)

For every \(c\ne a\), both endpoint differences are positive by the promise, so their convex combination is positive. Hence \(a\) remains the unique winner.

This construction uses positive through-flow at internal vertices whose mass is zero. That satisfies the specified derivative equations. The next argument shows that this feature is unnecessary under the endpoint promise.

3. A construction using one edge at a time

Assume \(m\geq 2\); when \(m=1\), the sole candidate wins throughout any permitted evolution.

Write \(b_c(r)\) for candidate \(c\)’s Borda score in ranking \(r\), using scores from \(m-1\) down to zero. For each competitor \(c\ne a\), define

\(g_c(r)=b_a(r)-b_c(r).\)

Then

\(-(m-1)\leq g_c(r)\leq m-1.\)

Define the minimum endpoint winning margin

\(\delta=\min_{c\ne a}\min\{S_a^0-S_c^0,\ S_a^1-S_c^1\}.\)

There are finitely many competitors, and every endpoint margin is positive. Therefore \(\delta>0\). Choose an integer \(N\) satisfying

\(N>2(m-1)/\delta.\)

Conceptually divide the mass of every history into \(N\) packets, each of mass \(\eta_h/N\). These packets are only bookkeeping; they do not change the history labels.

Perform \(N\) rounds. During each round, take one packet from each history and transport it all the way along that history’s chosen shortest path. Process histories sequentially, and move each packet across one edge at a time. A packet is transferred completely across one edge before its next transfer begins.

Thus every transfer draws on mass already present at its source.

Finite piecewise-linear realization

Let \(K=\sum_h D_h\). If \(K=0\), all histories are stationary.

Otherwise, the construction has \(NK\) edge transfers. Give each transfer a time interval of length \(1/(NK)\). When moving a packet belonging to history \(h\), assign rate

\((\eta_h/N)/(1/(NK))=\eta_h K\)

to its current edge, and zero rate to every other edge.

During that interval, the packet’s mass decreases linearly at the source and increases linearly at the destination. Its remaining source mass stays nonnegative. Consequently, all coordinates are continuous, nonnegative, and piecewise linear, with constant nonnegative permitted transfer rates on finitely many intervals.

After every round, exactly one further packet of each history has reached its destination. After all \(N\) rounds, every history is concentrated at \(r_1\).

4. Winner preservation during every transfer

Fix a time during round \(k+1\), where \(k\) rounds have been completed and \(0\leq k<N\).

For each history \(h\), let \(p_{h,r}(t)\) describe the normalized distribution of its packet for the current round. This distribution is concentrated:

  • at \(r_0\), if that history has not yet been processed;
  • on the current edge’s two endpoints, during a transfer;
  • at \(r_1\), if that history has already been processed.

In particular, \(p_{h,r}(t)\geq0\) and \(\sum_r p_{h,r}(t)=1\). The history’s actual coordinates are

\(x_{h,r}(t)=\eta_h\left(\frac{N-k-1}{N}\mathbf 1_{\{r=r_0\}}+\frac{k}{N}\mathbf 1_{\{r=r_1\}}+\frac1N p_{h,r}(t)\right).\)

Compare this with the society at the beginning of the round,

\(y_{h,r}^{k}=\eta_h\left(\frac{N-k}{N}\mathbf 1_{\{r=r_0\}}+\frac{k}{N}\mathbf 1_{\{r=r_1\}}\right).\)

This reference society is the endpoint mixture with weights \(1-k/N\) and \(k/N\). Hence its winning margin against every competitor is at least \(\delta\).

For a fixed competitor \(c\), the change in its margin relative to this reference is

\(\displaystyle \frac1N\sum_h\eta_h\left(\sum_r p_{h,r}(t)g_c(r)-g_c(r_0)\right).\)

Both \(\sum_r p_{h,r}(t)g_c(r)\) and \(g_c(r_0)\) belong to the interval from \(-(m-1)\) to \(m-1\). Their difference is therefore at least \(-2(m-1)\). Using \(\sum_h\eta_h=1\), the actual winning margin satisfies

\(S_a(t)-S_c(t)\geq\delta-\frac{2(m-1)}N>0.\)

This holds for every competitor and every time. Therefore the sequential construction also preserves \(a\) as the unique winner throughout.

5. Complexity and the discrete hardness result

On the promised input domain, the decision algorithm simply returns YES. If input validity and the endpoint promise must also be checked, rankings and rational masses can be checked and endpoint scores computed using polynomial-time rational arithmetic in \(m,\tau,L\).

The sequential construction may require exponentially many intervals when the endpoint margin is small. This does not affect the decision result: the definition requires finitely many intervals, without a polynomial bound. Moreover, the exact equations admit the one-interval construction in Section 2.

The cited NP-completeness theorem concerns shortest paths through discrete voter profiles. Divisibility permits arbitrarily small packets, whose temporary score effects fit inside the positive endpoint margin; the discrete theorem therefore does not extend to this model. [Obraztsova et al., Theorem 4.7](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2013/docs/p383.pdf)
