The anchor (named result in the paper)

Proposition 3 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Input \(m\) item classes with rational per-person supplies \(q_j\), common prices \(p_j\), a finite set \(T\) of types with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and additive valuations \(v_{tj}\). For each type, let \(\theta_t\) be the largest \(z\ge 0\) satisfying \(z=\sum_j q_j\max\{p_j,\min\{v_{tj},z\}\}\). Choose sold quantities \(s_j\ge 0\) and a finite-support mass assignment \(x_t(a,P)\ge 0\) to packages with integral goods vectors \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^m\) and cash \(P\ge 0\), such that \(\sum_{a,P}x_t(a,P)=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,a,P}x_t(a,P)a_j+s_j=q_j\), \(\sum_{t,a,P}x_t(a,P)P=\sum_jp_js_j\), and every positive-mass package for type \(t\) gives \(v_t\cdot a+P\ge\theta_t/2\). Is there an algorithm to find such an allocation in time polynomial in \(m\), \(|T|\), and the binary input length \(L\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) item classes, with per-person supplies \(q_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\), common prices \(p_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\), and additive per-item valuations \(v_{tj}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length. Cash has unit utility for every type; only sales finance cash allocations, and proceeds may be distributed freely.
Define the truncated proportional share \(\theta_t\) as the largest \(z\geq0\) satisfying \(z=\sum_{j=1}^m q_j\max\{p_j,\min\{v_{tj},z\}\}\).
An allocation consists of rational sold quantities \(s_j\geq0\) and finite-support rational masses \(x_t(a,P)\geq0\) assigned to packages \((a,P)\), where \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0}^m\) and \(P\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq0}\). Individuals receive integral item counts; masses of individuals and sold quantities are divisible.
Require \(\sum_{a,P}x_t(a,P)=\mu_t\) for every type, \(\sum_{t,a,P}x_t(a,P)a_j+s_j=q_j\) for every class, and \(\sum_{t,a,P}x_t(a,P)P=\sum_jp_js_j\).
The required guarantee is \(v_t\cdot a+P\geq\theta_t/2\) for every package with \(x_t(a,P)>0\).

Is finding such an allocation solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies of \(n\) agents with individually listed goods, an allocation guaranteeing each agent \(\frac{n}{2n-1}\) of her truncated proportional share is computable in polynomial time. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24600v1)
No result settling this precise mass-assignment problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Supplies, prices, and valuations are nonnegative; valuations and all numerical inputs are binary rationals, with no normalization of valuations or prices.
  • The society support is nonempty and there is at least one item class; zero supplies, prices, valuations, and share thresholds remain permitted.
  • Finite allocations use rational package cash amounts, assignment masses, and sold quantities, providing an exact finite computational representation.
  • Cash has common unit utility and may be distributed arbitrarily, but must come entirely from selling; the stated equalities require full allocation of goods and proceeds.

Result

Finding the required allocation is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Moreover, there is a polynomial-time construction using at most \(m+\tau\) positive package records.

The [cited finite-society result](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24600v1) establishes its guarantee for individually listed agents and goods. The proof below works directly with rational masses and supplies.

1. Computing the truncated proportional shares

Fix a type \(t\), and define
\[ f_t(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_j\max\{p_j,\min\{v_{tj},z\}\}, \qquad U_t=\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_j\max\{p_j,v_{tj}\}. \]

The function \(f_t\) is continuous and satisfies \(0\leq f_t(z)\leq U_t\). Thus
\[ f_t(0)-0\geq0, \qquad f_t(U_t)-U_t\leq0. \]
Consequently, it has a fixed point in \([0,U_t]\). Every nonnegative fixed point belongs to this interval, so the closed set of fixed points has a largest element, namely \(\theta_t\).

We can compute \(\theta_t\) exactly in polynomial time. If \(v_{tj}\leq p_j\), the corresponding summand is constant. Otherwise, its expression changes only at \(p_j\) and \(v_{tj}\). Sort these rational breakpoints within \([0,U_t]\), including both endpoints. On each resulting interval, write
\[ f_t(z)=A+Bz. \]
If \(B\neq1\), test whether \(A/(1-B)\) belongs to the interval. If \(B=1\), the whole interval consists of fixed points exactly when \(A=0\). Also test the breakpoints themselves, and take the largest fixed point found.

All coefficients, roots, and breakpoints have polynomial binary length: they involve sums and products of input rationals, followed by at most one division. Hence all \(\theta_t\) are computable in polynomial time.

Set
\[ h_t=\frac{\theta_t}{2}, \qquad c_{tj}=\max\{p_j,\min\{v_{tj},\theta_t\}\}. \]
By definition,
\[ \sum_jq_jc_{tj}=\theta_t. \]

Types with \(\theta_t=0\) immediately receive the empty package \((0,0)\) on their entire mass. They require no resources. Below, every active type has \(\theta_t>0\).

2. A resource invariant

Let \(\rho_t\) be the unserved mass of type \(t\), initially \(\mu_t\) for types with positive share. Let \(Q_j\) be the remaining quantity of class \(j\), initially \(q_j\). Write
\[ D=\sum_{t:\rho_t>0}\rho_t, \qquad \Phi_t=\sum_jQ_jc_{tj}. \]

We maintain, for every active type,
\[ \Phi_t\geq D\theta_t. \tag{1} \]

Initially, \(\Phi_t=\theta_t\) and \(D\leq1\), so (1) holds.

Suppose a batch serves mass \(w>0\) and consumes quantities \(\delta_j\), counting both allocated and sold items. If
\[ \sum_j\delta_jc_{tj}\leq w\theta_t \tag{2} \]
for every type active before the batch, then each type remaining active satisfies
\[ \Phi_t^{\mathrm{new}} \geq D\theta_t-w\theta_t =(D-w)\theta_t. \]
Thus (2) preserves the invariant.

In particular, if any type remains active, then its \(\Phi_t\) is positive. Therefore some remaining class has both positive quantity and positive \(c_{tj}\).

Throughout the construction, each sale immediately finances exactly the cash assigned in its batch.

3. The construction

Phase A: cash from sufficiently expensive classes

While active types remain, choose an active type \(r\) minimizing \(\theta_r\).

If some class \(j\) has \(Q_j>0\) and \(p_j\geq h_r\), define
\[ w=\min\left\{\rho_r,\frac{Q_jp_j}{h_r}\right\}, \qquad \delta=\frac{wh_r}{p_j}. \]
These expressions are well-defined because \(h_r>0\) and \(p_j\geq h_r\).

Sell quantity \(\delta\) of class \(j\), and give the cash-only package \((0,h_r)\) to mass \(w\) of type \(r\). Reduce \(\rho_r\) by \(w\) and \(Q_j\) by \(\delta\).

The package meets the required guarantee, and its cash is financed exactly:
\[ \delta p_j=wh_r. \]

To check (2), take any active type \(t\). Minimality of \(\theta_r\) gives \(h_r\leq\theta_t/2\), and
\[ c_{tj}\leq\max\{p_j,\theta_t\}. \]
Therefore
\[ \frac{\delta c_{tj}}{w} =\frac{h_rc_{tj}}{p_j} \leq \max\left\{h_r,\frac{h_r\theta_t}{p_j}\right\} \leq\theta_t. \]
The last inequality uses \(h_r\leq\theta_t/2\) and \(h_r\leq p_j\). Thus the invariant is preserved.

Each batch exhausts either type \(r\) or class \(j\). Continue until no such class exists, or no type remains active.

If active types remain when this phase ends, then, for every remaining class and active type,
\[ p_j<h_t. \tag{3} \]
Indeed, the stopping condition gives \(p_j<h_r\) for a type with minimum share, and \(h_r\leq h_t\). Property (3) persists because types and classes only leave the active sets.

Phase B: single-item packages

While there are an active type \(r\) and a class \(j\) with
\[ Q_j>0, \qquad v_{rj}\geq h_r, \]
set
\[ w=\min\{\rho_r,Q_j\}. \]
Give one item of class \(j\), with no cash, to mass \(w\) of type \(r\). Reduce \(\rho_r\) and \(Q_j\) by \(w\).

This package has utility at least \(h_r\). For every active type \(t\), property (3) implies
\[ c_{tj} =\max\{p_j,\min\{v_{tj},\theta_t\}\} \leq\theta_t. \]
Thus the batch consumes at most \(w\theta_t\) in type \(t\)'s potential and preserves (1).

Again, each batch exhausts a type or class.

When this phase ends, every remaining class and active type satisfy
\[ p_j<h_t, \qquad v_{tj}<h_t. \tag{4} \]

Phase C: packages consisting of repeated copies of one class

For the remaining types and classes, define
\[ b_{tj}=\max\{p_j,v_{tj}\}. \]
By (4),
\[ c_{tj}=b_{tj}<h_t. \tag{5} \]

Assume \(D>0\). Choose any active type \(t\). The invariant gives
\[ \sum_jQ_jb_{tj} =\Phi_t \geq D\theta_t >0. \]
Hence we can choose a class \(j\) with \(Q_j>0\) and \(b_{tj}>0\) for at least one active type.

For that class, compute
\[ k= \min_{\substack{u:\rho_u>0\\b_{uj}>0}} \left\lceil\frac{h_u}{b_{uj}}\right\rceil, \]
and choose an active type \(r\) attaining this minimum. Then \(k\) is a positive integer and
\[ kb_{rj}\geq h_r. \tag{6} \]

For every active type \(t\) with \(b_{tj}>0\),
\[ k\leq\left\lceil\frac{h_t}{b_{tj}}\right\rceil, \]
so
\[ (k-1)b_{tj}<h_t. \]
Together with (5), this gives
\[ kb_{tj}<h_t+b_{tj}<2h_t=\theta_t. \tag{7} \]
If \(b_{tj}=0\), inequality (7) also holds because \(\theta_t>0\).

Set
\[ w=\min\left\{\rho_r,\frac{Q_j}{k}\right\}. \]
Consume quantity \(wk\) of class \(j\), serving mass \(w\) of type \(r\), as follows:

  • If \(v_{rj}\geq p_j\), give each person \(k\) items of class \(j\), with no cash.
  • If \(v_{rj}<p_j\), sell quantity \(wk\) and give each person cash \(kp_j\), with no items.

In either case, each assigned package has utility \(kb_{rj}\geq h_r\), by (6). In the sale case, the assigned cash equals the proceeds exactly.

For every active type \(t\), the potential decrease is
\[ wk c_{tj}=wk b_{tj}\leq w\theta_t, \]
by (7). Therefore the invariant is preserved.

Each batch exhausts either type \(r\) or class \(j\). Repeat while any type remains active.

Termination

Every batch in all three phases exhausts at least one previously unexhausted type or class. Exhaustion is permanent. Consequently, there are at most \(m+\tau_+\) batches, where \(\tau_+\) is the number of positive-share types.

Phase C always has a valid next batch whenever \(D>0\), as proved using the invariant. It therefore terminates with every type fully served.

Completing the accounting

Sell all quantities \(Q_j\) remaining after the three phases. Their total proceeds are
\[ R=\sum_jp_jQ_j. \]
Choose any existing positive package record with mass \(x_*>0\), and increase its per-person cash by \(R/x_*\). Such a record exists because the total assigned population mass is \(1\).

This adjustment preserves its utility guarantee and distributes exactly the additional proceeds.

All quantities consumed earlier were either allocated or sold. All remaining quantities are now sold. Hence
\[ \sum_{t,a,P}x_t(a,P)a_j+s_j=q_j \]
for every class. Every type's mass is fully assigned, and every sale's proceeds are distributed exactly. Thus
\[ \sum_{a,P}x_t(a,P)=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t,a,P}x_t(a,P)P=\sum_jp_js_j. \]
Every positive package record satisfies
\[ v_t\cdot a+P\geq h_t=\frac{\theta_t}{2}. \]

4. Binary running time and output size

The construction has polynomially many batches. Including one empty-package record for each zero-share type, the number of positive records is at most
\[ (m+\tau_+)+(\tau-\tau_+)=m+\tau. \]
Identical records can be merged.

It remains to check that large item counts and rational arithmetic do not cause an encoding problem.

All thresholds and fixed coefficients have polynomial binary length. In Phase C, each candidate integer
\[ \left\lceil\frac{h_t}{b_{tj}}\right\rceil \]
also has polynomial binary length, because it is obtained from a ratio of two positive rationals of polynomial binary length. The construction computes this integer directly; it does not iterate through its \(k\) copies.

For completeness, consider the evolving quantities. Every batch updates one type and one class in the form
\[ w=\min\{\rho_r,Q_j/\beta\}, \qquad \rho_r\leftarrow\rho_r-w, \qquad Q_j\leftarrow Q_j-\beta w, \]
where \(\beta\) is respectively \(h_r/p_j\), \(1\), or \(k\).

There is a polynomial bound \(B\) on the binary lengths of the numerator and denominator of every possible \(\beta\). Suppose the denominators of all current \(\rho_t,Q_j\) divide a common integer \(H\), and write \(\beta=u/v\) with positive integers \(u,v\). The updated denominators divide \(Huv\):

  • If \(w=\rho_r\), the new class quantity has denominator dividing \(Hv\).
  • If \(w=Q_j/\beta\), the new type mass has denominator dividing \(Hu\).

Initially, a suitable \(H\) is the product of the input denominators of the masses and supplies, which has binary length \(O(L)\). After \(N\leq m+\tau\) batches, a common denominator has binary length at most
\[ O(L+NB). \]
Furthermore, \(0\leq\rho_t\leq1\) and \(0\leq Q_j\leq q_j\), so their numerators also have polynomial binary length.

Batch masses, accumulated sold quantities, package cash values, and the final adjustment \(R/x_*\) consequently have polynomial binary length. Each batch requires only polynomially many comparisons and rational arithmetic operations.

This proves polynomial running time in \(m,\tau,L\), with a polynomial-size rational output satisfying every required constraint.
