The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given rational type masses \(\mu_j\) summing to \(1\), values \(v_j>0\), a corruption fraction \(\theta\), and a selection fraction \(\lambda\), find an optimal selection rate \(q_j\in[0,1]\) for each type, with \(\sum_j\mu_jq_j=\lambda\), maximizing \(\min_{0\le b_j\le\mu_j,\ \sum_j b_j\le\theta}\sum_j(\mu_j-b_j)v_jq_j\). Here \(b_j\) is corrupted mass chosen before selection randomness, and \(q_j\) is each member's marginal selection probability; return an optimal \(q\) and its per-capita expected payoff.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_j\), where \(\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j=1\). Here \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types.
Each type has a positive binary-rational value \(v_j\); the corruption and selection fractions are binary rationals \(0<\theta<1\) and \(0<\lambda<1\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; there is no separate alternative-set parameter \(m\).
The organizer commits to marginal selection probabilities \(q_j\in[0,1]\), identical for all members of type \(j\), satisfying \(\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jq_j=\lambda\).
An adversary observes these probabilities before selection randomness and chooses divisible corrupted masses \(b_j\in[0,\mu_j]\) with \(\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}b_j\leq\theta\). Corrupted members contribute zero; honest selected members contribute \(v_j\), additively.
Find an optimal \(q\) and its worst-case expected payoff per unit of total society mass, \(F(q):=\min_{0\leq b_j\leq\mu_j,\ \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}b_j\leq\theta}\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}(\mu_j-b_j)v_jq_j\).

Is this exact optimization problem solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finitely many agents with positive values and integers \(1\leq t,\ell<n\), optimal randomized selection of exactly \(\ell\) agents against an oblivious adversary corrupting at most \(t\) agents admits linear-time sampling and an explicit optimal distribution in \(O(n^2)\) time, under the arithmetic-operation model. Result

The exact optimization problem is solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\). An optimal rational vector \(q\) and its exact worst-case payoff can be obtained from a linear program with \(2\tau+1\) variables and \(O(\tau)\) constraints.

The proof applies directly to the listed types and divisible masses.

1. An exact formula for the adversary’s loss

Fix any feasible \(q\). Define \(w_j:=v_jq_j\), and let

\[ C(q):=\max_{\substack{0\leq b_j\leq\mu_j\\ \sum_jb_j\leq\theta}}\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}b_jw_j. \]

Thus \(F(q)=\sum_j\mu_jw_j-C(q)\). Write \(V:=\max_jv_j\), so \(0\leq w_j\leq V\).

Lemma. The corruption loss satisfies

\[ C(q)= \min_{\substack{0\leq a\leq V,\;0\leq z_j\leq V\\ a+z_j\geq w_j\;\text{for every }j}} \left(\theta a+\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jz_j\right). \]

Proof. First take any feasible \(b\) and any feasible pair \((a,z)\). Because all their coordinates are nonnegative,

\[ \sum_jb_jw_j \leq a\sum_jb_j+\sum_jb_jz_j \leq\theta a+\sum_j\mu_jz_j. \]

Consequently, every feasible auxiliary objective is an upper bound on \(C(q)\).

To prove equality, choose a permutation \(\pi\) with \(w_{\pi(1)}\geq\cdots\geq w_{\pi(\tau)}\). Set \(S_0:=0\) and \(S_i:=\sum_{h=1}^{i}\mu_{\pi(h)}\). Since \(S_\tau=1>\theta\), there is a smallest index \(k\) such that \(S_k\geq\theta\). It satisfies \(S_{k-1}<\theta\leq S_k\).

Define a corruption strategy by

  • \(b_{\pi(i)}:=\mu_{\pi(i)}\) for \(i<k\);
  • \(b_{\pi(k)}:=\theta-S_{k-1}\);
  • \(b_{\pi(i)}:=0\) for \(i>k\).

These assignments respect every capacity bound and have total mass exactly \(\theta\). Their loss is

\[ G:=\sum_{i<k}\mu_{\pi(i)}w_{\pi(i)} +(\theta-S_{k-1})w_{\pi(k)}. \]

Now choose \(a:=w_{\pi(k)}\) and \(z_j:=\max\{w_j-a,0\}\). These satisfy the auxiliary constraints, including \(a,z_j\leq V\). By the ordering,

\[ \theta a+\sum_j\mu_jz_j =\theta a+\sum_{i<k}\mu_{\pi(i)}(w_{\pi(i)}-a) =G. \]

The first inequality in the proof gives \(C(q)\leq G\), while the constructed corruption strategy gives \(C(q)\geq G\). Hence equality holds throughout, proving the lemma. Ties cause no difficulty because their contributions \(w_j-a\) vanish. ∎

2. A polynomial-size linear program

Consider the following linear program in \(q_1,\ldots,q_\tau,a,z_1,\ldots,z_\tau\):

\[ \text{maximize}\qquad H(q,a,z):= \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jv_jq_j-\theta a-\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jz_j, \]

subject to

\[ \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_jq_j=\lambda, \qquad 0\leq q_j\leq1\quad\text{for every }j, \]

\[ a+z_j\geq v_jq_j\quad\text{for every }j, \qquad 0\leq a\leq V, \qquad 0\leq z_j\leq V\quad\text{for every }j. \]

Proposition. If \((q^*,a^*,z^*)\) is an optimal solution of this linear program and \(W\) is its objective value, then \(q^*\) is optimal for the original problem and \(F(q^*)=W\).

Proof. For each feasible \(q\), the lemma gives

\[ F(q)=\max_{\substack{0\leq a\leq V,\;0\leq z_j\leq V\\ a+z_j\geq v_jq_j}} H(q,a,z). \]

In particular, every feasible LP point satisfies \(H(q,a,z)\leq F(q)\). Conversely, every feasible \(q\) has an auxiliary extension attaining \(H(q,a,z)=F(q)\).

The LP is nonempty: take \(q_j=\lambda\), \(a=V\), and \(z_j=0\). Its feasible region is closed and bounded, so its continuous linear objective attains a maximum \(W\).

Every feasible \(q\) has an extension with objective \(F(q)\), and therefore \(F(q)\leq W\). At an optimal LP point,

\[ W=H(q^*,a^*,z^*)\leq F(q^*)\leq W. \]

Thus \(F(q^*)=W\), and no feasible \(q\) has larger payoff. ∎

This establishes exact equivalence without exchanging the organizer’s and adversary’s optimization order.

3. Exact computation and bit complexity

We use the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem:

Given a linear program with explicitly encoded rational coefficients, one can determine infeasibility or unboundedness, or return an exact rational optimal solution and optimal value, in time polynomial in its binary encoding length.

Exact polynomial-time LP algorithms and their distinction from approximate optimization are discussed in [Gleixner and Steffy, Section 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.12820v1#S1.SS3).

Here every hypothesis is satisfied:

  • Polynomial dimensions. The LP has \(2\tau+1\) variables, \(5\tau+2\) inequalities, and one equality.
  • Rational coefficients. Its coefficients are input rationals, constants, \(V\), and products \(\mu_jv_j\). Computing \(V\) requires rational comparisons. Multiplication adds the numerator and denominator bit lengths, so each product has \(O(L)\) bits.
  • Polynomial encoding length. Even a dense encoding has length \(O(\tau^2(L+1))\), and it can be constructed in polynomial time.
  • Existence of a finite optimum. This follows from the nonempty, compact feasible region proved above.

Therefore the exact LP theorem returns \(q^*\) and \(W=F(q^*)\) in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

For completeness, exact output has polynomial length as well. A linear objective on a nonempty polytope has an optimal vertex. Such a vertex is determined by \(2\tau+1\) linearly independent active constraints. Clearing denominators yields an integer linear system whose coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in the LP encoding length. Cramer’s rule expresses each coordinate as a ratio of determinants; the determinant expansion bounds their bit lengths polynomially. The objective value consequently also has polynomial bit length. The returned probabilities are rational; they need not have finite binary expansions, and the problem imposes no such restriction on \(q\).

4. The resulting algorithm

Construct and solve the LP above using an exact polynomial-time rational LP algorithm. Return its \(q\)-coordinates and objective value.

If a worst-case corruption strategy is also desired, sort the values \(v_jq_j^*\) and apply the construction in Section 1. It attains the returned payoff exactly and requires \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) rational comparisons and polynomial-time rational arithmetic.

No expansion into individual agents or common-denominator population is needed. The argument settles the stated bit-complexity question; it does not assert the arithmetic-operation bounds of the finite-agent result.
