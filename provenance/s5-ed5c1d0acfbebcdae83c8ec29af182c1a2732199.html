The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given platforms \(P\), supported types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), eligibility sets, rankings, disjoint group labels, type-level top-\(k\) bounds \(L_{t,k},U_{t,k}\), and per-capita platform and platform-group quota bounds, compute a mass matrix \(x_{t,p}\) maximizing \(\sum_{t,p}x_{t,p}\), subject to \(\sum_p x_{t,p}\leq\mu_t\), \(L_{t,k}\mu_t\leq\sum_{p\in R_{t,k}}x_{t,p}\leq U_{t,k}\mu_t\), and the quota bounds. Give an induced randomized assignment in which each person receives at most one platform, every realization meets the quotas, and each type’s top-\(k\) assignment probabilities meet the stated bounds.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Type \(t\) consists of an atomless interval \([0,\mu_t)\) with Lebesgue measure.
There are \(m\geq1\) platforms \(P\). Each type has an eligibility set \(E_t\subseteq P\), a strict ranking of \(P\), and exactly one group label \(g(t)\in\{1,\ldots,\chi\}\); the groups partition the society. Let \(R_{t,k}\) contain its top \(k\) platforms, for \(1\leq k\leq m\).
The input gives binary rational probability bounds \(0\leq L_{t,k}\leq U_{t,k}\leq1\) and quota bounds \(0\leq\ell_p\leq u_p\leq1\), \(0\leq\ell_{p,h}\leq u_{p,h}\leq1\). Quotas measure assigned mass as a fraction of the whole society, including platform-group quotas.
Find a matrix \(x\) maximizing \(\sum_{t,p}x_{t,p}\), subject to \(x_{t,p}\geq0\), \(x_{t,p}=0\) for \(p\notin E_t\), \(\sum_p x_{t,p}\leq\mu_t\), and \(L_{t,k}\mu_t\leq\sum_{p\in R_{t,k}}x_{t,p}\leq U_{t,k}\mu_t\).
Also require \(\ell_p\leq\sum_t x_{t,p}\leq u_p\) and \(\ell_{p,h}\leq\sum_{t:g(t)=h}x_{t,p}\leq u_{p,h}\) for every platform and group, or report infeasibility.
Together with an optimum, compute a finite description of a jointly measurable randomized assignment of people to eligible platforms or an unmatched outcome. Each person receives at most one platform; every realization satisfies all platform and platform-group mass quotas; each person of type \(t\) receives platform \(p\) with probability \(x_{t,p}/\mu_t\).
Is this exact optimization and randomized implementation problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,\chi\) and the total input bit length \(L\).
For finite societies with disjoint groups and integer quotas, an exact polynomial-time algorithm computes an optimal distribution satisfying individual probability bounds and all quotas in every realization, or reports infeasibility ([Panda, Louis and Nimbhorkar](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.09951v4)).
No result settling this precise atomless, support-encoded problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total society mass to one and represented each supported type by an atomless interval, resolving the unspecified population measure.
  • Interpreted all per-capita quotas as fractions of total society mass, rather than fractions of a group or of the assigned population.
  • Specified binary rational, nonnegative, ordered quota bounds and probability bounds in the unit interval.
  • Used strict rankings of all platforms, with eligibility enforced separately; top-k constraints range over every k from one through m.
  • Required exactly one group label per type, so disjoint groups cover the society.
  • Made randomized implementation mean a finitely described jointly measurable assignment with identical platform marginals for every person of a type, and quotas satisfied in every realization.

Exact polynomial-time optimization and implementation

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. The ranking-prefix constraints and the disjoint-group quotas admit a bounded-flow formulation. Moreover, every feasible matrix has a finitely described, jointly measurable randomized implementation that preserves its type–platform masses in every realization.

Let

\(S=\tau m+m\chi+m+1.\)

An explicit algorithm uses \(O(S^3)\) arithmetic operations. With standard binary integer arithmetic, a conservative bound for optimization and construction of the implementation is

\(O\!\left(S^3(L+\log S)+SL^2\right)\)

bit operations. The implementation description has \(O(\tau mL)\) bits.

1. A network encoding all constraints

Write the ranking of type \(t\) as

\(p_{t,1}\succ p_{t,2}\succ\cdots\succ p_{t,m}.\)

For a matrix \(x\), define its prefix masses by

\(F_{t,k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{t,p_{t,j}},\qquad F_{t,0}=0.\)

Construct a directed network with source \(s\), sink \(z\), and the following additional vertices:

  • A vertex \(v_{t,k}\) for every type \(t\) and rank \(k\).
  • A vertex \(w_{p,h}\) for every platform \(p\) and group \(h\).
  • A vertex \(w_p\) for every platform \(p\).

The edges and their lower and upper bounds are as follows.

| Edge | Lower bound | Upper bound |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(s\to v_{t,m}\) | \(\mu_tL_{t,m}\) | \(\mu_tU_{t,m}\) |
| \(v_{t,k+1}\to v_{t,k}\), for \(1\leq k<m\) | \(\mu_tL_{t,k}\) | \(\mu_tU_{t,k}\) |
| \(v_{t,k}\to w_{p_{t,k},g(t)}\), when \(p_{t,k}\in E_t\) | \(0\) | \(\mu_t\) |
| \(w_{p,h}\to w_p\) | \(\ell_{p,h}\) | \(u_{p,h}\) |
| \(w_p\to z\) | \(\ell_p\) | \(u_p\) |

There is no assignment edge for an ineligible platform.

A feasible bounded flow satisfies the edge bounds and flow conservation at every vertex other than \(s,z\). Its value is the total flow leaving \(s\).

From a feasible matrix to a bounded flow

Given a feasible matrix \(x\), put:

  • Flow \(F_{t,m}\) on \(s\to v_{t,m}\).
  • Flow \(F_{t,k}\) on \(v_{t,k+1}\to v_{t,k}\).
  • Flow \(x_{t,p_{t,k}}\) on each eligible assignment edge.
  • Flow \(\sum_{t:g(t)=h}x_{t,p}\) on \(w_{p,h}\to w_p\).
  • Flow \(\sum_t x_{t,p}\) on \(w_p\to z\).

At a rank vertex, conservation follows from

\(F_{t,k}=x_{t,p_{t,k}}+F_{t,k-1}.\)

If the platform is ineligible, its matrix entry is zero and the corresponding assignment edge is absent. Conservation at the group and platform vertices follows directly from the definitions.

The prefix bounds give the bounds on the chain edges. Each assignment-edge flow is at most \(\mu_t\), because it is nonnegative and at most the type’s total assigned mass. The remaining bounds are precisely the quota constraints.

The flow value is

\(\sum_tF_{t,m}=\sum_{t,p}x_{t,p}.\)

From a bounded flow to a feasible matrix

Conversely, define \(x_{t,p_{t,k}}\) as the flow on the corresponding assignment edge, or as zero if that edge is absent.

Starting at \(v_{t,1}\) and proceeding upward through the chain, conservation shows that the flow entering \(v_{t,k}\) is exactly

\(\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{t,p_{t,j}}.\)

Thus the chain-edge bounds enforce every ranking-prefix constraint. At the top of the chain,

\(\sum_p x_{t,p}\leq\mu_tU_{t,m}\leq\mu_t,\)

so the type-mass constraint also holds.

Conservation at \(w_{p,h}\) makes its outgoing flow equal to \(\sum_{t:g(t)=h}x_{t,p}\). Conservation at \(w_p\) similarly makes its outgoing flow equal to \(\sum_t x_{t,p}\). Their edge bounds therefore enforce exactly the platform-group and platform quotas.

This proves an objective-preserving correspondence between feasible matrices and feasible bounded flows. It requires no additional monotonicity assumptions on the supplied prefix bounds.

2. Exact feasibility and optimization

The network has

\(n=\tau m+m\chi+m+2\)

vertices and

\(e=\tau m+\sum_t|E_t|+m\chi+m=O(S)\)

edges.

Feasibility

Add a return edge \(z\to s\) with bounds \([0,1]\). This upper bound is sufficient because the total available mass is \(1\). A feasible bounded flow extends to a circulation by putting its value on this edge; conversely, deleting this edge from a feasible circulation gives a feasible bounded flow.

Here is the explicit reduction of circulation feasibility to ordinary maximum flow. For an edge \(a\), write its bounds as \(l_a,u_a\), and define

\(b(v)=\sum_{a\text{ entering }v}l_a-\sum_{a\text{ leaving }v}l_a.\)

Replace each original edge’s bounds by capacity \(u_a-l_a\). Add auxiliary vertices \(s^\star,z^\star\), together with:

  • An edge \(s^\star\to v\) of capacity \(b(v)\) whenever \(b(v)>0\).
  • An edge \(v\to z^\star\) of capacity \(-b(v)\) whenever \(b(v)<0\).

Since \(\sum_vb(v)=0\), the total capacities of these two families agree.

Suppose an auxiliary flow saturates every edge leaving \(s^\star\). It then also saturates every edge entering \(z^\star\). If \(y\) denotes its flow on the original edges, conservation at \(v\) gives

\(\sum_{a\text{ leaving }v}y_a-\sum_{a\text{ entering }v}y_a=b(v).\)

Consequently, \(f_a=l_a+y_a\) satisfies circulation conservation and all original bounds.

Conversely, any feasible circulation \(f\) provides such an auxiliary flow by taking \(y_a=f_a-l_a\) and saturating the auxiliary edges. Thus one maximum-flow computation decides feasibility and produces a feasible circulation when one exists.

Optimization

Delete the return edge from a feasible circulation, obtaining a bounded flow \(f\). Its residual network has, for each original edge \(a=(u,v)\),

  • A forward residual edge of capacity \(u_a-f_a\).
  • A backward residual edge of capacity \(f_a-l_a\).

Compute a maximum \(s\)-to-\(z\) flow in this residual network and apply its changes to \(f\). These changes preserve every bound and conserve flow at every internal vertex.

To verify optimality, let \(f'\) be the resulting bounded flow. There is no positive-capacity residual path from \(s\) to \(z\). Let \(A\) be the vertices reachable from \(s\) using positive-capacity residual edges. Then \(s\in A\) and \(z\notin A\).

Every original edge leaving \(A\) has \(f'_a=u_a\); otherwise its forward residual edge would leave \(A\). Every original edge entering \(A\) has \(f'_a=l_a\); otherwise its backward residual edge would leave \(A\).

For any feasible bounded flow \(g\), conservation gives

\(\operatorname{val}(g)=\sum_{a\text{ leaving }A}g_a-\sum_{a\text{ entering }A}g_a.\)

Therefore,

\(\operatorname{val}(g)\leq\sum_{a\text{ leaving }A}u_a-\sum_{a\text{ entering }A}l_a=\operatorname{val}(f').\)

Hence the second maximum-flow computation produces an exact optimum.

3. Running time and exact rational encoding

Both maximum-flow computations involve \(O(S)\) vertices and edges. Using the shortest-augmenting-path algorithm of [Edmonds and Karp](https://ftp.eecs.umich.edu/~pettie/matching/Edmonds-Karp-network-flow.pdf), each takes \(O(ne^2)=O(S^3)\) arithmetic operations.

For completeness, the bound follows because residual shortest-path distances never decrease. Whenever a directed residual edge becomes a saturated bottleneck and later becomes a bottleneck again, its tail’s distance has increased by at least two: between those events its reverse must occur on an augmenting path. Each directed residual edge can therefore be a bottleneck only \(O(n)\) times. Every augmentation saturates a bottleneck, giving \(O(ne)\) augmentations, each found and applied in \(O(e)\) operations.

To bound the bit complexity, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input rational numbers, counting each input occurrence. Then

\(\log D=O(L).\)

Every network bound becomes an integer after multiplication by \(D\). In particular, a product \(\mu_tL_{t,k}\) or \(\mu_tU_{t,k}\) has a denominator dividing \(D\), because its two factors come from distinct input occurrences.

All scaled original bounds are at most \(D\). Auxiliary capacities are sums of at most \(O(S)\) such integers, so their bit lengths are

\(O(L+\log S).\)

Integer maximum flow therefore performs additions, subtractions, and comparisons on numbers of this bit length. Constructing the scaled bounds by schoolbook arithmetic costs at most \(O(SL^2)\) bit operations. This yields the stated bound

\(O\!\left(S^3(L+\log S)+SL^2\right).\)

Dividing the resulting integer assignment-edge flows by \(D\) gives an exact optimal rational matrix whose entries have \(O(L)\)-bit representations.

This algorithm uses the listed \(\tau\) types directly. It never enumerates unlisted possible types or expands rational masses into individual agents.

4. A finite randomized implementation

We now implement any feasible matrix \(x\), including the computed optimum.

Fix an ordering \(p_1,\ldots,p_m\) of the platforms and introduce the unmatched outcome \(\bot\). For each type \(t\), define

\(q_{t,j}=x_{t,p_j}/\mu_t\quad(1\leq j\leq m),\qquad q_{t,m+1}=1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}q_{t,j}.\)

These numbers are nonnegative and sum to \(1\). Put

\(c_{t,0}=0,\qquad c_{t,j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}q_{t,i}\quad(1\leq j\leq m+1).\)

Thus \(c_{t,m+1}=1\). Associate outcome \(p_j\) with the interval

\(I_{t,j}=[c_{t,j-1},c_{t,j})\)

for \(1\leq j\leq m\), and associate \(\bot\) with \(I_{t,m+1}\).

Take the probability space \(\Omega=[0,1)\) with uniform Lebesgue probability. For person \(a\in[0,\mu_t)\) and seed \(\omega\in\Omega\), define

\(r_t(a,\omega)=\frac{a}{\mu_t}+\omega-\left\lfloor\frac{a}{\mu_t}+\omega\right\rfloor.\)

Assign this person the outcome whose interval contains \(r_t(a,\omega)\). The intervals partition \([0,1)\), so every person receives exactly one outcome.

The finite description consists of the rational endpoints, the outcome labels, and this formula.

Eligibility and at most one platform

The outcome is single-valued, so every person receives at most one platform.

If \(p_j\notin E_t\), then \(x_{t,p_j}=0\). Hence \(q_{t,j}=0\) and \(I_{t,j}\) is empty. No person can receive an ineligible platform.

The half-open convention resolves all endpoint cases.

Joint measurability

For each type, \((a,\omega)\mapsto r_t(a,\omega)\) is Borel measurable: it is formed from continuous functions and the Borel measurable floor function.

Each outcome’s inverse image is therefore Borel measurable. Since the society is a finite disjoint union of type intervals, the whole assignment is jointly measurable.

Every person has the required probabilities

Fix a type \(t\) and a person \(a\). Translation modulo \(1\) preserves uniform Lebesgue probability, so \(r_t(a,\omega)\) is uniform on \([0,1)\). Consequently,

\(\Pr[\text{person }(t,a)\text{ receives }p_j]=|I_{t,j}|=q_{t,j}=\frac{x_{t,p_j}}{\mu_t}.\)

This holds for every person, including interval-boundary points.

In particular,

\(\Pr[\text{person }(t,a)\text{ receives a platform in }R_{t,k}]=\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_{p\in R_{t,k}}x_{t,p}\in[L_{t,k},U_{t,k}].\)

Every realization satisfies every quota

Fix any seed \(\omega\). The map \(a\mapsto a/\mu_t\) changes Lebesgue mass by the factor \(\mu_t\), while translation modulo \(1\) preserves Lebesgue measure on \([0,1)\). Therefore,

\(\lambda\{a\in[0,\mu_t):\text{person }(t,a)\text{ receives }p_j\}=\mu_t|I_{t,j}|=x_{t,p_j}.\)

Thus every realization has exactly the prescribed type–platform masses. Its platform and platform-group masses are respectively

\(\sum_t x_{t,p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{t:g(t)=h}x_{t,p}.\)

They satisfy all quotas because \(x\) is feasible. These equalities hold for every seed, not merely almost surely. Every realization also has total assigned mass \(\sum_{t,p}x_{t,p}\), so implementing an optimal matrix gives optimal mass in every realization.

Description size

The endpoints can be computed directly from the integer-scaled solution. If \(x_{t,p_i}=f_{t,i}/D\) and \(\mu_t=a_t/b_t\), then, for \(j\leq m\),

\(c_{t,j}=\frac{b_t\sum_{i=1}^{j}f_{t,i}}{Da_t}.\)

Each endpoint has \(O(L)\) bits. Computing all endpoints takes \(O(\tau mL^2)\) bit operations, and their description uses \(O(\tau mL)\) bits.

The random seed is continuous; its uniform law and the assignment rule have finite descriptions. No enumeration of realizations is required by the task.

5. Conclusion

The bounded-flow correspondence settles exact feasibility and optimization. The explicit random-shift construction settles implementation, including joint measurability, pointwise marginal probabilities, eligibility, and quotas in every realization. Hence the entire stated problem is polynomial-time solvable.
