The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite product domain \(X\) and a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over complete GAI utility types \(\theta\), with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), utilities \(u_\theta\), positive weights \(w_\theta\), and benchmarks \(r_\theta>\max_{x\in X}u_\theta(x)\), find an alternative minimizing \(F_\mu(x)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\theta\sim\mu} w_\theta(r_\theta-u_\theta(x))\). Let \(A_\mu(x)=\int w_\theta u_\theta(x)\,d\mu(\theta)\) and \(B_\mu=\int w_\theta r_\theta\,d\mu(\theta)\). If \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) are ranked by decreasing \(A_\mu\) and \(x^*\) minimizes \(F_\mu\) among them, does \(A_\mu(x_k)\le B_\mu-F_\mu(x^*)\) certify that \(x^*\) globally minimizes \(F_\mu\)?

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type also specifies rational \(w_\theta>0\) and \(r_\theta>\max_{x\in X}u_\theta(x)\).
Define \(F_\mu(x)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\theta\sim\mu}w_\theta(r_\theta-u_\theta(x))\), \(A_\mu(x)=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta w_\theta u_\theta(x)\), and \(B_\mu=\sum_\theta\mu_\theta w_\theta r_\theta\). Alternatives are deterministic elements of \(X\).
For the associated decision problem, a rational threshold \(q\) is supplied. For the stopping question, \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) are the first \(k\) distinct alternatives in a ranking of all of \(X\) by nonincreasing \(A_\mu\), with arbitrary tie-breaking, and \(x^*\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{1\le i\le k}F_\mu(x_i)\).
State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), the total input bit length \(L\), and, when relevant, \(k\).

Is deciding whether \(\min_{x\in X}F_\mu(x)\le q\) in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it NP-hard?
Does \(A_\mu(x_k)\le B_\mu-F_\mu(x^*)\) always certify global optimality of \(x^*\), or is there an admissible counterexample?

For finite agent lists, the analogous stopping implication is established; it is a correctness certificate, without a polynomial bound on the number of alternatives enumerated. 1. Conclusions and finite-support identity

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=1\), every attribute is Boolean, and every factor involves at most three attributes. On the promised admissible inputs, yes-instances have polynomially verifiable certificates.

The stopping condition always certifies global optimality. There is no admissible counterexample.

Since every listed type has positive mass and the support is finite, define \(d_\theta(x)=w_\theta(r_\theta-u_\theta(x))\). Then
\[ F_\mu(x)=\max_{1\leq\theta\leq\tau}d_\theta(x). \]
The strict benchmark assumption ensures \(d_\theta(x)>0\), although the stopping proof below does not require this positivity.

2. Hardness with a strict benchmark

We give a polynomial-time reduction from \(\mathrm{3SAT}\), the \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete satisfiability problem for conjunctions of clauses containing at most three literals.

Let the formula have variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_m\) and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_h\). Set \(X_j=\{0,1\}\), interpreting alternatives as truth assignments.

Use one supported type, with \(\mu_1=w_1=1\). For each clause \(C_a\), let \(Z_a\) contain its variables and explicitly list the factor table
\[ u_{1,a}(x_{Z_a})= \begin{cases} 0,&\text{if }C_a\text{ is satisfied},\\ -1,&\text{if }C_a\text{ is violated}. \end{cases} \]
Each table has at most eight entries. Add a zero unary factor for every attribute, ensuring that the scopes cover all attributes.

Write \(v(x)\) for the number of violated clauses. The resulting utility is \(u_1(x)=-v(x)\). Choose \(r_1=1\) and \(q=1\). These choices are admissible because
\[ \max_{x\in X}u_1(x)\leq 0<1=r_1. \]
Furthermore,
\[ F_\mu(x)=r_1-u_1(x)=1+v(x). \]
Consequently, \(\min_xF_\mu(x)\leq1\) holds exactly when some assignment violates no clauses, which is exactly satisfiability of the original formula.

The construction has \(h+m\) factors and bit length \(O((h+m)\log(m+1))\) under ordinary indexed encodings. Thus it is a polynomial-time reduction whose outputs satisfy every premise, including the strict benchmark inequality.

Therefore the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).

For verification, an alternative can be specified by its coordinate indices. Evaluating its selected table entries and comparing the resulting rational \(F_\mu(x)\) with \(q\) takes polynomial time, as quantified below. Thus feasibility has polynomially verifiable certificates. This verification uses the stated admissibility promise; it does not attempt to verify \(r_\theta>\max_xu_\theta(x)\) for arbitrary malformed inputs.

3. Proof of the stopping certificate

Let \(1\leq k\leq|X|\), and assume the supplied alternatives are indeed the first \(k\) distinct alternatives in a ranking by nonincreasing \(A_\mu\).

For every alternative \(y\), the definitions give
\[ B_\mu-A_\mu(y) =\sum_{\theta=1}^{\tau}\mu_\theta w_\theta(r_\theta-u_\theta(y)) =\sum_{\theta=1}^{\tau}\mu_\theta d_\theta(y). \]
Each \(d_\theta(y)\leq F_\mu(y)\). Multiplying these inequalities by the positive masses and summing, using \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\), yields
\[ B_\mu-A_\mu(y)\leq F_\mu(y). \]

Now assume the stopping condition
\[ A_\mu(x_k)\leq B_\mu-F_\mu(x^*) \]
holds. For every unenumerated alternative \(y\), the ranking property gives \(A_\mu(y)\leq A_\mu(x_k)\). Hence
\[ F_\mu(y) \geq B_\mu-A_\mu(y) \geq B_\mu-A_\mu(x_k) \geq F_\mu(x^*). \]
For every enumerated alternative \(x_i\), the choice of \(x^*\) gives \(F_\mu(x_i)\geq F_\mu(x^*)\). These two cases cover all of \(X\), proving global optimality.

Equality throughout is harmless, so arbitrary tie-breaking does not invalidate the certificate. This proves the finite-support formulation directly, extending the average-versus-maximum argument in the cited [Gonzales–Perny–Queiroz paper](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2008/AAAI08-164.pdf).

4. Running times

Let \(N=|X|=\prod_{j=1}^{m}|X_j|\). The following conservative bounds count bit operations.

Exact arithmetic remains polynomial in \(L\). To see this explicitly, let \(D\) be the product of all positive denominators appearing in the input. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Every input rational can be represented as an integer divided by \(D\). Utilities then have denominator \(D\), weighted losses denominator \(D^2\), and \(A_\mu,B_\mu\) denominator \(D^3\). All corresponding integers have \(O(L)\) bits: their numerators arise from sums and products of input numbers, with at most polynomially many summands.

Using schoolbook integer arithmetic, preprocessing costs \(O(L^3)\). A conservative per-alternative evaluation bound is
\[ T=O((m+\tau+L)L^2). \]
It includes factor lookup, utility evaluation, aggregation, and rational comparison. Therefore:

  • Checking a decision witness costs \(O(L^3+T)\).
  • Given a prefix satisfying the ranking hypothesis, computing its incumbent and checking the stopping inequality costs \(O(L^3+kT)\).
  • Exhaustive minimization costs \(O(L^3+NT)\).
  • Evaluating and sorting all alternatives to obtain a ranking costs \(O(L^3+NT+N\log(N+1)L^2)\).

Because domains are explicitly listed, \(N\leq(L+1)^m\), giving corresponding bounds purely in \(m,\tau,L\). The prefix-checking bound assumes the ranking property; evaluating the prefix alone does not verify that no omitted alternative has larger \(A_\mu\).

5. The certificate can require exponentially many alternatives

An explicit admissible example shows why the certificate supplies no polynomial bound on \(k\).

Take even \(m=2t\geq2\), Boolean attributes, and two types with \(\mu_1=\mu_2=1/2\) and \(w_1=w_2=1\). Define unary-factor utilities
\[ u_1(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_j,\qquad u_2(x)=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_j, \]
and benchmarks \(r_1=m+1,\ r_2=1\). They are strictly above the respective maxima \(m\) and \(0\).

Writing \(H(x)=\sum_jx_j\), direct calculation gives
\[ A_\mu(x)=0,\qquad B_\mu=t+1,\qquad F_\mu(x)=\max\{2t+1-H(x),\,1+H(x)\} =t+1+|H(x)-t|. \]
Thus precisely the alternatives with \(H(x)=t\) are optimal. Since all alternatives tie under \(A_\mu\), an allowed ranking places every nonoptimal alternative first. Before the first optimum appears, \(F_\mu(x^*)>B_\mu\), so the stopping inequality fails. It first holds at
\[ k=2^m-\binom{m}{t}+1. \]
Moreover, \(\binom{m}{t}\leq2^{m-1}\): Pascal’s identity expresses it as the sum of the two equal middle coefficients in row \(m-1\), whose entire sum is \(2^{m-1}\). Hence \(k\geq2^{m-1}+1\).

This family has input length \(O(m\log(m+1))\). Consequently, an admissible tie-breaking order can require exponentially many alternatives before the valid certificate fires.
