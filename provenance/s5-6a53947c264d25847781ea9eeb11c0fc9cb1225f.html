The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite seller-type and consumer-type sets \(S\) and \(C\), rational type masses \(\mu_s,\nu_c\) summing to \(1\), per-household block supplies \(d_s\) and demands \(b_c\), and each type's strict ranking of counterpart types, compute a rational flow \(x_{sc}\ge 0\) in time polynomial in the number of listed types and input bit length. Require \(\sum_c x_{sc}\le \mu_s d_s\) and \(\sum_s x_{sc}\le \nu_c b_c\). For every pair \((s,c)\), define \(U_s(c)=\mu_s d_s-\sum_{c'}x_{sc'}+\sum_{c':\,c\succ_s c'}x_{sc'}\) and \(V_c(s)=\nu_c b_c-\sum_{s'}x_{s'c}+\sum_{s':\,s\succ_c s'}x_{s'c}\); require \(U_s(c)=0\) or \(V_c(s)=0\), so no positive amount of flow can be shifted to a pair both sides prefer to unused capacity or less-preferred trades.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(S\) and \(C\) be nonempty finite sets of seller and consumer types. A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau=|S|+|C|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Set \(m=|S||C|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Masses satisfy \(\sum_s\mu_s=\sum_c\nu_c=1\). Per-household supplies \(d_s\) and demands \(b_c\) are positive integers encoded in binary. Each type has a strict total ranking of all opposite types, all acceptable and preferred to unused capacity; rankings are fixed, and there are no additional pair capacities.
A feasible allocation is a rational flow \(x_{sc}\geq0\) satisfying \(\sum_c x_{sc}\leq\mu_s d_s\) and \(\sum_s x_{sc}\leq\nu_c b_c\).
Define \(U_s(c)=\mu_s d_s-\sum_{c'}x_{sc'}+\sum_{c':\,c\succ_s c'}x_{sc'}\) and \(V_c(s)=\nu_c b_c-\sum_{s'}x_{s'c}+\sum_{s':\,s\succ_c s'}x_{s'c}\). Stability requires \(U_s(c)=0\) or \(V_c(s)=0\) for every \((s,c)\), allowing blocking trades to use unused capacity or replace less-preferred trades.

Can a feasible stable rational allocation always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this search problem NP-hard?

The discrete energy-matching algorithm is asserted to produce feasible stable block allocations. [Singha, Shreyas and Kumar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07994).
The continuous problem stated here is already covered by polynomial-time algorithms for bipartite stable allocation; consequently this target is settled rather than open. [Dean and Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

Conclusion

A feasible stable rational allocation always exists and can be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The stated problem reduces to bipartite stable allocation. The reduction preserves precisely the given blocking condition, and the algorithm can be implemented with exact arithmetic of polynomial bit complexity.

1. The applicable algorithmic result

We use the following established result: bipartite stable allocation with strict preferences, vertex quotas, and edge upper capacities admits an algorithm using \(O(M\log N)\) operations, where \(N\) and \(M\) are the numbers of vertices and edges. The model permits nonintegral data; unused capacity is represented by least-preferred dummy partners. See Sections 2–3, particularly Lemma 5, of [Dean and Munshi, *Faster Algorithms for Stable Allocation Problems*](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

We give the reduction explicitly because the edge-capacity condition and treatment of unused capacity must agree with this question.

Write \(a_s=\mu_s d_s\) and \(q_c=\nu_c b_c\). These are positive rationals. Let \(A=\sum_s a_s\) and \(B=\sum_c q_c\). Although both sides’ masses sum to one, their aggregate supply and demand need not agree.

2. Constructing a balanced stable-allocation instance

Add a dummy seller \(s_0\) and a dummy consumer \(c_0\). Set their quotas to \(a_{s_0}=B\) and \(q_{c_0}=A\). The augmented bipartite graph is complete, with

\(N=\tau+2,\qquad M=(|S|+1)(|C|+1)=m+\tau+1.\)

Every real vertex retains its given ordering of real partners and ranks its dummy partner last. Each dummy vertex ranks all real partners in any strict order, followed by the other dummy.

For every augmented edge, set \(u_{sc}=\min(a_s,q_c)\), using the dummy quotas where appropriate. Require every augmented vertex to receive exactly its quota.

These edge bounds impose no additional restriction: any nonnegative allocation satisfying the vertex quotas already satisfies \(y_{sc}\leq a_s\) and \(y_{sc}\leq q_c\).

To verify feasibility, take any original feasible allocation \(x\), and let \(T=\sum_{s,c}x_{sc}\). Define its extension by

\(y_{sc}=x_{sc}\) for real pairs,

\(y_{s c_0}=a_s-\sum_c x_{sc},\qquad y_{s_0 c}=q_c-\sum_s x_{sc},\qquad y_{s_0c_0}=T.\)

All entries are nonnegative. Real vertices receive exactly their quotas. The dummy seller receives \((B-T)+T=B\), and the dummy consumer receives \((A-T)+T=A\). Consequently all edge bounds also hold.

Conversely, restricting any feasible augmented allocation to real pairs gives an original feasible allocation. Its dummy-edge entries necessarily equal the corresponding unused capacities, by the real vertices’ quota equalities.

3. Exact equivalence of stability

In the augmented model, an edge \((s,c)\) blocks if its allocation is below \(u_{sc}\) and each endpoint has a positive allocation to a strictly less-preferred partner.

Fix an original feasible allocation \(x\) and its extension \(y\). For a real pair \((s,c)\), the total allocation of \(s\) to partners worse than \(c\), including the dummy, is exactly

\(y_{s c_0}+\sum_{c':\,c\succ_s c'}y_{sc'}=U_s(c).\)

Similarly, the total allocation of \(c\) to partners worse than \(s\) is exactly

\(y_{s_0c}+\sum_{s':\,s\succ_c s'}y_{s'c}=V_c(s).\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Thus \(s\) has a positive allocation to a worse augmented partner if and only if \(U_s(c)>0\); the analogous equivalence holds for \(V_c(s)>0\).

It remains to check that the artificial edge bound cannot prevent an original blocking pair. Partitioning the other consumers into those better and worse than \(c\) gives

\(U_s(c)=a_s-x_{sc}-\sum_{c':\,c'\succ_s c}x_{sc'}\leq a_s-x_{sc}.\)

Likewise,

\(V_c(s)=q_c-x_{sc}-\sum_{s':\,s'\succ_c s}x_{s'c}\leq q_c-x_{sc}.\)

Therefore, if both \(U_s(c)>0\) and \(V_c(s)>0\), then \(x_{sc}<a_s\) and \(x_{sc}<q_c\), hence \(y_{sc}<u_{sc}\). The real edge blocks in the augmented instance.

Conversely, an augmented blocking real edge has positive worse-partner allocations at both endpoints, so both \(U_s(c)\) and \(V_c(s)\) are positive. We have proved that a real pair blocks in one model exactly when it blocks in the other.

Finally, no edge involving a dummy can block:

  • On \((s,c_0)\), the real seller ranks \(c_0\) last and has no worse partner.
  • On \((s_0,c)\), the real consumer ranks \(s_0\) last and has no worse partner.
  • On \((s_0,c_0)\), both endpoints rank each other last.

Because augmented quotas are filled exactly, there is no further unused-capacity condition to check. Thus the extension is stable exactly when \(x\) satisfies the question’s condition on every pair.

The cited algorithm consequently produces a stable augmented allocation whose restriction is a stable allocation for the original problem.

4. Rational output and polynomial bit complexity

An operation bound alone does not establish polynomial time for binary input. We now justify exact computation.

Write each input mass with its positive integer denominator, and let \(D\) be the product of all these denominators. Its binary length is at most the sum of their binary lengths, hence \(O(L)\).

Scale every quota and edge capacity by \(D\). The real quotas become the integers

\(\widehat a_s=D\mu_s d_s,\qquad \widehat q_c=D\nu_c b_c.\)

The dummy quotas and all edge capacities are then integers too. Multiplication by \(d_s\) or \(b_c\) increases binary length by at most that integer’s input length. Summing at most \(\tau\) quotas adds at most \(O(\log\tau)\) bits. Thus the scaled numerical data have bit length \(h=O(L+\log\tau)\), and preprocessing takes polynomial time.

The cited algorithm augments along paths and cycles by minima of residual capacities and quota slacks, updating allocations by addition and subtraction. Its numerical work uses additions, subtractions, comparisons, and minima. Consequently integral input remains integral throughout. These update rules are given in Section 3 of [Dean and Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf).

For completeness, even a loose bound suffices to control all intermediate arithmetic. Let \(K\) be the polynomial number of elementary numerical operations. Initially every stored integer has \(O(h)\) bits. An addition or subtraction increases the maximum current bit length by at most a constant; a minimum selects an existing value, and a comparison creates no larger integer. Hence every intermediate integer has \(O(h+K)\) bits. Each operation therefore takes polynomial bit time.

Let \(z_{sc}\) be the resulting integral allocation on real edges. Output \(x_{sc}=z_{sc}/D\). Feasibility follows by dividing the scaled inequalities by \(D\). Both blocking quantities also scale by \(D\), so their zero conditions are preserved exactly.

Moreover, \(z_{sc}\leq\min(\widehat a_s,\widehat q_c)\). Every output numerator and denominator therefore has polynomial bit length. There are only \(m\) output entries.

Since \(N=\tau+2\), \(M=m+\tau+1\), and all preprocessing, arithmetic, and output costs are polynomial, the full computation is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). No expansion into individual households or units of supply is required.
