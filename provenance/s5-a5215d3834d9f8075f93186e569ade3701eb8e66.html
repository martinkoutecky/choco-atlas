The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(m\) goods, a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) normalized, monotone submodular valuation types, and per-good capacity \(1/\rho\), find a common price menu over integral bundles and masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) summing to \(\mu_t\) for each type. Require each type's assigned mass to use only bundles maximizing \(v_t(S)-p(S)\), and each good's total assigned mass to respect its capacity. Can a value-query algorithm achieve a \(\rho/10\)-approximation to the maximum welfare over all feasible mass assignments, with running time stated in \(m,\tau,L,\rho\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(M\) contain \(m\ge 1\) goods, and let \(\rho\ge 10\) be an integer. A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) normalized, monotone submodular valuation types \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb Q_{\ge 0}\), accessible only through value queries, with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) include the binary input encoding and a uniform bound on oracle-answer bit lengths.
A mass assignment consists of rational \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le 1/\rho\) for every good \(j\). Its welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_t\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\); let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the maximum welfare over all such assignments, without price restrictions.
Output an assignment and a common bundle-price menu \(p:2^M\to\mathbb Q_{\ge 0}\cup\{+\infty\}\), with \(p(\varnothing)=0\). Infinite prices make bundles unavailable. Require \(x_{t,S}>0\) only when \(S\in\arg\max_{T\subseteq M}\{v_t(T)-p(T)\}\); mass may split arbitrarily among tied maximizers. Goods may remain unused. The menu may depend on the entire input society; no additional truthfulness requirement across inputs is imposed.
Assignments are output as lists of their positive entries. Menus may have succinct representations; description length and price-evaluation time count toward running time.

Can a deterministic value-query algorithm always output such a menu and assignment with \(W(x)\ge (10/\rho)\mathrm{OPT}\), using polynomially many queries and time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\rho\), or is this approximation impossible under those bounds?

For discrete auctions with \(n\) bidders, deterministic truthful \(n/10\)-approximation mechanisms require at least \(e^{m/n^2}/(10n^2m^6)\) value queries. [Dobzinski](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.1830)
For the related discrete configuration relaxation, optimal fractional welfare can be computed with demand queries, a stronger oracle model. [Feige–Vondrák](https://theory.stanford.edu/~jvondrak/data/submod-improve-ToC.pdf) No result settling the exact continuous problem stated here was verified.

Result

The requested algorithm is impossible under the stated bounds. A direct value-oracle lower bound applies already at the fixed value \(\rho=13\), with two valuation types and dyadic rational masses. It holds even if the algorithm is required to output only an assignment, without prices.

Thus the obstruction is not confined to the exact-optimization endpoint \(\rho=10\). The construction below also rules out the guarantee for every fixed \(\rho\in\{10,11,12,13\}\).

For perspective, I also prove that a deterministic polynomial-time value-query algorithm can output a valid common menu with welfare at least \(\frac12\mathrm{OPT}\). Consequently, the requested guarantee is achievable for every \(\rho\ge20\).

1. An explicit hidden-partition valuation

Let \(m=2N\), and partition the goods into two sets \(A,B\), each of size \(N\). Fix \(\delta=\frac1{100}\). Define

\[ \text{ } \]

For compliance with the requested delimiter convention, the formulas below use \( \)\) throughout.

Let
\(\displaystyle G(s)=s-\frac{s^2}{4}\)
and
\(\displaystyle H(d)=\frac14\bigl(\max\{|d|-\delta,0\}\bigr)^2.\)

For \((x,y)\in[0,1]^2\), set
\(\displaystyle F(x,y)=G(x+y)+H(x-y).\)

The hidden-partition valuation is
\(\displaystyle v_A(S)=F\!\left(\frac{|S\cap A|}{N},\frac{|S\cap B|}{N}\right).\)

The comparison oracle is the cardinality-based valuation
\(\displaystyle v_0(S)=G\!\left(\frac{|S|}{N}\right).\)

They agree whenever
\(\displaystyle \bigl||S\cap A|-|S\cap B|\bigr|\le\delta N.\)

Both valuations satisfy the required hypotheses

The function \(G\) is nondecreasing and concave on \([0,2]\), so \(v_0\) is normalized, monotone, and submodular.

Here is a direct verification for \(v_A\). The function \(F\) is continuously differentiable. In the region \(|x-y|\le\delta\), its two first derivatives equal
\(\displaystyle 1-\frac{x+y}{2}\ge0.\)

In the region \(x-y>\delta\), they are
\(\displaystyle F_x=1-y-\frac{\delta}{2},\qquad F_y=1-x+\frac{\delta}{2}.\)

Since \(x\le1\) and \(y<x-\delta\le1-\delta\), both derivatives are nonnegative. The remaining region follows by exchanging \(x\) and \(y\). Therefore \(F\) is coordinatewise nondecreasing.

Where the second derivatives exist, they are

| Region | \(F_{xx}=F_{yy}\) | \(F_{xy}\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(|x-y|<\delta\) | \(-\frac12\) | \(-\frac12\) |
| \(|x-y|>\delta\) | \(0\) | \(-1\) |

The first derivatives are continuous across the boundaries. Consequently, each first derivative is nonincreasing in either coordinate.

To obtain set-function submodularity, suppose \(S\subseteq T\) and \(j\notin T\). Their normalized count vectors satisfy \(z_S\le z_T\) coordinatewise. If \(j\in A\), its marginal value is the integral of \(F_x\) along an interval of length \(\frac1N\). The coordinatewise decrease of \(F_x\) gives
\(\displaystyle v_A(S\cup\{j\})-v_A(S)\ge v_A(T\cup\{j\})-v_A(T).\)
For \(j\in B\), the same argument uses \(F_y\). This proves submodularity.

Also, \(F(0,0)=0\) and \(F(1,1)=1\). Monotonicity therefore implies that every oracle answer lies in \([0,1]\). All answers are rational, with a common possible denominator \(40000N^2\), so their bit lengths are \(O(\log N)\).

2. A feasible assignment exploiting the hidden partition

Fix \(\rho=13\). Use two types:

  • Type \(1\) has valuation \(v_A\) and mass \(\mu=\frac5{32}\).
  • Type \(2\) has the zero valuation and mass \(\frac{27}{32}\).

Write \(c=\frac1{13}\). The two hidden bundles have the same value
\(\displaystyle v_A(A)=v_A(B)=V:=\frac34+\frac{(1-\delta)^2}{4}=\frac{39801}{40000}.\)

Consider the assignment
\(\displaystyle x_{1,A}=c,\qquad x_{1,B}=c,\qquad x_{1,\varnothing}=\mu-2c=\frac1{416},\qquad x_{2,\varnothing}=\frac{27}{32}.\)

Every good is used by exactly mass \(c\), and both type-mass constraints hold. Hence
\(\displaystyle \mathrm{OPT}\ge 2cV=\frac2{13}V.\)

This witness is itself compatible with a common menu: price \(A\) and \(B\) at \(V\), price \(\varnothing\) at zero, and make every other bundle unavailable. All three assigned bundles tie at utility zero for type \(1\), while type \(2\) can receive the empty bundle.

3. Welfare obtainable from balanced bundles

Suppose every positive bundle assigned to type \(1\) is balanced, meaning that \(v_A(S)=v_0(S)\).

Condition on type \(1\), and let \(S\) have probability \(x_{1,S}/\mu\). Feasibility gives
\(\displaystyle \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{|S|}{N}\right] =\frac1{\mu N}\sum_{j\in M}\sum_{S\ni j}x_{1,S} \le\frac{2c}{\mu}=\frac{64}{65}.\)

Because \(G\) is concave and nondecreasing,
\(\displaystyle W(x)=\mu\,\mathbb E\!\left[G\!\left(\frac{|S|}{N}\right)\right] \le\mu G\!\left(\frac{2c}{\mu}\right) =2c-\frac{c^2}{\mu} =\frac2{13}\cdot\frac{49}{65}.\)

On the other hand, the requested welfare is at least
\(\displaystyle \frac{10}{13}\mathrm{OPT}\ge\frac2{13}\cdot\frac{10}{13}V.\)

The inequality is strict in the wrong direction:
\(\displaystyle \frac{10}{13}V-\frac{49}{65}=\frac{601}{52000}>0.\)

Thus an assignment consisting entirely of balanced bundles cannot meet the guarantee.

4. Polynomially many queries and output bundles can all be balanced

Let \(A\) be uniformly random among the \(N\)-element subsets of \(M\), with \(B=M\setminus A\).

For any fixed bundle \(S\), I claim
\(\displaystyle \Pr\!\left[\bigl||S\cap A|-|S\cap B|\bigr|>\delta N\right] \le 2(2N+1)e^{-\delta^2N/4}. \)

To verify this, first choose independent uniform signs \(Z_j\in\{-1,1\}\), and then condition on \(\sum_{j\in M}Z_j=0\). The positive-sign set under this conditioning is a uniform \(N\)-element set.

For \(k=|S|>0\), the exponential-moment inequality \(\cosh u\le e^{u^2/2}\) gives
\(\displaystyle \Pr\!\left[\left|\sum_{j\in S}Z_j\right|>\delta N\right] \le2e^{-\delta^2N^2/(2k)} \le2e^{-\delta^2N/4}, \)
because \(k\le2N\). The empty-set case has probability zero.

The conditioning event has probability
\(\displaystyle \frac{\binom{2N}{N}}{2^{2N}}\ge\frac1{2N+1}, \)
since the central binomial probability is the largest of \(2N+1\) probabilities summing to one. Dividing by this lower bound proves the claim.

Now run a proposed deterministic algorithm with type \(1\) answered by the comparison oracle \(v_0\), and type \(2\) answered by zero. This fixes its entire transcript and its output.

Let \(q\) be its number of value queries and \(K\) its number of listed positive assignment entries. Collect all queried bundles and all output bundles into a family \(\mathcal C\), so \(|\mathcal C|\le q+K\). The union bound gives
\(\displaystyle \Pr[\text{some bundle in }\mathcal C\text{ is unbalanced}] \le(q+K)\,2(2N+1)e^{-\delta^2N/4}. \)

For polynomial \(q+K\), this expression is less than one for sufficiently large \(N\). Thus there exists a partition \(A,B\) for which every bundle in \(\mathcal C\) is balanced.

Replace the comparison oracle by the corresponding hidden oracle \(v_A\). Every queried answer remains unchanged. By induction over its adaptive queries, the deterministic algorithm follows the same transcript and outputs the same assignment.

Every output bundle is balanced, so Section 3 proves that this assignment fails the required welfare guarantee. Its price menu cannot repair this welfare failure.

The answer bit lengths are \(O(\log N)\), and the other input parameters have polynomial encoding length. Therefore this contradicts the stated polynomial query and time bounds. More quantitatively, avoiding this argument requires
\(\displaystyle q+K\ge\frac{e^{m/80000}}{2(m+1)}\)
on the comparison transcript.

Extension to the other small values of \(\rho\)

The same proof works with any dyadic mass \(\mu\ge\frac2\rho\). Its balanced-welfare bound, divided by the hidden witness welfare before multiplication by \(V\), is
\(\displaystyle \beta=1-\frac1{2\rho\mu}.\)

The following choices all satisfy \(\beta<\frac{10}{\rho}V\):

| \(\rho\) | Hard-type mass \(\mu\) | \(\beta\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| \(10\) | \(\frac14\) | \(\frac45\) |
| \(11\) | \(\frac3{16}\) | \(\frac{25}{33}\) |
| \(12\) | \(\frac3{16}\) | \(\frac79\) |
| \(13\) | \(\frac5{32}\) | \(\frac{49}{65}\) |

Hence the requested guarantee is impossible for each of these fixed values.

5. A constructive guarantee for \(\rho\ge20\)

Here is a complementary positive result that respects the common-menu requirement.

A deterministic fractional greedy assignment

Write \(c=\frac1\rho\). Maintain pieces of population, each described by a type, a rational mass, and its currently assigned bundle. Initially, type \(t\) has one piece of mass \(\mu_t\) assigned the empty bundle.

Process the goods in a fixed order. For good \(j\), compute its marginal value for every current piece. Give \(j\) to pieces in decreasing order of marginal value, stopping when mass \(c\) has received it or all positive marginal values have been exhausted. Split the last piece if necessary.

Each good creates at most one additional piece. Thus there are at most \(\tau+m\) pieces, and the procedure uses polynomially many value queries and rational operations.

Let \(D_j\) be the welfare increase at this step. If mass \(c\) is allocated at positive marginal values, let \(\lambda_j\) be the smallest selected marginal. Otherwise let \(\lambda_j=0\). Then
\(\displaystyle D_j\ge c\lambda_j.\)

For every final piece with bundle \(S\),
\(\displaystyle v_t(S\cup\{j\})-v_t(S)\le\lambda_j.\)

Indeed, this marginal is zero if the piece received \(j\). Otherwise its marginal at the processing step was at most the cutoff, or was zero when positive demand was exhausted. Subsequent additions only decrease the marginal by submodularity.

Let the resulting assignment be \(g\). For any final piece \(S\) of type \(t\) and any bundle \(T\), monotonicity and submodularity give
\(\displaystyle v_t(T)\le v_t(S)+\sum_{j\in T}\lambda_j.\)

Average this inequality over the final pieces of type \(t\). Then multiply by the entries of any feasible comparison assignment \(y\) and sum. The type-mass equalities and good-capacity inequalities yield
\(\displaystyle W(y)\le W(g)+c\sum_j\lambda_j \le W(g)+\sum_jD_j =2W(g). \)

Therefore \(W(g)\ge\frac12\mathrm{OPT}\).

Turning the assignment into a common menu

Let \(\mathcal B\) contain the empty bundle and every final greedy bundle. It has at most \(\tau+m+1\) members.

Query every \(v_t(S)\) for \(S\in\mathcal B\), and solve the configuration linear program restricted to this common bundle family. Its optimum \(x\) satisfies \(W(x)\ge W(g)\).

Its dual minimizes
\(\displaystyle \sum_t\mu_tu_t+c\sum_ja_j\)
subject to
\(\displaystyle u_t+\sum_{j\in S}a_j\ge v_t(S)\quad(t,\ S\in\mathcal B),\qquad a_j\ge0.\)

The empty-bundle constraints imply \(u_t\ge0\). Define
\(\displaystyle p(S)=\sum_{j\in S}a_j\ \text{for }S\in\mathcal B, \qquad p(S)=+\infty\ \text{otherwise}. \)

Dual feasibility gives \(v_t(S)-p(S)\le u_t\) for every available bundle. Complementary slackness gives equality whenever \(x_{t,S}>0\). Hence every assigned bundle maximizes utility.

This is a finite rational linear program with polynomially many variables and constraints and polynomial coefficient bit lengths. Standard polynomial-time rational linear programming produces the required assignment and dual prices. The finite bundle list and good prices give a polynomial-size menu with polynomial-time evaluation.

Consequently,
\(\displaystyle W(x)\ge\frac12\mathrm{OPT}\ge\frac{10}{\rho}\mathrm{OPT}\)
whenever \(\rho\ge20\).
