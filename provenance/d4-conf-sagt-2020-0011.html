Use deterministic algorithms; let \(L\) bound the binary lengths of the explicit input and oracle replies, and state query bounds and running times in \(m,\tau,L,k,\epsilon,\rho\).

Must every deterministic value-oracle algorithm guaranteeing a feasible allocation with \(W(x)\geq\rho\,\mathrm{OPT}\) on this promised class use at least \(\binom{m}{k}\) queries in the worst case, or can this guarantee be achieved with fewer queries?

1. Structural consequences of the continuous promise

Proposition 1: the unique optimum is a partition allocation

Every promised instance has a partition
$$ (O_1,\ldots,O_\tau) $$
of $M$, possibly containing empty parts, such that
$$ x^*_{t,O_t}=\frac1\tau $$
and all other coordinates vanish.

Proof. Suppose one row $t$ has positive mass on distinct bundles $A$ and $B$. Split that row with $\delta=\mu_t/2$, using any item $j$.

In the proportional lift, both split rows have positive mass on both bundles. For sufficiently small $h>0$, make the following exchange: in the perturbed row, add $h$ on $A$ and subtract $h$ on $B$; in the unperturbed row, do the reverse.

Both this exchange and its negative are feasible. They preserve row masses and aggregate masses on every bundle, hence preserve item usages. Because welfare is linear, either one direction improves welfare, or the exchange produces a distinct optimum. Both conclusions contradict the split promise.

Thus each original row uses exactly one bundle. Its mass is $1/\tau$, so the supply constraints force these bundles to be disjoint.

If an item were unassigned, adding it to any row’s bundle would preserve feasibility. Monotonicity would either increase welfare, contradicting optimality, or preserve welfare, contradicting uniqueness. Thus the bundles cover $M$. ∎

Proposition 2: checking full-type perturbations suffices

Suppose an instance has a unique optimum that is a partition allocation. Then its complete split promise holds if and only if every allowed full-type perturbation, corresponding to $\delta=\mu_t$, preserves that allocation as the unique continuous optimum.

Proof. Necessity follows by taking $\delta=\mu_t$.

For sufficiency, fix a split of type $t$ and an item $j$. Normalization gives the perturbation bonus
$$ c=(1-\epsilon)v_t(\{j\})\geq0 $$
on bundles containing $j$. Aggregate the two split rows of a feasible split allocation $z$ into an original row, obtaining a feasible original allocation $y$.

If $j\in O_t$, then
$$ W_{\mathrm{split}}(z) = W(y)+c\sum_{S\ni j}z^{\mathrm{pert}}_S \leq \mathrm{OPT}+c\delta. $$
The proportional lift attains this bound. Equality forces $W(y)=\mathrm{OPT}$, hence $y=x^*$ by original uniqueness.

If $j\notin O_t$, nonnegativity gives
$$ W_{\mathrm{split}}(z) \leq W(y)+c\sum_{S\ni j}y_{t,S} = W_{\mathrm{full}}(y) \leq \mathrm{OPT}. $$
The last inequality uses the full perturbation’s unique optimum, which receives no bonus. Again, the proportional lift attains the bound, and equality forces $y=x^*$.

In either case, aggregation to a row supported only on $O_t$ forces both split rows to use only $O_t$. Their prescribed masses determine the proportional lift uniquely. The argument also covers the omission of a zero-mass row. ∎

In particular, when $\epsilon=1$, every unique partition optimum automatically satisfies the split promise.

2. A universal lower bound of $\tau$ queries

This argument uses additive valuations, so it applies throughout the promised class.

Theorem 3

For every admissible $\epsilon$ and $\rho$, every deterministic guaranteeing algorithm requires at least $\tau$ queries in the worst case.

Proof. Since $\rho>1-1/(2k)\geq1/2$, put
$$ g=2\rho-1>0. $$
Choose positive dyadic rationals $b$ and $C$ satisfying
$$ b\leq\frac g8, \qquad C\geq\frac4g. $$

Suppose an algorithm uses fewer than $\tau$ queries on every promised instance. Let $a$ be the type in its first query. If it makes no queries, choose any type $a$.

Define a baseline instance by
$$ v_t(S)=\lambda_t|S|, \qquad \lambda_a=1, \qquad \lambda_t=\frac{bt}{\tau+1}\quad(t\ne a). $$
Thus all nonowner coefficients are positive, distinct, and at most $b$.

The unique optimum gives all of $M$ to type $a$ and empty bundles to all other types. To verify the full perturbation promise, note that an item’s additive coefficient in any competing type can increase to at most
$$ (2-\epsilon)b\leq2b<1. $$
Type $a$ therefore remains the unique highest-valued recipient of every item. The continuous optimum is unique: equality in the itemwise welfare bound forces every item’s entire supply to type $a$, which forces that row to use $M$ and every other row to use the empty bundle. Proposition 2 establishes the split promise.

Run the algorithm on this baseline. There is a type $h\ne a$ that it never queries: when queries occur, $a$ is queried first; when none occur, any other type suffices.

Define a second instance by replacing only $\lambda_h$ by $C$. The transcript is unchanged. This second instance is also promised, because
$$ C\geq4>2\geq(2-\epsilon)\max_{t\ne h}\lambda_t. $$
Its unique optimum gives all items to type $h$.

For the common output, normalize
$$ y_{t,S}=\tau x_{t,S}. $$
Each row sums to one, and feasibility implies
$$ \sum_{t,S}|S|y_{t,S}\leq m. $$
Let
$$ A=\sum_S|S|y_{a,S}, \qquad H=\sum_S|S|y_{h,S}. $$

On the baseline,
$$ \tau W(x)\leq A+b(m-A), \qquad \mathrm{OPT}=\frac m\tau. $$
Consequently, a successful baseline output must satisfy
$$ \frac Am\geq r:=\frac{\rho-b}{1-b}. $$
Because $b\leq1/8$,
$$ r = \rho-\frac{b(1-\rho)}{1-b} \geq\rho-2b \geq\rho-\frac g4. $$

On the second instance, all coefficients other than $C$ are at most one. Hence
$$ \frac{W'(x)}{\mathrm{OPT}'} \leq \frac Hm+\frac1C \leq 1-\frac Am+\frac1C \leq 1-r+\frac g4 \leq 1-\rho+\frac g2 = \frac12 < \rho. $$
Here $A+H\leq m$ follows from feasibility.

Thus the same output cannot satisfy the approximation guarantee on both promised instances, a contradiction. ∎

When $k=1$, we have $\tau=m=N$. Therefore this proves the requested literal lower bound for the entire allowed range of $\epsilon$ and $\rho$ in that case.

These instances have explicit-input and oracle-reply binary lengths
$$ O\left(\log(m+1)+\log(\tau+1)+\log\frac1{2\rho-1}\right). $$

3. Hidden partitions: continuous stability and approximation loss

I use the hidden-partition valuations appearing in the proof of Theorem 2 of [Fikioris and Fotakis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.09889v2), but verify their continuous properties directly.

Choose an ordered partition $(O_1,\ldots,O_\tau)$ with $|O_t|=k$. Define
$$ v_t(S)= \begin{cases} |S|,& |S|\leq k-1,\\ k-\frac12,& |S|=k,\ S\ne O_t,\\ k,& S=O_t\text{ or }|S|\geq k+1. \end{cases} $$

3.1. Valuation properties

Normalization and monotonicity follow directly from the definition. The possible marginal values when adding an item to a bundle of cardinality $s$ are
$$ \begin{array}{c|c} s&\text{possible marginals}\\ \hline s\leq k-2&1\\ s=k-1&\frac12,\ 1\\ s=k&0,\ \frac12\\ s\geq k+1&0. \end{array} $$
For strictly nested bundles, cardinality increases, and every possible earlier marginal is at least every possible later marginal. This proves diminishing returns, hence submodularity. For $k=1$, the first row is absent and the same reasoning applies.

Giving each type $O_t$ yields welfare $k$. Every bundle has value at most $k$, and total recipient mass is one, so
$$ \mathrm{OPT}=k. $$

3.2. A continuous uniqueness certificate

First recall a direct price argument. Suppose nonnegative prices $p_j$ make each $O_i$ the unique maximizer of
$$ u_i(S)=v_i(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j. $$
For every feasible allocation,
$$ W(x) \leq \frac1\tau\sum_i u_i(O_i) + \frac1\tau\sum_jp_j. $$
The partition allocation attains equality. Positive mass on any other bundle makes the utility inequality strict, proving uniqueness.

For the original valuations, uniform prices $p_j=1/2$ give this certificate.

Now perturb an entire type $t$ on item $j$. Set
$$ d=\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}, \qquad p_j=\frac12+d, \qquad p_\ell=\frac12\quad(\ell\ne j). $$
For an unperturbed type $i$ and a bundle $S\ne O_i$, write $r=|S|$. Its utility gap below $O_i$ is
$$ u_i(O_i)-u_i(S) = \frac{a_r}{2} + d\bigl(\mathbf1[j\in S]-\mathbf1[j\in O_i]\bigr), $$
where
$$ a_r= \begin{cases} k-r,&r\leq k-1,\\ 1,&r=k,\\ r-k,&r\geq k+1. \end{cases} $$
Since $a_r\geq1$, this gap is at least
$$ \frac12-d=\frac\epsilon2>0. $$

For the perturbed type, the bonus is
$$ c=(1-\epsilon)v_t(\{j\})\leq1-\epsilon. $$
If $j\in O_t$, the bonus increases each gap by
$$ c(1-\mathbf1[j\in S])\geq0. $$
If $j\notin O_t$, alternatives excluding $j$ have unchanged gaps. Alternatives containing $j$ have an original gap at least $1/2+d$, so their perturbed gap is at least
$$ \frac12+d-c \geq \frac12+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}-(1-\epsilon) = \frac\epsilon2. $$

Thus every type uniquely demands its designated bundle at these prices after every full perturbation. The price argument and Proposition 2 prove the complete continuous promise.

Oracle replies and masses have binary length $O(\log(m+1)+\log(\tau+1))$.

3.3. Welfare of an arbitrary fractional allocation

For every bundle,
$$ v_t(S) \leq \frac{|S|+k-1}{2} + \frac12\mathbf1[S=O_t]. $$
For cardinality below $k$, this follows from $|S|\leq k-1$; above $k$, it follows from $|S|\geq k+1$. At cardinality $k$, equality holds.

For a feasible output, put
$$ y_{t,S}=\tau x_{t,S}, \qquad H(O)=\sum_t y_{t,O_t}. $$
Each row of $y$ sums to one. Summing item constraints gives
$$ \sum_{t,S}|S|y_{t,S}\leq m=\tau k. $$
Therefore
$$ W(x)\leq k-\frac12+\frac{H(O)}{2\tau}. $$
Since $\mathrm{OPT}=k$, the requested guarantee requires
$$ H(O)\geq\tau\alpha. $$

4. Query lower bounds from compatible partitions

4.1. The general near-$N$ lower bound

Answer queries using the common baseline: value $|S|$ below cardinality $k$, value $k-1/2$ at cardinality $k$, and value $k$ above it.

Let an algorithm have worst-case query bound $q<N$. A uniformly random ordered balanced partition has each $O_t$ uniform among the $N$ subsets of cardinality $k$. Every fixed query excludes at most a $1/N$ fraction of partitions. Thus the event $A$ that a partition agrees with all baseline replies satisfies
$$ \Pr(A)\geq1-\frac qN>0. $$

The baseline execution must terminate within $q$ queries. Otherwise a compatible promised instance after the first $q$ replies would force the same deterministic execution to exceed its query bound.

Its output is fixed across all compatible partitions. For that output,
$$ \mathbb E[H(O)] = \frac1N\sum_t\sum_{|S|=k}y_{t,S} \leq\frac\tau N. $$
As $H(O)\geq0$,
$$ \mathbb E[H(O)\mid A] \leq \frac{\tau}{N-q}. $$
Some compatible instance therefore satisfies
$$ \frac{W(x)}{\mathrm{OPT}} \leq 1-\frac1{2k} + \frac1{2k(N-q)}. $$
This is strictly smaller than $\rho$ whenever $q<N-1/\alpha$. Consequently,
$$ Q\geq \max\left\{0,\left\lceil N-\frac1\alpha\right\rceil\right\}. $$

4.2. The full $N$ bound for sufficiently high accuracy

The following argument strengthens the preceding expectation bound.

Theorem 4

If $\tau\geq3$ and
$$ \rho>1-\frac1{2m}, $$
every deterministic guaranteeing algorithm requires at least $N$ queries.

Proof. Suppose its worst-case bound is $q\leq N-1$, and use the same baseline transcript.

The number of ordered balanced partitions is
$$ P=\frac{m!}{(k!)^\tau}. $$
Each query excludes at most $P/N$ partitions. Hence at least
$$ P-\frac{qP}{N} \geq \frac PN = \frac{(m-k)!}{(k!)^{\tau-1}} $$
partitions remain compatible.

When $\tau\geq3$, this last number is at least two: after fixing $O_1$, there are at least two ways to partition the remainder, obtained, for example, by exchanging an item between two remaining nonempty parts.

Choose distinct compatible partitions $O$ and $\widetilde O$. They differ in at least two rows, because if all but one part agreed, the final parts would also agree.

For a row where they differ, the row-sum constraint gives
$$ y_{t,O_t}+y_{t,\widetilde O_t}\leq1. $$
For a row where they agree, the corresponding contribution to
$H(O)+H(\widetilde O)$ is at most two. Therefore
$$ H(O)+H(\widetilde O)\leq2\tau-2. $$
At least one of these two compatible instances has $H\leq\tau-1$. On that instance,
$$ W(x) \leq k-\frac12+\frac{\tau-1}{2\tau} = k-\frac1{2\tau}, $$
and hence
$$ \frac{W(x)}{\mathrm{OPT}} \leq 1-\frac1{2k\tau} = 1-\frac1{2m} < \rho. $$
This contradicts the guarantee. ∎

This establishes the literal lower bound, including for exact optimization, whenever $\tau\geq3$.

5. Exact optimization at $\epsilon=1$: a stronger lower bound

The previous family leaves an elimination loophole when $\tau=2$: all but one candidate bundle can identify the last candidate. The construction below removes that loophole by allowing both upward and downward changes.

Theorem 5

For $\epsilon=1$ and $\rho=1$, every deterministic algorithm requires at least
$$ R=\binom{m}{\lfloor m/2\rfloor} $$
queries on the full promised class.

Proof. Put
$$ r=\lfloor m/2\rfloor, \qquad s=m-r. $$
Since $m\geq2$, both are positive. Define the cardinality valuation
$$ f_n(S)= \begin{cases} |S|,&|S|\leq n-1,\\ n-\frac14,&|S|=n,\\ n,&|S|\geq n+1. \end{cases} $$
Its marginal sequence is
$$ 1,\quad\frac34,\quad\frac14,\quad0, $$
with absent ranges omitted. Thus it is normalized, monotone, and submodular.

Index items by $1,\ldots,m$ and set
$$ a_j=\frac{2^{j-1}}{100\cdot2^m}, \qquad a(S)=\sum_{j\in S}a_j. $$
Distinct subsets have distinct $a(S)$, and
$$ 0\leq a(S)<\frac1{100}. $$

The baseline valuations of the first two types are
$$ v_1(S)=f_r(S)+a(S), \qquad v_2(S)=f_s(S). $$
If $\tau>2$, give every other type the additive valuation
$$ v_t(S)=\frac{t}{10\tau}|S|. $$

Let $B$ be the unique subset of cardinality $r$ maximizing $a(S)$. For every subset $D$ of cardinality $r$, define a variant changing only the first type:
$$ v_1^D(S) = f_r(S)+a(S)+\sigma_D\frac1{16}\mathbf1[S=D], $$
where
$$ \sigma_D= \begin{cases} -1,&D=B,\\ 1,&D\ne B. \end{cases} $$

Valuation properties of every variant

Before adding the modular term, the possible marginals have ranges
$$ \begin{array}{c|c} \text{current cardinality}&\text{marginal range}\\ \hline \text{at most }r-2&\{1\}\\ r-1&[11/16,13/16]\\ r&[3/16,5/16]\\ \text{at least }r+1&\{0\}. \end{array} $$
These ranges are nonnegative and decrease with cardinality. Thus every signed variant is monotone and submodular by diminishing returns. Adding a nonnegative modular function preserves both properties. Normalization holds because $D$ is nonempty.

Unique continuous optima

Use uniform prices $p_j=1/2$.

For $f_n$, utility is maximized exactly at cardinality $n$, with value
$$ \frac n2-\frac14. $$
Every other cardinality has utility at most
$$ \frac n2-\frac12. $$
The modular term is smaller than $1/100$, and the signed change is confined to cardinality $r$. Consequently, type $1$ always has its utility maximum at cardinality $r$.

In the baseline, its unique utility maximizer is $B$. If $D\ne B$, the positive change makes $D$ the unique maximizer, because
$$ \frac1{16}>\frac1{100}. $$
If $D=B$, the negative change makes the unique second-largest subset under $a(S)$ the maximizer. In all variants, denote the resulting unique maximizer by $C$; then $C\ne B$.

Type $2$ maximizes utility exactly on all subsets of cardinality $s$. Every remaining type uniquely maximizes utility at the empty bundle, because its additive coefficient is smaller than $1/2$.

The partition assigning $C$ to type $1$, $M\setminus C$ to type $2$, and empty bundles to the remaining types attains the price upper bound.

Equality forces type $1$ to receive only $C$ and every remaining type after type $2$ to receive only the empty bundle. Type $1$ exhausts the supply of each item in $C$. Thus type $2$ can use only subsets of $M\setminus C$; its utility-maximizing bundles have cardinality $s=|M\setminus C|$, so it must receive exactly $M\setminus C$.

This proves uniqueness of the continuous optimum. The baseline has the corresponding unique partition with first bundle $B$. Since $\epsilon=1$, Proposition 2 establishes every required split promise.

Indistinguishability

Run an exact algorithm on the baseline. If it makes fewer than $R$ queries, there is a subset $D$ of cardinality $r$ whose value it never queries for type $1$.

The variant indexed by $D$ changes exactly that oracle coordinate. Determinism therefore gives the same transcript and output on the baseline and variant. But their unique optima differ, so that output cannot be optimal on both.

This proves the lower bound. ∎

Because $k=m/\tau\leq m/2$ and binomial coefficients increase up to the middle,
$$ R\geq\binom{m}{k}. $$
The construction has binary reply length
$$ O(m+\log(m+1)+\log(\tau+1)). $$

As a supplementary check, all signed variants were exhaustively checked for submodularity and uniqueness of type $1$’s utility maximizer for $2\leq m\leq6$. The proof above does not depend on this check.

6. Why the remaining cases are still open

The hidden-partition family alone cannot establish the full lower bound throughout the requested approximation range.

Indeed, give each type equal mass on all subsets of cardinality $k$:
$$ x_{t,S}=\frac1{\tau N}\quad(|S|=k). $$
A uniformly chosen such subset contains each item with probability $k/m=1/\tau$. Thus this allocation is feasible. Its welfare on every hidden-partition instance is
$$ W(x)=k-\frac12+\frac1{2N}, $$
so its ratio is
$$ 1-\frac1{2k}+\frac1{2kN}. $$
It uses zero queries. This is only a restricted-family guarantee.

For $\tau=2$, the same family also admits exact identification using $N-1$ queries: query type $1$ on all but one candidate bundle, infer the remaining candidate if necessary, and give the complement to type $2$. This also is only a restricted-family algorithm.

The signed construction in Section 5 handles exact optimization at $\epsilon=1$. It does not establish stability for $\epsilon<1$, and I make no such claim.

7. Full-class upper bound and computational costs

For completeness, exact optimization on the entire promised class is achievable by:

  • Querying every type on every nonempty bundle.
  • Enumerating all $\tau^m$ assignments of items to types.
  • Returning the partition with greatest welfare.

Proposition 1 ensures that a continuous optimum occurs among the enumerated partitions. Normalization supplies empty-bundle values without queries.

The query count is
$$ \tau(2^m-1). $$
Using ordinary exact rational arithmetic, a valid bit-time bound, excluding the oracle’s internal computation, is
$$ O\left( (\tau2^m+\tau^{m+1}) (m+\tau L+\log(\tau+1))^2 \right). $$
This returns the exact optimum for every allowed $\epsilon$ and $\rho$, with no additional dependence on those parameters.

Every query lower bound above also gives the same order of running-time lower bound in a model charging at least one unit per oracle call. The constructions’ reply lengths were stated explicitly; the lower bounds are witnessed at those input sizes.

Thus the full $\binom{m}{k}$ lower bound is now proved in the parameter ranges stated at the beginning, but the complete dichotomy remains open.
