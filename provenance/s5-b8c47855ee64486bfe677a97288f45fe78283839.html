The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite horizon of \(H\) slots, a finite support \(T\) of job types \(\theta=(r_\theta,d_\theta,n_\theta,l_\theta,v_\theta)\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta>0\) summing to \(1\), per-capita capacity \(c\), and welfare threshold \(K\), does there exist a feasible schedule-mass vector \(x_{\theta,s}\ge 0\) with welfare at least \(K\)? Legal start slots satisfy \(r_\theta\le s\le d_\theta-l_\theta\), each type satisfies \(\sum_s x_{\theta,s}\le\mu_\theta\), and each slot \(u\) satisfies \(\sum_{\theta,s:\,s\le u<s+l_\theta} n_\theta x_{\theta,s}\le c\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) job types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau=|T|\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(H\ge1\) slots, indexed by \(0,\ldots,H-1\), and one homogeneous resource with per-capita capacity \(c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\). A type is \(\theta=(r_\theta,d_\theta,n_\theta,l_\theta,v_\theta)\), with integer \(0\le r_\theta\le d_\theta\le H\), positive integers \(n_\theta,l_\theta\), and \(v_\theta\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\). The threshold is \(K\in\mathbb Q\).
All integers are binary encoded, including \(H\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length and \(m=H\) the temporal ground-set size. Express running times in \(m,\tau,L\); membership in P means polynomial time in \(L\).
All types are known in advance. Let \(S_\theta=\{s\in\mathbb Z:r_\theta\le s\le d_\theta-l_\theta\}\). A variable \(x_{\theta,s}\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) represents mass receiving its full demand \(n_\theta\) continuously in slots \(s,\ldots,s+l_\theta-1\); incomplete service earns zero value, and interrupted service must restart.
Feasibility requires \(\sum_{s\in S_\theta}x_{\theta,s}\le\mu_\theta\) for every type and \(\sum_{\theta\in T}\sum_{s\in S_\theta:s\le u<s+l_\theta}n_\theta x_{\theta,s}\le c\) for every slot \(u\). Welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_{\theta\in T}\sum_{s\in S_\theta}v_\theta x_{\theta,s}\).

Is deciding whether a feasible schedule-mass vector satisfies \(W(x)\ge K\) in P, or is it NP-hard?

The discrete offline welfare-threshold problem is NP-complete, already when every job has release time \(0\), deadline \(1\), and length \(1\). [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.1896v1)
No prior complexity classification for this exact normalized mass problem was verified.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The horizon encoding was unspecified; H and all other integers are binary encoded, and P is measured in total input bit length L.
  • The generic ground-set parameter m is identified with H, the number of slots.
  • Slot boundaries, release times, deadlines, and lengths are integers; slots begin at zero and deadlines are completion boundaries.
  • Resource demands and lengths are positive integers; values and per-capita capacity are positive binary rationals, and the threshold is an arbitrary binary rational.
  • Welfare is value per completed unit of mass, summed over types and legal starts.
  • The decision problem is offline: the entire support is available initially.
  • Types with no legal start are permitted and contribute no completed mass; demands may exceed per-capita capacity because the stated capacity constraints apply to mass.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Classification

The problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when all types have release time \(0\), deadline \(H\), the same demand, and positive dyadic masses.

It also admits a \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\)-time algorithm by explicit linear programming. Since \(m=H\) is binary encoded, this does not establish membership in \(\mathrm P\).

Reduction

We reduce from Unbounded Subset Sum: given distinct positive binary-encoded integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_k<B\), decide whether

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{k}a_i z_i=B,\qquad z_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.\)

This decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; its hardness holds under these restrictions. See the introduction and abstract of [Aggarwal, Joux, Santha, and Węgrzycki](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05435v1).

Let \(q\) be the smallest power of two with \(q\ge k\), and put \(N=qB\). Construct a society with \(\tau=k\) types and parameters

\(\displaystyle H=B,\qquad c=1,\qquad K=B,\)

\(\displaystyle \theta_i=(0,B,N,a_i,Na_i)\qquad (1\le i\le k).\)

Set the masses to

\(\displaystyle \mu_i=\frac1q\quad (1\le i<k),\qquad \mu_k=\frac{q-k+1}{q}.\)

These masses are positive dyadic rationals, each is at least \(1/q\), and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_i=\frac{k-1+q-k+1}{q}=1.\)

The distinct lengths ensure that these are distinct support types.

Correctness

A subset-sum solution gives a feasible schedule

Suppose nonnegative integers \(z_i\) satisfy \(\sum_i a_i z_i=B\).

Arrange \(z_i\) intervals of length \(a_i\), for every type \(i\), consecutively in any order. Together they partition the slots \(0,\ldots,B-1\). At each interval's start, assign mass \(1/N\) to its type; set all other variables to zero.

Every interval lies within its type's window. The total mass assigned to type \(i\) is \(z_i/N\). Since every length is at least one,

\(\displaystyle z_i\le \sum_j a_jz_j=B,\)

so

\(\displaystyle \frac{z_i}{N}\le\frac{B}{qB}=\frac1q\le\mu_i.\)

Exactly one assigned interval covers each slot, consuming capacity \(N(1/N)=1\). The schedule is therefore feasible. Its welfare is

\(\displaystyle W(x)=\sum_i Na_i\frac{z_i}{N}=\sum_i a_i z_i=B=K.\)

A feasible schedule meeting the threshold gives a subset-sum solution

Suppose a feasible real schedule-mass vector satisfies \(W(x)\ge B\). Write \(x_{i,s}\) for its variables, extending them by zero outside the admissible start set.

Define the resource load in slot \(u\) by

\(\displaystyle U_u=N\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{s:\,s\le u<s+a_i}x_{i,s}\qquad (0\le u<B).\)

Feasibility gives \(0\le U_u\le1\). Each interval contributes its demand times its length to the sum of slot loads, and its value was chosen to equal exactly that quantity. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle W(x)=\sum_i\sum_s Na_i x_{i,s}=\sum_{u=0}^{B-1}U_u\le B.\)

Thus \(W(x)\ge B\) forces

\(\displaystyle U_u=1\qquad (0\le u<B).\)

For every integer \(t\) with \(0\le t\le B\), define the resource mass starting and ending at \(t\):

\(\displaystyle A_t=N\sum_i x_{i,t},\qquad E_t=N\sum_i x_{i,t-a_i}.\)

Set \(U_{-1}=U_B=0\). Comparing consecutive slot loads gives

\(\displaystyle U_t-U_{t-1}=A_t-E_t\qquad (0\le t\le B).\)

Indeed, intervals starting at \(t\) enter the load, intervals ending at \(t\) leave it, and every other interval contributes equally to both sides of the time boundary. Because lengths are positive and intervals lie inside the horizon, \(E_0=0\) and \(A_B=0\). Hence full occupancy implies

\(\displaystyle A_0=1,\qquad A_t=E_t\quad (1\le t<B),\qquad E_B=1.\)

Now consider the finite directed graph with vertices \(0,\ldots,B\). Whenever \(x_{i,s}>0\), include an arc from \(s\) to \(s+a_i\), carrying positive flow \(Nx_{i,s}\).

Since \(A_0=1\), a positive-flow arc leaves \(0\). If such an arc reaches an interior vertex \(t<B\), then \(E_t>0\). The equality \(A_t=E_t\) guarantees a positive-flow arc leaving \(t\).

Following these arcs must eventually reach \(B\): every arc strictly increases the integer time, every endpoint is at most \(B\), and the path cannot stop at an interior vertex.

Let \(z_i\) count the arcs of length \(a_i\) on this path. These are nonnegative integers, and telescoping the successive endpoints yields

\(\displaystyle \sum_i a_i z_i=B.\)

This proves the reverse implication for arbitrary real schedule masses.

Encoding and running time

The reduction writes only \(k\) types. Since \(q\le2k\), the numbers \(N=qB\) and \(Na_i\) have bit lengths \(O(\log k+\log B)\). All masses have binary encodings of length \(O(\log k)\). Thus the construction takes polynomial time in the source input length; it never enumerates the \(B\) slots.

For a general instance, each type has at most \(m\) admissible starts. The explicit linear program therefore has at most \(\tau m\) variables, \(m+\tau\) capacity and mass constraints, and one welfare-threshold constraint. Its rational encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), so standard exact rational linear programming decides feasibility in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\) time.

For the special case \(H=1\), this is polynomial in \(L\). The hardness proved above arises from the binary-encoded horizon: although masses are divisible, achieving full utilization forces an exact integer composition of that horizon.

The reduction establishes the requested \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness classification. A polynomial-time algorithm in \(L\) would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).
