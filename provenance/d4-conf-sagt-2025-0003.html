The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(n\), sorted rational locations \(x_1<\cdots<x_\tau\), and rational masses \(\mu_j>0\) summing to \(1\) with \(n\mu_j\in\mathbb Z\), can one compute from the support a deterministic two-facility mechanism in time \(\mathrm{poly}(\tau,L)\) that preserves constant approximation, one-person strategyproofness, and the paper’s \(n\)-scaled Lipschitz guarantee?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(P=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j\delta_{x_j}\), with sorted rational locations \(x_1<\cdots<x_\tau\), positive binary rational masses summing to \(1\), and a binary integer \(n\ge2\) satisfying \(n\mu_j\in\mathbb Z\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, \(L\) is the total input bit length, and the number of facilities is fixed at \(m=2\).
An anonymous deterministic mechanism \(M_n\) returns a lexicographically ordered pair \(T=(p_1,p_2)\) of facilities in \(\mathbb R^2\), allowing coincident facilities. Define \(d(x,T)=\min_{\ell\in\{1,2\}}\|(x,0)-p_\ell\|_1\), with service ties assigned to the first facility, \(C(P,T)=\sum_j\mu_jd(x_j,T)\), and \(\operatorname{OPT}(P)=\min_T C(P,T)\).
Require absolute constants \(A,B,K\ge1\), independent of all inputs, such that \(C(P,M_n(P))\le A\operatorname{OPT}(P)\), including when the optimum is zero.
One-person constant-strategyproofness means \(d(x_j,M_n(P))\le B\,d(x_j,M_n(P-n^{-1}\delta_{x_j}+n^{-1}\delta_y))\) for every supported location \(x_j\) and every report \(y\in\mathbb R\); only mass \(1/n\) moves.
For any two societies \(P,Q\) with the same \(n\), require \(\sum_{\ell=1}^2\|M_n(P)_\ell-M_n(Q)_\ell\|_1\le KnW_1(P,Q)\), where \(W_1\) uses absolute distance on the line. These guarantees apply also to real locations; computation is required on rational inputs, with exact rational facility coordinates as output.

Can such a mechanism be evaluated from the support in time \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), or is this computation NP-hard?

For explicitly listed agents, a deterministic mechanism with constant approximation, constant-strategyproofness, and constant Lipschitz continuity in the sorted-profile \(\ell_1\) distance is known ([Fullerton, Hu, and Plaxton](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04485v3)).
No result settling the computation with binary multiplicities and support-based running time was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Interpreted one-person strategyproofness as multiplicative constant-strategyproofness, matching the anchor, with a deviation moving exactly mass 1/n rather than an entire type.
  • Specified Manhattan service distance, planar facilities, lexicographic facility ordering, and first-facility service ties.
  • Expressed the sorted-profile Lipschitz guarantee as K n W_1(P,Q) for societies with the same n.
  • Used normalized mass-weighted social cost and the optimal two-facility cost as the approximation benchmark.
  • Required n at least two and retained singleton supports, coincident facilities, and zero-optimum instances.
  • Included binary n in the total input bit length and fixed m at two facilities; required exact rational output on rational inputs while retaining guarantees for arbitrary real reports.

A polynomial-time support-based evaluation

The intended existence question is resolved positively. The mechanism \(M_4\) of Fullerton, Hu, and Plaxton can be evaluated exactly from the support in time \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\). Binary multiplicities need not be expanded.

We prove this by giving a compressed evaluation algorithm and verifying that its output is exactly the published mechanism.

1. The mechanism and the theorem being used

Write \(c=\operatorname{OPT}(P)\) and \(b=\sum_j\mu_jx_j\). Define \(h_1,h_2\) by

\(h_1\ge x_1,\qquad \sum_j\mu_j\max(0,h_1-x_j)=c,\)

\(h_2\le x_\tau,\qquad \sum_j\mu_j\max(0,x_j-h_2)=c.\)

Put \(f_1=\min(h_1,b)\), \(f_2=\max(h_2,b)\), and \(\Delta=f_2-f_1\). If \(\Delta=0\), set \(\xi_1=\xi_2=0\). Otherwise define

\(u_j=\dfrac{\max(0,\min(f_2,x_j)-f_1)}{\Delta},\qquad v_2=\sum_j\mu_ju_j,\qquad v_1=1-v_2,\)

\(\xi_\ell=\min(\Delta,2c/v_\ell).\)

The mechanism returns

\(M_n(P)=\bigl((f_1,\max(8c,\xi_1)),(f_2,\max(8c,\xi_2))\bigr).\)

These are the normalized formulas for \(M_4\). The published result gives approximation factor \(15\) and absolute constants \(B_0,\kappa\) for strategyproofness and Lipschitz continuity on every real sorted agent profile. Its conventions for facility ordering, distance, and service ties agree with those in the question. [Fullerton–Hu–Plaxton, Sections 3.2 and 4.2, Theorem 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04485v3#S4.SS2).

The remainder proves the computational claim and verifies the transfer of that theorem to the present input representation.

2. Computing the optimum from support pairs

We first establish

\(c=\min_{1\le r\le s\le\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j\min(|x_j-x_r|,|x_j-x_s|).\)

Proof. Take any facility pair in the plane. Projecting both facilities onto the horizontal axis weakly decreases every service distance.

For the resulting pair, assign each support point to a nearest facility, using the prescribed tie rule. For each nonempty assigned group, replace its facility by a weighted median of that group. Such a median can be chosen at a support location and minimizes the group's sum of weighted absolute distances: between consecutive group locations, the slope of that sum is the weight strictly to the left minus the weight strictly to the right. At a weighted median, these slopes change from nonpositive to nonnegative.

If a group is empty, place its facility at any support location. The replacements do not increase the cost of the fixed assignment. Allowing agents to choose their nearest replacement facility can only decrease it further.

Consequently, every facility pair has cost at least that of some pair of support locations. Conversely, every support pair is an admissible facility pair. Taking the minimum over the finite collection proves the formula. \(\square\)

There are \(O(\tau^2)\) candidate pairs, each evaluated using \(O(\tau)\) arithmetic operations. Thus this step uses \(O(\tau^3)\) operations, independently of the magnitude of \(n\).

3. Solving the defining equations exactly

The function

\(G_1(t)=\sum_j\mu_j\max(0,t-x_j)\)

is continuous, satisfies \(G_1(x_1)=0\), and tends to infinity as \(t\) tends to infinity. It is strictly increasing on \([x_1,\infty)\): for \(t'>t\ge x_1\), the first summand alone increases by \(\mu_1(t'-t)>0\). Hence the equation defining \(h_1\) has exactly one solution.

Similarly,

\(G_2(t)=\sum_j\mu_j\max(0,x_j-t)\)

is continuous and strictly decreasing on \((-\infty,x_\tau]\), ranges from infinity to zero, and therefore uniquely defines \(h_2\).

These roots require only linear equations. For \(1\le k\le\tau\), let

\(A_k=\sum_{j\le k}\mu_j,\qquad Z_k=\sum_{j\le k}\mu_jx_j.\)

On \([x_k,x_{k+1}]\), with the final interval extending to infinity, we have \(G_1(t)=A_kt-Z_k\). Therefore the root candidate is

\(h_1=\dfrac{c+Z_k}{A_k}.\)

Scan the intervals and select a candidate that belongs to its interval. Uniqueness guarantees that all accepted candidates agree, including when the root is a breakpoint.

For \(h_2\), use suffix sums

\(A^k=\sum_{j\ge k}\mu_j,\qquad Z^k=\sum_{j\ge k}\mu_jx_j.\)

On \([x_{k-1},x_k]\), with the first interval extending to negative infinity, the candidate is

\(h_2=\dfrac{Z^k-c}{A^k}.\)

Both scans use \(O(\tau)\) arithmetic operations after computing prefix and suffix sums.

All subsequent quantities are obtained by rational arithmetic and comparisons. We must also check that their denominators are nonzero. Since placing both facilities at \(x_\tau\) is feasible,

\(c\le G_1(x_\tau),\)

so \(h_1\le x_\tau\). Similarly, \(h_2\ge x_1\). Together with the mean bounds, this gives

\(x_1\le f_1\le b\le f_2\le x_\tau.\)

When \(\Delta>0\), we have \(u_1=0\) and \(u_\tau=1\). Consequently,

\(v_1\ge\mu_1>0,\qquad v_2\ge\mu_\tau>0.\)

Thus every division in the mechanism is valid. When \(\Delta=0\), that branch performs no division by \(\Delta\) or by either \(v_\ell\).

The facilities are lexicographically ordered: if \(\Delta>0\), their first coordinates are strictly ordered; otherwise their first coordinates coincide and their heights both equal \(8c\).

4. Polynomial bit complexity

A polynomial number of arithmetic operations is insufficient without controlling operand sizes. Here those sizes are polynomial as well.

Set \(a_j=n\mu_j\), which is a positive integer by hypothesis. Compute these integers directly from the binary input; no agent expansion is required.

Write \(x_j=p_j/q_j\), with positive integer denominators, and choose

\(D=\prod_{j=1}^{\tau}q_j,\qquad z_j=Dx_j\in\mathbb Z.\)

The integers \(D,z_j,a_j\) have \(O(L)\) bits. Compute the integer

\(H=\min_{r\le s}\sum_j a_j\min(|z_j-z_r|,|z_j-z_s|).\)

Then \(c=H/(nD)\). Every candidate sum has \(O(L)\) bits because it is bounded by \(n(z_\tau-z_1)\).

The scaled roots satisfy

\(Dh_1=\dfrac{H+\sum_{j\le k}a_jz_j}{\sum_{j\le k}a_j},\qquad Dh_2=\dfrac{\sum_{j\ge k}a_jz_j-H}{\sum_{j\ge k}a_j}.\)

The scaled mean is \(Db=\sum_ja_jz_j/n\). Each numerator has \(O(L)\) bits, and each denominator is a positive integer at most \(n\). Hence the selected scaled coordinates \(s_1=Df_1\) and \(s_2=Df_2\) also have \(O(L)\)-bit rational representations.

For completeness, consider the heights when \(s_2>s_1\). Choose a common denominator \(q\) for \(s_1,s_2\) by multiplying their denominators. Write

\(s_1=\alpha/q,\qquad s_2=\beta/q,\qquad d=\beta-\alpha>0.\)

All these integers have \(O(L)\) bits. Define

\(t_j=\max(0,\min(d,qz_j-\alpha)),\)

\(N_2=\sum_ja_jt_j,\qquad N_1=nd-N_2.\)

Then \(u_j=t_j/d\), and

\(v_\ell=\dfrac{N_\ell}{nd},\qquad \dfrac{2c}{v_\ell}=\dfrac{2Hd}{DN_\ell},\qquad \Delta=\dfrac{d}{qD}.\)

The positivity proved above gives \(N_1,N_2>0\). Moreover, \(N_\ell\le nd\), so these integers and all height numerators and denominators have \(O(L)\) bits.

Thus the complete evaluation uses \(O(\tau^3)\) exact arithmetic operations on polynomial-size operands. Standard integer arithmetic implements it in \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\) bit time, with exact rational output.

5. Transfer of the three guarantees

Equality with the expanded mechanism

Let \(\pi(P)\) be the sorted profile containing \(a_j\) copies of \(x_j\). This profile is used only in the proof.

Its unnormalized optimal cost is \(nc\), and its mean is \(b\). In the published defining equations, replacing sums over agents by multiplicity-weighted sums and dividing by \(n\) gives exactly the equations for \(h_1,h_2\) above.

Likewise, the published fractional counts are \(nv_\ell\). Its vertical terms therefore become

\(\dfrac{8(nc)}{n}=8c,\qquad \dfrac{2(nc)}{nv_\ell}=\dfrac{2c}{v_\ell}.\)

It follows that

\(M_n(P)=M_4(\pi(P)).\)

This identity holds for real locations as well as rational locations.

Approximation and one-person strategyproofness

The expanded profile has exactly \(n\ge2\) agents, so the published theorem applies. Dividing its social-cost inequality by \(n\) gives

\(C(P,M_n(P))\le15\operatorname{OPT}(P).\)

Moving mass \(1/n\) from a supported location to any real report \(y\) changes exactly one agent report in the expanded profile. Thus its strategyproofness inequality gives precisely the required inequality, with \(B=\max(1,B_0)\).

Zero optimum is included. Indeed, positive masses imply \(c=0\) exactly when \(\tau\le2\). For one support point, the mechanism places both facilities there. For two support points, it places one at each, with both heights zero.

The required Wasserstein inequality

For two societies with the same \(n\), write their expanded sorted profiles as \(\pi,\rho\). We claim

\(\|\pi-\rho\|_1=nW_1(P,Q).\)

To justify this, let \(F_P,F_Q\) be their cumulative distribution functions. The identity

\(|u-v|=\int_{\mathbb R}|\mathbf1_{\{u\le t\}}-\mathbf1_{\{v\le t\}}|\,dt\)

shows that every coupling has cost at least \(\int_{\mathbb R}|F_P(t)-F_Q(t)|\,dt\), by the inequality between the absolute value of an expectation and the expectation of an absolute value.

The coupling pairing the \(i\)-th sorted agent with the \(i\)-th sorted agent attains this bound. For every \(t\), the indices satisfying \(\pi_i\le t\) and those satisfying \(\rho_i\le t\) are initial segments. Their symmetric difference therefore has size equal to the difference of their sizes. Integrating yields the claimed identity.

Applying the published Lipschitz bound now gives

\(\sum_{\ell=1}^2\|M_n(P)_\ell-M_n(Q)_\ell\|_1 \le\kappa\|\pi-\rho\|_1 =\kappa nW_1(P,Q).\)

Choose \(K=\max(1,\kappa)\).

6. Conclusion

There exists an anonymous deterministic mechanism satisfying every stated guarantee, with absolute constants \(A=15\), \(B\), and \(K\), whose exact rational evaluation takes \(\operatorname{poly}(\tau,L)\) time. This settles the support-based existence question for fixed \(m=2\).
