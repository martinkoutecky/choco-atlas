The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite supported type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), a source \(s\), and symmetric nonnegative rational type-pair costs \(c_{tu}\) and binary qualities \(q_{tu}\), partition an atomless population \(X\) into type classes \(X_t\) of measure \(\mu_t\). Find a measurable parent map \(p\) to other individuals or \(s\), with every parent path reaching \(s\) in finitely many steps, that lexicographically maximizes \(\left(\int_X \prod_{e\text{ on }x\text{’s path}}q_e\,dx,\;-\int_X c_{\theta(x),\theta(p(x))}\,dx\right)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\geq1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Let \(m=\tau+1\) count the type labels together with the source label, and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Take \(X=[0,1)\) with Lebesgue measure, partitioned into consecutive intervals \(X_t\) of lengths \(\mu_t\). Write \(\theta(x)=t\) on \(X_t\), and give the external source \(s\) its own label \(\theta(s)=s\).
Input symmetric costs \(c_{tu}\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) and qualities \(q_{tu}\in\{0,1\}\) for labels in \(T\cup\{s\}\). Every pair of distinct individuals, and every individual–source pair, may be linked. Entries \(c_{tt},q_{tt}\) govern links between distinct individuals of the same type; self-links are forbidden.
An admissible network is a Borel measurable map \(p:X\to X\cup\{s\}\) with \(p(x)\ne x\), such that every individual's iterated parent path reaches \(s\) after finitely many steps. There is no bound on the number of children, and individuals of measure zero may be parents.
For that finite path \(P_p(x)\), define \(H(p)=\int_X\prod_{(y,z)\in P_p(x)}q_{\theta(y),\theta(z)}\,dx\) and \(C(p)=\int_Xc_{\theta(x),\theta(p(x))}\,dx\). The objective is to maximize \((H(p),-C(p))\) lexicographically; ties require no further selection.
Use its threshold decision version: given binary rational \(h\in[0,1]\) and \(b\geq0\), does an admissible \(p\) satisfy \((H(p),-C(p))\geq_{\mathrm{lex}}(h,-b)\)?

Is this continuous lexicographic threshold problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite societies, maximizing the number receiving high quality and then minimizing total edge cost is polynomial-time solvable by a modified Prim algorithm ([Subiza–Peris](https://web.ua.es/es/dmcte/documentos/qmetwp1902.pdf)).
No result settling the exact atomless parent-map problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Made the atomless space concrete as the Lebesgue interval with consecutive type intervals, and used Borel measurable parent maps to fix the measurable structure.
  • Included source–type costs and qualities by assigning the external source a separate label; the source carries no population mass.
  • Specified that same-type entries describe distinct individuals, rather than importing the discrete individual's zero self-edge convention into type-level entries.
  • Retained complete availability of links, prohibited self-parenting, and imposed finite termination for every individual, including individuals of measure zero.
  • Allowed unrestricted numbers of children and parents of measure zero, since the discrete network definition imposes no capacity restriction.
  • Used lexicographic threshold feasibility as the decision formulation of the optimization question, with binary rational thresholds and no additional tie-breaking.
  • Defined the otherwise unused parameter m as the number of supported type labels plus the source, and included all rational data and thresholds in L.

Result.

The problem is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\). Moreover, its lexicographic optimum is attained by a Borel parent map whose paths have uniformly bounded length.

The decisive feature is that finitely many representative individuals can supply all necessary connecting paths while their outgoing edges contribute zero to the cost integral.

1. Computing the maximum quality.

Let \(G_1\) be the undirected graph on \(T\cup\{s\}\) whose edges join distinct labels \(t,u\) satisfying \(q_{tu}=1\). Let \(R\subseteq T\) consist of the type labels in the connected component of \(s\). Define

\(H_*=\sum_{t\in R}\mu_t.\)

For any admissible map \(p\), an individual receiving high quality has a finite parent path on which every edge has quality one. The labels along this path give a walk from that individual's type to \(s\). Consecutive repetitions of a label can be deleted, so its type belongs to \(R\). Consequently,

\(H(p)\leq H_*.\)

The quality indicator is measurable: extend \(p\) by \(p(s)=s\); the sets on which the first arrival at \(s\) occurs at each fixed iteration are Borel, and the finite quality product is Borel on each such set.

We will construct a map attaining \(H_*\).

2. The exact minimum cost at maximum quality.

For \(t\in R\), define

\(a_t=\min\{c_{tu}:u\in R\cup\{s\},\ q_{tu}=1\}.\)

This minimum exists: a path in \(G_1\) from \(t\) to \(s\) provides at least one eligible neighbor. The minimum includes \(u=t\) when \(q_{tt}=1\).

For \(t\notin R\), define

\(d_t=\min\{c_{tu}:u\in T\cup\{s\}\}.\)

Here too \(u=t\) is allowed: it represents a link between distinct individuals of type \(t\).

Set

\(C_*=\sum_{t\in R}\mu_ta_t+\sum_{t\notin R}\mu_td_t.\)

We first prove that every admissible \(p\) satisfying \(H(p)=H_*\) has \(C(p)\geq C_*\).

Write \(Q_p(x)\in\{0,1\}\) for the quality product along the parent path. Individuals outside the types in \(R\) always have \(Q_p(x)=0\), as proved above. Thus equality of quality masses implies

\(\sum_{t\in R}\int_{X_t}(1-Q_p(x))\,dx=H_*-H(p)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative. Therefore almost every individual of each type \(t\in R\) receives high quality.

For such an individual, its first edge has quality one. If its parent is an individual, that parent's remaining path also has quality one, so the parent's type belongs to \(R\). Hence its first-edge cost is at least \(a_t\). For every individual of type \(t\notin R\), its first-edge cost is at least \(d_t\), regardless of quality. Integrating proves

\(C(p)\geq C_*.\)

3. A Borel map attaining both bounds.

Choose one representative \(r_t\in X_t\) for every supported type. This is possible because \(\mu_t>0\). Put \(r_s=s\).

Choose a spanning tree of the component \(R\cup\{s\}\) in \(G_1\), rooted at \(s\). For \(t\in R\), let \(\pi(t)\) be the next label toward \(s\) in this tree. Define the representatives' parents by

\(p(r_t)=r_{\pi(t)}\quad(t\in R),\qquad p(r_t)=s\quad(t\notin R).\)

When \(R\) is empty, the rooted tree consists only of \(s\).

For each \(t\in R\), choose a label \(u_t\) attaining \(a_t\), and define

\(p(x)=r_{u_t}\quad\text{for }x\in X_t\setminus\{r_t\}.\)

For each \(t\notin R\), choose a label \(v_t\) attaining \(d_t\), and define

\(p(x)=r_{v_t}\quad\text{for }x\in X_t\setminus\{r_t\}.\)

We verify all requirements:

  • Borel measurability. The map is constant on each Borel set \(X_t\setminus\{r_t\}\), with separately specified values at finitely many representatives.
  • No self-links. An ordinary individual maps to a representative or \(s\), never to itself. A representative in \(R\) maps to a distinct label along the rooted tree; every other representative maps to \(s\).
  • Finite termination. Every representative reaches \(s\), either through the rooted tree or directly. Every ordinary individual reaches a representative or \(s\) in one step. Thus every path has length at most \(\tau+1\).
  • Maximum quality. Representatives of types in \(R\) have entirely quality-one paths. Every ordinary individual of such a type has a quality-one first edge to one of these representatives or to \(s\). Individuals of types outside \(R\) cannot receive high quality by Section 1. Therefore \(H(p)=H_*\).
  • Exact cost. Outside the finite set of representatives, the first-edge cost is \(a_t\) on each type in \(R\) and \(d_t\) on every other type. The representatives have measure zero, so \(C(p)=C_*\).

This construction also explains why same-type minima are legitimate despite the prohibition on self-links: an ordinary individual can link to its distinct representative.

Together with the lower bound, the construction proves that the attained lexicographic optimum is exactly \((H_*,-C_*)\).

4. Decision algorithm and bit complexity.

The threshold answer is determined as follows:

  • If \(h<H_*\), answer yes.
  • If \(h>H_*\), answer no.
  • If \(h=H_*\), answer yes exactly when \(C_*\leq b\).

Indeed, strict improvement in quality satisfies the lexicographic threshold regardless of cost. At equal maximum quality, Section 2 gives the necessary cost bound and Section 3 attains it.

The component \(R\) is computable by graph traversal. Computing all minima requires at most \(O(m^2)\) rational comparisons. Computing \(H_*\), \(C_*\), and the final comparisons uses exact rational arithmetic.

These operations have polynomial bit complexity. Each selected input rational has bit length at most \(L\); products and sums of at most \(\tau\) such terms can be computed with intermediate bit lengths bounded by \(O(\tau L)\), using ordinary integer arithmetic. No enumeration of individuals or expansion of rational masses is required.

Therefore the exact atomless threshold problem belongs to \(\mathrm{P}\).
