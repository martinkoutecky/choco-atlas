The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), indexed team templates \(\mathcal E\), integer requirements \(a_{te}\ge 0\), and strict preferences \(\succ_t\) over each type's incident templates, with at most \(3\) templates per type, find a stable allocation \(x_e\ge 0\). Under the promise that one exists, require \(\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}a_{te}x_e\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\), and for each template \(e\), some \(t\) with \(a_{te}>0\) must satisfy \(\sum_{f\in\mathcal E(t):\,f=e\text{ or }f\succ_t e}a_{tf}x_f=\mu_t\). What is the computational complexity of finding such an allocation?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\ge 1\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types.
There are \(m=|\mathcal E|\) indexed team templates, with binary-encoded integer requirements \(a_{te}\ge 0\). Every template requires at least one type. Define \(\mathcal E(t)=\{e\in\mathcal E:a_{te}>0\}\); each type has a strict total preference order \(\succ_t\) on \(\mathcal E(t)\), and \(|\mathcal E(t)|\le 3\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation assigns a nonnegative intensity \(x_e\) to each template, consuming \(a_{te}x_e\) mass of type \(t\). It is feasible when \(\sum_{e\in\mathcal E(t)}a_{te}x_e\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\).
It is stable when every \(e\in\mathcal E\) has some \(t\) with \(a_{te}>0\) such that \(\sum_{f\in\mathcal E(t):\,f=e\text{ or }f\succ_t e}a_{tf}x_f=\mu_t\). There are no additional template capacities. Given the promise of existence, find an exact rational stable allocation.

Is this search problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard under polynomial-time search reductions?

For finite vertices with unit capacities and unit incidence requirements, finding a stable fractional hypergraph matching is PPAD-complete at maximum degree three and polynomial-time solvable at maximum degree two. [Ishizuka–Kamiyama](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2018.11)
For continuous allocations, existence with heterogeneous capacities and contributions is established for the broader stable-allocation model; this existence result supplies no polynomial-time bound. [Bíró–Fleiner](https://real.mtak.hu/26264/7/BF16do_last.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Excluded templates with no positively required type, since stability requires a witness belonging to each template.
  • Specified binary encoding of requirements and defined m as the number of templates and L as the total input bit length.
  • Specified exact rational output to make the continuous search problem a finite computational task.
  • Made explicit that templates have no additional capacities, following the supplied feasibility and stability conditions.

1. Definitions and a characterization of stability

If $m=0$, the empty allocation is stable. Henceforth assume $m\ge 1$.

Write $A=(a_{te})$, and define
$$ P=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{m}:Ax\le\mu\}. $$
For $t\in T$, let $d_t=|\mathcal E(t)|$. For an incident template $e$, define its preference rank by
$$ r_t(e)=1+|\{f\in\mathcal E(t):f\succ_t e\}|. $$
Thus rank $1$ is best. Define the prefix consumption
$$ S_{te}(x)= \sum_{\substack{f\in\mathcal E(t)\\r_t(f)\le r_t(e)}}a_{tf}x_f. $$
A type $t$ is saturated at $x$ if $(Ax)_t=\mu_t$.

Lemma. For $x\in P$ and $e\in\mathcal E(t)$, the equality $S_{te}(x)=\mu_t$ holds exactly when $t$ is saturated and every template strictly worse than $e$ at $t$ has zero intensity.

Proof. Feasibility and nonnegativity give
$$ S_{te}(x)\le (Ax)_t\le\mu_t. $$
If the prefix equals $\mu_t$, both inequalities are equalities. Consequently,
$$ \sum_{\substack{f\in\mathcal E(t)\\r_t(f)>r_t(e)}}a_{tf}x_f=0. $$
Every summand is nonnegative, and every coefficient appearing in this sum is positive. Hence each corresponding $x_f$ is zero.

Conversely, saturation and these zero intensities imply that the prefix contains all consumption at $t$, so it equals $\mu_t$. $\square$

2. Membership in $\mathrm{PPAD}$ and totality

I use the following known computational form of Scarf’s lemma. Let $B,C$ be rational matrices with $r$ rows and $n>r$ columns, and let $b$ be a nonnegative rational vector. Suppose the first $r$ columns of $B$ form the identity, the nonnegative feasibility set is bounded, and
$$ C_{ii}\le C_{ik}\le C_{ij} \qquad (i,j\le r,\ i\ne j,\ k>r). $$
The computational problem is in $\mathrm{PPAD}$: it returns a rational feasible vector $z$, supported on a set $J$ of $r$ columns, such that every column $k$ is dominated:
$$ \text{for some row }i,\qquad C_{ik}\le C_{ij}\quad\text{for every }j\in J. $$
Degeneracy and repeated ordinal entries are permitted. This is the computational Scarf theorem in [Kintali, Theorem 1.1, Theorem 3.5, and Corollary 3.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.1601).

Introduce slack variables $s_t$, and set
$$ B=[I_\tau\mid A],\qquad z=(s,x),\qquad b=\mu. $$
Then
$$ Bz=b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_t+\sum_e a_{te}x_e=\mu_t \quad(t\in T). $$
This system is feasible: take $s=\mu$ and $x=0$.

It is bounded. Indeed, $0\le s_t\le\mu_t$. For every template $e$, choose an incident type $t$; then
$$ 0\le x_e\le\frac{\mu_t}{a_{te}}\le 1. $$
Here $\mu_t\le 1$ follows from normalization, and $a_{te}\ge 1$ follows from positive integer incidence.

Define $C$ in row $t$ by:

  • The own slack column $s_t$ has value $0$.
  • Every other slack column has value $5$.
  • An incident template $e$ has value $d_t-r_t(e)+1$.
  • A nonincident template has value $4$.

Incident values belong to $\{1,2,3\}$, with larger values representing better templates. Thus all required ordinal inequalities hold. Both matrices have polynomial encoding size.

Let $(s,x)$ be the feasible vector returned by computational Scarf. Domination with respect to $J$ implies domination with respect to its subset $\operatorname{supp}(s,x)$.

Fix a template $e$, and let $t$ dominate its column. This row must be incident to $e$. Otherwise its value for $e$ would be $4$, whereas
$$ s_t+\sum_{f\in\mathcal E(t)}a_{tf}x_f=\mu_t>0 $$
forces either a positive own slack, with ordinal value $0$, or a positive incident intensity, with ordinal value at most $3$. Either contradicts domination.

Because $t$ is incident to $e$, domination forces $s_t=0$. It also forces every incident intensity strictly worse than $e$ to vanish. The row equation and the lemma therefore yield
$$ S_{te}(x)=\mu_t. $$
Every template has such a witness, so $x$ is stable.

This proves both $\mathrm{PPAD}$ membership and existence for every well-formed input. In particular, the existence promise introduces no additional restriction.

3. Polynomial-size exact rational solutions

The polytope $P$ is nonempty, closed, and bounded. Suppose $x$ is stable, and select a witness $w(e)$ for every template. Define
$$ F_w= P\cap \bigcap_{e\in\mathcal E} \{y:S_{w(e),e}(y)=\mu_{w(e)}\}. $$
Each prefix inequality is valid throughout $P$. Its equality set is a face: equality at a strict convex combination forces equality at both endpoints. Thus $F_w$ is a nonempty face of $P$.

Every point of $F_w$ is stable. A nonempty bounded polytope has a vertex, and a vertex of a face is a vertex of the original polytope. Therefore some vertex of $P$ is stable.

For an explicit encoding bound, write $\mu_t=p_t/q_t$ and set
$$ Q=\prod_{t\in T}q_t, \qquad H=\max\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{a_{te}:t\in T,e\in\mathcal E\}\bigr). $$
At a vertex, choose $m$ linearly independent active constraints, giving
$$ Mx=b. $$
Such constraints exist: otherwise a nonzero direction annihilating every active normal would permit sufficiently small feasible perturbations in both directions, contradicting extremality.

The matrix $M$ is integral. Each capacity row has at most three nonzero entries, bounded by $H$; nonnegativity rows have a single entry of absolute value $1$. Every row therefore has Euclidean norm at most $2H$. Hadamard’s determinant inequality gives
$$ 1\le|\det M|\le(2H)^m. $$
The vector $Qb$ is integral. Cramer’s rule consequently expresses each coordinate as an integer divided by $Q\det M$. After reduction, its denominator is at most
$$ Q(2H)^m. $$
Because $0\le x_e\le 1$, its reduced numerator is no larger than its denominator.

Thus each coordinate requires
$$ O\bigl(\log Q+m\log(2H)\bigr) = O\bigl(L+m(L+1)\bigr) $$
bits. The whole allocation has polynomial encoding length.

Feasibility and stability of a rational allocation can be checked in polynomial time by exact rational arithmetic.

4. $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hardness under the mass normalization

The source problem is stable fractional hypergraph matching with strict preferences, unit vertex capacities, unit incidence requirements, and maximum vertex degree three. Its stability condition is the same prefix-saturation equality used here. It is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete by [Ishizuka–Kamiyama, Section 2 and Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2018.11).

Take a source instance with vertex set $V$, nonempty hyperedges $E$, and $n=|V|\ge 1$. Let $N$ be the least power of two with $N\ge n$.

Construct a society with:

  • One type for each original vertex and $N-n$ additional isolated types.
  • Mass $1/N$ for every type.
  • One indexed template for each hyperedge.
  • Requirement $a_{ve}=1$ when $v\in e$, and $0$ otherwise.
  • The original preference orders.

All $N$ types have positive dyadic masses summing to one. Original types have degree at most three, and isolated types have degree zero. The latter are permitted supported types; their preference orders are empty. Every template requires at least one type. Since $N<2n$, the construction has polynomial size.

For a society allocation $x$, define
$$ y_e=Nx_e. $$
At each original vertex $v$,
$$ \sum_{e\ni v}x_e\le\frac1N \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{e\ni v}y_e\le 1. $$
Every corresponding stability equality scales identically:
$$ \sum_{\substack{f\ni v\\f=e\text{ or }f\succ_v e}}x_f=\frac1N \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{\substack{f\ni v\\f=e\text{ or }f\succ_v e}}y_f=1. $$
Isolated types cannot witness template stability. Hence $x$ is stable exactly when $y$ is a stable fractional matching in the source instance.

The construction and decoding are polynomial-time operations, and every target solution decodes correctly. Together with Section 2, this proves $\mathrm{PPAD}$-completeness.

5. Additional result: an exact algorithm parameterized by $\tau$

Proposition. The stable allocations form a union of at most
$$ \prod_{t\in T}(d_t+1)\le 4^\tau $$
explicitly described faces of $P$. Consequently, an exact stable allocation can be found in
$$ 4^\tau\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L) $$
time.

Proof. Choose, independently for each type, an integer
$$ c_t\in\{0,1,\ldots,d_t\}. $$
Interpret $c_t=0$ as imposing no additional condition at that type. For $c_t>0$, impose
$$ (Ax)_t=\mu_t, \qquad x_f=0 \quad\text{whenever }f\in\mathcal E(t),\ r_t(f)>c_t. $$
Let $F_c$ be the resulting subset of $P$. When nonempty, it is a face, because all imposed conditions make valid inequalities tight.

Retain only choices satisfying the following combinatorial covering condition:
$$ \text{for every }e,\text{ some }t\text{ has } a_{te}>0,\quad c_t>0,\quad r_t(e)\ge c_t. $$
Every point of a retained $F_c$ is stable. For the type covering $e$, all consumption lies within ranks at most $c_t$, hence within the prefix for $e$; saturation gives $S_{te}(x)=\mu_t$.

Conversely, let $x$ be any stable allocation. For each unsaturated type choose $c_t=0$. For each saturated type choose
$$ c_t=\max\{r_t(f):f\in\mathcal E(t),\ x_f>0\}. $$
This maximum exists: positive mass and saturation imply positive consumption.

These choices give $x\in F_c$. If $t$ witnesses stability of $e$, the lemma shows that every strictly worse intensity is zero. Therefore $c_t\le r_t(e)$, so the covering condition holds.

This proves the exact union description.

Enumerate all choices $c$, discard those failing the covering condition, and test the remaining $F_c$ for feasibility using rational linear programming. Every such system has polynomial size. The standard polynomial-time theorem for rational linear programming returns an exact rational feasible point when one exists. Section 2 guarantees that at least one retained face is nonempty. $\square$

This uses $\tau$ as the number of supported types. Also,
$$ m\le\sum_{t\in T}d_t\le 3\tau, $$
because every template has at least one incidence. In particular, the algorithm is polynomial-time when $\tau=O(\log(L+2))$.

6. Additional result: an explicit conversion from approximation to exactness

For a feasible allocation $x$, define its stability deficit by
$$ \Delta(x)= \max_{e\in\mathcal E} \min_{t:\,a_{te}>0} \bigl(\mu_t-S_{te}(x)\bigr). $$
All deficits are nonnegative, and $\Delta(x)=0$ exactly when $x$ is stable.

Using $Q,H$ from Section 3, set
$$ D=(2H)^{m+1}, \qquad \varepsilon=\frac{1}{2QD}. $$

Proposition. Given a rational feasible allocation $\widetilde x$ with $\Delta(\widetilde x)\le\varepsilon$, one can find an exact rational stable allocation in polynomial time in the society input and the encoding length of $\widetilde x$.

Proof. For each template $e$, choose an incident type $w(e)$ minimizing its deficit at $\widetilde x$. Solve the rational linear program
$$ \text{minimize }\eta $$
subject to
$$ y\in P,\qquad 0\le\eta\le 1, \qquad S_{w(e),e}(y)+\eta\ge\mu_{w(e)} \quad(e\in\mathcal E). $$
The point $(\widetilde x,\varepsilon)$ is feasible, so the optimum $\eta^*$ satisfies
$$ 0\le\eta^*\le\varepsilon. $$
The feasible set is compact: every $y_e$ lies in $[0,1]$, and $\eta$ lies in $[0,1]$. Thus an optimal vertex exists.

There are $m+1$ variables. At an optimal vertex, choose $m+1$ independent active constraints. Their integral coefficient matrix $M$ has row norms at most $2H$: a prefix constraint has at most three template coefficients and one coefficient of absolute value $1$ for $\eta$, giving
$$ \|M_i\|_2\le\sqrt{3H^2+1}\le 2H. $$
Capacity and bound constraints satisfy the same estimate. Hence
$$ 1\le|\det M|\le(2H)^{m+1}=D. $$
All right-hand sides become integral after multiplication by $Q$. Cramer’s rule therefore implies that, if the vertex’s $\eta$ coordinate is positive, then
$$ \eta^*\ge\frac{1}{Q|\det M|} \ge\frac{1}{QD} =2\varepsilon. $$
This contradicts $\eta^*\le\varepsilon$. Consequently $\eta^*=0$.

Exact rational linear programming returns an optimal solution $(y,0)$. For every template,
$$ S_{w(e),e}(y)\ge\mu_{w(e)}. $$
Feasibility gives the reverse inequality, so equality holds. Thus $y$ is stable. $\square$

The tolerance has polynomial encoding length:
$$ \log_2(1/\varepsilon) = 1+\log_2 Q+(m+1)\log_2(2H). $$
The proposition assumes that the approximate allocation is already supplied; it provides no polynomial-time method for finding it. Indeed, finding a feasible rational allocation with this tolerance is itself $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete: exact stable allocations supply approximate ones, and the proposition decodes every qualifying approximate allocation into an exact solution.
