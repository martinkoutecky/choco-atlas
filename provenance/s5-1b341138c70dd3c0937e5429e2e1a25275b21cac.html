The continuous mirror question, as graded

For an atomless, labelled unit-mass population whose type map has rational masses \(\mu_t\) over a finite set \(T\), with \(v_t(s)\in\{0,1\}\), does there exist a deterministic mechanism computable in time polynomial in \((|T|,L)\) that outputs a measurable pairing and singletons, achieves welfare at least half the optimum over measurable coalition partitions, is acceptable, and is strategyproof against every individual’s unilateral report change? The mechanism must specify how aggregate pairing rates are realized as assignments to individuals.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

The population is \(X=[0,1)\) with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\). A type \(t\) specifies an immutable recipient category \(a(t)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and a private valuation row \(v_t\in\{0,1\}^m\); valuations need not be symmetric. Distinct individuals of the same type may value one another; self-value is zero.
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) positive-mass types and their binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. In the listed order, consecutive half-open intervals of lengths \(\mu_t\) supply the individual labels; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
An admissible coalition partition has measurable blocks, each finite or of positive mass, and a measurable individual utility function. For a finite block \(C\), \(u_x(C)=|C|^{-1}\sum_{y\in C\setminus\{x\}}v_x(a(y))\); for a positive-mass block, \(u_x(C)=\lambda(C)^{-1}\int_C v_x(a(y))\,d\lambda(y)\).
Define \(SW(\mathcal C)=\int_Xu_x(\mathcal C^x)\,d\lambda(x)\) and \(\mathrm{OPT}=\sup_{\mathcal C}SW(\mathcal C)\) over admissible partitions. Acceptability means nonnegative reported welfare at every report profile.
A mechanism uses no payments and is defined on measurable binary-row report profiles, including changes at individual labels. Reports change only the reporter’s valuation row, never their recipient category. Strategyproofness requires \(u_x(\mathcal M(d_{-x},v_x))\geq u_x(\mathcal M(d_{-x},r_x))\) for every label \(x\), every other-report profile, and every binary row \(r_x\), with utilities evaluated using \(v_x\).
A pairing output is specified by a measurable, measure-preserving involution \(p:X\to X\): fixed points are singletons and other orbits are pairs. The computational output must finitely describe the individual assignment, including null-set behavior; aggregate pairing rates alone are insufficient.
Does there exist a deterministic mechanism, constructible in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) on the supported inputs above, that outputs such a pairing, is acceptable and pointwise strategyproof, and always achieves \(SW\geq\mathrm{OPT}/2\)?
For finite simple fractional hedonic games, consistently tie-broken maximum-weight matching gives a polynomial-time deterministic, acceptable, strategyproof \(2\)-approximation. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.09007)
The expanded treatment also establishes a matching-based \(2\)-approximation for finite symmetric fractional hedonic games. [Source](https://ricerca.unich.it/bitstream/11564/795054/3/JAIR%202021%20Strategyproof%20mechanism.pdf) No result settling the exact atomless, pointwise-strategyproof question was located.

Conclusion for the stated benchmark

No such mechanism exists. The obstruction is that admissible finite coalitions need not preserve Lebesgue measure, whereas the required pairing must preserve it. This yields an unbounded approximation gap even with two symmetric types and a piecewise affine individual assignment.

I prove this first. To address the substantive matching question beyond that obstruction, I then prove that the answer becomes affirmative if the benchmark permits only positive-mass blocks and singletons. That construction also handles pointwise strategyproofness and explicitly specifies every label’s assignment.

1. A two-type counterexample

Fix a binary-rational number \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\). Let \(m=\tau=2\), with types

| Type | Category | Valuation row | Mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(A\) | \(1\) | \((0,1)\) | \(1-\varepsilon\) |
| \(B\) | \(2\) | \((1,0)\) | \(\varepsilon\) |

Thus individuals value precisely those in the other category. Their label sets are

\(A=[0,1-\varepsilon)\), \(B=[1-\varepsilon,1)\).

Define the affine bijection \(f:A\to B\) by

\(f(x)=1-\varepsilon+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}x\).

Consider the partition

\(\mathcal C=\bigl\{\{x,f(x)\}:x\in A\bigr\}\).

Because \(f\) is a bijection, these pairs are disjoint and cover the population. Every block is finite and measurable. Every individual has utility \(1/2\), so the individual utility function is measurable and

\(SW(\mathcal C)=1/2\).

Consequently,

\(\mathrm{OPT}\geq 1/2\).

This witness has a finite description and specifies every label’s partner. Its induced involution is piecewise affine and maps null sets to null sets; the obstruction does not require irregular measurable sets.

2. Every measure-preserving pairing has welfare at most \(\varepsilon\)

Let \(p\) be any measurable, measure-preserving involution, including any possible mechanism output. Define

\(E_A=A\cap p^{-1}(B)\), \(E_B=B\cap p^{-1}(A)\).

The involution property gives \(p(E_A)=E_B\) and \(p^{-1}(E_B)=E_A\). Measure preservation therefore implies

\(\lambda(E_A)=\lambda(E_B)\leq\lambda(B)=\varepsilon\).

An individual has utility \(1/2\) precisely when paired across categories. Same-category pairs and singletons give utility zero. Hence

\(SW(p)=\dfrac{\lambda(E_A)+\lambda(E_B)}2=\lambda(E_B)\leq\varepsilon\).

Taking \(\varepsilon=1/8\), we obtain

\(SW(p)\leq 1/8<1/4\leq\mathrm{OPT}/2\).

Thus no permitted output satisfies the approximation requirement on this supported input. This rules out the requested mechanism regardless of strategyproofness, determinism, or computational power.

The bound \(SW(p)\leq\varepsilon\) is attainable: translate \([0,\varepsilon)\) onto \(B\), pair corresponding labels, and leave the remaining labels fixed. Therefore the best permitted pairing has welfare exactly \(\varepsilon\), and

\(\dfrac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\max_p SW(p)}\geq\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon}\).

As \(\varepsilon\) decreases through binary-rational values, this ratio becomes unbounded.

3. Why the finite matching theorem does not transfer

In the finite setting, welfare uses counting measure. A bijection between the two sides of a collection of pairs preserves their counting mass. This compatibility underlies the cited finite matching approximation theorem. [Flammini, Monaco, and Zhang, Theorem 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09007).

Here, finite-coalition utility still uses cardinality, but population welfare uses Lebesgue measure. The affine bijection above pairs sets of unequal Lebesgue mass. It is admissible for the benchmark and forbidden for the output.

Thus the negative answer already holds for a benchmark consisting entirely of pairs. Larger finite coalitions are unnecessary.

4. A substantive comparison: positive-mass blocks and singletons

Define \(\mathrm{OPT}_{+}\) by restricting the benchmark to partitions whose blocks are positive-mass sets or singletons.

Proven claim. For this modified benchmark, there exists a deterministic mechanism defined on all measurable binary-row report profiles that is acceptable and pointwise strategyproof, and that, on supported inputs, produces a finitely described measure-preserving pairing in polynomial time with

\(SW\geq\mathrm{OPT}_{+}/2\).

This is a different benchmark from the question’s \(\mathrm{OPT}\).

4.1 A polynomial-size pairing linear program

For a supported profile, let \(S_t\) be the set of labels of type \(t\), with mass \(\mu_t\). Introduce variables \(q_{st}\), interpreted as the mass of type \(s\) assigned partners of type \(t\). Impose

\(q_{st}\geq0\), \(q_{st}=q_{ts}\), \(\sum_tq_{st}\leq\mu_s\).

Diagonal entries are allowed: distinct individuals of the same type can be paired.

Maximize

\(F(q)=\dfrac12\sum_{s,t}v_s(a(t))q_{st}\).

There are \(\tau^2\) variables and polynomially many rational constraints. The feasible set is nonempty and compact: the zero vector is feasible, and every variable is bounded by its row capacity.

Use a fixed ordering of the variables and select the lexicographically least maximizer. Exact rational linear programming, followed by polynomially many coordinate optimizations, computes this deterministic solution in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\). The standard rational linear-programming theorem applies because all coefficients and capacities have polynomial total encoding length.

4.2 Explicit realization, including every endpoint

Each \(S_t\) is a finite union of half-open intervals. Concatenating these intervals in label order gives a bijection

\(H_t:[0,\mu_t)\to S_t\)

that is a translation on each of finitely many half-open pieces and preserves measure.

In the coordinate interval \([0,\mu_t)\), allocate consecutive half-open slots \(I_{ts}\) of lengths \(q_{ts}\), in a fixed order of \(s\). Leave the remaining coordinate interval unallocated.

For \(s\ne t\), the slots \(I_{ts}\) and \(I_{st}\) have equal lengths. Pair their labels by equal offsets through \(H_t\) and \(H_s\).

For a diagonal slot of positive length \(q_{tt}\), pair its first half with its second half by translation through \(H_t\). Leave every unallocated label fixed.

These rules define an involution. No allocated label is fixed, and every rule preserves measure. The use of half-open intervals specifies all endpoints without exceptions.

The description consists of the interval maps \(H_t\), the rational allocation matrix, and these pairing rules. Its size and construction time are polynomial. Its welfare is exactly \(F(q)\), since an individual of type \(s\) paired with type \(t\) has utility \(v_s(a(t))/2\).

4.3 The approximation inequality

Take any partition allowed in the definition of \(\mathrm{OPT}_{+}\). Its positive-mass blocks are countable: for every positive integer \(n\), at most \(n\) disjoint blocks can have mass at least \(1/n\), and every positive-mass block meets such a threshold.

For a positive-mass block \(C\), write

\(\beta_s=\lambda(C\cap S_s)\), \(b=\lambda(C)=\sum_s\beta_s>0\).

Its welfare contribution is

\(SW(C)=\dfrac1b\sum_{s,t}\beta_s\beta_t v_s(a(t))\).

Define

\(q^C_{st}=\dfrac{\beta_s\beta_t}{b}\).

This matrix is symmetric, nonnegative, and satisfies

\(\sum_tq^C_{st}=\beta_s\), \(F(q^C)=SW(C)/2\).

Sum these matrices over all positive-mass blocks. Countable additivity and disjointness give a feasible matrix \(q\), because

\(\sum_tq_{st}=\sum_C\lambda(C\cap S_s)\leq\mu_s\).

Singleton utilities are zero. Nonnegativity permits summing the welfare expressions, yielding

\(F(q)=SW(\mathcal C)/2\).

The optimal linear-program value therefore satisfies

\(F(q^\star)\geq SW(\mathcal C)/2\)

for every such partition. Taking the supremum proves

\(SW(p^\star)=F(q^\star)\geq\mathrm{OPT}_{+}/2\).

No attainment assumption about \(\mathrm{OPT}_{+}\) is needed.

4.4 Extension to all reports and pointwise strategyproofness

Fix the immutable category function supplied by the society. Regard report profiles as equivalent when they agree almost everywhere.

If an equivalence class contains a right-continuous report function constant on finitely many half-open intervals with rational endpoints, use that representative and the construction above. Such a representative is unique: if two finite step functions differ at a label, right continuity makes them differ on a right-hand interval of positive measure.

If no such representative exists, output the identity involution, consisting entirely of singletons.

This defines a deterministic mechanism on every measurable report profile. Its computational guarantee is required only on supported inputs, whose report profiles have the specified finite step representatives.

Crucially, changing one label’s row does not change the almost-everywhere equivalence class. Therefore, for every label \(x\), every other-report profile, and every alternative row, the mechanism produces exactly the same involution before and after the change. Evaluating both outputs using the same true row gives equal utilities at \(x\). This proves pointwise strategyproofness, including at endpoints and exceptional labels.

Acceptability holds at every profile because binary valuations give nonnegative utility in every singleton or pair.

Finally, the factor \(2\) is sharp for this modified benchmark: with one type that values its own category, the grand coalition has welfare \(1\), while every pairing gives individual utility at most \(1/2\).
