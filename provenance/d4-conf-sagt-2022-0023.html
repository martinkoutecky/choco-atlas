The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite-support rational report measure over public supplier classes \(q\) and declared costs \(b\), with per-capita budget \(\bar B\), does there exist a polynomial-time anonymous mechanism with report-indexed allocation and payment schedules \(x_q(r;\nu)\in[0,1]\) and \(p_q(r;\nu)\ge 0\) such that aggregate payments satisfy \(\int p_q(b;\nu)\,d\nu(q,b)\le\bar B\), each supplier is individually rational and truthful against every alternative report \(r\) when tested as a tagged supplier against a fixed measure of the others, and the allocation achieves a \(2.62\) approximation to \(\mathrm{OPT}(\nu)=\max_{z\in[0,1]}\{\int v_qz(q,b)\,d\nu(q,b):\int bz(q,b)\,d\nu(q,b)\le\bar B\}\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

There are \(m\ge1\) public supplier classes, with binary-rational values \(v_q\ge0\), and a binary-rational per-capita budget \(\bar B>0\). Costs are linear and private, with \(c\in[0,\bar B]\); reports may be any \(r\ge0\), and classes cannot be misreported.
A society is a probability measure \(\nu\) given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) report types \((q,b)\) with positive mass, each with its mass as a binary rational. Thus \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
A deterministic anonymous mechanism provides measurable schedules \(x_q(r;\nu)\in[0,1]\) and \(p_q(r;\nu)\ge0\). Suppliers with the same class and report receive the same allocation fraction and payment per unit mass.
A tagged supplier has zero mass, so changing its report leaves the measure of others \(\nu\) fixed. For every \(\nu,q,c\in[0,\bar B]\) and \(r\ge0\), require \(p_q(c;\nu)-c x_q(c;\nu)\ge p_q(r;\nu)-c x_q(r;\nu)\); require also \(p_q(r;\nu)\ge r x_q(r;\nu)\).
For every report measure, require \(\int p_q(b;\nu)\,d\nu(q,b)\le\bar B\). On every measure supported on \(b\in[0,\bar B]\), require \(2.62\int v_qx_q(b;\nu)\,d\nu(q,b)\ge\mathrm{OPT}(\nu)\), where \(\mathrm{OPT}(\nu)=\max_{0\le z(q,b)\le1}\{\int v_qz(q,b)\,d\nu(q,b):\int bz(q,b)\,d\nu(q,b)\le\bar B\}\).
Computational efficiency means constructing a finite representation of the schedules in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with evaluation at rational reports polynomial also in the query bit length.

Does a polynomial-time deterministic anonymous mechanism satisfying these conditions and achieving approximation factor \(2.62\) exist, or is this approximation guarantee unattainable under these requirements?

For finitely many divisible suppliers with costs bounded by the total budget, a polynomial-time deterministic truthful, individually rational, budget-feasible mechanism achieves factor \((3+\sqrt5)/2\), with a deterministic lower bound of \(5/4\) ([Klumper–Schäfer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13124v2)).
The finite-supplier upper bound was subsequently improved to \(2\) ([Amanatidis et al.](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/35197/35197.pdf)); neither result treats the zero-mass tagged-supplier model above. No result settling this precise distributional question was located.

Conclusion

Yes. A polynomial-time deterministic anonymous mechanism exists; under the stated assumptions it achieves factor \(1\).

The per-capita normalization makes full procurement feasible: the society has total mass \(1\), and every true unit cost is at most \(\bar B\). I first prove the exact guarantee. I then give a factor-\(2\) construction that also handles the nontrivial extension in which costs may exceed \(\bar B\).

1. An exact mechanism for the stated problem

For every society \(\nu\), class \(q\), and report \(r\ge0\), define

\[ x_q(r;\nu)= \begin{cases} 1,&0\le r\le\bar B,\\ 0,&r>\bar B, \end{cases} \qquad p_q(r;\nu)= \begin{cases} \bar B,&0\le r\le\bar B,\\ 0,&r>\bar B. \end{cases} \]

These schedules are measurable, deterministic, and anonymous. They do not depend on \(\nu\).

Truthfulness. Fix \(\nu,q\), and a true cost \(c\in[0,\bar B]\). Truthful utility is \(\bar B-c\). Every report \(r\le\bar B\) gives exactly the same utility; every report \(r>\bar B\) gives utility \(0\). Since \(\bar B-c\ge0\), truthful reporting maximizes utility.

Individual rationality at every report. If \(r\le\bar B\), then \(p_q(r;\nu)=\bar B\ge r=r x_q(r;\nu)\). If \(r>\bar B\), both payment and allocation are zero.

Budget feasibility for every report measure. Because \(\nu\) is a probability measure,

\[ \int p_q(b;\nu)\,d\nu(q,b) =\bar B\,\nu\bigl(\{(q,b):b\le\bar B\}\bigr) \le\bar B. \]

This argument does not require reports to lie within the true-cost bound.

Optimality on every required society. Suppose \(\nu\) is supported on \(b\in[0,\bar B]\). Full allocation is feasible for the benchmark because

\[ \int b\,d\nu(q,b)\le\bar B\int1\,d\nu(q,b)=\bar B. \]

Also, for every feasible allocation \(z\), nonnegativity of the public values and \(z\le1\) imply

\[ \int v_qz(q,b)\,d\nu(q,b)\le\int v_q\,d\nu(q,b). \]

Consequently,

\[ \mathrm{OPT}(\nu)=\int v_q\,d\nu(q,b) =\int v_qx_q(b;\nu)\,d\nu(q,b). \]

Thus the mechanism achieves factor \(1\), including when all values are zero.

Computational efficiency. The finite representation consists of the rational threshold \(\bar B\) and the two constant outcomes. Constructing it and evaluating it at a rational report require only copying input data and an exact rational comparison. Both operations have polynomial bit complexity.

2. A nontrivial extension: factor \(2\) without a cost bound

The preceding conclusion follows directly from the stated normalization. Nevertheless, zero-mass truthfulness also permits an efficient constant-factor mechanism when full procurement need not be affordable.

Here is a self-contained construction for finitely supported societies with arbitrary nonnegative costs. It satisfies truthfulness for every true cost \(c\ge0\).

Write the support as \((q_i,b_i)\), with masses \(\mu_i>0\). For entries with \(v_{q_i}>0\), define

\[ w_i=\mu_i v_{q_i}, \qquad d_i=\frac{b_i}{v_{q_i}}, \qquad S=\sum_{i:v_{q_i}>0}w_i. \]

If \(S=0\), use zero allocation and payment everywhere. Its benchmark value is zero. Henceforth assume \(S>0\).

For \(s\ge0\), define

\[ W_{<}(s)=\sum_{i:d_i<s}w_i, \qquad W_{\le}(s)=\sum_{i:d_i\le s}w_i. \]

Choose

\[ t=\inf\{s>0:sW_{\le}(s)\ge\bar B\}. \]

This threshold exists and is positive. Indeed, \(sW_{\le}(s)\le sS\), so every member of the defining set is at least \(\bar B/S>0\). For sufficiently large \(s\), all positive-value support entries are included and \(sW_{\le}(s)=sS\), which eventually exceeds \(\bar B\).

The function \(sW_{\le}(s)\) is nondecreasing and right-continuous. Its left limit at \(t\) is \(tW_{<}(t)\). The definition of \(t\) therefore gives

\[ tW_{<}(t)\le\bar B\le tW_{\le}(t). \]

Put \(\Delta=W_{\le}(t)-W_{<}(t)\). If \(\Delta>0\), set

\[ \alpha=\frac{\bar B/t-W_{<}(t)}{\Delta}. \]

The preceding inequalities imply \(0\le\alpha\le1\). If \(\Delta=0\), those inequalities imply \(W_{<}(t)=\bar B/t\); set \(\alpha=0\).

For a class with \(v_q>0\), define

\[ x_q(r;\nu)= \begin{cases} 1,&r<t v_q,\\ \alpha,&r=t v_q,\\ 0,&r>t v_q, \end{cases} \qquad p_q(r;\nu)=t v_qx_q(r;\nu). \]

For \(v_q=0\), both schedules are zero.

Truthfulness, individual rationality, and budget feasibility

Fix \(\nu\) and a positive-value class \(q\). Zero mass means that changing the tagged supplier’s report leaves \(t\) and \(\alpha\) fixed. Its utility at report \(r\) is

\[ (t v_q-c)x_q(r;\nu). \]

If \(c<t v_q\), truthful reporting gives allocation \(1\), which maximizes this expression. If \(c>t v_q\), truthful reporting gives allocation \(0\), which maximizes it. If \(c=t v_q\), every report gives utility zero. This proves truthfulness, including at the threshold.

A positive allocation occurs only when \(r\le t v_q\), so \(p_q(r;\nu)\ge r x_q(r;\nu)\). Moreover, total payment is exactly

\[ \int p_q(b;\nu)\,d\nu(q,b) =t\bigl(W_{<}(t)+\alpha\Delta\bigr) =\bar B. \]

Thus the mechanism is individually rational and budget feasible.

Approximation guarantee

Let \(V\) be its achieved value. By construction,

\[ V=W_{<}(t)+\alpha\Delta=\frac{\bar B}{t}. \]

Consider any feasible benchmark allocation \(z_i\in[0,1]\). Its value from entries with \(d_i<t\) is at most \(W_{<}(t)\). For entries with \(d_i\ge t\), the inequality \(b_i\ge t v_{q_i}\) gives

\[ \sum_{i:d_i\ge t}\mu_i v_{q_i}z_i \le\frac1t\sum_{i:d_i\ge t}\mu_i b_i z_i \le\frac{\bar B}{t}. \]

Zero-value entries contribute nothing. Therefore every feasible benchmark allocation has value at most

\[ W_{<}(t)+\frac{\bar B}{t} \le\frac{2\bar B}{t} =2V. \]

This proves factor \(2\) even without the original cost bound.

Polynomial-time representation

Sort the distinct rational ratios \(d_i\), and compute their cumulative weights. The threshold \(t\) either equals a support ratio or lies between consecutive ratios, where \(W_{\le}(s)\) is a constant positive cumulative weight \(W\). In the latter case, \(t=\bar B/W\).

Thus one can enumerate the support ratios and the numbers \(\bar B/W\) for all positive cumulative weights, and select a candidate satisfying

\[ tW_{<}(t)\le\bar B\le tW_{\le}(t). \]

There are at most \(2\tau\) candidates. Exact rational arithmetic finds \(t\) and \(\alpha\) in polynomial time, with polynomial output bit length. Each schedule is represented by its rational threshold \(t v_q\), its boundary allocation \(\alpha\), and its payment multiplier \(t v_q\); rational-report evaluation is polynomial in the input and query bit lengths.

3. Scope of the resolution

The factor-\(1\) mechanism completely settles the problem as stated. The factor-\(2\) construction additionally shows that the requested factor \(2.62\) remains achievable in the zero-mass model even after removing the assumption that makes full procurement affordable.
