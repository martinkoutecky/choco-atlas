The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many store types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), holding and penalty costs \(h,p\), and a finite encoded set of common-shock scenarios \(\omega\) with rational probabilities \(\pi_\omega\) and nonnegative per-capita demands \(d_{t\omega}\), compute type rates \(a_t\) in the core of the resulting continuum cost game: for every submass vector \(0\le\nu\le\mu\), let \(D_\omega(\nu)=\sum_t\nu_t d_{t\omega}\) and \(c_\mu(\nu)=\min_{q\ge0}\sum_\omega\pi_\omega[h(q-D_\omega(\nu))_++p(D_\omega(\nu)-q)_+]\); find \(a\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=c_\mu(\mu)\) and \(\sum_t\nu_ta_t\le c_\mu(\nu)\) for all such \(\nu\), or report that none exists.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) store types with positive masses \(\mu_t\), each a binary rational; \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type represents divisible, nonatomic mass; total mass need not equal \(1\).
There are \(m\ge1\) explicitly listed common-shock scenarios \(\Omega\), with binary-rational probabilities \(\pi_\omega>0\) summing to \(1\), and binary-rational per-capita demands \(d_{t\omega}\ge0\). Holding and penalty costs \(h,p\) are positive binary rationals, identical for all types and coalitions; there is no ordering cost.
A coalition is any real submass vector \(\nu\) satisfying \(0\le\nu_t\le\mu_t\). Define \(D_\omega(\nu)=\sum_{t\in T}\nu_t d_{t\omega}\) and \(c_\mu(\nu)=\min_{q\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\pi_\omega\bigl[h(q-D_\omega(\nu))_++p(D_\omega(\nu)-q)_+\bigr]\), where \((x)_+=\max\{x,0\}\); thus \(c_\mu(0)=0\).
Compute signed rational type rates \(a_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=c_\mu(\mu)\) and \(\sum_t\nu_ta_t\le c_\mu(\nu)\) for every real submass vector \(\nu\), or report that none exist. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Is this continuum core-allocation problem solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite players, an [allocation-process convergence result](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04149v1) states almost-sure core membership of accumulation points when nonnegative stochastic games converge coalitionwise to a balanced game; it gives no polynomial-time bound.
[Chen and Zhang](https://publish.illinois.edu/xinchen/files/2012/12/SPinventorygame.pdf) give a computable core allocation for finite-scenario discrete inventory games.
[Montrucchio and Scarsini](https://www.carloalberto.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/no.15.pdf) establish nonempty cores for bounded-demand measure newsvendor games and uniqueness under additional nonatomicity and continuous aggregate-demand conditions; they do not state the complexity of this encoded finite-scenario problem.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Set holding and penalty costs to strictly positive binary rationals, as required by the discrete inventory model.
  • Use a nonempty support of positive masses without imposing unit total mass; a single type is allowed because nonatomic mass already represents more than one store.
  • Require scenario probabilities to sum to one and retain only positive-probability scenarios; explicitly require binary-rational nonnegative demands.
  • Interpret submass coalitions and order quantities as real and divisible, with common-shock demands scaling linearly within each type.
  • Allow signed allocation rates, consistent with the cost-core definition, and request rational rates for exact encoded computation.
  • Use m for the number of explicitly encoded scenarios and L for total binary input length.

Result

The problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). Under the stated hypotheses, a rational core allocation always exists.

We prove this by constructing scenario coefficients that give a lower bound on every coalition’s cost and attain equality for the grand coalition.

1. A lower bound valid for every real coalition

Suppose coefficients \(u_\omega\) satisfy

\( \displaystyle -h\le u_\omega\le p\quad\text{for every }\omega,\qquad \sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\pi_\omega u_\omega=0. \)

Define signed type rates by

\( \displaystyle a_t=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\pi_\omega u_\omega d_{t\omega}. \)

For every real number \(x\),

\( \displaystyle u_\omega x\le h(-x)_++p x_+. \)

Indeed, if \(x\ge0\), this follows from \(u_\omega\le p\). If \(x\le0\), multiplication of \(u_\omega\ge-h\) by \(x\) gives \(u_\omega x\le-hx\).

Consequently, for every real coalition vector \(0\le\nu\le\mu\) and every \(q\ge0\),

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \sum_t\nu_ta_t &=\sum_\omega\pi_\omega u_\omega D_\omega(\nu)\\ &=\sum_\omega\pi_\omega u_\omega\bigl(D_\omega(\nu)-q\bigr)\\ &\le\sum_\omega\pi_\omega\left[h\bigl(q-D_\omega(\nu)\bigr)_+ +p\bigl(D_\omega(\nu)-q\bigr)_+\right]. \end{aligned} \)

The second equality uses the zero weighted sum of the coefficients. Taking the minimum over \(q\ge0\) yields

\( \displaystyle \sum_t\nu_ta_t\le c_\mu(\nu). \)

This argument applies directly to arbitrary real submass vectors.

For completeness, the minimum exists: the objective is continuous, and for \(q>\max_\omega D_\omega(\nu)\) it equals \(h\bigl(q-\sum_\omega\pi_\omega D_\omega(\nu)\bigr)\), which is strictly increasing. Thus minimization may be restricted to the compact interval from zero to the maximum demand.

2. Constructing coefficients that attain the grand-coalition cost

Compute the grand coalition’s scenario demands

\( \displaystyle X_\omega=D_\omega(\mu)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t d_{t\omega}, \)

and set

\( \displaystyle \alpha=\frac{p}{h+p}. \)

Because \(h,p>0\), we have \(0<\alpha<1\).

Sort the distinct values among the \(X_\omega\). Choose \(q^*\) to be the first such value whose cumulative probability is at least \(\alpha\). Define

\( \displaystyle B=\sum_{X_\omega<q^*}\pi_\omega,\qquad E=\sum_{X_\omega=q^*}\pi_\omega,\qquad G=\sum_{X_\omega>q^*}\pi_\omega. \)

The choice of \(q^*\) implies

\( \displaystyle E>0,\qquad B+E+G=1,\qquad B<\alpha\le B+E. \)

Now put

\( \displaystyle \kappa=\frac{hB-pG}{E} =p-(h+p)\frac{\alpha-B}{E}, \)

and assign

\( \displaystyle u_\omega= \begin{cases} -h,&X_\omega<q^*,\\ \kappa,&X_\omega=q^*,\\ p,&X_\omega>q^*. \end{cases} \)

Since \(0<(\alpha-B)/E\le1\), the displayed expression for \(\kappa\) gives

\( \displaystyle -h\le\kappa<p. \)

Thus every coefficient lies in the required interval. Moreover,

\( \displaystyle \sum_\omega\pi_\omega u_\omega=-hB+\kappa E+pG=0. \)

These conclusions also cover tied demands, zero demands, and a single scenario.

3. Proof of efficiency and core membership

Use the coefficients just constructed and output

\( \displaystyle a_t=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\pi_\omega u_\omega d_{t\omega}. \)

Section 1 already proves all coalition inequalities. It remains to prove efficiency.

For each scenario,

\( \displaystyle u_\omega(X_\omega-q^*) =h(q^*-X_\omega)_++p(X_\omega-q^*)_+. \)

To verify this equality, there are three cases:

  • If \(X_\omega<q^*\), then \(u_\omega=-h\), and both sides equal \(h(q^*-X_\omega)\).
  • If \(X_\omega>q^*\), then \(u_\omega=p\), and both sides equal \(p(X_\omega-q^*)\).
  • If \(X_\omega=q^*\), both sides are zero.

Therefore, using the zero weighted sum again,

\( \displaystyle \begin{aligned} \sum_t\mu_ta_t &=\sum_\omega\pi_\omega u_\omega X_\omega\\ &=\sum_\omega\pi_\omega u_\omega(X_\omega-q^*)\\ &=\sum_\omega\pi_\omega \left[h(q^*-X_\omega)_++p(X_\omega-q^*)_+\right]. \end{aligned} \)

All \(X_\omega\) are nonnegative, so \(q^*\ge0\). The last expression is consequently the cost of a feasible grand-coalition order quantity, and hence is at least \(c_\mu(\mu)\).

Conversely, Section 1 applied to \(\nu=\mu\) gives \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t\le c_\mu(\mu)\). Combining these inequalities proves

\( \displaystyle \sum_t\mu_ta_t=c_\mu(\mu). \)

Thus the output is an efficient allocation satisfying every continuum coalition constraint.

4. Exact polynomial-time computation

The algorithm consists of:

  • Computing the \(m\) aggregate demands \(X_\omega\).
  • Sorting them and finding the threshold \(q^*\) by exact cumulative-probability comparisons.
  • Computing \(B,E,G,\kappa\) and the scenario coefficients.
  • Computing the \(\tau\) rates \(a_t\).

This requires \(O(m\tau+m\log m)\) rational arithmetic operations and comparisons. All divisions are valid: \(h+p>0\) and \(E>0\).

We also need polynomial bit complexity. Each input rational has numerator and denominator length at most \(L\). A product of a constant number of such rationals has length \(O(L)\); a sum of \(n\) rationals of length at most \(b\) has length \(O(n(b+1))\), even using the product of their denominators as a common denominator.

Applying these bounds:

  • Each \(X_\omega\) has length \(O(\tau(L+1))\).
  • The probability sums and \(\kappa\) have length \(O(m(L+1))\).
  • Each output rate has length \(O(m^2(L+1))\).

Exact comparisons use cross multiplication of integers of these polynomial lengths. Standard integer arithmetic therefore implements every step in polynomial time. In particular, the algorithm outputs exact rational rates rather than numerical approximations.

The construction is consistent with the dual-allocation principle in [Chen and Zhang’s inventory-game paper](https://publish.illinois.edu/xinchen/files/2012/12/SPinventorygame.pdf). Here the explicit finite-scenario encoding permits a direct complexity proof, and the pointwise inequality establishes validity for all real submass coalitions.
