The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given providers \(C\), requests \(Q\), epoch steps \([T]\), supported user types \(i\in[\tau]\) with rational masses \(\mu_i\) summing to \(1\), type-specific request probabilities \(p_{i,t}(q)\), rewards \(r_i(q,c)\), nonnegative weights \(\alpha_t\), and per-capita provider thresholds \(\beta_c\), choose viable-provider indicators \(y_c\in\{0,1\}\) and routing probabilities \(\pi_{i,q,t,c}\in[0,1]\). Maximize per-capita welfare \(\sum_{i,t,q,c}\mu_i p_{i,t}(q)\alpha_t\pi_{i,q,t,c}r_i(q,c)\), subject to \(\sum_c\pi_{i,q,t,c}=1\), \(\pi_{i,q,t,c}\le y_c\), and \(\sum_{i,t,q}\mu_i p_{i,t}(q)\pi_{i,q,t,c}\ge\beta_c y_c\). Does this problem admit a \(1/e\)-factor approximation in time polynomial in \(\tau,|C|,|Q|,T\), and the binary input length \(L\)?

Let \(C,Q\) be nonempty finite sets, \(m=|C|\), and \(T\ge1\) an integer. A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\) summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type submits one request per epoch step, with explicitly supplied probabilities \(p_{i,t}(q)\ge0\), \(\sum_qp_{i,t}(q)=1\). Rewards \(r_i(q,c)\), weights \(\alpha_t\), and per-capita thresholds \(\beta_c\) are nonnegative binary rationals. Assume \(\min_c\beta_c\le T\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Choose \(y_c\in\{0,1\}\) and divisible routing probabilities \(\pi_{i,q,t,c}\in[0,1]\); provider engagement is routed request mass, evaluated exactly over the epoch.
Maximize \(W=\sum_{i,t,q,c}\mu_i p_{i,t}(q)\alpha_t\pi_{i,q,t,c}r_i(q,c)\), subject to \(\sum_c\pi_{i,q,t,c}=1\), \(\pi_{i,q,t,c}\le y_c\), and \(\sum_{i,t,q}\mu_i p_{i,t}(q)\pi_{i,q,t,c}\ge\beta_c y_c\), for all applicable indices.

Does this problem admit a \(1/e\)-factor approximation in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|Q|,T,L\)?

Conclusion and the theorem used

Yes. The rational mass formulation has a polynomial-size, approximation-preserving reduction to the fractional matching problem covered by the cited approximation theorem.

We invoke Theorem 1 of [Mladenov et al., *Optimizing Long-term Social Welfare in Recommender Systems*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mladenov20a/mladenov20a.pdf). Its one-step specialization maximizes
\[ \sum_{j,c} a_{jc}x_{jc} \]
subject to
\[ \sum_c x_{jc}=1,\qquad 0\le x_{jc}\le y_c,\qquad \sum_j d_jx_{jc}\ge \nu_cy_c,\qquad y_c\in\{0,1\}. \]
Here the query labels form an explicitly supplied finite set, the objective coefficients are nonnegative, and the request-count coefficients \(d_j\) may be arbitrary nonnegative numbers. The theorem gives a polynomial-time \(1/e\)-factor approximation. The paper explicitly permits fractional routing and arbitrary nonnegative request-count functions in its formulation.

To check that arbitrary nonnegative coefficients \(a_{jc}\) fit its additive objective, take one synthetic user with a uniform distribution over the \(N\) query labels, one epoch step with weight \(1\), and rewards \(Na_{jc}\). Its expected-reward coefficients are then exactly \(a_{jc}\). Supply \(d_j\) as the request-count coefficients, as permitted by the formulation.

We use this published theorem as a known result; the reduction below does not require the more general weighted-engagement extension.

Exact reduction

For each triple \(j=(i,q,t)\), define
\[ d_j=\mu_i p_{i,t}(q),\qquad v_{jc}=\alpha_t r_i(q,c),\qquad a_{jc}=d_jv_{jc}. \]
Retain only the triples with \(d_j>0\), and call their set \(J\). Its size satisfies
\[ 1\le N:=|J|\le \tau |Q|T. \]
Moreover,
\[ \sum_{j\in J}d_j =\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\mu_i \sum_{q\in Q}p_{i,t}(q) =T. \]

Construct the theorem’s instance with query labels \(J\), providers \(C\), request-count coefficients \(d_j\), objective coefficients \(a_{jc}\), and thresholds \(\nu_c=\beta_c\).

For every retained triple, identify
\[ x_{jc}=\pi_{i,q,t,c}. \]
The resulting objective is exactly the original welfare:
\[ \sum_{j\in J,c\in C}a_{jc}x_{jc} = \sum_{i,t,q,c}\mu_i p_{i,t}(q)\alpha_t \pi_{i,q,t,c}r_i(q,c). \]
Likewise, for every provider \(c\), its engagement is exactly
\[ \sum_{j\in J}d_jx_{jc} = \sum_{i,t,q}\mu_i p_{i,t}(q)\pi_{i,q,t,c}. \]

Thus restricting any original feasible solution to \(J\) produces a feasible reduced solution with identical welfare.

Conversely, let \((x,y)\) be a feasible reduced solution. Since \(J\) is nonempty, the constraints
\[ \sum_c x_{jc}=1,\qquad x_{jc}\le y_c \]
imply that at least one provider \(c_0\) has \(y_{c_0}=1\). For every omitted triple, define its routing distribution to put probability \(1\) on \(c_0\). These distributions satisfy the original routing constraints. Their request mass is zero, so they change neither engagement nor welfare.

Consequently, the two instances have identical optimal values, and every reduced solution lifts to an original solution with exactly the same value.

Feasibility and binary encoding

The feasibility assumption is sufficient. Choose \(c_0\) with \(\beta_{c_0}\le T\), open only \(c_0\), and route every request to it. Its engagement is \(T\), so all constraints hold.

The reduction is polynomial in the stated input parameters:

  • There are at most \(\tau |Q|T\) retained query labels and at most \(m\tau |Q|T\) routing variables.
  • Each \(d_j\) and \(a_{jc}\) is a product of a fixed number of input rationals. Numerator and denominator bit lengths grow only additively.
  • The synthetic uniform probabilities and reward scaling introduce only \(O(\log N)\) additional bits per coefficient.
  • Lifting the output requires at most \(m\tau |Q|T\) routing entries.

In particular, no rational mass is expanded into a list of individual users.

Apply the cited theorem to this polynomial-size rational instance and lift its output. Because feasibility and objective values are preserved exactly, its \(1/e\)-approximation guarantee is preserved exactly, with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,|Q|,T,L\).
