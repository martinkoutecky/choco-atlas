The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set \(T\) of complete hospital templates, rational masses \(\mu_t\), and a split \(q_{t,b}\) of each type across the two sides of a fixed bipartition, compute a feasible mass matching of maximum total matched-pair mass. Each template specifies finitely many client slots, its internal compatibility graph, and its slot compatibilities with every other template; each unit of hospital mass uses an internal maximum matching, and external matches join only compatible slots on opposite sides, with each slot used at most once per unit. Break ties by a fixed order on types.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau\) hospital types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible templates.
Each type has a finite labeled slot set \(S_t\), a simple undirected internal compatibility graph on \(S_t\), and explicitly listed symmetric slot compatibilities between distinct hospitals of every pair of types, including equal types. Put \(m=\sum_t|S_t|\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
The fixed bipartition is supplied as binary-rational masses \(q_{t,b}\geq0\), where \(b\in\{1,2\}\) and \(q_{t,1}+q_{t,2}=\mu_t\). Each hospital belongs wholly to one side; hospital mass is nonatomic.
A feasible mass matching partitions each cell \((t,b)\) into finitely many nonnegative rational submasses, each labeled by an actual maximum-cardinality matching of the internal graph. These internal matchings are chosen jointly with the external matches.
External matches are nonnegative rational pair masses between compatible slot classes on opposite sides, including opposite-side hospitals of the same type. For each slot class, total incident external mass cannot exceed its mass left uncovered by the chosen internal matchings; no slot is used twice in any hospital unit.
The objective is total internal matched-pair mass plus total external matched-pair mass, counting each pair once. Among maximizers, lexicographically maximize matched-slot mass in each cell, ordering side \(1\) before side \(2\) and types within each side by a supplied fixed order; any remaining tie is allowed. Represent a solution by its nonzero internal submasses and external pair masses.

Is computing an optimal feasible mass matching with this tie-breaking solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

For finite hospitals, maximizing matching cardinality subject to maximum internal matchings and opposite-side external edges, with serial hospital-utility tie-breaking, is polynomial-time computable ([source](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/131324/7/131324.pdf)).
No result settling this exact continuous problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized positive hospital masses to sum to one; permitted zero mass in either side of a type's split.
  • Defined m as the total number of explicitly represented template slots and L as the total input bit length.
  • Made compatibility undirected, explicit, and uniform across hospital copies; distinguished internal compatibility from compatibility between distinct hospitals, including hospitals of equal type.
  • Specified nonatomic mass and mixtures of actual maximum internal matchings, chosen jointly with external matches, rather than fixing an internal matching in advance.
  • Defined external feasibility by the residual mass of each slot class and supplied a finite rational solution representation.
  • Interpreted tie-breaking as lexicographic maximization of matched-slot masses, with the first side preceding the second and the fixed type order used within each side; allowed residual ties.

Result

Computing the required optimal mass matching, including the lexicographic tie-breaking and the specified output representation, is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\).

The key is that rational mixtures of internal matchings are permitted. Their feasible marginal vectors form matching polytopes, for which both optimization and constructive decomposition are polynomial-time operations.

1. Algorithmic facts used

We use the following standard results.

  • Maximum-weight matching in a general undirected graph, with rational edge weights, is computable in polynomial time. Its matching polytope is the convex hull of the incidence vectors of actual matchings. [Edmonds’s original paper](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/69B/jresv69Bn1-2p125_A1b.pdf)
  • For rational polytopes with polynomially bounded defining-inequality encoding lengths, strong separation and exact linear optimization are polynomial-time equivalent. These results accommodate lower-dimensional polytopes.
  • Given such a polytope, a strong separation oracle, and a rational point in the polytope, one can compute a rational convex decomposition into at most its dimension plus one vertices in polynomial time.

The latter two are the rational-polyhedral oracle and constructive decomposition results of [Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1988.pdf). We verify their encoding and oracle hypotheses below.

2. Internal matching distributions form a face of a matching polytope

For each type \(t\), write its internal graph as \(G_t=(S_t,E_t)\), and let \(k_t\) be its maximum matching cardinality. Compute \(k_t\) using a maximum-cardinality matching algorithm.

Define its matching polytope by

\(\displaystyle P_t=\operatorname{conv}\{\chi^M:M\text{ is a matching of }G_t\}.\)

Here \(\chi^M\in\{0,1\}^{E_t}\) is the edge-incidence vector of \(M\). Define

\(\displaystyle F_t=\left\{p\in P_t:\sum_{e\in E_t}p_e=k_t\right\}.\)

Claim. \(F_t\) is exactly the convex hull of the incidence vectors of maximum-cardinality internal matchings.

Proof. Every matching \(M\) satisfies \(|M|\leq k_t\). If

\(\displaystyle p=\sum_j\lambda_j\chi^{M_j},\qquad \lambda_j\geq0,\qquad \sum_j\lambda_j=1,\)

then

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in E_t}p_e=\sum_j\lambda_j|M_j|\leq k_t.\)

Equality holds only if every \(M_j\) with \(\lambda_j>0\) has cardinality \(k_t\): otherwise the weighted average is strictly smaller. Conversely, every convex combination of maximum-cardinality matchings has edge sum \(k_t\). This proves the claim.

For cell \(c=(t,b)\), let \(z_{c,e}\) denote the mass internally matched along edge \(e\). The exact condition for an allowable internal distribution is therefore

\(\displaystyle z_c\in q_{t,b}F_t.\)

When \(q_{t,b}=0\), this means \(z_c=0\).

For \(q_{t,b}>0\), a rational decomposition

\(\displaystyle \frac{z_c}{q_{t,b}}=\sum_j\lambda_{c,j}\chi^{M_{c,j}}\)

gives the required internal submasses \(y_{c,j}=q_{t,b}\lambda_{c,j}\), each labeled by an actual maximum-cardinality matching.

3. An exact linear programming formulation

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the set of allowed external pairs. Each element has one endpoint \(v=(t,1,s)\) and the other \(w=(u,2,r)\). Include precisely the explicitly compatible pairs, including \(t=u\).

Introduce a nonnegative variable \(x_a\) for each \(a\in\mathcal A\). For slot class \(v=(t,b,s)\), define

\(\displaystyle I_v(z)=\sum_{\substack{e\in E_t\\s\in e}}z_{(t,b),e},\qquad X_v(x)=\sum_{\substack{a\in\mathcal A\\v\in a}}x_a.\)

Consider the polytope \(Q\) specified by

\(\displaystyle z_{(t,b)}\in q_{t,b}F_t\quad\text{for every cell},\)

\(\displaystyle x_a\geq0\quad\text{for every }a\in\mathcal A,\)

\(\displaystyle I_{(t,b,s)}(z)+X_{(t,b,s)}(x)\leq q_{t,b}\quad\text{for every slot class}.\)

The last inequality says exactly that external mass cannot exceed the mass of that slot left uncovered internally.

Every feasible mass matching gives a point in \(Q\)

Given internal submasses \(y_{c,j}\) labeled by maximum internal matchings \(M_{c,j}\), put

\(\displaystyle z_{c,e}=\sum_{j:e\in M_{c,j}}y_{c,j}.\)

The internal distribution satisfies the scaled-face constraint from Section 2. The supplied external masses satisfy the slot-capacity constraints by feasibility. Thus the resulting \((z,x)\) belongs to \(Q\).

Every rational point in \(Q\) is realizable

For each positive-mass cell, decompose \(z_c/q_c\) into maximum internal matchings, obtaining rational submasses \(y_{c,j}\).

We must also check that aggregate slot capacities suffice for actual simultaneous external matching. Choose a common positive integer denominator \(D\) for all \(q_c,y_{c,j},x_a\). Represent cell \(c\) by \(Dq_c\) hospital copies, each carrying mass \(1/D\), and assign matching \(M_{c,j}\) to \(Dy_{c,j}\) copies.

The number of exposed copies of slot class \(v=(t,b,s)\) is

\(\displaystyle D\bigl(q_{t,b}-I_v(z)\bigr).\)

For each incident external pair \(a\), allocate \(Dx_a\) exposed copies of this slot to \(a\). The allocations can be disjoint because

\(\displaystyle \sum_{a\ni v}Dx_a\leq D\bigl(q_{t,b}-I_v(z)\bigr).\)

For each external pair \(a=\{v,w\}\), pair its allocated endpoints bijectively. Every slot is used at most once. Its endpoints belong to opposite sides, so they belong to distinct hospitals, including when their types agree.

The allocations for different slot labels can be made independently: a hospital may use several distinct slots, and the prohibition concerns reusing a slot. Interpreting each copy as a nonatomic submass of size \(1/D\) proves realizability.

The algorithm outputs rational masses directly; this denominator construction is an existence proof of their simultaneous feasibility.

4. Objectives and tie-breaking are linear

Because every internal matching assigned to type \(t\) has cardinality \(k_t\), the internal matched-pair mass is the constant

\(\displaystyle C=\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{b=1}^2k_tq_{t,b}=\sum_{t\in T}k_t\mu_t.\)

Consequently, maximizing the primary objective is equivalent to maximizing

\(\displaystyle H(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}x_a.\)

For cell \(c=(t,b)\), its matched-slot mass is

\(\displaystyle U_c(z,x)=2k_tq_{t,b}+\sum_{s\in S_t}X_{(t,b,s)}(x).\)

The internal term counts both endpoints of every internal pair. The external term counts each external endpoint belonging to the cell. Thus \(U_c\) is exactly the requested utility.

Let \(c_1,\ldots,c_{2\tau}\) be the supplied cell order. Apply sequential exact linear optimization:

  • Maximize \(H\) over \(Q\), obtaining \(h^\star\), and impose \(H=h^\star\).
  • Maximize \(U_{c_1}\) over that face and impose its optimal value.
  • Continue through \(U_{c_{2\tau}}\).

At each stage the feasible region is the set of maximizers of the preceding objectives. Therefore the final point has precisely the required lexicographic tie-breaking. Any residual tie is allowed.

5. Polynomial-time exact optimization

There are at most

\(\displaystyle 2\sum_t|E_t|+|\mathcal A|=O(m^2)\)

variables: \(\sum_t|E_t|\leq m(m-1)/2\), and there are at most \(m^2\) opposite-side slot pairs.

We now verify the oracle hypotheses.

Polynomial encoding lengths

Edmonds’s description of \(P_t\) consists of nonnegativity, degree constraints, and odd-set constraints:

\(\displaystyle p_e\geq0,\qquad \sum_{e\ni s}p_e\leq1,\)

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in E_t[W]}p_e\leq\frac{|W|-1}{2}\quad\text{for every odd }W\subseteq S_t.\)

Scaling by \(q_{t,b}\) multiplies the right-hand sides by this input rational. Add the maximum-cardinality equality

\(\displaystyle \sum_{e\in E_t}z_{(t,b),e}=q_{t,b}k_t.\)

Every individual inequality has polynomial encoding length in \(m,\tau,L\), despite the potentially exponential number of odd-set inequalities. The remaining constraints of \(Q\) also have polynomial encoding length.

Moreover, \(Q\) is nonempty: choose any maximum internal matching in each cell and set every external mass to zero. It is bounded, since every coordinate is nonnegative and at most \(1\).

Polynomial-time strong separation

Linear optimization over \(P_t\) is maximum-weight matching in \(G_t\), hence polynomial-time computable. The rational optimization–separation equivalence gives a polynomial-time strong separation oracle for \(P_t\).

For a positive-mass cell, test \(z_c/q_c\) against this oracle and separately test its cardinality equality. If the oracle returns

\(\displaystyle a^\top p\leq\beta\)

violated by \(z_c/q_c\), then

\(\displaystyle a^\top z_c\leq q_c\beta\)

is a violated valid inequality for the scaled polytope. Division and scaling have polynomial bit complexity because \(q_c\) is an input rational. Zero-mass cells are checked directly.

Together with direct checks of external nonnegativity and slot capacities, these procedures give strong separation for \(Q\). The rational-polyhedral oracle theorem therefore solves its linear optimization problems exactly in polynomial time.

Successive tie-breaking preserves polynomial bit complexity

The sequentially optimized regions are faces of \(Q\). Each optimal objective value is attained at a vertex of the current face, hence at a vertex of \(Q\).

Vertex coordinates of \(Q\) have a uniform polynomial encoding bound: choose a full-rank collection of active defining constraints and apply Cramer’s rule, using the polynomial bounds on dimension and constraint encoding length. Evaluating any \(H\) or \(U_c\) at such a vertex likewise produces a rational of polynomial encoding length.

Thus the added equalities retain polynomial encoding lengths throughout all \(1+2\tau\) optimization stages.

6. Polynomial-size recovery of the required output

Take a rational point \((z^\star,x^\star)\) in the final face.

For every positive-mass cell \(c=(t,b)\), apply the constructive decomposition theorem to

\(\displaystyle z_c^\star/q_{t,b}\in F_t.\)

The polytope \(F_t\) has the separation oracle and polynomial inequality-encoding bound established above. The theorem returns, in polynomial time,

\(\displaystyle z_c^\star/q_{t,b}=\sum_{j=1}^{r_c}\lambda_{c,j}\chi^{M_{c,j}},\qquad r_c\leq |E_t|+1.\)

All coefficients are rational with polynomial encoding length. Since \(F_t\) is a face of the matching polytope, its vertices are incidence vectors of actual maximum-cardinality matchings.

Output the positive submasses

\(\displaystyle y_{c,j}=q_{t,b}\lambda_{c,j}\)

with their matching labels, and the positive external masses \(x_a^\star\).

There are at most

\(\displaystyle 2\sum_t(|E_t|+1)=O(m^2+\tau)\)

internal submasses and \(O(m^2)\) external masses. Empty internal graphs cause no difficulty: their unique matching is the empty matching.

The output is feasible by Section 3 and satisfies every optimization criterion by Section 4. Both its encoding length and the time to compute it are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).
