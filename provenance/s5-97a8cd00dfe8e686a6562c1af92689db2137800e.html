The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t>0\), item types \(J\) with per-capita supplies \(\sigma_j\), utilities \(u_{tj}\), rankings, and \(\varepsilon>0\), compute a mass allocation \(X\) satisfying \(\sum_j X_{tj}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t X_{tj}\le\sigma_j\). For \(v_t(X)=\sum_j u_{tj}X_{tj}/\mu_t\), require \(v_t(X)\ge\sum_j u_{tj}X_{t'j}/\mu_{t'}\) for every pair of positive-mass types \(t,t'\), and require that no feasible \(Y\) satisfies \(v_t(Y)\ge(\mathrm e^{1/\mathrm e}-\varepsilon)v_t(X)\) for every \(t\), with strict inequality for some type.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) item types \(J\), per-capita supplies \(\sigma_j\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), and utilities \(u_{tj}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\). Each type has a strict ranking satisfying \(j\succ_t k\Rightarrow u_{tj}\ge u_{tk}\); utility ties may be ordered arbitrarily. Supplies need not sum to one.
A feasible mass allocation is \(X\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T\times J}\) with \(\sum_jX_{tj}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_tX_{tj}\le\sigma_j\). Define \(v_t(X)=\sum_ju_{tj}X_{tj}/\mu_t\).
An allocation is envy-free if \(v_t(X)\ge\sum_ju_{tj}X_{t'j}/\mu_{t'}\) for every \(t,t'\in T\). It is \(\gamma\)-approximately Pareto efficient if no feasible \(Y\) satisfies \(v_t(Y)\ge\gamma v_t(X)\) for all \(t\), with strict inequality for at least one type; \(Y\) need not be envy-free.
The input includes a binary-rational \(0<\varepsilon<\mathrm e^{1/\mathrm e}-1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.

Can an envy-free, \((\mathrm e^{1/\mathrm e}-\varepsilon)\)-approximately Pareto-efficient rational mass allocation always be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?

For finite agents, a polynomial-time algorithm is stated to achieve exact envy-freeness and \((\mathrm e^{1/\mathrm e}-\varepsilon)\)-approximate Pareto efficiency, with polynomial dependence on \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\). [Garg, Tao and Végh](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03359v2)
No result settling this exact support-encoded mass problem was identified in the literature search.

Status and proved results

The full question remains unresolved. I prove the following support-polynomial result, repairing the computational gap identified in the verifier’s report.

Theorem. For every positive integer $k$, an exactly envy-free, rational, $(\frac43+2^{-k})$-approximately Pareto-efficient mass allocation can be computed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,k$.

Consequently, the requested guarantee is computable in polynomial time whenever $0<\varepsilon\le\frac1{10}$. I also resolve the full parameter range when all utility vectors are proportional, including the cases $m=1$ and $\tau=1$.

The algorithm operates on the support. Population replication is used only to prove an equilibrium existence lemma.

1. Reduction and normalization

Call a type active if it has positive utility for some item with positive supply:
$$ A=\{t\in T:\text{some }j\text{ satisfies }\sigma_j>0,\ u_{tj}>0\}. $$

Every feasible allocation gives utility zero to every type outside $A$. Indeed, an item with zero supply receives zero allocation, and an inactive type assigns zero utility to every item with positive supply.

If $A=\varnothing$, the zero allocation is envy-free and Pareto efficient. Henceforth assume $A\ne\varnothing$, and define
$$ d=|A|,\qquad M=\sum_{t\in A}\mu_t,\qquad w_t=\frac{\mu_t}{M}. $$
Thus $w_t>0$ and $\sum_{t\in A}w_t=1$.

For active types use per-capita bundles $x_{tj}=X_{tj}/\mu_t$. Their feasible polytope is
$$ P=\left\{x\ge0: \sum_jx_{tj}\le1\quad(t\in A),\qquad \sum_{t\in A}\mu_tx_{tj}\le\sigma_j\quad(j\in J) \right\}. $$
Let $P_{\mathrm{EF}}$ be its intersection with
$$ \sum_ju_{tj}x_{tj}\ge\sum_ju_{tj}x_{sj} \qquad(t,s\in A). $$
Both polytopes are described by polynomially many rational inequalities of polynomial total encoding length.

Give inactive types zero bundles. Active types do not envy these bundles. Inactive types evaluate every feasible allocated bundle at zero. Therefore envy-freeness on $A$ extends to envy-freeness on $T$.

Define
$$ q_j=\frac12\min\left\{\frac{\sigma_j}{M},\frac1m\right\}. $$
Giving $q$ to every active type is feasible: $\sum_jq_j\le\frac12$ and $Mq_j\le\sigma_j$. It is envy-free because all bundles coincide.

For each $t\in A$, let
$$ a_t=\sum_ju_{tj}q_j>0,\qquad h_t(b)=\frac{\sum_ju_{tj}b_j}{a_t}. $$
Positivity of $a_t$ follows from activity. These positive rescalings preserve envy-freeness and multiplicative Pareto comparisons, and
$$ h_t(q)=1. $$

Finally, set
$$ U=\max_{t\in A,j\in J}\frac{u_{tj}}{a_t}. $$
Every feasible bundle satisfies $0\le h_t(x_t)\le U$. Since $q$ is feasible and has normalized utility one, $U\ge1$.

All quantities introduced so far are rational and have polynomial encoding length.

2. An equilibrium comparison lemma

We use this precise existence result: for finitely many agents with nonnegative linear utilities, arbitrary nonnegative item supplies, unit budgets, and bundle-size bounds of at most one, there exist nonnegative prices and feasible demand-maximizing bundles; every positively priced item clears. This is [Garg–Tao–Végh, Proposition 3.3, with Definitions 3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03359v2#S3.SS1).

Lemma 1. There exist $z\in P_{\mathrm{EF}}$ and prices $p_j\ge0$ such that:

  • Each $z_t$ maximizes $h_t(b)$ subject to $b\ge0$, $\sum_jb_j\le1$, and $p\cdot b\le1$.
  • If $p_j>0$, then $\sum_{t\in A}\mu_tz_{tj}=\sigma_j$.

Proof. Choose an integer $N$ such that every $N\mu_t$ is an integer. Create $N\mu_t$ identical agents of each active type, with item supplies $N\sigma_j$.

This finite instance satisfies all hypotheses of the cited existence theorem. At an equilibrium, average the bundles of replicas of each type. The demand set of a type is the maximizing face of a linear program, hence is convex. Its average therefore remains demand-maximizing.

Averaging preserves affordability and bundle-size bounds. Dividing aggregate allocations by $N$ gives feasibility and the asserted clearing condition. Each other type’s averaged bundle is an affordable bundle of size at most one. Demand optimality therefore implies envy-freeness. $\square$

Let $y\in P$ be arbitrary, and put
$$ b_t=p\cdot y_t. $$
Feasibility of $y$, positive-price clearing, and affordability of $z$ imply
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t\in A}w_tb_t &\le \frac1M\sum_jp_j\sigma_j\\ &=\frac1M\sum_{t\in A}\mu_t(p\cdot z_t) \le1. \end{aligned} $$
Zero-price items contribute zero to the equality.

The bundle $y_t/\max\{1,b_t\}$ is affordable and has size at most one. Thus demand optimality and linearity give
$$ h_t(z_t)\ge\frac{h_t(y_t)}{\max\{1,b_t\}}. $$

For every $b\ge0$,
$$ \frac1{\max\{1,b\}}\ge1-\frac b4. $$
For $b\le1$ this is immediate. For $b>1$, the difference is
$$ \frac1b-1+\frac b4=\frac{(b-2)^2}{4b}\ge0. $$
Consequently,
$$ \sum_{t\in A}\frac{w_t}{\max\{1,b_t\}} \ge1-\frac14\sum_{t\in A}w_tb_t \ge\frac34. $$

3. The exact weighted Nash optimum

Consider
$$ F(x)=\sum_{t\in A}w_t\log h_t(x_t), \qquad F^\star=\max_{x\in P_{\mathrm{EF}}}F(x), $$
where the objective is defined on allocations with positive active utilities.

An optimizer $x^\star$ exists and has positive active utilities. To prove this, note that $P_{\mathrm{EF}}$ is compact and the common allocation $q$ has objective zero. Each utility is bounded above by $U$. If any active utility approaches zero, its positive-weight logarithmic term approaches negative infinity, while the other terms remain bounded above. A maximizing sequence therefore has a cluster point with all utilities positive. Continuity proves attainment there.

For any $z\in P_{\mathrm{EF}}$, the segment from $x^\star$ toward $z$ is feasible and has positive utilities near $x^\star$. Its right derivative at an optimizer is nonpositive:
$$ \sum_{t\in A}w_t \frac{h_t(z_t)-h_t(x^\star_t)}{h_t(x^\star_t)} \le0. $$
Equivalently,
$$ \sum_{t\in A}w_t\frac{h_t(z_t)}{h_t(x^\star_t)}\le1. $$

Applying this inequality to the common allocation gives
$$ \sum_{t\in A}\frac{w_t}{h_t(x^\star_t)}\le1. $$
Each summand is nonnegative, so
$$ h_t(x^\star_t)\ge w_t\qquad(t\in A). $$

Proposition 2. Every optimizer $x^\star$, extended by zero bundles for inactive types, is envy-free and $\frac43$-approximately Pareto efficient.

Proof. Suppose a feasible $y$ satisfies
$$ h_t(y_t)\ge\frac43h_t(x^\star_t)\qquad(t\in A), $$
with strict inequality for at least one active type.

Use the equilibrium allocation $z$ from Lemma 1. The preceding comparison inequalities imply
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t\in A}w_t\frac{h_t(z_t)}{h_t(x^\star_t)} &\ge \sum_{t\in A} \frac{w_t\,h_t(y_t)/h_t(x^\star_t)} {\max\{1,b_t\}}\\ &> \frac43\sum_{t\in A}\frac{w_t}{\max\{1,b_t\}} \ge1. \end{aligned} $$
Strictness holds because all weights and denominators are positive. This contradicts the optimizer’s directional inequality.

Inactive types cannot supply the strict inequality in a Pareto comparison because their attainable utilities are identically zero. $\square$

The exact optimizer need not be rational. The next section proves the required rational approximation algorithm.

4. Exactly feasible rational objective approximation

The following lemma closes the computational gap in the previous argument.

Lemma 3. For every rational $0<\zeta<1$, one can compute a rational $x\in P_{\mathrm{EF}}$ such that
$$ h_t(x_t)\ge\ell\qquad(t\in A), \qquad F^\star-F(x)\le\zeta, $$
where
$$ \ell=\frac14\min_{t\in A}w_t. $$
The running time is polynomial in the data’s encoding length and the encoding length of $\zeta$. In particular, for dyadic accuracies it is polynomial in $\log(1/\zeta)$.

The proof uses exact rational linear programming and a quantitatively specified rational ellipsoid procedure. It requires no feasibility projection.

4.1. A utility polytope with explicit interior

Define
$$ K=\left\{r\in\mathbb R^d: r_t\ge\ell\ \text{for all }t,\quad \text{some }x\in P_{\mathrm{EF}}\text{ satisfies }h_t(x_t)\ge r_t \right\}. $$
This is a compact convex polytope contained in $[\ell,U]^d$.

Put
$$ r^0=\left(\frac12,\ldots,\frac12\right). $$
Every point in the Euclidean ball $B(r^0,\frac14)$ has coordinates between $\frac14$ and $\frac34$. Since $\ell\le\frac14$ and the common allocation has every normalized utility equal to one,
$$ B\left(r^0,\frac14\right)\subseteq K. $$
Thus $K$ has an explicit interior ball even if $P_{\mathrm{EF}}$ has empty interior.

For $r\in K$, define
$$ f(r)=\sum_{t\in A}w_t\log r_t. $$
Its maximum over $K$ equals $F^\star$. Indeed, any witnessing allocation has objective at least $f(r)$, while
$$ r^\star=(h_t(x^\star_t))_{t\in A}\in K $$
and $f(r^\star)=F^\star$.

On $K$,
$$ \|\nabla f(r)\|_2 = \left(\sum_t\frac{w_t^2}{r_t^2}\right)^{1/2} \le\frac1\ell=:G. $$
The mean-value theorem along segments in $K$ therefore shows that $f$ is $G$-Lipschitz there.

Take the rational outer radius
$$ R=dU $$
and put
$$ D=2dU+1,\qquad \rho=\frac{\zeta}{8GD}. $$
Then $K\subseteq B(0,R)$.

The superlevel set
$$ C_\zeta=\{r\in K:f(r)\ge F^\star-\zeta/2\} $$
contains a ball of radius $\rho$. To verify this, set
$$ \theta=\frac{\zeta}{2GD},\qquad c=(1-\theta)r^\star+\theta r^0. $$
Here $0<\theta<1$. Convexity gives
$$ B(c,\theta/4)\subseteq K. $$
For any $r$ in this ball,
$$ \|r-r^\star\|_2 \le\theta\left(\|r^0-r^\star\|_2+\frac14\right) \le\theta D. $$
The last inequality follows from $r^\star\in[\ell,U]^d$ and $U\ge1$. Hence
$$ f(r)\ge F^\star-G\theta D=F^\star-\zeta/2. $$
Since $\theta/4=\rho$, the assertion follows.

Both $\log R$ and $\log(1/\rho)$ are polynomially bounded by the input size and $\log(1/\zeta)$.

4.2. An exact separation oracle for $K$

We use the standard rational linear-programming theorem: a rational linear system can be tested for feasibility in polynomial Turing time, returning either an exact rational feasible point or a rational Farkas certificate; returned numbers have polynomial encoding length.

Write the inequalities defining $P_{\mathrm{EF}}$ as
$$ Ax\le b, $$
including nonnegativity, and write its normalized utility map as $Hx$.

At a rational query $r$, first check $r_t\ge\ell$. A violated coordinate gives a rational separating inequality. Otherwise solve the rational feasibility system
$$ Ax\le b,\qquad -Hx\le-r. $$

If feasible, it certifies $r\in K$. If infeasible, Farkas’ lemma supplies rational $\lambda,\beta\ge0$ such that
$$ \lambda^{\mathsf T}A-\beta^{\mathsf T}H=0, \qquad \lambda^{\mathsf T}b-\beta\cdot r<0. $$
For every $s\in K$, take a witnessing allocation $x_s$. Then
$$ 0 =\lambda^{\mathsf T}Ax_s-\beta^{\mathsf T}Hx_s \le\lambda^{\mathsf T}b-\beta\cdot s. $$
Consequently,
$$ \beta\cdot s\le\lambda^{\mathsf T}b<\beta\cdot r. $$
This is an exact separating inequality. Moreover, $\beta\ne0$: otherwise the certificate would contradict nonemptiness of $P_{\mathrm{EF}}$.

All these linear systems have polynomially many variables and constraints. Their encoding lengths are polynomial in the original input and the query’s encoding length.

4.3. The cutting procedure and its stopping bound

Start with the ball $B(0,R)$ as an ellipsoid. At each rational center $r$:

  • If $r\notin K$, use the preceding separation oracle and weaken its inequality to a central cut containing $K$.
  • If $r\in K$, save $r$ and use the central cut

$$ \nabla f(r)\cdot(s-r)\ge0. $$

The latter cut contains every $s\in K$ with $f(s)\ge f(r)$, because concavity gives
$$ f(s)\le f(r)+\nabla f(r)\cdot(s-r). $$
Its normal is rational and nonzero.

Choose an integer $B\ge0$ such that $2^B\ge R/\rho$, and perform
$$ N=8d(d+1)(B+1) $$
cuts. The rational implementation in the next subsection preserves the chosen half-ellipsoid and decreases volume by a factor at most
$$ \exp\left(-\frac1{4(d+1)}\right) $$
per cut when $d\ge2$. For $d=1$, exact interval bisection provides the required stronger bound.

At least one saved point has value at least $F^\star-\zeta/2$. Otherwise every cut contains $C_\zeta$, so every subsequent ellipsoid contains its radius-$\rho$ ball. But after $N$ cuts the volume is less than that of such a ball, since
$$ \exp\left(-\frac{N}{4(d+1)}\right) < \left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^d. $$
This is a contradiction.

Thus the procedure does not need an oracle that recognizes near-optimality or compares a logarithm exactly with a threshold.

4.4. Rational ellipsoid updates and bit complexity

Here are the quantitative implementation details.

For $d\ge2$, let the current ellipsoid have rational center $c$ and positive-definite rational shape matrix $Q$. For a central cut
$$ a^{\mathsf T}(s-c)\le0,\qquad a\ne0, $$
put $q=a^{\mathsf T}Qa$. The unrounded central-cut update is
$$ \widehat c=c-\frac{Qa}{(d+1)\sqrt q}, $$
$$ \widehat Q= \frac{d^2}{d^2-1} \left(Q-\frac{2}{d+1}\frac{Qaa^{\mathsf T}Q}{q}\right). $$

The containment calculation can be checked after transforming the old ellipsoid into the unit ball and the cut into $s_1\le0$. Writing $z=s_1\in[-1,0]$, the new ellipsoid’s quadratic expression is at most
$$ 1+\frac{2(d+1)}{d^2}z(z+1)\le1. $$
Its volume ratio is
$$ \beta_d= \frac d{d+1} \left(\frac{d^2}{d^2-1}\right)^{(d-1)/2} \le \exp\left(-\frac1{2(d+1)}\right). $$
Indeed, applying $\log(1+x)\le x$ to the two factors gives
$$ \log\beta_d \le-\frac1{d+1} +\frac{d-1}{2(d^2-1)} =-\frac1{2(d+1)}. $$
These are the standard central-cut formulas; see also [Goemans’ ellipsoid notes, pages 3–4](https://math.mit.edu/~goemans/18433S07/ellipsoid.pdf).

To control rational rounding, fix
$$ \lambda_0=R^2\,4^{-N},\qquad g=\frac1{8d(d+1)},\qquad \delta=\frac1{16(d+1)}. $$
Choose positive dyadic grid sizes satisfying
$$ h_c\le\frac{R\,2^{-N}}{64d^2(d+1)}, \qquad h_Q\le\frac{g\lambda_0}{2d}. $$
Choose each within a factor of two of its displayed upper bound.

Compute a rational $s>0$ with
$$ \left|\frac{\sqrt q}{s}-1\right|\le\delta. $$
Such an approximation is obtained by rational bisection, using polynomially many bit operations in the encoding length of $q$ and $\log(1/\delta)$.

Replace $\widehat c$ by $c-Qa/((d+1)s)$ and round every coordinate to the $h_c$ grid, obtaining $c_{\mathrm{new}}$. Set
$$ Q_0=(1+g)^2\widehat Q. $$
Round its entries symmetrically to the $h_Q$ grid, obtaining $Q_{\mathrm{round}}$, and use
$$ Q_{\mathrm{new}}=Q_{\mathrm{round}}+dh_QI. $$

We verify containment and volume reduction.

First,
$$ \left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^2Q \preceq\widehat Q \preceq\frac{d^2}{d^2-1}Q. $$
Consequently, inductively throughout the first $N$ updates,
$$ \lambda_{\min}(Q)\ge R^2\,4^{-N}=\lambda_0. $$
The same lower bound holds for $\widehat Q$ at every update.

Writing $v=Qa/\sqrt q$, direct calculation gives
$$ \|v\|_{\widehat Q^{-1}}=\frac{d+1}{d}. $$
The square-root approximation therefore moves the center by at most $\delta/d$ in this norm. Coordinate rounding contributes at most
$$ \frac{\sqrt d\,h_c}{\sqrt{\lambda_0}} \le\frac1{64d(d+1)}. $$
The combined error is at most
$$ \frac5{64d(d+1)}<g. $$
The triangle inequality in the $\widehat Q^{-1}$ norm proves that the ellipsoid with center $c_{\mathrm{new}}$ and shape $Q_0$ contains the unrounded updated ellipsoid.

Second, the entrywise shape-rounding error has operator norm at most $dh_Q$. Hence
$$ Q_0\preceq Q_{\mathrm{new}} \preceq Q_0+2dh_QI \preceq(1+g)Q_0, $$
where the last inequality uses $2dh_Q\le g\lambda_0$ and $Q_0\succeq\lambda_0I$. Shape rounding therefore also preserves containment.

The total volume ratio is at most
$$ \begin{aligned} \beta_d(1+g)^d(1+g)^{d/2} &\le \exp\left( -\frac1{2(d+1)}+\frac3{16(d+1)} \right)\\ &\le\exp\left(-\frac1{4(d+1)}\right). \end{aligned} $$

Finally, these updates have polynomial bit complexity. The displayed matrix inequalities give
$$ \lambda_{\max}(Q_{\mathrm{new}}) \le (1+g)^3\frac{d^2}{d^2-1}\lambda_{\max}(Q) <2\lambda_{\max}(Q). $$
Thus shape entries have magnitude at most $2^NR^2$. Center displacements are bounded by twice the square root of the current maximum eigenvalue, so centers have magnitude at most $2N2^NR$.

The grid sizes have encoding lengths polynomial in $N$, the encoding length of $R$, and $\log d$. Rounding consequently keeps every center and shape matrix at polynomial encoding length. Every cut normal has polynomial encoding length by the LP certificate bound or the formula for $\nabla f$. Intermediate update arithmetic and square-root bisection therefore also take polynomial time.

For $d=1$, retain the indicated half of the current rational interval. After $N$ bisections its length is $2R\,2^{-N}<2\rho$, and its endpoints have polynomial encoding length.

This proves all implementation properties used in the stopping argument.

4.5. Selecting a saved point and recovering an allocation

Evaluate $f(r)$ at each saved point to absolute error at most
$$ \kappa=\zeta/8, $$
and select one with largest approximate value. Since some saved point has true value at least $F^\star-\zeta/2$, the selected point $\bar r$ satisfies
$$ f(\bar r)\ge F^\star-\zeta/2-2\kappa =F^\star-3\zeta/4. $$

These logarithm evaluations are polynomial-time rational computations. Explicitly, for a positive rational $r$, write $r=2^a s$ with $1\le s<2$. Then
$$ \log r=a\log2+\log s, $$
and, for $z=(s-1)/(s+1)\in[0,\frac13]$,
$$ \log s=2\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2i+1}}{2i+1}. $$
The tail after index $n$ is at most
$$ \frac{2z^{2n+3}}{(2n+3)(1-z^2)}. $$
The same series with $z=\frac13$ evaluates $\log2$. Thus polynomially many terms in the rational input length and $\log(1/\kappa)$ suffice. Since the weights sum to one, approximating each individual logarithm within $\kappa$ approximates their weighted sum within $\kappa$.

Finally solve the exact rational LP
$$ x\in P_{\mathrm{EF}},\qquad h_t(x_t)\ge\bar r_t\quad(t\in A). $$
It is feasible because $\bar r\in K$. Its returned rational solution satisfies
$$ h_t(x_t)\ge\ell,\qquad F(x)\ge f(\bar r)\ge F^\star-\zeta. $$
This proves Lemma 3, including exact envy-freeness and polynomial bit complexity. $\square$

5. From objective accuracy to approximate Pareto efficiency

An objective approximation alone does not provide the needed directional inequality. We now derive it quantitatively.

Suppose Lemma 3 returns $x$, and set
$$ B_0=1+\frac U\ell,\qquad H_0=4B_0^2. $$
For arbitrary $z\in P_{\mathrm{EF}}$, define
$$ g(s)=F((1-s)x+sz), $$
$$ D_z=g'(0) =\sum_{t\in A}w_t\frac{h_t(z_t)}{h_t(x_t)}-1. $$

For $0\le s\le\frac12$, all segment utilities are at least half their initial values. Since both endpoint utilities lie in $[0,U]$,
$$ \begin{aligned} g''(s) &=-\sum_{t\in A}w_t \frac{(h_t(z_t)-h_t(x_t))^2} {((1-s)h_t(x_t)+sh_t(z_t))^2}\\ &\ge-\frac{4U^2}{\ell^2} \ge-H_0. \end{aligned} $$

If $D_z>0$, then $D_z\le B_0$, so $s=D_z/H_0<\frac12$. Integrating the second-derivative bound gives
$$ g(D_z/H_0)-g(0)\ge\frac{D_z^2}{2H_0}. $$
This segment allocation is envy-free with positive utilities, so its objective is at most $F^\star$. Consequently,
$$ D_z\le\sqrt{2H_0\zeta}. $$
The resulting upper bound also holds when $D_z\le0$.

Fix
$$ \eta=2^{-k},\qquad \delta=\frac{3\eta}{8},\qquad \zeta=\frac{\delta^2}{2H_0}. $$
Then every $z\in P_{\mathrm{EF}}$ satisfies
$$ \sum_{t\in A}w_t\frac{h_t(z_t)}{h_t(x_t)}\le1+\delta. $$

Suppose a feasible $y$ gives every active type at least $\frac43+\eta$ times its utility at $x$. Lemma 1 and the expenditure bound imply
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t\in A}w_t\frac{h_t(z_t)}{h_t(x_t)} &\ge \left(\frac43+\eta\right) \sum_{t\in A}\frac{w_t}{\max\{1,b_t\}}\\ &\ge\frac34\left(\frac43+\eta\right) =1+\frac{3\eta}{4} >1+\delta, \end{aligned} $$
a contradiction. This excludes even a weak improvement by that factor for every active type.

Return
$$ X_{tj}=\mu_tx_{tj}\quad(t\in A), \qquad X_{tj}=0\quad(t\notin A). $$
The allocation is rational, feasible, and exactly envy-free. A strict Pareto comparison cannot occur only at inactive types, so it is $(\frac43+2^{-k})$-approximately Pareto efficient.

The encoding length of $H_0$ is polynomial in $m,\tau,L$, and that of $\zeta$ is polynomial in $m,\tau,L,k$. Lemma 3 therefore proves the stated running-time bound.

6. Consequences for the requested guarantee

6.1. The range $0<\varepsilon\le\frac1{10}$

Choose $k=8$. The algorithm’s factor satisfies
$$ \frac43+\frac1{256} =\frac{1027}{768} <\frac{67}{50}. $$

We verify the requisite comparison with $\mathrm e^{1/\mathrm e}$. The exponential series gives
$$ \mathrm e < \frac83+\frac1{24}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac15\right)^r =\frac{87}{32} <\frac{30}{11}. $$
Therefore $1/\mathrm e>\frac{11}{30}$, and
$$ \mathrm e^{1/\mathrm e} > 1+\frac{11}{30}+\frac{121}{1800}+\frac{1331}{162000} =\frac{233621}{162000} >\frac{36}{25}. $$
For $0<\varepsilon\le\frac1{10}$,
$$ \mathrm e^{1/\mathrm e}-\varepsilon > \frac{36}{25}-\frac1{10} =\frac{67}{50} > \frac43+\frac1{256}. $$

Approximate Pareto efficiency is monotone in its factor. Indeed, for nonnegative utilities, a strict dominating comparison at a larger factor is also a strict dominating comparison at every smaller factor. Thus the computed allocation satisfies the requested guarantee throughout this range.

6.2. Proportional utilities: the full range

Suppose there are rational numbers $c_t\ge0$ and $a_j\ge0$ with
$$ u_{tj}=c_ta_j\qquad(t\in T,\ j\in J). $$
Solve the rational LP
$$ \max_{q\ge0}\sum_ja_jq_j \quad\text{subject to}\quad \sum_jq_j\le1,\qquad q_j\le\sigma_j. $$
Return $X_{tj}=\mu_tq_j$.

This is feasible and envy-free because every type receives the same per-capita bundle.

It is also Pareto efficient. For any feasible $Y$, let $y_t=Y_t/\mu_t$ and
$$ r_j=\sum_t\mu_ty_{tj}=\sum_tY_{tj}. $$
Then $r$ is feasible for the displayed LP, so
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\sum_ja_jy_{tj} =\sum_ja_jr_j \le\sum_ja_jq_j. $$
If $Y$ Pareto dominated the proposed allocation, every type with $c_t>0$ would satisfy $a\cdot y_t\ge a\cdot q$, and one such inequality would be strict. Types with $c_t=0$ cannot have a strict utility improvement.

If some $c_t=0$, its bundle can first be replaced by $q$: this is not necessarily feasible, so instead restrict the preceding weighted argument to positive-$c_t$ types and give zero-$c_t$ types zero bundles in the construction. Specifically, let
$$ M_+=\sum_{t:c_t>0}\mu_t. $$
When $M_+>0$, solve the same LP with bounds $q_j\le\sigma_j/M_+$, give $q$ to positive-$c_t$ types, and give zero bundles to the others. For any competing $Y$, the aggregate bundle
$$ r_j=\frac1{M_+}\sum_{t:c_t>0}Y_{tj} $$
is feasible for this modified LP. A Pareto improvement would give
$$ a\cdot r =\sum_{t:c_t>0}\frac{\mu_t}{M_+}a\cdot y_t >a\cdot q, $$
contradicting optimality. If $M_+=0$, all utilities vanish and the zero allocation suffices.

Thus the modified construction is exactly envy-free and Pareto efficient, in polynomial time. It settles every permitted $\varepsilon$ in the proportional-utility case. In particular, it covers $m=1$ and $\tau=1$.

7. What remains open

The general theorem gives factors approaching $\frac43$ from above. The requested factors approach $1$ as $\varepsilon$ approaches $\mathrm e^{1/\mathrm e}-1$. Hence the theorem does not settle the full allowed range.

The finite-agent algorithm, taken as stated in the question, does not by itself supply a support-polynomial implementation. Clearing mass denominators can produce exponentially many replicas. Averaging within types preserves envy-freeness and own utilities—identical envy-free replicas have equal own utilities—but does not remove the running-time dependence on the expanded population.

No hardness conclusion follows from the results proved here.
