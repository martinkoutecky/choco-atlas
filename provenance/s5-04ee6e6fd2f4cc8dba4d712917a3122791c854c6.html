The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite set of valuation types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), and rational piecewise-linear nonatomic densities \(f_t\) on \([0,1]\), consider each population size \(N\) for which every \(N\mu_t\) is integral. For legal recursive \(m\)-proportional divisions of that population, let \(e_N\) be \(1/N\) times the largest number of players envied by any one player after the paper’s trade-elimination convention. Compute \(E^*(\mu,f)=\inf_{(\mathcal A_N)}\limsup_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\ \forall t} e_N(\mathcal A_N)\) and return an asymptotically optimal recursive policy.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Type \(t\) has a nonnegative piecewise-linear density \(f_t\) on \([0,1]\), with rational breakpoints and coefficients and integral \(1\); zero-density intervals are allowed. Write \(V_t(I)=\int_I f_t(x)\,dx\), let \(m\) be the total number of density segments, and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
For every admissible integer \(N\ge2\) with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\) for all \(t\), create \(N\mu_t\) distinct players of type \(t\), each entitled to \(1/N\). Players of the same type may envy one another.
A legal recursive division starts with all players and \([0,1]\). At a node containing interval \(I=[a,b]\) and \(s\ge2\) players, choose an integer \(r\) with \(1\le r<s\); each player \(i\) places a truthful mark \(z_i\) satisfying \(V_{t(i)}([a,z_i])=(r/s)V_{t(i)}(I)\).
Order the marks, resolving ties arbitrarily. Cut strictly between the \(r\)th and \((r+1)\)st marks when they differ, and at their common point otherwise. Assign the first \(r\) players to the left interval and the others to the right, and recurse until each player receives one interval. Thus there are exactly \(N-1\) cuts; no cake is discarded. Nonunique marks and all legal cut locations are policy choices.
After division, draw an envy edge \(i\to j\) exactly when \(V_{t(i)}(P_j)>V_{t(i)}(P_i)\). While a directed cycle exists, rotate its pieces so each participant receives its successor's piece, recompute envy, and continue until the graph is acyclic. The policy chooses the cycles; trades add no cuts.
Let \(\mathcal A_N\) include the division and completed trades, and define \(e_N(\mathcal A_N)=N^{-1}\max_i|\{j\ne i:V_{t(i)}(P_j)>V_{t(i)}(P_i)\}|\) using the final pieces.
Define \(E^*(\mu,f)=\inf_{(\mathcal A_N)}\limsup_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\ \forall t}e_N(\mathcal A_N)\). The optimization asks for this value and an asymptotically optimal recursive policy, meaning a finite algorithm producing legal \(\mathcal A_N\) whose limsup equals \(E^*\).
Is deciding whether \(E^*(\mu,f)\le q\), for a binary-rational threshold \(q\in[0,1]\), in \(\mathrm P\) or \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,L\), including the threshold in \(L\), and include \(N\) when analyzing policy execution.
For finite populations, balanced recursion with \(r=\lfloor s/2\rfloor\), called D&C, has worst-case maximum individual envy \(N-\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor-1\) under the no-trades-possible convention, minimal among recursive proportional algorithms. 1. Proportionality, trading, and exact finite optimization

Write
$$ F_t(x)=\int_0^x f_t(y)\,dy. $$

Lemma 1. At a node containing an interval $I$ and $s$ players, every player present values $I$ at least $s/N$. Consequently, every final player receives value at least $1/N$, including after trading.

$$ \frac rs V_t(I)\ge \frac rN. $$
Every right-assigned player has a mark at least $c$, and therefore values the right interval at least
$$ \frac{s-r}{s}V_t(I)\ge \frac{s-r}{N}. $$
These implications use only monotonicity of cumulative value, so they also cover nonunique marks and ties. Induction proves the node invariant and the leaf guarantee. Trading strictly improves every participant’s own value, so it preserves that guarantee. $\square$

In particular, every child interval has positive length: it has positive value for a player assigned to it.

Lemma 2. Trading never increases any player’s envy outdegree. There are at most $\lfloor N(N-1)/2\rfloor$ rotations, and at most $N(N-1)$ participant occurrences in the entire trading history.

Proof. Fix the geometric partition $\mathcal P$ produced by division. If player $i$ currently holds value $u$, their outdegree is
$$ d_i(u)=|\{P\in\mathcal P:V_{t(i)}(P)>u\}|. $$

A participant receives a piece of strictly greater value $u'>u$. The pieces counted at $u'$ form a proper subset of those counted at $u$: the received piece was counted before the rotation and is not counted afterward. Thus the participant’s outdegree decreases by at least one. Nonparticipants’ outdegrees remain unchanged.

Initially the sum of outdegrees is at most $N(N-1)$. Every rotation has at least two participants. Both bounds follow, and termination follows as well. $\square$

2. A uniform mesh bound for continuous positive densities

This section assumes that every density is continuous and strictly positive on $[0,1]$. Density ratios need not be monotone.

Define
$$ \delta_t=\min_{x\in[0,1]}f_t(x)>0, \qquad D_t=\max_{\text{segments of }t}|f_t'|, $$
and
$$ K=\max_t\frac{D_t}{\delta_t}, \qquad M=\max_{t,x}f_t(x), \qquad C=e^K-1, \qquad B=e^C. $$
These are constants for a fixed society.

Continuity and piecewise linearity imply that $f_t$ is Lipschitz with constant $D_t$. Moreover,
$$ |\log f_t(x)-\log f_t(y)|\le K|x-y|. $$

2.1. Every piece has length of order $1/N$

Theorem 5. Every geometric piece $P$ in every legal recursive division satisfies
$$ \frac1{MN}\le |P|\le \frac BN. $$
The same bounds hold after trading.

Proof. Consider a node interval of length $\ell$, and one child of length $u$, containing the fraction $\theta$ of the node’s players. Let the sibling length be $v=\ell-u$.

There exists a player whose mark gives
$$ V_t(\text{child})\le \theta V_t(\text{parent}). $$
For a left child, choose the player at position $r+1$; their mark is at least the cut. For a right child, choose the player at position $r$; their mark is at most the cut.

Let $A$ and $A'$ be the average density of this type on the child and sibling. Positivity gives
$$ \frac{A'}A\le e^{K\ell}. $$
Thus
$$ uA\le \theta(uA+vA'), $$
and hence
$$ u \le \theta\ell \left(1+\frac v\ell(e^{K\ell}-1)\right). $$
Convexity of the exponential on $[0,1]$ gives
$$ e^{K\ell}-1\le \ell(e^K-1)=C\ell. $$
Therefore
$$ u\le \theta\ell(1+Cv)\le \theta\ell e^{Cv}. $$

Follow a root-to-leaf path. The player-count fractions telescope to $1/N$. Its sibling intervals have disjoint interiors and total length at most $1$. Multiplying the preceding inequalities yields
$$ |P| \le \frac1N \exp\left(C\sum_{\text{sibling intervals }J}|J|\right) \le \frac BN. $$

By Lemma 1, the initial recipient values $P$ at least $1/N$. Since every density is at most $M$,
$$ \frac1N\le V_t(P)\le M|P|, $$
which proves the lower bound. Trading does not change the geometric pieces. $\square$

In particular, any collection of $k$ pieces has length at most $Bk/N$ and value at most $MBk/N$ for every type. Thus $o(N)$ exceptional pieces carry $o(1)$ value uniformly over types.

2.2. Vanishing envy forces a limiting price structure

For a completed outcome, let

Then
$$ 1\le u_t\le MB. $$

$$ p_N(x)=\frac1{N|P|}, $$

$$ p_u(x)=\max_t\frac{f_t(x)}{u_t}. $$

Proposition 6.
$$ \int_0^1|p_N(x)-p_u(x)|\,dx \le \varepsilon_N, \qquad \varepsilon_N=\frac{DB}{N}+MB\tau e. $$

$$ \left|\int_{R_t}p_u(x)\,dx-\mu_t\right|\le\varepsilon_N $$

$$ 0\le \int_{R_t}\left(p_u(x)-\frac{f_t(x)}{u_t}\right)\,dx \le\varepsilon_N. $$

The equality follows because $u_j/N$ is the minimum among type-$j$ own values.

For $x\in P$, Lipschitz continuity gives
$$ \left|f_t(x)-\frac{V_t(P)}{|P|}\right| \le D|P|\le \frac{DB}{N}. $$
Since $u_t\ge1$, these inequalities imply
$$ |p_N(x)-p_u(x)|\le \frac{DB}{N} $$

On bad pieces, both functions belong to $[0,M]$: the bound for $p_N$ follows from Theorem 5, and that for $p_u$ follows from $u_t\ge1$. Integrating proves the first assertion.

Every piece has $p_N$-integral $1/N$, so
$$ \int_{R_t}p_N=\mu_t. $$
This proves the first budget estimate.

Finally,
$$ p_u\ge \frac{f_t}{u_t} $$
pointwise, while every type-$t$ own piece has value at least $u_t/N$. Therefore
$$ \int_{R_t}\frac{f_t}{u_t}\ge\mu_t, \qquad \int_{R_t}p_u\le\mu_t+\varepsilon_N. $$
Subtracting gives the remaining assertion. $\square$

Corollary 7. If a sequence of completed outcomes has $e_N\to0$, then along a subsequence there are numbers $a_t\in[1,MB]$ and measurable functions $h_t:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ satisfying
$$ \sum_t h_t=1 \quad\text{almost everywhere}, $$
$$ p(x)=\max_t\frac{f_t(x)}{a_t}, \qquad \int_0^1p(x)\,dx=1, $$
$$ \int_0^1h_t(x)p(x)\,dx=\mu_t, $$
and
$$ h_t(x)>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{f_t(x)}{a_t}=p(x) \quad\text{almost everywhere}. $$
The vector $(a_t)_t$ is unique among vectors admitting such functions.

The indicators of the sets $R_t$ lie in the unit ball of $L^\infty([0,1])$. By weak-star compactness, with sequential compactness following from separability of $L^1([0,1])$, a further subsequence converges weak-star to functions $h_t$. Testing against nonnegative integrable functions verifies $0\le h_t\le1$ and $\sum_t h_t=1$ almost everywhere.

$$ \int_0^1h_t(x)\left(p(x)-\frac{f_t(x)}{a_t}\right)\,dx=0. $$
The integrand is nonnegative, proving the stated support condition.

$$ \int_0^1g_t f_t=b_t\mu_t. $$
Consequently,
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\frac{b_t}{a_t} = \int_0^1\sum_tg_t(x)\frac{f_t(x)}{a_t}\,dx \le \int_0^1p(x)\,dx=1. $$
Interchanging the two solutions gives
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\frac{a_t}{b_t}\le1. $$
If the vectors differed, strictness in $\log x\le x-1$, together with $\mu_t>0$, would give both
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\log\frac{b_t}{a_t}<0 \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_t\mu_t\log\frac{a_t}{b_t}<0. $$

3. An envy-free construction for arbitrarily many ordered types

Assume now that $\tau\ge2$, every density is continuous and strictly positive, and the types are indexed so that
$$ \frac{f_{t+1}(x)}{f_t(x)} $$
is strictly increasing on $[0,1]$ for every $t<\tau$.

Theorem 8. Under these hypotheses, every admissible $N$ admits a legal recursive division with no envy. Hence
$$ a_N=0 \quad\text{for every admissible }N, \qquad E^*(\mu,f)=0. $$

3.1. Construct the type intervals

For ordered cut coordinates
$$ 0=c_0\le c_1\le\cdots\le c_{\tau-1}\le c_\tau=1, $$
write
$$ W_t=V_t([c_{t-1},c_t]). $$
Maximize the continuous function
$$ \Phi(c_1,\ldots,c_{\tau-1}) = \prod_{t=1}^{\tau}W_t^{\mu_t} $$
over this compact simplex.

A strictly ordered partition gives $\Phi>0$. A partition with a zero-length type interval gives $\Phi=0$. Thus a maximizer has
$$ 0=c_0<c_1<\cdots<c_{\tau-1}<c_\tau=1. $$
Set
$$ a_t=\frac{W_t}{\mu_t}>0. $$

At an interior maximum, differentiation of $\log\Phi$ with respect to $c_t$ gives
$$ \frac{\mu_t f_t(c_t)}{W_t} = \frac{\mu_{t+1}f_{t+1}(c_t)}{W_{t+1}}, $$
or equivalently,
$$ \frac{f_t(c_t)}{a_t} = \frac{f_{t+1}(c_t)}{a_{t+1}}. $$
This differentiation remains valid at density breakpoints because each density is continuous and its cumulative function is continuously differentiable.

Let
$$ I_t=[c_{t-1},c_t]. $$
Strict increase of adjacent ratios implies
$$ x<c_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{f_t(x)}{a_t}> \frac{f_{t+1}(x)}{a_{t+1}}, $$
with the reverse inequality when $x>c_t$.

Chaining these comparisons shows that, for $x$ in the interior of $I_t$,
$$ \frac{f_t(x)}{a_t}> \frac{f_j(x)}{a_j} \quad\text{for every }j\ne t. $$
Define
$$ p(x)=\max_j\frac{f_j(x)}{a_j}. $$
Then $p=f_t/a_t$ on $I_t$, and
$$ \int_{I_t}p=\frac{W_t}{a_t}=\mu_t, \qquad \int_0^1p=1. $$

For a union of consecutive type intervals
$$ I=[c_{p-1},c_q], \qquad S=\sum_{t=p}^q\mu_t, $$
containing at least two types, strict dominance on another type’s interval gives
$$ V_t(I)<a_tS \quad\text{for every }t\in\{p,\ldots,q\}. $$

3.2. Conditional mark order

For any positive-length interval $[a,b]$, define
$$ G_t(x)=\frac{V_t([a,x])}{V_t([a,b])}. $$
For $a<x<b$,
$$ G_t(x)>G_{t+1}(x). $$

To verify this, put $R=f_{t+1}/f_t$. Cross-multiplying the two cumulative fractions reduces the assertion to positivity of
$$ \int_a^x\int_x^b f_t(u)f_t(v)\bigl(R(v)-R(u)\bigr)\,dv\,du. $$
The densities are positive, and $R(v)>R(u)$ whenever $u<v$. The integral is therefore strictly positive.

Consequently, at every common marking fraction, the unique marks occur in increasing type order.

3.3. A legally separable boundary always exists

Consider a node intended to contain precisely types $p,\ldots,q$ on
$$ I=[c_{p-1},c_q], $$
with
$$ S=\sum_{t=p}^q\mu_t. $$
For $p\le j<q$, define
$$ \theta_j=\frac{\sum_{t=p}^j\mu_t}{S}, $$
$$ d_j=G_j(c_j)-\theta_j, \qquad e_j=G_{j+1}(c_j)-\theta_j, $$
where the cumulative functions are conditioned on $I$.

The inequalities $V_t(I)<a_tS$ imply
$$ d_p = \frac{a_p\mu_p}{V_p(I)}-\frac{\mu_p}{S}>0 $$
and
$$ e_{q-1} = 1-\frac{a_q\mu_q}{V_q(I)} -\left(1-\frac{\mu_q}{S}\right)<0. $$

For $p<i<q$,
$$ \begin{aligned} d_i-e_{i-1} &= G_i(c_i)-G_i(c_{i-1}) -\frac{\mu_i}{S}\\ &= \frac{a_i\mu_i}{V_i(I)} -\frac{\mu_i}{S}>0. \end{aligned} $$

Choose the first $j$ for which $e_j<0$. Such a $j$ exists. If $j=p$, then $d_j>0$ by the first inequality. Otherwise $e_{j-1}\ge0$, and the displayed difference gives $d_j>0$. Hence
$$ G_j(c_j)>\theta_j>G_{j+1}(c_j). $$

At this node choose
$$ r=N\sum_{t=p}^j\mu_t, \qquad s=NS. $$
The marking fraction is $r/s=\theta_j$. Thus the type-$j$ mark lies strictly left of $c_j$, and the type-$(j+1)$ mark lies strictly right of $c_j$.

All marks are ordered by type. The first $r$ players are therefore exactly those of types $p,\ldots,j$, and $c_j$ is strictly between the two boundary marks. Cutting at $c_j$ is legal.

Apply this argument recursively to both children. It separates all types into their intended intervals using $\tau-1$ cuts.

3.4. Divide homogeneous intervals and verify envy

For type $t$, let
$$ n_t=N\mu_t. $$
Within $I_t$, use balanced homogeneous recursion to give every type-$t$ player value
$$ \frac{W_t}{n_t}=\frac{a_t}{N}. $$
All players at such a node have the same unique mark, so these cuts are legal.

Players of the same type value all their type’s pieces equally. For a piece $P\subseteq I_j$ assigned to another type $j\ne t$, strict density dominance gives
$$ V_t(P) < \frac{a_t}{a_j}V_j(P) = \frac{a_t}{N}. $$
The integral inequality is strict because the piece has positive length and dominance is strict in the interior of $I_j$.

Thus there are no envy edges. No trades are required. The total number of cuts is
$$ (\tau-1)+\sum_t(n_t-1)=N-1. $$
This proves Theorem 8. $\square$

4. Computing and executing the ordered-ratio policy

4.1. Checking the hypotheses

Continuity and positivity are checked at segment endpoints and joins.

On a common segment, write
$$ f_t(x)=\alpha_tx+\beta_t, \qquad f_{t+1}(x)=\alpha_{t+1}x+\beta_{t+1}. $$
Then
$$ \left(\frac{f_{t+1}}{f_t}\right)' = \frac{\alpha_{t+1}\beta_t-\alpha_t\beta_{t+1}}{f_t(x)^2}. $$
Thus strict increase is equivalent to positivity of the numerator on every nondegenerate common segment.

If the order is not supplied, sort the types by
$$ \frac{f_t(1)}{f_t(0)}. $$
Any order satisfying strict adjacent ratio increase must have these quantities strictly increasing. Checking adjacent pairs in the sorted order therefore recognizes precisely the promised class.

These checks take
$$ (m+\tau+L)^{O(1)} $$
bit operations. On this class the stipulated threshold answer is always yes, since $q\ge0$ and $E^*=0$.

4.2. Exact preprocessing

The logarithmic maximization above is an existence proof. Exact computation instead solves the rational semialgebraic system
