The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_a\ge0\), \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\), intrinsic opinions \(s_a\), initial public opinions \(z_a^0\), rational weights, and block influences \(K_{ab}\), define \(M_\mu(z)=\min\{x:\sum_{a:z_a\le x}\mu_a\ge\tfrac12\}\). Each positive-mass type \(a\) asynchronously replaces its common public opinion by a best response minimizing \(\alpha_a|s_a-y|+\sum_bK_{ab}|z_b-y|+\gamma_a|M_\mu(y,z_{-a})-y|\). Find the minimum integer \(\delta^\star\ge0\) such that, after replacing every \(\gamma_a\) by \(\gamma_a+\delta^\star\), all equilibria reachable from \(z^0\) have the same global median \(M_\mu\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\ge1\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), where \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Input also includes \(s_a,z_a^0\in\mathbb Q\) and nonnegative binary-rational coefficients \(\alpha_a,\gamma_a,K_{ab}\), with \(K_{aa}=0\). Influences may be directed; \(K_{ab}\) is the total coefficient of type \(b\)'s opinion, with no additional mass factor.
Each type has one common public opinion \(z_a\in\mathbb R\). Define the lower mass median by \(M_\mu(z)=\min\{x\in\mathbb R:\sum_{a:z_a\le x}\mu_a\ge\tfrac12\}\).
For \(\delta\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), let \(C_a^\delta(y;z_{-a})=\alpha_a|s_a-y|+\sum_{b\ne a}K_{ab}|z_b-y|+(\gamma_a+\delta)|M_\mu(y,z_{-a})-y|\), where \((y,z_{-a})\) replaces the entire mass of type \(a\)'s opinion by \(y\).
A legal asynchronous move selects one type and any \(y\in\arg\min_{u\in\mathbb R}C_a^\delta(u;z_{-a})\), provided \(C_a^\delta(y;z_{-a})<C_a^\delta(z_a;z_{-a})\); indifferent moves are excluded, and no further tie-breaking is imposed.
An equilibrium has \(z_a\in\arg\min_{y\in\mathbb R}C_a^\delta(y;z_{-a})\) for every type. It is reachable if obtained from \(z^0\) by finitely many legal moves, including zero moves.
Let \(\delta^\star\) be the least nonnegative integer for which the reachable equilibria are nonempty and all have the same \(M_\mu\); output \(\infty\) if no such integer exists. Let \(m=|\{s_a,z_a^0:a\in[\tau]\}|\), and let \(L\) be the total binary input length.

Can \(\delta^\star\) be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is computing it NP-hard?

For finite individual-agent societies with nonnegative integer coefficients, the minimum uniform integer increase is computable in polynomial time ([discrete algorithm](https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p271.pdf)).
Extremal equilibria reachable by improvement dynamics are also computable in polynomial time ([extended results](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/551682/file/551682_Fulltext.pdf)); no result settling the specified mass-based problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Opinions supplied as input are binary rationals, while public strategies and best-response minimization range over the real line.
  • All cost coefficients are nonnegative rationals; directed influences are allowed. K_ab is the total block influence appearing in the supplied objective, without an extra factor mu_b, and diagonal coefficients are set to zero.
  • Legal moves must strictly reduce cost. This follows the intended improvement dynamics and excludes indifferent moves that invalidate the discrete extremal-reachability claim; any minimizing response is allowed when the current opinion is not optimal.
  • Reachability means a finite sequence of legal moves, including the empty sequence; a move changes the whole type's common opinion and recomputes the median using its entire mass.
  • Stability requires at least one reachable equilibrium, preventing vacuous stabilization; infinity records the absence of any stabilizing integer without assuming the discrete existence guarantee carries over.
  • The otherwise unspecified parameter m denotes the number of distinct intrinsic and initial public opinions, and L denotes total binary input length.

1. The unilateral median interval

Fix a type $a$, its opponents’ opinions, and write $w=\mu_a$. Define
$$ R_a(x)=\sum_{\substack{b\ne a\\z_b\le x}}\mu_b. $$
Set
$$ \ell_a= \begin{cases} \min\{z_b:b\ne a,\ R_a(z_b)\ge\tfrac12-w\},&w<\tfrac12,\\ -\infty,&w\ge\tfrac12, \end{cases} $$
and
$$ u_a= \begin{cases} \min\{z_b:b\ne a,\ R_a(z_b)\ge\tfrac12\},&w\le\tfrac12,\\ +\infty,&w>\tfrac12. \end{cases} $$
Whenever an endpoint is required to be finite, it exists because the residual total mass is $1-w$. The thresholds also give $\ell_a\le u_a$. Write $J_a=[\ell_a,u_a]$, allowing infinite endpoints.

Lemma 1. For every $y\in\mathbb R$,
$$ M_\mu(y,z_{-a})=\operatorname{proj}_{J_a}(y), $$
and therefore
$$ |M_\mu(y,z_{-a})-y|=\operatorname{dist}(y,J_a). $$

Proof. If $y<\ell_a$, the endpoint $\ell_a$ is finite. Below $\ell_a$, residual cumulative mass is less than $\tfrac12-w$, so even after adding type $a$ the cumulative mass is less than $\tfrac12$. At $\ell_a$, adding $w$ reaches the threshold. Thus the median is $\ell_a$.

If $\ell_a\le y\le u_a$, residual mass strictly below $y$ is less than $\tfrac12$. This follows from the definition of $u_a$ when it is finite; otherwise the entire residual mass is less than $\tfrac12$. Residual mass at or below $y$, together with $w$, reaches $\tfrac12$, by the definition of $\ell_a$ or by $w\ge\tfrac12$. Hence the lower median is $y$.

Finally, if $y>u_a$, the endpoint $u_a$ is finite. Residual mass first reaches $\tfrac12$ at $u_a$, before encountering type $a$. The median is therefore $u_a$. $\square$

Consequently,
$$ C_a^\delta(y;z_{-a}) = \alpha_a|s_a-y| +\sum_{b\ne a}K_{ab}|z_b-y| +(\gamma_a+\delta)\operatorname{dist}(y,J_a). $$
This is a continuous convex piecewise linear function. Its finite breakpoints are intrinsic or opponent opinions. Sorting these points and scanning the exact rational slopes computes its minimizer interval in polynomial time.

We will also use the consequence
$$ M_\mu(z)\in J_a(z_{-a}) $$
for every type $a$: the projection formula identifies the current median as a point of that interval.

2. An exact polynomial criterion for the pure median case

Assume throughout this section that
$$ \alpha_a=0,\qquad K_{ab}=0 $$
for every $a,b$. Intrinsic opinions then have no effect.

For a fixed $\delta$, call a type active if $\gamma_a+\delta>0$, and let
$$ S_\delta=\{a:\gamma_a+\delta>0\}. $$
An inactive type has identically zero cost and cannot make a legal move. An active type has best-response set $J_a$. Its current opinion is a best response precisely when it equals the current median.

Let
$$ q=M_\mu(z^0),\qquad B=\sum_{a:z_a^0<q}\mu_a,\qquad H=\sum_{a:z_a^0>q}\mu_a. $$

Theorem 2. In the pure median case, all reachable equilibria at a fixed $\delta$ have the same median if and only if both of the following conditions hold:

  • For every $a\in S_\delta$ with $z_a^0<q$,

$$ \mu_a+H\le\tfrac12. $$

  • For every $a\in S_\delta$ with $z_a^0>q$,

$$ \mu_a+B<\tfrac12. $$

When these conditions hold, the unique reachable equilibrium median is $q$.

Proof: completing a profile to an equilibrium

First consider any profile $z$ with median $r$. If an active type has $z_a\ne r$, it can legally move to $r$.

Indeed, $r\in J_a(z_{-a})$, so the new opinion has zero cost. The old cost is
$$ (\gamma_a+\delta)|r-z_a|>0. $$
By Lemma 1, replacing this opinion by $r$ preserves the median $r$.

Repeat for every active type not already at $r$. After at most $\tau$ moves, all active types have opinion $r$, while inactive types retain their opinions. The resulting profile is an equilibrium with median $r$.

Thus every median occurring at a reachable profile also occurs at a reachable equilibrium. In particular, an equilibrium with median $q$ is always reachable from $z^0$.

Proof: characterizing singleton intervals

At any profile with median $q$, write $B_z$ and $H_z$ for the masses strictly below and strictly above $q$.

Suppose $z_a<q$. Lemma 1 gives $\ell_a=q$. Also $\mu_a<\tfrac12$, since otherwise the median could not lie strictly above $z_a$. Residual mass strictly below $q$ is less than $\tfrac12$, while residual mass at or below $q$ is
$$ 1-H_z-\mu_a. $$
Hence $u_a=q$ precisely when this last quantity is at least $\tfrac12$. Therefore
$$ z_a<q \quad\Longrightarrow\quad J_a=\{q\} \ \Longleftrightarrow\ H_z+\mu_a\le\tfrac12. $$

Now suppose $z_a>q$. Lemma 1 gives $u_a=q$. If $\mu_a\ge\tfrac12$, then $\ell_a=-\infty$, so the interval is not a singleton, and $B_z+\mu_a<\tfrac12$ is impossible.

If $\mu_a<\tfrac12$, residual mass strictly below $q$ is $B_z$. Residual mass at or below $q$ is at least $\tfrac12$. Thus the residual quantile at threshold $\tfrac12-\mu_a$ is exactly $q$ precisely when
$$ B_z<\tfrac12-\mu_a. $$
Consequently,
$$ z_a>q \quad\Longrightarrow\quad J_a=\{q\} \ \Longleftrightarrow\ B_z+\mu_a<\tfrac12. $$
The different strictness of the two inequalities comes from the lower median convention.

Proof: necessity

Suppose a stated condition fails for an active type initially away from $q$. The preceding characterization gives
$$ J_a(z_{-a}^0)\ne\{q\}. $$
Since this interval contains $q$, choose $y\in J_a$ with $y\ne q$. Moving type $a$ to $y$ is strictly improving: its old cost is positive and its new cost is zero. Lemma 1 says the new median is $y$.

The completion argument produces a reachable equilibrium with median $y$. It also produces one with median $q$. Thus uniqueness fails.

Proof: sufficiency

Suppose both conditions hold. We prove inductively that every legal move replaces an opinion by $q$ and preserves the median.

Initially, every active type away from $q$ has best-response set $\{q\}$. Active types already at $q$ have zero cost and cannot move; inactive types cannot move either.

After moves to $q$, the masses strictly below and above $q$ can only decrease. Every type still away from $q$ retains its initial opinion and side of $q$. Therefore the inequalities ensuring that its best-response set is $\{q\}$ remain valid:
$$ H_z+\mu_a\le H+\mu_a\le\tfrac12 $$
for a type below $q$, and
$$ B_z+\mu_a\le B+\mu_a<\tfrac12 $$
for a type above $q$.

The next legal move must therefore also be to $q$, preserving the median. Every reachable profile, and hence every reachable equilibrium, has median $q$. $\square$

Consequence for $\delta^\star$

At $\delta=0$, the active types are those with $\gamma_a>0$. For every integer $\delta\ge1$, all types are active.

If the criterion holds at $\delta=0$, then $\delta^\star=0$. If it fails there, an initially active type witnesses its failure. That same type remains active for every larger $\delta$, so uniqueness fails forever. Thus
$$ \boxed{ \delta^\star= \begin{cases} 0,&\text{if the criterion of Theorem 2 holds for }S_0,\\ \infty,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases}} $$

Computing the weighted median, $B,H$, and the inequalities takes polynomial time in $\tau,L$.

Infinite output with equal masses below $\tfrac12$

Take four types with
$$ \mu_a=\tfrac14,\qquad \alpha_a=0,\qquad K_{ab}=0,\qquad \gamma_a=1, $$
and initial opinions
$$ z^0=(0,1,2,3). $$
For every $\delta\ge0$ and every $r\in(1,2)$, the following sequence is legal:

| Moving type | Move | Best-response interval before the move |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $0\longrightarrow r$ | $[1,2]$ |
| $4$ | $3\longrightarrow r$ | $[1,r]$ |
| $3$ | $2\longrightarrow r$ | $[1,r]$ |
| $2$ | $1\longrightarrow r$ | $\{r\}$ |

These intervals follow directly from the residual thresholds $\tfrac14$ and $\tfrac12$. In each row, the old opinion lies outside the interval and the new opinion belongs to it.

The terminal profile is $(r,r,r,r)$, an equilibrium with median $r$. Thus every $\delta$ admits uncountably many reachable equilibrium medians, and
$$ \delta^\star=\infty. $$
This phenomenon requires neither a type of mass at least $\tfrac12$ nor zero median coefficients.

3. Polynomial extremal reachability when best responses are unique

Fix $\delta$, and define
$$ I=\{s_a,z_a^0:a\in[\tau]\},\qquad A=\min I,\qquad B=\max I. $$

The hypothesis in this section is:

For every type $a$ and every opponents’ profile in $[A,B]^{\tau-1}$, the global best-response set consists of exactly one point.

Write that point as $f_a(z)$.

Theorem 3. Under this hypothesis, the componentwise largest and smallest reachable equilibria can be computed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$ and the binary length of $\delta$. Comparing their medians decides whether the reachable equilibrium median is unique.

Monotonicity of the response map

Increasing opponents’ opinions coordinatewise weakly increases both residual quantiles $\ell_a,u_a$.

For a fixed argument $t$, the right derivative of an influence term is
$$ D^+\bigl(K_{ab}|z_b-t|\bigr) = K_{ab}\bigl(2\mathbf 1_{\{z_b\le t\}}-1\bigr). $$
Increasing $z_b$ weakly decreases this derivative. The right derivative of the distance term is
$$ D^+\operatorname{dist}(t,[\ell_a,u_a]) = -\mathbf 1_{\{t<\ell_a\}} +\mathbf 1_{\{t\ge u_a\}}, $$
with the natural interpretation for infinite endpoints. Increasing either endpoint weakly decreases this derivative as well.

Therefore, increasing opponents’ opinions weakly decreases the entire right derivative at every $t$.

A convex piecewise linear function with a unique minimizer has negative right derivative strictly below that minimizer and positive right derivative strictly above it. Otherwise convexity would give a nontrivial interval of minimizers. The derivative comparison consequently implies
$$ z\le z' \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_a(z)\le f_a(z'). $$
Thus $f$ is coordinatewise monotone.

Every reachable opinion belongs to $I$

At a profile whose opinions belong to $I$, every finite cost breakpoint belongs to $I$. A unique minimizer must be a finite breakpoint: a minimizer strictly inside an interval of linearity would force the slope there to be zero, giving multiple minimizers.

It follows inductively that every legal path from $z^0$ stays in $I^\tau$. In particular, the uniqueness hypothesis applies throughout every such path.

Constructing the largest reachable equilibrium

Starting from $z^0$, repeatedly select any type satisfying
$$ z_a<f_a(z) $$
and replace its opinion by $f_a(z)$. Every move is legal and strictly increases one coordinate. Since coordinates stay in $I$, this phase uses at most $\tau(m-1)$ moves.

Let $u$ be its terminal profile. Then
$$ f(u)\le u. $$
Moreover, $u$ bounds every profile reachable from $z^0$.

To prove this, initially $z^0\le u$. If a reachable profile $v$ satisfies $v\le u$, its next legal move replaces one coordinate by
$$ f_a(v)\le f_a(u)\le u_a. $$
All other coordinates remain bounded by $u$. Induction proves the assertion.

Now, starting from $u$, repeatedly select a type satisfying
$$ z_a>f_a(z) $$
and replace its opinion by $f_a(z)$.

The invariant $f(z)\le z$ is preserved. For the moving type, opponents are unchanged, so its new coordinate equals its response. For every other type, monotonicity weakly decreases its response while its coordinate remains unchanged.

This phase strictly decreases coordinates and also uses at most $\tau(m-1)$ moves. At termination, $z=f(z)$, so the resulting profile $E^+$ is a reachable equilibrium.

Let $e$ be any reachable equilibrium. We have $e\le u$. During the second phase, if $e\le z$, then the new coordinate satisfies
$$ f_a(z)\ge f_a(e)=e_a. $$
Thus $e\le z$ remains invariant, and
$$ e\le E^+. $$
Hence $E^+$ is the componentwise largest reachable equilibrium.

Constructing the smallest reachable equilibrium

Reverse the two directions: first perform all available downward moves to $f_a(z)$, then all available upward moves.

At the end of the first phase, its profile $\ell$ satisfies $f(\ell)\ge\ell$ and bounds every reachable profile from below. During the second phase, $f(z)\ge z$ remains invariant. The same finite-chain argument gives a reachable equilibrium $E^-$, and comparison with any reachable equilibrium shows
$$ E^-\le e. $$

Both constructions use polynomially many best-response calculations.

Finally, the mass median is coordinatewise monotone. Indeed, $x\le y$ implies
$$ \{a:y_a\le t\}\subseteq\{a:x_a\le t\} $$
for every $t$, so $M_\mu(x)\le M_\mu(y)$. Therefore every reachable equilibrium $e$ satisfies
$$ M_\mu(E^-)\le M_\mu(e)\le M_\mu(E^+). $$
Since both endpoint equilibria are reachable, uniqueness holds exactly when
$$ M_\mu(E^-)=M_\mu(E^+). $$
This proves Theorem 3.

An efficiently recognizable sufficient condition

The uniqueness hypothesis holds for every nonnegative integer $\delta$ if
$$ \alpha_a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}+\tfrac12, \qquad \gamma_a,K_{ab}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}. $$

Between consecutive breakpoints, a cost slope has the form
$$ \varepsilon_0\alpha_a +\sum_{b\ne a}\varepsilon_bK_{ab} +(\gamma_a+\delta)h, $$
where $\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_b\in\{-1,1\}$ and $h\in\{-1,0,1\}$. This slope is a half-integer and hence cannot vanish.

Also $\alpha_a>0$, so the cost tends to infinity as $|y|$ tends to infinity and attains its minimum. Multiple minimizers would, by convexity, give a nontrivial interval on which the function is constant, contradicting the absence of zero slopes. Thus every unilateral minimizer is unique, for every real opponents’ profile.

4. Median uniqueness is not monotone in $\delta$

The following example satisfies the sufficient condition just proved.

Take three types with
$$ (\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3) = \left(\tfrac38,\tfrac38,\tfrac14\right), $$
$$ (s_1,s_2,s_3)=(0,0,1),\qquad z^0=(1,1,0), $$
and
$$ \alpha_1=\alpha_2=\alpha_3=\tfrac12,\qquad \gamma_1=\gamma_2=\gamma_3=0. $$
The only nonzero influences are
$$ K_{13}=K_{23}=1. $$
Every mass is strictly below $\tfrac12$, and every best response is unique for every integer $\delta\ge0$.

At $\delta=0$, the equilibrium is unique

For $a=1,2$, the unilateral cost is
$$ \tfrac12|y|+|z_3-y|. $$
Its unique minimizer is $z_3$. Indeed,
$$ \tfrac12|y|+|z_3-y| \ge \tfrac12|z_3|+\tfrac12|y-z_3|, $$
with strict inequality over the minimum whenever $y\ne z_3$.

Type $3$ has cost $\tfrac12|1-y|$, whose unique minimizer is $1$. Thus the only equilibrium is
$$ (1,1,1). $$
It is reachable by moving type $3$ first. Hence the reachable equilibrium median is unique at $\delta=0$.

At every integer $\delta\ge1$, two medians are reachable

First, $(1,1,1)$ remains reachable. Initially, type $3$ has $J_3=\{1\}$, so its cost is
$$ \left(\delta+\tfrac12\right)|1-y|. $$
Its move from $0$ to $1$ is strictly improving and produces the equilibrium $(1,1,1)$.

There is also a legal path to $(0,0,0)$:

  • Initially, type $1$ has $J_1=[0,1]$. Its cost is

$$ \tfrac32|y|+\delta\operatorname{dist}(y,[0,1]), $$
uniquely minimized at $0$. It legally moves from $1$ to $0$.

  • At $(0,1,0)$, type $2$ has $J_2=\{0\}$. Its cost is

$$ \left(\tfrac32+\delta\right)|y|, $$
so it legally moves from $1$ to $0$.

At $(0,0,0)$, types $1,2$ have unique best response $0$. Type $3$ has cost
$$ \tfrac12|1-y|+\delta|y|. $$
For $\delta\ge1$, its unique minimizer is $0$, because
$$ \tfrac12|1-y|+\delta|y| \ge \tfrac12+\left(\delta-\tfrac12\right)|y|. $$
Thus $(0,0,0)$ is an equilibrium.

The two reachable equilibrium medians are $0$ and $1$. Therefore, for this instance,
$$ \{\delta\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}: \text{the reachable equilibrium median is unique}\} = \{0\}. $$

In particular, uniqueness is not an upward-closed property of $\delta$, even under globally unique best responses and with all masses below $\tfrac12$.

The completion argument is valid. Moving an active type from an opinion different from the current median $r$ to $r$:

After at most $\tau$ such moves, every active type is at $r$, and every inactive type remains a best responder. Hence an equilibrium with that median is reached.

This establishes the required nonemptiness of reachable equilibria throughout this subclass.

4. Extremal reachability under unique responses

Monotonicity

The right-derivative formulas are correct, including at breakpoints.

Increasing an opponent’s opinion decreases or preserves the right derivative of its influence term. Increasing either residual quantile likewise decreases or preserves the right derivative of the distance term. The intrinsic term is unchanged.

For a convex piecewise linear function with a unique minimizer, the right derivative is strictly negative below that minimizer and strictly positive above it. Otherwise a zero-slope interval would contradict uniqueness.

Consequently, the derivative comparison yields the stated monotonicity of $f_a$.
