Here \(E_{t,R}\) is the type’s posterior expectation after the broadcasts; determine the worst-case scaling in \(|T|\), the diameter of \(H\), \(\varepsilon\), and \(\delta\).

1. Results and scope

The computational question is settled: deciding whether $R_*\le K$ is $\mathsf{NP}$-complete, even with two states, $\varepsilon=1/4$, and directed diameter at most $3$.

For communication length, the following improves the upper bound in the notes:
$$ R_*\le \min\left\{ \sum_{t\in T}(m-|\Pi_t|),\; (\tau-1+d)\left\lceil\frac{d}{\delta\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil \right\} \qquad(\tau\ge2). $$
In particular,
$$ R_*=O\!\left(\frac{\tau d}{\delta\varepsilon^2}\right). $$

I also prove a joint lower bound in the tolerances. For two types, directed diameter $1$, and every binary-rational pair
$$ 0<\varepsilon,\delta\le\frac1{32}, $$
there is an instance satisfying
$$ R_*>\frac1{16384\,\delta\varepsilon^2}. $$
Thus the uniform worst-case scaling for two types is
$$ \Theta\!\left(\frac1{\delta\varepsilon^2}\right) $$
in this parameter range. In particular, the accuracy exponent $2$ is necessary.

The sharp joint dependence on $\tau$ and $d$ remains unresolved. All results below are proved independently of the notes. For $\tau=1$, the endpoint condition holds initially and $R_*=0$.

2. Finite-state bounds and polynomial verification

Write $\mathcal P_{t,r}$ for type $t$'s partition after $r$ messages, and define
$$ W_r= \sum_{t,u\in T}\mu_t\mu_u \Pr_{\mathcal D}\bigl(|E_{t,r}-E_{u,r}|>\varepsilon\bigr). $$
Set
$$ B=\sum_{t\in T}(m-|\Pi_t|)\le\tau(m-1). $$

2.1 Polynomial-length certificates

A message is ineffective if it leaves its recipient's partition unchanged throughout $\Omega$.

Deleting such a message leaves the complete tuple of partitions unchanged. The specified update rule then implies, inductively, that every subsequent tuple is unchanged as well. Deletion therefore preserves the endpoint condition.

Every effective message strictly refines one partition, increasing its number of cells by at least one. A partition has at most $m$ cells, so a schedule containing only effective messages has length at most $B$.

Consequently, every yes-instance has a certificate of length at most
$$ \min\{K,B\}. $$
This argument uses no assumption that agreement persists after further messages.

2.2 Exact arithmetic

For every current cell $C$,
$$ E_t(C)= \frac{\sum_{\omega\in C}\mathcal D(\omega)f(\omega)} {\sum_{\omega\in C}\mathcal D(\omega)}. $$
The denominator is positive because the prior is strictly positive.

To bound bit lengths, take common denominators $Q_{\mathcal D}$ and $Q_f$ for the original prior and values. Their products of input denominators have $O(L)$ bits. Writing
$$ \mathcal D(\omega)=\frac{a_\omega}{Q_{\mathcal D}}, \qquad f(\omega)=\frac{b_\omega}{Q_f}, $$
gives
$$ E_t(C)= \frac{\sum_{\omega\in C}a_\omega b_\omega} {Q_f\sum_{\omega\in C}a_\omega}. $$
These numerators and denominators have $O(L)$ bits, independently of the number of messages.

A message can be simulated by grouping recipient states according to their current cell and the sender's exact expectation. Equality and strict tolerance comparisons are implemented by integer cross multiplication. The weighted endpoint condition is also an exact rational comparison.

Using elementary integer arithmetic, a deliberately loose bound for verifying an $r$-message schedule is
$$ O\!\left((L+r m^2+\tau^2m)L^2\right) $$
bit operations. Certificates have polynomial length because $m$ and $\tau$ are explicitly represented. Hence the decision problem belongs to $\mathsf{NP}$.

2.3 Exact consensus within $B$ messages

For functions on $\Omega$, use the inner product and norm
$$ \langle g,h\rangle=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[gh], \qquad \|g\|_2^2=\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[g^2]. $$

Suppose an edge $(t,u)$ is ineffective. Then $E_t$ is constant on each cell of $\mathcal P_u$, so
$$ \langle E_u,E_t\rangle =\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[fE_t] =\|E_t\|_2^2. $$
The first equality conditions on $\mathcal P_u$, and the second conditions on $\mathcal P_t$. Therefore
$$ \|E_u-E_t\|_2^2 =\|E_u\|_2^2-\|E_t\|_2^2. $$
Thus squared norms are nondecreasing along ineffective directed edges.

If every edge is ineffective, strong connectivity supplies paths in both directions between any two types. Their squared norms must consequently be equal. The displayed identity then gives equality of expectations along every edge, and hence between every pair. Strict positivity of the prior makes this equality hold at every state.

Repeatedly choose an effective edge, stopping when none remains. There are at most $B$ such choices, and the final tuple has exact consensus. Choices inspect the full partition tuple, so the resulting schedule is state-independent.

This proves
$$ R_*\le B. $$
A straightforward implementation takes
$$ O\!\left((L+(B+1)\tau^2m^2)L^2\right) $$
bit operations. Thus constructing some successful schedule is polynomial-time feasible, independently of the numerical tolerances.

3. $\mathsf{NP}$-hardness with two states

We reduce from the standard $\mathsf{NP}$-complete Set Cover problem. Its input consists of a universe
$$ U=\{1,\ldots,n\}, $$
sets $S_1,\ldots,S_s$, and a nonnegative budget $k$.

We may restrict to $n,s\ge1$, $\bigcup_iS_i=U$, and $k\le s$: excluded instances can be classified directly, so this restriction preserves hardness.

3.1 Two-state information propagation

Take
$$ \Omega=\{0,1\},\qquad \mathcal D(0)=\mathcal D(1)=\frac12,\qquad f(\omega)=\omega. $$

Call a type informed if its partition is the singleton partition, and uninformed if its partition is trivial. An informed posterior is $f(\omega)$; an uninformed posterior is $1/2$.

The update rule therefore has the following exact consequences:

  • An uninformed sender changes nothing.
  • An informed sender makes an uninformed recipient informed.
  • Informed types remain informed.

Set $\varepsilon=1/4$. If $b$ is the total uninformed mass, precisely informed–uninformed ordered pairs disagree. Hence
$$ W=2b(1-b). $$

3.2 Graph and masses

Create a root $r$, set types $s_i$, and element types $e_j$. Initially only $r$ is informed.

Use the edges
$$ r\to s_i,\qquad s_i\to e_j\ \text{if }j\in S_i,\qquad v\to r\ \text{for every }v\ne r. $$
Every vertex reaches the root directly, and the root reaches every element through a containing set. The graph is strongly connected and has directed diameter at most $3$. Its actual diameter can be computed and supplied with the instance.

Let $p$ and $q$ be the least powers of two satisfying $p\ge n$ and $q\ge s$. Define
$$ w=\frac1{4p},\qquad \eta=\frac1{64pq}, $$
and assign
$$ \mu_{e_j}=w,\qquad \mu_{s_i}=\eta,\qquad \mu_r=1-nw-s\eta. $$
These are positive dyadic rationals summing to $1$. Moreover,
$$ \mu_r\ge1-\frac14-\frac1{64}>\frac12, \qquad \beta:=s\eta\le\frac1{64p}. $$
Choose
$$ \delta=\frac1{8p},\qquad K=n+k. $$

The root remains informed, so $b\le1/2$. If any element remains uninformed, then
$$ W=2b(1-b)\ge b\ge w>\delta. $$
If every element is informed, only set types can remain uninformed, and
$$ W\le2\beta\le\frac1{32p}<\delta. $$
Thus agreement holds exactly when all element types are informed.

3.3 Exact correspondence with Set Cover

A cover containing $h$ sets gives a schedule of length $n+h$: inform those set types from the root, then inform every element from one selected containing set.

Conversely, consider a schedule informing every element. Each element's first informative incoming message must come from an informed containing set. The distinct sender sets form a cover.

If there are $h$ such sets, the schedule contains at least $n$ messages first informing elements and $h$ separate messages first informing those sets. These messages are distinct because their recipients differ. Hence its length is at least $n+h$.

Therefore
$$ R_*=n+\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{SC}}, $$
and
$$ R_*\le n+k \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{SC}}\le k. $$
The construction and all numerical encodings have polynomial size. Together with Section 2, this proves $\mathsf{NP}$-completeness.

4. An improved uniform communication upper bound

The following argument improves the diameter dependence in the $O(\tau d^2/(\delta\varepsilon^2))$ bound of [Aaronson's Theorem 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0406061). It concerns an input-dependent, state-independent schedule.

4.1 Projection identities for one message

Suppose a message has sender expectation $X$, recipient's old expectation $Z$, and recipient's new expectation $Y$.

The new recipient partition makes both $X$ and $Z$ measurable. Conditional expectation therefore gives
$$ \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[YX] =\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[fX] =\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[X^2], $$
and similarly
$$ \mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[YZ] =\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[Z^2]. $$
Consequently,
$$ \|Y-X\|_2^2=\|Y\|_2^2-\|X\|_2^2, \qquad \|Y-Z\|_2^2=\|Y\|_2^2-\|Z\|_2^2. $$
In particular, the recipient's new squared norm is at least both old squared norms.

Let
$$ q_t=\|E_t\|_2^2,\qquad q_{\min}=\min_tq_t. $$
By Jensen's inequality and conditional expectation,
$$ (\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}f)^2\le q_t\le\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}[f^2]. $$
Thus the total possible increase of $q_{\min}$ is at most
$$ \operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal D}(f)\le\frac14. $$
For the last inequality, use $f^2\le f$ and
$$ \mathbb E[f]-(\mathbb E[f])^2\le\frac14. $$

4.2 A failing endpoint supplies a separated pair

Set
$$ a=\delta\varepsilon^2. $$
If $W>\delta$, then
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t,u}\mu_t\mu_u\|E_t-E_u\|_2^2 &\ge \varepsilon^2 \sum_{t,u}\mu_t\mu_u \Pr(|E_t-E_u|>\varepsilon)\\ &>a. \end{aligned} $$
Because the weights sum to $1$, some pair $i,j$ satisfies
$$ \|E_i-E_j\|_2^2>a. $$

Choose a shortest directed path
$$ v_0=i,\ v_1,\ldots,v_\ell=j, \qquad 1\le\ell\le d. $$
Send successively along this path. It is simple, so each recipient's expectation immediately before its path message is its expectation at the beginning of the phase.

Let $X_0=E_i$, and let $X_k$ be $v_k$'s expectation just after receiving its path message. The projection identities imply
$$ \|X_k-X_{k-1}\|_2^2 =\|X_k\|_2^2-\|X_{k-1}\|_2^2. $$
Let $M'$ be the largest squared norm after these $\ell$ messages. Summing gives
$$ \sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\|X_k-X_{k-1}\|_2^2 =\|X_\ell\|_2^2-\|X_0\|_2^2 \le M'-q_{\min}. $$
The recipient identity at the last message also gives
$$ \|X_\ell-E_j\|_2^2 =\|X_\ell\|_2^2-\|E_j\|_2^2 \le M'-q_{\min}. $$

The triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities now yield
$$ \begin{aligned} \|E_i-E_j\|_2 &\le \sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\|X_k-X_{k-1}\|_2 +\|X_\ell-E_j\|_2\\ &\le (\sqrt\ell+1)\sqrt{M'-q_{\min}}. \end{aligned} $$
Therefore
$$ M'-q_{\min}> \frac{a}{(\sqrt\ell+1)^2} \ge\frac{a}{4d}. $$

4.3 Broadcasting the norm increase

Choose a type attaining $M'$, and an outward directed spanning tree rooted there. Such a tree exists by strong connectivity.

Send along its $\tau-1$ edges in an order placing every parent edge before its descendant edges. Each recipient's new squared norm is at least the sender's squared norm. Induction down the tree shows that every final squared norm is at least $M'$.

One phase therefore uses at most
$$ d+\tau-1 $$
messages and increases $q_{\min}$ by more than
$$ \frac{\delta\varepsilon^2}{4d}. $$

Repeat phases only while their starting endpoint fails. Since $q_{\min}$ can increase by at most $1/4$, the procedure terminates within
$$ \left\lceil\frac{d}{\delta\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil $$
phases. This proves
$$ R_*\le (\tau-1+d)\left\lceil\frac{d}{\delta\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil. $$

All choices depend on functions over the entire state space, never on the realized state. Fixing deterministic tie-breaking therefore produces a permitted state-independent schedule.

5. A sharp joint lower bound in $\delta$ and $\varepsilon$ for two types

Fix binary-rational tolerances
$$ 0<\varepsilon,\delta\le\frac1{32}. $$
We construct a finite instance with exactly two types $A,B$, masses $1/2$ each, and both directed edges. Thus $d=1$.

5.1 Parameters and state space

Choose dyadic powers $c,p$ satisfying
$$ 2\varepsilon\le c<4\varepsilon, \qquad 4\delta\le p<8\delta. $$
Such powers exist by rounding each positive quantity upward to the next power of two. These choices imply
$$ c<\frac18,\qquad p<\frac14. $$
Set
$$ \eta=pc,\qquad h=\frac14,\qquad n=\left\lfloor\frac1{128pc^2}\right\rfloor, \qquad N=n+1. $$
In particular,
$$ 0<\eta<\varepsilon<c. $$

A state specifies independent variables
$$ b_1,\ldots,b_N\in\{0,1\}, \qquad z_1,\ldots,z_N\in\{-1,1\}, $$
where
$$ \Pr(b_j=1)=p, \qquad \Pr(z_j=1)=\Pr(z_j=-1)=\frac12. $$
Set $z_0=1$.

Both types initially know the entire vector $b$. Type $A$ additionally knows all odd-indexed signs, and type $B$ all even-indexed signs.

The state space has $m=4^N$ states. Every state has strictly positive dyadic prior probability. The partitions can be explicitly listed.

5.2 A bounded, delayed stopped sum

Define the commonly known amplitudes
$$ \gamma_j= \begin{cases} c,&b_j=1,\\ \eta,&b_j=0. \end{cases} $$
Recursively define
$$ S_0=0, $$
$$ a_j= \gamma_j\, \mathbf 1\left\{ |S_\ell|<h \text{ for every }0\le\ell\le j-2 \right\}, $$
$$ S_j=S_{j-1}+a_jz_{j-1}z_j. $$
For $j=1$, the condition has no indices and is true. Finally set
$$ f=\frac12+S_N. $$

The stopping condition omits the most recent partial sum. This one-step delay is essential.

To verify boundedness, suppose $T$ is the first index with $|S_T|\ge h$. Before that crossing, $|S_{T-1}|<h$, so
$$ |S_T|<h+c. $$
At most one further nonzero increment occurs: $a_{T+1}=\gamma_{T+1}$ if that index exists, and every subsequent coefficient is zero. Hence
$$ |S_N|<h+2c<\frac12. $$
If there is no crossing, $|S_N|<h$. Thus $f\in[0,1]$ at every state. Its values are dyadic rationals.

5.3 Why future private signs do not leak

For a type $t$, let $\mathcal G_{t,r}$ denote the information consisting of its initial information together with the prefix
$$ z_1,\ldots,z_r. $$

For every $j\ge r+2$,
$$ \mathbb E[a_jz_{j-1}z_j\mid\mathcal G_{t,r}]=0. $$
Indeed:

  • If $t$ does not own $z_j$, that sign remains independent and uniform conditional on the other factors.
  • If $t$ owns $z_j$, it does not own $z_{j-1}$. The coefficient $a_j$ depends only on $b$ and signs through $j-2$, so the unrevealed sign $z_{j-1}$ remains independent and uniform conditional on those factors.

The same argument applies to $j=r+1$ for the type not owning that sign. For the owning type, the coefficient is determined by the shared prefix. Consequently, for $r<N$,
$$ \mathbb E[f\mid\mathcal G_{t,r}] = \begin{cases} \displaystyle \frac12+S_r+a_{r+1}z_rz_{r+1}, & t\text{ owns }z_{r+1},\\[1ex] \displaystyle \frac12+S_r, & t\text{ does not own }z_{r+1}. \end{cases} $$

These are exact identities, including the stopping rule.

5.4 Exact partition evolution

Consider the alternating schedule beginning with $A\to B$. Define
$$ A_r= \left\{ |S_\ell|<h \text{ for every }0\le\ell\le r-1 \right\}, $$
with $A_0=\Omega$.

The following invariant holds after $r$ messages:

  • On $A_r$, each type's information cell is exactly its initial-information cell intersected with the revealed prefix through $r$.
  • Outside $A_r$, both types know $f$ exactly.

At $r=0$, this is the initial partition specification.

Suppose it holds at $r<N$. On a live cell in $A_r$,
$$ a_{r+1}=\gamma_{r+1}>0. $$
The next sender owns $z_{r+1}$. Its message is
$$ \frac12+S_r+\gamma_{r+1}z_rz_{r+1}. $$
The recipient knows $S_r,\gamma_{r+1},z_r$, so this message reveals exactly $z_{r+1}$. Section 5.3 shows that the message contains no dependence on later signs. The updated live cells therefore have precisely the claimed prefix information.

If this prefix leaves $A_{r+1}$, the first crossing occurred at $r$. After the newly revealed sign $z_{r+1}$, every remaining coefficient is zero, so both types know
$$ f=\frac12+S_{r+1}. $$
Previously stopped cells remain stopped with $f$ known. This completes the induction.

At $r=N$, both types know $f$ everywhere.

For $r<N$, their posterior difference is $\gamma_{r+1}$ on $A_r$ and zero outside it. Since $\eta<\varepsilon<c$, their weighted disagreement is exactly
$$ W_r= \frac12\Pr(A_r\cap\{b_{r+1}=1\}) =\frac p2\Pr(A_r). $$
The last equality holds because $A_r$ depends only on variables with indices at most $r-1$, whereas $b_{r+1}$ is independent of them.

5.5 Survival probability

Define
$$ \xi_j=z_{j-1}z_j, \qquad U_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\gamma_j\xi_j. $$
The signs $\xi_j$ are independent and uniform: the transformation is bijective, with inverse
$$ z_j=\prod_{\ell=1}^j\xi_\ell. $$
They are also independent of $b$.

The increments of $U_k$ are independent, have mean zero, and satisfy
$$ \mathbb E[(\gamma_j\xi_j)^2] =c^2\bigl(p+(1-p)p^2\bigr) \le2pc^2. $$
Thus
$$ \mathbb E[U_n^2]\le2npc^2. $$

We use the elementary maximal inequality
$$ \Pr\left(\max_{1\le k\le n}|U_k|\ge h\right) \le\frac{\mathbb E[U_n^2]}{h^2}. $$
For completeness, let $F_k$ be the event that the first crossing occurs at $k$. The future increment $U_n-U_k$ is independent of $F_k$ and $U_k$, with mean zero. Hence
$$ \mathbb E[U_n^2\mathbf1_{F_k}] \ge \mathbb E[U_k^2\mathbf1_{F_k}] \ge h^2\Pr(F_k). $$
Summing over the disjoint events $F_k$ proves the inequality.

The stopped and unstopped sums coincide through their first crossing. Since $h^2=1/16$ and $n\le1/(128pc^2)$,
$$ \Pr\left(\max_{1\le k\le n}|U_k|\ge h\right) \le32npc^2\le\frac14. $$
Therefore, for every $0\le r\le n$,
$$ \Pr(A_r)\ge\frac34, $$
and
$$ W_r\ge\frac{3p}{8} \ge\frac{3\delta}{2}>\delta. $$

5.6 Optimality over all schedules

Initially, $B$'s expectation is the constant $1/2$, so an initial $B\to A$ message is ineffective.

With only two directed edges, consecutive repetitions of an edge are also ineffective: the sender has received no intervening information, and the recipient already knows that sender expectation.

Delete ineffective messages from any schedule. The remaining schedule necessarily alternates, beginning with $A\to B$. Every endpoint of length at most $n$ fails by Section 5.5. The alternating schedule of length $N=n+1$ succeeds by Section 5.4. Thus
$$ R_*=N. $$
Finally,
$$ N\ge\frac1{128pc^2} > \frac1{128(8\delta)(4\varepsilon)^2} = \frac1{16384\,\delta\varepsilon^2}. $$

Combined with Section 4 for $\tau=2,d=1$, this proves the sharp two-type scaling claimed in Section 1. The exponentially large explicit state space is permitted because this communication bound is required uniformly over the remaining input data.

6. Linear support dependence remains necessary

Here is a separate lower-bound family with constant tolerances and diameter.

Use the two-state propagation model from Section 3 on the complete directed graph. Let $\tau=q+1$, with one informed root and $q$ uninformed types. Let $p$ be the least power of two with $p\ge q$, and give each uninformed type mass $1/(2p)$. Give the root the remaining mass, which is at least $1/2$.

Fix
$$ \varepsilon=\frac14,\qquad \delta=\frac18. $$
After $j$ additional types have become informed,
$$ b=\frac{q-j}{2p}, \qquad W=2b(1-b)\ge b. $$
Agreement requires
$$ j\ge q-\frac p4>\frac q2, $$
because $p<2q$. Each message informs at most one new type. Thus
$$ R_*=\Omega(\tau). $$
Informing every remaining type takes $q$ messages, so this family has $R_*=\Theta(\tau)$ with $d=1$.

This family and Section 5 establish separate necessary dependencies. They do not establish that the support factor multiplies the tolerance factors.

7. Running times and the remaining question

Let $e=|E(H)|$ and
$$ b=\min\{K,B\}. $$
Enumerating all schedules of length at most $b$ and verifying their endpoints gives an exact decision algorithm with the coarse running-time bound
$$ O\!\left( (e+1)^b (L+b m^2+\tau^2m)L^2 \right). $$
The numerical budget $K$ is capped by $B$; its binary encoding creates no certificate-length problem.

The exact-consensus construction of Section 2 runs in
$$ O\!\left((L+(B+1)\tau^2m^2)L^2\right) $$
bit operations. This is polynomial in the binary input length.

For the improved communication construction, let
$$ Q=\left\lceil\frac d{\delta\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil. $$
A straightforward exact implementation uses polynomial work per phase; one sufficient coarse bound is
$$ O\!\left((L+Q\tau^3m^3)L^2\right) $$
bit operations. Here sums defining squared norms may have $O(mL)$-bit denominators. This bound depends on the numerical quantities $1/\delta$ and $1/\varepsilon$, which can be exponential in their encoding lengths. In contrast, $d\le\tau-1$ for $\tau\ge2$.

The established communication bounds are therefore
$$ R_*=O\!\left(\frac{\tau d}{\delta\varepsilon^2}\right), $$
with sharp tolerance dependence already for two types, and necessary linear support dependence at constant tolerances. Determining the sharp joint dependence on $\tau$ and $d$ requires an additional argument.
