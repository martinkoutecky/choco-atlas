Theorem 3.4 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given candidates \(C\), a bipartite action graph \(A\), a rational campaign mass \(\gamma>0\), a target \(c^\star\), a fixed tie-breaking priority, and a finite-support background society \(\mu\) of total mass \(1-\gamma\) over pairwise reports, does there exist a distribution \(\nu\) of total mass \(\gamma\) over strict orientations of every edge in \(A\) such that \(c^\star\) wins under pBorda? Campaign members share a utility ranking that places \(c^\star\) above the current winner; cycles in their reports are permitted.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(C\) contain \(m\geq2\) candidates, let \(A\subseteq\binom{C}{2}\) be a simple bipartite action graph, and fix a strict tie-breaking priority and target \(c^\star\in C\).
A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts listed types, not possible types. A type is an irreflexive, antisymmetric pairwise report; comparisons may be omitted and cycles are permitted.
The input background society \(\mu\) has total mass \(1-\gamma\), where \(\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}\) and \(0<\gamma\leq1\). Empty support is allowed when \(\gamma=1\). Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
For any society \(\rho\), define \(M_{ij}(\rho)=\sum_{R:iRj}\rho(R)\) and the pBorda score \(s_i(\rho)=\sum_{j\in C\setminus\{i\}}M_{ij}(\rho)/(M_{ij}(\rho)+M_{ji}(\rho))\), with \(0/0=0\). The winner maximizes this score, using the fixed priority to break ties.
The current winner is the winner of \(\mu\); campaign members share a strict utility ranking placing \(c^\star\) above that winner. This ranking imposes no restrictions on their reports.
An admissible campaign society \(\nu\) has total mass \(\gamma\), arbitrary finite support with positive binary-rational masses, and each report orients every edge of \(A\) exactly once and compares no pair outside \(A\); cycles are allowed.
Decide whether an admissible \(\nu\) makes \(c^\star\) the winner of \(\mu+\nu\).

Is this continuous pBorda manipulation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|A|,L\), or is it NP-hard?

Discrete pBorda manipulation by one voter required to orient every action edge is NP-complete even for bipartite action graphs. [Source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2016/pdfs/p358.pdf)
No result settling this exact continuous problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Total society mass is normalized to one, so the campaign mass satisfies 0 < gamma <= 1; the endpoint permits an empty background support.
  • Background types are irreflexive, antisymmetric reports that may omit comparisons and contain cycles.
  • Campaign reports must compare every action edge and no pair outside the action graph.
  • Campaign societies have finite support with binary-rational masses and no imposed support-size bound, following the required society convention.
  • The current winner means the pBorda winner of the background society; the shared strict utility ranking is an eligibility promise rather than a constraint on strategic reports.
  • Uncompared pairs contribute zero to pBorda, and the given strict priority resolves all score ties.
  • The candidate count is denoted by m, and L is the total binary input length.

Result

The problem is solvable in polynomial time. In fact, bipartiteness is unnecessary: the same algorithm works for every simple action graph under the stated report rules.

The proof has two ingredients: campaign societies realize exactly the rational points of a box of edge marginals, and all final scores are affine functions of those marginals.

1. Exact characterization of campaign marginals

Write \(q=|A|\). For each edge \(e\in A\), choose an ordered labeling \((u_e,v_e)\), and define

\(x_e=M_{u_ev_e}(\nu)\).

Because every campaign report orients every action edge exactly once and the campaign has total mass \(\gamma\),

\(0\leq x_e\leq\gamma,\qquad M_{v_eu_e}(\nu)=\gamma-x_e.\)

All coordinates are rational because campaign masses are rational.

Lemma. Every rational vector \(x\in[0,\gamma]^q\) is realized by an admissible campaign society with at most \(q+1\) positive-mass types.

Proof. Sort the distinct values in \(\{0,\gamma\}\cup\{x_e:e\in A\}\):

\(0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_k=\gamma.\)

There are at most \(q+2\) distinct values, so \(k\leq q+1\). For each interval \((t_{\ell-1},t_\ell)\), choose its midpoint \(z_\ell\), and define a report \(R_\ell\) by orienting edge \(e\) as follows:

  • \(u_eR_\ell v_e\) if \(z_\ell<x_e\);
  • \(v_eR_\ell u_e\) otherwise.

Make no comparisons outside \(A\), and assign mass

\(\nu(R_\ell)=t_\ell-t_{\ell-1}.\)

Each mass is positive and rational, and their sum is \(\gamma\). Each report is irreflexive and antisymmetric and orients every action edge exactly once. Cycles cause no obstruction because they are permitted.

For a fixed edge \(e\), the intervals whose midpoints satisfy \(z_\ell<x_e\) partition \((0,x_e)\). Their masses therefore sum to \(x_e\), proving

\(M_{u_ev_e}(\nu)=x_e.\)

This also covers \(A=\varnothing\): use the empty report with mass \(\gamma\). Thus the construction realizes every claimed vector. \(\square\)

Consequently, there are no additional consistency constraints coupling different edge marginals.

2. Final scores are affine

Compute the background quantities

\(a_{ij}=M_{ij}(\mu)\)

from the listed support. For each action edge \(e\), put

\(d_e=a_{u_ev_e}+a_{v_eu_e}+\gamma.\)

Since \(\gamma>0\), we have \(d_e>0\). The campaign adds exactly \(\gamma\) to the total comparison mass on this edge, independently of its orientation distribution. Its contributions to the endpoint scores are therefore

\(\displaystyle \frac{a_{u_ev_e}+x_e}{d_e}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{a_{v_eu_e}+\gamma-x_e}{d_e}.\)

For each candidate \(i\), define the fixed contribution from pairs outside \(A\):

\(\displaystyle b_i=\sum_{\substack{j\ne i\\\{i,j\}\notin A}}\frac{a_{ij}}{a_{ij}+a_{ji}},\)

using the stipulated value \(0\) when the denominator is zero. Campaign reports never compare these pairs, so their contributions remain fixed.

Thus the final score is exactly the affine function

\(\displaystyle S_i(x)=b_i+\sum_{\substack{e\in A\\u_e=i}}\frac{a_{u_ev_e}+x_e}{d_e}+\sum_{\substack{e\in A\\v_e=i}}\frac{a_{v_eu_e}+\gamma-x_e}{d_e}.\)

All its coefficients are rational.

3. A linear program handling tie-breaking exactly

Let \(H\) consist of the candidates other than \(c^\star\) that precede \(c^\star\) in the tie-breaking priority. Let \(K=C\setminus(H\cup\{c^\star\})\).

By the definition of the winner, \(c^\star\) wins exactly when

\(S_{c^\star}(x)>S_i(x)\quad(i\in H),\qquad S_{c^\star}(x)\geq S_i(x)\quad(i\in K).\)

Introduce a common strictness margin \(\delta\), and solve the rational linear program

\(\displaystyle \text{maximize }\delta\)

subject to

\(0\leq x_e\leq\gamma\quad(e\in A),\qquad 0\leq\delta\leq1,\)

\(S_i(x)+\delta\leq S_{c^\star}(x)\quad(i\in H),\)

\(S_i(x)\leq S_{c^\star}(x)\quad(i\in K).\)

The algorithm answers yes precisely when this program is feasible and its optimal value is positive.

Correctness, forward direction. Suppose an admissible campaign makes \(c^\star\) win. Its marginals give a rational vector \(x\) in the box. If \(H\ne\varnothing\), choose

\(\displaystyle \delta=\min\left(\{1\}\cup\{S_{c^\star}(x)-S_i(x):i\in H\}\right).\)

Every gap in this finite minimum is positive, so \(\delta>0\), and all program constraints hold. If \(H=\varnothing\), choose \(\delta=1\). In either case, the optimal value is positive.

Correctness, reverse direction. Suppose the program is feasible and has positive optimal value. A bounded rational linear program has a rational optimal solution; take such a solution \((x,\delta)\). Since \(\delta>0\), the constraints give all required strict inequalities for \(H\) and weak inequalities for \(K\). The lemma constructs an admissible rational campaign realizing \(x\). Its scores are the functions \(S_i(x)\), so \(c^\star\) wins.

This argument deals with exact ties and does not rely on numerical tolerances.

4. Polynomial running time and representation size

We use the standard rational linear programming theorem: feasibility and bounded optimization for explicitly given rational linear programs can be performed exactly in time polynomial in their binary encoding length, returning a rational optimum when one exists. Polynomial-time solvability originates with [Khachiyan’s linear programming algorithm](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan42319).

Its hypotheses hold here:

  • The program has \(q+1\) variables and \(2q+m+1\) scalar inequalities.
  • Every coefficient is rational and explicitly computable.
  • Its feasible set is closed and bounded by the box constraints, so every feasible instance has an attained optimum.
  • Its binary encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,q,L\).

For the last point, each \(a_{ij}\) is a sum of input masses. A common denominator can be chosen as the product of the input denominators; the product’s bit length is at most the sum of their bit lengths. Hence these sums have polynomial bit length. Adding \(\gamma\), taking the reciprocals of the positive \(d_e\), and summing at most \(m-1\) rational terms to form each score preserves polynomial bit length. The quantities can be computed by scanning the explicitly listed types, without enumerating possible types.

Exact rational linear programming therefore decides the problem in polynomial time. Moreover, when the answer is yes, it returns marginals of polynomial bit length. Sorting those marginals and applying the lemma produces at most \(q+1\) campaign types, with polynomial total encoding length.

5. Why discrete hardness does not transfer

For a campaign consisting of one report of mass \(\gamma\), each marginal must belong to \(\{0,\gamma\}\). Arbitrary rational mixtures instead permit every rational point of \([0,\gamma]^q\), as proved above. Thus the discrete problem imposes endpoint constraints that are absent from this continuous problem. The stated discrete hardness result does not obstruct the linear programming algorithm.

The utility-ranking premise adds no constraints to this formulation because the task explicitly allows unrestricted campaign reports.
