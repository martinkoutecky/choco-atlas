The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational ideal locations \(p_t\) in a rational interval \(I\), and a positive rational piecewise-linear project-cost function \(G(q)\) on \(0\le q\le\mu\), find a finite partition \(q^1,\ldots,q^K\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\setminus\{0\}\) with \(\sum_jq^j=\mu\) such that no nonzero mass vector \(r\le\sum_{j:c(q^j)>c(r)}q^j\) exists, where \(c(q)=\frac{G(q)+\min_{p\in I}\sum_tq_t|p-p_t|}{\sum_tq_t}\); if no such partition exists, decide that.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type has a rational ideal location \(p_t\in I=[a,b]\), with rational \(a\le b\).
The project cost \(G\) is continuous and strictly positive on \([0,\mu]=\{q:0\le q_t\le\mu_t\}\), explicitly specified by a rational polyhedral subdivision with \(m\) cells and a rational affine formula on each cell, agreeing on intersections. Let \(L\) be the total binary input length.
Mass is divisible, and types may be split among jurisdictions. For any nonzero coalition \(q\in[0,\mu]\), its members all bear cost \(c(q)=\bigl(G(q)+\min_{p\in I}\sum_tq_t|p-p_t|\bigr)/\sum_tq_t\). Monetary contributions may be negative; any minimizing project location is allowed.
A finite partition consists of nonzero \(q^1,\ldots,q^K\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{T}\) with \(\sum_jq^j=\mu\), with no prescribed bound on \(K\). It is core-stable if there is no nonzero \(r\in[0,\mu]\) satisfying \(r\le\sum_{j:c(q^j)>c(r)}q^j\), componentwise. Only strict improvements block. Exact constructed coordinates use standard real-algebraic encoding.

Is finding such a finite core-stable partition, or determining that none exists, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finite societies, a core-stable partition always exists under egalitarian sharing, even with arbitrary positive coalition-dependent project costs; this is an existence result without a polynomial running-time guarantee ([Bogomolnaia et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0503035)).
No result settling the precise continuous problem above was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Resolved the unspecified piecewise-linear input representation as an explicit rational polyhedral subdivision with compatible affine formulas; m counts its cells and L is the total binary input length.
  • Interpreted piecewise-linear as continuous and retained strict positivity on the entire stated domain, including the zero vector.
  • Made unrestricted monetary contributions, including subsidies, explicit; minimizing-location ties require no tie-breaking because they give the same total cost.
  • Specified standard exact real-algebraic encoding for constructed partition coordinates, without restricting feasible coalitions to rational masses.

Partial result: polynomial-time construction with binary multiplicities

There is a stronger algorithmic result than the ones in the notes:

Every instance admits a core-stable partition that can be constructed in polynomial time as a polynomial-size list of rational coalition vectors with binary-encoded multiplicities.

Expanding this representation into an explicit list of jurisdictions can require exponential time, and some instances require exponentially many jurisdictions in every stable partition. Thus explicit-list construction is not polynomial-time solvable. This does not, by itself, establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness of that search problem.

The proof below settles the compressed-output question for a precise representation. The formal $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness classification of the original explicit-list task remains unclaimed.

1. Notation and output representation

For a coalition vector $q$, write

$$ Q(q)=\sum_{t\in T}q_t, \qquad D(q)=\min_{p\in[a,b]}\sum_{t\in T}q_t|p-p_t|. $$

For $q\ne0$, its cost is

$$ c(q)=\frac{G(q)+D(q)}{Q(q)}. $$

Use closed cells $P_1,\ldots,P_m$ of the rational subdivision. If necessary, taking closures preserves their affine formulas by continuity. Write

$$ P_i=\{q:A_iq\le b_i\}, \qquad G(q)=\gamma_i+\alpha_i\cdot q \quad\text{on }P_i. $$

Here the cells use the usual explicit rational inequality encoding; equations can be written as two inequalities. Append the domain inequalities $0\le q\le\mu$ if they are implicit.

A multiplicity representation is a list

$$ (v^1,N_1),\ldots,(v^H,N_H), $$

where each $v^\ell$ is a nonzero rational coalition vector and each $N_\ell$ is a positive integer encoded in binary. Its expansion consists of $N_\ell$ copies of $v^\ell$ for each $\ell$. It represents a partition precisely when

$$ \sum_{\ell=1}^H N_\ell v^\ell=\mu. $$

This representation is specified explicitly here; it changes the output requirement from listing every jurisdiction individually.

2. A minimizing coalition can be chosen at a truncated-cell vertex

Lemma 1. For every nonzero rational residual vector $u$ with $0\le u\le\mu$, one can compute, in time polynomial in the instance length and the encoding length of $u$, a coalition minimizing $c(q)$ over

$$ 0\ne q\le u. $$

Moreover, one can choose the minimizer to be a vertex of

$$ P_i\cap[0,u] $$

for some input cell $P_i$.

Transportation cost

Define the rational numbers

$$ d_{kt}=|p_k-p_t|. $$

Then

$$ D(q)=\min_{1\le k\le\tau}d_k\cdot q. $$

To justify this, suppose $q\ne0$. The location objective is continuous and affine between consecutive distinct ideal locations. On any such interval, a minimum can be moved to an endpoint. To the left of the smallest ideal location carrying positive mass, its slope is $-Q(q)$; to the right of the largest, its slope is $Q(q)$. Consequently, some minimum occurs at an ideal location. The identity also holds at $q=0$, where both sides vanish.

Existence of a minimum

Continuity, strict positivity, and compactness give

$$ g=\min_{q\in[0,\mu]}G(q)>0, \qquad G_{\max}=\max_{q\in[0,\mu]}G(q). $$

These rational extrema are computable by linear programming over the cells. Put

$$ C=G_{\max}+b-a. $$

Since $Q(q)\le1$ and all ideal locations lie in $[a,b]$,

$$ c(q)\ge\frac{g}{Q(q)}, \qquad G(q)+D(q)\le C. $$

For a nonzero residual $u$, define

$$ \eta=\frac{gQ(u)}{C}>0. $$

We have $\eta\le Q(u)$. If $0<Q(q)<\eta$, then

$$ c(q)\ge\frac{g}{Q(q)} > \frac{C}{Q(u)} \ge c(u). $$

Therefore the unrestricted minimum is attained on the nonempty compact set

$$ \{q:0\le q\le u,\ Q(q)\ge\eta\}. $$

Exact linear programs

For a cell-location pair $(i,k)$, consider

$$ f_{ik}(q)= \frac{\gamma_i+(\alpha_i+d_k)\cdot q}{Q(q)}. $$

Make the substitutions

$$ z=\frac1{Q(q)}, \qquad y=zq. $$

The corresponding linear program is

$$ \begin{aligned} \text{minimize}\quad& \gamma_i z+(\alpha_i+d_k)\cdot y\\ \text{subject to}\quad& A_i y\le b_i z,\\ &0\le y\le uz,\\ &\sum_t y_t=1,\\ &\eta z\le1,\\ &z\ge0. \end{aligned} $$

Every feasible point has $z>0$: otherwise $y\le uz=0$, contradicting $\sum_t y_t=1$. Its inverse image $q=y/z$ belongs to $P_i\cap[0,u]$ and satisfies $Q(q)\ge\eta$. Conversely, every such coalition yields a feasible point with exactly the displayed objective value.

The feasible region is bounded, since $z\le1/\eta$ and $0\le y_t\le1$. Discard infeasible pairs and take the smallest optimum over all $m\tau$ pairs. The transportation identity shows that this value is exactly

$$ \lambda=\min_{0\ne q\le u}c(q). $$

The algorithmic fact used here is exact polynomial-time solvability of rational linear programming, including feasibility testing and computation of rational optimal values and solutions. These hypotheses hold for the explicitly described rational programs above. See [Gleixner and Steffy, Section 1.3](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/7507.pdf).

Selecting a vertex without losing optimality

Choose a pair $(i,k)$ attaining $\lambda$, and let

$$ P=P_i\cap[0,u]. $$

Define the affine function

$$ F(q)=\gamma_i+(\alpha_i+d_k)\cdot q-\lambda Q(q). $$

For every nonzero $q\in P$,

$$ f_{ik}(q)\ge c(q)\ge\lambda, $$

so $F(q)\ge0$. If $0\in P$, then

$$ F(0)=\gamma_i=G(0)>0. $$

The chosen pair has an optimizer attaining $F(q)=0$. Hence

$$ \mathcal F=\{q\in P:F(q)=0\} $$

is a nonempty exposed face of $P$, and it excludes the origin.

Choose a vertex of this face by successively minimizing its coordinates, fixing each minimum before proceeding to the next coordinate. After all $\tau$ coordinates are fixed, the remaining set is a singleton $\{v\}$.

For completeness, $v$ is a vertex of $P$. If $v$ were the midpoint of two points $x,y\in P$, nonnegativity of $F$ and $F(v)=0$ would force $F(x)=F(y)=0$. The first coordinate minimization would then force $x_1=y_1=v_1$; each subsequent coordinate minimization gives the corresponding equality. Thus $x=y=v$.

Finally,

$$ f_{ik}(v)=\lambda, \qquad \lambda\le c(v)\le f_{ik}(v), $$

so $c(v)=\lambda$. This proves the lemma. Polynomial encoding bounds for the vertex selection and the successive construction are established below.

3. The batched construction

Start with $u^0=\mu$. At a stage with nonzero residual $u^{\ell-1}$:

  • Use Lemma 1 to choose a minimum-cost coalition $v^\ell$ that is a vertex of some $P_i\cap[0,u^{\ell-1}]$.
  • Compute

$$ N_\ell= \left\lfloor \min_{t:v^\ell_t>0} \frac{u^{\ell-1}_t}{v^\ell_t} \right\rfloor. $$

  • Record $(v^\ell,N_\ell)$ and set

$$ u^\ell=u^{\ell-1}-N_\ell v^\ell. $$

Since $v^\ell\le u^{\ell-1}$, we have $N_\ell\ge1$, and the new residual is nonnegative.

The next two sections prove that this algorithm has polynomially many stages and polynomial bit complexity.

4. Polynomially many stages

There are two kinds of stages.

An exhausting stage has a coordinate satisfying

$$ v^\ell_t=u^{\ell-1}_t>0. $$

At such a stage the minimum ratio is $1$, so $N_\ell=1$, and that residual coordinate becomes zero. There are at most $\tau$ exhausting stages.

At every other stage, the selected coalition is a vertex of an original input cell.

The original-cell vertex property

Lemma 2. Let $v$ be a vertex of $P_i\cap[0,u]$. If there is no coordinate with $v_t=u_t>0$, then $v$ is a vertex of $P_i$.

Proof. Suppose $v$ is not a vertex of $P_i$. There are a nonzero direction $h$ and a positive number $\varepsilon$ such that

$$ v+\varepsilon h,\ v-\varepsilon h\in P_i. $$

Because $P_i$ lies in the nonnegative orthant, $v_t=0$ implies $h_t=0$. For every coordinate with $v_t>0$, the hypothesis gives $v_t<u_t$. By decreasing $\varepsilon$, both perturbed points therefore satisfy the upper bounds $q_t\le u_t$. They belong to $P_i\cap[0,u]$, contradicting that $v$ is its vertex. $\square$

A uniform lower bound on positive vertex coordinates

Clear denominators separately in each input-cell inequality. Every resulting integer coefficient and right-hand side has absolute value at most

$$ M=2^{2L}. $$

Indeed, every numerator and the product of denominators used in any row have binary length bounded by the total input length.

Set

$$ B= \tau\left(2L+\left\lceil\log_2\max\{2,\tau\}\right\rceil\right)+1, \qquad \delta=2^{-B}. $$

At a vertex of an input cell, choose $\tau$ linearly independent active constraint rows. Their integer coefficient matrix $A$ satisfies

$$ 0<|\det A| \le\tau!M^\tau <2^B. $$

Cramer's rule expresses each coordinate as an integer divided by $\det A$. Therefore every positive coordinate of every input-cell vertex satisfies

$$ v_t\ge\delta. $$

This argument also covers lower-dimensional cells: a vertex still has active constraint normals spanning the ambient space.

Each nonexhausting stage halves a coordinate

At a nonexhausting stage, select a coordinate $t$ attaining the minimum ratio, and write

$$ x=\frac{u_t}{v_t}, \qquad N=\lfloor x\rfloor. $$

Since $N\le x<N+1$,

$$ \frac{u_t-Nv_t}{u_t} = 1-\frac{N}{x} < \frac1{N+1} \le\frac12. $$

Charge this stage to coordinate $t$. By Lemma 2, its selected coalition is an original-cell vertex, so before the stage,

$$ u_t\ge v_t\ge2^{-B}. $$

Residual coordinates never increase and initially are at most $1$. Before the $k$th stage charged to a particular coordinate, its residual is at most $2^{-(k-1)}$. Thus such a stage can occur only if

$$ 2^{-(k-1)}\ge2^{-B}, $$

which implies $k\le B+1$.

There are at most $\tau(B+1)$ nonexhausting stages and at most $\tau$ exhausting stages. Consequently,

$$ H\le\tau(B+2). $$

This proves termination and a polynomial bound on the number of recorded pairs.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

A polynomial stage count alone does not control rational encoding lengths. The following common-denominator argument supplies the missing bound.

Suppose the current residual has a common positive integer denominator $D$:

$$ u_t=\frac{w_t}{D}, \qquad w_t\in\mathbb Z. $$

Let $v$ be any selected vertex of $P_i\cap[0,u]$. Choose $\tau$ linearly independent active constraints defining this vertex. After clearing the original-cell rows, their coefficient matrix $A$ is integer. Its rows come from the cleared cell inequalities or from coordinate bounds, whose coefficients are unit vectors.

Hence

$$ 0<|\det A|<2^B. $$

The active right-hand sides are integers or residual coordinates $w_t/D$. Cramer's rule therefore shows that all coordinates of $v$ have a common denominator dividing

$$ D|\det A|. $$

Because $N$ is an integer, the new residual $u-Nv$ has the same common-denominator bound. Thus each stage increases the logarithm of a usable common denominator by at most $B$.

Initially, the product of the input mass denominators is a common denominator of binary length at most $L$. Throughout the construction, we can therefore use common denominators with

$$ \log_2 D\le L+HB. $$

All residual and coalition coordinates lie in $[0,1]$, so their numerators have comparable encoding lengths. Since $H$ and $B$ are polynomially bounded, every constructed coordinate has polynomial encoding length.

Multiplicity lengths are also bounded:

  • At an exhausting stage, $N=1$.
  • At a nonexhausting stage, every positive selected coordinate is at least $2^{-B}$. Consequently,

$$ N\le\frac{u_t}{v_t}\le2^B $$
for every positive selected coordinate.

Thus every multiplicity uses at most $B+1$ binary digits.

The linear programs in Lemma 1 consequently have polynomial-size inputs at every stage. Vertex selection also has polynomial bit complexity. Its initial optimal-face equation has a rational coefficient $\lambda$ of polynomial encoding length by exact linear programming. Each successive coordinate minimum is attained at a vertex of the original truncated polytope, because a nonempty face of a polytope contains a vertex. The determinant bound above therefore controls each new coordinate equality.

There are polynomially many stages and polynomially many exact linear programs per stage. We have proved:

Theorem 1. A multiplicity representation of a core-stable partition can be computed in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L$. It has at most $\tau(B+2)$ recorded coalition vectors, all rational, with polynomial-size coordinate encodings and binary multiplicities.

Stability is proved next. Rational coordinates are admissible under standard real-algebraic output encoding.

6. Core stability and consequences

Termination gives $u^H=0$, so

$$ \sum_{\ell=1}^H N_\ell v^\ell=\mu. $$

Thus expansion produces a finite partition.

Suppose a nonzero coalition $r$ blocks this partition. Choose the smallest batch index $\ell$ such that

$$ c(v^\ell)>c(r). $$

Such an index exists because the blocking inequality has a nonzero left-hand side. Every eligible jurisdiction belongs to a batch with index at least $\ell$. Hence

$$ r \le \sum_{i:c(v^i)>c(r)}N_i v^i \le \sum_{i\ge\ell}N_i v^i = u^{\ell-1}. $$

But $v^\ell$ minimized cost over all nonzero coalitions in $[0,u^{\ell-1}]$. Therefore

$$ c(v^\ell)\le c(r), $$

contradicting its selection as an eligible batch. This uses exactly the stated strict-improvement blocking rule.

The construction proves, independently of finite-society existence results, that every promised instance admits a finite core-stable partition with rational coordinates. The “none exists” outcome never occurs.

It also supplies an explicit-list algorithm whose running time is polynomial in the input length and the length of its emitted list: compute the multiplicity representation first, then expand it. The number of jurisdictions satisfies

$$ K=\sum_{\ell=1}^H N_\ell \le H2^B. $$

The binary encoding of $K$ is polynomially bounded. After computing the multiplicity representation, any indexed jurisdiction can also be retrieved in polynomial time by comparing its index with the cumulative multiplicities.

As a further special case, if $G$ is globally affine, the single truncated cell is $[0,u]$. Every nonzero vertex has coordinates in $\{0,u_t\}$, so every stage exhausts a type and has multiplicity $1$. The construction then emits an explicit partition with at most $\tau$ jurisdictions in polynomial time.

7. Compressed partitions can also be verified in polynomial time

This verification claim concerns rational coalition vectors and binary multiplicities.

Given a valid multiplicity representation, let its distinct jurisdiction costs be

$$ h_1<h_2<\cdots<h_s, $$

and define

$$ R_i=\sum_{\ell:c(v^\ell)\ge h_i}N_\ell v^\ell. $$

The expanded partition is stable if and only if

$$ \min_{0\ne r\le R_i}c(r)\ge h_i \quad\text{for every }i. $$

If this inequality fails, the violating coalition has cost below $h_i$ and can be formed from jurisdictions all costing at least $h_i$, so it blocks.

Conversely, if $r$ blocks, choose the smallest jurisdiction cost $h_i$ strictly exceeding $c(r)$. Its eligible jurisdictions are exactly those counted in $R_i$. Therefore

$$ r\le R_i, \qquad \min_{0\ne q\le R_i}c(q)\le c(r)<h_i. $$

All costs, aggregate vectors, and comparisons are rational. There are at most as many thresholds as recorded pairs, and Lemma 1 computes each required minimum. Verification is polynomial in the instance length and the compressed candidate's encoding length.

8. Explicit output can still be exponentially large

The output-size obstruction must be established separately.

Fix $n\ge1$ and put $\varepsilon=2^{-n}$. There is one type of mass $1$, ideal location $0$, and project interval $[0,1]$. Transportation cost is zero. Define

$$ G(q)= \begin{cases} 2\varepsilon-q,&0\le q\le\varepsilon,\\ 2q-\varepsilon,&\varepsilon\le q\le1. \end{cases} $$

The formulas agree at $\varepsilon$, and $G(q)\ge\varepsilon>0$. This instance has two cells, one supported type, and input length $\Theta(n)$.

Its coalition costs are

$$ c(q)= \begin{cases} 2\varepsilon/q-1,&0<q\le\varepsilon,\\ 2-\varepsilon/q,&\varepsilon\le q\le1. \end{cases} $$

Consequently,

$$ c(q)\ge1, \qquad c(q)=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ q=\varepsilon. $$

Let a finite stable partition be given, and let $R$ be the total mass in jurisdictions costing more than $1$. If $R\ge\varepsilon$, a coalition of mass $\varepsilon$ blocks at cost $1$. Stability therefore implies

$$ 0\le R<\varepsilon. $$

Every remaining jurisdiction has mass $\varepsilon$. If there are $N$ such jurisdictions, mass conservation gives

$$ R=1-N\varepsilon=(2^n-N)\varepsilon. $$

The right-hand side is an integer multiple of $\varepsilon$. The displayed bounds force $R=0$ and $N=2^n$.

Conversely, $2^n$ jurisdictions of mass $\varepsilon$ form a stable partition, since no coalition has cost below $1$.

Thus every stable partition of this instance has exactly $2^n$ jurisdictions. This conclusion permits arbitrary real jurisdiction masses; it does not depend on rationality of the proposed output. Any encoding listing jurisdictions individually has exponentially many entries.

The following conclusions are proved:

  • Finite core-stable partitions always exist.
  • Explicit-list construction cannot run in polynomial time in the input length.
  • For every instance, a stable partition has a polynomial-size multiplicity representation computable in polynomial time.
  • Rational multiplicity representations can be verified in polynomial time.
