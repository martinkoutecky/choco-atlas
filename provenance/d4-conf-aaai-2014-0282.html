The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), per-capita weights \(w_t\), two indivisible blocs of weights \(a,b>0\), and quota \(Q\), take finite approximants with \(N\mu_t\) background agents of type \(t\), each of weight \(w_t/N\), along values of \(N\) for which all counts are integral. Let \(W=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t w_t\) and \(F_W(z)=\min\{1,\max\{0,z/W\}\}\). What is the sign of the limiting difference between the Shapley value of the second bloc when the first commits IN and when it commits OUT? The limit is \(\Delta=F_W(Q-a)-F_W(Q-a-b)-F_W(Q)+F_W(Q-b)\); classify it as positive, negative, or zero.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support \(T\): the list of its \(\tau\geq 1\) positive-mass types, each with a binary rational mass \(\mu_t>0\), with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type has a positive binary rational per-capita weight \(w_t\). There are \(m=2\) indivisible blocs, of positive binary rational weights \(a,b\), and a positive binary rational quota \(Q\), with no upper restriction on \(Q\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
For each positive integer \(N\) with all \(N\mu_t\) integral, form a weighted voting game with these two blocs and \(N\mu_t\) background agents of weight \(w_t/N\) for each type. A coalition has value one exactly when its total weight is at least \(Q\), and zero otherwise.
Committing the first bloc IN removes it from the player set and changes the quota to \(Q-a\); committing it OUT removes it and leaves quota \(Q\). No weights or quotas are renormalized; a nonpositive reduced quota makes every coalition winning.
Let \(\Delta\) be the limit, as admissible \(N\to\infty\), of the second bloc's Shapley value after IN minus its Shapley value after OUT. Equivalently, with \(W=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t w_t\) and \(F_W(z)=\min\{1,\max\{0,z/W\}\}\), the specified limit is \(\Delta=F_W(Q-a)-F_W(Q-a-b)-F_W(Q)+F_W(Q-b)\). The output is positive, negative, or zero according to its exact sign.

Is this continuous sign-classification problem solvable in polynomial time, or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and \(L\), with \(m=2\) fixed.

For finite weighted voting games, deciding positive or negative IN-minus-OUT Shapley difference is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and deciding zero difference is \(\mathrm{coNP}\)-hard. [Source](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/synergies.aaai14.pdf)
A value theory for voting games with finitely many major players and a continuum of insignificant minor players is established, but the verified source does not state the complexity of this exact classification problem. [Source](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.3.4.290)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Required strictly positive per-capita weights, following the discrete weighted voting game definition; this also ensures the denominator W is positive.
  • Specified binary rational encoding for weights, bloc weights, and quota, and defined L as total input bit length.
  • Chose a positive quota without an upper bound; the discrete definition imposes no upper bound and permits reduced games with no winning coalitions.
  • Made removal semantics explicit: the committed bloc disappears from the player set, IN subtracts its weight from the quota, OUT leaves the quota unchanged, and neither operation renormalizes weights.
  • Used m for the fixed number of indivisible blocs, since this problem has no candidate-count parameter.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

The continuous sign-classification problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time. For the promised case \(m=2\), an exact algorithm uses \(O(\tau+m)\) integer arithmetic operations and \(O((\tau+m)L^2)=O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations with elementary arithmetic.

Proof: reducing classification to an integer sign. By positivity of the masses and per-capita weights, \(W=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t w_t>0\). Define the clipped function \(H_W(z)=\min\{W,\max\{0,z\}\}\). Then \(F_W(z)=H_W(z)/W\), so the formula supplied in the question gives

\(\displaystyle W\Delta=H_W(Q-a)-H_W(Q-a-b)-H_W(Q)+H_W(Q-b).\)

Thus it suffices to compute the sign of this expression exactly. The following construction performs that computation using integers.

Write every input rational as an integer numerator divided by a positive integer denominator. Let \(D\) be the product of all these denominators, including those of the masses, per-capita weights, bloc weights, and quota. Each denominator divides \(D\); consequently all the following quantities are integers:

\(\displaystyle x_t=D\mu_t,\qquad y_t=Dw_t,\qquad S=\sum_{t\in T}x_ty_t=D^2W,\)

\(\displaystyle A=D^2a,\qquad B=D^2b,\qquad R=D^2Q.\)

Moreover, \(S>0\). Define the integer clipping function \(h(z)=\min\{S,\max\{0,z\}\}\). Since multiplication by a positive constant commutes with clipping,

\(\displaystyle h(D^2z)=D^2H_W(z)\)

for every real \(z\). Therefore the integer

\(\displaystyle K=h(R-A)-h(R-A-B)-h(R)+h(R-B)\)

satisfies \(K=D^2W\Delta=S\Delta\). Because \(S>0\), the signs of \(K\) and \(\Delta\) agree. The algorithm returns positive, negative, or zero according to the exact integer comparison of \(K\) with zero.

This computation handles every boundary case in the question. In particular, when \(Q-a\leq0\), both terms corresponding to the IN Shapley value vanish. When \(Q\geq a+b+W\), all four clipped terms equal \(W\), giving \(\Delta=0\). Equality at a clipping boundary requires no approximation or special numerical convention.

Proof of the running-time bound. The total binary length of all input numerators and denominators is at most \(L\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \log_2D=\sum_d\log_2d=O(L),\)

where the sum counts each input denominator once. Thus \(D\) has \(O(L)\) bits.

For an input rational \(n/d\), compute its integer multiple \(D(n/d)\) as \(n(D/d)\), using exact integer division. Constructing \(D\) and scaling all input rationals require \(O(\tau+m)\) multiplications and divisions. Computing \(S\) requires \(\tau\) multiplications and \(O(\tau)\) additions. Constructing \(A,B,R\) and evaluating \(K\) require only a constant number of further operations when \(m=2\).

Every intermediate integer has \(O(L)\) bits. Indeed, the scaled numerators and their products have \(O(L)\) bits, and summing \(\tau\) such products increases the length by at most \(O(\log\tau)\). Since the input explicitly lists its \(\tau\) positive-mass types, \(\tau=O(L)\), so this increase is also \(O(L)\).

Elementary multiplication and division on \(O(L)\)-bit integers take \(O(L^2)\) bit operations; addition and comparison take \(O(L)\). The resulting upper bound is therefore \(O((\tau+m)L^2)\), or \(O(\tau L^2)\) for \(m=2\). In particular, it is \(O(L^3)\) solely in terms of total input length. These bounds concern the promised two-bloc problem; they assert no extension to arbitrary numbers of blocs.

Relation to the cited results. The finite-game hardness statements are precisely the results of Theorem 2 in [Procaccia, Shah, and Tucker](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~nisarg/papers/synergies.aaai14.pdf). They do not establish hardness for this limit problem: under the supplied limit formula, all background-type information enters through the single rational aggregate \(W\), which the algorithm computes directly.

[Milnor and Shapley](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.3.4.290) establish the oceanic-game value-theory setting. The complexity conclusion here follows from the explicit integer algorithm above and does not require attributing a complexity theorem to that paper.

Thus all three sign decisions belong to \(\mathrm{P}\). An \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness result under polynomial-time many-one reductions would consequently imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
