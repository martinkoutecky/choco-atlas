The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a shared unknown \(S\)-state, \(A\)-action MDP with horizon \(H\), known reward functions for \(\tau\) stakeholder types, and rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, characterize the minimax cumulative regret over \(K\) episodes against the policy maximizing \(\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}(V_t^\pi)^{\mu_t}\), and determine whether an efficient learner attains the resulting rate.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
Each type specifies a known binary-rational reward table \(r_t:\mathcal S\times\mathcal A\to[0,1]\). Write \(S=|\mathcal S|\), \(A=|\mathcal A|\), and \(m=SA\); \(L\) is the total bit length of the known reward tables, masses, and initial distribution.
The environment has one shared, unknown, time-homogeneous transition kernel \(P\). Each of \(K\) episodes starts independently from the known binary-rational distribution \(\rho\) and lasts \(H\) steps. The learner observes its trajectory and has no generative-model access.
At the start of episode \(k\), using previous episodes, the learner selects a randomized nonstationary Markov policy \(\pi^k\). Comparator policies belong to the same policy class \(\Pi\).
Define \(V_t^\pi=\mathbb E_{P,\rho,\pi}[\sum_{h=1}^{H}r_t(s_h,a_h)]\), \(W_\mu(\pi)=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}(V_t^\pi)^{\mu_t}\), and \(\pi^\star\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}W_\mu(\pi)\). Zero values are allowed, with \(0^{\mu_t}=0\); any maximizing comparator may be used.
Let \(R_K=\sum_{k=1}^{K}[W_\mu(\pi^\star)-W_\mu(\pi^k)]\). For fixed \(\mu\), define \(\mathfrak R_K=\inf_{\mathcal B}\sup_{I\in\mathcal I_{\mu,L}}\mathbb E_I[R_K]\), where \(\mathcal I_{\mu,L}\) contains all environments above with the specified dimensions and known-data bit length at most \(L\); learners receive that known data.
Consider \(S\ge3\), \(A\ge2\), \(\tau\ge1\), \(H\ge\max\{2,c_1\log S\}\), and \(K\ge\max\{1,c_2\log H\}\), for sufficiently large fixed universal constants. An efficient learner has total bit running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,H,K,L\).

What is the minimax expected cumulative regret \(\mathfrak R_K\), including its dependence on \(S,A,H,K,\tau,\mu,L\), and can an efficient learner attain that rate, or is there a computational obstruction?

For \(n\) discrete stakeholders and the unnormalized objective \(\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_i^\pi\), [Mandal–Gan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.12584v2) report an expected-regret lower bound \(\Omega(n(H/2)^n\sqrt{SAK})\) under \(K\ge\Omega(\log H)\), \(H\ge\Omega(\log S)\), and an upper bound \(\widetilde O(nH^{n+1}S\sqrt{AK})\).
[Kim et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25ac.html) give approximation guarantees for policy portfolios across generalized means, including Nash welfare. No result settling the weighted-geometric-mean minimax regret problem above was identified in the search.

Partial solution: the sharp power of $K$ and an efficient upper bound

The full joint minimax rate remains unresolved. However, one can improve the upper bound in the notes enough to determine the power of $K$ for fixed dimensions and masses, and repair the polynomial bit-complexity argument.

The improvement comes from measuring error between trajectory distributions, rather than applying welfare continuity directly to absolute value errors.

1. Proven bounds and their scope

Write
$$ m=SA,\qquad w=\min_{t\in[\tau]}\mu_t, \qquad a_t=\min\{\mu_t,1/2\}, \qquad \kappa_t=\max\{1,2\mu_t\}. $$
All logarithms below are natural unless a base is indicated. Define
$$ \lambda=\left\lceil\log_2\!\bigl(8mS HK(K+1)\bigr)\right\rceil. $$

There are universal constants $c,C>0$ with the following properties.

Lower bound. Whenever the known-data budget admits the constructions in Section 3,
$$ \mathfrak R_K \ge cH\max\left\{ \min\{K,\sqrt{mK}\}, \; w\min\{K,m^wK^{1-w}\} \right\}. $$
Under the usual explicit rational-table encoding, it suffices that
$$ L\ge L_0, \qquad L_0= O\!\left( \ell_\mu+\tau m\log_2(\tau+1)+S\log_2(S+1) \right), $$
where $\ell_\mu$ is the encoded length of the specified masses. More precisely, the budget must exceed the actual encoded lengths of both constructions.

Efficient upper bound. For every nonempty instance class in the question, a learner with total bit running time polynomial in $m,\tau,H,K,L$ satisfies
$$ \mathbb E[R_K] \le \min\left\{ HK,\; 2H+ H\sum_{t=1}^{\tau} \kappa_t K^{1-a_t}D^{a_t} \right\}, $$
where
$$ D= \min\left\{ K,\; C Sm\lambda\log(2SHK)\log(1+HK) \right\}. $$

Consequently, for fixed $S,A,H,\tau,\mu$, and fixed $L$ large enough for the lower-bound constructions,
$$ \mathfrak R_K = \widetilde\Theta\!\left( K^{\max\{1/2,\,1-w\}} \right) \qquad\text{as }K\to\infty. $$
Here the implicit constants may depend on those fixed parameters, and $\widetilde\Theta$ allows powers of $\log K$.

Thus the episode exponent is determined, and an efficient learner attains it up to logarithms. The bounds do not match uniformly in dimensions and masses: in particular, the upper bound contains $Sm$ where the lower bound contains $m$.

2. Welfare continuity in trajectory distance

2.1 Existence and elementary bounds

The policy space $\Pi$ is a finite product of compact simplices. Each value is a finite sum of trajectory probabilities multiplied by rewards, and therefore is continuous in the policy probabilities. Since every mass is positive, the weighted geometric mean is continuous even when some values vanish. A maximizing comparator consequently exists.

Every value belongs to $[0,H]$. Since the masses sum to one,
$$ 0\le W_\mu(\pi)\le H, \qquad 0\le R_K\le HK. $$

2.2 A trajectory-distance lemma

For probability distributions $p,q$ on a finite set, define
$$ \mathsf h^2(p,q)= \sum_\omega \left(\sqrt{p(\omega)}-\sqrt{q(\omega)}\right)^2. $$
Thus $\mathsf h$ is the Euclidean distance between square-root probability vectors.

Suppose $f_t$ are functions taking values in $[0,1]$, and put
$$ u_t=\mathbb E_p[f_t], \qquad v_t=\mathbb E_q[f_t], \qquad e=\min\{1,\mathsf h^2(p,q)\}. $$
Then
$$ \left| \prod_tu_t^{\mu_t} - \prod_tv_t^{\mu_t} \right| \le \sum_t\kappa_t e^{a_t}. $$

To prove this, the reverse triangle inequality gives
$$ \begin{aligned} |\sqrt{u_t}-\sqrt{v_t}| &= \left| \|\sqrt{f_t}\sqrt p\|_2 - \|\sqrt{f_t}\sqrt q\|_2 \right|\\ &\le \|\sqrt{f_t}(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)\|_2 \le \mathsf h(p,q). \end{aligned} $$
The left side is also at most one, so it is at most $\sqrt e$.

For $x,y\in[0,1]$ and $0<b\le1$,
$$ |x^b-y^b|\le |x-y|^b. $$
Indeed, subadditivity of the power function follows from
$$ z^b+(1-z)^b\ge z+(1-z)=1 \qquad(0\le z\le1), $$
after normalization. For $1<b\le2$, the mean value theorem gives
$$ |x^b-y^b|\le b|x-y|. $$
Apply these inequalities with $b=2\mu_t$, $x=\sqrt{u_t}$, and $y=\sqrt{v_t}$. They imply
$$ |u_t^{\mu_t}-v_t^{\mu_t}| \le \kappa_t e^{a_t}. $$
Telescoping the products proves the claim, because every other factor is at most one.

For a trajectory $\omega$, take
$$ f_t(\omega)=\frac1H\sum_{h=1}^H r_t(s_h,a_h). $$
The lemma therefore implies that the welfare difference between two trajectory laws is at most
$$ H\sum_t\kappa_t e^{a_t}. $$

The exponent here is $\min\{\mu_t,1/2\}$. This is the key improvement over applying a $\mu_t$-Hölder bound to absolute value errors.

3. Lower bounds, proved independently

3.1 Construction and testing inequality

Let $N$ be the largest power of two not exceeding $S-2$. Then
$$ N\ge S/6. $$
Use $N$ decision states, a good absorbing state $g$, and a bad absorbing state $b$. Remaining states, if any, are unreachable. The initial distribution is uniform on the decision states and is binary-rational.

From decision state $j$, the first action immediately transitions to either $g$ or $b$. Thus each episode contains exactly one informative transition. The kernel is time-homogeneous.

Index environments by $\theta\in[A]^N$, where $\theta_j$ is the distinguished action at state $j$, and give $\theta$ the uniform product prior. For the policy selected in episode $k$, define
$$ q_k=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\pi_1^k(\theta_j\mid j). $$

Fix a state $j$ and all distinguished actions outside $j$. In a reference environment $M_{j,0}$, every action at $j$ succeeds with probability $p_0$. In $M_{j,a}$, only action $a$ at $j$ changes, to success probability $p_1$. Let
$$ d= \operatorname{KL}\!\left( \operatorname{Bern}(p_0) \,\middle\|\, \operatorname{Bern}(p_1) \right). $$
For the observation history preceding episode $k$, the relative-entropy chain rule gives
$$ \operatorname{KL}\!\left( \mathbb P_{j,0}^{k-1} \,\middle\|\, \mathbb P_{j,a}^{k-1} \right) = d\,\mathbb E_{j,0}[T_{j,a}^{k-1}], $$
where $T_{j,a}^{k-1}$ counts previous episodes starting at $j$ and taking action $a$.

The learner’s internal randomization may be included in the history. Its conditional distribution is identical in both environments, so it adds no relative-entropy term.

Pinsker’s inequality applied to the bounded statistic $\pi_1^k(a\mid j)$ yields
$$ \mathbb E_{j,a}[\pi_1^k(a\mid j)] \le \mathbb E_{j,0}[\pi_1^k(a\mid j)] + \sqrt{\frac d2\,\mathbb E_{j,0}[T_{j,a}^{k-1}]}. $$
Under the reference environment,
$$ \sum_a\mathbb E_{j,0}[T_{j,a}^{k-1}] =\frac{k-1}{N}. $$
Averaging over $a$ and using concavity of the square root gives
$$ \frac1A\sum_a \mathbb E_{j,a}[\pi_1^k(a\mid j)] \le \frac1A+\sqrt{\frac{dK}{2NA}}. $$
Averaging also over $j$ and the remaining distinguished actions identifies the left side with the prior expectation of $q_k$.

3.2 Square-root lower bound

Choose distinct dyadic constants $c_t\in[1/2,1]$ and set
$$ r_t(s,a)=c_t\mathbf 1\{s=g\}. $$
Then
$$ W_\mu(\pi)=C_\mu V^\pi, \qquad C_\mu=\prod_tc_t^{\mu_t}\ge1/2, $$
where $V^\pi$ is the value for reward $\mathbf 1\{s=g\}$.

Give nondistinguished actions success probability $1/2$, and distinguished actions success probability $1/2+\varepsilon$, where
$$ \varepsilon=\frac1{16}\min\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{NA}{K}}\right\}. $$
The relative entropy in the preceding test is
$$ d=-\frac12\log(1-4\varepsilon^2)\le4\varepsilon^2. $$
Therefore, since $A\ge2$,
$$ \mathbb E[q_k] \le \frac12+\sqrt2\,\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac K{NA}} \le\frac58. $$

The optimal policy selects every distinguished action. Its episode-$k$ welfare advantage is exactly
$$ C_\mu(H-1)\varepsilon(1-q_k). $$
Summing and using $H-1\ge H/2$ and $NA\ge m/6$ gives
$$ \mathfrak R_K\ge cH\min\{K,\sqrt{mK}\}. $$

3.3 Rare-transition lower bound

Choose a type $t_0$ of mass $w$ and set
$$ r_{t_0}(s,a)=\mathbf 1\{s=g\}, \qquad r_t(s,a)=c_t\quad(t\ne t_0), $$
where the constants for the remaining types are distinct dyadics in $[1/2,1]$.

Give nondistinguished actions success probability zero, and distinguished actions success probability
$$ p=\frac1{64}\min\left\{1,\frac{NA}{K}\right\}. $$
The testing relative entropy is now
$$ d=-\log(1-p)\le2p. $$
Hence
$$ \mathbb E[q_k] \le\frac12+\sqrt{\frac{pK}{NA}} \le\frac58. $$

For any selected policy,
$$ V_{t_0}^{\pi^k}=(H-1)pq_k, \qquad V_t^{\pi^k}=Hc_t\quad(t\ne t_0). $$
Writing
$$ C_-=\prod_{t\ne t_0}c_t^{\mu_t}\ge1/2, $$
the welfare gap is
$$ C_-H^{1-w}(H-1)^wp^w(1-q_k^w). $$
Concavity gives the tangent inequality
$$ q^w\le1-w(1-q) \qquad(0\le q\le1). $$
Consequently,
$$ \mathbb E[R_K] \ge \frac{3wK}{8}\, C_-H^{1-w}(H-1)^wp^w. $$
Using
$$ H^{1-w}(H-1)^w\ge H/2, \qquad 64^{-w}\ge1/64, \qquad N\ge S/6, $$
proves
$$ \mathfrak R_K \ge cwH\min\{K,m^wK^{1-w}\}. $$

Both constructions apply to every randomized learner. Its prior-average regret is bounded above by its worst-instance regret, which verifies the minimax quantifiers.

3.4 Known-data length

The constants $c_t$ can be chosen with $O(\log_2(\tau+1))$ bits each. The initial probabilities are $1/N$, with $N$ a power of two. Thus the construction lengths have the asserted bound $L_0$.

Neither $\varepsilon$ nor $p$ contributes to $L$: they belong to the unknown transition kernel. In particular, the known-data budget does not prohibit arbitrarily rare unknown transitions.

These lower bounds require only $H\ge2$ and $K\ge1$, so they apply under the stronger assumptions in the task.

4. A polynomial bit-time optimistic planner

This section establishes the computational guarantee explicitly through rational linear programming.

4.1 Rational confidence intervals

Only transitions at stages $1,\ldots,H-1$ are used. Let $N_k(s,a)$ count these transitions before episode $k$. For a row with count $n\ge1$, let $x_i$ be its empirical next-state probabilities and put
$$ a=\lambda/n. $$
Let $u_i$ be the smallest power of two at least $\sqrt{x_i a}$; set $u_i=0$ when $x_i=0$. Define the rational widths
$$ b_i=\min\{1,4u_i+4a\}. $$
For $n=0$, use the uniform empirical row and widths $b_i=1$.

The row confidence set consists of probability vectors $Q$ satisfying
$$ |Q_i-x_i|\le b_i \quad\text{for all }i. $$

For a Bernoulli mean $p$ and empirical mean $x$ based on $n$ independent samples, Bernstein’s inequality gives
$$ \Pr\left( |x-p|>\sqrt{2p\lambda/n}+\frac{2\lambda}{3n} \right) \le2e^{-\lambda}. $$
To express the bound through $x$, write $d=|x-p|$ and $a=\lambda/n$. On the complementary event,
$$ \begin{aligned} d &\le \sqrt{2(x+d)a}+\frac23a\\ &\le \sqrt{2xa}+\sqrt{2da}+\frac23a\\ &\le \sqrt{2xa}+\frac d2+\frac53a. \end{aligned} $$
Here $\sqrt{2da}\le d/2+a$. Thus
$$ d\le2\sqrt{2xa}+\frac{10}{3}a \le4\sqrt{xa}+4a. $$
The constructed widths cover this bound. Capping at one is harmless for probabilities.

For each state-action row, its observations can be represented as successive draws from an independent sample stream with that row’s distribution. Adaptive visits select prefixes of these streams. Union-bounding over all $mS$ coordinates and all prefix lengths up to $HK$ therefore proves an event $E$ with
$$ \Pr(E^c)\le\frac1{K+1}, $$
on which the true kernel belongs to every episode’s row confidence sets.

4.2 A useful Hellinger consequence

There is a universal constant such that any probability vector $q$ in a confidence set satisfies
$$ \sum_i(\sqrt{q_i}-\sqrt{x_i})^2 \le644S\lambda/n \qquad(n\ge1). $$
Indeed,
$$ |q_i-x_i|\le8\sqrt{x_i a}+4a. $$
If $x_i\le256a$, then $q_i\le388a$, so the squared square-root difference is at most $644a$. If $x_i>256a$, then
$$ (\sqrt{q_i}-\sqrt{x_i})^2 \le\frac{(q_i-x_i)^2}{x_i} \le128a+\frac{32a^2}{x_i} \le129a. $$

On $E$, apply the triangle inequality between the true row, the empirical row, and any confidence-compatible row. With
$$ B=4096S\lambda, \qquad \alpha_n=\min\left\{1,\frac{B}{n+1}\right\}, $$
the result is
$$ \mathsf h^2(P(\cdot\mid s,a),Q(\cdot\mid s,a)) \le2\alpha_n. $$
For $n=0$ this follows from the universal bound $\mathsf h^2\le2$; for $n\ge1$ it follows from the preceding calculation and $n+1\le2n$.

4.3 The occupancy polytope

During planning, permit a different confidence-compatible transition row at each stage. This is a relaxation of the shared-kernel planning problem.

Use nonnegative variables $q_h(s,a)$ and $z_h(s,a,s')$, with $z$ present only for $h<H$. Impose
$$ \sum_aq_1(s,a)=\rho(s), \qquad \sum_{s'}z_h(s,a,s')=q_h(s,a), $$
and
$$ \sum_aq_{h+1}(s',a)=\sum_{s,a}z_h(s,a,s'). $$
For the current empirical row and widths, impose the linear inequalities
$$ (x_{s'}-b_{s'})q_h(s,a) \le z_h(s,a,s') \le (x_{s'}+b_{s'})q_h(s,a). $$
This is a bounded rational polytope. Every stage has total occupancy one.

Define
$$ v_t(q)=\sum_{h,s,a}q_h(s,a)r_t(s,a). $$
For positive rows, recover
$$ \pi_h(a\mid s)= \frac{q_h(s,a)}{\sum_bq_h(s,b)}, \qquad Q_h(s'\mid s,a)= \frac{z_h(s,a,s')}{q_h(s,a)}. $$
At zero rows choose arbitrary action probabilities and the empirical transition row. The flow equalities verify inductively that $q$ is precisely the occupancy of the recovered policy and stage-dependent kernel.

On $E$, the true-kernel comparator occupancy is feasible.

4.4 Positive utility floors

Choose the dyadic number
$$ \eta=2^{-\lceil\log_2(8HK)\rceil}. $$
Then
$$ \frac1{16HK}<\eta\le\frac1{8HK}. $$
The reference kernel
$$ Q^{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot\mid s,a) =(1-\eta)\widehat P_k(\cdot\mid s,a) +\eta\,\mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal S) $$
is confidence-compatible: its coordinate displacement is at most $\eta$, while every width is at least
$$ \min\{1,4\lambda/n\}\ge\eta. $$

If any reward table is identically zero, welfare and regret are identically zero. Otherwise, every reward table contains a positive entry of size at least $2^{-L}$.

Use uniform actions under the reference kernel. At stage two, every state-action pair has occupancy at least $\eta/m$, regardless of $\rho$. Hence its occupancy $q^{\mathrm{ref}}$ satisfies
$$ v_t(q^{\mathrm{ref}})\ge v_0, \qquad v_0=\frac{2^{-L}\eta}{m}. $$

Set $\zeta=1/(8K)$ and restrict the planning polytope by
$$ v_t(q)\ge v_{\min}, \qquad v_{\min}=\zeta v_0. $$
If $q^\star$ maximizes welfare in the unrestricted polytope, then
$$ \bar q=(1-\zeta)q^\star+\zeta q^{\mathrm{ref}} $$
satisfies these restrictions. Monotonicity and degree-one homogeneity of the geometric mean give
$$ G_\mu(v(\bar q)) \ge(1-\zeta)G_\mu(v(q^\star)). $$
Thus the utility-floor restriction loses at most $H/(8K)$.

4.5 Replacing logarithmic optimization by an explicit LP

On the restricted polytope, maximizing welfare is equivalent to maximizing
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\log v_t(q). $$
We approximate this objective by the minimum of rational affine functions.

Put
$$ \varepsilon=\frac1{16K}, \qquad \delta=\frac1{32K}, \qquad a_i=v_{\min}(1+\varepsilon)^i, $$
and stop at the first index $J$ for which $a_J\ge H$. Then
$$ J=O\!\left(K\,[L+\log(mHK)]\right). $$
Each $a_i$ is rational of polynomial encoding length.

Compute rational numbers $\ell_i$ satisfying
$$ |\ell_i-\log a_i|\le\delta. $$
Define
$$ F(v)=\min_{0\le i\le J} \left\{\ell_i+\frac{v-a_i}{a_i}\right\}. $$
For $v\in[v_{\min},H]$,
$$ \log v-\delta \le F(v) \le\log v+\delta+\varepsilon. $$
For the lower inequality, every exact logarithmic tangent lies above $\log v$. For the upper inequality, choose $i$ with $a_i\le v\le(1+\varepsilon)a_i$. Writing $x=v/a_i$ gives tangent error
$$ x-1-\log x\le x-1\le\varepsilon. $$

Introduce variables $y_t$ and maximize $\sum_t\mu_ty_t$ subject to
$$ y_t\le \ell_i+\frac{v_t(q)-a_i}{a_i} \quad\text{for every }t,i, $$
together with the restricted occupancy constraints. The $y_t$ may additionally be bounded by a rational interval $[-M,M]$, where, for example,
$$ M=2L+2H+20\lceil\log_2(2mHK)\rceil+20. $$
This interval contains every relevant value of $F$.

The LP therefore maximizes $\sum_t\mu_tF(v_t(q))$. Because the masses sum to one, its exact optimal occupancy $\widehat q$ satisfies
$$ \sum_t\mu_t\log v_t(\widehat q) \ge \max_q\sum_t\mu_t\log v_t(q) -(2\delta+\varepsilon). $$
Since $2\delta+\varepsilon=1/(8K)$,
$$ G_\mu(v(\widehat q)) \ge e^{-1/(8K)} \max_qG_\mu(v(q)). $$
This loses at most another $H/(8K)$.

For completeness, the computational theorem used here is the exact rational linear-programming theorem: an explicitly encoded rational LP can be classified as infeasible, unbounded, or having finite optimum in polynomial bit time; in the last case an exactly feasible rational optimal solution of polynomial encoding length can be returned. Equalities and lower-dimensional feasible regions are allowed. This is the classical rational LP result; exact-solution recovery is also described explicitly in Section 1.3 of [Gleixner–Steffy](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/7507.pdf).

Its application here requires no nonlinear oracle or interior-radius estimate. The LP has polynomially many variables and inequalities, polynomial coefficient lengths, a feasible reference occupancy, and bounded variables.

The logarithms used in its coefficients are computable in polynomial time. Range-reduce a positive rational as $2^jz$, with $1\le z<2$, and use
$$ \log z= 2\sum_{d=0}^{\infty} \frac{((z-1)/(z+1))^{2d+1}}{2d+1}. $$
The ratio is at most $1/3$, so truncation to the required precision uses polynomially many bit operations.

4.6 Smoothing the optimistic kernel and implementing the policy

Recover $\pi,Q$ from $\widehat q$. Replace the optimistic rows by
$$ Q'_h=(1-\eta)Q_h+\eta\,\mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal S). $$
Thus every coordinate of $Q'_h$ is at least $\eta/S$.

Round the recovered action probabilities to dyadics so that
$$ \pi'_h(a\mid s)\ge(1-\eta)\pi_h(a\mid s). $$
This is possible with polynomially many bits: the recovered rational policy has polynomial encoding length, so its smallest positive entry is at least $2^{-p}$ for a polynomially bounded $p$. Choose a dyadic grid finer than $\eta$ times that entry, round down, and assign the residual mass to one action.

Every trajectory probability under $(\pi',Q')$ is at least $(1-\eta)^{2H-1}$ times its probability under $(\pi,Q)$. Nonnegative rewards and homogeneity imply the same multiplicative lower bound for welfare. The resulting welfare loss is at most
$$ H\bigl[1-(1-\eta)^{2H-1}\bigr] \le2H^2\eta \le\frac{H}{4K}. $$

Combining all planning losses proves
$$ W_\mu(\pi',Q') \ge \max_{q\text{ in the unrestricted polytope}} G_\mu(v(q))-\frac HK. $$
The implemented policy is $\pi'$. Sampling its actions takes polynomially many random bits per step.

In particular, no precision proportional to $1/w$ appears in the construction.

5. Accumulated trajectory error

The remaining issue is to bound trajectory distance without paying an additional polynomial factor in $H$ for counts fixed throughout an episode.

5.1 From row Hellinger distance to row relative entropy

If probability vectors $p,q$ satisfy $q_i\ge\eta/S$, then
$$ \operatorname{KL}(p\|q) \le 8\bigl(1+\log(S/\eta)\bigr)\mathsf h^2(p,q). $$
To verify this, put $M=S/\eta$ and $x=p_i/q_i\le M$. The scalar inequality
$$ x\log x-x+1 \le 8(1+\log M)(\sqrt x-1)^2 $$
holds for $0\le x\le M$.

Here is a direct check. For $x\le1/4$, the left side is at most one and the squared factor is at least $1/4$. On $[1/4,1]$, Taylor’s theorem and $f''(x)=1/x\le4$ give $f(x)\le2(x-1)^2$, whose ratio to $(\sqrt x-1)^2$ is at most eight. On $[1,4]$, the corresponding ratio is at most $9/2$. For $x\ge4$, the squared factor is at least $x/4$, while $f(x)\le x\log M$.

Multiplying the scalar inequality by $q_i$ and summing proves the claim.

On $E$, the smoothing operation and the Hellinger triangle inequality give
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathsf h^2(P,Q'_h) &\le 2\mathsf h^2(P,Q_h)+2\mathsf h^2(Q_h,Q'_h)\\ &\le4\alpha_{N_k(s,a)}+4\eta, \end{aligned} $$
because $\mathsf h^2(Q_h,Q'_h)\le\|Q_h-Q'_h\|_1\le2\eta$.

Write

5.2 A stopped-trajectory lemma

Consider two trajectory laws $\mathsf P,\mathsf Q$ having the same initial distribution and policy. Let a stopping rule depend on the observed trajectory prefixes. Define $\widetilde{\mathsf Q}$ to use the transition law of $\mathsf Q$ until stopping and the transition law of $\mathsf P$ thereafter.

Let
$$ d=\operatorname{KL}(\mathsf P\|\widetilde{\mathsf Q}), \qquad p_{\mathrm{stop}}=\Pr_{\mathsf P}(\text{stop}). $$
Then
$$ \mathsf h^2(\mathsf P,\mathsf Q) \le10d+8p_{\mathrm{stop}}. $$

Indeed, $\mathsf h^2(\mathsf P,\widetilde{\mathsf Q})\le d$. This follows by summing
$$ -\log x\ge2(1-\sqrt x) $$
with $x=\widetilde{\mathsf Q}(\omega)/\mathsf P(\omega)$.

Let $q_{\mathrm{stop}}$ be the stopping probability under $\widetilde{\mathsf Q}$. It equals the stopping probability under $\mathsf Q$, since those laws agree until stopping. The reverse triangle inequality restricted to the stopping event gives
$$ |\sqrt{q_{\mathrm{stop}}}-\sqrt{p_{\mathrm{stop}}}| \le \mathsf h(\mathsf P,\widetilde{\mathsf Q}), $$
and hence
$$ q_{\mathrm{stop}}\le2p_{\mathrm{stop}}+2d. $$
The laws $\widetilde{\mathsf Q}$ and $\mathsf Q$ coincide on trajectories without stopping, so
$$ \mathsf h^2(\widetilde{\mathsf Q},\mathsf Q) \le2q_{\mathrm{stop}}. $$
Finally,
$$ \begin{aligned} \mathsf h^2(\mathsf P,\mathsf Q) &\le 2\mathsf h^2(\mathsf P,\widetilde{\mathsf Q}) + 2\mathsf h^2(\widetilde{\mathsf Q},\mathsf Q)\\ &\le2d+4q_{\mathrm{stop}} \le10d+8p_{\mathrm{stop}}. \end{aligned} $$

5.3 Stopping when a within-episode count exceeds its starting count

For episode $k$, stop immediately before the transition associated with the $(N_k(s,a)+2)$-nd visit to any pair $(s,a)$ within that episode. Visits and transitions here range only over stages $1,\ldots,H-1$.

Let $n_k(s,a)$ be the episode’s actual transition count, and let $c_k(s,a)$ count its transitions preceding stopping. Then, pathwise,
$$ c_k(s,a)\le\min\{n_k(s,a),N_k(s,a)+1\}. $$

For each pair,
$$ \begin{aligned} \sum_k\frac{c_k(s,a)}{N_k(s,a)+1} &\le 2\sum_k \log\left( 1+\frac{n_k(s,a)}{N_k(s,a)+1} \right)\\ &= 2\log\bigl(N_{K+1}(s,a)+1\bigr)\\ &\le2\log(1+HK). \end{aligned} $$
The first inequality uses
$$ \min\{x,1\}\le2\log(1+x)\qquad(x\ge0). $$
It follows that
$$ \sum_{k,s,a}c_k(s,a)\alpha_{N_k(s,a)} \le2Bm\log(1+HK). $$

Also, if stopping occurs, some pair satisfies
$$ n_k(s,a)>N_k(s,a)+1. $$
For that pair, the updated quantity $N_k(s,a)+1$ more than doubles. Each pair can have this happen at most $\log_2(1+HK)$ times. Thus, pathwise,
$$ \sum_k\mathbf 1\{\text{episode }k\text{ stops}\} \le m\log_2(1+HK). $$
