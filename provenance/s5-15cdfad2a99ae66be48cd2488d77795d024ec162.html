Here \(\tau\) is support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,L\).

Distance ties may be ordered arbitrarily; distances within a ranking type need not be constant.

Result

Every rational society in the question admits distortion at most \(2.02613\). The generalized-metric LP is instance-optimal in the atomless model and computes an optimal lottery in polynomial time for \(m=3\).

However, the displayed decimal is not the exact sharp constant. Using the finite-election extremal-profile reduction from the cited paper, the exact supremum is a constant \(K\) satisfying

\(2.02612809290<K<2.02612809292<2.02613.\)

The proof below identifies the finite result being used, proves the transfer to measurable realizations, and evaluates the sharp constant without relying on numerical optimization.

1. Exact compression of measurable realizations

This argument works for any finite number \(m\) of candidates.

Let \(V_\sigma=\{v:r(v)=\sigma\}\), where \(\sigma\) ranges over the \(\tau\) supported ranking types. Given an admissible realization \(d\), define

\(q_{\sigma i}=\dfrac{1}{\mu_\sigma}\int_{V_\sigma}d(i,v)\,d\lambda(v),\qquad \delta_{ij}=d(i,j).\)

Every \(q_{\sigma i}\) is finite because social costs are finite and \(\mu_\sigma>0\). Integrating the triangle inequalities gives

\(q_{\sigma i}\le \delta_{ij}+q_{\sigma j},\qquad \delta_{ij}\le q_{\sigma i}+q_{\sigma j}.\)

Furthermore, \(i\succ_\sigma j\) implies \(q_{\sigma i}\le q_{\sigma j}\).

Construct a finite weighted graph with one vertex for each candidate and each supported type. Give candidate–candidate edges lengths \(\delta_{ij}\), and candidate–type edges lengths \(q_{\sigma i}\).

Its shortest-path pseudometric preserves these specified lengths. Indeed:

  • An internal segment \(i,\sigma,j\) can be replaced by the candidate edge \(i,j\) without increasing length, because \(\delta_{ij}\le q_{\sigma i}+q_{\sigma j}\).
  • After making these replacements, any path from candidate \(i\) to type \(\sigma\), ending through candidate \(j\), has length at least \(\delta_{ij}+q_{\sigma j}\ge q_{\sigma i}\).
  • Candidate–candidate edges are preserved by the same replacements and the triangle inequalities for \(\delta\).

Consequently, this weighted realization has exactly the original social-cost vector:

\(\operatorname{SC}(i)=\sum_\sigma\mu_\sigma q_{\sigma i}=\operatorname{SC}_d(i).\)

Conversely, any such finite weighted realization lifts to the prescribed population by mapping every voter of type \(\sigma\) to its type vertex. The resulting pseudometric is measurable and has finite range. Any unsupported types occurring on a null set can be assigned an arbitrary supported vertex; consistency is required only almost everywhere.

Thus allowing distances to vary within a ranking type does not enlarge the attainable set of social-cost vectors.

For rational \(\mu\), choose a common denominator \(N\) and make a finite electorate containing \(N\mu_\sigma\) voters of each type. Compression and lifting show, for every lottery \(p\),

\(D_{\mathrm{atomless}}(p,\mu)=D_{\mathrm{finite}}(p,\mu).\)

This is an exact equality, not a limiting argument. The source explicitly permits coincident voters and candidates and uses weak distance inequalities for ordinal consistency, so its finite model has the required conventions. [Charikar–Ramakrishnan, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.03694)

2. The six constraints are exact in the atomless model

Write \(t_\mu\) for the optimum of the LP in the question. We prove directly that

\(D^*(\mu)=1+t_\mu.\)

In fact, the proof applies to real as well as rational ranking masses.

Necessity

First verify that the generalized metrics are admissible. Give candidate–candidate distances zero within \(I\), and distance two between every other pair of distinct candidates.

For a voter in \(A_I\), the specified distances are zero to insiders and two to outsiders. For every other voter, distances are one to insiders and either one or three to outsiders. Hence, for each candidate pair,

\(\lvert d_I(i,v)-d_I(j,v)\rvert\le d_I(i,j)\le d_I(i,v)+d_I(j,v).\)

The graph argument from Section 1 therefore shows that shortest-path completion preserves the specified edges. Rankings are consistent: an outsider receives distance three only when every insider precedes it; among outsiders, this condition is preserved when moving downward in a ranking. The construction factors through finitely many ranking types, so it is measurable and bounded.

Its social costs are

\(\operatorname{SC}_{d_I}(i)=1-a_I\quad(i\in I),\qquad \operatorname{SC}_{d_I}(j)=1-a_I+2b_{I,j}\quad(j\notin I).\)

If \(a_I<1\), its distortion under \(p\) is exactly

\(1+\dfrac{2\sum_{j\notin I}b_{I,j}p_j}{1-a_I}.\)

The case \(a_I=1\) must also be retained. Here every \(b_{I,j}=1\). Add \(\varepsilon\) times the discrete metric to \(d_I\). Candidate–voter distances all increase by \(\varepsilon\), preserving consistency, and the resulting distortion is

\(1+\dfrac{2\sum_{j\notin I}p_j}{\varepsilon}.\)

Thus finite distortion requires \(p_j=0\) for every outsider, exactly as required by the zero-right-hand-side LP row.

These observations prove necessity of every LP constraint.

Sufficiency

Fix an admissible \(d\), and choose a minimum-cost candidate. Relabel candidates so that this candidate is \(1\) and

\(x_1=0,\qquad x_2=u,\qquad x_3=w,\qquad 0\le u\le w,\)

where \(x_i=d(1,i)\). Put \(q(v)=d(1,v)\), and define

\(y(v)=\dfrac12\max_{i\succeq_{r(v)}j}(x_i-x_j),\qquad z_i(v)=\min_{j:\,i\succeq_{r(v)}j}x_j.\)

For almost every voter, consistency and the triangle inequality imply

\(x_i\le d(i,v)+q(v)\le d(j,v)+q(v)\le x_j+2q(v)\)

whenever \(i\succeq_{r(v)}j\). Therefore \(y(v)\le q(v)\).

Likewise, for every candidate \(j\) weakly below \(i\),

\(d(i,v)\le d(j,v)\le q(v)+x_j,\)

so

\(d(i,v)-q(v)\le z_i(v).\)

The six rankings give the following values:

| Ranking | \(y\) | \(z_2\) | \(z_3\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(123\) | \(0\) | \(u\) | \(w\) |
| \(132\) | \((w-u)/2\) | \(u\) | \(u\) |
| \(213\) | \(u/2\) | \(0\) | \(w\) |
| \(231\) | \(w/2\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(312\) | \(w/2\) | \(u\) | \(0\) |
| \(321\) | \(w/2\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |

Consequently,

\(\int y=\dfrac u2(1-a_{\{1\}})+\dfrac{w-u}{2}(1-a_{\{1,2\}}),\)

and

\(\int(p_2z_2+p_3z_3) =u(b_{\{1\},2}p_2+b_{\{1\},3}p_3) +(w-u)a_{\{1,2\}}p_3.\)

Suppose \((p,t)\) satisfies the LP. Its rows for \(I=\{1\}\) and \(I=\{1,2\}\), multiplied respectively by \(u/2\) and \((w-u)/2\), yield

\(\int(p_2z_2+p_3z_3)\le t\int y\le t\operatorname{SC}_d(1).\)

Since \(z_1=0\), integration of the preceding pointwise bounds gives

\(\sum_i p_i\operatorname{SC}_d(i)-\operatorname{SC}_d(1) \le t\operatorname{SC}_d(1).\)

The denominator is positive by admissibility. Thus the distortion is at most \(1+t\).

Together with necessity, this proves the asserted LP equality and instance optimality.

3. The finite structural result used for the global bound

Let \(K_{\mathrm{fin}}\) denote the supremum of optimal distortion over finite three-candidate elections.

We use the following structural consequence of the reductions in Sections 5.2–5.3 of the cited paper:

\(K_{\mathrm{fin}}\le \max\left\{2,\, 1+\dfrac{2}{\displaystyle\inf_{\substack{0<a<1\\0\le b\le1-a}}\beta(a,b)} \right\},\)

where

\(\beta(a,b)= \dfrac{1-a-b(1-b)}a+ \dfrac{(1-b)^2+b(a+b)}{1-a}.\)

The exceptional family consists of rankings \(132,231,321\), with masses \(a,b,1-a-b\). This is the paper’s extremal-profile reduction underlying its rounded numerical bound; we use that reduction, rather than assuming the numerical matching bounds establish an exact decimal. [Charikar–Ramakrishnan, Sections 5.2–5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.03694)

Its hypotheses apply here by Section 1: each rational society has an equivalent finite electorate, with the same attainable social-cost vectors and the same optimal distortion.

4. Exact evaluation and matching rational limits

Define

\(b_0(a)=\dfrac{1+a-a^2}{2(1+a)},\qquad g(a)=\dfrac{-a^4+6a^3+a^2-2a+3}{4a(1-a)(1+a)}.\)

Completing the square gives the exact identity

\(\beta(a,b)=g(a)+\dfrac{1+a}{a(1-a)}(b-b_0(a))^2.\)

Furthermore,

\(g'(a)= \dfrac{H(a)}{4a^2(1-a)^2(1+a)^2},\qquad H(a)=a^6-2a^4+8a^3+10a^2-3.\)

On \(0<a<1\),

\(H'(a)=2a(3a^4-4a^2+12a+10)>0.\)

Indeed, the parenthesized expression is greater than \(10-4=6\). Since \(H(0)=-3\) and \(H(1)=14\), there is a unique root \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and \(g\) decreases before \(\alpha\) and increases afterward.

Exact rational evaluations give \(\alpha\in(0.47,0.48)\). The formula for \(b_0\) then gives \(b_0(\alpha)\in(0.42,0.43)\). Hence \(b_0(\alpha)>0\) and \(\alpha+b_0(\alpha)<1\): the unconstrained minimizer in \(b\) is feasible. Therefore

\(\inf_{\substack{0<a<1\\0\le b\le1-a}}\beta(a,b)=g(\alpha).\)

Also \(g(\alpha)\le g(1/2)=47/24<2\). The finite structural bound and exact compression consequently imply

\(D^*(\mu)\le K:=1+\dfrac{2}{g(\alpha)}\)

for every rational society.

Matching lower bound using rational societies

For the three-type family, write

\(M=\begin{pmatrix}0&a&a\\1-a&0&b\\1-a&1-b&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad h=\begin{pmatrix}1-a\\1-b\\a+b\end{pmatrix}.\)

The singleton generalized metrics give distortion

\(1+\dfrac{2(Mp)_i}{h_i}\)

for each \(i\). Define the row vector

\(w^\top= \left( \dfrac{1-a-b(1-b)}{a(1-a)}, \dfrac{1-b}{1-a}, \dfrac b{1-a} \right).\)

Direct multiplication verifies

\(w^\top M=\mathbf1^\top,\qquad w^\top h=\beta(a,b).\)

Near \((\alpha,b_0(\alpha))\), all entries of \(w\) are positive. In particular, \(a<0.48\) implies

\(1-a-b(1-b)>0.52-\tfrac14>0.\)

If all three singleton metrics had distortion at most \(1+t\), then

\(Mp\le \dfrac t2h.\)

Multiplying by \(w^\top\) yields

\(1=w^\top Mp\le\dfrac t2\beta(a,b),\)

and thus every lottery satisfies

\(D(p,\mu)\ge1+\dfrac2{\beta(a,b)}.\)

Choose dyadic rational sequences \(a_n\to\alpha\) and \(b_n\to b_0(\alpha)\), with \(a_n,b_n,1-a_n-b_n>0\). Their societies are valid even if “binary rational” is interpreted as requiring dyadic masses. The preceding bound gives

\(\liminf_nD^*(\mu_n)\ge 1+\dfrac2{g(\alpha)}=K.\)

Together with the upper bound, this proves

\(\displaystyle \sup_{\mu\in\Delta(S_3)\cap\mathbb Q^{S_3}}D^*(\mu)=K.\)

Certified comparison with the stated decimal

The approximation can be certified using rational arithmetic. Put

\(\ell=0.473355941112,\qquad u=0.473355941113.\)

Exact substitution gives \(H(\ell)<0<H(u)\), so \(\ell<\alpha<u\). Define

\(N_-=-u^4+6\ell^3+\ell^2-2u+3,\qquad N_+=-\ell^4+6u^3+u^2-2\ell+3,\)

\(Q_-=4\ell(1-u)(1+\ell),\qquad Q_+=4u(1-\ell)(1+u).\)

All four quantities are positive, and termwise interval bounds give

\(\dfrac{N_-}{Q_+}\le g(\alpha)\le\dfrac{N_+}{Q_-}.\)

Cross-multiplication of these rational quantities verifies

\(2.02612809290< 1+\dfrac{2Q_-}{N_+} \le K\le 1+\dfrac{2Q_+}{N_-} <2.02612809292.\)

In particular, the exact supremum is strictly below \(2.02613\).

5. Polynomial-time attainment of the sharp guarantee

Compute the six LP rows by summing the supplied masses over their supported ranking types. No electorate expansion is necessary.

For \(m=3\), this takes \(O(m^2\tau)\) rational arithmetic operations. Every resulting coefficient has bit length \(O(L)\): a common denominator can be chosen with bit length bounded by the sum of the input denominator lengths.

The LP has four variables and six distortion constraints, together with normalization and nonnegativity. Exact rational LP optimization therefore takes polynomial bit time. For example, fixed-dimensional vertex enumeration gives the conservative bound

\(O((m^2\tau+1)L^3)\qquad(m=3).\)

Section 2 proves that the returned lottery satisfies

\(D(p,\mu)=D^*(\mu)\le K<2.02613.\)

Thus the LP attains the sharp uniform guarantee, as well as the optimum for every supplied society.

If \(m\) were variable, the generalized LP would have \(2^m-2\) rows; constructing and solving it gives a bound polynomial in \(2^m,m,\tau,L\), not a polynomial bound in variable \(m\). Here \(m=3\), and \(\tau\) is exactly the supplied support size.
