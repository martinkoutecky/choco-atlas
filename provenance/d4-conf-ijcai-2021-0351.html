The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), a base rectangular estate \(L\), one integrable density \(f_t\) per type, and a fixed separation distance \(s\), consider each integer scale \(q\ge2\) for which \(q\mu_t\) is integral. Can one compute \(q\mu_t\) axis-parallel rectangles for each type \(t\), all pairwise \(s\)-separated, such that each type-\(t\) household receives value at least \(\mathrm{MMS}^{m_q,s}_{t,q}-\varepsilon\), where \(\mathrm{MMS}^{m_q,s}_{t,q}\) is that type’s \(1\)-out-of-\(m_q\) maximin share on \(L_q\)?

2. A geometric selection lemma

For this section, work directly in the scaled land $L_q$, with separation $s$.

For a nonempty rectangle $P$, its $\ell_\infty$ diameter is the maximum of its two side lengths.

Lemma 1. Suppose there are $p\le q$ recipients. Recipient $i$ has a pool of
$$ r=3q-2 $$
rectangles. For each pool there is a number $d_i>0$ and a choice of coordinate axis such that:

  • every rectangle in the pool has length $d_i$ along that axis;
  • its other side length is at most $d_i$;
  • the pool rectangles are arranged consecutively along the chosen axis, with gaps of length $s$.

One can select one rectangle from each pool so that the selected rectangles are jointly feasible.

Proof. Process recipients in nondecreasing order of $d_i$. For each recipient, select any candidate that is compatible with all previously selected rectangles, then delete incompatible candidates from the remaining pools.

We show that a selection deletes at most three candidates from any remaining pool.

Let $P$ be the selected rectangle, and consider a remaining pool with parameter $d$. By the processing order, the diameter of $P$ is at most $d$. Its projection onto this pool’s arrangement axis is therefore an interval $[u,v]$ with
$$ v-u\le d. $$
The candidates’ projections are
$$ [a+j(d+s),\,a+j(d+s)+d], \qquad j=0,\ldots,r-1. $$

If $s>0$, incompatibility implies that these projected intervals have distance less than $s$. Hence the corresponding starting coordinate lies in
$$ (u-d-s,\,v+s). $$
This interval has length at most
$$ (v-u)+d+2s\le2(d+s). $$
Four distinct candidate starts span at least $3(d+s)$, so this interval contains at most three candidate starts.

If $s=0$, incompatibility means overlapping interiors. It implies overlapping interiors of the projections, so the candidate start lies in
$$ (u-d,\,v), $$
whose length is at most $2d$. Again, at most three candidate starts can occur.

Before recipient number $j+1$ is processed, at most $3j$ candidates have been deleted from its pool. Since $j\le p-1\le q-1$, at least
$$ r-3j\ge(3q-2)-3(q-1)=1 $$
candidate remains. The procedure therefore succeeds. $\square$

This lemma is algorithmic: constructing the pools and testing pairwise compatibility requires polynomially many coordinate comparisons.

3. Constructing pools inside density cells

Let $C\subseteq L_q$ be a scaled partition cell. Denote its longer and shorter side lengths by $D$ and $E$, respectively.

Fix $r=3q-2$. Call $C$ eligible if
$$ D\ge2(r-1)s. $$

For an eligible cell, define
$$ \ell=\frac{D-(r-1)s}{r}, \qquad u=\min\{E,\ell\}. $$
Place $r$ rectangles of side lengths $\ell$ and $u$ inside $C$, arranged along its longer side with gaps $s$. All rectangles use the same transverse interval.

They fit because
$$ r\ell+(r-1)s=D. $$
Their $\ell_\infty$ diameter is $\ell$, so they satisfy Lemma 1.

The eligibility condition implies
$$ \ell\ge\frac{D}{2r}. $$
Since $E\le D$, it also implies
$$ u=\min\{E,\ell\}\ge\frac{E}{2r}. $$
Consequently, every candidate has area at least
$$ \ell u\ge\frac{DE}{4r^2}. $$

If type $t$ has constant density $c_{t,C}$ on $C$, write
$$ V_t(C)=c_{t,C}DE $$
for its value of the cell. Every candidate in this pool has value
$$ \beta_t(C)=c_{t,C}\ell u \ge\frac{V_t(C)}{4r^2}. $$

For each type, define
$$ \beta_t=\max_{C\text{ eligible}}\beta_t(C), $$
taking $\beta_t=0$ if no eligible cell exists.

The algorithm assigns empty plots to recipients with $\beta_t=0$. Every other recipient receives a copy of the pool from a maximizing cell. Lemma 1 selects jointly feasible plots, each worth exactly $\beta_t$ to its recipient.

It remains to relate $\beta_t$ to the MMS benchmark.

4. Comparing the cell pools with MMS

Lemma 2. For every type $t$ and integer $k$ satisfying
$$ k\ge4r^2m^2, $$
we have
$$ \beta_t\ge\mathrm{MMS}^{k,s}_{t,q}. $$

Proof. Consider any feasible list of $k$ plots with minimum value $\alpha>0$ to type $t$. Every plot is then nonempty.

The $m$ cells partition the land up to boundaries of measure zero. For each plot $R_j$, at least one cell contributes at least $\alpha/m$ to its value:
$$ v_{t,q}(R_j\cap C)\ge\frac{\alpha}{m}. $$
Choose one such cell for each $R_j$. By the pigeonhole principle, some cell $C$ is chosen by at least
$$ n\ge\frac{k}{m} $$
plots.

Their intersections with $C$ are positive-area rectangles and remain jointly feasible, because taking subsets preserves separation and disjoint interiors.

We first show that $C$ is eligible. Suppose instead that
$$ D<2(r-1)s. $$
This implies $s>0$. Both side lengths of $C$ are then less than $2(r-1)s$.

In each direction, use $2(r-1)$ half-open intervals of length $s$, beginning at the cell’s lower endpoint. Their Cartesian products cover $C$ and form
$$ 4(r-1)^2 $$
bins. Two rectangle centers in one bin have $\ell_\infty$ distance strictly less than $s$. Centers belong to their rectangles, so centers of different feasible rectangles must have distance at least $s$. Thus each bin contains at most one center, yielding
$$ n\le4(r-1)^2. $$
But
$$ n\ge\frac{k}{m}\ge4r^2m>4(r-1)^2, $$
a contradiction. Hence $C$ is eligible.

The selected intersections have disjoint interiors. Since each contributes at least $\alpha/m$, nonnegativity gives
$$ V_t(C)\ge n\frac{\alpha}{m} \ge\frac{k\alpha}{m^2}. $$
The pool construction therefore gives
$$ \beta_t\ge\beta_t(C) \ge\frac{V_t(C)}{4r^2} \ge\frac{k\alpha}{4r^2m^2} \ge\alpha. $$

This holds for the minimum value of every feasible list with positive minimum. Lists with minimum zero cause no difficulty because $\beta_t\ge0$. Taking the supremum proves the claim. $\square$

No attainment assumption is needed in this argument.

5. Uniform algorithm when one land side is at most $s$

Theorem 4. Suppose one side of $L_q$ has length at most $s$. Then the requested additive guarantee is computable uniformly in time polynomial in
$$ m,\tau,B,q,\log(1/\varepsilon). $$

Proof. Rotate the coordinate roles if necessary so that the height of $L_q$ is at most $s$.

For two positive-area rectangles inside $L_q$, their vertical projections cannot have distance at least $s$: their positive lengths imply that the distance between them is strictly less than the land’s height, which is at most $s$.

For axis-parallel rectangles, the $\ell_\infty$ distance is the maximum of the distances between their two coordinate projections. Hence every feasible pair must have horizontal projection distance at least $s$.

It follows that every feasible positive-value list can be ordered horizontally. Extending each rectangle to the full land height preserves separation and can only increase its value. Conversely, separated full-height strips are permitted plots. Therefore, for every $k$, the two-dimensional MMS equals the corresponding one-dimensional separated-interval MMS.

Work now on the unscaled land. Its height is at most $\sigma$. Define the rational piecewise constant marginal
$$ g_t(x)=q\int_c^d f_t(x,y)\,dy $$
and its cumulative value
$$ G_t(x)=\int_a^x g_t(u)\,du. $$
The value of a full-height strip $[x,x']\times[c,d]$ is
$$ G_t(x')-G_t(x). $$

5.1 Exact threshold feasibility

Fix a positive rational threshold $\alpha$.

Starting at $x_0=a$, find the smallest endpoint $e_1$ satisfying
$$ G_t(e_1)-G_t(x_0)=\alpha. $$
If it does not exist, declare failure. Otherwise, begin the next interval at
$$ x_1=e_1+\sigma, $$
and repeat until $q$ intervals have been obtained or the procedure fails.

This greedy procedure succeeds exactly when $q$ separated intervals of value at least $\alpha$ exist.

To prove the nontrivial direction, suppose a feasible ordered list is
$$ [\ell_1,r_1],\ldots,[\ell_q,r_q]. $$
The greedy first interval begins no later than $\ell_1$. Nonnegativity implies
$$ G_t(r_1)-G_t(a)\ge\alpha, $$
so its earliest endpoint satisfies $e_1\le r_1$. Therefore,
$$ e_1+\sigma\le r_1+\sigma\le\ell_2. $$
Inductively, if the greedy interval $j$ begins no later than $\ell_j$, its endpoint is at most $r_j$, and the next greedy interval begins no later than $\ell_{j+1}$. All $q$ greedy intervals therefore exist.

Each successful interval has positive length, because its value is positive.

5.2 Approximating the $q$-piece benchmark

Let $M_t$ denote the one-dimensional $q$-piece MMS and let
$$ V_t=G_t(b). $$
We have $0\le M_t\le V_t$.

Binary search on $[0,V_t]$, using the exact feasibility procedure, produces a rational feasible lower bound $\alpha_t$ such that
$$ M_t-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\le\alpha_t\le M_t. $$
The lower endpoint starts at zero, where empty intervals certify feasibility. Stop when the search interval has length at most $\varepsilon/2$.

The search uses
$$ O\!\left(1+\log^+\frac{V_t}{\varepsilon}\right) $$
decisions. Since $V_t$ has polynomial binary encoding length, this number is polynomial in the required input parameters.

5.3 Allocating the individual thresholds

Recipients with $\alpha_t=0$ receive empty plots. Suppose $p\le q$ recipients remain.

Maintain a right-hand remaining interval and the following invariant: if $r$ recipients remain, every one of them has a feasible list of $r$ intervals inside it, each worth at least her threshold.

Initially, every active recipient has a feasible list of $q$ intervals, and deleting intervals establishes the invariant for $r=p$.

Each recipient marks the earliest endpoint of a prefix worth her threshold. Choose the leftmost mark $e$, allocate that prefix to its marker, and, if further recipients remain, retain the interval starting at $e+\sigma$.

For another recipient, let
$$ [\ell_1,r_1],\ldots,[\ell_r,r_r] $$
be an invariant witness. Her earliest mark is at most $r_1$, and the chosen mark is no later. Consequently,
$$ e+\sigma\le r_1+\sigma\le\ell_2. $$
Her last $r-1$ witness intervals remain available. This proves the invariant after the allocation.

The procedure produces separated full-height strips, each worth $\alpha_t$ to its recipient. Since the narrow-land geometry gives
$$ M_t=\mathrm{MMS}^{q,s}_{t,q}, $$
Section 1 yields
$$ \alpha_t \ge\mathrm{MMS}^{q,s}_{t,q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2} \ge\mathrm{MMS}^{m_q,s}_{t,q}-\varepsilon. $$

The polynomial bit-complexity justification follows in Section 6. $\square$

6. Explicit bit-length bounds for successive cuts

This section justifies the implementation in Theorem 4 and repairs the bit-growth issue identified in the notes.

All vertical partition endpoints are rational. Between consecutive endpoints, $g_t$ is constant and $G_t$ is affine:
$$ G_t(x)=a_jx+b_j. $$
There are $O(m)$ such pieces. Their rational coefficients are obtained from sums and products of input rationals, so all coefficients have bit length bounded by a polynomial in $B+\log q$.

Suppose a greedy interval begins at $x$, lying in a piece with coefficients $a_i,b_i$. If its target endpoint $e$ lies in a positive-slope piece with coefficients $a_j,b_j$, then
$$ a_je+b_j=a_ix+b_i+\alpha, $$
and hence
$$ e=\frac{a_i}{a_j}x+ \frac{b_i-b_j+\alpha}{a_j}. $$
The next starting coordinate is
$$ e+\sigma. $$

Thus each cut coordinate is obtained from one preceding coordinate by an affine operation with rational coefficients, followed by addition of the fixed quadratic number $\sigma$. The formula never multiplies two evolving coordinates.

For an explicit bound, let $P$ bound the numerator and denominator lengths of all rational coefficients appearing in these affine operations, including the current threshold $\alpha$. Represent an evolving coordinate as
$$ x=\frac{U+V\sqrt q}{D}, $$
with integers $U,V,D$ and $D>0$.

Multiplication by a rational of bit length at most $P$ increases these integer lengths by $O(P)$. Addition of a rational or a rational multiple of $\sqrt q$ of bit length at most $P$ does the same. A bounded number of these operations therefore increases the representation length by $O(P)$ per cut.

After at most $q$ cuts, the length is
$$ O(L_0+qP), $$
where $L_0$ is the initial coordinate length. Both $L_0$ and $P$ are polynomial in
$$ B,q,\log(1/\varepsilon). $$

Finding the relevant affine piece requires only scanning the breakpoints and performing exact comparisons in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt q)$. Binary search thresholds have polynomial bit length, and each feasibility test starts afresh. Hence the complete threshold searches and allocation procedure have uniform polynomial bit complexity.

Zero separation

For completeness, the same affine bound proves the implementation claim for $s=0$.

Let $V_i$ be recipient $i$’s value for the entire scaled land, and set
$$ \gamma_i=\frac{V_i}{q}. $$
Give recipients with $V_i=0$ empty plots. With $r$ active recipients, maintain
$$ v_i(\text{remaining full-height strip})\ge r\gamma_i. $$
Initially this holds because $r\le q$.

Each active recipient marks a prefix worth exactly $\gamma_i$. Allocate the leftmost marked prefix. Every other recipient loses at most her own $\gamma_i$, so the invariant continues with $r-1$ recipients.

These positive-value prefixes have positive area and disjoint interiors. The total-value bound gives
$$ \mathrm{MMS}^{m_q,0}_{t,q}\le\frac{V_t}{m_q} \le\frac{V_t}{q}. $$
Thus the algorithm satisfies the guarantee exactly. Its successive cuts have the affine form analyzed above, establishing polynomial bit complexity.
