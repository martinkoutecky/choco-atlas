The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finitely many buyer types with rational masses, budgets, and valuation vectors, item supplies, and linear utilities, compute an \(\epsilon\)-Fisher equilibrium of the mass-weighted Eisenberg–Gale program using randomized item-block updates across types, with complexity measured in the number of types, items, input bits, and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(a_t\), with \(\sum_t a_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\ge1\) divisible items. Per-person budgets \(B_t>0\), supplies \(s_j>0\), and valuations \(v_{tj}\ge0\) are binary rationals; every type values some item, and every item is valued by some type. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation \(x_t\in\mathbb R_+^m\) is a per-person bundle, with utility \(u_t=\langle v_t,x_t\rangle\) and feasibility \(\sum_t a_tx_{tj}\le s_j\). The mass-weighted Eisenberg–Gale program maximizes \(\sum_t a_tB_t\log u_t\) over feasible allocations. At exact equilibrium, each bundle maximizes utility under \(\langle p,x_t\rangle\le B_t\), and positive-price items clear.
Put \(W=\sum_t a_tB_t\). For rational \(0<\epsilon<1\), an \(\epsilon\)-Fisher equilibrium means a feasible allocation with positive utilities and dual certificates \(p\ge0\), \(\beta_t>0\), satisfying \(p_j\ge\beta_tv_{tj}\) and normalized primal–dual gap \(\sum_j s_jp_j-W+\sum_t a_tB_t\log\!\bigl(B_t/(\beta_tu_t)\bigr)\le\epsilon W\).
The required dynamics start at \(x^0_{tj}=s_jB_t/W\). Set \(c_t=\langle v_t,x_t^0\rangle\), and obtain \(\widetilde f\) from \(-\sum_t a_tB_t\log u_t\) by replacing each logarithmic term below \(c_t\) with its second-order Taylor continuation at \(c_t\).
Each iteration samples an item \(j\) independently and uniformly, and updates only its allocation column by \(x_{\cdot j}^{+}=\operatorname{Proj}_{K_j}(x_{\cdot j}-\eta_j\nabla_{\cdot j}\widetilde f(x))\), where \(K_j=\{z\ge0:\sum_t a_tz_t\le s_j\}\) and the projection is Euclidean. Positive stepsizes may be chosen adaptively; feasible finite-precision approximations are allowed, with all precision and auxiliary computation charged to runtime.

Does this mass-market problem admit randomized projected item-block computation in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\), with success probability at least \(2/3\), or is computing the specified approximate equilibrium NP-hard?

Known results: [Nan, Gao, and Kroer](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00506) establish geometric convergence in expected objective gap for discrete projected item-block dynamics, with a rate depending on smoothness and a Hoffman constant, without a polynomial input-bit bound. [Orlin](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/16dagstuhl-seminar-proceedings/dsp-vol10171/DagSemProc.10171.2/DagSemProc.10171.2.pdf) gives a strongly polynomial algorithm for ordinary linear Fisher equilibrium computation; it does not establish the requested bound for these randomized projected item-block dynamics.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized masses to a probability distribution; budgets and bundles are per person, so both resource constraints and the objective are mass-weighted.
  • Required positive rational supplies and budgets, nonnegative rational valuations, at least one positively valued item per type, and at least one interested type per item, excluding degeneracies incompatible with logarithmic optimization or meaningful item updates.
  • Defined the otherwise unspecified approximation by a primal–dual gap divided by total budget, consistent with the source's use of duality gap as an accuracy measure.
  • Interpreted randomized item-block updates as the anchor's Euclidean projected-gradient updates, with uniform independent item sampling and the second-order logarithmic continuation.
  • Fixed proportional initialization, allowed adaptive positive stepsizes and feasible finite-precision implementations, and counted all computation and precision costs.
  • Specified a constant success probability for randomized computation.

2. Properties of the continued objective

The specified initialization satisfies

Then
$$ F(x)=\widetilde f(x)=\sum_t h_t(u_t). $$
Each $h_t$ is differentiable and strictly convex on $[0,\infty)$: its second derivative is $w_t/c_t^2$ below $c_t$ and $w_t/u^2$ above $c_t$, with matching derivatives at the junction.

The equilibrium target minimizes the continuation

Summing the equilibrium dual inequalities against supplies gives
$$ \beta_t^\star V_t\le W. $$
Hence
$$ u_t^\star=\frac{B_t}{\beta_t^\star}\ge c_t. $$

For every feasible $x$,
$$ \sum_t a_t\beta_t^\star u_t \le \sum_j p_j^\star\sum_t a_tx_{tj} \le W. $$
Since $h_t'(u_t^\star)=-a_t\beta_t^\star$, it follows that
$$ \sum_t h_t'(u_t^\star)(u_t-u_t^\star) = W-\sum_t a_t\beta_t^\star u_t \ge0. $$
Convexity therefore implies $F(x)\ge F(x^\star)$.

Moreover, every minimizer has utility vector $u^\star$. Otherwise, strict convexity in utilities would make its midpoint with $x^\star$ have smaller objective. Thus
$$ u\ne u^\star \quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(x)>F(x^\star). $$

Rational marginal utilities

Define
$$ \alpha_t(u)=-\frac{h_t'(u)}{a_t} = \begin{cases} B_t/u,&u\ge c_t,\\ B_t(2c_t-u)/c_t^2,&0\le u<c_t. \end{cases} $$
This function is positive and strictly decreasing, and
$$ \alpha_t(u_t^\star)=\beta_t^\star. $$
The column gradient is
$$ g_{tj}:=\nabla_{tj}F(x) = -a_t\alpha_t(u_t)v_{tj}. $$
For rational allocations, all these derivatives are rational; evaluating them requires no logarithms.

Finally, if $u\ne u^\star$, the convexity inequality gives
$$ \sum_j \left\langle g_{\cdot j}, x_{\cdot j}^\star-x_{\cdot j}\right\rangle \le F(x^\star)-F(x)<0. $$
Thus at least one column points strictly downhill toward its target.

3. Positive steps that implement waiting

Both algorithms below permanently settle columns. Their allocations always consist of columns drawn from the two rational allocations $x^0$ and $x^\star$.

Here is a finite-precision implementation that preserves a settled column when it is sampled.

Let $D$ be the product of the denominators of all coordinates of $x^0$ and $x^\star$. Given an integer precision parameter $P\ge1$, set
$$ Q=2^PD. $$
Every coordinate of either allocation belongs to the grid $Q^{-1}\mathbb Z$.

For a column $z$ that should remain unchanged, compute its gradient $g$ and choose the positive rational step
$$ \eta=\frac{1}{4Q(1+\|g\|_1)}. $$
If
$$ r=\operatorname{Proj}_{K_j}(z-\eta g), $$
the Euclidean projection inequality, using $z\in K_j$, yields
$$ \|r-z\|_2 \le\eta\|g\|_2 < \frac{1}{4Q}. $$
For example, this follows directly from the projection variational inequality:
$$ \|r-z\|_2^2 \le \langle-\eta g,r-z\rangle. $$

Coordinatewise nearest-grid rounding of $r$ therefore returns exactly $z$. The returned allocation is feasible, and its error relative to the exact projected update is less than
$$ \frac{1}{4Q}\le2^{-P-2}. $$
The bound certifies the rounded answer without explicitly computing $r$.

This implements an actual positive stepsize followed by a feasible finite-precision approximation. Taking
$$ P=1+\left\lceil\log_2(1/\epsilon)\right\rceil $$
gives polynomial precision cost. The resulting allocation is exactly unchanged, so these rounding errors do not accumulate in the stored state.

Sampling bound

Suppose a phase designates one unsettled item and succeeds when that item is sampled. Waiting updates leave the state unchanged, so the designated successful update remains valid throughout the phase.

Allow
$$ T=m\left\lceil\log_2(3m)\right\rceil $$
independent uniform samples per phase. Conditionally on any preceding history, the probability of missing its designated item is at most
$$ (1-1/m)^T \le e^{-T/m} \le\frac{1}{3m}. $$

$$ \sum_t a_t\beta_t^\star u_t \le W = \sum_t a_t\beta_t^\star u_t^\star $$
unless every utility were equal.

$$ R_t>1, \qquad u_t<u_t^\star. $$

$$ \alpha_t(u_t)v_{tj} = R_t\beta_t^\star v_{tj} > R_t\beta_r^\star v_{rj} \ge R_r\beta_r^\star v_{rj} = \alpha_r(u_r)v_{rj}. $$
Thus $t$ is the unique current winner as well.

$$ \gamma_r=\alpha_r(u_r)v_{rj}. $$

All denominators are positive. Put
$$ y_r=z_r+\eta a_r\gamma_r, \qquad \lambda=\frac{z_t+\eta a_t\gamma_t-s_j/a_t}{a_t}. $$
The first bound in the maximum ensures $\lambda\ge0$. Also,
$$ y_t-\lambda a_t=s_j/a_t, $$
while, for $r\ne t$,
$$ y_r-\lambda a_r = a_r\left( \frac{z_r}{a_r}-\frac{z_t}{a_t} +\frac{s_j}{a_t^2} -\eta(\gamma_t-\gamma_r) \right) \le0. $$
Therefore
$$ z_r^+=\max\{0,y_r-\lambda a_r\} $$
is exactly the target owner endpoint. It clears supply and satisfies the projection KKT conditions, proving that it is the Euclidean projection.

Designate this unsettled item, use rounded waiting updates until it is sampled, and install its target endpoint. Each successful phase settles one new column. Hence at most $m$ phases are needed, and Section 3 gives the required success probability.

All stored columns remain initial or target. The strict differences $\gamma_t-\gamma_r$ are nonzero rationals with polynomial encoding length; their reciprocals likewise have polynomial encoding length. Thus even extremely small winner separations do not invalidate the polynomial arithmetic bound.

$$ x_1^\star=(2,0), \qquad x_2^\star=(1,1), \qquad x_3^\star=(0,2). $$

$$ p^\star=(1/2,1/2), \qquad \beta^\star=(1/6,1/2,1/2). $$
