The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given alternatives \(C\), a societal axis \(L\), a target \(p\), a bound \(k\), and finite-support rational distributions \(\rho\) and \(\nu\) over single-peaked rankings, where \(\rho\) is registered mass and \(\nu\) is eligible mass, choose \(S\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\nu)\) with \(|S|\le k\). Activating \(S\) adds once the \(\nu\)-mass on rankings at Kendall swap distance at most one from some ranking in \(S\). Does there exist such an \(S\) making \(p\) a Plurality co-winner or a Condorcet winner?

A society is given by its support: the list of types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational. Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Classification

Both problems are \(\mathsf{NP}\)-complete and \(\mathsf{W}[1]\)-hard parameterized by \(k\). Thus neither is polynomial-time solvable unless \(\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{NP}\), and neither is \(\mathsf{FPT}\) in \(k\) unless \(\mathsf{FPT}=\mathsf{W}[1]\).

The central point is that aggregating identical discrete voters into masses preserves both attainable electorates and the selection budget.

For \(m=1\), both questions are trivially affirmative. The hardness classifications concern unbounded \(m\).

1. Exact aggregation of discrete voters

Consider a discrete instance with registered voter set \(V\), eligible voter set \(W\), ranking \(t(x)\) for each voter \(x\), and full distance-one bundles

\(\displaystyle \kappa(w)=\{u\in W:d_K(t(u),t(w))\leq 1\}.\)

Registered and eligible voters are distinct individuals, although their rankings may coincide. Define integer mass measures by

\(\displaystyle \rho(r)=|\{v\in V:t(v)=r\}|,\qquad \nu(r)=|\{w\in W:t(w)=r\}|.\)

Retain the candidates, common societal axis, target, and budget.

Lemma. For either voting rule, the resulting mass instance is affirmative if and only if the discrete instance is affirmative.

Proof. Let \(T\subseteq W\) be a discrete selection with \(|T|\leq k\), and put \(S=\{t(w):w\in T\}\). Then \(|S|\leq |T|\leq k\).

For any ranking \(r\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)\), an eligible voter \(u\) with \(t(u)=r\) belongs to \(\kappa(T)\) exactly when

\(\displaystyle \exists w\in T:\ d_K(r,t(w))\leq 1.\)

This condition depends only on \(r\), not on the identity of \(u\). Consequently, either all eligible voters of type \(r\) are added or none are. Their added number is exactly

\(\displaystyle \nu(r)\mathbf{1}_{r\in B(S)}.\)

Thus the discrete resulting electorate has precisely the mass measure \(\mu_S\). Overlapping bundles count each eligible voter once, just as the mass formulation counts each eligible type’s mass once.

Conversely, given a mass selection \(S\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\nu)\), choose one eligible representative \(w_s\) with ranking \(s\) for every \(s\in S\). The representatives are distinct, so \(T=\{w_s:s\in S\}\) satisfies \(|T|=|S|\). The same argument shows that the discrete resulting electorate has mass measure \(\mu_S\).

Pointwise equality of these measures implies equality of every Plurality score and every pairwise tally. It therefore preserves both Plurality co-winner status and strict Condorcet winner status. This proves both directions.

The transformation is polynomial-time. If the discrete input explicitly lists \(N\) voters, there are at most \(N\) resulting types, and each multiplicity is an integer at most \(N\), requiring \(O(\log(N+1))\) bits. Identical rankings can be grouped in polynomial time. The budget is unchanged.

2. Classical and parameterized hardness

The discrete theorem needed here is the following: for both Plurality and Condorcet, combinatorial constructive control by adding voters is \(\mathsf{W}[1]\)-hard parameterized by the number of selected voters, even when all rankings share a single-peaked axis and the bundling function is full distance-one. Its construction explicitly supplies a common axis. It uses Plurality co-winners and strict Condorcet winners, matching this task. [Bulteau et al., Theorem 8 in the preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.6859).

The construction is also a polynomial reduction from Partial Vertex Cover. Requiring coverage of every edge specializes its source problem to Vertex Cover, establishing classical \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hardness under the same election restrictions. [Same theorem and construction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.6859).

Apply the aggregation lemma to these discrete instances. Every resulting mass is an allowed nonnegative binary rational—in fact, an integer—and all support rankings remain single-peaked on the supplied axis. The transformation preserves the answer and preserves \(k\) exactly.

It follows that:

  • The mass-based Plurality decision problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard and \(\mathsf{W}[1]\)-hard parameterized by \(k\).
  • The mass-based Condorcet decision problem is \(\mathsf{NP}\)-hard and \(\mathsf{W}[1]\)-hard parameterized by \(k\).

In particular, a mass-instance algorithm with running time \(f(k)(m+\tau+L)^c\), for a constant \(c\) independent of \(k\), would compose with aggregation to give an \(\mathsf{FPT}\) algorithm for the discrete hard problem. Hence such an algorithm would imply \(\mathsf{FPT}=\mathsf{W}[1]\).

3. Membership in \(\mathsf{NP}\) and exact arithmetic

Write \(n=|\operatorname{supp}(\nu)|\) and \(q=\min(k,n)\). A certificate lists at most \(q\) eligible type indices. Its length is polynomial in \(L\), even when \(k\) is encoded in binary.

We can verify the certificate using exact integer arithmetic. Write every input mass as \(a_i/b_i\), with \(b_i>0\), and let

\(\displaystyle D=\prod_i b_i,\)

using \(D=1\) when there are no masses. The binary length of \(D\) is at most the sum of the denominator lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Multiplying both measures by \(D\) produces integer masses of \(O(L)\) bits. Their totals also have \(O(L)\) bits.

Because \(D>0\), this common scaling preserves every non-strict Plurality comparison and every strict pairwise comparison.

For the proposed selection, determine whether each eligible type lies within Kendall distance one of a selected type. Form the activated union once, then compute either:

  • the score of each candidate, accepting exactly when \(p\)’s score is at least every other score; or
  • for every \(c\ne p\), the margin

\(\displaystyle M_S(p,c)=\sum_r\mu_S(r)\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{p\succ_r c}-\mathbf{1}_{c\succ_r p}\bigr),\)

accepting exactly when all these margins are strictly positive.

Kendall distances, support activation, integer additions, and comparisons are all polynomial-time operations. Therefore both problems belong to \(\mathsf{NP}\). Together with Section 2, this proves \(\mathsf{NP}\)-completeness.

4. A support-based running-time bound

For completeness, exhaustive search gives an exact algorithm whose running time depends on the actual input support.

Precompute the eligible distance-one adjacency relation. Comparing relative orders of every candidate pair gives a simple \(O(\tau^2m^2)\) bound on the number of elementary comparisons. Enumerate all eligible selections of size at most \(q\); their number is

\(\displaystyle H(n,q)=\sum_{j=0}^{q}\binom{n}{j}.\)

For each selection, compute its activated union and verify the winner as above. With schoolbook exact arithmetic, a conservative bit-operation bound valid for either rule is

\(\displaystyle O\!\left(\left[(\tau+1)^2m^2+H(n,q)(q+1)(\tau+1)m\right]L^2\right).\)

This bound includes common-denominator scaling and handles empty supports. Since \(H(n,q)\leq(n+1)^q\), it gives membership in \(\mathsf{XP}\) parameterized by \(k\). It does not give \(\mathsf{FPT}\), because the exponent of the support size depends on \(k\).

Conclusion

The rational-mass formulation retains the discrete problem as a polynomially representable special case. Selecting types instead of individual voters provides no parameterized escape: identical voters already have identical full distance-one bundles, so selecting multiple representatives of one type is redundant.
