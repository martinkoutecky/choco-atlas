Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\ge1\) agreement outcomes and a separate disagreement outcome. Conditional on initiator type \(t\), an explicitly listed rational kernel \(q_\mu(\cdot\mid t)\) draws an ordered tuple of \(g-1\) responder types once per negotiation, where \(g\ge2\); the tuple remains fixed and hidden from the initiator.
Each type specifies feasibility flags, rational values for feasible outcomes, a nonnegative rational initial point budget and bargaining cost \(\beta_t\), audience-dependent nonnegative rational privacy sensitivities \(\theta_{tu}\), positive rational privacy weights \(w_{t,k}\), and ideal beliefs \(D_{tu,k}\in[0,1]\). Disagreement has value \(-K\), with rational \(K>0\); infeasible agreements cannot be selected.
DSM parameters are integers \(1\le\ell_{\min}\le\ell_{\max}\le m\), \(D\ge2\), and a supplied finite nonnegative rational reward table \(r(s,A,F,\ell)\), with \(r(D-1,A,F,\ell)=0\). Each type also supplies a deterministic scoring map on offer sets: scores lie in \(\{0,\ldots,D-1\}\), are zero exactly on infeasible offers, preserve value order, maximize the number of distinguished value levels subject to the budget, and give the highest feasible offer score \(D-1\). Maps are encoded by Boolean circuits returning binary scores; their encoding sizes belong to the input length \(L\).
For a responder's score vector, let \(H_s\) count occurrences of score \(s\), \(A=\sum_{s=1}^{D-1}H_s\), \(F=\sum_{s=1}^{D-1}(A+1)^{s-1}H_s\), and \(c=\sum_{s=1}^{D-1}(D-s-1)H_s\). Scoring temporarily costs \(c\) points; these points are refunded after an unsuccessful round. On agreement, the initiator collects the scoring payments and pays each responder \(r(s,A,F,\ell)\) for its score on the selected outcome.
At each round the initiator offers a nonempty set of previously undiscussed outcomes feasible for itself, of size at most \(\ell_{\max}\). If an affordable offer set of size at least \(\ell_{\min}\) exists, it must use such a set. An offer set is affordable only if, for every score profile possible under the current posterior, the net payment required for any jointly feasible offer does not exceed the initiator's budget. If none is affordable, negotiation ends in disagreement.
On observing the scores, the initiator must select a jointly feasible offer if one exists. It maximizes its outcome value plus \(H_t(b')\), where \(b'\) is its resulting budget and \(H_t\) is a supplied nondecreasing rational piecewise-linear continuation-value function on nonnegative budgets. Ties use a fixed outcome order. Otherwise it discards the offers permanently and continues only if their number was at least \(\ell_{\min}\) and outcomes remain; all other cases end in disagreement.
Privacy beliefs start from the common type prior \(\mu\) and are updated by Bayesian conditioning on the truthful feasibility bits received directly from the source agent; offer selection itself supplies only the feasibility of offered outcomes. For source type \(t\) and recipient type \(u\), privacy leakage is \(\mathcal L_{tu}=\sum_k w_{t,k}(|B^{\mathrm{final}}_{u,t,k}-D_{tu,k}|-|B^{\mathrm{initial}}_{u,t,k}-D_{tu,k}|)\), where \(B\) denotes posterior feasibility probabilities. Only initiator–responder message channels incur privacy costs.
The initiator optimizes its expected forward payoff \(U_t+H_t(b')\), where \(U_t=v_t(o)-\beta_t Z-\sum_{j=1}^{g-1}\theta_{t,u_j}\mathcal L_{t,u_j}\), \(Z\) counts executed rounds, and \(v_t(o)=-K\) on disagreement. Its finite MDP state contains its observed history and posterior over the fixed responder tuple. The requested aggregate is \(\sum_t\mu_t\mathbb E[U_t]\), with every type evaluated in the initiator role under its optimal policy; remaining policy ties use fixed lexicographic order.
Is computing these optimal DSM offering policies and the exact aggregate utility polynomial-time solvable, or NP-hard? State running times in \(m,\tau,g,D,\ell_{\min},\ell_{\max}\), and the total input bit length \(L\).
For finite-agent DSM, Q-learning converges almost surely under bounded rewards, infinite state–action exploration, and learning rates whose sums diverge while their squared sums converge; this is an asymptotic convergence guarantee, not a polynomial-time bound. [DSM convergence result](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42979-021-00650-4)
The underlying convergence theorem also covers undiscounted absorbing finite MDPs. No result settling the support-encoded DSM problem above was identified in the search. [Watkins–Dayan](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~dayan/papers/cjch.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized masses to sum to one and interpreted group size as including the initiator; the explicit kernel draws responder slots once, permits repeated types, and keeps their identities hidden and fixed.
  • Separated infeasibility flags from the finite disagreement penalty: infeasible agreements remain prohibited, and disagreement has value minus the positive input penalty.
  • Retained the lower-offer rule, budget feasibility, mandatory selection of a jointly feasible current offer, refunds, and prohibition on returning to rejected offers.
  • Resolved nonunique faithful scoring by supplying deterministic scoring circuits as part of complete types, with input size counted in L; supplied the reward table rather than requiring its construction.
  • Specified the otherwise unencoded future-utility function as a nondecreasing rational piecewise-linear input and retained it in assessment and policy optimization.
  • Specified privacy priors and message semantics: Bayesian updating uses directly received feasibility disclosures, with privacy charged on initiator–responder channels.
  • Used observed histories and posteriors as MDP states because a fixed responder tuple drawn from a type distribution need not satisfy outcome independence; consequently, arbitrary new feasible offer sets are allowed rather than assuming that offering the highest-valued remaining outcomes is always optimal.
  • Interpreted each type's aggregate contribution as its instantaneous utility when initiating a negotiation, while its offering policy optimizes forward utility; fixed lexicographic tie-breaking makes the aggregate unambiguous.

Result

Computing optimal DSM offering policies and computing the exact aggregate utility are both \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. Hardness already holds when \(\tau\) counts only explicitly listed positive-mass types, and when

\[ g=2,\qquad D=2,\qquad \ell_{\min}=\ell_{\max}=m/2, \]

with zero privacy sensitivities, zero scoring payments, zero rewards, zero point budgets, and identically zero continuation-value functions.

The reduction below proves hardness even for computing an optimal first offer, so the conclusion does not depend on requiring an exponentially large policy table.

1. Reduction from satisfiability

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness of satisfiability for conjunctive formulas whose nonempty clauses contain at most three literals. This is one of the problems established in [Karp’s original paper](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~madhusudan/courses/Fall2020/Lectures/L19-after.pdf).

Let the formula have variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), with \(n\ge1\), and clauses \(C_1,\ldots,C_h\). Define

\[ E=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n,c_1,\ldots,c_h\},\qquad N=n+h. \]

There are \(m=2n\) agreement outcomes, denoted \(b_i^+\) and \(b_i^-\). Associate the following subsets of \(E\) with these outcomes:

\[ S_i^+=\{a_i\}\cup\{c_j:x_i\text{ occurs in }C_j\}, \]

\[ S_i^-=\{a_i\}\cup\{c_j:\neg x_i\text{ occurs in }C_j\}. \]

Every element of \(E\) belongs to at least one of these sets: variable markers belong to both sets for their variable, and clauses are nonempty.

Covering equivalence. There are exactly \(n\) outcomes whose associated sets cover \(E\) if and only if the formula is satisfiable.

To prove the forward direction, each marker \(a_i\) requires selecting at least one of \(b_i^+,b_i^-\). Since only \(n\) outcomes are selected, exactly one is selected for each variable. Interpret these selections as a truth assignment. Covering every \(c_j\) means that some selected literal occurs in \(C_j\), so every clause is satisfied.

Conversely, a satisfying assignment selects one outcome per variable and covers every variable marker and clause marker.

2. The DSM instance

Create one distinguished initiator type \(t^\star\), one type \(u_e\) for every \(e\in E\), and one dummy type \(z\). Thus the support size is

\[ \tau=N+2. \]

Society and responder kernels

Enumerate \(E\) as \(e_1,\ldots,e_N\). Give the types positive dyadic masses

\[ \mu_{t^\star}=\frac12,\qquad \mu_{u_{e_i}}=2^{-(i+1)},\qquad \mu_z=2^{-(N+1)}. \]

These sum to one. Their explicit binary encodings have polynomial total length.

Set \(g=2\). The supplied kernels are

\[ q_\mu(u_e\mid t^\star)=\frac1N\quad(e\in E), \]

and

\[ q_\mu(z\mid t)=1\quad(t\ne t^\star). \]

These are explicitly listed rational kernels of polynomial size.

Feasibility and utilities

The distinguished type finds every agreement feasible. Type \(u_e\) finds outcome \(b_i^\sigma\) feasible exactly when \(e\in S_i^\sigma\). The dummy type finds no agreement feasible.

Every feasible agreement has value one for every type. Set

\[ K=1,\qquad \beta_{t^\star}=1,\qquad \beta_t=0\quad(t\ne t^\star). \]

All initial point budgets are zero. All continuation functions are \(H_t\equiv0\), which are rational, nondecreasing, and piecewise linear.

Set every privacy sensitivity to zero. Privacy weights can all be one and ideal beliefs can all be zero; consequently privacy updates have no effect on utility.

Scoring and rewards

Set

\[ D=2,\qquad \ell_{\min}=\ell_{\max}=n. \]

For every type and offer set, give a feasible offer score one and an infeasible offer score zero.

These maps satisfy all the scoring requirements:

  • Zero scores occur exactly on infeasible offers.
  • All feasible values are equal, so their value order is preserved.
  • The unique feasible value level receives the highest score \(D-1=1\).
  • Its scoring cost is \(D-1-1=0\), so the zero budget suffices.
  • There is only one feasible value level to distinguish.

The circuits merely return the corresponding feasibility flags and have polynomial size.

Set every reward-table entry to zero. For \(D=2\), every realizable positive-score histogram has \(F=A\), and there are only polynomially many realizable table arguments in this construction. An explicit zero table therefore has polynomial size.

Thus scoring costs and net payments are always zero. Every otherwise admissible offer set is affordable.

The complete construction, including the audience-dependent privacy data, has bit length polynomial in the formula length.

3. Exact behavior and aggregate utility

At the root, \(t^\star\) must offer exactly \(n\) outcomes: affordable sets of size \(n\) exist, and \(n\) is also the maximum offer size.

For such an offer set \(B\), let

\[ C(B)=\left|\bigcup_{b_i^\sigma\in B}S_i^\sigma\right|. \]

The first round succeeds precisely for those \(C(B)\) responder types whose associated element is covered.

If the first round fails, its \(n\) offers are discarded and exactly \(n\) outcomes remain. The initiator must offer all of them in the second round. Since every element is covered by the union of all \(2n\) outcomes, a responder that rejected every first-round offer accepts at least one remaining offer. Agreement therefore occurs in the second round.

Consequently:

  • First-round agreement gives \(U_{t^\star}=1-1=0\).
  • Second-round agreement gives \(U_{t^\star}=1-2=-1\).

The selected agreement outcome and its prescribed tie-breaking do not change these values. Hence

\[ \mathbb E[U_{t^\star}\mid B] =-\left(1-\frac{C(B)}N\right). \]

Let

\[ C^\star=\max_{\lvert B\rvert=n}C(B). \]

Because \(H_{t^\star}\equiv0\), the optimal first offer maximizes \(C(B)\), and

\[ \mathbb E[U_{t^\star}] =-\frac{N-C^\star}{N}. \]

Every other initiator type meets the dummy responder. No agreement is jointly feasible, so negotiation ends in disagreement. Its bargaining and privacy costs are zero, giving \(U_t=-1\), independently of its policy.

The requested aggregate is therefore exactly

\[ G=\sum_t\mu_t\mathbb E[U_t] =-\frac12-\frac{N-C^\star}{2N}. \]

By the covering equivalence,

\[ G=-\frac12 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{the formula is satisfiable}. \]

If the formula is unsatisfiable, the integer \(N-C^\star\) is at least one, so

\[ G\le-\frac12-\frac1{2N}. \]

This proves that deciding whether the optimal aggregate is at least \(-1/2\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, and therefore exact aggregate computation is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

Likewise, an optimal first offer decides satisfiability: compute it and test whether its associated sets cover \(E\). Thus optimal offering-policy computation is also \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard.

4. An exact finite algorithm and its running time

For the general supplied instance, define

\[ N_t=\left|\operatorname{supp}q_\mu(\cdot\mid t)\right|, \qquad N_{\max}=\max_tN_t, \]

\[ A_{\max}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell_{\max}}\binom mi, \qquad R=\left\lceil\frac m{\ell_{\min}}\right\rceil. \]

The explicit kernel encoding gives

\[ N_{\max}\le\min\{\tau^{g-1},L\}. \]

The following bounds apply to promised-valid inputs; they do not require checking the scoring circuits’ semantic promises.

Horizon bound

Suppose a negotiation executes \(z\ge1\) rounds. Every round preceding the last must have failed while permitting continuation, and therefore discarded at least \(\ell_{\min}\) outcomes. The last round offers at least one outcome. Thus

\[ (z-1)\ell_{\min}+1\le m, \]

which implies \(z\le R\).

History enumeration

For a fixed initiator and fixed responder tuple, scores and subsequent mandatory agreement choices are deterministic once the offering actions are specified.

An ordered sequence of \(d\) disjoint offer sets can be encoded by assigning each outcome a label in \(\{0,\ldots,d\}\): zero means unoffered, and a positive label records its round. There are at most \((d+1)^m\) such sequences.

Each sequence generates at most \(N_t\) observable histories. Therefore the total number of histories for initiator \(t\) is bounded by

\[ N_t\sum_{d=0}^{R}(d+1)^m \le N_t(R+1)^{m+1}. \]

This count includes counterfactual histories needed to construct a policy table.

Backward induction

At each history:

  • Restrict the listed tuple kernel to tuples consistent with the observed scores and normalize it.
  • Enumerate the at most \(A_{\max}\) candidate offer sets.
  • Evaluate the scoring circuits for every remaining tuple.
  • Check affordability for every possible score profile and every jointly feasible offer. Apply the required minimum-size rule.
  • Group tuples by observed score profile. Where agreement is possible, apply the prescribed maximization of \(v_t(o)+H_t(b')\) and its outcome tie-breaking. Otherwise recurse or terminate according to the continuation rule.
  • Compare expected terminal forward payoffs and apply the prescribed policy tie-breaking.

Privacy beliefs are computed separately from the common prior \(\mu\): retain precisely the types consistent with the directly received feasibility bits. Every realized transcript has positive probability under its true source type because that type has positive prior mass.

At termination, compute both \(U_t+H_t(b')\) and \(U_t\). Backward induction optimizes the former; propagating the latter along the selected policy produces the requested aggregate. This distinction matters when \(H_t\) is nonzero.

All computations are exact rational computations. In particular, terminal expressions involve only polynomially many input quantities. A deterministic policy has one terminal trajectory per listed tuple, so each conditional expectation is a sum over at most \(N_t\) rational terminal payoffs; their bit lengths remain polynomial in the input and numerical parameters. Computing \(F\) can require \(O(D\log(m+1))\) bits, so the bound below explicitly retains numerical dependence on \(D\).

A conservative bit-operation bound for the complete history table and exact aggregate is

\[ \tau N_{\max}^2 A_{\max}(R+1)^{m+1} \operatorname{poly}(L,m,\tau,g,D). \]

Equivalently, substituting the kernel bound gives

\[ \tau\min\{\tau^{g-1},L\}^{2} \left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell_{\max}}\binom mi\right) \left(1+\left\lceil\frac m{\ell_{\min}}\right\rceil\right)^{m+1} \operatorname{poly}(L,m,\tau,g,D). \]

These bounds are deliberately loose. Since \(A_{\max}\le2^m\) and \(R\le m\), they imply an upper bound of

\[ 2^{O(m\log(m+1))} \operatorname{poly}(L,m,\tau,g,D). \]

The polynomial dependence on numerical \(D\) should not be read as polynomial dependence on its binary encoding length.

Although the full counterfactual history table can be large, the tree reachable under a fixed deterministic policy has at most \(1+N_tR\) nodes: each listed tuple follows one path of length at most \(R\). The hardness proof therefore concerns finding the optimal decisions, rather than merely printing a large output.

5. Relation to Q-learning convergence

This model terminates under every policy within \(R\) rounds. It is therefore a finite absorbing problem, with bounded rewards for every fixed input. The undiscounted absorbing extension in [Watkins–Dayan](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~dayan/papers/cjch.pdf) is compatible with this structure.

Its almost-sure convergence guarantee under infinite exploration and the stated learning-rate conditions supplies no polynomial running-time bound for computing an exact optimal policy. It therefore does not contradict the reduction above.
