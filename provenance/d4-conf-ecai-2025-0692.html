The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given projects \(P\), rational per-capita costs \(c_j>0\), a public budget \(L\), and a binary-encoded rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete voter types \(t=(s_t,d_t,u_t)\), with weight \(w_t=s_t-d_t\), compute the DA-Pareto outcome and a feasible funding witness. First choose \(B^*\in\arg\max\{S_\mu(B):\sum_{j\in B}c_j\le L\}\), where \(S_\mu(B)=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\sum_{j\in B}u_t(p_j)\). Then choose a support-maximizing bundle among \(B^*\) and bundles \(B\) that weakly improve every positive-mass type’s utility over \(B^*\), strictly improve at least one type, and can be funded within \(L\) plus donations from strictly benefiting types. Aggregate donations \(x_{t,j}\) must satisfy \(x_{t,j}=0\) when \(u_t(p_j)=0\), \(\sum_{j\in B}x_{t,j}\le\mu_t d_t\), and \(\sum_{j\in B}x_{t,j}>0\) only for types strictly benefiting over \(B^*\); public funding and donations together must cover each selected project’s cost. Return the selected bundle and its funding witness.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_m\}\), with \(m\ge1\), have indivisible projects of rational per-capita costs \(c_j>0\), and let \(\ell\ge0\) be the rational public budget.
A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. All numerical inputs are binary rationals; let \(L\) be their total encoding length.
Each complete type is \(t=(s_t,d_t,u_t)\), where \(s_t>0\), \(0\le d_t<s_t\), \(w_t=s_t-d_t\), and \(u_t(p_j)\ge0\) with \(\sum_j u_t(p_j)=1\). Define \(u_t(B)=\sum_{p_j\in B}u_t(p_j)\) and \(S_\mu(B)=\sum_t\mu_t w_tu_t(B)\); donations do not directly reduce utility.
First choose \(B^*\in\arg\max\{S_\mu(B):B\subseteq P,\ \sum_{p_j\in B}c_j\le\ell\}\).
A funding witness for \(B\) consists of nonnegative rational public payments \(\beta_j\) and aggregate donations \(x_{t,j}\), all zero outside \(B\), satisfying \(\sum_j\beta_j\le\ell\), \(\sum_jx_{t,j}\le\mu_td_t\), and \(\beta_j+\sum_tx_{t,j}=c_j\) for every \(p_j\in B\).
Require \(x_{t,j}=0\) whenever \(u_t(p_j)=0\) or \(u_t(B)\le u_t(B^*)\).
Let \(\mathcal T\) contain \(B^*\) and every bundle admitting such a witness with \(u_t(B)\ge u_t(B^*)\) for all support types and strict inequality for at least one. A DA-Pareto outcome maximizes \(S_\mu\) over \(\mathcal T\). Either maximization may choose any maximizer.
Is computing a DA-Pareto outcome together with a feasible funding witness polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?
For finite electorates, computing the initial public-budget optimum is NP-hard even when all voting weights are equal; DA-Greedy is polynomial-time computable. [Verified source](https://www.iog.io/api/research/pdf/ZHUBN4XC).

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Theorem. Computing a DA-Pareto outcome together with a funding witness is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even when \(\tau=1\), the public budget and donation limit are strictly positive, and the sole type assigns positive utility to every project. The reduction is independent of both maximizations’ tie choices.

Zero donations already yield a knapsack special case. The proof below goes further: on every yes-instance, every final outcome strictly improves on the initial optimum and necessarily uses donations.

Construction. Reduce from SUBSET SUM: given positive binary-encoded integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) and \(T\ge1\), decide whether some \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=T\). This problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete; it is the exact-sum problem called KNAPSACK in [Karp’s original paper](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/Karp.pdf).

Let \(A=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\), and choose the smallest power of two \(M>A\). Create projects \(p_1,\ldots,p_n,q\), with

\( c_i=a_i,\qquad c_q=T+1,\qquad \ell=T-\frac12. \)

Use one type, of mass \(\mu=1\), with

\( s=\frac32,\qquad d=\frac12,\qquad w=s-d=1, \)

and utilities

\( u(p_i)=\frac{a_i}{M},\qquad u(q)=\frac{M-A}{M}. \)

Every utility is strictly positive, and their sum is \(1\). All numbers are dyadic rationals, so the construction also satisfies the narrower interpretation of “binary rational.” Their encoding lengths are polynomial in the SUBSET SUM input length: \(\log M=O(\log A)\), and \(\log A\le\log n+\max_i\log a_i+O(1)\).

Since \(\mu=w=1\), the objective is \(S_\mu(B)=u(B)\).

Properties of the initial and final bundles. Fix any permitted initial maximizer \(B^*\).

First, \(q\notin B^*\), because \(c_q>\ell\). The remaining project costs are integers, so public-budget feasibility implies

\( \sum_{p_i\in B^*}a_i\le T-1, \qquad u(B^*)\le\frac{T-1}{M}. \)

Second, every bundle admitting a funding witness satisfies

\( \sum_{p_j\in B}c_j =\sum_j\beta_j+\sum_jx_{1,j} \le\ell+\mu d =T. \)

The baseline bundle \(B^*\) also has a witness: pay its entire cost publicly and set all donations to zero. Thus every bundle in \(\mathcal T\) has total cost at most \(T\). In particular, none contains \(q\), whose cost is \(T+1\). Consequently, every \(B\in\mathcal T\) satisfies

\( S_\mu(B)=u(B)=\frac1M\sum_{p_i\in B}a_i\le\frac{T}{M}. \)

Yes-instances. Suppose \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i=T\), and let \(B_I=\{p_i:i\in I\}\). Then

\( u(B_I)=\frac{T}{M}>\frac{T-1}{M}\ge u(B^*). \)

Hence the sole type strictly benefits and is eligible to donate to every selected project.

Because \(T>0\), the set \(I\) is nonempty. Choose \(i_0\in I\), and define a witness by

\( x_{1,i_0}=\frac12,\qquad \beta_{i_0}=a_{i_0}-\frac12, \qquad \beta_i=a_i\ \text{for }i\in I\setminus\{i_0\}, \)

with all other payments and donations zero. These amounts are nonnegative because \(a_{i_0}\ge1\). They exactly fund each selected project, use donations totaling \(d=\frac12\), and use public payments totaling

\( \sum_j\beta_j=T-\frac12=\ell. \)

The donation restrictions hold because \(u(p_{i_0})>0\) and \(u(B_I)>u(B^*)\). Therefore \(B_I\in\mathcal T\).

The preceding upper bound now implies that every DA-Pareto outcome \(B\) has objective \(T/M\), hence \(\sum_{p_i\in B}a_i=T\). Moreover, any witness for such an outcome must donate at least \(T-\ell=\frac12\); donations are necessary.

No-instances and decoding. If the SUBSET SUM instance has no solution, every bundle in \(\mathcal T\) excludes \(q\) and has integer total cost at most \(T\), but cannot have cost exactly \(T\). Thus every DA-Pareto outcome has objective strictly less than \(T/M\).

Given any valid output bundle \(B\), we therefore recover the SUBSET SUM answer by testing whether

\( \sum_{p_i\in B}a_i=T. \)

This test takes polynomial time. It works for every initial maximizer and every final maximizer, and does not require examining the returned witness.

Therefore, a polynomial-time algorithm in \(m,\tau,L\) for the requested computation would solve SUBSET SUM in polynomial time. The requested problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard; a polynomial-time algorithm would imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\).
