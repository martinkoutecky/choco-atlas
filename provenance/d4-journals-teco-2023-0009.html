The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), positive integer weights \(w_t\), and rational quota \(0<\theta<\sum_t\mu_t w_t\), form, for each admissible integer \(q\), a weighted voting game with \(q\mu_t\) players of type \(t\), each of weight \(w_t\), and quota \(q\theta\). As \(q\) tends to infinity through values for which every \(q\mu_t\) and \(q\theta\) is integral, determine whether \(a_t^{(q)}=q x_i^{(q)}\) converges for players \(i\) of type \(t\), where \(x^{(q)}\) is the finite-game nucleolus, and compute the limit or an \(\varepsilon\)-approximation to it.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) is the support size. Each type has a binary-encoded weight \(w_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Let \(m\) be the number of distinct weights and \(L\) the total input bit length.
Given a binary-rational quota \(0<\theta<\sum_{t\in T}\mu_tw_t\), call a positive integer \(q\) admissible if every \(q\mu_t\) and \(q\theta\) is integral and \(q\theta>\max_t w_t\). The latter restriction removes only finitely many replication sizes.
For each admissible \(q\), let \(N_q\) contain \(q\mu_t\) players of type \(t\), and define \(v_q(S)=1\) exactly when the total weight of \(S\) is at least \(q\theta\), and \(v_q(S)=0\) otherwise. Let \(x^{(q)}\) be its nucleolus over imputations. By symmetry, \(a_t^{(q)}=q x_i^{(q)}\) is independent of the chosen player \(i\) of type \(t\).
The computational problem is to decide whether \(a^{(q)}\) converges along all admissible integers and, if it does, given binary-rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\), compute a rational vector within \(\varepsilon\) of its limit in the maximum norm.

Is this convergence-and-limit computation problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), including the encoding of \(\varepsilon\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite weighted voting games, the nucleolus is computable in pseudopolynomial time ([Pashkovich](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02670)).
For integer-weight voting games, sufficiently many proportional replicas have nucleolus equal to normalized weights ([Kurz–Napel–Nohn](https://www.vwl4.uni-bayreuth.de/pool/Publikationen_Napel/2014_the_nucleolus_of_large_majority_games.pdf)); this covers exactly the replication family above and already settles the stated problem in polynomial time. Thus the requested target is not open.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restrict admissible replication sizes to a tail with quota exceeding every individual weight, ensuring a nonempty imputation set without changing convergence.
  • Define m as the number of distinct numerical weights and L as total binary input length.
  • Use the imputation nucleolus, as in the discrete definition.
  • Specify approximation by absolute maximum-norm error, with binary-rational epsilon included in the input.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Conclusion

The problem is polynomial-time solvable. For every valid input, the sequence is eventually constant, with limit \(a_t=w_t/W\), where \(W=\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s w_s\). This rational vector can be computed exactly.

2. The replication theorem and its hypotheses

We use Proposition 2 of [Kurz–Napel–Nohn, *The Nucleolus of Large Majority Games*](https://www.vwl4.uni-bayreuth.de/pool/Publikationen_Napel/2014_the_nucleolus_of_large_majority_games.pdf). Its precise sufficient condition is: for a weighted voting game with nonnegative integer weights, let \(M\) be the maximum weight, \(d\) the number of distinct weights, \(r\) the minimum multiplicity of any weight, and \(\alpha\) the quota divided by total weight. If

\(\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}\,r>2dM^2,\)

then the nucleolus equals the normalized weight vector.

For the given input, define

\(W=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t w_t,\qquad M=\max_{t\in T}w_t,\qquad \alpha=\theta/W,\qquad \delta=\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}.\)

The quota assumption \(0<\theta<W\) implies \(\delta>0\).

Let \(\Omega=\{w_t:t\in T\}\). For each \(\omega\in\Omega\), put

\(p_\omega=\sum_{\{t\in T:w_t=\omega\}}\mu_t,\qquad p_{\min}=\min_{\omega\in\Omega}p_\omega.\)

Every \(p_\omega\) is positive, so \(p_{\min}>0\).

For an admissible replication size \(q\), the game has total weight \(qW\), quota \(q\theta\), and exactly \(qp_\omega\) players of weight \(\omega\). Consequently, its normalized quota is \(\alpha\), its number of distinct weights is \(m\), and its minimum weight multiplicity is \(qp_{\min}\). Its weights are positive integers, as required.

The imputation convention also agrees with the task: admissibility makes every singleton losing, while \(\theta<W\) makes the grand coalition winning.

3. Eventual equality and convergence

Define the positive integer

\(Q=\left\lfloor\frac{2mM^2}{\delta p_{\min}}\right\rfloor+1.\)

For every admissible \(q\ge Q\), we have

\(\delta\,qp_{\min}>2mM^2.\)

Thus all hypotheses of the cited proposition hold. It follows that every player \(i\) of type \(t\) receives

\(x_i^{(q)}=\frac{w_t}{qW},\)

and therefore

\(a_t^{(q)}=q x_i^{(q)}=\frac{w_t}{W}.\)

There are arbitrarily large admissible integers. Indeed, let \(D\) be the least common multiple of the reduced denominators of the masses and quota. Every sufficiently large multiple \(q\) of \(D\) satisfies both the integrality conditions and \(q\theta>M\).

Hence, along all admissible integers, the vector \(a^{(q)}\) is eventually equal to the same vector. This proves convergence and identifies its limit exactly.

4. Polynomial-time computation

The algorithm reports convergence and outputs the rational vector \(b\) defined by \(b_t=w_t/W\).

To verify its bit complexity explicitly, write each mass as \(\mu_t=u_t/d_t\), and take the common denominator \(B=\prod_{t\in T}d_t\). Then

\(W=A/B,\qquad A=\sum_{t\in T}u_t w_t(B/d_t),\)

so the output coordinates can be represented as \(b_t=w_tB/A\).

The bit lengths of \(B\), each summand defining \(A\), and \(A\) itself are polynomial in the total input length \(L\): multiplication adds operand bit lengths, and summing \(\tau\) integers adds at most \(\lceil\log_2\tau\rceil\) bits beyond the largest summand. Standard integer arithmetic therefore computes all \(\tau\) coordinates in polynomial time.

Because the output is exact, its maximum-norm error is \(0<\varepsilon\). No finite replicated game needs to be constructed, and no finite-game nucleolus computation is required.
