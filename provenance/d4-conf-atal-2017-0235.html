The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a population with rational type masses \(\mu_r\), specify a directed sparse random network by giving each ordered pair of type-\(r\) and type-\(s\) agents an edge independently with probability \(\lambda_{rs}/N\), where \(N\) is population size and the rational contact rates \(\lambda_{rs}\) are bounded. Assign edges to a fixed finite set of tie categories, each with an uncertain propagation parameter \(\theta_j\in[a_j,b_j]\). Given a seeding budget \(\kappa\), a feasible action is a type-wise seed-mass profile \(z\) with \(0\le z_r\le\mu_r\) and \(\sum_r z_r\le\kappa\). Let \(g_\mu(z,\theta)\) be the limiting expected influenced mass after a fixed finite cascade horizon, and let \(g^G_\mu(\theta)\) be the corresponding type-wise greedy benchmark. Can one compute a finitely represented randomized strategy over feasible profiles with the \((1-1/e,\epsilon)\)-minimax guarantee of Theorem 1, in time polynomial in the number of types, input bit length, horizon, and \(1/\epsilon\), independently of \(N\)?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_r\), with \(\sum_r\mu_r=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types; \(L\) is the total input bit length.
Input also specifies rational rates \(0\le\lambda_{rs}\le C\), for a fixed constant \(C\), a category map \(c(r,s)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), rational intervals \(0\le a_j\le b_j\le1\), rational \(0<\kappa\le1\), an integer horizon \(T\ge1\), and rational \(0<\epsilon<1\). The number \(m\) of tie categories is fixed; write \(P=\prod_j[a_j,b_j]\).
For population sizes \(N\) with integral \(N\mu_r\), each ordered pair of distinct agents of types \(r,s\) has an edge independently with probability \(\lambda_{rs}/N\); its category is \(c(r,s)\). The graph is sampled once and is not observed before seeding.
All seeds are activated at time zero and attend certainly. In each of \(T\) synchronous rounds, every already active agent independently attempts to activate each inactive out-neighbor with probability \(\theta_{c(r,s)}\); failed attempts may be repeated next round, and activation is permanent. There are no observations, later interventions, or additional random propagation parameters.
Let \(F_N(k,\theta)\) be the expected final active fraction, averaging over the graph and propagation, when \(k_r\) agents of each type are seeded. Define \(Z=\{z\in\mathbb R^\tau:0\le z_r\le\mu_r,\ \sum_rz_r\le\kappa\}\) and \(g_\mu(z,\theta)=\lim_{N\to\infty}F_N(\lfloor Nz\rfloor,\theta)\), along these population sizes.
Define the type-wise greedy benchmark by starting with no seeds and adding one seed at a time to maximize the marginal increase in \(F_N\), until \(\lfloor N\kappa\rfloor\) seeds are chosen; exclude exhausted types and break ties by smallest type index. If its allocation is \(k_N^G(\theta)\), set \(g_\mu^G(\theta)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}F_N(k_N^G(\theta),\theta)\).
For any Borel probability distribution \(Q\) on \(Z\), put \(V(Q)=\inf_{\theta\in P}\mathbb E_{z\sim Q}[g_\mu(z,\theta)/g_\mu^G(\theta)]\) and \(V^*=\sup_Q V(Q)\). A finitely represented strategy is an explicit finite list of feasible rational profiles and rational probabilities summing to one.
Does this problem admit an algorithm computing a finitely represented strategy \(Q\) with \(V(Q)\ge(1-1/e)V^*-\epsilon\) in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,T,1/\epsilon\) for each fixed \(m\), independently of \(N\), or is achieving this approximation guarantee NP-hard?
For discrete directed networks with a fixed number of tie categories, a polynomial-time \((1-1/e,\epsilon)\)-minimax approximation relative to the greedy benchmark is established by [Wilder et al.](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2017/pdfs/p1305.pdf).
No result settling this precise limiting mass problem was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalize total population mass to one and interpret influenced mass as the active fraction; take a strictly positive budget at most one so the benchmark denominator is positive.
  • Interpret bounded contact rates as bounded by a fixed constant C, and preserve the anchor's assumption that the number of tie categories is fixed.
  • Assign categories through an input map on ordered type pairs; exclude self-edges and sample the graph once, without revealing it before seeding.
  • Use the repeated-attempt propagation variant, with synchronous rounds, permanent activation, certain attendance, and all seeds selected initially.
  • Set lower-order prior width to zero: the graded question specifies propagation parameters but no additional distribution over edge probabilities.
  • Define the population limit along sizes with integral type counts, rounding seed counts down.
  • Define the unspecified type-wise greedy benchmark through finite-population greedy on graph-averaged expected influence, with deterministic type-index tie-breaking; use its limit superior to avoid assuming convergence.
  • Allow all real feasible profiles and Borel mixed strategies in the optimal benchmark, but require the computed strategy to have explicit finite rational support.
  • Use infima and suprema in the robust objective so its definition does not assume extrema are attained.

Partial progress: polynomial parameter discretization and a weaker approximation

Put
$$ \alpha=1-\frac1e. $$

I do not settle whether the requested guarantee is polynomial-time achievable or NP-hard. I prove two additional results:

  • There is an unconditional polynomial-time algorithm producing a finitely represented strategy satisfying

$$ V(Q)\ge \alpha^3V^*-\epsilon. $$

  • The requested $\alpha$ guarantee follows from a precisely specified oracle: approximate, from above, the supremum of the limiting greedy benchmark over each of polynomially many rational parameter cells.

The discretization uses multiplicative sensitivity of the diffusion numerator. It accommodates zero propagation parameters and does not assume continuity of the greedy benchmark.

Everything needed from the earlier notes is proved below, including a valid branching-process domination and an accumulated numerical-error bound.

1. The limiting objective and its basic properties

Write
$$ p_{rs}(d;\theta) = \theta_{c(r,s)}(1-\theta_{c(r,s)})^{d-1}, \qquad d\ge 1. $$

At propagation probability zero, the waiting time is infinite. At probability one, its distribution puts probability one on waiting time one; thus the formula means $p_{rs}(1;\theta)=1$ and $p_{rs}(d;\theta)=0$ for $d>1$.

Define
$$ x_s(0)=z_s, $$
and, for $1\le t\le T$,
$$ H_s(t) = \sum_{r=1}^{\tau}\lambda_{rs} \sum_{d=1}^{t}p_{rs}(d;\theta)x_r(t-d), $$
$$ x_s(t) = \mu_s-(\mu_s-z_s)e^{-H_s(t)}. $$

Proposition 1: existence and uniform convergence

The limit defining $g_\mu$ exists, and
$$ g_\mu(z,\theta)=\sum_{s=1}^{\tau}x_s(T). $$

For each fixed input society and horizon,
$$ \sup_{\theta\in[0,1]^m} \sup_k \left| F_N(k,\theta)-g_\mu(k/N,\theta) \right| \longrightarrow 0, $$
where $k$ ranges over all allocations respecting type capacities.

Proof

Presample an independent geometric waiting time on every existing directed edge. Its clock starts when its source becomes active. This reproduces the repeated-attempt process. A vertex is active by time $T$ exactly when a seed reaches it along a directed path whose waiting times sum to at most $T$. Such a path has at most $T$ edges.

For a uniformly selected type-$s$ vertex, the incoming graph neighborhood through depth $T$ converges to a multitype branching tree. A type-$s$ vertex has independent Poisson numbers of type-$r$ incoming children, with means
$$ \mu_r\lambda_{rs}. $$
A type-$r$ vertex is independently marked as seeded with probability $z_r/\mu_r$.

Here is a valid uniform truncation bound. Fix an integer
$$ D\ge \max\{1,2C\}, \qquad R=1+D. $$
For sufficiently large $N$, every edge probability is at most $1/2$. A Bernoulli variable of parameter $p$ is stochastically dominated by a Poisson variable of mean $-\log(1-p)$: the latter is positive with probability exactly $p$. Consequently, a fresh incoming exploration count is dominated by a Poisson variable of mean at most
$$ \sum_{\text{candidate agents}} -\log(1-\lambda_{rs}/N) \le 2\sum_{\text{candidate agents}}\lambda_{rs}/N \le 2C. $$
It is therefore dominated by a Poisson variable of mean $D$. The limiting offspring count is also dominated by this law.

Thus both neighborhood sizes can be bounded by a Poisson-$D$ branching process through depth $T$. If $Z_d$ denotes its generation size, then
$$ \mathbb E Z_{d+1}^2 = D^2\mathbb E Z_d^2+D\mathbb E Z_d \le R^2\mathbb E Z_d^2. $$
For its total size $M=\sum_{d=0}^T Z_d$, this gives
$$ \mathbb E M\le (T+1)R^T, \qquad \mathbb E M^2\le (T+1)^2R^{2T}. $$

Truncate explorations after $K$ vertices. For fixed $K$, binomial-to-Poisson approximation and the probability of repeated vertex labels give convergence of the truncated neighborhoods. Conditional on types, seed indicators are sampled without replacement within each type. Coupling this sampling to sampling with replacement gives an error bounded by a constant times
$$ \frac{K^2}{N\mu_{\min}}, \qquad \mu_{\min}=\min_r\mu_r, $$
uniformly over the seed allocation. Propagation marks can be coupled using common uniforms, uniformly over $\theta$.

The probability that truncation changes the activation event is at most $\mathbb E M/K$. Taking first $N$ and then $K$ to infinity proves the asserted uniform convergence.

To evaluate the tree, let $q_s(t)$ be its type-$s$ root activation probability. A type-$r$ child activates its parent by time $t$ with probability
$$ \sum_{d=1}^t p_{rs}(d;\theta)q_r(t-d). $$
Independent Poisson thinning gives
$$ 1-q_s(t) = \left(1-\frac{z_s}{\mu_s}\right) \exp\left( -\sum_r\mu_r\lambda_{rs} \sum_{d=1}^t p_{rs}(d;\theta)q_r(t-d) \right). $$
Substituting $x_s(t)=\mu_sq_s(t)$ yields the stated recursion. Its continuity in $z$ also establishes the limit for $\lfloor Nz\rfloor$. $\square$

For brevity, write
$$ g(z,\theta)=g_\mu(z,\theta), \qquad b(\theta)=g_\mu^G(\theta), \qquad O(\theta)=\max_{z\in Z}g(z,\theta). $$

Proposition 2: derivatives and benchmark bounds

Define
$$ U=\frac{(T+1)R^T}{\mu_{\min}}, \qquad H=\frac{(T+1)^2R^{2T}}{\mu_{\min}^2}. $$
On the seed-capacity box,
$$ e^{-C}\le \partial_r g(z,\theta)\le U, $$
and
$$ -H\le \partial_r\partial_s g(z,\theta)\le 0. $$

Moreover,
$$ \alpha O(\theta)\le b(\theta)\le O(\theta), \qquad b(\theta)\ge\kappa. $$
In particular,
$$ 0\le V^*\le \frac1\alpha<2. $$

Proof

Choose the branching-tree root type according to the mass vector $\mu$. Let $n_r$ count type-$r$ vertices whose weighted distance to the root is at most $T$, including the root when applicable. Conditional on the tree and waiting times, inactivity has probability
$$ \prod_r\left(1-\frac{z_r}{\mu_r}\right)^{n_r}. $$
Therefore
$$ g(z,\theta) = 1-\mathbb E\left[ \prod_r\left(1-\frac{z_r}{\mu_r}\right)^{n_r} \right]. $$

The conditional activation polynomial has nonnegative first derivatives and nonpositive second derivatives, including pure second derivatives. Their absolute values are bounded by $M/\mu_{\min}$ and $M^2/\mu_{\min}^2$. The preceding moment bounds justify differentiation under the expectation and continuity up to the boundary.

The lower derivative bound follows from the recursion. All activation derivatives are nonnegative, so
$$ \partial_r x_r(T)\ge e^{-H_r(T)}. $$
Also,
$$ H_r(T) \le C\sum_u\mu_u\sum_{d=1}^T p_{ur}(d;\theta) \le C. $$

For the benchmark bounds, fix the graph and edge waiting times. Each target is activated precisely when its reverse reachable set intersects the seed set. Final activation is consequently a coverage function, hence monotone and submodular. Expectation preserves both properties.

Exchangeability within each type makes the prescribed type-wise greedy algorithm an individual-agent greedy algorithm. With $K_N=\lfloor N\kappa\rfloor$, the usual greedy recurrence gives
$$ F_N(k_N^G(\theta),\theta) \ge \left[1-\left(1-\frac1{K_N}\right)^{K_N}\right] \operatorname{OPT}_N(\theta) \ge \alpha\operatorname{OPT}_N(\theta). $$
Indeed, submodularity bounds the remaining optimal gain by the sum of at most $K_N$ current marginals; greedy chooses at least their average.

Uniform convergence from Proposition 1 implies
$$ \operatorname{OPT}_N(\theta)\longrightarrow O(\theta). $$
For one direction, every finite allocation divided by $N$ belongs to $Z$. For the other, floor a maximizing profile in $Z$. Taking a limsup proves the benchmark approximation bounds.

Finally, all $K_N$ seeds are certainly active, giving $b(\theta)\ge\kappa$. The payoff bound follows from $g(z,\theta)\le O(\theta)\le b(\theta)/\alpha$.

Each finite greedy value is Borel in $\theta$, because its allocation is determined by finitely many comparisons of continuous functions with a specified tie rule. Its limsup $b$ is Borel. Thus all expectations used below are well-defined. $\square$

2. A polynomial weighted optimization oracle

The following algorithm concerns numerator optimization only. It does not evaluate $b$.

Proposition 3

Let $\theta^1,\ldots,\theta^J\in[0,1]^m$ be rational, and let $w_i$ be nonnegative rational weights summing to one. For rational $0<\eta<1/4$, a feasible rational profile can be computed satisfying
$$ f(z)\ge(\alpha-\eta)\max_{y\in Z}f(y), \qquad f(z)=\sum_{i=1}^Jw_i g(z,\theta^i). $$
The profile has total seed mass $\kappa$. The running time and output bit length are polynomial in the supplied bit length, $J,\tau,T,1/\eta$.

Proof

The function $f$ inherits the derivative bounds of Proposition 2. Choose a fixed positive rational
$$ \ell\le e^{-C}. $$
For example, $\ell=2^{-2D}$ suffices.

Put $\delta=\eta/2$ and
$$ a=\frac{\delta\ell}{32}. $$
Start at $z=0$. At each step, compute every eligible derivative to absolute error at most $a$ and select the coordinate with largest computed derivative.

Let $d$ be its true derivative and $d_{\max}$ the largest eligible true derivative. Then
$$ d\ge d_{\max}-2a \ge \left(1-\frac{\delta}{16}\right)d_{\max}. $$

Let $\widetilde d$ be its computed derivative and set
$$ h=\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\widetilde d. $$
The largest allowed coordinate increment ends at capacity or at the remaining budget. Accept that endpoint if its computed derivative is at least $h$. Otherwise, bisect, maintaining an accepted lower endpoint and a rejected upper endpoint, until their distance is at most $a/H$, then take the lower endpoint.

At every accepted endpoint, the true derivative is at least
$$ h-a \ge \left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)d-2a \ge (1-\delta)d. $$
Since the derivative is nonincreasing along the coordinate, the same lower bound holds throughout the step.

For a bisection-ending step, the Hessian bound and the rejected upper endpoint give
$$ d_{\mathrm{end}} \le h+2a \le \left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)d+3a \le \left(1-\frac{\delta}{4}\right)d. $$
Such a step is positive. If its lower endpoint were zero, the rejected upper endpoint would be within $a/H$ of the start, so its true derivative would be at least $d-a$. Rejection instead implies
$$ d_{\mathrm{upper}}<h+a\le d-\frac{\delta d}{2}+2a<d-a, $$
a contradiction.

All coordinate derivatives decrease as the profile increases. Hence each coordinate can undergo at most
$$ 1+\frac4\delta\log\frac U\ell $$
bisection-ending steps. Capacity-ending steps exhaust a coordinate; there is at most one budget-ending step. The total number of steps is polynomial.

Parametrize the path by seeded mass $s\in[0,\kappa]$. Throughout each step, the chosen derivative is at least $(1-2\delta)$ times the largest currently eligible derivative.

Let $f^*=\max_{y\in Z}f(y)$ and choose a maximizer $y$. Monotonicity and diminishing returns on the full capacity box imply
$$ f^*-f(z) \le f(z\vee y)-f(z) \le \sum_r(y_r-z_r)_+\partial_r f(z). $$
Exhausted coordinates contribute zero, and
$$ \sum_r(y_r-z_r)_+\le\kappa. $$
Thus, almost everywhere along the path,
$$ \frac{d}{ds}f(z(s)) \ge \frac{1-2\delta}{\kappa}\bigl(f^*-f(z(s))\bigr). $$
The budget is filled because all eligible derivatives are positive and the total capacity is one. Since $f(0)=0$, integration yields
$$ f(z(\kappa)) \ge \left[1-e^{-(1-2\delta)}\right]f^* \ge (\alpha-2\delta)f^*. $$

It remains to justify numerical complexity. The recursion and all first derivatives form an arithmetic-and-exponential circuit with
$$ S=O(J\tau^3T^2) $$
gates. Exact exponential arguments lie in $[-C,0]$. Exact intermediate magnitudes are bounded by a quantity $A$ satisfying
$$ \log A=O\bigl(\log(J\tau T)+\log U+1\bigr). $$
For instance, a sufficiently large fixed multiple of
$$ 2+(1+C)J\tau T(1+U) $$
works.

As long as intermediate errors are at most one, additions, multiplications, and exponentials increase the maximum preceding error by a factor at most
$$ K=4A+8, $$
plus their local rounding error. For exponentials this uses that the perturbed arguments remain in $[-C-1,1]$.

If every local operation has absolute error at most
$$ \nu=\frac{a}{2(S+1)K^S}, $$
induction gives a final error at most
$$ \nu(S+1)K^S\le a/2. $$
The required precision is therefore
$$ O\bigl(\log(1/a)+S\log K+\log(S+1)\bigr), $$
which is polynomial. On this fixed bounded argument interval, Taylor expansion computes the exponential to that precision in polynomial time: the remainder is bounded by a constant times a fixed constant raised to the truncation order, divided by its factorial.

Each bisection needs $O(\log(H/a)+1)$ iterations. Its endpoints are rational, and the cumulative number of midpoint operations is polynomial, so profile bit lengths are polynomial as well. This proves the claimed implementation. $\square$

The same oracle applies to any nonzero nonnegative rational linear combination of scenarios, by normalizing its coefficients.

3. New sensitivity bounds

The next bounds concern fixed seed profiles. They do not assert regularity of the greedy benchmark.

Proposition 4: multiplicative sensitivity

If
$$ \theta\le\theta'\le\rho\theta $$
coordinatewise, where $\rho\ge1$ and both vectors belong to $[0,1]^m$, then
$$ g(z,\theta)\le g(z,\theta')\le\rho^Tg(z,\theta). $$

In particular, the hypothesis forces a zero coordinate of $\theta$ to remain zero.

Proof

First work at finite $N$ with a fixed seed set and graph. Presample, for each existing edge and each absolute round, an independent Bernoulli attempt bit. This is another valid representation of the specified process.

Activation of a target is a monotone Boolean function of these bits. Whenever activation occurs, it has a certificate consisting of a time-respecting path from a seed, using at most $T$ successful bits.

A successful pivotal bit must belong to every successful certificate: turning off a bit outside one certificate preserves that certificate. Consequently, on an activation outcome, there are at most $T$ successful pivotal bits.

For a Bernoulli bit of parameter $p$, the probability that it is both successful and pivotal equals $p$ times the derivative of the activation probability with respect to that bit’s parameter. Summing over bits and averaging over targets and graphs gives
$$ \sum_{j=1}^m \theta_j\partial_jF_N(k,\theta) \le T F_N(k,\theta). $$
The identity and inequality also hold at endpoints by their polynomial expressions.

Interpolate positive coordinates logarithmically:
$$ \theta_j(u) = \theta_j\exp\left( u\log\frac{\theta'_j}{\theta_j} \right), \qquad 0\le u\le1, $$
and leave zero coordinates fixed. Monotonicity of the derivatives gives
$$ \frac{d}{du}F_N(k,\theta(u)) \le T\log(\rho)\,F_N(k,\theta(u)). $$
Integration yields
$$ F_N(k,\theta')\le\rho^TF_N(k,\theta). $$
Taking the population limit proves the result. $\square$

Proposition 5: absolute sensitivity proportional to seed mass

Let $s=\sum_rz_r$, and define the rational bound
$$ B=TD(1+D)^{T-1}. $$
Then
$$ |g(z,\theta)-g(z,\theta')| \le sB\|\theta-\theta'\|_\infty. $$

Proof

It suffices to compare coordinatewise ordered vectors, by replacing arbitrary vectors with their coordinatewise minimum and maximum. Let their maximum coordinate difference be $h$, and couple attempt bits by common uniforms.

If a target activates under the larger vector but not the smaller one, some successful path under the larger vector contains a differing attempt bit. An activation path can be chosen with distinct vertices and strictly increasing attempt rounds.

For a specified path of length $\ell$, the probability its graph edges exist is at most $(C/N)^\ell$. Conditional on their existence, the probability of at least one differing bit is at most $\ell h$.

If the seed set has size $k$, there are at most $kN^\ell$ ordered paths of length $\ell$, summed over targets, and $\binom T\ell$ possible increasing round sequences. A union bound therefore gives
$$ |F_N(k,\theta)-F_N(k,\theta')| \le \frac{k}{N}h \sum_{\ell=1}^T \ell\binom T\ell C^\ell. $$
The sum equals
$$ TC(1+C)^{T-1}\le B. $$
Passing to the limit proves the assertion. $\square$

The factor $s$ is useful: after normalization by a denominator at least $\kappa$, it removes dependence on $1/\kappa$ from this discretization.

4. Polynomially many parameter cells

Fix rational $0<t<1$, and set
$$ h_0=\frac{t}{2B}, \qquad \delta=\frac{t}{4T}. $$

In each coordinate, use knots
$$ 0,\quad h_0,\quad h_0(1+\delta),\quad h_0(1+\delta)^2,\quad\ldots,\quad1, $$
replacing the first knot exceeding one by one. Intersect the resulting intervals with $[a_j,b_j]$. Their products form finitely many closed rational cells covering $P$.

For every coordinate interval $[\ell_j,u_j]$, either
$$ u_j\le h_0 $$
or
$$ u_j\le(1+\delta)\ell_j. $$

Let $\ell_i,u_i$ be the lower and upper corners of cell $i$, and put
$$ A=(1+\delta)^T\le e^{t/4}\le1+\frac t2. $$
Apply multiplicative sensitivity to the coordinates above the cutoff, and then absolute sensitivity to the coordinates below it. For every feasible profile,
$$ g(z,\ell_i) \le g(z,\theta) \le g(z,u_i) \le A g(z,\ell_i)+\frac{\kappa t}{2}, \qquad \theta\text{ in cell }i. $$
Taking maxima over profiles gives
$$ O(\theta)\le A O(\ell_i)+\frac{\kappa t}{2}. $$

The number of knots per coordinate is
$$ O\left( 1+\frac{T}{t} \left(T+\log(T+1)+\log(1/t)\right) \right). $$
Indeed, $\log B=O(T+\log(T+1))$ and $\log(1+\delta)\ge\delta/2$.

Thus the number $J$ of cells is polynomial in $T,1/t$ for each fixed $m$. Repeated multiplication constructs rational knots with polynomial bit length. Interval clipping adds only the input endpoints.

Proposition 6: a finite envelope game approximates the original game

Define
$$ \beta_i=\sup_{\theta\text{ in cell }i}b(\theta) $$
and
$$ W(Q)= \min_{1\le i\le J} \frac{\mathbb E_Q g(z,\ell_i)}{\beta_i}. $$
Then, for every Borel strategy $Q$,
$$ W(Q)\le V(Q)\le W(Q)+2t. $$

Proof

For any $\theta$ in cell $i$, monotonicity and $b(\theta)\le\beta_i$ give
$$ \frac{\mathbb E_Q g(z,\theta)}{b(\theta)} \ge \frac{\mathbb E_Q g(z,\ell_i)}{\beta_i} \ge W(Q). $$
Taking the infimum proves the first inequality.

Choose a cell attaining the finite minimum in $W(Q)$, and choose a sequence $\theta_n$ in that cell satisfying
$$ b(\theta_n)\longrightarrow\beta_i. $$
No attainment or continuity assumption is needed. The numerator bound gives
$$ V(Q) \le A\frac{\mathbb E_Q g(z,\ell_i)}{\beta_i} +\frac{\kappa t}{2\beta_i} \le AW(Q)+\frac t2. $$
Also,
$$ \beta_i\ge b(\ell_i)\ge\alpha O(\ell_i), $$
so $W(Q)\le1/\alpha<2$. Therefore
$$ V(Q)-W(Q) \le \frac{t}{2\alpha}+\frac t2 <2t. $$
$\square$

This is a polynomial-size reduction, but its coefficients $\beta_i$ are not yet supplied by a polynomial-time algorithm.

5. Computing strategies for finite scenario games

We will use a finite-game procedure whose benchmark coefficients are rational and given explicitly.

Proposition 7

Suppose rational $\theta^i\in[0,1]^m$ and positive rational $d_i$ are supplied, and the payoffs
$$ h_i(z)=\frac{g(z,\theta^i)}{d_i} $$
belong to $[0,2]$. Define
$$ W_d^*=\sup_Q\min_i\mathbb E_Qh_i(z). $$
For rational $0<t<1/64$, a polynomial-time algorithm outputs a rational empirical strategy satisfying
$$ \min_i\mathbb E_Qh_i(z) \ge (\alpha-t)W_d^*-t. $$
Its profiles have total mass $\kappa$.

Proof

Use unnormalized adversarial weights initialized to one. At iteration $n$, normalize them to rational weights $w_{n,i}$ and apply Proposition 3 to
$$ \sum_i w_{n,i}h_i(z) $$
with approximation tolerance $t$.

For every choice of adversarial weights,
$$ \max_z\sum_iw_{n,i}h_i(z)\ge W_d^*. $$
This follows because, for any distribution $Q$,
$$ \min_i\mathbb E_Qh_i \le \sum_iw_{n,i}\mathbb E_Qh_i \le \max_z\sum_iw_{n,i}h_i(z). $$
Hence the returned profile $z^n$ satisfies
$$ \sum_iw_{n,i}h_i(z^n)\ge(\alpha-t)W_d^*. $$

Round each payoff to a fixed dyadic mesh, obtaining $\widetilde h_i(z^n)\in[0,2]$ with absolute error at most
$$ \sigma=\frac t8. $$
With $\rho=t/8$, update unnormalized weights by
$$ v_{n+1,i} = v_{n,i} \left(1-\frac{\rho}{2}\widetilde h_i(z^n)\right). $$

For $0\le u\le1$ and $\rho\le1/2$,
$$ \log(1-\rho u)\ge-\rho u-\rho^2, \qquad \log(1-\rho u)\le-\rho u. $$
Comparing the total weight with each individual weight after $K$ iterations yields
$$ \min_i\frac1K\sum_{n=1}^K\widetilde h_i(z^n) \ge \frac1K\sum_{n=1}^K \sum_iw_{n,i}\widetilde h_i(z^n) - \frac{2\log J}{\rho K} -2\rho. $$
Replacing rounded payoffs by true payoffs costs at most $2\sigma$.

Choose
$$ K\ge \frac{64J}{t^2}. $$
Since $\log J\le J$, the total regret is at most
$$ \frac t4+\frac t4+\frac t4<t. $$
The empirical distribution on $z^1,\ldots,z^K$, with probabilities $1/K$, has the stated guarantee.

All updates are rational. Because rounded payoffs use a fixed dyadic mesh, every multiplicative factor has a fixed polynomial bit bound. Each unnormalized weight is a product of at most $K$ such factors, so its bit length is polynomial. This avoids recursive growth of payoff precision with weight precision.

The circuit evaluation argument in Proposition 3 computes the required payoff approximations in polynomial time. $\square$

6. An unconditional polynomial-time weaker guarantee

Theorem 8

For every fixed $m$, there is an algorithm, independent of $N$, running in time polynomial in $m,\tau,L,T,1/\epsilon$ and outputting a finitely represented strategy such that
$$ V(Q)\ge\alpha^3V^*-\epsilon. $$

Proof

Set
$$ t=\frac{\epsilon}{64} $$
and construct the cells from Section 4. Write $O_i=O(\ell_i)$.

Then
$$ \kappa\le d_i\le O_i, \qquad d_i\ge(\alpha-t)O_i. $$
For the lower bound, the downward estimate loses at most $t\kappa/2$, and $O_i\ge\kappa$.

Put $q=\alpha-t$. The finite payoffs $g(z,\ell_i)/d_i$ are bounded by $1/q<2$, so Proposition 7 applies.

$$ d_i\le O_i\le\frac{b(\ell_i)}{\alpha}, $$
and hence

Consequently,

The finite-game output satisfies
$$ \min_i\frac{\mathbb E_Qg(z,\ell_i)}{d_i} \ge q\alpha V^*-t. $$

Now take arbitrary $\theta$ in cell $i$. Section 4 gives
$$ b(\theta) \le O(\theta) \le A O_i+\frac{\kappa t}{2} \le \left(\frac A q+\frac t2\right)d_i. $$

Here the coefficient multiplying the final error is at most one.

$$ A+qt/2\le1+t. $$

$$ (\alpha^3+2\alpha^2)t\le3t. $$
Using $V^*<2$, we conclude
$$ V(Q) \ge \alpha^3V^*-7t \ge \alpha^3V^*-\epsilon. $$

7. A sufficient missing oracle for the requested factor

The following is a conditional result, not an assertion that its oracle exists.

Theorem 9

Suppose that, for the rational cells constructed in Section 4, an algorithm can compute rational numbers $\widehat\beta_i$ satisfying
$$ \beta_i\le\widehat\beta_i\le(1+t)\beta_i, \qquad \beta_i=\sup_{\theta\text{ in cell }i}b(\theta), $$
in time polynomial in the input length and $1/t$.

Then the requested guarantee
$$ V(Q)\ge\alpha V^*-\epsilon $$
is polynomial-time achievable for fixed $m$.

Proof

Again take $t=\epsilon/64$. Define the rational finite game
$$ \widehat W(Q) = \min_i \frac{\mathbb E_Qg(z,\ell_i)}{\widehat\beta_i}. $$
Its payoffs are bounded by $1/\alpha<2$, since
$$ \widehat\beta_i\ge\beta_i\ge b(\ell_i)\ge\alpha O(\ell_i). $$

For every $Q$, Proposition 6 and the oracle bounds give
$$ \frac{W(Q)}{1+t} \le \widehat W(Q) \le W(Q) \le V(Q). $$
Thus
$$ \widehat W^* \ge \frac{V^*-2t}{1+t}. $$

Applying Proposition 7,
$$ V(Q) \ge \widehat W(Q) \ge \frac{\alpha-t}{1+t}(V^*-2t)-t. $$
Since
$$ \frac{\alpha-t}{1+t}\ge\alpha-2t, \qquad \frac{\alpha-t}{1+t}\le1, \qquad V^*<2, $$
the right-hand side is at least
$$ \alpha V^*-7t \ge \alpha V^*-\epsilon. $$
$\square$

This isolates a specific sufficient computational step. Approximating $b$ at the cell corners is not the oracle hypothesis: the required numbers approximate its supremum throughout each cell.

For comparison, the finite-network result in [Wilder et al., Section 5](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2017/pdfs/p1305.pdf) obtains the requested greedy-normalized factor through parameter discretization. The argument here supplies a polynomial discretization for the limiting mass model, but leaves the cellwise greedy coefficients uncomputed.

As a fully resolved special case, if $P$ is a singleton, then
$$ V^*=\frac{O(\theta)}{b(\theta)}. $$
Proposition 3 directly produces a single rational profile with the requested guarantee, without evaluating $b$.

The unconditional theorem has factor $\alpha^3$, rather than the requested $\alpha$. It therefore does not settle the task.
