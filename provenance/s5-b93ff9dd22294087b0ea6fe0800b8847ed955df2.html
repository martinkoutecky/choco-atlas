The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given plus and minus classes \(k\) with rational population masses \(\rho_k\), fixed impacts \(a_k\), and explicitly encoded regular distributions \(H_k\) over private costs or values, treat \((k,z)\) as the realized agent type with population measure \(\rho_k\,dH_k(z)\). Choose direct allocation and payment rules \(x_k(\hat z)\) and \(t_k(\hat z)\), DSIC and IR for each realized type, to maximize per-capita utility \(\sum_k \rho_k a_k \int x_k(z)\,dH_k(z)\), subject to aggregate minus-agent payments covering plus-agent payments, \(\sum_{k\in T_-}\rho_k\int t_k(z)\,dH_k(z)\ge\sum_{k\in T_+}\rho_k\int t_k(z)\,dH_k(z)\). What is the optimal utility and can an optimal mechanism be computed from the encoded instance?

Proven result

Exact optimal utility and an optimal mechanism are computable in polynomial time. Under the stated regularity assumptions, an explicit algorithm uses \(O(m+\tau\log(2+\tau))\) arithmetic operations and, with elementary integer arithmetic, \(O((m+\tau\log(2+\tau))L^2)\) bit operations.

Consequently, deciding whether the optimum is at least \(q\) belongs to \(\mathrm P\). An NP-hardness result for this problem would imply \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\).

The proof treats the explicitly listed supported types directly. It does not require a reduction to the atomless setting.

1. Exact discrete payment formulas

Fix one class and temporarily suppress its index. Write its support as \(z_1<\cdots<z_r\), its probabilities as \(h_1,\ldots,h_r\), and let \(\Delta_\ell=z_{\ell+1}-z_\ell\). Empty sums below are zero.

Plus classes

Define truthful utility by \(u_\ell=t_\ell-z_\ell x_\ell\). For \(i<j\), the two DSIC inequalities give

\[ (z_j-z_i)x_j\le u_i-u_j\le(z_j-z_i)x_i. \]

Thus DSIC requires \(x_1\ge\cdots\ge x_r\). Using adjacent inequalities and IR at the highest cost gives

\[ u_\ell\ge\sum_{j=\ell}^{r-1}\Delta_jx_{j+1}. \]

Consequently, every DSIC, IR payment rule satisfies the pointwise lower bound

\[ t_\ell\ge t_\ell^+ :=z_\ell x_\ell+\sum_{j=\ell}^{r-1}\Delta_jx_{j+1}. \]

For every nonincreasing allocation, these lower bounds are simultaneously attainable. Indeed, the resulting utilities satisfy, for \(i<j\),

\[ u_i^+-u_j^+ =\sum_{\ell=i}^{j-1}\Delta_\ell x_{\ell+1}. \]

Every allocation appearing in this sum lies between \(x_j\) and \(x_i\), so both DSIC inequalities hold. All utilities and payments are nonnegative. Hence \(t^+\) is a DSIC, IR, nonnegative payment rule minimizing every plus payment.

Minus classes

Here truthful utility is \(u_\ell=z_\ell x_\ell-t_\ell\). For \(i<j\), DSIC gives

\[ (z_j-z_i)x_i\le u_j-u_i\le(z_j-z_i)x_j. \]

Thus DSIC requires \(x_1\le\cdots\le x_r\). Adjacent inequalities and IR at the lowest value imply

\[ u_\ell\ge\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\Delta_jx_j. \]

Every DSIC, IR payment rule therefore satisfies

\[ t_\ell\le t_\ell^- :=z_\ell x_\ell-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\Delta_jx_j. \]

For every nondecreasing allocation, these upper bounds are simultaneously attainable: the associated utility differences are

\[ u_j^--u_i^-=\sum_{\ell=i}^{j-1}\Delta_\ell x_\ell, \]

which lie between \((z_j-z_i)x_i\) and \((z_j-z_i)x_j\). This proves DSIC, and the utilities are nonnegative. Payments are also nonnegative, because

\[ t_\ell^-\ge z_\ell x_\ell-(z_\ell-z_1)x_\ell=z_1x_\ell\ge0. \]

Thus \(t^-\) maximizes every minus payment among DSIC, IR rules.

Replacing any feasible mechanism’s payments by these canonical payments preserves its allocation and utility objective, decreases payments to plus agents, and increases payments collected from minus agents. It therefore preserves budget feasibility.

2. Eliminating payments exactly

Let \(F_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^j h_\ell\). Taking expectations of the canonical payment formulas gives

\[ \sum_{\ell=1}^r h_\ell t_\ell^+ =\sum_{\ell=1}^r h_\ell z_\ell x_\ell +\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\Delta_jF_jx_{j+1} =\sum_{\ell=1}^r h_\ell\psi_\ell x_\ell, \]

and

\[ \sum_{\ell=1}^r h_\ell t_\ell^- =\sum_{\ell=1}^r h_\ell z_\ell x_\ell -\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\Delta_j(1-F_j)x_j =\sum_{\ell=1}^r h_\ell\phi_\ell x_\ell. \]

These are precisely the virtual quantities in the question.

Restore the class index and define utility coefficients \(w_{k,\ell}=\rho_kh_{k,\ell}a_k\). Define budget coefficients by

\[ c_{k,\ell}=\rho_kh_{k,\ell}\psi_{k,\ell} \quad\text{for }k\in T_+, \qquad c_{k,\ell}=-\rho_kh_{k,\ell}\phi_{k,\ell} \quad\text{for }k\in T_-. \]

The mechanism problem is therefore exactly the linear program maximizing \(\sum_{k,\ell}w_{k,\ell}x_{k,\ell}\), subject to:

  • \(0\le x_{k,\ell}\le1\);
  • \(x_{k,\ell}\ge x_{k,\ell+1}\) for plus classes;
  • \(x_{k,\ell}\le x_{k,\ell+1}\) for minus classes;
  • \(\sum_{k,\ell}c_{k,\ell}x_{k,\ell}\le0\).

Both directions of this equivalence have been proved: every feasible mechanism yields a feasible allocation for the linear program, and every feasible allocation yields a feasible mechanism through the canonical payments.

There are \(\tau\) variables and \(3\tau-m+1\) inequalities. The feasible region is nonempty, since the zero allocation is feasible, and compact, since all variables lie in \([0,1]\). Thus an optimum exists.

This formulation already proves polynomial-time solvability without regularity, by polynomial-time rational linear programming. See, for example, [MIT’s linear programming algorithm notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html). For regular inputs, the following direct algorithm gives the stated sharper bound.

3. A fractional-knapsack relaxation

Temporarily omit the within-class monotonicity inequalities.

For plus classes, \(\psi_{k,\ell}\ge0\) directly from its definition. If \(\psi_{k,\ell}=0\), set \(x_{k,\ell}=1\): this has zero budget cost and nonnegative utility gain.

For minus classes with \(\phi_{k,\ell}\le0\), set \(x_{k,\ell}=0\). Their budget coefficient is nonnegative and their utility coefficient is nonpositive, so changing their allocation to zero cannot worsen either feasibility or utility.

The remaining coordinates become fractional-knapsack items:

| Supported type | New variable | Positive weight | Nonnegative gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plus, \(\psi_{k,\ell}>0\) | \(y_{k,\ell}=x_{k,\ell}\) | \(b_{k,\ell}=c_{k,\ell}\) | \(g_{k,\ell}=w_{k,\ell}\) |
| Minus, \(\phi_{k,\ell}>0\) | \(y_{k,\ell}=1-x_{k,\ell}\) | \(b_{k,\ell}=-c_{k,\ell}\) | \(g_{k,\ell}=-w_{k,\ell}\) |

Define

\[ R=\sum_{\substack{k\in T_-\\\phi_{k,\ell}>0}} \rho_kh_{k,\ell}\phi_{k,\ell}. \]

The budget constraint becomes exactly \(\sum_i b_i y_i\le R\). The utility becomes \(U_0+\sum_i g_i y_i\), where \(U_0\) is the utility of the fixed zero-cost plus allocations together with all positive-virtual-value minus allocations.

Thus the relaxation is an ordinary fractional knapsack with at most \(\tau\) items.

Sort items by nonincreasing \(d_i=g_i/b_i\). Break equal-ratio ties lexicographically by class index and then support index. Fill items in that order until capacity is exhausted. At most one item is fractional.

For completeness, this greedy rule is optimal. If every item fits, selecting every item is optimal because all gains are nonnegative. Otherwise let \(j\) be the first item not selected fully and put \(\lambda=d_j\). The greedy vector \(y^*\) uses exactly capacity \(R\). For every feasible \(y\),

\[ \sum_i g_i(y_i-y_i^*) = \sum_i(g_i-\lambda b_i)(y_i-y_i^*) +\lambda\sum_i b_i(y_i-y_i^*) \le0. \]

The inequality holds term by term: before \(j\), the first coefficient is nonnegative and \(y_i-y_i^*\le0\); after \(j\), that coefficient is nonpositive and \(y_i-y_i^*\ge0\); at \(j\), it is zero. The final budget term is nonpositive.

4. Regularity makes the relaxation exact

It remains to check that the greedy allocation satisfies the omitted monotonicity constraints.

For a plus item,

\[ d_{k,\ell}=\frac{a_k}{\psi_{k,\ell}}. \]

Because \(a_k\ge0\) and regularity makes \(\psi_{k,\ell}\) nondecreasing, these ratios are nonincreasing along the positive-virtual-cost portion of the support. Equal ratios are ordered by increasing support index. Consequently, within each plus class the greedy allocations are nonincreasing.

Any zero virtual costs form a prefix, by regularity and nonnegativity. That prefix was allocated fully, so monotonicity also holds across its boundary.

For a minus item,

\[ d_{k,\ell}=\frac{-a_k}{\phi_{k,\ell}}. \]

On the positive-virtual-value portion of the support, these ratios are nonincreasing because \(-a_k\ge0\) and \(\phi_{k,\ell}\) is nondecreasing. The tie rule therefore makes \(y_{k,\ell}\) nonincreasing, so \(x_{k,\ell}=1-y_{k,\ell}\) is nondecreasing.

The nonpositive virtual values form a prefix, and their allocations were fixed at zero. Hence monotonicity holds across that boundary as well.

These arguments include zero impacts and equal virtual quantities.

The greedy solution is therefore feasible for the original allocation linear program. Since it optimizes a relaxation of that program, it also optimizes the original program. Applying the canonical payments produces an optimal DSIC, IR, budget-balanced mechanism with nonnegative payments.

In particular, there exists an optimal mechanism with at most one fractionally allocated supported type.

5. Exact arithmetic and running time

All steps can be performed exactly.

Computing prefix and suffix probabilities, coefficients, and canonical payments requires \(O(m+\tau)\) arithmetic operations. Sorting at most \(\tau\) items requires \(O(\tau\log(2+\tau))\) comparisons. Greedy filling requires \(O(\tau)\) further operations.

To control bit lengths, let \(D\) be the product of all denominators appearing in the input, including the denominator of \(q\). Its binary length is \(O(L)\), and every input rational has denominator dividing \(D\).

Budget coefficients need not be computed by dividing by probabilities. For example, for a plus class and \(\ell>1\),

\[ c_{k,\ell} =\rho_k\left( h_{k,\ell}z_{k,\ell} +(z_{k,\ell}-z_{k,\ell-1})\sum_{s<\ell}h_{k,s} \right). \]

For a minus class and \(\ell<r_k\),

\[ c_{k,\ell} =-\rho_k\left( h_{k,\ell}z_{k,\ell} -(z_{k,\ell+1}-z_{k,\ell})\sum_{s>\ell}h_{k,s} \right). \]

At the respective endpoints, only the first term occurs. Therefore every \(c_{k,\ell}\) and \(w_{k,\ell}\) has denominator dividing \(D^3\). Scaling all weights and gains by \(D^3\) gives integers with \(O(L)\) bits. Capacity and accumulated weights also have \(O(L)\) bits, since the support is explicit and \(\tau\le O(L)\).

Sorting ratios uses cross-products of these integers. Greedy filling uses integer subtraction, followed by at most one division to determine the fractional item. Thus every allocation has a common denominator \(E\) of \(O(L)\) bits: \(E=1\) if all allocations are integral, and otherwise \(E\) can be the scaled weight of the fractional item.

The canonical payments have denominator dividing \(DE\); the optimal utility has denominator dividing \(D^3E\). Their numerators also have \(O(L)\) bits. Payments are computed by prefix or suffix recurrences, avoiding repeated evaluation of the sums.

Hence the complete algorithm, including outputting all \(\tau\) allocation-payment pairs and comparing the exact optimum with \(q\), uses:

  • \(O(m+\tau\log(2+\tau))\) exact arithmetic operations and comparisons;
  • \(O((m+\tau\log(2+\tau))L^2)\) bit operations using elementary integer arithmetic;
  • \(O(\tau L)\) bits of storage and output.

The dependence is on the number of supported types. No Cartesian product of possible reports or numerical expansion of rational masses is used.
