Proposition 1 — class: other

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a unit-mass continuum of identical firms, a symmetric worth function \(v(a,\mathcal P)\), and a belief distribution \(h_a\) over mass partitions containing a coalition of mass \(a\), determine whether there is a measurable allocation \(x\) of grand-coalition worth \(W\) such that every measurable coalition \(A\) satisfies \(\int_A x(i)\,di\geq b(|A|)\), where \(b(a)=\mathbb E_{\mathcal P\sim h_a}[v(a,\mathcal P)]\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive mass, each mass a binary rational; \(\tau\) counts supported types, not possible types. Here \(\tau=1\), its mass is \(1\), and firms occupy \([0,1]\) with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\).
A mass partition \(\mathcal P\) is a finite multiset of positive real masses summing to \(1\). Worth \(v(a,\mathcal P)\in\mathbb R\) depends only on the distinguished mass \(a\) and this multiset. Merging two outside blocks strictly increases its worth. Write \(W=v(1,\{1\})\), and assume \(W>\sum_{c\in\mathcal P}v(c,\mathcal P)\) for every \(\mathcal P\ne\{1\}\). Also assume \(v(a,\mathcal P)/a>v(c,\mathcal P)/c\) whenever \(a<c\) are blocks of the same partition.
For each \(0<a\leq1\), \(h_a\) is a Borel probability distribution over mass partitions containing \(a\), with integrable worth; beliefs need not predict actual outside behavior. Set \(b(a)=\mathbb E_{h_a}[v(a,\mathcal P)]\), \(b(0)=0\), and \(b(1)=W\).
The finite input supplies \(W\in\mathbb Q\) and the induced function \(b\) directly: \(m\) polynomial pieces of degree at most \(d\), with dense binary-rational coefficients, rational breakpoints, and explicit breakpoint values. The primitive assumptions above are promises. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length; state running times in \(m,\tau,d,L\).
Core feasibility means existence of a real-valued \(x\in L^1([0,1],\lambda)\) satisfying \(\int_0^1x\,d\lambda=W\) and \(\int_Ax\,d\lambda\geq b(\lambda(A))\) for every Lebesgue-measurable coalition \(A\).

Is this continuous core-feasibility problem solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,d,L\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?

Discrete: under positive externalities and grand-coalition efficiency, a sufficient nonemptiness result uses expected-worth contraction by \(n/(n+1)\) as the firm count grows, the same bound for singleton-outsider worth, sufficiently pessimistic singleton beliefs at three firms, and nondecreasing grand worth; it gives no complexity classification. [Lekeas–Stamatopoulos](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01521v1).
Continuous: a sufficient nonemptiness theorem covers a special class of games on nonatomic measure spaces under measure-relative convexity; it does not classify this encoded decision problem. [Sagara–Vlach](https://www.ktiml.mff.cuni.cz/KTIML-70-version1-saga_vlach.pdf). No classification of this exact encoded problem was located.

  • Mass partitions have finitely many positive-mass blocks and are considered without block labels; their probability distributions use the Borel structure inherited from the finite-dimensional simplices.
  • Retained strict positive externalities and strict grand-coalition efficiency. Excluded the grand partition from the efficiency comparison, since including it would require worth to exceed itself.
  • Made decreasing per-member worth with block size an explicit assumption, because the discrete proof uses this property although the stated positive-externality definition alone does not imply it.
  • Worth and allocation densities may be signed, as in the discrete real-payoff model. Allocations must be Lebesgue integrable, expectations must exist, empty-coalition worth is zero, and grand-coalition expected worth equals W.
  • The function representation was unspecified. Chose an explicit piecewise-polynomial representation of the induced expected-worth function, with rational data and separately specified breakpoint values; primitive-game properties are promises rather than additional decision questions.
  • Beliefs depend only on coalition mass, as the graded question specifies, and need not be consistent with outsider behavior.
  • The unit-mass question supplies no family indexed by population size. Retained unrestricted beliefs at this fixed population rather than inventing a continuous counterpart of the discrete cross-population admissibility recursion; reported that recursion among the nearest discrete results.

Result

The problem is solvable in deterministic polynomial time for the stipulated case \(\tau=1\). Precisely, the core is nonempty if and only if

\[ b(a)\leq aW\qquad\text{for every }a\in[0,1]. \]

When this condition holds, the constant allocation \(x(s)=W\) is feasible.

1. Exact characterization of feasibility

We prove the characterization without using the additional promises on the primitive worth function.

Necessity. Suppose \(x\in L^1([0,1],\lambda)\) satisfies all core constraints and \(\int_0^1x(s)\,ds=W\). Choose a Borel representative of \(x\); replacing \(x\) on a null set does not change any coalition integral.

Fix \(a\in(0,1)\). Identify \([0,1)\) with the circle and, for each \(t\in[0,1)\), define the measurable coalition

\[ A_t=\{s\in[0,1): (s-t)\bmod 1\in[0,a)\}. \]

Each \(A_t\) has measure \(a\), so feasibility gives \(\int_{A_t}x(s)\,ds\geq b(a)\).

For each fixed \(s\), the set of translations \(t\) for which \(s\in A_t\) has measure \(a\). Consequently,

\[ \int_0^1\int_0^1 |x(s)|\mathbf 1_{A_t}(s)\,ds\,dt =a\int_0^1|x(s)|\,ds<\infty. \]

Thus Fubini’s theorem applies. Averaging the coalition inequalities over \(t\) yields

\[ b(a)\leq \int_0^1\int_{A_t}x(s)\,ds\,dt =\int_0^1 ax(s)\,ds =aW. \]

The endpoint conditions hold by the given identities \(b(0)=0\) and \(b(1)=W\).

Sufficiency. Suppose \(b(a)\leq aW\) for every \(a\in[0,1]\). Set \(x(s)=W\). This function is integrable, has total integral \(W\), and for every Lebesgue-measurable coalition \(A\),

\[ \int_Ax(s)\,ds=W\lambda(A)\geq b(\lambda(A)). \]

This proves both directions.

2. Exact decision algorithm

Let \(0=r_0<r_1<\cdots<r_m=1\) be the breakpoints, and let \(p_i\) describe \(b\) on \((r_{i-1},r_i)\).

First check each explicit breakpoint value by exact rational arithmetic:

\[ b(r_j)\leq Wr_j. \]

These checks are essential because the supplied value at a breakpoint can differ from either polynomial limit.

For every open piece, define \(q_i(a)=p_i(a)-Wa\). We must determine whether \(q_i(a)\leq0\) throughout its open interval.

Write \(\ell=r_{i-1}\), \(r=r_i\). Choose a positive integer \(C_i\) clearing the denominators after the affine substitution, and form the integer polynomial

\[ Q_i(t)=C_iq_i\bigl(\ell+(r-\ell)t\bigr). \]

Because \(C_i>0\) and \(r>\ell\), the required condition is equivalent to \(Q_i(t)\leq0\) for \(t\in(0,1)\). Polynomial continuity makes this equivalent to the same inequality on \([0,1]\): a positive endpoint value would imply positive values at nearby interior points.

Test the following:

  • \(Q_i(0)\leq0\) and \(Q_i(1)\leq0\).
  • If \(Q_i\) is nonconstant, \(Q_i(\alpha)\leq0\) at every real root \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) of \(Q_i'\).

These tests are necessary and sufficient. Indeed, a continuous polynomial attains its maximum on the compact interval \([0,1]\). An interior maximizer has derivative zero; an endpoint maximizer is covered by the first test. Constant polynomials are handled directly.

All roots and signs are computed exactly using integer-polynomial real-root isolation and sign determination at real algebraic numbers. Repeated roots and equality to zero are included in these procedures.

The algorithm accepts precisely when every breakpoint and every piece passes. Section 1 then supplies the feasible allocation \(x=W\).

3. Polynomial bit complexity

Put \(D=\max\{1,d\}\). Each \(q_i\) has degree at most \(D\).

Clearing its rational coefficient denominators produces an integer polynomial with coefficient bit length \(O(L+1)\). To check the substitution bound explicitly, write \(\ell=A/H\) and \(r=R/H\), using a common positive denominator. Here \(A,R,H\) have \(O(L+1)\) bits. If \(F\) is the cleared polynomial, then

\[ Q_i(t)=H^D F\bigl((A+(R-A)t)/H\bigr) \]

has integer coefficients. Expanding its powers shows that their bit lengths are bounded by \(B=O(D(L+1))\): each term contains at most \(D\) factors of \(O(L+1)\) bits, a binomial coefficient of \(O(D)\) bits, and at most \(D+1\) terms are added. The derivative coefficients satisfy the same bound. We may choose the bound \(B\) large enough that \(B\geq D\).

For integer polynomials of degree bounded by \(D\) and coefficient bit length bounded by \(B\), exact real-root isolation, including multiplicities, takes \(\widetilde O(D^4B^2)\) bit operations. The isolating endpoints have \(O(DB)\) bits, and exact sign evaluation at an isolated real root takes \(\widetilde O(D^3B)\). These are the bounds in Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 6.1 of [Emiris–Mourrain–Tsigaridas](https://who.paris.inria.fr/Elias.Tsigaridas/files/emt-lncs-2006.pdf); choosing \(B\geq D\) satisfies the degree-versus-bit-length qualification noted there.

Apply these procedures to \(Q_i'\), using its square-free part to represent distinct roots. At each of its at most \(D-1\) distinct real roots, determine the signs of \(t\), \(1-t\), and \(Q_i(t)\). These signs identify interior roots and test their values exactly. The total cost per piece is therefore \(\widetilde O(D^4B^2)\). Rational endpoint checks and polynomial construction fit within the resulting bound.

A conservative overall running time is

\[ \widetilde O\bigl(L+(m+1)(d+1)^6(L+1)^2\bigr) \]

bit operations, where the tilde suppresses logarithmic factors.

Thus the running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,d,L\), with \(\tau=1\) fixed. No enumeration of mass partitions, construction of beliefs, or verification of the primitive promises is required: the input directly supplies the induced function \(b\).
