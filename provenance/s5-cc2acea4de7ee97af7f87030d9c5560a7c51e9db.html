For the upper bounds, assume that valuation evaluation has a uniform polynomial running-time bound in the valuation-description length and the endpoint encoding length. A separate polynomial bound for each program, with arbitrarily varying degree, does not by itself imply this convention. Without uniformity, the evaluation-dependent running times below must instead include the supplied bounds.

There is also an encoding distinction. The requested complexity is polynomial in the *value* of $q$, although $q$ is encoded in binary. The explicit output contains $q^2$ lengths. Thus, for general $q$, ordinary $\mathrm{PPAD}$ membership applies to the expanded encoding in which $q$ is written in unary, or to instances where $q$ is polynomially bounded in the input length. 2. Fixed witnesses: flow feasibility and short masses

Fix an allowed list of $q$ witnesses. Define
$$ A_t=\left\{j:\text{piece }j\text{ maximizes type }t\text{’s valuation at some witness}\right\}. $$

Construct a directed network with a source, one vertex for each type, one vertex for each piece position, and a sink. Its capacities are:

  • source to type $t$: $\mu_t$;
  • type $t$ to position $j\in A_t$: $1$;
  • position $j$ to sink: $1/q$.

Claim. The witnesses admit feasible masses exactly when this network admits a flow of value $1$.

Proof. Given feasible masses, set
$$ y_{t,j}=\sum_r x_{t,r,j}. $$
The conservation equations make these numbers a flow of value $1$. Every positive $y_{t,j}$ uses an eligible edge.

Conversely, a flow of value $1$ saturates all source-to-type and position-to-sink edges, since each family has total capacity $1$. For each eligible pair $(t,j)$, choose a witness $r(t,j)$ at which $j$ maximizes type $t$’s valuation. Assign
$$ x_{t,r(t,j),j}=y_{t,j}, $$
and set the other entries to zero. This satisfies both mass-conservation equations and the preference requirement. Pooling across witnesses makes this construction valid. $\square$

Write each input mass as $\mu_t=a_t/b_t$, and define
$$ D=\prod_t b_t. $$
The bit length of $D$ is at most the sum of the input denominator bit lengths, up to a constant. Scaling all capacities by $qD$ gives integer capacities. An integral maximum flow therefore yields masses with denominators dividing $qD$.

Thus every feasible witness list admits masses of polynomial encoding length, and such masses can be constructed in polynomial time under the uniform evaluation convention. This is an existence guarantee; arbitrary valid mass assignments can have unnecessarily large denominators.

$$ \Delta=\left\{X\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{q}:\sum_jX_j=1\right\}. $$

Use cumulative coordinates
$$ z_i=N\sum_{j=1}^{i}X_j,\qquad 1\leq i\leq q-1. $$
They identify $\Delta$ affinely with
$$ C=\left\{z:0\leq z_1\leq\cdots\leq z_{q-1}\leq N\right\}. $$

Triangulate every integer unit cube in $[0,N]^{q-1}$ by staircase simplices. For a cube base $h$ and a permutation $\pi$, their vertices are
$$ h,\quad h+e_{\pi(1)},\quad\ldots,\quad h+\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}e_{\pi(k)}. $$
These triangulations agree on shared cube faces.

Each hyperplane $z_i=z_{i+1}$ is a union of simplex faces. Indeed, if $h_i=h_{i+1}$, the increment order fixes the sign of $z_i-z_{i+1}$ throughout a staircase simplex. If the base coordinates differ, equality can occur only on a cube boundary, or cannot occur. Consequently, restricting the cube triangulation to $C$ gives a triangulation of $C$.

Transform back by setting $z_0=0$, $z_q=N$, and
$$ X_j=\frac{z_j-z_{j-1}}{N}. $$
Every resulting vertex is an allowed grid vector.

For an internal length coordinate, its change along a staircase chain is the difference of two increment indicators. Depending on their order, this difference takes values only in $\{0,1\}$ or only in $\{-1,0\}$. The first and last length coordinates involve a single indicator. Hence every length coordinate has range at most $1/N$ within a simplex. In particular, its vertices satisfy
$$ \|v-w\|_\infty\leq\frac1N. $$

Denote this triangulation of $\Delta$ by $\mathcal T_N$.

3.2. Demand vectors and their interpolation

At every grid vertex $v$, choose one maximizing piece $c_t(v)$ for each type, using a fixed deterministic tie-breaking rule. Define
$$ d_j(v)=\sum_{t:c_t(v)=j}\mu_t. $$
Then $d(v)\in\Delta$.

A zero-length piece has value zero, whereas some positive-length piece has strictly positive value for every type. Therefore
$$ v_j=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_j(v)=0. $$
More quantitatively,
$$ 0\leq d_j(v)\leq Nv_j. $$
For $v_j=0$ this follows from the preceding implication; otherwise $v_j\geq1/N$ and $d_j(v)\leq1$.

Extend $d$ affinely over every simplex of $\mathcal T_N$, obtaining a continuous map
$$ F:\Delta\longrightarrow\Delta. $$
The extensions agree on shared faces because they use the same vertex values. Affine interpolation preserves the preceding inequality:
$$ 0\leq F_j(X)\leq NX_j \qquad\text{for every }X\in\Delta. $$
In particular, $F$ preserves every face.

3.3. Efficient evaluation and a Lipschitz bound

A containing simplex can be located without enumerating the triangulation. Given rational $z\in C$, set
$$ h_i=\min\{\lfloor z_i\rfloor,N-1\}, \qquad \theta_i=z_i-h_i. $$
Order the coordinates by nonincreasing $\theta_i$, breaking ties by decreasing index. The corresponding staircase simplex contains $z$. Its vertices lie in $C$: when adjacent base coordinates agree, monotonicity of $z$ and the tie-breaking rule increment the larger index first.

Writing $d=q-1$ for this paragraph, its barycentric coefficients are
$$ \lambda_0=1-\theta_{\pi(1)},\qquad \lambda_k=\theta_{\pi(k)}-\theta_{\pi(k+1)}\quad(1\leq k<d), \qquad \lambda_d=\theta_{\pi(d)}. $$
They are nonnegative, sum to $1$, and reproduce $z$.

Thus evaluating $F$ requires only $q$ grid demand vectors and rational arithmetic. Each demand vector requires $q\tau$ valuation evaluations at grid endpoints. This is polynomial in the requested parameters and the encoding length of the evaluation point.

Within one staircase simplex,
$$ F(z)=d(v^{(0)})+ \sum_{k=1}^{q-1}\theta_{\pi(k)} \left(d(v^{(k)})-d(v^{(k-1)})\right). $$
Each demand-vector difference has infinity norm at most $1$, so
$$ \|F(z)-F(z')\|_\infty \leq(q-1)\|z-z'\|_\infty $$
within that simplex. Partitioning a line segment at its finitely many simplex crossings proves the same bound throughout the convex domain $C$.

Since
$$ \|z(X)-z(Y)\|_\infty \leq N(q-1)\|X-Y\|_\infty, $$
the map in length coordinates satisfies
$$ \|F(X)-F(Y)\|_\infty\leq A\|X-Y\|_\infty, \qquad A=N(q-1)^2. $$

4. Existence and approximate inversion

Let
$$ c=(1/q,\ldots,1/q). $$
For $X\in\Delta$, define
$$ a_j(X)=X_j+c_j-F_j(X),\qquad w_j(X)=\max\{a_j(X),0\}, $$
and
$$ S(X)=\sum_jw_j(X),\qquad H_j(X)=\frac{w_j(X)}{S(X)}. $$

Because $\sum_j a_j(X)=1$ and $w_j(X)\leq X_j+c_j$,
$$ 1\leq S(X)\leq2. $$
Thus $H$ is a continuous self-map of $\Delta$.

4.1. Every promised instance has a solution

Brouwer’s theorem applies because $\Delta$ is nonempty, compact and convex. Let $X^\ast=H(X^\ast)$.

If $X_j^\ast=0$, face preservation gives $F_j(X^\ast)=0$. Hence
$$ a_j(X^\ast)=1/q>0, $$
contradicting $H_j(X^\ast)=X_j^\ast=0$. All coordinates of $X^\ast$, and therefore all $a_j(X^\ast)$, are positive. Consequently $S(X^\ast)=1$ and
$$ F(X^\ast)=c. $$

Choose a containing simplex with vertices $v^{(1)},\ldots,v^{(p)}$, where $p\leq q$. Then
$$ c\in\operatorname{conv}\{d(v^{(1)}),\ldots,d(v^{(p)})\}. $$
The equations
$$ \lambda_r\geq0,\qquad \sum_r\lambda_r=1,\qquad \sum_r\lambda_r d(v^{(r)})=c $$
define a nonempty compact rational polytope. An extreme point is determined by independent rational equations, so rational coefficients $\lambda_r$ exist.

Set
$$ x_{t,r,j}=\mu_t\lambda_r\,\mathbf1_{\{c_t(v^{(r)})=j\}}. $$
Then
$$ \sum_{r,j}x_{t,r,j}=\mu_t, \qquad \sum_{t,r}x_{t,r,j} =\sum_r\lambda_r d_j(v^{(r)})=\frac1q. $$
Every positive mass uses a selected maximizer. The simplex vertices satisfy the required proximity bound. Pad with repeated witnesses carrying zero mass if $p<q$. This proves existence.

4.2. A small fixed-point residual implies a small demand residual

Lemma. If $X\in\Delta$ satisfies
$$ \|H(X)-X\|_\infty\leq\rho, \qquad \rho\leq\frac1{8qN}, $$
then
$$ \|F(X)-c\|_\infty\leq\rho. $$

Proof. Suppose $X_j\leq1/(4qN)$. Using $F_j(X)\leq NX_j$ gives
$$ a_j(X)\geq\frac1q-NX_j\geq\frac3{4q}. $$
Since $S(X)\leq2$,
$$ H_j(X)\geq\frac3{8q}. $$
As $N\geq2$, we also have $X_j\leq1/(8q)$. Therefore
$$ H_j(X)-X_j\geq\frac1{4q}>\rho, $$
a contradiction. Hence every $X_j>1/(4qN)$.

It follows that
$$ H_j(X)\geq X_j-\rho>\frac1{8qN}>0. $$
Thus all $a_j(X)>0$, so $S(X)=\sum_j a_j(X)=1$. Consequently
$$ H(X)-X=c-F(X), $$
which proves the assertion. $\square$

Finally, $H$ has an explicitly bounded Lipschitz constant. The maps $a_j$ and $w_j$ have constant at most $1+A$, and
$$ |S(X)-S(Y)|\leq q(1+A)\|X-Y\|_\infty. $$
Using $S\geq1$ and $w_j/S\leq1$ yields
$$ \|H(X)-H(Y)\|_\infty \leq(q+1)(1+A)\|X-Y\|_\infty. $$
The bit length of this constant is polynomial in $q$ and $\log N$.

5. A rational separation bound

Every coordinate of every grid demand vector has denominator dividing $D$. Put
$$ M=qD,\qquad B=(q+1)!\,M^{q+1}. $$

Lemma. For any $p\leq q$ grid demand vectors $d^{(1)},\ldots,d^{(p)}$, either
$$ c\in\operatorname{conv}\{d^{(1)},\ldots,d^{(p)}\}, $$
or every point $y$ in that convex hull satisfies
$$ \|y-c\|_\infty\geq\frac1B. $$

Proof. Consider the linear program minimizing $s$, subject to
$$ \lambda_r\geq0,\qquad \sum_r\lambda_r=1,\qquad 0\leq s\leq1, $$
and, for every $j$,
$$ -s\leq\sum_r\lambda_r d_j^{(r)}-\frac1q\leq s. $$
Its feasible set is nonempty: any convex combination is feasible with $s=1$. It is compact, so an optimum exists. The optimum is zero exactly when the convex hull contains $c$.

Suppose the optimum is positive. Choose an optimal vertex. There are $n=p+1\leq q+1$ variables, so $n$ independent active constraints determine this vertex.

Multiply each residual constraint by $M$. All constraint coefficients and right-hand sides are integers of absolute value at most $M$: indeed, $Md_j^{(r)}$ and $M/q=D$ are integers. The remaining constraints already have integer coefficients bounded by $M$.

Let $Q$ be the nonsingular matrix of the chosen active constraints. The determinant expansion gives
$$ |\det Q|\leq n!M^n\leq B. $$
By Cramer’s rule, the positive coordinate $s$ is an integer divided by $\det Q$. Hence
$$ s\geq\frac1{|\det Q|}\geq\frac1B. $$
Since the optimum is the minimum infinity-norm distance from the convex hull to $c$, the lemma follows. $\square$

The crucial encoding estimate is
$$ \log B=O\!\left(q\log q+q\log D\right), $$
which is polynomial in $q$ and $L$.

6. The $\mathrm{PPAD}$ upper bound

Use the following standard theorem: for a uniformly polynomially computable family of continuous self-maps of rational compact convex polytopes, with a polynomial modulus of continuity, finding a rational point $X$ satisfying a prescribed binary-rational residual bound
$$ \|H(X)-X\|_\infty\leq\rho $$
belongs to $\mathrm{PPAD}$. A Lipschitz constant with polynomial encoding length supplies such a modulus. This is Proposition 2, part 2, of [Etessami–Yannakakis, *On the Complexity of Nash Equilibria and Other Fixed Points*](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/kousha/nash_focs07_full_j_spec_issue_sub.pdf).

The hypotheses hold for the constructed $H$:

  • its domain $\Delta$ has a rational inequality description of size polynomial in $q$;
  • it is a continuous self-map;
  • rational evaluation is uniformly polynomial-time;
  • its Lipschitz constant has polynomial encoding length.

Choose
$$ \rho=\min\left\{\frac1{8qN},\frac1{2B}\right\}. $$
This precision has polynomial encoding length. Apply the theorem to obtain rational $X\in\Delta$ with residual at most $\rho$. Section 4.2 gives
$$ \|F(X)-c\|_\infty\leq\rho<\frac1B. $$

Locate a containing coarse simplex of $\mathcal T_N$, with vertices $v^{(1)},\ldots,v^{(p)}$. By interpolation,
$$ F(X)\in\operatorname{conv}\{d(v^{(1)}),\ldots,d(v^{(p)})\}. $$
The separation lemma therefore forces
$$ c\in\operatorname{conv}\{d(v^{(1)}),\ldots,d(v^{(p)})\}. $$

Solve the rational feasibility system for $\lambda$ from Section 4.1 by polynomial-time linear programming, and construct the masses displayed there. These are exact masses, with no relaxation of the preference or capacity constraints. The witnesses are coarse grid vertices, so their pairwise distance is at most $1/N$.

This proves the claimed reduction to $\mathrm{PPAD}$. Approximate fixed points serve only as an intermediate computational device.

7. $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hardness with one type

For completeness, the hardness transfer is proved here.

7.1. Nearby witnesses control utility differences

Let $X,Y\in\Delta$ satisfy $\|X-Y\|_\infty\leq h$. Since the total length difference is zero, summing from either end gives
$$ |s_i(X)-s_i(Y)|\leq\min\{i,q-i\}h. $$
For piece $j$, the sum of its endpoint displacements is therefore at most
$$ \left(\min\{j-1,q-j+1\}+\min\{j,q-j\}\right)h \leq(q-1)h. $$
Thus the valuation promise implies
$$ |u_t(I_j(X))-u_t(I_j(Y))|\leq K(q-1)h. $$

Take any valid mass solution and use $X^{(1)}$ as reference. If $x_{t,r,j}>0$, then $j$ maximizes at $X^{(r)}$. Applying the preceding estimate twice gives, for every $k$,
$$ u_t(I_k(X^{(1)}))-u_t(I_j(X^{(1)})) \leq\frac{2K(q-1)}N. $$

When $\tau=1$, each position receives positive mass $1/q$, so each position has a maximizing witness. Consequently,
$$ \max_j u_1(I_j(X^{(1)})) -\min_j u_1(I_j(X^{(1)})) \leq\frac{2K(q-1)}N. $$
This holds for every valid solution.

7.2. Reduction from identical-agent approximate envy-freeness

Theorem 8.3 of Hollender–Rubinstein establishes $\mathrm{PPAD}$-completeness of finding a connected $\delta$-envy-free allocation for four identical nonmonotone valuations. Their hard family uses nonnegative valuations vanishing on degenerate intervals. The valuations can be normalized to be $1$-Lipschitz; rational grid values and affine interpolation provide exact rational evaluation, and the construction is polynomial-time. These properties are supplied by Sections 3, 8.2 and 8.4 of [*Envy-Free Cake-Cutting for Four Agents*](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.02075v2).

Take a hard-family valuation $v$ and tolerance $0<\delta\leq1$. Choose a positive dyadic rational $\eta\leq\delta/2$ of polynomial encoding length, and define
$$ u(a,b)=v(a,b)+\eta(b-a). $$
Then
$$ u(a,a)=0,\qquad u(a,b)>0\quad(a<b), $$
because $v$ is nonnegative. Moreover,
$$ |u(a,b)-u(a',b')| \leq(1+\eta)(|a-a'|+|b-b'|) \leq2(|a-a'|+|b-b'|). $$
Exact rational evaluation and uniform polynomial running time are preserved.

Construct the target instance with
$$ q=4,\qquad\tau=1,\qquad\mu_1=1,\qquad K=2, \qquad N=\left\lceil\frac{24}{\delta}\right\rceil. $$
All target promises hold, and the instance has polynomial encoding length.

From any target solution, Section 7.1 yields, at its first witness,
$$ u(I_k)-u(I_j)\leq\frac{12}{N} $$
for all $j,k$. If $\ell_j$ are the reference lengths, then
$$ v(I_k)-v(I_j) =u(I_k)-u(I_j)+\eta(\ell_j-\ell_k) \leq\frac{12}{N}+\eta \leq\delta. $$
Assigning the four reference intervals to the four identical agents in any order therefore gives a connected $\delta$-envy-free allocation.

Both construction and extraction are polynomial-time, and extraction works for every target solution. Hence the target problem is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-hard under the stated fixed parameters. Together with Section 6, this restriction is $\mathrm{PPAD}$-complete.

8. What remains unresolved

In the uniform model, a polynomial-time algorithm in the requested parameters exists if and only if every $\mathrm{PPAD}$ search problem admits a polynomial-time algorithm. The forward implication follows from Section 7; the reverse implication follows from Section 6, using the expanded encoding for general $q$.

This equivalence does not establish $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness.

There is a specific obstruction to one natural satisfiability-to-search hardness notion. Suppose a polynomial-time reduction maps each Boolean formula to a promised instance with polynomially bounded $q$, and a polynomial-time decoder correctly determines satisfiability from every valid solution. For an unsatisfiable formula, existence and the short-mass construction give a polynomial-length certificate consisting of a valid solution whose decoder returns “unsatisfiable.” Verification checks the witness grid, proximity, masses and exact preferences. For a satisfiable formula, the decoder condition excludes every such certificate. Thus unsatisfiability would belong to $\mathrm{NP}$, implying
$$ \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}. $$

This conditional argument concerns that explicit reduction notion. It does not rule out every possible meaning of $\mathrm{NP}$-hardness for search problems.

The proved advance is therefore an exact $\mathrm{PPAD}$ upper bound, complementing the hardness result; the requested dichotomy remains unsettled.
