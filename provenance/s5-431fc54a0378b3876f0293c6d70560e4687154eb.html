The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given supported types \(a\in T\) with rational masses \(\mu_a\), \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\), integer endowments \(e_a\), acceptable supplier-type sets \(A_a\), quotas \(q_{ab}\), and a priority order on types, choose rational flows \(x_{ab}\ge0\) and retained masses \(r_a\ge0\) satisfying \(r_a+\sum_b x_{ab}=\mu_a e_a=r_a+\sum_c x_{ca}\), \(x_{ab}=0\) when \(b\notin A_a\), and \(x_{ab}\le\mu_a q_{ab}\) when \(b\in A_a\). Maximize \(\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}\), then lexicographically maximize each type’s received exchange mass in priority order, requiring that the aggregate flows admit a distribution over members’ integral, balanced bundles.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is atomless and given by its support: the list \(T\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_a\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Each type supplies one distinct service, so \(m=\tau\).
Each type \(a\) has a positive integer endowment \(e_a\), an acceptable supplier-type set \(A_a\subseteq T\), and nonnegative integer quotas \(q_{ab}\) for \(b\in A_a\); integers are binary encoded. A strict total priority order \(a_1,\ldots,a_\tau\) is given. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Let \(x_{ab}\) denote the mass of service received by type \(a\) from type \(b\), and \(r_a\) the retained endowment mass. Exchanges involve distinct members; exchanges between members of the same type are allowed when \(a\in A_a\), and are distinct from retention.
Choose nonnegative rational \(x_{ab},r_a\) satisfying \(r_a+\sum_bx_{ab}=\mu_ae_a=r_a+\sum_bx_{ba}\), with \(x_{ab}=0\) for \(b\notin A_a\) and \(x_{ab}\leq\mu_aq_{ab}\) otherwise.
Individual implementability means that, for every \(a\), there exists a finite rational probability distribution \(p_a\) over integral records \((s,u,v)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\times\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{T}\times\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{T}\). Here \(s\) is retained units, \(u_b\) units received from type \(b\), and \(v_b\) units supplied to type \(b\).
Every supported record satisfies \(s+\sum_bu_b=s+\sum_bv_b=e_a\), \(u_b=0\) for \(b\notin A_a\), and \(u_b\leq q_{ab}\) otherwise; its expectations satisfy \(\mu_a\mathbb E_{p_a}s=r_a\), \(\mu_a\mathbb E_{p_a}u_b=x_{ab}\), and \(\mu_a\mathbb E_{p_a}v_b=x_{ba}\).
Among implementable flows, first maximize \(S=\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}\), then lexicographically maximize \((h_{a_1},\ldots,h_{a_\tau})\), where \(h_a=\sum_bx_{ab}\). Any remaining tie may be resolved arbitrarily.

Is computing such an optimal aggregate allocation polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents with integral endowments and supplier quotas, maximizing exchanges and then applying agent priorities is polynomial-time computable by capacitated maximum-weight matching ([Andersson et al.](https://hpi.de/friedrich/docs/publications/2021/AEJM.pdf)).
For finite agents with rational endowments and quotas, polynomial-time computation is also established, but without the requirement of integral bundles for individual members of a mass society ([rational-data extension](https://hpi.de/friedrich/docs/publications/2021/AEJM.pdf)). No result settling the precise mass problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified an atomless society and interpreted implementability as finite rational distributions of joint incoming, outgoing, and retained integral records, with balance and endowment constraints holding for every record.
  • Specified positive integer endowments and nonnegative integer quotas, following the discrete domains, with binary encoding.
  • Interpreted each quota as a member's total incoming quota from a supplier type, consistent with the graded aggregate bound.
  • Distinguished self-retention from exchanges with other members of the same type; allowed the latter exactly when that supplier type is acceptable.
  • Defined m as the number of distinct supplier-type services, equal to tau in this formulation.
  • Specified a strict total priority order, aggregate received exchange mass as the priority objective, and arbitrary resolution of remaining ties.
  • Specified that the computational output is the optimal aggregate allocation; the bundle distributions are existential feasibility requirements.

Result

Computing the requested optimal aggregate allocation is polynomial-time solvable in \(m,\tau,L\).

The key fact is that every rational flow satisfying the stated aggregate constraints is individually implementable. We prove this first, then handle the optimization and its bit complexity.

1. The polytope of records for one type

Fix a type \(a\). Write \(e=e_a\), and define \(Q_b=q_{ab}\) for \(b\in A_a\), and \(Q_b=0\) otherwise.

Consider the polytope

\(P_a=\{(s,u,v)\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{1+2\tau}:s+\sum_bu_b=e,\ s+\sum_bv_b=e,\ u_b\leq Q_b\text{ for every }b\}.\)

Its integral points are exactly the allowed individual records for type \(a\).

The polytope is nonempty because \((e,0,0)\) belongs to it. It is bounded because nonnegativity and the two equations imply that every coordinate lies between \(0\) and \(e\).

Lemma 1. Every vertex of \(P_a\) is integral.

Proof. Fix a vertex \(z=(s,u,v)\).

First, at most one coordinate \(u_b\) satisfies \(0<u_b<Q_b\). Otherwise, for two such coordinates \(u_i,u_j\), increasing one by a sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\) and decreasing the other by \(\varepsilon\) gives a feasible perturbation in both directions. The point \(z\) would be the midpoint of two distinct feasible points, contradicting that it is a vertex.

Likewise, at most one coordinate \(v_b\) is positive. If \(v_i,v_j>0\) for distinct indices, opposite small perturbations of these coordinates preserve feasibility and give the same contradiction.

We next prove that \(s\) is integral. Suppose otherwise. Since \(e\) is integral, \(e-s\) is nonintegral. Consequently, the equation

\(\sum_bu_b=e-s\)

implies that some \(u_i\) lies strictly between \(0\) and its integral upper bound \(Q_i\). Also, \(s>0\), and \(\sum_bv_b=e-s>0\), so some \(v_j>0\).

For a sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both perturbations

\((s,u_i,v_j)\longmapsto(s+\varepsilon,u_i-\varepsilon,v_j-\varepsilon)\)

and

\((s,u_i,v_j)\longmapsto(s-\varepsilon,u_i+\varepsilon,v_j+\varepsilon)\)

are feasible, with all other coordinates unchanged. Both defining equations are preserved. Again, \(z\) is the midpoint of two distinct feasible points, a contradiction. Thus \(s\) is integral.

All but at most one coordinate of \(u\) are at their integral bounds. Any remaining coordinate equals \(e-s\) minus the sum of those bound coordinates, so it too is integral. Finally, the at most one positive coordinate of \(v\) equals \(e-s\), which is integral. Therefore every coordinate of \(z\) is integral. \(\square\)

2. Rational points admit rational distributions over records

Lemma 2. Every rational point of \(P_a\) is the expectation of a finite rational probability distribution over allowed integral records.

Proof. A nonempty bounded polyhedron is the convex hull of its finitely many vertices. The hypotheses hold for \(P_a\), and Lemma 1 shows that its vertices are allowed integral records.

It remains to ensure that the convex coefficients can be rational. Let \(z\in P_a\) be rational, and choose a representation

\(z=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_iw_i,\qquad \lambda_i>0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_i=1,\)

using vertices \(w_i\), with \(k\) as small as possible.

The \(w_i\) are affinely independent. Otherwise, there would be real numbers \(\delta_i\), not all zero, satisfying

\(\sum_i\delta_iw_i=0,\qquad \sum_i\delta_i=0.\)

There is an index with \(\delta_i>0\). Taking \(t=\min_{\delta_i>0}\lambda_i/\delta_i\), the coefficients \(\lambda_i-t\delta_i\) remain nonnegative, preserve the representation, and include at least one zero. This contradicts minimality.

Affine independence means that the equations specifying the coefficients have a unique solution. Their coefficient matrix is integral and their right-hand side is rational, so Gaussian elimination gives rational coefficients. These coefficients define the required probability distribution. \(\square\)

3. Aggregate feasibility equals individual implementability

Let \(F\) be the aggregate polytope defined by

\(x_{ab}\geq0,\qquad r_a\geq0,\)

\(r_a+\sum_bx_{ab}=\mu_ae_a,\qquad r_a+\sum_bx_{ba}=\mu_ae_a,\)

together with \(x_{ab}=0\) when \(b\notin A_a\), and \(x_{ab}\leq\mu_aq_{ab}\) otherwise.

Proposition. A rational aggregate allocation is individually implementable if and only if it belongs to \(F\).

Proof. Necessity follows by taking expectations of the individual record equations and inequalities and multiplying them by \(\mu_a\).

For sufficiency, take a rational point \((x,r)\in F\). For each \(a\), define

\(\bar s_a=r_a/\mu_a,\qquad \bar u_{ab}=x_{ab}/\mu_a,\qquad \bar v_{ab}=x_{ba}/\mu_a.\)

Every \(\mu_a\) is positive, so these expressions are well-defined and rational. Dividing the aggregate equations and quota constraints by \(\mu_a\) shows that

\((\bar s_a,\bar u_a,\bar v_a)\in P_a.\)

Lemma 2 supplies a finite rational distribution \(p_a\) over allowed integral records having precisely these expectations. Multiplying the expectations by \(\mu_a\) gives all three identities required in the task. Doing this for every type proves implementability. \(\square\)

This argument includes same-type exchanges: \(u_a\) and \(v_a\) remain exchange coordinates, separate from retention \(s\). We use exactly the implementability definition in the question; it imposes no additional constraint equating these coordinates within an individual record.

4. Computing the priority optimum

The polytope \(F\) has at most \(\tau^2+\tau\) variables and \(O(\tau^2)\) constraints. It is nonempty: take \(x=0\) and \(r_a=\mu_ae_a\). It is bounded because

\(0\leq r_a\leq\mu_ae_a,\qquad 0\leq x_{ab}\leq\mu_ae_a.\)

Use the standard theorem that a rational linear program can be solved exactly in time polynomial in its encoding length, including returning a rational optimizer when the feasible set is nonempty and bounded. Polynomial-time algorithms and exact recovery are discussed in the [MIT linear-programming notes](https://courses.csail.mit.edu/6.854/21/Notes/n14-LP_algorithms.html).

Apply this theorem sequentially:

  • Maximize \(S=\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}\) over \(F\), obtaining \(S^\star\).
  • Add \(S=S^\star\).
  • For \(k=1,\ldots,\tau\), maximize \(h_{a_k}=\sum_bx_{a_kb}\) subject to the original constraints and all previously fixed objective values. Fix the resulting value before proceeding.

Each stage restricts a nonempty compact polytope to its maximizing face, so every subsequent stage is feasible and attains its maximum.

The optimizer returned at the last stage maximizes \(S\), and then maximizes each priority coordinate subject to the optimal values of all preceding coordinates. This is precisely the requested lexicographic objective. By the proposition, its rational aggregate flow is individually implementable.

5. Bit complexity

Sequential optimization requires checking that the fixed objective values have polynomial encoding length.

The original constraints have coefficient entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). Their right-hand sides are \(0\), \(\mu_ae_a\), or \(\mu_aq_{ab}\), each with polynomial bit length.

More explicitly, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of the input masses. Its bit length is \(O(L)\), and multiplying every original right-hand side by \(D\) gives integers of bit length \(O(L)\).

Let \(n\leq\tau^2+\tau\) be the number of variables. At any vertex of \(F\), choose \(n\) linearly independent tight constraints. They give a system

\(Bz=c/D,\)

where \(B\) is an invertible integer matrix with entries in \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and \(c\) is an integer vector of polynomial bit length. Cramer’s rule gives

\(z_j=\det(B_j)/(D\det(B)),\)

where \(B_j\) replaces column \(j\) of \(B\) by \(c\). Hadamard’s inequality bounds the bit lengths of these determinants by a polynomial in \(n,L\). Thus every original vertex has polynomial coordinate encoding length.

Every successive maximizing set is a face of \(F\). A linear objective on a nonempty face attains its optimum at a vertex of that face, and every such vertex is a vertex of \(F\). Therefore each fixed value \(S^\star\) or \(h_{a_k}^\star\) is a sum of coordinates of an original vertex and has polynomial bit length. Adding the at most \(\tau\) previously fixed objective equations consequently preserves polynomial encoding length at every stage.

There are \(\tau+1\) linear programs, each of polynomial size. Their total running time is polynomial in \(\tau,L\), and hence in \(m,\tau,L\), since \(m=\tau\). No expansion proportional to the numerical endowments or quotas is required.
