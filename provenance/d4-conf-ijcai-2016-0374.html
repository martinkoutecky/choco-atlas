For a fixed pair, each vehicle acts independently at each observed tree node during its stay. Classification

The stated problem is solvable exactly in polynomial time in \(H,S,m,\tau,K,L\), and its prescribed transfers satisfy the stated ex-post truthfulness requirement.

The proof requires two facts: work is nonincreasing in reported cost for the precise projection rule, and the transfer integral has only polynomially many relevant breakpoints. Both are proved below.

1. Feasibility and simplified transition formulas

Fix a cohort, a candidate pair, and a realized price path. Suppress cohort indices. Define, for \(a\leq t\leq d\),

\(b_t=\max\{s_{\min},q-C(d-t)\}\).

The initial assumptions imply \(b_a\leq s_a\leq q\). Suppose inductively that \(b_t\leq s_t\leq q\). Then

\(b_{t+1}\leq s_t+C\), and \(b_{t+1}\leq q\).

Indeed, both entries defining \(b_{t+1}\) are at most \(s_t+C\): we have \(s_{\min}\leq s_t\), and \(q-C(d-t-1)\leq s_t+C\).

Consequently, the projection interval

\([\ell_t,h_t]=[\max\{-D,b_{t+1}-s_t\},\min\{C,q-s_t\}]\)

is nonempty. To verify this, each entry defining \(\ell_t\) is at most both entries defining \(h_t\): use \(D,C>0\), \(q-s_t\geq0\), \(b_{t+1}-s_t\leq C\), and \(b_{t+1}\leq q\).

Projection therefore gives

\(-D\leq r_t\leq C\), and \(b_{t+1}\leq s_{t+1}\leq q\).

Induction proves feasibility throughout the stay. Since \(b_d=q\), it also proves \(s_d=q\).

Writing \(s=s_t\) and \(b=b_{t+1}\), the three possible requests have the following exact next-state formulas:

| Requested rate | Next state |
|---|---|
| \(C\) | \(\min\{q,s+C\}\) |
| \(0\) | \(\max\{s,b\}\) |
| \(-D\) | \(\max\{s-D,b\}\) |

For request \(C\), projection selects the upper endpoint. For request \(-D\), it selects the lower endpoint. For request \(0\), the upper endpoint is nonnegative, so projection gives rate \(\max\{0,b-s\}\). These observations establish all three formulas.

2. Proven cost monotonicity

Fix the same cohort, pair, and price path, and compare reports \(x<y\), with \(x,y\geq0\). Let \(s_t(x)\) and \(s_t(y)\) be their respective states.

We first prove the state-order invariant

\(s_t(x)\leq s_t(y)\)

at every time. It holds at arrival because the initial state is public and identical.

Define the discharge-test indicator

\(E_t(z)=\mathbf{1}\{p_t^{\mathrm{sell}}-\alpha z\geq\rho_{\mathrm{high}}\}\).

Since \(\alpha>0\), we have \(E_t(x)\geq E_t(y)\).

Suppose the state-order invariant holds at time \(t\). There are two cases.

Case 1: \(p_t^{\mathrm{buy}}>\rho_{\mathrm{low}}\). Neither report requests priority charging. The transition is therefore

\(s_{t+1}(z)=\max\{b_{t+1},s_t(z)-DE_t(z)\}\).

Because \(s_t(x)\leq s_t(y)\) and \(E_t(x)\geq E_t(y)\),

\(s_t(x)-DE_t(x)\leq s_t(y)-DE_t(y)\).

Taking maxima with the same \(b_{t+1}\) preserves this inequality.

Case 2: \(p_t^{\mathrm{buy}}\leq\rho_{\mathrm{low}}\). Since \(y>x\geq0\), we have \(y>0\). The price and threshold assumptions give

\(p_t^{\mathrm{sell}}-\alpha y<p_t^{\mathrm{sell}}\leq p_t^{\mathrm{buy}}\leq\rho_{\mathrm{low}}\leq\rho_{\mathrm{high}}\).

Thus \(E_t(y)=0\). The higher report’s next state is consequently

\(s_{t+1}(y)=\min\{q,s_t(y)+C\}\),

including when its current state is already \(q\).

If \(s_t(x)<q\), priority charging gives the same transition formula for the lower report, and state order follows from monotonicity of \(s\mapsto\min\{q,s+C\}\).

If \(s_t(x)=q\), the induction hypothesis forces \(s_t(y)=q\). The higher report remains at \(q\), whereas feasibility guarantees \(s_{t+1}(x)\leq q\).

This completes the state-order induction, including the boundary case \(x=0\).

Now define total charging and discharging energies:

\(Q^+(z)=\sum_{t=a}^{d-1}\max\{0,r_t(z)\}\), and \(Q^-(z)=\sum_{t=a}^{d-1}\max\{0,-r_t(z)\}\).

We prove that the lower report receives at least as much positive charging in every slot.

  • If \(p_t^{\mathrm{buy}}>\rho_{\mathrm{low}}\), either possible request has positive actual rate \(\max\{0,b_{t+1}-s_t(z)\}\). This expression is nonincreasing in the state, so state order proves the assertion.
  • If \(p_t^{\mathrm{buy}}\leq\rho_{\mathrm{low}}\) and \(s_t(x)<q\), both reports have positive charging rate \(\min\{C,q-s_t(z)\}\). Again, state order proves the assertion.
  • If \(p_t^{\mathrm{buy}}\leq\rho_{\mathrm{low}}\) and \(s_t(x)=q\), both states equal \(q\). Neither report has a positive rate.

Therefore \(Q^+(x)\geq Q^+(y)\). Both schedules start at \(s_a\) and end at \(q\), so telescoping the state changes gives

\(Q^+(z)-Q^-(z)=q-s_a\).

It follows that \(Q^-(x)\geq Q^-(y)\). Multiplication by \(\alpha>0\) proves

\(W(x)\geq W(y)\).

Thus work is nonincreasing on the entire nonnegative report domain. This verifies the monotonicity needed for the stated rule directly; the [cited IJCAI paper](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/16/Papers/048.pdf) develops the corresponding threshold-rule and integral-transfer approach.

3. Exact evaluation of the transfer integral

Continue to fix one cohort, one candidate pair, and one realized path through departure. At every slot during the stay, define

\(\beta_t=\dfrac{p_t^{\mathrm{sell}}-\rho_{\mathrm{high}}}{\alpha}\).

The discharge test for report \(z\) is exactly \(z\leq\beta_t\).

On any open interval containing no \(\beta_t\), every discharge-test indicator is constant. The entire schedule is then constant on that interval: the initial state is fixed, and induction through the slots shows that the requested rates, projected rates, and subsequent states are identical. Hence \(W(z)\) is constant there.

There are at most \(d-a\leq H\) distinct breakpoints. Moreover, if

\(B=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{\beta_t:a\leq t<d\}\bigr)\),

then \(W(z)=0\) for every \(z>B\). Such a report never requests discharge; requests \(C\) and \(0\) both produce nonnegative rates.

To compute the integral for a supported rational cost \(c\), list the distinct breakpoints strictly greater than \(c\) in increasing order:

\(z_1<\cdots<z_M\), with \(z_0=c\).

If the list is empty, the integral is zero. Otherwise \(z_M=B\), and the exact formula is

\(\displaystyle \int_c^\infty W(z)\,dz=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}(z_{i+1}-z_i)\,W\!\left(\frac{z_i+z_{i+1}}{2}\right)\).

Every midpoint lies in an interval on which work is constant. Values at the finitely many endpoints do not affect the integral.

Compute \(W(c)\) separately using the stipulated weak inequalities and charge priority. This preserves the exact allocation at a breakpoint. The required transfer is then exactly

\(\displaystyle P(c)=cW(c)+\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}(z_{i+1}-z_i)\,W\!\left(\frac{z_i+z_{i+1}}{2}\right)\).

Sorting uses \(O(H\log(1+H))\) comparisons. Simulating every midpoint uses \(O(H^2)\) arithmetic operations. All quantities are rational.

4. Ex-post dominant-strategy truthfulness

Fix a realized path and the selected pair. By the zero-mass assumption, a unilateral report leaves the support masses and selected pair unchanged. Therefore the preceding monotonicity result applies to all counterfactual reports of that vehicle.

Write

\(I(z)=\int_z^\infty W(v)\,dv\).

For true cost \(c\) and reported cost \(y\), utility is

\(U(y;c)=(y-c)W(y)+I(y)\),

whereas truthful utility is \(U(c;c)=I(c)\).

If \(y>c\), then

\(\displaystyle U(c;c)-U(y;c)=\int_c^y\bigl(W(v)-W(y)\bigr)\,dv\geq0\).

If \(y<c\), then

\(\displaystyle U(c;c)-U(y;c)=\int_y^c\bigl(W(y)-W(v)\bigr)\,dv\geq0\).

Both inequalities follow from the proved monotonicity. Equality of reports gives equality of utilities.

These arguments hold for every realized path and every nonnegative report, including breakpoint reports. Thus truthful reporting is an ex-post dominant strategy under the precise unilateral zero-mass requirement.

5. Polynomial-time algorithm and bit complexity

The complete algorithm is as follows.

  • Compute node-reach probabilities by multiplying transition probabilities along the tree.
  • For each candidate pair, traverse the explicit tree and simulate each supported type. At its arrival nodes, initialize its public state to \(s_a\); at subsequent active nodes, propagate the state from its parent.
  • At every node, sum the mass-weighted rates and degradation costs, evaluate the specified welfare contribution, and sum the probability-weighted contributions.
  • Select the largest exact welfare value, breaking ties by index, and retain that pair’s schedules.
  • For each supported type and each distinct price prefix through its last stay slot, compute its departure transfer by Section 3.

The tree structure is essential: every node has one ancestral history. Thus a type has one state per active node. Also, the number of distinct prefixes ending at any departure time is at most \(S\), including prefixes of probability zero.

Precomputing feasibility bounds costs \(O(mH)\) operations. Evaluating all candidates costs \(O(K\tau S)\). Computing all departure transfers costs at most

\(O\bigl(\tau S[H^2+H\log(1+H)]\bigr)\).

Schedules and departure transfers have at most \(O(\tau S)\) entries.

It remains to check that exact rational arithmetic has polynomial bit cost.

Let \(Q\) be the product of the denominators of all input rationals, and let \(A\) be the product of the positive numerators of the public degradation coefficients, each written as a reduced fraction. Both products have bit length \(O(L)\).

  • States and rates have denominators dividing \(Q\). Their computation uses only additions, subtractions, integer multiples of \(C\), and selection by minima or maxima. Their magnitudes are bounded by the public battery and rate parameters; computing the bounds adds only \(O(\log(1+H))\) bits.
  • A node probability has denominator dividing \(Q\), since its path uses distinct input edges. Aggregate rates have denominators dividing \(Q^2\). Every probability-weighted welfare term, and hence every candidate’s welfare, has denominator dividing \(Q^5\).
  • Each breakpoint has denominator dividing \(Q^2A\). Midpoints add at most a factor of \(2\). Work has denominator dividing \(Q^2\), so integral summands have denominators dividing \(Q^4A\). The term \(cW(c)\) has denominator dividing \(Q^3\), which also divides \(Q^4A\).

The magnitudes of these quantities are bounded by sums of polynomially many terms involving a fixed number of input rational factors, with at most an additional factor \(H\). Consequently, their numerators also have polynomial bit length. Exact comparisons, sorting, arithmetic, and output encoding therefore take polynomial time.

Finite candidate enumeration, explicit-tree simulation, and finite-breakpoint integration give an exact polynomial-time algorithm. The state-order and energy-balance argument proves cost monotonicity for the specified projection rule, and the prescribed integral transfer then proves ex-post dominant-strategy truthfulness.
