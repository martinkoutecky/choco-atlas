The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a common set of binary features, a finite list of supported acyclic partial CP-net types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and outcomes \(\alpha,\beta\), classify each type’s comparison of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). Let \(P,N,I\) be the total masses of types reporting \(\alpha\succ\beta\), \(\beta\succ\alpha\), and incomparability, respectively. Decide whether \(P>N+I\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\geq1\) acyclic partial CP-net types, each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m\geq1\) binary features. Type \(t\) uses a subset \(V_t\); its ranked features have explicit conditional preference tables giving a strict total order for every parent assignment, while its unranked features have no preference table. Every feature is ranked by some supported type. Put \(k=\max_t|V_t|\), and let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A flip changes one feature, holding all others fixed. A ranked-feature flip is worsening when its table prefers the old value. For an unranked-feature flip, compare the positions of each child's fixed value in that child's old and new conditional orders: the flip is worsening if every child moves to a strictly worse position, and indifferent if every child's position stays unchanged. A childless unranked flip is indifferent, never worsening; all other unranked flips are unavailable in the chains below.
For outcomes restricted to \(V_t\), define \(\alpha\succ_t\beta\) by a chain from \(\alpha\) to \(\beta\) of worsening or indifferent flips containing at least one worsening flip. Define \(\alpha\approx_t\beta\) by an all-indifferent chain, allowing the empty chain; they are incomparable when neither strict comparison nor indifference holds.
Given distinct complete outcomes \(\alpha,\beta\), let \(P,N,I\) be the masses of types reporting, respectively, \(\alpha\succ_t\beta\), \(\beta\succ_t\alpha\), and incomparability. Indifferent types abstain. The decision problem asks whether \(P>N+I\); equality gives a negative answer.
State running times in \(m,\tau,L,k\).

Is this decision problem in \(\mathrm{P}\), or is it \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard?
If it is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, is it fixed-parameter tractable with parameter \(k\)?

For a finite society of \(a\) agents, majority dominance has a reported \(O(a2^k)\) algorithm ([Rossi–Venable–Walsh](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~twalsh/rvwaaai2004.pdf)).
Discrete majority dominance is also \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even for one fully ranked acyclic binary CP-net of indegree at most three ([Lukasiewicz–Malizia](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/files/147926248/1_s2.0_S0004370218303266_main_1_.pdf)). No result specifically treating the rational-mass partial-CP-net problem was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Specified explicit conditional preference tables with strict total orders: partiality means wholly unranked features, not partially ordered table rows.
  • Restored indifferent comparisons and their abstention from the majority inequality, including indifferent flips within strict dominance chains.
  • Restored the requirement that every global feature be ranked by at least one supported type.
  • Allowed types to use feature subsets and projected global outcomes onto those subsets, matching the discrete definition; defined k as the largest such subset.
  • Made the unranked-feature rule precise using changes in the positions of children's fixed values, requiring strict worsening for every child rather than permitting a mixture of worsening and unchanged positions.
  • Resolved the vacuous childless case as indifference only, preventing both flip directions from being strictly worsening.
  • Required distinct queried outcomes, consistent with the intended comparison domain; allowed empty indifferent chains for equal projected outcomes.
  • Defined L as total input bit length and treated exact equality in the majority comparison as rejection.

Result

The decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard, even with one fully ranked supported type of mass \(\mu_1=1\), whose dependency graph has indegree at most three. It is also fixed-parameter tractable with parameter \(k\).

A conservative running-time bound, accounting for binary arithmetic on a bit-cost RAM, is

\(T(m,\tau,L,k)=O\!\left(\bigl(L+\tau m+\tau(k+1)^2 2^k\bigr)L^2\right).\)

Consequently, it is not polynomial-time solvable unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). Both claims are proved below.

1. A potential function makes the voting categories disjoint

Fix a supported type, and write \(V\) for its feature set. List its ranked features as \(X_1,\ldots,X_r\), in the order induced by a topological ordering of its acyclic dependency graph.

For an outcome \(x\) on \(V\), let \(\rho_i(x)\in\{0,1\}\) denote the position of its value of \(X_i\) in the conditional order selected by \(x\): the preferred value has position zero, and the other value has position one. Define

\(\Phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}2^{r-i}\rho_i(x).\)

If there are no ranked features, set \(\Phi(x)=0\).

Lemma. Every worsening flip strictly increases \(\Phi\), and every indifferent flip preserves \(\Phi\).

Proof. First consider a worsening flip of ranked feature \(X_j\). Its parents remain fixed, so \(\rho_j\) changes from zero to one. The only other conditional positions that can change belong to children of \(X_j\), which occur later in the topological ordering. Thus all \(\rho_i\) with \(i<j\) remain unchanged. Even if every later coordinate decreases, the potential increases by at least

\(2^{r-j}-\sum_{i=j+1}^{r}2^{r-i}=1.\)

Next consider a flip of an unranked feature. Ranked values remain fixed, and their conditional positions can change only at children of the flipped feature. By the prescribed semantics, a worsening flip has at least one child and strictly worsens every child’s position. Thus at least one coordinate increases, and none decreases. An indifferent flip leaves every child’s position unchanged, including vacuously when there are no children. Therefore it preserves every coordinate of \(\Phi\). This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

It follows that \(x\succ_t y\) implies \(\Phi(x)<\Phi(y)\), whereas \(x\approx_t y\) implies \(\Phi(x)=\Phi(y)\). Hence neither opposite strict comparisons nor strict comparison and indifference can coexist.

Indifferent flips are reversible: equality of the old and new positions is symmetric. Therefore \(\approx_t\) is symmetric; reflexivity follows from the empty chain, and transitivity follows by concatenation.

Consequently, each type reports exactly one of forward strict preference, reverse strict preference, indifference, or incomparability. This also handles equal restricted outcomes: they are indifferent and cannot be strictly comparable.

2. Hardness by a restriction of the input

We use the following established result: dominance testing for an explicitly represented, fully ranked acyclic binary CP-net is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard even when its indegree is at most three. This is [Lukasiewicz–Malizia, Theorem 3.3](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/files/147926248/1_s2.0_S0004370218303266_main_1_.pdf).

Given such a dominance instance, consisting of a CP-net and distinct outcomes \(\alpha,\beta\), construct the society as follows:

  • There is one supported type, namely that CP-net.
  • Its feature set contains all \(m\) features.
  • Its mass is \(\mu_1=1\).
  • The queried outcomes are \(\alpha,\beta\), with orientation chosen so that the source question asks whether \(\alpha\) dominates \(\beta\).

This construction preserves the explicit tables and dependency graph and takes polynomial time. All required hypotheses hold: the mass is a positive binary rational, the support masses sum to one, the graph is acyclic, and every feature is ranked.

Because every feature is ranked, the allowed chains contain only ordinary worsening flips. Since the outcomes are distinct, they cannot be indifferent. Section 1 excludes opposite strict comparisons occurring simultaneously. Thus:

  • If \(\alpha\succ_1\beta\), then \(P=1\) and \(N=I=0\).
  • Otherwise, either \(N=1\) or \(I=1\), and \(P=0\).

Therefore \(P>N+I\) holds exactly when the source dominance instance is affirmative. This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction, proving \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under the claimed restrictions.

3. Deciding each type’s contribution by reachability

Fix type \(t\), put \(n_t=|V_t|\leq k\), and let \(a_t=\alpha|_{V_t}\) and \(b_t=\beta|_{V_t}\).

Construct two graphs with vertex set \(\{0,1\}^{n_t}\):

  • \(G_t\) contains a directed edge for every allowed worsening or indifferent flip.
  • \(H_t\) contains only the indifferent-flip edges.

Both graphs have \(2^{n_t}\) vertices and at most \(n_t2^{n_t}\) directed edges.

By definition, \(b_t\) is reachable from \(a_t\) in \(H_t\) exactly when \(a_t\approx_t b_t\).

Moreover,

\(a_t\succ_t b_t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b_t\text{ is reachable from }a_t\text{ in }G_t\text{ but not in }H_t.\)

For the forward implication, a strict chain gives reachability in \(G_t\), and Section 1 excludes indifference. For the reverse implication, a path in \(G_t\) that contained no worsening edge would be a path in \(H_t\). Therefore any witnessing path must contain a worsening edge.

Two graph searches consequently determine the signed contribution

\(s_t=\begin{cases}0,&a_t\approx_t b_t,\\1,&a_t\succ_t b_t,\\-1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\)

In the last case the type reports either reverse preference or incomparability; both count against the queried majority. Hence

\(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}s_t\mu_t=P-N-I.\)

The answer is affirmative exactly when this sum is strictly positive.

4. Graph construction and exact arithmetic

Graph work

After parsing the explicit tables into arrays indexed by parent assignments, precompute every ranked feature’s conditional position at every local outcome. For type \(t\), there are at most \(n_t2^{n_t}\) such positions. Extracting a parent assignment takes at most \(O(n_t)\) elementary operations, giving \(O((n_t+1)^2 2^{n_t})\) operations.

Using these positions, a ranked flip is checked in constant time. An unranked flip is checked by comparing its children’s positions at the old and new outcomes. At each outcome, checking all unranked flips costs at most the number of dependency arcs, which is \(O(n_t^2)\). The childless case is classified as indifferent, exactly as required.

Thus graph construction and both searches take

\(O((n_t+1)^2 2^{n_t})\)

elementary RAM operations. Across the support, including input parsing and a straightforward projection of the complete outcomes for each type, the bound is

\(O\!\left(L+\tau m+\tau(k+1)^2 2^k\right).\)

Rational arithmetic

Write the explicitly encoded mass as \(\mu_t=p_t/q_t\), where \(p_t,q_t\) are positive binary integers. Define

\(D=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}q_t,\qquad B=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}s_tp_t(D/q_t).\)

Each division is exact, and

\(B=D(P-N-I).\)

Because \(D>0\), testing \(B>0\) gives precisely the required answer, including a negative answer at equality.

The binary length of \(D\) is at most one plus the sum of the denominator lengths, hence \(O(L)\). Each summand has magnitude \(\mu_tD\), and every partial sum has magnitude at most

\(D\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t=D.\)

Thus all these integers have \(O(L)\) bits. Schoolbook multiplication and division yield a conservative total arithmetic bound of \(O(\tau L^2)\) bit operations.

The graph algorithm uses addresses and counters of \(O(k+\log(L+2))\) bits. Since complete outcomes are explicitly given, \(k\leq m=O(L)\); charging \(O(L^2)\) bit operations per elementary graph operation gives the stated overall bound

\(O\!\left(\bigl(L+\tau m+\tau(k+1)^2 2^k\bigr)L^2\right).\)

This has the form \(f(k)\operatorname{poly}(L)\), proving fixed-parameter tractability. In particular, the algorithm processes the \(\tau\) supported types directly; it does not expand rational masses into individual agents.

5. Scope of the conclusion

The reported finite-society bound concerns explicit agents; see [Rossi–Venable–Walsh, Theorem 5](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~twalsh/rvwaaai2004.pdf). The proof above establishes the parameterized result directly for the rational-mass representation, including its flip semantics and exact threshold arithmetic.

The requested classification is therefore settled: \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard and fixed-parameter tractable in \(k\). This does not assert \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness, which would additionally require an upper bound placing the unrestricted problem in \(\mathrm{NP}\).
