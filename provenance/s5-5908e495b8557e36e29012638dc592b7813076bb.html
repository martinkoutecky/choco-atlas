The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite hospital set, a support of τ complete resident types with rational masses μ_r, rational per-capita capacities κ_h, and a current stable allocation state with hospital tie data, can a polynomial-time flow construction reroute mass f while preserving stability, increasing b = ∑_h min(a_h, κ_h) by f, and decreasing e = ∑_h max(a_h − κ_h, 0) by f, where a_h is the mass assigned to hospital h?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_r\), summing to \(1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. There are \(m\geq1\) hospitals with positive binary-rational per-capita capacities \(\kappa_h\). Each type specifies a strict ranking of its acceptable hospitals and its priority class at each such hospital; hospital priorities may have ties. Unmatched is worse than every acceptable assignment.
The input also specifies current reduced preference lists and hospital tie refinements, obtained by deleting reciprocal acceptable pairs and splitting existing ties without reversing strict priorities. For representing the current state, otherwise identical types with different current lists, priorities, or assignments are listed separately. Each type's entire mass is currently assigned to the first hospital on its reduced list, or is unmatched with an empty reduced list.
An allocation may split type masses among acceptable hospitals and unmatched. Write \(a_h\) for assigned mass. If \(a_h>\kappa_h\), the mass \(u_h\) in its worst assigned priority class must satisfy \(u_h>a_h-\kappa_h\). Stability means there is no positive mass preferring an acceptable hospital to its present assignment while that hospital either has \(a_h<\kappa_h\) or strictly prefers that mass to positive assigned mass. The input is stable under both original and current preferences. At every full or oversubscribed hospital, its worst assigned class is the final class of its reduced list, called its tail.
Define \(b(A)=\sum_h\min(a_h,\kappa_h)\) and \(e(A)=\sum_h\max(a_h-\kappa_h,0)\). Define a network with source \(s\), sink \(t\), hospital vertices, and a vertex for each tail-assigned type \(r\) at a full or oversubscribed hospital \(h\) having another hospital on its reduced list. Include \(s\to h\) of capacity \(a_h-\kappa_h\) for oversubscribed hospitals, \(h\to t\) of capacity \(\kappa_h-a_h\) for undersubscribed hospitals, and \(h\to r\) of capacity \(\mu_r\). From \(r\), include capacity-\(\mu_r\) edges to consecutive hospitals below \(h\), through and including the first hospital that is undersubscribed, does not have \(r\) in its tail, or is last on the list. Let \(f\) be this network's maximum flow value.
An admissible update reroutes fractions of each type along these edges according to a flow of value \(f\), retaining its remaining mass at its current hospital. A moved fraction may be demoted immediately below the tail at every hospital preceding its destination on its current list; fractions sharing a destination may remain tied. Original strict priorities must be preserved. The resulting allocation must satisfy the overcapacity condition and be stable under both original and refined preferences.
Is finding an admissible update with \(b(A')=b(A)+f\) and \(e(A')=e(A)-f\), or deciding that none exists, polynomial-time solvable by a flow construction, or NP-hard? State running times in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the total input bit length \(L\).
For discrete residents, a maximum-flow update followed by renewed applications preserves stability and changes bound and excess by exactly its flow value. [Irving–Manlove](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/25733/1/25733.pdf)
For divisible allocations with strict preferences and respected capacities, polynomial-time algorithms are known; that result does not address this tied, temporarily overcapacity update. No result settling the exact continuous update above was located. [Dean–Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf)

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total resident mass to one so capacities are per-capita quantities; retained strictly positive hospital capacities.
  • Interpreted complete resident types as fully specified types, allowing incomplete acceptable-hospital lists as in the discrete model.
  • Restored the reduced-list state, reciprocal deletions, hospital tie refinements, first-choice assignments, and exhausted unmatched residents required by the discrete update.
  • Allowed state information to distinguish otherwise identical resident types, so initially each listed type has a single assignment and reduced list.
  • Extended weak blocking to positive masses and retained the strict worst-tie overcapacity condition.
  • Defined the unspecified quantity f as the maximum flow value of the discrete update network with unit resident capacities replaced by type masses.
  • Allowed rational splitting of type masses, including separate demotion of moved fractions; fractions with identical destinations may remain tied.
  • Required stability for original preferences as well as the updated refinement, and allowed a determination that the requested update does not exist.

Result

The problem as stated is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\). This holds even with one oversubscribed hospital, current resident lists of length at most two, and strict hospital priorities everywhere except at that oversubscribed hospital.

The proof uses the permitted reciprocal deletions and the requirement of stability under original preferences. It assumes exactly the stated input promises, without an additional requirement that the reduced lists arise from a particular application algorithm.

1. Flow conservation guarantees the numerical identities

Let \(F\) be any feasible flow of value \(v\), and reroute type masses according to \(F\). For each hospital \(h\), write \(q_h=F(s,h)\) and \(z_h=F(h,t)\), taking either quantity to be zero when its edge is absent.

Hospital flow conservation gives

\[ a'_h=a_h-q_h+z_h. \]

At an initially oversubscribed hospital, \(z_h=0\) and \(q_h\leq a_h-\kappa_h\), so it remains full or oversubscribed. At an initially undersubscribed hospital, \(q_h=0\) and \(z_h\leq\kappa_h-a_h\), so it remains undersubscribed or becomes full. An initially full hospital stays full.

Consequently,

\[ b(A')=b(A)+\sum_h z_h=b(A)+v, \qquad e(A')=e(A)-\sum_h q_h=e(A)-v. \]

Thus the numerical identities are automatic. The difficulty is finding a maximum flow whose rerouting satisfies stability and the overcapacity condition.

2. Reduction from binary SUBSET SUM

We use the standard \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\) SUBSET SUM problem: given positive binary integers \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) and a target \(B\), determine whether some subset sums to \(B\). Its binary formulation and hardness are recalled in [Polak–Rohwedder–Węgrzycki](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04035).

Put \(W=\sum_i w_i\). We may restrict to \(1\leq B\leq W\): instances outside this range are immediately decidable.

Choose a power of two \(Q>4W\), and define

\[ \lambda_i=\frac{w_i}{Q}, \qquad \delta=\frac{Q-4W}{Q}. \]

All masses and capacities below are positive binary rationals.

Hospitals and types

Create one hospital \(S\). For each item \(i\) and sign \(\sigma\in\{0,1\}\), create hospitals \(H_i^\sigma\) and \(D_i^\sigma\).

There are two types for each \((i,\sigma)\), both of mass \(\lambda_i\):

| Type | Original resident preferences | Current resident preferences | Current assignment |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(p_i^\sigma\) | \(S\succ H_i^\sigma\) | unchanged | \(S\) |
| \(r_i^\sigma\) | \(H_i^\sigma\succ H_i^{1-\sigma}\succ D_i^\sigma\) | \(H_i^\sigma\succ D_i^\sigma\) | \(H_i^\sigma\) |

Add a type \(z\) of mass \(\delta\), accepting only \(S\), and assign it to \(S\).

The current lists delete precisely the reciprocal pairs \((r_i^\sigma,H_i^{1-\sigma})\).

The original priorities at \(H_i^\sigma\) are strict:

\[ p_i^\sigma\succ r_i^{1-\sigma}\succ r_i^\sigma. \]

After the reciprocal deletion, its current list is

\[ p_i^\sigma\succ r_i^\sigma. \]

Hospital \(D_i^\sigma\) accepts only \(r_i^\sigma\). Hospital \(S\) ties all its acceptable types in one priority class. No initial tie refinement is needed.

Set capacities to

\[ \kappa_{H_i^\sigma}=\kappa_{D_i^\sigma}=\lambda_i, \qquad \kappa_S=\frac{Q-2W-2B}{Q}. \]

The last capacity is positive because \(Q>4W\) and \(B\leq W\).

3. Verification of every input promise

The total type mass is

\[ \sum_{i,\sigma}\bigl(\mu_{p_i^\sigma}+\mu_{r_i^\sigma}\bigr)+\mu_z =\frac{4W}{Q}+\delta=1. \]

Every type is assigned its first hospital under both original and current preferences. Hence no positive mass prefers any other acceptable hospital, proving initial stability under both preference systems.

Each \(H_i^\sigma\) has assigned mass \(\lambda_i=\kappa_{H_i^\sigma}\). Its only assigned type, \(r_i^\sigma\), is the final class of its current hospital list, so the required tail property holds.

Each \(D_i^\sigma\) is empty and undersubscribed.

At \(S\), assigned mass and excess are

\[ a_S=\frac{Q-2W}{Q}, \qquad a_S-\kappa_S=\frac{2B}{Q}. \]

All assigned mass belongs to its single tail class. Its tail mass therefore satisfies

\[ u_S=a_S>a_S-\kappa_S, \]

because \(\kappa_S>0\). Thus the overcapacity condition holds.

Finally, the deletions are reciprocal, and no strict priority is reversed. The construction satisfies all stated input promises.

4. The prescribed network and its maximum flow

Apart from the edge \(s\to S\), the network consists exactly of the chains

\[ S\longrightarrow p_i^\sigma \longrightarrow H_i^\sigma \longrightarrow r_i^\sigma \longrightarrow D_i^\sigma \longrightarrow t. \]

Every edge on such a chain has capacity \(\lambda_i\), while \(s\to S\) has capacity \(2B/Q\).

To verify that these are exactly the prescribed edges:

  • Each \(p_i^\sigma\) is tail-assigned at oversubscribed \(S\). Its only lower hospital is \(H_i^\sigma\), where it is strictly above the tail.
  • Each \(r_i^\sigma\) is tail-assigned at full \(H_i^\sigma\). Its only lower current hospital is undersubscribed \(D_i^\sigma\).
  • Type \(z\) has no second hospital and creates no type vertex.

The chains have total capacity \(2W/Q\). Since \(B\leq W\), they can carry all the source capacity. Therefore

\[ f=\frac{2B}{Q}=e(A). \]

In particular, computing the maximum-flow value does not require solving SUBSET SUM.

5. Original stability forces entire item masses to move

Consider any admissible maximum-flow update. Let \(x_i^\sigma\in[0,\lambda_i]\) be the amount moved along the chain indexed by \((i,\sigma)\).

Flow conservation at \(H_i^\sigma\) means that it receives \(x_i^\sigma\) of \(p_i^\sigma\) and loses the same amount of \(r_i^\sigma\). Thus it remains full, with allocation

\[ p_i^\sigma:\ x_i^\sigma, \qquad r_i^\sigma:\ \lambda_i-x_i^\sigma. \]

Suppose \(x_i^0>0\) but \(x_i^1<\lambda_i\). Then:

  • Positive mass of \(r_i^0\) is assigned to \(D_i^0\).
  • Under its original preferences, that mass prefers \(H_i^1\) to \(D_i^0\).
  • Positive mass \(\lambda_i-x_i^1\) of \(r_i^1\) remains assigned to \(H_i^1\).
  • Hospital \(H_i^1\) strictly prefers \(r_i^0\) to \(r_i^1\).

These positive masses form an original blocking pair. Therefore original stability implies

\[ x_i^0>0\ \Longrightarrow\ x_i^1=\lambda_i. \]

The symmetric argument gives

\[ x_i^1>0\ \Longrightarrow\ x_i^0=\lambda_i. \]

Since \(\lambda_i>0\), these implications force

\[ (x_i^0,x_i^1)\in \{(0,0),(\lambda_i,\lambda_i)\}. \]

This conclusion holds for arbitrary real fractions, however small. Optional demotions cannot remove the obstruction: it uses original strict priorities.

Let \(I\) be the set of items whose two masses move. The maximum-flow requirement now yields

\[ \frac{2B}{Q} =\sum_{i,\sigma}x_i^\sigma =\frac{2}{Q}\sum_{i\in I}w_i. \]

Hence \(\sum_{i\in I}w_i=B\). Every admissible update therefore supplies a solution to SUBSET SUM.

6. Every solution to SUBSET SUM gives an admissible update

Conversely, suppose \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in I}w_i=B\).

For every \(i\in I\), send \(\lambda_i\) along both chains. For \(i\notin I\), send zero. The flow value is \(2B/Q=f\).

The resulting allocation has:

  • \(S\) exactly full;
  • each selected \(H_i^\sigma\) filled entirely by \(p_i^\sigma\);
  • each unselected \(H_i^\sigma\) retaining \(r_i^\sigma\);
  • selected \(D_i^\sigma\) full and unselected \(D_i^\sigma\) empty.

No hospital is oversubscribed, so the overcapacity condition holds.

We verify original stability by exhausting the types that now prefer another assignment.

A moved \(p_i^\sigma\) prefers \(S\) to \(H_i^\sigma\). But \(S\) is full and ties that type with every assigned type, so it neither has spare capacity nor strictly prefers the moved mass.

A moved \(r_i^\sigma\) prefers both \(H_i^\sigma\) and \(H_i^{1-\sigma}\) to \(D_i^\sigma\). Both hospitals are full of their respective \(p\)-types, which they strictly prefer to \(r_i^\sigma\).

Every unmoved type retains its original first choice. These cases exhaust all possible blocking pairs.

The unchanged current priorities also give stability: current acceptability deletes some original pairs, and every surviving comparison is unchanged. No further refinement is required, since demotions are optional.

Finally, Section 1 gives the required numerical identities. Equivalently, here \(b(A)=1-2B/Q\), \(e(A)=2B/Q\), and the update has \(b(A')=1\), \(e(A')=0\).

Thus an admissible maximum-flow update exists if and only if the SUBSET SUM instance is positive.

7. Running times and scope

The reduction produces

\[ m=\tau=4n+1. \]

Its network has \(8n+3\) vertices and \(10n+1\) edges. Choosing \(Q\) as the smallest power of two exceeding \(4W\) gives \(\log Q=O(\log W)\). The constructed input length is

\[ L=O\bigl(n(\log W+\log n)\bigr), \]

which is polynomial in the source input length \(\ell\). An elementary implementation constructs it in \(O(n\ell^2)\) bit operations.

For a general instance, the prescribed network has \(O(m+\tau)\) vertices and \(O(m\tau+m)\) edges. Edmonds–Karp computes \(f\) in

\[ O\bigl((m+\tau)(m\tau+m)^2\bigr) \]

arithmetic operations. Clearing rational denominators gives integers with \(O(L)\) bits; including preprocessing, a conservative bit bound is

\[ O\bigl((m+\tau)(m\tau+m)^2L^2\bigr). \]

Nevertheless, deciding whether this value can be realized by an admissible update is \(\mathrm{NP}\text{-hard}\). A general algorithm running in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), whether based on flow or otherwise, would therefore imply \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). The reduction does not establish strong hardness.

The distinction from the cited discrete procedure matters. [Irving–Manlove](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/25733/1/25733.pdf), Section 3.1, use specific deletion rules and invariants in proving original stability. Initial stability under both lists does not imply those invariants for arbitrary reciprocal deletions. The present reduction exploits precisely that distinction. The strict-preference, capacity-respecting setting of [Dean–Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf) likewise does not supply an admissibility algorithm for the stated update.
