The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given valuation types \(t\) with masses \(\mu_t\), good types \(q\) partitioned into categories \(\mathcal Q_h\), cardinality limits \(k_h\), and per-capita supplies \(\lambda_q\), let \(\mathcal B\) be the integral bundles satisfying \(\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_h}b_q\le k_h\). Compute a mass assignment \(x_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_qx_{t,b}=\lambda_q\), together with the largest \(\alpha\) such that every used bundle satisfies \(v_t(b)\ge\alpha z_t^\infty\), where \(z_t^\infty=\max_\rho\min_{\rho_b>0}v_t(b)\) over distributions \(\rho\) on \(\mathcal B\) with the same per-capita supplies; decide whether \(\alpha\ge 1/3\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: \(\tau\) valuation types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\); \(\tau\) counts supported types, not all possible types.
There are \(m\ge1\) good types, partitioned into nonempty categories \(\mathcal Q_1,\ldots,\mathcal Q_\ell\), with integer limits \(k_h\ge0\). Values \(v_{tq}\ge0\) and per-capita supplies \(\lambda_q\ge0\) are binary rationals; assume \(\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_h}\lambda_q\le k_h\) for every category.
Bundles contain integral numbers of copies: \(\mathcal B=\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}:\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_h}b_q\le k_h\text{ for every }h\}\), with additive value \(v_t(b)=\sum_qv_{tq}b_q\).
Let \(\mathcal D(\lambda)=\{\rho\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\mathcal B}:\sum_b\rho_b=1,\ \sum_b b_q\rho_b=\lambda_q\text{ for every }q\}\) and define \(z_t^\infty=\max_{\rho\in\mathcal D(\lambda)}\min_{b:\rho_b>0}v_t(b)\).
A feasible mass assignment has \(x_{t,b}\ge0\), \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,b}b_qx_{t,b}=\lambda_q\); thus all supplies are allocated. It provides factor \(\alpha\ge0\) when \(x_{t,b}>0\) implies \(v_t(b)\ge\alpha z_t^\infty\).
Let \(L\) be the total binary input length; state running times in \(m,\tau,\ell,L\), and the limits \(k_h\).

Is the \(1/3\) guarantee universally achievable by an algorithm polynomial in the binary input size that computes a feasible mass assignment with \(v_t(b)\ge z_t^\infty/3\) for every used bundle, or can this guarantee fail existentially or computationally?

For finite agents and indivisible goods, nonnegative additive valuations and feasible category limits admit a polynomial-time \(1/3\)-MMS allocation, using the constrained MMS benchmark ([Biswas–Barman](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/2018/0013.pdf)).
No result settling the exact distributional model above was identified in the literature search.

1. Proven result. The guarantee is universally achievable in polynomial time in the binary input size. In fact, the construction below achieves factor \(\frac{729}{2000}>\frac13\).

The assignment is returned sparsely, as a list of nonzero entries \((t,b,x_{t,b})\). It uses at most \(\tau+m\) such entries. Its running time is

\[ (m+1)^{10}\operatorname{poly}\!\left(m,\tau,\ell,L,\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}\log(k_h+1)\right) \]

bit operations. Thus the dependence on each limit is through its binary encoding, not its numerical value.

The proof uses a stronger finite-agent result than the supplied \(\frac13\) theorem: Hummel–Hetland’s Theorem 5 gives a feasible allocation attaining factor \(\frac{n}{2n-1}\), hence at least \(\frac12\), of constrained MMS. Its setting has nonnegative additive valuations, category limits, and allocation of every item. [Hummel–Hetland, *Maximin shares under cardinality constraints*, Theorem 5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-026-09731-1)

We use that theorem only to prove existence. The algorithm below never expands the population or the supplies.

2. Exact transfer of the finite existence theorem. Let

\[ P=\left\{u\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{m}:\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_h}u_q\le k_h\text{ for every }h\right\}. \]

Each category contributes a simplex whose vertices are integral: zero and the vectors placing all \(k_h\) copies on one good type. Therefore \(P=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal B)\). The supply hypothesis implies \(\lambda\in P\), so \(\mathcal D(\lambda)\) is nonempty.

For a type \(t\), every distribution’s minimum supported value belongs to the finite set \(\{v_t(b):b\in\mathcal B\}\). Consequently \(z_t^\infty\) is attained. Moreover, there is a rational attaining distribution \(\rho^{(t)}\): restrict the defining linear system to bundles satisfying \(v_t(b)\ge z_t^\infty\), and take a rational basic feasible solution.

Choose a positive integer \(N\) clearing the denominators of all masses, supplies, and the distributions \(\rho^{(t)}\). Construct a finite instance with:

  • \(N\mu_t\) agents of valuation type \(t\);
  • \(N\lambda_q\) individually labeled copies of good type \(q\);
  • the original category limits.

Categories containing no items may be omitted. If there are no items at all, every benchmark is zero and the empty assignment solves the problem.

For each \(t\), the counts \(N\rho_b^{(t)}\) describe a partition of these items into \(N\) feasible bundles, each worth at least \(z_t^\infty\) to \(t\). Indeed, their total number is \(N\), and their total count of each good type is \(N\lambda_q\). Labeled copies can therefore be assigned to these bundles exactly.

Conversely, any feasible partition into \(N\) bundles induces a distribution in \(\mathcal D(\lambda)\) by giving each bundle probability \(1/N\). Hence the constrained finite MMS of every agent of type \(t\) is exactly \(z_t^\infty\).

The finite \(\frac12\) theorem applies: valuations are nonnegative and additive, and the displayed partitions verify feasibility. Aggregating its allocation by type and bundle gives a mass assignment satisfying

\[ x_{t,b}>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_t(b)\ge \frac12z_t^\infty. \]

This proves universal existence of a \(\frac12\) assignment. Clearing denominators alone does not give the required binary-polynomial algorithm; the next steps supply it.

3. A polynomial bundle oracle with value loss and no cost loss. Fix \(\varepsilon=\frac1{10}\). For a nonnegative additive valuation \(v\), threshold \(a\ge0\), and arbitrary rational prices \(p\in\mathbb Q^m\), we construct an oracle with the following properties:

  • It returns either a bundle \(b\in\mathcal B\) satisfying \(v(b)\ge(1-\varepsilon)a\), or a declaration that no bundle satisfies \(v(b)\ge a\).
  • Whenever a bundle satisfying \(v(b)\ge a\) exists, the returned bundle satisfies

\[ p\cdot b\le \min\{p\cdot c:c\in\mathcal B,\ v(c)\ge a\}. \]

Prices may have either sign. This is essential for preserving supplies as equalities later.

For \(a=0\), minimize \(p\cdot b\) directly: in each category, allocate its entire capacity to a cheapest type if that price is negative, and otherwise allocate nothing.

Suppose \(a>0\). Call a good type *large* if \(v_q>\varepsilon a\), and *small* otherwise. Enumerate all integer vectors \(h\), supported on large types, with \(\sum_qh_q\le10\), discarding those violating a category limit.

If \(\sum_qh_q=10\), then \(v(h)>a\). Find a cheapest integral extension of \(h\). This is again done category by category: fill the remaining capacity with a cheapest negative-price type, or leave it unused. Record that extension.

If \(\sum_qh_q<10\), fix the large-type coordinates exactly to \(h\), and permit only small types in the remainder. Solve the linear program

\[ \min p\cdot(h+u) \]

subject to

\[ u\ge0,\qquad \sum_{\substack{q\in\mathcal Q_h\\q\text{ small}}}u_q \le k_h-\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_h}h_q,\qquad v(h+u)\ge a. \]

Skip this vector \(h\) if the program is infeasible. Otherwise obtain an optimal vertex.

To justify rounding that vertex, let \(Q\) be the product of the residual category simplices, before imposing the value inequality. Its vertices are integral. A vertex of \(Q\cap\{u:v(h+u)\ge a\}\) is either a vertex of \(Q\), or lies on an edge of \(Q\). Indeed, if its smallest containing face in \(Q\) had dimension at least two, one additional active inequality would leave a nontrivial local segment, contradicting extremality.

An edge changes only one category. Its direction is either \(e_q\) or \(e_q-e_r\), up to scaling. A nonintegral point on that edge lies between two neighboring integral points. They differ by adding one small copy or exchanging two small types. Their values consequently differ by at most \(\varepsilon a\).

Choose the cheaper of these two integral points. Its cost is no greater than the fractional point’s cost, because the latter is their convex combination. Its value is at least the fractional point’s value minus \(\varepsilon a\). Adding \(h\) therefore produces a feasible bundle worth at least \((1-\varepsilon)a\). Record it.

Return a cheapest recorded candidate. If there is none, declare the original threshold unattainable.

To prove the cost guarantee, take a cheapest original bundle \(c\) worth at least \(a\).

  • If \(c\) contains at least ten large copies, some enumerated vector \(h\le c\) contains exactly ten. Its cheapest extension costs no more than \(c\).
  • Otherwise, the enumerated vector recording exactly the large copies of \(c\) has a feasible residual LP. Its optimum costs no more than \(c\), and the rounding step does not increase cost.

Thus an original qualifying bundle always supplies a candidate of no greater cost. This also proves that a declaration of unattainability is sound.

There are \(O((m+1)^{10})\) enumerated vectors. Each LP has at most \(m\) variables and \(O(m+\ell)\) constraints, with binary-encoded capacities. The oracle therefore runs in \(O((m+1)^{10}\operatorname{poly}(m,\ell,\text{input bit length}))\) bit operations.

4. Turning the oracle into exact mass feasibility. For thresholds \(a=(a_t)\), define

\[ G_t(a_t)=\{b\in\mathcal B:v_t(b)\ge a_t\}. \]

We now prove a polynomial procedure that returns either:

  • a sound declaration that no assignment exists using \(G_t(a_t)\); or
  • an exact feasible mass assignment whose bundles satisfy \(v_t(b)\ge(1-\varepsilon)a_t\).

These outcomes deliberately allow overlap: the procedure is not an exact threshold-decision algorithm.

Put \(d=\tau+m\), \(y=(\mu,\lambda)\), and associate the integer column

\[ A_{t,b}=(e_t,b)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{d} \]

with each type–bundle pair. Original threshold feasibility is precisely the existence of nonnegative coefficients satisfying \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}A_{t,b}=y\), using only \(b\in G_t(a_t)\).

By Farkas’ lemma, infeasibility is equivalent to the existence of \(u=(r,p)\) satisfying

\[ r_t+p\cdot b\ge0\quad\text{for all original qualifying pairs},\qquad u\cdot y\le-1. \]

The normalization to \(-1\) follows by scaling any strictly separating vector.

We run an ellipsoid cutting procedure in this dual space. The following explicit bounds justify polynomial termination and extraction of a primal solution.

Let \(C=\max(1,k_1,\ldots,k_\ell)\), and let \(D\) clear the denominators of \(y\). Then every coordinate of every column and of \(y\) is at most \(C\), and \(Dy\) is integral. Set

\[ F=(2d)!(DC)^{2d},\qquad H=16(F+1). \]

For any finite set \(S\) of possible columns, if \(y\) is outside their generated cone, there is a normalized separating vector \(u_0\) with every coordinate of absolute value at most \(F\). Here is the bit-bound justification. Split each coordinate of \(u_0\) into two nonnegative variables. The resulting feasible LP has \(2d\) variables and integral coefficients bounded by \(DC\), after clearing the normalization inequality’s denominator. It has a vertex: minimizing the sum of its nonnegative variables yields a nonempty compact optimal face. At a vertex, choose \(2d\) independent active equations. Cramer’s rule, with right-hand sides bounded by \(DC\), bounds each coordinate by \((2d)!(DC)^{2d}\). The difference of two nonnegative coordinates has the same absolute bound.

Let \(f=(\mathbf1_\tau,0_m)\). Since \(f\cdot A_{t,b}=1\) and \(f\cdot y=1\), the vector \(\widehat u=4u_0+f\) satisfies

\[ \widehat u\cdot A\ge1\quad(A\in S),\qquad \widehat u\cdot y\le-3,\qquad |\widehat u_j|\le H/2. \]

Thus the system

\[ |u_j|\le H,\qquad u\cdot y\le-1,\qquad u\cdot A\ge0\quad(A\in S) \]

contains a Euclidean ball of radius \(\eta=1/(4dC)\). To check this, perturb \(\widehat u\) by a vector of Euclidean norm at most \(\eta\). Each column and \(y\) has norm at most \(\sqrt d\,C\), so every relevant dot product changes by at most \(1/4\). The box bounds also remain satisfied.

Start the ellipsoid with a ball of radius \(dH\), containing the box. At a query point \(u=(r,p)\):

  • If a box constraint or \(u\cdot y\le-1\) is violated, cut by that constraint.
  • Otherwise, query the bundle oracle for every type with threshold \(a_t\) and prices \(p\).
  • If an oracle declares its original threshold unattainable, declare original mass feasibility impossible; \(\mu_t>0\) justifies this conclusion.
  • If some returned bundle has \(r_t+p\cdot b<0\), record its column and cut by \(u\cdot A_{t,b}\ge0\).
  • If no returned bundle violates its inequality, declare original mass feasibility impossible.

The last declaration is sound. For every original qualifying bundle \(c\), the oracle’s cost guarantee gives

\[ r_t+p\cdot c\ge r_t+p\cdot b\ge0. \]

If that type’s original qualifying set is empty, the assertion is vacuous. Hence the query point is a genuine Farkas certificate for original infeasibility.

The standard rational ellipsoid cutting theorem reduces the enclosing volume below that of an \(\eta\)-ball after \(O(d^2\log(dH/\eta))\) central cuts. It uses polynomial-bit arithmetic here: the constraint coefficients, \(\log H\), and \(\log(1/\eta)\) all have polynomial encoding length.

If no infeasibility declaration occurs by then, let \(S\) be the recorded columns. Necessarily \(y\) belongs to their generated cone. Otherwise, the ball constructed above satisfies every recorded inequality and every box and normalization constraint. Every ellipsoid cut contains that ball, contradicting the final volume bound.

Solve the finite rational LP \(\sum_{A\in S}x_AA=y\), \(x_A\ge0\). Every recorded bundle satisfies its relaxed threshold, so this is the promised exact mass assignment. There are polynomially many recorded columns.

In particular, whenever the original threshold assignment exists, the procedure must return a relaxed assignment.

5. Polynomially computable lower estimates of the benchmarks. We compute numbers \(w_t\) satisfying

\[ \frac{81}{100}z_t^\infty\le w_t\le z_t^\infty. \]

If all values of type \(t\) are zero, set \(w_t=0\). Otherwise let \(u_t\) be its smallest strictly positive single-copy value, and put

\[ U_t=\sum_h k_h\max_{q\in\mathcal Q_h}v_{tq}. \]

Every feasible bundle has value at most \(U_t\). Also, if \(z_t^\infty>0\), then \(z_t^\infty\ge u_t\), because every positive bundle value is at least \(u_t\).

Apply the preceding feasibility procedure to the one-type society of mass one with valuation \(v_t\) and supplies \(\lambda\). Maintain lower endpoint \(l=0\) and upper endpoint \(h=U_t+u_t\). Query their midpoint repeatedly:

  • on a returned relaxed assignment, replace \(l\) by the midpoint;
  • on an infeasibility declaration, replace \(h\) by the midpoint.

Stop when \(h-l\le u_t/10\), and set \(w_t=9l/10\).

The procedure’s outcomes need not be monotone. The argument uses only these two sound invariants:

  • \(h>z_t^\infty\), because an infeasibility declaration at threshold \(a\) implies \(a>z_t^\infty\);
  • \(9l/10\le z_t^\infty\), because a successful query at \(l\) supplies a distribution in \(\mathcal D(\lambda)\) supported on bundles worth at least \(9l/10\). The initial endpoint \(l=0\) also satisfies this invariant.

At termination, \(l\ge z_t^\infty-u_t/10\). If the benchmark is positive, this gives

\[ w_t\ge\frac9{10}\left(z_t^\infty-\frac{u_t}{10}\right) \ge\frac{81}{100}z_t^\infty. \]

If the benchmark is zero, nonnegativity and \(w_t\le z_t^\infty\) give \(w_t=0\).

The number of queries is \(O(1+\log((U_t+u_t)/u_t))\), polynomial in the binary input length. All queried thresholds have polynomial encoding length.

6. Final assignment and complexity. Run the mass-feasibility procedure with thresholds \(a_t=w_t/2\).

Section 2 proves that an assignment exists giving every type at least \(z_t^\infty/2\). Since \(w_t\le z_t^\infty\), the original thresholds \(a_t\) are feasible. The procedure therefore returns an exact assignment, and every used bundle satisfies

\[ v_t(b)\ge\frac9{10}\frac{w_t}{2} \ge\frac{729}{2000}z_t^\infty \ge\frac13z_t^\infty. \]

Its defining linear system gives exactly \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_qx_{t,b}=\lambda_q\). Thus every supply is allocated.

Choose a basic feasible solution of the final finite LP. It has at most \(d=\tau+m\) positive entries. Its matrix consists of integer columns bounded by \(C\), and its right-hand side is the original rational vector \(y\). Cramer’s rule gives polynomial encoding length for its nonzero masses; each bundle also has polynomial encoding length because \(b_q\le k_h\).

Combining the polynomial number of ellipsoid queries and benchmark queries with the bundle oracle’s enumeration bound yields

\[ (m+1)^{10}\operatorname{poly}\!\left(m,\tau,\ell,L,\sum_h\log(k_h+1)\right) \]

bit operations. This resolves both the existential and computational parts of the stated question.
