The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 2 — class: algorithmic

The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given valuation types \(t\) with masses \(\mu_t\), good types \(q\) partitioned into categories \(\mathcal Q_h\), cardinality limits \(k_h\), and per-capita supplies \(\lambda_q\), let \(\mathcal B\) be the integral bundles satisfying \(\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_h}b_q\le k_h\). Compute a mass assignment \(x_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_qx_{t,b}=\lambda_q\), together with the largest \(\alpha\) such that every used bundle satisfies \(v_t(b)\ge\alpha z_t^\infty\), where \(z_t^\infty=\max_\rho\min_{\rho_b>0}v_t(b)\) over distributions \(\rho\) on \(\mathcal B\) with the same per-capita supplies; decide whether \(\alpha\ge 1/3\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Definitions

Let \(T,Q\) be finite sets, with rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(\lambda_q\), nonnegative additive values \(v_t(b)=\sum_q b_qv_t(q)\), and a partition \(Q=\biguplus_h\mathcal Q_h\) with limits \(k_h\). Let \(\mathcal B=\{b\in\{0,1\}^Q:\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_h}b_q\le k_h\ \forall h\}\).
For each \(t\), let \(z_t^\infty=\max_\rho\min_{\rho_b>0}v_t(b)\), where \(\rho\) ranges over distributions on \(\mathcal B\) satisfying \(\sum_b\rho_bb_q=\lambda_q\) for every \(q\). A mass assignment has \(x_{t,b}\ge0\), \(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t,b}b_qx_{t,b}=\lambda_q\). It is \(\alpha\)-fair if \(x_{t,b}>0\) implies \(v_t(b)\ge\alpha z_t^\infty\).

Target

Is deciding whether a \(1/3\)-fair mass assignment exists solvable in polynomial time, or is it NP-hard?

Nearest known results

For finite-agent fair division with additive valuations and the same category-wise cardinality constraints, a \(1/3\)-MMS allocation is computable in polynomial time.
No result is verified here for this exact distribution-over-types, per-capita-supply formulation.

Prover output (gpt-6-astra / medium, Bolzano prover prompt)

Under the definitions as written, the decision problem is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hard. This uses the fact that no condition \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\) is imposed. With that additional normalization, every instance with well-defined benchmarks admits even a \(1\)-fair assignment. Both statements have direct proofs.

  • Hardness construction.

Reduce from the classical \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete problem \(\mathrm{PARTITION}\): given positive integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) with \(\sum_i a_i=2B\), determine whether some subset sums to \(B\). Restricting to even total sum preserves hardness: doubling every number transforms an unrestricted instance into an equivalent instance with even total sum. [Garey–Johnson, problem SP12](https://perso.limos.fr/~palafour/PAPERS/PDF/Garey-Johnson79.pdf).

Construct one type \(t\), with mass \(\mu_t=3\). Let \(Q=\{q_1,\ldots,q_n,q_*\}\), with one category containing all goods and limit \(n+1\). Thus every subset of \(Q\) is an admissible bundle. Identify bundles with their subsets, and set

\(\lambda_q=1\quad\text{for every }q\in Q,\qquad v_t(q_i)=a_i,\qquad v_t(q_*)=B.\)

All data have polynomial encoding length. The benchmark is well-defined because the distribution concentrated on \(Q\) has the required marginals. Ordinary mass feasibility also holds independently of the partition instance: assign mass one to \(Q\) and mass two to the empty bundle.

  • Exact computation of the benchmark.

Any distribution used to define \(z_t^\infty\) must include each good with probability one, since \(\lambda_q=1\). Because \(Q\) is finite, its only positive-probability bundle is consequently \(Q\). Hence

\(z_t^\infty=v_t(Q)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i+B=3B.\)

Therefore the fairness threshold is exactly \(z_t^\infty/3=B\).

  • A partition yields a fair assignment.

Suppose \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{i\in S}a_i=B\). Assign mass one to each of the three bundles

\(\{q_*\},\qquad \{q_i:i\in S\},\qquad \{q_i:i\notin S\}.\)

Their total mass is three, every good occurs in exactly one bundle, and every bundle has value \(B\). Thus all mass, supply, and fairness constraints hold.

  • A fair assignment yields a partition.

Conversely, let \(x_b=x_{t,b}\) be a fair mass assignment. Additivity and the supply equalities imply

\(\sum_b x_bv_t(b)=\sum_{q\in Q}v_t(q)\sum_{b\ni q}x_b=\sum_{q\in Q}v_t(q)=3B.\)

Since \(\sum_bx_b=3\), we obtain

\(\sum_b x_b\bigl(v_t(b)-B\bigr)=0.\)

Every summand is nonnegative: whenever \(x_b>0\), fairness requires \(v_t(b)\ge B\). Consequently every positive-mass bundle has value exactly \(B\).

The supply constraint for \(q_*\) gives \(\sum_{b\ni q_*}x_b=1\). Therefore

\(\sum_{b\not\ni q_*}x_b=3-1=2>0.\)

Choose a positive-mass bundle excluding \(q_*\). Its value is exactly \(B\), so its original goods identify a subset of the input integers summing to \(B\).

This proves the required equivalence and establishes \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness under the stated definitions.

  • Why normalized masses give a different answer.

Suppose additionally that \(\mu_t\ge0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and at least one distribution on \(\mathcal B\) has marginals \(\lambda\), so the benchmarks are defined.

For each type \(t\), an optimal benchmark distribution \(\rho^t\) exists. Indeed, \(\mathcal B\) is finite, and the minimum support value of any feasible distribution belongs to the finite set \(\{v_t(b):b\in\mathcal B\}\). The nonempty set of attainable minimum support values therefore has a largest element, attained by a feasible distribution.

Define \(x_{t,b}=\mu_t\rho^t_b\). Then

\(\sum_bx_{t,b}=\mu_t,\)

and, for every good \(q\),

\(\sum_{t,b}b_qx_{t,b}=\sum_t\mu_t\sum_b b_q\rho^t_b=\sum_t\mu_t\lambda_q=\lambda_q.\)

Moreover, \(x_{t,b}>0\) implies \(\rho^t_b>0\), and therefore \(v_t(b)\ge z_t^\infty\). The assignment is \(1\)-fair, hence also \(1/3\)-fair.

Thus, under normalization and the promise that the benchmarks are defined, the existence decision always answers yes. This is an existence proof; it does not assert that optimal benchmark distributions can be computed in polynomial time.
