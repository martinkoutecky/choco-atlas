The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given recurring indivisible good categories \(G\), valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(0\le\rho_g\le1\), and monotone XOS valuations \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{R}_+\) accessed through per-type demand and XOS oracles, find a feasible mass allocation \(x_{t,S}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:g\in S}x_{t,S}\le\rho_g\), with \(\operatorname{NSW}_{\infty}(x)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S)\right)\) under the usual zero convention; determine whether achieving \(\operatorname{NSW}_{\infty}(x)\ge\left(1-\frac1e+\varepsilon\right)\max_y\operatorname{NSW}_{\infty}(y)\) requires exponentially many per-type oracle queries, independently of the finite realization size \(N\).

The main new result is a polynomial-query algorithm attaining every fixed factor strictly below $e^{-1/e}$. Its proof occupies Sections 2–4. Section 5 gives a self-contained proof of the lower bound above that factor.

1. The logarithmic formulation and the distinction from discrete allocation

All logarithms below are natural. Write
$$ F_v(x)=\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}\log v_t(S) $$
when every bundle receiving positive mass has positive value.

Restricting the allocation variables to positive-valued bundles produces a nonempty compact polytope: nonemptiness follows from the promise $\mathrm{OPT}>0$. On this polytope, $F_v$ is a continuous linear function. Consequently,
$$ \log\mathrm{OPT}=\max_x F_v(x). $$

The discrete query lower bound does not automatically apply to this polytope. The soundness argument in [Barman et al., Section 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.00767) uses disjointness of discrete bundles.

For example, on four goods consider
$$ v_1(S)=\max\{|S\cap\{1,2\}|,\ |S\cap\{3,4\}|\}, $$
$$ v_2(S)=\max\{|S\cap\{1,3\}|,\ |S\cap\{2,4\}|\}. $$
Every pair giving the first agent value $2$ intersects every pair giving the second agent value $2$. Thus the discrete optimum for two agents is $\sqrt2$.

In contrast, with type masses $1/2$ and supplies $1/2$, the mass allocation
$$ x_{1,\{1,2\}}=x_{1,\{3,4\}} =x_{2,\{1,3\}}=x_{2,\{2,4\}}=\frac14 $$
is feasible and gives every individual value $2$. Its welfare is $2$, which is optimal.

A separate argument for mass allocations is therefore necessary.

2. An approximate logarithmic demand oracle

We first prove the algorithmic lemma needed for the mass allocation LP.

Lemma. Let $v$ be a normalized, monotone XOS valuation, and let $p\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^m$. Suppose some bundle has positive value. For every rational $\eta\in(0,1)$, demand and XOS queries suffice to find a positive-valued bundle $S$ satisfying
$$ \log v(S)-p(S) \geq \max_{A:v(A)>0}\{\log v(A)-p(A)\} -\frac1e-\eta. $$
If $B_p$ bounds the encoding length of $p$, the query count and running time are at most
$$ (m+1)^{O(1/\eta)} \operatorname{poly}\bigl(m,L,B_p,\log(1/\eta)\bigr). $$
All queries and responses have polynomial encoding length. Arbitrary oracle tie-breaking is permitted.

Here $p(A)=\sum_{g\in A}p_g$.

2.1 An optimal bundle contains few expensive goods

Set
$$ \gamma=\eta/2, \qquad E=\{g:p_g>\gamma\}. $$

Let $S^*$ maximize logarithmic demand, let $V=v(S^*)>0$, and choose a maximizing additive clause $w$ on $S^*$. Thus
$$ \sum_{g\in S^*}w_g=V. $$

Every coefficient $w_g$ with $g\in S^*$ is nonnegative. Indeed, if $w_g<0$, then
$$ v(S^*\setminus\{g\}) \geq V-w_g>V, $$
contradicting monotonicity.

There are two cases.

Case 1: $w_g\leq V/2$ for every $g\in S^*$.

The set $S^*\setminus\{g\}$ has value at least $V-w_g>0$. Optimality of $S^*$ therefore gives
$$ p_g \leq \log\frac{V}{v(S^*\setminus\{g\})} \leq -\log\left(1-\frac{w_g}{V}\right). $$
For $u\in[0,1/2]$, integration of $1/(1-u)\leq2$ gives
$$ -\log(1-u)\leq2u. $$
Hence
$$ p(S^*)\leq 2\sum_{g\in S^*}\frac{w_g}{V}=2. $$
It follows that $|S^*\cap E|<2/\gamma$.

Case 2: $w_g>V/2$ for some $g\in S^*$.

Since $v(\{g\})\geq w_g>V/2$, comparison with the singleton $\{g\}$ gives
$$ p(S^*\setminus\{g\}) \leq \log\frac{V}{v(\{g\})} <\log2. $$
Thus
$$ |S^*\cap E|\leq1+\frac{\log2}{\gamma} <1+\frac2\gamma. $$

In both cases, the set
$$ H^*=S^*\cap E $$
has size at most
$$ K=\left\lceil\frac2\gamma\right\rceil+1. $$

The algorithm enumerates every $H\subseteq E$ of size at most $K$. For each such $H$, it considers only bundles in
$$ \mathcal D_H=\{H\cup A:A\subseteq G\setminus E\}. $$
The domain corresponding to $H^*$ contains $S^*$.

There are at most $(m+1)^{K+1}$ domains.

2.2 A concave relaxation on one domain

Fix an enumerated $H$. Let $C=G\setminus E$ and define the residual cost
$$ c(S)=p(S\setminus H), \qquad S\in\mathcal D_H. $$
Every optional good in $C$ has price at most $\gamma$. The fixed cost $p(H)$ will be subtracted at the end.

If $v(H\cup C)=0$, this domain has no positive-valued bundle and is skipped. Otherwise consider
$$ \mathcal K_H= \operatorname{conv}\{(v(S),c(S)):S\in\mathcal D_H\}. $$
Maximize
$$ \log V-C' $$
over $(V,C')\in\mathcal K_H$ with $V>0$.

A maximizer exists. The hull is compact, it contains a point with positive first coordinate, and the objective tends to $-\infty$ as $V$ tends to zero. Let $\lambda>0$ be the first coordinate of a maximizer.

The first-order optimality condition says that the maximizer lies on the face maximizing
$$ v(S)-\lambda c(S). $$
Conversely, any convex combination of maximizers of this expression whose mean value is $\lambda$ maximizes the concave relaxation. This follows from
$$ \log z\leq\log\lambda+\frac{z-\lambda}{\lambda}. $$

Strict concavity of $\log V$ implies that every optimum of the relaxation has the same first coordinate $\lambda$.

Consequently, either one demanded point has value exactly $\lambda$, or there are demanded bundles $S^-$ and $S^+$ such that
$$ v(S^-)\leq\lambda\leq v(S^+). $$
Define
$$ b=\frac{v(S^-)}{\lambda}, \qquad a=\frac{v(S^+)}{\lambda}, $$
and their common normalized demand profit
$$ r= \frac{v(S^-)}{\lambda}-c(S^-) = \frac{v(S^+)}{\lambda}-c(S^+). $$
Because $H$ is an available bundle of residual cost zero,
$$ r\geq \frac{v(H)}{\lambda}\geq0. $$
Since $c(S^-)\geq0$,
$$ r\leq b\leq1. $$

The relaxation optimum is
$$ M_H=\log\lambda-1+r. $$

2.3 Finding these demanded bundles with polynomially many queries

A demand query maximizing $v(S)-s c(S)$ on $\mathcal D_H$ can be implemented through the original demand oracle:

  • give goods in $H$ a sufficiently large negative price;
  • give goods outside $H\cup C$ a price greater than $v(G)$;
  • give goods in $C$ price $s p_g$.

Monotonicity forces inclusion of $H$. Removing a forbidden good improves demand profit because its price exceeds the maximum possible value. Thus the returned set belongs to $\mathcal D_H$ and maximizes the required expression.

The value of each returned bundle is recovered by one XOS query.

Here is a finite-bit procedure for finding $\lambda$ and the adjacent demanded bundles. Let
$$ U=v(H\cup C)>0, \qquad C_{\mathrm{all}}=p(C). $$
At
$$ s_{\mathrm{lo}}=\frac{U}{4(1+C_{\mathrm{all}})}, $$
a demand maximizer has value at least $3U/4>s_{\mathrm{lo}}$: the full bundle has profit at least $3U/4$, and residual costs are nonnegative. At $s_{\mathrm{hi}}=2U$, every bundle has value less than $s_{\mathrm{hi}}$.

Binary search now uses the comparison between the returned value and $s$. The validity of this search follows from the supporting-face characterization above. If a demanded value exceeds $s$, then $s\leq\lambda$; if it is less than $s$, then $s\geq\lambda$. Equality yields a demanded point attaining the relaxation optimum directly.

For completeness, exact recovery does not require searching to an unspecified real precision. Every bundle value and residual cost has a uniform polynomial bit bound. Therefore the same holds for every rational in
$$ \{v(S):S\in\mathcal D_H\} $$
and every defined ratio
$$ \frac{v(A)-v(B)}{c(A)-c(B)}. $$
Let $D$ be a known polynomial bound on their numerator and denominator lengths. Distinct such rationals are separated by at least $2^{-2D}$.

The number $\lambda$ belongs to this collection: it is either a demanded value or the slope of a supporting segment. After polynomially many bisections, the bracket has width less than $2^{-2D-2}$ and hence contains only one distinct candidate.

If its endpoint demand points have equal value, they also have equal cost, and $\lambda$ is that common value. Otherwise their lines intersect at
$$ \lambda=\frac{v(S^+)-v(S^-)}{c(S^+)-c(S^-)}. $$
Both endpoint points remain maximizing at this intersection. If either ceased maximizing earlier inside the bracket, another intersection ratio would occur there, contradicting uniqueness of the candidate.

At the intersection, their values bracket $\lambda$. This also follows from the supporting-face characterization: the values of other points tied there lie between the values selected on its two sides.

Thus the required data are found exactly with polynomially many polynomial-bit queries, regardless of ties.

2.4 Rounding the large demanded bundle

If a demanded bundle has value exactly $\lambda$, its logarithmic utility equals $M_H$, and no rounding is needed.

Otherwise take an XOS clause $w$ maximizing on $S^+$. Its coefficients on $S^+$ are nonnegative by monotonicity. Write
$$ h_0=\frac{w(H)}{\lambda}. $$
Since $w(H)\leq v(H)$,
$$ 0\leq h_0\leq r. $$

If $h_0\geq1$, then $v(H)\geq\lambda$. As $r\leq1$,
$$ \log v(H)\geq\log\lambda\geq M_H. $$
So $H$ itself suffices.

Suppose instead that $h_0<1$. The optional goods in $S^+\setminus H$ have total clause value $\lambda(a-h_0)$ and total cost $a-r$.

Choose a fractional subset of these goods with clause value exactly $\lambda(1-h_0)$, minimizing cost. Sorting by cost per unit of clause value gives an optimum with at most one fractional good. Its cost is at most that of taking the same fraction of every optional good:
$$ \frac{1-h_0}{a-h_0}(a-r)\leq1-h_0\leq1, $$
where the first inequality uses $r\geq h_0$.

Round the single fractional good upward. Its price is at most $\gamma$. The resulting bundle $B\in\mathcal D_H$ satisfies
$$ v(B)\geq\lambda, \qquad c(B)\leq1+\gamma. $$
Therefore
$$ \log v(B)-c(B)\geq\log\lambda-1-\gamma. $$
Its loss against $M_H$ is at most $r+\gamma$.

If $b>0$, the small demanded bundle has loss
$$ M_H-\bigl(\log v(S^-)-c(S^-)\bigr) =b-1-\log b. $$
For $b\in(0,1]$,
$$ \min\{b,\ b-1-\log b\}\leq\frac1e. $$
Indeed, for $b\leq1/e$ the first expression is at most $1/e$. For $b\geq1/e$, the second expression is decreasing and equals $1/e$ at $b=1/e$.

Using $r\leq b$, the better of $B$ and $S^-$ consequently has loss at most $1/e+\gamma$. If $b=0$, then $r=0$, and $B$ has loss at most $\gamma$.

Subtracting the fixed cost $p(H)$ preserves every comparison. Comparing all positive-valued candidates using rational logarithm approximations of absolute error at most $\eta/4$ introduces at most $\eta/2$ further loss. Since $\gamma=\eta/2$, the total loss is at most
$$ \frac1e+\eta. $$

One enumerated domain contains a globally optimal logarithmic-demand bundle. Its relaxation upper-bounds that bundle’s utility. The returned candidate therefore proves the lemma.

3. Turning logarithmic demand into an exactly feasible allocation

We next show how the lemma solves the allocation problem approximately.

Theorem. For every fixed $\alpha<e^{-1/e}$, a deterministic algorithm returns a feasible rational allocation $x$ satisfying
$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(x)\geq\alpha\,\mathrm{OPT}. $$
Put
$$ \Delta=-\log\alpha-\frac1e>0. $$
The total oracle count across all types, and the running time, are bounded by
$$ \tau(m+1)^{O(1/\Delta)} \operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,1/\Delta). $$
The allocation has at most $m+\tau$ nonzero coordinates, each of polynomial encoding length.

3.1 A feasible initial set of columns

Goods of supply zero can be removed. Restrictions of valuations to the remaining goods can be implemented with the original oracles by assigning excluded goods sufficiently large prices in demand queries.

For any XOS valuation,
$$ v(S)\leq\sum_{g\in S}v(\{g\}). $$
To verify this, choose a maximizing clause on $S$; each coefficient on a good is at most its singleton value.

Thus every positive-valued bundle contains a positive-valued singleton. Starting from any positive-welfare feasible allocation, replace each allocated bundle by one such singleton. Type masses are preserved and consumption of every good weakly decreases.

It follows that a feasible allocation using only positive-valued singletons exists. Recover all singleton values with $\tau m$ XOS queries and solve the resulting explicit rational feasibility LP. Its columns will be included throughout the algorithm.

3.2 Rational objective coefficients

Choose a dyadic rational $h$ satisfying
$$ 0<h<\min\{1/10,\Delta/10\}. $$
For every positive-valued bundle define, by a fixed deterministic computation, a dyadic coefficient $c_{t,S}$ such that
$$ |c_{t,S}-\log v_t(S)|\leq h. $$
These coefficients need only be computed when their bundles are queried.

Every positive bundle value is a sum of at most $m$ polynomial-bit rationals. Hence the coefficients have a uniform polynomial bit bound and satisfy
$$ |c_{t,S}|\leq B $$
for a known integer $B$ polynomial in $m,L,\log(1/h)$.

Consider the rational configuration LP
$$ \max_x \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}c_{t,S} $$
with the original allocation constraints and only positive-valued bundles.

Because every feasible allocation has total mass one, its rational objective differs from $F_v(x)$ by at most $h$.

The dual is
$$ \min_{p,z} \sum_g\rho_gp_g+\sum_t\mu_tz_t $$
subject to
$$ p_g\geq0, \qquad z_t+p(S)\geq c_{t,S} $$
for every positive-valued bundle of every type.

Apply the logarithmic-demand lemma with accuracy $h$ to each type at prices $p$. If it returns $S_t$, then
$$ c_{t,S_t}-p(S_t) \geq \max_{S:v_t(S)>0}\{c_{t,S}-p(S)\}-\kappa, $$
where
$$ \kappa=\frac1e+3h. $$
The additional $2h$ comes from approximating the logarithms of the returned and optimal bundles.

If a returned column violates its dual constraint, add that exact rational constraint as a cut. If no returned column violates, then
$$ \max_S\{c_{t,S}-p(S)\}\leq z_t+\kappa $$
for every type. Therefore $(p,z+\kappa\mathbf1)$ is feasible for the full dual. Since $\sum_t\mu_t=1$, its objective exceeds the tested objective by exactly $\kappa$.

3.3 Recovering primal columns rather than merely a dual estimate

Here are the feasibility and recovery details.

We use the standard rational-LP ellipsoid feasibility theorem: in a fixed dimension, with uniform bounds on rational inequality encoding lengths and a bounding box, a cut-based feasibility procedure makes polynomially many polynomial-bit queries. This is the rational-polyhedral application of the [ellipsoid separation method](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/10046/10046D.pdf).

We also need the following consequence. Suppose the procedure is interrupted whenever a supplied oracle accepts a queried point. If it instead finishes by declaring infeasibility, the inequalities actually returned, together with the explicit inequalities, form an infeasible subsystem. To verify this consequence, replay the computation with an exact separation oracle for that finite subsystem, returning the same violated inequality at every query. The computation is unchanged. Feasibility of the subsystem would contradict correctness of the exact feasibility procedure.

A uniform polynomial-bit bounding box can be used for every restricted dual containing the initial singleton columns. Indeed:

  • Its primal is nonempty and bounded, so its dual has an attained finite optimum.
  • Its dual polyhedron is pointed. A lineality direction has zero price coordinates because of $p\geq0$, and then zero type coordinates because each type has at least one column.
  • Hence an optimal dual vertex exists.
  • Its defining matrix has entries in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and polynomial-bit right-hand sides. Cramer’s rule and Hadamard’s inequality give a uniform polynomial bit bound on its coordinates.

Choose a box containing all these possible optimal vertices.

For a rational target $q$, run the feasibility procedure on the boxed dual with the additional constraint
$$ \sum_g\rho_gp_g+\sum_t\mu_tz_t\leq q. $$
Check the explicit constraints and the initial singleton constraints exactly. Check other configuration constraints with the approximate oracle above.

There are two outcomes.

Acceptance. The shifted point is feasible for the full dual, so the full rational primal optimum is at most
$$ q+\kappa. $$

Infeasibility. The recorded columns’ restricted dual cannot have objective at most $q$. Otherwise its optimum would lie in the chosen box and satisfy every recorded inequality. By LP duality, the restricted primal optimum is greater than $q$.

Now test targets on a grid of spacing $h$, starting at $-B$ and ending at or above $B$, retaining all recorded columns. Acceptance must occur: at a target at least $B$, a full optimal dual point exists in the box, so infeasibility cannot be certified.

At the first accepted target $q$, the accumulated restricted primal has optimum at least $q-h$, unless acceptance occurs at the first target, in which case its optimum is at least $-B=q$. Thus, solving that explicit restricted primal exactly returns $\widehat x$ with
$$ \sum_{t,S}\widehat x_{t,S}c_{t,S} \geq \mathrm{OPT}_c-\kappa-h, $$
where $\mathrm{OPT}_c$ denotes the full rational primal optimum.

The grid has polynomially many points. Each feasibility run makes polynomially many calls to the approximate separation procedure. Every such procedure invokes the lemma for at most $\tau$ types. This proves the stated total query and running-time bounds.

3.4 Welfare guarantee and output representation

Accounting for both rational-objective errors gives
$$ F_v(\widehat x) \geq \log\mathrm{OPT}-\kappa-3h = \log\mathrm{OPT}-\frac1e-6h. $$
Since $6h<\Delta$,
$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widehat x) \geq e^{-1/e-6h}\mathrm{OPT} > \alpha\,\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Return an optimal basic feasible solution of the final restricted primal. After adding supply slacks, the system has $m+\tau$ equality rows and a matrix with entries in $\{0,1\}$. Therefore at most $m+\tau$ allocation coordinates are positive.

Clearing the input denominators and applying Cramer’s rule gives each returned mass encoding length
$$ O\bigl(L+(m+\tau)\log(m+\tau)\bigr). $$
The allocation constraints are satisfied exactly. No supply relaxation or rounding of masses is required.

4. Consequence for the previously unresolved interval

For the requested factor,
$$ \alpha=1-\frac1e+\varepsilon. $$
The inequality $\alpha<e^{-1/e}$ is equivalent to
$$ 0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_*. $$

The theorem therefore proves polynomial query sufficiency throughout this open interval, with success probability one.

More generally, the algorithm attains every fixed welfare factor strictly below $e^{-1/e}$. Its dependence on the distance from that factor is explicit through
$$ \Delta=-\log\left(1-\frac1e+\varepsilon\right)-\frac1e. $$

At $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_*$, this quantity is zero. The proof consequently provides approximations approaching the requested factor, but does not establish the factor itself.

5. Exponential necessity above $e^{-1/e}$

We reproduce the mass-specific lower bound, including arbitrary prices, adaptive queries, randomized algorithms, and unrestricted output support.

Theorem. Fix $\alpha>e^{-1/e}$. Any randomized algorithm that succeeds with probability at least $2/3$ on every promised instance requires
$$ 2^{\Omega_\alpha(m)} $$
total demand and XOS queries in the worst case. This holds with one type and $L=O_\alpha(m)$.

5.1 Construction and welfare separation

Choose dyadic constants
$$ a\in(2,3),\qquad \delta\in(0,1/4), \qquad b=a-1+\delta $$
such that
$$ d=\log(\alpha a)-b/e>0. $$
Such choices exist: as $a\to e$ and $\delta\to0$, the last expression tends to
$$ \log\alpha+\frac1e>0. $$
Continuity and density of dyadic rationals justify the choice.

Put
$$ \theta=\frac{\delta}{4a}, \qquad C=\frac{2a}{\delta}, $$
and choose a power of two $R$ with
$$ R\geq\frac{4eC}{\theta}. $$
All these constants depend only on $\alpha$.

Let $m=Rk$ for sufficiently large $k$. There is one type of mass one, and every good has supply $1/R$. Hence every feasible allocation satisfies
$$ \sum_Sx_S|S|\leq k. $$

The baseline valuation is
$$ v_0(\varnothing)=0, \qquad v_0(S)=\max\{1,b|S|/k\}\quad(S\ne\varnothing). $$
Its representation contains the singleton clauses of coefficient one and the uniform clause of coefficient $b/k$.

Choose a uniformly random ordered partition
$$ \Pi=(T_1,\ldots,T_R) $$
into blocks of size $k$, and add a clause of coefficient $a/k$ on each block. The hidden valuation is
$$ v_\Pi(S)= \max\left\{ v_0(S),\frac ak|S\cap T_1|,\ldots,\frac ak|S\cap T_R| \right\}. $$
These are normalized, monotone, rational XOS valuations. Supplies are dyadic, coefficient lengths are $O_\alpha(\log m)$, and explicit input length is $O_\alpha(m)$.

For $z>0$,
$$ \log z\leq z/e, $$
because $\log z-z/e$ has maximum zero at $z=e$. Thus, for every nonempty $S$,
$$ \log v_0(S) =\max\{0,\log(b|S|/k)\} \leq \frac{b|S|}{ek}. $$
Every positive-welfare feasible baseline allocation therefore satisfies
$$ F_{v_0}(x)\leq b/e, \qquad \mathrm{OPT}_0\leq e^{b/e}. $$

For each hidden partition, allocating mass $1/R$ to each $T_i$ is feasible and gives every individual value $a$. Consequently,
$$ \mathrm{OPT}_\Pi\geq a, \qquad \alpha a>e^{b/e}. $$

The baseline also satisfies the promise: mass $1/m$ on every singleton is feasible and has welfare one.

5.2 A counting bound

Let $T$ be a uniformly random $k$-subset of a ground set of size $Rk$. For fixed $J$ with $|J|\leq Ck$,
$$ \Pr[|T\cap J|>\theta k]\leq4^{-\theta k}. $$

To prove this, put $r=\lceil\theta k\rceil$. For any specified $r$ positions in a uniformly sampled ordered $k$-subset, the probability that all lie in $J$ is at most $(|J|/(Rk))^r$. Therefore
$$ \Pr[|T\cap J|\geq r] \leq \binom{k}{r}\left(\frac{|J|}{Rk}\right)^r \leq \left(\frac{eC}{R\theta}\right)^r \leq4^{-r} \leq4^{-\theta k}. $$

Every partition block has this marginal distribution. A union bound over blocks gives
$$ \beta_k=R4^{-\theta k}. $$
No independence between blocks is required.

5.3 XOS queries rarely expose a hidden clause

Fix a queried set $S$. Strict improvement over the baseline requires some block $T_i$ to satisfy
$$ \frac ak|S\cap T_i|>v_0(S). $$
For nonempty $S$, this implies
$$ |S\cap T_i|>k/a, \qquad |S|<ak/b. $$
The parameter choices give $|S|<Ck$ and $k/a>\theta k$. The counting bound therefore limits the probability of strict improvement to $\beta_k$.

For $S=\varnothing$, strict improvement is impossible.

Whenever no strict improvement occurs, the baseline maximizing clause remains a legal response in the fixed hidden representation. Ties can preserve the baseline response.

5.4 Demand queries rarely expose a hidden clause, even at negative prices

Fix any rational price vector $p$, and put
$$ q_g=\max\{p_g,0\}, \qquad Z=\sum_g\max\{-p_g,0\}. $$

For an additive clause with nonnegative coefficients $w$, maximum demand profit is
$$ \sum_g(w_g-p_g)_+. $$
Subtracting $Z$ gives
$$ \sum_g(w_g-q_g)_+. $$
Thus the normalized baseline profit is
$$ D_0= \max\left\{ \max_g(1-q_g)_+,\ \sum_g(b/k-q_g)_+ \right\}, $$
and a hidden block has normalized profit
$$ H_T=\sum_{g\in T}(a/k-q_g)_+. $$

A baseline demand maximizer remains valid unless some block satisfies $H_T>D_0$.

Define the query-dependent set
$$ J=\{g:q_g<(b-\delta/2)/k\}. $$
Suppose $H_T>D_0$. Since $H_T\leq a$ and
$$ D_0\geq |J|\delta/(2k), $$
we obtain $|J|<Ck$.

Also $H_T>0$, so some $q_g<a/k$. For large enough $k$,
$$ D_0\geq1-a/k. $$
For goods outside $J$,
$$ (a/k-q_g)_+\leq(1-\delta/2)/k. $$
Therefore
$$ H_T\leq1-\delta/2+\frac ak|T\cap J|. $$
Taking $k\geq4a/\delta$ and combining with $H_T>D_0$ yields
$$ \frac ak|T\cap J| >\frac\delta2-\frac ak \geq\frac\delta4. $$
Hence $|T\cap J|>\theta k$.

The counting bound again gives probability at most $\beta_k$. If $|J|\geq Ck$, the necessary condition already rules out strict improvement.

This removes negative prices exactly through their common contribution $Z$; it does not restrict demand queries to nonnegative prices.

5.5 A large output support does not circumvent the lower bound

Fix any feasible allocation $x$ independent of $\Pi$. Positive empty-bundle mass makes its welfare zero on every constructed instance. Otherwise define
$$ \Delta_\Pi(x) = F_{v_\Pi}(x)-F_{v_0}(x). $$
For every nonempty $S$,
$$ 0\leq \log\frac{v_\Pi(S)}{v_0(S)} \leq\log a. $$
The term is nonzero only on the strict-improvement event from Section 5.3. Since $\sum_Sx_S=1$,
$$ \mathbb E_\Pi[\Delta_\Pi(x)] \leq(\log a)\beta_k. $$

Successful $\alpha$-approximation requires
$$ F_{v_\Pi}(x)\geq\log(\alpha a). $$
The baseline bound then implies $\Delta_\Pi(x)\geq d$. Markov’s inequality gives
$$ \Pr_\Pi[x\text{ succeeds}] \leq\frac{(\log a)\beta_k}{d}. $$
This bound allows arbitrary support size and even real allocation masses.

5.6 Adaptive randomized algorithms and legal tie-breaking

Fix deterministic baseline oracles. For each hidden instance, use legal oracles that preserve the baseline response whenever it remains valid, and otherwise select a valid response deterministically.

Fix the random coins of an algorithm making at most $Q$ calls. Its baseline query sequence and baseline output are independent of $\Pi$. Any first transcript departure occurs at a query on that sequence. Sections 5.3–5.4 and a union bound give
$$ \Pr_\Pi[\text{transcript departure}]\leq Q\beta_k. $$

Without departure, the output is the fixed baseline output. Section 5.5 bounds the unconditional probability that this fixed output succeeds. Thus
$$ \Pr_\Pi[\text{success}] \leq Q\beta_k+\frac{(\log a)\beta_k}{d}. $$
Averaging over random coins preserves the inequality.

If success probability is at least $2/3$ for every hidden instance, then
$$ Q\geq\frac{2}{3\beta_k}-\frac{\log a}{d}. $$
Since $m=Rk$,
$$ \beta_k = R\,2^{-(2\theta/R)m}. $$
This proves the exponential lower bound.

6. An exponential upper bound for every requested factor

For completeness, an exhaustive algorithm applies throughout the entire requested interval, including the unresolved endpoint.

Query every nonempty bundle of every type with the XOS oracle. Summing the returned coefficients on the queried bundle recovers its value exactly. This uses
$$ \tau(2^m-1) $$
calls.

Retain only positive-valued bundles. Approximate each logarithmic coefficient to absolute error at most $\zeta/2$, and solve the resulting rational LP exactly. Because total allocation mass is one, its optimizer $\widehat x$ satisfies
$$ F_v(\widehat x)\geq\log\mathrm{OPT}-\zeta. $$
Therefore
$$ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\widehat x)\geq e^{-\zeta}\mathrm{OPT}. $$

Choose a dyadic $\zeta$ with
$$ 0<\zeta<(1-\alpha)/2. $$
Then $e^{-\zeta}\geq1-\zeta>\alpha$.

An optimal basic feasible solution has at most $m+\tau$ nonzero allocation coordinates, with the polynomial mass encoding bound proved in Section 3.4. Running time is
$$ 2^{O(m)} \operatorname{poly}\left( m,\tau,L, 1+\log\frac1{1/e-\varepsilon} \right). $$

For every fixed $\varepsilon>\varepsilon_*$, Sections 5–6 consequently establish exponential worst-case query complexity in $m$.

7. Precisely what remains open

The polynomial-query approximation factors have supremum $e^{-1/e}$: every strictly smaller fixed factor is attainable, while every strictly larger fixed factor requires exponentially many queries.

The proof does not determine whether that supremum is attainable with polynomially many queries. At
$$ \varepsilon=\varepsilon_*, \qquad \alpha=e^{-1/e}, $$
the algorithm supplies every strictly smaller factor, and the lower bound supplies no obstruction at equality.

Thus the target question is settled for every allowed $\varepsilon$ except this endpoint.
