The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite cost types \(T\), rational population masses \(\mu\), chore categories \(E\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho\) with \(\sum_{e\in E}\rho_e\le 2\), and additive costs \(c_t(e)\), find a finite-support rational assignment \(x_{t,b}\ge 0\), indexed by \(t\in T\) and \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{E}\), satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_e x_{t,b}=\rho_e\), such that whenever \(x_{t,b}>0\) and \(x_{u,d}>0\), every individual receiving \(b\) satisfies \(c_t(b\setminus\{e\})\le c_t(d)\) for every chore copy \(e\) in \(b\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(T\) of \(\tau\) cost types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
There are \(m=|E|\) chore categories, nonnegative binary-rational supplies \(\rho_e\) with \(\sum_{e\in E}\rho_e\le 2\), and nonnegative binary-rational additive costs \(c_t(e)\); zero costs are allowed. Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An individual bundle is a multiplicity vector \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\), including the empty bundle; its cost is \(c_t(b)=\sum_{e\in E}b_e c_t(e)\). Removing one copy of category \(e\) gives \(b-\mathbf1_e\).
An assignment is a finitely supported family of nonnegative rational masses \(x_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_e x_{t,b}=\rho_e\). It is EFX if, for every \((t,b),(u,d)\) with \(x_{t,b}>0\) and \(x_{u,d}>0\), and every \(e\) with \(b_e>0\), \(c_t(b-\mathbf1_e)\le c_t(d)\). This includes comparisons between individuals of the same type and removal of zero-cost copies.

Is finding an EFX assignment, or reporting that none exists, solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this computational problem NP-hard?

For finitely many agents with nonnegative additive costs, an EFX allocation always exists and is computable in polynomial time when the number of chores is at most twice the number of agents ([Kobayashi–Mahara–Sakamoto](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04168)). The [journal version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2024.115010) retains this algorithmic result. No result settling the exact mass-assignment problem above was located.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total population mass to one so the per-capita supply bound faithfully represents the discrete bound of two chores per agent.
  • Required a nonempty support with positive binary-rational masses and nonnegative binary-rational costs and supplies; retained zero costs as allowed by the discrete definition.
  • Interpreted category bundles as multisets and removal as deletion of exactly one copy, including zero-cost copies; allowed empty bundles and comparisons within the same type.
  • Made the search problem total by allowing a report of nonexistence, since continuous existence was not supplied as a promise; represented an assignment by its finite list of positive entries with binary-encoded multiplicities and rational masses.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result. The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), including when costs are zero. Every instance satisfying the stated hypotheses has an EFX assignment, so the algorithm never needs to report nonexistence.

Clearing denominators and applying the [Kobayashi–Mahara–Sakamoto theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04168) already proves existence, but may create exponentially many agents and chores. The construction below avoids that expansion. It combines batched greedy insertions with a compression lemma that handles zero-cost copies explicitly.

Throughout, bundles and masses are represented in binary. Zero-mass entries are omitted.

2. Preliminary cases and the invariant. Write \(R=\sum_e\rho_e\).

If \(R\le 1\), set
\(x_{t,\mathbf1_e}=\mu_t\rho_e\) and \(x_{t,\mathbf0}=\mu_t(1-R)\).
These masses meet every marginal constraint. Every nonempty bundle is a singleton, so every required removal leaves cost zero; hence the assignment is EFX.

If every cost is zero, another immediate construction is
\(x_{t,2\mathbf1_e}=\mu_t\rho_e/2\) and
\(x_{t,\mathbf0}=\mu_t(1-R/2)\).
Thus assume henceforth that \(1<R\le2\) and some cost is positive.

Define
\(C=\max_{t,e}c_t(e)>0\),
\(\gamma=\min\{c_t(e):c_t(e)>0\}>0\), and
\(H=1+\lceil 3C/\gamma\rceil\).
The binary encoding length of \(H\) is polynomial in \(L\).

An intermediate state allocates supplies \(\sigma_e\), where \(0\le\sigma_e\le\rho_e\). For each type \(t\), a prescribed mass \(q_t\) is designated *active*. We maintain:

  • the assignment is EFX and meets the type masses \(\mu_t\);
  • every supported bundle is nonempty;
  • active individuals hold singletons;
  • the active mass of type \(t\) is exactly \(q_t\).

An active or inactive designation is bookkeeping; it does not alter costs or the EFX comparisons.

For a bundle distribution of total mass one, define
\(a_t=\min\{c_t(b):b\text{ has positive mass}\}\).
EFX is exactly the condition that each bundle assigned to type \(t\) satisfies
\(c_t(b)-c_t(e)\le a_t\) for every \(e\) with \(b_e>0\).

Two bounds will be used repeatedly. First, minimum cost is at most average cost, so
\(a_t\le\sum_e\sigma_e c_t(e)\le2C\).
Second, for any nonempty bundle assigned to type \(t\), EFX gives
\(c_t(b)\le a_t+c_t(e)\le3C\),
using any category \(e\) occurring in \(b\).
These statements allow zero costs.

3. Compression lemma, including zero costs. Suppose an intermediate state satisfying the invariant is given by finitely many rational positive entries. Suppose also that \(\sigma\) and \(q\) have encoding lengths polynomial in the original input length.

We will replace it by another state with the same \(\sigma,q,\mu\), using at most
\((2\tau+m)(m+1)\)
positive entries. Its entire encoding length is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) and the number of entries supplied to the compression procedure. Crucially, this bound does not depend on the sizes of the original bundle coordinates.

Treat active and inactive individuals as separate rows. There are at most \(2\tau\) rows, with required masses \(q_t\) and \(\mu_t-q_t\).

Index the given positive entries by \(j\). Entry \(j\) has row \(v_j\), underlying type \(t_j\), bundle \(b^j\), and mass \(y_j>0\). Define its truncated bundle by
\(\bar b^j_e=\min\{b^j_e,H\}\).

We first establish three properties of this truncation.

*Every truncated bundle remains above all comparison thresholds.* For every type \(u\),
\(c_u(\bar b^j)\ge a_u\).
Indeed, if no truncated coordinate has positive cost for \(u\), truncation does not change its cost for \(u\). Otherwise, one retained coordinate alone contributes at least
\(H\gamma>3C\ge a_u\).

*Every deleted copy has zero cost for its recipient.* If \(b^j_e>H\) and \(c_{t_j}(e)>0\), then
\(c_{t_j}(b^j)\ge b^j_e\gamma>3C\),
contradicting the bound from Section 2. Consequently,
\(c_{t_j}(\bar b^j)=c_{t_j}(b^j)\).
Truncation also preserves which categories occur, because \(H\ge1\). Thus \(\bar b^j\) still satisfies every recipient removal inequality against \(a_{t_j}\).

*If any copies are deleted, the recipient's cost equals its threshold.* A deleted copy has zero recipient cost. Removing that copy in the original EFX assignment gives
\(c_{t_j}(b^j)\le a_{t_j}\).
The reverse inequality follows from the definition of \(a_{t_j}\). Hence, whenever \(b^j\ne\bar b^j\),
\(c_{t_j}(\bar b^j)=a_{t_j}\).
Moreover, such an entry is inactive, because an active bundle is a singleton.

For entry \(j\), allow a surplus variable \(z_{j,e}\) precisely when the entry is inactive and
\(c_{t_j}(e)=0\) and \(c_{t_j}(\bar b^j)=a_{t_j}\).
All other surplus variables are absent.

The original assignment proves feasibility of the following linear equations, with nonnegative variables \(x_j,z_{j,e}\):

  • for each row \(v\), \(\sum_{j:v_j=v}x_j=r_v\), where \(r_v\) is its prescribed active or inactive mass;
  • for each category \(e\),

\(\sum_j\bar b^j_e x_j+\sum_j z_{j,e}=\sigma_e\).

Specifically, take \(x_j=y_j\) and, for allowed variables,
\(z_{j,e}=y_j(b^j_e-\bar b^j_e)\).
The properties just proved ensure that every nonzero surplus is allowed.

Now perform two rational linear programs.

First, maximize \(\eta\), subject to these equations, nonnegativity, and \(x_j\ge\eta\) for every \(j\). The original feasible assignment shows that the optimum \(\eta_*\) is positive. The feasible region is bounded: \(x_j\le1\), \(z_{j,e}\le2\), and \(\eta\le1\). Its coefficients are bounded by \(H\); its right-hand sides are the prescribed marginals. Therefore standard rational linear programming gives an exact optimum \(\eta_*>0\) of polynomial encoding length.

Set \(M=2/\eta_*\).

Second, retain the marginal equations and nonnegativity, replace the lower bounds on \(x_j\) by
\(z_{j,e}\le Mx_j\),
and compute a vertex of this polytope. It is feasible: the first optimum has \(x_j\ge\eta_*\) and \(z_{j,e}\le2\). It is again bounded.

At this vertex, at most \(d=2\tau+m\) groups have \(x_j>0\). To prove this, associate to each positive group its contribution vector \(w_j\) to the row and category equations. Its row contribution is \(x_j\), and its category contributions are \(\bar b^j_e x_j+z_{j,e}\). If more than \(d\) groups were positive, these vectors would have a nontrivial linear dependence
\(\sum_j\alpha_j w_j=0\).
For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), multiplying each group's entire vector of variables by either \(1+\varepsilon\alpha_j\) or \(1-\varepsilon\alpha_j\) produces two distinct feasible points. The global equations remain unchanged; all within-group constraints are homogeneous and remain valid. Their midpoint is the proposed vertex, a contradiction. Also, \(x_j=0\) forces every associated \(z_{j,e}=0\).

It remains to turn a positive group into integer bundles. Put
\(u_e=z_{j,e}/x_j\),
\(k_e=\lfloor u_e\rfloor\), and
\(f_e=u_e-k_e\).
For absent surplus variables, take \(u_e=0\).

Partition the unit interval at the distinct values among \(0,1,f_1,\ldots,f_m\). On each positive-length interval choose a point \(U\), and create the bundle with coordinates
\(\bar b^j_e+k_e+\mathbf1_{\{U<f_e\}}\),
giving it mass \(x_j\) times the interval length. There are at most \(m+1\) bundles. Since the total interval length on which \(U<f_e\) is \(f_e\), their aggregate category-\(e\) contribution is exactly
\(x_j\bar b^j_e+z_{j,e}\).

All these bundles are EFX-compatible. Each remains above every threshold \(a_u\), because it dominates \(\bar b^j\) coordinatewise. If the group has positive surplus, every added copy has zero recipient cost and the recipient's bundle cost is \(a_{t_j}\); consequently every removal leaves cost at most \(a_{t_j}\). If there is no surplus, compatibility was already established for \(\bar b^j\). Active bundles receive no surplus and remain singletons.

Finally, \(u_e\le M\). The output coordinates are therefore bounded by \(H+\lfloor M\rfloor+1\), and the interval masses have polynomial encoding length. The first LP's coefficient sizes depend only on \(H\) and the prescribed marginals; the second LP and rounding consequently have polynomial encoding bounds independent of the incoming bundle magnitudes. This proves the compression lemma.

4. A singleton-swap insertion lemma. Suppose a state satisfies the invariant. Let type \(t\) have active mass at least \(h>0\). Suppose category \(e\) satisfies
\(c_t(e)\le c_t(f)\)
for every category \(f\) currently occurring.

Insert mass \(h\) of copies of \(e\), repeatedly adding a copy to a bundle of minimum cost for type \(t\). There is an EFX assignment to the resulting bundle distribution in which the active mass of type \(t\) has decreased by \(h\), while all other active masses are unchanged.

Here is a finite argument proving this statement; it is not an algorithmic expansion. Clear the denominators of the current assignment, \(h\), and any rational subdivisions used in the insertions. This yields finitely many individuals of equal mass and an integer number of insertions. Select a distinct active type-\(t\) individual for each insertion.

For one insertion, let \(b\) be the selected minimum-cost bundle, and let \(s\) be the selected active individual's current singleton.

Give \(b+\mathbf1_e\) to that individual, mark them inactive, and give \(s\) to the previous owner of \(b\). If the selected individual already owned \(b\), simply add the copy and mark them inactive.

The updated bundle is EFX-compatible for type \(t\): removing the new copy leaves \(c_t(b)\), and removing any old copy \(f\) leaves
\(c_t(b)+c_t(e)-c_t(f)\le c_t(b)\).
The new minimum bundle cost for type \(t\) is at least the old minimum \(c_t(b)\).

For every other type, adding a nonnegative-cost copy cannot decrease the minimum bundle cost. Thus all unchanged assigned bundles remain compatible. The transferred singleton is compatible with every type. Every other active individual still holds a singleton.

Induction proves the insertion lemma, including insertion of zero-cost copies and comparisons within the same type.

5. Computing an insertion batch without expansion. Suppose the current bundle distribution consists of \(K\) bundles \(b^i\) with positive rational masses \(p_i\). We must carry out the insertion from Section 4.

If \(c_t(e)=0\), choose any minimum-cost bundle class of mass \(p>0\). Write
\(h/p=k+f\), where \(k=\lfloor h/p\rfloor\) and \(0\le f<1\).
Give \(k\) extra copies to mass \(p(1-f)\) of this class and \(k+1\) extra copies to mass \(pf\). Every involved bundle remains minimum-cost for type \(t\), so this is a permissible greedy insertion sequence. It creates at most \(K+1\) bundle classes.

Now suppose \(c_t(e)>0\). Multiply costs by a common denominator so that the starting loads
\(s_i=c_t(b^i)\)
and the increment \(A=c_t(e)\) are integers, with \(A>0\). In this paragraph these symbols denote the scaled costs.

For an integer \(z\ge0\), define
\(G(z)=\sum_i p_i\max\{0,\lfloor(z-s_i)/A\rfloor+1\}\).
This is the mass of insertion opportunities whose pre-insertion load is at most \(z\).

Find the smallest integer \(z\ge0\) for which \(G(z)\ge h\). Binary search suffices: a valid upper bound is
\(\max_i s_i+A\lceil h/\min_i p_i\rceil\).

Set
\(k_i=\max\{0,\lceil(z-s_i)/A\rceil\}\).
Exactly \(k_i\) insertions per individual in class \(i\) have pre-insertion load strictly below \(z\). Their total mass is
\(F=\sum_i p_i k_i<h\).
The remaining mass \(h-F\) is at most the combined mass of classes whose resulting load \(s_i+k_iA\) equals \(z\). Distribute one further copy across that much mass of those classes.

This produces at most two final bundles per original class. It is exactly a greedy insertion sequence: all opportunities below \(z\) are used, then an appropriate portion of the opportunities at \(z\) is used. All counts, subdivisions, and binary-search operations use rational or integer arithmetic.

After either kind of batch, compute an assignment to the new distribution by a transportation flow. Row capacities are the new active and inactive masses; bundle-class capacities are their computed masses. Inactive row \(t\) can receive a bundle precisely when all its removal costs are at most the current threshold \(a_t\); active rows can receive singletons. Section 4 proves this flow is feasible. Exact rational maximum flow computes it in polynomial time.

Apply the compression lemma to its positive entries.

6. Reserving the insertion batches. We now specify an initialization and insertion order that satisfy the insertion lemma's hypotheses.

Reserve a total chore mass \(R-1\), using at most the mass \(\mu_t\) of individuals of each type. Process types in any fixed order. While processing type \(t\), select a cheapest category \(e\) among those with remaining positive chore supply. Reserve a mass
\(h=\min\{\text{remaining type capacity},\text{remaining supply of }e,\text{remaining reservation target}\}\).
Record the block \((t,e,h)\), and subtract its mass from all applicable remaining quantities.

The procedure succeeds because \(R-1\le1=\sum_t\mu_t\). Every nonfinal block exhausts a type capacity or a category supply, so there are at most \(m+\tau+1\) blocks.

Let \(q_t\) be the total mass reserved using type \(t\). The unreserved chore supply has total mass exactly one. Make one singleton class per unreserved category, with mass equal to that category's unreserved supply. Assign these classes to active and inactive rows in proportions \(q_t\) and \(\mu_t-q_t\). All bundles are singletons, so the invariant holds.

Restore the recorded blocks in reverse order.

For a block \((t,e,h)\), every currently allocated category was still available when this block was reserved, or is \(e\) itself. Therefore
\(c_t(e)\le c_t(f)\)
for every currently occurring category \(f\). This remains true throughout the block's insertions.

The active mass of type \(t\) is the total mass of its blocks not yet restored, so it is at least \(h\). Thus Sections 4–5 apply. Restore the block, decrease \(q_t\) by \(h\), and compress.

After all blocks are restored, the supplies are exactly \(\rho\), every \(q_t\) is zero, and the invariant gives the required EFX assignment.

7. Polynomial running time and encoding length. The essential issue is to prevent repeated compression from causing growing denominators or bundle coordinates. The construction does prevent this.

Let \(Q\) be a common denominator of all input masses and supplies. Its binary encoding length is at most polynomial in \(L\). Every reservation amount, every intermediate supply \(\sigma_e\), and every prescribed active mass \(q_t\) is an integer multiple of \(1/Q\), obtained by subtraction and taking minima of such quantities. Their encoding lengths consequently remain polynomial in \(L\).

After compression, the number of supported entries is at most
\(S=(2\tau+m)(m+1)\).
A positive-cost batch creates at most \(2S\) bundle classes; a zero-cost batch creates at most \(S+1\). Its transportation flow therefore has polynomial size, and its positive entries provide polynomially many groups for the compression LPs.

The first compression LP has integer bundle coefficients bounded by \(H\), together with coefficients of magnitude one. Its right-hand sides are the prescribed marginals just bounded. Standard vertex and determinant bounds for rational linear programs imply a polynomial encoding bound for its positive optimum \(\eta_*\), depending only on \(m,\tau,L\) and the polynomially bounded number of groups. Thus \(M=2/\eta_*\) also has a uniform polynomial encoding bound.

The second compression LP has the same polynomial dimension and coefficient lengths. Its vertex, the rounded masses, and the resulting coordinates bounded by \(H+\lfloor M\rfloor+1\) all have uniform polynomial encoding bounds. These bounds are independent of the preceding state's denominators and coordinates: those preceding values establish feasibility but do not appear as coefficients or right-hand sides in the compression LPs.

It follows inductively that every stored state has polynomial encoding length. The batching arithmetic, threshold comparisons, binary searches, transportation flows, and two rational LPs therefore each take polynomial time. There are at most \(m+\tau+1\) restoration blocks.

The algorithm consequently runs in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\) and outputs a finitely supported rational EFX assignment of polynomial total encoding length. The proof includes removal of zero-cost copies and all comparisons between individuals of the same type.
