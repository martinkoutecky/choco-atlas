The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a finite support \(\mathcal T\subseteq\{0,1\}^d\) of valuation types with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\) for \(d\) divisible resource pools, choose \(y_{tj}\ge 0\) subject to \(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\mu_t y_{tj}\le q_j\). Define \(u_t=\sum_{j=1}^d a_{tj}y_{tj}\), where \(a_t\in\{0,1\}^d\) is type \(t\)'s valuation vector. Maximize \(W=\sum_t\mu_tu_t\), then among its maximizers minimize \(\sum_t\mu_tu_t^2\); return an allocation matrix \(y\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(m=d\geq1\) be the number of divisible resource pools. A society is given by its support: a nonempty list \(\mathcal T\subseteq\{0,1\}^{m}\) of \(\tau\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Supplies \(q_j\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}\) are per capita; let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
An allocation specifies per-person quantities \(y_{tj}\geq0\), with \(\sum_t\mu_ty_{tj}\leq q_j\) for every pool \(j\). Require cleanliness: \(y_{tj}=0\) whenever \(a_{tj}=0\), where \(a_t=t\); unused resources are allowed.
Define \(u_t=\sum_j a_{tj}y_{tj}\). First maximize \(W=\sum_t\mu_tu_t\), then among its maximizers minimize \(\sum_t\mu_tu_t^2\). The computational problem is to return any exact optimal allocation matrix.

Is computing such an allocation solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\), or is it NP-hard?

For an explicitly listed finite society with binary additive valuations and unit-supply divisible items, an allocation minimizing squared utilities among welfare maximizers is computable in polynomial time ([Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.00835v3)).

  • Allocation quantities are per person within each type, and the requested allocation is exact; any optimizer is acceptable.

Exact polynomial-time solvability

Theorem. The problem is solvable in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). An exact optimal allocation can be computed using \(O(\tau^2)\) rational linear programs, each of polynomial encoding length.

The proof below works directly with the listed types and their masses.

1. Characterizing welfare maximizers

Introduce aggregate quantities
\(x_{tj}=\mu_t y_{tj}\), and define
\(r_t(x)=\sum_j x_{tj}\).
Because every mass is positive, this change of variables is invertible. Cleanliness gives
\(u_t=r_t(x)/\mu_t\), and hence
\(W=\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}\).

Let
\(J=\{j:q_j>0\text{ and }a_{tj}=1\text{ for some }t\}\).
All quantities outside these pools must be zero: either the supply is zero or cleanliness prohibits every recipient.

Consequently,
\(W\le Q:=\sum_{j\in J}q_j\).
This bound is attainable by assigning each pool in \(J\) entirely to any type that values it. Furthermore, equality holds precisely when every such pool is exhausted, since all supply deficits are nonnegative.

Thus the aggregate welfare-maximizing allocations form the polytope
\(P\) defined by

  • \(x_{tj}\ge0\), with \(x_{tj}=0\) unless \(j\in J\) and \(a_{tj}=1\);
  • \(\sum_t x_{tj}=q_j\) for every \(j\in J\).

This polytope is nonempty and compact: every coordinate is nonnegative and bounded by its pool’s supply. The secondary objective becomes

\( \displaystyle F(x)=\sum_t\frac{r_t(x)^2}{\mu_t}. \)

If \(J\) is empty, the zero allocation solves the problem. Assume henceforth that \(Q>0\).

2. Constructing utility levels by linear programming

We use the standard exact rational linear-programming theorem: a rational LP with a nonempty feasible region and an attained finite optimum admits computation of an exact rational optimal solution in time polynomial in its encoding length. Exact polynomial-time LP methods, including the distinction from approximate optimization, are discussed in [Gleixner and Steffy, Section 1.3](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/7507.pdf).

Define the rational upper bound
\(U=Q/\min_t\mu_t\).
Every \(x\in P\) satisfies \(r_t(x)/\mu_t\le U\), because \(r_t(x)\le Q\).

The algorithm maintains a set \(H\) of types whose utilities have been fixed, with fixed values \(\alpha_t\) for \(t\in H\). Initially \(H=\varnothing\). At each stage, put \(R=\mathcal T\setminus H\) and proceed as follows.

  • If \(R\) is empty, stop.
  • Solve the LP minimizing \(\lambda\), subject to

\(x\in P\), \(0\le\lambda\le U\),
\(r_t(x)=\mu_t\alpha_t\) for \(t\in H\), and
\(r_t(x)\le\mu_t\lambda\) for \(t\in R\).
Denote its optimum by \(\lambda_k\).

  • Let \(K_k\) be the feasible allocation set at that optimum:

\( \displaystyle K_k=\{x\in P:r_t(x)=\mu_t\alpha_t\ (t\in H),\quad r_t(x)\le\mu_t\lambda_k\ (t\in R)\}. \)

  • For each \(t\in R\), compute the exact LP value

\(b_t=\min_{x\in K_k}r_t(x)\).
Define
\(A_k=\{t\in R:b_t=\mu_t\lambda_k\}\).

  • Fix \(\alpha_t=\lambda_k\) for every \(t\in A_k\), add \(A_k\) to \(H\), and repeat.

Upon termination, solve the feasibility LP
\(x\in P,\ r_t(x)=\mu_t\alpha_t\) for every type, obtaining \(x^*\). Return
\(y^*_{tj}=x^*_{tj}/\mu_t\).

We first prove termination and correctness; Section 5 establishes the bit complexity of these exact LP calls.

Every stage fixes at least one type

Suppose \(A_k\) were empty. For each \(t\in R\), there would then be a witness \(x^{(t)}\in K_k\) satisfying
\(r_t(x^{(t)})<\mu_t\lambda_k\).

Average these witnesses:
\(\bar x=|R|^{-1}\sum_{t\in R}x^{(t)}\).
Convexity gives \(\bar x\in K_k\). For every \(s\in R\), all witnesses satisfy the upper bound for \(s\), and the witness indexed by \(s\) satisfies it strictly. Therefore
\(r_s(\bar x)/\mu_s<\lambda_k\).

Since \(R\) is finite,
\(\max_{s\in R}r_s(\bar x)/\mu_s<\lambda_k\).
Using this maximum as \(\lambda\) contradicts the defining optimality of \(\lambda_k\). Hence \(A_k\ne\varnothing\).

Moreover, every \(x\in K_k\) already satisfies
\(r_t(x)=\mu_t\lambda_k\) for all \(t\in A_k\): its row total is both at most this value and at least the minimum \(b_t\). Thus fixing these types preserves feasibility.

The initial LP is feasible because \(P\ne\varnothing\) and \(\lambda=U\) is sufficient. The preceding argument inductively preserves feasibility, and compactness ensures attainment of every optimum. At least one type is fixed per stage, so there are at most \(\tau\) stages.

The levels are nonincreasing

Any allocation in \(K_k\) remains feasible after the new types are fixed, while every remaining type has utility at most \(\lambda_k\). It is therefore a feasible witness for the next minimax LP with \(\lambda=\lambda_k\). Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \lambda_{k+1}\le\lambda_k. \)

In particular, the final allocation \(x^*\) belongs to every earlier set \(K_k\): it satisfies that stage’s previously fixed equalities, and all types fixed at that stage or later have utilities at most \(\lambda_k\).

3. A certificate for the final allocation

Write \(u_t^*=r_t(x^*)/\mu_t\). We prove

\( \displaystyle x^*_{tj}>0,\quad a_{sj}=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_t^*\le u_s^*. \)

Thus each pool is assigned only to eligible types having the lowest final utility among its eligible recipients.

Suppose instead that \(x^*_{tj}>0\), \(a_{sj}=1\), and \(u_t^*>u_s^*\). Let \(k\) be the stage at which \(t\) was fixed. Then \(u_t^*=\lambda_k\).

The type \(s\) was still unfixed at stage \(k\): a type fixed earlier would have utility at least \(\lambda_k\), by the nonincreasing order of the levels. Also \(s\notin A_k\), because its final utility is strictly smaller.

Choose
\(0<\delta<\min\{x^*_{tj},\,\mu_s(\lambda_k-u_s^*)\}\).
Transfer aggregate quantity \(\delta\) of pool \(j\) from \(t\) to \(s\).

This transfer preserves nonnegativity, cleanliness, and every pool total. It changes no previously fixed type. The utility of \(t\) decreases below \(\lambda_k\), while that of \(s\) remains below \(\lambda_k\); all other utilities are unchanged. Therefore the modified allocation still belongs to \(K_k\).

But \(t\in A_k\) means every allocation in \(K_k\) has its utility exactly \(\lambda_k\). This contradiction proves the certificate.

4. The certificate proves quadratic optimality

For each \(j\in J\), define
\(c_j=\min\{u_t^*:a_{tj}=1\}\).
The certificate and exhaustion of pool \(j\) imply

\( \displaystyle \sum_t u_t^*x^*_{tj}=c_jq_j. \)

For any competing welfare-maximizing aggregate allocation \(z\in P\), cleanliness and nonnegativity give

\( \displaystyle \sum_t u_t^*z_{tj}\ge c_j\sum_tz_{tj}=c_jq_j. \)

Summing over pools yields
\(\sum_{t,j}u_t^*(z_{tj}-x^*_{tj})\ge0\).

Put \(v_t=r_t(z)/\mu_t\). Expanding the squares gives

\( \displaystyle F(z)-F(x^*)=\sum_t\mu_t(v_t-u_t^*)^2+2\sum_t\mu_tu_t^*(v_t-u_t^*). \)

The second term equals
\(2\sum_{t,j}u_t^*(z_{tj}-x^*_{tj})\), which is nonnegative. The first term is also nonnegative. Hence
\(F(z)\ge F(x^*)\) for every \(z\in P\).

Therefore \(x^*\), and consequently \(y^*\), solves both optimization stages exactly. The same calculation also shows that the optimal utility vector is unique: equality forces \(v_t=u_t^*\) for every type because every \(\mu_t\) is positive.

5. Polynomial bit complexity

Counting LP calls alone would not suffice: we must bound the encoding lengths of the utility levels introduced along the way.

For a positive final utility level \(\lambda\), define

\(A_\lambda=\{t:u_t^*=\lambda\},\qquad J_\lambda=\{j\in J:c_j=\lambda\}.\)

By the certificate, every recipient of a pool in \(J_\lambda\) belongs to \(A_\lambda\). Conversely, every positive allocation to a type in \(A_\lambda\) comes from a pool in \(J_\lambda\). Summing these quantities in the two ways gives

\( \displaystyle \lambda\sum_{t\in A_\lambda}\mu_t=\sum_{j\in J_\lambda}q_j. \)

Consequently,

\( \displaystyle \lambda=\frac{\sum_{j\in J_\lambda}q_j}{\sum_{t\in A_\lambda}\mu_t}. \)

The denominator is positive. Every stage level \(\lambda_k\) is realized by its nonempty set \(A_k\), so this formula bounds every positive stage level. Zero levels have constant encoding length.

To make the bound explicit, let \(D\) be the product of the denominators of all input masses and supplies. Its binary length is at most \(L+1\). Each input rational multiplied by \(D\) is an integer of \(O(L)\) bits. Thus any subset sum of supplies or masses, after multiplication by \(D\), has
\(O(L+\log(m+\tau))\) bits. In the displayed ratio, the common denominator \(D\) cancels. Every level therefore has polynomial encoding length, independently of the number of stages.

It follows that:

  • Every LP has at most \(m\tau+1\) variables and polynomially many constraints.
  • Its coefficients are input rationals, zero or one, the bound \(U\), or products \(\mu_t\lambda_k\); all have polynomial encoding length.
  • There are at most \(\tau\) minimax LPs, at most \(\tau^2\) auxiliary LPs, and one final feasibility LP.
  • Exact rational LP algorithms therefore perform all these computations in polynomial bit time and return \(x^*\) with polynomial encoding length.
  • Dividing its coordinates by the positive input masses preserves polynomial encoding length and takes polynomial time.

This proves exact polynomial-time solvability for the support-encoded mass problem.
