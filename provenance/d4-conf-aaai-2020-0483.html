The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given categories \(G\), a recipe \(r_g\), and rational masses \(\mu_{g,v}\) of agents with category \(g\) and value \(v\), compute the divisible-mass ascending-price clock outcome. Trade quantities \(x_{g,v}\) and total trade mass \(q\) must satisfy \(0\le x_{g,v}\le\mu_{g,v}\) and \(\sum_v x_{g,v}=r_gq\); determine the outcome’s gain from trade \(\sum_{g,v}v x_{g,v}\) relative to the maximum over feasible mass trades, and establish its budget-balance, individual-rationality, and pointwise incentive properties as the largest individual mass tends to zero.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(G\) contain \(m\) public categories, with one public recipe \(r_g\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and a category order fixed independently of values. Values \(v\in\mathbb Q\) may have either sign and are private; utilities are quasilinear, with signed payments to the auctioneer.
A society is given by its support: the list of the \(\tau\) types \((g,v)\) with positive mass, each mass \(\mu_{g,v}\) a binary rational. Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Normalize total mass to \(1\); let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
A feasible trade satisfies \(q\geq0\), \(0\leq x_{g,v}\leq\mu_{g,v}\), and \(\sum_v x_{g,v}=r_gq\). Write \(\operatorname{GFT}(x)=\sum_{g,v}v x_{g,v}\) and \(\operatorname{OPT}=\max_x\operatorname{GFT}(x)\), allowing no trade; consider approximation ratios only when \(\operatorname{OPT}>0\).
Define the mass clock through refinements: choose a common denominator \(D\) of the masses and, for each positive integer \(n\), split every type into packets of mass \(h=1/(Dn)\). Each packet has its type's value and acts independently; equal-valued packets have a public exit order independent of reports.
For each refinement, initialize \(p_g=-\infty\). With \(N_g\) packets in category \(g\), set \(c=\min_g\lfloor N_g/r_g\rfloor\); if \(c=0\), make no trade. Otherwise raise prices in oversized categories until their remaining packet counts have floor quotient \(c\).
Cycle through categories in the fixed order. Raise \(p_g\) until its remaining count becomes \(r_gc\), or until \(\sum_g r_gp_g=0\). Skip a category already at its target count; after a complete cycle decrement \(c\), stopping without trade if it becomes zero.
Packets exit irreversibly when their value is reached, processing equal-valued packets one at a time in the public order. If balance and an exit coincide, process that exit and then stop. Other packets at the same value may remain until their scheduled exit.
At balance, select uniformly \(r_gc\) remaining packets from each category, each trading packet paying \(hp_g\). Let \(x^{(h)}\) be expected trading masses and \(t^{(h)}_{g,v}=p_gx^{(h)}_{g,v}\) be expected signed payments; both are zero when there is no trade. The divisible outcome is their limit as \(n\to\infty\), if it exists.
Pointwise incentive compatibility means that an infinitesimal trader, whose deviation leaves aggregate masses unchanged, cannot improve expected utility per unit by changing its exit strategy; its category is fixed. Result

The specified divisible outcome exists for every society. It attains exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}\), retains strong budget balance and individual rationality, and is pointwise incentive compatible.

Moreover, write \(V_g=\max_v v\) and \(A=\sum_g r_gV_g\). Whenever \(\operatorname{OPT}>0\), every category is nonempty, \(A>0\), and every refinement satisfies

\(\displaystyle 0\leq \operatorname{OPT}-\operatorname{GFT}(x^{(h)})\leq hA.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \max\left\{0,1-\frac{hA}{\operatorname{OPT}}\right\}\leq \frac{\operatorname{GFT}(x^{(h)})}{\operatorname{OPT}}\leq 1.\)

Thus no positive efficiency loss persists for any fixed society with positive optimum.

The finite clock is analyzed in [Gonen and Segal-Halevi, Section 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.08094v2). The proof below directly handles the ties and refinement rule specified in the question.

1. The optimal divisible allocation

Relabel categories according to their public order as \(1,\ldots,m\). Let \(M_g=\sum_v\mu_{g,v}\) and \(Q=\min_g M_g/r_g\). If a category is empty, then \(Q=0\), and every feasible allocation and every refinement make no trade. Henceforth assume all categories are nonempty.

For a fixed quantity \(q\in[0,Q]\), let \(z_{g,v}(q)\) allocate mass \(r_gq\) to the highest values in category \(g\), taking a fraction of the cutoff type if necessary. This is optimal at that quantity: exchanging selected lower-value mass for available higher-value mass weakly increases gains from trade.

Define

\(\displaystyle F(q)=\sum_{g,v}v\,z_{g,v}(q).\)

Each category’s contribution is continuous and piecewise linear, with nonincreasing marginal value. Therefore \(F\) is continuous, concave, and piecewise linear, and

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{OPT}=\max_{q\in[0,Q]}F(q).\)

Let

\(\displaystyle q^*=\max\{q\in[0,Q]:F(q)=\operatorname{OPT}\}.\)

The largest maximizer exists by continuity and compactness. We will prove that the clock converges to

\(\displaystyle x^*_{g,v}=z_{g,v}(q^*).\)

In particular, the clock selects the largest optimal quantity, including any terminal interval of zero marginal gains.

2. A bound valid for every refinement

Fix a refinement with packet mass \(h\).

Remaining packet counts

Suppose balance occurs in round \(c\), and let \(K_g\) be the number of remaining packets in category \(g\). Then

\(\displaystyle r_gc\leq K_g\leq r_g(c+1).\)

Indeed, initialization leaves each category with floor quotient \(c\), hence fewer than \(r_g(c+1)\) packets. After any completed round with target \(c+1\), every category has exactly \(r_g(c+1)\) packets. During the next round, counts decrease only toward \(r_gc\).

Prices only rise, and exits occur in increasing value order. Thus every exited packet has value at most its final category price, and every remaining packet has value at least that price. The stipulated treatment of equal values preserves these weak inequalities.

A price bound on the optimum

At balance all prices are finite and \(\sum_g r_gp_g=0\). For every feasible allocation \(y\),

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{GFT}(y)=\sum_{g,v}(v-p_g)y_{g,v}\leq\sum_{g,v}\mu_{g,v}(v-p_g)_+,\)

where \((a)_+=\max\{a,0\}\). The equality uses \(\sum_v y_{g,v}=r_gq\) and price balance.

Let

\(\displaystyle S_g=h\sum_{\text{remaining packets }i\text{ in }g}(v_i-p_g).\)

All positive-surplus packets remain, and any exited packet has nonpositive surplus. Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{g,v}\mu_{g,v}(v-p_g)_+=\sum_g S_g.\)

Each remaining packet is selected with probability \(\alpha_g=r_gc/K_g\). Expected payments sum to zero, so

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{GFT}(x^{(h)})=\sum_g\alpha_gS_g.\)

It follows that

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} \operatorname{OPT}-\operatorname{GFT}(x^{(h)}) &\leq\sum_g(1-\alpha_g)S_g\\ &\leq h\sum_g(K_g-r_gc)(V_g-p_g)\\ &\leq h\sum_g r_g(V_g-p_g) =hA. \end{aligned}\)

Here \(V_g-p_g\) is nonnegative because category \(g\) has remaining packets. Feasibility of the expected allocation also gives \(\operatorname{GFT}(x^{(h)})\leq\operatorname{OPT}\).

Refinements making no trade

It remains to establish the same bound when the clock makes no trade.

If \(Q\leq h\), then \(F(q)\leq qA\) implies \(\operatorname{OPT}\leq hA\).

Suppose instead that \(Q>h\). Every category then has more than \(r_g\) packets. If round \(1\) completes without balance, its final price in category \(g\) is the value of the \((r_g+1)\)-st highest packet. The weighted sum of these prices is strictly negative; otherwise balance would have occurred.

That sum is precisely the right derivative \(F'_+(h)\): immediately beyond quantity \(h\), the next packet in category \(g\) supplies its marginal value with weight \(r_g\). Hence \(F'_+(h)<0\). Concavity implies that \(F\) decreases beyond \(h\), so

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{OPT}=\max_{0\leq q\leq h}F(q)\leq hA.\)

Finally, positive optimum implies \(A>0\), since \(F(q)\leq qA\). This completes the refinement bound.

3. The limiting prices

Efficiency convergence alone does not establish convergence of payments. We now identify the eventual price vector.

Assume first that \(q^*>0\). At mass \(r_gq^*\), define:

  • \(b_g\): the marginal value immediately below that mass;
  • \(a_g\): the marginal value immediately above that mass, taking \(a_g=-\infty\) when \(r_gq^*=M_g\).

These are well defined because each category has finite support. At a cumulative-mass breakpoint, \(b_g\) is the value on the higher side and \(a_g\) the value on the lower side. Inside a type’s mass interval, they coincide. Always \(a_g\leq b_g\).

Optimality gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_g r_gb_g\geq0.\)

If \(q^*<Q\), the weighted marginal value immediately to its right is strictly negative. A positive value would contradict optimality; zero would give a larger maximizer because \(F\) is piecewise linear. If \(q^*=Q\), some \(a_g=-\infty\). Thus, in either case,

\(\displaystyle \sum_g r_ga_g<0.\)

For \(j=0,\ldots,m\), define the extended-real sums

\(\displaystyle B_j=\sum_{g\leq j}r_gb_g+\sum_{g>j}r_ga_g.\)

They are nondecreasing, with \(B_0<0\) and \(B_m\geq0\). Let \(k\) be the first index with \(B_k\geq0\). Define

\(\displaystyle p_g^*= \begin{cases} b_g,&g<k,\\ -\dfrac{\sum_{j<k}r_jb_j+\sum_{j>k}r_ja_j}{r_k},&g=k,\\ a_g,&g>k. \end{cases}\)

Because \(B_k\geq0\), no \(a_g=-\infty\) remains after \(k\). Thus every displayed price is finite. Moreover, \(B_{k-1}<0\) and \(B_k\geq0\) imply

\(\displaystyle a_k<p_k^*\leq b_k,\qquad \sum_g r_gp_g^*=0.\)

This is the price vector reached by raising coordinates, in public order, from \(a_g\) toward \(b_g\) until balance.

4. The discrete rounds eventually reach these prices exactly

For a refinement, let \(w_g(j)\) denote the value of the \(j\)-th highest packet in category \(g\), and set \(w_g(j)=-\infty\) when \(j>N_g\).

If a round with target \(c\) completes without balance, its endpoint price is

\(\displaystyle E_g(c)=w_g(r_gc+1).\)

When any packets must exit, this is the last exited packet’s value. In the initial round, a category already exactly at its target retains price \(-\infty\), which agrees with the convention above.

The sum \(\sum_g r_gE_g(c)\) is nondecreasing as \(c\) decreases. Starting each round with a negative price sum, balance occurs in that round exactly when its hypothetical endpoint sum is nonnegative.

Set

\(\displaystyle \widehat c_h=\left\lceil\frac{q^*}{h}\right\rceil-1.\)

For all sufficiently small \(h\), \(\widehat c_h\geq1\), and

\(\displaystyle E_g(\widehat c_h)=b_g,\qquad E_g(\widehat c_h+1)=a_g,\)

whenever the latter round exists.

Here is the key rounding check. If \(r_gq^*\) is a cumulative type mass, then its packet count

\(\displaystyle J_g=\frac{r_gq^*}{h}\)

is an integer. The definition of \(\widehat c_h\) gives

\(\displaystyle r_g\widehat c_h+1\leq J_g<r_g(\widehat c_h+1)+1.\)

Thus the two queried ranks lie on opposite sides of the breakpoint. If \(r_gq^*\) lies inside a type, both ranks lie inside that type for sufficiently small \(h\). Finite support supplies a positive distance to every other relevant breakpoint, justifying these statements simultaneously across categories.

There is one initialization case to check: \(q^*=Q\) and \(Q/h\) is nonintegral. Then \(\widehat c_h\) is the initial target. Capacity-attaining categories start at \(-\infty=a_g\). Every other category is oversized for sufficiently small \(h\); initialization leaves \(r_g(\widehat c_h+1)-1\) packets, and its price is \(w_g(r_g(\widehat c_h+1))=a_g\). Hence this round also starts at the vector \(a\).

Therefore the round preceding \(\widehat c_h\) has negative endpoint sum, while round \(\widehat c_h\) has nonnegative endpoint sum. It follows that, for all sufficiently small \(h\),

\(\displaystyle c_h=\widehat c_h,\qquad p^{(h)}=p^*.\)

The price vector is therefore eventually constant, rather than merely convergent. This also shows independence from the chosen common denominator and from the exit order within a type.

5. Allocation and payment convergence

Write \(q_h=c_hh\). The preceding formula implies

\(\displaystyle q^*-h\leq q_h<q^*.\)

In category \(g\), the remaining packets include the highest \(r_gc_h\) packets, with at most \(r_g\) additional packets. Comparing uniform selection with selecting exactly those highest packets gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_v\left|x^{(h)}_{g,v}-z_{g,v}(q_h)\right|\leq2r_gh.\)

Indeed, both allocations have the same total mass; their distance is at most twice the mass that can be selected from the additional packets.

The top-value allocations are nested as quantity increases. Hence

\(\displaystyle \sum_v\left|z_{g,v}(q_h)-z_{g,v}(q^*)\right|=r_g(q^*-q_h)\leq r_gh.\)

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle \sum_{g,v}|x^{(h)}_{g,v}-x^*_{g,v}|\leq3h\sum_g r_g.\)

Since prices are eventually \(p^*\),

\(\displaystyle t^{(h)}_{g,v}\longrightarrow t^*_{g,v}=p_g^*x^*_{g,v}.\)

If \(q^*=0\), the initial marginal value \(F'_+(0)\) is strictly negative: a positive value contradicts optimality at zero, while zero would give a positive interval of maximizers. For sufficiently small \(h\), the endpoint sum of round \(1\) equals this negative marginal value. The clock therefore makes no trade. Allocations and transfers are eventually zero.

Together with the empty-category case, this proves existence for every society. By construction,

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{GFT}(x^*)=F(q^*)=\operatorname{OPT}.\)

6. Budget balance, rationality, and pointwise incentives

For \(q^*>0\), feasibility and price balance imply

\(\displaystyle \sum_{g,v}t^*_{g,v}=q^*\sum_g r_gp_g^*=0.\)

Thus the limiting outcome is strongly budget balanced. Each finite trading realization also has this identity, since it selects exactly \(r_gc_h\) packets per category.

Individual rationality follows because every selected packet has value at least its category price. Passing to the limit gives

\(\displaystyle (v-p_g^*)x^*_{g,v}\geq0.\)

For pointwise incentives, consider an infinitesimal trader whose deviation leaves the aggregate clock unchanged, as stipulated in the question. Its final category price is therefore fixed.

When \(q^*>0\):

  • If \(v>p_g^*\), truthful behavior keeps it in the auction. Its lottery probability tends to \(1\), since \(r_gc_h/K_g\to1\).
  • If \(v<p_g^*\), truthful behavior exits before settlement and yields zero utility.
  • If \(v=p_g^*\), every possible allocation yields zero utility.

Any alternative exit strategy produces a trading probability \(\beta\in[0,1]\), giving utility per unit \(\beta(v-p_g^*)\). This cannot exceed \(\max\{0,v-p_g^*\}\), which truthful behavior attains. When \(q^*=0\), the aggregate outcome makes no trade, so an aggregate-invariant deviation also yields zero utility.

This proves pointwise incentive compatibility under the specified notion.

7. Computation time

Let \(B_r=\sum_g\lceil\log_2(r_g+1)\rceil\) denote the recipe encoding length. All computations below use the support representation.

For the divisible outcome, sort values within categories and form normalized cumulative-mass breakpoints. Sweeping these breakpoints computes \(F\) and its largest maximizer \(q^*\); the formulas above then compute allocations and prices. This takes

\(\displaystyle O\bigl(\tau\log(\tau+1)+m\bigr)\)

exact rational arithmetic operations, with polynomial bit complexity in \(m,\tau,L,B_r\).

For an arbitrary refinement, compute the integer type counts \(\mu_{g,v}/h\). The endpoint sums \(\sum_g r_gE_g(c)\) are monotone, so binary search finds the stopping round, or establishes no trade. A rank lookup uses cumulative type counts, and the stopping round can then be reconstructed in public order, including the single exit at a coincident balance event.

The expected masses and payments can therefore be computed using

\(\displaystyle O\bigl(\tau\log(\tau+1)+m\log(\tau+1)\log(1/h+1)+m\bigr)\)

arithmetic operations. Operand lengths, and hence total bit complexity, are polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,B_r,\log(1/h)\). In particular, these bounds have no dependence on the number of possible unsupported types or a linear dependence on recipe magnitudes.
