The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given a rooted binary hierarchy with τ leaf types, binary-rational masses μ_t summing to \(1\), rational hierarchy weights λ, a population scale \(Q\), a seed-mass budget κ, and a target spread ρ, decide whether there are seed masses \(s_t\in[0,μ_t]\) with \(\sum_t s_t\leκ\) such that the threshold-one cascade reaches terminal mass at least ρ.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\) leaf types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types.
The hierarchy is a rooted full binary tree with \(m\) nodes and these leaves. Every node \(v\) has a nonnegative rational weight \(\lambda_v\), with \(\lambda_u\le\lambda_v\) whenever \(u\) is an ancestor of \(v\). Define \(w_{tu}=\lambda_{\operatorname{lca}(t,u)}\), including \(w_{tt}=\lambda_t\); influence is bidirectional.
Inputs also include a binary-encoded integer population scale \(Q\ge1\), a rational budget \(0<\kappa\le1\), and a rational target \(\rho\in[0,1]\). Let \(L\) be the total input bit length.
Seeds are divisible masses \(s_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_t s_t\le\kappa\). Starting with \(x_t^{(0)}=s_t\), update synchronously and irreversibly: \(x_t^{(r+1)}=\mu_t\) if \(Q\sum_u w_{tu}x_u^{(r)}\ge1\), and otherwise \(x_t^{(r+1)}=x_t^{(r)}\). Within-type infected mass contributes; an individual point has zero mass.
Let \(\sigma(s)=\sum_t x_t^{(\infty)}\), including seeds. The spread decision asks whether \(\sigma(s)\ge\rho\) is feasible; its optimization counterpart has value \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_s\sigma(s)\).

For each fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), does maximizing terminal spread admit a \(Q^{1-\varepsilon}\)-factor approximation in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is achieving this factor NP-hard?

For finite populations of \(N\) agents, bidirectional hierarchical influence with additive weights and deterministic unit thresholds is NP-hard to approximate within \(N^{1-\varepsilon}\) for every fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\). [Verified source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02827v2)
No result settling this approximation question for the defined divisible-mass model was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Restored the full binary hierarchy, nonnegative weights, ancestor-to-descendant weight monotonicity, bidirectional influence, and leaf weights governing within-type influence.
  • Defined the unspecified cascade by synchronous, irreversible activation of all remaining mass of a type when its additive influence reaches one; terminal spread includes seeds.
  • Used a weak threshold inequality because the hardness construction explicitly activates vertices at influence exactly one, despite preliminary prose saying 'exceeds'.
  • Interpreted Q as a positive integer population scale encoded in binary, multiplying normalized infected mass; imposed no integrality on scaled type or seed masses.
  • Fixed the budget domain to positive normalized mass at most one and the target domain to the unit interval.
  • Defined m as the number of hierarchy nodes and L as total input bit length.
  • Used the nonatomic convention that within-type infected mass contributes without subtracting a discrete individual's contribution.
  • Carried the approximation factor from finite population size N to population scale Q.

Result

For every fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), achieving a \(Q^{1-\varepsilon}\)-factor approximation is NP-hard for the defined divisible-mass model.

Here an approximation algorithm must return feasible seeds \(s\) satisfying \(\sigma(s)\ge \mathrm{OPT}/Q^{1-\varepsilon}\). The reduction below permits arbitrary fractional seeds. All masses in the constructed instances are dyadic rationals, and all node weights belong to \([0,1]\).

The comb hierarchy is inspired by the [finite-population construction of Schoenebeck and Tao](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02827v2). The proof below supplies a separate soundness argument for divisible seeds.

1. Reduction parameters and change of units

Start with a Vertex Cover instance consisting of a simple graph on \(n\) vertices, edges \(e_1,\ldots,e_p\), and budget \(k\). We restrict to \(n\ge 2\), \(p\ge 1\), and \(1\le k<n\). Vertex Cover remains NP-hard under these restrictions: the excluded cases are decidable directly.

Define

\[ K=n+k,\qquad A=n(p+2),\qquad R=A+K+10. \]

Choose \(W\) to be the smallest power of two satisfying \(W\ge 1024K^3R\), and define

\[ D=\frac{1}{64K^2R},\qquad \delta=\frac{D}{W},\qquad d=\frac{7\delta}{4},\qquad B=AW+K. \]

For the fixed \(\varepsilon\), choose a fixed integer \(r\ge 1/\varepsilon\). Let \(T\) be the smallest power of two satisfying \(T\ge (4B)^r\), and put

\[ M=T-AW. \]

The constructed instance will have population scale \(Q=T\), budget \(\kappa=K/T\), \(A\) small types of mass \(W/T\), and one reservoir type of mass \(M/T\).

It is convenient to prove the cascade statements in auxiliary units: write \(y_t=Ts_t\) for seeds and \(z_t^{(q)}=Tx_t^{(q)}\) for infected mass. Then the seed budget is \(\sum_t y_t\le K\), capacities are \(W\) or \(M\), and the activation condition is exactly

\[ \sum_u w_{tu}z_u^{(q)}\ge 1. \]

This is precisely the specified model after normalization; the auxiliary units impose no integrality restriction on seeds.

Two useful inequalities are

\[ \frac{K}{W}\le \frac{D}{16} \]

and, with \(\eta=dB+K/W\),

\[ \eta =\frac{7D}{4}\left(A+\frac{K}{W}\right)+\frac{K}{W} <\frac{7}{256K^2}+\frac{1}{1024K^2} =\frac{29}{1024K^2} <\frac{1}{K+1}. \]

Here we used \(W\ge1\), \(R>A+K\), and \(K/W\le D/16<1/(1024K^2)\). In particular, \(\eta<1\).

2. Types, hierarchy, and weights

For each graph vertex \(i\), create types \(C_i,D_i\), and gate types \(G_{i1},\ldots,G_{ip}\), each of capacity \(W\) in auxiliary units. Create one reservoir type \(Z\), of capacity \(M\).

For each \(i,j\), define

\[ c_{ij}= \begin{cases} \frac12,&\text{if }i\text{ is incident to }e_j,\\ \frac54,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad a_{ij}= \frac{1-D((n-1)j+k+c_{ij})}{jW}. \]

Also define

\[ b=\frac{1-D((n-1)(p+1)+k)}{(p+1)W}. \]

Construct the hierarchy as follows.

  • Join \(n\) arms by any full binary tree whose internal nodes all have weight \(\delta\).
  • Arm \(i\) has a root of weight \(d\), with children \(D_i\) and a comb.
  • Start the comb at \(C_i\). For each \(j=1,\ldots,p\), create a node of weight \(a_{ij}\) whose children are the previous comb and \(G_{ij}\).
  • In arm \(1\), add a final node of weight \(b\), whose children are the completed comb and \(Z\).
  • Give \(C_i,D_i\) leaf weight \(1\), give \(G_{ij}\) leaf weight \(a_{ij}\), and give \(Z\) leaf weight \(b\).

Thus different arms interact with weight \(\delta\); \(D_i\) interacts with the rest of its own arm with weight \(d\). Gate \(G_{ij}\) interacts with \(C_i\) and every earlier gate in its arm with weight \(a_{ij}\). Reservoir \(Z\) interacts with every type in its own comb with weight \(b\).

We verify the required monotonicity of node weights. All numerators defining \(a_{ij}\) and \(b\) exceed \(1/2\), because their coefficients of \(D\) are smaller than \(R\) and \(DR=1/(64K^2)\). Consequently,

\[ a_{ij}W\ge \frac{1}{2p}>D, \qquad bW\ge \frac{1}{2(p+1)}>D. \]

Moreover,

\[ a_{ij}-a_{i,j+1} = \frac{1-D(k+(j+1)c_{ij}-jc_{i,j+1})}{j(j+1)W}>0. \]

The coefficient of \(D\) in this numerator is at most \(k+3j/4+5/4<R\). Similarly,

\[ a_{ip}-b= \frac{1-D(k+(p+1)c_{ip})}{p(p+1)W}>0, \]

because \(k+(p+1)c_{ip}<R\). Finally, \(dW=7D/4<1/(2(p+1))\), so \(b>d>\delta\). Therefore,

\[ 1\ge a_{i1}>\cdots>a_{ip}>b>d>\delta>0. \]

All ancestor-weight constraints hold.

We will repeatedly use another consequence: every influence weight into a gate or into \(Z\) is at most \(1/W\). Thus all seed mass in types that have not become saturated contributes at most \(K/W\) to any such target.

3. Completeness: a vertex cover activates the reservoir

Suppose the graph has a vertex cover of size at most \(k\). Pad it to a set \(S\) of size exactly \(k\), which is possible since \(k<n\).

Seed one auxiliary unit in each \(C_i\), and one in each \(D_i\) with \(i\in S\). This uses exactly \(K\) units. Since these types have diagonal weight \(1\), they all become saturated in the first update.

Assume inductively that every gate in levels below \(j\) is saturated. Ignoring any additional infections, the influence on \(G_{ij}\) is

\[ a_{ij}jW+\delta((n-1)j+k)W +(d-\delta)W\,\mathbf 1_{\{i\in S\}} = 1-c_{ij}D+\frac34D\,\mathbf 1_{\{i\in S\}}. \]

Because \(S\) covers \(e_j\), at least one endpoint \(i\) belongs to \(S\). Its gate receives influence \(1+D/4\) and activates.

The other endpoint gate then receives an additional \(\delta W=D\). Even if its endpoint is outside \(S\), its influence is at least \(1-D/2+D>1\), so it activates too.

A nonendpoint gate whose vertex belongs to \(S\) activates after the first endpoint gate: its influence is at least \(1-5D/4+3D/4+D>1\). A nonendpoint gate outside \(S\) activates after both endpoint gates: its influence is at least \(1-5D/4+2D>1\).

Hence every gate in level \(j\) becomes saturated. Induction proves saturation of all gates.

The reservoir then receives influence

\[ b(p+1)W+\delta((n-1)(p+1)+k)W +(d-\delta)W\,\mathbf 1_{\{1\in S\}} = 1+\frac34D\,\mathbf 1_{\{1\in S\}}\ge1. \]

Therefore \(Z\) activates, and terminal spread is at least \(M/T\).

4. Soundness for arbitrary divisible seeds

Now suppose there is no vertex cover of size at most \(k\). Fix any feasible real seed vector in auxiliary units, with total at most \(K\). We prove that \(Z\) never activates.

Call a type saturated when its infected mass equals its capacity. Since \(W>K\) and \(M>K\), no type is saturated initially.

4.1. Gates require their own \(C_i\), and at most \(K\) clique types can saturate

Before \(Z\) activates, no gate in arm \(i\) can saturate before \(C_i\).

Indeed, consider the first gate activation in that arm while \(C_i\) is still unsaturated. All saturated sources outside its comb have influence weight at most \(d\), and their total mass is at most \(AW\). All unsaturated sources together contribute at most \(K/W\). Thus its influence is at most

\[ dAW+\frac{K}{W}\le\eta<1, \]

a contradiction. This also excludes simultaneous first activation of \(C_i\) and a gate: their thresholds are evaluated on the preceding state.

When \(C_i\) first saturates, its own gates are consequently still unsaturated. Its influence is at most \(y_{C_i}+dB+K/W\), so

\[ y_{C_i}\ge 1-\eta. \]

For \(D_i\), every off-diagonal influence weight is at most \(d\). Its first saturation therefore implies \(y_{D_i}\ge1-dB\ge1-\eta\).

If \(K+1\) types among the \(C_i,D_i\) saturated before the reservoir, their seeds would total at least

\[ (K+1)(1-\eta)>K. \]

Therefore at most \(K\) such types can saturate before \(Z\).

4.2. Reservoir activation would require all \(C_i\) and all gates

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(Z\) first activates in some update. Examine the state immediately before that update.

If \(C_1\) is unsaturated, all gates in arm \(1\) are unsaturated by the preceding argument. Reservoir influence is then at most \(dAW+K/W\le\eta<1\). Hence \(C_1\) must already be saturated.

Let \(h\) be the number of unsaturated \(C_i\) among the other arms. Their gates are also unsaturated. Since at most \(K\) clique types saturate, at most \(k+h\) types \(D_i\) are saturated.

An upper bound on reservoir influence is consequently

\[ b(p+1)W +\delta\big((n-1-h)(p+1)+k+h\big)W +\frac34D+\frac{K}{W} = 1-hpD+\frac34D+\frac{K}{W}. \]

The extra \(3D/4\) allows for saturated \(D_1\). If \(h\ge1\), this is at most

\[ 1-\frac14D+\frac{K}{W}<1. \]

Thus every \(C_i\) is saturated, and at most \(k\) types \(D_i\) are saturated.

If any gate outside arm \(1\) is unsaturated, the reservoir loses at least \(\delta W=D\) relative to the upper bound with all gates saturated. Its influence is at most \(1-D+3D/4+K/W<1\).

If any gate inside arm \(1\) is unsaturated, the corresponding loss is \(bW>D\). Its influence is then at most \(1-bW+3D/4+K/W<1\).

Therefore reservoir activation requires all \(C_i\) and all gates to be saturated beforehand.

4.3. An uncovered edge prevents completion of the gates

Let \(S\) be the graph vertices whose \(D_i\) are saturated immediately before the proposed reservoir activation. We have \(|S|\le k\). Since the Vertex Cover instance is a NO instance, choose an edge \(e_j\) with neither endpoint in \(S\).

We prove by induction over updates, up to the proposed reservoir activation, that no gate in levels \(j,\ldots,p\) saturates.

The statement holds initially. Assume it holds in the current state. Every saturated \(D_i\) in this state belongs to \(S\).

For \(G_{ij}\), upper-bound influence by saturating every \(C_i\), every gate in levels below \(j\), and allowing \(k\) saturated \(D_i\) in the baseline cross-arm contribution. Its influence is at most

\[ 1-c_{ij}D+\frac34D\,\mathbf 1_{\{i\in S\}}+\frac{K}{W}. \]

If \(i\) is an endpoint of \(e_j\), then \(i\notin S\) and \(c_{ij}=1/2\). If \(i\) is a nonendpoint, then \(c_{ij}=5/4\), and its possible bonus is at most \(3D/4\). In both cases the upper bound is at most \(1-D/2+K/W<1\).

Next consider \(G_{i\ell}\) with \(\ell>j\). The analogous baseline assumes all levels below \(\ell\) saturated. But its own preceding gate \(G_{i,\ell-1}\) is unsaturated by the induction hypothesis. Removing that fictitious saturated source reduces influence by \(a_{i\ell}W\). Unsaturated seeds remain covered by the \(K/W\) allowance. Therefore influence is at most

\[ 1-c_{i\ell}D+\frac34D-a_{i\ell}W+\frac{K}{W} \le 1+\frac14D-a_{i\ell}W+\frac{K}{W}<1, \]

using \(a_{i\ell}W>D\) and \(K/W\le D/16\).

No gate in these levels activates in the next update, completing the induction. This contradicts the necessity that all gates saturate before \(Z\).

Thus the reservoir never activates for any feasible fractional seed vector.

The nonreservoir types have total capacity \(AW\), while the reservoir retains at most its seeded mass \(K\). Hence every NO instance satisfies

\[ \mathrm{OPT}\le \frac{AW+K}{T}=\frac{B}{T}. \]

5. Approximation gap and encoding

In a YES instance,

\[ \mathrm{OPT}\ge\frac{M}{T} =1-\frac{AW}{T}\ge\frac34, \]

because \(T\ge4B\). In a NO instance, \(\mathrm{OPT}\le B/T\).

Moreover, \(T^\varepsilon\ge4B\), since \(T\ge(4B)^r\) and \(r\varepsilon\ge1\). A \(Q^{1-\varepsilon}\)-approximation, with \(Q=T\), would therefore return in a YES instance spread at least

\[ \frac{3}{4T^{1-\varepsilon}} = \frac{3T^\varepsilon}{4T} \ge\frac{3B}{T} >\frac{B}{T}. \]

This distinguishes YES from NO instances.

The construction meets all encoding requirements:

  • There are exactly \(\tau=A+1\) supported leaf types and \(m=2\tau-1\) hierarchy nodes.
  • The masses \(W/T\) and \(M/T\) are positive dyadic rationals summing to \(1\).
  • The budget is \(K/T\), and \(Q=T\) is an integer.
  • All weights are nonnegative rational numbers at most \(1\), with the required ancestor monotonicity.
  • For fixed \(\varepsilon\), \(W\), \(B\), and \(T\) are polynomially bounded in the Vertex Cover input size. Their binary encodings and all rational weight encodings therefore have polynomial length.

There are only \(A+1\) types: \(W\) and \(M\) represent auxiliary masses, not explicitly listed individuals.

Finally, spread for rational output seeds is evaluable in polynomial time. Each changing update saturates at least one additional type; thus the process stabilizes after at most \(\tau\) changing updates. Rational arithmetic has polynomial bit complexity.

Consequently, a polynomial-time algorithm achieving the requested approximation would decide Vertex Cover in polynomial time. This proves the claimed NP-hardness for the divisible-mass model.

4.3. The uncovered-edge obstruction
