The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite type and housing-class sets \(T\) and \(H\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita capacities \(q_h\), and each type’s acceptable classes, weak ranking, and voting weight \(w_t\), can one find in time polynomial in \(|T|\), \(|H|\), and input bit length \(L\) a rational array \(y_{t,a,b}\)? Each entry is the mass of type \(t\) receiving acceptable class \(a\) or \(\bot\) in the first matching and \(b\) or \(\bot\) in the second; each plan must respect the capacities. Require that for every feasible competing assignment, including any split of each \((t,a,b)\) cell among allowed assignments \(c\), the total weight of residents who strictly prefer their better assignment from the pair to \(c\) is at least the total weight who strictly prefer \(c\) to that pair.

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list \(T\) of \(\tau=|T|\) types with positive binary rational masses \(\mu_t\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Here \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. Let \(H\) be the housing classes, \(m=|H|\), and \(L\) the total input bit length.
Each class has a nonnegative binary rational per-capita capacity \(q_h\). Each type has a nonnegative binary rational voting weight \(w_t\), an acceptable set \(A_t\subseteq H\), and a weak ranking on \(A_t\). Extend it to \(D_t=A_t\cup\{\bot\}\) by ranking every acceptable class strictly above being unmatched, \(\bot\); indifference contributes no vote.
A pair of assignments is a nonnegative rational array \(y_{t,a,b}\), indexed by \(a,b\in D_t\), satisfying \(\sum_{a,b}y_{t,a,b}=\mu_t\) and, for every \(h\in H\), \(\sum_{t,a,b}y_{t,a,b}\mathbf1_{a=h}\le q_h\) and \(\sum_{t,a,b}y_{t,a,b}\mathbf1_{b=h}\le q_h\). Its entries describe the same residents’ assignments in the two plans.
A competitor may split every cell arbitrarily: it is any nonnegative real array \(z_{t,a,b,c}\), with \(a,b,c\in D_t\), satisfying \(\sum_c z_{t,a,b,c}=y_{t,a,b}\) and \(\sum_{t,a,b,c}z_{t,a,b,c}\mathbf1_{c=h}\le q_h\) for every \(h\in H\). There is no capacity constraint on \(\bot\).
Let \(d_t(a,b)\) be either weakly best member of \(\{a,b\}\). Call \(y\) winning if every competitor satisfies \(\sum_{t,a,b,c}w_tz_{t,a,b,c}\bigl(\mathbf1_{d_t(a,b)\succ_t c}-\mathbf1_{c\succ_t d_t(a,b)}\bigr)\ge0\).

Is finding a winning rational \(y\), or determining that none exists, solvable in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), or is this problem NP-hard?

For finite agents and individual unit-capacity houses, a popular winning set of at most two matchings always exists and can be found in polynomial time, even with nonnegative voting weights and preference ties. [Connor et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25150v3)
No result settling this precise continuous problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized the positive type masses to total mass one, so capacities are measured per capita.
  • Specified capacities and voting weights as nonnegative binary rationals; zero values are allowed.
  • Placed being unmatched strictly below every acceptable class, following the discrete preference definition; unacceptable classes cannot be assigned.
  • Allowed empty acceptable sets and unused capacity, as the discrete matching model permits both.
  • Specified competitors as arbitrary real mass splits of the joint assignment cells, with the same class capacities and unrestricted unmatched mass.
  • Allowed the two plans to coincide, interpreting the discrete size-two guarantee as at most two.
  • Included determining nonexistence to define the computational problem without assuming the continuous existence guarantee.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

1. Result. The problem is polynomial-time solvable, and a winning rational \(y\) always exists.

We give a construction operating directly on the listed types and binary rational capacities. It is inspired by the top-choice bottleneck construction of [Connor et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25150v3), but the proof below handles arbitrary real-valued splitting by competitors and establishes polynomial complexity in the compressed input.

2. Reduction to an allocation under doubled capacities. Consider a rational array \(x_{t,d}\), indexed by \(d\in D_t\), satisfying

\(\displaystyle x_{t,d}\geq 0,\qquad \sum_{d\in D_t}x_{t,d}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_t x_{t,h}\leq 2q_h.\)

From such an array, define

\(\displaystyle y_{t,h,\bot}=y_{t,\bot,h}=\frac{x_{t,h}}2\quad(h\in A_t),\qquad y_{t,\bot,\bot}=x_{t,\bot},\)

with all other entries zero. Each plan uses capacity at most \(q_h\), and the residents’ best assignment in the two plans has distribution \(x\).

For any competitor \(z\), aggregate its assignments into

\(\displaystyle \xi_{t,h,c}=z_{t,h,\bot,c}+z_{t,\bot,h,c},\qquad \xi_{t,\bot,c}=z_{t,\bot,\bot,c}.\)

Then

\(\displaystyle \sum_c\xi_{t,d,c}=x_{t,d},\qquad \sum_{t,d}\xi_{t,d,h}\leq q_h.\)

Define the competitor’s vote advantage on a resident with baseline assignment \(d\) by

\(\displaystyle b_t(d,c)=w_t\bigl(\mathbf1_{c\succ_t d}-\mathbf1_{d\succ_t c}\bigr).\)

It therefore suffices to construct \(x\) for which every such real array \(\xi\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,d,c}b_t(d,c)\xi_{t,d,c}\leq 0.\)

Classes with \(q_h=0\) can be omitted throughout the construction: neither the constructed plans nor a feasible competitor can assign positive mass to them.

3. The flow construction. Maintain a set \(R\) of remaining positive-capacity classes and residual masses \(r_t\), initially

\(\displaystyle R=\{h:q_h>0\},\qquad r_t=\mu_t.\)

Initially all entries of \(x\) are zero. At the start of each round, assign to \(\bot\) any residual type mass having no acceptable class in \(R\), and set that residual mass to zero.

For each remaining active type, let

\(\displaystyle F_t(R)=\{h\in A_t\cap R:h\succeq_t h'\text{ for every }h'\in A_t\cap R\}.\)

These are its current favorite classes.

For a subset \(P\) of active types, use the network with source \(s\), sink \(v\), and arcs

\(\displaystyle s\longrightarrow t\ \text{ of capacity }r_t,\qquad t\longrightarrow h\ \text{ of capacity }2\quad(h\in F_t(R)),\qquad h\longrightarrow v\ \text{ of capacity }2q_h.\)

The middle capacity is effectively unlimited: the entire residual population has mass at most \(1\).

A prefix consists of all active types whose weights are at least a specified weight level. Examine these prefixes in decreasing order of weight.

If all active types can be fully served on their current favorite classes, take a flow saturating all source arcs, add its type-to-class flows to \(x\), and terminate.

Otherwise, let \(W\) be the weight level of the first infeasible prefix. Thus all active types of weight strictly greater than \(W\) can be fully served.

Choose a maximum flow \(f\) for the prefix of weights at least \(W\), subject to saturating every source arc of a type of weight strictly greater than \(W\). Such a maximum flow exists: start with a flow serving those higher-weight types, add the weight-\(W\) types, and augment along simple residual source-to-sink paths. These augmentations preserve the already saturated higher-weight source arcs. When no augmenting path remains, the flow is maximum.

Let \(U\) be the type vertices reachable from \(s\) in this maximum flow’s residual graph, and let \(B\) be the reachable class vertices.

The following facts hold:

  • Every class in \(B\) is saturated. Otherwise its residual arc to \(v\) would make the sink reachable, contradicting maximality.
  • For every \(t\in U\), all its current favorites lie in \(B\). Every middle arc has positive forward residual capacity, since its flow is at most \(1\), whereas its capacity is \(2\).
  • Every positive flow into \(B\) comes from \(U\). A positive flow from a type outside \(U\) into a class in \(B\) would provide a reverse residual arc making that type reachable.

Assign the mass \(f_{t,h}\) to \(x_{t,h}\) for \(t\in U\), \(h\in B\). Subtract these assigned amounts from the corresponding residual masses, and remove all classes in \(B\) from \(R\). Call this a bottleneck round, with threshold \(W\).

By the three facts above, the assigned mass fills every removed class exactly:

\(\displaystyle \sum_t x_{t,h}=2q_h\qquad(h\in B).\)

Moreover, \(B\) is nonempty. The prefix is infeasible, so some weight-\(W\) source arc is unsaturated. Its type is reachable and has a nonempty favorite set, which makes at least one class reachable.

Consequently, there are at most \(m\) bottleneck rounds, followed by at most one terminal round.

4. Properties of the construction. Number the bottleneck rounds chronologically, with thresholds \(W_1,W_2,\ldots\) and removed class sets \(B_1,B_2,\ldots\).

We first prove that the thresholds are nonincreasing.

Consider a round with threshold \(W\). A higher-weight type in \(U\) is fully served, since its source arc is saturated. A higher-weight type outside \(U\) sends no flow into \(B\). Its flow therefore still serves its entire residual mass after \(B\) is removed, using classes that remain current favorites.

Thus, after the round, all residual types of weight strictly greater than \(W\) remain jointly feasible on their favorite classes. The next infeasible prefix, if any, must have threshold at most \(W\). Hence

\(\displaystyle W_1\geq W_2\geq\cdots.\)

We also need a preservation property:

*If a type has weight strictly greater than \(W_s\), every portion of its mass residual at the start of round \(s\) ultimately receives a favorite class from \(R_s\), the set of classes remaining at that round.*

Indeed, such a type is either fully assigned during round \(s\), or retains a flow serving its entire residual mass on favorites that survive the round. In the latter case, its best available preference level has not changed. Every subsequent threshold is at most \(W_s\), so the same argument applies at each later bottleneck. If the type survives to the terminal round, it is fully assigned to its current favorites, still at the original preference level. It cannot become unmatched, because this argument preserves a nonempty favorite set.

Finally, every type assigned positive mass during bottleneck round \(s\) has weight at least \(W_s\). Its assigned class is a current favorite, and all its current favorites belong to \(B_s\). Consequently, it strictly prefers its assigned class to every acceptable class remaining after that round, and to \(\bot\).

5. A certificate against every real-valued competitor. Give each class a nonnegative number

\(\displaystyle \pi_h=W_s\quad\text{if }h\in B_s,\qquad \pi_h=0\quad\text{if }h\text{ survives to the terminal round},\)

and set \(\pi_\bot=0\).

We claim that whenever \(x_{t,d}>0\), and \(c\in D_t\) is either \(\bot\) or a positive-capacity class,

\[\displaystyle b_t(d,c)\leq 2\pi_c-\pi_d. \tag{1}\]

We verify all preference cases.

Case 1: \(d\succ_t c\). If \(d\) was assigned in bottleneck round \(s\), then \(w_t\geq W_s=\pi_d\). If \(d\) was assigned in the terminal round, then \(\pi_d=0\), so the same inequality holds. Therefore

\(\displaystyle b_t(d,c)=-w_t\leq-\pi_d\leq 2\pi_c-\pi_d.\)

Case 2: \(d\) and \(c\) are indifferent. If \(\pi_d=0\), inequality (1) follows from \(\pi_c\geq0\).

Otherwise, \(d\) was assigned in a bottleneck round. Its type strictly prefers \(d\) to every acceptable class surviving that round. Thus \(c\) was removed in the same round or an earlier round. The nonincreasing thresholds imply \(\pi_c\geq\pi_d\), and hence

\(\displaystyle b_t(d,c)=0\leq 2\pi_c-\pi_d.\)

Case 3: \(c\succ_t d\). The class \(c\) must have been removed in a bottleneck round before the mass assigned to \(d\) received its assignment.

To justify this, every real-class assignment is to a current favorite, so a strictly preferred class cannot still be available when \(d\) is assigned. Likewise, mass is assigned to \(\bot\) only after all its acceptable remaining classes have disappeared.

Suppose \(c\in B_s\). The preservation property shows that \(w_t\leq W_s\): otherwise the mass eventually assigned to \(d\) would receive a favorite from \(R_s\), which it could not rank strictly below \(c\in R_s\).

Therefore \(w_t\leq\pi_c\). Also \(\pi_c\geq\pi_d\), by the chronological order and the nonincreasing thresholds, with \(\pi_d=0\) for terminal or unmatched assignments. It follows that

\(\displaystyle b_t(d,c)=w_t\leq\pi_c\leq 2\pi_c-\pi_d.\)

This proves (1).

Now let \(\xi\) be any feasible real-valued competitor. Zero-capacity classes receive zero competitor mass, so (1) applies to every relevant term. Summing gives

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,d,c}b_t(d,c)\xi_{t,d,c} \leq 2\sum_{h\in H}\pi_h\sum_{t,d}\xi_{t,d,h} -\sum_{t,d}\pi_d x_{t,d} \leq 2\sum_{h\in H}\pi_h q_h-\sum_{t,d}\pi_d x_{t,d}. \)

Every class with nonzero \(\pi_h\) was filled to doubled capacity by the construction. Thus

\(\displaystyle \sum_{t,d}\pi_d x_{t,d} =\sum_{h\in H}\pi_h\sum_t x_{t,h} =2\sum_{h\in H}\pi_hq_h.\)

The competitor’s vote advantage is therefore at most zero. By the reduction in Section 2, the constructed \(y\) is winning against every competitor allowed in the question.

6. Rationality and polynomial bit complexity. Each round uses at most \(\tau\) prefix-feasibility flow computations and one additional maximum-flow computation. The networks have at most \(\tau+m+2\) vertices and \(O(\tau m+\tau+m)\) arcs. There are at most \(m\) bottleneck rounds.

For exact arithmetic, let \(K\) be the product of the input denominators of the masses and capacities. Its bit length is \(O(L)\). Scale all flow capacities and masses by \(K\). Initial capacities are integers, and integral maximum flows ensure that all residual masses and constructed entries remain integer multiples of \(1/K\). All scaled numbers have polynomial bit length.

A polynomial-time maximum-flow algorithm, such as Edmonds–Karp, therefore implements every step in polynomial bit complexity. Weight comparisons also require only polynomial time on the input binary rationals.

The constructed \(x\) has at most \(\tau(m+1)\) entries. The resulting \(y\) has at most \(2\tau m+\tau\) nonzero entries, each an integer multiple of \(1/(2K)\), and consequently has polynomial encoding length.

This completes the proof of polynomial-time solvability and universal existence.
