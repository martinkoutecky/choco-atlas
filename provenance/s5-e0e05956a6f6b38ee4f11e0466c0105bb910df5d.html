The anchor (named result in the paper)

Theorem 7 — class: hardness

The continuous mirror question, as graded

For a fixed number \(m\) of alternatives and a Condorcet-consistent rule \(f\), let the hidden atomless society be a measurable ranking map ρ from \([0,1]\) to the complete rankings of the alternatives, with finite support. A deterministic online policy may query a measurable cohort \(S\) at rank \(k\) after ranks \(1,\ldots,k-1\) have been revealed on \(S\); it receives the exact mass partition by rank-\(k\) response, and the query costs λ(S). It must stop only when every ranking map consistent with the transcript has the same winner set. Does every such policy have competitive ratio Ω(m), measured against the minimum mass-query cost of a hindsight certificate for the hidden society?

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

Let \(A\) contain \(m\ge2\) alternatives. A society is given by its support: the list of its \(\tau\) strict complete ranking types with positive binary-rational masses summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible rankings. Let \(L\) be this description's bit length. The support description is hidden from the elicitation policy.
Realize the society by a measurable map \(\rho:[0,1]\to\mathcal R(A)\), with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\), allowing arbitrary measurable placement of its types. A fixed known rule \(f\) depends only on ranking masses, returns a nonempty winner set, and returns \(\{a\}\) whenever \(\lambda\{x:a\succ_{\rho(x)}b\}>1/2\) for every \(b\ne a\). No tie-breaking is imposed.
A query \((S,k)\), with measurable \(S\subseteq[0,1]\) and \(1\le k\le m\), is legal only after ranks \(1,\ldots,k-1\) have been revealed almost everywhere on \(S\). It reveals the labeled measurable response cohorts \(S_a=\{x\in S:\rho(x)(k)=a\}\) and their exact masses, and costs \(\lambda(S)\); response cohorts may be selected in subsequent queries. Sets and transcripts are identified modulo null sets.
A deterministic online policy chooses queries and stopping solely from its transcript and must terminate after finitely many queries on every admissible map. It may stop only when every admissible map producing that transcript has exactly the same winner set; consistent maps need not have the same support or masses as the true map.
A hindsight certificate is any finite legal query sequence satisfying this stopping condition, selected with knowledge of \(\rho\). Define \(\operatorname{OPT}_f(\rho)\) as the infimum of certificate costs, \(\operatorname{cost}_{\mathcal P}(\rho)=\sum_t\lambda(S_t)\), and \(\operatorname{CR}_m(\mathcal P,f)=\sup_\rho\operatorname{cost}_{\mathcal P}(\rho)/\operatorname{OPT}_f(\rho)\), over all admissible societies and placements. Queries are ideal exact oracles; any running-time bounds must specify dependence on \(m,\tau,L\) and query/transcript representation size.

Does the universal lower bound \(\operatorname{CR}_m(\mathcal P,f)=\Omega(m)\) hold for every Condorcet-consistent rule and every deterministic online policy, as \(m\) varies, or can this bound fail in the atomless cohort-query model?

For finite electorates, every online next-best elicitation algorithm for every Condorcet-consistent rule has competitive ratio \(\Omega(m)\), with unit cost per voter-rank query and certification of the entire winner set ([Shah and Yu](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29087v1#S5)). No result settling this exact atomless cohort-query model was located in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • Normalized total mass to 1 and imposed positive binary-rational support masses; the support description remains hidden because the policy performs elicitation.
  • Allowed arbitrary measurable placement of ranking types, with no known support-size restriction on consistent completions.
  • Interpreted the response partition as labeled measurable cohorts together with their masses, permitting subsequent queries to those cohorts; this preserves individual prefix revelation in the discrete model.
  • Interpreted λ as Lebesgue measure and used almost-everywhere legality and transcript equality.
  • Made the voting rule depend only on ranking masses, require a nonempty winner set, and preserve all tied winners without additional tie-breaking.
  • Required finite termination and finite hindsight certificates, matching the discrete query-sequence model.
  • Used an infimum for hindsight cost because divisible mass and strict majority certification need not admit a minimum.
  • Treated measurable queries and exact responses as ideal oracles, since arbitrary measurable cohorts have no specified finite encoding.
  • Interpreted the asymptotic claim across varying m rather than at one fixed number of alternatives.

Prover output (gpt-6.1-sol / high, Bolzano prover prompt)

Result

The universal lower bound holds in the stated atomless model. More precisely, for every \(m\ge3\), every Condorcet-consistent rule \(f\), and every deterministic online policy \(\mathcal P\) satisfying the stated termination and certification requirements,

\[ \operatorname{CR}_m(\mathcal P,f)\ge m-1. \]

The construction uses only four ranking types. Its essential feature is that a small cohort can reverse the Condorcet winner while remaining indistinguishable until the penultimate rank is queried.

The finite-electorate result appears in [Shah and Yu, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29087v1#S5). The proof below establishes the bound directly for arbitrary measurable queries, without discretizing the population.

1. Two preliminary facts

Measurable subsets of prescribed mass. If \(V\subseteq[0,1]\) is measurable and \(0\le r\le\lambda(V)\), there is a measurable \(E\subseteq V\) with \(\lambda(E)=r\).

Indeed, the function \(t\mapsto\lambda(V\cap[0,t])\) is continuous: its change between \(s\) and \(t\) is at most \(|t-s|\). Its endpoint values are \(0\) and \(\lambda(V)\), so the intermediate value theorem applies.

Every certificate costs at least \(1/2\). Let a finite query sequence have cost less than \(1/2\), and let \(U\) be the union of its queried sets. Then \(\lambda(U)\le\sum_t\lambda(S_t)<1/2\).

Fix an admissible map producing the transcript, and partition \(U^c\) by its finitely many ranking types. Because \(\lambda(U^c)>1/2\), we can choose, within these pieces, measurable subsets of dyadic masses whose total mass is greater than \(1/2\). This follows from density of dyadic rationals and the preceding subset fact.

Let \(E\subseteq U^c\) be their union. Replace the rankings on \(E\) by one fixed ranking with alternative \(a\) first. The resulting map remains admissible: every ranking mass is obtained from the original dyadic masses by subtracting and adding dyadic amounts. It produces the same transcript, and \(a\) is its strict Condorcet winner because \(\lambda(E)>1/2\).

Doing the same with a different alternative \(b\) produces another consistent admissible map with winner set \(\{b\}\). Thus the transcript cannot certify the entire winner set.

Consequently, for every admissible map \(\rho\),

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_f(\rho)\ge \frac12. \]

In particular, the denominators used below are positive.

2. A baseline society and a small modification

Fix \(m\ge3\), distinct alternatives \(w,c\), and a fixed ordering \(B\) of the remaining \(m-2\) alternatives.

Choose any dyadic number \(\delta\) satisfying \(0<\delta<1/6\). Partition \([0,1]\) into measurable sets \(G_1,G_2,D\) with the following masses and baseline rankings:

| Cohort | Mass | Baseline ranking |
|---|---:|---|
| \(G_1\) | \(1/2-\delta\) | \(w\succ c\succ B\) |
| \(G_2\) | \(2\delta\) | \(c\succ w\succ B\) |
| \(D\) | \(1/2-\delta\) | \(B\succ c\succ w\) |

Call this map \(\rho_0\). Its three ranking masses are positive dyadic numbers summing to \(1\).

On \(\rho_0\), alternative \(c\) defeats \(w\) on \(G_2\cup D\), of mass \(1/2+\delta\). It defeats every alternative in \(B\) on \(G_1\cup G_2\), also of mass \(1/2+\delta\). Hence \(c\) is a strict Condorcet winner and \(f(\rho_0)=\{c\}\).

For any measurable \(E\subseteq D\) of mass \(2\delta\), define \(\rho_E\) by changing only the ranking on \(E\), to \(B\succ w\succ c\). Its four ranking masses are

\[ \frac12-\delta,\qquad 2\delta,\qquad \frac12-3\delta,\qquad 2\delta, \]

so \(\rho_E\) is admissible.

On \(\rho_E\), alternative \(w\) defeats \(c\) on \(G_1\cup E\), of mass \(1/2+\delta\). It defeats every alternative in \(B\) on \(G_1\cup G_2\), again of mass \(1/2+\delta\). Therefore \(f(\rho_E)=\{w\}\).

Crucially, \(\rho_0\) and \(\rho_E\) agree through rank \(m-2\) everywhere.

3. What the baseline run forces the policy to spend

Run \(\mathcal P\) on \(\rho_0\). For a finite transcript prefix \(T\), define its deeply queried portion of \(D\) by

\[ H(T)=D\cap\bigcup_{t:\,k_t\ge m-1}S_t. \]

This is measurable. By next-best legality, querying rank \(m\) cannot add points that have not already had rank \(m-1\) revealed, modulo null sets.

Set \(\theta=1/2-3\delta\). We claim that the policy cannot stop at any prefix with \(\lambda(H(T))<\theta\).

Indeed, at such a prefix,

\[ \lambda(D\setminus H(T)) =\frac12-\delta-\lambda(H(T)) >2\delta. \]

Choose a measurable \(E\subseteq D\setminus H(T)\) of mass \(2\delta\). The map \(\rho_E\) produces exactly the same transcript prefix:

  • Queries at ranks at most \(m-2\) have unchanged responses everywhere.
  • Queries at ranks \(m-1\) or \(m\) avoid \(E\), modulo null sets, by its construction.

These statements preserve the labeled response cohorts themselves, and therefore also their exact masses. Yet the two consistent maps have different winner sets, \(\{c\}\) and \(\{w\}\). The stopping condition consequently fails.

The policy terminates after finitely many queries on the admissible baseline map. Thus there is a first query index \(q\) for which \(\lambda(H(T_q))\ge\theta\).

Every point of \(H(T_q)\), outside a null set, has received queries at all ranks \(1,\ldots,m-1\). Therefore

\[ \sum_{t=1}^{q}\lambda(S_t) =\int_{[0,1]}\sum_{t=1}^{q}\mathbf 1_{S_t}(x)\,d\lambda(x) \ge(m-1)\lambda(H(T_q)) \ge(m-1)\theta. \]

The integral counts query incidences, including repetitions; next-best legality supplies the \(m-1\) incidences on each deeply queried point.

Now choose \(E\subseteq D\setminus H(T_{q-1})\) with \(\lambda(E)=2\delta\), which is possible because \(\lambda(H(T_{q-1}))<\theta\).

On this particular map \(\rho_E\), all responses before query \(q\) equal their baseline responses. Determinism forces the policy to issue the same first \(q\) queries. The response to query \(q\) may differ, but its cost is already incurred. Hence

\[ \operatorname{cost}_{\mathcal P}(\rho_E) \ge(m-1)\left(\frac12-3\delta\right). \]

This reasoning permits arbitrary measurable query sets and arbitrary use of their geometric identities.

4. A cheap hindsight certificate

For the selected \(\rho_E\), use the following legal certificate:

  • Query rank \(1\) on \(G_1\).
  • Query ranks \(1,2\) on \(G_2\).
  • Query ranks \(1,\ldots,m-1\) on \(E\).

The certificate establishes that \(w\) is above every alternative in \(B\) throughout \(G_1\cup G_2\), whose mass is \(1/2+\delta\). It also establishes that \(w\) is above \(c\) throughout \(G_1\cup E\), of the same mass. On \(E\), revealing rank \(m-1\) as \(w\) leaves \(c\) as the uniquely determined last alternative.

Thus every admissible map producing this transcript has strict Condorcet winner \(w\). Condorcet consistency certifies the entire winner set \(\{w\}\), regardless of the completion’s support or masses.

The certificate cost is

\[ \left(\frac12-\delta\right)+2(2\delta)+(m-1)(2\delta) =\frac12+(2m+1)\delta. \]

It follows that

\[ \operatorname{CR}_m(\mathcal P,f) \ge \frac{(m-1)(1/2-3\delta)} {1/2+(2m+1)\delta}. \]

For each fixed \(m\), arbitrarily small positive dyadic \(\delta\) are allowed. Taking their limit in this lower bound gives

\[ \boxed{\operatorname{CR}_m(\mathcal P,f)\ge m-1}. \]

The argument concerns a supremum: it does not require a single instance attaining the limiting ratio.

5. Matching order of growth

There is also a universal policy with competitive ratio at most \(2(m-1)\). Query every positive-mass revealed prefix cohort at its next rank, continuing through rank \(m-1\). Each level costs \(1\), so total cost is \(m-1\).

At termination, every ranking is fully determined, with its last alternative inferred from strict completeness. The terminal prefix-cohort masses determine the whole society, so its winner set is certified. The preliminary certificate lower bound gives competitive ratio at most \(2(m-1)\).

For true support size \(\tau\), this policy uses at most \(1+(m-2)\tau\) queries. These are oracle-query bounds; no running-time bound is asserted for arbitrary measurable-set representations or evaluation of an arbitrary rule \(f\).

Thus the best possible competitive ratio has linear order in \(m\). Atomless splitting does not defeat the lower bound: it allows the small decisive cohort to be placed outside the portion already deeply inspected.
