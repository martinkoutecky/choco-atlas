The continuous mirror question, as graded

Given finite types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu\), an allowed undirected type-support graph, a trace budget, an estimated innate-opinion vector \(\hat{s}\) obtained from at most \(b\) representative queries, and a certificate \(\|s-\hat{s}\|_{\mu}\le\delta\), compute an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal type-interaction mass \(w\) minimizing \(\Phi_{\mu}(\hat{s},L(w))\), and certify \(\Phi_{\mu}(s,L(w))-\min_L\Phi_{\mu}(s,L)\le 2K\delta+\varepsilon\).

Packaged proof task (what the prover saw — nothing else)

A society is given by its support: a list of \(\tau\ge2\) types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_i\), summing to \(1\); \(\tau\) is the support size, not the number of possible types. An allowed simple undirected graph \(H=(T,E)\) has \(m=|E|\ge1\). Let \(L\) denote the total input bit length.
Write \(M=\operatorname{diag}(\mu)\), \(\langle x,y\rangle_\mu=x^\top My\), and \(\|x\|_\mu=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle_\mu}\). A type has one shared, signed real innate opinion.
For nonnegative interaction masses \(w\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}\), let \(Q(w)\) be the ordinary weighted graph Laplacian and \(\mathcal L(w)=M^{-1}Q(w)\). Given a positive rational trace budget \(B\), feasibility means \(w\in\mathcal F:=\{v\ge0:\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal L(v))=B\}\); weights may become zero and individual degrees need not be preserved.
For an opinion vector \(u\), the Friedkin–Johnsen equilibrium is \(z=(I+\mathcal L(w))^{-1}u\). Define polarization plus disagreement by \(\Phi_\mu(u,\mathcal L(w))=\|z\|_\mu^2+\langle z,\mathcal L(w)z\rangle_\mu\).
The input supplies rational \(\hat s\), \(R>0\), \(\delta\ge0\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1\), with the promise that the unknown \(s\) satisfies \(\langle s,\mathbf1\rangle_\mu=\langle\hat s,\mathbf1\rangle_\mu=0\), \(\|s\|_\mu,\|\hat s\|_\mu\le R\), and \(\|s-\hat s\|_\mu\le\delta\). Set \(K=2R\).
The estimate and certificate are already supplied after at most \(b\) exact representative queries, where \(1\le b\le\tau\); querying type \(i\) reveals \(s_i\). No additional queries are available.
The computational problem is to produce rational \(w\in\mathcal F\) satisfying \(\Phi_\mu(\hat s,\mathcal L(w))\le\min_{v\in\mathcal F}\Phi_\mu(\hat s,\mathcal L(v))+\varepsilon\), together with the guarantee \(\Phi_\mu(s,\mathcal L(w))-\min_{v\in\mathcal F}\Phi_\mu(s,\mathcal L(v))\le2K\delta+\varepsilon\) for every promised \(s\).

Can this additive approximation problem, including its error-transfer guarantee, be solved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,b,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), or is it NP-hard?

For finite agents, estimated-opinion minimizers satisfy the additive error-transfer bound \(2K\|s-\hat s\|\), conditional on uniform Lipschitz continuity; this is not an algorithmic complexity result. [Cinus et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0819.pdf)
Finite-agent polarization-plus-disagreement minimization with a fixed total edge weight has a polynomial-time convex optimization formulation. [Musco et al.](https://tsourakakis.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/polarization-disagreement-www18.pdf)
No result settling this precise mass-input approximation problem was identified in the literature search.

Modeling choices made when packaging the task

  • The undefined objective is taken to be polarization plus disagreement, the combined undirected objective; the anchor also covers other objectives.
  • Masses are normalized to sum to one, with one shared innate opinion per type.
  • Interaction mass means symmetric total conductance between types; per-unit-mass influence is represented by the mass-normalized Laplacian.
  • The trace budget is an equality on the ordinary matrix trace of the mass-normalized Laplacian, preserving the discrete trace constraint.
  • The allowed graph is simple and nonempty, and the trace budget is positive; zero final edge weights are permitted.
  • Mean-centering uses the mass-weighted mean for both true and estimated opinions.
  • A supplied norm bound R makes the opinion domain bounded and fixes K=2R; unrestricted quadratic objectives cannot have the claimed global Lipschitz constants.
  • Representative queries reveal the exact opinion shared by a type; the estimate and reconstruction certificate are supplied promises, so query selection and reconstruction are outside this task.
  • The approximation is additive, epsilon is a positive binary rational below one, and the output interaction masses are rational.

Result

The problem is polynomial-time solvable, including the error-transfer guarantee. More precisely, a deterministic algorithm produces the required rational weights in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,b,\log(1/\varepsilon)\), without making additional representative queries.

The proof establishes convexity, verifies the hypotheses needed for polynomial-time optimization in the bit model, and converts approximate feasibility into exact rational feasibility.

1. The objective is an inverse quadratic form

For an edge \(e=\{i,j\}\), choose either orientation and put \(a_e=e_i-e_j\). Then

\(\displaystyle Q(w)=\sum_{e\in E}w_ea_ea_e^\top,\qquad A(w)=M+Q(w).\)

Since every mass is positive and \(Q(w)\) is positive semidefinite, \(A(w)\) is positive definite. The equilibrium equation is therefore equivalent to

\(\displaystyle A(w)z=Mu.\)

Consequently,

\[\displaystyle \Phi_\mu(u,\mathcal L(w)) =z^\top A(w)z =(Mu)^\top A(w)^{-1}(Mu). \tag{1} \]

This identity holds for arbitrary signed opinions. Moreover, multiplying the equilibrium equation by \(\mathbf1^\top\) shows that \(\langle z,\mathbf1\rangle_\mu=\langle u,\mathbf1\rangle_\mu\), so the promised mean-zero condition is preserved.

We will also need a uniform contraction bound. Define

\(\displaystyle S(w)=M^{-1/2}Q(w)M^{-1/2}\succeq0.\)

The equilibrium operator, expressed in mass-normalized coordinates, is \((I+S(w))^{-1}\). Its eigenvalues belong to \((0,1]\). Hence

\[\displaystyle \|z\|_\mu\le\|u\|_\mu,\qquad 0\le\Phi_\mu(u,\mathcal L(w))\le\|u\|_\mu^2. \tag{2} \]

No connectivity assumption is needed.

2. The trace constraint gives a rational simplex

For \(e=\{i,j\}\), define the positive rational number

\(\displaystyle c_e=a_e^\top M^{-1}a_e=\frac1{\mu_i}+\frac1{\mu_j}.\)

By linearity of trace,

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal L(w))=\sum_{e\in E}c_ew_e.\)

Thus the substitution

\(\displaystyle p_e=\frac{c_ew_e}{B},\qquad w_e=\frac{B}{c_e}p_e\)

is a bijection between \(\mathcal F\) and the simplex

\(\displaystyle \Delta_m=\left\{p\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m:\sum_ep_e=1\right\}.\)

In particular, \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty and compact. Continuity of (1) implies that all minima appearing in the question are attained.

Set \(h=M\hat s\). The estimated-opinion objective becomes

\[\displaystyle F(p)=h^\top\left(M+\sum_e\frac{B}{c_e}p_ea_ea_e^\top\right)^{-1}h. \tag{3} \]

It is convex: for every positive definite \(A\), completing the square gives

\(\displaystyle h^\top A^{-1}h=\max_{x\in\mathbb R^\tau}\{2h^\top x-x^\top Ax\}.\)

For fixed \(x\), the expression on the right is affine in \(p\). Its supremum is therefore convex. This extends the inverse-quadratic-form argument used in the finite-agent formulation of [Musco, Musco, and Tsourakakis](https://tsourakakis.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/polarization-disagreement-www18.pdf).

Convexity alone is insufficient for the requested complexity bound; the following steps address rational arithmetic and feasibility.

3. Exact rational evaluation and a quantitative continuity bound

At rational \(p\in\Delta_m\), solve the rational linear system

\(\displaystyle A(p)\hat z=h,\qquad A(p)=M+\sum_e\frac{B}{c_e}p_ea_ea_e^\top. \)

Differentiating (3), using \(d(A^{-1})=-A^{-1}(dA)A^{-1}\), gives

\[\displaystyle \frac{\partial F}{\partial p_e} =-\frac{B}{c_e}(a_e^\top\hat z)^2. \tag{4} \]

Both the objective and its derivatives are rational and computable exactly in polynomial time. Indeed, rational linear systems of dimension \(\tau\) and polynomial entry bit length can be solved by fraction-free elimination with polynomial bit complexity.

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and (2) give

\(\displaystyle (a_e^\top\hat z)^2 \le(a_e^\top M^{-1}a_e)(\hat z^\top M\hat z) \le c_eR^2. \)

Thus

\[\displaystyle -BR^2\le\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_e}\le0.\tag{5}\]

If \(m=1\), feasibility fixes the unique weight \(w_e=B/c_e\), which is automatically optimal. Henceforth suppose \(m\ge2\), and let \(d=m-1\).

Eliminate the last simplex coordinate by writing

\(\displaystyle p=(y_1,\ldots,y_d,1-\mathbf1^\top y),\qquad P=\{y\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^d:\mathbf1^\top y\le1\}.\)

Write \(f(y)=F(p(y))\). By (5),

\(\displaystyle \left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial y_j}\right| =\left|\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_j}-\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_m}\right| \le BR^2. \)

Integrating along the segment between any two points of \(P\) therefore yields

\[\displaystyle |f(y)-f(y')|\le G\|y-y'\|_2,\qquad G=1+mBR^2.\tag{6}\]

The rational number \(G\) has polynomial encoding length, even if its numerical value is large.

4. Polynomial-time optimization in the bit model

The optimization theorem used

We use the standard finite-precision ellipsoid theorem: given a compact convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\), rational data certifying an enclosed ball and an enclosing ball, and a polynomial-time rational separation oracle, weak linear optimization is possible in polynomial bit time. For rational \(c\) and tolerance \(\eta>0\), it returns rational \(x\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{dist}(x,C)\le\eta,\qquad c^\top x\le\min_{v\in C}c^\top v+\eta. \)

The complexity is polynomial in the dimension, the bounding-data encoding lengths, the objective encoding length, and \(\log(1/\eta)\), together with the oracle cost. This is the separation-to-weak-optimization result of [Grötschel, Lovász, and Schrijver](https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1981a.pdf).

We now verify its hypotheses explicitly.

A bounded, full-dimensional epigraph

By (2), \(0\le f(y)\le R^2\) on \(P\). Define

\(\displaystyle C=\{(y,t)\in\mathbb R^{d+1}:y\in P,\ f(y)\le t\le R^2+1\}. \)

This set is compact and convex. It contains the ball centered at

\(\displaystyle a_0=\left(\frac1m,\ldots,\frac1m,R^2+\frac12\right)\)

with radius \(r=1/(4m^2)\).

To check this, a perturbation of Euclidean norm at most \(r\) leaves every \(y\)-coordinate nonnegative and gives

\(\displaystyle \mathbf1^\top y \le\frac{m-1}{m}+\sqrt{m-1}\,r \le1. \)

The perturbed \(t\)-coordinate lies between \(R^2\) and \(R^2+1\), so \(f(y)\le t\). Also, \(C\) lies in the ball about the origin of radius \(\rho=R^2+2\), because \(\|y\|_2\le1\) and \(0\le t\le R^2+1\).

These bounding data are rational and have polynomial encoding length.

A rational separation oracle

Given a rational point \((\bar y,\bar t)\), first check the linear constraints defining \(P\) and the upper bound on \(t\). Any violation directly gives a rational separating hyperplane.

Otherwise compute \(f(\bar y)\) and \(g=\nabla f(\bar y)\) exactly. If \(\bar t\ge f(\bar y)\), the point belongs to \(C\). If \(\bar t<f(\bar y)\), convexity implies that every \((y,t)\in C\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle g^\top y-t\le g^\top\bar y-f(\bar y). \)

The queried point strictly violates this inequality. Thus it is a rational separating hyperplane.

Section 3 proves that this oracle runs in polynomial time in the original input length and the query-point encoding length. All hypotheses of the optimization theorem are satisfied.

Converting weak feasibility into exact feasibility

Let \(f_*=\min_{y\in P}f(y)\). Apply weak optimization to the objective \(t\), with

\(\displaystyle \eta=\frac{\varepsilon}{G+2}.\)

It returns rational \((\tilde y,\tilde t)\) such that

\[\displaystyle \operatorname{dist}((\tilde y,\tilde t),C)\le\eta,\qquad \tilde t\le f_*+\eta. \tag{7} \]

Let \(q\) be the Euclidean projection of \(\tilde y\) onto \(P\). This projection is rational and computable exactly in polynomial time:

  • If the positive parts of \(\tilde y\) sum to at most \(1\), take those positive parts.
  • Otherwise take \(q_j=\max\{\tilde y_j-\theta,0\}\), where \(\theta\) makes the sum equal to \(1\). Sorting the coordinates identifies the active set \(I\), and then \(\theta=(\sum_{j\in I}\tilde y_j-1)/|I|\).

Choose \((y',t')\in C\) at distance at most \(\eta\) from \((\tilde y,\tilde t)\); such a point exists because \(C\) is compact. The projection optimality inequality gives

\(\displaystyle \|q-y'\|_2\le\|\tilde y-y'\|_2\le\eta.\)

Combining (6), (7), and \(f(y')\le t'\), we obtain

\(\displaystyle f(q) \le f(y')+G\eta \le t'+G\eta \le\tilde t+(G+1)\eta \le f_*+(G+2)\eta =f_*+\varepsilon. \)

Finally output

\(\displaystyle p=(q_1,\ldots,q_d,1-\mathbf1^\top q),\qquad w_e=\frac{B}{c_e}p_e. \)

These weights are rational and nonnegative, and satisfy the budget exactly:

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal L(w))=\sum_ec_ew_e=B.\)

They also satisfy the requested estimated-opinion approximation.

All derived rational data have polynomial bit length. In particular, the dependence on \(G,\rho,\eta^{-1}\) is through their encoding lengths; thus the running time is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\varepsilon)\). Only the supplied support is used, and no queries are made.

5. Uniform error transfer

For fixed feasible \(v\), let \(T_v=(I+\mathcal L(v))^{-1}\). The normalized-operator argument in Section 1 shows that \(T_v\) is self-adjoint in the mass inner product and satisfies

\(\displaystyle \|T_vx\|_\mu\le\|x\|_\mu.\)

Using (1), for opinions \(u,u'\) with norms at most \(R\),

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} |\Phi_\mu(u,\mathcal L(v))-\Phi_\mu(u',\mathcal L(v))| &=|\langle u-u',T_v(u+u')\rangle_\mu|\\ &\le\|u-u'\|_\mu(\|u\|_\mu+\|u'\|_\mu)\\ &\le2R\|u-u'\|_\mu. \end{aligned} \)

Thus the objective is uniformly \(K\)-Lipschitz on the promised ball, with \(K=2R\).

For any promised \(s\), choose a true-opinion minimizer \(v_s\in\mathcal F\). The output weights satisfy

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} \Phi_\mu(s,\mathcal L(w))-\Phi_\mu(s,\mathcal L(v_s)) &\le K\delta+ \Phi_\mu(\hat s,\mathcal L(w)) -\Phi_\mu(\hat s,\mathcal L(v_s)) +K\delta\\ &\le2K\delta+\varepsilon. \end{aligned} \)

The second inequality uses estimated-opinion \(\varepsilon\)-optimality and feasibility of \(v_s\). This proves the guarantee simultaneously for every promised \(s\). It is the approximate-minimizer version of the transfer argument in [Cinus et al.](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0819.pdf), with the required uniform Lipschitz bound proved here.

The algorithm is independent of the query budget \(b\), so its running time satisfies the requested polynomial bound including \(b\).
