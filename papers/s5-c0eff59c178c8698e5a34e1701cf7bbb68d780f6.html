The case FOR (proponent)

There is no eligible named anchor in this thesis under your rule. It has the numbered LP formulation in (4.1)–(4.4), Algorithm 1, and empirical runtime tables, but no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a complexity result. Section 6.1 mentions coNP-hardness for general combinatorial option bids, but attributes it to Wang et al. [24]; it is not a numbered result proved in this thesis. So I cannot honestly present an anchor-based case as requested.

The strongest positive case I can make is a candidate mirror of the thesis’s core clearing model, anchored only if its formulation is accepted as a computational result. Call it Mass Payoff-Dominant Option Clearing. Fix one underlying and expiration. A type \(t\) is a one-contract order with its side, call or put, strike, and bid or ask price. The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over the active types, where \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of unit-contract orders of type \(t\). Given that distribution, choose filled masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) and a per-capita margin \(\ell\) to maximize bid revenue minus ask cost minus margin. Require that, at every terminal underlying price \(S\ge 0\), the payoff from filled buy orders minus the payoff from filled sell orders is at most \(\ell\); also require nonpositive net call exposure as \(S\to\infty\). Output an optimal \(x\) and \(\ell\). In the thesis’s notation, these are the mass versions of (4.1)–(4.4), with each option payoff \(h_t(S)\) equal to \(\max\{S-K,0\}\) for a call or \(\max\{K-S,0\}\) for a put.

This is a population continuization: the objects distributed across types are order units, and mass represents shares of the batch’s contract demand. The option payoffs stay unchanged. If the number of unit-contract orders is \(N\), the discrete book is the distribution assigning mass \(1/N\) to each order; multiplying the continuous solution by \(N\) recovers aggregate fills and margin. The variable \(x_t\) is the clearing decision, and the objective and payoff-dominance condition preserve the thesis’s underlying problem.

A plausible regime is a large periodic auction for a liquid index-option expiration: many customers and trading systems submit one-contract requests at repeated side, strike, and limit-price combinations. The support \(\tau\) is the number of distinct active order types, potentially much smaller than the number \(N\) of contract units. Each type contains all attributes this clearing model uses; if the mechanism used additional restrictions or priorities, those would need to be included too. The thesis’s sample suggests repeated unit contracts are possible, but it does not report the number of distinct order types, so it does not establish that \(\tau\ll N\) in its data.

For this base mirror, I expect tractability (Class A). The payoff constraints need only be checked at \(S=0\) and at the distinct strikes, because the payoff difference is piecewise linear; the call-exposure condition handles the infinite-price limit. Thus the mass model is an LP with polynomially many variables and constraints in \(\tau\) and the rational input bit length. This is the same structural reason the paper’s finite-order LP is manageable, and Section 5 reports sub-second solves on its tested bundles. Those runtimes are empirical evidence, not a polynomial-time theorem.

The authors would likely recognize the question: it retains their order sides, prices, cross-strike fills, revenue objective, and worst-case payoff protection. Its weakest point is also clear: the thesis already permits fractional fills, so this could look like an aggregation and scaling interpretation of its LP rather than a distinct continuous-population result. The population reading is most convincing when there are many indistinguishable unit orders; the thesis neither names that population model nor proves its complexity classification. A natural follow-up is to compare integral clearing with this mass optimum as \(N\) grows at fixed support, then ask where the tractability boundary lies when the thesis’s general combinatorial option bids are allowed.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that this thesis has no eligible named complexity result to mirror. Equations (4.1)–(4.4) specify an LP, Algorithm 1 describes how to solve it, and Section 5 reports runtimes; none is a numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a complexity result. The coNP-hardness claim in Section 6.1 is attributed to Wang et al., not proved here. Under the requested anchor rule, there is no paper-native result for a continuous mirror to explain.

The proposed order-flow mirror is still a sensible scenario: a large batch could contain many one-contract requests with the same side, strike, and limit price. But its mass formulation is already the thesis’s fractional LP in compressed form. The thesis splits larger trades into unit orders and permits each fill variable to lie in \([0,1]\). For identical orders of type \(t\), summing their fill variables gives a type-level fill \(x_t\in[0,n_t]\). The objective, strike-payoff constraints, and call-exposure constraint depend only on those sums. Dividing by total order volume gives the proposed mass variables and per-capita margin. Conversely, any aggregate fill can be distributed across the identical unit orders. This is an exact reformulation, not a new limiting result or a new algorithmic problem.

A richer type definition could include linked orders, time priority, or trader-specific constraints. But then the model is no longer the thesis’s static clearing problem; it becomes a different market-design question. For the problem actually studied, the continuous mirror is already solved by the paper’s LP, and grouping identical orders adds no complexity classification to discover.

So I would reject this as a worthwhile ChoCo target on the evidence here: there is no named result to anchor, and the best candidate mirror duplicates the thesis’s fractional formulation. The negative case is weak on the universal claim, though. Repeated order types in a large auction are plausible, so I cannot honestly argue that no sensible continuous-population scenario exists; the case is that this particular mirror appears already covered by the paper’s own LP.
