What the mirror covers

The proposed extension computes a weighted endpoint aggregate corresponding to the endpoint rules. It does not turn Theorems 1–3 into computational results; their axiomatic characterizations remain outside the computational mirror.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is that this paper has a natural population to continuize. In the legal community-standard setting, many people can share the same permissible interval, and the relevant quantities are naturally shares of the community. A type would be a complete interval judgment \((a_t,b_t)\); \(\mu_t\) would be the fraction of the community holding it. The number of people could be very large while the number \(\tau\) of distinct judgments remains modest.

But I cannot honestly supply a qualifying anchor. The closest is Theorem 3, proved here: an aggregation rule is anonymous, strategyproof, and translation equivariant if and only if it is an endpoint rule. Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are also axiomatic characterizations. None asserts a computational complexity result such as tractability or hardness. Strategyproofness here is a property of a rule, not a complexity result.

An illustrative continuous problem would be Continuous Endpoint Aggregation. Given finite support \(T\), rational intervals \((a_t,b_t)\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and rational quotas \(\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)\) with \(\alpha+\beta\le1\), compute the aggregate interval \((a^*,b^*)\), where

\[ a^*=\min\left\{a_t:\sum_{s:a_s\le a_t}\mu_s\ge\alpha\right\}, \qquad b^*=\max\left\{b_t:\sum_{s:b_s\ge b_t}\mu_s\ge\beta\right\}. \]

This replaces the finite rule’s lower and upper order statistics with weighted quantiles. It asks for the community’s aggregate standard itself, so the authors would likely recognize the object as a population version of their aggregation problem. It should be tractable: sort the supported endpoints and accumulate their masses. The regime is plausible; the computational contribution is slight.

That distinction is decisive for the requested case. Continuous Endpoint Aggregation is a possible new computational question, not a problem whose complexity is established by a named result in this paper. A mass-transfer or manipulation problem could be more substantial, but the paper does not study costs or computational manipulation, so I would not present one as its mirror.

My weakest point is therefore the gap between a credible continuous population model and the paper’s actual results. The paper’s contribution is axiomatic, with a finite society. The continuous quantile problem is a defensible extension, but it cannot serve as an honest computational anchor for this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The population model is plausible here. A legal community, expert panel, or legislature can contain many people sharing the same interval judgment, so a distribution over interval types is sensible. That establishes a possible setting; it does not give this paper a computational result to mirror.

Theorem 3 is the proponent’s strongest anchor, but it characterizes which aggregation rules satisfy anonymity, strategyproofness, and translation equivariance. Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are likewise axiomatic characterizations. None states a complexity bound, algorithm, or hardness result. A continuous version could characterize weighted endpoint quantiles by axioms, but that would still be an axiomatic question, outside ChoCo’s computational scope.

The proposed Continuous Endpoint Aggregation problem is a coherent extension: given finitely many supported interval types and rational masses, compute the lower and upper weighted quantiles. Its direct algorithm sorts the supported endpoints and accumulates their masses. That simple computation is not itself a reason to dismiss the question; the decisive issue is that it is a newly posed computational task, not a computational result established by the paper. The paper presents endpoint rules as formulas and characterizes them through axioms.

The strategyproofness result does not supply a better computational anchor. In an atomless population, a single agent has zero mass, so unilateral manipulation loses its usual meaning. Giving a type’s entire mass the power to report strategically would instead make the manipulator a bloc and change the incentive question. Adding costs for changing interval judgments could create an optimization problem, but those costs and that optimization objective are absent from the paper.

So the case against is strong under ChoCo’s stated gate: this paper offers plausible population types but no named computational result for continuization to mirror. The universal claim is weaker. One could develop worthwhile algorithms for new problems involving interval communities, but the paper alone does not identify those problems as its computational mirrors.
