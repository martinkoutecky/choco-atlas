The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set of item kinds \(G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_m\}\), finite complete agent types \(\Theta\) with rational additive values \(v_{\theta,g}\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\), and rational per-capita copy supplies \(b_g\ge0\), promised that every item kind is subjective (\(v_{\theta,g}\ge0\) for some \(\theta\)), find a finite-support mass assignment \(\lambda_{\theta,a}\ge0\) over integral bundle-count vectors \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\), together with nonnegative cell payments \(\pi_{\theta,a}\), satisfying \(\sum_a\lambda_{\theta,a}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,a}\lambda_{\theta,a}a_g=b_g\) for every \(g\). For every two occupied cells \((\theta,a)\) and \((\theta',a')\), require \(\mathrm{EF1}\) for type \(\theta\): either \(u_\theta(a)\ge u_\theta(a')\), or for some \(g\) with \(a_g>0\) or \(a'_g>0\), \(u_\theta(a^{\setminus g})\ge u_\theta(a'^{\setminus g})\); also require \(u_\theta(a)+\pi_{\theta,a}\ge u_\theta(a')+\pi_{\theta',a'}\), where \(u_\theta(a)=\sum_g a_gv_{\theta,g}\) and \(a^{\setminus g}\) removes one copy when present. Return such \((\lambda,\pi)\).

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity indivisible-copy model in which agent types \(\theta\) carry masses \(\mu_\theta\), item kinds \(g\) have per-capita supplies \(b_g\), each unit of mass receives an integral bundle-count vector \(a\), and the decision variables are configuration masses \(\lambda_{\theta,a}\) and nonnegative cell subsidies \(\pi_{\theta,a}\).

The objection that survived

The stated \(\lambda_{\theta,A}\) model silently limits a bundle to one copy of each kind, so it must be repaired to use count-vector bundles \(a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\) for an exact denominator-cleared finite interpretation.

The case FOR (proponent)

There is a credible, though deliberately narrow, positive mirror here. The paper contains no named \(P\), NP-hardness, W[1]-hardness, or approximation theorem; its results are existence and constructive fairness results. My lead anchor is therefore constructive rather than a formal complexity classification.

The strongest anchor is Proposition 4.10, proved in this paper. It states that, given indivisible items partitioned into meta-goods \(M_1,\ldots,M_\ell\) and subjective goods \(X\), with \(X\cup\bigcup_j M_j=M\), Algorithm \(\mathrm{Meta\text{-}Goods}\) first refines the meta-goods until they are good-minimal and then applies iterative maximum-weight matching. The resulting allocation is always both \(\mathrm{EF1}\) and envy-freeable. Here \(\mathrm{EF1}\) is the paper’s item-removal fairness condition, while envy-freeability means that nonnegative monetary payments can be added so that the allocation becomes envy-free. The proof establishes envy-freeability through the absence of positive cycles in the envy graph and \(\mathrm{EF1}\) through the round-by-round matching inequalities plus good-minimality.

I would mirror the underlying allocation problem, not the meta-good representation itself.

Consider a large allocation system with a finite set of item kinds \(G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_m\}\). There are finitely many complete agent types \(\Theta\), where type \(\theta\) is determined by its additive values \(v_{\theta,g}\in\mathbb{Q}\) for every item kind. A society is a mass vector \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{\Theta}\) with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). Item supplies are also normalized per capita: \(b_g\in[0,1]\) means that item kind \(g\) is available to a mass \(b_g\) of agents. Each physical item remains indivisible: an individual receives an integral bundle \(A\subseteq G\), never a fractional item. Only the aggregate assignment is fractional.

The regime is a standardized high-multiplicity one: \(|\Theta|=\tau\) and \(m\) are modest, while the number of agents and the number of repeated item copies are large. A plausible setting would be a national or institutional allocation of standardized benefit-and-obligation packages, where many people have the same utility vector over the available items. This is much more convincing than applying the mirror to a seven-person divorce case.

The continuous problem is:

\(\mathrm{HM\text{-}EF1/EFable}\). Given \((\Theta,\mu,G,b,v)\), find nonnegative masses \(\lambda_{\theta,A}\), where \(\lambda_{\theta,A}\) is the mass of type-\(\theta\) agents receiving the integral bundle \(A\subseteq G\), such that \(\sum_A\lambda_{\theta,A}=\mu_\theta\) for every \(\theta\), and \(\sum_{\theta,A:g\in A}\lambda_{\theta,A}=b_g\) for every item \(g\).

For every two bundle-type cells \((\theta,A)\) and \((\theta',B)\) with positive mass, the allocation must satisfy \(\mathrm{EF1}\): either \(u_\theta(A)\ge u_\theta(B)\), or there is an item in \(A\cup B\) whose removal makes \(A\) at least as good as \(B\) for type \(\theta\), where \(u_\theta(A)=\sum_{g\in A}v_{\theta,g}\). It must also be envy-freeable: there must exist nonnegative payments \(p_{\theta,A}\) such that \(u_\theta(A)+p_{\theta,A}\ge u_\theta(B)+p_{\theta',B}\) for every pair of occupied cells. The output is the mass allocation \(\lambda\) and such a payment schedule. The objective is feasibility, exactly matching the existence/constructional nature of Proposition 4.10.

This is recognisably the authors’ problem. The utility model, indivisibility, item-removal fairness, and subsidy interpretation are unchanged. The only substantive change is replacing named agents by masses of indistinguishable agents and replacing a finite list of repeated item copies by per-capita supply. The meta-good procedure remains an algorithmic device; it is not being smuggled into the definition of the continuous problem.

I would tentatively expect this restricted mirror to be Class A. The variables \(\lambda_{\theta,A}\) form a finite configuration or transportation formulation, and once the occupied configurations are known, envy-freeability is a system of difference constraints, equivalently a no-positive-cycle condition. The matching steps in the paper should become capacitated matchings between agent types and item configurations, rather than matchings with one row per named agent. The main open issue is whether good-minimality and support selection can be compressed polynomially in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the input bit length.

The high-multiplicity bridge is genuine. For rational \(\mu\) and \(b\), replicate the instance so that there are \(N\mu_\theta\) agents of each type and \(Nb_g\) item copies of each kind. Apply the paper’s finite allocation theorem, record the frequencies of the resulting integral bundles, and take a limit. Because the bundle universe is finite, the frequencies have a convergent subsequence; \(\mathrm{EF1}\) is closed under this limit, and envy-freeability reduces to the finite no-positive-cycle condition on occupied type-bundle cells. Thus the continuous allocation is not merely “fractional fair division”: it is a distribution over integral allocations to individual agents.

This mirror covers the indivisible-items part of Proposition 4.10, especially the subjective-good/meta-good regime. It does not yet cover the paper’s cake result or the full mixed-goods-and-objective-chores construction. Those generate natural follow-up problems: an \(\mathrm{EFM}_\infty\) version with per-capita cake, a full objective-chore version corresponding to Theorem 4.2, and optimization variants minimizing total subsidy or maximizing welfare subject to \(\mathrm{EF1}\) and envy-freeability.

The weakest point is that Proposition 4.10 is not a complexity theorem, and the paper never analyzes the encoding length or the high-multiplicity algorithm. The proposed tractability classification is therefore a research conjecture, not something inherited from the paper. Moreover, the lead mirror omits cake and deliberately focuses on one constructive subproblem. Still, it is a faithful population continuization: the agents become continuous, the resources scale with population, and every individual continues to receive an integral bundle.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is strongest at the level of the anchor, but it is not airtight.

Proposition 4.10 is constructive, yet it is not a computational-complexity result. It gives an allocation for a finite instance already partitioned into meta-goods, using iterative maximum-weight matching, but it specifies neither an input-size model nor a running-time, approximation, or parameterized claim. The proposed continuous problem therefore does not continuize a computational theorem from the paper; it creates a new high-multiplicity fair-allocation problem.

There is also a mismatch in the proposed model. Proposition 4.10 allocates one finite set of individually identifiable items. The proposed mirror supplies many copies of each item kind and lets agents receive bundles \(A\subseteq G\). If an agent may receive at most one copy of each kind, that is an additional constraint absent from the paper; denominator-clearing the continuous instance does not produce that finite problem. If agents may receive multiple copies, configurations must instead be vectors in \(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{G}\), and the paper’s subset-based meta-good and good-minimality arguments no longer directly describe the model.

Nor is the suggested configuration formulation automatically an LP. The supply constraints are linear, but \(\mathrm{EF1}\) requires, for every pair of occupied configurations, a support-dependent disjunction:
\[ \lambda_{\theta,A}>0\ \wedge\ \lambda_{\theta',B}>0 \Longrightarrow \left[ u_\theta(A)\ge u_\theta(B) \ \vee\ \bigvee_{g\in A\cup B} u_\theta(A\setminus\{g\})\ge u_\theta(B\setminus\{g\}) \right]. \]
Envy-freeability likewise depends on the graph of occupied type-bundle cells. Thus the continuous problem involves choosing an exponentially large support and then checking difference constraints on that support. The finite proof’s ordered sequence of matchings does not itself yield a capacitated type-level algorithm.

The proponent’s compactness argument is semantically sound: for rational data, finite allocations can yield limiting mass allocations, and the fairness conditions are preserved. But that proves existence, not a compact algorithm. Clearing denominators and running the finite procedure is only pseudo-polynomial in the population multiplicity, while the genuinely interesting question—whether the mass allocation and payment schedule have a polynomial-size representation in the bit length—has not been supplied by the paper.

A stronger mirror could ask for minimum total subsidy or maximum welfare subject to \(\mathrm{EF1}\) and envy-freeability. Those would be legitimate research questions, but they are new optimization problems, not continuizations of a result actually proved here. Their formulation also requires choices about payment budgets, repeated item copies, and whether fairness is imposed between every occupied cell.

The negative case cannot honestly claim that fair division has no high-multiplicity regime. The proposed standardized package setting is plausible, and mass remains meaningful there. Nor is the mirror invalid merely because the finite theorem implies existence. Consequently, I would reject this as a strong ChoCo anchor—not because no continuous question can be formulated, but because the paper supplies no computational result whose population continuization is yet well-defined and algorithmically anchored. The universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario is therefore weak; the positive analyst’s mirror survives as a legitimate, though substantially new, research direction.
