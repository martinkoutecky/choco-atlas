A more ambitious mirror could put mass over agent states and ask whether coalition formation converges, what taxes emerge, or how the system responds to leader failure. The network structure makes that gap harder to bridge. The reported outcomes depend on local neighborhoods, hubs, and clustering. A continuum model that preserves those features may be possible, but then its central object is a limiting network and its dynamics; the paper provides no complexity question about that object to carry over. So the proponent raised no eligible anchor, and none survives as a paper-grounded computational mirror. This argues against spending ChoCo effort on this paper; it does not show that continuous models of networked cooperation are inherently uninteresting.
