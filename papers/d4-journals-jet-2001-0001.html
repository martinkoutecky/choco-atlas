The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The three theorems the proponent names are implementation results. Theorem 1 implements the Shapley value, Theorem 2 its weighted version, and Theorem 3 the Shapley value of the super-additive cover. Each characterizes equilibrium outcomes; none states a computational problem’s complexity or gives an algorithm with a running-time bound. Lemma 1 is a recursive identity. The paper says the equilibrium strategies are easy to compute, but it gives no input representation or complexity analysis. There is no computational result here for ChoCo to continuize.

The strongest possible reconstruction is Theorem 3: describe agents by types, give the value of a coalition as a function of its type masses, then study how to compute an efficient coalition partition and a corresponding continuum allocation. That could be a coherent research question, perhaps even one suited to continuous optimization. But the paper supplies neither that type-composition value function nor a way to encode it. Its game allows an arbitrary \(v:2^N\to\mathbb R\); a distribution over types does not determine coalition values unless additional symmetry assumptions are imposed. Those assumptions may describe sensible high-multiplicity settings, but choosing them, specifying the input, and defining the continuum allocation would create the computational problem rather than mirror one the paper studied.

The same gap applies to Theorems 1 and 2. A continuum version of their bidding mechanisms would require a specified mass-based game and rules for choosing a proposer and handling acceptance. Those may be worthwhile mechanism-design questions, but they do not supply a missing complexity result. The continuous bids, weights, and payoffs in the paper concern actions and outcomes, not a continuous population.

This is a strong reason to exclude the paper as a ChoCo mirror. It does not prove that no adjacent continuous study could be worthwhile: a carefully specified coalition-mass optimization problem might be. The narrower conclusion is that such a study would introduce the model and computational target; it would not continuize a computational result of this paper.
