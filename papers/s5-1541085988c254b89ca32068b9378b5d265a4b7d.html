The continuous mirror question

Given rational capability types \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^2\) and positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), let \(x^U_t=\mu_t\) for \(t\in U\) and \(0\) otherwise, and let \(Q_k(x)=\max\{v_t^k:x_t>0\}\) with \(Q_k(0)=0\). Output every \(U\subseteq T\) such that \(Q_1(x^U)+Q_2(x^U)>Q_1(\mu-x^U)+Q_2(\mu-x^U)\) and no proper \(V\subsetneq U\) satisfies this, together with \(\mu(U)=\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t\) and a completeness certificate.

The model it lives in

A type-support quotient of the max-presence game: types are exact capability profiles, mass is \(\mu\), the decision variable is \(U\subseteq T\), and the objective is to enumerate all inclusion-minimal winning supports and report their masses.

The objection that survived

With many identical copies of types \((10,0)\) and \((0,10)\), the finite game has distinct individual minimal winning coalitions while the atomless mass game has no inclusion-minimal coalitions, so the full-block support convention is not the literal high-multiplicity limit.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 2’s minimal-winning-coalition enumeration and the type-level consequence of Theorem 1’s bound; it leaves the four power-index algorithms, local monotonicity, and winning-coalition descriptions untouched.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can one formalize the discrete-to-support dictionary for \(K\) identical agents of a type and relate support outputs to the finite family of individual minimal winning coalitions?
  • Is there a nontrivial mass-sensitive analogue of minimal winning coalitions or the four power indices that remains faithful to coordinatewise maxima?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a deliberately narrow one: a high-multiplicity, two-dimensional C-WMMG whose primitive input is a distribution over finitely many exact player types. I would lead with the paper’s \(O(n\log n)\) minimal-winning-coalition result, not with its local-monotonicity theorem, since the latter is primarily axiomatic.

The regime is a large labour or sports population in which many agents share the same two-dimensional capability profile: for example, certified ability in two complementary roles. Let \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) be the distinct profiles, with type \(t\) having vector \(v_t=(v_t^1,v_t^2)\) and mass \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The total population may be \(n\gg\tau\); \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of the population of type \(t\). Types are complete descriptions: agents with the same type are interchangeable for the coalition problem.

A coalition is represented by a mass vector \(x\in[0,\mu]^T\), where \(x_t\) is the mass of type \(t\) assigned to it. Its complementary coalition has mass \(\mu-x\). Define

\[ Q_k(x)= \begin{cases} \max\{v_t^k:x_t>0\},&\text{if }x\neq 0,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \]

and let \(Q(x)=Q_1(x)+Q_2(x)\). The coalition wins precisely when

\[ Q(x)>Q(\mu-x). \]

For the minimal-coalition question, the correct continuum object is a minimal winning type support. For \(U\subseteq T\), let \(x^U_t=\mu_t\) if \(t\in U\), and \(x^U_t=0\) otherwise. Call \(U\) minimal winning if \(x^U\) wins and no proper subset \(V\subset U\) wins. The mass \(\mu(U)=\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t\) is the size of the winning coalition.

This is not an arbitrary restriction to whole type blocks. Under the paper’s max-based cooperation rule, adding more mass of an already-present type cannot reduce a coalition’s capability and can only reduce the complement’s capability. Thus every winning mass profile has a winning full-support representative \(x^{\operatorname{supp}(x)}\). The meaningful high-multiplicity output is therefore the list of minimal winning type supports, together with their masses, rather than a list of named individuals.

My lead anchor is Theorem 2 (\(t2\), the theorem at the end of Appendix 6, proved in this paper): in the two-dimensional C-WMMG, the complete set \(\mathcal{MWC}\) of minimal winning coalitions can be computed in \(O(n\log n)\) time. The preceding Theorem 1 (\(t1\), §3, also proved here) supplies the structural reason: \(|\mathcal{MWC}|\leq n+1\), and this bound is tight. The paper’s result is not a cited hardness claim; the hardness statements concern WMG and are cited comparisons, so I would not present them as C-WMMG anchors.

The corresponding continuous problem is:

Given rational type vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_\tau\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^2\), rational masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau>0\) summing to \(1\), and their encoding length \(L\), output the complete family

\[ \mathcal{MWC}_\infty = \left\{ U\subseteq T: Q(x^U)>Q(\mu-x^U) \text{ and no }V\subsetneq U\text{ satisfies this} \right\}. \]

A correct solution must list every such \(U\), report \(x^U\) or \(\mu(U)\), and certify completeness: every winning type support must contain one listed minimal support. The expected guarantee is

\[ |\mathcal{MWC}_\infty|\leq \tau+1, \]

with an \(O(\tau\log\tau)\)-time algorithm, polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(L\).

I expect this problem to be Class A. The proof does not fundamentally use the identities of the \(n\) players. It identifies the two coordinate-maximizing sets, sorts the remaining “benchwarmer” types by their first and second coordinates, constructs the blocking sets \(D_{1t}\) and \(D_{2t}\), tests the candidate coalitions, and removes duplicates. Running exactly that procedure on the \(\tau\) type representatives gives the continuous algorithm. The paper’s own Theorem 1 becomes the sharper high-multiplicity statement that the output is linear in the number of distinct types, even when the underlying society contains arbitrarily many agents. This is a genuine computational-population question, not merely a restatement: the input is \((\mu,v)\), the output is a compressed family of mass coalitions, and the relevant complexity parameter is \(\tau\), not the number of named agents.

The authors should recognize this as their problem. It preserves the distinctive content of their model: complementary rather than additive cooperation, coordinatewise maxima, comparison with the complementary coalition, busy players, benchwarmers, and minimal winning coalitions. Nothing has been softened into a tractable surrogate. The only change is the high-multiplicity representation of the population. The paper already treats identical characteristic vectors as interchangeable for the coalition rule; the continuous mirror makes that interchangeability explicit and records how much population has each profile.

The main further question is whether the individual-level power indices admit a similarly clean mass version. The natural next step would be to ask for continuum Holler-Packel and Deegan-Packel indices of types, with type mass replacing the multiplicity of named agents. But this is not automatic. Literal atomless Banzhaf or Shapley sampling can degenerate, because a positive mass of every type may appear on both sides and therefore reveal every coordinate maximum. A useful power-index mirror would need an explicit type-support sampling or high-multiplicity limiting convention.

That is also my weakest point. In the bare winning predicate, \(\mu\) affects the size of a coalition but not its capability: any positive mass of a type supplies the same maximum. If a referee insists that a continuum mirror must allow arbitrary splitting of identical types and must retain the literal definition of minimal measurable coalitions, minimality can fail to exist or become non-informative. My defence is that the paper’s max operation makes type support the economically meaningful object in the high-multiplicity regime, and that the proposed problem explicitly asks for minimal winning type supports rather than pretending that atomless individual minimality is well behaved. If that convention is rejected, the positive case becomes much weaker; under it, the paper offers a particularly clean Class A mirror.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Both anchors fail for the same structural reason: C-WMMG is a max-presence game, not a mass game. Let \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) be the mass of type \(t\) in a coalition. Then

\[ Q_k(x)=\max\{v_t^k:x_t>0\},\qquad Q_k(\mu-x)=\max\{v_t^k:x_t<\mu_t\}. \]

Whenever \(0<x_t<\mu_t\), changing the amount of type \(t\) changes neither maximum. The winning predicate therefore sees only whether a type is absent, partially present, or completely captured; it does not see the amount of mass. This is precisely the opposite of the population continuization ChoCo is designed to study.

The proponent’s repair—taking \(x_t\) to be either \(0\) or \(\mu_t\)—is not forced by high multiplicity. It imposes an all-or-nothing rule on each type. The paper’s coalitions are subsets of individual players, and minimality means that deleting one player makes the coalition lose. Identical players are interchangeable, but they are not required to enter a coalition together.

Indeed, arbitrary measurable coalitions have pathological minimality. Take types \(A=(10,0)\) and \(B=(0,10)\), both of positive mass. A coalition containing all of \(B\) and any positive amount of \(A\) wins: its value is \(20\), while its complement has value \(10\). But the amount of \(A\) can always be reduced while remaining positive, so no such coalition is inclusion-minimal. The proposed type-support object declares \(\{A,B\}\) minimal only because it has silently replaced measurable coalitions by whole type blocks.

This is not merely a harmless representation choice. With \(K\) finite copies of \(A\) and one copy of \(B\), the paper has \(K\) distinct minimal winning coalitions, one for each choice of the decisive \(A\)-player. The proposed continuum object has one support, \(\{A,B\}\), and does not represent any of those minimal coalitions. It is a new quotient game whose coalitions are type classes, not a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s \(\mathcal{MWC}\).

Consequently, Theorem 1’s bound \(|\mathcal{MWC}|\le n+1\) does not yield the claimed continuous result in the relevant sense. If one asks for minimal measurable coalitions, they may not exist. If one asks for minimal type supports, one has changed the object being minimized. The fact that filling an occurring type to its full mass preserves winning only proves that a winning full-support representative exists; it does not preserve minimality.

The same defeats Theorem 2. Running the paper’s \(O(n\log n)\) sorting procedure on the \(\tau\) distinct vectors gives an \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) algorithm, but this is just deduplication followed by the original finite algorithm. The masses \(\mu_t\) play no role in deciding winners, minimality, or the algorithm; they merely label the output with a mass after the fact. The result is therefore a compressed computation on a finite catalogue of capability vectors, not a computation over a continuous society.

A more faithful mirror cannot rescue the anchors. Minimizing coalition mass produces unattained infima in examples like the one above. Imposing a minimum mass, a granularity, or an all-or-nothing type rule either reintroduces a finite population or explicitly creates the type-support quotient. Making capability depend on accumulated mass would be a potentially interesting complementary-production model, but it would no longer mirror the paper’s coordinatewise-max rule or its two theorems.

The negative case is not logically airtight: if ChoCo is willing to count a distribution-labelled type-support quotient as a continuous mirror, then this paper supplies a clean Class A example. Under the programme’s stronger requirement that population mass be a meaningful computational object, however, both proposed anchors collapse before any complexity question begins.
