The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that this paper gives no named computational result to mirror. It defines Audience Selection and reports empirical comparisons, but proves no complexity bound, algorithm, or hardness result for the optimization problem. Its named Hypothesis 1 is a claim about how centrality rankings compare under a particular simulation setup, not a result about computational complexity. A continuous version could be a new study, but it would not mirror a computational result established here.

The best candidate is to ask whether the ranking discrepancy in Hypothesis 1 persists for audiences represented by masses of network types. That runs into a modeling problem: influence depends on who is connected to whom. To preserve that information, a type must encode an agent’s relevant network relationships as well as their diffusion parameters. In the paper’s sparse social networks, those descriptions may be nearly unique, leaving little repeated mass to aggregate. Coarsening types to a few centrality or demographic classes risks discarding the neighborhood overlap that drives cascade spread.

A graphon or finite-block model could supply a continuous-population question, but it would add a network-limit model and ask whether an empirical ranking pattern persists there. That may be worth studying on its own; the paper does not establish the result that would make it a continuous mirror of this work. This negative case is strongest on the lack of a computational anchor and weaker as a universal claim: a plausible block-structured audience model could still make a worthwhile related problem.
