The continuous mirror question

Given a connected finite site graph \(H=(V,E)\), valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational additive utilities \(u_t(v)\), site capacities \(\rho_v\), and an average allocation target \(r\), decide whether there exist nonnegative variables \(x_{t,B}\), indexed by nonempty connected bundles \(B\subseteq V\), such that \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B\ni v}x_{t,B}\le\rho_v\), \(\sum_{t,B}|B|x_{t,B}=r\), and \(x_{t,B}x_{s,D}>0\Rightarrow u_t(B)\ge u_t(D)\) for all supported bundles, where \(u_t(B)=\sum_{v\in B}u_t(v)\).

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity connected fair-division configuration model: agent types carry masses \(\mu\), each agent receives an integral connected bundle, \(x_{t,B}\) records bundle frequencies, site capacities scale per capita, and feasibility requires support-wise ex-post envy-freeness.

The objection that survived

The item graph's multiplicity is not canonical: site capacities do not by themselves specify how copies inherit connectivity, and the explicit mixture shows the proposed support-wise EF problem can be Yes when the paper's finite instance is No.

fatal: False

The case FOR (proponent)

There is a defensible mirror, but it should continuize the agents, not the items. My lead is a high-multiplicity version of EF-ICFD, with integral connected bundles and fractional assignment frequencies.

The best anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper. It states that EF-ICFD is W[1]-hard parameterized by \(p+|A|\), even when the item graph is a star. The reduction is from the cited Proposition 10, which says that \((k,N^{2k})\)-SUM is W[1]-complete parameterized by \(k\). Given \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\) and target \(t\), the paper constructs a star with center \(d_1\), leaves \(v_1,\ldots,v_N\), and a further leaf \(d_2\). There are two agents with identical valuations,
\[ u(v_i)=a_i,\qquad u(d_1)=1+\sum_i a_i,\qquad u(d_2)=u(d_1)+t, \]
and \(p=k+2\). Lemma 11, proved in the paper, establishes that the ICFD instance has an envy-free allocation exactly when some \(k\) of the \(a_i\) sum to \(t\). Thus the parameter becomes \(p+|A|=k+4\), and the hardness already holds with two agents, identical valuations, and a star graph. The paper also notes that the construction works with binary valuations bounded polynomially in the relevant instance parameters.

I would mirror this with Continuous High-Multiplicity EF-ICFD. An instance contains a connected item-site graph \(H=(V,E)\), a finite set of agent valuation types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), additive utilities \(u_t(v)\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_v\) of the item sites, and a rational per-capita allocation target \(r\). A connected bundle pattern is a nonempty connected subset \(B\subseteq V\), with value
\[ u_t(B)=\sum_{v\in B}u_t(v). \]

The decision variable is \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\), the mass of type-\(t\) agents receiving the integral connected bundle \(B\). It must satisfy
\[ \sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t \]
for every type \(t\),
\[ \sum_{t,B\ni v}x_{t,B}\le \rho_v \]
for every item site \(v\), and
\[ \sum_{t,B}|B|x_{t,B}=r. \]
Thus every unit of population receives an integral connected bundle, while \(x\) records only the fraction of the population receiving each bundle. The unused item density is \(\sum_v\rho_v-r\).

To preserve the paper’s EF notion as closely as possible, require ex-post type-level envy-freeness: whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\) and \(x_{s,D}>0\),
\[ u_t(B)\ge u_t(D). \]
In words, almost every type-\(t\) agent must weakly prefer its own integral bundle to every bundle actually assigned to any other type. The continuous problem asks whether such an \(x\) exists, and returns \(x\) as a solution.

This is genuinely a population continuization. A rational solution can be scaled by a common denominator \(Q\): it becomes \(Q\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\), \(Q\rho_v\) copies of each item-site class, and \(Qx_{t,B}\) agents assigned to each connected bundle pattern. Conversely, a repeated finite ICFD instance normalizes to these masses. The graph and bundles remain discrete; only the population frequencies are continuous. Scaling per-capita resource supply is forced by the fact that every agent must receive a nonempty bundle.

A plausible regime is a large organisation assigning connected office or equipment blocks to a very large recurring population of teams. Teams may fall into only a few valuation types—for example, research groups with the same requirements—while the campus graph and its repeated resource sites are large. Here \(Q\) is enormous but \(\tau=|T|\) is small. Saying that \(37\%\) of teams have one valuation profile and \(63\%\) another is more natural than naming every team individually.

The authors should recognise this as their problem: the graph, additive utilities, incomplete allocation, connected bundles, and envy-freeness are all retained. The only co-modification is that item supply scales with population, so that \(r\) is the analogue of \(p/|A|\). The paper’s Theorem 1 reduction is especially suggestive because its hardness is already present with only two identical agents. The combinatorics live in selecting connected resource bundles and matching their values, not in differences between named agents.

I would expect this mirror to fall in Class B, although proving the transfer is itself a worthwhile problem. Repeat the star gadget at high multiplicity, with per-capita supplies corresponding to the two-agent construction. Since bundles remain integral and EF is imposed on every bundle in the support, mixing population mass over several bundles does not automatically average away the subset-sum condition: every positive-mass bundle must still meet the relevant envy comparison. A continuous solution should therefore still expose a connected bundle containing the center and a \(k\)-subset of leaves whose values sum to \(t\). The natural parameter becomes \(r+\tau+\operatorname{vcn}(H)\), and the reduction has \(\tau=1\) and \(\operatorname{vcn}(H)=1\).

The main further questions are whether the W[1]-hardness can be lifted formally to this parameterization; whether allowing several bundle patterns in the support creates a genuine fractional escape; and whether an ex-ante variant, in which fairness compares type-average utilities rather than every realized bundle, collapses to a configuration LP with a tractable pricing problem. That ex-ante version may become Class A or continuum-specifically hard, but I would not use it as the lead mirror because it changes EF more visibly.

My weakest point is precisely the blow-up theorem. The paper proves hardness for two named agents and a fixed total \(p\), whereas the mirror replaces this by a large population, per-capita supply, and an average allocation target. A continuous mixture of bundles could introduce solutions absent from the two-agent instance unless the repeated gadget and capacity constraints are designed carefully. So I would claim a strong, faithful continuous question and a credible expectation of hardness transfer—not that Theorem 1 already proves the continuous result.

This mirror covers Theorem 1 only. I would not stretch it to claim that Theorems 3–5 automatically continuize: their kernel bounds and color-coding algorithm depend on the finite-agent and integer-\(p\) formulations in ways that require separate analysis.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that Theorem 1 does not survive the proposed population lift. The original instance has one copy of each graph vertex, so every agent receives a distinct nonempty bundle and \(n\le p\le |V(G)|\). Letting the population become large therefore requires replicating the item graph as well. The proposed capacities \(\rho_v\) do not specify how those copies are connected. Quotienting many copies into one “site” preserves utilities and capacities, but not the connected-bundle relation. Different graph blow-ups produce different configuration spaces, so this is not a canonical high-multiplicity version of ICFD; it is a new multi-unit connected-configuration problem.

More seriously, the claimed reduction already fails under the proponent’s own model. Take the paper’s star construction with \(k=3\), \(t=10\), and leaf values
\[ 5,5,2,2,2,4. \]
There is no three-element subset summing to \(10\): the relevant two- and three-element sums are \(5+5=10\) and \(2+2+2+4=10\), but no three-element sum is \(10\). Hence the paper’s two-agent EF instance is a No-instance.

In the proposed continuous model, set every site capacity to \(1/2\), set the type mass to \(1\), and set the average allocation target to
\[ r=\frac{k+2}{2}=\frac52. \]
Use mass \(1/4\) on the connected bundle consisting of \(d_1\) and the two \(5\)-valued leaves, mass \(1/4\) on the bundle consisting of \(d_1\) and the four leaves valued \(2,2,2,4\), and mass \(1/2\) on the singleton bundle \(\{d_2\}\). Every supported bundle has utility
\[ u(d_1)+10=u(d_2), \]
so support-wise EF holds. The average bundle size is
\[ \frac14\cdot3+\frac14\cdot5+\frac12\cdot1=\frac52, \]
and all site capacities are respected. Clearing denominators gives an ordinary finite repeated instance with four identical agents and two copies of each site. Thus the fractional escape is not a measure-theoretic artefact: high multiplicity genuinely changes the yes/no question encoded by the reduction.

One can repair this only by adding a constraint that is absent from ICFD: require every centre-containing bundle to have exactly \(k\) leaves, force agents to be partitioned into designated gadget copies, or otherwise prevent mixtures of different bundle cardinalities. Those repairs either add a new bundle constraint or restore a finite copy-by-copy allocation, so they no longer mirror Theorem 1. Replacing support-wise EF by average utility makes the problem even further removed: it is a lottery/configuration-LP variant, not the paper’s ex-post envy-freeness.

The office-and-equipment story is plausible, but it supports a new high-multiplicity connected-resource problem, not yet a faithful continuization of the paper’s theorem. If the standard for “worthwhile” includes any natural new configuration problem, then the universal negative cannot honestly be maintained. The negative case defeats the proposed anchor and its advertised hardness transfer, but it does not establish that every carefully designed population model would be uninteresting.
