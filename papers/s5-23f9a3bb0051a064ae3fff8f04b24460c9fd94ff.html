The continuous mirror question

Given child types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), home types \(K\) with capacities \(\nu_k\), feasible edges, and complete preference and evaluation rankings, compute a feasible assignment flow \(x_{\theta k}\), allowing unmatched mass, for which no feasible flow \(y\) admits, for each \(\theta\), a coupling \(z^\theta\) with old-destination marginal \(x_{\theta\cdot}\) and new-destination marginal \(y_{\theta\cdot}\) such that every positive \(z^\theta_{kk'}\) has \(I_\theta(k')\subseteq I_\theta(k)\), while positive mass is sent to some \(k'\in I_\theta(k)\). Here \(I_\theta(k)=\{k' : k'\succ_\theta k\text{ and }k'>_\theta k\}\), and \(I_\theta(\varnothing)=\{k:k\succ_\theta\varnothing,\ k>_\theta\varnothing\}\).

The model it lives in

A two-sided high-multiplicity market with child-type masses \(\mu_\theta\), home-type bed capacities \(\nu_k\), and assignment flow \(x\); complete rankings determine each type’s unanimous-improvement sets, and couplings compare old and new assignments.

The case FOR (proponent)

There is a real, but bounded, case for a continuous mirror. The paper gives no numbered NP-hardness, FPT, or polynomial-time complexity theorem. Its best anchors are constructive mechanism results: they prove that particular assignment procedures have particular properties, but do not analyze their running time. So this would be a new complexity study of the paper’s matching criteria, not a claim that the paper already charted their complexity.

My lead anchor is Proposition 4, proved in the appendix: Unanimous Top Trading Cycles (UTTC) produces a matching that is both unanimous and constrained-efficient. In the paper, unanimity means that no other matching weakly expands both the set of children assigned to unanimous homes and the set assigned to mutually acceptable homes, with a strict expansion in at least one set. Constrained efficiency means there is no Pareto improvement for the children that weakly expands those two sets. UTTC starts from a unanimous matching and uses restricted trades to improve efficiency while preserving unanimity.

A plausible high-multiplicity regime is a state-level or multi-county foster-care placement exchange that pools referrals and available beds over a planning window. A child’s type would include their caseworker ranking and the algorithm’s evaluation ranking over a finite catalogue of home types, along with any other matching-relevant information. A home type would group beds that are indistinguishable in the model’s feasibility and ranking data. If thousands of children and beds enter the exchange but the observed support contains relatively few distinct child types, the high-multiplicity premise is credible. Home capacity scaling with population is a natural part of this mirror: every individual receives one bed, while a fraction of a child type can be assigned to each home type.

Continuous Unanimity-Constrained Assignment (CUCA). An instance gives finite sets \(R\) of paired child-ranking types and \(K\) of home types, rational child masses \(\mu_\theta\) and bed capacities \(\nu_k\), and feasible type-to-home edges. The masses and capacities are normalized to the same total. A solution is a rational flow \(x_{\theta k}\ge 0\), with unmatched mass allowed, satisfying each type’s mass and each home type’s capacity.

For each child type \(\theta\), let \(H^*_\theta\) and \(H^-_\theta\) be its unanimous and other mutually acceptable homes, as defined in the paper. Write \(u_\theta(x)=\sum_{k\in H^*_\theta}x_{\theta k}\) for mass assigned to unanimous homes and \(a_\theta(x)=\sum_{k\in H^*_\theta\cup H^-_\theta}x_{\theta k}\) for mass assigned to mutually acceptable homes. Call \(x\) mass-unanimous if no feasible \(y\) has \(u_\theta(y)\ge u_\theta(x)\) and \(a_\theta(y)\ge a_\theta(x)\) for every \(\theta\), strictly for at least one type. Call it mass-constrained-efficient if no such \(y\) also Pareto-improves the child rankings. That Pareto condition can be stated through a coupling of old and new assignments: mass may move from \(k\) to \(k'\) only when \(k'\) is weakly preferred to \(k\), with positive mass moving to a strictly preferred home.

The question is to output a flow with both properties. I expect Class A, tractable, for explicit finite \(R\) and \(K\). The conditions are about masses in a finite transportation polytope. A likely route is sequential linear optimization over the unanimity and acceptable-mass coordinates, followed by preference-rank mass coordinates to rule out Pareto improvements. That algorithm is a proposed continuous result, not a runtime claim made by the paper.

A second, distinct anchor is Theorem 1, proved in the appendix: Adaptive Serial Dictatorship with Indifferences (ASDI) outputs an unimprovable matching. The paper calls a matching unimprovable when there is no reallocation under which every child’s set of unanimous-improving homes weakly shrinks, while some child is assigned a home that unanimously improves on their current one.

Continuous Unimprovable Assignment (CUA) uses the same instance and flow. For type \(\theta\) and home \(k\), let \(I_\theta(k)=\{k'\in K:k'\succ_\theta k\text{ and }k'>_\theta k\}\). A flow is mass-unimprovable if no feasible reassignment admits a coupling from old to new homes such that \(I_\theta(k')\subseteq I_\theta(k)\) for all moved mass, while positive mass moves to \(k'\in I_\theta(k)\). The solution is a feasible flow with no such reassignment. I also expect Class A here: maximizing the linear potential \(\sum_{\theta,k}x_{\theta k}(-|I_\theta(k)|)\) rules out the prohibited reallocation. Set inclusion makes every allowed move weakly increase this potential, and a unanimous improvement strictly increases it.

The strongest weakness is the type-compression assumption. In this paper, rankings and welfare evaluations are match-specific; if they make nearly every child’s full ranking pair unique, then \(\tau\) may grow with the population and the high-multiplicity regime disappears. The manuscript itself describes small daily matching markets, so the larger pooled exchange is a plausible alternative scenario, not the paper’s demonstrated one. The practical test is whether a real exchange has a moderate support of complete ranking types. If it does, these mirrors ask the same central question as the paper—how to allocate children to beds while respecting human and algorithmic rankings—at mass scale.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that neither anchor is a computational result of the kind ChoCo is set up to mirror. Proposition 4 proves that UTTC returns a unanimous, constrained-efficient matching from a chosen unanimous starting matching. Theorem 1 proves that ASDI returns an unimprovable matching. Neither studies the complexity of a decision problem, specifies a complexity measure, or claims a running-time bound. CUCA and CUA therefore formulate new continuous problems around the paper’s solution concepts; they do not continue computational questions the paper itself asks.

The proponent’s strengthened versions are mathematically coherent. CUCA could retain an initial assignment and ask for a feasible mass flow that preserves unanimity while ruling out coupled Pareto improvements. CUA could define mass-unimprovability through reassignment couplings. In fact, the proposed potential is sound: if \(k'\in I_\theta(k)\) and \(I_\theta(k')\subseteq I_\theta(k)\), the inclusion is strict, since \(k'\) belongs to the latter set and not the former. That gives a genuine continuous characterization. I cannot dismiss either mirror as a bad translation.

The practical case for the required type regime is weaker. A complete child type must preserve the caseworker’s ranking, the algorithm’s evaluation ranking, and every other matching-relevant distinction. The paper emphasizes match-specific welfare and qualitative caseworker information, features that can make exact ranking pairs rare. Its foster-care markets are local and fluid; pooling counties over a planning window and grouping beds into interchangeable classes changes the institutional setting. A repeated cohort with a modest support is possible, but the paper does not establish that its application has one. Coarse types would make support smaller by discarding distinctions the paper says matter.

So the negative case is a fit argument: these anchors support a new study of continuous matching mechanisms, but they do not expose a computational gap in this paper, and the motivating application gives little evidence of moderate support over complete types. That is enough to question whether they merit ChoCo’s time. It is not enough to establish the universal claim. If ChoCo accepts mechanism-property theorems as anchors and a pooled market as a credible regime, both mirrors remain worth asking. The honest negative verdict is therefore weak.
