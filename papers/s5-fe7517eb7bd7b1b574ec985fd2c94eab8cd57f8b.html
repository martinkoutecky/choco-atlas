The continuous mirror question

Given finite rational supports \(T_1\) and \(T_2\) of upstream and downstream user types, with masses \(\mu_t\) and type-specific endowments, benefit parameters, pollution contributions, and fallback payoffs, aggregate utilities and fallback payoffs using \(\mu_t\) and the paper's forms in Eqs. (4)–(5). Decide whether a common nonnegative transfer rate \(\alpha\) and type-level water allocations \(x_t\) satisfy both associations' aggregate participation conditions against their ATS/ATI fallbacks, and compute a feasible agreement if one exists.

The model it lives in

Keep two bargaining parties, upstream and downstream associations, and represent each by a finite type support with masses \(\mu_t\). The decisions are type-level water allocations \(x_t\) and a common balanced transfer rate \(\alpha\); each association evaluates the treaty through the mass-weighted sum of its members' utilities and fallback payoffs.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive finding is that this paper has no named computational result to anchor a continuous mirror. Proposition 1 gives an upstream agent’s participation condition; Proposition 2 gives the downstream agent’s; Lemma 3 gives a necessary condition for agreement; and Corollary 1 characterizes agreement by \(\alpha_1^l \le \alpha^* \le \alpha_2^u\). These are analytical bargaining results, not complexity claims or algorithmic results. Equations 6–7 set up the agents’ utility maximizations, but the paper does not analyze their computational complexity or present a computational method.

One could propose a new model of a continuum of repeated, similarly typed river-bargaining pairs, with mass over pairs’ endowments and benefit parameters, and treaty choices for each type. But the paper’s society is a single pair of agents; making water quantities or transfers continuous changes the outcomes, not the population. That extension would not mirror a named computational result of this paper, so there is no anchored continuous problem—or Class A/B/C prediction—to offer here.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has no theorem about complexity, but that does not leave its named results without a computational question. Propositions 1 and 2 and Corollary 1 characterize whether an acceptable transfer exists and identify the agreement point. With rational parameters as input, “does an individually rational agreement exist, and what transfer supports it?” is a decision and search problem directly anchored in those results. A continuous mirror can ask how that problem changes when the agents are populations of water users.

A plausible setting is a shared basin with many upstream and downstream users, such as farms or municipalities. A type records a user’s riparian side, water entitlement, benefit parameters, pollution contribution, and outside option; the finite support has masses \(\mu_t\). The users bargain through their associations over per-capita water allocations and balanced transfers, with the same negative-water responsibility and ATS/ATI fallback ideas as in the paper. Given the type masses and rational parameters, one can ask whether a treaty satisfying the relevant participation conditions exists, and compute an allocation and transfer. That is a high-multiplicity population model; scaling the basin’s supplies with its population is a natural part of the formulation.

The repeated-independent-basins idea is a weaker mirror, since it treats a distribution over bargaining instances as the population. The user-population scenario avoids that problem: types describe claimants within the basin itself. It does extend the paper’s two-agent setup to collective representation and possibly type-level participation constraints, so author recognition is the main uncertainty. If the two sovereign jurisdictions must remain the only agents in every scenario, the mirror case is weak. But the paper’s named agreement results and a plausible population of claimants are enough to reject the broader claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario.
