The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive point is that the paper has no named computational result to mirror. Its Gibbs updates are an inference procedure; its MIP is a formulation; and its runtime, likelihood, and recovery findings are empirical. None states a complexity bound, an exact or approximation algorithm theorem, or a hardness result. The MIP exception does not apply: the paper presents no theorem establishing its formulation.

The strongest salvage would start from a large class and ask about inference when grader or submission types recur. But the paper’s inference uses the bipartite grading graph \(G=(U,V,E)\), the reports on its edges, and latent parameters for individual graders and submissions. A distribution over grader types alone loses which graders evaluated which submissions. A “complete” type would need to encode enough of each agent’s grading neighborhood and reports to preserve that information; in a plausible class, those profiles may be mostly unique. Pooling more coarsely changes the target from individual grade and grader estimates to type-level averages. A graphon or other continuum model might be worth studying, but it would pose a new computational problem, not mirror a result established here.

The proposed MOOC scenario makes high multiplicity plausible, but it supplies no missing anchor. So the negative case is strong against counting this paper as a continuization target under the stated task. It is weaker as a claim that no worthwhile continuous problem could ever be inspired by the paper; that broader impossibility cannot honestly be established from the text.
