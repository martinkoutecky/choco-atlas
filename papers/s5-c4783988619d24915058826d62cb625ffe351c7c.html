The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is qualified: the paper has a natural continuous-population mirror, but it has no qualifying computational anchor under ChoCo’s strict rule.

The paper contains no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition establishing membership in \(\mathrm{P}\), hardness, approximation, or parameterized complexity. Its numbered statements are empirical “Results.” The strongest possible conditional anchor is therefore Result 1 (Observed Efficiency), reported and statistically supported in this paper: the observed efficiency of TTC is significantly higher than that of RSD, with one-tailed permutation-test \(p=0.0079\) in the original treatment, \(p<0.0001\) in the large treatment, and \(p=0.0198\) in the random treatment. This is not a computational theorem, so it cannot strictly satisfy the anchor requirement; it is nevertheless a genuine result about the underlying house-allocation problem, not merely about the experimental apparatus.

The natural mirror would be the following.

Continuous TTC–RSD Efficiency Gap. Let \(H\) be a fixed finite set of interchangeable house categories, with per-capita supply \(q_h\) for each \(h\in H\). Let \(\Theta\) be a finite set of complete resident types. A type \(\theta\) specifies whether the resident is a newcomer or incumbent, the incumbent’s current house category \(e_\theta\), a strict ranking \(\succ_\theta\) over \(H\), utility values \(u_\theta(h)\), and any behavioural parameters used to model participation or misreporting. The society is a rational mass distribution \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{\Theta}\), with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\).

For each incumbent type, let \(x_{\theta,\bot}\) be the mass that keeps its current house and let \(x_{\theta,\pi}\) be the mass that enters the lottery and reports ranking \(\pi\). Thus

\[ x_{\theta,\bot}+\sum_{\pi}x_{\theta,\pi}=\mu_\theta . \]

The mechanism is either RSD or TTC, with the same squatting rights, priority lottery, and house-allocation rules as in the paper. The continuous mechanism is defined as the high-multiplicity limit: replicate every mass by a factor \(N\), run the finite mechanism on the resulting \(N\)-replicated economy, divide welfare by \(N\), and take the limit as \(N\to\infty\) through denominators that make all masses integral.

If \(W_M^\infty(\mu,x)\) denotes the resulting expected aggregate utility under mechanism \(M\), the computational question is: given \(\mu,q,u,e,x\), and rational \(\delta,\varepsilon>0\), compute \(\varepsilon\)-accurate values of \(W_{\mathrm{RSD}}^\infty\) and \(W_{\mathrm{TTC}}^\infty\), and decide whether

\[ W_{\mathrm{TTC}}^\infty(\mu,x) \ge W_{\mathrm{RSD}}^\infty(\mu,x)+\delta . \]

Efficiency can be normalized by the mass-assignment optimum

\[ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu)= \max_{y} \sum_{\theta,h}u_\theta(h)y_{\theta h}, \]

subject to

\[ \sum_h y_{\theta h}=\mu_\theta, \qquad \sum_\theta y_{\theta h}=q_h, \qquad y_{\theta h}\ge0. \]

Here \(y_{\theta h}\) is only aggregate resident mass assigned to category \(h\): every individual resident still receives one indivisible house. The fractional object is population mass, not a divisible outcome.

This is a plausible high-multiplicity regime for large universities, public-housing systems, or corporate accommodation pools. There may be tens of thousands of residents but only a small number of standardized room categories and repeated resident types: newcomers, returning residents of each room class, and cohorts sharing the same preferences and utility table. The type description includes all relevant characteristics, so grouping residents is not an arbitrary loss of individual prices or preferences. The paper’s large treatment and simulations up to \(n=1{,}500\) already point toward this scaling regime; Result 10 reports that, across \(50\) random environments, TTC’s mean efficiency remains significantly higher than RSD as market size grows.

The authors should recognize this as their problem: it retains houses, squatting rights, strict preferences, RSD, TTC, participation, priority lotteries, and the efficiency objective. It also does not collide with the paper’s prior art, because the paper studies finite named residents and finite queues; the continuous model makes repeated resident types and population masses the primary input.

I would expect the fixed-\(|H|\), fixed-\(|\Theta|\), approximation version to be a plausible Class A problem. The benchmark is a transportation LP, and a type-level description may permit fluid-limit or configuration-based algorithms. The unrestricted exact version may be harder: evaluating RSD’s expectation over priority orders can retain counting-like complexity, while TTC may admit a more direct mass-cycle formulation. The resulting boundary—exact versus approximate evaluation, truthful versus behavioural reports, and fixed versus growing type support—is itself a worthwhile computational programme.

The weakest point is decisive: TTC and RSD are defined on finite priority queues, and the paper’s main evidence concerns boundedly rational human subjects. A referee could reasonably say that the replication limit is a new model rather than a result of this paper, especially unless existence and uniqueness of the limit are proved. More fundamentally, because Result 1 is empirical rather than computational, this remains a strong subject-matter mirror but not a formally compliant ChoCo anchor. Under the anchor rule as stated, the correct verdict is therefore: natural and potentially valuable continuous problem, but no qualifying positive case based on this paper’s named computational results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive negative point is that this paper has no qualifying computational anchor. Results 1–12 are empirical findings, not theorems about complexity, algorithms, approximation, or parameterized computation. The theoretical claims about RSD and TTC—strategy-proofness, individual rationality, and Pareto efficiency—are axiomatic properties. Under ChoCo’s explicit gate, there is therefore no named computational result whose continuous mirror can earn a green verdict.

Result 1 does not repair this. It says that, in finite one-shot experiments, TTC generated higher observed efficiency than RSD. The proposed mirror changes what is being studied. If the masses \(x_{\theta,\pi}\) are given as inputs, the behavioural content of the experiment has been conditioned away: the problem is merely to evaluate two allocation procedures on an imposed population of reports and participation decisions. If those masses are meant to be generated endogenously, the paper supplies no population-level behavioural model. Its logit analysis concerns opting out, while misreporting is classified descriptively; there is no canonical law governing boundedly rational agents in the limit. Adding one would create a new model rather than continuize Result 1.

The mechanism itself also does not have an automatic continuum interpretation. RSD is defined through a finite priority queue with a first agent. A nonatomic population has no first uniformly random agent. One must introduce a priority-time process and specify how agents with identical types and houses are interleaved. TTC is even more dependent on identity: its cycles use the ownership relation between particular tenants and particular houses. Replacing those identities by masses requires a new rule for selecting owners and forming cycles. Different replication and correlation conventions can define different fluid mechanisms. Saying “replicate and take the limit” does not by itself select one.

The proposed efficiency benchmark is not the paper’s benchmark either. The paper normalizes by Pareto-efficient group earnings; in the random treatment it uses a Monte Carlo average over efficient allocations. The proposed

\[ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu) = \max_y \sum_{\theta,h} u_\theta(h)y_{\theta h} \]

is a utilitarian transportation optimum. It is a perfectly legitimate continuous assignment benchmark, and aggregate fractional mass is not itself a problem. But it changes the dependent variable. Recovering the paper’s benchmark would require a canonical probability distribution over continuum Pareto-efficient allocations, which the paper does not define.

The strongest repair would be to specify a finite type space, an explicit priority-time fluid mechanism, a type-level ownership convention, and an exogenous behavioural kernel. That could be a sensible high-multiplicity assignment project; the large treatment is evidence in its favour, not evidence against it. But it would be a new computational study of fluid RSD and TTC, not a continuous counterpart of any computational result in this paper. Results 10–12 do not help: they are simulation and education findings, not computational theorems.

So the strict ChoCo verdict is negative: this paper offers no qualifying anchor. The broader claim that no worthwhile continuous assignment problem can ever be formulated is weaker and should not be overstated. A finite-type fluid version of RSD/TTC may well be worth studying. What fails is the claim that this experimental paper itself supplies a computational result whose continuization is the programme’s object.
