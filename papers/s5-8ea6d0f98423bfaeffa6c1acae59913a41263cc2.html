The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is the gap between what this paper measures and what ChoCo studies. Its findings compare fairness and efficiency in experiments with three partners; it gives no complexity result or algorithmic theorem. The formal result it mentions for the Impartial Division Rule is an axiomatic characterization, and the paper’s own equilibrium discussion is expressly limited by unspecified fairness preferences. There is therefore no computational result here to continue into a continuous society.

The many-owner oil-field setting is plausible, but making it a ChoCo instance requires substantial new structure. Each partner reports a claim vector over named partners, \(m^i\in\mathbb{R}^n\), with \(\sum_j m^i_j=S\). Aggregating partners into types requires symmetry in those recipient-specific claims, plus rules for translating type-level mass into individual shares. That could be a sensible model for repeated, nearly identical rights; the paper’s three-partner experiment does not establish that model or study its computation.

The strongest candidate would be a population problem about changing reports to reach a target allocation, or finding strategic equilibria. But that needs report-change costs, a target, and a formal account of how agents value fairness. Those ingredients supply the computational problem; the paper does not. For the paper’s three-agent mechanisms, computing an allocation uses formulas or a small sort. Under symmetric finite-type extensions, aggregation remains direct. A more complex algorithmic problem might make a worthwhile successor project, but it would be a new model built from the paper’s mechanisms, rather than a continuous mirror of one of its results.

So the negative case is strong as a reason to exclude this paper as a ChoCo mirror. It is weaker as a universal claim that no worthwhile continuous project could ever grow from it: the many-owner application gives a plausible starting point, though the paper itself leaves the essential computational question to be supplied.
