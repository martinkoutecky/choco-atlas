The continuous mirror question

Given a graph template \(H=(V,E)\) in the blow-up-of-subdivided-stars class, a common binary valuation \(w:V\to\{0,1\}\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\lambda_v\in[0,1]\), does there exist a distribution \(x_B\geq 0\) over connected bundles \(B\subseteq V\), including the empty bundle, such that \(\sum_B x_B=1\), \(\sum_{B\ni v}x_B=\lambda_v\) for each \(v\in V\), and every pair \(B,B'\) with \(x_B,x_{B'}>0\) satisfies the paper’s \(EF1_{\text{outer}}\) condition: either \(B'=\emptyset\), or some outer vertex \(u\in B'\) has \(w(B)\geq w(B'\setminus\{u\})\)?

The model it lives in

Use a graph template \(H\) whose vertices are resource roles, a common binary role valuation \(w\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\lambda_v\). Distribute unit agent mass across connected bundles \(B\subseteq V(H)\), with each agent receiving at most one of each role, aggregate mass \(\lambda_v\) receiving role \(v\), and pairwise \(EF1_{\text{outer}}\) across supported bundles.

What the mirror covers

The mirror targets Theorem 27’s common-binary valuation result on blow-ups of subdivided stars; it leaves the bounded-bandwidth hardness result and the paper’s structural and existence results aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can feasibility be decided in polynomial time in \(|V(H)|\) and the bit length of \(\lambda\) using an LP with an efficient pricing oracle for connected bundles?
  • Can a mass solution be rounded to finite allocations while preserving or approximating \(EF1_{\text{outer}}\)?
  • Does the pricing approach extend to the bounded-bandwidth class or to common nonbinary additive valuations?

The case FOR (proponent)

The lead is a continuous mirror of Theorem 27, proved in this paper. It states that deciding whether a graph admits an EF1\(_{\text{outer}}\) allocation is NP-complete even (i) on blow-ups of subdivided stars with common binary additive valuations, and (ii) on bounded-bandwidth graphs with common additive valuations. I rely on the first restriction: the underlying problem is to partition the graph’s vertices into connected bundles so that every agent’s envy can be removed by deleting an outer vertex. The paper proves this result here, by reduction from Partition; the displayed construction has two agents.

Call the mirror Mass-Connected-EFO. Take a graph template \(H=(V,E)\) from that first graph class and a common binary additive valuation \(w:V\to\{0,1\}\). There is a unit mass of agents, all of this one complete type, so the type support is \(\tau=1\); a finite-type extension could have fixed or moderate \(\tau\). Think of many residents with the same use-value profile competing for connected land or network parcels. As the population grows, the stock of each vertex role grows with it: \(\lambda_v\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]\) is the per-capita supply of role \(v\). Each unit of agent mass still receives an integral connected bundle \(B\subseteq V\), containing at most one copy of each role. Only the mass receiving each bundle is fractional.

Precisely, the input is \(H,w,\lambda\). The question is whether there are masses \(x_B\geq 0\), one for each connected \(B\subseteq V\) (including the empty bundle), such that
\[ \sum_B x_B=1,\qquad \sum_{B\ni v}x_B=\lambda_v\quad\text{for every }v\in V, \]
and the bundles in the support of \(x\) satisfy EF1\(_{\text{outer}}\): for every two supported bundles \(B,B'\), either \(B'=\emptyset\), or there is a vertex \(u\in B'\) whose removal leaves \(B'\setminus\{u\}\) connected and
\[ w(B)\geq w(B'\setminus\{u\}). \]
The equalities allocate all per-capita supply. A solution is the supported bundle family and its rational masses. The role supplies are the necessary scaling with population; recipients’ bundles, connectivity, utilities, and outer-good test retain the paper’s structure.

I expect this restricted mass problem to be Class A, though that is a conjectured direction, not a result of the paper. For common binary valuations, let \(\delta(B)=1\) if \(B\) has a value-one outer vertex and \(0\) otherwise. If \(q\) is the minimum value among supported bundles, EF1\(_{\text{outer}}\) is equivalent to every supported \(B\) satisfying \(q\leq w(B)\leq q+\delta(B)\). Enumerate \(q\in\{0,\ldots,|V|\}\); for each value, feasibility is an LP over connected bundles in that value band. There may be exponentially many bundle columns, but on a blow-up of a subdivided star a pricing dynamic program can choose center vertices and arm prefixes while tracking total binary value and whether a valuable outer vertex exists. That makes an LP-plus-pricing algorithm plausible. It also explains how the discrete Partition obstruction might dissolve: the mass allocation can split across bundle patterns instead of assigning each chain wholly to one of two named agents. Further questions are whether this pricing argument is polynomial for the stated blow-up class, whether it extends to arbitrary graphs or common nonbinary additive valuations, and whether a mass solution can be rounded to a discrete EF1\(_{\text{outer}}\) allocation with a useful guarantee.

The weakest point is direct continuity from the anchor: Theorem 27’s reduction uses just two agents, so its NP-hardness is driven by the item graph and Partition structure, not by population multiplicity. The theorem therefore does not itself imply that the hardness survives—or disappears—in Mass-Connected-EFO. The mirror is a credible new instance regime for the same allocation question, but its tractability case rests on the proposed LP and pricing structure, not on a transfer theorem from this paper. It covers Theorem 27’s common-binary blow-up-star result only.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest skeptical point is that this paper’s population shares a finite stock of uniquely located vertices. For a fixed graph with \(m\) vertices, normalizing an \(n\)-agent instance gives each vertex supply \(1/n\). The total mass receiving nonempty bundles is at most \(m/n\), so as \(n\) grows the fixed-graph limit puts all mass on the empty bundle. Keeping the allocation substantive requires scaling the resource graph with the population. That makes this a less direct population limit than a society whose members repeat a fixed set of preference types.

Theorem 27 reinforces that concern: its hardness construction uses two agents and encodes Partition in the graph and its valuations. That construction gives little evidence that the difficult regime itself has high multiplicity. A mirror would need to choose how graph roles are replicated, how copies are connected, and whether an agent may receive more than one copy of a role. Those choices affect the allocation problem, so the mirror’s naturalness depends on the authors accepting a particular resource-scaling model.

That is a real reason to rank the mirror as less immediate. It does not defeat the best version. Per-capita supplies \(\lambda_v\) for graph roles, with each unit of agent mass receiving an integral connected bundle, are a coherent model of many residents sharing a growing land or network system. With common binary valuations, the agents form a single complete type, and rational bundle masses recover finite high-multiplicity instances after scaling by a common denominator. The paper’s cited work on graphical cakes concerns continuous resources; it does not already answer the computational question for a continuous population.

Theorem 27’s two-agent reduction does not establish how its complexity result behaves under that scaling. That limits what the paper itself contributes to the mirror, but the programme asks whether the new computational question is worthwhile, not whether the old proof transfers. I cannot honestly make the case that no scenario produces a worthwhile mirror. The fixed-resource limit is degenerate, and the scaled-resource story requires care; the proposed Mass-Connected-EFO question remains plausible, so this anchor survives.
