The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent gives no computational anchor: both main theorems characterize which social preferences satisfy axioms. A continuum version of Theorem 2’s characterization may be coherent, but it remains axiomatic, outside ChoCo’s stated question. The proposed distribution over complete types \((u,P)\) makes a large population plausible; it does not supply a computational problem from this paper.

Theorem 1 is an especially poor high-multiplicity anchor. It assumes c-diversity, equivalent here to linear independence of individuals’ taste functions. Repeated complete types violate that condition, and with the usual finite-dimensional outcome spaces, the number of independent taste functions is bounded. A continuum version with many repeated types would need a new assumption in place of c-diversity, so it would no longer mirror this theorem’s stated setting.

Theorem 2 does allow repeated types, but its conditions do not use their population masses. A natural finite-support construction is to set \(P_0=\operatorname{conv}(\bigcup_t P_t)\): this satisfies the Common-Taste Pareto upper bound and contains a convex combination for every tuple of individual priors, as Theorem 2 requires. Assigning utility weights by type mass would add an equal-per-capita influence rule; the theorem itself allows the weights to be chosen freely. One could invent an optimization task around selecting or representing \(P_0\), but the choice of objective and constraints would supply the computational problem, not the paper.

A richer type space makes the proposed mirror harder to formulate computationally: each type includes a utility function and a compact convex prior set, generally requiring a finite encoding or approximation scheme. Such choices may support future work, but this paper specifies none. Its weakness as a negative case is that it cannot rule out a worthwhile adjacent project on computational aggregation. It does show that the paper supplies no computational anchor for one, and that the most natural mass-based repair would add a new problem rather than continuize a result the authors prove.
