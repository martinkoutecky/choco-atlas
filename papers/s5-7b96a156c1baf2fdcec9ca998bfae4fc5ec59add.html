The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(\mathcal T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a rational seed-mass budget \(\kappa\), and rational piecewise-linear monotone diminishing-returns maps \(F_t:\prod_{s\in\mathcal T}[0,\mu_s]\to[0,1]\) with \(F_t(0)=0\), choose seed masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) such that \(\sum_t x_t\le\kappa\). Set \(a^{(0)}=x\) and \(a_t^{(r+1)}=x_t+(\mu_t-x_t)F_t(a^{(r)})\), and define \(\Phi(x)=\sum_t a_t^\infty\). Determine the complexity of finding a feasible rational \(x\) with \(\Phi(x)\ge(1-1/e-\epsilon)\max_{y}\Phi(y)\), where \(y\) ranges over the same seed-mass constraints.

The model it lives in

A society has \(\tau\) blocks with rational masses \(\mu_t\); type \(t\) shares an activation response to active mass across all blocks. Choose seed masses \(x_t\), and maximize total eventual active mass under the stated threshold recursion.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Corollary 1’s cardinality influence-maximization objective in a block-structured population; it leaves aside arbitrary network neighborhoods, general terminal weights, and the coupling proof of Theorem 1.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the finite block-threshold model converge to the stated mass recursion?
  • Is \(\Phi(x)\) monotone and diminishing-returns in the seed masses?
  • Can the limit and its value be approximated in time polynomial in \(\tau\), the input bit length, and \(1/\epsilon\)?
  • What exact or parameterized hardness remains when \(\tau\) is small?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest anchor is Corollary 1, stated in this paper. It says that for the diffusion process of Definition 4, if the activation functions \(F\) and terminal weight \(w\) are monotone and submodular, and \(w\) takes values between \(1\) and \(\operatorname{poly}(n)\), then a greedy algorithm finds a \((1-1/e-1/\operatorname{poly}(n))\)-approximation to the maximum of \(\sigma_w(S)\) over all initial sets \(S\) of size \(k\), in polynomial time. Cardinality of the final active set is one stated case. The corollary is in this paper; its guarantee follows from this paper’s Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 of KKT05. Theorem 1 proves the key structural fact: expected terminal weight is submodular in the seed set. The paper’s discussion of hardness of influence maximization cites KKT03; it is not a numbered hardness result of this paper, so I would not use it as an anchor.

I would mirror the cardinality case in a type-symmetric, block-structured population. Let \(\mathcal T\) be a finite set of user types, with \(\tau=|\mathcal T|\) and rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). A type records the user’s role, activation response, and threshold law. There are many users of each type, with \(N\gg\tau\); within a type, users have the same response to active mass in each type. This fits, for example, a large platform whose users fall into a modest number of language, community, or cohort groups and whose recommendation system produces group-level exposure. Thresholds remain independent random draws as in the paper: they are part of the diffusion process, not idiosyncratic prices or costs that split otherwise identical users into different types.

Here is the continuous problem I would hand to a prover: Continuous Influence Maximization in a Block Threshold Population. An instance gives \(\mathcal T\), the rational masses \(\mu_t\), a rational seed-mass budget \(\kappa\), and for each type \(t\) a monotone, diminishing-returns activation response \(F_t:[0,\mu]\to[0,1]\), where \([0,\mu]=\prod_{s\in\mathcal T}[0,\mu_s]\) and \(F_t(0)=0\). The responses can be supplied as rational piecewise-linear functions. Choose seed masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) with \(\sum_t x_t\le\kappa\). Starting from \(a^{(0)}=x\), the type-\(t\) mass active at the next round is \(a_t^{(r+1)}=x_t+(\mu_t-x_t)F_t(a^{(r)})\): among the unseeded mass of type \(t\), exactly the fraction whose uniform thresholds have been crossed activates. The increasing sequence has a limit \(a^\infty(x)\). The objective is to maximize \(\Phi(x)=\sum_t a_t^\infty(x)\), the final active fraction. A solution to the approximation version is a feasible rational seed vector with spread at least \((1-1/e-\varepsilon)\max_x\Phi(x)\).

This is a high-multiplicity version of their problem, not continuity in the outcome or a probability model over finite profiles. A finite population with many copies of each type gives the discrete counterpart; seeding \(x_t\) is seeding a fraction of type \(t\), and the objective is eventual adoption. The paper’s exact named problem is “choose \(k\) individuals”; the mirror asks the same influence-maximization question when both the population and intervention are measured in mass. I expect the approximation problem to be tractable, via diminishing returns and continuous-greedy methods over the \(\tau\) type masses. Establishing that the limiting spread retains the needed diminishing-returns property, and evaluating it efficiently from the encoded \(F_t\), are part of the work rather than consequences I would claim are already proved by Corollary 1. The discrete corollary’s polynomial bound is in \(n\), not the high-multiplicity parameters \(\tau\) and \(L\).

The scope is just Corollary 1’s cardinality objective in this block-structured regime. My weakest point is the symmetry restriction: an arbitrary sparse social graph often gives each person a distinct interaction neighborhood, so its number of genuine types may grow with the population and the continuous advantage disappears. The proposed mirror is therefore for a real but specific regime—large populations with a small set of shared response types—not for every social network or every result in the paper. Further questions are whether the mirror admits a bit-efficient \((1-1/e-\varepsilon)\)-approximation in \((\tau,L,1/\varepsilon)\), what assumptions ensure efficient evaluation of the limiting diffusion, and whether exact optimization is hard even when \(\tau\) is small.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only anchor is real: Corollary 1 gives a named approximation result, backed by Theorem 1’s submodularity of expected terminal influence. So the negative case cannot dismiss the paper as lacking a computational problem to mirror.

The main obstacle is that the theorem’s object is a function of *sets of named vertices*. Each \(f_v\) can depend on which particular neighbors are active, and two vertices with the same activation law need not have the same neighborhood. Their active fractions by coarse group therefore need not determine the next step: local patterns and correlations can matter. In a typical sparse network, preserving those patterns may require making neighborhood position part of the type, leaving few repeated types. That undercuts the proposed mirror for a broad class of networks.

The best version of the mirror avoids that problem by assuming an anonymous, block-structured network: a user’s response depends only on active mass in each block. Then the threshold distribution can yield a deterministic mass recursion, and seeding fractions is a sensible high-multiplicity question. But this assumption does substantial modeling work. The recursion in the proposal is not a compression of the paper’s general network model merely because users share a response function; it describes a more specialized, mean-field regime. Also, Corollary 1’s set-function submodularity does not by itself establish diminishing returns for this deterministic mass objective. A continuous-greedy guarantee would need a proof for the chosen extension, and an efficient way to evaluate the limiting recursion from its encoding.

Those gaps weaken the proposal as stated, but they do not defeat the strongest mirror. An authors-recognizable question about influence maximization in a large, block-symmetric population remains plausible, and obtaining complexity bounds in \((\tau,L)\) could add something beyond an algorithm polynomial in the number of individuals. The finite result does not settle that high-multiplicity question. The honest negative case is therefore weak: the general-network mirror may fail to compress, and the proposed algorithmic claim is not yet justified, but I cannot make a convincing case that no worthwhile scenario exists.
