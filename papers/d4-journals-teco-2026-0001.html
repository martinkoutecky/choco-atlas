The continuous mirror question

Fix a series-parallel network with one origin–destination pair, a maximum coalition size \(A\), and a finite set \(T\) of commuter types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Type \(t\) has preferences \((\alpha_t,\beta_t)\); all types share capacity-sharing disutilities \(\Delta\alpha(k),\Delta\beta(k)\). Given rational per-capita edge capacities \(\kappa_e\), compute a welfare-maximizing market equilibrium. For each integral coalition composition \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{T}\) with \(1\le |b|\le A\) and route \(r\), let \(x_{r,b}\ge 0\) be its allocation intensity per unit of population, and let \(V_r(b)=\sum_{t\in T}b_t(\alpha_t-\beta_t d_r-\Delta\alpha(|b|)-\Delta\beta(|b|)d_r)\). Maximize \(\sum_{r,b}V_r(b)x_{r,b}\), subject to \(\sum_{r,b}b_t x_{r,b}\le\mu_t\) for each type and \(\sum_{r,b:e\in r}x_{r,b}\le\kappa_e\) for each edge. Return supporting type utilities \(u_t\ge 0\) and edge prices \(\pi_e\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_t b_tu_t+\sum_{e\in r}\pi_e\ge V_r(b)\) for every \((b,r)\), with equality on allocated patterns and zero price on slack edges; payments can then be recovered per assigned trip.

The model it lives in

A normalized continuum of commuter mass on a fixed network, described by a distribution over complete preference types. Coalitions retain finite integral compositions and trips retain discrete routes; the decision variables are intensities of each coalition pattern on each route, with type supplies and edge capacities measured per capita. The objective is total welfare, supported by type utilities and edge prices.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the static, single-origin–destination, edge-priced market’s equilibrium characterization in Proposition 1, in the series-parallel, homogeneous-disutility regime supported by Theorem 1. It leaves aside Theorem 2’s individual strategyproofness claim, the multi-period extension, and the general-network capacity-allocation problem.

Open questions for a prover

  • For fixed \(A\), can an equilibrium be computed in time polynomial in the network size, \(|T|\), and the input bit length, including rational per-capita capacities?
  • When \(A\) varies with the input, what parameterized complexity follows from the number of integral coalition compositions?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest lead is Proposition 1, proved in this paper. It says that a market equilibrium exists exactly when the LP relaxation of the optimal trip allocation problem has an integer optimal solution. Any integer LP optimum gives an equilibrium trip allocation; any optimal dual solution gives the agents’ utilities and edge prices; payments are then recovered from equation (11). The paper computes through this characterization. The excerpt does not give a numbered theorem asserting a P or NP-hard classification: its NP-hardness claim in Section 4.2 is in prose, so I would not use that as an anchor.

Here is the continuous mirror I would put to the authors. Consider a large commuter market on a single-origin, single-destination series-parallel network. A type \(t\) records the complete preference parameters relevant to the static model, here \((\alpha_t,\beta_t)\); all types share the same capacity-sharing disutility parameters \(\Delta\alpha(k),\Delta\beta(k)\), and the same origin and destination. The population is a distribution \(\mu\) over the \(\tau\) types, with rational masses \(\mu_t\). Edge capacities scale with the market: \(\kappa_e\) is capacity per unit of population. This is a plausible high-multiplicity regime—many commuters in a small number of preference cohorts, with service capacity scaled to the size of the market.

Coalitions remain finite and integral. For a coalition composition \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\tau}\), require \(1\le |b|=\sum_t b_t\le A\); each such coalition has an ordinary route \(r\) and value \(V_r(b)=\sum_t b_t(\alpha_t-\beta_t d_r-\Delta\alpha(|b|)-\Delta\beta(|b|)d_r)\). The decision variable \(x_{r,b}\ge0\) is the mass of coalitions of composition \(b\) taking route \(r\), measured per unit of population. Ask for a welfare-maximizing allocation subject to \(\sum_{r,b}b_t x_{r,b}\le\mu_t\) for every type \(t\), and \(\sum_{r,b:e\in r}x_{r,b}\le\kappa_e\) for every edge \(e\).

The requested solution is an allocation together with supporting utilities \(u_t\ge0\), edge prices \(\tau_e\ge0\), and payments. Stability requires \(\sum_t b_tu_t+\sum_{e\in r}\tau_e\ge V_r(b)\) for every coalition composition and route. An allocated coalition type and route must meet this inequality with equality; an edge with slack capacity must have zero price. For a type-\(t\) commuter in an assigned \((b,r)\) trip, set the payment to that trip’s value for the commuter minus \(u_t\). Equality makes the coalition’s total payments equal the route’s edge prices. This names the underlying continuous problem: compute the market equilibrium, not merely an encoding.

The paper’s Theorem 1, proved here, supplies the most relevant regime: a series-parallel network and homogeneous capacity-sharing disutilities guarantee equilibrium in the finite-agent model. It is support for the mirror’s tractable regime, not a separate complexity anchor. In the mass model, the allocation variables are already continuous, so the LP’s optimal solution and dual prices provide an equilibrium directly; the finite model’s integrality obstruction is removed. I expect this mirror to be Class A. For fixed \(A\), the number of coalition compositions is \(O(\tau^A)\); on a series-parallel network the paper’s greedy route-flow construction uses at most \(|E|\) supported routes. Thus the resulting rational LP and its dual have polynomial size in the network, \(\tau\), and the input bit length, for fixed \(A\). If \(A\) is part of the input, this enumeration alone gives no FPT claim.

This covers the paper’s static, single-OD, edge-priced market and the existence/computation question around Proposition 1 and Theorem 1. It does not try to mirror the multi-period extension or the general-network, multiple-population capacity-allocation problem. Natural follow-ups are whether continuous equilibria can be rounded to finite high-multiplicity markets with bounded welfare loss and small incentive violations; whether the dependence on \(A\) can be improved; and whether the paper’s utility-maximizing, VCG-equivalent equilibrium in Theorem 2 has a direct mass-market counterpart.

My weakest point is also the source of the tractability: coalition *counts* become divisible, so equilibrium follows from LP duality. The authors could reasonably ask whether that leaves enough of the original finite-coalition market intact. I think the mirror survives that objection because it keeps finite coalition sizes, discrete routes, capacity scarcity, edge pricing, and every coalition’s stability test; it makes only the population and the aggregate mass of each integral coalition pattern continuous. The per-capita capacity scaling needs a convincing application setting, but it is an explicit co-modification of the population model rather than a change to the problem’s strategic question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that this paper has no numbered result classifying a computational problem by running time or complexity. Proposition 1 is the closest candidate: it characterizes equilibrium existence by whether the LP relaxation has an integral optimum. Its proof is a primal–dual argument, not a complexity result. Theorem 1 gives sufficient conditions for equilibrium existence, and Theorem 2 identifies a VCG equilibrium. The polynomial algorithm and the NP-hardness claim appear in the surrounding prose. If the programme requires a named complexity result as its anchor, the paper does not supply one.

That objection does not make Proposition 1 a bad mirror. The proposed commuter model is coherent: preference cohorts can be complete types, coalition sizes can stay finite, and capacity can scale per capita. Its variables represent the intensity of each integral coalition pattern. I cannot honestly argue that this is an invalid or inherently nonsensical high-multiplicity regime. Nor can I use the fact that its equilibrium follows from LP duality as a reason against asking the question; a tractable answer is still an answer.

The more substantial limit appears in a mirror of Theorem 2. Its strategyproofness claim concerns what each named agent reports. In an atomless population, one agent’s report leaves the aggregate type distribution unchanged. The distribution-level model therefore cannot, on its own, represent the individual incentive comparison in the theorem. One could model a deviation by positive mass, but that asks about strategic behavior by a cohort or coalition, rather than truthful reporting by an individual. An individual-scale perturbation also vanishes in the continuum limit. This defeats a direct continuum mirror of Theorem 2, though the proponent presents it only as a follow-up.

Theorem 1 is harder to dislodge. A fixed series-parallel network, a fixed small set of preference cohorts, and capacity scaled with commuter volume make a plausible continuous regime. The paper’s structural result supplies a natural question there. Keeping capacity fixed as the population grows would make per-capita capacity vanish, but scaling capacity is an allowed co-modification and does not by itself defeat the mirror.

So the negative case has one real boundary: individual strategyproofness does not pass cleanly to an atomless distribution model. It does not defeat the Proposition 1 mirror. The claim that *no* worthwhile continuous mirror exists is weak; the proponent’s equilibrium-existence mirror remains credible.
