The case FOR (proponent)

This paper contains no named computational result of the required kind, so I can’t give a compliant anchor for a continuous mirror.

Theorem 1, proved here, characterizes when every CPI extension with RVT couple preferences has a stable matching: exactly when the fixed preferences satisfy RF. Theorems 2 and 3, also proved here, give the SCPI existence result and its converse; Corollary 1 states the resulting characterization for unrestricted extensions. Theorems 4 and 5 give further stability existence results. These are existence and preference-characterization claims, not results that a problem is in P, NP-hard, or otherwise computationally classified. Algorithms in their proofs do not supply a named complexity result.

A continuum of repeated doctor and couple preference types could be a sensible new stability-existence question. But presenting it as a mirror of this paper’s computational result would overstate what the paper establishes.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a continuous mirror is the mismatch with ChoCo’s computational brief. Theorem 1 characterizes RF as necessary and sufficient for stable matchings to exist at every CPI extension. Theorems 2 and 3, and Corollary 1, characterize universal existence under SCPI; Theorem 4 gives the SRF characterization, and Theorem 5 gives a sufficient SCPC condition. These are structural existence results about preference profiles. Their proofs construct matchings using algorithms, but the paper gives no complexity classification, running-time result, or approximation problem. There is no named computational result here for a continuous population to mirror.

The best version of the proposed mirror would use finitely many complete doctor and couple types, assign them rational masses, scale hospital capacities per capita, and define stability for mass assignments. Large placement markets make that a credible high-multiplicity setting. But it would add a computational task—such as deciding or computing a stable mass assignment—that the paper does not study. Recasting RF, SRF, SCPI, or SCPC in terms of type masses would still be an existence characterization; a complexity analysis would be a new project attached to it.

The strongest candidate is the SCPI result: its conditions involve doctor ranks and hospital capacities, so a mass version could compare the mass above a priority type with per-capita capacity. That is a reasonable model change under the programme’s rules. It still does not supply the missing computational anchor. Likewise, the continuum stability-existence question the proponent suggests may be sensible, but it falls outside ChoCo’s stated focus on computational questions.

The negative case is strongest within that scope. It is weaker as a universal claim that no worthwhile follow-up could exist: large matching markets are a natural setting for repeated types, and studying algorithms for mass assignments could be valuable. That would be a new computational direction inspired by this paper, rather than a continuous mirror of a computational result it establishes.
