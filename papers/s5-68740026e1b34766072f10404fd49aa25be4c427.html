The continuous mirror question

Given a finite-support rational society \(T=\{p_1,\ldots,p_\tau\}\subseteq\mathbb Q^2\) with mass vector \(\mu\), sample \(\theta\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,\pi/2)\), take weighted medians of the two coordinates of \(R_\theta p_t\), and return the inverse-rotated point; compute this mechanism, prove universal strategyproofness under finite-replication deviations, and determine \(\sup_{(T,\mu)}\mathbb E_\theta[\mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\infty)]/\min_x\mathrm{SC}_\mu(x)=4/\pi\).

The model it lives in

A finite-support high-multiplicity population of location types \(p_t\) with masses \(\mu_t\); the output is the inverse-rotated pair of weighted coordinate-wise medians, optimized by expected weighted Euclidean social cost relative to the weighted geometric-median optimum.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 2 and supporting Theorem 1 for \(\mathbb R^2\), including the exact ratio, universal strategyproofness, and compressed running time; it leaves the \(\mathbb R^d\) bounds, learning-augmented theorem, and projection-median discussion aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Is \(4/\pi\) optimal among all universally strategyproof randomized mechanisms on finite-support mass profiles?
  • Can the weighted high-multiplicity formulation yield exact guarantees in \(\mathbb R^d\) or for the learning-augmented variants?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is a Class A, high-multiplicity mirror of the paper’s classical \(\mathbb R^2\) problem. It is not a hardness-dissolution example: the paper contains no NP-hardness or parameterized-complexity theorem. Its value is that the paper’s mechanism and approximation question survive naturally when the electorate is represented by population mass, with the algorithm becoming a compressed weighted-median computation.

Take a finite set of location types \(T=\{p_1,\ldots,p_\tau\}\subseteq\mathbb Q^2\). A society is a rational mass vector \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), where \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of residents whose ideal facility location is \(p_t\). The social cost of placing the facility at \(x\in\mathbb R^2\) is \(\mathrm{SC}_\mu(x)=\sum_t\mu_t\|p_t-x\|_2\), and \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=\min_x\mathrm{SC}_\mu(x)\).

This is a genuine population continuization: the facility remains one point in Euclidean space, while the society becomes a distribution. A rational \(\mu\) is the compressed description of every finite electorate with \(N\mu_t\) residents of type \(p_t\). The intended regime is a city, municipality, or service region with many residents but relatively few repeated location types—for example, residents clustered in a small number of neighbourhoods, all having the same ideal site and the same distance cost. The lead lower-bound instance uses only \(\tau=3\) types, while \(N\) can be arbitrarily large.

To preserve the paper’s incentive claim, I would define strategyproofness through finite replications of the mass profile. For every \(N\) with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\), every agent of type \(p\), every alternative report \(q\), and every fixed random seed, changing the profile to \(\mu+\frac1N(e_q-e_p)\) must not reduce that agent’s distance to the facility. This is the appropriate high-multiplicity interpretation: it avoids the vacuity of asking whether a literally atomless individual can change an aggregate distribution.

My lead anchor is Theorem 2, the theorem labelled \(\texttt{thm:alg-R2}\), proved in this paper. It states in full that the randomly rotated coordinate-wise median mechanism \(\mathrm{RR\mbox{-}CWM}\) has approximation ratio exactly \(\frac{4}{\pi}\) in \(\mathbb R^2\). The mechanism samples a uniform rotation, applies the coordinate-wise median in the rotated coordinates, and rotates the result back. The paper’s supporting Theorem 1 proves that this mechanism is universally strategyproof and runs in time \(O(d^3+nd^2)\), hence in linear time for fixed dimension.

The corresponding continuous problem is:

“Given a finite-support society \((T,\mu)\), design and implement a universally strategyproof randomized facility-location mechanism \(M_\infty\) whose input is \(\mu\), whose output is one facility location \(x\in\mathbb R^2\), and whose objective is to minimize the worst-case ratio \(\mathbb E[\mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\infty(\mu))]/\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\). Determine the exact ratio achieved by the weighted random-rotation coordinate-wise median.”

Concretely, for a sampled rotation \(R\), let each coordinate of \(R p_t\) have weight \(\mu_t\), take a weighted median in each coordinate, and return the inverse-rotated point. In \(\mathbb R^2\), it is enough to sample \(\theta\) uniformly from \([0,\frac{\pi}{2})\). A solution is therefore a mechanism that returns \(R^{-1}(m_1,m_2)\), together with the universal strategyproofness proof and the guarantee \(\sup_\mu \mathbb E_\theta[\mathrm{SC}_\mu(M_\theta(\mu))]/\mathrm{OPT}_\mu=\frac{4}{\pi}\). On the compressed input, weighted median selection takes time polynomial, and in fact linear-time selection is possible in \(\tau\) for each coordinate; the population size \(N\) disappears.

The paper’s proof transfers for a substantive reason, not merely by renaming \(n\). The coordinate-wise median is still the weighted \(\ell_1\)-minimizer, and the identity \(\mathbb E_\theta[\|R_\theta v\|_1]=\frac{4}{\pi}\|v\|_2\) is unchanged. The lower-bound construction also survives with only three types: put mass \(M^2/(2M^2+1)\) at each of \(A=(1,0)\) and \(B=(0,1)\), and mass \(1/(2M^2+1)\) at \(C=(-M,-M)\). As \(M\to\infty\), the expected ratio tends to \(\frac{4}{\pi}\). Thus the rare outlier remains a positive population mass, while the society can still have an arbitrarily large number of agents. This is exactly the kind of high-multiplicity regime the programme asks for.

I would expect this problem to be tractable, in Class A. The paper already supplies the central structural insight and the continuous version is a weighted-median computation. The further questions are nevertheless meaningful: is \(\frac{4}{\pi}\) optimal among all universally strategyproof randomized mechanisms on mass profiles? Does the conjectured \(\sqrt{2}\) upper bound hold in every dimension? Can the same compressed formulation yield exact or efficient guarantees in the learning-augmented variants?

The authors should recognise this as their problem. Nothing essential has been weakened: the private type is still an ideal location, the cost is still Euclidean distance, the objective is still utilitarian social cost, strategyproofness is still universal, and the mechanism still uses random rotations and coordinate-wise medians. Only the representation of the population has changed from an explicit list to a distribution over repeated types. The continuous question is therefore the high-multiplicity form of their own mechanism-design problem, not an unrelated tractable surrogate.

A useful second anchor is Theorem 5, labelled \(\texttt{thm:lb-rand-dict-R2}\), also proved in this paper. It states that every GRD mechanism for utilitarian facility location in \(\mathbb R^2\)—that is, every randomized mechanism that outputs one of the reported locations with probability \(1\)—has approximation ratio at least \(\frac{4}{\pi}\).

Its continuous counterpart is the following named problem, “Continuous Type-Supported Facility Location.” The input is again \((T,\mu)\). A mechanism must choose a type \(p_t\in T\) according to some distribution \(q(\mu)\); it may depend arbitrarily on the entire mass profile, and strategyproofness may either be imposed or omitted. The objective is to determine the smallest possible worst-case ratio \(\sup_\mu \mathbb E_{t\sim q(\mu)}[\mathrm{SC}_\mu(p_t)]/\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\), or at least to prove the universal lower bound \(\frac{4}{\pi}\).

The paper’s regular-polygon proof transfers directly. Take \(\tau\) equally spaced points on the unit circle, give each mass \(1/\tau\), and, if desired, realise each type with \(K\) identical residents so that \(N=K\tau\) and \(K\gg\tau\). Every type-supported facility has limiting cost \(\frac{4}{\pi}\), while placing the facility at the origin has cost \(1\). Hence every type-supported rule has ratio approaching \(\frac{4}{\pi}\). Randomly rotated coordinate-wise median escapes this boundary because it may output a facility not belonging to the reported support.

This second question is not a computational-hardness result; it is a structural boundary that survives continuization. It says that the benefit of the paper’s mechanism is genuinely tied to allowing the facility to be an aggregate location rather than selecting one resident’s location. Further questions include whether the infimum over type-supported mechanisms is strictly greater than \(\frac{4}{\pi}\), and whether the same boundary holds for arbitrary probability measures rather than finite-support societies.

The mirror covers the paper’s central classical result in \(\mathbb R^2\), its universal-strategyproof implementation, and the GRD limitation. I would not claim that it automatically covers the paper’s high-dimensional bounds, its learning-augmented theorem, or the projection-median discussion; those can be separate extensions, but they are not needed for the case.

The weakest point is that this is a modest computational mirror. The paper’s basic finite-profile problem is already easy to compute, and rational finite-support distributions can be expanded back into finite electorates exactly. Thus the mirror does not demonstrate that discrete computational hardness dissolves in the continuum. Moreover, literal nonatomic strategyproofness would be vacuous, so the finite-replication definition is essential.

That weakness does not destroy the positive case. The main anchor’s lower-bound witness has only three location types and arbitrarily high multiplicity, and the continuous formulation makes the population distribution—not the facility outcome—the primary object. The honest verdict is therefore: a strong, recognisable Class A mirror of the paper’s main mechanism-design result, with a clean type-supported boundary, but not a hardness-driven flagship example for ChoCo.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The best negative case is genuinely limited. The GRD anchor is weak: Theorem \(\texttt{thm:lb-rand-dict-R2}\) is a geometric lower bound over all mechanisms, not a computational result. Its continuous version merely replaces “choose one reported point” by “choose one support type” and repeats the regular-polygon calculation after normalization. It adds no mass optimization, algorithmic problem, or complexity question. At most, it records that type-supported mechanisms cannot reach the origin.

That criticism does not defeat the first anchor, however. For rational \(\mu\), choose \(N\) so that \(N\mu_t\) is integral. The profile with \(N\mu_t\) copies of each \(p_t\) has

\[ \mathrm{SC}_\mu(x)=\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{SC}_P(x). \]

For every fixed rotation, the weighted coordinate-wise median of \((T,\mu)\) is exactly the ordinary coordinate-wise median of the replicated profile. Thus universal strategyproofness, the running time, and the ratio analysis transfer exactly. The lower-bound construction needs only three types, with positive masses approaching the required proportions; the outlier does not disappear unless one incorrectly insists on an atomless measure.

Nor is finite-replication strategyproofness vacuous in the proposed model. Defining a deviation as \(\mu+\frac1N(e_q-e_p)\) gives a coherent high-multiplicity mechanism-design notion, and it is inherited directly from the paper’s finite mechanism. The stronger version—optimizing over all universally strategyproof mechanisms on compressed mass profiles—remains a legitimate question, as do weighted versions of the learning-augmented and higher-dimensional results.

The only substantial objection is that this is a modest Class A mirror: weighted median selection is elementary, the finite profile can be expanded exactly, and no hardness dissolves or exponential pricing problem appears. But that is an argument about the mirror’s ambition, not its validity. It cannot support the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. The first anchor is a genuine population continuization with meaningful multiplicity, nontrivial approximation guarantees, and an algorithm whose input size depends on the number of types rather than the number of residents. So the honest negative verdict is weak: the GRD mirror is largely decorative, but the RR-CWM mirror survives.
