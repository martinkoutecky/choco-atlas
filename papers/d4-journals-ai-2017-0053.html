The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no qualifying anchor to defeat: the proponent names none. The closest candidate, Theorem 6, is a theorem about axioms of fusion operators: Standard Domain, Pareto, and Independence imply Dictatorship. The paper states no algorithmic problem, complexity classification, or running-time result. Under ChoCo’s stated scope, that is decisive.

The strongest proposed mirror would replace each finite profile with a distribution over epistemic-state types and ask whether continuous analogues of the axioms still force dictatorship. That is a coherent research question, but its result would still be axiomatic. The continuum changes the society’s representation; it does not turn the paper’s central question into a computational one. Defining a new task—such as deciding whether a given encoded operator satisfies those axioms—would require an encoding and computational problem absent from the paper. That would be a new project, not a mirror of a named result.

There is also a modeling obstacle to that rescue. The paper allows operators to depend on agent identity and order—for example, its projective operators select the last agent in a finite society. A distribution over epistemic states forgets that identity. One could put identity into types, but then the relevant agents need not have repeated types; or define a positive-mass decisive bloc, but that changes the dictatorship question. These are workable choices for a new axiomatic study, not grounds for a computational mirror.

So the negative case is strong for the programme’s decision: this paper offers no paper-anchored computational mirror.
