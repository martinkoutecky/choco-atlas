The strongest proposed repair would be a continuum of roommate types, each ranking the other types, with a stable matching of mass. That could be a worthwhile problem in its own right, but it changes the paper’s random-instance model. Here each person ranks the *identities* of all other people. Those rankings change with \(n\), and almost surely distinguish individuals; aggregating them into finitely many types loses the pairwise preferences needed to determine which matched pairs block. Replacing those rankings with preferences over categories and allowing fractional matches restores an aggregate model, but changes both the preferences and the stability question. It would not mirror the paper’s analysis of Irving’s algorithm or its simulations of uniform random preference lists.

One could preserve individual distinctions with a continuum of labels and random preference kernels, but then the types are effectively uncountable and the finite-support input regime disappears. I would not claim this rules out every interesting continuous roommates model. It does make the universal negative case strongest at the point that matters here: any such model would need its own computational question and justification, rather than being a continuous mirror anchored in this paper’s proved results.
