The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest point against a mirror is that the paper has no named computational result to carry over. Definitions 1 and 2 state preference and stability concepts; the equations specify a model. The closest candidate is the runtime estimate \(O(TPn^2m)\) in §4.3.2, plus empirical comparisons of PSO, GA, and simulated annealing. Those are not a theorem about an algorithm’s correctness, approximation quality, or worst-case complexity. So the proponent’s proposed mass-matching optimization would not mirror a result established by the paper.

The best version of that proposal is still easy to imagine: represent recurring cohorts of candidates and employers as types, include competencies, preferences, and rationality parameters in each type, scale hiring capacities, then optimize type-level offers against aggregate QRE-based matching flows. Standardized graduate hiring is a plausible setting. Neither aggregation nor the paper’s stochastic choice model rules out that question.

The negative case is that this requires specifying a new fluid matching model: how capacities scale, how QRE choices generate mass flows through deferred acceptance, and how a package chosen by an employer type affects its cohort. The paper instead optimizes one organization’s package in a finite market of individually indexed candidates and organizations. Keeping that target organization as a single agent while making the rest of the market atomless gives a mixed model; replicating it into an employer type changes the unit of decision. The paper’s runtime estimate offers little guidance on the computational questions for either version.

That makes this paper a weak anchor for ChoCo’s complexity landscape, but it does not establish the requested universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists. The cohort model remains plausible, and the authors might recognize it as an extension of their matching framework. The honest negative case is narrower: the paper provides no formal computational result to mirror, and the plausible continuous model would need substantial new formulation.
