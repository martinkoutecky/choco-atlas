The continuous mirror question

Given a finite chore catalogue \(M\), finite agent-type set \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), restricted-additive costs \(c_t(e)\in\{0,c(e)\}\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_e\), compute a mass allocation \(\lambda_{t,B}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq M\) satisfying \(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:e\in B}\lambda_{t,B}=\rho_e\). Require support-wise EFX: for every \(t\), every \(B\) with \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\), every supported bundle \(B'\), and every \(e\in B\), \(c_t(B\setminus\{e\})\le c_t(B')\). Define \(z_t^\star\) as the minimum, over distributions \(\pi\) on integral bundles with item marginals \(\rho\), of \(\max_{B:\pi_B>0}c_t(B)\), require \(c_t(B)\le(1+\varepsilon)z_t^\star\) whenever \(\lambda_{t,B}>0\), and require \(\mathrm{SC}(\lambda)=\sum_{t,B}\lambda_{t,B}c_t(B)\le2\,\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\rho)\); determine whether this can be computed in time polynomial in the compressed input.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity configuration-allocation model over integral chore bundles: \(\mu_t\) is type mass, \(\rho_e\) is per-capita supply, \(\lambda_{t,B}\) is assigned mass, and costs are \(c_t(B)\), with support-wise EFX and MMS constraints.

The objection that survived

The finite corollary provides no polynomial algorithm in the compressed encoding of \(\mu\) and \(\rho\), while support-wise EFX and the continuum MMS benchmark may require exponentially many configurations; the proponent acknowledges but does not resolve this.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the support-wise EFX and MMS feasibility system admit a polynomial-size configuration formulation or separation oracle?
  • Can the finite repair and scheduling arguments be lifted to compressed rational masses without enumerating all configurations?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is the paper’s main algorithmic result, not its expressive-separation proposition. The paper contains no named NP-hardness theorem about its central repair problem, so this is necessarily an algorithmic mirror rather than a hardness-dissolution example.

My lead anchor is Theorem 1.1, “Polynomial PR,” proved in this paper. It states that, given a monotone cancelable valuation or cost function \(v\) over \(m\) items and an \(n\)-partition \(\mathbf X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\) of \(S\subseteq M\), the modified Plaut–Roughgarden procedure terminates after at most \(\lvert S\rvert\) item transfers. It outputs an EFX-feasible partition \(\mathbf Y\), never decreases the minimum bundle value, never increases the maximum bundle value, and runs in polynomial time in the value-oracle model. The modification is specific: fix an item order consistent with singleton values and always transfer the highest-ranked eligible item to a minimum-valued bundle.

A faithful continuous version should preserve integral bundles. The population becomes continuous; the bundle received by each unit of population does not.

Consider many identical local fair-division environments: for example, a large collection of wards, housing blocks, classrooms, or work teams, each distributing the same finite catalogue \(M\) among \(n\) homogeneous recipients. Every recipient has the same monotone cancelable valuation \(v\). A local state is an integral partition \(\theta=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\in\Pi_n(M)\). The continuous society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over such states: \(\mu_\theta\) is the fraction of local environments currently in state \(\theta\). Equivalently, an individual’s complete type is \((v,\theta,i)\), where \(i\) is their position in the local partition. There may be millions of agents but only \(\tau\) distinct local-state types, with \(\tau\ll N\).

The continuous problem is:

Given \(M\), \(v\), \(n\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over integral partition states, produce a new distribution \(\mu'\) and a mass-transfer plan such that every state in the support of \(\mu'\) is EFX-feasible. A transfer applied to mass \(\delta\) of state-\(\theta\) environments chooses a minimum-valued bundle \(X_i\), an eligible item \(g\in X_j\) satisfying \(v(X_i)<v(X_j\setminus\{g\})\), and replaces \(\theta\) by the partition obtained from \(X_i\leftarrow X_i\cup\{g\}\) and \(X_j\leftarrow X_j\setminus\{g\}\). The output must also preserve, for every repaired lineage, the lower bound on the minimum bundle value and the upper bound on the maximum bundle value inherited from its initial state.

This is a genuine population mirror rather than fractional fair division. The decision variable is mass over integral allocation states, and the action variable is mass of environments undergoing an item transfer. Items remain indivisible inside every environment. The high-multiplicity assumption is that many agents and environments share exactly the same valuation, catalogue, and local state structure.

I expect this problem to be Class A. Apply the paper’s deterministic priority rule independently to every state in the support of \(\mu\). Along each lineage, the transferred items strictly decrease in the fixed singleton-value order, so there are at most \(m\) transfers. With explicit support size \(\tau\), the total work is polynomial in \(m\), \(n\), \(\tau\), and the encoding length of the masses. The number of physical agents disappears from the running time; only the number of distinct population states matters. This is precisely the kind of high-multiplicity compression the programme is meant to expose.

The model is plausible to the authors because it preserves the central object of their paper: repairing a prescribed integral partition while controlling its minimum and maximum bundle values. It does not replace EFX by an average utility condition or make an individual bundle fractional.

The main weakness is that this formulation uses a distribution over jointly specified local partitions. A naive distribution merely over independent bundle types would lose which bundles coexist in the same partition, and the paper’s EFX condition is inherently relational. Extending the theorem to an unconstrained global mass allocation with overlapping recurring item copies would require additional work, perhaps a multiset-valued notion of cancelability. I would not claim that Theorem 1.1 already proves that stronger version. The local-partition formulation is nevertheless a sensible high-multiplicity regime, especially for repeated institutions or service units.

A worthwhile secondary anchor is Corollary 1.2, “Restricted additive chores,” also proved here. It states that, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a polynomial-time algorithm which, on every restricted additive chore instance, computes an allocation simultaneously satisfying EFX, \((1+\varepsilon)\)-MMS, and a \(2\)-approximation to optimal social cost. The proof combines a scheduling PTAS of Hochbaum and Shmoys, the paper’s polynomial EFX repair, and the extension framework of Lin, Wu, and Zhou.

Its continuous mirror is more directly a population-distribution problem. Let \(T\) be a finite set of agent types, with rational masses \(\mu_t\). Each type \(t\) has a disliked-chore set \(D_t\), and every chore category \(g\) has a global cost \(c(g)\); type \(t\) pays \(c(g)\) for \(g\in D_t\) and zero otherwise. Let \(\rho_g\) be the per-capita supply of chore category \(g\). A solution is a mass allocation \(\lambda_{t,B}\), where \(\lambda_{t,B}\) is the mass of type-\(t\) agents receiving the integral bundle \(B\subseteq M\), subject to

\(\sum_B\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t\)

and

\(\sum_{t,B:g\in B}\lambda_{t,B}=\rho_g\).

The objective is to produce an allocation satisfying support-wise EFX, a \((1+\varepsilon)\)-MMS guarantee, and

\(\mathrm{SC}(\lambda)\leq 2\,\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\rho)\),

where \(\mathrm{SC}(\lambda)=\sum_{t,B}\lambda_{t,B}c_t(B)\). MMS can be defined in the natural high-multiplicity way as the smallest \(z\) for which the per-capita inventory \(\rho\) admits a distribution over integral bundles all having type-\(t\) cost at most \(z\).

This is a plausible Class A candidate for fixed \(\varepsilon\): the common-cost component becomes a high-multiplicity configuration-scheduling problem, while the type-dependent zero-cost component resembles a mass transportation problem. The unresolved issue is whether the EFX support constraints admit a polynomial-size configuration or column-generation formulation when \(\tau\) is part of the input.

Thus my positive case covers Theorem 1.1 decisively and Corollary 1.2 as a natural extension. The paper’s Proposition 1.3, separating cancelable from additive orderings, is not a computational anchor. The principal limitation is also clear: these mirrors are strongest as compressed, integral-bundle population problems. They do not yet establish that arbitrary continuous populations make every fair-division computation tractable.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case turns on a mismatch between the paper’s computational object and a continuous society. The paper does contain named algorithmic results, so the source gate is passed. But neither proposed anchor makes population mass do the relevant computational work.

For Theorem 1.1, the population is not really the object being repaired. The theorem takes one finite item set \(M\), one common valuation \(v\), and one partition \(\mathbf X\). The \(n\) agents are bundle positions inside that single partition; they are not interchangeable copies whose multiplicities can be compressed. If \(M\) is held fixed while the number of agents grows, at most \(\lvert M\rvert\) bundles can be nonempty and the remaining agents receive empty bundles. The interesting finite allocation is therefore capped by the number of items.

The proponent’s rescue is to distribute mass over complete partition states \(\theta\). But then the “population” is a population of independent allocation instances, not a society participating in one allocation. If \(F_v(\theta)\) denotes the deterministic repair map, the proposed computation is simply

\[ \mu'_{\eta} = \sum_{\theta:F_v(\theta)=\eta}\mu_\theta . \]

That is a pointwise push-forward of a distribution through the paper’s existing algorithm. It introduces no mass-level optimization, separation problem, or new high-multiplicity phenomenon. The continuous input is useful only as a batch-processing format. If the support is explicit, the computation is immediate; if the support is given compactly, the paper’s theorem supplies no method for evaluating the push-forward without enumerating exponentially many partitions.

Calling \((v,\theta,i)\) an agent type does not fix this. The partition \(\theta\) is an allocation state or local institutional context, not an agent preference type. The construction has changed from “many agents in one society” to “many independent societies, each with its own finite catalogue.” That may be a legitimate ensemble model, but it is not a worthwhile continuous mirror of the theorem’s repair problem.

The stronger alternative—one global population of agents receiving integral bundles from per-capita item supplies—also fails to inherit the theorem. The theorem’s invariant compares the minimum and maximum bundles in one common partition. In a mass allocation, one must instead impose EFX across every bundle in the positive-mass support. Those constraints depend on support, not smoothly on mass: an arbitrarily small positive mass of a bundle imposes the same EFX obligations as a large mass. Mixing two separately EFX-supported allocations can introduce cross-support violations. Using average or expected EFX would restore a mass-linear problem, but it would no longer be the paper’s individual EFX guarantee. Keeping support-wise EFX produces a new configuration-and-support problem, not a continuization of Theorem 1.1.

Corollary 1.2 is the closer case, but the proposed formulation changes even more. The paper has one finite set of named chores, one finite agent set, and an MMS benchmark defined through partitions of that finite set. The proposed model introduces per-capita chore supplies, distributions over integral bundles, a new continuum MMS definition, and support-wise EFX for heterogeneous types. Those are reasonable modelling choices, but they are not consequences of the corollary.

The finite algorithm cannot simply be run “for each mass state.” Its PR step applies to a single common cost function on one partition of \(M^+\). The full corollary then relies on finite ownership of the remaining chores and on the particular extension argument of Lin, Wu, and Zhou. In the proposed population model, a type may be split across many bundles, the support may contain exponentially many configurations, and EFX and MMS must hold for every positive-mass bundle. The paper proves no polynomial representation, separation oracle, bounded-support theorem, or rounding theorem for that object.

Clearing rational denominators does not supply the missing result. It produces a very large finite replicated instance, but the paper’s polynomial bound is in the explicit number of agents and chores, not in the bit length of the rational masses. Thus the finite theorem does not yield an algorithm polynomial in the compressed continuous input. Proving such compression would be a new fair-division result whose difficulty lies precisely in the support-constrained configuration problem introduced by the mirror.

There is also little distinctive computational content left in the social-cost part. Under restricted additive costs, every chore can be assigned to a type that values it at zero whenever such a type has positive mass; chores disliked by everyone contribute a fixed unavoidable amount. The nontrivial issue is the simultaneous support-wise EFX/MMS requirement, but that is exactly the part not covered by the corollary’s proof.

So the proponent has identified a plausible research direction: repeated finite chore markets with per-capita supplies and integral bundles. I would not claim that such a problem is mathematically meaningless. The negative conclusion is narrower but still decisive for this paper: the first anchor is merely an independent batch of finite repairs, while the second is a new, unproved configuration-allocation problem whose essential difficulty is created by the re-modelling. No continuous-population computational result from this paper survives as a worthwhile mirror in its own right.
