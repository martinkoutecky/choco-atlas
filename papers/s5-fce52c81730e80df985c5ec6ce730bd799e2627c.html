The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent has no qualifying computational anchor. Theorem 4.5 is the closest named result, but it is a homological characterization: it relates a game’s symmetry and quota to Betti numbers. The paper gives no algorithmic problem with a complexity result, and no computational task for which that characterization is the solution. Replacing players by population mass therefore does not relax a problem the paper studies.

The strongest repair would be to turn Theorem 4.5 into an algorithmic question: given a succinctly represented game, decide whether it is a symmetric quota game or compute an invariant that certifies this. That could be a worthwhile new question. It is not anchored by the theorem as written, though. If the losing complex is explicitly listed, homology is computed on that finite complex; if the game is represented by a rule, the recognition problem depends on the chosen encoding. The paper neither formulates those problems nor gives a complexity result for them.

A natural mass model also exposes the difficulty with mirroring the theorem. Group players by identical role and represent a coalition by its mass vector \(x\). The losing coalitions then form a downward-closed region in a box of possible mass vectors. For a symmetric quota game, this region is simply the set where total coalition mass is below the quota. It is contractible, so its ordinary homology carries none of the finite theorem’s binomial signature. That signature comes from incidences among particular \(q\)-subsets of individually named players. Aggregating identical players by mass removes that face structure. Keeping individual measurable coalitions to retain it would require a new model of coalition-space topology, with no computational question supplied by this paper.

One could build a later programme around algorithms for games on type-mass vectors, or around topology of continuum coalition spaces. The paper gives no reason to treat either as its continuous mirror: both would introduce the computational question that the mirror is meant to carry over. So the negative case is strong for this paper, though it cannot rule out a valuable adjacent project.
