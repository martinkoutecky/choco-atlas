The continuous mirror question

Given finite utility types \(\Theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), item types \(i\in[m]\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_i\), fixed finite satiation bounds \(B_i\), and marginal utilities \(v_{\theta i1}\ge\cdots\ge v_{\theta iB_i}\ge0\), define \(u_\theta(z)=\sum_i\sum_{j=1}^{z_i}v_{\theta ij}\) for \(z\in\mathcal Z:=\prod_i\{0,\ldots,B_i\}\). Choose mass variables \(x_{\theta,z}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_zx_{\theta,z}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,z}z_i x_{\theta,z}\le q_i\), maximize \(W_\infty(x)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{\theta,z}x_{\theta,z}\log u_\theta(z)\right)\), with \(\log 0=-\infty\), and output a feasible allocation with \(W_\infty\ge\mathrm{OPT}_\infty/2\).

The case FOR (proponent)

My strongest mirror is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s main NSW problem, with the population—not the goods or utilities—made continuous.

The lead anchor is Theorem 1.1, proved in this paper. Its content is: for allocating \(m\) item types, with \(k_i\) indivisible copies of item type \(i\), to \(n\) agents having separable piecewise-linear concave utilities, the spending-restricted algorithm in Figure 2 runs in polynomial time and produces a fractional allocation which, after rounding by Figure 3, gives a factor-\(2\) approximation to Nash social welfare. The theorem concerns the underlying allocation problem, not merely the market encoding.

A natural regime is a national-scale allocation of standard goods, benefit packages, or school places. There are \(N\) agents but only \(\tau\) recurring utility profiles, where \(N\gg\tau\). A type \(\theta\) completely specifies an agent’s marginal utilities \(v_{\theta ij}\) for every item type \(i\) and copy number \(j\), with \(v_{\theta i1}\ge v_{\theta i2}\ge\cdots\). The mass \(\mu_\theta\) is the fraction of the population of type \(\theta\). Item type \(i\) has \(q_i\) copies per capita, so a finite realization with \(N\) agents has \(N\mu_\theta\) agents of type \(\theta\) and \(Nq_i\) indivisible copies of item \(i\). Thus the goods remain indivisible; only the population is continuous.

I would hand the prover the following problem.

Continuous SPLC Nash Welfare. An instance consists of finite utility types \(\Theta\), masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), item types \(i\in[m]\), per-capita supplies \(q_i\), and separable concave utilities \(u_\theta(z)=\sum_i\sum_{j=1}^{z_i}v_{\theta ij}\) for integral bundles \(z\). Find a mass allocation \(x_{\theta,z}\ge0\), where \(x_{\theta,z}\) is the mass of type-\(\theta\) agents receiving the integral bundle \(z\), satisfying \(\sum_zx_{\theta,z}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,z}z_i x_{\theta,z}\le q_i\) for every \(i\). The objective is to maximize the continuum Nash welfare \(W_\infty(x)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{\theta,z}x_{\theta,z}\log u_\theta(z)\right)\), or, for the direct analogue of Theorem 1.1, to output \(x\) with \(W_\infty(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}_\infty/2\).

This is not fractional allocation to an individual. Every unit of mass receives an integral bundle; \(x\) is only a distribution of bundles across indistinguishable agents. For rational data, sufficiently large finite populations realize these masses exactly. Moreover, \(\log W_\infty\) is precisely the limiting average log welfare of the finite NSW objective, so this is a genuine high-multiplicity relaxation rather than a new fairness criterion.

I expect this mirror to be Class A, at least for the natural succinct SPLC representation. Written over bundles, it is a configuration LP: the objective is linear in \(x\), and the only apparent difficulty is the exponentially large bundle set. Its dual pricing problem is to maximize, for each type \(\theta\), an expression of the form \(\log u_\theta(z)-\sum_i p_i z_i\). The diminishing-marginal structure, together with the paper’s spending-restricted market and convex/stable-polynomial techniques, gives a credible route to a polynomial pricing or approximation oracle. The interesting theorem would therefore be whether Theorem 1.1 compresses from \(N\) named agents to \(\tau\) types and per-capita supplies. If pricing is hard when \(m\) is variable, that would identify a meaningful boundary—possibly a continuum-specific hard case—rather than invalidate the mirror.

The paper contains no named NP-hardness theorem for its NSW problem, so there is no hardness anchor to prefer. The PPAD-completeness statements in the introduction concern previously studied SPLC market-equilibrium problems and are cited background, not a hardness result proved here about NSW.

The weakest point is that Theorem 1.1 does not transfer mechanically. Its rounding algorithm rounds a fractional allocation for named agents, whereas my continuum problem optimizes over distributions of integral bundles and uses average log utility. The continuous configuration LP is therefore a genuine reformulation, not merely Figure 2 with \(n\) replaced by \(\tau\). Still, the finite-\(N\) realization argument and the exact identity between normalized finite NSW and continuum geometric welfare make the underlying problem recognizably the authors’ own problem. The mirror covers their central SPLC-NSW result, while raising useful follow-up questions about compressed pricing, exact versus approximate continuum optimization, sparse bundle support, and the quality of finite-\(N\) recovery.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is weak here. The proponent has a genuine surviving anchor in Theorem 1.1, and the usual objections do not defeat it.

There is, however, a real defect in the proposed formulation as written. If the paper’s total supplies \(k_i\) remain fixed while \(N\) grows, then the per-capita supply tends to zero. Almost every atomless agent receives no item, so when \(u_\theta(0)=0\), the continuum Nash welfare is identically zero. Thus the literal limit is useless.

Scaling supplies to \(Nq_i\) repairs that, but creates a second problem. In the paper, an SPLC type includes the marginal values \(u_{\theta i1},\ldots,u_{\theta i k_i}\). If \(k_i\) scales with \(N\), then the description of a type also changes with \(N\). Unless one imposes a fixed satiation bound or a succinct parametric utility curve, there is no fixed finite type space whose high-multiplicity limit is being studied. The proponent’s “natural succinct SPLC representation” is therefore an additional modelling assumption, not something supplied by the paper.

That objection does not survive the better mirror, though. One can give each type a fixed finite satiation point \(B_i\), set later marginal utilities to zero, and let supplies scale per capita. Then every mass element still receives an integral bundle, while rational masses give exact finite realizations. For any such realization,

\[ \left(\prod_{a=1}^{N}u_a\right)^{1/N} = \exp\!\left(\sum_{\theta,z}x_{\theta,z}\log u_\theta(z)\right), \]

so the proposed objective is exactly the normalized finite NSW objective, not a different fairness criterion.

The stronger criticism is algorithmic rather than semantic. Theorem 1.1 does not yield the proposed compressed algorithm. Its spending-restricted construction has one node per named agent, and its rounding theorem acts on a finite-agent spending graph. Passing to masses requires solving a new configuration problem over integral bundles \(z\), with pricing of the form

\[ \max_z\left\{\log u_\theta(z)-\sum_i p_i z_i\right\}. \]

Concavity of the utilities does not by itself make this pricing problem easy: the logarithm couples the item types while the bundle remains integral. Nor do the paper’s market or stable-polynomial arguments provide the required type-compressed oracle or a continuum analogue of the rounding proof. So the proponent has proposed a worthwhile research question, but has not established that Theorem 1.1 actually transfers.

That gap cannot support the requested universal negative conclusion. If the pricing problem is hard, the mirror may become a Class B or Class C result; if it is tractable, it becomes a genuine Class A high-multiplicity theorem. Either outcome is computationally meaningful. The repaired model is also plainly a sensible national-scale allocation setting with repeated utility profiles. Consequently, the strongest honest negative verdict is only that the stated mirror is under-specified and its claimed algorithmic inheritance is unproved. It does not defeat the mirror itself, so I cannot honestly sustain “no worthwhile continuous mirror in any scenario.”
