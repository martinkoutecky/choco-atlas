The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent gives no computational anchor to defeat. Theorem 1, Corollary 1, and Propositions 1 and 2 characterize what preference axioms imply about social utility sets; none poses an algorithmic problem or gives a complexity result. That leaves no named computational result in this paper for a continuous mirror to carry forward.

The strongest plausible mirror would take each complete utility set \(U_i\) as an agent type, put a distribution \(\mu\) over those types, and ask how Pareto⋆ constrains the planner’s utility set. This can describe a sensible high-multiplicity population: many people may share the same uncertainty about their tastes. But Pareto⋆ is a unanimity axiom. With finite support, it depends on which types are present, not on their positive masses. In an atomless model, an almost-everywhere version depends on null sets, not on the population shares themselves. The theorem also leaves the aggregation weights free; it does not make them proportional to \(\mu\). A model in which shares determine weights, or in which agreement among only a specified fraction of society counts, would introduce a different aggregation principle.

One could devise an algorithmic task—for example, test whether a planner’s represented utility set satisfies the continuum axiom for a finite list of rational polyhedral types. That might be interesting on its own. But the paper specifies neither that input representation nor an algorithmic output, and the resulting task would be a new computational study of an axiomatic characterization. The population distribution supplies little computational content when changing positive masses leaves the axiom unchanged.

So the negative case is strong against treating this paper as a computational mirror. It cannot establish that no related algorithmic project would ever interest the authors; it can establish that the paper itself offers no computational anchor, and that its most natural population continuization makes the mass values irrelevant to its central axiom.
