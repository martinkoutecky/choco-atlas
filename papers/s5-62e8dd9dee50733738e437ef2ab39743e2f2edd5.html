The continuous mirror question

Given finite applicant and position type sets \(A\) and \(B\), binary-rational masses \(\mu_a,\nu_b\) summing to \(1\) on each side, and integer scores \(p_a(b),q_b(a)\), let \(\ell_{ab}=p_a(b)+q_b(a)\). Assume the \(\ell_{ab}\) values are distinct across \(b\) for each \(a\), and across \(a\) for each \(b\). Find, in time polynomial in \(\tau=|A|+|B|\) and input bit length \(L\), a flow \(x_{ab}\ge0\) with row sums \(\mu_a\) and column sums \(\nu_b\), maximizing \(\sum_{a,b}\ell_{ab}x_{ab}\), subject to every \((a,b)\) satisfying \(\bigl(\sum_{b':\,\ell_{ab'}<\ell_{ab}}x_{ab'}=0\bigr)\lor\bigl(\sum_{a':\,\ell_{a'b}<\ell_{ab}}x_{a'b}=0\bigr)\).

The model it lives in

An atomless balanced matching market with complete score-vector types on both sides. The decision variable \(x_{ab}\) is the mass of type \(a\) matched to type \(b\); each unit of mass receives one partner, while type-level averages may be fractional. Both sides rank pairings by the paper’s additive link value \(\ell_{ab}\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Proposition 7’s additive-link stability and highest-total-link guarantee in the tie-free case. It leaves α-stability, lex-male-optimality, and the link-maximal branch aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Prove the exact correctness and bit-complexity of the descending-link greedy flow algorithm for binary-rational type masses.
  • Characterize stable optimal flows when distinct types tie in their link values.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest anchor is the additive branch of Proposition 7, proved in this paper. It says that, given an SMQ \(P\), the link-additive-stable-GS algorithm returns a link-additive-stable marriage; when the induced link preferences have no ties, that marriage also has the highest additive-link value. The paper contains no named hardness result, so this is an algorithmic anchor, not a case about hardness dissolving. Its companion maximal-link branch is outside my mirror.

My lead mirror is Maximum-Link Stable Population Matching. Consider a one-to-one placement market with a very large number \(N\) of applicants and an equal mass of individual positions. For example, a national internship clearinghouse might have many applicants and vacancies, but relatively few complete score profiles: applicants score position types, and positions score applicant types. A type includes the whole score vector relevant to the problem, so members of a type are interchangeable. Here \(N\) can be millions while the total number of types \(\tau\) is modest.

Formally, let \(A\) and \(B\) be finite sets of applicant and position types, with masses \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\), each summing to \(1\). The input gives these masses as binary rationals and gives scores \(p_a(b)\) and \(q_b(a)\). Set the pair’s link strength to
\[ \ell_{ab}=p_a(b)+q_b(a). \]
The decision variable is a mass flow \(x_{ab}\ge 0\), with \(\sum_b x_{ab}=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}=\nu_b\). This is an aggregate description: because the population is nonatomic, a flow can be realized by assigning each unit of mass one actual partner. It does not give individuals fractional partners.

Call \((a,b)\) a positive-mass blocking pair if positive mass of type \(a\) is matched to types with strictly lower link strength than \(\ell_{ab}\), and positive mass of type \(b\) is matched to types with strictly lower link strength than \(\ell_{ab}\). Equivalently, for every \((a,b)\), require
\[ \left(\sum_{b':\,\ell_{ab'}<\ell_{ab}} x_{ab'}=0\right) \quad\text{or}\quad \left(\sum_{a':\,\ell_{a'b}<\ell_{ab}} x_{a'b}=0\right). \]
The problem asks for a link-additive-stable flow maximizing \(\sum_{a,b}\ell_{ab}x_{ab}\). To mirror Proposition 7’s tie-free case, restrict to instances where distinct counterpart types have distinct link values in each row and column. The exact computational target is an algorithm polynomial in \(\tau\) and the input bit length \(L\), rather than in \(N\).

I expect this mirror to be tractable. The paper’s link construction and blocking condition carry over directly, and batched deferred acceptance—making proposals in rational quantities rather than one person at a time—looks like a natural route to an exact algorithm. Whether that can be proved to run in \(\mathrm{poly}(\tau,L)\) is the computational question; Proposition 7 itself does not establish that bit-complexity bound. This mirror covers the link-additive stability and highest-total-link result, not the paper’s \(\alpha\)-stability, lex-male-optimality, or link-maximal objective.

The main weakness is the no-ties premise. Proposition 7 states it over named individuals, while copies of a population type have equal link values to one another. My mirror treats such copies as one interchangeable counterpart type, so “no ties” applies between distinct types. That is natural for the aggregate model, but it is a real modeling step, not a literal consequence of the proposition. It also generates the next questions: does the highest-link guarantee survive type-level batching exactly, and what changes when distinct types tie? A discrete-to-continuous comparison would then ask whether the optimal stable flow can be converted back to a stable matching of finitely many people with controlled loss.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is that the proponent has not isolated a substantial computational problem. In the tie-free additive branch, both sides rank a pair by the same link value \(\ell_{ab}\). For the proposed mass-flow model, sort the type pairs by decreasing \(\ell_{ab}\), then assign each pair the minimum of its two remaining masses. With ties between disjoint pairs, process them together. Each assignment exhausts at least one endpoint at that link level; any unassigned pair therefore has an endpoint matched only to higher-link pairs. The flow is stable. The same argument forces those assignments in every stable flow, so the maximum-link objective adds no further choice. This makes the proponent’s suggested unresolved issue—whether a polynomial bit-complexity bound exists—look like a straightforward greedy result.

That is a priority objection, not a disqualifier. ChoCo counts tractable mirrors, and a short Class A result can still be useful. Nearby work already studies computational optimization of stable fractional matchings, though it fractionates assignments among named finite agents rather than continuizing the population; under the programme’s rules, it is not a collision. ([Stable Fractional Matchings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06698); [Fractional Matchings under Preferences: Stability and Optimality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12259))

The stronger objections do not hold. A clearinghouse with many applicants and positions but relatively few complete, interchangeable score types is a plausible high-multiplicity regime. Shared link scores can represent mutual match quality, and a type-level mass flow can be realized by assigning whole individuals in an atomless population. That preserves the anchor’s stability question without tracking personal identities. A more careful scenario can also avoid relying on arbitrary score scales by treating the link as a common compatibility measure.

So the honest negative case is weak: Proposition 7 offers little apparent algorithmic depth to transfer, and its mirror may be a low-priority project. But the type-mass problem is well-posed, the multiplicity story is credible, and the adjacent fractional-matching literature does not settle the continuous-population complexity question. I cannot defend the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
