The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raises no continuous anchor to defeat. Tables 1–4 report frequencies of criterion violations in 362 observed elections and simulated finite profiles; they do not state a complexity result. In particular, Table 3’s finding about LBC and IEA is an empirical comparison, not a theorem about an algorithm or computational problem. On the paper’s own terms, there is no named computational result here to mirror.

The population itself is a natural high-multiplicity object: a large RCV election can be represented by a distribution \(\mu\) over ballot types, and voters sharing a ballot are interchangeable. So “no multiplicity” is not a sound objection. The difficulty is that the paper’s main quantity is a *frequency across elections or simulated profiles*. For one fixed \(\mu\), criterion violation is a yes-or-no property. Recovering a frequency requires a probability law over societies. Under impartial culture, as the number of voters grows, the empirical ballot distribution converges to the uniform distribution over rankings; the finite-sample violation rate does not automatically become a computational property of that one limiting society. Choosing a law over \(\mu\) would shift the object from a continuous population to uncertainty over populations.

The strongest alternative is a continuous monotonicity-vulnerability problem: given \(\mu\), find the least mass whose rankings can be raised on the winner’s ballot to make that winner lose. That is a plausible computational question, and the paper’s MoveW procedure makes it more than a fanciful one. But the article reports neither that minimum mass nor its complexity. Its result is the frequency of binary violations in finite profiles. The mass-minimization problem would be a worthwhile new ChoCo question inspired by the paper, not a continuous counterpart of a named result it establishes.

So the strongest negative case is narrow: this paper offers no *anchored* continuous mirror under the requested standard. I cannot honestly turn that into the universal claim that no worthwhile ChoCo question can arise from it. The continuous monotonicity-vulnerability problem is the weak point in the negative case; if “mirror” includes a new optimization problem suggested by the authors’ detection algorithm, it may survive.
