The continuous mirror question

Given item classes \(G\), finitely supported valuation types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and additive valuations \(v_t(g)\), determine whether a finite rational distribution \(x_{t,b}\ge0\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{G}\) exists that satisfies \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\), and continuous \((\phi-1)\)-GMMS; if it exists, compute one in time polynomial in \(|G|\), \(\tau\), and the input and output bit lengths.

The model it lives in

Each type \(t\) has mass \(\mu_t\), distributed across integral bundles \(b\) with masses \(x_{t,b}\); the total mass receiving class \(g\) is \(q_g\). For any selection of submass \(z_{t,b}\le x_{t,b}\), let \(\beta=\sum_{t,b}z_{t,b}\) and \(Q_g=\sum_{t,b}z_{t,b}b_g\). Define \(\operatorname{MMS}^{\mathrm{cont}}_t(\beta,Q)\) as the largest \(\lambda\) for which the pooled inventory \(Q\) can be repartitioned among mass \(\beta\) into integral bundles, each positive-mass bundle worth at least \(\lambda\) to type \(t\). Require \(v_t(b)\ge(\phi-1)\operatorname{MMS}^{\mathrm{cont}}_t(\beta,Q)\) for every selected group \((t,b)\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 2's general (\phi-1)-GMMS guarantee and polynomial-time computation. It does not cover the EF1 and EFX guarantees, the restricted-setting improvements in Theorems 3, 5, and 6, or Theorem 4's tightness result.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does every rational instance admit a continuous \((\phi-1)\)-GMMS allocation?
  • Can such an allocation be computed in time polynomial in \(|G|\), \(\tau\), and the input and output bit lengths?
  • How does this continuous MMS definition relate to GMMS in finite instances obtained by replicating the type masses and per-capita supplies?

The case FOR (proponent)

A credible mirror is a high-multiplicity fair-division problem: a large relief program allocates indivisible, standardized goods to households, where many households share one of a small number of complete valuation profiles. A type is its additive valuation vector over the goods; its mass is the fraction of households with that vector. The supply of each good class is stated per household. If the population is scaled to \(N\), type \(t\) has \(N\mu_t\) households and class \(g\) has \(Nq_g\) items. This is a regime where population size can be enormous while the number \(\tau\) of distinct types stays modest.

Call the mirror Continuous \((\phi-1)\)-GMMS Allocation. An instance has a finite set \(G\) of item classes, finitely supported types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and nonnegative additive valuations \(v_t(g)\). A household still receives an integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{G}\); only the mass of households receiving each bundle can be fractional. A solution is a finite list of rational masses \(x_{t,b}\ge0\) such that \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) for every type and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\) for every item class.

To make “groupwise” precise, any subpopulation is a selection of mass \(z_{t,b}\in[0,x_{t,b}]\) from each type-and-bundle group. Let its mass be \(\beta=\sum_{t,b}z_{t,b}\), and its pooled inventory be \(Q_g=\sum_{t,b}z_{t,b}b_g\). Define \(\operatorname{MMS}^{\mathrm{cont}}_t(\beta,Q)\) as the greatest \(\lambda\) for which that inventory can be repartitioned among mass \(\beta\) into integral bundles, with every positive-mass bundle \(b\) satisfying \(v_t(b)\ge\lambda\). The allocation is \(\alpha\)-GMMS if, for every such subpopulation and every selected group \((t,b)\) with \(z_{t,b}>0\), \(v_t(b)\ge\alpha\operatorname{MMS}^{\mathrm{cont}}_t(\beta,Q)\). The computational question is to produce such an \(x\) for \(\alpha=\phi-1\), or report that none exists; the target running time is polynomial in \(|G|\), \(\tau\), and the input and output bit lengths, rather than in the expanded population \(N\).

My anchor is Theorem 2, proved in this paper. It states that Match-Draft-and-Eliminate, run with \(\theta=\phi=(\sqrt5+1)/2\) on any finite additive fair-division instance, returns in polynomial time an allocation that is \((\phi-1)\)-GMMS, EF1, and \((\phi-1)\)-EFX. I rely on its GMMS guarantee and its polynomial-time computation; I am not claiming mirrors for the EF1 or EFX guarantees. The paper gives no comparable named hardness result, so this is an algorithmic anchor.

This mirror keeps the paper’s central fairness question intact: for every group containing a household, compare that household’s own integral bundle with its maximin share of the group’s pooled goods. It does not give each household a fractional bundle. The fractional quantity is the distribution of a large population across types and integral bundles, just as an aggregate description should allow. That makes the formulation recognizable as a population-continuous version of the paper’s underlying allocation problem.

I would expect the fixed-factor search to be tractable (Class A), perhaps through a configuration formulation over integral bundles and a separation procedure for the groupwise constraints. This is a research expectation, not a consequence of Theorem 2: the paper’s algorithm handles named agents, and its input size can grow with \(N\), whereas the proposed input encodes masses in binary. The useful questions for a prover are whether every rational instance admits a \((\phi-1)\)-GMMS solution, whether it can be found in time polynomial in \(|G|,\tau,L\), and whether the guarantee can improve for restricted type distributions.

The soft spot is the continuous definition of GMMS. Requiring fairness for every submass is a deliberate attempt to preserve the paper’s quantification over every subgroup, but the paper does not establish that this is the unique or even the correct limit of discrete GMMS. Its algorithm also uses individual-level matching, envy graphs, and cycle elimination, none of which automatically compresses to type masses. Those are real gaps to solve; they do not make the proposed question a different fairness problem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only substantial negative point is that the population cannot simply grow while the paper’s fixed set of goods stays fixed. For any subgroup with positive limiting mass, goods per person then tend to zero, and its continuous maximin share can collapse to zero. That would make the mirror vacuous.

But the stronger mirror scales supplies with population, as the other analyst proposes. The programme explicitly allows that co-modification, and a large relief program with standardized goods and many households sharing valuation types is a credible high-multiplicity regime. Groupwise fairness still concerns each household’s integral bundle; only the population’s distribution across bundles is fractional.

That model is a real change to the paper’s finite instance, but the change does not defeat the anchor. Theorem 2 is a named polynomial-time computational result, and neither its envy-cycle algorithm nor its guarantee automatically transfers to arbitrary fractional subpopulations. The paper’s own discussion of duplication sensitivity makes a direct transfer especially uncertain. Those gaps leave meaningful questions about existence, computation in \(|G|,\tau,L\), and the right continuous definition. They do not show that the questions are out of scope or already answered.

A limit defined through replicated finite instances might be more directly tied to the theorem, but it would still need to address fractional subpopulations and compressed computation. I cannot honestly defeat the Theorem 2 anchor: the negative case is weak, and the proposed mirror survives the strongest objections I can find.
