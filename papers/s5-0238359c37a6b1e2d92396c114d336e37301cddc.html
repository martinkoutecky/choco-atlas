The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no positive mirror anchor in the proponent’s case; it argues that none exists. That conclusion fits the paper. Although the paper labels several statements as theorems, none gives a computational result such as a complexity classification or an algorithm. They characterize when monotonicity implies implementability and describe the geometry of monotonicity domains. The paper poses a structural question about valuation domains, not a computational problem whose complexity the continuous-population programme could chart.

The strongest proposed bridge would put a distribution \(\mu\) over buyer valuation types and ask whether monotonicity still implies implementability on the support or its convex closure. But \(\mu\) does no work in the original incentive constraints: dominant-strategy truthfulness compares reports \(v,w\) pointwise, regardless of how common either valuation is. Weighting those types leaves the result unchanged. Restricting the theorem to positive-mass types instead changes which reports the mechanism must handle; it does not reveal a population-driven complexity question.

Nor does treating buyers as a continuum rescue the mirror. The paper says its setting reduces to one buyer, with implementability checked for each buyer while others’ valuations are fixed. Replicating that buyer across a population leaves the result’s object intact; coupling buyers through shared supply or aggregate allocations creates a different mechanism-design problem. Aggregating allocations by type also discards the report-to-report comparisons that monotonicity and cyclic monotonicity require.

One could invent a computational task around recognizing suitable valuation domains from a chosen encoding. That might be worth studying on its own, but it would be a new problem about the paper’s domain geometry, not a continuous-population mirror of a computational result in the paper. The negative case is therefore strong: this paper offers structural mechanism-design results over a buyer’s valuation domain, not a complexity landscape that continuizing society could extend.
