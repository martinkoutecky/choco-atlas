The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that neither anchor is a computational result. Theorem 1 characterizes whole allocation rules: functions on a rich domain of preference profiles that satisfy egalitarian-equivalence, strategy-proofness, individual rationality, and no subsidy for losers. Proposition 5 rules out such a rule when \(n\ge3\) and minimal no-wastage is added. Neither gives an instance-level decision or optimization problem, an algorithm, or a complexity bound.

A continuous version of Theorem 1 would still ask which mechanisms satisfy those axioms; a continuous version of Proposition 5 would still ask whether such mechanisms exist. Those may be worthwhile mechanism-design questions, but they are axiomatic questions, outside ChoCo’s computational remit. Turning either into a computational task requires adding a finite encoding of preferences and mechanisms, and choosing what to compute for a given society. Restricting the rich preference domain to a finite type set does not by itself solve this: strategy-proofness is a property across possible reports, not just across the types present in one input distribution. Any resulting algorithmic problem would need new assumptions and a new target, rather than mirror a result in this paper.

There is a further obstacle for the most natural continuous-population reading. The paper has \(m\) indivisible objects and \(n>m\) agents. With fixed \(m\) and an atomless population, the people who receive those objects have zero mass, so a distribution over preference types cannot represent who receives them. Scaling the supply with the population or using type-level average assignments could make a sensible model, and the programme explicitly allows such co-modifications. But that changes the resource regime; it still does not supply a computational question from either theorem.

I would not claim that no related computational project could ever be worthwhile. The paper offers a plausible high-multiplicity setting, and repeated auction cohorts are easy to imagine. The negative case is strongest for the narrower claim at issue: neither named result provides a computational mirror, and the more natural continuous restatements remain axiomatic unless substantial new structure is introduced.
