The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The absence of a numbered complexity theorem is real, but it does not settle whether there is a worthwhile mirror. Theorem 1 anchors a direct computational question because self-selectivity is defined at a particular society and a particular menu of rules: can the rule select itself under some compatible tie-breaking of the induced preferences?

For a concrete mass version, take a finite-support society \(\mu\) over ranking types and a menu \(\mathcal A\) of homogeneous rules, including \(F\). Restrict to inputs where each rule has a unique outcome on \(\mu\). Each type then ranks the rules by ranking their outcomes. If that ranking has ties, let \(\nu_{t,\ell}\) be the mass of type \(t\) assigned to compatible linear refinement \(\ell\), with \(\sum_\ell \nu_{t,\ell}=\mu_t\). Ask whether some such distribution \(\nu\) makes the mass extension \(F_\infty\) select \(F\) from \(\mathcal A\). This is the paper’s own profile-level self-selectivity question with a society represented by type masses; the tie refinements preserve its original definition.

For scoring rules, selecting \(F\) can be expressed by linear score inequalities in \(\nu\). There may be exponentially many compatible refinements, so the algorithmic question includes whether the resulting LP can be solved through an efficient pricing problem. Its natural parameters are the number of underlying alternatives, the support size \(\tau\), the menu size, and the rational input length. That gives the programme a real tractability question to chart, even though the paper itself does not classify its complexity.

This is not a continuous version of the dictatorship characterization as an axiom. It is the computational task of checking the paper’s central notion for a specified society and rule menu. Since the property itself is defined through voter preferences over rule outcomes, ranking types are a faithful population description. The other analyst is right that the paper contains no named computational result; that fact blocks a claim that the paper already established a complexity landscape, but it does not establish that its central problem has no worthwhile continuous computational mirror.
