The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make a case that meets your anchor rule: the paper has no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a computational complexity result. Theorems 1 and 2 establish that PUT is independent of the computation tree under stated conditions, and is the unique continuous, non-singular extension agreeing with the rule on non-tied profiles. Theorem 3 establishes stability under sufficiently small perturbations, and Proposition 1 gives a perturbation radius for plurality. Theorem 4—proved here, with a proof sketch—characterizes NIVP as the unique symmetric perturbation scheme satisfying the paper’s four properties. These are analytic or axiomatic claims, not complexity classifications. The paper does mention that computing PUT winners can be NP-hard for STV and ranked pairs, but that is an unnumbered claim pointing to prior work, so it cannot serve as a required anchor.

The strongest positive lead I can see is a *new* continuous bribery question motivated by the paper’s closing suggestion to study bribery under its tiebreaking schemes. Consider a large election with standardized rankings, represented by a rational distribution \(\mu\) over the \(\tau\) rankings present. Types are complete rankings, and mass is each ranking’s population share. Given a target \(c^\star\), swap costs \(\gamma(r,r')\), a budget \(B\), and a probability threshold \(q\), ask whether there is a mass transfer \(x_{r,r'}\ge 0\), with \(\sum_{r'}x_{r,r'}\le\mu_r\) and total cost at most \(B\), that makes \(c^\star\)’s NIVP winning probability at least \(q\). The transferred mass defines the new society \(\mu'\); its NIVP probability is the probability that \(c^\star\) wins after an infinitesimal perturbation with independent \(Z_r\sim N(0,\mu'_r)\). A solution is such a transfer.

This has a credible high-multiplicity regime: millions of voters using relatively few ballot types, with bribery measured as a share of the electorate. It also keeps the population distribution as the object being changed; the perturbation is part of the tie-breaking rule. I would treat this as a research question whose complexity is open, not predict a class without further analysis.

The weakness is substantial: the paper already extends NIVP to real-weight profiles, and it gives no numbered computational result about bribery or its complexity. So this is a plausible follow-on to the authors’ work, but it is not an anchored mirror of a computational result in this paper.
