The continuous mirror question

Given a finite loopless weighted type graph \(G=(T,E;w)\), with positive rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\) and positive rational edge weights, define each coalition by a mass vector \(q\in[0,\mu]\) and its profit by \(v(q)=\max\{\sum_{e\in E} w_e y_e : y_e\ge 0,\ \sum_{e\ni t}y_e\le q_t\text{ for every }t\}\). Does there exist a per-capita allocation function \(a_t(q)\), for every nonzero \(q\), such that \(\sum_{t:q_t>0}q_ta_t(q)=v(q)\) and \(a_t(q)\le a_t(q')\) whenever \(q\le q'\) and \(q_t>0\)?

The model it lives in

A society is a distribution \(\mu\) over finitely many complete interaction types. Coalitions are mass vectors \(q\le\mu\); their value is the maximum weight fractional matching, with \(y_e\) denoting matched mass on edge \(e\). The decision is whether per-capita type allocations can be budget-balanced at every coalition and nondecreasing as coalition mass grows.

The objection that survived

The loopless type graph excludes matches between members of the same type, which the paper's player graph permits; allowing within-type matches would broaden the mirror.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the PMAS existence decision in Corollary 12 for loopless type graphs and poses a continuous analogue of Theorem 11's structural criterion. It leaves the paper's other allocation concepts and the within-type matching variant aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Characterize the loopless weighted type graphs and masses that admit a continuous PMAS, and determine whether the proposed edge-free criterion holds.
  • How does the characterization change when two members of the same type may match?

The case FOR (proponent)

I would anchor the case on Corollary 12 (proved here): whether a matching game is population monotonic can be determined efficiently. Its operative criterion is Theorem 11 (also proved here): this holds exactly when every connected component is a double-star whose two centers form a dominant pair. A double-star has two adjacent centers and an independent set of other vertices, each joined to one or both centers. The center edge must outweigh any pair of edges incident to opposite centers; the paper also specifies the corresponding condition when one center has no other incident edge. The paper has no comparable named hardness result, so I would not manufacture one.

A plausible regime is a large doubles-partnership pool with a small number of standardized player profiles. People with the same profile have the same compatibility and expected surplus with every other profile. The number of participants is large while the number of profiles, \(\tau\), is small. This is a high-multiplicity regime: coalition growth is naturally described by adding a fraction of a profile group.

My lead mirror is Type-Anonymous Continuous PMAS Existence. The input is a finite, loopless weighted type graph \(G=(T,E;w)\), with positive rational mass \(\mu_t\) for each type, \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and positive rational edge weights. A coalition is a mass vector \(q\in[0,\mu]\). Its profit is the maximum weight of a fractional matching:
\[ v(q)=\max\left\{\sum_{e\in E}w_e y_e: y_e\ge 0,\quad \sum_{e\ni t}y_e\le q_t\ \text{for every }t\right\}. \]
Here \(y_e\) is the mass matched along edge \(e\); individuals still form ordinary pairs, while the aggregate matching mass can be fractional. The question is whether there is a per-capita allocation function \(a_t(q)\), defined for every nonzero coalition, such that
\[ \sum_{t:q_t>0}q_ta_t(q)=v(q) \]
and \(a_t(q)\le a_t(q')\) whenever \(q\le q'\) and \(q_t>0\). A yes solution is such a function; a no solution is an impossibility proof. This changes the population into a distribution, while retaining the paper’s matching-profit objective and population-monotonicity requirement.

I expect this version to be tractable, but with a stark continuous answer: a scheme exists exactly when \(E\) is empty. If \(uv\) is any positive-weight edge, choose \(r\le\min(\mu_u,\mu_v)/2\), and consider coalitions with only \(u,v\) present, of masses \((r,r)\), \((2r,r)\), and \((r,2r)\). At the first coalition, efficiency gives \(a_u+a_v=w_{uv}\). Each expanded coalition still has value \(w_{uv}r\); efficiency and monotonicity then force \(a_u\le0\) and \(a_v\le0\) at the first coalition, a contradiction. With no edges, the zero allocation works. Thus the decision reduces to checking whether the type graph has an edge.

The weak point is that the mass-vector model gives identical members of a type the same per-capita allocation. The paper does not impose that symmetry: its allocations may distinguish named players. I think anonymity is a defensible consequence of describing the coalition only by type masses, but authors could reasonably say it narrows their PMAS question. That is why I would present this as a focused mirror, not as a continuous version of their full structural classification. Its scope is the existence test in Corollary 12; it does not cover the paper’s other allocation concepts. A natural next question is whether allowing allocations to distinguish individuals within a type changes the continuous answer.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against starts with Corollary 12, whose structural basis is Theorem 11. The paper’s input is an arbitrary weighted graph on named players; compatibility and surplus can vary from pair to pair. Replacing those players by a small set of types works only when players of a type have the same interactions with every other type. In a doubles tournament, that symmetry may be artificial: compatibility can depend on the particular pair. Then the number of distinct interaction profiles may grow with the population, leaving little scope for a high-multiplicity algorithm.

That objection does not defeat the stronger mirror, though. A matching market with a small number of standardized worker and employer profiles is plausible. There, a type-level interaction matrix is natural, and a mass matching model is a sensible relaxation. The paper’s PMAS compares a player’s allocation across nested coalitions; an anonymous mass model instead compares per-capita allocations. That changes the representation, but in a symmetric finite model allocations can be averaged over players of the same type. It is hard to argue that this makes the question unrecognizable.

The proponent’s particular model could be improved: it should allow compatible pairs within a type, represented by matching two units of that type’s mass. But that repair strengthens the mirror rather than defeating it. I can’t honestly make the universal negative persuasive. The pair-specific graph structure limits how broadly a type model applies, but a credible fixed-type matching regime exists, and the continuous PMAS question remains well posed. The case against is therefore weak; neither the lack of multiplicity in one tournament story nor the proposed mirror’s stark answer rules out a worthwhile mirror.
