The case FOR (proponent)

There is no qualifying named computational result in this paper: it contains no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a complexity or algorithmic classification. Section 2 says that computing a weighted voting game’s Banzhaf power is NP-hard, citing [11], but that is background about a different task—not a named result of this paper about inverse Banzhaf design. The paper’s own claims about its algorithms and their performance are empirical. So I cannot honestly supply an anchor-linked mirror.

The most plausible follow-up would mirror the *underlying inverse-design problem*, rather than the paper’s evaluation of the Laruelle–Widgrén heuristic. Consider a large membership organization with a fixed set \(\mathcal T\) of member types—say, membership categories whose members are interchangeable for the design problem. Let \(\mu_t\) be each type’s population share and \(\rho_t\) the desired share of total Banzhaf power for that type. Choose a per-member voting weight \(a_t\geq 0\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_t a_t=1\), and a quota fraction \(\theta\in(0,1]\). For each admissible replication \(N\), form a finite weighted voting game with \(N\mu_t\) members of type \(t\), each of weight \(a_t\), and quota \(Q_N=\theta N\). Let \(B_t^{(N)}(a,\theta)\) be the sum of those members’ normalized Banzhaf indices. The continuous problem is: given rational \(\mu\) and \(\rho\), choose \(a,\theta\) to minimize
\[ \limsup_{N\to\infty,\;N\mu_t\in\mathbb Z\ \forall t} \left\|B^{(N)}(a,\theta)-\rho\right\|_1, \]
and return an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal choice. The mass is the share of members of each type; the decision is their common weight and the quota; the objective is the asymptotic error in the type-level power distribution.

This is a plausible high-multiplicity version of inverse Banzhaf design: many members, a fixed number of complete types, and a target distribution over those types. I would expect it to be tractable under suitable regularity conditions; at a half-total-weight quota, a local-limit argument may make aggregate type power proportional to \(\mu_t a_t\). But that is a proposed research question, not a result anchored in this paper. Its weakest point is that it needs a defensible limiting Banzhaf model, and its asymptotic behavior may not capture the finite-instance heuristic failures the authors study.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper supplies no computational result to continuize. Its NP-hardness statement concerns computing a finite game’s Banzhaf index and is cited as background; its enumeration algorithm is also prior work. The paper’s own contribution is an empirical evaluation of the Laruelle–Widgrén heuristic and three modifications. It proves no complexity classification or algorithmic guarantee for inverse Banzhaf design.

The proposed type-level design problem is coherent, but it is a new high-multiplicity asymptotic question, not a mirror of a result this paper establishes. Banzhaf power is defined through finite coalitions and whether a particular player is pivotal. In an atomless population, a single member has zero mass; to obtain nonzero type-level power, the proposal therefore defines a limit of replicated finite games. That limit is a deliberate asymptotic model, not a power index supplied directly by the continuous society. It could be studied, but it does not recover the paper’s empirical claims about how the heuristic behaves on finite instances.

This is a strong case that the paper has no anchor for a continuous mirror. It is not an honest proof that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario: the proposed replication-limit problem may be worthwhile as a new research question. The negative case is strongest on the absence of a paper-grounded result, and weak on the universal claim.
