The continuous mirror question

Given rational masses \(\lambda_r\) over finitely many reported-ranking blocks, per-capita chore supplies \(\rho\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^q\), and latent additive cost vectors consistent with each ranking, choose a mass-serial block order, integer quota densities \(a_\ell\) satisfying \(\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell a_\ell=\sum_j\rho_j\), and a feasible measurable allocation of integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^q\) minimizing the worst-case ratio \(c\cdot B/\mathsf{MMS}^{\infty}(c,\rho)\); determine whether the optimum is \(\Theta(\log R)\) for \(R=\sum_j\rho_j\).

The model it lives in

Use finitely many reported-ranking types with masses \(\lambda_r\), latent complete cost refinements, repeated chore classes with per-capita supply \(\rho\), integer quota densities, block order, and configuration-flow masses \(z_{\ell,B}\). Define \(\mathsf{MMS}^{\infty}(c,\rho)\) as the least \(K\) admitting \(\sum_B z_B=1\), \(\sum_B z_BB=\rho\), and \(c\cdot B\le K\).

The objection that survived

The \(\Omega(\log(m/n))\) proof relies on successive named positions, whereas a positive-mass block may instead become a simultaneous configuration flow, so the corresponding \(\Omega(\log R)\) transfer is unproved and may fail.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers deterministic \(\mathsf{CP}\) quota mechanisms and can include Theorem 4.2's \(O(\log(m/n))\) strategyproof design after specifying pointwise allocations. It leaves the finite \(n=2,3\) constants of Theorem 3.7, the randomized Theorem 4.6, and the \(\mathsf{SRR}\) result without a direct mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does a canonical mass-serial implementation retain nonvacuous pointwise strategyproofness for positive-mass ranking blocks?
  • Is the optimum for the mass-serial quota class \(\Theta(\log R)\), or can configuration mixing beat the finite \(\Omega(\log(m/n))\) barrier?
  • Which representation of latent cost refinements preserves ordinal input while keeping the configuration-flow problem computationally finite?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is real, but narrower than for a paper containing an NP-hardness theorem. This paper proves approximation and strategyproofness results, not a named P/NP classification. Its contribution nevertheless has a natural high-multiplicity mirror because the indivisible chores, ordinal reports, MMS benchmark, and sequential mechanisms can all be retained while only the population is continuized.

Consider a large organization assigning repeated unpleasant tasks: cleaning shifts, inspection duties, on-call slots, or care tasks. There are \(N\) workers but only \(q\) recurring chore classes. Workers belong to a small set of complete types \(T\), where a type includes its full additive cost vector and ordinal ranking. Let \(\mu_t\) be the mass of type \(t\), and let \(\rho_j\) be the per-capita supply of chore class \(j\). Thus an \(N\)-agent realization has approximately \(N\mu_t\) workers of type \(t\) and \(N\rho_j\) copies of chore \(j\), with \(N\gg |T|,q\). Each worker still receives an integral bundle; only the mass of workers receiving a given integral bundle is fractional.

For a cost vector \(c\), define the continuum MMS benchmark by the smallest \(K\) for which the per-capita inventory can be decomposed into integral bundles of cost at most \(K\): there must exist masses \(z_B\ge 0\) over \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^q\) with \(\sum_B z_B=1\), \(\sum_B z_BB_j=\rho_j\), and \(z_B=0\) whenever \(c\cdot B>K\). Rational \(z_B\) lifts exactly to a finite MMS partition after clearing denominators. This keeps the paper’s indivisibility while replacing named agents by a distribution over repeated types.

My lead anchor is Lemma 4.5, “Limits of \(\mathsf{CP}\),” proved in this paper. It states that every consecutive-picking algorithm \(\mathsf{CP}\), for every choice of quotas \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) summing to \(m\), has approximation ratio \(\Omega(\log(m/n))\). Here \(\mathsf{CP}\) orders the agents and lets agent \(i\) choose \(a_i\) items. The result says that no quota-based serial-picking rule can asymptotically beat a logarithmic MMS approximation.

The corresponding problem is Continuous Quota-Picking MMS. Given rational type masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita chore supplies \(\rho_j\), an order of finitely many reported-ranking blocks, and integer quota densities \(a_\ell\) satisfying \(\sum_\ell \mu_\ell a_\ell=\sum_j\rho_j\), choose the order and quotas minimizing the worst-case MMS ratio over all latent additive costs consistent with the reported rankings. A solution is a quota schedule together with the induced measurable allocation of integral bundles; its value is the essential maximum of \(c_t(B)/\MMS^\infty_t\).

The natural continuous parameter is \(R=\sum_j\rho_j\), the number of chores per capita. The paper’s lower bound becomes an \(\Omega(\log R)\) barrier, not a meaningless \(\log N\) term. I expect this obstruction to transfer: denominator-clearing and replication preserve the quota argument. The matching upper bound is supplied by Theorem 4.2, so this is a plausible Class-A question with an exact \(\Theta(\log R)\) answer for the quota mechanism class.

The second anchor is Theorem 4.2, proved here: “There exists a deterministic strategyproof ordinal algorithm with approximation ratio \(O(\log(m/n))\).” The theorem concerns the original indivisible-chore problem with additive costs, where the algorithm sees only ordinal rankings, is strategyproof, and guarantees \(O(\log(m/n))\)-approximate MMS fairness for every cardinal profile consistent with those rankings.

Its continuous mirror is Continuous Strategyproof Ordinal MMS Design. Given \((\mu,\rho)\), find a deterministic rule that observes only the population’s ordinal reports and assigns integral bundles with the prescribed per-capita supplies. The rule must be pointwise strategyproof: for almost every worker, reporting a different ranking cannot reduce that worker’s true cost, holding the other reports fixed. It must also guarantee \(c_t(B)\le \alpha\MMS^\infty_t\) for every worker and every complete latent type consistent with the report. The question is to minimize \(\alpha\), or decide whether a given \(\alpha\) is achievable.

This is recognisably the authors’ problem: the objective remains approximate MMS fairness, the information restriction remains ordinal input, the allocation remains indivisible, and strategyproofness remains pointwise. Only the repeated-agent regime and per-capita inventory have changed. Theorem 4.2 suggests a tractable Class-A direction using type-level configuration optimization, with \(O(\log R)\) as the target guarantee. Important follow-ups are whether the guarantee can be made exact in terms of \((q,|T|)\), whether the optimum depends only on \(R\), and whether unrestricted strategyproof rules can beat the quota-picking barrier.

The third anchor is Theorem 3.7, “Hardness for Deterministic Algorithms,” also proved here. It states that no deterministic ordinal algorithm has approximation ratio smaller than \(4/3\) when \(n=2\), or smaller than \(7/5\) when \(n=3\). The proof uses identical ordinal rankings but different cardinal cost vectors, showing that the information loss from ordinal reports alone creates unavoidable approximation loss. This is not NP-hardness, and I would not mislabel it as such; it is a deterministic approximation lower bound.

Its continuous question is Continuous Robust Ordinal MMS Threshold. Take a large population with two or three latent standardized cost types sharing one reported ranking, and repeated copies of the four-item gadgets used in the theorem. Decide whether a deterministic ordinal allocation rule can guarantee a factor below \(4/3\) for the two-type regime or below \(7/5\) for the three-type regime. A solution is either a measurable integral-bundle allocation satisfying the target factor for every consistent latent cost refinement, or a cost refinement and type assignment witnessing failure.

I expect the lower bounds to survive by replication and adversarial placement of latent types, making this a Class-B-style transfer question. But the continuum could permit finer balancing of bundle masses, so an actual proof is needed; if the constants disappear, that would be a genuine continuum-specific improvement rather than a failure of the mirror. Theorem 3.3 provides the natural constructive counterpart: \(\mathsf{SRR}\) achieves \(4/3\) for \(n=2\), \(7/5\) for \(n=3\), and \(5/3\) for \(n\ge4\).

I would not stretch this case to Theorem 4.6. Its \(O(\sqrt{\log n})\) guarantee is tied to the finite population size through \(K=\lfloor n\sqrt{\log n}\rfloor\), so its direct continuum interpretation is much less clean. The proposed mirror covers the deterministic ordinal and strategyproof results, especially the paired \(O(\log(m/n))\) upper and \(\Omega(\log(m/n))\) lower bounds.

The weakest point is that the paper contains no named NP-hardness or polynomial-time theorem, so this is not the strongest possible ChoCo anchor. Moreover, strategyproofness can become vacuous if one models only aggregate mass reports: an individual has zero effect on an atomless distribution. The mirror must therefore retain pointwise allocations, or equivalently be defined through finite denominator lifts. With that safeguard, the problem remains a genuine continuous-population version of the authors’ allocation problem rather than a fractional-outcome relaxation.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is not that repeated chores are implausible. They are. The problem is that all three anchors obtain their force from finite-agent structure that a population distribution cannot retain while remaining a genuine high-multiplicity model.

The proposed continuous MMS benchmark is coherent: with per-capita supplies, integral bundles, and a configuration decomposition \(z_B\), rational instances can indeed be lifted to finite ones. So fractional type masses and integral individual bundles are not objections. The difficulty is the mechanism-level information that is lost in the limit. If an allocation rule depends only on the aggregate report distribution, one agent’s report has measure zero, so strategyproofness becomes vacuous. If the rule remains responsive to that individual, it needs a priority, identity, or position coordinate. Since that coordinate affects the allocation, it is part of the mechanism-relevant type. The population is then no longer compressed to finitely many complete types. A denominator-clearing lift does not fix this: the same \((\mu,\rho)\) can have different serial orders and different individual allocations.

This defeats the proposed reading of Lemma 4.5. The lemma is about a quota vector \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) attached to \(n\) successive named positions. Its induction forces a quota-growth condition at every position, and the \(\Omega(\log(m/n))\) conclusion comes from summing that sequence. A positive-mass block has no first or last member. If its members act simultaneously, the serial quota sequence has disappeared and one has a configuration-flow problem instead. If they act sequentially, their position within the block must be represented by a continuum coordinate, restoring precisely the individualization that continuization was meant to remove. Replicating each finite type does not help: either identical copies may receive different quotas because of hidden identities and order, or they must be treated symmetrically and the proof’s position-by-position recurrence is gone. Thus the proposed \(\Theta(\log R)\) result is either a new aggregate scheduling problem or an identity-indexed finite problem; the lemma itself does not transfer.

Theorem 4.2 has the same defect, with an additional informational one. The complete type must include the cardinal cost vector, because MMS is defined from it; but the mechanism is forbidden to observe that vector. If the proposed \(\mu\) exposes complete types, the ordinal restriction has vanished. If the costs are latent, then the input is not \(\mu\) but an observed ranking distribution together with a set of compatible hidden refinements and their coupling to bundle slots. That may define an interesting robust mechanism-design problem, but it is an extra model, not the theorem’s high-multiplicity relaxation. Making strategyproofness non-vacuous again requires pointwise bundle assignments indexed by an order or position, bringing back the finite serial structure.

Theorem 3.7 is even less suitable. Its \(n=2\) and \(n=3\) cases use \(n\) both as the population size and as the number of bundles in each agent’s MMS benchmark. Replicating the gadget \(L\) times produces \(2L\) or \(3L\) agents, so the benchmark is now a partition into \(2L\) or \(3L\) bundles; it is not the original two- or three-bundle MMS instance. Keeping only four special items makes their per-capita mass vanish, so the gadget disappears. Replicating the item classes gives the continuum allocator positive mass of each class and permits mixtures of integral bundle patterns, eliminating the finite disjunction on which the proof rests. To preserve that disjunction one must keep each gadget’s items local to its own two- or three-agent cohort, which introduces a new eligibility or locality constraint absent from the paper.

The “two or three latent cost types with one common ranking” repair does not evade this. If their costs are observed, the problem is no longer ordinal; if they are hidden, the proposed threshold is a new robust allocation problem over hidden refinements, not a replication of Theorem 3.7. It could be worth studying, but its value would come from the new continuous configuration model, not from the paper’s lower bound.

I would therefore reject these three anchors as worthwhile ChoCo mirrors: the first two lose their non-vacuous serial-strategyproof content, while the third loses its finite MMS gadget under population scaling. This negative case is not airtight universally. A carefully formulated hidden-type configuration problem with integral bundles could be a legitimate new project. But that would be a new continuous fair-allocation programme inspired by the paper, not a faithful continuization of any of its named results.
