The continuous mirror question

Given \(A\), lexicographic tie-breaking, a rational \(\mu\) over \(T=L(A)\), and \(a,b\) with \(p_\mu(a)=p_\mu(b)>\max_{c\notin\{a,b\}}p_\mu(c)\), determine the equilibrium winner under the paper's truthful-start sequential direct-best-response dynamics for every replication \(N\) with \(N\mu_R\in\mathbb Z\); equivalently, compute the high-multiplicity limit. Output \(a\) iff \(M_{a\succ b}\ge M_{b\succ a}\), otherwise \(b\), where \(M_{a\succ b}=\sum_{R:a\succ_R b}\mu_R\).

The model it lives in

Types are complete rankings \(R\in L(A)\) with masses \(\mu_R\); use integer replicas \(N\mu_R\), truthful initial reports, sequential direct best responses, lexicographic plurality, and output the reachable equilibrium winner and normalized welfare gap.

The objection that survived

Under standard nonatomic unilateral deviations, an individual cannot change a plurality winner, while positive mass moves are coalition-like; exact-tie outcomes can also depend on finite rounding. The finite-replica formulation handles rational tied societies but leaves the mirror boundary-sensitive.

The case FOR (proponent)

There is a genuine, qualified positive case here. The strongest mirror is the paper’s worst-case welfare-loss result, not its Impartial Culture expectation theorem.

My lead anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper. It states that for fixed \(m\ge3\) and rank-utility vector \(\mathbf u=(u_1,\ldots,u_m)\), iterative plurality has \(ADPoA(r_{\mathrm{plu}},\mathbf u,n)=\Theta(n)\). More precisely, for \(n>2m\), the paper proves \((u_2-u_m)(n/m-2)\le ADPoA(r_{\mathrm{plu}},\mathbf u,n)\le nu_1\). The lower bound is substantive when \(u_2>u_m\), for example with Borda utilities; when \(u_2=u_m\), the displayed lower bound collapses, so I would not rely on the paper’s unqualified “\(\Theta(n)\)” wording in that degenerate case.

The continuous problem I would put to a prover is Continuous Iterative-Plurality Welfare Loss. An instance consists of \(m\) alternatives, a common rank-based utility vector \(\mathbf u\), and a rational society distribution \(\mu\) over the complete ranking types \(T=L(A)\). The initial population is truthful. To represent the evolving reports, let \(\nu_{R,a}\) be the mass whose true ranking is \(R\) and whose current plurality report names \(a\) first, with \(\sum_a\nu_{R,a}=\mu_R\). The plurality score is \(p_\nu(a)=\sum_R\nu_{R,a}\), ties being broken lexicographically.

A continuous best-response move transfers some mass \(x>0\) from \((R,a)\) to \((R,b)\). It is legal when the resulting winner changes, the new winner is strictly preferred by type \(R\) to the old winner, and it is the best outcome that a mass block of type \(R\) could obtain by changing its reported top alternative. A terminal state has no such move. Let \(EW_\infty(\mu)\) be the winners of terminal states reachable from the truthful state. The task is to compute \(D_\infty^+(\mu)=W_\mu(r(\nu^0))-\min_{c\in EW_\infty(\mu)}W_\mu(c)\), where \(W_\mu(c)=\sum_R\mu_Ru_{\operatorname{rank}_R(c)}\), or equivalently to optimize this quantity over \(\mu\).

This is a population continuization of exactly the paper’s problem: same alternatives, plurality rule, truthful starting point, rank-based utilities, adversarial scheduling, best-response dynamics, and welfare comparison. The only co-modification is forced by atomlessness: an individual has zero mass and cannot change the winner, so the continuous action is a transfer of interchangeable type mass. Restricting every move to \(x=1/N\) recovers the finite \(N\)-agent process.

The regime is natural for large iterative polls or elections with many repeated preference types: \(N\) is very large, while \(m\) is small and the number of ranking types is at most \(m!\). A type records the complete ranking and the common utility convention, so agents of one type are genuinely interchangeable. The relevant welfare is per-capita welfare; multiplying it by \(N\) recovers the paper’s total-welfare scale. Thus the paper’s linear \(ADPoA\) becomes a constant per-capita loss in the continuum, which is the correct normalized analogue.

The phenomenon does not disappear. For \(0<r<1/3\), let \(q=(1-r)/2\), and use three ranking types with masses \(q,q,r\): type \(R_1\) ranks \(1\) first and \(2\) last; type \(R_2\) ranks \(2\) first and \(1\) second; type \(R_3\) ranks \(3\) first, \(2\) second, and \(1\) last. The initial plurality scores of \(1\) and \(2\) are both \(q\), while candidate \(3\) has score \(r\), so only \(1\) and \(2\) are relevant. A positive mass of type \(R_3\) can change its report from \(3\) to \(2\), making \(2\) win and improving that type’s outcome. The welfare gap is \(W_\mu(1)-W_\mu(2)=(q-r)(u_2-u_m)>0\), a constant independent of population size. This is the same strategic-welfare effect as Theorem 1, expressed in population mass.

I would expect the two-potential-winner version to be Class A: the state transitions and welfare are linear in mass, and the relevant winner conditions are linear inequalities. For the unrestricted dynamic, the main open issue is reachability under an adversarial scheduler; it may still be Class A after a structural characterization, although continuum-specific hardness caused by dynamic reachability is a legitimate possibility. I would not claim hardness transfers: this paper contains no hardness theorem, and the population multiplicity is not itself carrying a reduction.

A useful secondary anchor is Lemma 1, proved in Appendix A.1. It states that if the potential-winner set is exactly \(\{a,b\}\), with \(a\) earlier than \(b\) in tie-breaking, then the reachable equilibrium winner is \(a\) when \(P[a\succ b]\ge P[b\succ a]\), and is \(b\) otherwise. This includes the relevant tied-score case and the one-vote score-gap case.

Its continuous mirror is Continuous Two-Winner Self-Selection. The instance is a rational mass distribution \(\mu\) satisfying \(p_\mu(a)=p_\mu(b)>p_\mu(c)\) for every other candidate \(c\), together with the lexicographic order. The question is which of \(a\) and \(b\) survives the infinitesimal direct-response process at the tie frontier. Write \(M_{a\succ b}=\sum_{R:a\succ_Rb}\mu_R\) and \(M_{b\succ a}=\sum_{R:b\succ_Ra}\mu_R\). The continuous answer is \(a\) if \(M_{a\succ b}\ge M_{b\succ a}\), and \(b\) otherwise. This is plainly tractable and is a clean mass version of the paper’s “self-selection” mechanism.

I would not use Theorem 2 as a primary continuous anchor. Its \(-\Omega(1)\) EADPoA relies on finite Impartial Culture profiles having two-way ties with probability \(\Theta(n^{-1/2})\), while the associated welfare gap is \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\). After per-capita normalization, the effect is only \(\Omega(1/n)\), and a genuinely continuous random mass vector with a density has exact plurality ties with probability zero. That result instead generates a worthwhile boundary question: whether a finite-mass or near-tie perturbation of the continuum retains the expected welfare improvement.

The weakest point is the mass-block interpretation of an iterative move. If one insists that only literally individual, atomless deviations are allowed, every deviation has zero electoral effect and the dynamics becomes vacuous. The proposed block-transfer convention is therefore essential, and a referee could reasonably ask whether it is the authors’ intended limit. I think it is defensible because it is the minimal modification needed to preserve the paper’s strategic object under high multiplicity, and because the three-type witness uses only an arbitrarily small positive mass transfer.

Overall, I would argue for a strong but qualified “yes”: Theorem 1 supplies a meaningful continuous-population welfare problem, Lemma 1 supplies an exactly solvable structural subproblem, and the paper’s finite-population probabilistic result usefully marks where the strict continuum stops preserving the original phenomenon.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case begins with scope: this paper has no named computational result in ChoCo’s sense. Theorem 1 is an asymptotic welfare bound, and Lemma 1 is a reachability characterization. Neither gives a decision problem, an algorithm, or a complexity classification. The NP-completeness result mentioned in the related work belongs to Rabinovich et al., not this paper. Defining a new problem such as “compute continuous ADPoA” would therefore be inventing a computational project around the paper, not continuizing one of its computational results.

Even setting that aside, Theorem 1 depends on an essentially atomistic feature that does not survive high multiplicity. A finite best-response move is unilateral and pivotal: one voter changes a plurality score by \(1\), and can therefore break a tie or overturn a one-vote margin. In a society distribution \(\mu\), an individual of type \(R\) has mass zero. A unilateral change leaves every plurality mass unchanged. Thus the ordinary nonatomic game has no strategic voting dynamics: away from a tie no deviation changes the winner, and at a tie an individual still has no positive aggregate effect.

The proposed rescue—allowing a positive mass \(x\) of one type to move together—is coherent, but it is not the high-multiplicity limit of the paper’s game. In an \(N\)-voter approximation, \(x\) represents \(xN\) voters coordinating simultaneously, whereas the paper permits one voter to move at a time. Same type means interchangeable agents, not a coalition with a common action. Taking moves of size \(1/N\) gives the inert nonatomic limit; taking fixed positive moves produces a new coalition game. A differential-inclusion or fluid formulation would require an additional choice of block size, movement rates, or boundary convention. None is determined by the society distribution.

The proponent’s three-type example exposes rather than removes this problem. The masses \(q,q,r\) create an exact plurality tie, and a positive block of type \(R_3\) can move from reporting \(3\) to reporting \(2\). That is a valid example of coordinated bloc manipulation. But under the original finite process, the corresponding move has mass \(1/N\), so the post-move society converges to the same \(\mu\) even though its winner changes. The limiting distribution therefore does not determine the strategic outcome. On the score-gap and pairwise-preference boundaries, finite profiles differing by only \(O(1)\) votes converge to the same \(\mu\) while Lemma 1 can select different equilibrium winners. The missing information is precisely the vanishing pivotal margin that the continuum was supposed to eliminate.

Lemma 1 does not rescue the mirror. The formula involving \(M_{a\succ b}\) is a valid statistic for a newly stipulated mass-bloc process on the exact tie surface, but it is not a computational result and it is not stable under the finite-to-continuous passage. The one-vote-gap case in the appendix already uses a different strictness condition, which disappears when the gap is divided by \(N\). Encoding that distinction requires retaining infinitesimal or integer-scale information beyond \(\mu\).

Theorem 2 is even less suitable. Its effect is the product of a finite-population lattice event—two-way ties occur with probability \(\Theta(n^{-1/2})\)—and a \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) welfare gap. A random mass vector with an absolutely continuous distribution lies on an exact plurality-tie hyperplane with probability zero. Putting positive probability on those hyperplanes would be an extra singular stochastic model, not the continuum analogue of Impartial Culture. Keeping the \(n^{-1/2}\) boundary layer is finite-population asymptotics, not a strict continuous society.

The honest qualification is that a continuum of coordinated voting blocs could be a worthwhile new model, and the proponent’s witness is mathematically sensible under that interpretation. But it changes the strategic actors and introduces a coalition convention absent from the paper. Under ChoCo’s high-multiplicity criterion, the paper offers a natural type distribution but no faithful continuous population dynamics and no named computational result to mirror. I would therefore reject it as a worthwhile continuous mirror for this programme.
