The case FOR (proponent)

The anchor audit is a real limitation: the paper has no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting an algorithmic or complexity result. Observations 1–5 are numbered experimental findings about human bidding. The closest formal computational object is the Section 3 winner determination problem (WDP), whose integer program the paper uses to calculate allocations and feedback. It asks, given bids \(B\), which bids to accept to maximize total revenue, subject to each item appearing in at most one accepted bid. The paper introduces and computes this problem, but states no numbered theorem about it. So WDP is the strongest positive lead I can offer, but it is not a compliant named anchor under the stated rule. The cited algorithmic results on deadness and winning levels are results of other papers.

A concrete continuous mirror of that WDP would model a large auction with many copies of each item category. An instance has item categories \(I\), a finite set of bidder types \(\Theta\) with masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), and per-capita supplies \(q_i\) for each \(i\in I\). A type fully specifies a valuation function and a current OR-bid menu \(B_\theta\) of package-price pairs \((S,p)\). For each such bid, \(x_{\theta,S,p}\) is the mass of type-\(\theta\) bidders whose bid is accepted. The problem is to maximize revenue
\[ \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{(S,p)\in B_\theta} p\,x_{\theta,S,p} \]
subject to \(0\le x_{\theta,S,p}\le\mu_\theta\) and
\[ \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{\substack{(S,p)\in B_\theta\\ i\in S}}x_{\theta,S,p}\le q_i \quad\text{for every }i\in I. \]
A solution is an optimal assignment of accepted bid mass. The supply scaling is part of the model: a finite market with \(K\) bidders has roughly \(K\mu_\theta\) bidders of each type and \(Kq_i\) units of each item category. That is a natural co-modification for a repeated market with many interchangeable licenses, capacity slots, or similar goods.

The regime I find most plausible is a large auction run with automated agents: many bidders use a small number \(\tau=|\Theta|\) of valuation and bidding templates, so \(K\gg\tau\). The paper itself raises automated bidding agents as a setting for coalitional feedback. Defining type to include both valuation and current bid menu makes the aggregation faithful to the high-multiplicity premise. For explicitly listed bid menus, the continuous WDP is an LP, solvable in time polynomial in the number of item categories, types, listed bids, and input bit length. I would expect this version to be tractable. If menus are implicit and include exponentially many packages, efficient solution depends on whether the pricing problem can be solved; the paper does not answer that. Further questions include how quickly the finite-\(K\) WDP approaches the LP optimum and whether the paper’s deadness, winning-level, and coalitional-feedback computations admit efficient mass-based versions.

This mirror covers the allocation computation underlying each auction round. It does not mirror the paper’s main empirical claim that feedback changes human behavior: the proposed WDP takes bid menus as input and says nothing about how bidders choose them. That is the weakest point, and it limits the positive case to a genuine but subsidiary problem. The authors would likely recognize the continuous WDP as their allocation subproblem in a scaled market; I would not claim it captures their headline result about coalitional feedback.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that this paper supplies no named computational result to continuize. Its numbered Observations 1–5 report experimental effects of feedback on human bidding; they do not establish complexity or an algorithm. The winner determination problem (WDP) in Section 3 is the nearest candidate, but the paper gives its integer-program formulation as a tool for running the experiment, not a result about its computational complexity. The algorithmic results on deadness and winning levels are attributed to earlier work.

The proposed continuous WDP also needs a repair. Its variable \(x_{\theta,S,p}\) tracks accepted bid mass, but the constraints do not ensure that the mass comes from enough bidders to submit those bids without conflict. For example, if a type has mass \(1/5\) and every bidder has two bids on the same item, the proposed constraints allow accepting mass \(1/5\) of each bid when the item supply is \(1\). That uses only \(2/5\) of the item supply, yet it assigns conflicting bids to \(2/5\) of a population that contains only \(1/5\) mass of bidders. The correct model needs variables for each feasible set of non-overlapping bids a bidder can win, with total assigned mass for each type at most its population mass.

That repaired model is coherent: it describes a replicated market with per-capita item supplies, and its configuration LP could raise an interesting pricing question. The programme explicitly permits supply to scale with population, so that change alone does not disqualify it. But it is a new computational study of allocation in a replicated auction, not a continuous version of the paper’s experimental result. The paper’s contribution is about how feedback changes behavior. Its feedback is addressed to individual bids and bidders; in a continuum, the analyst must decide how bid-level coalition messages become messages to positive-mass groups. Broadcasting to a whole type changes the intervention, while preserving individual targeting brings back the finite-agent identities the mirror was meant to remove. The proposed WDP mirror says nothing about this behavioral claim.

So the negative case is strong under the requested anchor test: the paper has no named computational result, and the proponent’s WDP is an internal subproblem rather than one. It is weaker as the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists. The repaired, replicated-market WDP is plausibly worth studying on its own, and the brief’s rules leave room for it. I cannot honestly rule out that mirror; I can say it does not continuize the result this paper actually establishes.
