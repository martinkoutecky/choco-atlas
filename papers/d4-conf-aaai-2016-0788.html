The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The other analyst has not proposed a continuous mirror to rebut; their point is that the paper has no numbered complexity theorem. Under the stated anchor rules, that is a real obstacle. The paper does name the Station Repacking Problem and says it is NP-complete, but the main SATFC results are empirical solver measurements, not numbered complexity results.

The stronger substantive objection is that repacking is feasibility on a particular interference graph. A station’s role includes its domain and which other stations it conflicts with. Grouping stations just by domain loses those relationships; making the type complete enough to preserve them tends to make each station its own type, so the population distribution gives little compression. A continuum interaction kernel could retain pairwise conflicts, but then the object is a graph limit, not a distribution over station types. And in the auction setting, the solver checks exact feasibility after adding one identified station to a previously feasible set. In a population of size \(n\), that station has mass \(1/n\): an atomless limit can lose precisely the exceptional constraint that makes the instance infeasible. Allowing a negligible fraction of stations to fail would not answer the paper’s feasibility question.

A replicated-market scenario is conceivable, but independent repeated markets decompose into smaller instances; coupling them through their interference networks brings back the network structure that a mass-only model omits. That makes the negative case persuasive for the paper’s setting, though not airtight as a universal claim: a recurring, connected family of station-role networks could still motivate a continuous or high-multiplicity computational problem.
