The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set of capped integral bundle patterns \(\mathcal{B}\) over goods \(G\), complete additive valuation types \(T\), type masses \(\mu\), and per-capita supplies \(q\), find \(x_{t,b}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\), together with a normalized certificate distribution \(z^{t,b}\) over integral bundles for every \(x_{t,b}>0\), with marked focal bundle \(b\), such that \(v_t(b)\ge (4/5)\operatorname{pmm}_t(b,c)\) for every certificate-supported bundle \(c\), where \(\operatorname{pmm}_t(b,c)=\max_a\min\{v_t(a),v_t(b+c-a)\}\).

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity fair-division configuration model: \(x_{t,b}\) assigns mass of complete type \(t\) to integral bundle \(b\), goods obey per-capita supply \(q\), and each used \((t,b)\) has an independent hypothetical certificate distribution tested by \(\operatorname{pmm}_t\).

The objection that survived

The continuous certificate has not been formally tied to finite \(N\) EPMMS: the focal bundle has vanishing mass, while the paper's polynomial-time bound is for the expanded instance rather than the compressed encoding of \(\mu\) and \(q\).

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 1's algorithmic \(4/5\)-EPMMS result and represents the two-type setting of Theorem 4; it leaves EGMMS, IMMP, separations, valuation-class structure, and tightness analyses untouched.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can certificate-feasible bundle columns be separated or generated in time polynomial in \(|G|\), \(|T|\), and the bit length of \(\mu,q\), rather than the denominator-cleared population size?
  • What finite-\(N\) rounding theorem preserves \(4/5\)-EPMMS while handling the measure-zero focal bundle and valuation ties?
  • Does the exact two-type existence result admit a compressed algorithm in the same representation?

The case FOR (proponent)

I think the strongest honest case is a yes, with EPMMS as the mirror’s central object. This is a population continuization, not a fractional-goods model: each individual still receives an integral bundle.

Let \(G=\{1,\ldots,m\}\) be a finite catalogue of indivisible goods and \(T\) a finite set of complete valuation types. Type \(t\) has additive values \(w_{tg}\), so \(v_t(b)=\sum_g w_{tg}b_g\) for an integral bundle \(b\). The society is a mass vector \(\mu\), with \(\mu_t\) the fraction of agents of type \(t\). There is also a per-capita supply \(q_g\) of each good: in a finite approximation with population \(N\), there are \(Nq_g\) indistinguishable copies of good \(g\).

The decision variable is \(x_{t,b}\), the mass of type-\(t\) agents receiving integral bundle \(b\). It must satisfy \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g\). Thus type-level averages may be fractional, but every individual bundle remains integral.

For two bundles \(b,c\), define the type-\(t\) pairwise maximin value by \(\operatorname{pmm}_t(b,c)=\max_a\min\{v_t(a),v_t(b+c-a)\}\), where \(a\) ranges over integral partitions of the multiset \(b+c\).

To retain the epistemic part of EPMMS, every used pair \((t,b)\) must have a certificate: a hypothetical mass reallocation \(y^{t,b}\) of the same typed population and goods supply, with the focal agent’s bundle \(b\) marked and every other bundle \(c\) in the certificate satisfying \(v_t(b)\ge \alpha\operatorname{pmm}_t(b,c)\). The continuous problem is to maximize \(\alpha\), or, for a fixed target, to find such an allocation for \(\alpha=4/5\) or \(\alpha=1\). A marked individual has measure zero, exactly as one focal agent becomes negligible in the high-multiplicity limit.

The natural regime is a very large population of students, households, or applicants receiving bundles from a finite catalogue of courses, benefits, or services. There may be millions of agents but only a few dozen or hundred complete valuation types. The types encode every economically relevant distinction; agents with different prices, budgets, eligibility, or values are different types. This is therefore genuinely a high-multiplicity regime, not an assumption that named individuals are interchangeable when they are not.

My lead anchor is Theorem 1 (Additive Valuations), proved in this paper. It states: every instance with additive valuations admits a \(4/5\)-EPMMS allocation, and such an allocation can be computed in polynomial time. The paper proves this for arbitrary additive instances, not merely identical rankings; Proposition 3.4 is used to reduce to ordered instances, followed by the envy-cycle construction.

The corresponding continuous problem is:

\(\mathrm{Continuous}\text{-}4/5\text{-}\mathrm{EPMMS}\): given \(G,T,\mu,q\), and additive type valuations \(v_t\), find a mass allocation \(x\) of integral bundles satisfying the supply constraints, together with an epistemic certificate for every used type–bundle pair, such that every focal agent receives at least \(4/5\) of the pairwise maximin value against every bundle in their certificate.

This is recognisably the authors’ problem. It preserves additive valuations, indivisible bundles, pairwise maximin shares, and the epistemic operation of hypothetically rearranging the remaining goods. Only the population is replaced by mass. It is not asking for a fractional bundle or a fractional maximin share.

I would expect this problem to be Class A in the intended finite-type, finite-catalogue regime. Once the relevant integral bundle columns and certificate-safe pairs are available, the mass constraints are transportation/LP constraints. The interesting continuous-computation question is whether the paper’s ordering and envy-cycle arguments can be batched or implemented through column generation in time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the encoding length of \(\mu,q\), rather than in the expanded population size \(N\). The implicit version has a genuine separation issue because there are exponentially many bundle patterns and pairwise maximin computations resemble partition problems. That is an algorithmic caveat, not a semantic objection to the mirror.

The finite-to-continuous bridge is concrete. Rational \(\mu\) and \(q\) with denominator \(N\) give a finite instance with \(N\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\) and \(Nq_g\) copies of good \(g\). Normalized allocation counts become \(x\). Conversely, a rational continuous solution can be rounded to a large finite population, with errors of order \(1/N\). The exact treatment of ties would require the paper’s perturbation idea from Proposition F.4 or a closed-limit formulation.

A useful secondary anchor is Theorem 4 (Two Types of Agents), also proved here. It states that every instance with two types of additive agents admits an EPMMS allocation; more generally, the conclusion holds whenever at least one of the two valuation types is PMMS-feasible. Here “two types” means that arbitrarily many agents may share one of two complete valuation functions.

Its continuous mirror is \(\mathrm{Continuous}\text{-}\mathrm{EPMMS}_{2\text{-type}}\): the input has masses \(\mu_\alpha,\mu_\beta\), per-capita supplies \(q\), and two additive valuation vectors \(v_\alpha,v_\beta\); the output is an exact EPMMS mass allocation and certificates. I would expect exact feasibility in this regime, again Class A in the explicit finite-catalogue representation. This anchor is especially persuasive about plausibility because the paper already identifies “few valuation types” as a meaningful regime rather than an artificial restriction. Existing two-type results would support the mirror; they would not constitute a novelty collision.

The paper has no numbered NP-hardness or hardness-transfer theorem of its own that I would use as an anchor. The statement that computing general leximin allocations is NP-hard is cited background, not a named result proved here. Consequently, this positive case is principally about continuizing a proved algorithmic/existence landscape, not about making a discrete hardness theorem dissolve.

My weakest point is the treatment of the epistemic certificate in a nonatomic population. In the finite problem, the certificate keeps one concrete agent’s bundle fixed; in the continuum, that agent has measure zero, so the bundle is marked rather than counted in the aggregate supply. I believe this is the correct high-multiplicity limit, but it needs a formal limit-and-rounding theorem. A second weakness is that Theorem 1’s polynomial bound for ordinary finite instances does not automatically imply a polynomial bound in the compressed mass encoding.

Still, the mirror itself is strong: it covers the paper’s additive \(4/5\)-EPMMS theorem and its exact two-type EPMMS theorem, while leaving EGMMS, IMMP, and the other groupwise notions outside scope because their continuum generalization requires an additional measure-valued definition.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the proposed mirrors quietly change the object being studied. With the paper’s fixed finite set of goods, letting the population grow makes almost everyone receive the empty bundle; EPMMS then becomes largely vacuous. Scaling goods per capita repairs this, but only by introducing recurring resource categories, copy supplies, and usually per-agent bundle caps. That is a new configuration-allocation model, not the paper’s finite indivisible-goods problem.

Theorem 1 is therefore not automatically mirrored by the displayed mass constraints. The outer equations

\[ \sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}b_g=q_g \]

are linear, but EPMMS requires every supported pair \((t,b)\) to possess a certificate. The certificate condition is support-wise and involves integral pairwise maximin computations; choosing compatible bundle supports is not an ordinary transportation LP. The paper’s polynomial-time envy-cycle algorithm gives no denominator-independent algorithm for this compressed problem, nor does it establish the claimed \(O(1/N)\) rounding guarantee. At best, the proposed formulation is a new recurring-course configuration problem inspired by Theorem 1.

The same objection is stronger for Theorem 4. Its “two types” hypothesis already permits arbitrarily many agents sharing two complete valuation functions. Replacing the integer counts by masses is initially just normalization. If goods are not scaled, the limit degenerates; if they are scaled, one has again introduced the new recurring-resource model. Moreover, the theorem’s certificates are partitions into \(n\) indivisible bundles, whereas a mass model needs a new measure-valued certificate notion. Retaining a positive-mass focal block would preserve the coupling, but that would be a new block-EPMMS concept rather than EPMMS.

This is the best case against the anchors, but it is not enough for the requested universal verdict. The programme explicitly permits per-capita supplies and integral bundles at the individual level. Under a finite catalogue with explicit unit-per-agent bundle caps, the repaired formulation is coherent: rational masses can be denominator-cleared into finite cloned-agent instances, and certificates can be lifted bundle-support-wise. The measure-zero focal agent is not fatal because EPMMS already depends only on the focal agent’s bundle and a hypothetical rearrangement of the rest.

Thus the proponent’s exact expected Class A claim is unearned, but the mirror itself survives. I cannot honestly maintain that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists; the negative case is, at most, a warning that the paper motivates a new compressed configuration problem rather than already supplying its algorithm.
