The continuous mirror question

Given \(C\), \(k\), and a support \(T\) of \(\tau\) trichotomous ballot types with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), find \(W\subseteq C\), \(|W|=k\), such that for every \(\ell\in[k]\) and \(J\subseteq T\), if \(\sum_{t\in J}\mu_t\ge\ell/k\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{t\in J}A_t^+\right|\ge\ell\), then \(\left|W\cap\bigcup_{t\in J}A_t^+\right|\ge\ell\); determine whether this can be done in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The model it lives in

Each type is a complete trichotomous ballot carrying mass \(\mu_t\); the decision is an integral size-\(k\) committee \(W\), constrained by WNCR for every qualifying group of types, with no additional welfare objective.

What the mirror covers

This covers Proposition 3's polynomial-time WNCR guarantee; it leaves the other axioms, voting-rule results, and simulations outside the mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can a WNCR committee be computed in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) without expanding the rational masses into individual voters?
  • Does a weighted implementation of Sequential Phragmén guarantee WNCR for binary-encoded type masses?

The case FOR (proponent)

Lead: High-Multiplicity WNCR Committee. The strongest numbered computational anchor is Proposition 3, proved in the paper by relating WNCR to PJR on dichotomous ballots and invoking the cited PJR guarantee of Sequential Phragmén. In full, it says there is a polynomial-time algorithm that, given a trichotomous voter profile and committee size \(k\), returns a committee satisfying WNCR: for every \(\ell\in[k]\) and voter group \(V'\) with \(|V'|\ge \ell n/k\), if the voters in \(V'\) share at least \(\ell\) approved candidates, then at least \(\ell\) candidates from their combined approval sets are in the committee.

The mirror I would ask a prover to solve is this. The input is a candidate set \(C\), a committee size \(k\), and a distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) complete trichotomous ballot types \(t=(A_t^+,A_t^-,A_t^0)\), with binary rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\). Return a committee \(W\subseteq C\) of size exactly \(k\) such that, for every \(\ell\in[k]\) and every choice of coalition masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\), let \(J_x=\{t:x_t>0\}\). Whenever \(\sum_t x_t\ge\ell/k\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{t\in J_x}A_t^+\right|\ge\ell\), require \(\left|W\cap\bigcup_{t\in J_x}A_t^+\right|\ge\ell\). A solution is any committee meeting all these conditions; the paper gives no welfare objective for this result, so I would not add one. The complexity question is whether such a committee can be found in time polynomial in \(m,\tau\), and the mass bit length \(L\).

A plausible regime is a large membership organization electing a small advisory committee from a fixed slate. For instance, hundreds of thousands of members might repeatedly cast the same trichotomous ballots because of shared chapter or caucus endorsements, yielding dozens of recurring types. A type means the same complete ballot, including the same approvals, disapprovals, and neutral candidates. The mass \(x_t\) lets a coalition include part of a type, and the threshold \(\ell/k\) is the population-share version of \(\ell n/k\). The committee remains an ordinary size-\(k\) set: the population is what becomes continuous. That is a close match to the paper’s group-representation question.

There is an exact feasibility link to finite high multiplicity. Choose a common denominator \(D\) for the rational masses and make a finite profile with \(D\mu_t\) copies of each type. A continuous coalition’s condition depends on its positive-mass types and whether their total mass reaches \(\ell/k\). The corresponding types in the clone profile form a PJR witness at threshold \(\ell D/k\), and vice versa. Thus the continuous question asks for a committee satisfying the same representation constraints as that finite expansion. This also shows why Proposition 3 is a meaningful anchor: the paper studies precisely the proportionality guarantee that survives this translation.

I expect the mirror to be tractable, probably through a weighted version of the PJR construction that works over the \(\tau\) types. Proposition 3 does not establish that compressed-input running time: the common denominator \(D\) can be exponentially large in \(L\), so running its finite algorithm on the expansion is not enough. The prover’s task is to get a polynomial bound in the support and bit length, or identify a genuine obstacle. Further questions include whether weighted Sequential Phragmén guarantees this version of WNCR and how efficiently a proposed committee’s constraints can be checked.

The paper’s Section 4 also makes NP-hardness claims for \(\alpha\)-CC and TPAV, but they are unnumbered claims attributed to cited work. The provided text contains no numbered hardness result to use as an anchor under the requested standard, so Proposition 3 is my single anchor. This mirror covers that proposition only, not the paper’s other axioms or its winner-determination results.

The weak point is that WNCR itself depends only on approval sets; its proposition reduces directly to PJR, even though the paper’s domain is trichotomous. That makes this a less distinctive mirror of the paper’s treatment of negative feelings. It still survives as a positive case because WNCR is one of the paper’s stated trichotomous guarantees, and its computational question is directly about representation of population groups.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that Proposition 3’s content is ordinary PJR on the approval projection. Its witness condition uses only shared approvals and the union of approvals; disapproval and indifference never affect the result. The paper’s own proof makes that collapse explicit. So the proposed mirror would study compressed, weighted PJR, not computation that depends on the paper’s distinctive treatment of negative feelings. That makes it a thin mirror of this paper’s contribution.

But the stronger version of the mirror survives that objection. Given rational masses, take a common denominator \(D\) and make \(D\mu_t\) identical voters of each type. A coalition is a PJR witness exactly when its supported types have total mass at least \(\ell/k\) and share at least \(\ell\) approved candidates. Conversely, any continuous witness can include all voters of its supported types and gives such a finite witness. This gives an exact bridge to high multiplicity, and the exponentially large possible \(D\) leaves a real question about algorithms polynomial in \(\tau\) and the mass bit length. A large membership organization with recurring trichotomous ballots is a plausible setting.

So the negative case is limited: this anchor may be less distinctive than the proponent suggests, but I can’t honestly defeat its best mirror. It is a well-posed high-multiplicity computational question, and the paper supplies a named result to anchor it.
