The most promising reconstruction would model users by rating profiles and put a graphon or other type-level kernel over social connections, then ask how to choose a filter or aggregation rule to improve population prediction accuracy. Its filter is evaluated empirically using user-level ratings, agreement edges, and connected components; turning that procedure into a mass-level optimization problem requires choosing a new objective and network model. That could be worthwhile research, but it is not a continuous mirror of a result the paper establishes. Continuizing the Prisoners’ Dilemma and rewiring instead would lead toward equilibrium or dynamics analysis, which is outside the programme’s computational scope.

There is a real limitation to this negative case: a large platform with repeated rating profiles and block-structured social ties could support a sensible continuous model. The paper gives no basis for ruling out such a regime, or for claiming that no researcher could formulate a computational question around it. The defensible verdict is narrower: this paper does not provide an anchored continuous mirror, and the stronger candidates require adding the computational problem that the paper itself never asks.
