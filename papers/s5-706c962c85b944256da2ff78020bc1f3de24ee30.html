The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a ChoCo mirror is that this paper supplies no computational problem to continuize. Theorem 1 characterizes mechanisms satisfying fairness and incentive axioms; Corollary 1 is an impossibility under those axioms. Theorems 2 and 3 characterize further mechanism classes, and Proposition 4 selects a welfare-dominant mechanism. None gives a complexity result, an algorithmic formulation, or a computational subproblem. Calling the corollary a “negative result” does not make it a computational hardness result.

There are plausible high-multiplicity settings here. Public housing or licenses could involve large cohorts with repeated preferences, and one could scale the number of objects with the population and assign fractional masses by type. That would make a continuous allocation model sensible. But the paper’s results concern mechanisms defined over all preference profiles, including each agent’s unilateral misreports. A single distribution of types does not specify those deviations. A better continuum model could preserve an individual report channel alongside the population distribution, but then it is a new mechanism-design model whose fairness and incentive axioms need to be formulated and studied; it is not a continuous version of a computational result in this paper.

The obvious allocation query—given a finite-support population, choose the highest-valuation agents and apply one of the characterized payment rules—also does not supply a missing anchor. It introduces an input-output task the paper never studies. Its characterization results are about which rules satisfy universal properties, not about computing an allocation from an encoded instance.

So I would not claim that continuous populations are implausible for this application. The case is narrower: none of the named results survives as a worthwhile *computational mirror*. A new continuous mechanism-design project might be valuable, but the paper does not identify the computational question ChoCo is meant to chart.
