The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raises no anchor-eligible result. The numbered theorems characterize winners and exclusion zones; the corollary and Lemma 1 give asymptotic probabilities; Propositions 1 and 2 derive winner-position densities. None classifies the complexity of a computational problem or gives an algorithm for one. Theorem 3’s limit is in the number of candidates \(k\), not the size of the voting population.

That distinction matters here because the paper already models the population continuously: voters are the distribution \(F\), and each candidate’s vote share is population mass. For a fixed candidate set, that same society can be represented by masses on the induced ranking types. Recasting Theorem 1 or Theorem 4 in those coordinates would restate a result about a continuous population, not continue a discrete population. The exact-density proposal has the same issue: it studies random candidate configurations while voters are already continuous. It may be worthwhile computational geometry, but it is not a population-continuization mirror.

A stronger nearby question would be to find the least-cost transfer of voter mass between ranking types that makes a target candidate win, or that breaks an exclusion-zone guarantee. That is a plausible continuous computational-social-choice problem. But it adds an intervention objective the paper does not analyze; it is a new research direction, not a computational version of one of these results. The paper does explicitly point toward strategic follow-up work, so I cannot honestly argue that its authors would find such a question irrelevant.
