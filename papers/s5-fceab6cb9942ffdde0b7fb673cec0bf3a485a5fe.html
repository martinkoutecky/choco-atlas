The strongest possible rescue is the paper’s own ambition to scale to thousands of agents: define a continuous market and ask whether its coalition dynamics stabilize or improve aggregate gains. But preserving the mechanism requires much more than types for role, preferences, and economic category. Trust is recorded between named pairs, updated from their interaction histories, and used through pair-specific sums, counts, and maxima. A type that preserves those states must encode an agent’s trust relationships to the rest of the population; as the population grows, the number of distinct types can grow with it. Compressing those relationships into type-level averages or imposing exchangeability may yield a manageable model, but changes the mechanism whose outcomes the paper evaluates.

The continuum also needs new dynamics. In the paper, agents move between disjoint coalitions, and a single agent can form a coalition with a particular other agent. In an atomless population, one agent has zero mass. To model meaningful coalition formation, one must specify how masses move, which groups form coalitions, and how trust updates interact with those moves. The paper assumes other agents’ actions are static when an agent decides; it does not specify the simultaneous mass-flow rule or a stationary equilibrium problem. Adding one could make a coherent new model, but it would not mirror a computational result in this paper.

A fixed-state, one-step version is possible: aggregate trust by type and calculate each strategy’s preferred coalition. That is essentially arithmetic once the aggregate inputs are supplied, and it omits the learning, coalition merging, and long-run behavior that motivate the paper. A richer continuous dynamics or equilibrium project may be worthwhile in its own right, but it would require choosing new assumptions and posing a new problem.

So the strongest defensible negative case is that this is a poor ChoCo mirror: there is no named computational result to continue, and the most plausible population-level extension either simplifies away the mechanism’s pairwise learning or replaces it with new dynamics. The absolute claim that no continuous-market study could ever be valuable is stronger than the evidence supports; what the paper does not support is a worthwhile *mirror of its computational results*.
