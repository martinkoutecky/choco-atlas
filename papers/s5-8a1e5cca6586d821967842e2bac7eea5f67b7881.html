What the mirror covers

The candidate mirror covers the post-RSD ex-post equilibrium step; it leaves the sequential choices, strategic incentives, interim and pairwise trading results, and simulations outside the model.

The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t identify a qualifying computational anchor in this paper. It gives no named result classifying a problem as polynomial-time solvable, hard, FPT, or similar. The closest is Theorem 8.1, proved here: with quasilinear utilities, unrestricted group trades, and no transaction costs, RSD with ex-post transfers yields total welfare at least as high as plain RSD. That is a welfare guarantee, not a complexity result. Theorem 5.1 is also proved here, but its welfare-maximization guarantee depends on a feasibility assumption. Neither meets the anchor requirement, so there is no compliant anchor to pair with a continuous problem.

The strongest prospective mirror I see is a continuous ex-post housing-equilibrium problem. Let \(T\) be the finite set of complete agent types—valuation vectors over room categories, budgets, and endowed room—with rational masses \(\mu_t\). Let \(R\) be the room categories, with rational per-capita supplies \(q_r\), and let \(e_t\) be type \(t\)’s endowed room. Given values \(v_t(r)\), compute an assignment mass \(x_{tr}\), room prices \(p_r\), and type-level transfers
\[ z_t=p_{e_t}-\frac{1}{\mu_t}\sum_{r\in R}x_{tr}p_r. \]
Require \(\sum_r x_{tr}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_{tr}=q_r\), and \(x_{tr}>0\) only when \(r\) maximizes \(v_t(r)-p_r\). Then \(\sum_t\mu_t z_t=0\). Each person still receives one room; fractional \(x_{tr}\) records how the mass of a type is distributed across room categories.

A repeated large campus-housing cohort with relatively few shared valuation, budget, and endowment types is a plausible high-multiplicity regime. This equilibrium-computation question looks tractable via the assignment LP and its dual, but it is an unanchored extension of the paper, not a continuous mirror of a named complexity result. Its weakest point is that the paper’s strategic choices during RSD disappear from the equilibrium computation; the paper’s 10,000-agent simulations also do not establish that real valuation types remain few.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The required anchors are missing. Theorem 4.1 establishes Pareto efficiency, Theorem 5.1 gives welfare maximization under a feasibility assumption, and Theorem 8.1 compares total welfare with plain RSD. None classifies a computational problem. The equilibrium and simulation discussions do not supply one either: step 5 treats reaching equilibrium as a black box, and the simulations report outcomes rather than algorithmic complexity. So there is no named computational result here to mirror.

The proposed mass assignment is a credible *new* computational question. A repeated housing cohort with a small number of complete valuation and endowment types is a sensible high-multiplicity regime; the fact that assignments and transfers become fractional at the type level is not an objection. But the proposal takes the post-RSD endowment distribution as input and computes a competitive assignment from it. That leaves out the sequential choices and strategic incentives that motivate the paper’s mechanisms. Restoring those features means modeling how RSD generates endowments and how behavior depends on priority and information. In a continuum, individual pairwise trades move zero mass; representing them as trades by whole types would change who bargains and with what power.

The authors do call equilibrium a black box, so computing it for a continuous population is the strongest possible rescue. Still, that is a continuation of an intermediate step, not a continuous version of a computational result the paper proves. The negative case is strongest on that distinction. It is weaker as a universal claim: the post-RSD equilibrium computation could be worth studying in a high-multiplicity housing setting, and the paper’s explicit black box gives its authors a plausible reason to recognize the question. I would reject it as an anchored mirror under the stated standard, but I cannot honestly rule out every worthwhile continuous project suggested by the paper.
