The nearest tempting result is Theorem 1, proved in this paper: for an agenda \(X\), there is no independent, implicit-consensus-preserving, non-dictatorial aggregation rule \(F:J^n\to J\) if and only if \(X\) is non-nested and non-tiny. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that the paper has no computational result to mirror. Theorem 1 is a possibility result about aggregation rules \(F:J^n\to J\): under specified axioms, non-dictatorial rules exist exactly for nested or tiny agendas. Theorems 2–4 likewise characterize agenda-invariance through properties of aggregation systems. None studies an encoded computational problem, its complexity, or an algorithm.

A natural attempt to make a computational question would be to represent a consultation by masses \(\mu_J\) over rational judgment sets, then ask whether some feasible agenda reverses the collective judgment on a target proposition. But that requires choices the paper does not make: a particular aggregation rule or system of rules, a feasible agenda set, and a computational objective. The paper’s axioms quantify over profiles and rules; they do not specify this search problem. Choosing those ingredients could produce a sensible new agenda-control problem, but it would not be a continuous version of Theorem 1 or the paper’s other results.

Nor does directly extending the axioms supply an in-scope mirror. A rule on a continuum of agents would raise a new axiomatic question about how to aggregate a measure of judgments—precisely the kind of continuized axiomatic question the programme excludes. Some of the paper’s conclusions also turn on named individuals: dictatorship selects one person’s judgment, while a distribution over types forgets which person supplied which judgment. Restoring that identity, or changing dictatorship into a positive-mass bloc, changes the object being studied.

So the proponent’s consultation scenario establishes that a continuous population is plausible here; it does not establish a computational result to continuize. The negative case is strong for this paper’s contribution. It cannot rule out every useful computational problem inspired by agenda manipulation, but such a problem would need its own justification rather than count as a mirror of this paper.
