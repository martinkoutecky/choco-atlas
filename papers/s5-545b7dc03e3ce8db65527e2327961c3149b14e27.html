The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set of complete pair types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), hospital ownership, type-level compatibility and weights, fixed opponents' internal mass matchings, a player \(p\), a threshold \(\rho\), and \(\sigma\in\{\mathrm{pessimistic},\mathrm{optimistic}\}\), decide whether \(p\) has a feasible internal mass matching \(x^p\) such that the worst-case or best-case utility over all maximum-cardinality residual external mass matchings \(y\) is at least \(\rho\). Internal and external matching variables must satisfy the type-mass capacities \(\sum_u x^p_{tu}+\sum_u y_{tu}\le\mu_t\), and utility is the paper's weighted internal-plus-external matched-patient benefit.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity decentralized kidney-exchange game in which \(T\) contains complete exchangeable patient-donor types, \(\mu\) gives their masses, \(x^p\) are fractional internal matchings, \(y\) is a residual capacitated external matching maximizing matched mass, and hospitals optimize weighted utility under explicit pessimistic or optimistic type-level tie-breaking.

The objection that survived

The proponent does not establish that realistic national kidney-exchange data contain large cohorts sharing complete compatibility and weight signatures; under the paper's random-edge generator, the number of types may track the number of pairs.

Open questions for a prover

  • Is \(\mathrm{CBR}^{\mathrm{optimistic}}_{\infty}\) solvable by a polynomial-size LP or separation oracle when the type compatibility matrix is explicit?
  • Does the pessimistic max-min response admit a polynomial robust-flow formulation, or is it continuum-specifically hard?

The case FOR (proponent)

There is a credible mirror, and I would make the paper’s hardness result the lead. The strongest case is not that every kidney-exchange instance has a useful continuum limit, but that large regional or national programmes naturally contain a high-multiplicity regime in which the paper’s strategic game remains meaningful after individual pairs are replaced by masses of exchangeable types.

The population consists of patient–donor pairs, while hospitals, provinces, or countries remain the finitely many strategic players. A type \(t\) records the complete information relevant to the game: its owner, blood-group and tissue-compatibility signature, clinical-quality category, and all type-level edge weights. Two pairs have the same type only when the programme treats them identically in every respect. Let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of all pairs having type \(t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\).

This is plausible for a national exchange programme receiving thousands of pairs, many of which fall into a fixed or moderately sized collection of compatibility and quality classes. The intended regime is \(N\gg \tau\), where \(N\) is the number of pairs and \(\tau\) is the number of complete types. If detailed HLA profiles or pair-specific medical predictions make almost every pair unique, the regime disappears; that is a limitation of the mirror, not something to hide.

For each player \(p\), the action is a fractional internal matching \(x^p_{tu}\ge 0\) between types owned by \(p\). The constraint is that no type’s consumed mass exceeds its available mass: \(\sum_u x^p_{tu}\le \mu_t\). The fractionality is at the population level: every individual pair still either participates in an integral two-way exchange or does not, but a large homogeneous cohort may be split among several exchange patterns.

After the internal decisions, the independent agent chooses an external matching \(y_{tu}\) between types owned by different players. It may use only residual mass and maximizes total external matched mass. For fixed internal actions \(x\), write \(\mathcal Y(x)\) for the set of all such maximum-cardinality external mass matchings. Player \(p\)’s weighted utility is

\[ U_p(x^p,y)=\sum_{\{t,u\}\subseteq T_p} w^p_{tu}x^p_{tu} +\sum_{t\in T_p,\;u\notin T_p}w^p_{tu}y_{tu}. \]

The paper’s cardinality utility is the special case in which an internal exchange contributes two matched patients and an external exchange contributes one patient to each participating player.

My lead problem is Continuous Pessimistic/Optimistic Best Response, denoted \(\mathrm{CBR}^{\sigma}_\infty\). An instance contains the finite type set, rational masses \(\mu\), type-level compatibility and quality weights, a player \(p\), fixed internal mass matchings for the other players, a rational threshold \(\rho\), and a mode \(\sigma\in\{\mathrm{pessimistic},\mathrm{optimistic}\}\). The question is whether there exists a feasible internal mass matching \(x^p\) such that

\[ \operatorname{int}_p(x^p)+ \begin{cases} \displaystyle \min_{y\in\mathcal Y(x)} \operatorname{ext}_p(y), & \sigma=\mathrm{pessimistic},\[1ex] \displaystyle \max_{y\in\mathcal Y(x)} \operatorname{ext}_p(y), & \sigma=\mathrm{optimistic}, \end{cases} \ge \rho. \]

A solution is the mass matching \(x^p\), together with the corresponding worst- or best-case external response. This is recognisably the authors’ problem: the player still controls internal exchanges, the independent agent still maximizes external cardinality, and the strategic question is still whether a unilateral deviation achieves a prescribed profit. Only repeated, indistinguishable pairs have replaced named vertices.

The anchor is Theorem 2.1, proved in this paper, with the proofs supplied in Appendix 8 through Lemmas 8.1 and 8.4. It states that both \({\sc BestReponsepes}\) and \({\sc BestReponseopt}\) are NP-complete. In the paper, the pessimistic version asks whether a player can choose an internal matching so that, after the independent agent chooses a maximum-cardinality external matching minimizing that player’s utility, the player’s profit is at least a given positive integer \(P\). The optimistic version asks the same question when the independent agent chooses a maximum-cardinality external matching maximizing the player’s utility. The pessimistic hardness reduction is from Feedback Vertex Set; the optimistic one is from 3-Dimensional Matching.

I would expect the continuous versions to separate the sources of difficulty. In the optimistic case, the integral 3-Dimensional Matching gadget is replaced by fractional incidence among type masses. The selected triples become a transportation or fractional-matching object, and the external reaction is an LP over residual masses. My expectation is therefore Class A: tractable by a polynomial-size LP or a separation-based formulation, at least when the type compatibility structure is explicit and the number of types is the complexity parameter.

The pessimistic case is more interesting. Fractionalizing the Feedback Vertex Set gadget should destroy the original integral deletion obstruction, so the paper’s NP-hardness does not automatically transfer. But the worst-case choice among all maximum-cardinality external responses creates a genuine max–min reaction problem. I would expect either a polynomial robust-LP formulation or, if the lexicographic response map cannot be dualized efficiently, a Class C result: hardness caused by the continuum’s adversarial response structure rather than by population multiplicity. In either event, this is exactly the kind of boundary question the continuization programme is meant to expose.

A useful supporting anchor is Theorem 1.2, also proved here, not merely cited. It states that for any finite set of players and any deterministic cardinality-maximizing algorithm \(\mathcal A\) used by the independent agent, a social-welfare equilibrium exists and can be computed in polynomial time. A social-welfare equilibrium is a Nash equilibrium that is also a maximum matching of the overall compatibility graph.

Its continuous counterpart is Continuous Social-Welfare Equilibrium, \(\mathrm{SWE}_\infty\). Given rational type masses, a finite set of players, type-level compatibility, and a fixed deterministic rule \(\mathcal A_\infty\) selecting a maximum external mass matching, output internal mass matchings \(x^1,\ldots,x^n\) and the induced external matching \(y=\mathcal A_\infty(x)\) such that:

  • the combined internal and external matching maximizes total matched population mass; and
  • for every player \(p\), no alternative feasible internal mass matching \(x'^p\) gives that player a larger utility after \(\mathcal A_\infty\) recomputes the external matching.

I would expect \(\mathrm{SWE}_\infty\) to be Class A. The atomless matching problem is a capacitated transport LP, and the paper’s augmenting-path intuition should have a mass-flow analogue. The central follow-up is whether the computed equilibrium can be rounded to a finite exchange plan with a controlled additive loss, and how the loss depends on the number of types and the denominators of the masses.

The weakest point is that the paper’s original compatibility graph permits arbitrary pair-specific structure. If every HLA profile, quality estimate, or strategic value is unique, then \(\tau\) grows with \(N\), and the proposed mirror becomes little more than a fractional encoding of the original graph. The positive case therefore depends on a real high-multiplicity scenario: repeated clinical and institutional types, with type-level weights and compatibility. That is plausible for large exchange pools, but not universal.

Subject to that qualification, this is a strong mirror. It preserves the decentralized game, the player incentives, the independent agent’s cardinality objective, and the paper’s two named computational questions. It also gives a meaningful programme of work: determine whether the NP-completeness of Theorem 2.1 dissolves under population mass, whether pessimistic tie-breaking creates the first continuum-specific hardness, and whether the polynomial social-welfare equilibrium of Theorem 1.2 survives as an exact or approximate mass equilibrium.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the proposed type space is not yet a credible model of a kidney-exchange population. In this paper, compatibility is fundamentally relational: a pair’s complete description includes its compatibility with every other pair, and its edge-specific transplant weights. Blood group, cPRA, age, and similar attributes are not complete types. The paper’s own generator uses these attributes to create essentially pair-specific random edges.

This creates a sharp dichotomy. If types contain complete compatibility and weight-incidence information, then the Feedback Vertex Set and 3-Dimensional Matching gadgets in Theorem 2.1 require a distinct type for almost every logical vertex or triple. The continuous model then merely clones the paper’s named vertices and replaces multiplicities by capacities. If types are restricted to a small collection of biological classes, the arbitrary incidence structure that drives both hardness reductions disappears, and the result is a different aggregate transport game rather than a mirror of the paper’s game. The proponent has not supplied a convincing clinical regime in the middle.

That objection applies to both sides of Theorem 2.1. The proposed continuous pessimistic and optimistic best-response problems also introduce a new object: a deterministic rule \(\mathcal A_\infty\) selecting one maximum mass matching among many. The paper’s \(\mathcal A\) selects a matching of named residual pairs. An identity-based tie-break cannot survive quotienting by type; a type-level lexicographic rule is an additional modelling choice, not something inherited from the paper. Thus the proposed \(\mathrm{CBR}^{\sigma}_\infty\) is really a family of new Stackelberg flow games, parameterized by an externally imposed response rule.

The same concern is even sharper for Theorem 1.2. Its proof is built around integral alternating paths and a deterministic selection among individual maximum matchings. In the mass game, a player can make arbitrarily small deviations, type mass can be split among several exchanges, and the selected external flow can change discontinuously when capacities change. There is no canonical reason that the paper’s social-welfare-equilibrium theorem should have a meaningful type-level counterpart. Preserving individual priorities restores the original problem; discarding them produces a new equilibrium-selection problem.

These are genuine modelling weaknesses, but they do not establish the requested universal negative. A stronger version of the proponent’s model could explicitly posit large cohorts of pairs with identical complete compatibility and quality signatures, use the correct capacitated matching polytope rather than a naive transportation relaxation, and define \(\mathcal A_\infty\) by a fixed type-level lexicographic rule. That is a legitimate high-multiplicity regime, and the paper’s additive utilities do not depend on individual identity once such cohorts exist. The resulting questions remain recognisably about the paper’s best responses and equilibria.

So the honest negative case is only that the proponent has not yet justified the biological type regime or the response rule. It cannot defeat the anchors themselves. The paper contains named computational results, and a repeated-cohort kidney-exchange model is formally sensible. I would therefore not endorse the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists; at most, I would require the mirror to make its complete types, matching constraints, and tie-breaking rule explicit.
