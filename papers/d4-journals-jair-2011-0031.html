The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is conditional: the paper has a natural continuous-population analogue, but it fails the programme’s formal anchor requirement.

The supplied text contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result. Its relevant claims are prose summaries: the abstract says that STV manipulation is NP-hard; the introduction says that many voting rules are NP-hard to manipulate, citing [3]; and the final paragraph says that manipulating round-robin and cup tournaments is polynomial-time decidable, citing [12]. None is proved or stated as a named result in this paper. Thus there is no qualifying anchor to restate, and no fully compliant positive case can honestly be claimed.

If the anchor rule were relaxed to permit the paper’s prose-level claim about STV, my lead mirror would be:

Continuous STV Mass Manipulation. An instance consists of a finite candidate set \(C\), rational masses of sincere voter types \(\sigma_r\) indexed by rankings \(r\in S_C\), rational masses \(\rho_t\) of manipulable types, a preferred candidate \(c^\star\), and a fixed unique-winner or co-winner convention. A type records the voter’s truthful ranking and manipulability status; manipulable mass may report any ranking. A solution is a family of transfers \(x_{t,r}\ge 0\) satisfying
\[ \sum_{r\in S_C}x_{t,r}=\rho_t \]
for every manipulable type \(t\). The resulting ranking mass is
\[ \mu'_r=\sigma_r+\sum_t x_{t,r}. \]
The question is whether some feasible \(x\) makes \(c^\star\) win under the ordinary STV elimination rule applied to the weighted profile \(\mu'\).

This is a genuine population continuization of manipulation, not a change to fractional outcomes: candidates remain discrete, STV remains unchanged, and only the electorate becomes a distribution. The natural regime is a large platform, membership organisation, or mass election with millions of voters but relatively few recurring preference and eligibility templates. A campaign can influence a fraction of a homogeneous segment rather than target named individuals. That is precisely a high-multiplicity interpretation.

The authors would recognise the question: it asks whether a preferred candidate can be made to win by changing the reports of a manipulable coalition. The only change is that the coalition is represented by mass transfers rather than an explicit list of voters. For rational masses, finite elections embed directly by setting \(\sigma_r=n_r/n\) and \(\rho_t=k_t/n\); conversely, rational continuous solutions can be scaled to a finite electorate after clearing denominators.

I would expect this problem to be Class B when the number of candidates is variable: the combinatorics of STV elimination remain, and discrete manipulation instances embed into the continuous model. There is also a useful positive possibility: for fixed \(m=|C|\), one can enumerate elimination orders and express the conditions for each order as a linear program over the transfer variables. Thus the continuous model may expose an \(m\)-parameterized tractable regime even where the general problem remains hard.

The weakest point is decisive: this mirror is anchored only to an informal claim in a tutorial, not to a named result proved in the paper. A second vulnerability is that divisibility may dissolve hardness caused by individual coalition structure. The case survives only if the relevant hardness is genuinely in the candidate/elimination structure rather than in identity-level constraints. I would therefore record the paper as having a plausible continuous mirror, but no admissible computational anchor under ChoCo’s stated rules.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive negative case is formal: this paper supplies no admissible computational anchor. It is a tutorial survey, not a paper proving computational results. There is no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition establishing an algorithm, hardness result, approximation guarantee, or parameterized classification.

The proposed STV anchor is only the sentence that STV manipulation is NP-hard, followed by citation [3]. That result is neither formulated nor proved here. The same is true of the later claims about the NP-hard stable-marriage procedure and polynomial-time tournament manipulation: they report results from [10] and [12], rather than contributing results of this paper. The empirical observations about smooth phase transitions are not named complexity results either.

The proponent’s STV construction is nevertheless a perfectly coherent *new* ChoCo problem. A distribution over complete rankings, augmented with truthfulness or manipulability status, gives a sensible mass-manipulation model. It has a credible high-multiplicity interpretation in a large election or platform, and neither individual identity nor fractional outcomes is essential: STV depends on aggregate reported mass. Rational finite elections embed by normalisation, and the model can be strengthened with type-specific eligibility, utilities, budgets, or manipulation costs without becoming ill-defined.

That is precisely why it cannot rescue this paper. None of those modelling choices appears in Walsh’s paper, and no continuous or high-multiplicity manipulation problem is analysed there. The proposed LP-by-elimination-order observation would be a new theorem for a new problem, not a continuization of a result established in this tutorial. It is worthwhile as a possible ChoCo project, but it is not anchored to this paper under the programme’s rule.

The alternative topics do not provide a better rescue. Individual manipulation in stable marriage is tied to a named participant and their partner; in an atomless population, one participant has zero mass, while a positive-mass deviation becomes a different coalition-manipulation problem. Tournament manipulation is even more identity-dependent: teams, opponents, schedules, and individual games disappear under an unstructured population limit.

So the defensible verdict is: no paper-anchored continuous mirror. I cannot honestly defend the stronger substantive claim that no worthwhile continuous question exists in any scenario, because the STV mass-manipulation problem is natural. The negative result is that this paper is ineligible as its source, not that its subject matter is intrinsically unsuitable for continuization.
