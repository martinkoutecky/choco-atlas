The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The case against starts with the strongest ground available: there is no named computational result here to mirror. Definitions 1–13 formalize the framework and its metrics; the paper states no theorem, lemma, or corollary about the complexity of a computational problem. Its main results are empirical comparisons on VAST. So there are no eligible anchors and no per-result continuous questions. This is a scope objection, not a claim about what a continuous version would find.

The proposed population model is credible. A deliberative community can have many people sharing the same complete vote vector \(v:\mathrm{Ar}\to\{-1,0,1\}\), and representing those shares by \(\mu_v\) is sensible high multiplicity. I would not reject it on the ground that voters are individuated. But the best version of that proposal—a mass-weighted VAST evaluation, perhaps mapping regions of the vote-distribution simplex where a COS recovers \(E_{\mathrm{true}}\)—extends the benchmark rather than mirrors a computational result in the paper. For rational \(\mu\), it is directly the histogram of a finite vote profile; CSS’s utilitarian scores, for example, become weighted sums. That fact supports the model, but it does not supply the missing complexity question.

One could invent a harder problem, such as asking whether a COS tracks truth for every vote distribution meeting a reliability bound. That would be a robust truth-tracking problem, with a new worst-case objective and new constraints on the benchmark. It may be worthwhile in its own right, but the paper neither formulates nor solves it. The negative case is therefore strong as a reason not to count this paper as an anchored ChoCo mirror. It is weaker as the universal claim that no useful continuous research could grow out of the paper: the authors’ central truth-tracking question admits a natural mass-based extension, even though this paper gives no computational-complexity result to anchor one.
