The continuous mirror question

Given a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over types \(t=(p_t,w_t)\), where \(p_t\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), \(w_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and each individual has mass \(1/N\), along with a rational prediction \(\hat{o}\) and \(c\in[0,1)\) such that \(cN\) is integral, construct a deterministic mechanism \(F(\mu,\hat{o})\), computable in time polynomial in support size \(\tau\) and input bit length \(L\). A unilateral report changes one individual’s location and moves mass \(1/N\) between types, with that individual’s public weight fixed. Writing \(C_\mu(f)=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\lVert f-p_t\rVert_2\), \(o_\mu\in\arg\min_f C_\mu(f)\), and \(W_{\min},W_{\max}\) for the least and greatest supported weights, require strategyproofness and \(C_\mu(F(\mu,o_\mu))/C_\mu(o_\mu)\leq\frac{\sqrt{(1+c)^2W_{\min}^2+(1-c)^2W_{\max}^2}}{(1+c)W_{\min}}\), with \(C_\mu(F(\mu,\hat{o}))/C_\mu(o_\mu)\leq\frac{\sqrt{(1-c)^2W_{\min}^2+(1+c)^2W_{\max}^2}}{(1-c)W_{\min}}\) for every prediction \(\hat{o}\).

The model it lives in

Types are preferred locations paired with public weights; \(\mu\) gives each type’s population mass, with individuals retaining quantum \(1/N\). The objective is weighted Euclidean social cost \(C_\mu\); the mechanism chooses a facility location. The candidate is the coordinate-wise lower median of the location mass augmented by mass \(c\) at \(\hat{o}\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 1’s weighted CMP guarantee. It leaves Theorem 2’s impossibility and the paper’s tightness analyses aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the guarantees extend to genuinely atomless populations under a non-vacuous strategic solution concept?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is narrow: Theorem 1 is a constructive algorithmic anchor, though it is not a standard complexity-class result. It is proved in this paper; the weighted extension and its bounds are the authors’ result, building on the earlier unweighted CMP mechanism. The paper gives no named NP-hardness, \(P\), FPT, or similar classification. If “anchor” requires one of those classifications specifically, then there is no qualifying anchor here.

Theorem 1 says that in weighted facility location on \(\mathbb{R}^2\), there exists a deterministic strategyproof mechanism with consistency
\[ \frac{\sqrt{(1+c)^2W_{\min}^2+(1-c)^2W_{\max}^2}}{(1+c)W_{\min}} \]
and robustness
\[ \frac{\sqrt{(1-c)^2W_{\min}^2+(1+c)^2W_{\max}^2}}{(1-c)W_{\min}}, \]
for \(c\in[0,1)\). The paper’s mechanism adds \(\lfloor cn\rfloor\) copies of the predicted optimum and returns the coordinate-wise median. The reported weights affect the social cost and the bounds, but not the mechanism’s output.

A plausible mirror is Mass-CMP for Weighted Facility Location. Let each type be \(t=(p_t,w_t)\): a preferred point and its public weight. The input is a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over such types, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), a prediction \(\hat{o}\), and \(c\); let \(\tau\) be the support size. Social cost is
\[ C_\mu(f)=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\lVert f-p_t\rVert_2, \qquad o_\mu\in\arg\min_f C_\mu(f). \]
The question is: construct a deterministic direct-revelation mechanism \(F(\mu,\hat{o})\) that is strategyproof for each individual and achieves Theorem 1’s consistency bound when \(\hat{o}=o_\mu\), and its robustness bound for every \(\hat{o}\). The candidate solution is the coordinate-wise lower median of the location marginal of \(\mu\), augmented by mass \(c\) at \(\hat{o}\).

For individual incentives to remain meaningful, take the mass input to have a common denominator \(N\): one agent’s deviation moves mass \(1/N\), with \(cN\) integral. Then this is exactly the high-multiplicity version of the paper’s finite profile: a type’s mass records how many agents share its location and weight. The mechanism can sort the \(\tau\) supported coordinates and scan cumulative masses, so it is computable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the input bit length. The expected class is tractable; the proposed result is the same guarantee as Theorem 1, expressed on a rational mass input.

The regime is credible for public-service siting: many residents share a building or service-point location and a public priority class, with millions of residents but far fewer distinct location-weight types. The paper itself motivates weighted facility location with hospitals and public services, so the authors should recognize this as a high-multiplicity version of their problem. This mirror covers Theorem 1 alone. It does not turn the paper’s geometric outcome space into a population-continuous model; the population is the object being aggregated.

The weakest point is that the rational-mass version lifts directly to a finite clone profile by choosing denominator \(N\), so it adds little new computational difficulty. Also, a genuinely atomless population makes an individual deviation have zero mass and can make strategyproofness vacuous. The precise question therefore needs the finite individual mass quantum above; extending the guarantee to arbitrary real masses, and deciding whether Theorem 2’s \(n\)-dependent impossibility has a meaningful atomless counterpart, are further questions. Theorem 2 is not a computational-hardness anchor, so I would not use it to strengthen this case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is strongest on scope: this paper has no named complexity result for a computational problem. Theorem 1 proves a mechanism’s consistency and robustness guarantees; Theorem 2 gives an impossibility about those guarantees. Neither asks whether a problem is in \(P\), is hard, or admits an exact, approximate, or parameterized algorithm. The paper’s central work is mechanism design and distortion analysis. That leaves ChoCo without the kind of computational landscape its programme is set up to chart.

The proponent’s Mass-CMP is still the best possible anchor. Its residents-by-building-and-priority-class scenario is plausible, and weights can be included in the type without losing the information the model uses. But preserving the paper’s individual strategyproofness forces a choice. In a genuinely atomless model, one person’s report has zero mass, so it cannot change the mechanism’s output; strategyproofness becomes vacuous. If instead each person has mass \(1/N\), the model is exactly a finite profile encoded by rational proportions. Mass-CMP is then the paper’s coordinate median with phantom mass \(c\), and the stated bounds lift by replacing counts with proportions. Computing the coordinate medians from the \(\tau\) supported types is just sorting and scanning. That is a valid high-multiplicity formulation, but it exposes no new computational obstacle in this paper.

That distinction matters here: the proposed mirror asks for the same mechanism and guarantees on a compressed representation, rather than posing a complexity question the paper left open. The programme treats high multiplicity as meaningful, so compression alone is not a reason to reject the mirror. Still, the computational content available to mirror is thin: the paper already specifies the mechanism, and its output has an elementary implementation.

Theorem 2 does not offer a stronger anchor. Its lower bound depends on \(n\) and unilateral deviations. In an atomless society, the singleton deviation again has no effect; with mass quantum \(1/N\), the \(n\)-dependence returns along with the finite clone model. A positive-mass coalition formulation might avoid that collapse, but it would change the strategic question from individual strategyproofness to coalition behavior.

So I cannot make the universal negative airtight: the high-multiplicity facility-location regime is sensible, and the proponent’s mirror survives as a well-posed lift. The honest case against is narrower. This paper supplies no compelling computational result for ChoCo to classify, and its best mirror looks like a straightforward mass reformulation of a mechanism whose computation is already simple. That makes it a weak use of the programme’s time, though the argument is about fit and payoff, not about the model being incoherent.
