That is the strongest case against this paper: it reports no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition about computational complexity. Its TFP hardness claim is attributed to Aziz et al. (2014). This paper’s contribution is empirical: a solver handled the tested finite tournaments easily, and the authors compared distributions of tournament-winning probabilities. Those findings do not supply a named complexity result to continuize.

The most plausible rescue is a high-multiplicity TFP: group entrants with identical matchup behavior into types, give each type a mass, mark a target entrant, and ask whether a seeding lets that target win. But this paper studies tournaments of distinct clubs and players, each occupying a leaf of a finite binary bracket. A type distribution alone does not specify those leaves or represent the single target; adding a marked entrant and a limiting sequence would define a new tournament model. The paper offers no evidence that such a regime is natural for its instances. This is a serious objection to calling that model a mirror, though it does not prove that no useful model could be built.

One could instead make a continuous electorate over rankings of the entrants, derive each matchup from pairwise majority, and then ask whether the target can win. That gives the population a genuine role, but it adds a voter aggregation layer absent from the paper’s sports data and is not a continuous version of either reported empirical finding. Likewise, continuizing the CR probabilities would continuize the noise model, not the population.

So the negative case is strong for the paper as written: it gives no eligible computational anchor, and its other results concern finite tournaments or probability distributions. The universal claim that no scenario could yield a worthwhile mirror is weaker. A voter-generated tournament or a carefully defined high-multiplicity entrant pool could be a legitimate adjacent problem; the paper itself does not establish that either is a worthwhile mirror of its results.
