What the mirror covers

The proposed model would recast Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.1, and Theorem 3.2 as questions about allocation fields over role-mass coalitions; the paper's other results and class comparisons are also structural rather than computational.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive seed is that population is already the paper’s central variable: its results compare allocations across nested coalitions and subgames. A plausible high multiplicity regime would be a large cooperative enterprise with many agents in a small number of genuinely interchangeable roles, where coalition worth depends on the amount of each role participating. Then a coalition could be represented by a mass vector rather than a list of named players. This makes a continuous population model conceivable, though it would require new primitives for coalition worth and for egalitarian transfers.

But the paper has no named computational result of the kind required for an anchor. The closest results are Theorem 3.1 (proved here: every game with an egalitarian population monotonic allocation scheme is an exact partition game, and the Dutta–Ray solution induces its unique such scheme), Corollary 3.1 (proved here: this is the maximal domain on which population monotonicity and egalitarian core selection are compatible), and Theorem 3.2 (proved here: the Dutta–Ray solution is the unique solution satisfying efficiency and population monotonicity on that domain). These are structural and axiomatic results; none establishes a problem as tractable, hard, or parameterized tractable.

So there is no qualifying anchor, and I cannot write one continuous problem per anchor under your rules. The paper’s real-valued payoffs do not themselves continuize the population: the model still has a finite player set \(N\), coalitions \(S\subseteq N\), and axioms about adding named players. The main weakness in the positive case is that moving to masses would require deciding how the paper’s individual transfer condition and arbitrary coalition worth function behave in an atomless, type-based society. That looks like a possible new research direction, but not a result-anchored continuous mirror of this paper’s computational question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no computational result here to continuize. Theorem 3.1 is a structural statement about egalitarian population monotonic allocation schemes and the Dutta–Ray solution; Corollary 3.1 characterizes a maximal domain of compatibility between two axioms; Theorem 3.2 is a uniqueness characterization. None poses an algorithmic problem or gives a complexity result. The paper also supplies no input-size model: its primitive is an arbitrary characteristic function \(v:2^N\to\mathbb R\), not a succinctly represented instance to optimize over.

The strongest possible mirror would put many interchangeable agents into a few roles and represent a coalition by its type-mass vector \(q\). Give it a worth function \(V(q)\), and ask whether there is a measurable allocation field \(x(q)\) that is core-feasible and population-monotone, or whether a continuum version of the Dutta–Ray scheme is unique. That is a coherent continuous-population model. But it carries Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 into questions about existence and characterization of allocation fields, and Corollary 3.1 into a question about axiomatic compatibility on a new domain. Those are continuized axiomatic questions, which ChoCo explicitly places outside its computational scope.

One could add computation: decide whether \(V\) admits such a scheme, or compute the Dutta–Ray allocation. That would be a new problem, not one this paper studies. The choice of representation for \(V\) would determine the problem: an oracle over a continuum does not give a finite input, while a formula or piecewise-linear encoding imposes new structure whose complexity could dominate the result. Nor does grouping agents by type settle that issue. With arbitrary coalition complementarities, the worth of a coalition is still an unrestricted function of its type composition; making the population continuous does not by itself make that function compact or tractable.

So the three proposed anchors all fail for the same central reason, even under a much better high-multiplicity scenario than the paper suggests: their content is axiomatic, and turning it into an algorithmic task requires supplying a different computational model. A continuous cooperative-game project could certainly be worthwhile on its own. The honest negative case is narrower: this paper offers no result-anchored continuous computational mirror for the ChoCo programme.
