The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), a symmetric graph \(H=(T,F)\) with \(\deg_H(t)=4\) for every \(t\in T\), and positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), decide whether the population can be divided into exactly three nonempty coalitions while keeping every enemy pair apart. Equivalently, is there a rational matrix \(x_{tj}\geq 0\), for \(t\in T\) and \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\), with \(\sum_j x_{tj}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_t x_{tj}>0\) for each \(j\), and, for every \(\{t,u\}\in F\) and \(j\), at least one of \(x_{tj},x_{uj}\) equal to zero?

The model it lives in

Types are vertices of a symmetric four-regular enemy graph; all members of adjacent types are enemies, and all other pairs, including members of the same type, are neutral. The input gives each type's positive rational mass \(\mu_t\); the decision variables \(x_{tj}\) assign its mass across three coalitions, subject to full allocation, nonempty coalitions, and no enemy pair sharing a coalition.

What the mirror covers

Covers the symmetric, no-friends graph-coloring restriction of \(\IRkfo\); it leaves the balanced-size clause and the paper's other preference, size, and parameterized results aside.

The case FOR (proponent)

Lead: 3-Coalition Enemy-Type IR. The anchor is the paper’s result labeled thm:FO:k:fourEnemies:NPh; the supplied text gives its cross-reference label but not a rendered theorem numeral. Proved in this paper, it says that both \(\IRkfo\) and \(\IRBSCfo\) are NP-complete even on symmetric instances with \(k=3\) and exactly four enemies per agent. Its \(\IRkfo\) reduction uses a graph with no friendships: an individually rational coalition must be an independent set in the enemy graph, so the construction carries 3-coloring hardness. The paper relies on known hardness of 3-coloring on 4-regular graphs. I use the \(\IRkfo\) clause as my anchor; I am not claiming a mirror of the theorem’s \(\IRBSCfo\) clause.

Here is the continuous problem. The instance is a finite type set \(T\), a positive rational mass \(\mu_t\) for each type with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), and a symmetric enemy graph \(H=(T,F)\). Everyone has no friends; members of type \(t\) consider every member of type \(u\) an enemy exactly when \(\{t,u\}\in F\), and are neutral to everyone else, including members of their own type. The question, 3-Coalition Enemy-Type IR, is whether the population can be divided among exactly three nonempty coalitions so that every agent is individually rational. A solution is a matrix \(x_{tj}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge 0}\), for \(t\in T\) and \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\), such that
\[ \sum_{j=1}^{3}x_{tj}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t\in T}x_{tj}>0, \]
and, for every \(\{t,u\}\in F\) and coalition \(j\), at least one of \(x_{tj},x_{uj}\) is zero. Each unit of mass belongs to one coalition; \(x_{tj}\) records how much of type \(t\) goes to coalition \(j\). With no friends, the paper’s IR condition is precisely that an agent share no coalition with an enemy.

This has a credible high-multiplicity regime: for example, a large residential programme with many participants drawn from recurring cohorts whose members have the same group-level incompatibilities. If there are \(q\) people per type, then \(n=q\tau\); \(q\) can be large even while the number \(\tau=|T|\) of distinct relationship profiles is much smaller than \(n\). The finite 4-regular hardness instances give type graphs of degree four. Replicating each type makes its members enemies of the people in four other types, so their individual enemy counts grow with \(q\).

That replication does not erase the hardness. In any feasible mass allocation, each type has positive mass in at least one coalition. Choose one such coalition for each type. Adjacent types cannot choose the same coalition, so these choices give a proper coloring of \(H\) with at most three colors. Conversely, a proper 3-coloring assigns each type’s mass to its color’s coalition. If fewer than three colors are used, split a color class across an unused coalition; since that class is independent, this preserves IR and makes all three coalitions nonempty. Thus feasibility is equivalent to 3-colorability, regardless of the positive rational masses. The expected classification is hardness transfers (Class B): the hardness lives in the type graph’s coloring structure, not in the number of individual agents.

The mirror covers the paper’s \(\IRkfo\) problem in the no-friends, symmetric graph-coloring case. It does not cover the balanced-size result or the paper’s broader parameterized landscape. My weakest point is that the theorem’s literal “four enemies per agent” restriction is not preserved: a replicated person has many individual enemies, though exactly four enemy *types*. So this is a mirror of the underlying IR coalition-feasibility problem and its coloring mechanism, not of that bounded-individual-degree restriction. The mass values also do not affect feasibility in this \(k\)-coalition variant. Those limits narrow the claim, but the stated continuous problem remains a precise high-multiplicity version of the authors’ own decision problem, and splitting types across coalitions leaves its hardness intact.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is a mismatch between the theorem’s bounded-degree condition and the proposed high-multiplicity lift. In thm:FO:k:fourEnemies:NPh, each agent has exactly four enemies. If a type has \(q\) copies and every member of each neighboring type is an enemy, each person has \(4q\) enemies. The mirror preserves four enemy *types*, not four enemy *agents*. To keep each person’s degree at four, one would need a sparse pattern of individual links; then the distribution over types and a type-level enemy graph no longer determine who is enemies with whom. That weakens the claim that this particular theorem has been mirrored.

The stronger type-level version nevertheless survives that objection. Cohorts with blanket incompatibilities are a coherent instance regime, and their mass-allocation problem is well defined. In fact, any feasible allocation can be rounded to a coloring: choose one coalition receiving positive mass from each type. Adjacent types cannot choose the same coalition, so these choices give a proper 3-coloring. Conversely, a coloring assigns each type’s mass to a coalition, splitting a color class if needed to make all three coalitions nonempty. Thus mass values and fractional splitting do no work in this instance; the question is exactly 3-colorability of the type graph.

That makes the mirror less faithful to the theorem’s “four enemies per agent” restriction, and leaves no mass-sensitive computation to study. But the programme explicitly treats hardness transfer as a meaningful Class B outcome, so neither point defeats the improved mirror. The paper has named computational results, and I see no sound basis here for claiming that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. The negative case is weak: it identifies a limitation of the proposed correspondence, but the cohort-level coloring question remains a credible mirror of the paper’s IR feasibility problem.
