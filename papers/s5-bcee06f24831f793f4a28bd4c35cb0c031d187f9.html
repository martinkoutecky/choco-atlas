The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The positive case has no result anchor. In the text supplied, the claims are simulation findings about TF-OCP, GF-OCP, and MF-OCP, plus comparisons among control-selection heuristics. The NP-hardness of finding distance centers is cited background, not a complexity result about ferment control. Those experiments can motivate new questions, but none is a named computational result of this paper to continuize.

The best TF-OCP mirror would use many agents in recurring network roles, with a mass and control level for each role. But the paper’s control choices depend on who is connected to whom: degree and distance heuristics behave differently, and the greedy method uses network structure. A role label such as degree, stubbornness, or community does not preserve those relations. Preserving them exactly tends to make types encode growing neighborhoods, so the support need not stay moderate. A block model or interaction kernel would give a legitimate mean-field control problem, but it would replace the paper’s sparse, instance-specific network with a different model.

GF-OCP is the strongest candidate: its target already concerns a fraction of nodes, so a mass version of the sigmoid group-ferment condition is natural. That is not, by itself, continuization of the population—the agents remain finite—but it makes a population-level formulation especially plausible. Still, the reported dependence on \(a\) and \(k\) is experimental, not a computational theorem. The role-level version would require its own analysis of a nonlinear control problem over a limiting interaction model. That could be worthwhile research, but it would not mirror a result established here.

For MF-OCP, keeping a fixed number of controlled nodes as the population grows can make their mass vanish in a homogeneous model. Scaling the budget with population size repairs that problem, but then the question is a new per-capita control problem, and the reported budget curve remains an experimental result rather than a complexity claim.

So the case against is strongest on the stated burden: none of the proposed TF, GF, or MF mirrors is anchored in a named computational result of the supplied text, and the most faithful network versions do not yield a compact type description. I would not claim that no useful continuum model could be made. GF-OCP in particular has a credible mass-based extension, and the universal negative case is weak there. The narrower conclusion is that the proponent has identified a plausible research direction, not a result-grounded continuous mirror of this paper.
