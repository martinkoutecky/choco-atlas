The strongest reconstructed candidate is the primary task: rank or classify alternatives from their criterion values. One could define a distribution over alternative feature and label types and study expected ranking loss. But that makes the alternatives or data continuous, rather than making a society continuous. Treating alternatives as interchangeable population members would also change their role: they are the objects being ranked, with their own features and labels, not agents whose preferences or actions form a society. A distribution over decision-makers would be another possible project, but it would change the paper’s task from modeling a decision-maker’s preferences to aggregating a population’s preferences.

The auxiliary criterion-relation task, LLM-derived weights, and ablation results offer no stronger anchor: they too are model components or empirical findings, not computational results. A continuous reformulation might be worth studying on its own, but it would be a new statistical-learning or optimization question, not a continuous mirror of a result in this paper.

The broader claim that *no scenario* could yield worthwhile research is weaker: the manuscript cannot rule out a useful new project; it simply provides no computational result that makes that project a mirror of this paper.
