What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers PGJCR’s distribution-based witness guarantee; it leaves the paper’s finite-sample elicitation and query-complexity results aside.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest mirror is the mass version of the paper’s EJR+ committee-selection problem. It has a natural high-multiplicity regime: for example, a large participatory-budgeting electorate in which many residents share one of a modest number of issue-tolerance rectangles. A type is a rectangle \(R_j\), and its mass \(\mu_j\) is the fraction of residents with that complete preference type. The committee remains a set of at most \(k\) actual projects.

My anchor is Theorem [PGJCR] (\(\Cref{thm:pgjcr}\), Section 4.1), proved in the paper. The supplied text does not show a numeric theorem number, so I cite its printed name and label rather than invent one. It says that when the rectangle distribution \(\mathcal D\) is known and voters are drawn i.i.d. from it, PGJCR uses no voter pool and makes no queries, and deterministically returns a committee of at most \(k\) candidates satisfying (PW) with margin \(\delta=1/(k(k+1))\). In full, for every unelected candidate \(c\) and level \(\ell\in[k]\), its witness probability obeys
\[ p_{c,\ell}\leq \frac{\ell}{k}-\frac{1}{k(k+1)} \]
where \(p_{c,\ell}\) is the probability that a voter approves \(c\) and approves fewer than \(\ell\) committee members.

The corresponding continuous problem is Mass-EJR+ Committee Selection. Given rational candidate locations \(C\subseteq[0,1]^d\), a committee size \(k\), and \(\tau\) rectangle types \(R_1,\ldots,R_\tau\) with binary-rational masses \(\mu_j\geq0\) summing to \(1\), return \(W\subseteq C\), \(|W|\leq k\), such that for every \(c\in C\setminus W\) and \(\ell\in[k]\),
\[ \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j\, \mathbf{1}\!\left[c\in R_j,\ \left|W\cap R_j\right|<\ell\right] <\frac{\ell}{k}. \]
This is exactly the mass form of the paper’s cohesive-group condition: a violating group would have to fit inside the mass of voters counted by that indicator. A solution is the committee \(W\), with the displayed inequalities as its guarantee.

I expect this problem to be tractable for explicit finite-support input. PGJCR tests the same witness masses and gives the stronger bound \(\ell/(k+1)<\ell/k\). With the support listed explicitly, those masses can be computed by scanning the \(\tau\) types; the resulting running time is polynomial in \(m,d,k,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the rational input bit length. This keeps the paper’s fairness question and discrete committee intact; it changes the electorate from sampled individuals to divisible mass.

The mirror is plausible to the authors because their PGJCR module already reasons about precisely these distributional witness probabilities. That is also its main limitation: the mass problem is nearly solved inside the paper, so it is a good positive mirror but a weak claim of new territory. It covers distribution-based committee selection, not the paper’s finite-sample query guarantees. The paper supplies no named hardness result to anchor a hardness-transfer question. Natural follow-ups are whether the same mass guarantee is tractable for succinctly specified continuous distributions, and what query complexity remains when the distribution is unknown rather than given explicitly.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The PGJCR anchor is a direct collision with a continuous-population result already in the paper. For the proposed finite-support society, the mass in its definition is exactly \(p_{c,\ell}=\sum_j\mu_j\mathbf{1}[c\in R_j,\ |W\cap R_j|<\ell]\). PGJCR takes the distribution over ballots as input and proves \(p_{c,\ell}\leq \ell/(k+1)<\ell/k\) for every unelected \(c\) and every \(\ell\). That is the proposed Mass-EJR+ guarantee, with slack. With \(\tau\) listed types, each test is computed by summing their rational masses, so the stated mirror follows directly from the paper’s algorithm in time polynomial in \(m,d,k,\tau,L\). This is not merely a finite-sample result that happens to resemble a mass result: PGJCR reasons directly about the distribution’s witness probabilities and returns the committee from those aggregate quantities.

The strongest rescue is to make the distribution unknown or to add elicitation. But NGJCR already estimates those same witness probabilities and supplies (PW) with high probability; its guarantee is itself a mass guarantee. The paper’s further verification and query bounds address the distinct task of ensuring EJR+ for a finite sampled electorate. A continuum mirror of that task either asks directly for mass-EJR+, already covered by PGJCR or NGJCR, or retains sampled voters and their query costs, returning to the paper’s original setting.

A succinctly encoded continuous density over rectangle types might create a new integration or optimization problem. Its difficulty would depend on how the density is represented, though, and it is outside the proposed explicit-support mirror. That makes the negative case strong for the only anchor raised and for the programme’s canonical finite-support model. The universal claim is weaker: a carefully chosen density encoding could support a worthwhile follow-up, but it would need to be framed as a new representation-sensitive problem, not as a missing mirror of PGJCR.
