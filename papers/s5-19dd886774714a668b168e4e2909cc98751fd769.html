What the mirror covers

The case FOR (proponent)

The paper contains no named computational result of the kind required for an anchor. Theorem 2, proved here, characterizes the solutions satisfying efficiency, linearity, symmetry, and null player neutrality as \(\{\alpha\phi^{ED}+(1-\alpha)\phi^{Sh}:\alpha\in\mathbb R\}\). Theorem 4, also proved here, characterizes the equal division solution using efficiency, symmetry, and nullifying player neutrality. These are axiomatic characterization results, not claims that a problem is in P, NP-hard, or otherwise computationally classified. Theorem 1 and Theorem 3 are cited results, and they too are characterizations.

There is a possible high-multiplicity setting behind the subject: a large cooperative enterprise with many interchangeable participants in each role, where a type is a participant’s complete coalition-contribution profile and its mass is that role’s share of the population. One might ask how to compute each type’s per-capita allocation from a representation of the coalition worth function. But the paper does not define that computational problem, and its characterizations do not establish its complexity. Making it precise would require choices about how coalition worths scale with mass and how to define a Shapley-style marginal contribution in a continuum; those choices could change the problem the authors studied.

So I cannot give a paper-anchored continuous problem or predict its place in the tractability–hardness trichotomy without inventing an anchor. The strongest honest positive observation is that the subject has a plausible high-multiplicity economic setting. The weakness is decisive for this task: the paper’s named results concern axioms for finite-player solutions, not computational questions about a continuously described society.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I would not green this paper as a continuous-computational mirror. Its main results are Theorem 2, an axiomatic characterization of the family \(\{\alpha\phi^{ED}+(1-\alpha)\phi^{Sh}:\alpha\in\mathbb R\}\), and Theorem 4, an axiomatic characterization of equal division. Neither states a complexity result or poses an algorithmic problem. Theorem 4 also recasts a characterization the paper shows is equivalent to van den Brink’s earlier one. The proponent’s concession that there is no named computational result is therefore the strongest point against a mirror.

The proposed enterprise with interchangeable roles is plausible, but it starts a new problem. One could define worth as a function of role counts or masses and ask how to compute a type-level allocation. That requires choices the paper does not make: how worth scales with population, how the Shapley value extends to a continuum, and how the worth function is represented as input. For a generic TU-game, a player’s type would have to capture their marginal contributions across every coalition context; repeated types arise only after imposing structure, such as worth depending on role counts. Different choices produce different computational questions, none anchored by the paper’s theorems.

The most natural limiting versions also put pressure on the axioms being mirrored. With one fully interchangeable population, symmetry and efficiency make Shapley and equal division coincide, so Theorem 2’s family no longer distinguishes them. In a smooth atomless model, an individual has zero mass, so the paper’s individual-level notion of a null player needs a new type-level interpretation or a different continuum value concept. Those changes may be reasonable, but they define the new research question; they do not continuize a computational result in this paper.

The universal negative is not airtight: a structured cooperative enterprise could support worthwhile continuous computational work. But the proponent offers it as a possible setting, not a computational question derived from a named result. The honest case against is that this paper supplies no computational anchor to mirror; any stronger proposal would be a separate modelling and algorithmic project.
