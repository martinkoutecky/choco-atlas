The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent is right in a narrow sense: the paper has no numbered theorem classifying the complexity of a problem. But that does not establish that there is no worthwhile continuous mirror. The paper’s main contribution is the named algorithm PrefAcq, with a specified input, preference query interface, target soft constraint network, and convergence goal. A continuous mirror can extend that computational task; it need not pretend the paper proved a complexity theorem.

Consider a large organization choosing a schedule or allocation. Its stakeholders share the same feasible assignments and hard constraints, but belong to a small number \(\tau\) of preference types, each described by a complete soft constraint network. Let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of stakeholders of type \(t\). For a partial preference query \(q=(e,e')\), the aggregate oracle returns the fractions preferring \(e\), indifferent, or preferring \(e'\). The computational question is whether an adaptive algorithm can learn the population’s aggregate response function over all allowed queries, and with what query and running-time bounds in \(|X|\), \(\tau\), the bias size, and the mass bit length \(L\). This asks about learning from a continuous population: “31% prefer this schedule” is the relevant observation, not which individuals gave each answer.

The target should be the aggregate response function, or the population of query-distinguishable types, rather than an unobservable record of individual identities. That handles a real limitation: aggregate answers may not identify the exact mixture of types. If all stakeholders share one type, the problem reduces to the paper’s original setting; a small heterogeneous support is the regime that makes the population model interesting.

So the absence of a theorem blocks a strict result-to-result mirror, but it is not a fundamental obstruction to a computational population question. The case against a mirror is weak here: the paper supplies a concrete learning problem and algorithm to extend, even though it does not supply a formal complexity result to mirror.
