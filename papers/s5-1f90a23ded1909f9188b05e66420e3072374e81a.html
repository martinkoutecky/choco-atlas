The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper presents a hydro-economic allocation method, solves a case study with SDDP, and specifies a transfer rule; it does not state a theorem, lemma, or other result about computational complexity. The proponent raised no anchors, so there is none to defeat. That leaves their proposed farm-population model as a plausible extension, not a mirror tied to a result of this paper.

The strongest version of that extension would treat farms as a continuum within each irrigation area, with type recording location, crop, demand profile, and productivity. Basin supplies and flow constraints would couple the aggregate allocations, and the authority would calculate charges and compensation as in the paper. Large irrigation populations make this a credible high-multiplicity regime, and the authors could plausibly recognize it.

The difficulty is that the paper already aggregates irrigation users into site-level agents. With the basin’s finite network fixed, a mass of farms at each site would aggregate back into those agents’ demand and benefit inputs. The optimization’s defining structure remains the hydrological network, reservoir decisions, and uncertain inflows; the paper does not identify population multiplicity as a computational problem. A continuous treatment might still be useful to water-resource research, but it would be a new application of continuous optimization, not a computational mirror of a named result here.

So the negative case is strong against an anchor-backed mirror from this paper. It does not support the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists: the farm-population extension is too plausible to dismiss honestly.
