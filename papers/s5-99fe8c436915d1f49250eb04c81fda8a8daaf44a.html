What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers simultaneous-composition smoothness and its correlated- and Bayesian-equilibrium welfare consequences; it leaves sequential composition, valuation characterizations, weak and conservative smoothness, budget constraints, and auction-specific applications alone.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can arbitrary \(N\)-player correlated-equilibrium constraints be represented by finitely many type-level variables as \(N\to\infty\)?
  • Does a genuinely nonatomic equilibrium notion preserve the paper’s smoothness guarantee without replacing unilateral deviations by coalition deviations?
  • For succinct mechanisms and valuation representations, is the worst-equilibrium welfare threshold decidable or approximable?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is qualified: a recognizable high-multiplicity mirror exists, but this paper has no qualifying computational-complexity anchor under ChoCo’s strict rule. Its numbered results concern smoothness, equilibrium welfare, and composability—not membership in \(\mathrm{P}\), hardness, approximation algorithms, or parameterized complexity. Thus I would not present it as a fully compliant positive case. The mirror below is the best rescue if welfare-guarantee theorems count as algorithmic anchors.

The lead anchor is Theorem 5.1, proved in this paper. It states that for a simultaneous composition of \(m\) mechanisms, if every component mechanism \(M_j\) is \((\lambda,\mu)\)-smooth for a specified class of component valuations \(V_i^j\), and every player’s valuation across mechanisms is fractionally subadditive and has an XOS representation whose component valuations belong to \(V_i^j\), then the global composed mechanism is also \((\lambda,\mu)\)-smooth. Together with Theorem 4.2, this yields a welfare guarantee of \(\lambda/\max\{1,\mu\}\) for every correlated equilibrium; Theorem 4.3 gives the corresponding Bayesian guarantee. Those are welfare theorems, not polynomial-time results.

A plausible mirror is a high-multiplicity market with many advertisers or procurement agents participating in the same finite collection of simultaneous auctions. A type \(t\) completely specifies an agent’s valuation over all local outcomes, budget or participation restrictions if relevant, and any other mechanism-relevant parameter. The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over finitely many such types. There are \(N\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\) whenever \(N\mu_t\) is integral, with \(N\gg |T|\). This is plausible for repeated campaign templates, recurring procurement profiles, or large populations of advertisers with the same value and budget pattern.

The local outcomes remain integral: each agent receives either an item or no item in each auction. Aggregate mass may be split across different integral bundles, but no individual receives a fractional bundle. If supply scales with population, an auction with per-capita supply \(q_j\) supplies \(Nq_j\) units in the \(N\)-clone realization. The action variable is each type’s distribution over bids in each mechanism; the objective is aggregate welfare, \(\mathrm{SW}_\mu=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\,\mathbb{E}[v_t(x_t)]\).

The precise continuous problem I would give a prover is:

HM–Simultaneous-Composition Welfare Threshold. The input consists of finitely described simultaneous mechanisms \(M^1,\ldots,M^r\), a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), complete type valuations \(v_t\) that are fractionally subadditive across mechanisms, and a rational threshold \(\rho\). For every admissible clone count \(N\), form the finite game with \(N\mu_t\) clones of each type. Let \(\operatorname{OPT}_{N,\mu}\) be its maximum welfare and let \(\operatorname{CE}(N,\mu)\) be its correlated equilibria. Decide whether

\[ \liminf_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{|T|}}} \inf_{\sigma\in\operatorname{CE}(N,\mu)} \frac{\mathbb{E}_\sigma[\mathrm{SW}_{N,\mu}]} {\operatorname{OPT}_{N,\mu}} \ge \rho . \]

A positive solution should provide a uniform smoothness certificate: local \((\lambda,\mu')\)-smoothness witnesses together with the XOS component representations. Theorem 5.1 then supplies the global certificate, and Theorems 4.2–4.3 imply the bound \(\rho=\lambda/\max\{1,\mu'\}\) uniformly over all clone counts. A negative solution should provide an admissible clone sequence and equilibria violating the threshold.

The expected direction is Class A only for this certificate version: the paper’s composition argument transfers the welfare guarantee without searching over the population. The exact computation of the worst equilibrium, or of succinct XOS representations and optimal welfare, is not shown to be polynomial-time by this paper. Combinatorial allocation difficulty can remain after continuization, so one should not claim that the continuum dissolves it.

The mirror is author-recognizable because it preserves exactly their central question: how local mechanism guarantees compose when many agents participate in several mechanisms and have complement-free valuations across them. It is not a novelty collision: the paper studies finitely many named players, whereas this mirror studies a rational type distribution and its high-multiplicity realizations.

The weakest point is decisive. A literal atomless game changes unilateral deviations: one individual has zero influence on aggregate bids and allocations. Replacing that individual by an entire type mass creates a coalition deviation and is no longer Theorem 5.1’s game. The clone-limit formulation avoids that defect and is a faithful high-multiplicity mirror, but it is scientifically modest; the genuinely nonatomic version is an extension requiring new equilibrium semantics and new proofs.

So the positive conclusion is: there is a sensible, author-recognizable high-multiplicity mirror of Theorem 5.1, with a plausible Class-A certificate transfer. But under the programme’s strict anchor gate, the paper should be recorded as having no qualifying computational result, and this mirror should be labelled a qualified welfare-theoretic extension rather than a full ChoCo computational case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive point is that this paper has no qualifying computational anchor. “Efficient” in its title means welfare-efficient, not polynomial-time. The numbered results establish smoothness inequalities, equilibrium welfare bounds, valuation characterizations, and composability. None asserts membership in \(\mathrm{P}\), hardness, approximation complexity, or parameterized complexity. Theorem 5.1 is therefore not a computational result to continuize; Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 merely convert its inequality into welfare guarantees for correlated and Bayesian equilibria.

The proposed HM–Simultaneous-Composition Welfare Threshold does not repair that omission. It is a new equilibrium-limit problem, with a \(\liminf\) over clone counts and an infimum over equilibria, that the paper neither formulates nor analyses. A smoothness certificate gives the same inequality for every finite \(N\), so the continuous limit contributes no new computational object. Conversely, deciding the exact worst correlated-equilibrium welfare would require specifying finite or continuous action spaces, encodings for mechanisms and valuations, and a representation of equilibria—none of which exists in the paper. That may inspire a new anonymous-game project, but it is not a mirror of a named computational theorem.

There is also a substantive mismatch in the proposed population reduction. In the paper, smoothness is a statement about unilateral deviations by named players, with deviations \(a_i^\ast(v,a_i)\), individual payments \(P_i\), and correlated-equilibrium obedience constraints. In an atomless population, one agent has zero effect on aggregate bids, allocations, and prices. If deviations remain unilateral, the limit changes the strategic problem; if an entire type-mass may deviate, the deviation is a coalition deviation and the equilibrium concept is no longer the one used by Theorems 4.2, 4.3, or 5.1.

Keeping finite clones avoids that semantic problem, but then the continuous society is only shorthand for a sequence of finite games. Aggregating their strategies into type-level masses loses the arbitrary correlations that correlated equilibrium permits; retaining those correlations leaves an \(N\)-player object rather than a finite-dimensional population model. Either choice requires a new equilibrium theory, not a continuous version of the paper’s result.

The proposed advertiser or procurement scenario does establish that high multiplicity is plausible here; that is not the objection. The failure is narrower and fatal under ChoCo’s rules: no choice of type space can manufacture a computational-complexity theorem where the paper contains none. The honest caveat is that a worthwhile continuous anonymous-auction paper could certainly be written from this material, but it would be a new research programme, not a continuous mirror of this paper.
