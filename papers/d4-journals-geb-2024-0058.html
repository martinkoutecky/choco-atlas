The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive objection is that this paper has no computational result to mirror. Theorem 7 characterizes a restriction operator under axioms; Theorem 14 characterizes the Ewens partition and MPW solution under further axioms; Theorem 2, which the paper cites from earlier work, gives a formula for the TU-game potential. None poses a search or decision problem, analyzes complexity, or gives an exact, approximation, or parameterized algorithm. These are the strongest candidate anchors, and each is a mathematical characterization rather than a computational result.

The industry example does suggest a plausible high-multiplicity setting. One could model many interchangeable firms or workers as types and let coalition worth depend on how the remaining population forms coalitions. But the paper gives no computational question for that setting. A natural follow-on might ask for the limiting MPW payoff per type, or an algorithm to approximate it. The first is an asymptotic or analytic question; the second requires a new input model and representation of coalition worths. Neither mirrors a result proved here.

That gap is substantive. In the paper, worth is an arbitrary function of embedded coalitions \((S,\pi)\), and externalities make the partition of the outsiders relevant. A type-mass distribution alone does not capture that information. An anonymous, mass-based version might be sensible, but imposing it and deciding how to encode its worth function would define a new problem. With the paper’s explicit finite-game representation, computing its finite sums is proportional to the size of the input table; succinct representations could yield different complexity questions, but their answers would depend on choices the paper never makes.

So the strongest negative case is that this paper is outside the continuization programme’s computational remit: there is no computational finding for a continuous population model to test, and the best proposed mirror becomes a new research project once one supplies the missing model and task. That is a strong case against a *mirror of this paper*. It is not a proof that no worthwhile computational study could grow from its ideas; that broader impossibility claim is the weak point, and would overstate what the paper lets us conclude.
