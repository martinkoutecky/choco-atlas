The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set of complete buyer types \(T=[\tau]\), rational population masses \(\mu_t\), valuations \(v_t\), per-capita budgets \(b_t\), a finite item space \(\Theta=[M]\), and an unknown i.i.d. item distribution \(s\), with aggregate budgets \(B_t=\mu_t b_t\), compute an \(O(\tau)\)-state online policy that assigns each arriving item, sets its price, updates type-level pacing multipliers, and achieves \(\mathbb{E}\lVert\beta^H-\beta^\star\rVert_2^2\le (6+\log H)G^2/(H\sigma^2)\) together with type-level hindsight-regret and envy errors of order \(O((\log H)^2/H)\).

The model it lives in

A mass-normalized online Fisher market with finite complete types, population mass \(\mu_t\), aggregate budget \(B_t=\mu_t b_t\), type-level multiplier \(\beta_t\), per-arrival assignment variables, prices, and within-type load balancing; its static target is the dual objective \(\sum_{\theta}s_\theta\max_t\{\beta_t v_t(\theta)\}-\sum_t B_t\log\beta_t\).

The objection that survived

Pooled pacing may guarantee only type-average regret and envy, while preserving the paper's individual guarantees requires additional load-balancing and arrival-scaling arguments not supplied by the paper.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorems 3 and 6 through type-level multiplier tracking, hindsight regret, envy, and asymptotic fair allocation. It leaves the quasilinear results in Theorems 7 and 8, the experiments, and the paper's structural theorem unmirrored as separate anchors.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the convergence bound be expressed in a mass-weighted norm without dependence on \(\min_t B_t\)?
  • Can pooled PACE be load-balanced to preserve individual rather than only type-average regret and envy?
  • What arrival-rate and horizon scaling gives a faithful finite-\(N\) interpretation of the normalized continuum model?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a Class A, high-multiplicity mirror of PACE. The paper has no hardness theorem to anchor on, so I would not manufacture one. Its best named computational anchors are Theorems 3 and 6, both proved in the paper: Theorem 3 gives convergence of PACE’s pacing multipliers, while Theorem 6 gives convergence to online market equilibrium, including regret and envy guarantees.

The mirror should continuize the buyers, not the items. In fact, take a finite item space \(\Theta=[M]\), so that the only continuous object is the population. Let \(T=[\tau]\) be a moderate set of complete buyer types. Type \(t\) contains its valuation vector \(v_t\in\mathbb Q_+^M\), budget \(b_t>0\), eligibility conditions, and any tie-breaking class. The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\in\mathbb Q^\tau\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Thus \(\mu_t\) is the mass of buyers having exactly the same valuation, budget, and other relevant characteristics.

A convincing regime is a large platform allocating arriving compute slots, advertising impressions, or service opportunities to millions of standing requestors. Requestors can be grouped into a few workload, geographic, or budget classes with identical valuation vectors and budgets. Here \(N\) may be in the millions while \(\tau\) is in the tens or hundreds. This is genuinely a high-multiplicity regime: clearing denominators in \(\mu\) gives \(N_t=N\mu_t\) identical buyers of type \(t\).

Write \(B_t=\mu_t b_t\) for the type’s aggregate budget. If \(X_{t\theta}\) is the aggregate amount of item \(\theta\) assigned to type \(t\), then the type’s aggregate utility is \(U_t=\sum_\theta v_t(\theta)X_{t\theta}\), and its per-capita utility is \(U_t/\mu_t\). The static equilibrium target is therefore the type-aggregated Eisenberg–Gale problem \(\max_X\sum_t B_t\log(U_t/\mu_t)\), subject to \(\sum_tX_{t\theta}\le s_\theta\). At the microscopic level, every item can remain indivisible; fractional quantities arise only as averages across the many identical members of a type and across time.

The corresponding dual is the type-level version of the paper’s equation (3): minimize \(F(\beta)=\sum_\theta s_\theta\max_{t\in T}\{\beta_tv_t(\theta)\}-\sum_tB_t\log\beta_t\), over the same clipped interval for \(\beta\). Given an arriving item \(\theta_r\), type-level PACE chooses \(w_r\in\arg\max_t\beta_t^rv_t(\theta_r)\), assigns the item to that type, charges \(p_r=\beta_{w_r}^rv_{w_r}(\theta_r)\), records \(g_{r,t}=v_t(\theta_r)\mathbf 1\{w_r=t\}\), and updates \(\beta_t\) from the running average of \(g_{r,t}\). The winning items can then be distributed among the \(\mu_t\)-mass of identical buyers.

My lead problem is:

Continuous-Type PACE Price Tracking. Given rational \(\mu\), type data \((v_t,b_t)\), an item-arrival sampling oracle for an unknown i.i.d. distribution \(s\), and a horizon \(H\), compute an online allocation-and-pricing policy using only the current item and \(O(\tau)\) type-level state. The output is the pacing vector \(\beta^H\), item prices, and type allocations. A solution must achieve a prescribed bound on the distance between \(\beta^H\) and the equilibrium multiplier \(\beta^\star\).

The anchor is Theorem 3, proved in this paper, although its proof invokes the general dual-averaging result cited as Theorem 2 from [48]. In the paper’s notation, under i.i.d. arrivals, bounded valuations, and the normalizations \(\|B\|_1=1\), \(s(\Theta)=1\), and \(\langle v_i,s\rangle=1\), PACE satisfies \(\mathbb E\|\beta^t-\beta^\star\|_2^2\le (6+\log t)G^2/(t\sigma^2)\), where \(G^2=\max_i\mathbb E_{\theta\sim s}[v_i(\theta)^2]\le\max_i\|v_i\|_\infty^2\) and \(\sigma=\min_iB_i/(1+\delta_0)^2\). In the mirror, the index \(i\) is replaced by type \(t\), and \(B_i\) by \(B_t=\mu_tb_t\).

I expect this mirror to be Class A. The dual has only \(\tau\) variables, each arrival requires \(O(\tau)\) bids and one update, and the static target is a finite-dimensional convex program. The important point is that the population masses are not merely decorative coefficients: they determine aggregate budgets, per-capita utilities, the equilibrium multipliers, and how much item mass must be allocated to each type. Clearing denominators gives the finite cloned market, while the type-level algorithm avoids maintaining \(N\) separate buyer states.

The second problem is:

Continuous-Type Online Market Equilibrium and Fair Allocation. On the same input, compute an online allocation-price policy whose type-level allocations satisfy supply feasibility and market clearance, and whose hindsight utility and envy errors fall below a requested tolerance after \(H\) arrivals. For type \(t\), let \(\widehat U_t^H\) be its hindsight-optimal utility under the realized prices and aggregate budget \(B_t\), let \(\bar u_t^H\) be its realized average utility, and let \(r_t^H=\max\{\widehat U_t^H-\bar u_t^H,0\}\). Define \(\bar u_{tq}^H\) as type \(t\)’s valuation for type \(q\)’s allocation and \(\rho_t^H=\max_{q\ne t}\bar u_{tq}^H/B_q-\bar u_t^H/B_t\).

The anchor is Theorem 6, proved here. It defines \(\xi_i^t=|\bar u_i^t-u_i^\star|\), \(\Delta_i^t=|\bar b_i^t-B_i|\), and \(\gamma^t=\|v\|_\infty t^{-1}\sum_{\tau=1}^t\|\beta^\tau-\beta^\star\|_\infty\), and proves \(r_i^t\le\xi_i^t+\gamma^t/B_i\) and \(\mathbb E(r_i^t)^2=O((\log t)^2/t)\). For envy, with \(\eta_i^t=t^{-1}\sum_{\tau=1}^t(p^\star(\theta^\tau)-\beta_i^\tau v_i(\theta^\tau))x_i^\tau\), it proves \(\rho_i^t\le B_i^{-1}\bigl(\xi_i^t+\max_{k\ne i}(\Delta_k^t+\eta_k^t)/B_k\bigr)\), together with \(\mathbb E(\eta_i^t)^2\le \|v\|_\infty^2G^2\bigl(6(1+\log t)+(\log t)^2/2\bigr)/(t\sigma^2)\), and hence \(\mathbb E(\rho_i^t)^2=O((\log t)^2/t)\). The paper concludes that PACE asymptotically attains online market equilibrium, no regret, envy-freeness, Pareto optimality, and proportional share.

This is also expected Class A. The paper’s exact OME definitions survive after replacing individual buyers by type-average buyers, while microscopic items remain integral. Further questions include finite-horizon sample complexity in \((\tau,M,\varepsilon)\), rounding type-average allocations to individual buyers, individual rather than type-average envy, non-i.i.d. arrivals, strategic valuation reports, and the quasilinear analogue based on Theorems 7 and 8.

The authors should recognize this as their problem: it is the same online Fisher market, the same dual, the same pacing rule, and the same hindsight equilibrium notion, with duplicate buyer rows quotiented into high-multiplicity types. The paper’s continuous item space is not being mistaken for population continuity; it can be kept finite here. Theorem 1 supplies additional support because it proves the EG/dual formulation and its equilibrium KKT characterization, but I would not use it as a third anchor.

The weakest point is that this is a strong high-multiplicity compression, but not a dramatic new continuum phenomenon. After aggregation, population mass enters largely through \(B_t\), and the type-level proof is likely a symmetry reduction of the paper’s existing argument rather than a new exponential-column LP or a continuum-specific hardness boundary. Moreover, one must prove that pooled pacing and within-type load balancing preserve the desired type-level guarantees; Theorems 3 and 6 do not state that extension explicitly. That makes this a modest but honest positive case: a natural, author-recognizable continuous-population version with a clear tractability prediction, rather than a claim that the paper already contains a new continuous-population theorem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the proposed mirror is either an exact symmetry reduction or a different, substantially harder model.

For Theorem 3, with a finite type set \(T\), the type-level dual is

\[ F(\beta)=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}s_\theta\max_{t\in T}\{\beta_t v_t(\theta)\} -\sum_{t\in T}B_t\log\beta_t, \qquad B_t=\mu_t b_t. \]

This is simply the paper’s dual restricted to the symmetric subspace in which all cloned buyers of type \(t\) share one multiplier. Expanding \(\mu_t\) into \(N\mu_t\) identical buyers recovers the finite market; quotienting those buyers is an immediate sufficient-statistic reduction. Thus the continuous population contributes no new separation problem, convex formulation, or complexity phenomenon. It is a useful implementation compression, but not obviously a worthwhile ChoCo problem.

Trying to make the population genuinely nonatomic does not repair this. The dual becomes function-valued, roughly

\[ F(\beta)= \int_\Theta \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{z} \{\beta(z)v_z(\theta)\}\,s(d\theta) -\int_Z \log\beta(z)\,B(dz), \]

so the state is no longer \(O(\tau)\), and each arrival requires optimization over a type space. Theorem 3’s finite-type proof and its positive-mass coordinate updates no longer apply directly. Moreover, its rate contains

\[ \sigma=\frac{\min_t B_t}{(1+\delta_0)^2}, \]

so the advertised bound deteriorates as the type masses become small. A \(K\)-bin approximation to a continuous type distribution can therefore have a convergence guarantee worsening like \(1/\min_t B_t^2\). One can seek a new functional-analytic or measure-weighted theory, but that is no longer the proposed finite-type mirror of Theorem 3.

Theorem 6 has a similar choice. If the guarantee is only for pooled type averages, it is a legitimate but weaker market notion: it permits the aggregate budget and aggregate allocation of a type to be redistributed across all its clones. That is not the paper’s original per-buyer hindsight-regret and envy guarantee. If the intended guarantee remains individual, then a single type multiplier is insufficient to describe the different cumulative utilities and expenditures of its members; an additional load-balancing argument is required. The paper proves neither implication. The error bounds also become non-uniform because replacing \(B_i\) by \(B_t=\mu_t b_t\) introduces factors \(1/\mu_t\) for rare types. Mass-weighted error metrics can avoid that explosion, but then they no longer guarantee fairness for every type.

There is also a scaling problem. With one indivisible item arriving per period, a population of \(N\) buyers receives only \(O(H)\) total allocation, so per-capita utility vanishes unless the horizon or item-arrival rate scales with \(N\). Scaling arrivals produces a fluid market in which stochastic fluctuations may disappear; not scaling them produces a degenerate per-capita limit. Either choice can motivate a new model, but neither is directly the online market studied in the paper.

This is not an objection that high multiplicity is nonsensical, nor that the answer might be unchanged. The finite-type construction is a coherent high-multiplicity relaxation, and existing high-multiplicity work supports it. The negative point is narrower: Theorem 3 is already essentially closed under exact type aggregation, while a genuinely continuous population requires a new infinite-dimensional model whose representation, oracle access, and fairness notion are unspecified. That makes the case against a worthwhile ChoCo mirror plausible, but not airtight. The proponent’s finite-\(T\), type-average version remains a modest and defensible positive anchor.
