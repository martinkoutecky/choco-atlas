Why no mirror

The ATBCR fit finding is empirical with no stated guarantee, so it cannot meet the computational-result gate; the proposed continuum calibration cannot supply that missing anchor.

What the mirror covers

The candidate mirror covers the Section 4.4 calibration objective and Section 5 ATBCR experiments; it leaves out a complexity claim, which the paper does not make, as well as the FJ and DW comparisons and the qualitative interpretation of fitted monthly parameters.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can a type-level kernel preserve the sparse scale-free network’s opinion-neighborhood correlations in a regime with fixed support size?
  • Can the resulting continuum calibration problem be optimized globally with an additive-\(\delta\) guarantee?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a narrow mirror of the paper’s ATBCR calibration problem. There are no numbered theorems, lemmas, corollaries, or propositions in the paper that classify a problem as tractable or hard, so there are no qualifying anchors to restate. The closest result is experimental: across the 36 calibrations, ATBCR had the lowest observed MAPE for each concern threshold, and the best run was DE with ATBCR at \(c_{\mathrm{th}}=0.90\), with MAPE \(6.89\times10^{-2}\). That is evidence about model fit, not a complexity result or a certificate of optimality.

A plausible continuous question would be Continuum ATBCR Calibration. Consider a large population whose members fall into a small number of survey-response and interaction-role types. Let \(\mu_z\) be the fraction of the population in type \(z\), and let a type-level interaction kernel specify how often agents in each pair of roles interact. The population is large enough that \(N\gg\tau\), where \(\tau\) is the number of represented types. Within each type, opinions evolve under the paper’s ATBCR update rule. To keep the interaction rate meaningful as population size grows, the number of pairwise interactions must scale with \(N\).

An instance gives the type masses, initial opinion distributions, interaction kernel, monthly observed concern proportions \(h(p)\), concern threshold \(c_{\mathrm{th}}\), and number of interaction steps per period. The decision variable is the monthly schedule \(\theta(p)=(\mu(p),\varepsilon(p),\vartheta(p))\), subject to the paper’s parameter bounds. The question is to find a schedule minimizing the average MAPE between the observed proportions and the continuum model’s predicted mass with opinion at least \(c_{\mathrm{th}}\). A solution would be a rational parameter schedule with a certificate that its objective is within a requested additive \(\delta\) of the global minimum.

The scenario is a national survey or large online community: many respondents share a small set of answer profiles and broad interaction roles, while individual identities are irrelevant to the calibration. The paper’s authors could recognize the central question—the same monthly ATBCR parameters are being fitted to the same aggregate concern trajectory—but this mirror changes the fixed Barabási–Albert network into a type-level interaction model and replaces finite Monte Carlo runs with population-level dynamics.

For this proposed question, I would tentatively expect continuum-specific hardness for exact global calibration: the trajectory depends nonlinearly on the monthly parameters, and the thresholded concern objective need not be convex. That is a conjecture about the proposed problem, not a conclusion supported by the paper. Approximation may be easier under suitable stability conditions, but threshold crossings make those conditions consequential.

The mirror covers the calibration in Sections 4.4–5, focused on ATBCR; it does not mirror a complexity theorem, because the paper supplies none. Its weakest point is the altered interaction structure. If bounded-confidence and repulsion dynamics depend on correlations in the sparse network that the type-level kernel cannot retain, this may not be the authors’ problem in a continuous form. The positive case survives only if a plausible large-population regime can preserve those interactions well enough for the authors to recognize the calibration question as theirs.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The ATBCR fit is the only proposed anchor, and it is an empirical finding, not a computational result of the kind this programme is meant to continuize. Section 5.2 reports that one heuristic search, under one survey, initialization scheme, network, and set of parameter bounds, found a lower MAPE for ATBCR than for FJ or DW. It gives no complexity classification or guarantee about the calibration optimum. The optimization formulation in Section 4.4 is a computational task, but turning it into a continuum task would create a new algorithmic question; it would not mirror a computational result established by this paper.

The strongest version of that new question would keep the monthly ATBCR parameters and survey targets, represent opinions by a measure, and preserve the paper’s scale-free interaction structure through a sparse-network limit. The obstacle is that the update depends on which agents meet and how their opinions compare. A distribution of opinion and survey-response types, even with a type-level interaction kernel, generally loses correlations between opinions and network neighborhoods, including the influence of hubs. A faithful type description would need to retain increasingly detailed local network structure as the population grows, so its support need not remain small relative to the population. That undermines the high-multiplicity compression the proposed mirror is meant to gain.

A well-mixed or finite-role interaction model could yield a coherent continuum ATBCR calibration problem. But it changes the paper’s chosen network and becomes a problem in opinion-dynamics model calibration: there is no election, collective choice rule, winner, or intervention in the choice process. The paper’s authors might recognize it as a follow-on OD study, but it does not supply a continuous computational-social-choice problem for ChoCo. Adding a winner or strategic intervention would put such a problem in scope, while changing the question the paper actually studies.

This negative case is strongest on programme fit and the loss of network structure; it is not a proof that continuum ATBCR calibration is uninteresting. A measure-valued calibration problem could be worth studying in its own right. I cannot honestly rule out every plausible continuous OD scenario, but the proponent’s scenario does not establish a worthwhile mirror of this paper’s computational results, because the paper has no such result to mirror.
