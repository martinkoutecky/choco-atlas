The continuous mirror question

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), a proposed committee \(W\) with \(|W|=k\), and finite approval-type support \(\mathcal T\subseteq 2^C\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_A\) summing to \(1\), decide whether, for every \(\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}\) and every coalition submass vector \(0\le\lambda_A\le\mu_A\) with \(\sum_A\lambda_A\ge\ell/k\), the condition \(\left|\bigcap_{\lambda_A>0}A\right|\ge\ell\) implies \(\left|W\cap\bigcup_{\lambda_A>0}A\right|\ge\ell\).

The model it lives in

Approval sets \(A\) are types with population mass \(\mu_A\); variables \(\lambda_A\) select coalition submass. The task is a yes/no verification, equivalently checking active type sets for a violation.

What the mirror covers

Covers generalized Hare-PSC verification on dichotomous approval types, equivalently PJR testing; leaves the broader weak-order taxonomy and computation of EAR outcomes aside. The coNP-completeness answer is already given by Corollary 2 and the discrete-to-mass correspondence.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the analogous verification problem over unrestricted weak-order types admit useful algorithms or hardness classifications beyond the dichotomous case?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead mirror is continuous PJR / generalized Hare-PSC verification. I think the authors would recognize it: their paper studies whether a given committee provides proportional representation, and on dichotomous preferences it explicitly connects generalized Hare-PSC to PJR.

The regime is a large membership election—for example, a union or party choosing a representative committee—where stable slates or endorsements lead many members to submit the same approval ballot. A type is an approval set \(A\subseteq C\); its mass \(\mu_A\) is the fraction of members with that ballot. It is plausible for the number of members \(n\) to greatly exceed the number \(\tau\) of distinct ballot types. For this verification problem, the approval set fully describes a member’s preferences.

The anchor is Corollary 2: testing generalized PSC and generalized weak PSC is coNP-complete even under dichotomous preferences. The corollary appears in the paper, but its hardness relies on coNP-completeness of PJR testing established in cited prior work; the paper supplies the equivalence bridge. In particular, Corollary 1 establishes that, under dichotomous preferences, PJR, weak generalized Hare-PSC, and generalized Hare-PSC are equivalent. I use the generalized Hare-PSC claim as the lead. [Aziz and Lee, Corollaries 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.07580)

Precisely, define Continuous Generalized Hare-PSC Verification as follows. An instance consists of candidates \(C\), a committee size \(k\), a proposed committee \(W\subseteq C\) with \(|W|=k\), and a finite support of approval types \(\mathcal T\subseteq 2^C\), with positive rational masses \(\mu_A\) summing to \(1\). For each \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), a coalition may take mass \(\lambda_A\in[0,\mu_A]\) from each type \(A\). Its total mass is \(\sum_A\lambda_A\). The question is whether every coalition of mass at least \(\ell/k\) whose members share at least \(\ell\) commonly approved candidates has at least \(\ell\) committee members in the union of its approvals.

Equivalently, answer YES exactly when, for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and every \(S\subseteq\mathcal T\),
\[ \sum_{A\in S}\mu_A\ge \ell/k \quad\text{and}\quad \left|\bigcap_{A\in S}A\right|\ge\ell \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left|\left(\bigcup_{A\in S}A\right)\cap W\right|\ge\ell. \]
Here \(W\) is part of the input; the task is a yes/no test, with \(\ell\) and a violating set \(S\) serving as a certificate for NO. Allowing fractional mass within a type is the continuous coalition model. Since the conditions depend on which types have positive coalition mass, a violating coalition can be represented by the set of those types.

I expect hardness to transfer. A finite approval election with \(n_A\) voters of type \(A\) maps to \(\mu_A=n_A/n\). The discrete quota condition \(|N'|\ge \ell n/k\) becomes mass at least \(\ell/k\), while the common-approval and represented-union conditions are preserved. Conversely, a continuous violating set of types yields a discrete violating coalition by taking the voters of those types. Thus the question inherits the known coNP-hardness, and has a short certificate for failure. This is a Class B mirror: it identifies a faithful continuous-population version and predicts that the discrete hardness survives. Its natural complexity parameters are \(m\), \(\tau\), and the mass bit length \(L\).

The mirror covers the paper’s generalized Hare-PSC testing result on its dichotomous domain, not the full taxonomy of weak-order axioms or the computation of EAR outcomes. It also generates useful follow-ups: whether verification becomes tractable or FPT for bounded \(\tau\), how the result changes under restrictions on approval types, and whether the hardness extends cleanly to richer weak-order types.

The weak point is that this mirror mostly expresses the paper’s existing coalition test in population proportions; it offers little prospect of making the problem easier. But that is a valuable boundary result for this programme. The population distribution is the central object, the authors’ quota and representation condition carry over directly, and the hardness transfer is itself the answer to the continuization question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against this mirror is that its continuous version adds almost no new coalition structure. For a proposed coalition, let \(S\) be the approval types it contains with positive mass. The common approvals and the committee members represented in the coalition depend only on \(S\). A coalition of mass at least \(\ell/k\) using those types exists exactly when \(\sum_{A\in S}\mu_A\ge \ell/k\). Fractional selection within a type creates no further condition.

For a discrete profile with \(n_A\) voters of each type, setting \(\mu_A=n_A/n\) gives the same test: \(\sum_{A\in S} n_A/n\ge\ell/k\) exactly when the coalition’s available voters meet the discrete quota. Conversely, any rational mass profile can be represented by a discrete profile after scaling by a common denominator. The mirror is therefore an exact change of representation for PJR verification. A continuous generalized-PSC mirror over weak-order types would retain this feature: witnesses are still sets of active types, and the quota is still a mass threshold.

That makes this a limited fit for the programme’s continuous-optimization motivation. The proposed verification problem has no apparent mass-allocation optimization to solve; its computational content remains a combinatorial search over type sets. A skeptical reader could reasonably ask how much research value lies in restating this scale-free test with binary rational masses.

That objection does not defeat the anchor under ChoCo’s stated aims. The model still treats society as a distribution, and binary masses give a high-multiplicity input whose complexity can be studied in \(m,\tau,L\). The paper has a named coNP-completeness result to mirror, and large elections with repeated approval ballots are a plausible regime. The exact equivalence with discrete profiles is also relevant to the programme’s requested dictionary. Existing high-multiplicity election work supports that regime.

So the negative case is weak. I can argue that this mirror may yield little new mathematical structure, but I cannot honestly rule it out as worthwhile. The proponent’s anchor survives.
