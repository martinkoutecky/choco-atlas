Every anchor argued

  • Theorem 5 — survived: Its minmax case carries over to a society of formula-types whose shared assignment minimizes the worst type’s satisfaction degree.

The continuous mirror question

Given a finite variable set \(X\), a finite support \(S\) of QCL preference types represented by formulas over \(X\), positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\psi\) summing to \(1\), and a classical query \(\chi\), consider shared assignments \(I\subseteq X\) with finite satisfaction degree for every \(\psi\in S\). Define \(D_\mu(I)=\max_{\psi\in S}\deg(I,\psi)\). Decide whether \(\chi\) holds in every admissible assignment minimizing \(D_\mu\), taking the universal condition as true if there is no admissible assignment.

The model it lives in

A society is a distribution \(\mu\) over QCL preference types, each type fully described by its satisfaction-degree function over assignments to \(X\). The population chooses one shared assignment \(I\), admissibility requires finite degree for every type in the support, and the objective minimizes the worst supported type’s degree. The mass values do not affect this worst-type objective; they identify the support.

The objection that survived

The paper treats formulas as jointly assessed knowledge-base constraints and preferences, while the mirror interprets each formula as a constituency’s preference; QCL’s preference language supports that reading, but the paper does not develop the collective interpretation.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the minmax branch of Theorem 5, leaving the inclusion-based population interpretation, the formula-level translation details, and lexicographic semantics aside. The paper reports that prior work establishes finite minmax entailment as \(\Theta_2^P\)-complete, which appears to answer this support-based mirror’s complexity question; that outside result does not erase the mirror.

Open questions for a prover

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest mirror I see is a worst-type collective choice version of minmax QCL entailment. Picture a large population choosing one shared plan—say, a public service design—with each preference type represented by a QCL formula. A formula gives its holders a satisfaction degree for each possible plan, including the ordered fallback preferences that make QCL useful for soft constraints. A plausible regime is a very large population with relatively few distinct preference types.

My lead anchor is Theorem 5, proved in this paper. It says that for a QCL theory \(T\), a classical formula \(\varphi\), and either \(\pi\in\{\mathrm{mm},\mathrm{inc}\}\), \(T\models_\pi\varphi\) holds exactly when the argument for \(\varphi\) at degree \(1\) belongs to every nonempty semi-stable extension of the SETAF formed by combining the framework for \(T\) with the framework for \(\varphi\). The paper uses this correspondence to compute preferred-model entailment with SETAF solvers. This is a formulation and correctness result, rather than a theorem classifying the problem’s complexity; it qualifies as an anchor because the paper uses the formulation to decide the underlying problem.

Here is the continuous problem I would hand to a prover: Mass-Minmax QCL Entailment. An instance consists of a finite variable set \(X\), a finite support \(S\) of QCL preference types, positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_\psi\) for \(\psi\in S\) summing to \(1\), and a classical query \(\chi\). A type is completely described, for this problem, by its QCL satisfaction-degree function over assignments to \(X\); agents with the same function are grouped into one type. A decision is one assignment \(I\) to \(X\), shared by the population. It is admissible if every positive-mass type has finite degree on \(I\). Its worst-type degree is
\[ D_\mu(I)=\max_{\psi\in S}\deg(I,\psi). \]
The task is to decide whether \(\chi\) holds in every admissible assignment minimizing \(D_\mu\). If there are no admissible assignments, the universal condition is vacuously true, as it is for entailment with no preferred models.

For every such instance, the positive-mass support is a QCL theory, and minimizing \(D_\mu\) is exactly its minmax preferred-model criterion. Thus Theorem 5’s \(\pi=\mathrm{mm}\) case applies directly to the underlying question; the continuous instance can also be translated to the paper’s SETAF construction. The authors have a plausible reason to recognize this mirror: QCL is expressly a language for jointly representing hard constraints and preferences, and the mirror asks which statements hold across all jointly chosen plans that minimize the worst preference degree. It covers the paper’s minmax preferred-model entailment result. It does not claim to continuize the SETAF itself.

I expect hardness to transfer (Class B) for this mirror: a finite QCL theory embeds by assigning positive mass to each formula, for example uniformly, and the answer is unchanged. Section 5 reports minmax entailment as \(\Theta_2^P\)-complete, citing Bernreiter, Maly, and Woltran (2022); I treat that as the paper’s reported context, not as a second anchor, since it is not stated as a numbered result here. A natural next question is whether the inclusion-based case admits an equally meaningful population interpretation, and whether a mass-sensitive preference criterion can be defined while retaining a recognisable connection to QCL.

The weakest point is substantial: under this minmax criterion, the values of positive masses do not matter—only whether a type has positive mass. So this is a credible population story for worst-case fairness, where even a small group’s concern counts, but the continuous arithmetic itself adds little to the computation. The mirror’s strongest claim is that the high-multiplicity regime is sensible for collective choice among a small number of constraint types; it is not that the paper’s mass distribution creates a new algorithmic advantage.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against this anchor is that the paper proves a compilation result, not a complexity result. Theorem 5 equates preferred-model entailment with a SETAF query; Propositions 3 and 4 establish the corresponding model-to-extension maps. None classifies the complexity of a problem. Section 5 reports complexity results from earlier work, but those are not results proved here.

More fundamentally, the proposed Mass-Minmax problem uses only the support of \(\mu\). Its objective is
\[ D_\mu(I)=\max_{\psi:\,\mu_\psi>0}\deg(I,\psi), \]
so changing any positive mass leaves the answer unchanged. The continuous input reduces exactly to the finite theory consisting of its positive-mass types. That exposes the modeling gap: QCL formulas in the paper are jointly assessed constraints and preferences in a knowledge base, not voter groups. Calling each formula a constituency preference changes its role and adds a social aggregation interpretation that the paper does not supply.

The best version of the mirror would make that interpretation explicit: each type is a complete QCL preference over shared plans, and the society chooses a plan minimizing the worst type’s satisfaction degree. That is a plausible robust-fairness problem, and a very small group having equal standing could be a deliberate feature. I cannot rule out the authors recognizing it. But its population is still represented only by which types are present; the mass distribution itself does no work.

Making mass matter—for example, by minimizing the average degree or comparing the mass at each degree—would address that weakness, but would require a new social preference criterion. The paper gives no basis for choosing one: satisfaction degrees encode ordered fallback levels, and weighting or aggregating them across agents adds semantics beyond its minmax and inclusion criteria. Such a problem might be worthwhile, but it would no longer be a mirror of Theorem 5.

So the negative case is real but not airtight. It defeats the claim that Theorem 5 already yields a substantive continuous-population question. It does not defeat the robust-fairness scenario itself; that is the point where this case is weakest.
