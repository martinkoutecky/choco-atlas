The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no eligible anchor here. The paper states no complexity result for a computational problem. Its equations derive distributions for delegate competency and rates for replacement and dissolution cascades; they do not analyze the running time or approximability of an algorithm. The cited voting theorems are background, not results established by this paper.

The strongest candidate for a mirror is the competency calculation: given \(g\) and \(f\), the paper obtains \(h(x)=f(x)g(x)/k\), then composes selection effects across tiers. But this is already an analytic population model, and the paper poses no computational task around it. Asking how to optimize delegate selection over a distribution of citizen types would require adding an objective, feasible choices, and an input representation. That could be a worthwhile new project, but its computational question would come from those additions, not from a result in this paper.

The cascade equations offer a similar temptation. One could ask how to choose cooling periods or initiation delays to limit instability. Yet the paper gives qualitative tuning advice, not a specified optimization problem: it supplies no cost for those policies, target stability level, or formal design constraints. The cascade model also depends on which lower-tier nodes feed into which higher-tier nodes. Aggregating citizens into type masses does not by itself represent that evolving network. Formalizing and optimizing it would again create a new problem.

The paper’s continuous treatment is evidence that population distributions can be natural in this setting, but it does not supply the computational result a mirror programme needs. My negative case is strong for a mirror *of this paper’s results*. It cannot establish that no adjacent computational problem would be worthwhile; it establishes that such a problem would have to be developed independently rather than presented as a continuous mirror of this paper.
