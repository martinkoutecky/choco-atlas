The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a population version of the paper’s distance-based fuzzy judgment aggregation. Imagine a large recurring consultation where people choose from a standardized set of fuzzy response templates over a small agenda. A type is one person’s complete vector of trapezoidal truth values for all propositions; its mass is the share of respondents using that vector. Millions of respondents could then be represented by a much smaller support of types. The collective interpretation \(w\) remains the decision variable, subject to the same fuzzy integrity constraints. This makes the population continuous; the paper’s use of fuzzy truth values alone would not.

A concrete candidate is Continuous Fuzzy Judgment Aggregation. Given an agenda \(P\), the paper’s doctrinal-paradox integrity constraint, a population size \(N\), and types \(K^1,\ldots,K^\tau\) with rational shares \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), find a feasible \(w\) minimizing \(N\sum_t\mu_t d^*(w,K^t)+d^*(w,\bar K)\), where \(\bar K=\sum_t\mu_tK^t\) is the componentwise average and \(d^*(w,K)=\frac14\sum_{p\in P}\sum_{r=1}^4|w(p)_r-K(p)_r|\). Feasibility includes valid trapezoidal endpoints, the paper’s min/max bounds, and its displayed fuzzy implication condition for the chosen agenda. The solution is an exact minimizing collective interpretation and its objective value. Including \(N\) preserves the paper’s sum-of-individual-disutilities objective alongside its separate penalty for distance from the average.

For this restricted version, I would expect tractability: the distance objective is piecewise linear, and the stated endpoint constraints can be expressed as linear or piecewise-linear inequalities, giving an LP formulation. The resulting question is whether the continuous optimum can be computed in time polynomial in \(|P|\), \(\tau\), and the input bit length. Natural follow-ups are the complexity for general integrity constraints, how ties should be resolved, and how the optimum relates to the finite profile. The main modeling weakness is that the population story relies on standardized templates, and the paper’s fixed-weight average penalty makes the optimum sensitive to electorate size. Both should be explicit in any mirror.

There is, however, a decisive limitation under the requested anchor rule: the paper contains no qualifying named computational result. Its numbered items include definitions and worked examples—especially Examples 2.8 and 3.1—but neither asserts a complexity classification or gives a general algorithm. The optimization formulation is unnumbered, and the examples report solutions for small instances. So Continuous Fuzzy Judgment Aggregation is the best concrete mirror of the paper’s central optimization model, but it cannot honestly be presented as a problem anchored to a named theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition from this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative is that there is no qualifying result here to mirror. The paper states no theorem, lemma, or corollary about the complexity of a computational problem. Examples 2.8 and 3.1 give solutions for small instances; the distance minimization is a modeling proposal, not a named complexity result. So Continuous Fuzzy Judgment Aggregation is a possible new problem, but it is not anchored to a result of this paper as the proponent was asked to do. Generalizing it to arbitrary agendas and integrity constraints would add a computational problem the paper never analyzes.

That does not establish that the continuous question is unworthwhile. A large consultation using a small set of standardized fuzzy response templates is a plausible high-multiplicity setting. Types can be complete preference vectors; the objective and constraints need not depend on individual identity, and the population limit need not degenerate. The proposed weighted optimization therefore survives the substantive modeling objections I can test. I cannot honestly argue that no scenario yields a worthwhile continuous mirror. The case against succeeds only on the narrower, stated requirement of a named computational result to anchor it.
