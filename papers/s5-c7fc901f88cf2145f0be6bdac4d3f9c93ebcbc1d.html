The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), population masses \(\mu_t\), bounds \(0\le\alpha<\beta\), and a super-additive coalition-worth function \(V(x)\) on \(0\le x\le\mu\), encoded by a listed rational polyhedral partition with affine pieces and vertices, find parameters \(\sigma\) and \(a_t\) such that \(W(x)=\sigma V(x)+\sum_{t\in T}a_t x_t\) satisfies \(\alpha\sum_t x_t\le W(x)\le\beta\sum_t x_t\) for every coalition mass vector \(x\); report infeasibility otherwise.

The model it lives in

A cooperative production game with finitely many agent types and population masses \(\mu_t\). Coalitions are mass vectors \(x\in[0,\mu]\), and their worth \(V(x)\) is given by rational affine pieces on an explicit polyhedral partition. The decision variables are a scale \(\sigma\) and type-specific per-capita terms \(a_t\); the objective is to satisfy the per-capita bounds for every coalition, checkable at the listed vertices.

The objection that survived

The paper gives no computational representation for a continuous coalition-worth function \(V\), so complexity claims depend on specifying how \(V\) is encoded.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror extends Theorem 1’s construction of normalization parameters. It leaves Lemma 1’s dividend identity and the Rank-Shapley value relations and examples unaddressed.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive intuition is a high-multiplicity cooperative production setting: many interchangeable agents, grouped into finitely many types with masses \(\mu_t\). A coalition is described by its type-mass vector \(x\), its worth by a super-additive function \(V(x)\), and a natural analogue of the paper’s transformation is \(W(x)=\sigma V(x)+\sum_t a_t x_t\). The parameters would rescale coalition worth and add type-specific per-capita terms, much as the paper adds stand-alone terms for individual players.

But I cannot make this a qualifying anchor-based case under your rules. Theorem 1, proved in this paper, establishes that an essential finite TU game has an \((\alpha,\beta)\)-normalized strategically equivalent game and gives formulas for the transformation parameters. Lemma 1 gives a Harsanyi-dividend identity. Neither states a computational complexity result or computes with a formulation of one. So the paper has no named computational result to anchor a continuous problem to, and there is no honest per-anchor question or trichotomy prediction to provide.

The weakness in the possible extension is also substantive: the paper bounds every coalition’s *total* worth in \([\alpha,\beta]\), whereas an atomless population has arbitrarily small coalitions. The direct mass-based transform above tends to zero with coalition mass, so preserving the paper’s fixed lower bound would fail. Replacing it with per-capita bounds might make a sensible new question, but it changes what the paper proves. That would be a research proposal inspired by Theorem 1, not a continuous mirror of a named computational result.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive problem is that this paper gives ChoCo no computational result to continuize. Theorem 1 derives normalization parameters in closed form; Lemma 1 relates Harsanyi dividends; the Rank-Shapley section gives value identities. None defines a computational problem and establishes its complexity or an algorithm. The paper’s claim that normalization can simplify computation does not supply one. There is therefore no result here to anchor a continuous complexity question to.

The proposed high-multiplicity production setting is plausible as a new model: let coalition worth be \(V(x)\) for a vector of type masses \(x\), and consider \(W(x)=\sigma V(x)+\int a(t)\,dx\). But making that into a computational project requires choosing how \(V\) is represented and what must be computed. The paper’s formulas use each player’s stand-alone worth and the grand coalition’s surplus. In an atomless model, singleton coalitions have zero mass; replacing their worths with per-capita values or density bounds may be sensible, but it changes the question. Any resulting complexity would depend on those new modeling and input choices, not on a computational result in this paper.

So the strongest case against is that no scenario rescues this as a *ChoCo mirror*: the best proposed extension is a potentially worthwhile project in continuous cooperative-game theory, but it is not a continuous version of a computational result the paper establishes. That does not prove no valuable continuous research could grow from the paper; it marks the limit of the negative case.
