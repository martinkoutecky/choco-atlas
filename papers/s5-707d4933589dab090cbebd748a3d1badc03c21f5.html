The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make an honest anchor-based case for a continuous mirror here: the paper contains no named computational result asserting complexity or an algorithmic classification.

The nearest named result is Proposition 1, proved in this paper. It says that, when the two agents have identical secure land, human capital, and reference-point payoffs, the symmetric equilibrium under TA has more guns and lower payoffs than under GL, \(G_{\mathrm{TA}}>G_{\mathrm{GL}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{TA}}<V_{\mathrm{GL}}\), if and only if
\[ \gamma_{\mathrm{GL}}(W^1_{G_1}-W^2_{G_1}) < \gamma_{\mathrm{TA}}(A^1_{G_1}-A^2_{G_1}) +(1-\gamma_{\mathrm{TA}})(U^1_{G_1}-U^2_{G_1}). \]
That is an equilibrium comparison in a two-agent bargaining game, not a result about computing a solution or the complexity of a problem.

One could imagine a continuum of bilateral negotiations, with mass distributed over types defined by the pairs’ endowments, technology, contest, and reference point. But that makes the negotiations the population and aggregates independent two-agent games; it does not continuize the population in this paper’s problem. A continuum of agents bargaining together would change the bilateral model and its solution concepts. Neither construction gives a strong, author-recognizable mirror anchored in a named computational result.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only plausible anchor is Proposition 1. It compares the symmetric equilibria under GL and TA through a condition on marginal effects; it makes no claim about computing an outcome, an algorithm, or complexity. The paper also gives no finite input representation for its smooth production and contest functions. So Proposition 1 has no computational result to continuize in ChoCo’s sense.

The strongest population version would treat each bilateral negotiation as a unit and define its type by its endowments, technology, contest, and reference point. That is a plausible high-multiplicity regime, so the objection is not that repeated negotiations cannot exist. But in this model each pair has its own contested resource and chooses its own gun investment. Different pairs do not interact. A distribution over pair types therefore aggregates independent games; with finite support, it adds weighted aggregation to the per-type analysis, while a genuinely continuous type space would need a finite encoding the paper never supplies. That does not produce a computational mirror of Proposition 1.

A continuum of people bargaining together is the stronger alternative, but it needs a new model: how the two-agent GL and TA comparisons extend to a continuum of utility coordinates, how a common resource or contest couples participants, and how strategic investments work at vanishing individual mass. Those choices might support worthwhile research, but they replace the paper’s bilateral game rather than continuize its population. They do not rescue the proposed anchor.

So the negative case is strong for a faithful, result-anchored mirror. The universal claim is less secure: a researcher could build a valuable continuum bargaining model inspired by this paper. The supplied paper, however, does not identify a computational question that such a model would mirror.
