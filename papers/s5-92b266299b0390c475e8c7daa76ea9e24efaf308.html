What the mirror covers

Open questions for a prover

The case FOR (proponent)

Strictly, this paper contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Proposition, or Corollary asserting a complexity or algorithmic result. Its strongest computational statement is unnumbered: Section 1 says that maximizing transplants with 2- and 3-way exchanges is NP-hard, indeed APX-hard, citing Abraham, Blum, and Sandholm [1] and Biró, Manlove, and Rizzi [10]. That claim is cited rather than proved here. Thus there is no formal theorem anchor under the stated rubric.

The strongest positive case is nevertheless a conditional one anchored in the paper’s numbered computational specification, Definition 1, which the authors implement through the IP sequence in Section 3. Definition 1 says that an exchange set is optimal when it lexicographically: (1) maximizes the number of effective 2-cycles; (2) subject to that, maximizes its size; (3) subject to that, minimizes the number of 3-cycles; (4) subject to that, maximizes the number of back-arcs in 3-cycles; and (5) subject to that, maximizes total cycle weight. An effective 2-cycle is either a 2-cycle or a 3-cycle containing a back-arc. This definition is not proved in the paper; it is the NHSBT policy specification.

I would call the mirror Lexicographic Kidney-Exchange Packing\(_\infty\).

The agents are donor–recipient pairs, together with altruistic donors represented by dummy recipients. A type \(t\) is a complete matching-relevant signature: donor and recipient blood and tissue characteristics, sensitization and clinical-priority classes, altruistic status, and every other feature determining compatibility, arc weight, eligibility, or policy treatment. Two pairs have the same type only when they are indistinguishable for the entire optimization problem.

A continuous society is a distribution \(\mu\) over a finite type set \(T\), where \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of the pool having type \(t\). The intended regime is a mature national or international registry with thousands or tens of thousands of pairs but comparatively few recurring compatibility and priority types, so many pairs share each type. This is not the current small UK matching run taken literally; it is the high-multiplicity version of the same centralized exchange problem.

Let \(\mathcal C\) contain every compatible directed cycle of length 2 or 3 in the type graph. For \(C\in\mathcal C\), let \(x_C\ge 0\) be the mass of type-level cycles of pattern \(C\). If \(a_{C,t}\) is the number of occurrences of type \(t\) in \(C\), feasibility requires

\[ \sum_{C\in\mathcal C} a_{C,t}x_C\le \mu_t \]

for every type \(t\). For an altruistic type \(a\), unused mass \(u_a\ge0\) may be sent directly to the deceased-donor waiting list, with

\[ u_a+\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}a_{C,a}x_C=\mu_a. \]

Thus \(x_C\) is not a fractional transplant for an individual: it is the mass of a large cohort assigned to that exchange pattern.

Define

\[ E(x)=\sum_C e_Cx_C, \quad S(x)=\sum_C |C|x_C+\sum_a u_a, \]

\[ Q(x)=\sum_{C:\,|C|=3}x_C, \quad B(x)=\sum_C b_Cx_C, \quad W(x)=\sum_C w_Cx_C, \]

where \(e_C=1\) for a 2-cycle or a 3-cycle with a back-arc and \(0\) otherwise; \(b_C\) is the number of back-arcs; and \(w_C\) is the sum of the paper’s arc weights. The problem is to output a feasible \(x,u\) lexicographically maximizing

\[ \bigl(E(x),\,S(x),\,-Q(x),\,B(x),\,W(x)\bigr). \]

This is the continuous form of the authors’ underlying exchange-selection problem, not a question about the IP encoding. It preserves their cycle lengths, compatibility graph, altruistic donors, clinical weights, back-arc fault tolerance, and all five policy priorities. Only the population unit has changed: many indistinguishable pairs can be divided among exchange patterns.

I expect this mirror to be Class A. With maximum cycle length three, there are at most \(O(|T|^3)\) type-cycle variables, and the five lexicographic stages are rational linear programs. Hence the continuous problem is solvable exactly in polynomial time in \(|T|\) and the input bit length. The paper’s discrete hardness comes from selecting indivisible cycles; in the high-multiplicity regime, mass from one type can participate in several cycle patterns, making that indivisibility disappear. The result would therefore be a meaningful continuization of the paper’s central problem, not merely a different objective-space relaxation.

The main further questions are whether continuous optima can be rounded to finite pools with controlled loss, how the answer changes when 4-cycles or NEAD chains are allowed, and whether failure probabilities, dynamic arrivals, hospital capacities, or fairness constraints preserve the LP structure.

The weakest point is the type regime. Real kidney-exchange compatibility and clinical scores can be highly individualised, making the number of complete types nearly as large as the number of pairs. In that case fractional cycle mass may look like an LP relaxation rather than a faithful mirror. The positive case survives only for a genuine cohort regime—large pools with repeated, policy-relevant compatibility signatures. The paper’s discussion of large markets, cycle formulations, and branch-and-price gives that regime some plausibility, but it does not establish it empirically or theoretically.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is that this paper supplies no numbered computational theorem to continuize. Its NP-hardness and APX-hardness statements are imported from earlier work, while its own contribution is an implementation and empirical case study. Definition 1 is a policy specification, not a proved complexity result. Thus the proposed mirror would primarily be a new high-multiplicity kidney-exchange model, rather than a continuization of one of this paper’s results.

The proposed mirror is nevertheless mathematically coherent, so the more substantive objection must concern whether it represents a worthwhile regime. Faithfully defining a type requires including every feature relevant to compatibility, eligibility, waiting priority, sensitization, age-dependent weights, hospital constraints, and possibly failure probabilities. In the paper, the compatibility graph and arc weights are fundamentally patient-specific. Nothing establishes that the number of complete matching-relevant types is substantially smaller than the number of donor–recipient pairs. If \(\tau\) is essentially the population size, \(\mu\) is just a normalized encoding of named vertices and \(x_C\) is a fractional relaxation of the original cycle-packing IP, not a genuine population model.

The obvious rescue is to use coarser recurring classes—blood group, HLA class, sensitization band, age band, and so forth. But then compatibility and clinical weights are no longer exact. One must introduce expected compatibility, stochastic arcs, or average weights. That may produce an interesting mean-field kidney-exchange problem, but it no longer mirrors Definition 1: effective 2-cycles, back-arcs, and the lexicographic policy are properties of a realized patient-level graph, not of expected mass flows. Including arrival dates, waiting time, hospital identity, or bridge-donor status as types restores fidelity only by making the type space increasingly individualized.

The computational content is also unusually thin. With cycles limited to lengths two and three, the type-level formulation has only \(O(\tau^3)\) possible cycle variables and reduces directly to five linear programs. That is not itself a defect—the programme allows useful Class A results—but here it means the proposed result is principally “fractional cycle packing is easier than integral cycle packing,” with no pricing or separation phenomenon of the kind motivating the programme. Extending the model to longer chains, failures, or dynamic arrivals would be a new research programme rather than a mirror of this paper’s actual result.

Still, the universal negative claim does not survive its best counterexample. A genuinely large registry could contain many pairs sharing a finite, complete matching signature. In that deliberately high-multiplicity regime, the proposed mass-cycle model is a legitimate continuous population problem, and normalized integer solutions may converge to it with controllable rounding loss. The paper’s national-market setting makes that scenario conceivable, even though the paper does not establish it.

So the best defensible negative verdict is narrow: this paper itself does not deliver a named computational result, and its patient-level data give no evidence that an exact, low-dimensional type regime exists. The proposed mirror therefore looks more like a new aggregate kidney-exchange model than a continuation of the paper. But I cannot honestly claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario; the high-multiplicity version of Definition 1 remains a valid and potentially useful Class A problem.
