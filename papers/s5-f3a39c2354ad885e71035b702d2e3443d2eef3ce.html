The continuous mirror question

Given a finite catalogue of local network motifs \(g\), each with single-minded users, integral channel bundles, values, and an explicit conflict graph, and a rational mass \(\mu_g\) of independent copies of each motif, let \(\mathcal A_g\) be the conflict-free integral allocations of one copy, including the empty allocation. Choose rational masses \(x_{g,A}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A_g}x_{g,A}=\mu_g\), maximizing \(\sum_g\sum_{A\in\mathcal A_g}x_{g,A}\,\mathrm{SW}_g(A)\). Determine the exact and approximation complexity in motif size, support, input bit length, and \(\kappa\), with each unit of motif mass receiving a whole-bundle integral allocation.

The model it lives in

A continuous mass of repeated, mutually noninterfering network motifs; each motif copy retains its finite user roles and local conflict graph, and the decision distributes mass over that motif's feasible integral allocations. Role masses are correlated through the motif, preserving the paper's spatial feasibility.

The objection that survived

Pairwise virtual-resource capacities permit fractional assignments that no conflict-free allocation can realize, and preserving feasibility requires compact local motif structure and configuration constraints that the proposed LP omits.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

Covers Theorem 2's social-welfare approximation for populations built from repeated local conflict motifs. It does not cover Theorem 1's strategy-proofness, AEGIS-MP's results, or the empirical evaluations.

Open questions for a prover

  • With motif allocations represented implicitly by conflict graphs and bundles, what are the exact and approximation complexities as functions of motif size, support, input bit length, and \(\kappa\)?
  • Does the greedy allocation's \(\kappa\)-approximation guarantee extend to arbitrary rational masses of motif copies?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case I believe is a mirror of the welfare allocation in Theorem 2, proved in this paper. It says that AEGIS-SG’s greedy allocation achieves a \(\kappa\)-approximation to maximum social welfare for unknown single-minded buyers, where \(\kappa=m\Delta_{\max}\) bounds the size of a virtual-channel bundle. The paper’s Theorem 1, also proved here, establishes strategy-proofness for that mechanism, so the welfare guarantee applies when buyers report truthfully. Theorem 2 is my only anchor: the paper says in Section 3.4 that the integer allocation problem is NP-hard, but that claim is not a numbered result, so I would not use it as the required hardness anchor.

Here is the continuous question I would hand to a prover: Fluid Heterogeneous Channel Allocation. There are \(m\) physical channels and a finite set \(H\) of virtual-channel resources, each with rational per-capita capacity \(b_h\). The society has \(\tau\) types. Type \(t\) has rational mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), a value \(v_t\), a single demanded physical bundle \(D_t\), and the set \(R_t\subseteq H\) of virtual resources that bundle uses. Each type is complete: its members share the same value, demand, and interference profile. Choose masses \(x_t\) to serve, maximizing

\[ \sum_{t} v_t x_t \quad\text{subject to}\quad 0\le x_t\le\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t:h\in R_t}x_t\le b_h\quad\text{for every }h\in H. \]

A solution is the rational vector \(x\): mass \(x_t\) receives the whole physical bundle \(D_t\), and the remaining mass is unserved. Thus channels and individual allocations remain integral; the fractional quantity is how much of each type is served. The capacities are part of the mirror: they describe the per-capita supply of repeated virtual-channel conflict opportunities as the deployment grows.

This is close to the paper’s underlying question. AEGIS-SG chooses which single-minded buyers receive their demanded bundles, and Section 3.4 expresses that allocation as weighted set packing over virtual channels. The mirror keeps that packing structure and the same welfare objective, while allowing a fraction of a repeated type to be served. With the bundle incidences listed explicitly, the continuous problem is a rational LP with \(\tau\) allocation variables and \(|H|\) resource constraints. I expect Class A: it is solvable exactly in polynomial time in \(m,\tau,|H|\), the input bit length \(L\), and the bundle-incidence size. For a discrete instance with integer counts \(n_t\) and resource capacities, scaling the LP solution and rounding each \(x_t\) down gives a feasible allocation; the lost welfare is at most \(\sum_t v_t\). So for fixed support, the normalized loss vanishes as the population grows.

A plausible regime is a large deployment of managed wireless access points across repeated venues or neighborhood layouts. Many devices may share a small menu of service contracts, channel bundles, and local interference roles; exact willingness-to-pay tiers and the repeated layout are part of the type. Then the number of buyers can be very large while \(\tau\) and the number of resource classes remain modest. Spatial reuse remains in the model through distinct, reusable conflict resources. The paper’s own virtual-channel and set-packing formulation makes this a recognizable continuation of its allocation problem, rather than a change to fractional spectrum outcomes.

The weakest point is that an arbitrary conflict graph cannot automatically be compressed into type-level capacities: if every access point has a unique neighborhood, \(\tau\) may approach the population size, and the capacity constraints may fail to capture which local conflicts occur together. The mirror therefore covers repeated network structures, not every spectrum auction instance. It also covers the welfare-allocation result in Theorem 2, not Theorem 1’s strategic guarantee: a mass allocation model needs a separate account of incentives and payments, especially if whole cohorts can coordinate their reports. Further work would be to validate the capacity representation for repeated deployments, give a sharper discrete-to-continuous approximation bound, and determine what pricing or configuration machinery is needed once type bundles or local network patterns are not explicit.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 2 is a genuine computational result, so the negative case cannot start by dismissing the paper as purely axiomatic. The problem is that the proposed LP does not preserve the feasibility condition Theorem 2 analyzes: allocations must be conflict-free in the buyers’ graph, and conflict constraints can interact.

Take many repeated copies of a three-buyer triangle, with one buyer of each role in every copy. Give each role unit value and let each triangle edge be a virtual resource. The proposed capacity constraints allow serving half of each role: every edge is used by total mass \(1\), yet the LP claims welfare \(3/2\) per copy. A feasible allocation can serve only one buyer per triangle, for welfare \(1\). The pairwise resource capacities lose the joint constraint \(x_A+x_B+x_C\leq 1\). This is not a technical issue about fractional bundles: each served buyer can still receive their entire bundle. It is a mismatch between aggregate type masses and the correlated interference pattern.

A stronger mirror would retain the whole repeated network motif and describe feasible allocations within it, then optimize over their convex hull. That fixes the triangle example, but the continuum is now over motif configurations rather than just buyer types. For generic spectrum networks, an agent’s interference neighborhood is tied to other particular agents; recording enough of that structure can make the support grow with the network. If the deployment really is many independent copies of a fixed motif, optimizing welfare per copy reduces to solving the allocation problem within that motif and scaling the result. This may be a useful model for a specific deployment, but it does not give the paper’s general buyer population a type distribution that captures its feasibility constraints.

The proposed mirror also sets aside the strategic mechanism that gives AEGIS-SG its meaning: Theorem 2’s welfare guarantee is paired with Theorem 1’s truthfulness result. In an atomless model, one agent’s report has zero effect on aggregate allocation, so individual incentives become vacuous; treating a positive-mass cohort as the strategic player changes the mechanism and its agents.

I would therefore reject this as a strong mirror of Theorem 2: the simple LP is not faithful to spatial feasibility, and the natural exact repair shifts the model toward repeated network configurations. The negative case is not airtight. A deployment with many genuinely repeated, independent interference motifs could support a useful specialized mirror if the authors accept motif roles as types. But that would need a concrete model showing that the motif structure remains compact as the population grows; the paper itself gives no such regime.
