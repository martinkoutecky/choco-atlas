The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the paper offers no computational result to continuize. Proposition 1 proves that a cost structure exists; Proposition 2 describes the set of such structures as a convex polyhedron. Propositions 3 and 4 concern subgame-perfect equilibrium, and Proposition 5 concerns membership in the core. None states a complexity bound or analyzes an algorithm for a computational problem. Their claims are about existence and economic properties, so they do not supply the kind of result this programme is meant to mirror.

The best rescue is to treat Proposition 1’s constructive proof as an implicit algorithmic question: given a network, find an incentive-compatible cost structure, perhaps optimizing some feature of the resulting cost shares. That could be a worthwhile new problem. But it is not a computational result in this paper, and its continuous version needs more than replacing counts with masses.

The proposed population of users in a few standardized connection-cost classes is plausible; the obstacle is what the population distribution retains. In this model, links join particular agents, and an agent’s cost depends on who connects through them as well as on their own parent. The transfers are indexed by agents and possible network positions. A type-level distribution of connection costs does not record those roles. One could add mass flows and per-capita link costs, or let types serve as network nodes, but those choices change the spanning-tree and cost-sharing objects in different ways. The paper gives no basis for selecting one as its population mirror. This is not just a literal-formalism problem: the missing information is part of what determines each person’s incentives.

The equilibrium and core results offer no cleaner route. The SPE statements rely on a finite sequence in which named agents move in a permutation; that order has no direct analogue for a continuum population. A nonatomic strategic game could be defined, but it would be a new game with a new equilibrium notion. Likewise, a continuum core question would require defining network costs for measurable coalitions. Either could become a research problem, but neither is already posed by these propositions.

So the proponent identifies a sensible high-multiplicity *finite* setting, but does not establish a continuous mirror of a named result. The negative case is strongest against this paper as a source of computational mirrors. It is weaker against the broader claim that no worthwhile extension exists: an author might embrace a mass-flow formulation and turn it into a genuine algorithmic problem. On the text provided, though, that would be a new model and computational agenda, not a continuization already anchored in the paper.
