The continuous mirror question

Given a finite project set \(C\), rational costs \(q_c=\mathrm{cost}(c)/B\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over exact additive utility vectors \(u_t\), can one compute in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) a feasible \(P\subseteq C\) such that every feasible completion \(W\supseteq P\) has no fractional FJR1+ witness? A witness would consist of \(\beta>0\) and nonnegative \(z_t,y_c,x_{tc}\) with \(0<z_t\le\mu_t\) whenever \(z_t>0\), \(\sum_t z_t\ge\sum_c q_cy_c\), and, for each \(t\) with \(z_t>0\), \(u_t(W)<\beta\) and \(\sum_c u_t(c)x_{tc}=\beta z_t\). Require \(x_{tc}\le\mu_t y_c\), \(x_{tc}\le z_t\) for \(c\in W\), \(x_{tc}=0\) when \(u_t(c)=0\), and \(x_{tc}=0\) when \(c\notin W\) and \(u_t(W)+u_t(c)>\beta\).

The model it lives in

Types are exact additive utility vectors over the fixed project set, with masses \(\mu_t\); project costs are normalized as \(q_c=\mathrm{cost}(c)/B\), and outcomes remain sets of indivisible projects. The certificate uses group masses \(z_t\), fractional project weights \(y_c\), and aggregate per-type assignments \(x_{tc}\). The target is a support-sensitive RBG algorithm that returns a feasible seed set \(P\) whose every feasible completion satisfies the distributional FJR1+ condition.

What the mirror covers

Covers Theorem 4.1’s general-cost FJR1+ completion guarantee and Theorem 6.1’s unit-cost Droop and priceability guarantee; it leaves the exact-FJR hardness result and the other numbered results outside the proposed anchors.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the remaining-budget invariant be implemented with type-level per-capita budgets and aggregated payments in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?
  • Does that weighted construction preserve the every-feasible-completion guarantee for all rational type distributions?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is Continuous Additive PB FJR1+ Completion, anchored on Theorem 4.1, proved in this paper. The theorem says that for additive utilities and arbitrary nonnegative project costs, the paper’s Residual-Budget Greedy algorithm runs in polynomial time and returns a feasible partial set \(P\) such that every budget-feasible completion of \(P\) satisfies FJR1+. This is the lead because it covers the paper’s general-cost additive model and gives a guarantee robust to how the remaining budget is spent.

A credible regime is a citywide or regional PB process with many residents but relatively few repeated valuation profiles—for example, residents grouped into a modest number of neighborhood or survey-derived cohorts. A type \(t\) is the residents’ complete additive utility vector over the public projects; its mass \(\mu_t\) is its fraction of the population, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Project costs are public and common to all residents. If the total budget is \(B\), write \(q_c=\mathrm{cost}(c)/B\). Then \(W\subseteq C\) is feasible when \(\sum_{c\in W}q_c\le1\), and a group of mass \(z\) can afford projects of normalized cost at most \(z\). The intended regime has population size \(N\) far larger than support size \(\tau=|\{t:\mu_t>0\}|\); the input gives the rational masses and type vectors, not \(N\) individually named voters.

Here is a precise continuous version of the property in Theorem 4.1. Call \(W\) continuously FJR1+ if there is no \(\beta>0\) and no nonnegative variables \(z_t,y_c,x_{tc}\) satisfying \(\sum_t z_t>0\), \(z_t\le\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t z_t\ge\sum_c q_cy_c\), such that, for every \(t\) with \(z_t>0\), \(u_t(W)<\beta\) and \(\sum_c u_t(c)x_{tc}=\beta z_t\). The assignments must also satisfy \(x_{tc}\le\mu_ty_c\) for every \(t,c\); \(x_{tc}\le z_t\) when \(c\in W\); and \(x_{tc}=0\) if \(u_t(c)=0\), or if \(c\notin W\) and \(u_t(W)+u_t(c)>\beta\). This is the paper’s fractional strengthening expressed for a population distribution: type mass replaces voter counts, while the selected outcome remains an ordinary set of projects. The question is: given the rational type distribution and project data, find a feasible \(P\) such that every feasible \(W\supseteq P\) is continuously FJR1+.

I expect this mirror to be tractable (Class A). The natural extension of RBG keeps a per-capita remaining budget \(b_t\) for each type. At utility level \(h\), type \(t\), if \(u_t(P)<h\), offers total mass \(\mu_t\min\{b_t,b_tu_t(c)/(h-u_t(P))\}\) toward project \(c\). A project is affordable when the offers cover \(q_c\); payments can be spread uniformly across that type’s mass. The paper’s remaining-budget potential argument appears to aggregate over identical agents, and the computation then depends on \(m,\tau\), and the rational bit lengths rather than the number of residents. The fractional assignment certificate also has only type-by-project variables. This is a plausible weighted extension, though the paper proves Theorem 4.1 for an explicitly listed finite electorate; the type-aggregated proof and its bit-complexity bound are work for the mirror, not claims already proved there.

A second, narrower anchor is Theorem 6.1, also proved here. It says that for additive utilities, unit costs, and budget \(k\), a size-\(k\) outcome satisfying Droop-FJR1+ can be computed and verified in polynomial time. If at least \(k\) projects are valued positively by some voter, the outcome can also be made priceable. In the approval case it satisfies Droop-FJR+, priceability, and the stated sub-core guarantee.

Its continuous counterpart is Continuous Droop-FJR1+ Priceable PB: use the same type distribution and require \(|W|=k\). In the certificate above, replace the affordability condition by the strict Droop condition \(\sum_t z_t>\sum_c y_c/(k+1)\). Ask for such a \(W\), also priceable, when at least \(k\) projects receive positive utility from some positive-mass type. In the continuous priceability condition, payments \(p_{tc}\) are total payments by type \(t\): selected projects each receive total payment \(1\), only positively valued projects may be paid for, type \(t\)’s total payment is at most \(\mu_tB'\) for some \(B'\ge k\), and each unselected project’s positively interested types have at most \(1\) total unused budget available for it. This also looks tractable: the paper’s Droop construction and sequential-Phragmén completion have natural mass-weighted counterparts. It is a useful second case, but the general-cost theorem is the stronger lead.

The paper’s numbered anchors here are algorithmic; it supplies no numbered hardness result to prefer. Its introduction cites prior strong NP-hardness for exact additive FJR, but that is an external result and not the problem Theorem 4.1 solves: the theorem concerns the one-project relaxation, with FJR1+ as a stronger fractional guarantee. The continuous mirror should preserve that distinction rather than suggest population continuity makes exact FJR easy.

The weakest point is that a resident’s type is the full utility vector over all projects. A plausible PB process can have millions of residents and a small number of repeated profiles, but many real processes may have large \(\tau\), so the high-multiplicity regime is scenario-dependent. Also, the paper’s own fractional axiom is close to this model, which makes the mirror natural but means the new contribution would be the distribution-input formulation and support-sensitive complexity analysis, not the general idea of fractional representation. The mirror covers the RBG completion guarantee and its fixed-\(k\) Droop strengthening; it does not claim to continuize the project outcomes or to resolve the paper’s exact-FJR hardness boundary.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is about research priority, not whether these mirrors can be defined. In additive PB, a type is the complete utility vector over the entire project agenda. A large electorate therefore does not by itself give a small support: with project-specific cardinal ratings, many residents may have distinct types. Grouping people by rough neighborhood or average preferences would change the information that FJR uses. That makes the proposed low-\(\tau\) regime convincing for some fixed-agenda or approval settings, but much less representative of general additive PB.

The proposed version of Theorem 4.1 also needs a correction before it is a sound mirror. Its \(x_{tc}\) is aggregate assignment to a represented mass \(z_t\), yet it bounds \(x_{tc}\) by \(\mu_t y_c\). That lets a small represented part of a type draw utility from the whole type’s mass. For example, with \(\mu_t=1\), \(z_t=0.1\), \(y_c=0.5\), and utility \(u_t(c)=1\), the proposed bound permits \(x_{tc}=0.5\), even though that project share supplies only \(0.05\) aggregate utility to the represented mass. Under this aggregate interpretation, the bound should scale with \(z_t\), such as \(x_{tc}\le z_t y_c\). This undermines the stated formulation, but it does not defeat the better mirror: the corrected type-level version appears to fit the paper’s potential argument.

That same fit is why I would rank both anchors as low-priority examples for the programme. RBG depends on utility vectors and remaining budgets; identical voters can plausibly be kept symmetric, with offers and payments aggregated by type. The type-level algorithm then looks like a support-compressed form of the existing proof, rather than a case where population continuity exposes a new computational boundary. Theorem 6.1 adds a fixed-\(k\) Droop quota and priceability, but its Phragmén completion likewise appears to have a mass-weighted counterpart. These are real formalization and bit-complexity tasks, not reasons the questions are invalid.

I cannot honestly make the universal negative case. A large city using a fixed project list and a small ballot or rating alphabet can have many residents per exact type; that is a plausible high-multiplicity regime, and neither anchor is already a continuous-population treatment. The defect in the proposed FJR1+ constraints is repairable, and the narrowness of the Droop setting does not disqualify it. My strongest case against is therefore that these look like comparatively direct, Class A transfers and may be less valuable than mirrors that test where continuization changes tractability. It does not defeat either repaired anchor.
