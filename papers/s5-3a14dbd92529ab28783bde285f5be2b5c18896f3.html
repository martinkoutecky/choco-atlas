The continuous mirror question

Fix a finite public catalogue \(T\) of valuation vectors over item classes \(J\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), with \(\sum_j q_j\leq 1\). At every scale \(N\) for which the counts \(N\mu_t\) and \(Nq_j\) are integral, these counts specify the population and item copies; the mechanism knows the catalogue and aggregate counts but not individual types, and each bidder privately reports its valuation vector on arrival in a uniformly random permutation. Among mechanism families truthful in dominant strategies at each scale, what is the supremal worst-case asymptotic expected welfare ratio \(\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[W_N]/(N\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q,v))\), over instances with at most \(\tau\) types and \(m=|J|\), where \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q,v)=\max_{y\geq0}\sum_{t,j}\mu_t v_{tj}y_{tj}\) subject to \(\sum_j y_{tj}\leq1\) and \(\sum_t\mu_t y_{tj}\leq q_j\)?

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity online matching market: each type \(t\) has mass \(\mu_t\) and valuation vector \(v_t\); each item class \(j\) has per-capita supply \(q_j\). At scale \(N\), individuals arrive in random order, report privately, receive at most one item irrevocably, and pay a price. Welfare is compared with the fluid assignment benchmark over type-level fractions \(y_{tj}\).

The objection that survived

Dominant-strategy truthfulness is defined for individual bidders at each finite scale; a mass-only limit does not itself define that incentive constraint, while the type flow becomes deterministic when \(\mu\) is fixed. The proposed finite-refinement question preserves the individual constraint, but its answer need not describe a standalone continuum mechanism.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive mirror is the paper’s online weighted bipartite matching problem, in a market with a large cohort of repeated bidder types. For example, many advertisers might arrive in random order to compete for a catalogue of ad placements. Each type has the same valuation vector across placements, and each placement class has inventory proportional to cohort size. There are \(N\) bidders but only \(\tau\) distinct valuation types, with \(\tau\) fixed or moderate as \(N\) grows.

My anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper. It states that when the items are known and bidders arrive in uniformly random order, there is a truthful online mechanism for weighted bipartite matching with expected competitive ratio \(e\): each arriving bidder reports nonnegative values for the items, the mechanism assigns at most one item irrevocably, and its expected welfare is compared with the maximum-weight offline matching. The paper presents this as optimal, relying on the secretary lower bound; its displayed analysis gives a welfare guarantee of \(\lfloor n/e\rfloor/n\) times optimum, which approaches \(1/e\) as \(n\) grows. The paper’s cited lower-bound background is not a second theorem proved here.

Here is the continuous question I would hand to a prover: \(\textsc{Truthful-Secretary-WBM}_{\infty}\). An instance has a finite set \(T\) of bidder types, a finite set \(J\) of item classes, rational type masses \(\mu_t\geq0\) summing to one, per-capita item supplies \(q_j\geq0\) with \(\sum_j q_j\leq1\), and nonnegative rational values \(v_{tj}\). A type is a complete valuation vector: agents of type \(t\) value every item class \(j\) at \(v_{tj}\). At scale \(N\), there are \(N\mu_t\) agents of each type and \(Nq_j\) copies of item class \(j\), with integral counts; agents arrive in a uniformly random permutation. On arrival, each agent reports a value vector, and the mechanism must assign at most one available item copy irrevocably and charge a payment. Truthful reporting must be a dominant strategy.

The fluid offline benchmark per agent is

\[ \mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q,v)= \max_{y\geq0}\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{j\in J}\mu_t v_{tj}y_{tj} \quad\text{subject to}\quad \sum_{j\in J}y_{tj}\leq1,\qquad \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t y_{tj}\leq q_j. \]

Here \(y_{tj}\) is the fraction of type \(t\) assigned to item class \(j\). The problem is to determine the best worst-case asymptotic welfare ratio \(\rho^*(\tau,m)\), over truthful online mechanism families, as \(N\to\infty\), for instances with at most \(\tau\) types and \(m=|J|\) item classes. A solution is such a family of allocation and payment rules, with its truthful guarantee measured against \(N\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q,v)\).

I expect this to land on the tractable side: the paper’s mechanism supplies a concrete baseline of at least \(1/e\) welfare along the finite refinements, since item classes can be represented by copies. The question worth pursuing is whether the optimal ratio improves when each type has large multiplicity, and how it depends on \(\tau\), the mass profile, and item supply. The result I am mirroring is specifically Theorem 1’s truthful competitive guarantee for weighted matching; this does not attempt to mirror the paper’s broader implications for matroids or combinatorial auctions.

The weak point is the arrival model. A continuum of agents has no canonical uniformly random permutation, and at fluid scale the random permutation of repeated types converges to deterministic type flows. That may erase the uncertainty that drives the secretary guarantee, so \(1/e\) is a baseline rather than a prediction of the continuous optimum. I use the sequence of finite refinements to define the limit precisely; the authors could reasonably accept this as their high-multiplicity matching question, but the limit’s value depends on whether that asymptotic interpretation is the one they want to study.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 1 is a genuine computational anchor, and the repeated advertiser cohort is a plausible high-multiplicity setting. I can’t make a strong case by denying either. The best negative case is that the theorem’s defining uncertainty—random order among named bidders with unrestricted private values—does not survive the proposed fixed-type continuum in a nontrivial form.

Take a finite set of valuation types with fixed positive masses \(\mu_t\), and refine it to \(N\) bidders. Among the first \(\alpha N\) arrivals, the number of type-\(t\) bidders is \(\alpha N\mu_t+O_p(\sqrt N)\). In the fluid limit, each type therefore arrives at the known rate \(\mu_t\); every positive-mass type appears in any positive-length sample. With fixed values and welfare normalized by \(N\), the aggregate randomness that the secretary analysis exploits vanishes. This is a degeneration of the online information pattern, not an objection that the continuous answer might differ from the paper’s answer.

There are plausible ways to repair the model, but each changes what is being studied. Give the mechanism \(\mu\), as in the proposed society input, and the limiting type flow is deterministic. Hide \(\mu\), and it is no longer an operational input to the mechanism; the question needs an additional model of what the mechanism knows and how it learns. Keep finite-\(N\) random permutations, and the question is about asymptotic guarantees for a sequence of discrete mechanisms. Or let \(\tau\) or the masses vary with \(N\) so rare types retain substantial influence; then some types have only a bounded number of agents, so the high-multiplicity premise has been lost. A richer random type-flow model could be interesting, but its stochastic law would be an extra ingredient, not something supplied by the paper’s random permutation in the continuum.

Truthfulness sharpens this mismatch: the paper requires a dominant-strategy guarantee for an individual bidder’s report. A distribution over types records aggregate mass, not the effect of one bidder changing their report. Finite refinements preserve that incentive question, but the limiting society alone does not specify it.

There is also a gap in the proponent’s claimed \(1/e\) baseline: the paper’s independence argument assumes the number of items is at most the sampling length \(k\). If item copies scale as \(N\sum_j q_j\), that condition need not hold. Restricting supplies might recover the baseline, but it would not restore the missing continuum arrival uncertainty.

So the negative case is limited. A finite-refinement study of truthful matching with repeated types could be worthwhile. What I do not see is a compelling continuous mirror of this theorem as stated: the natural fluid limit erases its central random-order phenomenon, while the repairs that preserve that phenomenon retain finite-agent structure or add new assumptions.
