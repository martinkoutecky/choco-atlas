The suggested “continuous voting with abstention” does not supply the missing anchor. Large bodies with repeated member types are plausible, so I would not argue that a high-multiplicity regime is impossible. The harder problem is that the paper’s power indices measure an individual voter’s ability to change the outcome. In an atomless population, one person has zero mass: changing their ballot does not change the aggregate vote, so individual pivotality vanishes. One could instead measure a type’s bloc power or the effect of shifting a positive mass between yes, no, and abstention. But that changes the object being measured; a mass-sensitivity or bloc-control problem is not the paper’s individual voting-power result.

A continuous model of abstention could still be interesting, and this paper might motivate it. The honest negative case is narrower: it is a poor fit for ChoCo’s programme of continuizing populations to study computational complexity, because the paper offers no computational result to mirror and its central measure loses its individual meaning in the atomless limit. I cannot rule out every useful reformulation; the case is against treating one as a continuous mirror of this paper.
