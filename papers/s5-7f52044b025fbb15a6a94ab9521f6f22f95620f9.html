The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest anchor is Theorem 8, proved in this paper. It says that deciding whether an integral stable multicommodity flow exists is NP-complete, even when every commodity has the same source and sink, every vertex has the same preferences for every commodity, and there are no commodity-specific edge capacities. Edges may still rank commodities differently. The paper proves this by reduction from 3-SAT.

A plausible population mirror is Mass-Stable Integral-Unit Flow. Take a directed network with common source and sink, rational aggregate capacities, and a finite set \(T\) of shipment types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to one. A type completely specifies a shipment’s terminals, edge priority class, vertex preferences, and per-shipment edge capacities. In the Theorem 8 subcase, types share the same vertex preferences and per-shipment capacities; edges can rank types differently.

Each shipment still chooses an *integral* feasible flow vector. The population of type \(t\) may be divided among different such vectors: let \(\lambda_t\) give the mass assigned to each integral flow, and let \(y_{t,e}\) be the resulting aggregate mass of type \(t\) on edge \(e\). Require \(\sum_t y_{t,e}\) to respect the population-level capacity. A solution is a collection of these assignments whose aggregate flow is stable: no type has a directed blocking walk under the paper’s endpoint-preference and edge-priority conditions, applied to positive masses and aggregate capacities. Thus a shipment’s action remains integral while type-level totals may be fractional.

This fits a high-multiplicity regime such as a large regional freight network: millions of shipments, but far fewer recurring combinations of origin–destination pair, service class, and route-related preferences. Their masses are fractions of the shipment population. Capacities can be normalized per person, so scaling the population scales aggregate supply as well. This is population continuization; fractional aggregate flows are the consequence of combining many individual integral choices.

For this mirror of Theorem 8, the decision question is: does a stable mass assignment of integral individual flows exist? The answer is expected to be yes for every instance. The paper’s Theorem 6, attributed to Király and Pap [27] rather than proved here, says that a stable fractional multicommodity flow exists for every instance. With integral per-shipment capacity bounds, each type’s feasible flow set has an integral flow polytope, so a type-level fractional flow can be represented as a mass distribution over integral individual flows. The finite stable-flow result therefore gives a concrete reason to expect the discrete integral-feasibility obstruction in Theorem 8 to disappear in this population model.

There is still a computational question: finding such a stable mass flow. Theorem 6 also says that finding a stable multicommodity flow is PPAD-hard. For a mirror allowing general type-specific capacity profiles, the mass model compresses to a finite multicommodity-flow instance, so that result is relevant to the constructive problem. I would not claim that PPAD-hardness persists in the narrower common-capacity subcase matching Theorem 8; that restriction needs its own analysis. Further questions include whether fixed support size \(\tau\) makes construction tractable, and which assumptions make the discrete integral obstruction survive aggregation.

The mirror covers Theorem 8’s integral stable multicommodity-flow existence result, not the paper’s single-commodity algorithms or its restricted-edge results. The weakest point is that the paper already treats commodities as categories and allows fractional flow quantities. A referee could regard the aggregate type model as too close to its existing stable multicommodity-flow model. The case for the mirror rests on the distinct population interpretation: shipments are the individuals, each retains an integral action, and the model asks what stability means when their aggregate is a continuum. Theorem 6 is useful evidence for the answer, but also makes this a particularly close neighbor.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The shipment-population model has a sharper problem than being “close” to Király–Pap’s stable multicommodity flow model: with the proposed aggregate notion of stability, it is the same computational object.

For type \(t\), let \(\mu_t\) be its population mass and \(a_t(e)\) its per-individual capacity on edge \(e\). Set the commodity capacity to \(c_t(e)=\mu_t a_t(e)\), the shared edge capacity to the population-level capacity, and the commodity flow \(f^t(e)\) to the type’s aggregate mass \(y_{t,e}\). Conservation, capacity constraints, edge priorities, and blocking walks then match exactly. The distribution \(\lambda_t\) over integral individual flows adds no further stability condition: when an individual flow polytope is integral, each feasible type-level flow can be represented as a mixture of integral flows.

That resolves both computational questions the proponent raises. The population version’s existence follows from Theorem 6, and the search problem is the stable multicommodity flow problem. Theorem 7 transfers the search hardness to instances with shared terminals, identical vertex preferences across commodities, and no commodity-specific edge capacities—the restrictions highlighted in the proposed mirror. Its bijection preserves stable flows, so the PPAD-hardness applies there too. This is more than observing that the paper allows fractional flow values: the mass distribution itself is absorbed into the type capacities, and the support size \(\tau\) is just the number of commodities. A study parameterized by \(\tau\) could still be useful, but it would be a parameterized study of stable multicommodity flow, not a new continuous-population problem.

Nor does insisting on integral individual routes restore a distinct mirror. In a continuum, the type mass can be divided among integral routes; the aggregate is precisely the fractional type flow above. If instead a single named individual may block, the distribution over types no longer records enough information to decide stability: changing the assignment of a measure-zero set leaves the society unchanged. Retaining finite individual granularity brings back the discrete model rather than a population continuum.

So the strongest anchor—the NP-completeness of integral stable multicommodity-flow existence—does not yield a fresh continuous question in this framing. Its mass-assignment version is already covered by the known stable multicommodity-flow existence and search results. A new question about population perturbations or robustness might be worth studying, but it would need a different computational target; it would not rescue this mirror of Theorem 8.
