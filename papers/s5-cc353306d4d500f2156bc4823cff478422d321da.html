The continuous mirror question

Given candidates \(C\), approval types \(A_1,\ldots,A_\tau\subseteq C\), and positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau\) summing to \(1\), compute the exact Hamming Pairwise Agreement for \(0<p<1\), where \(q_j=\sum_{t:c_j\in A_t}\mu_t\) and \(p=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m q_j\): return \(1-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^m q_j(1-q_j)}{mp(1-p)}\).

The model it lives in

The society is a distribution over approval-ballot types \(A_t\subseteq C\), with mass \(\mu_t\) on each type. Compute the exact normalized expected pairwise agreement from the candidate marginals \(q_j=\sum_{t:c_j\in A_t}\mu_t\); there is no action variable.

The objection that survived

The index depends only on the \(m\) candidate marginals, so the continuous task reduces to aggregating the support and applying the existing formula, without revealing a new complexity boundary. The proponent acknowledges the computation is modest but does not answer this concern about its limited computational substance.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 5.5's exact computation of Hamming Pairwise Agreement and its \(O(mn)\) speedup over pairwise evaluation. It leaves the other agreement results, heuristic clustering-based diversity and polarization indices, and experimental election map without algorithmic mirrors.

The case FOR (proponent)

The best positive case is narrow: continuous computation of the paper’s Hamming Pairwise Agreement index.

The anchor is Theorem 5.5, proved in this paper (Appendix B.5). For an election with \(m\) candidates, \(n\) voters, and saturation \(p\), it states that
\(\mathrm{pairagr}(E)=1-\frac{\sum_{c\in C}|A(c)|(n-|A(c)|)}{n^2m\,p(1-p)}\).
The surrounding text explains that this formula reduces computation from considering all voter pairs to \(O(mn)\). There is no numbered hardness result here; this is the paper’s clearest algorithmic anchor.

A plausible regime is a large survey or participatory budgeting vote over a fixed, short slate of projects. Let each type be an approval ballot \(A\subseteq C\), and let \(\mu_A\) be the fraction of residents submitting that ballot. The same ballot makes residents indistinguishable for this index. With \(m\) fixed, there are at most \(2^m\) types, so an electorate of tens of thousands can have far fewer distinct types than voters.

I would pose Continuous Hamming Pairwise Agreement Evaluation as follows. The input is a candidate set \(C\), a support of \(\tau\) approval types \(A_1,\ldots,A_\tau\), and binary rational masses \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_\tau\) summing to one. Let \(q_j=\sum_{t:c_j\in A_t}\mu_t\) be the mass approving candidate \(c_j\), and let \(p=\frac{1}{m}\sum_j q_j\). For \(0<p<1\), compute the exact value
\(\mathrm{pairagr}_\infty(\mu)=1-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^m q_j(1-q_j)}{mp(1-p)}\).
Equivalently, this is one minus the expected Hamming distance between two independent draws from \(\mu\), divided by \(2mp(1-p)\). A solution is the resulting rational score. There is no action variable: the underlying task is to evaluate an index, so adding an optimization decision would change the problem.

The mirror is direct: every finite election induces an empirical distribution over its ballots, and this formula gives exactly the paper’s index on that distribution. Theorem 5.5’s computation carries over with \(n\) replaced by support size \(\tau\): form the candidate marginals and evaluate the expression in \(O(m\tau)\) arithmetic operations, with polynomial bit complexity. I expect this to be Class A, tractable. It gives a clean continuous-population version of one of the paper’s results, though it does not show discrete hardness dissolving, because the paper proves no hardness result for this index.

The scope is only Theorem 5.5 and Hamming Pairwise Agreement. The paper says exact clustering for its diversity and polarization indices is not computationally feasible and uses heuristics, but supplies no numbered complexity classification to anchor a continuous mirror of those tasks.

The weak point is that the authors already define this index through averages over voters, and Theorem 5.5 reduces it to candidate-wise approval proportions. The continuous formulation is natural and useful for compressed population data, but the algorithmic contribution of the mirror is modest: most of the computation is already present in the paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proposer has found a valid continuous formulation. The type masses are sensible for a large approval electorate, and the fact that the finite and continuous formulas agree is not an objection. The problem is that this mirror offers almost no new computational question for ChoCo.

For a society \(\mu\), let \(q_j\) be the mass approving candidate \(c_j\), and let \(p=\frac{1}{m}\sum_j q_j\). The mirrored index is
\[ \mathrm{pairagr}_\infty(\mu) =1-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^m q_j(1-q_j)}{mp(1-p)}. \]
Computing it from the ballot support takes \(O(m\tau)\) rational operations; given the marginals, it takes \(O(m)\). That is essentially a scan of the input followed by elementary arithmetic. Theorem 5.5 already removes the pairwise-voter computation in the finite model. Replacing integer counts with rational masses preserves the same calculation; it adds no optimization, pricing problem, or complexity boundary for continuous computation to uncover.

The strongest version of the mirror still has this limitation: the index depends only on the candidate marginals \(q_j\), so the full distribution over ballot types is unnecessary once those marginals are computed. A high-multiplicity input can compress repeated ballots, but the index computation does not exploit population structure beyond that routine aggregation. Adding constraints to make optimization harder, or asking for statistical estimates from samples, would introduce a different problem rather than deepen this theorem’s mirror.

So the anchor is formally sound, but the case against its value is that its continuous version is an immediate weighted-count extension of an already input-linear formula—not a worthwhile complexity result for the continuization programme. That is a credible negative case, though it cannot establish the universal claim: the high-multiplicity scenario itself is sensible, and the weakness is the mirror’s computational substance, not its model.
