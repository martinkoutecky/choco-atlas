The case FOR (proponent)

Strictly applying the requested anchor rule, this paper has no named computational-complexity result: it proves no problem is in P, NP-hard, FPT, or a comparable class. The closest constructive result—and the best basis for a positive case—is Theorem 3.11, proved in this paper. It says that, under the paper’s assumptions on two participants’ effort choices, costs, and requester value, for every \(\delta>\epsilon>0\), a sufficiently concentrated Dirichlet density yields a finite-task VMI mechanism that is practical, dominantly truthful, and prior-independent. For even \(C\), it uses the squared VMI estimator and \(T\ge 2(\alpha-C)\) tasks; for odd \(C\), it uses the VMI estimator directly and \(T\ge\alpha-C\). If effort incentives need only satisfy \(\delta\)-equilibrium, the requester’s expected utility is at least \(v^*-4\epsilon\). This is a mechanism-existence and approximation guarantee, not a running-time result.

The strongest positive setting I can make is a large crowdsourcing platform running many similar peer-evaluation contracts. There may be millions of evaluator pairs but relatively few distinct specifications. Define a pair type \(\theta\) by the complete descriptions of its two evaluator types and contract: the signal alphabet, full-effort joint signal law \(U_G^\theta\), available effort matrices and costs, and requester value function. Let \(\mu_\theta\) be the fraction of contracts of that type. Mass here counts contracts in the population; \(U_G^\theta\) remains the within-contract distribution over signals. This makes the society itself the distributed object, rather than treating the signal distribution as the continuum.

The lead problem I would put to a prover is High-Multiplicity VMI Design. An instance gives finite support \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to one, and, for each type, rational \(U_G^\theta\), finite listed sets of rational effort matrices and costs, and a continuous information-monotone value function represented by a finite rational piecewise-affine description. The platform has a task limit \(T\). Find, for each type, an effort profile and VMI payment rules whose unbiased estimators use at most \(T\) tasks, make truthful reporting dominant, and make the selected effort profile a \(\delta\)-equilibrium. The objective is to maximize
\[ \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta\left[ v_\theta(N_A^\theta U_G^\theta N_B^{\theta\top}) -\mathcal P_{A,\theta} -\mathcal P_{B,\theta} \right]. \]
A solution is the profiles and explicit payment rules, or an \(\epsilon\)-optimal portfolio of them. The algorithmic question is whether this can be computed in time polynomial in the support size \(\tau\), \(C\), the input bit length \(L\), \(T\), and the accuracy parameters. The expected direction is plausibly tractable for fixed \(C\) and explicitly listed effort choices: the paper’s threshold characterization suggests reducing each type to a finite effort-profile search and aggregating by mass. That is a conjecture for this new problem, not a result of Theorem 3.11.

The mirror covers only the paper’s effort-incentive and finite-task mechanism-construction strand. The paper’s VMI geometry and binary visualizations do not themselves become population-complexity questions. The strongest support for plausibility is that the paper already studies crowdsourced evaluation and gives a VMI mechanism for \(n\) participants; repeated cohorts of near-identical evaluator pairs are a natural high-multiplicity regime.

The weak point is substantial: the proposed portfolio is a heterogeneous, many-contract extension of a theorem about two participants, and if the contracts are independent, aggregation by \(\mu\) may add little computational difficulty. Most of all, Theorem 3.11 establishes existence for sufficiently large \(\alpha\); it does not give an efficient algorithm for finding the thresholds or a polynomial bound on the required tasks. So this is a credible continuous-population research question adjacent to the paper, but the paper does not supply the qualifying computational anchor that a strict ChoCo mirror requires.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 3.11 is the proponent’s strongest anchor, but it is a mechanism existence and approximation theorem, not a computational result: it gives no complexity bound for finding the threshold or the required \(\alpha\). The rest of the paper likewise constructs information measures and mechanisms; it does not classify an algorithmic problem. Under ChoCo’s stated gate, there is no computational result here to mirror.

The model’s scale parameter is the number of tasks \(T\), not the number of agents. Its central effort theorem studies two participants receiving repeated i.i.d. signals, and assumes each uses the same reporting strategy across tasks. Taking \(T\) to a continuum would replace finite samples with knowledge of the underlying signal distribution. That continuizes observations or probability, not the population—the distinction the programme explicitly puts out of scope. The paper does define pairwise payments for \(n\) participants, but that construction is a sum of pairwise mechanisms; its effort optimization theorem remains about two participants.

The proposed portfolio of contract types does not fix the mismatch. If each contract is an independent copy of a pair, the objective is a mass-weighted sum of separate optimizations. The masses simply weight the answers; they create no shared computational problem for the continuous population to solve. A stronger version could model a large crowd whose members share types and interact through pairwise rewards. But then an individual’s type alone does not specify the joint signal law with each partner. Including partners or interaction patterns in the type brings the model back to dyads or edges; coupling those interactions would require new incentive and effort analysis that Theorem 3.11 does not provide. That could be a worthwhile extension, but it is a new problem, not a continuous mirror of this result.

I cannot rule out a useful high-multiplicity peer-prediction project in another model. The negative case is strongest for this paper: its substantive results concern finite-sample mechanism construction and optimization for fixed participants, while a population version either averages independent copies or needs additional structure that changes the problem.
