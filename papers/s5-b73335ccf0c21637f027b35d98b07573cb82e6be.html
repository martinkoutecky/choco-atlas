What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror would concern the paper’s main \( \mathrm{EF} \)/\( \mathrm{EF1} \) compatibility result and its Hall-type decomposition, but no qualifying computational result from the paper remains to anchor it.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a genuine high-multiplicity mirror of the paper’s main result, not merely a divisible-goods reformulation.

There is one important limitation: the supplied paper contains no named theorem asserting membership in \(P\), NP-hardness, W[1]-hardness, or similar. Its named results are existence and decomposition theorems. I therefore use those honestly as anchors, while making the continuous versions computational questions.

My lead anchor is Theorem 1.1, proved in this paper: under mixed goods and chores with additive valuations, ex-ante EF and ex-post EF1 are compatible. In full, for every finite instance with agents having additive utilities, there exists a lottery over integral allocations such that the induced fractional allocation is envy-free for every agent, while every integral allocation in the lottery’s support is envy-free up to one item. The theorem is existential; it does not claim a polynomial-time algorithm. Indeed, Section 6 says that all components are polynomial except the probabilistic Hall-type decomposition.

A natural high-multiplicity regime is a large organization assigning recurring work packages to many employees: desirable and undesirable shifts, office locations, training slots, or other indivisible assignments. There are \(N\) employees and \(\Theta(N)\) item copies, but only a fixed or slowly growing number of employee types. A type records the complete additive valuation vector over assignment classes: for example, night shifts may be goods for some types and chores for others. Employees of the same type have identical valuations, budgets, and other parameters. Thus \(N\gg \tau\), where \(\tau\) is the number of distinct types. This is precisely the high-multiplicity regime, not a claim that every fair-division instance has many interchangeable agents.

The continuous problem I would hand to a prover is:

BoBW-Mixed\(_\infty\) — Given a finite type set \(T\), a rational population distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), finitely many item classes \(H\), rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_h\) for \(h\in H\), additive type valuations \(v_t(h)\), and a finite set \(\mathcal B\) of allowed integral bundles, decide whether there exists a randomized allocation of unit mass of agents to bundles in \(\mathcal B\) satisfying the following conditions.

Let \(p_t(b)\) be the marginal probability that a type-\(t\) agent receives bundle \(b\). The aggregate supply constraint is \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}p_t(b)b_h=\sigma_h\) for every item class \(h\). The ex-ante EF constraints are \(\sum_b p_t(b)v_t(b)\ge \sum_b p_u(b)v_t(b)\) for every pair of types \(t,u\). Finally, there must be a probability law over measurable integral allocations whose type marginals are \(p_t\), and which is EF1 with probability one: for every pair of agents in every realized allocation, envy is removable by deleting one indivisible item from either the envious or envied bundle, exactly as in Definition 2.2.

The decision variable is the type-to-bundle mass \(x_{t,b}=\mu_t p_t(b)\), together with its decomposition into globally correlated integral allocations. The pure problem is feasibility—construct any such lottery. One can add a linear assignment cost and minimize \(\sum_{t,b}x_{t,b}\kappa_{t,b}\), but that is an optional optimization extension rather than part of Theorem 1.1.

This is not continuization of the outcome space. In every realization, each agent receives an integral bundle and every copy of an item is indivisible. The quantities \(x_{t,b}\) are only masses of agents receiving different integral bundles. The supplies \(\sigma_h\) are the necessary per-capita scaling: a finite realization with \(N\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\) and \(N\sigma_h\) copies of item class \(h\) is an ordinary finite instance of the paper’s model. Conversely, rational high-multiplicity instances give finite approximations to the continuous question.

I expect this lead problem to be Class A in the finite-type, finite-item-class regime, though this is not proved by the paper. Ex-ante EF becomes a finite family of linear inequalities, and the paper’s own proof already reduces the difficult correlation requirement to flow and decomposition machinery. The remaining algorithmic question is whether the EF1-support condition admits a polynomial separation or column-generation oracle. With unrestricted item classes or bundle size, continuum-specific hardness is also plausible: the difficulty may lie in correlating all agents’ integral bundles, rather than in population multiplicity. That boundary is exactly worth charting.

A second worthwhile anchor is Theorem 4.10, proved here. It states that if \(X\in[0,1]^{n\times m}\) has every row sum \(r/n\), every column sum at most \(1\), and \(0<r<n\), then for any pairwise disjoint family of agent sets \(T_1,\ldots,T_\ell\), \(X\) has a decomposition into size-\(r\) matchings such that, for every \(i\) and every \(J\subseteq T_i\), the probability that the matched agents meet \(T_i\) nontrivially but all lie in \(J\) is at most \(|J|/|T_i|\). This is the probabilistic Hall-type decomposition used to correlate subjective goods with objective chores.

Its continuous mirror is Continuum-Hall-Decomposition\(_\infty\). The instance consists of a type distribution \(\mu\), per-capita item supplies, a fractional first-round matching \(x\) with \(\sum_hx_{t,h}=\rho\mu_t\) for \(0<\rho<1\), and pairwise disjoint measurable interest regions \(S_1,\ldots,S_\ell\) of the agent population. The solution is a probability law over measurable integral partial matchings: each agent and each item copy is matched at most once, the expected type-item matching mass equals \(x\), and for every measurable \(J\subseteq S_i\),

\[ \Pr\!\left(0<\operatorname{mass}(K\cap S_i)\ \text{and}\ K\cap S_i\subseteq J\right) \le \frac{\operatorname{mass}(J)}{\operatorname{mass}(S_i)}. \]

Here \(K\) is the matched-agent set in the realized matching. This is the exact mass analogue of Theorem 4.10: \(|J|/|T_i|\) becomes the fraction of the interest population contained in \(J\), while the matched objects remain integral.

I expect this second problem to be tractable through measure-valued flows or a configuration LP with separation, at least for finitely many types and item classes. It generates a sharp computational question: does the paper’s biased-flow construction yield an efficient continuous oracle, and can that oracle be composed with the EF1 allocation problem?

The authors should recognize both mirrors as their problems. The paper already distinguishes fractional marginals from integral support allocations, uses probabilistic coupling rather than divisible ownership, and proves fairness by controlling how mass is correlated across lotteries. The proposed mirror preserves additive utilities, mixed signs, ex-ante EF, ex-post EF1, and integral bundles. Existing mixed-manna work is not a collision: it continuizes the manna or outcome space, whereas this proposal continuizes the population while retaining indivisibility ex post.

The weakest point is that Theorem 1.1 is existential rather than a complexity result, and the continuum EF1 requirement involves a measurable global coupling over infinitely many agents. The paper itself admits that its key decomposition is not yet known to have a polynomial implementation. Thus I would not claim that continuization automatically makes the problem easy. The positive claim is narrower: this is a coherent high-multiplicity computational problem, directly anchored in the paper’s main theorem, and its unresolved decomposition/separation question is exactly the kind of complexity landscape the continuization programme is meant to expose.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive objection comes before the modelling details: this paper has no named computational anchor. Theorem 1.1 is an existence/compatibility theorem; Theorem 4.10 is an existence theorem for a decomposition. Neither states membership in \(P\), hardness, approximation, parameterized complexity, or even an algorithmic decision problem. Section 6 explicitly says that the crucial decomposition may require exponential enumeration and that a polynomial implementation is only suggested future work. Thus the proposed computational problems are new problems inspired by the paper, not continuizations of computational results proved in it.

The best version of the first anchor, Theorem 1.1, still does not repair this. One can legitimately introduce repeated item classes and per-capita supplies; that is the right high-multiplicity repair, not a literal objection. But it changes the role of the BoBW theorem. In an atomless population, a marginal \(p_t(b)\) can be implemented by partitioning the mass of type \(t\) into measurable pieces, assigning each piece a whole bundle \(b\). Every individual unit receives an integral bundle, but no lottery is needed to realize the type-level fractional assignment. The finite-agent distinction between “ex ante randomization” and “ex post integral support” has therefore largely disappeared.

There is a forced choice. If type mass may be split, the paper’s probabilistic correlation problem becomes a new mass-configuration problem: choose which integral bundles occur on which pieces of each type while ensuring pairwise EF1 among all used bundles. That may be worth studying, but it is not the paper’s BoBW decomposition result. If type mass may not be split, then types are being treated as indivisible super-agents; preserving the finite theorem requires reintroducing the very atomic population structure that continuization was supposed to remove.

The proposed finite bundle set \(\mathcal B\) also conceals the new difficulty. If \(\mathcal B\) is explicitly supplied, the problem is essentially a configuration or marginal-feasibility LP with additional support constraints. If it is implicit, then the important complexity lies in generating bundles and certifying global EF1 compatibility—machinery absent from the paper and potentially capable of encoding an arbitrary new problem. Either way, Theorem 1.1 does not identify the computational landscape claimed by the proponent.

The second anchor, Theorem 4.10, is weaker still. It is a finite matching decomposition lemma, not a computational result. Its interesting content is the control of which named rows are matched in a random integral matching. With an atomless population, that row-level obstruction is no longer naturally present. A fractional type-item matrix can be realized by partitioning each positive-mass type into measurable pieces and matching each piece to a whole item unit. The fractional marginals arise from population mass, not from a lottery over indivisible finite matchings.

The Hall condition itself then faces the same dichotomy. If arbitrary measurable subpopulations may be selected, one can use atomless randomization or measure-preserving couplings to spread matched mass through an interest region; the finite concentration phenomenon that motivated the biased chain network disappears. If an entire type must be matched or unmatched as a block, then the relevant rows are again finitely many indivisible blocks, and the purported continuum advantage has been removed. The proposed “continuum Hall” problem is consequently either a degenerate transport/coupling problem or a newly imposed finite-type combinatorial problem—not a meaningful continuous version of Theorem 4.10’s contribution.

The paper’s ordinal impossibility theorem does not rescue the case. Replacing its three named agents by positive-mass types either preserves the same finite-support obstruction or allows atomless splitting that changes the obstruction; neither produces a computational population problem.

The honest qualification is that one could invent a worthwhile high-multiplicity fair-allocation paper inspired by this work. A configuration-LP study of integral bundle supports, type masses, and EF1 compatibility might be interesting. But it would require an independently motivated computational objective and new theorems. On the evidence supplied here, neither proposed anchor survives as a ChoCo computational mirror: the paper proves no qualifying computational result, and the strongest population models erase or replace the finite lottery/decomposition phenomena that make the paper technically distinctive.
