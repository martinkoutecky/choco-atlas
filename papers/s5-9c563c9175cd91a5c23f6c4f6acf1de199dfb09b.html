The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_o\) for item categories \(o\in O\), priorities \(q_t>0\), and equal-magnitude additive values \(v_t(o)\in\{-a_t,0,a_t\}\), let \(\bar q=\sum_t\mu_tq_t\), \(u_t=v_t/a_t\), \(r_t=\sum_o\sigma_ou_t(o)\), \(\Lambda_t=\max\{\lambda:\sum_s\mu_s\lceil\lambda q_s/\bar q\rceil\le r_t\}\), and \(h_t=\lceil(q_t/\bar q)\Lambda_t\rceil\). Compute a mass distribution \(\pi_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{O}\) satisfying \(\sum_B\pi_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}\pi_{t,B}B_o=\sigma_o\), \(u_t(B)\ge h_t\) whenever \(\pi_{t,B}>0\), and maximum normalized welfare \(\sum_{t,B}\pi_{t,B}u_t(B)=\sum_o\sigma_o\max_tu_t(o)\), in time polynomial in the type and category counts and the input bit length.

The model it lives in

High-multiplicity weighted fair division with type masses \(\mu\), integral bundle configurations \(B\), per-capita supplies \(\sigma\), priorities \(q_t\), equal-magnitude valuations \(u_t\), WMMS targets \(h_t\), and a maximum normalized-welfare objective.

The case FOR (proponent)

The paper supports a credible continuous mirror, with Theorem 4.1 as the strongest anchor and Theorem 3.1 as a broader second anchor. It has no named NP-hardness theorem of its own, so I would not manufacture one from its impossibility examples.

The right mirror is high-multiplicity fair division, not fractional fair division. Let \(T\) be a finite set of agent types with masses \(\mu_t\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). A type contains the complete information relevant to allocation: a priority \(q_t>0\), an additive valuation \(v_t\), and, where relevant, a magnitude \(a_t\). Let \(O\) be a finite set of standardized item or task categories, with supply \(\sigma_o\) copies per unit population. At population size \(N\), there are \(N\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\) and \(N\sigma_o\) copies of item \(o\). Each individual still receives an integral bundle \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{O}\); only the distribution of integral bundles is continuous.

Formally, an allocation is a mass distribution \(\pi_{t,B}\), where \(\pi_{t,B}\) is the mass of type-\(t\) agents receiving \(B\). It satisfies \(\sum_B\pi_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B}\pi_{t,B}B_o=\sigma_o\) for every item category \(o\). Thus type-level averages may be fractional, but no individual receives a fractional item.

The strongest anchor is Theorem 4.1, proved in this paper. It states that every equal-magnitude mixed instance, meaning that each agent \(i\) has some \(a_i>0\) with every singleton value in \(\{-a_i,0,a_i\}\), and arbitrary positive entitlements, admits an exact WMMS allocation computable in polynomial time. The allocation also maximizes \(\sum_i v_i(A_i)/a_i\) even over fractional allocations, hence is fractionally Pareto optimal; when all \(a_i\) are equal, it maximizes ordinary utilitarian welfare.

The corresponding continuous problem is Continuous Exact WMMS for Equal-Magnitude Types. Put \(\bar q=\sum_t\mu_tq_t\), \(u_t(o)=v_t(o)/a_t\), and \(r_t=\sum_o\sigma_o u_t(o)\). Define the high-multiplicity WMMS threshold by \(\Lambda_t=\max\{\lambda:\sum_s\mu_s\lceil\lambda q_s/\bar q\rceil\le r_t\}\), and let \(h_t=\lceil(q_t/\bar q)\Lambda_t\rceil\). The continuous WMMS value of type \(t\) is \(a_t(q_t/\bar q)\Lambda_t\).

The task is to output an allocation \(\pi\) satisfying the supply constraints above such that every bundle \(B\) with \(\pi_{t,B}>0\) gives type \(t\) normalized utility \(u_t(B)\ge h_t\). In addition, require maximum normalized welfare: \(\sum_{t,B}\pi_{t,B}u_t(B)=\sum_o\sigma_o\max_t u_t(o)\). This is not an arbitrary definition: for rational data, cloning the types \(N\) times gives \(R_t=Nr_t\), individual entitlement \(q_t/(N\bar q)\), and the paper’s finite WMMS formula becomes exactly \(N\Lambda_t\). The continuous threshold is therefore the rational-clone limit of the paper’s WMMS benchmark.

I expect this problem to be Class A. The paper’s matching proof becomes a mass-flow construction: type \(t\) demands \(\mu_t\max\{h_t,0\}\) mass of positively valued items, Hall’s inequalities become flow inequalities, and common chores are assigned subject to residual capacities. The main open questions are whether this flow formulation has polynomial bit complexity under binary-encoded supplies, and whether the exact result survives at ceiling discontinuities or under broader value domains. Proposition 1 gives a natural boundary test: allowing a second positive magnitude should plausibly destroy exactness even after cloning.

The second anchor is Theorem 3.1, also proved here. It states that every additive mixed-manna instance with arbitrary positive entitlements admits a complete WEF1 allocation, and that such an allocation can be computed in polynomial time. This is the paper’s broadest result and my second, more general mirror.

The corresponding problem is Continuous Complete WEF1 Allocation. For a type-\(t\) bundle \(B\), write \(v_t(B)=\sum_oB_ov_t(o)\). Require that for every two types \(t,t'\), and every bundles \(B,B'\) receiving positive mass from those types, at least one of the following holds:

\(v_t(B)/q_t\ge v_t(B')/q_{t'}\);

there is an item \(o\) in \(B'\) with \(v_t(o)>0\) such that \(v_t(B)/q_t\ge v_t(B'-e_o)/q_{t'}\); or

there is an item \(o\) in \(B\) with \(v_t(o)<0\) such that \(v_t(B-e_o)/q_t\ge v_t(B')/q_{t'}\).

The solution must allocate all item mass, and every individual bundle remains integral. This support-wise condition is exactly the continuum version of pairwise WEF1: it prevents a positive-mass group of type-\(t\) agents from envying a positive-mass group of type \(t'\) agents, while allowing different agents of one type to receive different integral bundles.

The natural regime is a large platform serving many standardized teams: recurring advertising slots, licences, servers, and data-processing tasks are supplied in proportion to the population; teams share valuation and priority profiles; some tasks are goods and others chores. The number of agents is enormous, while the number of valuation-entitlement types and item categories is comparatively small. The paper’s bundling argument is type-compatible, and its use of a continuous representation of weighted picking sequences strongly suggests a batch version of the algorithm. I would therefore ask whether this support-WEF1 problem is polynomial in \(|T|\), \(|O|\), and the encoding length of \(\mu,\sigma,q,v\), rather than in the cloned population size.

The weakest point is that neither WEF1 nor WMMS has a canonical atomless definition if one simply replaces agents by points of a continuum. The mirror must therefore make two explicit extensions: item supplies scale per capita, and fairness is imposed on distributions of integral bundles rather than on fractional individual bundles. The WMMS benchmark additionally has to be defined as the high-multiplicity limit above. A referee could reject that as too much remodelling; using average utility or divisible bundles would indeed be a failure. But the proposed construction preserves the paper’s agents’ information, integral bundles, entitlements, pairwise comparisons, and exact finite-clone semantics. On that basis, I think it is an author-recognisable continuous-population mirror, strongest for Theorem 4.1 and still substantial for Theorem 3.1.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that ordinary atomless fair division is the wrong limit here. If the item set stays fixed while the population grows, almost everyone receives the empty bundle; WMMS and WEF1 either collapse or become dominated by a finite exceptional set. If items are instead divisible, the paper’s indivisibility—the source of both fairness notions—has been removed. Thus there is no useful literal continuum limit.

That objection does not defeat the proponent’s stronger model, however. Scaling item supplies per capita while retaining integral individual bundles is explicitly within the programme’s scope, and it gives an exact high-multiplicity interpretation of Theorem 4.1. Let \(N\) clear the denominators of the type masses and supplies. A type \(t\) then has \(N\mu_t\) identical agents, entitlement \(q_t/(N\bar q)\), and normalized total value \(Nr_t\). The paper’s finite WMMS condition becomes

\[ N\sum_s\mu_s \left\lceil\frac{\Lambda q_s}{\bar q}\right\rceil \le Nr_t, \]

so the clone-level threshold is exactly the proposed \(\Lambda_t\), not merely an asymptotic approximation. Likewise, the individual target is exactly

\[ h_t=\left\lceil\frac{q_t}{\bar q}\Lambda_t\right\rceil. \]

Rational mass allocations and finite clone allocations translate into one another by clearing denominators, while every individual bundle remains integral. The matching proof also genuinely lifts: type \(t\) demands \(\mu_t h_t\) units of liked-item mass, Hall’s inequalities become flow inequalities, and residual capacity distributes common chores. This is not a fractionalization mistake or an identity loss. It is a clean high-multiplicity version of the theorem, with a real compressed-input question: can the construction be represented and computed polynomially in the number of types, item categories, and the bit length of the supplies rather than in \(N\)?

The WEF1 anchor is substantially weaker. The proposed support-wise definition is the only defensible direct translation: average bundle values would not preserve the paper’s individual guarantee. But support-wise WEF1 is nonconvex, and the polynomial algorithm in Theorem 3.1 is polynomial in the explicitly listed agents and items, not in a compressed description of many identical agents and item copies. Its bundling and weighted-picking steps may require a new configuration or finite-support theorem. The proponent’s claim that the algorithm “suggests” a batch version is therefore not a result. A mass-flow formulation that checks only type averages would be a different, weaker problem.

That is a legitimate reason to downgrade the WEF1 mirror from established to open and technically delicate. It is not a reason to say that no worthwhile mirror exists: the rational-clone version is well-posed, author-recognisable, and could expose whether WEF1 admits a genuinely high-multiplicity algorithm.

I therefore cannot honestly make the requested universal negative case. The WEF1 mirror is speculative, but Theorem 4.1 survives the strongest objections. Calling it “already done” would also be the wrong objection: the paper gives an algorithm polynomial in the expanded population, whereas the continuous question asks for a compressed high-multiplicity algorithm. The best-supported verdict is consequently that the paper has at least one worthwhile continuous mirror, with Theorem 4.1 as a strong Class-A candidate.
