The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s strongest point is also the decisive one for ChoCo: this paper has no result that classifies the complexity of a computational problem. Proposition 4 and Proposition 5 prove stochastic-stability claims for particular finite-player games. Turning either into a computational question—such as “compute the stochastically stable aspirations”—would introduce a problem the paper does not state or analyze. A well-posed new problem might be worthwhile, but it would not be a continuous mirror of a computational result in this paper.

For Proposition 4, the natural candidate is to take symmetric majority games with \(q/n\to\alpha\in(1/2,1)\) and ask which aspiration distributions are stochastically stable in an atomless society. The finite proof relies on selecting one player, having that player mutate, and then tracing how other players’ demands adjust. In an atomless population, one agent has zero mass: their update leaves the aggregate state unchanged. The paper’s one-player mutation resistance therefore has no direct aggregate counterpart. Updating a positive-mass cohort could produce nontrivial dynamics, but it changes the revision rule; a finite-population approximation also needs a choice of how population size and mutation probability approach their limits. Neither choice is supplied by the proposition, and each defines a new model.

Proposition 5 has a stronger possible mirror: retain the apex player as a distinguished atom and take the minor players to an atomless population. That is a plausible high-multiplicity regime, but the finite proof again uses individual minor-player mutations and adjustments. Each minor’s demand tends to zero as the population grows, and a single minor’s mutation does not change the aggregate state. Treating the minor population as a bloc would restore a nonzero update, but would add coordinated behavior absent from the paper. The hybrid model might support a worthwhile study; its stochastic-stability question would depend on modeling choices beyond the stated result.

So neither proposition supplies a surviving anchor for this programme. The natural limit points toward mean-field dynamics, which may be interesting analytic work but does not by itself fill the computational gap ChoCo targets. The negative case is strong for a *paper-grounded computational mirror*. It cannot rule out a worthwhile new continuum model of demand adjustment; the material here does not establish that broader universal claim.
