The case FOR (proponent)

My lead is Corollary 2, supported by Theorem 7. The paper has no proved NP-hardness or polynomial-time hardness classification of its own; the APX-hardness of Nash-welfare maximization is only cited from [Lee17]. So I would not pretend this is a hardness anchor. The strongest eligible anchor is its positive algorithmic result.

Corollary 2, proved in the paper via [GM21], states:

For additive valuations, there exists a fractionally Pareto-efficient, EF1, and NOM mechanism, running in pseudo-polynomial time.

The paper’s own Theorem 7 supplies the mechanism-design reduction behind this: given an algorithm \(M^\ast\) computing clean, non-wasteful, EF1 allocations, Mechanism 1 constructs a deterministic NOM mechanism that outputs EF1 partial allocations; moreover, \(\alpha\)-Pareto efficiency and \(\alpha\)-fractional Pareto efficiency of \(M^\ast\) are preserved. The pseudo-polynomial component comes from the cited algorithm of [GM21], while the reduction and its correctness are proved here.

The continuous problem I would put before a prover is:

Continuous High-Multiplicity EF1–fPO–NOM Mechanism Design. An instance consists of a finite set of item classes \(M=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), a finite set of complete additive valuation types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\) for the item classes, and valuation vectors \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^{m}\).

The population is atomless, with mass \(\mu_t\) of type \(t\). Each individual still receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq M\); continuity occurs only in how much population receives each bundle. The decision variable is therefore a configuration measure \(x_{t,S}\ge0\), where \(x_{t,S}\) is the mass of type \(t\) receiving bundle \(S\). It must satisfy

\[ \sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}=\mu_t \]

for every \(t\), and

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le q_j \]

for every item class \(j\).

The output must be clean and non-wasteful, fractionally Pareto efficient, and EF1 almost everywhere: whenever \(x_{t,S}>0\) and \(x_{u,R}>0\), a type-\(t\) individual receiving \(S\) must satisfy

\[ v_t(S)\ge v_t(R\setminus\{g\}) \]

for some \(g\in R\). Fractional Pareto efficiency means that no measurable reassignment of integral bundles can weakly improve every individual’s utility and strictly improve a positive-measure set.

The mechanism-design version asks for a direct mechanism \(F\) that maps reported type distributions to such assignments and satisfies the paper’s two NOM inequalities. Since a single atomless agent has zero mass, the nondegenerate continuum convention should require these inequalities for every positive rational mass \(\varepsilon\) of identical agents jointly misreporting, with utility evaluated per member of that cohort:

\[ \inf_{\widehat{\mu}_{-t}} \bar u_t(F(t,\widehat{\mu}_{-t})) \ge \inf_{\widehat{\mu}_{-t}} \bar u_t(F(b,\widehat{\mu}_{-t})) \]

and

\[ \sup_{\widehat{\mu}_{-t}} \bar u_t(F(t,\widehat{\mu}_{-t})) \ge \sup_{\widehat{\mu}_{-t}} \bar u_t(F(b,\widehat{\mu}_{-t})). \]

Thus the question is whether one can compute, from \((T,\mu,q,v)\), a finite description of an integral-bundle mass assignment and a mechanism satisfying the continuous analogues of fPO, EF1, and NOM.

This is a genuine population continuization. It does not turn goods into divisible outcomes: every individual bundle remains integral. The fractional object is the distribution of integral bundles across a large population. A rational instance has the expected high-multiplicity dictionary: clearing denominators turns \(\mu_t\) into clone counts and \(q_j\) into repeated item supplies, while a rational configuration measure \(x\) becomes a finite allocation. Conversely, every such finite cloned allocation normalizes to \(x\).

The natural regime is a large public-service or employment platform allocating repeated packages of indivisible benefits—training seats, equipment, housing slots, or service entitlements—to millions of users. Users fall into a small number \(\tau\) of exact valuation types because their contracts, priorities, and needs are standardized. Thus \(N\) is very large, while \(m\) and \(\tau\) are modest. The per-capita supplies \(q_j\) are the necessary catalogue-level version of the paper’s finite inventory.

I would expect the allocation part to be Class A in this regime. The variables \(x_{t,S}\) form a configuration LP, and the population masses are divisible even though configurations are integral. The reduction’s key objects—positive-support sets \(D_t=\{j:v_t(j)>0\}\), EF1 witnesses, and clean allocations—are type-level objects, so Mechanism 1 has a plausible mass-based analogue. With fixed \(m\), bounded valuation values, or suitable separation oracles, column generation should give an exact or pseudo-polynomial algorithm. The main open issue is whether EF1 support constraints and the NOM layer admit a polynomial-size separation procedure when \(m\), \(\tau\), and the valuation bit length are all variable.

The paper’s authors should recognize this as their problem: it preserves additive valuations, integral individual bundles, EF1, fractional Pareto efficiency, and NOM. It merely replaces a long list of named agents by repeated valuation types and asks whether their black-box reduction survives the resulting high-multiplicity regime.

My weakest point is NOM. Literal single-agent NOM becomes nearly vacuous in an atomless population because one report has zero mass. The positive-mass-cohort formulation above is therefore an explicit co-modification, not something silently inherited from the paper. If the authors reject cohort deviations, the safer remaining mirror is the continuous \(M^\ast\)-allocation problem underlying Theorem 7—computing clean, non-wasteful, EF1, fPO mass assignments—while treating the mechanism layer as a separate follow-up. That weakness does not undermine the allocation mirror itself, but it prevents me from claiming that Corollary 2 transfers automatically in full.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that Corollary 2 does not survive as a mirror of the paper’s actual contribution. Its computational content is the finite-agent NOM mechanism, not merely the existence of an EF1 allocation.

With the paper’s original goods, an atomless population immediately degenerates. There are only \(m\) indivisible items, so allocating them to individuals serves only a measure-zero set. Almost every agent receives nothing, and EF1, fPO, and utility guarantees become largely vacuous. To avoid this, the proposed mirror introduces per-capita supplies \(q_j\) and replaces each named item by a continuously replenished item class. That is a defensible repeated-goods model, but it is not the high-multiplicity version of the paper’s finite-inventory problem: it changes the supply side as well as the population. Granting that co-modification is permitted, the more serious problem is NOM.

NOM is defined by unilateral deviations of a named agent while the reports of the other named agents vary adversarially. In an anonymous atomless mechanism depending only on \(\mu\), one individual’s report has zero mass and cannot change the outcome. Both the best- and worst-case utilities therefore remain unchanged, so every such mechanism is NOM automatically. The paper’s main separation between truthfulness and NOM disappears.

The proposed positive-mass-cohort repair does not restore the paper’s notion. A cohort deviation is a coalition deviation, and its meaning depends on choices absent from the paper: the cohort’s mass, whether its members coordinate, whether identical types may split across bundles, and whether utilities are evaluated per capita or in aggregate. For fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), this is a new coalitional mechanism-design problem; as \(\varepsilon\to0\), its strategic effect disappears. There is no canonical high-multiplicity dictionary selecting one of these variants.

The proof of Theorem 7 also does not lift formally. Lemma 4 relies on the fact that, for one named agent, every clean EF1 allocation consistent with her report can be induced by choosing the other individual reports appropriately. With fixed type masses and per-capita supplies, an arbitrary configuration measure \(x_{t,S}\) cannot generally be induced this way: the other reports must respect the prescribed masses, and identical members of one type may need to receive different bundles. Mechanism 1 additionally uses individual indices and tie-breaking. Aggregating its output over clones is not a well-defined anonymous mechanism unless extra randomization or labelling structure is introduced, which again changes the result.

Even the proposed allocation-only fallback is not the configuration LP suggested in the case for it. If \(x_{t,S}>0\) and \(x_{u,R}>0\), EF1 requires the support-compatibility condition \(v_t(S)\ge v_t(R)-\min_{g\in R}v_t(g)\). These are disjunctive constraints on which bundles may simultaneously have positive mass. Mixing two EF1 allocations can introduce a cross-support pair that violates EF1. Thus the mass variables do not produce the convex LP structure that motivates the programme’s usual continuization; they produce a support-selection problem over integral bundles. It may be interesting, but Theorem 7 supplies neither the formulation nor a separation method for it.

The honest limit of the negative case is important. If NOM is discarded, a repeated-item, high-multiplicity EF1–fPO allocation problem is mathematically coherent and potentially worthwhile. It preserves integral bundles for individuals and has a plausible standardized-services interpretation. I therefore cannot honestly defend the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario. What the argument does establish is narrower: Corollary 2 and Theorem 7 do not provide a clean continuous-population mirror of their central NOM result. The proponent’s full anchor should not be accepted; only a substantially new allocation problem survives.
