The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only anchor the proponent raises is Proposition 18: majority preserves every 2-clause when the electorate is odd. Its natural continuous version is easy to state—represent rational ballots by their masses and use a mass-based majority rule, with a convention for ties. This is a sensible high-multiplicity model: voters are anonymous, and ballots form a finite type space.

That does not give ChoCo a computational mirror of this paper. Proposition 18 is an axiomatic preservation result, and the paper supplies no named computational problem, complexity result, or algorithmic objective to continuize. One could add a task such as checking whether a given mass profile’s outcome satisfies a formula, or finding a minimum mass transfer that breaks rationality. But those are new computational questions: the first adds an instance-level task to an abstract characterization; the second adds an objective and costs absent from the paper. They may be worth studying, but they do not mirror a computational result here.

So the negative case is strong on the programme’s stated scope: this paper offers no computational anchor. It is weak as a universal claim that no worthwhile continuous study could arise from the paper. The population model itself is natural, and an axiomatic continuous study could be worthwhile under a broader agenda.
