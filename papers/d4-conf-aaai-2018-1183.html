The most plausible unanchored angle would treat a large panel’s pairwise judgments about standardized products as a distribution over comparison types. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that this paper supplies no named computational result to mirror. It defines able2rank, describes its pairwise-preference and BTL steps, and reports experiments. Its only explicit complexity observation is that naive analogy retrieval scales quadratically with the training data; the paper presents that as a direction for future work, not as a proved result or complexity classification. So there is no eligible result here to anchor a continuous question.

The best version of the proposed mirror would use a large panel making judgments on standardized product comparisons. A type could include the compared feature vectors, the judgment, and the relevant context; its mass would be the fraction of judgments of that type. One could then replace the top \(k\) analogical examples with the top \(\alpha\) mass and fit a weighted BTL model. That is coherent if the comparisons are repeated and standardized. But it changes the data-generating problem: the paper’s training data are rankings of object sets, not individual raters’ preferences or a population of agents. The panel version would ask about learning from a distribution of judgments, a new problem rather than a mirror of a result in this paper.

Treating the training records themselves as the population has the same gap. It could motivate useful distributional retrieval and aggregation algorithms, but the continuous object would be a dataset distribution, and the computational question would be newly proposed. The paper gives no named complexity result for it to restate.

This is a strong negative case against a continuous mirror *of this paper*. It is weaker as a claim that no continuous treatment of learning from large populations of judgments could ever be worthwhile; the panel scenario could motivate one. That possibility does not give this paper the required computational anchor.
