The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest possible positive lead is Theorem 2, proved in this paper. It says that with \(n\ge 3\), an economic environment, the sympathizer property, and a nonempty set \(S(f)\) of rational-consistent profiles, the planner can infer that \(f\) is implementable by a guidance mechanism in Nash\(^*\) equilibrium—even without knowing the profile’s full specification. In every such equilibrium, everyone announces the same \(S\in S(f)\).

There is a plausible high-multiplicity setting behind that result: a large organization or stakeholder population with relatively few complete types, where a type records an agent’s state-contingent rankings and sympathy status, and mass is the fraction of the population with that type. A type-indexed version of the guidance mechanism would ask whether the planner can still implement \(f\) while eliciting only a rational-consistent profile.

But I cannot present that as a qualifying continuous mirror of a computational result. Theorem 2 is a mechanism-existence result, not a result that a problem is in P, NP-hard, FPT, or otherwise computationally classified. And the theorem’s strategic force comes from named individuals making unilateral deviations, including a privately identified sympathizer. In an atomless society, a single agent’s report has zero mass; treating whole types as strategic blocs would change the game. The other named results, including Theorem 1 and Proposition 1, also concern implementation or inference, not computational complexity.

So the honest positive case is that Theorem 2 suggests a high-multiplicity extension worth investigating, especially for large stakeholder populations with repeated preference types. It does not supply a named computational anchor under the requested standard, and I can’t state a continuous problem per qualifying anchor without inventing one.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 2 is the proponent’s strongest anchor, but it is not a computational result in ChoCo’s sense. It proves existence of a guidance mechanism that fully Nash\(^*\)-implements an SCC under stated assumptions. It does not classify the complexity of an algorithmic problem: there is no input-output task involving an intervention, a cost, or an optimization objective. Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 concern inference and characterization, so they do not supply another computational anchor. The proponent effectively concedes this gap.

One could invent a nearby decision problem: given a finite description of \(f\) and the preference environment, decide whether a rational-consistent profile exists, or construct the mechanism. But that makes a new algorithmic question out of a paper whose result is about what a planner can implement with strategic agents. If the input includes the full finite preference association, the test concerns Maskin monotonicity; adding population proportions does not give those proportions a role in the paper’s theorem. If the input instead hides that association, the paper studies what agents can reveal—not how to compute an intervention from a distribution. A problem about minimizing disclosure or optimizing over masses would add an objective the paper never poses.

The high-multiplicity setting is plausible at the level of the story: a large organization could contain many people with the same state-contingent preferences. But the theorem’s force comes from individual deviations and a privately identified sympathizer. In an atomless population, one person’s report has zero mass. Giving the sympathizer a positive mass makes them a group, whose coordinated deviation is a different strategic premise. A type-indexed mechanism might be worth studying, but it would be a new implementation model, not a continuous mirror of a computational result in this paper.

So the negative case is strongest on the programme’s central gate: there is no named computational result here to mirror. It is less airtight on whether a high-multiplicity implementation model could inspire worthwhile algorithmic work. That possibility remains, but it does not rescue any of the proposed anchors as a continuous computational mirror of this paper.
