The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), goods categories \(G\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and nonnegative rational values \(v_{gt}\), does there exist a finite-support mass plan \(\pi_{t,b}\ge 0\) over integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{G}\) such that \(\sum_b\pi_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}\pi_{t,b}b_g=q_g\) for each \(g\), and every positively assigned bundle \(b\) of type \(t\) satisfies \(v_t(b)\ge v_t(c)-\min_{g:c_g>0}v_{gt}\) for every positively assigned bundle \(c\) of any type, with comparison to an empty bundle vacuous?

The model it lives in

An atomless population has finitely many complete valuation types with masses \(\mu_t\); each person receives an integral bundle of goods from replicated categories, and total category use equals the per-capita supply \(q_g\). The decision is whether a finite-support mass plan satisfies EFX across all positively assigned bundles.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers linear-valuation EFX existence from Theorem 3. It leaves aside the paper’s fractional relaxation, approximate EFX discussion, and fixed-point proxy claims.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the compatible integral bundle configurations be represented with a finite description and an efficient pricing or separation procedure?
  • Can the finite-replica correspondence yield useful algorithms or approximation guarantees in terms of \(|T|\), \(|G|\), and input bit length?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is High-Multiplicity EFX Existence. Let \(T\) be a finite set of complete agent types, where a type specifies an agent’s value for every good category, and let \(G\) be a finite set of categories of interchangeable indivisible goods. The input gives rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), and nonnegative values \(v_{gt}\). A bundle is an integer vector \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{G}\), so every individual still receives whole goods.

The decision variable is a finite-support mass plan \(\pi_{t,b}\ge 0\): the mass of type \(t\) receiving bundle \(b\). It must satisfy
\[ \sum_b \pi_{t,b}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,b}\pi_{t,b}b_g=q_g\quad\text{for each }g. \]
For every pair of bundles \(b,c\) receiving positive mass to types \(t,s\), require
\[ v_t(b)\ge v_t(c)-\min_{g:c_g>0}v_{gt}, \qquad v_t(b)=\sum_g v_{gt}b_g. \]
If \(c\) is empty, the comparison is vacuous. This is exactly the EFX comparison: an agent of type \(t\) must not envy another assigned bundle after removal of any one good. The objective is feasibility—find such a plan, or equivalently one whose worst EFX violation is at most zero. A plan is realizable as a measurable assignment over an atomless population, with each person receiving an integral bundle.

A plausible regime is a large cohort of residents or students sharing a handful of value profiles, dividing replicated categories of household or school goods. The number \(N\) of people and the inventory scale with \(N\), while the number \(\tau=|T|\) of valuation types and the number of good categories stay small. Thus, \(N\) can be huge even though the instance is described by a short list of types and per-capita masses and supplies. This is a coherent high-multiplicity setting: people of one type are interchangeable for everything the problem uses.

My anchor is Theorem 3, proved in this paper. For \(m\) indivisible goods, \(n\) agents, and linear values \(v_{\ell i}\), the authors define
\[ f(y)= \max_{i\ne j,\,k}\left( y_{kj}+ \sum_{\ell\ne k} \max\left\{ y_{\ell i}-v_{\ell i},\ y_{\ell j}+v_{\ell i},\ \max_{r\ne i,j}y_{\ell r} \right\} \right) -\sum_\ell\max_r y_{\ell r}. \]
The theorem says an EFX allocation exists if and only if
\[ \inf_{y\in\mathbb R^{mn}} f(y)\le 0. \]
This is a formulation of the underlying EFX-existence question, not a complexity classification or a population model. It counts as an anchor because the paper computes with the formulation in its DCA section. That computation gives no global-optimality or polynomial-time guarantee: the paper says the method converges to a stationary point.

For the mirror, the question is whether the mass plan above exists when there are continuously many agents but only finitely many valuation types, while each person’s bundle remains integral. This covers the paper’s linear-valuation EFX existence result, not its discussion of EF, approximate EFX, or other valuation classes. The authors should recognize the problem: it preserves their fairness condition and allocation object, and changes the regime by grouping indistinguishable agents and scaling the inventory per capita. The paper’s own fractional-allocation and continuous-optimization variables do not themselves make its population continuous; this mirror does.

There is a useful high-multiplicity dictionary to establish. A finite allocation of \(N\mu_t\) agents of each type and \(Nq_g\) copies of each good category gives a mass plan by dividing bundle counts by \(N\). Conversely, for a finite-support mass plan with rational masses, scaling by a common denominator yields a finite replicated instance. With rational \(\mu\) and \(q\), a feasible mass balance on a fixed finite support can be represented by rational masses. The central research question is whether this bridge can be made into a clean two-way statement for the full problem and used to design algorithms.

My provisional expectation is Class A as a possibility worth pursuing, not a conclusion of Theorem 3. Once compatible integral bundle configurations are selected, the mass-balance constraints are linear; a configuration LP and an efficient pricing or separation procedure could make the large-population version tractable. The hard part is exactly whether that procedure exists. The paper supplies no numbered hardness result for EFX existence: its cited NP-hardness concerns computing EF, not this anchor. So there is no hardness theorem here to transfer, and no basis to claim tractability.

The main follow-up questions are whether compatible bundle configurations admit efficient pricing; whether runtime can be bounded in \(\tau\), \(|G|\), and the input bit length; and whether the finite-replica dictionary supports useful rounding or additive guarantees.

The soft spot is that Theorem 3 concerns finitely many named agents and does not automatically extend to mass distributions. Splitting a type’s mass among bundles may make an EFX plan feasible even when a particular finite cohort cannot realize it. Also, the paper’s continuous extension is not evidence by itself that this new problem is easier. The case survives because the proposed mirror keeps integral bundles and applies only in a regime with genuinely repeated valuation types and per-capita-scaled supplies; it is a distinct, precise high-multiplicity question, not a relabeling of the paper’s fractional relaxation.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The anchor does not establish the kind of computational result ChoCo is meant to mirror. Theorem 3 says that, for a finite instance, EFX existence is equivalent to \(\inf_y f(y)\le 0\). Lemma 2 and Theorem 4 give related optimization and fixed-point characterizations. None gives a complexity bound, an exact algorithm, or a hardness result. The paper’s DCA discussion does not fill that gap: it offers no global-optimality or running-time guarantee, only convergence to a stationary point. So the proposed mass-plan question is not a continuous mirror of a computational result in the paper.

The stronger version of the proposed mirror is coherent: use finitely many complete valuation types, scale supplies with population, and assign positive masses of agents integral bundles. That preserves the individual EFX comparison and has a plausible high-multiplicity interpretation. But it asks a new existence and complexity question. Theorem 3’s variables and equivalence do not supply its configuration set or solve its pricing problem; those depend on the new bundle and supply model. The paper offers no computational guarantee from which to derive them. That could be a worthwhile follow-on to EFX research, but it is not an anchor in this paper’s computational results.

The negative case is strongest on that scope point and weak as a universal claim. I cannot honestly rule out the mass-plan problem as a worthwhile research direction; its weakness is that it is motivated by the paper’s open EFX problem, rather than mirroring a complexity result the paper actually proves.
