The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set \(J\) of voucher categories with rational costs \(c_j\), a rational per-capita supply vector \(b\) with \(\sum_{j\in J} b_j=1\), and a rational population distribution \(\mu\) over a finite support of approval types, define \(u_t(z)=\sum_{j\in t}c_jz_j\) for integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{J}\). For each type \(t\), let \(M_t(b,c)\) be the supremum of \(v\) such that some distribution \(\pi\) over integral bundles has \(\mathbb E_\pi[z]=b\) and \(\pi(u_t(z)\ge v)=1\). Does there exist a direct mechanism, polynomial in the input bit length, that outputs polynomially represented bundle lotteries \((\pi_r^\mu)_{r\in\mathcal T}\) whose aggregate use is \(b\), gives every truthful type \(t\) its benchmark \(M_t(b,c)\) with probability one, is Pareto efficient among feasible measurable assignments, and satisfies \(\mathbb E_{\pi_t^\mu}[u_t(z)]\ge\mathbb E_{\pi_r^\mu}[u_t(z)]\) for every pair of types \(t,r\)?

The model it lives in

A continuum of residents with finitely many approval types and rational masses, receiving indivisible voucher bundles from rational per-capita supply. Within each type, mass can be distributed across bundle lotteries; the mechanism must meet type-specific MMS guarantees, aggregate feasibility, Pareto efficiency, and truthful reporting incentives.

The objection that survived

The finite picking sequence relies on two extra goods and privileged final agents, and the paper gives no limit or implementation result connecting it to menus over a continuum of types.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Proposition 2's joint strategyproofness, Pareto efficiency, and MMS guarantee. It leaves Theorems 1 and 2, Proposition 1, and Proposition 3 without continuous questions.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does such a polynomial-time mechanism exist, with complexity measured in \(|J|\), support size, and input bit length?
  • Can the finite \(n+2\)-good picking-sequence guarantee be recovered in any limit, or do the vanishing slack and priority structure prevent it?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest clean anchor is Proposition 2, proved in this paper. For \(n\) agents with cost utilities and \(n+2\) indivisible goods, it gives a picking sequence for Sequential Allocation that is strategyproof and returns a Pareto-efficient MMS allocation. Goods are ordered by cost. If \(c(g_4)>c(g_2)+c(g_3)\), the sequence is \(1,2,\ldots,n-1,n,n,n\); otherwise it is \(1,2,\ldots,n-1,n,n,n-1\). The paper presents this as a polynomial-time algorithm. The proposition’s contribution is the joint guarantee of strategyproofness, Pareto efficiency, and every agent receiving their maximin fair share in that \(n+2\)-good setting.

A plausible high-multiplicity setting is a large region allocating standardized, indivisible benefit vouchers to residents. Voucher categories have public costs; each resident either finds a category useful or does not. Millions of residents may fall into a small number \(\tau\) of approval types, while the number \(N\) of residents is large. Voucher stock scales with the population. In the regime of Proposition 2, there are \(N+2\) vouchers, so supply approaches one voucher per resident. This is a co-modification of the model forced by continuizing the population: the supply must scale too. Each resident still receives an integral bundle; only the distribution of bundles across a type’s mass can be fractional.

Here is the corresponding prover-ready question.

Unit-Supply High-Multiplicity MMS Mechanism. An instance specifies a finite set \(J\) of voucher categories, nonnegative rational costs \(c_j\), a finite report domain \(\mathcal T\) of approval sets, and a rational population distribution \(\mu\) over \(\mathcal T\), with support size \(\tau\). The per-capita supply vector \(b\) is rational and satisfies \(\sum_{j\in J} b_j=1\), representing limits of \(N+2\) indivisible vouchers for \(N\) residents. A resident of true type \(t\) values an integral bundle \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{J}\) at
\[ u_t(z)=\sum_{j\in t} c_jz_j. \]

Define the continuous MMS benchmark \(M_t(b,c)\) as the largest \(v\) for which there is a distribution \(\pi\) over integral bundles with \(\mathbb E_\pi[z_j]=b_j\) for every \(j\), and \(\pi\)-almost every bundle has \(u_t(z)\ge v\). It is the limiting version of partitioning the scaled stock into one bundle per resident and maximizing the least-valued bundle for type \(t\).

The question is whether there is a polynomial-time direct mechanism which, for every rational \(\mu\), returns a menu of bundle distributions \((\pi_r^\mu)_{r\in\mathcal T}\) such that:

  • under truthful reports, aggregate use is feasible and each type-\(t\) resident receives a bundle worth at least \(M_t(b,c)\) with probability one;
  • the resulting allocation is Pareto efficient among feasible measurable assignments of integral bundles; and
  • truthful reporting is dominant: for every pair of types \(t,r\), \(\mathbb E_{\pi_t^\mu}[u_t(z)]\ge \mathbb E_{\pi_r^\mu}[u_t(z)]\).

A single resident has zero mass, so a unilateral report changes their own menu choice while leaving the population distribution \(\mu\) unchanged. That makes the incentive condition meaningful at the individual level. The question does not impose strategyproofness against a positive-mass coalition.

I would initially expect this full mirror to be continuum-specifically hard (Class C). The continuous allocation side has a natural configuration formulation over integral bundles, but simultaneously satisfying MMS, Pareto efficiency, and incentive constraints across reports may create its own obstruction. A solution could instead uncover a tractable configuration or column-generation method, especially for fixed \(J\) and \(\mathcal T\). That is an open classification question, not a complexity conclusion from Proposition 2.

The paper’s Theorem 2 supports the plausibility of this domain: it proves that MMS allocations always exist for cost utilities with laminar approval sets, for any number of agents. It is proved here, but it asserts existence rather than a complexity class, so I would not use it as a computational anchor. The paper’s numbered results also include Proposition 3, which proves that for \(n=2,m=5\) there is an instance with cost utilities for which no strategyproof mechanism can guarantee a Pareto-efficient MMS allocation. That is a significant strategic impossibility, though it is not an NP-hardness result and I would not count it as a second computational anchor. The paper’s NP-hard MMS-value statement appears in the prose, not as a numbered hardness theorem.

The mirror covers Proposition 2’s combined strategic and fairness guarantee in its high-multiplicity regime. It does not extend the paper’s three-agent theorem or laminar-approval existence theorem to all continuous societies, and it does not continuize the outcome space: bundles remain indivisible. Its intended question is whether the per-capita society admits a strategyproof, Pareto-efficient MMS mechanism.

The weakest point is the anchor’s reliance on a finite picking order and exactly two extra goods. In the proposed limit, those two goods vanish per capita, and the priority order becomes continuous. The paper’s mechanism therefore does not establish the mirror or its polynomial-time solvability. The authors would still recognize the question’s underlying problem—cost-utility allocation with MMS, Pareto efficiency, and strategyproofness—but the continuous problem tests whether their guarantee survives after the resource slack and priority structure change.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that Proposition 2’s guarantee is tied to a very specific finite regime: \(n\) agents and \(n+2\) goods. Its picking sequence gives extra turns to the last one or two agents, and its branch depends on the costs of the first few goods. In a population limit, those agents and the two surplus goods have zero mass. The proposed mirror also replaces a deterministic picking mechanism with menus of bundle lotteries and expected-utility truthfulness. That is a real change in the object being studied. A direct transfer would need to show that finite clone instances converge while preserving MMS, Pareto efficiency, and incentives together.

That objection falls short of defeating the better mirror. Standardized vouchers, finitely many approval types, and stock that grows with the population give a plausible high-multiplicity regime. For rational masses, one can form finite clone instances with \(q\) agents and \(q+2\) goods; the paper’s guarantee applies to each. In the limit, integral bundles can remain individual outcomes, with type mass split across them. A menu deviation can also keep individual truthfulness meaningful even though one person has zero mass. Whether the guarantees admit a compact, efficient type-level implementation is precisely a computational question.

So the negative case is weak. It shows that Proposition 2 does not automatically yield the proposed continuous mechanism. It does not rule out a worthwhile mirror: the supply model is sensible, the source result is a named algorithmic guarantee, and the remaining limit and compression questions are substantial. I cannot honestly defeat this anchor.
