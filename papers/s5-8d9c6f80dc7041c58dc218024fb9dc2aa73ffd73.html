The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is narrow: this paper has a natural continuous-population version of its irrigation allocation problem, but it has no eligible named computational result to anchor that version. There is no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a complexity or algorithmic result. Table 1 summarizes axiomatic properties and attributes them to [34]; the paper’s allocation comparisons are reported results, not named computational results. So I cannot supply a valid anchor of the kind requested.

A plausible mirror of the underlying allocation task would take a large irrigation community in which many farmers share the same claim per farmer. A type \(t\) is a claim level \(c_t\), and \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of farmers with that claim. The instance gives the finitely supported rational distribution \(\mu\) and the available water per farmer \(e\), with \(0\le e\le\sum_t\mu_t c_t\). The question is to compute the per-farmer constrained equal awards allocation: output \(y_t=\min(c_t,\lambda)\), where \(\lambda\) is chosen so that \(\sum_t\mu_t y_t=e\). The mass of type \(t\) receives total water \(\mu_t y_t\). This is a population continuization: farmers remain the agents, while repeated claim classes are represented by mass. It does not make the outcome continuous in place of the population; the paper already treats water as divisible.

That scenario is plausible for a large irrigation district with recurring entitlement levels and many farmers per level. In the paper’s data there are 1,357 farmers and repeated claim values, though the number of distinct claims is not tiny. Under the proposed mirror, computing the CEA allocation is tractable by sorting the supported claim levels and finding the threshold \(\lambda\). The paper reports CEA’s favorable axiomatic and inequality comparisons, but it does not establish a complexity result for this calculation.

The limitation is central: the proposed problem formalizes computation that the paper does not study as a complexity question. Its contribution is to model a particular drought allocation and compare rules using axioms and inequality measures. A prover could analyze the continuous CEA calculation, but that would be a new algorithmic question attached to the paper’s model—not a continuous mirror of a named computational result. The mirror therefore has some model-level plausibility, but no qualifying anchor to support a stronger positive case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that this paper has no named computational result to mirror. Section 2 defines the claims rules; Table 1 summarizes axiomatic properties, drawing on [34]; Sections 3–4 apply the rules to the Riudecanyes data and compare their allocations. The paper states no theorem about computational complexity, algorithms, or an optimization problem. Under the requested anchor standard, that leaves no result for a continuous mirror to extend.

The proponent’s CEA model is nevertheless coherent. Group farmers by claim \(c_t\), let \(\mu_t\) be each group’s population share, and scale the water endowment to a per-farmer amount \(e\). The allocation \(y_t=\min(c_t,\lambda)\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t y_t=e\), genuinely continuizes the population. Repeated entitlement levels in a large irrigation district make the scenario plausible. Divisible water and the prospect of high multiplicity are no grounds to reject it.

The problem is that this model applies the CEA rule already defined in the paper to a distribution of claims. Computing that allocation could be a worthwhile new algorithmic study, but it is not a computational result established by this paper. Extending the input to a continuous claim distribution would add further modeling and representation choices without supplying the missing anchor. The paper’s axiomatic comparisons and reported statistics do not supply one either.

So the negative case is strong against treating this paper as an anchored ChoCo mirror. It is weaker as the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous question could arise from the paper’s subject: the mass-based CEA model is sensible, and studying it could be useful new work. I would reject the proposed mirror as a mirror of this paper, rather than claim that the continuous problem itself has no value.
