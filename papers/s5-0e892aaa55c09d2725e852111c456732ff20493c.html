The continuous mirror question

Given valuation types \(T\) with masses \(\mu_t\), item classes \(J\) with supplies \(q_j\), and a population scale \(N\) such that \(N\mu_t\) and \(Nq_j\) are integers, can one sample the paper’s exponential matching allocation and compute its type-dependent payments in time polynomial in \(\tau=|T|\), \(|J|\), the input bit length \(L\), \(\log N\), \(\varepsilon^{-1}\), \(\gamma^{-1}\), and \(\log(\delta^{-1})\)? The aggregate flow \(x_{tj}=y_{tj}/N\) has law proportional to \(\Omega_N(x)\exp((\varepsilon N/2)\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}v_t(j))\), where \(\Omega_N(x)=\prod_t(N\mu_t)!\prod_j(Nq_j)!/\prod_{t,j}y_{tj}!\); uniformly lifting the sampled flow to labeled matchings should give a \(\gamma\)-IC, \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-DP mechanism with the stated per-capita welfare guarantee.

The model it lives in

A matching market described by complete valuation types and their masses, with item-class supplies scaling with population size. Decisions are aggregate type-to-item flows \(x\), while each individual receives one integral item through a uniform lift; the finite-\(N\) flow multiplicity \(\Omega_N(x)\) captures the entropy contributed by labeled matchings.

The objection that survived

The proposed sampler still targets a finite-\(N\) distribution on integer flow tables, so the theorem’s individual-level guarantees do not themselves establish a fully atomless limit mechanism.

The case FOR (proponent)

The lead is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s unit-demand matching auction. It is a strong fit because large markets can have many participants but relatively few complete valuation types.

My anchor is Theorem 8, proved here, using the permanent FPRAS of Jerrum, Sinclair, and Vigoda [19]. It says that for any \(\delta\in(0,1)\), \(\varepsilon>0\), and \(\gamma>0\), the authors give a polynomial-time approximate implementation of the exponential mechanism for the multi-item auction, with running time polynomial in \(n,\varepsilon^{-1},\gamma^{-1},\log(\delta^{-1})\). The mechanism is \(\gamma\)-IC and \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-differentially private, and its welfare satisfies
\[ \Pr\!\left[\sum_i v_i(\widehat{\mathrm{Exp}}^{R_M}_\varepsilon) < \mathrm{opt}-\gamma n-\frac{\ln(n!)}{\varepsilon}-\frac{z}{\varepsilon}\right] \le e^{-z} \]
for every \(z\ge 0\). The approximation and payment-estimation details are in the paper’s appendix. The paper does not give a named computational-hardness theorem for this problem; its numbered lower bounds concern the privacy–welfare tradeoff, so I would not present them as hardness anchors.

Here is the mirror I would ask a prover to study: High-Multiplicity Private Matching Auction. An instance has a population scale \(N\), a set \(J\) of item classes, rational supplies \(q_j\) with \(\sum_j q_j=1\), and a support \(T\) of complete valuation types. Each type \(t\) has a valuation vector \(v_t:J\to[0,1]\); the society is \(\mu\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Require \(N\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\) and \(Nq_j\) copies of item class \(j\) to be integers. The type list and the proportions are encoded in binary, with support size \(\tau=|T|\). A report type includes every attribute the mechanism uses, so agents in a type are interchangeable for the problem.

A feasible aggregate allocation is a mass flow \(x_{tj}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_jx_{tj}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_tx_{tj}=q_j\). Its per-capita welfare is \(\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}v_t(j)\). Each person still receives one integral item; \(x_{tj}\) records the fraction of the population of type \(t\) assigned to class \(j\).

The question is whether, given these data and \(\varepsilon,\gamma,\delta\), one can produce a succinct sampler and payment rule for the paper’s exponential matching mechanism in time polynomial in \(\tau,|J|,L,\log N,\varepsilon^{-1},\gamma^{-1},\log(\delta^{-1})\), where \(L\) is the input bit length. On a reported profile, the target distribution on labeled matchings \(M\) has probability proportional to \(\exp((\varepsilon/2)\sum_i b_i(M))\). The sampler may return the aggregate flow \(X\), then lift it to a labeled matching by choosing uniformly among matchings with that flow. In particular, for \(x_{tj}=y_{tj}/N\), the number of labeled matchings represented by that flow is
\[ \Omega_N(x)=\frac{\prod_t(N\mu_t)!\prod_j(Nq_j)!}{\prod_{t,j}y_{tj}!}. \]
Thus the induced flow law is proportional to \(\Omega_N(x)\exp((\varepsilon N/2)\sum_{t,j}x_{tj}v_t(j))\). The solution must implement the paper’s type-dependent payments well enough that the induced direct mechanism is \(\gamma\)-IC and \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-DP for changes to one named agent’s report. Its normalized welfare guarantee should be
\[ \Pr\!\left[\frac{\mathrm{SW}}N< \mathrm{OPT}_\mu-\gamma-\frac{\ln(N!)}{\varepsilon N}-\frac{z}{\varepsilon N}\right]\le e^{-z}, \]
where \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mu\) is the maximum of the mass-flow LP above. The complexity target counts the compressed sampler and price oracle; materializing all \(N\) assignments naturally takes output-linear time.

The regime is a large matching market—say, standardized positions or service slots—with \(N\) applicants and \(N\) slots, but only \(\tau\ll N\) distinct valuation profiles and \(|J|\) slot classes. The exact type requirement matters: cohort members must share the full valuation vector and any other relevant attributes. This is the same unit-demand matching problem the authors study, with per-capita supplies and a mass representation added to express the high-multiplicity regime. That co-modification seems natural, rather than a change to the underlying question.

I expect Class A for a fixed-accuracy high-multiplicity version. For large \(N\), the logarithm of \(\Omega_N(x)\), divided by \(N\), yields an entropy term. The limiting allocation is therefore governed by a finite-dimensional entropy-regularized assignment problem over the \(\tau\times |J|\) flow matrix. That points toward continuous optimization and offers a credible route to compressed computation. But an optimizer is not enough: the mechanism needs a sampler and prices with error small enough to preserve its privacy and incentive guarantees.

The weakest point is that those guarantees protect a named individual, while the continuous input records only mass. Without a population scale \(N\), the privacy unit is unclear and an individual’s effect on aggregate allocation vanishes. Retaining \(N\) for one-agent deviations and lifting the aggregate output preserves the paper’s strategic and privacy questions, but means this is a high-multiplicity continuous mirror rather than a completely atomless model. A further question is whether the \(\Omega(\log N)\) privacy parameter needed for constant per-capita welfare loss in the paper’s finite market persists in this compressed regime.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The best case against this anchor is that Theorem 8’s computational object is a Gibbs distribution over labeled matchings, together with payments that make sampling truthful. A mass-flow LP captures the best welfare allocation, but it does not by itself capture the matching distribution or its payment rule. In the proposed mirror, those depend on the finite-\(N\) multiplicities \(\Omega_N(x)\), the integer contingency tables, and deviations by one named agent. If \(N\) is removed in taking the continuum limit, the flow distribution concentrates and the single-agent privacy and incentive guarantees are no longer the same questions. If \(N\) is retained, the core task is a high-multiplicity algorithm for a finite matching mechanism, rather than a continuous optimization problem of the kind the programme most directly targets.

The proponent’s stated welfare guarantee also becomes uninformative at fixed \(\varepsilon\): \(\ln(N!)/(\varepsilon N)\) grows like \((\ln N)/\varepsilon\). The paper’s Theorem 9 says that, in its general market, constant per-capita welfare loss requires \(\varepsilon=\Omega(\log N)\). But that lower bound does not defeat the best mirror: its hard instances use many distinct valuation types, so it does not settle what happens when \(\tau\ll N\). A sharper low-support analysis could still yield a useful result.

Those objections identify a real proof obligation, not a fundamental failure. With \(N\), supplies, and type counts retained, the proposed flow law and uniform lift can reproduce the original matching distribution when members of each type are interchangeable. The privacy unit and payments can then remain individual-level, while computation works on the compressed representation. That is exactly the kind of high-multiplicity bridge the programme allows; requiring output-linear time to assign named agents does not erase the value of compressed computation.

So I cannot honestly defeat Theorem 8’s anchor. The negative case is that this should not be presented as a simple LP mirror, and its value depends on whether one can compress the sampler and payment computation while preserving the mechanism’s guarantees. The paper’s own result makes that question concrete and plausible. Under the stated rule that one surviving anchor is enough, this paper should be green.
