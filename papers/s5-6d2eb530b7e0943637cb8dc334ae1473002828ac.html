The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s main point is decisive under ChoCo’s stated gate: this paper proves no computational complexity result. Its numbered propositions characterize convergence, steady states, and policy choices. That leaves no named computational problem for a continuous mirror to inherit. The paper’s continuous action \(x_i^t\in[0,1]\) does not change this: the population remains a finite set of individually networked agents.

Proposition 4 is the strongest candidate for an upgrade. It selects a named agent for the government leader to target by maximizing a Katz–Bonacich-based score. A continuous version could give mass to agent types and represent peer influence with a type-to-type kernel. But that would introduce a new continuum targeting problem, not mirror a computational result in the paper. And the network structure is the obstacle to making it a high-multiplicity problem: an agent’s influence depends on their links and paths through the graph. In a generic network, those roles differ, so treating the complete network role as part of an agent’s type leaves roughly one type per agent. A block model or other repeated-role assumption could make the support small, but that assumption supplies a new regime; it does not reveal a computational question the paper studied.

Proposition 6 is similarly promising as a policy problem, but not as a mirror. Given the network and a target norm vector, it gives the required incentives in closed form, \(\alpha^*=(1+\omega)(I-\theta G)s^*\). A continuum analogue would replace vectors and matrices with functions and an influence operator. That could be a sensible analytic model. To turn it into a computational question, one would need to add something such as constrained target selection or a budgeted policy optimization. The paper does not pose that problem: its formula calculates the incentives for a specified target. The added optimization would be a new contribution, not a continuous version of one of its computational results.

So the strongest honest case is that this paper is a poor candidate for a *mirror*: its network dependence makes generic high multiplicity hard to justify, and its results are analytic rather than computational. I would not claim that continuous network policy problems are worthless in every scenario. A structured large-population model might be worthwhile for ChoCo, but that would be a new direction grounded in additional assumptions, not a computational mirror of this paper.
