The continuous mirror question

Given finite type sets \(T_M,T_W\), rational masses \(p_i,q_j\), strict rankings of opposite-side types, and hidden utilities \(u_i(j),v_j(i)\) consistent with those rankings, define \(\mathcal S_\infty\) as the closure of aggregate pair-mass matrices of stable one-to-one matchings in rational blow-ups under a fixed refinement of within-type ties. An ordinal algorithm sees only the masses and type rankings and outputs an ex-post stable mass plan \(x\); determine \(\inf_A\sup_I \frac{\max_{y\in\mathcal S_\infty(I)}SW_I(y)}{SW_I(A(I))}\), in particular whether it equals \(2\).

The model it lives in

Two atomless unit-mass populations with finite complete applicant and position types, mass matrix \(x_{ij}\), hidden cardinal utilities \(u_i(j),v_j(i)\), and ex-post stability defined through aggregate limits of stable microscopic blow-ups; welfare is \(SW(x)=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}(u_i(j)+v_j(i))\).

The objection that survived

The aggregate stable-plan set depends on how strict rankings among same-type counterpart copies are refined, and the proponent does not fully specify that refinement; replication alone also adds no new asymptotic phenomenon.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers ordinal randomized distortion, endpoint \(2\)-guarantees, and type-level query tradeoffs. It leaves the deterministic ordinal impossibility, average-case theory, experiments, and detailed rotation-poset results largely unmirrored.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the ordinal type-level problem have optimal worst-case distortion exactly \(2\)?
  • Can \(\mathcal S_\infty\) and the endpoint mass matchings be represented and computed in polynomial time in the number of types and the mass encoding length?
  • What is the minimum number of adaptive value queries per type needed for a \(1+\varepsilon\) distortion guarantee?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a high-multiplicity stable-matching market in which the continuum turns the paper’s randomized outcomes into population-mass allocations.

Take two atomless populations, each of total mass \(1\): applicants and positions. There are finite type sets \(T_M\) and \(T_W\). A man type \(i\in T_M\) is a complete type: every member has the same strict ranking of woman types and the same cardinal utility vector \(u_i(j)\). Likewise, every woman type \(j\in T_W\) has ranking and utility vector \(v_j(i)\). The masses \(p_i\) and \(q_j\) are rational and sum to \(1\). Thus a finite realization with population size \(N\) contains \(Np_i\) identical applicants of type \(i\) and \(Nq_j\) identical positions of type \(j\), with \(N\gg |T_M|+|T_W|\).

A microscopic outcome is still an integral one-to-one matching of people. Its aggregate representation is a matrix \(x=(x_{ij})\), where \(x_{ij}\) is the mass of type-\(i\) applicants matched to type-\(j\) positions. The social welfare is

\[ SW(x)=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}\bigl(u_i(j)+v_j(i)\bigr). \]

To preserve the paper’s stability notion, I would use ex-post stable mass plans: a plan is a probability distribution over microscopic stable matchings, and \(x\) is its expected type-pair mass. Equivalently, for rational masses, one may use a sufficiently large finite blow-up and a finite-support distribution over stable matchings of that blow-up. This is not continuity of the outcome space in place of continuity of the population: the underlying assignments remain integral; only their aggregate mass is reported.

My lead problem is:

Continuous Rand-MoWo Distortion. Given the type masses and ordinal type rankings, compute the man-optimal and woman-optimal stable mass matchings, and output the ex-post stable plan that chooses each with probability \(1/2\). Is its worst-case distortion at most \(2\), where the benchmark is the highest welfare of any stable mass plan?

The paper’s anchor is Theorem 3.2, proved in this paper: “The distortion of the Rand-MoWo algorithm is at most \(2\).” Rand-MoWo outputs the man-optimal stable matching and the woman-optimal stable matching equiprobably. Its proof transfers directly after replacing sums over agents by mass-weighted sums. In the man-optimal matching, every man type receives at least as much utility as in any other stable matching; symmetrically, the woman-optimal matching dominates on the women’s side. Therefore the average welfare of the two endpoint matchings is at least half the welfare of the optimal stable matching.

This is a credible Class A mirror. A capacity version of deferred acceptance should compute the two endpoint mass matchings in time polynomial in the number of types and the encoding length of the rational masses. The genuine continuous-programme question is whether this remains true for a compact type-level representation, and whether the output plan admits a polynomial-size decomposition into stable microscopic assignments.

The matching lower-bound question is:

Continuous Ordinal \(2\)-Barrier. Over all algorithms that receive only the ordinal type rankings and masses and output an ex-post stable mass plan, determine the infimum worst-case distortion. In particular, does every such algorithm have distortion at least \(2\)?

The anchor is Theorem 3.3, proved in this paper: “Let \(A\) be an ordinal randomized stable algorithm. Then \(\operatorname{dist}(A)\ge 2\).” The theorem constructs a preference profile and a valuation profile under which any ordinal randomized algorithm loses a factor approaching \(2\) against the best stable matching. The proof actually uses less than stability: as noted in Remark 3, the lower bound applies even to ordinal randomized matching algorithms that may output unstable matchings.

The continuous version is not merely a restatement. In the finite paper, randomization is an algorithmic device. In the continuum, the same object is a deterministic population allocation at the aggregate level: half the mass follows one stable assignment and half follows the other, while each microscopic realization remains stable. The high-multiplicity bridge is exact. Take the paper’s cyclic construction, replicate every role \(K\) times, and let \(K\) tend to infinity. The number of people grows as \(K n\), while the number of complete types is only \(2n\). The lower-bound combinatorics live in the arrangement of preference types, not in the number of named individuals. I therefore expect the \(2\)-barrier to transfer; it is population-invariant rather than continuum-specific.

A third, more algorithmic question is:

Continuous Stable-TSF. Given the same type distribution, an \(\varepsilon\in(0,1]\), and adaptive query access that returns \(u_i(j)\) or \(v_j(i)\) when a type \(i\) queries a partner type \(j\), output an ex-post stable mass plan \(x\) satisfying

\[ SW(x)\ge \frac{1}{1+\varepsilon} \max_{y\in\mathcal S_\infty}SW(y). \]

What is the minimum number of value queries per type required in the worst case?

The anchor is Theorem 4.4, proved in this paper: for every \(\varepsilon\in(0,1]\), Stable-TSF performs at most

\[ \min\left\{n,\frac{4\log_2 n}{\varepsilon^2}\right\} \]

queries per agent and achieves distortion at most \(1+\varepsilon\). Stable-TSF first identifies stable partners, then uses binary searches on each agent’s value scale to create threshold buckets, constructs simulated utilities, and computes a welfare-maximizing stable matching under those simulated utilities.

The continuous analogue asks whether this compression can be done once per type rather than once per individual. The expected target is roughly \(O(\log \tau/\varepsilon^2)\) queries per type, where \(\tau=|T_M|+|T_W|\), rather than \(O(\log N/\varepsilon^2)\) queries per person. This looks Class A if the stable-partner structure and the optimal-stable-matching subroutine admit type-capacitated representations. The paper’s Theorem 4.3 generates a natural follow-up: does its \(\Omega(\log n)\) adaptive-query lower bound become an \(\Omega(\log \tau)\) lower bound per type in the high-multiplicity model? The path-structured result Theorem D.1 suggests a further \(O(\log\tau/\varepsilon)\) target for type-level rotation posets that are paths.

The scenario is plausible: a national placement market, residency allocation, or large standardized recruitment process can contain large cohorts of applicants and positions sharing complete preference and utility types. The type is not merely a ranking; it includes all information used by the problem, including cardinal values and query responses. Thus the model is genuinely high multiplicity rather than pretending that heterogeneous named agents are identical.

I would not claim that this paper supplies a hardness anchor in the usual NP-hardness sense. Its named results are distortion and information-complexity theorems. Nor would I use Theorem 3.1 as the lead: an atomless population naturally permits mass splitting, so the distinction between deterministic and randomized matching must be handled explicitly. That ex-post stability issue is the weakest point of the mirror. If a referee rejects ex-post stable mass plans and insists on a single aggregate matching with no blocking type pair across the entire population, Theorem 3.2 does not transfer automatically. Even so, the paper’s own randomized stable-matching model gives the continuous formulation a direct conceptual home, and the high-multiplicity setting turns that model into an economically meaningful mass-allocation problem rather than an artificial fractional relaxation.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the proposed mirrors do not actually arise from the paper’s primitive. Stable matching preferences are rankings over named counterpart agents. If \(N\) grows while the number of types remains fixed, identical agents cannot retain strict rankings over counterpart identities without adding identity-level tie-breaking data. A type-mass model therefore has to choose between two different objects: a blow-up with hidden individual labels, or a capacitated stable-matching model with type-level ties. The latter may be worthwhile, but it is a new model rather than a direct continuization.

This is fatal to the proponent’s formulation of Theorem 3.2 unless that choice is made explicitly. Under the blow-up interpretation, the result is just the finite theorem multiplied by mass: \(SW_N=N\,SW_1\), and the endpoint-dominance proof is unchanged. Under the type-level interpretation, “ex-post stable mass plan” is not determined by the type distribution alone: it depends on which microscopic stable matchings and tie-breakings are admitted. Calling the benchmark the convex hull of those matchings imports the missing individual structure; defining it as all stable type flows changes the stability notion. The proposed question is therefore either a scaled restatement or an under-specified new matching model.

Theorem 3.3 does not rescue the case. Its lower bound is driven by the number \(n\) of distinct cyclic roles, not by population multiplicity. Replicating each role \(K\) times merely multiplies every welfare contribution by \(K\); the lower-bound construction and its ratio are already present before continuization. To obtain the asymptotic factor \(2\), one must let the number of role types grow as well. Thus the claimed “continuum” contributes no limiting phenomenon. It is a finite type-indexed theorem with redundant copies.

The same problem affects Theorem 4.4. The paper’s query model asks for the value of one agent for one named counterpart. The proposed \(O(\log \tau/\varepsilon^2)\) bound instead grants a type query whose answer applies to an entire mass class. That is a materially stronger oracle, not the paper’s query model under a limit. If the original oracle is retained, the type-level compression is unavailable; if it is replaced by type queries, the result becomes an ordinary query problem on a smaller finite domain. The paper’s \(\Omega(\log n)\) lower bound consequently says nothing automatically about the proposed continuum.

This is still not a convincing universal negative. Large cohorts of applicants and positions with common rankings and utilities are plainly a sensible high-multiplicity regime, and a capacitated stable-flow model could be made precise. In that model, the mass version of the endpoint welfare inequality is mathematically natural, and the query problem may be genuinely useful. So the honest conclusion is that the proponent has not supplied a fully specified mirror, but I cannot defend the stronger claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. Theorem 3.2 survives once the type-level stability semantics are fixed; the case should therefore be graded green, with the main reservation being model specification rather than relevance.
