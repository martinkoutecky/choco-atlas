The continuous mirror question

Given two unit-mass populations with finite supported type sets \(U,V\), rational type masses \(p_u,q_v\), strict rankings over counterpart types, and unknown pairwise reward means \(a_{uv},b_{vu}\), each query of a supported pair \((u,v)\) returns a reward sample for each side. Determine the sample complexity needed to output, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), a type-level matching flow \(x\) maximizing \(\sum_{u,v}x_{uv}(a_{uv}+b_{vu})\), subject to \(\sum_v x_{uv}=p_u\), \(\sum_u x_{uv}=q_v\), \(x_{uv}\ge0\), and, for every \((u,v)\), the exact stability condition \(\bigl(\sum_{v':\,v'\succeq_u v}x_{uv'}=p_u\bigr)\lor\bigl(\sum_{u':\,u'\succeq_v u}x_{u'v}=q_v\bigr)\).

The model it lives in

An unknown-reward stable matching problem over two atomless populations represented by rational masses on complete preference and reward types. The decision variable is a nonnegative type-to-type matching flow with the prescribed marginals; stability means that for each type pair, at least one side has all its mass matched to that type or a preferred type. A query samples rewards for a selected supported type pair, and the objective is to learn a flow that is stable under the true rankings and maximizes total expected welfare.

The objection that survived

The proposed stability inequality admits flows with positive mass on both sides willing to block, so replacing it with the exact disjunction removes the claimed LP and means the proposed vertex-gap sample bound is unproved.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 3's utilitarian PAC guarantee. It leaves Theorems 1 and 2's horizon-regret guarantees and Theorem 4's maximin PAC guarantee aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • What is the sample complexity as a function of the support sizes, input bit length, preference gaps, welfare gaps, and \(\alpha\) under the stated type-pair sampling oracle?
  • Can welfare optimization over flows satisfying the exact stability disjunction be done efficiently, or does it introduce additional computational hardness?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest anchor is Theorem 3, proved in Appendix C. The paper gives no named NP-hardness or polynomial-time classification theorem; this is its explicit PAC sample-complexity result. It says that, in an \(N\)-by-\(N\) market with unknown utilities, ETC combined with the utilitarian-optimal stable-matching algorithm finds a utilitarian-optimal stable matching with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), using
\[ \frac{2N^2}{\beta^2}\log\frac{4N^2}{\alpha} \]
samples, where \(\beta=\min\{\delta/(4N),\Delta_a/2,\Delta_b/2\}\). Here \(\delta\) is the welfare gap to the second-best stable matching, and \(\Delta_a,\Delta_b\) are the minimum within-agent and within-arm preference gaps. This is a learning guarantee, rather than a theorem that the underlying problem is in \(P\).

A convincing high-multiplicity regime is a large placement market with repeated cohorts: many applicants and positions, but relatively few complete applicant and position types. A type includes its preferences and reward distributions against every type on the other side. Members of one type are indistinguishable in every feature used by the market. For instance, an applicant type might specify a shared distribution of satisfaction rewards across vacancy families, while a vacancy type specifies rewards across applicant profiles. Millions of participants with a few hundred such types would make type masses a natural description. This is a special market regime, not a claim about every hiring market.

Here is the mirror I would put to a prover: Continuous Utilitarian Stable-Matching PAC Learning. The instance has supported type sets \(U,V\), with \(|U|=\tau_A\), \(|V|=\tau_B\), and rational masses \(p_u,q_v>0\) summing to one on each side. For every supported pair \((u,v)\), members of \(u\) and \(v\) have unknown, independent, \(1\)-subgaussian reward distributions with means \(a_{uv}\) and \(b_{vu}\). These means induce strict rankings \(\succ_u\) over \(V\) and \(\succ_v\) over \(U\). A query selects a supported pair and returns one reward sample for each side.

The decision variable is a type-level matching flow \(x_{uv}\), where \(x_{uv}\) is the fraction of population matched between types \(u\) and \(v\). It must satisfy
\[ \sum_v x_{uv}=p_u,\qquad \sum_u x_{uv}=q_v,\qquad x_{uv}\ge 0. \]
Call the flow stable when, for every \(u,v\),
\[ x_{uv}+\sum_{v':\,v'\succ_u v}x_{uv'} +\sum_{u':\,u'\succ_v u}x_{u'v}\ge p_u+q_v. \]
Among these stable flows, utilitarian welfare is
\[ W(x)=\sum_{u,v}x_{uv}(a_{uv}+b_{vu}). \]
After making queries, the algorithm must output a flow that is stable under the true rankings and maximizes \(W\), with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). At the individual level, every participant still receives one partner; the flow records how the population of each type is distributed across partner types.

The natural prediction is tractable. Let \(P=\tau_A\tau_B\), and let \(\Delta_A,\Delta_B\) be the minimum within-type ranking gaps. For the continuous version of Theorem 3’s welfare-gap parameter, define \(g\) as the gap between the optimum welfare and the best strictly lower-welfare vertex of the stable-flow polytope. Set \(g=+\infty\) if every vertex is optimal, and let
\[ \beta_\infty=\min\{g/4,\Delta_A/2,\Delta_B/2\}. \]
The type-pooled analogue to investigate is a bound of
\[ \frac{2P}{\beta_\infty^2}\log\frac{4P}{\alpha} \]
pair queries: sample each supported type pair uniformly often, estimate both means, and optimize the estimated welfare over the estimated stable-flow polytope. Correct rankings preserve the feasible polytope; the vertex gap controls whether estimation preserves its optimal face. The optimization step is a linear program with \(O(P)\) variables and stability constraints, so its size depends on the type supports and input bit length, rather than on the number of people.

The paper’s authors should recognize this as their problem: it keeps unknown two-sided rewards, stable matching, utilitarian welfare, and sampling. The masses govern capacities, stability, and welfare. Type-level fractional flows arise from making the population continuous; they do not make the outcome of an individual divisible. This covers Theorem 3’s utilitarian PAC result. It does not claim to mirror the horizon-regret guarantees in Theorems 1 and 2, whose per-round feedback needs a separate scaling choice.

The weakest point is the exact-optimizer margin. The paper’s \(\delta\) separates finitely many discrete stable matchings; a continuous stable-flow polytope has infinitely many feasible points, so the direct analogue must compare optimal and suboptimal vertices. The result also assumes that the learner can obtain samples from every supported type pair. In a market with passive interaction logs, rare pairs may instead impose costs depending on their masses. Those are real conditions on this mirror, though they leave a recognizable question: when repeated types are the market’s natural units, can pooled feedback and LP optimization replace dependence on the individual population size?

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 3 is the only anchor, and the proposed mirror’s claimed LP is not yet a faithful continuous version of the paper’s stability condition. With type masses \(p_u,q_v\), no blocking pair requires that either all mass of type \(u\) be matched to \(v\) or a type it prefers, or all mass of type \(v\) be matched to \(u\) or a type it prefers. That is a disjunction, not the proposed inequality with right-hand side \(p_u+q_v\).

For example, take two types on each side, each of mass \(1/2\), and match \(u_1\) to \(v_2\) and \(u_2\) to \(v_1\). Let \(u_1\) prefer \(v_2\) to \(v_1\), \(v_1\) prefer \(u_1\) to \(u_2\), and the other two types prefer their assigned partners. The matching is stable: neither unmatched pair blocks. But for \((u_1,v_1)\), the proposed inequality has left side \(1/2\) and right side \(1\), so it rejects this stable matching. Using that LP would answer a different optimization problem.

There is also a modeling choice hidden in the PAC guarantee. If every repeated participant supplies independent feedback each round, growing the cohort supplies growing numbers of observations, so the learning cost changes with population scale. If instead one charges one query per type pair, even for a type of arbitrarily small mass, that is a useful sample-oracle model, but it abstracts away from the paper’s per-round market interactions. Neither choice is forced by Theorem 3.

These points weaken the stated argument for the mirror, but they do not defeat its best repair. Exact continuum stability can be stated with the disjunction above, and learning a shared reward matrix from type-pair samples remains a sensible PAC question. Its optimizer may be harder than an LP, which is itself a computational question the programme could study. I cannot honestly make the universal negative case: the proposed linear formulation is flawed, but the corrected high-multiplicity mirror is still credible.
