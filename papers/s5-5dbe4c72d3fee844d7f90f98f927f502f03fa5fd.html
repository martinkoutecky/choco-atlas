The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is that the *allocation problem* has a plausible population version: in a large organization assigning shares of recurring tasks, let a type be a complete single-dipped preference over an individual’s share, and let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of staff with type \(t\). A candidate problem is: given finitely many such types, rational masses, and a fixed per-capita supply \(q\in(0,1)\), find a type-level allocation \(x_t\in[0,1]\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t x_t=q\) that is Pareto efficient and envy-free among type-level allocations. The supply must scale with the population to avoid a degenerate limit. This is a plausible research question, but it is not an algorithmic result established by this paper.

There is no qualifying computational anchor in the paper: none of its numbered results asserts a complexity classification or gives an algorithm. The nearest result is Theorem 5, proved here. It characterizes Pareto-optimal envy-free allocations: if some agent strictly prefers \(1\) to \(0\), there must be a set \(S\) receiving \(1/|S|\) each, with every member’s sharing index at least \(|S|\), while everyone outside \(S\) receives \(0\) and has sharing index below \(|S|\). If nobody prefers \(1\) to \(0\), but someone is indifferent between them, an endpoint-indifferent agent receives \(1\). If everyone strictly prefers \(0\) to \(1\), envy-freeness and Pareto optimality require everyone to receive \(1/|N|\), and each must prefer that share to \(1\). This is a substantive characterization, but it does not say whether computing such an allocation is in P, hard, or otherwise computationally classified.

The central weakness of the proposed mirror is that scaling supply changes the feasible set and the share thresholds in Theorem 5. Keeping the paper’s one total unit while making the population atomless instead drives the equal share toward zero. Also, although the commodity is divisible in the paper, its society remains finite; outcome-space continuity alone is not a continuous-population result.

So the paper offers a credible *setting* for a future population continuization, but no named computational result to anchor the requested case. The per-capita envy-free allocation question above is exploratory, not a faithful mirror of a computational finding in the paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that this paper has no computational result to continuize. Theorem 5 characterizes Pareto-optimal envy-free allocations, and Corollary 5 gives an existence condition; the other numbered results likewise characterize allocations or compatibility. None studies the complexity of deciding existence or computing an allocation. So a continuous version of Theorem 5 would extend an axiomatic characterization, not chart the computational landscape that ChoCo is meant to fill.

The proposed department model is coherent, and scaling supply with population avoids the degeneration of keeping one unit fixed. But its question—whether a type distribution admits a Pareto-optimal, envy-free allocation, or how to find one—adds a computational task the paper never posed. A stronger candidate would continuousize Corollary 5 directly: replace the finite coalition size \(|S|\) and sharing index over integer sizes with a coalition mass \(\alpha\) and a corresponding share \(q/\alpha\). That is a reasonable new model, but it requires choices about which preference data are encoded, whether allocations are uniform within each type, and how envy and Pareto efficiency apply to measurable groups. Those choices define a new computational problem; they do not reveal a computational result already in the paper.

This is not a claim that the resulting problem is uninteresting or that authors would reject it. The negative case is strong on fit with the programme, but weak as a universal claim: a continuous allocation problem with finitely represented preference types could be a worthwhile project in its own right. The paper itself, however, supplies no computational anchor for it.
