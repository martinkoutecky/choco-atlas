The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest plausible continuous angle is to represent a large electorate by a distribution \(\mu\) over ranking types, then study whether abstaining mass \(\alpha\) of type \(t\) changes the winner to an outcome those voters prefer. A setting with many voters but relatively few ballot types makes those proportions natural. The paper’s no-show and P-BOT examples give this angle something concrete to investigate.

But I find no eligible anchor for it. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive negative is that this paper has no computational result to mirror. The authors describe their contribution as conceptual and theoretical. Its results concern voting axioms, paradoxes, power measures, and relations among them; it gives no computational problem with an algorithm, complexity bound, or hardness result.

The strongest version of the proposed no-show mirror is sensible as a *new* question: given a distribution over ranking types, find the smallest mass whose abstention changes the winner to an outcome that mass prefers. A similar threshold question could be asked for the paper’s Kemeny P-BOT example. But those formulations add an optimization or search task to results that ask whether a voting rule has a property across profiles. Moulin’s and Pérez’s cited results are axiomatic impossibility and classification results, not computational ones; the Kemeny passage is a witness, not an algorithmic claim. The paper gives no reason to treat those newly posed tasks as its results continued into a limit.

A high-multiplicity electorate is quite plausible for the no-show question: the paper’s own examples group voters by ballot. That is the weak point in a broad claim that no continuous population model could be worthwhile. But it does not supply the missing computational anchor. The voting-power discussion offers no stronger one: its indices count players, coalitions, or permutations, and a continuum version would need a new way to define power for mass, rather than continuing a computational result from this paper.

So I would reject this paper as a continuous-mirror candidate for ChoCo’s complexity programme. I would not claim that every adjacent computational question is unworthy; the no-show threshold problem could be worth exploring on its own. That would be a new project motivated by the paper’s topic, not a mirror of one of its contributions.
