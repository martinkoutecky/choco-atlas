The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The affirmative case identifies the decisive gap: it offers no named computational result to mirror. The paper studies a multistage citizen-candidate game in which citizens decide whether to run and then vote; its stated contribution concerns forward induction and Mertens stable sets. A generic question about computing stable sets could be attached to many equilibrium papers, but that would be a new computational project, not a mirror of a result here. [Publisher’s description](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0899825603001489)

The strongest version of the proposed direction would give voter types masses \(\mu\), retain a finite set of possible candidates, and ask how the stable-set analysis varies with those masses. That is a coherent hybrid model, and a high-multiplicity electorate may be sensible. But the paper’s forward-induction question turns on an individual citizen’s discrete decision to enter. In an atomless society, a citizen has zero voting mass; preserving their discrete candidacy decision requires keeping candidate citizens as separate players. Alternatively, letting a type’s mass enter changes the deviation being assessed from an individual’s entry to mass-level entry. Either formulation may motivate new work, but neither supplies a computational anchor from this paper.

The negative case is strong against calling this paper a ChoCo mirror. It is weaker as the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous computational project could grow from it: the hybrid model could be interesting to game theorists. The paper itself gives no reason to treat that added computation as part of its contribution.
