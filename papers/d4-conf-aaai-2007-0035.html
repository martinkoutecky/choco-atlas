The case FOR (proponent)

The paper has computational content, but no qualifying named anchor: in the supplied text, the only numbered formal result is Definition 1. The LP formulations, optimization routines, complexity discussion, and empirical findings are presented in prose, not as a numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a complexity classification. The theorem-format formulation exception does not apply because there is no such numbered theorem here. So I can’t honestly name a lead anchor or write one continuous problem per anchor.

The nearest unanchored research lead would be population partial-revelation AMD: take a large exchangeable customer population with a finite set \(T\) of complete utility types and rational masses \(\mu_t\); choose a partial-type reporting scheme and an anonymous mechanism that assigns outcomes and transfers, optimizing a stated objective subject to \(\varepsilon\)-IC, \(\varepsilon\)-IR, and a communication budget. That has a plausible high-multiplicity regime when the population is large and the number of utility classes is small.

But the bridge is uncertain. The paper’s agents are finitely many and named; its mechanisms act on their report vectors. A nonatomic population changes the effect of an individual report on aggregate reports, and the paper’s finite-agent LPs do not establish the complexity of that new problem. Also, its continuous utility domains and priors are not themselves continuous populations. Under your anchor requirement, this is a possible research question, not a supported positive case for this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I would vote against treating this as a strong ChoCo mirror. The proponent supplied no named computational result to anchor one: the only numbered formal item is Definition 1. The paper gives LP formulations, routines, and empirical comparisons, but no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition establishing a complexity result. Its discussion of avoiding exponential growth counts variables after \(s\) partition-refinement steps; it does not establish how many steps suffice for a target guarantee. That leaves no specific computational claim for the programme to continuize.

The proposed population PR-AMD question is the best candidate, and a plausible setting exists: many exchangeable users drawn from a small set of complete utility types, with a common partial-report scheme and a mechanism for allocating a service whose capacity scales with the population. But that would be a newly specified large-market mechanism-design problem. In the paper, a mechanism maps a vector of individual reports to allocations and payments, and incentive constraints compare an agent’s reports while holding the others’ reports fixed. An aggregate distribution of reports does not by itself retain that tagged-agent comparison. If outcomes depend only on aggregate reports, one person’s deviation leaves the distribution unchanged. To keep the incentive question meaningful, the model needs an individual’s report and utility alongside the population state, plus a rule for that individual’s allocation and payment. That is a coherent model, but it changes the strategic object and the optimization variables the paper studies.

The continuous utility domains and priors in the paper do not supply the missing population limit: they describe uncertainty about finitely many agents. Nor do the empirical examples supply it; they concern two agents, with the auction example already using a known exact-IC construction for that restricted setting. None of this rules out a worthwhile large-market PR-AMD project. It does make the connection to this paper’s results indirect: the work would be to define and analyze a new anonymous mechanism-design problem, rather than to continue a computational result established here.

So the negative case is strongest as a judgment about fit: this paper offers a promising topic, but no qualifying named result for a continuous mirror, and its best replacement requires a substantive change to the strategic model. The universal claim that no scenario could make that new problem worthwhile is weaker; an anonymous, tagged-agent large-market model is plausible.
