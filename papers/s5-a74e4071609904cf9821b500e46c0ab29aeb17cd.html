The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is that this matching model has a plausible high-multiplicity regime. In a large labor market, many employers could share the same preferences and per-trade financial limits, and many workers could share the same preferences. Treat each complete employer or worker profile as a type, with its population share as mass. A natural follow-up would ask for a quantity-constrained competitive equilibrium consisting of type-pair prices, availability expectations, and matching flows. Each type’s mass could split among its utility-maximizing trades and no-trade option, while each employer and worker type’s matched mass stays within its supply. That is a genuine population continuization; the paper’s continuous contract payments do not themselves make its population continuous.

But I cannot offer that as a mirror anchored in this paper’s computational results: the paper has no named result classifying a computational problem as tractable or hard. Proposition 3.4 says a stable outcome exists; Corollary 4.4 says a QCCE exists; Theorem 5.1 says the nonempty sets of stable and QCCE outcomes coincide; Proposition 5.5 says those sets also coincide with the core. These are existence and characterization results, not complexity results. Lemma 3.3 proves finite termination of an adjustment process on a discretized payment set, but gives no complexity bound for computing an outcome in the original model.

So there is no qualifying anchor and no continuous problem per anchor to hand to a prover under your criteria. The mass-flow QCCE question is a credible direction for a computational follow-up, but whether it has an efficient algorithm, and whether the paper’s equivalences survive in that model, would need new results. I would not assign it a tractability or hardness class from this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper offers no named computational result to anchor a ChoCo mirror. Proposition 3.4 proves stable-outcome existence; Corollary 4.4 proves QCCE existence; Theorem 5.1 and Proposition 5.5 identify equivalent solution concepts. The lattice and rural-hospital results are also structural. Lemma 3.3 gives finite termination only on a discretized payment grid; the original-model existence proof then takes a limit, with no complexity bound.

The proponent’s type-level mass-flow QCCE is nevertheless a credible population continuization. In a large labor market, types can include preferences and financial constraints, and flows can represent how much of each type matches with another. Identity need not be the obstacle: the paper’s stability and equilibrium notions already depend on agents’ characteristics, so complete types can preserve them. The problem is that “compute a QCCE” would be a new algorithmic question, not a mirror of a computational result in this paper. Its utility functions are arbitrary continuous functions, with no finite input encoding or bit-length parameter. Restricting them to, say, rational piecewise-linear utilities could make a complexity question precise, but that would supply new computational content the paper does not provide. Mirroring its existence, equivalence, lattice, or rural-hospital results would remain a structural question rather than the computational question this programme targets.

So the best negative case is narrow but solid: this paper supplies no paper-anchored computational mirror. The broader claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario is weak. The labor-market regime is plausible, and a computational study of a suitably encoded mass-flow model may be worthwhile; it would simply be a follow-up problem rather than a continuization of a named computational result here.
