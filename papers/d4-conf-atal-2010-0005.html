The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t supply a qualifying anchor. The paper has no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a computational result. Its closest candidate is the winner-determination LP in equations (4)–(7), which D’ACCORD computes with a standard LP solver. But that is not a numbered result, and its fractional variables describe divisible energy allocations, not a continuous population. I would not present it as an anchor.

The strongest honest positive case is therefore an unanchored candidate mirror of that market-clearing problem:

Type-Aggregated Welfare Clearing. At one clearing interval, each type \(t\) records an agent’s side of the market, reservation price \(v_t\), and requested or offered quantity \(q_t\). The society is a finite-support distribution with rational masses \(\mu_t\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). For buyer types \(b\) and seller types \(s\), choose \(x_{bs}\ge 0\), the energy allocated from seller type \(s\) to buyer type \(b\) per average agent in the society. Maximize \(\sum_{b,s}(v_b-v_s)x_{bs}\), subject to \(\sum_s x_{bs}\le\mu_bq_b\) for every buyer type and \(\sum_b x_{bs}\le\mu_sq_s\) for every seller type. A solution is an optimal allocation \(x\).

This preserves the underlying question in equation (4): how to allocate energy to maximize total surplus from the submitted bids. A plausible regime is a large retail aggregation of households and small producers using standardized device configurations, tariffs, and bidding policies: millions of agents, but far fewer distinct bid types in a clearing interval. The authors already describe agents representing consumers, producers, and prosumers, and devices that share policies; they could plausibly recognize clearing over masses of identical bids as their market’s high-multiplicity version.

This candidate is an LP with at most \(\tau_B\tau_S\) allocation variables, so it is tractable in the support size and input bit length. That is a proposed consequence of the formulation, not a result proved by the paper. Its scope is only the welfare-allocation kernel. It does not mirror the paper’s generic strategy framework, platform implementation, or claims about incentive-compatible payments. Those raise further questions: whether prices and payments can be defined consistently for type masses, and how the mass model relates exactly to a finite market with repeated agents.

The weak point is also the reason this cannot carry the requested anchored case: the paper studies market clearing over submitted bids, but proves no numbered complexity result about that problem. And while bid type may be complete for clearing, it does not capture everything relevant to the paper’s broader agent architecture.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that the paper proves no numbered computational result. Equations (4)–(7) define a winner-determination LP, but the paper presents it as part of a market mechanism; it does not establish a complexity result or an algorithmic boundary. The rest of the contribution is an agent-strategy framework and its platform implementation. So there is no theorem here for a continuous mirror to reproduce.

The proposed Type-Aggregated Welfare Clearing is a better candidate than a literal continuization of the paper’s agents. But it is a newly posed problem, not a mirror of a result the paper claims. It preserves the welfare-allocation objective while leaving out the paper’s incentive-compatible payment rule and its policy-driven agent strategies. Its LP is also just the paper’s allocation LP expressed over repeated bid types. That makes the candidate look more like a compact reformulation of the clearing model than a complexity question exposed by continuizing the population.

That is a scope objection, not a decisive one. Repeated households or producers with the same bid, quantity, and policy form a plausible high-multiplicity regime, and clearing over their masses is a well-posed computational question. Because the authors already discuss aggregated devices and large populations, I cannot honestly rule out their recognizing it as their problem. The negative case can reject an *anchored* claim about this paper, but it is weak against the broader claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
