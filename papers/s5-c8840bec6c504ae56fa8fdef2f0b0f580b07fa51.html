The case FOR (proponent)

My strongest honest case is a qualified one, anchored on Theorem 3, and it is weaker than the programme’s usual complexity anchors. The paper contains no named \(P\), NP-hardness, FPT, or approximation theorem. Its strongest usable result is instead a mechanism-existence impossibility.

Lead mirror: Continuous Non-wasteful Truthful Envy-free Chore Mechanism. The paper’s Theorem 3, proved in this paper, states:

There does not exist a deterministic, truthful, envy-free chore-cutting mechanism satisfying non-wastefulness, even with two players.

The result is for piecewise-constant chore valuations and entire allocation of the chore. It does not assume connected pieces, dictatorship, or hungry agents. That makes it a better anchor than Theorems 1, 2, and 4, which impose additional structural restrictions.

The continuous population would be a large workforce divided into a few repeated valuation cohorts: for example, many employees in two standardized job classes who have the same disutility profile over task categories. A type \(t\) is a complete piecewise-constant density function \(f_t:[0,1]\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), including all relevant zero-value regions. The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over a finite type set \(T\), with \(\tau=|T|\ll N\); \(N\mu_t\) is the number of workers of type \(t\) in a finite realization.

The chore is normalized per capita. An allocation is a measurable type-level allocation kernel \(x_t(z)\), where \(x_t(z)\) is the normalized amount of chore location \(z\) assigned to a member of type \(t\). Aggregate feasibility is \( \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_t(z)=1 \) almost everywhere. A type-\(t\) worker’s disutility is \( V_t(x)=\int_0^1 f_t(z)x(z)\,dz \).

A deterministic direct mechanism must assign a bundle \(x_{\hat t,\mu}\) to a tagged worker reporting \(\hat t\), while the aggregate distribution remains \(\mu\), as is standard in an atomless population. The required conditions are:

  • truthfulness: \(V_t(x_{t,\mu})\le V_t(x_{\hat t,\mu})\) for every possible report \(\hat t\);
  • envy-freeness: \(V_t(x_{t,\mu})\le V_t(x_{u,\mu})\) for every type \(u\) present in the population;
  • non-wastefulness: \(x_{t,\mu}(z)=0\) wherever \(f_t(z)=0\);
  • entire allocation: all chore mass is assigned.

The problem is:

Given a finite catalogue of piecewise-constant valuation types \(T\), does there exist one deterministic mechanism \(M\) satisfying these conditions for every rational population distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\)? If not, produce a finite-support distribution and a proof of incompatibility.

This is a genuine population continuization rather than a restatement of cake divisibility. The chore is already divisible in the paper; the new continuous object is the society. The natural high-multiplicity regime is many workers in a small number of standardized cohorts, with type masses representing workforce proportions. The original authors should recognize the model: it preserves their underlying question—whether incentive compatibility and envy-freeness can coexist under restrictions on chore assignment—while replacing named-agent multiplicity by type mass.

I would expect the general mirror to remain impossible, but not because NP-hardness transfers. The obstruction is likely continuum-specific: non-wastefulness interacts with type-dependent zero-value regions and aggregate feasibility. On the other hand, the impossibility can disappear on the hungry subdomain. If every \(f_t(z)>0\), assigning every type the same normalized bundle \(x_t\equiv 1\) is immediately truthful and envy-free. Thus the interesting continuous question is precisely which zero-patterns admit a feasible mechanism and whether that characterization is tractable.

The major weakness is strategic. In a nonatomic population, one agent’s report has zero mass, so truthfulness no longer changes the aggregate distribution. Truthfulness therefore becomes a type-level menu condition and partly collapses into envy-freeness. The paper’s finite-agent proof relies on cross-profile effects, and its argument does not automatically survive this limit. A stronger follow-up would require truthfulness against positive-mass coalitions, or an \(\varepsilon\)-mass deviation model, but that would be a deliberate strengthening rather than a literal mirror.

There is also a serious textual caveat: in the printed proof of Theorem 3, the density \(h_1\) is zero on \(A_1\), yet the proof says non-wastefulness forces agent 1 to receive \(A_1\); under the paper’s own definition, non-wastefulness appears to force the opposite. I would therefore present this as a mirror of the paper’s claimed Theorem 3, not as an independently verified impossibility theorem. The continuous question itself is plausible, but this paper is not a strong ChoCo complexity anchor.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is strong, but it is about the paper’s kind of result rather than about repeated chore-workers being implausible.

Theorem 3 is not a computational result. It gives no finite input model, output problem, complexity parameter, algorithm, approximation guarantee, or decision procedure. It is an axiomatic nonexistence theorem for a mechanism over named agents. Replacing the finite electorate by a distribution does not make it a ChoCo complexity question. One could invent a problem asking whether a mechanism exists for a given type catalogue, but that would be a new mechanism-design problem, not a computational mirror of Theorem 3.

More importantly, the proposed mirror changes the strategic object at its centre. In the paper, a report by agent 1 changes the entire allocation, including agent 2’s piece. The proof exploits exactly this profile-level effect. In an atomless society with fixed \(\mu\), one individual’s report has zero mass and cannot change \(\mu\) or the aggregate allocation. Truthfulness becomes a menu condition for a representative type, \(V_t(x_{t,\mu})\le V_t(x_{\hat t,\mu})\), rather than the finite-agent incentive constraint used in the paper.

There are repairs, but each changes the problem materially. Requiring a positive-mass cohort to misreport gives a coalitional or type-level strategyproofness notion absent from the paper. Treating each type as one weighted agent gives a finite weighted chore-division problem. Neither is the individual dominant-strategy question in Theorem 3. Conversely, if \(x_t(z)\) is only an average allocation, it need not preserve envy-freeness or truthfulness for the actual individuals. If it is an identical fractional or lottery bundle for every member of a type, the model has also introduced outcome-space randomization/fractionalization that is doing substantial work.

The finite-type formulation has a further problem. The theorem’s witness is mechanism-dependent: \(h_1\) is defined using the set \(A_1\) produced by the mechanism on the uniform profile. A fixed finite catalogue \(T\) cannot generally contain such a witness chosen after the mechanism is known. Including all piecewise-constant valuations instead makes the type space infinite and leaves no finite encoding for the proposed existence problem. Restricting the catalogue restores finiteness only by creating a new, catalogue-specific question with no result in this paper to mirror.

The printed proof also contains a substantive reversal: when \(h_1=0\) on \(A_1\), non-wastefulness forbids assigning \(A_1\) to agent 1; it does not force that assignment. The impossibility may be repairable, but the paper as written supplies no reliable computational anchor in any event.

A stronger programme could study repeated valuation cohorts, cohort deviations, or finite-\(N\) approximations with a chosen incentive scaling. Those may be worthwhile new projects. But they do not provide a continuous computational mirror of this paper. The proponent has shown that a plausible repeated-cohort story exists; they have not shown that this axiomatic impossibility can be converted into the population-continuum complexity question ChoCo is meant to study.
