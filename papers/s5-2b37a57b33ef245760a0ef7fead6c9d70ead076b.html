The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a substantive mirror, but not a formally qualifying ChoCo case: this paper contains no named theorem or proposition proving NP-hardness, membership in \(P\), FPT, approximation, or an LP/MILP formulation. Its results are structural, axiomatic, and worst-case welfare statements. The algorithms for TTC and Crawler are described, and complexity claims appear only through cited work. Thus there is no qualifying computational anchor under the programme’s strict rule.

The best substantive anchor is the theorem in Section 6.2 labelled theo:PO in the supplied source; the pasted version suppresses its printed numeral. It is proved in this paper. Its full content is: if \(I\) is a single-peaked housing-market instance, then every allocation stable with respect to improving swap-deals, that is, stable with respect to \(C_2\), is also stable with respect to \(C_n\), where \(C_k\) contains all improving cycle-deals of size at most \(k\). Since Pareto-optimality is equivalent to \(C_n\)-stability in a housing market, the following corollary says that, in a single-peaked housing market, an allocation is Pareto-optimal if and only if it is stable with respect to bilateral swaps.

A natural mirror would be the following.

Call it Continuous Mass-Swap Pareto Certification. Let \(R=\{r_1,\ldots,r_q\}\) be resource classes with a common axis \(r_1\lhd\cdots\lhd r_q\). Let \(\Theta\) be a finite set of complete agent types. A type \(\theta\) contains a strict single-peaked ranking \(\succ_\theta\) and an initial resource class \(e_\theta\). It has rational population mass \(\mu_\theta\), with \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta=1\). Resource class \(r\) has supply \(\rho_r=\sum_{\theta:e_\theta=r}\mu_\theta\).

A state is a matrix \(y_{\theta r}\ge 0\), where \(\sum_r y_{\theta r}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta y_{\theta r}=\rho_r\). The quantity \(y_{\theta r}\) is the mass of type-\(\theta\) agents receiving one indivisible unit of class \(r\); no individual receives a fractional house.

A mass swap chooses types \(\theta,\phi\), resource classes \(r,q\), and \(0<\varepsilon\le\min(y_{\theta r},y_{\phi q})\) such that \(q\succ_\theta r\) and \(r\succ_\phi q\). It transfers \(\varepsilon\) units of mass in both directions. The decision problem is: given \(y\), either output such a mutually improving mass swap, or certify that \(y\) is Pareto-optimal among all feasible mass assignments. Pareto dominance is defined agentwise through a coupling: nobody may receive a worse resource, and a positive mass must receive a strictly better one.

This is recognisably the authors’ problem. Their “agents meet in pairs and exchange resources when both benefit” becomes exchange of positive mass between two cohorts. The many agents could be households in a large public-housing exchange, with perhaps \(10^4\) households but only \(q=10\)–\(30\) interchangeable housing classes and \(\tau\) preference/endowment cohorts. A type is complete because it includes both the full preference order and initial resource class. The high-multiplicity regime is therefore \(N\gg\tau\), not an artificial claim that individually distinct households have identical preferences.

I would expect this mirror to be Class A. Swap stability can be checked by scanning all \((\theta,\phi,r,q)\). Pareto dominance can be tested by a transportation LP over variables describing how mass currently holding \(r\) is reassigned to \(q\). For rational inputs, clearing denominators converts the instance into an ordinary finite housing market, so the paper’s theorem implies the continuous statement exactly. The new computational question is whether this certification and, more ambitiously, computation of terminal outcomes can be done in time polynomial in \((q,\tau,L)\), independent of the named population size.

The weakest point is substantial: the original model has \(n\) individually owned resources. Replacing them by exchangeable resource classes is legitimate only in a setting such as a large housing estate with many equivalent units. If exact addresses, ownership identities, or idiosyncratic features matter, they belong in the type description and the high-multiplicity advantage disappears. Moreover, because the paper proves no computational result, this is a natural continuous-population reformulation of a structural theorem, not a genuine continuization of a complexity result.

The mirror covers only the Section 6.2 swap-stability/Pareto-optimality result. It does not claim to mirror the paper’s strategy-proofness, TTC/Crawler reachability, price-of-anarchy bounds, or experiments. Those would generate further questions, especially optimization over reachable terminal states—for example maximizing average rank or minimum rank among all mass-swap-stable outcomes reachable from an initial distribution.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is strongest at the programme’s own gate: this paper contains no qualifying computational anchor. Its central theorem in Section 6.2 is a structural characterization: on a single-peaked domain, swap-stability is equivalent to Pareto-optimality. It does not define a complexity problem, prove membership in \(P\), establish hardness, give an approximation result, or identify an optimization formulation whose tractability depends on separation. The TTC and Crawler procedures are described, but their complexity is not a result of this paper. The strategy-proofness results are axiomatic impossibilities, and the price-of-anarchy results are welfare bounds. Thus the proposed mirror continuizes an allocation theorem, not a computational result.

The proponent’s high-multiplicity construction is nevertheless coherent. A large housing estate with interchangeable units, and types recording both preferences and initial housing class, is a sensible regime. The objection is not that types improperly erase personal prices or identities: here the type description can indeed be complete.

But that construction exposes how little computational content the mirror has. For rational masses, choose a common denominator \(D\), create \(D\mu_\theta\) agents of every type, and create \(D\rho_r\) copies of every resource class. The mass state \(y\) becomes an ordinary finite assignment. The proposed continuous theorem is then exactly the finite theorem applied to a replicated instance. This equivalence is not itself a defect; a discrete-to-continuous identity can be valuable. The problem is that there is no computational question behind it to investigate. Given \(y\), one can scan all tuples \((\theta,\phi,r,q)\) with positive mass and test whether \(q\succ_\theta r\) and \(r\succ_\phi q\), in \(O(\tau^2q^2)\) time. If no such tuple exists, the paper’s structural theorem supplies Pareto-optimality. The suggested transportation LP is a standard finite-dimensional flow certificate, not an exponential-variable LP with a meaningful pricing frontier.

The mass operation also changes the paper’s motivating object. A bilateral swap in the paper is a deal between two identifiable owners, with the central issue being decentralised proposal, acceptance, coordination, and history. In an atomless population, a named pair has zero mass. A positive-\(\varepsilon\) exchange requires matching and synchronising two cohorts of agents; that is a bulk coordinated transfer, not a bilateral encounter. One can define such a process, but then its terminal states depend on a newly specified matching rule, rate kernel, or schedule. The paper supplies none of these, and different choices produce different continuous dynamics. The mass process is therefore a new aggregate assignment model, not a computational continuization of the swap dynamics studied here.

Retaining the paper’s exact resources does not rescue the mirror. If every house remains individually identifiable, then a complete type must encode preferences and ownership relative to those named houses. The number of relevant types and resources grows with the population, so the high-multiplicity compression disappears. If houses are collapsed into classes, the result is a legitimate but materially redesigned market with interchangeable units.

The other plausible anchors fare no better. A continuous version of the manipulation theorem makes unilateral deviations measure zero and therefore vacuous; making a whole positive-mass cohort deviate turns it into a new group-strategy problem. A continuous price-of-anarchy statement would be a welfare-limit result, not a computational question from the paper. TTC/Crawler reachability depends on finite ownership identities and an ordered sequence of cycles; a mean-field version again requires new dynamics rather than following from the paper.

So I would not claim that no sensible high-multiplicity housing model exists. The proponent has shown one. The stronger and more defensible verdict is that this paper offers no worthwhile ChoCo mirror: its results are structural and axiomatic, while the natural mass reformulation is either an immediate replicated finite assignment problem or a newly invented aggregate dynamics problem. Under the programme’s strict requirement of a computational anchor, that should not green the paper.
