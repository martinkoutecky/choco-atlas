The continuous mirror question

Given rational \(\gamma>1\), ordered slot click-through rates \(x_k\), per-capita capacities \(q_k\) with \(\sum_k q_k\leq 1\), and a finite-support rational distribution \(\mu\) over types \(\theta=(v,\tau)\), does there exist an anonymous direct mechanism computable in time polynomial in \(K\), \(\tau_0=|\operatorname{supp}(\mu)|\), and the input bit length? It must assign advertisers integral slots, possibly by lottery, be truthful and individually rational for a tagged advertiser against fixed background \(\mu\), enforce \(p\leq v\) for every realized payment \(p\) to an \(\mathrm{RB}\), and agree with the replicated-market limits of VCG and Cavallo’s mechanism in the respective pure-class cases. The mechanism must guarantee expected liquid social welfare at least \(\frac{2}{3}\) of the feasible assignment optimum.

The model it lives in

Types are \(\theta=(v,\tau)\), where \(\tau\in\{\mathrm{UM},\mathrm{RB}\}\), with population masses \(\mu_\theta\). A mechanism maps the background distribution and a tagged advertiser’s report to a lottery over integral slot and payment pairs; its aggregate assignment masses \(y_{\theta,k}\) satisfy \(\sum_k y_{\theta,k}\leq\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta y_{\theta,k}\leq q_k\). The welfare objective is \(\sum_{\theta,k}v_\theta x_k y_{\theta,k}\), with the paper’s ROI and incentive constraints applied to each advertiser.

What the mirror covers

Targets a finite-support mass-market analogue of Theorem 4’s BIN-Pri welfare guarantee and mechanism properties. It leaves the public-class BIN-Pub result and Theorem 5’s lower-bound claim without continuous counterparts.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can BIN-Pri’s allocation and payment rules be extended to tied cohorts and fractional masses while preserving truthfulness, individual rationality, consistency, and the \(\frac{2}{3}\) welfare guarantee?
  • Can the resulting mechanism and its tagged-advertiser offers be computed in time polynomial in \(K\), \(\tau_0\), and the input bit length?

The case FOR (proponent)

The paper’s strongest continuous mirror is a large batch auction for standardized ad opportunities. Imagine a platform clearing many comparable opportunities together, with many advertisers using a small number of repeated campaign profiles. A type is \(\theta=(v,\tau)\), where \(v\) is the advertiser’s value per click and \(\tau\in\{UM,RB\}\) is its class. For an \(RB\), \(\gamma\) is the paper’s shared ROI parameter. A mass \(\mu_\theta\) is the fraction of advertisers with that complete profile, and \(\tau_0=|\operatorname{supp}(\mu)|\) is the number of distinct profiles. This fits settings where millions of advertisers fall into relatively few value and bidding-policy groups.

To keep scarce supply meaningful as the population grows, give the batch a per-advertiser capacity \(q_k\) of opportunities at each of \(K\) click-through rates \(x_k\), with \(\sum_k q_k\leq 1\). This is a co-modification of the market’s supply, not the object being continuized: the population is represented by \(\mu\). A mechanism assigns each advertiser an integral slot, possibly by lottery; in aggregate, \(y_{\theta,k}\) is the mass of type \(\theta\) assigned to slot class \(k\). The liquid social welfare benchmark is the optimum of
\[ \max_y \sum_{\theta,k} v_\theta x_k y_{\theta,k} \quad\text{subject to}\quad \sum_k y_{\theta,k}\leq\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_\theta y_{\theta,k}\leq q_k,\qquad y_{\theta,k}\geq0. \]
The \(RB\)’s value here is the paper’s willingness-to-pay \(v\), not its click revenue \(\gamma v\).

A direct mechanism must specify, for every background distribution \(\mu\), what an infinitesimal advertiser receives and pays under each reported type. Truthfulness compares those offers at fixed \(\mu\): a true \(UM\) with value \(v\) gets utility \((v-p)x\), while a true \(RB\) gets \((\gamma v-p)x\) if \(p\leq v\), and \(-\infty\) otherwise. This tagged-advertiser formulation keeps the paper’s unilateral incentive question meaningful in an atomless population. Require individual rationality and consistency as well: when everyone is a \(UM\), the rule agrees with the replicated-market limit of VCG; when everyone is an \(RB\), it agrees with the replicated-market limit of Cavallo’s mechanism. Replicating rational masses and capacities by a common factor gives a precise way to define those limits.

My lead anchor is Theorem 5, proved in this paper. It states that no incentive-compatible, individually rational, consistent mechanism can guarantee a liquid-social-welfare approximation ratio below \(5/4\) when \(\gamma\geq2\), or below \((3+\gamma)/(2+3\gamma-\gamma^2)\) when \(1<\gamma<2\). The paper proves this with a two-slot, three-bidder construction. Its continuous counterpart is the named problem Continuous BIN-Pri Approximation Frontier: given rational \(\gamma\), slot qualities and per-capita capacities, determine whether there is an anonymous direct mechanism satisfying the pointwise truthfulness, IR, and consistency conditions above whose worst-case ratio over finite-support rational \(\mu\) is strictly below
\[ r_\gamma= \begin{cases} 5/4,&\gamma\geq2,\\ (3+\gamma)/(2+3\gamma-\gamma^2),&1<\gamma<2. \end{cases} \]
The output is a mechanism or an impossibility proof. I expect the lower bound to transfer in some form: the proof’s pressure comes from incentive constraints across reports and consistency with the pure-class mechanisms, not from a search over many bidders. But whether its exact constants survive the atomless limit is open; the three-bidder witness does not itself prove that.

A second, constructive anchor is Theorem 4, also proved here. It states that BIN-Pri achieves approximation ratio at most \(3/2\) on LSW, equivalently \(W_{\text{our}}\geq(2/3)W_{\text{OPT}}\). BIN-Pri’s incentive compatibility, IR, and consistency are established earlier in the paper. Its continuous counterpart is Continuous BIN-Pri Mass Allocation: for a rational finite-support population distribution and per-capita slot capacities, construct a truthful, IR, consistent mechanism with welfare at least \(2/3\) of the feasible assignment optimum, and compute its outcome in time polynomial in \(K\), \(\tau_0\), and the input bit length. I expect this to be tractable: BIN-Pri sorts bidders and allocates slots iteratively, operations that appear to become a sweep over type masses and capacity breakpoints. That extension still needs a proof, particularly for cohorts of equal value.

These anchors cover the paper’s binary-class, private-value, ex-post ROI setting and its LSW guarantees; they do not try to continuize all of auction design. The named results are approximation and mechanism-design results, not NP-hardness or polynomial-time classifications. The continuous questions are therefore about the approximation frontier and its constructive mechanism, not a complexity-class claim the paper never makes.

The weakest point is the change from a finite auction to a batch market with per-capita slot supply. The paper studies a fixed set of advertisers and positions; it does not establish that its IC proofs or lower-bound witness persist when equal-type cohorts are split across capacity classes. The finite model’s tie-breaking and the atomless pointwise incentive conditions also need careful treatment. I think the mirror is still worth asking: advertisers grouped by value and ROI class, competing for shares of standardized opportunities, is a recognizable version of their mechanism-design problem. The key open questions are whether the \(3/2\) guarantee survives mass splitting, whether the \(r_\gamma\) obstruction survives replication, and how either result changes with multiple ROI classes.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against both anchors is that they translate the paper’s mechanism-design guarantees into another mechanism-design problem, rather than opening the computational question this programme is meant to study. Theorem 4 proves a welfare approximation guarantee; Theorem 5 proves an impossibility bound for truthful mechanisms. Neither classifies the complexity of a computational problem. BIN-Pri is an executable rule, but the paper’s results concern its incentive and welfare properties. A continuous search for a better mechanism or a stronger impossibility bound could be worthwhile auction theory, yet it does not by itself supply the missing complexity landscape.

Theorem 5’s witness is especially tied to a finite market: one bidder’s report changes the allocation and prices faced by the other two. In an atomless market, a tagged advertiser’s deviation leaves the background distribution unchanged. Their own report can still affect their allocation, so incentives need not become vacuous; but the finite witness no longer supplies the same cross-case constraints. Preserving the witness would require a limit analysis of replicated auctions, with a specified scaling of slots and a definition of consistency and truthfulness along that sequence. That is a coherent research question, but it is not the paper’s lower-bound theorem carried over to a continuous society.

Theorem 4 has a more plausible mass version. Still, the proposed per-capita slot capacities change the market from a fixed set of ranked positions to a batch of repeated inventory, and the mass mechanism must define how BIN-Pri’s prices, rank order, and tie handling apply when a cohort is split across slots. A stronger formulation could give every advertiser a lottery over integral slots and impose the paper’s ROI condition on every realized outcome. That would be recognizable to the authors, and the paper’s sorting rule gives a credible starting point. So the model change alone does not defeat this anchor; the case against is that the resulting contribution would be a new mechanism-design guarantee, with no computational barrier or complexity question identified by the paper.

I cannot honestly make the universal negative case airtight. A batch of standardized ad opportunities with many advertisers sharing a finite set of value and ROI profiles is a plausible high-multiplicity regime, and scaling supply with population is within the programme’s stated allowance for co-modification. The strongest objection is therefore about fit: these anchors do not yet show a worthwhile continuous computational mirror of the paper’s results. If “worthwhile” includes a new nonatomic auction-design theorem, the constructive anchor in particular survives, and the evidence here does not justify claiming that no suitable scenario exists.
