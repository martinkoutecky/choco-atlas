The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make an anchor-based positive case for this paper: it contains no named computational result asserting tractability or hardness.

The closest candidate is Theorem 2.1, proved in this paper. It says that for a location function \(f:V^k\to V\) on a finite tree, \(f\) satisfies Weak Pareto Efficiency and Replacement Domination if and only if it is a generalized target function for some target \(a\) and satisfies the convexity condition. Here, Weak Pareto Efficiency means \(f(\pi)\in\langle\pi\rangle\); Replacement Domination says that when one customer’s location changes, all unchanged customers weakly prefer one of the two outcomes; and a generalized target function returns \(a\) when \(a\in\langle\pi\rangle\), otherwise the gate of \(a\) into the hull or a neighboring vertex in that hull. The convexity condition requires \(f(\pi)=f(\rho)\) whenever \(f(\pi)\in\langle\rho\rangle\) and \(f(\rho)\in\langle\pi\rangle\). This is an axiomatic characterization, not a complexity result or an algorithm for an underlying computational problem.

A plausible high-multiplicity setting would be a city whose many residents have peak locations among the vertices of a tree-shaped road network: each vertex is a type, and \(\mu_v\) is the fraction of residents with peak \(v\). But asking which location rules on \(\mu\) satisfy measure-based versions of the axioms would continue an axiomatic question. Replacing that with minimizing total distance would create a different optimization problem that Theorem 2.1 does not address. The paper also mentions Vohra’s continuous-location result, but that concerns allowing locations inside edges, not making the population continuous.

So there is a conceivable population-continuum extension, but no named computational anchor here to support the requested mirror.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that the paper has no computational result to continue. Its only named theorem, Theorem 2.1, characterizes location functions satisfying Weak Pareto Efficiency and Replacement Domination. It gives no algorithmic problem, complexity bound, or optimization task. The lemmas support that characterization; they do not introduce a computational problem either.

The city example is a sensible high-multiplicity regime: many residents can share a peak location, with their fractions recorded in a distribution \(\mu\). But the best population-level translation of the theorem would be an axiomatic characterization of rules \(F(\mu)\). One could restate WPE in terms of the support’s convex hull and define replacement domination through transfers of mass between types. That would make the *population* continuous, but the question would still be which rules satisfy the axioms, not a computational question of the kind ChoCo is charting.

A computational version needs an extra task the paper does not supply. Given \(\mu\), asking for the ordinary target location is just finding a gate into the support’s hull. Asking for the closest location under aggregate distance introduces a different objective. Asking the algorithm to choose among generalized target functions does not determine an output: the theorem permits multiple rules, with the convexity condition constraining their behavior across profiles. One could study how to represent and recognize such rules, but the computational problem would then depend on a newly chosen representation and encoding, rather than arise from the paper’s result.

Vohra’s continuous-tree result does not close the population-continuum question: it allows locations inside edges while keeping a finite set of customers. So “already done continuously” is not a sound objection here. The more direct point is that neither Vohra’s result nor this paper offers a computational anchor to mirror.

This case does not show that no nearby continuous-computational location problem could be worthwhile. It shows that proposing one would require adding a computational task of our own; the paper itself gives the programme no such task to continuize.
