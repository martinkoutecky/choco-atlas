The continuous mirror question

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), finite approval types \(t\in T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), a rational mass-normalized candidate-load scale \(\beta>0\), and threshold \(\alpha\), decide whether there exist \(y_c\in\{0,1\}\) with \(\sum_c y_c=k\) and mass-weighted loads \(z_{t,c}\) satisfying \(z_{t,c}=0\) for \(c\notin A_t\), \(0\leq z_{t,c}\leq\mu_t y_c\), \(\sum_t z_{t,c}=\beta y_c\), and \(\sum_t\mu_t\ell_t^2<\alpha\), where \(\ell_t=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_c z_{t,c}\).

The model it lives in

A finite distribution of complete approval types with masses \(\mu_t\); an integral committee \(y\); divisible load-allocation variables \(z_{t,c}\); type-average loads \(\ell_t\); and the mass-weighted variance objective \(\sum_t\mu_t\ell_t^2\), with \(\beta=1/N\) recovering the normalized \(N\)-voter formulation.

The objection that survived

The stated bound \(z_{t,c}\leq\beta\mu_t y_c\) makes candidates with approving mass below \(1\) infeasible; the correct mass-weighted bound is \(z_{t,c}\leq\mu_t y_c\), with \(\sum_t z_{t,c}=\beta y_c\).

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the var-Phragmén hardness in Theorem 5.1 and the seq-Phragmén computation result in Theorem 5.4. It leaves the representation axioms, apportionment results, and most leximax-specific results untouched.

Open questions for a prover

  • Is the continuous var-Phragmén threshold problem NP-complete under rational-input encoding, including membership in NP?
  • Which parameterizations by \(k\), \(|T|\), or candidate-incidence structure yield fixed-parameter or approximation algorithms?
  • How accurately can arbitrary continuous societies be rounded to finite elections while preserving the committee and variance threshold?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s variance-minimizing rule. I would treat it as a Class B problem: the population becomes continuous, but the committee remains an integral \(k\)-subset, and the hardness survives because the reduction’s combinatorics live in the candidate-incidence structure.

The paper’s lead anchor is Theorem 5.1, proved in the paper. Its relevant assertion is that the decision problem var-Phragmén is NP-complete even when every voter approves at most two candidates, \(s(A)=2\), and every candidate is approved by at most three voters, \(d(A)=3\). The problem asks, given an approval profile, committee size \(k\), and threshold \(\alpha>0\), whether there is a valid load distribution \(x\) whose voter loads \(\bar x_i\) satisfy \(\sum_i \bar x_i^2<\alpha\). The paper proves NP-hardness by reducing from Independent Set on cubic graphs, and establishes membership in NP through a mixed-integer quadratic formulation, invoking the cited result that MIQP is in NP.

Here is the continuous problem I would attach to that theorem.

Continuous var-Phragmén Threshold. An instance consists of candidates \(C\), a committee size \(k\), finitely many approval types \(t\in T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), a rational per-capita load scale \(\beta>0\), and a rational threshold \(\alpha\). Type \(t\) is an approval set \(A_t\subseteq C\); all agents of that type are identical for the purposes of Phragmén’s rule. The committee must remain integral: choose \(y_c\in\{0,1\}\) with \(\sum_c y_c=k\).

Let \(z_{t,c}\) be the total load assigned by candidate \(c\) to type \(t\). Require \(z_{t,c}=0\) whenever \(c\notin A_t\), \(0\le z_{t,c}\le \beta\mu_t y_c\), and \(\sum_t z_{t,c}=\beta y_c\) for every candidate \(c\). Thus each selected candidate contributes total load \(\beta\), distributed only among its approving population; \(\beta=1/N\) is the natural normalization of the paper’s unit candidate load for a population of \(N\) voters. The average load of type \(t\) is \(\ell_t=\mu_t^{-1}\sum_c z_{t,c}\). The question is whether there is a feasible integral committee and load allocation with \(\sum_t\mu_t\ell_t^2<\alpha\).

This is not continuizing the committee or the alternatives. It is exactly the paper’s load-balancing problem with the voter list replaced by a distribution over complete approval types. If one allows measurable allocations within a type, convexity means that an optimum for the variance objective can be taken uniform within each type, giving precisely the finite-dimensional formulation above.

The regime is natural in a large approval election or validator-selection process: millions of nominators may share one of a few hundred or thousand approval patterns because they belong to the same organization, constituency, issue coalition, or platform community. The number of agents can be \(N=R|E|\), while the number of types is only \(|E|\), with \(R\) arbitrarily large. This is genuine high multiplicity, not a claim that every small committee election is a continuum.

The hardness mechanism survives this limit. Given a cubic graph \(G=(V,E)\), create one candidate for each vertex and one approval type for each edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), with \(A_e=\{u,v\}\) and \(\mu_e=1/|E|\). Let the population contain \(R\) identical voters of every edge type, so \(\beta=1/(R|E|)\). If \(G\) has an independent set of size \(k\), the corresponding committee has \(3k\) disjoint incident edge types, and the loads can be equalized at \(1/(3R)\), giving objective \(k/(3|E|R^2)\). If the selected vertices are not independent, they jointly touch at most \(3k-1\) edge types. Since the total selected-candidate load is \(k\beta\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives objective at least \(k^2/(|E|R^2(3k-1))\), which is strictly greater than \((k/3+1/9)/(|E|R^2)\). Therefore the continuous instance can use threshold \(\alpha=(k/3+1/9)/(|E|R^2)\), and the Independent Set reduction remains valid for arbitrarily large \(R\).

So I expect Continuous var-Phragmén Threshold to be NP-hard, probably NP-complete under the usual rational-input convention. This is a transferred-hardness Class B mirror: population multiplicity disappears, but the graph structure among candidates does not. The natural follow-up questions are whether the problem becomes tractable for fixed \(k\), fixed number of approval types, or restricted candidate-incidence graphs; whether there are useful approximation schemes; and how much finite-election error is introduced when an arbitrary distribution is rounded to finitely many voters.

A second, weaker but very clean anchor is Theorem 5.4, also proved in the paper. It states that the output of seq-Phragmén can be computed exactly in \(O(k^3mn(\log n)^2)\) time, including the cost of rational arithmetic. The paper’s algorithm computes, in each round, \(s_c^{(j)}=(1+\sum_{i\in N_c}\bar x_i^{(j-1)})/|N_c|\), chooses a remaining candidate minimizing this value, and raises the loads of its approvers to that common level.

The corresponding problem is Continuous seq-Phragmén Output. Given \(C,k,(A_t,\mu_t)_{t\in T}\), a positive mass \(\rho_c=\sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_t\) for every candidate, a load scale \(\beta\), and a fixed tie-breaking order, begin with \(\ell_t^{(0)}=0\). In round \(j\), for every remaining candidate \(c\), compute \(q_c^{(j)}=(\beta+\sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_t\ell_t^{(j-1)})/\rho_c\). Select a candidate minimizing \(q_c^{(j)}\), and set \(\ell_t^{(j)}=q_c^{(j)}\) for approving types and \(\ell_t^{(j)}=\ell_t^{(j-1)}\) otherwise. After \(k\) rounds, output the selected committee and the type loads.

This is an exact Class A mirror. The discrete sums in the paper become mass-weighted sums, and the direct implementation uses \(O(km\tau)\) rational updates. With rational masses, the bit lengths grow polynomially in the input length and \(k\), so the type-compressed problem should be solvable in polynomial time. The paper’s own “cylindrical vessels” interpretation makes this especially plausible: the base area of a vessel is already proportional to the number of voters, and the continuum simply replaces that area by population mass.

I would not claim that the whole paper has been continuized. The axiomatic results in Section 6 are outside the computational scope here, and the paper’s fractional \(x_{i,c}\) variables do not themselves make the society continuous: they are load-allocation variables over a finite voter set. The mirror changes the input object from a named list of voters to a mass distribution over complete approval types while preserving the integral committee decision.

The weakest point is that the paper does not itself prove a continuous theorem. Its hardness construction has one edge type per graph edge, so the high-multiplicity interpretation requires the explicit replication argument above; and the variance mirror uses uniform within-type loads plus the per-capita scaling \(\beta\). Those are genuine co-modifications, although they are forced by normalization and are exact for the variance objective. The case is therefore strongest for the underlying load-balancing problem, not as a claim that every theorem in the paper automatically extends unchanged.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that both proposed mirrors are exact type-compressions of finite Phragmén elections, rather than genuinely new continuum problems. That is a legitimate concern about novelty, but under ChoCo’s rules it is not enough to reject them: high-multiplicity compression is precisely the intended regime.

There is, however, a real error in the first formulation. With \(\beta=1/(R|E|)\), the constraints \(z_{t,c}\leq \beta\mu_t y_c\) and \(\sum_t z_{t,c}=\beta y_c\) make most candidates infeasible. If \(\rho_c<1\) is the approving mass, their total capacity is only \(\beta\rho_c<\beta\). The correct bound, when \(z_{t,c}\) is mass-weighted original voter load, is \(z_{t,c}\leq \mu_t y_c\). This is only a repair, though, not a substantive defeat.

After that repair, Theorem 5.1 survives cleanly. For the cubic-graph construction, give each edge type mass \(1/|E|\) and replicate it arbitrarily many times. An independent set yields objective \(k/(3|E|R^2)\); a non-independent committee touches at most \(3k-1\) edge types, and Cauchy–Schwarz gives objective at least
\[ \frac{k^2}{|E|R^2(3k-1)}. \]
The same separating threshold works for arbitrarily large multiplicities. The graph incidence structure remains, but that is exactly a Class B hardness transfer, not a permissible objection.

Indeed, for variance minimization the continuum is mathematically exact: conditional on the total load assigned to an approval type, Jensen’s inequality makes equal loads within that type optimal. Thus
\[ \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \bar x_i^2 \]
is exactly represented by
\[ \sum_t \mu_t \ell_t^2 \]
for repeated voter types. This is a compressed high-multiplicity formulation, not a distortion of the rule.

The same defeats the objection to Theorem 5.4. Identical approval types have identical loads in every sequential round, so the paper’s recurrence becomes
\[ q_c^{(j)} = \frac{\beta+\sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_t\ell_t^{(j-1)}}{\sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_t}. \]
The committee and type loads can be computed in \(O(km\tau)\) rational updates, with polynomially bounded bit lengths. This is a real high-multiplicity algorithm: its dependence is on the number of types \(\tau\), not on an exponentially larger population size \(n\).

A natural large-election or validator-selection interpretation is also available: many nominators may share organizational or issue-based approval patterns. Nothing in either objective tracks personal identity, arrival time, or position, so mass is meaningful and does not erase information the problem needs.

Consequently, the honest negative case is weak. The first formulation needs a normalization correction, and both mirrors may be viewed as straightforward type aggregation rather than a profound new model. But neither anchor can be rejected on a fundamental ground, and Theorem 5.4 in particular is a clean Class A continuous-population question.
