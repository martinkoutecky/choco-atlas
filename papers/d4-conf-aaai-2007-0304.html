The case FOR (proponent)

The tempting anchors are axiomatic results: Theorem 3 proves neutrality for the order-independent composition of two binary plurality correspondences; Theorem 12 proves near-neutrality for sequential compositions of neutral local correspondences; and Theorem 14 proves near-neutrality for the order-independent composition under an additional condition on equal-sized domains. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has no computational result to continuize. Its numbered theorems concern properties of voting rules, permutations of legal profiles, and decomposability; none classifies the complexity of a decision, search, or optimization problem. The introduction’s claim that sequential composition is “computationally economical” is motivation, not a complexity analysis.

The strongest candidates still fail as anchors. Theorem 3 establishes neutrality for a composition of two binary plurality correspondences; Theorems 12 and 14 establish near-neutrality under their stated conditions. These are axiomatic claims. A population distribution over complete preference types for repeated referenda could be sensible, but asking whether neutrality or near-neutrality survives there would continuize an axiomatic question, outside ChoCo’s scope. The other decomposition and permutation results have the same problem.

One could define a new problem—say, computing the outcome of a mass-weighted sequential composition from a rational distribution over legal types. But that would need its own rule, input representation, and complexity question. It would be an algorithmic extension inspired by the paper, not a mirror of any result in it. The negative case is strong on that distinction; it cannot rule out the value of such an independent extension, only say that this paper supplies no eligible anchor for a continuous mirror.
