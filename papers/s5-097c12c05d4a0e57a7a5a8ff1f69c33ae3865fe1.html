The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a narrow one: the paper’s symmetric public-good agents or identical firms give a plausible high-multiplicity regime. Each agent has the same complete strategic type, mass represents the fraction of the population in a coalition, and a coalition coordinates its members’ actions. A continuous follow-up could ask for a stable payoff allocation, or for the least supermajority threshold that restores stability when a majority can extract \(z\) from the minority. That is a natural population-level question: the paper itself reasons in terms of coalition shares and majority thresholds.

But the paper has no named computational result to anchor that mirror to. Lemma 1 gives a payoff ordering by coalition size; it is attributed to Currarini and Marini (2006). Proposition 1, proved here, says the \(M\)-core is nonempty under the stated symmetry, monotonicity, strategic-complements-or-substitutes, and contraction assumptions, for all expectations. Proposition 2, also proved here, says that under A1–A3 and the stated bound on extraction power \(z\), some \(\beta\in(1/2,1)\) makes the \(M_\beta\)-core nonempty, with the required \(\beta\) increasing in \(z\). These are existence and stability results; none asserts that a problem is in P, hard, FPT, or otherwise computationally classified.

So I would not present either proposition as a computational anchor or claim that the paper puts a core-finding problem in P. A continuous core-finding or threshold-computation problem could be a sensible new research question, but establishing its computational status would be new work rather than a mirror of a named computational result in this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that there is no computational result here to mirror. Lemma 1 is attributed to earlier work; Propositions 1 and 2 prove existence and comparative statics. None classifies an algorithmic problem. The proponent’s proposed tasks—finding a stable allocation or computing a supermajority threshold—would be new computational questions, not continuized versions of computational results in the paper.

The homogeneous public-good or identical-firm regime is a sensible high-multiplicity setting, so I would not reject it on population grounds. But it makes the Proposition 1 mirror especially small: symmetry reduces an allocation to a common per-capita payoff, and the proposition supplies the equal-split stability argument. Finding that witness means evaluating the grand-coalition outcome; checking the theorem’s conclusion is not a search over a society distribution. If equilibrium computation is difficult for a broadly represented game, that difficulty lies in the game representation and equilibrium calculation, not in the population becoming continuous.

Proposition 2 offers the better candidate: find the least coalition share \(\beta\) that restores stability for extraction level \(z\). In the continuous version this is a scalar boundary, defined by the point where the per-capita payoff gap offsets extraction. The paper’s monotonicity condition makes the crossing ordered, and its examples give direct formulas or calculations. For an unrestricted game, computing that boundary may be hard, but the paper does not specify a finite encoding that would make this a complexity problem; the challenge would again be evaluating equilibrium payoffs and their gap. Adding heterogeneous types to create a richer input would undermine the symmetry assumptions on which these propositions rest, making it a new theory rather than a mirror of their results.

That is a strong reason to decline this paper as a ChoCo anchor, not proof that no useful continuous project could grow from it. The case is weakest precisely at that broader claim: the coalition-share threshold is a coherent continuous question, and the paper gives it a natural starting point. But its worth as a computational research programme would have to be established independently; these results do not supply the computational problem the programme is meant to continuize.
