The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper states no named theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition about the complexity of a problem, and it formulates no algorithmic decision or optimization task. Equations (1)–(3) define the agents’ updates; Sections 4 and 5 report simulation findings and an approximate influence calculation. Those may motivate new questions, but they do not give us a computational claim to continuize.

The best seed is the observed stratification when homophily and kernel-based initial trust occur together. A population distribution over opinions and extremist status could ask whether that outcome persists in a continuum. But the paper’s mechanism depends on graph structure and pairwise trust that changes over time: its conclusions vary with the network model and initial trust model. A mass distribution over a small set of types would lose that structure. Encoding each agent’s neighborhood and evolving trust relationships in its type could preserve it, but would tend to make types individual-specific and lose the high-multiplicity advantage. A graphon or mean-field formulation could retain aggregate network effects; its natural questions, however, concern the limiting dynamics and their equilibria—the analytic line of work the ChoCo brief distinguishes from its computational-complexity gap.

One could add a computational objective, such as finding the least extremist mass or cheapest intervention that produces stratification. That is a plausible adjacent project, but this paper supplies no intervention choices, costs, or optimization result to mirror. It would introduce a new problem rather than continuize one of the paper’s results.

So the negative case is strongest as a judgment about this paper: none of its findings supplies a qualifying anchor, and the most tempting extension either becomes an analytic mean-field question or adds a new control problem. The honest weakness is that extremist influence makes such a control problem naturally motivated; this paper cannot rule out its value as separate research. It does not, though, establish a worthwhile continuous mirror of its own computational results.
