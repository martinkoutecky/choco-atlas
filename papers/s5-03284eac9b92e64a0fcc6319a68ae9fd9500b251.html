The continuous mirror question

Given \(T\) rounds of labeled indivisible slot types \(r\), common additive values \(v_r\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q_{tr}\in[0,1]\), decide whether a measurable irrevocable allocation of unit population mass exists such that each microscopic agent receives \(b_r\in\{0,1\}\) for every slot, aggregate slot usage equals the supplies, and almost every agent meets the continuum MMS benchmark after every round. For goods, the benchmark is \(\sup_{\lambda:\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}[b_r]=q_r}\operatorname{ess\,inf}_{b\sim\lambda}\sum_r v_rb_r\); for chores, use the corresponding \(\inf\operatorname{ess\,sup}\) cost benchmark.

The model it lives in

A unit-mass population with one valuation type, integral microscopic bundles, and fractional aggregate mass variables \(y_{\mathbf b^1,\ldots,\mathbf b^T}\ge0\) over nested bundle trajectories. The constraints enforce per-round slot supplies and irrevocability; feasibility asks whether every positive-mass trajectory satisfies the exact goods or chores TMMS threshold at every round.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 7.2 for both goods and chores. It leaves the TEF1 algorithms, Pareto-optimality results, bounded-value dynamic program, and the \(1/n\)-TMMS guarantee outside its scope.

Open questions for a prover

  • What is the computational complexity of continuous exact TMMS feasibility for goods and chores under the one-copy-per-slot convention?
  • Does the Partition hardness persist when repeated copies of a slot type may be accumulated by one microscopic agent?
  • Can the continuous problem be formulated as a configuration LP, and when does its pricing problem become tractable?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is a continuous-population version of the paper’s exact temporal maximin-share problem.

The anchor is Theorem 7.2, proved in this paper via the reduction in Appendix G.2, not merely cited: deciding whether an exact TMMS allocation exists is NP-hard even with two agents, two rounds, and identical valuations, for both goods and chores. The reduction is from Partition. Given numbers \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\), it creates round-one items of values \(s_j=2a_j\), followed by two round-two items of value \(W=\sum_j a_j\). Exact TMMS forces both agents’ round-one value to be \(W\), which exists exactly when the \(s_j\) can be partitioned into two equal-sum sets. The chore reduction is analogous.

I would call the mirror Continuous Exact-TMMS. The population is \(U=[0,1]\), with Lebesgue mass, and initially one agent type: all agents have the same additive valuation. In round \(t\), item slots \(r\) arrive with per-capita supply \(q_{tr}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\). A microscopic agent may receive an item slot at most once, so her bundle is an integral vector \(b\in\{0,1\}^{R_{\le t}}\). The aggregate allocation is a mass distribution \(y_{\mathbf b^1,\ldots,\mathbf b^T}\): it gives the mass of agents following each integral bundle trajectory, with bundles nested over time and satisfying the supply equations \(\sum_{\mathbf b} y_{\mathbf b}b_r^t=\sum_{\rho\le t}q_{\rho r}\). Thus the type-level allocation is fractional, while every unit of population receives an integral bundle.

For goods, define the continuum MMS at round \(t\) by

\(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t=\sup_{\lambda}\operatorname{ess\,inf}_{b\sim\lambda} v(b)\),

where \(\lambda\) ranges over distributions on integral bundles whose expected item counts equal the per-capita supplies. Exact continuous TMMS requires every positive-mass agent to receive value at least \(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t\) at every round. For chores, replace this by the corresponding minimum possible essential maximum cost,

\(\operatorname{CMM}^{\infty}_t=\inf_{\lambda}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{b\sim\lambda} d(b)\).

The decision question is whether such an irrevocable measurable allocation exists. Its objective is feasibility; equivalently, one can maximize the largest temporal MMS factor \(\alpha\).

This is a genuine high-multiplicity regime: think of a large population of statistically identical households receiving successive classes of indivisible parcels, slots, or duties. The population has one valuation type, while the number of agents tends to infinity. Per-capita item supplies are the natural co-modification: \(q_{tr}\) records the fraction of the population receiving an item slot. The original authors should recognise the problem immediately: same temporal arrivals, same additive goods/chores, same irrevocability, and the same exact maximin-share requirement, with named agents replaced by a distribution of indistinguishable agents.

Here the hardness should transfer, placing this mirror in Class B. Normalize the paper’s Partition reduction by taking \(q_{1,g_j}=1/2\) for every round-one item and \(q_{2,h_1}=q_{2,h_2}=1/2\). The total final value per unit population is \(2W\). The continuum MMS is \(2W\): half the population can receive all round-one items and half can receive both round-two items, giving every bundle value \(2W\), while \(2W\) is also the average-value upper bound.

Consequently, any exact goods allocation gives almost every agent final value exactly \(2W\). The second-round value is \(0\), \(W\), or \(2W\); the positive round-one MMS rules out the \(2W\) second-round case. Since the average round-one value is \(W\), every supported round-one bundle must therefore have value exactly \(W\). Such a bundle is a subset of the \(g_j\), so its existence yields a solution to Partition. Conversely, a Partition solution lets half the population receive one side and half the other, with the two second-round slots distributed appropriately. The chore argument is parallel: exact final cost forces cost \(2W\), the round-one chore benchmark is strictly below \(2W\), and the average-cost argument forces every supported round-one bundle to have cost \(W\).

The important point is that fractional population mass does not erase the combinatorics. It merely randomizes over integral bundle supports. The reduction’s structure lives in which item slots occur together in an individual bundle, not in the number of named agents. This is precisely the kind of integrality/support-preservation mechanism through which hardness can survive continuization.

The mirror covers Theorem 7.2 only. It does not claim that the paper’s TEF1 existence algorithms or Pareto-optimality results automatically extend.

The weakest point is the one-copy-per-slot convention. If a high-multiplicity model instead allows an agent to receive arbitrarily many copies of the same item type, the Partition argument may admit new fractional or repeated-item witnesses. That variant is still a sensible continuous problem, but its complexity could change—possibly to a configuration LP, or to continuum-specific hardness through its pricing problem. I would therefore present the slot-integral version as the clean primary mirror, and explicitly pose the uncapped-copy version as its first boundary question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I cannot make a persuasive negative case against this anchor. The proponent has found a genuine high-multiplicity mirror, and the usual objections fail.

Theorem 7.2 is an explicit computational result, not merely an axiom. Its hardness is already proved with identical valuations, so replacing the two named agents by a continuum of statistically identical agents does not discard information relevant to the reduction. The natural finite approximation has \(2K\) agents and \(K\) copies of every round-one item and of each round-two item. Its per-capita supplies converge exactly to the proposed values \(1/2\).

Nor does aggregate fractional mass fractionalize an individual bundle. Each agent still receives an integral subset of item slots; only the distribution over such subsets is fractional. In the goods reduction, the final average value is \(2W\). Exact TMMS forces almost every agent to attain exactly \(2W\). The positive first-round benchmark rules out agents receiving both second-round goods, while the average first-round value is \(W\). Hence every bundle in the support must contain a subset of round-one items of value exactly \(W\), which is precisely a Partition witness. This is a valid Class B transfer, not an artefact of named-agent identity. The chore argument has the same structure.

The best objection is that “one copy of each slot per microscopic agent” is an additional modelling choice. If copies may be accumulated without that cap, the continuous problem becomes a different configuration problem and the reduction no longer transfers verbatim. But that is a boundary variant, not a fatal flaw: one-copy quotas are the direct high-multiplicity interpretation of repeatedly distributing one parcel or assignment slot of each category, and the programme explicitly permits such co-modification when indivisibility is retained microscopically.

Calling the construction merely a lottery or saying that the hardness lives in the items also does not work. A high-multiplicity allocation is naturally represented by a distribution over integral bundle types, and hardness in the bundle-support structure is exactly the kind of hardness that survives population continuization.

Thus the honest negative verdict is weak: Theorem 7.2 supplies a worthwhile continuous mirror. At most, one can require the authors to formalize the slot-capacity convention and compare it with the uncapped-copy variant.
