The case FOR (proponent)

This paper has no eligible named anchor: it contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result. The strongest continuous mirror I see is the paper’s underlying winner determination problem. I would call it the *Continuous-Type Double-Auction WDP*. Let \(B\) be buyer types and \(S\) seller types. A buyer type \(b\) has population share \(\mu_b\), bundle \(r_b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{\ell}\), and value \(v_b\). A seller type \(s\) has share \(\nu_s\), per-seller capacity \(q_s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^{\ell}\), and reserve prices \(a_s\). The type description includes all the preferences, capacities, and prices relevant to the auction. Let \(\rho\) be the ratio of seller population to buyer population, with \(\sum_b\mu_b=\sum_s\nu_s=1\).

The decision variables are \(x_b\), the mass of buyers of type \(b\) served, and \(z_{bsk}\), the amount of resource \(k\) supplied by sellers of type \(s\) to buyers of type \(b\). Given these rational data, compute a feasible allocation maximizing social welfare:
\(\max \sum_{b\in B}v_bx_b-\sum_{b\in B,s\in S,k\in[\ell]}a_{sk}z_{bsk}\)\),
subject to \(0\le x_b\le\mu_b\), \(z_{bsk}\ge0\), \(\sum_{s\in S}z_{bsk}=r_{bk}x_b\), and \(\sum_{b\in B}z_{bsk}\le\rho\nu_sq_{sk}\) for every \(s,k\). A solution is an optimal allocation \(x,z\) and its welfare.

This preserves the paper’s single-minded buyer bundles, additive seller costs, and welfare objective. A buyer still receives their entire requested bundle or nothing; \(x_b\) is the mass of identical buyers who receive it. In a large market, that mass can be selected from a nonatomic population without splitting any individual’s bundle. The mirror therefore fits a plausible cloud regime: many customers drawn from recurring workload and bundle templates, served by providers offering standardized resource profiles. The population can be very large while the numbers of distinct buyer and seller types remain moderate.

This candidate is a finite LP, so I would expect it to be tractable (Class A) in the support sizes, number of resource types, and input bit length. It mirrors the underlying WDP, not the paper’s empirical algorithm comparison or CAGE generator. The paper’s Eqs. (1)–(4) are the clearest textual foothold for this mirror, but they are not a numbered result and cannot serve as the requested anchor.

The soft spot is substantial: the paper studies a synthetic regime with individual bids and asks, and does not establish that these instances have few repeated types. Its hardness motivation may depend on heterogeneous bundle choices, which this continuum mirror smooths away. So this is a credible high-multiplicity question suggested by the paper’s model, but not an anchor-based claim about what the paper proved. Further work would need to establish how the finite WDP relates to this LP as the population grows, including approximation or rounding guarantees, and identify which additional constraints—such as resource locality—would preserve tractability.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent supplies no eligible named anchor. The paper has no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition establishing a computational result: Table 1 reports algorithm runtimes, Table 3 reports empirical winners, and the paper formulates the winner determination problem. So there is no named result for their case to defend.

Their fallback, a continuous-type winner determination problem, is the strongest possible mirror here. A scenario with many buyers and providers repeating complete bundle, value, capacity, and price profiles is plausible. Serving a mass of buyers of one type can preserve the all-or-nothing bundle requirement for each individual, and seller capacity can scale with seller mass. I would not reject this mirror on grounds of identity, integral bundles, or the fact that it changes the model.

The strongest negative point is that its computation is already the paper’s LP relaxation in §3.3. Group identical buyers and sellers in that relaxation: the sums of their fractional allocation variables become the proposed type masses and flows, with the same welfare objective and capacity constraints. Conversely, the mass for a type can be distributed among its identical agents. With finite type support, the resulting problem is an explicit LP; it needs no new pricing method or continuous-optimization machinery. The paper already computes this LP as the basis for RLPS and SS. Thus the proposed mirror appears to give a new population interpretation to an optimization problem the paper already uses, rather than exposing a new computational question. The paper’s CAGE instances also do not establish a moderate-support regime: their varied bundles and bid values can make complete types nearly unique.

That objection is not conclusive. The paper relaxes allocation variables, not population size, and proving a useful dictionary between those models—or deriving high-multiplicity guarantees—could be worthwhile. A cloud market with repeated contract and workload templates is credible. So the negative case is weak on the universal claim: the proposed mirror survives the substantive modelling test, though it has no named-result anchor and its apparent computational contribution is already present in the paper’s LP relaxation.
