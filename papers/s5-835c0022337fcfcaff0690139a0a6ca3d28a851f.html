The committee itself is the most plausible population to continuize, but the paper’s mechanism depends on individual votes. In its finite model, champions means \(k=1\): one partner’s support suffices. In an atomless committee, a rule accepting any positive mass of supporting votes behaves differently. With independent signals and a positive probability of support, that mass is positive in the continuum limit, so the champions rule loses its finite committee meaning. Replacing it with a positive quota \(q\) restores a meaningful mass threshold, but changes the mechanism; moreover, an individual agent has no pivotal effect, so the paper’s voting-threshold argument no longer carries over without a new equilibrium model. Large panels or repeated expertise groups may be plausible settings for that new model, but they do not supply the missing computational result.
