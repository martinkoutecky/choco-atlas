The strongest unanchored candidate is *Continuum Product-Mix Clearing*. For two varieties, let the input give \(\tau\) bidder types with rational masses \(\mu_t\), summing to one. Each type is a complete bid schedule: a finite list of the paper’s mutually exclusive offers, each specifying a quantity \(q_{tb}\) and prices \(r_{tb,w},r_{tb,s}\) for weak and strong varieties. Also give a fixed total quantity \(Q\) and a rational, piecewise-linear supply rule \(\sigma(\Delta)\), where \(\Delta=p_w-p_s\) and \(\sigma(\Delta)\) is the share supplied as weak variety. The task is to find uniform cutoff prices \(p_w,p_s\) and accepted mass for each offer, so that offers are accepted and selected according to the paper’s rules, and aggregate allocations match \(Q\sigma(\Delta)\) weak and \(Q(1-\sigma(\Delta))\) strong. A solution is the prices and accepted masses satisfying those conditions, including the stated tie-breaking and rationing rules.

A plausible regime is a large electricity market with many small counterparties using a relatively small number of standard bid schedules: \(N\) agents but \(\tau\ll N\) complete types. The type captures all features relevant to bids, so the model genuinely continuizes the bidder population. The authors could plausibly recognize this as computing their product-mix outcome for a population distribution. For the two-variety version, I would expect tractability by sorting offer breakpoints and intersecting the resulting demand curve with the supply rule; that is an inference from Section 2’s construction, not a result established by the paper.

This candidate covers only computing the auction’s cutoff prices and allocation. Its weak point is substantial: the paper already describes a procedure for finding those outcomes from finite bids, and it identifies no discrete hardness that might dissolve under continuization. This is a plausible follow-on computational question, but it is not a mirror anchored in a named result of this paper.
