The continuous mirror question

Given a finite location grid \(L\subseteq[0,1]\), fixed \(K\), and tolerance \(\eta>0\), construct an anonymous direct mechanism mapping every reported distribution \(\nu\in\Delta(L)\) to a distribution over integral facility tuples \(s\in L^K\), with report-dependent integral nearest-facility reactions, such that for every true \(\mu\in\Delta(L)\) truthful reporting is strictly dominant and \(\mathbb{E}[C(\mu,s)]\leq\min_{s\in L^K}C(\mu,s)+\eta\), where \(C(\mu,s)=\sum_{x\in L}\mu_x\min_{j\leq K}|x-s_j|\); determine the mechanism and its complexity in the explicit input size.

The model it lives in

Types are locations \(x\in L\) carrying mass \(\mu_x\); the aggregate rule uses \(e^{-\lambda C(\nu,s)}\) over integral facility tuples plus a positive report-indexed commitment lottery, while each mass element receives an integral nearest-facility reaction.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the exponential-plus-commitment construction and the finite-grid pricing and facility-location approximation results. It leaves out arbitrary labelled interdependent environments without an anonymity restriction, Section 5's continuous type domain with a finite population, and the paper's non-computational background discussion.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the weighted \(K\)-median aggregate and report-indexed commitment rule be implemented with polynomial bit complexity for fixed \(K\) and rational \(L\) and \(\eta\)?
  • What changes when \(K\), the facility space, or the type support is implicit or continuously parameterized?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a population mirror of the paper’s large-market implementation results. The paper contains no NP-hardness or complexity-class theorem, so the anchors are algorithmic approximation theorems proved in the paper. I would lead with the generic theorem and use pricing and facility location as two concrete instantiations.

The main anchor is Theorem 2 (Main Theorem), proved in the paper. It considers a finite type space, a finite set \(S\) of social alternatives, a non-trivial environment with gap \(\gamma\), a separating commitment distribution \(P\) with minimum relevant probability \(\tilde p\), and a \(d\)-sensitive objective \(F\). For sufficiently large \(n\), it forms the mixture \(\hat M=(1-q)M^{\epsilon/(2d)}+qM^P\), where \(M^{\epsilon/(2d)}\) is the exponential mechanism, \(\epsilon=\sqrt{\frac{\tilde p\gamma d}{n}}\sqrt{\ln\left(\frac{n\tilde p\gamma|S|}{2d}\right)}\), and \(q=\frac{2\epsilon}{\tilde p\gamma}\). The theorem proves that \(\hat M\) is ex-post Nash truthful; with private reactions it is strictly truthful. It also proves an additive implementation error of \(6\sqrt{\frac{d}{\tilde p\gamma n}}\sqrt{\ln\left(\frac{n\tilde p\gamma|S|}{2d}\right)}\), in ex-post Nash equilibrium or, with private reactions, in strictly dominant strategies.

The corresponding continuous problem is:

Anonymous Approximate Implementation\(_\infty\). An instance consists of a finite type set \(T\), finite alternatives \(S\), reaction sets and utilities, a distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), and an aggregate objective \(F(\mu,s)\). The mass \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of a nonatomic society of type \(t\). A unit of mass receives one integral reaction; only the reported population distribution is continuous. Assume the continuum analogue of sensitivity, namely that moving mass \(\rho\) between two types changes \(F(\mu,s)\) by at most \(d\rho\), and assume a separating reaction system with gap \(\gamma\).

The task is to output an anonymous direct mechanism \(\mathcal M\) mapping reported distributions \(\nu\) to a distribution over \(S\), together with report-dependent reaction restrictions, such that truthful reporting is an ex-post Nash equilibrium and \(F(\mu,\cdot)\) under truthful play is within \(\eta\) of \(\max_{s\in S}F(\mu,s)\). With private reactions, the requirement can be strict dominance. A natural solution is the continuum rule \(\mathcal E_\lambda(\nu)(s)\propto e^{\lambda F(\nu,s)}\), mixed with a report-independent separating commitment mechanism.

This is a genuine population continuization: \(\mu\) is the society, not a lottery over outcomes. The price, facility set, public-good alternative, or other \(s\) remains an ordinary integral social decision. The discrete instance with \(n_t\) agents is recovered by \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), so the mirror is exactly the high-multiplicity relaxation.

I would expect this problem to be Class A when \(T\) and \(S\) are explicit and finite. The mechanism is described by finite probability formulas and the aggregate objective is evaluated from \(\mu\). If \(S\) is given implicitly, the computational bottleneck becomes the underlying optimization over \(S\), and hardness may transfer from that outcome space rather than from population multiplicity.

A particularly convincing concrete anchor is Corollary 3, proved in the paper’s pricing application. It states that for every fixed cohort size \(D\), there is \(N_0\) such that for all \(N>N_0\), the digital-goods mechanism \(M_{dg}\) implements the average-revenue objective \(F\) in ex-post Nash equilibrium with error \(O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{m^2}{N}\ln\left(\frac{N}{m}\right)}\right)\). Here there are \(ND\) buyers, prices lie on an \(m\)-dependent finite grid, and a unilateral signal change affects at most the \(D\) members of one cohort, making the objective \(D\)-sensitive. This corollary is derived in the paper from its general theorem.

The continuous counterpart is:

Digital-Goods Pricing\(_\infty\). Fix a finite price grid \(P\subseteq[0,1]\). A cohort type \(\theta\) specifies the complete signal or valuation profile of a \(D\)-member cohort, with valuations \(v_1(\theta),\ldots,v_D(\theta)\). The society is a distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(\Theta)\) over cohort types. Thus \(\mu_\theta\) is the fraction of cohorts of type \(\theta\), while every individual buyer still either buys or does not buy.

For price \(p\), the per-buyer revenue is \(R(\mu,p)=\sum_{\theta}\mu_\theta\frac{p}{D}\sum_{d=1}^{D}\mathbf 1[v_d(\theta)\ge p]\). The mechanism receives type reports, chooses a price, and may impose the buy/not-buy reaction associated with each reported type. Given \(\mu\) and tolerance \(\eta\), the task is to output a finite-description anonymous mechanism whose truthful expected revenue is at least \(\max_{p\in P}R(\mu,p)-\eta\), while truthful reporting is an ex-post Nash equilibrium. For private values, strict dominance is the natural strengthened version.

This is a very plausible mirror of the authors’ problem. The paper already identifies repeated cohorts as the source of correlation and uses average revenue as its objective. Replacing a long list of repeated cohort types by their distribution is precisely the high-multiplicity version. A market with millions of buyers but a small number of recurring customer or risk profiles is a convincing regime: \(|\Theta|\ll N\), while the mass \(\mu_\theta\) is the economically meaningful quantity.

The expected classification is again Class A. With an explicit price grid, the exact optimum is found by scanning the prices, and the exponential-plus-commitment construction gives the paper’s strategic guarantee. Further questions would concern continuous valuation supports, a growing price grid, growing cohort size \(D\), or robustness against coordinated deviations by positive-mass coalitions.

A second concrete anchor is Corollary 5, the paper’s facility-location result usually labelled the \(\hat M_{LOC2}\) corollary. It proves that, for sufficiently large \(n\), \(\hat M_{LOC2}\) implements optimal \(K\)-facility location in strictly dominant strategies with error \( \frac{6m}{\sqrt n}\sqrt{\ln\left(\frac{n(m+1)^K}{2m^2}\right)} \). The result uses locations on \(L=\{0,\frac1m,\ldots,1\}\), \(K\) facilities, private reactions, and a commitment distribution supported on adjacent facility configurations.

Its continuous mirror is:

Facility-Location\(_\infty\). The input is a finite location grid \(L\), a fixed number \(K\) of facilities, and a population distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(L)\), where \(\mu_x\) is the fraction of residents at location \(x\). The outcome is an integral facility tuple \(s\in L^K\); no fractional facilities are introduced. Each unit of mass is assigned to a nearest facility, and the objective is \(C(\mu,s)=\sum_{x\in L}\mu_x\min_j|x-s_j|\).

The task is to output an anonymous mechanism over \(L^K\), with report-dependent assignments, such that truthful location reports are strictly dominant and the expected cost is at most \(\min_{s\in L^K}C(\mu,s)+\eta\). The paper’s adjacent-facility commitment distribution is a direct candidate for the commitment component.

This is also Class A for fixed \(K\) on the line: the underlying aggregate optimum is the one-dimensional \(K\)-median problem and can be solved by dynamic programming or an equivalent structured optimization method. The continuous population is not the same as the paper’s continuous location set in Section 5. There, the society is still finite and only the type domain is continuous; here the population itself is represented by \(\mu\).

The weakest point is that nonatomicity makes an individual’s effect on the aggregate outcome literally zero. Consequently, differential privacy and the finite-\(n\) influence bounds lose some of their strategic force; in the pure limit, truthful reporting can become nearly automatic, and the commitment mechanism carries most of the incentive content. A hostile reader could therefore call the mirror strategically thinner than the original paper.

I do not think that defeats the positive case. The paper’s own central principle is that vanishing individual influence makes near-optimal implementation possible. The continuous model is the exact limit of that principle, and its remaining computational question is still meaningful: compute aggregate-optimal prices or facilities, represent the outcome rule over a distribution of types, and preserve the paper’s reaction and implementation structure. The honest claim is not that this paper supplies a new hardness landscape. It supplies a strong, recognizable Class A population mirror whose finite instances are precisely the paper’s high-multiplicity large-market regime.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The best case against is not that a continuous population is intrinsically meaningless here. It is that all three proposed anchors turn a large-market mechanism-design theorem into a computational theorem that the paper never states, and the natural population quotient removes the paper’s substantive strategic object.

The Main Theorem is not a complexity result. It quantifies over arbitrary finite environments and arbitrary \(d\)-sensitive objectives \(F\), but specifies no input encoding, computational model, running time, or algorithmic access to \(F\). Its exponential mechanism requires evaluating \(F(t,s)\) for every \(s\in S\) and normalizing the resulting probabilities. Its commitment mechanism requires computing the reaction \(r_i(t,s)\) and finding a separating distribution. With explicit finite tables this is merely finite enumeration; with succinctly represented \(S\), \(F\), or reactions, the computational difficulty is unspecified and may be entirely outcome-side. Thus there is no named computational problem whose continuous complexity landscape the theorem identifies.

More importantly, the theorem does not naturally quotient by a type distribution. Its primitives are labelled and potentially heterogeneous: \(T_i\), \(R_i\), \(u_i\), and even the dependence of \(u_i\) and \(r_i\) on \(t_{-i}\) may vary with \(i\). Two labelled profiles can have the same histogram of individual types but different utilities, reactions, or objective values. To make \(\mu\) sufficient, one must impose exchangeability, anonymous reactions, and an aggregate representation of \(F\). That is a legitimate new model, but it is not a continuization of the theorem’s general interdependent-value environment. If one instead puts role, cohort membership, or all relevant context into the type, the distribution either ceases to have repeated types or changes the strategic unit being modelled.

There is also a more fundamental limit. In a nonatomic society, one individual’s report changes \(\mu\) by zero. Hence any outcome rule depending only on the reported distribution is completely insensitive to unilateral reports. Without imposition, reports are payoff-irrelevant and truthfulness is merely one among many weak best responses. Strict dominance disappears. The paper’s finite-\(n\) trade-off between privacy, influence, commitment, and the \(n^{-1/2}\) approximation rate therefore has no nontrivial infinite-population counterpart.

The proponent’s addition of report-dependent reaction restrictions does not repair this without changing the object. If restrictions depend on each individual’s own report, the mechanism must retain a report field or individual-level information in addition to \(\mu\); a distribution alone is insufficient. If instead a positive mass is allowed to deviate together, the solution concept becomes coalition or mass-truthfulness, not the ex-post Nash or dominant-strategy notion in the paper. And if the aggregate outcome is computed from \(\mu\) while imposition handles incentives pointwise, the strategic theorem has split into a trivial aggregate optimization plus a local punishment rule. That may be a useful model, but it is not the paper’s computational content.

The pricing corollary does not escape this problem. Its \(D\)-sensitivity comes from a finite cohort: one member’s signal can affect the valuations of the other \(D-1\) named members. Replacing the \(N\) cohorts by a distribution over cohort types is economically plausible, but a single cohort then has zero mass and cannot affect the price. A positive-mass deviation is a coalition of cohorts, while a single buyer’s report affects only whatever reaction restriction the mechanism imposes locally. The paper’s pricing theorem has consequently lost its central influence argument. If one ignores incentives and asks only for

\[ R(\mu,p)=\sum_{\theta}\mu_\theta \frac{p}{D} \sum_{d=1}^{D}\mathbf 1[v_d(\theta)\ge p], \]

one has a sensible high-multiplicity revenue calculation, but not a computational mirror of Corollary 3: it is simply aggregate pricing over a weighted type histogram. Allowing continuous valuations or an implicit price space would make a new optimization problem, not recover the theorem’s missing computational content.

The facility-location corollary is the strongest positive case, but it fails for the same reason. Its natural population version is weighted \(K\)-median,

\[ \min_{s\in L^K}\sum_{x\in L}\mu_x\min_j |x-s_j|. \]

That is a perfectly sensible high-multiplicity object. Yet the paper’s result is not merely the computation of this objective; it is strict dominant-strategy implementation by using report-dependent imposition. In a nonatomic population, the facility tuple is unaffected by one person’s report. Without imposition, reports do not matter. With imposition, truthful reporting is enforced pointwise by assigning the reaction appropriate to the report, while the global computation is just weighted facility location. The adjacent-facility commitment distribution is no longer doing the finite-population work expressed by the paper’s \(\gamma\), \(q\), and \(n\)-dependent analysis.

Section 5 does not create a novelty collision: it continuizes the location domain while keeping the society finite, which is outside ChoCo’s target. But it underscores the distinction. The paper contains continuous domains and large-market limits; it does not contain a computational theory of societies represented by distributions.

So the negative case is weakest against the facility-location narrative: weighted \(K\)-median over a population measure is genuinely sensible. What it does not provide is a worthwhile continuous mirror of this paper’s named theorem. To obtain one, the analyst must replace the paper’s labelled strategic environment by an anonymous aggregate optimization problem and add a separate pointwise imposition device. That may deserve study in its own right, but it is a new high-multiplicity optimization model, not a computational continuation of the results proved here.
