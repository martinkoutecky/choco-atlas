The case FOR (proponent)

I cannot give a qualifying anchored case: the paper has no named computational result asserting tractability, hardness, or a parameterized complexity classification. Theorem 1 proves the many-to-one identity between individually rational worker-quasi-core and worker-quasi-stable matchings; Theorem 2 proves its firm-side counterpart; Theorem 3 gives a many-to-many inclusion; Theorem 4 proves equality between worker-quasi-setwise-stable and worker-quasi-stable matchings; and Theorem 5 relates joint worker- and firm-quasi-stability to pairwise stability and the core. These are structural results, not complexity results, so none qualifies as an anchor under the requested standard.

There is a plausible population regime behind a *future* mirror: large labor-market cohorts in which workers share complete preference types and employers share complete substitutable choice-function types, including relevant capacities. The type masses would be fractions of workers and employers; an allocation could distribute each type’s mass over feasible discrete partner bundles. A continuous worker-quasi-setwise-stability question could then ask whether such an allocation admits a positive-mass coalition deviation that leaves workers’ existing links intact and weakly benefits coalition members, with a positive mass strictly benefiting. That keeps the society continuous while retaining the paper’s matching and stability concepts. It is an extension one could discuss with the authors, but it is not a mirror anchored in a named computational result of this paper.

The regime is most credible when both sides have many participants and relatively few complete behavior types, as in repeated large labor cohorts. The weak point is substantial: the paper neither analyzes such a mass-allocation model nor poses a computational problem. Moreover, the finite-market requirement that *at least one* coalition member strictly benefits needs care in an atomless population, where a single agent has zero mass. So the paper supports a plausible continuous research direction, but not the requested complexity-mirroring case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a continuous mirror is that the paper supplies no computational result to mirror. Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 characterize individually rational quasi-core matchings in many-to-one markets; Theorem 3 gives an inclusion, Theorem 4 an equality of stability sets, and Theorem 5 a relation between stability and the core. None classifies an algorithmic problem by complexity, gives an exact or approximate algorithm, or proves hardness. The appended case proposes no qualifying anchor either. Its strongest candidate, Theorem 4, is a structural characterization, not a computational result.

The proposed labor-cohort model is plausible: workers and firms could be grouped by complete choice-function types, with mass distributed over partner bundles. The atomless strict-improvement issue can be handled by requiring a positive mass of agents to benefit. Those are not persuasive objections to the model. They still leave the mirror without a computational question supplied by the paper. Asking whether a quasi-stable allocation exists is trivial in the paper’s setting, since the empty matching is quasi-stable. Asking for complexity of membership or optimization would require specifying a new input, objective, or decision problem. That could become worthwhile research, but it would be a computational extension built around this paper’s concepts, rather than a mirror of one of its results.

So I would reject this paper as an anchored continuous-complexity mirror.
