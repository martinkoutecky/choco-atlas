The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that the paper supplies no qualifying computational result to mirror. Its numbered equations formulate 0–1 optimization models, and it solves one \(4\times6\) instance; it proves no complexity, approximation, or algorithmic result. So there is no paper result from which to derive the requested anchor and continuous question.

The best replacement would be a high-multiplicity version of model (M–5): group agents by their complete evaluation profiles, missing-information patterns, and regret parameters; give each type a mass; and optimize a transport plan over compatible type pairs. Regret comparisons could be computed against the distribution of opposite-side types, with the paper’s bilateral satisfaction and matching-willingness objectives applied to the resulting mass flows. That is coherent. For repeated, genuinely interchangeable suppliers and demanders, the mass assignment represents individual one-to-one matches without tracking names. I cannot reject it on grounds of identity, fractional outcomes, or because its answer might be tractable.

What weakens it as a *mirror of this paper* is that the paper never poses a population-size question. Its scoring recipe compares each agent’s options across named counterpart agents, then normalizes satisfaction and willingness across the finite matrices. A type-level version must choose how those comparisons and normalizations depend on counterpart masses, and how unmatched mass is handled. Reasonable choices exist, but they define a companion model rather than extend a computational result the paper established.

That is the honest negative case: no paper-anchored computational mirror is available. It does not support the universal claim that no scenario could make a continuous version worthwhile. A large platform with repeated agent profiles could make the proposed mass-matching problem sensible, so the broader negative case is weak.
