Why no mirror

The mass-allocation coalition question is statable and could be a worthwhile extension. But the paper has no numbered computational result to anchor it: its NP-completeness statement is unnumbered and attributed to prior work, while its large-scale study reports empirical results. The required computational anchor is therefore absent.

What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers coalition formation and scaled utilitarian welfare; it leaves the paper's experimental search results, benchmark comparisons, and general partition-function-game formulation outside the mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does a principled normalization of the relative-flexibility term preserve the finite model's intended incentives under a continuum population?
  • For unrestricted support size \(\tau\), what are the exact and approximation complexity of this mass-allocation problem, and does any discrete hardness result transfer?

The case FOR (proponent)

A credible mirror exists for the coalition formation problem. In a VPP or feeder with many similar households, define a type by the attributes the game uses: flexibility volume and profile, price, consumer category, location, and demand-response history. The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over those types. This is a plausible high-multiplicity regime: repeated household and device profiles could give \(n\gg\tau\). The paper’s 20,310-consumer case makes the setting concrete, though it does not report \(\tau\), so that regime remains to be checked.

Call the candidate Continuous Flexibility Coalition Formation. Given types \(T\), masses \(\mu_t\), their attributes, utility weights \(a_1+a_2+a_3=1\), and a finite operational cap \(K_{\max}\), choose \(K\le K_{\max}\) nonempty coalitions and masses \(x_{tk}\ge0\) such that \(\sum_k x_{tk}=\mu_t\). Thus each unit of mass belongs to one coalition, while a type’s mass may be split across coalitions. For coalition \(k\), let \(q_k=\sum_t x_{tk}\), \(\nu_{tk}=x_{tk}/q_k\), and let \(\bar M_k\) and \(\bar V_k\) be its mass-weighted average price and flexibility. Compute \(D_k\) from the paper’s normalized, mass-weighted standard deviations of the coalition’s characteristics.

For a unit of type \(t\) in coalition \(k\), use the utility
\[ \tilde u_{tk}=\bar M_k\left(a_1\frac{V_t}{\bar V_k} +a_2\frac{1}{K}+a_3(1-D_k)\right). \]
The question is to return a coalition count and mass allocation maximizing
\[ W=\sum_{k,t}x_{tk}\tilde u_{tk}, \]
or, with rational input and an accuracy parameter \(\epsilon\), an allocation within \(\epsilon\) of the supremum. The relative-flexibility term compares a consumer with the coalition’s average consumer; this is the per-capita version of the paper’s individual share term, which would otherwise vanish when each agent has infinitesimal mass. The cap \(K_{\max}\) represents the finite number of aggregation slots available in the market.

This keeps the paper’s underlying decision: form coalitions under externalities, with player-specific non-transferable utility, and evaluate the result by aggregate welfare. It does not replace coalition formation with clustering or make only the outcomes continuous. I would investigate hardness transfer first when \(\tau\) is unrestricted, since the coalition-assignment choices remain; whether the paper’s cited hardness applies to this particular utility family, and whether it gives approximation hardness, still needs proof. Bounded-\(\tau\) tractability is a separate question.

There is a strict anchor problem, though. The paper has no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational complexity result. Its closest statement is the unnumbered claim in Section III-A that finding the optimal coalition structure is NP-complete, attributed to [40] and [41], rather than proved there. The large-scale experiments are empirical results, not named complexity results. So the mirror above is a plausible research question, but it cannot honestly be presented as a continuous problem anchored to a qualifying named result from this paper.

That is also the mirror’s weakest point: the paper models named finite players, and the continuum version must let identical consumers split their mass among coalitions. The population setting and welfare objective are recognizable, but the type-level share term and finite coalition cap need the authors’ acceptance.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proposed anchor is coalition structure generation, but the paper does not prove a named computational result about its own model. Section III-A says that finding an optimal coalition structure is NP-complete and cites general results; it gives no numbered theorem or model-specific complexity analysis. The paper’s own computational evidence is a genetic algorithm evaluated experimentally. So Continuous Flexibility Coalition Formation may be a worthwhile new problem, but it is not a mirror of a qualifying result this paper established.

The proposed utility also does not preserve the flexibility-share term it claims to continuize. Since \(\bar V_k=\sum_t x_{tk}V_t/q_k\), its contribution to welfare is
\[ \sum_t x_{tk}\frac{V_t}{\bar V_k}=q_k. \]
It therefore measures coalition mass, rather than a player’s relative share of coalition flexibility. In the finite model, the term is \(V_i/\sum_{j\in C}V_j\). With a bounded number of coalitions, each individual’s share tends to zero as the population grows; rescaling it to keep the term consequential requires choosing a different normalization and changes how that part of the objective works. The paper does not settle which continuous objective its authors would recognize.

That is the strongest negative case I can support: the proposed question lacks a paper-specific computational result to mirror, and its stated welfare function needs repair. I cannot honestly turn that into the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists. A feeder with many repeated consumer profiles is a plausible high-multiplicity regime, and a corrected mass-allocation model could be worth studying. The evidence supports withholding a green on this proposed anchor, not ruling out every continuous mirror of the paper.
