The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s only anchor is that the paper has no named computational complexity result, and that is its strongest point. Lemma 1 characterizes payments that preserve DSIC; Theorems 1 and 3 establish weak budget balance; Theorem 2 bounds a redistribution ratio. None studies the running time or complexity of a computational problem. The simulations measure how much revenue is redistributed, not how difficult it is to compute a mechanism. So there is no result here of the kind ChoCo is set up to mirror.

A plausible high-multiplicity setting does exist: many electric-vehicle sessions or cloud jobs could share the same arrival window, departure window, and value. But the paper’s key constraints are about an individual’s counterfactual report. Its rebates use \(\theta_{-i}\), its critical payments depend on whether one agent changes the allocation, and Lemma 5 relies on one agent displacing one winner. In an atomless population, removing one agent leaves the type distribution unchanged. A fluid model can preserve a tagged, infinitesimal agent’s incentives, or study deviations by a positive mass, but those choices lead to different incentive questions; neither directly carries over the paper’s single-agent results.

For the last-period rule, the most natural fluid question would be about the limiting rebate ratio. Yet the paper already shows that this ratio approaches one as \(n\) grows for fixed \(T\). A fluid formulation would largely restate that asymptotic result. A fluid version of the departure-time rule could give a useful analysis of how rebates vary with overlap and value distributions, but that would extend the paper’s mechanism-design and simulation agenda; it would not supply the computational-complexity mirror ChoCo seeks.

So the negative case is strong for this paper as a source of ChoCo anchors: it has no qualifying computational result, and its central claims depend on individual deviations. The universal claim is weaker. One could formulate a new computational problem about optimizing redistribution over a fluid online market, but that would be a new research direction rather than a continuous mirror of a result in this paper.
