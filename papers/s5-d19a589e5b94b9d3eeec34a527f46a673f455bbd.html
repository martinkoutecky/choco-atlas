The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), symmetric rational affinities \(w_{ts}\), and an interaction graph on distinct types with edges where \(w_{ts}\ne 0\) and vertex-cover number at most \(4\), decide for rational \(r\) whether the mass can be partitioned into finitely or countably many nonzero coalition compositions \(x^j\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{T}\) satisfying \(\sum_j x^j_t=\mu_t\) and \(\frac{\sum_s w_{ts}x^j_s}{\sum_s x^j_s}\ge r\) whenever \(x^j_t>0\); equivalently, compute the maximum egalitarian welfare.

The model it lives in

A symmetric weighted fractional hedonic game over complete affinity types with rational masses: divide type mass among coalitions, give each type-\(t\) member in a coalition of composition \(x\) utility \(\frac{\sum_s w_{ts}x_s}{\sum_s x_s}\), and maximize the minimum utility, with vertex-cover number at most \(4\) in the type-interaction graph.

The objection that survived

Theorem 6 bounds the vertex cover of the individual-agent graph; cloning the four hubs makes that cover grow with multiplicity, while the proposed mirror bounds the type-interaction graph instead. The proponent acknowledges that the reduction does not establish continuous hardness, but does not address this specific change in the structural restriction.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the weighted egalitarian objective in Theorem 6’s fixed-cover regime. It leaves the utilitarian results and the treewidth, block-graph, and other vertex-cover algorithms in Theorems 2–5 aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Is threshold feasibility polynomial-time for type-interaction graphs with vertex-cover number at most \(4\), perhaps via a finite configuration formulation?
  • Does an independent hardness reduction survive when type mass can split among coalitions under this fixed-cover restriction?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead is Four-Hub Continuous Egalitarian FHG Welfare. The anchor is Theorem 6, proved in this paper: *Egalitarian Welfare Maximization is NP-hard on graphs of bounded vertex cover number.* The proof gives hardness even when the vertex cover has size four, by reducing from Partition. Its instance has four cover vertices and one peripheral vertex per input integer; the partition of those peripheral vertices determines whether the target minimum utility can be reached. The theorem concerns weighted symmetric fractional hedonic games.

Here is the continuous problem I would hand to a prover. An instance has a finite set \(T\) of agent types, symmetric rational affinities \(w_{ts}=w_{st}\), and positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one. The type interaction graph has an edge between types with nonzero affinity and is required to have vertex cover size at most four. A solution is a finite or countable collection of coalition compositions \(x^j\in\mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{T}\) such that \(\sum_j x^j_t=\mu_t\) for every type \(t\). Each infinitesimal agent belongs to one coalition; the mass of a type may be divided among coalitions. For a coalition \(j\) of mass \(M_j=\sum_s x^j_s>0\), every member of type \(t\) in it gets utility
\[ U(t,j)=\frac{\sum_s w_{ts}x^j_s}{M_j}. \]
The task is to maximize \(\inf_{j,t:x^j_t>0}U(t,j)\). Equivalently, given a rational threshold \(r\), ask whether there is such an allocation in which every occupied type-coalition pair has utility at least \(r\).

The paper’s reduction gives a particularly direct test family: use four hub types and one peripheral type for each Partition item, with the same symmetric edge weights as in its construction and equal mass \(1/(n+4)\) for every type. The type interaction graph still has a vertex cover of size four. A finite high-multiplicity realization has \(q\) copies of each type, so it has \(q(n+4)\) agents but only \(n+4\) types. The setting could be repeated project-team formation: many agents share one of a set of skill-and-role profiles, affinities depend on the two profiles, and teams are judged by the least satisfied member. In the theorem’s gadget, four recurring hub profiles interact with many peripheral specialist profiles.

This is a close analogue of the authors’ underlying question: the population is now mass over complete affinity types, while agents still form coalitions and value the average affinity within their coalition. The decision is an allocation of the population, not an encoding or an axiomatic question. The mass denominator is the continuum counterpart of coalition size. This is population continuization; it does not merely make the coalition outcomes fractional while leaving a finite society.

For this fixed-four-cover mirror, I would expect tractability (Class A), though that is a conjecture about the mirror, not a result of the paper. The Partition gadget relies on assigning each peripheral item wholly to one side. In the continuous model its type mass can be divided, so the subset-sum choice that drives the reduction may disappear. More broadly, types outside the cover do not interact with one another; their contribution to coalition utilities is mediated by at most four hub types. That suggests a configuration or separation approach over hub compositions. The natural follow-up questions are whether threshold feasibility has a polynomial-time LP or convex formulation, whether its pricing problem is tractable, and how the answer changes as the type-graph cover grows.

The weakest point is exactly that the anchor’s reduction does not establish anything about this continuous problem. Its \(n\) peripheral items remain \(n\) distinct types, and permitting their masses to split may erase the hardness mechanism. Also, the many-copy regime is a natural equitable blow-up of the paper’s instances, not a regime studied by the authors. I still think the mirror is worth asking: it preserves their agents, affinities, coalition utility, and egalitarian objective, while making explicit the computational question of what happens when those types recur at scale.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 6 is the proponent’s strongest anchor, but its “four” counts individual vertices in the paper’s graph. Those four hubs are single agents. If each type is repeated \(q\) times to create the proposed high-multiplicity population, the agent graph becomes a weighted blow-up: its cover may require all \(4q\) hub copies. So it no longer lies in the graph class of Theorem 6.

Keeping just four hub agents does not fix the mirror. In a population limit they have zero mass, and an egalitarian objective over the population no longer captures their individual utility constraints. The proposed type-interaction graph, with cover size four, repairs this by changing the structural parameter from vertex cover of the agent graph to vertex cover of a quotient over types. That is a coherent new model, but Theorem 6 does not establish hardness for it; the mirror would need its own justification.

This is a real gap in the claimed connection to the theorem, not an objection that the continuous answer might be easy. And the project-team story makes repeated affinity types plausible. The strongest repaired version therefore remains a sensible continuous FHG question. I can reject the direct Theorem 6 mirror, but I cannot honestly turn that into the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists. The negative case is weak precisely at that repaired type-level model.
