The case FOR (proponent)

The best positive foothold is that this paper’s *model* has a plausible high-multiplicity regime, but it has no named computational result to anchor the requested mirror.

The named results are about existence and refinement: Theorem 1 says consistent conjectures make the solution set nonempty and include the candidate matchings; Proposition 1 establishes consistency and nonemptiness for CVR dynamic stability; Corollary 1 establishes nonemptiness for sophisticated dynamic stability; and Proposition 2 shows sophisticated dynamic stability is contained in dynamic stability. None classifies an algorithmic problem as tractable or hard, or gives an algorithm with a running-time bound. These are existence results, not computational-complexity anchors.

A plausible setting for a continuous extension would be a large, repeated placement market: each period, cohorts of applicants and positions arrive, with many agents sharing the same side, arrival period, discount factor, and full utility profile over counterpart types. Mass would represent each type’s share of the cohort; the decision variable would be flows of mass matched across types and periods, with matches irreversible and unmatched mass carried forward. One could ask whether such flows satisfy a continuum version of CVR or sophisticated dynamic stability. That is a coherent population-continuum question, though defining pairwise blocking for an atomless population would require care.

But that would be a new existence question, not a continuous mirror of a named computational result in this paper. I therefore can’t honestly supply the required anchor-backed complexity question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that this paper has no computational result to mirror. Theorem 1 gives a sufficient condition for nonemptiness; Proposition 1 and Corollary 1 establish nonemptiness for two solution concepts; Proposition 2 compares those concepts. None poses a decision, optimization, or complexity problem.

The cohort-market scenario is coherent. With types recording side, arrival period, discount factor, and the full utility profile, rational masses and flows of irreversible one-to-one matches could represent a high-multiplicity market. Individual matches can remain integral while the type-level flows are fractional. I would not reject this scenario on grounds of identity, multiplicity, or the matching formalism.

The difficulty is what the mirror would ask. Extending Theorem 1 to mass allocations would be an existence question about a continuous stability concept. That could be mathematically worthwhile, but it is outside ChoCo’s computational brief. Turning it into a computational question—say, finding a CVR-stable mass allocation—requires new choices about how to encode the recursively defined conjectures and how to represent blocking and continuation values for flows. The paper supplies no canonical computational task or complexity claim for that work. Those choices might yield a worthwhile project, but they would create a new agenda rather than mirror one of this paper’s named results.

So the negative case is strong for this paper as an input to the programme: its natural continuous lift remains an existence problem. I cannot make the universal claim airtight; a carefully specified algorithmic problem for the cohort model might prove worthwhile. But the paper itself gives no anchor for that problem, and that is the clearest reason to pass on it as a continuous computational mirror.
