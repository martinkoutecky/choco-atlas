The case FOR (proponent)

My strongest honest case is the paper’s order-design result, not a claim that its hardness result survives continuization. A plausible regime is a large hiring market with recurring positions and applicants in a modest number of preference types: the number of people is very large relative to the number of types, and members of a type share the preferences and other features the market uses.

Here is a concrete mirror. Let \(F\) and \(W\) be finite sets of firm and worker types, with rational masses \(\mu_f\) and \(\nu_w\). Each unit of firm mass represents a one-position firm; each unit of worker mass represents a worker. A type specifies a strict ranking over acceptable types on the other side. A feasible matching is a flow \(x_{fw}\geq 0\), with \(\sum_w x_{fw}\leq\mu_f\) and \(\sum_f x_{fw}\leq\nu_w\). The target \(x^Q\) is the worker-optimal stable flow given by worker-proposing deferred acceptance in this mass market.

Call the proposed problem Mass-SPE Order Implementation. Its instance is those type sets, masses, rankings, and acceptable pairs. A solution is a schedule ordering the acceptable type-pair offer blocks, consistent with each firm type’s preferences, and a proof that the resulting sequential mass game has an aggregate subgame-perfect outcome equal to \(x^Q\). At the block for \((f,w)\), the available mass can accept or reject; accepted mass is matched and leaves, and rejected mass remains available for later offers. Strategies specify decisions after every history, and the outcome is the resulting matching flow. The continuous question is whether one can construct a schedule for which the aggregate SPE outcome is uniquely \(x^Q\).

The anchor is Theorem 5.1, proved in this paper: for every finite matching instance \(I\), there is an offering order \(\sigma\in\Sigma_I\) such that \(\spe(I,\sigma)=\qopt(I)\). The authors also give a polynomial-time construction. In full, this says that whatever the preference profile, one can order firms’ acceptable offers so that the unique SPE matching is the worker-optimal stable matching. The mirror asks whether that same market-design objective can be achieved when the agents are represented by masses of preference types and the outcome is a flow.

I expect this mirror to be tractable: compute \(x^Q\) by mass deferred acceptance, then adapt the paper’s construction, which places offers better than the target first and orders the remaining target pairs using the DA acceptance order. That is a research expectation, not a consequence already proved by Theorem 5.1. Natural follow-up questions are whether the construction runs in time polynomial in the number of types, acceptable type pairs, and mass bit length; whether fractional type splits need extra tie-breaking rules; and whether schedules for finite clone markets converge to the mass schedule and its outcome.

The scope here is deliberately just the paper’s result on implementing worker-optimal stability by designing the offer order. The paper’s computational results about predicting SPEs are not thereby mirrored.

The soft spot is the game definition at the mass level. A continuum permits fractional accepted mass, while the paper’s decision makers are individual workers whose choices can change which future offers occur. I have specified a type-block mass game to make the mirror concrete, but its aggregate SPE need not inherit the paper’s uniqueness or its order construction. That is the main fact a prover—and ultimately the authors—would need to accept. The case is still plausible because it keeps the paper’s sequential offers, irrevocable acceptance and rejection, strategic anticipation, and order-design target; the continuum is in the population itself, and the flow is the natural aggregate matching outcome.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest anchor is Theorem 5.1: for any finite matching instance, the authors construct an offer order whose unique SPE outcome is the worker-optimal stable matching. The best continuous question is whether, in a large market with finitely many preference types and rational masses, one can schedule type-pair offers so that the unique aggregate SPE flow is the worker-optimal stable flow.

The obstacle is not that matching markets lack plausible high-multiplicity regimes. They have them, and the flow version of deferred acceptance gives a natural target. The trouble is that this theorem’s implementation mechanism depends on *individual* decisions and their effect on the remaining game. At each node, one worker’s accept or reject action contracts or deletes a particular pair. The construction orders those individual offers using the DA acceptance sequence. A mass flow can split one worker type across several firm types, so it has no single target partner or individual acceptance event to order.

A type-pair block schedule does not by itself fix this. If the whole worker mass acts together, the type is being treated as a coordinated player, which is not the high-multiplicity version of individual workers making choices. If workers act individually, an atomless worker’s deviation changes zero aggregate mass, and the flow state does not say which workers get scarce future offers. Pro-rata rationing would make those future assignments lotteries; strict ordinal preferences alone do not determine how a worker compares a current offer with a lottery. A deterministic priority rule would restore individual continuations, but it adds a micro-order over workers that the finite-support type distribution does not contain.

So the proponent’s proposed schedule construction is not yet a continuous mirror: its crucial “adapt the DA acceptance order” step has no defined counterpart until the game specifies how offers are rationed and how workers compare the resulting continuations. Those choices could yield a worthwhile new model, but they would determine the equilibrium question rather than merely implement the paper’s theorem. My negative case is therefore a serious modeling objection, not a proof that no author-recognizable mirror exists. Given the natural mass-matching regime, I cannot honestly make the universal “no worthwhile mirror in any scenario” case airtight.
