The continuous mirror question

Given finitely many advertiser classes \(r\), rational class masses \(\mu_r\), finite rational type distributions \(h_r\), finite state spaces \(\Theta_r\), finite full-information experiments \(\tau_r\), and values \(v_r(t;\theta)\), with agents’ states independent, consider a unit-mass population with supply \(q\) indivisible items per unit mass and unit demand per agent. Compute an exact revenue-maximizing symmetric direct mechanism whose allocation probability and payment depend on an agent’s class, report, signal, and the aggregate measure of reports and signals. Require feasibility \(\int x\,d\nu\le q\) for every aggregate input measure \(\nu\), individual rationality under truthful full revelation, and ex-post IIC against truthful, fully revealing others for every type misreport and every garbling \(\lambda_r\) with \(\tau_r\succeq_{\mathrm{MPS}}\lambda_r\); maximize expected revenue per unit population mass. What is the complexity in the number of classes, support size, and input bit length?

The model it lives in

A unit-mass continuum of agents in finitely many classes, where \(\mu_r\) is class \(r\)'s mass and each agent has the class’s type distribution, information experiment, and value function. Agents report a type and reveal a signal; a symmetric direct mechanism maps those inputs and their aggregate distribution to individual allocation probabilities and payments, subject to per-capita supply, IIC, and IR. The objective is expected revenue per unit mass.

The case FOR (proponent)

Strictly under the requested anchor rule, this paper has no named result about a problem being in P, NP-hard, FPT, or another complexity class. Its strongest usable computational anchor is instead a constructive optimality result.

My lead anchor is Proposition 6.5, proved in the paper (with the proof in Appendix C): under information sufficiency, regular values, and regular virtual values, truthfulness is a Bayes–Nash equilibrium in the ex-post stage of the Simulated-Myerson Auction, and that auction achieves optimal revenue. The mechanism simulates Myerson’s auction at each possible state profile and averages the resulting allocations and payments according to the posterior. This is an optimal-mechanism result, not a runtime bound or a hardness result.

A plausible continuous question is High-Multiplicity Reticent Revenue Design. Consider a population of total mass \(1\), divided among finitely many advertiser classes \(r\), with rational masses \(\mu_r\). Each class has the same value function, finite private-type and state spaces, type distribution, and information experiment. The population’s support atoms can be written as \((r,t,\sigma)\), with mass determined by \(\mu_r\), the type distribution, and the full-information experiment. Agents sharing an atom are indistinguishable in every respect the mechanism uses. Assume independent state processes across agents, a subcase of the paper’s model.

Because a single item cannot be allocated in positive mass to a nonatomic population, let the auction sell a batch of \(q\) interchangeable items per unit of population mass. Each individual still receives an integral allocation, possibly through a lottery; type-level allocation rates can be fractional. The input is the rational class masses and the finite descriptions of values, type distributions, states, and experiments, along with \(q\). A solution is a finite exact description of a symmetric direct mechanism: from an agent’s reported type and signal, and the aggregate distribution of reports and signals, it specifies an allocation probability and payment. It must satisfy aggregate feasibility, individual rationality under truthful full revelation, and the paper’s ex-post IIC condition: against truthful, fully revealing others, no agent gains in expected utility by misreporting their type or using any experiment that is a Blackwell garbling of their full information. The objective is to maximize total expected revenue per unit of population mass.

The regime could be a large batch market for interchangeable ad impressions, with many campaigns using a small number of shared campaign templates. A class would include the campaign’s value model, type distribution, and data-sharing technology; its members could still have different private type and signal realizations. The number of agents grows with the batch, while the number of distinct classes stays moderate. That makes the population itself the continuous object. Scaling the supply with population is a necessary co-modification; the single-item model in Proposition 6.5 has no useful nonatomic limit with positive allocation.

For finite rational supports and the corresponding regularity assumptions, I would expect this problem to be tractable: the continuous analogue should use state-dependent virtual values and allocate the available mass by rank or threshold, while preserving the paper’s IIC logic. The precise result to seek is an exact algorithm, polynomial in the class count, support sizes, and input bit length, that outputs an optimal mechanism and verifies IIC against all allowable garblings. The theorem in the paper does not establish that algorithmic bound, and the batch-supply extension would need its own proof. Further questions include whether the mechanism has a finite exact representation, whether the algorithm remains tractable without regularity, and how discrete high-multiplicity instances approximate the continuous optimum.

I think the authors would recognize this as their auction-design problem: it retains private type misreporting, strategic information revelation, IIC, and revenue optimization. The weakest point is the change from one item to per-capita batch supply; it is defensible under the programme’s co-modification rule, but it is not the result they prove. The other limitation is sharper: if an anchor must literally be a complexity-class theorem, this paper provides none. Proposition 6.5 supports a constructive continuous analogue, not a claim that the paper has already charted computational complexity.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has no named computational-complexity result to continuize. Proposition 6.5 is the strongest candidate, but it establishes an optimal mechanism under regularity assumptions; it gives no algorithmic problem, running time, or complexity classification. The other named results likewise concern incentives, mechanism transformations, and optimality. That leaves ChoCo without the kind of computational anchor its programme is meant to classify.

The best attempt to turn Proposition 6.5 into such an anchor runs into a limit problem. With one indivisible item and \(N\) bidders, normalizing the population to mass \(1\) makes supply \(1/N\), which vanishes as \(N\) grows. A mass allocation then cannot represent giving the item to one bidder: the winner has zero population mass. Keeping total revenue instead makes the outcome depend on an extreme bidder, rather than on the distribution of bidder types. The proposed repair—supply \(q\) items per unit of population mass—does give a nondegenerate market, but changes the paper’s single-item auction into a multi-unit auction with a different feasibility constraint and mechanism-design problem. The authors themselves list multi-item allocation as future work. That looks like a worthwhile extension, but it is not a continuous mirror of Proposition 6.5 as stated.

This is the strongest case against the proponent’s anchor: the proposed population problem requires a substantive change to the auction, while the paper’s theorem supplies no computational result for that new problem. I would not press a broader objection that the change is out of scope, though. The programme permits supply to scale with population when that is needed for a meaningful limit, and the authors’ future-work discussion suggests they would recognize a batch auction as related.

So I cannot honestly defend the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario. A large market with repeated advertiser classes and per-capita impression supply is plausible, and the proponent has sketched a coherent question about revenue optimization and information compatibility there. My negative case is narrower: it is a new multi-unit research problem, not a continuization of a computational result this paper proves. The case is weak precisely where the programme allows that kind of co-modification.
