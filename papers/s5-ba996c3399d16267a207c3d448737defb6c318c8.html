The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The other analyst raises no continuous question tied to a named result, so there is no anchor for a continuous mirror to survive. That is the strongest objection here: the paper’s Propositions 1–6 characterize equilibria, allocations, and coalition selection. They do not state the complexity of a computational problem, give an algorithm, or classify an algorithmic task. A continuous version of one of those propositions would continue an equilibrium analysis, not the computational programme ChoCo is meant to advance.

The best nearby candidate would be to ask how hard it is to compute an equilibrium when there are many legislators or parties. But that requires defining a new problem and input: the paper’s strategic actors are parties whose identities, coalition options, and places in the formateur order matter. Grouping them into types would have to preserve those features, and in general would leave as many types as parties. A continuum of legislators with ideological or policy types could be a sensible model, but it would change the actors and strategic structure; it would not mirror a computational result in this paper.

This negative case is strong against treating the paper as a continuization target for ChoCo. It cannot establish the broader claim that no worthwhile continuous model could be inspired by the paper. Such a model may be valuable research, but the case for it would need to come from a newly posed computational question, not from the results this paper proves.
