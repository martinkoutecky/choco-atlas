The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The public consultation scenario is the strongest one, and it is a credible high-multiplicity setting: many submissions could share recurring argument types. I would not reject it on the grounds that submissions are individual or that minority views disappear in a continuum.

But the proposed task—choosing or organizing material for a summary or recommendation—needs a type definition, an output rule, and a measure of representativeness or error. The paper supplies none of these as a computational problem. Its discussion of consultation summarization motivates the idea and evaluates its democratic risks; it does not state an algorithmic result to mirror. A continuous model could make a worthwhile *new* research question, but it would not be a mirror of this paper’s result.

That is the decisive problem for the proponent’s case: this paper has no numbered computational result, and its discussion of computational social choice is comparison, not a result of its own. Citing results from the projects it surveys would not provide an anchor from this paper. So no proposed scenario can supply the requested result-by-result continuous mirror.

It is weaker as the broader claim that nothing worthwhile could ever be developed from the paper: the consultation idea could motivate new work.
