Why no mirror

Both anchors are computational mechanism results, but the proposed distribution is over patient-donor inventory units, not over the hospitals whose reporting incentives Theorems 1 and 2 study. A continuum of hospitals would change the finite strategic-agent setting, while the proposed pair-mass model leaves that strategic population finite and makes mass allocation the continuous object.

What the mirror covers

The proposed mirrors concern Theorems 1 and 2, the paper's two constructive mechanism results. Neither mirror covers their hospital-level strategic population; the lower bounds mentioned in related work are not numbered results proved in this paper.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest case is for a high-multiplicity version of the pair population in the exchange, while keeping hospitals as the strategic decision-makers. It is an extension of the paper’s model, not a claim that hospitals themselves become nonatomic.

A plausible regime is a large regional or national pool with many patient-donor pairs but a modest number of recurring medical compatibility profiles. Define a type \(t=(i,a)\) by its hospital \(i\) and its pair profile \(a\), detailed enough that compatibility is identical for every pair of types in the model. Let \(\mu_t\) be its population share, with support size \(\tau\), and retain a total population scale \(N\): the type has mass \(N\mu_t\), measured in patient-pair equivalents. This fits the high-multiplicity story when \(N\gg\tau\). A hospital can hide any submass of its reported types, and can privately match hidden pairs using only within-hospital compatibility.

An allocation is a mass matching: for each compatible unordered pair of types \(t,u\), choose an exchange mass \(x_{tu}\ge 0\). It must use no more than \(N\mu_t\) units of any type; a same-type exchange consumes two units. Hospital \(i\)’s utility \(U_i(x)\) is the mass of its pairs matched, and social welfare is \(\sum_i U_i(x)\). These flows still describe pairwise exchanges: in the continuum, each mass unit is paired with one compatible unit. They do not give an individual pair a fractional transplant.

Theorem 1, proved in this paper, is the lead anchor. It says that there exists a truthful randomized 2-approximation mechanism for multi-agent kidney exchange in which every hospital’s utility variance is at most \(2+\epsilon\), for any \(\epsilon>0\). The authors construct it by repeatedly combining outputs of Ashlagi et al.’s Mix and Match mechanism; the paper establishes polynomial running time for the construction. The corresponding continuous problem, Bounded-Risk Truthful Mass Kidney Exchange, is: given \(H\), a finite compatibility-profile set, its symmetric compatibility relation, \(N\), and rational type shares \(\mu\), design a uniform randomized polynomial-time rule \(F\) that maps every reported mass vector to a feasible mass-matching lottery. For every true \(\mu\), every hospital \(i\), and every hidden submass \(h_i\le N\mu_i\), truthful reporting must give at least as much expected utility as reporting \(N\mu_i-h_i\) and privately matching the hidden mass. On the truthful report, the rule must satisfy
\[ \mathrm{OPT}(N\mu)\le 2\,\mathbb E[\mathrm{SW}(F(N\mu))] \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{Var}(U_i(F(N\mu)))\le 2+\epsilon \]
for every hospital \(i\). The expectation and variance use pair-equivalent units.

I expect this mirror to be Class A, but the theorem does not already prove the mass version. Matching mass can be optimized with standard matching machinery, and repeated medical profiles offer a genuine support-compression regime. The missing result is a mass-native way to preserve truthfulness and the paper’s utility-balancing argument, with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log N\) (and \(1/\epsilon\)), rather than expanding a binary-encoded population into \(N\) explicit pairs. That is the computational question for a prover.

Theorem 2, also proved here, supports a separate mirror. It gives an almost-truthful deterministic 2-approximation mechanism for the multi-agent game, with deviation gain at most \(2\lceil\log_2 m\rceil\) matched vertices. Its continuous counterpart, Almost-Truthful Deterministic Mass Kidney Exchange, asks for a deterministic polynomial-time rule over the same typed mass reports that is a 2-approximation and limits every hospital’s gain from hiding any submass to \(2\lceil\log_2 m\rceil\) pair-equivalent units. I also expect this to be tractable if the authors’ balancing construction lifts to mass matchings. The paper’s limited-independence labeling argument suggests a concise construction, but the proof still needs to handle mass-valued exchanges and all submass deviations.

Neither anchor is a hardness result: the paper states no numbered NP-hardness or completeness theorem. The named results here are constructive approximation results; the discrete lower bounds in the related-work discussion are not this paper’s numbered anchors. Questions raised by the mirrors include whether the balanced-pairing lemma extends to rational mass flows, whether the guarantees admit support-sensitive algorithms, and whether exact truthfulness survives deviations that hide an arbitrarily small mass.

My weakest point is that kidney exchange graphs can encode pair-specific compatibility and identity. The mirror only applies where compatibility is genuinely determined by a relatively small set of repeated medical profiles; arbitrary compatibility graphs may have \(\tau\) nearly as large as the population. The strategic actors are still hospitals, while the continuized population is their stock of exchange pairs. That is a narrower regime than continuizing a population of voters, but it preserves the paper’s core actions—reporting pairs, matching pairs, and privately matching withheld pairs—rather than replacing its mechanism-design question. One scaling issue also matters: if utilities are normalized to shares in \([0,1]\), the variance bound becomes trivial. Keeping \(N\) and measuring utility in pair-equivalent units preserves the theorem’s intended low-risk guarantee; the deterministic theorem’s additive deviation bound should be interpreted in the same units.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the proposed mirror continuizes the hospitals’ inventory, not the population of agents whose incentives the paper studies. In the paper, a hospital is the agent: it chooses which vertices to report, may hide a subset, and receives utility from the vertices it serves. A patient-donor pair is a vertex the hospital controls, not an independent decision-maker. A distribution over hospital-and-pair profiles therefore gives a continuous supply of exchange units while retaining the paper’s finite, individually strategic hospitals. That may be a useful high-multiplicity matching problem, but it misses ChoCo’s population axis.

A mirror that continuizes hospitals instead has a harder fit. A hospital’s complete type would have to describe its portfolio and the compatibility relationships relevant to its exchanges. Pooling hospitals by a pair’s medical profile would discard which hospital can hide which vertices and whose utility is being measured. Pooling identical hospitals would need a regime with repeated complete portfolios and compatible network positions; aggregating them could also turn separate hospital deviations into a deviation by a whole mass of hospitals. The paper offers no natural repeated-hospital regime of that kind.

Even if pair-mass matching is accepted as the population mirror, the two named results lose much of their substance. In that model, feasible mass matchings are convex and hospital utility is linear in the flow. The barycenter of any lottery over mass matchings is itself a feasible mass matching, with the same expected utility and welfare. Returning that flow deterministically gives zero variance, so the \(2+\epsilon\) risk guarantee in Theorem 1 becomes automatic once the lottery and its barycenter can be computed. Theorem 2’s almost-truthful guarantee likewise loses its point: a truthful randomized mass mechanism’s barycenter is a deterministic, exactly truthful mass mechanism. The remaining challenge would be efficient construction of the mass mechanism or barycenter, not the paper’s risk-reduction or derandomization contribution.

Neither theorem is an absent computational result: both are named constructive mechanism results. And this is not a claim that the continuous answer would be dull; an efficient mass-native truthful mechanism could be a worthwhile result. The negative case is that its strategic population would still be finite, while the two headline guarantees become redundant under mass allocation.

That case is not airtight. A large pool of interchangeable patient-donor pairs controlled by a modest number of hospitals is plausible, and the authors might recognize its mass-matching question as their problem. If that interpretation passes ChoCo’s scope gate, the pair-mass mirror survives; I would argue against it, but I cannot honestly rule it out in every scenario.
