The continuous mirror question

Given finite supports of doctor and hospital types, rational type masses, capacities per hospital type, responsive rankings, and a target stable matching flow \(x_{ij}\), does there exist a finite preference-preserving augmentation and a finite sequence of mass-cohort entries and exits whose stable proposal-chain continuations reach \(x_{ij}\), with all temporary mass absent at termination?

The model it lives in

Represent doctors and hospitals by finite preference types with rational masses; let \(x_{ij}\) be the mass of type-\(i\) doctors assigned to type-\(j\) hospitals, subject to doctor supplies and hospital capacity \(q_j\) per hospital. Define stability by the absence of a positive mass of mutually blocking doctor-hospital pairs. A cohort proposal is processed by mass: each hospital type keeps its highest-ranked acceptable doctor mass up to its active capacity, and displaced mass re-proposes, splitting when needed. Temporary types may be added with preferences that preserve each original type’s rankings over original types.

The objection that survived

A cohort can split across partner types, so acceptance, displacement, and re-proposal of its mass need an explicit rule; different rules could give different reachable sets.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the augmented-history completeness results in Theorems 2 and 3. It leaves the terminal-agent obstruction in Theorem 1 and the minimum auxiliary-mass question aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the explicit scaffold construction extend when one preference type’s mass is split among several target hospital types under the stated fluid proposal-chain rule?
  • What support-size and bit-complexity bounds apply to a smallest supporting augmentation?

The case FOR (proponent)

There is a plausible high-multiplicity setting here: large matching cohorts with many agents sharing a finite set of complete preference types. A continuous version could represent each side by type masses, a target matching by a mass flow between types, and ask whether that target can be reached through stable histories with temporary cohorts.

But I can’t anchor that as requested. Theorem 1 proves that terminal-agent histories can miss a stable matching; Theorem 2 proves that a target-dependent augmentation restores completeness in marriage markets; Theorem 3 extends that existence result to responsive many-to-one markets. These are structural reachability results, not named complexity classifications or computational formulations. The introduction’s NP-completeness result for the random-order mechanism is cited from Cheng (2016), not proved as a numbered result here.

So the paper supplies no qualifying computational anchor for a continuous mirror. The cohort matching idea is a reasonable direction to investigate, but its history dynamics need a mass-level definition, and the paper’s target-specific temporary partners do not immediately extend when one preference type is split across several target partners. I would not claim a tractability, hardness-transfer, or continuum-specific-hardness prediction from these results alone.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that the paper has no computational result for ChoCo to continuize. Theorem 1 exhibits one finite market with an unreachable stable matching; Theorems 2 and 3 prove that suitable target dependent augmentations can reach every stable matching. None classifies the complexity of deciding reachability, finding a history, or optimizing an augmentation. The NP completeness result in the introduction is cited background about a different mechanism, not a result of this paper. Under the programme’s stated test, all three proposed anchors fail.

That is not because matching lacks a plausible high multiplicity regime. Large hiring cohorts with repeated preference types are credible, and existing high multiplicity work would support that regime. The issue is what the paper studies: its reachability results concern the structure of histories, not computational questions about them. A plausible population setting alone does not supply the missing computational anchor.

The best rescue would define type masses on both sides, represent a target matching as a flow between types, and ask whether that flow is reachable through histories of entering and exiting cohorts. One could also turn the paper’s open quantity \(\kappa_M(\mu)\) into a question about minimum auxiliary mass. Those are sensible research directions, but they require new transition rules and objectives. In the paper’s process, one identified entrant starts a proposal chain; in a nonatomic society, a single agent has zero mass. Cohort events or a limiting process could address that, but the choice affects what histories and transitions mean. The paper neither makes that choice nor derives a computational problem from it. Its finite constructions with private temporary partners do not establish how the resulting mass problem should be encoded or solved.

So the negative case is strong for this paper as a mirror candidate: none of the results the other analyst named survives as a computational anchor, and the more promising mass formulation would be a new project motivated by the paper. I would not claim that every such project is unworthy; the auxiliary mass question is the clearest weakness in a universal “no scenario” claim. But the proponent has not shown that any of these structural theorems yields a worthwhile continuous computational mirror.
