The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set \(T\) of worker types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and task roles \(J\) with rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), rewards \(r_j\), and type-specific parameters \((p_{tj},c_{tj})\), compute an optimal EFS contract. Each role's copies share a contract share \(\alpha_j\), and each worker receives an integral bundle \(B\subseteq J\). Let \(x_{t,B}\) be the mass of type-\(t\) workers receiving \(B\), and \(s_{t,B}\geq 0\) their subsidy. Require \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B:j\in B}x_{t,B}=b_j\), and effort feasibility \(\alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj}\geq 0\) for every assigned task in every occupied configuration. For every pair of occupied configurations \((t,B)\) and \((t',B')\), require \(\sum_{j\in B}(\alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj})+s_{t,B}\geq\sum_{j\in B'}\max\{\alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj},0\}+s_{t',B'}\). Maximize per-capita revenue \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}\bigl(\sum_{j\in B}(1-\alpha_j)p_{tj}r_j-s_{t,B}\bigr)\), or decide whether this optimum is at least a given rational \(R\).

The model it lives in

A distribution of worker mass over complete types and integral task bundles, with role-level contract shares, configuration-level subsidies, exact envy constraints between occupied configurations, and per-capita principal revenue as the objective.

What the mirror covers

This mirror covers Theorem 5.2's optimal EFS computation problem. It leaves the price-of-fairness results, the revenue comparison with EF contracts, the reduction to EF contracts, and the constant-task algorithm unmirrored.

Open questions for a prover

  • For variable \(|J|\), what is the complexity of Mass-EFS Contract Optimization, including whether two worker types already suffice for hardness?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest anchor is Theorem 5.2, proved in this paper. It says that, unless \(P=NP\), no polynomial-time algorithm computes an optimal envy-free contract with subsidies (EFS) in general. The reduction is from strongly NP-complete 3-Partition: it builds \(q+1\) agents and \(3q+1\) tasks so that the principal can obtain revenue at least \(1/2\) exactly when the 3-Partition instance is feasible. This is a particularly promising anchor for a population mirror because the reduction already has only two complete agent types: one efficient agent and \(q\) otherwise identical normal agents. The normal agents have the same success probabilities and costs on every task.

My lead mirror is Mass-EFS Contract Optimization. Picture a large contracting platform with recurring task roles and cohorts of workers who share the same task-specific success probabilities and effort costs. The principal chooses task-level shares, assigns integral bundles of task roles to workers, and pays nonnegative subsidies. Workers compare their own bundle and subsidy with those of every other occupied worker configuration, exactly as in EFS. The number of workers can be enormous while the number \(\tau\) of worker types stays small.

Here is a finite formulation. The input has a finite set \(T\) of worker types, with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), and a finite set \(J\) of task roles. Type \(t\) specifies the complete vector of success probabilities and effort costs \((p_{tj},c_{tj})_{j\in J}\). Each role \(j\) has reward \(r_j\) and per-capita supply \(b_j\): a fraction \(b_j\) of the population receives one copy of that role. Copies of a role share the same task-level contract \(\alpha_j\). Each worker gets an integral bundle \(B\subseteq J\).

Let \(\lambda_{t,B}\) be the mass of type-\(t\) workers assigned bundle \(B\), and \(\sigma_{t,B}\geq 0\) their subsidy. The masses must satisfy
\[ \sum_{B\subseteq J}\lambda_{t,B}=\mu_t \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{B\ni j}\lambda_{t,B}=b_j \quad\text{for every }j\in J. \]
For every occupied configuration \((t,B)\), assigned tasks must satisfy
\[ \alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj}\geq 0 \quad (j\in B). \]
Define the worker’s utility from their own bundle as
\[ u_t(B;\alpha)=\sum_{j\in B}(\alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj}), \]
and their utility from considering another bundle as
\[ v_t(B';\alpha)=\sum_{j\in B'}\max\{\alpha_jp_{tj}r_j-c_{tj},0\}. \]
For every pair of occupied configurations, require
\[ u_t(B;\alpha)+\sigma_{t,B} \geq v_t(B';\alpha)+\sigma_{t',B'}. \]
The objective is to maximize per-capita revenue
\[ \sum_{t,B}\lambda_{t,B} \left( \sum_{j\in B}(1-\alpha_j)p_{tj}r_j-\sigma_{t,B} \right). \]
The computational question is: given rational \(R\), do there exist shares, configuration masses, and subsidies satisfying these conditions with revenue at least \(R\)? The output is a finite list of occupied configurations and their masses, together with the shares and subsidies. Workers of the same type receiving the same bundle must receive the same subsidy under exact envy-freeness, so configuration-level subsidies preserve the paper’s individualized-transfer feature.

This regime has a concrete connection to the theorem’s reduction. Replicate its instance \(K\) times: there are \(K\) efficient workers, \(Kq\) normal workers, and \(K\) copies of each task role. In normalized population units, the two worker masses are \(1/(q+1)\) and \(q/(q+1)\); each task role has supply \(1/(q+1)\). Thus the worker population grows while \(\tau=2\), and each person still receives an integral bundle. A 3-partition gives a feasible mass allocation at revenue \(1/[2(q+1)]\) per capita, by using the paper’s shares and assigning each triple as one bundle pattern. That is a direct and recognizable mirror of the paper’s EFS objective, effort constraints, envy comparisons, and revenue criterion. The scenario could be a platform repeatedly assigning a catalogue of task roles to large, interchangeable worker cohorts.

I would not claim that Theorem 5.2’s hardness transfers. Its no-instance proof eventually concludes that each of the \(q\) named normal agents must receive a triple summing to \(B\), hence those triples form an integral 3-partition. In the mass model, normal workers can be split across different integral bundle patterns. The same revenue threshold could therefore be reached by a fractional mixture of triples even when no integral partition exists. The exact step that proves the discrete lower side is precisely where the continuum may dissolve the reduction.

My provisional expectation for unrestricted Mass-EFS Contract Optimization is continuum-specific hardness (Class C), with modest confidence. The 3-Partition argument itself is not enough: its final integrality step disappears. But the mass model still chooses among exponentially many integral bundles, while simultaneously choosing shares and enforcing envy constraints across the bundles that receive positive mass. Pricing or selecting those bundles may retain difficult combinatorics. The central next questions are whether that configuration search has an efficient separation method, whether the two-type case is already hard as the number of task roles grows, and whether fractional 3-partition instances actually erase the theorem’s revenue gap.

This case covers Theorem 5.2 only. It does not mirror the paper’s price-of-fairness bound or its revenue-separation result. Its weakest point is the task-side scaling: recurring copies of a role share one contract, and each worker receives at most one copy of each role. That is a natural cohort model for repeated task slots, and the population—not the outcome space—is the object made continuous. Still, the theorem’s reduction gives finite named tasks, not recurring roles. If the authors reject that supply-scaling model, this particular mirror loses force; the resulting fractionalization of normal workers is also why I would treat the hardness claim as an open question rather than inherited from their theorem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The best case against the Theorem 5.2 mirror is that the reduction’s hardness comes from indivisible task items. It has \(3q+1\) distinct tasks, each assigned once, and its no-instance argument ends by forcing each named normal agent to receive an integral triple summing to \(B\). In the proposed Mass-EFS model, workers of one type can be split across bundle configurations. Replicating each task role \(K\) times also lets copies of an item role be distributed fractionally across worker groups. The reduction’s integral-partition argument therefore does not establish hardness for the continuous problem.

That is a real limit on what the theorem gives the mirror, but it does not defeat the better version of the mirror. A platform with recurring task roles and large worker cohorts is plausible; task supplies scaling with the population is an allowed co-modification. The resulting EFS optimization question is well-defined, and fractionalizing the partition may make it tractable—which is exactly the kind of change this programme is meant to investigate. The paper’s constant-task algorithm does not settle the variable-task case.

So the honest negative case is weak: Theorem 5.2’s hardness does not transfer automatically, but I cannot argue that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. This anchor survives.
