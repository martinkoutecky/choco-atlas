The strongest attempt to construct one would be to ask how hard it is to find or verify a robust mechanism for a large market described by masses of valuation types. But the paper’s conditions concern each seller’s and buyer’s possible report, and require budget balance at every valuation profile. A distribution of types does not by itself capture those pointwise constraints or the mechanism’s choices among individual traders. Keeping identities and unilateral deviations preserves the original strategic object, but leaves no clear population compression. Replacing them with aggregate type masses creates a different model: in an atomless market, one agent’s deviation has no effect on the aggregate, changing the incentive problem.

That makes this a poor fit for ChoCo’s computational continuization programme. A continuous-market model or a computational mechanism-synthesis project might be worthwhile on its own, but neither is a mirror anchored in this paper’s results. I cannot establish the literal universal claim that no scenario could ever make such a project worthwhile; the paper supports the narrower conclusion that no worthwhile computational mirror is apparent.
