The strongest salvage is a high-multiplicity cooperative game: many agents share a small number of types, and one asks how to compute Shapley allocations or boycott impacts from the type masses. That is a plausible regime. But this paper supplies neither a representation of coalition worths over type masses nor an algorithmic problem whose complexity could be studied. In an atomless population, deleting one agent or changing the interaction of one specified pair affects zero mass, so the individual-level deletion and boycott impacts can vanish. Boycotting whole positive-mass types could avoid that degeneration, but it changes the unit of the axiom from individual players to blocs. That may be a sensible new model, but it does not rescue a computational mirror of these results. The attached case raises no positive mirror that survives these gaps.
