The continuous mirror question

Given \(m\) binary public issues, \(\tau\) complete types \(t\) with preferred sides \(a_{tj}\), rational CES weights and budgets \(b_t\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to one, and rational tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\), compute an \(\varepsilon\)-approximate pairwise-pricing market equilibrium in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\). The output is a common randomized decision \(z\), per-type bundles \(y_t\), and symmetric nonnegative prices \(P_{tu,j}\) for disagreeing type pairs; with \(q_{tj}=\sum_{u:a_{uj}\ne a_{tj}}\mu_uP_{tu,j}\), each \(y_t\) maximizes its CES utility subject to \(\sum_jq_{tj}y_{tj}\le b_t\), each disagreeing pair satisfies \(y_{tj}+y_{uj}\le1+\varepsilon\) and \(P_{tu,j}>\varepsilon\Rightarrow y_{tj}+y_{uj}\ge1-\varepsilon\), and \(z^{j,0}+z^{j,1}\le1+\varepsilon\), \(y_{tj}\le z^{j,a_{tj}}+\varepsilon\), and \(q_{tj}>\varepsilon\Rightarrow y_{tj}\ge z^{j,a_{tj}}-3\varepsilon\).

The model it lives in

A society has type mass \(\mu_t\), and type \(t\) agents share their complete preferences, CES utility, and budget \(b_t\). For each issue and disagreeing type pair \(\{t,u\}\), use a pairwise commodity of supply \(\mu_t\mu_u\); per-capita demands \(y_{tj}\) and \(y_{uj}\) induce aggregate demands \(\mu_t\mu_u y_{tj}\) and \(\mu_t\mu_u y_{uj}\), with effective type price \(q_{tj}=\sum_{u:a_{uj}\ne a_{tj}}\mu_uP_{tu,j}\). The welfare target is \(W_\mu(z)=\exp\!\left(\frac{\sum_t\mu_tb_t\log u_t(z)}{\sum_t\mu_tb_t}\right)\).

The objection that survived

The paper does not establish that aggregating individual-pair goods into type-pair commodities preserves the tâtonnement convergence guarantee, especially when type masses are small.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 5.1 and its polynomial-time CES tâtonnement application; it leaves the issue-pricing inefficiency results and other utility classes aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the pairwise goods be aggregated by type while preserving the tâtonnement's approximate-equilibrium guarantee?
  • Can the compressed algorithm achieve a runtime polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\), including when some rational type masses are small?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is the paper’s Theorem 5.1, proved here. It says: if a Fisher-market tâtonnement \(\mathcal T\) reaches a \(\delta\)-equilibrium for \(\mathcal H\)-nested Leontief utilities in \(O(\kappa(m,n,\delta))\) steps, where \(m\) is the number of goods and \(n\) the number of agents, then the induced public-market tâtonnement \(\mathcal R^{\leftarrow}(\mathcal T)\) reaches a \(3\delta\)-PME for the public decision market with \(\mathcal H\) utilities in \(O(\kappa(n^2m,n,\delta))\) steps. The paper applies this to a polynomial-time Fisher-market tâtonnement for nested CES-Leontief utilities, cited from earlier work, and thereby obtains a polynomial-time tâtonnement for public markets with CES utilities. [Theorem 5.1 and its application](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10139081)

The mirror I would put to a prover is Mass-compressed CES public-market equilibrium. Imagine a citywide ballot on \(m\) public issues, such as transit, schools, and capital projects, with many residents but a modest number of recurring, fully specified preference-and-importance profiles. A type \(t\) consists of its preferred side \(a_{tj}\in\{0,1\}\) on every issue, its CES utility weights \(w_{tj}\), its budget \(b_t\), and the fixed CES parameter \(\rho\). The society is a distribution \(\mu\) over the \(\tau\) supported types, with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to one. A type is complete: residents assigned to it share the same ballot, weights, and budget. The regime of interest has \(n\) residents much larger than \(\tau\).

Given this input and a tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\), ask for a randomized common decision \(z\), per-type bundles \(y_t\), and pairwise issue prices \(P_{tu,j}\) for each disagreeing pair of types. Type \(t\)’s effective price for issue \(j\) is
\[ q_{tj}=\sum_{u:\,a_{uj}\ne a_{tj}}\mu_u P_{tu,j}. \]
Require each \(y_t\) to maximize its CES utility subject to \(\sum_j q_{tj}y_{tj}\le b_t\). For every disagreeing type pair, require \(y_{tj}+y_{uj}\le 1+\varepsilon\), with \(y_{tj}+y_{uj}\ge 1-\varepsilon\) whenever \(P_{tu,j}>\varepsilon\). Also require a common outcome with \(z^{j,0}+z^{j,1}\le1+\varepsilon\), \(y_{tj}\le z^{j,a_{tj}}+\varepsilon\), and \(y_{tj}\ge z^{j,a_{tj}}-3\varepsilon\) whenever \(q_{tj}>\varepsilon\). This is a type-level approximate pairwise-pricing equilibrium: each type chooses a best response, while all types’ choices remain compatible with one public decision.

The pairwise construction gives a concrete route to this mirror. For each issue and disagreeing type pair \(\{t,u\}\), aggregate the individual-pair goods into one commodity with supply \(\mu_t\mu_u\). A type-\(t\) resident’s per-capita purchase on that commodity scales with \(\mu_u\); aggregate demand from both types then corresponds to \(\mu_t\mu_u(y_{tj}+y_{uj})\). Thus the mass factors preserve the paper’s pairwise clearing interpretation, and the induced outcome’s exact welfare target is the mass-weighted Nash welfare
\[ W_\mu(z)=\exp\!\left( \frac{\sum_t\mu_t b_t\log u_t(z)} {\sum_t\mu_t b_t} \right). \]
The fractional issue outcome is inherited from the paper; the proposed continuization is of the society through \(\mu\).

I expect this mirror to be tractable for fixed CES parameter \(\rho\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,1)\): the quotient has at most \(O(\tau^2m)\) type-pair goods, suggesting an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau\), the binary input length \(L\), and \(1/\varepsilon\). The question is whether the mass-weighted quotient retains the paper’s price dynamics and approximate-equilibrium guarantee. That is a computational question about the population representation, rather than the analytic existence of a continuum equilibrium.

The paper’s numbered welfare-gap results are not hardness results, so I would not present them as computational anchors. Theorem 5.1 is the strongest qualifying anchor; it gives an algorithmic result, not a hardness result. The mirror covers its CES tâtonnement application, not the paper’s separate inefficiency examples or every utility class.

The weak point is substantial: Theorem 5.1’s reduction creates goods for individual pairs, and its running time depends on \(n^2m\). The paper does not prove that these goods can be collapsed to type-pair commodities with mass-scaled supplies while preserving convergence rates. Tiny type masses and the resulting price scaling may also affect the approximation guarantees. Still, this is a plausible mirror: exact type symmetry is what makes the quotient natural, and pairwise issue expansion already exposes the structure needed to attempt it.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only computational anchor is Theorem 5.1: a polynomial-round tâtonnement for approximate public-market equilibrium. The strongest case against mirroring it is that it is a lifting theorem. Its public-decision-specific construction expands the instance into \(O(n^2m)\) pairwise goods and imports an existing Fisher-market process. For repeated types, those goods can be aggregated: a type pair \(\{t,u\}\) has mass proportional to \(\mu_t\mu_u\), and type \(t\)’s per-capita demand scales with \(\mu_u\). The resulting \(O(\tau^2m)\)-size weighted market captures the same pairwise clearing structure. The natural type model therefore does not expose an identity-dependent computational problem.

That compression is also why the best version of the mirror is hard to dismiss. Define types completely, including preferences, utilities, and budgets; weight welfare by type mass; and normalize approximate clearing to the mass of each type pair. Then the question—whether the tâtonnement has a convergence bound polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\)—is well-posed. The paper’s convex program for maximum Nash welfare already suggests a finite-dimensional weighted formulation, but it does not establish that this particular decentralized price process converges with the desired mass-sensitive bound. Tiny rational masses and their effect on approximation guarantees are a real gap in the supplied theorem, not a defect that can be fixed by rejecting the population model.

So the negative case is weak at its core. One could argue that the remaining contribution is a specialized convergence analysis for a compressed weighted market, rather than a broad new complexity landscape. But that is a judgment about the contribution’s scale, not a fundamental obstruction. Theorem 5.1 is a named algorithmic result, and a society with many residents sharing a modest number of complete profiles is credible. I cannot honestly defeat this anchor or support the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
