The case FOR (proponent)

So I cannot honestly give a continuous problem per anchor.

A plausible *new* problem is a type-based population version of the paper’s binary quantized-message model. Imagine a large, highly mixed neural population with a few cell classes. A type records everything relevant to an agent’s update, including its sensory-noise scale and communication rule; \(\mu_a\) is the fraction of the population in type \(a\). The high-multiplicity regime is many neurons but few types. This is credible for the paper’s homogeneous cortical model, though it is a much weaker story for its small ant simulation.

For a concrete mirror, scale each contact’s social weight as \(\alpha/N\), so total social influence remains finite as population size \(N\) grows. Let \(p_{a,s}\) be the fraction of type \(a\) sending a \(+1\) message at phase \(s\) of a periodic environment \(G_s\in\{-1,+1\}\), and let \(q_s=\sum_a\mu_a(2p_{a,s}-1)\) be the population’s mean message. With Gaussian observation noise of scale \(\sigma_a\), the fully mixed limit gives
\[ p_{a,s+1}=\Phi\!\left(\frac{G_s+\alpha q_s}{\sigma_a}\right), \]
where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal CDF. This retains the paper’s noisy observations, social feedback, binary messages, and changing environment; the continuous object is the mass of each type taking each action.

One precise computational question would be Robust Population Weight Design: given rational type masses, noise scales, a periodic environment, a permitted interval for \(\alpha\), and an accuracy tolerance, choose a common social weight that maximizes long-run average population accuracy in the worst attracting periodic orbit of this map. The output is a weight within the requested tolerance and the corresponding type-by-phase action fractions. A promise that the relevant long-run attractors are periodic would make the problem’s scope precise while retaining the hysteresis that makes this paper interesting. I would expect a tractable route when the number of types is fixed and attractors are separated from bifurcation boundaries; when the number of types grows, the search over stable orbits may become continuum-specifically hard. That is a conjecture, not a result of this paper.

This candidate covers the binary quantized-message model’s accuracy collapse and lock-in, and follows the paper’s existing mean-field analysis. The authors would likely recognize the population model as close to their setup: they already reduce agent-level dynamics to a mean-field map and study accuracy as social weight changes. The proposed computational question adds heterogeneous type masses and an optimization objective. It does not mirror a named complexity result, and it does not cover the limited-attention, ant, ring-attractor, or payoff-feedback results.

The weakest point is that this is partly an algorithmic reframing of a dynamical-systems paper whose authors already derive mean-field equations. A referee could reasonably see the proposed task as a new computational question *about* their model, rather than the continuous analogue of a result they proved.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive point is that the paper has no named computational result to continuize. Its findings concern accuracy curves, bifurcations, hysteresis, and mean-field dynamics. It states no complexity theorem or algorithmic result for a computational problem. The positive analyst acknowledges this, so none of their claims is an anchor of the kind requested.

The proposed neuron-type model is the strongest repair, and it is credible as a population model. In a fully mixed network with finitely many cell classes, type masses and a scalar mean message can describe the large-population dynamics. I would not reject it on the ground that types are artificial. But Robust Population Weight Design adds a new optimization objective: the paper studies accuracy as social weight varies and discusses tuning, while the proposal asks an algorithm to choose a weight against the worst attracting orbit. That is a follow-up parameter-design problem on a mean-field map, not a continuous version of a computational result in the paper. In the periodic, fully mixed setting, the type fractions feed into a scalar aggregate map; the hard questions are about roots, attractors, and bifurcations. This belongs to nonlinear dynamics and control, the analytic territory the programme distinguishes from its computational-complexity gap.

Keeping the network detail gives no cleaner mirror. Agents with the same sensory parameters can have different incoming neighbors and therefore different messages and histories. Preserving those distinctions in a general network requires types that encode network position or neighborhood, undermining the moderate-support population description. The fully mixed case avoids that problem, but it is precisely the special case that reduces to the aggregate map above. The ant, animal, and neural examples offer more such dynamical models; none supplies a discrete computational task whose complexity the paper establishes.

So the negative case is strong for the paper as a mirror candidate: it supplies a biological and dynamical phenomenon, not a computational result to mirror. It is weaker as the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous computational question could ever be built around the paper. Robust weight selection could be a legitimate new project, and the authors might recognize it as relevant. The honest objection is that it is an added control problem, outside the paper’s demonstrated computational contribution and the programme’s intended mirror.
