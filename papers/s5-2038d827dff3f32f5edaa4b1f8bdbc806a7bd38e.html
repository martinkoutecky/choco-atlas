The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), additive valuation vectors \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}^m\), relative entitlements \(\omega_t>0\), and per-capita supplies \(\rho\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}^m\), compute a finite-support mass assignment \(z_{t,b}\ge0\) over integral bundle vectors \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\) satisfying \(\sum_b z_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}z_{t,b}b_e=\rho_e\) for every item kind \(e\), such that whenever \(z_{t,b}z_{u,d}>0\), either \(v_t(b)/\omega_t\ge v_t(d)/\omega_u\), or there is an \(e\) with \(d_e>0\), \(v_t(e)>0\), and \(v_t(b)/\omega_t\ge (v_t(d)-v_t(e))/\omega_u\), or there is an \(e\) with \(b_e>0\), \(v_t(e)<0\), and \((v_t(b)-v_t(e))/\omega_t\ge v_t(d)/\omega_u\); the target complexity is polynomial in \(m\), \(|T|\), and the encoding length, independently of the population multiplier \(K\).

The model it lives in

A finite-type high-multiplicity mixed-manna allocation model: \(\mu\) is type mass, \(\rho\) is per-capita item supply, \(z_{t,b}\) is mass assigned to integral bundle \(b\), and the constraints are feasibility plus support-wise \(\mathrm{WEF1}\); the objective is to compute any feasible \(z\), with complexity measured independently of \(K\).

The objection that survived

The proposed polynomial algorithm in \(m\), \(|T|\), and encoding length is unproved: after repairing bundles to integer multiplicity vectors, splitting a type across bundles creates support-wise \(\mathrm{WEF1}\) disjunctions and potentially exponentially many configurations.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 3.1's polynomial-time \(\mathrm{WEF1}\) construction. It leaves Theorem 4.1's fPO coexistence theorem without a computational anchor and does not attempt the randomized Theorem 5.1.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does every finite-type instance admit a finite-support mass allocation satisfying support-wise \(\mathrm{WEF1}\)?
  • Can the meta-good and weighted picking-sequence proof be converted into a witness-preserving mass-flow or configuration algorithm polynomial in \(m\), \(|T|\), and encoding length?

The case FOR (proponent)

I think there is a genuine positive mirror here, with Theorem 3.1 as the lead anchor. The paper does not itself study a continuous population, so this would be a new high-multiplicity formulation rather than a collision.

Take a finite set \(T\) of complete agent types. A type \(t\) specifies an additive valuation vector \(v_t\in\mathbb{Q}^m\), an entitlement index \(\omega_t>0\), and any other eligibility information used by the allocation problem. The society is a distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\). Let \(\rho_e\in[0,1]\) be the per-capita supply of item kind \(e\): in a population of size \(K\), there are approximately \(K\rho_e\) interchangeable copies of \(e\). Each individual still receives an integral bundle \(B\subseteq M\); only the population distribution over such bundles is continuous.

Equivalently, an allocation is a family \(z_{t,B}\ge 0\), where \(z_{t,B}\) is the mass of type-\(t\) agents receiving bundle \(B\), satisfying

\[ \sum_{B\subseteq M}z_{t,B}=\mu_t \]

and

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{B\ni e}z_{t,B}=\rho_e \qquad\text{for every }e\in M. \]

For every pair \((t,B)\), \((u,D)\) with positive mass, require the paper’s WEF1 comparison with \(v_t\) and entitlement indices \(\omega_t,\omega_u\): either

\[ \frac{v_t(B)}{\omega_t}\ge \frac{v_t(D)}{\omega_u}, \]

or some positive item \(e\in D\) can be removed from \(D\), or some negative item \(e\in B\) can be removed from \(B\), so that the corresponding inequality holds. Thus the question is about a distribution over integral bundles, not about replacing indivisible bundles by fractional ones.

This is a genuine high-multiplicity regime. For rational data, instantiate \(K\mu_t\) agents of each type, give each such agent weight \(\omega_t/K\), and provide \(K\rho_e\) copies of item \(e\). The finite allocation induces \(z\), and rational \(z\) can be expanded back into such a finite instance. The hidden population size \(K\) grows while \(m\) and \(\tau=|T|\) remain small. A plausible setting is a recurring allocation of standardized equipment, benefits, rooms, and undesirable duties to a large population of households or employees partitioned into a small number of valuation-and-entitlement classes.

My lead anchor is Theorem 3.1, proved in this paper. It states:

Given any mixed-manna instance with \(n\) agents, \(m\) items, additive valuations, and arbitrary positive entitlements, an algorithm computes a WEF1 allocation in time \(O(nm^2\log m)\).

The corresponding continuous problem is:

Continuous WEF1 Mixed-Manna Allocation. Given \((T,\mu,(v_t,\omega_t)_{t\in T},\rho)\), find a feasible mass allocation \(z\) over integral bundles such that every two positive-mass agent classes satisfy WEF1.

This is exactly the paper’s underlying allocation problem with the population replaced by its empirical distribution. It does not ask for EF in expectation, divisible goods, or a fractional bundle for an individual. The meta-good construction and the forward/reversed weighted picking sequences suggest a Class A outcome: a mass version should replace serial picks by weighted mass flows, while preserving the paper’s key property that a meta-level witness can be converted back to one original item. The natural complexity target is polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the encoding length of \(\mu,\rho,v,\omega\), rather than in the hidden population size \(K\).

The second anchor is Theorem 4.1, also proved here. It states:

Given any mixed-manna instance, there exists an allocation satisfying both WEF1T and fractional Pareto optimality.

Its continuous mirror is:

Continuous WEF1T–fPO Allocation. Given the same continuous-population instance, find an integral-bundle assignment whose positive-mass bundle classes satisfy WEF1T, and for which no measurable fractional assignment \(y_e(a)\in[0,1]\), with total item mass \(\rho_e\), gives almost every individual at least her current utility and a positive-measure set of individuals strictly higher utility.

For a pair \((t,B)\), \((u,D)\), WEF1T means that either ordinary WEF holds, or a positive item \(e\in D\) may be transferred to \(B\), or a negative item \(e\in B\) may be transferred to \(D\), with the paper’s normalized inequality holding afterward. The output remains integral at the individual level; fPO is only the comparison class, exactly as in the paper.

I would also expect this problem to be tractable in the explicit finite-type regime, though with less confidence than the first mirror. The paper’s KKM argument and welfare-price construction already reduce the existence proof to finite-dimensional weights and item prices. With finitely many types, the continuum version should invite a configuration LP or convex-programming formulation over type masses, with one-transfer certificates checked at the bundle level. The further boundary question is whether the paper’s impossibility Lemma 8 for WEF\((x,y)\) plus fPO survives the high-multiplicity limit, or whether population continuization makes some of those finite obstructions disappear.

I would not use Theorem 5.1 as a third anchor. Its ex-ante lottery result is mirrorable, but it adds a second source of continuity—randomization—and would make the positive case less clean. The two anchors above already cover the paper’s main deterministic results: one algorithmic theorem and one fairness–efficiency theorem.

The authors should recognize these as their problems because the finite-bundle semantics, additive mixed valuations, weighted envy comparisons, and fPO benchmark are unchanged. The only substantive modification is that agent multiplicity and copy supplies are represented by mass. That modification is not cosmetic: \(z_{t,B}\) records how many same-type agents receive each integral bundle, rather than merely averaging utilities.

The weakest point is computational, not conceptual. A naive formulation has up to \(2^m\) bundle configurations, and Theorem 3.1’s \(O(nm^2\log m)\) algorithm does not automatically become a polynomial algorithm in \((m,\tau)\) after \(n\) is replaced by a continuum. The positive case therefore rests on the claim that the paper’s meta-good structure and picking-sequence proof admit a genuine mass-flow implementation. That is an open algorithmic obligation, but it is a natural one. If the per-capita-copy regime is accepted—and it is precisely the co-modification forced by making the population continuous—then these are credible continuous mirrors, with Continuous WEF1 Mixed-Manna Allocation as the strongest case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is strongest against the second anchor, but it does not honestly defeat the first.

Theorem 4.1 is not a computational result. It is an existence theorem proved through KKM and a limiting argument; it gives no algorithm, complexity bound, approximation guarantee, or parameterized statement. Its proposed mirror therefore falls outside ChoCo’s central computational gap. Moreover, the proof’s private item for every named agent cannot be carried over with finitely many agent types: replacing those items by type-private items changes the WEF1T and fPO problem, while retaining individual-private items creates a continuum of item types. A new theorem might still be possible, but it would not be a continuization of the paper’s computational contribution.

Theorem 3.1 is harder to defeat. With the paper’s original finite manna, a continuum of agents is degenerate: finitely many indivisible items can reach only finitely many agents, so almost all population mass receives the empty bundle. A nontrivial mirror therefore needs per-capita supplies of item kinds. That is an admissible co-modification, but it changes the problem substantially. The correct bundles are then integer multiplicity vectors, not the subsets \(B\subseteq M\) used in the proposed formulation; otherwise the model forbids an individual from receiving two copies of the same item kind.

Even after that repair, WEF1 does not become a mass constraint. It is a support-wise disjunction: every positive-mass bundle class must compare fairly with every other positive-mass bundle class, with a possibly different witnessing item for each pair. Splitting one type across several bundles creates all of these additional comparisons. The serial proof of Theorem 3.1 cannot simply be replaced by a mass flow: its argument depends on an ordering of individual picks, and averaging allocations can preserve expected utilities while destroying every realized one-item witness. A configuration formulation would have exponentially many bundle states and pairwise witness constraints, with no pricing oracle supplied by the paper.

The stronger modelling choices do not rescue the claimed mirror cleanly. Restricting each type to one bundle removes the troublesome support structure but also forbids ordinary high-multiplicity allocations in which identical agents receive different bundles. Encoding bundle assignment into the type space makes the number of types exponential in \(m\). Replacing pairwise WEF1 by an expected or aggregate condition produces a different fairness notion.

Thus the proposed formulation does not establish a useful continuous algorithmic mirror; it establishes a plausible new high-multiplicity fair-division problem whose difficulty may come entirely from item configurations and indivisible witnesses rather than from population continuity. That is a legitimate research problem, but under the programme’s own rules it is not enough to claim a negative answer merely because the continuous version may be hard.

So the honest verdict is not universal rejection. Theorem 4.1 should not count as a computational anchor, while Theorem 3.1 remains a credible, technically open continuous-population mirror. The negative case is therefore materially weaker than the positive case.
