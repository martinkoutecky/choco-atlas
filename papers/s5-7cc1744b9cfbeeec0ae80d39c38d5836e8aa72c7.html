The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The case against starts at the anchor requirement: this paper has no numbered theorem, lemma, or corollary establishing a computational result. Algorithm 1 gives a sampling heuristic, and the paper reports empirical runtimes and accuracy. Its “low computational complexity” claim is not a complexity bound. There is therefore no named complexity result here for a continuous mirror to explain or classify.

The strongest route to a mirror is a distribution over annotator types: for example, a crowd made up of types with different error rates. That is a sensible high-multiplicity scenario, and the paper’s discussion of unreliable annotators makes it more than an arbitrary story. But it would change the model. The method represents observations in an aggregate comparison matrix \(M\) and uses a Bradley–Terry model; it does not track annotator types or their shares. A type distribution would alter the observation probabilities and the posterior used to compute EIG. Asking how to select comparisons under that mixture could be a worthwhile active-learning problem, but it is a new problem, not a continuous version of a computational result established here.

Nor does that cohort model put the paper’s central computation in the continuous population. The algorithm selects pairs among \(n\) named objects, then uses a minimum spanning tree to choose a batch. Type masses might change the EIG weights on the edges, but they do not change which objects are queried or the \(n-1\) edge structure. Continuizing the objects or their scores would put continuity in the candidate or outcome space, which is outside ChoCo’s scope.

So the proponent has supplied no surviving anchor, and the best upgraded proposal is better described as a follow-on model of crowd-aware experimental design. The negative case is strong for this paper as presented. It is weaker as a universal claim: a type-aware mirror could be worth studying if the authors regard the changed observation model as their problem. The text offers some motivation for that extension, but no computational result to anchor it.
