The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent names no surviving computational anchor. Proposition 3.1 establishes nonexistence, Proposition 3.3 gives the fixed threshold \(\delta^*\approx0.0019\) and identifies the unique allocation at that threshold, and Result 2.4 characterizes efficient partitions geometrically. None classifies an algorithmic problem by complexity. The proposed question about computing subsidies for finitely many rationally weighted locations may be worthwhile, but it adds a variable input distribution that this paper does not study; it is not a mirror of a computational result here.

There is a further obstacle to a faithful mirror: the paper already makes the population continuous. It identifies citizens with locations and uses Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb{R}^2\). For that exact uniform-plane model, the hexagon geometry and subsidy threshold are characterized analytically; varying \(g\) changes the scale, not the threshold. Asking for computational complexity on this fixed family offers little input to vary, while asking about arbitrary measurable allocations first requires an encoding the paper does not provide. A finite-support location model would supply such an encoding, but it replaces the paper’s uniform spatial economy with a different problem.

The negative case is strongest against these proposed anchors, not against every adjacent research direction. Repeated residents at a finite set of locations could be a sensible high-multiplicity regime, and studying the complexity of stability there might be valuable. The limitation is that this would be a new computational extension of a paper whose population is already continuous—not a continuous mirror of one of its computational results.
