The case FOR (proponent)

There is no qualifying anchor in this paper: it contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a complexity result. The closest is the unnumbered “Main Result” in §5.1, proved here: a rounded seller distribution on the \(H=20{,}000\) grid paired with a discrete equal-revenue buyer distribution gives \(\mathrm{GFT}^{\mathrm{FB}}\approx1.2322\), \(\mathrm{GFT}^{\mathrm{RO}}\approx0.5939\), and a ratio of \(2.0749\). That is a numerical witness establishing a lower bound on the worst-case ratio, not a complexity classification. So I can’t honestly present it as one of the requested named anchors.

The strongest plausible population reading is a large platform running many independent, standardized one-unit trades. Let \(N\) be the number of trades, with \(N\) much larger than the numbers of distinct seller-cost and buyer-value types. The types are values in \(V=\{0,\ldots,H\}\); \(\mu_s\) and \(\mu_b\) are the fractions of sellers and buyers of each type. Assume independent pairing and run the paper’s RO mechanism in each trade, using its price choices and tie-breaking. Then the aggregate expected first-best and RO gains are the paper’s distributional quantities:
\[ \mathrm{GFT}^{\mathrm{FB}}=\sum_{s,b}\mu_s\mu_b(b-s)^+, \qquad \mathrm{GFT}^{\mathrm{RO}}=\tfrac12\mathrm{GFT}^{\mathrm{SO}}+\tfrac12\mathrm{GFT}^{\mathrm{BO}}. \]
A precise follow-up problem would be: given \(H,\tau,L\), and a rational threshold \(q\), decide whether there are rational mass vectors \(\mu_s,\mu_b\), each with support at most \(\tau\) and encoding length at most \(L\), for which \(\mathrm{GFT}^{\mathrm{RO}}>0\) and \(\mathrm{GFT}^{\mathrm{FB}}/\mathrm{GFT}^{\mathrm{RO}}\ge q\). A yes-instance should provide the two mass vectors and the corresponding optimal offer prices. The paper’s reported distribution supplies a witness for this kind of question at its stated threshold and grid size.

The weakness is decisive: the paper already optimizes over seller and buyer distributions in Eq. (6). Reading those distributions as population shares in repeated independent markets leaves the mathematics unchanged; the original paper still has one seller and one buyer, not a high-multiplicity population. The proposed threshold problem is a possible complexity question to study, but it is not a named result of this paper, and the paper gives no basis for predicting whether it is tractable, transfers discrete hardness, or is continuum-specifically hard. Thus the mirror covers only the lower-bound phenomenon in §5.1, and it is a plausible population interpretation rather than a strong, anchored continuization case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive problem is that the paper has no named computational result to mirror. Its §5.1 “Main Result” is a numerical witness: one pair of distributions on a finite value grid gives an approximation ratio of at least \(2.0749\). The paper does not classify the complexity of an algorithmic problem on an input society. AlphaEvolve’s search is experimental, with no complexity or algorithmic guarantee.

The strongest salvage is the repeated-market scenario: many independent bilateral trades, with seller and buyer value types occurring at specified shares. That is a plausible high-multiplicity setting. But for independent trades, the aggregate gains are just the per-trade expectations in the paper multiplied by the number of trades; the ratio is unchanged. The proposed threshold question over mass vectors therefore re-expresses the paper’s own supremum over distribution pairs in Eq. (6). Its reported distribution is a witness for that search. Restricting the search to rational masses of bounded support and encoding length makes a new decision problem, but the paper neither states nor analyzes that problem as a computational result.

To make population size or composition matter beyond those shares, one would need interactions such as shared capacity or market clearing. Then the mechanism and its performance measure would change, and the paper’s RO lower bound would no longer be the anchor.

This is a strong case against calling the proposed threshold task a continuous mirror of a result in this paper. It does not prove that no useful population model could be devised; the repeated-market story is credible. The honest weakness is that its credibility also makes a worthwhile *new* problem possible, even though the paper itself supplies no qualifying computational result to continuize.
