The continuous mirror question

Given balanced rational supplies \(a_i,b_j\) over finite employer and worker types, rational productivities \(\alpha_{ij}\), and dummy types for reservation options, find a \(P\)-optimal stable mass matching: choose \(x_{ij}\ge 0\) and type payoffs \(u_i,v_j\ge 0\) such that \(\sum_j x_{ij}=a_i\), \(\sum_i x_{ij}=b_j\), \(u_i+v_j\ge\alpha_{ij}\), and \(x_{ij}>0\Rightarrow u_i+v_j=\alpha_{ij}\). Under the promise that the maximum-productivity type-flow matrix is unique, return a compatible flow and payoffs with employer payoffs componentwise maximal and worker payoffs componentwise minimal among stable solutions.

The model it lives in

An atomless hiring market with employer and worker populations distributed over complete productivity types. The decision variables are matching masses \(x_{ij}\) and type payoffs \(u_i,v_j\); the assignment LP maximizes \(\sum_{ij}\alpha_{ij}x_{ij}\), then selects the \(P\)-optimal compatible dual payoffs.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers stable-outcome existence and \(P\)-optimality for the all-flexible assignment-game subcase. It leaves the mixed rigid–flexible RiFle algorithm and the paper's general lattice results without a continuous mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does aggregation and lifting between rational type supplies and finite markets preserve stable outcomes and the \(P\)-optimality order exactly?
  • Can the paper's non-degeneracy condition be characterized directly on type-flow instances, including uniqueness modulo relabelings of identical copies?

The case FOR (proponent)

I’d defend a partial but clean mirror: the all-flexible assignment-game subcase of RiFle, in a market with many agents but few complete productivity types. It is a real high-multiplicity question because the population is represented by type masses and the decision variable is how much mass of each type gets matched to each opposing type. The fractional flow represents a continuum of whole partnerships; no individual receives a fractional match.

Take a hiring market with \(r\) employer types and \(s\) worker types. An employer’s type includes its productivity against every worker type, and a worker’s type includes its productivity against every employer type. Thus agents in a type are indistinguishable in every respect the model uses. Let \(a_i\) and \(b_j\) be the fractions of the two populations in each type, with \(\sum_i a_i=\sum_j b_j=1\), and let \(\alpha_{ij}\ge 0\) be the productivity of an \(i\)-\(j\) match. A plausible regime is a very large market with, say, a few dozen standardized job and qualification profiles. Reservation options can be represented with dummy types, as the paper proposes.

Theorem 5.1, proved in the paper, says that the set of stable outcomes in the RiFle assignment game is nonempty. Section 5 gives an algorithm to find one; the theorem does not give a polynomial-time bound. Its continuous mirror, which I would name High-Multiplicity Assignment Stability, is:

Given rational type masses \(a_i,b_j\) and rational productivities \(\alpha_{ij}\), find a matching mass \(x_{ij}\) and per-type payoffs \(u_i,v_j\) such that every type’s mass is matched, no type pair can block, and each used match is compatible with its payoffs.

Precisely, a solution consists of \(x_{ij}\ge 0\) and \(u_i,v_j\ge 0\) satisfying
\( \sum_j x_{ij}=a_i,\quad \sum_i x_{ij}=b_j,\quad u_i+v_j\ge\alpha_{ij}, \)
with \(x_{ij}>0\) only when \(u_i+v_j=\alpha_{ij}\). The natural objective is to maximize total productivity, \(\sum_{ij}\alpha_{ij}x_{ij}\). This is the assignment LP and its dual: an optimal primal flow and compatible optimal dual payoffs give exactly a stable mass matching. It is solvable in time polynomial in the number of supported types and the input bit length. This is a Class A mirror, though that tractability comes from the assignment subcase’s LP structure; Theorem 5.1 itself does not assert a complexity class.

A second worthwhile anchor is Theorem 6.10, also proved in the paper, though its proof is presented as a sketch: when the RiFle game is non-degenerate, the paper’s algorithm returns an outcome that is optimal among stable outcomes for the \(P\)-agents. In the assignment-game special case, the paper identifies non-degeneracy with uniqueness of the optimal assignment. The corresponding mass problem, High-Multiplicity \(P\)-Optimal Assignment, asks for a \(P\)-optimal stable mass matching under the analogous promise that the optimal type-flow matrix is unique. Here \(P\)-optimal means that among stable outcomes \(u_i\ge \tilde u_i\) and \(v_j\le \tilde v_j\) for every employer and worker type. In the assignment subcase this can also be found by LP: optimize a positive weighted \(P\)-preference objective over the optimal dual solutions, then pair it with a compatible optimal flow. This, too, is expected to be tractable.

The type-flow model has a useful two-way connection to finite markets. A finite market of identical copies of these types gives rational masses \(a_i,b_j\); its stable matching aggregates to a stable type flow. Conversely, a rational type flow can be expanded into a matching of sufficiently many identical copies. In this assignment subcase, the mirror therefore describes a genuine high-multiplicity regime, rather than merely replacing a discrete outcome with a fractional one.

The paper explicitly includes the assignment game as a special case, so authors should recognize these questions as their problem. The cited Roth–Sotomayor article’s “continuous models” terminology refers to models such as the assignment game and to continuous preferences and price adjustment; it does not, on its abstract’s account, make the population itself a distribution over types. The paper here still begins with finite sets \(P\) and \(Q\). [Roth and Sotomayor’s abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sbe/breart/v16y1996i2a2873.html) supports that distinction. This is not a claim of novelty for the assignment LP; prior high-multiplicity work on it would support the regime’s plausibility.

The soft spot is scope: these mirrors capture an explicit submodel, but they do not continuize the paper’s distinctive mixed rigid–flexible game. In that game, stability for rigid pairs uses a disjunction, and the simple assignment LP may not survive. So the honest claim is that the paper has a strong, tractable continuous mirror for its assignment-game results, not that its entire RiFle theory has already been lifted.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that neither anchor transfers the paper’s distinctive mixed rigid–flexible result. Theorem 5.1 gives an algorithm for that finite RiFle game, but no running-time bound. The proposed mass-flow formulation removes rigidity and turns stability into the ordinary assignment LP’s primal–dual conditions. It gives a clean model for the all-flexible special case, but does not continuize the structure that makes the RiFle algorithm different.

Theorem 6.10 is a weaker fit. Its non-degeneracy condition concerns labeled matchings; with repeated agents, permuting identical copies creates multiple optimal matchings even when the type-level flow is unique. Replacing the paper’s condition with uniqueness modulo those permutations, and replacing individual \(P\)-optimality with type-level payoff order, is a sensible repair—but it is a modified question, not a direct transfer of the theorem.

Those are real limits on how much of this paper the anchors cover. They do not defeat the repaired mirrors, though. The paper explicitly includes the assignment game, and a market with many employers and workers sharing a small set of complete productivity types is plausible. Its mass-flow stability question is well-posed and computational. The \(P\)-optimal version can likewise be stated modulo within-type relabeling. The fact that the resulting assignment problem has a standard LP solution is not, under the programme’s rules, a reason to reject the question. Nor does Roth and Sotomayor’s “continuous model” supply a collision: there, continuity concerns prices and preferences while the market remains a set of agents ([Roth and Sotomayor, 1996](https://web.stanford.edu/~alroth/papers/Roth%20and%20Sotomayor%201996.pdf)).

So the honest negative case is weak. It can argue that these are narrow mirrors of the assignment subcase, not of the paper’s central mixed model, but I cannot defend the stronger claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
