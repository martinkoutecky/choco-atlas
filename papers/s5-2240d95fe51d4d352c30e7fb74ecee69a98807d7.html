The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The positive case fails at the anchor gate. Proposition 1 compares incentive axioms; Theorem 1, Corollary 1, and Theorem 2 characterize the TTC rule; the examples show where related characterizations fail. None gives a complexity result or studies an algorithmic problem. The TTC procedure itself is background machinery, not a computational contribution of this paper. So there is no named result here whose computational question a continuous mirror could carry forward.

The better housing-market version would give mass to preference and house categories, with supply scaling along with population, then ask for a mass-valued TTC allocation or a computational characterization of the core. That may be a worthwhile project in its own right, but it is not a mirror of these theorems: their claims concern which *individual-allocation rules* satisfy individual rationality, efficiency, and truncation-invariance. In an atomless population, one agent’s report has zero mass, so unilateral truncation leaves the aggregate unchanged and the central incentive condition risks becoming vacuous. Making a positive-mass group’s report matter instead studies collective manipulation, a different strategic question.

There is a related obstacle in the paper’s exact model. Each agent owns a distinct house and ranks the named houses, so a complete type includes a unique endowment; the support has one type per agent. Repeated types require changing the house domain to interchangeable categories or units. That co-modification could be sensible, but it takes the proposed question further from the paper’s individual-level characterizations.

So the strongest negative case is that this paper offers no computational result to mirror, and its central incentive condition does not pass unchanged to a nonatomic society. I cannot honestly rule out a valuable continuous housing-market project under a redesigned model; I can say that such a project would be new work on housing exchange, rather than a continuous computational mirror of this paper.
