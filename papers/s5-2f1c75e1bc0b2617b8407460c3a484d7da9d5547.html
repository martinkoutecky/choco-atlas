The case against is strongest at the level of the paper’s results: it has no named computational result to mirror. None classifies the complexity of an algorithmic problem. That leaves no paper result whose high-multiplicity version could yield the tractability or hardness comparison ChoCo is set up to chart.

Theorem 3 is the best candidate for a rescue, but it already studies a unit-mass continuum economy and proves uniqueness there. Asking how to compute that equilibrium would be a potentially useful new question, but it would not be a population-continuous mirror of a discrete result in this paper. The theorem’s input is an arbitrary atomless distribution \(\eta\), with no finite encoding or precision convention. Restricting \(\eta\) to an explicitly represented family of densities could make a computational problem, but its complexity would depend on that new representation. Replacing \(\eta\) with a finite list of rational masses would change the model’s atomless assumption. Those choices might support a separate project; they are not an algorithmic question already posed by the paper’s theorem.

The finite framework also gives little reason to expect the usual high-multiplicity compression. A complete type includes the student’s college-specific utilities, eligibility information, and payoffs. Under the paper’s atomless type distribution, a finite sample has distinct complete types with probability one, so its support size is \(n\). In the Danish setting, many applicants and programs make a large market plausible, but the individual values and disclosure decisions that drive the mechanisms are precisely the features that distinguish applicants. Grouping students by coarser characteristics could produce repeated types, but it would discard information central to the model.

I cannot rule out a different, well-motivated admission regime with many applicants who share exactly the same relevant attributes. That would make the universal claim—no worthwhile continuous mirror in any scenario—too strong to establish from this paper alone.
