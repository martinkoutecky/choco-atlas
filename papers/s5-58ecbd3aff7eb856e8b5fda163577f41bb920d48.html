The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make a valid positive case under your anchor rule: the paper has no numbered result classifying a computational problem as NP-hard, polynomial-time solvable, FPT, or similar.

The closest results are analytical. Theorem 1, proved in the paper, gives asymptotic formulas for the expected Shapley values of the highest- and lowest-weight agents when weights are sampled i.i.d. from a continuous distribution satisfying the theorem’s regularity assumptions. The formulas express those values using the corresponding extreme order statistic and the truncated mean of the weight distribution. Corollary 1, also proved there, says that for an interior quota \(Q=q n\mathbb E[X]\), the scaled expected values converge to \(\chi_{\max}/\mathbb E[X]\) and \(\chi_{\min}/\mathbb E[X]\) when the upper support endpoint is finite; for unbounded support, the scaled maximum value diverges. These are meaningful results, but they do not classify the complexity of computing or optimizing a solution. The paper’s mention of exact Shapley computation being intractable points to Matsui and Matsui (2001), an outside result, not a numbered result proved here.

There is also a problem with treating the paper’s random weight distribution as a continuous population. The paper still has \(n\) agents; the distribution describes uncertainty over their weights. If exact weight is the type, continuous i.i.d. sampling gives distinct weights almost surely, so the support size is \(n\), not a small number of repeated types. A limit question based on a continuum of weight mass is conceivable, but it would need a definition of Shapley power for that population and would be an analytical limit question, not a computational mirror anchored by this paper’s results.

So the strongest honest positive-side finding is: there is no qualifying computational anchor here. The paper’s asymptotic characterization may be relevant background for another continuous-population study, but presenting it as a ChoCo computational result would overstate the case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive point is that this paper has no computational result to mirror. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 characterize expected Shapley values under random sampling; neither classifies an algorithmic problem by complexity. The paper’s statement that exact Shapley computation is intractable cites Matsui and Matsui, whose result is outside this paper. So the proponent is right that there is no qualifying computational anchor here.

The strongest population-level version would group agents into weight types and study each type’s share of total Shapley power. That is a sensible high-multiplicity scenario—for example, many shareholders with the same share allotment—so I would not reject it on the ground that repeated types are implausible. But it changes the paper’s setup: its weights are sampled from a continuous distribution, so exact weight types are almost surely all distinct. In a continuum of weights, the highest- and lowest-weight agents also have zero type mass. One can avoid that problem by studying a rescaled power profile across quantiles instead. The paper itself points toward that analytic extension: its Conjecture 3 and Corollary 4 predict the limiting scaled power at an interior quantile from the corresponding weight and the mean.

That stronger version is still a limit characterization, not a computational question about worst-case complexity or algorithms. Turning it into one—for example, by asking how to choose a quota to optimize a type-level power distribution—would define a new problem, not supply a computational mirror of a result proved here. The paper’s analytical treatment may be useful background for such work, but it does not make that work this paper’s ChoCo contribution.

So the negative case is strong as a verdict on this paper as a computational anchor. The universal claim that no worthwhile continuous-population study could grow out of its subject is weaker: repeated weight classes and a type-level power measure are defensible. They would be a separate research direction, not a mirror of a named computational result in this paper.
