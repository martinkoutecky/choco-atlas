The continuous mirror question

Given seeker types \(A\), vacancy types \(B\), rational masses \(\mu\) summing to \(1\), an explicit compatibility relation \(E\subseteq A\times B\), a group color \(c(a,b)\) for each compatible pair, and fairness bounds \(0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq1\), choose matched masses \(x_{ab}\geq0\). Require \(\sum_{b:(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\leq\mu_a\) and \(\sum_{a:(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\leq\mu_b\); for \(z=\sum_{(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\), require \(\alpha z\leq\sum_{(a,b)\in E:c(a,b)=c}x_{ab}\leq\beta z\) for every group \(c\). What is the maximum feasible \(z\)?

The model it lives in

Each seeker and vacancy type represents agents identical in all matching-relevant respects, and \(\mu\) records their population masses. The variables \(x_{ab}\) assign matched mass between compatible types, subject to type capacities and the original proportional group-share bounds; maximize total matched mass \(z\). Each individual still receives an integral match, while the aggregate type flow can be fractional.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 5's maximum-size proportionally fair matching problem in type-homogeneous markets. It leaves Theorems 2–4's approximation and size-\(k\) algorithmic results outside the mirror, and it does not preserve Theorem 5's path reduction.

Open questions for a prover

  • For finite markets obtained by scaling each type mass to integral counts, what additive loss in matching size and group-share error suffices to round an optimal mass flow to a discrete matching?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest case I believe is a type-continuous version of the paper’s maximum proportionally fair matching problem, anchored on its hardness result, Theorem 5.

A plausible regime is a large standardized hiring round: many job seekers and vacancies, but relatively few complete matching types. A job seeker’s type records their protected group and all qualifications relevant to eligibility; a vacancy’s type records its requirements and other matching-relevant details. For example, \(10^5\)–\(10^6\) people might fall into hundreds or thousands of types. Compatibility is determined by the pair of types, and a match is colored by the job seeker’s group. This is close to the paper’s own assignment motivation, while making the high-multiplicity assumption explicit.

Call the continuous problem Type-Continuous Proportionally Fair Matching. Its instance has disjoint sets of seeker types \(A\) and vacancy types \(B\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) normalized over \(A\cup B\), a set \(E\subseteq A\times B\) of compatible type pairs, an edge color \(c(a,b)\in[\ell]\), and fairness bounds \(0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq1\). A solution assigns nonnegative matched mass \(x_{ab}\) to each compatible pair. It must satisfy
\[ \sum_{b:(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\leq\mu_a,\qquad \sum_{a:(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\leq\mu_b. \]
Writing \(z=\sum_{(a,b)\in E}x_{ab}\), proportional fairness requires, for every color \(c\),
\[ \alpha z\leq\sum_{(a,b)\in E:\,c(a,b)=c}x_{ab}\leq\beta z. \]
The task is to maximize \(z\), and a solution is the matching-mass vector \(x\). This is an aggregate description: each individual still receives at most one partner; a type’s mass can be divided among compatible types. It does not make the matched outcome itself fractional.

Lead anchor — Theorem 5. The paper proves this result here, by reduction using Proposition 1, which it cites from elsewhere: under ETH, there is no \(2^{o(\ell)}n^{O(1)}\)-time approximation for Proportionally Fair Matching, even on paths. The reduction encodes a 3SAT-3 instance as a path with clause and connector colors, sets \(\alpha=\beta=1/\ell\), and makes a fair matching of the required size correspond to a satisfying assignment.

There is a notation problem in the theorem as printed: it says “for any \(\gamma>1\),” although the paper defines its approximation guarantee as a retained fraction \(\gamma\leq1\), and the proof discusses \(\gamma<1\). I read the intended claim as ruling out every fixed positive retained-fraction guarantee in \(2^{o(\ell)}n^{O(1)}\) time, under ETH.

For the continuous question, I expect Class A: tractable. The objective and fairness constraints above are linear, as are the type-capacity constraints. With the compatible type pairs listed explicitly, this is a linear program with \(O(|E|)\) variables and \(O(\tau+\ell)\) constraints, solvable in time polynomial in the input size and the rational bit lengths. That gives a concrete reason the discrete hardness might dissolve: the reduction’s choices among individual edges are replaced by divisible flows among type populations. It also suggests a follow-up question: how closely can such an optimal flow be rounded to an integral matching when each type mass is a large finite count, while keeping the group shares within a small additive error?

This mirrors the paper’s maximum-size fair matching objective and its fairness constraints; it covers Theorem 5’s hardness result, not the paper’s approximation algorithms or its size-\(k\) FPT result. The authors’ assignment motivation makes the high-multiplicity market a plausible setting for their problem.

The weak point is that the reduction exploits an individual path graph. In a type-based market, compatibility is shared by all members of a type pair, so the relation between types represents a replicated compatibility pattern rather than the original path’s one-off edges. The resulting LP is therefore not a continuous encoding of that specific hardness construction. The case stands if the authors accept type-homogeneous markets as a meaningful regime for proportionally fair matching; it weakens if they require the original graph’s individual adjacency structure to be preserved.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that Theorem 5’s hardness is built from *individual* conflicts. Each clause occurrence is a particular edge on a particular path; the path’s shared vertices enforce the logical consistency of a truth assignment. In a type-mass model, replacing those vertices with seeker and vacancy types makes a compatibility between two types available across all mass of both types. That is a blow-up of the path, not a continuous representation of its one-off edges. Keep each edge’s identity and the types become singletons; aggregate them and the reduction’s conflicts change. Dense graph limits would also erase this sparse path structure, while a type model that records the full adjacency pattern risks needing a type for nearly every vertex.

That defeats Theorem 5 as a *hardness-preserving* anchor. It does not defeat the proponent’s better model. Their hiring-market formulation is a coherent high-multiplicity version of fair matching: workers of a type share relevant qualifications and group membership, vacancies of a type share requirements, and compatible type pairs carry matched mass. Its capacity and fairness constraints form a linear program. The result would concern a natural market regime, even though that LP would not preserve the paper’s SAT reduction.

Calling the LP too easy, or observing that the continuous answer differs from the paper’s lower bound, would not be a valid objection under the programme’s standards. Theorem 5 is the only anchor the proponent raised, and it does not transfer to the type model. But the model itself survives: the paper motivates matching with customers and products, and nothing in that scenario rules out large cohorts with shared matching-relevant attributes. So I cannot honestly defend the universal claim that no scenario yields a worthwhile continuous mirror. The negative case is strong against treating Theorem 5’s path hardness as the mirror, but weak against the actual type-population question.
