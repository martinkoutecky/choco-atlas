The continuous mirror question

Given an integer \(\rho\ge5\), a finite type set \(T\) with distribution \(\mu\), chore classes \(E\) with \(\rho q_e\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), and additive costs \(c_\theta\), minimize \(\alpha\) over nonnegative flows \(x^0_{\theta,b},x^1_{\theta,b}\) on integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\), satisfying \(\sum_b(x^0_{\theta,b}+x^1_{\theta,b})=\rho\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,b}b_e(x^0_{\theta,b}+x^1_{\theta,b})=\rho q_e\), and \(\sum_{\theta,b}x^1_{\theta,b}\le1\), where the nonexceptional support has \(c_\theta(b)\le\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)\), the exceptional support has \(c_\theta(b)\le\alpha\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)\), and \(\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)=\min_{\sum_{j=1}^{\rho}b^j=\rho q}\max_j c_\theta(b^j)\).

The model it lives in

Worker types \(\theta\in T\) carry masses \(\rho\mu_\theta\); integral chore configurations \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\) receive continuous flow; supplies are \(\rho q_e\); minimize \(\alpha\) with at most one unit of exceptional mass.

The objection that survived

The paper never proves that its unit-cardinality reductions lift to fractional type masses, while \(\gamma_\rho\sim\rho/4\) makes the exceptional guarantee asymptotically weak and configuration pricing may remain knapsack-hard; this challenges tractability and asymptotic usefulness, not the existence of a valid mirror.

What the mirror covers

It covers Theorem 5.1 and its constructive partition, matching, and reduction machinery for \(\rho\ge5\); it leaves the \(9/8\) three-agent and \(4/3\) four-agent results, their finite-agent case analyses, and polynomial-time classification outside the mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can \(\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)\) and the required integral configurations be generated within useful high-multiplicity complexity bounds?
  • Do capacitated Hall conditions and mass-valued valid reductions preserve each type's \(\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)\) benchmark?
  • Does the \(\rho\to\infty\) regime admit a non-vacuous normalization while retaining the AMMS interpretation?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead, and the only anchor I would use, is Theorem 5.1. The paper proves it itself:

Theorem 5.1. For every instance with \(n\geq 5\) agents, there exists a \(\gamma\)-AMMS allocation, where \(\gamma=(n+1)^2/(4n)\).

In full, this means that all chores can be allocated so that \(n-1\) agents receive bundles costing at most their individual MMS values, while the remaining agent receives a bundle costing at most \(\gamma\) times her MMS. The paper gives a constructive procedure: it repeatedly builds a \((\gamma,1,\ldots,1)\)-partition for one agent, uses an MMS-feasibility matching, and either completes the allocation or removes matched agents and bundles through a valid reduction. The theorem is proved here, although the paper does not claim a polynomial-time complexity classification.

The natural mirror is a high-multiplicity workforce assigning repeated indivisible jobs. Imagine a large facilities operation with \(\rho\) units of worker mass, perhaps thousands of workers, but only a small number of worker profiles: training, shift, location, and equipment determine a worker’s costs. Let \(T\) be the finite set of complete worker types, with \(\tau=|T|\ll \rho\), and let \(\mu_\theta\) be the fraction of workers of type \(\theta\). Two workers have the same type precisely when the problem uses them identically, including the same additive cost for every job class.

Let \(E\) be a finite catalogue of chore classes. There are \(\rho q_e\) copies of chore class \(e\), where \(q_e\) is its per-worker supply. Copies remain indivisible: an individual worker receives an integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}^{E}\), with cost \(c_\theta(b)=\sum_{e\in E}b_e c_\theta(e)\). The continuous object is the worker population, not the bundle received by an individual. An allocation is represented by \(x_{\theta,b}\geq 0\), the mass of type-\(\theta\) workers receiving integral bundle \(b\), subject to \(\sum_b x_{\theta,b}=\rho\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,b}b_e x_{\theta,b}=\rho q_e\) for every chore class \(e\). Thus fractional \(x_{\theta,b}\) means a fractional mass of workers, never a fractional bundle for one worker.

For this instance, define the high-multiplicity MMS threshold of type \(\theta\) by \(\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)=\min\{\max_{j\in[\rho]}c_\theta(b^j): b^1,\ldots,b^\rho\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{E},\ \sum_{j=1}^{\rho}b^j=\rho q\}\). This is exactly the MMS benchmark for a representative type when the total chore inventory is \(\rho q\) and there are \(\rho\) population units.

The continuous problem I would hand to a prover is therefore One-Unit-Exception Continuous AMMS:

Given \(\rho\geq 5\), the finite type set \(T\), the distribution \(\mu\), chore supplies \(q\), and cost vectors \(c_\theta\), find the smallest \(\alpha\) for which there is a feasible bundle-flow \(x\) and an exceptional mass of at most one unit such that every nonexceptional type-\(\theta\) worker receives a bundle of cost at most \(\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)\), while every exceptional worker receives a bundle of cost at most \(\alpha\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)\).

The one-unit exception is deliberate. In a normalized continuum, “one agent” would have measure zero and the AMMS condition would collapse. Keeping total population mass \(\rho\) and allowing one unit of exceptional mass is the faithful high-multiplicity analogue of “all but one agent.” It is the same co-modification that makes per-capita chore supplies meaningful.

The expected positive result is that every such instance admits \(\alpha=\gamma_\rho=(\rho+1)^2/(4\rho)\). This is not claimed automatically by Theorem 5.1 when type masses are arbitrary reals; it is the continuous theorem suggested by its proof. The paper’s machinery is unusually well suited to aggregation: the MMS-feasibility graph becomes a capacitated type-to-bundle flow, Hall’s theorem becomes a min-cut condition, and valid reductions remove mass rather than named agents. Partition-Merging and Capped-Bag-Filling depend on additive costs and bundle capacities, not on personal identities. For fixed numbers of chore and agent types, this is a credible Class A candidate based on configuration optimization plus capacitated matching.

The mirror is not merely making the chores divisible. Every worker still receives an integral bundle, and the indivisibility is retained in the configuration vectors \(b\). What becomes continuous is the distribution of workers among those integral bundle configurations. That is exactly the high-multiplicity regime in which the paper’s named agents cease to be informative while type counts and per-capita supplies remain meaningful.

I would not use Theorems 3.2 or 4.1 as additional anchors: their \(n=3\) and \(n=4\) guarantees are valuable finite results, but they do not themselves describe a many-agent regime. Theorem 5.1 is the paper’s only anchor that naturally survives population scaling.

The weakest point is that MMS is intrinsically tied to a finite indivisible inventory. The threshold \(\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)\) and the one-unit exception are therefore a carefully chosen high-multiplicity reformulation, not an identity already established by the paper. Computing the thresholds may itself conceal bin-packing-type difficulty, and the paper proves existence rather than a polynomial-time algorithm. Still, the authors should recognise this as their problem: it preserves additive indivisible chores, MMS partitions, the AMMS objective, and their matching/reduction proof architecture, while replacing an uninformative list of named workers by the type distribution that a large repeated-workforce instance actually contains.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s single anchor does not pass the programme’s computational gate. Theorem 5.1 is an existence theorem:

\[ \text{every } n\text{-agent instance has a } \frac{(n+1)^2}{4n}\text{-AMMS allocation}. \]

It gives no running-time bound, complexity classification, parameterized algorithm, or approximation algorithm. Its procedures invoke an MMS partition as if it were available, without analysing how that partition is found. “Find a partition” is precisely where the computational difficulty may lie. The cited NP-hardness and approximation results belong to other papers and cannot serve as this paper’s anchor. Theorems 3.2 and 4.1 have the same defect. Thus the paper supplies no named computational result whose population-compressed analogue the programme is being asked to study.

Even granting Theorem 5.1 as a generous constructive anchor, the proposed mirror has an uncomfortable dichotomy.

If \(\rho\) is a genuine population scale, then the permitted exceptional mass is \(1/\rho\) of the population. In the continuum limit it disappears. The defining feature of AMMS—sacrificing exactly one agent—therefore vanishes, and the problem approaches an all-agents MMS requirement. Meanwhile

\[ \gamma_\rho=\frac{(\rho+1)^2}{4\rho} =\frac{\rho}{4}+\frac12+\frac{1}{4\rho} \]

diverges. With fixed chore classes and fixed per-capita supplies, the MMS threshold of a type is ordinarily \(O(1)\), so the exceptional worker may receive a bundle whose cost is \(\Theta(\rho)\). The claimed bound then places essentially no meaningful restriction on the exceptional mass. The continuum has swallowed the exception while the approximation factor has become unbounded.

If, instead, \(\rho\) is retained as a finite number of worker units, the model is not really atomless. It is a compressed high-multiplicity finite instance. When \(\rho\mu_\theta\) and \(\rho q_e\) are integers, it can be expanded into \(\rho\) named agents and \(\rho q_e\) indivisible chores, after which Theorem 5.1 already gives the existential guarantee. The only potentially new question is whether one can compute the answer without expanding the instance—a legitimate high-multiplicity algorithmic problem, but one not established or even formulated by the paper.

The proposed extension is also not an automatic translation of the proof. The original argument relies on a unit-cardinality structure: one agent is matched to one whole bundle, Hall deficiency is expressed as \(|S|>|L(S)|\), and reductions remove whole agent–bundle pairs. With type masses, one needs a capacitated Hall theorem, fractional residual populations, and a proof that every reduction preserves the type-specific MMS benchmarks. The partition-merging and capped-bag-filling arguments use an integer number \(k\) of residual agents and item-count inequalities such as \(|P'_k|\le k-1\); those statements do not simply become true for arbitrary real type masses.

Nor is the resulting problem merely a flow problem. The flow assigns masses to integral configurations, but generating those configurations requires solving questions of the form

\[ \max\left\{\sum_e y_e b_e: b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{E},\ \sum_e c_\theta(e)b_e\le \lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)\right\}, \]

which is an integer-knapsack pricing problem. Computing the thresholds \(\lambda_{\theta,\rho}(q)\) is itself an integral partition problem. The proponent has therefore replaced the paper’s unanalysed MMS-partition step by a new configuration-generation problem; the paper gives no structural result making that step tractable.

The obvious repairs change the anchor. Allowing an exceptional fraction \(\varepsilon>0\) produces a scale-free continuum model, but it is no longer “all but one” AMMS. Requiring a constant approximation factor as \(\rho\to\infty\) would be a worthwhile new asymptotic fair-division programme, but it is not Theorem 5.1. Keeping one exceptional unit gives a sensible compressed finite problem, but then the central contribution would be a new high-multiplicity complexity theory absent from the paper.

The workforce interpretation is not absurd, and high multiplicity is not itself an objection. A new paper could study this model. But the proponent has not identified a computational result of this paper that survives continuization: the true continuum loses the one-agent exception, while the finite-scale version is merely a new compressed formulation whose main algorithmic content must be supplied from scratch. Under the programme’s anchor requirement, that is enough for a negative verdict.
