The continuous mirror question

Given a finite item-type catalogue \(O\), a finite set \(T\) of complete utility types, rational population mass \(\mu\) on \(T\), rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\), item magnitudes \(w_j\), and a finite catalogue \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{O}\) of integral bundles satisfying \(u_\theta(j)\in\{-w_j,+w_j\}\) with zero-valued dummies allowed, find \(\pi_{\theta,b}\ge0\) such that \(\sum_b\pi_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,b}b_j\pi_{\theta,b}=s_j\). Require the paper's EFX inequalities for every pair of positive-mass classes \((\theta,b)\) and \((\eta,d)\), evaluated using the first type's utilities, and require Pareto-optimality against all measurable allocations with the same population masses and item supplies; item units remain integral and only type mass is splittable.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity configuration/transportation model with agent mass \(\mu\) over complete utility types, integral bundle configurations \(b\), transport variables \(\pi_{\theta,b}\), exact supply constraints, and support-level EFX plus continuum Pareto constraints; the computational objective is feasibility/search rather than utility maximization.

The objection that survived

Exact EFX is support-sensitive: an arbitrarily small positive-mass bundle class creates the same constraints as a large class, so the formulation may be discontinuous and potentially non-LP; the proponent identifies this risk but does not resolve it.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

It directly covers the constructive guarantees in Theorems 3 and 6, and naturally extends to Theorems 1, 2, 4, and 5; it leaves the impossibility Propositions 1 and 2 and unresolved table entries unanchored.

Open questions for a prover

  • Is exact EFX-PO feasibility solvable in time polynomial in the catalogue size, number of types, and encoding length without expanding the population?
  • Can the support-sensitive EFX constraints be handled by a finite configuration or column-generation method?
  • Does symmetric ternary EFX3 remain constructively tractable for arbitrary rational population masses and per-capita supplies?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s mixed-manna allocation problem, with many anonymous agents, repeated indivisible item types, and a fixed catalogue of utility types. The lead anchor is Theorem 3; Theorem 6 gives a second, stronger-fairness mirror in a narrower domain.

The finite model is not already continuous: it has named agents, individually allocated indivisible items, and pairwise fairness. The proposed mirror continuizes the agent population only. Items remain indivisible for every agent.

Take a finite catalogue of item types \(O=\{1,\ldots,r\}\). A population type \(\theta\) is a complete utility vector over \(O\), including every feature relevant to allocation. Let \(T\) be the finite set of types and \(\mu_\theta\) the fraction of the population of type \(\theta\). Item supplies also scale with the population: \(s_j\) is the number of copies of item type \(j\) per unit population. For a literal one-copy-per-category version, an individual bundle is \(b\in\mathcal B=2^O\); bounded integral multiplicities can be handled by replacing \(\mathcal B\) with a finite set of integral bundles.

The decision variable is \(\pi_{\theta,b}\), the mass of type-\(\theta\) agents receiving integral bundle \(b\). Feasibility requires \(\sum_b\pi_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\) for every \(\theta\), and \(\sum_{\theta,b:j\in b}\pi_{\theta,b}=s_j\) for every item type \(j\). Thus \(\pi\) may split a type across several bundles, but no individual receives a fractional item. The type-average utility is fractional, as it should be: \(\bar u_\theta=\mu_\theta^{-1}\sum_b\pi_{\theta,b}u_\theta(b)\). Fairness, however, is imposed on the realized integral bundles, not merely on these averages.

This is genuinely high multiplicity. For rational \(\mu\), \(s\), and \(\pi\), multiplying by a common denominator produces a finite instance with \(N\mu_\theta\) agents of each type and \(Ns_j\) item copies. Conversely, normalized finite allocations give such mass allocations. The continuous question is therefore not an unrelated divisible-goods problem; it is the high-multiplicity limit of repeated mixed-manna instances.

My lead anchor is Theorem 3, proved in this paper: “With absolute identical utilities, Algorithm 2 returns an EFX and PO allocation in \(O(\max\{m\log m,mn\})\) time.” In full, the utility domain requires that, for every item, all agents assign utilities of the same absolute magnitude, though signs may differ. The theorem says that Algorithm 2 constructs an allocation satisfying both EFX and Pareto-optimality, and does so in the stated polynomial running time.

The corresponding continuous problem is:

Continuous Absolute-Identical Mixed-Manna EFX-PO. The input is a finite type set \(T\), a rational population distribution \(\mu\), item types \(O\) with rational per-capita supplies \(s_j\), and magnitudes \(w_j\), where every type has utility \(u_\theta(j)\in\{-w_j,+w_j\}\) whenever \(w_j>0\), with dummy items having value zero for everyone. Find a feasible mass allocation \(\pi_{\theta,b}\) such that the induced integral allocation is EFX and Pareto-optimal.

“EFX” is applied pointwise to positive-mass realized agent classes. For every pair of classes \((\theta,b)\) and \((\eta,d)\), the EFX inequalities from Definition 2 must hold for the evaluator \(\theta\): either \(\theta\) does not envy \(d\), or removing any negatively valued item from \(b\) eliminates the envy and removing any positively valued item from \(d\) eliminates it. Pareto-optimality means that there is no alternative measurable allocation of the same population and item supplies that makes almost every individual weakly better off and a positive-mass set strictly better off.

This is recognizably the same question. The type is still an additive utility vector, the action is still assigning integral bundles, EFX is still individual envy up to items, and PO is still the usual efficiency condition. Only the number of agents and repeated supply have been continuized.

I would expect this mirror to be Class A, at least when \(r\) and \(\tau=|T|\) are explicit or moderate. Algorithm 2 already sorts items by magnitude and maintains a monotone current-utility invariant: liked items go to currently least-satisfied likers, pure bads to currently best-off agents, and dummies to zero-valued agents. In the continuum, those operations become batched transfers of mass between finitely many type-and-bundle states. The natural formal tools are a configuration or transportation LP, possibly with column generation if the bundle space is large.

That is not an automatic polynomiality claim. The exact EFX requirement is a compatibility condition on the support of \(\pi\), and the proof must show that mass-splitting and tie-handling preserve the invariant. The resulting pricing problem may itself be difficult when the item catalogue is unrestricted. But this is precisely a meaningful continuous computational question, rather than a weakened fractional relaxation.

A second anchor is Theorem 6, also proved in this paper: “With ternary utilities from \(\{-\alpha,0,\alpha\}\), where \(\alpha\in\mathbb R_{>0}\), Algorithm 1 returns an EFX3 and PO allocation.” Here EFX3 requires EFX not only for the complete allocation but also separately for the positive-item and negative-item parts. The theorem therefore gives the stronger by-parts fairness property together with Pareto-optimality in the symmetric ternary domain. Algorithm 1’s polynomial running time is established earlier in Theorem 1.

Its continuous counterpart is:

Continuous Symmetric-Ternary Mixed-Manna EFX3-PO. The input is a finite type distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\subseteq\{-1,0,+1\}^O\), a rational vector of per-capita item supplies \(s\), and a positive rational \(\alpha\). Find a feasible mass allocation of integral bundles such that, for almost every pair of realized agents, EFX holds for the complete bundles, for the bundles restricted to items that are positive for their owners, and for the bundles restricted to items that are negative for their owners; additionally, the allocation must be Pareto-optimal.

The expected classification is again Class A in the fixed-type regime. Algorithm 1’s proof uses only finite sign information, round-robin balancing, and fake zero-valued items to make item counts divisible by the number of agents. In the high-multiplicity version, those counts become rational masses and the fake-item argument becomes exact mass balancing. The interesting question is whether this batched construction remains polynomial in \(r,\tau\), and the encoding length of \(\mu\) and \(s\), rather than in the expanded population size.

This second mirror also gives a useful boundary question. The paper’s Proposition 1 shows that EFX3 can fail for ternary utilities when positive and negative magnitudes differ, while Theorem 6 restores EFX3 when they are equal. A continuous study could ask whether that boundary survives high multiplicity, whether mass makes any additional obstructions disappear, and whether exact EFX3 support compatibility creates a genuinely continuum-specific hardness phenomenon.

I am deliberately not presenting Proposition 1 or Proposition 2 as complexity anchors: they are impossibility results, not NP-hardness or membership results. The paper contains no named hardness theorem. That makes this a weaker case for the programme’s central “does discrete hardness dissolve?” question than a paper with an NP-hardness result would provide. Its value is instead that it supplies constructive polynomial anchors whose high-multiplicity versions may expose new LP, configuration, or pricing structure.

The weakest point is the co-modification of the resource side. If one merely lets the number of agents tend to infinity while keeping the paper’s finite set of items fixed, almost everyone receives the empty bundle and the problem degenerates. A meaningful mirror must therefore scale item supplies with the population, using repeated item types or recurring food/task slots. That is not a literal copy of the paper’s finite instance, although it is exactly the high-multiplicity regime the continuization programme permits.

A second vulnerability is EFX itself: replacing individual bundles by type averages would be too weak and would no longer be the authors’ problem. The proposed mirror avoids that shortcut. Every mass element receives an integral bundle, and EFX is required pairwise for every positive-mass realized class. If the authors accept repeated indivisible supplies and anonymous utility types as their natural large-population setting, these are credible continuous analogues of Theorems 3 and 6.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the proposed mirrors are not genuinely continuous computational-social-choice problems; they are compressed encodings of finite allocation problems whose fairness notion remains entirely individual and combinatorial.

For Theorem 3, the best formulation is indeed the proposed one: repeated item types, per-capita supplies, a finite catalogue of utility types, and variables \(\pi_{\theta,b}\) assigning mass of type \(\theta\) to integral bundles \(b\). This avoids the obvious mistake of giving agents fractional items. It is also a legitimate high-multiplicity regime, so neither “prices are properties of types” nor “the items are still indivisible” defeats it.

The difficulty is that exact EFX is not a continuous property of \(\pi\). Let \(S_\theta=\{b:\pi_{\theta,b}>0\}\). EFX depends only on which bundles occur, not on their masses. A bundle held by mass \(\varepsilon>0\) creates exactly the same constraints as a bundle held by half the population; when its mass reaches zero, all those constraints disappear. Thus the feasible set is support-sensitive and discontinuous. Replacing bundles by type-average utilities would restore a genuinely continuous optimization problem, but it would no longer be EFX as defined in the paper.

Keeping pointwise EFX avoids that alteration, but then \(\pi\) is merely a histogram of finite integral bundles. For rational data, multiplying by a common denominator reconstructs a finite instance with repeated agents and repeated item copies, and every EFX-PO solution corresponds exactly to an allocation of that instance. The continuum has compressed multiplicities; it has not produced a population-level fairness object. A configuration formulation or column-generation method may still be technically interesting, but its central problem is support compatibility among discrete bundles, not a continuous mirror of Theorem 3.

Scaling item supplies is allowed by the programme, so it cannot simply be rejected as an illegitimate co-modification. It does, however, expose the limit of the analogy: with fixed items, the population limit degenerates; with supplies scaling, every individual still receives a discrete bundle and the combinatorial item side scales along with the population. The only new issue is whether a large finite allocation can be represented compactly. That is a high-multiplicity encoding question, not evidence that EFX itself has a meaningful continuous limit.

Theorem 6, and the underlying Theorem 1, fare no better. The round-robin proof and its fake zero-valued items can certainly be batched over masses. But EFX3 is even more support-sensitive: for each evaluator type, one must inspect the positive and negative parts of every bundle appearing with positive mass, and impose EFX separately on those parts. A type may evaluate the same bundle’s item as positive, zero, or negative depending on the evaluator. Consequently, the proposed mass allocation is again just a finite collection of integral bundle classes with pairwise constraints between them. Averaging destroys precisely the “by parts” guarantee that Theorem 6 proves.

The equal-magnitude condition \(\{-\alpha,0,\alpha\}\) does not rescue the continuous interpretation. It makes the finite balancing argument especially simple: positive and negative loads differ by at most the amount needed for EFX. In the mass model this becomes a distribution over integer load states. The theorem’s insight survives batching, but nothing specifically continuous is created. If one instead permits fractional loads or fractional bundles, one obtains a different fairness concept.

The paper also supplies no hardness result whose population dependence could dissolve. Theorem 3 and Theorem 6 are constructive polynomial-time results, not discrete intractability results awaiting a high-multiplicity relaxation. That does not make a continuous question invalid, but it removes the programme’s strongest reason to invest in this paper: there is no apparent computational barrier for continuization to transform.

This negative case is not airtight. The proposed \(\pi_{\theta,b}\) model is a coherent high-multiplicity formulation, and the programme explicitly values such compressed allocation problems. If ChoCo regards an exact high-multiplicity histogram of indivisible bundles as a worthwhile continuous mirror, then Theorems 3 and 6 survive. The most defensible negative conclusion is therefore not that no mirror can be written, but that this paper offers no strong case that one would study a genuinely continuous computational question rather than a compressed finite fair-allocation problem.
