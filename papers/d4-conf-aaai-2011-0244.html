The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no anchor to defeat: the case for names none. Its central point is right under the requested standard. Proposition 1 characterizes which constraint systems are propositionally definable; Theorem 1 gives a recursive identity for enumerating feasible coalitions; and Lemmas 1 and 2 support the finite search procedures. None states a complexity bound or algorithmic guarantee. Algorithms 1 and 2 are contributions, but their speed claims are empirical. So this paper offers no named computational result whose complexity landscape a continuous mirror could chart.

The most promising reconstruction would take many interchangeable agents in a few types, describe each coalition by its type composition, and optimize over fractional quantities of coalition templates. That could be a sensible continuous coalition-formation problem. But it needs values to depend only on type composition and constraints to respect those types. The paper’s model instead permits arbitrary values and constraints on named subsets. Preserve all those distinctions in the types and the support may grow with the population; collapse them and the resulting problem is a new symmetric restriction.

The same issue blocks the closest theorem-level candidates. Theorem 1 branches on whether a particular agent belongs to a coalition. In a continuous population, mass of one type can be split across many coalitions, so the branch becomes a new mass-allocation model. Lemmas 1 and 2 likewise rely on each individual being assigned once and on finite lists of coalitions. A fractional packing LP over coalition templates might be worthwhile, but it would not be a continuous restatement of those results.

This is strong grounds against a *mirror of this paper’s results*, not proof that every continuous coalition-formation model is uninteresting. The negative case is weaker if “mirror” is broadened to include new, type-symmetric welfare models; under the stated anchor requirement, however, that broadening cannot supply the missing result.
