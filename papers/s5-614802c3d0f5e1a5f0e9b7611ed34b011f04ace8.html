The continuous mirror question

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), and a finite list of approval types \(A\subseteq C\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_A\) summing to \(1\), find a committee \(W\subseteq C\) of size \(k\) such that for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and every choice of coalition masses \(x_A\in[0,\mu_A]\), if \(\sum_A x_A\ge \ell/k\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{A:x_A>0}A\right|\ge\ell\), then \(\sum_{A:|A\cap W|\ge\ell}x_A>0\).

The model it lives in

A nonatomic electorate represented by approval types \(A\subseteq C\) and rational masses \(\mu_A\); the decision is an indivisible committee \(W\) of size \(k\), and the requirement is EJR for every cohesive coalition represented by submasses \(x_A\).

What the mirror covers

Covers Corollary 2 and the SEJR construction; leaves other EJR-Exact instantiations and the cited PAV hardness result aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the SEJR construction be formally shown to preserve EJR for arbitrary rational type masses with polynomial bit complexity in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the input bit length \(L\)?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive mirror is the paper’s EJR committee computation. The paper does not give a numbered hardness result of its own: the NP-completeness of PAV is cited as prior work on a different rule. Its cleanest anchor is therefore algorithmic.

Corollary 2, proved here, says that for any approval ballot profile, candidate set, and target committee size \(k\), one can compute a size-\(k\) committee providing EJR in \(O(nmk)\) arithmetic operations. The corollary follows from the paper’s SEJR construction; the authors note that it uses integer arithmetic. This is a result about finding an EJR committee, not about computing PAV.

A natural population regime is a large election on a fixed or moderately sized candidate slate, such as a large membership ballot. Many voters can cast the same approval ballot, so the number \(n\) of voters may be far larger than the number \(\tau\) of distinct ballots. A type is an approval set \(A\subseteq C\); its mass \(\mu_A\) is the fraction of the electorate with that ballot. The instance is \(C\), \(k\), and the list of \(\tau\) supported types with positive rational masses summing to one.

Here is a precise mirror, Continuous Approval-EJR Committee. A solution is a committee \(W\subseteq C\) of size \(k\). There is no objective to optimize: as in Corollary 2, the task is to find any committee satisfying the representation condition. For each \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), consider any coalition described by masses \(x_A\) with \(0\le x_A\le\mu_A\), total mass \(\sum_A x_A\ge\ell/k\), and at least \(\ell\) candidates common to every type with \(x_A>0\). The committee must give positive mass of that coalition at least \(\ell\) approved winners:
\[ \sum_{A:\,|A\cap W|\ge\ell} x_A>0. \]
This is the mass version of EJR’s cohesive-group quota \( \ell n/k \) and its requirement that some member of the group approve at least \(\ell\) winners. A type is complete for this problem because approval sets are the only voter attributes the axiom uses.

The paper’s SEJR method supplies a credible route to an algorithm. It maintains integer dissatisfaction levels for candidates, initialized using \(\lfloor k\sum_{A\ni c}\mu_A\rfloor\), repeatedly selects an unelected candidate of maximum current level, and updates levels using the masses of types with specified numbers of already selected approvals. The finite proof’s threshold arguments carry over with voter counts divided by \(n\) replaced by type masses. Summing over the \(\tau\) types gives a support-sensitive polynomial algorithm; with the paper’s update accounting, this can be implemented in \(O(\tau mk)\) rational-arithmetic operations, with bit complexity polynomial in \(m,\tau\), and the input bit length \(L\). For empirical societies \(\mu_A=n_A/n\), the quota matches the paper’s exactly; the continuous formulation also allows arbitrary rational proportions.

I expect this mirror to be tractable (Class A). Its plausibility rests on preserving the paper’s underlying question: which size-\(k\) committee meets EJR for an approval society? Fractional population mass changes the electorate representation, while the committee and the approval relation retain their roles. The paper already reasons with fractions of votes, though it keeps voters as explicit profile entries.

This case covers Corollary 2 and its SEJR construction, not the cited PAV hardness result or every rule in the EJR-Exact family. Further questions include whether the entire EJR-Exact family admits the same support-sensitive treatment, and what precise bit-complexity bounds hold for its variants with rational masses.

The weak point is that the anchor is already polynomial in the discrete model. The mirror is a sound high-multiplicity formulation and removes dependence on repeated voter entries, but it does not make discrete hardness dissolve or expose a new tractability boundary. Its strength is the direct, recognizable population-level version of the paper’s EJR computation, not a claim of a surprising complexity reversal.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest anchor is Corollary 2: it gives a numbered, constructive result for computing an EJR committee. Its output is a committee, not an assignment to named voters, and the axiom depends on voters only through their approval sets and how many winners each set approves. A large membership election with many repeated ballots is a credible regime for it. The voters’ identities carry no information the result needs.

The proponent’s mass version is also well formed. For a coalition of type masses \(x_A\), require total mass at least \(\ell/k\) and at least \(\ell\) candidates common to every type with \(x_A>0\). EJR then requires positive coalition mass on types \(A\) with \(|A\cap W|\ge\ell\). That positive-mass condition captures “some member” when members of each type are interchangeable. Fractional quotas and arbitrary rational masses preserve the point of the axiom; they do not make it vacuous. The SEJR updates can use these masses in place of normalized voter counts, so the proposed support-sensitive algorithm has a plausible direct route. A measure-theoretic version with measurable coalitions would express the same condition, not expose a deeper obstruction.

The negative case therefore cannot honestly rest on the paper lacking a computational result, on identity dependence, or on the absence of a plausible high-multiplicity setting. Nor is the mirror already done continuously: the paper’s fractional residual votes still attach to a finite list of voters. High-multiplicity work supports the regime rather than colliding with it.

The one credible reservation is about research priority. SEJR already reasons with normalized approval counts, and this corollary appears unusually easy to aggregate by type. The mirror may add less methodological insight than a problem whose continuous formulation creates a new optimization or pricing challenge. But that is a judgement about likely yield, not a fundamental defect in the model. I cannot make a strong case that this anchor fails, much less that no worthwhile scenario exists; on the material given, the universal negative is weak.
