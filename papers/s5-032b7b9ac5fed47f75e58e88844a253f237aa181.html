The case FOR (proponent)

I find no named computational result in this paper to anchor a continuous mirror. Its theorems and propositions characterize aggregation rules by axioms; none asserts a complexity bound, gives an algorithm for a computational problem, or presents an encoding that the paper computes with.

The closest result is Theorem 6, proved here. For \(|X|\ge 4\), a generalized social welfare function on the paper’s rich domain of preference profiles satisfies extended Pareto if and only if there is a positive weight function \(w:X\times R_x\to\mathbb R_{++}\) such that, for every coalition \(A\) and profile \(R\), its normalized aggregate utility is
\[ u_H(f(R_A))=\sum_{i\in A} \frac{w(i,R_i)}{\sum_{j\in A}w(j,R_j)}\,u_H(R_i). \]
The weights are unique up to a common positive factor. This is a representation theorem, not a computational result.

There is a plausible *adjacent* population question: take a large society with \(\tau\) distinct normalized vNM preference types, each carrying a positive welfare weight, and rational masses \(\mu_t\); compute the aggregate preference represented by
\[ \bar u=\frac{\sum_t \mu_t w_t u_t}{\sum_t \mu_t w_t}. \]
A setting with many stakeholders and relatively few recurring preference types makes the population interpretation credible; evaluating this specified rule is tractable by summing over the support. But that changes the task from characterizing all extended-Pareto rules to evaluating one whose weights are supplied. It is not an anchor supplied by the paper, so I would not present it as the requested continuous problem per anchor. The paper’s social-choice results make population continuization plausible, but they do not provide the computational result this programme needs to mirror.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a ChoCo mirror is that the paper has no computational result to mirror. Its theorems characterize aggregation rules from axioms; they do not define an encoded computational problem, an algorithm, or a complexity bound. That applies to Theorem 6, the proponent’s closest candidate, and to the paper’s other representation results.

The population interpretation is plausible. In fact, Theorem 6’s person-specific weights need not defeat it: one can take the anonymous variant in Corollary 9, where weights depend on preferences, or treat the weight as part of a complete agent type. With finitely many types \(r\), masses \(\mu_r\), and weights \(w(r)\), the natural aggregate is
\[ \bar u=\frac{\sum_r \mu_r w(r)u(r)}{\sum_r \mu_r w(r)}. \]
A stronger continuous version would characterize extended-Pareto aggregators over measurable subpopulations using the corresponding integral. That is a coherent population continuization, but it remains an axiomatic representation question—the very kind of result this programme sets aside.

One could ask how to compute the displayed aggregate from a finite rational support, but that evaluates a specified weighted sum; it does not mirror a computational question posed or solved in the paper. Asking instead about robustness, weight recovery, or choosing among welfare weights would add an operation or objective absent from the results. Those could be worthwhile new projects, but they do not supply a computational anchor here.

So I would vote against a ChoCo mirror, while granting that the paper admits a sensible continuous-population axiomatization. The negative case is strong on programme fit, not proof that no useful computational problem could ever be built around this subject.
