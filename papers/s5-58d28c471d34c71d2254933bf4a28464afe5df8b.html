The case FOR (proponent)

The best positive case is the paper’s shared-input classification setting. In a large online survey or crowdsourced labeling task, let \(X\) be the common set of questions and let a type \(t\in\{+,-\}^{X}\) be an expert’s complete label vector. The mass \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of experts with that vector. For a classifier \(c\), define \(r_t(c)=|\{x\in X:c(x)\ne t(x)\}|\); then aggregate risk is \(R_\mu(c)=\sum_t\mu_t r_t(c)\). This is a plausible high-multiplicity regime when the number of experts is large and the number \(\tau\) of distinct label vectors is small.

A precise candidate is the continuum limit of shared-input strategyproof classification. At resolution \(q\), societies have masses in multiples of \(1/q\), and a mechanism \(M_q\) maps each society to a lottery over \(C\). It must satisfy strategyproofness for every one-unit report change: for any type \(t\) with \(\mu_t\ge 1/q\) and any alternative report \(t'\),
\[ \mathbb E_{c\sim M_q(\mu)}[r_t(c)] \le \mathbb E_{c\sim M_q(\mu-\tfrac1q e_t+\tfrac1q e_{t'})}[r_t(c)]. \]
The objective is to find mechanisms minimizing worst-case risk relative to the empirical risk minimizer, and to determine the limiting optimal approximation ratio as \(q\to\infty\). This keeps the experts’ incentives and the paper’s approximation objective in view. The paper’s prose suggests candidate limiting bounds of \(3\) for shared inputs and \(2\) under individual realizability.

There is a decisive qualification: the paper contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result. The approximation claims in §§2.1–2.2 are unnumbered summaries attributed to earlier work: the dictator bound \(3-2/n\) and its tightness, and the individually realizable bound \(2-2/n\). The weakest point of the candidate mirror is that an infinitesimal expert cannot change \(\mu\), making ordinary strategyproofness vacuous in the literal continuum. The finite-resolution deviations above preserve the original incentive question, but the limiting problem needs a principled account of how those constraints survive as \(q\to\infty\). A coalition-based continuum condition might address that issue, but it would change the strategic question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest anchor is shared-input classification, but it fails the programme’s first gate: this article states no numbered theorem or other named computational result. The proposed finite-resolution formulation also does not supply the missing anchor. At denominator \(q\), it asks about ordinary finite-agent strategyproof mechanisms on a finer grid. In the nonatomic limit, a single expert’s report change has no effect on the population distribution, so individual strategyproofness becomes vacuous. Requiring derivatives or allowing positive-mass coalitions could preserve strategic content, but those choices define a different incentive question. The paper gives no computational result whose continuous version would settle either one.

The same point covers the individually realizable case and its \(2-2/n\) guarantee: it is another unnumbered mechanism-performance summary, not a complexity result. Taking a limit of the reported \(n\)-dependent bounds would re-express those summaries; it would not create the algorithmic question the proposed mirror needs.

There is a real weakness in this negative case: a large survey or crowdsourcing population with repeated label vectors is a sensible high-multiplicity regime, and a continuous study of strategyproof mechanism design there could be worthwhile. So I would not claim that no useful continuous research can grow from this subject. The stronger, narrower conclusion is that the paper itself offers no qualifying computational result to anchor such a mirror; the proposed mechanism-design agenda is adjacent new work.
