The continuous mirror question

Given types \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, rational interaction rates \(a_{ts}\ge 0\), susceptibilities \(\lambda_t\in[0,1]\), and initial opinions \(u_t\in[-1,1]\), assume each type has positive total influence, the type influence graph is strongly connected, and some \(\lambda_t<1\). Compute the stable opinion \(z_t\) for every type, where \(W_\mu(t,s)=\mu_s a_{ts}/\sum_r\mu_r a_{tr}\) and \(z_t=\lambda_t\sum_s W_\mu(t,s)z_s+(1-\lambda_t)u_t\).

The model it lives in

A population of homogeneous interaction cohorts with masses \(\mu_t\), shared type-level susceptibility \(\lambda_t\) and initial opinion \(u_t\), and per-capita influence rates \(a_{ts}\). The state variables are the stable opinions \(z_t\), computed from the mass-dependent influence matrix \(W_\mu\).

The case FOR (proponent)

The narrowest honest lead is Type-Continuum FJ Equilibrium Computation, based on Theorem 1. The paper contains no numbered result classifying the complexity of an FJC problem. Theorem 1 is a convergence result, not a complexity theorem; the case for using it as an algorithmic anchor is that it gives a closed-form computation that the authors actually use for their FJ baseline.

Theorem 1 [FJ convergence], cited from Proskurnikov et al. rather than proved in this paper, says that if the graph is strongly connected and \(\Lambda\ne I\)—so at least one agent is partially anchored to their initial opinion—the FJ dynamics converge to the stable opinion vector
\[ \bm z=(I-\Lambda W)^{-1}(I-\Lambda)\bm u. \]
The paper uses this formula to compute its classical FJ baseline.

A plausible high-multiplicity regime is a large platform whose users fall into a modest number of exact interaction cohorts. For example, users in each cohort share the same susceptibility, initial opinion on the topic, and distribution of influence across cohorts. Millions of users could then be represented by a distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) such types, with \(\tau\ll n\). This is an exact model only when those within-type properties really agree; individual follow graphs are replaced by a type-level influence kernel.

For an instance, take types \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, rational nonnegative interaction rates \(a_{ts}\), rational susceptibilities \(\lambda_t\in[0,1]\), and rational initial opinions \(u_t\in[-1,1]\). Require every type to have positive total influence, the directed type influence graph to be strongly connected, and at least one type to have \(\lambda_t<1\). Define the normalized influence from type \(s\) on type \(t\) by
\[ W_\mu(t,s)=\frac{\mu_s a_{ts}}{\sum_r \mu_r a_{tr}}. \]
The question is to compute the stable opinion \(z_t\) for every type, satisfying
\[ z_t=\lambda_t\sum_s W_\mu(t,s)z_s+(1-\lambda_t)u_t. \]
A solution is the exact rational vector \(\bm z\). This is an equilibrium-computation problem, so its output is the state vector rather than an intervention or an optimized objective.

I expect this mirror to be tractable: it is a \(\tau\)-dimensional linear system, solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the input bit length. The population is the continuous object—the \(\mu_t\) are population shares—while the support stays finite. The result covers only the FJ baseline computation in Theorem 1, not the paper’s main FJC claims.

The weak point is substantial: Theorem 1 concerns classical FJ dynamics, not cascade-driven FJC, and its contribution here is the convergence formula rather than a computational-complexity result. The continuum model also averages away the cascade tree, exposure order, and central-node effects that motivate the paper. I would therefore not present this as a mirror of the paper’s main FJC result. Further questions—such as computing expected terminal opinions under type-level cascades or choosing seed mass to change polarization—could be worthwhile, but the paper’s named results do not anchor their complexity.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 1 is the only named anchor, and it is a poor one for ChoCo. It is a classical FJ convergence theorem cited from prior work; it gives the stable vector \(z=(I-\Lambda W)^{-1}(I-\Lambda)u\), which this paper uses as a baseline. It does not classify the complexity of a computational problem. Calling “compute the equilibrium” its mirror creates a new task around a formula, rather than continuizing a computational result of this paper.

The proposed type version also needs a careful boundary. If cohorts share the same susceptibility, initial opinion, and influence into every cohort, then the type-level system is a sensible exact FJ model—but it describes the paper’s classical baseline, not its cascade-driven contribution. If cohorts merely share average interaction rates, the resulting matrix need not reproduce the aggregate equilibrium of the original network: averaging the interactions does not generally commute with solving the FJ system. So the proposed formulation is either a special block-structured model or a new approximation, rather than a high-multiplicity relaxation of the networks studied here.

A stronger mirror would keep the FJC mechanism: give types masses and an interaction kernel, then compute expected terminal opinions or choose a mass of cascade seeds to change polarization. That is more recognizably about this paper. But type-level averages do not generally preserve the cascade’s first-exposure tree, propagation order, and dependence on network position. To retain those exactly, types must encode increasingly detailed structural and cascade histories; in the general case, that can bring the support back toward the number of individuals. A positive seed mass avoids the vanishing effect of a single seed in an atomless population, and multiple sources are compatible with the paper’s setup, but it changes the single-source question into a mass-seeding model. This could be a worthwhile new FJC project, yet it is not anchored by a computational result in this paper.

So the strongest negative case is that this paper offers no result-anchored computational mirror: Theorem 1 belongs to the FJ baseline, and its closer FJC alternatives require new, restrictive modelling choices. I cannot honestly make the universal claim airtight. Large, genuinely repeated interaction cohorts and mass seeding could form a plausible regime the authors would recognize. The case against is strong for the proposed Theorem 1 anchor, but weak as a claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario.
