The case FOR (proponent)

There is no named computational-complexity result in this paper that meets the required anchor test. Its numbered results characterize welfare ratios, rather than placing an underlying computational problem in P, proving it hard, or giving a parameterized classification. The protocol and complexity claims in the introduction are cited from other work.

The closest positive anchor is Theorem 3.20, proved here: \(\mathrm{POIUf}(n,k)\ge k\) for \(k\le n\), and \(\mathrm{POIUf}(n,k)=n\) for \(k>n\). It concerns the maximum gain in utilitarian welfare from allowing up to \(k\) pieces per player, subject to envy-freeness. It is a welfare theorem, not a complexity result.

A plausible population mirror would model a large land-division or service-allocation market with \(N\) residents but only \(\tau\) valuation types, where \(N\gg\tau\). A type \(t\) is a normalized valuation density \(d_t\), and \(\mu_t\) is its population share. For each type, let \(Q_t\) describe the fractions of its residents receiving each individual bundle \(A\), where every \(A\) is a union of at most \(k\) intervals. With per-capita supply profile \(\rho\), feasibility requires
\[ \sum_t \mu_t\,\Pr_{A\sim Q_t}[x\in A]\le \rho(x) \]
almost everywhere. Each resident still receives an integral bundle; \(Q_t\) records how bundles are distributed within the cohort. Require envy-freeness between every pair of bundles assigned positive mass, and measure welfare by \(\sum_t\mu_t\mathbb{E}_{A\sim Q_t}[V_t(A)]\). The concrete question suggested by Theorem 3.20 is whether the corresponding \(k\)-piece versus one-piece welfare-gain bound persists when population counts are replaced by type masses, and how to compute or approximate that gain for piecewise-constant rational inputs.

This is a plausible mirror of the theorem’s welfare question, but it cannot serve as a compliant computational anchor: the theorem supplies no algorithm or complexity claim. My tentative expectation is tractability for fixed \(\tau\), \(k\), and input partition size, perhaps through a finite configuration optimization; the variable-parameter problem is open. The soft spot is substantial: the mirror adds per-capita-scaled supply and cohort-level randomization, while the paper’s extremal constructions use player-specific pieces. Its existing continuity is chiefly in the resource, not the population.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive weakness is that Theorem 3.20 is not a computational result. It bounds an extremal welfare ratio; it gives no algorithm, hardness result, or complexity classification. The introduction’s protocol results are cited from other work, and Section 4 leaves algorithmic complexity as future work. So the paper offers no named computational theorem for a continuous-complexity mirror to inherit.

The strongest version of the proposed welfare mirror is coherent: give each valuation type a mass, scale supply per capita, let individuals receive integral bundles, and use \(Q_t\) to describe how bundles are distributed within a type. Envy-freeness can be required across every pair of bundles assigned positive mass. The difficulty is the connection to Theorem 3.20. Its \(n\)-dependent bounds are witnessed by constructions where each player has private cake that every other player values at zero. Preserving that structure requires essentially one distinct valuation type per player, so the support size grows with \(n\). With a fixed number \(\tau\) of repeated types, the number of residents disappears from the normalized model; the relevant scale is \(\tau\), not population size. The theorem therefore supplies little guidance about the weighted finite-type problem’s bounds or algorithms.

That is a strong case against treating Theorem 3.20 as evidence for a continuous mirror. It does not establish that no worthwhile mirror exists. A computational study of envy-free welfare optimization for a finite support of valuation types could be sensible on its own. My negative case is weakest exactly there: I can show the theorem does not anchor that study, but I cannot honestly rule out its independent value.
