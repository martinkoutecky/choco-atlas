The case FOR (proponent)

There is a plausible high-multiplicity mirror here. A school district with many teachers and relatively few complete teacher types is a convincing regime: a type records each teacher’s disutility for every school, along with any other feature the allocation uses. If \(N\) teachers fall into \(\tau\) such types, with \(N\gg\tau\), let \(\mu_t\) be each type’s mass and \(x_{tf}\) the mass assigned to school \(f\). Schools have known utilities \(U_f(x_f)\), and efficient assignment maximizes
\[ \sum_f U_f(x_f)-\sum_{t,f}d_t(f)x_{tf}, \qquad \sum_f x_{tf}\le\mu_t. \]
Firing workers becomes removing a vector \(y\le x_f\) of type mass. This is close to the paper’s own school-district motivation, though extending utility from worker subsets to type-mass vectors is a substantive co-modification.

The strongest potential anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper: the VCG mechanism is individually rational if and only if every firm’s utility satisfies weak substitutes. Theorem 2, also proved here, says VCG is strongly individually rational if and only if every firm’s utility is submodular. These suggest continuous analogues: for differentiable \(U_f\), ask whether universal firm IR corresponds to
\[ U_f(x)\ge \nabla U_f(x)\cdot x, \]
and universal strong IR to
\[ U_f(x)-U_f(x-y)\ge \nabla U_f(x)\cdot y \quad\text{for all }0\le y\le x. \]
Those are plausible limit forms of the paper’s marginal-product inequalities, not results established by it. The continuous VCG transfers would also need a careful definition, likely via limits of finite high-multiplicity markets.

There is a hard constraint on the case: neither theorem is a computational result. They characterize when participation properties hold; neither classifies an algorithmic problem as tractable or hard. The paper contains no named theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a computational complexity result, so it supplies no eligible anchor under the requested rule. The continuous characterization above is a credible research direction, but I cannot honestly present it as the required per-anchor computational question or assign it a tractability, transfer-hardness, or continuum-specific-hardness prediction.

The weakest point is therefore more than the lack of an algorithm: the paper’s results depend on discrete worker subsets and individual VCG payments, and the continuous utility and payment model is not canonical. The school-district regime makes the population mirror recognizable; it does not fill that gap in the paper’s named results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that this paper has no computational result to continuize. Theorem 1 characterizes when the VCG mechanism is individually rational; Theorem 2 characterizes when it is strongly individually rational. Neither studies an algorithmic problem: the paper gives no input representation for firm utilities, running time, or complexity classification. The proponent’s own observation is decisive under ChoCo’s stated scope: these are possible continuous mechanism-design questions, but they are not computational mirrors of the paper’s results.

For Theorem 1, the strongest mirror is the proposed high-multiplicity teacher market, with firm utility \(U_f(x_f)\) over assigned type masses, and a continuous version of the weak-substitutes condition. That is a coherent mathematical question. But it remains a characterization of when a participation property holds, not a complexity question. To turn it into one, someone must choose how \(U_f\) is represented and what is to be computed or certified. A succinct formula, a piecewise-linear description, and an oracle access model pose different computational problems; Theorem 1 selects none of them. That choice would define a new project rather than mirror this result.

The same problem applies to Theorem 2. One can ask whether a continuous condition such as
\[ U_f(x)-U_f(x-y)\ge \nabla U_f(x)\cdot y \]
characterizes strong firm rationality against firing a submass \(y\). But the paper’s result is again a valuation-property characterization, not an algorithmic classification. There is also a modeling cost: in an atomless society, one worker’s report has no effect on the allocation, so the paper’s individual strategy-proofness and VCG externality become vacuous at the limit. A finite-market approximation can retain individual incentives, but then convergence of payments and participation conditions becomes a new asymptotic question. Neither route supplies the missing computational anchor.

The school-district setting makes repeated teacher types plausible, and the permitted co-modification to mass-based firm utilities does not itself disqualify the mirror. It does not, however, turn either theorem into a computational result. One could build a worthwhile computational paper around a chosen representation of \(U_f\), for example by studying how to certify these properties or compute outcomes. That would be an adjacent research direction, not a continuous mirror of a computational contribution in this paper.

So the negative case is strong under ChoCo’s gate, but not a proof that no useful continuous question could ever be asked here. The proposed continuous characterizations may be worthwhile on their own; what fails is their eligibility as computational mirrors of this paper’s named results.
