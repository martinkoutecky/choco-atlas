The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the proponent supplied no qualifying anchor. Propositions 4 and 6 show when IMB produces a maximal individually rational matching; Propositions 5 and 7 add equivalence and welfare properties. None states a complexity result for a computational problem. The paper gives algorithms, but does not analyze their running time or classify the complexity of finding an outcome. Under the requested anchor rule, there is no named computational result here to mirror.

The best candidate for a mirror would model students and schools as populations of types, scale capacities with population, and ask whether a mass version of IMB still produces a maximal individually rational matching or agrees with mass versions of DA and TTC. That is a coherent matching question. But it would add a computational problem that the paper does not pose. There is also a more basic obstacle to the usual finite-support high-multiplicity model: schools have strict priorities over individual students. Since those priorities affect who gets a seat, a student’s priority positions are part of their complete type. Aggregating students by preference alone discards information the mechanisms use; retaining the strict priority distinctions can make the types essentially individual, so support size grows with the population. A diffuse distribution over priority ranks is possible, but it requires a new continuous-type model. Making finite cohorts possible would generally mean changing strict priorities to type-level ties or another priority system, and the results would then need to be re-established for those primitives. Individual strategyproofness also needs care: a single student has zero mass in an atomless population, so the paper’s unilateral-deviation property does not transfer directly.

This is a strong case against calling any such model a mirror of this paper’s computational results, because the paper has no such results. It is weaker as a universal claim that no worthwhile continuous matching project exists: mass versions of maximal individual rationality or mechanism equivalence could be worthwhile new computational questions. They would be follow-on work inspired by the paper, rather than continuizations of a complexity result it proves.
