What the mirror covers

No result is covered: Theorems 1–5 and Lemmas 1–2 concern axioms and representability; Propositions 1–2 give explicit social welfare functions as representation examples without computational guarantees.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive lead is to treat an equitable redistribution as a mass-pairing problem. Let the population have total mass \(1\), with each agent’s type recording their utility before and after a proposed change: \(t=(p,q)\), where \(p\) is the old utility and \(q\) the new utility. Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over finitely many such types, ask whether there is a symmetric coupling \(\lambda\) that pairs all mass into inequality-reducing transfers. In particular, \(\lambda_{t,t'}>0\) only when, writing \(t=(p,q)\) and \(t'=(p',q')\), either \(p<q<q'<p'\) or \(p'<q'<q<p\); its row sums must equal \(\mu\). A coupling \(\lambda\) is the solution. This is a concrete continuous-population analogue of the paper’s Weak Equity pairing condition, and its finite-support feasibility version is a linear program.

But it is an unanchored research lead, not a valid mirror of a named computational result in this paper. Theorem 1, proved here, says no real-valued social welfare function satisfies Infinite Equity and Anonymity when \(|Y|\ge4\). Theorem 2, also proved here, characterizes representation under Generalized Equity and Monotonicity (equivalently, Infinite Equity and Monotonicity): it exists exactly when \(|Y|\le5\). Theorem 4, proved here, says Weak Equity with Monotonicity and Anonymity admits a real-valued representation exactly when \(Y\) is well-ordered. These are existence and representation results about social welfare relations, not complexity or algorithm results. The paper contains no named computational result to use as an anchor.

The pairing idea has some claim to author recognition: it retains the paper’s concern with inequality-reducing transfers and its use of pairings, while making population mass explicit. Its weak point is substantial: it replaces countable utility streams and their axioms with a finite-support computational question about finding a transfer witness. The paper’s theorems do not establish that this is their problem or imply the LP result. So I would present it as a possible computational follow-up, not as a continuous mirror of an anchored result; there is no responsible Class A/B/C forecast for the paper’s actual results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that the paper has no computational result to mirror. Theorem 1, Theorem 2, and Theorem 4 are named results, but they characterize when social welfare functions exist under combinations of equity, anonymity, and monotonicity. None gives the complexity of a computational problem. Turning one into an algorithmic question would require adding a new task and arguing that it is the authors’ problem.

The three results the proponent names do not supply that missing task. Theorem 1 concerns representation of rankings over *all* streams in \(Y^{\mathbb N}\), where Infinite Equity can require strict preference after changes on any infinite set of coordinates. Theorem 2’s threshold \(|Y|\le5\) is about the size of the utility domain, not the support size \(\tau\) of a society. Theorem 4 turns on whether the entire domain \(Y\) is well-ordered. Those are properties of a space of possible streams and a preference relation on that space; none is a population input whose manipulation or optimization the theorem studies.

The proposed coupling LP is the most plausible bridge, but it changes the object. Given masses of transition types \((p,q)\), it asks whether the transitions can be paired to witness inequality-reducing transfers. That is a concrete new feasibility problem. The paper instead asks how a social welfare relation should rank streams, and whether such a relation has a real-valued representation. A pairing witness does not answer either question. A better variant could minimize the cost of moving a distribution to one that admits such a pairing, but that adds a cost model and a planner’s objective absent from the paper. It may be a worthwhile adjacent project; the paper gives no computational result that would make it a mirror.

There is also a substantive mismatch in what mass can preserve. In the paper, an infinite set of generations may be countable and have zero mass in any ordinary continuum model. Infinite Equity still requires strict ranking after transfers on that set. Replacing “an arbitrary infinite set” with “a set of positive measure” would produce a coherent continuum axiom, but it would be a different equity condition. And a distribution over utility values erases the stream’s coordinates: finite anonymity permits finite permutations, while the representation results concern rankings of ordered, infinite streams. A richer type space could retain position, but then it loses the repeated-type regime that makes population masses useful.

So I would reject all three theorem anchors and the proposed coupling as mirrors. The honest limit is that this does not prove no related continuous project could interest these authors. It does make the universal negative case strongest where it can be: every plausible route found here changes the paper’s axiomatic representation problem into a newly specified population-optimization problem. That is not enough to establish a worthwhile continuous mirror of this paper.
