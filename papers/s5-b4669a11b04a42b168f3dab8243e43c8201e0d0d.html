This paper has no named computational result in the required sense: no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition establishes a problem’s complexity or algorithmic status. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The absence of a computational theorem is real, but it only rules out a direct mirror of a complexity result stated in this paper. It does not rule out a computational question grounded in the paper’s central formal result: whether the mass of fair-minded voters is enough to move the majority outcome, depending on the income-class structure.

Appendix B makes that a natural population problem. Voters have an income class and a preferred redistribution level; the paper explicitly lets each class occupy a share of a continuum electorate and determines the outcome by the median vote. A direct computational question is: given a rational distribution over these voter types, what mass of fair-minded voters is sufficient to move the outcome past the pocketbook prediction? A stronger control version asks for the minimum-cost transfer of mass between preference types that makes a specified redistribution level win. Both keep the policy space finite and make the population the continuous object.

The instance regime is credible: a large electorate can have substantial numbers of citizens with the same income class, redistribution preference, and intervention cost. Those features form complete types. Appendix B’s continuum analysis supports that regime; it does not collide with the proposed work, because it studies the outcome analytically rather than its computational complexity.

The honest limitation is that the paper supplies no intervention costs, and its one-dimensional median structure may make the simplest version easy. That limits the likely complexity story; it does not establish that no worthwhile mirror exists. A tractable algorithm would still answer the paper’s pivotality question computationally, while a costed control version gives a more substantial problem directly tied to it.
