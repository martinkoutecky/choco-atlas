The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a research extension, not a continuous mirror of a computational result in this paper. The paper contains no named computational result about complexity or algorithms: its numbered results concern implementation, incentives, budget balance, participation, and the number of mechanism stages.

The closest foothold is Theorem 3, proved here: for any procurement context, the dynamic elaboration reverse second price auction implements a utilitarian ex-post efficient outcome under pooled awareness in conditionally dominant strategies, with budget balance and ex-post participation constraints for all sellers. Proposition 1 also says the dynamic elaboration VCG mechanism implements its outcome in at most three stages. Those are substantive mechanism-design results, but neither gives a running-time bound or a complexity classification. The three-stage bound is about interaction rounds, not the computational complexity of solving a problem.

A plausible setting for a sequel would be a large procurement program with many repeated, near-standard projects and many suppliers drawn from a small number \(\tau\) of complete payoff types. A type would include its cost description at each awareness level, its projections between awareness levels, and all other payoff-relevant terms; \(\mu_t\) would be the fraction of supplier capacity of type \(t\). One could ask how hard it is, given rational \(\mu_t\), capacities, costs, and project demand, to compute an efficient pooled-awareness allocation \(y_t\) and transfers, with \(0\le y_t\le \kappa_t\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t y_t=d\), that meet truthfulness, budget, and participation requirements.

That is a concrete high-multiplicity question, but it is not an anchor the paper establishes. It extends the paper from awarding a single project to allocating mass across repeated projects. The weak point is especially sharp: the paper’s awareness incentive rewards a unique first agent to reveal pooled awareness, while its reverse auction depends on the lowest and second-lowest individual bids. Neither feature has an automatic population-mass counterpart. Under the requested anchor rule, therefore, there are zero valid anchors and no qualifying continuous problem to state per anchor.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no computational result in this paper to anchor a continuous mirror. Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 establish implementation properties; Theorem 3 adds budget balance and participation for a procurement auction. Proposition 1’s three-stage bound counts rounds of interaction, not computational steps. The paper gives no encoded input, algorithm, or complexity claim. Its type spaces and outcome sets are deliberately general, so even computing an efficient outcome or the transfer term \(m_i\) is not posed as a computational problem.

The best candidate for a sequel is Theorem 3: a large procurement program with many suppliers drawn from a small set of complete cost and awareness types. Grouping suppliers by those types would be legitimate high multiplicity. But the theorem’s mechanism depends on features a type distribution does not preserve: it awards one project to a named lowest bidder, pays the second-lowest bid, and compensates the unique supplier who first reveals pooled awareness. With a diffuse continuum, a lowest-cost supplier may not exist as an individual; if a positive mass shares the lowest cost, selecting one supplier is again an individual-level choice. And a single zero-mass report cannot change the type distribution’s support or be the unique pivotal awareness revealer. Those details are central to the theorem’s incentive and participation guarantees.

Scaling demand with the population and allocating a mass of projects across supplier types is the strongest repair. It could support a worthwhile continuous procurement model, but it needs a new allocation rule, pricing rule, and treatment of awareness incentives. Treating each type cohort as a strategic player would also change the paper’s agents and their incentive constraints. That is a plausible research extension; the paper does not supply a reason to regard it as a continuous mirror of Theorem 3.

Proposition 1 offers no independent anchor: its round bound does not depend on population size, and making the stages continuous would continuize time rather than society. The VCG and Clarke results have the same individual-report structure as the procurement result.

So the negative case is strong for the requested classification: there are no valid computational anchors here, and the best proposed population model loses the pivotal individuals that make the paper’s main mechanism work. The universal claim is less secure: a repeated-project procurement extension may be worth studying in its own right, but it would begin by creating a new computational problem rather than continuizing one this paper establishes.
