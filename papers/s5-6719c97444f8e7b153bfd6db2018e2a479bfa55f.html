The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The other analyst raises no anchor to defeat: this paper states no numbered theorem, lemma, or corollary about the complexity of a computational problem. Its questions are about what makes Council voting fair and how the Nice and Lisbon systems compare with proposed alternatives. The power-index tables are finite evaluations, several adapted from earlier work; they do not establish an algorithmic result. There is therefore no computational result here for ChoCo to continuize.

The strongest possible rescue would replace the EU’s 27 member states with a much larger federation whose state-like units fall into recurring types. One could ask how Banzhaf or Shapley–Shubik power behaves as the distribution of unit types changes, or how to choose weights and a quota to balance citizen-level power. That is a more credible proposal than treating EU citizens as Council voters: the Council’s voting agents are states, not citizens.

But this rescue changes the object that the indices measure. A state’s Banzhaf power comes from being able to swing a coalition. If the states become an atomless mass, an individual state has measure zero, so its swing probability vanishes. A type-level sensitivity of the outcome to added mass is not the same quantity. One could define a limit of normalized swing powers, but that would require choices about the finite populations, coalition probabilities, and scaling. It might be a worthwhile new study of asymptotic voting power; it does not mirror a result established in this paper.

I cannot prove that no such study could be valuable. A federation with many repeated state-like units is a plausible high-multiplicity setting, and the existence of high-multiplicity work would support its plausibility. The negative case is strongest, and decisive for this paper, on narrower grounds: there is no named computational result to anchor the mirror. It is weaker if “mirror” is stretched to include a new asymptotic research programme suggested by the paper’s subject.
