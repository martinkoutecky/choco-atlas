The continuous mirror question

Given finite item kinds \(E\), valuation types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), rational per-person supplies \(q_e\ge 0\), and normalized monotone submodular type valuations on integral bundles, define \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)=\sup_{\sigma}\min_{x:\sigma_x>0}v_t(x)\), where \(\sigma\) is a finite-support distribution on integral bundles with mean bundle \(q\). With comparison-oracle access to the valuations, can a randomized algorithm polynomial in the binary input length, \(|E|\), and \(|T|\) find rational masses \(\pi_{t,x}\ge0\) of finite support satisfying \(\sum_x\pi_{t,x}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,x}\pi_{t,x}x_e=q_e\) for each \(e\), and \(v_t(x)\ge 0.142\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) for every assigned bundle, while satisfying EFL across all positive-mass bundles?

The model it lives in

The instance has rational masses \(\mu_t\) of complete valuation types and rational per-person supplies \(q\). The decision variables are finite-support masses \(\pi_{t,x}\) over integral bundles \(x\); they conserve each type’s mass and each item supply. The goal is to find an assignment in which every supported bundle meets \(0.142\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) for its type and each supported bundle is EFL relative to every bundle in the support.

The objection that survived

The finite theorem guarantees a fraction of finite-instance MMS, whereas \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}\) may be larger; replicating rational inputs can also take time exponential in their binary description. The proponent flags these gaps but does not establish benchmark transfer or a compressed-input algorithm.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the simultaneous EFL and \(0.142\)-MMS allocation guarantee for submodular types, anchored by Theorem 7.3 and supported by Theorem 1.3. It leaves the paper’s \(0.2219\) ex-ante guarantee and its detailed analysis of RECE1’s concentration behavior outside the stated question.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) be approximated or characterized efficiently from the stated valuation-oracle access?
  • Can one find a finite-support assignment satisfying the EFL constraints in time polynomial in the succinct input length?
  • Do finite-replica guarantees transfer to the continuous MMS benchmark, and with what approximation ratio?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I see is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s simultaneous EFL and approximate-MMS allocation problem. Imagine a large city distributing standardized relief supplies among households. A type is a household’s complete submodular valuation over supply bundles; many households share each type, so the number of agents is large while the number of types \(\tau\) is much smaller. As the population grows, supply grows with it, and the input records the amount of each supply kind per person. Each person still receives an integral bundle; only the masses assigned to types and bundles are fractional.

My anchor is Theorem 1.3, proved here. It says that every finite allocation instance with submodular valuations has an allocation that is both EFL and \(\rho\)-MMS for a universal \(\rho>0\), with the proof establishing \(\rho\ge 0.142\). The theorem’s statement is existential; the text immediately after it says the proof also gives a randomized polynomial-time algorithm using comparison queries, with high probability and a slightly worse constant. Theorem 7.3 gives a concrete endpoint: RECE1 produces a \(0.142\)-MMS allocation for every agent with positive probability, and Proposition 3.5 says RECE1 always produces an EFL allocation. These are the paper’s positive computational results; it gives no named hardness result to anchor a hardness mirror.

Here is the continuous problem I would hand to a prover. Let \(E\) be a finite set of item kinds, \(T\) a finite set of valuation types, \(\mu_t\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) the mass of type \(t\), and \(q_e\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) the supply of item kind \(e\) per person. Assume \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Each type has a normalized, monotone submodular valuation \(v_t\) over integral bundles \(x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\), where \(x_e\) counts copies of item kind \(e\). Copies are individually indivisible, with diminishing marginal values.

Define the type-\(t\) continuous maximin share by
\[ \operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q) = \sup_{\sigma} \min_{x:\,\sigma_x>0} v_t(x), \qquad \sum_x \sigma_x=1,\quad \sum_x \sigma_x x=q, \]
where \(\sigma\) ranges over finite-support distributions on integral bundles. This is the maximin benchmark for a homogeneous unit-mass population of type \(t\) facing the same per-capita supply \(q\). At a finite replication scale, an ordinary MMS partition gives an equal-weight distribution of bundles; allowing arbitrary masses gives its continuum counterpart.

The problem, Continuous EFL–\(\rho\)-MMS for Submodular Types, is: given \((E,T,\mu,q,(v_t)_{t\in T})\), find a finite-support mass assignment \(\pi_{t,x}\ge0\) such that
\[ \sum_x\pi_{t,x}=\mu_t \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{t,x}\pi_{t,x}x_e=q_e\ \text{for each }e\in E. \]
Every bundle assigned positive mass to type \(t\) must satisfy \(v_t(x)\ge 0.142\,\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\). The assignment must also be EFL: for every type \(t\), every \(x\) assigned positive mass to \(t\), and every bundle \(y\) assigned positive mass to anyone, either \(|y|\le1\), or some \(e\) with \(y_e>0\) satisfies
\[ v_t(x)\ge v_t(y-\mathbf e_e) \quad\text{and}\quad v_t(\mathbf e_e)\le v_t(x). \]
Thus the solution specifies which fraction of each type receives each integral bundle, conserves every item supply, and satisfies the fairness conditions for every positive-mass bundle in the support.

I expect this question to be tractable, through a continuous configuration formulation and an efficient way to handle the bundle choices, though that is a research conjecture rather than a consequence already proved by the paper. For any common-denominator scale, the instance becomes a finite replica with many agents of each type and many copies of each item kind. The paper’s theorem applies to those finite instances, since the symmetric valuations over copies are submodular. That gives a concrete route toward the mirror, while keeping the question about the population itself.

The weakest point is the passage from finite replicas to an efficient continuous algorithm. The paper does not prove that its finite MMS guarantees converge to \(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}\) in a way that preserves an EFL allocation, and its polynomial running time is in the expanded finite instance size—not necessarily in the binary description of \(\mu\) and \(q\). Moreover, EFL imposes comparisons across the entire bundle support, so the continuous problem is not automatically a plain LP. Those are the questions the prover should address: whether the finite guarantees pass to the continuum, whether the bundle-support constraints admit an efficient pricing or separation method, and whether the \(0.142\) ratio can be retained or improved.

This mirror covers the paper’s submodular simultaneous-fairness result, not its additive comparisons or every result surveyed in the related work. The model changes supply alongside population size so that per-person resources remain meaningful; it keeps individual bundles integral and uses continuous mass only for the population distribution.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only anchor that needs answering is Theorem 1.3 and its algorithmic counterpart, Theorem 7.3. The paper does have a named computational result: a randomized polynomial time algorithm, with comparison-query access, that finds simultaneous EFL and constant approximate-MMS allocations. So “there is nothing computational to mirror” cannot carry the negative case.

The strongest objection is that the proposed continuum may add little to the existence result while making its algorithm harder to state. For rational type masses and per-capita supplies, take a common denominator \(N\), make \(N\mu_t\) agents of each type, and supply \(Nq_e\) copies of each item kind. The finite theorem applies to these replicas. But it guarantees a fraction of the finite MMS, while the proposed \(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) optimizes over arbitrary bundle distributions and could be larger. And the replica algorithm runs in the expanded instance size, which can be exponential in the binary input length. Thus neither the exact continuous guarantee nor a polynomial-time algorithm in the compressed input follows for free.

That objection does not defeat the better mirror. Its population regime is coherent: many agents can share a complete valuation type; standardized indivisible supplies can scale with the population; and mass can describe which fractions receive which integral bundles. EFL remains a meaningful comparison among supported bundles, and MMS need not vanish. Those are substantive features of the problem, not defects that can be removed by insisting on literal finite-agent definitions. Nor does the paper’s use of named agents in envy-cycle elimination show that a type-level algorithm is impossible; it only leaves a real algorithmic question about compressing the allocation and its fairness constraints.

So the negative case is weak at its universal burden. It can say the proposed mirror needs a precise valuation-query model, and that the finite theorem does not settle its benchmark or bit complexity. It cannot honestly say no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario. The anchor survives those objections: whether simultaneous EFL and approximate MMS can be computed for a continuous population with a succinct, type-level input remains a well-posed computational question.
