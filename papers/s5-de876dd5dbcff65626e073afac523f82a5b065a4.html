The continuous mirror question

Given finite type sets \(F\) and \(W\), compatible pairs \(E\subseteq F\times W\), rational type masses \(\alpha_f,\beta_w\) totaling \(1\) on each side, strict rankings of compatible types, and rational pair costs \(c_{fw}\), compute a minimum-cost stable assignment \(x_{fw}\geq 0\). Require \(\sum_w x_{fw}\leq\alpha_f\), \(\sum_f x_{fw}\leq\beta_w\), and, for every \(fw\in E\), require \(p_f(w;x)=0\) or \(p_w(f;x)=0\), where \(p_f(w;x)=\alpha_f-\sum_{w':\,w'\succeq_f w}x_{fw'}\) and \(p_w(f;x)=\beta_w-\sum_{f':\,f'\succeq_w f}x_{f'w}\). Minimize \(\sum_{fw\in E}c_{fw}x_{fw}\); characterize its complexity in the number of types, compatible pairs, and input bit length.

The model it lives in

A nonatomic bipartite market with ranked agent types carrying rational masses. The decision variables \(x_{fw}\) are the masses matched across compatible type pairs; each person receives at most one partner, while type-level averages may be fractional. Minimize total pair cost subject to mass capacities and the condition that no compatible pair has positive mass on both sides willing to improve.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I see is Minimum-Cost Stable Matching for Typed Populations, anchored on Theorem 6.2, proved in this paper. It states that, for the paper’s CBM model, given an edge cost \(c(e)\), one can find a stable matching minimizing total cost in strongly polynomial time, counting standard operations and choice-function oracle calls. The theorem covers arbitrary plottian, cardinally monotone choice functions on the firm side and linearly ordered preferences with quotas on the worker side. Its method turns costs on edges into weights on rotations, then minimizes over ideals of the rotation poset.

The paper’s introduction also recalls a cited \(\#\mathrm P\)-hardness result for *counting* stable marriages. That is a different problem, attributed to earlier work, and is not a numbered result of this paper; I would not use it as an anchor.

A plausible regime is a large, repeated-profile hiring market: many applicants and vacancies, but far fewer distinct preference and cost profiles. A type includes an agent’s side, acceptable counterpart types, ranking of those types, and relevant cost schedule. For example, a large hiring cohort might have tens of thousands of applicants and posts but only hundreds of such types. Individuals within a type are interchangeable for the market’s purposes.

Here is the continuous problem. The instance has finite type sets \(F\) and \(W\), compatible pairs \(E\subseteq F\times W\), and rational masses \(\alpha_f\) and \(\beta_w\), each side totaling \(1\). Each type ranks its compatible types on the other side; each agent has unit quota, and each match of types \(f,w\) has rational cost \(c_{fw}\). The question is to return a minimum-cost stable mass assignment: nonnegative rational values \(x_{fw}\), where \(x_{fw}\) is the mass of type-\(f\) agents matched to type-\(w\) agents, subject to
\[ \sum_w x_{fw}\leq \alpha_f,\qquad \sum_f x_{fw}\leq \beta_w. \]
Its cost is \(\sum_{fw\in E}c_{fw}x_{fw}\).

Stability is defined at the individual level, even though the solution records type-level masses. For a compatible pair \(fw\), let
\[ p_f(w;x)=\alpha_f-\sum_{w':\,w'\succeq_f w}x_{fw'},\qquad p_w(f;x)=\beta_w-\sum_{f':\,f'\succeq_w f}x_{f'w}. \]
These are the masses of agents on each side who are unmatched or strictly prefer the proposed type to their current partner type. Require \(p_f(w;x)=0\) or \(p_w(f;x)=0\) for every \(fw\in E\). If both were positive, some positive mass on each side could be paired to form a blocking match. Any feasible flow can be realized by splitting the nonatomic type populations into matched and unmatched subgroups, while each individual still receives at most one integral partner.

This is population continuization: \(x_{fw}\) is an aggregate of individual matches, not a fractional bundle assigned to an individual. Scaling rational masses and flows by a common denominator gives the corresponding high-multiplicity market with repeated types. The proposed question asks for the exact complexity in the number of types, compatible pairs, and input bit length.

I would provisionally expect Class A, though Theorem 6.2 does not prove it. Its rotation-to-closure argument suggests a route: if type-level mass shifts admit a compressed rotation representation, minimum cost might again reduce to optimization over an order structure. The useful next questions are whether such a representation exists with size polynomial in the type support and whether every stable mass assignment arises from it.

The soft spot is that the paper’s individual preferences are orders over named edges, while repeated agents who regard same-type partners as interchangeable are indifferent among those individuals. That symmetry may break the paper’s lattice and rotation structure; the stability conditions above are disjunctive, so a continuous mirror could instead be continuum-specifically hard. I still think the market is a credible mirror: the population is the continuous object, and the underlying question—choosing a minimum-cost stable matching—is the theorem’s own computational problem. This case covers the theorem’s unit-quota, ranked-choice slice, not every choice-function or sequential-choice result in the paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 6.2 is a real computational result, so the “nothing here to mirror” objection does not apply. My best case against the proponent’s anchor is narrower: the proposed type-mass problem is already the minimum-cost stable allocation problem in continuous form.

Take one stable-allocation vertex for each type, give it capacity equal to that type’s mass, and let \(x_{fw}\) be the amount assigned along each compatible type pair. Rankings over partner types give the stable-allocation preferences; a dummy outside option handles unmatched mass. A blocking type pair in the proponent’s model is exactly a pair with positive mass on both sides able to improve by increasing their assignment and dropping unmatched or less-preferred mass. The objective \(\sum_{fw} c_{fw}x_{fw}\) is the same edge-cost objective. So this is not just a known answer to a newly posed question: it is the same mass-flow model, with the vertices read as cohorts of interchangeable people. Stable-allocation work studies real-valued assignments and gives polynomial algorithms for the minimum-cost version; the foundational model also gives a strongly polynomial algorithm for finding stable allocations ([Dean and Munshi](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~bcdean/stable_alloc.pdf), [Baïou and Balinski](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.27.3.485.310)).

That interpretation matters. The assignment values are fractional at the type-average level, while the underlying population can still be split into people who each receive at most one partner. This is therefore a credible population-continuous formulation, not merely a fractional bundle for named agents. Existing high-multiplicity work supports the regime’s plausibility; my objection is that this particular mirror appears to restate an already studied continuous model.

A broader mirror retaining arbitrary choice-function oracles is less clean. The paper’s Boolean choice functions act on sets of named edges; aggregating them requires an additional rule for how a cohort chooses among fractional masses. Natural structured versions of continuous stable assignment already have their own algorithmic literature, including work on minimum-cost stable assignments ([Karzanov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11556)). Without such extra semantics, that broader version is not yet a well-defined escape from the overlap.

This is still a narrow negative case, and its weak point is the boundary between “stable allocation with divisible quantities” and “a continuum of people grouped by type.” The proponent’s large-market scenario is credible. If the reader does not accept the type-capacity equivalence as making stable allocation an existing continuous-population model, then the anchor survives and I do not see a stronger objection in the supplied material.
