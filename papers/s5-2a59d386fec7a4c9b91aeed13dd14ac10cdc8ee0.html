The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has no complexity theorem, so there is no existing complexity classification to carry over. That is a real limitation, but it does not establish that every computational question grounded in the paper is unworthy. The appended case raises no specific mirror; it argues from that limitation to a universal conclusion.

Result 1 gives a concrete computational question in its bargaining application. Let a continuous society have finite support \(\mathcal T\), with mass \(\mu_t\) of each complete risk type \(t\). A type records its nullification probability \(p_t\), stand-alone worth \(a_t\), and utility \(w_t\). Ask for the per-capita transfer \(\pi_t\) that balances the budget and makes each type’s certainty-equivalent allocation equal its average allocation under risk.

The paper’s proof reduces this to a single scalar \(c\). Define
\[ T_t(x)=w_t\big((1-p_t)a_t-x\big)-(1-p_t)w_t(a_t). \]
The transfer schedule satisfies
\[ \pi_t=T_t^{-1}(c), \qquad \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\mu_t\pi_t=0, \]
so \(c\) is the unique root of
\[ \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\mu_tT_t^{-1}(c)=0. \]
For CARA utilities, for example, a computational version could ask for the schedule to precision \(\varepsilon\), with complexity measured in support size \(\tau\), input bit length \(L\), and \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

This is a population mirror: the input is a distribution over economically complete types, transfers are assigned by type, and identical agents can be aggregated by their masses. It preserves individual risk while replacing the list of people with type frequencies. Result 1 already supplies the uniqueness and monotonicity that make the calculation natural; the paper does not analyze its bit complexity or how the calculation scales with high multiplicity.

That may prove to be a modest algorithmic result, and it is not a continuous version of a complexity theorem in the paper. Still, it is enough to challenge the claim that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario. The case for that claim is strongest if “mirror” means only transferring an already-proved complexity classification; Result 1 supports a meaningful computational question under the programme’s broader aim of charting such questions.
