Mass would represent the fraction of travelers of each type, and a continuous model could assign mass to different coalition and action patterns. That makes a population-level version conceivable, though the paper does not specify a corresponding optimization objective or algorithmic problem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that the paper supplies no named computational result to continuize. Definitions 1–4 and Examples 5–7 introduce its model; the numbered results in Section 4 are simulation measurements. The paper gives no worst-case complexity, correctness, approximation, or parameterized guarantee for a computational problem. Under the requested anchor standard, there is nothing here to hand a prover as the paper’s result.

The commuter-cohort idea is a plausible way to invent a related problem. One could group travelers by route, schedule, budget, preferences, and plan, then ask how to assign type mass to carpools. But the paper studies a decentralized negotiation process: individual agents propose coalition structures, accept or reject them against their own reference utilities, and infer others’ preferences from proposal histories. Its experiments compare heuristics using message counts, runtime, and utility ratios; they do not optimize any of those metrics. Turning the cohort model into a mass-allocation or message-minimization problem would require specifying an objective and aggregate negotiation rules that the paper does not provide. That could be worthwhile follow-on work, but the paper offers no computational claim for it to mirror.

This is a strong objection to the proposed mirror as an extension of this paper’s results. It does not establish the broader claim that no scenario could yield a worthwhile continuous problem: a sufficiently regular commuter population might support one. The negative case is strongest at the boundary between a new high-multiplicity coalition-formation problem and a continuous mirror of this paper; the text gives no basis for treating them as the same contribution.
