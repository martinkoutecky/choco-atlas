The continuous mirror question

Given supported valuation types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), value-query access to normalized nondecreasing valuations \(v_t:\{0,\ldots,M\}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), and per-capita supply \(q\), choose masses \(x_{t,s}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_{s=0}^{M}x_{t,s}=\mu_t\) for each \(t\) and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{s=0}^{M}s x_{t,s}\le q\), maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{s=0}^{M}x_{t,s}v_t(s)\). Does there exist an algorithm that returns a feasible solution with welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/(1+\epsilon)\) in time polynomial in \(\tau=|T|\), \(\log M\), the input bit length \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\)? Each unit of mass receives an integral bundle; the type-level allocation may be fractional.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity market with \(\tau\) complete valuation types, rational masses \(\mu_t\), integral bundle choices \(s\in\{0,\ldots,M\}\), and per-capita supply \(q\). The decision variables \(x_{t,s}\) assign mass of each type to each bundle size; maximize \(\sum_{t,s}x_{t,s}v_t(s)\) subject to type-mass and supply constraints.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the welfare-approximation component of Theorem 3.20. It does not preserve the structured lottery range or establish truthfulness in expectation, and it leaves Theorem 4.1’s restricted-auction separation and communication lower bound aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the mass-allocation problem be approximated in time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(\log M\), \(L\), and \(1/\epsilon\) using value-query access, including an efficient pricing step over bundle sizes?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I believe is for the welfare-allocation problem behind Theorem 3.20, proved in this paper. It says that for \(n\) bidders with normalized, nondecreasing valuations for bundles of \(m\) identical items, there is a truthful-in-expectation FPTAS: it returns an allocation with welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/(1+\epsilon)\), in time polynomial in \(n\), \(\log m\), and \(1/\epsilon\). The valuations are accessed by value queries. The algorithm optimizes over a structured range of lotteries over integral allocations.

A plausible high-multiplicity setting is a large market allocating identical units of cloud capacity or permits. Many firms may share the same complete valuation schedule because they operate under the same contract and business conditions. Let \(T\) be the supported schedules, with \(\tau=|T|\ll n\), and let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of firms of type \(t\). Normalize supply too: if there are \(m\) units for \(n\) firms, set per-capita supply \(q=m/n\). The type is the full valuation function \(v_t:\{0,\ldots,M\}\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), with \(v_t(0)=0\) and \(v_t\) nondecreasing.

I would hand a prover this problem:

Continuous Multi-Unit Welfare (CMUW). Given the supported types and rational masses \(\mu_t\), their value-query oracles, a maximum bundle size \(M\), per-capita supply \(q\), and \(\epsilon>0\), find a sparse vector \(x_{t,s}\ge0\). Here \(x_{t,s}\) is the mass of type \(t\) assigned exactly \(s\) items. Require
\[ \sum_{s=0}^{M}x_{t,s}=\mu_t\quad\text{for every }t, \qquad \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{s=0}^{M}s\,x_{t,s}\le q. \]
The objective is to maximize
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{s=0}^{M}x_{t,s}v_t(s). \]
The question is whether an algorithm can return a feasible \(x\) achieving at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/(1+\epsilon)\) in time polynomial in \(\tau,\log M,L,1/\epsilon\), where \(L\) is the input bit length. A sparse solution can use at most \(\tau+1\) positive entries. Each agent still receives an integral bundle; only the type-level average can be fractional.

This is a direct high-multiplicity relaxation of the paper’s welfare allocation: a discrete profile with \(n_t\) bidders of type \(t\) maps to \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), and any discrete allocation maps to \(x_{t,s}\) by counting how many type-\(t\) bidders receive \(s\) items and dividing by \(n\). Conversely, rational masses in \(x\) can be realized exactly in finite replications whose counts clear the denominators. Allowing arbitrary masses removes the finite granularity while preserving the identical-item welfare objective and integral bundles per recipient.

I expect CMUW to be Class A, though that is a conjecture about the mirror, not a consequence of Theorem 3.20. Its mass-allocation formulation is an LP with bundle choices \(s\) as columns. The question is whether the paper’s breakpoint techniques can control the pricing step over those bundle choices in time polynomial in \(\log M\) and the number of supported types. The paper’s algorithm gives a concrete reason to try: it already uses structured lottery ranges and valuation breakpoints to avoid searching over every bundle size.

The limitation is substantial. Theorem 3.20’s headline includes truthfulness in expectation; CMUW, as stated, asks only for its allocation and welfare optimization core. In a genuinely atomless population, one agent’s report has zero mass, so dominant-strategy truthfulness for individuals is not captured by \(\mu\) alone. A fuller mirror would need a defensible incentive notion—perhaps one obtained from consistent finite replications—and a truthful-in-expectation implementation of the continuous optimizer. Authors would recognize CMUW as the high-multiplicity form of their allocation problem; they might reasonably say it does not mirror the theorem’s full mechanism-design claim.

I would not use Theorem 4.1 as my anchor even though it contains a hardness result: its lower bound is for restricted auctions with exactly two bidders, and the hard parameter is the number of items. It offers no growing population to continuize. My case therefore covers the welfare-allocation component of Theorem 3.20, not the paper’s separation between universally truthful and truthful-in-expectation mechanisms.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that CMUW does not preserve the result that makes Theorem 3.20 distinctive. The theorem gives a truthful-in-expectation mechanism, optimizing over a structured range of lotteries on integral allocations. CMUW drops truthfulness and replaces that range with a linear program over type-level masses. It is a mirror of the welfare problem underneath the theorem, but not of its mechanism-design contribution.

A stronger version would add incentives back in. That is difficult to express using \(\mu\) alone: an individual report has zero mass, so it cannot change the reported society or its aggregate allocation. A finite-replication formulation retains individual deviations, but the paper’s running time is polynomial in the number of bidders, so it does not yield the desired dependence on \(\tau\). This makes the mechanism-design version substantially less direct than CMUW.

That objection still doesn’t defeat the best mirror. In an atomless market, a price computed from the aggregate society can be fixed from an individual’s perspective. An allocation that gives each type bundles maximizing \(v_t(s)-\lambda s\), with payment \(\lambda s\), offers a plausible truthful-in-the-limit interpretation. And the welfare LP remains a legitimate computational question: its pricing step must optimize over bundle sizes using value-query access. Whether that step is efficient is exactly something worth asking.

The proposed market of repeated firms with shared valuation schedules is plausible under the programme’s definition of a complete type. The paper has a named computational theorem; the population can be made large; and the continuous question is not already answered by the discrete FPTAS. I can argue that CMUW weakens the anchor by setting aside truthfulness, but I cannot honestly show that it has no worthwhile continuous mirror. The negative case is weak at this anchor.
