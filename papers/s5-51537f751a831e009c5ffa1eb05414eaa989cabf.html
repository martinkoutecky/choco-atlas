The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that this paper supplies no named computational result to continue. Its numbered items define simulation models; its contribution is comparing those models and recommending one. The proposed range-voting mirror—a distribution \(\mu\) over evaluation vectors \(x\in[0,1]^m\), with winner \(\arg\max_c \int x_c\,d\mu(x)\)—is a sensible population-level question. It is a new rule-evaluation problem, though, rather than a continuous version of a computational result in this paper.

The better version would retain the paper’s probabilistic purpose and ask how often a voting paradox occurs under a continuous population. Conditional on a simulator’s parameters and candidate positions, however, replacing voter draws with their population distribution removes finite-electorate sampling variation. The resulting aggregate is fixed; the simulated frequencies the paper compares become indicators conditional on that distribution. Randomizing candidate positions or simulator parameters can preserve probabilities, but then the target depends on an outer random model rather than on population mass.

Deepest Voting is the strongest foothold for a richer mirror: put a mass distribution on evaluation vectors and find a point of maximum depth. The cited companion paper develops that family and studies its axiomatic properties and paradoxes; it does not chart computational complexity, and it leaves other depth functions for future research. A continuous-mass version could be a worthwhile new algorithmic question, especially for depth choices beyond weighted \(L^p\). That possibility is the honest weakness in a universal negative case. It still does not supply an anchor in this paper’s simulation results. [The companion paper on Deepest Voting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04228)
