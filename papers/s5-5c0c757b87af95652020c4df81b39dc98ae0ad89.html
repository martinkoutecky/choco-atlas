The continuous mirror question

Given a finite state space \(\Omega\), target events \(\mathcal E\), a finite family \(\mathcal A\) of event securities with payoffs \(x_A(\omega)=\mathbf 1[\omega\in A]\), and a rational society \(\mu\) over signal types \(t\), each described by a partition \(\Pi_t\) of \(\Omega\), find a minimum-cardinality subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal A\) whose payoff vector \(X_S=(x_A)_{A\in S}\) is informative. Here \(\Pi_\mu=\bigvee_{t:\mu_t>0}\Pi_t\); distinguishability requires that for every cell \(B\in\Pi_\mu\) and distributions \(P,P'\) supported on \(B\), \(\mathbb E_P[X_S]=\mathbb E_{P'}[X_S]\) imply \(P(E)=P'(E)\) for all \(E\in\mathcal E\). Separability uses the paper's criterion over every type with positive mass: there must be no distribution \(P\) and vector \(v\) for which all those types have conditional payoff expectation \(v\) at every \(P\)-possible state while the pooled conditional expectation differs from \(v\) at some such state.

The model it lives in

A rational distribution \(\mu\) over signal-partition types defines the society; its supported types induce the pooled partition \(\Pi_\mu=\bigvee_{t:\mu_t>0}\Pi_t\). Choose a smallest subset of candidate event securities whose payoffs are separable across the supported types and distinguish the target events within every cell of \(\Pi_\mu\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the NP-hard minimum informative event-security selection result in Theorem 7 and the arbitrary-security extension in Corollary 1. It leaves the aggregation theorems, always-informative security results, and fixed-signal-structure construction outside its scope.

Open questions for a prover

  • Do restricted families of event securities admit tractable algorithms or useful approximation guarantees?
  • Can parameters such as the number of states, target events, or payoff-matrix rank yield fixed-parameter algorithms?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is the paper’s constrained security-design problem. Imagine a large forecasting market whose participants fall into a small number of recurring information-access profiles: for example, many traders share one polling feed, another group shares a regional data source, and a third has access to both. Each profile is a type \(t\), described by the partition \(\Pi_t\) of world states its members can distinguish. The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over these types, with support size \(\tau\) much smaller than the population. The pooled information is the common refinement \(\Pi_\mu=\bigvee_{t:\mu_t>0}\Pi_t\).

The market organizer has a set of candidate securities and events to forecast. The action is to choose a subset of candidate securities; the objective is to minimize the number selected while ensuring that the subset is informative about the target events relative to \(\Pi_\mu\). For a selected set \(S\), let \(X_S(\omega)\) be the vector of its payoffs in state \(\omega\). It distinguishes target events \(\mathcal E\) if, for every pair of distributions \(P,P'\) supported within one cell of \(\Pi_\mu\), equality \(\mathbb E_P[X_S]=\mathbb E_{P'}[X_S]\) implies \(P(E)=P'(E)\) for every \(E\in\mathcal E\). It is separable if there is no distribution \(P\) and vector \(v\) for which every positive-mass type has conditional expected payoff \(v\) at every \(P\)-possible state, while the pooled conditional expectation differs from \(v\) somewhere. As in the paper, informative means both separable and distinguishing. A solution is a smallest subset \(S\) meeting both conditions.

My lead anchor is Theorem 7, proved in this paper’s appendix: the *informative event set* problem is NP-hard. In full, given a signal structure, events of interest, and a predefined collection of event securities—each paying \(1\) when its event occurs and \(0\) otherwise—the task is to find the smallest subcollection that is informative. The paper’s reduction corresponds to Set Cover, even for a single fully informed trader. The continuous mirror is Continuous Informative Event-Set Design: replace the listed traders by the mass distribution \(\mu\) over their signal-partition types, and ask for the minimum informative subset of the available event securities. I expect Class B: the hardness already occurs with one signal type, so it embeds in the continuous population with \(\mu_t=1\). This is the strongest anchor because it retains the paper’s natural binary event securities and its exact design objective.

A second, broader anchor is Corollary 1, stated here as an immediate consequence of Theorem 7: the *informative set* problem is NP-hard when the allowed securities come from a general predefined set, rather than only event securities. Its continuous mirror, Continuous Informative-Security-Set Design, has the same population and target-event inputs, but allows arbitrary candidate payoff vectors on \(\Omega\); the solution is a smallest subset that is separable and distinguishes the target events. It too is Class B, since the event-security instances of Theorem 7 are special cases. This is a genuine enlargement of the design question, though its hardness argument is inherited rather than independent.

This is a faithful mirror of Section 5.4: the candidates, informativeness test, and minimum-cardinality objective remain the paper’s. For rational \(\mu\), clearing denominators gives a finite electorate with the same type proportions; conversely, finite trader counts give such a distribution. The paper’s Definitions 3–4 supply the exact criteria, so the continuous problems can be stated without inventing a new notion of “informative.” I would scope the claim to these two security-selection results. The equilibrium-aggregation theorems and the results about always-informative securities are not computational-complexity anchors.

The main weakness is substantial: the masses \(\mu_t\) affect which signal types are present, but their precise magnitudes do not affect separability or distinguishability. Those properties depend on the support and its join. Thus this is a credible high-multiplicity formulation, but not a case where fractional mass creates a new LP or dissolves discrete hardness. Indeed, Theorem 7’s one-type reduction places the difficulty in the state space and candidate events, not in population multiplicity. That limits the novelty of the continuization while making its boundary informative: the next questions are whether restricted event families admit tractable design, what approximation guarantees are possible, and which parameters—such as the number of states, target events, or payoff-matrix rank—can yield useful algorithms. The paper is about prediction-market design rather than conventional voting, which is another scope caveat, but its core problem is plainly computational design for a population with heterogeneous information.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case rests on a mismatch between a population and the object these results actually use. Theorem 7 and Corollary 1 are two versions of the same security-selection problem: choose the smallest subset of candidate securities that is informative for a given signal structure. That structure is a finite family of partitions of the state space. The probabilities in the definitions are distributions over *states of the world*, not weights on a population of traders.

The proposed measure \(\mu\) over partition types is a plausible description of traders who share information sources. But the paper’s exact informativeness tests do not use those masses. The join depends on which partitions have positive mass; separability repeats the same condition for duplicate partitions; distinguishability quantifies over state distributions within the join’s cells. So changing the proportions while keeping the positive-mass types fixed changes none of the input the security-selection problem uses. Treating the same signal structure as a distribution therefore supplies a population story without making the population continuous in the sense relevant to the result.

The market mechanism sharpens the problem. Traders take turns in a specified sequence and observe previous reports. A continuum of traders does not inherit that schedule or the role of each trader’s turn automatically. A continuous dynamic mirror would need a new participation protocol and an account of how it connects to the paper’s aggregation conditions. If instead one keeps the paper’s static security-selection criterion, the masses disappear as above. Theorem 7’s one-trader construction makes this particularly plain; Corollary 1 inherits the same issue even with arbitrary payoff securities.

A better story involving large groups with shared data feeds does not fix the mismatch: under the paper’s full-informativeness objective, every positive-mass information class matters, while duplicate traders add no new partition. Making mass matter would require a partial-information objective with a choice of error measure. The paper itself leaves partial aggregation for future work, but such a weighted objective would be a new question rather than a mirror of Theorem 7 or Corollary 1.

This is a real objection to the *continuous-population* framing, but it is not airtight. The support-based security-selection problem remains well posed, and a reader could regard expressing its signal structure as \(\mu\) as a useful high-multiplicity formulation even when the masses are redundant. The paper gives little reason to think those proportions are a meaningful computational input, though; that is the strongest case against spending the programme’s effort on these anchors.
