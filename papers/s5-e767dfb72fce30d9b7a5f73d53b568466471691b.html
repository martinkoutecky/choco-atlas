The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The positive case does not supply a qualifying computational anchor. Its closest candidate is Proposition 1, which characterizes when a discursive dilemma can occur. The proposition quantifies over profiles in general; it does not give a computational problem with an input and an algorithmic or complexity result. Propositions 2–3 and Corollaries 1–6 are also analytical, and the normative comparison is based on simulations.

The strongest mirror I can construct is a large panel of forecasters represented by a continuous distribution over complete judgment types. Given a finite support of \(\tau\) types, each with a rational mass, one could ask whether the conclusion-based and premise-based decisions differ. But that computation is just weighted medians: take the median of each premise for PBP; map each supported type through \(D\) and take the median of those decisions for CBP. With \(k\) premises, this takes \(O(k\tau\log\tau+\tau C_D)\) time, where \(C_D\) is the cost of evaluating \(D\). The paper’s characterization already answers the general existence question. This mirror offers no evident complexity landscape for the continuous-optimization tools ChoCo is meant to bring to bear.

One could make the question harder by asking how much mass must be reweighted or moved between types to align the procedures. That might be a worthwhile problem about designing or manipulating a forecasting panel, but it adds an intervention and its costs that the paper does not study. It is inspired by the paper’s topic, rather than a computational version of one of its results.

The paper’s expected-loss comparison does not provide a stronger anchor. It evaluates losses across finite committee draws under specified judgment-error assumptions. A fixed continuous population makes the aggregate medians deterministic; retaining a nontrivial expectation requires adding a distribution over the underlying truth or sampling process. That could support a new model, but it would supply a new computational objective rather than continuize a computational result in this paper. The potential large-panel setting is imaginable, so the negative case is not a proof that no useful adjacent research could ever be built.
