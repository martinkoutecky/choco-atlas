The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent has raised no continuous computational anchor, and that is the strongest case against a ChoCo mirror. Theorem 1 characterizes safe deviations from DA; Theorems 2 and 3 characterize transparency and efficient safe deviations; Propositions 1–3 give further structural conditions. None classifies an algorithmic problem by complexity or gives an algorithmic bound. A continuous version of one of these characterizations could be an interesting mechanism-design question, but it would not mirror a computational result in this paper.

The strongest candidate is a large school-assignment market with cohort masses and capacities scaled with the population. That scaling is a sensible co-modification. But the paper’s matching and transparency claims depend on preferences and priorities: its types would need to include both. Since each agent has a distinct score at every object, no two agents have the same complete type. Grouping them by preferences alone discards priority information that determines blocking and what an observer can verify. Cloning cohort members with identical scores creates priority ties; breaking those ties restores individual distinctions.

A continuum with priority quantiles and pointwise assignments could preserve individual observations, but it would require a new model of stable assignment and safe deviations over a continuum of priority positions. A mass-allocation model could instead track the fraction of each cohort assigned to each school, but that replaces the paper’s observation of an agent’s own matching with an aggregate observation. That may be a worthwhile extension if the authors recognize it as their question; it is not supplied by their results. In either version, the work would be to formulate and characterize a new mechanism, not to map the paper’s computational landscape.

Theorem 2 is the strongest structural candidate for that extension, and Theorem 1 is another. Yet a continuum analogue of “transparent exactly when sequentially dictatorial” or “safe exactly when stable” would still be a structural characterization. Turning either into a computational project would require adding a new encoded input and a decision or optimization task—such as searching for a safe deviation under a specified objective. The paper defines no such task.

So I find no worthwhile computational mirror for this paper under ChoCo’s scope. This does not show that no continuous matching theory could be valuable; it shows that the plausible population models lead to new mechanism-design questions, while the paper itself contributes structural transparency results rather than computational ones.
