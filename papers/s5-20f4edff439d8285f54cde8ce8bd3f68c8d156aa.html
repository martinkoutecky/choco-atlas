The continuous mirror question

Given \(C\), \(k\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) approval types, compute a partial committee \(P\subseteq C\), with \(|P|\le k\), such that every size-\(k\) completion \(W\supseteq P\) has no type-weighted fractional \(\mathrm{FJR}+\) violation, where \(Z=\sum_t\mu_t z_t\), \(Y=\sum_c y_c\), and a Hare violation requires \(Z\ge Y/k\); likewise, require no Droop violation with \(Z>Y/(k+1)\).

The model it lives in

Use approval sets \(A_t\) as types with rational masses \(\mu_t\). Maintain per-member budgets \(b_t\), select candidates using the mass-weighted score \(\sum_{t:c\in A_t,\ u_t(P)<h}\mu_t b_t/(h-u_t(P))\), and collect per-member payments \(p_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t p_t=1/k\) (or \(1/(k+1)\) for Droop). The fractional witness uses the paper’s typewise constraints, with \(Z=\sum_t\mu_t z_t\) and \(Y=\sum_c y_c\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the Hare and Droop completion guarantees of Theorem 3.3 and the mass-weighted verification LPs of Theorem 3.2. It leaves the priceable-completion and sub-core results, and the participatory-budgeting extensions, unclaimed.

Open questions for a prover

  • Prove exact polynomial-time implementation of mass-weighted RBG for binary-encoded rational masses, with running time polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\).
  • Formalize the equivalence between the type-weighted fractional verification constraints and finite elections with repeated approval types.

The case FOR (proponent)

A credible mirror is an approval election for a very large membership organization choosing a fixed-size board. Let \(C\) be the candidates and \(k\) the committee size. A type is an approval ballot \(A_t\subseteq C\): everyone of that type has the same approvals and therefore the same utility for every committee. The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over the \(\tau\) ballot types, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). In a large association with recurring caucuses or endorsement patterns, the number \(n\) of members could be far larger than \(\tau\). Each \(\mu_t\) is a population share, so the Hare quota \(n/k\) becomes a mass threshold of \(1/k\).

The outcome remains an ordinary committee \(W\) of \(k\) candidates. The population—not the committee or its outcomes—is what becomes continuous. For this mirror, define \(u_t(W)=|A_t\cap W|\). A continuous fractional violation at level \(\ell\) uses nonnegative variables \(z_t,x_{tc},y_c\) satisfying the paper’s conditions type by type: \(z_t=0\) if \(u_t(W)\ge\ell\); \(x_{tc}=0\) unless \(c\in A_t\); \(\sum_c x_{tc}=\ell z_t\); \(x_{tc}\le y_c\); and \(x_{tw}\le z_t\) for each \(w\in W\). Here \(z_t\) and \(x_{tc}\) are per-member weights for type \(t\), while the total underrepresented mass is \(Z=\sum_t\mu_tz_t\); let \(Y=\sum_c y_c\). A Hare violation has \(Z>0\) and \(Z\ge Y/k\). A Droop violation has \(Z>0\) and \(Z>Y/(k+1)\). Thus the paper’s fractional comparison test is retained, but its electorate is represented by masses rather than named voters. The approval ballot is a complete type for these questions because all the utilities and eligibility tests used here depend on approvals.

Lead — Continuous RBG Completion. The anchor is Theorem 3.3, proved in this paper. In full, it says that Residual-Budget Greedy returns a set \(P\) such that, at price \(q_H=n/k\), every size-\(k\) committee containing \(P\) satisfies FJR+; at price \(q_D=n/(k+1)\), every such completion satisfies Droop-FJR+, equivalently, no level has a fractional violation with \(Z>q_DY\). Both versions run in polynomial time, and the guarantees hold for every candidate and payment choice allowed by the rule.

The corresponding continuous problem is: given \(C,k,\mu\), find a partial committee \(P\), with \(|P|\le k\), such that every size-\(k\) completion satisfies continuous FJR+ (or, in the Droop version, continuous Droop-FJR+). A type-aggregated RBG uses per-member budgets \(b_t\), initially \(1\). At level \(h\), its score for candidate \(c\) is
\[ \operatorname{score}^{\mu}_h(c;P,b) =\sum_{\substack{t:\ c\in A_t\\u_t(P)<h}} \mu_t\frac{b_t}{h-u_t(P)}. \]
It may select \(c\) when this score reaches the price \(\rho\), then choose per-member payments \(p_t\) within the rule’s eligibility and caps, with \(\sum_t\mu_t p_t=\rho\), and update the budgets. The continuous task’s solution is \(P\); any size-\(k\) completion is then a valid committee. The expected direction is tractable: the paper’s weighted potential argument carries over with sums over voters replaced by \(\mu\)-weighted sums. Runtimes should be polynomial in \(m,\tau,k,L\), where \(L\) is the bit length of the masses. This is my strongest anchor because it gives a robust partial outcome: subsequent choices can vary without losing the representation guarantee.

Continuous FJR+ / Droop-FJR+ Verification is a second, distinct anchor. Theorem 3.2, also proved here, says that given a finite election and a committee \(W\), both FJR+ and Droop-FJR+ can be verified in polynomial time. Its continuous mirror takes \(C,k,\mu,W\) as input and asks whether \(W\) has either kind of mass-weighted fractional violation. For each level \(\ell\), normalize \(Y=1\) and maximize \(Z\) subject to the type-level constraints above. A Hare violation exists if the optimum is at least \(1/k\); a Droop violation exists if it is greater than \(1/(k+1)\). These are polynomial-size rational LPs in \(m,\tau,L\), so I expect this mirror to be tractable as well.

The paper does not give a numbered computational hardness result that I can use as an anchor. It cites coNP-completeness for verifying ordinary FJR, but that is an external result and concerns a different axiom; I would not present it as a result proved or numbered here. The two anchors above therefore make a positive case for continuous computation, but they do not test whether population continuization dissolves a discrete hardness barrier.

This is still a sensible problem for the authors’ model. Their quota is already population-relative, their paper motivates proportional representation in large groups, and they show FJR+ agrees with lower quota on party-list elections. There is also a clean high-multiplicity bridge: any rational \(\mu\) can be expanded into a finite electorate by clearing denominators, and any finite electorate maps to \(\mu_t=n_t/n\). The type-level constraints aggregate the original voter constraints, while the RBG price scales from \(n/k\) to \(1/k\). With masses encoded in binary, the continuous input can represent a very large electorate succinctly.

The weakest point is that the regime must be argued for a particular election setting. Approval ballots can be numerous, so \(\tau\) may be large; this case is persuasive for large organizations with repeated ballot patterns, not for every approval election. And these anchors are already tractable in the paper’s finite model. Their continuous mirrors mainly give a type-compressed, mass-based version of those results, rather than a new answer about whether hardness disappears. I would keep the scope to FJR+ verification and the Hare/Droop RBG completion guarantee. Whether priceable completion and the sub-core carry over would be a useful next question, but I would not claim those results here.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a continuous mirror is that both anchors look like high-multiplicity reformulations of results the paper already proves. In the proposed setting, the outcome is still a size-\(k\) committee, representation levels are still integers, and the electorate is summarized by at most \(2^m\) approval types. Clearing denominators in rational type masses gives a finite election; conversely, every finite election gives such a distribution. Since these results depend on voters through additive sums, grouping identical ballots preserves the verification constraints and the RBG calculations. The likely gain is an algorithm polynomial in the number of supported types and the bit length of their masses, rather than in an explicitly listed electorate.

That makes Theorem 3.2’s mirror especially direct. For each level, the verification LP can use one block of variables per supported type, with total underrepresented weight \(Z=\sum_t\mu_t z_t\). The theorem’s normalization and threshold comparison carry over. Theorem 3.3 is similarly amenable to aggregation: use mass-weighted scores and payments, and its potential argument becomes a sum over types. Neither mirror seems to require new continuous-optimization machinery or to expose a difficulty the finite analysis could not express.

This objection limits the likely novelty, but it does not defeat either anchor. A binary encoding of huge multiplicities can make a type-polynomial algorithm materially useful even when the explicit-voter version is already polynomial. More fundamentally, the programme counts high-multiplicity formulations as part of its target, and treats an exact discrete-to-continuous relationship as a result worth establishing. So “the proof probably transfers” is not enough to dismiss the research question.

The other plausible objections also fail here. Approval ballots are complete types for these results: the axioms and algorithm use approvals and resulting utilities, not voter identities. Large organizations with recurring endorsement patterns provide a credible regime with many members and relatively few types. The quota remains \(1/k\) of the population, so the relevant group threshold does not vanish as the population grows. The paper’s fractional violation variables do not already make the society continuous; they are witnesses evaluated on a finite electorate. And the cited lottery and axiomatic work does not supply the missing continuous-population computational treatment.

I cannot honestly defeat the proponent’s best versions of either anchor. The case against is that these may be short, predictable weighted extensions whose contribution is mainly succinct high-multiplicity input handling. That supports a judgment of modest novelty or priority, but it does not support the requested universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. Both mirrors survive the authors-recognition test, and the negative case is weak.
