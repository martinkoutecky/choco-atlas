The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita supplies \(q_j\), complete diminishing-marginal utility laws \(u_{t,j,\ell}\), caps \(c_t\), and \(\varepsilon\), compute a finite-support feasible mass allocation \(\lambda_{t,b}\) over integral bundles \(b\) achieving the paper's NSW approximation \( (1+\varepsilon)e^{e^{-1/(1+4\varepsilon)}} \) and satisfying, for every uncapped supported \((t,a)\) and supported \((s,b)\), \(u_t(b-e_j)\le(2+4\varepsilon)u_t(a)\) for some \(j\) with \(b_j>0\).

The model it lives in

A configuration optimization model with type masses \(\mu_t\), variables \(\lambda_{t,b}\ge0\), constraints \(\sum_b\lambda_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}b_j\lambda_{t,b}=q_j\), integral individual bundles \(b\), and objective \(\operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\lambda)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{t,b}\lambda_{t,b}\log\min\{c_t,u_t(b)\}\right)\), with EF1 checked on every positive-mass atom pair.

The objection that survived

The theorem's proof uses globally indexed marginal utilities and per-agent shortest paths, so a clean compressed model needs a fixed marginal-utility law or bounded useful-bundle regime; the paper does not provide that aggregation.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers multi-copy capped allocation, population Nash welfare, and the atom-wise EF1 guarantee of Theorem 10. It leaves the explicit iteration bound of Theorem 15, the lower-bound construction, the large-market theorem, and the CG/BMV equivalence untouched.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the configuration problem be solved or approximated in time polynomial in \( |T| \), the number of goods, and the encoding length, independently of the expanded population?
  • Under fixed marginal-utility laws or bounded useful bundle sizes, does the paper's price-adjustment method admit a type-level aggregation?
  • Does the unrestricted configuration problem retain discrete hardness, or is there a continuum-specific hardness boundary?

The case FOR (proponent)

There is a credible continuous mirror, although I would not promise that it is tractable in the compressed representation. My lead mirror is the high-multiplicity version of the paper’s multi-copy, capped Nash-social-welfare allocation problem.

Let \(T\) be a finite set of complete agent types. A type \(t\) specifies the entire diminishing-marginal utility profile \(u_{t,j,\ell}\) for every good \(j\), together with its cap \(c_t\). The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\). Goods remain indivisible for each individual: an individual bundle is an integer vector \(b=(b_1,\ldots,b_m)\), with \(0\le b_j\le K_j\), and utility \(u_t(b)=\sum_j\sum_{\ell=1}^{b_j}u_{t,j,\ell}\).

What becomes continuous is only the assignment of whole bundles to population mass. Let \(\lambda_{t,b}\) be the mass of type-\(t\) agents receiving bundle \(b\). Thus

\[ \sum_b\lambda_{t,b}=\mu_t \]

and, with \(q_j\) denoting the per-capita supply of good \(j\),

\[ \sum_{t,b}b_j\lambda_{t,b}=q_j. \]

The objective is the population Nash welfare

\[ \operatorname{NSW}_\infty(\lambda) = \exp\!\left( \sum_{t,b}\lambda_{t,b} \log\bigl(\min\{c_t,u_t(b)\}\bigr) \right), \]

with \(\log 0=-\infty\). Thus \(\lambda\) may split a type across several bundles, but no individual receives a fractional item. If all masses and supplies have denominator \(N\), multiplying by \(N\) gives exactly a finite instance with \(N\) agents and \(Nq_j\) copies of good \(j\). Conversely, every finite election-like instance with repeated utility profiles normalizes to such a distribution.

A plausible regime is a large public or employer-sponsored allocation programme: millions of people, perhaps \(N=10^6\), receive indivisible training places, childcare slots, devices, or aid packages. There may be only \(\tau=20\)–\(100\) standardized need or preference profiles, and the supply of each good scales with \(N\). The diminishing values and caps are natural for repeated units of the same service. The type description includes every relevant attribute, so this is genuine high multiplicity rather than an assumption that unlike people have been merged.

My strongest anchor is Theorem 10, proved in the paper. In the manuscript’s parameterization it states that the allocation produced by the algorithm satisfies

\[ \min_{j\in x_k}u_i(x_k-j) \le (2+4\varepsilon)u_i(x_i) \]

for every agent \(k\) and every uncapped agent \(i\). The published version numbers this result Theorem 10 and writes the bound as \(2+\varepsilon\) under a different parameter convention. The result is proved here, not cited. [Published paper and numbering](https://d-nb.info/1365950646/34)

The corresponding continuous problem is:

Continuous Multi-Copy Capped NSW-EF1. Given \((T,\mu,(u_{t,j,\ell}), (c_t),q,\varepsilon)\), output a finite-support mass allocation \(\lambda\) whose population Nash welfare is within the paper’s approximation factor of the optimum and such that, for every supported bundle \(a\) of every uncapped type-\(t\) population and every supported bundle \(b\) of every type \(s\), there is a good \(j\) with \(b_j>0\) satisfying

\[ u_t(b-e_j)\le (2+4\varepsilon)u_t(a). \]

Here \(e_j\) removes one copy of good \(j\). This is not merely “fractional fair division”: the indivisibility survives inside each bundle, while only the mass of agents receiving each bundle is fractional.

I expect the unrestricted version to be Class B, because a finite instance embeds by taking one type per agent and assigning each type mass \(1/n\). The interesting genuinely high-multiplicity subproblem, with \(\tau\ll N\), is less clear. Its exact formulation is an LP over exponentially many integral bundle configurations:

\[ \max_\lambda \sum_{t,b}\lambda_{t,b}\log\bigl(\min\{c_t,u_t(b)\}\bigr). \]

The pricing problem is to optimize over integral bundles \(b\), and with arbitrary marginal utilities it resembles a knapsack or covering problem. Thus population multiplicity may disappear while bundle combinatorics remain. I would expect tractability for restricted cases—single-copy goods, bounded bundle sizes, or structured utility levels—but possible continuum-specific hardness for the full compressed problem. That boundary is precisely a worthwhile ChoCo question.

A second, narrower anchor is Theorem 15, also proved here. It states that the paper’s algorithm terminates after at most

\[ n^3M^2\log_r(MU) \]

iterations, where \(M=\sum_j k_j\), \(r=1+\varepsilon\), and \(U\) is the ratio between the largest and smallest nonzero utility.

Its continuous counterpart is the following certificate-construction problem:

Compressed Price-EF1 Construction. Given the same type distribution and per-capita supplies, output a finite-support allocation together with prices \(p_j\) and, for every supported type–bundle pair \((t,b)\), a multiplier \(\alpha\) satisfying

\[ \frac{u_{t,j,b_j+1}}{p_j} \le \alpha \le \frac{u_{t,j,b_j}}{p_j} \]

for every good \(j\), together with the population analogue of price-EF1: for every uncapped supported atom \((t,a,\alpha)\) and every supported atom \((s,b,\beta)\), some \(j\) with \(b_j>0\) satisfies

\[ \frac{u_s(b-e_j)}{\beta} \le (1+\varepsilon)\frac{u_t(a)}{\alpha}. \]

The question is whether this can be computed in time polynomial in the number of types, goods, utility bit length, and the encoding lengths of \(\mu\) and \(q\), independently of the expanded population size \(N\). I would expect an A/C split: Class A when a polynomial demand oracle exists, but possible Class C behaviour for arbitrary multi-copy concave utilities because finding a best integral bundle at given prices can itself encode a knapsack-like problem.

The paper’s authors should recognize both mirrors. The utilities, caps, indivisible bundles, diminishing returns, Nash objective, and EF1 notion are unchanged. Only named agents with identical complete descriptions are replaced by population mass, and supplies are expressed per capita. The paper’s fractional auxiliary allocation does not create a novelty collision: it is a proof and certification device, whereas the proposed model keeps every individual allocation integral.

The weakest point is that the paper never proves that its price-adjustment algorithm can be aggregated over identical types. Its proof repeatedly chooses a particular least-spending agent, tracks that agent’s bundle, and assigns it an individual MBB multiplier. A single type may be split across many bundles and multipliers in the continuous model. Therefore I would present this as a precise, substantial mirror and an open computational question—not as a claimed theorem transfer. The paper’s cited NP-completeness and APX-hardness statements are not numbered results proved in this paper, so I would not use them as anchors; the two anchors above are the paper’s own proved computational results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the proponent has not actually mirrored Theorem 15. Its running-time theorem concerns an explicitly represented allocation of named agents and physical items. The proof charges progress to individual agents, individual copies, and shortest alternating paths. Replacing those by type masses does not aggregate the algorithm: one type may occupy many different bundles, each with a different spending level and multiplier. The proposed “compressed price-EF1 construction” is therefore a new configuration-allocation problem, not the continuous form of the theorem. It also omits the auxiliary Nash-welfare upper-bound certificate used in Section 4, so price-EF1 alone is not the paper’s certificate.

There is a related modelling difficulty with the proposed high-multiplicity instance. In the paper, \(u_{i,j,\ell}\) is indexed by \(\ell\) up to the globally available number \(k_j\). If \(k_j\) is held fixed while the population grows, per-capita supply tends to zero and population Nash welfare degenerates. If \(k_j\) scales with population, each type carries a utility description whose length also scales with population. A genuinely compressed continuum model therefore needs an additional assumption—such as a fixed per-capita supply and a fixed marginal-utility law or bounded useful bundle size. That is a defensible new model, but it is not forced by the paper.

This does not defeat the first anchor. Theorem 10 has a perfectly coherent high-multiplicity version. With positive-mass type–bundle atoms, its condition

\[ u_t(b-e_j)\le (2+4\varepsilon)u_t(a) \]

is exactly the individual EF1 guarantee applied to every agent represented by those atoms. It is not vacuous, and the fact that rational masses can be multiplied into a finite repeated instance is expected high-multiplicity structure, not a collision. A stronger formulation using the full configuration LP and atom-wise EF1 would be a legitimate ChoCo question.

Thus the negative case can disqualify the claimed direct mirror of Theorem 15, but it cannot honestly establish the universal conclusion. The public-allocation scenario is natural, agents can have complete repeated types, indivisibility survives inside bundles, and Theorem 10 supplies a genuine continuous-population question. The case against is therefore weak: one anchor is poorly matched, while the other survives as a worthwhile high-multiplicity mirror.
