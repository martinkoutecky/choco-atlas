The continuous mirror question

Given a finite goods set \(O\), a finite utility-type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), per-capita supplies \(q_o\), and additive utilities \(u_t\), decide whether nonnegative masses \(x_{t,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\subseteq O\) satisfy \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,B:o\in B}x_{t,B}=q_o\), while no two positive-mass bundles \(B,D\) have an assigned type \(t\) with \(x_{t,B}>0\), \(u_t(D)>u_t(B)\), and \(u_r(D)>u_r(B)\) for every active type \(r\) with \(\mu_r>0\).

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity configuration-allocation model in which types \(t\) carry mass \(\mu_t\), each infinitesimal agent receives an integral bundle \(B\), \(x_{t,B}\) is divisible assignment mass, and \(q_o\) is per-capita supply; utilities are \(u_t(B)=\sum_{o\in B}u_t(o)\).

The objection that survived

Exact unanimity is sensitive to vanishingly small dissenting type masses, so the hardness mirror needs a fixed active type space or an explicit mass-quota convention; this limits its stability but does not make the question unrecognizable.

What the mirror covers

The mirrors cover Corollary 2's unanimous-envy avoidance and Proposition 9's House Allocation optimization, while leaving the hierarchy results, cycle arguments, MIP formulation, probability bounds, and experiments untreated.

Open questions for a prover

  • What is the complexity of Continuous Unanimity-Avoidance when \(O\) and \(T\) vary?
  • When does the configuration formulation admit a polynomial pricing or separation algorithm?
  • For the house-allocation mirror, what finite-population rounding guarantees preserve approval mass within additive error \(O(1/n)\)?
  • How should exact unanimity be replaced by a mass quota \(\alpha\) to obtain stable limits as type masses approach zero?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a two-branch mirror built around repeated utility types. Its lead is the paper’s hardness result, Corollary 2; Proposition 9 supplies a cleaner tractable special case.

The common regime is a large population of students or households, with \(n\gg \tau\) and only \(\tau\) distinct complete utility types. A type \(t\) is the entire additive utility vector \(u_t\) over the available goods, so agents of the same type are interchangeable. Population mass is \(\mu_t\). Because an atomless population cannot share a single indivisible item, the natural co-modification is a per-capita supply \(q_o\) for each good category \(o\). Each individual still receives an integral bundle \(B\subseteq O\); only the mass of agents receiving each bundle is divisible.

A feasible continuous allocation is therefore a collection \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\), where \(x_{t,B}\) is the mass of type \(t\) receiving integral bundle \(B\), satisfying \( \sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t \) and \( \sum_{t,B:o\in B}x_{t,B}=q_o \). Utilities remain \(u_t(B)=\sum_{o\in B}u_t(o)\). Thus this is genuinely population continuization, not fractionalizing an individual’s bundle.

My lead problem is Continuous Unanimity-Avoidance. Given \(O,T,\mu,q\), and additive utilities \(u_t\), decide whether there exists a feasible \(x\) such that there are no two positive-mass bundles \(B,D\) with the following property: some type \(t\) receiving \(B\) strictly prefers \(D\) to \(B\), and every active type \(r\) with \(\mu_r>0\) also strictly prefers \(D\) to \(B\). In other words, no envy relation is unanimously approved by the population. A solution is the mass assignment \(x\), not merely a yes/no certificate.

This is the direct continuous analogue of the paper’s Corollary 2. The printed statement is: “Deciding whether an allocation exhibits unanimous envy is NP-Complete.” The surrounding discussion makes the intended decision problem clearer: for an add-MARA instance with additive rational utilities, decide whether an allocation avoiding unanimous envy exists, equivalently whether the instance admits a \(K\)-approval-envy-free allocation for some \(K\). Since \(K=n\) is the weakest approval requirement, this is the same as asking whether unanimous envy can be avoided. Corollary 2 is proved in the paper, using Proposition 5 and the NP-membership argument.

Proposition 5 states that when all agents have identical preferences, \((1\)-app envy)-freeness and \((n\)-app envy)-freeness coincide. Hence, on identical-preference instances, avoiding unanimous envy is exactly ordinary envy-freeness. The paper uses the known hardness of envy-free allocation to obtain Corollary 2. The continuous mirror preserves precisely this consensus-based envy question; it does not replace it with utilitarian welfare or a fractional allocation criterion.

I expect Continuous Unanimity-Avoidance to remain hard, with hardness transferring from the goods and bundle structure rather than from population multiplicity. For example, take a PARTITION instance with item weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\) summing to \(2W\). Use one utility type, set \(u(o_j)=w_j\), and give every item category per-capita supply \(q_{o_j}=1/2\). Since everyone has the same preferences, avoiding unanimous envy requires every bundle receiving positive mass to have the same utility. Its expected utility is \(W\), so a feasible solution exists exactly when some subset of items has total weight \(W\). A partition gives a solution by assigning mass \(1/2\) to each complementary bundle, and any solution contains a bundle of weight \(W\).

Thus the continuous population does not erase the discrete obstruction: each individual still receives an integral bundle, and the hard combinatorics live in the item set. A finite realization uses many identical agents and \(N/2\) copies of each item category, so this is a genuine high-multiplicity regime. The expected classification is therefore Class B, although the exact complexity of the general configuration problem remains a worthwhile question.

The supporting anchor is Proposition 9. It states, proved in the paper, that for every House Allocation Problem instance—where the number of agents equals the number of objects and each agent receives exactly one object—one can find an optimal \((K\)-approval envy)-free allocation in \(O(n^3\log n)\) time. The algorithm computes, for every agent-object pair, the maximum approval count generated by objects that agent prefers, builds a bipartite graph for a candidate \(K\), tests for a perfect matching, and uses dichotomous search over \(K\).

Its continuous mirror is Continuous Approval-Threshold House Allocation. The input consists of a type distribution \(\mu\), house categories \(O\) with per-capita supplies \(\rho_o\) satisfying \(\sum_o\rho_o=1\), and utilities \(u_t(o)\). A feasible solution is \(x_{t,o}\ge0\) with \( \sum_o x_{t,o}=\mu_t \) and \( \sum_t x_{t,o}=\rho_o \); every unit of population still receives exactly one integral house.

For two house categories \(o,o'\), define their approval mass by \(a(o,o')=\sum_{r:u_r(o')>u_r(o)}\mu_r\). If type \(t\) receives \(o\), its continuous approval-envy level is \(h(t,o)=\max_{o':u_t(o')>u_t(o)}a(o,o')\), with value \(0\) when \(o\) is top-ranked. The problem asks for a feasible \(x\) minimizing \( \max\{h(t,o):x_{t,o}>0\} \). This is exactly the normalized high-multiplicity version of minimizing \(K\).

I expect this problem to be Class A. For a candidate threshold \(\alpha\), simply forbid assignments \(t\to o\) with \(h(t,o)>\alpha\), then test whether the remaining type-house transportation instance is feasible. There are only finitely many candidate values \(a(o,o')\), and each feasibility test is a flow problem. The result is a polynomial algorithm in \(m,\tau\), and the encoding length of the rational masses and utilities. The paper’s matching argument survives as a type-level flow argument, while individual houses remain indivisible.

The authors should recognize both mirrors as their problems. The first retains their central notion—an agent’s subjective envy filtered through the population’s agreement—and asks whether unanimous envy can be avoided in a large repeated society. The second is their exact House Allocation problem in a natural setting such as thousands of students assigned to repeated course seats or households assigned to many equivalent housing units.

The weakest point is the inventory scaling. The original paper has a finite set of named objects, whereas a genuine atomless population requires repeated object categories or per-capita supplies. That changes the finite instance, although it is the natural co-modification forced by preserving indivisible individual bundles. There is also a wording/complement ambiguity in Corollary 2, so I would not claim that the paper itself proves the continuous hardness result. The honest claim is narrower: its named NP-complete envy result motivates a precise continuous problem, and the one-type partition restriction shows that the resulting mirror is not merely a tractable restatement.

Further questions are whether general Continuous Unanimity-Avoidance admits a configuration-LP characterization, when its bundle-pricing problem is tractable, and how its optimum approximates finite elections with \( \mu_t=n_t/n \). For House Allocation, one can also ask whether the flow solution can be rounded with bounded loss in approval mass for a finite population.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is that the lead citation is not cleanly stated. Corollary 2 says that “deciding whether an allocation exhibits unanimous envy” is NP-complete, but if an allocation is given, that property is directly checkable. The surrounding text instead concerns whether an instance admits an allocation avoiding unanimous envy; the definition of a “unanimous envy instance” is the complement of that existence question. Thus the paper appears to conflate three different problems: checking a fixed allocation, finding an allocation avoiding unanimous envy, and deciding that no such allocation exists.

That weakens the citation, but it does not defeat the repaired mirror. With one utility type, population mass \(1\), and per-capita supply \(\rho_o=1/2\) for every item, each individual can still receive an integral bundle while bundle assignments have divisible mass. Avoiding unanimous envy then requires every positive-mass bundle to have the same utility. If the item weights sum to \(2W\), the average utility is \(W\), so a feasible allocation exists exactly when some subset has weight \(W\): assign mass \(1/2\) to that subset and \(1/2\) to its complement. A finite realization has \(N/2\) copies of every item and \(N\) identical agents.

That is a genuine high-multiplicity population model, not fractionalizing an individual’s bundle. The fact that the obstruction lies in the goods rather than in population multiplicity is not a valid objection under the programme: it is precisely a Class B outcome. The discontinuity of exact unanimity—one vanishingly small dissenting type can matter finitely but disappear in the limit—is a real caveat, but it can be handled by fixing the active type space or using a mass quota. It does not make the repaired question meaningless.

Proposition 9 is even harder to defeat. If houses remain individually named and available only once, there is no useful high-multiplicity regime: a fixed inventory cannot serve an atomless population. But the natural repeated-category version is exactly the one proposed. With type masses \(\mu_t\), house-category supplies \(\rho_o\), and integral assignment for each individual, define

\[ a(o,o')=\sum_{r:u_r(o')>u_r(o)}\mu_r \]

and

\[ h(t,o)=\max_{o':u_t(o')>u_t(o)}a(o,o'). \]

For a threshold \(\alpha\), retain the edge \(t\to o\) precisely when \(h(t,o)\le\alpha\). Feasibility is then the transportation problem

\[ \sum_o x_{t,o}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_t x_{t,o}=\rho_o,\qquad x_{t,o}\ge0. \]

This is a legitimate continuous-population analogue of the paper’s matching theorem. It preserves integral houses per individual; only the mass of identical assignments is divisible. The resulting flow algorithm is routine, but routine is not disqualifying, especially when it gives the desired high-multiplicity transfer and a natural rounding question.

So the honest negative case is weak. I can challenge the wording and quantifiers of Corollary 2, and I can warn that exact unanimity is limit-sensitive. I cannot honestly show that either repaired mirror is nonsensical or unworthy of the programme. Both anchors survive once the obvious modelling defects are corrected.
