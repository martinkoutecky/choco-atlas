The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent is right about the only result they raised: Theorem 5 is not a computational-complexity result. It characterizes implementability in a two-bidder auction using RIC and closure; it gives no algorithm, running time, or complexity classification. The paper’s other numbered results are likewise about implementation and incentive conditions. So none supplies an eligible anchor for a continuous computational mirror.

The strongest proposed rescue is to read Theorem 5 as asking whether an SCS is implementable, then ask how hard it is to decide that question. But the paper does not specify a finite input representation for the SCS or a decision algorithm. Its conditions range over continuous valuation types, social choice functions, and deceptions, and its implementation theorem permits abstract mechanisms. Supplying a finite encoding and studying the resulting decision problem could be worthwhile, but that would define a new computational problem; it would not mirror a computational result in this paper.

Nor does the auction’s continuum of valuations supply the missing population continuum. The bidders are still two named strategic agents. A bidder’s valuation is a private type, and the distribution over valuations describes uncertainty about that bidder’s opponent. Reinterpreting that distribution as population mass changes the model: rationalizability here is defined through each agent’s beliefs and best responses, while a continuum population would require a different strategic and allocation structure. With one indivisible good, a literal continuum limit also makes any one agent’s allocation share vanish. Scaling supply to avoid that would be a defensible co-modification, but it would create a new market model, not a continuous version of Theorem 5’s result.

Theorem 1’s RIC necessity result and Theorem 4’s sufficiency construction do not furnish stronger anchors: they are structural claims, with no computational task or complexity bound to continue. A continuous-population study of implementation theory might still be interesting, but the paper gives no computational result to carry into ChoCo’s programme.
