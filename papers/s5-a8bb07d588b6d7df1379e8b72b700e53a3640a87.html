The case FOR (proponent)

This paper has no qualifying named computational result to anchor a ChoCo mirror. Theorem 1, proved here, gives sufficient conditions under which a generalized partition mechanism remains strategy-proof with budget-constrained bidders. Theorem 2, also proved here, says the modified VCG mechanism is strategy-proof for such bidders. Neither classifies a computational problem as tractable or hard. The social-surplus calculations and experiments do not supply a named complexity result either.

A plausible population setting would be many towns sharing health-screening vehicles, with type \(\theta\) giving a town’s full gross-utility function and budget, and \(\mu_\theta\) the fraction of towns of that type. One could ask for a mass-based redistribution mechanism that is strategy-proof, individually rational, and strongly budget-balanced. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raises no qualifying anchor: they explicitly concede that Theorems 1 and 2 concern strategy-proofness, not the complexity of a computational problem. The town example makes a continuous population plausible, but it does not supply the missing result. A question about whether a mass-based mechanism remains strategy-proof mirrors an incentive guarantee; it does not mirror a tractability, hardness, approximation, or parameterized-complexity result. The surplus comparisons likewise do not establish one.

There is also a real modeling gap in the proposed mirror. The paper’s strategy-proofness claim concerns a bidder’s unilateral deviation. In a mass-transfer model, shifting a positive amount of a type’s mass is a collective change, not one bidder’s deviation. If instead a bidder is infinitesimal, changing their report does not change aggregate prices or transfers. One can design a richer continuum mechanism with individual-level allocation and payment rules, but that is a new mechanism-design question, not a direct continuation of the paper’s partition mechanisms. The finite exclusions and sequential allocations in those mechanisms also need substantial reinterpretation in a nonatomic population.

This is enough to reject the town proposal as a ChoCo anchor. It is not enough to prove that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario. The honest negative case is strongest under the programme’s stated standard: this paper supplies no named computational result to mirror. A new computational study of evaluating or optimizing these mechanisms for a continuous population might still be worthwhile, but the paper does not establish that question.
