The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s one point is the strongest case against a mirror: the paper has no named computational result to continuize. Gale–Shapley’s existence theorem is background. The paper’s own findings are Monte Carlo estimates of how disruption statistics vary with \(n\) and the correlation parameter \(\omega\); it proves no complexity or algorithmic claim. The deferred acceptance algorithm is the procedure used in the simulations, not a computational result of this paper.

The best candidate mirror would be to represent each side of a matching market by masses of complete preference types, add entrant mass, run deferred acceptance on those masses, and compare the pre- and post-entry allocations. That can be made coherent. But one entrant pair has vanishing mass in a continuum. Scaling entry to a positive mass makes the question meaningful, while changing the experiment from the paper’s one-pair expansion to a bulk market shock. And because agents of one type are interchangeable, the natural comparison tracks partner types or type-to-type allocation mass, rather than the paper’s exact-partner statistic. Those are reasonable modelling choices, but they define a follow-on study; they do not mirror a named computational result in the paper.

So the negative case is strong if the standard is an anchor in the paper, and weak if the claim is literally that no worthwhile continuous question could be built from its subject. A typed mass-entry question is plausible. I can defend rejecting this paper as an anchored example for the continuization programme, but not the universal claim that no scenario could make a worthwhile mirror.
