The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a ChoCo mirror is that none of the paper’s named results is computational. Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 establish rationality; Proposition 1 compares assumptions; Propositions 2–4 establish inefficiency and monotonicity violations; Proposition 5 gives a set-inclusion result about Failure 3. The pre- and post-deliberation jury theorems are bounds and convergence claims about competence. The simulations are not algorithmic results. None gives a complexity classification or an algorithm whose continuous-population counterpart this paper invites us to study.

The closest candidate is the post-deliberation jury theorem. A natural population model would assign mass \(\mu_A\) to each evidence-access type \(A\subseteq S\), then let deliberation move mass between access types. One could ask for the resulting majority competence \(p_{\rm maj}\), or whether deliberation raises it. That is a coherent continuous population model, and repeated epistemic types are plausible. But the paper presents no computation problem: competence is a probability over evidence and deliberation, while access and sharing parameters are inputs to an analysis, not choices in an optimization problem. Turning the question into algorithm design requires specifying an input encoding and an approximation target. Turning it into deliberation design requires adding constraints or costs; without them, letting everyone share and absorb all available evidence makes the optimization essentially trivial. Those choices would define a new computational project, rather than mirror a named result.

The same gap applies to the other results. A mass version of the inefficiency propositions could describe when a weighted majority disagrees with the evidence-based decision, but the propositions are existence claims, not computational tasks. The Failure 3 result remains a structural fact about access and complementarity under monotone deliberation. Neither becomes a complexity question merely because access sets carry rational masses.

The paper’s infinite-population jury theorems do not count as a continuous computational treatment; they are analytic results, not a collision. Nor is high multiplicity itself a reason to reject the mirror. The negative case is therefore strong against treating this paper as an anchored ChoCo contribution: it lacks the computational result the programme is meant to continuize. It is weak as a universal claim that no worthwhile computational extension exists. Computing or approximating competence for a mass distribution over access types could be worth studying, but the paper does not supply that problem as a mirror of one of its named computational results.
