The continuous mirror question

Given finite districts \(D\), schools \(C\), demographic types \(R\), complete repeated student types \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), per-capita capacities \(Q_c\), school-level ceilings \(U_c^r\), and a fully specified mass-valued analogue of the paper's rationed and weakly acceptant admissions rules, decide whether every bulk-SPDA outcome satisfies \(\left|y_d^r/\kappa_d-y_{d'}^r/\kappa_{d'}\right|\leq\alpha\) for every \(r\) and \(d\neq d'\). Here \(x_{\theta,c}\geq0\) is assigned mass, \(\sum_c x_{\theta,c}=\mu_\theta\), \(y_c^r=\sum_{\theta:r(\theta)=r}x_{\theta,c}\), and \(\kappa_d=\sum_{\theta:h(\theta)=d}\mu_\theta\); equivalently, compute \(\underline p_d^r\) and \(\overline q_d^r\) by min/max flow over legitimate mass assignments and test \(\overline q_d^r/\kappa_d-\underline p_{d'}^r/\kappa_{d'}\leq\alpha\) for all \(r,d\neq d'\).

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity interdistrict school-choice model with cohort masses \(\mu\), mass assignments \(x_{\theta,c}\), demographic aggregates \(y_c^r\), per-capita school capacities and ceilings, and a mass-valued SPDA; the computational task is universal \(\alpha\)-diversity verification via min/max flow.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is narrow but real. This paper has no named NP-hardness, membership-in-\(\mathrm{P}\), FPT, or approximation theorem. Its closest computational anchor is Theorem 3, proved in the paper: it reduces a universal SPDA diversity guarantee to min/max linear programs, which the paper observes are minimum-cost flow problems. If the anchor rule requires an explicitly numbered complexity classification, this paper has no qualifying anchor; the following is therefore a formulation-based case.

Theorem 3 says the following in full. Suppose every district admissions rule has school-level type-specific ceilings, is rationed and weakly acceptant, and the profile of admissions rules accommodates unmatched students. Let \(\hat p_d^t\) and \(\hat q_d^t\) be, respectively, the minimum and maximum number of type-\(t\) students that can be assigned to district \(d\) in any legitimate matching. Then SPDA satisfies the \(\alpha\)-diversity policy for every preference profile if and only if, for every type \(t\) and every distinct pair of districts \(d,d'\), \(\hat q_d^t/k_d-\hat p_{d'}^t/k_{d'}\leq\alpha\). The theorem is proved here; the polynomial min-cost-flow machinery used to calculate the bounds is cited from the network-flow literature.

My lead mirror would be Continuous \(\alpha\)-Diversity Verification for Interdistrict SPDA.

An instance contains finite districts \(D\), schools \(C\), demographic types \(R\), and full student types \(\Theta\). A full type \(\theta\) includes home district, demographic type \(r(\theta)\), initial school, preference ranking, and every priority or admissions attribute used by the mechanism. The society is given by rational masses \(\mu_\theta\geq 0\) with \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta=1\). Thus \(\mu_\theta\) is the fraction of students of type \(\theta\), not a fractional school assignment for an individual.

Let \(\kappa_d=\sum_{\theta:h(\theta)=d}\mu_\theta\) be the mass originating in district \(d\), and let \(M_r=\sum_{\theta:r(\theta)=r}\mu_\theta\). Schools have per-capita capacities \(q_c\), and school-level type ceilings \(u_c^r\). Districts use the mass-scaled analogue of the paper’s rationed, weakly acceptant admissions rules with reserves.

A feasible population assignment is a family \(x_{\theta,c}\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_c x_{\theta,c}=\mu_\theta\). Each unit of mass is still assigned one integral school; \(x_{\theta,c}\) records only how much of a large homogeneous cohort goes to each school. Write \(y_c^r=\sum_{\theta:r(\theta)=r}x_{\theta,c}\). A legitimate assignment satisfies

\(\sum_{c:d(c)=d}\sum_\theta x_{\theta,c}=\kappa_d\) for every district \(d\), \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta,c}\leq q_c\) for every school \(c\), and \(y_c^r\leq u_c^r\) for every school and demographic type.

Define the implied continuous floor and ceiling by

\(\underline p_d^r=\min\{\sum_{c:d(c)=d}y_c^r:x\text{ is legitimate}\}\)

and

\(\overline q_d^r=\max\{\sum_{c:d(c)=d}y_c^r:x\text{ is legitimate}\}\).

The question is:

Given this instance and \(\alpha\), does every outcome of the bulk, type-level SPDA satisfy

\(\left|\frac{y_d^r}{\kappa_d}-\frac{y_{d'}^r}{\kappa_{d'}}\right|\leq\alpha\)

for every demographic type \(r\) and every pair of districts \(d,d'\)?

The continuous problem has a direct solution: compute the \(2|D||R|\) extrema above by network-flow optimization and answer yes exactly when

\(\frac{\overline q_d^r}{\kappa_d}-\frac{\underline p_{d'}^r}{\kappa_{d'}}\leq\alpha \] for every r\) and every \(d\neq d'\). Thus I would expect this mirror to be Class A, with running time polynomial in the number of schools, districts, types, and the rational input length, assuming the admissions rule is given explicitly through the paper’s finite reserve/priority representation.

The regime is plausible for a large regional transfer programme: tens of thousands of students, but relatively few recurring cohorts sharing home district, demographic category, initial school, priority class, and preference template. The relevant comparison is \(N\gg|\Theta|\), not merely “school choice is large.” If a school uses a fully individualized priority lottery, those lottery ranks must be included in the type, and the advantage may disappear. That is a limitation of the scenario, not of the continuous formulation.

This is recognizably the authors’ problem. Their own central objects are distributions \(\xi_c^t\), school-level type ceilings, implied district floors and ceilings, and SPDA outcomes. The mirror changes the population from integer counts to rational mass and scales capacities and ceilings per capita; it does not fractionalize schools, candidates, or student outcomes. Conversely, any rational continuous instance can be multiplied by a common denominator and realized as a finite high-multiplicity instance, with each unit of mass receiving one school.

The mirror covers Theorem 3 only. I would not claim that it automatically continuizes the paper’s impossibility results for district-level ceilings or all of its TTC incentive results.

The weakest point is that Theorem 3 is fundamentally a structural characterization, not an explicitly numbered theorem saying “this problem is in \(\mathrm{P}\).” The polynomial conclusion comes from the paper’s surrounding min-cost-flow computation. A strict reader may therefore reject it as a ChoCo anchor. Substantively, however, it is a good population mirror: it turns the paper’s integer distributional analysis into an exact high-multiplicity mass problem, with a concrete algorithmic question and a clear tractable answer. Further work would study minimum achievable \(\alpha\), optimal design of school ceilings, finite-\(N\) rounding error, and what happens when the number of full types grows with the population.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strict negative case begins with a formal problem: the paper has no qualifying computational anchor. Theorem 3 is a structural if-and-only-if characterization of when SPDA satisfies an \(\alpha\)-diversity requirement. It does not state a complexity bound, define an input encoding, or formulate a decision or optimization problem. The minimum-cost-flow observation appears in the proof as cited machinery. On the programme’s stated standard, there is therefore no named computational result to continuize.

Even granting Theorem 3 as an anchor, the proposed mirror does not create a new computational object. Let \(N=\sum_d k_d\), and normalize the paper’s variables by setting
\[ \bar y_c^t=\frac{y_c^t}{N},\qquad \kappa_d=\frac{k_d}{N},\qquad \kappa^t=\frac{k^t}{N},\qquad Q_c=\frac{q_c}{N},\qquad U_c^t=\frac{q_c^t}{N}. \]
The paper’s flow constraints become exactly
\[ \sum_{c:d(c)=d}\sum_t \bar y_c^t=\kappa_d,\qquad \sum_c\bar y_c^t=\kappa^t,\qquad \sum_t\bar y_c^t\le Q_c,\qquad \bar y_c^t\le U_c^t. \]
Thus the proposed continuous problem is the same network-flow polytope after changing units from students to population shares. The paper already observes that this flow formulation has the integrality property. For rational masses, multiplying by a common denominator recovers the finite instance exactly. The continuous formulation therefore adds no new type-space issue, pricing problem, or algorithmic phenomenon; its central discrete-to-continuous identity is already contained in the paper’s own LP argument.

The proposed full population type \(\theta\) does not rescue the mirror. Theorem 3’s verification question depends only on \(k_d\), \(k^t\), capacities, and ceilings. Two societies with entirely different distributions of preferences and priorities but the same aggregate quantities produce the same answer. The \(\mu_\theta\) data are consequently dead input: the computation immediately aggregates them away. What does the work is the aggregate assignment polytope, not the society as a continuous object.

There is also no canonical continuum version of the mechanism being verified. The paper’s admissions rules are choice functions over finite sets of contracts and may use arbitrary priority orders over named students. If priority rank is included in \(\theta\), a strict priority order generally creates one effective type per student, destroying high multiplicity. If students are collapsed into finitely many priority classes, the resulting anonymous or tie-broken admissions rule is a new model. One can certainly define such a model, but then Theorem 3 is being applied to a newly designed bulk mechanism rather than continuized as stated.

A stronger proposal could optimize the minimum achievable \(\alpha\), choose ceilings, or study finite-population rounding. Those may be worthwhile research problems, but they are policy-design and approximation problems not asserted by this paper. They cannot repair the absence of a computational result in the proposed anchor.

This negative case is nevertheless not universal. Large regional school-choice systems are a plausible high-multiplicity regime, and a carefully specified finite-type bulk mechanism could make a sensible continuous model. The honest conclusion is therefore narrower: the proponent has not shown a new, qualifying ChoCo mirror of this paper. Claiming that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario would be too strong.
