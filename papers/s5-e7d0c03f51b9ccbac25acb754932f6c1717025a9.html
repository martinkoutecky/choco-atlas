The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest foothold is Theorem 1, proved in this paper. It says that a social choice function on the finite-voter, single-peaked-on-a-tree domain is contraction consistent, unanimous, and anonymous if and only if it is a \(q\)-threshold rule: for a terminal node \(r\), each extremal path from \(r\) has monotone decreasing thresholds; the rule selects the unique alternative in the profile’s range that is smallest in the path order and whose cumulative top votes meet its threshold. The thresholds must also satisfy the paper’s compatibility condition where paths intersect. Proposition 1, also proved here, says that contraction consistency and unanimity imply the rule is tops-only. These are substantive results, but neither asserts a complexity classification.

There is a plausible population setting behind them. Imagine a large city choosing one public-good site from locations on a fixed tree. A type is a resident’s complete single-peaked ranking of those locations; mass is the fraction of residents with that ranking. The number of residents can be very large while the support remains modest if residents share peaks and branch priorities. A natural continuous question would be: given a fixed tree, characterize all rules \(F\) from rational distributions over these types to locations such that unanimity holds and, whenever the selected location remains available, restricting every ranking to the available set leaves the outcome unchanged. One candidate is a share-based version of the paper’s \(q\)-threshold rules, replacing vote counts and integer thresholds with cumulative mass and threshold shares.

That is a coherent continuous-population analogue of the paper’s axiomatic question. It is not a qualifying mirror for this programme, though: the requested anchors must be named results asserting computational properties such as NP-hardness or membership in P, and this paper has none. Its main result characterizes social choice functions; it does not study algorithms or complexity. The candidate above therefore cannot be presented as a problem per computational anchor, and there is no discrete hardness result here whose fate in a continuum could be investigated.

The weak point is decisive for the requested case: even with a convincing high-multiplicity scenario, the paper’s contribution is axiomatic rather than computational. Under ChoCo’s scope, the share-threshold characterization is a nearby idea, not an anchored computational mirror.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the paper’s results have no computational content to continuize. Theorem 1 characterizes which social choice functions satisfy contraction consistency, unanimity, and anonymity; Proposition 1 shows that two of those axioms imply tops-onlyness. Neither gives an algorithm, complexity bound, or computational problem. The proponent’s proposed question—characterize rules on rational distributions that satisfy the corresponding axioms—is therefore a continuous version of the paper’s axiomatic question, outside ChoCo’s computational scope.

The city choosing a public-good site is a convincing high-multiplicity setting, and a distribution over residents’ single-peaked rankings is natural. So I would not argue that mass is meaningless here. But that setting does not supply the missing computational result. A share-threshold characterization would still be axiomatic. Given a specified threshold rule and a sparse rational society, evaluating it uses only the mass at each peak and cumulative masses along paths. That is ordinary evaluation of a stated rule, not a computational question raised or answered by this paper.

One could add bribery, control, or rule-design to create an optimization problem over this domain. But those would require introducing costs, actions, or objectives absent from the paper. They may be worthwhile research questions; they are not mirrors of its named results. The proponent’s best scenario survives as a sensible population model, but the proposed mirror does not survive as a ChoCo contribution. The honest negative case is strongest on that scope boundary, not on any claim that the continuous population itself is ill-motivated.
