The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I see is the paper’s unit-demand, multiple-item result. The scenario is a large ad market with \(N\) advertisers and \(qN\) identical placements, where \(0<q<1\). Advertisers fall into a relatively small number \(\tau\) of cohorts: members of a cohort share the same signal and valuation model, perhaps because they use the same market data and business model. The population grows with \(N\), while \(\tau\) stays fixed or moderate. A type is the complete cohort description the model uses; \(\mu_t\) is that cohort’s fraction of the population. Scaling supply with population is part of making this a sensible large-market auction.

My anchor is Theorem 5.1, proved in this paper. It says that for \(n\) unit-demand bidders competing for \(m\) identical items, there is an EPIC-IR mechanism with an \(O(d)\)-approximation to optimal welfare when valuations are \(d\)-self-bounding. The proof adapts Randomized Candidate Filtering, establishes fractional feasibility for \(\eta=4(d+1)\), and then obtains ex-post feasibility through randomized rounding. This is the closest anchor because it already includes multiple items and unit-demand bidders, so the supply scaling has a direct counterpart in the paper.

Here is the continuous problem I would ask a prover to study. Let \(T\) be a finite set of cohorts, with binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\). Each infinitesimal agent is unit-demand and belongs to one cohort. The input gives a rational per-capita supply \(q\), signal domains \(S_t\), and valuation oracles \(v_t:\prod_{u\in T}S_u\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) for the cohort members. For a cohort signal vector \(s\), define
\[ v_t(s_{-u})=\inf_{o_u\in S_u}v_t(o_u,s_{-u}). \]
Assume the cohort-coordinate analogue of \(d\)-self-bounding:
\[ \sum_{u\in T}\bigl(v_t(s)-v_t(s_{-u})\bigr)\le d\,v_t(s) \quad\text{for every }t\in T\text{ and }s. \]
The benchmark is the optimal fractional welfare
\[ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu,s)= \max_{x_t\in[0,1],\ \sum_t\mu_t x_t\le q} \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_t v_t(s). \]
The question is whether, using time and value-oracle queries polynomial in \(\tau\) and the input bit length \(L\), one can compute an anonymous randomized direct mechanism. It must give each member of cohort \(t\) the same allocation probability \(x_t\) and per-agent payment, be EPIC-IR against individual deviations, and have ex-post allocated mass at most \(q\). Its expected welfare should be at least \(\operatorname{OPT}/O(d)\). The allocation variable is per-agent probability; \(\sum_t\mu_t x_t\) is the allocated population mass.

I expect this version to be tractable for fixed \(d\). A natural route is a mass-weighted version of RCF: adapt its thresholding and tie-breaking to cohort masses, prove the corresponding candidate-mass bound, and implement the resulting allocation measurably within supply \(q\). That is a research question, not a corollary of Theorem 5.1. It would also generate questions about whether the \(O(d)\) ratio is tight for mass instances, what happens when \(d\) grows, and whether the guarantee extends to continuous type distributions or to the paper’s SOS subclasses.

This covers only the multi-unit, unit-demand welfare mechanism of Theorem 5.1. The authors would likely recognize the allocation and truthfulness problem as theirs: private interdependent values and capacity-constrained welfare are preserved, while the population and supply are expressed per capita. The main weakness is the self-bounding condition. The paper defines it over individual bidder coordinates; this mirror defines it over cohort signal coordinates. A rigorous continuum model must justify that choice and spell out EPIC-IR for a zero-mass individual deviation. If that change is rejected, the proposed question is not their continuous version. The positive case is that the cohort model retains the mechanism-design problem and makes population mass central to both supply and welfare, rather than merely making outcomes fractional.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 5.1 is a real computational anchor: it gives a polynomial-query truthful mechanism with an \(O(d)\) welfare guarantee. So the negative case cannot rest on “there is no result to mirror.” It has to turn on whether the proposed population model preserves the problem that theorem solves.

As stated, the cohort model does not define the individual incentive problem. Its input has one signal coordinate \(s_u\) per cohort, while EPIC-IR in the paper compares one bidder’s truthful report with that bidder’s unilateral deviation, holding everyone else’s reports fixed. If a cohort coordinate represents its members’ shared signal, changing it changes the reported state of the whole cohort; that is a group deviation. If one member’s report changes only that member’s signal, the model needs an individual coordinate and a rule for how that perturbation affects every valuation and allocation. The proposed type-level allocation \(x_t\) and value function \(v_t(s)\) do not specify that rule. Thus the claimed individual EPIC condition cannot be checked from the proposed input.

The natural repair is to keep each person’s private signal distinct, while expressing values and supply through a population state—for example, a distribution of signals or an aggregate statistic. That could be a sensible large-market model. But it requires new assumptions about how each bidder’s valuation function acts on that state and how its sensitivity scales with population size. The paper’s \(d\)-self-bounding condition sums the effects of changing each of \(n\) individual signal coordinates. Replacing those coordinates with \(\tau\) cohort coordinates is not implied by having repeated valuation models. The cohort condition in the proposal is a new regularity condition; the more faithful aggregate-state version would need its own definition and an argument that it preserves the paper’s structural bound.

That change matters to the algorithm, not just to notation. RCF compares a bidder’s value with other bidders’ lower estimates when that one bidder’s signal changes. A compressed type profile must still supply those unilateral counterfactuals. If the model uses only type-level aggregates, a single deviation may leave the aggregate unchanged; if it changes a whole cohort coordinate, it is no longer unilateral. A repaired model might retain a person’s signal separately and use aggregate information for the others, but then the valuation oracle and the relevant self-bounding property are new parts of the problem. The paper’s polynomial query bound in \(n\) does not establish a bound polynomial in \(\tau\) for that representation.

The multi-unit setting and proportional supply make a large market plausible, so low bidder counts are not a persuasive objection. Nor does the fact that a repaired continuum version may be tractable count against asking the question. The stronger negative claim is narrower: the proponent’s concrete mirror gets its finite-dimensionality by aggregating precisely the coordinates needed to state the theorem’s individual incentive constraints. Restoring those constraints takes us to a different model whose complexity and relation to Theorem 5.1 remain open.

That is the best case against this anchor, but it is not airtight. An aggregate-state model with a distinguished individual signal could be recognized by the authors as their problem and might yield a worthwhile mirror. I would reject the proposed cohort formulation as insufficiently specified for EPIC-IR; I cannot honestly rule out every faithful continuous-population version of Theorem 5.1.
