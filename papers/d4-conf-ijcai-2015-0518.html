The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no anchor to mirror here. The paper states no theorem, lemma, or corollary about the complexity of a computational problem. Its contributions are empirical: it compares simulated spreading across vertices, relates spreading scores to centrality measures, clusters users, and reports results from rewiring edges in three fixed networks. So the proponent’s proposed optimization is an extension of the paper’s subject, not a continuous version of one of its named results.

The strongest version of that extension would give user classes population shares and a contact kernel, then choose how much mass to rewire to maximize the final informed share under a continuous Maki–Thompson model. That is a coherent computational question, but its answer depends on a network model the paper does not supply. The reported rankings use each vertex’s position in a particular graph; the intervention redirects a particular edge toward a particular high-centrality vertex. A coarse type model can lose the local graph structure that drives those scores and the spreading dynamics. A type detailed enough to retain that structure may need to distinguish an ever-growing number of network positions. Either way, the optimization studies a new modeled diffusion problem, not the paper’s result.

That is the strongest negative case: the paper offers no computational result to anchor, and its main intervention is tied to specific graph instances. It does not establish the broader claim that no worthwhile continuous model exists. A population model with recurring interaction roles could still be a useful neighboring project; arguing that it is not worth the programme’s time would go beyond what this paper supports.
