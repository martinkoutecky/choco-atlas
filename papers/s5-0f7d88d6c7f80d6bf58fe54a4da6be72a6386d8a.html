The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

No qualifying computational anchor was raised. The strongest candidate is Proposition 4: its level-\(1\) agents draw a sophistication \(s\) from a distribution \(g\), then communicate and vote according to functions of \(s\). That gives a natural population description, but the proposition predicts behavior. It does not define a computational problem or classify the cost of solving one. The paper’s other propositions and results likewise concern equilibrium predictions and experimental findings.

The best population mirror would put a distribution over complete participant types—preference and cognitive type—and sample three participants to form each committee. For finite support of size \(\tau\), expected behavior and payoffs can be computed by enumerating at most \(\tau^3\) type triples and the game’s finite signals, messages, and votes. Population masses affect aggregate frequencies; they do not make the committee’s decision problem larger. If sophistication is continuous, the predictions involve integrating functions such as \(l(z,s)\) and \(d(s)\) against \(g\). The paper gives no input representation for those functions, so a complexity question would need a new computational model before it could be asked.

Scaling the committee itself offers a more substantial proposal, though it changes the game’s core incentives. In a nonatomic committee, one participant’s message or vote has measure zero, so the individual pivotality that drives strategic lying disappears. Preserving influence by treating a cohort as a coordinated positive-mass bloc would introduce a different kind of agent and a different game. One could study equilibrium computation in either extension, but that would be a new research question rather than a continuous mirror of a result in this paper.

The negative case is strongest at the anchor gate: there is no computational result here for continuization to mirror, and the natural population aggregation leaves a fixed three-person game intact. I cannot rule out a worthwhile large-committee computational model. The paper itself supplies no reason to treat that extension as its continuous computational mirror.
