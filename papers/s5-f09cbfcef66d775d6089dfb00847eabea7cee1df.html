The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t give this paper an anchor that meets your requirement: it contains no named computational result about an algorithmic problem’s complexity. Theorem 1 says the MOU system proves exactly the consequences valid under optimality semantics; Theorem 2 says the maximality, optimality, and unmatchedness semantics can simulate one another while preserving formulas; Theorem 3 gives the corresponding soundness and completeness result for acceptability. These are logical results, not claims about problems being in P, hard, or otherwise computationally classified. The conclusion explicitly leaves the complexity-theoretic perspective for future work.

The strongest population-based follow-on I can see comes from the paper’s majority-preference example. Take a large population with rational mass \(\mu_t\) on each of \(\tau\) complete ranking types over a finite set of alternatives. Let \(a\preceq_\mu b\) when \(a=b\) or more than half the population ranks \(b\) above \(a\). Given propositional descriptions \(\varphi,\psi\) of alternatives, ask whether \(B(\varphi\mid\psi)\) holds under the paper’s acceptability semantics: does every minimal retentive set among the alternatives satisfying \(\psi\) consist only of alternatives satisfying \(\varphi\)? The scenario is plausible for a large electorate with relatively few ballot types, and it follows the paper’s example of a society whose majority comparisons cycle.

But that is a proposed computational problem, not a result this paper establishes or studies. Its theorems concern arbitrary comparison relations and axiomatizing the resulting logics; they do not analyze relations generated by a mass distribution over voter rankings. So I would not present this as a valid mirror anchor or claim a complexity classification for it. The weakest point is the missing anchor, and it is decisive for the requested case: there is a plausible continuous-population extension, but no named computational result here whose hardness or tractability can be mirrored.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that this paper has no computational result to mirror. Theorem 1 and Theorem 3 establish soundness and strong completeness for two axiom systems; Theorem 2 shows that three semantics agree in expressive power. These are results about logical consequence over general comparison relations, not complexity classifications of computational problems. The authors say in the conclusion that the complexity-theoretic perspective remains future work.

The proponent’s majority-preference construction is the most plausible population-based follow-on. Give ranking types rational masses \(\mu_t\), use the resulting majority comparisons to define a relation among alternatives, and ask whether every acceptable alternative satisfying \(\psi\) also satisfies \(\varphi\)—that is, whether \(B(\varphi\mid\psi)\) holds under acceptability semantics. This fits the paper’s cyclic-majority example and describes a plausible high-multiplicity society.

A stronger version could study the complexity of deciding \(B(\varphi\mid\psi)\), or of finding the minimal retentive sets, when the relation is induced by a supported distribution over rankings. That is a well-posed candidate for future work. Yet it is a new complexity question about a population-generated subclass of the paper’s models. The paper’s axiomatization results already range over arbitrary binary relations, and its theorems neither depend on voter types and masses nor specify an algorithmic task. The distribution supplies one way to generate the relation; it does not turn any of the paper’s theorems into computational results.

So the negative case is strong against calling this a continuous mirror of a named result. It is weaker against the broader claim that no worthwhile continuous project could grow from the paper: the proposed majority-relation study may be worth pursuing precisely as new work. The paper itself points toward computational complexity as an open avenue, but that prospect cannot supply the missing mirror anchor.
