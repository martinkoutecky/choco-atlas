The paper proves no complexity or tractability claim. Treating Equation (1) as an anchor would require first specifying an exact computational problem the paper does not analyze.

The strongest candidate is a large crowdsourcing pool, with workers grouped by price and reliability characteristics, where a decision-maker chooses how much of each group to consult across repeated tasks. That is a plausible setting, and the authors might recognize it. But it is a new model: MADDM’s key state is each advisor’s trust estimate, updated from that advisor’s history using a truth estimate that depends on the other selected advisors. Pooling workers either changes that learning rule or requires types that track evolving histories. Replacing discrete consultations with queried mass also changes the evidence and exploration process. These are substantial changes to the object being optimized, even if they produce a useful research question.

That is the strongest case against calling this a continuous mirror of MADDM: the paper gives no computational theorem to carry across, and its most natural population-scale variant would need a new formalization of the dynamic learning problem. But it does not support the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario. Repeated high-volume annotation with interchangeable worker groups is credible, and a continuous consultation model could be worth studying on its own. The negative case is strong against an anchor-based mirror of this paper, but weak against that broader possibility.
