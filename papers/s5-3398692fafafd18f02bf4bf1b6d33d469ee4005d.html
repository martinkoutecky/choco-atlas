The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that this paper has no computational result to mirror. Proposition 1 characterizes the planner’s optimum; Proposition 2 gives equilibrium policies in closed form; the corollaries and hypotheses describe efficiency and comparative statics. None defines an algorithmic problem or establishes a complexity result. The experiment then tests those predictions. Calling Proposition 2 a computational anchor would confuse deriving an equilibrium with studying the complexity of computing one.

The best continuous version of the paper’s model would put a mass of representatives into types, scale resources per capita, and ask for equilibrium policy. But that changes the strategic setting at its core. The bargaining protocol selects one individual as proposer, and proposals target a minimal winning coalition. An atomless population has no uniformly random individual proposer; keeping a proposer or a finite set of offices as atoms preserves a finite strategic core. Alternatively, a continuous population of constituents could be added beneath the paper’s finite legislature, but in the paper constituents do not bargain or vote: their population is already aggregated into utility terms. Making their types heterogeneous enough to affect policy would add a new model.

One could study a continuum analogue of the equilibrium, or devise a richer bargaining model whose equilibria are computationally hard. Those could be worthwhile projects, but the first is analytic equilibrium work—the kind of continuization the programme distinguishes from its computational gap—and the second would require inventing the missing computational problem. The paper’s propositions and experimental findings supply no anchor for either.

So the proponent’s retreat is consequential: there is no named computational anchor here, and no prover-ready continuous question tied to one. This does not show that continuous legislative bargaining is uninteresting. It does make this paper a poor use of ChoCo’s time as a continuous mirror.
