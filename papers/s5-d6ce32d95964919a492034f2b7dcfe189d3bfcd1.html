The continuous mirror question

Given rational \(N>0\) and a support of \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational locations \(x_t\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), and rational per-person resources \(r_t>0\), each type has mass \(N\mu_t\). For any group mass vector \(q\in[0,N\mu]\), let \(R(q)=\sum_t q_t r_t\), let \(D(q)\) be the greatest distance between locations with positive mass (zero for a single location), and set \(U(q)=R(q)/(1+D(q))\), with \(U(0)=-\infty\). Compute a partition of the population that recursively selects, from the remaining mass, a group maximizing \(U\), breaking ties by largest total mass, and satisfies the SAE condition: for every source group \(j\), destination group \(k\ne j\) or empty destination, and real submass vector \(0\le s\le q^{(j)}\), either \(U(q^{(j)})\ge U(q^{(k)}+s)\) or \(U(q^{(k)})>U(q^{(k)}+s)\). Is such a partition computable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the binary input length \(L\)?

The model it lives in

The \(d=2\) group-formation game with utility \(U(R,D)=R/(1+D)\), represented by \(\tau\) complete location-resource types carrying rational masses; groups are mass vectors, and deviations may move any submass. The task is to construct the paper’s greedy PSAE and ensure its acceptance stability against all such deviations.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest mirror is narrow: compute the paper’s most powerful strong acceptance equilibrium when the population is represented by type masses. It mirrors a result the paper actually proves, but it is an algorithmic anchor, not a case where hardness might disappear.

My anchor is Proposition 1, proved in this paper. It states that Algorithm SAE has complexity \(\mathcal{O}(n^3)\) when group power has the form \(P_G(R_G,D_G)\), with \(D_G=\max_{i,j\in G}\|x_i-x_j\|_2\). The point of that bound is to construct a particular equilibrium, not just to test a proposed partition. Theorem 2, also proved here, supplies the correctness interpretation: Algorithm SAE constructs a PSAE, an SAE whose first group has maximum utility among all possible groups. Theorem 2 is supporting context for the anchor, not a separate mirror.

Here is the continuous problem I would hand to a prover: Continuous PSAE for diameter-based group formation. Fix dimension \(d=2\) and utility \(U(R,D)=R/(1+D)\), a form used in the paper. An instance gives a rational population scale \(N>0\) and \(\tau\) types \(t\), each with rational location \(x_t\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), positive per-person resource \(r_t\), and rational population fraction \(\mu_t>0\), where \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Type \(t\) therefore has mass \(N\mu_t\). A type completely describes the agent for the purposes of this game: its members have the same location and resources. The regime of interest has very large \(N\) and much smaller \(\tau\).

A group is a vector of masses \(q_t\in[0,N\mu_t]\). Its total resources are \(R(q)=\sum_t q_t r_t\), its territory is the convex hull of the locations with \(q_t>0\), and its diameter \(D(q)\) is the greatest distance between those locations. The output is a finite partition into groups, represented by vectors \(q^{(j)}\) satisfying \(\sum_j q^{(j)}_t=N\mu_t\). It must be a PSAE: recursively, each group is a maximum-utility submass of the population remaining at that stage, with ties broken in favour of the largest mass.

The stability condition still allows genuine continuous deviations. For any source group \(j\), any submass \(s_t\le q^{(j)}_t\), and any other group \(k\) (including an empty destination), the deviators must either not gain utility, \(U(q^{(j)})\ge U(q^{(k)}+s)\), or the receiving group must reject them, \(U(q^{(k)})>U(q^{(k)}+s)\). A solution must satisfy this for every such submass. The computational question is whether a PSAE can be produced in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the binary input length \(L\), with the paper’s pair-and-singleton candidate argument suggesting an \(\mathcal{O}(\tau^3)\) arithmetic-operation bound.

This is a plausible analogue of the authors’ problem: it keeps their shared group utility, resources-versus-diameter tradeoff, consent to join, and PSAE objective. It changes the population representation to mass. In this model, any maximizing group takes all remaining mass of each type it includes: increasing that mass raises resources without changing the group’s type support or diameter. Thus the greedy construction needs at most \(\tau\) rounds. The geometric candidate search can then work over support locations rather than over all \(N\) agents. The expected direction is tractable. The \(\mathcal{O}(\tau^3)\) bound is a proposed weighted-support extension of Proposition 1; the proposition as printed proves \(\mathcal{O}(n^3)\), not that extension.

A credible scenario is a large urban population forming shared protection or emergency-response groups, where many residents live at a relatively small set of sites and fall into a small number of resource categories. Mass records the fraction of residents at each site-and-resource type; group utility measures pooled capability against geographic spread. If the application uses further attributes that affect decisions, those belong in the type too, as high multiplicity requires.

The mirror covers Theorem 2’s PSAE construction and Proposition 1’s runtime result. It does not extend the paper’s convergence theorem or its encroachment-graph results. Its weakest point is also clear: the paper supplies no hardness anchor here, and its discrete algorithm is already polynomial. The continuous version’s gain is support-sensitive computation for large repeated populations, not a demonstration that continuous populations dissolve discrete hardness. Also, the proposed runtime requires proving that the paper’s geometric candidate characterization survives aggregation by rational type masses. The mirror remains faithful because it asks for the same PSAE under the same utility and deviation rules; the aggregation is the high-multiplicity regime, not a weakened equilibrium notion.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has a real computational anchor: Proposition 1 bounds the runtime of the PSAE algorithm, and Theorem 2 says what that algorithm constructs. So the case cannot rest on the absence of a named computational result.

The strongest objection is that the proposed mirror has little continuum-specific content. With positive resources and \(U(R,D)=R/(1+D)\), a utility-maximizing group includes all remaining mass of every type in its support: adding that mass raises \(R\) without changing \(D\). The PSAE the algorithm constructs therefore never splits a type. Its output is a partition of finitely many weighted type records, and the paper’s candidate search appears to transfer directly to those records. The proposed gain is support-sensitive computation on a high-multiplicity input, rather than a new continuous-optimization problem.

That makes the mirror’s research payoff look modest, but it does not defeat it under ChoCo’s stated standard. A population represented by rational masses over a small support is precisely the programme’s target. Group formation at a finite set of sites among residents with shared resource categories is a plausible regime; treating repeated agents as one type is part of high multiplicity, not a distortion. Nor does the PSAE anchor collapse into a vacuous individual-deviation condition: it asks for stability against arbitrary submasses, and the proposed construction must establish that stability.

A scope objection is also available: this paper studies stable coalition partitions, not a social choice rule or winner-changing problem. If ChoCo excludes computational game theory, the paper may sit outside the programme. But the stated scope does not establish that boundary, and the population-level algorithmic question remains recognizable.

So I can make a case that this is a thin mirror with an anticipated algorithm. I cannot honestly make the stronger case that no worthwhile mirror exists. Proposition 1 and Theorem 2 survive the strongest objections I can support; under the grading rule given, the negative case is weak.
