The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent is right about the main threshold: Theorems 3.1 and 3.3 establish manipulability bounds, not computational complexity. Their argument alone does not prove that no useful continuous question could be invented, but the better objection is that the paper’s central object has no natural population measure to continuize.

Here \(n\) counts competitors, and the input is the outcome of every pairwise match. The rule selects one named competitor; the manipulation measure compares the winning probabilities of the particular pair \(i,j\). Those identities and their pairwise results are the substance of the model. A type distribution cannot summarize them unless types retain the relevant match behavior against all other types. With finitely many such types, the masses do not enter the paper’s pointwise worst-case bounds. Treating a distribution over tournaments as the continuous object would instead continuize outcomes; Theorem 3.5 already handles randomized tournaments while keeping the competitors finite.

The strongest alternative is to group many entrants into cohorts and measure the chance that a cohort wins. But a single pair of entrants has zero mass in an atomless population, so its manipulation has zero population-level effect. Giving a cohort positive mass and letting it manipulate means changing many match outcomes, not one bilateral match. That may define a new collective-strategy problem, but it changes the paper’s strategic object rather than making the same object continuous. The same problem appears in a continuum bracket: the finite random bracket is over permutations of named competitors, while a mass-valued winner requires a new tournament and winner concept.

So the negative case is strong, though not a proof that no interesting model could be built. Theorem 3.1’s three-player cycle and Theorem 3.3’s bracket coupling are meaningful results about finite, identified competitors; neither supplies a computational problem for the continuous-population programme. A redesigned cohort model might be worth studying on its own, but this paper gives little basis for calling it a continuous mirror.
