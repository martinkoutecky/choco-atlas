The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest conclusion is that this paper has a natural continuous-population extension, but no qualifying computational anchor under the ChoCo rules. Its named results are structural probability theorems, not claims of NP-hardness, membership in \(P\), approximation, or parameterized complexity.

The nearest possible anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper. It states that there is a constant \(C>0\) such that, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), any social choice function \(f\) for \(n\) voters and three alternatives that is more than \(\varepsilon\) away from every dictatorship satisfies
\[ \sum_{i=1}^n M_i(f)\ge C\varepsilon^2, \]
where \(M_i(f)\) is the probability, over a uniformly random preference profile and uniformly random alternative report by voter \(i\), that the report is a profitable manipulation. The paper’s abstract and “Main Theorem” state neutrality explicitly; the numbered Theorem 1 is printed without that word. In either formulation, this is a quantitative manipulability theorem, not a computational-complexity result.

The paper’s discussion of NP-complete manipulation cites earlier work such as [BTT89] and [BO91], but those are not results proved or numbered in this paper. Theorem 2 is attributed to Kalai and concerns distance from dictatorships and social-welfare functions; Theorems 3 and 4 concern Condorcet-winner probabilities. None qualifies as a computational anchor.

If the anchor gate were relaxed, the strongest mirror would be a positive-mass version of manipulation power. Let \(C=\{a,b,c\}\), let \(T=S_3\) be the six complete rankings, and let a society be a rational distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\). A type is a complete ranking, and \(\mu_t\) is its population mass. Let \(F:\Delta(T)\to C\) be a neutral deterministic social-choice rule, represented for finite input by a rational polyhedral partition of the simplex. A bloc of mass \(\alpha\) of type \(t\) may report \(t'\), producing
\[ \mu^{t\to t'}=\mu+\alpha(e_{t'}-e_t), \qquad \mu_t\ge\alpha. \]
The bloc succeeds when
\[ F(\mu^{t\to t'})\succ_t F(\mu). \]

A precise problem is therefore:

\[ \textsc{Continuous-Manipulation-Power}_\alpha \]

Given \(F\), rational \(\alpha\), and rational threshold \(\theta\), compute or decide whether
\[ \Pr_{\mu,t,t'}\!\left[ F(\mu^{t\to t'})\succ_t F(\mu) \right]\ge\theta, \]
where \(\mu\) is drawn from normalized Lebesgue measure on \(\Delta(T)\), \(t\) is drawn according to \(\mu_t\), and \(t'\) is uniform over \(T\). A solution is the exact probability, or a correct yes/no comparison with \(\theta\).

This is plausible in a high-multiplicity regime: a large electorate contains millions of voters but only a few repeated ballot types, and a campaign or coordinated organization can move a positive fraction of one homogeneous cohort. The model preserves the paper’s candidates, rankings, strategic reporting, neutrality, and notion of a profitable outcome. For a fixed three-candidate polyhedral rule, I would expect the problem to be tractable: the successful events are unions of fixed-dimensional rational polyhedra, so their volumes can plausibly be computed by polyhedral algorithms. With variable \(m\), succinct rule representations, or exact probability under arbitrary circuits, continuum-specific volume complexity may arise.

The serious weakness is also decisive. In the original theorem, the manipulator is one named voter and “far from dictatorship” refers to dependence on named coordinates. In a pure population distribution, an individual has zero mass, and identities disappear. Replacing that voter by an \(\alpha\)-mass bloc and replacing dictatorship by some cohort-level analogue are substantive changes. The proposed problem is therefore a credible new continuous manipulation problem, but only an extension of Theorem 1—not a compliant mirror of a named computational result.

So the paper supports a well-motivated continuous-population research question, covering only its manipulability theorem. It does not support a positive ChoCo case under the programme’s required computational-anchor standard.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent has conceded the decisive point: this paper contains no qualifying computational result. Its Main Theorem (also labelled Theorem 1 in the body) is a quantitative structural statement about manipulability. The other numbered results concern distance from dictatorships, Condorcet-winner probabilities, and reductions between those notions. None asserts hardness, tractability, approximation, or parameterized complexity. The references to NP-complete manipulation are background citations, not results established here. Under ChoCo’s gate, there is therefore no computational anchor to mirror.

The proposed positive-mass problem does not repair that defect. It is a sensible new problem, but it changes every feature that makes the paper’s theorem what it is. The paper studies a named voter \(i\), a named coordinate of \(L^n\), and the quantity \(M_i(f)\). Its premise measures distance from functions that read one named coordinate. After collapsing a profile to \(\mu\in\Delta(S_3)\), those coordinates and identities no longer exist. Two social choice functions can agree on every population histogram while differing completely in how they depend on voter identities, and hence have different dictatorship distances and manipulation powers.

The best attempted repair is to restrict to anonymous rules, write \(f(x)=F(\mu_x)\), and replace one voter by a bloc of mass \(\alpha\). That is a legitimate high-multiplicity model, but it is not a continuization of the theorem’s manipulator. For the actual high-multiplicity limit, one voter has mass \(1/n\). For a regular population rule, changing that mass affects the outcome only in a shrinking boundary layer; the unscaled probability of successful manipulation therefore disappears as \(n\to\infty\). Taking fixed \(\alpha>0\) avoids the degeneration, but studies coalition manipulation rather than random single-voter manipulation. Normalizing the vanishing quantity, or taking a derivative at \(\alpha=0\), produces a new boundary-sensitivity invariant that the paper neither defines nor motivates.

The same problem affects dictatorship. A named individual has measure zero in the continuum. A positive-mass “dictatorial cohort” is a different object, with a threshold that must be chosen. A rule that follows one fixed type is not the analogue of a voter dictatorship; a rule that follows the plurality of a type is not a dictatorship at all. Tagging a special atom of positive mass preserves dictatorship only by retaining a discrete exceptional agent, rather than making the population itself the continuous object.

The proposed probability measure is also an imported choice, not the limit of the paper’s experiment. The paper samples profiles uniformly from \(S_3^n\). The induced distribution on empirical societies is multinomial and concentrates near the uniform type distribution; it is not normalized Lebesgue measure on the simplex. One may deliberately choose Lebesgue measure, a Dirichlet prior, or an entropy-weighted limit, but each gives a different manipulation-power problem. The paper supplies no projectively consistent family \(f_n\) or measure that selects among them.

This is not an objection that high multiplicity is unnatural for elections. Repeated ballot types are entirely sensible. The obstruction is specific to this paper: its theorem is about arbitrary labelled social choice functions and individual-coordinate influence, not about a family of anonymous rules on population histograms. Restricting to such a family would be a new continuous social-choice programme, not a mirror of the stated result.

The honest negative conclusion is therefore strong under ChoCo’s rules, though not metaphysical: one can invent interesting continuous questions inspired by this paper. What cannot be obtained is a worthwhile continuous-population mirror of one of its named computational results, because there is no such result, and the obvious structural theorem loses its identity when individual coordinates are replaced by mass.
