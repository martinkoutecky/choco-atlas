The strongest negative point is an anchor audit: the paper reports no new theorem, lemma, or corollary classifying the complexity of a computational problem. Its formal-looking experts-algorithm loss bound is attributed to Cesa-Bianchi et al.; the paper evaluates variants of that algorithm rather than establishing a new computational result. The best repaired candidate would make a large population of forecasters the continuous object. Give each expert a complete strategy, including their forecasts across events, and represent the crowd by a measure \(\nu\). At event \(t\), an average forecast could be written as \(\int f(h_t)\,d\nu(f)\), where \(h_t\) is the available history. A large, recurring forecasting crowd is a plausible high-multiplicity regime. But this proposes a new continuous online-learning problem; it does not mirror a computational claim made by this paper. With finitely represented types, aggregation and updates reduce to sums over the support. With a continuum of strategies, the problem needs a representation of \(\nu\), and complexity parameters that the paper never specifies.

A more ambitious version would optimize the weights or estimate a variance for each type. That runs into a modeling choice the paper’s findings do not resolve: variance alone does not identify an expert’s forecasts, while grouping experts by performance can discard the cross-event history used by the algorithms. Preserving that history requires types that encode full forecast trajectories. A finite or parametric encoding might lead to a worthwhile new study, but its tractability would rest on assumptions supplied by that study, not on a result in this paper.

So the negative case is strong against presenting this as a mirror of the paper’s computational results. It does not establish the universal claim that no scenario could yield a worthwhile continuous question. A large crowd of repeat forecasters is sensible, and a carefully specified continuous online-learning model could be interesting. The honest limitation is that those possibilities are adjacent new work, not continuizations anchored in this paper.
