Why no mirror

The approval-welfare question is well formed and the authors would recognize it, but its objective is exactly weighted \(\varphi\)-MaxCoverage with universe elements indexed by approval types. The rational weights already represent the distribution's masses, so the proposed mirror restates a model the paper already permits rather than bridging a discrete population to a continuous one.

What the mirror covers

The proposed question covers approval-based welfare maximization and the approximation boundary for sublinear \(\varphi\); it leaves the resource-allocation and broader coverage applications aside.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is the paper’s multiwinner approval problem: a large electorate may have many voters but relatively few distinct approval ballots. Each ballot is a voter type, and its mass is the fraction of the electorate using it. The committee remains an integral set of \(k\) candidates; only the population becomes continuous.

For the numbered hardness anchor, I use Theorem 3.10, cited from Manurangsi20 and adapted in the paper. It says that, assuming Gap-ETH, for every \(\delta>0\), every \(h\ge 2\), and sufficiently large right alphabet \(R\) depending on \(\delta,h\), no algorithm running in \(f(k)N^{o(k)}\) time can solve \((\delta,h)\)-AryGapLabelCover with right alphabet \([R]\). Here \(k\) is the number of vertices, \(N\) is the instance size, and \(f\) is any function. The paper uses this hardness source in its reduction to coverage.

The paper’s direct coverage results are printed as unnumbered Theorem* statements in the supplied text, so I will not invent a theorem number for them. The main one says: for every normalized, nondecreasing, concave \(\varphi\), \(\varphi\)-MaxCoverage has a polynomial-time \(\alpha_\varphi\)-approximation; when \(\varphi(j)=o(j)\), it is NP-hard to approximate within any factor greater than \(\alpha_\varphi+\varepsilon\), for constant \(\varepsilon>0\). The paper also derives, from Theorem 3.10, that under Gap-ETH and \(\varphi(j)=o(j)\), no \((\alpha_\varphi+\varepsilon)\)-approximation runs in \(f(k)m^{o(k)}\) time, where \(m\) is the number of cover sets and \(k\) the cardinality constraint. That derived coverage statement is the endpoint relevant to the mirror.

Lead problem — Continuous Approval-Welfare Maximization. Fix a normalized, nondecreasing, concave \(\varphi\) with \(\varphi(j)=o(j)\). An instance consists of \(m\) candidates, a committee size \(k\), and a finite support of approval types \(\mathcal A\subseteq 2^{[m]}\), each with positive binary-rational mass \(\mu_A\), where \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A=1\). Find a committee \(S\subseteq[m]\) of size \(k\) maximizing
\[ W_\mu^\varphi(S)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A\,\varphi(|S\cap A|). \]
The parameters are \(m\), support size \(\tau=|\mathcal A|\), and mass bit length \(L\). The expected answer is hardness transfers: the unweighted discrete coverage instances used for hardness embed as empirical voter distributions, with one approval ballot per universe element and mass \(1/n\) (aggregating identical ballots if desired). Their objective is the same up to the factor \(n\), so the approximation ratio is unchanged. The reduction’s combinatorics lie in the candidate sets and committee choice, not merely in a large count of voters.

This is a plausible mirror of the authors’ problem: their multiwinner section explicitly identifies voters with approval sets and \(\varphi(j)\) with utility from \(j\) approved winners. A society with millions of voters and a much smaller set of recurring ballots is a credible high-multiplicity regime. The distributional input also makes the welfare objective an average per-voter quantity. The mirror covers the approval-based voting application and the hardness boundary for sublinear \(\varphi\), not the paper’s resource-allocation applications or its full coverage framework.

The main weakness is that this mirror is unlikely to make the headline hardness disappear. Rational masses can encode ordinary finite electorates, and the hardness appears driven by the candidate and set-system structure. The additional question worth pursuing is whether restricted regimes—especially small \(\tau\)—permit better algorithms than the general \(\alpha_\varphi\) guarantee.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proposed approval-welfare problem is a particularly natural case for a mirror, but it also exposes the strongest objection: it is already the paper’s weighted problem written as a distribution over ballot types. Given support \(\mathcal A\), create one universe element for each \(A\in\mathcal A\), set its weight to \(w_A=\mu_A\), and put it in candidate \(i\)’s set exactly when \(i\in A\). Then the paper’s objective is exactly
\[ \sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A\,\varphi(|S\cap A|). \]
Normalizing the weights to sum to one only rescales the objective. Repeated ballots can be aggregated by adding their weights. Thus the proposed input parameters \((m,\tau,L)\) are already the paper’s weighted input parameters with \(n=\tau\); the continuous formulation adds no distinct optimization problem or population regime.

The proponent’s named anchor, Theorem 3.10, is a Gap-ETH hardness result for AryGapLabelCover, the source problem in the reduction. It is not a result about coverage or elections. The paper’s relevant coverage theorem is printed as Theorem*, but it does assert computational results, so the absence of a numbered coverage theorem is not a sound objection. The issue is that the approval mirror restates the weighted coverage model those results already address. Turning an unweighted electorate into masses \(1/n\), or aggregating equal ballots, gives an equivalent encoding; that is not a discrete-to-continuous identity newly discovered by the paper.

The best alternative I see is to make the *agents* in the resource-allocation application continuous: group agents with identical action menus, and let each type’s mass split across its available actions. But the resulting optimization has one variable for each type-action pair, constraints that each type’s action masses sum to its population mass, and a concave \(\varphi\)-objective over resource loads. That is the paper’s matroid relaxation applied to a partition constraint; its piecewise-linear \(\varphi\) already makes the formulation an LP. This alternative therefore lands inside the paper’s existing continuous optimization machinery as well.

So the negative case is strongest as a novelty collision: the most convincing population reading is already represented by the paper’s weighted formulation, and the natural agent-type extension is already its relaxation. I cannot make the universal claim airtight—an application with type-dependent costs or other constraints not captured by these formulations could still yield a worthwhile mirror—but those additions would need to supply the new computational question. The approval anchor as stated does not.
