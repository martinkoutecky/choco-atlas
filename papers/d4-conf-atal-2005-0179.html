The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Section 4 gives a substantive computational result even though the paper does not package it as a numbered theorem: its MDP-based winner-determination formulation replaces the flat auction’s exponentially many bundle-allocation variables with occupation measures and \(|M||O|\) resource-use indicators. The paper presents this as its main contribution and reports the resulting computational gains. The missing theorem number is a real weakness under a strict eligibility rule, but it does not show that the result has no worthwhile continuous mirror.

A plausible regime is a large delivery fleet with many agents drawn from a small set of complete types: each type fixes the agent’s MDP, initial conditions, resource requirements, and capacities. Let \(\mu_t\) be the population share of type \(t\), and let \(P_t\) contain its feasible plans, each pairing an integral resource bundle with a policy. Assign mass \(z_{tp}\) to each plan, with per-capita resource supply \(\beta_o\). The welfare problem is

\[ \max \sum_{t,p} z_{tp} U_t(p) \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_p z_{tp}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,p} z_{tp} a_o(p)\le \beta_o,\qquad z_{tp}\ge 0, \]

where \(a_o(p)\) records whether plan \(p\) uses resource \(o\). This allows different portions of a type cohort to receive different plans; it does not give a fractional bundle to an individual. Per-capita supplies and type-level averages are the natural co-modifications for this mirror.

The master problem has potentially exponentially many plan columns. Its pricing question is whether, for resource prices \(\lambda_o\), one can efficiently find a type’s plan maximizing \(U_t(p)-\sum_o\lambda_o a_o(p)\). That brings the paper’s MDP structure into direct contact with column generation and makes the complexity question precise in the number of types, resources, MDP size, and input bit length. The answer might expose tractable structure or show that the local resource-use choices remain hard; either would locate what the population relaxation changes. This case supports a mirror of the welfare and winner-determination result, without claiming that the paper’s individual VCG payments or distributed strategy-proofness carry over unchanged.
