The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set \(\Theta\) of complete valuation types with population distribution \(\mu\in\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Theta)\), an ordered stream of indivisible item classes with per-capita supplies, and normalized DAB buffer capacity \(\beta\), construct a compact deterministic policy that, for every sufficiently large integral replication \(q\), allocates the \(q\mu_\theta\) agents and integral item copies online, maintains individual EF at every nonterminal update, ends EF1, and supplies an integral lift of the type-level assignment.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity DAB fair-division model in which \(\mu\) distributes complete valuation types, item supplies and buffer capacity scale with \(q\), and online type-to-item transport must be lifted to indivisible bundles while preserving temporal EF/EF1.

The objection that survived

The proponent has not shown that a compact type-level description admits an integral lift preserving individual EF at every online prefix; the required state may involve a large distribution over bundle histories.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the DAB positive theorem and the DAB buffer-boundary question, while leaving the lookahead impossibilities, SAS results, SAS lower bound, and general-additive reductions aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can a compact state over \(\Theta\), live item classes, and bundle histories produce an integral lift preserving individual EF at every prefix?
  • Can the ordered arrival stream be represented by a population-scaled arrival path without losing feasibility changes at scale \(1/q\)?
  • Does the normalized critical DAB buffer threshold equal \(k\), or does the finite one-copy obstruction vanish in the limit?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s buffered online fair-division problem. The lead anchor is Theorem 4.2, proved in this paper, rather than a hardness theorem: for every personalized \(k\)-value instance with \(n\) agents, Store-and-Match in the DAB model, using a buffer of size \((n-1)k\), efficiently computes a temporal-\(\mathrm{EF}/\mathrm{EF1}\) allocation. While items continue arriving, every allocation is envy-free; after the stream ends, the remaining buffer can be allocated so that the final allocation is EF1.

A natural continuous population is a large population of clients, students, patients, or employees divided into finitely many valuation cohorts. Let \(\Theta\) be the finite set of complete agent types and let \(\mu\in\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Theta)\) give their population masses. A type includes its entire valuation profile, so two agents of the same type agree on every item class that can arrive. Each type has at most \(k\) valuation levels.

Item supply must scale with population size. For a replication factor \(q\), there are \(q\mu_\theta\) agents of type \(\theta\), and each item class arrives with \(q\lambda_g\) indivisible copies, one copy at a time. The buffer has \(q\beta\) positions, where \(\beta\) is buffer capacity per unit population. Thus the continuous society is represented by \(\mu\), while the \(q\)-realization still consists entirely of indivisible agents, indivisible items, and integral bundles. Type-level assignment variables may be fractional only because they summarize many integral assignments.

This gives the following precise problem.

\[ \textsc{Continuous-DAB-TEF1}_{\infty} \]

Given \(\Theta\), \(\mu\), \(k\), an online stream of item classes with per-capita multiplicities \(\lambda_g\), and a normalized buffer capacity \(\beta\), construct a deterministic online DAB policy. At every arrival, the policy may allocate any subset of the newly arrived copies and buffered copies, but may not split an individual copy. The policy must maintain envy-freeness at every nonterminal update and EF1 after the final buffered mass is allocated. Correctness is required for every sufficiently large integral realization \(q\), and the output must include a compact mass assignment together with an integral lift to the \(q\)-agent realization.

The continuous analogue of Theorem 4.2 uses \(\beta=k\): since the finite buffer is \((n-1)k\), its normalized size tends to \(k\) as \(n\) grows. The likely algorithm is a measure-valued Store-and-Match. The top-choice graph becomes a type-to-item-class incidence graph with mass on both sides; Hall’s matching condition becomes a transportation feasibility condition. The key computational question is whether each fractional matching can be represented compactly by a cyclic or measurable integral assignment while preserving EF at every online update. With explicit type support, this looks like Class A: a polynomial transportation or flow computation over \(\tau\) agent types and the currently live item classes, followed by an integral decomposition.

The mirror is recognisably the authors’ problem. It retains online revelation, indivisible items, additive mixed-manna valuations, buffer storage, DAB allocation, and temporal EF/EF1. Only the population regime changes: instead of \(n\) unrelated named agents, there are many repeated complete valuation types. The accompanying scaling of item supply and buffer capacity is forced by that regime, just as per-capita supply is forced in any meaningful high-multiplicity relaxation.

The paper’s lower-bound boundary is also worth mirroring. Theorem 4.9, proved here, states that for every \(k\ge2\), every deterministic DAB algorithm with buffer \((n-1)k-1\) can be forced to fail to produce an EF allocation on at least half of its allocation updates, even for \(k\)-value instances consisting solely of goods or solely of chores.

This suggests a second problem:

\[ \textsc{Critical-DAB-Buffer}_{\infty}(k) \]

Determine the smallest normalized buffer capacity \(\beta\) that guarantees temporal EF for every continuous \(k\)-value stream, or produce an adversarial stream witnessing failure on a positive fraction of allocation updates. The finite theorem gives an upper bound \(\beta\le k\), but its lower-bound construction is delicate in the limit: it uses only \(n-1\) copies of each value level, and the missing one copy has normalized mass \(1/n\), which disappears as \(n\to\infty\). Hence the continuous question is genuinely interesting. Does the exact threshold \(k\) survive, or does the finite obstruction dissolve into a smaller transportation threshold? My expectation is that the pure limit problem is tractable and that the one-copy shortage in Theorem 4.9 does not by itself survive continuization. If one instead requires exact implementation for every finite \(q\), including \(O(1/q)\) supply deficits, then the theorem’s obstruction transfers in a Class-B-like way.

The paper therefore supports a credible positive mirror even though it contains no named NP-hardness result. Its computational anchors are Theorem 4.2’s polynomial-time algorithm and Theorem 4.9’s sharp finite buffer obstruction. The mirror covers the core DAB results, not the entire paper; the SAS theorem could generate a further variant with “EF after every population-mass unit,” but I would not use it as a third anchor unless the integral-prefix issue were settled.

The weakest point is exactly that integral-prefix issue. A fractional transport solution over types is not enough: if it merely gives every type an average bundle, it has replaced indivisible fair division by a fractional outcome-space problem. The mirror is defensible only if every mass assignment has an integral lift in which almost every actual agent receives an integral bundle and temporal EF1 holds at every relevant prefix. Establishing that lift is the main technical obligation, but it is also a natural computational question arising directly from the paper rather than a simplified problem invented solely to obtain tractability.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is not that repeated valuation types are implausible. They are perfectly plausible in fair division. The problem is that the paper’s substantive object is not the population distribution; it is the exact synchronization of indivisible bundles under an item-by-item arrival order. A distribution over agent types does not retain enough state to represent that problem.

Take Theorem 4.2. If the item stream is held fixed while the population is replicated, the proposed buffer \((n-1)k\) eventually exceeds the entire stream. The online problem then collapses into offline EF1 allocation. That is a genuine continuum degeneration.

The natural repair is the proponent’s: scale item supplies and buffer capacity with \(q\), retain indivisible copies, and use \(q\) repeated agents. But this does not yield a finite-dimensional continuous society problem. A transportation flow between agent types and live item classes only says how much mass of each type receives each class. It does not specify an integral assignment to current bundles. Exact EF is individual: agents of one valuation type may have different bundles with equal utility, while agents of another type may value those bundles differently. The relevant state is therefore a distribution over current bundle histories, not merely a distribution over initial valuation types.

One can repair this by carrying a measure over \((\text{valuation type},\text{current bundle})\). But as the number of item classes grows with \(q\), that state space grows with the stream and may be exponentially large. Alternatively, one can define fairness only for type averages. That turns the problem into fractional outcome allocation and no longer mirrors the paper’s individual EF guarantee. The proposed “integral lift” is therefore not a routine implementation detail; it is the missing problem that carries essentially all of the paper’s difficulty.

The online aspect creates a second obstruction. The input is an ordered word of item arrivals, not just per-capita item multiplicities. If the order is compressed into a limiting mass path, events of mass \(1/q\) disappear. Yet exact feasibility can change at precisely that scale. With \(q\) identical agents and unit goods, the first \(q-1\) goods cannot be allocated while preserving EF: any nonempty allocation makes some agent strictly better off. At the \(q\)-th good, all \(q\) goods can be matched simultaneously and EF is restored. The normalized difference is only \(1/q\), but the feasible action changes discontinuously. Retaining this information requires microscopic arrival order and lattice residues; discarding it produces a fluid problem with different temporal semantics.

Theorem 4.9 is an even weaker anchor. Its obstruction uses exactly \(n-1\) copies of each value level. Under population scaling, the missing copy has mass \(1/q\), so the theorem’s sharp finite obstruction is invisible in the natural limit. The proposed \(\textsc{Critical-DAB-Buffer}_{\infty}\) is a sensible new question, but it is not the continuous form of Theorem 4.9 unless one adds a new macroscopic supply-deficit construction. Scaling the shortage to \(\Theta(q)\) would study a per-capita inventory threshold, not the paper’s one-copy buffer boundary; preserving the one-copy boundary requires precisely the finite arithmetic that continuization removes.

Thus the two possible repairs both lose:

  • keep the stream finite, and the buffer becomes effectively offline;
  • scale the stream, and either retain a discrete bundle/history process or relax to a divisible fluid model.

This is not an objection based on whether the continuous answer is easy or hard, nor an objection to high-multiplicity fair division generally. It is an objection that the proposed mirrors do not produce a continuous population problem of the ChoCo kind. The population measure is not the sufficient state, while the faithful state retains the discrete online process that the continuization was supposed to remove.

The negative case is not airtight. If one proves a compact integral-lift theorem for a carefully specified mass-valued arrival path, while preserving individual EF at every macroscopic update and all sufficiently large integral realizations, then this becomes a worthwhile Class A programme. But that theorem is absent from the proposed case; until it exists, Theorem 4.2 gives a promising research specification, not a convincing continuous mirror, and Theorem 4.9 does not survive as an anchor.
