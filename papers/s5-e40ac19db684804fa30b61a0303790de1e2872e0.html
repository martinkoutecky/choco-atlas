The closest numbered results do different work: Lemma 1, proved here, shows that QMR satisfies quantum transitivity, quantum unanimity, and QIIA; Theorem 2, proved here, shows that the Quantum Arrow Conjecture is false by giving a QMR counterexample. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a continuous mirror is that this paper offers no result of the kind ChoCo is meant to continuize. Lemma 1 is an axiomatic verification of QMR’s properties; Theorem 2 is an axiomatic counterexample to the Quantum Arrow Conjecture; and Theorem 1 is Arrow’s classical theorem, cited from elsewhere. None asserts a computational complexity result or gives an algorithmic guarantee for a named problem. A continuous version of those claims would continuize axioms, which is outside the programme’s scope.

The imagined mass version of QMR is still a natural model: put masses on rankings, compare pairwise masses to form the majority graph, and apply the SCC construction. That makes it a possible continuous *axiomatic* mirror of QMR, not a computational anchor in this paper. To ask a ChoCo-style question, one would have to add a problem such as optimizing a campaign or finding a manipulation. The paper does not formulate or analyze one. Its strategic examples instead rely on particular voters’ entanglement and relative phases, information a ranking-mass distribution alone does not represent.

I would not claim that no sensible continuous population model can be made here. QMR’s majority graph itself depends on aggregate counts, and its three-voter cycle can be represented by masses. So the negative case is strong as a reason to reject this paper as a target for the programme, but weak as a universal claim that no worthwhile computational question could ever be built from its model. That would be a new research question, rather than a mirror of a computational result in this paper.
