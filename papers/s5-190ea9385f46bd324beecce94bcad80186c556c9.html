The continuous mirror question

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), finite approval types \(T\), approval sets \(A_t\subseteq C\), and rational population distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), compute a fractional committee \(p\in[0,1]^C\) and a lottery \(X\) over integral \(k\)-committees with \(p_c=\Pr_{W\sim X}[c\in W]\), such that for every submass vector \(z\le\mu\), \(\sum_{c\in\bigcup_{z_t>0}A_t}p_c\ge\sum_t z_t\min\{k,|A_t|\}\); for every type \(t\) and \(q\in[0,\mu_t]\), \(\sum_{c\in A_t}p_c\ge\min\{qk,|A_t|\}\); and every supported committee \(W\) satisfies continuous EJR: for every \(\ell\in[k]\) and every \(z\le\mu\) with \(\sum_tz_t\ge\ell/k\) and \(\left|\bigcap_{z_t>0}A_t\right|\ge\ell\), some type \(t\) with \(z_t>0\) has \(|A_t\cap W|\ge\ell\).

The model it lives in

A type-compressed weighted BW-MES model in which type \(t\) has mass \(\mu_t\), per-capita budget \(b_t\) initialized to \(k\), total budget \(\mu_tb_t\), and candidate affordability determined by \(\sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_t\min\{b_t,\rho\}=1\). Residual type budgets define \(p\), and dependent rounding produces \(X\) while preserving the MES committee in every realization; the objective is to compute any feasible \((p,X)\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 4.1 through weighted BW-MES and Proposition 3.6 through type-compressed Random Dictator. It leaves the paper's axiom relations, examples, Theorem 5.3 on BW-GCR, and the remaining open questions without direct mirrors.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can weighted BW-MES be proved polynomial in \(m\), \(|T|\), and the encoding length of \(\mu\)?
  • Does the type-compressed construction also guarantee continuous EJR\({}^+\)?
  • Can continuous GFS, Strong UFS, and ex-post FJR be achieved simultaneously?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is that this paper has a natural high-multiplicity mirror, although it offers algorithmic rather than hardness anchors. Its finite population is not already continuous: the paper’s fractional committees and lotteries continuize the outcome, while voters and EJR coalitions remain finite. The mirror makes the population itself continuous.

My lead anchor is Theorem 4.1, proved in this paper. It states that BW-MES, Algorithm 1, outputs a randomized committee that is simultaneously ex-ante GFS, ex-ante Strong UFS, and ex-post EJR, and that the algorithm can be implemented in polynomial time. The theorem relies on previously known facts that MES produces an EJR committee and that fractional committees can be rounded to lotteries, but the combined result is proved here.

The natural continuous problem is Continuous BW-MES Feasibility. An instance consists of candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), a finite list of approval types \(T\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over those types. Type \(t\) has approval set \(A_t\subseteq C\), and \(\mu_t\) is its population mass. A coalition is represented by a submass vector \(z\) with \(0\le z_t\le\mu_t\). Write \(U(z)=\bigcup_{z_t>0}A_t\), \(M(z)=\sum_tz_t\), and \(I(z)=\bigcap_{z_t>0}A_t\).

The solution is a fractional committee \(p\in[0,1]^C\), with \(\sum_{c\in C}p_c=k\), together with a lottery \(X\) over integral \(k\)-committees such that \(p_c\) is the probability that \(c\) is selected. It must satisfy, for every coalition \(z\),

\( \sum_{c\in U(z)}p_c\ge\sum_t z_t\min\{k,|A_t|\} \)

for continuous GFS; for every type \(t\) and every \(q\le\mu_t\),

\( \sum_{c\in A_t}p_c\ge\min\{qk,|A_t|\} \)

for continuous Strong UFS; and, for every \(\ell\in[k]\), every coalition with \(M(z)\ge\ell/k\) and \(|I(z)|\ge\ell\), every committee in the support of \(X\) must contain at least \(\ell\) approved candidates for some positive-mass type in that coalition. This is the direct mass version of EJR’s “some voter in the group” clause.

The objective is feasibility: find such a lottery and fractional committee. This is not merely a restatement of fractional committees. The new object is \(\mu\), and the discrete threshold \(\ell n/k\) has become the population-mass threshold \(\ell/k\). If \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), the instance is exactly the high-multiplicity form of a finite profile with \(n_t\) voters of type \(t\).

The plausible Class-A algorithm is a type-compressed BW-MES. Give each unit of population an initial budget \(k\), so type \(t\) holds total budget \(\mu_tk\). A candidate \(c\) is affordable at threshold \(\rho\) when

\( \sum_{t:c\in A_t}\mu_t\min\{b_t,\rho\}=1 \),

where \(b_t\) is the remaining per-capita budget of type \(t\). Add candidates as in MES, then spend residual type budgets on approved unselected candidates to form \(p\), and use dependent rounding while forcing the MES committee into every realized committee. All operations are over the \(\tau\) types rather than the \(n\) voters, suggesting a running time polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the encoding length of \(\mu\).

This is exactly the kind of result the authors should recognize as their problem: no approval information is discarded, and mass replaces only the irrelevant identity-level repetition. The main proof obligation is a weighted version of the paper’s MES and payment arguments. I would expect this mirror to be tractable, but I would not claim that Theorem 4.1 transfers automatically without proving those weighted lemmas.

A second, cleaner anchor is Proposition 3.6, also proved in this paper. It states that Random Dictator computes, in polynomial time, a randomized committee whose fractional committee is ex-ante GFS. For the continuous mirror, call the problem Continuous Random-Dictator GFS. The input is again \((C,k,T,\mu)\). For each type \(t\), choose a most-preferred \(k\)-committee \(W_t\), and output the lottery assigning probability \(\mu_t\) to \(W_t\). Its fractional committee is \(p=\sum_t\mu_t\mathbf 1_{W_t}\).

For every coalition \(z\), every type in its support contributes a committee containing \(\min\{k,|A_t|\}\) candidates from its own approval set, which is contained in \(U(z)\). Hence the lottery satisfies

\( \sum_{c\in U(z)}p_c\ge\sum_t z_t\min\{k,|A_t|\} \).

This continuous problem is unambiguously in Class A: the lottery has at most \(\tau\) support committees and can be constructed in polynomial time in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the input precision. It is a narrower result than the BW-MES mirror, but it establishes the population continuization cleanly even if the weighted BW-MES proof encounters difficulties.

A convincing regime is a large public committee election in which millions of residents repeatedly use a relatively small collection of approval ballots: for example, recurring neighbourhood, union, professional, or issue-based approval patterns over a moderate set of public projects. Then \(n\) is enormous while the number of distinct ballots \(\tau\) is much smaller. A type is precisely an approval set, since approvals are the only voter attribute used by the paper. Mass is the fraction of residents of that type. There are no invented individual prices or budgets; the only budgets are the paper’s endogenous MES budgets, which can naturally be assigned per unit of population.

The mirror therefore covers the paper’s central Theorem 4.1 and, more securely, Proposition 3.6. It does not claim that every result in the paper should be continuized. In particular, the fractional committee and lottery remain outcome-side objects because ex-ante fairness is part of the original question; that is a co-modification forced by the paper’s problem, not a substitution of outcome continuity for population continuity.

The weakest point is the combined BW-MES claim. The paper proves its theorem for individually indexed voters, and the continuum version needs a genuine weighted-MES argument, especially for the GFS payment bound and the interpretation of EJR’s existential voter clause. Also, polynomiality is only in the explicit type count \(\tau\); if all \(2^m-1\) approval types are supplied, no compression miracle is being claimed. The Random-Dictator mirror survives these objections and gives a fully precise positive case, while the stronger BW-MES mirror is a credible Class-A research problem rather than an overstated transferred theorem.

The natural follow-up questions are whether continuous EJR\({}^+\) also follows, whether continuous Strong UFS and FJR can coexist with GFS, whether the type-compressed algorithms admit a formal discrete-to-continuous approximation theorem, and whether welfare or Pareto-efficiency objectives can be optimized over the feasible lottery polytope.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that Proposition 3.6 is not a worthwhile continuous mirror. If \(n_t\) voters have approval type \(t\), Random Dictator already produces

\[ p=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \mathbf 1_{W_i} =\sum_t \frac{n_t}{n}\mathbf 1_{W_t}. \]

Replacing \(n_t/n\) by \(\mu_t\) is therefore an exact regrouping of identical voters. There is no search, optimization, separation problem, or nontrivial feasibility boundary: each type independently chooses a favorite committee, and \(\mu\) merely supplies the lottery weights. The GFS proof survives pointwise. A public-election interpretation makes the compression plausible, but does not give this anchor new computational content.

Theorem 4.1 is harder to defeat. Its best mirror would use weighted MES, with type \(t\) carrying mass \(\mu_t\), and define continuous EJR by requiring that every cohesive submass contain a positive-mass type receiving the required representation. That is a natural high-multiplicity model: approvals are the voters’ complete relevant types, and the weighted MES affordability test is well-defined. The theorem’s payment argument and the ex-post condition would require new weighted proofs, but those are legitimate computational questions rather than formal obstacles.

One could object that the lottery and fractional committee were already present in the paper, but that would confuse outcome continuity with population continuity. One could also object that weighted EJR is a redefinition, but the programme explicitly permits the natural mass version. Nor can the public-ballot regime be dismissed as lacking multiplicity: millions of voters sharing a modest number of approval types is precisely a sensible high-multiplicity instance.

Thus the negative case can disqualify Random Dictator as a substantive anchor, reducing it to an encoding identity. It cannot honestly defeat the weighted BW-MES mirror. The universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists is therefore too strong; the paper has at least one credible Class-A population-continuation question, even though the proponent was right to present its transfer as requiring proof rather than as automatic.
