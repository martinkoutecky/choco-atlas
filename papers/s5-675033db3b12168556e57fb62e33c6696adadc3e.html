The case FOR (proponent)

There is no qualifying named computational result in this paper: it contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a complexity result. The strongest hardness statement is in prose: finding a maximum-cardinality weakly stable matching for SMTI is NP-hard, with the claim attributed to Manlove et al. [4]. The paper also cites hardness for a restricted case with ties on one side, at the ends of lists, of length two. Those are useful leads, but they are not numbered results of this paper. The reported \(O(n\log n)\) behavior for SML2 is an inference from experiments, not a theorem. So strictly, there is no eligible anchor for the requested anchor-backed case.

The most plausible positive lead, if the cited hardness statement is allowed as an unnumbered anchor, is a continuous version of SMTI’s maximum-cardinality weakly stable matching problem. Consider a large market with two sides, such as applicants and one-person positions, where many agents share the same acceptability and preference ranking over categories on the other side. Let \(\mu_i\) and \(\nu_j\) be the rational masses of the two sides’ types. A solution assigns mass \(x_{ij}\) of type \(i\) to type \(j\), plus unmatched masses \(u_i\) and \(v_j\), respecting each type’s supply. It maximizes \(\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}\). Every unit of mass receives one partner; only the aggregate amount assigned between types is fractional.

For each mutually acceptable pair of types \((i,j)\), define \(A_{ij}\) as the mass of type \(i\) that is unmatched or strictly prefers \(j\) to its assigned partner, and \(B_{ij}\) analogously for type \(j\). Weak stability requires \(A_{ij}=0\) or \(B_{ij}=0\) for every such pair: otherwise positive masses on both sides could block. This is a precise continuous optimization question over rational type distributions. A large cohort market with \(N\) agents and relatively few preference categories, \(\tau\ll N\), is a plausible high-multiplicity regime. The authors could reasonably recognize this as their same maximum-cardinality weak-stability objective under clone symmetry, though their paper itself ranks named individuals rather than categories.

I would expect a parameterized result first: for fixed \(\tau\), enumerate which side must have zero blocking mass for each type pair and solve the resulting linear programs. This gives a \(2^{O(\tau^2)}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) approach. For unbounded \(\tau\), whether the problem is tractable or continuum-specifically hard is open; I would not claim that the paper’s discrete NP-hardness transfers, since fractional mass assignments can change the optimum. Further questions include whether the restricted one-sided-tie hardness survives, and whether the dependence on \(\tau\) can be made polynomial.

The case’s weakest point is the mismatch between named-individual preferences in the paper and preferences over categories in this mirror, alongside the change from indivisible matchings to mass assignments. The type-based market makes a credible high-multiplicity problem, but that is a proposed analogue—not a numbered result of the paper.

The SMTI maximum-cardinality problem is the only substantial candidate raised. Its best high-multiplicity version would group interchangeable applicants and positions, rank counterpart categories, and represent assignments by masses \(x_{ij}\). That is a coherent continuous matching problem. It also changes the paper’s named-person rankings: if those identities remain relevant, types must retain rankings over named counterparts, and a small fixed type space no longer captures the usual large-market regime. The paper’s random-ranking experiments provide no evidence about the category-based regime.

That is a case against attributing this mirror to the paper’s results, not a case that the mirror is unworthy. The proposed finite-type model is a sensible clone-market question, and the stated \(2^{O(\tau^2)}\operatorname{poly}(L)\) enumeration appears to be a valid parameterized starting point. Parameterized algorithms are within the programme’s scope. I cannot honestly dismiss this candidate by saying the answer is easy, or by treating prior analytic matching work as having settled its computational questions.
