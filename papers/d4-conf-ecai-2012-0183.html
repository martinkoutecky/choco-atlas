The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is the programme’s anchor requirement: this paper has no named result about the complexity of a computational problem. Theorem 1 characterizes which postulates optimistic and pessimistic semantics satisfy. Propositions 2 and 3 identify redundancies, and Proposition 1’s existence results are attributed to earlier work. The paper mentions algorithms for computing possibility distributions but refers elsewhere for them. None of these is a complexity result whose continuous-population counterpart this programme could chart.

A plausible population scenario would be a large consultation in which people report comparative statements about possible worlds. Give each person a complete report as their type, then assign mass to the types. But the paper represents an information base \(\Gamma\) as a set: repeating a statement does not change it. Requiring the semantics to satisfy every statement with positive mass therefore makes the masses irrelevant; only the support matters. To make proportions matter, one must add a threshold, weighted objective, or rule for resolving conflicting reports. That could motivate a new study of collective preference representation, but it adds the central aggregation problem that this paper does not address. The representation theorem itself contains no population whose behavior could be continuized.

The more promising computational question—finding a least- or most-specific possibility distribution from comparative constraints—does not repair the mismatch. It concerns computation over the paper’s finite set of worlds, and the paper presents no complexity claim for it. Adding a population of report types either leaves that task unchanged when reports are combined as a set, or turns it into a new weighted aggregation problem when mass affects the answer.

So the proposed mirrors—Theorem 1, a panel of reporters, and a computational study of distribution construction—do not supply an eligible anchor. A multi-agent extension may be worthwhile on its own, but on the material in this paper it is not a continuous mirror of a computational result.
