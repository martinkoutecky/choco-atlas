The continuous mirror question

Given finite good types \(G\), complete agent types \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(\kappa_g\in[0,1]\), and additive leveled valuations \(v_\theta\), compute an exact allocation kernel \(x_{\theta,S}\ge0\) over \(S\subseteq G\) satisfying \(\sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{\theta,S}=\mu_\theta\), \(\sum_{\theta,S\ni g}x_{\theta,S}=\kappa_g\), and support-wise EFXWC: whenever \(x_{\theta,S},x_{\theta',R}>0\), every \(g\in R\setminus S\) satisfies \(v_\theta(S\setminus R)\ge v_\theta((R\setminus S)\setminus\{g\})\). Determine whether this can be done with a succinct representation in time polynomial in the type counts and input bit-length, without expanding a common denominator.

The model it lives in

A continuum of agents is represented by complete valuation types \(\Theta\) with masses \(\mu\); each agent receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq G\), while \(x_{\theta,S}\) records the mass receiving it. Goods have per-capita supplies \(\kappa_g\), the objective is feasibility, and fairness is support-wise EFXWC.

The objection that survived

The continuous problem lacks a fixed succinct output or complexity model: \(x\) ranges over exponentially many bundle columns, and the finite \(O(n|T|^2)\) potential argument does not itself yield denominator-independent computation.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 6 and the leveled-preference EFXWC existence problem. It leaves the duality results, the \(4/11\)- and \(1/3\)-MMS guarantees, counterexamples, and other structural claims without a direct computational population-continuum mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can one compute a sparse or implicit \(x\) in time polynomial in \(|\Theta|\), \(|G|\), and the input bit-length, independently of the common denominator?
  • Does support-wise EFXWC admit a finite column-generation, flow, or separation formulation?
  • Does the continuous problem remain Class A, or is it hard specifically because fairness is sensitive to every positively supported bundle type?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is a qualified yes. This paper contains a particularly clean high-multiplicity target: exclusive allocation of typed goods with copies. Its main limitation is that it has no named NP-hardness result of its own, so the best anchor is algorithmic rather than a hardness result.

My lead anchor is Theorem 6, proved in the paper. It states:

There is an algorithm that always finds an EFXWC exclusive allocation for goods with copies when preferences are leveled, with running time \(O(n|T|^2)\).

Here \(n\) is the number of agents, \(T\) is the set of good types, and “leveled” means that every agent strictly prefers every bundle of larger cardinality: if \(|B_1|>|B_2|\), then \(v_i(B_1)>v_i(B_2)\). EFXWC means that for every pair of agents \(i,j\), and every good \(g\in A_j\setminus A_i\), \(v_i(A_i\setminus A_j)\ge v_i((A_j\setminus A_i)\setminus\{g\})\).

A natural high-multiplicity regime is a large university or licensing system. There may be millions of students, but only finitely many complete student types: the type includes the student’s entire additive valuation vector over the available courses or licences, together with any eligibility or priority information used by the allocation problem. Let \(\Theta\) be these agent types, with population distribution \(\mu_\theta\). Let \(G\) be the finite set of good types, and let \(\kappa_g\in[0,1]\) be the number of copies of good \(g\) per agent. Thus \(N\mu_\theta\) students have type \(\theta\), and \(N\kappa_g\) copies of course \(g\) exist. We have \(N\gg |\Theta|,|G|\).

The continuous problem I would hand to a prover is:

Continuum-Leveled EFXWC Search. An instance consists of finite \(G\) and \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita supplies \(\kappa_g\in[0,1]\), and additive valuations \(v_\theta\) satisfying leveled preferences. Find a measurable allocation of population mass to integral bundles, represented by variables \(x_{\theta,S}\ge0\) for \(S\subseteq G\), such that

\( \sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{\theta,S}=\mu_\theta \)

for every \(\theta\), and

\( \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{S\ni g}x_{\theta,S}=\kappa_g \)

for every \(g\). In addition, whenever \(x_{\theta,S}>0\), \(x_{\theta',R}>0\), and \(g\in R\setminus S\), require

\( v_\theta(S\setminus R)\ge v_\theta((R\setminus S)\setminus\{g\}) \).

The output is the allocation kernel \(x\). Each individual still receives an indivisible bundle \(S\); only the mass of agents receiving each bundle is fractional. The objective is feasibility—find any EFXWC allocation—exactly matching Theorem 6’s objective.

This is recognisably the authors’ problem, not a softened welfare problem. It preserves goods with copies, exclusivity, additive valuations, leveled preferences, and EFXWC. Only the population is continuized, and the copy counts are normalized into per-capita supplies, which is the necessary co-modification.

The existence part already follows for rational inputs by a high-multiplicity blow-up. Choose \(N\) clearing all denominators, create \(N\mu_\theta\) agents of each type and \(N\kappa_g\) copies of each good, apply Theorem 6, and aggregate the resulting allocation into \(x\). The genuinely new computational question is whether this can be done directly in time polynomial in \(|\Theta|\), \(|G|\), and the input length, rather than in time proportional to the potentially enormous denominator \(N\). I would expect this restricted mirror to be Class A: the finite algorithm is based on local exchanges and a finite preference potential, and those exchanges appear amenable to type-level mass operations or a flow formulation.

A second, supporting anchor is Theorem 2, also proved in the paper. It states that for every comparison criterion \(f\), an exclusive allocation \(A\) is \(f_{\mathrm{WC}}\)-fair with respect to \(v\) if and only if its dual allocation \(A^\circ\) is \(f_{\mathrm{WC}}^c\)-fair with respect to \(v^\circ=-v\). The dual allocation complements every bundle, \(A_i^\circ=T\setminus A_i\), and a type with \(k_t\) copies becomes a type with \(n-k_t\) copies.

Its continuous counterpart is:

Mass-Dual Fair Allocation. Given the same \((G,\Theta,\mu,\kappa)\) and a comparison criterion \(f\), find an \(f_{\mathrm{WC}}\)-fair allocation \(x\), or determine that none exists. The dual instance has the same agent distribution \(\mu\), valuation \(-v_\theta\), per-capita supplies \(1-\kappa_g\), and allocation

\( x^\circ_{\theta,G\setminus S}=x_{\theta,S} \).

The continuous question is whether \(x\) is fair exactly when \(x^\circ\) is fair under the complementary criterion. This is a precise mass-level version of the paper’s goods/chores duality: the population is continuous, but bundles remain integral. I expect exact status transfer here—tractability or hardness on one side transfers to the other, with no new continuum-specific obstruction. The finite Corollary 1 is a special case of this complement operation.

The weakest point is the algorithmic compression. Theorem 6’s proof uses an integer-valued potential and a sequence of individual-agent swaps bounded by \(O(n|T|^2)\). In the continuous problem, mass can move in arbitrarily small quantities, so the integer potential argument does not itself yield a polynomial algorithm in the binary encoding of \(\mu\) and \(\kappa\). Exact support-wise EFXWC is also deliberately strong: it requires fairness between every pair of bundle types occurring with positive mass, not merely almost-everywhere fairness under a random pair.

So I would not claim that Theorem 6 already proves a polynomial continuous algorithm. I would claim something narrower and defensible: the paper’s goods-with-copies model has a natural, socially plausible high-multiplicity regime; its finite theorem gives the continuous mirror existence for rational instances; and the resulting type-compressed search problem is a genuine Class A candidate rather than a change of subject.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is weak here, and I cannot honestly defeat the first anchor.

Theorem 6 is a genuine computational result, and its population axis is meaningful. A large course-allocation or licensing system can plausibly contain many agents sharing a complete valuation type, while supplies scale as per-capita quantities \(\kappa_g\). For rational \(\mu_\theta\) and \(\kappa_g\), choosing a common denominator and applying Theorem 6 to the resulting finite instance gives exactly the proposed mass allocation \(x\). This is not merely an analogy: it is a legitimate high-multiplicity version of the paper’s problem.

There are serious weaknesses in the proposed formulation, but they do not defeat the better mirror. EFXWC is support-sensitive rather than measure-continuous: a bundle type occurring with arbitrarily small positive mass must still satisfy the same pairwise condition as a large type. An “almost-everywhere” interpretation would lose the paper’s individual fairness guarantee, while literal fairness for all agents is not determined by \(\mu\) on null sets. Moreover, \(x_{\theta,S}\) has exponentially many possible bundle columns, and the proponent has not specified a succinct output or oracle model. These are real modelling and complexity gaps, but they are repairable; they do not show that the high-multiplicity question is misguided.

The best attack is therefore narrower: Theorem 6’s finite existence theorem transfers mechanically by blow-up. The genuinely new task would be a denominator-independent, succinct algorithm for the mass allocation, and that is a new high-multiplicity fair-division problem rather than a continuous consequence already established by the paper. But that distinction does not make it unworthwhile under ChoCo’s programme; compression of precisely this sort is part of the programme.

Theorem 2 is easier to dismiss as an anchor. Its continuous version is just the pointwise involution

\[ x^\circ_{\theta,G\setminus S}=x_{\theta,S},\qquad \kappa_g^\circ=1-\kappa_g, \]

with valuation negation. It neither concerns computational complexity nor makes the population continuous. The mass-level duality is an exact post-processing bijection, already implicit at every finite population size. A search problem for EFXWC allocations could still be posed, but that would be a new high-multiplicity problem, not a computational mirror supplied by Theorem 2.

Thus the negative case can reject Theorem 2 as non-computational and criticize the formulation of the Theorem 6 mirror. It cannot honestly establish that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists: the leveled-preference allocation problem remains a plausible, socially recognizable Class A candidate.
