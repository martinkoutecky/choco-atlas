The other numbered theorems do not supply such an anchor either: Theorem 2 establishes allocation properties, Theorem 3 establishes rationality of the allocation, and Theorem 4 gives equivalence with the interval-valued Shapley value for complete communication graphs. The paper’s continuous ingredients are fuzzy participation degrees, hesitation, and interval-valued payoffs over a finite set of players. Its population remains \(N=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). A many-agent version might be imagined, but extending the A–T solution to a continuum of agents and communication links would itself require defining a new model; it is not a continuous-population computational question established by this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raised no computational anchor, and the paper supplies none to rescue: Theorem 1 concerns monotonicity in the confidence parameter \(\lambda\); Theorems 2–4 concern allocation properties and equivalence. The A–T formula is a finite sum over admissible tuples, but the paper gives no complexity classification for computing it. A complexity question about evaluating that formula could be worth asking, but it would be a new question rather than a continuous mirror of a computational result in this paper.

The strongest candidate mirror would use many interchangeable participants in repeated communication structures, with types recording each participant’s role and payoff data. That can be a sensible high-multiplicity regime. For independent copies of a fixed structure, though, the allocation reduces to the same finite A–T calculation for each copy; a distribution over a finite library of structures just aggregates those results. If the copies are coupled into one large network, preserving the links and coalition worths that determine each participant’s allocation may require types to encode much of that network. Coarsening those types risks losing exactly the information the A–T solution uses.

A graphon or other continuum-network version might still be worthwhile research. It would need to supply a new continuous allocation model and computational question, since the paper’s finite graph definition and axiomatic results do not establish one. So the case against a *mirror of this paper* is strong; the universal claim that no useful continuous extension could be built is weaker and cannot be established from the paper alone.
