The case FOR (proponent)

There is no qualifying computational anchor in this paper. It reports no named result that puts a problem in P, proves it hard, or gives an algorithmic formulation that the authors compute with. Its numbered Propositions establish semantic and postulate properties of the merging operators, not computational results. So I cannot honestly give the continuous question below as a mirror of a qualifying anchor.

The nearest supporting result is Proposition 7, proved in the paper: for \(d\in\{\mathrm{wCh},\mathrm{Ch}\}\), the Sum-based operators \(\Delta_{(d,\mathrm{Sum})}\) and \(\Delta_{(d,\mathrm{Sum})}^{\preceq}\) satisfy the majority postulate Maj, while the corresponding Max-based operators satisfy Arb. Maj says that if \(E\) contains a PLP \(P\), then adding \(n\) more copies of \(P\) makes the weak change from \(P\) to any selected merge result tend to zero as \(n\) grows. This makes source replication a meaningful part of the paper’s model, but it is not a complexity result.

A plausible, unanchored continuous question would be *Minimum-Change PLP Population Merge*. Imagine a very large network of clinics or monitoring sources using a bounded set of recurring, complete PLP templates. A type \(t\) is one entire canonical PLP \(P_t\), including its syntax and probability bounds; \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of sources using that template. There are \(n\) sources but only \(\tau\) distinct PLP types, with \(\mu_t=n_t/n\).

Given the finite-support distribution \(\{(P_t,\mu_t)\}_{t=1}^{\tau}\), find a canonical, satisfiable merged PLP \(Q\) with \(\Gamma(Q)=\bigcup_t\Gamma(P_t)\), minimizing
\[ D_\mu(Q)=\sum_t \mu_t \sum_{\theta\in\Gamma(P_t)} \left( |\ell_{t\theta}-\ell_\theta| + |u_{t\theta}-u_\theta| \right). \]
Here \([\ell_{t\theta},u_{t\theta}]\) is the tight interval for conditional event \(\theta\) entailed by \(P_t\), and \([\ell_\theta,u_\theta]\) is its interval in \(Q\). As in the paper’s weighted-combination operators, each output interval must be a convex combination of the contributing source intervals, using the same weights for its lower and upper endpoints. A solution is an optimal rational \(Q\) and its combination weights. For \(n\) sources with \(n_t\) of each type, this objective is the paper’s Sum change divided by \(n\); duplicating a type changes its mass and its contribution.

The authors could plausibly recognize this as their merging problem in a high-multiplicity regime: their input is already a multiset of PLPs, and Proposition 7 explicitly studies what happens as copies of a source accumulate. The proposed regime would be a population of sources with repeated full belief templates, not a claim that arbitrary individuals have identical beliefs.

I would expect the broad version’s complexity to remain open rather than claim tractability: checking consistency of \(Q\) involves its probabilistic models, and the paper provides no complexity analysis of that step. The weakest point is also the main qualification: this is a natural continuous variant of their source-multiplicity model, but the paper supplies no named computational result for it—or for the finite problem it extends. The mirror is plausible; the required computational anchor is absent.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no computational result here to mirror. The numbered propositions establish semantic facts about PLPs, consistency, change measures, and whether the proposed operators satisfy postulates. In particular, Proposition 7 proves a property of those operators; it gives no algorithm or complexity classification for computing a merge. On the paper’s results, there is no computational anchor.

Proposition 7 is the strongest foothold for a population reading: it studies what happens when copies of one source are added. But its majority postulate concerns the behavior of a merging operator under discrete source replication. It does not analyze the cost of finding a merge. Treating the copies as mass is a sensible interpretation of that setup, but it turns the postulate into a new optimization question rather than a continuous version of a computational result in the paper.

The proponent’s best model is also the most faithful one: take a type to be the source’s complete canonical PLP, including its syntax and bounds. That respects the paper’s emphasis on which conditional events a source explicitly states. But if sources vary substantially, their complete PLPs give little reason to expect small support. If many sources share exact PLPs, the proposed objective is simply the Sum-change objective with duplicate counts collected into rational weights. The model is coherent, and it may be useful to study how to compute weighted PLP merges. Its case as a *mirror of this paper*, though, depends on adding a computational problem the paper never analyzes and selecting one operator and objective from several alternatives.

So the negative case is strongest as a scope judgment, not as a proof that the proposed optimization is uninteresting. This paper can motivate a new computational study of weighted PLP merging, but the supplied results do not give that study a named computational target to continuize. The case is weakest if “worthwhile mirror” is allowed to include a worthwhile new problem merely suggested by the paper.
