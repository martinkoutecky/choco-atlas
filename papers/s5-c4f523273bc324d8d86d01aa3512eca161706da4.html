What the mirror covers

The proposed anchors are Theorems 1 and 2, on density and category of individual manipulability. Proposition 1 constructs a locally non-manipulable rule for replicated economies, but makes no computational claim; none of these results supplies a continuous computational mirror.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest possible positive intuition is a continuous exchange market for a large cohort of households. A type would be a complete preference and endowment pair \((u_t,\omega_t)\); mass \(\mu_t\) would be that type’s share of households. A rule would assign per-household bundles while respecting aggregate supplies. But in an atomless population, one household’s report has no effect on the outcome. To study manipulation, I would have to ask whether a positive mass of a type can profit by changing reports together. That is a coherent population-continuous research question, but it changes the paper’s individual-manipulation question.

The closest result is Theorem 1, proved in this paper: for any min-stable rule, the set \(M^\varphi\) of economies at which the rule is manipulable is dense in \(D\), the set of economies with a Walrasian allocation in which every agent receives positive money. Theorem 2, also proved here, says \(M^\varphi\) is nowhere meagre in \(D\). These are significant topological results, but neither asserts a computational complexity bound or classification. The paper’s other numbered results do not supply a computational anchor either.

So I cannot honestly give the requested continuous problem *per anchor*: the paper contains no named computational result of the required kind. The main weakness of the possible mirror is also substantive: replacing one agent’s deviation with a profitable deviation by positive mass is a change to the strategic question, and the paper’s results do not analyze it. The proposed exchange-market scenario may motivate a new computational study, but it does not make this paper a strong anchor for the continuization programme.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I would reject this as a ChoCo mirror. Theorem 1 gives density of manipulable economies; Theorem 2 strengthens that with a Baire-category claim. Neither asks for an algorithm, classifies a computational problem, or gives a complexity bound. The paper’s object is whether manipulability is topologically pervasive for any min-stable rule, across a large function space of preferences. That is not a computational result waiting to be lifted to a continuous population.

The strongest population model is still coherent: let types be complete preference–endowment pairs \((u_t,\omega_t)\), give them rational masses, and scale supplies per capita. The paper’s replica-economy result supports taking repeated agents seriously; it is evidence for the regime, not a novelty collision or an objection. But in an atomless society an individual report has zero effect. A meaningful manipulation question would need a positive-mass coalition or a flow of reports. That change alone need not invalidate a mirror, but it leaves the paper’s theorems behind: they say nothing about coalition manipulation or its computation.

Trying to recover those theorems exposes the harder obstacle. The rule \(\varphi\) is an arbitrary map from profiles of preferences to allocations, and preferences range over an infinite-dimensional function space. There is no finite input encoding for the rule or the preferences, hence no defined running-time question. Restricting utilities to a finite rational parameterization could supply one, but it changes the domain on which the density and category results were proved. The paper itself notes that the conclusions may fail on subdomains with budget-balanced voluntary VCG rules. Keeping the original domain preserves the topological question but offers no computational input; making it computationally manageable risks losing the result.

One could study the complexity of finding a profitable mass deviation for a specified, effectively represented exchange mechanism. That might be worthwhile research, but it is a new problem whose mechanism, deviation model, and utility encoding would all have to be supplied from outside this paper. The proponent’s market scenario makes repeated types plausible; it does not turn Theorems 1 or 2 into computational anchors.

I cannot establish the literal universal claim that no scenario could ever make such a follow-up worthwhile. The honest negative case is narrower: none of the paper’s anchors survives as a ChoCo complexity mirror, and the most promising replacement is a new research lead rather than a continuous counterpart of the paper’s results.
