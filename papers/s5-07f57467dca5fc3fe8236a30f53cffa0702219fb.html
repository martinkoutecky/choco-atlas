The strongest positive lead I can offer is therefore unanchored: continuous PAMSAC winner determination. Let \(C\) be the candidates, \(k\) the committee size, and let the society be a rational distribution \(\mu\) over approval types \(A\subseteq C\), with support size \(\tau\). For each proposed committee \(W\) of size \(k\), choose a mass split \(x_{A,B}\ge 0\), where \(A\setminus W\subseteq B\subseteq A\) and \(\sum_B x_{A,B}=\mu_A\). This splits each type’s mass across ballots formed by removing some approvals for elected candidates. Apply CFAT to each residual ballot \(B\): each \(D\subseteq B\) receives mass \(\nu_D=\sum_{A,B:D\subseteq B}x_{A,B}/2^{|B|}\). Let \(q_c=\sum_{D\ni c}\nu_D\) and give a transformed ballot \(D\) load \(\ell_W(D)=\sum_{c\in W\cap D}1/q_c\). The objective is \(J(W,x)=\sum_D\nu_D\ell_W(D)^2\), with infinite objective if an elected candidate has no approvers. Choose \(W\) and a feasible split minimizing \(J\), then apply the paper’s approval-count tie-breaks: maximize \(\sum_{A,B}x_{A,B}|B\cap W|\), then \(\sum_A\mu_A|A\cap W|\). A solution is the selected committee and its optimal mass-removal plan.

The regime is a large election or membership committee where many people cast the same approval ballot: \(N\) is large while \(\tau\) is comparatively small. Types are complete approval ballots; mass is the fraction of society with each ballot. This is plausible as the authors’ problem: Section 3.4 explicitly allows arbitrary fractional division of voters for approval removal, and Section 3.7 discusses the cost of finding the best removals. Rational type masses also correspond to integer ballot counts after clearing denominators, so this is a natural high-multiplicity formulation. It covers the computational task of selecting a PAMSAC committee, not the paper’s criterion claims.

But this paper establishes no discrete hardness result to transfer, so that expectation is a conjecture, not an anchor-based conclusion. Further questions include whether the removal optimization for fixed \(W\) is tractable, and how winner determination depends on \(m\), \(\tau\), and the input bit length.
