The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), a union map \(g:T\to[m]\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\), an explicitly represented differentiable rational polynomial \(F\) on the feasible mass vectors, a type \(t\) with \(\mu_t>0\), and accuracy and confidence parameters \(\varepsilon,\alpha\), estimate \(\Phi_t^O=\mathbb{E}[\partial_t F(Z)]\) within additive error \(\varepsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). Here \(K\) is uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), \(R\) is uniform among the \(K\)-element subsets of \([m]\setminus\{g(t)\}\), \(U\) is uniform on \([0,1]\), and \(Z_s=\mu_s\mathbf{1}[g(s)\in R]+U\mu_s\mathbf{1}[g(s)=g(t)]\).

The model it lives in

The society is the distribution \(\mu\) over complete agent types, each assigned to a union. Coalition worth is \(F(z)\) for type-mass vector \(z\); a unit of type \(t\) has limiting marginal worth \(\partial_tF(z)\). The target is its expected marginal worth under the continuous Owen predecessor law, estimated by randomized sampling. The parallel Banzhaf–Owen limit uses independent inclusion of other unions and the half-mass vector for the target union.

The objection that survived

The propositions concern finite games and do not supply \(F\); the continuous question therefore requires restricting to smooth aggregate games, and the paper’s examples do not establish that restriction as a regime for those applications.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the additive estimation guarantees in Propositions 3.2 and 3.5 for Owen and Banzhaf–Owen values in smooth type-aggregate games. It does not cover arbitrary TU-games, the paper’s particular finite examples, or its empirical comparisons.

Open questions for a prover

  • Prove convergence, with explicit rates, from finite Owen and Banzhaf–Owen values of \(v_n(S)=nF((|S\cap t|/n)_{t\in T})\) to their continuous type-mass limits.
  • Establish finite-bit running-time bounds for sampling and evaluating the continuous estimators, including the representation of range and smoothness bounds.
  • Determine whether continuous stratification improves variance over direct sampling for these limiting predecessor laws.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a high-multiplicity approximation of the paper’s marginal-contribution averages. The paper contains no named NP-hardness, polynomial-time, or parameterized-complexity result. Its best computational anchors are its proved guarantees for randomized estimation.

My lead anchor is Proposition 3.2, proved in this paper. For a TU-game with a priori unions, it defines \(w_{i,kh}\) as the range of player \(i\)’s marginal contributions in stratum \(C_{kh}\), and \(w_i=\max_{k,h}w_{i,kh}\). It states that for \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1]\), a total sample size
\[ \ell\ge \frac{\ln(2/\alpha)w_i^2}{2\varepsilon^2} \]
ensures
\[ \Pr\!\left(\left|O_i^{st}-O_i\right|\ge\varepsilon\right)\le\alpha, \]
where \(O_i^{st}\) is the paper’s stratified-sampling estimate of player \(i\)’s Owen value.

A second, substantive anchor is Proposition 3.5, also proved here. With the same definition of \(w_i\), it states that the same sample-size condition ensures
\[ \Pr\!\left(\left|BzO_i^{st}-BzO_i\right|\ge\varepsilon\right)\le\alpha, \]
for the paper’s stratified-sampling estimate of player \(i\)’s Banzhaf–Owen value. These are parallel guarantees for two distinct coalition-value notions, not complexity-class results.

Here is a concrete population mirror. Fix a finite set of unions \([m]\) and a finite set of complete agent types \(T\). A type \(t\) includes its union \(g(t)\), preferences or claims, costs, and every other feature the game uses. The continuous society is a rational distribution \(\mu\), with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). A coalition is described by its type-mass vector \(z\), where \(0\le z_t\le\mu_t\). Let its worth be \(F(z)\), for an explicitly represented differentiable rational polynomial \(F\) with \(F(0)=0\).

This has a direct high-multiplicity interpretation. In a finite society of size \(n\), take \(n\mu_t\) interchangeable agents of type \(t\), and define the worth of coalition \(S\) as
\[ v_n(S)=nF\!\left(\left(\frac{|S\cap t|}{n}\right)_{t\in T}\right). \]
Adding one agent of type \(t\) then has limiting marginal worth \(\partial_tF(z)\). The continuous value is therefore a per-agent type value; the total assigned to type \(t\) is \(\mu_t\) times that value.

For type \(t\) in union \(j=g(t)\), define the continuous Owen value as follows. Choose \(K\) uniformly from \(\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), then choose \(R\subseteq[m]\setminus\{j\}\) uniformly among the \(K\)-element subsets. Independently choose \(U\) uniformly from \([0,1]\), and set
\[ Z_s=\mu_s\mathbf 1[g(s)\in R]+U\mu_s\mathbf 1[g(s)=j]. \]
The target value is
\[ \Phi_t^O=\mathbb E[\partial_tF(Z)]. \]
This is the large-population limit of the Owen predecessor-coalition calculation: other unions are included as whole unions, while the fraction of the target union preceding a member converges to a uniform fraction \(U\).

Continuous Owen-value estimation is a precise problem for Proposition 3.2 to mirror. Its instance is \(m,T,g,\mu,F\), a type \(t\) with \(\mu_t>0\), and rational accuracy and confidence parameters \(\varepsilon,\alpha\). Its solution is a randomized algorithm returning a rational estimate \(\widehat\Phi_t^O\) with
\[ \Pr\!\left(|\widehat\Phi_t^O-\Phi_t^O|\le\varepsilon\right)\ge1-\alpha. \]
When the derivative range over possible predecessor vectors is at most \(w\), sampling independent predecessor vectors and averaging their marginal values gives the Hoeffding sample bound in Proposition 3.2’s form. For a finite-bit implementation, one can sample \(U\) on a sufficiently fine rational grid; a supplied Lipschitz bound controls the resulting quadrature error.

For Proposition 3.5, define the continuous Banzhaf–Owen value using a different predecessor law. Independently include each other union in \(R\) with probability \(1/2\), and set
\[ Z_s=\mu_s\mathbf 1[g(s)\in R]+\frac12\mu_s\mathbf 1[g(s)=j], \qquad \Phi_t^{BO}=\mathbb E[\partial_tF(Z)]. \]
The half-mass of the target union is the limit of independently including each of its many members with probability \(1/2\). Continuous Banzhaf–Owen-value estimation has the same input and output guarantee as the Owen estimation problem, with \(\Phi_t^{BO}\) as its target. If the marginal-value range is at most \(w\), the same Hoeffding sample bound as Proposition 3.5 suffices.

For both questions I expect Class A for additive randomized approximation: the number of marginal-value evaluations is polynomial in \(1/\varepsilon\), \(\log(1/\alpha)\), and the encoded instance size, given explicit \(F\) and the stated range bounds. That does not establish exact polynomial-time computation. Exact aggregation over all predecessor coalitions remains a separate question, especially when the number of unions grows.

The regime is plausible when many interchangeable members belong to a small number of standing cooperatives or cost-sharing blocs. For example, a large producer network might have many members sharing the same union, production class, and contribution terms, while coalition worth depends on aggregate participation. Then \(n\) can be very large while the number \(\tau=|T|\) of distinct types and the number \(m\) of unions remain modest. Any genuine idiosyncrasy belongs in the type definition; grouping agents with different costs or claims would change the problem.

This mirror covers the paper’s two estimation results, not every TU-game or every numerical example. Its plausibility rests on retaining the paper’s central object—marginal contributions averaged over compatible predecessor coalitions—and taking the standard smooth, high-multiplicity limit. The sampling randomness here is part of those value definitions; the continuization is of the population.

The weakest point is that the paper allows arbitrary TU-games, while this mirror requires a smooth, type-aggregative worth function and a scaling under which discrete marginal differences converge to derivatives. Its country, council, and aircraft examples do not themselves establish a convincing many-agent, few-type regime. The mirror is strongest for the broader class of repeated cooperative settings, and its finite-population convergence and variance advantages would need to be proved rather than assumed.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that Propositions 3.2 and 3.5 estimate values of a finite game supplied by its coalition worths. They bound the error when averaging \(v(S\cup\{i\})-v(S)\) over compatible predecessor coalitions. Neither proposition gives a worst-case complexity result, and neither specifies how a continuous worth function should be obtained from the finite game.

That gap matters for both proposed mirrors. A finite game with type counts \(n_t\) reveals worths only at coalition-mass vectors whose coordinates are \(k_t/n\). Many continuous functions agree at every such point but have different derivatives at the fractional predecessor vectors used in \(\Phi_t^O\) and \(\Phi_t^{BO}\). So those derivative expectations are not determined by the paper’s input. Defining \(v_n(S)=nF((|S\cap t|/n)_t)\) resolves the ambiguity, but it adds a family of smooth, anonymous games and an explicit \(F\) that the propositions do not assume.

The same objection applies to the best improved mirrors: one could ask for exact or approximate computation of Owen and Banzhaf–Owen values for these aggregate games. Those are coherent questions, but they are questions about a new, restricted model. The paper’s numerical examples do not supply a common smooth aggregate representation: a weighted-majority worth is a threshold, while the airport game uses a maximum-cost rule. For threshold games in particular, the ordinary derivative limit can vanish away from the boundary, so preserving nontrivial voting power may require a separate boundary or noise scaling.

That is a serious scope objection, but it does not establish that no worthwhile mirror exists. A large cooperative with many interchangeable members and worth determined smoothly by aggregate production or claims is a plausible high-multiplicity setting; its continuous Owen-value question could be worthwhile. The airport example’s repeated movements even make multiplicity plausible. I therefore cannot honestly defeat every anchor across all scenarios: the paper itself does not define the continuous problem, but a recognizable and potentially useful continuization can be supplied.
