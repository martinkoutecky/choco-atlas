The continuous mirror question

Given finite complete agent types \(\Theta\) with masses \(\mu_\theta\), bad types \(O\) with per-capita supplies \(\rho_o\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_o\rho_o\le1\), and positive additive disutilities \(d_{\theta o}\), compute an assignment \(x_{\theta o}\ge0\) minimizing \(\sum_{\theta,o}x_{\theta o}\log d_{\theta o}\), subject to \(\sum_o x_{\theta o}\le\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta o}=\rho_o\), and return an atomless allocation realizing \(x\) that is \(m\)NDW\(^{-}_\infty\), Pareto efficient, and XEF.

The model it lives in

A capacitated transportation or min-cost-flow model over type masses: \(x_{\theta o}\) assigns mass of type \(\theta\) to bad \(o\), the objective is the logarithm of continuous Nash diswelfare, and each individual receives either \(\emptyset\) or one bad.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 7 in the positive-disutility, \(m\le n\) regime. It leaves the paper's other goods and bads results, impossibility theorems, general anti-monotone valuations, zero-disutility reduction issues, and multi-bad configuration problems untouched.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the mirror be extended to \(\sum_o\rho_o>1\) using configuration variables \(x_{\theta,B}\) while retaining a meaningful XEF constraint?
  • Does the resulting configuration pricing problem remain polynomial for structured additive disutilities?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is a narrow but genuine Class A mirror of Theorem 7, proved in this paper. The paper has no numbered NP-hardness theorem; its intractability statement about MNDW is an unnumbered citation to earlier work, so I would not use it as an anchor.

Theorem 7 states, in full: with \(m\le n\) bads and additive valuations, an XEF, Pareto-efficient, and \(m\)NDW\(^{-}\) allocation can be computed in \(O(n^3)\) time. Here \(m\)NDW\(^{-}\) means minimizing Nash diswelfare in the reduced problem over a largest set of agents that can simultaneously receive negatively valued items. The proof reduces the problem to a minimum-weight bipartite matching after negating valuations and taking logarithms.

My lead mirror is the following.

Call it Continuous Reduced Nash-Diswelfare Allocation. There is a population of total mass \(1\), divided among finitely many complete agent types \(\Theta\), with masses \(\mu_\theta\) satisfying \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta=1\). A type includes the agent’s entire additive disutility vector \(d_{\theta o}>0\) over a finite set \(O\) of recurring bad types. Thus an agent of type \(\theta\) receiving bad \(o\) has utility \(-d_{\theta o}\), and receiving nothing has utility \(0\).

There is a per-capita supply \(\rho_o\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\) of each bad type, with \(\sum_{o\in O}\rho_o\le1\). This is exactly the high-multiplicity translation of \(m\le n\): there are no more bad units than agents, so every agent can receive at most one bad.

The decision variable is \(x_{\theta o}\), the mass of type-\(\theta\) agents receiving bad \(o\). It must satisfy

\[ x_{\theta o}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{o\in O}x_{\theta o}\le\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}x_{\theta o}=\rho_o. \]

The remaining mass of type \(\theta\) receives the empty bundle. Importantly, each individual receives an integral bundle, either \(\emptyset\) or \(\{o\}\); only the aggregate assignment is fractional.

Among feasible assignments, minimize the continuous Nash diswelfare

\[ \operatorname{NDW}_{\infty}(x) = \exp\!\left( \frac{1}{r} \sum_{\theta,o}x_{\theta o}\log d_{\theta o} \right), \qquad r=\sum_o\rho_o. \]

This is the normalized geometric mean of the disutilities of the agents who receive bads, and is the natural high-multiplicity limit of the finite product objective. Equivalently, one minimizes the linear objective \(\sum_{\theta,o}x_{\theta o}\log d_{\theta o}\).

The requested output is such an \(x\), together with an atomless allocation realizing it, that is \(m\)NDW\(^{-}_\infty\), Pareto efficient, and XEF. XEF is preserved exactly: every nonempty individual bundle contains one bad, removing it leaves \(\emptyset\), whose utility \(0\) is at least the utility of every other bundle. The strict marginal condition holds because \(d_{\theta o}>0\).

This is a real high-multiplicity regime. Think of a large workforce receiving one recurring chore or shift per period. There may be millions of workers but only a few dozen role/preference types; the chore categories recur with frequencies \(\rho_o\). A type records all relevant disutilities and eligibility information, so agents of one type are genuinely interchangeable. The same model could describe repeated hospital shifts, municipal tasks, or service assignments.

The finite-to-continuous dictionary is exact. For \(N\) agents, \(n_\theta\) agents of type \(\theta\), and \(M_o\) copies of chore \(o\), set \(\mu_\theta=n_\theta/N\), \(\rho_o=M_o/N\), and \(x_{\theta o}=X_{\theta o}/N\). Conversely, rational masses can be multiplied by a common denominator to obtain a finite allocation. Thus this is not divisible-goods fair division: divisibility appears only in the population aggregate.

The expected classification is Class A. The optimization is a transportation problem:

\[ \min \sum_{\theta,o}x_{\theta o}\log d_{\theta o} \]

subject to the displayed mass and supply constraints. It can be solved by min-cost flow or a transportation LP in time polynomial in \(|\Theta|\), \(|O|\), and the encoding length. Pareto efficiency follows because a Pareto improvement would weakly reduce every assigned agent’s positive disutility and strictly reduce one, thereby lowering the logarithmic objective.

The authors should recognize this as their problem: nothing changes in the welfare objective, the additive valuation model, the XEF definition, or the \(m\le n\) regime. Their Hungarian matching proof is simply replaced by its high-multiplicity capacitated version, with named agents becoming type capacities and individual bads becoming supply masses.

The main weakness is also clear. In the \(m\le n\) regime, XEF is essentially automatic, so the mirror mainly tests the welfare-and-efficiency part of Theorem 7. It therefore gives a clean continuous optimization problem, but not yet a dramatic example of discrete hardness dissolving. The genuinely richer follow-up is to allow \(\sum_o\rho_o>1\), so agents may receive several bads, or to restore zero disutilities and the paper’s reduced-problem convention. Then one must use variables \(x_{\theta,B}\) over integral bundles \(B\), and XEF becomes nontrivial. The resulting configuration problem could be tractable by column generation—or could expose a continuum-specific obstruction.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only serious anchor is Theorem 7, and it cannot be defeated by claiming that high multiplicity is unnatural. A perfectly credible regime has many workers with the same disutility vector, so the proposed type model is legitimate.

The stronger objection is a scaling dichotomy. If the paper’s \(m\) bads remain a fixed finite set while the population grows, then their mass is \(m/N\to0\). Almost every agent receives nothing; the normalized log Nash diswelfare tends to zero, while the agents receiving bads form a measure-zero exceptional set. XEF and the welfare objective therefore degenerate in the continuum. This is a genuine population-continuum obstruction.

The proponent’s repair is to scale the number of bads with the population, introducing supplies \(\rho_o\). That repair is permitted by the programme, but it changes the character of the result. The resulting model is a capacitated transportation problem with recurring item types, not merely Theorem 7 with a continuous society. In the stated regime \(\sum_o\rho_o\le1\), every assigned agent receives at most one bad. Consequently XEF is automatic:

\[ u_\theta(\emptyset)=0\ge u_\theta(B) \]

for every other bad bundle \(B\). Pareto efficiency is then a monotonicity consequence of the assignment objective, and \(m\)NDW\(^{-}\) is just minimum-cost flow after taking logarithms. The theorem’s fair-division content disappears; what remains is the routine high-multiplicity version of bipartite matching. This is a valid question, but its payoff for ChoCo is slight: it reveals no population-driven complexity phenomenon, no nontrivial fairness constraint, and no pricing problem over a rich type space.

The normalized geometric mean is not itself a fatal objection. Taking the root preserves the finite optimizer when the number of recipients is fixed, and the reduced-problem convention can handle zero disutilities. Nor is it legitimate to object merely that the answer is polynomial. The negative case rests instead on the fact that the only nondegenerate repair has already collapsed the paper’s substantive problem into transportation.

A better repair would allow \(\sum_o\rho_o>1\), retain integral bundles for individual agents, and use configuration variables \(x_{\theta,B}\) for bundle types \(B\):

\[ \sum_B x_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_{\theta,B:o\in B}x_{\theta,B}=\rho_o. \]

That would make XEF nontrivial and could lead to an interesting configuration-LP or pricing problem. But it is no longer a continuous mirror of Theorem 7’s \(m\le n\) result; it is a new multi-bad allocation problem that the paper does not study. It may well be worth studying, which is precisely where the universal negative case fails.

So the strongest honest conclusion is narrow: the proponent’s particular mirror is mathematically sound but computationally routine and fair-division-light. I cannot honestly conclude that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. Under the programme’s explicit permission to scale per-capita supplies, Theorem 7 remains a defensible, if modest, Class A mirror.
