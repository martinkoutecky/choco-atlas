The case FOR (proponent)

Strictly, the paper has no qualifying named computational result: it states no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a complexity result. The closest foothold is the unnumbered claim in §2.3.6 that Chamberlin–Courant winner determination is NP-hard, attributed to Procaccia et al. (2008); §2.4 and Table 2 repeat the claim. The paper does not prove it, and its complexity discussion does not fully specify the formal problem. So there is no anchor-compliant positive case to make.

If that §2.3.6 claim is accepted provisionally as an anchor, my strongest mirror would be Continuous-Profile Chamberlin–Courant Winner Determination. An instance gives candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), and a support of \(\tau\) complete rankings \(t\), each with binary-rational mass \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Using the paper’s satisfaction function \(u(t,c)=(m-\operatorname{rank}_t(c))/(m-1)\), find a size-\(k\) committee \(W\) maximizing
\[ \sum_t \mu_t \max_{c\in W} u(t,c). \]
Equivalently, given a rational threshold \(q\), decide whether some such \(W\) achieves value at least \(q\). The solution is the committee \(W\). The expected classification is hardness transfers: a discrete profile with \(n_t\) voters of each ranking gives \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), preserving the objective up to normalization.

A plausible regime is a large membership organization choosing a small representative council: many members, but relatively few recurring preference rankings. That is close to the paper’s committee-selection problem and its large-electorate motivation. Its uniform-random-ranking experiments do not establish that regime; with many candidates, their support could be large. The weakest point is that this is a provisional mirror of an unnumbered, cited claim, not an eligible anchor under your rules. Even if accepted, it yields a high-multiplicity formulation of the same winner-determination problem, with no reason to expect the hardness to disappear. The natural follow-up is whether algorithms parameterized by \(\tau\), or approximation guarantees for the weighted-support problem, offer anything beyond that hardness transfer.
