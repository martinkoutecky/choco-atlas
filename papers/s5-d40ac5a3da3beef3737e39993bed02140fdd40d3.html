The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s case identifies the strongest objection: this paper has no named computational result to mirror. Theorem 5.1 is a conditional equilibrium statement about when a market and disclosure process is entangled with \(H\). Theorem 5.2 is a conditional information-pooling statement about intersections of experts’ information. Neither defines an algorithmic task, an input encoding, or a complexity claim. They are not weak computational anchors; they are not computational anchors at all.

The best recognizable continuization would give each expert type a complete description of their information, beliefs, and relevant market characteristics, then represent the population by a distribution over those types. A mass version of Theorem 5.1 would impose its belief conditions on the types present; a mass version of Theorem 5.2 would consider the information carried by types with positive mass. But the theorems supply no computation over those masses: they do not optimize disclosure, choose a market outcome, or classify the complexity of verifying the conditions. In the formal model, price stabilization is an assumption, and the result concerns the sequence of public information states.

The obvious attempt to make multiplicity matter also exposes the gap. If identical experts form a cohort, one disclosure makes their shared information public; if experts have distinct information, the paper’s one-at-a-time entry process is about individuals, and a continuum requires a new rule for how disclosures occur. Either way, turning the model into a computational problem—say, optimizing a disclosure schedule or testing an equilibrium—requires choices the paper does not make. Such a project might be worthwhile, but it would be a new problem attached to the mechanism, not a mirror of either theorem.

The negative case is strongest on this missing computational object, not on the claim that no plausible expert population could be described by masses. Standardized research cohorts could make such a population plausible. The paper also uses a non-atomic prior over possible states, but that is continuity of the state space, not of the expert population. Neither point supplies the computational result that a ChoCo mirror needs.
