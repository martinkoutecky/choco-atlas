The continuous mirror question

Given finite good classes \(H\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_h\), a finite catalogue \(T\) of complete XOS valuation types with finite rational descriptions and distribution \(\mu\), a finite-description periodic priority schedule including a tagged position, and a threshold \(R\), run the paper’s mechanism on sufficiently divisible \(N\)-copy instances with \(N\mu_t\) agents of type \(t\) and \(N\rho_h\) goods of class \(h\). Decide whether \(\limsup_{N\to\infty} U_{t\to w}^{(N)}/U_t^{(N)}\le R\) for every type \(t\) and finite-description XOS report \(w\), using \(+\infty\) when truthful utility is zero and deviating utility is positive; otherwise output a violating \(t,w\).

The case FOR (proponent)

Strictly speaking, the paper contains no named NP-hardness, polynomial-time, W[1]-hardness, or FPT theorem. Its named results are mechanism guarantees rather than complexity classifications. The strongest admissible anchor is therefore the quantitative result below; if “computational result” is read narrowly, the correct conclusion is that this paper has no qualifying complexity anchor.

My lead anchor is Theorem B.1, proved in the paper. It states:

For \(n\ge 2\) and \(m\ge n\), the paper’s Round-Robin-for-Submodular-Valuations mechanism has incentive ratio exactly \(\lceil m/n\rceil\) for XOS valuations.

The upper bound is that each agent receives at most \(\lceil m/n\rceil\) goods. For an XOS valuation, every marginal value satisfies \(v(g\mid S)\le v(g)\), while truthful Round-Robin gives the agent a first good of maximum singleton value. Hence any manipulated bundle is worth at most \(\lceil m/n\rceil\) times the truthful bundle. The lower bound is constructive: the paper partitions the goods into groups \(G_1,\ldots,G_n\), gives agent \(1\) an XOS valuation that values either one distinguished good or the whole \(G_2\)-block, and constructs the other valuations so that truthful reporting gives agent \(1\) value \(1\), whereas a report causes him to receive \(G_2\), worth \(\lceil m/n\rceil\). Thus the exact ratio is \(\lceil m/n\rceil\).

The natural continuous mirror is the following.

Take a finite catalogue of good classes \(H=\{1,\ldots,q\}\), with per-capita supply \(\rho_h\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) of class \(h\). A microscopic agent still receives an integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^q\); the continuum only appears in how many agents receive each bundle. Let \(\mathcal T\) be a finite set of complete XOS valuation types, with type \(t\) having valuation \(v_t(b)\), and let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of society of type \(t\). An allocation is a mass-flow \(\lambda_{t,b}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_b\lambda_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}\lambda_{t,b}b_h=\rho_h\) for every good class \(h\). Thus \(\lambda_{t,b}\) may be fractional, but every unit of mass receives an integral bundle.

Define the continuum Round-Robin mechanism as the high-multiplicity limit of the paper’s mechanism: for scale \(N\), run it on \(N\mu_t\) agents of each type and \(N\rho_h\) copies of each good class, using a fixed public priority rule, and take the induced limiting conditional bundle distribution \(\kappa_t\). A tagged type-\(t\) agent may report any XOS valuation \(w\); its report affects its local Round-Robin choice but not the aggregate masses. Its truthful utility is \(U_t=\mathbb E_{b\sim\kappa_{t\to v_t}}[v_t(b)]\), and its utility after reporting \(w\) is \(U_{t\to w}=\mathbb E_{b\sim\kappa_{t\to w}}[v_t(b)]\).

The continuous problem is:

Given \((H,\rho,\mathcal T,\mu)\), the priority rule, and a rational threshold \(R\), decide whether \(\sup_{t,w}U_{t\to w}/U_t\le R\), and if not, output a type and misreport witnessing a larger ratio.

This is recognisably the authors’ problem: the valuation class is unchanged, the mechanism is unchanged, the action is still a strategic valuation report, the objective is still the incentive ratio, and indivisibility is retained at the level of each agent. Only the population and supply are continuized. The intended regime is a large platform allocating \(N\rho_h\) repeated indivisible service slots or goods among \(N\) households, where \(N\gg|\mathcal T|\) and households fall into a small number of XOS preference archetypes. The type records the entire XOS valuation, not merely an ordinal preference.

I would expect the aggregate version to be Class A for explicit finite XOS descriptions and fixed per-capita supply: its allocation is a configuration-flow problem, and the relevant bundle-price queries inherit the max-of-additive structure of XOS valuations. The finite theorem’s scale \(\lceil m/n\rceil\) becomes the natural per-capita quantity \(\lceil\sum_h\rho_h\rceil\). Whether the exact lower bound survives is a substantive question rather than something assumed in the definition.

The weakest point is the tagged-agent semantics. In a genuinely nonatomic population, one agent has zero mass and cannot change aggregate availability; the finite lower-bound construction relies precisely on one report changing what later agents receive. If the continuum mechanism is specified only by \(\lambda\), individual manipulation may become trivial or even undefined. The tagged-agent kernel, or alternatively a positive-mass coalition deviation, must therefore be made part of the model. That is an extra modelling choice, and the opposing case can argue that it changes the paper’s strategic question. Still, it is a legitimate and sharply stated continuous question, and it exposes an important boundary: does the paper’s incentive phenomenon survive high multiplicity, or is it entirely a finite-pivotality effect?

Further questions are whether the ratio converges as \(N\to\infty\), whether \(\lceil m/n\rceil\) becomes exactly \(\lceil\sum_h\rho_h\rceil\), and whether the corresponding finite-\(N\) mechanisms obtain additive approximation guarantees from the continuum solution.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is stronger than the proposed case admits. Under the programme’s anchor rule, this paper has no qualifying computational anchor. Theorem B.1 is a numbered theorem, but it is a guarantee about one mechanism’s incentive ratio, not a theorem about worst-case complexity, an exact or approximation algorithm, or a parameterized problem. The proposed decision problem is new; it is not a computational question formulated or classified by the paper.

Even treating Theorem B.1 as admissible, its proposed mirror is not yet a faithful high-multiplicity object. The theorem’s lower-bound construction is fundamentally population-poor. With \(n=2\) and \(m\) growing, it has only two agents and therefore no high-multiplicity population. If one scales \(n\) as well, the construction introduces a distinct role and valuation for essentially every agent \(i\ge 3\), supported on its private block \(G_i\). Preserving those roles makes the type catalogue grow with \(n\), rather than giving many agents per type. Replicating a fixed set of types does not preserve the proof: one agent’s report no longer redirects an entire \(G_2\)-block, because many indistinguishable agents compete for it.

The same problem appears on the goods side. The witness depends on distinguished goods such as \(g_1\) and \(g_{s_1+1}\), together with agent-specific blocks. Collapsing them into repeated good classes destroys the valuation distinctions; retaining them as singleton classes merely preserves named finite objects rather than producing a population continuization. Thus the paper supplies no evidence that its exact ratio survives the intended high-multiplicity regime.

More fundamentally, Round-Robin is a sequential, identity-sensitive process. The mass-flow \(\lambda_{t,b}\) records final bundle frequencies, but not the priority order, residual-good sequence, or report-induced cascade that defines the mechanism. The same \(\mu\) and \(\rho\) can be realized by microscopic orderings with different conditional bundle distributions \(\kappa_t\). A “fixed public priority rule” does not repair this: its continuum limit depends on how copies are ordered, and that ordering is not determined by the population distribution.

The tagged-agent repair faces a genuine dichotomy. If one agent has zero mass, their report cannot change aggregate availability, so the cascade used in Theorem B.1 disappears. If a positive mass of agents deviates together, the object becomes a coalition or type-level manipulation problem, not the paper’s individual incentive ratio. If priority position is added to the type to preserve the cascade, agents become distinguished by position and the high-multiplicity premise is lost. A random-order or Palm-process construction could be interesting, but it would be a new sequential mean-field model requiring its own definition and analysis, not a mirror supplied by this paper.

Nor does the claimed configuration-flow tractability follow. XOS valuations make certain value queries convenient, but Round-Robin is not an optimization problem over \(\lambda\); it is a report-dependent greedy trajectory. A flow formulation that optimizes over final configurations would describe a different planner or randomized-allocation problem unless it also encoded the entire sequential state and strategic deviation. The proposed supremum over arbitrary XOS reports additionally needs a finite representation and report-size bound; otherwise it is not a computational decision problem.

I cannot honestly prove that no bespoke continuum process inspired by Round-Robin could ever be worthwhile. A positive-mass coalition model or a carefully defined stochastic sequential limit might be a new research direction. But that concession confirms the negative verdict here: the paper itself contains no computational anchor, and its only plausible anchor collapses either into a passive distribution with no strategic cascade or into a materially different problem. It should not be greened as a worthwhile continuous mirror of this paper.
