The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), integer voting weights \(w_t\), a fixed finite affiliation set \(\mathcal{K}\), nonempty memberships \(A_t\subseteq\mathcal{K}\), and a rational quota fraction \(\theta\in(0,1)\), let \(\bar w=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\) and \(Q=\theta\bar w\). For each admissible scale \(N\) with \(N\mu_t\) and \(NQ\) integral, form the weighted-majority game with \(N\mu_t\) delegates of type \(t\), quota \(NQ\), and affiliation coalitions containing exactly the delegates whose type belongs to that affiliation. Assuming \(Q\) is not an endpoint \(B_R\) or \(B_R+\Delta_{k,R}\) for any relevant \(t,k,R\), determine and compute the exact limit, for each type \(t\), of the sum of its delegates’ configuration indices as \(N\to\infty\) through admissible scales.

The model it lives in

A distribution \(\mu\) over complete delegate types, each fixing an integer voting weight and an affiliation set. At each finite scale, delegates are exact clones within types; the game retains its weighted-majority rule and overlapping affiliation coalitions. The output is the limiting aggregate configuration-index power of each type.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the configuration-index computation based on Theorem 3.6 in a fixed-affiliation, high-multiplicity regime. It leaves the generalized Banzhaf-Coleman index, the finite-instance algorithm’s complexity, and the paper’s individual-country calculations unaddressed.

Open questions for a prover

  • Prove the type-power limit and determine its complexity when the number of affiliations is fixed or part of the input.
  • Characterize the limit when \(Q=B_R\) or \(Q=B_R+\Delta_{k,R}\) for some relevant \(t,k,R\).

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is the paper’s configuration index, aggregated over large classes of identical delegates. It preserves the weighted-majority game and its overlapping coalition structure; the continuous step is to make the population a distribution over delegate types and ask for each type’s total power.

The anchor is Theorem 3.6, proved in this paper. For a weighted-majority game \([q;w_1,\ldots,w_p]\), configuration \(C\), player \(i\), and coalition \(C_k\) containing \(i\), it constructs
\[ G'_{i,k}(x,(t_j),u,v) = \prod_{j\in C_k\setminus\{i\}}(1+x^{w_j}t_j u) \prod_{C_\ell\notin C^i} \left(1+x^{w(C_\ell)}\left[\prod_{j\in C_\ell}t_j\right]v\right). \]
For each monomial, the prescribed correction is to multiply by \(x^{-(e_j-1)w_j}\) for every factor \(t_j^{e_j}\), then remove the \(t_j\) factors. The resulting \(G'_{i,k}(x,u,v)\) has coefficient of \(x^m u^t v^r\) equal to \(\nu^{i,k}_{m,r,t}\): the number of configuration-consistent coalitions omitting \(i\), of weight \(m\), with \(r\) external coalitions and \(t\) members of \(C_k\). Summing those coefficients over \(q-w_i\le m\le q-1\) gives the significant-coalition counts \(\sigma^{i,k}_{r,t}\). The configuration index then weights each count by
\[ \frac{r!(c-r-1)!}{c!}\frac{t!(c_k-t-1)!}{c_k!} \]
and sums over \(r,t\) and every \(C_k\in C^i\) to obtain \(\Phi_i\). The theorem supplies the generating-function computation; it is not a claim that the finite problem is polynomial-time.

Here is a regime where its population mirror makes sense: a large federation or association with many voting delegates, drawn from a small number of recurring institutional profiles. A type \(t\) fixes everything relevant to the game: its delegate’s integer voting weight \(w_t\), and its memberships \(A_t\) in a fixed set \(\mathcal K\) of overlapping caucuses or affiliations. The society is \(\mu\), with rational type masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\). There can be millions of delegates but only \(\tau\) types and a small fixed number \(c=|\mathcal K|\) of affiliations. This is the high-multiplicity regime: delegates of the same type are indistinguishable for the index.

I would hand a prover the following problem, Coalition-Configuration Type Power Limit, as the mirror of Theorem 3.6. Its instance is \((T,\mu,(w_t),(A_t),\theta)\), where \(\theta\in(0,1)\) is the quota as a fraction of total voting weight. Write \(\bar w=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\) and \(Q=\theta\bar w\). For each \(N\) that makes all \(N\mu_t\) and \(NQ\) integers, form a finite game with \(N\mu_t\) delegates of type \(t\), quota \(NQ\), and coalition \(C_k^{(N)}\) containing exactly those delegates whose type has \(k\in A_t\). For each type, sum the paper’s individual configuration indices:
\[ \Phi_t^{(N)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N\mu_t}\Phi_{(t,j)}. \]
The requested output is the exact type-power vector \(\Psi\), the limit of these sums as \(N\) grows through admissible values. Each coordinate is the total configuration-index power of that type, not an individual delegate’s index.

For a generic quota—excluding equality at the interval endpoints below—the limit has a concrete formula. For \(R\subseteq\mathcal K\), let \(B_R=\sum_{s:A_s\cap R\ne\varnothing}\mu_s w_s\), the weight already covered by the external coalitions in \(R\). For \(k\in\mathcal K\), let \(\Delta_{k,R}=\sum_{s:k\in A_s,\ A_s\cap R=\varnothing}\mu_s w_s\), the additional weight contributed as the within-\(C_k\) order progresses. Then
\[ \Psi_t= \sum_{k\in A_t}\ \sum_{R\subseteq\mathcal K\setminus A_t} \frac{|R|!(c-|R|-1)!}{c!} \;\mathbf 1\!\left[B_R<Q<B_R+\Delta_{k,R}\right]\; \frac{\mu_t w_t}{\Delta_{k,R}}, \]
with a term defined as zero when \(\Delta_{k,R}=0\). The factorial factor is the same external-coalition ordering weight as in the paper. Within a large coalition, delegate types appear in their mass proportions; when the quota is crossed, type \(t\)’s share of that marginal weight is \(\mu_t w_t/\Delta_{k,R}\). The endpoint exclusion avoids finite-size tie effects.

I expect Class A for fixed \(c\): the displayed formula can be evaluated by enumerating \(R\), in \(O(\tau c2^c\operatorname{poly}(L))\) rational operations, so it is polynomial in support size and bit length when the number of coalition labels is fixed. For unbounded \(c\), whether that dependence can be improved or hides subset-counting hardness is an open question for this mirror. Further questions include how quickly \(\Phi_t^{(N)}\) approaches \(\Psi_t\), and what happens at the excluded quota boundaries.

The authors should recognize this as their underlying problem: the finite games remain weighted-majority games with coalition configurations, and the reported quantity is the sum of their own configuration indices over exact clones. This mirror covers Theorem 3.6’s configuration-index computation, not every result in the paper.

The weak point is that the paper’s motivating example treats countries as individual players, while this mirror treats delegates with the same weight and affiliations as a type and reports their aggregate power. That changes the reporting unit. It is still a direct high-multiplicity version of the same game and index, but the case depends on the authors accepting a class total as a meaningful power quantity.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is about fit with ChoCo’s complexity programme. Theorem 3.6 proves a generating-function encoding for counting significant coalitions; it gives no complexity classification or running-time bound. The paper’s computational contribution is exact index calculation for a supplied game, rather than an intervention or optimization problem. That makes it a weaker anchor for ChoCo’s usual complexity questions.

The EU example also offers little evidence of a high-multiplicity regime: its 28 countries have individual weights and coalition memberships. A type that fixes both voting weight and the full membership pattern could be unique in many such applications. Still, multiplicity is an instance property. Repeated delegate profiles in a large legislature or association are plausible, and the aggregate power of those delegates is a meaningful quantity.

I cannot defeat the proposed mirror on more fundamental grounds. Taking finite populations of exact clones and summing their configuration indices gives a well-posed population limit. The vanishing power of each individual does not make the type total vanish, and the generic-quota formula provides a concrete computation. The paper’s configuration index supplies a direct reason to ask that question.

So the negative case can argue that this paper is a peripheral fit for a programme focused on complexity classifications. It cannot honestly support the stronger claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario. The proposed anchor survives that test.
