The continuous mirror question

Given a finite propositional vocabulary, a consistent integrity constraint \(\Delta\), a support \(\mathcal K\) of consistent belief bases, and positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_0(K)\) summing to \(1\), define \(M_r=\arg\min_{\omega\models\Delta}\sum_{K\in\mathcal K}\mu_r(K)d_H(\omega,K)\), where \(d_H(\omega,K)=\min_{\nu\models K}d_H(\omega,\nu)\). For each type \(K\), define its sceptical update \(R_r(K)=\{\nu\models K:d_H(\nu,M_r)=\min_{\nu'\models K}d_H(\nu',M_r)\}\), where \(d_H(\nu,M_r)=\min_{\omega\in M_r}d_H(\nu,\omega)\), and set \(\mu_{r+1}(K')=\sum_{K:R_r(K)\equiv K'}\mu_r(K)\). Compute the first stationary round, the resulting distribution, and its merged model set; determine the formula-input complexity and a bound in the vocabulary size, support size \(\tau\), and mass bit length \(L\).

The model it lives in

Types are consistent belief bases over a fixed finite propositional vocabulary, with rational population masses \(\mu_K\). Each round selects interpretations minimizing the mass-weighted sum of Dalal distances, then maps each type to the models in its own base closest to the merged model set; masses of equivalent updated types are added. The population distribution is continuous, while interpretations and each unit’s belief base remain discrete.

The objection that survived

Each round still optimizes over the interpretation space, so grouping agents by type may yield little algorithmic gain when formula encoding dominates; the proponent flags this risk but does not resolve it.

The case FOR (proponent)

This paper has no named result classifying a problem as being in \(\mathrm P\), NP-hard, FPT, or similar. Its strongest computational anchor is Proposition 5, proved in the paper: for every finite belief profile \(E=(K_1,\ldots,K_n)\), integrity constraint \(\Delta\), IC merging operator, and tuple of AGM revision operators, the sceptical merge-then-revise sequence becomes stationary. The proof bounds the number of changing rounds by \(\sum_{j=1}^n |[K_j]|-n\), and concludes that the sceptical IMC operator and its associated IM operator are total functions. This is a termination guarantee, not a polynomial-time result.

A plausible high-multiplicity regime is a large network of autonomous knowledge-base agents at replicated sites: many agents use the same finite vocabulary, the same complete belief base, and the same agreed merge-and-revision protocol. The paper itself motivates merging with distributed information sources. Here the agent count \(N\) could be very large while the number \(\tau\) of distinct belief bases is much smaller. A type is a complete consistent belief base \(K\); mass \(\mu_K\) is the fraction of agents with that base. Each unit of mass still has a whole belief base. The continuous object is the population distribution, while interpretations remain discrete.

My lead mirror is Rational-Mass Sceptical Dalal–Sum Conciliation. An instance consists of a finite propositional language, a consistent integrity constraint \(\Delta\), a support of \(\tau\) consistent belief bases \(K\), and positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_K\) summing to \(1\). All types use the compatible Dalal revision induced by Dalal-distance sum merging. At round \(r\), the group chooses the constrained interpretations minimizing total mass-weighted distance:
\[ [M_r]=\arg\min_{\omega\models\Delta} \sum_{K}\mu_r(K)\min_{\nu\models K}d_H(\omega,\nu). \]
Each type then revises sceptically by that merged result: \(R_{M_r}(K)\) has as models the interpretations in \([M_r]\) closest in Hamming distance to \([K]\). Mass moves by this deterministic update, so \(\mu_{r+1}(K')=\sum_{K:R_{M_r}(K)\equiv K'}\mu_r(K)\). The question is to compute the stationary mass distribution and its final merged model set, including the number of rounds to stationarity.

This is a faithful population continuization of the paper’s sceptical problem. A discrete profile with \(n_K\) copies of each base is exactly the distribution \(\mu_K=n_K/N\): multiplying the weighted sum objective by \(N\) recovers the discrete objective, and identical copies undergo identical revisions. Thus the continuous formulation preserves the process while allowing repeated agents to be represented compactly. It does not replace the belief-merging question with fractional outcomes.

I would provisionally expect hardness to transfer for the formula-input version: discrete profiles are exact special cases, so any hardness of computing their terminal result carries over. But this paper proves no such discrete hardness, and Proposition 5 does not settle the complexity of computing a round or the full terminal output. The useful prover questions are whether the continuous process has a bound in \(\tau\), the number of propositions, and the mass bit length that avoids expanding the population; what its exact formula-input complexity is; and whether the bound can be sharpened. The iteration count alone may be easier than computing each Dalal–Sum merge.

The scope is deliberately narrow: the paper’s sceptical termination result, instantiated with a mass-weighted sum merge. It does not mirror the credulous branch, whose stationarity is only conjectured in the paper, or the paper’s rationality-postulate results.

The weakest point is that Proposition 5 is about termination and gives a bound in terms of model-set sizes and the number of agents; it does not establish a complexity classification. Also, the paper does not itself develop the weighted population model. The positive case rests on the natural replacement of repeated profile entries by rational masses for the sum aggregator, not on a complexity result already proved by the authors.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I do not see a defensible way to defeat Proposition 5’s mirror. The paper proves a termination bound, not a complexity classification, so the proponent is right that the result leaves computational questions open. But it is still a named result about a computational process: every sceptical run reaches a fixed point after finitely many changes. That is enough to support an algorithmic mirror.

The proposed population regime is credible. Consider many autonomous agents or software agents using the same finite language and protocol, with only \(\tau\) distinct belief bases. Give each base its population fraction \(\mu_K\). For Dalal–sum merging, the group’s chosen interpretations minimize
\[ \sum_K \mu_K \min_{\nu\models K} d_H(\omega,\nu). \]
Agents with the same complete type then revise identically, and their mass moves together. This preserves the paper’s discrete process while making the population distribution its input. The fact that the beliefs themselves remain discrete is no objection: the population is what has been continuized.

Nor do the other likely objections hold. The resulting profile does not track which named agent receives which outcome; its object is the group’s evolving beliefs. The limit does not make the process vacuous, since positive-mass types still affect the merge and retain nontrivial belief bases. And heterogeneous revision rules can be included in the type if a broader mirror is wanted. The paper’s own distinction between low-level sources and autonomous agents makes some scenarios less convincing, but it does not rule out large populations of agents with repeated types.

The strongest negative point is about likely yield. In this uniform Dalal–sum version, the mass update is a deterministic map on a finite support, and the merger still searches over the finite interpretation space. Grouping identical agents may give a sharper support-sensitive termination bound, but the paper’s monotonicity argument appears to carry over directly. A complexity study would therefore depend heavily on how formulas and outputs are encoded, and the continuous model may add less algorithmic structure than the programme’s optimization-focused examples.

That limits the expected payoff; it does not invalidate the question. The exact mass model is well-posed, and whether its computation admits useful bounds in \(\tau\), the number of propositions, and the mass bit length remains open in the text provided. My case against this anchor is consequently weak: I can argue that it may be a modest extension, but I cannot honestly claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
