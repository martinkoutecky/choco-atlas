What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers none of the named results; they concern equilibrium existence and impossibility, not computation.

The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make an honest anchored case for a ChoCo mirror: the paper has no named computational result. Theorem 1 gives a threshold condition under which every equilibrium is uninformative; Theorem 2 establishes that some geometric evidence-supply distributions admit an informative equilibrium; Proposition 1 constructs an equilibrium that learns with arbitrary precision given sufficiently strong incentives; and Theorem 3 gives an impossibility result when witnesses are added. These are equilibrium existence and impossibility results, not claims that a computational problem is in P, NP-hard, or otherwise classified.

The nearest possible scenario would group many investigators by their role, incentives, and information technology, with mass representing their share of an investigative workforce. But the paper’s central object is a sequential game: each investigator’s incentives depend on reports and evidence remaining at that point in the chain. Replacing that chain with a continuum of agents changes the model, and the paper supplies no computational problem for such a version to anchor. A formal question could be developed as new work, but it would not be a continuous mirror of a named computational result here.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is decisive: none of the named results states the complexity of a computational problem. Theorem 1 and Theorem 3 give conditions under which informative equilibria do not exist; Theorem 2 establishes that an informative equilibrium can exist for some evidence distributions and incentives; Proposition 1 constructs incentives that make learning arbitrarily precise. These are equilibrium results, not algorithmic classifications. There is no input problem here with a finite representation, objective, or algorithm whose complexity a continuous mirror could investigate.

The closest mirror for Theorems 1 and 3 would ask whether an informative equilibrium exists when investigators or witnesses are represented by a distribution over types. That is a conceivable new model, but the paper’s agents arrive in sequence, and each report changes what later agents believe about the remaining evidence. Their payoffs may depend on the entire report sequence, so round and continuation position matter to a complete type. A mass distribution over investigator types does not encode that order. Giving each position its own type preserves the sequence but offers no high-multiplicity compression; putting many agents at each position creates cohorts and changes the game. A continuum-time or simultaneous-cohort version could be worth studying, but it would be a new dynamic model rather than a mirror of either theorem.

Theorem 2 and Proposition 1 do not provide a stronger computational anchor. One could ask an algorithm to find incentives that sustain learning, or to minimize the incentive budget needed for a target precision. But the paper imposes no such optimization objective: its result is existence under bounded, freely designed payoff functions. Adding a design cost and asking for an optimum would create a new mechanism-design problem. A continuum of investigators would not turn the stated existence construction into a complexity result.

There are plausible settings with many similar investigators—laboratories, researchers, or reviewers—so I would not rest the case on saying that high multiplicity is never sensible here. The more fundamental mismatch is that the paper studies strategic learning through an ordered chain, not a social-choice computation over a population distribution. Its probability distributions describe uncertainty about evidence and the state, not a continuous society. A continuous-agent extension may be interesting, but the best versions require a new population structure and a new computational question. None of the four named results supplies that question for ChoCo to mirror.
