The continuous mirror question

The model it lives in

Let \(x_{t,o}\) be the mass of role \(t\) assigned object type \(o\), allowing fractional type-level averages while each unit of mass receives one object. Seek an assignment with no blocking measurable coalition under the lifted core rules.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent has no qualifying anchor. Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 establish that two solution concepts are nonempty. Theorem 3 says that every outcome of YRMH-IGYT belongs to two cores. These are existence and mechanism-correctness results, not complexity results for a computational problem. The paper gives no complexity classification or algorithmic boundary to continuize.

The strongest replacement would be a large exchange economy with finitely many complete preference-and-ownership types, scaled supplies of object types, and a question such as whether one can compute a measurable mass allocation in the intersection of the rectified and refined exclusion cores. That could be a sensible research question, but it adds the computational task the paper does not study. It also requires defining how coalition control works over masses: the paper’s blocking rules depend on whether a coalition contains all owners of an endowment, while YRMH-IGYT proceeds through a finite order of agents, cycles, and removals. A type-mass version would need new definitions and a new mechanism; it would not simply continue Theorem 3.

That is a strong reason to reject this paper as a *computational mirror*. It is weaker as the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario. Repeated cohorts with recurring ownership arrangements could make a high-multiplicity exchange economy plausible.
