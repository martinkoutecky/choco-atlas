The closest result is Proposition 3, proved here. It establishes existence of a complete uniform-price equilibrium with some positive investment when condition (14) holds, and an equilibrium in which every positive-index buyer and seller invests when condition (15) holds. That is an equilibrium existence theorem, not a computational result. Proposition 1 gives an efficiency characterization, and Proposition 2 a necessary condition for supporting an efficient outcome; neither supplies a computational anchor either.

So the strongest honest positive point is that the paper offers a natural economic setting for a future high-multiplicity equilibrium-computation study. I would not present that as a continuous mirror anchored in this paper’s named computational results: there are none.
