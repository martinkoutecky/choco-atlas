The case FOR (proponent)

The honest positive case is that this paper has no qualifying computational anchor, so it cannot support a compliant ChoCo mirror under the stated rules.

Its named results are axiomatic, not computational. For example, Theorem 1, proved in this paper, says that no non-wasteful online mechanism is strategy-proof, even with identical monotone utilities and \(0/1\) marginals. Theorem 6 says that no online mechanism is EF1, even with positive additive utilities. Conversely, Theorems 3, 7, 11, and 12 establish envy-freeness, EF1, or online strategy-proofness for particular mechanisms and domains. None asserts membership in \( \mathrm{P} \), NP-hardness, parameterized complexity, approximation, or an algorithmic formulation with a runtime. The cited results about earlier mechanisms do not become anchors merely because this paper mentions them.

The strongest substantive rescue would be a new problem such as Continuous Online Mechanism Design. An instance would contain a finite set \(T\) of complete utility types, a rational population distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), an ordered stream of item types \(o_1,\ldots,o_m\), and per-capita supplies \(\lambda_j\). Each individual receives an integral bundle, while the atomless mass of agents receiving each bundle is represented by transition variables \(x_{t,B,B\cup\{o_j\}}\). A policy sees only the current marginal bids and past allocation state, must be non-wasteful, and the question is whether there exists a policy with zero worst-case incentive violation, or alternatively one minimizing that violation.

This is a genuine population continuization: many households or organizational units share a small number of complete utility profiles, while item supplies scale per capita. It preserves the paper’s online arrival, marginal bidding, monotonicity, and indivisible-bundle structure. Theorem 1 would be the natural substantive motivation, and the authors would likely recognize the model as their setting.

But it is not an anchor under the programme’s gate. Moreover, Theorem 1’s obstruction already occurs with two agents and temporal complementarities, so it is unclear that high multiplicity removes it. A fixed-\(m\), fixed-\(|T|\) version might admit a finite-state LP or flow formulation; general policy existence could instead be continuum-specifically hard. Those would be new ChoCo results, not consequences of this paper.

Thus the best honest verdict is: a plausible future continuous-population research question exists, but this paper supplies no named computational result to mirror.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s conclusion is too broad. The absence of a runtime theorem in the paper is a real admission problem, but it does not establish that no worthwhile population mirror exists.

Theorem 1 gives a natural continuous incentive-design problem. Consider a large population with finitely many complete monotone-utility types, and let each item class arrive in per-capita supply \(\lambda_j\). Each individual still receives an integral bundle; only the mass of agents in each bundle is fractional. One can ask whether a non-wasteful online policy can satisfy \(\mathrm{SP}\) or \(\mathrm{OSP}\), or can minimize worst-case incentive gain subject to fairness constraints. A unilateral deviation need not be vacuous in an atomless population: the deviating agent’s own bundle and future allocation can still change, even though their report does not change aggregate state. The temporal complementarities in Theorem 1 therefore remain a meaningful computational object, whether they survive or disappear under high multiplicity.

Theorem 6, together with Theorem 8, supports an equally concrete mirror. Given a type distribution \(\mu\), per-capita item supplies, and an online order, compute whether there is a policy whose realized integral bundles are \(\mathrm{EF1}\), perhaps simultaneously with \(\mathrm{OSP}\), or minimize the maximum EF1 violation. The natural formulation has variables \(x_{t,B,j}\) for the mass of type \(t\) moving from bundle \(B\) to \(B\cup\{o_j\}\). This is a population flow problem, not outcome-space fractionalization: the bundles remain indivisible for every individual. For explicit finite type and bundle descriptions it yields a finite LP or flow formulation; with succinct monotone utilities, its state explosion and separation problem become precisely the sort of complexity question ChoCo studies.

The constructive results are not empty either. Theorems 3, 7, 11, and 12 invite computation of the attainable fairness–incentive region for \(\myminlike\), \(\myminutil\), and competing policies under a type distribution. One can ask whether the continuous policy can be implemented using only aggregate type-and-bundle masses, whether its guarantees are exact, and whether finding an optimal policy is polynomial, NP-hard, or parameterized by the number of types. The online order and marginal dependence give the model genuine temporal structure; it is not merely a fractional allocation problem.

The paper’s agents are not individuated by arrival time, location, or private identity in the way that would make high multiplicity artificial. They share utility functions over a common catalogue of items, and its examples—course places, food parcels, charging slots, or cloud-service resources—naturally admit repeated cohorts with identical preferences. Scaling item supply per capita is the correct high-multiplicity analogue; otherwise a fixed number of items would indeed vanish in the limit.

The strongest point for the proponent remains the formal gate: none of Theorems 1, 6, 8, 11, or 12 states a complexity classification or runtime bound. If ChoCo requires an existing named computational result as a strict prerequisite, this paper should be rejected on that procedural ground. But that is narrower than claiming that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario. The honest negative case is therefore weak: the paper lacks a qualifying computational anchor, but it has several natural, non-degenerate population-computational mirrors whose tractability is genuinely open.
