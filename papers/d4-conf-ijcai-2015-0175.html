The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make a defensible positive case from this paper. It contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result to anchor a mirror. Example 1 constructs an MDD for a constraint set; the variable-ordering and preference-extension items are proposed future questions, not results. The GAC complexity claim is cited to prior work by Cheng and Yap, not proved here.

So there is no honest per-anchor continuous problem to hand to a prover, and no lead mirror. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The case for offers no positive anchor, so there is no surviving mirror to defeat. For this paper, the negative case is strong: it reports no computational theorem to continuize. Its variable-ordering and preference-extension questions are proposed future work; Example 1 is an illustration; and the GAC complexity result belongs to cited prior work.

The best repair would be to imagine many users with preferences represented by constraints or weighted logics, then ask for collectively optimal feasible outcomes. That could be a worthwhile continuous social-choice problem. But it adds the population, an aggregation rule, and an optimization objective. The paper studies none of those: its questions concern representing a feasible set and extending that representation with preference information.

Treating assignments or MDD variables as the population does not supply the missing bridge. Assignments are outcomes, and constraints couple their components; a distribution over them would change the object being represented. Even a carefully designed high-multiplicity model would therefore be a new problem built around MDDs, not a continuous mirror of a result in this paper.

I would not claim that no conceivable scenario could connect MDDs to continuous social choice. The honest negative claim is narrower: this paper gives ChoCo no computational result to mirror, and turning its subject into a population problem requires adding the central social-choice task rather than continuizing one already present.
