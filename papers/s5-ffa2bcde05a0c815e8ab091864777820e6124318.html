The strongest concrete mirror I can offer is an unanchored candidate for the paper’s proposal clustering in §4.3. In a mass online deliberation, let \(P\) be the finite set of proposals under consideration, and let \(T\) be the supported types of active participants. A type records a participant’s complete appraisal pattern over \(P\), with each appraisal in \(\{\text{agree},\text{disagree},\text{don’t understand}\}\). Its mass \(\mu_t\) is its fraction of the active population, with rational masses summing to \(1\). For proposal \(p\), let \(S_p\) be the types that agree with it. Given a rational threshold \(x\), compute the graph on \(P\) whose edge weight is
\[ J_\mu(p,q)= \frac{\sum_{t\in S_p\cap S_q}\mu_t} {\sum_{t\in S_p\cup S_q}\mu_t}, \]
taking the weight as \(0\) when the denominator is \(0\). Retain an edge exactly when \(J_\mu(p,q)\ge x\); one specified clustering output is the connected components of the retained graph. A solution is that threshold graph and its component partition.

This has a plausible high-multiplicity regime: a consultation may involve thousands or tens of thousands of participants, while many share the same appraisal pattern on a fixed proposal set. The paper itself motivates mass participation and bases clustering on shared agreement. In that regime the graph captures the paper’s aggregation idea directly: participant counts become masses, while proposals remain discrete. The candidate is expected to be tractable by computing all pairwise weights in time polynomial in \(|P|\), \(\tau=|T|\), and the input bit length. That runtime observation is a consequence of this proposed formulation, not a result proved in the paper. It covers the agreement-based clustering described in §4.3; it does not cover the system’s rewriting mechanism or its request-selection procedure.

The weakest point is substantial: appraisal patterns may be highly varied, so \(\tau\) could approach the number of participants. Also, §4.3 does not prescribe a particular graph clustering algorithm; connected components are one reasonable completion of its thresholding step. Most of all, this candidate is not anchored by a named computational result.
