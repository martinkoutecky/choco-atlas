The case FOR (proponent)

Strictly, this paper has no named computational result in the requested sense: it gives no complexity classification or algorithm. Its Theorems 1 and 2 and Corollary 1 are structural characterizations. So there is no qualifying computational anchor, and no discrete hardness result here whose fate in a continuum can be tested.

The strongest nearby anchor for a positive case is Corollary 1, proved in this paper using Lemma 3 and Theorems 1 and 2. It says that, in a many-to-one matching market with responsive preferences, a set of matchings is a von Neumann–Morgenstern farsightedly stable set if and only if it is a singleton whose matching is setwise stable. This is a genuine result about the authors’ solution concept, but it is not a computational result; treating it as one would overstate the paper.

A plausible population regime is a large centralized hiring or residency market: many applicants and employers, but relatively few recurring profiles. Applicant types encode complete rankings of employer categories and other relevant attributes; employer types encode capacity and responsive preferences over applicant types. For example, a market could have tens of thousands of applicants but only hundreds of distinct profiles on each side. Mass is the fraction of the applicant or employer population of each type. That is a sensible high-multiplicity setting for matching, though it depends on the market actually having repeated profiles.

Here is the continuous question I would put to a prover, as a mirror of Corollary 1. Call it Mass-Responsive Farsighted Stability. An instance gives finite type sets \(H\) and \(S\), rational masses \(\mu_h\) and \(\nu_s\) for employer and applicant types, quotas \(q_h\), and responsive preferences over the opposite side’s types and the outside option. The masses on each side may be normalized to sum to one. A matching assigns each individual an integral bundle of applicants or one employer, subject to quotas; at the type level, assignments can be fractional.

Represent the outcome by \(y_{h,b}\), the mass of employers of type \(h\) receiving bundle \(b\), where \(b\) is an integral vector of applicant-type counts and \(\sum_s b_s\le q_h\). Feasibility requires
\[ \sum_b y_{h,b}=\mu_h \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{h,b} b_s y_{h,b}\le \nu_s. \]
Unassigned applicants account for the remaining mass. Thus type-level averages may be fractional even though each employer receives an integral bundle. Define deviations by measurable coalitions of positive mass, preserve the paper’s setwise-enforceability condition, and define indirect dominance by finite sequences in which deviators prefer the eventual matching to their current one. The decision problem is: given such an instance and a feasible rational \(y\), is \(\{y\}\) a vNM farsightedly stable set under these mass-based deviations? A solution is a yes/no answer with a proof or a counterexample. The paper has no optimization objective to carry over; its result characterizes a solution concept.

My provisional expectation for this problem is Class A, if the finite characterization survives: with finitely many types, setwise stability may reduce to checking feasible type-level deviations, using a stable-flow or configuration-LP formulation. That is a research bet, not an implication of Corollary 1. The useful follow-on questions are whether the singleton characterization extends, whether the decision problem is polynomial in the support sizes and input bit length, and how to define the discrete-to-continuous dictionary for rational masses.

The case’s weakest point is substantial: repeated agents of one type are indifferent among copies of that type, whereas the paper assumes strict preferences over individual agents. Also, its finite coalitions and matching sequences become measurable mass coalitions and sequences here. Those changes may alter the farsighted-stability result rather than merely scale it up. Still, the proposed question keeps the paper’s many-to-one structure, responsiveness, setwise deviations, and farsighted endpoint comparisons. That makes it a plausible extension of the authors’ problem, even though the paper itself supplies no computational anchor for it.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a ChoCo mirror is that this paper has no named computational result to continuize. Theorem 1, Theorem 2, and Corollary 1 characterize a game-theoretic solution concept. They give no algorithm, hardness result, running-time bound, or computational problem with an input and output. Corollary 1 is a substantial structural anchor, but it does not supply the complexity question the programme is designed to chart.

The proponent’s residency-market model is a credible population continuization. Rational masses over applicant and employer types, with distributions over integral bundles, preserve individual assignments while making the population continuous. I would not reject it on the grounds that matching is inherently about named people, or that fractional type-level assignments are an illegitimate change.

The strongest proposed mirror is: given a rational mass assignment \(y\), decide whether \(\{y\}\) is a farsightedly stable set. In the paper’s finite model, Corollary 1 reduces that question to whether the matching is setwise stable. A mass version therefore needs a new definition of positive-mass deviations and a new result showing how farsighted paths relate to stability there. The suggested stable-flow or configuration-LP approach is a research conjecture; the paper supplies no computational result that motivates it or determines what must be computed. Turning the new decision question into an algorithmic project may be worthwhile, but it would be a project built from the paper’s subject matter, not a computational mirror of one of its results.

There is also a real modeling choice behind the proposed types. Retaining strict rankings over named counterpart agents can make profiles depend on individual identities and market size. Ranking counterpart *types* instead gives a natural high-multiplicity model, but requires adapting the paper’s strict-preference setting to anonymous or tied counterparts. That adaptation may be entirely acceptable to matching theorists; it reinforces that the proposed result would need its own formulation and proof.

So the negative case is strong for ChoCo’s stated scope: the paper has no computational anchor, and Corollary 1 cannot supply one by itself. The honest weakness is that the mass-based extension could still be valuable matching research. I would reject it as a continuous computational mirror of this paper, not as a potentially worthwhile new problem.
