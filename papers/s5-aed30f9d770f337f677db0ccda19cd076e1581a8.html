The case FOR (proponent)

The paper has no named computational result to anchor a continuous mirror to. There is no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a complexity classification or algorithm. Section 3 reports simulation findings, including the effects of information access and advertising on the median belief. The median-voter statement in Section 2.4 is background attributed to Black (1948), not a computational result of this paper.

The strongest positive case is therefore a plausible extension of the model, rather than a mirror of one of its results. A natural regime would have many agents in repeated social roles: polluters and non-polluters, with shared information access, resources, and network position. A type’s mass would be its fraction of society. A finite step graphon could represent recurring network roles, preserving some structure the original model assigns to individual agents.

One possible computational problem would be Finite-Horizon Mass Campaigning. Its instance would give a rational horizon, persuasion and information parameters, a finite network-role partition with contact rates, the initial belief and fund distributions, and the policy and profit rules. In each period, a campaign chooses how to allocate its available advertising mass among recipients; beliefs and funds evolve by the paper’s update rules, and policy is the median belief. The task is to find a feasible campaign maximizing the median policy at the horizon, or decide whether it can reach a specified threshold. A solution would be an advertising policy with a value certificate.

That proposal connects population mass, policy feedback, and the paper’s explicit comparison of advertising strategies. Its computational status is open: optimization over targeting and the median objective could make it continuum-specifically hard, while a structured contact model might permit continuous-optimization methods. There is no result in this paper that supports either prediction, so this is not an anchor-backed prover question.

The weakest point is substantial: the authors study stochastic agent-based simulations, not the complexity of choosing an optimal campaign. A graphon and mass-allocation control could be a credible research extension, but the paper gives no named computational result that establishes it as the continuous form of a problem the authors already solved. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The case for has no result anchors to defend. Section 3 reports simulation findings, not the complexity or algorithmic results of a named computational problem. The median-belief rule is background from Condorcet theory, not a result proved here. So the paper offers no computational result whose continuous mirror the programme could chart.

Finite-Horizon Mass Campaigning is a plausible extension, but it adds the central decision the paper never studies: an optimizer choosing how to allocate advertising. In the paper, type A spends its available funds under one of three specified targeting rules; the authors compare their simulated effects. Choosing a campaign to maximize policy, adding a threshold objective, and issuing a value certificate define a new control problem. The paper supplies neither that problem nor a discrete algorithmic problem for the continuous version to mirror.

The strongest version would preserve a natural large-population regime: many agents in shared social roles, with role masses and a median policy. One could represent recurring network positions with a finite-step graphon and ask how advertising mass changes the median over a fixed horizon. That is coherent, and the groups in the paper make mass meaningful. But it would turn the paper’s simulation model into a new mean-field campaigning problem. Preserving evolving beliefs, funds, and network structure requires choosing a new state representation and computational input-output task; simplifying those features changes what the model captures. Either way, the computational question would be designed for a follow-on paper, rather than extracted from a computational result here.
