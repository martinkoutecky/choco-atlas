The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t give an anchor-backed positive case under your rules: the paper has no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result. Its numbered items are equations, and the computational discussion reports that the authors solve convex QCQPs and run experiments; neither supplies an eligible named anchor. So there are no anchors to restate, and no result-backed mirror to grade.

The best candidate I see is the paper’s Integrated routing optimization, given in Eq. (8), but this is only a candidate, not a valid anchor. Consider a repeated microgrid deployment with many prosumers in a small number of standardized feeder roles. A type would include a participant’s per-capita net supply or demand, network role, and all relevant line and pricing characteristics. Its mass \(\mu_t\) is that role’s share of the population. Let \(\rho_e\) be the number of identical links of class \(e\) per participant, and let \(f_e\) and \(g_e\) be power sent and received on that link class, measured per participant. With \(a_e=R_e/U_e^2\), the loss constraint can be written \(0\le g_e\le f_e-a_e f_e^2/\rho_e\). The instance also gives the type-level network, transformer-loss parameter \(\beta\), and rational per-capita supplies and demands. The problem is to choose link-class flows satisfying each type’s power balance and minimizing net power drawn from the outside grid, using Eq. (8)’s objective; a solution is the flows and the resulting minimum. I would expect the finite-type version to be tractable by convex optimization. It covers integrated routing only, not the paper’s participation and stability claims.

I think the authors could recognize this as their routing problem in a plausible high-multiplicity regime: standardized feeder roles repeated across a large microgrid, with \(\tau\ll n\). The qualification is substantial. In the paper, location, neighbors, and private-line control can make individual nodes distinct. The mirror is convincing only when repeated network roles and link classes are a sensible description; otherwise \(\tau\) may grow with the population and the aggregation buys little. The paper’s continuous flow variables do not themselves make its population continuous.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The case for raises no eligible anchor. Its only candidate is Integrated routing in Eq. (8), and it correctly concedes that this is an optimization in the model, not a numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a computational result. The paper reports solving convex QCQPs and gives experimental comparisons; it does not establish a complexity result for a named problem. The claim about stabilizing payments is weaker still: the authors say they have not proved that such payments always exist. By the programme’s own anchor standard, there is no result here for a continuous mirror to continue.

The proposed high-multiplicity version of Eq. (8) is coherent only under substantial structural conditions. In this paper, agents occupy vertices of a physical network. Their incident lines, locations, private-line ownership, and supply or demand all affect feasible flows and losses. A complete type must include those relevant network features. On a general graph, that can make nearly every vertex its own type. If types instead group vertices merely by feeder role and average link class, the resulting masses need not determine a feasible routing: they can hide which endpoints are connected and how load is distributed across lines. The proposed loss expression \(f_e-a_e f_e^2/\rho_e\) relies on a symmetric distribution across \(\rho_e\) identical links.

One can repair that by studying repeated, symmetric feeder modules with identical participants and link structure. That gives a plausible continuous-population routing model, and the authors might recognize it as an extension of their integrated model. But it does not supply a mirror of a computational result in this paper: the symmetry assumptions create a new restricted network model, while the paper’s reported routing results remain numerical evaluations of finite-network optimization. So this is a possible research question, but not the result-backed anchor the positive case needs.

The negative case is strongest on that mismatch, not on proving that no useful model could ever be built. Symmetric feeder populations are plausible, so claiming that every continuous formulation would be meaningless would overreach. The narrower conclusion is that this paper does not offer a named computational result to mirror, and the best proposed candidate becomes a separate, symmetry-dependent model before it becomes a genuine population continuization.
