The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent supplied no anchor, so there is no named result to defend or defeat under the requested standard. The article defines the decision problem SEED-SRTI-SAT, but gives no numbered complexity result for it. Proposition 1 and Corollary 1 establish that the seed-combining method preserves stable matchings and their product lower bound; they do not classify a computational problem. Egalitarian SRI’s NP-hardness is cited as background, not proved in this paper. The article’s contribution is a benchmark generator and experiments, rather than a complexity result for a continuous mirror to take up.

The closest mirror of that contribution also runs into a substantive mismatch. Its target is to construct finite instances with many distinct pairings of named agents, so that enumerating stable matchings becomes hard. In a continuous model, matchings would naturally be represented as flows between types. Many distinct pairings of individuals can collapse to the same type-level flow, so the product count that motivates the generator no longer measures the number of solutions. Keeping individual identities to preserve that count, in turn, removes the high-multiplicity advantage.

A stronger adjacent proposal would optimize egalitarian welfare over stable flows between roommate types, where agents rank categories of people rather than named individuals. That is a coherent problem, but it changes the paper’s identity-specific preference model and is not a continuous version of a result established here. It may merit its own study; this paper gives no named computational anchor for it.

So the negative case is strongest on the paper’s actual contribution: it offers no qualifying complexity result, and its benchmark objective depends on distinctions a type-level continuum would erase. The universal claim is weaker: the type-based welfare problem is plausible, even if it is better described as a neighboring research direction than as a mirror of this paper.
