The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s refusal to name an anchor is decisive here: the paper states no computational complexity result to continuize. Proposition 1 is a representability claim. Algorithms 1 and 2 describe how to generate candidate formulas, and the case study reports accuracy on one database, but the paper does not classify the complexity of learning those formulas or establish an algorithmic guarantee. There is therefore no named computational result whose continuous mirror would meet the programme’s stated brief.

The strongest repair would treat each complete feature-and-rank record \(t=(z,r)\) as a type, give it rational mass \(\mu_t\), and learn a QCL model maximizing \(\sum_t \mu_t\,\mathbf{1}[\widehat R(z)=r]\), perhaps with the paper’s support and confidence thresholds. That is a coherent weighted-learning question. But it mostly replaces counts by weighted sums: the paper’s support, confidence, and accuracy are already proportions, and identical records can already be aggregated. The computational work is driven by the feature vocabulary and the search over formula syntax, not by the number of records. With the paper’s fixed, small feature set, the search is bounded; with an unrestricted feature set, its difficulty remains in formula learning whether the examples are discrete or weighted.

There is also a semantic gap. The records describe antibiotics and their expert-assigned ranks; they are not agents with preferences over a common set of alternatives. A distribution over antibiotics would make common feature profiles count more in training, which is a possible modeling choice but does not itself describe a society. A distribution over clinical encounters could be more natural, but this paper studies one clinical situation and does not model a population of encounters. A distribution over clinicians would require individual judgments that the paper’s consensus database does not provide. Those could motivate new projects, but each changes the data or task rather than mirrors a result in this paper.

So the negative case is strong for the paper as written: there is no qualifying anchor, and the nearest weighted version is a routine reformulation of its learning objective. It cannot rule out a worthwhile future project built from new population data; that would be an adjacent question, not a continuous mirror of a computational result established here.
