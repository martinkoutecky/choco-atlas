What the mirror covers

The proposed cohort question concerns clearing under the pairwise netting proportional rule; it leaves the paper's axiomatization, clearing characterization, solvency result, and axiom-independence examples without a computational mirror.

The case FOR (proponent)

I do not find an eligible computational anchor in this paper. The closest candidates are Proposition 4.4, proved here, which says that for every finite financial network the system (4.3) has the unique solution \(\mathrm{pnp}(F)-L\); and Theorem 5.2, proved here, which says that the pairwise netting proportional rule is the only bankruptcy rule satisfying net impartiality, invariance to mitosis, claims boundedness, limited liability, priority of creditors, and continuity. Proposition 4.4 is a uniqueness characterization, not a complexity or algorithmic result. Theorem 5.2 is an axiomatic result. Neither qualifies under the requested anchor rule.

There is nevertheless a plausible continuous-population problem close to the paper’s underlying clearing model. Consider a large cohort of banks with \(\tau\) complete types: banks of type \(t\) share an initial endowment \(z_t\) and the same liabilities to each type. Let \(\mu_t>0\) be the fraction of banks of type \(t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Write \(\ell_{tu}\) for one type-\(t\) bank’s total gross liability to type \(u\), spread uniformly across that cohort. In a finite population of size \(N\), each such bilateral liability is \(\ell_{tu}/(N\mu_u)\). Pairwise netting then gives per-bank net liabilities
\[ d_{tu}=\max\left\{\ell_{tu}-\frac{\mu_u}{\mu_t}\ell_{ut},\,0\right\}. \]
The decision variables are per-bank payments \(p_{tu}\) from type \(t\) to type \(u\). The continuous clearing problem is: given binary-rational \((\mu,z,\ell)\), compute the clearing payments satisfying
\[ a_t=z_t+\sum_s\frac{\mu_s}{\mu_t}p_{st},\qquad p_{tu}=\min\!\left\{\frac{d_{tu}}{\sum_v d_{tv}}a_t,\ d_{tu}\right\}, \]
with the payment share taken as zero when \(\sum_v d_{tv}=0\). A solution is the resulting payment vector \(p\), with type-\(t\) equity \(a_t-\sum_u p_{tu}\). This asks for the computation of payments under the paper’s rule, not for an optimization objective.

The regime is plausible for many small, standardized institutions with broadly diversified exposures: \(N\) can be large while the number of distinct balance-sheet and exposure types \(\tau\) is small. The paper’s invariance-to-mitosis axiom supports the relevance of splitting identical agents. I would expect this finite-support clearing problem to be tractable through the proportional-clearing equations after netting, but the paper does not establish that complexity result. Its strongest connection is to Proposition 4.4’s clearing characterization; that is a structural connection, not an eligible computational anchor.

The soft spot is that financial networks often have concentrated, institution-specific counterparties. The proposed model needs liabilities spread uniformly across types, so it captures a particular dense, homogeneous regime. More fundamentally, because the paper proves no named computational result, this is a plausible new computational question about its model, not a continuous mirror of a computational result in the paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is the anchor test: the paper proves no computational complexity or algorithmic result to continuize. Theorem 5.2 characterizes the pairwise netting proportional rule by axioms. Proposition 4.4 proves uniqueness of a payment solution to a system of equations. Neither gives a running time, a decision problem, or an algorithm. A continuous version of Theorem 5.2 would still be an axiomatization; a continuous version of Proposition 4.4 would extend a structural characterization. Neither supplies the computational question this programme asks for.

The proposed cohort model is coherent, and invariance to mitosis gives it unusually direct motivation. Its limitation is the exchangeability assumption: every bank of a type must have the same exposure pattern, spread uniformly over each other type. Bilateral netting happens edge by edge, so aggregate exposures alone generally do not determine the result. For type groups \(A\) and \(B\), the net exposure depends on \(\sum_{i\in A,j\in B}(L_{ij}-L_{ji})_+\), which cannot generally be recovered from the two gross totals. Keeping arbitrary bilateral structure in the type description usually makes the number of distinct types grow with the number of banks. The proposed model avoids that problem by defining a particular homogeneous block network. That is a plausible regime, but the resulting clearing computation is a new problem on that restricted network family; it does not mirror a computational result in this paper.

A stronger variant could preserve heterogeneous bilateral exposures with a continuum of counterparties or a liability kernel. That would retain more of the paper’s setting, but it would introduce a new infinite-dimensional input model and new computational questions with no corresponding theorem here. The paper’s axiom of mitosis supports studying replication of identical institutions; it does not itself establish a computational task. Net impartiality and the other axioms constrain which rule is selected.

So I would reject the proposal as a *continuous mirror of this paper’s results*: neither proposed anchor survives the computational-result requirement. I cannot honestly claim that no worthwhile ChoCo project could grow from homogeneous-bank clearing. The negative case is strongest on the distinction between that adjacent project and a mirror of this paper, and weaker as a universal claim that the model can yield no useful continuous computational research.
