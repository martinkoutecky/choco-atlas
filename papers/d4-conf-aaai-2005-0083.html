A continuous population version follows the paper’s own open question about combining users’ preferences when they must agree on one subset. Suppose a large community must choose one shared slate of \(k\) images or documents from a discrete catalogue \(U\). Let each user type \(t\) have a complete DD-PREF statement \(P_t\), with rational population mass \(\mu_t\). The computational question is to maximize
\[ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t F_{\rm dd}(S,P_t,\alpha_t) \quad\text{over } S\subseteq U,\ |S|=k, \]
and determine the exact, approximation, or parameterized complexity in \(n,k,m,\tau,L\). The preference-type support can be small even when the audience is large: users choosing the same DD-PREF template are indistinguishable for this decision, so grouping them into mass preserves the model’s relevant information.

This keeps the catalogue and selected slate discrete; the population of people whose preferences determine the common choice is what becomes continuous. It is therefore a population mirror, not outcome-space continuity. It also has a strong author-recognition test: the paper itself identifies agreement among multiple users over a single subset as a remaining challenge. The absence of a numbered theorem makes the anchor less formal, but it does not erase the paper’s named algorithms, stated runtimes, or this direct route to a continuous computational question.
