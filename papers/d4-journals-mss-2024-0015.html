The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 1 is the paper’s only main anchor, and it characterizes TTC by axioms; Propositions 1–3 describe its assignment properties. None states a computational result. The paper gives a finite TTC procedure, but does not analyze its complexity. So a continuous project about computing assignments would be a new question suggested by the subject, not a mirror of a computational result in this paper.

The strongest candidate would model a large school district with preference cohorts and capacities proportional to population. That scenario is plausible, but preserving the paper’s strict priorities creates a problem for aggregation. A student’s position in each school’s priority order is part of the complete type. Keeping those positions can make every student a distinct type, so the support grows with the population. Replacing individual positions with lottery scores gives a continuum of priority types, where a school’s single highest-priority student has zero mass; the discrete TTC cycle steps then remove no positive mass. Collapsing students into priority classes allows positive-mass cohorts, but introduces ties and requires a new mass-allocation version of TTC.

That revised model also changes the characterization’s key axioms. In an atomless population, one student’s report or removal changes no mass, so strategy-proofness and consistency become vacuous at individual scale. Making them substantive requires group or positive-mass deviations; mutual best and the theorem’s top-ranked-student monotonicity likewise need mass-level versions. Those may be sensible axioms, but the result would no longer mirror Theorem 1’s characterization.

This is a strong case against a continuous mirror of this paper’s contribution, but not proof that no worthwhile related project exists. A mass model with preference cohorts and priority classes could be independently valuable. Its weakness as a mirror is that it replaces the paper’s identity-sensitive priority structure and incentive axioms, while the paper itself supplies no computational claim for continuization to transform.
