What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers the representation diameter studied in Theorem 4.4; it does not cover the power-index applications or provide a computational result from them.

Open questions for a prover

The case FOR (proponent)

The anchor requirement exposes a real gap: this paper has no numbered result asserting a computational complexity classification. Section 2 does say, without numbering the claim, that the diameter can be computed in polynomial time given the minimal winning and maximal losing coalitions. Theorem 4.4 and the later propositions give mathematical bounds, not named complexity results. I can’t present any of them as a qualifying anchor.

The closest mathematical support for a positive case is Theorem 4.4, but it is not an eligible complexity anchor. It says that if normalized representations \((q;w)\) and \((\bar q;\bar w)\) define the same weighted game, with \(0<q,\bar q<1\) and \(\Delta=\max_i w_i\), then
\[ \|w-\bar w\|_1\le \min\left\{2,\frac{4\Delta}{\min\{q,1-q\}}\right\}. \]
If \(q>\Delta\), it also gives the stronger bound
\[ \|w-\bar w\|_1\le \frac{2\Delta}{\min\{q-\Delta,1-q\}}. \]
That makes the diameter of the representation set a clear object to carry into a continuous-population model.

My lead candidate, if a mathematical theorem may motivate a mirror without satisfying the required anchor rule, is *Continuous Weight-Polytope Diameter*. Consider a large cooperative or association with \(n\gg\tau\) members in \(\tau\) standardized voting-entitlement classes. Let \(\mu_t>0\) be the fraction in class \(t\). A coalition is now any mass vector \(x\in\prod_t[0,\mu_t]\), since a nonatomic class can contribute any fraction of its members. A type-symmetric representation assigns per-member weight \(u_t\ge0\), normalized by \(\sum_t\mu_tu_t=1\), and a quota \(r\in(0,1]\); it accepts \(x\) exactly when \(\sum_tu_tx_t\ge r\).

An instance consists of rational \(\mu\) and a rational normalized representation \((q,w)\). The question is to compute the maximum weighted \(\ell_1\) distance \(\sum_t\mu_t|u_t-u'_t|\) between normalized type-level representations inducing exactly the same threshold function on the whole box, taking the closure as the paper does. A solution is the diameter and a pair attaining it in that closure.

In this model the expected answer is tractable: if \(q<1\), the threshold boundary crosses the interior of the coalition-mass box, which identifies the weight ratios; normalization then fixes the representation, so the diameter is \(0\). If \(q=1\), let \(A=\{t:w_t>0\}\). The game requires every member of each active class to support the proposal. Its representation set closes to the normalized simplex on \(A\), giving diameter \(2\) when \(|A|\ge2\), and \(0\) when \(|A|=1\). This is a precise population-continuous version of the paper’s representation-diameter question, and it covers only that object, not the power-index applications.

The soft spot is substantial: Theorem 4.4 is not a computational anchor, and the paper permits player-specific representations, whereas this model requires weights to be uniform within each entitlement class. That restriction may lead the authors to reject it as their problem. The result also hinges on arbitrary fractional coalitions, so it changes the combinatorial structure that creates the paper’s finite-player diameter. I would treat this as a plausible exploratory mirror, not a positive case that meets the stated anchor condition.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The case against is strongest at the anchor gate: this paper has no numbered theorem, lemma, or corollary classifying the complexity of a computational problem. Theorem 4.4 and the propositions are bounds, not complexity results. Section 2 says the diameter can be computed in polynomial time given the minimal winning and maximal losing coalitions, but that is an LP observation measured in the size of those lists; it gives no complexity classification in the number of players when those lists may be exponential. So the proponent’s proposed diameter question is not an eligible anchor.

Even if we let Theorem 4.4 motivate a mirror, the natural continuum makes its central object collapse. Group identical players into types of masses \(\mu_t\), and allow a coalition to take any amount \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) from each type. A type-level weight vector \(u\) represents the threshold \(u\cdot x\ge r\), with \(\sum_t\mu_tu_t=1\). For \(r<1\), the threshold boundary cuts through the interior of this box. Since every fractional coalition is available, that boundary identifies the weight ratios; normalization fixes their scale. The representation diameter is therefore zero. At \(r=1\), the same-coalition condition reduces to requiring all mass from each positively weighted type, so the closure is a simplex: its diameter is \(2\) if there are at least two such types, and \(0\) otherwise. That endpoint case is a simple degeneracy, not the quota- and maximum-weight-dependent geometry studied in the paper.

This collapse is not a complaint that the continuous answer differs from the discrete answer. It is a structural feature of the natural population mirror: the paper’s representation ambiguity comes from testing weights only on indivisible coalitions. A continuum of fractional coalitions probes the threshold boundary directly and removes that ambiguity. To preserve the paper’s object, one must keep the lattice of attainable coalitions—effectively retaining discrete membership granularity. That may support a high-multiplicity discrete study, but it is not the continuous society the programme is meant to continuize.

The power-index applications do not rescue the computational case. One could define a measure-valued power index for a nonatomic society, but this paper gives no computational result for such an index. Defining it would add a new problem, including a new account of what power means when each individual has measure zero; it would not mirror a result here.

I cannot prove that no adjacent continuous question inspired by this paper could ever be useful. But the proposed diameter mirror fails both on the programme’s anchor requirement and, in its natural fractional-coalition version, by losing the paper’s main object. That is a strong reason to pass on this paper as a continuization target.
