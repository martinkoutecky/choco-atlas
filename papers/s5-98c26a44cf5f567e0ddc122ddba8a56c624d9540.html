The continuous mirror question

Given a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) bidder types \(\theta=(a,E)\), item classes \(K\), and rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_k\), where all profiles share class values \(v_k\) and sizes \(w_k\), determine whether a succinct polynomial-time direct randomized mechanism can be computed. It must assign each bidder an integral feasible bundle \(B\subseteq E\), keep total assigned mass of each class \(k\) at most \(\sigma_k\), be truthful against every compatibility-hiding report \(E'\subseteq E\) at every fixed background report distribution, and achieve expected welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\sigma)/4\).

The model it lives in

Types are \(\theta=(a,E)\), with mass \(\mu_\theta\), capacity \(C_a\), and compatibility set \(E\); each individual receives an integral feasible bundle. Mass variables \(y_{\theta B}\) split each type among bundles, subject to \(\sum_B y_{\theta B}=\mu_\theta\) and class-supply constraints \(\sum_{\theta,B\ni k}y_{\theta B}\leq\sigma_k\). The mechanism is a succinct report-dependent rule over these mass allocations, with individual expected-value truthfulness constraints; its objective is welfare per capita.

The objection that survived

The finite-to-continuous truthfulness dictionary remains unproved: in an \(N\)-bidder market, one report can change aggregate allocation data by \(1/N\), while an atomless deviation leaves the aggregate distribution fixed. The proponent flags this difference but does not establish that finite DSIC mechanisms converge to the proposed atomless constraints.

fatal: False

  • Do finite truthful mechanisms for replicated populations converge to the stated atomless incentive constraints, or does the \(1/N\) effect require an additional condition?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is a large market of budgeted bidders or advertisers. There may be millions of bidders but only \(\tau\) distinct profiles: bidders in a profile share a capacity and the same public values and sizes for each item class, while their private information is which classes they are willing to receive. Each class has many interchangeable copies, with supply proportional to the population. This makes the mass meaningful: \(\mu_\theta\) is the fraction of bidders of type \(\theta\), and \(\sigma_k\) is the supply of item class \(k\) per bidder. Each bidder still receives an integral bundle. The continuum splits the mass of identical bidders among bundles; it does not make an individual’s bundle divisible.

Here is a common model for the two mirrors. Let \(K\) be the finite set of item classes, and let a public profile \(a\) specify capacity \(C_a\) and values and sizes \(v_{ak},w_{ak}\) for each \(k\in K\). A complete type is \(\theta=(a,E)\), where \(E\subseteq K\) is the bidder’s true compatibility set. The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over a finite support of \(\tau\) such types. A feasible bundle for \(\theta\) is \(B\subseteq E\) with \(\sum_{k\in B}w_{ak}\le C_a\), and its value is \(v_a(B)=\sum_{k\in B}v_{ak}\).

A randomized direct mechanism gives each reported type a lottery over feasible bundles. Write \(\pi_{a,E}(B;\nu)\) for the probability of bundle \(B\) when the other bidders’ report distribution is \(\nu\). Feasibility requires that the mass assigned each class not exceed its supply: at a reported distribution \(\nu\), \(\sum_{\hat\theta}\nu_{\hat\theta}\sum_{B\ni k}\pi_{\hat\theta}(B;\nu)\le\sigma_k\) for every \(k\). Truthfulness requires that for every true type \((a,E)\), every background \(\nu\), and every lie \(E'\subseteq E\), \(\sum_B\pi_{a,E}(B;\nu)v_a(B)\ge\sum_B\pi_{a,E'}(B;\nu)v_a(B)\), with bundles after the lie restricted to \(E'\). Because the population is atomless, a unilateral report does not change \(\nu\); the lottery for each type can be realized by splitting that type’s mass among integral bundles.

The welfare benchmark is \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,\sigma)\): maximize \(\sum_{\theta,B}y_{\theta B}v_\theta(B)\) over masses \(y_{\theta B}\ge0\) of type-\(\theta\) bidders assigned each feasible integral bundle, subject to \(\sum_B y_{\theta B}=\mu_\theta\) and \(\sum_{\theta,B\ni k}y_{\theta B}\le\sigma_k\). This is still the GAP-BS welfare objective, with the same private compatibility reports and incentives, evaluated per capita.

My lead is Continuous-GAP-BS Truthful Approximation. Its instance is the rational finite-support distribution, public profile data, and per-capita supplies just described. The task is to compute a succinct, polynomial-time direct randomized mechanism that is feasible and truthful and whose expected welfare is at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/O(1+\ln(U/L))\), where \(U\) and \(L>0\) are the largest and smallest compatible value densities \(v_{ak}/w_{ak}\). The \(1+\) handles the equal-density boundary, which has a constant-factor result in the paper. This mirrors Theorem 5, proved in this paper: GAP-BS has a truthful \(O(\ln(U/L))\)-approximation mechanism. Here “truthful” retains the paper’s meaning: a bidder cannot improve expected value by hiding compatible item-bin edges, and every returned assignment is feasible. The theorem’s mechanism uses density bucketing and endpoint certification; the continuous question is whether it can be implemented from the support and rational masses without expanding the population into individual bidders.

I expect this question to be Class A. The paper already supplies the matching finite-market algorithmic idea, and the mass formulation lets the mechanism work directly with the \(\tau\) types and item classes. That does not prove the compressed continuous implementation: pricing, incentive constraints, or succinct representation may still be the hard part. The question for a prover is precisely whether such a mechanism can be computed in time polynomial in \(|K|\), \(\tau\), and the input bit length.

A second, cleaner anchor is Continuous-MKP Truthful Four-Approximation. Use the same instance and solution definition, but require \(v_{ak}=v_k\) and \(w_{ak}=w_k\) across all bidder profiles; capacities and compatibility sets may vary. The task is to compute a feasible, truthful randomized mechanism with welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/4\). This mirrors Theorem 3, proved in the paper using its density-ordered greedy fractional algorithm and a meta-rounding implication cited as Theorem 2 of [6]. It establishes a truthful 4-approximation for the multiple knapsack problem in the paper’s model. I also expect the continuous question to be Class A: the density order and the LP argument are explicit, though one must still show they yield a type-compressed mechanism that preserves individual truthfulness. Theorem 4’s equal-density GAP case is an intermediate result supporting Theorem 5, so I would not add it as a separate anchor.

These mirrors cover the paper’s truthful welfare-approximation results for multiple knapsack and general GAP-BS. They do not turn the paper into a voting problem or continuize its outcomes: the agents are the population being scaled, and each agent’s bundle remains integral. The authors should recognize the mirror because it keeps their bins, compatibility-hiding deviations, capacity constraints, feasibility, and welfare objective. The per-capita supply of repeated item classes is a co-modification that makes a large population meaningful.

The weak point is that the paper’s theorems are approximation-mechanism results, not NP-hardness or standard complexity-class results. Its lower-bound discussion concerns approximation quality in a tiny market, so it does not itself show where continuous high multiplicity helps or stops helping. There is also a real limit difference: an infinitesimal bidder cannot change the aggregate report distribution, whereas a finite bidder changes it by \(1/N\). The positive case survives that caveat because the proposed questions preserve the paper’s individual incentive comparison and ask whether its mechanism can be implemented over population masses; the finite-to-continuous truthfulness dictionary remains an explicit further question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I cannot make a defensible case that defeats either anchor. The paper has named computational results: Theorems 3 and 5 give truthful approximation mechanisms, with algorithms and proofs. So the “no computational result to mirror” ground does not apply.

The strongest objection is to the proposed market regime. If the number of items stays fixed while the number of bins grows, welfare per bidder collapses. To get a nondegenerate limit, item supplies must grow with the population, turning finite GAP-BS into a market with repeated item classes and per-capita inventory. That is a substantial modeling choice. But it is exactly the kind of co-modification the programme allows, and the paper’s budgeted-advertiser application gives it a plausible setting: many similar advertisers competing for recurring classes of impressions.

That leaves Theorem 3 standing. Advertisers with a small number of public profiles, common item-class values and sizes, and repeated compatibility types form a sensible high-multiplicity regime. Randomizing the order among otherwise identical bins can remove the apparent dependence of the greedy algorithm on arbitrary identities. The continuous mechanism question remains whether the truthful four-approximation can be computed from type masses and per-capita supplies. I see no identity or degeneracy obstruction to asking it.

Theorem 5 also survives the stronger version of the mirror. Its extreme-density certification may not transfer directly when endpoint types have very small mass: the finite mechanism can single out a bin, while a single individual has zero mass in the limit. But that challenges one implementation, not the question. A positive-mass type can be split into mechanism roles, and endpoint types still have individual capacities and utilities. The paper’s density-dependent approximation result therefore motivates a real question about truthful implementation over masses, even if the answer is not supplied by simply scaling up its algorithm.

The continuous formulation keeps each bidder’s bundle integral and uses masses only to represent how many bidders receive each bundle. It keeps the paper’s compatibility-hiding incentives and welfare objective. I know of no continuous computational treatment in the supplied material that already answers those questions. The defensible caution is that the proposed polynomial-time bounds and Class A predictions are unproved; neither anchor is defeated by that uncertainty. My negative case is weak, and I would not recommend rejecting these mirrors.
