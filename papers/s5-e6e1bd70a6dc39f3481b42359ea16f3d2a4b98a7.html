The case FOR (proponent)

The paper contains no eligible named computational result: it has no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition establishing a complexity or algorithmic claim. The closest anchor is its optimization problem in Equation (3), which the authors train against and evaluate experimentally. Table 1 reports welfare, regret, and budget-balance measurements; Algorithm 1 gives a training procedure. Neither is a numbered result of the required kind. The cited Myerson–Satterthwaite impossibility is external and economic, not a computational result. So there is no honest per-anchor mirror to supply.

The strongest *unanchored* candidate I see is an atomless double-auction welfare-design problem, closely following Equation (3). Picture a large exchange for one homogeneous commodity: buyers and sellers share a finite set of value or cost ticks, with many participants at each tick and far fewer distinct types than participants. A type is a side and a value or cost; mass is that type’s fraction of the whole market. Supply scales with the market, so each unit of mass can trade at most once.

For an instance, give finite report sets \(\mathcal V_B,\mathcal V_S\subseteq\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\) and binary-rational masses \(\mu^B_v,\mu^S_s\) summing to \(1\). The mass distributions are truthful reports in the continuum. Find buyer allocation probabilities \(x_v\), seller allocation probabilities \(y_s\), per-agent payments \(p_v\) and revenues \(r_s\), and traded mass \(q_{vs}\). Require \(q_{vs}\ge 0\), \(\sum_s q_{vs}=\mu^B_vx_v\), \(\sum_v q_{vs}=\mu^S_sy_s\), and \(0\le x_v,y_s\le1\). Require truthful individual rationality, \(vx_v-p_v\ge0\) and \(r_s-sy_s\ge0\); dominant-strategy truthfulness across all allowed reports; and aggregate budget balance, \(\sum_v\mu^B_vp_v\ge\sum_s\mu^S_sr_s\). Maximize social surplus, \(\sum_{v,s}q_{vs}(v-s)\). A solution is an allocation and transfer rule attaining the maximum. In this limit, a single agent’s misreport does not change the aggregate report distribution.

For fixed finite report sets, this candidate is a linear program, so I would expect it to be tractable. It preserves the paper’s core design objective—welfare subject to incentive, individual-rationality, and budget constraints—and its matching and transfer decisions. Further questions include how its optimum relates to finite markets as their size grows, and what changes with a continuous value domain or stronger budget requirements.

The weakness is substantial: the paper studies small finite auctions, and the atomless limit removes each agent’s effect on the market outcome. The candidate therefore gives a concrete continuum version of the paper’s design objective, but it does not mirror a named theorem or complexity result from the paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is that this paper offers no named computational result to mirror. Equation (3) formulates a learning objective, Algorithm 1 gives a training procedure, and Table 1 reports experiments. None establishes the complexity of a computational problem or gives a complexity-guaranteed algorithm. So there is no result-anchored continuous mirror in the sense the proponents were asked to identify.

The proposed atomless-market LP also needs more specification before it mirrors the paper. DoubleRegretNet maps individual bid profiles to matches, payments, and revenues, and measures a player’s gain from a unilateral misreport against other agents’ reports. A type distribution alone cannot record one agent’s deviation: that agent has zero mass. The mechanism must retain the deviator’s report separately and say how that report interacts with the background distribution. Moreover, an LP for one fixed distribution \(\mu\) enforces incentives only in that background. A closer analogue needs either to average regret over a specified prior, as the paper does, or to define the mechanism and its incentive constraints over all relevant background distributions. That is a larger object than the candidate’s stated fixed-distribution LP.

Still, these are repairs, not a decisive rejection. With finite valuation ticks, one can define report-specific allocations and transfers at a fixed background distribution and impose constraints such as
\[ v x_v(\mu)-p_v(\mu)\ge v x_r(\mu)-p_r(\mu) \]
for every buyer value \(v\) and report \(r\), alongside mass feasibility and aggregate budget balance. Those are finite linear constraints. A market with many traders per tick and supply that scales with the population is also a plausible high-multiplicity regime. The paper’s small experimental instances do not rule it out.

There is continuum-agent mechanism-design work in neighboring settings, including optimal allocation with a continuum of agents and private budgets, but that is not this double-auction computational problem and does not settle it. [Mechanism design with budget constraints and a population of agents](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0899825619300193).

So the negative case is strong against claiming that this paper already supplies a complexity result to continuize. It is weak against the broader claim that a worthwhile mirror exists. A repaired finite-type market-design problem remains credible, and I cannot honestly rule it out in every scenario.
