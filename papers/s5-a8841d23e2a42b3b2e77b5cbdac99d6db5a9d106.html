The case FOR (proponent)

Strictly, this paper has no qualifying named computational-complexity result: it proves no theorem asserting NP-hardness, membership in P, FPT, or a comparable classification. Its strongest anchor is therefore a theorem-level equilibrium result, not a formal complexity result. The best positive case is consequently qualified.

The strongest anchor is Theorem 4.3 (proved in this paper), the Unimodularity Theorem: for a demand type \(D\), every pair of concave valuations of type \(D\) has a competitive equilibrium for every relevant integer supply bundle if and only if \(D\) is unimodular. Corollary 4.4 extends this from pairs to every finite set of such valuations. The paper’s Algorithm 6.5 then gives a procedure for checking equilibrium existence using demand-type tests, mixed-volume/intersection calculations, and discrete-convexity checks, although the authors explicitly decline to give a clean overall complexity bound.

My lead mirror would be Mass-Demand-Type Competitive Equilibrium\(_\infty\).

An instance contains finitely many exact valuation types \(T\), with \(|T|=\tau\). Type \(t\) has a finite integral bundle domain \(A_t\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) and valuation \(u_t:A_t\to\mathbb Q\). A rational mass \(\mu_t\) of the population has type \(t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The regime is \(N\gg\tau\): for example, a large product-mix auction or labour market with many recurring firm or bidder profiles and only a small number of distinct bundle valuations. Every unit of mass receives an integral bundle; only the population assignment is divisible.

Given a rational per-capita supply \(q\in\mathbb Q^n\), the task is to find masses \(y_{t,x}\ge 0\) and prices \(p\in\mathbb R^n\) satisfying

\[ \sum_{x\in A_t}y_{t,x}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,x}y_{t,x}x=q, \]

and

\[ y_{t,x}>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\in\arg\max_{z\in A_t}\{u_t(z)-p\cdot z\}. \]

A solution is therefore an exact competitive equilibrium: an integral bundle for every infinitesimal agent, an aggregate supply balance, and supporting prices. The objective is feasibility/search, not welfare maximisation.

This is a genuine population mirror of Theorem 4.3. A finite election-sized market with \(N\mu_t\) copies of each type is recovered when the masses are rational and denominators are cleared. But the continuum can split a type across several demanded bundles. Thus the hotel-room failure behind the non-unimodular direction disappears: the population can divide between bundles even though no individual receives a fractional bundle.

The expected result is Class A in the explicit representation. The equilibrium is obtained from the configuration LP

\[ \max \sum_{t,x}u_t(x)y_{t,x} \]

subject to the two balance equations above. Its dual supplies prices \(p\), and complementary slackness gives the demand condition. The running time is polynomial in the explicitly listed bundles, \(\tau\), \(n\), and the encoding length. With exponentially described bundle domains, the natural question becomes a pricing problem,

\[ \max_{x\in A_t}\{u_t(x)-p\cdot x\}, \]

which is exactly the sort of separation issue that the ChoCo programme wants to expose.

The authors should recognise this as their problem: it retains their valuations, indivisible bundles, prices, demand types, aggregate demand, and equilibrium notion. The only substantive change is replacing named repeated agents by an atomless mass of interchangeable agents. The paper itself already treats aggregation and identical copies geometrically, so the high-multiplicity regime is economically credible.

The weak point is substantial: the continuous model may be viewed as ordinary convexification of the finite-agent equilibrium problem, making the paper’s central non-unimodularity phenomenon vanish rather than illuminating it. Moreover, because Theorem 4.3 is not a complexity theorem, this is not a fully compliant ChoCo anchor under the strictest reading. The strongest follow-ups would be to determine whether compact demand-type representations admit polynomial pricing, quantify the finite-\(N\) integrality gap, and identify which non-unimodular discrete failures persist under approximate or partially indivisible populations.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s anchor is not formally eligible. Theorem 4.3 is a structural equilibrium-existence characterization, not a computational result: it proves neither membership in \(\mathrm{P}\), hardness, approximation, nor parameterized tractability. Algorithm 6.5 does not repair this; the authors explicitly decline to give a complexity bound, and its mixed-volume and discrete-convexity subroutines are only described procedurally.

More importantly, Theorem 4.3 loses its mathematical content under the proposed atomless mirror. Let \(D_t(p)\) be type \(t\)’s demanded bundles. With mass \(\mu_t\), the attainable aggregate demand at price \(p\) is

\[ Q_\mu(p)=\sum_t \mu_t\operatorname{conv}(D_t(p)), \]

because an atomless mass of identical agents can be divided among several integral bundles. The feasible aggregate-supply region is

\[ Q_\mu=\sum_t\mu_t\operatorname{conv}(A_t). \]

For every \(q\in Q_\mu\), the configuration LP

\[ \max \sum_{t,x} y_{t,x}u_t(x) \]

subject to

\[ \sum_x y_{t,x}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,x} y_{t,x}x=q,\qquad y_{t,x}\ge 0 \]

has an optimum. Its dual supplies supporting prices, and complementary slackness gives precisely the proposed equilibrium condition. Thus equilibrium exists for every feasible \(q\), independently of unimodularity, demand types, tropical intersections, or subgroup indices. This is not merely a different answer: the integer-lattice obstruction that Theorem 4.3 studies has disappeared from the object being computed.

The hotel-room counterexample illustrates the collapse. In the finite economy, the aggregate demand set can contain \((1,1)\) in its convex hull without actually demanding it, because agents must receive whole bundles. In the atomless economy, the relevant type masses can be split at the indifference price, implementing \((1,1)\) exactly. Keeping each infinitesimal agent’s bundle integral does not preserve the obstruction; fractional assignment of mass is already enough to convexify aggregate demand.

The obvious repairs do not produce a better mirror. Requiring the total supply to be integral still permits fractional mass assignments. Requiring each type’s entire mass to receive one bundle makes the mass block indivisible; then the model is a finite weighted-agent problem with type weights, not an atomless continuous society. Restricting prices to be generic removes the ties needed to clear intermediate supplies and makes the model fail for an artificial reason.

The proponent’s pricing objection also does not restore the anchor. If bundle domains are explicitly listed, the configuration LP is an ordinary polynomial-size LP. If domains are exponentially or implicitly represented, the difficulty is the per-type valuation or winner-determination problem

\[ \max_{x\in A_t}\{u_t(x)-p\cdot x\}, \]

which already exists with a single type and is independent of population multiplicity. It may be a worthwhile optimization problem, but it is not a computational consequence of continuizing the population, nor a mirror of Theorem 4.3.

The same diagnosis defeats an attempted upgrade to Theorem 5.12: its intersection counts and lattice multiplicities measure precisely the indivisible finite-agent failures that atomless mixing erases. Matching or auction applications could motivate separate fractional-market research, but that would be a new project rather than a continuous computational mirror of a result proved here.

The negative case is therefore strong on eligibility and on the proposed anchor’s substance, but not airtight as a universal modelling claim. A genuine high-multiplicity market with repeated valuation types is economically plausible, and one could study finite-population rounding gaps or residual infeasibility after the continuous relaxation. Those may be worthwhile ChoCo questions. They are, however, new questions without a qualifying computational theorem in this paper; the proposed equilibrium-existence mirror itself collapses to generic LP duality.
