What the mirror covers

The candidate mirror captures the Kemeny objective and average Kendall distance described in the preliminaries; it does not capture the DDP-Helnaksort mechanism, its privacy guarantees, or the experimental results.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest plausible population mirror is Continuous Kemeny Aggregation. Take types to be complete rankings of a fixed set \(A\) of alternatives. An instance is a finite support \(T\subseteq S_m\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) summing to \(1\). Given many participants with relatively few distinct rankings—say, a large crowd ranking a short list—return a ranking \(R\) minimizing
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t K(R,t), \]
where \(K\) is Kendall distance. This makes the population itself continuous, and the objective matches the paper’s description of Kemeny aggregation and its average Kendall distance utility measure. Any discrete-profile hardness for this optimization would transfer through the encoding \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), so the natural expectation would be Class B.

But this is not anchored by a named computational result in the paper. The only numbered results are Theorem 1, cited from Cheu et al., which states a privacy amplification guarantee for a shuffled protocol, and Theorem 2, proved here, which claims LDP and DP guarantees for DDP-Helnaksort when \(K=1\). Neither states a complexity bound, algorithmic tractability result, or hardness result. The paper reports experiments, but gives no named computational result of the kind required for an anchor.

The mirror’s weakest point is also substantial: Continuous Kemeny Aggregation captures the paper’s aggregation objective, but the paper’s contribution is a private HRA-based mechanism, not an exact Kemeny optimizer. Its privacy guarantees depend on a finite number \(n\) of individual records and the shuffle process; they do not directly yield a continuous-population privacy result. So there is a sensible continuous population question nearby, but no qualifying anchor here on which to build the requested positive case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that this paper has no named computational result for ChoCo to mirror. Theorem 1 is a cited privacy amplification guarantee; Theorem 2 gives privacy guarantees for DDP-Helnaksort. Neither establishes an algorithmic complexity bound or a hardness result. The paper proposes and experimentally evaluates a private aggregation mechanism. That is a real computational contribution, but its stated result is about privacy and empirical utility, not the complexity landscape of a computational social choice problem.

Continuous Kemeny Aggregation is the best nearby candidate, and its population model is sensible: many people can share rankings, represented by a finite support of ranking types with rational masses. But it changes the target. The paper describes Kemeny optimal aggregation and average Kendall distance, while its proposed mechanism uses HRA; it does not prove that HRA minimizes the Kemeny objective. Optimizing that objective over a continuous society would therefore be a worthwhile question about Kemeny, but not a mirror of a computational result in this paper.

A closer mirror would retain DDP-Helnaksort and replace empirical frequencies with ranking masses. That captures the population, but the paper’s privacy question still depends on finite individuals: adjacency concerns one person’s record, and shuffling relies on messages whose sources can be hidden. In an atomless population, one person has zero mass; defining a nonzero privacy unit requires retaining a finite population scale such as \(n\), and with it the individual messages and shuffle process. The resulting question might be useful, but it would be a new privacy analysis rather than a continuous computational mirror of a result the paper establishes.

So the negative case is strongest as a verdict on this paper as an anchor: its nearest continuous questions are plausible neighboring projects, but none mirrors a named computational result here. The broader claim that no scenario could make any adjacent population question worthwhile is weaker; the paper alone cannot rule out such a project.
