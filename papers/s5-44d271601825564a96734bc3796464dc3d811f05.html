The continuous mirror question

Given τ agent types with rational masses μ, deterministic grand-coalition value g, and K i.i.d. uncertainty samples represented by rational piecewise-affine functions U_k on a listed common triangulation of Q(μ) = {q : 0 ≤ q_t ≤ μ_t}, with U_k(μ) = g and a promise that C_K is nonempty, compute C_K = {a ≥ 0 : μ^⊤a = g, q^⊤a ≥ max_{k ≤ K} U_k(q) for every q ∈ Q(μ)} and a minimum-cardinality I ⊆ {1,…,K} such that C_I = C_K; then instantiate the PAC instability certificate using |I|.

The model it lives in

A large prosumer population is partitioned into τ payoff-equivalent types with masses μ. A coalition chooses any mass vector q in Q(μ) and receives q^⊤a under per-type payoff rates a; each uncertainty realization determines a coalition-value function U(q,δ), represented by the listed piecewise-affine functions, while the grand coalition has deterministic value g.

The objection that survived

The claim that samples supporting nonredundant vertex constraints yield a smallest compression set is unsupported: redundancy among constraints can interact across the full core description.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 1’s collective stability guarantee for a nonempty scenario core; it leaves the relaxed least-core result, Theorem 2, and the numerical experiments aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • What is the complexity of finding a minimum-cardinality sample subset that preserves C_K, even with a listed triangulation?
  • How do the continuous core and its PAC certificate relate to finite societies whose type counts approximate μ?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a continuous-population version of the paper’s scenario core for a large pool of standardized energy prosumers. The paper itself names no theorem establishing a classical complexity result such as membership in P or NP-hardness. My lead anchor is therefore computationally relevant, but it is a PAC guarantee rather than a complexity classification.

Theorem 1, proved in this paper by adapting [23, Theorem 6], is the anchor. Assuming every sampled scenario core is nonempty, the paper takes the algorithm that maps \(K\) uncertainty samples to the scenario core and a compression function for that algorithm. For confidence parameter \(\beta\), it defines \(\varepsilon(K)=1\) and, for smaller \(s\), chooses \(\varepsilon(s)\) so that
\[ \sum_{s=0}^{K-1}\binom Ks(1-\varepsilon(s))^{K-s}=\beta. \]
If \(s_K\) is the size of a smallest compression set, then with probability at least \(1-\beta\) over the \(K\) samples, the probability that a fresh uncertainty realization makes some allocation in the scenario core unstable is at most \(\varepsilon(s_K)\).

Here is the continuous problem I would hand to a prover, Continuous Scenario-Core Compression. There are \(\tau\) agent types with masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). A coalition is described by its mass vector
\[ q\in Q(\mu):=\{q\in\mathbb R^\tau:0\le q_t\le\mu_t\}. \]
An allocation is a per-agent payoff vector \(a\in\mathbb R^\tau_{\ge0}\), so coalition \(q\) receives \(q^\top a\). The grand coalition has deterministic value \(g\). Each of the \(K\) observed uncertainty samples supplies a coalition-value function \(U_k(q)\), with \(U_k(\mu)=g\). The instance also gives a shared rational triangulation of \(Q(\mu)\) on which every \(U_k\) is affine, and promises that the scenario core is nonempty.

The task is to return the exact scenario core
\[ C_K=\left\{a\ge0:\ \mu^\top a=g,\quad q^\top a\ge\max_{k\le K}U_k(q)\ \text{for every }q\in Q(\mu)\right\}, \]
and a smallest sample subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}\) for which replacing \(K\) by \(I\) leaves this set unchanged. The solution is the resulting core representation and compression set; its size \(s_K=|I|\) gives the Theorem 1 risk certificate.

For this explicit input, I expect Class A. On each cell of the triangulation, the stability inequality is affine in \(q\), so it is enough to check the cell’s vertices. The core therefore has a finite linear description. At each vertex the strongest sampled value is a finite maximum; under the paper’s own nondegeneracy convention of unique maximizing samples, the samples supporting the nonredundant vertex constraints give a smallest compression set. The work is polynomial in \(\tau\), \(K\), the number of listed vertices, and the rational bit length. This tractability claim belongs to the proposed mirror and its explicit encoding; Theorem 1 itself does not establish that running-time bound.

The high-multiplicity regime is plausible in energy markets, which the paper itself cites as an application area. Think of a very large pool of households with the same generation assets, demand pattern, and contract terms, grouped into tens or hundreds of complete payoff-relevant types. Mass is the fraction of the pool in each type, and \(\delta\) represents a shared price or weather realization. Coalitions can then form from any real-valued amount of each type. This keeps the paper’s central objects—coalitional value, defection, allocation, and the core—while making the population, rather than just the data, continuous.

The scope is Theorem 1’s collective guarantee for a nonempty scenario core. I would not stretch this mirror to cover the paper’s relaxed least-core result or its other claims. It generates follow-up questions about convergence from discrete type-count grids to the continuous core, and about where tractability ends when coalition values are given by succinct formulas instead of an explicit piecewise-linear representation.

The weakest point is the value-function model. The paper specifies values for discrete coalitions; it does not supply values at fractional type compositions or establish that these functions are piecewise linear. Those are natural modeling choices for aggregated populations, but they are additional assumptions. The positive case survives if the authors accept that composition-indexed value functions preserve their core-stability question. If a grader requires an anchor that is itself a named worst-case complexity result, this paper has no such anchor; Theorem 1 supports a genuine computational mirror, but does not meet that narrower criterion.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 1 is the only anchor in the positive case, and its main result is a PAC stability guarantee. It assumes a map from uncertainty samples to the scenario core and bounds the risk of instability using the size of a compression set. The paper does not analyze the running time of that map or prove a complexity classification for computing the core or its compression set. Its \(K\) samples describe uncertainty; turning the agents into a continuum leaves that statistical sample size in place. So the theorem supplies little of ChoCo’s target complexity landscape.

The strongest mirror is still coherent: a large pool of interchangeable prosumers, grouped into \(\tau\) types, with coalitions described by composition vectors \(q\) and payoffs described by per-type rates \(a\). That gives the paper’s stability question a continuous-population version. The cost is a new modeling assumption: the paper gives coalition values \(u(S,\delta)\) for discrete subsets, while the mirror needs values \(U(q,\delta)\) at fractional compositions. In the prosumer example this can be plausible, but it is an additional game model, not data supplied by the paper.

Under the proposed shared piecewise-affine triangulation, the core computation is ordinary finite-dimensional linear optimization. On each cell, impose \(q^\top a\ge U_k(q)\) for every sample \(k\); because each side is affine there, it suffices to impose those inequalities at the cell’s vertices. The resulting computation is polynomial in the listed representation. That result would chiefly show that an explicitly listed polyhedral model is an LP. If the triangulation is exponentially large in \(\tau\), the input already carries that cost. If the value functions are encoded compactly instead, the difficulty moves to their separation or evaluation problem, which the paper does not specify.

The compression task needs more care than the positive case gives it. A sample can support a constraint at a vertex and still be unnecessary to preserve the whole core because other constraints imply it. Conversely, constraints from several samples can interact across vertices. Thus identifying samples that maximize values at vertices does not by itself establish a *smallest* subset preserving the core. Finding that subset may be a separate combinatorial optimization problem; its difficulty would come from minimizing an equivalent constraint system, rather than from continuizing the population.

The empty-core extension does not supply an independent escape from this issue. A continuous ζ-core can be defined, and the paper’s relaxation idea can be carried over, but its computational content again depends on the representation and separation of \(U(q,\delta)\). The population limit alone does not determine those.

This is a strong objection to treating Theorem 1 as a promising ChoCo complexity mirror: the theorem’s core contribution is statistical, and the proposed tractable case gets its answer from an explicit polyhedral encoding. It does not establish that *no* worthwhile mirror exists. A symmetric prosumer continuum remains a sensible model, and asking how its core behaves under compact value-function descriptions could be worthwhile. The honest negative case is therefore strongest as a claim about fit with this programme’s complexity agenda, and weak as the universal claim that no scenario could merit study.
