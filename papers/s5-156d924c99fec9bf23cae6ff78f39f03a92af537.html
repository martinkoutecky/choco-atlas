The continuous mirror question

Given \(m\) item classes, rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), and a rational mass distribution \(\mu\) over a finite support \(T\) of normalized, monotone, submodular valuation types, determine whether there is a truthful-in-expectation direct mechanism achieving expected welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q)/O(\log m/\log\log m)\). Each individual must receive an integral bundle and a payment lottery; the mechanism must specify how a named individual’s report, including an off-support report, selects their lottery while aggregate allocations respect supply. State the communication or query complexity for representing and evaluating this rule in \(m,\tau,L\).

The model it lives in

A continuum of buyers with valuation types \(t\in T\), masses \(\mu_t\), and item supplies \(q_j\) per unit mass. The benchmark maximizes \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\) subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le q_j\), and \(x_{t,S}\ge0\). A mechanism assigns integral bundles and payments to individuals through report-contingent lotteries, while satisfying aggregate feasibility, individual truthfulness in expectation, and the stated approximation guarantee.

The objection that survived

The mass distribution alone does not encode a unilateral report by a zero-mass agent, and communicating with individual agents may make the paper’s total communication bound depend on population size rather than \(m,\tau,L\).

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 2.9’s submodular-bidder approximation guarantee and raises its constant-ratio question for the high-multiplicity setting. It does not establish the same guarantee for subadditive valuations or mirror the paper’s comparisons with other mechanism classes.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the \(O(\log m/\log\log m)\) guarantee be achieved with a precise individual-report interface and complexity polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\)?
  • Can the high-multiplicity setting achieve a constant approximation, or extend the guarantee to subadditive valuations?

The case FOR (proponent)

My strongest case rests on Theorem 2.9, proved in this note: “There exists a truthful-in-expectation \(O(\log m/\log\log m)\)-approximation mechanism for combinatorial auctions with submodular bidders. The algorithm uses polynomial communication.” I take the theorem’s stated scope literally: the abstract says “subadditive,” but Theorem 2.9 says “submodular,” so my mirror uses submodular valuations. The theorem is proved here using the paper’s range and rounding arguments, alongside ingredients cited from earlier work.

A plausible high-multiplicity setting is a large procurement or service-allocation platform with many buyers and \(m\) standardized item classes. Buyers in the same operational segment have the same complete valuation for bundles, drawn from a relatively small set of types. The population may be enormous while the number \(\tau\) of distinct valuation types remains modest. To keep the market meaningful as the population grows, supply of each item class scales with population: each agent has per-capita supply \(q_j\) of class \(j\), so a finite population of \(N\) buyers has \(Nq_j\) copies. Each buyer still receives an integral bundle. The fractional quantity is the mass of a type assigned to each bundle, not a fractional bundle handed to an individual.

I would call the mirror High-Multiplicity Truthful Welfare Approximation for Combinatorial Auctions. An instance has item classes \(M\), a finite support \(T\) of normalized, monotone, submodular valuation types \(v_t:2^M\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}\), rational population masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\). The masses and supplies are given in binary; valuations use the paper’s black-box communication model. The welfare benchmark is

\[ \mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q)= \max \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}v_t(S) \]

subject to

\[ \sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}\le\mu_t \quad\text{for each }t, \qquad \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le q_j \quad\text{for each }j, \qquad x_{t,S}\ge0. \]

Here \(x_{t,S}\) is the mass of type \(t\) receiving bundle \(S\). A mechanism must output a randomized, globally feasible assignment in which each individual receives an integral bundle, together with payments. It must be truthful in expectation for each individual under quasilinear utility, achieve expected welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}(\mu,q)/O(\log m/\log\log m)\), and use communication polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), where \(L\) is the input bit length. For truthfulness, the mechanism supplies an allocation-and-payment lottery for each report given the population’s report distribution; an individual must prefer the lottery for their true type to the lottery for any misreport. A solution is such a mechanism plus proofs of feasibility, truthfulness, approximation, and the communication bound.

I expect this mirror to be Class A, though the theorem does not prove it automatically. The paper already optimizes a fractional allocation LP using proxy valuations and then rounds while preserving the incentive guarantee. Grouping identical agents into mass types makes the LP smaller in the natural high-multiplicity representation. The main work is to adapt the rounding and payment argument to per-capita supplies and measurable population assignments. The authors could recognize this as their auction problem: valuation, bundle, welfare, and truthful-in-expectation approximation all remain central. Scaling supply is the co-modification needed to avoid a vanishing-welfare limit when the number of buyers grows but the number of copies stays fixed.

This case covers Theorem 2.9 only; it does not claim that the paper’s comparisons with deterministic or universally truthful mechanisms, or its open constant-ratio question, have already been mirrored. The mirror raises those questions again in the high-multiplicity regime: does the stated ratio hold with complexity depending on \(\tau\) and \(L\)? Can it improve to a constant? And does the theorem’s guarantee extend to subadditive valuations, as the abstract suggests?

The weakest point is that both supply scaling and truthfulness need careful treatment. The paper has one copy of each item, while this limit has supply proportional to population. And in an atomless population, an individual has zero mass, so a continuum mechanism must define report-contingent individual lotteries and payments, not merely optimize over aggregate masses. Those choices are substantial proof obligations. Still, they preserve the recognizable combinatorial-auction question rather than replacing it with welfare optimization alone.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only anchor is Theorem 2.9, and its guarantee is about communication, not running time. The paper assumes bidders can compute without limit and provide black-box answers; it even says the mechanism has no known implementation using demand queries or another natural query model, and calls the result unsuitable as a “real” mechanism. So this theorem supplies no polynomial-time algorithm or pricing result for a continuous population to inherit. Nor do \(m,\tau,L\) bound the size of the valuation input: the proposed mirror leaves each valuation as a black box. One could pose a continuous communication question, but that would not recover the computational optimization story the theorem appears to invite.

The buyer-segment example and per-capita supply make high multiplicity plausible. The obstacle is preserving the theorem’s incentive and communication model at the same time. The mass vector \(\mu\) supports the aggregate allocation LP, but it does not specify what happens to a particular person who misreports. In an atomless population, that person’s report does not change \(\mu\). If the mechanism sees only \(\mu\), it cannot make their own lottery depend on their report; if it also sees each person’s report and returns individual lotteries and payments, it must retain person-level communication. Compressing reports to one record per type instead assumes away an individual deviation. And the support \(T\) describes types present in the population, not necessarily the off-support reports a truthful mechanism must handle.

A stronger repair would define a continuum direct mechanism with report-contingent menus, individual payments, aggregate feasibility, and an explicit communication measure—perhaps per person or for describing the menu. That could be a worthwhile research problem. But it changes the resource being measured and adds incentive structure not captured by the aggregate LP; it is not a continuization of Theorem 2.9’s stated communication bound.

This is the strongest negative case I can make, but it does not establish that no worthwhile mirror exists. A high-multiplicity auction is a sensible regime, and the repaired direct-mechanism question may be valuable. The case against the mirror is strongest when the target is the theorem as written: it offers no computational bound to continuize, while the proposed mass-only model does not by itself preserve the theorem’s individual incentive problem.
