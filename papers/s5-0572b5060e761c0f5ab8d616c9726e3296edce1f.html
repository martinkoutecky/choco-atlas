The continuous mirror question

Given a candidate set \(C=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), a binary-rational society \(\mu\) supported on \(\tau\) complete rankings, and distinct Borda scores under \(\mu\), compute the infimum mass that must be transferred between ranking types so the resulting Borda ranking differs from the original by Kendall–\(\tau\) distance at least \(1\). A fixed candidate order resolves ties. Transfers \(x_{\sigma,\pi}\ge 0\) have total outgoing mass at most \(\mu_\sigma\) for each source type, and the objective is to minimize \(\sum_{\sigma\ne\pi}x_{\sigma,\pi}\).

The model it lives in

Ranking types are permutations in \(S_n\), each with mass \(\mu_\sigma\). Decision variables \(x_{\sigma,\pi}\) transfer mass from type \(\sigma\) to type \(\pi\), with outgoing mass bounded by \(\mu_\sigma\); minimize total transferred mass subject to changing the Borda order by at least one pairwise reversal.

The objection that survived

The opponent notes that distribution-level breakdown under total-variation attacks is already studied for ranking statistics, and that the distance-one Borda threshold may reduce to a simple fractional calculation.

The case FOR (proponent)

The best positive case is a narrow one: Continuous Borda Breakdown, mirroring the paper’s robustness analysis. The paper has no named result classifying a problem as NP-hard, in P, FPT, or similar. My anchor is therefore a named robustness result, not a complexity theorem: Lemma 1, proved in this paper in Appendix D.6.

Lemma 1 says that for an empirical society \(\hat P_N\), any score rule with decomposition \(S(\hat P_N)=\psi(\mathbb E_{\hat P_N}\varphi)\), and any \(\delta>0\), the breakdown fraction satisfies
\[ \varepsilon^-_{\hat P_N,S} :=\frac{r_\psi(\bar x)}{2\|\!|\varphi|\!\|} \le \varepsilon^*_{\hat P_N,S}(\delta) \le \frac12, \]
where \(\bar x=\mathbb E_{\hat P_N}\varphi\), \(\|\!|\varphi|\!\|=\max_{\sigma\in S_n}\|\varphi(\sigma)\|\), and \(\varepsilon^*\) is the minimum fraction of votes that can be changed to move the consensus by Kendall–\(\tau\) distance at least \(\delta\). The lower bound comes from the stability radius of the ranking under score perturbations; the upper bound says a majority can enforce a consensus. It is proved here, rather than cited as a prior result.

Here is the corresponding continuous problem. Continuous Borda Breakdown, distance one takes a candidate set \(C=\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and a binary-rational distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings in \(S_n\), given by its \(\tau\) positive-mass rankings. Assume the Borda scores under \(\mu\) are distinct, and break any later ties by a fixed candidate order. A ranking \(\sigma\) has Borda score vector \((\sum_{\rho\in S_n}\nu_\rho\rho(i))_{i\in C}\) under society \(\nu\), sorted in ascending order. The question is: what is the infimum mass that must be reassigned among ranking types to produce a Borda ranking at Kendall–\(\tau\) distance at least one from the Borda ranking under \(\mu\)? A solution gives the infimum and, for any requested tolerance, a mass transfer within that tolerance.

Formally, choose \(x_{\sigma,\pi}\ge 0\), the mass moved from ranking type \(\sigma\) to type \(\pi\), with total outgoing mass from each \(\sigma\) at most \(\mu_\sigma\). The resulting society is
\[ \mu'_\rho=\mu_\rho-\sum_{\pi\ne\rho}x_{\rho,\pi} +\sum_{\sigma\ne\rho}x_{\sigma,\rho}. \]
Minimize \(\sum_{\sigma\ne\pi}x_{\sigma,\pi}\) subject to the Borda rankings under \(\mu\) and \(\mu'\) differing. This is the continuous counterpart of changing an integer number of votes in Lemma 1: mass is the share of the population whose ranking changes.

A plausible regime is a large preference-annotation service where many people rank the same small set of candidate responses. Each complete ranking is a type; its mass is the fraction of annotators holding it. With many annotators and fixed \(n\), the population can be large while the number of possible types is at most \(n!\), and often the observed support \(\tau\) is much smaller. The authors should recognize the question: it keeps their Borda consensus and vote-corruption objective, while replacing integer vote counts with mass.

I expect this distance-one version to be tractable. A changed total order must reverse at least one candidate pair. For each pair, the mass needed to reverse its Borda score difference can be found by taking mass from the supported rankings with the largest effect on that difference and moving it to rankings that put the pair in the opposite order. That is a fractional selection problem over the \(\tau\) input types; taking the best pair gives the threshold. This suggests an algorithm polynomial in \(n,\tau,L\), without enumerating all \(n!\) destination rankings. Lemma 1 supplies general bounds for this threshold, but does not compute its exact value. Natural follow-ups are the threshold for distance at least \(\delta>1\), the corresponding problem for Copeland, and how a communication network changes the amount of corruption needed over finite time.

The soft spot is substantial: this mirrors a secondary robustness result, not the paper’s main decentralized-convergence results, and it leaves out the communication graph. Also, Section 2 already defines ranking aggregation over distributions, so this is a fairly close extension of the paper’s own language; the discrete part of the mirror lies specifically in its empirical breakdown measure and integer vote changes. The case is strongest as a focused, plausible mass-optimization question—not as evidence that the paper’s full decentralized algorithm has a continuous counterpart.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The Borda breakdown anchor is the proponent’s strongest choice. A large annotation service with many people ranking the same candidate responses is a sensible high-multiplicity regime, and ballot types preserve everything the central Borda breakdown question uses. I cannot reject it on the ground that people are individually identifiable.

The difficulty for the positive case is that the paper already defines ranking aggregation on a probability distribution \(P\) over rankings and Borda scores as expected ranks. Its empirical breakdown definition restricts the adversary to changing finitely many votes. Allowing arbitrary mass transfers changes that restriction, but does not introduce a new continuous society model. The cited literature already defines breakdown for statistics on probability distributions using a total-variation attack budget, and studies that notion for consensus-ranking medians. That is close conceptual prior art, though it does not by itself settle the exact Borda threshold or its complexity. [Goibert et al., *Robust Consensus in Ranking Data Analysis*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/goibert23a/goibert23a.pdf)

The stronger Borda version is also unusually small as a computational question. A ranking changes once some pair of candidates reverses order. For each pair, the score difference is linear in the society, and the minimum moved mass to cross its boundary can be found by a fractional selection over the supported rankings and the most effective destination rankings. Taking the minimum over candidate pairs gives the distance-one threshold. This looks like a direct finite calculation from the support, rather than a new complexity landscape. That is not a valid objection merely because the answer may be easy; it does limit the case for spending programme effort on this anchor when its continuous robustness formulation is already familiar.

A network-aware extension might add more substance, but then a type must capture the node’s position and role in the communication graph because those determine how quickly contamination spreads. In many graphs that would leave little repeated type mass. A continuum graph or a continuum of communication locations could restore multiplicity, but it would make the communication structure another continuous object and move away from the paper’s simple Borda breakdown result.

So the negative case is about novelty and programme-level payoff, not about whether a mass formulation is sensible. I cannot honestly establish the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists: this anchor is well posed, and a clean exact mass threshold could be a useful small result. The case against is that it is already close to the paper’s distributional formulation and to existing continuous breakdown work, while its natural Borda computation appears elementary.
