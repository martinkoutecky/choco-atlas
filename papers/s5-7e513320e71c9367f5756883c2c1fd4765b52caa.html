A large committee with a few voter types might motivate a new type-level extension of empirical power indices, but individual pivotality and within-type coalition behavior would need careful redefinition. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no eligible anchor in this paper. It gives formulas for empirical Banzhaf and satisfaction indices, then compares numerical integration methods and reports a Gibbs-sampling example. It proves no complexity result, and gives no exact or approximation guarantee for those methods. So there is no computational theorem whose population-continuous mirror could change what is known.

The strongest possible repair would study large decision-making bodies with repeated voter types and estimate type-level power or satisfaction from historical votes. But the paper’s \(p\) is a distribution over the \(2^m\) joint ballot outcomes; a continuous society \(\mu\) would describe masses of voter types. They are different objects. In an atomless electorate, an individual has zero mass and cannot be pivotal. Treating each type as a bloc could restore a notion of power, but it changes the object by replacing stochastic coalitions among judges with coherent bloc votes. Satisfaction is easier to retain as a type-level alignment statistic, though that would be a new empirical question rather than a mirror of a computational result here.

The negative case is strongest on the absence of an anchor and weaker as a universal claim. A useful high-multiplicity study of empirical voting behavior is conceivable. The paper gives no reason to rule that out; it gives no computational result for such a study to mirror.
