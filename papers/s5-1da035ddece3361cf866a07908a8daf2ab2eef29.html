The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case holds at the threshold: this note has no named computational result to mirror. Its Theorems 1 and 2 characterize preference orderings over sets of possible outcomes using axioms. The central contribution is to show that two previously characterized rules violate Independence and to offer replacement axiomatizations. There is no algorithmic problem, complexity claim, or intervention on a society of agents.

The most plausible population model would make each decision maker’s underlying ordering \(R\) a type, then assign mass to those types. But to get a computational social choice problem, one must add a social aggregation rule over their preferences for prospects and then specify a task such as bribery or winner robustness. Those additions might support a worthwhile new project; they are not mirrors of a computational problem in this note, because there is none. The alternative—putting mass on possible outcomes or prospects—continuizes uncertainty or the outcome space, while the paper treats outcomes as elements of sets, not as people whose mass matters.

So no anchor from the proposed case survives: there are no computational anchors here. I would not claim that every related project is unworthy. A population voting model built from these preference rules could be interesting on its own.
