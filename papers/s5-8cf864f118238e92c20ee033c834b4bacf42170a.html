The case FOR (proponent)

There is a plausible high-multiplicity version of this river problem, but the paper has no named computational result to anchor it to under your criterion. Theorem 1, proved here, says that the downstream incremental distribution (DID) is the only efficient cost distribution satisfying every coalition’s non-cooperative cost upper bound and aspiration cost lower bound. Proposition 2, also proved here, establishes existence and uniqueness of the efficient abatement allocation minimizing total costs. Neither result says that a problem is tractable, hard, or in a complexity class. The other numbered results concern strategic or cost-bound properties, not computational complexity. Continuous abatement choices do not themselves continuize the population.

The best positive case I can make is an unanchored candidate: *High-Multiplicity River Agreement*. Suppose a river has \(\tau\) ordered reaches, each containing many identical small emitters. Type \(i\) consists of emitters at reach \(i\) with the same per-capita emissions \(e_i\), abatement-cost function \(c_i\), damage-cost function \(d_i\), and flow coefficients \(\gamma_{ji}\). The rational mass \(\mu_i\) is their share of the total population, with \(\sum_i\mu_i=1\). This is plausible where many facilities use a small set of standard technologies in the same reaches, so the number of emitters is much larger than \(\tau\).

A type-level abatement rate \(x_i\in[0,e_i]\) gives ambient pollution
\[ q_i(x)=\sum_{j<i}\gamma_{ji}\mu_j(e_j-x_j). \]
The efficient allocation minimizes
\[ K(x)=\sum_i\mu_i\bigl(c_i(x_i)+d_i(q_i(x))\bigr). \]
A solution also assigns each type a per-unit cost \(z_i\), including transfers, with \(\sum_i\mu_i z_i=K(x^\star)\).

To state the coalition constraints, let \(y_i\in[0,\mu_i]\) be the mass of type \(i\) in a coalition. Define \(V(y)\) as the coalition’s minimum aggregate cost when its members choose abatement rates and outsiders do not abate:
\[ V(y)=\min_{0\le u_i\le e_i} \sum_i y_i\left[c_i(u_i)+d_i\left(\sum_{j<i}\gamma_{ji}(\mu_j e_j-y_j u_j)\right)\right]. \]
Define \(A(y)\) as the coalition’s minimum aggregate cost when outsiders fully abate:
\[ A(y)=\min_{0\le u_i\le e_i} \sum_i y_i\left[c_i(u_i)+d_i\left(\sum_{j<i}\gamma_{ji}y_j(e_j-u_j)\right)\right]. \]
The computational question is: given rational masses and a finite encoding of the parameters and convex cost functions, compute the efficient allocation \(x^\star\) and determine whether there is a unique vector \(z\) such that, for every coalition mass vector \(y\),
\[ A(y)\le \sum_i y_i z_i\le V(y). \]
This keeps the paper’s efficient abatement and two-sided coalition bounds; the mass variables and per-unit cost rates are the co-modification required by a continuous population. My provisional guess is tractability for a finite number of reaches with suitably represented convex costs, if the all-coalitions constraints admit efficient separation. That is a conjecture for this new problem, not a result of the paper.

The soft spot is substantial: the paper treats a finite ordered set of agents, whereas this candidate permits fractional coalitions within a reach. Theorem 1 does not establish existence or uniqueness of \(z\) in that setting, and the computational question adds a layer the paper never studies. So this is a credible high-multiplicity research direction, but it is not an anchor-backed positive case as requested.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that the paper contains no named computational result to mirror. Proposition 2 proves existence and uniqueness of the efficient abatement allocation; Theorem 1 characterizes the unique cost distribution satisfying the paper’s coalition bounds. Propositions 1, 3, and 4 establish strategic or structural properties. None addresses complexity or an algorithm. The paper’s continuous choices are abatement levels for a finite set of agents, not a continuous society.

The proposed river model with masses at reaches is a credible high-multiplicity setting. If many emitters at the same reach share preferences, costs, emissions, and flow effects, grouping them into types respects the programme’s definition; I would not reject it on multiplicity or identity grounds. But its two anchors still do not supply computational results: a mass version of Proposition 2 asks how to compute an optimizer, and a mass version of Theorem 1 asks how to find or characterize a cost distribution. The theorem does not establish that its conclusion extends to every fractional coalition vector \(y\).

There is also a gap before either becomes a complexity problem. The paper allows general smooth convex cost functions, without a finite input encoding. To ask for running time or approximation guarantees, the mirror must choose how those functions are represented. A particular finite parameterization could define a legitimate new problem, but the computational question would then depend on a modelling choice the paper does not make. Likewise, asking for separation over all fractional coalition masses adds a computational problem that the paper neither states nor studies.

So the case against is decisive only under a strict reading of “mirror”: neither Theorem 1 nor Proposition 2 anchors a continuous *complexity* question from this paper. The broader claim that no version could be worth pursuing is weaker. The proponent’s mass-at-reaches model is plausible, and studying its fractional coalition bounds could be a worthwhile new direction; it just is not an extension of a computational result this paper established. I cannot honestly rule out that research direction across all scenarios.
