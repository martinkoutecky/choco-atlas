The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

This paper has no computational complexity result to mirror. Its formal propositions characterize preference properties; its polling algorithms generate questions and analyze responses. Even the \(O(|V|\log^2|V|+k)\) bound concerns selecting pairs from a finite grid of budget allocations. None studies the complexity of a social-choice problem such as bribery, control, or winner determination.

The strongest version of the proposed population-level direction would give each citizen a complete type—an ideal budget and their full preference or choice behavior—and represent the electorate by a distribution over those types. One could then choose questions to maximize how well they distinguish competing models across that population. But that changes the task: the paper studies what individuals’ preferences are, while this proposal is a new problem in population-level survey design or active learning. If types are complete, they may be so diverse that grouping respondents offers little high-multiplicity compression. If types are restricted to the utility families the paper tests, the grouping assumes away its central finding that many respondents do not fit those families. A distribution over observed answer patterns could support aggregate statistics, but that is not a computational mirror of a result in the paper.

One could instead ask how to aggregate budgets for a continuous population with heterogeneous utilities. That may be worthwhile work, but this paper neither formulates nor analyzes that computational problem; its discussion of aggregation mechanisms supplies motivation, not a result to continuize. The same is true of optimizing question pairs over a continuous budget simplex: it adds outcome-space optimization to a new population-design objective, rather than mirroring a named computational result here.

So the negative case is strong on the programme’s stated gate: there is no eligible anchor, and the best proposed mirror starts a different research project. The universal claim that no such adjacent project could ever be worthwhile is harder to establish. Population-level experimental design may be valuable; the paper just does not provide a continuous computational result for it to mirror.
