The continuous mirror question

Given \(k\) divisible tasks, requester types \(a\) with positive rational masses \(r_a\), linear values \(v_{a\ell}\), and per-person budgets \(B_a\), and worker types \(b\) with positive rational masses \(s_b\), disutilities \(c_{b\ell}>0\), and per-person earning requirements \(E_b\), assume \(\sum_a r_a B_a=\sum_b s_b E_b\). Find prices \(p_\ell>0\) and per-type bundles \(x_a,y_b\geq 0\) such that each requester type maximizes \(\sum_\ell v_{a\ell}x_{a\ell}\) subject to \(\sum_\ell p_\ell x_{a\ell}\leq B_a\), each worker type minimizes \(\sum_\ell c_{b\ell}y_{b\ell}\) subject to \(\sum_\ell p_\ell y_{b\ell}\geq E_b\), and \(r_a\sum_a x_{a\ell}= \sum_b s_b y_{b\ell}\) for every task \(\ell\).

The model it lives in

Represent each type by its population mass and per-person parameters; use per-type bundles as the allocation variables and impose mass-weighted market clearing. Equivalently, use aggregate monetary flows \(b_{a\ell},e_{b\ell}\), with type totals \(r_aB_a,s_bE_b\) and taskwise flow balance, minimizing \(\sum_{a,\ell}(-\log v_{a\ell})b_{a\ell}+\sum_{b,\ell}(\log c_{b\ell})e_{b\ell}\), omitting requester edges with \(v_{a\ell}=0\). The objective is the paper’s flow LP objective; its logarithmic coefficients may be irrational, so exact equilibrium recovery is part of the computational question.

The objection that survived

The exact synthetic-agent mapping makes the support-size algorithm a direct application of Theorem 5 after encoding rational aggregate endowments, so this mirror adds little new algorithmic structure; the proponent notes aggregation but does not spell out that full reduction.

fatal: False

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest anchor is Theorem 5, proved in this paper: for a linear labor market, one can compute an exact competitive equilibrium in polynomial time by solving a rounded LP and then applying a truncation-and-extraction procedure. The procedure rounds the logarithmic market-flow costs, solves the resulting minimum-cost-flow problem, adjusts prices until the MBB/MPB graph is connected, and extracts prices for the original market while keeping the flow on optimal edges. The paper has no comparable hardness theorem for this labor-market equilibrium problem; the hardness it cites concerns a different, chores-only exchange market. [The paper’s arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15060)

A natural continuous counterpart is a platform with a large population of requesters and workers, organized into a modest number of complete types. A requester type \(a\) specifies a linear task-valuation vector \(v_a\) and per-person budget \(B_a\); a worker type \(b\) specifies a disutility vector \(c_b\) and earning requirement \(E_b\). Let their type shares be \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\), each summing to one, and let the total population masses be \(N_U\) and \(N_W\). For example, many clients may share a project template and budget, while many contractors share a task-cost profile and weekly earning target. The populations may be very large while the supports \(\tau_U,\tau_W\) remain small.

Here is the problem I would hand to a prover: Type-Compressed Exact Labor-Market CE. The input is \(k\) divisible tasks; finite lists of requester and worker types with binary-rational parameters; rational type shares and population masses; and balanced aggregate budgets,
\[ N_U\sum_a\mu_a B_a=N_W\sum_b\nu_b E_b. \]
Find prices \(p_\ell>0\) and per-person bundles \(x_a,y_b\geq0\) such that every requester type maximizes \(\sum_\ell v_{a\ell}x_{a\ell}\) subject to \(\sum_\ell p_\ell x_{a\ell}\leq B_a\), every worker type minimizes \(\sum_\ell c_{b\ell}y_{b\ell}\) subject to \(\sum_\ell p_\ell y_{b\ell}\geq E_b\), and each task clears:
\[ N_U\sum_a\mu_a x_{a\ell}=N_W\sum_b\nu_b y_{b\ell} \quad\text{for every }\ell. \]
There is no separate welfare objective: the requested solution is an equilibrium witness. Its paper-derived optimization objective is the type-aggregated market-flow LP: minimize
\[ \sum_{a,\ell}(-\log v_{a\ell})b_{a\ell} +\sum_{b,\ell}(\log c_{b\ell})e_{b\ell}, \]
over nonnegative flows, omitting requester edges with \(v_{a\ell}=0\), subject to type totals \(N_U\mu_aB_a\) and \(N_W\nu_bE_b\), and taskwise balance \(\sum_a b_{a\ell}=\sum_b e_{b\ell}\). As in the paper, the logarithmic costs need not be rational; rounding and exact extraction are part of the computational question.

I expect this mirror to be Class A, tractable. With linear preferences, agents of the same complete type have the same optimality edges. Their spending and earnings can be aggregated into one flow node with rational capacity equal to their type mass times their per-person endowment. Thus the problem has a direct finite-support representation; the natural target is running time polynomial in \(k,\tau_U,\tau_W\), and the total binary input length, rather than in the headcounts. This is a reasoned extension of Theorem 5’s flow-and-extraction method, not a theorem proved in the paper. The finite support also makes the type-level bundle an honest representation: the average bundle of identical agents is optimal under their linear preferences.

The authors should recognize the question: it keeps their two-sided demand-and-supply equilibrium, task prices, requester budgets, worker earning targets, and exact equilibrium output. Their own platform examples motivate populations in which many agents share those parameters. The paper already makes task allocations divisible; the proposed mirror’s substantive change is that the requester and worker populations become distributions over types. It does not continuize outcomes in place of the population.

This case covers Theorem 5’s exact equilibrium-computation result and relies on the paper’s LP-to-equilibrium correspondence as machinery. It does not claim to mirror the existence, welfare, fairness, or price-geometry results. Further questions include whether the type-compressed algorithm admits an explicit bit-complexity bound for rational masses, and whether an analogous support-based result survives beyond linear preferences.

The soft spot is that the anchor is already a polynomial-time result for the finite-agent problem. So this is a credible high-multiplicity mirror, but it does not offer the programme’s most striking payoff—showing discrete hardness dissolve in a continuum. Its value would be a precise complexity result in support size and a formal weighted-population version of the authors’ equilibrium computation.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only serious case against the proposed anchor is that its continuous computation appears to be an immediate reduction to the paper’s existing finite-agent computation. For requester type \(a\), set \(\widehat B_a=N_U\mu_aB_a\); for worker type \(b\), set \(\widehat E_b=N_W\nu_bE_b\). Replace each type by one synthetic agent with that aggregate endowment. If \(x_a,y_b\) are the type-level per-person bundles, the synthetic bundles are \(X_a=N_U\mu_a x_a\) and \(Y_b=N_W\nu_b y_b\). Linearity makes optimality equivalent under this scaling, and the continuous market-clearing equations become exactly the synthetic market’s clearing equations. Dividing the synthetic bundles by the type masses reverses the mapping.

Thus the proposed problem has the same equilibrium witnesses as a finite instance with \(\tau_U\) requesters and \(\tau_W\) workers. Rational masses give rational aggregate endowments; clearing denominators preserves prices and yields an instance to which the paper’s exact-computation result applies, with encoding length polynomial in the mass data. The claimed support-size algorithm is therefore a corollary of the paper’s own algorithm, rather than a new continuous-optimization problem or a new computational boundary. The paper already reduces equilibrium computation to a flow problem, so this mirror offers no new pricing or separation question for the continuous-optimization toolbox to address.

That makes this a low-marginal-value ChoCo case: the natural population mirror exists, but its computational deliverable is largely aggregation plus an application of Theorem 5. This argument does not establish that no worthwhile mirror exists in any plausible regime. The platform scenario is credible, and a clean weighted-population dictionary may itself be useful. Under the programme’s standard, I cannot honestly say the anchor is defeated; I can only argue that it is less compelling than a mirror whose continuous formulation creates a genuinely new complexity question.
