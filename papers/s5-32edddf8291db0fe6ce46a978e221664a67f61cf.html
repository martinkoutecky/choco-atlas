What the mirror covers

The nearest extension would cover DRM's incentive, IR, WBB, and non-winner-transfer properties for repeated network roles; it leaves allocation complexity and approximation questions untouched, and the paper gives no runtime analysis.

The case FOR (proponent)

Under the anchor rule, I do not see a valid positive case to make: the paper has no numbered result about computational complexity. The NP-hardness sentence in Related Work concerns VCG winner determination, is unnumbered, and is cited from elsewhere. The paper’s numbered results instead establish mechanism properties.

The closest candidate is Theorem 2 (proved here): the Dealer Retail Mechanism, using graph exploration CDP, Greedy BDP, and IDM DRP, satisfies incentive compatibility, individual rationality, and weak budget balance. Theorem 3 (also proved here) says DRM satisfies EPI4NW: for some report profile, a bidder who receives no items has a negative payment and thus positive utility. These are meaningful results, but neither gives the complexity anchor needed for the programme’s question about which computational problems become easier or remain hard.

There is a plausible *future* mirror. Imagine a large online marketplace with a fixed catalog, many buyers, and relatively few complete buyer types. A type would need to include a buyer’s valuation for every bundle and their full role in the diffusion network. If \(n\gg\tau\), represent the population by rational masses \(\mu_t\); scale the stock of items with market size so that positive mass can receive bundles. A continuous analogue of DRM would assign each buyer an integral bundle and payment, while \(x_{t,b}\) records the mass of type \(t\) receiving bundle \(b\). The question could be whether a measurable extension of DRM exists that preserves truthful valuation reports and full invitations as dominant strategies, IR, WBB, and EPI4NW, and agrees with DRM on specified finite blow-ups of the role network. Its output would be the allocation and payment rule; the paper supplies no optimization objective to carry over.

That is a plausible research question, but not an anchored mirror of a computational result in this paper. Its main weakness is the network: DRM’s critical diffusion nodes and invitation incentives depend on who is connected to whom. Replacing named buyers with masses of role types changes those incentives, and a mechanism using only aggregate reports could erase the individual’s strategic effect. Making the network role itself the strategic unit might restore that effect, but would change the authors’ problem. The paper also gives no running-time analysis that could ground a computational classification of this extension.

So the positive case is limited to a sensible large-market motivation for studying a new continuum mechanism-design problem. I would not present it as an anchor-based continuous mirror of this paper’s results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that this paper supplies no computational result for ChoCo to mirror. Theorem 2 establishes that DRM satisfies IC, IR, and WBB; Theorem 3 establishes EPI4NW. Neither classifies the complexity of a problem or gives an algorithm. The NP-hardness statement concerns VCG winner determination in prior work, so a continuous version of that problem would mirror the cited work, not this paper.

The proposed continuous extension of DRM is the closest case for a mirror, but its network is the obstacle. DRM’s allocations and invitation incentives depend on individual graph positions: who can reach whom, which nodes are critical, and which bidders are critical children. A distribution over valuation and role types does not generally retain that information. If a type records exact neighbors, the number of types can grow with the network, defeating the intended high-multiplicity compression.

A stronger version could use many copies of a fixed finite network module, with repeated buyer types and item supply scaled to the population. That is a sensible regime, but it leaves a dilemma. If modules are independent, applying DRM module by module largely reproduces the finite mechanism; the continuous population adds little computational content. If modules share scarce items or network links, the interactions that make the problem interesting also require preserving their graph structure. The result is a new aggregate-network mechanism-design problem, not a computational mirror of a result in this paper.

There is a related issue with carrying over the properties. In an aggregate rule that sees only type masses, one agent’s report changes no mass. The individual incentive effects of diffusion can therefore disappear; restoring them requires individual or module-level information. And EPI4NW is an existential claim about one non-winner receiving a negative payment. In a continuum, a single agent has measure zero, so making that claim visible in the aggregate model would require a positive-mass version—a distinct property, not a direct mirror.

This is a strong case against treating the paper as a ChoCo target: its contributions are mechanism-property theorems, and the most promising population models either wash out the strategic structure or require substantial new structure and questions. It does not prove that no worthwhile continuous mechanism-design project can be built from the paper’s setting. Repeated network modules are the honest weak point in the negative case; they could motivate such a project, but the paper offers no computational anchor for it.
