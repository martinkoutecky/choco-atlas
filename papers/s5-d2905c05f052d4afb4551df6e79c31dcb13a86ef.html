The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), weights \(w_t\), tied rankings, and eligibility profiles, together with a normalized polymatroid rank function \(r_a\) for each of \(m\) categories, compute feasible allocation masses \(x_{ta}\). Require \(x_{ta}\ge 0\), \(\sum_a x_{ta}\le\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t\in S}x_{ta}\le r_a(S)\) for every \(S\subseteq T\), and \(x_{ta}=0\) for unacceptable assignments. Each unit of mass receives at most one indivisible category, while \(x\) induces a lottery for each type. Pareto optimality means that no feasible reassignment makes every type’s lottery first-order stochastically dominate its current lottery, with strict improvement for positive mass. Is there an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), given polynomial-time separation oracles for the \(r_a\), that returns a Pareto-optimal allocation with weighted matched mass at least \((1-1/e)\) times the maximum among feasible Pareto-optimal allocations?

The model it lives in

Types carry tied rankings, weights, and eligibility profiles; category supplies scale with population mass and are represented by polymatroid rank functions. The output is a feasible vector of type-to-category masses, realizable by partitioning each nonatomic type into groups that each receive one indivisible category.

The objection that survived

The allocation-only repair drops Theorem 15’s universal-truthfulness guarantee, and the paper’s named-agent analysis has no direct atomless counterpart; the proponent flags this loss but does not establish that the authors would regard the welfare-only version as a full analogue.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

Covers the weighted, tied MHA welfare and Pareto-allocation guarantee under scaled type populations; it leaves individual truthfulness, KHA, and the online applications unmirrored.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the welfare approximation and Pareto requirement be achieved in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\) with rank-function separation oracles?
  • Can a meaningful individual truthfulness guarantee be retained in a pointwise implementation over an atomless population?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest anchor is Theorem 15, proved in this paper in §4.3. It says that for Matroid House Allocation with weighted agents and ties, DRTMHA is universally truthful and Pareto optimal, achieves approximation ratio \(e/(e-1)+O(1/\kappa)\) for every \(\kappa\in\mathbb N^+\), and runs in \(O(n_1^4\gamma\log\kappa)\) time, where \(\gamma\) is the largest indifference-class size. This is the paper’s result, not a citation to an outside theorem.

A convincing high-multiplicity setting is a large university consortium assigning millions of residents to housing categories. Residents with the same tied preferences, priority weight, and room-eligibility profile form a type; there might be millions of residents but only dozens or hundreds of such types. Housing categories can contain many rooms with different features. Eligibility for those rooms induces the transversal matroid constraints used in the paper. As the resident cohort grows, the room supplies and eligibility ranks scale with it. Each resident still receives at most one indivisible room; only the type-level allocation rates become fractional. This keeps the paper’s allocation problem intact while making population mass the natural unit.

I would pose Continuous MHA Approximation as follows. An instance gives a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and, for each type, its reported ranking with ties, weight \(w_t\), and eligibility profile. There are \(m\) housing categories. For each category \(a\), the instance gives a normalized matroid-rank function \(r_a\) on subsets of types, describing the limiting per-capita feasibility of assigning those types to \(a\). For example, \(r_a(S)\) can be the maximum mass from types \(S\) that can be matched to the category’s room-feature slots.

An allocation has type-level masses \(x_{ta}\): the mass of type \(t\) receiving category \(a\). It must satisfy \(x_{ta}\ge 0\), \(\sum_a x_{ta}\le\mu_t\), and \(\sum_{t\in S}x_{ta}\le r_a(S)\) for every type subset \(S\) and category \(a\); unacceptable assignments have \(x_{ta}=0\). Thus every unit of mass gets at most one category, and the matroid constraints remain present in their aggregate form. Each \(x\) can be realized by dividing each type’s nonatomic population into measurable groups, each of whom receives a single category or nothing.

For Pareto optimality, treat the assignment rates for a type as that type’s distribution over ranked categories, with unmatched agents ranked last. An allocation is Pareto optimal if no feasible reassignment gives every type a lottery that first-order stochastically dominates its current lottery, with a strict improvement for positive mass. The question is: construct, or rule out, a universally truthful randomized mechanism whose every realized allocation is feasible and Pareto optimal, and whose expected weighted matched mass is at least \((1-1/e)\) times the maximum weighted mass of any feasible Pareto-optimal allocation. The computational target would be a description of that mechanism in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), with access to the rank functions through an efficient separation oracles model. This asks about allocating housing mass, not about an encoding.

I expect this question to be Class A, through a continuous random-priority allocation rule and matroid or polymatroid optimization. Theorem 15 gives a concrete finite-population sequence approaching the target ratio as \(\kappa\) grows. It does not, by itself, give an algorithm polynomial in support size: its stated running time depends on the number of agents. Establishing a support-compressed mechanism and a valid continuum limit is therefore real work. Further questions include whether the exact \(e/(e-1)\) ratio survives the limit, how broadly the rank-oracle model can be supported, and what changes when agents have positive atomic mass.

The weakest point is the incentive guarantee. In an atomless population, changing one person’s report does not change the aggregate mass vector, so a mechanism defined solely on \(\mu\) makes individual truthfulness vacuous. A nontrivial strategyproof implementation would need to specify how it uses pointwise reports and assigns particular individuals within each type; Theorem 15 does not supply that continuum construction. The allocation, feasibility, welfare, and Pareto questions remain meaningful and closely track the paper’s MHA problem, but the mirror is strongest as a continuization of its allocation guarantee, not as an already-established continuation of its mechanism-design content. This covers Theorem 15’s weighted, tied MHA result, especially the room-eligibility transversal-matroid regime; it does not attempt to mirror KHA or the paper’s online applications.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 15 is a real algorithmic result: it gives an approximation guarantee and running time, so the “nothing computational to mirror” objection does not apply. Nor does the housing example fail the high-multiplicity test. If many residents share preferences, weights, and room eligibility, and room capacity scales with the population, type masses are natural. The resulting aggregate matroid constraints are a legitimate co-modification of the population model.

The strongest objection is to the proposed mirror’s claim to preserve Theorem 15’s mechanism-design content. If the mechanism sees only the mass vector \(\mu\), one person’s report does not change it in an atomless population. Universal truthfulness is then vacuous or undefined for an individual: \(\mu\) specifies type masses, not what happens to a particular person who deviates. The proposed type-lottery Pareto criterion is a defensible extension, but it is not by itself a construction of a truthful mechanism that assigns individuals and returns Pareto-optimal allocations. So the formulation does not yet mirror the theorem’s combination of truthfulness, Pareto optimality, and welfare approximation.

A stronger repair would attach random priority marks to individuals and define a continuous version of the priority allocation rule. That could preserve individual choices and might admit an analysis extending the finite mechanism. But it is a new continuous mechanism, not a limit supplied by Theorem 15: its proof relies on comparing runs with and without a named agent, while deleting one person leaves the aggregate distribution unchanged. That is a serious gap, though a gap to investigate rather than evidence that the question is unworthy.

If the mirror instead asks only for a welfare-approximating allocation over type masses, the central incentive objection disappears—but so does the theorem’s distinctive truthful-mechanism guarantee. That still leaves a coherent computational question about mass allocation under matroid constraints. I cannot rule out that the authors would recognize it as their problem, or that it would be useful to the programme.

So the negative case is weak against the anchor as a whole. It exposes a mismatch in the proposed formulation’s truthfulness claim, but the scaled-supply, repeated-type housing scenario remains credible, and an allocation-only mirror survives. I cannot honestly conclude that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
