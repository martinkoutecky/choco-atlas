The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There are no computational anchors to defeat in the proponent’s case. That absence is decisive for this paper: its named results characterize aggregation rules by axioms, but none gives a complexity classification, algorithm, or hardness result for a finitely encoded problem. Theorem 1’s characterization of weighted arithmetic means is a mathematical result about functions on profiles, not an algorithmic claim.

The strongest candidate mirror would give each fuzzy classification a type in \(C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{mp}\), represent a society by finitely many such types and their masses, and ask for the aggregate classification. If the rule’s weights are part of the input, this is just computing weighted coordinate averages in \(O(mp\tau)\) arithmetic operations. If the rule or its weights are instead given as an oracle or an arbitrary function, the paper provides no input representation or computational model for the resulting task. Testing its axioms would also require checking conditions over all profiles, not just the encoded society. Adding a welfare objective or an optimization question might create worthwhile research, but it would supply a new problem absent from the paper.
