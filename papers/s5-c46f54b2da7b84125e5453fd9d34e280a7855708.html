The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest conclusion is a qualified one: this paper admits a natural continuous-population mirror, but it has no eligible computational anchor under the programme’s rule.

Its numbered results are axiomatic or structural. Theorem 6, proved in the paper, says that on the conditionally lexicographic domain, a rule satisfies BAL, IGE, WELB, and DSP exactly when it equals ATTC. Proposition 3 says that, on this domain, IGE and PE are equivalent. Theorems 1–5 give analogous characterizations for TTC. None states NP-hardness, membership in P, FPT, approximation, or a computational formulation. The NP-hard manipulation claim in Section 6.2 is attributed to Fujita et al. (2018), not proved as a numbered result here; the polynomial-time claims about checking IGE are likewise cited from elsewhere. Thus these cannot strictly serve as computational anchors.

The strongest substantive mirror would nevertheless be:

Continuous-ATTC Evaluation, provisionally anchored to Theorem 6.

Take a finite set \(O\) of recurring object kinds, such as shift slots. A population type \(\theta\) consists of:

  • a mass \(\mu_\theta\);
  • a complete LP-tree preference over bundles;
  • an integral endowment bundle \(\omega_\theta\);
  • any eligibility or ownership data relevant to the exchange.

The masses satisfy \(\sum_\theta \mu_\theta=1\). Object supply is scaled with the population:
\[ \sigma_o=\sum_\theta \mu_\theta \mathbf{1}[o\in\omega_\theta]. \]

A feasible aggregate allocation is \(z=(z_{\theta,B})\), where \(z_{\theta,B}\) is the mass of type-\(\theta\) agents receiving the integral bundle \(B\subseteq O\), subject to
\[ \sum_{B\subseteq O}z_{\theta,B}=\mu_\theta, \qquad \sum_{\theta}\sum_{B\ni o}z_{\theta,B}=\sigma_o, \]
and \(z_{\theta,B}>0\) only when \(|B|=|\omega_\theta|\). Thus individual agents still receive indivisible bundles; only the distribution over identical agents is fractional.

The continuous ATTC process maintains masses of active agent packets and object packets. Each agent packet points to its most-preferred remaining object conditional on its current bundle; each object packet points to its owner packet. A directed cycle transfers the bottleneck mass on that cycle, updates the agents’ bundles, and removes the transferred object mass. Splitting a type across several cycles is permitted. The problem is to output the exact rational allocation \(z\), together with the cycle-flow certificate that generated it.

This is a genuine population continuization, not outcome-space fractionality: the objects and each individual bundle remain integral. It is also recognisably the authors’ problem. Theorem 6 concerns exactly the same endowments, LP-tree preferences, ATTC procedure, and properties BAL, PE/IGE, WELB, and DSP. The continuous question asks whether that rule can be evaluated on a high-multiplicity society rather than on a list of named agents.

A plausible regime is a large shift-exchange programme: perhaps \(N\) employees but only a few dozen recurring slot, location, and contract types. Many employees share the same LP tree, endowment pattern, and eligibility constraints, so \(|\Theta|\ll N\). The same story could apply to a large tuition-exchange programme with repeated student and course-seat types. This is a legitimate high-multiplicity regime, not an arbitrary relabelling of a seven-agent committee.

I would expect Continuous-ATTC Evaluation to be Class A, provided \(O\) and the type support are explicit and LP-tree operations are polynomial-time. The main algorithmic question is whether the cycle process can be compressed to polynomially many type packets, rather than generating exponentially many partial-bundle states. Further questions include exact bit complexity, continuous checking of PE/IGE, and manipulation by a positive-mass block of identical agents.

The substantive weakness is that the original model has named heterogeneous objects with unique owners. Replacing them by recurring object kinds and owner masses is a co-modification of the model, not a literal limit of every finite instance. Moreover, a single atomless agent has no aggregate influence, so the paper’s individual manipulation results do not transfer without defining block manipulation. That is why the mirror should be presented as a new high-multiplicity extension of the authors’ exchange problem, not as an established consequence of Theorem 6.

So the best positive case is: a very plausible continuous exchange problem exists, and Theorem 6 gives it a strong structural anchor. But under the programme’s strict requirement for a numbered computational result, this paper cannot support a compliant positive case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that this paper has no eligible computational anchor at all. Theorems 1–6 are axiomatic characterizations: they identify TTC or ATTC as the unique rule satisfying specified properties on finite, named-agent economies. Proposition 3 is a structural equivalence between PE and IGE, and Proposition 5 is a domain-maximality result. None states a complexity classification, algorithmic formulation, approximation guarantee, or parameterized result. The NP-hard manipulation claim in Section 6.2 is attributed to Fujita et al. (2018), not established here. Thus “Continuous-ATTC Evaluation” is not a continuous version of a computational result in this paper; it is a new computational problem inspired by its mechanism.

The proposed mirror also cannot preserve the paper’s model and obtain genuine high multiplicity simultaneously. In the paper, an agent’s complete description includes her endowment, and endowments are nonempty disjoint subsets of a finite set of named objects. Consequently, two agents cannot have the same complete type: their endowments necessarily differ. With fixed \(O\), the number of complete types is therefore at least \(n\), so there is no high-multiplicity regime.

The positive case escapes this only by replacing named objects with recurring object kinds and assigning each kind a mass supply. That is a defensible multi-unit exchange model, but it is a substantial new model, not a limit operation on the paper’s TTC or ATTC. The original graph has an object pointing to its unique owner. The proposed graph has a mass of object copies pointing to an owner type. Once a type receives only part of a mass-flow cycle, the remaining agents of that type have a different current bundle and hence a different conditional preference under an LP tree. The “type” must split into states indexed by partial bundles and histories. The paper’s finite rule does not specify how that aggregate state should be represented or resolved.

There is an even sharper incompatibility. The paper assumes strict rankings over heterogeneous named objects. Collapsing copies into object kinds introduces indifference between copies. Retaining strictness requires labelling the copies, but then agents’ complete endowments and rankings again become individualized. An arbitrary common tie-breaking order could produce a mass mechanism, but that tie-breaking is an additional modelling choice whose effects are not covered by Theorem 6.

The axioms do not transfer automatically either. DSP in the paper concerns a unilateral report by one named agent. In an atomless population, one agent has zero mass and cannot change the aggregate allocation, so individual DSP becomes vacuous. The proposed repair—allowing a positive-mass block of identical agents to misreport—is potentially meaningful, but it is block manipulation, not the paper’s DSP, and requires a new quantification over coalition masses. Likewise, PE and IGE must be reformulated for allocation measures: “no one is better off” becomes an almost-everywhere or positive-mass condition, while a trading cycle becomes a feasible mass flow. Those are legitimate research questions, but they are new axioms and a new rule, not continuizations of the stated characterization.

The same objection applies to a supposedly stronger mirror of Theorems 1–5. Replicated housing or shift objects can yield an interesting high-multiplicity TTC model, but it is a multi-unit or typewise exchange problem. The paper’s theorems provide no computational claim whose continuous analogue could be tested. Asking whether the newly defined flow implementation is polynomial is worthwhile only as a new algorithmic paper, not as the computational mirror of this one.

The negative case is therefore strong on the programme’s gate: the proponent has identified a plausible continuous exchange model, but not a worthwhile continuous mirror of this paper. I cannot honestly claim that large shift-exchange programmes with recurring slot types are implausible; they are a real possible research direction. If ChoCo permits an axiomatic paper to serve merely as inspiration for inventing a new mass-exchange mechanism and its complexity theory, this paper should pass. Under the stated requirement of mirroring a named computational result, however, the anchor fails decisively.
