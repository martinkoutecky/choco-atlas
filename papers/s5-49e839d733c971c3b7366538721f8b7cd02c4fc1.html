The anchor — hardness

Theorem 4.6

Every anchor argued

  • Value-EJR committee-search hardness (cited as Theorem 4.5 by the proponent; numbered Theorem 4.6 in the paper) — survived: The stated hardness result has a faithful mass analogue; the citation is off by one, since Theorem 4.5 is the preceding Value-GCR guarantee.

The continuous mirror question

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), qualities \(h_c\in(0,1]\), and approval types \(t\in\mathcal T\subseteq 2^C\) with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), find a committee \(W\subseteq C\) of size \(k\) such that, for every \(\ell\in[k]\) and type subset \(U\subseteq\mathcal T\) with \(\sum_{t\in U}\mu_t\ge\ell/k\) and \(|Q_U|\ge\ell\), where \(Q_U=\bigcap_{t\in U}t\), we have \(\max_{t\in U}\sum_{c\in t\cap W}h_c\ge h(Q_U\mid\ell)\). Here \(h(Q_U\mid\ell)\) is the sum of the \(\ell\) highest qualities in \(Q_U\).

The model it lives in

Approval sets are types carrying mass \(\mu_t\); candidates, their qualities, and the size-\(k\) committee remain indivisible. The objective is feasibility: find a committee satisfying every value-EJR demand from a coalition of type mass at least \(\ell/k\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers value-EJR committee search. It leaves the paper’s axiomatic characterizations, compatibility results, and quality-price guarantees aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does continuous value-EJR committee search admit an efficient algorithm when support size \(\tau\) is bounded or approval types have additional structure?

The case FOR (proponent)

My strongest positive case is a direct, population-only mirror of the paper’s value-EJR committee search. It keeps the candidates, their qualities, the committee size, and the representation requirement intact; it expresses voter groups by mass instead of headcount.

The anchor is Theorem 4.5, proved in this paper in Appendix A. It says that if a polynomial-time algorithm could output a value-EJR committee on every approval-based multiwinner instance with candidate qualities, then \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). Equivalently, computing such a committee is NP-hard. The hardness remains when candidate qualities are restricted to \(1\) and one other unary-encoded value. The proof reduces from Balanced Biclique.

Here is the corresponding continuous problem. An instance has a candidate set \(C\), committee size \(k\), rational qualities \(h_c\in(0,1]\), and a finite set \(\mathcal T\subseteq 2^C\) of approval types with positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Type \(t\) means that every member of that type approves \(A_t=t\). Choose a committee \(W\subseteq C\) of size \(k\).

For any \(\ell\in[k]\), consider a coalition formed by taking mass \(z_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) from each type. Let \(U=\{t:z_t>0\}\) be the types represented in the coalition, and let \(Q_U=\bigcap_{t\in U}A_t\) be their common approvals. If \(\sum_{t\in U}z_t\ge\ell/k\) and \(|Q_U|\ge\ell\), require
\[ \max_{t\in U} h(A_t\cap W)\ \ge\ h(Q_U\mid \ell), \]
where \(h(Q_U\mid\ell)\) is the total quality of the \(\ell\) highest-quality candidates in \(Q_U\). The problem, Continuous Value-EJR Committee Search, asks for such a \(W\). Every person still receives the same integral committee; only the population and its proportionality threshold have become continuous.

This is a natural regime for, say, a large city choosing a fixed-size slate of projects after public consultation. The city may have hundreds of thousands of participants, while many share approval ballots; a plausible regime has \(N\) far larger than the number \(\tau=|\mathcal T|\) of distinct ballots. Candidate qualities can remain exogenous project assessments, as in the paper. Approval set plus the shared model parameters is a complete type for this question.

The mirror preserves the authors’ problem. Their entitlement is based on a group’s size relative to \(n/k\), its common approvals, and the best utility attained by one member. The continuous version uses mass relative to \(1/k\), the same common-approval test, and the maximum type utility. It does not fractionalize the committee or replace individual satisfaction by average utility.

I expect hardness to transfer (Class B). For a finite profile with \(n_t\) voters of each approval type, set \(\mu_t=n_t/n\). The mass threshold \(\ell/k\) then corresponds to the paper’s headcount threshold \(\ell n/k\). Because members of a type have identical approvals and utility, allowing a coalition to take fractional mass does not change which committees satisfy value-EJR: a violating coalition is characterized by a set of low-utility types with enough total mass and a sufficiently large common approval set. The hardness reduction itself gives a polynomial-size continuous instance with masses \(1/n\), so a polynomial algorithm in \(m,\tau,k\), and the input bit lengths would solve the Balanced Biclique instances used in Theorem 4.5. Rational clone expansion also means these instances have feasible committees; the paper’s value-GCR rule supplies one on the corresponding finite profile.

The mirror covers the paper’s result about computing a value-EJR committee, not its full axiomatic landscape or its quality-price-of-representation guarantees. It also generates a focused next question: does value-EJR committee search become tractable for bounded \(\tau\), or for structured approval types?

The weakest point is that the hardness reduction uses private candidates, making its voter types distinct. It therefore does not show hardness in the especially attractive regime \(\tau\ll N\), nor does it demonstrate a computational gain from compression. That limits what this anchor says about the high-multiplicity advantage, but it does not undermine the mirror: the paper’s computational problem has a faithful continuous formulation, and its agenda-driven hardness survives that formulation.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I can’t honestly defeat this anchor. Theorem 4.5 is a named computational hardness result, and the proposed mirror keeps the committee, candidate qualities, and value-EJR demand intact while replacing voter counts with mass over approval types. A large public consultation with many repeated ballots is a credible setting; an approval set is a complete voter type for this problem.

The coalition condition also has a coherent continuous reading. A coalition’s common approvals are the intersection across the types it contains, and its best represented member is the type with the highest utility among those present. Since everyone of a type has the same ballot and receives the same committee, that maximum remains meaningful in a nonatomic population. For empirical masses \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), the mass threshold \(\ell/k\) matches the paper’s headcount threshold \(\ell n/k\), including when the latter is not an integer.

The strongest skeptical point is that the hardness construction uses private candidates and therefore has essentially one approval type per voter. It establishes hardness for the continuous formulation, but does not show an advantage from compressing a high-multiplicity population. That limits the construction’s evidence for the programme’s practical payoff; it does not defeat the mirror. Bounded-support or structured-type complexity remains a natural question, and inherited hardness is a legitimate result under the programme’s stated scope.

Nor can I use the paper’s related additive-utility work as a continuous collision: the supplied text identifies it as discrete computational work, and the programme explicitly treats high-multiplicity work as supporting evidence. The honest negative case is therefore weak: this anchor survives, and I have no sound basis for claiming that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
