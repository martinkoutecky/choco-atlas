The cited work on cooperative games is background, not a computational result proved here. So there is no anchor I can restate as required.

The strongest plausible mirror, offered as a candidate rather than an anchor-backed case, is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s maximum-loss allocation problem. Consider many creditors in a mass-market insolvency, where each creditor’s complete type is their vector of references across asset categories or economic scenarios. Suppose there are \(n\) creditors but only \(\tau\) distinct reference vectors, with \(\tau\ll n\). Let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of creditors of type \(t\), \(c_{tj}\) type \(t\)’s reference in attribute \(j\), and \(e\) the available resource per creditor. Choose a per-creditor allocation \(a_t\geq0\) for each type, with \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=e\). The question is to find an allocation whose per-attribute maximum losses \(r_j=\max_t(c_{tj}-a_t)\) are Pareto-undominated: no feasible allocation weakly lowers every \(r_j\) and strictly lowers at least one.

This is a sensible population-continuous analogue of (P.1): it preserves the paper’s central objective of minimizing the worst loss in each reference dimension, while making the population itself a distribution over types. It should be tractable: for any strictly positive weights \(\lambda_j\), minimizing \(\sum_j\lambda_j r_j\), subject to \(c_{tj}-a_t\leq r_j\), nonnegative allocations, and the resource constraint, is an LP with size polynomial in \(\tau\), the number of attributes, and the input bit length. That gives a Class A expectation for finding a Pareto-undominated allocation.

The soft spot is substantial: the paper’s contribution is to propose egalitarian rules and selection procedures, not to establish a computational result. This candidate also captures the Pareto frontier of (P.1), not the paper’s exact selection in (P.2) or its cooperative-game construction. And the finite-agent allocation problem is already formulated as an LP, so the continuous population may offer useful aggregation without creating a new complexity landscape. The mirror is plausible; the paper does not provide the named computational result needed to anchor it under your criteria.
