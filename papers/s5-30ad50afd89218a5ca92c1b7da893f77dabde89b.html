The continuous mirror question

Given a finite road network, a finite set of commuter types θ with rational masses μ_θ summing to one, feasible route–arrival-time options \(A_θ\), rational per-capita link–time capacities κ_e, and valuations \(v_{θ a}\), compute a welfare-maximizing allocation \(x_{θ a}\) of type mass to options, subject to \(∑_{a∈A_θ}x_{θ a}=μ_θ\), \(∑_{θ,a}A_{ea}x_{θ a}≤κ_e\), and \(x_{θ a}≥0\), maximizing \(∑_{θ,a}v_{θ a}x_{θ a}\).

The model it lives in

Types carry the same origin–destination pair and full valuation vector over route–arrival-time options. Rational type masses share per-capita link–time capacities; decision variables allocate each type’s mass across options, maximizing total welfare.

The objection that survived

The proposed LP assumes valuations are known and computes the welfare optimum directly; it does not establish that a continuous version of the paper’s repeated auction reaches that optimum.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

Covers the welfare-optimality target of 命題4. It leaves the dominant-strategy claim, the repeated auction’s convergence guarantee in a continuum, and implicit route generation unresolved.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the paper’s two-phase day-to-day update be defined over type masses and shown to terminate at the LP optimum in finitely many days?
  • With routes represented implicitly and valuations given compactly, can the needed options or prices be generated efficiently?
  • Can truthful elicitation remain non-vacuous for atomless commuters without making an entire type mass a single strategic bidder?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead anchor is 命題4 (“Proposition 4”), proved in this paper in Appendix III. It claims that the proposed day-to-day auction mechanism’s achieved social surplus is exactly maximum once the number of users is sufficiently large. The stated regime keeps the network and route set fixed, keeps the ratio of users to total capacity constant as both grow, and bounds users’ valuations between \(1\) and a fixed constant \(V\).

That regime has a natural interpretation in commuting: many people make daily trips through the same road network, while a relatively small set of commuter types captures their origin–destination pair, schedule preferences, time value, and valuations of route–arrival-time options. Thus the number \(Q\) of commuters can be much larger than the support size \(\tau\). A type must include the whole valuation vector over those options; people with different preferences or costs belong to different types.

Here is the continuous problem I would hand to a prover: the Continuum Cohort Permit Auction. An instance consists of a finite road network and discrete time horizon; a finite set \(A_\theta\) of feasible route–arrival-time options for each type \(\theta\); rational type masses \(\mu_\theta\geq 0\) with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\); rational per-capita link–time capacities \(\kappa_e\); and rational valuations \(v_{\theta a}\) for each option. Let \(A_{ea}=1\) when option \(a\) uses link–time resource \(e\), and \(0\) otherwise. The task is to return a welfare-maximizing mass allocation \(x_{\theta a}\) and its value, subject to
\[ \sum_{a\in A_\theta}x_{\theta a}=\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_{\theta}\sum_{a\in A_\theta}A_{ea}x_{\theta a}\leq\kappa_e,\qquad x_{\theta a}\geq 0, \]
maximizing \(\sum_{\theta,a}v_{\theta a}x_{\theta a}\).

The mass \(x_{\theta a}\) means that this fraction of type \(\theta\)’s cohort receives option \(a\). Each actual commuter still gets one integral route–time bundle; it is the cohort that is divided across options. This is a population continuum, while the road network, time slots, and each person’s choice remain discrete.

To mirror the paper’s mechanism, the question can also require a finite day-to-day certificate: for each proposed vector \(q_a\) of route–time capacities satisfying \(\sum_a A_{ea}q_a\leq\kappa_e\), clear the type masses by welfare-maximizing allocation under \(x\)’s additional constraints \(\sum_\theta x_{\theta a}\leq q_a\). Use the resulting dual prices and type values in the capacity-adjustment master problem, and output an allocation together with a certificate that its welfare is optimal. With an explicit list of route–time options and valuations, the welfare problem is a rational linear program, hence tractable in the input size. A bound on the number of day-to-day rounds is a separate question: finite termination need not imply polynomially many rounds. If routes are represented compactly rather than listed, efficient pricing over them must be established; the paper itself leaves route generation as future work.

This is a credible mirror of 命題4 because it keeps the same objective, permit bundles, capacity constraints, and day-to-day capacity adjustment, while replacing repeated individuals by type masses. The paper’s proof supplies relevant evidence: its displayed bound makes the relative effect of the floor operation vanish as \(Q\) grows. But that displayed argument establishes a vanishing relative gap; on its own, it does not appear to establish the proposition’s stronger claim of exact optimality for all sufficiently large finite \(Q\). The continuous question is still well-posed and its LP optimum is exact, but a finite-population exactness theorem needs that additional step.

The expected direction is tractable for explicit route–time options and rational input. The further questions are whether the day-to-day certificate has a polynomial round bound, and whether column generation can handle compactly represented route sets. The paper’s NP-hard winner-determination claim for heterogeneous users appears in prose in §4(3), rather than as a numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition; I therefore do not use it as an anchor under your rule. It is a natural next mirror question: does relaxing the population remove the integer multi-commodity-flow hardness?

The weakest point is that Proposition 4 already studies a large-\(Q\) regime, so the continuous mirror is close to the paper’s own asymptotic argument. The distinction is that the paper’s model still assigns indivisible bundles to individually represented users, while this mirror treats a finite-support distribution of commuter types as the input object and asks for an exact mass allocation and its computational complexity. It covers that welfare result, not the paper’s dominant-strategy claim: with an atomless population, an individual commuter has zero marginal effect, so individual strategy-proofness would need a separate formulation.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection to Proposition 4 as a continuous anchor is that the proposed mass LP solves a different task from the proposition. Proposition 4 concerns the outcome of a repeated auction: the mechanism adjusts route capacities using information revealed through bidding, and its achieved welfare approaches the planner’s optimum. The LP takes every type’s valuation as known input and returns the planner’s optimum directly. It neither elicits private information nor implements the day-to-day process. Its tractability therefore does not establish that the paper’s mechanism has a meaningful continuous counterpart.

That gap becomes sharper if the mirror retains the paper’s incentive claim. In an atomless population, one commuter’s report changes neither aggregate demand nor the allocation in any material way. Individual strategy-proofness risks becoming vacuous. Treating each whole type mass as a strategic bidder would make a cohort act as one coordinated agent, which is a different strategic model from many commuters acting individually. The mass LP is a sound welfare benchmark, but it does not preserve the auction’s central implementation problem.

There is also a representation issue. With an explicit list of route–time options, the proposed problem is an LP. For the paper’s network model, however, paths can be numerous, and the paper itself leaves route generation as future work. If valuations have a compact, additive description, pricing may reduce to a shortest-path computation and yield a useful continuous algorithm. If valuations are arbitrary across routes, they require an explicit list or an oracle whose cost is unspecified. The proponent’s formulation thus gives a clean result for one input model; it does not yet settle the computational question for the network problem as represented in the paper.

These objections do not dispose of the better welfare mirror. A time-expanded, type-indexed flow LP with rational masses and additive route costs is a natural continuous model, and the authors could reasonably recognize it as their social-optimum problem. Its algorithmic answer may be immediate, but that still records a tractable case; it is not a collision with an existing continuous treatment. Nor does the mismatch in individual incentives invalidate a mirror of Proposition 4’s welfare claim alone.

So the negative case is weak against the requested universal claim. It shows that the proposed LP is not a continuous version of the full auction mechanism, and that a compact route representation needs careful specification. It does not rule out a worthwhile continuous welfare mirror of Proposition 4.
