The continuous mirror question

Given a distribution \(\mu\) over sincere voter types \(\theta\), each a strict transitive preference over a finite candidate set \(A\), and current ballot masses \(x_{\theta,b}\) with \(\sum_b x_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta\), does every sequence of permitted positive-mass best-response updates under \(1\)-approval reach a stable state? A permitted update moves mass of one sincere type from ballot \(b\) to ballot \(b'\), using only additions or flips allowed by the paper's minimal-divergence rule, when the resulting winner is strictly preferred by that type; stability means no such positive-mass update can improve its winner.

The model it lives in

Represent the electorate by \(\mu\) over complete sincere preference types and its current reports by masses \(x_{\theta,b}\). Compute \(1\)-approval scores from reported-ballot mass, retain the paper's tie-breaking rule and permitted best-response changes, and treat a positive-mass cohort update as one round.

The objection that survived

The paper's \(nm\) bound counts individual moves, while a continuum has no effective one-person move; the mass-update granularity must be specified, and convergence must be reproved.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the convergence guarantee for iterative \(1\)-approval when omission is disallowed. It leaves the truth-tracking optimality theorem, axiomatic characterizations, and the other approval and veto convergence results aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does every permitted positive-mass best-response sequence stabilize, and if so, can its number of rounds be bounded in \(m\), support size \(\tau\), and input bit length \(L\)?

The case FOR (proponent)

I don’t see a qualifying computational anchor in this thesis. Its numbered results establish axiomatic characterizations, strategyproofness conditions, convergence of iterative voting, and probabilistic optimality. None asserts that a computational problem is in \(P\), NP-hard, FPT, or another complexity class.

The closest result is Theorem 6.2, proved in the thesis: for any fixed or free task assignment and sincere judgments, \(F_{\mathrm{opt}}\) maximizes the probability of a correct collective judgment; every other maximizing rule differs only in tie-breaking. That is a Bayesian optimality result, not a complexity result. Proposition 5.35, also proved there, says that if omission is disallowed, iterative 1-approval and \((m-1)\)-veto are guaranteed to converge. The surrounding text bounds convergence by \(nm\) rounds, but this is a bound on a finite-agent dynamic, not a complexity classification of a computational problem.

A large pool of reviewers grouped by task assignment and reliability could make population continuization plausible for the truth-tracking chapter. But the thesis does not give a named computational result to anchor that mirror, and its optimality theorem does not by itself pose the complexity question ChoCo requires. Under the requested standard, I would report no valid anchor, rather than present a continuous mirror as if the paper had established one.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against treating this thesis as a ChoCo mirror is that it lacks a numbered result about the complexity of one of its own computational problems. Most of its results characterize aggregation rules axiomatically, analyze incentives, prove convergence, or establish probabilistic optimality. The thesis does mention that a weight rule’s outcomes can be computed in polynomial time, then cites prior work for NP-completeness of possible-winner determination under Borda. That passage is the closest candidate, but it is not a numbered complexity result of this thesis.

Theorem 6.2 is the strongest proposed anchor. A large crowdsourcing population grouped by task and reliability is a sensible high multiplicity setting; the thesis’s small committee examples do not rule it out. For a distribution over those types, the optimal rule would aggregate mass-weighted log-likelihoods. That is a natural continuous version of the truth-tracking result. It still mirrors a theorem about which rule maximizes accuracy, rather than a computational problem whose complexity or algorithms the thesis studies. Asking how to compute the aggregate would introduce a new task; asking which rule is optimal restates the theorem’s kind of question, outside ChoCo’s computational remit.

Proposition 5.35 is a weaker anchor. It proves convergence for a finite sequence of individual preference changes; the \(nm\) bound counts rounds. In a continuum, one person has zero mass and cannot change the aggregate winner. Giving an entire cohort the power to move changes the strategic unit, while allowing infinitesimal updates leads to a population dynamics question. Either version could be studied, but neither is the computational problem classified by Proposition 5.35. The thesis itself lists computing minimally diverging moves as an open question; that is a possible research lead, not a result to mirror.

The Chapter 3 comparison with possible winners deserves a stronger attempt than the proponent gave it. One could distribute mass over partial ballots and ask which Borda winners are possible under completions. That is a recognizable high multiplicity computational question. Yet the thesis’s numbered result there compares outcomes of its weight rules with possible and necessary winners; the NP-completeness claim is cited prior work, and the thesis does not classify a new computational problem. A mass-valued completion problem might be worthwhile, but it would be a new construction rather than a mirror of a numbered computational result in this paper.

So the negative case is strong under ChoCo’s entry criterion: the thesis offers no qualifying anchor. It does not establish that continuization of its aggregation models could never be worthwhile. The truth-tracking chapter has a plausible population regime, which is evidence for the model’s sense, and its continuous optimum could be a useful result in another programme. The case against is that this thesis does not supply the computational result ChoCo is seeking to continuize.
