The closest result is Theorem 11 (proved here): in the proposed strategy-proof SRM, an agent reports both its final belief and private signal; after the market closes, its payment is the larger of the two scoring gains, each measured against the other agents’ private signals that maximize that gain. The theorem says this makes truthful reporting optimal without the PPO, OP-MI, or NNI conditions. But the paper’s market depends on agents’ ordered, individually consequential reports and payments. Treating forecasters as infinitesimal mass risks making each person’s report have no effect; grouping them into consequential cohorts changes the strategic unit. The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a continuous mirror is that the paper supplies no named computational result to mirror. Theorem 11 establishes truthfulness for a proposed scoring-rule market; the other theorems and lemmas characterize incentive conditions and worst-case losses. None classifies the complexity of a computational problem or gives an algorithmic result of the kind ChoCo is charting.

The proposed type distribution over \((p^{\mathrm{prv}},p^{\mathrm{pub}},M)\) is plausible as a model of a large forecasting population. But it does not preserve the paper’s central strategic object without substantial changes. A participant’s report changes the market estimate, can affect later participants’ public signals, and earns a payment tied to particular reports by other agents. If each participant becomes infinitesimal, their effect on the market and on others disappears. If cohorts retain the power to make consequential reports, the strategic unit becomes a cohort, and the sequential game and its truthfulness claims change. Scaling reports into mass contributions could define a new dynamic population market, but the paper provides no computational question or result for that model.

One might instead ask for the minimum per-capita payment needed to enforce truthfulness in a market with a distribution of types. That is a reasonable research question, but in the paper’s setting it risks reducing to rescaling the existing loss formulas; a richer version would require new modeling choices about mass updates, timing, and incentives. The authors’ recognition gate is therefore a real obstacle: this would be a proposed extension of their market, not a clear continuous mirror of a result they prove.

This is not a proof that no author-recognizable continuous market model could be built.
