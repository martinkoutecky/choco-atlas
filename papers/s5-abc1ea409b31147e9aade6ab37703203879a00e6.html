What the mirror covers

The support-bounded restatement translates Theorem 1's frontier to societies with at most \(\tau\) ranking types in its stated parameter ranges; it does not claim computational results for the paper's other structural characterizations.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a type based guarantee problem for a large cooperative choosing among a finite set of projects. Many members have the same ranking, so the society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over ranking types, with far more members than distinct types. A type is a complete ranking; its mass is its share of the cooperative. The mechanism’s decision is a lottery \(\ell_\mu\) over projects. A rank lottery \(\lambda\) is feasible if, for every allowed society, each type present gets a rank distribution that stochastically dominates \(\lambda\). The goal is to find all feasible \(\lambda\) that no feasible guarantee dominates.

To make that question match the paper’s worst case guarantee, let the allowed societies be all distributions over at most \(\tau\) ranking types. This is a plausible high multiplicity regime: membership can be large while the number of distinct rankings stays small. The mass values define the society, though this guarantee criterion uses them only to determine which types are present. That limitation matters.

The closest anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper. It says that for \(3\le n<p\), the maximal guarantees include the intervals from \(\mathrm{uni}(p)\) to \(\mathrm{vt}(n,p)\) and from \(\mathrm{uni}(p)\) to \(\mathrm{rd}(n,p)\). Here \(\mathrm{uni}(p)\) assigns probability \(1/p\) to every rank; \(\mathrm{vt}(n,p)\) assigns equal probability \(1/(p-n)\) to ranks \(2\) through \(p-n+1\); and \(\mathrm{rd}(n,p)\) assigns \(1/n\) to the first \(n-1\) ranks and the last rank. The theorem says these two intervals are the entire set of maximal guarantees when \(p\le 2n-2\), and also when \(p=2n\), except for \((n,p)=(4,8)\) and \((5,10)\).

Replacing \(n\) with the support bound \(\tau\) gives a real, if narrow, bridge: the continuous problem above is exactly the finite guarantee problem with \(\tau\) representatives, one per type. Duplicating a ranking does not add a new individual constraint. Thus Theorem 1 supplies the full frontier for the stated parameter ranges, and the expected result there is tractability by inherited structural characterization. If the referee accepts a named structural characterization as an anchor, this is the lead mirror.

There is a qualification required by your anchor rule: the paper has no named computational complexity result—none asserting membership in P, hardness, or a related algorithmic classification. Theorem 1 characterizes a mathematical set; it does not state a complexity result or running time. So it is only a near miss, not a compliant computational anchor. The paper’s named results are constructive and structural, but treating them as complexity results would overstate the case.

The weakest point is also the main reason the mirror may not survive review: the continuous masses do no work beyond indicating support. The mirror is recognizable as the authors’ individual guarantee question, expressed using preference types, but it does not reveal a new complexity landscape or a meaningful mass-sensitive relaxation. A stronger follow-up would be to study a fixed society \(\mu\)’s type-level guarantee frontier; however, the paper’s universal characterization does not anchor that different question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that Theorem 1 is not a computational result. It characterizes which guarantees are maximal for given \(n,p\); the paper gives no complexity classification or algorithmic problem with an input society, running time, or approximation target. The positive case acknowledges this, so calling Theorem 1 an anchor does not bridge the gap to ChoCo’s computational agenda.

The support-based proposal is the closest possible mirror. But with the paper’s original guarantee, repeated agents of the same ranking impose identical constraints: one lottery must give each of them a rank distribution that dominates \(\lambda\). Repeating that constraint changes nothing. Thus a society supported on \(\tau\) rankings has the same guarantee-feasibility question as one representative per supported ranking; its positive rational masses do not enter. Theorem 1 may therefore describe a support-count version of the frontier, but that is a structural transfer, not a mass-sensitive computational landscape. The fact that a large population can have few ranking types makes this a plausible instance regime; it does not supply the missing computational question.

The proposed fixed-\(\mu\) frontier does not repair that gap. If it keeps the paper’s individual guarantee, the mass assigned to a ranking still adds no constraint beyond that ranking’s presence. If instead \(\mu\) weights welfare or makes each type’s bloc the unit to be protected, the objective changes: individual protection against adversarial others becomes an aggregate or coalition guarantee. That could be a worthwhile new bargaining model, but the paper’s theorems do not anchor it.

There is also a deeper difficulty for a literal nonatomic mechanism. A single individual has zero mass, so changing their report cannot affect a mechanism that depends only on the population distribution. The paper’s guarantee is an individual’s safe action in a game; preserving that interpretation in a continuum would require the mechanism to retain individual-level information, while replacing individuals with type blocs changes who is acting. The paper’s lotteries are continuous mixtures over outcomes, but that is outcome-space continuity, not continuization of the population.

I cannot establish that no related nonatomic bargaining question could ever be useful. A new model of type-level or mass-weighted guarantees might be. The negative case is strongest and fairly solid for this paper as a ChoCo mirror: the best faithful reformulation discards the masses, and the proposed anchor supplies no computational result to carry into the programme.
