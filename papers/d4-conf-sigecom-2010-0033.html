The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The appended case offers no continuous-mirror anchor to defeat. It says the paper has no named computational result to mirror, which supports the negative case rather than the case for a mirror.

The strongest candidate in the paper is Section 5.1’s maximum-social-welfare benchmark: choose the largest set of deals subject to the network’s edges and each node’s deal limit. But the paper uses this optimization to normalize experimental performance; it does not present a computational result about the problem. The reported finding is that human subjects achieved roughly the greedy algorithm’s average efficiency, while leaving some feasible deals unclosed. Turning that benchmark into a high-multiplicity problem would require a society made of repeated network roles, with the links and deal limits preserved. That could define a continuous population model, but its question would be about centralized feasible deal selection, not the paper’s finding about how people negotiate.

The better version of a behavioral mirror would keep bargaining power, offers, deal limits, and network position in each agent’s type, then study a large population of such agents. Yet the paper’s behavioral findings concern outcomes of specific network structures and a specific interaction protocol: who is connected to whom, who proposes, who waits, and who refuses to close. Replacing those findings with computations over masses would require a new formal bargaining problem. Treating the continuum analytically would instead move toward the mean-field dynamics that the programme distinguishes from computational questions.

The other results do not supply a stronger anchor. The correlations between degree, deal limits, neighborhood structure, patience, and earnings are empirical findings, not complexity results. The stable-and-balanced outcome results are cited background, not results established by this paper. And the paper’s comparisons of human behavior with theory do not define an algorithmic task whose continuous-population complexity the paper studies.

So the strongest negative case is that this paper provides no computational result for ChoCo to continuize. A repeated-network bargaining or matching project might be worth pursuing on its own, but it would be a new project rather than a continuous mirror of this paper. That distinction is the weak point in a universal “no scenario” claim: a large repeated network is conceivable. The case against a *mirror of this paper* is much firmer than the case against every worthwhile continuous study of bargaining networks.
