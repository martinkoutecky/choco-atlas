The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t provide a compliant computational anchor: the paper has no numbered result asserting a complexity classification or giving an algorithm for a computational problem. The nearest results are structural. Result 2.1 says a winning coalition in the one-dimensional Gamson game is stable exactly when it has minimum total endowment among winning coalitions; the authors give a direct proof and note that it follows from Corollary 2 of BKS. Result 3.3, proved here, says a game with a proper family of winning coalitions is stable exactly when it is congruent. Result 4.2, also proved here using Lyapunov’s theorem, says that in the atomless, multidimensional double-division game a coalition is stable exactly when each of its \(K\) characteristic totals is \(1/2+\epsilon\), and that such a coalition exists. None of these states a computational result.

The nearest positive candidate, if Result 4.2 is allowed as a non-computational hook, is Stable Coalition Construction\(_\infty\). An instance gives \(K\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) over \(\tau\) complete types, each with a positive characteristic vector \(\theta_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^K\), normalized so \(\sum_t\mu_t\theta_t^k=1\) for every \(k\), and the paper’s \(\epsilon\). A solution gives coalition masses \(x_t\in[0,\mu_t]\) whose aggregates satisfy
\[ \sum_t x_t\theta_t^k=\frac12+\epsilon \quad\text{for every }k. \]
Each type is a group of indistinguishable participants, and its mass is the fraction of that group in the coalition. This is plausible in a large membership body with repeated contribution profiles and few types relative to its population. It is tractable by the direct construction \(x_t=(1/2+\epsilon)\mu_t\).

That candidate is also the reason I would not present it as a strong ChoCo mirror: Section 4 already studies this continuous-population problem, and Result 4.2 gives precisely its stability condition. The proposed construction merely spells out that result for finite support. My weakest point is therefore decisive for the requested case: the paper supplies no computational anchor, and its most natural continuous candidate is already part of the paper’s model.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a paper-anchored ChoCo mirror is that the paper has no numbered result about computational complexity, algorithms, approximation, or parameterized complexity. Its results characterize stable coalitions, establish existence or nonexistence, and describe equilibrium structure. Turning “find a stable coalition” into a computational problem would add a search task the paper does not analyze.

The proposed Stable Coalition Construction\(_\infty\) is a poor rescue. Result 4.2 already studies an atomless population and says stability is equivalent to each characteristic total being \(q=1/2+\epsilon\). In the proposed finite-support input, with \(\sum_t\mu_t\theta_t^k=1\), set \(x_t=q\mu_t\). Then every characteristic total is \(q\), so this directly constructs a stable coalition. It is a sensible mass model, but the output follows immediately from the paper’s condition. The paper’s analytic proof is not, by itself, prior computational work; the problem is that the natural finite-support construction adds no meaningful computational question. The same scaling also attains the minimum weighted total in Results 2.1 and 3.1. A version with different thresholds would be a new feasibility problem, expressible as a linear program, rather than a mirror of Result 4.2.

The best alternate hook is the policy extension: one could imagine a large stakeholder body with repeated \((\theta,x)\) types and ask for a stable coalition of type masses. That is more plausible than treating a small set of political parties as a continuum. But Result 4.3 proves only a three-player equilibrium result; it supplies no computational problem to mirror. A computational formulation would need to specify how utilities and blocking mass sets are represented. That could be worthwhile adjacent research, but it is not a computational result of this paper.

I would not claim that the atomless section is a novelty collision just because it is continuous; the brief explicitly rules out that shortcut. My negative case is strongest for the proposed anchor and for mirrors tied to the paper’s stated results. The universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario is weaker: the policy extension leaves room for a plausible new population model, even though this paper does not give it a computational anchor.
