The case FOR (proponent)

The strict answer is that this paper has no named computational result of the required kind. It proves no theorem classifying a problem as tractable, hard, FPT, or similar. The closest anchor is Proposition 1, proved here: with a uniform initial ranking and large \(N,\kappa\), for interior \(\gamma\), the final clicking probability on a group of sites carrying the same signal decreases as that group grows, apart from the excluded sizes \(0\), \((M-1)/2\), and \(M-1\). That is the advantage-of-the-fewer result, not a complexity result. Corollary 1 and the other propositions likewise concern analytical properties of the dynamics. So there is no qualifying anchor, and no anchor-backed mirror question to submit under your rules.

There is a plausible computational extension of the paper’s asymptotic-learning question. Consider a large cohort of users making the same query. A type could be \(t=(g,x,z)\), where \(g\) records the user’s preference parameter \(\gamma_g\), and \(x,z\) are their two signals. Its mass \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of the cohort with that complete behavioral description. With \(G\) preference groups, there are at most \(4G\) types, while the number of users \(N\) can be much larger. The websites retain their fixed binary signals \(y_m\).

For a precise candidate, let \(r\) be the ranking distribution, let \(h_m\) be the number of sites with the same signal as site \(m\), and define \(v_{t,m}\) by the paper’s content values:
\[ v_{t,m}=\frac{1}{h_m} \begin{cases} 1 & x=z=y_m,\\ \gamma_g & x=y_m\ne z,\\ 1-\gamma_g & z=y_m\ne x,\\ 0 & x\ne y_m,\ z\ne y_m. \end{cases} \]
For valid instances where each type has at least one site of positive value, set
\[ \rho_{t,m}(r)=\frac{r_m v_{t,m}}{\sum_j r_jv_{t,j}}, \qquad \Theta_m(r)=\sum_t\mu_t\rho_{t,m}(r), \qquad \dot r_m=\Theta_m(r)-r_m, \]
starting from a positive rational ranking \(r_1\). The continuous problem is: given the rational type masses, website signals, \(r_1\), and truth \(\omega\), return an \(\varepsilon\)-approximation to
\[ A_\infty=\lim_{s\to\infty}\sum_{m:y_m=\omega}\Theta_m(r(s)). \]
This asks for the same asymptotic correct-site traffic quantity studied in the paper. I would expect this fixed-parameter version to be tractable, but the paper supplies no bit-complexity or convergence-rate result.

The regime is credible: a query handled by millions of users can have a small number of repeated preference-and-signal types. The authors would likely recognize the model, since its flow equation comes directly from their mean-dynamics analysis. That is also the weak point: the paper already develops this analytical limit, so the proposed question adds a computational-complexity layer rather than mirroring one of its named computational findings. The most defensible positive case is therefore a plausible research extension, not a qualifying case anchored in this paper’s computational results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has no result to anchor a ChoCo mirror. Proposition 1 proves the advantage of the fewer as a comparative static of limiting traffic; Corollary 1 and Propositions 2–7 likewise analyze traffic, learning, polarization, and ranking dynamics. None poses a computational problem or gives a complexity, algorithm, or approximation result. The ranking algorithm is part of the model, not an algorithmic task studied by the paper. Under the requested anchor standard, that is decisive.

The proposed \(A_\infty\) question does not supply a missing anchor. Appendix A already derives the mean-dynamics ODE, and Section 4 studies the same limiting correct-site traffic. Asking for a finite-precision algorithm to evaluate that limit adds a computational layer to an existing analytic object. In the candidate’s \(\alpha=1\) model, websites with the same signal retain their relative ranking shares, so the dynamics reduce to the aggregate ranking mass on the two signal classes. Each type contributes a rational function of that single aggregate mass. This may support a numerical-analysis question, but it does not mirror a computational result of the paper.

A stronger proposal might ask for the least population intervention that makes traffic favor the wrong signal, or for a ranking policy that optimizes accuracy or polarization. But the paper supplies no intervention an agent can choose: users’ signals are realized privately, website signals are fixed, and the ranking rule is prescribed. Defining a campaign, controller, action set, and objective would add a new problem to the model. The issue is not that individual users cannot be grouped into types; it is that the paper contains no manipulable social-choice process to control.

So the negative case is strong against a *mirror of this paper’s results*. It is weaker as the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous research question can be built from the model: intervention and design questions are conceivable, but they would be follow-on projects rather than mirrors anchored here.
