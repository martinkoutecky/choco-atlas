The continuous mirror question

Given rational type masses \(\mu_a\) over \(\tau\) agent types, \(k\) item classes with rational per-capita supplies \(q_j\), additive values \(v_{a,j}\), and fixed bundle bound \(B\), choose \(x_{a,b}\ge 0\) for integral bundles \(b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^k\) with \(\sum_j b_j\le B\), subject to \(\sum_b x_{a,b}=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_{a,b}b_jx_{a,b}=q_j\), maximizing \(W(x)=\exp(\sum_{a,b}x_{a,b}\log v_a(b))\), with zero welfare if positive mass receives zero value.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity NSW fair-division market in which agent types carry mass \(\mu\), item classes have per-capita supplies \(q\), each mass unit receives an integral bundle, and the normalized geometric-mean welfare is optimized over bundle-count masses.

The objection that survived

The mirror co-replicates agents and item classes and relies on a fixed bundle bound \(B\), so it does not directly relax the theorem's full arbitrary valuation matrix; its tractability is therefore a property of a restricted market regime.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the paper's NSW approximation result and its allocation problem, while leaving the stable-polynomial generalization, permanent connection, and algebraic proof machinery without separate continuous analogues.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the fixed bundle bound \(B\) be removed while retaining an efficient pricing oracle for the configuration formulation?
  • What approximation and finite-population transfer guarantees relate the continuous optimum to rational finite markets?
  • Can heterogeneous item identities be incorporated through an item-side type distribution without changing the intended NSW problem?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a single algorithmic anchor: Theorem 1.1, proved in this paper. It states, in full, that for allocating \(m\) indivisible items to \(n\) agents with nonnegative additive valuations, there is a randomized polynomial-time algorithm which, with high probability, returns an allocation whose Nash social welfare is at least \(1/e\) of optimum. This is the paper’s own theorem, not a cited result. The paper has no numbered hardness theorem of its own; its NP-hardness statement in the introduction cites other papers. [The published version labels this result Theorem 1.1.](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol067-itcs2017/LIPIcs.ITCS.2017.36/LIPIcs.ITCS.2017.36.pdf)

My lead mirror would be Continuum Nash-Welfare Maximization with Integral Bundles.

Take \(\tau\) agent types \(a\in[\tau]\), with rational masses \(\mu_a\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_a\mu_a=1\). Type \(a\) is specified by its complete additive valuation vector \(v_{a,1},\ldots,v_{a,k}\) over \(k\) item classes. There are \(q_j\) copies per capita of item class \(j\), where \(q_j\) is rational. Copies remain indivisible.

To keep the intended large-market regime concrete, suppose every agent receives at most \(B\) items, where \(B\) is a fixed household-size bound. An integral bundle is \(b\in\mathcal B_B=\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^k:\sum_j b_j\le B\}\), and its value to type \(a\) is \(v_a(b)=\sum_j b_jv_{a,j}\).

The decision variable is \(x_{a,b}\ge0\), the mass of type-\(a\) agents receiving bundle \(b\). Feasibility requires \(\sum_bx_{a,b}=\mu_a\) for every \(a\), and \(\sum_{a,b}b_jx_{a,b}=q_j\) for every item class \(j\). Thus, each unit of mass receives an integral bundle; only the number of agents receiving each bundle is continuous.

The objective is the normalized Nash welfare

\(\displaystyle W(x)=\prod_{a,b}v_a(b)^{x_{a,b}}=\exp\!\left(\sum_{a,b}x_{a,b}\log v_a(b)\right)\),

with \(W(x)=0\) if positive mass receives zero value. The problem asks for a feasible mass allocation maximizing \(W(x)\), or for an \(\alpha\)-approximation satisfying \(W(x)\ge\alpha W^\star\).

This is a genuine high-multiplicity version of the paper’s problem. If the masses and supplies are rational, choose \(N\) clearing all denominators. The instance expands to \(N\) named agents, \(N\mu_a\) agents of type \(a\), and \(Nq_j\) physical copies of item class \(j\). If \(Nx_{a,b}\) is integral, assigning that many agents bundle \(b\) gives finite-population geometric mean exactly \(W(x)\), since the finite product is \(W(x)^N\). The continuous problem is therefore not fractional allocation of goods: it is the population-level limit of allocating indivisible goods to many exchangeable agents.

The regime is a large allocation market with \(N\gg\tau\): for example, millions of households receiving small indivisible packages from a catalogue of standardized goods, with only a modest number of valuation or eligibility profiles. A type includes everything relevant to the problem, so agents in one type have identical values, eligibility, and bundle constraints. This is precisely the high-multiplicity interpretation rather than an assumption that named individuals have interchangeable idiosyncratic prices.

I would expect the stated bounded-bundle problem to be Class A. After taking logarithms, its objective is linear in the mass variables and its constraints are linear. For fixed \(B\), there are \(\binom{k+B}{B}\) bundle configurations, so the configuration LP is polynomial in \(\tau\), \(k\), and the encoding length, independently of \(N\). This is the continuous optimization gain: a huge labelled-agent problem collapses to a mass-allocation problem over repeated types, while bundles themselves remain integral.

The connection to Theorem 1.1 is substantive. The paper’s object is still exactly additive indivisible-goods NSW; the permanent and stable-polynomial machinery is only the authors’ finite-population algorithmic apparatus. The mirror asks whether their approximation problem becomes an exact or more efficiently approximable configuration optimization problem once repeated agents are represented by mass. The paper’s Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 3.3 support this direction, but I do not count them as separate anchors: Theorem 1.1 is the result being mirrored.

The main follow-up is whether the bounded-bundle assumption can be removed. With \(B\) variable, the configuration family is exponentially large, and the pricing problem becomes the relevant question. One would need to determine whether additive valuations yield an efficient pricing oracle, whether the paper’s \(1/e\) guarantee has a meaningful continuum analogue, and how accurately a continuous optimum can be transferred back to a finite population. If hardness arises from the item catalogue or bundle structure, it may be Class B; if it arises only from finite-population granularity, it should disappear.

My weakest point is that the fixed bundle bound is a regime assumption not present explicitly in the paper. If a referee insists on unrestricted bundle sizes and arbitrary repeated item classes, the positive complexity claim becomes substantially less secure: the pricing problem may itself be hard. The mirror nevertheless survives as a faithful continuous-population question, because its essential transformation is only the aggregation of identical agents into mass; indivisible goods and the Nash-welfare objective remain intact.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The attack must focus on whether the proposed construction is genuinely a continuization of Theorem 1.1. The theorem allows an arbitrary valuation matrix \(v_{i,j}\), with every item potentially having a distinct value profile. The proposed mirror instead replaces the item set by repeated item classes and assumes that every agent type sees identical copies of each class. Its finite expansion is therefore not merely \(N\) copies of the society: it replicates both rows and columns of the valuation matrix. It describes an \(N\)-fold product market, a strict special case of the paper’s problem.

That modification is necessary for nondegeneracy. If only the agents are replicated while the paper’s \(m\) individual items remain fixed, almost all agents receive nothing and normalized Nash welfare tends to zero. But once per-capita supplies and repeated item classes are introduced, the model is no longer a population-only mirror of the theorem. A still more faithful construction would need to retain heterogeneous item identities, which requires a second population or an item-side measure. That is a new two-sided matching or market model, not the continuous-agent version of the paper’s assignment problem.

The fixed bundle bound \(B\) is another substantive restriction. It is what makes the configuration family \(\binom{k+B}{B}\) polynomial. Without it, the natural configuration LP has exponentially many bundles, and its pricing problem is

\[ \max_{b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^k} \left\{\log\!\left(\sum_j b_jv_{a,j}\right)-\sum_j p_jb_j\right\}, \]

subject to the bundle and supply constraints. There is no tractability consequence of Theorem 1.1 for this pricing problem; with variable bundle sizes it has the character of an integer knapsack-type optimization. Thus the only clean Class A result supplied by the proposed mirror comes from imposing a bounded-household regime absent from the paper.

The mirror is also algorithmically disconnected from the paper’s contribution. The stable-polynomial and permanent machinery concerns the finite coupling created by assigning each physical item to one named agent. The configuration formulation removes that coupling at the modelling stage and becomes a linear program over bundle counts. That may be a useful large-market fair-division problem, but it does not illuminate the paper’s theorem or its central algebraic technique.

This is not an airtight negative case. The programme explicitly permits per-capita supplies and integral bundles with continuously varying masses, and under that permissive standard the proposed bounded-bundle market is a coherent high-multiplicity problem. NSW is symmetric in agents, so identity is not an obstruction either. The honest conclusion is therefore that the universal negative is weak: the construction is a narrowed, co-replicated market rather than a faithful mirror of the full theorem, but it probably still qualifies as a worthwhile continuous-population problem under the programme’s broad definition.
