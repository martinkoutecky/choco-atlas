An instance would give a finite party set \(P\), an integer seat total \(S\), a rule \(R\) such as Largest Remainder-Hare or Sainte-Laguë, and rational masses \(\mu_i\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{i\in P}\mu_i=1\). Compute the rule’s seat allocation \(s_i\), then ask whether there is a coalition \(X\subseteq P\) such that
\[ \sum_{i\in X}\mu_i < \tfrac12 \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{i\in X}s_i > \tfrac S2. \]
A solution is such a coalition, or a certificate that none exists. Here voter types are their party choices, mass is each party’s share of voters, and the natural regime is a large electorate with relatively few distinct party types. Large elections with relatively few party types make that a sensible high-multiplicity regime. Keeping seats integral is also a natural co-modification.
