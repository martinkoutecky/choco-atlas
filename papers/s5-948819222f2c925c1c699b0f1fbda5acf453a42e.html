The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The positive case leaves no eligible anchor to defend. Its closest named results are Theorem 1’s IC/IR characterization, Proposition 1’s VCG guarantees, Theorem 2’s CNA guarantees, and Propositions 2 and Theorem 3’s revenue comparisons. They establish mechanism properties; none classifies the complexity of a computational problem. The paper does describe how to find an efficient allocation, but that procedure is not stated as a named complexity result. A continuous question about that procedure would have to supply the missing anchor itself.

The strongest rescue is a mass version of Theorem 2 or Theorem 3: distribute buyers over value and cost types, scale supply with the market, and ask whether CNA retains its incentive and revenue guarantees. That could be a sensible auction-design project, but it asks for continuum mechanism guarantees rather than the computational questions this programme targets. It also changes what the guarantees mean. With atomless buyers, one buyer’s report changes zero mass, so individual IC and critical-bid payments can become vacuous. Keeping finite supply makes the winners zero measure; scaling supply with the population requires rewriting the auction’s allocation and payment rules.

The network structure makes aggregation harder. An intermediary’s complete type includes its neighbors and its place in the diffusion graph, because removing it can also remove access to downstream agents. Grouping intermediaries by local costs or buyer values loses those reachability and counterfactual details. Including full graph position in the type generally leaves each intermediary with a distinct type, so the support grows with the network. Symmetric repeated branches can avoid that problem, but making the gateways atomless also removes the individual pivotality that gives their diffusion incentives force. A buyer continuum with a fixed finite intermediary graph remains plausible; it is an extension of the auction model, rather than a continuous mirror of a named computational result here.

I can’t establish that no scenario could ever yield a worthwhile continuous project. A large cohort of similar buyers behind a fixed intermediary network is credible, and the authors might recognize it as a natural extension. That is the weak point in the negative case. The firmer conclusion is narrower: none of the proposed anchors supplies the computational result this programme is meant to continuize, and the best rescue requires a new model of incentives and allocation before it can supply one.
