The continuous mirror question

Given two agent types \(r\in\{L,S\}\) with masses \(\mu_r\), finite house types \(h\in H\) with per-capita supplies \(\rho_h\), weights \(w_r>0\), and utilities \(v_r(h)\), decide whether there exist \(x_{rh}\ge0\) and \(p_{rh}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_h x_{rh}=\mu_r\), \(\sum_r x_{rh}\le\rho_h\), and, whenever \(x_{rh}>0\) and \(x_{sg}>0\), \(\frac{v_r(h)+p_{rh}}{w_r}\ge\frac{v_r(g)+p_{sg}}{w_s}\); if so, output \(x\) and \(p\).

The model it lives in

A two-type high-multiplicity house-allocation model with agent masses \(\mu_r\), house supplies \(\rho_h\), aggregate assignment variables \(x_{rh}\), and block subsidy variables \(p_{rh}\); the task is feasibility rather than optimization.

The objection that survived

The proponent does not prove that block subsidies \(p_{rh}\) and support-wise inequalities are exactly equivalent to arbitrary per-agent subsidies in finite high-multiplicity realizations, especially when one type is split across several houses.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror directly covers Theorem 5.5, proposes a broader high-multiplicity extension of Theorem 3.5, and captures Theorem 4.5 as a support-cycle certificate; it leaves minimum-subsidy optimization and general unbounded-type existence without a full treatment.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the two-type mass problem admit exactly the supply-quantile criterion \(\frac{d_L^-}{w_L}\ge\frac{d_S^+}{w_S}\), including ties and unused supply?
  • Can weighted envy-free allocation existence for arbitrary finite agent types \(T\) and house types \(H\) be solved in time polynomial in \(|T|\), \(|H|\), and the encoding length?
  • What is the complexity of minimizing total subsidy in the continuous model once a feasible support and subsidy witness are required?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a high-multiplicity house-allocation mirror, with my lead anchor being Theorem 5.5.

Take a large population of families and a repeated supply of house types. An agent type \(r\) consists of a weight \(w_r\) and a utility vector \(v_r(h)\) over house types \(h\). Let \(\mu_r\) be the population mass of type \(r\), and let \(\rho_h\) be the per-capita supply of house type \(h\), with \(\sum_h\rho_h\ge 1\). Each infinitesimal agent still receives one whole house; only the aggregate assignment is fractional. Thus a solution is a mass matrix \(x_{rh}\) satisfying

\[ \sum_h x_{rh}=\mu_r,\qquad \sum_r x_{rh}\le \rho_h. \]

This is a natural regime for public housing: thousands of large and small families, perhaps only two or a few entitlement-and-preference types, and many repeated apartments of each relevant kind. The number of named families is enormous compared with the number of agent types and house types. Rational masses correspond exactly to finite high-multiplicity instances after clearing denominators.

The lead anchor is Theorem 5.5, proved in this paper. It states that, when there are two agent types—large agents with common \((w_L,v_L)\) and small agents with common \((w_S,v_S)\)—Algorithm 2 decides in polynomial time whether a weighted envy-freeable allocation exists and computes one if it does. The algorithm sorts houses by

\[ d(h)=v_L(h)-v_S(h) \]

in descending order. If \(n_L\) and \(n_S\) are the numbers of large and small agents, it allocates the top \(n_L\) houses to large agents and the bottom \(n_S\) houses to small agents, and succeeds exactly when

\[ \frac{d(h_{n_L})}{w_L} \ge \frac{d(h_{m-n_S+1})}{w_S}. \]

The continuous problem I would hand to a prover is therefore:

Two-Type WEFable House Allocation\(_\infty\). Given \(\mu_L,\mu_S\), house supplies \(\rho_h\), weights \(w_L,w_S\), and utilities \(v_L(h),v_S(h)\), decide whether there exist \(x_{Lh},x_{Sh}\) and nonnegative subsidies \(p_{Lh},p_{Sh}\) on the positive-support assignment blocks such that, whenever \(x_{rh}>0\) and \(x_{sg}>0\),

\[ \frac{v_r(h)+p_{rh}}{w_r} \ge \frac{v_r(g)+p_{sg}}{w_s}. \]

If so, output \(x\) and a subsidy witness. The expected Class A solution is to allocate mass \(\mu_L\) to the highest-\(d\) house supply and mass \(\mu_S\) to the lowest-\(d\) supply. Writing \(d_L^{-}\) for the lowest \(d\)-value used by large agents and \(d_S^{+}\) for the highest \(d\)-value used by small agents, feasibility is exactly

\[ \frac{d_L^{-}}{w_L}\ge \frac{d_S^{+}}{w_S}. \]

The finite order statistics in Theorem 5.5 become supply quantiles. The two-type cycle argument remains intact: a positive envy cycle exists exactly when some small-assigned house \(g\) and large-assigned house \(h\) violate \(d(h)/w_L\ge d(g)/w_S\). This is a genuine population mirror, not divisible housing: every unit of mass receives an integral house, while \(x\) records the aggregate assignment.

A broader secondary anchor is Theorem 3.5, also proved here. It states that Algorithm 1 decides in polynomial time whether weighted envy-free allocations exist and computes one if they do; the proof gives running time \(O(n^3m^2)\). Its continuous counterpart is:

Weighted EF House Allocation\(_\infty\). Given finitely many agent types \(T\), masses \(\mu_t\), house supplies \(\rho_h\), weights \(w_t\), and utilities \(v_t(h)\), decide whether there is a feasible mass assignment \(x\) such that, for every positive-support pair \(x_{th}>0\) and \(x_{ug}>0\),

\[ \frac{v_t(h)}{w_t} \ge \frac{v_t(g)}{w_u}. \]

Output such an \(x\), or report that none exists. I would expect this to be Class A: the paper’s virtual-assignment filtering becomes a problem over \(|T|\cdot |H|\) type-house pairs, and its matching tests become capacitated-flow or min-cut tests. The natural target is a running time polynomial in \(|T|\), \(|H|\), and the encoding length of the rational data, independent of the number of repeated agents.

The paper’s Theorem 4.5 strengthens the second mirror conceptually: an allocation is weighted envy-freeable exactly when its weighted envy graph has no positive-weight cycle, equivalently when it is permutation resistant. In the continuous formulation, the graph has one node for every positive-support block \((t,h)\), with edge weight

\[ \frac{v_t(g)}{w_u}-\frac{v_t(h)}{w_t} \]

from \((t,h)\) to \((u,g)\). Thus the subsidy part of the mirror has a finite type-level certificate even though the population is atomless.

Both mirrors should be recognisable to the authors. Their own motivating example is explicitly larger versus smaller families, and Theorem 5.5 already treats exactly two agent types. The only necessary co-modification is scaling house supplies with population mass; indivisibility is retained at the individual level.

The main weakness is that this paper contains no named NP-hardness theorem of its own. Its statement that minimum-subsidy envy-free house allocation is NP-hard is a literature summary, not a numbered result proved here, so it cannot honestly serve as a hardness anchor. Moreover, the two-type continuous theorem is close to a quantile reformulation of Algorithm 2, and the general high-multiplicity compression of Theorem 3.5 still needs to be proved rather than assumed. The resulting case is therefore a credible Class A mirror and a useful starting point, but not evidence that a named discrete hardness result dissolves in the continuum.

Natural follow-ups are minimum total subsidy, the complexity of WEFable existence for an unbounded number of agent types, and whether some multi-type variants become continuum-specifically hard even though the two-type case remains tractable.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is about value, not legitimacy—and it is ultimately not strong enough to support the universal claim.

Theorem 5.5 is the easiest anchor to resist. Its algorithm already depends on the population only through the two integers \(n_L\) and \(n_S\); individual agents have disappeared from the theorem. Replacing those counts by masses and replacing repeated houses by per-capita supplies turns the order-statistic condition into a quantile condition,

\[ \frac{d_L^-}{w_L}\ge \frac{d_S^+}{w_S}, \qquad d(h)=v_L(h)-v_S(h). \]

That is a valid high-multiplicity formulation, but it is almost entirely a change of encoding. It introduces no new population-level combinatorics or continuous-optimization issue. The paper’s theorem has already compressed the population to its sufficient statistics. One could therefore argue that this is too slight a contribution to justify a continuization programme.

That argument does not actually defeat the mirror, however. The better version—with finitely many repeated house types, rational supply masses, and integral houses assigned to individual agents—is a legitimate co-modified high-multiplicity model. It is not outcome-space fractionalization: the mass matrix merely records an aggregate of integral assignments. The two-type theorem genuinely lifts to it, and the authors would plainly recognise the question.

Theorem 3.5 gives a somewhat stronger negative opening. The proponent’s claim that the virtual-assignment procedure “becomes flow” is not automatic. The proof uses individual virtual assignments, ordinary Hall violators, and the fact that each agent receives exactly one named house. A type-level version needs a capacitated deletion lemma, a precise treatment of split type mass, and a proof that the support-based envy constraints survive the passage to atomless assignments. None of that follows merely from replacing matching by flow.

But this is a criticism of the proposed proof, not of the mirror. The finite-type, finite-house-type mass problem is well-defined, and rational mass solutions have a direct finite high-multiplicity interpretation. Its complexity could be genuinely different from the paper’s \(O(n^3m^2)\) algorithm. Thus this anchor cannot be dismissed: it is precisely the sort of unproved continuous computational question the programme is meant to formulate.

Theorem 4.5 is weaker as an anchor. It characterizes whether a fixed allocation is subsidy-enviable; it does not solve the existence problem over allocations. A graph whose nodes are positive-support type–house blocks is a reasonable continuous analogue, but it requires a measurable realization of the mass assignment and a careful argument that subsidies may be symmetrized within each block. Once those details are supplied, the certificate is finite and meaningful—but it is a certificate for a chosen support, not by itself a population-complexity result.

So the negative case can say that Theorem 5.5 is mainly a quantile restatement, Theorem 3.5 has been overclaimed rather than proved, and Theorem 4.5 is only a fixed-allocation characterization. It cannot honestly say that no worthwhile mirror exists. The paper has exactly the ingredients the programme wants: repeated agent types are explicitly motivated by family size, the population can be replaced by masses without losing integral housing, and the general type-level version may expose a real algorithmic boundary. The strongest defensible recommendation is therefore “do not sell the two-type quantile reformulation alone; pursue the general high-multiplicity problem only with a formal finite-to-continuous dictionary and a genuine complexity result.”
