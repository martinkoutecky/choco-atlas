The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T=R\sqcup S\), rational mass vector \(\mu\), rational type interaction rates \(\lambda_{uv}\), trust parameters \(\theta_{uv}\), and stubborn beliefs \(x_s\), define \(Q^\mu_{uv}=\mu_v\lambda_{uv}\theta_{uv}\) for regular \(u\), with diagonal entries making regular rows sum to zero. Compute the exact stationary type means \(h_u\), or an additive-\(\varepsilon\) approximation, satisfying \(\sum_v Q^\mu_{uv}h_v=0\) for \(u\in R\) and \(h_s=x_s\) for \(s\in S\).

The model it lives in

A finite-type block-network model with type mass \(\mu\), type-level contact rates \(\lambda_{uv}\), trust parameters \(\theta_{uv}\), and decision variables \(h_u\) representing stationary mean beliefs; the task is exact or approximate linear-algebra computation.

The objection that survived

The block construction is a restricted blow-up regime and does not preserve arbitrary graph geometry, so it mirrors a meaningful instance class rather than the paper's full network model.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers computation of stationary type means through the harmonic and hitting-probability equations in Theorem 4.1. It leaves the full stationary law, finite-\(N\) convergence rates, named-agent coalescing cross-products, graph-specific examples, and the high-fluidity concentration theorem outside the secure mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Prove convergence of finite-\(N\) exchangeable block networks to the \(Q^\mu\)-harmonic stationary means.
  • Determine the correct \(N\to\infty\) two-lineage limit for cross-products when same-type agents are distinct.
  • Formulate a two-measure high-fluidity theorem using population mass \(\nu\) and exposure stationary measure \(\pi\).

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a Class A mirror, anchored by the paper’s exact harmonic-characterization results. This is not a paper with a named NP-hardness or polynomial-time theorem: its results are probabilistic and analytic. I would therefore not claim that the authors already studied computational complexity. But the paper does provide unusually clean finite-dimensional equations from which a genuine high-multiplicity computational problem follows. The original paper is explicitly finite-agent: its nodes are individuals, even when \(n\) becomes large. [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.2653) therefore does not itself collide with a continuous-population formulation.

The mirror is a society of exchangeable communication types. Let \(T=R\sqcup S\) be a finite set of types, where \(R\) contains regular types and \(S\) contains stubborn types. A type records everything relevant to the dynamics: whether agents are regular or stubborn, a stubborn type’s fixed belief \(x_s\), its contact intensities with every other type, and its trust parameters. The society is a rational mass vector \(\mu\in\Delta_T\). Thus \(\mu_u\) is the fraction of the population belonging to type \(u\).

A finite realization with \(N\mu_u\) agents of type \(u\) gives every ordered pair consisting of types \(u,v\) an interaction clock of rate \(\lambda_{uv}/N\). Consequently, a type-\(u\) agent meets type \(v\) at aggregate rate \(\mu_v\lambda_{uv}\). When \(u\) is regular, the update is exactly the paper’s update, \(X_u\leftarrow(1-\theta_{uv})X_u+\theta_{uv}X_v\); stubborn agents retain \(x_s\). This is a high-multiplicity block-network regime: \(N\) may be millions while \(|T|=\tau\) is fixed or modest. A plausible setting is a large platform whose users fall into a few recurring demographic, institutional, or communication-exposure cells, with each cell having the same interaction profile and trust parameters. The stubborn types can represent many institutional or party accounts following the same fixed message.

The continuous object is the population mass \(\mu\), not the opinion space. The opinion remains a scalar state variable, just as in the paper. The finite-to-continuous dictionary is direct: rational \(\mu\) gives a finite block society, while the block society converges to the type model as \(N\to\infty\).

My lead anchor is Theorem 4.1, proved in this paper. It states that, under the reachability assumption that every regular agent is influenced by a stubborn agent, the stationary expected belief and stationary cross-products are determined by hitting probabilities of the associated Markov chains. In the paper’s notation, if \(\gamma_s^v\) is the probability that the chain starting at \(v\) first hits stubborn agent \(s\), and \(\eta_{ss'}^{vv'}\) is the corresponding joint hitting probability for the coupled pair chain, then

\( \mathbb E[X_v]=\sum_s\gamma_s^v x_s \)

and

\( \mathbb E[X_vX_{v'}]=\sum_{s,s'}\eta_{ss'}^{vv'}x_sx_{s'} \).

The theorem further says that the vector of stationary expectations is the unique solution of the harmonic equations

\( \sum_v Q_{av}\mathbb E[X_v]=0 \)

for regular \(a\), with boundary conditions \(\mathbb E[X_s]=x_s\) for stubborn \(s\). The stationary cross-products are likewise the unique solution of the coupled-chain harmonic system

\( \sum_{w,w'}K_{(a,a'),(w,w')}\mathbb E[X_wX_{w'}]=0 \),

for regular \(a,a'\), together with the boundary conditions

\( \mathbb E[X_vX_{v'}]=\mathbb E[X_v]\mathbb E[X_{v'}] \)

whenever at least one coordinate is stubborn. This is a theorem proved here, not a result cited from elsewhere.

The corresponding continuous problem is:

Stationary Type Influence\(_\infty\). An instance consists of \(T=R\sqcup S\), rational \(\mu\), rational contact intensities \(\lambda_{uv}\), rational trust parameters \(\theta_{uv}\), and rational stubborn beliefs \(x_s\). Define the type-level generator by \(Q^\mu_{uv}=\mu_v\lambda_{uv}\theta_{uv}\) for regular \(u\) and \(v\neq u\), with diagonal entries making each regular row sum to zero and stubborn rows absorbing. Construct the associated two-lineage generator \(K^\mu\) using the paper’s coupling: off the diagonal, the two lineages evolve independently; on the diagonal, a regular update sends them together with rate \(\theta_{uv}Q^\mu_{uv}\), or separates one lineage with rate \((1-\theta_{uv})Q^\mu_{uv}\). The task is to output the exact stationary type means \(h_u\) and cross-products \(M_{uv}\), or an additive-\(\varepsilon\) approximation to each.

A solution is therefore a vector \(h\) and matrix \(M\) satisfying the two displayed type-level harmonic systems, with \(h_s=x_s\) and the corresponding stubborn boundary conditions. The original authors should recognize this as their problem: it preserves regular and stubborn agents, stochastic gossip, trust-weighted updates, stationary beliefs, Markov-chain duality, and the exact observable they compute. Only the population representation changes from individually named vertices to repeated exchangeable types.

This mirror should be Class A. The mean problem is a rational Dirichlet linear system of dimension \(|R|\), and the general second-moment problem is a linear system of dimension \(O(\tau^2)\). Hence it is solvable in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and the input bit length. For the particularly natural voter-model case \(\theta_{uv}=1\), the variance is even simpler: \(h_u\) solves the harmonic system with boundary \(x_s\), while the second moment solves the same system with boundary \(x_s^2\).

My second, weaker anchor is Theorem 6.1, also proved here. It states that, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), the fraction of agents whose stationary expected belief differs from the weighted-mean belief \(\mathbb E[Z]\) by more than \(\Delta_*\varepsilon\) is at most \(1/(\varepsilon\Psi)\):

\( \frac{1}{n}\left|\left\{v:\left|\mathbb E[X_v]-\mathbb E[Z]\right|>\Delta_*\varepsilon\right\}\right|\le \frac{1}{\varepsilon\Psi}. \)

When all trust parameters equal \(1\), it also states

\( \frac{1}{n}\left|\left\{v:\left|\sigma_v^2-\sigma_Z^2\right|>\Delta_*^2\varepsilon\right\}\right|\le \frac{1}{\varepsilon\Psi}. \)

Here \(Z\) is the stubborn-belief random variable obtained by starting the auxiliary chain from its stationary distribution and recording which stubborn type it hits, and \(\Psi\) is the paper’s fluidity parameter. The theorem is proved in the paper; the underlying mixing estimate is based on cited Markov-chain results.

Its continuous counterpart is Homogeneous Type Influence\(_\infty\). Given the same type society, an auxiliary irreducible exposure chain \(P\) with stationary distribution \(\mu\), and a tolerance \(\varepsilon\), compute the hitting distributions \(\gamma^u\), the reference distribution \( \bar\gamma_s=\sum_u\mu_u\gamma_s^u \), and the associated \(Z\). The required output is the mass

\( \mu\!\left(\left\{u:\left|h_u-\mathbb E[Z]\right|>\Delta_*\varepsilon\right\}\right) \)

and, when \(\theta=1\),

\( \mu\!\left(\left\{u:\left|\sigma_u^2-\sigma_Z^2\right|>\Delta_*^2\varepsilon\right\}\right). \)

A solution may also be required to decide whether these quantities are at most a supplied threshold \(\delta\). Under a supplied high-fluidity certificate, the theorem gives \(\delta=1/(\varepsilon\Psi)\). This is again Class A: compute the hitting probabilities and moments by linear algebra, then sum the masses of the violating types. The theorem’s asymptotic conclusion becomes exactly the continuous statement that only a vanishing fraction of society has exceptional influence.

The scope is deliberately limited. These mirrors cover stationary influence, stationary moments, disagreement, and homogeneous influence in a high-multiplicity block-network regime. They do not claim to mirror every graph-specific example, the full stationary law, convergence rates, or arbitrary individually idiosyncratic networks.

The weakest point is that this paper’s original model is more heterogeneous than the mirror. Its finite graph permits every individual to have a unique neighbourhood and interaction rate, whereas the mirror imposes exchangeability within types and scales pairwise contact rates by \(1/N\). In particular, the paper’s coupled-lineage treatment of second moments may require care in the infinite-population limit. The safest positive claim is therefore the first-moment mirror, and the \(\theta=1\) variance mirror; the general cross-product version should be presented as a natural extension requiring a formal propagation-of-chaos argument.

A second weakness is strategic: there is no named hardness result here, so this cannot be used to argue that discrete computational hardness dissolves in the continuum. The honest positive conclusion is narrower but still substantive: the paper supplies a genuine, recognizable high-multiplicity population model whose central influence questions become finite-dimensional polynomial-time computation over type masses.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive point is prior to the modelling details: this paper contains no named computational result to mirror. Theorem 4.1 is an analytic characterization of moments for a fixed finite graph, and Theorem 6.1 is an asymptotic probabilistic estimate. Neither states the complexity of a problem, an optimization task, or an exact, approximation, or parameterized algorithm. Turning any displayed linear system into “given masses, compute its solution” would make every analytic paper eligible for a computational mirror. That is not the programme’s claimed gap.

The proposed mirror of Theorem 4.1 also changes the central object. In the paper, a vertex is identified by its position in the graph: its individual neighbourhood, rates \(r_{vw}\), trust parameters \(\theta_{vw}\), and routes to stubborn vertices matter. For two agents to be one high-multiplicity type, they must be indistinguishable with respect to all of that information. In a general graph, this forces types to encode neighbourhood structure, and the number of types grows with the population. Then \(\mu\) is merely a weighting of essentially individual vertices, not a genuine finite-type continuum.

The proponent’s repair is a block mean-field network with pairwise rates \(\lambda_{uv}/N\). That is a coherent new model, but it is not the high-multiplicity version of the paper’s graph problem except for specially chosen blow-up graphs. It replaces graph geometry by random type-to-type mixing. The authors’ harmonic equations survive only as equations for type means, not as a computational formulation of their original per-agent network problem.

The second-moment part is more seriously mismatched. The paper’s coupled chain \(K\) relies on two lineages meeting the same named agent and then coalescing. Equality of types does not mean equality of agents. In an infinite population, two independently sampled agents of the same type are distinct with probability one, so the diagonal of \(T\times T\) is not the coalescence diagonal of \(\mathcal V\times\mathcal V\). The proposed type-level \(K^\mu\) therefore does not reproduce the paper’s cross-product theorem without a new limit theorem and a different coupling. The proponent’s own concession about propagation of chaos effectively abandons this anchor.

The first-moment version is the negative case’s weak point. A finite block population with exchangeable communication profiles is a plausible high-multiplicity scenario, and its type-level mean \(h_u\) can reasonably be called a new population model. But that concession does not rescue the proposed anchor as a worthwhile computational mirror: the paper itself never poses “compute \(h\)” as a problem. It proves the harmonic characterization analytically. The proposed task is an invented linear-algebra exercise attached to the theorem, not a computational question extracted from the paper.

Theorem 6.1 has an additional mismatch. Its population average is the uniform vertex count \(n^{-1}\sum_v\), while its reference distribution \(\bar\gamma\) is weighted by the auxiliary chain’s stationary distribution \(\pi\). There is no reason for \(\pi\) to equal the population mass vector \(\mu\); in the paper’s canonical graph construction, \(\pi_v\) is proportional to degree. A faithful continuum version would need separate population and exposure measures, say \(\nu\) and \(\pi\), together with the full type-level transition kernel. The proponent’s version silently identifies them.

More fundamentally, the highly fluid regime usually has \(\pi(\mathcal S)\to0\): stubborn agents are few while the population grows. In a mass-weighted mean-field limit, stubborn types with vanishing mass are never contacted, so their influence disappears. Keeping them influential requires either positive stubborn mass, which changes the regime, or zero-mass exogenous sources with singular contact rates, which are no longer ordinary population types. A spatial continuum with boundary sources could be interesting, but that would be a different hydrodynamic or PDE model, not the finite-type computational mirror proposed here.

Thus both anchors fail as computational anchors: Theorem 4.1 supplies an analytic identity, and Theorem 6.1 supplies an analytic concentration bound whose measures and vanishing-source regime do not transfer directly. The honest qualification is that a block mean-field reinterpretation is mathematically defensible. I cannot honestly claim that no researcher could ever find it useful. But under ChoCo’s stated gate—computational questions arising from the paper, with a meaningful high-multiplicity population object—the proponent has not established a worthwhile continuous mirror.
