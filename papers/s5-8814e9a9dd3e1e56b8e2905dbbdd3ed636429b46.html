The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The positive case has not identified a computational result to mirror. Its proposed DAO setting may support a continuous distribution over valuation and belief types, but the paper’s “Efficient” and “SA-proof” claims are mechanism properties at equilibrium, not complexity results. That distinction is central to ChoCo’s scope: a continuous statement about what outcome a mechanism selects is not, by itself, a computational question about an algorithm or its complexity.

The strongest candidate for a mirror is the efficiency claim. For a type distribution \(\mu\), its continuous form is clear: select an alternative maximizing \(\sum_t \mu_t u_{tj}\), with deposits given type by type by equation (8). That is a coherent extension of the mechanism. But it changes the paper’s result into an aggregate-welfare statement; the paper does not pose or analyze a computational problem over such distributions. Calling the formula’s evaluation a complexity mirror would supply a new problem, rather than mirror a computational result in the paper.

The central Sybil claim is a poorer fit. It concerns whether *one player* benefits by splitting participation across accounts, and its proof compares that player’s utility across \(w\) accounts. In an atomless population, an individual has zero mass, so splitting their mass cannot affect the aggregate outcome. The anti-Sybil property then becomes vacuous. Giving a whole positive-mass type the role of one player preserves influence only by turning the type into a coordinated bloc, which changes the coalition-free model. Keeping finite account owners preserves the original Sybil question, but leaves the strategic units atomic.

There is also a scale problem in the paper’s specific mechanism: \(a=(3/2)^h\) depends on the number of participants, and the equilibrium votes and utility gain in equations (8) and (9) scale as \(1/a\). As \(h\) grows without bound, individual effects vanish. Replacing \(h\) with population mass avoids that limit only by removing the count of accounts that drives the Sybil deterrent. A new mass-based deterrence rule might be worth studying, but it would be a redesign, not a continuous mirror of this result.

So the negative case is strong for a ChoCo *computational* mirror of this paper: its natural continuization is a mechanism-design extension, while its signature incentive property depends on individual identity and account counts. It is not an airtight claim that no worthwhile follow-up exists. A continuous typed version of the welfare mechanism is plausible; what it lacks is a clear computational result from this paper for ChoCo to carry over.
