Why no mirror

The proposed distributional merge is a well-posed analogue, but neither anchor states an algorithm, a running-time bound, or hardness for computing the merge. Theorem 4 gives a small-witness characterization, while Lemma 8 bounds the size of an explicit weight-profile representation; the paper therefore fails the required computational-result gate.

What the mirror covers

The proposed question concerns the all-positive-weight Hamming merge over report-formula types, including the witness and profile-size phenomena of Theorem 4 and Lemma 8; it leaves the paper's other distance, postulate, and source-aggregation results aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • What is the complexity of robust-merge membership as a function of the number of report types and the formula encoding?
  • What is the minimum size of a finite reliability-profile set that reproduces the full merge?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a high-volume source population: millions of hospitals, labs, or monitoring services issue logical reports over a shared finite vocabulary, and many issue the same report. A type is the report formula \(F_t\), together with the same reliability uncertainty; its mass \(\pi_t\) is the fraction of sources of that type. There may be \(N\) source units with \(N\gg\tau\), where \(\tau\) is the number of distinct report types.

For integrity constraints \(\kappa\), define \(d_H(I,F_t)\) as the minimum Hamming distance from interpretation \(I\) to a model of \(F_t\). Give each type an unknown positive per-source reliability intensity \(\rho_t\). The collective decision is an interpretation minimizing \(\sum_t \pi_t\rho_t d_H(I,F_t)\) for some possible reliability profile. This is faithful to the paper’s all-weights operator: the aggregate coefficient \(\lambda_t=\pi_t\rho_t\) can be any positive rational, just as the sum of individual source weights can. Repeated sources of one report type therefore collapse exactly to one coefficient. The paper’s input is still a finite source list; the mirror makes the source population a rational distribution over report types.

The lead anchor is Theorem 4, proved in the paper. It says that for any distance \(d\), integrity formula \(\mu\), source formulas \(F_1,\ldots,F_m\), and model \(I\models\mu\), \(I\) is excluded from the all-positive-weights merge exactly when there is a formula \(\mu'\) satisfied by \(I\), with \(\mu'\models\mu\), and with at most \(m+1\) models, such that \(I\) is excluded from the merge under \(\mu'\). In the continuous mirror, after grouping identical reports, this gives the following problem.

Continuous Robust-Merge Membership: Given \(\kappa\), a rational distribution \((\pi_t)_{t\in T}\) over \(\tau\) report types, and a target interpretation \(I\), decide whether
\[ I\in\bigcup_{\rho\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\tau}} \arg\min_{J\models\kappa}\sum_{t\in T}\pi_t\rho_t d_H(J,F_t). \]
If the answer is no, a solution certificate can list at most \(\tau\) other interpretations satisfying \(\kappa\). Their distance comparisons make the inequalities needed for \(I\) to be optimal infeasible for every positive reliability profile. The paper’s bound is \(m+1\) worlds including \(I\); aggregation of duplicate reports gives the \(\tau+1\) version. This is the strongest anchor because it turns a potentially huge population into a support-size bound. [Theorem 4 in the paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.02516)

The second anchor is Lemma 8, also proved in the paper. It states that there are integrity and source formulas for which *every* finite set \(W_r\) of positive Hamming-distance weight vectors that reproduces the all-weights merge has size exponential in the formula size. It motivates a second mirror question.

Minimum Continuous Profile Cover: On the same population and integrity-constraint input, find a smallest finite set \(\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\tau}\) such that the union of the minimum-distance interpretations over \(\lambda\in\Lambda\) equals the full continuous merge. The solution is the profile list \(\Lambda\), and the objective is to minimize \(|\Lambda|\). Lemma 8’s construction lifts to a population with positive rational masses on those report types, and each type can then represent arbitrarily many identical sources. Thus some mirrors require exponentially many profiles even when \(N\) is much larger than \(\tau\). This is an output-size lower bound, not an NP-hardness result. [Lemma 8 in the paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.02516)

I expect the membership problem to follow the hardness-transfers direction: the Boolean interpretations and integrity constraints retain their combinatorics when the source list becomes a population distribution. Theorem 4 also gives a useful derived bound: exclusion has a polynomial-size certificate in \(\tau\) and the interpretation length, checkable with exact linear feasibility, so the membership problem is in coNP under standard formula encodings. The article itself proves no P, NP-hardness, or parameterized-complexity classification, so that upper bound does not establish tractability. For profile cover, the exponential output requirement already persists in the discrete instances and therefore carries over; the paper does not show that finding a minimum cover is NP-hard.

The scope here is the paper’s one-formula-per-source, unknown-positive-reliability Hamming merge. The continuized object is the source population; interpretations remain discrete. Further questions include whether membership is coNP-complete, whether fixed \(\tau\) gives an efficient algorithm, and how the profile-cover optimum depends on \(\tau\) and the number of Boolean variables.

The weak point is that the masses \(\pi_t\) can be absorbed into unrestricted \(\rho_t\), so the merge depends on which types have positive mass, not on their proportions. That makes this a modest continuous mirror: it studies support-size and high-multiplicity structure rather than sensitivity to population shares. The invariance is exact for the paper’s unknown-reliability model, and Theorem 4’s bound in terms of \(\tau\), rather than the raw number of sources, is where the mirror adds its clearest value.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper’s Theorem 4 and Lemma 8 are structural results, not complexity classifications. Theorem 4 bounds how many competing interpretations can witness that a candidate interpretation is excluded. Lemma 8 shows that some merges need exponentially many weight vectors to reproduce the full result. Neither gives a running time or a hardness result for a computational problem. The paper does describe an elimination algorithm, but gives no complexity bound for it. The proposed membership and profile-cover tasks are reasonable questions one could ask about the paper’s operator; they are not, as stated, continuous-population results.

The population mirror has a more basic problem. For the proposed distribution over report types, set \(\lambda_t=\pi_t\rho_t\). Since every \(\pi_t\) is positive, this is a bijection on positive rational profiles, and

\[ \bigcup_{\rho\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\tau}} \arg\min_{J\models\kappa}\sum_t\pi_t\rho_t\,d_H(J,F_t) = \bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\tau}} \arg\min_{J\models\kappa}\sum_t\lambda_t\,d_H(J,F_t). \]

So the merge is constant across all mass distributions with the same support. Theorem 4’s witness bound is just the finite bound with duplicate report types removed. For profile cover, multiplying each profile coordinate by \(\pi_t\) preserves cardinality, so the minimum cover is exactly the finite-profile cover. The paper itself notes that repeating a formula does not change this operator. Inflating the source population with copies of existing reports changes \(N\) and the shares, but changes neither proposed problem. I am not arguing that a complexity result carrying over would be dull; the issue is that these mirrors have no operative population-share variable.

A stronger alternative would constrain the reliability profiles—for example, give each type a bounded reliability range, or model the paper’s \(W_a\) case where one source is highly reliable but its identity is unknown. Then the masses need not cancel. But those are different uncertainty sets from \(W_\exists\), and the cited theorem and cover lower bound do not transfer automatically. In the \(W_a\) case especially, a population limit needs a rule for how the exceptional source’s reliability scales with population size. That could support a separate, worthwhile study, but it does not rescue either anchor as presented.

I cannot honestly defend the literal claim that *no* continuous mirror could be worthwhile: a carefully specified, mass-sensitive reliability model might be. My negative case is strong for the two proposed mirrors. They turn the paper’s structural results into finite-support computational questions, while the continuous shares themselves disappear.
