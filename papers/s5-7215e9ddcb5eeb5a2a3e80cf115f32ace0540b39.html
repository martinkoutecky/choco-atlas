The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make a valid positive case anchored in this paper: it contains no named computational result. Theorem 1, proved in the paper, characterizes the unique \((j,k)\) Public Good value that admits a potential and distributes the worths of minimal critical vectors. It gives
\[ \Psi_i(v)=\sum_{\substack{x\in\operatorname{MCV}(v)\\x_i\ne0}}v(x), \qquad P(v)=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{MCV}(v)}v(x). \]
Theorem 2, also proved here, is an axiomatic uniqueness result for the normalized value. Neither theorem classifies an algorithm or the complexity of a computational problem. So neither is an eligible anchor under your criteria. The paper keeps the player set finite; its “average game” averages over finite input profiles, rather than making the population continuous.

The strongest adjacent candidate I can see is an asymptotic, type-level Public Good value computation. Consider a large weighted electorate with a fixed finite set \(T\) of member types, each type \(t\) having rational population share \(\mu_t\). Members of type \(t\) have the same weight \(a_t\). Fix \(j,k\) and positive ordered thresholds \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_{k-1}\). For each replication factor \(q\) with \(q\mu_t\) integral, create \(q\mu_t\) members of each type and define
\[ v_q(x)=\left|\left\{\ell:\frac{1}{q}\sum_{t\in T}a_t \sum_{i\in N_t}x_i\ge\beta_\ell\right\}\right|, \quad x\in J^{N_q}. \]
For each type, sum the paper’s Theorem 1 value over its members,
\[ A_t(q)=\sum_{i\in N_t}\Psi_i(v_q), \]
and normalize to \(\theta_t(q)=A_t(q)/\sum_s A_s(q)\). The continuous question would be: given \((\mu_t)_t\), the weights, and the thresholds, compute \(\lim_{q\to\infty}\theta(q)\), or certify that the limit does not exist. The regime is a large organization with many members but only a few voting classes. The answer would be a vector of type-level influence shares. For fixed \(|T|,j,k\), I would tentatively hope for a tractable entropy or convex-optimization characterization.

That is a plausible high-multiplicity extension of the index, but it is not a mirror of a computational result in this paper. Its central risk is substantive: Theorem 1 counts minimal critical *individual vectors*. Passing to mass profiles loses that counting information unless the limit retains the appropriate multiplicities, likely through an entropy term. Deriving and computing that limit would be new work beyond the paper’s axiomatic results. Thus this is the best nearby positive direction I can offer, but there is no qualifying anchor—and no honest ChoCo complexity verdict to attach to it.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that the paper has no named computational result to mirror. Theorem 1 characterizes a value by a potential and a distribution axiom; Theorem 2 characterizes a normalized value by axioms. Neither studies the complexity of computing the value or solves another computational problem. The paper’s computer science conference example motivates measuring influence, but the paper’s contribution is axiomatic. So there is no eligible anchor here.

The proposed high multiplicity question is the strongest nearby direction: take a large weighted electorate with a fixed set of member types, sum each type’s Public Good value, and ask for the limiting normalized shares \(\theta_t(q)\). But that is not a continuization of a computational result in this paper. It selects a weighted threshold family, adds a replication regime, aggregates individual outputs by type, and asks for an asymptotic limit. It could be a sensible new problem; the authors’ interest in weighted voting makes it plausible they would recognize the setting. That plausibility does not make it a mirror of one of their results.

There is also a real obstacle to carrying the index itself over directly. The paper counts discrete minimal critical vectors. In a continuum model, a threshold rule can have a whole boundary of minimal winning mass profiles, with no canonical way to count them; meanwhile, removing one individual has zero effect on the population measure. One can repair this by defining the answer as a limit of finite populations, as the proposed question does. But then the result depends on asymptotic counting of discrete coalitions—the entropy of the finite models—not on a direct continuous version of the paper’s index. That repair may produce worthwhile research, but it is an additional computational project.

So the negative case is strong for the narrow verdict: this paper supplies no computational anchor, and its most plausible high multiplicity extension is adjacent new work rather than a mirror. The broader claim that no scenario could yield a worthwhile continuous question is much harder to defend. A large organization with repeated member types is a credible setting, and a type-level influence limit could be useful. I would not claim the universal negative is established; the honest finding is that this paper itself gives no qualifying mirror.
