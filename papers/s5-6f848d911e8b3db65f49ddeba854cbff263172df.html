The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest plausible mirror is the paper’s coalition load-optimization problem. A type would be a complete apartment specification: thermal parameters, AC power, temperature preferences, and comfort period. The mass \(\mu_t\) of type \(t\) is its fraction of a large population. For each type, let \(Z\in\{0,1\}^k\) be a feasible cooling schedule that satisfies the type’s temperature constraints and periodic boundary conditions. Choose masses \(x_{t,Z}\ge 0\) assigned to those schedules, with \(\sum_Z x_{t,Z}=\mu_t\). Minimize per-capita electricity use, \(\sum_{t,Z}x_{t,Z}P_t\Delta\sum_j Z_j\), subject to the aggregate load staying below the per-capita threshold at every time. This keeps each apartment’s cooling actions integral; only the distribution of schedules across many same-type apartments is fractional. The natural parameters are the time horizon \(k\), support size \(\tau\), and input bit length \(L\).

That is a credible high-multiplicity regime: for example, a large apartment block with many households sharing a small set of thermal and comfort profiles. The formulation mirrors the underlying coordination decision in Eq. (8), rather than making fractional cooling inside an individual apartment the question. Its complexity would hinge on whether pricing over feasible schedules is efficient.

The proposed schedule-distribution model is still a credible continuous-population question. Large buildings or portfolios could have many apartments drawn from a small set of complete thermal and comfort profiles. Keeping each apartment’s schedule integral, while assigning mass across schedules and scaling the load cap with population, is a sensible high-multiplicity formulation. It targets the paper’s coalition-optimization subproblem, and the authors would likely recognize it. I cannot defeat that formulation on the grounds that its types are implausible or that the population disappears in the limit.
