What the mirror covers

The proposed candidate concerns aggregate NTU identification through moment inequalities; it leaves the fixed-individual model, the TU analysis, and confidence-set inference outside its scope.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is the paper’s aggregate NTU matching model. Its empirical setting is already a convincing high-multiplicity regime: many marriages grouped into a small number of age categories, with state-level matching tables. A type would be a man or woman category containing all characteristics relevant to preferences and matching; its mass would be its fraction of the market. The paper’s seven age groups per side illustrate how a large population can have small type support. In this mirror, each observed integer matching table becomes a fractional matching flow \(\pi\), while preferences, stability restrictions, and the identified-set objective retain their meaning.

There is a qualification about the anchors. The paper contains no named computational result of the required kind: no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a complexity classification. The closest named result is Claim 1, proved in Appendix B. It says that in the NTU model, individual-level preference shocks make the aggregate stability restrictions trivially satisfied. The appendix derives an aggregate inequality and shows it reduces to a vacuous bound. That is a useful result about identification, but it does not classify an algorithmic problem, so I would not present it as a ChoCo complexity anchor. The aggregate stability and moment inequalities in Sections 3.1 and 3.3 are the paper’s best material for a proposed mirror, but they are equations, not named computational results.

A concrete follow-on question would be Continuous Aggregate NTU Identification. Given finitely many observed markets \(r=1,\ldots,s\), each instance contains rational male and female type masses \(\mu^r,\nu^r\), and a rational matching flow \(\pi^r\) with those type masses as its row and column sums. Each flow entry is a fraction of the market. The input also gives market weights \(\lambda_r\), the paper’s four age-preference parameters \(\beta\in[-2,2]^4\), the utility specification and shock distribution, and the communication scale \(\gamma\). For distinct male types \(i,k\) and female types \(j,l\), let \(\psi_{ijkl}(\beta)\) be the paper’s probability that the pairwise NTU blocking conditions fail. Set \(\delta^r_{ijkl}=\min\{2\gamma\pi^r_{ij}\pi^r_{kl},1\}\), the continuous counterpart of the paper’s relative-mass communication probability. The solution is the identified set
\(\mathcal B_\infty=\{\beta\in[-2,2]^4:\sum_r\lambda_r\mathbf 1[\pi^r_{ij}>0,\pi^r_{kl}>0]\delta^r_{ijkl}\le\psi_{ijkl}(\beta)\ \text{for every }i\ne k,\ j\ne l\}\).
One can ask whether this set is empty and, for a specified direction \(a\), compute the extrema of \(a^\top\beta\) over it. The outer distribution over observed markets remains finite; it is the population within each market that has become continuous.

This is recognizable as the paper’s own partial-identification question: its NTU anti-edge restrictions and communication adjustment remain in place, while matching counts become masses. It covers the aggregate NTU analysis, not the paper’s TU model or its confidence-set inference. For a fixed four-dimensional parameter space, I would expect additive approximate optimization to be tractable with suitable regularity and probability-evaluation assumptions. I would leave the exact complexity of the identified-set problem open. Under the individual-level-shock version of Claim 1, the restrictions become vacuous, so that subcase is trivial; the paper’s mass-dependent communication model is the more interesting target.

The weakest point is also the central one: this is a proposed computational extension, not an algorithmic result the authors establish. The paper already aggregates agents into types and uses normalized population frequencies in its communication model, so the proposed mirror changes the mathematical domain less than it would for a paper about named individuals. That makes the scenario unusually plausible, but it also means the mirror could look like a natural reformulation of their econometric problem unless the computational question is stated and studied explicitly.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper’s only named result is Claim 1, and it points toward a weak mirror: with individual-level preference shocks, the aggregate NTU stability restrictions become trivial. Carrying that result into a continuum would preserve the vacuity, not yield a computational problem. I would not object that the authors already aggregate agents into age types; under ChoCo’s standard, that is evidence that a high-multiplicity regime makes sense, not a novelty collision. The difficulty is that their paper offers no named computational result to anchor.

The proponent’s Continuous Aggregate NTU Identification is the best candidate anyway. But its proposed inequalities do not yet give a well-founded continuous-market mirror. They treat \(1[\pi_{ij}>0,\pi_{kl}>0]\) as an anti-edge event and compare it with \(\psi_{ijkl}(\beta)\), the probability that a sampled pair satisfies the no-blocking conditions. In a continuum with homogeneous preferences by type, blocking is instead a deterministic condition on the support of the matching flow: if two positive-mass cells create a mutually preferred cross-match, a positive amount of mass can block. A probability that a sampled pair avoids blocking does not express that requirement. With individual-level shocks, the problem changes in the other direction: shocks must be represented in the agents’ types or in a distribution of types, and the paper’s Claim 1 warns that aggregate NTU restrictions then become trivial. Averaging matching tables across finitely many markets does not settle the distinction; that averages over markets, not over the population inside each market.

One could formulate a continuous population with type-level masses and deterministic rankings, then ask about stability or parameter identification. That would be a coherent new model, but it would no longer be the proposed stochastic moment-inequality problem. Conversely, retaining the paper’s inequality estimator and replacing counts with normalized flows leaves the main task as estimating a four-parameter identified set from finitely many moment inequalities. With \(K\) male types and \(L\) female types, it has \(O(K^2L^2)\) constraints and no exponential assignment or permutation space. It may pose a numerical optimization problem, especially if one studies more general specifications, but its computational content comes from nonlinear econometric set estimation; making the tables fractional does not itself expose a new population-scale algorithmic problem.

The TU section does not supply a stronger escape. A continuous matching flow with fixed type supplies and given surpluses is a standard assignment or transportation optimization problem. The paper’s own pairwise inequalities identify only a scalar combination of its parameters. One could study that identified set computationally, but again the paper states no complexity result, and converting integer counts to masses does not create the central algorithmic structure. A continuous age-type space could generate a richer follow-on problem, but that would require choosing a new type distribution and studying a new infinite-type identification model. It is conceivable work; it is not a mirror secured by this paper’s results.

So the negative case is strongest against the proposed anchor, not against every imaginable matching model. A continuous population matching project could be worthwhile. What I cannot honestly claim is that this paper establishes one: its named result collapses under the natural shock-based extension, and its strongest alternative converts an already aggregate econometric analysis into a low-dimensional mass-valued estimation problem. The universal “no scenario” claim remains vulnerable to a deliberately richer continuous-type model, but that would be a new research question rather than a computational mirror of this paper.
