The continuous mirror question

Given a total seller mass \(N\), budget \(B\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) truthful types \((v_t,c_t)\), does there exist a deterministic direct mechanism, evaluable in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the input bit length, that assigns each infinitesimal seller with public value \(v\), true cost \(c\), and report \(b\) an allocation \(x(v,b;\nu)\in[0,1]\) and payment \(p(v,b;\nu)\), where \(\nu\) is the fixed distribution of other sellers’ reports? Require dominant-strategy truthfulness and individual rationality for each seller, aggregate payments at most \(B\) for every report profile, and truthful aggregate value at least half of \(\max\{N\sum_t\mu_t v_t x_t: N\sum_t\mu_t c_t x_t\le B,\ x_t\in[0,1]\}\).

The model it lives in

An atomless population of total mass \(N\) has normalized type distribution \(\mu\) over public values and private costs, with \(N\mu_t\) mass of type \(t\). Each individual seller reports a cost and receives a service fraction \(x\) and payment \(p\); the mechanism uses that report and the aggregate report distribution. The objective is to maximize total value subject to aggregate payments within budget \(B\), with pointwise dominant-strategy truthfulness and individual rationality.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I see is Theorem 4.2, proved in this paper. It says that for divisible agents with linear valuations, under the assumption that the buyer can afford each agent’s full service, PRUNE-&-ASSIGN is a deterministic mechanism that is truthful, individually rational, budget-feasible, and a \(2\)-approximation to the fractional knapsack optimum; it runs in time polynomial in the number of agents \(n\). The proof is given here through Lemmas 4.4–4.6. This is an algorithmic anchor, not a hardness result. The paper’s statement that the integral \(k\)-bounded knapsack problem is NP-hard is not a numbered theorem, and the cited approximation lower bounds are results from other papers.

Here is a population version that keeps the mechanism-design question intact. Consider a large crowdsourcing platform procuring standardized units of service, such as shifts or delivery capacity. Let \(N\) be the population scale, and let \(\mu\) be a rational distribution over \(\tau\) complete seller types \(t=(v_t,c_t)\), where \(v_t\) is the public value of a full service unit and \(c_t\) is its true private cost. There is an atomless mass \(N\mu_t\) of sellers of type \(t\), each able to provide a fraction in \([0,1]\) of one unit. Assume \(c_t\le B\) for every type, as in the paper’s all-in setting. Such a regime is plausible when the market has many sellers but only a modest number of standardized service, quality, and cost categories; \(N\) can be much larger than \(\tau\).

The Atomless Linear Procurement question is: given \(N,\mu,B\) and the public values and reported costs, compute a deterministic direct mechanism in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the input bit length. The mechanism must give each individual a service fraction \(x\in[0,1]\) and payment \(p\), with measurable allocation and payment rules based on that individual’s report and the aggregate report distribution. For every fixed distribution of other sellers’ reports, it must satisfy dominant-strategy truthfulness,
\[ p_t(c;\nu)-c\,x_t(c;\nu)\ \ge\ p_t(b;\nu)-c\,x_t(b;\nu) \]
for every true cost \(c\) and report \(b\), as well as individual rationality and aggregate budget-feasibility. At truthful reports, its aggregate value must be at least half of
\[ \mathrm{OPT}(\mu)= \max_{x_t\in[0,1]} \left\{ N\sum_t\mu_t v_t x_t: N\sum_t\mu_t c_t x_t\le B \right\}. \]
A solution is the mechanism, including its allocation and payment rules, with proofs of these properties and a polynomial-time evaluation or synthesis procedure. This targets the paper’s underlying procurement problem, rather than treating a formulation as the result.

I expect this mirror to be tractable, in the sense of a Class A result. The benchmark is a fractional knapsack over \(\tau\) type masses, and the paper’s mechanism obtains its guarantee from density ordering, pruning, and threshold payments. Those ingredients give a credible route to calculations over types and masses rather than over all \(N\) individuals. The theorem does not itself establish that compression: its running time is polynomial in \(n\), and a proof for the atomless model would be new. Nor is this a claim that hardness dissolves; the anchor gives a positive approximation result, not a hardness boundary.

This mirrors only Theorem 4.2’s linear-valuation divisible-agent setting and its all-in affordability assumption. It does not claim to cover the paper’s multi-level model or its general concave-valuation results. Fractional service is already in the source model; the continuization here is the seller population, with the fractional allocation retained as a necessary feature of the problem.

The weak point is the strategic limit. A continuum has no pivotal individual, while PRUNE-&-ASSIGN is proved for a finite list of agents and singles out a highest-value seller. The mirror therefore needs a pointwise individual truthfulness definition and a fresh argument that aggregate pruning and payment thresholds work for mass distributions. Treating each type cohort as one strategic bidder would make the mathematics simpler, but would change the seller incentives and would be a weaker mirror. The question above keeps individual sellers strategic, which is why I think it remains recognizable as the authors’ problem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is the strategic jump from a finite list of sellers to a distribution, but it does not defeat Theorem 4.2 as a mirror.

PRUNE-&-ASSIGN is a finite-agent mechanism. Its truthfulness proof uses each seller’s own bid and allocation curve; its budget proof sums individual threshold payments. If the continuous input is only a distribution \(\nu\) of reports, one seller’s deviation has zero mass and leaves \(\nu\) unchanged. Truthfulness then has no content unless the mechanism also specifies that seller’s allocation and payment from their individual report. The proposed \(x_t(c;\nu)\) tries to supply that missing piece. It also leaves a modeling choice: if \(t\) records true cost, the mechanism cannot observe the input; if \(\nu\) records reported costs, individual deviations are invisible to it.

A stronger mirror can address this by defining a report-dependent schedule for each seller against a fixed aggregate report distribution, and scaling the budget with population size. That gives a coherent large-market version of the paper’s mechanism-design question. It is plausible for crowdsourcing or standardized service procurement, and it preserves the linear-value divisible-agent problem. Neither “there are no repeated types” nor “the paper already allows fractional service” defeats it.

What remains is a real new obligation: show that the finite mechanism’s individual incentive and payment arguments yield a measurable, aggregate budget-feasible mechanism on the proposed population model. The paper does not prove that result. But I can’t make the objection stronger without treating the need for that proof as a reason not to ask the question. Theorem 4.2 is a named computational result, and a mass-based procurement setting is credible. The negative case against this anchor is therefore weak; I cannot honestly conclude that no worthwhile continuous mirror survives.
