The continuous mirror question

Given \(\tau\) additive valuation types with rational shares \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), population scale \(N\), and \(m\) goods categories with integral supplies \(b_g\) scaling with \(N\), does every instance admit a sparse rational allocation \(x_{t,z}\ge 0\) of mass \(N\mu_t\) of each type to integral bundles \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^m\), using at most \(b_g\) units of each category, such that whenever \(x_{t,z},x_{s,z'}>0\), \(v_t(z)\ge(1-\varepsilon)v_t(z'-e_g)\) for every \(g\) with \(z'_g>0\), and at most \(O((N/\varepsilon)^{0.67})\) goods are unused? Can such an allocation be found in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\), where \(L\) is the input bit length?

The model it lives in

A society is a distribution \(\mu\) over complete additive valuation types. Variables \(x_{t,z}\) assign mass of each type to integral bundles; supplies scale per capita, and every bundle type in the allocation's support must satisfy the scaled EFX comparisons.

What the mirror covers

Covers the approximate-EFX guarantee in Corollary 1.2 through the paper's constructive bound on \(R_f(d)\); it leaves the Ramsey bounds as graph results, the permutation and group-label results, and exact EFX outside the mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the support-wide EFX constraints be handled in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\) without expanding the population?
  • How much reserve is needed to round a continuous allocation back to integral recipient counts and supplies?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest anchor is Corollary 1.2, proved here: for every \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]\), there is an efficiently computable \((1-\varepsilon)\)-EFX allocation for \(n\) agents that leaves \(O((n/\varepsilon)^{0.67})\) goods unallocated. The paper gets this from its constructive bound on \(R_f(d)\) and Theorem 1.1, which it attributes to Chaudhury, Garg, Mehlhorn, Mehta, and Misra: a constructive \(R_f(d)\in O(d^c)\) bound gives a polynomial-time \((1-\varepsilon)\)-EFX allocation leaving \(O((n/\varepsilon)^{c/(1+c)})\) goods unallocated, for constant \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]\). The supplied paper contains no named hardness result, so this algorithmic result is the appropriate lead.

A plausible high-multiplicity regime is a large public programme distributing repeated categories of household goods to recipients with standardized valuation profiles. There might be hundreds of thousands of households but only a few dozen distinct profiles. A type is a complete valuation profile; its mass is the number, or fraction, of recipients with that profile. This is a credible instance regime for EFX: recipients of a type are interchangeable for the problem, while the goods remain indivisible for each recipient.

I would hand a prover HM-EFX-Reserve. The input has \(\tau\) agent types with rational shares \(\mu_t>0\), \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), a population scale \(N\), and \(m\) categories of goods with supplies \(b_g\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). In the regime of interest, each supply grows with \(N\), so \(b_g/N\) is the per-capita supply. For concreteness, let each type \(t\) have additive values \(a_{tg}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), with \(v_t(z)=\sum_g a_{tg}z_g\) for an integral bundle \(z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\).

A solution is a finite list of rational masses \(x_{t,z}\ge0\): mass \(x_{t,z}\) of type \(t\) receives the integral bundle \(z\). The type and supply constraints are
\[ \sum_z x_{t,z}=N\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t,z}z_gx_{t,z}\le b_g\quad\text{for every }g. \]
For every two bundle types \((t,z)\), \((s,z')\) assigned positive mass, require
\[ v_t(z)\ge(1-\varepsilon)v_t(z'-e_g) \quad\text{for every }g\text{ with }z'_g>0. \]
Thus every unit of mass gets an integral bundle and satisfies the scaled EFX comparison. The unused supply is
\[ U(x)=\sum_g\left(b_g-\sum_{t,z}z_gx_{t,z}\right). \]
Given a reserve budget \(K\), HM-EFX-Reserve asks for such an allocation with \(U(x)\le K\). The continuous analogue of Corollary 1.2 asks whether one can always find one with \(K=O((N/\varepsilon)^{0.67})\), using time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,1/\varepsilon\), where \(L\) is the input bit length and the output is a sparse list of assigned bundles. Unused supply can be fractional in this aggregate accounting because a fractional mass of identical agents may receive each bundle; the bundle each receives is still integral.

This mirrors the allocation guarantee directly. It continuizes the population represented by \(n\) while retaining the EFX question and allowing supplies to scale per capita. It does not aim to continuize goods into fractional bundles. The scope I am claiming is Corollary 1.2’s approximate-EFX guarantee, rather than the paper’s Ramsey bounds as standalone graph results, its group-label corollary, or exact EFX.

My tentative expectation is Class A for this aggregate problem: masses assigned to type-and-bundle columns give linear type and supply constraints, and a continuous optimization approach may avoid expanding the \(N\) recipients. The decisive open step is pricing or selecting bundle columns while enforcing EFX across every bundle in the allocation’s support. Those support-dependent comparisons are not automatically an LP, so this is a tractability conjecture, not a consequence of Corollary 1.2.

The mirror also raises a rounding question: when the type shares and supplies come from a finite high-multiplicity instance, how closely can a continuous allocation be converted back to integral recipient counts without increasing the reserve by much? Further questions include whether the \(O(N^{0.67})\) reserve can be improved in the continuous setting, and whether the required bundle pricing is polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\).

The weakest point is that Corollary 1.2 gives an efficient construction in the discrete setting; it does not establish a running time polynomial in the compressed high-multiplicity input. The paper’s constructive argument works with finite combinatorial structures, and the continuous EFX support constraints may retain substantial difficulty. The proposed mirror remains plausible because the agents themselves are the population being continuized, and identical valuation profiles make the type aggregation exact.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is against the paper’s central Ramsey results. In \(R_f(d)\), \(R_p(d)\), and \(R_i(d)\), the \(n\) vertices are linked by independently chosen edge labels. A vertex’s “type” would have to encode its relations to the other \(n-1\) vertices, so the type space grows with the graph and may distinguish every vertex. Replacing vertices with masses would lose those pairwise labels; retaining them would continuize the graph structure as well as its population. That is a poor fit for ChoCo’s population mirror.

Corollary 1.2 is a much stronger anchor. A credible setting is a large programme distributing goods from a fixed catalogue to households with a small number of standardized valuation profiles. Scaling inventory with the population and assigning each unit of mass an integral bundle gives a coherent high-multiplicity EFX problem. The paper’s bound then suggests a concrete question about the unallocated reserve as population mass \(N\) grows. Neither the graph formulation nor the fact that goods remain indivisible defeats that mirror.

The best objection is that EFX’s pairwise conditions apply to every bundle type in the allocation’s support. Even an arbitrarily small positive mass assigned a bundle can impose envy constraints on the whole allocation; removing that mass can change feasibility discontinuously. The proposed mass variables therefore do not turn the problem into a routine LP: one must choose a mutually compatible support of integral bundles as well as assign masses to it. This may leave the main combinatorial difficulty untouched.

But that is a warning about tractability, not a reason the question is unworthy. The model keeps the population continuous, preserves integral bundles, and asks about the same approximate-EFX guarantee. The resulting complexity landscape could be Class A or Class C; either answer would matter to the programme. So I cannot honestly defeat this anchor. The negative case is strong for the paper’s graph-theoretic core, but weak against its EFX corollary and does not support the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
