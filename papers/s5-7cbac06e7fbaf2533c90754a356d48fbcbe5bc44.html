The continuous mirror question

Given \(\delta<1/2\) and a finite-support prediction distribution \(\nu\) on rational locations with rational masses, compute a location \(y\) from \(\nu\) and \(\delta\) such that, for every finite-support joint distribution \(\pi\) over true and predicted locations \((x,z)\) whose \(z\)-marginal is \(\nu\) and whose mass on \(x\ne z\) is at most \(\delta\), \(\int |x-y|\,d\mu(x)\le\left(1+\frac{4\delta}{1-2\delta}\right)\min_{w\in\mathbb{R}}\int |x-w|\,d\mu(x)\), where \(\mu\) is the \(x\)-marginal of \(\pi\).

The model it lives in

A society is a distribution over complete types \((x,z)\), pairing each resident's true location with its predicted location. The observed prediction marginal is \(\nu\); at most \(\delta\) population mass has \(x\ne z\). Choose a facility location \(y\) using \(\nu\), and measure its cost by the true population's integral distance to \(y\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the 1-median robustness result in Corollary 4, supported by Theorem 3 and Lemma 2. It leaves the paper's strategyproof mechanism guarantees, including the two-facility results, outside its coverage.

Open questions for a prover

  • Is the factor \(1+\frac{4\delta}{1-2\delta}\) minimax-optimal among rules that observe only \(\nu\)?
  • How does the guarantee change when up to \(\delta\) mass may have prediction error greater than a positive \(\varepsilon\)?
  • What weighted robustness guarantees are possible for multiple facilities under balanced-cluster assumptions?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I believe is the paper’s robust 1-median result. It is a narrow case: the paper’s named results give approximation and mechanism guarantees, not a classification such as NP-hard or in P. Corollary 4, proved here from Lemma 2 and Theorem 3, says that for \(\delta<1/2\), the 1-median of a dataset with at most a \(\delta\)-fraction of points changed has cost on the original dataset at most \(1+\frac{4\delta}{1-2\delta}\) times the original optimum.

Here is a continuous-population version I would hand to a prover: Weighted-MAC 1-Median Robust Prediction. The instance gives \(\delta<1/2\) and a finite-support prediction distribution \(\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}q_j\delta_{z_j}\) on the line, with rational masses summing to one. The unknown population has a finite-support distribution \(\mu\), and there is a coupling of \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) under which at most \(\delta\) mass has \(x\ne z\). The task is to return a location \(y\), using only \(\nu\) and \(\delta\), such that for every population and coupling satisfying that promise,
\[ \int |x-y|\,d\mu(x) \le \left(1+\frac{4\delta}{1-2\delta}\right) \min_{w\in\mathbb R}\int |x-w|\,d\mu(x). \]
A weighted median of \(\nu\) is a solution. On the line it can be found in \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) comparisons, with bit complexity polynomial in the input length \(L\). The expected direction is tractability; the paper’s robustness argument extends from counts to rational masses.

The corresponding regime could be a large city population represented by a much smaller set of resident cohorts: for example, residents sharing a building or catchment location and the same predicted location. A type is the complete pair \((x,z)\) of true and predicted locations; its mass is its fraction of the population. The prediction error promise limits the mass of mismatched pairs, with no independence assumption. This is the paper’s robust-estimation subproblem, before the strategic-report layer.

Further questions include whether that factor is minimax-optimal over all rules using only the prediction distribution, how an \(\varepsilon\)-error allowance changes the bound, and what analogous guarantees hold for multiple facilities. The paper’s multi-facility results suggest those questions will need more structure.

My weakest point is scope: this mirrors Corollary 4, which the paper presents as an independently interesting robustness result; it does not mirror the headline strategyproof mechanisms. In an atomless population, an individual’s report has zero effect on the outcome, so ordinary individual strategyproofness becomes vacuous. Recovering the mechanism-design contribution would require a careful finite-population or cohort-deviation model, and I would not claim this mirror supplies one.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that Corollary 4 is a robustness bound for an estimator, not a complexity classification or a mechanism-design result. Its proposed mirror asks for a location computed from a finite-support prediction distribution; sorting its \(\tau\) support points gives a weighted median in \(O(\tau\log\tau)\) time. The paper’s proof carries over by replacing counts with masses. For rational masses, scaling by a common denominator even recovers a finite multiset instance. That makes this a very direct weighted restatement, with little of the pricing or complexity landscape that motivates ChoCo.

A mechanism-design version faces a deeper modeling choice. In an atomless population, one person’s report cannot change the outcome, so individual strategyproofness becomes vacuous. Making deviations meaningful by allowing whole cohorts to misreport changes the strategic model. The prediction-only median question avoids that problem, but it leaves behind the paper’s strategic contribution.

That is a real case for treating the mirror as modest in scope. It does not defeat the anchor. The cohort scenario is credible, mass is the natural unit for outliers, and the weighted robust-estimation question is well-posed. The fact that its answer follows by a short weighted proof does not make the question invalid under the programme’s stated standard; nor does the atomless mechanism issue apply to the estimator result. I can’t honestly make the case that no worthwhile mirror survives. The strongest defensible negative judgment is that this is a narrow, low-complexity Class A mirror of the robustness theorem, rather than a continuization of the paper’s main mechanism-design result.
