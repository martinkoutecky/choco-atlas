The continuous mirror question

Given \(L\) tiers, rational per-capita supplies \(\bar q_1,\ldots,\bar q_L\), and \(\tau\) types \(\theta\) with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) summing to \(1\), each type specified by a minimum acceptable tier \(\ell_\theta\) and a nonincreasing marginal-value schedule \(u_\theta(1),\ldots,u_\theta(K)\), find a welfare-maximizing allocation. Let \(y_{\theta,k}\) be the mass of type \(\theta\) receiving exactly \(k\) units, where each individual receives an integral bundle. Require \(\sum_{k=0}^{K}y_{\theta,k}=\mu_\theta\) and, for every threshold \(t\), \(\sum_{\theta:\ell_\theta\ge t}\sum_{k=0}^{K} k y_{\theta,k}\le \sum_{s=t}^{L}\bar q_s\); maximize \(\sum_\theta\sum_{k=0}^{K}y_{\theta,k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_\theta(j)\).

The model it lives in

A rational LP over type masses assigned to integral bundle sizes: \(y_{\theta,k}\) records how much of type \(\theta\) receives \(k\) units, with per-capita nested supply constraints and aggregate welfare as the objective.

What the mirror covers

Covers the welfare allocation problem of Theorem 3 over a distribution of buyer types. It does not carry over the tiered clinching auction, its VCG payments, or its incentive guarantees.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest case is a narrow mirror of the paper’s welfare-allocation problem, rather than of its entire ascending auction.

My lead anchor is Theorem 3, proved in the paper. Given tier supplies \(q_1,\ldots,q_L\) and bidders with minimum acceptable tiers and nonincreasing marginal values, the paper’s greedy algorithm processes tiers from low to high. At tier \(t\), it assigns the \(q_t\) items to the highest remaining marginal values among bidders who accept that tier, then shifts those bidders’ value sequences to account for their allocations. The theorem proves that this produces an allocation with maximum social welfare. It is an exact algorithmic result, though the theorem itself does not state a complexity class; the paper’s greedy algorithm runs in polynomial time when the marginal-value lists are explicit. The cited NP-hardness of unrestricted winner determination is background, not a hardness result for this tiered problem.

A plausible high-multiplicity setting is an offset marketplace serving many independently choosing branches, facilities, or municipal buyers that use a modest number of procurement templates. A type records a buyer’s minimum acceptable quality tier and its full decreasing marginal-value schedule. Buyers sharing a type are alike in every feature relevant to the welfare calculation. In a market with \(n\) buyers, the support might be a few dozen or hundred types, with \(n\) much larger. Tier supply scales with the market: if there are \(q_t\) units of tier \(t\), the normalized supply is \(\bar q_t=q_t/n\).

Call the mirror Continuous Tiered Welfare Allocation. An instance gives \(L\) tiers; a finite set of \(\tau\) types \(\theta\), with rational masses \(\mu_\theta\geq 0\) summing to \(1\); each type’s minimum acceptable tier \(\ell_\theta\) and nonincreasing marginal-value list \(u_\theta(1),\ldots,u_\theta(K)\); and rational per-capita supplies \(\bar q_1,\ldots,\bar q_L\). Here \(K\) is the maximum relevant bundle size for one market round. The question is to maximize welfare and return an allocation. Let \(y_{\theta,k}\) be the mass of type \(\theta\) receiving exactly \(k\) units, where each individual receives an integer number of units. Then \(\sum_{k=0}^{K}y_{\theta,k}=\mu_\theta\), and feasibility requires, for every tier threshold \(t\), \(\sum_{\theta:\ell_\theta\geq t}\sum_{k=0}^{K} k y_{\theta,k}\leq\sum_{s=t}^{L}\bar q_s\). These nested constraints ensure that the assigned units can be matched to tiers each recipient accepts. The objective is to maximize \(\sum_{\theta}\sum_{k=0}^{K}y_{\theta,k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}u_\theta(j)\). Unused supply can be left over or assigned to a zero-value dummy buyer.

This is a population continuization: only the mass of a type may be split among bundle sizes; a person’s bundle remains integral. The formulation is a rational LP with \(\tau(K+1)\) variables, so it is solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau,L,K\), and the input bit length. I would expect this mirror to be Class A, tractable. A finite market embeds with \(\mu_\theta=n_\theta/n\) and \(\bar q_t=q_t/n\); the continuous version permits mass splits beyond the finite market’s grid and thus gives its high-multiplicity relaxation.

The authors should recognize the question: it preserves the tier cutoffs, diminishing marginal values, nested supply constraints, and welfare objective of their direct-revelation allocation problem. It also targets a result the paper proves explicitly, rather than treating fractional outcomes or an auction clock as the continuization.

The weakest point is that the paper’s main contribution is the clinching mechanism and its incentive properties, while this mirror covers the welfare computation behind it. The paper also assumes distinct marginal values; repeated complete types create ties, so an exact mirror needs a tie-aware version of Theorem 3’s greedy argument. That seems manageable for welfare, but it does not transfer the paper’s payment or incentive results: in an atomless population, a single buyer has no effect on the allocation, so the usual individual externality can vanish. I would therefore leave Theorems 13–14 outside the claimed scope and treat an aggregate clinching rule, tie handling, and meaningful payments as further questions.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I do not think a strong negative case defeats the proponent’s anchor, Theorem 3. It is a named algorithmic result: the greedy allocation computes an optimal welfare allocation. The paper does not give a numbered complexity theorem, but that alone does not erase the result the proponent chose to mirror.

The best objection is that this mirror retains the planner’s welfare calculation while leaving out the paper’s main contribution: a dynamic auction with individual clinching, VCG payments, and incentive guarantees. In an atomless population, one buyer’s report has no effect on aggregate demand, so the paper’s individual externality and incentive arguments do not carry over directly. The mirror is therefore narrower than the paper’s headline result.

That objection does not defeat the welfare mirror. Repeated facilities, branches, or municipal buyers with the same procurement template give a plausible high-multiplicity regime. A type can include its tier cutoff and full marginal-value schedule; supplies can scale per capita; and mass can be split across integral bundle sizes. The resulting question preserves the paper’s nested feasibility constraints and welfare objective. It is a population continuization, not merely fractional goods or lottery outcomes.

The stronger version of the mirror also survives the obvious modeling objections: tie handling can accommodate repeated types, and rational masses and supplies give a well-defined aggregate allocation problem. The LP formulation appears coherent. Calling it routine, or pointing out that the discrete greedy algorithm is already polynomial, would not establish that the question is out of scope; under the programme’s stated standard, a tractable or unsurprising answer can still be worth obtaining.

So the negative case is strongest against carrying over Theorems 13–14 and the auction mechanism as a whole. It does not rule out a worthwhile continuous mirror of Theorem 3. I cannot honestly defend the requested universal “no scenario” conclusion on this paper.
