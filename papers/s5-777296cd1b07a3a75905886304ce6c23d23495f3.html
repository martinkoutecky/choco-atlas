The case FOR (proponent)

The paper has no eligible named computational anchor: it contains no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a complexity result. Its findings are experimental, including the regressions in Table 8. Section 3 does describe the CVCM computer selecting an allocation that maximizes trading surplus, but it gives no numbered result about that computation’s complexity. I can’t honestly attach a per-anchor mirror to a result that isn’t there.

The strongest positive foothold is an unanchored extension of the CVCM clearing step. Call it *Continuous Land Consolidation Clearing*. An instance has parcel classes \(P\) with specified spatial relationships and rational supplies \(q_p\); a finite support \(T\) of household types with rational masses \(\mu_t\); and, for each type, its initial endowment and a complete package-bid schedule incorporating the paper’s fragmentation costs. Let \(b_t(S)\) be the declared surplus for type \(t\) to finish with bundle \(S\). Choose \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\), the mass of type \(t\) assigned the integral bundle \(S\), to maximize

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq P} x_{t,S}b_t(S) \]

subject to \(\sum_{S\subseteq P}x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) for each \(t\), and \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S:p\in S}x_{t,S}=q_p\) for each parcel class \(p\). A solution is a feasible rational allocation attaining the maximum. Each unit of mass gets a whole bundle; only the type-level aggregate can be fractional.

A plausible regime would be a large land-consolidation program where many households share a moderate number of complete endowment and bid types—for example, because holdings follow standardized cadastral templates. Then the number of households could greatly exceed \(\tau=|T|\). The authors could plausibly recognize this as the continuous-population form of the package-based surplus-clearing computation: it retains initial ownership, bundle trades, and spatial externalities. For the paper’s particular line-gap and distance costs, I would investigate tractability, using a configuration LP and a pricing algorithm that exploits the spatial structure. That is a conjectured research direction, not a result of this paper.

The mirror is narrow: it covers the clearing computation, not the paper’s experimental claims about holdouts, learning, training, or which mechanism performs best. Its weakest point is the regime assumption. Real landowners’ endowments and valuations may be too heterogeneous for a moderate number of complete types. And because the paper does not analyze the clearing algorithm’s complexity, this candidate cannot substitute for a numbered hardness or algorithmic anchor.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive problem is that the paper supplies no named computational result to mirror. Its findings concern how people performed across mechanisms, including holdout, learning, and the effects of training. Table 8 reports regressions on experimental outcomes, not the complexity of a computational problem. The CVCM does select a surplus-maximizing allocation, but the paper neither states a formal clearing problem nor studies the algorithm’s complexity. That leaves the proposed mirror without the required anchor.

The clearing extension is coherent as a possible research project, but it changes the object under study. It takes bids or valuations as fixed input and asks how to optimize an allocation. The paper asks how trading mechanisms perform when people form and revise bids, learn about others, and sometimes hold out. A static LP over type-level bundle assignments cannot represent those strategic and learning effects. Those effects are the paper’s central results, not incidental details of the solver.

The proposed large-market regime also has a serious fit problem. A complete type here must include preferences over spatially distinct parcels and an initial endowment. In a connected landscape, parcel locations and ownership patterns make households’ types highly specific; keeping them complete can make \(\tau\) grow with the number of households. Grouping by a general holding template risks dropping the parcel identities that determine adjacency and fragmentation costs. One could posit many repeated copies of the same cadastral template, but then the resulting type distribution describes a constructed setting whose repeated geography and behavior the paper does not study.

That objection is not airtight: a repeated-template regime could make the allocation LP a sensible extension, and the CVCM description gives it a foothold in the mechanism. But it does not rescue an anchor in this paper. The case against is strongest on that distinction: the paper’s computational-looking step is a mechanism component, while its actual findings concern population behavior that the proposed continuum replaces with fixed type-level inputs.
