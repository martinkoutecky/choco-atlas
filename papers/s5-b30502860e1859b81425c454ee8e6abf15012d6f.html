What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers the size-five existence and quality guarantees in `five` and `five-specific` for rational type distributions; it leaves the variable-\(k\) asymptotic guarantee in `apx-stable` and the proof exposition aside.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is for the *population model*, not for a computational contribution in this paper. The supplied text has no named result asserting that a problem is in P, NP-hard, FPT, or otherwise computationally classified. Its theorem labeled five says that every finite election has a Condorcet winning set of size at most five; five-specific gives the bound \(\min_{t\in[0,1]}(1-t+t^k)=1-\frac{k-1}{k^{k/(k-1)}}\) for committees of size at most \(k\); and apx-stable gives the \(4.910\ldots/k\) bound. These are existential results, not complexity results. The first two are credited to Song et al.; the exposition presents their proofs. The pasted source exposes LaTeX labels rather than rendered theorem numbers, so I can’t reliably quote the printed numbers.

The most plausible continuous question suggested by the results is Continuous Five-Committee Search. An instance is a candidate set \(C\) and a support of \(\tau\) complete rankings, each with a nonnegative rational mass \(\mu_t\) summing to one. A type is one ranking; its mass is the fraction of the population with that ranking. Return an integral committee \(S\subseteq C\), \(|S|\leq 5\), minimizing
\[ B_\mu(S)=\max_{a\in C}\sum_{t:\,a\succ_t c\ \text{for every }c\in S}\mu_t. \]
In particular, a committee with \(B_\mu(S)<\tfrac12\) is a Condorcet winning set. This is a credible high-multiplicity regime: a very large electorate may have relatively few distinct ballot types, and the committee remains a set of candidates, not a fractional outcome. The theorem labeled five says every finite election has such a committee; the five-specific bound gives a stronger guarantee.

For this fixed-size search problem, the expected classification is tractable: enumerate the \(O(m^5)\) committees and compute \(B_\mu(S)\) from the \(\tau\) weighted rankings. This takes polynomial time in \(m,\tau,L\). But that algorithm is immediate enumeration, not a computational result established by the paper. A variable-\(k\) optimization question could be a follow-on, but these theorems do not classify it.

The weak point is decisive for the requested case: the paper offers no qualifying computational anchor. Also, every rational \(\mu\) can be represented by duplicating voters in a finite election, so the continuous formulation is a natural high-multiplicity encoding of the paper’s aggregate fractions, rather than a new theorem supplied by its analysis.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that the paper has no computational result to mirror. Its three named claims—five, five-specific, and apx-stable—are existence and quality guarantees: some committee has bounded size or leaves a bounded fraction of voters preferring any candidate to it. The paper does not pose a search problem, give an algorithm or running time, or classify a problem’s complexity. Its fixed-point and probabilistic arguments establish existence.

Continuous Five-Committee Search is a sensible way to *add* a computational question: given ranking types and rational masses, find an integral committee minimizing the mass that ranks some candidate above every member. But that objective and search task are not computational results of this paper. The stronger variable-\(k\) version, based on the \(4.910\ldots/k\) guarantee, has the same gap: it turns an existence bound into an algorithmic agenda the paper does not pursue. The fact that this is an exposition strengthens the mismatch; its stated purpose is to streamline a proof, not to study computation.

This is a narrow negative case, not a claim that the population model is misguided. Rankings are natural types, and high multiplicity is plausible here. If the programme counts algorithmic versions of existence theorems as mirrors—and the authors would recognize this search task as theirs—then the proposed anchor may survive. I can’t honestly make the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario; the material supports only the more limited objection that this paper supplies no computational result for one to mirror.
