The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a continuous version of the paper’s winner determination problem, but there is a strict limitation: the paper contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result. Section 2 gives the winner determination formulation and its dual; Section 3 describes CRAB’s implementation. Neither is a named complexity result, so neither can serve as one of the required anchors. In particular, the paper does not establish that winner determination is NP-hard, or prove a runtime bound for CRAB. There is therefore no compliant “one continuous problem per anchor” to report.

The best *unanchored candidate* is Continuous High-Multiplicity Winner Determination. Let \(M\) be a set of \(m\) item types, \(T\) a finite set of bidder types, and \(\mu_t\) the fraction of buyers of type \(t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). A type specifies a buyer’s complete valuation table \(v_t(S)\) for bundles \(S\subseteq M\). Let \(q_j\) be the available supply of item \(j\) per buyer. Given these rational data, find nonnegative masses \(x_{t,S}\) maximizing per-capita revenue
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}v_t(S) \]
subject to
\[ \sum_{S\subseteq M}x_{t,S}\le \mu_t \quad(t\in T),\qquad \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le q_j \quad(j\in M). \]
Here \(x_{t,S}\) is the fraction of the population assigned bundle \(S\) from type \(t\). The requested solution is an optimal mass allocation.

A plausible regime is a large market with many repeat buyers whose bundle valuations fall into a small number \(\tau=|T|\) of profiles, and with item stocks growing with the market. Dividing buyer counts and stocks by population size yields \(\mu_t\) and \(q_j\). The mirror preserves the paper’s revenue objective, bundle bids, and constraints on each buyer and item; it replaces individual assignment decisions with aggregate masses. It also makes a substantive co-modification: the paper has single-unit items, whereas this large-market version uses per-capita supplies.

I would expect this candidate to be tractable when the valuation tables are explicit: the displayed optimization problem is an LP, with a number of variables proportional to the supplied type-by-bundle data. Further questions include how accurately solutions can be rounded to finite high-multiplicity auctions, and whether tractability persists for succinct valuation descriptions that require a pricing or separation oracle. The weakness is that this is a natural relaxation of the winner-determination model, not a result the paper proves. The paper labels equation (2) an LP relaxation, but as printed it still specifies \(x_i(S)\in\{0,1\}\), so even that passage needs clarification before it can support a formal claim.

This is a plausible mirror of the paper’s central optimization problem, but it cannot be presented as an anchored case under the requested standard: there are no named computational results in the paper to anchor it to.
