The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raises no anchor that satisfies the requested standard. The closest computational claim—the polynomial-time solvability of optimal approval-vote manipulation—is attributed to Meir et al.; this paper takes it as background. Its own findings are experimental frequencies and simulations for four finite scenarios, not numbered complexity results. There is therefore no result here to classify as tractable, inherently hard, or continuum-specifically hard.

The proposed extension to a large society with types recording utility vectors and heuristic policies is coherent; repeated types are plausible in that setting. But aggregating fixed policies into approval-score masses is a different question from the paper’s: the paper measures how individual subjects choose ballots, including a focal voter acting last against specified vote totals. If the extension instead asks which policies people use, that remains an empirical or statistical question. Neither version supplies a computational problem from this paper for ChoCo to mirror.

The strongest repair is to keep that focal voter and make the rest of the electorate continuous. But then the voter’s unit ballot has zero mass in the limit, so it cannot change winner scores except at exact ties. Keeping a positive-mass voter or cohort preserves influence, but changes the question into coordinated mass control rather than individual heuristic choice. The paper’s uncertainty simulations also involve only a few future votes: under ordinary population scaling, their effect on score proportions vanishes, while preserving their pivotal effect requires finite-size or tie-boundary structure.

So the case against is strongest as a paper-specific screening verdict: this paper offers no eligible computational result, and its most natural continuous extensions change its subject. The absolute claim that no worthwhile continuous model could ever grow from these ideas is less secure; a type-based behavioral population model is possible. It would be a new project, though, not a continuous mirror of a computational result in this paper.
