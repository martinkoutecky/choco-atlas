Even setting that aside, the MAX-SAT mirror is just its existing selection rule written over masses: for each genre \(j\), compute \(\sum_t \mu_t (IP_{t,j}+2)^2\), then choose the largest of 91 values. That is a coherent high-multiplicity formulation, but the paper asserts no algorithmic question about it. EQUITABLE needs a richer state distribution over preferences, cash, and accumulated satisfaction; attendance histories may matter too. That is a strong case against a worthwhile *mirror of this paper’s computational results*. It is not proof that no useful continuous study could arise in any scenario: a large workplace with many repeated preference profiles is plausible, and a new long-run policy problem might be worth studying. The case is weakest at that broader universal claim; its firmest point is that such work would be a new problem, not a continuous mirror of a named computational result here.
