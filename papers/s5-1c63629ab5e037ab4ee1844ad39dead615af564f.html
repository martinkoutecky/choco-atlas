The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t honestly give this paper an eligible named anchor. Its “Proposição 1” says that each relevant feasible partition appears exactly once as a path in the search tree, under the stated child-selection rule. “Proposição 2” says the estimated cost of unallocated lines is an upper bound. Neither classifies the underlying problem as tractable or hard. The conclusion calls the WDP NP-complete, and the thesis reports comparisons with CPLEX, but those claims are not numbered results of the required kind.

There is still a plausible positive-side mirror at the model level. Consider a shipper with many recurring freight movements. Let each demand type \(t\) specify an origin–destination lane and its service requirements, with rational mass \(\mu_t\) of total freight. Group carriers by complete type: the same eligible lanes, cost schedule, capacity, and service characteristics. Each carrier type \(k\) has capacity \(\rho_k\) and a finite set \(B_k\) of recurring service configurations. Configuration \(b\) supplies \(a_{kbt}\) units of each demand type at cost \(c_{kb}\). The continuous problem is to choose masses \(x_{kb}\ge 0\) minimizing

\[ \sum_k\sum_{b\in B_k} c_{kb}x_{kb} \]

subject to

\[ \sum_k\sum_{b\in B_k} a_{kbt}x_{kb}=\mu_t \quad\text{for every }t, \qquad \sum_{b\in B_k}x_{kb}\le \rho_k \quad\text{for every }k. \]

Here \(x_{kb}\) is the mass of repeated freight cohorts assigned to that carrier configuration. Each individual cohort still receives an integral configuration; fractions describe the type-level average. When configurations are explicit, this is an LP and therefore a Class A candidate. If they are implicit, the pricing problem for generating useful bundles may itself be hard. Further questions include the discrete-to-continuous approximation or integrality gap, the complexity of that pricing problem, and the effect of capacity or regional constraints.

The regime I find plausible is a large shipper’s recurring truckload procurement: many shipments over a comparatively small support of lane and service types, with carriers grouped by identical bid and capability profiles. The thesis itself notes that volume-based procurement can let several carriers serve one line by volume. This makes the model recognizable as a related procurement problem, but the thesis’s main WDP awards each indivisible line to exactly one carrier.

That change is the weakest point: scalable configuration masses alter the thesis’s whole-line contract model. A shipper and the authors might accept it for repeated, high-volume movements, but I cannot claim they would accept it as *their* continuous analogue without that bridge being established. So this is a concrete positive candidate, not an anchored case meeting the paper’s required named-result criterion.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent names no eligible result to mirror. Proposition 1 concerns how feasible partitions appear in the search tree; Proposition 2 bounds a heuristic estimate. Neither classifies a computational problem’s complexity. The thesis reports that the WDP is NP-complete and compares its algorithm with CPLEX, but it gives no numbered complexity theorem or algorithmic classification of that kind. Under the requested standard, there is no anchor.

The proposed LP changes the object being allocated. In the thesis’s WDP, each distinct lane must be covered exactly once by a whole, pre-submitted package at its fixed price. Shipment volume and frequency are assumed known and folded into the bids. The number of shipments therefore does not create a population parameter in this problem; the relevant combinatorics depend on the distinct lanes and the package bids. The proposed \(\mu_t\) instead makes freight demand a mass, while \(x_{kb}\) makes assignments divisible. That is a continuous-volume procurement model, rather than a continuous society for the thesis’s WDP.

The strongest repair is to treat repeated procurement cycles as agents: each cycle receives an integral package allocation, while masses describe the share of cycles assigned to each configuration. If cycles are identical and independent, this aggregates repetitions of the same WDP; the mass formulation adds no new computational question. If cycles interact through shared carrier capacity, service targets, or route synergies across periods, those constraints may make a useful model. They also define a multi-period, capacity-coupled procurement problem absent from the thesis. The authors might recognize it as related work, especially given their discussion of volume-based procurement, but that does not supply a named thesis result for the mirror to continue.

I cannot honestly defend the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous question could be built from this topic. A high-volume, repeated-demand procurement model could be worth studying. The negative case is narrower and stronger: the paper offers no eligible named computational result, and the proponent’s concrete LP continuizes demand and allocation rather than the population in the thesis’s stated WDP. If the standard is relaxed to include adjacent logistics models, the negative case is weak.
