The continuous mirror question

Given a finite artist set \(N\) and an explicitly listed rational distribution \(\mu=(\mu_L)_{\varnothing\ne L\subseteq N}\) over user types \(L\), compute exactly \(r_i=\sum_{L\ni i}\mu_L/|L|\) for every \(i\in N\), or decide whether \(r_i\ge q\) for a rational threshold \(q\).

The model it lives in

Types are complete streamed-artist supports \(L\); \(\mu_L\) is subscriber mass; the output is \(r\in\mathbb{R}_+^N\); total revenue \(R\) yields artist payment \(Rr_i\).

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is that the paper’s underlying Shapley-allocation object has a clean high-multiplicity mirror, although the paper itself contains no qualifying computational anchor. Proposition 1 is the closest thing: it states that the Shapley value equals the sum, over users who streamed artist \(i\), of \(1/|L^j|\). The paper presents this as an easily computed identity, with the proof omitted as similar to Ginsburgh and Zang (2003). But it does not state a complexity result such as membership in P. Theorem 1, Theorem 2, Theorem 3, Corollary 1, and Proposition 2 are respectively axiomatic characterizations, axiom substitutions, or game duality—not computational results. Thus no theorem in the paper can honestly serve as an NP-hardness, FPT, or formal polynomial-time anchor.

Subject to that qualification, the natural mirror is Continuous Shapley-Streaming Evaluation. Fix a finite artist set \(N\). A user type is a nonempty subset \(L\subseteq N\), namely the set of artists that user streamed during the accounting period. A continuous society is a rational distribution \(\mu=(\mu_L)_{\varnothing\ne L\subseteq N}\), where \(\mu_L\) is the fraction of subscribers of type \(L\) and \(\sum_L\mu_L=1\). Each unit of user mass pays one unit of subscription revenue. The allocation variable is \(r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\), and the problem is to output the unique Shapley allocation

\[ r_i=\sum_{L\ni i}\frac{\mu_L}{|L|}. \]

Equivalently, for a rational threshold \(q\), one may ask whether artist \(i\) receives at least \(q\) of the per-capita revenue. The objective is exact evaluation of the revenue-allocation rule; with total revenue \(R\), artist \(i\) receives \(Rr_i\).

This is genuinely the population continuization. The artists remain a finite set of recipients, while the user population becomes mass. A finite profile embeds by setting

\[ \mu_L=\frac{|\{j\in M:L^j=L\}|}{|M|}. \]

The resulting allocation is exactly the finite Shapley allocation divided by \(|M|\). The paper’s stream counts \(t_{ij}\) can be retained in the underlying type description, but Proposition 1 shows that the Shapley index itself depends only on their zero-pattern \(L^j\). Thus identifying users with the same \(L^j\) is not an arbitrary simplification of the target rule.

A plausible regime is a large subscription platform with millions of users, a fixed catalogue or revenue pool of relevant artists, and a relatively small number of recurring listening-support patterns generated by playlists, recommendation cohorts, or genre bundles. Then \(|M|\) is enormous while the number \(\tau\) of distinct observed types is much smaller. The type is complete for this computational task: two users with the same streamed-artist set are indistinguishable to the Shapley index, even if their stream counts differ.

The problem is plainly tractable. Given the explicitly listed nonzero \(\mu_L\), one pass computes all \(r_i\) in \(O(\sum_L |L|)\) arithmetic operations. This is therefore a Class A mirror in a very weak sense: the continuous version is a direct linear aggregation. Proposition 2 also has an immediate continuous analogue. Define

\[ v_\mu(S)=\sum_{L\subseteq S}\mu_L, \qquad v_\mu^{o}(S)=\sum_{L:L\cap S\ne\varnothing}\mu_L. \]

Then \(v_\mu^{o}\) is the dual of \(v_\mu\), exactly as in Proposition 2, with user counts replaced by masses.

The mirror covers the computational content implicit in Proposition 1, not the paper’s main axiomatic contribution. Theorems 1–3 and Corollary 1 could inspire continuous versions of the characterizations, but those would be axiomatic continuizations, which the ChoCo programme explicitly excludes as its primary target. In particular, “equal global impact of users” becomes delicate in a nonatomic population: deleting one user has zero mass, so its finite-population formulation cannot simply be copied.

The weakest point is therefore substantial: the paper does not study a hard or nontrivial computational problem. Its central question is which axioms characterize an allocation rule, not how difficult it is to compute that rule. The continuous mirror is sensible and faithful, but it yields an elementary evaluation problem and supplies no discrete hardness whose dissolution could be tested. It generates worthwhile follow-up questions—minimum mass changes needed to alter the ranking of artists, robust allocation under uncertain \(\mu\), or optimization when stream intensities rather than mere support sets matter—but those are new problems, not results established by this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive objection is that the paper contains no qualifying computational anchor. Proposition 1 is a closed-form identity, not a theorem about the complexity of a decision, optimization, approximation, or parameterized problem. Theorem 1, Theorem 2, Theorem 3, and Corollary 1 are axiomatic characterizations; Proposition 2 is a duality identity. Thus the paper does not actually study a computational problem whose continuous version could advance ChoCo’s complexity landscape.

The strongest rescue is the proposed Continuous Shapley-Streaming Evaluation problem. It is certainly well-defined: with user types \(L\subseteq N\),

\[ r_i=\sum_{L\ni i}\frac{\mu_L}{|L|}. \]

But this is only the paper’s finite computation with repeated users aggregated. Given the original matrix, the finite Shapley index is already computable by one scan in \(O(nm)\) time. Given an explicitly listed distribution \(\mu\), the continuous version is the same weighted incidence sum. No integrality, optimization, separation problem, or algorithmic bottleneck has been introduced. Calling this a Class A result would stretch the programme’s use of that classification beyond recognition: there is no structural tractability phenomenon to uncover, only a linear formula already present in the paper.

The obvious stronger formulations do not repair this. Retaining the full stream vector \(t_{\cdot j}\) makes the type space artificially continuous, since Proposition 1 ignores all positive stream magnitudes and depends only on the support \(L^j\). If \(\mu\) is listed explicitly, the problem remains aggregation. If \(\mu\) is given by a succinct probabilistic description, then the difficulty comes from the newly chosen representation, not from the paper’s result; different representations can encode entirely different counting or integration problems. Minimum mass changes, robustness, and uncertainty over \(\mu\) could be interesting, but they are new problems rather than mirrors of anything proved here.

Proposition 2 fares no better. Its continuous form,

\[ v_\mu(S)=\sum_{L\subseteq S}\mu_L, \qquad v_\mu^{o}(S)=\sum_{L\cap S\neq\varnothing}\mu_L, \]

with \(v_\mu^{o}(S)=1-v_\mu(N\setminus S)\), is just the same partition-of-user-types identity with cardinalities replaced by masses. It contributes no computational question. The axioms likewise do not help: continuizing users merely replaces finite sums by integrals in an axiomatic theory that is explicitly outside ChoCo’s primary target. Continuizing artists instead would require inventing a continuum analogue of the finite-player Shapley value, changing the central object rather than mirroring it.

The negative case is therefore strong on the programme’s actual standard: the paper offers no worthwhile computational mirror, only a sensible but elementary high-multiplicity restatement. It is not strong enough to prove the literal universal claim that no future optimization or robustness problem inspired by streaming could be valuable. Such problems may well be worthwhile; they simply would be new ChoCo problems, not continuous mirrors of this paper’s named results.
