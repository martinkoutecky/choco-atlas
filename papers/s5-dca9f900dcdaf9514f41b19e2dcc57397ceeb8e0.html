The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent names no result to mirror, and the paper gives no computational result to anchor one. Its main contribution here is a rating formula and a conversion of the old ranking into starting points. Given a match history, the formula updates ratings match by match; producing the ranking then means sorting the teams. The paper poses no complexity question about that computation. A continuous model of rating changes could be built, but it would supply a new problem rather than continuize a result in this paper.

The strongest obstacle to such a model is the population. FIFA’s national teams are a small, fixed set of named institutions, each with its own opponents, fixtures, competition stages, and rating history. Teams with the same current score need not be the same type: their next match can have a different importance, and the knockout rule can affect them differently. A type rich enough to preserve those distinctions is likely to be unique to each team. Aggregating teams by score would lose information the update uses; aggregating them by complete state would leave little meaningful mass.

One could imagine a much larger, symmetric competition and track a distribution of rating-and-context states under repeated matches. That may be a sensible model of rating dynamics, but its natural questions concern the evolution of that distribution. Adding an optimization task—say, choosing matches or results to move a particular team in the ranking—would make it computational, but would also restore the named team and its individual schedule. It would be a new intervention problem, not a mirror of a computational result in this paper.

So the negative case is strong for this paper as written: there is no named computational result, and the natural population behind the ranking has little high-multiplicity structure. I cannot rule out a worthwhile continuous study of a deliberately generalized rating system, but that possibility does not establish a worthwhile continuous mirror of this paper.
