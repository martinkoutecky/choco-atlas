The continuous mirror question

Given \(d\ge 1\) and a finite-support society \(\mu=\sum_t\mu_t\delta_{x_t}\) with \(x_t\in\mathbb{Q}^d\), define weighted RR-CWM by Haar-rotating the support, taking weighted coordinatewise medians, and rotating back; does this replication-compatible strategyproof mechanism satisfy \(C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M),C_{\mathrm{post}}(\mu,M)\le 2r(\mu)\), and is its worst-case factor exactly \(2\)?

The model it lives in

Types are locations \(x_t\) with masses \(\mu_t\); the mechanism outputs a distribution over facility locations, uses weighted coordinatewise medians after random rotation, and is evaluated by \(C_{\mathrm{ante}}\), \(C_{\mathrm{post}}\), and the minimum enclosing-ball radius \(r(\mu)\). Incentive constraints are imposed on every finite rational replica of \(\mu\), with convergence as replica size grows.

The objection that survived

The proposed continuum incentive condition is not ordinary atomless-agent strategyproofness, which is vacuous for individual deviations, and its faithfulness to the paper's finite-agent notion still requires a formal bridge.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorems 11 and 21 as weighted-population mechanism guarantees and gives a plausible transfer of Theorem 2's geometric obstruction; it does not cover the paper's auxiliary geometric lemmas or establish optimality of the continuous mechanism-design problem.

Open questions for a prover

  • Prove that weighted RR-CWM satisfies the stated guarantees under the replication-compatible incentive definition, including irrational masses by approximation.
  • Determine whether a replication-compatible strategyproof mechanism can improve on the \(2\)-approximation in general dimension or on the \(1.5974\) bound in \(\mathbb{R}^2\).

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a qualified yes: this paper has a very natural high-multiplicity mirror, although it is weaker as a ChoCo case because its named results are approximation and impossibility theorems, not explicit \(P\)/\(NP\)/FPT classifications.

The population mirror is a weighted facility-location society
\[ \mu=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t\delta_{x_t}, \qquad x_t\in\mathbb{Q}^d,\quad \mu_t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0},\quad \sum_t\mu_t=1. \]
A type is a location \(x_t\); since the paper gives every agent the same Euclidean cost and strategic possibilities, location is a complete type description. The mass \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of residents at that location. This represents, for example, a city with millions of residents distributed over a few hundred apartment blocks, neighbourhood centres, or geographic zones: \(N\) is very large while \(\tau\ll N\).

A randomized continuum mechanism maps \(\mu\) to a distribution \(\Pi_\mu\) over facility locations \(Y\in\mathbb{R}^d\). Define
\[ \ell_\mu(x_t)=\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\Pi_\mu}\|x_t-Y\|_2, \]
\[ C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M)=\max_{t:\mu_t>0}\ell_\mu(x_t), \]
\[ C_{\mathrm{post}}(\mu,M) =\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\Pi_\mu}\left[\max_{t:\mu_t>0}\|x_t-Y\|_2\right], \]
and
\[ r(\mu)=\min_{z\in\mathbb{R}^d}\max_{t:\mu_t>0}\|x_t-z\|_2. \]

To preserve the paper’s strategic content, I would define strategyproofness through finite replicas rather than ordinary atomless-agent truthfulness, which would be vacuous. For every rational \(\mu\) and every \(N\) with \(N\mu_t\in\mathbb{Z}\), instantiate \(N\mu_t\) copies of type \(x_t\), require ordinary strategyproofness in expectation for one-agent deviations, and require the resulting outcome laws to converge as \(N\) grows. Equivalently, at the measure level, a deviation replaces a vanishing mass \(1/N\) at \(x\) by mass at \(x'\), and the true type \(x\) must not lower its expected cost. This is the replication-compatible continuum analogue of the paper’s incentive constraint.

My lead mirror is Continuous Random-Rotated-Corner Design. Given \(d=2\), a type domain, and a target factor \(q\), the problem is to construct an anonymous replication-compatible strategyproof-in-expectation mechanism \(M\) such that
\[ C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M)\le q\,r(\mu) \]
for every finite-support society \(\mu\). A solution is a complete rule: sample a uniform rotation, rotate the support of \(\mu\), compute the coordinatewise minimum and maximum, choose one of the four indexed corners uniformly, and rotate back.

The anchor is Theorem 11, proved in this paper: the Random-Rotated-Corner mechanism is strategyproof in expectation and has exact ex-ante approximation ratio
\[ c=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \sqrt{3-2\sqrt{2}\cos(t)}\,dt \approx 1.5974. \]
The proof already depends only on the support’s geometric extrema and the enclosing ball, not on individual identities or unequal multiplicities. Thus the same statement is natural for \(\mu\). Its tight example is especially compatible with continuization: equally spaced points on the unit circle converge to the uniform circle distribution, while each type can still represent a large replicated population.

I expect this mirror to be tractable in the constructive sense: extrema, rotations, and approximate evaluation of the expected cost are straightforward in the finite-support representation. The stronger open question is whether the optimal continuum strategyproof mechanism in \(R^2\) has a better ratio than \(1.5974\), and whether the paper’s lower bound of approximately \(1.09\) remains tight under the continuum incentive convention.

A second, broader mirror is Continuous RR-CWM Egalitarian Design. Given \(d\) and \(\mu\), the mechanism samples a Haar-uniform rotation, computes weighted coordinatewise medians of the rotated type distribution, and rotates the resulting point back. If the weighted median is nonunique, the appropriate boundary order statistics are randomized, just as in the finite mechanism. The design problem asks for a replication-compatible strategyproof mechanism with both
\[ C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M)\le 2r(\mu) \quad\text{and}\quad C_{\mathrm{post}}(\mu,M)\le 2r(\mu) \]
for every \(\mu\).

The anchor is Theorem 21, proved here: for every \(d\ge 1\), RR-CWM has tight egalitarian approximation ratio \(2\) in \(\mathbb{R}^d\), for both the ex-ante and ex-post objectives. The weighted-median formulation is exactly what high multiplicity asks for: individual counts disappear and are replaced by cumulative masses, while the facility and incentive problem remain the same. This is a Class A-style constructive mirror, subject to formalizing the real-randomness and bit-complexity model, which the paper does not do.

The population-specific lower-bound mirror is Continuous High-Dimensional Egalitarian Impossibility. Its instance is \(d\ge25\) together with a weighted society, and its task is to determine the infimum approximation ratio over all replication-compatible strategyproof-in-expectation mechanisms. The anchor is Theorem 2, proved here: for every \(d\ge25\), every strategyproof-in-expectation mechanism in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) has expected approximation ratio at least
\[ 2-O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt d}\right) \]
for both ex-ante and ex-post egalitarian objectives.

This lower bound transfers particularly cleanly. The proof repeatedly splits a large colocated mass among the vertices of regular simplices in fresh orthogonal coordinate blocks. The combinatorics live in the dimension and the geometry, not in the identities of the agents. Replicating every occupied type preserves the same weighted society and lets the construction be expressed directly as mass splitting. Thus continuization does not dissolve this barrier; it exposes that the obstruction is geometric and incentive-theoretic rather than caused by population multiplicity. This is an impossibility-transfer result, not an NP-hardness result.

The weakest point is the incentive convention. In a genuinely atomless population, one individual has zero influence on \(\mu\), so naïve strategyproofness says nothing. The mirror therefore needs replication-compatible or infinitesimal-mass strategyproofness as an explicit modelling choice. That is defensible and directly connected to the paper’s finite deviations, but it is not stated by the authors and would require a formal bridge theorem. A second weakness is that the paper proves approximation guarantees, not computational complexity classifications. If ChoCo’s anchor gate is restricted strictly to named \(P\), \(NP\)-hardness, FPT, or comparable results, then this paper has no qualifying computational anchor. Under the broader reading in which a named approximation mechanism is an algorithmic result, it supports a credible, concrete continuous mirror—strongest for Theorem 11, and reinforced by Theorems 21 and 2.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is strongest at the programme’s eligibility gate: none of the three anchors is a named computational result in the relevant sense. Theorem 11, Theorem 21, and Theorem 2 are approximation and mechanism-impossibility statements. They do not define a finite computational problem, establish membership in \(P\), prove hardness, or give an exact, approximation, or parameterized algorithm whose complexity depends on the population representation. Recasting “design a strategyproof mechanism” as a continuous problem does not supply that missing computational content.

Theorem 11 is the weakest population mirror. For a weighted society with support \(S_\mu\), the faithful definitions are
\[ C_{\mathrm{ante}}(\mu,M)=\max_{x\in S_\mu}\mathbb{E}\|x-Y\|, \qquad r(\mu)=\min_z\max_{x\in S_\mu}\|x-z\|. \]
Random-Rotated-Corner depends only on the coordinate extrema of \(S_\mu\), not on \(\mu\). Any two societies with the same support and completely different masses therefore have exactly the same mechanism, costs, and approximation ratio. One copy of every support point already gives the same instance for this theorem. The mass vector is dead input, so this is a support-set reformulation rather than a continuous-population computational problem.

The equally spaced circle example does not repair this. The paper already takes finite profiles \(P_n\) with increasingly many distinct locations and passes to a circle as a tightness limit. That is geometric closure, not high multiplicity: the number of distinct types grows with \(n\), while the theorem remains entirely insensitive to how many agents repeat each type. The proposed “continuous Random-Rotated-Corner Design” is consequently just the original mechanism with integer multiplicities erased.

Theorem 21 gives the proponent the best apparent rescue because multiplicities can enter through weighted medians. But this still produces no new result. For rational \(\mu\), choose \(N\) clearing the denominators and replace each type \(x_t\) by \(N\mu_t\) finite copies. The weighted RR-CWM is precisely the corresponding finite RR-CWM, with the same tie randomization. The \(2\)-approximation bound follows by applying Theorem 21 to every such finite replica and taking a limit. The weighted-median formulation is useful notation, but it is not a new optimization problem, pricing problem, or complexity phenomenon.

There is also a strategic gap that affects all three proposed mirrors. Under ordinary atomless-agent truthfulness, one individual has zero effect on \(\mu\), so truthful and deviating reports induce the same outcome law:
\[ \mathbb{E}_{\Pi_\mu}\|x-Y\| = \mathbb{E}_{\Pi_\mu}\|x-Y\|. \]
Strategyproofness becomes vacuous. The proponent’s finite-replica convention avoids that, but it is not equivalent to continuum strategyproofness; it is an additional family of finite-game constraints. Alternatively, treating an entire positive-mass type as a deviating player changes the paper’s individual strategyproofness into a coalition or type-level incentive notion. Neither choice is supplied by the paper, and the weighted-median theorem does not establish the resulting stronger statement.

Theorem 2 is the cleanest transfer, but that is precisely why it is not a worthwhile continuous anchor. Its recursive argument moves agents one at a time and uses each individual’s incentive constraint to obtain monotonicity of expected distance. In an atomless model that step disappears. Under the finite-replica convention, it survives only because one has retained the finite game at every \(N\); the claimed continuous lower bound is then the finite theorem applied to normalized rational masses. The simplex mass-splitting language changes the notation, not the mathematical problem. Moreover, the result is an impossibility theorem about mechanism quality, not a computational classification of a population problem.

One could formulate a genuinely new question: optimize the worst approximation ratio over all anonymous maps \(\mu\mapsto\Pi_\mu\), impose a carefully chosen replication-stability or type-coalition incentive condition, and study the complexity of that mechanism-design problem. That might be worthwhile. But it is not a mirror of Theorem 11, 21, or 2; it is a new mechanism-design programme for which this paper supplies only finite geometric ingredients. If that broader freedom is allowed, the negative case is not airtight. Under ChoCo’s stated computational-anchor requirement, however, all three proposed anchors collapse either to a support-only restatement or to a finite-replica restatement, and the paper offers no qualifying continuous-computational mirror.
