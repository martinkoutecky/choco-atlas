The continuous mirror question

Given rational agent-type masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(q_e\) for recurring chore classes \(e\), and each type's capacity \(c_t\) and size profile \(s_{t,e}\), let \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{E}\) be an integral bundle and \(p_t(b)\) its minimum bin count. Define \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)=\inf_{\lambda}\max_{\lambda_b>0}p_t(b)\), subject to \(\lambda_b\ge0\), \(\sum_b\lambda_b=1\), and \(\sum_b\lambda_b b=q\). Find masses \(\pi_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_b\pi_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}\pi_{t,b}b_e=q_e\), and \(p_t(b)\le2\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(\pi_{t,b}>0\), or compute the minimum achievable factor.

The model it lives in

Agent types \(t\) carry masses \(\mu_t\) and complete bin capacities \(c_t\) and class-size profiles \(s_{t,e}\); chore classes have per-capita supply \(q_e\). Each individual receives an integral bundle \(b\), with cost \(p_t(b)\) equal to the minimum number of bins of capacity \(c_t\), while \(\pi_{t,b}\) is fractional mass and the objective is to minimize the worst MMS factor.

The objection that survived

The paper does not formalize the limiting benchmark \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\), finite encodings for type and chore data, or output support, and Algorithm 3's finite guarantee does not by itself prove the continuous optimization's complexity.

What the mirror covers

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the configuration LP for this high-multiplicity bin-packing mirror admit polynomial-time separation under natural encodings of \(c_t\), \(s_{t,e}\), and \(q_e\)?
  • When do finite replications of the continuous allocation preserve the \(2\)-MMS guarantee for rational supplies and type masses?
  • Is the minimum-factor version hard because of bin-packing pricing, and is that hardness inherited from the discrete problem or continuum-specific?

The case FOR (proponent)

The paper has no numbered theorem explicitly proving an NP-hardness or membership-in-\(\mathrm P\) result. Its only such statement is the unnumbered remark that computing MMS is NP-hard even for additive costs, by Partition. I therefore use its named approximation and impossibility results as anchors.

My lead is a high-multiplicity mirror of Theorem 1, because its obstruction is specifically caused by having one agent of each relevant type.

Theorem 1, proved in this paper, states that for every \(n\ge2\), there is an instance with submodular costs for which no allocation is better than \(n\)-MMS, and hence no allocation is better than \(\frac{\log m}{\log\log m}\)-MMS. The construction has \(m=n^n\) items indexed by \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in[n]^n\). Agent \(i\)'s cost is the minimum number of coordinate-\(i\) planes \(C^i_\ell=\{x:x_i=\ell\}\) needed to cover her assigned items. Each agent has MMS value \(1\), but every allocation gives some agent cost \(n\). The costs are monotone submodular coverage functions.

The continuous question I would put to a prover is \(\mathsf{PlaneCover\text{-}MMS}_\infty\). There are \(r\) agent types \(i\in[r]\), each with mass \(\mu_i=1/r\), and chore classes \(x\in[r]^r\), with per-capita supply \(q_x=1/r\). A type-\(i\) agent receives an integral multiset \(B\) of chore copies and has cost

\[ v_i(B)=\left|\{\ell\in[r]:B\text{ contains some }x\text{ with }x_i=\ell\}\right|. \]

An allocation is a family of masses \(\pi_{i,B}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_B\pi_{i,B}=\mu_i\) and \(\sum_{i,B}\pi_{i,B}\#_B(x)=q_x\) for every \(x\). The continuous MMS benchmark is the least \(z\) for which the supply vector \(q\) can be represented as the expected bundle of a distribution whose every bundle has cost at most \(z\). The problem is to minimize \(\alpha\) such that \(\pi_{i,B}>0\) implies \(v_i(B)\le\alpha\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_i\).

This is genuinely a population mirror: every individual still receives an integral bundle and incurs an integral coverage cost; only the mass of identical agents and bundles is fractional. It is also exactly the high-multiplicity lift obtained by repeating each original agent type and each original chore type.

For this family, the discrete lower bound disappears completely. The continuous MMS is \(1\): type \(i\) can randomly receive one coordinate plane \(C^i_\ell\), with \(\ell\) uniform. Moreover, assign mass \(1/r^2\) of type \(i\) to the bundle \(C^i_\ell\), for every \(i,\ell\). Each chore \(x\) belongs to exactly \(r\) such planes, so its total assigned mass is \(r/r^2=1/r=q_x\). Every receiving agent has cost \(1\). Thus the continuous optimum is exactly \(1\)-MMS, whereas Theorem 1 proves that the one-agent-per-type instance cannot beat \(n\)-MMS.

I would expect this particular mirror to be tractable, and more broadly to support Class A results for structured coverage costs. It also generates the right research questions: which submodular lower bounds survive when each type has positive mass, when does the type-level allocation reduce to a flow or configuration LP, and which remaining hardness comes from the number of chore types rather than population multiplicity?

My second, more application-facing anchor is Corollary 2, proved here. It states that every bin-packing instance admits a \(2\)-MMS allocation. Here agent \(i\)'s cost for a set of chores is the minimum number of bins of capacity \(c_i\) needed to pack items whose sizes are \(s_{i,j}\). The paper gives a constructive proof through its modified bag-filling algorithm.

The corresponding problem, \(\mathsf{HM\text{-}2MMS\text{-}BinPacking}\), has finitely many agent types \(t\), with mass distribution \(\mu_t\), and finitely many recurring chore classes \(e\), with per-capita supply \(q_e\). Type \(t\) includes its complete bin capacity and size profile \(s_{t,e}\). An individual receives an integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^k\), and \(p_t(b)\) is the minimum number of bins needed to pack it. Define \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) as the minimum worst value of an integral bundle in any distribution whose expected item-count vector is \(q\). The task is to output masses \(\pi_{t,b}\) satisfying \(\sum_b\pi_{t,b}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,b}\pi_{t,b}b_e=q_e\), with \(p_t(b)\le2\,\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(\pi_{t,b}>0\).

The regime is a large fleet, warehouse, or service workforce: many agents, a small number of recurring capacity/equipment profiles, and recurring job classes. The paper’s bin-packing applications make this recognizable to its authors, while the mass formulation is the natural high-multiplicity version. The aggregate problem is a configuration LP over integral bundles. For fixed numbers of agent and chore types, I would expect Class A methods where configuration pricing is controllable. With unrestricted item classes and arbitrary rational sizes, the pricing problem may itself create continuum-specific hardness; Corollary 2 guarantees existence, not a numbered polynomial-time theorem.

The weakest point is the MMS benchmark. \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}\) is the high-multiplicity limit of the paper’s finite MMS, not literally its original finite partition definition. If chores are genuinely one-off objects with no repeatable profiles, or if individual bundles cannot be replicated, this mirror is weak. But when there are many agents with complete shared types and recurring indivisible chores, the modification is exactly the required population continuization: bundles remain integral, while only their frequencies become continuous.

This case covers Theorem 1 and Corollary 2. I would not claim that the paper’s entire general-subadditive or job-scheduling theory automatically continuizes; those would require separate type-level configuration models.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case starts with the paper’s poor computational fit. None of its numbered results is a complexity theorem. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 are existential approximation impossibilities; Theorem 2 and Corollaries 2–3 are existence guarantees whose proofs rely on MMS-defining partitions, themselves NP-hard to compute even for additive costs. The only explicit computational statement is an unnumbered remark about computing MMS. Thus the paper does not naturally supply a named algorithmic result whose continuous complexity landscape can be charted.

That objection does not defeat the first anchor, however. The proposed PlaneCover mirror is a legitimate high-multiplicity construction. It repeats both agent types and chore types, keeps every individual bundle integral, and fractionalizes only the frequencies of identical agents and bundles. Its normalization is coherent: \(q_x=1/r\) is the per-agent supply, and a uniformly random coordinate plane has exactly that expectation. The fact that the lower bound disappears is not an objection; it is precisely the sort of high-multiplicity phenomenon ChoCo is meant to investigate.

The real weakness is that PlaneCover-MMS\(_\infty\), as stated, is not yet a computational result. For the displayed instance the optimal allocation is explicit, and the formulation does not specify an input representation for the submodular costs, a decision problem, or an output-complexity guarantee. But the stronger repair is obvious: give each type an explicit coverage system, give rational per-capita supplies, and ask for the optimal fractional configuration allocation over integral bundles. That is a genuine continuous-population problem, with a nontrivial pricing question. I cannot honestly claim that repair is worthless.

The same applies to Corollary 2. The paper proves existence, not an efficient algorithm, and the proposed mirror leaves the representation of \(\mathrm{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) and the bundle distribution underspecified. A strict negative reading could insist that the original paper has one-off chores and named agents, so its finite model has no continuum. But that is too literal under the programme’s rules. Recurring job classes, many workers with identical capacity and size profiles, and per-capita chore supplies are a natural high-multiplicity regime. The resulting configuration LP has integral bundles and a bin-packing pricing problem, exactly the sort of boundary between tractability and continuum-specific hardness that ChoCo wants.

So the honest negative case is procedural rather than substantive: the paper supplies no numbered computational theorem, and both proposed mirrors need a sharper encoding and complexity statement. It does not support the universal conclusion that no worthwhile mirror exists. The PlaneCover mirror survives, and the bin-packing mirror survives even more naturally once its configuration and pricing problems are formalized.
