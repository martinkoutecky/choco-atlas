The case FOR (proponent)

There is a credible continuous mirror here, but the paper has no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result. Its closest support is unnumbered: §2.2 says the mechanism can be implemented by repeatedly solving polynomial-time assignment problems; §2.3 says its output is feasible, \(\bar g\)-acceptable, constrained efficient, and strategy-proof under the paper’s preference assumptions. The authors present these as their results and say the proofs are in the Supplementary Information. Those statements support a positive case, but they are not eligible numbered anchors under your rule.

The strongest mirror I see is for the paper’s constrained-efficient assignment result, not its strategy-proof mechanism. Consider a large refugee resettlement cohort with a fixed set \(L\) of locations. A type \(t\) records a complete preference ordering over \(L\) and the type’s outcome score \(g_t(l)\) at every location. The society is a distribution \(\mu\) over the \(\tau\) types present. Let \(\kappa_l\) be location \(l\)’s capacity per person, obtained by scaling its seat count by population size. Each individual still receives one location; mass records how many indistinguishable individuals of a type receive each location.

Mass-Constrained Efficient Assignment takes rational \(\mu_t\), \(\kappa_l\), \(g_t(l)\), and a feasible threshold \(\bar g\). It asks for an assignment \(x_{tl}\geq0\) such that
\[ \sum_l x_{tl}=\mu_t,\qquad \sum_t x_{tl}\leq\kappa_l,\qquad \sum_{t,l}g_t(l)x_{tl}\geq\bar g, \]
and no other feasible assignment gives every type a weakly better distribution over locations and some type a strictly better one. A solution is the mass assignment \(x\). The continuous assignment is an LP: maximize total type-level rank utility subject to these constraints. With \(\tau\) explicit types and rational input, this is polynomial in \(\tau\), \(|L|\), and the input bit length. Any optimum is constrained efficient under the stated preference comparison. I expect Class A.

This is plausible in the paper’s refugee setting: many families are assigned among roughly 40 locations, and repeated combinations of family characteristics, location rankings, and predicted scores could yield many more families than distinct types. The paper’s own top-ten ranking setup makes repeated preference types conceivable. The provided data do not establish that its 561-family sample has this property, so the stronger case is for a large operational cohort with repeated exact profiles. Existing fractional assignment work would be relevant machinery, but the mirror’s continuous object is the population distribution itself.

The scope is deliberately limited to the feasible, constrained-efficient assignment the mechanism produces. The paper’s strategy-proofness claim concerns reports by named agents; in a continuum, an individual has zero mass, so that incentive question needs a careful reformulation. The weakest point is therefore substantive: this LP mirrors the paper’s allocation problem and efficiency property, but not the priority mechanism as a whole. It also gives a simple algorithmic result, not a new complexity boundary. Further questions include whether its solutions approximate finite assignments near a binding outcome threshold, and what nonvacuous incentive notion for type mass best corresponds to the paper’s strategy-proofness result.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the paper supplies no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition establishing a computational result. Its computational claim is an implementation discussion in §2.2: the mechanism repeatedly solves assignment problems. The strategy-proofness and constrained-efficiency claims in §2.3 are also unnumbered. Under the requested anchor standard, there is no eligible computational result to mirror.

The proponent’s mass LP is a sensible nearby problem. Types can record a location ranking and a score vector, capacities can scale with population, and \(x_{tl}\) can describe the mass of type \(t\) assigned to location \(l\). But maximizing rank-weighted utility selects an allocation by a cardinal objective the paper does not use. Asking instead for any Pareto-efficient mass allocation drops that objective, but also drops the paper’s priority mechanism: the paper studies a rule mapping named agents’ reports and a random order into an assignment, not the computation of an arbitrary efficient assignment. Adding a continuum of priority positions does not recover the strategic result. A single agent has zero mass and cannot affect the aggregate constraint, so individual incentive compatibility becomes vacuous; allowing a positive mass to coordinate reports defines a different incentive problem.

The serious weakness in this negative case is that the allocation question itself is well-posed. A large cohort with repeated complete profiles, fixed locations, scaled capacities, and a feasible threshold could yield a genuine high-multiplicity assignment problem. Its LP may be a small Class A result. Simplicity alone does not make that question unworthy, and the paper’s sample does not rule out such a regime. So the best case against is narrower: that LP does not mirror the paper’s distinctive computational contribution, and the paper offers no eligible named computational result to anchor it. I cannot honestly rule out every worthwhile adjacent continuous project.
