The strongest candidate mirror would take a household type to be \((e,s)\), its labor productivity and initial capital share, with mass \(p(e,s)\). A policy is a tax vector \(\tau=(\tau_c,\tau_n,\tau_k)\), and each type ranks policies by its lifetime utility \(V(\tau;e,s)\). A complexity analysis of those computations could be a useful follow-up, but it would not continuize the population in a distinct mirror. Keeping the policy menu finite makes the proposed task close to restating the paper’s computation; letting policies range continuously would shift continuity to the outcome space, outside ChoCo’s scope. So the paper offers a clear high-multiplicity setting, but no named anchor for the requested positive case, and its population is already continuous.
