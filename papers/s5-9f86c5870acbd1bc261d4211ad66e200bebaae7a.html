The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raises no anchors, and that is the strongest point against a mirror of this paper’s results. Its numbered statements are definitions, not computational theorems. The paper names two heuristics, analyzes their running times, and reports experiments; it does not prove a complexity classification or a hardness result. So there is no theorem-level result here to mirror.

The deeper obstacle is the paper’s query model. Its actions ask a particular user for a particular item rating, and its “definite winner” must win under every completion of the unanswered ratings. Grouping users by their rating distributions does not make one person’s answer stand for the others: their realized ratings may differ. In a continuum, querying finitely many individuals changes none of the aggregate mass, while leaving positive mass of ratings unknown. If instead the system uses the known distributions to compare expected aggregate scores, it can do so before asking anyone; that no longer captures the paper’s exact necessary-winner task.

The strongest repair is to posit cohorts whose members share the same unknown rating for each item, so one representative’s answer resolves the cohort’s mass. That is a coherent weighted version of partial-information range voting, but the shared latent rating changes the paper’s per-user uncertainty model. And once a cohort is resolved by one answer, the number of queries depends on the number of cohorts and items, not the population mass. This makes the continuous population chiefly a weighting of scores, rather than the source of the paper’s query-complexity problem.

I would not claim that this rules out every worthwhile question. A weighted necessary-winner problem over shared latent cohorts may be a sensible project in its own right. But the paper gives no named complexity result to anchor it, and its faithful model does not carry over cleanly to a continuous population. The case for “no worthwhile mirror in any scenario” is strongest on those grounds; it is weakest against the cohort variant.
