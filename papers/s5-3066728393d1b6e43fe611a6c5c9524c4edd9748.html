The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t identify a qualifying anchor in this dissertation. Its named results characterize voting rules by axioms; none asserts a complexity result such as NP-hardness, membership in P, or fixed-parameter tractability, and the paper does not compute with a formulation that could serve as an anchor.

The strongest nearby result is Theorem 3.31, proved in the dissertation: for two alternatives, majority rule is the only rule satisfying faithfulness, cancellation, and consistency on any rich ballot space; for three alternatives, exactly when the ballot space is not \(B_1\); and for at least four alternatives, exactly when it is not \(B_1\), \(B_2\), or \(B_1\cup B_2\). This is an axiomatic characterization, not a computational result.

A natural population version would make each admissible ballot \(B\) a type, assign it a mass \(\mu_B\), and choose the alternatives maximizing approval mass, \(\sum_{B\ni x}\mu_B\). One could ask whether analogous axioms characterize that rule. Large electorates with relatively few distinct ballots make this a plausible continuous-population scenario. But the question remains axiomatic, so it does not meet ChoCo’s computational scope and cannot rescue a positive case under the stated anchor requirement. The paper offers no basis for assigning it a tractability or hardness class.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 3.31 is the only anchor the proponent raises, and it is an axiomatic classification: it specifies when faithfulness, consistency, and cancellation uniquely identify majority rule on rich ballot spaces. The dissertation’s main results follow that pattern; it states no complexity result for a computational problem.

The best population mirror is quite natural. Give each admissible ballot \(B\) a rational mass \(\mu_B\), define approval mass by \(v_\mu(x)=\sum_{B\ni x}\mu_B\), and ask whether analogous axioms characterize the rule selecting the alternatives with greatest approval mass. Large electorates with repeated ballot types make that a sensible continuous society. But the result would still be an axiomatic characterization. Replacing integer counts with masses does not turn Theorem 3.31 into an algorithmic or complexity question.

One could instead add mass-based bribery, campaigning, or winner robustness for approval voting.
