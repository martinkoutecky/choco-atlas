The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has no qualifying computational result to mirror. Its numbered findings are statistical comparisons and parameter estimates; the unique-equilibrium result is Proposition 4 of Xiao (2018), which the authors use to derive predictions for their two-seller experiment. A continuous analysis of that equilibrium would be an analytic bargaining question, outside ChoCo’s computational remit. Asking for the complexity of computing equilibrium after scaling up the seller population would supply a new problem, not mirror a computational result in this paper.

The strongest version of the proposed extension is a buyer procuring complementary inputs from large pools of sellers, with types recording each seller’s contribution and inside option. It runs into a structural choice. If sellers of a type supply interchangeable units, mass procurement can be modeled, but the unit of bargaining becomes a pool or a divisible quantity; the original game’s sequential deals with two individually pivotal owners have been replaced. If each seller retains a unique complementary contribution—as with parcels at distinct locations—then preserving that role requires distinguishing sellers by their contributions, so the type space grows with the population and mass no longer compresses the problem. Making sellers atomless also removes each seller’s individual veto; restoring veto power requires treating positive-mass groups as bargaining parties.

A continuous procurement problem built around type supplies and a chosen surplus objective could still be worthwhile research. But its algorithmic questions would come from the newly specified supply and bargaining model, not from a computational claim or task this experimental paper establishes. Likewise, continuizing its fairness estimates or transparency effects would be an empirical or analytic extension, not the missing complexity landscape.

So the negative case is strong for a *mirror* of this paper, but cannot prove that no adjacent continuous bargaining project would ever merit attention. The proponent’s own extension identifies a possible new project; it does not provide a surviving anchor in the paper.
