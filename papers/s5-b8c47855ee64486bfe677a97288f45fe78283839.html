The continuous mirror question

Given a finite horizon of \(H\) slots, a finite support \(T\) of job types \(\theta=(r_\theta,d_\theta,n_\theta,l_\theta,v_\theta)\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta>0\) summing to \(1\), per-capita capacity \(c\), and welfare threshold \(K\), does there exist a feasible schedule-mass vector \(x_{\theta,s}\ge 0\) with welfare at least \(K\)? Legal start slots satisfy \(r_\theta\le s\le d_\theta-l_\theta\), each type satisfies \(\sum_s x_{\theta,s}\le\mu_\theta\), and each slot \(u\) satisfies \(\sum_{\theta,s:\,s\le u<s+l_\theta} n_\theta x_{\theta,s}\le c\).

The model it lives in

A rational LP over type masses \(x_{\theta,s}\): maximize \(\sum_{\theta,s}v_\theta x_{\theta,s}\), subject to type-mass and per-slot capacity constraints. Each unit of assigned mass receives its type’s full \(n_\theta\)-instance bundle for \(l_\theta\) consecutive slots; a finite population of size \(N\) corresponds to counts \(N\mu_\theta\) and capacity \(Nc\).

What the mirror covers

Covers Theorem 2.1’s offline maximum-welfare allocation decision; leaves the paper’s online DSIC mechanisms and their competitive-ratio theorems unmirrored.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the mass optimum be rounded to a feasible population of size \(N\), with welfare loss bounded in terms of \(\tau\), \(H\), and \(N\)?
  • Can an online mass-allocation mechanism retain a meaningful individual-level DSIC notion and a competitive guarantee?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is Theorem 2.1, proved in this paper. It says the offline allocation problem is NP-hard; specifically, deciding whether an allocation with social welfare at least \(k\) exists is NP-complete. The proof reduces Knapsack: an item of size \(s_i\) and value \(v_i\) becomes a job of type \(\theta_i=(0,1,s_i,1,v_i)\). A subset fits in the capacity-\(C\) knapsack exactly when those jobs can run in the single available slot and achieve the target welfare.

A plausible high-multiplicity setting is a large batch of standardized cloud workloads: many customers submit jobs from a relatively small catalog of resource demands, durations, service windows, and contract values. Those fields together define the type, so agents of the same type are identical for this allocation problem. The batch is known to the offline allocator, as in the benchmark optimized by Theorem 2.1. Its population can be large while its support size \(\tau\) stays moderate. I would model a scale family in which capacity grows with the customer population; this is a per-capita supply co-modification, while time and each person’s all-or-nothing job remain discrete.

Call the mirror Continuous Population Welfare Allocation (CPWA). An instance has a finite horizon of \(H\) slots; a finite support \(T\) of types \(\theta=(r_\theta,d_\theta,n_\theta,l_\theta,v_\theta)\); rational masses \(\mu_\theta>0\) summing to \(1\); per-capita capacity \(c\); and a welfare threshold \(K\). Here \(\mu_\theta\) is the fraction of the population with type \(\theta\), and \(c\) is the number of VMs available per population member in each slot. A finite population of size \(N\) realizes this scaling with \(N\mu_\theta\) agents of each type and capacity \(Nc\).

For each legal start slot \(s\), where \(r_\theta\le s\le d_\theta-l_\theta\), choose mass \(x_{\theta,s}\ge0\) to complete. Each member of that mass receives their entire \(n_\theta\)-VM bundle for \(l_\theta\) consecutive slots, or receives nothing. The constraints are \(\sum_s x_{\theta,s}\le\mu_\theta\) for each type and \(\sum_{\theta,s:\,s\le u<s+l_\theta}n_\theta x_{\theta,s}\le c\) for every slot \(u\). Maximize \(\sum_{\theta,s}v_\theta x_{\theta,s}\); the decision question is whether this maximum is at least \(K\). The solution is the schedule-mass vector \(x\), or the exact optimum value.

I expect CPWA to be tractable (Class A). With the slot horizon explicit, this is a rational linear program with at most \(O(\tau H)\) variables and \(O(\tau+H)\) constraints, so it is solvable in time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(H\), and the input bit length \(L\). The continuum makes the amount of a type selected a real mass: individuals still complete jobs integrally, but any fraction of an indistinguishable cohort can be assigned. That is precisely the high-multiplicity relaxation being tested. Theorem 2.1’s one-slot witness becomes fractional knapsack in this model.

This case covers the paper’s offline welfare-allocation problem, not its DSIC mechanisms or their competitive-ratio results. The main weakness is that Theorem 2.1’s Knapsack reduction uses one job per item, so its support can grow with the population; it does not itself establish hardness in the moderate-\(\tau\), scaled-capacity regime. Nor does CPWA solve the original indivisible instance. The most useful follow-up questions are how its optimum can be rounded for a finite population—with guarantees in terms of \(\tau\)—and whether an online, incentive-compatible mass-allocation version preserves a meaningful analogue of the paper’s mechanism problem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against starts with Theorem 2.1’s reduction: each knapsack item becomes a job, so the hard instances can have support size \(\tau\) as large as the population. In a cloud stream, the complete type includes release time, deadline, demand, duration, and value; exact matches across all of those fields may be uncommon. The proposed mirror therefore studies a narrower regime of repeated, standardized job cohorts. And once that regime is imposed, CPWA is a direct linear program with \(O(\tau H)\) variables. It omits the paper’s online and incentive constraints, which are where its mechanism results lie. That makes it fair to question whether this particular mirror would be a substantial ChoCo project.

That objection does not defeat the anchor. Standardized cohorts with common windows and contract values are a plausible population regime, and the program explicitly treats high multiplicity as a property of an instance regime. CPWA also preserves each job’s all-or-nothing service: only the assigned fraction of an indistinguishable cohort is fractional. With capacity scaled per capita, rounding each scheduled mass down to a finite count preserves feasibility; for bounded values, the normalized welfare loss is at most on the order of \(\tau H/N\) in a population of size \(N\). So the model has a concrete bridge back to large finite instances. Its LP may be immediate, but that is the very tractability question the programme is meant to chart; the source theorem’s hardness need not survive.

A stronger mirror could retain online arrivals and ask for incentive-compatible allocation of mass. There, a single agent has zero mass, so the mechanism would need an explicit individual allocation and utility rule for truthfulness to remain meaningful. That is a real modeling burden, but it does not rule out the offline mirror already anchored by the paper.

I therefore cannot make a convincing universal case that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. The support-size mismatch and the mirror’s narrower offline scope justify caution about its research depth; they do not invalidate the high-multiplicity allocation question. Theorem 2.1’s anchor survives.
