The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raised no anchor, and the paper supplies none: it has no named computational problem with a complexity or algorithmic result to mirror. Its contributions are a modular description of opinion models and a simulator validated by reproducing results for Relative Agreement and CODA. Those replications are empirical comparisons, not theorems about a computational task.

The strongest proposed mirror would start from the simulations themselves. For Relative Agreement on a complete graph, one could replace the finite population with a distribution over opinions and uncertainty, then study its evolution. For CODA on a lattice, one could seek a spatial continuum model. But the former is a mean-field dynamics question; the latter also has to preserve spatial correlations that a distribution over agent types alone loses. Either could motivate new work, but neither continues a computational result in this paper. The fact that agents already have continuous-valued opinions does not make the society continuous: the models still simulate finitely many agents and their interactions.

A new algorithmic question—say, computing whether a specified model reaches consensus—might be worth studying. But it would be a new problem, not a mirror of one of the paper’s results. So the negative case is strong for this paper under ChoCo’s stated remit. The literal claim that no scenario could ever make related continuous-population work worthwhile is harder to defend; this paper gives no anchor for it.
