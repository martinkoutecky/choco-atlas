But it would require specifying the learning-state distribution, stochastic transitions, and the computational task. Asking for the complexity of deciding convergence, for example, would create a new problem rather than mirror one this paper established. Without that added construction, the natural continuous version is a study of population learning dynamics, which falls outside ChoCo’s focus on computational complexity.

I can’t make the universal claim airtight: that labor-market project might be worthwhile on its own. The negative case is strongest as a scope judgment.
