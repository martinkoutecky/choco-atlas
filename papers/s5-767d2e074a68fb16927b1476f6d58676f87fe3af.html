The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is strongest at the anchor rule: this paper states no numbered theorem, lemma, or corollary establishing a computational result. Its only explicit complexity claim is that Kemeny aggregation is NP-hard, which it cites as known background; it also says the dominance calculations are linear programs and uses a heuristic for aggregation. The proposed mirror therefore has no qualifying result in this paper to mirror.

The best version of that mirror would model each complete decision-maker type by its induced ranking and ask for a ranking minimizing expected Kendall distance under a population distribution \(\nu\):
\[ \min_{\pi}\sum_{\sigma}\nu_{\sigma}\,d_K(\pi,\sigma). \]
But the paper’s aggregation stage already takes ranked lists with relative-importance weights and minimizes a weighted sum of distances. Since the ranking space is finite, the integral over a continuous population reduces to this same finite weighted sum. Calling those weights population shares would change their interpretation; mathematically, it adds no new aggregation problem.

That defeats the candidate as an anchor, but it does not establish that no plausible large-population setting exists. A many-stakeholder application could make population mass meaningful. The honest negative case is therefore narrow: the paper supplies no named computational result for the programme to mirror, and its strongest computational candidate already has the weighted-distribution form a continuous mirror would take.
