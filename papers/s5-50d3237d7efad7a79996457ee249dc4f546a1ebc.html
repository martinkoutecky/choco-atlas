The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

That is the strongest case against a mirror: none of the numbered theorems asks for an algorithm, classifies complexity, or studies an input-output computational problem.

The best candidate mirror would give a distribution over additive utility types, scale supplies of item types per capita, and assign integral bundles to units of mass. One could then define continuous versions of egalitarian welfare and the fairness or efficiency properties. This is a coherent high-multiplicity fair-division model. But the paper’s theorems take a supremum over instances and establish welfare-ratio bounds; they do not provide a computational task for that model to mirror. Theorems 16 and 19 derive infinite ratios from constant-size instances with vanishing utilities. Theorem 11 analyzes the welfare of round-robin allocations. Recasting any of these as continuous welfare-bound questions would yield new extremal results, while asking for algorithms to compute allocations would add a computational problem the paper never studies.

I cannot make the universal claim airtight. High-multiplicity fair division with per-capita supplies could be a sensible, potentially valuable ChoCo project. The negative case is strongest for the narrower claim that this paper supplies a computational result to continuize: it does not. A worthwhile project inspired by it would be an extension, rather than a mirror of its results.
