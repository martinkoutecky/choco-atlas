The continuous mirror question

Given goods \(G\), rational masses \(\beta_b\) of buyer types \(b\), and rational masses \(\sigma_s\) of seller types \(s\), with \(\sum_b\beta_b+\sum_s\sigma_s=1\), each buyer type specified by a complete valuation vector \(v_{bj}\) and integer capacity \(d_b\), and each seller type by complete reservation-price and stock vectors \(r_{js}\) and \(q_{js}\), compute a competitive equilibrium. The decision variables \(x_{bjs}\ge 0\) are masses of units of good \(j\) traded from seller type \(s\) to buyer type \(b\); they satisfy \(\sum_{j,s}x_{bjs}\le\beta_b d_b\) and \(\sum_bx_{bjs}\le\sigma_s q_{js}\), and maximize \(\sum_{b,j,s}(v_{bj}-r_{js})x_{bjs}\). Return a nonnegative price vector \(p\) such that each buyer type's trades are a mass average of individually optimal integral demand plans at \(p\), each seller type's supply follows its individual price-taking supply rule, and the trades clear the market.

The model it lives in

The type-level assignment LP has variables \(x_{bjs}\), buyer-capacity bounds \(\beta_b d_b\), seller-stock bounds \(\sigma_s q_{js}\), and net-surplus objective \(\sum_{b,j,s}(v_{bj}-r_{js})x_{bjs}\); its dual provides supporting prices. Fractional \(x\) represents average trades across agents of a type, each of whom still receives an integral bundle.

The objection that survived

Complete types may be nearly as numerous as participants outside standardized recurring markets, limiting when the support-sensitive formulation helps; the proposed wholesale regime and the paper's own market example support the existence of a plausible repeated-type regime.

fatal: False

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest anchor is Theorem 2, proved in this paper. It is not a hardness result, and the paper does not state a named theorem classifying the problem as polynomial-time solvable. Theorem 2 gives a computational LP characterization: (2.1) for any equilibrium price vector \(p\), a feasible assignment is optimal if and only if it is compatible with \(p\); and (2.2) \(p\) is an equilibrium price vector if and only if the associated pair \((\omega(p),\pi(p))\) is an optimal solution of the paper’s dual LP. The authors use this relationship, together with the primal LP, to compute optimal assignments and supporting prices. That makes it a good algorithmic anchor, though a weaker prize question than a hardness anchor would be.

My lead mirror is High-Multiplicity Assignment Equilibrium. Consider a recurring wholesale market with many retailers and growers trading a fixed set \(G\) of goods. A buyer type \(b\) specifies both its per-unit valuations \((v_{bj})_{j\in G}\) and its integer demand capacity \(d_b\). A seller type \(s\) specifies both its per-unit reservation prices \((r_{js})_{j\in G}\) and its integer stock vector \((q_{js})_{j\in G}\). These are complete types: two agents have the same type only if every preference, cost, and capacity relevant to the market is the same. Let their population masses be \(\beta_b\) and \(\sigma_s\), rational numbers summing to one across both sides. In a market day with thousands or millions of participants but only dozens or hundreds of repeated valuation, stock, and capacity profiles, mass is the natural measure of the market. The paper itself points to local fresh-produce markets as an example where such a regime could arise.

For the instance, the sets of supported types and goods are explicit; values, reservation prices, masses, and capacities are given in binary; and each good has positive stock in at least one seller type. The question is to compute an equilibrium price vector \(p\) and an optimal aggregate assignment \(x\), where \(x_{bjs}\) is the mass of units of good \(j\) traded from seller type \(s\) to buyer type \(b\). The assignment must satisfy \(\sum_{j,s}x_{bjs}\le\beta_b d_b\) for every buyer type and \(\sum_bx_{bjs}\le\sigma_s q_{js}\) for every seller type and good. It maximizes total net surplus, \(\sum_{b,j,s}(v_{bj}-r_{js})x_{bjs}\).

To count as an equilibrium solution, \(p\) must make the aggregate assignment consistent with individual optimization. Write \(y_{bj}=\sum_s x_{bjs}\), \(z_{js}=\sum_b x_{bjs}\), and \(\eta_b=\max(0,\max_j(v_{bj}-p_j))\). A buyer type trades only goods whose net value equals \(\eta_b\); if \(\eta_b>0\), it uses its full capacity, so \(\sum_j y_{bj}=\beta_b d_b\), while if \(\eta_b=0\), it may use any amount up to capacity. A seller type supplies all its stock of a good if \(p_j>r_{js}\), none if \(p_j<r_{js}\), and any amount between zero and its aggregate stock if \(p_j=r_{js}\). The \(x\) values must match those buyer and seller quantities. Fractional \(x\) is mass averaged across agents: individuals still receive and sell integral bundles, while different members of a type can follow different optimal plans.

The expected classification is tractable. The mirror’s welfare problem is the paper’s primal LP with each buyer’s right-hand side changed to \(\beta_b d_b\) and each seller’s to \(\sigma_s q_{js}\). Its dual has the same type-level size; Theorem 2’s correspondence, with the paper’s dual-to-price construction, supplies supporting prices. Thus standard rational LP algorithms give a polynomial-time algorithm in the number of goods \(m\), the buyer and seller support sizes \(\tau_B,\tau_S\), and the input bit length \(L\). The output depends on the supported types, not on the number of individuals. This also has a direct high-multiplicity bridge: multiplying rational masses by a common denominator gives integer type counts, and the paper’s totally unimodular assignment matrix gives an integral optimal assignment for that finite market.

The mirror covers the paper’s optimal assignment and competitive-equilibrium computation. It does not try to continuize the paper’s lattice and utility results, or its other structural claims. Natural follow-up questions include whether the full set of aggregate equilibrium prices and type utilities has a comparably useful structure, and how to compute its extreme points efficiently.

The soft spot is that the paper already formulates and computes its finite-agent problem by LP, and its assignment polytope has integral vertices. The continuous mirror therefore does not reveal a new tractability gain over a previously hard problem; it gives the same market with a continuous population and a support-sensitive input. That makes the proposed question credible as the authors’ high-multiplicity analogue, but less compelling as a new complexity frontier. The paper’s LP uses real variables, but its agents are still individually listed, so it has not already made the population continuous.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I cannot honestly defeat the Theorem 2 anchor. The paper names a computational relationship between optimal assignments, equilibrium prices, and primal and dual LP solutions, and says those solutions can be computed with LP algorithms. That gives a clear question to mirror: compute an optimal aggregate assignment and supporting prices from buyer and seller type masses.

The stronger version of the proposed mirror is coherent. In a recurring wholesale market, buyer types can include the full valuation vector and demand capacity, and seller types the full reservation-price and stock vectors. Standardized retailers and suppliers could produce many participants per type. Aggregate fractional trades can be split among members of a type, each following an integral optimal plan; scaling rational masses to integer counts also connects the model directly to the paper’s integral assignment LP. The paper’s examples of fresh-produce markets make this scenario especially difficult to dismiss.

The best negative point is narrower: exact types include every capacity, valuation, reservation price, and stock entry, so in many markets the support could be nearly as large as the participant list. That weakens the case that this is a broadly useful regime. But the programme asks about instance regimes, and standardized recurring markets remain plausible. This concern does not defeat the mirror. Nor does the fact that the paper lacks a numbered polynomial-time theorem: Theorem 2 and the paper’s computational discussion supply the algorithmic anchor.

So the negative case is weak. I can question how often the compressed regime occurs, but I cannot support the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
