The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive lead is a type-mass version of Section 6, but the paper has no numbered computational-complexity result to anchor it under your rule.

The closest named result is Theorem 1, proved here. Given a finite lottery implementing the competitive congestion profile, each deterministic assignment in the lottery is top-\(n\)-fair up to one unit of congestion, each agent’s utilities across any two assignments differ by at most \(2\delta_i\), each assignment is \(2\delta_i\)-competitive and \((2\delta_i+\varepsilon)\)-efficient, and—if every post has competitive congestion at least one—the competitive utility profile is efficient and each realized utility exceeds its competitive value minus \(\delta_i\). Lemma 2 establishes that such a lottery exists. These are meaningful approximation and implementation guarantees, but neither result gives a runtime for finding the congestion profile or the lottery. Proposition 1’s greedy proof is a constructive fairness result, not a stated complexity classification. The paper’s explicit polynomial-time claim about deciding whether a deterministic competitive assignment exists is unnumbered and attributed to Chen et al. [10], so it is not an eligible anchor here.

If that anchor requirement were relaxed, I would propose Atomless Type-Mass Competitive Congestion Assignment. A society has finitely many posts \(A\), types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and a complete utility function \(u_t(a,z)\) for each type, strictly decreasing in post congestion \(z\). The solution is an assignment of mass \(x_{ta}\geq 0\) and loads \(\sigma_a=\sum_t x_{ta}\), with \(\sum_a x_{ta}=\mu_t\), such that \(x_{ta}>0\) only when \(a\) maximizes \(u_t(a,\sigma_a)\). A plausible regime is a large hospital or service assignment with many residents but relatively few shared preference and crowding profiles, so \(n\gg\tau\). This is recognizable from the paper’s competitive-demand model; normalization replaces individual congestion units with population mass, and type-level assignment replaces individual lotteries.

I would expect tractability for a restricted version with a shared congestion cost at each post, where convex optimization may apply. For arbitrary type-specific utility schedules, the paper gives no basis to predict an efficient algorithm or classify the problem. That is the soft spot: this is a credible new computational extension of the paper’s model, but it is not a continuous mirror anchored in a qualifying result of the paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that this paper has no eligible computational result to mirror. Theorem 1 gives guarantees for assignments in a lottery whose existence Lemma 2 establishes; neither states an algorithm or a complexity bound. The fixed-point proof supplies existence, not a computational procedure. Proposition 1’s greedy construction is also a fairness-existence result. The paper’s polynomial-time claim about deciding whether a deterministic competitive assignment exists is unnumbered and attributed to Chen et al. [10]; it concerns the discrete problem. So none of these anchors gives ChoCo a named computational result whose population-continuous complexity counterpart the paper itself establishes.

The best reconstruction is the proposed type-mass assignment: find flows \(x_{ta}\) with \(\sum_a x_{ta}=\mu_t\), loads \(\sigma_a=\sum_t x_{ta}\), and \(x_{ta}>0\) only when \(a\) maximizes \(u_t(a,\sigma_a)\). That is a sensible atomless model, but its computational formulation needs more than the paper provides. The utility input is an arbitrary function on a continuum; without a finite representation and precision model, worst-case complexity is undefined. Restricting to rational piecewise-linear utilities repairs this, but makes the computational question depend on a new encoding choice.

Even then, the proposed equilibrium condition is the type-mass form of Section 6’s competitive-demand fixed point, after grouping identical agents and normalizing loads. It is a real population-continuous model, but the paper’s distinctive theorem is about guarantees for implementing expected congestion by lotteries over discrete assignments. Computing a typed equilibrium is a plausible new project; it is not a complexity result already anchored by that theorem. Under the stated scope, that distinction matters. I am not treating aggregation as a distortion, or high-multiplicity work as a collision.

So the negative case is strong against the submitted anchors, but it cannot honestly establish that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario. A finite-type equilibrium-computation problem with a specified utility encoding could be worth studying; it would be a new computational question motivated by the paper, rather than a continuous mirror of one of its named computational results.
