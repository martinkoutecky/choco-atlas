The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is limited: this paper has no named computational result of the kind your anchor rule requires. Theorems 4.1 and 6.1 are axiomatic characterizations of Split Cycle as a defeat relation and as a winner correspondence. Theorem 4.5 and Theorem 6.5 prove refinement claims. None asserts a complexity classification or gives an algorithmic formulation that the paper computes with. Continuizing those characterizations would also be continuizing an axiomatic question, which your scope puts aside.

A plausible *new computational question suggested by the paper* would be Split Cycle Winner Computation\(_\infty\). An instance is a candidate set \(C\) and a sparse distribution \(\mu\) over complete rankings, with binary rational masses summing to \(1\). A type is a ranking; its mass is the fraction of society with that ranking. Compute the undefeated candidates, or decide whether a specified candidate is undefeated. For each pair, calculate the margin
\[ \Delta_\mu(x,y)=\sum_t\mu_t\bigl(\mathbf{1}[x\succ_t y]-\mathbf{1}[y\succ_t x]\bigr). \]
Then \(x\) defeats \(y\) when \(\Delta_\mu(x,y)>0\) and it exceeds the strength of every majority path from \(y\) to \(x\), where a path’s strength is its weakest edge. A solution is the resulting winner set.

This would fit a large electorate with many voters but relatively few distinct ballots: for instance, recurring electorates with a modest support of shared preference types. The authors would likely recognize it as computing the rule they study. It is also tractable: compute the pairwise margins and the strongest bottleneck path strengths, then identify undefeated candidates. A discrete profile embeds by setting \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), preserving the Split Cycle outcome under uniform scaling of margins.

That is a credible computational follow-on, but it is not an anchored mirror of a computational result in this paper. Under the requested standard, there are no eligible anchors and therefore no anchor-backed continuous problems to submit.
