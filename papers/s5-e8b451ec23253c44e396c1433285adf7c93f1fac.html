What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers the one-period objective using \(\mathrm{Intra}_5\), \(\mathrm{Inter}_3\), and optionally distance-based Customer-Care; it leaves the other fairness measures, repeated matching over time, and the paper’s relaxation claim aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can an optimal mass assignment be rounded to a discrete matching with a controlled additive loss in the fairness objectives?
  • Do the paper’s other within-group and across-group fairness measures admit similarly tractable type-level formulations?

The case FOR (proponent)

There is no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition in the paper asserting a computational result, so I cannot give a compliant named anchor. The closest claim is in §5.1: the authors say their nonconvex augmented-Lagrangian problem can be approximated to any requested precision in time polynomial in its dimension, citing results by Poljak, Rendl, and Wolkowicz and by Porkolab and Khachiyan. That is an unnumbered claim about the relaxation, supported by results from elsewhere. The paper’s statement that mixed-integer quadratic problems are generally NP-hard is not a hardness result about this market-clearing problem.

Still, the paper suggests a concrete continuous question. Consider a large online labor market in which many workers and jobs share a modest number of exact market-relevant types: a worker type records subgroup, availability, accumulated utility and workload, and its suitability for each job class; a job type records its payment and suitability profile. Mass is the number in each type, normalized by total worker mass. A plausible regime has \(N\) workers far exceeding the number \(\tau\) of distinct worker types—for example, a platform-wide shift with standardized roles and task classes, rather than the paper’s small live dispatch batches.

Here is a continuous version of the paper’s one-period market-clearing problem using its Intra-5 and Inter-3 measures. Let \(w_r\) be the mass of worker type \(r\), and \(\ell_q\) the mass of job class \(q\). Let \(x_{rq}\) be the mass of workers of type \(r\) assigned a whole job of class \(q\); include \(q=0\) for available workers who receive no job. Require
\[ \sum_{q\in Q\cup\{0\}}x_{rq}=w_r,\qquad \sum_r x_{rq}=\ell_q\ \ (q\in Q),\qquad x_{rq}\ge 0. \]
Thus each job is assigned once and each worker gets at most one job; fractional mass splits a type across whole-job outcomes. For an available worker of type \(r\), let \(U_r,\Lambda_r\) be prior utility and workload, and let \(p_q,d_{rq}\) be job payment and suitability. The resulting return rate is
\[ \rho_{rq}=\frac{U_r+p_q-d_{rq}}{\Lambda_r+1} \]
for a job, and \(\rho_{r0}=U_r/(\Lambda_r+1)\) for no job. With \(h=\max_r U_r/\Lambda_r\), define
\[ \mathrm{Intra}_5(x)=\sum_{r,q}x_{rq}|h-\rho_{rq}|,\qquad \mathrm{Inter}_3(x)=|\bar\rho_f(x)-\bar\rho_m(x)|, \]
where \(\bar\rho_s(x)\) is the mass-weighted mean return rate for subgroup \(s\). The problem is to return an optimal feasible mass assignment minimizing
\[ \gamma_1\mathrm{Intra}_5(x)+\gamma_2\mathrm{Inter}_3(x) +\gamma_3\sum_{r,q\in Q}x_{rq}d_{rq}, \]
for given nonnegative weights. The last term is the paper’s Customer-Care objective, written as the equivalent cost of distance.

I expect this version to be tractable: with rational input, both fairness terms are absolute values of linear expressions in \(x\), so they can be linearized and the problem becomes an LP with size polynomial in the numbers of worker and job types and the input bit length. This is a candidate Class A result about the underlying market-clearing problem, not a continuization of the paper’s encoding. It covers the Intra-5 plus Inter-3 one-period objective, optionally including Customer-Care; it does not claim the same LP treatment for the paper’s other inequality measures or its dynamic extensions. Natural follow-up questions are whether a solution rounds to a discrete matching with a controlled additive loss, and whether the richer Intra- and Inter-fairness measures retain tractable formulations.

My weakest point is that the paper does not establish a discrete hardness result for this objective, so this candidate does not yet pose the programme’s central “does hardness dissolve?” question. There is also a regime gap: the authors emphasize small dispatch batches, and exact types that include full suitability profiles may proliferate in less standardized markets. The mirror is most credible for large, repeated cohorts with genuinely repeated worker and job signatures.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent did not supply a named result of the kind the requested protocol asks us to mirror. Section 5.1 claims polynomial-time approximation of an augmented-Lagrangian relaxation, drawing on results from other papers; it does not state a numbered complexity result for the market-clearing problem itself. The solver comparisons are empirical. That leaves no compliant anchor.

The proposed mass-flow LP is still a coherent new question, and its most important weakness is the gap between its compact type representation and the paper’s market. A worker type must preserve subgroup, availability, prior utility and workload, and the full suitability profile across job classes. In the taxi setting, suitability is pair-specific and jobs are sampled in small batches; workers with different locations can therefore have different profiles. Requiring exact profiles may make the number of types grow with the number of workers. Repeated operation adds another pressure: assignments change workers’ accumulated utility, so cohorts can split into new types over time. If that happens, the LP remains defined, but its high-multiplicity advantage may disappear.

A large standardized market could avoid that problem: many workers could share a small set of histories and suitability profiles, and many jobs could share a small set of classes. In that regime, the proposed LP’s fractional mass assignments are a natural continuum model, and the absolute-value terms can be linearized. That is the strongest version of the mirror, and I cannot rule it out. Its limitation is that it establishes a tractable static allocation model for selected fairness measures; the paper supplies no discrete complexity theorem for it to relax, and the paper’s repeated setting would need an additional argument that the type representation stays small as histories evolve.

So the negative case is strongest against treating this as an anchored continuization of a paper result. It is weaker against treating it as a worthwhile new ChoCo problem: a suitable standardized market scenario is plausible, and the mass-flow formulation may be useful there. I cannot honestly defend the universal claim that no scenario yields a worthwhile continuous mirror.
