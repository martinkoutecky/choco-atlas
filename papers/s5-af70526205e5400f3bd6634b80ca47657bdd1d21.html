The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a continuous mirror is that the paper supplies no computational result to mirror. Theorem 4.5 characterizes Walrasian expectations allocations by robust efficiency. Theorems 3.6 and 3.9 establish that certain blocking coalitions can be found at any prescribed measure. These are existence and equivalence results; none gives a runtime, hardness result, or algorithmic problem with an input encoding. The proponent’s three examples therefore do not survive as anchors.

The best attempt to rescue Theorem 4.5 would be to ask whether a given allocation is Walrasian, or robustly efficient, in a finitely encoded economy. But the paper’s theorem already concerns economies with a continuum of agents. The computational question would need new choices about how to encode commodities, utilities, endowments, and allocations. Keep the paper’s full model, with arbitrary ordered Banach spaces and measurable economic data, and there is no finite input language or size parameter for a complexity analysis. Restrict it to finite-dimensional commodities and specified rational utility functions, and the result may be a useful new computational-economics problem, but it is no longer a computational mirror of a result this paper proves.

The strongest versions of Theorems 3.6 and 3.9 face the same gap. One could ask for an algorithm that constructs a blocking coalition of a prescribed measure. The paper proves such coalitions exist using atomless measure arguments; it gives no finite representation of a coalition or algorithmic input from which to construct one. A finite-type, finite-dimensional version could make that question precise, but again the encoding and computational task would be newly introduced.

Multiplicity itself is a strength here, not an objection: the paper explicitly studies atomless agents, mixed economies, and agents sharing complete characteristics. That makes a high-multiplicity regime entirely sensible. It also means the population is already continuous in the paper. That modeling fit cannot supply the missing discrete computational result.

So the negative case is strong about the designation: there is no worthwhile *mirror of this paper’s computational results*, because it has no such results. A computational project built from its equilibrium or blocking theorems could be worthwhile in its own right. Calling that a mirror would overstate the connection.
