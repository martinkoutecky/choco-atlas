The continuous mirror question

Given a finite support \(\mathcal T\) of \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\), \(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\mu_t=1\), additive values \(u_t(e)\ge 0\) for resource categories \(e\), per-capita supplies \(q_e\ge 0\), and an explicitly listed family \(\mathcal F\) of groups \(F\subseteq\mathcal T\), choose amounts \(x_{t,e}\ge 0\) per person of type \(t\), subject to \(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\mu_t x_{t,e}\le q_e\) for each \(e\). Find the largest \(\rho\) and a feasible allocation such that, for every group with positive benchmark, \(\sum_{t\in F}\mu_t\sum_e u_t(e)x_{t,e}\ge \rho\,\mu(F)\sum_e q_e\max_{t\in F}u_t(e)\), where \(\mu(F)=\sum_{t\in F}\mu_t\).

The model it lives in

A population of types with masses \(\mu_t\), complete valuation and group-membership descriptions, divisible resource categories with per-capita supplies \(q_e\), and type-uniform allocations \(x_{t,e}\); maximize the common fraction \(\rho\) of every group’s proportional-share benchmark.

What the mirror covers

This mirror covers Proposition 2.2.1 for divisible goods. It leaves the paper’s indivisible-goods guarantees, chores results, and group-reporting truthfulness result aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • For a protected-group family represented implicitly over types, can the group constraints be separated in polynomial time?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a high-multiplicity mirror of the paper’s divisible-goods computation. I would use one anchor: Proposition 2.2.1, proved in this paper. It says that, for any divisible-item instance with \(n\) agents, \(m\) items, valuations \(V\), and reference family \(\mathcal G\), one can find in time polynomial in \(n,m,|\mathcal G|\) a fractional allocation maximizing the approximation ratio to every group’s proportional share. The paper proves this by an LP: each item’s allocated fractions sum to at most one, and each group’s welfare must be at least \(\rho\) times its proportional share. The proposition is a direct algorithmic anchor; the paper’s numbered negative results bound achievable fairness guarantees rather than classify a problem as NP-hard.

A plausible regime is a large university system or employer allocating divisible resources—such as training budgets or computing capacity—to many people. Let there be \(n\) people but only \(\tau\) recurring combinations of additive valuation and group-membership pattern, with \(\tau\ll n\). The groups might be departments, job families, or protected cohorts, and may overlap. A type must include the person’s full valuation vector and membership in every group, so people assigned the same type are indistinguishable for this allocation problem. The total stock of each resource category scales with population: write \(q_e\) for the amount of category \(e\) available per person. This per-capita scaling is part of the mirror, not a change to the population-continuization goal.

Call the lead problem Continuous Max-PS Protected Allocation. Its input is a finite support \(\mathcal T\) of \(\tau\) types with rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\), \(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\mu_t=1\); rational additive values \(u_t(e)\ge0\) for each category \(e\); per-capita supplies \(q_e\ge0\); and an explicit finite family \(\mathcal F\) of groups, each a subset of \(\mathcal T\). Define \(\mu(F)=\sum_{t\in F}\mu_t\). A type-level allocation chooses \(x_{t,e}\ge0\), the amount of category \(e\) assigned per person of type \(t\), subject to
\[ \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\mu_t x_{t,e}\le q_e \qquad\text{for each }e. \]
For a group \(F\), its normalized welfare and proportional-share benchmark are
\[ W_F(x)=\sum_{t\in F}\mu_t\sum_e u_t(e)x_{t,e}, \qquad \mathrm{PS}_\mu(F)=\mu(F)\sum_e q_e\max_{t\in F}u_t(e). \]
The question is to find the largest \(\rho\) and a feasible allocation \(x\) such that \(W_F(x)\ge \rho\,\mathrm{PS}_\mu(F)\) for every group with positive benchmark. A type-uniform allocation is without loss: averaging assignments among agents with the same valuation and group memberships preserves both resource use and all group welfares.

This is the population-continuous form of the paper’s question: group entitlement becomes group mass, the group valuation remains the maximum value among its members, and the objective remains the best simultaneous proportional-share guarantee. The paper explicitly discusses external group selection, and Proposition 2.2.1 does not require groups to be laminar or disjoint. A setting with many people in a small number of valuation and membership patterns is therefore a credible instance regime for the authors’ model.

I expect this mirror to be tractable. Its LP has \(\tau m+1\) variables and, for explicitly listed groups, \(m+|\mathcal F|\) principal constraints, so rational input gives a polynomial-time algorithm in \(\tau,m,|\mathcal F|\) and the input bit length. That is a type-compressed version of the proposition’s LP, not a claim that the paper already proves this bound in \(\tau\). The discrete-to-continuous dictionary is exact for rational masses: multiply by a common denominator to obtain a finite population with those type proportions, and scale each category’s inventory accordingly.

The scope is just Proposition 2.2.1’s divisible-goods optimization. It does not claim to mirror the paper’s indivisible-goods APS results, chores, or group-reporting truthfulness. The further questions are whether groups can be given implicitly with an efficient separation procedure, and whether analogous type compression works for the paper’s indivisible-bundle results.

My weakest point is that this is a modest Class A mirror: the paper already has the core LP, and the continuous version mainly replaces individual-agent variables with type-level variables. That makes it less compelling as a flagship new complexity result. But the mirror preserves the paper’s actual fairness objective and group valuation, while making a meaningful high-multiplicity regime explicit; it is a solid positive case even if it is not a dramatic one.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Proposition 2.2.1 is the proponent’s strongest anchor, and its best mirror is clear: make each type’s complete additive valuation vector and membership in every protected group part of the type; scale resource supplies per person; and optimize the simultaneous proportional-share guarantee over type masses. Averaging across agents of the same type preserves feasibility and every group’s welfare, so this is an exact formulation, not an approximation.

The negative case is that this formulation adds little computational structure. The paper already gives an explicit LP for the finite problem. The proposed mirror is that same LP after identical agents’ variables have been aggregated: its size changes from \(nm\) allocation variables to \(\tau m\), but it introduces no new pricing problem, implicit constraints, or continuous-optimization method. When group memberships vary person by person, they are part of the type, so \(\tau\) can remain close to \(n\). In the cases where \(\tau\) is small, the compression follows directly from the LP and the symmetry of identical agents. That makes this a thin mirror: a useful way to state an instance regime, but a limited addition to the computational landscape.

That objection does not rule out a sensible scenario. A large organization with recurring valuation profiles and a few recurring group-membership patterns could have \(\tau\ll n\), even when groups overlap. Per-capita supplies also give a coherent model. The original LP does not already treat the population as continuous, so this is not a novelty collision; the fact that the items are divisible does not settle the population question.

So the strongest case against is about the mirror’s contribution, not its validity: it appears to be an exact type aggregation of a problem the paper has already made easy to solve. But the case is weak against the user’s universal burden. The low-support regime is plausible, and the resulting algorithmic dependence on \(\tau\) may matter for high-multiplicity instances. I cannot honestly argue that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario.
