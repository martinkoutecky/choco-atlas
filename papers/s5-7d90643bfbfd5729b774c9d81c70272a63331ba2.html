Why no mirror

The proposed distributional EFX formulation is a coherent high-multiplicity extension: agents retain integral bundles while type masses and per-capita good supplies are aggregated. However, the only anchor raised is Theorem 4, an existence result with a protocol but no computational guarantee. Under the required gate, the paper therefore has no named computational result to mirror, so the grade is red.

What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers Theorem 4's EFX existence and quota protocol; it leaves the MMS results, truthful mechanisms, and axiomatic characterizations without qualifying computational anchors.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the quota protocol be implemented in time polynomial in \( |\Theta| \), \( |G| \), and the valuation encoding length under an explicit valuation representation or demand oracle?
  • Does the resulting type-level EFX feasibility problem admit a compact formulation or a hardness classification when bundle configurations are given implicitly?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is qualified: the paper supports a very natural continuous-population mirror, but it does not contain a qualifying computational anchor under ChoCo’s strict rule. Its own Section 1.1 says that it studies existence rather than computational aspects. None of its numbered results proves NP-hardness, membership in \(P\), FPT, W[1]-hardness, or a comparable complexity statement. The hardness facts in the related-work discussion are cited from elsewhere.

The best substantive anchor is therefore Theorem 4, proved in this paper. It states that every finite fair-allocation instance with general leveled valuations admits an EFX allocation. The accompanying protocol writes \(m=kn+r\), chooses an arbitrary agent order, lets the first \(n-r\) agents choose their favourite available bundle of \(k\) goods, and lets the remaining \(r\) agents choose their favourite available bundle of \(k+1\) goods. The proof relies on the fact that only an earlier agent with a smaller quota could envy a later larger bundle; after removing one item from that larger bundle, the resulting \(k\)-set was available when the earlier agent chose, so she would have selected it if she preferred it.

A direct high-multiplicity mirror is:

Continuous Leveled-EFX. An instance consists of a finite set of agent valuation types \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\) with \(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta=1\), finitely many good kinds \(G\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_g\), and for every \(\theta\) a complete copy-symmetric leveled valuation \(v_\theta\). A bundle is still integral: \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{G}\), where \(b_g\) is the number of copies of good kind \(g\), and \(|b|=\sum_g b_g\).

Let \(\bar m=\sum_g\rho_g\), \(k=\lfloor\bar m\rfloor\), and \(r=\bar m-k\). The decision variable is \(x_{\theta,b}\), the mass of type-\(\theta\) agents receiving integral bundle \(b\), with \(b\) restricted to \(|b|\in\{k,k+1\}\). It must satisfy

\[ \sum_b x_{\theta,b}=\mu_\theta \]

for every \(\theta\), and

\[ \sum_{\theta,b} b_gx_{\theta,b}=\rho_g \]

for every good kind \(g\). It is EFX if, whenever \(x_{\theta,b}>0\), \(x_{\theta',b'}>0\), and \(b'_g>0\),

\[ v_\theta(b)\ge v_\theta(b'-e_g). \]

The problem is to find such an \(x\). The objective is feasibility: every infinitesimal agent receives an integral bundle, all per-capita goods are allocated, and every positive-mass agent satisfies EFX against every positive-mass bundle.

This is a genuine population continuization. The regime is a large university, advertising platform, or public organisation with \(N\) agents and \(N\rho_g\) copies of each slot or resource. Agents share one of \(\tau=|\Theta|\) complete valuation types—same quantity-first preferences, same relative preferences over good kinds—with \(N\gg\tau\). Mass is the fraction of agents, not a fractional item assigned to an individual.

There is an exact finite bridge. For rational \(\mu\) and \(\rho\), choose \(N\) clearing all denominators, create \(N\mu_\theta\) agents of type \(\theta\) and \(N\rho_g\) copies of good \(g\), and apply Theorem 4. Aggregating the resulting bundles gives \(x\). Conversely, a rational \(x\) can be expanded into a finite allocation by clearing its denominators. Thus the mirror preserves indivisible bundles while removing dependence on the number of named agents.

I would expect this problem to be Class A in an explicit configuration representation, especially for fixed numbers of good kinds and quota sizes: the serial-quota proof suggests a type-level greedy procedure combined with finite transportation over bundle configurations. The real ChoCo question is whether that construction can be compressed to time polynomial in \(\tau\), \(|G|\), the valuation encoding length, and \(\log N\), rather than \(N\). With unrestricted succinct valuation oracles and many good kinds, a new hardness result could still emerge.

The mirror covers Theorem 4 only. I would not claim that it covers the paper’s MMS or truthfulness results without separate work.

Its weakest point is also decisive: Theorem 4 is an existence theorem with a protocol, not a stated complexity theorem. Consequently, this is a convincing substantive continuous analogue, but not a fully compliant ChoCo anchor unless constructive existence results are admitted. A strict reader could therefore conclude: natural mirror, yes; qualifying computational paper, no.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive objection is that this paper supplies no qualifying computational anchor. Section 1.1 explicitly says that it studies existence rather than computational aspects. Theorem 4 is an existence theorem with a picking protocol, not a theorem about complexity, query complexity, approximation, or parameterized computation. The paper gives no valuation encoding, oracle model, or running-time bound under which “pick a favourite bundle” is an algorithmic claim. Thus, under ChoCo’s strict remit, there is no computational result here to continuize.

The proposed Continuous Leveled-EFX model is nevertheless a legitimate high-multiplicity reformulation. If \(\mu\) and \(\rho\) are rational, clearing denominators produces \(N\) finite agents and \(N\rho_g\) goods, and Theorem 4 supplies an allocation whose aggregation gives \(x\). This exact bridge is not a defect; it is evidence that the model is mathematically coherent. But it also exposes the problem: the proposed feasibility question is always feasible, by the theorem itself. Masses only record how many agents need each integral bundle; the EFX condition remains a pointwise comparison between supported bundles. The continuous formulation therefore adds no computational target to the paper’s result.

Calling the protocol “constructive” does not repair this. To ask for an algorithm computing \(x\), one must add a representation for valuations and a complexity measure. With explicit bundle values, the input may already be exponentially large; with succinct valuations or demand oracles, the complexity depends on a new modelling choice. One could instead optimize welfare, minimize support, or study the complexity of finding a quota order, but each would be a new fair-allocation problem, not a mirror of Theorem 4.

Nor does the paper hide another computational anchor. The MMS theorems and bounds are existence and impossibility statements; Corollaries 4 and 5 establish mechanisms; Proposition 5.3 and Theorem 5 are axiomatic characterizations. A continuous truthfulness mirror is especially fragile: if the outcome is only the aggregate \(x\), one agent has measure zero and cannot change it, making unilateral truthfulness vacuous. Restoring non-vacuous incentives requires an individual priority order, cohort structure, or another mechanism-level object not present in the proposed population model.

So the strongest negative verdict is: this is not a worthwhile ChoCo mirror of this paper, because the paper does not pose the computational question ChoCo is meant to continuize. The proponent is right that a sensible high-multiplicity EFX model can be written down; I cannot honestly claim that model is incoherent or that no separate research project could study it. The no-go is narrower but decisive: it would be a new computational fair-division project built around an existence theorem, not a continuous computational mirror of the paper.
