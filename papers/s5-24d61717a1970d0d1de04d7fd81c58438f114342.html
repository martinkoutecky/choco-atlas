The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the paper gives no named computational-complexity result to mirror. Its central outputs are finite calculations of power indices and a proposed iterative reweighting procedure for councils of 15 or 27 members. The paper does not classify the complexity of computing those indices or of finding fair weights. That leaves no result whose continuous counterpart would complete the kind of complexity landscape ChoCo is building.

The proposed federation of many jurisdictions is the best repair, but its voting-power measure runs into a basic limit problem. A Banzhaf swing is defined by whether one member’s indivisible vote moves a coalition across the quota. In an atomless population of jurisdictions, one member has zero mass and cannot change the aggregate vote. The individual swing probabilities collapse; normalized Banzhaf power is then undefined without an additional scaling or influence convention. A finite-size limit with a central-limit scaling, or a functional derivative of passage probability, could restore a meaningful quantity. But that is a newly specified asymptotic voting model, not a continuous version of a computational result established in this paper. It may be interesting on its own, but its answer would depend on modelling choices the paper does not make.

Continuizing the citizens instead does not rescue the mirror. Citizens are not the Council’s voting units: member states cast indivisible bloc votes. In the paper’s two-stage fairness model, a continuum citizen has zero chance of being pivotal, making the literal individual-power criterion degenerate. A finite-population approximation leads back to the Penrose scaling the paper already uses.

So the proposed scenario does not establish a worthwhile continuous mirror of this paper’s results. The negative case is weakest at the boundary: the reweighting procedure is genuinely algorithmic, and a different federation could motivate a new high-multiplicity or asymptotic study of fair weights. I cannot rule out that adjacent project. But it would need to supply both the voting-power notion and the computational problem; neither is furnished by a named result here.
