The case FOR (proponent)

I think the strongest positive case is a genuine high-multiplicity mirror of the paper’s priority-neutral matching problem. The continuous object should be the population of applicants and positions, not preferences, outcomes, or randomness.

Let \(A\) be a finite set of applicant types and \(H\) a finite set of institution types. An applicant type \(a\) has a complete preference order \(\succ_a\) over \(H\) and an outside option \(\varnothing\). An institution type \(h\) has a strict priority order \(\succ_h\) over \(A\). The instance gives rational masses \(p_a\) of applicants of each type and rational supplies \(q_h\) of each institution type. A matching is a transport plan \(x=(x_{ah})\), where \(x_{ah}\) is the mass of type \(a\) assigned to type \(h\), subject to \(\sum_h x_{ah}=p_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ah}=q_h\); unmatched mass is assigned to \(\varnothing\).

This is a natural regime for school admission, residency placement, or large-scale assignment: for example, millions of applicants may fall into a few dozen preference/eligibility types, while positions fall into a few dozen priority/capacity types. A type includes every feature used by the model, so agents with the same type are genuinely interchangeable. Mass means population share or seat capacity; each infinitesimal applicant still receives one integral institution.

The continuous priority-violation notion can be defined without averaging away the paper’s structure. A positive mass of type \(a\) is priority-violated at \(h\) if it is assigned to some \(g\), prefers \(h\) to \(g\), and \(h\) currently assigns positive mass to applicant types below \(a\) in \(\succ_h\). Formally, \(a\) is violated in \(x\) if there are \(g,h\in H\) such that \(x_{ag}>0\), \(h\succ_a g\), and \(\sum_{b:a\succ_h b}x_{bh}>0\). Let \(\operatorname{vio}(x)\) be the set of such types.

For two mass matchings \(x\) and \(y\), write \(y_a\succeq_a x_a\) when the mass distribution of type \(a\) under \(y\) first-order stochastically dominates its distribution under \(x\): equivalently, there is a coupling of the two distributions in which every applicant receives an institution weakly preferred under \(y\), with strict improvement on positive mass for \(y_a\succ_a x_a\). This is the anonymous, high-multiplicity version of comparing the same applicant’s assignment in two finite matchings.

Call \(y\) a continuous priority-correcting adjustment of \(x\) if some \(a_\star\in\operatorname{vio}(x)\) satisfies \(y_{a_\star}\succ_{a_\star}x_{a_\star}\), and every \(a\in\operatorname{vio}(y)\) satisfies \(y_a\succeq_a x_a\). Define \(x\) to be \(\operatorname{PN}_\infty\) if it has no such adjustment. The decision variable is therefore a mass transport plan, and the objective is exact membership: either certify that no continuous PCA exists or return one as a witness. This is the paper’s question, with individuals replaced by interchangeable mass—not a welfare problem quietly substituted for it.

My lead anchor is the paper’s “Main Theorem; Characterization of \(\PN\) lattices,” proved here. Its full content is that a lattice arises as the priority-neutral lattice of some preference-and-priority profile if and only if it is isomorphic to a movement lattice with the good chain property. A movement lattice is generated by a base matching, rotations \(R\), terminal pairs \(U\), predecessor relations \(P\), and movement relations \(M\). A set of rotations \(S\subseteq R\) is admissible exactly when no predecessor relation violates \(S\) and no PCA cycle corrects \(S\); the good chain property requires an ordering of the rotations whose successive prefixes are all admissible.

The continuous problem I would hand to a prover is:

Given a typed market \(\mathcal I=(A,H,p,q,\succ)\), construct a finite capacitated movement representation \(G_\infty\). A rotation is now a type-level cyclic transfer template with a capacity: applying an amount \(\lambda\) moves \(\lambda\) mass around the cycle while preserving every applicant-type and institution-type marginal. The representation must include terminal-pair states, predecessor/capacity constraints, and movement cycles. It must provide an explicit map \(\Phi\) from feasible fractional rotation states to mass matchings such that \(\Phi(Z)=\operatorname{PN}_\infty(\mathcal I)\) exactly. Every forbidden fractional state must yield an actual continuous PCA, and every continuous PCA must yield either a violated predecessor constraint or an enabled movement cycle. Conversely, every admissible fractional movement system with the continuous good-chain property should be realizable by some typed market.

This is not merely “write down the definition of \(\operatorname{PN}_\infty\).” The requested output is a finite graph or polyhedral/capacitated object, of size polynomial in the number of types and the encoding length of the masses, together with a membership or separation algorithm. The natural prediction is Class A: the mass-balance constraints are linear, fractional rotations are flow operations, and PCA detection should reduce to finite cycle separation or linear programming. If the exact movement characterization fails, the failure would itself identify a continuum-specific obstruction rather than showing that the mirror was misguided.

A second, supporting anchor is the paper’s “Good Chain Property of \(\PN\),” proved here. It states that if \(G\) is the rotation-DAG representation of \(\K\), then there is an ordering \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_N\) of its rotations such that every prefix matching \(\operatorname{match}(\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k\})\) is priority-neutral.

Its continuous counterpart is the following named problem: given a typed market, compute a sequence of type-level rotation templates and their admissible mass ranges connecting the institution-optimal mass matching \(\operatorname{IPDA}_\infty\) to the terminal Kesten–Tang–Yu/EADAM-type mass matching. Each step must alter only one cyclic transfer, and every intermediate mass matching, including partial transfers \(0\le\lambda\le\bar\lambda\), must be \(\operatorname{PN}_\infty\). A solution is the sequence, capacities, and a certificate that every intermediate state has no continuous PCA; a negative answer must provide a mass value and a PCA witness.

I expect this also to be tractable for bounded or moderately sized type sets, by adapting the paper’s Reny-feasible chains and replacing unit rotations by flow amounts. The further questions are substantive: does \(\operatorname{PN}_\infty\) form a complete lattice under stochastic dominance; are its joins and meets piecewise-linear; and can rational finite-population matchings be rounded to it with controlled additive loss?

The authors should recognize this as their problem. The primitives remain applicants, institutions, preferences, priorities, priority violations, Pareto improvement, PCA, and the applicant-dominance order. Only the regime changes from a finite list of named agents to a distribution over complete types. The paper is not already continuous: its matchings are discrete bijections, its rotations are Boolean eliminations, and its lattice is finite. Existing high-multiplicity matching work would support the regime rather than collide with it.

The weakest point is that the paper’s proof relies heavily on Boolean rotations and on every applicant having one named partner. In the continuum, a type can be split across institutions, and stochastic dominance is a modelling choice. Partial rotations may create new PCA witnesses, and \(\operatorname{PN}_\infty\) may fail to be a lattice even though every finite \(\PN\) is one. I would concede that the finite characterization should not be claimed to lift verbatim. But this is precisely a valuable continuous mirror: it preserves the authors’ central question while exposing whether the movement-graph structure is genuinely structural or merely an artefact of indivisibility.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The positive case does not establish a mirror of either named result. It changes three central primitives at once: strict one-to-one matching becomes capacitated many-to-one allocation, strict applicant-by-applicant priorities become anonymous type priorities, and pointwise dominance becomes stochastic dominance. That may define a worthwhile new market model, but it is not yet a continuization of the paper’s object.

The strict-priority issue is fundamental. In the paper, an institution ranks named applicants strictly. If many applicants are genuinely the same type, the institution must either tie them or distinguish them by an identity-level tie-break. With tie-breaks, the identity is part of the problem and multiplicity disappears; with ties, the model has moved to weak priorities and capacities. That is a defensible modification, but priority-neutrality must then be rebuilt, rather than transported.

The proposed \( \operatorname{vio}(x) \) also loses the paper’s individual structure. It declares an entire type violated whenever a positive amount of that type is assigned below a lower-priority type. This is support-sensitive and discontinuous. Take two applicant types \(a,b\), two institutions \(h,g\), unit masses and capacities, with \(a\) above \(b\) at \(h\), and both applicants preferring \(h\) to \(g\). Let \(x^\varepsilon\) assign \(1-\varepsilon\) of \(a\) and \(\varepsilon\) of \(b\) to \(h\), and the remaining mass to \(g\). For every \( \varepsilon>0 \), \(a\) is priority-violated; at \( \varepsilon=0 \), it is not. Thus an arbitrarily small fractional transfer changes the PCA predicate.

This is not merely a technical nuisance. The finite paper’s movement graph exploits a Boolean fact: a rotation is either eliminated or not, and a subset of rotations determines a matching. In the proposed model, the same allocation can have many cycle decompositions, partial rotations are not covers, and whether a partial transfer is admissible depends on which support face of the transportation polytope it occupies. A “fractional movement graph” is therefore either representation-dependent or simply an encoding of the entire PCA predicate. The proponent’s requested polynomial-size \(G_\infty\) and map \( \Phi \) are not defined by the paper’s primitives.

This defeats the lead anchor, the “Main Theorem; Characterization of \( \PN \) lattices.” That theorem is a characterization of finite lattices generated by Boolean rotation sets; it is not itself a computational theorem. A continuous analogue would need a canonical lattice or polyhedral object to characterize. Aggregate allocations under stochastic dominance do not generally have the required joins and meets: coordinatewise maxima of two feasible transport plans can violate both applicant and institution marginals. Taking convex hulls does not repair this, because support-based priority violations behave differently under mixtures. Restricting to unsplit allocations abandons the continuous population model.

The “Good Chain Property” fares no better. In the paper, adding one rotation means moving to a cover. In a divisible allocation space there are no analogous intrinsic covers: every positive transfer can be subdivided arbitrarily. Requiring every partial transfer \(0\leq\lambda\leq\bar\lambda\) to remain priority-neutral is an extra and generally unstable condition. In the example above, an infinitesimal partial cycle creates a positive support edge and hence a new priority violation. Replacing this by a path through selected extreme points would be a new connectivity problem, not the paper’s one-rotation chain theorem.

The strongest repaired proposal—anonymous applicant and institution types, capacities, weak type priorities, and a carefully defined aggregate PCA—is certainly a sensible research direction. Existing high-multiplicity matching work supports that regime. But it should be presented as a new theory of fractional priority neutrality, not as a direct continuous mirror of this paper’s lattice characterization. The honest negative conclusion is therefore limited: the proposed anchors do not yet survive as faithful continuizations. I cannot honestly prove the stronger claim that no worthwhile anonymous capacitated variant exists; that part of the negative case is weak.
