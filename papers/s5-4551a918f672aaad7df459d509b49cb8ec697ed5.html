The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s central point holds: there is no named computational result here to mirror. Theorem 3.1 characterizes when immediate agreement at Rubinstein prices exists; Theorem 3.4 gives conditions for a Nash-in-Nash limit equilibrium; Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 concern equilibrium prices and uniqueness; Theorem A.1 gives another existence result. None defines an algorithmic problem or establishes its complexity. Checking the assumptions may require examining many agreement subsets, but that is not a computational result proved by the paper. The only explicit limit, \(\Lambda\to0\), concerns time between offers.

The proposed health-care setting is a plausible high-multiplicity regime, so scale alone is no objection. But if many firms are copies with the same bargaining environment, replication repeats the paper’s per-agreement analysis: the Nash-in-Nash price is determined by marginal contributions and bargaining weights, not the number of copies. A more substantive continuum with payoffs depending on aggregate network structure could be worth studying, but it would require defining a new game and a new computational task. That would be an extension, not a continuous mirror of any result named here.

The negative case is strongest on programme fit, not on the universal claim. I can’t rule out a worthwhile computational equilibrium project built from this model; I can say the paper itself supplies no computational anchor for one.
