The continuous mirror question

Given \(m\), a positive rational valuation vector \(w\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^m\), and rational per-capita supplies \(q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^m\), compute a finite-support distribution \(\lambda\) over integral bundles \(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^m\) such that \(\sum_k\lambda_k=1\), \(\sum_k\lambda_k k=q\), and, for every supported \(k,\ell\), \(w\cdot k\ge w\cdot\ell-w_j\) for every \(j\) with \(\ell_j>0\).

The model it lives in

A population of mass \(1\) with one identical valuation type \(w\), and per-capita supplies \(q\) of \(m\) indivisible good classes. Choose masses \(\lambda_k\) over integral bundles, subject to the supply and EFX constraints; the proposed finite LP minimizes \(\sum_k\lambda_k(w\cdot k-w\cdot q)^2\) over bundles satisfying \(w\cdot k\le w\cdot q+w_{\max}\), where \(w_{\max}=\max_j w_j\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Proposition 1’s identical positive additive valuations case. It leaves aside the empirical simulated-annealing results for heterogeneous valuations, general additive EFX, and the reported runtime patterns.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the bundle pricing problem for the proposed LP be solved in time polynomial in \(m\) and the binary input length?
  • What running time and output encoding are possible for computing an EFX distribution from rational \(w\) and \(q\)?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest mirror is narrow: the identical additive valuations result. The paper has no numbered theorem classifying a problem as NP-hard, polynomial-time, or FPT. Its strongest named computational anchor is Proposition 1, proved in this paper. It says that when all agents have identical additive valuations and every good has positive common value \(w_g\), the potential
\[ \Phi(A)=\sum_i (Y_i-\mu)^2,\qquad Y_i=\sum_{g\in A_i}w_g,\qquad \mu=\frac1n\sum_g w_g \]
strictly decreases along some single-good transfer whenever the allocation is not EFX. Thus every strict-descent process using those transfers terminates at an EFX allocation. The paper presents this as a new proof of an existence result previously established in [PR20], not as a polynomial-time bound.

I would mirror Proposition 1 with Identical-Type Mass-EFX. There are \(m\) standardized good classes. Every agent has the same complete additive valuation vector \(w\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^m\). The population has mass \(1\), and \(q_j\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\) is the supply of indivisible copies of class \(j\) per unit of population. A bundle is an integer vector \(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m\), so each individual still receives whole copies. The decision variable is a finite-support distribution \(\lambda\) over bundles: \(\lambda_k\) is the fraction of the population receiving bundle \(k\). Feasibility requires
\[ \sum_k\lambda_k=1,\qquad \sum_k\lambda_k k_j=q_j\quad\text{for each }j. \]
A solution is such a distribution for which every pair \(k,\ell\) in its support satisfies
\[ w\cdot k\ \ge\ w\cdot\ell-w_j \quad\text{for every }j\text{ with }\ell_j>0. \]
That is exactly EFX for any agent receiving \(k\) compared with any agent receiving \(\ell\).

The regime is a large cohort of recipients with the same additive priorities over a fixed set of standardized goods, with the number of recipients far larger than the number of good classes. For example, a large relief programme might distribute repeated copies of a fixed catalogue of supplies among households sharing the same valuation schedule. The type is the entire valuation vector \(w\), and its mass is \(1\). Supply scales with population: for \(N\) agents, take \(Nq_j\) copies of class \(j\). This co-modification is needed to keep per-capita supply meaningful; it leaves goods indivisible for each person. It is precisely a high-multiplicity regime, not a claim about every fair-division setting.

The continuous question is: given rational \(w\) and per-capita supplies \(q\), compute a finite-support EFX distribution \(\lambda\), with running time measured against \(m\) and the binary input length. This is a genuine extension of the proposition’s transfer argument. Let \(\bar u=w\cdot q\) and \(w_{\max}=\max_j w_j\). Consider the finite set of bundles
\[ \mathcal K=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}^m: w\cdot k\le \bar u+w_{\max}\}. \]
Minimize the continuous variance potential
\[ \sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\lambda_k(w\cdot k-\bar u)^2 \]
subject to the mass and supply constraints above. The LP is feasible: choose \(N\) so every \(Nq_j\) is an integer, apply Proposition 1 to the resulting finite instance with \(N\) identical agents, and aggregate their bundles. Any EFX allocation has all supported bundle values within \(w_{\max}\) of one another, so its bundles lie in \(\mathcal K\).

At an LP minimizer, an EFX violation between supported bundles \(k,\ell\), with \(\ell_j>0\), would mean \(w\cdot\ell-w\cdot k>w_j\). Moving a small mass \(\epsilon\) from \(k\) to \(k+e_j\) and from \(\ell\) to \(\ell-e_j\) preserves the supplies and changes the objective by
\[ 2\epsilon w_j(w\cdot k-w\cdot\ell+w_j)<0, \]
a contradiction. So the minimizer is EFX. This gives a finite LP formulation; a basic optimal solution needs at most \(m+1\) bundle types. The mass transfer between bundle groups is the continuous counterpart of the proposition’s single-good transfer.

My directional bet is Class A, tractable, as a research target: the potential argument reduces the mirror to a configuration LP, and its central algorithmic question is whether the pricing problem can be handled efficiently. That is not a polynomial-time result in the paper. The finite configuration set may be enormous relative to the succinct binary input, so the pricing problem and output bit complexity remain open questions. I see no basis in this paper for claiming hardness transfers, since it supplies no named hardness anchor; continuum-specific hardness is possible if pricing proves difficult, but I would not predict it from Proposition 1 alone.

This mirror covers only Proposition 1’s identical-additive-valuation case. It does not mirror the paper’s empirical simulated-annealing results for heterogeneous valuations or settle EFX for general additive valuations. Its weakest point is the severe homogeneity of the type distribution, combined with the fact that the anchor proves termination and existence rather than an efficient algorithm. The case survives that limitation because identical valuations are explicitly the paper’s theorem-bearing setting, and the mass-transfer proof preserves both EFX comparisons and integral individual bundles.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The anchor does not establish a computational-complexity result. Proposition 1 proves that every strict-descent process on the finite allocation space eventually reaches an EFX allocation for identical additive valuations. It gives no running-time bound, and the paper’s heterogeneous-valuation results are empirical. Under ChoCo’s brief, there is no named complexity classification here to continuize.

The proposed mass model is mathematically coherent, but it adds a new computational task. For rational per-capita supplies \(q\), scaling to an integer number of agents and goods lets Proposition 1 supply a finite EFX allocation; aggregating its bundles gives a feasible mass distribution. The interesting question is then whether one can find a compact distribution efficiently—perhaps through the proposed LP and its pricing problem. That is a plausible research question, but the LP, its objective, and the pricing challenge are not results about the paper’s local search. They are a new high-multiplicity allocation problem built from its existence argument.

A stronger variant would allow several valuation types and per-capita supplies, then ask for an exact or approximate EFX allocation across those types. That broadens the population model, but it leaves Proposition 1’s identical-valuations setting and its potential proof behind. The paper supplies no computational guarantee for that heterogeneous problem, while exact EFX existence there remains unresolved. It could motivate worthwhile work, but it does not rescue this anchor as a mirror of a computational result in the paper.

This is the strongest negative case I can make, and it is limited. The identical-type mass model is a sensible high-multiplicity regime, the proposed feasibility argument appears sound, and its compressed-allocation question may be worthwhile on its own. So I would reject Proposition 1 as evidence that this paper contains a computational result for ChoCo to continuize; I would not claim the paper admits no worthwhile continuous mirror in any scenario.
