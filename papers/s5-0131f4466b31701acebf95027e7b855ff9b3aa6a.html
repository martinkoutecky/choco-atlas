The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), additive valuations \(v_{tg}\geq 0\) for \(m\) resource classes, per-person welfare weights \(a_t\geq 0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=1\), and rational per-capita supplies \(s_g\), find a finite-support mass assignment \(x_{t,k}\geq 0\) to integral bundles \(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^m\). Require \(\sum_k x_{t,k}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,k}k_gx_{t,k}\leq s_g\), and define \(u_t(k)=\sum_g v_{tg}k_g\) and \(W(x)=\sum_{t,k}a_tx_{t,k}\log u_t(k)\). With \(\nu_t=\mu_ta_t\), does there exist an algorithm polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\) that returns a feasible \(x\) with \(W(x)\geq \mathrm{OPT}-2\log 2-\frac{1}{2e}-2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu\| \mu)-\epsilon\), where \(L\) is the rational input bit length and feasible welfare is positive?

The model it lives in

Types \(t\in T\) carry valuation vectors \((v_{tg})_{g=1}^m\) and welfare weights \(a_t\), with population mass \(\mu_t\); resource class \(g\) has per-capita supply \(s_g\). Variables \(x_{t,k}\) assign type mass to integral bundles \(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^m\), subject to type-mass and supply constraints, and the objective is \(W(x)=\sum_{t,k}a_tx_{t,k}\log(\sum_g v_{tg}k_g)\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 1's approximation result for additive valuations. It leaves the relaxation-equivalence results, including Theorems 2 and 17, and the supporting rounding lemmas unaddressed.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can configuration pricing over integral bundles \(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^m\) yield the stated approximation in time polynomial in \(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon)\)?
  • Can a finite mass plan be translated into finite high-multiplicity allocations along denominator-compatible population sizes with a controlled welfare loss?

The case FOR (proponent)

The lead mirror I would defend is a continuous-population version of weighted Nash Social Welfare allocation. The paper’s strongest eligible anchor is Theorem 1, proved here. It says that for a weighted NSW instance with additive nonnegative valuations and weights summing to one, a polynomial-time algorithm returns an allocation \(\sigma\) with
\(\mathrm{NSW}(\sigma)\ge \mathrm{OPT}-2\log 2-\frac{1}{2e}-2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf w\|\mathbf u)\),
where \(\mathbf u\) is uniform over the \(n\) agents and \(\mathrm{NSW}(\sigma)=\sum_i w_i\log(\sum_{j\in\sigma^{-1}(i)}v_{ij})\). This is an additive guarantee for the log objective, equivalently a multiplicative approximation for the product objective.

Here is a continuous problem that keeps the agents’ bundles indivisible. Consider a large benefits programme with standardized resource units—say, service slots or vouchers—and many recipients. A type \(t\) records the recipient’s complete valuation vector \(v_{tg}\) over the \(m\) resource classes and their per-person welfare weight \(a_t\). The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over \(\tau\) such types, with \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t=1\). Resource class \(g\) has per-capita supply \(s_g\). Each unit of mass receives an integral bundle \(k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), with utility \(u_t(k)=\sum_g v_{tg}k_g\).

Let \(x_{t,k}\) be the fraction of the whole population of type \(t\) assigned bundle \(k\). A feasible mass assignment satisfies \(\sum_k x_{t,k}=\mu_t\) for each \(t\), and \(\sum_{t,k}k_gx_{t,k}\le s_g\) for each resource class \(g\). Its welfare is \(W(x)=\sum_{t,k}a_tx_{t,k}\log u_t(k)\). Continuous Weighted NSW Approximation asks, given rational type data, masses, supplies, and valuations with positive feasible welfare, for a feasible mass assignment \(x\) whose welfare is within the target additive loss in Theorem 1 of the optimum.

That target has a natural population-level form. Define the welfare-weight distribution \(\nu_t=\mu_ta_t\). In a finite approximation with \(N\) people, \(N\mu_t\) people of type \(t\), and individual weight \(a_t/N\), the paper’s divergence becomes \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf w\|\mathbf u_N)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu\|\mu)=\sum_t\mu_ta_t\log a_t\). Thus the continuous question is whether one can find \(x\) with \(W(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}-2\log 2-\frac{1}{2e}-2D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu\|\mu)-\epsilon\), in time polynomial in \(m,\tau\), the rational input length, and \(\log(1/\epsilon)\). When welfare weight is proportional to headcount, the divergence vanishes and the loss is constant.

The regime is a recurring, large programme with millions of recipients but a modest set of stable valuation and priority profiles. Members of a type share their entire valuation vector and welfare weight, so they are indistinguishable in every respect the problem uses; the number of recipients can greatly exceed \(\tau\). The finite approximants have \(N\mu_t\) recipients of each type and \(Ns_g\) units of each resource class. This does co-scale supplies with population, but that is precisely the permitted co-modification needed to make a continuum meaningful. The output \(x\) describes how actual portions of each cohort receive integral bundles; it does not make each person’s bundle fractional.

I expect the approximation question to be Class A, provisionally. The paper’s algorithm already combines a convex relaxation with structural rounding, and identical types and resource classes create a plausible route to aggregate the relaxation by type and class. In a continuum, mass can be split among integral bundles without rounding cohort counts to integers. The objective and the welfare-weight divergence both survive the limit in a particularly clean form. This makes the paper’s underlying optimization question recognizable to its authors: maximize the same weighted log welfare under the same additive utilities and item constraints, now for a population distribution.

The paper gives no numbered hardness result to anchor a hardness-transfer question. Its related-work prose mentions discrete NP-hardness and APX-hardness with citations, but those are not numbered results of this paper. I would not use them as anchors here; Theorem 1 is the single anchor.

The mirror covers that theorem’s additive-valuations approximation result, not the paper’s separate equivalence results for mathematical programs. It also creates concrete follow-up questions: can the relaxation and rounding be aggregated to depend on \(\tau\), rather than on the number of people; can a finite mass plan be found with the stated bound; and under what utility conditions can continuous plans be rounded back to finite high-multiplicity instances with controlled welfare loss?

The weak point is that the proposed runtime does not follow from Theorem 1’s runtime, which is polynomial in the finite instance size and may still grow with \(N\). A configuration formulation has a variable for each integral bundle, and its pricing problem ranges over integer bundle vectors; that may preserve hard combinatorics even after population multiplicity disappears. So Class A is a credible conjecture to investigate, not a consequence of the paper. The mirror is strongest for recurring settings with repeated resource classes; it is a poor fit for one-off allocations of unique goods whose supplies cannot scale with the population.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is to the proponent’s runtime claim. Theorem 1 is polynomial in the *finite* number of agents \(n\), but that does not imply a polynomial algorithm for the proposed continuous input. In an \(N\)-person approximation with \(N\mu_t\) people of type \(t\) and weights \(a_t/N\), its divergence does converge exactly to
\[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu\|\mu)=\sum_t\mu_ta_t\log a_t. \]
But the finite algorithm’s running time may be polynomial in \(N\), which can be exponential in the bit length of the rational masses. And the continuous plan ranges over integral bundles, giving a configuration problem whose possible bundles are not bounded by the input as stated. So the paper’s theorem does not deliver the proposed \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L,\log(1/\epsilon))\) guarantee.

That defeats a claimed *transfer of the algorithm*, but it does not defeat the mirror. A recurring programme with many recipients sharing valuation and welfare-weight types, and supplies scaled per capita, is a credible high-multiplicity regime. The type retains everything the objective uses; recipients get integral bundles; mass represents how many people receive each bundle. The allowed co-scaling of supplies makes this a population-continuous problem, not merely continuous outcomes for a finite society. A configuration or column-generation formulation is a natural next question, even if its pricing problem proves hard. The divisible-goods literature is not an existing continuous-population computational answer, and high-multiplicity work would support the regime rather than collide with it.

Theorem 1 is therefore a genuine named computational anchor. Theorem 2, which equates two relaxations, is not an additional complexity result. I can make the case that the proponent has not justified the proposed complexity bound; I cannot honestly make the universal case that no worthwhile mirror exists. That is where the negative case is weak: its only solid objection challenges an unproved algorithmic expectation, while the underlying continuous question survives.
