One could try to promote those results into computational questions. Computing the bound and intervals from a finite cost vector is already given by explicit formulas. Computing the characterized allocation is also a natural task, but the rule directly specifies each entry; asking for its continuous analogue would add a problem rather than mirror one of the paper’s results. Making rivers longer does not supply the missing population structure: in a single river, a region’s upstream position and local costs matter, so a complete type must preserve its position. Grouping repeated, identical river systems does permit multiplicity, but the systems are independent copies; their aggregate allocation just repeats the same calculation.

A continuous spatial model of a river could be a worthwhile project, but it would need its own input representation, computational question, and results. The paper’s discussion of networks, changing transfer rates, and alternative priors may motivate such an extension, yet it does not turn its axiomatic characterization into a computational result. I cannot prove that no useful continuous project could be built from these ideas. The narrower and stronger conclusion is that none of the paper’s named results supplies a ChoCo mirror without introducing a new computational problem.
