The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The claim that there is no computational theorem to mirror is accurate, but it does not rule out a worthwhile continuous mirror of the paper’s central result. Model 10 and the conclusion give a clear computational question: how should an institution assign decision procedures across issues to maximize acceptance among the electorate? The paper finds that the relative acceptance of direct democracy rises with issue importance, and recommends a mix of direct and representative decision-making based on the distribution of that importance.

A continuous version would represent voters by types that include their issue importance, agreement with the proposed outcome, and acceptance scores under each procedure. Given the mass of each type and a set of issues, choose a procedure for each issue to maximize aggregate acceptance. With no shared constraint, this reduces to picking the best procedure issue by issue. But the paper itself discusses voter fatigue, so a limit on how many issues can go to referendum gives a natural, nontrivial version: choose the issue procedures that maximize acceptance within that capacity. Its algorithmic questions could include exact, approximation, and parameterized methods as the type support or issue agenda grows.

This is a credible high-multiplicity regime: a large electorate can contain many voters with the same relevant response profile, and the procedure is applied to the electorate rather than targeted at named people. The experimental sample is small, but the paper’s subject is a state electorate of millions; the sample estimates acceptance values, while population masses describe the deployment setting.

The mirror is less direct than a complexity theorem transfer: the paper does not supply type-level counterfactual scores for every procedure, and the capacity constraint would need to be specified for a real application. Those are substantive limits on calibration and scope, not reasons the question is incoherent. The paper’s own conditional institutional recommendation supplies a stronger anchor than the proponent allows.
