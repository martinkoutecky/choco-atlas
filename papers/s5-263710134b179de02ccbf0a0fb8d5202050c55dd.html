The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The appended case raises no continuous mirror anchor, so there is nothing anchor-specific to defeat. Its strongest point is also the strongest case against a mirror here: this paper proves no computational complexity result. Proposition 1 counts all seedings; Propositions 2–7 and 18 count seedings with specified properties; Propositions 8–12 and 14–16 characterize particular seedings; Propositions 13 and 17 establish that certain combinations of properties are impossible. None classifies the complexity of a decision, optimization, or counting problem.

A large tournament with many entrants in a few strength classes is a plausible high-multiplicity setting. The paper’s strict ordering of entrants is not, by itself, a reason to reject that setting: one could define strength types, use quantiles, and allocate mass through a match tree. But asking whether such an allocation satisfies a mass version of delayed confrontation, or optimizing some new criterion over those allocations, would formulate a new computational problem. The paper gives no such problem or result to mirror. Its enumeration formulas might instead prompt an asymptotic study of property-respecting brackets, but that would be a different kind of question, not the computational complexity programme’s target.

So the negative case is strong for a precise reason: there is no named computational result here from which to build the requested mirror. I would claim that any such model would be an extension inspired by this paper, rather than a continuous mirror of one of its results.
