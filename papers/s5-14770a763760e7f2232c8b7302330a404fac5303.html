The continuous mirror question

Given finite worker-type and firm-type sets \(A,B\), rational masses \((\mu_a)_{a\in A}\), \((\nu_b)_{b\in B}\), and strict rankings over acceptable opposite-side types, find a stable mass matching. The variables \(x_{ab}\ge 0\), \(u_a\ge 0\), and \(v_b\ge 0\) must satisfy \(\sum_b x_{ab}+u_a=\mu_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}+v_b=\nu_b\), with \(x_{ab}=0\) for unacceptable pairs. Each unit of mass receives one integral partner or remains unmatched, while aggregate masses may be fractional. For each acceptable pair \((a,b)\), let \(R_{ab}=u_a+\sum_{c:\,b\succ_a c}x_{ac}\) and \(S_{ab}=v_b+\sum_{d:\,a\succ_b d}x_{db}\); require \(R_{ab}=0\) or \(S_{ab}=0\), so no positive masses on both sides prefer to match with each other.

The model it lives in

A finite-support, nonatomic version of the paper's zero-salary marriage market: types encode complete strict preferences and unit demand, rational masses give population shares, and \(x_{ab}\) records matched mass while each individual receives an integral match.

The objection that survived

Lemma 4.9 operates on individually indexed integral allocations; the proposed mutual-positive-mass blocking condition needs its own continuum stability definition, so the paper's algorithm does not itself establish an algorithm for the mirror.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers only the zero-salary, unit-demand marriage special case identified in Remark 1; it leaves the general \(M^\natural\)-concave multi-unit market and bounded side payments aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can stable mass matching for finite type support be computed in time polynomial in \(|A|+|B|\) and the binary input length of the masses and rankings?

The case FOR (proponent)

There is no named result here that classifies a problem as polynomial-time, NP-hard, FPT, or similar. The closest computational anchor is Lemma 4.9, proved in the paper: under (A′), the algorithm *Pairwise Stable* terminates after finitely many iterations. The algorithm constructs the outcome whose existence is asserted by Theorem 3.2: for M♮-concave agent utilities satisfying (A), and any permitted salary bounds, a pairwise strictly stable outcome exists, hence a pairwise stable one. The paper gives no input-size running-time bound, so this is a constructive existence result, not a claim that the search problem is in P.

My lead mirror is Mass-Stable Marriage. The paper explicitly includes the marriage model as a special case: set both salary bounds to zero, and restrict each agent to being unmatched or matched to one partner. The continuous instance has finite sets \(A\) and \(B\) of worker and firm types, rational masses \(\mu_a,\nu_b\), and a strict ranking of opposite-side types for each type. A type completely specifies an agent’s preference order, acceptability, and unit-demand constraint. This describes, for example, a large labor market where millions of workers and positions fall into tens or hundreds of standardized preference types.

A solution is a nonnegative matrix \(x_{ab}\) of matched mass and unmatched masses \(u_a,v_b\), satisfying
\[ \sum_b x_{ab}+u_a=\mu_a,\qquad \sum_a x_{ab}+v_b=\nu_b. \]
Set \(x_{ab}=0\) when the pair is unacceptable. Each unit of mass receives an integral outcome—one partner or no partner—while \(x_{ab}\) may be fractional because it records how much of each type is matched. For each acceptable pair \((a,b)\), let \(R_{ab}\) be the mass of type-\(a\) workers who prefer \(b\) to their current match, and \(S_{ab}\) the mass of type-\(b\) firms who prefer \(a\) to theirs. Stability requires \(R_{ab}=0\) or \(S_{ab}=0\) for every such pair. If both masses were positive, some worker and firm from them could block.

This is a credible mirror of the paper’s marriage-market special case: the population is the continuous object, and fractional quantities are aggregate masses of people with integral matches. The authors should recognize it as their problem because their Remark 1 identifies the zero-payment marriage model as a special case. I expect this version to be tractable: a deferred-acceptance procedure over types can propose and reject masses, with at most \(|A||B|\) type-pair proposals and rational arithmetic. That is a prediction for this mirror, not a complexity result proved in the paper.

The mirror covers only the one-to-one, zero-payment special case of Theorem 3.2. It does not capture the paper’s multi-unit M♮-concave utilities or bounded side payments. Those motivate the next questions: can the full model be compressed to type masses while preserving pairwise stability, and can the paper’s finite-termination algorithm be bounded in the support size and input bit length?

That restriction is the weak point. The paper’s distinctive market structure largely drops out, and this anchor cannot support a claim that discrete hardness dissolves under continuization: the paper provides no hardness result to compare against. It does, however, give a faithful and concrete population-level search problem based on a named special case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent has found the paper’s only plausible anchor, but it is a weak bridge to a computational-complexity result. Theorem 3.2 proves existence; Lemma 4.9 proves that the constructive algorithm terminates. The paper gives no running-time bound in an encoded input size, and does not formulate a complexity problem for computing a stable outcome. A question about the algorithm’s dependence on type support and binary input length would be worthwhile in its own right, but it would add a computational agenda the paper never states.

The proposed marriage mirror also sits close to existing continuum matching work. A continuum model with a measure of student types, finite-capacity schools, stable outcomes, and a deferred-acceptance analogue already gives a population-level treatment of this unit-demand market; aggregating identical firms into capacity types makes the overlap especially close. [Arnosti’s continuum stable-matching model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12881) therefore weakens the claim that this particular mirror opens a new modeling question. Its deferred-acceptance iteration need not finish in finitely many steps, so it does not settle the proposed finite-support complexity question. It also does not cover the paper’s general \(M^\natural\)-concave utilities and pair-specific salary bounds.

That gap is the limit of the negative case. A mass model of the full market can preserve integral contracts for each agent while allowing fractional aggregate allocations, and repeated worker and firm cohorts are a plausible regime. The paper’s individual-agent indexing does not rule that out under the programme’s definition of type. I can argue that the marriage anchor is overfamiliar and that the source offers no complexity theorem to mirror; I cannot honestly argue that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. The high-multiplicity version of the full \(M^\natural\)-concave stability problem remains a credible open computational question.
