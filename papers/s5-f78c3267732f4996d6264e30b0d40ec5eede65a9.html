The continuous mirror question

Given a finite rational location set \(T\), rational distributions \(\alpha\) and \(\mu\) over \(T\), and a rational share \(\lambda\in[0,1]\) for the reporting group, find \(y\in\mathbb{R}\) minimizing \(C(y)=\sum_{t\in T}(\lambda\alpha_t+(1-\lambda)\mu_t)|t-y|\).

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity one-facility location problem with types \(t\in T\) recording location and group role, and rational type masses \(\lambda\alpha_t\) for reporters and \((1-\lambda)\mu_t\) for future users. The objective is total mass-weighted absolute distance; a weighted median minimizes it.

The paper already aggregates reporting agents through their empirical distribution, so the mirror adds a compressed mass input more than a new objective; this limits its conceptual distance and novelty.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest mirror is the paper’s optimal one-facility location problem, extended so both groups are represented by type masses. A plausible regime is a city planning a public facility for millions of residents: a consultation group and future users are concentrated in a much smaller number \(\tau\) of neighborhood-location types, with \(\tau\ll n\). A type records a location and its role in the model; everyone has the paper’s absolute-distance cost.

Call the problem High-Multiplicity FLPAA Median. An instance gives a finite rational location set \(T\), rational distributions \(\alpha\) and \(\mu\) over \(T\), and a rational share \(\lambda\) of the population in the reporting group. The remaining share follows \(\mu\). The combined population is \(\nu_t=\lambda\alpha_t+(1-\lambda)\mu_t\). Find a location \(y\) minimizing \(C(y)=\sum_{t\in T}\nu_t|t-y|\); any weighted median of \(\nu\) is a solution. With the masses given as binary rationals, sorting the locations and scanning cumulative mass solves the problem in time polynomial in \(\tau\) and the input bit length. The problem preserves the paper’s aggregate travel-cost objective while replacing the reporting list with a high-multiplicity description.

The anchor is Theorem 2, proved in this paper. It states that, given \(n,n_r,n_u\) and \(\mu\), for odd \(n\) the set \(X\cup F_{n_u}\) contains an optimal solution for every report vector \(\vec x\), where \(X=\{x_i\}\) and \(F_{n_u}=\{F_\mu^{[-1]}((2j-1)/(2n_u)):j\in[n_u]\}\). For even \(n\), the quantiles in \(F_{n_u}\) are \(F_\mu^{[-1]}(j/n_u)\). The theorem concludes that Problem 1 is solvable in polynomial time. Its finite-report case is the empirical-distribution special case of the mirror; the weighted-median algorithm solves the mass-valued extension. If one insists on the paper’s absolute-continuity assumption, finite-type masses can be represented by arbitrarily narrow continuous distributions around their locations.

I expect Class A: tractable. The obstacle here is locating a weighted median, and the explicit finite-type input makes that computation well-defined in the bit model. This mirrors the paper’s optimum computation, not its mechanism-design results: individual truthfulness has no direct mass-level counterpart without defining how a positive-mass type can deviate.

The soft spot is substantial. The paper already describes \(\mu\) as a population distribution, and its additive expected cost equals the cost of a continuum mass with distribution \(\mu\). Continuizing only the aleatory group would therefore largely restate its model. The distinct ChoCo question is the extension to type masses in the reporting group as well. That is a plausible high-multiplicity version of the authors’ optimization problem, but it does not by itself produce a new hardness-versus-tractability result; Theorem 2 already supplies the finite-input tractability anchor.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is a narrow collision with the optimization anchor. For that result, the identities of the reporting agents disappear once their empirical distribution is formed. Writing \(\eta_{\vec x}=\frac{1}{n_r}\sum_i\delta_{x_i}\), the paper’s objective is \(n\int |z-y|\,d\nu(z)\), where \(\nu=\lambda\eta_{\vec x}+(1-\lambda)\mu\). Theorem 1 already characterizes its minimizers as medians of this population measure, and Theorem 2 gives a finite candidate set and a polynomial-time solution. Replacing \(\eta_{\vec x}\) with rational type masses \(\alpha\) changes the empirical CDF to a weighted CDF; the same characterization applies. The paper’s individual strategic agents matter for its mechanism results, but those are not the proponent’s anchor.

That makes the proposed mirror look like the paper’s existing aggregate optimization model with a different input representation. But I can’t honestly make that a decisive objection under ChoCo’s gate. The paper does not establish a running-time bound in \(\tau\) and bit length \(L\); its polynomial-time result takes an explicit report vector. A compressed mass input is a sensible high-multiplicity regime, and the mirror asks a named computational question. Its easy weighted-median solution is not grounds to reject it. I see no sound identity, degeneration, or multiplicity objection, so this anchor survives my negative case.
