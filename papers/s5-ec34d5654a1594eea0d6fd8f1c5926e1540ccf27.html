The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The appended “case for” actually makes the negative case: it offers no continuous mirror anchor to defeat. The paper’s clearest proved result, Proposition 10, bounds the diameter of certain stable trees by \(8\). That is a structural theorem, not a complexity result. Lemma 9 and Proposition 4 give related structural and payoff results; Theorem 1 is an axiomatic characterization. Conjecture 11 is not a proved result. Turning any of these into “compute or decide something about equilibria” would invent a computational task the paper does not pose.

There is a deeper obstacle to making that invention a faithful mirror. Strong pairwise stability depends on individual link deletions and bilateral link additions; Myerson payoffs depend on the graph’s paths and intermediaries. A distribution over agent types records neither who is linked to whom nor the resulting network positions. A continuum graph or graphon could retain that structure, but then the model’s central object is a continuum of edges and neighborhoods, not simply a society represented by type masses. In a regular atomless model, changing one agent’s links changes a measure-zero part of the graph; treating positive mass as making the change instead changes pairwise deviations into bloc deviations. Either route alters the strategic question.

One could propose a more specific regime, such as many interchangeable leaves attached to a hub. That makes multiplicity plausible, and it should not be dismissed merely because the paper uses named agents. But it still requires a careful extension of individual link costs, pairwise deviations, and Myerson allocation to the limit. The resulting question would be new continuum network-game theory, with no computational result in this paper to mirror.

So the negative case is strong against treating this paper as a ChoCo computational mirror: it has no named computational result, and its equilibrium analysis relies on topology and individual deviations that type masses do not preserve. It is weaker as the universal claim that no worthwhile scenario could ever exist. A well-designed continuum network model may be possible, but this paper does not give a computational anchor for it.
