The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent offers no positive anchor, and for this paper that is the strongest case against. Theorem 3.5 characterizes Nash equilibria; Theorem 4.5 characterizes Pareto optimal Nash equilibria; Theorem 4.8 relates those to strong equilibria; and Theorem 5.1 characterizes a social choice correspondence axiomatically. None states a complexity result for a computational problem. Moreover, the paper allows arbitrary single-dipped preference relations and arbitrary mechanisms in a broad class, without giving them finite encodings. To ask for running times, one must first choose what information about preferences and mechanisms is input and what task an algorithm must perform. That would be a newly specified computational problem, not a mirror of a complexity claim in the paper.

There is a plausible better mirror than simply replacing \(N\) with a continuum: model a large workforce by a distribution over complete preference types, and study which positive-mass coalition can share the commodity without its members wanting to leave. That is a sensible high-multiplicity scenario, so finite \(N\) and divisible outcomes are not objections by themselves. But the paper’s maximal coalitions depend on finite membership and shares \(1/|S|\). In a continuum model, a coalition of mass \(q\) receives share \(1/q\); individual deviations may have zero effect on aggregate reports, while blocking must be defined for measurable positive-mass coalitions. The authors could recognize that as an interesting extension, but it is a new equilibrium model, not a computational question supplied by the paper.

One could then define a computational task, such as finding a stable mass coalition from a finite-support rational distribution. That would require choosing representations for the preferences and mechanism, as well as an equilibrium notion for the continuum. With natural cutoff data, the task may reduce to threshold comparisons; a more complicated computational challenge would depend on choices added to the model. Either way, the paper gives no complexity result or computational bottleneck for the ChoCo programme to chart.

So the negative case is strong as a judgment about programme fit: this paper is a poor target for a computational continuization. Its weak point is the word “any.” A continuum equilibrium theory built around its mechanisms could be worthwhile in its own right; the available argument does not rule out every such project. It does show why that possibility is not yet a continuous computational mirror of a result in this paper.
