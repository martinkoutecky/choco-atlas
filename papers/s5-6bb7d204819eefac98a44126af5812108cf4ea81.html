The case FOR (proponent)

The paper contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a complexity result. It calls the three SMTI variants “hard” and cites earlier work, but does not state those claims as numbered results of this paper. Its own contributions are solver formulations and empirical comparisons. So there are no eligible anchors under your rules; treating the timing tables as a complexity result would overstate the paper.

The strongest plausible mirror, offered only as an unanchored candidate, is Fractional-Type Max-Cardinality SMTI. Take finite sets of male and female preference types, each with rational mass summing to one. A type specifies a complete weak preference order over acceptable types on the other side, including ties. Let \(x_{ab}\geq 0\) be the mass of male type \(a\) matched to female type \(b\), with the total matched mass of each type no greater than its supply. For each mutually acceptable pair \((a,b)\), define
\[ u_a= \mu_a-\sum_b x_{ab},\qquad v_b= \nu_b-\sum_a x_{ab}. \]
Require weak stability by forbidding positive mass on both sides that would strictly prefer each other:
\[ \left(u_a+\sum_{b':\,b\succ_a b'}x_{ab'}\right) \left(v_b+\sum_{a':\,a\succ_b a'}x_{a'b}\right)=0. \]
The question is to find a rational matching flow \(x\) satisfying those constraints that maximizes \(\sum_{a,b}x_{ab}\).

A plausible regime is a large recurring placement market: many applicants and positions, with a modest number of role or qualification types whose members share rankings and acceptability. Mass is the population fraction in each type; \(x\) is the fraction placed between two types. This preserves the paper’s max-cardinality objective and weak stability while making the population large relative to its type support. The paper’s Max Cardinality ILP formulation and experiments are the closest material this mirrors, but neither is an eligible numbered anchor.

For this candidate, I would leave the complexity class open. A direct hardness transfer from finite SMTI is not justified: fluid mass can split across matches, and the stability constraints have complementarity structure. Whether that structure yields a tractable optimization method, or leaves the problem hard, is a natural follow-up; further questions include when the fractional optimum agrees with finite high-multiplicity instances and whether the other two fairness objectives admit similar formulations.

The weak point is that this regime groups partners by type and removes identity-level distinctions. It is plausible for category-based markets, and ties make those categories natural to represent, but it is not the random instance regime tested in the paper. Most importantly, without a qualifying named result, this remains a reasonable research question rather than a positive case anchored in the paper’s stated results.
