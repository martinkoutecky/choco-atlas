The case FOR (proponent)

The paper contains no named computational-complexity result: its numbered results concern allocation, core, equilibrium, and price decentralization, not whether a problem is in P, NP-hard, or otherwise computationally classified. So I cannot honestly supply a paper-result anchor or claim that this mirror is what one of its theorems establishes.

The strongest positive case is that a continuous population is a natural model for the economies the paper studies. A plausible scenario is a large market with many agents but relatively few complete economic types: each type fixes preferences, endowment, and relevant club characteristics. Let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of society of type \(t\). A candidate computational problem is Continuous Club Blocking. Its input is such a society, an incumbent allocation with utility \(u_t^0\) for each type, and an explicit finite list of club configurations. Each configuration \(k\) specifies its number \(a_{tk}\) of members of each type, its resource requirements, and the utility \(u_{tk}\) its members receive. Ask whether there are nonnegative masses \(\lambda_k\), not all zero, such that
\[ \sum_k a_{tk}\lambda_k\le \mu_t \]
for every type, the selected clubs’ resource requirements are covered by the participating agents’ endowments, and \(u_{tk}>u_t^0\) for every type with a seat in a selected configuration. A solution is the vector \(\lambda\), giving a feasible blocking coalition and its club formation. Equivalently, maximize the participating mass subject to those constraints and ask whether the optimum is positive.

That explicit-configuration version is an LP, so I would expect it to be tractable. The more substantial follow-up is whether club configurations can be generated efficiently when they are implicit; that becomes a pricing or separation question. This proposed mirror covers the paper’s core and cooperative-allocation analysis, not every equilibrium or decentralization result.

Its weakest point is also decisive for the grading: this is a plausible computational question suggested by the paper, not a complexity question anchored by a named result in it. The explicit configuration list also makes the LP claim narrower than the paper’s full economic model. So the positive case is that the high-multiplicity regime makes sense and gives a concrete continuous problem; it cannot claim that the paper itself supplies the required computational anchor.
