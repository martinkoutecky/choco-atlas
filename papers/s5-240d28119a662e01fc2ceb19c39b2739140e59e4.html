The case FOR (proponent)

There is no qualifying anchor in this paper. It has Definition 2.1, Assumptions 3.1 and 3.2, and numbered algorithms, but no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result such as hardness or tractability. The closest computational object is Algorithm 2: it uses observed reports and a quantal-response rule in a Gibbs sampler to estimate the causal effect on truthfulness, with exchangeability reducing agents’ relevant behavior to the number of truthful agents. That is a method, not a complexity result of the kind your anchor rule requires. So I cannot honestly offer a result-anchored mirror.

The strongest case for a *prospective* mirror is the paper’s causal estimand. Definition 2.1 already averages individual truthfulness differences across the population, and Assumption 3.2 already invokes exchangeability. In a large advertising platform experiment, for example, many advertisers could share the same mechanism-relevant type: valuation and budget class, product market, report and deviation rules, and payoff parameters. If there are \(N\) advertisers but only \(\tau\) such types, with \(\tau \ll N\), type mass is a natural description. The action remains truthful reporting or the specified deviation; the mechanisms and their allocation outcomes can remain discrete. This continuizes the population, not the outcome space.

One precise follow-on question would be Continuum QRE Causal-Effect Estimation. An instance gives a finite type set \(\Theta\), rational masses \(\mu_\theta\), two mechanisms, type-dependent report distributions \(f_\theta\) and \(g_\theta\) under truthful and deviating behavior, observed report counts \(n_z(r)\) for each mechanism \(z\), a parameter \(\beta\), and utility-difference functions \(D_{z\theta}(q_z)\) computable from the type-level behavior profile. Let \(q_{z\theta}\) be the fraction of type-\(\theta\) mass that reports truthfully under mechanism \(z\). Require the continuum logit response equations
\[ q_{z\theta}=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-\beta D_{z\theta}(q_z))}. \]
The predicted report distribution is
\[ p_z(r\mid q_z)=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta \bigl(q_{z\theta}f_\theta(r)+(1-q_{z\theta})g_\theta(r)\bigr). \]
The task is to find a QRE-consistent profile maximizing
\[ \sum_{z\in\{0,1\}}\sum_r n_z(r)\log p_z(r\mid q_z), \]
and return the resulting causal contrast
\[ \widehat{\Delta}=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\mu_\theta(q_{1\theta}-q_{0\theta}). \]
This is a specified equilibrium-constrained, maximum-likelihood version of the paper’s inference problem. It is a plausible follow-on, but it is not a theorem or algorithmic guarantee established in the paper, and it changes Algorithm 2’s sampling-based prior into an equilibrium constraint.

I would provisionally expect the unrestricted problem to face continuum-specific difficulty from the nonlinear equilibrium constraints and likelihood optimization; under fixed \(\tau\) and suitable uniqueness or monotonicity conditions, there may be a tractable regime. That is a research conjecture, not a classification supported by an anchor here. Natural next questions are how the complexity depends on \(\tau\) and input precision, whether the estimate is identifiable when \(f_\theta\) and \(g_\theta\) overlap, and what finite-population approximation guarantees hold.

The scope of this candidate is only inference about truthfulness effects from early reports under a game-theoretic behavior model. The authors could plausibly recognize it as a population limit of their estimand and exchangeability setup. Its weakest point is substantial: the paper models finitely many individually indexed agents, and does not prove that its Gibbs-sampling prior converges to this type-level QRE problem. The kidney-exchange example also has relatively few, heterogeneous hospitals, so the more convincing high-multiplicity scenario would be a large platform experiment—not the paper’s own application.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is strongest at the programme’s anchor requirement: this paper has no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition establishing the complexity of a computational problem. Definition 2.1 defines an estimand; the algorithms describe inference procedures; the results compare estimates in simulations. The paper gives no runtime bound, correctness guarantee, convergence result, or complexity classification for those procedures. There is therefore no computational result here to continuize.

The proposed QRE estimation problem does not supply one. It changes the paper’s method: the paper uses a game-theoretic prior over finite agents’ latent potential outcomes and a Gibbs sampler; the proposal imposes fixed-point equations on type-level truthfulness rates and maximizes a likelihood subject to them. That is a possible new estimation problem, but it is not a population limit of a computational guarantee the paper proves. It also leaves out the paper’s central time distinction: estimating \(\Delta(100)\) from reports at \(t=5\) requires a model of how behavior evolves between those rounds. Adding that model could make the problem better specified, but its computational questions would be new ones.

A continuum version that preserves the paper’s likelihood and posterior more closely is possible too. Yet the paper establishes no finite-population complexity result for that inference task, so a continuous version would still be a new algorithmic study rather than a mirror of a named result.

I cannot make the broader claim that no worthwhile scenario exists. A large platform with many advertisers in a modest number of mechanism-relevant types is plausible, and the paper’s average truthfulness contrast could be expressed using type masses. That is the strongest weakness in the negative case: such a project might be worthwhile. But its promise cannot turn this paper’s simulations and methods into a result-anchored continuous mirror.
