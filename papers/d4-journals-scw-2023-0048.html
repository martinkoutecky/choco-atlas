The continuous mirror question

Given rational support points \(x_1,\ldots,x_\tau\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), rational masses \(\mu_j\ge0\) summing to \(1\), and a precision parameter \(\varepsilon\), compute the lower weighted coordinate-wise median \(c_\mu\), an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal geometric median, and \(R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)=\frac{\sum_j\mu_j\lVert c_\mu-x_j\rVert_2}{\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^2}\sum_j\mu_j\lVert y-x_j\rVert_2}\); then determine \(\sup_\mu R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu)\) and provide a witness family.

The model it lives in

Types are ideal points \(x_j\in\mathbb{Q}^2\) carrying masses \(\mu_j\). The fixed decision rule is the coordinate-wise lower weighted median \(c_\mu\); the benchmark decision variable is \(y\in\mathbb{R}^2\), minimizing the mass-weighted minisum objective \(\mathrm{SC}_1(y;\mu)=\sum_j\mu_j\lVert y-x_j\rVert_2\).

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a continuous mirror of the paper’s minisum approximation problem, not of its strategic-mechanism theorem.

A caveat comes first: this paper contains no named NP-hardness, membership-in-\(\mathrm P\), FPT, or similar complexity result. I therefore cannot honestly present it as a hardness-transfer case. The best available anchors are exact quantitative optimization results.

My lead anchor is Theorem 2, proved in this paper. It states that for \(X=\mathbb R^2\), an odd number \(n\) of agents, and minisum social cost
\[ \mathrm{sc}(y,x)=\sum_{i=1}^n\|y-x_i\|_2, \]
the coordinate-wise median mechanism has worst-case approximation ratio
\[ \mathrm{AR}(\mathrm{CM}) = \sqrt{2}\,\frac{\sqrt{n^2+1}}{n+1}. \]
Thus the paper exactly computes how well coordinate-wise median approximates the geometric-median optimum.

The natural mirror is Continuous Minisum CM-Ratio. A society is a finite-support distribution
\[ \mu=\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j\delta_{x_j}, \qquad x_j\in\mathbb R^2,\quad \mu_j\ge 0,\quad \sum_j\mu_j=1. \]
A type is an ideal point \(x_j=(a_j,b_j)\); \(\mu_j\) is the fraction of residents sharing it. The intended regime is a city with millions of residents but perhaps tens or hundreds of neighbourhood/cohort types, so \(\tau\ll N\). Residents of one type have identical location preferences and are interchangeable for the problem.

Fix the lower weighted median
\[ q^-_{1/2}(a) = \inf\left\{z:\sum_{j:a_j\le z}\mu_j\ge \frac12\right\}, \]
and similarly for \(b\). The continuous coordinate-wise median is
\[ c_\mu= \left(q^-_{1/2}(a),q^-_{1/2}(b)\right). \]
For a facility location \(y\in\mathbb R^2\), define
\[ \mathrm{SC}_1(y;\mu) = \sum_{j=1}^{\tau}\mu_j\|y-x_j\|_2, \qquad \mathrm{OPT}_1(\mu) = \min_{y\in\mathbb R^2}\mathrm{SC}_1(y;\mu). \]
The instance consists of rational support points, rational masses, and a precision parameter \(\varepsilon\). The task is to output \(c_\mu\), an \(\varepsilon\)-optimal geometric median, and an \(\varepsilon\)-accurate value of
\[ R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu) = \frac{\mathrm{SC}_1(c_\mu;\mu)}{\mathrm{OPT}_1(\mu)}. \]
Its worst-case version asks for
\[ \rho^*=\sup_\mu R_{\mathrm{CM}}(\mu), \]
together with a matching witness or limiting family and an upper-bound certificate.

This is recognisably the authors’ problem: same facility, same Euclidean ideal points, same coordinate-wise median, same minisum objective, same geometric-median benchmark, and the same worst-case ratio. Only the electorate has changed from a list of agents to masses. If \(\mu_j=k_j/N\), expanding type \(j\) into \(k_j\) identical agents recovers a discrete profile with exactly the same normalized costs and ratio. Conversely, every finite profile is its empirical distribution. This is a genuine high-multiplicity dictionary, not merely an analogy.

I would expect this mirror to be Class A. Weighted coordinate-wise medians are obtained by sorting the \(\tau\) coordinates, while the geometric-median benchmark is a constant-dimensional convex optimization problem. The finite theorem suggests
\[ \lim_{n\to\infty} \sqrt{2}\,\frac{\sqrt{n^2+1}}{n+1} = \sqrt{2}. \]
That value also has a direct continuous witness: take half the mass at \((1,0)\) and half at \((0,1)\), with lower-median tie-breaking. The coordinate-wise median is \((0,0)\), whose average distance is \(1\), while the optimum average distance is \(1/\sqrt2\), giving ratio \(\sqrt2\). The matching upper bound is the continuous extension of the paper’s dimension-\(2\) coordinate-median bound. Tie-breaking can alternatively be avoided by using a three-type sequence with vanishing third mass.

A second worthwhile anchor is Theorem 3, also proved here. It states that for \(p\ge2\),
\[ 2^{1-1/p} \le \sup_{n\in\mathbb N}\mathrm{AR}(\mathrm{CM}) \le 2^{3/2-2/p}. \]
Corollary 6 establishes equality \(\sqrt2\) for \(p=2\); Corollary 7 establishes equality \(2\) for \(p=\infty\); and Conjecture 8 proposes equality
\[ \sup_n\mathrm{AR}(\mathrm{CM})=2^{1-1/p} \]
for every \(p\ge2\).

Its continuous counterpart is the same problem with
\[ \mathrm{SC}_p(y;\mu) = \left(\sum_j\mu_j\|y-x_j\|_2^p\right)^{1/p}, \]
or the essential maximum for \(p=\infty\). The task is to compute the ratio for a given distribution and determine its worst-case value. I would expect the exact continuous answers \(\sqrt2\) for \(p=2\), \(2\) for \(p=\infty\), and conjecturally \(2^{1-1/p}\) in general. The per-instance problem remains tractable by convex optimization; the interesting open question is the global sharp bound.

This mirror does not cover Theorem 1’s full claim that coordinate-wise median is optimal among deterministic, anonymous, strategyproof mechanisms. In a genuinely nonatomic society, one individual has zero mass, so individual strategyproofness can become vacuous. Preserving that part would require an additional finite-population-limit or positive-mass-coalition model. That is the weakest point of the case, and it is substantial.

Nevertheless, it does not undermine the lead mirror. Theorem 2’s central object is the welfare performance of a fixed mechanism, and that transfers cleanly from counts to population masses. The paper’s \(\mathbb R^2\) domain is continuous already, but its society is not; the proposed mirror changes precisely the population object that the ChoCo programme is meant to continuize.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is not that a high-multiplicity facility-location society is incoherent. It is coherent. The problem is that this paper supplies no qualifying computational result to mirror.

Theorem 2, Theorem 3, and their corollaries are approximation analyses, not complexity or algorithmic results. They do not define an input-output problem whose worst-case complexity is studied, nor give an exact, approximation, or parameterized algorithm. The proponent’s tasks—compute a weighted median, approximate a geometric median, and determine a supremum over distributions—are new problems added to the paper, not continuous versions of computational results in it. That matters under ChoCo’s stated remit.

Theorem 2 nevertheless gives the strongest possible case for the proponent, and it is a legitimate high-multiplicity model. If
\[ \mu=\sum_j \mu_j\delta_{x_j}, \]
then
\[ \mathrm{SC}_1(y;\mu)=\sum_j\mu_j\|y-x_j\|_2 \]
is exactly the normalized minisum cost of a discrete profile with repeated ideal points whenever the \(\mu_j\) are rational. Thus I would not object that facility-location agents cannot have repeated types, or that the population must be finite. A city with many residents in a small number of spatial cohorts is a sensible regime.

That concession also identifies the limitation. The proposed mirror is a measure-theoretic reformulation of the paper’s welfare calculation, not a computational-social-choice problem whose complexity landscape is exposed by continuization. The weighted coordinate-wise median and the geometric-median benchmark are already the natural aggregate objects. Asking for their ratio over a distribution may be worthwhile analysis, but it does not supply the missing computational phenomenon that motivates ChoCo.

The obvious stronger version—retain the strategic mechanism-design content—does not repair this. In a nonatomic society, one agent’s report has zero mass and cannot change the distribution, so individual strategyproofness becomes vacuous. If reports by an entire positive-mass type or coalition are allowed instead, the model requires a new coalitional strategyproofness concept and is no longer the theorem proved in the paper. If one drops strategyproofness and keeps only the welfare ratio, one is back to the proponent’s non-computational restatement.

The same objection defeats the Theorem 3 anchor. For \(p\ge2\), the continuous objective would be
\[ \mathrm{SC}_p(y;\mu) = \left(\sum_j\mu_j\|y-x_j\|_2^p\right)^{1/p}, \]
with the essential maximum when \(p=\infty\). This is again a sensible distributional model, and rational masses again reproduce repeated-agent profiles exactly. But Theorem 3 is only a bound on a fixed mechanism’s worst-case ratio, while Corollaries 6 and 7 identify two special values and Conjecture 8 proposes the general value. A continuous version would therefore be an extremal analysis problem, not a continuous counterpart of a computational result. Making the support, \(p\), and precision part of the input would create a new convex-optimization problem; it would not reveal a computational question already present in the paper.

So the negative case is strong only at the programme’s gatekeeping level: this paper contains no named computational result of the kind ChoCo is designed to continuize. It is not honest to claim that no worthwhile continuous population model exists here. The proponent has found a natural one, and if ChoCo includes distributional approximation-mechanism analysis, Theorem 2 in particular survives. Under the narrower computational mandate, however, neither proposed anchor establishes a worthwhile mirror of this paper.
