The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s only named anchor, Corollary 16, is not a computational result. It uniquely characterizes \(wH_L\) through axioms. Equations (18)–(20) give a formula and a worked example, but the paper states no computational problem, input encoding, complexity bound, algorithm, or hardness result. Turning Corollary 16 into a complexity anchor would change what the paper proves.

The strongest repair is the proponent’s large-firm scenario: many interchangeable workers in a few roles, perhaps under a manager, with coalition worth depending on role masses. That could motivate a separate question about computing type-level payoff densities. But it requires choices the paper does not make: how coalition worth is represented as a function of masses, how workers’ links to individual managers are encoded, and how the finite Shapley-based allocation is scaled into a continuum. A type distribution alone may lose the parent-child structure that drives the hierarchy’s reallocations. Preserving that structure could require an additional hierarchy or matching object. These are plausible modelling choices, but they create a new computational project rather than mirror a result here.

The encoding issue matters too. With the full coalition-worth table as input, the Shapley-based calculations can be done in time polynomial in the table’s length. To pose a harder computational question, one would need a succinct representation of coalition worth or a specified restricted game class; the paper supplies neither, and its generalization allows any efficient CO value. On a continuous population, a computational question would additionally need a finite representation of the worth function. Its complexity would depend on these added choices.

So the best negative case is that there is no computational finding here to continuize, and the most credible workforce model would need substantial new structure before it even defines a computational problem. I would not claim that such a model could never be worthwhile; the universal “no scenario” claim is weaker. But any resulting value-computation problem would be an extension of this axiomatic paper, not a continuous mirror of one of its results.
