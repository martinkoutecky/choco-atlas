The continuous mirror question

Given a nonempty \(k\)-uniform hypergraph \(H=(V,E)\) and nonnegative rational weights \(w_e\), form a unit-mass population with one type \(e\) of mass \(1/|E|\) and valuation \(v_e(S)=w_e\) if \(e\subseteq S\), and \(0\) otherwise; give each item category \(v\) supply \(1/|E|\). Choose masses \(x_{e,S}\ge 0\) of type \(e\) receiving each integral bundle \(S\subseteq V\), with \(\sum_{S\subseteq V}x_{e,S}=1/|E|\) and \(\sum_{e\in E,\,S\ni v}x_{e,S}=1/|E|\) for every \(v\in V\), to maximize \(\sum_{e\in E,\,S\subseteq V}x_{e,S}v_e(S)\).

The model it lives in

There is one buyer type per hyperedge, with mass \(1/|E|\), value \(w_e\) for any bundle containing \(e\), and zero value otherwise. Each item category has supply \(1/|E|\); the decision assigns type mass to integral bundles. Equivalently, choose the mass \(y_e\) of type \(e\) receiving its full edge, maximizing \(\sum_{e\in E}w_e y_e\) subject to \(\sum_{e\ni v}y_e\le 1/|E|\) for each \(v\) and \(0\le y_e\le 1/|E|\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Proposition 2.2's single-minded valuation subclass. It leaves aside Theorems 2.5 and 2.6 and the paper's approximation results for general valuation functions.

Open questions for a prover

  • What sharp finite-\(R\) error bound relates the normalized welfare optimum of an \(R\)-fold discrete replication to the continuous LP optimum?
  • Which broader succinct valuation classes retain a polynomial-size LP or an efficient pricing algorithm in this repeated-buyer model?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead is a high-multiplicity mirror of Proposition 2.2. Proved in this paper, it gives an approximation-preserving reduction from maximum weighted \(k\)-set packing to welfare maximization with dependency degree at most \(k-1\): for every hyperedge \(e\), create an item for each vertex and a player whose valuation is \(w(e)\) exactly when the player receives every item in \(e\), and \(0\) otherwise. A welfare allocation and a set packing have the same value. The paper notes that weighted \(k\)-set packing is NP-hard for \(k>2\) and hard to approximate; that hardness is prior work, while the reduction is proved here.

The continuous problem — Single-Minded Welfare Maximization\(_\infty\). An instance has a \(k\)-uniform hypergraph \((V,E)\), nonnegative rational weights \(w_e\), and a population of mass \(1\). There is one type for each \(e\in E\), with mass \(\mu_e=1/|E|\) and valuation \(v_e(S)=w_e\) if \(e\subseteq S\), and \(0\) otherwise. Each person receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq V\); let \(x_{e,S}\) be the mass of type \(e\) receiving \(S\). Each item category \(v\in V\) has supply \(1/|E|\), and all supply must be allocated. Find a feasible \(x\) maximizing \(\sum_{e,S}x_{e,S}v_e(S)\). This is a population of repeated buyers with shared preferences and values; the per-capita supply scales with the population.

The allocation can be optimized by choosing \(y_e\), the mass of type \(e\) that receives a bundle containing \(e\). The resulting problem is the fractional set-packing LP:

\[ \max \sum_{e\in E} w_e y_e \quad\text{subject to}\quad \sum_{e\ni v}y_e\le \frac{1}{|E|}\ \ (v\in V),\qquad 0\le y_e\le \frac{1}{|E|}. \]

The remaining item mass can be assigned without lowering anyone’s value, since valuations are monotone. The type bounds are also implied by the item constraints when \(k\ge1\). Thus an optimal mass allocation can be recovered from a polynomial-size LP. I expect this mirror to be Class A: the discrete packing hardness is replaced by fractional packing over the mass of each type.

The regime is \(R\) repeated buyers of each type, so \(N=R|E|\) buyers and \(\tau=|E|\) types, with \(R\) large; the supply of each item category is replicated proportionally. This could describe repeated procurement buyers with the same package requirements. Each buyer still receives an integral bundle. What becomes fractional is how much of a type’s population receives each bundle.

This covers Proposition 2.2’s single-minded welfare subclass, not welfare maximization for arbitrary valuations or the paper’s general approximation theorems. The weak point is that scaling item supplies as well as the population changes the scarcity regime from the paper’s finite auction. The authors should still recognize the result as a high-multiplicity version of their allocation problem: buyer types are complete preference descriptions, type mass can split across integral bundles, and per-capita supply scales with the population. Further questions include whether this LP remains tractable for broader valuation classes, and how its solutions approximate finite replicated auctions.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against Proposition 2.2 is that its natural high-multiplicity limit depends on changing the goods along with the population. If we simply repeat each edge-type buyer while keeping the original stock of items fixed, only finitely many people receive anything. Per-capita welfare tends to zero; without normalization, the limit reduces to choosing which types receive the fixed items. That is a degenerate population limit.

The proponent’s scaled-supply version avoids that problem: repeat each buyer type and each item category in proportion to the population. But that is a replicated, multi-unit market, whereas the paper’s auction has a fixed set of indivisible items, each allocated once. The proposed LP is then fractional set packing. This makes the mirror’s connection to the paper less direct, and its computation less ambitious: the LP itself is familiar.

That objection is weak under ChoCo’s stated rules, though. Scaling per-capita supply is an allowed co-modification, and repeated buyers with repeated goods form a sensible high-multiplicity regime. The mass allocation still assigns integral bundles to each unit of population; only the amount of each type receiving each bundle is fractional. The authors could plausibly recognize this as their welfare-allocation problem in a replicated economy. And the fact that the resulting LP is familiar limits novelty but does not invalidate a well-posed mirror.

So I can identify a real weakness in the fixed-stock version, but I cannot honestly defeat the scaled-supply version. This anchor survives the strongest case against it.
