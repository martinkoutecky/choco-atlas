The case FOR (proponent)

There is a sensible continuous population model here, but the paper gives no qualifying computational anchor for it.

A natural type is a complete ranking of the alternatives, and its mass is the fraction of society with that ranking. Given a finite support of \(\tau\) rankings with rational masses summing to one, define each Borda score by averaging over those masses. Then apply the paper’s elimination rule recursively to the surviving alternatives. The output is the sequence of elimination tiers, hence the complete social ranking. This is a credible high-multiplicity version of the paper’s model: populations may be large while the number of distinct rankings is moderate.

For example, one could ask for Continuous Baldwin Ranking: given \(m\) alternatives and a rational distribution over \(\tau\) ranking types, compute the full ranking produced by repeatedly eliminating the alternatives with minimum Borda score. The strict Nanson and Nanson rules have equally direct population versions. These questions seem recognizable to the authors because they preserve the rule’s recursive Borda decisions and complete-ranking output. Direct score computation at each round appears polynomial in \(m,\tau\), and the mass bit length, but that would be a new computational observation—not a result this paper establishes.

The obstacle is the required anchor. The numbered results here—such as Theorems 1–3 and Propositions 1–3—concern axiomatic characterizations and Condorcet consistency. None asserts a computational complexity result or presents a formulation that the paper computes with. The NP-hardness results mentioned in the related literature are cited from other papers, so they cannot serve as anchors for this paper. Thus I can make a plausible case for a continuous model, but not the requested anchored case for a computational mirror. The strongest limitation is precisely that the paper studies which recursive rules satisfy which axioms, not the complexity of computing with those rules.
