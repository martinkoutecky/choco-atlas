The continuous mirror question

Given \(m\) item categories, a unit-mass atomless population with finite support \(T\) of submodular valuation types \(v_t:2^{[m]}\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), and value oracles for each \(v_t\), does there exist a finite-round posted-price mechanism and polynomial-time useful advice, computable in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), such that each infinitesimal buyer receives an integral bundle and every measurable strategy profile fixed by the advice attains expected welfare within \(O((\log\log m)^3)\) of the continuous optimum \(\max_x \sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\)?

The model it lives in

An atomless market of unit mass with \(\tau\) complete submodular valuation types, where \(\mu_t\) is type \(t\)'s mass, each buyer receives an integral bundle, and item supplies scale per capita. The mechanism posts prices over finite rounds, advice maps a buyer’s tentative action to an equal or dominating action, and welfare is measured by the resulting allocation of type mass.

The objection that survived

The theorem’s advice and welfare guarantee concern atomic buyers acting in a finite sequence; in an atomless market, an individual deviation has zero effect on future supply, so the strategic condition and aggregate mechanism need a fresh definition. The proponent acknowledges that the guarantee does not transfer automatically, but does not specify that revised advice game.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror concerns Theorem 1.1’s welfare approximation for submodular buyers under advised strategies; it does not claim continuous analogues for the paper’s other demand-oracle results or its XOS and subadditive results.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the finite-round posted-price mechanism and useful advice be defined as aggregate state updates computable in \(\operatorname{poly}(m,\tau,L)\), while retaining the stated welfare guarantee under measurable advised behavior?
  • What correspondence between finite high-multiplicity markets and the atomless advised-strategy model captures the change in individual incentives and supports approximation or rounding guarantees?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead is Continuous Type-Mass Submodular Welfare, the type-mass version of the welfare problem in Theorem 1.1. That theorem is proved here: it establishes a polynomial-time mechanism with an \(O((\log\log m)^3)\)-approximation for any number of submodular buyers, in implementation in advised strategies. In full, the guarantee is that there is polynomial-time advice such that, whenever every buyer uses a strategy fixed by that advice, the mechanism achieves the stated expected-welfare approximation. The continuous question below mirrors the theorem’s welfare objective; it does not claim that the paper’s strategy guarantee transfers automatically.

A plausible regime is a large market for standardized software or service components. Many customers have the same complete valuation over bundles—perhaps because they use one of a modest menu of procurement templates—while the number of customers is far larger than the number of valuation types. Let \(T\) be the supported types, with \(|T|=\tau\); type \(t\) has submodular valuation \(v_t\), and \(\mu_t\) is its fraction of the population. Product category \(j\) has supply \(b_j\) per capita. In a finite market of \(N\) customers, this represents roughly \(N\mu_t\) buyers of type \(t\) and \(Nb_j\) units of category \(j\). Scaling supply with the population is part of this mirror: each customer still receives an integral bundle of categories.

Precisely, an instance gives \(m\), the supported valuation types and rational masses \((\mu_t)_{t\in T}\) summing to \(1\), rational supplies \((b_j)_{j=1}^m\), and polynomial-time value oracles for the \(v_t\). Let \(L\) be the input bit length. For each type \(t\) and bundle \(S\subseteq [m]\), \(x_{t,S}\) is the mass of that type receiving the integral bundle \(S\). The continuous optimum is the maximum of \(\sum_{t,S}x_{t,S}v_t(S)\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) for each type and \(\sum_{t,S:j\in S}x_{t,S}\le b_j\) for each category \(j\). The problem asks for a polynomial-time algorithm, measured in \(m,\tau,L\) and value-oracle calls, that outputs a feasible sparse allocation within \(O((\log\log m)^3)\) of this optimum. This is the continuous allocation problem, not a question about an encoding.

I expect this target to be Class A, though a proof is needed. The aggregate problem is a configuration LP with exponentially many bundle variables; its dual pricing step asks, for each type, about bundles maximizing value minus item prices. The paper’s Proposition 3.2 gives a polynomial-time \((1/2,1/2)\)-approximate demand oracle for submodular valuations, and Theorem 4.4 shows how bicriterion demand approximations support posted-price welfare guarantees. That makes approximate pricing and continuous optimization a concrete route to study. The new result would need to run in \(\mathrm{poly}(m,\tau,L)\): simply expanding the rational masses into \(N\) buyers and applying Theorem 1.1 would not meet that compressed-input bound.

The scope is just Theorem 1.1’s welfare allocation objective. The regime makes type mass meaningful—many customers share complete valuations, and the finite-market allocation counts become mass allocations in the limit—while preserving integral bundles for each unit of mass.

The weakest point is that the theorem’s guarantee is specifically about individual strategies and advice, whereas the question I have stated is about computing an aggregate allocation. Also, the paper’s computational bottleneck is bundle pricing in \(m\); increasing the number of buyers does not make that subproblem easier. So this is a credible population mirror and a focused tractability question, not a claim that continuization removes the paper’s demand-query hardness. Further questions are whether the \(O((\log\log m)^3)\) guarantee survives with runtime polynomial in \(m,\tau,L\), whether a useful advised-strategy version can be implemented on type masses, and what additive rounding guarantee relates its solutions to finite high-multiplicity markets.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against this anchor is a mismatch between the proposed problem and Theorem 1.1. The theorem guarantees welfare through a mechanism, under a specific strategic condition: each buyer follows polynomial-time advice that improves any strategy the advice rejects. The proposed configuration LP instead gives a planner the type valuations and masses, then asks it to compute an allocation. Its variables \(x_{t,S}\) contain no strategies, advice, or buyer incentives. Proposition 3.2’s approximate demand oracle and Theorem 4.4’s posted-price guarantee do not by themselves establish an algorithm for this LP.

That is a real gap in the proponent’s route, but it does not defeat the anchor. A stronger mirror would retain the mechanism and advice, define buyers as an atomless population with finite valuation support, and scale item supplies per capita so each unit of mass still receives an integral bundle. The question would be whether the paper’s welfare guarantee and advice can be made to work for that population. The paper’s own machinery might not transfer; finding out is a computational question the authors could recognize. The need to scale supplies is a co-modification the programme explicitly allows.

The usual negative grounds do not rule it out. Theorem 1.1 is a named computational result; many buyers can share a complete valuation type; the welfare objective does not depend on buyer identity; and per-capita supplies avoid a vanishing allocation limit. The paper itself provides no continuous-population treatment of this guarantee.

So the best negative case identifies a fidelity problem in the stated LP and an unproved algorithmic bridge. It cannot honestly establish that no worthwhile mirror exists. A negative verdict would be weak here: the repaired advised-strategy question survives.
