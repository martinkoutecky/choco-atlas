The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The appended case raises no affirmative anchor. Proposition 3.2 is the closest candidate: it gives an exact formula for the optimal fine in the paper’s symmetric Cournot family, and Corollary 3.3 gives the resulting contraction core. But neither states a complexity result for an input-output problem; Theorem 4.17 is an axiomatic characterization. Turning the fine calculation into a complexity problem for general games would be a reasonable new project, but it would not mirror a computational result this paper established.

The strongest redesigned mirror would take a large market with finitely many firm types, each type specifying complete characteristics such as cost and capacity, and mass vector \(\mu\). For a coalition with type masses \(z\), one could define its Cournot worth \(v_\mu(z)\) and ask how hard it is to compute the optimal fine and contraction core. That population regime is plausible. Its difficulty is that the paper’s deterrence measure excludes only the grand coalition. In a regular mass model, proper coalitions can leave out arbitrarily small mass. Balanced mixtures of coalitions with mass \((1-\epsilon)\mu\) have worth approaching \(v_\mu(\mu)\): their value is \(\frac{v_\mu((1-\epsilon)\mu)}{1-\epsilon}\), which converges to \(v_\mu(\mu)\) when the worth is continuous there. The second-best benchmark then reaches the grand-coalition worth as a supremum, so the optimal fine is zero and the contraction core reduces to the ordinary core. The finite model’s nearest proper coalition, which must leave at least one whole firm out, has no counterpart in that limit.

A singular Cournot limit might preserve a nonzero fine, but then its value would depend on the scaling and on how even an arbitrarily small outsider mass affects supply. Requiring a minimum outsider share or retaining a strategically significant outsider could avoid the collapse, but each changes the question or restores an atomic population feature. This is not proof that no useful extension can ever be devised. It is the strongest honest negative case: the most natural mass version loses the paper’s deterrence object, while a nondegenerate computational problem would need substantial new modeling and would not be anchored in a computational result of this paper.
