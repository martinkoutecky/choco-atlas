The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s strongest point is also the strongest case against a mirror: this paper has no named result about computational complexity. Theorem 1 represents arbitrary coalition conditional probabilities; Theorem 2 characterizes the pivotal information mechanism through axioms. The propositions establish incentive compatibility. None states an algorithmic problem or gives a complexity classification. The paper’s probabilities are continuous, but that is continuity in beliefs and reports, not in the population.

Theorem 1 is the best candidate to rescue. One could group agents by complete information type, give each type mass \(\mu_t\), and ask about the coalition value or Shapley rewards as a function of coalition masses. But the paper’s coalition value is based on intersecting reported information sets. Repeating a type’s report adds no information: the intersection depends on which types are present, not what fraction of each type is present. So the theorem’s arbitrary function on named-agent coalitions does not supply a mass-sensitive result. One could introduce an evidence aggregator that makes quantities of information matter, or ask how to compute type-level Shapley rewards under a specified prior representation. Either could be a worthwhile new project; neither is a computational result from Theorem 1. Its construction says which functions can arise, not how hard they are to compute.

Theorem 2 admits a stronger population version too: treat each information type as a bloc and define aggregate pivotal payments, perhaps using a mass-weighted ordering. That avoids the atomless limit in which removing one individual changes nothing. But it changes the unit of participation and the axioms whose uniqueness Theorem 2 proves. Computing such bloc payments would be a new problem, with its complexity depending on how the prior and conditional probabilities are represented. The paper provides no complexity claim for that task.

These are not objections that the continuous answer would be dull, or that individual identity cannot be grouped. They are the narrower point that every computational question one can build here requires adding the computational object the paper does not study. The honest limit is that this does not rule out a valuable future project inspired by the mechanisms. It does rule out a worthwhile *continuous mirror of a named computational result in this paper*.
