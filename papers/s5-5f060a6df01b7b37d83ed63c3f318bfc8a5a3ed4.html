The case FOR (proponent)

My lead is Continuous Wireless-FL Welfare Allocation (\(\mathrm{CWFL}_{\infty}\)). The paper has no numbered hardness theorem: its introduction calls the welfare problem combinatorial NP-hard, but that claim is unnumbered. The strongest named computational anchor is therefore Proposition 3, proved in this paper: the RMA mechanism is computationally efficient. Given \(N\) data owners, it partitions them into EMD groups, selects channel-compatible workers, and determines their payments; the paper bounds each group’s work by \(O(|\Theta_j|^3)\) and the total running time by \(O(N^3)\). It outputs both the worker set and payments. Proposition 3 is an algorithmic anchor, and the algorithm computes the paper’s welfare-based allocation approximately. It does not establish that exact welfare maximization is in P.

A plausible high-multiplicity setting is an operator-run FL task for a large, standardized device or app cohort. Many users share the same task, data-volume and label-skew class, service-cost tier, and radio class. The type records those attributes, including the user’s channel requests and channel quality. For example, millions of eligible devices might fall into a few hundred or thousand such types. Each type’s members are indistinguishable for the paper’s allocation objective; the mass of a type is its fraction of the eligible population.

Here is the continuous problem. The input is a finite support \(T\), with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), and, for each type \(t\), data size \(d_t\), EMD \(\sigma_t\), reported service cost \(b_t\), and requested channel set \(C_t\). The instance also gives normalized channel-time capacities \(q_k\), the paper’s fitted quality parameters, and an enrollment floor \(\ell>0\) for a viable FL session. The decision is a selected mass \(x_t\) from each type. Set
\[ n(x)=\sum_t x_t,\qquad D(x)=\sum_t d_t x_t,\qquad \Delta(x)=\frac{\sum_t \sigma_t x_t}{n(x)}. \]
The data utility uses the paper’s function
\[ a(\Delta)=\kappa_4\exp\!\left(-\left(\frac{\Delta+\kappa_5}{\kappa_6}\right)^2\right), \qquad \Phi(D,\Delta)=\kappa_7\!\left[a(\Delta)-\kappa_1 \exp\!\left(-\kappa_2(\kappa_3D)^{a(\Delta)}\right)\right]. \]
For type \(t\), let \(\chi_t\) be the platform’s communication cost per unit mass, as given by the paper’s channel-cost model. The objective is to maximize
\[ S(x)=\Phi(D(x),\Delta(x)) -\delta_gM\widehat{\alpha}\,n(x) -\sum_t (b_t+\chi_t)x_t \]
subject to
\[ 0\le x_t\le\mu_t,\qquad \sum_{t:k\in C_t}x_t\le q_k\quad\text{for each channel }k, \]
and either \(x=0\) or \(n(x)\ge\ell\). The empty allocation is the option to decline the task. The requested solution is a rational allocation \(x\) with \(S(x)\ge\mathrm{OPT}-\varepsilon\), for a supplied additive tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\). Complexity should be measured in support size \(\tau=|T|\), number of channel classes, input bit length \(L\), and \(1/\varepsilon\), with the task’s fitted quality parameters fixed.

I expect an additive approximation to be Class A. The reason is concrete: the nonlinear quality term depends on the allocation only through three aggregate quantities, \(D\), \(n\), and \(\sum_t\sigma_tx_t\). For fixed values or small intervals for those aggregates, finding the least-cost feasible allocation is a linear program over the type masses and channel capacities. Since the aggregates range over a bounded three-dimensional region, a discretization with an error bound from the fixed quality function suggests a polynomial-in-\(\tau\), channel count, and \(1/\varepsilon\) algorithm. That is a proposed route, not a result of the paper. The corresponding exact-optimization question, and the effect of the fitted parameters on approximation guarantees, remain open questions.

The mirror preserves the paper’s central allocation question: recruit a population to maximize the same data-quality, service-cost, and wireless-resource trade-off. It covers the welfare allocation in equations (19)–(21), with its aggregate data-quality model, and takes Proposition 3 as the named algorithmic anchor. It does not attempt to continuize the RMA’s per-agent critical payments, universal truthfulness, individual rationality, or the learned DRLA policy.

My weakest point is the spectrum model. The paper imposes pairwise non-overlap of selected workers’ requested channels. A positive mass of repeated types cannot satisfy that literal simultaneous-use rule when all members request the same channels. \(\mathrm{CWFL}_{\infty}\) therefore treats channel supply as normalized channel-time capacity, consistent with scheduling participants across FL rounds, and scales that supply with the cohort. This is an explicit co-modification; an author who requires the original one-session conflict graph could reject it. The allocation objective still retains their data-quality and cost model, so the mirror remains a serious question about their FL market rather than a fractional version of an unrelated outcome.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Proposition 3 is the named computational result, but the proposed \(\mathrm{CWFL}_{\infty}\) does not continuize it. Proposition 3 bounds the running time of RMA on \(N\) individually reported data owners, including its worker selection and critical payments. The proposed mirror drops those payments and the strategyproof mechanism, then asks for a fractional welfare allocation under different resource constraints. The paper’s welfare maximization problem is a computational target, but it is not what Proposition 3 proves tractable.

Trying to continuize RMA itself exposes the harder problem. A complete type would include the bid, data attributes, channel quality, and requested channels. Under the paper’s rule, selected owners must have pairwise disjoint channel sets. With a fixed finite spectrum, repeated owners of the same type conflict with one another; if every owner needs at least one channel, at most \(K\) owners can be selected from \(K\) channels. In a population of size \(N\), the selected share is therefore at most \(K/N\), which vanishes as the population grows. An atomless population cannot represent the finite set of winners that remains, and an individual’s report no longer has the pivotal effect needed to define RMA’s critical payment.

The proposed channel-time capacities address that obstruction by changing the wireless model. The paper requires workers to transmit at a fixed rate and treats overlapping channel requests as conflicts; reusing a channel over time would require scheduling and could change round duration and participation. Scaling channel-time capacity with the cohort also removes the finite-resource bottleneck that motivates the paper’s congestion constraint. That may be a sensible extension, but it is not a co-modification forced by making the population continuous: it changes how the scarce resource limits the allocation.

There is a related scaling problem even if one sets the channel issue aside. The paper’s quality utility is bounded: \(\Phi(D,\Delta)\leq \kappa_7\kappa_4\). With finite support and positive per-owner costs, preserving total data volume makes the welfare-maximizing number of contributors bounded as the population grows, so their share again tends to zero. Using an unscaled mass-weighted average for \(D\) avoids that collapse only by treating average data per person as the paper’s total selected data, which changes the meaning of its fitted quality function.

I cannot establish that no related continuous FL question could ever be worthwhile. A recurring, time-shared service with a suitable rate-based utility could support a coherent high-multiplicity model. But it would change the single-session resource and welfare semantics that this paper studies. Against the stated anchor, the negative case is strongest: the proposed mirror is a plausible new model, but the named result and the fixed-spectrum population limit do not support it as a continuous mirror of this paper.
