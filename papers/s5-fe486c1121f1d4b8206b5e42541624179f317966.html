The case FOR (proponent)

The honest first point is that this paper contains no eligible anchor under your rule. The full text has no printed Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition, and no named NP-hardness, polynomial-time, FPT, or parameterized-complexity result. Its main computational claims are exhaustive enumeration results, such as \(993{,}061{,}482\) weighted voting games for \(9\) voters and \(76{,}586\) games without a unique minimum-sum representation. The LP/ILP systems in Sections 2–4 are formulations used in the computation, not numbered named results. Thus there is formally no anchor from which to build a compliant mirror.

The strongest positive case, if the paper’s central computational contributions may serve as an explicitly unnumbered anchor, is a continuous version of its weighted-rule realization problem.

Take a finite set \(T\) of voter types and a rational mass vector \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{T}\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). A coalition is no longer a subset of named voters but a vector \(x\in[0,\mu]\), where \(x_t\) is the mass of type \(t\) voting “yea”. The target voting rule is an upward-closed set \(\mathcal W\subseteq[0,\mu]\), given by a finite rational polyhedral description. A weighted representation is a vector \(w\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{T}\) and quota \(q>0\) satisfying \(x\in\mathcal W\) exactly when \(\sum_t w_t x_t\ge q\). Because weights and quota are scale-equivalent, minimize the normalized total weight \(\sum_t\mu_t(w_t/q)\). Equivalently, set \(a_t=w_t/q\), require \(\mathcal W=\{x:a\cdot x\ge1\}\), and minimize \(\sum_t\mu_ta_t\). A solution is an optimal \(a\), or a certificate that no such weighted representation exists; uniqueness can also be asked.

This is a genuine population mirror of the paper’s underlying problem. The types are large classes of constitutionally identical voters—for example, millions of members of several unions, regions, or institutions, with only \(\tau\) voting roles. If \(N\mu_t\) members of type \(t\) are instantiated, the finite coalitions are the grid points \(x_t=k_t/N\). The normalized objective corresponds to the original sum of individual weights divided by \(N\). The paper already uses voter equivalence classes, desirability ordering, weighted threshold representations, and LP feasibility, so the authors should recognize the question as their weighted-voting problem rather than an unrelated relaxation.

I would expect the real-weight version, with an explicit polyhedral description of \(\mathcal W\), to be Class A: feasibility and optimization reduce to continuous linear optimization, with separation over the listed polyhedral pieces. The exact integer analogue—requiring \(w_t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}\) and asking for minimum per-capita integer weight—would be a separate and harder follow-up. The natural questions are whether its finite high-multiplicity restrictions converge to the continuous optimum, and whether nonuniqueness of minimum representations survives in the limit.

The weakest point is substantial: the paper’s headline object is minimum-sum *integer* representations of a finite Boolean game on named voters, whereas the proposed mirror uses mass vectors and scale-normalized real weights. That modification is mathematically forced by the loss of the discrete integer lattice, but it means the mirror is strongest for the paper’s weightedness/LP layer, not for its exact \(9\)-voter classification. Therefore this is a respectable prospective mirror, but not a fully anchored positive case under the stated grading rule.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is unusually strong under the programme’s formal rules: this paper supplies no eligible anchor. It contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition, and no named complexity or algorithmic result. Its computational contributions are exhaustive counts and implementation reports—\(993{,}061{,}482\) weighted games for \(9\) voters, \(76{,}586\) with nonunique minimum representations, warm starts, cutting planes—not assertions of complexity that the programme can mirror. The proponent’s admission of this is decisive. There is no compliant anchor to green.

Even if the rule is relaxed to allow the paper’s unnumbered LP layer, the proposed mirror is not a faithful high-multiplicity version. The paper starts with a Boolean rule \(\chi:2^N\to\{0,1\}\). The proposed object supplies an entirely new polyhedral set \(\mathcal W\subseteq[0,\mu]\). The values of \(\chi\) on integral coalitions do not determine what should happen at fractional coalition vectors. Replicating voters therefore requires an additional lifting rule: several inequivalent continuum extensions can agree on the original Boolean points while differing everywhere in the interior. The polyhedral description of \(\mathcal W\) is extra model input, not a consequence of the paper.

The type interpretation does not remove that gap. The paper’s equivalence classes are endogenous symmetry classes of a particular finite game; they can range from a few classes to \(9\) singleton classes. They are not a fixed population-type space underlying the enumeration. Choosing a society of millions of voters in a handful of constitutional roles is a plausible new application, but it is no longer the paper’s \(9\)-voter classification problem.

More seriously, the proposed objective removes the paper’s defining arithmetic. If \(N\mu_t\) copies of type \(t\) receive an integer weight \(w_t\), the original sum is

\[ N\sum_t \mu_t w_t. \]

Increasing \(N\) merely multiplies the objective; it does not make the integer coefficients converge to arbitrary real coefficients. Replacing \(w_t/q\) by real \(a_t\) therefore does not continuize the minimum-integer-sum problem. It replaces it with normalized real threshold representation. That may be a legitimate optimization problem, but it is precisely the paper’s central object that has been discarded.

The strongest rescue would retain integer weights and define a canonical count-based lift of a typed voting game. That is a sensible high-multiplicity problem, but it becomes a finite-dimensional parametric family whose objective is already determined by \(\mu\); the population limit adds no new relaxation. It is also a new problem, not a mirror of any named result in this paper.

Thus the paper should be rejected under the programme’s strict anchoring rule: there is no named computational result to mirror, and the proposed substitute changes both the input object and the integer optimization objective. The broader universal claim—that no worthwhile typed high-multiplicity version could ever be devised—is weaker and cannot honestly be proved from this paper. The defensible conclusion is narrower but strong: this paper does not itself provide a worthwhile, properly anchored continuous mirror.
