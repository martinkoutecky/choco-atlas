The continuous mirror question

For a fixed constant ε from Theorem B.3, given a finite set of utility types \(T\), schools \(J\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), positive rational per-capita capacities \(s_j\) summing to \(1\), and rational utilities \(u_{tj}\in[0,1]\), can one find allocations \(x_{tj}\geq0\) and prices \(p_j\geq0\) such that \(\sum_j x_{tj}=1\), \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}=s_j\), \(\min_j p_j=0\), and for every type \(t\), \(\sum_jp_jx_{tj}\leq1+ε\), \(\sum_j u_{tj}x_{tj}\geq\operatorname{val}_p(t)-ε\), and \(\sum_{j:u_{tj}=0}p_jx_{tj}=0\), where \(\operatorname{val}_p(t)=\max\{\sum_j u_{tj}y_j:y_j\geq0,\ \sum_jy_j=1,\ \sum_jp_jy_j\leq1\}\)?

The model it lives in

A school-assignment market with a continuum of students represented by finitely many complete cardinal-utility types. Type \(t\) has mass \(\mu_t\), each student receives a unit-demand lottery \(x_t\) over schools, and school \(j\) has per-capita capacity \(s_j\), so market clearing is \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}=s_j\). Prices \(p_j\) support an approximate HZ equilibrium, including the paper's restriction against assigning a type positive-price schools it values at zero.

The reduction's support grows with the threshold graph, while each type's multiplicity is fixed once the error constants are fixed; it therefore establishes neither fixed-support hardness nor asymptotic compression.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is a high-multiplicity school assignment market. A type is a complete cardinal-utility vector over schools; equal-income students with the same vector are interchangeable. A type of mass \(\mu_t\) represents that fraction of the cohort. School capacity \(s_j\) is measured per student, with \(\sum_j s_j=1\), so a cohort of size \(N\) has \(Ns_j\) seats of type \(j\). The bundle \(x_t\) remains the paper’s lottery over schools; the continuous object is the population distribution. Since utilities, budgets, and equilibrium conditions are linear, averaging the bundles of students with the same type preserves those conditions.

Here are two precise search problems on that model. In both, the input is a finite type set \(T\), a finite school set \(J\), rational masses \(\mu_t\geq0\) summing to \(1\), positive rational capacities \(s_j\) summing to \(1\), and rational utilities \(u_{tj}\in[0,1]\). The complexity parameters are \(\tau=|T|\), \(|J|\), and the input bit length \(L\); the output is an allocation \(x\) and prices \(p\).

For fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), Continuous Restricted HZ (\(\mathrm{CRHZ}_\infty(\varepsilon)\)) asks for \(x_{tj}\geq0\) and \(p_j\geq0\) satisfying
\[ \sum_j x_{tj}=1,\qquad \sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}=s_j,\qquad \min_j p_j=0, \]
and, for each type \(t\),
\[ \sum_jp_jx_{tj}\leq1+\varepsilon,\qquad \sum_j u_{tj}x_{tj}\geq \operatorname{val}_p(t)-\varepsilon, \]
where
\[ \operatorname{val}_p(t)= \max\left\{\sum_j u_{tj}y_j: y\geq0,\ \sum_jy_j=1,\ \sum_jp_jy_j\leq1\right\}. \]
It also imposes the paper’s restriction, \(\sum_{j:u_{tj}=0}p_jx_{tj}=0\) for every \(t\). There is no welfare objective to optimize: a solution is a price-supported approximate equilibrium.

This is my lead because Theorem B.3, proved in the paper’s Appendix B, says that finding a restricted \(\varepsilon\)-HZ equilibrium is PPAD-hard for some fixed \(\varepsilon>0\). The theorem is unconditional; its reduction uses the known PPAD-hardness of bounded-degree threshold games. The paper motivates the restriction as ruling out purchases of positive-price goods a player values at zero. [Theorem B.3 and its reduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00951v2).

The mirror has a direct high-multiplicity bridge to that result. In the paper’s construction, each graph vertex has a block of \(5m^{10}\) agents with the same utility vector, and each edge has a fixed collection of further identical-utility groups. Here \(m\) is a large constant in the hardness theorem. Thus the construction already has repeated complete agent types: its support is \(O(m(|V|+|E|))\), while the population is \(5m^{10}|V|+(64m^5+48m^3+50m)|E|\). Each block of identical goods can likewise be represented by one school category with capacity equal to its block size divided by the population. Splitting a type’s category allocation evenly among the corresponding copies lifts a continuous solution to a solution of the paper’s market. I therefore expect \(\mathrm{CRHZ}_\infty(\varepsilon)\) to be Class B: hardness transfers.

A second, broader question is Continuous HZ (\(\mathrm{CHZ}_\infty(\varepsilon)\)): the same input and equilibrium conditions, with the no-waste restriction omitted. Its anchor is Theorem 3.2, proved in the paper conditional on Conjecture 3.1. That conjecture says that for some fixed positive constants \(\varepsilon,\delta\), finding a generalized-circuit assignment satisfying all but a \(\delta\)-fraction of gates to additive error \(\varepsilon\) is PPAD-hard. Under that assumption, Theorem 3.2 says that finding an ordinary \(\varepsilon\)-HZ equilibrium is PPAD-hard for some constant \(\varepsilon>0\). The paper proves the reduction from the conjectured circuit problem; it does not prove the conjecture. I expect the same type and capacity aggregation to give conditional Class B hardness for \(\mathrm{CHZ}_\infty\). [Theorem 3.2 and Conjecture 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00951v2).

The authors should recognize both questions: they retain cardinal utilities, equal budgets, unit demand, price support, and market clearing in the paper’s school-assignment setting. The mirror covers the restricted approximate-equilibrium result unconditionally and ordinary approximate HZ conditionally. It does not cover the paper’s allocation-only PO+EF open problems.

The soft spot is the regime’s strength. The reduction has many interchangeable students per type, but its support grows with the threshold graph; it does not establish hardness with fixed \(\tau\), or show that every large school market has few utility types. The unconditional anchor also depends on the paper’s added no-waste restriction. Useful next questions are whether ordinary \(\mathrm{CHZ}_\infty\) is unconditionally hard at any constant error, how hardness changes with \(\tau\) and mass bit length, and whether that restriction is essential.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative I can defend is a priority objection: the paper’s reductions do not show that population continuization makes these markets easier. For both anchors, the graph gadget contributes a growing number of utility types, so the continuous formulation may simply inherit the finite problem’s full combinatorial size.

In the hardness construction, \(m=\lceil C/\kappa\rceil\) is fixed once the error constants are fixed. Each type’s repeated-agent count is therefore a large but fixed constant, while the number of types grows as \(\Theta(|V|+|E|)\), just as the number of agents does. The construction supports a continuous mirror, but does not demonstrate asymptotic compression or reveal an algorithmic gain from large cohorts.

That objection does not defeat either anchor. Set each type’s mass to its agent count divided by \(n\), and each school category’s per-capita capacity to its item count divided by \(n\). A solution to the aggregated market can be lifted by giving each agent of a type that type’s bundle and each copy of a school category the category’s price. The budget, utility, and clearing constraints are preserved. Thus the unconditional restricted result, Theorem B.3, transfers to a coherent continuous-population question; the ordinary HZ result, Theorem 3.2, does too, conditional on PCP-for-PPAD. [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00951)

Nor do the other proposed objections hold: the relevant type is a complete utility vector, so agents are interchangeable for this problem; mass remains meaningful under unit demand and per-capita capacities; and the paper supplies named computational hardness results. The fact that bundles and goods are already divisible does not make the population continuous—the mirror changes the population representation.

So the negative case is weak. I can argue that these anchors are poor evidence of a *tractability gain* from continuization, especially since their support grows with the instance. I cannot honestly argue that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario: the high-multiplicity restricted-HZ question is well-posed, the paper’s reduction gives it an unconditional hardness boundary, and the ordinary-HZ mirror gives a conditional one.
