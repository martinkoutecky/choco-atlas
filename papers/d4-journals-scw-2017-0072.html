The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive objection is that this paper has no computational result to mirror. Theorem 1 characterizes functions that preserve agreement; Theorem 2 characterizes thoroughly commutative Boolean functions by incidence counts. Neither states the complexity of an encoded problem, gives an algorithm, or establishes a hardness result. Their quantified objects are functions on sets and relations, with no finite input representation or parameters like \(m,\tau,L\) from which to pose the programme’s complexity questions.

The best-looking alternative is to replace voter counts in Theorem 2 with population fractions, or to give a finite-support society over rankings and ask whether an aggregation rule satisfies the paper’s conditions. Those could be sensible new axiomatic questions. But the fraction version extends the theorem’s characterization; the aggregation-rule version adds a computational task the paper never formulates. Asking how to compute or optimize over such rules would be a new project, not a continuous mirror of a result here. The same applies to a continuous-population route toward Arrow’s theorem: it may be worth studying, but this paper presents it as an open axiomatic direction, outside ChoCo’s computational scope.

A society with many voters sharing ranking types is entirely plausible, and that fact does not rescue the anchor. The negative case is strong that this paper offers no worthwhile paper-anchored mirror.
