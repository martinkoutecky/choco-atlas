What the mirror covers

The proposed direction concerns Theorem 1 and Theorem 2. It leaves Theorems 3 and 4, which also characterize stability, without a computational anchor.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a large, recurring labor market: many workers and firms, but relatively few complete profiles of skills, productivity, information, beliefs, and status quo terms. Those profiles are the types; their masses describe the population. The default allocation can be represented by a matching flow across types and type-pair wages. A plausible continuous question is whether a representative worker of every positive-mass type can rationalizably abstain from offering, when agents test deviations individually and beliefs follow a type-level version of the paper’s forward-induction conditions. This preserves the paper’s stability question; simply asking whether a positive mass can move would change its unilateral-deviation model.

The closest named result is Theorem 1, proved in this paper: for every matching outcome in the finite market \(M\), stability under forward induction holds if and only if incomplete-information stability holds. Theorem 2, also proved here, bounds the two elimination procedures at each level: \(S_{3\ell}\subseteq \Lambda_\ell\subseteq S_{1+2\ell}\). These results make the proposed extension recognizable as a continuation of the paper’s central object.

But neither theorem asserts a computational result. The paper gives no named result establishing that a problem is in P, NP-hard, FPT, or another complexity class. So the proposed continuum question has no qualifying computational anchor under your criteria, and I would not present it as a valid anchored mirror. The positive case is a plausible high-multiplicity research direction; it does not meet the paper’s required result-to-mirror test.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the proposed anchors do not meet ChoCo’s computational test. Theorem 1 equates two stability concepts on each finite market; Theorem 2 bounds how their elimination rounds relate. Neither states the complexity of a computational problem or gives an algorithm. A continuous version of either theorem would extend an epistemic characterization, not mirror a computational result.

One could define a new problem: given rational type masses and a type-level matching and belief structure, decide whether abstaining is rationalizable for every type. But the paper does not pose that problem or provide a complexity result to continue. Its formal input is a finite set of named matching outcomes and a finite game of offers between named workers and firms. Turning that into an algorithmic question over a continuum would require choosing a new input representation and new complexity parameters; it would be a research direction inspired by the paper, not a mirror of Theorem 1 or 2.

There is also a harder modeling issue behind the proposed labor-market example. The paper’s reasoning turns on a worker making a unilateral offer to a particular firm, whose beliefs depend on what that firm knows about its particular current match. In a mass model, a single worker has measure zero. Treat deviations as changes to positive mass and the game becomes one of group or type-level deviations; preserve unilateral offers and the model needs individual agents, their matched partners, and their higher-order beliefs alongside the mass distribution. A careful continuum model might supply all of that, and a large labor market is a plausible high-multiplicity setting. But the more of this individual structure it must restore, the less the paper itself supports the computational continuization.

So the case against is strong as a claim about fit with ChoCo: the positive case has no qualifying computational anchor, and its proposed question needs a substantial new model. It is weaker as a universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror could exist. The paper’s finite market and identity-sensitive epistemics make such a mirror look remote, but they do not rule out a version the authors would recognize.
