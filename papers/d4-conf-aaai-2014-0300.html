The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raised no result-level anchor, so there is nothing to defeat one by one. Under the stated standard, no continuous mirror has yet been established. The paper’s own contribution is FUTUREMATCH, a learning framework supported by simulation results; its only complexity claim is that finite cycle clearing is NP-hard, attributed to earlier work. It proves no numbered computational result whose continuous counterpart this case can assess.

The strongest objection to a proposed mirror is that kidney exchange is built around a particular compatibility graph: which individual pairs can exchange kidneys, which cycles and chains are feasible, and how failures affect each planned exchange. A type description rich enough to preserve those facts may give nearly every pair a distinct type, leaving no useful reduction in support size. A coarse description by blood type, sensitization, or other medical classes can produce a finite population model, but aggregate compatibility rates may not preserve feasible disjoint cycles, chain structure, or expected transplants. Recovering those details could require a new stochastic graph model, rather than a continuization of a result in this paper.

That objection does not establish the universal claim. A mass-allocation model over recurring patient-donor cohorts, with type-level compatibility and failure rates, is plausible; cycles, chains, and fairness could be reformulated for it. The paper’s national-exchange setting gives such a regime some credibility. Whether that model is tractable or faithful would need investigation, and neither difficulty alone makes the question unworthy. So the defensible negative is narrower: this paper offers a weak target for the programme’s result-by-result continuization exercise. I cannot honestly rule out a worthwhile continuous mirror in every scenario.
