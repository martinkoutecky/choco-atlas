The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raises no eligible anchor, and that is the strongest case against a mirror here. The paper reports simulation findings—most notably \(1-R\propto F_{\rm rep}^{-1/2}\) and a decline in normalized committee size as \(N_e\) grows—but proves no named computational result. The rule itself is a sequence of local choices and graph operations; the paper poses no complexity question or optimization problem for a continuous society.

The best attempt to build one would take a large-population limit of the network and opinion model, then ask how representativeness and committee size behave as the threshold varies, perhaps choosing a threshold to minimize committee size at a target representativeness. That is a coherent research question, but it would be a new stochastic or analytic study of network limits, not a computational mirror of a result in this paper. The reported scaling laws are simulation observations, and their proposed explanation is sampling error; turning them into limit theorems would not supply the missing algorithmic problem.

There is also a substantive obstacle to a mass-only model. At fixed representativeness, the paper’s simulations suggest \(F_{\rm rep}\) falls roughly as \(1/N_e\), leaving a committee with a bounded number of members as the population grows. Its members are selected through individual trust links, cycles, and accumulated vote flow. A distribution over voter types does not retain those particular nodes or their graph structure. In the sparse network settings studied, compressing people by opinion type loses the neighborhoods that determine who represents whom; keeping enough neighborhood detail risks making the number of types grow with the population. A graphon or block-model formulation could preserve some aggregate structure, but then the useful questions concern that newly specified network model and its asymptotics.

The negative case is strongest against treating this paper as a ChoCo computational anchor. It is weaker as a claim that no useful continuous version could ever be devised: a structured population with repeated network roles might support one. But such a version would need a new model and a new computational question. The paper itself supplies neither.
