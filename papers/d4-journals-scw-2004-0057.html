The case FOR (proponent)

I find a plausible population mirror, but no qualifying computational anchor in this paper. Theorem 1 characterizes continuation equilibria for Pareto efficient voting rules; Theorem 2 relates their outcomes to full agendas; and Corollary 2 identifies the outcomes under successive elimination with the Banks set. These are equilibrium characterizations, not claims that a problem is in P, NP-hard, or otherwise has a stated complexity classification. Theorem 1’s reference to a “simple algorithm” likewise comes with no input model or runtime bound.

A natural candidate mirror would take strict rankings as types, assign each type a rational population share \(\mu_t\), and use those shares to determine the majority tournament. Agenda extensions and stopping would follow the paper’s continuation-equilibrium conditions; the solution would be the set of equilibrium outcomes from a given starting agenda. A large convention or participatory electorate with many members but relatively few ranking types is a possible regime. Corollary 2 is the closest named result to anchor that question: for finite-voter profiles in \(P^*\), under successive elimination, equilibrium continuation outcomes equal the outcomes of full agenda extensions—the generalized Banks set—when consistency is imposed. But it does not assert a computational result, and its continuous analogue is not proved here.

So, under the requested anchor standard, I have no valid anchor and cannot make the required anchored positive case. The candidate mirror is an extension to investigate, not a computational result of this paper.
