The continuous mirror question

Given rational masses over \(K\) payoff-complete types, \(T\) probe environments, and finitely represented type-conditional mixed strategies in a specified anonymous aggregate-interaction model, decide whether there exist type-specific concave, continuous, locally non-satiated utilities under which each observed population profile maximizes mass-weighted utilitarian welfare among feasible profiles. Determine whether a finite revealed-preference certificate characterizes this consistency and its computational complexity.

The model it lives in

A nonatomic population with distribution \((p_1,\ldots,p_K)\) over payoff-complete types. Each type has a conditional action distribution; payoffs and constraints may depend on specified population aggregates. The objective is the mass-weighted expected welfare \(\sum_k p_k\,\mathbb{E}[U_k]\), and the decision is whether the observed profiles across probes can be rationalized as welfare-maximizing.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a narrow mirror of the paper’s revealed-preference test, not of its entire mechanism-learning pipeline.

Consider a shared-spectrum system with millions of users and a modest number \(K\) of exact, payoff-relevant types. A type records everything the model uses: valuation, feasible power choices, pricing terms, and network position. Users within a type are interchangeable; their interactions run through block-level congestion or other population aggregates. The regime is credible when \(K\ll N\), where \(N\) is the number of users. Each type’s mass \(p_k\) is its fraction of the population, and the continuous society is the distribution \((p_1,\ldots,p_K)\). A regulator probes the system under \(T\) constraint environments and observes each type’s mixed-strategy response \(\sigma_{k,t}\).

My anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper, in the main text’s Section 3.5 and Appendix C.2. It says that, under Assumption 3, three conditions are equivalent: (i) there exist concave, continuous, locally non-satiated utility functions under which every observed mixed-strategy profile is socially optimal in its constraint environment; (ii) the data satisfy MM-GARP; and (iii) for each agent \(i\), there are values \(u_t^i\in\mathbb R\) and multipliers \(\lambda_t^i>0\) satisfying \(u_s^i-u_t^i-\lambda_t^i g_{\phi,t}^i(\mu_s)\leq 0\) for every pair \(t,s\). Here \(g_{\phi,t}^i(\mu_s)\) is the expected constraint value for agent \(i\)’s strategy at observation \(s\). The paper proves the utility-rationalization/inequality equivalence; its link from the inequalities to MM-GARP uses the result of Forges and Minelli.

The continuous question I would put to a prover is Mass-MM-GARP Consistency. An instance gives rational type masses \(p_k>0\) summing to one; \(T\) probe environments with constraint functions \(g_{k,t}\); and observed type-conditional mixed strategies \(\sigma_{k,t}\). For a finite input representation, the strategies can be finite-support distributions with rational probabilities, and the instance can include the exact rational evaluations \(q_{k,t,s}=\int g_{k,t}(a)\,d\sigma_{k,s}(a)\). The constraints must satisfy the paper’s structural assumptions, and the profiles are observed responses of the type-continuum system.

The question is whether there exist type-specific concave, continuous, locally non-satiated utilities \(U_k\) such that, for every probe \(t\), the observed profile \(\sigma_t\) maximizes the population’s utilitarian welfare \(\sum_k p_k\,\mathbb E[U_k]\) over feasible type-conditional mixed strategies. A yes instance has a type-indexed Afriat-style certificate \(u_{k,t},\lambda_{k,t}>0\) satisfying \(u_{k,s}-u_{k,t}-\lambda_{k,t}q_{k,t,s}\leq 0\) for all \(k,s,t\); a no instance can be certified by infeasibility of those conditions. Establishing that this certificate exactly characterizes social optimality in the stated nonatomic, aggregate-interaction model is part of the problem.

I expect the finite-support version to be Class A, provided that extension goes through. Its candidate feasibility system has \(O(KT)\) variables and \(O(KT^2)\) inequalities, so with rational input summaries it is polynomial in \(K,T\), and the bit length \(L\). The positive masses can enter the welfare objective directly; since the utilities are unknown separately by type, the characterization must make clear when those weights can be absorbed into utility scales. The decision being computed is whether observed population behavior is consistent with social optimality—not whether a particular LP encoding is feasible in isolation.

The paper’s result is a good anchor because it already asks whether observed multi-agent mixed strategies are socially optimal and supplies a finite revealed-preference test. The mirror preserves that question while grouping interchangeable agents into types and replacing per-agent counts with population mass. It is also a natural high-multiplicity regime: a discrete system with \(n_k\) users of type \(k\) induces \(p_k=n_k/N\), while the limit description records fractions. I would not claim Theorem 1 itself proves the continuum extension. The paper establishes a blueprint for it. This covers Theorem 1’s social-optimality consistency result; it does not cover the outer search for a best mechanism in Theorem 2, the DRO results, or the paper as a whole. The paper offers no named hardness result to anchor here.

My weakest point is the move from finitely many strategic agents to an atomless population. In a nonatomic system, an individual’s effect on congestion vanishes, while a finite agent’s deviation can affect the joint outcome; the type-indexed test needs a proof that this change preserves the relevant rationalizability conditions. Exact type compression also requires genuine sameness in payoff-relevant details, and block-structured interaction may be a demanding assumption in some networks. These are real proof obligations. They do not make the mirror merely an outcome-space or noise continuization: the society itself is represented by a distribution over types, and the computational target remains the paper’s revealed-preference question. The next questions are whether the characterization extends to continuously distributed types, how its complexity changes as \(K\) grows, and whether finite samples suffice for a robust test.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The best objection to Theorem 1 / Mass-MM-GARP is that the proposed mirror sits between two models, and the paper’s theorem does not bridge them. The finite-agent theorem allows each utility \(f^i\) to depend on the full joint action. Its spectrum example also has pair-specific interference coefficients \(\alpha_{ji}\). A fixed set of types cannot capture those dependencies as the population grows unless the model adds anonymity or aggregate-interaction assumptions. Those assumptions may be sensible, but they are substantive new structure.

The strongest repair is an anonymous congestion model with \(K\) payoff-relevant types, type masses \(p_k\), and utilities that depend on actions and population aggregates. That is plausible for a large shared-spectrum system. But the proposed \(q_{k,t,s}\) summaries and type-indexed inequalities do not, by themselves, establish the claimed characterization when welfare depends on aggregate effects. A proof must account for how changing one type’s behavior changes the aggregate and hence other types’ payoffs. The paper’s finite-agent result does not supply that step.

There is also a narrower computational objection. Theorem 1 is a revealed-preference characterization, not a complexity theorem; its finite-data test is an ordinary LP. In a type-separable version, the masses \(p_k\) multiply type-specific welfare terms and can be absorbed into the unrestricted utility scales. The feasibility test then reduces to one LP certificate per type, much like compressing duplicate agents in the finite model. If aggregate interactions make masses affect the optimization in a substantive way, the simple certificate needs new justification. That weakens the claim that this particular theorem already yields a population-sensitive computational problem.

I still cannot honestly defeat the anchor in every reasonable scenario. A type-based continuum of users in an anonymous congestion model is coherent, and an exact characterization or complexity analysis for it could be worthwhile. The negative case is therefore a real warning about what must be proved and modeled, rather than a decisive reason to reject the mirror. The proponent’s narrow mirror probably survives if it is stated as a new theorem for a clearly specified aggregate-interaction model.
