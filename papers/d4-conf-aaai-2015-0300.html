The continuous welfare LP is plausible and tractable, but the proponent explicitly presents it as unanchored, so it cannot supply the required mirror.

What the mirror covers

The proposed LP covers welfare-maximizing buyer, seller, and unmatched mass assignments over valuation types; it leaves out the paper's truthfulness results, payments, budget balance, and backup-capacity trade-offs.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive foothold is a plausible continuous model of the paper’s efficient allocation problem. In a large ridesharing exchange, let each type \(t\) be a pair of buy and sell valuations \((v^b_t,v^s_t)\), and let \(\mu_t\) be that type’s share of the commuters. The decision variables are masses \(b_t,s_t,u_t\) assigned as buyers, sellers, or unmatched. A precise continuous problem is to maximize
\[ \sum_t (v^b_t b_t+v^s_t s_t) \]
subject to \(b_t+s_t+u_t=\mu_t\), \(b_t,s_t,u_t\ge 0\), and \(\sum_t b_t=\sum_t s_t\). The constraint \(b_t+s_t\le\mu_t\) preserves the paper’s rule that a trader cannot buy and sell at once; fractional masses represent fractions of a large cohort, each member of which still gets an integral role. A citywide exchange with many commuters but relatively few shared valuation types is a credible high-multiplicity regime. This allocation problem is an LP, so I would expect it to be tractable.

But that is an unanchored extension, not a continuous mirror of a named computational result in the paper. Theorems 1–3 concern whether McAfee’s mechanism is truthful in general, truthful under marginally decreasing valuations, and whether balanced trade reduction is truthful. Propositions 1–2 concern efficiency relative to McAfee and the existence of a \(k\) that yields budget balance. None asserts a running-time or complexity result. The paper’s closing discussion of maximum-weighted matching explicitly leaves polynomial solvability uncertain; it does not establish a named complexity result. So there is no qualifying anchor, and no anchor-based question for me to hand to a prover.

The main weakness in extending the mechanism results is that their incentive arguments turn on individual traders changing the allocation. In an atomless population, one commuter has zero mass, so that pivotal role can disappear. A continuous version of balanced trade reduction would need to say how mass allocations and individual incentives interact; it cannot inherit Theorem 3 merely by replacing counts with proportions. The LP above is a plausible continuous computational question about the paper’s allocation model, but the required anchor-based positive case is absent.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

A commuter cohort with repeated \((v^b,v^s)\) pairs is a credible high-multiplicity regime. The type objection does not carry this case. The difficulty is what the paper’s results ask us to compute: none of its numbered results states the complexity of a computational problem. Theorems 1–3 establish when particular mechanisms are or are not truthful; Propositions 1–2 establish an efficiency comparison and the existence of a budget-balancing \(k\). Those are mechanism properties, not complexity results of the kind this programme is meant to chart.

The incentive results are especially resistant to a population mirror. Their content is about what one trader can gain by changing her report and, in Theorem 1 and Theorem 3, by switching between buyer and seller. In an atomless society, one trader’s report has zero mass, so changing it cannot change the aggregate report distribution on which the mechanism operates. The truthfulness condition is then either vacuous or undefined for an aggregate mechanism that does not say how an individual gets their allocation and payment. Giving a positive mass to the deviator makes the question meaningful, but it becomes a coalition or type-level manipulation question, not the paper’s individual incentive question. Keeping a single positive-mass trader in an otherwise atomless population instead makes that trader a special atom, rather than a population mirror.

That problem reaches all three theorem anchors: the general failure in Theorem 1, its marginally decreasing exception in Theorem 2, and the balanced trade reduction guarantee in Theorem 3. A limit might still study how finite-market truthfulness or manipulation gains behave as the market grows. But that is an asymptotic incentive analysis, with finite individuals retained in the model; it does not turn these theorems into computational questions over a continuous society.

The allocation LP is the strongest alternative. For \(\tau\) valuation types, the proposed model has only a constant number of variables and constraints per type, so its welfare optimum is a routine rational linear program. It is a coherent continuous allocation problem, but it does not compute the payments, selected sides, or individual incentives that are the subject of the paper’s contributions. Nor does it provide a difficult pricing problem or a substantial complexity landscape. It is a useful formulation of the welfare relaxation; by itself, it is thin evidence for spending this programme’s effort on a mirror of this paper.

Propositions 1 and 2 do not supply a stronger computational anchor. A continuous version of Proposition 1 would compare welfare across mechanisms, while a continuous version of Proposition 2 might ask how much backup capacity is needed for budget balance. The latter could be a sensible new design problem, but preserving the paper’s truthfulness claim brings back the individual-deviation issue; dropping it changes the target to aggregate reserve planning. Neither proposition asks for an algorithm or complexity classification.

So the negative case is strongest against a *paper-anchored computational mirror*: the named results concern individual incentives and mechanism performance, while the clean population LP answers a separate, easy allocation question. I cannot honestly rule out every adjacent exchange-market project someone might find worthwhile. The case is weakest at the allocation LP, but I do not think that unanchored LP makes a compelling continuous mirror of this paper’s main results.
