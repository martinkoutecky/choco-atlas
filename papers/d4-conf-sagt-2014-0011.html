The continuous mirror question

Given a graph \(G=(V,E)\) and a finite-support report measure over edge-cost roles, consider repeated network deployments whose aggregate hired vector must lie in \(\mathcal{B}(G)/m\), where \(\mathcal{B}(G)\) is the spanning-tree base polytope. What is the minimum worst-case approximation ratio achievable by a no-money mechanism that assigns each tagged agent an inclusion probability \(q_e(d;\nu)\), is truthful under binding reports, and satisfies aggregate feasibility?

The model it lives in

Use types \(t=(e,a)\), where \(e\) is an edge role and \(a\) its shared actual cost, with mass \(\mu_t\). A mechanism maps the report measure and a tagged agent's report to inclusion probabilities; their integral yields a hired vector in \(\mathcal{B}(G)/m\). Minimize the worst-case truthful social cost divided by the minimum spanning-tree cost, subject to \(a q_e(a;\nu)\leq \max\{a,d\}q_e(d;\nu)\).

The objection that survived

The atomless role cohorts remove the individual influence that drives Theorem 5's lower bound, so the theorem itself does not imply any lower bound for the repeated-network model; the proponent acknowledges this and presents the continuous ratio as the question to investigate.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 5's approximation lower-bound question for graphical matroids in the repeated-network regime. It does not mirror the greedy mechanism guarantees in Theorems 3 and 6 or the general matroid results.

Open questions for a prover

  • Under the stated aggregate feasibility and individual truthfulness constraints, is ratio \(1\) optimal for the repeated-network regime?
  • What guarantees remain when an edge role contains multiple cost types or when the population is finite?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead, and sole anchor, is Theorem 5, proved in this paper. It says that for graphical matroids, no mechanism that is truthful in expectation can achieve an approximation ratio better than \((m-r)/2+1\), where \(m\) is the number of elements and \(r\) is the matroid rank. This is an approximation lower bound for truthful mechanisms, not an NP-hardness theorem.

A concrete mirror is Repeated-Network Continuum Hiring. Fix a connected graph \(G=(V,E)\) with \(m\) edges and rank \(r\). Imagine \(N\) identical network deployments, each needing a spanning tree. For each edge role \(e\), there are \(N\) interchangeable contractors, all with the same actual unit cost \(a_e\). Thus there are \(mN\) people but only \(m\) types, \(t_e=(e,a_e)\). Normalizing population mass by \(mN\), each type has mass \(\mu_{t_e}=1/m\). This preserves every edge-cost vector from the paper’s graph instances while making each edge role a large cohort.

The decision variable \(x_e\) is the fraction of the whole population hired from edge role \(e\). Feasibility is
\[ x\in \frac{1}{m}\mathcal B(G), \qquad \mathcal B(G)=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf 1_T:T\text{ is a spanning tree of }G\}. \]
This is the aggregate limit of hiring a spanning tree in each copy. The truthful optimum is
\[ \operatorname{OPT}_\infty(G,a) =\min_{x\in \frac{1}{m}\mathcal B(G)}\sum_{e\in E}a_ex_e =\frac{1}{m}\min_{T\text{ spanning tree}}\sum_{e\in T}a_e. \]

The problem to hand a prover is: given \(G\) and a finite-support report measure over its edge roles, find the minimum worst-case approximation ratio of a no-money mechanism that selects feasible hired mass and is truthful in expectation under binding reports. More precisely, the mechanism must provide an inclusion probability \(q_e(d;\nu)\) for a tagged agent of role \(e\) reporting cost \(d\), given the measure \(\nu\) of the other agents’ reports. Its aggregate hired vector must lie in \(\mathcal B(G)/m\), and for every actual cost \(a\) and alternative report \(d\) it must satisfy
\[ a\,q_e(a;\nu)\le \max\{a,d\}\,q_e(d;\nu). \]
The objective is to minimize the worst-case truthful social cost divided by \(\operatorname{OPT}_\infty(G,a)\). A solution is a mechanism rule with a proved feasibility, truthfulness, and ratio guarantee.

For this regime I expect Class A, with ratio \(1\). Here is the reason. For each role, take a weighted median of the reports from the other members of that role, compute a minimum spanning tree using those medians, and hire all the mass in roles on that tree. In the atomless limit, one person’s report does not change the report measure, so their inclusion probability is independent of their own report. If selected, truthful reporting minimizes their binding cost; if not selected, they incur no hiring cost. At a truthful population, each role’s report measure is concentrated at \(a_e\), so the mechanism chooses a minimum-cost spanning tree. The hired mass is \(\mathbf 1_T/m\), and the mechanism attains the optimum. Medians and a minimum spanning tree are computable in polynomial time. The same idea has a finite repeated-deployment version using leave-one-out reports: each deployment still receives a spanning tree, and a person’s own report does not affect the tree for their deployment.

The authors should recognize this as a mirror of their problem: the agents are still hired to perform network tasks, the objective is still minimum total cost, reports remain binding, and the outcome remains a matroid base. The continuous object is the population mass in each edge-cost type. The fractional base polytope describes the aggregate of repeated bases; it is part of the mirror forced by the population scaling.

The weak point is substantial: this is a specific high-multiplicity regime, with one shared cost per edge role. If a role contains several cost types, selecting the right fractions of people within it becomes a real incentive problem, and the median mechanism gives no general ratio guarantee. Theorem 5 also does not itself imply a lower bound for repeated copies. That is why this mirror is a useful test of whether the theorem’s obstruction survives continuization, rather than a claim that the paper’s full lower bound disappears.

This case covers Theorem 5 only. It leaves open how the best ratio depends on support size when roles have heterogeneous costs, whether useful guarantees hold for finite \(N\), and whether the lower bound returns when coalitional misreports or more general cost profiles are included.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 5 is a weak anchor for ChoCo: it bounds the approximation ratio of truthful mechanisms for a finite set of strategic agents, but says nothing about the computational complexity of finding a base or implementing a mechanism. Minimum-cost spanning tree is already polynomial-time solvable. That does not make the theorem irrelevant to algorithmic mechanism design, but it leaves the proposed mirror outside ChoCo’s central complexity question.

The repeated-network example is a sensible high-multiplicity regime, so “there are no repeated agents” is not a good objection. The problem is what happens to truthfulness in its stated continuum limit. With one cost per edge role, an individual has zero mass, so changing their report cannot change the report distribution or the chosen tree. If the mechanism hires all the mass in each selected role, that individual’s inclusion probability is fixed by their role and the tree. Under binding reports, truthful reporting then minimizes their cost simply because it cannot change whether they are hired. The mechanism can choose a minimum spanning tree and attain ratio \(1\), but the theorem’s strategic constraint has disappeared: its lower bound concerns how an agent’s report can affect their chance of selection.

That is a degeneration of the incentive problem, not an objection that the continuous answer differs from the discrete one. The finite-\(N\) repair does not remove it: at finite \(N\), a person’s report can still affect the outcome, and a leave-one-out rule needs to account for effects on their assignments across the other deployments. Those effects do not vanish from the finite mechanism merely because they vanish in the limit.

A stronger mirror would allow heterogeneous costs within each edge role and ask for a truthful mass-allocation mechanism over repeated bases. Then the mechanism may select different fractions of contractors at different reported costs; an individual’s report can change their own inclusion probability even though it does not change aggregate mass. The median-and-hire-whole-roles rule no longer gives the claimed ratio \(1\), and Theorem 5 does not establish a bound for this richer model. Treating a positive-mass cohort as a strategic reporter could also restore influence, but that changes individual truthfulness into coalition truthfulness.

So the proposed mirror does not preserve Theorem 5’s nontrivial strategic question in the continuum. But the honest negative case is weak on the broader claim that no worthwhile mirror exists: the heterogeneous-cost mass-allocation version is a coherent continuous procurement problem, and I cannot rule out that it merits study.
