The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that this paper’s computational contribution is a heuristic, not a computational result. It presents VALCAM’s auction and learning rules, says the method is “soon-enough, good-enough,” and reports classroom outcomes. It does not establish the complexity of a defined problem, or even give a global objective that VALCAM optimizes. The payoff equations describe how agents update rewards and experience; they do not define an optimization problem whose high-multiplicity version could be analyzed. There is therefore no named complexity result here to continuize.

The proposed large online CS1 setting is plausible, but a faithful type would need to capture more than a student’s competence and currency. VALCAM uses directed, accumulated experience between particular agents, peer evaluations, and histories that change across tasks. Encoding those exactly makes types depend on the interaction network, so the support can grow with the class. Aggregating experience into a finite type-to-type compatibility table could make a continuum model manageable, but it changes the learning mechanism. Likewise, optimizing a mass allocation to group compositions would require a new objective and would replace VALCAM’s sequential bidding process. Either could be a useful research project; neither is a mirror of a computational result in this paper.

That is a strong paper-specific negative case, not a proof that no scenario could ever yield a worthwhile continuous model. A future, explicitly designed system with stable learner classes and aggregate compatibility might support one. But the proponent’s scenario needs those modeling choices precisely because the paper supplies neither the objective nor the finite type structure; it establishes a possible adjacent project, not an anchored continuization.
