The continuous mirror question

Given a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over types \(t\), each a nonempty ordered tuple of rational locations in \([0,1]\), find a deterministic direct mechanism \(F\) returning \(F(\mu)\in[0,1]\). Define \(D_t(y)=\sum_j |y-x_{tj}|\), \(\delta_t=\min_{z\in[0,1]}D_t(z)\), \(\Delta_t=\max_{z\in[0,1]}D_t(z)\), and \(s_t(y)=1-(D_t(y)-\delta_t)/(\Delta_t-\delta_t)\). Require that no positive-mass coalition, jointly changing its reported types, can make almost all its members strictly better off, and that \(\max_{y\in[0,1]}\sum_t\mu_t s_t(y)\leq 2\sum_t\mu_t s_t(F(\mu))\).

The model it lives in

Use complete location tuples as types, with mass \(\mu_t\) and satisfaction determined by the full tuple. Return the weighted lower median of the reported type medians; evaluate social satisfaction as \(\sum_t\mu_t s_t(y)\).

The objection that survived

The positive-mass coalition condition omits finite-agent deviations that vanish in the continuum; its fit depends on whether positive-mass coalitions are the relevant deviations in the intended application.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

Covers only Theorem 1: desirable games with sum-variant satisfaction and social satisfaction. It leaves the other desirable-game results, the obnoxious-game results, and any claim of optimality among continuous mechanisms unaddressed.

Open questions for a prover

  • Prove the weighted-lower-median guarantee for every finite-support \(\mu\), including atoms and ties, under the stated positive-mass coalition condition.
  • Determine the best approximation ratio achievable by deterministic group-strategyproof mechanisms on this continuous domain.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper. It says that, in the desirable facility game with sum-variant satisfaction and social satisfaction as the objective, the mechanism that takes each agent’s median location and returns the median of those medians is group strategy-proof and achieves approximation ratio \(2\). The paper gives a two-agent example where this mechanism’s ratio is \(2\); that shows the mechanism’s bound is tight, not that no better strategy-proof mechanism exists. The paper has no named NP-hardness, polynomial-time-complexity, or parameterized-complexity theorem. Its relevant result is a constructive mechanism guarantee.

A credible high-multiplicity regime would be a regional service hub for many organizations, such as franchise units or service cooperatives, each with several sites and a preference for locating the hub nearby. Suppose sites lie on a corridor divided into standard zones, and many units share the same multi-site footprint. There might be tens of thousands of units but only dozens of distinct footprints. A type must include the whole ordered location tuple: the satisfaction normalization depends on the full tuple, even though the mechanism uses only its median.

Continuous Desirable Sum-Satisfaction Facility Mechanism. Let a type \(t\) be a nonempty ordered tuple \(x_t=(x_{t1},\ldots,x_{t\omega_t})\) of rational points in \([0,1]\). An instance is a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over these types, with rational masses summing to \(1\). For each type, let \(D_t(y)=\sum_j|y-x_{tj}|\), \(\delta_t=\min_{z\in[0,1]}D_t(z)\), and \(\Delta_t=\max_{z\in[0,1]}D_t(z)\). Its satisfaction at facility location \(y\) is
\[ s_t(y)=1-\frac{D_t(y)-\delta_t}{\Delta_t-\delta_t}, \]
and the social objective is \(\mathrm{SS}_\mu(y)=\sum_t\mu_t s_t(y)\).

The question is to give a deterministic direct mechanism \(F\), computable on every such distribution, that returns \(F(\mu)\in[0,1]\), is group strategy-proof, and satisfies
\[ \max_{y\in[0,1]}\mathrm{SS}_\mu(y)\ \leq\ 2\,\mathrm{SS}_\mu(F(\mu)). \]
For the incentive condition, treat the population as nonatomic and require that no positive-mass coalition, jointly changing its reported types, can make almost all its members strictly better off. This carries the paper’s coalitional incentive requirement into the distribution model while ignoring null-mass deviations.

The natural candidate solution is the weighted lower median of the reported type medians: return the smallest \(z\) for which the total mass of types with \(\operatorname{med}(t)\leq z\) is at least \(1/2\). It is polynomial-time computable in the support size and input bit length. The incentive rationale is the standard median argument for single-peaked preferences: each type’s sum-variant satisfaction is single-peaked at its median, and the weighted-median rule resists coordinated moves toward a coalition’s preferred side. The approximation bound also has a direct continuous analogue of the paper’s pairing proof. Pair types at opposite quantiles; their medians bracket the weighted median. The sum of their satisfactions is concave in the facility location and is at least \(1\) at either median, so it is at least \(1\) between them. Integrating these pairs gives \(\mathrm{SS}_\mu(F(\mu))\geq 1/2\), while the optimum is at most \(1\). A distribution with equal mass on the two profiles in the paper’s tight example preserves ratio \(2\) for this rule.

I expect this mirror to be tractable, analogous to Class A: the continuous mechanism is a weighted order statistic, and the proof structure survives mass weighting. The mirror covers only the paper’s desirable, sum-variant, social-satisfaction result in Theorem 1. It does not establish that ratio \(2\) is optimal over all continuous group-strategy-proof mechanisms. A natural further question is the best achievable ratio on this domain and how it depends on support size or restrictions on the set of site profiles.

The weakest point is the regime. Exact repeated multi-site profiles are plausible for organizations using a finite set of standard zone templates, but much less so for households whose locations vary freely. There is also a substantive modeling choice in the continuum incentive condition: a nonatomic individual has no effect on the outcome, so the meaningful requirement concerns positive-mass coalitions. The proposed mirror preserves the paper’s group-incentive focus, but a referee would reasonably ask whether that coalition notion fits the intended application.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the paper has no computational-complexity result to mirror. Theorem 1 proves group strategy-proofness and a distortion bound of \(2\) for a median mechanism. The other named results likewise concern mechanism guarantees and approximation bounds; none classifies the complexity of a computational problem or gives a complexity result for an algorithm. The proponent’s formulation is coherent, but it adds a new question to the paper rather than continuizing one of its computational results.

The proposed organization-template setting is plausible enough that I would not reject the mirror on multiplicity grounds. Treating each full site tuple as a type and applying a weighted median gives a reasonable continuous version of the mechanism. But its central claims concern incentive compatibility and distortion. Saying the weighted median is computable from the support makes the procedure explicit; it does not turn the paper’s mechanism-design theorem into a computational-complexity result.

A stronger follow-up—asking for the best distortion attainable by continuous group-strategy-proof mechanisms—still asks a structural mechanism-design question. Asking for the optimal facility location drops the incentive constraint and creates a different planner problem. Neither repairs the missing computational anchor.

So the negative case is strong against treating this as a ChoCo mirror, but weak against the broader claim that it could be worthwhile continuous mechanism-design research. The paper supports no such universal rejection: the proposed regime is sensible, and the continuous mechanism question appears well-posed. I cannot honestly argue that no scenario makes it worthwhile beyond the programme’s stated computational scope.
