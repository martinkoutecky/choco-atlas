The continuous mirror question

Given finite types \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu\), good classes \(G\) with rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\), additive values \(v_t\), and normalized influence weights \(a_t,k_{s,t}\), choose an integral bundle \(S_0\) for a tagged type-\(t\) agent and a distribution \(\pi\) over integral bundles for the remaining population. Require \(\sum_S\pi_S=1\), \(\sum_{S\ni g}\pi_S=q_g\), and \(S_0\subseteq\{g:q_g>0\}\); for couplings \(\lambda\) with row sums \(\mu_s\) and column sums \(\pi_S\), compute and return a maximizing pair \((S_0,\pi)\) for \(a_t v_t(S_0)+\min_{\lambda}\sum_{s,S} k_{s,t}\lambda_{s,S}v_t(S)\), with a minimizing coupling certificate.

The model it lives in

Use a mass distribution over complete agent types and over integral bundle patterns, with each good class supplied at rate \(q_g\), plus one explicitly marked focal agent whose bundle remains indivisible. Optimize the focal agent’s self-weighted bundle value plus the worst type-to-bundle mass coupling for all other agents.

The objection that survived

The stated \(\min_{S:\pi_S>0}\) lets a bundle of mass \(\frac{1}{N}\) impose the full self-weight term, so the distribution-only objective is not stable under weak convergence; an explicit marked focal bundle retains that information.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I see is the paper’s computation of an agent’s extended maximin share, in a regime where both the population and the inventory grow together. Imagine a city distributing standardized, indivisible benefit goods to many residents. Residents fall into a small number of types: each type fixes an additive value for every good class and a complete profile of influence on the other types. Each resident still receives an integral bundle; the city’s stock of each class scales at a fixed rate per resident. The number of residents \(N\) grows while the number of types \(\tau\) stays small.

The paper’s anchor is Observation 4.1, stated in this paper and supported by the cited NP-hardness of maximin partitioning from Woeginger (1997). It says that, given \(m\) items and \(n\) agents with externalities, finding the optimal partition is NP-hard. Here “optimal” means a partition maximizing the focal agent’s utility under the worst bijection of bundles to agents—the partition determining that agent’s \(\EMMS_i\). The paper’s observation transfers the earlier maximin-partition hardness to this problem; it does not present a new proof of the underlying maximin-partition hardness.

Here is a continuous version of that underlying problem. Let \(T\) be a finite set of agent types, with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one. Let \(G\) be a finite set of good classes, with rational per-capita supplies \(q_g\in[0,1]\); each resident can receive at most one indivisible copy of each class. Type \(t\) has additive value \(v_t(S)=\sum_{g\in S}v_{t,g}\) for bundle \(S\subseteq G\). Its influence profile has a self weight \(a_t\) and type-level weights \(k_{s,t}\geq0\), normalized by \(\sum_s\mu_s k_{s,t}=1-a_t\).

A partition is now a distribution \(\pi\) over integral bundles: \(\pi_S\) is the fraction of the population receiving bundle \(S\), and \(\sum_{S:g\in S}\pi_S=q_g\) for every good class \(g\). For a focal type \(t\), let \(\Lambda(\mu,\pi)\) be the set of mass couplings between recipient types and bundles: \(\lambda_{s,S}\geq0\), with row sums \(\mu_s\) and column sums \(\pi_S\). The Continuous EMMS Partition Problem asks, for a specified type \(t\), to compute
\[ \operatorname{CEMMS}_t(\mu,q) = \max_{\pi} \left[ a_t\min_{S:\pi_S>0}v_t(S) + \min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\mu,\pi)} \sum_{s,S} k_{s,t}\lambda_{s,S}v_t(S) \right] \]
and return an optimal \(\pi\), with a worst-case coupling as a certificate. Equivalently, its decision version asks whether some feasible \(\pi\) guarantees at least a given rational threshold. The first term captures the focal agent’s own bundle; the second captures the worst assignment of the remaining bundle mass across recipient types.

This has a concrete high-multiplicity interpretation. If the rational masses and bundle frequencies have common denominator \(N\), a finite realization has \(N\mu_t\) residents of each type, \(Nq_g\) indivisible copies of each good class, and \(N\pi_S\) residents receiving each bundle. Thus \(\pi\) is the limiting frequency of integral bundles, not a fractional bundle given to an individual. The worst coupling is the type-level version of the paper’s adversarial bijection. The intended running-time parameters are \(m=|G|\), \(\tau=|T|\), and the input bit length \(L\).

I think the authors would recognize this as their optimal-partition problem in a sensible high-multiplicity regime: preferences and influence are properties of types, the inventory scales with the population, and the adversary still reallocates whole bundles. This case covers Observation 4.1’s EMMS-partition computation. It does not claim to mirror the paper’s separate existence or allocation guarantees.

My provisional expectation is Class B, hardness transfers: the population counts become fractional, but the choice of which integral bundle patterns can meet the supply constraints still carries combinatorics in the goods. The central question for a prover is whether that bundle geometry keeps the problem hard, or whether a configuration LP and tractable pricing make this version Class A. A related question is how quickly finite high-multiplicity instances converge to the mass optimum, and whether the answer depends on \(\tau\).

The weak point is that replication can change the optimum: the continuous model admits rational bundle frequencies that may not exist in the paper’s original finite instance. That is a real relaxation, not a technicality, and could dissolve the discrete hardness. The mirror’s case rests on the problem remaining meaningful after that relaxation; it does not rest on claiming that Observation 4.1’s NP-hardness automatically survives.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Observation 4.1 is the only serious anchor, and it is a named computational claim: finding the optimal partition is NP-hard. Its cited hardness comes from maximin partitioning, so I can’t dismiss it as an axiomatic result or as prior continuous work. The strongest mirror is a homogeneous population with self-only influence, reducing the question to maximin share, with a fixed set of good classes supplied at positive rates per resident. That is a coherent high-multiplicity regime, and the authors could recognize its mass allocation over integral bundles as their partition problem. This already makes a universal case against difficult.

The best objection I can make is that the proposed CEMMS objective is not a stable limit of the paper’s objective. In the finite model, the focal agent’s self weight \(a_t\) is attached to one distinguished recipient. As the population grows, the empirical type and bundle distributions forget that individual. The formula restores the self contribution by taking the minimum over the support of \(\pi\). Consequently, a bundle with mass \(1/N\) can affect the focal agent’s utility by \(a_t v_t(S)\), even as its mass vanishes and the limiting distribution no longer records it. The common-denominator construction shows that a particular rational distribution has a finite realization; it does not show that the EMMS values converge stably to the proposed objective.

A repair would tag a focal agent, give that agent a separate whole bundle, and model everyone else continuously. That is a sensible hybrid, but the focal individual and their indivisible bundle remain outside the population measure. If instead the model erases the tag, it loses the paper’s self-weight contribution. This makes the proposed mirror’s connection to a continuous society less clean than its notation suggests, and could leave the essential EMMS question dependent on a vanishing-mass exception.

That objection does not defeat the tagged-agent version. Nor can I rule out a useful continuous optimization question for the homogeneous, repeated-goods regime: the paper’s partitioning hardness does not make that question unworthy, regardless of its answer. So I can challenge the claimed limit semantics, but I cannot honestly establish that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario.
