The continuous mirror question

Given finite male and female type sets \(A,B\), rational masses \(\lambda_a,\nu_b\), male rankings \(R_a\) over \(B\), and each female type's true and reported rankings \(P_b,Q_b\) over \(A\), compute the reported-profile deferred-acceptance flow \(x^0\). For each \(b\), split off tagged mass \(\epsilon\) with true ranking \(P_b\) and an arbitrary report \(Q'_b\); run a specified type-level deferred-acceptance rule on the split population, including an explicit rule for male-side indifference between the tagged and ordinary parts. Decide whether some \(b,Q'_b\) gives a flow that is \(P\)-stable for every sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\) and satisfies \(\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_a x^\epsilon_{a,b,*}\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a)<\frac{1}{\nu_b}\sum_a x^0_{ab}\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a)\), where \(P\)-stability means no type pair has positive mass on both sides preferring each other to their assigned partners.

The model it lives in

A two-sided population with finite support over preference types and rational masses; deferred acceptance returns a matching flow, and a tagged infinitesimal receiver type may change its report. The decision compares its limiting expected true rank with its type's baseline and requires true stability of the perturbed flow.

The objection that survived

The model replaces rankings over named individuals with rankings over types, and the tagged subgroup's allocation needs a specified rule for male-side indifference; the proponent acknowledges the coarsening but does not fully resolve that mechanism detail.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 8's unilateral total-stability test. It leaves group manipulation, the paper's structural lemmas as separate results, and identity-level partner outcomes outside its scope.

Open questions for a prover

  • Which explicit rule for allocating proposals across the tagged and ordinary parts yields a replica-consistent fluid deferred-acceptance outcome?
  • Can the decision problem be solved in time polynomial in the type support sizes and input bit length?
  • Under what conditions does the tagged-agent limit agree with one-agent deviations in large finite replications?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case I can defend is a mirror of Total Stability, which I would call *Infinitesimal Total-Stability Testing*.

The anchor is Theorem 8, proved in this paper. It says that, given true preferences \(P=(P(M),P(W))\) and stated preferences \(Q=(P(M),Q(W))\), with complete strict lists and men proposing in Gale–Shapley, Total Stability can be decided in \(O(n^4)\) time. The answer is No exactly when some woman can permute her list so that the resulting men-optimal matching is stable under \(P\) and gives her a strictly better partner under her true ranking than \(\mu_Q\); otherwise the answer is Yes. This is the paper’s main computational result, and the proposed mirror keeps its core question: can a receiver-side agent misreport, improve her true outcome, and still leave a truly stable match?

A plausible regime is a very large resident–position or worker–job matching market in which many people share a small number of complete preference and priority profiles. For example, thousands of applicants might fall into a few hundred types distinguished by their rankings of job categories and the priorities those categories assign them. A type records every feature the model uses: a male-side type’s ranking of female types, or a female-side type’s true ranking and stated ranking of male types. The support sizes \(\tau_M,\tau_W\) may be much smaller than the population. Continuum matching and deferred-acceptance models already provide suitable market machinery; the relevant prior work is background for the model, not a result about the computational complexity of manipulation with a true-stability constraint. [Greinecker and Kah on pairwise stable matching in large economies](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.3982/ECTA16228), [Carmona and Laohakunakorn on deferred acceptance in large marriage markets](https://openresearch.surrey.ac.uk/esploro/outputs/bookChapter/A-Deferred-Acceptance-Algorithm-for-Large/991120436102346).

Here is the continuous problem. The input has finite sets of male types \(A\) and female types \(B\), with binary-rational masses \(\lambda_a,\nu_b\) summing to one on each side. Each male type \(a\) has a strict order \(R_a\) over \(B\). Each female type \(b\) has a true order \(P_b\) and a reported order \(Q_b\) over \(A\). The fluid version of men-proposing Gale–Shapley operates on these masses: proposals are made by type, each female type holds its most-preferred incoming male-type mass up to its mass \(\nu_b\), and any excess rejected mass continues down the proposing types’ lists. The resulting matching is a flow \(x_{ab}\geq 0\) with row and column sums \(\lambda_a\) and \(\nu_b\). Each individual still gets one partner; fractional \(x_{ab}\) records the share of a type matched to another type.

For this mirror, a flow is \(P\)-stable when no type pair blocks with positive mass on both sides. In particular, \((a,b)\) blocks if
\[ \sum_{a':\,a\succ_{P_b}a'}x_{a'b}>0 \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{b':\,b\succ_{R_a}b'}x_{ab'}>0. \]
That is, some positive mass of type \(b\) is matched below \(a\) in its true ranking, and some positive mass of type \(a\) is matched below \(b\) in its ranking.

To keep the deviation unilateral, split a female type \(b\) into its ordinary mass \(\nu_b-\epsilon\) and a tagged subpopulation of mass \(\epsilon\), both with true order \(P_b\), but let the tagged subpopulation report a single alternative order \(Q'_b\). Run fluid Gale–Shapley on this perturbed input, obtaining \(x^\epsilon\). Define its expected true rank by
\[ \rho^\epsilon_{b,*} =\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_a x^\epsilon_{a,b,*}\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a), \]
and define the ordinary type’s baseline expected rank from the unperturbed flow \(x^0\) by
\[ \rho_b^0 =\frac{1}{\nu_b}\sum_a x^0_{ab}\operatorname{rank}_{P_b}(a). \]
Infinitesimal Total-Stability Testing asks whether there is a type \(b\) and a list \(Q'_b\) such that, for every sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\), the perturbed flow is \(P\)-stable and
\[ \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\rho^\epsilon_{b,*}<\rho_b^0. \]
The decision answer is Yes if no such deviation exists; a No instance has a type, a report, a stability witness, and a strict limiting gain. In a replication with \(N\) people, the tagged mass corresponds to one person’s \(1/N\) share as \(N\) grows. This is a limit version of the paper’s unilateral deviation, not a positive-mass coalition deviation.

I would expect this question to be tractable (Class A) in \(|A|,|B|,\tau_M,\tau_W\) and the input bit length, perhaps with the continuous answer collapsing to a direct stability check in some regimes. The flow algorithm works over finitely many types, and a deviator’s outcome ranges over finitely many partner types; the paper’s active-partner search suggests a way to avoid enumerating all reported permutations. That is a conjectural route, not an implication of Theorem 8. The paper proves no hardness anchor here, so hardness transfer is not the expectation. Useful follow-up questions are whether the tagged-agent limit agrees with large finite replications, whether an analogue of the paper’s uniqueness-for-a-fixed-partner result holds for limiting flows, and what precision bounds control finite-\(N\) approximations.

The scope is deliberately narrow: this mirrors Theorem 8’s Total-Stability test, not every result in the paper. The soft spot is that the paper uses strict rankings over named individuals, while this model ranks type categories and averages over within-type assignments. The \(\epsilon\)-split preserves a single deviator in the limit, but the fluid mechanism and within-type allocation still need to be made acceptable to the authors as a faithful high-multiplicity version. If named identities and exact one-to-one outcomes are essential to their problem, this mirror may fail.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has one clear computational anchor: Theorem 8, the \(O(n^4)\) test for total stability. So the case against cannot rest on the absence of a result or on the prospect that a continuous answer might be easy. The difficulty is that the theorem’s problem is built around individually named partners: a woman may permute a list of \(n\) men, and the test checks whether the resulting *individual matching* is stable under the true lists.

That identity matters when defining types. In a complete, strict stable-marriage instance, each person has a distinct rank in every opposite-side list. A type that preserves how an agent is treated in the matching problem therefore needs to retain those rank positions, not just the agent’s own preference order. In ordinary instances, this leaves essentially one type per person as the market grows, so the proposed population compression does not take hold.

The best case for a mirror groups people by preference for *categories* and matches flows between those categories. That is a coherent population matching model, but it changes what the paper tests. Members of a category become interchangeable as partners, so the tagged \(\epsilon\)-mass deviator can improve by changing the order of categories, while the paper’s woman can improve by changing the order of named men. Expected category rank does not recover her true rank among individuals. The infinitesimal split preserves the size of the deviation; it does not preserve its identity-level consequences.

One could keep those consequences by giving agents rank coordinates within each category and specifying how preference lists vary over those coordinates. Then the input must encode a preference field over identities, and the mechanism must say how the infinitesimal agent is matched and how stability is evaluated at that resolution. That may define a worthwhile new continuum matching problem, but it is no longer a finite-support mirror of Theorem 8: the aggregate type masses do not determine the data the test needs.

This is the strongest negative case, but it has a real weak point. If the authors accept preferences over categories and a tagged infinitesimal deviator as a natural version of their game, the mirror is well-posed and the paper’s computational question survives in that new model. The argument against depends on the authors regarding that change in partner identity and preference semantics as too large for the question to remain their problem.
