The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raised no eligible anchors, and I agree: the paper has no numbered result about the complexity of a computational problem. It presents itself as a visual exploration. Its main calculations enumerate 282,376 normalized weight vectors for \(n=m=3\), compute the generalized Penrose–Banzhaf index, and plot the results. The index is defined in equation (1); the equivalence-class counts are computational findings, with several credited to earlier work. There is no algorithmic, approximation, parameterized, or hardness result here for a continuous-population version to mirror. The programme’s complexity trichotomy has nothing to classify.

The strongest rescue would be to model a society as a distribution \(\mu\) over the six rankings of three candidates, then define a continuous analogue of the paper’s influence measure. But equation (1) asks whether changing *one player’s* ranking changes the winner. In a continuum population, changing one individual’s ranking leaves \(\mu\), the scores, and the winner unchanged: that person has measure zero. A tie does not help, since the change still leaves the aggregate scores unchanged. To get nonzero influence, one must instead perturb a positive mass of voters. That creates a different question—such as how much mass must switch rankings to change the winner—and requires choices about the size and form of the perturbation. It could be a worthwhile mass-robustness or bribery problem, but it is not the paper’s individual pivotality measure.

A second rescue is to treat the paper’s three players as large, homogeneous blocs and let each bloc change its ranking as a unit. That is coherent, but it keeps three players whose weights already range over a continuous simplex; it does not introduce a continuous population of individual voters. If bloc members instead vote separately, the unit of influence changes and the paper’s index no longer applies.

I cannot establish that every nearby continuous influence or robustness question would be uninteresting. Some may be good ChoCo projects. The negative case is narrower and stronger: none is a mirror of a named computational result in this paper, because the paper establishes no such result.
