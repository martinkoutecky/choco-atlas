The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has no named computational result to mirror. Its contribution is empirical: across sampled finite instances, LLMs tend to retain high social-welfare approximation as EF1 approximation falls, and prompt changes have limited effects. The MNW guarantee cited as a baseline is prior work, not a result of this paper. The proponent is right that there is no anchor to restate as a continuous computational question.

The strongest candidate would be to represent agents by a distribution \(\mu\) over complete valuation types. But with finitely many indivisible goods, any allocation gives nonempty bundles to at most finitely many agents. A mass description either discards those zero-mass recipients—and with them the allocation the paper evaluates—or keeps pointwise constraints on them, which cannot be captured by the type masses alone. Scaling the goods with the population and tracking a distribution of bundles could make a sensible continuous fair-division problem. It would also change what the LLM is asked to output: the paper tests a model’s choice of one allocation for a finite instance, not its construction of a mass allocation.

Another option is to sample larger finite societies from \(\mu\) and average the LLM’s fairness and efficiency scores. That could extend the empirical study, but it would test how results scale with sample size; it would not create a complexity question about computation over a continuous society. Prompting the LLM with weighted valuation types instead would be a new experiment, since the paper’s results do not establish that its behavior is invariant to that compressed input.

So the negative case is strong within ChoCo’s stated scope: the best proposed continuizations lead to a new fair-division model or a larger empirical study, not a continuous mirror of a computational result in this paper. The universal claim that no related continuous study could ever be worthwhile is too broad to prove; a type-based large-market study might be worthwhile on its own. But that possibility does not supply the missing anchor.
