The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is that the paper has no computational result to continuize. Propositions 3 and 4 construct disconnected fallback coalitions, with Proposition 4 giving a seven-player threshold; Proposition 10 constructs a disconnected build-up coalition with five players. These are structural existence claims. The paper gives no complexity classification for finding the first majority coalition, testing whether it is disconnected, or computing a build-up outcome. Its “algorithmic” approach describes a formation process; it does not analyze the computational cost of running it. None of those propositions supplies the kind of result ChoCo is meant to mirror.

One could formulate a new mass version: represent the society by a distribution over complete preference types, define a threshold at which a mutually acceptable coalition has majority mass, and ask whether the first such coalition is disconnected. A build-up version could merge masses using weighted positions. Those questions may be coherent, and the authors might recognize them as extensions of FB or BU. But the paper does not give a computational claim whose continuous counterpart they would be. Establishing complexity for those variants would start a new computational study from the paper’s models; it would not continuize one of its results.

That is a strong negative verdict under the requested anchor standard. It does not prove the broader claim that no worthwhile research question can be built from this paper: the mass versions could themselves be worth studying. The honest limit is that their potential value cannot rescue this paper as a mirror of a computational result, because it has no such result to anchor the mirror.
