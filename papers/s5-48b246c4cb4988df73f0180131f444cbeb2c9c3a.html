The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a paper-anchored mirror is that the paper proves no computational result of the kind ChoCo is meant to continuize. Theorem 2 gives a structural correspondence between stable matchings and closed subsets of a meta-rotation poset. Theorem 1 is also structural, and is cited from earlier work. The paper presents enumeration, counting, and optimization algorithms as consequences to develop, not as results with established complexity. The polynomial algorithms mentioned in the introduction are Abraham et al.’s background results. Under the programme’s stated gate, there is no named computational result here to mirror.

Theorem 2 is the proponent’s strongest hook, but asking whether stable *mass* assignments have a rotation-poset representation remains a structural question. Turning it into a continuous optimization problem—say, finding a minimum-cost stable mass assignment—could be worthwhile, and fits the paper’s stated future directions. But it would mirror an unproved research agenda, not a result of this paper. The same applies to a continuous version of finding any stable matching: the paper cites a discrete algorithm for that task, but does not contribute it.

The proposed cohort model also needs more than student-preference types. In SPA-S, lecturers have strict rankings over students, so a type complete for this problem must account for lecturer priorities too. Repeating a type while retaining strict individual rankings can make the support grow with the cohort. A small support would require a natural variant with shared priority tiers or ties, plus mass-level blocking rules and capacities \(c_j,d_k\) that scale with the population. That may be a sensible model, but the paper’s meta-rotation proof depends on a unique worst student and cycles of individual students; a mass version would need new definitions and results. This is a reason the poset claim cannot be carried over as stated, not a reason the model is inherently uninteresting.

So the proponent establishes no surviving *paper-anchored computational* mirror. The universal claim that no scenario could yield a worthwhile continuous question is harder to defend: a cohort model with type-level priorities could support a sensible computational study. Its weakness is that it attaches to the paper’s future agenda, not to a computational result proved here.
