The case FOR (proponent)

I cannot make the required anchor-based case: the paper contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational-complexity result. Its closest formal result says the generalized mechanism produces an R-stable matching that is optimal among R-stable outcomes. Other results concern strategy-proofness, Bayesian equilibrium, or stability. Those are mechanism properties, not claims that a computational problem is in P, hard, or otherwise classified; the paper also reports simulations. The deferred-acceptance algorithm’s efficiency is mentioned in the introduction as established background, not as a numbered result proved here.

There is a plausible *new* ChoCo question in the paper’s admissions setting, but it would not mirror a qualifying anchor. Consider a large annual applicant cohort in which many students share the same relevant attributes: exam score, preference ranking, and any other information used by the mechanism. Let each type \(t\) have population mass \(\mu_t\). Treat programme capacities as per-capita capacities, and let \(x_{t,c}\) be the mass of type \(t\) assigned to programme \(c\), with an unmatched option. The computational question would be to return the student-proposing R-deferred-acceptance allocation for this mass society. It is plausible that the authors would recognize the admissions model, and a regime with many applicants but relatively few observed types is credible. A mass-valued version of deferred acceptance looks likely to be tractable when the type support is moderate, though that would need to be established.

The weakest point is decisive for this task: that proposed problem is a follow-up motivated by the paper’s subject, not a continuous mirror of one of its required named complexity results. There are therefore no anchors and no lead anchor to enumerate. The paper’s properties of stability and incentives may support future computational questions, but they do not supply the required result for this case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no named computational-complexity result here to mirror. The paper’s formal results concern stability, optimality and incentives. Its statement that deferred acceptance is efficient is background attributed to Gale and Shapley; the paper uses the algorithm as a black box. The proposed mass-valued deferred-acceptance problem therefore starts a new computational question rather than continuing one this paper poses.

That proposed question does pass a key modelling test. Large applicant cohorts with repeated combinations of exam score and preference ranking are plausible, and per-capita programme capacities give a sensible mass allocation. I would not dismiss it on the grounds that a continuous admissions model is unnatural. Still, the paper does not study computing allocations over types, optimizing over stable allocations, or classifying the complexity of any such task. Adding welfare optimization would introduce a new objective; turning the incentive results into computation would require a different question about individual or coalition deviations. Those may be worthwhile projects, but they are not mirrors of the paper’s results.

So the negative case is strong for this paper as a source of ChoCo anchors: the proponent supplies none, and the allocation problem they suggest is a follow-up. It is weaker as the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous question could arise from the admissions setting. The cohort and type structure are credible enough that a separate study of mass-valued deferred acceptance could be useful. I cannot honestly rule out that possibility; it does not, however, provide the computational mirror this case was asked to assess.
