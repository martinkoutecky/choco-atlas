The continuous mirror question

Given institutions \(I\), rational capacities \(q_i\), finite complete student types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), type preferences and institution priorities, categories \(C\), and rational bounds \(\ell_i^c,u_i^c\), choose masses \(x_{t,i}\ge 0\) with \(\sum_i x_{t,i}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{t,i}\le q_i\). Require positive-mass stability: no type \(t\) has mass assigned to an institution it ranks below \(i\) while \(i\) has capacity or assigns positive mass to a lower-priority type. Minimize \(\delta\) subject to \(\ell_i^c-\delta\le\sum_{t:c\in A_t}x_{t,i}\le u_i^c+\delta\) for every \(i,c\), and return an aggregate student-optimal solution among the minimizers.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity school-choice market with finitely many complete student types carrying masses \(\mu_t\), per-capita institutional capacities, positive-mass stability, and a minimax objective over overlapping diversity-quota violations.

The objection that survived

The positive-mass stable assignments need not be convex, as the opponent's midpoint example shows, so the claimed finite-to-continuous bridge and the suggested LP or DC-DA route require a new stability theorem.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers minimax overlapping-category diversity optimization and its zero-violation feasibility case from Theorems 5 and 4; it leaves arbitrary institutional objectives, sibling co-assignment, and two-sided objectives outside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does positive-mass stability over finitely many types admit a lattice or polyhedral characterization supporting an efficient analogue of DC-DA?
  • Can rational continuous solutions be rounded to finite replicated markets with a controlled additive diversity-violation guarantee?
  • Do finite priority bands preserve the intended high-multiplicity regime without changing the relevant stability notion?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a high-multiplicity school-choice mirror of the paper’s diversity results, with Theorem 5 as the lead anchor.

The paper’s Theorem 5, proved in this paper, states that a student-optimal stable matching among the \(\egalopt\) stable matchings can be found in \(O(|E||S|)\) time. Here \(\egalopt\) means minimizing the maximum diversity violation \(\divv_\infty\), including both lower- and upper-quota violations. The result is obtained by repeatedly relaxing the transformed upper bounds and invoking the paper’s DC-DA algorithm. The supporting Theorem 4, also proved here, says that DC-DA finds a diverse stable matching if one exists, reports that none exists otherwise, and returns the student-optimal one among all diverse stable matchings.

A natural continuous problem is therefore \(\textsc{Minimax-Diverse-Stable-Matching}_{\infty}\).

An instance contains a finite set of institutions \(I\), rational capacities \(q_i\), a finite set of student types \(T\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{T}\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\). A type \(t\) completely specifies the relevant student information: its acceptable institutions, strict preference order \(\succ_t\), category membership \(A_t\subseteq C\), and all priority-relevant attributes. Each institution has a strict priority order over the types. Thus two students belong to the same type only when the problem treats them identically.

The decision variable is \(x_{t,i}\ge 0\), the mass of type \(t\) assigned to institution \(i\). We also write \(x_{t,\emptyset}=\mu_t-\sum_i x_{t,i}\) for unmatched mass. The constraints are \(\sum_i x_{t,i}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{t,i}\le q_i\). Each unit of mass receives one integral institution assignment; only the aggregate assignment is fractional.

Stability is the direct positive-mass version of the paper’s blocking-edge definition. A pair \((t,i)\) blocks \(x\) if some positive mass of type \(t\) is assigned to an institution, or left unmatched, that it ranks below \(i\), and institution \(i\) either has unused capacity or assigns positive mass to a type that it ranks below \(t\). A feasible \(x\) is stable if no such positive-mass blocking move exists.

For every category \(c\), define \(a_{i,c}(x)=\sum_{t:c\in A_t}x_{t,i}\). The problem asks for a stable mass assignment minimizing

\[ \Delta_\infty(x)= \max_{i,c}\left\{ (\ell_i^c-a_{i,c}(x))^+,\, (a_{i,c}(x)-u_i^c)^+ \right\}, \]

and, among all minimizers, returns one that is student-optimal in aggregate preference order: for every type \(t\), every preference prefix \(P\), and every other optimal stable assignment \(y\), it maximizes \(\sum_{i\in P}x_{t,i}\) relative to \(\sum_{i\in P}y_{t,i}\). The zero-value case is exactly the continuous diverse-stable-matching problem from Theorem 4.

This is a genuine high-multiplicity regime. Consider a national school-allocation system with millions of applicants but perhaps hundreds or a few thousand recurring profiles. A profile can encode a common school ranking, eligibility, socioeconomic and geographic categories, and priority band. Capacities and quota bounds are naturally expressed as fractions of the applicant population. The relevant quantity is not “\(547\) named students” but “\(0.03\) of the applicant population.” This is precisely the kind of setting in which \(N\gg |T|\) is credible. A small hiring committee would not be a convincing scenario; a large recurring admissions market would.

The finite-to-continuous bridge is exact for rational data. If \(N\mu_t\), \(Nq_i\), \(N\ell_i^c\), and \(Nu_i^c\) are integers, one obtains a finite market by replicating each type \(t\) \(N\mu_t\) times and scaling capacities and quotas by \(N\). A finite matching induces \(x_{t,i}\) by division by \(N\); conversely, a rational mass assignment can be scaled into an integral cohort assignment. Thus the mirror is not merely replacing counts by percentages: it is the high-multiplicity version of the same stable-allocation problem. The algorithmic target is polynomial in \(|I|\), \(|T|\), \(|C|\), and the rational input length, without expanding a denominator \(N\) that may be exponentially large.

I would expect this problem to be Class A, although that is a proposed continuation rather than a theorem of the paper. The natural formulation is to optimize \(\delta\) over the positive-mass stable-assignment region subject to

\[ \ell_i^c-\delta\le a_{i,c}(x)\le u_i^c+\delta. \]

The continuous analogue of DC-DA would operate on type masses, forbidding or partially removing low-priority mass when a bound is violated. Alternatively, a type-level stable-matching polytope or a configuration LP could be combined with these linear quota constraints. The important research question is whether the paper’s rural-hospitals and stable-lattice structure survives aggregation strongly enough to give a polynomial algorithm in the number of types rather than in the expanded population.

The authors should recognize this as their problem. Stability remains the primary feasibility condition; diversity remains a soft institutional objective; overlapping categories remain allowed; institutions and capacities remain discrete market-side objects. The only continuization is the student population, with per-capita capacities and quotas scaling as required by the high-multiplicity interpretation. This is not a lottery over fractional seats or an outcome-space relaxation.

The weakest point is that Theorem 5’s proof is written for individually distinct students and integer, one-student-at-a-time relaxations. In the mirror, a type may have mass split at an institutional cutoff, and the maximum violation \(\delta\) is rational rather than an integer number of students. A batch version of BreakMarriage, together with a proof that the type-level stable region retains the needed lattice or polyhedral structure, is still required. If institutional priorities are genuinely idiosyncratic lotteries, the number of complete types may grow with \(N\), destroying the intended regime. The mirror therefore needs finite priority bands or another repeated-priority application.

That vulnerability affects whether the continuous problem is tractable, not whether it is a legitimate mirror. It could lead to a Class A algorithm, or reveal a continuum-specific boundary where the finite DC-DA argument fails. The main follow-up questions are whether one-to-one overlapping-category counting remains tractable, what additive rounding guarantee returns from the continuous solution to a finite market, and whether arbitrary institutional objectives from Theorem 6 remain efficiently optimizable once stable local assignments can contain fractional cutoff masses. I would deliberately leave the sibling-family objective and the full arbitrary-set-function theorem outside the primary claim: the diversity result alone is a strong, concrete continuous mirror.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the proponent’s “exact high-multiplicity bridge” is not yet exact. The paper’s model has strict priorities over named students. Once students are compressed into types, either the priority order within a type is discarded, or it is supplied by an individual tie-breaker that the type distribution does not contain. The former gives a matching problem with ties, where the lattice, Rural Hospitals, and BreakMarriage arguments used by Theorems 4 and 5 no longer follow automatically; the latter makes the hidden tie-breaker part of the type, potentially destroying high multiplicity.

Nor does ordinary fractional assignment repair this. In the one-to-one special case, consider two stable matchings
\[ M=(a,A),(b,B),(c,C) \]
and
\[ M'=(a,B),(b,C),(c,A), \]
with preferences
\[ a,b:A\succ B\succ C,\qquad c:C\succ B\succ A, \]
and priorities
\[ A:c\succ a\succ b,\quad B:a\succ c\succ b,\quad C:a\succ b\succ c. \]
Both matchings are stable. Their midpoint assigns half of every type according to each matching. At \(B\), positive mass of \(c\) is assigned below \(B\), while positive mass of \(b\), whom \(B\) ranks below \(c\), is present. Thus \((c,B)\) is a positive-mass blocking pair. The midpoint is therefore not stable under the proponent’s natural mass-blocking definition.

This matters because the continuous feasible region is not the ordinary stable-matching polytope. A linear fractional relaxation admits assignments that are not stable in the paper’s sense; preserving positive-mass stability introduces complementarity conditions rather than linear constraints. Consequently, the suggested type-level LP or configuration formulation is not available without a new stability theorem. The integer relaxation loop in Theorem 5 also does not transfer: \(\delta\) is rational, and “remove the worst student” becomes a partial-mass operation whose preservation of the stable lattice and student-optimality must be proved afresh. Theorem 4 does not rescue this, since its correctness relies on exactly those individual-level lattice and rejection arguments.

There are three possible repairs, but each changes something important. Linear fractional stability changes the blocking notion and can accept mixtures that are not finite stable matchings. Positive-mass stability preserves the blocking intuition but produces a nonconvex, not obviously LP-solvable problem. Arbitrary strict tie-breaking preserves the paper’s formal model but makes identity information computationally relevant and can eliminate the intended small type space.

These objections do defeat the proponent’s claim that Theorem 5 already supplies an exact continuous mirror with an evident LP/DA route. They do not, however, defeat the stronger negative claim requested here. A school system with finitely many preference and priority bands, per-capita capacities, and a carefully defined type-level stability notion is plainly a sensible high-multiplicity problem. Its difficulty may be Class A, Class C, or require a new rounding theorem, but that unresolved boundary is itself worthwhile under ChoCo. Thus the honest negative case is weak: the stated formulation is under-specified and its bridge is unproved, but I cannot convincingly argue that no repaired continuous mirror deserves study.
