The continuous mirror question

Given a fixed finite asset set \(A\) and rational masses \(\mu_t\) over complete rational utility tables \(u_t:2^A\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), let integer populations \(N_t\) satisfy \(\sum_t N_t=N\) and \(N_t/N\to\mu_t\). For each type, compute \(Q_t^+=\max_{\pi\in\Pi(A)}\sum_{S\in\pi}u_t(S)\) and \(Q_t^-=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(A)}\sum_{S\in\pi}u_t(S)\), where \(\Pi(A)\) contains all partitions into nonempty blocks. Output partitions attaining both extrema, the limiting aggregate guarantee \(\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_tN_t\Gamma_N^{DC}(u_t)=\sum_t\mu_tQ_t^-\), and the D&C1 scaled safe-bid density \(Q_t^+-Q_t^-\) for each type.

The model it lives in

The society is a distribution \(\mu\) over complete utility-table types; each owner still receives an integral bundle of indivisible goods. For each type, the decision is to find utility-maximizing and utility-minimizing partitions, and the objective is the limiting aggregate D&C guarantee \(\sum_t\mu_tQ_t^-\), with D&C1 bid density \(Q_t^+-Q_t^-\).

The objection that survived

At fixed \(m\), only finitely many agents can receive nonempty bundles, so the limit is an aggregate guarantee rather than a nonatomic implementation; the proponent acknowledges this limitation but does not establish that the authors would treat the limiting statistic as a standalone computational problem.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Proposition 2's D&C guarantee and D&C1 safe-bid asymptotics. It leaves the B&S guarantee, implementation details, and the paper's welfare-efficiency comparisons aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the type-mass limit of the B&S guarantee be characterized and computed?
  • What asset-supply scaling with population size yields a nondegenerate limit that retains bundle-allocation effects?

The case FOR (proponent)

Lead mirror: the large-cohort Divide & Choose guarantee-density problem. Imagine a large group of co-owners dividing a fixed collection of \(m\) indivisible assets, with cash available for transfers. The number of owners is much larger than the number \(\tau\) of distinct valuation types. A type \(t\) is a complete utility table \(u_t:2^A\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), with \(u_t(\varnothing)=0\); agents of that type share the same preferences over every bundle. The society is a distribution \(\mu\) over these types. Each finite approximation still gives every owner an integral bundle; only the type counts and aggregate guarantee are represented by masses.

My anchor is Proposition 2, proved in this paper. It says that in D&C1 an agent’s safe play is to bid \(\mathrm{MaxMin}_n(u)-\mathrm{MinMax}_n(u)\), choose a partition maximizing \(\sum_{S\in\pi}u(S)\) if made Divider, and report transfers that equalize utility across its shares. In D&C2, safe play is to propose such a maximizing partition, equalize utility across the proposed partition guarantees, then report truthful transfers in the final auction. Both rules give the same guarantee:
\[ \Gamma_n^{DC}(u)=\frac{1}{n^2}\max_{\pi\in\mathcal P(n,A)}\sum_{S\in\pi}u(S) +\frac{n-1}{n^2}\min_{\pi\in\mathcal P(n,A)}\sum_{S\in\pi}u(S). \]
Proposition 2 also establishes that this guarantee is positive, responsive, and lies in the paper’s duality interval \([\mathrm{MinMax}_n(u),\mathrm{MaxMin}_n(u)]\).

Here is the continuous question. Given \(A\), the \(\tau\) rational utility tables, and masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, compute the large-cohort D&C guarantee density. For each type define
\[ M_t^+=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal P(A)}\sum_{S\in\pi}u_t(S), \qquad M_t^-=\min_{\pi\in\mathcal P(A)}\sum_{S\in\pi}u_t(S), \]
where \(\mathcal P(A)\) contains partitions into nonempty blocks. Return
\[ G(\mu)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\mu_t M_t^-, \]
along with partitions attaining \(M_t^+\) and \(M_t^-\) for each type. In D&C1, also return the limiting scaled safe bid \(M_t^+-M_t^-\).

This is the aggregate guarantee from Proposition 2 in the large-population limit. Once \(n\ge m\), allowing \(n\) shares—including empty shares—gives the same maximum and minimum as \(\mathcal P(A)\). Thus
\[ n\Gamma_n^{DC}(u_t)=\frac{M_t^+}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}M_t^-\longrightarrow M_t^-. \]
If \(N_t\) agents have type \(t\) and \(N_t/N\to\mu_t\), their total guaranteed welfare tends to \(G(\mu)\). Proposition 2’s safe bid is \((M_t^+-M_t^-)/N\), so \(N\) times that bid tends to the reported bid density. The individual guarantee shrinks with population size while the sum across the population has a finite limit.

The authors should recognize this mirror: it keeps their Divide & Choose rule, utility domain, safe-play guarantees, and aggregate-welfare interpretation. It changes the population description to type masses, exactly grouping agents who are indistinguishable under the utilities the problem uses. The mirror does not make bundles fractional.

For explicit utility tables, the problem is tractable: subset dynamic programming computes both partition extrema in \(O(\tau 3^m\operatorname{poly}(L))\) arithmetic time, where \(L\) bounds rational bit length. That is polynomial in the explicit table input size and independent of population size in the limit; it remains exponential in \(m\). I expect Class A for this encoding. This is not a hardness-dissolution result: the paper offers no comparable hardness anchor here, and the partition computation is still driven by the goods, not by how many agents there are. The paper’s Section 8.1 likewise identifies the extremal partitions as the computational step.

The weakest point is that the paper does not define D&C for a literal nonatomic society: selecting an individual Divider and allocating indivisible goods are atomic acts. My mirror is the limit of the paper’s aggregate guarantee, not a claim that a continuum of owners can directly execute the rule. That narrower claim still has a strong foothold in Proposition 2 and the paper’s own collective-welfare analysis. It also suggests further questions: whether the general B&S guarantee has a similarly useful large-cohort limit, how the picture changes when the asset supply grows with the population, and whether there is a direct continuum mechanism with indivisible bundles.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proposed anchor has a concrete error: the paper allows empty shares, but the proposed \(M_t^-\) minimizes only over partitions into nonempty blocks. For Frugal utility \(u_F\), the paper’s minimum is \(1\): put all goods in one share and leave the others empty. The proposed minimum is \(m\), attained by splitting the goods into singletons. So the stated guarantee density and scaled safe bid do not follow from Proposition 2.

The natural repair is to minimize over partitions with *at most* \(m\) nonempty blocks, allowing empty shares. Call the corrected extrema \(Q^+(u)\) and \(Q^-(u)\). Then, for \(n\ge m\),
\[ n\Gamma_n^{DC}(u)=Q^-(u)+\frac{Q^+(u)-Q^-(u)}{n}. \]
The aggregate limit \(\sum_t\mu_tQ^-(u_t)\) is coherent. But it is only a limit of the paper’s individual guarantee: with a fixed set of goods, at most \(m\) agents receive any goods, while every individual guarantee tends to zero. A continuum of owners cannot carry out the rule’s pivotal act of selecting a Divider and assigning indivisible bundles. The aggregate is a type-weighted sum of per-agent worst-case values, rather than a computation over mass transfers or a continuous version of the mechanism.

Scaling the goods with the population is the strongest alternative. The paper does study identical goods, so this is not automatically outside its scope. But preserving its general utility domain would require describing preferences over bundles of a growing set of goods; compressing that description requires extra structure such as utilities that depend only on the number of identical goods. That could produce a worthwhile separate question, but it is not supplied by the proposed mirror, and I cannot rule out that the authors would recognize it.

So the original anchor is wrong as stated, and its simplest correction yields a thin asymptotic statistic rather than a continuous D&C mechanism. Still, that does not establish the requested universal negative: a growing supply of identical goods remains a plausible direction, especially given the paper’s own treatment of that case. The honest negative case is weak there.
