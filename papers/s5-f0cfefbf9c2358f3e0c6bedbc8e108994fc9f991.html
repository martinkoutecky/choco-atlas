The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is the paper’s max-of-average result. The population becomes continuous while the districts and finite set of alternatives remain as in the paper. In each district \(d\), let \(\mu_d\) be a probability distribution over voter locations on the line. For a finite-support instance, write \(\mu_d=\sum_j p_{dj}\delta_{z_{dj}}\), with rational masses \(p_{dj}\). A type is a district and a location: that determines the voter’s ranking and their distance cost to every alternative. The total population can be large while the number \(\tau\) of distinct district-location types stays moderate. A plausible regime is a large electorate in a handful of districts, with many residents sharing a small number of location or distance profiles.

For alternative \(a\), the continuous version of the paper’s objective is
\[ F_\mu(a)=\max_{d\in D}\sum_j p_{dj}|z_{dj}-a|. \]
Each district’s measure is normalized separately because the original objective averages within districts and then takes the maximum across districts.

My anchor is Theorem 3.1, proved in this paper. It states that for max-of-average cost, the distortion of the Rightmost-of-\(\alpha\)-Leftmost mechanism is at most
\[ \max\left\{\frac{3-\alpha}{1-\alpha},\frac{2}{\alpha}-1\right\}. \]
That mechanism chooses, in each district, the favorite alternative of its \((\alpha n_d)\)-th leftmost agent, then returns the rightmost district representative. Corollary 3.2, also proved here, sets \(\alpha=(3-\sqrt5)/2\) and gives the bound \(2+\sqrt5\). These are algorithmic performance results, not complexity-class results: the paper does not name an NP-hard, polynomial-time, or parameterized-complexity theorem. The anchor is therefore a mechanism-design result at the edge of ChoCo’s complexity remit.

Here is the corresponding continuous problem. Given finitely many alternatives at rational positions, finitely many districts, and rational finite-support measures \(\mu_d\), design a deterministic ordinal distributed mechanism that chooses one alternative per district and then selects one of those representatives. The objective is to minimize the worst-case ratio of \(F_\mu\) at the selected alternative to \(\min_{a\in A}F_\mu(a)\). A solution is a rule and a proof of its worst-case guarantee. For fixed \(\alpha\), define \(q_d(\alpha)\) as the left \(\alpha\)-quantile of \(\mu_d\); choose as \(y_d\) the alternative preferred by a voter at that location, then return the rightmost \(y_d\). The question is whether this rule always satisfies
\[ F_\mu(w)\le \max\left\{\frac{3-\alpha}{1-\alpha},\frac{2}{\alpha}-1\right\} \min_{a\in A}F_\mu(a). \]
At \(\alpha=(3-\sqrt5)/2\), the target factor is \(2+\sqrt5\).

This is a natural lift of the paper’s problem: voter counts and rank positions become district mass and mass quantiles; sums become weighted sums; the ordinal information, representative selection, finite alternatives, and objective retain their roles. The proof’s cardinality arguments become mass arguments, so the same bound is expected to hold. On a finite-support encoding, quantiles, representatives, costs, and the best alternative can all be computed in time polynomial in \(k,m,\tau,L\). The paper reports that prior work [5] supplies a matching \(2+\sqrt5\) lower bound for the finite line setting; finite profiles embed as atomic measures, so that lower bound carries to this continuous version. Thus the expected answer is a tractable mechanism with the same optimal distortion, rather than continuum-specific hardness.

The mirror covers Theorem 3.1 and its optimized bound in Corollary 3.2; it does not claim to continue every result in the paper. Useful follow-up questions are whether the same guarantee extends to arbitrary compactly supported measures without finite support, and how the mass-based analysis behaves for the paper’s other objectives or for general metrics.

The soft spot is that this paper studies distortion guarantees for voting mechanisms, not the complexity of bribery, control, campaigning, or robustness. Its named theorem does not establish a complexity classification, and its mechanism looks easy to compute. The positive case is strongest as a direct, author-recognizable population mirror of a named algorithmic result; it is weaker as an example of the specific complexity landscape ChoCo aims to chart.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is the kind of result being continued. Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 give distortion guarantees for a distributed voting mechanism. They do not classify the complexity of an instance problem or give an algorithm for bribery, control, campaigning, or winner robustness. The mechanism itself is simple rank-based selection; the theorem’s contribution is its worst-case social-cost guarantee. So this paper supplies no named complexity result for ChoCo to mirror.

The proposed continuous model is nevertheless coherent. With finitely many districts and alternatives, each district can have a measure over voter locations, and the max-of-average objective becomes \(F_\mu(a)=\max_d\int |z-a|\,d\mu_d(z)\). Mass quantiles naturally replace rank positions. Large districts with repeated location profiles are a plausible high-multiplicity regime. That defeats objections based on identity, vanishing costs, or confusing population continuity with continuous alternatives.

The best repair would ask for the optimal ordinal distortion over mechanisms on these measure-valued societies, perhaps with an algorithm that synthesizes such a mechanism. But that is a global mechanism-design problem: its output is a rule over a family of societies, and its computational content depends on how rules are represented and what mechanism class is allowed. It does not become a ChoCo-style instance problem merely by encoding each society with finite-support rational measures. Specifying those representation and search questions would create a new project, rather than continue a computational result in this paper.

So the negative case is strong for excluding this paper from ChoCo’s target complexity landscape, but weak as a universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. The max-of-average result has a sensible population mirror; the case against is that it belongs to adjacent distortion and mechanism-design work, not to the programme’s stated problem family.
