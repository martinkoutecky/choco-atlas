The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The appended “CASE FOR” raises no continuous mirror or named result to defend. Its central point is sound: the paper’s Theorem 1 characterizes cascade propagation analytically, while the cited NP-hardness result concerns prior work on a different detection model. Neither supplies the computational result that ChoCo normally uses as an anchor.

There is one qualification: Algorithm 1 is a genuine computational contribution, with running time \(O(2^kN)\) for memory length \(k\). But that bound concerns evaluating the thresholds of the paper’s chosen scheme. It does not characterize the complexity of finding an optimal strategy or establish a tractable or hard class of instances. The paper formulates that optimization in (6), then leaves it open as formidable. A continuous mirror of the algorithm’s runtime would therefore mirror a specific implementation, not a complexity result about the problem.

The strongest alternative would take a large sensor deployment with a few sensing and attack profiles and ask for its limiting detection performance, or for an optimal policy under a false-alarm constraint. Such a deployment is a plausible high-multiplicity regime. The obstacle is that each node’s decision depends on its place in the sequence and on the history of earlier broadcasts. A distribution over sensor types does not preserve that history. Adding sequence position and observation state to each type makes the input grow with the deployment; replacing the history with an aggregate fraction changes the information available to nodes. Either way, the resulting problem needs substantial new modeling beyond this paper’s named results.

A continuum limit of the cascade dynamics could still be a useful research question. It would primarily study limiting behavior of a sequential stochastic process, though; the paper offers no computational theorem for that limit to inherit. The negative case is therefore strongest as a judgment about this paper’s fit for ChoCo, rather than as proof that no worthwhile continuous model could ever be built. The plausible sensor population is the main weakness in the case against: it makes the regime credible, even though this paper does not turn that regime into a computational complexity result.
