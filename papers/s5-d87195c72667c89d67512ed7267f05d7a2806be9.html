The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no computational result here for a continuous mirror to inherit. Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 characterize classes of rules and their dominance lattices; the propositions establish geometric and axiomatic relations. None defines a computational problem or gives an algorithm or complexity bound. That is a substantial gap under ChoCo’s remit.

The household-by-peak scenario is the strongest repair. A distribution \(\mu\) over peak allotments and a type-level allocation \(x(p)\) could describe a continuum economy. Scaling the resource with population would be a legitimate co-modification, so divisibility and resource normalization are not objections. But this changes what the paper studies. Its strategy-proofness and peak-only conditions are properties of a rule across preference profiles, and its lattice orders whole rules across profiles. For one society \(\mu\), those are not instance-level questions; replacing them with a search for an envy-free allocation and an ordering of allocations changes the object of the theorems.

Keeping the global rule questions would require specifying how rules over distributions are represented, what deviations count, and how to encode or compare the resulting functions. The paper supplies none of that. Those choices could support a new project, but they would create its computational problem rather than mirror one in this paper.

So the negative case is strong within the programme’s stated scope, though not a claim that no worthwhile research could grow out of the paper. Its axiomatic results may inspire new continuous-computation questions; the paper itself does not anchor one.
