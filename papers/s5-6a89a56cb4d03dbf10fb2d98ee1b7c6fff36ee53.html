The continuous mirror question

Given finite item categories \(G\), valuation types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), weights \(\eta_t\), and monotone submodular valuations \(v_t:2^G\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}\), choose masses \(x_{t,S}\ge 0\) of type-\(t\) agents receiving each integral bundle \(S\subseteq G\), subject to \(\sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le b_j\), maximizing \(\left(\prod_{t,S}v_t(S)^{\eta_t x_{t,S}}\right)^{1/\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t}\), or determine the best achievable approximation factor.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity allocation model with valuation types \(t\), population mass \(\mu_t\), integral per-agent bundles \(S\subseteq G\), per-capita category supplies \(b_j\), allocation masses \(x_{t,S}\), and population Nash welfare given by the mass-weighted geometric mean of \(v_t(S)\).

The objection that survived

The mirror replaces distinct one-copy goods by replicated categories whose valuation depends on the supported set \(S\subseteq G\), so the claimed two-way correspondence with the theorem’s exact hard instances requires an explicit valuation-on-copies construction.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the submodular NSW hardness result in Theorem 4.1 and the associated optimization problem; it leaves the additive and submodular approximation algorithms, constant-agent result, and fairness results unanchored.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the Khot–Lee–Moitra–Moshkovitz gap remain valid when each item category has per-capita supply and mass can be split across integral bundles?
  • What is the complexity of the pricing problem \(\max_{S\subseteq G}\{\eta_t\log v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j\}\) for the relevant valuation representations?
  • For which valuation classes or fixed \(|G|\) does the resulting configuration formulation admit an efficient approximation algorithm?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s submodular Nash-welfare problem, anchored in Theorem 4.1.

The paper proves Theorem 4.1 itself, although its reduction uses the hardness result of Khot et al.: unless \(\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}\), there is no polynomial-time \(e/(e-1)\)-factor approximation for submodular \(\NSW\), even when all agents are symmetric and have identical monotone submodular valuations. Equivalently, one cannot always obtain more than a \(1-1/e\) fraction of optimum in polynomial time.

A natural continuous population is a large population of customers, households, or service recipients drawn from a small number of recurring demand profiles. A type \(t\) consists of the complete information relevant to the problem: a monotone submodular valuation \(v_t:2^{G}\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\), entitlement weight \(\eta_t\), and any other parameters affecting allocation. The population is a rational distribution \(\mu=(\mu_t)_{t\in T}\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The intended regime is \(N\gg |T|\): for example, millions of recipients but only a few dozen exact valuation and entitlement types. The strongest special case is \(|T|=1\), corresponding to a large cohort of equally entitled agents with identical diminishing-returns preferences—the very restriction used by Theorem 4.1.

Items remain indivisible for every individual. What scales with the population is the supply: item category \(j\) has per-capita supply \(b_j\), so a finite population of size \(N\) would have \(Nb_j\) copies. This is a co-modification forced by high multiplicity, not continuity of the outcome space. A person still receives an integral bundle \(S\subseteq G\); only the fraction of the population receiving each bundle is divisible.

The resulting problem can be stated precisely.

Continuous Submodular Nash Welfare. An instance consists of a finite item-category set \(G\), finite type set \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\), rational positive weights \(\eta_t\), and value-oracle descriptions of monotone submodular valuations \(v_t\). A solution is a family of nonnegative masses \(x_{t,S}\), one for every type \(t\) and integral bundle \(S\subseteq G\), satisfying

\[ \sum_{S\subseteq G}x_{t,S}=\mu_t \]

for every type \(t\), and

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\ni j}x_{t,S}\le b_j \]

for every item category \(j\). The mass \(x_{t,S}\) is the fraction of type \(t\) receiving the integral bundle \(S\). The objective is the population Nash welfare

\[ \operatorname{CNSW}(x) = \left( \prod_{t\in T}\prod_{S\subseteq G} v_t(S)^{\eta_t x_{t,S}} \right)^{1/\sum_t\eta_t\mu_t}, \]

with the usual convention that assigning positive mass to a zero-valued bundle makes the objective zero. The computational question is to output a feasible \(x\) maximizing this quantity, or, in the approximation version, to achieve a specified factor such as \(e/(e-1)\).

This is recognisably the authors’ problem: the valuation functions, integral bundles, weights, and geometric-mean welfare objective are unchanged. Only the agent population is represented by masses over repeated complete types. For rational \(x\), clearing denominators produces a finite allocation with repeated agents and repeated item categories; conversely, every such finite high-multiplicity allocation induces an \(x\). Thus this mirror has the required two-way high-multiplicity dictionary.

I would expect the general problem to be Class C, with a possible Class B result if the Khot–Lee–Moitra–Moshkovitz gap can be shown to survive fractional assignment of type mass. The configuration LP has exponentially many bundle variables, and its pricing problem is essentially

\[ \max_{S\subseteq G} \left\{ \eta_t\log v_t(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j \right\}, \]

for dual item prices \(p_j\). Even after population continuization, optimizing over integral bundles for a general submodular valuation remains the computational bottleneck. For restricted valuation classes or fixed \(m\), the configuration LP may become tractable; for general submodular valuations, the pricing problem itself may generate continuum-specific hardness.

The main unresolved issue—and the weakest point—is that atomless mass can be split across bundles. Theorem 4.1’s finite reduction does not automatically transfer: a fractional mixture of integral bundles may achieve higher Nash welfare than any allocation of the original finite number of agents. Therefore one must not simply claim that the paper’s \(e/(e-1)\) hardness carries over. The correct downstream question is whether the reduction’s welfare gap remains valid for these mass mixtures. If it does, the mirror is Class B; if it does not, the failure identifies exactly how continuization dissolves the discrete obstruction.

That vulnerability does not undermine the mirror itself. The question remains a faithful continuous-population formulation of the paper’s central optimization problem, in a plausible repeated-type regime, and it exposes a substantive new boundary between configuration-LP tractability, transferred hardness, and hardness created by integral bundle pricing.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s formulation does not cleanly continuize Theorem 4.1. It replaces the paper’s one-copy allocation problem by a different ex-ante configuration problem, and the distinction is structural.

Take the theorem’s hard instances: \(n\) identical agents, one copy of each of \(m\) distinct items, and one common submodular valuation \(v:2^G\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\). If the item supply remains fixed while the population becomes atomless, each item can reach only measure zero of the population. For the usual \(v(\varnothing)=0\), the continuous Nash welfare is then \(0\). Thus a nondegenerate mirror must scale item supply with population.

But scaling supply requires a new valuation model. The proponent treats each original item as a category and restricts every bundle to \(S\subseteq G\), so an individual can receive at most one copy of each category. Clearing denominators therefore produces repeated item categories with perfect-substitute semantics, not an instance of the paper’s allocation problem with distinct indivisible items. If repeated copies may be assigned together, the valuation must instead be defined on multisets, and the fixed-domain model \(v:2^G\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\) disappears. The claimed two-way dictionary is consequently not established for the theorem’s actual instances.

Under the substitute-category repair, the continuous problem becomes

\[ \max_x \sum_{t,S} x_{t,S}\log v_t(S) \]

subject to linear type-mass and category-supply constraints. In the anchor case \(|T|=1\), the population distribution is just \(\mu_1=1\); all substantive computation is a fractional configuration LP for one valuation function. The pricing problem

\[ \max_{S\subseteq G} \left\{\eta\log v(S)-\sum_{j\in S}p_j\right\} \]

is a single-agent demand problem. That may be an interesting allocation problem, but the “continuous society” has effectively vanished: what remains is a lottery over integral bundles. Making heterogeneous types meaningful would produce a genuinely new model, but then the identical-agent reduction underlying Theorem 4.1 no longer anchors its computational behavior.

The theorem’s hardness gap also does not transfer automatically. Its low case bounds the welfare of an integral allocation of the original items. A mass mixture over repeated categories can use fractional configuration structure unavailable to that allocation. The proponent correctly admits that the gap may disappear; without resolving that issue, the theorem supplies no continuous hardness result, nor even evidence that the particular obstruction survives.

This is not an airtight universal negative. A plausible cohort model with scarce per-capita service categories and substitute-copy semantics does exist, and its configuration LP could be worth studying. The strongest honest conclusion against the proponent is narrower: their formulation is under-specified at the item-replication step, and after the natural repair its population component collapses to a standard fractional allocation model. I would therefore regard the negative case as weak against the broader programme, though sufficient to reject the claimed direct continuization of Theorem 4.1 as written.
