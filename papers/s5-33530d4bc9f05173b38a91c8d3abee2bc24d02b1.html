The continuous mirror question

Given \(l\) lanes with initial queue masses \(Q_j\) and \(\tau\) ordered cohorts with rational masses \(\mu_t\), values of time \(\theta_t\), and accessible lane sets \(A_t\), compute a feasible routing \(x_{tj}\) minimizing \(\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\theta_t\sum_{j\in A_t}\left[x_{tj}\left(Q_j+\sum_{s<t}x_{sj}\right)+\frac{x_{tj}^{2}}{2}\right]\), subject to \(x_{tj}\ge 0\), \(x_{tj}=0\) for \(j\notin A_t\), and \(\sum_{j\in A_t}x_{tj}=\mu_t\). What is the exact computational complexity in \(l\), \(\tau\), and the input bit length?

The model it lives in

A continuous vertical-queue model with ordered cohorts as types, rational mass \(\mu_t\), shared value of time \(\theta_t\), lane access \(A_t\), and initial queue masses \(Q_j\). The decision variables \(x_{tj}\) route each cohort's mass across lanes; the objective is total value-of-time-weighted delay, including delay from initial queues, earlier cohorts, and mass within the cohort.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the vertical-queue grand-coalition efficiency subproblem computed by Proposition 1. It leaves the partition-specific coalition values, strong-core and relaxed-core stability claims, and the dynamic horizontal-queue results aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the ordered structure yield an exact efficient algorithm for the mass-routing objective, particularly for fixed \(l\) and growing \(\tau\)?
  • Does the cohort fluid objective arise as a limit or useful additive approximation of finite vertical-queue instances?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I see is the paper’s static vertical-queue efficiency problem, in the regime of large, repeated arrival cohorts. This is a narrower claim than continuizing the paper’s whole partition-function game, but it keeps the feature the authors are optimizing: routing vehicles among parallel queues to reduce value-of-time-weighted delay.

My anchor is Proposition 1, proved in this paper. It says that the recursion for the vertical-queue case has running time \(O(n(n+l)^l)\). The proof bounds the number of terminal histories by the number of \(l\)-combinations with repetition, \(\binom{n+l-1}{n}\), and multiplies by the \(n\) recursion levels. Thus, for a fixed number \(l\) of lanes, this bound is polynomial in the number \(n\) of vehicles; when \(l\) varies, the exponent grows with \(l\). This is a runtime result for the paper’s recursion, not a proof that the strong core is nonempty. I would mirror its grand-coalition efficiency subproblem, which the recursion computes, rather than claim a continuous result about core stability.

Call the proposed problem Vertical-Queue Mass Routing. There are \(l\) lanes with initial queue lengths \(Q_j\), and an ordered list of \(\tau\) arrival cohorts. Cohort \(t\) has mass \(\mu_t\), value of time \(\theta_t\), and a set \(A_t\) of lanes it can access. Masses are rational proportions of a large platoon, with \(n\gg\tau\): for example, many connected vehicles from a commuting corridor, arriving in a few scheduled waves, with a small number of shared VOT and access classes. A type records those shared attributes and the cohort’s arrival position. Within a cohort, vehicles are treated as an anonymous fluid stream that preserves FCFS order.

Choose \(x_{tj}\), the mass of cohort \(t\) routed to lane \(j\), subject to
\[ x_{tj}\ge 0,\qquad \sum_{j\in A_t}x_{tj}=\mu_t,\qquad x_{tj}=0\ \text{if }j\notin A_t. \]
Find an \(x\) minimizing total weighted delay:
\[ \sum_{t=1}^{\tau}\theta_t \sum_{j\in A_t} \left[ x_{tj}\left(Q_j+\sum_{s<t}x_{sj}\right) +\frac{x_{tj}^{2}}{2} \right]. \]
The first two terms account for the initial queue and earlier cohorts routed to the same lane. The \(x_{tj}^{2}/2\) term is the accumulated delay within that cohort’s fluid mass. A solution is a feasible lane-flow matrix \(x\) attaining minimum cost. In the finite election-style bridge, \(x_{tj}\) is the fraction of that cohort’s vehicles routed to lane \(j\); individual vehicles still choose a lane.

The authors would, I think, recognize this as a fluid version of their vertical-queue routing objective: it retains parallel lanes, queue lengths, heterogeneous VOT, arrival order, and aggregate delay minimization. I expect a Class A result in the intended regime of fixed \(l\) and moderate \(\tau\): the mass formulation has only \(\tau l\) routing variables, and the paper’s fixed-lane recursion gives some reason to look for tractability. But that is a conjecture, not a consequence of Proposition 1. The displayed objective is not obviously convex when a later cohort has higher VOT than an earlier one, so the algorithmic question is whether its ordered structure permits an exact tractable method. With both \(l\) and \(\tau\) unrestricted, continuum-specific hardness remains possible.

The important caveat is that this mirrors efficiency, not the paper’s central strong-core stability claim. The finite recursion distinguishes named vehicles by their position in a multilevel Stackelberg game and computes coalition values for partitions. Vertical-Queue Mass Routing has no named-agent blocking coalitions or imputation constraints. The continuum makes cohort fractions and fluid queue loads natural, but it does not by itself continuize the paper’s core.

That is also the mirror’s weakest point. Arrival position affects delay, so a fully literal type description could make every position a distinct type and destroy the small-support regime. My proposal treats ordered cohorts as types and internal rank as an anonymous fluid coordinate. That is a familiar traffic-flow idealization and keeps the mass model meaningful, but it is a co-modification of the paper’s vehicle-level model that the authors or a referee might reject. The next questions would be whether finite discrete instances converge to this objective with a useful additive error, and whether a mass-based imputation can satisfy a continuous analogue of the paper’s strong-core constraints.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that Proposition 1 analyzes an ordered recursion, not an anonymous population problem. At each step, the next vehicle acts on a history of lane choices, and its position determines which earlier choices affect its delay. Equal value of time does not make two positions interchangeable: changing one vehicle’s lane changes the delays faced by later vehicles. So in an exact high-multiplicity encoding, arrival position must remain part of the type, and the support can grow with the number of vehicles.

The proposed cohort mirror gets around this by grouping consecutive vehicles with the same value of time and lane access, then allowing the group’s mass to split across lanes. That is a plausible fluid traffic model, but it changes the computational object. Its variables describe aggregate lane flows; they do not retain the one-at-a-time decisions whose histories Proposition 1 counts. A still more faithful continuum could keep arrival rank as a continuous coordinate, but then the input is a distribution over an ordered coordinate, rather than the paper’s finite list of types with rational masses. A finite approximation returns to cohorts, with results depending on how the order is partitioned.

This does not establish that mass routing is uninteresting. A large, repeated platoon with a few VOT and access classes is credible, and its aggregate delay objective is well-defined. The negative case is that this is a new fluid optimization model, not a clear continuization of the paper’s computational result; the paper’s main stability claims are conjectural or simulation-based, and the only numbered complexity result concerns the ordered recursion. That case is real but weak: the cohort model may be a mirror the authors would recognize, so I cannot honestly rule out a worthwhile continuous mirror in every scenario.
