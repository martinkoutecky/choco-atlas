The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 40 classifies Arrovian voting systems; Theorem 67 extends that axiomatic classification to measurable societies. Neither states a computational problem, gives an algorithm, or establishes a complexity bound. Theorem 67 is therefore the closest foothold only because it says “measurable society”—but that does not make it a computational result to mirror. Its continuous treatment is already an axiomatic one, which is outside ChoCo’s scope.

A better computational proposal might ask how to evaluate one of these rules from a population distribution over preference types. But that would add a new task to the paper’s results. The classification is organized around \(\Delta\), a map on subsets of named voters, or, in the measurable version, filters over measurable subsets. A distribution over types records how much mass holds each preference; it does not record those arbitrary coalitions. Preserve the coalition structure and the input is no longer just a high-multiplicity society. Require the rule to depend only on type masses, and anonymity removes the rich classification: the paper itself shows that, for finite societies, anonymous Arrovian rules are only the trivial rule and the strong Pareto rule (Corollary 29). Computing those from a support of types would not mirror the classification theorem.

So none of the proposed anchors survives as a continuous computational mirror. The negative case is strongest on the absence of a computational result; it is not a proof that no useful computational question could ever be attached to Arrovian aggregation. The objection is that such a question would be new work adjacent to this paper, rather than a continuization of a result it establishes.
