These are meaningful results, but none supplies the computational-complexity anchor your rubric requires.

If I had to offer a lead candidate, unanchored mirror, it would be *Continuous Cost-Type Committee Design*. Imagine a large civic cohort with many citizens but only \(\tau\) participation-cost classes: class \(j\) has cost \(c_j\) and population mass \(\mu_j\). Given the paper’s common-value parameters \(q,\pi,p\), a planner chooses drafted masses \(x_j\in[0,\mu_j]\) and a voting threshold \(\kappa\) to maximize per-capita expected decision welfare minus participation costs. This has a plausible high-multiplicity regime, \(N\gg\tau\), and it mirrors the paper’s planner choosing jury size and aggregation rule. It covers only planner design, not the voluntary-participation games or subsidy mechanism.

The difficulty is that the atomless limit erases the paper’s central sampling trade-off. For any positive drafted mass, the continuum has signal shares \(1-p\) under innocence and \(p\) under guilt. A threshold \(\kappa\in(1-p,p]\) therefore distinguishes the states perfectly, regardless of how small the drafted mass is. With positive participation costs, the planner can approach zero cost while retaining perfect accuracy, so the optimum may not be attained. Individual pivotality also disappears, changing the participation game. Requiring a minimum drafted share would make the design problem tractable by selecting low-cost mass, but it adds a condition that does not preserve the paper’s finite-jury error trade-off.

That is the soft spot: the population-and-cost-type story is credible, but the clean continuum model changes the informational mechanism the paper studies. Preserving its finite-sample noise would require additional scaling or stochastic structure, and the paper’s named results do not anchor that extension.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s strongest concession is also the main problem: the paper has no named computational result to mirror. Proposition 2 is an asymptotic claim about optimal jury size; Propositions 3 and 5 compare equilibrium participation with the social optimum. Lemmas 1 and 2 restate results from earlier work. The paper’s computation is a scalar choice of jury size and voting threshold using binomial error probabilities. That leaves no complexity result for ChoCo to continuize.

The proposed cost-type committee design is the best candidate, but it runs into a structural problem. If the committee is a positive fraction \(x\) of an atomless population and signals retain the paper’s fixed accuracy \(p>1/2\), then the fraction of guilty signals is exactly \(1-p\) under innocence and \(p\) under guilt. A threshold between those values distinguishes the states perfectly for every \(x>0\). With positive per-member costs, the planner can approach zero committee mass while retaining perfect accuracy. The limiting design has no attained optimum: at \(x=0\) there are no signals, while every positive \(x\) incurs a cost. The paper’s claimed \(n^*(N)/N\to0\) points to the same boundary, as do its vanishing participation rates. The mass-based model erases the finite-sample error trade-off those results study.

A stronger version could keep the committee as a finite random sample from a continuous pool of cost types. That retains binomial errors, but when \(n=o(N)\), every positive-mass type has enough members to supply the finite committee as \(N\) grows. The type masses then stop affecting the design; \(n\) remains an integer and the central stochastic problem is the paper’s original one. If instead the committee has positive mass, aggregation becomes exact and the trade-off collapses. The voluntary-participation results face a related limit: an individual of zero mass cannot be pivotal, so the paper’s participation incentive and informational externality disappear.

One could preserve nontrivial aggregate noise by adding ingredients such as signal accuracy \(p_N=\tfrac12+\theta/\sqrt{N}\), or correlated signals. Those could support interesting models, but they change the paper’s fixed-accuracy, conditionally independent signal environment and supply no computational anchor from its results. The negative case is strongest for the paper as written; it cannot rule out every altered model. Its point is that those alterations look like new projects, while the paper’s own results either lack a computational target or lose their defining trade-off under population continuization.
