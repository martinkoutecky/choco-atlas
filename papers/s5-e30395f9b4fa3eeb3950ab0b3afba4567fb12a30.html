The case FOR (proponent)

There is no qualifying anchor in this paper. Its numbered statements are Definitions 1–7, not Theorems, Lemmas, Corollaries, or Propositions about computational complexity. Tables 1–3 and Figures 2–16 report empirical prediction accuracy; they do not establish a named complexity result. The universal approximation results and the NP-hardness of Kemeny–Young and Dodgson are cited from other works, not proved here as numbered results. So I cannot honestly give an anchor-linked continuous problem or assign it a place in the programme’s trichotomy.

The strongest plausible positive lead is a new, unanchored formulation of the paper’s winner-prediction task. Let each type \(t\) be a complete ranking of \(q\) alternatives, and let a society be a rational distribution \(\mu\) over rankings. For a voting rule \(R\), its label is the winner set \(F_R(\mu)\), computed from the type masses. Given a finite training set \(\{(\mu^{(i)},F_R(\mu^{(i)}))\}_{i=1}^s\), a specified two-layer MLP architecture, and a bound on the bit length of its rational weights, ask for weights minimizing the number of training societies on which the network predicts the wrong winner set. The solution is the weight matrices. A plausible regime is a large electorate with many repeated voters but few distinct ranking types, so \(\tau\ll N\); archived elections or constituencies could supply the training societies.

This preserves the paper’s central activity—training a network to predict winners from profiles—while making the population the input as type masses. But the paper’s experiments use small finite profiles sampled under impartial culture. Moving to societies with low support relative to electorate size, and choosing a distribution over such societies, changes that experimental setting. Under impartial culture, as the electorate grows, the empirical type distribution concentrates near uniform, which may leave little variety for learning. The proposed formulation is therefore a credible direction for a mirror, but it is not a result of this paper, and it cannot support the requested complexity comparison.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent is right that this paper supplies no named computational result to mirror. Its numbered items are definitions; its own results are empirical comparisons of neural-network predictions. The NP-hardness claims are cited from other papers, not established here. So there is no result of this paper to place in ChoCo’s complexity trichotomy.

The proposed learning problem is the strongest possible rescue: let each example be a rational distribution \(\mu\) over rankings, label it with \(F_R(\mu)\), and train a fixed MLP on examples drawn from a chosen distribution over societies. That is coherent, but it is a new experiment rather than a continuous mirror of the paper’s result. The paper trains on fixed-size profiles of individual ballots, encoded as concatenated pairwise comparisons, and measures performance on profiles drawn under impartial culture. Its sample size is the number of profiles used for training—not the number of voters in a society.

The proposed task changes both the network’s input, from individual ballots to type masses, and the sampling model, by requiring a distribution over societies. Under impartial culture, growing the electorate concentrates those masses near the uniform distribution; avoiding that concentration requires choosing a different distribution over societies. The resulting findings would depend on that choice and on the new architecture. Even a careful version with sparse rational distributions and a permutation-invariant network would study learnability over a simplex, not the paper’s reported phenomenon: how an MLP trained on finite profiles aligns with familiar voting rules.

I cannot rule out that this new learning study would be worthwhile. That is the weak point in the negative case: it is hard to establish that no sensible distribution over societies could make it valuable. But the proposed study is not anchored in a computational result of this paper, and the paper does not determine the extra modelling choices it needs. On this evidence, it is a possible follow-up project, not a worthwhile continuous mirror of this paper for ChoCo.
