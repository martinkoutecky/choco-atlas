What the mirror covers

Theorem 1 and Corollaries 1–3 are axiomatic impossibility results; no computational result is covered by a continuous mirror.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive foothold is the housing-market setting itself. A plausible high-multiplicity regime is a large community sharing facilities by time slot: each type is a complete strict ranking of the houses together with its per-capita fractional endowment rights, and \(\mu_t\) is that type’s share of residents. An allocation would specify a per-capita bundle \(x_t\), with feasibility
\[ \sum_t \mu_t x_t(h)=\sum_t \mu_t e_t(h) \]
for each house \(h\). This is a coherent population-continuous version of the market, and the authors’ discussion of time shares makes the scenario plausible.

But the paper has no named computational result to anchor a continuous-computational-choice mirror. Theorem 1, proved here, says that no fractional housing-market mechanism can be simultaneously weakly SD-strategyproof, SD-efficient, and SD-individually rational, even with strict preferences and single-unit endowments and allocations. Corollaries 1–3 give related axiom incompatibilities. These are mechanism-existence results, not claims about an algorithm, complexity, or the computational difficulty of a specified problem.

One could propose a follow-on question: given a rational mass distribution over preference-and-endowment types, determine whether there is a direct mechanism satisfying those three properties. But that is not yet a faithful, ready-to-hand mirror. In a nonatomic population, one person’s report has zero mass; requiring incentive compatibility against a positive-mass cohort instead changes the paper’s individual strategyproofness condition. And asking only for a feasible SD-IR, SD-efficient allocation would drop the strategyproofness issue that Theorem 1 is about.

So the honest case for a continuous *population model* is plausible, but the paper supplies no qualifying computational anchor, and I cannot give the requested continuous computational problem per anchor without changing the result’s question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper’s main result is Theorem 1, but it is an existence theorem about axioms, not a computational result: no mechanism can be simultaneously weakly SD-strategyproof, SD-efficient, and SD-individually rational. The corollaries add related incompatibilities, not algorithmic claims. That leaves no named computational problem for the programme to continuize.

The proposed time-sharing market is a plausible continuous-population model. Give each type a ranking and a per-capita endowment, and impose feasibility on the mass-weighted allocation. But the best attempt to mirror Theorem 1 still runs into a mismatch. In a nonatomic society represented only by its type distribution, one person’s report changes no mass. If the mechanism depends only on that distribution, unilateral strategyproofness becomes vacuous. To make an individual deviation affect their own assignment, the mechanism must also see individual identities or reports; then the type distribution alone no longer specifies its input. Treating each positive-mass type as a strategic bloc restores consequential deviations, but changes individual strategyproofness into a bloc-deviation condition.

Even if that bloc model is worthwhile, the paper’s theorem would not supply a computational question. Strategyproofness is a property across reports and markets, not something to test from one rational distribution. One could define a new computational problem by specifying a restricted domain and a representation of mechanisms, or study how to compute SD-IR, SD-efficient allocations. Neither is a complexity mirror of the result here; the latter also drops the strategyproofness that makes the theorem interesting.

So I would reject this paper as a ChoCo mirror candidate. The negative case is strongest on programme fit, not on the claim that no useful continuous housing-market research could ever be done. The paper supports a sensible population-continuous model, but supplies no computational anchor for the programme to chart.
