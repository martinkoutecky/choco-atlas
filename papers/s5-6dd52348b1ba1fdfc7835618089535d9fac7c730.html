The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a mirror is that the paper does not establish a computational result. Theorem 1 characterizes mechanisms satisfying OIR, I4OA, and SE; Theorems 2 and 3 are axiomatic uniqueness results; Theorem 4 derives symmetry; and Section 7 proves an incompatibility between solution classes. Algorithm 1 constructs a mechanism, but the paper gives no runtime or complexity analysis. There is no stated computational problem whose discrete complexity landscape the programme could continuize.

A stronger version of the hiring-cohort idea is plausible: group interchangeable employees into finitely many types, represent their population by masses, and describe arrivals as a path through cumulative type masses. One could ask whether a sequentially efficient, OIR, optimal-arrival reward allocation exists or can be computed. That is a sensible research direction, and the paper’s hiring motivation gives it some connection to the authors’ interests.

But it requires replacing the paper’s arbitrary worth table on finite sequences with a worth functional on mass-arrival paths, and specifying a finite representation for that functional. If arrivals are grouped into a finite number of cohorts, the model reduces to a game on type labels. If arrivals vary continuously, the question becomes an optimization problem over paths—potentially a useful one, but not a continuous version of a computational result this paper proved. The paper supplies neither an encoding nor a complexity claim to carry across that change.

So the proponent’s cohort scenario does not establish a defensible *mirror of this paper*. It may motivate a new computational model of sequential reward sharing. The honest limit of the negative case is that this does not support the universal claim that no scenario could yield worthwhile work: repeated cohorts are a credible high-multiplicity setting. The firm case against is narrower: under the programme’s result-anchoring standard, this paper offers no computational result to continuize.
