The strongest possible repair would be to ask how to compute an optimal or \(\epsilon\)-efficient network for a high-multiplicity society, perhaps represented by a continuum graph kernel. That regime is plausible: many agents could share the same signal and issue characteristics. But it does not mirror the cited results. For fixed \(\epsilon\), their construction uses \(M(\epsilon)\) celebrities, so \(M/N\) tends to zero as the population grows. A population measure loses those zero-mass agents, even though their actions influence almost everyone. Preserving that channel requires a hybrid model with finitely many distinguished agents attached to a continuum, or a more elaborate multi-scale limit. Either may be a worthwhile new model, but neither is the population-distribution mirror of the stated theorems. Arbitrary arrival distributions also refer to named agents and permutations; moving them to types requires a new order model.

The same issue applies to Theorem 6.2. One could optimize layer masses in a continuum model, but that would pose a new optimization problem. The theorem itself only establishes a welfare improvement; it gives no computational result to continuize.
