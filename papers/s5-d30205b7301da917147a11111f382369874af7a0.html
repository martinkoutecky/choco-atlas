The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is that the paper describes a plausible high-multiplicity setting: large labor or education cohorts whose members share an attribute and signal technology. A type could be a pair of market side and attribute; mass would be the fraction of the cohort with that attribute. Agents choose costly signals, signals determine rank, and matching pairs agents assortatively. The objective is the equilibrium outcome or aggregate net welfare. That is a sensible population model, but it is already the model in Section 7.

I can’t give the required anchor-based case. None of the paper’s numbered results asserts a computational complexity result. Propositions 1–2 characterize equilibrium and derive output, signaling, and welfare; Propositions 3–7 give comparative statics and welfare comparisons; Proposition 8 derives the continuum equilibrium; and Proposition 9 gives its welfare criterion. Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are stochastic-order results attributed to Barlow and Proschan, not computational results of this paper. Proposition 9, for example, says that in the continuum model assortative matching with signaling beats random matching exactly when \(\operatorname{Cov}(X,\psi(X))/(\mathbb{E}X\,\mathbb{E}\psi(X))\ge 1\); that is an analytical criterion, not a complexity classification.

One could propose a new task such as deciding, from rational finite-support attribute distributions, whether equilibrium signaling yields greater welfare than random matching. But that would be an unanchored extension, not a continuous mirror of a named computational result in the paper. The paper already explicitly studies a continuum population, so presenting Proposition 8 or 9 themselves as the mirror would simply restate its existing model.

The weak point is decisive: the setting is plausible, but the paper supplies no eligible computational anchor, and its continuum-population analysis is already present. There is therefore no valid lead mirror problem to hand to a prover under the stated criteria.

Under the programme’s rules, that is not a novelty collision; it is evidence that the continuous population model is meaningful.
