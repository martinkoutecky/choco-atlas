The continuous mirror question

Given finite type sets \(T_M,T_W\), rational masses \(\mu^M_a,\mu^W_b\geq 0\) with \(\sum_a\mu^M_a+\sum_b\mu^W_b=1\), direct match values \(p^a_b\) and \(p^b_a\), and per-capita externality coefficients \(e^z_{cd}\), does there exist a measurable one-to-one matching with no neutral blocking coalition of positive mass? A matching is represented by flows \(x_{ab}\geq 0\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{ab}\leq\mu^M_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\leq\mu^W_b\); each type's utility is its direct value from its match, or zero if unmatched, plus \(\sum_{c,d}x_{cd}e^z_{cd}\). A blocking coalition may select fractions of types, rearrange its members' matches, and sever their matches to outsiders while outsider-to-outsider matches remain unchanged; almost every member must form or sever a match, members must weakly gain almost everywhere, and a positive mass must strictly gain.

The model it lives in

An atomless two-sided market with finite complete payoff types, rational type masses, one-to-one matching flows \(x_{ab}\), direct match values, and per-capita additive externalities. Ask whether some flow has no positive-mass neutral blocking coalition under the paper's setwise deviation rule.

The objection that survived

The Knapsack reduction encodes items with distinct payoff signatures, so it does not establish that the paper's hard instances have a small number of recurring types; the proposed market supplies a plausible regime independently of that reduction.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 13's neutral setwise-stable-set nonemptiness question in one-to-one matching; it leaves the other stability concepts, pairwise stability, membership results, and the remaining algorithms and hardness results aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can neutral-core existence be decided in time polynomial in \(|T_M|+|T_W|\) and the input bit length, or do externality-driven blocking deviations remain hard?
  • Can the no-block condition be characterized or separated using the finite type-flow representation?

The case FOR (proponent)

My strongest case is narrow: Theorem 13, proved in this paper through the appendix’s Theorem 25, says that in one-to-one matching games with additive externalities, deciding whether the neutral setwise-stable set is nonempty is NP-hard. The proof reduces from Knapsack; the appendix notes that the construction can be made one-to-one. In this setting the paper identifies setwise stability with the core. This is a useful anchor because the reduction’s item choices are discrete—the feature a population continuum could change.

I would mirror it in a regional placement market with a very large cohort of applicants and a very large supply of job slots. An applicant’s type records their side of the market, their values for each slot type, and their response to matches elsewhere in the market. A slot type records the corresponding information for applicants. These are complete payoff types: two applicants share a type only if the problem treats them identically. A market with millions of people but dozens of applicant and slot types is a plausible high-multiplicity regime. Externalities could represent, for example, congestion or the effects of other placements on a type’s value for a match.

The continuous question I would give a prover is Atomless Neutral-Core Existence for Additive-Externality Matching. An instance consists of finite applicant and slot type sets \(T_M,T_W\); rational mass supplies \(\mu^M_a,\mu^W_b\), each summing to \(1\); rational direct match values \(p^a_{ab}\) for an agent of type \(a\) matched to type \(b\); and rational externality coefficients \(e^a_{cd}\), giving the effect on type \(a\) of matches between types \(c\) and \(d\).

A candidate solution is a measurable one-to-one matching of atomless populations with those type supplies. Its type-level representation is a matrix of matched masses \(x_{ab}\geq0\), with \(\sum_b x_{ab}\leq\mu^M_a\) and \(\sum_a x_{ab}\leq\mu^W_b\). A matched agent of type \(a\), paired with type \(b\), receives

\[ u_a=p^a_{ab}+\sum_{c\in T_M,d\in T_W}x_{cd}e^a_{cd}; \]

an unmatched agent has direct value \(0\), plus the same aggregate externality term. The decision question is whether there is a matching with no neutral blocking coalition: no measurable coalition of positive mass can change its own matches and sever its existing matches to outsiders, while leaving all outsider-to-outsider matches unchanged, so that its members weakly gain almost everywhere and a positive mass strictly gains. Almost every coalition member must take an action, matching the paper’s requirement that deviators actively form or sever matches. Because agents are atomless and indistinguishable within type, a coalition may select any fraction of a type’s existing matched mass.

The normalization in that utility formula is a deliberate model co-modification. Each individual retains a direct value from their own match, while the influence of matches throughout the population is measured per capita. That keeps both components meaningful as the market grows. The variables \(x_{ab}\) describe the matching induced by a continuous population; fractional outcomes are not the object being continuized.

I expect this question to be Class A, tractable through a finite type-flow formulation and a characterization or separation procedure for blocking deviations. The reason to investigate that direction is specific to the anchor: its Knapsack reduction chooses a subset of item edges. In the mass model, each item type can contribute fractionally, so the reduction’s integral selection constraint becomes fractional selection, like fractional Knapsack. That suggests the discrete hardness may dissolve. It does not prove tractability: the externality changes caused by a coalition may make the stability conditions hard even when the matching variables are flows. The prover should determine whether the no-block condition has a polynomial characterization in \(|T_M|+|T_W|\) and the input bit length, or whether there is a different source of hardness.

The main weakness is that the paper’s agents and coalitions are discrete, while this mirror replaces them with atomless populations and positive-mass coalitions. A blocking pair of individuals may have zero mass, and the paper’s requirement that every coalition member act needs an almost-everywhere interpretation. There is also a payoff-scaling choice in separating direct match value from per-capita externality. An author could reasonably ask whether those changes preserve the problem they studied. I think the mirror remains worth asking: it keeps one-to-one matching, additive effects on utility, and neutral coalition deviations, while making large repeated cohorts and their aggregate match flows the model’s central objects. It covers Theorem 13’s neutral-core existence result; it does not claim to continuize the paper’s other stability concepts or results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against the anchor is that Theorem 13’s hardness construction is driven by distinct payoff signatures, not by many interchangeable agents. In the Knapsack reduction, each item is encoded by its own agents and its own pair \((s_i,v_i)\), alongside a few special agents that impose the size and value thresholds. Under the programme’s definition of a complete type, those item agents generally become distinct types. The theorem therefore gives little evidence that its hard instances describe a high-multiplicity market with a small set of recurring types.

That objection weakens the connection to the reduction, but it does not defeat the better mirror. A market with repeated applicant and slot types, whose members share the same match values and externality responses, is plausible. Its neutral-core existence question can be stated with type masses and matching flows, and its blocking coalitions can select fractions of each type. The resulting model preserves the paper’s one-to-one matching, additive externalities, and neutral deviations. Requiring positive-mass coalitions and per-capita externalities gives those concepts a sensible atomless interpretation.

I cannot make the universal negative case convincingly. The reduction does not establish that this high-multiplicity regime matters to the authors, but neither does it rule the regime out; and the proposed mirror is concrete enough to ask them about. Whether its answer is simple or its model needs refinement is not a reason to discard the question. The honest negative case is weak: Theorem 13 is a valid anchor, and the continuous mirror appears worth investigating.
