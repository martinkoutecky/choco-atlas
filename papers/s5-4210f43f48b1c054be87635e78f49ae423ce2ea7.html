The continuous mirror question

Given a finite agenda \(\Phi\), integrity constraint \(\Gamma\), required starting agenda \(\Phi_0\subseteq\Phi\), target conclusions \(L\subseteq\Phi\), and a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over complete, \(\Gamma\)-consistent judgment types on \(\Phi\) with binary-rational masses summing to \(1\), is there an agenda \(A\) with \(\Phi_0\subseteq A\subseteq\Phi\) such that every complete, feasible judgment minimizing \(D_\mu^A(J)=\sum_t\mu_t d_A(J,t|_A)\) contains \(L\)?

The model it lives in

Types are complete, \(\Gamma\)-consistent judgments on \(\Phi\), with support size \(\tau\) and rational masses \(\mu_t\). The decision variable is the selected agenda \(A\); its Kemeny outcomes minimize the mass-weighted Hamming disagreement \(D_\mu^A(J)\), and cautious success requires every minimizer to contain \(L\).

The objection that survived

The expected Kemeny score depends only on issue marginals, and rational masses can be denominator-cleared, so the mirror introduces no new fractional agenda or outcome choices; this bounds its algorithmic novelty but does not defeat the stated high-multiplicity question.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

This mirrors Proposition 19's cautious exact issue-addition control. It leaves the paper's manipulation, bribery, issue-deletion, and other control variants unmirrored.

Open questions for a prover

  • Is cautious continuous issue-addition control \(\Sigma_2^P\)-complete when instances use support size \(\tau\) and binary-rational masses of total bit length \(L\)?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead and only anchor is Continuous Kemeny Issue-Addition Control. It is a direct high-multiplicity version of the paper’s cautious control problem: the people become a distribution over judgment types, while the agenda setter still chooses which issues to include.

A plausible setting is a large membership organization consulting its members on a fixed bank of logically linked policy questions. Many members may share the same complete, coherent bundle of answers. Each such bundle is a type; its mass is the fraction of members holding it. The organization’s agenda setter chooses which optional questions to put to a Kemeny aggregation, hoping to make a specified conclusion appear in every optimal group judgment. Here the population can be very large while the observed support of \(\mu\) has only \(\tau\) types.

The continuous problem. An instance consists of a finite agenda \(\Phi\), integrity constraint \(\Gamma\), required starting agenda \(\Phi_0\subseteq\Phi\), target conclusions \(L\subseteq\Phi\), and a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over complete, \(\Gamma\)-consistent judgment types on \(\Phi\). Each mass \(\mu_t\) is a binary rational and the masses sum to \(1\). For any allowed agenda \(A\), let \(\mathcal J_A\) be the feasible complete judgments under the paper’s convention, and define the population’s average disagreement with outcome \(J\) by
\[ D_\mu^A(J)=\sum_{t\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu)}\mu_t\,d_A(J,t|_A). \]
The Kemeny outcomes are the minimizers of \(D_\mu^A\) over \(\mathcal J_A\). The question is whether there is an agenda \(A\) with \(\Phi_0\subseteq A\subseteq\Phi\) such that every minimizer includes \(L\). A yes-solution is such an agenda \(A\); the universal condition over its Kemeny outcomes is part of what makes it cautious control.

The anchor is Proposition 19, proved in this paper: \(\textsc{CautiousExactControlByAddingIssues}(\text{Kemeny})\) is \(\Sigma_2^P\)-hard. Its reduction is from quantified Boolean formulas \(\exists X\,\forall Y\,\psi\), with \(\psi\) in 3DNF. It constructs an agenda, integrity constraint, starting agenda, target \(L=\{b\}\), and a profile of three judgment sets such that the formula is true exactly when some permitted agenda makes every Kemeny outcome contain \(b\). That is the full computational claim of the proposition, and it is proved here rather than cited.

This mirror preserves the paper’s underlying computational question closely: issue selection is still the decision variable, Kemeny still minimizes Hamming disagreement among feasible judgments, and success still requires the target to occur in all optimal outcomes. The change is that the profile is represented by type masses and the objective is per-capita disagreement.

The expected classification is hardness transfers (Class B). In Proposition 19’s reduction, map its three voters to three types of mass \(1/3\). For every candidate agenda and judgment, \(D_\mu^A\) is exactly one third of the paper’s cumulative Hamming distance. Multiplying the objective by \(1/3\) leaves the entire set of Kemeny outcomes unchanged, so the reduction establishes the same \(\Sigma_2^P\)-hardness for the continuous problem. It even applies to a genuinely large population: take any population of size \(3q\), with \(q\) members of each type. The reduction’s combinatorics live in the issues and integrity constraint, not in the number of people, so population continuization should not dissolve this hardness.

My weakest point is that this is a faithful population-measure formulation of an anonymous rule, and the hardness anchor itself uses only three distinct judgments. It offers no reason to expect an algorithmic gain from continuity; indeed, its hardness survives because the hard part is agenda logic. That limits the novelty of this particular mirror as a tractability story, but it does not make the continuous-population question artificial: the normalized distribution is a sensible high-multiplicity instance, and the paper’s issue-control problem carries over exactly.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the proposed mirror adds no new continuous decision variable. For an issue \(\varphi\), let \(p_\varphi\) be the mass of judgment types accepting it. On any agenda \(A\), the expected Hamming distance is

\[ D_\mu^A(J)=\sum_{\varphi\in A} \begin{cases} 1-p_\varphi, & \varphi\in J,\\ p_\varphi, & \varphi\notin J. \end{cases} \]

So the full distribution over judgment types matters only through its issue marginals. Even with many types and a very large membership, correlations among their judgments have no effect on which Kemeny outcomes are optimal. The agenda setter still chooses a discrete subset, and each outcome is still a discrete, \(\Gamma\)-consistent judgment. Continuity changes the coefficients of that finite optimization; it does not relax either choice.

For rational masses, clearing denominators gives a finite profile with exactly the same Kemeny outcomes on every allowed agenda. Dividing its cumulative Hamming distances by the population size preserves all minimizers. The mirror is therefore an exact high-multiplicity encoding of issue control. Its computational difficulty remains the agenda choice and the search over feasible judgments under \(\Gamma\); the population limit supplies no fractional feasibility region for continuous-optimization methods to exploit. A more active bribery mirror, allowing the briber to move mass between judgment types, would still change only those issue marginals, while leaving the same discrete agenda and outcome problems in place.

That is the best case against, but it does not defeat Proposition 19’s mirror. The large-organization setting is plausible, and mapping its three types to equal masses gives a valid instance with arbitrarily large population. More importantly, ChoCo explicitly treats high-multiplicity formulations as supporting evidence and regards hardness that survives because of the agenda as a meaningful boundary result. Marginal compression may make this a less compelling example of new continuous-optimization machinery, but under the programme’s stated standard it is not a reason to reject the question. I cannot honestly claim this anchor has been defeated; the negative case here is weak.
