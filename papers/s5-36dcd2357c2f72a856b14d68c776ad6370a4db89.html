There is a plausible population setting behind the mechanism: a large course-allocation or computing-capacity system, with many users sharing valuation distributions and per-capita virtual-credit endowments. A possible continuous model would take user cohorts as types, their population fractions as masses, and their value-contingent bidding policies as decisions; the objective could be each cohort’s guaranteed utility against coordinated behavior by the others. But a single slot per round gives an individual vanishing allocation as the population grows, so a useful continuum model would need supply and credits scaled with population. That may be a sensible population extension of the mechanism, but it would ask an analytic security-guarantee question, not the computational-complexity question this programme targets.

The soft spot is therefore decisive for this assignment: the paper offers a credible setting for population continuization, but no eligible computational anchor.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent raises no eligible anchor, and that is the strongest case against a mirror here. The paper’s Theorem 1 proves a strategic guarantee; Theorems 2–4 sharpen its error bounds; and Corollary 1 derives a price-of-anarchy bound from a feasible-region result. None classifies the complexity of a computational problem. Even the open question about the exact finite-horizon minimax value concerns the paper’s finite-player game, not an algorithmic problem over a continuous society. The paper gives no input encoding, computational task, or complexity parameter for such a question.

There is a plausible population story: many users with common valuation laws sharing a repeated allocation system. But \(D_i\) describes one player’s uncertainty across rounds; it is not a distribution of voter or user types in the society. The guarantee is also playerwise, with budget share \(\alpha_i\), against a coalition of the other identified players. A natural continuum reformulation would need to decide what a type’s mass-level guarantee means. With one slot per round and \(N\) exchangeable users, each player’s share is on the order of \(1/N\), so the individual guarantee vanishes; aggregating it across a cohort changes the objective. Scaling supply with population could retain per-capita allocation, but then the model becomes a repeated multi-unit allocation problem with different strategic dynamics.

Those reformulations may be sensible research questions. They do not supply a computational mirror of a result in this paper: they add a population model and a computational objective that the paper never studies.
