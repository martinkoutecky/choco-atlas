The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent gives no result anchor, and I see none to defeat. The paper reports empirical findings about arbitrator preferences and strategic ranking. Its closest computational claim is that a finite-dimensional likelihood is globally concave. That is a property of an econometric model, not a complexity result for a social-choice problem.

The strongest proposed mirror would treat a large collection of bargaining cases as a distribution over complete case types: each type would include both parties’ preferences, the arbitrator panel, and relevant case information. That is a coherent high-multiplicity description. But the paper’s appointment procedure acts separately on each case. Passing from repeated cases to masses would aggregate their outcomes or reweight the likelihood; it would not create a shared population decision problem that the paper studies. The strategic-ranking analysis has the same limit: each game is between one union and one employer, so a distribution over cases is a distribution over separate games.

One could add a system-wide problem, such as assigning cases to arbitrators with limited capacity, then optimize outcomes across the resulting mass of disputes. The paper mentions arbitrator shortages, so that extension has a plausible motivation. It would still introduce a new allocation problem and a new computational result. It would not mirror a named result in this paper, because the paper establishes none.

So the negative case is strong for the paper as written: there is no computational result here for ChoCo to continuize. The universal claim is weaker. A population-level assignment problem could be worthwhile, but its case would rest on the new scarcity model, not on a continuous mirror of this paper’s contribution.
