What the mirror covers

The candidate mirrors cover median preservation for stable matchings or market-clearing prices; they leave the other regular predicates and the paper's general finite-lattice theorem without a population-continuous formulation.

The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make a qualifying positive case under your anchor rule: this paper has no named result classifying a computational problem as polynomial-time solvable, NP-hard, or similar.

The closest results are Theorem 1, cited from Teo and Sethuraman [13], which says that for \(k\) stable marriages, assigning each man his \(j\)-th-ranked partner among those marriages produces another stable marriage; Theorem 2, proved here, which generalizes that closure property to regular predicates on finite distributive lattices; and Corollaries 1 and 2, applying it to stable marriages and market-clearing prices. These are structural results, not complexity results. One can infer a simple procedure—sort the coordinates of the \(k\) inputs and form the \(k\) median elements—but the paper does not present or classify that as a computational problem.

The tempting mirror would put mass on a distribution of stable matchings and ask whether its coordinatewise quantiles remain stable. But that continuizes the set of solutions, not the society. Making the agents themselves a continuum instead changes the matching model, and the paper’s finite-lattice theorem does not establish that its median guarantee survives. In the paper, \(k\) counts solutions being aggregated; it is not a population size or a mass of agent types.

So there is no honest continuous problem per qualifying anchor to hand to a prover. The paper’s scope is median-feasibility preservation for finite lattices and its stable-matching and price-vector applications; it supplies no computational hardness or tractability anchor for ChoCo’s population-continuization programme.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that this paper supplies no computational-complexity result for ChoCo to continuize. Theorem 2 proves a structural closure property: given \(k\) elements already satisfying a regular predicate, coordinatewise medians also satisfy it. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 give the stable-matching version; Corollary 2 gives the market-clearing-price version. The median construction itself is just sorting coordinates. The paper does not study the complexity of finding a stable matching, finding clearing prices, or changing a society to reach a target outcome.

The most faithful proposed mirror would put a distribution over stable solutions and ask whether its quantiles remain stable. But that makes the solution set continuous, not the population. Here \(k\) counts input solutions, not agents or types. That is outside ChoCo’s population-continuization scope.

A stronger stable-matching proposal would give the two sides finitely many types with rational masses and represent an assignment by flows \(x_{uv}\) between types. That is a plausible high-multiplicity market. But the theorem’s median takes each named man’s ranked partner; the flow model has type-level mass split across partners. Taking medians of flow coordinates need not preserve the row and column totals: for example, the coordinatewise median of three distinct \(3\times3\) permutation assignments can be the zero matrix. One could define a different aggregation or add a canonical within-type ordering, but then the central question—what aggregation preserves stability, and at what computational cost—is new work, not a mirror of this theorem.

Corollary 2 offers the best alternative: a continuum of buyer types with rational masses and a question about computing market-clearing prices. That may be a sensible computational project. But the paper only says that medians of existing price vectors remain clearing in a finite market. A continuous-consumer equilibrium or price-optimization problem would need its own input, objective, and complexity question; the corollary supplies none.

So the negative case is strong as a case against treating this paper as a ChoCo mirror: its named results concern closure under aggregation, while its median coordinate is the set of solutions. It is weaker as a claim that no worthwhile neighboring project exists. Large matching and market populations are plausible, and new computational questions about them could be valuable. But that would be a new project inspired by the applications, not a continuous mirror of the paper’s results.
