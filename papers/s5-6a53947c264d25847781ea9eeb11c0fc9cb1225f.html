The continuous mirror question

Given finite seller-type and consumer-type sets \(S\) and \(C\), rational type masses \(\mu_s,\nu_c\) summing to \(1\), per-household block supplies \(d_s\) and demands \(b_c\), and each type's strict ranking of counterpart types, compute a rational flow \(x_{sc}\ge 0\) in time polynomial in the number of listed types and input bit length. Require \(\sum_c x_{sc}\le \mu_s d_s\) and \(\sum_s x_{sc}\le \nu_c b_c\). For every pair \((s,c)\), define \(U_s(c)=\mu_s d_s-\sum_{c'}x_{sc'}+\sum_{c':\,c\succ_s c'}x_{sc'}\) and \(V_c(s)=\nu_c b_c-\sum_{s'}x_{s'c}+\sum_{s':\,s\succ_c s'}x_{s'c}\); require \(U_s(c)=0\) or \(V_c(s)=0\), so no positive amount of flow can be shifted to a pair both sides prefer to unused capacity or less-preferred trades.

The model it lives in

A two-sided market of seller and consumer cohorts, each with a common ranking and per-household integral block capacity, represented by rational mass and a type-to-type flow whose averages may be fractional.

The objection that survived

Open questions for a prover

  • Can a stable flow be computed in polynomial time in the number of types and input bit length?
  • Can a type-level algorithm preserve the stability guarantee while representing EM's history-dependent reallocations?
  • Which rational stable flows can be realized by discrete populations in which each participant receives an integral bundle?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is for the paper’s EM stable energy matching problem. It continuizes the population of sellers and consumers while keeping their energy capacities, preferences, and stable-trading question. It does not replace that question with welfare maximization: the paper’s EM algorithm has no such objective.

A plausible regime is a large local energy market with many households but relatively few cohorts: for example, participants sharing a feeder area, tariff or bid tier, and standard supply or demand profile. A type records everything the matching process uses, including its per-household supply or demand and its strict preference order over counterpart types. Its mass is the fraction of the population in that cohort. A market with many thousands of participants and dozens or hundreds of such types is credible. Individualized bids or locations can create many types, so this is a scenario-specific claim, not a claim about P2P markets in general.

My anchor is Proposition 1, “Computational Complexity,” proved in this paper. It states that EM converges in \(O(NM L_{\max}\tau_{\max})\) time, where \(N\) and \(M\) are the numbers of actual consumers and sellers, \(L_{\max}\) is the maximum number of blocks demanded by any consumer, and \(\tau_{\max}\) is the maximum time a seller takes to allocate blocks. The paper’s surrounding notation swaps which side \(N\) and \(M\) count, but their product is unchanged. Proposition 2, also proved here, supports the mirror: it says EM produces a feasible and stable matching.

I would ask the prover to solve Continuous Stable Energy-Mass Matching (CSEMM). An instance lists seller types \(S\) and consumer types \(C\), their rational masses \(\mu_s,\nu_c\), each type’s per-household supply \(d_s\) or demand \(b_c\) in energy blocks, and each type’s strict ranking of counterpart types. The type data can encode the paper’s bid and distance-based preferences. Take the masses to sum to \(1\) across both sides.

A solution is a rational flow \(x_{sc}\ge 0\), where \(x_{sc}\) is the amount of energy blocks traded between seller type \(s\) and consumer type \(c\), measured per unit of society. It must satisfy
\[ \sum_c x_{sc}\le \mu_s d_s \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_s x_{sc}\le \nu_c b_c. \]
Require stability in the fluid sense: for no pair \((s,c)\) can a positive amount of trade be shifted to them that both sides prefer to their unused capacity or less-preferred current trades. More explicitly, define
\[ u_s(c)=\mu_s d_s-\sum_{c'}x_{sc'}+ \sum_{c':\,c\succ_s c'}x_{sc'} \]
and
\[ v_c(s)=\nu_c b_c-\sum_{s'}x_{s'c}+ \sum_{s':\,s\succ_c s'}x_{s'c}. \]
The flow is stable if, for every \(s,c\), at least one of \(u_s(c)\) and \(v_c(s)\) is zero. The computational question is to return any feasible stable flow, in time polynomial in the number of listed types \(\tau=|S|+|C|\) and the input bit length \(L\), rather than in the number of households. There is no scalar objective to optimize: a stable feasible flow is the requested output.

I expect CSEMM to be tractable (Class A), probably through a type-level deferred-acceptance or flow procedure. A discrete matching aggregates to such a flow when types and preferences agree; the converse—whether every rational stable flow has a suitable discrete replica realization—is a further question, not an assumption. Other natural questions are whether the mass solution can be computed independently of population size and how to extend it to the paper’s negotiated-price process. This mirror covers EM’s matching result, not NEM, blockchain implementation, or the paper’s price simulations.

The weak point is that Proposition 1 is not a bit-complexity result in \((\tau,L)\): it counts actual participants and includes a seller allocation-time parameter. The type-compressed bound would be a new theorem, not a restatement of the proposition. Also, strict preferences over individually named virtual users can defeat the small-type regime if bids or distances vary person by person. The mirror is convincing only in a market with genuine common cohorts and common rankings. Those are real limitations, but the resulting problem still asks for the paper’s central mathematical object—a feasible, stable seller-consumer allocation—when the population itself is represented continuously.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that Proposition 1 bounds execution of a particular distributed protocol. Its work includes consumers’ current unmatched blocks and latest seller, sellers’ requester lists, and repeated reallocations. Two participants with the same static preferences and capacities can reach different states after different rejections. A terminal flow \(x_{sc}\) records how much each type trades, but not the messages and state changes that produced it. A faithful continuous version of the protocol would need to track mass across histories; the number of those history types could grow with the run. The proposed polynomial-in-support question therefore seems to change what Proposition 1 measures.

That objection does not defeat the strongest mirror. Proposition 2 proves that EM produces a feasible, stable matching, and a static flow with type-level capacities and rankings is a natural continuous version of that object. Large cohorts sharing supply or demand profiles, bid tiers, locations, and rankings are plausible in a local energy market. For those cohorts, mass can represent participants in different allocation states, and the question of computing a stable flow remains recognizable as the paper’s group-formation problem.

So the negative case is weak. It challenges whether the mirror preserves the paper’s distributed execution analysis, but it does not rule out a worthwhile continuous mirror of stable matching. I cannot honestly defend the claim that no scenario or type-space choice works.
