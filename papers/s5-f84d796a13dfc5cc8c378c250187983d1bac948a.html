What the mirror covers

Proposition 8's distance-based stable-set characterization is the proposed anchor; the paper's other stability characterizations and its dynamics discussion are not mirrored.

The case FOR (proponent)

There is a plausible high-multiplicity setting nearby: a large communication platform whose users share the same link costs and distance-based benefits, with type determined by whether a user is myopic or farsighted. The two types could have rational masses \(\mu_M\) and \(\mu_F\), with many users per type.

The closest result is Proposition 8, proved in this paper. For distance-based utility with \(b(1)-b(2)<c<b(1)\), if \(n>\#F\ge 1+b(2)/(b(2)-b(3))\), then the unique myopic-farsighted stable set consists of all star networks whose center is myopic. That is useful structural evidence for a continuous-population research direction, but it does not classify the computational complexity of any problem.

I found no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition in the paper asserting that a problem is in P, NP-hard, FPT, or has another computational-complexity classification. So there is no eligible computational anchor, and I cannot honestly give a prover-ready continuous problem per anchor or predict its place in ChoCo’s trichotomy.

The main obstacle to turning Proposition 8 into one is substantive: its threshold can require only a fixed number of farsighted players, which may have zero mass in a normalized continuum. Also, a star’s center is one individual, and a pairwise link change affects zero population mass. A faithful continuum model would need to preserve those individual roles and deviations, or change them to positive-mass actions. The latter could be an interesting next question, but it would be a new stability model rather than a computational mirror anchored by this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s concession is decisive for ChoCo’s purpose: the paper states no computational complexity result to mirror. Proposition 8 characterizes a myopic-farsighted stable set; it gives no algorithmic or complexity classification. One could ask how hard it is to compute such stable sets, but that would introduce a computational question the paper does not answer or frame. That makes the paper a poor anchor for this programme, though it says nothing against the economic value of its stability results.

The platform example supplies many users, but a distribution over myopic and farsighted users does not describe the paper’s main object: a labeled network. Payoffs depend on individual degrees and shortest paths, and each move adds or deletes a particular pairwise link. Those features cannot be recovered from the two masses \(\mu_M,\mu_F\). The agents’ neighborhoods are endogenous relations in the network, not fixed attributes that can simply be added to their types.

A graphon or other network limit is the most plausible repair, but it changes what the stability question means. In an atomless population, changing one pairwise link is a measure-zero change to the aggregate relation. To make changes visible, one must model positive-mass link changes or coalitional deviations. The paper itself distinguishes groupwise from pairwise deviations in §5.2, and its distance-based result changes under that extension. The degree-based result in Proposition 10 poses the same problem in another form: its farsighted players form a \(d^*\)-regular network, whose edge density vanishes as the population grows. A dense graphon loses those links and the finite-degree payoffs they generate; preserving them requires a sparse or rooted network model with its own state and deviation rules.

A hybrid limit with a distinguished hub or other finite set of pivotal agents and a continuous periphery is conceivable. But it retains precisely the discrete actors that drive Proposition 8 and needs a separate computational theory. The honest weakness in this negative case is that I cannot rule out such a hybrid being worthwhile. What I can say is that it would be a new computational network-formation project, not a continuous mirror of a computational result in this paper.
