What the mirror covers

The proposed housing-exchange extension would pose a new mechanism-characterization problem over types and masses; it does not mirror a computational result among Theorem 1, Theorem 2, or Corollary 1.

The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t identify a qualifying anchor in this paper. The closest is Theorem 1, proved here: on any preference domain satisfying the top-two condition, TTC is the unique rule that is individually rational, Pareto efficient, and strategyproof; it is also the unique rule that is individually rational, pair efficient, and strategyproof. Theorem 2, also proved here, says that under its small-subset and extension conditions, a domain failing top-two admits a non-TTC rule that is individually rational, Pareto efficient, and strategyproof. Corollary 1 gives the top-two characterization for \(n\le4\). These are axiomatic characterization results, not results about computational complexity or algorithms. The paper describes the TTC procedure, but does not analyze its running time or another computational problem.

A plausible adjacent extension would consider a large housing exchange with a fixed set of object categories, many copies of each category, and many residents sharing types \((\succ,e)\), where \(\succ\) is a ranking and \(e\) an endowed category. The distribution \(\mu\) gives each type’s population share; category supplies scale with the population. A feasible outcome could be a measurable assignment of one indivisible object to each resident, represented in aggregate by masses \(x_{t,a}\), with \(\sum_a x_{t,a}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{t,a}=q_a\). That is a credible high-multiplicity housing scenario, but the paper’s strategyproofness condition concerns an individual’s deviation in a finite profile. Turning it into a continuum mechanism requires specifying how individual reports affect assignments and how TTC works with masses. Those choices would define a new axiomatic problem, not a continuous version of a named computational result here.

So there is no per-anchor prover question or honest tractability forecast to give. The paper may motivate a future continuous mechanism-characterization project, but it supplies no computational anchor for this programme.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

On ChoCo’s stated remit, the strongest case against is that none of the proposed anchors is a computational result. Theorem 1 characterizes when axioms select TTC; Theorem 2 constructs a different rule when they do not; Corollary 1 sharpens the characterization for \(n\le4\). The construction in Theorem 2 does not analyze the complexity of finding a rule, and the paper gives no complexity result for computing TTC. These are axiomatic mechanism-characterization results, so there is no named computational question here for the programme to continuize.

The proposed large housing exchange is a plausible high-multiplicity setting. One could group residents by preference over unit categories and endowed category, and scale the supply of each category with the population. That avoids the mistaken objection that object exchange can never have repeated types. But it still does not supply a computational mirror of these theorems. The paper’s rules map full profiles to bijective allocations, and their strategyproofness condition concerns one agent changing their report. In a mechanism whose input and output are only aggregate masses, one agent has measure zero, so that deviation leaves the aggregate unchanged. Retaining an individual-level assignment map may preserve meaningful incentives, but then the mechanism is not determined by the distribution \(\mu\) alone. Treating a positive-mass cohort’s deviation as the relevant test changes the axiom to a group or coalition condition, which is not the characterization studied here.

There are nearby computational questions one could invent: compute TTC from a compressed description of a replicated market, or test whether a succinctly represented domain satisfies the top-two condition. Either might be worth studying on its own. The first requires a new encoding and market model; the second concerns the domain representation, not the population. Neither is a continuous version of a computational result in this paper. The small configurations used to witness Theorem 2’s non-TTC rule also do not automatically become positive-mass witnesses when the economy is scaled up.

So I would exclude this paper from ChoCo’s computational-continuization queue: its anchors do not meet the programme’s entry criterion, and the most natural population model makes individual strategyproofness difficult to carry over. I cannot honestly support the stronger universal claim that no continuous mirror could be worthwhile in any scenario. A continuous axiomatic study of housing exchange, or a separate algorithmic study of compressed TTC, could be worthwhile; they would be new projects rather than mirrors of the paper’s named results.
