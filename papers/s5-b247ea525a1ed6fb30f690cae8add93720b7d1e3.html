The continuous mirror question

Given finite customer and supplier type sets \(A\) and \(B\), rational type masses \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\), nonnegative rational MNL weights \(v_{ab}\) and \(w_{ba}\), and fixed per-agent caps \(K_C,K_S\), let population size \(N\) range over values for which all type counts are integral. Each customer of type \(a\) assigns weight \(v_{ab}\) to each individual supplier of type \(b\), and each supplier of type \(b\) assigns weight \(w_{ba}\) to each individual customer of type \(a\); both outside options have weight \(1\). Over simple bipartite display graphs whose edges are reciprocal displays and whose degrees obey the caps, determine whether the limit exists and compute \(\limsup_{N\to\infty} N^{-1}\max_G \mathbb{E}[M(G)]\), where \(M(G)\) is the number of mutual selections.

The model it lives in

Use integer neighbor-type profiles \(\mathbf d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{B}\), \(\|\mathbf d\|_1\le K_C\), and \(\mathbf e\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{A}\), \(\|\mathbf e\|_1\le K_S\). Let \(r_{a,\mathbf d}\) and \(s_{b,\mathbf e}\) be customer and supplier profile masses, and \(z_{a,\mathbf d,b,\mathbf e}\) reciprocal edge mass. Maximize \(\sum_{a,b,\mathbf d,\mathbf e} z_{a,\mathbf d,b,\mathbf e}\frac{v_{ab}}{1+\sum_{b'}d_{b'}v_{ab'}}\frac{w_{ba}}{1+\sum_{a'}e_{a'}w_{ba'}}\), subject to \(\sum_{\mathbf d}r_{a,\mathbf d}=\mu_a\), \(\sum_{\mathbf e}s_{b,\mathbf e}=\nu_b\), \(\sum_{\mathbf e}z_{a,\mathbf d,b,\mathbf e}=d_b r_{a,\mathbf d}\), \(\sum_{\mathbf d}z_{a,\mathbf d,b,\mathbf e}=e_a s_{b,\mathbf e}\), and nonnegativity. This finite LP is a candidate characterization of the asymptotic optimum; its equality with the graph limit depends on proving simple-graph realizability.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 6.6 for fully static matching under two-way caps and MNL preferences. It leaves the paper’s adaptivity-gap results and its fully adaptive and general-choice approximation results unaddressed.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does every feasible point of the type-profile LP admit a sequence of simple reciprocal display graphs realizing its profile and edge masses, so the LP optimum equals the asymptotic optimum?
  • For fixed caps, can the LP characterization be established with running time polynomial in type support and bit length; what is the complexity when the caps are input?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I see is the paper’s fully static, two-way constrained MNL matching problem. It has a plausible high-multiplicity regime: a large labor or housing platform with many customers and suppliers, but only a moderate number of complete preference types. Everyone of a type shares the same MNL weights and display cap; the number of agents \(N\) is much larger than the number of types. A fixed per-person cap on displayed profiles is natural for a platform that sends a short daily shortlist.

My anchor is Theorem 6.6, proved in this paper: under two-way cardinality constraints, when both sides use MNL choice models, there is a polynomial-time algorithm achieving a \(0.067\)-approximation for the fully static problem. The theorem concerns the underlying matching problem: choosing assortments to maximize the expected number of mutual selections. The cap is useful for this mirror because it keeps each person’s local assortment bounded as the populations grow.

Here is a continuous version precise enough to hand to a prover. Let \(A\) and \(B\) be finite sets of customer and supplier types, with rational masses \(\mu_a\) and \(\nu_b\), each summing to \(1\). A customer of type \(a\) gives each individual supplier of type \(b\) MNL weight \(v_{ab}\); a supplier of type \(b\) gives each individual customer of type \(a\) weight \(w_{ba}\). Both have outside-option weight \(1\). Each customer may see at most \(K_C\) suppliers, and each supplier at most \(K_S\) customers.

At population scale \(N\), there are \(N\mu_a\) customers of type \(a\) and \(N\nu_b\) suppliers of type \(b\), rounding along a sequence of \(N\)’s for which these counts are integral. A reciprocal displayed pair is an edge: both agents see each other, and all assortments are chosen before anyone makes a choice. The objective is the limiting expected matches per \(N\).

A type-profile LP expresses that limit. Let \(\mathbf d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{B}\), \(\|\mathbf d\|_1\le K_C\), record how many suppliers of each type a customer sees; let \(\mathbf e\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{A}\), \(\|\mathbf e\|_1\le K_S\), do the same for suppliers. Use \(r_{a,\mathbf d}\) and \(s_{b,\mathbf e}\) for the mass of agents with each profile, and \(z_{a,\mathbf d,b,\mathbf e}\) for the mass of displayed edges between those profile groups. The constraints are
\(\sum_{\mathbf d}r_{a,\mathbf d}=\mu_a\), \(\sum_{\mathbf e}s_{b,\mathbf e}=\nu_b\),
\(\sum_{\mathbf e}z_{a,\mathbf d,b,\mathbf e}=d_b r_{a,\mathbf d}\), and
\(\sum_{\mathbf d}z_{a,\mathbf d,b,\mathbf e}=e_a s_{b,\mathbf e}\), with all variables nonnegative. Maximize
\(\sum_{a,b,\mathbf d,\mathbf e}z_{a,\mathbf d,b,\mathbf e} \frac{v_{ab}}{1+\sum_{b'}d_{b'}v_{ab'}} \frac{w_{ba}}{1+\sum_{a'}e_{a'}w_{ba'}}\).
Each edge earns the product of its endpoints’ MNL probabilities of choosing each other. The LP variables are the solution: they specify type-profile masses and reciprocal edge mass, and thus an asymptotically realizable sequence of display graphs.

For fixed \(K_C,K_S\), the profile sets are finite and the LP has size polynomial in the type support and input bit length, with an exponent depending on the caps. I therefore expect this mirror to be Class A for fixed caps, potentially exactly solvable by the LP. The main proof obligation is to establish the asymptotic realization of feasible profile and edge masses by simple bounded-degree bipartite graphs. If the caps are part of the input, the profile enumeration grows as \(\binom{|B|+K_C}{K_C}\) and \(\binom{|A|+K_S}{K_S}\); whether column generation, an FPT approach, or hardness is the right answer becomes a further question.

This is a credible mirror because it preserves the paper’s policy class, MNL choice behavior, reciprocal-match objective, and per-agent assortment limits. It keeps alternatives as individual agents; only the society and the display graph are summarized by type masses and local profiles. The paper’s related high-multiplicity assortment literature also supports treating this regime as sensible. This case covers Theorem 6.6’s fully static result, not the adaptivity-gap or fully adaptive results. The paper’s strong NP-hardness statement in Section 5 concerns the uncapped sibling and is derived from cited work, so I am not using it as a numbered in-paper anchor for this capped mirror.

My weakest point is the type assumption: every agent in a type must share the full preference vector and cap. If preferences vary idiosyncratically, the support may grow with \(N\), and the compression disappears. Also, Theorem 6.6’s polynomial running time is in the explicit population size; it does not itself imply an algorithm polynomial in the number of types. That gap is exactly what this continuous problem would test.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only anchor raised is Theorem 6.6, and I cannot make a convincing case that its mirror is unworthy. The strongest objection is that the proposed result applies to a narrower regime than the theorem: fixed assortment caps and a finite set of complete preference types. If caps grow with the population, the number of local profiles can grow as \(\binom{\tau+K}{K}\), so the proposed LP gives no uniform polynomial-time bound. And if pairwise preferences remain mostly idiosyncratic, the type support can grow with the market, eliminating the compression.

Those are limits on the regime, though, not fundamental failures of the mirror. Shortlists with fixed caps and repeated preference patterns are plausible in large matching platforms. With fixed caps and support, the number of profiles is polynomial in the type support, and the expected-match objective depends on local profiles and reciprocal edge mass. The main technical obligation—showing that feasible edge masses are asymptotically realizable by simple bounded-degree display graphs—looks like a real proof task, not an evident obstruction. The per-capita objective also need not degenerate: bounded display degrees keep each edge’s MNL choice probabilities at constant scale, allowing a linear number of expected matches.

So the negative case is weak. The mirror may yield an exact LP for a fixed-cap high-multiplicity regime, which would be a substantive Class A result even if it does not settle Theorem 6.6 uniformly over growing caps or arbitrary agent heterogeneity. The paper’s matching objective and policy class make this a recognizable version of its problem; existing high-multiplicity work supports the regime rather than colliding with it. I would report the scope limitation and the realization proof obligation, but I would not argue that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists.
