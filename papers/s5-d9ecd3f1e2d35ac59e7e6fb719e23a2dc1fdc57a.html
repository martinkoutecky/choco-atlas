The attached case names no continuous mirror to defend. Its point—that the paper has no computational result to mirror—is the strongest case against one.

Theorem 1 characterizes strategy-proof, type-anonymous rules through a mixed median and double-quota methods; Theorem 2 gives an equivalent characterization. Proposition 3 is an attributed structural result. None studies an algorithmic problem or asserts a complexity result. The paper’s contribution is axiomatic: it identifies which rules satisfy strategy-proofness and type-anonymity.

There is a credible continuous population model here. Residents with the same complete preference can form a type, and type-anonymity makes aggregating their mass natural. So the negative case should not rest on a lack of multiplicity or on identity being essential. But replacing counts with masses in the median and quota conditions would yield a continuous axiomatic characterization, still outside ChoCo’s computational target. A direct nonatomic version also needs an incentive definition: a single agent has zero mass, so unilateral reports do not change the population distribution. A coalition-level mass incentive condition could address that, but it would remain an axiomatic question.

One could add a computational task—say, evaluate a parameterized median-and-quota rule on a distribution, or verify whether a succinctly encoded mechanism satisfies the characterization. Neither task is posed by the paper, and either would require choosing a new input representation and problem definition. Likewise, adding control or robustness would introduce an objective absent from the results. Those may be worthwhile projects, but they are not continuous mirrors of the paper’s named results.

So the case is not that no continuous version can be written. It is that the paper offers no computational result to continuize; its natural population mirror stays on the axiomatic side of the programme’s boundary.
