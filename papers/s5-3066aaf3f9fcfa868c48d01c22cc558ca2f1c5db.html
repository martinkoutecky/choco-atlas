The continuous mirror question

Given a finite support \(T\) of complete constrained-PLC utility and budget types \((u_t,w_t)\), with positive binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), \(\min_t\mu_t\ge\tau^{-2}\), and one unit of each item supplied per capita, find prices \(p\ge0\) and per-person bundles \(x_t\ge0\) such that, for \(W=\sum_t\mu_t w_t\), \(u_t(x_t)\ge V_t(p,w_t)-\epsilon\), \(p^\top x_t\le w_t+\epsilon W\) for every type, \(\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\le1\) for every item, and \(\sum_jp_j(1-\sum_t\mu_t x_{tj})\le\epsilon W\).

The model it lives in

Represent each type \(t\) by one aggregate agent with bundle \(y_t=\mu_t x_t\), budget \(\mu_t w_t\), and utility μ_t u_t(y_t/μ_t); item supplies are \(1\). Applying Theorem 4.11 at accuracy \(\epsilon\mu_{\min}\), where \(\mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t\), gives the stated per-type guarantees in \(O((\tau^5/\epsilon^2)^\tau)\) linear programs under the mass promise.

What the mirror covers

Covers Theorem 4.11's approximate Fisher equilibrium result for constrained PLC utilities with a fixed number of agents. It leaves the fixed-item result, the matching-market results, the Arrow-Debreu extensions, and the equilibrium existence and non-convexity results unaddressed.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the lower bound on positive type masses be removed while retaining uniform per-type ε guarantees and running time controlled by their binary bit lengths?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest anchor is Theorem 4.11, proved in this paper. It says that, for a Fisher market with \(n\) agents, \(m\) items, regular constrained PLC utilities, and \(\sigma<1\), a \(\sigma\)-approximate market equilibrium can be found by solving \(O((n/\sigma^2)^n)\) linear programs, each polynomial in the input size. Here “\(\sigma\)-approximate” means each agent gets utility at least \(V_i(p,w_i)-\sigma\), spends at most \(w_i+\sigma\sum_i w_i\), the goods are not overallocated, and the value of unsold goods is at most \(\sigma\sum_i w_i\). The theorem also uses the paper’s utility normalization \(0<V_i^{\max}\le1\). The paper discusses PPAD-hardness, but attributes it to earlier work; it is not a numbered hardness result proved here, so I would not use it as an anchor.

My lead mirror is Macroscopic-Type PLC Fisher Equilibrium. A concrete regime is a large CEEI-style allocation market for divisible service capacity, with millions of participants but a short catalog of preference, constraint, and budget profiles. A type \(t\) includes everything the market model uses about an individual: their constrained PLC utility \(u_t\) and per-person budget \(w_t\). Its mass \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of the population of that type, with positive binary-rational masses summing to one. Assume \(\min_t\mu_t\ge\tau^{-2}\), where \(\tau\) is the number of supported types. Each item has one unit of supply per capita, so an \(N\)-person clone market has \(N\) units of each item. The population is continuous here; divisible goods are inherited from the paper’s model.

The problem is to find prices \(p\ge0\) and a per-person bundle \(x_t\ge0\) for every type. Let \(W=\sum_t\mu_tw_t\) be per-capita total budget and \(V_t(p,w_t)=\max\{u_t(z):p^\top z\le w_t\}\). For accuracy \(\epsilon\in(0,1)\), a solution must satisfy \(u_t(x_t)\ge V_t(p,w_t)-\epsilon\) and \(p^\top x_t\le w_t+\epsilon W\) for every type; aggregate use must satisfy \(\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj}\le1\) for every item \(j\), with \(\sum_jp_j(1-\sum_t\mu_tx_{tj})\le\epsilon W\). The goal is to compute this approximate competitive equilibrium, not to maximize a separate welfare objective.

This is a faithful high-multiplicity market question. Clearing denominators in the masses gives a finite clone market: if \(\mu_t=n_t/N\), type \(t\) has \(n_t\) identical participants and the market has \(N\) units of each item. Giving each clone bundle \(x_t\) yields precisely the weighted per-capita clearing condition above. Averaging identical agents’ optimal bundles preserves feasibility and optimal utility by concavity. Scaling supply with population is part of keeping per-person consumption in the same regime.

The expected direction is Class A, with a useful caveat on small groups. Set \(y_t=\mu_tx_t\) and give a synthetic agent of type \(t\) budget \(\mu_tw_t\) and utility \(\bar u_t(y)=\mu_tu_t(y/\mu_t)\). This is still a regular constrained PLC utility: if \(u_t(x)=\max\{q_t^\top x+s_t^\top z:A_tx+B_tz\le b_t\}\), then \(\bar u_t(y)=\max\{q_t^\top y+s_t^\top\theta:A_ty+B_t\theta\le\mu_tb_t\}\). The synthetic market has \(\tau\) agents, unit item supplies, and total budget \(W\). Applying Theorem 4.11 at accuracy \(\epsilon\mu_{\min}\), where \(\mu_{\min}=\min_t\mu_t\), gives the stated per-type guarantees after recovering \(x_t=y_t/\mu_t\). The resulting bound is \(O((\tau/(\epsilon^2\mu_{\min}^2))^\tau)\) polynomial-size LPs; under the mass promise, this is \(O((\tau^5/\epsilon^2)^\tau)\), fixed-parameter in \(\tau\) and \(1/\epsilon\).

The further question is whether the \(\mu_{\min}\) dependence can be removed while preserving uniform per-person guarantees for arbitrarily rare types. Without a lower bound on mass, the reduction above requires accuracy that can be exponentially small in the bit length of a mass. A mass-weighted error notion avoids that issue, but may let the algorithm neglect a small group, so it is a different guarantee.

My weakest point is that the mass promise excludes precisely those tiny positive types. The paper does not state this population-scaled model or its type-parameterized bound as a theorem; the mirror relies on the reduction above. Still, the underlying object remains their Fisher equilibrium problem, and the finite-clone interpretation makes the population change recognizable to the authors. This case covers Theorem 4.11’s constrained-PLC Fisher result; it does not try to mirror the paper’s matching-market or Arrow–Debreu results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only anchor raised is Theorem 4.11, and it is a named computational result. The proposed market regime is also credible: a large CEEI style population can have a short list of complete utility and budget types, with supply scaling per capita. Agents of one type are interchangeable for this market question, so aggregating their bundles preserves the relevant utility and clearing conditions.

In fact, for type mass \(\mu_t\), set \(y_t=\mu_t x_t\), give the aggregate type budget \(\mu_t w_t\), and use utility \(\bar u_t(y)=\mu_t u_t(y/\mu_t)\). This remains constrained PLC, and the resulting market has \(\tau\) agents and unit supplies. That is a strong bridge to the paper’s algorithm, rather than a modeling objection: the continuum makes the type distribution the input, and the algorithm’s dependence is on the number of types.

The best objection is about rare types. A uniform per-type utility guarantee of \(\epsilon\) translates to accuracy \(\epsilon\mu_t\) for the aggregate type, so the stated reduction depends on \(\mu_{\min}\). With an extremely small binary-rational mass, its running time need not be polynomial in the mass’s bit length. The proponent’s promise \(\mu_{\min}\ge\tau^{-2}\) avoids that problem in a plausible regime. A mass-weighted error guarantee could cover rarer types, though it gives a small group less protection.

I cannot honestly turn that limitation into a case against every worthwhile mirror. The exact aggregation and the paper’s existing fixed-agent algorithm make this mirror unusually easy to answer; under the programme’s rules, an answer already available from a reduction does not make the question unworthy. My negative case is weak: it identifies a restriction on the proposed guarantee, while Theorem 4.11’s mirror survives.
