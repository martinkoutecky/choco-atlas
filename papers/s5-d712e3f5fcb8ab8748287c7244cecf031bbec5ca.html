In an atomless population, each voter has zero mass, so changing their vote cannot change a majority. Strategic voting then loses its usual incentive basis. Treat each preference type as a strategic bloc, and the game instead has a small set of weighted players; use only the mass-weighted majority relation, and the question becomes one about an aggregate relation rather than the paper’s strategic-voting model.

The finite-committee regime does not rescue the mirror: a small legislature is not naturally a high-multiplicity population, while moving the model to a mass electorate changes who is voting and how agenda control works.
