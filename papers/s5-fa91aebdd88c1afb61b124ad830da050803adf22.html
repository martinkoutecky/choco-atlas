The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\) of complete types \(t=(x,y,c)\), a rational society distribution \(\mu\) with \(\mu_t=n_t/N\), a sparse-regression model class, and target accuracy and incentive parameters, find the minimum expected total payment of a type-mass mechanism. The mechanism maps reported-response mass to an estimator and payment rates; require error at most \(\eta_{\mathrm{acc}}\), \((\epsilon,\delta)\)-joint differential privacy under a one-participant mass change of \(1/N\), truthful reporting as an \(\eta_{\mathrm{IC}}\)-Bayesian Nash equilibrium for at least \(1-\alpha\) of the mass, and individual rationality for that mass.

The model it lives in

A direct mechanism over \(\mu\) with mass flows \(x_{t,\hat y}\) from true types \(t=(x,y,c)\) to reported responses \(\hat y\), inducing an estimator \(\bar\theta\) and payment rates. Minimize expected total payment \(\sum_{t,\hat y}x_{t,\hat y}\pi_{t,\hat y}\) subject to the accuracy, privacy, incentive, and rationality guarantees.

The objection that survived

The atomless limit can change what an individual deviation does to the estimator and how privacy adjacency is defined; the proposed \(1/N\) perturbation addresses this only if the continuous model retains that individual scale explicitly.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the integrated mechanism guarantees in Corollary 8, including the privacy, accuracy, truthfulness, rationality, and budget results. It does not separately mirror the sparse-covariance estimation by-product or the technical lemmas supporting the estimator.

Open questions for a prover

  • What is the complexity of the minimum-payment problem in the support size \(\tau\), dimension \(d\), denominator \(N\), and input bit length?
  • Can the finite-sample guarantees for accuracy, privacy, and incentives be retained under the type-mass formulation as \(N\) grows?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest plausible population regime is a large survey or health system with standardized, high-dimensional covariates, rounded response values, and a small number of privacy-cost bands. A complete type would be \(t=(x,y,c)\); its mass \(\mu_t=n_t/N\) is the fraction of the \(N\) participants with that record and cost. If \(\tau\ll N\), the analyst’s decision could be a type-level flow of reported responses, together with an estimated sparse regression parameter and payment per unit of mass. A continuous mechanism-design question would ask for a mechanism minimizing total payments subject to an accuracy guarantee, approximate truthful reporting for most of the mass, individual rationality, and privacy defined for changing one participant’s mass \(1/N\). This retains the paper’s problem: estimating the model while eliciting truthful responses from privacy-concerned agents. It requires a finite or quantized type universe, which is a meaningful co-modification for this regime.

But that is only a plausible research question, not one I can anchor to a qualifying result in the paper. Theorem 3 proves an accuracy guarantee for Algorithm 2, and Corollary 8 combines accuracy, joint differential privacy, approximate truthfulness, individual rationality, and a small budget under an asymptotic parameter choice. Neither states a running-time bound or a computational-complexity classification. The paper’s other named theorems concern privacy, incentives, rationality, and budget; it contains no named NP-hardness, tractability, or parameterized-complexity result.

So the positive case is that this high-multiplicity mechanism-design mirror is recognizable and plausible in the stated regime. Its weakness is decisive for the requested analysis: there is no named computational result to serve as an anchor, and the mass-level privacy condition would itself need careful formulation. I would not present the proposed question as an anchor-qualified continuous mirror of this paper’s complexity results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s proposed anchors do not survive the paper’s own scope. Theorem 3 gives an accuracy bound, and Corollary 8 combines accuracy, privacy, truthfulness, rationality, and budget guarantees. Neither is a result about computational complexity. The other named results likewise concern statistical or mechanism properties; the algorithms are constructions, not a study of running time, hardness, or approximation. So there is no computational result here for a continuous mirror to classify.

The strongest proposed setting—standardized, quantized data and a few privacy-cost bands—could support a sensible high-multiplicity mechanism-design question. But asking for the least payment budget subject to accuracy and incentive constraints adds a new optimization problem; it is not a continuous version of a problem this paper solves. The distinction persists under better modeling. With an atomless population distribution, changing one agent’s data has no positive mass, so the paper’s individual-level privacy and incentive conditions need replacement. Keeping each participant’s mass at \(1/N\) preserves those conditions, but leaves a high-multiplicity empirical dataset rather than supplying a missing complexity question. High multiplicity may make that regime sensible; it does not create a computational result to mirror.

The negative case is strongest as a scope finding, not as a proof that no continuous statistical mechanism-design problem could be worthwhile elsewhere. For this paper, though, neither Theorem 3 nor Corollary 8 is an anchor for ChoCo’s computational programme.
