The case FOR (proponent)

The paper contains no eligible named computational result: it has no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a complexity result. Sections 5–6 report experimental findings about MERGE, SPLIT, and CSS1. The NP-completeness claim in §2.1 and the search-node bound in §2.3 are attributed to Sandholm et al. (1999a), but the supplied paper gives neither as a numbered result. So I cannot honestly provide a numbered anchor or a continuous problem *per anchor*.

The best positive candidate is the underlying coalition-structure optimization in equation (3), though it is not an anchor. Consider a large population of firms in a virtual-enterprise setting, with finitely many complete types—such as role, resources, and task access—and \(\tau\) types among \(N\) firms, with \(N\gg\tau\). Let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of the population of type \(t\). A coalition with type-mass profile \(x\leq\mu\) has value \(w(x)\), where \(w\) is given explicitly as a rational, piecewise-linear, Lipschitz function and \(w(0)=0\).

The continuous problem is: given \(T,\mu,w,\varepsilon\), output a finite list of coalition profiles \(x^1,\ldots,x^q\) such that \(\sum_j x^j=\mu\) and \(\sum_j w(x^j)\) is within \(\varepsilon\) of the supremum over such decompositions. Each list is realizable by partitioning the divisible type masses into coalitions. This preserves the paper’s central decision—partition agents into disjoint coalitions to maximize total characteristic-function value—and gives a concrete high-multiplicity regime the authors could plausibly recognize.

The weak point is substantial: this candidate assumes coalition value depends only on type composition, whereas the paper’s general characteristic function permits arbitrary values for named subsets. It also uses divisible mass and an approximate solution. I would expect tractability only when the resulting pricing problem has exploitable structure; the paper establishes no such result. Its experiments also concern distributions over coalition values, so treating those distributions themselves as the continuous mirror would continue uncertainty, not the population.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no eligible result in this paper to anchor a continuous mirror to. Its central claims are experimental comparisons of MERGE, SPLIT, and CSS1 under four distributions over coalition values. The NP-completeness statement and node bound in §2 are attributed to Sandholm et al. (1999a), not established here as named results. So the paper supplies no theorem about a computational problem whose continuous counterpart could be asked for, as the proposed anchors required.

The strongest reconstruction is the proponent’s version of equation (3): represent a large population by finitely many agent types and maximize total value over decompositions of their masses into coalition profiles. That is a coherent problem, but it gets its tractability-ready form by assuming coalition value is a function \(w(x)\) of type composition, with a succinct piecewise-linear representation. The paper permits arbitrary values \(v_S\) for named subsets. Those values need not agree for coalitions with the same type composition; preserving them can require types that distinguish nearly every agent. The proposed model therefore narrows the game and changes its input representation.

It also does not carry over the paper’s empirical finding. The experiments randomize values independently across named coalitions. Making a coalition’s value depend only on its type profile instead ties together values for coalitions with the same profile. Studying random functions \(w\) would be a new uncertainty model, not a population continuization of the paper’s experimental result. And if the target is the algorithms’ anytime behavior, the paper’s search over a finite coalition-structure graph has no direct counterpart in the profile-decomposition optimization problem.

This case is strongest as a verdict about the paper: the candidate could be worthwhile as a separate study of composition-based coalition formation, but it is not a continuous mirror of a named computational result here. The universal claim remains the weak point. A large population of repeated firm types with composition-dependent values is plausible, so I cannot honestly rule out a worthwhile continuous coalition-formation problem in every scenario.
