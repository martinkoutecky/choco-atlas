What the mirror covers

The candidate mirrors concern Theorems 2 and 4; the paper's other results are left alone, and no continuous result is specified.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case fails at the anchor requirement: this paper has no named computational result in the sense you specified. Theorem 2 proves that the median of an odd number of stable roommate matchings is itself a well-defined stable matching. Theorem 4 proves the analogous generalized-median result for college admissions; both are structural existence results, not complexity classifications or algorithms for finding stable matchings from preferences. The paper also cites earlier LP and algorithmic work, but does not establish those results itself.

Theorem 4 is the closest support for a continuous research direction. Its result, proved here, is that given \(k\) stable college-admissions matchings with responsive preferences, assigning each student their \(l\)-th-best match and each college its \((k-l+1)\)-st-best roster yields a stable matching, for every \(l\). A high-volume placement market with many applicants and employer units sharing a small number of preference types is a plausible high-multiplicity setting. But turning this into a mass-matching question raises an extra issue: type-level flows alone do not say which individuals’ assignments across the \(k\) matchings should be compared to form a median. Resolving that would be new work, not a direct complexity mirror of Theorem 4.

So I would not nominate a per-anchor continuous problem: there is no eligible anchor. The best positive claim is that the paper’s median-stability results make high-multiplicity matching a plausible extension to investigate. The weak point is decisive for this task: the paper neither asks nor answers the computational questions that a continuous mirror is meant to chart.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s strongest point is also the decisive limit: this paper has no named computational result to mirror. Theorem 2 proves that a median of stable roommate matchings is stable; Theorem 4 proves the analogous generalized-median result for college admissions. Neither classifies the complexity of a problem on an input market. Given the stable matchings, constructing the stated median is just ranking each agent’s listed partners. The paper cites LP and algorithmic work by others, but that does not make those results computational findings of this paper. Under ChoCo’s gate, there is no eligible anchor.

The best candidate is Theorem 4 in a high-volume placement market: group students and college seats by complete preference type, scale capacities with population, and represent assignments as flows between types. That is a plausible high-multiplicity regime, so I would not argue that matching has no sensible continuous population model. But the theorem’s object is each agent’s \(l\)-th-best assignment across a given list of stable matchings. A flow by type may split a type across colleges, and its marginals do not say which agents’ assignments across the different matchings should be compared to take a median. A repaired version could define a rank-based operation directly on mass flows and ask whether it preserves stability. That would be new structural work about stable mass allocations, not a continuous version of a computational result in this paper.

The same gap applies to Theorem 2: replacing individuals with types and pairs with flows does not by itself say what a type’s median partner is when its mass is split, or how to define blocking by a pair of types. Those choices may support a worthwhile project. But to make the paper’s result into a computational question, one would have to add a task absent from it—such as computing stable mass allocations or optimizing a change to them. That could be valuable research, but it is not an anchor supplied by this paper.

So the negative case is strong at the paper-specific gate and weak as the requested universal claim. The high-multiplicity placement scenario is credible, and I cannot honestly rule out a worthwhile continuous matching project built from it. What I can defend is narrower: this paper offers median-stability structure, not a computational finding whose continuous mirror ChoCo can chart.
