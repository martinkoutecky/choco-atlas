The strongest reconstruction would take a population with finitely many demographic and clustering types, then ask how to allocate it across repeated rounds of discussion groups. But DREAM’s distinctive meeting objective tracks which *individual pairs* have met before. With small groups and many tables, a distribution over types records group compositions but not whether the same two people meet again. Tracking that requires encounter histories or a sparse interaction structure whose size can grow with the population. Replacing individual pairs with type pairs would discard the paper’s criterion; scaling groups with the population would change the setting of small deliberative groups. A richer sparse continuum model might be worth developing, but it would be a new modeling and algorithmic project, not a mirror enabled by the paper’s results.

A mirror limited to demographic balance is easier to imagine, but it sheds the meeting objective that motivates DREAM’s improvement, and the paper says some simple balancing cases are analytically easy. So the case against a *mirror of this paper* is strong: it offers no eligible computational result, and preserving its most distinctive objective resists a low-support mass model. The broader claim that no scenario could ever yield a worthwhile continuous project is weaker; a useful sparse-limit formulation cannot be ruled out from this paper alone.
