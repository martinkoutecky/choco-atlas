The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that there is no computational result here to mirror. The paper’s numbered contributions are definitions of sharing rules. Its other results concern axiomatic properties, formulas, an isomorphism between two game representations, and core non-emptiness. It does not state a computational problem and prove a complexity bound for it. The cited “Theorem 4.63” is an external lattice result, not a result of this paper. Computing the defined values could become a complexity question, but the paper neither studies that task nor specifies an input representation for the game that would make the question precise.

The proposed large-network setting is plausible as a high-multiplicity regime: many nodes could share a complete type comprising location class, radio capability, traffic role, and energy state. But type masses alone do not specify this paper’s input. Its game assigns worth to partitions of individual nodes, and its solutions allocate that worth to individual pairwise edges. To compute those allocations from type masses, one would need an additional model connecting type-level population data to the worth of every partition and to edge interactions. That could be a worthwhile new model, but the computational question would come from those added assumptions, not from a computational result in this paper.

The most favorable case for the proposed mirror is a symmetric network. There the paper already gives the sharing rule: each edge receives \(f(P^\top)/\binom{n}{2}\). Aggregating edges by type pair or rescaling their shares for a continuum may be sensible, but it leaves no algorithmic challenge from the paper’s result. In a less symmetric network, the individual topology and partition-dependent worths matter; encoding enough of that information in the “types” may make the support grow with the population, while compressing it requires further structure absent from the paper.

This is a strong reason to reject the paper as a source of anchored computational mirrors, not a proof that no worthwhile continuous network-game project could be built from it. The proponent’s scenario may motivate such a project, but it does not identify a computational result of this paper whose continuous version the programme could chart.
