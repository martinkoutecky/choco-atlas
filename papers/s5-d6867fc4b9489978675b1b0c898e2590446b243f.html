What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror extends the Proportional and Constrained Equal Award rule comparisons in Tables 2–3 to a distribution over vessel types. It leaves the paper’s numerical findings without a computational guarantee or complexity result.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest plausible continuous mirror is a large fleet of vessels sharing a small number of complete operating types. A type \(t\) would fix gear selectivity, catchability, price, cost, and historical claim; \(\mu_t\) would be the fraction of vessels of that type. The regime is plausible because the paper treats harvesters as vessels or vessel groups and already models outcomes through these shared characteristics.

For a normalized fleet, let \(q_t\) be the quota per vessel of type \(t\). Under the Proportional Rule, \(q_t=h_t\,\mathrm{TAC}/\sum_s\mu_s h_s\); under CEA, \(q_t=\min(h_t,\lambda)\), where \(\lambda\) satisfies \(\sum_t\mu_t\min(h_t,\lambda)=\mathrm{TAC}\). Given the paper’s stock and TAC parameters, the underlying question would be to compute the quota allocation, stock and profit paths, and steady-state outcomes under each rule. This extends the analysis in equations [5]–[9] and the comparisons in Tables 2–3. It is an exploratory evaluation problem, though, rather than a result about computational complexity.

There is no qualifying anchor in this paper: it has no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting that a computational problem is in P, NP-hard, or in another complexity class. Its numbered items are model equations; its CEA-versus-Proportional findings are numerical comparisons. So I cannot honestly name an anchor or give an anchor-specific complexity prediction. The main weakness of the possible mirror is that the paper’s numerical example has two already-aggregated fleet groups, and its continuous quantities are stock and TAC, not population. The fleet scenario gives a plausible population-continuous extension, but it does not turn the paper into an anchored computational case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent names no computational result to anchor. That is the strongest objection here: equations [1]–[9] define the fishery model, and Tables 2–3 report numerical comparisons between two quota rules. The paper proves no complexity result about computing an allocation, choosing a rule, or controlling the fishery. The properties of the bankruptcy rules are background, not new computational theorems.

The best version of the proposed mirror is sensible as a fishery model. Take a large fleet with finitely many complete vessel types \(t\), each fixing its claim, gear, price, and cost, and let \(\mu_t\) be each type’s fleet share. After normalizing quotas per vessel, Proportional gives \(q_t=h_t\,\mathrm{TAC}/\sum_s\mu_s h_s\); CEA gives \(q_t=\min(h_t,\lambda)\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t\min(h_t,\lambda)=\mathrm{TAC}\). Those allocations determine the selectivity-weighted stock dynamics and aggregate profits. This is a plausible high-multiplicity regime; the two-fleet example is no reason to reject it.

But it remains a mass-based version of the paper’s numerical evaluation, not a computational result the paper establishes. The TAC schedule is prescribed, and the rules prescribe the quotas; the analysis then traces stock and profit paths. A continuous population can make that economic comparison more representative of a large fleet, but it supplies no missing complexity question from this paper. Turning it into one would require adding a task such as optimizing TAC policy or choosing allocations under new constraints. That could be worthwhile research, but it would be a new optimal-control or allocation problem, not a mirror of the paper’s results.

So the negative case is strong for ChoCo’s stated scope: this paper offers no computational-complexity anchor, and its most plausible population mirror does not provide one. I cannot establish that no related problem could ever be valuable; the proposed fleet model could be useful in fisheries economics. The narrower conclusion is that its value would not be a continuous computational-social-choice mirror of this paper.
