The continuous mirror question

Given a finite machine set \(K\), a finite support \(T\) of job types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, compatible-machine sets \(E_t\), public values \(v_{tk}\), capacity uses \(c_{tk}\), and per-capita machine capacities \(b_k\), does there exist a polynomial-time universally truthful direct mechanism with a constant approximation to \(\mathrm{OPT}=\max_x\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{k\in E_t}v_{tk}x_{tk}\), subject to \(0\le x_{tk}\), \(\sum_k x_{tk}\le\mu_t\), and \(\sum_t c_{tk}x_{tk}\le b_k\)? Each individual job must receive at most one machine; its report may withhold compatible pairs, and truthful reporting must maximize its assigned-value utility in every deterministic component of the mechanism’s lottery. The mechanism must implement measurable individual assignments realizing the aggregate allocation and obey capacities for every report profile.

The model it lives in

A continuum of jobs with finite type support \(T\), where each complete type \(t\) specifies \(E_t\) and the job’s public value and capacity use for each compatible machine. Aggregate assignments \(x_{tk}\) split type mass across machines while each job receives an integral assignment; machine capacities scale with population size, giving per-capita capacities \(b_k\). The mechanism must also define individual outcomes by reported type, such as report-contingent assignment probabilities \(q_{r,k}\), so aggregate feasibility and pointwise incentive constraints both hold.

The objection that survived

A single job has zero mass, so its deviation cannot affect aggregate reports; the mechanism still needs report-contingent individual assignments that satisfy pointwise truthfulness and machine capacities while admitting a polynomial-time implementation. The proponent recognizes this requirement but does not show how to meet it.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 5.2’s unit-demand generalized assignment result. It leaves aside Theorem 5.1’s invariant special cases, the matroid, matching, and knapsack results, and the broader generalized packing framework.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can a measurable, polynomial-time version of the paper’s sampling and stable-matching mechanism achieve a constant approximation while preserving pointwise truthfulness?
  • Can report-contingent assignments satisfy incentive constraints for every allowed report and capacity constraints for every report profile?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is the paper’s generalized assignment result. A plausible regime is a large labor or service market: many interchangeable jobs are assigned to a fixed set of machines or work sites. A job type records its compatible machines and, for each, the job’s value and capacity use. Jobs with the same complete record are genuinely interchangeable; the number of jobs may be very large while the number \(\tau\) of distinct types and the number of machines stay modest.

The anchor is Theorem 5.2, proved in this paper. It says that for the generalized assignment problem, the mechanism \(\GapMainm\)—which selects uniformly among \(\lsizem(\lambda)\), \(\separationm(\lambda)\), and \(\Gapm(\lambda,\mu)\)—is universally truthful and has a constant approximation ratio. The theorem concerns jobs that each seek at most one machine, machines with capacity constraints, and social welfare equal to the total value of assigned jobs. The paper’s mechanism combines capacity-based filtering with a sampled stable matching and threshold-guided assignments. The paper mentions that optimizing generalized assignment is NP-hard, but does not give that fact as a numbered result, so I would not use it as an anchor.

Here is the lead mirror: continuous truthful generalized assignment. An instance has a finite machine set \(K\), a finite support of job types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, and, for each type \(t\), a compatible-machine set \(E_t\) and public values \(v_{tk}\) and capacity uses \(c_{tk}\) for \(k\in E_t\). Each machine has a per-capita capacity \(b_k\), representing its capacity scaled with the population. A feasible aggregate assignment is a vector \(x_{tk}\geq 0\) satisfying
\[ \sum_{k\in E_t}x_{tk}\leq\mu_t \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{t:k\in E_t}c_{tk}x_{tk}\leq b_k. \]
Its welfare is \(\sum_t\sum_{k\in E_t}v_{tk}x_{tk}\). Here \(x_{tk}\) is the mass of type \(t\) assigned to \(k\): each individual job still gets at most one machine, while a type’s mass may be split across machines.

The question is whether there is a polynomial-time, universally truthful direct mechanism for this domain that, on truthful reports, produces a feasible assignment with welfare at least \(\mathrm{OPT}/\alpha\) for some constant \(\alpha\), where \(\mathrm{OPT}\) is the maximum of the displayed continuous assignment problem. A solution must specify a mechanism over report profiles: each job can report a subset of its compatible pairs, its utility is the value of its assigned machine (zero if unassigned), and truth-telling must be a dominant strategy for each job in every deterministic mechanism in the lottery. The mechanism must produce actual per-agent assignments, or a measurable assignment of the mass that realizes them; an LP solution in \(x\) alone does not establish truthfulness. The desired running time is polynomial in \(|K|\), \(\tau\), and the binary input length, rather than in the number of jobs represented by the masses.

I expect this to be Class A, though the theorem does not transfer automatically. The paper’s sample-and-threshold construction and stable matching machinery look suited to a fluid version, and the aggregate feasibility benchmark is an LP. In the continuous model, \(x_{tk}\) remains a type-level share of integrally assigned jobs; it does not give any one job a fractional machine assignment. The authors would likely recognize this as their generalized assignment problem in a high-multiplicity regime: it preserves the jobs’ private compatibility reports, utilities, machine capacities, and welfare objective.

The weak point is that the paper’s truthfulness proof uses individual jobs, a fixed processing order, and independent sampling. A continuous mechanism must translate those features into measurable assignments among identical jobs and preserve pointwise incentives; solving the aggregate LP is not enough. Further questions include whether the sampled stable matching has a polynomial-time fluid implementation, what approximation ratio is possible in terms of \(|K|\), \(\tau\), and bit length, and how the continuous optimum compares with finite high-multiplicity instances. This mirror covers Theorem 5.2’s generalized assignment result only, not the paper’s full GPP framework.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 5.2 is the strongest anchor, and its generalized assignment setting has a credible high-multiplicity regime: large cohorts of interchangeable jobs competing for capacity at a fixed set of work sites. The strongest objection is that the theorem’s object is a mechanism for named individuals, while a continuous society records only type masses and aggregate assignments. An LP variable \(x_{tk}\) says how much type \(t\) goes to machine \(k\); it does not say which individuals get those assignments or what any one of them gains by hiding a compatible machine.

That gap matters because truthfulness is a pointwise condition. A job of type \(t\) must prefer truthful reporting to every allowed report \(s\subseteq t\), holding the other jobs fixed. In an atomless population, one job’s deviation leaves the mass distribution unchanged. The aggregate input and feasibility constraints therefore say nothing about that job’s outcome. A continuous mirror needs additional machinery: a report-contingent lottery \(q_{s,k}\) for each report, a measurable way to assign outcomes to individuals, and incentive constraints such as
\[ \sum_k v_{tk}q_{t,k}\geq \sum_k v_{tk}q_{s,k}. \]
It must also explain how those individual lotteries are realized while respecting machine capacities.

That repair is substantial, and the aggregate welfare LP does not provide it. The computation may remain centered on individual incentive constraints over possible reports, even when the welfare benchmark has only \(\tau |K|\) variables. This weakens the claim that the continuous formulation brings the problem within reach of continuous-optimization tools; the theorem’s main difficulty is designing a truthful mechanism, rather than pricing an exponential-variable welfare LP.

This is the best negative case, yet it does not defeat the improved mirror. The report space is finite, type masses can be split measurably, and the paper already uses random sampling plus assignments chosen separately for each job. A fluid mechanism could split each type’s mass exactly between test and remaining groups, then impose pointwise incentive constraints on its assignment policy. Large hiring or service cohorts make the scaling of capacity plausible, and the authors could reasonably recognize the resulting question as their generalized assignment problem. I cannot make the universal claim that no scenario yields a worthwhile mirror: the obstacle is real, but it does not rule out a well-posed continuous mechanism-design question.
