The continuous mirror question

Given a star \(S_m\), a finite set of additive valuation types \(T\), a distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\), and per-location capacities \(q_v\), find the maximum \(\alpha\) for which there is a mass allocation \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\) over connected integral bundles \(B\subseteq V(S_m)\), satisfying \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B\ni v}x_{t,B}=q_v\), and \(u_t(B)\ge \alpha\,\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q)\) whenever \(x_{t,B}>0\).

The model it lives in

A capacitated high-multiplicity star: finitely many complete valuation types carry population mass \(\mu_t\); each agent receives an integral connected bundle, while \(x_{t,B}\) records assignment mass and \(q_v\) supplies scale per location. The objective is the largest common MMS-approximation factor \(\alpha\).

The objection that survived

The stated \(N\)-copy construction does not specify a unique underlying graph or connectivity relation, so equality between the original finite theorem and the proposed limit is not yet established.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 3.16's star PoC formula and constructive MMS-approximation algorithm. It leaves the other PoC bounds, path results, envy-freeness theorems, and graph characterizations untouched.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does rational finite replication converge exactly to the mass-allocation model, and can every rational \(x\) be implemented without changing connectivity?
  • Does the homogeneous star factor remain \(m-r+1\) under the continuum formulation?
  • For fixed \(|T|>1\), can the optimal mass allocation and factor be computed in polynomial time?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is a high-multiplicity mirror of the paper’s Theorem 3.16, proved in this paper. It states that for a star with \(m\) goods and \(n\ge2\) agents,
\[ \operatorname{PoC}(G,n)= \begin{cases} m-n+1,&m\ge n,\\ 1,&m<n, \end{cases} \]
and, when \(m\ge n\), gives a polynomial-time algorithm computing a connected allocation in which every agent receives at least \(1/(m-n+1)\) of her MMS. The theorem’s proof is constructive: choose the \(n-1\) most valuable leaves as singleton bundles and give the remaining goods to the last agent.

I would not use the paper’s concluding remarks about NP-hard envy-free allocation as anchors: those are literature summaries, not numbered hardness results proved here.

The natural regime is a large collection of nearly identical households or research teams receiving connected bundles of offices arranged in a hub-and-spoke building. There are \(Nr\) agents, \(N\) indistinguishable copies of every graph location, and only finitely many utility types. A type \(t\) is a complete additive valuation vector \(u_t(v)\) over the \(m\) locations. A type distribution is \(\mu\), where \(Nr\mu_t\) agents have type \(t\). Thus the population grows with \(N\), while the number of types remains fixed. Each household receives an integral bundle whose occupied locations form a connected set of the star.

The per-capita supply of every location is \(q_v=1/r\): there are \(N\) copies of \(v\) for \(Nr\) agents. This scaling is forced by the high-multiplicity regime. The goods remain indivisible; only the population assignment becomes continuous.

Let \(\mathcal C(G)\) be the connected subsets of \(G\). A continuous allocation is a collection of masses \(x_{t,B}\ge0\), where \(x_{t,B}\) is the fraction of the population of type \(t\) receiving the integral connected bundle \(B\). It satisfies
\[ \sum_{B\in\mathcal C(G)}x_{t,B}=\mu_t \]
for every type \(t\), and
\[ \sum_{t}\sum_{B\ni v}x_{t,B}=q_v \]
for every location \(v\). Rational \(x\) can be implemented exactly by taking \(N\) large enough to clear denominators; conversely, finite replicated allocations converge to such an \(x\).

For a type \(t\), define its high-multiplicity MMS by
\[ \operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q) = \max_{y} \min_{B:y_B>0}u_t(B), \]
where \(y\) ranges over distributions on arbitrary integral bundles satisfying
\[ \sum_B y_B=1, \qquad \sum_{B\ni v}y_B=q_v. \]
The connected version restricts \(y\) to \(\mathcal C(G)\).

The continuous problem I would hand to a prover is:

\[ \textsc{Star-MMS}_{\infty} \]

Given a star \(S_m\), an integer \(r\), a finite type distribution \(\mu\), and additive valuations \(u_t\), compute the largest \(\alpha\) for which there exists a connected continuous allocation \(x\) satisfying
\[ u_t(B)\ge \alpha\,\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_t(q) \]
for every type \(t\) and every bundle \(B\) with \(x_{t,B}>0\), where \(q_v=1/r\). A solution is the mass allocation \(x\) and the optimal value \(\alpha\).

The lead subcase is homogeneous, \(\mu=(1)\), because the paper’s price-of-connectivity quantity itself is defined using one utility function. Its worst-case version asks whether
\[ \operatorname{PoC}^{\infty}(S_m,r) = \sup_u \frac{\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}(u;q)} {\operatorname{G\text{-}MMS}^{\infty}_{S_m}(u;q)} \]
equals
\[ \begin{cases} m-r+1,&m\ge r,\\ 1,&m<r. \end{cases} \]

I expect the homogeneous additive-star problem to be tractable, hence Class A. The star has a very simple configuration structure: a connected bundle is either a singleton leaf or a bundle containing the centre. The proof of Theorem 3.16 already supplies the natural continuous candidate allocation, and its counting argument survives the mass formulation. The interesting question is whether mixing many integral configurations in the continuum improves the finite factor. My expectation is that it does not: the paper’s sparse valuation witnessing the factor \(m-r+1\) should remain extremal, while the constructive upper bound should lift to the mass allocation.

This is not merely restating the theorem. At \(N=1\), the paper permits one partition of \(m\) goods among \(r\) agents. In the mirror, \(N\) grows, configurations may be mixed across the population, and the relevant feasibility object is a distribution over connected integral bundles. If the continuous price turns out to equal the finite price, that equality would itself be a genuine discrete-to-continuous result.

The mirror covers Theorem 3.16 and deliberately does not claim to cover the paper’s envy-freeness results. Further questions include whether Theorem 3.19’s path formula survives the same limit, whether fixed \(\tau>1\) introduces new coupling phenomena, and whether arbitrary graphs yield a continuum-specific hardness boundary.

My weakest point is that the scenario requires copying each graph location as the population grows and imposing connectivity on the support of those copies. A referee who insists on a fixed finite inventory may reject that co-modification. It is nevertheless exactly the kind of per-capita supply scaling that a genuine high-multiplicity mirror requires, and the individual bundles remain integral throughout.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the proposed anchor is not yet a continuization of Theorem 3.16’s graph-allocation problem. The theorem has \(m\) distinct goods, represented by the vertices of one star, and \(n\) agents partition those goods. The proposed limit instead has \(N\) copies of every vertex and \(Nr\) agents, while bundles remain subsets of the original \(m\)-vertex star. Thus its feasibility constraints describe a capacitated configuration market, not an instance of the paper’s graph model. The claim that rational \(x\) can be implemented by choosing \(N\) large establishes implementability in that new model, but does not establish a graph-theoretic high-multiplicity limit of the theorem.

There is no canonical way to repair this. If one uses \(N\) disjoint copies of the star, the graph is no longer connected and agents must be assigned to particular buildings. If one connects the copies, connected bundles may combine vertices from different copies, changing the configuration family completely. If one instead forces every bundle to remain within one copy, the problem decomposes into repeated finite star instances; the continuum contributes only bookkeeping. A blow-up graph with vertex capacities is a legitimate new fair-division model, but its connectivity notion and feasible allocations must be specified independently of Theorem 3.16.

The homogeneous anchor is especially vulnerable. With \(\mu=(1)\), the population has only one type, so the substantive object is a distribution over integral bundles with prescribed marginals. The proposed \(\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}\) and its connected counterpart are therefore primarily a convexification of the allocation/configuration space. The population continuum is doing little beyond permitting fractional frequencies. That risks turning an attempted population continuization into the outcome-space continuity that ChoCo explicitly treats as outside its target.

This objection does not defeat every repaired version. A capacitated star with finitely many valuation types is a sensible high-multiplicity fair-division problem: agents remain integral at the bundle level, types are meaningful, and rational mass allocations can be realized by replication. Theorem 3.16’s single-type restriction does not itself invalidate that extension. Consequently, I cannot honestly sustain the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists. The negative case can reject the proponent’s particular “copies of locations” construction as under-specified and not demonstrably faithful, but it cannot defeat the stronger capacitated, multi-type formulation without making an objection that the programme’s rules expressly disallow.
