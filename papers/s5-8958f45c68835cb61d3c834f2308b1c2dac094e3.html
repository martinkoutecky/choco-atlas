The best rescue would be to ask how to compute Compromises and Rewards for a large society, or to define a mass-matching version of its properties. Computing the rule from an explicit finite profile is a graph procedure over agents’ top choices. The paper does not present that as a complexity result. A mass version would need a different input and outcome: the paper’s agents rank named partners, and its rule uses cycles of those individuals. Grouping agents into preference types and matching mass between types replaces those identities with type-level flows. In an atomless model, one person’s report has zero mass and cannot change the aggregate outcome; preserving a substantive incentive question would require a new notion, such as manipulation by a positive-mass coalition. That could be a research project, but it would formulate a new computational problem rather than mirror one established in this paper.

Large matching markets make a continuous model plausible in a broad sense. The paper’s lotteries also involve fractional outcomes, but they leave the population finite; that is outcome-space continuity, not population continuization. Neither point supplies the missing computational anchor. The honest limit of this negative case is that it rules out a direct ChoCo mirror of the paper’s results.
