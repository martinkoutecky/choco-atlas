The continuous mirror question

Given rational type masses \(\alpha_t\) summing to \(1\), rational assignment bids \(b_{tj}\), and rational per-capita position supplies \(\kappa_j\) with \(\sum_j\kappa_j\leq 1\), compute maximal type-uniform bicore feedback. The aggregate assignment maximizes \(\sum_{t,j} b_{tj}x_{tj}\) subject to \(\sum_j x_{tj}\leq\alpha_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{tj}\leq\kappa_j\). Feedback must preserve every optimal aggregate assignment under every admissible combination of discount and raise updates to type submasses.

The model it lives in

Let \(W^*\) be the optimum of the aggregate assignment flow problem, with one type for each complete assignment-relevant profile. Compute \(d_t,r_t\geq0\) and \(p_j\geq0\), uniform within each type, maximizing \(\sum_t\alpha_t(d_t+r_t)\), subject to \(d_t-r_t\geq b_{tj}-p_j\) for every \(t,j\) and \(\sum_j\kappa_jp_j+\sum_t\alpha_td_t=W^*\). The aggregate allocation may be fractional, while each unit of mass receives at most one indivisible position.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is the assignment specialization, not the full video pod model. The paper itself singles out assignment as a special case and studies feedback that preserves an optimal allocation. Theorem 3 is the best anchor: it is proved in this paper, though its proof uses established assignment LP results. It says that every optimal solution \((\boldsymbol{\pi}^*,\boldsymbol{\mu}^*,\mathbf p^*)\) to the assignment dual \(D(N,\emptyset)\) has \((\boldsymbol{\pi}^*,\boldsymbol{\mu}^*)\) in the bicore; conversely, every bicore pair \((\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{\mu})\) has some price vector \(\mathbf p\) such that \((\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{\mu},\mathbf p)\) is optimal for that dual. This is a named LP characterization, and the paper says it computes core and bicore feedback by solving LPs. The paper does not give a named NP-hardness or complexity-class result; this formulation is its strongest computational anchor.

A plausible high-multiplicity regime is a large batch of one-slot ad requests matched to position opportunities across many comparable video pods. Let a type specify the complete assignment-relevant profile: its bid and value for each position, and any eligibility information. If millions of requests share only \(\tau\) such profiles, with \(\tau\ll n\), then type \(t\)’s mass \(\alpha_t\) is its fraction of the population. Position supply must scale with the batch: write \(\kappa_j\) for the supply of position \(j\) per unit population. Each individual request still receives at most one position; the type-level allocation can be fractional because it records the aggregate share assigned.

The continuous problem I would hand to a prover is High-Multiplicity Assignment Feedback. An instance gives rational masses \(\alpha_t\) summing to one, rational per-type bids \(b_{tj}\), and rational per-capita position supplies \(\kappa_j\). The underlying allocation problem is to maximize \(\sum_{t,j} b_{tj}x_{tj}\), subject to \(\sum_j x_{tj}\leq\alpha_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{tj}\leq\kappa_j\). Let its optimum be \(W^*\). The task is to compute discounts \(d_t\geq0\) and raises \(r_t\geq0\), uniform within each type, that maximize total population-weighted feedback, \(\sum_t\alpha_t(d_t+r_t)\), while preserving the paper’s bicore guarantee: every original optimal assignment remains optimal after any admissible combination of agents applies the recommended updates. A price vector \(p_j\geq0\) certifies a feasible solution through

\[ d_t-r_t\geq b_{tj}-p_j\quad\text{for every }t,j, \qquad \sum_j\kappa_jp_j+\sum_t\alpha_td_t=W^*. \]

The object being computed is the maximum-total-feedback policy; the prices are its certificate. These are the type-weighted assignment-dual conditions from the anchor. The expected direction is Class A: with rational input, this is an LP with a number of variables and constraints depending on the number of supported types and position types, not on the number of individual requests. A useful follow-up is whether the same compact certificate survives when requests have pod-specific exclusions or duration constraints.

This mirror covers the paper’s assignment result and its core/bicore feedback interpretation. It does not claim to continuize the general pod allocation problem, the empirical bidding dynamics, or the paper’s treatment of exclusion and duration constraints. The authors should recognize the underlying question: which coordinated discounts and raises keep current winners’ allocation optimal? The paper already answers that question for assignment and gives a linear-programming characterization.

The soft spot is the regime. The paper’s motivating example is a single pod auction, and its simulations use only three to five bidders; those are not high-multiplicity settings. The mirror requires a large batch of comparable one-slot requests and position opportunities, with a small number of complete bid profiles. That is a constructed but credible assignment-market setting, and assignment is expressly part of the paper’s scope. The limitation is that the full video pod model may not retain this compact type-level LP once its extra constraints are restored.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is to the scenario, not to the assignment mathematics. The theorem concerns one assignment problem with a shared set of items. The motivating video auctions are separate pods: a request eligible for one pod cannot generally take a position in another. Pooling many pods into one assignment market can erase pod-specific eligibility and constraints; restoring those details may make each request its own type and remove the claimed small-support regime. That makes the proposed batch of comparable requests a constructed setting whose fit with the paper’s central use case is uncertain.

That objection does not defeat the better mirror, though. The paper explicitly studies assignment as a special case. A market that allocates many standardized positions among requests with repeated bid and value vectors is a plausible high-multiplicity assignment instance in its own right. With per-capita supply, the type-level flow model captures that setting. The feedback guarantee would need to cover updates by every admissible submass of each type, so the right formulation is the continuous bicore, not merely an LP that finds one optimal allocation. But the paper’s assignment characterization gives a credible route to that formulation. Rational masses can be scaled to integer multiplicities, and the assignment structure preserves the aggregate allocation model.

Theorem 3 is a named LP characterization, even though the paper gives no complexity classification in the usual classes. Its continuous mirror would ask about computing feedback on a support-compressed population, measured in the number of types rather than individual bidders. Calling the finite version an LP, or expecting the continuous version to be tractable, would not show that the question is unworthy; tractability is an acceptable result in this programme.

The remaining concern is that realistic pod constraints and bidding dynamics may destroy the small-support assumption. Yet that only narrows the mirror: the assignment specialization can be posed without those features, and one emphatic mirror is enough. I cannot make the universal negative case convincingly. The pooling scenario needs careful justification, but the assignment mirror survives that objection and appears worthwhile.
