The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set of complete patient-donor compatibility types with program ownership, rational masses summing to one, a type-level directed compatibility relation, and a rational coverage threshold, determine whether there are nonnegative masses \(x_q\) on allowed two- and three-cycle templates \(q\) such that type use satisfies \( \sum_q a_{tq}x_q\le\mu_t \) for every type \(t\), total coverage satisfies \( \sum_q |q|x_q\ge\kappa \), and no program \(i\) can choose rejected masses \(0\le r_q\le x_q\) and replacement masses \(z_q\ge0\), supported only on cycles internal to \(i\), with \( \sum_q a_{tq}(x_q-r_q+z_q)\le\mu_t \) for every \(t\) and \( \sum_q b_{iq}(x_q-r_q+z_q)>\sum_q b_{iq}x_q \).

The model it lives in

An atomless exchange pool described by rational masses over complete compatibility and ownership types. The decision variables are masses assigned to feasible cycle templates; each type supplies its mass to those cycles, total covered mass must meet the threshold, and every program must lack a feasible internal replacement that increases its covered mass.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the ETH lower-bound question for \( \mathrm{RPKE}\text{-}3 \) and \( c\text{-}\mathrm{RPKE}\text{-}3 \) with \( c\ge2 \), recast over compatibility-type masses. It leaves the kernel and FPT results, the one-rejection algorithm, and the paper’s other parameterized results aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can existence of such a rejection-proof mass packing be decided in polynomial time in the number of types, programs, and input bit length?
  • Under what conditions does a finite rejection-proof packing yield a rejection-proof mass packing, given that the mass model permits fractional deviations?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead is Fractional Rejection-Proof 3-Cycle Exchange (FRPKE-3), anchored on the paper’s Lemma 4.1, proved there by a reduction from Subgraph Isomorphism. The lemma says that, assuming ETH, no \(c\)-RPKE-3 algorithm runs in \(2^{o(n\log n)}\) time for any fixed \(c\ge2\), even with two agents; the same lower bound applies to unrestricted RPKE-3 with two agents. The reduction encodes a subgraph mapping in cycles and uses an agent’s possible rejection to enforce adjacency. [The published paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol358-ipec2025/html/LIPIcs.IPEC.2025.9/LIPIcs.IPEC.2025.9.html)

Here is the continuous question. An instance gives a finite set \(T\) of patient-donor compatibility types, an owner \(\alpha(t)\in[p]\) for each type, rational masses \(\mu_t\ge0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), a type-level directed compatibility relation, and a rational threshold \(\kappa\in[0,1]\). A type completely describes the pair’s compatibility behavior and owning program. Let \(Q\) be the directed 2- and 3-cycle templates allowed by that relation. For template \(q\), let \(a_{tq}\) count its occurrences of type \(t\), and let \(b_{iq}=\sum_{\alpha(t)=i}a_{tq}\).

The question is whether there is a vector of cycle masses \(x_q\ge0\) such that \(\sum_q a_{tq}x_q\le\mu_t\) for every type and \(\sum_q |q|x_q\ge\kappa\), with no profitable rejection by any program. Precisely, program \(i\) must have no choice of rejected cycle masses \(0\le r_q\le x_q\) and replacement masses \(z_q\ge0\), where \(z_q\) is zero unless \(q\) is internal to \(i\), satisfying both \(\sum_q a_{tq}(x_q-r_q+z_q)\le\mu_t\) for every \(t\) and \(\sum_q b_{iq}(x_q-r_q+z_q)>\sum_q b_{iq}x_q\). A solution is such an \(x\): it says what fraction of the population participates in each cycle template. In an atomless society, people are still assigned to whole cycles; it is the mass assigned to each template that is continuous.

The regime is a large exchange pool drawing from a modest catalogue of compatibility profiles, with many pairs per profile and a small number of programs. Then population size \(N\) grows while support size \(\tau=|T|\) stays comparatively small. Standardized cohorts with compatibility determined by profile could fit this regime. The paper’s set-packing generalization makes this a recognizable version of its underlying packing and rejection problem: cycle feasibility, who owns a pair, and the agent’s gain from replacing joint cycles all remain explicit.

I expect this mirror to be tractable, so the hardness in Lemma 4.1 may dissolve. For 3-cycles there are only \(O(\tau^3)\) templates. For a fixed candidate \(x\), each program’s best rejection is a linear program over \(r\) and \(z\). Its dual gives linear conditions on \(x\) and dual variables; those certificates can be included in one feasibility LP for all programs. That suggests a polynomial-time algorithm in \(\tau\), the number of programs, and the input bit length. It is a concrete complexity question, not just “what happens to hardness”: can the existence of a mass packing meeting the coverage threshold and resisting every mass-valued internal deviation be decided in polynomial time?

The scope is deliberately narrow: this mirrors Lemma 4.1’s two-agent RPKE-3 hardness result, not the paper’s full parameterized landscape. Its main follow-up is the discrete-to-continuous dictionary: when does a finite rejection-proof packing yield a rejection-proof mass packing, and how much do newly available fractional deviations change the answer?

My weakest point is the regime. Real cross-match compatibility can be highly individualized, so exact compatibility types may be nearly as numerous as pairs. The other limitation is substantive: fractional deviations can reject a mass packing even when no whole-cycle deviation rejects a discrete packing. Those are questions the mirror should expose, not reasons to pretend the models coincide. The type-based version remains worth asking because it preserves the paper’s core incentive and packing structure while making population mass the object of study.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against the proponent’s anchor is that it does not preserve the scale or structure of Lemma 4.1’s hard instances. Its reduction uses individually chosen graph vertices to encode an injective map, and its target leaves exactly one vertex uncovered: \(k=3n_G\) out of \(3n_G+1\). Under normalization, that slack has mass \(1/(3n_G+1)\), which vanishes. Replacing named vertices with a modest catalogue of compatibility types removes the identities used by the reduction; retaining them makes the support grow with the instance. So the lemma gives little direct evidence about the complexity of the proposed mass problem.

There is also a gap in the discrete-to-continuous dictionary for rejection-proofness. A finite pool can be rejected because a program gains one patient, a normalized gain of \(1/N\). As \(N\) grows, such deviations vanish in mass, while the atomless definition still rejects every deviation that improves coverage by any positive mass. Requiring a fixed positive gain would make the continuum condition more stable, but would change the rejection criterion. This limits how much the continuous problem can tell us about the paper’s exact finite-pool guarantee.

Those objections do not defeat the better mirror, though. A large pool with repeated, complete compatibility profiles and a small number of programs is a sensible high-multiplicity regime. Patients still participate in whole cycles; only the mass assigned to each cycle template is fractional. With fixed cycle length and support size \(\tau\), there are polynomially many templates, and the proposed primal–dual formulation appears to express resistance to every program’s deviation as linear constraints. That is a clear computational question, and its tractability would be a result of the kind ChoCo is meant to surface.

So my honest negative case is weak. The ETH reduction itself is a poor anchor for predicting the continuous problem, and the exact rejection criterion complicates the dictionary. But the type-based mass model survives both objections. I cannot support the stronger claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario.
