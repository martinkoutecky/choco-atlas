The continuous mirror question

For any finite number \(k\) of constituencies, let each constituency \(g\) be a finite-support measure \(\nu_g\) on resident locations on the line, with rational masses and \(\sum_g \nu_g(\mathbb{R})=1\), and let the two sites be \(0\) and \(1\). Define \(\mathrm{SW}_{\nu}(a)=\sum_g\int |z-a|\,d\nu_g(z)\). Among deterministic local full-information two-step mechanisms that choose one site \(r_g\in\{0,1\}\) using only \(\nu_g\), then choose a winner from the proposed sites, determine the minimum worst-case ratio \(\sup_{k,(\nu_g)}\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\mathrm{SW}_{\nu}(a)/\mathrm{SW}_{\nu}(w)\). Is the optimum exactly \(3\)?

The model it lives in

Types are constituency-labeled locations \((g,z)\) carrying mass \(\nu_g(z)\). Each group proposes \(r_g\in\{0,1\}\) using its own distance information, and the final choice is among those proposals; welfare is the mass-weighted sum of distances. The paper’s candidate rule chooses each group’s welfare-maximizing site and then the site proposed by the greatest total constituency mass.

What the mirror covers

Covers the full-information two-site line lower bound in Theorem 2.4 and the matching mechanism guarantee in Corollary 2.2. It leaves the ordinal results and the general-metric bounds unaddressed.

Open questions for a prover

  • Formalize the weighted upper-bound and lower-bound arguments for all finite-support rational mass profiles, including unequal constituency masses.
  • Do tight continuous population bounds extend to the paper’s general-metric full-information setting or its ordinal mechanisms?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is narrow. The paper has no named theorem establishing a complexity class such as NP-hardness, membership in \(P\), or fixed-parameter tractability. Its named results establish distortion guarantees. If those do not count as computational anchors under your rule, then there is no qualifying anchor here. If approximation guarantees count, the best case is a continuous version of the paper’s full-information, two-alternative line result.

The regime is a set of large constituencies choosing between two possible sites for an obnoxious facility, with both sites on a corridor. Residents in each constituency occupy a small number of recurring locations, so the number of residents is much larger than the number of distinct location types. A type is a resident’s location \(z\), which determines their distances to the two sites; its mass is their fraction of the total population. For constituency \(g\), let \(\nu_g\) be its finite-support mass measure, with \(\sum_g \nu_g(\mathbb R)=1\). The continuous welfare of site \(a\in\{0,1\}\) is
\[ \mathrm{SW}_{\nu}(a)=\sum_g\int_{\mathbb R}|z-a|\,d\nu_g(z). \]
This preserves the paper’s two-step decision: each constituency proposes one site using its own full information, then the mechanism selects among the proposals.

My lead anchor is Theorem 2.4, proved in the paper: *the distortion of any full-information mechanism is at least \(3\), even when the metric space is a line and there are only two alternatives.* The matching upper anchor is Corollary 2.2, also proved here: *when there are only two alternatives, or when the metric space is a line, the distortion of Max-Weight-of-Optimal is at most \(3\).* These are approximation and impossibility bounds, not complexity classifications; I am using them as anchors only in that broader algorithmic sense.

The continuous problem is: Continuous Two-Site Distributed Distortion. Given the model above, find a deterministic, two-step, local full-information mechanism minimizing its worst-case distortion over all numbers of constituencies and all finite-support rational measures \((\nu_g)_g\). A solution is a mechanism together with a proof of its worst-case guarantee and a matching lower bound. The paper’s candidate mechanism has each constituency choose the site maximizing its own integrated distance, then selects the site represented by the greatest total constituency mass. The weighted version of the upper-bound argument gives distortion at most \(3\). Conversely, finite adversarial profiles embed as atomic rational measures, so Theorem 2.4 gives the lower bound. The continuous optimum is therefore \(3\).

I expect this mirror to retain that exact distortion. The authors should recognize it: the groups, locality restriction, full-information input, two-stage choice, and welfare objective are all preserved; sums over residents become integrals over their mass. This covers only the full-information line/two-site results, not the paper’s ordinal results or its general-metric bounds. It is not honest to label it Class A, B, or C in the programme’s complexity trichotomy: the anchor results do not classify computational complexity or establish hardness to transfer. Further questions include whether the general-metric lower bounds survive for unrestricted mechanisms, and whether ordinal mechanisms admit equally clean continuous formulations using mass matchings.

The weakest point is also the main qualification: this is a compelling population mirror of the paper’s distortion problem, but not a strong instance of ChoCo’s complexity programme. The paper does not study runtime or hardness, and in this two-site case the proposed mechanism is simple to compute. If the anchor rule requires a named complexity result rather than an approximation guarantee, the positive case fails that gate.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is a scope objection: Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.2 are bounds on distortion, not results about the computational complexity of a problem. They establish that mechanisms cannot do better than distortion \(3\) in the two-site line setting, and that a particular mechanism achieves \(3\). The paper gives no runtime, hardness, or parameterized-complexity result for those claims. That leaves no named computational result here for ChoCo to continuize.

The proposed population model itself is sound. Constituents can be large, with residents grouped by district and location; constituency and location together give a sensible type. Replacing sums with mass-weighted sums preserves the two-step mechanism and locality. I do not see a fundamental objection based on multiplicity, identity, or a degenerate limit.

But the proposed continuous question—find the local mechanism with the best worst-case distortion over all such societies—is a class-wide mechanism-design question, not a computational task on a society input. If instead the task is to compute the outcome for a supplied finite-support society, the two-site line mechanism just compares weighted distance totals, which does not recover the paper’s distortion theorem as a complexity result. Making it a harder algorithmic problem would require adding a new task that the paper does not study.

So this anchor is a legitimate continuous population mirror of the paper’s distortion analysis, but it does not appear to be a ChoCo computational mirror. That is where the negative case is weak: if the programme counts approximation guarantees for mechanisms as computational results, the mirror survives.
