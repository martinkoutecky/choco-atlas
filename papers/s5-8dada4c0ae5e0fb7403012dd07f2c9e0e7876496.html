The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent is right that this paper has zero qualifying computational anchors. Its numbered propositions establish algebraic identities, monotonicity, and axiomatic characterizations. Proposition 18, the closest candidate, identifies \(\beta_i\) with a derivative of a finite-dimensional multilinear extension; it gives no complexity bound or algorithmic result. Propositions 9 and 11 likewise describe the value, not the cost of computing it. There is no named computational result here for a continuous mirror to preserve.

The strongest repair would be to study many delegates grouped into a few complete types, such as delegates with the same voting role and cooperation ability, and ask how power is distributed across type masses. That is a plausible high-multiplicity setting. But the paper’s \(\beta_i\) measures a named player’s marginal contribution across random subsets of players. In an atomless population, a single member has zero mass and cannot change a coalition’s worth; under the natural mass version of Banzhaf’s random-coalition interpretation, the coalition mass at each type is deterministic. Individual power therefore collapses to zero. A nontrivial type-level quantity would need a new scaling, normalization, or coalition semantics. Those choices might lead to a worthwhile project, but they would define a new computational question rather than mirror a result proved here.

The paper’s multilinear extension does not supply an existing continuous-population treatment: its domain is still \([0,1]^n\), with \(n\) finite. Nor does it establish a computational collision. The strongest honest case is therefore that this article is a poor ChoCo target: any promising computation or limit question must be supplied from outside its results, and the direct power measure risks degenerating in the atomless limit. The broader claim that no scenario could ever make such a question worthwhile is not provable from this paper; the paper-based case against a mirror is strong.
