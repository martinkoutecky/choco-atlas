The case FOR (proponent)

Strictly speaking, this paper has no eligible computational anchor: it contains no named theorem asserting NP-hardness, membership in \(P\), fixed-parameter tractability, or similar. Its named results are structural mechanism-design results, all proved in the paper. The strongest positive case must therefore be a proposed computational mirror of those results, not a claim that the paper already studies its complexity.

My lead structural anchor is Theorem 3, proved here: if \(f\) is the minimal RPE mechanism on the NCBI domain \(\widetilde R\), then \(f\) is group non-manipulable. In full, for every admissible profile, \(f\) selects an RPE state whose price vector is the unique minimal RPE price vector; no nonempty coalition can jointly misreport preferences so that every coalition member strictly prefers her new priced assignment to her original one. Theorem 1, also proved here, supplies existence of a minimal RPE vector for every profile and priority structure. Theorem 2, again proved here, gives uniqueness of that vector on \(\widetilde R\). These form a natural existence–uniqueness–incentive chain.

The plausible high-multiplicity setting is a large public-housing or labour-market allocation. There is a unit mass of applicants and finitely many apartment or job classes \(H=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), with per-capita capacities \(q_h\). A type \(\theta\) includes the applicant’s complete valuation or preference parameters, eligibility characteristics, and priority class for every \(h\). Thus two applicants have the same type only when they are indistinguishable for the entire problem, including priorities. A society is \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\), with \(n\gg |T|=\tau\): for example, millions of applicants but a few hundred preference–eligibility–priority types. This is especially plausible because the paper itself explicitly interprets its model as a labour market with wage floors and ceilings.

The houses remain individually indivisible. Continuization occurs only in the population and aggregate supply: \(x_{\theta h}\) is the mass of type \(\theta\) receiving one integral house of class \(h\), with
\[ \sum_h x_{\theta h}=\mu_\theta,\qquad \sum_\theta x_{\theta h}\le q_h. \]
The fractional \(x_{\theta h}\) is bookkeeping for many identical individuals, not a fractional bundle received by one person.

The lead problem is Continuous Minimal-RPE Computation. An instance consists of finite \(T\) and \(H\); rational masses \(\mu_\theta\); rational capacities \(q_h\); rational quasi-linear values \(v_{\theta h}\); lower and upper prices \(\underline p_h,\overline p_h\); and type-level priority ranks \(\rho_h(\theta)\). Utilities are
\[ u_\theta(h,p_h)=v_{\theta h}-p_h,\qquad u_\theta(0,0)=0. \]
The input is promised to satisfy the type-level analogue of the paper’s NCBI condition.

The task is to output a price vector \(p^\star\) and an aggregate assignment \(x^\star\) such that:

  • every \(p_h^\star\) lies in \([\underline p_h,\overline p_h]\), and every slack house class has price \(\underline p_h\);
  • there is no positive-mass priority-blocking pair;
  • whenever some assigned type strictly prefers an occupied house class \(h\) to its own assignment, \(p_h^\star=\overline p_h\);
  • \(x^\star\) is constrained Pareto-efficient among priority-respecting aggregate assignments at \(p^\star\);
  • \(p^\star\) is coordinatewise minimal among all prices admitting such an RPE assignment.

Under the NCBI promise, Theorem 2 suggests that \(p^\star\) is unique; any compatible \(x^\star\) implements the continuous minimal-RPE mechanism. The corresponding incentive theorem to seek is that no measurable coalition of positive mass can change its reported type distribution so that every coalition member strictly improves.

I would expect this problem to be Class A in the finite-type, rational, quasi-linear version. Once named individuals disappear, the state has \(O(\tau m)\) mass variables and \(m\) prices. Priority feasibility and constrained improvements can be expressed through finite transportation or assignment systems, while price minimization invites an ascending-price or LP formulation. The paper does not prove this algorithmic claim; it supplies the equilibrium structure that makes the question meaningful.

The high-multiplicity bridge is exact for rational data: a finite instance with \(n_\theta\) applicants and \(s_h\) copies of house class \(h\) maps to
\[ \mu_\theta=\frac{n_\theta}{n},\qquad q_h=\frac{s_h}{n}. \]
Conversely, rational continuous solutions can be scaled to finite markets. Thus this is not merely replacing “agents” by a continuum; it is the high-multiplicity version of the same rent-controlled assignment problem.

The weakest point is substantial. The paper’s strict priority orders are over named individuals, whereas a genuine high-multiplicity model needs repeated priority classes and type-level tie handling. Also, the paper explicitly leaves the computation of minimal RPE outcomes and dynamic convergence open. Consequently, the proposed mirror is a new computational problem inspired by Theorems 1–3, not a complexity result already contained in the paper. If a named computational-complexity anchor is mandatory, the honest verdict is that this paper provides none.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that this paper has no computational anchor at all. Theorem 1 proves existence of a minimal RPE price vector, Theorem 2 proves its uniqueness on the NCBI domain, and Theorem 3 proves group non-manipulability. None gives an algorithm, a complexity classification, an encoded input model, or even a computational problem. Thus “Continuous Minimal-RPE Computation” is not the high-multiplicity relaxation of a computational result in the paper; it is a new computational problem inspired by an axiomatic mechanism-design paper.

The proposed high-multiplicity translation also loses the paper’s central structure. In the paper, each house is a single indivisible object and each \(\pi_h\) is a strict order of the named agents. A complete type must therefore include an agent’s priority rank. But under a strict priority order, two agents cannot have the same complete type: their ranks differ. If those ranks are retained, there is no genuine repeated-type regime. If they are discarded, the mass model forgets information that determines who receives a scarce house.

Keeping the number of houses fixed does not solve this. As the population grows, the finite stock of houses has vanishing mass, almost everyone receives the outside option, and the aggregate assignment and manipulation problem degenerates. To obtain nontrivial masses, one must clone houses into classes with per-capita capacities and replace strict priorities by priority classes or ties. That is a reasonable new many-to-one matching model, but it is not merely the population continuization of this paper. It changes the priority structure on which the RPE definition and the proofs rely.

This defeats the three named anchors separately. Theorem 1’s existence proof uses a finite set of individual assignments and named trading cycles; it supplies no existence theorem for fractional type-by-house assignments. Theorem 2’s uniqueness proof depends on those finite trading cycles and the paper’s individual-level CBI/NCBI condition; saying that an aggregate NCBI promise “suggests” uniqueness is not a transfer of the theorem. It is a new conjecture. Theorem 3 is more problematic still: group manipulation compares the priced assignments of the same named agents before and after a deviation. An aggregate matrix \(x_{\theta h}\) records masses, not which individuals moved, so it cannot express the theorem without adding a coupling or an identity-level priority coordinate. Adding that coordinate destroys finite type multiplicity; replacing it by anonymous priority classes changes the mechanism and its incentive claim.

The best repaired formulation—rational quasi-linear values, finitely many preference and priority classes, cloned house capacities, aggregate RPE conditions, and a measurable coalition notion—is certainly meaningful. It could even be worthwhile as a new continuous matching problem. But its value would come from the new model and whatever complexity theory it develops, not from a computational mirror of Andersson and Svensson’s results. Existing high-multiplicity matching work would support that model; it is not an objection. The objection is narrower and stronger: this paper supplies no named computational result, and its axiomatic results do not survive as the same objects once multiplicity is made genuine.

So the negative case is decisive if ChoCo requires the mirror to be anchored in a computational result of the paper. It is not an airtight universal claim against every related public-housing or labour-market continuization: the repaired model is a plausible research direction in its own right.
