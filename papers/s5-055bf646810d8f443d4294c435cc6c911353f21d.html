The continuous mirror question

Given a finite complete type set \(\Theta\), distribution \(\mu\), type-level disutilities \(v_\theta(q)\), and an online per-capita arrival stream of chore classes, determine whether there is a nonanticipating family of irrevocable policies for \(N\mu_\theta\) agents and \(N\)-scaled arrivals such that, at every normalized prefix, \(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{\sigma_N}\max_a v_{\theta(a)}(B_a)/\operatorname{MMS}^{(N)}_{\theta(a)}\le\alpha\); minimize \(\alpha\), with the \(k\)-value and \(D\) restrictions for Theorems 1 and 6 and without them for Theorem 5.

The model it lives in

Use \(N\mu_\theta\) clones of each complete type \(\theta\) and \(N\lambda_q\) copies of each arriving chore class \(q\). Allocate every copy irrevocably to an integral bundle, represent the limit by bundle-state mass flow, and evaluate policies by \(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{\sigma_N}\max_a v_{\theta(a)}(B_a)/\operatorname{MMS}^{(N)}_{\theta(a)}\), not by an almost-sure average.

The objection that survived

The proposed almost-sure bundle constraint discards \(o(N)\) exceptional agents, so the mirror must retain \(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\max_i\) rather than a type-level average.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the online competitive guarantees of Theorems 1 and 6 and the unrestricted boundary question corresponding to Theorem 5; it leaves the auxiliary Stacking game and the transfer of Theorem 5's specific adaptive construction unresolved.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the max-preserving sequence limit equal a measurable mass-flow formulation with a support-wise constraint rather than an almost-sure constraint?
  • For fixed \(\Theta\) and \(\mu\), is the unrestricted \(k,D\) boundary finite, and can Theorem 5's lower-bound mechanism be replaced by a symmetric type-level adversary?
  • Can the pressure and Stacking-game analysis yield the same \(O(k)\) and \(O(\log D)\) guarantees for the limiting mass flow?

The case FOR (proponent)

Yes. The strongest mirror is the paper’s online MMS problem in a genuine high-multiplicity workforce, with Theorem 1 as the lead anchor.

Let \(\Theta\) be a finite set of complete agent types and let \(\mu\in\Delta(\Theta)\) give the population mass of each type. A type \(\theta\) specifies the disutility \(v_\theta(q)\) of every possible chore class \(q\), including all eligibility and cost information relevant to allocation. Think of \(N\mu_\theta\) interchangeable workers of type \(\theta\), with \(N\gg|\Theta|\). Chores arrive online; a copy of \(q\) must be assigned irrevocably when it arrives.

The continuous object is not a divisible chore allocation. Each unit of population still receives an integral bundle \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{Q}\). What becomes fractional is the distribution of bundles: \(\pi_\theta(b)\) is the mass of type-\(\theta\) agents receiving bundle \(b\). Thus the average number of \(q\)-chores received by a type can be fractional even though every individual bundle is integral. Equivalently, this is the \(N\to\infty\) limit of \(N\mu_\theta\) agents and \(N\lambda_q\) copies of each chore class \(q\), where \(\lambda_q\) is per-capita supply.

For a prefix with per-capita supply vector \(\lambda\), define the continuous MMS benchmark for type \(\theta\) by

\[ \operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_\theta(\lambda) = \inf\left\{ z: \begin{array}{l} \exists \rho\in\Delta(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{Q})\text{ with } \mathbb E_\rho[b_q]=\lambda_q\ \forall q,\\ v_\theta\cdot b\le z\quad \rho\text{-almost surely} \end{array} \right\}. \]

This is exactly the high-multiplicity limit of the paper’s \(N\)-way MMS partition benchmark. A solution is a nonanticipating measurable mass-flow policy: after chore \(q\) arrives, it moves mass from bundle \(b\) to \(b+e_q\), without using future arrivals. Its objective is to minimize \(\alpha\) subject to

\[ v_\theta\cdot b \le \alpha\,\operatorname{MMS}^{\infty}_\theta(\lambda) \]

for almost every agent of every type, at every prefix. This is recognisably the paper’s problem: online irrevocable assignment, additive personalized disutilities, and MMS fairness remain unchanged; only repeated identical agent profiles and per-capita chore supplies have been made explicit.

The regime is plausible in, for example, a very large workforce of interchangeable employees with a small number of contractual or physical work-accommodation profiles. Millions of workers may share one of a few complete disutility profiles, while jobs arrive sequentially. Here \(|\Theta|=\tau\) and \(N\gg\tau\); the assumption is high multiplicity of agent types, not that workers are literally indistinguishable in every real-world respect.

My lead anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper. It states that Algorithm 1 guarantees a \(\min\{n,O(k),O(\log D)\}\)-MMS allocation for online indivisible chores, where \(k\) is the maximum number of distinct disutilities of any agent and \(D\) is the maximum ratio between that agent’s largest and smallest disutility. The theorem is not merely an encoding result: it supplies an online allocation algorithm and a competitive-ratio guarantee.

The corresponding continuous problem is Online-MMS\(_\infty(k,D)\): given \((\Theta,\mu)\), the type-level disutilities, and an online stream of per-capita chore supplies satisfying at most \(k\) distinct disutility levels per type and ratio at most \(D\), construct a nonanticipating integral-bundle mass-flow policy minimizing the worst type-level MMS ratio. I expect this to be Class A. The finite-\(n\) term disappears as \(N\to\infty\), leaving an \(O(\min\{k,\log D\})\) guarantee independent of population size. The paper’s pressure argument and its continuous Stacking game are especially good evidence: the Stacking game is not itself the population mirror—it continuizes the pressure state—but it is precisely the kind of uniform-in-\(n\) machinery one would use to prove this mirror.

A sharper separate mirror uses Theorem 6, also proved here. It states that when every agent has at most two distinct disutilities, there is an online algorithm guaranteeing a \(\min\{n,2+\sqrt3\}\)-MMS allocation. The continuous question is BiValue-Online-MMS\(_\infty\): under \(|\{v_\theta(q):q\in Q\}|\le2\) for every type, find an irrevocable integral-bundle mass-flow policy guaranteeing \(2+\sqrt3\)-MMS for every type at every prefix. I would expect the same Class A outcome, possibly with the constant surviving exactly in the limit. This is a particularly convincing scenario: two work-accommodation levels per worker type and a large repeated workforce are both natural, while the question remains exactly the paper’s online MMS question.

The strongest negative anchor is Theorem 5, proved in this paper. It states that for every \(n\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), no online algorithm can guarantee an \((n-\varepsilon)\)-MMS allocation in the \(n\)-agent online MMS problem for indivisible chores. Its continuous counterpart is Unrestricted-Online-MMS\(_\infty\): with no bound on \(k\) or \(D\), does there exist a single finite population-independent \(\alpha\) and a nonanticipating mass-flow policy guaranteeing \(\alpha\)-MMS for every finite type distribution and every admissible arrival stream?

I would expect this unrestricted mirror to expose a genuine boundary, probably requiring the ratio to depend on \(k\), \(D\), or the number of relevant types. That would be a continuum-specific hard regime rather than a straightforward transfer of Theorem 5. The paper’s adversary reacts to the allocation of named individual agents and repeatedly introduces new disutility scales; both mechanisms may weaken when each role is represented by a large mass of interchangeable agents. Thus Theorem 5 is a meaningful test of continuization, but not my strongest anchor: its finite-\(n\) lower bound does not automatically survive the high-multiplicity limit.

The weakest point is exactly this difference between individual and mass-level online decisions. In the original theorem, the adversary can exploit whether one particular agent received the previous chore. In the mirror, the policy can spread each arriving chore across a large population of identical agents, and some finite-population integrality effects disappear asymptotically. That is a real vulnerability, not a technicality. It does not undermine the positive case based on Theorems 1 and 6: their guarantees are already uniform in \(n\), their proof uses a continuous state abstraction, and the proposed mirror preserves indivisible bundles while making only the population high-multiplicity.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the proposed mirror smooths away the paper’s actual objective. Online MMS is an \(L^\infty\) guarantee: every named agent must remain within the target ratio. A population distribution records only positive-mass behaviour.

Take \(N\) identical agents and \(N\) unit chores. Their MMS is \(1\). An allocation in which one agent receives \(2\) chores, one receives none, and every other agent receives one has ratio \(2\). Yet its empirical bundle distribution is

\[ \pi_N = \left(1-\frac{2}{N}\right)\delta_1 + \frac{1}{N}\delta_0 + \frac{1}{N}\delta_2 \;\longrightarrow\; \delta_1. \]

The proposed continuous instance therefore reports exact MMS, although the finite instance violates it by a factor of \(2\). The same problem becomes worse when a single exceptional agent receives a very large chore. The exceptional mass disappears, while the paper’s objective is determined precisely by that agent.

This defeats the proposed mirrors for both Theorem 1 and Theorem 6. Algorithm 1 controls the maximum individual pressure \(H_i^\theta\), not the pressure of a typical agent. The Stacking game is a continuous representation of that auxiliary state, not a population limit: its usefulness comes from retaining the largest coordinate. A bundle distribution \(\pi_\theta\) with an almost-sure constraint loses vanishing exceptional sets. Replacing “almost surely” by an essential supremum does not help; an exceptional set still has measure zero. Requiring the bound pointwise on every continuum agent is not invariant under measure-preserving relabelling and brings back agent identity. Requiring \(\limsup_N\max_i\) instead produces a sequence of finite discrepancy problems, not a problem specified by the continuous society \(\mu\).

The two-value restriction does not repair this defect. The failure already occurs with one disutility value, so Theorem 6’s bi-valued workforce does not make the MMS objective measure-continuous.

There is also a scaling problem. If chores remain atomic and only the population becomes nonatomic, each countable collection of chores is assigned to a measure-zero set of agents, so almost every agent receives the empty bundle. To obtain a nontrivial \(\pi_\theta\), one must scale the number of chores with \(N\) and pass to per-capita arrival rates. At the macroscopic level, batches of chores can then be split across masses of agents. That is a reasonable fluid scheduling model, but it is no longer the paper’s online indivisible-item process at the scale where its guarantees are measured. The substantive relaxation is now fractionalization of the arrival/allocation process, not merely continuization of the population.

Theorem 5 is even less transferable. Its adversary defines \(d_i(j)\) according to whether a particular named agent received the previous item. Clones sharing one type cannot have that valuation history: after the first allocations they have different states, although their intrinsic disutility profiles are identical. Encoding those histories as types makes the number of types grow with the population and destroys high multiplicity. A symmetric, type-level adversary would be a new online fluid lower-bound problem, not a continuous version of Theorem 5. The theorem’s growing parameter \(n\) counts independently targetable identities; a fixed finite type space cannot represent that parameter.

The negative case is therefore strong against the proponent’s \(\pi\)-based mirrors: they either change “every agent” into “almost every agent,” or retain the max guarantee by carrying a discrete upper-tail state that defeats the claimed population-only limit.

It is not, however, an airtight universal impossibility. One can define a legitimate asymptotic problem by keeping finite \(N\) agents and \(N\)-scaled chore supplies, preserving the finite-\(N\) maximum-MMS objective, and studying

\[ \limsup_{N\to\infty} \inf_{\mathcal A} \sup_{\sigma_N} \max_i \frac{d_i(A_i)}{\operatorname{MMS}_i}. \]

That would be a sensible high-multiplicity online-load-balancing programme. If ChoCo accepts such sequence-level limits as continuous mirrors, Theorems 1 and 6 retain a viable anchor. Thus the honest conclusion is that the proposed formulation is not a faithful mirror, but the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists cannot be defended without excluding this stronger asymptotic formulation.
