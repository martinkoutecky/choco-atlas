The case FOR (proponent)

The paper has no qualifying computational anchor. Its numbered results are axiomatic or structural, not claims of membership in \(P\), NP-hardness, FPT, approximation, or similar. The strongest mathematical anchor is Theorem 4, proved here:

\(\mathrm{MPW}\) is the unique solution for finite TUX games satisfying efficiency, \(\sum_{i\in N}\varphi_i(w)=w(N,\emptyset)\), and balanced contributions, \(\varphi_i(w)-\varphi_i(w_{-j})=\varphi_j(w)-\varphi_j(w_{-i})\), using the paper’s restriction operator \(r^\star\). Theorem 8 and Theorem 9 give two further axiomatic characterizations of MPW, but neither is computational. Thus, strictly under the programme’s anchor rule, there is no eligible anchor and no fully compliant positive case.

The strongest substantive rescue would be a new problem based on Theorem 4, explicitly labelled unanchored:

Continuous-MPW Value Evaluation. Consider a market with many firms and finitely many complete firm types \(T=\{1,\ldots,\tau\}\). A type includes every feature relevant to coalition worth and externalities: technology, costs, demand parameters, and coalition behaviour. The society is a rational mass distribution \(\mu\in\Delta_\tau\), with \(\mu_t\) the fraction of firms of type \(t\). A coalition is represented by a type-mass vector \(x\leq\mu\). The outsiders are partitioned into blocks \(\rho=(B_1,B_2,\ldots)\), where each \(B_j\in\mathbb R^\tau_+\) and \(\sum_jB_j=\mu-x\). A scalable typed partition-function kernel \(W(x,\rho)\) gives the coalition’s aggregate worth.

For concreteness, \(W\) could be supplied by rational polynomials through \(W(x,\rho)=F(x,\sum_{B\in\rho}g_1(B),\ldots,\sum_{B\in\rho}g_k(B))\), with \(F\) and the \(g_\ell\) part of the input. This retains genuine externalities: worth depends on the distribution of outsider coalition sizes and types, not merely on \(x\).

For every replication factor \(q\) such that \(q\mu_t\) is integral, create \(q\mu_t\) labelled firms of type \(t\), and define the finite TUX game by \(w^{(q)}(S,\pi)=qW(x_S,\rho_\pi)\), where \(x_S\) and \(\rho_\pi\) are the normalized type masses induced by \(S\) and \(\pi\). Let \(u_t^{(q)}\) be the MPW payoff of a type-\(t\) firm. The continuous MPW payoff is \(u_t=\lim_{q\to\infty}u_t^{(q)}\), whenever this limit exists.

The computational problem is: given \((T,\mu,W,t,a,\varepsilon)\), output an \(\varepsilon\)-accurate value of \(u_t\), or decide, under a promised gap, whether \(u_t\geq a+\varepsilon\) or \(u_t\leq a-\varepsilon\). The decision variable is the type-level payoff vector \(u\), satisfying the continuum efficiency identity \(\sum_t\mu_tu_t=W(\mu,\emptyset)\).

This is recognisably the paper’s problem. It preserves the MPW average over externality-bearing partitions, the \(r^\star\) removal interpretation, and the Shapley marginal-contribution logic. Only the population is continuized: firms become mass, while coalition structure and payoffs remain the substantive objects. A natural regime is \(10^5\) firms in perhaps \(5\)–\(20\) technology types, not a seven-player committee.

For smooth kernels, the expected limit is a Class A object. If \(\overline W(x)\) denotes the limiting Ewens-average worth, then formally \(u_t=\int_0^1\partial_{x_t}\overline W(\lambda\mu)\,d\lambda\). Efficient computation of the relevant partition moments and this one-dimensional integral would give an algorithm. With unrestricted succinct externality kernels, hardness may instead survive; that would be a separate complexity result to prove.

The weakest point is decisive: this is a plausible high-multiplicity extension of the paper’s subject matter, but it is not a result anchored by the paper. Moreover, the continuum limit of the finite Ewens partition process and a finite representation for arbitrary typed partition functions require new modelling work. I would therefore present this as a worthwhile ChoCo research question, not as evidence that this paper itself contains a qualifying continuous-computational contribution.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is decisive at the programme’s eligibility gate: this paper contains no computational anchor. Theorem 4 is a uniqueness characterization of the MPW solution; Theorems 8 and 9 are two further axiomatic characterizations. None defines an input encoding, decision problem, algorithm, complexity class, approximation guarantee, or parameterized problem. Lemma 5 and Proposition 10 are structural commutation results. The discussion of implementation is motivation and citation, not a computational theorem. Thus a continuous computation built from this paper would be a new project, not a continuization of one of its results.

The proposed Continuous-MPW Value Evaluation does not repair that defect. The finite paper allows an arbitrary partition-function game \(w\) over all embedded coalitions and partitions. It gives no replication law connecting games on \(n\) players with games on \(qn\) players. To obtain the proposed limit, one must newly impose all of the following: anonymity within types, a scaling rule such as \(w^{(q)}=qW\), a parametric representation of \(W\), and a limiting probability law for partitions of a nonatomic typed population. Those assumptions define the research problem; they are not consequences of Theorem 4.

The obstruction is especially serious because externalities are the paper’s central object. In the finite model, \(r^\star\) averages over the finitely many ways a named player can join a named outsider block. In the limit, deleting one agent changes no mass at all, so the literal balanced-contributions condition collapses to \(0=0\). The same problem affects the consistency axioms: removing a measure-zero player leaves the society unchanged. Recovering a nontrivial object requires replacing deletion by a derivative or a vanishing-mass perturbation and proving convergence of the finite Ewens averages. That may produce an Aumann–Shapley-like line integral, but it is a new solution concept and a new limit theorem rather than the paper’s characterization continued.

Theorem 8 and Theorem 9 provide no stronger rescue. Their \(2\)-standardness conditions concern genuinely two-player games, while Sobolev and Hart–Mas-Colell consistency recursively remove finite players. One can invent type-level or infinitesimal versions, but the paper does not select a canonical one. Different choices of scaling, partition limit, or reduction rule produce different continuous values.

Nor does the suggested kernel \(W(x,\rho)\) come for free. If it is restricted to smooth polynomial moment kernels, the analyst has selected a narrow new model whose tractability is driven by that kernel representation. If it is allowed to encode arbitrary externalities, then a finite type distribution does not determine a finite computational instance without a substantial new representation theory. Either way, the central computational content lies outside the paper.

There is one genuine weakness in the universal negative claim: a Cournot-style model with many otherwise identical firms, per-capita demand, and anonymous coalition effects is a plausible high-multiplicity setting. A carefully specified typed partition kernel could yield a worthwhile ChoCo problem. I therefore would not claim that no continuous population model related to MPW could ever be interesting. The defensible verdict is narrower but strong: this paper offers no qualifying computational result to mirror, and its proposed continuous version is an unanchored new theory rather than a worthwhile continuization of the paper itself.
