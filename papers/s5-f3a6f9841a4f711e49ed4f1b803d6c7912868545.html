There is still a plausible *topic-level* mirror. Take a finite support of ideal-point types \(x_1,\ldots,x_\tau\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), with rational masses \(\mu_i>0\) summing to \(1\). Keep the policy space \(X=\mathbb{R}^2\). Say \(a\) beats \(b\) when the mass of types that prefer \(a\) to \(b\) exceeds \(1/2\); say \(a\) covers \(b\) when \(a\) beats \(b\) and also beats every point that \(b\) beats. The problem would be: given the types and masses, output an exact semialgebraic description of the uncovered set—the points not covered by any other point. Here type means a shared Euclidean ideal point, and mass is the fraction of the population with that ideal point.

A plausible regime is a large electorate with a modest number \(\tau\) of shared ideal-point types: mass records the size of each group without requiring one-person increments. That preserves Miller’s majority relation and uncovered-set question while making the population, rather than the policy space, continuous. It is a credible extension of the paper’s subject, but it is not a mirror of a named computational result in this paper.
