A plausible extension would study manipulation of a Matrix Vote in a large party membership or civic organization. Let \(A\) be the feasible joint outcomes—assignments of candidates to portfolios and options across issues—and let each type \(t\) be a complete preference ranking over \(A\). A society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over the finite set \(T\) of types. Given a fully specified Matrix Vote rule \(R\), a target outcome \(a^\star\), and per-unit costs \(\gamma(t,t')\), ask for a minimum-cost mass transfer \(x_{t,t'}\) of voters between ballot types that makes \(a^\star\) the unique outcome selected by \(R\). The plausible regime is many members and relatively few repeated preference types.

That is a concrete candidate for Matrix-Vote Mass-Bribery, but it is a proposed extension, not a mirror of a result in this paper. The review does not specify the voting rule in enough detail to fix \(R\), and it proves nothing about this manipulation problem. The best version of the proposal repairs its ballot model: the Matrix Vote uses a ranked list of candidates paired with posts, first selects officeholders through a Quota Borda count, then assigns them to posts using a Modified Borda count. A faithful mass-bribery problem could use valid ballots as types and ask for the cheapest transfer of ballot mass that changes a specified committee and assignment. A large party or association membership is a plausible high-multiplicity setting, so lack of repeated voters is not a convincing objection.
