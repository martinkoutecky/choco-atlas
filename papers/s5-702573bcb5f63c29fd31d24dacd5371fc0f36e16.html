The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I agree with the proponent that there is no eligible named computational result to mirror: the paper has no theorem, lemma, or proposition about an algorithm or complexity. Its main formal contribution is an economic mechanism claim: competitive trading of quotas can implement a constrained optimum under stated assumptions. The matching properties it invokes come from prior work. That is a strong reason against a mirror, but it does not by itself rule out a worthwhile computational question nearby.

The strongest nearby candidate is refugee assignment. Give each refugee a type consisting of their destination ranking, current asylum location, and any other attributes the model uses. Let \(\mu_t\) be the mass of type \(t\), and let \(x_{ti}\) be the mass assigned to destination \(i\). With quotas \(m_i\), assignment feasibility is described by constraints such as \(\sum_i x_{ti}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{ti}\le m_i\), with unacceptable destinations excluded. For fixed quotas, this is a transportation problem. If quotas are also chosen to minimize the countries’ costs \(c_i(m_i)\), the result is a finite-dimensional convex optimization problem when those costs are convex, as the paper assumes. The paper already treats the quota quantities as continuous, so this formulation adds a type distribution but appears to yield standard flow or convex-optimization machinery, not a new complexity landscape. With support size \(\tau\) and \(N\) destinations, the basic assignment model has \(O(\tau N)\) variables.

There is a harder-looking alternative: preserve the paper’s random serial dictatorship or top-trading-cycles assignment of indivisible visas, and ask for the aggregate outcome over a large population. But that brings back the individual priority order and indivisible assignments that the mass-flow model smooths away. In an atomless population, an individual has zero mass and cannot affect the outcome; modeling coordinated changes by a positive-mass group asks about coalition manipulation, a different incentive question. A type-level flow can retain feasibility and preferences, but it does not compute the outcome of the paper’s individual-level mechanism. Conversely, preserving that mechanism offers no evident high-multiplicity computational gain in the paper: the quota-cost optimization is over the finite set of countries, whose identities and cost functions matter and cannot plausibly be replaced by a representative continuum.

One could make refugee assignment computationally harder by adding family constraints, skill complementarities, or other coupled eligibility rules. Those could create real algorithmic questions, but they are absent from this paper’s model and are not needed to state its mechanism. On the paper as written, the population-continuum version either reduces to ordinary mass transportation plus convex costs, or changes the question to the computation and incentives of an indivisible-agent matching rule. The case against a worthwhile mirror is strongest on that mismatch and the apparent simplicity of the natural mass formulation. It is not airtight: richer matching constraints could make a valuable continuous computational problem, but that would be a substantive extension rather than a mirror of a result established here.
