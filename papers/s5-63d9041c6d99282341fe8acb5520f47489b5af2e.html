The case FOR (proponent)

This paper has no numbered complexity result that meets the required anchor test. Its numbered results concern axioms, integrity-constraint preservation, and query-answer preservation. For example, Proposition 5 (proved here) characterizes when quota rules lift functional dependencies; Lemma 13 (proved here) shows that union commutes with queries in the positive existential fragment. Neither classifies a computational problem as tractable or hard. The paper’s mentions of \(\Theta_2^p\)-complete distance-based aggregation and NP-complete conjunctive-query answering are background claims attributed to earlier work, not numbered results of this paper.

There is a plausible continuous-population model behind the paper. Fix a finite schema and shared finite domain. A type \(t\) is a complete database instance \(D_t\), and \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of sources submitting it. This fits, for example, a large network of data providers where many providers use the same extraction pipeline and submit identical instances, so the number of sources is much larger than the number \(\tau\) of distinct instances.

A natural follow-up problem would be: given the supported types and rational masses \(\mu_t\), plus an integrity constraint \(\phi\) satisfied by every \(D_t\), find an instance \(X\) satisfying \(\phi\) that minimizes
\[ \sum_t \mu_t \sum_{P} |D_t(P)\mathbin{\triangle}X(P)|. \]
This is the population version of the paper’s distance-based aggregation rule, with its suggested restriction to consistent outputs. I would expect hardness to transfer for suitable propositional-constraint restrictions, since discrete profiles embed by setting \(\mu_t=n_t/n\). That is a credible research question and a recognizable high-multiplicity version of the paper’s problem.

But it is not anchored by a qualifying result of this paper. Proposition 5 and the query-preservation lemmas cannot honestly stand in for the required computational anchor, and the cited complexity claims do not meet the stated numbering and provenance requirements. So the continuous mirror looks plausible; the paper supplies no named computational result on which to build the requested positive case.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s strongest concrete mirror is the consistent distance objective over a distribution of database types. The scenario is credible: if many sources submit identical instances, then \(\mu_t\) is a sensible population share. I would not reject it on high-multiplicity grounds. But it does not mirror a computational result in this paper. With \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), its objective is just the paper’s distance objective with repeated source records compressed:
\[ \sum_t \mu_t\, d(D_t,X)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i d(D_i,X). \]
The paper does introduce that aggregator, but its \(\Theta_2^p\)-completeness claim is attributed to prior work. The paper itself gives no numbered complexity result for it. Restricting the output to databases satisfying an integrity constraint makes a plausible follow-up problem; it does not supply the missing result to mirror.

The named results the proponent points to do not fill that gap. Proposition 5 is a characterization of when a quota rule preserves a functional dependency. Its population version replaces the quota \(q>n/2\) with a share threshold above \(1/2\); that is an axiomatic restatement, not a computational question. Lemma 13 is a semantic preservation result for unions and positive existential queries. Since union depends only on whether a type has positive mass, changing source counts to proportions leaves the claim unchanged. Neither result studies an algorithm or complexity landscape.

The paper’s other complexity mentions do not rescue the case: the hardness of distance-based aggregation and NP-completeness of conjunctive-query answering are background claims, while the paper’s own numbered contributions concern axioms, constraint preservation, and query answers. A continuum model might motivate new algorithmic questions about these procedures, but it cannot turn those contributions into computational results. Even Lemma 3, the paper’s named equivalence between distance minimization and majority in the unconstrained odd case, would yield only a direct weighted-majority analogue, not a complexity result.

So the negative case is strong on the required anchor: no scenario can make this paper’s stated results computational results. It is weaker as the broader claim that no worthwhile continuous research question exists. The proposed distance problem is coherent and could be worth studying as a high-multiplicity extension of earlier work. On the stated standard, though, it is not a continuous mirror of a qualifying result in this paper.
