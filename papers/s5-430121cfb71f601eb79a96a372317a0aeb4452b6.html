The continuous mirror question

For fixed \(J\), rational per-capita supply \(q\), valuation domain \(\mathcal V\), and support cap \(\tau\), determine the largest \(\alpha\) achievable by one family of deterministic mechanisms \((M_N)\). Each \(M_N\) receives \(N\) reports, allocates integral bundles subject to \(Nq_j\) copies of each item \(j\), and is dominant-strategy truthful on every profile in \(\mathcal V^N\). For every rational society \(\mu\) with support at most \(\tau\) and positive benchmark, require \(\liminf_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Rev}(M_N;\mu)/N \ge \alpha\,\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)\), along admissible \(N\) for which all \(N\mu_t\) and \(Nq_j\) are integers. Here \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)\) is the maximum of \(\sum_{t,S}y_{t,S}v_t(S)\) over \(y_{t,S}\ge0\), with \(\sum_S y_{t,S}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,S\ni j}y_{t,S}\le q_j\).

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity value-auction model with complete valuation types \(t\), mass \(\mu_t\), and per-capita item supply \(q\). The benchmark is the mass-allocation LP over integral bundles; in finite replicas, individuals still receive integral bundles and truthfulness remains an individual incentive condition.

The objection that survived

Bounded support at truthful profiles does not bound the variety of reports at other profiles, so the proposed mirror supplies no finite-dimensional incentive formulation or clear computational saving for searching over mechanisms.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Corollary 3’s deterministic truthful revenue-approximation bound under scaled per-capita supply. It leaves the other results, including the randomized guarantee of Theorem 9 and the restricted-market results, aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • For fixed \(J\), \(q\), and \(\tau\), is the optimal asymptotic truthful revenue ratio bounded away from zero as \(N\) grows?
  • How does the optimal ratio depend on \(m\) and \(\tau\), and can a mechanism family attain it with a finite or efficient representation?
  • Does randomization improve the asymptotic ratio in this bounded-support regime?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I see is the paper’s deterministic truthful revenue guarantee, in a large market where many bidders share a small number of complete valuation types.

My anchor is Corollary 3, proved in this paper: in a combinatorial market with \(n\) value-maximizing bidders and general valuations, the best worst-case revenue ratio achievable by a deterministic truthful mechanism is exactly \(1/n\). The paper’s construction achieves \(1/n\), and its impossibility argument rules out any better guarantee. This is a mechanism-design barrier, not an NP-hardness result.

A plausible setting is a large ad exchange serving many small advertisers or campaigns. Advertisers with the same campaign objective and budget cap have the same type: a complete valuation \(v:2^J\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\) over bundles of standardized inventory, with the budget already reflected in their willingness to pay. There might be hundreds of thousands of campaigns but only dozens or hundreds of such types. Let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of campaigns of type \(t\). To keep the market meaningful as the population grows, inventory scales with the number of campaigns; its per-capita supply is public. That supply co-modification is part of the mirror.

Here is a precise problem to put to a prover. Fix an item set \(J\), a type domain \(\mathcal V\) of monotone valuation functions, a per-capita supply vector \(q\in[0,1]^J\), and a society \(\mu\) with rational masses and support \(T\), where \(|T|\le\tau\). Its unconstrained revenue benchmark is

\[ \operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q)= \max_{y\ge0}\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\subseteq J}y_{t,S}v_t(S) \]

subject to

\[ \sum_{S\subseteq J}y_{t,S}=\mu_t \quad\text{for every }t,\qquad \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{S\ni j}y_{t,S}\le q_j \quad\text{for every }j\in J. \]

Here \(y_{t,S}\) is the mass of type \(t\) assigned the integral bundle \(S\). Fractional assignments arise only as averages across people of the same type.

Call the problem High-Multiplicity Truthful Value-Auction Revenue. For each \(N\) making \(N\mu_t\) and \(Nq_j\) integral, a mechanism \(M_N\) receives \(N\) individual reports and assigns each person an integral bundle, subject to inventory \(Nq_j\). It must be deterministic and dominant-strategy truthful for the paper’s value utility: a bidder with true type \(v\) gets utility \(v(S)\) when charged at most \(v(S)\), and \(-\infty\) otherwise. Ask for the largest \(\alpha\) for which one mechanism family, truthful on every report profile, guarantees

\[ \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Rev}(M_N;\mu)}{N} \ge \alpha\,\operatorname{OPT}(\mu,q) \]

for every society with support at most \(\tau\). A solution is the mechanism family together with its limiting type-level allocation and revenue guarantee. This keeps each person’s bundle integral and truthfulness individual, while the population and realized allocations are described by masses.

I would investigate Class A for fixed \(\tau\): the aggregate allocation has a finite type–bundle description, so continuous optimization may help where the paper’s bound depends on the number of bidders. But Corollary 3 does not settle this question. Its worst cases may require support growing with \(N\); whether bounded support leaves a positive guarantee is exactly the open point. Further questions are whether the optimal ratio stays positive as \(N\) grows, how it depends on \(m\) and \(\tau\), and whether randomization changes the answer.

The mirror covers Corollary 3’s deterministic truthful revenue-approximation problem. It does not treat randomized mechanisms as the source of continuity: only population mass is continuous; each campaign still receives an integral bundle.

The soft spot is the regime assumption. Real advertisers may have nearly all distinct valuations, making \(\tau\) grow with \(N\); then the proposed benefit could disappear. There is also a real limit issue: a unilateral report has zero mass in a literal continuum, so I retain the paper’s individual incentive condition in every finite replication and take the population limit only for allocations and revenue. That makes this a high-multiplicity mirror rather than a claim that nonatomic truthfulness is automatically meaningful.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

I would grant the proposed setting its strongest premise: a large ad market can have many advertisers sharing a small number of complete valuation types, with inventory scaling alongside the population. That makes the high multiplicity regime credible. Corollary 3 is also a genuine named result: the paper proves a \(1/n\) bound for deterministic truthful mechanisms and gives a mechanism attaining it. [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03821)

The weakness is in what the proposed continuous formulation captures. Its LP computes the best aggregate allocation when each type’s mass can be distributed among integral bundles. It does not encode truthfulness. Truthfulness constrains a mechanism’s outcomes and payments across different report profiles; the variables \(y_{t,S}\) describe only an allocation at one profile. The result’s obstacle is those incentive constraints, not the allocation LP or a hard pricing problem that continuous optimization could replace.

Taking the society literally to a nonatomic limit makes that gap sharper: one advertiser’s deviation changes no mass, so a mass-based mechanism cannot respond to it. Truthfulness then becomes vacuous. Treating a deviation as positive mass instead models a coalition of advertisers, which is a different incentive condition from the paper’s dominant-strategy one.

The proposed finite-replication limit preserves individual truthfulness, but it does so by retaining a sequence of finite auctions. Bounded support at the truthful profile does not bound the variety of reports at other profiles, and the candidate specifies no runtime or finite-dimensional representation for the mechanism search. The limit measures performance; it has not yet made the strategic problem smaller.

A restricted version could become a worthwhile question: fix a finite valuation domain, restrict reports to that domain, and ask for the asymptotic best truthful revenue ratio with per-capita supply. That would define a cleaner computational problem, though it would narrow the paper’s general-valuation setting and need its own complexity formulation.

So the strongest case against is that the proposed mirror has not shown how continuization helps with the paper’s central computational burden. That challenges the claim that this particular mass LP is already a ChoCo contribution. It does not establish that no useful continuous or high multiplicity mechanism-design question exists; the negative case is weak against that universal claim.
