The proponent names no anchor, and that is the strongest case against a continuous mirror. The paper’s propositions and theorems concern the existence, efficiency, and stability of equilibrium binding agreements in finite coalition-formation games. None gives an algorithm, analyzes complexity, or states a computational problem. There is no computational result here for continuization to mirror. A continuum-agent version would lead into equilibrium theory, while ChoCo’s target is computational analysis.

The strongest candidate is to group agents into payoff-relevant types, represent coalitions by type masses, and ask whether an efficient equilibrium binding agreement exists or can be computed. That could be a coherent new problem, but the paper does not specify it. Its framework assigns payoffs across coalition structures and studies deviations among them. A mass model would need new assumptions about payoff functions, allowed deviations, and how those objects are encoded. To make masses preserve the relevant information, types must capture agents’ payoff responses across the possible structures and deviations; in a general instance, that may leave little multiplicity to exploit. Enforcing symmetry or structured payoffs could create a useful computational problem, but it would be a new model rather than a translation of one of this paper’s results.

So no proposed anchor survives: the paper offers no computational result to continue. The limit of this negative case is that it cannot establish that a separately formulated continuum coalition-formation problem would be unworthy.
