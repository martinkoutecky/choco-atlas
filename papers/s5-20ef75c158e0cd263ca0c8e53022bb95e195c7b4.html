The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent is right that the paper states no complexity theorem. But “no eligible computational object” is too strong: its central contribution is the API-2 allocation framework in Equations (14)–(25), which the authors solve and compare with alternatives. A natural computational question is how to find its allocation when the decision-makers are represented by \(\tau\) types with rational masses, claims, disagreement floors, and negotiation weights.

The strongest case against that mirror is that the paper’s agents are jurisdictions, not interchangeable water users. A type would need to preserve each jurisdiction’s hydrological position, existing claims, economic indicators, ecological contributions, and bargaining role. Those features make the nine Yellow River jurisdictions distinct. Modeling citizens as the population would put mass at the wrong level: they are not the negotiating agents whose willingness to cooperate the framework measures.

A better scenario would use many irrigation districts or municipal utilities with repeated profiles. Yet districts with the same indicators can still occupy different places in the river network, face different delivery constraints, and have different coalition options. Including those facts can make the type support grow with the number of districts; suppressing them changes the allocation problem. Treating a mass of users as a bargaining bloc also requires a rule for the bloc’s negotiation weight and willingness to cooperate. The paper supplies no canonical way to derive those quantities from individual agents, and splitting or merging blocs changes the agent-level optimization.

This makes a strong objection to continuizing the paper’s actual provincial case. It does not establish that every plausible mirror fails. A basin with many standardized water-user associations that bargain directly could support a recognizable high-multiplicity version, and the API-2 allocation problem gives that version a computational anchor. So the honest negative case is weak on the universal claim: the paper has no stated complexity result, and its real agents resist aggregation, but a worthwhile mirror remains plausible.
