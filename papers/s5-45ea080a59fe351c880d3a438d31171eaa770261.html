The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\), rational population distribution \(\mu\), and strict preferences over acceptable partner types, decide whether there exists a mass matching with blocking-agent mass at most \(b\). Let \(x_{tu}\) denote mass paired between distinct types \(t,u\), \(x_{tt}\) mass of same-type pairs, and \(x_{t\bot}\) unmatched mass, subject to \(2x_{tt}+\sum_{u\ne t}x_{tu}+x_{t\bot}=\mu_t\). A positive assigned cell is blocking-exposed when there is a positive-mass pair \(u\!-\!w\) with \(u\succ_t v\) and \(t\succ_u w\); minimize the total mass in exposed cells.

The model it lives in

An anonymous high-multiplicity roommates model with type masses \(\mu\), type-level acceptability and preferences, fractional aggregate matching variables \(x\), and objective \(B(x)\), the mass of residents involved in a positive-mass blocking relation.

The objection that survived

The normalized finite objective need not converge canonically: a single exceptional agent can witness blocking for many agents, whereas the proposed model requires a positive-mass witnessing cohort; the bounded-list condition also changes from named neighbours to acceptable types.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Corollary 1's Min-Block-Agents Stable Roommates result, including minimizing or thresholding blocking-agent mass. It leaves the egalitarian and Min-Block-Pair results, their parameterized algorithms, and their specific reductions untouched.

Open questions for a prover

  • What is the complexity of deciding whether \(B(x)=0\) for fixed or variable \(\tau=|T|\)?
  • Does minimizing blocking-agent mass remain hard when every type accepts at most five partner types?
  • When can an optimal mass matching be rounded to a finite matching without substantially increasing \(B(x)\)?

The case FOR (proponent)

My strongest positive case is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s Min-Block-Agents Stable Roommates problem. I would use this as the lead mirror because “number of blocking agents” has a direct population-scale interpretation: the fraction of residents who are involved in at least one instability.

The anchor is Corollary 1, proved in this paper. It states that Min-Block-Agents Stable Roommates is NP-hard and W[1]-hard with respect to the number \(\eta\) of blocking agents, even when every preference list has length at most five and contains no ties. It also proves that, for preferences without ties, the problem can be solved in \(O(2^{\eta^2}n^{\eta+2})\) time. The problem asks whether there is a matching with at most \(\eta\) agents involved in blocking pairs.

The continuous regime is a large roommate-allocation programme with many interchangeable residents. A type \(t\) might specify a resident’s work schedule, cleanliness requirements, smoking status, language, accessibility needs, and preferences over roommate profiles. Two residents have the same type exactly when these data are identical for the purposes of matching. A realistic instance might have \(10^5\) residents but only \(\tau=20\) or \(100\) types, each with at most five acceptable partner types. The input is a rational distribution \(\mu=(\mu_t)_{t\in T}\), where \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of residents of type \(t\).

I would name the mirror \(\textsf{Min-Block-Agent-Mass-Roommates}_\infty\). Each type \(t\) has an acceptable set \(A_t\) and a strict order \(\succ_t\) over \(A_t\), with unmatched status \(\bot\) ranked below every acceptable partner. A solution is a mass matching \(x\): \(x_{tu}\) is the mass of type-\(t\) residents assigned to type \(u\), \(x_{tu}=x_{ut}\), and \(x_{t\bot}\) is unmatched mass. Thus \(x\) must satisfy \(x_{tu}=0\) for unacceptable pairs and \(\sum_{u\ne t}x_{tu}+x_{tt}+x_{t\bot}=\mu_t\).

For \(v\in T\cup\{\bot\}\), call the type-\(t\) mass assigned to \(v\) blocking-exposed if there are types \(u,w\) with \(x_{uw}>0\), \(u\succ_t v\), and \(t\succ_u w\). In words, some positive mass of type \(u\) would prefer a type-\(t\) partner to its current partner, while the relevant type-\(t\) residents would prefer \(u\) to their current partners. Define the blocking-agent mass by \(B(x)=\sum_{t,v}x_{tv}\mathbf{1}[\text{type-t\) mass assigned to \(v\) is blocking-exposed}]\). The problem is: given \((T,\mu,\succ)\) and rational \(b\), does there exist a mass matching \(x\) with \(B(x)\le b\)? Equivalently, minimize \(B(x)\).

This is a genuine population continuization, not merely fractionalizing an outcome. Every unit of mass still receives one integral roommate; only the aggregate assignment is fractional. If \(\mu_t=q_t/N\) and the entries of \(x\) are multiples of \(1/N\), this is precisely a homogeneous finite roommate instance viewed through its type counts, and \(B(x)\) is the normalized number \(\eta/N\) of blocking agents. The replacement of individual names by complete types is the high-multiplicity assumption itself.

The authors should recognize this as their problem. The decision variable is still a matching, stability is still defined by mutually improving pairs, and the objective is still the number of agents involved in blocking pairs. The only change is that a large cohort is represented by its mass. The restriction to at most five acceptable partners also survives naturally as a bound on each type’s acceptable partner types.

My forecast is that this is likely a Class C candidate: hard for continuum-specific reasons rather than by an immediate transfer of Corollary 1. The paper’s reduction uses odd cyclic gadgets and the indivisibility of named agents. When those roles become masses, an odd gadget may be half-matched, so the original parity obstruction can disappear. On the other hand, the condition defining whether a positive mass is blocking is support-sensitive and nonconvex: it is a union of transportation regions indexed by which type-to-type assignments have positive mass. The type-interaction graph may therefore retain genuine combinatorial hardness even after population multiplicity has been removed. Determining which effect wins is exactly the computational question the continuous mirror should expose.

This mirror generates several further problems: deciding whether \(B(x)=0\) is possible; minimizing the total mass of blocking pairs rather than blocking agents; parameterizing by \(\tau\), maximum list length, or the number of positive-support type pairs; and determining when an optimal mass matching can be rounded to a finite matching without increasing blocking-agent mass substantially.

The weakest point is real: the paper’s anchor is stated for named agents, no ties, and a discrete count \(\eta\), whereas the continuum necessarily makes copies of the same type interchangeable. Thus I would not claim that the paper’s W[1]-hardness transfers unchanged. The claim is narrower and more defensible: Corollary 1 identifies a well-defined matching problem whose high-multiplicity version is natural, precise, and computationally nontrivial. This covers that result emphatically; it does not claim to continuize every egalitarian or stable-marriage result in the paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proposed anchor fails as a direct mirror because Corollary 1 is about a labelled, sparse preference graph, not merely about a population distribution.

The phrase “preference list of length at most five” does not survive the proposed cloning. In the paper, it means that an agent finds at most five named agents acceptable. If a type \(u\) has positive mass \(\mu_u\), then a type-\(t\) agent who accepts \(u\) accepts \(\Theta(N\mu_u)\) named agents in an \(N\)-agent realization. Thus “at most five acceptable types” is not the paper’s restriction: it produces dense blocks of acceptable partners. To preserve the actual five-neighbour condition, every acceptable cohort must have \(O(1)\) members, forcing either vanishing masses or a number of types growing with \(N\). The cyclic gadgets in Corollary 1 therefore cannot be blown up into a bounded-type society.

There is a deeper identity problem. A roommate instance is a directed relation among named endpoints: blocking depends on whether the particular agent \(j\) prefers the particular agent \(i\) to its assigned partner. Type marginals and type-to-type assignment masses do not retain this joint incidence information. Two labelled preference graphs can have the same type masses and the same type-pair matching masses while inducing different sets of blocking agents. Recovering that information requires either retaining the labels, making the number of types essentially proportional to the population, or adding a preference kernel/graphon encoding the full relational structure. The latter is a new population-plus-network model, not a continuization of a society represented by \(\mu\).

The objective itself also has no canonical atomless limit. A single exceptional agent can be a blocking partner for a macroscopic cohort: \(N-1\) agents may each prefer that one agent to their current partners, while the exceptional agent prefers them to its own partner. Then almost the entire population is a blocking-agent set, although the witnessing type has mass \(1/N\) and disappears in the limit. The proposed \(B(x)\) repairs this by requiring a positive-mass witness, but that changes the quantifier from “there exists a blocking individual” to “there exists a blocking cohort.” It is a support-sensitive cohort-blocking notion, not the normalized limit of the paper’s \(\eta\).

The strongest rescue is an anonymous roommate model in which residents rank partner types, copies of a type are tied, and \(x\) distributes mass across type pairs. That is coherent and recognizably related to stable roommates. But it abandons the paper’s no-ties, bounded-list, named-neighbour structure and replaces it with a fractional matching problem over dense type blocks. It may be a worthwhile new problem, but its value would come from that re-modelling, not from continuizing Corollary 1; nonconvex support conditions alone do not establish a distinctive continuous-complexity question.

So the negative case is strong against the proposed anchor as a faithful mirror. It is not honestly a proof that no anonymous stable-roommates extension could ever be worthwhile. The proponent has found a plausible adjacent research problem, but should label it an extension or re-modelling rather than a direct high-multiplicity mirror of this paper’s result.
