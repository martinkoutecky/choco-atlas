The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest nearby result is Theorem 2, proved in this paper: a distance function on roommate matchings satisfies betweenness, anonymity, monotonicity, independence of irrelevant newcomers, and standardization if and only if, for some \(\sigma>0\),
\[ \delta_P(\mu,\tilde\mu)=\sigma\sum_{i\in N}\left|\operatorname{rank}(\mu(i),R_i)-\operatorname{rank}(\tilde\mu(i),R_i)\right|. \]
That is a characterization of scaled Borda distances. It is not a result about a computational problem’s complexity. Theorem 1 and the named lemmas and propositions are likewise structural or axiomatic; the paper gives no named result asserting that a problem is in P, hard, FPT, or otherwise computationally classified.

A large roommate market with many agents sharing preference types suggests a possible continuous extension: types could rank partner cohorts, \(\mu_t\) could represent each cohort’s population share, and matching outcomes could be fractional assignments of mass. One could then study an aggregate rank-displacement objective inspired by the theorem. But that changes the paper’s strict rankings over named individuals, and any resulting computational question would be new. Theorem 2 does not anchor its complexity or establish that the authors would recognize it as their problem.

So there are no eligible anchors here, and therefore no honest per-anchor continuous problem or tractability prediction to give. The closest positive angle is that the paper’s Borda-distance formula may inspire a separate high-multiplicity project; it does not provide a computational mirror of this paper’s results.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent names no eligible anchor. Theorem 2 characterizes the distance functions satisfying five axioms; it does not classify a computational problem. The paper states no complexity result, and its characterized quantity is already the sum
\[ \delta_P(\mu,\tilde\mu)=\sigma\sum_{i\in N}\left|\operatorname{rank}(\mu(i),R_i)-\operatorname{rank}(\tilde\mu(i),R_i)\right|, \]
which can be evaluated by scanning the agents. That is the decisive obstacle for a ChoCo mirror: there is no computational contribution in the theorem for a continuous population model to preserve, simplify, or make hard.

The strongest plausible reformulation would group agents into finitely many preference types over partner categories, assign rational masses to those types, and represent a population matching by symmetric flows between types. That is a sensible high-multiplicity regime; the objection cannot simply be that large markets lack repeated types or that mass matchings are illegitimate. But a distance between two aggregate matchings also needs to say which agents of each type receive which partners across the two outcomes. Their separate type-to-type flow matrices give only marginal distributions. Supplying the cross-outcome pairing as part of the input makes the distance a direct integral. Minimizing over pairings gives a typewise Wasserstein distance, a plausible new anonymous metric that forgets named-agent identity. Either choice changes the object being measured; neither is characterized by Theorem 2. Retaining rankings over named partners instead makes types depend on the identities in each market.

The paper’s motivating applications suggest a more substantial follow-up: find a stable matching closest to a reference outcome, or a median stable matching, in a mass-based roommate market. Those could become computational questions, but the paper supplies neither their algorithms nor their complexity. They require a continuous stability model and a choice of how outcomes are coupled or compared. The resulting problem would draw its computational content from those additions, not from the paper’s characterization.

So the negative case is strong for this paper as a computational mirror: its nearest continuous versions are either direct distance evaluation, an axiomatic extension, or a newly added stable-matching optimization problem. The universal claim that no scenario could yield worthwhile related research is weaker. A good follow-up may exist; the supplied paper simply gives no computational result to anchor it.
