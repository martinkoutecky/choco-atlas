The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative is that this paper contains no named computational result to mirror. Theorem 10 is the closest candidate, but it characterizes which axioms on preferences yield a Choquet integral with a 2-additive capacity. It gives no computational problem, algorithm, or complexity bound. The same is true of the other theorems. The introduction’s observation that a general capacity has \(2^n\) coefficients motivates restricting the model; it is not a complexity result.

The most favorable population reading is to treat \(X\) as welfare recipients and extend income profiles and capacities to a continuum. That could lead to a worthwhile axiomatic or analytic question. But the paper’s results ask which preference axioms characterize a representation; they do not ask how to compute, optimize, or manipulate one. The cited infinite-domain result of Schmeidler is relevant background for such an axiomatic extension, not an existing answer to a computational question. Turning the model into optimization or learning would require adding a new task and its input model. Even a carefully designed mass-level version of A9 would not create a computational result in this paper to mirror.

So the proponent raised no surviving computational anchor: Theorem 10 does not qualify. This is a strong case against a *continuous computational mirror of this paper*. The broader claim that no scenario could ever yield worthwhile research is weaker: one could build a new computational programme around Choquet welfare models, but it would be a new programme rather than a mirror of the results here.
