The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case starts at the paper’s main weakness as a mirror candidate: it proves no computational result. Theorem 3 gives conditions for an efficient ex post equilibrium; Theorem 4 gives a conditional necessity result. Theorems 1 and 2 give comparative statics for factor prices. None defines an algorithmic problem or studies its complexity. The distribution \(\mu\) in the auction section is uncertainty over signals of a finite set of named bidders, not a distribution of population mass over types.

The strongest rescue of Theorem 3 would put many bidders into a few classes with shared valuation structure and ask whether an efficient equilibrium can be computed. That creates a basic modeling problem: with one indivisible object, a bidder in a continuum has zero mass, while the theorem’s efficiency criterion selects a highest-valued individual. One can give the population positive per-capita supply or make the prize divisible, but then the allocation, payoffs, and auction become a different game. The paper’s conditions and exit strategies are built around finite sets of bidders, winners, and auction histories. Even if that new game were worthwhile, the paper supplies no computational question or input representation for it. Adding those would start a separate algorithmic project.

The trade theorems offer no stronger anchor. Duplicating sectors or factor owners does not enter their zero-profit equations, so multiplicity has no natural role in the comparative statics. Replacing sectors with a distribution of cost functions could produce a continuous comparative-statics model, but that changes the sector space and still supplies no complexity problem. Theorem 4 remains an equilibrium-existence result, with \(\mu\) over finite-bidder signal profiles.

A computational study of a carefully specified nonatomic auction might be sensible in its own right. The weakness in the negative case is that one cannot rule out that future model; the paper gives little reason to treat it as a continuous mirror of these results.
