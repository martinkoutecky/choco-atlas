The continuous mirror question

Given finite buyer types \(\Theta\), seller types \((q,\sigma)\), positive rational masses \(\beta_\theta\) and \(\eta_{q,\sigma}\) normalized to one on each side, and rational valuations \(v_\theta\), costs \(c_\sigma\), and public qualities \(q\), choose matched mass \(y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\ge 0\) and per-agent transfers \(p^B_\theta,p^S_{q,\sigma}\). Require \(\sum_{q,\sigma}y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\le\beta_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\le\eta_{q,\sigma}\). Define \(a_\theta=\sum_{q,\sigma}q y_{\theta,q,\sigma}/\beta_\theta\) and \(b_{q,\sigma}=\sum_\theta y_{\theta,q,\sigma}/\eta_{q,\sigma}\). Impose buyer and seller incentive compatibility across reports, individual rationality, and mass-weighted budget balance. Find a feasible mechanism maximizing \(\sum_{\theta,q,\sigma}q(v_\theta-c_\sigma)y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\). A prover should also establish whether these truthful-mass variables admit a feasible extension to other aggregate report distributions.

The model it lives in

A two-sided atomless market with finite valuation, cost, and public-quality categories and rational type masses. The variable \(y\) records matched population mass, while per-type transfers define an incentive-compatible, individually rational menu. Each agent still receives either one whole service or none; only type-level mass and averages are fractional. The objective is quality-weighted welfare under ex-ante budget balance.

The objection that survived

The LP specifies allocations and transfers at the truthful type masses but does not define feasible outcomes for every other aggregate report distribution, so it has not yet been shown equivalent to a full direct mechanism.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the implementability constraints and welfare-maximizing allocation problem used in Equation (21), over a finite-support atomless market. It leaves the paper’s numerical efficiency estimates, sales probabilities, expected revenues, and their simulation procedure untouched.

Open questions for a prover

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I believe is a continuum version of the paper’s second-best buyer–seller matching problem. It continuizes the population: the outcome remains an ordinary match for each agent, while a variable records how much mass of each buyer type is matched to each seller type.

The anchor is Lemma 1, proved in this paper. It characterizes when a matching rule \(x_{ij}(\theta,\sigma)\) can be implemented with transfers so that the mechanism is Bayesian incentive compatible and individually rational. The buyer’s interim matching probability must be nondecreasing in their valuation, and the seller’s must be nonincreasing in their cost. In addition, the sum of buyers’ utilities at the lowest valuation and sellers’ utilities at the highest cost must equal the expected virtual surplus and be nonnegative:
\[ \sum_i U_B(\underline{\theta})+\sum_j U_S(\overline{\sigma}) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_i\sum_j q_j\bigl(\psi_B(\theta_i)-\psi_S(\sigma_j)\bigr)x_{ij}(\theta,\sigma)\right]\ge 0, \]
where \(\psi_B(\theta)=v(\theta)-\frac{1-F(\theta)}{f(\theta)}v'(\theta)\) and \(\psi_S(\sigma)=c(\sigma)+\frac{H(\sigma)}{h(\sigma)}c'(\sigma)\). The paper uses this characterization to formulate and compute its welfare-maximizing matching problem in Equation (21), using a mixed-integer solver and a numerical search. It does not give a named P-versus-NP-hardness result; Lemma 1 is the formulation-and-implementability anchor.

A plausible high-multiplicity regime is a large regional EV-charging platform pooling many sessions and charging slots over a standardized service window. A buyer type is a charging request with a particular valuation; a seller type is a slot with publicly observed power quality \(q\) and a particular private cost. Think tens or hundreds of thousands of sessions and slots, but tens or perhaps low hundreds of distinct valuation, quality, and cost types. Location or time differences that affect compatibility belong in the type. These are complete types: agents grouped together must be interchangeable for every preference, cost, and matching condition the model uses.

I would hand a prover Atomless Second-Best Service Matching. Its input is finite buyer types \(\Theta\), seller types \((q,\sigma)\), positive binary-rational masses \(\beta_\theta\) and \(\eta_{q,\sigma}\) on each side, and rational valuations \(v_\theta\), costs \(c_\sigma\), and public qualities \(q\). Normalize the total mass on each side to one. Its solution is a matching mass \(y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\ge 0\) and expected per-agent transfers \(p^B_\theta,p^S_{q,\sigma}\). Feasibility requires \(\sum_{q,\sigma}y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\le\beta_\theta\) and \(\sum_\theta y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\le\eta_{q,\sigma}\). Thus \(y\) measures the mass of matched pairs; fractional mass does not mean an individual receives a fractional service.

Let \(a_\theta=\sum_{q,\sigma}q\,y_{\theta,q,\sigma}/\beta_\theta\) be a buyer type’s quality-weighted match rate, and \(b_{q,\sigma}=\sum_\theta y_{\theta,q,\sigma}/\eta_{q,\sigma}\) a seller type’s service rate. Require buyer truthfulness, \(v_\theta a_\theta-p^B_\theta\ge v_\theta a_r-p^B_r\) for every buyer type \(\theta\) and report \(r\); and seller truthfulness, \(p^S_{q,\sigma}-q c_\sigma b_{q,\sigma}\ge p^S_{q,\sigma'}-q c_\sigma b_{q,\sigma'}\) for every cost type \(\sigma\) and report \(\sigma'\) at the same publicly observed quality \(q\). Require these truthful utilities to be nonnegative, and require mass-weighted transfers to balance: \(\sum_\theta\beta_\theta p^B_\theta=\sum_{q,\sigma}\eta_{q,\sigma}p^S_{q,\sigma}\). The question is to find a feasible mechanism maximizing \(\sum_{\theta,q,\sigma}q(v_\theta-c_\sigma)y_{\theta,q,\sigma}\).

I expect this mirror to be Class A: with finite rational type supports, the specified problem is a linear program whose size is polynomial in the numbers of buyer and seller types and the input bit length. That is a claim about this mirror, not a complexity theorem proved by the paper. It keeps the paper’s central question—how to maximize welfare while retaining incentives, participation, and budget balance—and its seller-quality effect. The paper’s simulations motivate the efficiency question, but this mirror does not reproduce its numerical estimates of efficiency, sales probabilities, or revenues.

The main follow-up questions are whether the continuum optimum is fully efficient under the paper’s assumptions, how quickly finite-market mechanisms approach it, and how to extend the finite-support computation to genuinely continuous valuation and cost distributions.

The soft spot is the move from a finite random profile, where an agent’s report can affect an integer matching, to fixed aggregate masses, where one agent’s deviation has zero effect on the population. The paper studies small finite markets as well as larger-market efficiency; it does not itself establish this atomless model. Still, the mirror preserves the same two-sided private information, quality-weighted welfare, incentive constraints, and balanced transfers. That makes it a credible large-market continuation of the authors’ problem rather than a change to fractional outcomes or a purely analytic question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Lemma 1 is the only named result the proponent anchors on, and it is an implementability characterization, not a complexity theorem. The paper’s computational object is closer to Equation (21): a matching rule contingent on the finite agents’ realized private information, with feasibility at each realization and an expected-welfare objective. The proposed LP replaces that with fixed masses of private types and per-type matching rates. Its incentive constraints make each agent a price taker: a unilateral report does not change the population composition. That is a meaningful change to the mechanism-design model, not merely a change from counts to proportions.

This objection defeats the anchor as a claim about what Lemma 1 establishes, but it does not defeat the better mirror. A large service market could have many interchangeable buyers in a few valuation categories and sellers in a few cost and public-quality categories. Those are complete types, and fractional matching mass can represent a population in which every individual still receives either a service or none. An atomless, incentive-compatible, budget-balanced matching problem over those types is a credible high-multiplicity continuation of the paper’s central question. The paper’s own interest in market-size effects makes that scenario especially natural.

The genuine weakness is narrower: the proposed LP is not yet a full mechanism specification. It gives allocations and transfers at the truthful type masses, but does not define feasible outcomes for every possible report distribution. That can likely be repaired by specifying how the mechanism handles other aggregate reports and proving that the type-level rates remain implementable. In the paper’s continuous valuation and cost setting, there is another limitation: exact values are almost surely distinct, so grouping them into a few positive-mass types would be an approximation unless the market actually has discrete categories. Both limitations leave plausible scenarios open.

So the strongest honest case against is that the proponent has not shown that their LP mirrors the paper’s finite, profile-contingent mechanism. I cannot honestly turn that into the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists: a discrete-type, high-multiplicity market appears to preserve the authors’ problem well enough that this anchor survives.
