The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no named computational result here to mirror. The paper proposes MNLDP, proves convergence of its propagation recurrence, and reports accuracy experiments. It does not classify the complexity of label integration or any other computational problem. Under the requested result-by-result standard, there is no anchor; the convergence analysis is not a complexity result.

The strongest unanchored candidate is to ask for the complexity of propagating labels through a continuous population of tasks. But the paper’s object is a finite feature graph: each instance has its own feature vector and noisy-label distribution, and its output is a label for that particular instance. The propagation depends on local neighborhoods built from those feature vectors. Collapsing repeated task types into masses can change those neighborhoods and the resulting outputs. To retain the original structure, a type would need to preserve enough of each task’s position in the graph that the proposed high-multiplicity compression loses its point.

One could instead define a measure over feature space and replace the \(k\)-nearest-neighbor graph with a continuous kernel or neighborhood operator. That may be a sensible continuum limit for graph-based learning, but it changes the central object into a field of labels over feature space. The paper supplies no optimization or complexity claim for that object, and the resulting question belongs to continuous learning and graph limits rather than a computational social-choice result this paper establishes.

So the negative case is strongest on scope and anchoring, not on impossibility: one can imagine useful continuous models of crowdsourced label propagation. What is missing is a paper result that makes a specific continuous computational-social-choice mirror worth pursuing. The proposed task-population mirror does not supply that missing anchor.
