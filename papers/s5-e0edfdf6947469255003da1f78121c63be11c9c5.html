The continuous mirror question

Given a finite agenda Φ, finitely many source types \(t\) carrying complete rational likelihood vectors \(a_t(\varphi)\), binary-rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a mass quota \(\lambda\in(0,1]\), and crispifying thresholds \(c_\varphi\), compute \(Q^\mu_\lambda(\varphi)=\max\{a\in[0,1]:\sum_{t:a_t(\varphi)\ge a}\mu_t\ge\lambda\}\). Does crispifying \(Q^\mu_\lambda\) yield exactly the same judgments as applying quota \(\lambda\) to the typewise crispified vectors—that is, is \(\varphi\) selected exactly when \(\sum_{t:a_t(\varphi)\ge c_\varphi}\mu_t\ge\lambda\) for every \(\varphi\in\Phi^\cup\)?

The model it lives in

Represent the society of information sources by a finite-support distribution \(\mu\) over types, each type being a complete rational likelihood-judgment vector. Use source mass in place of source counts and a fraction \(\lambda\) in place of integer quota \(q\); preserve the paper’s issuewise crispification, including that the resulting set may be inconsistent with Γ.

The objection that survived

The paper uses an integer quota over a finite source count, so the mirror must replace it with a quota fraction of total mass; the paper neither states nor studies that weighted rule.

fatal: False

The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make an anchor-based case for a continuous mirror: the paper has no numbered result asserting a computational complexity classification such as NP-hardness or polynomial-time solvability.

The closest computationally relevant result is Proposition 3.1, proved here: for every likelihood profile, crispifying vector, and quota \(q\), aggregating by the likelihood quota rule and then crispifying gives the same result as first crispifying each source’s judgments and then applying the classical uniform quota rule. That identity connects two computations, but it does not classify their complexity. The paper points to complexity results for classical aggregators in references [24] and [15]; those are not numbered complexity results of this paper. Theorem 4.1 is an impossibility characterization, not a computational-complexity result.

A population of information sources could plausibly be continuized, with source types given by complete likelihood-judgment vectors and mass representing their share. But without a qualifying named computational result, that would not meet the requested anchor requirement, so I’m not presenting it as the paper’s continuous mirror.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent supplies no anchor to defeat: they explicitly identify no named computational result in the paper. Theorem 4.1 is an axiomatic impossibility characterization. Proposition 3.1 is an identity between two ways of computing an aggregation rule, not a complexity result. The complexity results cited in its proof belong to other papers.

The strongest repair is to model sources as types carrying complete likelihood-judgment vectors, with society represented by masses over those types. The quota rules then become weighted quantiles followed by crispification; the median-likelihood rule becomes an optimization of \(\sum_t \mu_t\sum_{\varphi\in J}a_t(\varphi)\) over rational judgment sets \(J\). Those are coherent population-continuous formulations. But the paper does not analyze their computational complexity. Studying them would introduce new computational questions; it would not mirror a computational result the authors established here. For the quota rule, Proposition 3.1 instead reduces the computation to crispifying sources and applying the corresponding classical rule.

So the negative case is strong against calling this paper a source of continuous mirrors for ChoCo’s stated purpose. It is weaker as an absolute claim: computing the direct aggregators over a source population could be a worthwhile new project. That possibility does not supply an anchor from this paper, and it requires broadening the task from mirroring named computational results to proposing complexity questions around its aggregation rules.
