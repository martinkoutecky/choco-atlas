The continuous mirror question

Given a finite signal-type set \(T\) and an explicitly listed finite family \(\mathcal Q\) of rational C.I.I.D. models, each specifying \(\pi=\Pr[W=1]\) and conditional type distributions \(\lambda^0,\lambda^1\), consider the continuum limit in which \(W=w\) induces type masses \(\lambda_t^w\). The aggregator observes only the resulting mass distribution over truthful report pairs \((f_Q(t),p_Q(t))\), where \(p_Q(t)\) is the expectation of an independent peer's forecast conditional on type \(t\). Compute a rule \(A\) minimizing the worst-case expected squared error against the posterior given the full signal-type mass profile, over \(Q\in\mathcal Q\), and determine the exact minimax regret and its complexity in \(|T|\), \(|\mathcal Q|\), and input bit length.

The model it lives in

A continuum of forecasters with signal types \(t\in T\), carrying conditional mass \(\lambda_t^w\) when \(W=w\). The observable is the induced mass distribution over report pairs; the decision is a forecast \(A\) from that distribution to \([0,1]\); the objective is worst-case expected squared error relative to the posterior based on the full type-mass profile.

The objection that survived

The conditional type masses are induced by the model and hidden event rather than supplied as an exogenous society, and optimizing over a model family is a new task beyond the proposition's pointwise convergence guarantee.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

Covers the many-C.I.I.D. asymptotic aggregation result in Proposition 3.6.0.0.2; leaves the two-forecaster regret bounds, refinement-ordered result, and higher-expectation results outside the mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • For an explicitly listed finite \(\mathcal Q\), what are the exact minimax regret and optimal rule \(A\), and what is their bit complexity in \(|T|\), \(|\mathcal Q|\), and \(L\)?
  • Which report-mass profiles identify \(W\) in the continuum limit, and when does the optimal rule attain zero regret?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I can make is the paper’s many-C.I.I.D. regime, where identities already cease to matter and the number of forecasters grows. The natural continuous object is the population’s distribution over signal types—and the corresponding distribution over the reports those types produce.

My anchor is Proposition 3.6.0.0.2, proved in this paper (with its aggregator defined in Definition 3.6.0.0.1). For \(n\) forecasters whose signals are conditionally independent and identically distributed given the binary event \(W\), each reports a forecast \(f_i\) and an expectation \(p_i\) for the average of the other forecasters’ forecasts. The paper defines an aggregator that estimates the prior \(\mu=\Pr[W=1]\) by
\[ \widehat\mu= \begin{cases} \dfrac{f_jp_i-f_ip_j}{f_j-f_i-p_j+p_i},&\text{if some }f_i\ne f_j,\\[6pt] p_1,&\text{otherwise,} \end{cases} \]
and outputs
\[ a_{\mathrm{ciid}}^n(\{f_i,p_i\})= \frac{(1-\widehat\mu)^{n-1}\prod_i f_i} {(1-\widehat\mu)^{n-1}\prod_i f_i+\widehat\mu^{n-1}\prod_i(1-f_i)}. \]
The proposition states that for every fixed admissible information structure \(Q\), its regret relative to the optimal forecast given all the forecasters’ signals tends to zero as \(n\to\infty\). The paper notes related infinite-forecaster work by Chen et al.; its stated contribution here is the finite-\(n\) regret convergence. This is an algorithmic performance result, not a theorem placing a problem in P or establishing a hardness class.

Here is the continuous problem I would hand to a prover: Continuum C.I.I.D. Peer Aggregation. An instance consists of a finite signal-type set \(T\), with \(|T|=\tau\), and a rationally described family \(\mathcal Q\) of common-prior models. A model \(Q\) specifies a prior \(\pi=\Pr[W=1]\) and conditional signal distributions \(\lambda^0,\lambda^1\) on \(T\); forecasters’ signals are i.i.d. conditional on \(W\). The actual \(Q\) is unknown to the aggregator, which knows \(\mathcal Q\).

For each \(Q\), type \(t\) has its truthful forecast \(f_Q(t)=\Pr_Q[W=1\mid S=t]\) and peer expectation \(p_Q(t)=\mathbb E_Q[f_Q(S')\mid S=t]\), where \(S'\) is an independent peer’s signal. Conditional on \(W=w\), a unit-mass continuum of forecasters has type masses \(\lambda_t^w\). The aggregator observes the resulting report-mass distribution
\[ \nu_Q^w=\sum_{t\in T}\lambda_t^w\,\delta_{(f_Q(t),p_Q(t))} \]
and outputs a forecast \(A(\nu_Q^w)\in[0,1]\). The benchmark is the posterior \(q_Q^*(\lambda^w)=\Pr_Q[W=1\mid\text{the full signal-type mass profile }\lambda^w]\), corresponding to the paper’s optimal forecast given all signals. The task is to compute a rule \(A\) minimizing
\[ \sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\left[ (1-\pi)\bigl(A(\nu_Q^0)-q_Q^*(\lambda^0)\bigr)^2 +\pi\bigl(A(\nu_Q^1)-q_Q^*(\lambda^1)\bigr)^2 \right]. \]
A solution is a finite description of an optimal rule and its exact worst-case regret, with running time and representation size analyzed in \(\tau\) and the input bit length \(L\).

This is a credible population regime: there may be many forecasters but only a moderate number of distinct signal types, and agents of a type share the same signal-contingent reports. Their mass is the fraction of the population in that type. The paper itself studies the many-forecaster setting, so this is not a story imposed on an unrelated result. The peer expectation for an average is also compatible with the type-level model: by linearity and conditional symmetry, a type’s expectation of the average of its peers’ forecasts equals its expectation of one peer’s forecast. The mirror changes the finite report vector into a mass profile and benchmarks against the full type-mass profile, while retaining the same event, reports, and squared-error comparison.

I expect a tractable answer in this restricted C.I.I.D. setting: Proposition 3.6.0.0.2 gives a finite-population algorithm whose regret vanishes for each fixed \(Q\), and exact type masses should make the limiting inference problem more accessible. But the proposition does not prove this minimax classification. Its convergence is pointwise in \(Q\), so it does not establish uniform convergence over \(\mathcal Q\), nor a finite representation or running time for an optimal continuum rule. Those are central questions for the prover. One further question is whether an analogous result holds without conditional identical distribution.

The scope is only the paper’s many-C.I.I.D. asymptotic aggregation result. I would not use it to claim a mirror of the two-forecaster regret bounds, the refinement-ordered result, or the higher-expectation hierarchy. Nor does the anchor establish that discrete hardness disappears: the paper supplies no named P/NP/FPT classification or hardness result.

The weakest point is that this paper already takes \(n\to\infty\) and proves vanishing regret for each fixed information structure. That makes the continuous regime especially plausible, but narrows what the mirror adds: it must ask and answer a computational question about the exact mass-profile problem, not present the paper’s asymptotic accuracy result as new.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that Proposition 3.6.0.0.2 is an accuracy result, not a computational result. It shows that a specified aggregator’s regret vanishes as the number of c.i.i.d. forecasters grows, for each fixed information structure. It gives no complexity classification or algorithmic task over encoded instances. The proposed “Continuum C.I.I.D. Peer Aggregation” therefore does not mirror that proposition: it adds a new task—computing an optimal rule and exact worst-case regret over a model family. The paper supplies no canonical encoding for that task or for the unrestricted rule \(A\).

There is a genuine high-multiplicity story here: many forecasters can share a signal type, so lack of repeatable types is not a sound objection. The harder issue is what those masses represent. For fixed \(Q\), \(\lambda^w_t=\Pr_Q[S=t\mid W=w]\) is a conditional signal distribution, not a society’s supplied type-mass vector. In the continuum limit, the realized profile is one of two counterfactual profiles, selected by the hidden event \(W\); the aggregator observes only its report-space image. That is a robust inference problem about \(Q\) and \(W\), rather than a computational social-choice problem whose instance is a known society distribution. If one instead supplies a fixed ex ante mass vector over forecaster types, one has changed the signal-generation model and no longer mirrors the paper’s c.i.i.d. result.

The strongest repaired mirror—finite \(T\), a precisely encoded family \(\mathcal Q\), and a specified representation for \(A\)—can be a well-posed minimax optimization problem. But its computational content depends on choices the paper does not make. With a finite list of candidate models, only finitely many report profiles arise, and choosing outputs for them gives a finite convex minimax problem. With a broader model family, the complexity comes from how that family and the rule class are encoded. In neither case does the paper identify a complexity landscape driven by replacing a large population with its type masses. The paper’s output is also a forecast of a binary event, rather than a collective choice; its references to voter expectations do not make that the problem studied here.

So this anchor fails as a ChoCo computational mirror, though the many-forecaster regime is plausible and the resulting robust-inference problem could be worthwhile under a broader remit. The honest weakness in the negative case is that this is a boundary judgment, not a proof that no useful continuous formulation exists.
