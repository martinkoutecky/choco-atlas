Section 4 describes the greedy CLeRa learning algorithm, and Section 5 reports ranking experiments, but neither is a named complexity result of the kind required here.

A distributional version of learning a CLPT might be a sensible new question, but it would not mirror a named result from this paper. So there is no anchor for which I can honestly formulate the requested continuous problem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There are no anchors to defeat: the case for names none. Its proposed distributional version of CLPT learning is the strongest candidate, but it would be a new problem, not a continuous mirror of a result in this paper.

The paper’s computational contribution is CLeRa, a greedy heuristic for learning from finite pairwise comparisons, evaluated on benchmark data. It gives no complexity theorem or result about exact, approximate, or parameterized computation. A population version could replace the training set with a rational distribution over feature-label examples and optimize expected ranking performance. That is a sensible statistical-learning question, but its mass is over examples, not agents in a society. It therefore continuizes the data, rather than the population the ChoCo programme studies.

One could instead posit a large population of annotators and let each annotator’s type specify their complete judgments over object pairs. But that changes the paper’s setup: its comparisons are derived from class labels, not votes by individually modeled annotators. Aggregating annotators would introduce a social-choice problem and an aggregation rule that CLeRa neither studies nor supplies a result about. That could be a worthwhile project in its own right, but it does not rescue a mirror of this paper.

The negative case is strongest at the boundary between population learning and social choice; an annotator-based reformulation is conceivable. What it cannot do is provide the missing computational result to mirror.
