The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The absence of a numbered complexity theorem does not rule out a mirror here. The paper’s computational result is its §V-A(2) finding: in the tested mechanism class, welfare and disproportionality move together, while envy conflicts with both. NSGA-II produced that result by searching a defined mechanism space. A continuous mirror can ask whether the same Pareto geometry holds, and how to compute it, for a society given by a distribution over valuation types.

That population model fits the paper unusually well. Agents have the same one-dimensional type, \(\theta_i\in[0,1]\), the same valuation structure, and anonymous roles. A plausible large-market version gives the society mass one, a rational distribution \(\mu\) over valuation types, and resource supply per capita \(\rho\in(0,1)\). Scaling supply with population avoids the vanishing-resource limit of keeping \(m=1\). The resulting question is to compute or approximate the Pareto frontier of welfare, envy, and disproportionality over feasible redistribution mechanisms, with complexity in support size \(\tau\), bit length \(L\), and \(\rho\). That is a population-continuous computational problem, not just exact integration over the paper’s finite random samples.

There is a real caveat: for a fixed diffuse \(\mu\), order statistics converge to quantiles, and excluding one infinitesimal agent stops changing them. The paper’s rank-based rebates may then converge to a common rebate, simplifying the mechanism class and potentially eliminating envy trade-offs in the limit. But that is a substantive boundary to investigate, not proof that no worthwhile mirror exists. The paper already studies several supply settings and identifies nonuniform valuation distributions as future work; varying \(\mu\) and \(\rho\) gives the frontier question a natural parameter space. Even a result that some trade-offs collapse would clarify when population-level continuization preserves the design problem and when it simplifies it sharply.
