The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent names no anchors, and that is the strongest point against treating this paper as a positive case. Theorem 1 says IDP skips certain coalition splittings; it does not classify the complexity of a computational problem. The NP-completeness claim in the introduction is attributed to Sandholm et al., and the \(O(3^n)\) and \(O(n^n)\) bounds describe earlier algorithms. The paper gives no worst-case complexity result for its own hybrid; it lists that analysis as future work. So there is no named complexity result here for a continuous mirror to anchor to.

The strongest possible mirror would be coalition structure generation for a high-multiplicity population: for example, many interchangeable sensors, with types describing capabilities and coalition values depending on type composition. One could ask how to partition type masses into coalitions to maximize total value. That is a recognizable computational question, and a configuration LP with a pricing problem is a plausible approach. I cannot honestly rule it out as worthwhile; the paper gives no evidence that it is already answered or that the continuous formulation would be trivial.

That exposes the limit of the negative case. Under the requested anchor standard, the proponent has established no positive mirror. But the stronger claim that *no scenario* yields a worthwhile one is not supported: the high-multiplicity CSG formulation is a credible candidate, even though it is a new question rather than a mirror of a complexity theorem proved in this paper.
