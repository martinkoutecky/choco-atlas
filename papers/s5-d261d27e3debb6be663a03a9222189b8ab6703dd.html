What the mirror covers

The proposed question concerns evaluating the Individual Contribution rule in a replicated type population; it leaves all three characterizations and the paper's other rules and axioms outside the mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Prove that, under replication of rational type masses and task supplies, the uniform distribution over optimal integral assignments converges to the stated entropy-maximizing flow, including instances with ties and boundary flows.
  • Determine whether the type-level compensations can be approximated to additive error \(\varepsilon\) within the proposed polynomial running time, and formulate an appropriate mass-level analogue of Consistency.

The case FOR (proponent)

There is a plausible high-multiplicity extension here, but no qualifying computational anchor. Theorem 1 proves that the Egalitarian rule is the only rule satisfying Efficiency, Symmetry, and Group Productivity Monotonicity; Theorem 2 proves the corresponding uniqueness of the Shapley Value rule under Efficiency and Balanced Impact; and Theorem 3 proves that the Individual Contribution rule is the only rule satisfying Efficiency and Consistency. All three are proved in the paper. They characterize compensation rules axiomatically: none claims a problem is tractable or hard, and the paper gives no computational formulation that it then computes with. So none meets the requested anchor standard.

The strongest basis for a *possible* computational continuation is Theorem 3. Its rule pays each worker their average output over all output-maximizing assignments, and its characterization ties that rule to the paper’s central assignment model. But this is a lead suggested by the paper, not an anchor-compliant mirror.

A concrete candidate is Continuous High-Multiplicity Individual Contribution. Take worker types \(H\), task categories \(Q\), rational masses \(\mu_h>0\) summing to \(1\), rational per-capita task supplies \(\kappa_q\) with \(\sum_q\kappa_q\ge 1\), and rational productivities \(p_{hq}\). A type completely specifies productivity across task categories. Let \(z_{hq}\) be the mass of type \(h\) assigned to category \(q\), with \(\sum_q z_{hq}=\mu_h\) and \(\sum_h z_{hq}\le\kappa_q\). First maximize total output \(\sum_{h,q}p_{hq}z_{hq}\), and let \(F\) be the set of maximizing flows.

To select a particular flow in \(F\), maximize
\[ \Phi(z)=-\sum_{h,q}z_{hq}\log z_{hq} -\sum_q\left(\kappa_q-\sum_hz_{hq}\right) \log\left(\kappa_q-\sum_hz_{hq}\right), \]
using \(0\log 0=0\). This selection has a specific interpretation: replicate the society \(n\) times, make \(n\mu_h\) workers and \(n\kappa_q\) distinct task slots, and choose uniformly from all optimal integral assignments. The proposed continuous solution is the limiting type-to-task flow \(z^\dagger\); type \(h\)’s compensation is \(\rho_h=\sum_q p_{hq}z^\dagger_{hq}/\mu_h\). The question to hand a prover is: given \((H,Q,\mu,\kappa,p)\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), compute an additive-\(\varepsilon\) approximation to \((\rho_h)_{h\in H}\), with running time measured in \(|H|,|Q|\), input bit length, and \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

The regime is a large firm or service with many workers in a modest number of standardized productivity classes and recurring task slots—say, a large logistics operation assigning workers across station types. Both worker counts and task supplies scale with the population. The fractional flow is an average over integral assignments; no individual worker gets a fractional task. This is a natural co-modification of the paper’s assignment model, and the authors could plausibly recognize it as a computational question about their Individual Contribution rule. I would expect a tractability result via an optimal-flow LP followed by concave optimization on its optimal face, though that is a conjectural Class A direction, not a result of this paper.

The weakest point is also central: Theorem 3 averages over finitely many labeled assignments, whereas the continuous model needs a limiting selection. The entropy objective is motivated by that limit, but proving the limit for all instances—and showing the resulting compensation retains a meaningful form of Consistency—is further work. The mirror is therefore a credible research lead, but it cannot be presented as a continuous mirror of a named computational result in this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that none of the paper’s three named theorems makes a computational claim. Theorems 1–3 characterize compensation rules by axioms; the paper does not pose an algorithmic problem or analyze its complexity. A continuous population can supply a new computational question, but it cannot turn those characterizations into computational results to mirror.

For Theorem 1, a natural high-multiplicity model would give worker types rational masses and scale task supplies with the population. Computing the Egalitarian payment would then mean finding maximum output, which has a type-to-task optimization formulation. That may be a useful calculation, but it does not mirror the theorem’s claim: the theorem says which rule is uniquely characterized by three axioms, not how hard it is to calculate that rule.

For Theorem 2, the best extension would define a continuum Shapley allocation through expected marginal output along random orders, or through a limit over replicated type populations. But Balanced Impact compares what happens when a named worker is removed. Removing one worker has zero effect on a continuum society. A finite-mass deletion or directional-derivative version could be sensible, but it changes the axiom and its characterization. The resulting computation would be a new question about that replacement.

Theorem 3 is the closest case, and the proposed entropy limit is not a clumsy mirror. The finite rule averages output over optimal assignments; under replication, a type-to-task flow selected by assignment multiplicities is a plausible limiting object. Computing that flow could make a worthwhile project. But its central question is whether this new limit and its entropy optimization capture the rule’s finite behavior. Theorem 3 itself only characterizes Individual Contribution through Efficiency and Consistency; it gives no computational claim about evaluating the rule. A continuum version of Consistency would also need its own mass-level formulation.

So the negative case is strongest at the programme’s stated boundary: these theorems are axiomatic results, not computational anchors, and a new algorithmic problem built around one of their rules is an adjacent project rather than a mirror of a computational result in the paper. That leaves a real weakness in the universal claim: if ChoCo accepts computational evaluation of an axiomatically characterized rule as sufficient, the Individual Contribution limit is a credible candidate. It is the one case I would expect a thoughtful reader to keep alive.
