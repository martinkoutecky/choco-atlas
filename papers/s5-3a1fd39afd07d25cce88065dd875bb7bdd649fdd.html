What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers COM-BRADSO and a prospective mass analogue of Theorem 2; it leaves the equilibrium analyses, comparative statics, empirical comparisons, and simulations without a computational mirror.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the mass cumulative-offer process with rational data be evaluated exactly in time polynomial in \(|B|\), \(\tau\), and the encoding length?
  • What nonvacuous type-level or coalition-level incentive notion preserves a meaningful continuous counterpart of strategy-proofness?
  • Under what repeated-priority assumptions does a continuous uniqueness theorem corresponding to Theorem 2 hold, and how do finite-\(n\) outcomes converge to it?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is conditional. This paper does not contain a named computational-complexity result: Theorems 1 and 2, Proposition 1, Proposition 2, and Corollary 1 concern mechanism characterization, equilibrium, and comparative statics, not membership in \(P\), NP-hardness, FPT, or any other complexity class. Thus it supplies no fully compliant complexity anchor for ChoCo. Nevertheless, it contains a very credible structural mirror.

The lead anchor would be Theorem 2, proved in this paper, with strategy-proofness relying on the general result of Kominers and Sönmez. It states that, for fixed baseline priorities \((\pi_b)_{b\in B}\) and BRADSO policies \((\omega_b^+)_{b\in B}\), a direct mechanism is the COM-BRADSO mechanism if and only if it satisfies individual rationality, non-wastefulness, enforcement of the BRADSO policy, strategy-proofness, and no priority reversals. COM-BRADSO is the cumulative-offer mechanism using the branch choice rule \(C_b^{\mathrm{BR}}\): the first \(q_b^0\) positions use the native priority order, while the remaining \(q_b^+\) positions use the BRADSO priority order.

A natural high-multiplicity regime is the Army’s larger internal labour market: the paper itself mentions thousands of cadets and a possible market involving \(15{,}000\) officers per year. The type of a cadet would include her complete preference order over branch-cost contracts and unemployment, together with every talent, tier, score-band, and eligibility attribute used in branch priorities. Two cadets of the same type are therefore indistinguishable for the model’s purposes. A society is a distribution \(\mu\) over finitely many such types, with \(\tau\ll n\). Branch capacities become per-capita capacities \(\bar q_b\), and BRADSO caps become \(\bar q_b^+\). These are not fractional contracts: each unit of mass receives either \((b,t_0)\), \((b,t_+)\), or \(\emptyset\). Only the aggregate number of identical units is continuous.

The precise lead problem is:

Continuous COM-BRADSO Allocation. An instance consists of a finite branch set \(B\), a finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), rational capacities \(\bar q_b^0,\bar q_b^+\), a preference order \(\succ_t\) over \(B\times\{t_0,t_+\}\cup\{\emptyset\}\) for every type, and type-level versions of the native priority order \(\omega_b^0\) and BRADSO priority order \(\omega_b^+\).

A solution is an exact mass allocation
\[ x_{t,b,0},\;x_{t,b,+},\;u_t\ge 0 \]
such that
\[ \sum_{b,\ell}x_{t,b,\ell}+u_t=\mu_t, \]
\[ \sum_t(x_{t,b,0}+x_{t,b,+})\le \bar q_b^0+\bar q_b^+, \qquad \sum_t x_{t,b,+}\le \bar q_b^+, \]
and no positive mass receives a contract ranked below \(\emptyset\).

The allocation must be the mass version of COM-BRADSO. At each branch, the first \(\bar q_b^0\) units are selected by \(\omega_b^0\). From the remaining offered units, up to \(\bar q_b^+\) are selected by \(\omega_b^+\), with the increased-cost contract selected whenever that contract is offered and the corresponding type is not already selected in the native-priority part. Types propose their most-preferred non-rejected contracts, with rejected mass moving to its next choice, until no mass is rejected. The required output is the final allocation together with the proposal/choice certificate showing that it is a fixed point of this continuous cumulative-offer process.

The expected classification is Class A. With explicit finite support, branch choices reduce to priority-threshold operations on type masses, and the cumulative-offer process should be implementable exactly in time polynomial in \(|B|\), \(\tau\), and the encoding length of the rational data. The theorem-level companion asks whether this mass mechanism is the unique direct rule satisfying the continuous analogues of the five axioms in Theorem 2. Further questions include whether the uniqueness proof survives type masses cut at priority thresholds, how accurately finite-\(n\) outcomes converge to the mass outcome, and whether optimizing the BRADSO caps or policies across branches introduces genuine combinatorial hardness.

This is recognizably the authors’ problem. It preserves branches, service obligations, branch-specific priorities, BRADSO eligibility, cumulative offers, and exactly the five desiderata in their theorem. It is not merely “fractional matching”: every unit of mass still receives an integral branch and an integral service term. The original finite model is not already continuous, so this is not a novelty collision.

The weakest point is the priority structure. The paper formally gives every named cadet a strict baseline priority order. If the exact OML rank of each cadet is essential, then almost every cadet may be a distinct type, destroying the high-multiplicity regime. The mirror therefore requires a plausible setting in which priorities are determined by finitely many talent bands, standardized score classes, or other repeated attributes. That is defensible for a large policy-designed officer market, but it is not guaranteed for the specific West Point data.

So the positive verdict is: a genuine and persuasive continuous-population mirror exists, with Theorem 2 as a strong structural anchor and an apparently tractable continuous allocation problem. The important qualification is that the paper itself offers no named computational result, so this is a promising ChoCo extension of the paper, not yet a complexity result already established by it.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is unusually strong at the programme’s first gate: this paper contains no named computational result to continuize. Theorem 2 is an axiomatic characterization of COM-BRADSO; Theorem 1 is its single-branch counterpart; Proposition 1 concerns Nash implementation; Proposition 2 gives comparative statics. None states a complexity class, an algorithmic problem, an approximation guarantee, or a hardness result. A continuous version would therefore be a new axiomatic matching paper, not a continuous mirror of this paper’s computational contribution. The paper’s central object is the characterization of a mechanism, not the computational tractability of a problem over a population.

The proposed Theorem 2 mirror also loses the theorem’s essential data. The paper fixes strict baseline priority orders \( \pi_b \) and strict BRADSO orders \( \omega_b^+ \) over named cadets. Those orders are not merely attributes such as “high talent” or “medium talent”; they determine who is pivotal, who suffers a priority reversal, and which contract is selected at a capacity cutoff. A distribution \( \mu \) over preference and talent types does not contain those individual rank positions. If the exact priority position is included in the type, then under a generic strict order almost every cadet is a distinct type, so \( \tau \) grows with the population and the high-multiplicity regime disappears. If cadets are grouped into finite priority bands, the strict-priority model and its no-priority-reversal axiom have been changed. If a within-band tie-breaking order is retained, the supposedly continuous model has reintroduced an individualized rank coordinate.

The best repair—finite priority tiers with a measurable priority cutoff—does not preserve the theorem’s strategic content. Strategy-proofness in Theorem 2 is a unilateral-deviation property of a named cadet. In an atomless society, one cadet has mass zero and cannot change any branch’s choice set or cutoff. Consequently, essentially any allocation rule depending on aggregate reports is strategy-proof against individual deviations. The axiom that does the most work in the characterization therefore becomes vacuous. One could instead let an entire positive-mass type misreport, but that is a coalition or representative-type deviation, not the paper’s strategy-proofness, and the uniqueness theorem would have to be rebuilt from scratch.

The same problem affects the equilibrium anchor. Proposition 1 and the intricate Examples 2 and 3 concern how individual cadets manipulate BRADSO declarations, including knife-edge changes caused by one cadet’s type. Those phenomena vanish when an individual is measure zero. A type-level strategic model could retain them only by treating a whole mass of identical cadets as one strategic player; that is a new weighted game, not a continuization of the paper’s cadet-level mechanism.

What remains after these repairs is a finite bookkeeping problem: mass versions of deferred acceptance and \( C_b^{\mathrm{BR}} \) can be implemented by threshold operations over types. That may be a sensible weighted matching model, and the paper’s large markets provide some motivation for it. But it would neither mirror a computational result nor preserve the paper’s decisive individual-priority and incentive claims. Optimizing BRADSO caps over masses would be a new policy-optimization problem, not an anchor supplied by this paper.

So the honest negative verdict is strong, though not a logical impossibility theorem. A stylized army-like market with repeated preference and talent bands could be modelled continuously. What cannot be retained simultaneously is the paper’s exact strict priority structure, its individual strategy-proofness, and a genuinely nontrivial high-multiplicity population. The proponent has identified a plausible new matching model, but not a worthwhile ChoCo mirror of this paper.
