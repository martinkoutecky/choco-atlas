The strongest positive candidate is Continuous-Type Datacenter Welfare Allocation, a mirror of the market-clearing problem in §3.3—not a compliant anchor. Imagine a large cloud with many tenants running services through the same market. A type is a complete application profile: its task arrival rate, performance on each processor architecture, hardware eligibility, response-time target, and value for service. Its mass \(\mu_t\) is its share of tenants. This regime is plausible for standardized services or repeated deployment templates, where the number of tenants \(N\) is large and the number of distinct profiles \(\tau\) is comparatively small. The paper’s buyers are application proxies, so repeated buyers with identical profiles preserve the market’s central setup.

An instance has \(d\) processor architectures; rational type masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\); rational per-capita supplies \(b_j\) of each architecture; and, for each type \(t\), the paper’s performance and eligibility parameters plus a rational piecewise-linear bid \(g_t\) for equivalent service capacity. Let \(\kappa_{tj}\) be type \(t\)’s performance on architecture \(j\) relative to its canonical core. Each tenant still receives an integral bundle \(q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d\), subject to the type’s restrictions. The decision variable \(y_{t,q}\) is the fraction of the whole population that has type \(t\) and receives bundle \(q\). Require \(\sum_q y_{t,q}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,q}y_{t,q}q_j\le b_j\) for each architecture \(j\). Thus fractions describe how many tenants receive each bundle; no tenant receives a fractional core.

The question is to find a feasible allocation \(y\) maximizing per-capita welfare: total type value, \(\sum_{t,q}y_{t,q}g_t(\sum_j\kappa_{tj}q_j)\), minus the datacenter’s per-capita cost for the resulting hardware use. The cost schedule can include energy and costs of operating replicated hardware over the allocation period. A solution is the allocation distribution and its welfare. Capacities and costs scale with the tenant population; queueing-based performance and value remain part of the bid. This preserves the paper’s welfare objective and its architecture-sensitive allocation question.

My expectation is tractability when the number \(d\) of architectures is fixed and the bid has polynomially many pieces. The configuration LP has one column per integral tenant bundle; its pricing question is to maximize \(g_t(\kappa_t\cdot q)-\pi\cdot q\) over feasible bundles at given resource prices \(\pi\). With fixed \(d\), each linear piece reduces to integer optimization in fixed dimension, suggesting a route through pricing and the configuration LP. For variable \(d\), pricing may itself carry substantial combinatorics, so a full polynomial-time result is not established by this argument. Useful follow-up questions include the parameter dependence on \(d\), whether the paper’s full seller-cost model preserves the tractable case, and whether hardness for unrestricted architecture sets transfers from the finite-tenant problem or arises specifically in the continuous model.

This mirror covers the market’s welfare-maximizing allocation, especially §3.3 and the optimization experiments in §4.6. The proposed high-multiplicity setting is credible for repeated cloud services, but the article does not demonstrate that its own evaluated instances have this structure.
