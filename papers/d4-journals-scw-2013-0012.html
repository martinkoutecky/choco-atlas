The best proposed mirror would be a large housing market with residents grouped by roommate preference type, represented by a distribution over types. One could then study stable mass pairings and define what it means for positive mass to block, enter, or leave. That is a plausible model, and the issue is not that types or fractional mass are illegitimate. But it would turn an axiomatic characterization of solution correspondences on finite markets into a new computational problem about measure-valued matchings. The paper supplies no computational objective or algorithmic question to carry across that bridge.

Nor does the paper’s dependence on individually named partners rule out every such model: type-based preferences could make a mass formulation coherent. It does mean that a fixed, small type space is not already implicit in the paper’s general roommate domain. More fundamentally, even a convincing type-based model would establish an adjacent research direction, not a continuous mirror of either characterization.
