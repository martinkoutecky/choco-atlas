The continuous mirror question

Given a finite report/type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), valuation vectors \(v_t\), public budgets \(b_t\), service limits \(\delta_t\), per-capita service capacities \(\kappa_j\), and specified linear weights \(w_t\), compute report-contingent allocations \(x_{rj}\) and payments \(p_r\) maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t w_t(v_t,x_t,b_t)\), subject to \(0\le x_{rj}\le1\), \(\sum_j x_{rj}\le\delta_r\), \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_{tj}\le\kappa_j\), \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t w_t(v_t,x_t,b_t)\le\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t p_t\), \(p_t\le b_t\), \(\sum_j v_{tj}x_{tj}-p_t\ge0\), and \(\sum_j v_{tj}x_{tj}-p_t\ge\sum_j v_{tj}x_{rj}-p_r\) for every allowed report \(r\) with the same public budget and service limit as type \(t\).

The model it lives in

A nonatomic commuter society with rational mass \(\mu_t\) for each complete type \(t\). Each type’s mass can be split among integral service bundles, so \(x_{rj}\) is the average assignment for members reporting \(r\); payments are per traveler, and capacities scale as \(\kappa_j\).

The objection that survived

The fixed-\(\mu\) LP suppresses the original mechanism’s dependence on the full report profile; the proponent flags this scope loss but gives no equivalence to the all-profile mechanism.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

It mirrors Lemma 1’s LP and dual-based mechanism construction, along with the constraints corresponding to Theorems 1–3. It leaves the original individual-profile mechanism across all valuation profiles and an unrestricted valuation domain outside this finite-type version.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the per-society mechanisms be represented by one profile-dependent mechanism that remains truthful across all rational population distributions \(\mu\)?
  • What finite-population approximation guarantees follow from solving the type-mass LP?

The case FOR (proponent)

The best positive case is a high-multiplicity traveler–service assignment mechanism. The population would be urban commuters who repeatedly choose from the same finite menu of routes or mobility services. A type \(t\) records the complete information the mechanism uses: the commuter’s valuation vector \(v_t\), known budget \(b_t\), and service limit \(\delta_t\). The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over those types, with many commuters per supported type. For example, commuters sharing an origin–destination corridor, travel window, valuation profile, and budget class could form a type. Service capacities scale with the cohort, so \(\kappa_j\) is the per-capita capacity of service \(j\).

My lead anchor is Lemma 1, stated in this paper and not cited from elsewhere. It gives the dual of Problem 2; the paper then uses its dual variables to derive nominal assignments and reservation payments. In full, the dual minimizes
\[ \sum_{v\in\mathcal V}\left[ \sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\xi_1^i(v)\delta_i+ \sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\xi_2^j(v)\bar{\varepsilon}_j+ \sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\xi_4^i(v)b_i \right] \]
over nonnegative dual variables, subject to, for every valuation profile \(v\) and traveler–service pair \(i,j\),
\[ \xi_1^i(v)+\xi_2^j(v) +\sum_{\tilde v_i\in\mathcal V}\tilde v_{ij}\xi_4^i(v,\tilde v_i) -v_{ij}\sum_{\tilde v_i\in\mathcal V}\xi_4^i(v,\tilde v_i) -v_{ij}\xi_5^i(v)\ge 0, \]
and, for every \(v\) and \(i\),
\[ \sum_{\tilde v_i\in\mathcal V}\xi_4^i(v,\tilde v_i) -\sum_{\tilde v_i\in\mathcal V}\xi_4^i(v_{-i},\tilde v_i,v_i) -\xi_3(v)+\xi_5^i(v)+\xi_6^i(v)=0. \]
It also requires \(\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}\xi_3(v)=1\). The multiplier families are nonnegative and correspond to the constraint families listed in the lemma. This is a formulation anchor, rather than a complexity theorem.

I would hand a prover the following mirror, the Continuous Mobility Equity Assignment Problem. Its input is an explicit finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), type data \((v_t,b_t,\delta_t)\), per-capita service capacities \(\kappa_j\), and the paper’s explicit linear revenue weights \(w_t\). Find allocations \(x_{tj}\) and payments \(p_t\) maximizing
\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\,w_t(v_t,x_t,b_t), \]
subject to
\[ 0\le x_{tj}\le 1,\qquad \sum_j x_{tj}\le\delta_t,\qquad \sum_t\mu_t x_{tj}\le\kappa_j, \]
and the paper’s revenue, truthfulness, voluntary-participation, and budget-fairness conditions. In particular, for each true type \(t\) and allowed report \(r\) with the same public budget and service limit, truthfulness requires
\[ \sum_j v_{tj}x_{tj}-p_t \ \ge\ \sum_j v_{tj}x_{rj}-p_r; \]
participation requires \(\sum_jv_{tj}x_{tj}-p_t\ge0\), and budget fairness requires \(p_t\le b_t\). The revenue condition is \(\sum_t\mu_t w_t(v_t,x_t,b_t)\le\sum_t\mu_t p_t\).

Here \(x_{tj}\) is the fraction of type-\(t\) mass assigned to service \(j\), not a fractional service delivered to each traveler. Each traveler can still receive an integral bundle of services: the mass of each type can be split among such bundles, giving the stated type-level averages. This preserves the paper’s assignment, capacity, budget, and incentive questions while making the population itself the continuous object.

For an explicit finite type and report set, this mirror is an LP with polynomially many variables and constraints in \(|T|\), the number of services, and the input bit length; I therefore expect it to be tractable. It also yields follow-up questions: whether one mechanism should work across all population distributions \(\mu\), how the finite-type case relates to unrestricted valuations, and what finite-population approximation guarantees follow from the continuous solution.

The case is credible for recurring commuter cohorts with repeated preference and budget profiles, where the number of commuters is much larger than the number of supported types. I think the authors would recognize it as their traveler–service allocation and pricing problem under the same equity criteria. The soft spot is that the paper’s objective weight \(w\) is not fully specified, its valuation domain is continuous, and Lemma 1 is a dual formulation rather than a stated complexity result. The mirror therefore needs an explicit finite report domain and revenue weights, and it fixes a society \(\mu\); whether the authors would accept that as capturing the full scope of their all-profile mechanism is the main uncertainty.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the proposed LP changes the mechanism class while claiming to mirror Lemma 1. In the paper, assignments and payments are functions of the *entire* reported valuation profile, and truthfulness is required for every profile and every unilateral report. Lemma 1 is indexed by those profiles. The mirror instead uses one allocation and payment per type at a fixed population distribution, with deviations only among a finite report set. That is a sensible anonymous menu model, but the paper does not establish that its profile-dependent mechanism reduces to one.

There is also no specified computational problem in the paper to inherit. Theorem 1–3 establish incentive, budget, and participation properties; they give no complexity result. Lemma 1 is a dual formulation, while the revenue weight \(w\) is left unspecified and the valuation domain \(\mathcal V\) may be continuous. To make the proposed LP finite, one must choose an explicit revenue objective and report domain. Those choices may produce a useful new model, but they are doing substantial work beyond continuizing the stated result.

The best repair would define a finite rational valuation domain, fix the revenue objective, and assume an anonymous mechanism with per-capita service capacities. Then the type-level LP is well-posed and polynomial in the explicit domain size. That is a plausible high-multiplicity commuter model. Its weakness as a research anchor is that the tractability follows immediately from writing a finite LP; retaining the paper’s full profile dependence and unrestricted deviations requires a different, not-yet-specified problem.

So I can make a strong case against the claimed fidelity and significance of this particular anchor. I cannot honestly make the universal case that no worthwhile mirror exists. Repeated commuter cohorts with common valuations and budgets, and service capacities that scale with the cohort, remain a credible regime. If the authors accept that anonymous finite-type mechanism as their problem, the mirror survives. The negative case is therefore weak against the burden as stated.
