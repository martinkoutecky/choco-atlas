The continuous mirror question

Given \(q\) meetings with finite timeslot domains, a support list of \(\tau\) employee types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to one, each type’s attended meetings \(M_t\) and utility tables \(u_{t,e}(s)\), and the induced pairwise non-overlap constraints, compute a feasible schedule \(z\) maximizing \(U_\mu(z)=\sum_t \mu_t\sum_{e\in M_t}u_{t,e}(z_e)\); determine exact distributed message and memory bounds in terms of \(q,d,w,\tau,L\).

The model it lives in

Each type gives a complete attendance and utility profile with rational mass \(\mu_t\). The decision variables are discrete timeslots \(z_e\) for meetings; a schedule is feasible when no two meetings attended by any positive-mass type overlap, and its objective is the population-weighted average utility. ODPOP runs on the resulting meeting interaction graph, with rational weighted utility tables computed from the type list.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 4’s worst-case message and memory bounds for high-multiplicity meeting scheduling; it leaves the paper’s soundness and termination results, experimental savings, and other DCOP applications alone.

Open questions for a prover

  • For unrestricted induced width \(w\), what hardness or approximation boundary holds for type-mass meeting scheduling?
  • Which realistic attendance and utility profiles keep \(w\) bounded as the workforce grows?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is an algorithmic one. The paper contains no named NP-hardness result to anchor a hardness-dissolution question. Its best anchor is Theorem 4, proved in this paper. For a finite-domain DCOP with maximum domain size \(d\) and induced width \(w\), it states that ODPOP’s message count and memory are \(O(d^w)\). The proof makes the resource accounting more precise: in the worst case, each parent–child pair exchanges up to \(d^w\) ASK/GOOD pairs, giving \(O((n-1)d^w)\) total such pairs for \(n\) variables; memory is \(O(d^w)\) per recipient. The DFS and VALUE phases add \(O(n)\) messages. ODPOP messages are linear in the separator description, and its worst case sends the same information as DPOP’s UTIL phase. Thus the result is a parameterized resource bound, not a claim of polynomial time for unrestricted width.

A plausible continuous mirror is Type-Mass Meeting-Scheduling DCOP. Consider a large organization with \(N\) employees but only \(\tau\) recurring employee profiles. A type \(t\) specifies an employee’s meeting attendance and per-meeting utility for each timeslot; \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of employees of that type, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The instance also gives the meetings, their finite timeslot domains, and any hard scheduling constraints. Given a schedule \(z\), its population-average utility is
\[ U_\mu(z)=\sum_t \mu_t \sum_{e\in M_t}u_{t,e}(z_e), \]
where \(M_t\) is the set of meetings attended by type \(t\). A feasible schedule must respect the relevant constraints for every type with positive mass—for example, two meetings attended by the same type cannot overlap. The task is to find a feasible schedule maximizing \(U_\mu(z)\); a solution is the complete assignment of timeslots to meetings.

This matches the paper’s meeting-scheduling DCOP closely: its PEAV formulation gives each attendee variables for the meetings they attend, equality constraints make attendees agree on each meeting’s time, and unary utilities express their preferences. Grouping indistinguishable employees by attendance and utility profile turns their repeated contributions into type-weighted utilities. The setting is a recurring organization with many employees per profile and \(N\gg\tau\), not a seven-person committee. The action remains a discrete schedule; it is the population and its utility aggregation that are continuous. That is within the programme’s scope.

For this mirror, Theorem 4 motivates the question: can the exact optimal schedule be found by an ODPOP-style distributed algorithm with message and memory costs governed by the meeting interaction graph’s width, rather than by the headcount? With \(q\) meetings, maximum domain size \(d\), induced width \(w\), and rational masses and utilities of bit length \(L\), the expected answer is yes for the theorem’s finite-domain setting: the weighted relation values remain orderable, and the support-weighted objective can be computed from the type list. The paper’s proof suggests \(O(qd^w)\) worst-case ASK/GOOD pairs and \(O(d^w)\) memory per variable agent, with polynomial input processing in \(\tau,q,L\). I would expect a Class A result when \(d\) and \(w\) are bounded, and an exact width-parameterized algorithm more generally—not a polynomial-time claim for arbitrary width.

The scope is the finite-domain DCOP complexity result in Theorem 4, instantiated on high-multiplicity meeting scheduling. It does not turn fractional outcomes, continuous timeslots, or opinion dynamics into the mirror. Further questions are whether realistic type structure keeps \(w\) bounded as the workforce grows, whether ODPOP’s best-first savings persist with rational type-weighted utilities, and what hardness or approximation boundary applies when \(w\) is unrestricted.

The weakest point is that the continuous aspect changes the population weights while leaving the schedule finite and integral. This may look like a weighted DCOP written with proportions, and the theorem’s bound depends on \(d^w\), not on \(\tau\) alone. Still, the regime is credible: many employees can genuinely share complete attendance and utility profiles, and the paper’s own organizational scheduling model supplies the underlying problem. The mirror is a high-multiplicity version of their problem, not a claim that ODPOP discovers a new continuous optimization technique.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only anchor is Theorem 4’s bound for ODPOP, applied to type-mass meeting scheduling. The strongest case against it is that the proposed continuous instance compiles directly into the paper’s ordinary finite DCOP. Make one variable for each meeting, with its finite set of timeslots. Add pairwise constraints for meetings that cannot overlap, and fold the population into the unary values:
\[ r_e(s)=\sum_{t:\,e\in M_t}\mu_t u_{t,e}(s). \]
The support list constructs these values using rational arithmetic. ODPOP already accepts arbitrary utility functions, so Theorem 4 applies to the compiled instance; its message and memory bounds depend on the meeting graph, domain size, and width, not on workforce headcount. This also handles the PEAV formulation by merging equality-linked copies of each meeting variable and summing their contributions.

That makes the mirror look more like a direct corollary and an input compression than a new computational question. The distribution over employee types disappears into the DCOP’s utility tables, and the algorithm has no population-specific step. If the paper’s theorem is taken to cover this encoding, asking whether ODPOP solves the exact continuous scheduling problem adds little beyond spelling out the reduction.

But this is a limited objection. The paper does not present the population as a distribution over types, so the mirror is not simply a restatement of an explicitly continuous model. And under ChoCo’s standard, an answer already available from another formulation does not by itself make a well-posed mirror unworthy; the high-multiplicity regime is evidence in its favor. The workforce scenario is plausible, and rational type masses give a legitimate continuous-population input. The strongest negative case is therefore that this is a thin, already-covered mirror—not that the mirror fails. I cannot honestly defeat Theorem 4’s anchor, and the case against this paper is weak.
