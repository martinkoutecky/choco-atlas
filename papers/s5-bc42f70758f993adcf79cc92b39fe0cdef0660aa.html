The continuous mirror question

Given a finite support \(T\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), where each type \(t\) specifies an eligibility set \(E_t\subseteq C\), a priority \(\alpha_t\), and a complete availability vector \(v_t\in\{0,1\}^{|D|}\), together with \(\delta\in(0,1)\) and per-capita daily supplies \(\bar{s}_d\) and category-day quotas \(\bar{q}_{cd}\), find nonnegative assignment masses \(x_{tcd}\) maximizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{d\in D}\sum_{c\in E_t}\alpha_t\delta^{d-1}x_{tcd}\), subject to \(\sum_{c\in E_t,\,d:v_{td}=1}x_{tcd}\leq\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,c}x_{tcd}\leq\bar{s}_d\) for every day \(d\), and \(\sum_t x_{tcd}\leq\bar{q}_{cd}\) for every category-day pair \((c,d)\).

The model it lives in

A capacitated flow over type, day, and category nodes: type \(t\) supplies mass \(\mu_t\); eligible, available assignments carry utility \(\alpha_t\delta^{d-1}\); daily supply and category-day quotas are per-capita capacities. Find a feasible mass allocation of maximum total utility.

What the mirror covers

Covers Theorem 1, the offline optimal allocation for Model 1. It leaves the online competitive guarantees, the strategy-proofness result, and Model 2 untouched.

Open questions for a prover

  • Bound the flow algorithm's running time in terms of support size \(\tau\), \(|C|\), \(|D|\), and the input bit length.
  • Characterize the exact correspondence between rational mass instances and finite instances obtained by scaling masses and capacities to integer counts.

The case FOR (proponent)

My strongest honest case is a narrow mirror of the offline Model 1 result. The paper’s first theorem, referenced as thm:off-opt (Theorem 1; the supplied text omits the rendered numeral), says that Model 1 has a polynomial-time algorithm finding an optimal offline allocation. The authors prove this by reducing the allocation problem to minimum-cost flow and using flow integrality; the polynomial-time flow result is standard, but the reduction is given here. The excerpt contains no named hardness result to anchor on: its statement that Model 2’s offline complexity is open is not itself a complexity result.

Lead problem: Continuous Offline Model-1 Welfare Allocation. Let the input be a finite set of supported types \(T\), with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\). Each type \(t\) specifies an eligibility set \(E_t\subseteq C\), a priority value \(\alpha_t\), and an availability vector \(v_t\in\{0,1\}^{|D|}\). Days and categories remain discrete. The input also gives a rational discount factor \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and per-capita daily supply and category quotas \(\bar s_d,\bar q_{cd}\). A decision is a set of nonnegative masses \(x_{tcd}\), where \(x_{tcd}\) is the fraction of the whole population of type \(t\) assigned through category \(c\) on day \(d\). Find a feasible decision maximizing

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\sum_{d\in D}\sum_{c\in E_t} \alpha_t\delta^{d-1}x_{tcd}, \]

subject to

\[ \sum_{\substack{c\in E_t,\ d\in D\\v_{td}=1}}x_{tcd}\leq\mu_t \quad\text{for each }t,\qquad \sum_{t,c}x_{tcd}\leq\bar s_d \quad\text{for each }d,\qquad \sum_t x_{tcd}\leq\bar q_{cd} \quad\text{for each }c,d. \]

A solution is an allocation attaining the maximum. Each person still gets at most one whole unit; \(x_{tcd}\) records how much of a cohort receives that assignment. The objective is the paper’s total utility expressed per capita.

This is a direct type aggregation of the authors’ problem: the type records exactly the eligibility, priority, and scheduled availability that affect the allocation, while mass replaces the count of otherwise interchangeable agents. It also fits a plausible large-scale regime the paper itself motivates: a vaccination or planned-surgery campaign with many recipients but relatively few cohorts sharing risk priority, eligible facilities, and known appointment-availability patterns. For example, a population of hundreds of thousands might be represented by hundreds or thousands of supported cohort profiles. The regime depends on those profiles actually being few; highly individualized availability schedules could make the support nearly as large as the population.

The predicted classification is tractable (Class A). Aggregate each type into a flow node with capacity \(\mu_t\), and connect it to the eligible category-day options available to that type. Daily supplies and category-day quotas remain capacity constraints, and the flow cost represents negative utility. This has size polynomial in \(|T|,|C|,|D|\) and the input bit length. For a discrete population with integer counts and capacities, scaling by the population size gives integer capacities; flow integrality recovers an allocation of whole people. For arbitrary rational masses, scaling by a common denominator gives the same correspondence to a replicated finite population. The continuous question is therefore not just the paper’s individual-agent LP with different notation: it asks for the optimal mass allocation in a high-multiplicity input whose size depends on supported types.

The mirror covers the offline optimal-allocation theorem for Model 1, not the online competitive-ratio or strategy-proofness results, and it makes no claim about Model 2. Further questions include how the runtime depends on the support of availability profiles, how the discrete and mass optima correspond under population scaling, and whether a separately defined online fluid model preserves the paper’s competitive guarantees.

The weakest point is that the paper’s discrete problem is already polynomial-time solvable by a standard flow method. This mirror does not show that continuity dissolves hardness or reveals a difficult new boundary; its contribution is a succinct exact formulation for populations where the number of agents greatly exceeds the number of complete types. Also, the support can grow with the population if availability profiles become too individualized. Those limits make this a modest computational case, but the underlying welfare problem and its constraints remain the authors’ own.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only anchor is the offline Model 1 theorem: polynomial-time optimal allocation by a min-cost-flow reduction. The strongest objection is that a complete type must include the agent’s whole availability vector. In the paper’s experiment, availability is sampled independently over 30 days; if those are independent binary choices, most of 10,000 agents will have distinct calendars. Then the support size \(\tau\) is close to the population size, so the proposed aggregation offers little compression in the very setting the paper evaluates.

That objection has a limit. It weakens the paper’s experimental regime as a high-multiplicity example, but does not rule out a better mirror: a large campaign where people share a small number of appointment calendars, eligibility sets, and priority levels. Per-capita supplies and quotas make the mass model coherent, and fractional type-level assignments can describe whole people receiving indivisible doses. The paper’s objective does not track named recipients, so aggregation preserves what the offline theorem uses.

In that regime, however, the mirror is almost exactly capacitated min-cost flow with type nodes replacing individual-agent nodes. It adds an input-size parameterized by supported types, but brings no evident new optimization structure or boundary for the programme to chart. That is the best case against its *value*: the result looks like a compact high-multiplicity restatement of a flow theorem the paper already proves.

I cannot make that case decisive under the programme’s stated standard. The mirror remains a well-posed computational question, and the flow formulation gives it a direct answer. The objection about distinct calendars does not survive the shared-calendar scenario; the objection that the answer is straightforward is not grounds for rejecting a mirror here. So the negative case is weak, and this anchor survives.
