The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is unusually clean: the proponent is right that this paper gives no computational result to mirror. The strongest repaired mirror would put mass on complete community, preference, and endowment types, then compare segregated and integrated core allocations. In the literal model, each endowed house is unique, so recording it in the type leaves support growing with the population. One could coarsen houses into exchangeable classes or replicas and define an aggregate core and welfare measure; that may be a sensible model. Still, the paper’s propositions would become welfare or limit-analysis questions. Asking for the complexity of computing outcomes in that redesigned model would introduce a new computational problem rather than mirror one of the paper’s results.

Plausible high-multiplicity settings, such as cohorts in housing or kidney exchange, could make that redesigned model worth studying. They establish a credible population regime, but they do not supply the missing computational target.
