The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has no qualifying computational anchor. Theorem 1 says sDA is harmless; Theorem 2 characterizes it through stability, non-bossiness, and independence of irrelevant students; Theorem 4 establishes harmful manipulability for the finite-\(e\) school-proposing rules. These are mechanism properties, not complexity or algorithmic results. The paper specifies no computational problem whose continuous version could be charted.

The natural attempts to turn them into such problems expose a deeper mismatch. Harmlessness asks whether one student can misreport without hurting while another student is hurt. In a nonatomic population, that student has zero mass, so an individual report cannot change the aggregate assignment: harmlessness risks becoming automatic. Theorem 2’s “irrelevant student” is also a singleton; adding or removing a zero-mass student makes the axiom vacuous. The manipulations in Theorem 4 face the same problem. A more promising reformulation would let a positive-mass group deviate, but that changes the quantifier and turns the question into coalition manipulation. It may be worth studying, but it is not a continuous version of the paper’s individual-manipulation results.

Priority information makes the repair harder. The model gives each student a position in strict school priority orders. If priority rank is part of a complete type, as the high-multiplicity setup requires, exact ranks can make the support grow with the population. Grouping students by preferences while averaging away their ranks loses information the mechanism uses. Coarse priority classes or a continuum of priority scores could produce a sensible admissions model, but they require a new priority rule and a new manipulation question; they do not rescue these anchors.

So the best case against a mirror is that the candidate results either have no computational content or degenerate when individual agents become massless, while the repairs that avoid degeneracy change the problem. This is a strong reason not to treat this paper as a ChoCo complexity case study. It does not prove that no worthwhile continuous computational study of school choice exists; the case is weakest against a separately motivated study of positive-mass coalition manipulation. That would be a new research question, not a mirror anchored in this paper.
