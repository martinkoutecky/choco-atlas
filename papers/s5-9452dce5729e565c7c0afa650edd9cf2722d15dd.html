The paper does state two relevant equilibrium claims: Hart and Mas-Colell’s mechanism yields the Shapley value in expectation at its unique subgame-perfect equilibrium, while Perez-Castrillo and Wettstein’s bidding mechanism implements it as an a posteriori equilibrium payoff. But the paper attributes these claims to earlier work; they are not numbered computational results of this paper. The paper does not analyze the complexity of computing or implementing either outcome.

One could then define a bargaining mechanism over bids and offers between types. But that requires new choices about how a proposer is selected from a mass of agents, how an individual’s exit works, and what continuum analogue of the Shapley value is being implemented. Those choices are not settled by this paper, and a complexity question built on them would not mirror a named result here.

The paper’s evidence is about experimentally observed behavior in four-player games. Extending that to a large population may be conceivable, but it would change the strategic setting the experiments study.
