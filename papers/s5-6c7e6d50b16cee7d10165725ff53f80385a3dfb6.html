The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make an anchored positive case under the programme’s rules: the paper contains no named computational result. It gives no theorem that places a problem in P, proves it hard, or establishes another complexity classification.

The closest results are axiomatic. Theorem 3.6, proved in this paper, says that an LS-value belongs to the Harsanyi support levels set exactly when, for fixed \(N\) and \(B\), it coincides with a Harsanyi solution and satisfies the level game property. Corollary 3.7, also proved here, characterizes that set by efficiency, the null player property, positivity, additivity, and the level game property. Theorem 4.6 characterizes the weighted Shapley support levels values by efficiency, the null player property, strict monotonicity, additivity, and mutual dependence between components. These are substantive results, but they characterize value classes through axioms; they do not analyze the computational complexity of a problem. Theorem 2.2 is an axiomatization attributed to Winter (1989), also not a complexity result.

There is a plausible high-multiplicity *setting* in the paper’s software-company example: a large, distributed company with many developers in recurring roles, organized into teams and locations. A type could capture a developer’s role and position in that hierarchy, and mass could mean the fraction of staff of each type. One could ask how to allocate a bonus across those types. But turning that into a computational question—such as efficiently computing a continuous limit of Harsanyi support levels allocations—would introduce a new problem. None of the paper’s named results supplies a computational anchor for it.

So there is no honest continuous problem to state *per anchor*. The paper may motivate a population-level extension of its cooperative-game values, but its results are axiomatic rather than computational; that is the decisive weakness for this programme.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The decisive problem is that this paper has no named computational result to mirror. Theorem 3.6 and Corollary 3.7 characterize the Harsanyi support levels set by axioms; Theorem 4.6 characterizes the weighted Shapley support levels values. None gives an algorithm, a complexity bound, or a hardness result. Theorem 2.2 is an axiomatization attributed to Winter, also not a computational result. So there are no computational anchors here to defeat one by one: the closest candidates are axiomatic characterizations, and turning those into continuous axiomatizations would not meet ChoCo’s computational aim.

The strongest rescue is the company example. A large workforce with many interchangeable developers in the same roles and hierarchy could be a sensible high-multiplicity regime. One could describe coalitions by their type-level masses and ask for the resulting type-level bonus allocation. I would not reject that on the grounds that workers are individuated: with a complete type description, aggregation could be appropriate. But that rescues a population model, not a computational result from this paper.

To make the rescue computational, one would need to specify a compact representation of coalition worths and sharing weights, the input size, and what allocation must be computed. The paper instead permits an arbitrary worth function \(v:2^N\to\mathbb R\) and arbitrary sharing systems. A direct algorithmic question under an explicit table representation would be a new problem; choosing a succinct type-level worth function would introduce modeling assumptions that determine the complexity. Neither the axiomatizations nor their continuous reformulation selects that problem for us.

So the negative case is strong against treating this manuscript as a ChoCo mirror: every apparent anchor is axiomatic, and the plausible workforce scenario supplies no complexity question on its own. The universal claim is weaker. A worthwhile computational project could be built around type-level cooperative games, but it would be a newly specified research programme, not a continuous mirror of the results this paper proves.
