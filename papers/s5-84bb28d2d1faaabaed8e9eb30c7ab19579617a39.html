The case FOR (proponent)

A continuous mirror is plausible here. In a peak-period transit or taxi market, many riders can share a type defined by their origin–destination pair, route compatibility, utility, and travel disutility. The number of riders \(N\) could be large while the number of distinct types \(\tau\) is moderate. The paper itself treats demand \(q_s>1\) as identical users and gives examples with \(q_s=5\) and \(q_s=2\), so this regime is close to its model.

A natural *unanchored candidate* would take rational masses \(\mu_s\) over those types, fixed routes and link-incidence data, and per-capita line capacities. Its decision variable would be \(x_{sr}\), the mass of type \(s\) assigned to route \(r\); the question would be to find a surplus-maximizing assignment and a stable user/operator payoff allocation, including user-optimal or operator-optimal stable allocations. That is recognizably the paper’s assignment-and-pricing question with a continuous population.

Section 3.3 cites NP-hardness for capacitated multicommodity flow, but does not prove a complexity result for this paper’s model. So there is no named computational result here for a continuous prover question to mirror.

The best unanchored candidate is a population of OD types, with mass assigned across the paper’s fixed routes and capacities scaled per capita. But the paper already groups identical users with demand \(q_s>1\), and explicitly says to use the LP relaxation when flows are continuous. With the route set listed as input, the proposed mass assignment is essentially that relaxation; if routes are generated implicitly, the interesting issue becomes route pricing or enumeration. That may be a worthwhile problem, but it is not a computational result in this paper that continuization would mirror.

This does not support the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists. The negative case is therefore strong on the narrower point—there is no eligible result anchor, and the obvious continuous formulation is already signposted in the paper—but weak against the best broader proposal.
