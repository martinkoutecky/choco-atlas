The case FOR (proponent)

The most plausible positive mirror is a targeting problem for a large regulator. In a rural regime with many households but recurring neighborhood patterns, let each type describe a household’s position in a specified network pattern, including its eligibility and diffusion role. Let \(\mu_t\) be the population share of type \(t\), and let \(x_{t,B}\) and \(x_{t,T}\) be the shares sent baseline and threat mailings. Given a mailing budget, choose these shares to maximize predicted compliance, counting both direct responses and spillovers. That is a sensible policy question: the paper itself argues that targeting should account for network position.

But the paper has no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result. Its numbered equations specify empirical models; its tables report estimates. For example, Table 3 finds positive spillovers from both mailing treatments, and Table 6 finds that spillovers vary with treated households’ diffusion centrality. Those are relevant evidence for a targeting objective, but they are not complexity anchors. The paper therefore supplies no qualifying anchor to mirror, and no defensible tractability or hardness prediction tied to one.

The proposed targeting problem is only a plausible extension, not an anchor-valid mirror of a named result. Its weakest point is that preserving the paper’s central feature—communication through fine-grained network structure—may require many distinct network types, undermining the finite-support high-multiplicity regime.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s two anchors, Table 3’s spillover estimates and Table 6’s centrality heterogeneity, are empirical findings, not computational results. Equations (1) and (7) specify regressions; they do not define an algorithmic problem or establish a complexity bound. So the paper supplies no named computational result whose continuous version could be the proposed mirror. Its policy implications may motivate a new problem, but that is a different claim.

The strongest reconstruction is to choose baseline and threat mailing masses under a budget to maximize predicted compliance. If Table 3’s estimated marginal effects are taken as the response model, this reduces to a linear allocation: mail where the fitted return is higher, subject to the budget. It needs no pricing problem or other continuous-optimization machinery. A richer version could make returns depend on the allocation, but the paper’s reduced-form estimates do not establish how they would change under such a policy. That model would need additional assumptions and results before it could be called a mirror of Table 3.

Table 6 suggests targeting by diffusion centrality, and Table 7 suggests avoiding local concentration. But centrality values and treatment shares do not encode which households are linked to which others—the very structure the paper finds important. A finite library of repeated network motifs could provide a sensible high-multiplicity regime: households with the same role in the same motif are genuinely interchangeable. Yet if the motifs are fixed and small, one can enumerate each motif’s mailing patterns and optimize over their population shares; the paper’s findings then become inputs to a routine allocation problem. To retain richer, growing network structure, the type description must preserve more of the graph, and a distribution over types alone no longer captures the relevant links. A graphon or another continuum network model could address this, but it would add a structural model and computational question the paper does not formulate.

The weak point in this negative case is the universal claim. Repeated network motifs or a carefully specified continuum-network model could support a worthwhile computational project, and the paper’s targeting results give it a plausible motivation. But those would be new models, not continuous mirrors of the paper’s computational results: the paper has none. The case against is strongest on that boundary, not on any claim that network policy optimization can never be continuized.
