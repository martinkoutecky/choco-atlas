The case FOR (proponent)

There is a plausible population mirror for a large poll over a fixed set of alternatives: each type is a complete ranking \(\pi\), and \(\mu_\pi\) is the fraction of respondents with that ranking. In a regime with \(n\gg\tau\), where \(\tau\) is the number of ranking types present, this is a sensible high-multiplicity model. It keeps the paper’s central objects: private reports about rankings, score estimation, and manipulation risk.

One precise extension would be Mass-LDP Mechanism Design. Given candidates \(A\), a score vector \(w\), a rational type distribution \(\mu\), a privacy budget \(\epsilon\), and soundness bounds \(B,D\), choose a reporting kernel \(Q(\cdot\mid\pi)\) that minimizes
\[ V_\mu(Q)=\sum_{\pi}\mu_\pi\,\mathbb E_{Z\sim Q(\cdot\mid\pi)} \bigl[\|Z-v(\pi)\|_2^2\bigr]. \]
Require that \(Q\) is \(\epsilon\)-LDP for every pair of rankings, is unbiased for each ranking, and meets bounds \(B\) on report magnitude and \(D\) on report-space diameter. The solution is the kernel \(Q\). For a finite population with these type proportions and independent reports, \(V_\mu(Q)/n\) is the privacy-induced contribution to the paper’s mean squared error; the soundness bounds correspond to its per-report magnitude and diameter metrics. This would cover the paper’s theoretical usefulness and soundness analysis, not its experiments or its privacy mechanisms viewed merely as randomized outputs.

The authors could plausibly recognize this as their problem lifted to a population distribution: it retains rankings as types and asks about the same utility and attack trade-offs. The additive and weighted-sampling mechanisms give candidate kernels. With a finite report alphabet, the design problem is a linear program; for unrestricted report spaces, I would leave its complexity open. Natural follow-ups include whether optimal kernels can always have finite support, and how the best error-risk trade-off changes under a fixed mass of fraudulent participants.

But this is an extension, not an anchor-backed case under your criteria. The supplied paper has named theorems about LDP guarantees, unbiasedness, error bounds, and manipulation-risk bounds, but none asserts that an underlying computational problem is in P, hard, FPT, or another complexity class. Its \(O(dk)\) sampler complexity appears in prose, not as a numbered complexity theorem. The supplied text also does not print theorem numbers to quote. So there are no eligible anchors, and I cannot honestly give a separate continuous question per anchor or assign the paper’s results to the A/B/C trichotomy.

The weak point in the proposed extension is that a continuum of reports has zero aggregate sampling variance, while the paper’s error bounds and single-voter attack effects depend on finite \(n\). Rescaling to per-capita variance and report-level risk preserves a meaningful mechanism-design question, but it makes that question a new asymptotic formulation. That is why this is a credible lead for a mirror, but not a strong result-anchored positive case for this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against is that this paper offers no result-anchored computational mirror. Its theorems establish privacy guarantees, unbiasedness, estimation-error bounds, and manipulation-risk bounds. None classifies the complexity of a computational social choice problem or gives an exact, approximation, or parameterized algorithm for one. The \(O(dk)\) sampler is described in prose; its running time does not depend on the population model. That leaves no named computational result for ChoCo’s complexity programme to continuize.

The proposed Mass-LDP problem is coherent, but it changes the task from analyzing three mechanisms to synthesizing an arbitrary privacy channel. The population distribution \(\mu\) supplies weights in the loss; the LDP and unbiasedness constraints remain per-ranking constraints, independent of the population. With a finite report alphabet, the resulting LP is a channel-design problem that could be posed for one randomly drawn voter. It does not make a population-level social choice computation the object of study.

There is also a limit problem. In an exact nonatomic population, averaging independent local reports gives the population’s expected report vector, so the estimator’s sampling error vanishes; an individual report has zero mass. Keeping the paper’s nonzero error means retaining finite \(n\), or rescaling to a per-voter channel objective. Giving attackers positive mass avoids the vanishing-risk issue, and could yield a sensible continuous robustness question. But that is a new coalition-robustness model, not a mirror of a complexity result in this paper. The paper already studies attacks at fixed fractions of the electorate, so the extension would need to establish a distinct computational problem rather than just replace counts by proportions.

I would not call the negative case airtight: population-weighted channel design and positive-mass attack robustness could be worthwhile follow-on questions. The case is strongest on the narrower point that neither is a continuous mirror of a named computational result here.
