The continuous mirror question

Given a finite shift-class set \(K\), finite type set \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), integer bundle sizes \(q_t\), endowments \(e_t\), trichotomous marginal sets \(A_t,B_t\), and finite priority blocks, find a rational mass plan \(x_{t,b}\) over integral bundles \(b\) satisfying \(\sum_b x_{t,b}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,b}b_kx_{t,b}=\sum_t\mu_te_{t,k}\), and component-wise individual-rationality constraints, then run the type-block gradual-revelation procedure and compute a final plan guaranteed to be unambiguously individually rational and efficient for every consistent integral realization and responsive completion.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity balanced-exchange model with finitely many types, integral bundles, per-capita object supply, mass variables \(x_{t,b}\), lexicographic maximization of attractive-object mass by priority block, and robust welfare evaluated over responsive completions.

The objection that survived

The mass-level objective \(x\) does not by itself specify whether robustness must hold for every integral realization and every responsive completion, while replacing named serial agents by priority blocks changes the exact mechanism.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the constructive welfare guarantees of Proposition 2 and Theorem 5. It leaves the maximality results in Theorems 1 and 3 and the incentive results in Theorems 4 and 6 without a direct continuous analogue.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the configuration LP for variable \(|K|\) or \(q_t\) admit polynomial-time separation, approximation, or parameterized algorithms?
  • Under what type-block priority definition does gradual revelation terminate in at most \(|T|\) stages?
  • Does robustness for every integral realization of \(x\) imply unambiguous efficiency under all responsive completions, or is a stronger coupling condition needed?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a high-multiplicity mirror of the paper’s balanced shift-exchange problem. It is a plausible ChoCo problem, but there is an important qualification: the paper contains no numbered theorem asserting NP-hardness, membership in \(P\), FPT, W[1]-hardness, or a computational formulation. Its named results are structural and axiomatic. Thus this is not a fully compliant computational anchor under the strictest reading of the programme. The closest constructive anchor is Proposition 2.

Proposition 2 is stated in this paper, with its justification attributed to arguments from Manjunath and Westkamp (2021). It says:

For every trichotomous marginal-preference profile \((A,B)\), every allocation surviving the CIRP procedure is unambiguously individually rational and unambiguously efficient.

CIRP first considers all component-wise individually rational allocations. It then processes agents in a fixed priority order, and at step \(t\) retains only allocations maximizing agent \(t\)’s number of attractive objects. The proposition says that every allocation remaining after all agents have been processed is robustly individually rational and efficient for every responsive completion of the reported marginal preferences.

The lead mirror would be CIRP\(_\infty\): Continuous Balanced Exchange under Trichotomous Marginals.

Consider a large hospital network with a recurring finite template of shift classes \(K\): for example, weekday emergency shifts, weekend night shifts, outpatient shifts, and so on. There are many physicians, but only finitely many relevant types. A type \(t\) consists of:

  • mass \(\mu_t\);
  • an integral bundle size \(q_t\);
  • an endowment vector \(e_t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^{K}\), with \(\sum_k e_{t,k}=q_t\);
  • attractive, bearable, and crummy shift classes \(A_t,B_t,C_t\), with no endowed class in \(C_t\);
  • a finite priority class used by the exchange mechanism.

The object supply is not fractional per physician: it is scaled per capita,

\[ s_k=\sum_t \mu_t e_{t,k}. \]

A final bundle for type \(t\) is still integral,

\[ b\in\mathcal B_t=\left\{b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{K}:\sum_k b_k=q_t\right\}. \]

The decision variable is \(x_{t,b}\), the mass of type-\(t\) agents receiving bundle \(b\). It must satisfy

\[ \sum_{b\in\mathcal B_t}x_{t,b}=\mu_t \]

and

\[ \sum_{t}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_t} b_kx_{t,b}=s_k \qquad\text{for every }k\in K. \]

Only component-wise individually rational bundles are allowed: for every endowed class \(k\),

\[ \sum_{j:j\succeq_t k}b_j \ge \sum_{j:j\succeq_t k}e_{t,j}. \]

Order the finitely many types by the mechanism’s priority order. Starting from the feasible mass-allocation polytope \(\mathcal X_0\), define

\[ F_t(x)=\sum_{b}x_{t,b}\sum_{k\in A_t}b_k. \]

At step \(\ell\), maximize \(F_{t_\ell}\) over \(\mathcal X_{\ell-1}\), and let \(\mathcal X_\ell\) be the face attaining that maximum. The problem is:

Given \((K,T,\mu,e,A,B)\), compute a rational mass plan \(x\in\mathcal X_\tau\), together with an integral-bundle realization for the population, such that the resulting allocation is unambiguously individually rational and unambiguously efficient for every responsive completion of the reported marginals.

This is recognizably the paper’s problem: bundles remain indivisible, every agent keeps the same bundle cardinality, endowments matter, preferences are responsive, only marginal tiers are reported, and the mechanism maximizes attractive objects subject to robust individual rationality. The only genuine continuization is of the population. The per-capita supply \(s_k\) is the necessary co-modification.

The regime is plausible in shift exchange or time banking: thousands of participants may repeat a small number of role/location/time patterns, with the same endowment signature and the same three-tier availability report. The relevant comparison is \(N\gg \tau\), not that all physicians are identical. A type includes every mechanism-relevant feature, including endowment pattern and priority class.

The expected computational status is Class A in the bounded-template regime. The variables \(x_{t,b}\) form a configuration or transportation LP; with bounded \(q_t\) and finitely many shift classes, the bundles can be enumerated, and the lexicographic CIRP stages are ordinary LPs. With variable \(K\) or \(q_t\), the real question becomes whether the corresponding configuration-pricing problem has an efficient separation oracle. That is a legitimate ChoCo question, although it is not answered by this paper.

There is also a natural secondary mirror of Theorem 5, proved in this paper. Theorem 5 says that the gradual-revelation procedure terminates after finitely many rounds, that every round makes a strict addition to the set of agents whose attractive-object count can no longer be improved, and that every final allocation is unambiguously efficient and unambiguously individually rational. Its continuous version would reveal \(B_t\) for positive-mass type blocks only after their attractive-object promise becomes irrevocably maximal, and would ask whether the same finite LP-based termination and welfare guarantee survive.

The weakest point is the serial priority structure. A literal serial dictatorship over an atomless population is not well defined: an individual has zero mass, and type-level plans may split identical agents across different bundles. The proposed mirror repairs this with finitely many priority classes and mass-level lexicographic promises, but that is a real model modification. A second weakness is formal eligibility: Proposition 2 and Theorem 5 are constructive mechanism theorems, not computational-complexity results. So the positive conclusion should be modest:

The paper has a genuine, natural continuous-population analogue—CIRP\(_\infty\)—whose optimization layer is plausibly Class A and whose high-multiplicity interpretation is credible. But under ChoCo’s strict anchor rule, the paper does not itself provide a qualifying computational anchor.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against the mirror is that the paper supplies no qualifying computational anchor. Proposition 2 and Theorems 1, 3, 4, 5, and 6 are structural, axiomatic, or incentive results. They do not state a complexity theorem, define an encoded computational problem, or give an exact, approximation, or parameterized algorithm. The remark that the CIRP sequences can be computed in polynomial time is inherited background, not a named computational result of this paper. Thus \( \mathrm{CIRP}_\infty \) would be a new high-multiplicity exchange problem, not a continuization of the paper’s computational contribution.

Proposition 2 is also less portable than the proposed formulation suggests. The finite CIRP procedure processes named agents in a fixed serial order and retains allocations satisfying each named agent’s individual promise. The proposed LP instead maximizes

\[ F_t(x)=\sum_b x_{t,b}|b\cap A_t| \]

for an entire type block. That is an aggregate promise, not the paper’s per-agent serial promise. If the exact priority order is retained, priority rank becomes part of the type and typically destroys multiplicity. If agents are grouped into finitely many priority classes, the result is a new block-priority mechanism. If priority is discarded, the mechanism’s defining structure has been removed. This is not merely an integrality or tie-breaking repair.

There is a second semantic loss. The paper’s unambiguous efficiency quantifies over responsive completions for every named agent and evaluates integral allocations. A mass plan \(x\) does not by itself say whether agents of one marginal type may have different responsive completions, whether the guarantee is required for every integral realization of \(x\), or only in an aggregate Pareto sense. Each choice produces a different problem. The most charitable choice may be mathematically sound, but it requires a new theorem; Proposition 2 does not establish it.

Theorem 5 is even more dependent on finiteness. Its termination argument is that the set \(I^t\) of individually identified agents grows strictly until \(I^T=I\). In an atomless model there is no analogous finite subset of agents. Revealing whole type blocks gives a finite process only because the modeller has imposed a new block mechanism; revealing individual mass subsets can produce arbitrarily many or limit stages. The welfare claim may be recoverable, but it is not the continuous version of the paper’s finite termination result without substantial new definitions.

The negative case should not overclaim. A repeated shift-exchange environment with finitely many endowment and preference signatures is a credible high-multiplicity regime, and a clean continuous-population problem could be worth studying. The objection that “prices or endowments become type-level” would be mistaken. But under ChoCo’s strict anchor rule, the paper itself offers no computational result to mirror, and the proposed repairs replace named-agent serial exchange by a new anonymous mass-allocation model. The honest negative verdict is therefore strong on eligibility and fidelity, but not a proof that no worthwhile continuous exchange problem can ever be built from the paper.
