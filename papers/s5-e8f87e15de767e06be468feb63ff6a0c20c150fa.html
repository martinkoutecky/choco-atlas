The case FOR (proponent)

The most plausible positive angle is a cooperative sector with many repeated units—divisions, local associations, or firms—in a small number of genuinely interchangeable roles. Let \(T\) be the role types and \(\mu_t\) the fraction of units of type \(t\). A continuum TU game could assign worth \(V(z)\) to a coalition containing mass vector \(z\), and an allocation rule could return per-unit payoffs \(x_t\), with \(\sum_t \mu_t x_t=V(\mu)\). Composition, active-player, or nullified-game consistency might then be translated into conditions on these mass-based games. That is a recognizable population continuum, rather than continuity in the outcome space.

But the paper supplies no eligible computational anchor. Its strongest nearby result, Theorem 3, is proved here and says that efficiency, equal gain for two players, and HM-nullified-game consistency characterize the Shapley value; replacing HM consistency with F-nullified-game consistency characterizes CIS; and replacing it with M-nullified-game consistency characterizes ENSC. Theorem 1 characterizes the ELS values satisfying the composition axioms, and Theorem 2 characterizes those satisfying active-player consistency. These are axiomatic classification results, not claims about complexity, algorithms, or computational hardness.

The proposed continuum model would also need an extra restriction: a distribution over roles cannot represent the paper’s arbitrary TU game unless coalition worth depends only on the coalition’s type masses. And even with that restriction, translating the fixed-population axioms—especially equal gain for two players—to a continuum would itself require new choices. So this is a plausible setting for future axiomatic continuization, but it does not yield a continuous computational problem per named result, nor a defensible tractability or hardness prediction. The weakest point is decisive for ChoCo: the paper asks which rules satisfy axioms, while the programme’s target is computational complexity.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The paper has no computational result to mirror. Theorem 1, Theorem 2, and Theorem 3 characterize allocation rules by axioms; none studies an algorithm, a complexity class, or an optimization problem. In particular, “HM” in HM-nullified-game consistency is not high multiplicity: it names a reduced-game construction. The proponent’s three anchors therefore do not survive as ChoCo anchors.

Theorem 3 is the strongest candidate for a follow-on. One could define a game whose coalition worth depends only on the masses of a few interchangeable player types, then study computationally evaluating a Shapley-like value. But that requires both restricting the paper’s arbitrary TU games to type-symmetric worth functions and choosing an encoding for the worth function. Those choices create the computational problem; Theorem 3’s characterization does not supply one. A continuum version of its nullification and equal-gain axioms would still be axiomatic, and in a literal continuum individual players have zero mass, so the finite-player conditions would need substantive reformulation.

The same problem applies to Theorems 1 and 2. Composition and active-player consistency describe how a rule responds to transformed games and coalitions; their conclusions identify subclasses of ELS values. They do not ask how to compute an allocation. Translating them to mass-based games could produce new axioms about continuum allocation rules, but that would be an axiomatic extension outside ChoCo’s stated computational scope. A computational project could be built around a chosen succinct representation of coalition worth, but it would be a new question rather than a continuous mirror of these theorems.

Repeated divisions or associations may make a high-multiplicity cooperative-game model sensible. That establishes a possible setting, not a computational anchor in this paper. The negative case is strong on programme fit, though not a proof that no useful computational project could ever be built from these ideas. It is that the paper’s named results give ChoCo no computational question to continuize.
