What the mirror covers

The proposed mass-matching computation would address finding a stable aggregate outcome. It does not continuize any of the paper’s named results, which concern axiomatic characterizations and impossibilities.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is that one-sided partnership formation has a plausible high-multiplicity regime. In a large recurring partner market, agents could be grouped by complete skill and surplus profiles: two agents of a type are interchangeable in their value with every other type. Then \(n\) may be very large while the number of types \(\tau\) stays small, and mass is a natural way to describe the partnerships.

But the paper contains no named computational result of the required kind. Proposition 1 and Theorems 1–3 give axiomatic properties or characterizations of the core; Theorem 4 proves an axiomatic impossibility. None establishes a complexity classification or an algorithm. The paper mentions linear programming in connection with prior work, but does not compute with an LP as one of its results. So there is no eligible anchor, and Theorem 4 cannot honestly be presented as a hardness result.

A promising *new* computational question, though, would be: given finitely many types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), and symmetric values \(v_{tu}\) for partnerships between types, compute a stable mass assignment. Let \(x_{tu}\) be the mass of \(t\)-\(u\) pairs, with \(x_{tt}\) counting pairs of distinct agents of type \(t\). Find a maximum-surplus fractional matching subject to
\[ 2x_{tt}+\sum_{u\ne t}x_{tu}\le \mu_t, \]
and a dual payoff \(p_t\ge 0\) for each type, minimizing \(\sum_t\mu_t p_t\), subject to
\[ p_t+p_u\ge v_{tu}\quad(t\ne u),\qquad 2p_t\ge v_{tt}. \]
A matching and payoff vector with equal primal and dual values describe an efficient, stable mass outcome. This is expected to be tractable by linear programming. A large cohort of potential partners with a small number of recurring skill profiles is a credible setting for it.

The weak point is decisive for the requested anchoring: this is a computational study of the paper’s underlying core concept, not a continuous mirror of any named computational result in the paper. The authors’ “continuity” concerns changes in pair values, not population size; moreover, a mass market with common type payoffs may change core existence. So the population mirror is plausible as a new question, but the paper supplies no computational anchor for it.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is that the paper has no named computational result to mirror. Proposition 1 and Theorems 1–3 characterize the core through axioms; Theorem 4 proves an impossibility about consistency. None gives an algorithm, a complexity classification, or a computational lower bound. The paper’s reference to linear programming reports prior work, not a result of this paper. In particular, Theorem 4 cannot be recast as a hardness result.

The proposed mass-matching LP is a coherent population model: complete value profiles give natural types, and repeated cohorts make large multiplicities plausible. I cannot dismiss it on the grounds that matching is inherently individual or that no high-multiplicity setting exists. But it asks a new computational question about the paper’s central object; it does not continuize one of the paper’s computational findings, because there are none. The LP computes an aggregate stable outcome from pair masses and type payoffs. The paper’s theorems instead concern the full correspondence of labeled matchings and individual payoff vectors, and axioms governing how that correspondence behaves when particular agents leave. A type-level version of those axioms would be a new axiomatic project, outside ChoCo’s computational scope.

So the negative case is strong against presenting this as a paper-anchored continuous mirror: there are no eligible anchors, and the proposed LP does not supply one. It is weak as a claim that no worthwhile project could arise in any scenario. The LP may be a valuable adjacent computational study, and the article’s authors might recognize it as a problem about their core concept. I would reject the mirror as unanchored, but I cannot honestly conclude that the underlying project is worthless.
