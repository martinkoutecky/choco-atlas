The continuous mirror question

Given finite complete XOS types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_b\) of indivisible item kinds \(b\in B\), and valuations \(v_t(q)=\max_k\sum_{b\in B}w_{t,k,b}q_b\) for integral bundle count vectors \(q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{B}\), find aggregate masses \(x_{t,q}\ge 0\) satisfying \(\sum_q x_{t,q}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_{t,q}q_bx_{t,q}=\rho_b\), such that \(x_{t,q}>0\) implies \(v_t(q)\ge (3/13)M_t(\rho)\), where \(M_t(\rho)=\max\{\lambda:\exists y_q\ge0,\ \sum_qy_q=1,\ \sum_qq_by_q=\rho_b,\ y_q>0\Rightarrow v_t(q)\ge\lambda\}\).

The model it lives in

A configuration-allocation model in which repeated XOS types carry masses \(\mu_t\), each agent receives an integral count-vector bundle \(q\), supplies are normalized to \(\rho\), and the objective is to maximize the common \mathrm{MMS} approximation factor \(\alpha\) subject to \(x_{t,q}>0\Rightarrow v_t(q)\ge\alpha M_t(\rho)\).

The objection that survived

The proposed \(S\subseteq B\) formulation does not represent repeated copies, so count-vector XOS semantics and the exact continuum \mathrm{MMS} dictionary still need to be formalized.

What the mirror covers

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is a qualified one: this paper supports a genuine high-multiplicity mirror of its deterministic MMS-allocation problem, although not a clean claim that the continuum makes the problem polynomial-time solvable.

My lead anchor is Theorem 1.3 (the theorem labelled ExPost in the source), proved in this paper. It states that approxMMS, which first assigns single items, pairs, and triples that give an agent value at least \(3/13\) of her MMS and then maximizes welfare under the capped valuations \(\bar v_i(S)=\min\{6/13,v_i(S)\}\), returns an allocation in which every agent receives value at least \(3/13\) of her maximin share. This improves the previous \(0.219225\)-MMS guarantee for fractionally subadditive valuations. The result is constructive, although Section 6 explicitly notes that the algorithm is not necessarily polynomial-time because exact MMS values and MMS partitions may be hard to compute.

The corresponding continuous problem should preserve indivisible bundles while continuizing only the population. Let \(T\) be a finite set of complete XOS valuation types, with rational masses \(\mu_t\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Let \(B\) be a finite set of item kinds, with rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_b\). A bundle is still an integral set \(S\subseteq B\), and type \(t\) has XOS valuation \(v_t(S)\). An aggregate allocation is a collection of variables \(x_{t,S}\geq 0\), where \(x_{t,S}\) is the mass of type-\(t\) agents receiving the integral bundle \(S\), subject to

\[ \sum_S x_{t,S}=\mu_t \]

for every type \(t\), and

\[ \sum_{t,S:\,b\in S}x_{t,S}=\rho_b \]

for every item kind \(b\). Thus \(x\) is a distribution over complete integral bundles, not a fractional bundle received by an individual.

The appropriate continuous MMS benchmark for type \(t\) is

\[ M_t(\rho)=\max\left\{\lambda: \begin{array}{l} \exists\,y_S\geq 0,\ \sum_Sy_S=1,\\ \sum_{S:\,b\in S}y_S=\rho_b\ \text{for every }b,\\ v_t(S)\geq\lambda\ \text{whenever }y_S>0 \end{array} \right\}. \]

This is the high-multiplicity version of partitioning the available goods into equal-mass integral bundles. It does not give any individual a fractional item.

The resulting problem is:

Continuous XOS-MMS-\(3/13\) Allocation. Given \((T,\mu,B,\rho,(v_t)_{t\in T})\), output an aggregate integral-bundle allocation \(x\) such that, for every positive-mass type \(t\),

\[ x_{t,S}>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_t(S)\geq \frac{3}{13}M_t(\rho). \]

Equivalently, one can optimize the largest \(\alpha\) for which every positive-mass type receives only bundles worth at least \(\alpha M_t(\rho)\).

This is a credible mirror of the paper’s underlying problem. The regime is a large market of customers, households, students, or platform users who fall into a modest number of repeated XOS preference types, while the supply of indivisible goods scales with population. For example, millions of users may choose among repeated categories of scarce licenses, rental units, course seats, or advertising packages. The type contains the entire valuation representation and all relevant parameters; no idiosyncratic individual prices are being erased. We have \(|T|\ll N\), where \(N\) is the number of agents.

The finite/high-multiplicity dictionary is direct. Clearing denominators in \(\mu\), \(\rho\), and \(x\) produces a finite market with repeated valuation types and repeated item supplies. Conversely, any such finite allocation yields \(x\) by dividing bundle counts by the population size. The fact that MMS can change under duplication is not a defect: homogeneous MMS behaviour is precisely part of the high-multiplicity phenomenon the programme wants to study.

I would expect this problem primarily to be Class B. The difficult combinatorics live in indivisible item configurations and XOS bundle values, not in the number of named agents. A rational solution \(x\) can be denominator-cleared into a finite allocation, so any hardness arising from item configurations should transfer. There may also be a Class C boundary in the succinct-input version: the natural configuration LP has exponentially many bundle variables, while its pricing problem resembles finding a minimum-cost bundle reaching an XOS-value threshold, and the MMS benchmark itself is a max-min partition problem. Whether the population relaxation produces a genuine algorithmic collapse, or merely a compressed configuration problem, is an excellent question.

The paper’s randomized result gives a natural follow-up, though I would not use it as my primary anchor. Theorem 1.2, proved here, states that Algorithm ExPostExAnteMMS returns a randomized allocation that is \(1/4\)-MMS ex ante and \(1/8\)-MMS ex post. Its continuous extension would replace \(x\) by a finite-support distribution over aggregate integral-bundle allocations, require every supported bundle to have value at least \(M_t(\rho)/8\), and require each type-\(t\) individual to receive expected value at least \(M_t(\rho)/4\). The open questions are whether this guarantee survives exactly, whether population mass removes the need for randomization through within-type diversification, and whether better constants become possible.

The weakest point is that the paper never formulates a population distribution or proves a high-multiplicity theorem. Moving from named items to repeated item kinds with per-capita supplies is a substantive extension, and the paper’s XOS valuations are originally defined on finite sets rather than on a measure-valued market. Moreover, the anchor is an approximation theorem, not an NP-hardness or polynomial-time classification. If the source gate accepts only explicit complexity-class results, this paper would fail that gate.

The positive case survives because the extension keeps the paper’s defining object intact: every individual still receives an indivisible bundle, MMS is still defined through partitions into bundles, and the only continuously represented object is the population of agents. The resulting allocation problem is therefore recognizable to the authors as their fair-division problem in the high-multiplicity regime, with a real computational landscape—likely hardness transfer, possible configuration-LP tractability under restricted representations, and an open boundary between them.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that this paper does not actually supply a computational-complexity result for continuization to illuminate. The theorem labelled ExPost is an approximation guarantee, but its “algorithm” requires exact MMS values, MMS partitions, and an exact capped-welfare maximization. Section 6 explicitly concedes that it is not necessarily polynomial-time. Thus the continuous mirror would not be relaxing a stated complexity theorem; it would introduce a new configuration-allocation problem whose pricing and benchmark-computation questions are absent from the paper.

The deterministic anchor nevertheless comes closest to surviving. If one keeps the paper’s finite set of indivisible items and merely repeats agents of a few valuation types, the limit degenerates: once the number of agents exceeds the number of goods, many MMS values become zero or the guarantee becomes infeasible. To retain a nontrivial benchmark, one must also scale the goods. But then the model needs valuations on multisets or count vectors, not the paper’s \(v_i:2^{\items}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\). The proponent’s \(S\subseteq B\) formulation cannot even represent more than one copy of an item kind. Extending XOS valuations to repeated goods is plausible, but it is a new modelling choice, with several inequivalent possibilities. The resulting configuration LP is therefore a new large-market fair-division problem, not a direct continuous version of Theorem ExPost.

That objection is limited: the programme expressly permits per-capita supplies and natural co-modifications, so it cannot honestly be treated as a fatal scope violation. Once repeated goods and count-vector XOS types are admitted, the deterministic mirror is well posed and potentially interesting. The negative case cannot defeat it by saying that the answer may be hard or that the theorem may fail; those would be precisely the results worth studying.

The randomized anchor is substantially weaker. In a nonatomic population, a randomized allocation and an aggregate deterministic allocation are equivalent. Given masses \(x_{t,S}\), a type-\(t\) agent receives bundle \(S\) with probability \(x_{t,S}/\mu_t\); conversely, any lottery induces such masses. Ex-ante utility is

\[ \sum_S \frac{x_{t,S}}{\mu_t}v_t(S), \]

and the ex-post condition merely requires \(x_{t,S}=0\) for bundles below the threshold. Thus Theorem ExAnteExPost’s randomization, including its half-integral matching construction, disappears into measurable splitting of type mass. If one insists on preserving a lottery for each named individual, one has reintroduced identities that the continuous type model was supposed to remove. Lemma 3.3’s \(3/4\) upper bound similarly becomes an ordinary aggregate packing obstruction, not a distinctive randomized-population phenomenon.

So the best case against is a narrow one: the randomized theorem has no genuinely new continuous content, and the deterministic theorem is not a computational-complexity theorem but an existence result requiring substantial new modelling. That is not enough for the requested universal negative. Under the programme’s rules, the high-multiplicity deterministic MMS mirror remains a credible and worthwhile anchor; only the claim that the paper offers a compelling randomized mirror should be rejected.
