The continuous mirror question

Given a path of \(m\) standardized chore locations with per-capita supplies \(\rho_v\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_v\rho_v=1\), finitely many complete additive disutility types \(T\), rational type masses \(\mu_t\), population scale \(\lambda\), and \(c\ge1\), choose mass variables \(x_{t,I}\ge0\) for every path interval \(I\) and the empty bundle, subject to \(\sum_Ix_{t,I}=\lambda\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,I\ni v}x_{t,I}=\lambda\rho_v\), and \(x_{t,I}>0\Rightarrow u_t(I)\le cU_t(\lambda\rho)/\lambda\), where \(U_t(\lambda\rho)=\sum_v\lambda\rho_vu_t(v)\); decide whether such a mass allocation exists.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity connected chore-allocation model in which type \(t\) carries mass \(\lambda\mu_t\), each individual receives an integral connected path interval, each location has chore mass \(\lambda\rho_v\), and the variables \(x_{t,I}\) define a polynomial-size feasibility LP.

The objection that survived

With unit supply and threshold \(c/\lambda\), almost all agents become empty and positive-cost intervals eventually become forbidden; denominator clearing also needs an explicit repeated-location construction and proportionality normalization. This bounds the stated formulation but does not kill the repaired mirror.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 4.2 and its path additive proportionality problem, but not Theorems 4.4 or 5.10, the other fairness notions, or the star and complete-graph cases.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s path proportionality problem. It is a genuine population continuization, and it turns the paper’s discrete hardness into a natural polynomial-size LP.

My anchor is Theorem 4.2, proved in this paper using the reduction established in Lemma 4.1. It states that, for every \(c\ge1\), deciding whether a connected chore instance on a path admits an allocation satisfying \(u_i^{\mathrm{add}}(\pi(i))\le c/n\) for every agent has no polynomial-time algorithm unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). In particular, \({\sc Add\mbox{-}Prop\mbox{-}CCD}\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete even when the chore graph is a path. The reduction is from \((2,2)\)-E3-SAT and uses additive, normalized disutilities together with connectivity of every assigned bundle.

The mirror I would propose is called Continuous Additive Proportional Chore Division on a Path.

The graph is a path \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\), representing ordered rooms, work periods, or locations. The society has finitely many complete agent types \(T\). A type \(t\) specifies the entire disutility vector \(u_t(v)\), together with any eligibility or cost parameters used by the allocation problem. The population is described by rational type fractions \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), and a population scale \(\lambda\). Thus type \(t\) has mass \(\lambda\mu_t\). The intended regime is \(\lambda\gg\tau=|T|\): millions of workers or teams, but only a modest number of recurring disutility profiles.

Each infinitesimal agent receives an integral bundle of chore locations. Because the graph is a path, every nonempty bundle is an interval \(I=[a,b]\). The additive disutility of type \(t\) for that bundle is \(u_t(I)=\sum_{v\in I}u_t(v)\). The continuum allocation is described by variables \(x_{t,I}\ge0\), where \(x_{t,I}\) is the mass of type \(t\) receiving interval \(I\). Empty bundles are also allowed, as they are in the paper’s constructions.

The exact continuous problem is:

\[ \sum_I x_{t,I}=\lambda\mu_t \]

for every type \(t\), and

\[ \sum_t\sum_{I\ni v}x_{t,I}=1 \]

for every path vertex \(v\). The second constraint says that the unit chore supply at every location is allocated completely and exactly once at aggregate scale. Finally, every interval in the support must satisfy

\[ x_{t,I}>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_t(I)\le \frac{c}{\lambda}. \]

The question is whether such a mass allocation exists. A solution is the finite rational table \(x=(x_{t,I})\), not a fractional bundle: every column \(I\) is still an integral connected bundle, and only the amount of population assigned to each bundle is fractional.

This is recognisably the authors’ problem. It retains their path, their connected-share requirement, their additive disutilities, their normalization, and their proportionality threshold. The only change is that named agents of the same complete type are replaced by a mass of interchangeable agents. It is therefore a high-multiplicity version of \({\sc Add\mbox{-}Prop\mbox{-}CCD}\), not an unrelated divisible-chores problem.

The continuous version is tractable. A path has only \(O(m^2)\) connected bundles, so the formulation above has \(O(\tau m^2)\) variables and polynomially many rational constraints. It is a linear-feasibility problem and can be solved in polynomial time. Thus the expected classification is Class A: the paper’s discrete \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness disappears after continuization.

The reason is substantive. The finite reduction forces particular agents to make all-or-nothing choices between intervals encoding truth values. In the continuum, mass of one type may be divided between several individually fair intervals. The SAT choices are replaced by fractional interval-packing constraints. The graph and the integral nature of each individual bundle remain; what disappears is the indivisibility of the population assignment.

The high-multiplicity bridge is also explicit. Any rational solution \(x\) can have its denominators cleared: with \(q\) copies of every type and \(q\) copies of every path-location chore, \(qx_{t,I}\) agents can receive interval \(I\). Conversely, every such repeated finite allocation induces an \(x\)-table. This makes the continuum the limit of repeated populations and repeated standardized task locations, rather than an arbitrary fractional relaxation.

A plausible application is a large cleaning or maintenance service assigning many interchangeable teams to ordered work zones or successive time blocks. Two teams have the same type when they have the same disutility for every standardized location, the same eligibility, and the same relevant costs. A regime with \(\lambda\) in the millions and \(\tau\) in the tens or hundreds is entirely credible.

The weak point is clear: the original paper speaks of individually indivisible chores, whereas the continuum requires a mass of repeated chore copies at each graph location. If “one unique physical room” must remain literally unique, then a continuum of agents is degenerate: almost everyone receives an empty bundle. The proposed mirror therefore relies on the programme’s permitted co-modification, interpreting a vertex as a standardized location containing high-multiplicity task units. That is a real modelling commitment, not a technical trick. Its defence is that every individual still receives an integral connected bundle; only the population and the repeated supply are continuous.

This case deliberately covers only Theorem 4.2. Natural follow-up mirrors would ask whether the path hardness of Theorem 4.4 for binary maximum-based envy-freeness survives population continuization, and whether the Partition-based star hardness of Theorem 5.10 survives when the central-agent bottleneck and leaf supplies are given high-multiplicity interpretations.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case attacks the proposed formulation’s high-multiplicity interpretation, not Theorem 4.2 itself.

The LP silently needs an empty-bundle variable \(x_{t,\varnothing}\), because the paper allows agents to receive nothing. With one unit of each of the \(m\) chores, every nonempty interval consumes at least one unit of chore mass. Hence

\[ \sum_{t}\sum_{I\neq\varnothing}x_{t,I}\le m. \]

If \(\lambda\gg m\), at most mass \(m\) of a population of mass \(\lambda\) receives any chore at all. Almost the entire society is empty. At the same time, the proportionality threshold \(c/\lambda\) tends to zero. For a fixed finite type space and rational disutilities, once \(\lambda\) is sufficiently large, every positive-cost interval is forbidden; only zero-cost intervals remain admissible. The alleged continuum therefore degenerates into a finite zero-cost covering problem for a vanishing fringe of the population.

That is not the high-multiplicity regime represented by the paper’s hardness construction. There, \(m=5s+t\) and \(n=c(5s+t)+1\), so the number of agents is tied to the number of chores. The Boolean gadget uses the finite crowding between those two quantities. Replicating the agent types while retaining one copy of every chore merely gives the additional agents empty bundles and destroys the gadget’s forcing mechanism. Thus the proponent has not shown that Theorem 4.2 has a meaningful \(\lambda\gg\tau\) interpretation.

The claimed denominator-clearing bridge also needs more work. Clearing denominators in \(x\) produces multiple copies of every path location. Those copies are not the paper’s original path vertices: placing them consecutively creates new connected intervals, while treating them as parallel copies requires a new graph model. Utility normalization must also be respecified. The statement that “\(q\) copies of every path-location chore” gives the corresponding finite instance is therefore not automatic.

The natural repair is to scale chore supply with population, say by replacing the unit-supply constraint with

\[ \sum_{t,I\ni v}x_{t,I}=\lambda\rho_v. \]

That avoids the empty-population degeneration and is permitted by the programme’s co-modification rule. But it also exposes the limit of the negative case. With this repair, integral intervals can still be assigned to mass of interchangeable types, and the resulting problem is a perfectly coherent high-multiplicity continuous version of connected chore division. On a path it has only \(O(\tau m^2)\) interval configurations and is a polynomial-size LP. The fact that the answer changes, or that the LP is easy, is not a valid objection under the programme’s rules.

Thus the negative case genuinely defeats the proponent’s formulation as written: fixed finite chore supply plus \(\lambda\gg\tau\) makes the society mostly empty and does not preserve the theorem’s finite bridge. But it cannot honestly defeat the best repaired mirror with population-scaled task supply. The universal claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists is therefore weak; Theorem 4.2 remains a credible Class A anchor, provided the proponents state the supply scaling, repeated-location graph, and utility normalization explicitly.
