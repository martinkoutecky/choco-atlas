The case FOR (proponent)

The paper has no qualifying computational anchor under your rule. Its named theorems concern statistical guarantees: the Heterogeneous Efficiency Guarantee compares estimator covariance, the robustness result bounds squared error, and the Optimality Guarantee gives a probability that calibrated scores beat raw model scores. None classifies the complexity of a problem as polynomial-time, NP-hard, FPT, or similar. The paper describes PAV and iterative likelihood updates, but gives no numbered complexity result for them. Most theorem references in the supplied text are unresolved; the robustness result is called “Theorem 3.5” in the prose.

The strongest plausible extension is a Continuous Annotator-Type AtC problem. Consider a large assessment platform with many annotators but relatively few complete rater types: each type has a shared reliability parameter and comparison-exposure pattern. Let \(T\) be the type support, \(\mu_t\) each type’s population fraction, \(w_{tij}\) its comparison rate for items \(i,j\), and \(q_{tij}\) the fraction of those comparisons preferring \(i\). Given model scores \(p_i\), estimate consensus scores \(s_i\) and type reliabilities \(\gamma_t\) by maximizing

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\sum_{i<j}w_{tij}\left[ q_{tij}\log F\!\left(\gamma_t(s_i-s_j)\right) +(1-q_{tij})\log\!\left(1-F\!\left(\gamma_t(s_i-s_j)\right)\right) \right], \]

with the paper’s identifiability constraints. Then output calibrated scores \(z_i\) minimizing \(\sum_i(z_i-p_i)^2\), subject to the consensus order of the fitted \(s\). An instance gives the rational type masses, comparison rates and frequencies, model scores, and parameter bounds; a solution gives a global maximum-likelihood estimate and its calibrated score vector.

This is a credible high-multiplicity regime: a large crowd can contain many annotators but only a modest number of recurring reliability and assignment profiles. It preserves the paper’s Stage-1 question, and its Heterogeneous Efficiency Guarantee—the heterogeneous estimator has no larger asymptotic covariance than the homogeneous estimator, strictly smaller under genuine heterogeneity—supports the relevance of type-level heterogeneity. Once the order is known, the calibration step is weighted isotonic regression and is tractable by PAV. The complexity of the joint estimation problem remains unclassified: the likelihood couples \(s_i\) and \(\gamma_t\), and the paper’s iterative updates do not establish an efficient global algorithm or hardness result.

That last issue is also the weakness of the positive case. This is a plausible continuous extension of the paper’s statistical model, not a mirror anchored in a named computational result of the paper. The crowd-as-society interpretation is recognizable, but the article studies score estimation rather than a computational social choice problem with a stated complexity landscape.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There is no computational result in this paper to mirror. The Heterogeneous Efficiency Guarantee compares asymptotic covariance matrices; the robustness and optimality theorems bound estimation error and improvement probability. The paper gives iterative updates for Stage 1 and describes PAV for Stage 2, but proves no complexity classification for either. The proponent’s own qualification is therefore decisive under ChoCo’s stated criteria: a new complexity question about this framework would not be a mirror of a computational result in the paper.

The best proposed mirror is still coherent: a large assessment platform could have many annotators sharing a small number of reliability and comparison-exposure types. But with those types fixed, grouping annotators replaces repeated likelihood terms with their counts or frequencies. The proposed weighted likelihood is the original likelihood, aggregated over identical observations and scaled by the population size. The fitted scores and calibrated outputs still concern the same \(n\) named items; Stage 2 remains \(n\)-dimensional isotonic regression. The annotator mass changes the statistical weights and information, but does not supply a new computational structure in the sense ChoCo is pursuing.

Nor does the named efficiency theorem establish that mirror. It models annotator-specific parameters \(\gamma_u\); the proposed version shares one \(\gamma_t\) across each type, changing the nuisance-parameter model. The covariance comparison would need to be re-derived for that model, and even a matching result would remain a statistical efficiency statement, not an algorithm or hardness result. A more ambitious continuous distribution over reliabilities and exposure patterns would be a new statistical estimation problem, potentially infinite-dimensional, rather than a computational mirror of the theorem.

I would not claim that annotator multiplicity is implausible: crowdsourcing gives a sensible high-multiplicity scenario, and the proposal could support useful statistical work. The negative case is narrower. The case for this paper rests on extending its statistical model and then asking a new optimization-complexity question; it does not identify a paper result whose computational complexity continuization would chart. If ChoCo includes that broader kind of project, the mirror may be worth exploring. Under the programme’s stated focus, this is an adjacent research direction, not a strong candidate.
