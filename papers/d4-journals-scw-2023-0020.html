The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make a compliant positive case from this paper: it has no named computational result of the kind the brief requires. Its numbered theorems establish whether voting-power measures satisfy blocker postulates. For example, Theorem 3.3 says the Recursive Measure satisfies the strong subadditivity blocker postulate, and Theorem 4.3 says it satisfies the strong minimum-power blocker postulate. Neither is a complexity result or an algorithm for a computational problem. The recursive definition of the measure does not itself supply such an anchor.

The nearest plausible high-multiplicity setting would be a large weighted-voting institution with many shareholders sharing a voting weight. A type could be a weight and formal role; its mass would be its fraction of the population. One could then ask for a continuous or limiting vector of type-level Recursive Measure scores. But that is a new computational question, absent from the paper, and the individual pivotality and blocker notions may not survive when each person has zero mass. A distribution over yes/no divisions would retain a finite set of players and continuize the profile distribution instead of the population.

So there is no defensible anchor, lead problem, or per-anchor tractability prediction here. The weak point in any affirmative attempt is that it must replace the paper’s axiomatic question with a newly invented computation. That might be a worthwhile extension, but it is not a continuous mirror of a named computational result in this paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case is the paper’s subject: it states no computational result to continuize. Theorems 3.1–5.3 classify whether PB, SS, and RM satisfy axioms about voting power. They give no complexity bound or algorithm for bribery, control, winner robustness, or another computational problem. The proponent names no theorem that can anchor a mirror; their suggested type-level RM scores would introduce a new computation question.

The weighted-institution example does establish a plausible high-multiplicity regime: many voters can share a weight and role. The obstacle is what the paper measures. Its scores attach to individual players across the finite set of \(2^n\) vote divisions, and its blocker postulates track individual veto power and blocs formed by vote donation. In a nonatomic population, an individual has zero mass, so unilateral decisiveness disappears. The finite uniform distribution over divisions also has no direct counterpart as a uniform distribution over measurable coalitions. RM adds another finite structure: its efficacy recursively averages over a division’s loyal children. The paper supplies no canonical continuum version of that recursion.

One could define a type-level aggregate power or choose a limit of finite games. Either choice would require new definitions and a new computational question; the paper’s postulate results would provide motivation, not results to mirror. That could support a separate high-multiplicity project. The negative case is strongest for this paper as a mirror, and weaker as a claim that no worthwhile project could ever grow from it.
