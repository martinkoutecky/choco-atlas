The continuous mirror question

Given candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over approval types \(A\subseteq C\), with support \(\mathcal A\) of size \(\tau\), can one compute in time polynomial in \(|C|,\tau,L\) a size-\(k\) committee \(W\) satisfying core+ at \(q_*=\frac12\left(\frac1k+\frac1{k+1}\right)\)? Equivalently, find payments \(p_{A,c}\ge0\) for \(c\in A\cap W\) and reserves \(r_A\ge0\) such that \(r_A+\sum_{c\in A\cap W}p_{A,c}=1\), \(p_{A,c}\le r_A\), \(\sum_{A:c\in A}\mu_Ap_{A,c}\le q_*\) for each \(c\in W\), and \(\sum_{A:d\in A}\mu_A r_A<q_*\) for each \(d\notin W\).

The model it lives in

Types are approval sets \(A\) carrying mass \(\mu_A\); the chosen committee remains integral. For each committee \(W\), optimize the weighted harmonic-entropy objective \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A F((p_{A,c})_{c\in A\cap W},r_A)\) over type-level payment vectors and reserves subject to the weighted quota constraints, then use candidate replacements to find a core+ committee in time polynomial in \(|C|,\tau,L\).

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror is the algorithmic result, not the paper’s entropy objective by itself. I find no named hardness result in the paper to anchor on, so the lead is its polynomial-time result.

Lead mirror: Type-Compressed Continuous Core+ Search. The anchor is Theorem 7.1, proved here: “A committee satisfying core+ can be computed in polynomial time. More precisely, for \(q=\frac12\left(\frac nk+\frac n{k+1}\right)\), the algorithm below performs at most \(4k(k+1)H_k\) candidate replacements.” The theorem concerns finding a size-\(k\) committee satisfying core+ at that quota; the paper’s local-search algorithm gives the stated replacement bound.

For the mirror, an instance consists of candidates \(C\), committee size \(k\), and a rational distribution \(\mu\) over approval types \(A\subseteq C\). Its support \(\mathcal A\) has size \(\tau\), and \(\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}\mu_A=1\). A type is a complete approval set: its members have the same approvals and therefore the same utility \(u_A(W)=|A\cap W|\) for every committee \(W\). The normalized quota matching Theorem 7.1 is
\[ q_*=\frac12\left(\frac1k+\frac1{k+1}\right). \]

The task is to return a size-\(k\) committee \(W\) satisfying continuous core+ at quota \(q_*\). Concretely, it must admit per-unit-type payments \(p_{A,c}\ge 0\) for approved winners \(c\in A\cap W\), and reserves \(r_A\ge 0\), such that
\[ r_A+\sum_{c\in A\cap W}p_{A,c}=1,\qquad p_{A,c}\le r_A, \]
\[ \sum_{A:c\in A}\mu_Ap_{A,c}\le q_* \quad(c\in W),\qquad \sum_{A:d\in A}\mu_Ar_A<q_* \quad(d\notin W). \]
Equivalently, there must be no fractional blocking deviation: type masses \(y_A\in[0,\mu_A]\), candidate proposal levels \(z_c\in[0,1]\), and assignment masses \(x_{A,c}\ge0\) satisfying
\[ \sum_{c\in A}x_{A,c}=(|A\cap W|+1)y_A,\quad x_{A,c}\le\mu_Az_c,\quad x_{A,w}\le y_A\ (w\in A\cap W), \]
\[ \sum_A y_A\ge q_*\sum_c z_c,\qquad \sum_Ay_A>0. \]
The payment conditions are the type-weighted counterpart of the paper’s core+ certificate.

This is a plausible mirror of the authors’ problem: it keeps their approval ballots, integral committee, utilities, quota-based proportionality, and strengthened core criterion. The many agents are members of a large association or electorate choosing a small committee. Their ballot is their complete type; if millions of members share a few hundred approval sets, then \(n\gg\tau\). Their model uses only approvals, so grouping identical approval sets loses no information. The normalized quota and the \(\mu_A\)-weighted payment caps are the natural per-capita scaling of the paper’s model.

I expect this problem to be Class A, tractable, with running time polynomial in \(|C|,\tau\), and the binary encoding length of \(\mu\). This extension is not stated as Theorem 7.1 in the paper: the theorem uses an explicitly listed electorate. But its proof appears to transfer cleanly. For a fixed approval type, averaging voters’ payment vectors preserves feasibility, and concavity of harmonic entropy means it does not decrease the objective. Thus the truncated objective LP can use one payment vector per supported type, weighted by \(\mu_A\), rather than one per voter. The paper’s local-search argument then has the same \(4k(k+1)H_k\) replacement bound; each LP has size polynomial in \(\tau\) and \(k\), rather than \(n\). Here the population distribution is the continuous object. The committee remains integral, and payment optimization is the machinery used to find it.

The scope of this case is deliberately narrow: it mirrors Theorem 7.1’s computation of a core+ committee at its midpoint quota. It does not claim a polynomial-time result for every quota in the paper, or for other results about approval-based representation. Natural follow-up questions are whether the type-compressed algorithm works at the Hare quota \(1/k\) or throughout the full quota interval, and which extensions to type-dependent utilities or budgets preserve tractability.

The weakest point is that this is not a discrete-hardness result whose difficulty might dissolve in a continuum. The paper’s anchored result is already polynomial-time, and the proposed contribution is a type-compressed high-multiplicity version of its algorithm. That makes the case less useful for testing where continuization removes hardness. It still seems like an honest mirror: it asks for the same core+ outcome in a genuinely population-based input regime, with complexity measured in the number of distinct types and the encoding length of their masses.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only serious negative case is about marginal novelty. The mirror is nearly an aggregation corollary of Theorem 7.1, rather than a new continuous phenomenon. For a finite election with \(n_A\) voters of approval type \(A\), aggregate the paper’s fractional blocking variables and divide by \(n\):
\[ \mu_A=\frac{n_A}{n},\qquad y_A=\frac1n\sum_{i\in A}y_i,\qquad x_{A,c}=\frac1n\sum_{i\in A}x_{i,c}. \]
The finite constraints give \(y_A\le\mu_A\), \(x_{A,c}\le\mu_A z_c\), and \(x_{A,w}\le y_A\), with the quota scaled from \(n/k\) to \(1/k\). Conversely, any such type-level variables can be distributed evenly among the \(n_A\) voters. Thus the continuous core+ obstruction system is exactly the paper’s fractional system on a replicated electorate.

The computation compresses just as directly. For voters with the same approval set, average their payment vectors. Feasibility is preserved, and concavity of \(F\) means this averaging cannot lower the objective. So the entropy LP can use one vector per type, weighted by \(\mu_A\); the local-search proof and replacement bound scale with the population total. The remaining computational contribution is a compact-input bound in \((|C|,\tau,L)\), rather than a new optimization method or structural insight.

That is a credible reason to call this a low-yield mirror: the paper already uses fractional deviations, Farkas certificates, and linear programming, so the continuous version does not bring an unfamiliar toolbox to the problem. But it does not defeat the anchor. Large electorates with recurring approval types are plausible; approval sets fully describe voters for this model; the committee stays integral; and the type-weighted problem is well posed. Nor does divisible-committee Lindahl work settle this question, since it changes the outcome space.

So I cannot make an honest case that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario. The mirror’s novelty may be mostly a succinct high-multiplicity encoding, but the programme explicitly treats complexity in \(\tau\) and bit length as part of the question. Under that standard, Theorem 7.1’s type-compressed version is a sound mirror, and the negative case is weak.
