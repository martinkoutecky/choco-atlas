The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There are no anchors to defeat: the proponent names none, and their central objection is sound. Theorem 3.1 gives a unique formula for the interval Owen value; Theorem 4.1 does so for a special game class. The remaining theorems establish allocation, monotonicity, and core properties. None states an algorithmic or complexity result. That leaves no computational result in this paper for ChoCo to continuize.

The strongest attempted mirror would be to ask how hard it is to compute the interval Owen value. But the paper allows arbitrary coalition values: with \(n\) players, those values are specified over an exponentially large family of coalitions. In an atomless population, the analogous game assigns values across an uncountable family of measurable coalitions, with no finite input representation supplied by the paper. One could restrict games to a compact, structured class and compute a per-capita allocation density. That would be a plausible new continuous cooperative-game problem, but its tractability would come from the added representation and its allocation semantics—not from a computational result here. A large finite population of interchangeable players also needs a rule for extending coalition values and coalition structures as the population grows; the paper provides none.

This is a strong case against a *mirror of this paper* as a computational contribution. It is weaker as the universal claim that no scenario could ever produce a worthwhile continuous question: structured population games might do so, but they would need substantial modeling choices before there is a finite computational problem to study.
