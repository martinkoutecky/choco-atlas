What the mirror covers

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is that this paper supplies a very plausible *subject* for a high-multiplicity computational problem. But it supplies no qualifying computational anchor, and its central model already treats the population as continuous.

The closest structural hooks are Proposition 1, proved here, which says that under Condition TP both welfare- and profit-maximizing matching rules have a threshold structure; and Proposition 2, also proved here, which characterizes optimal rules under TP and MR: they are complete when the marginal surplus condition is nonnegative, and otherwise maximally separating, with thresholds satisfying an Euler equation. These are economic characterization results. Neither asserts that a computational problem is in P, hard, FPT, or otherwise has a complexity classification. So, under the requested anchor rule, the paper has no named computational result to anchor a mirror on.

A plausible follow-on problem would be Atomic High-Multiplicity Matching Design. Take two sides, with finitely many types on each side. Type \(i\) on side \(A\) has rational mass \(\mu_{Ai}\), valuation \(v_{Ai}\), and salience \(\sigma_{Ai}\); define the corresponding data for side \(B\). Each side’s masses sum to one. Let \(y_{ij}\in\{0,1\}\) say whether every member of type \(i\) is matched to every member of type \(j\). Then the matching intensities are
\[ q_{Ai}=\sum_j \mu_{Bj}\sigma_{Bj}y_{ij}, \qquad q_{Bj}=\sum_i \mu_{Ai}\sigma_{Ai}y_{ij}. \]
Given rational, piecewise-linear matching utility functions \(g_A,g_B\), choose \(y\) and payments \(p_{ki}\) to maximize
\[ \sum_i\mu_{Ai}v_{Ai}g_A(q_{Ai}) +\sum_j\mu_{Bj}v_{Bj}g_B(q_{Bj}), \]
subject to individual rationality and incentive compatibility:
\[ v_{ki}g_k(q_{ki})-p_{ki} \ge v_{ki}g_k(q_{ki'})-p_{ki'} \]
for every type \(i\) and alternative report \(i'\) on the same side, and truthful utility at least zero. The output is an optimal reciprocal matching matrix and payment vector.

A plausible regime is a large advertising exchange with very many browsers and advertisers but relatively few standardized cohorts—say, cohorts sharing advertising tolerance and campaigns sharing willingness-to-pay and salience. The masses represent population shares, and the number of agents is much larger than the number of types. Types must include every characteristic the mechanism uses, so this grouping is the high-multiplicity assumption rather than an additional simplification of it.

I think the authors would recognize this as a finite-support, high-multiplicity version of their problem: it retains reciprocal matching, private vertical valuations, screening, and the welfare objective. My tentative expectation is Class A for a finite-support version of the TP regime, because the paper’s threshold structure may reduce the search over match relations substantially. That is a hypothesis for the new computational problem, though—not a result of this paper. A first question for a prover would be whether the threshold characterization survives atomic type masses and, if so, yields an exact algorithm polynomial in the number of types and input bit length.

The obstacle is substantial: Gomes and Pavan already model each side as a unit-mass continuum with distributions over types and optimize over measurable matching rules. That is a direct continuous-population formulation, not merely fractional outcomes or noise over a discrete population. Thus a mirror that simply replaces their population by a continuum would restate their model. The finite-support computational problem above is a plausible distinct extension, but it does not give this paper a named computational anchor.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that this paper has already made the population continuous. Each side is a unit-mass continuum, types are drawn from absolutely continuous distributions, and the platform chooses among measurable matching rules. That is the object continuization would produce. The paper gives an analytical treatment of it, but it does not supply a discrete computational result whose continuous mirror this programme could classify.

That defeats both proposed anchors. Proposition 1 characterizes when an optimal rule has a threshold structure; Proposition 2 characterizes the optimal thresholds through an Euler equation. Neither is a complexity result, and neither begins with a finite-population computational problem. The paper also gives no finite encoding for its distributions and utility functions, so asking for bit complexity would require an additional input model.

The proposed finite-support matching problem could be a sensible adjacent project, but it changes the problem the propositions solve. It replaces absolutely continuous type distributions with atoms, and finite-report incentive constraints with the paper’s constraints over a continuum of possible reports. Establishing whether thresholds survive that change would itself be a new result. It would not be a computational mirror of Proposition 1 or 2. Encoding the paper’s existing continuous model with, say, rational piecewise-polynomial primitives might support a worthwhile algorithmic study, but that would computationalize an already continuous model rather than continuize a discrete one.

I would not claim that no algorithmic study of this matching problem could be worthwhile. The more defensible negative case is that this paper offers no qualifying computational anchor, and its central population model is already continuous. The proponent’s construction may motivate new work, but it does not establish a continuous mirror of this paper’s results.
