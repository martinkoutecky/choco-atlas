The continuous mirror question

Given a finite anonymous typed TU game with type set \(T=\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\), rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\), normalized grand worth \(W\), and per-unit grand-marginal contributions \(b_t\), find \(x\in\mathbb R^T\) minimizing \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t(b_t-x_t)^2\) subject to \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_t=W\).

The model it lives in

Types are complete game-relevant player descriptions, mass \(\mu_t\) replaces repeated players, and \(x_t\) is the common per-member allocation. The unique solution is \(x_t=b_t+\left(W-\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s b_s\right)\), the mass-weighted analogue of the ENSC projection.

The objection that survived

The thesis supplies no compact encoding for an anonymous \(V\), so a prover must specify whether \(W\) and \(b_t\) are input data or oracle-derived; this limits computational depth but does not invalidate the projection.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 2.1 and its ENSC projection. It leaves the other optimization characterizations, axioms, bidding mechanism, polluted-river identity, general compromise values, and stochastic-payoff results aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • What compact representation or oracle for the anonymous worth function makes \(W\) and \(b_t\) computationally available?
  • Does imposing core feasibility over coalition-mass vectors produce a nontrivial continuous extension?
  • How should grand-marginal contributions be defined when the type space itself is continuous?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is a qualified one. The thesis admits a natural high-multiplicity mirror, but it contains no named theorem asserting NP-hardness, membership in P, FPT, or any comparable complexity classification. Thus, under a strict ChoCo anchor rule, this is not a fully compliant computational anchor. The closest usable anchor is an exact optimization theorem.

The anchor is Theorem 2.1, proved in the thesis. It states that for every cooperative game \(v\in\Gamma_n\), Problem 2.1 has a unique solution: minimize \(\sum_{i\in N}(\bar v_i-x_i)^2\) subject to \(x\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\sum_i x_i=v(N)\), where \(\bar v_i=v(N)-v(N\setminus\{i\})\) is player \(i\)'s grand marginal contribution. The theorem proves that the solution is the ENSC value, namely \(x_i^*=\bar v_i+\frac{1}{n}\left(v(N)-\sum_j\bar v_j\right)\).

My mirror would be the following.

Continuous ENSC Projection. A society consists of finitely many player types \(T\), with mass distribution \(\mu\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{T}\) satisfying \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). A type completely specifies a member’s role in the cooperative enterprise: contribution technology, costs, singleton worth, and all other game-relevant characteristics. There may be \(N\mu_t\) physical members of type \(t\), with \(N\) large and \(|T|=\tau\) small.

Let \(W\) be the normalized worth of the grand coalition and let \(b_t\) be the per-unit grand marginal contribution of type \(t\). Equivalently, if the anonymous high-multiplicity game is represented by a worth function \(V(z)\) over coalition-mass vectors, then \(b_t\) is the per-unit marginal \( \partial_tV(\mu) \), when this derivative exists.

The decision variable is a per-member allocation \(x_t\) to each type. The problem is

\[ \min_{x\in\mathbb R^T}\ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t(b_t-x_t)^2 \]

subject to mass efficiency,

\[ \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t x_t=W. \]

A solution is the exact type-level allocation \(x\), assigning \(x_t\) to every member of type \(t\). The unique solution is

\[ x_t^*=b_t+\left(W-\sum_{s\in T}\mu_s b_s\right) \]

because total mass is normalized to \(1\). For total mass \(M\), the parenthesized correction is divided by \(M\).

This is a genuine population continuization. The finite objective becomes a mass-weighted integral, individual players are replaced by indistinguishable type mass, and the efficiency constraint becomes mass efficiency. It is not merely fractionalizing an outcome while retaining a finite society. A finite instance with \(n_t\) members of type \(t\) maps to \(\mu_t=n_t/N\), and the discrete objective divided by \(N\) converges exactly to the displayed objective.

The regime is plausible in the thesis’ own applications: a large river-cleaning or infrastructure consortium can contain many firms with the same location class, pollution profile, contractual role, and contribution technology. The polluted-river discussion is especially helpful here, although I would not use Theorem 4.1 as a second anchor: it is a sharing identity rather than a complexity result. In the high-multiplicity river regime, local costs become per-unit cost densities and aggregate downstream costs are divided by upstream mass, which is precisely the sort of co-modification that continuization naturally requires.

I would expect Continuous ENSC Projection to be Class A. Once \(W\) and the type-level marginals \(b_t\) are available, it is a strictly convex quadratic program with one equality constraint and has the closed form above, computable in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and the input precision. Further questions include whether the same conclusion survives core constraints over all coalition masses, whether the lexicographic result of Theorem 2.4 has a meaningful nonatomic analogue, and what happens when the type space itself becomes continuous.

The weakest point is substantial: Theorem 2.1 is an optimization characterization, not a complexity theorem, and the thesis does not study compact representations of anonymous high-multiplicity games. Moreover, replacing deletion of one player by a derivative of \(V\) is a natural limit operation, but it is not literally present in the paper. If ChoCo requires a named NP-hardness, P-membership, FPT, or explicit algorithmic-encoding result, then this thesis has no qualifying anchor and should receive no formal positive verdict. As a substantive mirror, however, the continuous ENSC allocation problem is faithful, mathematically precise, and recognizably the high-multiplicity version of the paper’s central optimization problem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is strongest on the programme’s formal gate: this thesis contains no named computational result. Theorem 2.1 is an optimization characterization, not a complexity theorem, algorithmic classification, approximation result, or parameterized result. Problem 2.1 is already a one-constraint Euclidean projection with the closed-form solution
\[ x_i=\bar v_i+\frac{v(N)-\sum_j\bar v_j}{n}. \]
Thus the proposed anchor is not, strictly speaking, a ChoCo anchor at all.

Even as a substantive mirror, the proposed model changes the thesis more than it first appears. The thesis works over arbitrary characteristic functions \(v:2^N\to\mathbb R\). In such a game, two firms with the same local technology or pollution profile need not be interchangeable: their worth can depend on their identity and on coalition-specific complementarities. A complete type would therefore have to encode the player’s behaviour in every coalition. For generic games, that produces essentially one type per player, eliminating the high-multiplicity regime. If instead one assumes an anonymous worth function \(V(z)\), that is a substantial restriction to a new class of games, not a high-multiplicity form supplied by the paper.

The proposed continuous problem also takes the difficult game-theoretic quantities as already available. The values \(W\) and \(b_t\) are not primitive population data; in the finite theorem,
\[ b_i=v(N)-v(N\setminus\{i\}). \]
Replacing this deletion by \(\partial_tV(\mu)\) requires choosing a population-scaling convention, an anonymous extension \(V\), regularity assumptions, and an encoding or oracle for \(V\). Without those choices, the derivative is not determined by the thesis. With them, the projection itself is trivial; any genuine computational question has been hidden in computing \(V\), \(W\), or the \(b_t\).

Indeed, after imposing type symmetry, the proposed objective is exactly the finite theorem with repeated coordinates:
\[ \frac1N\sum_{i\in N}(\bar v_i-x_i)^2 =\sum_t\mu_t(b_t-x_t)^2. \]
The continuum has only changed multiplicities into weights in an already separable quadratic. It does not expose a pricing problem, a coalition-separation problem, or any computational phenomenon caused by population multiplicity. The finite problem was already solved in closed form, so this is a valid weighted restatement but a very weak computational mirror.

The strongest repair would be to add anonymous core constraints over all coalition-mass vectors. That could become an interesting continuous cooperative-game problem, but it is no longer Theorem 2.1: the paper’s problem has no core constraint, only an efficiency hyperplane. One would also have to supply a compact representation of \(V\) and a separation oracle over coalition masses. At that point the worthwhile question is newly designed rather than extracted from a computational result of the thesis.

The river application does not rescue the anchor. A continuum of firms along a river may be a sensible modelling exercise, but the relevant Chapter 4 result is the identity
\[ \xi^\alpha=\alpha x^{\mathrm{LRS}}+(1-\alpha)x^{\mathrm{UES}}, \]
not a computational theorem. Turning segment counts into mass densities and upstream counts into cumulative mass may yield a coherent new allocation rule, but it still supplies no complexity landscape and does not make Theorem 2.1 computationally substantive.

So the proponent has established that a mathematically coherent mass-weighted projection can be invented. They have not established a worthwhile continuous-population mirror of this paper in ChoCo’s sense. The formal anchor fails decisively; the best substantive repair either remains a tautological weighted projection or becomes a new anonymous cooperative-game programme whose computational input and separation machinery are absent from the thesis.
