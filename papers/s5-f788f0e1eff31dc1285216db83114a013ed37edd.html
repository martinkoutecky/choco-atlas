What the mirror covers

The population reformulation covers the consistency and completeness claims in Theorems 2 and 8; it leaves the paper's logical semantics and future-work complexity questions without a computational mirror.

The case FOR (proponent)

A continuous population is natural for the paper’s judgment aggregation setting. Imagine a large public consultation on a fixed, finite policy agenda, where many respondents submit the same complete, classically consistent pattern of judgments. A type is one such pattern; \(\mu_t\) is its share of the population. For each proposition \(\phi\), the aggregate accepts it when \(\sum_{t:\phi\in t}\mu_t>1/2\). With no exact half splits, this also gives a complete aggregate.

The closest result is Theorem 2, proved in the paper: for any classical agenda and any profile of individually consistent, complete judgment sets, translating the majority outcome into the additive fragment \(\aILLs\) yields a set that is consistent and complete in \(\aILLs\). Its proof uses the fact that every minimally inconsistent set in that fragment has size two. The same overlap argument extends to the distribution above: two propositions each supported by more than half the mass share at least one voter type, whose judgments are consistent. Theorem 8, also proved here, extends the consistency-and-completeness result to the additive modal fragments for actions, beliefs, and obligations.

That is a plausible continuous analogue of the paper’s logical result, but it is not a qualifying computational anchor under the requested standard. Neither theorem classifies a computational problem as tractable or hard. The appendix’s soundness and completeness theorems are logical results too. The complexity claims in a footnote are cited from elsewhere, and the paper says that the complexity of the fused logics remains future work. It contains no named result asserting a problem is in P, NP-hard, FPT, or a related class.

So the strongest honest positive case is that the paper’s majority-aggregation result has a natural high-multiplicity formulation. But there is no named computational result here to anchor the required continuous computational problem. Treating Theorem 2 or 8 as such an anchor would overstate what the paper proves.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 2 and Theorem 8 have natural continuous versions. Fix a finite agenda and let each type be a complete, classically consistent judgment set. If each accepted proposition has support greater than half the population, the supports of any two accepted propositions overlap in positive mass. That carries the paper’s consistency argument over to a distribution of types; Theorem 8 extends the claim to the modal fragments. This is a sound continuous reformulation, and I would not object that the population or the type space is inherently unsuitable.

But these are preservation theorems, not computational results. They establish that majority aggregation has a logical property for every eligible profile. They give no complexity classification for a computational problem. The paper’s own footnote says the complexity of the logics is not treated and leaves the complexity of the fusion for future work. The cited complexity results concern earlier logics, not an algorithmic problem proved in this paper.

The best rescue would be to study a new task, such as deciding derivability from an aggregate profile, or optimizing changes to a society so that an aggregate meets some condition. Those may be worthwhile questions, but the paper specifies no such optimization problem, costs, or computational claim to mirror. Defining one would start a related project; it would not make Theorem 2 or 8 computational anchors.

So the negative case is narrow but strong under ChoCo’s stated remit: the continuous population model is sensible, and the preservation result transfers, but neither anchor opens a computational question the paper actually settles. I cannot honestly claim that no adjacent computational project could be worthwhile; the point is that this paper supplies no qualifying computational result for a continuous mirror to take up.
