The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent is right about the decisive point: this paper gives no computational problem whose complexity it classifies. Its results concern experimental differences in stated entitlements, offers, concessions, and agreements across 174 bargaining pairs. There is no algorithmic input, output, or computational claim to carry over. So there is no anchor here for the programme’s central complexity question.

The large-firm version deserves a fair hearing. A firm could have many bargaining pairs, with each pair’s type recording the parties’ roles, performance information, and production uncertainty. That is a plausible high-multiplicity regime; the fact that the original experiment used named subjects is no objection. But if pairs bargain independently, the continuous society describes a distribution of pair outcomes. The paper gives no shared allocation decision for a computational method to solve. The salary share is already continuous as an outcome, which does not make the population continuous.

The strongest extension would add a firm-wide decision: for example, choose which pairs receive performance information, subject to a budget, to improve agreement rates or total surplus. That would put the population on the continuous side of the model. But the objective, intervention costs, and strategic response model would all need to be supplied. The paper’s four experimental conditions do not specify them; its suggestion that information might be strategically manipulated points toward future research, rather than defining a problem this paper has already studied. That extension could be worthwhile, but it would be a new policy or bargaining-control question, not a mirror of a named result.

The negative case is strongest on that distinction and weaker as a universal claim. One can imagine a compelling follow-on with many bargaining pairs and a common intervention. What the paper itself supports, though, is empirical and behavioral analysis of bilateral negotiation, not a computational problem over a population. A continuous treatment may be useful for a new research project; the case that this paper has a worthwhile continuous computational mirror is not made.
