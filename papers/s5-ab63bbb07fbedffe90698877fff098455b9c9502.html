The continuous mirror question

Given \(q\) goods categories, rational per-capita supplies \(\rho_j\), and a finite set \(T\) of additive types with rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\) and values \(a_{tj}\), find a finite-support allocation \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\) over integral bundles \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge 0}^q\). Require \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), \(\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}B_j\le\rho_j\), and define residual supply \(u_j=\rho_j-\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}B_j\). For every pair \((t,B),(s,C)\) with positive assigned mass and every \(j\) with \(C_j>0\), require \(\sum_k a_{tk}B_k\ge\sum_k a_{tk}(C_k-\mathbf 1[k=j])\); also require \(\sum_k a_{tk}B_k\ge\sum_k a_{tk}u_k\) for every assigned type-bundle pair and \(\sum_j u_j\le 1\).

The model it lives in

A distribution over complete additive preference types, with each unit of mass receiving an integral bundle and category supplies \(\rho_j\) scaling with population. The decision variables \(x_{t,B}\) assign mass to bundles; the objective is to find a feasible EFX allocation with at most one unit of unallocated supply per unit of population.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 2’s \(n-2\) unallocated-item guarantee and its no-envy property, including the stated nice-cancelable extension. It does not cover Theorem 1’s four-agent bound, full EFX existence, or the paper’s simplification results.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can this allocation be found in time \(\mathrm{poly}(q,\tau,L)\) from the binary-encoded masses, supplies, and type values, without expanding the instance by the denominator-clearing factor?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive case is Theorem 2, proved in this paper: every setting with \(n\) additive agents has an EFX allocation leaving at most \(n-2\) items unallocated, and no agent envies the bundle of unallocated items. The authors also state that this guarantee extends to nice cancelable valuations. This is an existence theorem, not a result that puts a search problem in P or proves it hard. The paper has no numbered complexity-class result of that kind, so this is the closest honest anchor.

My lead mirror is Mass-EFX\(_{\le 1}\). Picture a large population receiving repeated copies of a finite set of goods, with only a few distinct additive preference profiles—for example, residents with a small number of preference types dividing standardized equipment. A type \(t\) is the complete vector of values \(a_{tj}\) it assigns to each of \(q\) good categories. Its population mass is \(\mu_t\), where the rational masses sum to \(1\). Category \(j\) has supply \(\rho_j\) per unit of population.

A solution is a finite-support collection \(x_{t,B}\ge 0\), where \(B\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^q\) is an integral bundle and \(x_{t,B}\) is the mass of type \(t\) receiving it. Require \(\sum_B x_{t,B}=\mu_t\), and let \(u_j=\rho_j-\sum_{t,B}x_{t,B}B_j\ge0\) be the unallocated supply. Every person receives an integral bundle; a type’s average bundle may be fractional. With \(v_t(B)=\sum_j a_{tj}B_j\), impose EFX on every pair of bundles used with positive mass: for all such \(x_{t,B},x_{s,C}>0\), and every \(j\) with \(C_j>0\), require \(v_t(B)\ge v_t(C-\mathbf e_j)\). Also require that no assigned bundle of type \(t\) is worth less to that type than the unallocated per-capita bundle \(u\), and that \(\sum_j u_j\le1\). The task is to find such an \(x\).

There is a direct reason this mirror is credible. Clear the denominators of \(\mu\) and \(\rho\) with an integer \(N\). Make a finite instance with \(N\mu_t\) agents of each type and \(N\rho_j\) copies of each good category. Theorem 2 supplies an EFX allocation with at most \(N-2\) unused goods. Group agents by type and bundle, then divide each group’s size by \(N\) to obtain \(x\). The leftover mass is at most \(1-2/N<1\); the theorem’s guarantee that nobody envies the entire unused pool also implies the stated per-capita residual condition. So the continuous existence question has a positive answer for rational inputs by the paper’s own theorem.

The authors should recognize this as their problem: preferences, individual bundles, item removal, and the EFX comparison all remain intact. Only the population is represented by type masses, while repeated supplies scale with it. This mirrors Theorem 2, not the paper’s special four-agent bound; I would not use Theorem 1 as an anchor for a large-population scenario.

For the requested direction, I expect the existence guarantee to remain on the positive, tractable side. But the theorem does not establish a polynomial-time algorithm on the compressed input \((q,\tau,L)\): clearing denominators can produce an \(N\) far larger than the input length, and the resulting allocation may have large bundles or support. A polynomial-time construction from masses and supplies is a worthwhile follow-up, but it is not a result of this paper. The mirror also promises only about one unused good per resident in the limit, not a vanishing leftover fraction. Those are the soft spots; the case that survives them is a precise, author-recognizable population version of a named theorem, with a positive existence result already in hand.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that Theorem 2 is not a computational result. It guarantees an EFX allocation with at most \(n-2\) unallocated items, but the paper gives no running time, input encoding, approximation guarantee, or complexity classification for finding one. So it is a weak anchor for a programme whose target is the computational landscape.

The proposed mass version is coherent, and the denominator-clearing argument validly transfers the theorem’s existence guarantee to rational masses and supplies. But that establishes existence, not efficient construction from the compressed input. Asking whether such a construction runs in time polynomial in the number of types and input bit length would be a worthwhile *new* algorithmic question; this paper’s finite progress argument supplies no such bound, and the replication factor can be exponential in the encoded input length.

That is a case against counting Theorem 2 as a computational-mirror result, not an honest case that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists. The type model is sensible, the co-scaling of supply is legitimate, and the EFX comparisons survive. Under a broader reading that includes continuous existence questions, this anchor survives; I cannot make the universal negative claim convincingly.
