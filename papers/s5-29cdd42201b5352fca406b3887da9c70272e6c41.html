The case FOR (proponent)

The most plausible mirror is a high-multiplicity version of the paper’s inverse power index problem. Let \(T\) be a finite set of voter types, with rational population fractions \(\mu_t\), and let \(\delta_t\) be the target share of total power for type \(t\). Members of a type share the same per-capita voting weight. For each replication \(K\) with \(K\mu_t\) integral, choose weights \(w_t\ge 0\) and quota \(Kq\), giving the weighted voting game
\[ \chi_K(S)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sum_{t\in T} w_t\,|S\cap N_t^K|\ge Kq, \]
where \(N_t^K\) is the set of \(K\mu_t\) voters of type \(t\). For a fixed index \(\mathcal P\in\{\mathcal{BZ},\mathcal{SS}\}\), define type \(t\)’s aggregate power at replication \(K\) by \(P_t^K=\sum_{i\in N_t^K}\mathcal P(\chi_K,i)\). The continuous decision problem is: given \(T,\mu,\delta,\mathcal P\) and \(\varepsilon\), do there exist \(w,q\) such that
\[ \limsup_{K\to\infty}\sum_{t\in T}|P_t^K-\delta_t|\le\varepsilon? \]
The solution is the per-type weights and quota. This preserves the paper’s task—design a weighted voting game to match a desired power distribution—while grouping genuinely interchangeable voters. A plausible regime is a large association or cooperative with many voters but few recurring membership or constituency classes. I would provisionally expect tractability under regular limiting behavior, with type support \(\tau\) as the main structural parameter. This covers only the paper’s weighted-voting inverse-design problem.

There is, however, no named result that can honestly anchor this as the requested computational mirror. The exact ILP in Section 4 is unnumbered; the enumeration and branch-and-bound methods and their computational results are also not stated as numbered theorems or propositions. The closest named result is Theorem 1, cited from Alon and Edelman, not proved by Kurz: if \(n>k\), \(0<\varepsilon<1/(k+1)\), and a simple game \(\chi\) on \(n\) voters has \(\sum_{i=k+1}^n B(\chi,i)\le\varepsilon\), then there is a simple game \(\chi'\) on \(k\) voters whose Banzhaf vector, padded with \(n-k\) zeros, satisfies
\[ \|\mathcal B(\chi)-\mathcal B(\chi')\|_1 \le \frac{(2k+1)\varepsilon}{1-(k+1)\varepsilon}+\varepsilon. \]
That is a power-vector compression bound, not a complexity or algorithmic result for inverse power design. Lemma 2, for example, gives the exact worst approximation error for two voters under either index: it is \(1/2\), attained only at \(d=(3/4,1/4)\). The later numbered lemmas likewise characterize small fixed-\(n\) cases; they do not establish a computational status for the problem.

So the positive case is a plausible model-level continuation of the paper, but it fails the required anchor test. Its weakest point is also substantial: individual power indices are defined for finite voters, and the paper does not provide a canonical continuum definition. The proposed type aggregate and replication limit are reasonable co-modifications for a high-multiplicity setting, but their existence and behavior would need to be proved.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against a ChoCo mirror is that the paper has no qualifying named computational result to mirror. It develops exact ILP, enumeration, and branch-and-bound approaches, then reports computational results, but it states no numbered theorem, lemma, or corollary establishing the complexity of inverse power-index design.

The proponent’s nearest anchors do not fill that gap. Theorem 1 is Alon and Edelman’s Banzhaf-vector compression bound, not a complexity result. Lemma 2 gives the worst approximation error for the two-voter case; Lemmas 3–5 characterize small finite cases. These are named results, but none asserts a computational status that continuization could chart. The paper’s algorithmic contributions are presented outside numbered complexity claims.

The proposed replication model is still coherent: large associations or cooperatives can have many members in recurring classes, and aggregate power by class is a sensible quantity. That makes “no worthwhile mirror in any scenario” hard to defend. The sharper objection is that this is a new asymptotic inverse-design problem. Banzhaf and Shapley–Shubik power are defined using finite coalitions and permutations; the paper supplies no continuous definition or limiting result for aggregate type power. A replication limit could provide one, but establishing it and studying its inverse problem would be new work, not a computational result from this paper carried into the continuum.

So the negative case is strong against treating this paper as an anchored contribution to the programme. It is weak against the broader claim that the proposed high-multiplicity problem could be worthwhile in its own right.
