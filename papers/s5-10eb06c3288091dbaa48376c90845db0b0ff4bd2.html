The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive reading I can defend is a continuous-population version of the site-selection problem apparently encoded by (F1). Let \(A\) be the candidate sites, and let each type \(t\) be a complete ranking of those sites. The society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over its \(\tau\) supported types. For a chosen set \(S\subseteq A\) of \(p\) sites, let \(r_t(S)\) be the rank of type \(t\)’s most-preferred open site. Choose \(S\) to minimize
\[ \sum_{t,u}\mu_t\mu_u\,|r_t(S)-r_u(S)|. \]
This is aggregate pairwise rank envy, normalized as an expectation over two residents. The reading of \(z_i\) as the rank of customer \(i\)’s best open site is inferred from F1’s \(O_{ij}\)-based constraints. Under that reading, the continuous objective matches F1’s pairwise objective up to the factor \(n^2/2\) when \(\mu\) records the type proportions of \(n\) customers.

A plausible regime is a large service population—such as residents choosing among public service locations—with many customers but few distinct rankings of the candidate sites. The population becomes continuous; the choice of \(p\) sites remains discrete. That is a credible population mirror, although it does not by itself imply a tractability gain.

The best version of the proposed mirror is nevertheless coherent. Treat residents as a distribution over complete site rankings, select \(p\) sites, and minimize the expected rank difference between two residents. With the objective normalized, a discrete electorate’s pairwise sum is exactly \(n^2/2\) times that expectation. A large public-service population with repeated rankings is plausible, and this objective need not track individual identities. So I cannot honestly claim the mirror is ill-posed or that no useful scenario exists.
