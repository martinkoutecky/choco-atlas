The case FOR (proponent)

The paper has no qualifying named computational result. Its numbered statements are Definition 1 (KAC Problem) and Definition 2 (Transitivity and Symmetry); it gives Algorithms 1 and 2 and reports experimental results, but states no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition establishing a complexity or algorithmic result. So there are no anchors to mirror, and no per-anchor continuous problem or A/B/C prediction I can honestly give.

But that is a proposed extension, not a result of this paper; the support could also grow with the number of distinct annotators. It cannot supply the anchored case you asked for.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s diagnosis is the strongest case against a mirror here: the paper states no numbered computational result. Definition 1’s \(\arg\max_f v(f)\) leaves the value function unspecified, so it does not define a concrete complexity problem. Algorithms 1 and 2 describe procedures, but the paper proves no runtime, convergence, or approximation guarantee for them. The reported \(35.88\%\) improvement is an empirical accuracy result on a filtered dataset, not a computational result of the kind ChoCo’s programme is set up to continuize.

The best candidate mirror is to model a large workforce as a distribution over worker types and run SEEK-style inference on the resulting masses. That could be a sensible *new* question. But the type would need to capture a worker’s responses across the task set, and the paper asks for an answer to each particular task. Making the tasks a population instead would discard which item receives which answer. Either route requires choosing a new computational objective and assumptions about the worker or task population; neither mirrors a computational claim this paper makes.

So I find no paper-anchored continuous mirror worth proposing. That conclusion does not establish that a continuous workforce-inference problem could never be worthwhile; it says the paper supplies no computational result for such a mirror to carry forward.
