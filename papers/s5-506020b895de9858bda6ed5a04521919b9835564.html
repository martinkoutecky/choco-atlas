Theorem 1 proves axiomatic properties of a social welfare relation and characterizes which extensions satisfy IPC; Corollaries 1 and 2 and Propositions 3 and 6 specialize or characterize those relations. None gives the complexity of a computational problem. The paper also supplies no finite input representation, algorithm, or complexity parameters. So the proponent’s proposed comparison problem for ultimately periodic streams would be a new computational question, not a mirror of a result in this paper.

Even that lead does not acquire ChoCo’s population interpretation by choosing a compact encoding. The paper compares two utility streams indexed by generations. If a continuous society assigns probability mass to generation types, finite changes to a stream have zero mass in a nonatomic model, while the paper’s Pareto conditions respond to improvements affecting even finitely many generations. Giving every generation positive weight instead would privilege some generations and break the paper’s permutation invariance; equal positive weights across countably many generations cannot form a probability distribution. A distribution over whole trajectories would preserve the data only by turning each infinite stream into a type, rather than making the society a population of types.

A more ambitious alternative would replace generations indexed by \(\mathbb N\) with a continuum of generations. But that continuizes the time axis, and the resulting measure-based axioms and computational questions would be a different project. Recasting the paper’s stream comparisons as an optimization or decision problem could still be worthwhile research, but it would not provide the anchored continuous-population mirror this programme is assessing.

So the case against a *paper-anchored ChoCo mirror* is strong: there is no computational result to anchor, and the obvious routes to a continuous population change the object being studied. The broader claim that no related computational project could ever be worthwhile is harder to defend; the paper does not rule out new questions, only a grounded mirror of its results.
