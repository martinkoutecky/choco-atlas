The continuous mirror question

Given a finite-support rational society \(\mu\) over location types \(x_t\in\mathbb{R}^2\), define \(\mathrm{SC}_p(\mu,y)=\left(\sum_t\mu_t\|x_t-y\|_2^p\right)^{1/p}\) for finite \(p\), and \(\mathrm{SC}_{+\infty}(\mu,y)=\max_{t:\mu_t>0}\|x_t-y\|_2\). Let mass-weighted CRD return \(\bar{x}_\mu=\sum_t\mu_t x_t\) with probability \(\frac12\), and return \(x_t\) with probability \(\frac{\mu_t}{2}\). For each \(p\in[1,+\infty]\), determine the sharp supremum over societies with positive optimum of \(\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{SC}_p(\mu,Y)]/\min_y\mathrm{SC}_p(\mu,y)\), and characterize societies that attain or approach it.

The model it lives in

Types are preferred locations \(x_t\in\mathbb{R}^2\) with rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\). The mechanism chooses the mass-weighted centroid with probability \(\frac12\), or samples a type according to \(\mu\) and outputs its location. The objective is the per-capita \(L_p\) cost \(\left(\sum_t\mu_t\|x_t-y\|_2^p\right)^{1/p}\), with the maximum over positive-mass types for \(p=+\infty\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers CRD’s \(L_p\) approximation analysis in Lemma 10 and its performance aspect in Theorem 3. It does not carry over nonvacuous individual strategyproofness at zero agent mass, or address CM, URCM, and their separate guarantees.

Open questions for a prover

  • For fixed \(1<p<2\) and \(2<p<+\infty\), what is the sharp worst-case ratio of mass-weighted CRD, and which mass distributions attain or approach it?

The case FOR (proponent)

The best positive case is a high-multiplicity mirror of the CRD approximation analysis, but it does not meet the required anchor rule. This paper has no named result classifying a problem as polynomial-time solvable, NP-hard, FPT, or otherwise computationally hard. Its theorems analyze strategyproofness and approximation ratios.

The nearest result is Theorem 3, proved here: CRD is strategyproof in any real normed vector space, and for every symmetric monotone norm objective its expected cost is at most \(2-\frac1n\) times optimum; that factor is tight for \(n\ge2\) when the space is nontrivial. This is a substantial result, but it is not a computational-complexity anchor. Lemma 10, proved here for finite \(p\), gives the sharper \(L_p\) upper bounds stated in the paper; its \(p=+\infty\) case is attributed to Tang et al. These too are approximation guarantees, not complexity results.

A plausible regime is a city planning a shared facility for a very large population whose preferred locations cluster at a small number of neighborhood centres. Let each type \(t\) be a preferred point \(x_t\in\mathbb R^2\), and let \(\mu_t\) be its population fraction, with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). The support size \(\tau\) could be small compared with the number of residents. For \(1\le p<\infty\), define per-capita social cost by
\[ \mathrm{SC}_p(\mu,y)=\left(\sum_t\mu_t\|x_t-y\|_2^p\right)^{1/p}, \]
and for \(p=+\infty\), use the largest distance among types of positive mass. Normalizing by population size does not change approximation ratios.

The natural CRD mirror outputs the mass-weighted centroid \(\bar x_\mu=\sum_t\mu_t x_t\) with probability \(1/2\), and otherwise samples a type according to \(\mu\) and outputs that type’s location. A concrete question is: over finite-support rational societies \(\mu\), what is this mechanism’s sharp worst-case approximation ratio under \(\mathrm{SC}_p\), and which societies attain or approach it? The finite-population CRD and this rule agree exactly on empirical distributions, and the normalized costs have the same ratios. This makes the population and action variables clear and gives the authors a recognizable analogue of their facility-location objective.

The main weakness is incentive compatibility. In an exact continuum, an individual has zero mass, so their report cannot affect \(\mu\); individual strategyproofness becomes vacuous. Requiring a positive mass to move instead models a coalition deviation, which is a different property. The faithful bridge is therefore a sequence of finite populations with repeated types, exact individual strategyproofness at each population size, and the distributional rule as the limit.

So there is a plausible continuous-population extension of the paper’s approximation analysis, especially around CRD and its open tightness questions. But under the programme’s stated gate, it supplies no eligible named anchor, and its sharp-ratio question is analytic mechanism analysis rather than a computational-complexity question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is that none of the paper’s named results is a computational result. The coordinate-wise median theorem, the URCM theorem, Theorem 3 for CRD, and Lemma 10 all bound worst-case social cost and establish strategyproofness. They do not classify the complexity of a problem with a finite input and computational output. Asking for the sharp ratio of CRD on distributions over locations is a coherent continuous question, but it remains an approximation analysis, not the complexity question this programme is set up to chart.

The most natural mirror is also easy to state: for a finite-support location distribution \(\mu\), use per-capita cost \(\mathrm{SC}_p(\mu,y)=(\sum_t\mu_t\|x_t-y\|_2^p)^{1/p}\), replace coordinate medians by weighted medians, and let CRD choose the weighted centroid or sample a location according to \(\mu\). That makes the society continuous in the relevant sense. But it yields performance questions about those rules, not a computational problem from the paper. The same issue defeats the best versions of the CM and URCM mirrors: weighted quantiles and random rotations give natural formulations, yet their ratio guarantees remain analytic statements.

CRD also exposes a deeper mismatch. Its strategyproofness concerns a named individual making a unilateral report. In an atomless society, one individual has zero mass, so changing their report leaves \(\mu\) unchanged and truthfulness becomes vacuous. Giving an entire type’s mass the power to change its report instead asks about coalition or type-level deviations, which is a different incentive property. Keeping positive individual masses preserves the paper’s question, but then the model remains a finite population, even if repeated types make it high-multiplicity.

The finite-\(n\) part of Theorem 3 and Lemma 10 makes that loss visible: their guarantees depend on \(n\), while the continuous distribution records population shares rather than the number of individuals represented by those shares. One can take a limit or add a granularity parameter, but the former turns the result into another analysis of the limiting ratio, and the latter restores discrete population information. Neither supplies the missing computational anchor.

This is a strong case against a worthwhile ChoCo complexity mirror, not proof that the distributional questions lack mathematical value. The proposed location-distribution model is sensible, and a separate programme on continuous approximation analysis or mechanism synthesis could pursue it. The negative case is weakest against that broader possibility; it is strongest on the narrower point that the paper supplies no named computational problem for continuization to mirror.
