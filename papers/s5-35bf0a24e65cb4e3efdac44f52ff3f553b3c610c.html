What the mirror covers

The case FOR (proponent)

There are no qualifying anchors in this paper: it contains no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition asserting a computational result. It cites [6] for an \(O(n\log n)\) bound on conventional TTC, but that is not a numbered result of this paper. Its own \(O(1)\) claim and claim that the singular-vector ordering reproduces TTC are asserted in the prose and illustrated with two examples, without a named result. So I can’t honestly give an anchor-specific continuous question.

The strongest positive angle I see is an unanchored mirror of the underlying TTC computation: Succinct Type TTC. Let \(J\) be a finite set of interchangeable object categories. Each type \(t\) consists of a strict ranking \(r_t\) over \(J\) and an endowed category \(e_t\); the society is rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). Category \(j\) has supply \(q_j=\sum_{t:e_t=j}\mu_t\). Given this input, compute \(x_{tj}\), the mass of type \(t\) assigned category \(j\), where \(x\) is the aggregate TTC assignment on a finite replica at the common denominator of the \(\mu_t\). Thus \(\sum_j x_{tj}=\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{tj}=q_j\). The computational question is whether this exact output can be found in time polynomial in \(|J|\), the support size \(\tau\), and the input bit length \(L\), without expanding the potentially enormous replica. The entries of \(x\) are aggregate shares; each unit of mass still receives an indivisible object.

A plausible regime is a large housing exchange with many households but relatively few combinations of ranked housing categories and initial endowment category. Those combinations are the types; mass records their population shares. This is a credible high-multiplicity version of the allocation problem the paper claims to accelerate, and it asks for the TTC allocation itself rather than for a continuous version of the paper’s Markov-matrix encoding. I would expect a tractability result (Class A), with the main follow-up questions being whether type-level cycle processing is polynomial in \(|J|,\tau,L\), and whether the aggregate assignment depends on how interchangeable copies are resolved.

The soft spot is substantial: the paper’s model ranks individually named objects, while this mirror treats copies within a category as interchangeable. If their identities affect preferences or ownership cycles, the category compression may fail and the number of types may grow with the population. More fundamentally, the paper does not prove its own claimed TTC or runtime result. So this is a plausible continuous question about its underlying problem, not a mirror anchored by a named computational result in the paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

There are no named computational results in the paper to anchor a mirror. Its \(O(1)\) runtime and claim to reproduce TTC appear only as assertions and examples; the \(O(n\log n)\) bound is attributed to reference [6]. That is a strong reason to reject an anchor-based case for this paper.

The proposed Succinct Type TTC also has a real mismatch with the paper’s model. In classic TTC, each object has a particular owner, and that ownership determines the cycles. If types include the exact endowment, distinct objects give agents distinct types, so support grows with the population. To get repeated types, the proposal groups objects into interchangeable categories. But then agents are indifferent among copies, and the finite-replica TTC assignment may depend on how those copies and their owners are resolved. Defining a mass-level cycle rule could address this, but it would require specifying a mechanism the paper neither defines nor computes with its SVD method. The proposal is therefore not a direct mirror of a result established here.

That does not defeat the proposal as a worthwhile research question. A housing market with repeated categories, per-capita supplies, and a carefully defined mass version of TTC is a plausible high-multiplicity model. Under the programme’s criteria, its continuous complexity could be worth studying even if it is unanchored and needs a co-modified mechanism. So the negative case is strong against claiming this paper has a qualifying computational result to mirror; it is weak against the broader claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists in any scenario.
