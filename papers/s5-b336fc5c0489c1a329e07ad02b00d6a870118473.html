The case FOR (proponent)

The paper contains no qualifying computational anchor. Its only numbered formal statements are Definitions 1–3; it has no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Proposition, or Corollary asserting NP-hardness, polynomial-time solvability, parameterized complexity, or similar. The Gale–Shapley existence result is cited from [1], not proved or computationally classified here. The claims about complete disruption under vertical preferences and at most two disrupted matches under single-peaked preferences are unnumbered prose claims.

The strongest positive case is therefore a conceptual mirror of Definition 1, “\(k\)-external stability,” rather than a mirror of a computational theorem.

Take finite male and female type sets \(T_M,T_W\). A type contains a complete preference ordering over the opposite-side types. Let \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) be the masses of male and female types, with total mass \(1\). A baseline stable matching is a measure \(\pi=(\pi_{tu})\) whose row and column sums are \(\mu\) and \(\nu\). An entrant cohort consists of mass vectors \(a,b\), with \(\sum_t a_t=\sum_u b_u=\varepsilon\). The post-entry market has masses \(\mu'=\mu+a\) and \(\nu'=\nu+b\).

The decision variable is a post-entry matching measure \(x\). It must have marginals \(\mu'\) and \(\nu'\), and be stable: for every type pair \((t,u)\), it cannot leave positive mass of type \(t\) matched strictly below \(u\) and positive mass of type \(u\) matched strictly below \(t\). Equivalently,

\[ \left(\mu'_t-\sum_{v\succeq_t u}x_{tv}\right) \left(\nu'_u-\sum_{s\succeq_u t}x_{su}\right)=0 \]

for every \((t,u)\).

Define retained old-match mass by

\[ R_\pi(x)=\sum_{t,u}\min\{\pi_{tu},x_{tu}\}. \]

The continuous external-stability problem is:

\[ \Delta^*(\pi,a,b) = 1-\min_{x\text{ stable after entry}}R_\pi(x). \]

It asks for a stable post-entry matching that maximizes the fraction of the original matching disrupted; equivalently, one may ask whether every post-entry stable matching retains at least \(k\) mass. This is exactly Definition 1 with “number of old couples” replaced by mass. A solution is the optimizing stable matching \(x^*\), together with the retained and disrupted mass.

A credible high-multiplicity regime is a large matching market with many applicants and positions, or a large marriage market, organized into a modest number of cohorts by location, qualification, age, or socioeconomic type. Agents within a cohort have the same preferences over opposite-side cohorts, so \(n\gg |T_M|+|T_W|\). The paper’s vertical setup becomes common ranking over type classes; its single-peaked setup becomes preferences based on distance between type locations. The entrant pair becomes a small entrant cohort of mass \(\varepsilon\), not one literal individual.

The mirror is plausible because it preserves the paper’s actual object: sensitivity of a stable matching to entry. It does not replace matching by fractional outcomes or merely invoke a continuous preference coordinate. The continuous object is the population distribution, while the matching itself is the action variable.

For fixed finite type sets and strict preferences, I would expect this problem to be Class A: capacitated stable matching and weighted optimization over stable matchings have flow, polyhedral, or rotation-based structure. The more ambitious problem of choosing the worst entrant type distribution subject to a mass budget may be harder and could become continuum-specific.

The weakest point is substantial. The paper’s “Beauty and Beast” cascade relies on strictly ranking named individuals. Under genuine high multiplicity, identical agents are indistinguishable, and a single entrant has zero mass. A cohort-level mirror may therefore preserve the external-stability question while failing to reproduce the claim that one entrant pair disrupts every old couple. If that identity-level cascade is essential, this is a weak mirror; if the paper’s central idea is sensitivity of matching to a positive entrant mass, the mirror remains convincing.

Thus my honest verdict is: there is a sensible continuous-population research question here, led by Definition 1, but the paper itself supplies no named computational result to anchor a ChoCo complexity classification.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s first observation is the decisive negative point: this paper contains no qualifying computational anchor. Definitions 1–3 introduce stability concepts; the vertical and single-peaked conclusions are informal examples; and Gale–Shapley existence is cited from another paper. Nothing here asserts a complexity bound, an algorithm, an optimization theorem, or a computational impossibility result. Thus the proposed optimization over stable post-entry matchings would be a new research problem inspired by the paper, not a continuous mirror of a result in it.

More fundamentally, the proposed \(R_\pi(x)\) does not preserve what Definition 1 measures. The paper counts old couples: whether the same man and woman remain matched. A finite type distribution records only how much mass travels from type \(t\) to type \(u\). It does not record which members of those types form the couples.

For example, suppose all old men and women belong to one preference type. The original matching may pair every man with a particular woman. A post-entry matching can permute all those partners while preserving exactly the same type-to-type mass. The proponent’s formula then gives \(R_\pi(x)=1\), hence zero disruption, although no original couple survives. Conversely, if one uses an atomless type coordinate and represents the original matching by a diagonal measure, an arbitrarily small entry shock can shift the matching graph off the diagonal. The overlap of the two matching measures is then zero, so every old couple is counted as disrupted. The quantity becomes either identity-blind or generically degenerate.

There is no clean high-multiplicity repair:

  • If types are coarse cohorts, the model can study changes in aggregate partner-type flows, but that is not the paper’s external stability concept.
  • If every rank or location is made a separate type, then the number of types grows with the number of agents and the “continuum” is merely a normalized finite matching instance.
  • If identities are retained so that exact old couples can be tracked, identity becomes part of the problem-relevant type description. The anonymous high-multiplicity compression has then disappeared.
  • If exact identity is replaced by a tolerance metric—say, the mass whose partner rank changes by more than \(\delta\)—that is a reasonable new robustness problem, but it is no longer Definition 1 and requires an additional modelling choice absent from the paper.

Replacing one entrant by a positive-mass cohort does not solve this problem. It produces a meaningful comparative-statics question about shocks to a matching market, but it changes the paper’s central phenomenon: the paper is specifically about a single new pair destroying existing named matches. A cohort-shock transport problem may be worth studying, but its motivation and object would have to be developed independently of this note.

The proponent’s expectation that the resulting problem should be tractable also cannot supply the missing anchor. Even a perfectly formulated continuous stable-matching optimization would need its own theorem and complexity analysis before it became ChoCo material. Here the continuous question is being invented precisely because the paper supplies no computational result to continuize.

So the strongest honest verdict is negative for the programme: there is a plausible continuous matching-sensitivity topic nearby, but no worthwhile continuous mirror of this paper’s contribution. The paper offers an intuition and a definition, not a computational result; and its one potentially continuizable definition depends on identity-level match retention, which high multiplicity either forgets, trivializes, or reintroduces at the cost of abandoning the continuum compression.
