What the mirror covers

No result is covered by a computational mirror: Theorem 31 and the other core characterizations are structural, while the concluding semi-infinite LP is not developed as a named computational result.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive intuition is that a high-multiplicity production economy is a sensible setting: agents share one of finitely many endowment or productivity types, \(\mu_t\) is the mass of type \(t\), and the question is to characterize the core of the resulting TU game. But this paper already takes the population to be nonatomic and studies games of the form \(\nu(E)=g(P(E))\). That makes the continuous population part of its starting model, rather than a mirror of a discrete problem.

The closest named result is Theorem 31, proved in the paper. For a positive strongly continuous vector charge \(P\), it characterizes exact games whose cores lie in \(\operatorname{span}\{P_1,\ldots,P_N\}\): this is equivalent to being an exact generalized linear production game, or to having \(\nu(E)=\min_{m\in\Gamma_\alpha}m(E)\) for a weak-* compact \(\Gamma_\alpha\) in that span. These conditions imply a representation \(\nu(E)=g(P(E))\) for a concave, degree-one homogeneous \(g\), and the core is the corresponding convex hull of coefficient charges. This is a structural classification of continuous games, not a result about an algorithm or computational complexity.

The concluding remarks give a semi-infinite LP formulation for computing the linear core, but the paper does not analyze its complexity or provide a named algorithmic result. So it supplies no qualifying computational anchor, and there is no honest per-anchor continuous problem to hand to a prover. The obstacle is not that a many-agent scenario is implausible; it is that the paper’s population is already continuous and its results are analytic rather than computational.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is that the paper states no computational problem whose complexity an analyst could mirror. Theorem 31 characterizes exact games with finite-dimensional cores; it does not give an algorithm, a complexity bound, or a decision problem. That is a decisive gap under ChoCo’s stated standard for a mirror.

Theorem 31 does make a high-multiplicity setting plausible. For example, a large production economy could have finitely many endowment or productivity types, with \(\mu_t\) the mass of type \(t\). Agents of a type are interchangeable for the model’s purposes, so identity is no objection. But the strongest computational version one could build from the theorem—given rational type masses and a finite encoding of production technology, decide whether the core has the theorem’s form or compute it—would be a new algorithmic problem. The paper gives neither a discrete computational problem to pair with it nor a finite input representation: its result allows general strongly continuous charges and a compact production index. Choosing how to encode those objects would shape the problem itself.

The concluding semi-infinite LP is the best counterargument. It gives a concrete optimization formulation for the linear core, and the authors explicitly discuss its computational features. Still, they do not analyze the LP’s complexity or its separation problem. To make that a computational task, one must specify how \(g\) and \(R(P)\) are represented or queried, as well as what output and precision are required. That could be worthwhile algorithmic work, but it would extend the paper’s continuous theory rather than mirror one of its computational results.

The paper’s existing nonatomic model does not, by itself, rule out a mirror: an analytic treatment of a continuous population does not answer computational questions about it. It does show that a proposed mirror would need a distinct computational target. Here, the natural candidates require adding that target and its input model from scratch. The negative case is weakest precisely at the LP passage: it gives a credible starting point for new work. But on the programme’s criterion, that makes it a promising adjacent project, not a continuous mirror of a named result in this paper.
