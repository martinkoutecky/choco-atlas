The continuous mirror question

Given \(n\) items and a supplied sample size \(r\), let \(\mu\) be a rational-mass distribution supported on \(\tau\) rankings, where \(\mu_t=N_t/r\) and the counts arise from \(r\) independent Mallows samples with fixed parameter \(\beta>0\) around an unknown order. Can a randomized algorithm, given \(\mu\), return a permutation minimizing \(\sum_t \mu_t d_K(t,\sigma)\) with error probability at most \(n^{-\alpha}\) and running time polynomial in \(n,\tau,L\) for fixed \(\alpha,\beta\)?

The model it lives in

Treat each complete ranking as a type and the empirical histogram \(\mu\) as the society. Find its weighted Kemeny median, minimizing \(\sum_t \mu_t d_K(t,\sigma)\), under the Mallows-sample promise.

The objection that survived

Theorem 7's guarantee is distributional in \(r\) Mallows samples, so a prover still needs to show that the promised histogram problem retains the stated guarantee with computation polynomial in \(n,\tau,L\).

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead, and sole anchor, is Theorem 7, proved in this paper. It says that if \(r\) rankings on \(n\) elements are drawn independently from Mallows’ model with parameter \(\beta>0\), then a randomized algorithm computes a maximum-probability order in time
\[ O\!\left(n^{1+O(\alpha/(\beta r))}\,2^{O(\alpha/\beta+1/\beta^2)}\log^2 n\right) \]
with error probability below \(n^{-\alpha}\), for any \(\alpha>0\). Its running time approaches almost linear in \(n\) as \(r\) grows. The theorem is proved here; it is not merely cited. The paper also mentions that unrestricted Mallow Reconstruction is NP-hard, citing [BTT89], but that statement is unnumbered, so I do not use it as an anchor.

The continuous mirror is Histogram Mallows Reconstruction. An instance gives \(n\) items and a finite support of \(\tau\) complete rankings \(t\), each with a positive rational mass \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). For the version anchored in Theorem 7, the masses have the form \(\mu_t=N_t/r\), where the counts \(N_t\) come from \(r\) independent Mallows samples around an unknown central order. The task is to return a permutation \(\sigma\) minimizing
\[ \sum_t \mu_t\,d_K(t,\sigma), \]
where \(d_K\) is Kendall distance. This is exactly the maximum-likelihood objective: its likelihood is proportional to \(\exp\!\left(-\beta r\sum_t\mu_t d_K(t,\sigma)\right)\). Any minimizing permutation is a valid solution, including when there are ties. The computational question is whether this promised problem admits an exact algorithm with running time polynomial in \(n,\tau,L\), for fixed \(\beta\) and confidence parameter \(\alpha\), where \(L\) is the bit length of the masses.

A plausible regime is a large consultation in which many people rank the same small slate of proposals. Each person’s complete ranking is their type; mass is the fraction of respondents with that ranking. With four proposals there are at most \(24\) types, even if millions of people respond. A Mallows model around a shared latent order is a recognizable assumption for noisy expert or public rankings. The mirror keeps the paper’s central decision variable, likelihood objective, and noise model. It replaces the sample list with its exact type histogram, which preserves the objective.

I expect this promised, support-compressed problem to be Class A. Theorem 7 already computes the same optimum on Mallows-generated samples, and its score depends on aggregates that can be recovered by summing the rational masses. A continuous-version proof should establish the promised high-probability guarantee and account for arithmetic in \(L\), rather than expand a large histogram back into \(r\) records. The resulting question is a real high-multiplicity one: it asks for the central ranking of a mass distribution over voter types.

The weakest point is the Mallows sampling promise. For arbitrary rational \(\mu\), this becomes weighted Kemeny aggregation; the paper’s cited unrestricted NP-hardness suggests hardness transfers, since discrete instances embed as rational histograms. I would treat that unrestricted case as a further Class B question, not silently claim Theorem 7 solves it. This mirror covers the paper’s Mallow Reconstruction result only; it does not claim to continuize the noisy-comparison results or the whole paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection to the sole anchor, Theorem 7, is that it is a distributional sample result, not a complexity theorem for rational type masses. Its guarantee uses \(r\) independent Mallows draws around a hidden order. The histogram \(\mu_t=N_t/r\) preserves the maximum-likelihood objective, but a normalized society does not by itself say how many draws produced it or how it was generated. Theorem 7 therefore does not establish an exact algorithm polynomial in \(n,\tau,L\) for the proposed continuous input; transferring the guarantee would require a new algorithmic argument.

The sharper version of the mirror faces a choice. For arbitrary rational \(\mu\), the task is weighted Kemeny aggregation, and the Mallows concentration that drives Theorem 7 no longer applies. For Mallows-generated histograms, it is the paper’s stochastic reconstruction task with its input compressed to a sufficient statistic. That can be worthwhile high-multiplicity work, but it is a narrower claim than “Theorem 7 gives a tractable continuous mirror.” There is also tension in the motivating regime: fixing a small slate makes the number of possible ranking types small but leaves little asymptotic problem, while growing the slate can make the support of a typical Mallows sample large.

These are real limitations, but they do not defeat the anchor. The histogram preserves the objective exactly, and its masses can plausibly support an algorithm that uses weighted pairwise scores without expanding all \(r\) observations. The consultation scenario is coherent, and a high-multiplicity algorithmic question remains even if it turns out to be hard. Under the programme’s standard, that is enough for the mirror to survive. I cannot make an honest case that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists here.
