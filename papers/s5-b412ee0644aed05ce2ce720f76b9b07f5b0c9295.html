The continuous mirror question

Given a finite type set \(T\) with rational population shares \(\mu_t\), \(k\ge3\) advertisers, and rational demand reports \(b_i\) with \(\sum_i b_i\le1\), is there a polynomial type-oracle direct mechanism over allocations \(x_{i,t}\ge0\) satisfying \(\sum_i x_{i,t}\le\mu_t\) and \(\sum_t x_{i,t}=b_i\), which is strategyproof for advertisers with true capacities \(\tilde b_i\) and achieves welfare at least \((e-1)/e\) times the optimal feasible welfare? The oracle specifies diffusion utilities \(F_i(x)\) under continuous counterparts of the paper’s submodularity, adverse-competition, MeI, and AgI assumptions.

The model it lives in

A finite-type network-user population with shares \(\mu_t\) and a diffusion oracle that accounts for interactions among types. The mechanism chooses advertiser-by-type seed masses \(x_{i,t}\u005c), subject to type-mass and reported-demand constraints; its objective is \(\sum_i F_i(x)\u005c), with advertisers retaining the paper’s strategic capacity reports.

The objection that survived

The mirror needs a finite-type interaction and diffusion model that makes mass allocations determine welfare; in a generic graph, preserving network effects may require a separate type for nearly every node.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror covers Theorem 3 and Corollary 1 as continuous-demand mechanism questions; it leaves the paper’s other mechanisms and their integer-demand analyses without continuous counterparts.

Open questions for a prover

  • What interaction or diffusion-oracle representation ensures type masses determine welfare while retaining the network effects of seed placement?
  • Which continuous submodularity conditions suffice for an exactly strategyproof mechanism with the \(e/(e-1)\) guarantee and polynomial type-oracle complexity?
  • Can the integer-budget recursive construction behind Corollary 1 be extended to rational mass demands while preserving strategyproofness and its factor-\(3\) guarantee?

The case FOR (proponent)

The most convincing regime is a large social platform whose users fall into a small number \(\tau\) of genuinely interchangeable network roles: for example, users in the same role have the same relevant propagation probabilities, node weights, and responses to each advertiser. The many agents in the continuum are the users, not the advertisers. Advertisers remain the paper’s finite set of strategic players.

Let \(T\) be the finite set of complete user types, with population shares \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\). For advertiser \(i\), \(x_{i,t}\) is the share of type \(t\) seeded for that advertiser. A feasible disjoint allocation has \(x_{i,t}\ge 0\) and \(\sum_i x_{i,t}\le\mu_t\). Each actual user still receives an integral seed assignment; \(x_{i,t}\) records the fraction of a large type cohort assigned to \(i\). Advertiser \(i\)’s reported demand \(b_i\) is a fraction of the user population, so \(\sum_t x_{i,t}=b_i\). The objective is total expected eventual adoption, \(F(x)=\sum_i F_i(x)\), under a type-level diffusion oracle. This is the paper’s seed-placement and welfare problem with the network-user population made continuous.

My lead anchor is Theorem 3, proved in this paper: with \(k\ge3\), under Mechanism Indifference (social welfare depends only on the union of allocated elements) and Agent Indifference (an advertiser’s utility depends on the other advertisers’ allocations only through their union), Algorithm 2 is strategyproof and achieves an \(e/(e-1)\)-approximation to optimal social welfare. Algorithm 2 greedily selects the union of the demanded number of seed nodes, then uniformly partitions those nodes among advertisers according to their demands. The paper’s base assumptions also require monotone submodular social welfare, nondecreasing own utility, and adverse competition.

The corresponding continuous problem is: given rational type shares \(\mu\), \(k\ge3\), and rational reported demand shares \(b_i\) with total at most \(1\), find a strategyproof direct mechanism over feasible mass allocations \(x\) that achieves welfare at least \((e-1)/e\) times the best feasible welfare for every report vector. The type-level utilities must satisfy the continuous versions of the paper’s base assumptions and MeI and AgI. One concrete route to investigate is to choose an aggregate seed-mass vector \(y\), with \(\sum_t y_t=\sum_i b_i\), then give advertiser \(i\) the proportional share \(x_{i,t}=b_i y_t/\sum_j b_j\). The question is whether this can be done with polynomially many type-level oracle calls in \(k,\tau\), and the input bit length \(L\), while retaining exact truthfulness and the stated ratio. I expect Class A: the aggregate optimization has the shape of continuous submodular optimization, and proportional mass allocation is a natural counterpart of the paper’s random partition. This is the strongest mirror because its allocation rule already separates selection of the seed pool from its division among advertisers.

A complementary anchor is Corollary 1, also proved in this paper: for two players, there exists a \(3\)-approximate strategyproof mechanism that achieves disjoint allocations. It applies under the paper’s base assumptions with anonymous players, meaning their valuations are symmetric under relabeling. Its two-player truthful construction uses budget-dependent distributions over greedy allocation orders.

Its continuous question is: for two advertisers with anonymous type-level diffusion utilities, does there exist a direct mechanism that, for every rational pair of reported mass demands, returns a distribution over disjoint feasible mass allocations satisfying \(\sum_t x_{i,t}=b_i\), is strategyproof for advertisers whose true capacities are \(\tilde b_i\), and achieves expected welfare at least one third of the best feasible welfare? The intended solution would be a continuous counterpart of the recursive greedy-order construction, with polynomial type-oracle complexity in \(\tau\) and \(L\). I also expect Class A, but less confidently: the paper’s recursion advances integer budgets one seed at a time, so an exact continuous version needs a new path or limit argument to preserve truthfulness. Further questions include whether the factor \(3\) can improve and whether anonymity can be weakened.

The paper contains no named hardness result, so these are algorithmic anchors; there is no discrete hardness claim here whose dissolution the mirror could test. The authors should recognize both questions: the allocation target, advertisers’ demands, strategic incentives, and diffusion welfare are preserved, while demand and seed allocation are measured as shares of a large user population.

The weakest point is the type compression. On a general social network, two users can have different neighborhoods and diffusion roles even if they look similar by coarse labels; the number of types needed for a faithful model may then grow with the population. The proposed regime therefore needs a platform where a small set of complete, exchangeable roles really does describe the diffusion-relevant structure. The integer-budget proofs also do not themselves establish the continuous mechanisms: both mirrors are questions to solve, not results already implied by the paper.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the proposed type model leaves out the structure that makes this paper’s problem what it is. The paper does have computational results to mirror: Theorem 3 and Corollary 1 are both real anchors. But its agents are the advertisers, while its ground set \(U\) consists of network nodes the mechanism assigns as seeds. Treating those nodes as the continuous population is plausible only if a type description preserves how a node’s position affects diffusion.

A node’s role is relational. Its value as a seed depends on its neighbors, on overlap with other seeds’ neighborhoods, and on the competing seed sets. Grouping nodes by degree, weight, or propagation parameters can therefore merge nodes whose allocations have different consequences. For example, two degree-two candidates may share a neighbor, while another degree-two candidate has disjoint neighbors; selecting the same number from that coarse type can produce different expected spread. A complete type would need to capture the relevant network structure. In a generic graph that can make nearly every node its own type, so \(\tau\) grows with the population and the proposed high-multiplicity compression disappears.

This directly weakens the Theorem 3 mirror. Mechanism Indifference and Agent Indifference concern how advertisers’ valuations respond to relabeling or regrouping *advertisers*. They do not make network nodes interchangeable. Nor does a type-mass vector \(y\) generally determine the welfare of an allocation: two seed sets with identical type counts can have different network effects. The proposed proportional allocation \(x_{i,t}=b_i y_t/\sum_j b_j\) consequently does not inherit the paper’s random-partition guarantee. The theorem’s discrete proof uses a uniformly random partition of a particular finite seed set; proportional masses do not reproduce the same outcomes unless the type model and diffusion process justify that extension.

The same issue reaches Corollary 1. Its anonymity is symmetry among advertisers, not symmetry among users. Its recursive construction indexes integer demand pairs and carefully chooses distributions over greedy allocation orders. A real-demand version needs a new mechanism over measurable allocations, and interpolation between integer budgets does not establish truthfulness or the \(3\)-approximation. That is a substantial gap, though it cannot by itself show the question is unworthy: designing such a mechanism could be the research problem.

A finite-type graphon or a population made of repeated, genuinely interchangeable network roles is the best rescue. With a specified interaction kernel and diffusion rule, type masses could determine a nontrivial continuous seed-allocation problem. But this adds essential input beyond the type shares, and its computational cost depends on how the network interaction and diffusion oracle are represented. The paper’s arbitrary finite-set oracle does not supply that continuous interface. This makes the proposed mirror underspecified as stated; it does not rule out a well-defined and worthwhile version.

So the negative case is a strong caution against treating “users of the same coarse role” as a valid type compression, and against claiming that either theorem already has a continuous counterpart. It does not honestly defeat every scenario. A finite-type network model could preserve the central seed-allocation and truthfulness question while making the population continuous. I would argue that this paper is a risky, model-intensive ChoCo target, but the universal claim that no worthwhile mirror exists is weak.
