The case FOR (proponent)

This paper has no named computational result to anchor a continuization case. Theorem 3.5, proved here, establishes non-emptiness of the generalised \(\lambda\)-Core for separable games under stated conditions; it does not classify the complexity of deciding or finding a core allocation, or give an algorithm. The examples and status-game application do not supply such a result either.

The paper’s continuous objects are the players’ strategy spaces \(A_i\), while its society remains a finite set of players \(N\). That is outcome or action-space continuity, not population continuization. I can’t honestly turn Theorem 3.5 into a computational anchor under the requested criteria.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s case raises no continuous-question anchors, and that is the strongest objection: this paper has no named computational result to mirror. Theorem 3.5 proves non-emptiness of the generalized \(\lambda\)-Core under specific assumptions; Examples 3.6 and 3.7 show emptiness in particular games, and the status game gives a solution directly. None states a complexity bound or gives an algorithm for deciding or finding a core allocation. The players’ strategy spaces are continuous, but the society is the finite set \(N\).

The strongest proposed rescue would give the game a population of finitely many complete player types with rational masses, define coalitions as measurable portions of those types, and extend the \(\lambda\)-worth using the complement’s equilibrium responses. That could be a coherent population-game question. But this paper supplies no computational problem for that model. Its setup allows arbitrary compact action sets and continuous payoff functions, without a finite input representation or input length. A complexity question would require choosing those encodings and specifying how to represent and compute the new equilibrium and worth functions. Those choices define a new research problem; they do not mirror a computational result in this paper. Extending Theorem 3.5 to a nonatomic population would instead be an existence theorem, outside ChoCo’s computational focus.

So I would reject this paper as a strong ChoCo mirror candidate. The limit of that case is its universal wording: a computational study of a specifically encoded population \(\lambda\)-Core could be worthwhile. The paper itself, however, gives no computational result to anchor that study.
