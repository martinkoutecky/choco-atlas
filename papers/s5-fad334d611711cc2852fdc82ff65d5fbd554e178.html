The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that the paper supplies no named computational result for a continuous mirror to answer. Its named theorem is an axiomatic representation result, cited from earlier work. The MDP section gives occupancy-measure formulations and describes LP or convex-programming solutions, but it does not state a numbered complexity theorem. So the proposed population-mass optimization is a new research question inspired by the paper, not a mirror of one of its computational results.

The proposed traveler or household setting is plausible, but it does not yet establish a worthwhile computational problem. If many agents are exact copies, aggregating them into types often just rescales the welfare terms; it does not change the feasible control problem or create a new algorithmic challenge. If they differ in battery state, location, dynamics, costs, or constraints, those differences belong in the type description. The number of relevant types can then grow with the system’s heterogeneity, while dynamic state changes can require tracking a distribution over types and states. That may lead to interesting mean-field or control work, but the paper does not identify a complexity question or result that this continuation would resolve.

This is a strong case against calling the proposal a mirror of the paper. It is weaker as a universal claim that no worthwhile continuous question can be built from it: a careful model of repeated allocation among large cohorts might produce one. But that possibility is not an anchor in the paper’s stated results, and the proponent has not shown that aggregation yields more than a reformulation of the existing control problem.
