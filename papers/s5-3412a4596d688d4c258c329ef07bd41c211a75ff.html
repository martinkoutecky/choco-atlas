Moving to growing committees would create a new computational project, not mirror a named result in this paper.

There is also a real modeling obstacle for the most natural power-index mirror. Banzhaf power counts whether adding an individual changes a coalition from losing to winning. In an atomless society, adding one person changes no mass, so that swing test gives zero for every individual and the normalized index becomes undefined. One can define type-level power or introduce a finite-population scaling limit, but each choice supplies a new coalition measure or power functional. Those may be worthwhile questions, but this paper does not select or compute one.

The paper’s geometric continuum is over weight vectors and quota for a fixed set of players; it does not make the population continuous. The negative case is strongest as a verdict on this paper: it offers no named computational result to mirror, and the natural high-multiplicity extension would go beyond its results. It is weaker as a universal claim about weighted voting: a continuous, type-level power-index problem might still be worth studying in its own right.
