The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest case is a genuine population mirror of Theorem 7.1, although it fails ChoCo’s strict computational-anchor requirement: the paper proves mechanism-impossibility and approximation results, not NP-hardness, membership in \(P\), FPT, or another complexity classification.

My lead anchor would be Theorem 7.1, proved in this paper:

For every \(K\ge3\), every deterministic anonymous strategyproof mechanism for \(K\)-Facility Location on the real line, with \(n\ge K+1\) agents, has an unbounded approximation ratio.

This is the paper’s cleanest anchor because it is anonymous and therefore naturally compatible with aggregation by population types.

A plausible mirror is the following. Consider a large population of residents along a one-dimensional corridor who have ideal facility locations. A type is a location \(x\in\mathbb R\); agents of the same type have identical preferences and all other relevant attributes. The society is a finitely supported rational distribution \(\mu\), with \(\mu_x\) denoting the population fraction at \(x\). The natural high-multiplicity regime has \(N\gg\tau\), where \(N\) is the number of residents and \(\tau=|\operatorname{supp}(\mu)|\) is small; the theorem’s stress case has \(K+1\) location types, each representing a large cohort.

Define the continuous problem Anonymous \(K\)-Facility Population Mechanism as follows. Given \(K\) and \(\mu\), a deterministic mechanism \(\mathcal F_K\) must output \(K\) facility locations \(y_1,\ldots,y_K\in\mathbb R\). Its social cost is

\[ \operatorname{SC}(\mu,\mathcal F_K(\mu)) = \sum_x \mu_x \min_j |x-y_j|, \]

and the benchmark is

\[ \operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu) = \min_{y_1,\ldots,y_K} \sum_x \mu_x \min_j |x-y_j|. \]

The mechanism must be anonymous, so it depends on the distribution rather than on named residents. To preserve a meaningful strategic condition in the continuum, require positive-mass strategyproofness: for every type \(x\), every \(0<\alpha\le\mu_x\), and every reported location \(z\), let

\[ \mu'=\mu-\alpha\delta_x+\alpha\delta_z. \]

Then the moved cohort must not strictly benefit:

\[ \min_j|x-\mathcal F_K(\mu)_j| \le \min_j|x-\mathcal F_K(\mu')_j|. \]

The question is whether there exists such a mechanism with a finite uniform approximation ratio

\[ \operatorname{SC}(\mu,\mathcal F_K(\mu)) \le \rho\,\operatorname{OPT}_K(\mu) \]

for every finite-support population distribution \(\mu\). A solution is either an explicit mechanism and proof of its ratio, or an impossibility construction showing that the ratio is unbounded.

I would expect the negative phenomenon of Theorem 7.1 to transfer under this cohort-strategyproof interpretation. The obstruction is driven by \(K+1\) strategically positioned locations and anonymity, not by a large number of distinct individuals. Replacing each of those locations by a large identical cohort preserves the high-multiplicity regime while retaining the geometric manipulation argument. In the ChoCo trichotomy this is closest to hardness transferring, although strictly speaking it is an approximation-impossibility transfer rather than a computational-hardness result.

The further questions are substantial: does the theorem already hold for equal-mass cohorts; what approximation ratio is possible when the minimum positive mass is bounded below by \(\eta\); and do randomized or imposing mechanisms behave differently in the population model? Theorem 8.1 suggests a related star-metric mirror, but I would not use it as a second anchor because its core statement concerns only three agents and is non-anonymous.

The weakest point is unavoidable. With genuinely atomless individuals, one person’s report has zero effect on \(\mu\), so ordinary strategyproofness becomes vacuous. Retaining named identities prevents genuine continuization; requiring positive-mass deviations instead changes individual strategyproofness into a cohort or partial-group condition. The paper’s discussion of partial group strategyproofness makes that modification defensible, but it is still a modification. Thus this is a credible substantive mirror of Theorem 7.1, not an honest qualifying ChoCo computational case under the programme’s present anchor rule.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is a category mismatch: this paper contains no computational result to continuize. Theorem 7.1 is an impossibility theorem about a class of mechanisms, not a complexity classification or algorithmic problem. It has no encoded input, decision problem, optimization problem, or algorithmic resource whose dependence on population multiplicity can be studied. The same is true of Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.2, and Theorem 8.1.

The proponent’s population model is nevertheless perfectly sensible. Locations can be types, large cohorts can share each location, and the social cost

\[ \operatorname{SC}(\mu,F(\mu)) = \int \min_j |x-y_j|\,d\mu(x) \]

is a natural mass-weighted objective. The obstruction in Theorem 7.1 plausibly survives replication into \(K+1\) large cohorts. Thus multiplicity, identity, and degeneration are not credible objections here.

The problem is what the proposed question actually asks: whether there exists an anonymous, strategyproof population mechanism with finite approximation ratio. That is an axiomatic mechanism-existence question, merely rewritten over measures. In an atomless population, unilateral deviations have zero effect and strategyproofness is vacuous. Making positive-mass deviations meaningful repairs this by imposing a cohort or measure-valued group-strategyproofness axiom. That is defensible, but it is a new strategic model, not a computational mirror of the paper’s theorem.

One might try to repair the anchor by asking for the best approximation ratio over all such population mechanisms. But then the mechanism is a function over every possible distribution, not a finite computational object. Restricting the mechanism to a finitely represented family would create a new problem whose complexity comes from that representation, not from continuization. Alternatively, computing optimal facility locations for a given \(\mu\) gives a high-multiplicity \(K\)-median problem, but drops strategyproofness and therefore no longer mirrors the paper.

The same objection defeats a star-metric version of Theorem 8.1: cohort replication may produce a coherent continuous impossibility theorem, but still not a computational result. Hence the proposed mirror is mathematically worthwhile as a population extension of mechanism-design impossibility, yet it does not qualify for ChoCo’s computational programme. The negative case is decisive under that gate, but weak if “worthwhile” is allowed to include axiomatic continuous mechanism theory.
