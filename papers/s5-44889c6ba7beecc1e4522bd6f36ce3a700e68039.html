The case FOR (proponent)

The paper has no named result asserting that a computational problem is in P, NP-hard, FPT, or another complexity class. Theorem 1 proves that the \(\otimes\) construction produces a Condorcet domain; Proposition 3 gives its exact cardinality. Those are named combinatorial results, not complexity results. The comparison \(g(40)>|F_{40}|\) in §3.2 is an important numerical conclusion, but it is not a numbered complexity result. So there is no qualifying anchor for a continuous mirror under your criteria.

The strongest plausible population question I can offer is an unanchored extension of the paper’s maximum-size peak-pit-domain problem. Given alternatives \(A\) and a rational population distribution \(\mu\) over a finite support of rankings, choose a nonempty set \(D\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\) maximizing \(\mu(D)=\sum_{t\in D}\mu_t\), subject to \(D\) being a peak-pit Condorcet domain. The solution is the set \(D\) of preference types the designer permits. A natural regime is a large population of artificial agents drawing from a small library of ranking templates; type is a complete ranking, and mass is the fraction of agents using it. This fits the paper’s motivation of restricting agents’ choices to ensure transitive majority decisions. With uniform mass over all rankings, the objective is exactly the paper’s domain-cardinality objective scaled by \(1/|A|!\).

The weak point is that this is a weighted reformulation, not a result the paper proves. The selected object remains a discrete set of rankings, and the paper gives no algorithm or complexity classification for the weighted problem. I therefore cannot honestly predict whether it is tractable, inherits hardness, or is continuum-specifically hard. The positive case is a plausible new question prompted by the paper, but it does not meet the required named-anchor standard.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

No named result in the paper gives a computational problem or complexity guarantee. Theorem 1 establishes closure of the tensor construction, Proposition 3 counts the resulting rankings, and the \(g(40)>|F_{40}|\) result is an extremal comparison. The paper’s profiles appear in the definition of a Condorcet domain: the domain must guarantee transitive majority for every odd profile drawn from it. There is no input society whose masses an algorithm must process.

The proponent’s weighted maximum-domain problem is the closest extension. With uniform mass over all rankings, it simply rescales \(g(n)\). With arbitrary masses, it asks a new question: which ranking types should an institution permit to preserve the greatest share of a given population? That has a plausible artificial-agent story, but its feasibility depends on which types are included; the masses only weight the objective. Since a type is complete, the optimization keeps all of a type’s mass or excludes it. The continuum supplies weights without relaxing the paper’s combinatorial choice.

A stronger version would start with an actual population and find the least costly mass transfer of rankings that puts its support inside a peak-pit Condorcet domain. That is a coherent continuous preference-repair problem, but it adds an intervention and costs that the paper never studies. Its core question is which safe domain to target; the tensor construction and cardinality formula give no computational result about that choice. It could be worthwhile as a separate project, but it does not rescue a paper-anchored mirror.

For a fixed continuous society, checking whether its majority relation is transitive amounts to computing pairwise margins and checking for cycles. Requiring safety for every distribution over a chosen support makes the masses irrelevant again: the condition is about the support. So the negative case is strong for the requested mirror: none of the paper’s results survives as a named computational anchor, and the proposed extension changes the task. The broader claim that no related continuous problem could be worthwhile is weaker; the preference-repair version is plausible independently of this paper.
