The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent therefore raises no result-level anchor for a continuous mirror.

The strongest repair is the admissions model: represent applicant and seat categories as finite types with rational masses, use mass flows to define matchings, and ask how to compute the compromise core or a men- or women-optimal selection. That is a plausible high-multiplicity scenario. Fractional type-level flows do not invalidate it under ChoCo’s scope. But it introduces an algorithmic problem the paper does not study, along with continuous definitions of blocking, dominance, and covering. It is a new research direction, not a mirror of a named result in this paper. A continuous restatement of the axiomatic characterization would fare no better for ChoCo, whose target is computational questions.

So the negative case is strong if the verdict is about whether this paper supplies a worthwhile continuous *mirror*: the proponent’s own lead does not establish one. The broader claim that no scenario could make the follow-on worthwhile is much weaker. The paper’s large admissions setting and its explicit call for algorithms give that project a credible motivation. I would reject the proposed mirror while stopping short of claiming that the broader continuous matching project has no value.
