The case FOR (proponent)

This paper contains no numbered Theorem, Lemma, Corollary, or Proposition asserting a computational result. Its NP-hardness statements in the introduction are attributed to earlier work, and its own computational evidence is experimental. Under your anchor requirement, there is no honest theorem-anchored mirror to state.

The strongest unanchored candidate is a continuous version of Most-\(\mathrm{SRTI}\). In a large dormitory cohort, let each type record a student’s complete relevant information: their roommate preferences over types, acceptability, criterion choices, and any diversity attributes. A society is a rational distribution \(\mu\) over finitely many such types. The decision variable is a symmetric mass matching \(x_{tu}\), with any unmatched mass recorded as single. Ask for a weakly stable matching of those masses that lexicographically minimizes the aggregate criterion-distance totals from the paper’s definition. A type pair blocks if positive mass on both sides would strictly prefer matching to their assigned partner types or being single.

That is a plausible high-multiplicity scenario: a large incoming class, with many students sharing a finite set of roommate-relevant profiles. It preserves the paper’s stability and criteria-based objective, and the authors could plausibly recognize it as a population version of Most-\(\mathrm{SRTI}\). The natural first guess is a continuum-specific optimization problem: fractional mass can split across roommate types, and stability constraints must account for those splits. Whether it is tractable, inherits hardness, or is harder for continuum-specific reasons is open.

The weak point is decisive for this assignment: Most-\(\mathrm{SRTI}\) is defined and evaluated here, but the paper gives no numbered complexity result for it. So this candidate cannot serve as one of the required result anchors.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against an anchored mirror is that the paper supplies no computational result to mirror. Its NP-hardness claims concern earlier work; its own contribution is an ASP framework, new problem definitions, and experimental runtimes. Those experiments do not establish a complexity result. So the proposed Most-\(\mathrm{SRTI}\) mirror, however plausible, is a new computational question rather than a continuous version of a result proved here.

That distinction does not defeat the question itself. A coherent version would model a large dormitory cohort with finitely many complete roommate-relevant types. Let \(x_{tu}\) be the mass of type \(t\) paired with type \(u\), with unmatched mass recorded separately. A type pair blocks if positive mass on both sides would strictly prefer each other to their assigned partner types or being single. One could then optimize the aggregate criterion scores lexicographically. With profiles and preferences over types, this is a finite-input computational problem parameterized by the support size and rational masses. Personalized criteria and diversity constraints could also be included.

Preserving the paper’s named-person rankings would make repeated types hard to justify: those rankings vary with the cohort and can encode specific roommate requests. But dropping named requests is not a decisive objection here. Attribute-based roommate matching is central to the paper’s application, so a large cohort matched by profiles is a scenario its authors could plausibly recognize. Nor does stability automatically become vacuous: positive-mass types can still form blocking pairs. The paper’s Most-\(\mathrm{SRTI}\) equation also maximizes criterion distance while its prose and ASP encoding aim to minimize it, but choosing the intended direction is a repair, not a fundamental obstacle.

So I can rule out the proposed mirror as an anchor to a computational theorem in this paper. I cannot honestly rule out a worthwhile continuous model of its roommate-matching problem. The case against the universal claim is weak at that point: the best type-level Most-\(\mathrm{SRTI}\) candidate survives, even though it is not a mirror of a result this paper proves.
