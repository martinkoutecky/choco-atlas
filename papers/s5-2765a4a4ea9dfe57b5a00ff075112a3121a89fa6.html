The continuous mirror question

Given rational masses over finitely many complete agent types on each side, with each type specifying integer quotas and rankings of acceptable counterpart types, compute a critical relaxed-stable assignment whose matched-pair mass is at least \(\frac{2}{3}\) of the maximum among such assignments. Agents remain individuals with integral bundles, and relaxed stability applies pairwise as in Definition 6; criticality minimizes the integrated total deficiency. Require a finite encoding of the assignment and running time polynomial in the number of supported types and the input bit length.

The model it lives in

Use atomless populations with finite support over complete types on both sides. Each agent has integer quotas and ranks counterpart types, with members of a type tied; each agent receives an integral bundle. Encode the assignment by distributions over integral bundle patterns together with a realizable, mass-conserving pairing, and evaluate deficiency and relaxed stability at the individual level.

The case does not establish that bundle distributions have a polynomial-size representation that can be realized as individual pairings while preserving the paper’s individual-level blocking conditions.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 1’s existence claim and its \(\frac{2}{3}\)-approximation for maximum-cardinality critical relaxed-stable matchings. It leaves aside the finite-instance hardness discussion, the proposal framework’s technical lemmas, and the repair to Király’s uncertain-proposal definition.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can bundle-pattern distributions always be realized as measurable pairings with integral individual bundles and finite encoding size polynomial in the supported types and input bit length?
  • Can individual-level relaxed stability and criticality be represented so that a polynomial-time \(\frac{2}{3}\)-approximation algorithm exists for these type-mass instances?

The case FOR (proponent)

The lead is Type-Mass CRSM \(2/3\)-Approximation. Its anchor is Theorem 1, proved in this paper: in a bipartite matching instance with nonnegative integer lower quotas, positive integer upper quotas, and possibly tied preferences, a critical relaxed-stable matching exists, and a polynomial-time algorithm finds a \(\frac23\)-approximation to a maximum-cardinality critical relaxed-stable matching.

The natural high-multiplicity setting is a large course-allocation market: many students and many course sections, with repeated profiles. A student type records a student’s quotas and ranking of section types; a section type records its quotas and ranking of student types. All members of a type share that full description, and members of a counterpart type are tied. For example, the market could have \(N\) students and sections but only \(\tau\) distinct profiles, with \(N\) much larger than \(\tau\). This is a restriction to a homogeneous regime, but it retains the paper’s two-sided preferences, ties, quotas, and matching objective.

An instance gives rational masses \(\mu_i^A,\mu_j^B\) for finitely many types on the two sides, normalized so their total is \(1\), along with each type’s quotas and rankings. Quotas remain per-agent. A matching assigns each unit of mass an integral bundle of partners, within its upper quota; different members of one type may receive different bundles. Thus the type’s average bundle can be fractional. The assignment can be represented by distributions over integral bundle patterns and pair masses \(x_{ij}\), with a mass-conserving realization of those partner slots. Pair masses alone are not enough to specify the answer.

Criticality minimizes total deficiency, for example
\[ D(M)=\sum_{a\text{-types }i}\int_{X_i^A}\max\{0,q_i^--\deg_M(a)\}\,da +\sum_{b\text{-types }j}\int_{X_j^B}\max\{0,q_j^--\deg_M(b)\}\,db. \]
Relaxed stability is the paper’s Definition 6 applied to individual agents: a blocking pair must be justified from at least one side by the same full-quota and non-surplus-partner condition. It does not replace individual blocking with a positive-mass coalition deviation.

The prover’s question is: given this finite-support rational instance, produce a critical relaxed-stable assignment \(M\) whose matched pair mass \(\sum_{ij}x_{ij}\) is at least \(\frac23\) of the maximum over all critical relaxed-stable assignments. Require time polynomial in the number of supported types \(\tau\), the input bit length \(L\), and the quota bit lengths; the output is a finite encoding of the bundle distributions and their mass-conserving realization. The paper’s stated result is the algorithmic approximation, so this is the closest numbered anchor. Its separate hardness discussion for maximum-size CRSM is useful context, but it is not a second numbered in-paper theorem asserting hardness.

I would expect the approximation question to be tractable (Class A), though that is a conjecture for the mirror, not a consequence of Theorem 1. Divisibility lets the solver split each cohort among integral bundle patterns while keeping quotas and type-level preferences explicit; a configuration or separation formulation is a plausible route. A further question is whether exact optimization remains hard in this regime, or whether the fractional cohort assignment removes the finite problem’s obstruction. The paper’s cited hardness does not settle that: mass can split within a type, so a finite hard instance does not automatically embed unchanged.

The authors could plausibly recognize this as their problem in a high-multiplicity regime: the matching, criticality, relaxed-stability predicate, and cardinality objective all remain. The weakest point is the stability semantics. Type-pair masses do not by themselves retain who is matched to whom or which individual partners make a blocking pair. The proposed mirror therefore has to keep distributions over integral bundles and a realization of partner assignments. If that structure cannot be encoded with finite, polynomial-size descriptions, the attractive mass formulation will not have delivered the claimed algorithmic mirror.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only strong anchor is Theorem 1: a critical relaxed-stable matching always exists, and the paper gives a polynomial-time \(2/3\)-approximation to a maximum-cardinality one. That is plainly a computational result about matching a population, so the “no named result to mirror” objection does not apply.

The strongest version of the proposed mirror is also mathematically coherent. In a large course-allocation or hiring market, many agents can share the same full profile: quotas and rankings over counterpart types. Give each supported profile a rational mass, keep each agent’s bundle integral, and represent the assignment by distributions over bundles plus a realization of partner slots. Define deficiency per capita and apply the paper’s relaxed-stability condition to individual agents. This preserves the theorem’s central objects and gives a specific question: can one compute a \(2/3\)-approximation in time polynomial in the number of supported profiles and the input bit length? Pair masses alone would lose relevant information, but the bundle representation addresses that.

There is a genuine limitation: when preferences are over named individuals, those identities are part of an agent’s profile. Grouping such agents by broad attributes would change the instance, and often leave as many types as agents. The mirror is therefore credible only in a homogeneous regime where agents rank counterpart types, with ties among members of a type. But that regime is plausible for students and sections with shared rankings, or workers and positions ranked by common categories. Since ties are part of the paper’s model, this is a recognizable setting, not a foreign replacement.

The main remaining concern is that integral bundles and individual blocking may make the resulting optimization hard to encode compactly. That is a real research obstacle, but it does not defeat the mirror: whether the continuous problem admits a compact algorithm is exactly the computational question the programme is meant to ask. I cannot honestly make the universal negative case. The identity issue narrows the useful regime, but it does not eliminate it, and Theorem 1 remains a worthwhile anchor.
