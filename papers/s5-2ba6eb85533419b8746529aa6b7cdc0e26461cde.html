The continuous mirror question

Given finite hospitals \(H\) partitioned into regions, hospital orders, doctor types \(T\), rational mass distribution \(\mu\), type rankings, hospital priorities, capacities \(q_h\), regional caps \(q_r\), and target capacities \(\bar q_h\), compute the canonical doctor-proposing continuous FDA mass matching \(x^{\mathrm{CFDA}}\) and output a certificate of feasibility, continuous stability, and constrained efficiency.

The model it lives in

Use variables \(x_{th}\) and \(x_{t\varnothing}\) for the mass of type \(t\) assigned to hospital \(h\) or unmatched, with \(\sum_h x_{th}+x_{t\varnothing}=\mu_t\), hospital and regional capacity constraints, and the deterministic FDA proposal-and-rejection process as the objective rather than a new welfare maximization.

The objection that survived

The paper does not determine how group strategy-proofness should evaluate a type whose mass is split across hospitals, so the continuous coalition guarantee needs a new pointwise or lottery-based convention and proof.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers FDA endpoint computation, termination, continuous stability, and constrained efficiency, with group strategy-proofness conditional on a specified mass welfare convention. It leaves the simulations, institutional examples, comparative statics, and unrelated mechanism details untouched.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can batched proposals compute \(x^{\mathrm{CFDA}}\) in time polynomial in \(|T|\), \(|H|\), \(|R|\), and input bit length \(L\), without expanding to the common denominator \(N\)?
  • Which positive-mass stability definition yields an exact finite-to-continuous correspondence and useful rounding guarantees?
  • Under pointwise micro-agent welfare, does group strategy-proofness survive partial acceptance of a type?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is a single Class-A mirror of the paper’s flexible deferred acceptance result.

The lead anchor is Theorem 2. It is stated and proved in this paper’s appendix, via the more general contract-choice result called Theorem 4; it is not merely cited. Theorem 2 says that the flexible deferred acceptance algorithm terminates after finitely many steps, produces a stable matching for every input, and is group strategy-proof for doctors. The paper then derives Corollary 1: every matching produced by the mechanism is constrained-efficient. These are the paper’s strongest algorithmic results, although the qualification matters: the paper does not state an explicit \(P\), NP-hardness, FPT, or approximation theorem.

The mirror should be called Continuous Flexible Deferred Acceptance under Regional Caps.

An instance consists of:

  • a finite set of hospitals \(H\), partitioned into regions \(R\);
  • a fixed order of hospitals within each region, as required by the paper’s algorithm;
  • a finite set \(T\) of doctor types;
  • a rational mass distribution \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{T}\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\);
  • for every type \(t\), a complete ranking \(\succ_t\) over \(H\cup\{\varnothing\}\);
  • for every hospital \(h\), a priority order over doctor types, with responsive preferences;
  • per-capita physical capacities \(q_h\), regional caps \(q_r\), and target capacities \(\bar q_h\), satisfying \(\sum_{h\in H_r}\bar q_h\le q_r\).

A type is complete: it includes the doctor’s hospital preferences, acceptability, and every attribute used by hospitals in their priorities. Thus the model does not erase idiosyncratic information; it assumes that the large market contains repeated preference-and-priority profiles.

A feasible solution is a mass matching \(x=(x_{th})\), where \(x_{th}\) is the mass of type \(t\) assigned to hospital \(h\), and \(x_{t\varnothing}\) is unmatched mass. It must satisfy

\[ \sum_{h\in H\cup\{\varnothing\}}x_{th}=\mu_t, \]

\[ \sum_{t\in T}x_{th}\le q_h, \qquad \sum_{h\in H_r}\sum_{t\in T}x_{th}\le q_r. \]

Assignments to unacceptable partners are forbidden.

The computational objective is not an invented welfare maximization. It is to compute the canonical doctor-proposing FDA endpoint on these masses. Unmatched type mass offers itself to its most preferred hospital not yet rejecting it. Each hospital first retains its highest-priority offered mass up to \(\bar q_h\); hospitals then take turns, in the paper’s regional order, accepting further offered mass until a hospital capacity or regional cap binds. Rejected mass continues down its preference list. The output is the resulting rational mass matching \(x^{\mathrm{CFDA}}\), together with a rejection trace or fixed-point certificate.

Stability is the mass-scaled version of the paper’s Definition 2. A positive-mass deviation moves some \(\delta>0\) from a current assignment \(h'\) to a preferred hospital \(h\). The receiving hospital must be able to accept that mass under its priority order or have a vacancy. Such a blocking move is tolerated only when the receiving region is full, the move is cap-induced in the paper’s sense, and—when the source and destination lie in the same region—the post-move target-capacity excess satisfies

\[ x'_h-\bar q_h>x'_{h'}-\bar q_{h'}. \]

The solution question is therefore:

Given the compressed type distribution and rational capacities, compute \(x^{\mathrm{CFDA}}\), and certify that it is feasible, continuously stable, and constrained-efficient; additionally determine whether any positive-mass coalition of doctor types can jointly misreport and make every participating unit strictly better off.

This is recognisably the authors’ problem. The hospitals, regional caps, target capacities, doctor rankings, flexible acceptance rule, stability concept, and group-strategy-proofness objective are unchanged. Only the doctor side is represented by masses. That is especially natural for the large matching markets motivating the paper: the authors discuss Japanese residency matching with roughly \(8{,}000\) doctors, Chinese graduate admission with hundreds of thousands of students, and U.K. medical matching with roughly \(7{,}000\) doctors. The paper’s formal model is finite, but these applications provide a credible high-multiplicity regime. [The paper’s working-paper version](https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/kojima-130306.pdf) and [the published AEA record](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20101552) document both the model and its applications.

A plausible type regime would be a national residency or graduate-admission clearinghouse in which many applicants share the same complete ranking because they belong to the same specialty, medical-school, score-band, geographic, and career-preference cohort. Hospitals rank applicants by the same observable bands. There may be \(N\) applicants but only \(\tau\) such complete profiles, with \(N\gg\tau\); hospitals and regions remain fixed or grow much more slowly. This is not claiming that every matching market has high multiplicity. It is one sensible instance regime for precisely the problem studied here.

The expected classification is Class A. For rational masses, choose a common denominator \(N\), replace \(\mu_t\) by \(N\mu_t\), and scale capacities by \(N\). This recovers a finite market with repeated doctor types. Conversely, aggregating a finite market by identical types gives the continuous input. The continuous algorithm should be implementable without iterating over the \(N\) individual doctors: each type can make at most \(|H|\) distinct proposals, while hospital and regional choices can be computed from cumulative type masses. The natural conjecture is an exact algorithm polynomial in \(|T|\), \(|H|\), \(|R|\), and the input bit length \(L\), together with finite-population rounding guarantees.

The mirror deliberately covers only Theorem 2 and its efficiency consequence, Corollary 1. It does not claim to continuize the paper’s simulations, its examples, or every comparative-statics result.

The weakest point is formal eligibility. Theorem 2 is an algorithmic and mechanism-design theorem, not a named complexity classification. If ChoCo’s anchor gate requires the paper itself to assert “in \(P\)” or a hardness class, this paper does not qualify cleanly. There is also a genuine mathematical question about whether group strategy-proofness survives arbitrary positive-mass coalitions and whether fractional threshold effects create new behaviour absent from the finite theorem. Those are not cosmetic details; they are the main things the mirror would need to prove.

That weakness does not make the mirror artificial. It means the paper offers a strong high-multiplicity algorithmic object, but not yet a complexity theorem. The natural follow-ups are the exact compressed running time, finite-to-continuous convergence, coalition-proofness for mass reports, and the extension from disjoint regions to the hierarchical caps discussed in the paper’s appendix.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The negative case is decisive at ChoCo’s anchor gate: Theorem 2 is not a computational-complexity result. It proves that the finite FDA procedure terminates, is stable, and is group strategy-proof; Corollary 1 derives constrained efficiency. Neither theorem specifies an input representation, a decision or optimization problem, a running-time bound, an approximation guarantee, or a complexity class. “The algorithm terminates” is not a theorem that computing its outcome lies in \(P\).

Thus the proposed task—compute the FDA endpoint from a compressed distribution \(\mu\), give a polynomial running time, prove rounding guarantees, or test positive-mass coalition deviations—is supplied by the proponent, not by the paper. It may be a worthwhile new matching paper, but it is not a continuous mirror of a named computational result. The simulations and comparative-statics examples do not repair this gap.

The high-multiplicity story itself is not the problem. A residency or graduate-admission market containing many applicants with identical complete preference-and-priority profiles is perfectly defensible. Nor is existing high-multiplicity work an objection. The problem is what remains after granting that regime. With rational masses, choose \(N\) clearing all denominators and replace mass \(\mu_t\) by \(N\mu_t\) identical doctors. A mass version of FDA that treats identical types symmetrically is then just the finite FDA run aggregated by type. Its stability, strategy-proofness, and efficiency properties are inherited from the finite theorem. That observation is not itself a reason to reject the mirror—identical answers are allowed—but it shows that the proposed guarantees are transferred finite properties, not new continuous computational content.

The claimed compression is the only possible source of novelty: perhaps identical doctors can make proposals in batches, avoiding iteration over \(N\). But that would be a new compressed-implementation theorem for an already explicit finite algorithm. The paper does not establish it, and it is not a continuous optimization problem of the kind ChoCo targets: there is no exponential type-indexed LP, pricing problem, separation oracle, or population-driven complexity boundary. The proposed follow-up could be useful, but it is an algorithmic extension invented around the paper rather than a mirror of its result.

The group-strategy-proofness part is even less securely continuizable. In the finite theorem, each doctor receives one hospital, so “every coalition member is strictly better off” is unambiguous. In a mass matching \(x_{th}\), one type may be split among several hospitals. A continuous coalition therefore needs a new welfare convention: pointwise improvement of micro-agents, stochastic dominance over hospital lotteries, expected utility, or something else. If the definition is pointwise at the micro-agent level, the result reduces to the replicated finite theorem. If it is type-level or lottery-based, it is a new mechanism-design question, and Theorem 2 gives no reason that group strategy-proofness survives it.

Continuous stability and constrained efficiency have the same status. One can define positive-mass blocking transfers and a sensible mass analogue of the paper’s cap condition, but the resulting concepts require fresh definitions and proofs. They are not consequences of a computational theorem in the paper. Recasting the project as cap design, welfare optimization, or finding a stable mass matching would be more computationally substantive, but those would be new problems rather than mirrors of Theorem 2 or Corollary 1.

So I would reject this as a ChoCo mirror—not because the population story is implausible, nor because the continuous answer happens to coincide with the finite one, but because the paper contributes no qualifying computational result. The proposed continuous FDA project is credible as a new high-multiplicity matching study; it is not a worthwhile continuization of this paper under the programme’s stated anchor rule. The universal claim that no conceivable related scenario could ever be valuable would be too strong, but the proponent has not produced a surviving anchor.
