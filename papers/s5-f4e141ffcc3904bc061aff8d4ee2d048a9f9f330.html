The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set of client types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a rational symmetric pair-allocation matrix \(A\), and an exact polynomial-time separation oracle for the rational polyhedral epigraph of \(r(x)=W(x)-x^{\mathsf T}Ax\) on \(X=\prod_{t\in T}[0,\mu_t]\), decide whether \(r(x)\geq 0\) for every \(x\in X\); if not, return a rational violating composition.

The model it lives in

A society is a distribution over complete client types. A coalition has type-mass vector \(x\in X\); a type-\(t\) member receives utility \(\sum_s A_{ts}x_s\), so total participant utility is \(x^{\mathsf T}Ax\). Given normalized transferable surplus \(W(x)\), its retained budget slack is \(r(x)=W(x)-x^{\mathsf T}Ax\).

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers only Proposition 6.1’s budget-feasibility verification. It leaves the stability, welfare, potential-maximization, and empirical results uncontinuized.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can realistic clustered-learning surplus models provide the stated exact polynomial-time separation oracle for \(r(x)\)?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest anchor I can defend is Proposition 6.1, “Submodular verification of budget feasibility.” It says that, for a fixed pair allocation \(v\), all coalition budget constraints hold exactly when \(\min_{S\subseteq\N} r_v(S)\geq 0\). If the retained-slack function \(r_v\) is polynomial-time evaluable and submodular, budget feasibility can be verified in polynomial oracle time. The proposition also gives a sufficient condition: if \(W\) is submodular and all \(v_{ij}\geq 0\), then \(r_v\) is submodular. The paper proves the slack characterization and the sufficient condition; the polynomial-time minimization step invokes the established submodular-minimization result it cites.

I would mirror that budget-verification problem, not the whole paper. The continuous setting is a large federated system with many clients drawn from a modest number \(\tau\) of complete types. A type records every feature relevant to the model’s value and costs: for example, data profile, sample-size class, communication reliability, and participation cost. Think of a large pool of edge clients with repeated operating profiles, rather than a small cross-silo federation. Let type \(t\) have population mass \(\mu_t\), with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). A coalition is any type-mass vector \(x\) in \(X=\prod_{t=1}^{\tau}[0,\mu_t]\); allowing fractional \(x_t\) means a type’s clients can be split among coalitions.

For a type-level symmetric pair allocation \(A\), let a type-\(t\) member’s utility in a coalition of composition \(x\) be \(u_t(x)=\sum_s A_{ts}x_s\). The coalition’s total participant utility, in the same normalized units as its physical surplus, is \(x^\mathsf{T}Ax\). The diagonal captures interactions among distinct members of the same type in the large-population limit. If \(W(x)\) is the coalition’s normalized transferable surplus, its retained slack is \(r(x)=W(x)-x^\mathsf{T}Ax\). This keeps the paper’s question intact: can the coordinator afford the promised transfers for every coalition that might form?

I would hand a prover this problem: Continuous Type-Coalition Budget Feasibility. The instance gives rational type masses \(\mu\), a rational symmetric pair-allocation matrix \(A\), and a type-composition surplus oracle \(W\). It also gives an exact polynomial-time separation oracle for the rational convex-polyhedral epigraph of \(r(x)=W(x)-x^\mathsf{T}Ax\) over \(X\). Decide whether \(r(x)\geq0\) for every \(x\in X\); if not, return a rational \(x\in X\) with \(r(x)<0\). A “yes” solution certifies affordability for every fractional coalition; a “no” solution supplies an unaffordable coalition composition. This is the continuous analogue of Proposition 6.1’s universal coalition check. Convex polyhedral slack is a natural structural counterpart to the submodular slack used there: where a Lovász-type extension applies, it connects the fractional-mass domain to the discrete submodular case.

For this stated problem I expect Class A: minimize the convex slack over the rational box using separation-based convex optimization; with the exact rational polyhedral oracle, the decision and a violating composition can be found in time polynomial in \(\tau\), the encoding size, and the oracle cost. That expectation is conditional on the slack representation in the problem statement. It does not establish tractability for arbitrary federated-learning surplus functions. In particular, the paper itself notes that data complementarity can break submodularity, and its reported experiments have only four participants. Whether a type-compressed oracle of this kind arises from realistic learning and cost models is a substantive further question.

This mirror covers only budget-feasibility verification for pairwise transfers, the result of Proposition 6.1. It does not continuize outcomes, training randomness, or time; the expected learning value remains a coalition-composition input. It also does not claim that the paper’s NP-hard global-potential maximization becomes easy. That hardness is stated in prose after Proposition 6.3 and attributed to prior work, rather than presented as a numbered computational result, so I would not use it as a numbered anchor under the requested standard.

The soft spot is the oracle and convex-slack condition. The authors may accept the fractional-coalition feasibility question as theirs, but they could reasonably say that a well-behaved exact oracle for \(W(x)\), or a convex extension of its slack, is precisely what clustered learning does not generally provide. The mirror is strongest as a focused extension of their budget-verification result in a genuine high-multiplicity regime; it should not be sold as a general complexity result for their coalition-formation model.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Proposition 6.1 is a real computational anchor: it is a named polynomial oracle result about checking all coalition budget constraints. Nor can I rule out a sensible high-multiplicity regime. A large pool of clients with repeated data, reliability, and cost profiles could make type masses a natural description of a federated system. So the negative case cannot rest on there being no result to mirror or no plausible population scenario.

The weakness is in the proposed mirror’s tractability premise. Proposition 6.1 assumes access to coalition values and uses submodularity of the discrete slack \(r_v(S)\). The proposed continuous problem instead supplies an exact polynomial-time separation oracle for the epigraph of \(r(x)\), and assumes that slack is convex and polyhedral. That oracle already gives the generic convex-optimization machinery that certifies nonnegativity. The paper’s coalition-specific learning model does not establish such an oracle or such geometry for \(W(x)-x^\mathsf{T}Ax\); its values come from expected training outcomes, and the paper explicitly flags the cost of constructing and querying them. The proposed Class A result therefore depends on a strong new representation assumption, rather than following from continuizing the paper’s submodular-verification result.

A closer mirror would start with repeated complete client types, normalize pair transfers with population size, and derive a limiting budget slack from the finite models. But discrete submodularity does not by itself establish that this particular type-mass slack is convex and polyhedral, or that it has a low-dimensional separation oracle. A Lovász extension could preserve a discrete minimization problem, but it would not automatically produce the proposed formula over type compositions or a tractable FL value oracle. The paper supplies no assumptions from which to derive that bridge.

That is a substantial objection to this anchor as stated, but it does not defeat every stronger mirror. A concrete distributional FL model might yield a well-defined type-mass surplus and a tractable extension, and that would be a coherent continuous budget-verification question. The paper gives too little structure to rule that out. My honest negative case is therefore limited: the proponent has not shown that their oracle problem captures a computational result arising from this paper’s FL model, but I cannot make a credible universal case that no worthwhile mirror exists in any scenario.
