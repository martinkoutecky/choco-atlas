The continuous mirror question

Given a finite alternative set \(A\) and a rational mass vector \(\bar\mu\) over rankings, compute an alternative \(x\) and a fractional domination coupling \(z\) with row and column marginals \(\bar\mu\), supported on pairs where ranking \(r\) ranks \(x\) at least as highly as the top alternative of ranking \(s\). Determine and certify the worst-case \(\operatorname{MaxAvg}\) distortion of this group-oblivious rule over all hidden finite type distributions with ranking marginal \(\bar\mu\), and whether a ratio of \(5\) is tight under the chosen group-multiplicity regime.

The model it lives in

A finite-type metric voting society has types \(t=(g(t),r(t),d_t)\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\), where types record group, complete ranking, and metric distance profile. The group cost is \(C_g(a)=M_g^{-1}\sum_{t:g(t)=g}\mu_t d_t(a)\), the objective is \(\max_g C_g(a)\), the mechanism observes only \(\bar\mu_r\), and its decision is an alternative \(x\) with a fractional domination certificate \(z\); the objective is worst-case distortion over hidden compatible \(\mu\).

The objection that survived

As written, the minimax over all mechanisms lacks a precise mechanism encoding and input-size measure; the mirror should therefore be stated as the explicit computation of an alternative and fractional domination coupling from the visible ranking-mass vector.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the ordinal-information, group-oblivious Max-of-Avg results in Section 4.1, including the mechanism guarantee and its mass-transport analogue. It leaves Avg-of-Max, full-information mechanisms, group-aware mechanisms, and the known-alternative-distance setting untreated.

Open questions for a prover

  • What is the exact minimax distortion when \(k\) is fixed and every group has positive mass?
  • Does a \(5-\varepsilon\) lower-bound family survive with bounded type support and fixed \(k\)?
  • Can an optimal mechanism and its worst-case guarantee be represented and computed polynomially, beyond finding one feasible fractional domination coupling?

The case FOR (proponent)

The honest qualification comes first: this paper contains no named theorem establishing NP-hardness, membership in \( \mathrm{P} \), W[1]-hardness, FPT, or any comparable complexity classification. Its numbered results are distortion theorems. Thus, under ChoCo’s strict anchor rule, it has no admissible complexity anchor and cannot by itself support a Class A/B/C verdict.

If named approximation theorems count as a broader algorithmic anchor, the strongest positive case is its Max-of-Avg result.

Theorem 4.3, proved in this paper, says: for Max-of-Avg, an ordinal-information group-oblivious mechanism that returns an alternative whose domination graph has a perfect matching has distortion at most \(5\). The groups are unknown to the mechanism; it sees only ordinal preferences. The supporting Lemma 4.2, also proved here, states that if \(x\)'s domination graph has a perfect matching, then for every alternative \(y\),
\[ d(x,y)\le \frac{4}{n}\sum_{i\in N}d(i,y). \]
Theorem 4.1, likewise proved here, gives the matching lower bound: every ordinal-information group-oblivious mechanism for Max-of-Avg has distortion at least \(5-\varepsilon\), for every \(\varepsilon>0\), even with two alternatives and symmetric groups.

My proposed mirror is:

\[ \textit{Continuous Group-Fair Max-of-Avg Voting}. \]

An instance has a finite alternative set \(A\), \(k\) hidden groups, and a finite set of complete ground types \(\Theta\). A type \(\theta\) records its group \(g(\theta)\), ordinal ranking \(r(\theta)\), and its cardinal metric profile \(d_\theta(a)\) over alternatives. Its mass is \(\mu_\theta\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge0}\), with \(\sum_\theta\mu_\theta=1\). The full type includes the group and cardinal profile, so agents of one type are genuinely interchangeable. The mechanism observes only the ranking distribution
\[ \bar\mu_r=\sum_{\theta:r(\theta)=r}\mu_\theta, \]
not the group labels or cardinal distances.

For a group \(g\), let \(M_g=\sum_{\theta:g(\theta)=g}\mu_\theta\). The continuous objective is
\[ \operatorname{MaxAvg}_\mu(a) = \max_{g\in[k]} \frac{1}{M_g} \sum_{\theta:g(\theta)=g}\mu_\theta d_\theta(a). \]

The mechanism must output an alternative \(x\). To mirror the paper’s perfect-matching rule, it may also output a mass coupling \(z_{r,s}\) between left-hand ranking mass and right-hand ranking mass, satisfying
\[ \sum_s z_{r,s}=\bar\mu_r, \qquad \sum_r z_{r,s}=\bar\mu_s, \]
with \(z_{r,s}>0\) only when ranking \(r\) ranks \(x\) at least as highly as the top alternative of ranking \(s\). This is the continuous domination-graph condition: a fractional perfect matching, or equivalently a transportation-feasibility certificate.

The question is to design a mechanism \(M\) mapping the visible distribution \(\bar\mu\) to such an \(x\), minimizing
\[ \sup_I \frac{\operatorname{MaxAvg}_{\mu^I}(M(\bar\mu^I))} {\min_{a\in A}\operatorname{MaxAvg}_{\mu^I}(a)}, \]
where the supremum ranges over all hidden group assignments and metric realizations consistent with the observed rankings. A solution is an alternative together with its feasible mass coupling, and globally a mechanism with the smallest worst-case ratio.

This is a convincing population mirror in a high-multiplicity regime: many residents belong to a bounded number of districts or communities, and within each district many residents share one of finitely many standardized preference-and-location profiles. Thus \(n\) is large while the number of distinct complete types is moderate. Rational masses expand exactly into finite populations, while finite populations aggregate back to rational masses. The group objective is unchanged: finite group averages become conditional expectations, not a different fairness criterion.

The paper’s proof transfers cleanly. A finite profile with \(n_{g,t}\) agents corresponds to \(\mu_{g,t}=n_{g,t}/n\), and
\[ \frac{\sum_t n_{g,t}d_t(a)}{\sum_t n_{g,t}} = \frac{\sum_t\mu_{g,t}d_t(a)}{M_g}. \]
The domination graph becomes a mass-transport polytope. The triangle-inequality argument in Theorem 4.3 then becomes an integral argument, yielding the same upper bound \(5\). Normalizing the constructions of Theorem 4.1 supplies the corresponding \(5-\varepsilon\) lower-bound family. I would therefore expect this mirror to retain the paper’s sharp \(5\) distortion threshold, with the matching certificate computable by transportation or max-flow methods.

This is not a weakened problem: it preserves the groups, the Max-of-Avg objective, ordinal information, metric uncertainty, and group-obliviousness. It merely replaces a repeated finite population by its empirical distribution.

The scope is deliberately limited to Section 4.1: ordinal-information, group-oblivious Max-of-Avg voting. I would not automatically extend it to Avg-of-Max, where a vanishing-mass outlier can still determine a group maximum, or to group-aware mechanisms without specifying whether maxima mean support maxima or essential suprema.

The weakest point is also decisive for the formal programme: Theorem 4.3 is an approximation guarantee, not a complexity theorem. The continuous matching subproblem is algorithmically tractable, but the paper does not establish a ChoCo-style complexity classification, nor does it show that any discrete NP-hardness dissolves. So this is a strong, natural continuous mirror of the paper’s problem, but only a candidate starting point for a later complexity study—not evidence that this paper itself fills the computational gap.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative case is formal and decisive: this paper has no admissible computational anchor. Theorem 4.1 is a lower bound on distortion, Lemma 4.2 is a metric inequality, and Theorem 4.3 is an approximation guarantee. None asserts membership in \( \mathrm{P} \), hardness, parameterized complexity, or an algorithmic classification. The paper never studies the complexity of computing the mechanism, its matching, or the optimal distortion. Under ChoCo’s stated anchor rule, the paper therefore cannot support a continuous-computational verdict.

The proposed mirror is nevertheless mathematically legitimate, so the negative case should not pretend otherwise. With rational masses, choose a common denominator \(D\) and replace \(\mu_{g,t}\) by \(D\mu_{g,t}\) agents. Then
\[ \frac{\sum_t \mu_{g,t}d_t(a)}{\sum_t\mu_{g,t}} = \frac{\sum_t D\mu_{g,t}d_t(a)}{\sum_tD\mu_{g,t}}, \]
and every Max-of-Avg value and distortion ratio is preserved exactly. Conversely, every finite instance yields such a distribution. Thus the proposed model is a sound high-multiplicity representation.

But that observation supplies no new computational object in this paper. The mechanism still outputs one of finitely many alternatives; the fractional matching \(z\) is only a proof certificate, not a social outcome or an optimization problem studied by the authors. Its feasibility is a routine transportation problem over the explicitly represented ranking support, with rational capacities, and does not create a complexity frontier. The proposed minimax over all mechanisms is also not yet a computational problem unless a representation of mechanisms and an input-size measure are specified.

The claimed preservation of the sharp \(5\) threshold is weaker than stated in the natural bounded-district interpretation. The lower-bound construction of Theorem 4.1 uses \(k=\lambda+1\) groups, so the number of groups grows with the population. It does not establish the same sharp family when there are a fixed number of positive-mass districts. The vanishing-size exceptional group is precisely what drives the limit toward \(5\); a fixed-group continuum needs a separate construction.

So the proponent has defeated the obvious modelling objections: mass, groups, and conditional averages can all be made coherent. The honest negative conclusion is narrower but firm: this is a plausible high-multiplicity reformulation, not a worthwhile continuous-computational mirror *of this paper’s contribution*. To become one, it would need a new complexity question—perhaps succinct rational-mass input, optimal mechanism computation, or a fixed-\(k\) classification—which would be a follow-up project rather than an anchor supplied by the paper.
