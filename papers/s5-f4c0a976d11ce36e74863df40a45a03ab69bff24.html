The continuous mirror question

Given a finite tree \(T\) with rational edge lengths and a finite candidate set \(C\subseteq T\), determine whether there is a deterministic selector \(F\) from finitely supported rational probability measures on \(T\) to \(C\), with image exactly \(C\), such that no positive submeasure \(\lambda\leq\mu\) can be replaced by an equal-mass report \(\hat\lambda\) to produce \(\mu'=\mu-\lambda+\hat\lambda\) satisfying \(d(x,F(\mu'))>d(x,F(\mu))\) for \(\lambda\)-almost every true location \(x\).

The model it lives in

Types are locations in \(T\); a society is a finitely supported rational probability measure \(\mu\). The selector chooses a facility location in \(C\), agents receive utility equal to their distance from it, and a coalition is any positive submeasure that may report an equal-mass distribution.

The objection that survived

A mass coalition can redistribute fractional portions of a location type, which may change the GSP condition relative to coalitions of whole agents.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 1's candidate-set characterization and constructive GSP mechanism. It leaves aside the paper's discussion of benefit ratios and its open questions about SP mechanisms and more general metric spaces.

Open questions for a prover

  • Does the perimetric-distribution condition remain necessary and sufficient when coalitions may move fractional submass?
  • Can a mass-GSP selector with image \(C\) be constructed from a finite encoding of \(T\) and \(C\)?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive lead is Theorem 1, proved in this paper: for a tree \(T\) and a candidate set \(C\subseteq T_{\mathrm{facility}}\) of \(p\geq1\) points, there is a \(p\)-candidate group-strategy-proof mechanism whose output set is exactly \(C\) if and only if \(C\) is a *perimetric distribution*: either \(|C|=1\), or some \(\mu\in T\) is equidistant from every candidate in \(C\).

That is a named structural result about mechanism existence, but it does not state a complexity classification or running-time bound. So under your strict anchor rule, this paper has no qualifying computational-complexity anchor. Theorem 1 is the closest result; the lemmas support its proof, and the line-metric result mentioned in related work is cited from elsewhere.

If a structural existence result is acceptable as a conditional anchor, the most faithful mass version is this: given a finite tree with rational edge lengths and a finite candidate set \(C\), does there exist a deterministic anonymous selector \(F\) from rational, finitely supported probability measures over tree locations to \(C\), with image exactly \(C\), that is group-strategy-proof? For a society \(\mu\), a coalition is a positive submeasure \(\lambda\leq\mu\); it may replace its reports with any measure \(\hat\lambda\) of the same total mass, producing \(\mu'=\mu-\lambda+\hat\lambda\). The selector is group-strategy-proof if it is never the case that \(d(x,F(\mu'))>d(x,F(\mu))\) for \(\lambda\)-almost every true location \(x\). A solution is either such a selector or a proof that none exists.

The types are locations, mass is the population share at each location, and utility remains distance from the undesirable facility. A plausible regime is a large regional siting decision: many residents, but a modest number \(\tau\) of occupied location types. Replacing voter counts with rational masses preserves the paper’s outcome set, distance utility, and coalition manipulation question, so the authors should recognize the model as closely related. I would expect the same perimetric criterion to hold, and hence a tractable existence test once the tree and candidates are finitely encoded. That expectation needs a proof: the paper’s argument uses finite profiles and counts, and it does not establish the mass version.

This proposed question covers Theorem 1 only. Its weakest point is also the central limitation: even if the mass characterization is unchanged, the paper’s result is not a hardness result whose complexity might dissolve under continuization. It would be a new mass-domain extension of a mechanism-existence theorem, not a direct continuization of a named complexity classification.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The only plausible anchor is Theorem 1. It characterizes which candidate sets can be implemented by a GSP mechanism; it does not state the complexity of a computational problem. The paper has no result about finding an outcome from a supplied society, optimizing an intervention, or deciding a complexity class. Under ChoCo’s stated scope, that leaves no named computational result to mirror.

The proposed mass version is coherent. Location can be a complete type, and a large population concentrated at a modest number of locations is plausible. The obstacle is not the scenario or the use of mass. The natural question—whether a GSP selector with image \(C\) exists for every finitely supported rational society—still asks for a global mechanism-existence characterization. The society is not an instance whose computational treatment Theorem 1 studies: the theorem’s condition is about \(T\) and \(C\), independent of the reported profile.

There are two ways to try to make it computational, and neither gives this paper a substantial ChoCo mirror. Given \(T\) and \(C\), deciding whether such a selector exists is a geometric recognition problem. Given a society, computing an outcome requires fixing a selector; evaluating the paper’s subtree-based rule would amount to aggregating subtree masses and making comparisons. Conversely, asking an algorithm to search over arbitrary selectors has no finite mechanism representation specified in the paper. Adding one would define a new problem rather than continuize a result the authors established.

The possibility that the finite proof extends by replacing subtree counts with masses does not itself count against the mirror; a matching result could be worthwhile mechanism theory. It does, however, leave no computational question here whose complexity could change under high multiplicity. That is the strongest negative case: this paper may support a clean continuous extension, but it supplies no computational anchor for the programme.

I would be cautious about the universal claim that no scenario yields a worthwhile mirror. The high-multiplicity setting is sensible, and the mass extension could be mathematically interesting. The case against is strong on programme fit, but weak as a claim that the extension has no value at all.
