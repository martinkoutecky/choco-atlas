The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent names no qualifying anchor. The paper reports empirical prediction errors, correlations, and runtimes; its Monte Carlo procedures and neural network are not named complexity results or algorithmic guarantees. So there is no paper result here to hand to a prover as a continuous mirror.

The suggested type-level limit does not fill that gap. The paper’s MCN rules are conjunctions over named agents, and its outputs are indices for those individuals. If identical agents are replicated while keeping those rules, a rule that requires a type’s members requires every copy. Under the Banzhaf model’s uniform random coalitions, the chance that all \(n_t\) required copies are present is \(2^{-n_t}\); individual pivotality can therefore disappear as the type grows. Avoiding that collapse by making rules depend on type counts or mass changes the game representation. Summing individual indices by type, \(\Phi_t=\sum_{i\in t}\phi_i\), may be meaningful, but it asks for group influence rather than the paper’s individual influence.

That is the strongest case against treating this paper as a ChoCo anchor: a plausible continuous project would require a new typed game model and a new target quantity, rather than continuizing a result the paper establishes. I cannot honestly claim that every such project would be worthless. In particular, type-level Shapley totals might have a meaningful limit in a redesigned model. But that possibility is a separate research direction, not a surviving anchor from this paper.
