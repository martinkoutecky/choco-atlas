The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent names no anchor, and the paper supplies none of the required computational results. Propositions 4.2 and 4.3 characterize Core partitions; Theorem 4.4 proves segregation; Theorem 5.2 identifies the Core with the Myopic Stable Set. Algorithms A.5 and A.7 generate Core partitions, but their correctness is not a complexity classification: the paper gives no running time or computational problem whose complexity it establishes. A continuous version of one of these results would therefore add a computational question to the paper, rather than mirror a computational result it contains.

The workforce scenario is plausible: many employees can share a productivity type, so multiplicity itself is no objection. Its strongest version would give each productivity class a rational mass and ask for a Core-stable allocation of mass among coalition compositions. But that requires choices the paper does not settle. Production depends on coalition size and saturates at an absolute threshold \(\bar\rho\). In a population normalized to unit mass, a coalition of positive mass has size proportional to the population, so the threshold’s role disappears as the population grows; coalitions small enough for it to matter have vanishing mass. Preserving the threshold requires retaining a population-size scale and defining how fractional coalitions and positive-mass deviations work. Rescaling the threshold with population instead changes the production assumption. Either route could define a research problem, but it would be a newly specified computational model, not a continuous mirror of a named computational result here.

So the negative case is strong under the requested mirror standard: none of the proponent’s proposed work is anchored to an eligible result. It is weaker as the universal claim that no worthwhile continuous project could come from this paper. A carefully defined mass-based stability problem might be worthwhile in its own right; the paper alone does not establish that it would be.
