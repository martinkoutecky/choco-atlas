The case FOR (proponent)

Droop’s subject has a natural population mirror: take voter types to be complete rankings of the candidates, and let \(\mu_t\) be the fraction of voters with ranking \(t\). The output would be the elected set and the transfers of ballot mass. That is a plausible analogue of his electoral problem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest case against starts with the paper’s contents: it has no named computational result to mirror. Droop proposes and compares electoral systems, reports historical election figures, and works through how their ballots would be counted. His quota argument is a mathematical fact about securing seats, not a complexity result for a defined computational problem. The paper gives no complexity classification, algorithmic guarantee, or hardness result. On the requested anchor standard, there is therefore no qualifying result.

The proponent’s population model is nevertheless coherent. A large election could be represented by a distribution \(\mu\) over voter rankings, and a fractional version of preferential transfers could return an elected slate and the flow of ballot mass. Large electorates with repeated ballots are a sensible high-multiplicity regime. Fractional transfer and tie handling are reasonable model choices, not objections to the mirror.

The stronger candidate is a robustness question: given \(\mu\), how little voter mass must change for the elected slate to change? Droop’s examples of narrow majorities make that a natural question. But it imports a new optimization problem; the paper does not state or prove a computational result about minimum changes. The same is true of asking for the complexity of tabulating the fractional procedure. Both could be worthwhile ChoCo research, but neither mirrors a named computational result in this paper.

So the negative case is strong under the requested anchor rule, but weak as the universal claim that no continuous mirror is worthwhile. The population itself continuizes naturally; what fails is the link from that model to a computational result in Droop’s paper.
