What the mirror covers

The case FOR (proponent)

I can’t make a result-anchored case for a continuous computational mirror: the paper contains no numbered result asserting a computational complexity or algorithmic claim. Theorem 1, the closest candidate, says that a bargaining solution satisfies weak Arrow, independence of irrelevant expansions, efficiency, scale invariance, and continuity if and only if it is a coarse Nash solution defined by an improving set. That is an axiomatic characterization, not a result about computing a solution. Corollary 1 adds anonymity and requires the improving set to be symmetric; it is axiomatic too.

A continuous population of bargaining participants might be a research extension, with types grouping participants who share all bargaining-relevant attributes and mass representing their population shares. But the paper’s model has a finite set of players, and its continuity is in utility-outcome space. Extending it to a continuum of participants would require new definitions and results; it would not mirror a computational anchor in this paper. Under the requested criteria, there are no anchors and therefore no continuous problems to state per anchor.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s case has no result anchor to preserve: Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 characterize bargaining solutions from axioms; neither asserts an algorithm, a complexity bound, or a computational problem. The paper’s “continuity” is continuity of a choice correspondence as feasible sets vary in the Hausdorff topology. Its fixed set of players indexes coordinates of utility vectors. Neither is continuization of a population.

The strongest possible extension would replace those finitely many players with an atomless population of bargaining participants, represent an outcome by a utility profile \(u(\theta)\), and define efficiency, scale invariance, and Nash-style improvements over profiles—perhaps using \(\int \log u(\theta)\,d\mu(\theta)\). One could then ask whether analogous axioms characterize coarse solution correspondences. That might be worthwhile bargaining theory. But it changes the theorem’s domain into an infinite-dimensional space of utility profiles; it does not mirror a computational result in this paper. The paper specifies no society given as a distribution over types, intervention to optimize, algorithm, or complexity parameter. To make the extension computational, one would first have to invent an encoding and a new computational task. Any resulting complexity landscape would belong to that new model, not to a result this paper established.

So the proponent is right that there are no anchors to state per result, but “no continuous problems to state” is too broad: an axiomatic continuum-player extension is conceivable. The stronger negative is that such an extension falls outside ChoCo’s stated computational programme. That is a solid case against a mirror for this programme, though not a proof that the extension lacks value in bargaining theory.
