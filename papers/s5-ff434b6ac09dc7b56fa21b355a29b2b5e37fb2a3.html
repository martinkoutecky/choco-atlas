The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set of complete patient–donor types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), block compatibility \(A\subseteq T\times T\), rational transplant weights \(w_{tu}\), and a fixed cycle cap \(k\), form the LP over compatible type cycles \(C\) of lengths \(2,\ldots,k\), with variables \(y_C\ge0\), supplies \(\sum_C a_{tC}y_C\le\mu_t\), and objective \(\max\sum_C y_C\sum_{(t,u)\in C}w_{tu}\). Compute its optimum and determine whether its cycle-formulation relaxation dominates the corresponding type-level PE and PRE relaxations, as in Lemma 2.

The model it lives in

Each type records all compatibility and weight attributes used by the model. The mass \(\mu_t\) is the available supply of that type, and \(y_C\) is the mass assigned to a compatible cycle pattern; \(a_{tC}\) counts the occurrences of type \(t\) in that pattern. Cycles still consist of distinct participants, including when a pattern repeats a type.

What the mirror covers

Covers the paper’s cycle-only weighted KEP and the type-level analogue of Lemma 2’s cycle-versus-edge-formulation LP comparison; leaves chain-and-cycle models and empirical runtime results aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • For which clinical profile and priority systems does exact block compatibility and weighting yield a small support size \(|T|\)?
  • Does the LP-dominance relation of Lemma 2 persist for natural type-level PE and PRE formulations?

The case FOR (proponent)

The paper has no numbered result classifying a KEP problem as NP-hard, polynomial-time solvable, or otherwise. It does state that bounded \(k\)-cycle KEP is strongly NP-hard for \(k\ge3\), citing earlier work, but that statement is not a numbered result here. My strongest named computational anchor is Lemma 2, a polyhedral result that the paper uses in its study of cycle-only formulations.

Lemma 2 says that the cycle formulation dominates both the partitioned edge formulation \((*\mathrm{PE})\) and the partitioned-and-reduced edge formulation \((*\mathrm{PRE})\). In the paper’s definition of dominance, this means the feasible region of the cycle formulation’s LP relaxation is contained in the feasible region of each of the other two relaxations. The lemma is stated here; the authors say it follows by the same proof used for the cycle-versus-edge comparison in [11]. The paper computes with these cycle-only formulations. This is an optimization-model anchor, not a new complexity classification.

My lead mirror is Type-Continuous Bounded-Cycle Kidney Exchange, with a fixed cycle cap \(k\), such as \(k=3\) or \(k=4\).

An instance consists of a finite set \(T\) of complete patient–donor-pair types, rational masses \(\mu_t\ge0\) summing to \(1\), a compatibility relation \(A\subseteq T\times T\), and nonnegative rational transplant weights \(w_{tu}\) for compatible pairs of types. A type records everything the problem uses: compatibility behavior, eligibility, and the relevant priorities or weights. A regime for this mirror would be a large national or multi-region exchange pool with many pairs but a much smaller catalog of recurring profiles—for example, \(n\) in the thousands and \(\tau=|T|\) in the tens or hundreds. Exact compatibility signatures matter: if two pairs differ in a way that changes a compatibility edge or its weight, they must be different types.

Let \(\mathcal C_k\) contain every compatible cyclic type sequence \(C=(t_1,\ldots,t_\ell)\) with \(2\le\ell\le k\), including sequences that repeat a type. A repeated type means distinct pairs of that type take the different positions in the cycle. The decision variable \(y_C\ge0\) is the mass of exchanges run with pattern \(C\). If \(a_{tC}\) counts how often \(t\) occurs in \(C\), the question is to maximize
\[ \sum_{C\in\mathcal C_k} y_C \sum_{i=1}^{\ell} w_{t_i t_{i+1}} \]
subject to
\[ \sum_{C\in\mathcal C_k} a_{tC}y_C\le\mu_t \qquad(t\in T), \]
where \(t_{\ell+1}=t_1\). A solution is an optimal mass allocation \(y\). Participants still receive whole transplants in whole cycles; the fractions describe how much of each type participates in each cycle pattern.

This is the same underlying problem the paper studies: maximize the weighted number of transplants subject to compatibility and the cycle-length limit. For a discrete pool with \(n_t\) pairs of type \(t\), setting \(\mu_t=n_t/n\) maps every discrete packing to a feasible continuous allocation. The reverse correspondence can require a larger cohort, so this is a high-multiplicity relaxation, not an identity for every finite instance.

For fixed \(k\), there are at most \(O(\tau^k)\) cycle patterns, so the stated problem is an explicit LP of polynomial size in \(\tau\) and the input bit length. I expect this version to be Class A. The tractability comes from the fixed operational cycle cap and the finite type support; Lemma 2 motivates the cycle-based formulation but does not itself prove this continuous algorithmic result. If \(k\) is part of the input, the explicit pattern list can be exponential, and pricing or other formulations become further questions.

The mirror covers the paper’s cycle-only weighted KEP and the cycle-packing structure compared in Lemma 2. It does not claim to continuize the paper’s chain-and-cycle variants or its empirical runtime results. Further questions include the integrality gap against finite pools, whether the paper’s LP-dominance relation persists under type aggregation, and how the continuous cycle model behaves when \(k\) is variable.

The weakest point is whether the proposed types stay few while preserving exact compatibility and weights. Kidney compatibility can be highly individualized; if exact profiles produce nearly one type per pair, the intended high-multiplicity regime disappears. The case holds only for programs whose compatibility and priority rules genuinely create recurring, indistinguishable classes. That is a plausible instance regime for a large pooled program, but the paper’s experiments do not establish that its own datasets have it.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative is that the proposed mirror changes the anchor. Lemma 2 compares the LP relaxations of formulations on a graph of named patient–donor pairs. The proposed mass LP instead asks for an optimum in a new type-level model. Asking whether formulation dominance survives aggregation could be a legitimate follow-up, but Lemma 2 does not establish that result. The paper gives no numbered complexity classification for the KEP; its own algorithmic contributions are constraint reduction and empirical performance comparisons.

There is a sharper modeling problem with the mass LP. In this paper, compatibility is a relation between a particular donor and a particular patient, and the arc weights are set for particular transplants. A pair’s blood and HLA profile alone need not make its outgoing compatibility and weights uniform across all pairs in a proposed type. To preserve the paper’s graph exactly, types would have to group pairs with matching compatibility and weight behavior throughout the pool. In the sparse, irregular graphs the paper studies, that can leave nearly every pair in its own type, removing the high-multiplicity advantage. This is not an objection to putting an individual price in a type; the difficulty is preserving a pairwise compatibility relation in a small set of types.

The best repair is a pool whose compatibility and weights really are block-structured by recurring clinical profiles. Then the type-cycle LP is coherent, including cycles that use several distinct pairs of the same type, and for fixed \(k\) it has at most \(O(\tau^k)\) patterns. But the paper does not show that its data have this structure. Replacing pairwise crossmatches with average compatibility would not repair the mirror: expected compatible edges do not guarantee executable cycles, and modeling that uncertainty would ask a different question.

So the case against is strongest as a claim about this paper: Lemma 2 is not the result the proposed LP computes, and the paper’s application gives no evidence for the small, exact type support that would make the LP a faithful mirror. It is not an airtight universal case. A genuinely block-structured kidney-exchange pool could make the proposed type-level problem worthwhile; the supplied paper simply does not establish that this is a credible regime for its results.
