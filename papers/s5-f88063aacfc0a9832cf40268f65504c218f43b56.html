The continuous mirror question

Given finite sets of employer types \(F\) and worker types \(W\), rational masses \(\alpha_f,\beta_w\) summing to \(1\) on each side, and strict rankings over opposite-side types and unemployment, consider atomless agents within each type. Outcomes are feasible matching flows \(x_{fw}\ge 0\), with unmatched mass allowed; a coalition can deviate by feasibly reassigning positive-mass subpopulations so every participating agent strictly improves. Is there an explicit computable type-pair deletion rule extending the paper’s Irving–Leather and EADA reductions for which the unique vNM stable family equals the core of the resulting reduced type profile?

The model it lives in

A two-sided atomless matching market with finitely many agent types, rational type masses, strict preferences over types, and matching-flow variables \(x_{fw}\). Its target is a constructive characterization and computation of the vNM stable family through the core of a reduced type profile, rather than optimization of a scalar objective.

The objection that survived

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive lead is Theorem 2, but it is not a qualifying anchor under your rule. Proved in this paper, it says that for a finite one-to-one market \(P\), if \(P^\star\) is formed by deleting the pairs in \(\mathcal E^{IL}\cup\mathcal E^{EADA}\), then the unique vNM stable set is \(\{C(P^\star)\}\). This is a constructive structural characterization; it does not assert that a problem is in P or give a runtime bound. Proposition 10, also proved here, says every matching outside \(C(P)\) has a path of at most three dominance steps to a core matching. That is a path-length bound, not a complexity result. The polynomial-time algorithm mentioned for Wako is cited in the related literature, not a numbered result of this paper.

If computational characterizations are allowed as near-miss anchors, the most plausible mirror is an Atomless Cohort Stable-Set Problem. Consider a large labor market with finitely many employer/job types and worker qualification types, each type comprising many otherwise interchangeable agents. An instance gives rational masses for those types and strict rankings over types on the other side. An outcome is a mass-preserving matching between the two sides, with unmatched mass allowed. A coalition can deviate when positive masses of firms and workers can be rematched among themselves so every participant strictly prefers the new partner type to their current assignment. The task is to return the vNM stable family of such outcomes, represented exactly, and determine whether it is unique and equals the core of a reduced type profile. The proposed reduction would need precise continuum versions of the paper’s Irving–Leather and EADA pair eliminations; those are part of the question, not results I can assume.

I would investigate tractability first: with finitely many types, matching outcomes are mass flows, and core computation has a finite-dimensional optimization structure. But the vNM external-stability condition and the reduced-profile construction could defeat that expectation, so I would not claim a class without further work. The mirror covers Theorem 2’s characterization only; Proposition 10 is outside its scope.

The weakest point is that this model replaces rankings over named individuals with rankings over types. That is a substantial change to the paper’s strict-preference model, and it is uncertain whether the authors would regard the resulting stable-set question as theirs. So this is a concrete research direction, but the paper supplies no numbered computational-complexity anchor for it. Under the stated anchor requirement, the honest positive case stops there.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest negative point is that this paper gives ChoCo no named complexity result to continuize. Theorem 2 is the nearest anchor: it characterizes the unique vNM stable set as the core of a reduced profile. It gives a constructive route to computation, but states no complexity bound for an input problem, runtime parameters, or algorithm. That leaves no computational landscape here to carry into a population model.

The proponent’s best version is a sensible research question: represent employer and worker cohorts by finitely many types with rational masses, outcomes by mass-preserving flows, and deviations by feasible positive-mass coalitions. One could then ask whether a suitably defined reduced profile’s core characterizes the vNM stable set. I would not reject that model as meaningless or use its new mass-matching features as a scope objection. Its value as a project, however, would come from establishing a new characterization and computational model for atomless matching. Theorem 2 does not provide a complexity result whose continuous counterpart this would be. Even a sharper version with a finite decision problem and a prescribed representation for the stable family would not change that gap.

Proposition 10 does not supply another computational anchor. Its three-step bound is a structural statement about dominance paths; a mass-cohort analogue would require its own definition of feasible deviations and paths. The polynomial-time algorithm by Wako is cited prior work on the finite marriage model, not a result of this paper and not a continuous-population treatment. Neither can substitute for a numbered complexity result in this paper.

So I would vote against treating this paper as a ChoCo computational-mirror case. That is a narrower claim than saying the cohort problem could never be worthwhile: the honest weakness in the negative case is precisely that it cannot rule out an independently valuable new project. It can say that the paper itself does not anchor one.
