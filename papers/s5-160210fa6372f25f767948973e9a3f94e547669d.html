What the mirror covers

The proposed mirror restates Top-\(k\)-Agg and its EPTAS; the paper’s broader Top-Agg approximation results also use distributional profiles, so they do not provide a separate discrete-to-continuous bridge.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest positive mirror is Continuous Top-\(k\) Population Aggregation. Imagine a large consumer panel ranking its top \(k\) choices from a fixed catalog. A type is one ordered top-\(k\) list, with every unlisted candidate tied below it; its mass is the fraction of respondents with that list. For example, with \(20\) products and \(k=3\), there are at most \(20\cdot19\cdot18=6{,}840\) possible types, so a panel of millions can have a much smaller support \(\tau\). Since this objective uses only the top-list, those respondents are indistinguishable for the problem.

Precisely, an instance gives \(C=[n]\), \(k\), a support \(T\subseteq\mathfrak T_n^k\), and binary-rational masses \(\mu_t>0\) summing to \(1\). For a complete ranking \(\sigma\), let \(K(\sigma,t)\) count pairs ordered oppositely by \(\sigma\) and \(t\), ignoring pairs tied because both candidates were omitted. The objective is
\[ D_\mu(\sigma)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t K(\sigma,t). \]
The task is to output a permutation \(\sigma\) with \(D_\mu(\sigma)\le(1+\varepsilon)\min_\rho D_\mu(\rho)\). Thus the population is continuous, while the decision remains a complete, integral ranking.

My lead anchor is Theorem 1.6, “EPTAS for Top-\(k\)-Agg,” proved in this paper. It states that for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), Score-then-Adjust returns a \((1+\varepsilon)\)-approximation, in time \(O(n\log n+q2^q)\), where \(q=\lceil(1+1/\varepsilon)(k-1)\rceil\). The algorithm sorts by top-list scores, then finds the best order of the first \(q\) candidates. [Theorem 1.6 in the paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.01537). For an explicit support encoding, accumulating scores and the pairwise weights for that prefix takes \(O(\tau k+\tau q^2)\) rational operations; the arithmetic has polynomial bit cost in the total mass encoding length \(L\). The paper’s displayed bound abstracts these support operations. The approximation is Class A in the short-list regime; its \(q2^q\) term makes the dependence on \(k\) and \(\varepsilon\) explicit.

This mirror is plausible because it preserves the paper’s actual question: choose a full ranking minimizing expected pairwise disagreement with the population’s partial rankings. It does not fractionalize the outcome or alter the loss. The paper also says in its introduction, citing Ailon, that Top-Agg is NP-hard even when each input list ranks exactly two candidates. That hardness is not a numbered result of this paper, so I do not use it as an anchor; it does indicate that exact optimization should be Class B. Any finite unweighted instance maps to masses \(n_t/N\), preserving the objective’s optimizer. Further questions include whether exact hardness persists at small support \(\tau\), and whether the EPTAS extends to broader regimes of list lengths.

The weak point is substantial: the paper already defines a voting profile as a distribution over top-lists, defines its cost as expected distance, and explicitly allows a weighted-support view. [Definitions 2.3–2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.01537v2). So this is an unusually clean demonstration that a high-multiplicity population is a sensible object—and the authors would plainly recognize it—but the proposed continuous model is almost exactly their existing model. That makes the positive case for a valid mirror strong, while leaving little room to claim that continuization itself is new here.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is a direct collision: the proposed “continuous” problem is already the paper’s input model. A voting profile \(p\) is defined as a distribution over top-lists, and the paper explicitly allows it to be viewed as a set of top-lists with weights. Set \(T\) to the profile’s support and \(\mu_t=p(t)\). Then the proposed population objective is exactly
\[ D_\mu(\sigma)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t K(\sigma,t)=K(\sigma,p). \]
For top-lists of length \(k\), this is precisely Top-\(k\)-Agg. The paper keeps the output ranking integral, as the mirror should; its population input is already a distribution of types.

This applies to the proponent’s strongest anchor, the EPTAS for Top-\(k\)-Agg. The theorem already gives an approximation scheme for arbitrary weighted profiles, not just equally weighted lists. Normalizing voter counts into fractions changes the objective only by a common scale factor. The algorithms also already compute quantities such as scores and pairwise disagreement probabilities from \(p\). Describing the explicit support by \(\tau\) and the rational masses by bit length \(L\) may sharpen the input accounting, but it does not create a new continuous problem or a new mirror.

A more vivid scenario—millions of consumers choosing ordered top-\(k\) lists from a catalog—does not escape the collision. It gives a plausible story for the paper’s distribution, but the story changes no mathematical object. The same holds for the paper’s broader Top-Agg PTAS: its profile is already a distribution over top-lists. This is stronger than finding that an outside high-multiplicity result happens to exist; the paper whose result is being mirrored already formulates and solves the distributional problem.

One could study uncertainty from a sampled profile, or allow mass transfers that change which ranking is optimal. Those may be worthwhile research questions, but they add a statistical or manipulation problem the paper does not ask. They cannot rescue a continuous mirror of its aggregation approximation results. I cannot rule out every valuable project inspired by this setting; I can say that a mirror preserving these results’ problem is already present in the paper.
