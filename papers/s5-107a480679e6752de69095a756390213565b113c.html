The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is an entropy-based continuous population mirror of the paper’s extremal problem. Let the alternatives be \([n]\), and let each voter type \(t\) be a ranking in \(T=L([n])\). An instance is \(n\); a society is a distribution \(\mu\) over \(T\), with \(\mu_t\) the fraction of voters of type \(t\). Call \(\mu\) Condorcet-safe when every odd finite electorate whose types lie in \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\) has a transitive pairwise-majority relation. The question is to find a safe distribution maximizing Shannon entropy, \(H(\mu)=-\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t\log\mu_t\).

This has a plausible scenario: a voting institution serves a very large, interchangeable population and wants the broadest possible repertoire of preference types while guaranteeing that majority voting stays transitive for every odd group drawn from the repertoire. The ranking is the complete type, mass is the population fraction, and a solution is the distribution itself. The population can be much larger than its number \(\tau\) of distinct ranking types.

There is an exact connection to the paper’s objective. For every safe \(\mu\), its support is a Condorcet domain \(D\), so \(H(\mu)\leq\log|D|\). The uniform distribution on any domain \(D\) attains \(\log|D|\). Thus the maximum entropy is \(\log f(n)\), and its maximizing supports are precisely maximum Condorcet domains. This is the strongest point for the mirror: its answer recovers the paper’s extremal object exactly.

There are no qualifying anchors under the requested criterion. The paper gives no numbered theorem, lemma, corollary, or proposition classifying a computational problem as tractable, hard, or parameterized tractable. The closest result is Theorem 1, proved here: it establishes \(\lim_{n\to\infty} f(n)^{1/n}\geq 2.198139\), using Fishburn’s inequality \(f(n+m-1)\geq f(n)f(m)\) and the found domain of size \(3{,}155{,}366\) on 20 alternatives. In the entropy formulation this gives the corresponding asymptotic lower bound on \(\max H(\mu)\). It is an extremal bound, not a complexity classification. The inductive search and its table report improved lower bounds, but do not provide a named runtime or complexity theorem. So there is no valid per-anchor complexity question or A/B/C prediction to report from this paper.

The mirror covers only the global maximum-domain-size problem and the asymptotic bound; it does not extend to the paper’s subclass results or search method. Its weakest point is substantial: the mass values do no work in the safety condition, and entropy makes the optimum uniform on its support. The formulation therefore repackages domain cardinality as population entropy rather than showing that high multiplicity changes the computational landscape. Authors could recognize the exact correspondence, but the paper supplies no complexity result for the proposed continuous problem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The strongest objection is that this paper supplies no named computational result for ChoCo to continuize. Theorem 1 is an asymptotic lower bound on \(f(n)\); Propositions 1–3 are construction and growth inequalities; the observations and tables report finite search results. Section 5 gives a heuristic search method, but no theorem about its runtime or the complexity of a computational problem. The paper’s computational work supports extremal combinatorics; it does not classify bribery, control, campaigning, robustness, or another computational task. By the stated criterion, that is decisive.

The entropy proposal is coherent, and it is the strongest possible counterexample to that objection. If \(\mu\) is safe exactly when its support is a Condorcet domain, then
\[ H(\mu)\leq \log |\operatorname{supp}(\mu)|\leq \log f(n), \]
with equality for the uniform distribution on a maximum domain. But this adds a population-design problem to the paper; it does not continuize a computational result the paper proves. The masses do no work in the safety constraint, and the optimum makes them uniform. The feasible distributions form a union of simplex faces indexed by Condorcet domains, so the apparent continuous optimization still turns on choosing the largest admissible support.

That mass-independence follows from the property being studied, not from a poor choice of type space. A Condorcet domain must protect against every odd finite electorate drawn from its support. Any positive mass for a type therefore gives it standing in that universal test. Even if the test is phrased over measurable subpopulations, a finite counterexample can be scaled down to fit beneath every positive type mass. The faithful population version remains support-based. Testing only the society’s aggregate weighted majority, or requiring safety only for subpopulations above a chosen mass threshold, would make mass matter, but would change the paper’s universal domain property into a different question.

So the paper can inspire worthwhile new questions about preference repertoires, but that is not enough to establish a worthwhile *computational mirror* under ChoCo’s stated remit. The negative case is strongest on this boundary: the entropy formulation is mathematically valid, yet crediting it requires expanding the remit from continuizing a paper’s computational results to inventing a continuous optimization problem related to its extremal object.
