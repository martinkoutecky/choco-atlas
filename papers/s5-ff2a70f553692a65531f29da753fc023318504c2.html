The continuous mirror question

Given candidates \(C\), complete ranking types \(T\), and rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), define \(\Delta_\mu(x,y)=\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t(\mathbf{1}[x\succ_t y]-\mathbf{1}[y\succ_t x])\). Let \(S_\mu(x,y)\) be the maximum minimum edge margin over directed paths from \(x\) to \(y\), with value \(0\) if no path exists. Determine the exact undefeated set \(SC_\infty(\mu)=\{a\in C:\nexists b\in C,\ \Delta_\mu(b,a)>0\ \wedge\ \Delta_\mu(b,a)>S_\mu(a,b)\}\), or decide whether a designated candidate belongs to it.

The model it lives in

A high-multiplicity election with ranking types \(t\in T\) carrying masses \(\mu_t\); compute pairwise margins and max–min return-path strengths, then output the undefeated candidates.

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is modest: this paper admits a natural high-multiplicity mirror for computing Split Cycle winners, but it is not a strong ChoCo anchor.

First, the qualification matters. The paper contains no numbered theorem asserting NP-hardness, membership in \(\mathrm{P}\), fixed-parameter tractability, or a comparable complexity classification. It is primarily axiomatic. Thus, under the anchor rule interpreted strictly, it has no qualifying computational anchor.

The closest defensible lead is Lemma 3.17, proved in this paper. It states that, for any finite profile \(\mathbf P\), candidate \(a\) defeats candidate \(b\) exactly when

\[ \mathrm{Margin}_{\mathbf P}(a,b)>0 \]

and this margin is larger than the strength of the strongest path from \(b\) back to \(a\), where path strength is the minimum margin on the path. The paper immediately observes that this yields an efficient Floyd–Warshall-style algorithm for computing the Split Cycle defeat relation and winners. This is an algorithmic characterization, not a formal theorem that winner determination is in \(\mathrm P\).

My lead mirror would be:

Continuous Split Cycle Winner Determination \((\mathrm{CSCWD}_\infty)\). An instance consists of a finite candidate set \(C\), a finite set \(T\) of complete ranking types over \(C\), rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_{t\in T}\mu_t=1\), and optionally a designated candidate \(a\). Define the normalized pairwise margin

\[ \Delta_\mu(x,y) = \sum_{t\in T}\mu_t \bigl(\mathbf 1[x\succ_t y]-\mathbf 1[y\succ_t x]\bigr). \]

For a directed path \(p\) from \(x\) to \(y\), let its strength be the minimum \(\Delta_\mu\)-margin on its edges, and let \(S_\mu(x,y)\) be the maximum strength of a path from \(x\) to \(y\), with value \(0\) if no such path exists. Candidate \(b\) defeats \(a\) when

\[ \Delta_\mu(b,a)>S_\mu(a,b). \]

The task is to output the exact undefeated set

\[ SC_\infty(\mu) = \{a\in C:\text{ no candidate defeats }a\}, \]

or, in the decision version, determine whether \(a\in SC_\infty(\mu)\). A valid solution is the exact winner set, or a certificate consisting of the relevant widest-path values and, for a losing candidate, an explicit defeating candidate.

This is a genuine continuous-population object: \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of a large society with ranking type \(t\), not an outcome lottery or noise distribution. A natural regime is a national or large online election with millions of voters, a fixed or moderately growing number of candidates, and perhaps hundreds or thousands of distinct ballot types. Voters sharing a complete ranking are indistinguishable for this problem; the relevant comparison is \(\tau=|T|\ll n\), not the number of named voters.

The mirror is especially plausible because the paper itself says that Split Cycle depends only on the margin graph (Remark 3.6). If \(\mu_t=n_t/n\), then

\[ \Delta_\mu(x,y) = \frac{\mathrm{Margin}_{\mathbf P}(x,y)}{n}. \]

All path strengths and all strict comparisons are merely rescaled by \(1/n\). Therefore

\[ SC_\infty(\mu)=SC(\mathbf P). \]

Conversely, every rational distribution can be expanded into a finite profile by multiplying by a common denominator. This gives the programme’s desired two-way high-multiplicity dictionary: the continuous instance is a compressed finite profile, and every rational continuous instance has a finite realization.

The expected classification is Class A. Compute all pairwise margins from the \(\tau\) types, then compute all widest return paths using a max–min Floyd–Warshall procedure. The running time is polynomial in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the encoding length of \(\mu\). This is not a deep continuous-optimization result: the underlying rule is already easy to evaluate on an explicitly listed finite profile. The benefit is compressed computation when the electorate is enormous and counts are given in binary.

The mirror covers only winner determination for Split Cycle, not the paper’s full axiomatic programme, Stable Voting’s recursive tiebreaking, the simulation results, or the impossibility theorem. It also generates natural follow-up problems: continuous candidate-entry stability using the distributions \(\mu\) and \(\mu_{-b}\), minimum-mass perturbations that make a chosen candidate uniquely undefeated, and continuous clone-splitting or winner-robustness questions.

The weakest point is decisive: because the paper has no genuine complexity result, this mirror does not demonstrate that discrete hardness dissolves in the continuum. It mostly changes the input representation of an already margin-based rule. I would therefore present it as a credible but low-impact Class A mirror, not as a flagship ChoCo result.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s own qualification is fatal under the programme’s anchor rule. This paper contains no named computational result to mirror. Lemma 3.17 is a structural characterization of Split Cycle’s defeat relation; the Floyd–Warshall procedure appears only as an accompanying algorithmic observation. It states no computational problem, input model, complexity bound, approximation result, or parameterized classification. The remaining numbered results concern axioms, impossibilities, resolvability, or probabilistic frequencies. Thus \(\mathrm{CSCWD}_\infty\) is not a continuous mirror of a computational result in the paper; it is a new problem invented around an axiomatic voting rule.

Grant the proponent the strongest possible repair: treat Lemma 3.17 and its algorithmic observation as an implicit anchor. The proposed high-multiplicity model is then perfectly legitimate. Voting is precisely a setting in which many agents can share complete ranking types, and rational \(\mu\) has an exact finite realization. Moreover,

\[ \Delta_\mu(x,y)=\frac{\mathrm{Margin}_{\mathbf P}(x,y)}{n}, \]

so all path strengths and defeat comparisons are preserved. There is no honest objection based on multiplicity, individual identity, or degeneration. The proponent is right that this is a valid continuous-population object.

But that concession also shows why it is a weak ChoCo anchor. The paper explicitly says that Split Cycle depends only on its margin graph. The proposed continuum therefore supplies no new population-level structure to the problem: it is simply another encoding of the same weighted directed graph. If the type list is explicit, the computation is the paper’s ordinary margin calculation followed by widest-path closure. If the type space is given succinctly, the real research issue is the chosen representation or oracle for \(\mu\), which the paper does not specify. Either way, the continuous distribution is not doing conceptual or algorithmic work of the kind illustrated by continuous bribery, where mass transfer creates a new optimization problem.

The suggested follow-ups do not repair the anchor. Candidate-entry stability is an axiomatic restatement of the paper’s stability results, not a computational problem. Clone-splitting is primarily a transformation of the candidate set, not continuization of the population. Minimum-mass perturbations or winner robustness could become worthwhile continuous optimization problems, but only after introducing a new intervention model, a cost metric, and a computational objective absent from the paper. They would be new bribery or robustness papers using Split Cycle, not mirrors of results proved here.

This is not the forbidden objection that “continuization does not help.” A well-posed continuous version could have a dull answer and still be worth asking. Nor is high multiplicity inappropriate: this is one of the more natural domains for it. The narrower and stronger point is that the paper offers no computational theorem whose continuous analogue the programme can study. Under the stated anchoring standard, the proposed mirror is an exact high-multiplicity encoding of an already axiomatic rule, not a worthwhile continuization of this paper’s computational contribution.

The negative case is therefore strong on the programme’s formal test but weak as a universal modelling claim. I cannot honestly claim that no useful continuous research question can ever be built around Split Cycle; I can claim that this paper, as written, does not supply one.
