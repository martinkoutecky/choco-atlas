The case FOR (proponent)

I can identify a credible high-multiplicity setting: a large virtual-democracy electorate with many agents sharing the same complete approval trajectory across a fixed sequence of issues. A type is one such trajectory, and its mass is its fraction of the electorate. The rule observes the approval masses round by round and chooses one alternative each time. This fits the paper’s population story, but it does not provide a qualifying anchor under your criteria.

The named results closest to an anchor are about rule properties, not computational complexity. Theorem 1, proved here, says that online independence of irrelevant alternatives implies online strategyproofness. Proposition 12, proved here, says Temporal Serial Dictator satisfies PJR asymptotically; Proposition 13, also proved here, says it is strategyproof. None asserts that a computational problem is in P, NP-hard, FPT, or a similar class. The paper’s statement that Perpetual Phragmén is polynomial-time computable cites [14]; it is not a numbered complexity result proved here.

There is also a serious modeling obstacle for those strategyproofness results: in an atomless society, one agent has zero mass and cannot affect an aggregate rule. Individual strategyproofness can therefore become vacuous; treating a positive-mass cohort as the manipulator changes the question to group strategyproofness. Temporal Serial Dictator also relies on ordering named voters, which has no direct continuum counterpart.

So my honest positive case stops at a plausible continuous-population regime. The paper has no named computational-complexity anchor that would support the required per-anchor continuous problem.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proponent’s proposed regime is credible: in a large virtual electorate, voters with the same complete approval trajectory can be represented by a type and its population share. That establishes a sensible continuous society. It does not supply this paper with a computational problem to mirror.

Theorem 1 is an implication between rule properties: OIIA guarantees OSP. Propositions 12 and 13 establish proportionality and strategyproofness properties of Temporal Serial Dictator. None asks for an algorithm, a complexity classification, or an approximation or parameterized result. The paper does describe TSD as efficiently computable, but gives no numbered complexity result for a computational problem; the polynomial-time claim for Perpetual Phragmén is cited as prior work. Recasting any of these as “decide whether a rule has this property” would introduce a new verification problem, rather than mirror a result the paper studies.

The strongest attempt to improve the mirror would keep approval trajectories as types and define proportionality bounds using their masses. That can make a continuous axiomatic question coherent. It still leaves the paper’s strategic results without their operative unit: a single voter. In an atomless society, one voter has zero mass, so an individual deviation cannot change an aggregate outcome. A positive-mass type changing its approvals instead models coordinated coalition manipulation. That may be worth studying, but it is a different strategic question. TSD also relies on cycling through named voters; the type distribution contains no order of individual voters from which to recover that rule.

There is a further axis mismatch in the asymptotic result. Proposition 12 takes the number of rounds \(T\) large. Continuizing \(T\) would continuize time, which is outside ChoCo’s scope. Holding \(T\) fixed and taking the population to a continuum could support a new analysis of mass-based fairness, but it would not be the result proved there.

So none of the named results raised by the proponent survives as a computational anchor. This is a strong case against spending ChoCo’s effort on a mirror of this paper, though not a proof that no related continuous research question could be worthwhile.
