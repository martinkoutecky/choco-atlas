The continuous mirror question

Given finite house categories \(H\), a finite set \(T\) of strict rankings closed under adjacent swaps, and rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma\in\Delta(H)\), let \(\rho_{\mu,t,h}\) be the allocation marginal for a tagged type-\(t\) student under the replica limit of random priority with background distribution \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\). Decide whether \(\rho\) is the unique Borel-measurable rule \(q_{\mu,t,h}\) such that, for every \(\mu\), \(q_{\mu,t,\cdot}\in\Delta(H)\), \(\sum_t\mu_tq_{\mu,t,h}=\sigma_h\), \(q_{\mu,t,h}=0\) whenever \(\rho_{\mu,t,h}=0\), and adjacent-swap reports \(t,t'\) satisfy \(q_{\mu,t,h}=q_{\mu,t',h}\) for every category \(h\) outside the swapped pair.

The model it lives in

A large replica housing market has a fixed finite set of room categories, per-capita supplies \(\sigma\), and a society described by masses \(\mu_t\) of students with complete ranking types \(t\). The decision variables \(q_{\mu,t,h}\) are tagged-student assignment marginals; the question is whether the replica-limit random-priority rule is uniquely determined by the supply, support-efficiency, and localizedness constraints for every \(\mu\).

The objection that survived

The paper compares profiles that differ in one named agent's report, while the mirror holds \(\mu\) fixed and compares reports by a zero-mass tagged agent; the proponent motivates this limit but does not establish the correspondence.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers the weakened characterization using equal treatment of equals, support efficiency, and localizedness. It leaves the full ex-post-efficiency and strategyproofness characterization, the deterministic-extreme-point result, and the restricted-domain counterexample aside.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can uniqueness for every \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\) be decided through a parametric rank test, with complexity stated in the numbers of categories and types and the input bit length?
  • Does fixed-\(\mu\) tagged-agent localizedness exactly capture the limit of the paper's finite adjacent-report comparisons?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest honest positive case is a large housing market with many students and many copies of a small set of room categories. Students who share a complete ranking and the same relevant circumstances form a type. Their masses \(\mu_t\) describe population shares; category supplies \(\sigma_h\) scale with the population. An individual still receives one room, while \(q_{\mu,t,h}\) is the share or probability of type-\(t\) students receiving category \(h\). This is a genuine population continuization, with replicated supplies as a necessary co-modification. Replica markets and their continuum assignment limits have been studied for random priority and probabilistic serial, including housing with a small number of room types.[Che and Kojima, “Asymptotic Equivalence of Probabilistic Serial and Random Priority Mechanisms”](https://www.columbia.edu/~yc2271/files/publications/psrp-Final.pdf)

My lead anchor is the paper’s displayed corollary on the rank test—the supplied text omits its numeral, but it is the sole corollary and appears to be Corollary 1. It says that the paper’s weakened characterization conjecture—RSD is the only rule satisfying equal treatment of equals, support efficiency, and localizedness—is equivalent to each of two statements: its matrix \(A\) has rank \(n^2 n!^n\), and \(\ker(A)=\{0\}\). This is proved in the paper, and the authors use the rank formulation in their algorithm to establish uniqueness through \(n=5\). I count it as an anchor under the prompt’s formulation exception: the paper computes with the formulation. It is not a polynomial-time or hardness classification. The NP-completeness claims about computing RSD probabilities in the introduction are cited from other papers, so they are not anchors here.

The mirror I would put to a prover is Continuum RSD Uniqueness. Its finite input is a category set \(H\), a finite report domain \(T\) of strict rankings closed under adjacent swaps, and rational per-capita supplies \(\sigma_h\) summing to one. The population state ranges over all distributions \(\mu\in\Delta(T)\); its support size is \(\tau\). Define \(\rho_{\mu,t,h}\) as the type-level marginal assignment in the replica limit of RSD, with a tagged type-\(t\) agent when needed. The question is whether \(\rho\) is the unique Borel-measurable assignment rule \(q_{\mu,t,h}\) satisfying, for every \(\mu\):

  • \(q_{\mu,t,h}\ge 0\), \(\sum_h q_{\mu,t,h}=1\), and \(\sum_t \mu_t q_{\mu,t,h}=\sigma_h\);
  • support efficiency: \(q_{\mu,t,h}=0\) whenever \(\rho_{\mu,t,h}=0\);
  • localizedness: if reports \(t,t'\) differ by swapping adjacent categories \(a,b\), then \(q_{\mu,t,h}=q_{\mu,t',h}\) for every \(h\notin\{a,b\}\).

Equal treatment is built into indexing the rule by complete type. A “yes” solution is a certificate that the corresponding parametric constraint operator has no nonzero kernel; a “no” solution is a population \(\mu\) and a feasible \(q_\mu\ne\rho_\mu\). As in the paper’s rank argument, support constraints set the same coordinates to zero as the reference rule, so a nonzero null direction can be mixed slightly with \(\rho\) to obtain a valid alternative rule.

I would tentatively expect the fixed-population linear test to be tractable: once the replica-limit support is known, uniqueness reduces to rank or LP checks on finitely many allocation variables. The uniform question over all \(\mu\) becomes parametric rank analysis. A credible algorithm would need running-time bounds in \(m\), \(\tau\), and the input bit length \(L\), and would need to account for computing the reference support. I would not claim that this paper supplies those bounds.

The weakest point is the gap between the anchor and the mirror. The paper characterizes rules over finite profiles with one distinct house per agent, and proves its main finite result only through \(n=5\). The mirror has per-capita supplies of repeated categories and takes a large-market limit; moreover, an infinitesimal agent’s report leaves \(\mu\) unchanged, while a finite agent’s report changes the profile. Localizedness at fixed \(\mu\) is a natural nonatomic interpretation, but it needs the authors’ assent. This case therefore covers only the paper’s weakened support-efficiency/localizedness characterization, not its full ex-post-efficiency and strategyproofness question.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The best case against the rank-corollary anchor is that it is an axiomatic uniqueness statement encoded as linear algebra, not a complexity result about a society supplied as an input. The matrix \(A\) has coordinates for a rule across *all* finite profiles, and \(n\) determines both the number of agents and the number of distinct houses. The paper gives no running-time classification in parameters like support size and bit length; its algorithm establishes the characterization only through \(n=5\). A continuum operator over distributions would therefore be a new characterization problem, not a high-multiplicity version of a computational problem this paper has solved.

That objection does not defeat the best mirror. A market with many students, a fixed set of room categories, and supplies that scale with the population is a sensible high-multiplicity regime. A tagged student can preserve meaningful report changes: in finite replica markets, changing one report shifts the population proportions by \(1/n\), while the tagged student’s limiting allocation remains meaningful. Fractional type-level averages are also natural even when each student receives one room. The cited continuous limits for random priority give a plausible reference allocation, though they do not settle its uniqueness under the paper’s axioms.

The remaining negative case is about fit with ChoCo’s computational remit. The proposed mirror asks whether a continuum assignment rule is uniquely characterized; it does not inherit the paper’s finite matrix or its cross-profile constraints in any immediate way. A pointwise version could become a finite rank test for each population, while the uniform question ranges over every distribution. But that is a reason to define and assess a new problem carefully, not a reason that the question is meaningless. I cannot honestly rule out a worthwhile mirror in this housing-market regime. The negative case is strongest as a warning that the paper supplies no computational result to mirror directly, and weakest precisely where the universal claim matters: the population continuization itself is plausible.
