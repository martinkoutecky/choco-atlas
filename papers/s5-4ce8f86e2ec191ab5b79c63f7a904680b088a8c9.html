The continuous mirror question

Given a finite set of types \(T\), rational masses \(\mu_t\), each type’s ranking of mutually acceptable partner types (with unmatched \(\bot\)), and an incumbent matching described by symmetric pair masses \(x^0_{tu}\) whose rows sum to \(\mu_t\), compute the incumbent’s unpopularity factor against all alternative matchings of the atomless population. A challenger can be represented by \(z_{tuv}\ge 0\), the mass of type \(t\) with incumbent partner type \(u\) and challenger partner type \(v\), subject to \(\sum_v z_{tuv}=x^0_{tu}\) and symmetric challenger pair masses \(y_{tv}=\sum_u z_{tuv}\). Maximize \(A(z)/B(z)\), where \(A(z)=\sum_{t,u,v:\,v\succ_t u}z_{tuv}\) and \(B(z)=\sum_{t,u,v:\,u\succ_t v}z_{tuv}\), using the paper’s \(0/0=1\) and positive-over-zero \(=\infty\) conventions.

The model it lives in

Use complete preference profiles over partner categories as types, with rational population mass and a fixed incumbent pair-mass matrix. Optimize over feasible incumbent-to-challenger assignment masses \(z_{tuv}\); the vote totals are linear functions of \(z\), and their ratio is a linear-fractional objective. Atomlessness permits realizing the feasible aggregate pairings while keeping each individual matched to at most one partner.

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Theorem 1’s computation for a given roommates matching. It leaves the marriage-problem results, the weighted variants, and results about finding a matching that minimizes unpopularity outside this anchor.

Open questions for a prover

  • Prove that the stated aggregate constraints characterize exactly the pairs of incumbent and challenger matchings realizable in an atomless population, including within-type pairings.
  • Formalize the replication dictionary between rational type masses and finite roommate instances, including degenerate instances with no distinct alternative matching.

The case FOR (proponent)

I would anchor the mirror on Theorem 1, proved in this paper in §3.1. It says that for a Roommates Problem instance \(I\) with \(n\) people and preference lists of total length \(m\), an algorithm computes the unpopularity factor of a *given* matching \(M\) exactly in \(O(m\sqrt n\log^2 n)\) time. Here \(u(M)\) is the maximum, over alternative matchings \(M'\), of the ratio of people who prefer \(M'\) to \(M\) to people who prefer \(M\) to \(M'\); the paper’s conventions make the ratio \(1\) when both counts are zero and \(\infty\) when only the denominator is zero. The paper permits ties. My proposed mirror covers this fixed-matching computation.

The plausible regime is a large roommate-placement system whose participants and preferences are organized around compatibility profiles. For example, a yearly intake of tens of thousands of residents might have only hundreds of profile types, each with the same acceptable partner profiles and the same ranking of them. A type is that complete preference description; its mass is its fraction of the population. The incumbent and any alternative are still roommate matchings: each individual gets at most one partner. Only the mass of people assigned across types can be divided. This is a population continuum, not a continuum of outcomes.

I would name the lead problem Roommates Unpopularity Factor\(_\infty\). Its input is a finite support \(T\) of \(\tau\) types, rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), each type’s weak ranking \(\succsim_t\) of acceptable partner types (with unmatched \(\bot\) ranked last), and an incumbent matching. Represent the incumbent by \(x^0_{tu}\), the mass of type-\(t\) people whose incumbent partner has type \(u\), for \(u\in T\cup\{\bot\}\). For distinct types \(t,u\), require \(x^0_{tu}=x^0_{ut}\); each row sums to \(\mu_t\). A diagonal entry \(x^0_{tt}\) is the mass of type-\(t\) people paired within their own type.

The question is to compute the exact unpopularity factor of this incumbent against all alternative roommate matchings of the same atomless population, or return \(\infty\). A finite certificate for a challenger is \(z_{tuv}\): the mass of type-\(t\) people whose incumbent partner is type \(u\) and challenger partner is type \(v\). Require \(z_{tuv}\ge 0\) and \(\sum_v z_{tuv}=x^0_{tu}\). The challenger’s type-to-partner masses are \(y_{tv}=\sum_u z_{tuv}\); these must respect mutual acceptability and satisfy \(y_{tv}=y_{vt}\) for distinct types. Atomlessness lets us split each type into the indicated cells and pair their members consistently under both matchings.

For each feasible challenger, its vote masses are \(A(z)=\sum_{t,u,v:\,v\succ_t u}z_{tuv}\) for people preferring the challenger, and \(B(z)=\sum_{t,u,v:\,u\succ_t v}z_{tuv}\) for people preferring the incumbent. Ties contribute to neither. The required answer is the maximum of \(A(z)/B(z)\), with the paper’s \(0/0=1\) and positive-over-zero \(=\infty\) conventions. The solution can include a maximizing \(z\) and a realization of its matching. A zero-vote re-pairing within a positive-mass cohort is a genuine alternative in the atomless model, so the \(0/0\) convention has an actual role here.

This preserves the paper’s central question: given a current matching, how badly can a different matching fare in a vote between them? It retains unmatched people, acceptability, ties, and the ratio objective. The types are complete for the decision rule: two residents belong to the same type only if the problem sees the same preference list for both. That makes the mass transfer a high-multiplicity version of the matching problem, rather than a relaxation of the matching outcome.

I expect this mirror to be Class A, tractable. The \(z\) variables and their matching constraints form a rational polytope with \(O(\tau^3)\) variables. Both vote masses are linear in \(z\), so the objective is linear-fractional. A prover can test the zero-denominator case separately and handle the finite optimum by standard rational linear-fractional or parametric LP methods. The target complexity would be polynomial in \(\tau\) and the binary input length \(L\), rather than in the raw population size. One useful bridge question is to formalize the replication relation: rational optimal masses can be scaled to integer cohort sizes, with a further factor if needed to pair members within a type.

The weakest point is that this model ranks partner types, whereas the paper describes lists of people. It is credible for a placement system whose decision rule actually uses compatibility categories, and the paper’s tie-permitting setting accommodates indifference among members of a category. It is less credible where people rank named individuals idiosyncratically: then complete types may proliferate, and the small-support advantage disappears. Category-level ties can also create neutral re-pairings with factor \(1\). Those are real properties of this mirror, not hidden implementation details.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

Theorem 1 is the strongest anchor, and the best objection is that the proposed compression may change what counts as a vote. In the paper, people compare the particular partners they receive. A complete type must therefore capture preferences over named people. In a large roommate market, those rankings can differ from person to person; then the number of types may grow with the population, and the incumbent matrix \(x^0\) loses information relevant to comparisons. That makes the proposed small-support advantage a poor fit for markets where partner choice is personal.

The proponent’s compatibility-profile scenario answers that objection, though. If people rank partner profiles and are indifferent among members of a profile, the model is coherent: it still matches individuals, and the paper already allows ties. The incumbent type-pair masses and challenger variables \(z_{tuv}\) preserve the information needed to count strict votes. Atomlessness makes the aggregate matching constraints realizable. The ratio can be optimized with linear-fractional programming, with the zero-denominator case checked separately. This is a genuine continuous-population version of the fixed-matching computation.

One might argue that profile rankings measure approval of partner categories rather than of actual partners, and that neutral re-pairings make the unpopularity factor less informative. But ties already permit indifference, and neutral alternatives only establish a baseline of \(1\); they do not determine whether some challenger has a larger ratio or makes it infinite. Neither point defeats the best version of the mirror.

So the negative case rests on the claim that plausible roommate markets lack a useful finite set of compatibility profiles. That may be true for some markets, but it is hard to defend universally, especially for cohort-based or category-driven placement. I cannot honestly defeat this anchor: Theorem 1 has a worthwhile continuous mirror in the profile-based regime, and the case against is weak.
