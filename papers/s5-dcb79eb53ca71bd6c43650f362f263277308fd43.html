The continuous mirror question

Given \(\tau\) complete binary CP-nets \(N_1,\ldots,N_\tau\), each with a chain or tree dependency graph, a rational mass vector \(\mu\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^{\tau}\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\), outcomes \(x,y\in\{0,1\}^n\), and \(q\in[0,1]\), compute \(D_\mu(x,y)=\sum_{t:x\succ_{N_t}y}\mu_t\), where \(x\succ_{N_t}y\) means that \(N_t\) entails an improving flipping sequence from \(y\) to \(x\), and decide whether \(D_\mu(x,y)\ge q\).

The model it lives in

Types are complete CP-net personas, including graph and conditional preference tables; \(\mu_t\) is each type’s population mass; the algorithm applies the paper’s dominance search per type, sums the supported mass, and returns flipping-sequence certificates, with \(q\) as the quorum threshold.

The objection that survived

The quorum computation is merely a weighted repetition of independent single-type dominance tests, and the paper does not establish a polynomial execution bound for the backtrack-free search.

fatal: False

What the mirror covers

The mirror covers Proposition 3’s chain- and tree-structured binary dominance-search guarantee; it leaves the suffix propositions, general-network heuristics, planning reformulation, pruning procedures, and application discussion alone.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can Proposition 3’s backtrack-free search be bounded and implemented in time polynomial in the CP-net and query encodings, and therefore in \(\tau\) for the quorum problem?
  • What happens when the support contains polytree or multiply connected CP-nets, or when \(x\) must be selected from a catalogue to maximise \(D_\mu(x,y)\)?

The case FOR (proponent)

My lead mirror is a cohort version of exact CP-net dominance, preserving the paper’s conditional-preference semantics rather than replacing them with averaged utilities.

The paper has no named NP-hardness, polynomial-time, FPT, or parameterized-complexity theorem. Its strongest named computational result is Proposition 3, proved in this paper: *the rightmost search heuristic, together with suffix-fixing and suffix-extension, is complete and backtrack-free for chain- and tree-structured CP-nets with binary variables*. Thus, for such a CP-net and query \(x \succ y\), the prescribed search finds a legal improving flipping sequence from \(y\) to \(x\) whenever one exists, without backtracking over alternative search branches. Propositions 1 and 2 provide the completeness-preserving suffix rules used by Proposition 3, but I would not treat them as separate anchors.

The continuous problem I would mirror is Quorum CP-Dominance\(_\infty\).

There are \(n\) Boolean features, outcomes \(x,y\in\{0,1\}^n\), and \(\tau\) voter types. A type \(t\) consists of a complete chain- or tree-structured binary CP-net \(N_t\), including its graph and all conditional preference tables. The society is given by rational masses \(\mu_t\ge 0\) with \(\sum_t\mu_t=1\). Agents of the same type have exactly the same conditional preferences and are indistinguishable for this problem. Given a quorum \(q\in[0,1]\), compute

\(D_\mu(x,y)=\sum_{t:\,x\succ_{N_t}y}\mu_t\),

where \(x\succ_{N_t}y\) means that the CP-net entails a legal improving flipping sequence from \(y\) to \(x\). The decision is whether \(D_\mu(x,y)\ge q\): should a product platform approve \(x\) as a replacement for \(y\) for at least a \(q\)-fraction of its users? A solution returns the exact supported mass, with flipping sequences as certificates for the supporting types.

This is a genuine continuous-population problem. The many agents are customers in a large product-configuration or recommendation service; their preferences have been elicited through a finite collection of recurring CP-net templates or user personas. For example, a service might have millions of customers but only hundreds or thousands of distinct preference types. Mass means the fraction of customers represented by each template. The type includes every feature relevant to the decision, so this is high multiplicity rather than pretending that heterogeneous individuals are identical.

The authors should recognise this as their problem’s continuous analogue. Their motivating applications explicitly include product databases, e-commerce recommendation, and configuration. The inner predicate is exactly their dominance query; the new element is that a platform must make a cohort-level decision based on the mass of types for which that query succeeds. Nothing is weakened to an additive score or majority over individual feature preferences.

I expect this problem to lie in Class A on the chain/tree binary regime. One runs the Proposition 3 search for each distinct type and sums the corresponding masses; the population layer itself is plainly polynomial in \(\tau\) and the encoding length of the \(\mu_t\). The important caveat is that Proposition 3 proves completeness and absence of backtracking, not a formal polynomial bound on the length of the flipping sequence. A complete positive result would therefore still need to establish such a bound, or give a compact implementation of the backtrack-free search. If general CP-net dominance turns out to be hard, that hardness would embed using a society with one type of mass \(1\), making it Class B rather than continuum-specific.

Natural follow-up questions include choosing \(x\) from an entire product catalogue to maximise \(D_\mu(x,y)\), handling incomplete CP-tables during elicitation, and extending the result to polytree or multiply connected CP-nets. The first may introduce hardness through the outcome space; the latter two would test the boundary of the structural result.

The weakest point is that the paper itself models one decision maker, not a population, so quorum aggregation is a new layer rather than an explicit problem in the article. Moreover, “backtrack-free” is not by itself a polynomial-time guarantee. Still, the proposed mirror keeps the paper’s exact underlying dominance question, gives mass a real operational role in cohort recommendation, and places the authors’ proved structural result directly inside a continuous high-multiplicity setting.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The proposed anchor does not actually mirror a computational result of the paper. Proposition 3 is a structural inference statement: on binary chain- and tree-structured CP-nets, a specified search heuristic is complete and backtrack-free. It gives no polynomial-time bound, complexity classification, or population-level problem. The paper contains no electorate, aggregation rule, intervention, or robustness objective.

The product-persona story is nevertheless plausible. A large recommendation service could contain many users sharing complete CP-net templates, and rational masses over those templates are a legitimate high-multiplicity representation. That is not the objection.

The problem is that the proposed quorum is only a wrapper around independent single-user queries:

\[ D_\mu(x,y)=\sum_t \mu_t\,\mathbf 1[x\succ_{N_t}y]. \]

One runs the original dominance procedure separately for each explicitly listed type and sums the answers. The masses do not constrain the searches, alter the CP-net semantics, or create an optimization problem. For rational masses, this is exactly the corresponding weighted count in a finite population after clearing denominators. Proposition 3 therefore “lifts” pointwise by repetition; it supplies no new continuous computational content. Nor does the paper establish that the backtrack-free search has polynomially bounded execution, so even the proposed Class A claim is not grounded in the stated result.

A stronger version could choose \(x\) to maximize \(D_\mu(x,y)\), or minimize the mass whose preferences must be changed. But then the substantive problem is new: outcome selection or campaigning over CP-net representations. The paper supplies neither a canonical distance between CP-nets nor a cost model for changing conditional tables and dependency graphs. Aggregating local CP-statements is no better: majority aggregation can create cycles, and aggregation does not commute with CP-net dominance closure. Such models may be worthwhile research, but they are not continuous mirrors of this paper’s result.

Thus the only convincing population construction is a sensible but computationally vacuous quorum wrapper; making it computationally substantive requires leaving the paper’s problem. The negative case is not airtight—if any population-level wrapper counts as a mirror, the persona scenario survives—but under ChoCo’s standard of anchoring a genuine computational result, this paper offers no worthwhile continuous mirror.
