The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The appended case supplies no continuous mirror anchor. Its only candidate is parameter estimation for the district models, and that does not survive as a paper-specific computational result: the paper reports ABC and regression workflows, simulation outcomes, and empirical fits, but no theorem or complexity result about a computational problem. That absence is the strongest objection under ChoCo’s criteria.

The best version of the parameter-estimation mirror would take rational masses over electoral types and districts, then infer concentration parameters from district-level vote-share data. That is a coherent statistical question, but it requires defining a new mass-valued model. DPM, ECM, and PCM generate outcomes through ordered voters, integer community counts, and finite-capacity checks such as \(m_k < v_k\). The paper gives no canonical scaling of those mechanisms into a continuum. Different choices for scaling \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), and \(\gamma\), or for retaining random community structure, define different limit models. If we simply normalize the observed vote counts, we get a finite-dimensional simulator-inference task; the paper’s claimed contribution remains empirical speed and fit, not an exact, approximation, or parameterized algorithm with a complexity result to mirror.

A stronger ComSoC proposal would optimize mass transfers among districts to increase a party’s seats or test winner robustness. But the paper fixes district capacities and treats assignments as random. It does not study control, campaigning, redistricting, or adversarial population movement. That proposal would add a decision problem and objective, rather than continuize one of the paper’s results.

So the paper-specific negative case is strong: its \(N\) voters remain discrete records, while its probability vector \(\theta\) describes party support, not a continuous population. The weakest part is the universal claim. Large electorates make continuous electoral models plausible, and a mass-allocation problem could be worthwhile for ChoCo. It would be a new problem inspired by this setting, though, rather than a continuous mirror anchored in this paper’s computational results.
