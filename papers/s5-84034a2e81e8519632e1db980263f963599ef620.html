The continuous mirror question

Given rational location types \(z_t\in\mathbb{Q}^2\) with rational masses \(\mu_t\geq 0\) summing to \(1\), a recommended site \(p\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), and rational \(\lambda\in[0,1)\), does the coordinatewise weighted median of the resident distribution together with advice mass \(\lambda\delta_p\) remain strategyproof on every common-denominator finite refinement and satisfy \(C_\mu(F_\lambda(\mu,p))/\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\leq\min\{\sqrt{2}\rho,\rho+\sqrt{2},\sqrt{2\lambda^2+2}/(1-\lambda)\}\), where \(C_\mu(a)=\sum_t\mu_t\|z_t-a\|_2\), \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=\min_a C_\mu(a)>0\), and \(\rho=C_\mu(p)/\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\)?

The model it lives in

Residents are location types \(z_t\in\mathbb{Q}^2\) with masses \(\mu_t\); the recommendation is fixed advice mass \(\lambda\delta_p\). The decision rule returns the coordinatewise weighted median, and performance is measured by \(C_\mu(a)=\sum_t\mu_t\|z_t-a\|_2\) against \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)\).

What the mirror covers

Covers the utilitarian two-dimensional facility-location guarantee of Theorem 2; leaves egalitarian facility location, scheduling, house allocation, auctions, and the empirical comparisons unmirrored.

Open questions for a prover

  • Can the bound be sharpened as a function of support size \(\tau\) or the geometry of the type locations?
  • What non-vacuous incentive notion, if any, should accompany the exact atomless limit beyond the finite-refinement guarantee?

The case FOR (proponent)

The strongest mirror I believe is High-Multiplicity Advice-Augmented Utilitarian Facility Location. It continuizes the residents, while keeping the paper’s facility outcome, recommendation, Euclidean costs, and incentive question.

An instance has location types \(z_t\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), positive rational masses \(\mu_t\) summing to \(1\), a recommended site \(p\in\mathbb{Q}^2\), and a rational confidence parameter \(\lambda\in[0,1)\). A type is a resident’s complete preference for facility locations: their cost at \(a\) is \(\|z_t-a\|_2\). The mass \(\mu_t\) is the fraction of residents of that type. The per-capita social cost and advice ratio are
\[ C_\mu(a)=\sum_t\mu_t\|z_t-a\|_2,\qquad \operatorname{OPT}(\mu)=\min_a C_\mu(a),\qquad \rho=\frac{C_\mu(p)}{\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)}. \]
Assume \(\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)>0\).

The question is: give a deterministic anonymous facility-location rule \(F_\lambda(\mu,p)\) whose finite refinements are strategyproof—each resident is one agent who may misreport their location—and whose population-level cost satisfies
\[ \frac{C_\mu(F_\lambda(\mu,p))}{\operatorname{OPT}(\mu)} \le \min\left\{\sqrt{2}\rho,\ \rho+\sqrt{2},\ \frac{\sqrt{2\lambda^2+2}}{1-\lambda}\right\}. \]
A solution is the rule and proofs of its incentive and approximation guarantees. The natural candidate is the coordinatewise weighted median of the resident distribution \(\mu\) together with recommendation mass \(\lambda\delta_p\). For any common denominator \(N\) with \(N\mu_t\) integral and \(N\lambda\) integral, this is exactly the paper’s mechanism: \(N\) residents plus \(N\lambda\) copies of the recommendation. Normalizing the paper’s total cost by \(N\) leaves all approximation ratios unchanged. The rule can be computed from the \(\tau\) support locations by sorting their coordinates, in polynomial time in \(\tau\) and the input bit length \(L\).

The anchor is Theorem 2, proved in this paper. It states that the Coordinatewise Median with Predictions mechanism—formed by adding \(\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor\) copies of the recommended point and taking a median separately in each coordinate—is \(\min\{\sqrt{2}\,\err,\ \err+\sqrt{2},\ \sqrt{2\lambda^2+2}/(1-\lambda)\}\)-approximate for utilitarian facility location, where \(\err\) is the recommendation’s cost divided by optimum. This paper proves the first two bounds; the robustness term is imported from Agrawal et al. The paper’s strategyproofness claim for this mechanism also comes from that prior work. On finite refinements, Theorem 2 gives the stated guarantee directly; the distribution formulation is the high-multiplicity representation of those profiles.

The regime is a large service-planning population whose residents share one of relatively few preferred service locations—for example, residents assigned to the same neighborhood hubs when planning a clinic. There may be millions of residents but only \(\tau\) distinct location types. This is a credible high-multiplicity regime because agents of one type have identical costs for every possible facility location. The authors should recognize the mirror: it is their same strategic facility-location problem and utilitarian objective, with repeated residents represented by type masses. The recommendation is still an advised facility site; the continuous object being introduced is the population.

This is a narrow Class A case: weighted medians make the selection rule easy to compute, while the paper supplies its approximation guarantees. It does not mirror the paper’s scheduling, house-allocation, or auction results. And the paper has no named NP-hardness, tractability-class, or parameterized-complexity result to anchor a complexity-transfer claim; Theorem 2 is an approximation result. So this is a genuine population mirror, but not a strong example of the programme’s central complexity-landscape contribution.

The weakest point is incentives in the exact atomless limit. A single resident has zero mass there, so their report cannot change \(\mu\), making individual strategyproofness vacuous. The finite-refinement formulation retains meaningful unilateral deviations at every \(N\), and the welfare guarantee carries over exactly for the stated rational masses and \(\lambda\); still, a referee could reasonably ask whether the limit itself is an acceptable strategic model. Further questions include whether the approximation bound can be improved when \(\tau\) is fixed, and how the guarantee changes in higher dimensions.

The case AGAINST (opponent, writing after the proponent)

The anchor is Theorem 2, and the strongest mirror is the one the proponent gives: residents are location types with masses, the recommendation contributes mass \(\lambda\), and the mechanism returns a coordinatewise weighted median. My best objection is about incentives. In a genuinely atomless society, one resident’s report changes the distribution by zero, so individual strategyproofness is vacuous. Treating each type as a strategic bloc would give a different incentive question.

Finite refinements avoid that problem: each resident has positive mass, the paper’s strategyproofness applies, and the approximation guarantee carries over. But that rescue also makes the mirror hard to dismiss. It is a coherent high-multiplicity family, and a city whose residents share a finite set of neighborhood hubs is a plausible regime. The continuum representation does not erase the strategic question at each finite refinement.

The remaining negative case is about fit with ChoCo’s computational aim. Theorem 2 gives an approximation guarantee for a specified mechanism; it does not pose a hard complexity problem. For rational masses, the mechanism can be evaluated by sorting the \(\tau\) support coordinates, and the paper’s guarantee transfers to common-denominator refinements. A mirror could be useful as a clean high-multiplicity corollary, but it is unlikely to expose the pricing, optimization, or complexity questions that motivate the programme.

That is a weak case against the anchor. The atomless incentive issue is real, but the finite-refinement formulation answers it in a way the authors could recognize. I cannot honestly claim that no worthwhile continuous mirror exists for this paper.
